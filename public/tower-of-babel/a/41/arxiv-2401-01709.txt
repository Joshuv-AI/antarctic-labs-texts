Abstract
. In this work, we investigate a mathematical model of nonlinear ultrasonic heating based on the Jordan–Moore–Gibson–Thompson equation (JMGT) with temperature-dependent medium parameters coupled to the semilinear Pennes equation for the bioheat transfer. The equations are coupled via the temperature in the coefficients of the JMGT equation and via a nonlinear source term within the Pennes equation, which models the absorption of acoustic energy by the surrounding tissue. Using the energy method together with a fixed point argument, we prove that our model is locally well-posed, provided that the initial data are regular, small in a lower topology and the final time is short enough.

1.
Introduction

The study of nonlinear wave propagation has garnered a lot of attention in recent years, notably due to recent applications in ultrasound imaging such as High-Intensity Focused Ultrasound (HIFU).
For instance, in medical procedures, focused ultrasound is used to generate localized heating that can destroy the targeted region. Indeed, this technique is proving its success in the treatment of both benign and malignant tumors
[
15
,
27
,
29
]
. This emerging method relies heavily on the ability to model accurately the nonlinear propagation of sound pulses in thermo-viscous elastic media.

In this paper, we consider the coupled system of the Jordan–Moore–Gibson–Thompson equation and the Pennes bioheat equation (JMGT–Pennes model):

(1.1a)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tau p_{ttt}+p_{tt}-c^{2}(\bar{\Theta})\Delta p-\delta(\bar{\Theta})\Delta p_{t}=K(\bar{\Theta})\left(p^{2}\right)_{tt},\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}\bar{\Theta}_{t}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\bar{\Theta}+\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})=\mathcal{Q}(\bar{\Theta},p_{t}),\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T).\end{aligned}\right.

The first equation in (
1.1a
) is the Jordan–Moore–Gibson–Thompson equation of nonlinear acoustics with temperature-dependent medium parameters, and the second equation is the Pennes bioheat equation
[
38
]
. The two equations are coupled via the temperature in the sound coefficients of the JMGT equation and via the function
\mathcal{Q}
in the Pennes equation modeling the acoustic energy being absorbed by the surrounding tissue.
Here
p
and
\bar{\Theta}
denote the acoustic pressure and the temperature fluctuations, respectively,
c(\bar{\Theta})
is the speed of sound, which is allowed to depend on the temperature, and

(1.1b)

\delta(\bar{\Theta})=\beta(\bar{\Theta})+\tau c^{2}(\bar{\Theta})

where
\beta
is a strictly positive function which represents the sound diffusivity and it is given by

(1.1c)

\beta(\bar{\Theta})=2\frac{\alpha c^{3}(\bar{\Theta})}{\omega^{2}},

where
\omega
stands for the angular frequency and
\alpha
for the acoustic amplitude absorption coefficient
[
12
]
.
The parameter

\tau>0
denotes a positive constant accounting for the relaxation time, and the function
K(\bar{\Theta})
is also allowed to depend on
\bar{\Theta}
and it is given by

K(\bar{\Theta})=\frac{\beta_{\text{acous}}}{\rho c^{2}(\bar{\Theta})},

where
\rho
is the mass density and
\beta_{\text{acous}}
is the parameter of nonlinearity.
The source term in the second equation
\mathcal{Q}(\bar{\Theta},p_{t})
represents the acoustic energy absorbed by the tissue.
We assume that the function
\mathcal{Q}
has the form
[
12
]

(1.1d)

\mathcal{Q}(\bar{\Theta},p_{t})=q(\bar{\Theta})(p_{t})^{2}\quad\text{with}\quad q(\bar{\Theta})=\frac{2\beta(\bar{\Theta})}{\rho_{\textup{a}}c^{4}(\bar{\Theta})}.

The medium parameters
\rho_{\textup{a}},C_{\textup{a}}
and
\kappa_{\textup{a}}
stand, respectively, for the ambient density, the ambient heat capacity and thermal conductivity of the tissue. The additional term
\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})
accounts for the heat loss due to blood circulation, with
\rho_{\textup{b}},C_{\textup{b}}
being the density and specific heat capacity of blood, and
W
expressing the tissue’s volumetric perfusion rate measured in milliliters of blood per milliliter of tissue per second.

We supplement (
1.1a
) with the initial conditions

(1.1e)

\displaystyle p|_{t=0}=p_{0},\quad p_{t}|_{t=0}=p_{1},\quad p_{tt}|_{t=0}=p_{2},\quad\bar{\Theta}|_{t=0}=\bar{\Theta}_{0}.

and Dirichlet boundary conditions

(1.1f)

\displaystyle p|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad\bar{\Theta}|_{\partial\Omega}=\Theta_{\textup{a}},

with
\Theta_{\textup{a}}
denoting the ambient temperature, that is typically taken in the human body to be
37^{\circ}C
; see
[
12
]
for more details.

When
c
,
\delta
and
K
are constants,
the first equation in (
1.1a
) reduces to the Jordan–Moore–Gibson–Thompson (JMGT)
[
16
]
, with the Westervelt nonlinearity written in terms of the acoustic pressure:

(1.2)

\tau p_{ttt}+p_{tt}-c^{2}\Delta p-\delta\Delta p_{t}=K\left(p^{2}\right)_{tt},

see
[
16
,
18
]
and reference therein for the derivation of (
1.2
).

Equation (
1.2
) is an alternative model to the classical Westervelt equation
[
44
]

(1.3)

p_{tt}-c^{2}\Delta p-\delta\Delta p_{t}=K\left(p^{2}\right)_{tt}.

By taking into account the nonlinear effect modeled by a quadratic velocity term, we obtain the JMGT equation with Kuznetsov nonlinearity (written in terms of the acoustic velocity potential
\psi
):

(1.4)

\tau\psi_{ttt}+\psi_{tt}-c^{2}\Delta\psi-\delta\Delta\psi_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(K(\psi_{t})^{2}+|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right).

The presence of the term
\tau p_{ttt}
in (
1.2
) (resp.
\tau\psi_{ttt}
in (
1.4
)) arises from employing the constitutive Cattaneo law of heat conduction instead of the classical Fourier law during the derivation process of the Westervelt (resp. Kuznetsov) equation. This, of course, changes completely the nature of the problem, since in equation (
1.3
), the presence of the term
-\delta\Delta p_{t}
yields a parabolic structure of the equation, thereby inducing a regulazation effect. This is not the case for the hyperbolic type equations (
1.2
) and (
1.4
), hence requiring different types of estimates than the related second-order equations.

[

The singular limit problem when
\tau\rightarrow 0
has been rigorously justified in
[

]
where the authors showed that the limit of (

) as

leads to the Kuznetsov equation. See also the recent contributions

[

For thermal modeling, the first equation in (
1.1a
) is coupled with the Pennes bioheat equation:

(1.5)

\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}\bar{\Theta}_{t}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\bar{\Theta}+\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})=0.

Equation (
1.5
) is a widely used model for studying heat transfer in biological systems. It takes into account the heat transfer by conduction in the tissues and the convective heat transfer due to blood perfusion. See
[
38
]
for the derivation of (
1.5
).

The presence of the constant
\tau
in (
1.1
) is due to the use of the Cattaneo model for hyperbolic heat conduction in the derivation of the JMGT equation
from the equations of fluid dynamics. It is well known that the Cattaneo law eliminates the so-called infinite speed of propagation paradox observed when parabolic heat models are used
[
8
,
9
]
.
In fact, when
\tau=0
, system (
1.1a
) reduces to the Westervelt–Pennes–Fourier system:

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &p_{tt}-c^{2}(\bar{\Theta})\Delta p-b\Delta p_{t}=k(\bar{\Theta})\left(p^{2}\right)_{tt},\\
&\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}\bar{\Theta}_{t}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\bar{\Theta}+\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})=\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\end{aligned}\right.

which has been investigated recently by Nikolić and Said-Houari in
[
34
]
and
[
35
]
. More precisely, for Dirichlet–Dirichlet boundary conditions, they proved in
[
34
]
a local well-posedness result of (
1.6
) by employing the energy method together with a fixed point argument. In
[
34
]
the global existence result and the asymptotic behavior of the solution of (
1.6
) under a smallness assumption on the initial data, was established. Using the maximal regularity estimate for parabolic systems, Wilke in
[
45
]
improves slightly the regularity assumptions in
[
34
]
and also considered the case
b=b(\bar{\Theta})
. We also mention the recent result in
[
31
]
where a local well-posedness result was shown for mixed Neumann and nonlinear absorbing boundary conditions in the acoustic component and Dirichlet boundary condition for the temperature.

Recently, the authors of this paper considered in
[
2
]
the Westervelt–Pennes–Cattaneo model where the second equation in (
1.6
) is replaced by its hyperbolic version (see
[
17
, Eq. 3]
and
[
46
, Eq. 7]
):

(1.7)

\displaystyle\tau\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}

\displaystyle\bar{\Theta}_{tt}+(\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}+\tau\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W)\bar{\Theta}_{t}+\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\bar{\Theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}).

Based on higher-order energy estimates and
on the contraction mapping principle, we proved the existence and uniqueness of a strong solution of the Westervelt–Pennes–Cattaneo system. In addition, using uniform estimates with respect to the relaxation parameter

\tau
, we showed that
the solutions to the Westervelt–Pennes–Cattaneo problem converge to solutions of the
Westervelt–Pennes–Fourier system provided the relaxation parameter
\tau
tends to zero.

In this paper, we consider the JMGT–Pennes system (
1.1
) and establish its local well-posedness, provided that the initial data satisfies

(1.8)

(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta_{0}\in H^{3}(\Omega),

with a smallness condition on the pressure data that can be imposed on a lower-order norm than the one needed in the energy analysis, provided that the final time is short enough. If we require a smallness assumption on the full energy norm for the pressure data, then the final time can be made arbitrarily large. From the technical point of view, the smallness assumption on the lower-topology makes the proof more involved since some extra estimates of lower-order energy are needed (see Proposition
4.1
). These estimates should be properly factored out in the nonlinear estimates using the interpolation inequality (
3.4
).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Section
3
contains the necessary theoretical preliminaries, where we collect some theoretical results that will prove useful in the sequel. In Section
2
, we introduce the general assumptions on the coefficients in system (
1.1
) and state the main result of this paper. Section
4
is devoted to the energy analysis of the JMGT equation with variable coefficients and we prove several energy estimates, while Section
5
treats the linearized Pennes equation. Although the two linearized problems in Sections
4
and
5
are decoupled, the primary difficulty lies in maintaining consistency in the estimates for each linearized equation. This is due to the
coupling in the nonlinear problem via the source term
\mathcal{Q}(\bar{\Theta},p_{t})
and via the coefficients (which depend on
\bar{\Theta}
) within the JMGT equation. Recognizing this nonlinear characteristic of the problem inherently suggests a correlation between the estimates obtained from the linearized problems.
In Section
6
, we prove the local well-posedness of the nonlinear problem (
1.1
).

2.
Statement of the problem and main result

In this section, we state our local well-posedness result. Before that, we introduce the essential assumptions concerning the coefficients and the source terms in (
1.1
).

For clarity of exposition, we set

(2.1)

m=\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ell=\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W,

and make the change of variables

\Theta=\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{a}

in the temperature, then we can recast system (
1.1
) as

(2.2a)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tau p_{ttt}+(1-2k(\Theta)p)p_{tt}-h(\Theta)\Delta p-\zeta(\Theta)\Delta p_{t}=2k(\Theta)\left(p_{t}\right)^{2},\,&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
&m\Theta_{t}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\Theta+\ell\Theta=\Phi(\Theta,p_{t}),\,&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T).\end{aligned}\right.

System (
2.2a
) is complemented with homogeneous boundary conditions

(2.2b)

\displaystyle p|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad\Theta|_{\partial\Omega}=0

and the initial conditions

(2.2c)

\displaystyle p|_{t=0}=p_{0},\quad p_{t}|_{t=0}=p_{1},\quad\quad p_{tt}|_{t=0}=p_{2},\quad\Theta|_{t=0}=\Theta_{0}:=\bar{\Theta}_{0}-\Theta_{\textup{a}}.

The involved functions
h,\zeta,k
and
\Phi
are given in terms of
c,\delta,K
and
\mathcal{Q}
as follows

(2.3)

\displaystyle k(\Theta)=K(\Theta+\Theta_{\textup{a}})=\frac{1}{\rho c^{2}(\Theta+\Theta_{\textup{a}})}\beta_{\text{acou}},\qquad h(\Theta)=c^{2}(\Theta+\Theta_{\textup{a}}),

\displaystyle\zeta(\Theta)=\delta(\Theta+\Theta_{\textup{a}}),\quad\Phi(\Theta,p_{t})=\phi(\Theta)(p_{t})^{2}\quad\text{with}\quad\phi(\Theta)=\frac{2\beta(\Theta+\Theta_{\textup{a}})}{\rho_{\textup{a}}h^{2}(\Theta)}.

In order to prove the well-posedness result of (
2.2
), we make the following assumptions on the functions
h,\,\zeta,\,k
and
\Phi
and on the initial data. Note that the conditions on the medium parameters reflect a polynomial dependence on the temperature, since typically the speed of sound
c=c(\Theta)
and the diffusivity of sound
\beta=\beta(\Theta)
are determined using a least-square fit of experimental observations, see
[
12
]
.

Assumption 1

(Assumptions on the coefficients and source terms)
.

We assume that
h\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and there exists
h_{1}>0
such that

(H1)

h(s)\geq h_{1},\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, assume that there exist
\gamma_{1}>0
and
C>0
, such that

(H2)

|h^{\prime\prime}(s)|\leq C(1+|s|^{\gamma_{1}}),\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Using Taylor’s formula, we also have

(H3)

|h^{\prime}(s)|\leq C(1+|s|^{1+\gamma_{1}}),\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Since the function
k
is related to the speed of sound by the formula (
2.3
), it follows that

(K1)

|k(s)|\leq k_{1}:=\frac{\beta_{\text{acous}}}{\rho h_{1}}.

Further, we have

(2.5a)

\displaystyle|k^{\prime\prime}(s)|

\displaystyle\lesssim k_{1}^{2}|h^{\prime\prime}(s)|+k_{1}^{3}|h^{\prime}(s)|^{2}\lesssim k_{1}^{2}(1+|s|^{\gamma_{1}})+k_{1}^{3}(1+|s|^{1+\gamma_{1}})^{2},

which by using Taylor’s formula, implies that there exists
\gamma_{2}>0
, such that

(K2)

|k^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim(1+|s|^{1+\gamma_{2}}),\qquad|k^{\prime\prime}(s)|\lesssim(1+|s|^{\gamma_{2}}).

Similarly, since
\zeta
is proportional to the speed of sound (see (
2.3
), (
1.1b
)), we can assume that there exit
\zeta_{1}>0
and
\gamma_{3}>0
such that

(Z)

\zeta(s)\geq\zeta_{1},\quad|\zeta^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim(1+|s|^{1+\gamma_{3}}),\qquad|\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)|\lesssim(1+|s|^{\gamma_{3}}),\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}.

In addition, from the definition of the functions
\phi,\beta,h
(
2.3
), (
1.1c
), we can see that

\phi(\Theta)\sim\frac{1}{c(\Theta)}.

Then,
\phi
satisfies

(F1)

|\phi(s)|\leq\phi_{1},\quad s\in\mathbb{R}

and as for the function
k
, there exists
\gamma_{4}>0
such that

(F2)

|\phi^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim(1+|s|^{1+\gamma_{4}}),\qquad|\phi^{\prime\prime}(s)|\lesssim(1+|s|^{\gamma_{4}}),\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}.

Assumption 2

(Assumptions on the initial data)
.

Let the initial data have the regularity

(2.7)

\displaystyle(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in

\displaystyle\big[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big]\times\big[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big]\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}\in

\displaystyle H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

and let the compatibility condition be satisfied

(2.8)

\Theta_{1}=\Theta_{t}|_{t=0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

2.1.
Main result

The main contribution of this work pertains to the existence and uniqueness of a local in-time solution to the coupled system (
2.2
).
In order to state and prove our main result, we introduce the following spaces of solutions

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{p\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),p_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle p_{tt}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\big\{\Theta\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\Theta_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\Theta_{tt}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\big\}.

Now, we are in position to state the local well-posedness result for the problem (
2.2
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Given
\tau>0
. Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. There exist a final time
T>0
, small
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(T)>0
and a constant
R>0
such that if the initial data satisfy

(2.10)

\|p_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|p_{1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\tau\|p_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\eta_{0}^{2},

the system (
2.2
) admits a unique solution
(p,\Theta)\in\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}\times\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}
that satisfies the estimate

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\tau\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}H^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}H^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{tt}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}\leq R^{2}.

Moreover, the solution depends continuously on the data with respect to the norm in
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}\times\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}
.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
will be given in Section
6
. But now we briefly make a few comments on the statement above.

1.

We arrive at the conclusion of Theorem
2.1
above by assuming a smallness condition on the pressure data on a lower-order norm than the one needed in the energy analysis, provided that the final time is short enough. Indeed, the requirement for the pressure data to be small arises naturally within the proof of the local well-posedness, and it is related to a nondegeneracy issue that characterizes nonlinear acoustic models as the one considered here, see
[
23
,
34
,
4
]
and reference therein. We point out that
T
can be made arbitrarily large if the smallness assumption is imposed on the full energy norm of the pressure data. See the estimate (
6.80
). However, we prefer the former scenario since it is more relevant in ultrasound applications
[
25
]
, where usually the final time is short and the data are smooth but not small. As for the bioheat pennes equation, no smallness assumption is required on the temperature data.

2.

It is known (see for instance
[
19
]
) that for the JMGT equation (
1.2
) there is a critical parameter

(2.12)

\chi=\delta-\tau c^{2},

that controls the behavior of the solution. More precisely, the authors in
[
19
]
proved that the problem (
1.2
) is globally well-posed and its solution is exponentially stable if
\chi>0
. Although, this condition seems necessary to study the global existence and asymptotic behavior of the solution of (
1.2
) and (
1.4
), it is unnecessary for the local existence theory as in the present paper (see also
[
21
]
).

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper, we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
d\in\{1,2,3\}
, is a bounded domain with a
C^{3}
boundary. We denote by
T>0
the final propagation time. The letter
C
denotes a generic positive constant
that does not depend on time, and can have different values on different occasions.
We often write
f\lesssim g
(resp.
f\gtrsim g
) when there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of parameters of interest such that
f\leq Cg
(resp.
f\geq Cg
).
We often omit the spatial and temporal domain when writing norms; for example,
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}L^{q}}
denotes the norm in
L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))
.

3.2.
Inequalities and embedding results

In the upcoming analysis, we shall employ the continuous embeddings
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
,
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. In particular, using Poincaré’s inequality we obtain for
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(see
[
42
, Theorem 7.18]
)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{if}\ d>2,\quad\|v\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq p\leq\frac{2d}{d-2},

\displaystyle\text{if}\ d=2,\quad\|v\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq p<\infty.

Moreover, taking into account the boundedness of the operator
(-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we obtain the inequality

\qquad\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{1}\|v\|_{H^{2}}\leq C_{2}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}.

We will also make a repeated use of Young’s
\varepsilon
-inequality

(3.2)

xy\leq\varepsilon x^{n}+C(\varepsilon)y^{m},\quad\text{where}\quad\ x,y>0,\quad 1<m,n<\infty,\quad\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n}=1,

and
C(\varepsilon)=(\varepsilon n)^{-m/n}m^{-1}
.

One particular form of (
3.2
) that we frequently use in the proof is the following:

(3.3)

xy\leq\varepsilon x^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}y^{2}.

We will also need Agmon’s interpolation inequality
[
1
, Ch. 13]
for functions in
H^{2}(\Omega)
:

(3.4)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\textup{A}}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{d/4}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-d/4},\qquad d\leq 4

and Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality for functions in
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
:

(3.5)

\|u\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\textup{A}}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{d/4}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-d/4},\qquad d\leq 4.

To keep the presentation self-contained, we also state here the precise version of Gronwall’s inequality that will be employed in the proofs.

Lemma 3.1
.

We assume that
u\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R})
,
T\in(0,\infty)
, satisfies the integral inequality

(3.6)

u(t)\leq u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}a(s)u(s)\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}b(s)\,\textup{d}s,\quad\text{on}\quad[0,T),

for some
0\leq a\in L^{1}(0,T)
and
b\in L^{1}(0,T)
. Then it holds that

(3.7)

u(t)\leq u_{0}e^{A(t)}+\int_{0}^{t}b(s)e^{A(t)-A(s)}\,\textup{d}s,\quad\forall t\in(0,T),

where

(3.8)

A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(s)\,\textup{d}s.

4.
Analysis of the linearization of the JMGT equation

The method of the proof of the local existence is based on the application of the contraction mapping theorem to solutions of a related linear problem. For this reason, we need to analyze two linear (decoupled) systems, one associated with the JMGT equation and the other one with the Pennes model. First, we consider the following linearization of the first equation in system (
2.2
):

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tau p_{ttt}+p_{tt}-r(x,t)\Delta p-b(x,t)\Delta p_{t}=f(x,t),\qquad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)\\
&p|_{t=0}=p_{0},\quad p_{t}|_{t=0}=p_{1},\quad p_{tt}|_{t=0}=p_{2},\\
&p|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We are able to establish the well-posedness of (
4.1
) under the following regularity and non-degeneracy conditions on the coefficients
b
and
r
.

Assumption 3
.

Assume that

•

r,\,b\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\Omega))
and
r_{t},\,b_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
,

•

There exist
0<b_{0}\leq b_{1}
and
0<r_{0}\leq r_{1}
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle b_{0}\leq b(x,t)\leq b_{1}\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T);

\displaystyle r_{0}\leq r(x,t)\leq r_{1}\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T).

In what follows, we prove two results on the existence of solution of the linear problem (
4.1
). Precisely, we establish well-posedness respectively for initial data
(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
(Proposition
4.1
) and for
(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in\big[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big]\times\big[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big]\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(Proposition
4.2
).
This is achieved by employing the energy analysis for smooth Galerkin approximations of the solutions. We will focus our attention on providing the energy bounds that will later be useful when we address the well-posedness of the nonlinear problem (
2.2
). We omit the other steps in Galerkin method since they are quite classical and are already exposed in details in the context of the JMGT equation in
[
22
]
. We note that in view of the hyperbolic nature of the equation (
4.1
) and the time-space variability of the coefficients of the higher-order derivatives, it is crucial that
r_{t},\,b_{t}
belong to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. It does not seem feasible that such assumption can be weakened even if we attempt to counteract by increasing the regularity of the initial data.

4.1.
First Energy estimate

In the first functional setting, the energies
E_{0},E_{1}
are defined by

(4.3)

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](t):=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{r}\nabla p(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle E_{1}[p](t):=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\tau\|p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big),\qquad t\geq 0.

The dissipation rate of the total energy
E_{0}+E_{1}
is

(4.4)

\displaystyle D_{0}[p](t):=\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad t\geq 0.

We define the space of solutions
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{1}
as

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{p}^{1}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{p\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),\quad p_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle p_{tt}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\Big\}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T>0,\,\tau>0
. Assume that
f\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and that the functions
r
and
b
satisfy Assumption
3
. Given

(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega),

then, the problem (
4.1
) has a unique solution
p\in\mathcal{X}_{p}^{1}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{p,1}(\tau)>0
, that does not depend on
T
, such that for all
0\leq t\leq T
it holds

(4.6)

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](t)+E_{1}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}D_{0}(s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,1}\Big[\Big(E_{0}[p](0)+E_{1}[p](0)\Big)\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{0}(s)\,\textup{d}s\right\}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}F_{0}(s)\exp\left\{\int_{s}^{t}\Lambda_{0}(r)\ \textup{d}r\right\}\,\textup{d}s\Big].

where

(4.7)

\Lambda_{0}(t)=1+\|r_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|r_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|b_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla b(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla r(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}},

and

(4.8)

F_{0}(t)=\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

We multiply the equation (
4.1
) by
p_{t}
and integrate over
\Omega
, using integration by parts, we get

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{r}\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}p_{t}\,\textup{d}x)+\tau\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\nabla b\cdot\nabla p_{t}p_{t}\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\nabla r\cdot\nabla pp_{t}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}r_{t}|\nabla p|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}fp_{t}\,\textup{d}x,

where we have used the identity

(4.10)

\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{ttt}p_{t}\,\textup{d}x=\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\big)-\tau\|p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Applying Hölder and Young inequalities yields

(4.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{r}\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}p_{t}\,\textup{d}x)+\tau\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon)(\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla r\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\varepsilon\|p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Since
p_{t}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, we can employ Poincaré’s inequality to get

(4.12)

\|p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\frac{1}{b}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

by using Assumption
3
. Plugging (
4.12
) into (
4.11
) and
choosing
\varepsilon
small enough, we obtain

(4.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}E_{0}[p](t)+\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\tau+\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla r\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})(\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\Big)

Integrating from
0
to
t
and keeping Assumption
3
in mind, it follows

(4.14)

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](0)+\int_{\Omega}\tau|p_{tt}(t)p_{t}(t)|\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\tau|p_{2}p_{1}|\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(\tau+\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla r\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\nabla b\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})(E_{0}[p](s)+E_{1}[p](s))\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s.

Next, we have for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tau|p_{tt}(t)p_{t}(t)|\,\textup{d}x\leq C(\varepsilon)\tau\|p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\tau\|p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\forall t\geq 0,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tau|p_{2}p_{1}|\,\textup{d}x\leq\frac{\tau}{2}\|p_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\|p_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By inserting the estimates in (
4.15
) into (
4.14
) and selecting
\varepsilon
suitably small, we obtain

(4.16)

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](0)+E_{1}[p](0)+\tau\|p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{0}(s)(E_{0}[p](s)+E_{1}[p](s))\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s.

Now, we multiply the equation (
4.1
) by
p_{tt}
and we integrate over
\Omega
to arrive at the identity

(4.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}E_{1}[p](t)+\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla r\cdot\nabla pp_{tt}\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega}r\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}b_{t}|\nabla p_{t}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla b\cdot\nabla p_{t}p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}fp_{tt}\,\textup{d}x.

Our next goal is to estimate the right-hand side of (
4.17
).
Applying Hölder and Young inequalities, we find

(4.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}E_{1}[p](t)+\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}r\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\|b_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon)(\|\nabla r\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla b\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}})+\varepsilon\|p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Hence, integrating in time and taking account of Assumption
3
, we have for
\varepsilon
small enough

(4.19)

\displaystyle E_{1}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|p_{tt}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{1}[p](0)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}r\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(\|b_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla b\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla r\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}})(E_{0}[p](s)+E_{1}[p](s))\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
4.19
) can be handled as follows

(4.20)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}r\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\left(\int_{\Omega}r\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\right)+\int_{\Omega}r_{t}\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{t}\,\textup{d}x+\|\sqrt{r}\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\left(\int_{\Omega}r\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\right)+\frac{1}{2}\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{r}\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Integrating over time and exploiting Assumption
3
, it results that

(4.21)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}r\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x\,\textup{d}s\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|r(0)\nabla p_{0}\cdot\nabla p_{1}|\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|r(t)\nabla p(t)\cdot\nabla p_{t}(t)|\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(1+\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})(\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\,\textup{d}s.

Using Hölder and Young inequalities, we can estimate the first two integrals on the right-hand side of the above inequality to find

(4.22)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}r\nabla p\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla p_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\left\|\frac{r(t)}{\sqrt{b(t)}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{b(t)}\nabla p_{t}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(1+\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})(E_{0}[p](s)+E_{1}[p](s))\,\textup{d}s.

Plugging inequality (
4.22
) into (
4.19
) and taking
\varepsilon
small enough so as to get the
\varepsilon
-term in (
4.22
) absorbed by the left-hand side of (
4.19
), we obtain

(4.23)

\displaystyle E_{1}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|p_{tt}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](0)+E_{1}[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{0}(s)(E_{0}[p](s)+E_{1}[p](s))\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\|\nabla p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s.

Observe that for
0\leq t\leq T
, we have

(4.24)

\displaystyle\|\nabla p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{b}\nabla p_{t}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}E_{1}[p](s)\,\textup{d}s.

Hence, combining (
4.24
) with (
4.23
), we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle E_{1}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|p_{tt}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](0)+E_{1}[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{0}(s)(E_{0}[p](s)+E_{1}[p](s))\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s.

Now with the aim of absorbing the term
\tau\|p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
from the right-hand side of (
4.16
), we add
N_{1}\times
(
4.25
) to (
4.16
) and select
N_{1}
large enough (i.e.,
\frac{N_{1}}{2}\gtrsim 1
), we conclude

(4.26)

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](t)+E_{1}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}D_{0}[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{0}[p](0)+E_{1}[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda_{0}(s)(E_{0}[p](s)+E_{1}[p](s))\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s.

Applying the Gronwall’s inequality (Lemma
3.1
) yields the desired result (
4.6
).
∎

4.2.
Higher-order energy estimate

Due to the coupling between the pressure equation and the bioheat equation being via the coefficients in the former and the source term in the latter, the regularity of the acoustic pressure guaranteed by Proposition
4.1
is not enough to give the temperature
\Theta
the right smoothness so that Assumption
3
will be meaningful when we get to the study of the nonlinear problem (
2.2
). Therefore, we need to show the existence of more regular solutions to the problem (
4.1
). This will be done again by relying on Galerkin’s smooth approximations of the solution and on higher-order energy estimates as in
[
21
]
.

For this purpose, we define the higher-order energy associated with the equation (
4.1
) by

(4.27)

\displaystyle E[p](t):=\frac{1}{2}\big(\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tau\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big).

We also introduce the corresponding dissipation

(4.28)

D[p](t):=\|\sqrt{r}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

We prove the following existence result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\tau>0
and
T>0
. Assuming that
f\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and that Assumption
3
holds. Suppose that

(4.29)

(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in\big[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big]\times\big[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big]\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Then, the system (
4.1
) admits a unique solution
p\in\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}
(see (
2.9
)). Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{p,2}(\tau)>0
, that does not depend on
T
, such that the solution satisfies the following estimate for
0\leq t\leq T

(4.30)

\displaystyle E[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}D[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,2}\Big[E[p](0)\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda(s)\,\textup{d}s\Big)+\int_{0}^{t}F(s)\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}\Lambda(r)\textup{d}r\Big)\,\textup{d}s\Big]

where
\Lambda
is defined as

(4.31)

\Lambda(t)=1+\|b_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|b_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|r_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

and

F(t)=\|f(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla f(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

We begin by multiplying the equation (
4.1
) by
-\Delta p
, and integrating over
\Omega
, using, integration by parts, we find

(4.32)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{r}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(p_{tt}-f)\Delta p\,\textup{d}x+\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}\Delta p\,\textup{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}b_{t}|\Delta p|^{2}\,\textup{d}x,

where we have used the identity

(4.33)

\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{ttt}\Delta p\,\textup{d}x=\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}\Delta p\,\textup{d}x\big)-\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x.

In order to estimate the right-hand side of (
4.32
), we first use Hölder and Poincaré inequalities to obtain

(4.34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{r}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}\Delta p\,\textup{d}x\Big)+\big(\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}+\|b_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}\big)\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\tau\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}.

From here, by applying Young’s inequality, we find

(4.35)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{r}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}\Delta p\,\textup{d}x\Big)+C(\varepsilon)\Big(\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|b_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{r}\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By selecting
\varepsilon
small enough, the
\varepsilon
-term on the right-hand side can be absorbed by the dissipative term on the left-hand side. Then, on account of Assumption
3
, we have

(4.36)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{r}\Delta p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\int_{\Omega}\tau p_{tt}\Delta p\,\textup{d}x\big)+\big(1+\frac{1}{\tau}+\tau+\|b_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)E[p](t)+\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating from
0
to
t
yields the following bound for
0\leq t\leq T

(4.37)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{b(t)}\Delta p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{r(s)}\Delta p(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tau|p_{tt}(t)\Delta p(t)|\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\tau|p_{2}\Delta p_{0}|\,\textup{d}x+\|\sqrt{b(0)}\Delta p_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big(1+\tau+\frac{1}{\tau}+\|b_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)E[p](s)\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s.

We can use Hölder and Young inequalities to bound the first two terms on the right-hand side as follows:

(4.38)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\tau|p_{tt}(t)\Delta p(t)|\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\tau|p_{2}\Delta p_{0}|\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\tau^{2}\|p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau}{2}\|p_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau}{2}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{b(0)}\Delta p_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Due to Assumption
3
, we can combine (
4.37
) and (
4.38
) and use Poincaré’s inequality to obtain, for small enough
\varepsilon
, the following bound

(4.39)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{b(t)}\Delta p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{r(s)}\Delta p(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+\tau)\|\sqrt{b(0)}\Delta p_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tau\|\nabla p_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big(1+\tau+\frac{1}{\tau}+\|b_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)E[p](s)\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+\tau)E[p](0)+\tau^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(1+\tau+\frac{1}{\tau}+\|b_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)E[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s.

Next, we multiply the equation (
4.1
) by
-\Delta p_{tt}
and we integrate over
\Omega
to get

(4.40)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\|\sqrt{\tau}\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}r\Delta p\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\Big)+\int_{\Omega}r_{t}\Delta p\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}r|\Delta p_{t}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}b_{t}|\Delta p_{t}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla f\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x

where we have used the identity

(4.41)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}r\Delta p\Delta p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x=-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}r\Delta p\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\Big)+\int_{\Omega}r_{t}\Delta p\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}r|\Delta p_{t}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x.

Applying Hölder’s inequality gives

(4.42)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\|\sqrt{\tau}\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+\|r\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|b_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}r\Delta p\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\Big).

Further, making use of Young’s inequality yields

(4.43)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\|\sqrt{\tau}\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\Big(1+\|r\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|b_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big)\Big(\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}r\Delta p\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\Big).

The
\varepsilon
-term on the right can be absorbed by the dissipation on the left-hand side for suitably small
\varepsilon
. Thus, recalling Assumption
3
, it follows that

(4.44)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\|\sqrt{\tau}\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+\|b_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|r_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)E[p](t)+\|\nabla f(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\int_{\Omega}r\Delta p\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x\Big).

To derive abound for the last term on the right-hand side of the above estimate, we first integrate in time to get for all
0\leq t\leq T

(4.45)

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{b(t)}\Delta p_{t}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla p_{tt}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla p_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{b(0)}\Delta p_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(1+\|b_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|r_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)E[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s+\int_{\Omega}|r(t)\Delta p(t)\Delta p_{t}(t)|\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|r(0)\Delta p_{0}\Delta p_{1}|\,\textup{d}x.

With the help of Hölder and Young inequalities together with Assumption
3
, we can show that for all
0\leq t\leq T
, the following estimate holds

(4.46)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|r(t)\Delta p(t)\Delta p_{t}(t)|\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|r(0)\Delta p_{0}\Delta p_{1}|\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|r(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\Big\|\frac{1}{b(0)}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}}\|r(0)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{b(0)}\Delta p_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{b(0)}\Delta p_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\|\Delta p(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|r\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{b(t)}\Delta p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Big\|\frac{1}{b}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|r\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\big(\|\sqrt{b(0)}\Delta p_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{b(0)}\Delta p_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big).

By putting together the estimates (
4.46
), (
4.45
) and keeping in mind Assumption
3
, we choose
\varepsilon
in (
4.46
) suitably small so that the corresponding term will be absorbed by the left-hand side of (
4.45
). Thus, it results that

(4.47)

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\|\sqrt{b(t)}\Delta p_{t}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla p_{tt}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}(1+\|b_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|r_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})E[p](s)\,\textup{d}s+\|\Delta p(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

Observe that we have

(4.48)

\displaystyle\|\Delta p(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Delta p_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta p_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Delta p_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sqrt{b}\Delta p_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

which implies according to Assumption
3

(4.49)

\displaystyle\|\Delta p(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle E[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}E[p](s)\,\textup{d}s.

Thus, the estimate (
4.47
) becomes

(4.50)

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\|\sqrt{b(t)}\Delta p_{t}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla p_{tt}(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\big(1+\|b_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|r_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\big)E[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

At this point, we can add up the estimates
\lambda_{0}\times
(
4.39
) and (
4.50
) where
\lambda_{0}>0
is suitably selected in order to absorb the term
\tau^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
from the right-hand side of (
4.39
). This leads to the estimate

(4.51)

\displaystyle E[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}D[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}(1+\|b_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|b_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|r_{t}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})E[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla f(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

The hidden constant in the above estimate depends on
\tau
but not on
T
. Therefore, we arrive at the estimate (
4.30
) by applying Gronwall’s inequality to (
4.51
). This ends the proof of Proposition (
4.2
).
∎

5.
Analysis of the bioheat equation

We focus in this section on establishing
a priori
estimates for the solution of the parabolic Pennes equation:

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &m\Theta_{t}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\Theta+\ell\Theta=g(x,t),\qquad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\Theta|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\\
&\Theta|_{t=0}=\Theta_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

When formulating these estimates, our aim is to assign the minimal regularity to the source term
g
. This is crucial for the nonlinear problem (
2.2
), where
g
serves as a placeholder for the source term
\Phi(\Theta,p_{t})
, given the limited regularity available for the variable
p
. In addition, one can expect that
\Theta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))
would be enough to prevent the degeneracy of the JMGT equation as was shown in the previous result
[
34
]
. However, here
the sort of coupling that we have in (
2.2
) together with the hyperbolic character of the JMGT equation and the fact that the variable coefficients are associated with the higher-order derivatives in the pressure equation suggest that we need solutions to (
5.1
) with more regularity than
\Theta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega))
.
To accomplish this, we require the following assumptions on the initial data and the source term in (
5.1
).

Assumption 4
.

Assume that

•

g\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
.

•

The initial data satisfy the compatibility condition

(5.2)

\Theta_{0}\in H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\qquad\Theta_{1}:=\Theta_{t}(x,0)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

such that
\Theta_{1}
is defined as

(5.3)

\Theta_{1}=\frac{1}{m}\big(\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\Theta_{0}-\ell\Theta_{0}+g(x,0)\big),

with

(5.4)

g(0)=\Phi(\Theta_{0},p_{1})=\phi(\Theta_{0})(p_{1})^{2}

where
\phi
is defined as in (
2.3
).

By relying again on the Faedo-Galerkin approach, we prove the next result. We only emphasize the portion of the proof leading to the energy bound (
5.5
) for the smooth approximations in space of the solutions (in terms of the eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet-Laplacian) and we skip the other steps that are standard in the context of the heat equation. The reader interested in more details is referred to
[
14
, Chapter 7]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Given
T>0
. Let
\Theta_{0},g
satisfy Assumption
4
. Then the problem (
5.1
) admits a unique solution
\Theta\in\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}
(cf. (
2.9
)). Further, there exists a constant
C_{\Theta,2}>0
, independent of
T
, such that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}H^{3}}^{2}+\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}H^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{tt}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\Theta,2}\big(\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla g(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}H^{1}}^{2}\big).

Proof.

We multiply the equation (
5.1
) by
\Theta
and integrate in space, we obtain

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}g\Theta\,\textup{d}x.

We can estimate the right-hand side using Hölder and Young inequalities, to get

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(\varepsilon)\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

The last term on the right of the inequality above can be absorbed into the left-hand side for sufficiently small values of
\varepsilon
. Thus, we obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Next, testing the equation (
5.1
) by
-\Delta\Theta_{t}
and integrating with respect to
x
, we get

(5.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+m\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{\Omega}g\Delta\Theta_{t}\,\textup{d}x.

Using Hölder and Young inequalities, we can estimate the right-hand side to obtain

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+m\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(\varepsilon)\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta\Theta_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Further, we differentiate the equation (
5.1
) with respect to time, we get

(5.11)

m\Theta_{tt}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\Theta_{t}+\ell\Theta_{t}=g_{t}.

We multiply equation (
5.11
) by
-\Delta\Theta_{t}
and integrate in space to find

(5.12)

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}g_{t}\Delta\Theta_{t}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\Delta\Theta_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Summing up (
5.8
), (
5.10
) and (
5.12
), then integrating the resulting inequality with respect to time, we get for suitably small values of
\varepsilon

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}\\
+\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}\\
\lesssim\,\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}L^{2}}^{2}.\end{multlined}

This implies

(5.14)

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}H^{2}}^{2}\\
\lesssim\,\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}L^{2}}^{2}.\end{multlined}

Recalling the compatibility condition (
5.3
), we have

(5.15)

\|\nabla\Theta_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla g(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Note that based on the assumptions on the source term
g
,
\nabla g(x,0)
is well-defined. Hence the estimate (
5.14
) becomes

(5.16)

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}H^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq\,C_{\Theta,1}\big(\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla g(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}L^{2}}^{2}\big)\end{multlined}

where the constant
C_{\Theta,1}
does not depend on
T
.

Now, observe that we have from equation (
5.1
)

(5.17)

\Delta\Theta=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\textup{a}}}\big(m\Theta_{t}+\ell\Theta-g\big).

Hence, for all
t\geq 0
, we obtain

(5.18)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\Theta(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla g(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Taking the estimate (
5.16
) into account and the fact that
-\Delta
is an isomorphism from
H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into
H^{1}(\Omega)
, we deduce that

(5.19)

\displaystyle\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}H^{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla g(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}H^{1}}^{2}.

Further, from the equation (
5.11
), we have

(5.20)

\|\Theta_{tt}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\Delta\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}+\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2},

which together with (
5.16
) implies that

(5.21)

\|\Theta_{tt}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla g(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{H^{1}L^{2}}^{2}.

The estimate (
5.5
) follows by adding up (
5.16
), (
5.19
) and (
5.21
). This concludes the proof of the Proposition
5.1
.
∎

6.
Well-posedness of the nonlinear problem (
1.1
)–Proof of Theorem
2.1

Looking at the groundwork covered in the previous sections for the linearized problems (
4.1
) and (
5.1
), we can transform the problem of showing the existence of solutions to (
2.2
) into a fixed-point problem for a suitably defined mapping, which will be solved by implementing Banach fixed-point theorem. We break down the proof of Theorem
2.1
into three main steps.

Step 1: Defining the fixed-point mapping
. Denote by
\mathbf{X}:=\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}\times\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}
.
We consider the mapping
\mathcal{T}
that associates to each
(p^{\ast},\Theta^{\ast})\in\mathbf{X}
the solution
(p,\Theta)\in\mathbf{X}
of the problem

(6.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tau p_{ttt}+p_{tt}-h(\Theta^{\ast})\Delta p-\zeta(\Theta^{\ast})\Delta p_{t}=2k(\Theta^{\ast})((p^{\ast}_{t})^{2}+p^{\ast}p^{\ast}_{tt}),&\,&\text{in}&\ &\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&m\Theta_{t}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\Theta+\ell\Theta=\mathcal{Q}(p^{\ast}_{t}),&\,&\text{in}&\ &\Omega\times(0,T)\end{aligned}\right.

supplemented with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and the initial data

(6.2)

\displaystyle(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in

\displaystyle\big[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big]\times\big[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big]\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}\in

\displaystyle\big[H^{3}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\big],

i.e.,
\mathcal{T}(p^{\ast},\Theta^{\ast})=(p,\Theta).

Since we intend to apply Banach fixed-point theorem, we are particularly interested in solutions of (
6.1
) corresponding to
(p^{\ast},\Theta^{\ast})\in B\subset\mathbf{X}
where the subset
B
is given as

(6.3)

\displaystyle B=\Big\{

\displaystyle(p^{\ast},\Theta^{\ast})\in\mathbf{X}:(p^{\ast}(0),p^{\ast}_{t}(0),p_{tt}^{\ast}(0),\Theta^{\ast}(0))=(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},\Theta_{0}),

\displaystyle\|p^{\ast}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}^{1}}\leq\eta,\quad\|p^{\ast}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}}\leq R_{1},\quad\|\Theta^{\ast}\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}}\leq R_{2}\Big\}.

The product space
\mathbf{X}
is endowed with the norm

\|(p,\Theta)\|_{\mathbf{X}}^{2}=\|p\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}}^{2}

where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}^{1}},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}}
denote, respectively the norms on the solutions spaces
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{1},\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2},\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}
(cf. (
2.9
), (
4.5
)); that is

(6.4)

\displaystyle\|p\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}^{1}}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\tau\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\|p\|_{\mathcal{X}_{p}^{2}}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\|p\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{2}+\tau\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\Theta\|_{\mathcal{X}_{\Theta}^{2}}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}H^{3}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}H^{2}}^{2}.

We can check that the set
B
is nonempty by considering the solution to the linear problems (
4.1
), (
5.1
) in the particular case where
r=b=1
and
f=g=0
. Then the solution
(p,\Theta)\in\mathbf{X}
will belong to the ball
B
provided that we select
R_{1},R_{2},\eta>0
such that

(6.5)

\displaystyle C_{p,1}\exp(T)\big(E_{0}[p](0)+E_{1}[p](0)\big)\leq C_{p,1}\exp(T)\eta_{0}^{2}\leq\eta^{2},

\displaystyle C_{p,2}\exp(T)E[p](0)\leq R_{1}^{2},\qquad C_{\Theta,2}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\leq R_{2}^{2}

where
C_{p,1},C_{p,2},C_{\Theta,2}
are the constants appearing respectively in the estimates (
4.6
), (
4.30
), and (
5.5
). Based on the findings in Propositions
4.2
and
5.1
, we can show that the mapping
\mathcal{T}
is indeed well-defined. This will be done next along with proving that
\mathcal{T}
is a self-mapping.

Step 2: Showing the self-mapping property
.
Motivated by previous results in the literature
[
4
,
23
,
20
]
, we can show that
\mathcal{T}
is a self-mapping under a smallness condition on the lower norm in
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{1}
. Since the existence of a fixed-point of
\mathcal{T}
means the existence of a solution to the nonlinear problem (
2.2
) that lies in
B
, this smallness constraint also serves to guarantee that the JMGT equation in (
2.2
) does not degenerate. Specifically, we need
1-2k(\Theta)p
to be positive at all times, which translates into choosing
\eta
small enough so as
1-2k_{1}\eta^{1-\frac{d}{4}}R^{\frac{d}{4}}>0
. Indeed, this is enough to avoid degeneracy. More precisely, using (
K1
) and inequality (
3.4
) give

(6.6)

\displaystyle 2\|k(\Theta)p\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle 2\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|p\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2k_{1}\|p\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\|p\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}^{\frac{d}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2k_{1}\eta^{1-\frac{d}{4}}R_{1}^{\frac{d}{4}}.

At this stage, no smallness conditions are imposed on
T
and
R_{1}
.
Our approach is similar to that taken in
[
4
,
34
]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Given
\tau>0
. Let
T>0
be arbitrary. There exist
0<\eta_{0}\ll\eta<1
are sufficiently small, and
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
such that if the initial data satisfy

E_{0}[p](0)+E_{1}[p](0)\leq\eta_{0}^{2}

and

(6.7)

C_{p,1}(\eta_{0}^{2}+C_{f}T(\eta^{4}+\eta^{4-\frac{d}{2}}R_{1}^{\frac{d}{2}}))\exp\big((1+\sqrt{T}+T)C_{1}(R_{2})\big)\leq\eta^{2},

then we have
\mathcal{T}(B)\subset B
. The constants
C_{p,1},C_{1},C_{f}
are independent of
T
.

Proof.

We aim to prove that for
(p^{\ast},\Theta^{\ast})\in B
, the solution to the linear initial boundary value problem (
6.1
) defined as
(p,\Theta)=\mathcal{T}(p^{\ast},\Theta^{\ast})
exists in
B
. To this end, we exploit what we have already established in Propositions
4.1
,
4.2
and
5.1
.
To apply the linear analysis, we need first to show that the
p^{\ast}
and
\Theta^{\ast}
terms in (
6.1
) satisfy all the hypotheses of the linear existence-uniqueness result. For this purpose,
we begin by setting

(6.8)

\displaystyle r(x,t)=h(\Theta^{\ast}),

\displaystyle b(x,t)=\zeta(\Theta^{\ast}),

\displaystyle f(x,t)=2k(\Theta^{\ast})\big((p^{\ast}_{t})^{2}+p^{\ast}p_{tt}^{\ast}\big),

\displaystyle g(x,t)=\Phi(\Theta^{\ast},p^{\ast}_{t})=\phi(\Theta^{\ast})(p_{t}^{\ast})^{2}.

We know from Assumption
1
that there exist
h_{1},\zeta_{1}>0
such that

h_{1}\leq h(\Theta^{\ast})=r(x,t)\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta_{1}\leq\zeta(\Theta^{\ast})=b(x,t).

For convenience, we recall the definitions of the functions
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda

(6.41)

\displaystyle\hat{p}=p_{1}-p_{2},\qquad\hat{\Theta}=\Theta_{1}-\Theta_{2},

\displaystyle\hat{p}^{\ast}=p_{1}^{\ast}-p_{2}^{\ast},\qquad\hat{\Theta}^{\ast}=\Theta_{1}^{\ast}-\Theta_{2}^{\ast}

solve the following system

(6.42)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tau\hat{p}_{ttt}+\hat{p}_{tt}-h(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})\Delta\hat{p}-\zeta(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})\Delta\hat{p}_{t}=f_{1},&\qquad&\\
&m\hat{\Theta}_{t}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\hat{\Theta}+\ell\hat{\Theta}=g_{1},\end{aligned}\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,T),\right.

with homogeneous initial and boundary conditions

(6.43)

\displaystyle\hat{p}(x,0)=\hat{p}_{t}(x,0)=\hat{\Theta}(x,0)=0,

\displaystyle\text{in}\quad\ \Omega,

\displaystyle\hat{p}=\hat{\Theta}=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,T).

The source terms
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
are given, respectively as

(6.44)

\displaystyle f_{1}=

\displaystyle\big(h(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-h(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\big)\Delta p_{2}+\big(\zeta(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-\zeta(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\big)\Delta p_{2t}

\displaystyle+2k(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})\big((p^{\ast}_{1t})^{2}+p_{1}^{\ast}p_{1tt}^{\ast}\big)-2k(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\big((p^{\ast}_{2t})^{2}+p_{2}^{\ast}p_{2tt}^{\ast}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(h(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-h(\Theta_{2}^{\ast}))\Delta p_{2}+\big(\zeta(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-\zeta(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\big)\Delta p_{2t}

\displaystyle+2(k(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-k(\Theta_{2}^{\ast}))((p^{\ast}_{1t})^{2}+p_{1}^{\ast}p_{1tt}^{\ast})+2k(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\big((p^{\ast}_{1t})^{2}-(p^{\ast}_{2t})^{2}+p_{1}^{\ast}p_{1tt}^{\ast}-p_{2}^{\ast}p_{2tt}^{\ast}\big)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle f_{11}+f_{12}+f_{13},

with

(6.45)

\left\{\begin{aligned} f_{11}=&\,(h(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-h(\Theta_{2}^{\ast}))\Delta p_{2}+\big(\zeta(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-\zeta(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\big)\Delta p_{2t},\\
f_{12}=&\,2(k(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-k(\Theta_{2}^{\ast}))((p^{\ast}_{1t})^{2}+p_{1}^{\ast}p_{1tt}^{\ast}),\\
f_{13}=&\,2k(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\big((p^{\ast}_{1t})^{2}-(p^{\ast}_{2t})^{2}+p_{1}^{\ast}p_{1tt}^{\ast}-p_{2}^{\ast}p_{2tt}^{\ast}\big),\end{aligned}\right.

and

(6.46)

Notice that the presence of the higher-order derivatives in the component

prevents from getting the contractivity of the mapping

with respect to the metric on

. Precisely,

belonging to

does not give

is a contraction with respect to the weaker topology on

.

As a first step, we work out appropriate estimates for the source terms
f_{1}
and
g_{1}
. We begin with the three components of

. We have

(6.47)

\displaystyle\|f_{11}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}=

\displaystyle\|(h(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-h(\Theta_{2}^{\ast}))\Delta p_{2}+\big(\zeta(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-\zeta(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\big)\Delta p_{2t}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|h(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-h(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p_{2}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\zeta(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-\zeta(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p_{2t}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}.

We obtain an upper bound of the right-hand side by appealing to the properties of the functions

and

. Observe that

(6.48)

\begin{aligned} \|h(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-h(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}&=\Big\|(\Theta_{1}^{\ast}-\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\int_{0}^{1}h^{\prime}(\Theta_{2}^{\ast}+\sigma(\Theta_{1}^{\ast}-\Theta_{2}^{\ast}))\textup{d}\sigma\Big\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\|\Theta_{1}^{\ast}-\Theta_{2}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\Big(1+\|\Theta_{2}^{\ast}+\sigma(\Theta_{1}^{\ast}-\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)\\
&\lesssim\|(\hat{p}^{\ast},\hat{\Theta}^{\ast})\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\Big(1+\|\Theta_{1}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}}+\|\Theta_{2}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big).\end{aligned}.

Similarly, we can show that

(6.50)

\displaystyle\|\zeta(\Theta_{1}^{\ast})-\zeta(\Theta_{2}^{\ast})\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|(\hat{p}^{\ast},\hat{\Theta}^{\ast})\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\Big(1+\|\Theta_{1}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{3}}+\|\Theta_{2}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{3}}\Big).

Combining (
6.48
), (
6.50
) with (

) yields

\displaystyle\|f_{11}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{T}\|\Delta p_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\Big(1+\|\Theta_{1}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}}+\|\Theta_{2}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)\|(\hat{p}^{\ast},\hat{\Theta}^{\ast})\|_{\mathbf{Y}}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{T}\|\Delta p_{2t}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\Big(1+\|\Theta_{1}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{3}}+\|\Theta_{2}^{\ast}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{3}}\Big)\|(\hat{p}^{\ast},\hat{\Theta}^{\ast})\|_{\mathbf{Y}}.

Then recalling the fact that

maps

\displaystyle\|f_{11}\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}\lesssim

The same ideas in (

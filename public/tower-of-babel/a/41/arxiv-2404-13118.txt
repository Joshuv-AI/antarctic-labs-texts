Galactic rotation curves in gravity with a nondynamical scalar
Sandipan Sengupta

Abstract
We introduce a four-dimensional gravity action and explore the idea that the Einsteinian and galactic laws of gravitation should emerge as two different dynamical phases of the resulting theory. A new set of spacetime solutions exhibiting asymptotically flat galactic rotation curves are found. These geometries correspond to anisotropic effective pressure. We also provide an explanation of the empirical correlation between the luminosity and the slope of the velocity curve. We find an enhancement in the deflection angle of a light ray penetrating the halo, which could provide a realistic testing ground for the model as a purely geometric alternative to ‘dark matter’. For very large halo radii, the leading nonbaryonic contribution to the bending angle is 3 ​ π ​ v 2 2 ​ c 2 \frac{3\pi v^{2}}{2c^{2}} ( v v being the asymptotic rotational velocity), a constant that is lesser than the deflection predicted for an isothermal CDM halo.



I
Introduction

Perhaps, a dynamical explanation of asymptotically flat rotation curves in spiral galaxies solely through spacetime geometry is far from being out of question. While Einstein’s gravity theory has enjoyed astonishing success within sub-galactic scales, its apparent failure at larger distances stems from the fact that the predicted Newtonian fall-off for the circular velocity
v(r)
of a test particle at a radius
r
far away from the luminous mass distribution happens to be contrary to what is in fact observed
Rubin and Ford 1970
;
*rubin1
;
Roberts and Whitehurst 1975
.

While one could either continue exploring new forms of (‘dark’) matter in the energy-momentum tensor of Einstein gravity or invoke arbitrary (geometric) modications of gravity Lagrangian itself, we shall adopt a rather conservative outlook here. We shall be concerned with the general question as to whether it is possible to construct a purely geometric theory of gravity, where it must:

a) obey 4D general covariance, b) follow from a gravity action linear in curvature tensor so that the field equations contain no higher than second order derivatives, c) involve no matter-coupling,
d) admit galactic spacetimes with asymptotically flat rotation curves as explicit solutions and
e) can reproduce Einstein gravity in vacuum or upto some restriction on the energy-momentum tensor in a suitable limit.

The quest for a modified theory of gravity towards an explanation of the asymptotic behaviour of rotation curves has a long history though. For instance, MOND
Milgrom 1983a
;
*milgrom1
;
Bekenstein and Milgrom 1984
and conformal gravity
Mannheim and Kazanas 1989
;
*mannheim1
represent two of the most extensively studied approaches in four dimensions.
However, finding a generally covariant action principle with metric as the only gravitational degree of freedom is not possible in the first case
Sanders 1997
;
Milgrom 2019
. In the latter, the issues of defining the test mass through a conformally invariant geodesic equation and of obtaining frame-independent results turn out to be quite subtle
Horne 2016
;
Hobson and Lasenby 2021
. Approaches such as Braneworld gravity
Mak and Harko 2004
;
Pal et al. 2005
,
f(R)
extensions
Sobouti 2007
;
*mendoza
and other modifications of gravity of specific nature
Moffat 2006
;
Myrzakulov et al. 2016
have also been explored in this context. In any case, none of these proposals do follow directly from the principles laid out in the previous paragraph.

Here, in this context, we introduce and explore a four dimensional action for gravity. Apart from the metric and spin-connection, the Lagrangian density depends on a non-dynamical scalar. The resulting equations of motion, depending on whether this field vanishes or not, defines two different phases corresponding to galactic and Einsteinian gravity, respectively. The first phase is shown to correspond to galactic gravity, which is the focus of this article. The other phase contains Einstein gravity in vacuum or with matter, details of which would be explored elsewhere.

For the galactic phase, the most general static spherically symmetric solutions are obtained under the assumption of a vanishing torsion. Remarkably, we find that the class where the metric exhibits a small deviation from the Newtonian form implies asymptotically flat rotation curves. Interpreted in terms of an effective energy-momentum tensor in the Einstein equation, these solutions are associated with anisotropic effective pressure. The circular velocity and dynamical mass formula are also derived. The other (Newtonian) class reproduces a generalized version of Einstein-Maxwell-(Anti)deSitter geometry with a purely geometric ‘charge’.

Next, we study the deflection of light passing through the galactic spacetime solution so obtained. Both cases, involving a finite and infinite halo radius respectively, are presented along with testable results which may be contrasted with the bending predicted by some of the existing models of ‘dark matter’ or its geometric alternatives. This latter analysis provides an example where the proposed theory could be of direct phenomenological relevance. We conclude with a summary and a few relevant remarks.

II
The Lagrangian formulation

Let us define the following action in four dimensions:

\displaystyle S[\xi,e,\omega,\psi]=\int d^{4}x~\xi e\big[e^{\mu}_{I}e^{\nu}_{J}{R}_{\mu\nu}^{~~IJ}(\omega)+\chi\big]+\int d^{4}x~{e\cal L}_{m}[\psi],

(1)

where
\xi
is a scalar,
e_{\mu}^{I}
are the tetrad,
\omega_{\mu}^{~IJ}
are the spin-connection defining the field-strength as:
R_{\beta\rho}^{~~LM}(\hat{w})=\partial_{[\beta}\omega_{\rho]}^{~LM}+\omega_{[\beta}^{~LK}\omega_{\rho]K}^{~~~M}
and
\chi
is a constant. The last term at the RHS denotes the matter action as a functional of the matter fields
\psi

Variation of (
1

\xi,e_{\mu}^{I},\omega_{\mu}^{~IJ}

\displaystyle R(\omega)+\chi=0,

\displaystyle\xi\big[R_{\alpha\beta}^{~~IJ}(\omega)e^{\beta}_{J}-\frac{1}{2}e_{\alpha}^{I}R(\omega)-\frac{1}{2}\chi e_{\alpha}^{I}\big]=e^{\beta I}T_{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}(\omega)\big[\xi ee^{[\alpha}_{I}e^{\beta]}_{J}\big]=0,

(2)

where
R(\omega)=e^{\alpha}_{I}e^{\beta}_{J}R_{\alpha\beta}^{~~IJ}
is the scalar curvature (with torsion in general),

is the matter stress-energy tensor and
D_{\mu}(\omega)
is the gauge-covariant derivative with respect to the connection. The above, along with the usual matter field equations, describes the full theory.

From the last equation of motion, we note that the the scalar
\xi
and its first derivatives define the torsion.
\xi
is non-dynamical since its second derivatives do not appear in the field equations. However, it plays an important role in the sense that solutions for
\xi=0
and
\xi\neq 0
are very different. For
\xi=0
, the energy momentum tensor disappears from the field equations, implying that gravity does not couple to ordinary matter in this phase, whereas it does for
\xi\neq 0
. We can thus anticipate intriguing implications, to be explored next.

For our purpose here, it would be sufficient to analyze the vacuum field equations. Setting
T_{\alpha\beta}=0
, eqs.(
II

\displaystyle\xi\chi=0,

(3)

which represents two possible classes of solutions.
For
\chi=0,~\xi\neq 0
, we obtain:

\displaystyle R_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}R(\omega)=0

(4)

Assuming the vanishing of torsion, we have:
\xi=const.
This case corresponds to Einstein’s gravity in vacuum, and requires no further elaboration.
The other case corresponds to
\chi\neq 0,~\xi=0
, which makes the last two field equations in (
II
) trivial. The corresponding general solution then becomes:

\displaystyle R_{\alpha\rho}^{~~IJ}(\omega)e_{\beta I}e^{\rho}_{J}\equiv R_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=t_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{4}\chi g_{\alpha\beta},

(5)

where
t_{\alpha\beta}
is an arbitrary symmetric traceless tensor.
The tensor
t_{\alpha\beta}
is constrained by the Bianchi identities as:
\nabla_{\alpha}t^{\alpha\beta}=0
. We demonstrate that it is this latter class which encodes galactic spacetimes as dynamical solutions.

Let us consider spherically symmetric static four-geometries of the standard form (
G=1=c

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+g(r)dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

\xi=0

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}+\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}\right)+\frac{2}{r}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}-\frac{g^{\prime}}{g}\right)-\frac{2}{r^{2}}(g-1)-\chi g=0.

(6)

In the following, we shall consider two classes of vacuum solutions of this full nonlinear equation above.

A) Newtonian solutions:
f(r)g(r)=1

In this case, the solution to eq.(
6

\displaystyle f(r)=1+\frac{B_{1}}{r}+\frac{B_{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\chi}{12}r^{2},

(7)

where
B_{1,2}
are arbitrary integration constants.
This is a generalized version of the Einstein-Maxwell-(Anti)deSitter solution of Einstein’s theory, in the sense that the geometric charge

where
|\delta|<<1

To be specific, we parametrize the behaviour above as:

\displaystyle f(r)g(r)=\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\alpha},

(8)

where

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\alpha}\left[\frac{1}{1+\alpha+\alpha^{2}}+\frac{\chi r^{2}}{2(6+4\alpha+\alpha^{2})}\right]+C_{1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{+}}+C_{2}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{-}};

\displaystyle g^{-1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\alpha+\alpha^{2}}+\frac{\chi r^{2}}{2(6+4\alpha+\alpha^{2})}+C_{1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{+}-2\alpha}+C_{2}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{-}-2\alpha},

III
Galactic rotation curves

Asymptotic flatness

To understand the implications of the vacuum solutions (
II
), which have no analogue in Einstein gravity with (at least) standard matter couplings, let us explore if these could be used to model the galactic halo. We assume its geometry to be spherically symmetric. In particular, the intriguing question is, if these exact solutions could reproduce the non-Newtonian behaviour of rotation curves for spiral galaxies.

The circular velocity
v^{2}(r)=\frac{rf^{\prime}}{2f}

Nucamendi et al. 2001
of a massive test particle moving in this geometry (at the equatorial plane
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
), under the practical assumption that the effect of the cosmological constant is negligible within the halo (
\chi\approx 0
), is found to be:

\displaystyle v^{2}(r)=\frac{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha+\alpha^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\gamma_{+}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{+}-2\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}C_{2}\gamma_{-}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{-}-2\alpha}}{\frac{1}{1+\alpha+\alpha^{2}}+C_{1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{+}-2\alpha}+C_{2}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{-}-2\alpha}}

Noting that
\gamma_{+}=-1+3\alpha+o(\alpha^{2}),~\gamma_{-}=-2-2\alpha+o(\alpha^{2})
, the expression above implies:
v^{2}(r)\rightarrow\alpha
as
r\rightarrow\infty
. This corresponds precisely to an asymptotically flat rotation curve, where the small parameter
\alpha
in the halo metric should be interpreted as the limiting rotation velocity of the test particle at the flat region.

Slope

The integration constants
C_{1,2}
could be provided a physical interpretation, in a manner similar to the ones arising in Einsteinian gravity. Noting that the
\alpha=0
limit of the metric (
II
) corresponds to the Reissner-Nordstr
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
m metric,
-C_{1}R>0
and
C_{2}R^{2}>0
are to be identified as the ‘mass’ and (squared) ‘charge’ of the source, respectively. The case
C_{2}<0
however admits no Einsteinian interpretation.

Let us now consider the the slope of the rotation curve to the leading order:

\displaystyle\frac{dv^{2}(r)}{dr}\approx\frac{C_{1}R}{2r^{2}}+\frac{C_{2}R^{2}}{r^{3}}

(10)

The first term is negative, and the second term is positive for a real ‘charge’
C_{2}>0
. Thus, this indicates that the the rotation curve would have a slight decline beyond the luminous mass distribution (vacuum)
for high luminosity galaxies, where the first term dominates the second. For sufficiently low brightness, on the other hand, the slope could be positive. This gross feature is indeed what is observed empirically.

The case of an imaginary or ‘tidal’ charge (
C_{2}<0
) clearly precludes any rise in the rotation curve beyond the luminous mass. This is inconsistent with observations. Hence, we conclude that ‘tidally charged’ spacetime geometries, first found in the context of braneworld models
Dadhich et al. 2000
, cannot exist in nature.

It is worthwhile studying what the solution (
II

\bar{R}_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\bar{R}=T^{(eff)}_{ab}

\displaystyle 8\pi\rho^{(eff)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\alpha(1+\alpha)}{1+\alpha+\alpha^{2}}-(1+\gamma_{+}-2\alpha)C_{1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{+}-2\alpha}-(1+\gamma_{-}-2\alpha)C_{2}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{-}-2\alpha}\Bigg],

\displaystyle 8\pi P^{(eff)}_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\alpha(1-\alpha)}{1+\alpha+\alpha^{2}}+(1+\gamma_{+})C_{1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{+}-2\alpha}+(1+\gamma_{-})C_{2}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{-}-2\alpha}\Bigg],

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1+\alpha+\alpha^{2}}-(1+\gamma_{+}-\alpha)C_{1}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{+}-2\alpha}-(1+\gamma_{-}-\alpha)C_{2}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\gamma_{-}-2\alpha}\Bigg]

\displaystyle 8\pi P^{(eff)}_{\phi}

If we define a spatially averaged pressure as

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(1+\alpha)}{2(1+\alpha+\alpha^{2})}r-\frac{C_{1}R}{2}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{1+\gamma_{+}-2\alpha}-\frac{C_{2}R}{2}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{1+\gamma_{-}-2\alpha}~+~m_{0},

V
Conclusions

We have shown that galactic spacetimes with asymptotically flat rotation curves emerge as exact dynamical solutions of a purely geometric theory, which reflects a number of attractive features compared to some other gravitational alternatives to ‘dark matter’. The action principle involves a scalar, which is non-propagating.
This makes the resulting theory inequivalent to Brans-Dicke gravity
Brans and Dicke 1961
or other scalar-tensor variants. Upto this absence of dynamics, the role of the scalar here is quite similar to the order parameter in superconductivity theories, where its zero and non-zero value respectively define a normal and field-free superconducting state. This indicates the possibility that the Einsteinian and galactic laws of gravitation are simply two phases of the same theory.

It could be of interest to note that the gravitational equations of motion elucidated here could also be generated, under certain special assumptions, from a five-dimensional theory of gravity where the extra dimension has vanishing proper length
Sengupta 2020
;
Sengupta 2022
. However, it is also important to observe that the action principle and the resulting dynamics explored here are in general different from the 4D emergent theory following from such an extra dimensional formulation.

Among the static spherically symmetric solutions, the ones corresponding to asymptotically flat rotation curves are associated with anisotropic effective pressure. Though in a different context, the possibility of ‘dark matter’ models with nontrivial stresses have been considered in the literature
Bharadwaj and Kar 2003
. We note that the spatially averaged equation of state is
\frac{\bar{P}}{\rho}=\frac{1}{3}
.

We also observe that the associated effective density within the halo, apart from the
r^{-2}
behaviour required for the flatness of rotation curves at sufficiently large distance, exhibits
r^{-3}
and
r^{-4}
contributions (upto relativistic corrections) which should dominate at scales significantly smaller than the halo radii. These encode the slope and hence the substructure of the rotation curves. Notably, these resemble the large distance behaviour of two well-known density profiles motivated from CDM simulation results, e.g. NFW
(r^{-3})

Navarro et al. 1996
and Hernquist (
r^{-4}
)
Hernquist 1990
, respectively. A deeper study in this regard could be pursued elsewhere.

Finally, we obtain the deflection angle of a light ray as it passes through the halo metric joined to an asymptotically flat spacetime. The correction with respect to the standard Schwarzschild result is obtained as a function of
\alpha=\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}
and
\frac{r_{0}}{R_{H}}
, the ratio of the distance of closest approach to the halo radius. Since
\alpha
could be determined empirically from the rotation curve, this provides the hope that lensing observations for a given
\frac{r_{0}}{R_{H}}
could be used to test the model set up here.
In the case of very large halo radii, we obtain the leading correction to the Einstein bending to be
\frac{3}{2}\pi\alpha
. This provides an intriguing contrast with the predicted value for the standard CDM halo modelled as an isothermal sphere.

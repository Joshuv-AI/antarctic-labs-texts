Renormalized Area for Minimal Hypersurfaces of 5D Poincaré-Einstein Spaces
Aaron J. Tyrrell

Abstract
. In this paper we derive a Gauss-Bonnet formula for the renormalized area of Graham-Witten minimal hypersurfaces of 5-dimensional Poincaré-Einstein spaces. The formula we derive expresses the renormalized area in terms of integrals of pointwise scalar Riemannian invariants. We also prove a result which gives a characterization of minimal hypersurfaces with L 2 L^{2} second fundamental form in terms of conformal geometry at infinity.



1.
Introduction

The renormalized volume of Poincaré-Einstein manifolds,
(X^{n+1},g_{+}),
and the renormalized area of their minimal submanifolds has been a focus of much study over the past 20 years in both geometry and physics. Renormalized volume arises via a construction carried out by Henningson and Skenderis
[
16
]
in the physics literature and was developed by Graham
[
14
]
in the math literature. It is defined by taking the order-zero term in the expansion in
\epsilon
of the quantity
\text{Vol}_{g_{+}}({r>\epsilon}),
where
r
is a special defining function for
\partial X
(section 2 will cover all preliminaries and definitions in detail). An important formula is Anderson’s
[
4
]
Gauss-Bonnet formula for the renormalized volume,
V(g_{+}),
of a 4D Poincaré-Einstein space which states that

(1.1)

8\pi^{2}\chi(X^{4})=6V(g_{+})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{X}|W_{g_{+}}|^{2}_{g_{+}}dv_{g_{+}}.

Here
W_{g_{+}}
is the Weyl tensor of
g_{+}.
Since
|W_{g_{+}}|^{2}_{g_{+}}
is a pointwise conformal invariant of weight -4, the integral is guaranteed to converge despite the infinite volume of
(X,g_{+})
. In his paper, Anderson notes that given a Poincaré-Einstein manifold
(X^{4},g_{+})
(
1.1
) implies the following rigidity result:

(1.2)

V(g_{+})\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}}{3}\chi(X^{4}),

with equality if and only if
g_{+}
is hyperbolic.

Renormalized area was introduced by Graham and Witten in
[
12
]
where they outlined a theory of area renormalization for a certain class of minimal submanifolds of Poincaré-Einstein spaces; we call these ”Graham-Witten” minimal submanifolds. The focus of this paper is Graham-Witten minimal hypersurfaces of a Poincaré-Einstein space
(X^{5},g_{+}).
These are complete minimal hypersurfaces,
(Y^{4},h_{+}),
dividing
X
into two pieces
X^{-}
and
X^{+}
such that
Y\cap\partial X=\Sigma^{3}\neq\emptyset.
Renormalized area has been studied in both geometry and physics. It has been applied to the asymptotic Plateau problem, which is the problem of finding a minimal submanifold of hyperbolic space bounding a pre scribed asymptotic boundary at infinity. This situation was treated conclusively by Anderson in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
. This problem can be posed on more general spaces including Poincaré-Einstein spaces. Renormalized area proves to be a useful tool here for generalizing many techniques from the compact case to these minimal submanifolds extending to infinity. There is a rich existence theory developed by Oliveira and Moret
[
10
]
, see also Coskunuzer
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
. A survey paper on this problem was also written by Coskunuzer
[
9
]
. Some regularity results for minimal hypersurfaces in this setting are given by
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
23
]
(an error in
[
18
]
is corrected in
[
23
]
). The renormalized area functional is also relevant in the Ads/CFT correspondence. Maldacena proposed in
[
19
]
that the expectation value of the Wilson loop operator corresponding to a closed loop
\gamma
in
\partial X,
within the context of the large t’Hooft coupling regime should be given by the renormalized area of a minimal surface bounding
\gamma.
This is also studied in
[
20
]
. Both of these motivated
[
12
]
which in turn was part of the motivation for the author of this paper. A physical context in which higher dimensional renormalized area, and in particular the case we consider for this paper, proves to be important is in calculating entanglement entropies for spatial regions within holographic QFTs. This was first observed by Ryu-Takayanagi
[
21
]
,
[
22
]
. They showed that given a holographic CFT on the boundary geometry at infinity of an AdS, the entanglement entropy of a spatial region within the CFT is equal to the renormalized area of a minimal hypersurface of the bulk AdS up to a constant factor. This was generalized further by Hubeny-Rangamani-Takayanagi
[
5
]
. Graham and Witten showed that renormalized area is well-defined for even-dimensional submanifolds of Poincaré-Einstein spaces. Alexakis and Mazzeo
[
1
]
studied this functional on properly embedded, minimal surfaces of Poincaré-Einstein spaces. The work presented in this paper was partly motivated by work from
[
1
]
. In particular the explicit formula they obtained for the renormalized area,
A(Y),
of a minimal 2 dimensional hypersurface Y within a 3 dimensional hyperbolic space:

(1.3)

A(Y)=-2\pi\chi(Y)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{Y}|\mathring{II}|^{2}dA,

where
\mathring{II}
is the trace-free second fundamental form of
Y.

An important property of renormalized area
\backslash
volume is that, when the (sub)manifold in question is even dimensional it is an invariant of that (sub)manifold. Otherwise it will depend on the choice of special defining function used in the construction. Therefore the natural case to consider in order to generalize (
1.3
) is the following:

Theorem 1.1

(
Gauss-Bonnet Formula for Renormalized Area
)
.

Let
(X^{5},g_{+})
be a Poincaré-Einstein space. Let
(Y^{4},h_{+})
be a Graham-Witten minimal hypersurface and set
A(Y)
as the renormalized area of
Y.
Then

(1.4)

\displaystyle 6A(Y)=8\pi^{2}\chi(Y)

\displaystyle-\int_{Y}\frac{|W|^{2}_{h_{+}}}{4}dA_{h_{+}}+\int_{Y}\frac{|E|^{2}_{h_{+}}}{2}dA_{h_{+}}

\displaystyle-\int_{Y}\frac{|\mathring{B}|_{h_{+}}^{4}}{24}dA_{h_{+}}-\int_{Y}\frac{\Delta^{Y}|\mathring{B}|_{h_{+}}^{2}}{2}+|\mathring{B}|_{h_{+}}^{2}dA_{h_{+}},

where
W
is the Weyl tensor on
Y,

E
is the trace-free Ricci tensor on
Y,
and
\mathring{B}
is the trace-free second fundamental form of
Y.
Note that we are also claiming that every term on the right-hand side above is finite.

The following theorem is interesting because it gives a characterization of minimal hypersurfaces with an
L^{2}
second fundamental form in terms of conformal geometry at infinity.

Theorem 1.2

(
Holographic Characterization of
L^{2}
Second Fundamental Forms
)
.

Let the setup be the same as in
Theorem

1.1
. Then either

(1)

\partial Y
is umbilic in
\partial X,
which is equivalent to

\int_{Y}\Delta^{Y}|\mathring{B}|^{2}dA_{h_{+}}=0,

which is equivalent to
|\mathring{B}|\in L^{2}(Y).

Or

(2)

\partial Y
is not umbilic in
\partial X
which is equivalent to the integral above diverging to
-\infty.

Remark 1.3
.

Renormalized area can be defined for a more general class of hypersurfaces than what we refer to as Graham-Witten hypersurfaces above. These results will also hold for those hypersurfaces. These can be defined as complete, non-compact, minimal hypersurfaces of
(X,g_{+})
which are asymptotic to a hypersurface contained in the boundary of
X,
such that under compactification they can be represented locally near their asymptotic boundary as graphs of functions with sufficiently regular polyhomogeneous expansions.

2.
Preliminaries

A complete Riemannian metric
g_{+}
on the interior
\mathring{X}^{n+1}
of a compact manifold with boundary
X
is said to be conformally compact if
\overline{g}:=r^{2}g_{+}
extends as a metric to
X
, where
r
is a defining function for
M=\partial X.
By a defining function for
M
we mean

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle>0\hskip 5.69054pt\text{on $\mathring{X},$}

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=0\hskip 5.69054pt\text{on $M,$}

\displaystyle dr

\displaystyle\neq 0\hskip 5.69054pt\text{on $M.$}

We call
[g_{+}]_{\infty}:=[\overline{g}|_{M}]
the conformal infinity of
(X,g_{+}),
where
[\overline{g}|_{M}]
is the conformal class of
\overline{g}|_{M}.
This is clearly a well-defined invariant of
g_{+}
as
r^{2}g_{+}|_{M}
is conformal to
s^{2}g_{+}|_{M}
for any two defining functions
r
and
s.
A less obvious invariant of
g_{+}
is
|dr|_{r^{2}g_{+}}^{2}\Big|_{M}
which is independent of defining function
r.
Conformally transforming the Riemann tensor of
g_{+}=:g
yields

(2.1)

R_{ijkl}^{g_{+}}=-|dr|^{2}_{\overline{g}}(g_{ik}g_{jl}-g_{il}g_{jk})+O_{ijkl}(r^{-3}),

where
O_{ijkl}(r^{-3})
is the component function of a covariant 4-tensor and is
O(r^{-3})
as
r\to 0.
Notice that this implies that the tensor corresponding to those components vanishes with respect to the
g_{+}-\text{norm}
as
r\to 0
since
(g_{+})^{ij}=r^{2}\overline{g}^{ij}.

This implies that
Sec_{p}\to-|dr|^{2}_{r^{2}g_{+}}
as
p\to M.

Asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds are complete manifolds that have asymptotic sectional curvatures equal to
-1.
A special defining function for
M
is a defining function
r
such that
|dr|_{\overline{g}}^{2}\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
M
; rather than just on
M
itself. It is a lemma of Graham’s
[
14
]
that for every metric
g_{\infty}
in the conformal infinity of an asymptotically hyperbolic metric
g_{+}
there is a special defining function,
r
, such that
r^{2}g_{+}|_{M}=g_{\infty}.
A conformally compact manifold that also satisfies the Einstein condition,
\text{Ric}(g_{+})=-ng_{+}
is called Poincaré-Einstein. For such a manifold, it can be checked by contracting (
2.1
) that
|dr|_{\overline{g}}^{2}\Big|_{M}=1.
It follows that
Poincaré-Einstein manifolds are special cases of asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds.

A defining function determines for some
r_{0}
an identification of
M\times[0,r_{0})
with a neighborhood of
M
in
X:

(p,t)\in M\times[0,r_{0})
corresponding to
\phi(p,t),
where
\phi
is the flow of
\nabla_{\overline{g}}r.
If
r
is a special defining function then
r(\phi(p,t))=t.
So we can think about the
t
coordinate as just being
r
and
\nabla_{\overline{g}}r
is orthogonal to the slices
M\times\{t\}.
The metric
\overline{g}
then takes the form

\overline{g}=g_{r}+dr^{2}

where
g_{r}
is a 1-parameter family of metrics on
M
(note that in our notation this implies
g_{0}=g_{\infty}
). Using the Einstein condition, one can compute what is known as the Fefferman-Graham expansion for
g_{r}.
When
n
is odd, this takes the form:

g_{r}=g_{0}+g^{(2)}r^{2}+\text{(even powers)}+g^{(n-1)}r^{n-1}+g^{(n)}r^{n}+O(r^{n+1}),

where
g^{(i)}
are tensors on
M
that are formally determined for
1\leq j\leq n-1,
and
g^{(n)}
is trace-free with respect to any metric in
[\overline{g}|_{M}]
. In
M\times[0,r_{0})_{r}
we can write the volume form of
g_{+}
as

d\nu_{g_{+}}=r^{-n-1}\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{det}g_{r}}{\mathrm{det}g_{0}}\bigg)^{1/\penalty 2}d\nu_{g_{0}}dr.

Then using Jacobi’s formula

\frac{d}{dt}(detA(t))=(detA(t))tr(A^{-1}(t)A^{\prime}(t))

coupled with the locally determined terms of the Fefferman-Graham expansion allows us to compute

(2.2)

\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{det}g_{r}}{\mathrm{det}g_{0}}\bigg)^{1/\penalty 2}=1+\nu^{(2)}r^{2}+\text{(even terms)}+\nu^{(n)}r^{n}+O(r^{n+1})

where
\nu^{(j)}
are determined functions on
M
with
\nu^{(n)}=0
if
n
is odd, which we now assume. Next we consider the asymptotics of
\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{+}}(r>\epsilon).
Let
C=\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{+}}(r>r_{0}),
then

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{+}}(r>\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{r>\epsilon}d\nu_{g_{+}}

\displaystyle=C+\int_{\epsilon}^{r_{0}}\int_{M}[1+\nu^{(2)}r^{2}+\text{(even terms)}+\nu^{(n)}r^{n}+O(r^{n+1})]r^{-n-1}d\nu_{g_{0}}dr

\displaystyle=c_{0}\epsilon^{-n}+c_{2}\epsilon^{-2+n}+\text{(odd powers)}+c_{n-1}\epsilon^{-1}+V+O(\epsilon).

An essential property of the renormalized volume
V
is
that it does not depend on this choice of special defining function. This is generally not true if
X
is odd-dimensional; see
[
14
]
for the proof. The argument in
[
14
]
shows that for
n
odd the renormalized volume of
g_{+}
is well-defined but since renormalized volume is a non-local invariant, finding an explicit expression is non-trivial.

Graham and Witten
[
12
]
outlined an analogous theory to that of renormalized volume but for a class of minimal submanifolds of Poincaré-Einstein manifolds. Here we sketch how that outline looks in the particular setting that we will be in for this paper. Let
(X^{5},g_{+})
be a Poincaré-Einstein space and let
(Y^{4},h_{+})
be a complete minimal hypersurface dividing
X
into two pieces
X^{-}
and
X^{+}
such that

be the boundary of

with

being the induced metric. Given a choice of special defining function

for
\partial X
let
\{x^{\mu}\}
be coordinates on a neighborhood
U_{0}
in
M
that intersects
\Sigma
non-trivially, in particular choose coordinates such that
\Sigma\cap U_{0}=\{x^{4}=0\}
and
|\partial_{4}|_{g_{\infty}}\equiv 1.
This can be done by writing

where

and defining

(2.4)

x^{4}(q)=\begin{cases}\hskip 5.69054ptd_{g_{0}}(q,\Sigma)&\text{if $q\in U_{0}\cap M^{+}$ }\\
-d_{g_{0}}(q,\Sigma)&\text{if $q\in U_{0}\cap M^{-}$ }.\end{cases}

Extend these coordinates to a local system on
X
via the flow of
\nabla_{\overline{g}}r.
Call this extended neighborhood

Because

is a special defining function for

we know that this coordinate extension is

We let
0\leq i,j\leq 4,

1\leq\mu,\lambda\leq 4
and

We let

correspond to the index
0
and

Now we let

This induces a natural local parametrization for

(2.5)

\phi(x^{a},r):=(x^{a},z(x^{a},r),r).

Note that our tuples are ordered such that the

coordinate comes last.
\phi
induces coordinates
\{\hat{x}^{\alpha}\}=\{\hat{x}^{a},\hat{r}\}=\{x^{a}|_{Y},r|_{Y}\}

The induced coordinate basis for

\phi_{*}(\partial_{\alpha})=\partial_{\hat{\alpha}}.
We will omit the hat from coordinate functions when the context is clear but not from the basis vectors. From now on, when we write a Greek index on an induced metric, we will mean this basis i.e.

(2.6)

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle:=g(\phi_{*}(\partial_{\alpha}),\phi_{*}(\partial_{\beta})).

Furthermore, whenever an index attached to an object on

It is straightforward to compute

\phi_{*}(\partial_{\alpha})=\partial_{\alpha}+z_{\alpha}\partial_{4},

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\overline{g}_{ab}+\overline{g}_{4a}z_{b}+\overline{g}_{4b}z_{a}+\overline{g}_{44}z_{a}z_{b},

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{ar}

\displaystyle=\overline{g}_{4a}z_{r}+\overline{g}_{44}z_{a}z_{r},

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{rr}

\displaystyle=1+\overline{g}_{44}z_{r}z_{r}.

Now consider the minimal surface equation on

for

then we get

\delta\int_{V^{r_{0}}_{\epsilon}}\sqrt{\mathrm{det}\hskip 1.42262pth}dxdr=\frac{1}{2}\int_{V_{\epsilon}^{r_{0}}}\sqrt{\mathrm{det}\hskip 1.42262pth}h^{\alpha\beta}\delta h_{\alpha\beta}dxdr=0.

The formula for the variation of the induced metric in our collar neighborhood is given by

\displaystyle\delta h_{\alpha\beta}=

\displaystyle\big[g_{\alpha\beta,4}+g_{4\alpha,4}z_{,\beta}+g_{4\beta,4}z_{,\alpha}+g_{44,4}z_{,\alpha}z_{,\beta}\big]\delta z

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt+g_{4\alpha}\delta z_{,\beta}+g_{4\beta}\delta z_{,\alpha}+g_{44}z_{,\alpha}\delta z_{,\beta}+g_{44}z_{,\beta}\delta z_{,\alpha}.

is a critical point of the area functional then

\bigg[\partial_{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}(\log(\mathrm{det}\hskip 1.42262pth))_{,\beta}\bigg]\bigg[h^{\alpha\beta}\bigg(g_{\alpha 4}+g_{44}z_{,\alpha}\bigg)\bigg]-\frac{1}{2}h^{\alpha\beta}\bigg[g_{\alpha\beta,4}+2g_{\alpha 4,4}z_{,\beta}+g_{44,4}z_{,\alpha}z_{,\beta}\bigg]=0.

3.
Proof of Main Results

Let
g_{\infty}
be a representative for the conformal infinity of
g_{+}
such that
\partial Y
is minimal in
\partial X
with respect to
g_{\infty}
and let
r
be a special defining function for
\partial X
such that

satisfies
\overline{g}|_{TM}=g_{\infty}.
This

let

be a function on

such that

\big(H-(\nabla_{g_{0}}\log s)^{\perp}\big)=0

where

is mean curvature of
\partial Y
as a submanifold of

. By the conformal transformation law for mean curvature,

would have

\partial Y
as a submanifold of

Then by Graham’s Lemma there exists a unique special defining function

We call

With this choice made, we first we prove the following intermediate result:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let the setup be the same as in
Theorem

1.1
and choose a special
{}^{\textit{2}}
defining function
r
as outlined above. Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle 6A(Y)=8\pi^{2}\chi(Y)

\displaystyle-\int_{Y}\frac{|W|^{2}_{h_{+}}}{4}dA_{h_{+}}+\int_{Y}\frac{|E|^{2}_{h_{+}}}{2}dA_{h_{+}}

\displaystyle-\int_{Y}\frac{|\mathring{B}|_{h_{+}}^{4}}{24}dA_{h_{+}}-f.p.\int_{Y_{\epsilon}}|\mathring{B}|_{h_{+}}^{2}dA_{h_{+}}

where
Y_{\epsilon}=Y\cap\{r\geq\epsilon\}
and

is the finite part as
\epsilon\to 0
of
\int_{Y_{\epsilon}}|\mathring{B}|_{h_{+}}^{2}dA_{h_{+}}.

Note: The above formula actually holds for an arbitrary special defining function

The strategy for proving this is to begin with the Chern-Gauss-Bonnet formula for 4-dimensional manifolds with boundary applied to

We proceed by conformally transforming all of the terms in correspondence to the change

One of these terms then gives us a constant, 6; this is where the

and arrive at the result in

3.1
.
The next result we need is one that gives us an explicit expression for

this can be thought of as the renormalized
L^{2}(Y)
norm of the second fundamental form of

Theorem 3.2
.

Let the setup be the same as that in

1.1
and choose a special
{}^{\textit{2}}
defining function

(3.2)

|\int_{Y}\frac{\Delta^{Y}|\mathring{B}|_{h_{+}}^{2}}{2}+|\mathring{B}|_{h_{+}}^{2}dA_{h_{+}}|<\infty

Proof of Theorem 3.2

5.1.
Outline of Approach

The strategy for proving

and of

and observe that the latter has no constant term and has a term which is singular as
\epsilon\to 0
that cancels exactly with the singular term in the expansion of the former.

5.2.
Proof

First we compute a
g
-unit-normal vector field to
Y
which we call
\mu_{Y}.

Let
w=x^{4}-z(x^{a},r)
then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\nabla_{g_{+}}w=g^{ij}[\delta_{i}^{4}-z_{i}(x^{a},r)]\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=g^{4j}\partial_{j}-g^{ij}z_{i}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=g^{4\mu}\partial_{\mu}-g^{a\lambda}z_{a}\partial_{\lambda}-r^{2}z_{r}\partial_{r}

\displaystyle=r^{2}\partial_{4}+O(r^{4})\partial_{4}+O^{\lambda}(r^{4})\partial_{\lambda}+g^{ab}z_{a}\partial_{b}-r^{2}z_{r}\partial_{r}

\displaystyle=r^{2}[1+O(r^{2})]\partial_{4}+O^{a}(r^{4})\partial_{a}-r^{2}z_{r}\partial_{r}.

It follows that

(5.2)

|\nabla_{g_{+}}w|=r\sqrt{[1+O(r^{2})]\overline{g}_{44}+O(r^{4})}=r[1+O(r^{2})]

this implies then that

(5.3)

\frac{1}{|\nabla_{g_{+}}w|}=\frac{1+O(r^{2})}{r}

Therefore

(5.4)

\mu_{Y}=\frac{\nabla_{g_{+}}w}{|\nabla_{g_{+}}w|}=r[1+O(r^{2})]\partial_{4}+O^{a}(r^{3})\partial_{a}-rz_{r}\partial_{r}+O(r^{6})\partial_{r}

hence

(5.5)

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}_{Y}

\displaystyle=[1+O(r^{2})]\partial_{4}+O^{a}(r^{2})\partial_{a}-z_{r}\partial_{r}+O(r^{5})\partial_{r}

\displaystyle=\partial_{4}+O(r^{3})\partial_{r}+O^{\mu}(r^{2})\partial_{\mu}.

Note that
z_{r}=O(r^{3})
because of our choice of special defining function.
Conformally changing the metric
g_{+}\to r^{2}g_{+}
then the second fundamental form changes according to

(5.6)

\displaystyle B_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{B}_{\alpha\beta}}{r}+\frac{\overline{\mu}_{Y}(r)}{r^{2}}\overline{h}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{B}_{\alpha\beta}}{r}+O_{\alpha\beta}(r)

But

(5.7)

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{\alpha\beta}=\overline{g}(\nabla^{\overline{g}}_{\hat{\alpha}}\partial_{\hat{\beta}},\overline{\mu}_{Y})

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\partial_{4}\overline{g}_{\alpha\beta}}{2}+O(r^{2}).

\displaystyle|\overline{B}|^{2}_{\overline{h}}=\overline{h}^{ab}\overline{h}^{cd}\overline{B}_{ac}\overline{B}_{bd}+\overline{B}_{rr}+O(r^{5}),

Then we get

\displaystyle|B|^{2}_{h_{+}}

\displaystyle=\big[\frac{\overline{B}_{\alpha\beta}}{r}+O_{\alpha\beta}(r)\big]\big[\frac{\overline{B}_{\gamma\delta}}{r}+O_{\gamma\delta}(r)\big]h^{\alpha\gamma}h^{\beta\delta}

corresponds to the norm of the second fundamental form of
\partial Y=\Sigma
within
\partial X=M

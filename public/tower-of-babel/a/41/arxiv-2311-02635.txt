Irreducible modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro Lie algebras
Priyanshu Chakraborty

Abstract
. We study irreducible modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras. In particular, we give a complete classification of irreducible Harish-Chandra modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras. We will also classify non-weight irreducible modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras whose restriction on the degree zero part of the universal enveloping algebra of Witt algebra is free of rank 1.

1.
Introduction

The Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields on
S^{1}
is known as Witt algebra. The Witt algebra Der
\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
is an infinite dimensional Lie algebra with basis
d_{n}=t^{n+1}\frac{d}{dt},

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}
with the bracket operation
[d_{n},d_{m}]=(m-n)d_{m+n}
. The universal central extension of Witt algebra is known as Virasoro algebra, we denote it by
Vir
. The Virasoro algebra
Vir=
Der
\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]\oplus\mathbb{C}C
is a Lie algebra with the bracket operation

[d_{n},d_{m}]=(m-n)d_{n+m}+\delta_{n,-m}\frac{n^{3}-n}{12}C,\hskip 28.45274pt[d_{n},C]=0,\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Harish-Chandra modules for Virasoro Lie algebras are extensively studied, for instance see
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
12
]
and references therein.

The Virasoro Lie algebra and its related Lie algebras have been widely used in several branches of mathematics and physics, for example string theory
[
8
]
, Conformal field theory
[
11
]
, modular form
[
10
]
, Kac-Moody Lie algebras
[
9
]
, vertex operator algebras and so on.

We concentrate on the Virasoro related Lie algebra called Heisenberg-Virasoro Lie algebra, which is the universal central extension of the Lie algebra
\{f(t)\frac{d}{dt}+g(t):f(t),g(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]\}
of differential operators of degree at most one, see section 2 for details. Representation of Heisenberg-Virasoro Lie algebra have been studied in
[
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
]
and references therein. In particular in
[
5
]
authors classified irreducible Harish-Chandra modules for Heisenberg-Virasoro Lie algebras and in
[
14
]
authors classified irreducible modules for Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras which are free
U(d_{0})
-modules of rank 1.

In this paper our aim to study irreducible modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras. Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Lie algebra and
Z
be a affine scheme of finite type. Then the Lie algebra of all regular maps from
Z
to
\mathfrak{L}
called the map algebra associated with
Z
and
\mathfrak{L}
and it is isomorphic to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}\otimes B
, where
B=\mathcal{O}_{Z}
. In recent days study of modules for map algebras gaining interest, for instance see
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
21
,
22
]
.

In this paper we classify irreducible Harish-Chandra modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras. In particular we prove that irreducible Harish-Chandra modules are either single point evaluation modules corresponding to Heisenberg-Virasoro modules of intermediate series or a finite tensor product of generalized single point evaluation modules corresponding to highest weight (or lowest weight) modules. To classify irreducible Harish-Chandra modules we used many idea from
[
1
]
but arguments are different. On the other hand we classify non-weight irreducibles for map Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras which are free of rank 1 over
U(d_{0})
. It should mention here that this kind of free modules were first introduced and studied in
[
4
]
using fractional representation of
Vir

U(h)

[

,
20
,
25
,
26
,
24
]
. In particular, in
[
14
]
authors classified
U(d_{0})
free modules of rank 1 for Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and in
[
20
]
authors classified
U(d_{0})
free modules of rank 1 for loop-Virasoro algebra. In this paper we generalize the results of
[
14
]
, see section 5.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we review some important definitions related to map algebras. Furthermore we introduce Verma module for Heisenberg-Virasoro Lie algebra. In section 3 we classify irreducible uniformly bounded modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro Lie algebras. In section 4 we prove that irreducible Harish-Chandra modules are either highest weight or lowest weight or uniformly bounded modules. Moreover we find a necessary sufficient condition for irreducible highest weight modules to be Harish-Chandra module. In section 5 we classify irreducible non-weight modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro which are free of rank 1 over
U(d_{0})
. Finally we find the isomorphism classes of modules for map Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras which are free of rank 1 over
U(d_{0})
.

Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

For a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a commutative associative algebra

[g_{1}\otimes b_{1},g_{2}\otimes b_{2}]=[g_{1},g_{2}]\otimes b_{1}b_{2}

.

For a

and

.

We call

Definition 2.3
.

(
Evaluation module
)
Let

for all

. Equivalently, if

Map Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra:

Consider the Lie algebra with basis
\{d_{n},I_{n},C,C_{D},C_{I}|n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and following bracket operations:

(1)

\displaystyle[d_{n},d_{m}]=(m-n)d_{n+m}+\delta_{n,-m}\frac{n^{3}-n}{12}C

(2)

\displaystyle[d_{n},I_{m}]=mI_{m+n}+\delta_{n,-m}(n^{2}+n)C_{D}

(3)

\displaystyle[I_{n},I_{m}]=n\delta_{n,-m}C_{I}

(4)

\displaystyle[C,d_{n}]=[C,I_{n}]=[C_{D},d_{n}]=[C_{D},I_{n}]=[C_{I},d_{n}]=[C_{I},I_{n}]=0,

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This Lie algebra is known as Heisenberg-Virasoro Lie algebra. We denote this Lie algebra as
\mathcal{HV}.
It is easy to observe that
\mathcal{C}=span\{C,C_{D},C_{I},I_{0}\}
is the center of
\mathcal{HV}
. Note that the subalgebra spanned by
\{d_{n},C:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is the classical Virasoro algebra. We denote Virasoro algebra by
Vir
.

For a commutative associative finitely generated unital algebra
B
, we consider the Lie algebra
\mathcal{L_{B}}=\mathcal{HV}\otimes B
. For a
\mathcal{L_{B}}
-module
V
and
(\lambda,\lambda_{I_{0}},\lambda_{C},\lambda_{C_{I}},\lambda_{C_{D}})\in\mathbb{C}^{5}
we define:

V_{(\lambda,\lambda_{I_{0}},\lambda_{C},\lambda_{C_{I}},\lambda_{C_{D}})}=\{v\in V:d_{0}.v=\lambda v,I_{0}.v=\lambda_{I_{0}}v,C.v=\lambda_{C}v,C_{I}.v=\lambda_{C_{I}}v,C_{D}.v=\lambda_{C_{D}}v\}

and call
V_{(\lambda,\lambda_{I_{0}},\lambda_{C},\lambda_{C_{I}},\lambda_{C_{D}})}
as weight space of
V
of weight
(\lambda,\lambda_{I_{0}},\lambda_{C},\lambda_{C_{I}},\lambda_{C_{D}})
. We call a module
V
for
\mathcal{L_{B}}
as weight module if it is a direct sum of its weight spaces. Note that when
V
is irreducible Harish-Chandra module, then
\mathcal{C}
acts as scalar on
V
and hence we can denote weight spaces just by
V_{\lambda}
.

Let us define

\mathcal{HV}^{+}=span\{d_{i},I_{i}:i>0\},\hskip 11.38092pt\mathcal{HV}^{0}=span\{d_{0},I_{0},C,C_{I},C_{D}\},\hskip 11.38092pt\mathcal{HV}^{-}=span\{d_{i},I_{i}:i<0\}.

Then
\mathcal{HV}=\mathcal{HV}^{-}\oplus\mathcal{HV}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{HV}^{+}
gives a triangular decomposition of
\mathcal{HV}
. Moreover
\mathcal{HV}
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded given by the following gradation

\mathcal{HV}=\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{HV}_{i}},\hskip 28.45274pt{\mathcal{HV}_{i}}=span\{d_{i},I_{i}\},i\neq 0,\hskip 28.45274pt{\mathcal{HV}_{0}}=\mathcal{HV}^{0}.

Now it is clear that
\mathcal{L_{B}}
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded and
\mathcal{L_{B}}=\mathcal{L_{B}}^{-}\oplus\mathcal{L_{B}}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{L_{B}}^{+},
where
\mathcal{L_{B}}^{\pm}=\mathcal{HV}^{\pm}\otimes B
and
\mathcal{L_{B}}^{0}=\mathcal{HV}^{0}\otimes B
gives a triangular decomposition of
\mathcal{L_{B}}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A module
V
for
\mathcal{L_{B}}
is said to be a highest weight (respectively lowest weight) module if there exists a non-zero weight vector
v\in V
such that
\mathcal{L_{B}}^{+}.v=0
(respectively
\mathcal{L_{B}}^{-}.v=0
) and
U(\mathcal{L_{B}}).v=V.

Definition 2.5
.

(
Verma module
)
Let
\phi:\mathcal{L_{B}}^{0}\to\mathbb{C}
be a one dimensional representation of
\mathcal{L_{B}}^{0}
. Let
\eta
be a basis vector of this one dimensional representation.
Extend
\mathbb{C}\phi=span\{\eta\}
to a module for
\mathcal{L_{B}}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{L_{B}}^{+}
by defining action of
\mathcal{L_{B}}^{+}
as zero on
\mathbb{C}\phi
. Then define the Verma module,

M(\phi)=U(\mathcal{L_{B}})\bigotimes_{U(\mathcal{L_{B}}^{0}\oplus\mathcal{L_{B}}^{+})}\mathbb{C}\phi.

Clearly
M(\phi)
is a highest weight module with highest weight
(\phi(d_{0}),\phi(I_{0}),\phi(C),\phi(C_{I}),\phi(C_{D}))
.
Let
N(\phi)
be the unique maximal proper sub-module of
M(\phi)
. Then

V(\phi)=M(\phi)/N(\phi)
is a highest weight irreducible module for
\mathcal{L_{B}}
. Let
v_{\phi}
be the highest weight vector of
V(\phi)
.

Let

\displaystyle I_{n}\otimes b.f(t)=I_{n}\otimes b.f(d_{0}).1=f(t-n)I_{n}\otimes b.1,

(31)

\displaystyle C_{D}\otimes b.f(t)=f(t)C_{D}\otimes b.1

(32)

\displaystyle C_{I}\otimes b.f(t)=f(t)C_{I}\otimes b.1,

for all
f(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t],b\in B,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Case I:
Let
I_{n}.1=0
, for some
n\neq 0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then we consider the bracket operation
[d_{i}\otimes b^{\bf r},I_{n}].1=nI_{n+i}\otimes b^{\bf r}.1
, which implies that
I_{n+i}\otimes b^{\bf r}.1=0
, for all
i\neq-n
. Now it is easy to see using the brackets of
\mathcal{L_{B}}
that
C_{I}\otimes b^{\bf r},C_{D}\otimes b^{\bf r},I_{0}\otimes b^{\bf r}
acts trivially on
M
. Hence in this case
M\simeq\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\bm{\mu},0)
.

Case II:
Let
I_{n}.1=g(t)\neq 0
, for
n\neq 0
. Let
g(t)=\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{r}}a_{i}t^{i}
be a polynomial of degree
r
. Now consider the following:

\displaystyle(nI_{0}+(n^{2}-n)C_{D}).1

\displaystyle=[d_{-n},I_{n}].1

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-n}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{r}}a_{i}(t+n)^{i}(t+n\alpha)-\lambda^{-n}(t-n+n\alpha)\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{r}}a_{i}t^{i}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-n}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{r}}a_{i}(t+n)^{i+1}+(n\alpha-n)\lambda^{-n}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{r}}a_{i}(t+n)^{i}

\displaystyle-\lambda^{-n}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{r}}a_{i}t^{i+1}-(n\alpha-n)\lambda^{-n}\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{r}}a_{i}t^{i}

Since LHS of above equation is constant, differentiation with respect to
t
yields the value zero. Now differentiating RHS
r
-times we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(r+1)!a_{r}(t+n)+a_{r-1}r!+(n\alpha-n)a_{r}r!-a_{r}(r+1)!t-a_{r-1}r!-(n\alpha-n)a_{r}r!

\displaystyle=na_{r}(r+1)!,

here
r!
represents the factorial of a non-negative integer.
This implies that
g(t)
is a polynomial of lower degree. Continuing this process we get that
g(t)
is constant.

Let
I_{n}.1=\beta_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z},\beta_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. Now consider
[d_{m},I_{n}].1=nI_{m+n}.1+(m^{2}+m)C_{D}.1
. This implies that

(33)

\displaystyle n\beta_{n}\lambda^{m}=n\beta_{m+n}+(m^{2}+m)C_{D}.1.

In particular taking
m=-1
we have,
\beta_{n}=\lambda\beta_{n-1}
for all
n\neq 0
. Then from the equation (
33
) we have
C_{D}.1=0
Therefore we have a recurrence relation
n\beta_{n}\lambda^{m}=n\beta_{m+n}
, this implies that
\beta_{n}=\lambda^{n}\beta
where
\beta=\beta_{0}
.

Now consider the action
[d_{0}\otimes b^{\bf r},I_{n}].1=nI_{n}\otimes b^{\bf r}.1
. From this we obtain that
I_{n}\otimes b^{\bf r}=\bm{\mu}^{\bf r}\lambda^{n-|{\bf r}|}\beta
, for all
n\neq 0
. Now consider
[d_{-1},I_{1}\otimes b^{\bf r}].1=I_{0}\otimes b^{\bf r}.1
to find
I_{0}\otimes b^{\bf r}.1=\bm{\mu}^{\bf r}\lambda^{-|\bf{r}|}\beta
. Now it is easy to see that
C_{D}\otimes b^{\bf r}
and
C_{I}\otimes b^{\bf r}
acts trivially on
M
. Hence
M\simeq\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\bm{\mu},\beta)
. Rest part of the theorem follows from Theorem
11
.

∎

Theorem 13
.

\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\bm{\mu},\beta)\simeq\Omega(\lambda^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\bm{\mu}^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
if and only if
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime},\alpha=\alpha^{\prime},\bm{\mu}=\bm{\mu}^{\prime},\beta=\beta^{\prime}.

Proof.

Let
\phi:\Omega(\lambda,\alpha,\bm{\mu},\beta)\to\Omega(\lambda^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\bm{\mu}^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
be the isomorphism with its inverse
\phi^{-1}
. Consider the restriction of
\phi
to
Vir
-module, then from
[
4
]
we have
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime},\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}.
Let
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k})
and
\bm{\mu}^{\prime}=(\mu_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\mu_{k}^{\prime})
. Note that the following relations holds:

(34)

\displaystyle\phi(f(t))=\phi(f(d_{0})1)=f(t)\phi(1)

(35)

\displaystyle\phi^{-1}(f(t))=\phi^{-1}(f(d_{0})1)=f(t)\phi^{-1}(1).

In particular we have
\phi^{-1}(\phi(1))=\phi(1)\phi^{-1}(1)=1
, which implies that
\phi(1)
is a non-zero scalar. Now consider

\displaystyle\phi(d_{1}\otimes b_{i}.1)=d_{1}\otimes b_{i}.\phi(1)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\mu_{i}(t-\alpha)\phi(1)=\mu_{i}^{\prime}(t-\alpha^{\prime})\phi(1)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}^{\prime},

for all
1\leq i\leq k
, using the fact that
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
. Again consider the equation
\phi(I_{n}.1)=I_{n}.\phi(1)
and deduce that
\beta=\beta^{\prime}
. This completes the proof.

∎

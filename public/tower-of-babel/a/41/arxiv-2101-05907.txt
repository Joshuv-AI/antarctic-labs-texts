Bohm potential for the time dependent harmonic oscillator
Francisco Soto-Eguibar, Felipe A. Asenjo, Sergio A. Hojman, Héctor M. Moya-Cessa

Abstract
In the Madelung-Bohm approach to quantum mechanics, we consider a (time dependent) phase that depends quadratically on position and show that it leads to a Bohm potential that corresponds to a time dependent harmonic oscillator, provided the time dependent term in the phase obeys an Ermakov equation.

2
One-dimensional Madelung-Bohm approach

The main equation in quantum mechanics is the Schrodinger equation, that in one dimension and for a potential
V(x,t)
is written as (for simplicity, we set
\hslash=1
)

i\frac{\partial\psi(x,t)}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}+V(x,t)\psi(x,t)

(1)

with
\psi(x,t)
the wavefunction of the quantum mechanical system. We may give a solution in terms of a polar decomposition
[
27
,
28
,
23
]

\psi(x,t)=A(x,t)e^{iS(x,t)}

(2)

with
A(x,t)
and
S(x,t)
real functions that depend on time and position. We may separate the real and imaginary parts that come from substitution of (
2
) in (
1
), the first equation being a Quantum Hamilton–Jacobi equation (QHJE), similar to its classical counterpart (where the dot represents the time derivative and the prime the space derivatives),

\frac{1}{2m}S^{\prime 2}+V_{B}+V+\dot{S}=0,

(3)

and the second one, the continuity (probability conservation) equation,

\frac{1}{2m}(2A^{\prime}S^{\prime}+AS^{\prime\prime})+\dot{A}=0,

(4)

with the Bohm potential defined by
[
29
,
30
]

V_{b}=-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{A^{\prime\prime}}{A}.

(5)

3
Operator approach to the solution of continuity equation

The probability equation (
4
) may be rewritten as a Schrodinger-like equation

\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{2m}\left(2S^{\prime}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+S^{\prime\prime}\right)A,

(6)

that, by using the momentum operator
\hat{p}=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
, we may write as

\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{2m}\left(i2S^{\prime}\hat{p}+S^{\prime\prime}\right)A.

(7)

Now, we choose
S(x,t)=Q(x)\dot{\nu}(t)+\mu(t)
, such that
S^{\prime}=Q^{\prime}\dot{\nu}
, and we use the property
[f(x),\hat{p}]=if^{\prime}(x)
,
to rearrange the term

2S^{\prime}\hat{p}=\dot{\nu}(Q^{\prime}\hat{p}+Q^{\prime}\hat{p})=\dot{\nu}[Q^{\prime}\hat{p}+\hat{p}Q^{\prime}+iQ^{\prime\prime}],

(8)

so that we may write Eq. (
7
) as

\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}=-i\frac{\dot{\nu}}{2m}\left(Q^{\prime}\hat{p}+\hat{p}Q^{\prime}\right)A.

(9)

The above equation is readily solvable, with solution

A(x,t)=\exp\left\{-\frac{i}{2m}\int\dot{\nu}(t)dt\left[Q^{\prime}(x)\hat{p}+\hat{p}Q^{\prime}(x)\right]\right\}A_{0}(x),

(10)

where
A_{0}(x)=A(x,t=0)
, the initial condition, is an arbitrary (square integrable) function of position.

Next, we assume
Q(x)=x^{2}/2
and
m=1
to find the solution

A(x,t)=\exp\left[-i\frac{\nu(t)}{2}\left(x\hat{p}+\hat{p}x\right)\right]A_{0}(x).

(11)

In the above equation, the operator
\exp\left[-i\frac{\nu(t)}{2}\left(x\hat{p}+\hat{p}x\right)\right]

is the so-called squeeze operator
[
31
,
32
]
. By choosing an initial condition
A_{0}(x)=\pi^{-1/4}\exp\left(-x^{2}/2\right)
,

A(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi^{1/4}}\exp\left[-\frac{x^{2}}{2}e^{-2\nu(t)}-\frac{\nu(t)}{2}\right],

(12)

where we have used the fact that
\exp\left[-i\frac{\nu(t)}{2}\left(x\hat{p}+\hat{p}x\right)\right]x\exp\left[i\frac{\nu(t)}{2}\left(x\hat{p}+\hat{p}x\right)\right]=x\exp[-\nu(t)]
that is easily found from the Hadamard formula that states that for two operators
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
,
e^{r\hat{A}}\hat{B}e^{-r\hat{A}}=\hat{B}+r[\hat{A},\hat{B}]+\frac{r^{2}}{2!}[\hat{A},[\hat{A},\hat{B}]]+\dots
.

Now we are ready to calculate the Bohm potential, for which we need
A^{\prime}(x,t)=-xe^{-2\nu(t)}A(x,t)
and
A^{\prime\prime}(x,t)=\left[{x^{2}e^{-4\nu(t)}}-{e^{-2\nu(t)}}\right]A(x,t)
,
to obtain

V_{B}(x,t)=-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\exp\left[-4\nu(t)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\exp\left[-2\nu(t)\right].

(13)

By choosing
\dot{\mu}=-\exp\left[-2\nu(t)\right]/2
, we obtain from equation (
3
),

V+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\left(\ddot{\nu}+\dot{\nu}^{2}-e^{-4\nu}\right)=0;

(14)

by changing variables to
\rho=\exp\left(\nu\right)
, we get
\dot{\mu}=-1/\left(2\rho^{2}\right)
,
\dot{\nu}=\dot{\rho}/\rho
and

\ddot{\nu}=\left(\rho\ddot{\rho}-\dot{\rho}^{2}\right)/2

that gives

V+\frac{x^{2}}{2\rho}\left(\ddot{\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}\right)=0.

(15)

If
\rho
obeys the Ermakov equation

\ddot{\rho}+\Omega^{2}(t)\rho=\frac{1}{\rho^{3}},

(16)

we end up with the potential for the time dependent harmonic oscillator

V(x,t)=\frac{\Omega^{2}(t)}{2}x^{2}.

(17)

The wave function for the above potential and conditions then reads

\psi(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi^{1/4}}\exp\left\{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}e^{-2\nu(t)}-\frac{\nu(t)}{2}+i\left[\frac{x^{2}}{2}\dot{\nu}(t)+\mu(t)\right]\right\}.

(18)

4
As example a rational function of time

We consider a time dependent frequency of the form

\Omega(t)=\frac{1}{a+bt},

(19)

that shows for which has different solutions for
b=2
as will be shown below.

4.1
Case
0<b<2

The solution for the Ermakov equation for the frequency chosen above gives the auxiliary function

\rho(t)=C\sqrt{a+bt},

(20)

with
C=\left(1-b^{2}/4\right)^{-1/4}
. Eq. (
11
) sets the value for
\rho(0)=1
, which in turn forces
A=\sqrt{1-b^{2}/4}
and gives the function

\nu(t)=-\frac{1}{4}\ln\left(1-\frac{b^{2}}{4}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[\left(1-\frac{b^{2}}{4}\right)^{1/2}+Bt\right].

(21)

The Bohm potential, Eq. (
13
), is then

V_{B}(x,t)=-{\frac{1-b^{2}/4}{2(a+bt)^{2}}}x^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{1-b^{2}/4}}{2(a+bt)},

(22)

that shows that there exists a Bohm potential only for
0<b<2
.

For
0<b<2
and
t\geq 0
, the Bohm potential (
22
) is well defined, without singularities, and has the form shown in Fig.
1
. The Bohm potential exhibits the same qualitative behavior for all values of the parameter
b
in the interval
(0,2)
.

) for
b=1
.

4.2
Case
b=2

The solution of the Ermakov equation for such frequency may be found to be

\rho(t)=\sqrt{a+2t}\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}(a+2t)}

(23)

and the value for the parameter
a=1
is set by the condition
\rho(0)=1
, yielding the time dependent frequency
\Omega(t)=(1+2t)^{-1}
; so,

\rho(t)=\sqrt{1+2t}\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}(1+2t)}.

(24)

Therefore, the Bohm potential results to be

V_{B}(x,t)=-\frac{x^{2}}{2(1+2t)^{2}\left[1+\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}(1+2t)\right]^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(1+2t)\left[1+\frac{1}{4}\ln^{2}(1+2t)\right]};

(25)

hence, there is a kind of phase transition in the system as for
b\rightarrow 2
the Bohm potential (
22
) is zero, while for
b=2
it has finite values (
25
), as can be seen in Fig.
2
.

6
Conclusion

We have shown that for a phase of the form

S(x,t)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}\dot{\nu}(t)+\mu(t)

(26)

in the Madelung-Bohm formalism, a Bohm potential that corresponds to the time dependent harmonic oscillator is obtained. The condition for this is that the quantity
e^{\nu(t)}
obeys the Ermakov equation. A solution via an Ansatz was given in terms of operators that form a closed algebra and in the example we give, namely a rational function of time, it was shown that, because the Ermakov equation presents to different solutions, the Bohm potential also presents two different solutions, one for
b<2
and a different one for
b=2
.

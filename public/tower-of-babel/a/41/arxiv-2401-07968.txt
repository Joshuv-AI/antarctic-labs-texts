Characterizing the minimax rate of nonparametric regression under bounded star-shaped constraints


Abstract
We quantify the minimax rate for a nonparametric regression model over a star-shaped function class ℱ \mathcal{F} with bounded diameter. We obtain a minimax rate of ε ∗ 2 ∧ diam ​ ( ℱ ) 2 {\varepsilon^{\ast}}^{2}\wedge\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F})^{2} where ε ∗ = sup { ε > 0 : n ​ ε 2 ≤ log ⁡ ℳ ℱ loc ​ ( ε , c ) } , \varepsilon^{\ast}=\sup\{\varepsilon>0:n\varepsilon^{2}\leq\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\}, where log ⁡ ℳ ℱ loc ​ ( ⋅ , c ) \log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\cdot,c) is the local metric entropy of ℱ \mathcal{F} , c c is some absolute constant scaling down the entropy radius, and our loss function is the squared population L 2 L_{2} distance over our input space 𝒳 \mathcal{X} . In contrast to classical works on the topic ( Yang and Barron 1999 , cf.) , our results do not require functions in ℱ \mathcal{F} to be uniformly bounded in sup-norm. In fact, we propose a condition that simultaneously generalizes boundedness in sup-norm and the so-called L L -sub-Gaussian assumption that appears in the prior literature. In addition, we prove that our estimator is adaptive to the true point in the convex-constrained case, and to the best of our knowledge this is the first such estimator in this general setting. This work builds on the Gaussian sequence framework of Neykov 2022 using a similar algorithmic scheme to achieve the minimax rate. Our algorithmic rate also applies with sub-Gaussian noise. We illustrate the utility of this theory with examples including multivariate monotone functions, linear functionals over ellipsoids, and Lipschitz classes.

1
Introduction

This paper investigates minimax rates for nonparametric regression models under star-shaped constraints. We build on the techniques of
Neykov 2022
and
Prasadan and
Neykov 2024
with a universal scheme whose error matches the well-known minimax lower bound due to Fano’s inequality. The minimax rate turns out to depend closely on the local geometry of the constraint set, in particular, in terms of its local packing numbers. We are able to handle cases where the functions in
\mathcal{F}
do not have a uniform sup-norm bound.

1.1
Problem Formulation and Notation

Let
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
and let
\mathbb{P}_{X}
be some distribution on
\mathcal{X}
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a function class over
\mathcal{X}
. We observe
\{(X_{i},Y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
where for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
X_{i}
is independently drawn from
\mathbb{P}_{X}
and
Y_{i}=\bar{f}(X_{i})+\xi_{i}\in\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Here
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{F}
denotes the true regression function and the
\xi_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
are i.i.d. noise terms for some fixed constant
\sigma>0
(which does not scale with the sample size). We also consider sub-Gaussian noise, where we say a random variable
Z
is sub-Gaussian with mean
\mu
and variance proxy
\tau^{2}
if
\mathbb{E}[\exp(t(Z-\mu)]\leq\exp(t^{2}\tau^{2}/2)
for all
t>0
and write
Z\sim\mathrm{SG}(\mu,\tau^{2})
. In the sub-Gaussian setting, we will assume
\xi_{i}\sim\mathrm{SG}(0,\sigma^{2})
are drawn i.i.d.

For any
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
, define the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-distance between
f
and
g
by

\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}=\sqrt{\int|f(x)-g(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)}.

The
L_{p}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-norm can be defined analogously for
p\geq 2
. We write
\mathcal{F}\subseteq L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
if
\int|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_{X}(x)<\infty
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
. We define
d:=\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F}):=\sup\{\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}:f,g\in\mathcal{F}\}
; we assume this quantity is finite throughout the paper. Let
B(f,r,\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})})=B(f,r)=\{g\in\mathcal{F}:\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq r\}
. Unless stated otherwise, we will always use the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-norm for balls and abbreviate the notation as
B(f,r)
. We write
B_{2}^{p}(\beta,r)
for the Euclidean
\ell_{2}
-ball in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
with radius
r
and center
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

The empirical
L_{2}
-distance given input points
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\sim\mathbb{P}_{X}
is defined by

\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}=n^{-1/2}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f(X_{i})-g(X_{i})|^{2}}.

We introduce the notation
\vec{Y}=(Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}),\vec{X}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. We denote by
g(\vec{X})
the vector with
g(X_{i})
in the
i
th component, and similarly for
f(\vec{X})
and
\bar{f}(\vec{X}).
We can equivalently write our
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})
distances as
\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}=n^{-1/2}\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}
, using the Euclidean
L_{2}
norm for vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We often abbreviate
\{1,\dots,n\}
with
[n]
. We write
x\lesssim y
(or
y\gtrsim x
) if there is some positive absolute constant
\alpha
such that
x\leq\alpha y
. If
x\lesssim y
and
y\lesssim x
, we write
x\asymp y
. We denote the
p\times p
identity matrix by
\mathbb{I}_{p}
.

Definition 1.1
.

We say a function class
\mathcal{F}
is star-shaped if there exists an
f_{c}\in\mathcal{F}
, called the center of
\mathcal{F}
, such that for any
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
t\in[0,1]
,
tf+(1-t)f_{c}\in\mathcal{F}
.

Henceforth we will assume
\mathcal{F}\subseteq L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
is a star-shaped and complete class of real-valued functions on
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Our goal is to quantify the minimax rate

\inf_{\hat{f}}\sup_{\bar{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}_{\vec{X},\vec{Y}}\|\hat{f}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}

and achieve it with a universal scheme depending only on the local geometry of
\mathcal{F}
. Here the infimum is over all measurable functions of the data
\hat{f}\colon\mathcal{X}^{n}\times\mathcal{Y}^{n}\to\mathcal{F}
.

A crucial assumption we impose on
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathbb{P}_{X}
is the following: for some fixed constant
L>0
and every integer
p\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{L_{2p}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2p}\leq\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}L^{p-1}p!/2.

(1.1)

This will later yield us a Bernstein-like concentration inequality from which we can derive our upper bound. The requirement is implied by having a bound on the sup-norm of
\mathcal{F}
, or an
L
-sub-Gaussian condition (explained in the Related Works section), and is in fact strictly more general than either of these assumptions.

An essential tool will be the local metric entropy of
\mathcal{F}
, which we now define. Observe that this quantity may be infinite if
\mathcal{F}
is not totally bounded, but our theorems will still hold (see Remark
3.16
).

Definition 1.2

(Local Metric Entropy)
.

We say a set
S
is an
r
-packing set in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
norm for a function class
\mathcal{G}
defined on
\mathcal{X}
if
S\subseteq\mathcal{G}
and for all distinct
f,g\in S
,
\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>r
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}(r,\mathcal{G})
the maximal cardinality of an
r
-packing set in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
norm of
\mathcal{G}
. Given our function class
\mathcal{F}
, we define for a constant
c>1
the following:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon/c,B(f,\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{F}).

Then
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)
is the local metric entropy of
\mathcal{F}
. We may omit the subscript
\mathcal{F}
unless necessary.

The local metric entropy has been used in several existing works to obtain minimax rates for non-parametric regression, including
Yang and Barron 1999
,
Yang 2004
,
Wang et al. 2014
, and
Mendelson 2017
.

1.2
Related Works

The literature on nonparametric regression is vast
(
Tsybakov 2009
, e.g.,)
, so we do not give an exhaustive summary. We do however highlight those works closest in spirit to our results.

In
Yang and Barron 1999
, the authors study minimax rates in terms of global entropies for density estimation, but apply their results to nonparametric regression. They obtain a similar result in their Theorem 6, where the minimax rate is
\varepsilon^{2}
for
\varepsilon
satisfying
\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F})\asymp n\varepsilon^{2}
. However, they require
\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{\infty}<L
for some
L>0
, while our results relax this assumption. As a consequence, we are able to derive minimax rates on linear functionals with unbounded sup norms. In addition,
Yang and Barron 1999
impose an assumption on the parameter space which essentially forces the local and global metric entropies to coincide. We do not require this condition in our work.

Mendelson 2017
considers prediction problems for convex function classes, including ones with unbounded sup-norm. The minimax rate we consider can be viewed as a special case of this prediction setting, although we have a much broader set of constraints. However, Mendelson bounds the error with high probability rather than in expectation. Moreover,
Mendelson 2017
requires an assumption that
\mathcal{F}
is
L
-sub-Gaussian. This definition has two components. First, for each
p\geq 2
and
f,g\in\mathcal{F}\cup\{0\}
, there must exist
L>0
such that
\|f-g\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq L\sqrt{p}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}
. Second, the centered canonical Gaussian process induced by
\mathcal{F}
must be bounded. That is, to each
f\in\mathcal{F}
, we associate a Gaussian random variable
G_{f}
with
\mathbb{E}[G_{f}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[G_{f}G_{g}]=\mathbb{E}[fg]
for all
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
, and the assumption is that
\sup_{f}G_{f}
is a bounded random variable almost surely. We impose a strictly weaker condition, which is implied, for example, by an
L
-sub-Gaussian assumption or a bounded sup-norm, but neither of those two conditions are necessary.

Moreover, in all cases we require a finite diameter with respect to the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
norm. This assumption about the diameter may be easier to verify in practice compared to the boundedness of the Gaussian process. Another distinction with our approach is that
Mendelson 2017
does not precisely match the lower and upper bounds on the error rate. In addition, since every convex set is star-shaped, we generalize the shape constraints.

Other works include
Yang 2004
, which examines taking data-dependent convex combinations of
n^{\tau}
for
\tau>0
many candidate regression procedures to obtain a rate of order of the minimax rate plus a penalty term. This work requires the function to be bounded in sup-norm, but relaxes assumptions on the distribution of the error term.
Yang and Tokdar 2015
studies the nonparametric regression setting under assumptions of sparsity or additivity of the function class. They also require assumptions that the distribution on the input space
\mathcal{X}=[0,1]^{p}
has a density lower bounded on a sub-hypercube (i.e., the data is not from a strictly lower dimensional subspace). The paper also uses global metric entropy conditions related to the minimax rate, as in
Yang and Barron 1999
.
Zhao and Yang 2023
study a very general set of non-parametric problems allowing for more general noise distributions as well as the noise varying with
X
. Their results impose a bounded sup-norm assumption on the function class, however, in contrast to our work. Moreover, we are interested in scenarios where the richness condition in the authors’ Definition 3 may not hold, i.e., when the local and global entropies do not match for small
\varepsilon
.

The techniques in this paper are similar to
Neykov 2022
in that we successively build finer local packings of the function class, resulting in an infinite tree of points that densely cover the set. However, we must perform an additional pruning step to correct a gap found in the argument of
Neykov 2022
. The procedure is described in full detail in
Prasadan and
Neykov 2024
, where the authors derive minimax rates for adversarially contaminated, sub-Gaussian mean location models with a star-shaped constraint.

We then consider in Section

, we consider functions of the form

and draw data

2
Lower Bound

Our lower bound results all use existing and well-known information theoretic arguments, but we provide the details for completeness. The same argument using the local entropy appears in
Yang and Barron 1999
,
Yang 2004
, and
Wang et al. 2014
, for example.
The upshot is that the minimax rate is at least
\varepsilon^{2}
up to constants where
\varepsilon
satisfies
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)>4(n\varepsilon^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})\vee\log 2)
.

We start by restating Fano’s Inequality:

Lemma 2.1

(Fano’s Inequality for Nonparametric Regression)
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathcal{F}
be a collection of
\varepsilon
-separated functions, i.e., satisfying
\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}>\varepsilon
for each
i\neq j
. Suppose
J
is uniformly distributed over the index set
[m]
, and
(Y_{i}|X_{i},J=j)=f_{j}(X_{i})+\xi_{i}
where
\xi_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
for each
i\in[n],j\in[m]
. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{f}}\sup_{\bar{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}_{\vec{X},\vec{Y}}\|\hat{f}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\bigg(1-\frac{I(\vec{X},\vec{Y};J)+\log 2}{\log m}\bigg),

where
I(\vec{X},\vec{Y};J)
is the mutual information between our data and
J
.

The next lemma gives an upper bound on the mutual information term that appears in Fano’s Inequality.

Lemma 2.2

(Bounding the Mutual Information in Nonparametric Setting)
.

Under the same set-up as in Lemma
2.1
, we have
I(\vec{X},\vec{Y};J)\leq\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}\max_{j}\|f_{j}-h\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}
for any
h\in\mathcal{F}
.

To complete the lower bound, we return to the local metric entropy defined earlier.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{f}}\sup_{\bar{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}_{\vec{X},\vec{Y}}\|\hat{f}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8c^{2}}

for any
\varepsilon
satisfying
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)>4\left(\frac{n\varepsilon^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\vee\log 2\right)
, where
c
is the constant from the Definition
1.2
fixed to some large enough value.

3
Upper Bound

We now establish our upper bound, simultaneously handling the cases where
\mathcal{F}
is bounded or unbounded in sup-norm, while always assuming the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-diameter is finite. More precisely, the bounded sup-norm scenario means there is some
r>0
such that
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq r
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
. This implies
d\leq 2r
by the triangle inequality. The unbounded sup-norm assumption means for all
r>0
, there is an
f\in\mathcal{F}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
such that
|f(x)|>r
). We implicitly assume
\mathcal{F}
is totally-bounded when using the local metric entropy, but Remark
3.16
details how this is not a necessary assumption.

To achieve the optimal rate, we first construct an algorithm that outputs an estimator achieving a squared error rate of
\varepsilon^{2}
when
\varepsilon
satisfies a (discretized) condition of the form
n\varepsilon^{2}>2\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}\left(\overline{C}\varepsilon,c\right)\vee\log 2
for some constant
\overline{C}
.

To tie together this upper bound with the lower bound from Lemma
2.3
, we show that either the minimax rate is
d^{2}
, in which case our algorithm clearly outputs a function in
\mathcal{F}
achieving this error rate, or it has an error rate
{\varepsilon^{\ast}}^{2}
satisfying both the lower and upper bound conditions mentioned above. In fact, we establish the rate is
{\varepsilon^{\ast}}^{2}\wedge d^{2}
, where

\varepsilon^{\ast}:=\sup\{\varepsilon:n\varepsilon^{2}\leq\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\}

(3.1)

for some sufficiently large constant
c
.

Throughout this section, we take
C=2(c+1)
and assume
c
is sufficiently large.

3.1
Packing and Pruning a Tree

Before applying our algorithm, we must perform an intricate construction of iteratively formed local packing sets, as described in
Neykov 2022
. This produces a directed tree whose nodes densely pack and cover
\mathcal{F}
, at finer distances the deeper down the tree. However, as is done in
Prasadan and
Neykov 2024
, a critical pruning step is required. Once this pruned tree is constructed, we use our data to traverse the tree (equivalently, updating our estimate of
\bar{f}
), forming a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{F}
. Due to our use of functions, we use a different criterion to update our estimate of
\bar{f}
, namely, minimizing the empirical least squares error among functions in the local packing at each step. Moreover, the tree itself is completely independent of the observed data and only depends on the function class.

We direct the reader to
Prasadan and
Neykov 2024
, where the tree construction is explained in detail for an arbitrary star-shaped set
K
with a bounded diameter. We will give a very brief synopsis and write an analogous version for our star-shaped function class.

The tree, denoted
G
, will be constructed level by level, with nodes corresponding to functions in
\mathcal{F}
. We will denote the
j
th level of
G
as
\mathcal{L}(j)
, where the first level is simply the root node. We write
\mathcal{P}(f)
as the parent set of a node
f
, meaning there is an edge from each node in
\mathcal{P}(f)
to
f
. The offspring set
\mathcal{O}(f)
are the set of nodes with a directed node pointing to them from
f
. Note that all notions of distance here are with respect to
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
, but we show in Lemma
3.10
that any equivalent norm may be used.

To construct
G
, first pick a root node
f_{R}
, forming
\mathcal{L}(1)
. Form
\mathcal{L}(2)
by taking the points of a
d/c

f_{R}
to each of these points. For
\mathcal{L}(3)

g

\mathcal{L}(2)
, form a
d/(4c)
-packing of
B(g,d/2)\cap\mathcal{F}

g
to these each of those points.

Now begins our first pruning step. Order the nodes lexicographically (justified by Remark
B.1
), say
f_{1}^{3},f_{2}^{3},\dots,f_{N_{3}}^{3}
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{3}
be a list of unprocessed nodes, initially set to
[f_{1}^{3},f_{2}^{3},\dots,f_{N_{3}}^{3}]
. Now construct
\mathcal{T}_{3}(f_{1}^{3})
to be the set of nodes in
\mathcal{U}_{3}
(other than
f_{1}^{3}
) within distance
d/(4c)
of
f_{1}^{3}
. If empty, update
\mathcal{U}_{3}
by removing
f_{1}^{3}
from it, and proceed to the steps in the following paragraph. Otherwise do the following. For each element
f_{j}^{3}
of
\mathcal{T}_{3}(f_{1}^{3})

f_{1}^{3}

f_{j}^{3}

\mathcal{T}_{3}(f_{1}^{3})
have been handled, update
\mathcal{U}_{3}
by removing
\{f_{1}^{3}\}\cup\mathcal{T}_{3}(f_{1}^{3})

Then we repeat: pick the remaining node in line from
\mathcal{U}_{3}
, say
f_{k}^{3}
, construct
\mathcal{T}_{3}(\cdot)
as the set of nodes in
\mathcal{U}_{3}
within distance

of

\mathcal{U}_{3}
. Once
\mathcal{U}_{3}
is empty, the pruning is done. The remaining nodes comprise level 3 of the tree,

Algorithm 1

Directed Tree Construction

Some basic lemmas for this directed tree construction are proven in

Neykov 2024

Lemma 3.1

(Lemma 3.1 of
Prasadan and
Neykov 2024
)
.

For any

\mathcal{F}

\tfrac{d}{2^{J-1}c}

\mathcal{F}
. In addition, for each

.

(Lemma 3.2 of

)
.

For any

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 3.3 of
Prasadan and
Neykov 2024
)
.

Let
[\Upsilon_{1},\Upsilon_{2},\dots,]
be the nodes of an infinite path in

for any

. Then for any integers

\|\Upsilon_{J^{\prime}}-\Upsilon_{J}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\frac{d(2+4c)}{c2^{J^{\prime}}}
.

Our algorithm traverses the tree as follows. Starting with our root node
\Upsilon_{1}=f_{R}
, given an update
\Upsilon_{k}
and its offspring set
\mathcal{O}(\Upsilon_{k})
, we set

\Upsilon_{k+1}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{f\in\mathcal{O}(\Upsilon_{k})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i}-f(X_{i}))^{2}.

By Lemma
3.3
and our assumption that
\mathcal{F}
is complete, we obtain a convergent Cauchy sequence. Observe that it is only at this stage that we actually use our data.

This procedure may encounter ties in the minimization step, so we must modify our algorithm to handle such cases and, in doing so, preserve measurability of the estimator it outputs. Our goal is to induce a lexicographic ordering on our functions and then choose the smallest function in the ordering to break ties. The details are given in the appendix (Remark
B.1
).

We can also show (Lemma
3.10
) that if an equivalent norm to

is known, then the packing procedure can be performed in this norm instead and we will achieve the same necessary bounds.

Input:

\Upsilon_{1}=f_{R}\in\mathcal{F}

\{(X_{i},Y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}

k\leftarrow 1
;

1

\Upsilon\leftarrow[\Upsilon_{1}]
;

2

TRUE

do

3

\Upsilon_{k+1}\leftarrow\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{f\in\mathcal{O}(\Upsilon_{k})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Y_{i}-f(X_{i}))^{2}

/*
Break ties lexicographically */

k\leftarrow k+1
;

4

return

\Upsilon=[\Upsilon_{1},\Upsilon_{2},\Upsilon_{3},\dots]

3.2
Moment Condition

Recall our assumption on both
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathbb{P}_{X}
given in (
1.1
). Earlier work on the convex constrained non-parametric regression setting by
Mendelson 2017
imposed an
L
-sub-Gaussian condition (defined in the following proposition). It turns out an
L
-sub-Gaussian assumption implies (
1.1
), as does having a bounded sup-norm. Similar to
L
-sub-Gaussianity, our new condition implies a Bernstein-like concentration inequality for
\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}
that we use to bound our algorithmic error.

We start by proving (
1.1
) is more general in the following proposition and example. Note that the proof requires
\mathbb{E}(f-g)^{2}\leq d^{2}
, where
d
is considered a fixed constant.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
diameter
d
is some fixed constant. If
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathbb{P}_{X}
satisfy an
L
-sub-Gaussian assumption, i.e.,

\|f-g\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq L\sqrt{p}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}

(3.2)

for all
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
and
p\geq 2
then (
1.1
) holds. Moreover, if
\mathcal{F}
has a uniform sup-norm bound, we again have (
1.1
).

Example 3.5
.

Suppose
X\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and the model is
Y=\bar{f}(X)+\xi
, where

\bar{f}\in\mathcal{F}=\{f|f\mbox{ is 1-Lipschitz},f(0)=0\}.

Then we have (
1.1
) but this example is neither uniformly bounded in sup-norm nor
L
-sub-Gaussian.

The proof that the preceding example is not
L
-sub-Gaussian follows the logic in
Han and Wellner 2018
using the failure of a necessary small-ball condition.

Next, we show our moment condition (
1.1
) implies a Bernstein-like concentration inequality. Indeed, the proof is very similar to that of Bernstein’s inequality.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume for a random variable
Z
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z^{2p}\leq\mathbb{E}Z^{2}L^{p-1}p!/2,

for all integers
p\geq 2
and for some
L>0
. Then if
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
are i.i.d. variables with the same law as
Z
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Z_{i}^{2}-\mathbb{E}Z^{2})\geq t\right)\vee\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(Z_{i}^{2}-\mathbb{E}Z^{2})\leq-t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{16n\mathbb{E}Z^{2}L+4Lt}\right).

3.3
Algorithm Error Bounds

To prove our algorithm has the desired properties, we first relate the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
norm to the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})
norm. In particular, Lemma
3.7
shows that if
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
are far apart but
f
is close to
\bar{f}
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-distances, then the same is true for
f,g,\bar{f}
with high probability in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})
-distance. The proof relies on applying our Bernstein-like concentration inequality in Lemma
3.6
. This result also applies when the noise is sub-Gaussian.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
satisfy
n\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\geq C^{2}\delta^{2}
for some
C>0
. Suppose further that
n\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}<\delta^{2}
. Then
n\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}\leq 2\delta^{2}
and
n\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}\geq(C^{2}-1)\delta^{2}
both simultaneously hold with probability at least

1-\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{20L}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4L(C^{2}+1)}\right).

The next lemma considers hypothesis testing whether the true function is
f
or
g
when
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
are at least
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-distance
\delta/\sqrt{n}
apart. When
f
is
\delta/\sqrt{n}
close to the truth, the probability our test claims
g
is the right choice is low, and similarly, when
g
is close to the truth, the probability our test claims
f
is the right choice is low.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose we are testing
H_{0}:\bar{f}=f
vs
H_{1}:\bar{f}=g
where
n\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\geq C^{2}\delta^{2}
for some
C>3
. Then the test
\psi(\vec{Y})=\mathbbm{1}\big(\|\vec{Y}-f(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\|\vec{Y}-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\big)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{\bar{f}:n\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}<\delta^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=1)\vee\sup_{\bar{f}:n\|g-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}<\delta^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=0)

\displaystyle\leq 3\exp\left(-\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot\delta^{2}\right),

where

\displaystyle\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)=\min\left\{\frac{\left(\sqrt{C^{2}-1}-2\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}},\frac{1}{20L},\frac{1}{4L(C^{2}+1)}\right\}.

(3.3)

We now build on the previous lemma by showing the function in a
\delta
-packing closest to our data will also be
\delta
-close (in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-distance) to the true function with high probability.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{M}
be a
\delta
-packing of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and suppose
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Let

i^{\ast}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{i\in[M]}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(Y_{j}-f_{i}(X_{j}))^{2}.

Then recalling the definition of
\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)
in (
3.3
), we have for
C>3
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\left(\|f_{i^{\ast}}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>\delta(C+1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3M\exp\left(-\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot n\delta^{2}\right).

Note that the previous lemma still holds if we have an equivalent norm, i.e.,
c_{1}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\|f-g\|^{\prime}\leq c_{2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}
for some absolute
c_{1},c_{2}
. As a consequence, we can build the packing local sets with the norm
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}
in place of
\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}
and obtain the same minimax rate.

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose there exists an equivalent norm
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{F}
to
\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}
, i.e., there exists
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\|f-g\|^{\prime}\leq c_{2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}
. Suppose
f_{1},\dots,f_{M}
is a
\delta
-packing in the
\|\cdot\|^{\prime}
norm of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
with
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and set
i^{\ast}=\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{i\in[M]}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(Y_{j}-f_{i}(X_{j}))^{2}.
Then so long as
C>4c_{2}-1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\left(\|f_{i^{\ast}}-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}>\delta(C+1)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3M\exp\left(-c_{2}^{-2}\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot n\delta^{2}\right).

Proof.

Observe that for
i\neq j
we have

\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\geq c_{2}^{-1}\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|^{\prime}>c_{2}^{-1}\delta,

i.e., we have a
(\tfrac{\delta}{c_{2}})
-packing in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-norm. Moreover, note that
\|f_{i^{\ast}}-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}>(C+1)\delta
implies
\|f_{i^{\ast}}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>c_{2}^{-1}(C+1)\delta
. Thus, we have by Lemma
3.9
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\left(\|f_{i^{\ast}}-\bar{f}\|^{\prime}>(C+1)\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\left(\|f_{i^{\ast}}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>c_{2}^{-1}(C+1)\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3M\exp\left(-c_{2}^{-2}\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot n\delta^{2}\right).

Note that
c_{2}^{-1}(C+1)=\tilde{C}+1
for
\tilde{C}=c_{2}^{-1}(C+1)-1
. Thus, to apply Lemma
3.9
above, we need
\tilde{C}>3
, which is implied by assuming
C>4c_{2}-1
.
∎

The function

Now we return to our algorithm and quantify its error in the

-distance. The structure of the proof can be seen as a special case of the robust analogue in

Theorem 3.13

Let

be the maximal integer

\varepsilon_{J}=\frac{d\sqrt{\Lambda(c/2-1,\sigma,L)}}{2^{J-2}c}

and

, up to absolute constants depending only on

,

, and

. We defined

in (

Theorem 3.15

Define

Remark 3.16

(Without Total Boundedness)
.

Consider the case where the local packing number is infinite for some
\varepsilon

\varepsilon/c
-packings of sets of the form
\mathcal{F}\cap B(f,\varepsilon)
for
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Note that Lemma

and Lemma

still hold since they only require the existence of

(\varepsilon/c)
-packing set of

is infinite. Then we use a finite subset of that infinite packing set and apply the same logic as in the rest of the proof.

For the upper bound, the algorithm may require solving an optimization over infinitely many functions, but our results still hold. Lemma

and Lemma

do not rely on any packing constructions but simply a concentration result. Lemma

4
Adaptive Bound for Convex Classes

We relax the definition of local metric entropy by removing the supremum and instead considering the pointwise value of the local metric entropies. This lets us produce an estimator that is adaptive to the true point
\bar{f}
, which is a difference from the work in
Yang and Barron 1999
and
Mendelson 2017
, and to our knowledge is the first example of an adaptive estimator in this general setting. However, we no longer permit arbitrary star-shaped function classes and require convexity.

Definition 4.1

(Adaptive Local Entropy)
.

Recall we defined
\mathcal{M}(r,\mathcal{F})
as the largest cardinality of a
r
-packing set in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
norm of
\mathcal{F}
. Let
c>0
. Then we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\varepsilon,c)=\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon/c,B(f,\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{F}).

Then
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\varepsilon,c)
is the adaptive local entropy of
\mathcal{F}
at
f
.

The adaptive local entropy appears in several existing works, including
Yang 2004
and
Wang et al. 2014
. A following lemma relates the adaptive local metric entropy of two nearby points.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
satisfy
\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\delta
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\varepsilon,c)\leq\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(g,2\varepsilon,2c)
provided
\varepsilon\geq\delta
.

Proof.

If we show
B(f,\varepsilon)\subseteq B(g,2\varepsilon)
, then the cardinality of the largest
(\varepsilon/c)
-packing of
B(f,\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{F}
will be less than or equal to the cardinality of the largest
(2\varepsilon/2c)
-packing of
B(g,2\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{F}
. Well, if
h\in B(f,\varepsilon)
, then
\|h-f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\varepsilon
. Using the triangle inequality,

\|h-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\|h-f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}+\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\varepsilon+\delta\leq 2\varepsilon,

showing that
h\in B(g,2\varepsilon)
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume
\mathcal{F}
is convex and pick
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Then the function
\varepsilon\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\varepsilon,c)
is non-increasing, and the function
c\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\varepsilon,c)
is non-decreasing.

Proof.

We prove the first claim as the second has a similar argument. Pick
0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}
. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
be a maximal
\varepsilon_{2}/c
packing set of
B(f,\varepsilon_{2})\cap\mathcal{F}
, so that
N=\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\varepsilon_{2},c)
. For each
1\leq i\leq N
, set
g_{i}=\alpha f_{i}+(1-\alpha)f
where
\alpha=\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\in(0,1)
, noting
g_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
by convexity. Then for each
i
we have

\displaystyle\|g_{i}-f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon_{2}}f_{i}+(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon_{2}})f-f\right\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\|f_{i}-f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdot\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon_{1},

using that
f_{i}\in B(f,\varepsilon_{2})
. Thus,
g_{1},\dots,g_{N}\in B(f,\varepsilon_{1})\cap\mathcal{F}
. Moreover, for
i\neq j
, we have

\displaystyle\|g_{i}-g_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}=\alpha\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\varepsilon_{2}}\cdot\tfrac{\varepsilon_{2}}{c}=\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{c},

verifying
g_{1},\dots,g_{N}
indeed form a (possibly non-maximal)
\varepsilon_{1}/c
-packing of
B(f,\varepsilon_{1})\cap\mathcal{F}
. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\varepsilon_{1},c)\geq N=\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\varepsilon_{2},c)
.
∎

We now show that the main estimator is adaptive to the true point
\bar{f}
, in which the key condition (
4.2
) does not require taking a supremum over all
f\in\mathcal{F}
in the metric entropy term. The proof also applies in the sub-Gaussian noise setting.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
J^{\dagger}
be the maximal integer
J\geq 1
with
\varepsilon_{J}=\frac{d\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}}{2^{J-2}c}
satisfying

n\varepsilon_{J}^{2}>\inf_{f\in\mathcal{F}}2\log\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f,\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{J}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},4c)\right]^{2}\vee\log 2

(4.1)

and
J^{\dagger}=1
if this condition is never satisfied. Let
f^{\ddagger}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})
be the output of
J^{\ddagger}
steps of
Algorithm
2
where

.

Next, let
J^{\ast}
be the maximal integer
J\geq 1
with
\varepsilon_{J}
satisfying

n\varepsilon_{J}^{2}>2\log\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(\bar{f},\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{J}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},4c)\right]^{2}\vee\log 2,

(4.2)

noting the use of the true
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{F}
. Set
J^{\ast}=1
if this condition never occurs. Then
\mathbb{E}_{\vec{X},\vec{Y}}\|\bar{f}-f^{\ddagger}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon_{J^{\ast}}^{2}
up to some universal constants,

, and

. We defined
\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)
in (

), where
C=\tfrac{c}{2}-1
and

is some sufficiently large universal constant.

5
Examples

We now give some examples using our theory, most of which are known. The known examples include linear functionals over the
\ell_{1}
ball, Lipschitz classes, and monotone functions in a multivariate setting. However, we obtain new minimax rates for linear functionals over ellipsoids.

5.1
Linear Functionals

We begin with function spaces defined as linear functionals over compact, convex sets
K
using sub-Gaussian isotropic data; such function spaces will not have a uniform bound on the sup-norm. For such spaces, local metric entropies in our function space using an
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-norm will be equivalent to local metric entropies in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
with the Euclidean 2-norm. We then specialize our results to the
\ell_{1}
-ball and ellipsoids.

We call a random variable
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
isotropic if
\mathbb{E}[Z]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[ZZ^{T}]=\mathbb{I}_{p}
. Moreover, we say
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a sub-Gaussian random vector with mean
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and variance proxy
\tau^{2}
if for any
v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
with
\|v\|_{2}=1
, the random variable
v^{T}(Z-\mu)
is sub-Gaussian with mean
0
and variance proxy
\tau^{2}
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a compact, convex set. Define
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{\beta}\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R},f_{\beta}(x)=x^{T}\beta;\beta\in K\}
. Now let us draw i.i.d. isotropic sub-Gaussian vectors
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
with mean
0
and variance proxy
\tau^{2}>1
. Importantly, functions in
\mathcal{F}
are not bounded in sup-norm despite
K
being compact. Observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{f_{\beta_{1}},f_{\beta_{2}}\in\mathcal{F}}\|f_{\beta_{1}}-f_{\beta_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}=\sup_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in K}\mathbb{E}\left[(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})^{T}X\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in K}\|\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}\|_{2}^{2}=\mathrm{diam}(K)^{2}.

The third equality used the fact that
X
is isotropic. Thus, the diameter of
\mathcal{F}
in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
-norm is the diameter of
K
which is finite.

Next, note that for any distinct
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in K
, we have
\|\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}\|_{2}^{-1}\cdot(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})^{T}X\sim\mathrm{SG}(0,\tau^{2})
. Let
W
denote this quantity. By
Vershynin 2018
, for some absolute constant
\alpha>0
we have
\mathbb{E}|W^{p}|\leq\tau^{p}\alpha^{p}p^{p/2}
. Then we compute that

\displaystyle\|f_{\beta_{1}}-f_{\beta_{2}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[(\beta_{1}-\beta_{2})^{T}X\right]^{p}\leq\|\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}\|_{2}^{p}\cdot\mathbb{E}|W^{p}|

\displaystyle\leq\|\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}\|_{2}^{p}\cdot\tau^{p}\alpha^{p}p^{p/2}.

Taking the
p
th root and noting by our above argument that
X
being isotropic implies
\|\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}\|_{2}=\|f_{\beta_{1}}-f_{\beta_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}
, we have

\|f_{\beta_{1}}-f_{\beta_{2}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\tau\alpha\|f_{\beta_{1}}-f_{\beta_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\sqrt{p}.

Hence the
L
-sub-Gaussian condition in (
3.2
) holds, so by Proposition
3.4
, our key condition (
1.1
) holds.

Moreover, since
\|f_{\beta_{1}}-f_{\beta_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}=\|\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}\|_{2}
holds for all
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in K
and since
B(f_{\beta},\varepsilon,\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})})=\{f_{\beta^{\prime}}:\beta^{\prime}\in B_{2}^{p}(\beta,\varepsilon)\}
, it follows that any
\varepsilon/c
-packing of
B(f_{\beta},\varepsilon,\|\cdot\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})})\cap\mathcal{F}
corresponds to a
\varepsilon/c
-packing of
B_{2}^{p}(\beta,\varepsilon)\cap K
in the Euclidean 2-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Hence
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)=\mathcal{M}_{K}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)
.

5.1.1
Linear Functionals on the
\ell_{1}
-ball

Let
K=B_{1}^{p}(0,1):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\|x\|_{1}\leq 1\}
be the
\ell_{1}
-unit-ball. Let
\mathbb{S}_{1}^{p-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\|x\|_{1}=1\}
be the
\ell_{1}
-unit sphere, and similarly set
\mathbb{S}_{2}^{p-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\|x\|_{2}=1\}
. We again take
\mathcal{F}
to be the set of linear functionals over
K
. Note that the equivalence of local metric entropies between our function space and
K
in our linear functionals setting with isotropic and sub-Gaussian data also applies to the adaptive local metric entropy. Suppose
\beta^{\ast}
is
s
-sparse, i.e., has at most
s
non-zero components, and that
\beta^{\ast}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{p-1}
. Our goal will be to use the adaptive local entropy to derive a minimax rate upper bound for
\mathcal{F}
under this sparsity assumption. Our result will match the work of
Raskutti et al. 2011
.

We will need some tools from
Chandrasekaran et al. 2012
. The authors begin with an atomic set
\mathcal{A}
which is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. The authors then construct an atomic norm induced by this atomic set using the gauge function, assuming
\mathcal{A}
is centrally symmetric about the origin. For our example, we take
\mathcal{A}
to be the set of
1
-sparse vectors of unit norm, so that the convex hull
\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{A})
is precisely the
\ell_{1}
-unit-ball
B_{1}^{p}(0,1)
. We then define the atomic norm induced by
\mathcal{A}
at a vector
x
by
\|x\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\inf\{t>0:x\in t\cdot\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{A})\}
. Then clearly
\|x\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\|x\|_{1}
for
x\neq 0
, since the smallest
t>0
such that
x/t\in B_{1}^{p}(0,1)
is
\|x\|_{1}
, and
\|0\|_{\mathcal{A}}=\|0\|_{1}
since
0\in t\cdot B_{1}^{p}(0,1)
for arbitrarily small
t>0
. Thus, our atomic set and atomic norm correspond exactly to the
\ell_{1}
-ball and
\ell_{1}
-norm.

Next, recalling that the cone of a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
is defined by
\mathrm{cone}(S):=S:=\{\alpha\cdot s:s\in S,\alpha>0\}
, we define the tangent cone at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
as

T_{\mathcal{A}}(x)=\mathrm{cone}\{z-x:\|z\|_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\|x\|_{\mathcal{A}}\}=\mathrm{cone}\{z-x:\|z\|_{1}\leq\|x\|_{1}\}.

Let us compare the set
S_{1}:=\frac{B_{2}^{p}(\beta^{\ast},\varepsilon)\cap B_{1}^{p}(0,1)-\beta^{\ast}}{\varepsilon}
to
S_{2}:=T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap B_{2}^{p}(0,1)
, and show
S_{1}\subseteq S_{2}
. Suppose
x\in S_{1}
. Then
\varepsilon x+\beta^{\ast}\in B_{2}^{p}(\beta^{\ast},\varepsilon)\cap B_{1}^{p}(0,1)
. This implies that
\|\varepsilon x+\beta^{\ast}\|_{1}\leq 1=\|\beta^{\ast}\|_{1}
and
\|\varepsilon x\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon
. The latter implies
\|x\|_{2}\leq 1
, i.e.,
x\in B_{2}^{p}(0,1)
. Next, observe that

x=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon x+\beta^{\ast}-\beta^{\ast}\right)\in\mathrm{cone}\{z-\beta^{\ast}:\|z\|_{1}\leq\|\beta^{\ast}\|_{1}\}=T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast}),

where we took
z=\varepsilon x+\beta^{\ast}
. Thus, we conclude
x\in S_{2}
, so
S_{1}\subseteq S_{2}
.

For any set
T
, define its Gaussian width
w(T)
by

w(T):=\mathbb{E}_{g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{p})}\Big[\sup_{t\in T}\langle t,g\rangle\Big].

We will use numerous properties of the Gaussian width. Namely,
w
is invariant under translations, is positive homogeneous, i.e.,
w(\alpha T)=\alpha w(T)
for any
\alpha>0
, and if
T_{1}\subseteq T_{2}
, then
w(T_{1})\leq w(T_{2})
. Since the family of random variables
\{\langle t,g\rangle:t\in T\}
is an example of a zero-mean Gaussian process, we apply the Sudakov minoration lower bound
(
Wainwright 2019
, Theorem 5.30)
to upper bound the local metric entropy.

\displaystyle\log M_{K}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(\beta^{\ast},\varepsilon,c)

\displaystyle=\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon/c,B_{2}^{p}(\beta^{\ast},\varepsilon)\cap B_{1}^{p}(0,1))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4c^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\cdot\left[w(B_{2}^{p}(\beta^{\ast},\varepsilon)\cap B_{1}^{p}(0,1))\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=4c^{2}\cdot\left[w\left(\frac{B_{2}^{p}(\beta^{\ast},\varepsilon)\cap B_{1}^{p}(0,1)-\beta^{\ast}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4c^{2}\left[w(T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap B_{2}^{p}(0,1))\right]^{2}.

We now relate the Gaussian width of
T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap B_{2}^{p}(0,1)
to
T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap\mathbb{S}_{2}^{p}(0,1)
, and do so by using the statistical dimension of a convex cone
C
, as is defined in
Amelunxen et al. 2014
:

\delta(C)=\mathbb{E}_{g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{p})}\left[\left(\sup_{t\in C\cap B_{2}^{p}(0,1)}\langle t,g\rangle\right)^{2}\right].

Amelunxen et al. 2014
implies

\displaystyle\delta(T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast}))

\displaystyle\leq[w(T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap\mathbb{S}_{2}^{p-1}(0,1))]^{2}+1.

Additionally by
Chandrasekaran et al. 2012
,

\displaystyle[w(T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap\mathbb{S}_{2}^{p-1}(0,1))]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2s\log\left(\frac{p}{s}\right)+\frac{5}{4}s,

where we recall
\beta^{\ast}
is
s
-sparse. Using Jensen’s inequality and the fact that
T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})
is a convex cone along with the previous two results, we obtain

\displaystyle\log M_{K}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(\beta^{\ast},\varepsilon,c)

\displaystyle\leq 4c^{2}\left[w(T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap B_{2}^{p}(0,1))\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=4c^{2}\left(\mathbb{E}_{g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{p})}\left[\sup_{t\in T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap B_{2}^{p}(0,1)}\langle t,g\rangle\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4c^{2}\mathbb{E}_{g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{p})}\left[\left(\sup_{t\in T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap B_{2}^{p}(0,1)}\langle t,g\rangle\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=4c^{2}\delta(T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})).

\displaystyle\leq 4c^{2}[w(T_{\mathcal{A}}(\beta^{\ast})\cap\mathbb{S}_{2}^{p-1}(0,1))]^{2}+4c^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4c^{2}s\left(2\log\left(\frac{p}{s}\right)+\frac{5}{4}\right)+2c^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim s\log(p/s).

Hence,
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f_{\beta^{\ast}},\varepsilon,c)\lesssim s\log(p/s).

Now recalling the definition of
\varepsilon_{J}
and
J^{\ast}
from Theorem
4.4
, we will have for some constant
\kappa>0
that

\displaystyle n(\varepsilon_{J^{\ast}}/2)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{adloc}}(f_{\beta^{\ast}},\kappa\cdot\varepsilon_{J^{\ast}},c)\vee\log 2\lesssim s\log(p/s).

Then using Theorem
4.4
and rearranging the previous inequality, we conclude

\mathbb{E}_{\vec{X},\vec{Y}}\|f_{\beta^{\ast}}-f^{\ast}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon_{J^{\ast}}^{2}\lesssim\frac{s\log(p/s)}{n},

where
f^{\ddagger}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})
is the output of our algorithm. This result matches that of
Raskutti et al. 2011
, although notably our estimator is different, our bound is in expectation rather than in high probability and we use data that are isotropic and sub-Gaussian.

5.1.2
Linear Functionals over Ellipsoids

Define the matrix
K_{c}
to be the
p\times p
diagonal matrix with
i
th entry
a_{i}^{-1}
, where
0<a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\dots\leq a_{p}
, where
a_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be a bounded constant independent of the sample size. Let
\Theta=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\theta^{T}K_{c}\theta\leq 1\}
. Our assumption on
a_{p}
implies that the set
\Theta
has a bounded diameter. We draw
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
isotropic and sub-Gaussian with variance proxy
\tau^{2}>1
, and for
1\leq i\leq n
we observe
Y_{i}=X_{i}^{T}\theta^{\ast}+w_{i}
where
w_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
and
\theta^{\ast}\in\Theta
is the true value of the parameter. Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
be the matrix with
X_{i}
in the
i
th-row. Our function class is therefore
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{\theta}(x)=x^{T}\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}
. Note that
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F})=\mathrm{diam}(\Theta)=2\sqrt{a_{p}}
. As we showed in Section
5.1
, since the
X_{i}
are isotropic,
\|f_{\theta_{1}}-f_{\theta_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}=\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|_{2}^{2}
.

Let us now compute the minimax rate for estimating
\theta^{\ast}
, or equivalently,
f_{\theta^{\ast}}
. We will follow
Neykov 2022
which has a very similar proof.

Consider the Kolmogorov width
W_{k}(\Theta)=\min_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\|P\theta-\theta\|,
for
\mathcal{P}_{k}
the set of linear projections into
k
-dimensional subspaces. One can check that
W_{k}(\Theta)=\sqrt{a_{p-k}}
where we take
a_{0}=0
.

If
a_{p}>1/n
, then let
k\in[p]
be such that
a_{p-k}\leq(k+1)/n
and
a_{p-k+1}>k/n
. This will imply
W_{k}(\Theta)^{2}\leq(k+1)/n
. We claim that
\varepsilon^{\ast}\asymp\sqrt{k/n}
, where
\varepsilon^{\ast}
is as defined in (
3.1
). As a result, the minimax rate will be
k/n\wedge a_{p}
if
a_{p}>1/n
, and
a_{p}
if
a_{p}\leq 1/n
, all up to constants.

We start with the upper bound. In the case
a_{p}\leq 1/n
, note that the minimax rate is trivially bounded by the squared diameter
4a_{p}
. Suppose instead
a_{p}>1/n.
Now take
\varepsilon^{2}\geq Ck/n
. Pick any
f_{\theta}\in\mathcal{F}
and let
M_{f_{\theta}}
be an
\varepsilon/c
-packing of
\mathcal{F}\cap B(f_{\theta},\varepsilon)
. Then this corresponds to some
\varepsilon/c
-packing
M_{\theta}
of the set
\Theta\cap B_{2}^{p}(\theta,\varepsilon)
, where we are using the
p
-dimensional Euclidean
\ell_{2}
-norm in the ball centered at
\theta
. Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
solve the outer minimization problem in the Kolmogorov width definition for
\theta
.

Then for any
g_{\theta_{1}},g_{\theta_{2}}\in M_{f_{\theta}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{c}

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{\theta_{1}}-g_{\theta_{2}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}=\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\theta_{1}-P\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}+P\theta_{2}\|_{2}+\|P\theta_{1}-P\theta_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2W_{k}(\Theta)+\|P\theta_{1}-P\theta_{2}\|_{2}.

Note that
\varepsilon^{2}\geq Ck/n>\frac{C(k+1)}{2n}\geq\frac{C}{2}\cdot W_{k}(\Theta)^{2},
which means
2W_{k}(\Theta)\leq\sqrt{\frac{8}{C}}\cdot\varepsilon
so by taking
C
sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle\|P\theta_{1}-P\theta_{2}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{c}-2W_{k}(\Theta)>\frac{\varepsilon}{c}-\sqrt{\frac{8}{C}}\cdot\varepsilon=\frac{\varepsilon}{C^{\prime}}

for some constant
C^{\prime}
. This means we have formed an
\frac{\varepsilon}{C^{\prime}}
-packing set
\{P\theta:\theta\in M_{\theta}\}
of the
k
-dimensional set
P\Theta
. On the other hand, a
k
-dimensional set contained in a
k
-dimensional sphere has a packing set with log cardinality bounded by
kC^{\prime\prime}
for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}
. Since
\|P\theta_{1}-P\theta_{2}\|_{2}>0
for each
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in M_{\theta}
, we have
|M_{\theta}|=|\{P\theta:\theta\in M_{\theta}\}|
. Hence the packing set
M_{\theta}
has log cardinality bounded by
kC^{\prime\prime}
, as does
M_{f_{\theta}}
by the equivalence of the Euclidean norm and
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
norm.

Hence we have
n\varepsilon^{2}\geq kC
while
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\leq kC^{\prime\prime}
, so taking
C
large enough, we have
n\varepsilon^{2}>\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c).
But since
n\varepsilon^{2}
is increasing in
\varepsilon
while
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)
is non-increasing in
\varepsilon
, we must have
\varepsilon^{\ast}\leq\varepsilon
. Since this holds for all
\varepsilon\geq\sqrt{Ck/n}
, it follows that
\varepsilon^{\ast}\leq\sqrt{Ck/n}
. The minimax rate will also be bounded by the diameter, so the minimax rate is therefore upper-bounded up to constants by
\sqrt{k/n}\wedge 4a_{p}
when
a_{p}>1/n
.

Now we prove the lower bound. We first handle the
a_{p}>1/n
case. Consider the subset
\Theta^{\prime}=\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\sum_{i=p-k+1}\theta_{i}^{2}/a_{i}\leq 1,\theta_{1}=\dots=\theta_{p-k}=0\}\subseteq\Theta
, i.e., we set the first
p-k
coordinates of
\theta\in\Theta
to
0
. But as
a_{p-k+1}>k/n
and
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\dots\leq a_{p}
, we conclude that
\Theta^{\prime}
and therefore
\Theta
contains a
k
-dimensional ball of radius
\sqrt{k/n}
. Hence
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)=\log\mathcal{M}_{\Theta}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\geq Ck
for some constant
C
. Now take
\varepsilon=C^{\prime}\sqrt{k/n}
for some sufficiently small constant
C^{\prime}
such that
n\varepsilon^{2}=(C^{\prime})^{2}k\leq Ck\leq\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)
. Since
\varepsilon^{\ast}
is the supremum of all such
\varepsilon
satisfying
n\varepsilon^{2}\leq\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)
, we conclude
\varepsilon^{\ast}\geq\varepsilon\gtrsim\sqrt{k/n}
. Combined with the upper bound, we conclude in the case where
a_{p}>1/n
that
{\varepsilon^{\ast}}^{2}\asymp k/n
and thus the minimax rate is
k/n\wedge 4a_{p}
up to constants.

Lastly, we consider the lower bound for when
a_{p}\leq n^{-1}
, which implies
a_{i}\leq n^{-1}
for all
i\in[p]
. Now take
\varepsilon=\frac{2\sqrt{a_{p}}}{C\sqrt{n}}
for some constant
C
. For sufficiently large
C
, we have
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)>1>\frac{4a_{p}}{C^{2}}=n\cdot\varepsilon^{2}.
Again, by the definition of
\varepsilon^{\ast}
as a supremum, we have
\varepsilon^{\ast}\geq\varepsilon=\frac{2\sqrt{a_{p}}}{C\sqrt{n}}
, so
\varepsilon^{\ast}\gtrsim\sqrt{a_{p}}
, noting that
\sqrt{a_{p}}
is the diameter up to constants. So the minimax rate is lower bounded by
a_{p}
up to constants, and we already know it is upper bounded by it in this case. This completes our proof that the minimax rate is
k/n\wedge 4a_{p}
if
a_{p}>1/n
, and
a_{p}
if
a_{p}\leq 1/n
, where
k\in[p]
is such that
a_{p-k}\leq(k+1)/n
and
a_{p-k+1}>k/n
.

Now, let us compare our result to that of
Pathak et al. 2023
. Define

\gamma_{n}=\sup_{\Omega\succ 0}\left\{\mathbb{E}\;\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Omega^{-1}+n^{-1}X^{T}X\right)^{-1}:\mathrm{Tr}(K_{c}^{-1/2}\Omega K_{c}^{1/2})\leq n\right\},

where we recall
K_{c}
is the diagonal matrix with
(i,i)
-entry
a_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq p
. The authors then conclude the minimax rate is of order
\gamma_{n}/n
.

Thus, if
a_{p}\leq n^{-1}
, we equate the two results and conclude that
a_{p}\asymp\tfrac{\gamma_{n}}{n}
, or if
a_{p}>n^{-1}
we conclude
\tfrac{k}{n}\wedge a_{p}\asymp\tfrac{\gamma_{n}}{n}
. Although calculating
\gamma_{n}
from its definition may be challenging, our approach with local metric entropies is able to indirectly recover it.

5.2
Lipschitz Classes

Given any function
f\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
, we extend
f
to
(1,\infty)
by setting
f(x)=f(1)
for
x>1
. For any such function
f
and real number
h>0
, define the function
\Phi_{f,h}\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by
\Phi_{f,h}(x)=f(x+h)-f(x)
. Now for each
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\gamma>0
, define

\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\gamma}=\{f\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}:\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\gamma,\|\Phi_{f,h}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\gamma h^{\alpha}\text{ for all }h>0\}.

Then this is a convex function class with bounded diameter, i.e.,
\gamma\leq\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\gamma})\leq 2\gamma
.

Then we have
\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\gamma})\asymp\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}
by
Yang and Barron 1999
and using the inequality

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon/c,\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\gamma})\geq\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\gamma}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\geq\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon/c,\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\gamma})-\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\gamma}),

we conclude
\log M_{\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\asymp\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}
. The relationship
n\varepsilon^{2}\asymp\varepsilon^{-1/\alpha}
occurs when
\varepsilon\asymp n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+1}}
, so we conclude the minimax-rate is determined by
n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+1}}\wedge\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha,\gamma})\asymp n^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\alpha+1}}\wedge\gamma.
This matches the rate given in
Stone 1982
.

5.3
Monotone functions in general dimensions

We now use results from
Gao and Wellner 2007
to derive the known minimax rate for monotone functions in a multivariate setting. We let
\mathcal{F}_{p}=\{f\colon[0,1]^{p}\to[0,1],f\text{ non-decreasing in each variable}\}
, where we say
f\colon[0,1]^{p}\to[0,1]
is non-decreasing if

f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{p})\leq f(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},x_{i}+y_{i},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{p})

for any
(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\in[0,1]^{p}
,
y_{i}>0
, and
1\leq i\leq p
. Using the least squares estimator,
Han et al. 2019
achieve an error rate of
n^{-1/p}\log^{4}n
for
p\geq 3
, which is the minimax rate up to logarithm factors. In fact, under some mild conditions
Deng and Zhang 2020
achieve
n^{-1/p}
without the logarithmic factors using a block estimator. We now explain how our algorithm achieves the same rate.

We restate a key theorem from
Gao and Wellner 2007
:

Lemma 5.1

(Theorem 1.1 of
Gao and Wellner 2007
)
.

For
p>2
, we have

\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{p})\asymp\varepsilon^{-2(p-1)}.

If
p=2
, then

\varepsilon^{-2}\lesssim\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{p})\lesssim\varepsilon^{-2}(\log 1/\varepsilon)^{2}.

Assume
p>2
at first. Recall once more by
Yang and Barron 1999
that

\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon/c,\mathcal{F}_{p})\geq\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{p}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\geq\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon/c,\mathcal{F}_{p})-\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{p}).

Therefore
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{p}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\asymp\varepsilon^{-2(p-1)}
. Next, the relationship
n\varepsilon^{2}\asymp\varepsilon^{-2(p-1)}
holds when
\varepsilon\asymp n^{-1/(2p)}
. Since the diameter of
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is 1, we conclude the minimax rate for
p>2
is given by
n^{-1/p}\wedge 1
up to constants. As discussed earlier, this matches the result of
Han et al. 2019
and
Deng and Zhang 2020
.

Let us next consider the
p=2
case. Since for any
\varepsilon>0
satisfying
n\varepsilon^{2}\leq\log M_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)
we have
n\varepsilon^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-2}(\log 1/\varepsilon)^{2}
, it follows that
\varepsilon^{\ast}
as defined in Theorem
3.15
satisfies
n{\varepsilon^{\ast}}^{4}\lesssim(\log 1/\varepsilon^{\ast})^{2}.
On the other hand, since
\log M_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-2}
, we have
n{\varepsilon^{\ast}}^{2}\gtrsim{\varepsilon^{\ast}}^{-2}
, i.e.,
\varepsilon^{\ast}\gtrsim n^{-1/4}
. Hence

n^{-1/4}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\ast}\lesssim n^{-1/4}\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon^{\ast})}.

Reusing
n^{-1/4}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\ast}
note that
\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon^{\ast})}\lesssim\sqrt{\log(n^{1/4})}\asymp\sqrt{\log n}.
Hence
n^{-1/4}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\ast}\lesssim n^{-1/4}\sqrt{\log(n)}.
The minimax rate therefore satisfies

n^{-1/2}\wedge 1\lesssim{\varepsilon^{\ast}}^{2}\wedge 1\lesssim(n^{-1/2}\log n)\wedge 1.

For
p=1
, we know
\log\mathcal{M}(\varepsilon,\mathcal{F}_{1})\asymp\varepsilon^{-1}
by
Van Der Vaart and Wellner 1996
. Reusing the result from
Yang and Barron 1999
we conclude
\log\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}_{2}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\varepsilon,c)\asymp\varepsilon^{-1}
, and this rate matches
n\varepsilon^{2}
when
\varepsilon\asymp n^{-1/3}
. The minimax rate is therefore
n^{-2/3}\wedge 1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

We use
[
Wainwright 2019
, (15.52)]
, the decoupling property of Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
, and the decomposition of the joint distribution of
(X_{i},Y_{i})
.
Let
\mathbb{P}_{Y_{i}|X_{i}}^{f_{j}}
be the distribution of
(Y_{i}|X_{i},J=j)
, which is a normal distribution with mean
f_{j}(X_{i})
and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Denote by
\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{f_{j}}
the distribution of
(Y_{i},X_{i}|J=j)
and
\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}
that of
X_{i}
, so that
\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{f_{j}}=\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}\times\mathbb{P}_{Y_{i}|X_{i}}^{f_{j}}
. Note that the
X_{i}
does not depend on
f_{j}
. Similarly, for any function
h\in\mathcal{F}
, if we set
Y_{i}=h(X_{i})+\xi_{i}
instead, we denote the analogous distributions by
\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{h}=\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}\times\mathbb{P}_{Y_{i}|X_{i}}^{h}
.
Then we obtain

\displaystyle I(\vec{X},\vec{Y};J)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\prod_{i}\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{f_{j}}\parallel\prod_{i}\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j}\sum_{i}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{f_{j}}\parallel\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}_{f_{j}}\left[\log\left(\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{f_{j}}/\mathbb{P}_{(X_{i},Y_{i})}^{h}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}_{f_{j}}\left[\log\left(\frac{\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}\mathbb{P}_{(Y_{i}|X_{i})}^{f_{j}}}{\mathbb{P}_{X_{i}}\mathbb{P}_{(Y_{i}|X_{i})}^{h}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}\mathbb{E}_{Y_{i}|(X_{i},f_{j})}\left[\log\left(\mathbb{P}_{(Y_{i}|X_{i})}^{f_{j}}/\mathbb{P}_{(Y_{i}|X_{i})}^{h}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(\mathcal{N}(f_{j}(X_{i}),\sigma^{2})\parallel\mathcal{N}(h(X_{i}),\sigma^{2})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}\left[\frac{|f_{j}(X_{i})-h(X_{i})|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}m}\sum_{j}\sum_{i}\|f_{j}-h\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}m}\sum_{j}\|f_{j}-h\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}\max_{j}\|f_{j}-h\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}.

∎

Remark B.1

(Breaking Ties in Algorithm)
.

By Theorem A.1 in
Györfi 2002
, the set of continuous functions is dense in
\mathcal{F}
. Hence we can construct all of our packing sets using continuous functions only. That is, if
\mathcal{F}_{c}
denote the set of all continuous functions in
\mathcal{F}
, then we construct maximal packing sets
M_{k}
of sets
B(\cdot,d/2^{k-1})\cap\mathcal{F}_{c}
prior to pruning. This
M_{k}
clearly has cardinality that is smaller than or equal to the
M_{k}
constructed with
\mathcal{F}
instead since
\mathcal{F}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
. In addition, since the set of all continuous functions is dense in
\mathcal{F}
, for any
g\in\mathcal{F}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
h\in\mathcal{F}_{c}
such that
\|g-h\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\varepsilon
. Pick the point
f\in M_{k}
which is
d/2^{k-1}
close to
h
and conclude that
\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\varepsilon+d/2^{k-1}
. Let
\varepsilon
go to zero, and we conclude that any point in
\mathcal{F}
can be approximated by a point in
\mathcal{F}_{c}
within distance
d/2^{k-1}
. Now suppose that the domain of the functions is
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Take the set
\mathbb{Q}^{p}
which is dense in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. This is a well-ordered set since there is a bijection between
\mathbb{Q}^{p}
and
\mathbb{N}
. Hence the functions in
M_{k}
can be restricted to
\mathbb{Q}^{p}
and then ordered lexicographically. There will not be any ties, since the functions are separated and continuous (hence the functions cannot be the same on
\mathbb{Q}^{p}
otherwise they are the same on
\mathbb{R}^{p}
which is a contradiction) so we can take the smallest function in the lexicographic ordering.

Proof of Proposition

A uniform sup-norm bound trivially implies (

-sub-Gaussian case. Also note that by Stirling’s approximation
[

, we have

\displaystyle p^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p!\exp(p)\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{12p+1}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi p}}=\frac{p!\cdot\exp\left(p-\frac{1}{12p+1}-\frac{1}{2}\log p\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{L_{2p}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(L\sqrt{2p}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\right)^{2p}

\displaystyle=(2L^{2})^{p}p^{p}\left(\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\right)^{p-2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\cdot

\displaystyle\leq(2L^{2})^{p}d^{p-2}p^{p}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{L}^{p-1}p!\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}}{2},

where

.

For
\theta>0

(B.2)

Similarly, still assuming
\theta>0
but this time multiplying by
-\theta

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg(\sum_{i\in[n]}(Z_{i}^{2}-\mathbb{E}Z^{2}\bigg)\leq-t)

\displaystyle=1+(2\theta L)^{2}\mathbb{E}Z^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\theta L)^{i}}{2L}

\displaystyle=2(f(\vec{X})+\eta(\vec{X})+\xi)^{T}(g(\vec{X})-f(\vec{X}))+\|f(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}-\|g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2f(\vec{X})^{T}(g(\vec{X})-f(\vec{X}))+2\eta^{T}(g(\vec{X})-f(\vec{X}))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad 2\xi(\vec{X})^{T}(g(\vec{X})-f(\vec{X}))+\|f(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}-\|g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta(\vec{X})^{T}(g(\vec{X})-f(\vec{X}))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+2\xi^{T}(g(\vec{X})-f(\vec{X})).

Observe that conditional on
\vec{X}
, by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, this quantity is normal with mean at most

-\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}+2\|\eta(\vec{X})\|_{2}\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}

and variance
4n\sigma^{2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}
.

We now derive estimates on
\|\eta(\vec{X})\|_{2}
and
\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}
that hold with high probability. Suppose
n\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}<\delta^{2}
. From Lemma
3.7
, this condition along with our assumption
n\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\geq C^{2}\delta^{2}
implies that with probability at least
1-\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{20L}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4L(C^{2}+1)}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle n\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}\geq(C^{2}-1)\delta^{2},\quad n\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}\leq 2\delta^{2}.

(B.5)

Let
E
denote the event that both inequalities of (
B.5
) hold, so

\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(E^{c})\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{20L}\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4L(C^{2}+1)}\right).

The first inequality in (
B.5
) implies

\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}=\sqrt{n}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}\geq\delta\sqrt{C^{2}-1}

in which case

\delta\leq\frac{\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}.

The other inequality simply states that
\|\eta(\vec{X})\|_{2}\leq\delta\sqrt{2}
. Thus, the inequalities of (
B.5
) imply

\|\eta(\vec{X})\|_{2}\leq\delta\sqrt{2}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}\cdot\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}.

Thus, conditional on
\vec{X}
, the event
E
implies
\|\vec{Y}-f(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}-\|\vec{Y}-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}
is normal with mean bounded from above by

\displaystyle-\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}+2\|\eta(\vec{X})\|_{2}\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(-1+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}\right)\cdot\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(-1+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}\right)}_{<0}\cdot n\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}

and variance
4n\sigma^{2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}
. Note that
\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}<1
because
C>3
.

Using a bound from
Van Der Vaart and Wellner 1996
, we note that for any
\rho<0<\tau
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}(\rho,\tau^{2})\geq 0)\leq\exp(-\rho^{2}/(2\tau^{2})).

So conditional on
\vec{X}
and the event
E
,
\psi=1
holds with probability bounded by

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(-\frac{\big(-1+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}\big)^{2}n^{2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{4}}{8n\sigma^{2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\frac{\big(-1+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}\big)^{2}n\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{\left(-1+\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}\right)^{2}(C^{2}-1)\delta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\frac{\left(\sqrt{C^{2}-1}-2\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}\delta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\bigg).

Since
\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=1|\vec{X},E)
has an upper bound not depending on
\vec{X}
, it follows (by taking an expectation) that

\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=1|E)\leq\exp\bigg(-\frac{\left(\sqrt{C^{2}-1}-2\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}\delta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\bigg).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=1)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=1|E)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(E)+\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=1|E^{c})\cdot\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(E^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=1|E)+\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(E^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\sqrt{C^{2}-1}-2\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}\delta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{20L}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4L(C^{2}+1)}\right).

If we instead assumed
n\|g-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}<\delta^{2}
, a completely symmetric argument would result in a identical bound for
\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=0)
. Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sup_{\bar{f}:n\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}<\delta^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=1)\vee\sup_{\bar{f}:n\|g-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}<\delta^{2}}\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(\psi=0)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\sqrt{C^{2}-1}-2\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}\delta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{20L}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4L(C^{2}+1)}\right).

We define

\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)=\min\left\{\frac{\left(\sqrt{C^{2}-1}-2\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}},\frac{1}{20L},\frac{1}{4L(C^{2}+1)}\right\},

and replace the preceding upper bound by
3\exp\left(-\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot\delta^{2}\right).

∎

Proof of Lemma
3.9
.

For some
i\in[M]
, possibly distinct from
i^{\ast}
, we have
\|f_{i}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\delta
which means
n\|f_{i}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}\leq n\delta^{2}
. By the triangle inequality, we observe

\displaystyle\|f_{i^{\ast}}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{i^{\ast}}-f_{i}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}+\|f_{i}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{i^{\ast}}-f_{i}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}+\delta.

Note that
\|\vec{Y}-f_{i}(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\|\vec{Y}-f_{i^{\ast}}(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}
since
f_{i^{\ast}}
was defined to be the least squares estimator.

Putting these facts together,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\big(\|f_{i^{\ast}}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>(C+1)\delta\big)\leq\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\left(\|f_{i^{\ast}}-f_{i}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>C\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\Big(\exists j:\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>C\delta\text{ and }

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\|\vec{Y}-f_{i}(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\|\vec{Y}-f_{j}(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\Big(\exists j:n\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}>C^{2}n\delta^{2}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\|\vec{Y}-f_{i}(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\|\vec{Y}-f_{j}(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}\Big(n\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}>C^{2}n\delta^{2}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\|\vec{Y}-f_{i}(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\geq\|\vec{Y}-f_{j}(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 3M\exp\big(-\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot n\delta^{2}\big).

(B.6)

where we used Lemma
3.8
in (
B.6
) with
\sqrt{n}\delta
in place of
\delta
.
∎

Proof of Remark
3.11
.

In the proof of Lemma
3.8
, we first showed that

\displaystyle\|\vec{Y}-f(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}-\|\vec{Y}-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}+2\eta(\vec{X})^{T}(g(\vec{X})-f(\vec{X}))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+2\xi^{T}(g(\vec{X})-f(\vec{X})).

Conditional on
\vec{X}
, this quantity is sub-Gaussian with mean bounded by

-\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}+2\|\eta(\vec{X})\|_{2}\|f(\vec{X})-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}.

Moreover, the variance proxy can be shown to be
4n\sigma^{2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}
using the definition of a sub-Gaussian random variable. The proof of Lemma
3.8
then defines the event
E
as when the inequalities in (
B.5
) hold, and
E
satisfies

\mathbb{P}_{\bar{f}}(E^{c})\leq\exp\left(-\tfrac{\delta^{2}}{20L}\right)+\exp\left(-\tfrac{\delta^{2}}{4L(C^{2}+1)}\right).

Noting that Lemma
3.7
still holds with sub-Gaussian noise, we obtain as in Lemma
3.8
that conditional on
\vec{X}
, the event
E
implies
\|\vec{Y}-f(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}-\|\vec{Y}-g(\vec{X})\|_{2}^{2}
is sub-Gaussian with mean bounded above by the negative quantity

\left(-1+\tfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{C^{2}-1}}\right)\cdot n\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}

and variance proxy
4n\sigma^{2}\|f-g\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{n})}^{2}
. A sub-Gaussian random variable
Z
with mean
\rho
and variance proxy
\tau^{2}
can be shown to satisfy
P(Z>t+\rho)\leq\exp(-t^{2}/2\tau^{2})
for any
t>0

[
Boucheron et al. 2013
, Section 2.3]
, and if
\rho<0
, we can take
t=-\rho
and obtain a bound of
P(Z>0)\leq\exp(-\rho^{2}/2\tau^{2})
. Applying this result, as in the previous lemma, we conclude that conditional on
\vec{X}
and the event
E
,
\psi=1
holds with probability bounded by

\exp\left(-\tfrac{\left(\sqrt{C^{2}-1}-2\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}\delta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\right).

The rest of the proof follows identically to Lemma
3.8
. Then we can use this generalized Lemma to obtain Lemmas
3.9
and
3.10
without any additional work, since other than using the bounds in Lemma
3.8
, normality is not required.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.13
.

If
J^{\ast}=1
, then
\varepsilon_{J^{\ast}}\asymp d
and the risk bound trivially holds since
d
is the
L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})
diameter. Suppose henceforth
J^{\ast}>1
.

Define the event
A_{j}=\{\|\Upsilon_{j}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>\tfrac{d}{2^{j-1}}\}
. Observe that
\mathbb{P}(A_{1})=0
by definition of the diameter, and basic set inclusion properties imply
\mathbb{P}(A_{J})\leq\mathbb{P}(A_{1})+\sum_{j=2}^{J}\mathbb{P}(A_{j}\cap A_{j-1}^{c})
. Let use now bound the probability of each of these intersections.

Part 1:
Bounding
\mathbb{P}(A_{j}\cap A_{j-1}^{c})
for
j\geq 2
.

We start with
j\geq 3
. Set
\delta=\tfrac{d}{2^{j-1}(C+1)}
. We claim the following holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{j}\cap A_{j-1}^{c})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\|\bar{f}-\Upsilon_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>\tfrac{d}{2^{j-1}},\|\bar{f}-\Upsilon_{j-1}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{g\in\mathcal{L}(j-1)\cap B(\bar{f},\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}})}\mathbb{P}(\|\bar{f}-\Upsilon_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>\tfrac{d}{2^{j-1}},\Upsilon_{j-1}=g)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{L}(j-1)\cap B(\bar{f},\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}})}\mathbb{P}(\|\bar{f}-f_{i^{\ast}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>(C+1)\delta,\Upsilon_{j-1}=g)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{g\in\mathcal{L}(j-1)\cap B(\bar{f},\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}})}\mathbb{P}(\|\bar{f}-f_{i^{\ast}}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>(C+1)\delta).

(B.7)

The second line applies a union bound: if

\|\bar{f}-\Upsilon_{j-1}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}\leq\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}}
, then by definition of
\mathcal{L}(j-1)
as the
(j-1)
th level of our tree,
\Upsilon_{j-1}=g
where
g\in\mathcal{L}(j-1)\cap B(\bar{f},\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}})
. For the third line, we substitute the definition of
\delta
and then recall
\Upsilon_{j}=f_{i^{\ast}}
where

and the
\mathcal{O}(g)=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{M}\}
. We then drop the intersection with
\Upsilon_{j-1}=g
for the final inequality.

Now, using the definition of
\mathcal{O}(g)
and Lemma
3.1
, note that
f_{1},\dots,f_{M}
form a
\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}c}=\tfrac{d}{2^{j-1}(C+1)}=\delta
-covering of the set
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=B(g,\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}})\cap\mathcal{F}
with cardinality of this set is bounded by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}},2c)
. Thus, we can apply Lemma
3.9
to (
B.7
), noting requiring

implies
C>3
as required. Moreover, the cardinality of
\mathcal{L}(j-1)\cap B(\bar{f},\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}})
is also bounded by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}},2c)
by Lemma
3.2
. Thus, continuing from (
B.7
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{j}\cap A_{j-1}^{c})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{g\in\mathcal{L}(j-1)\cap B(\bar{f},\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}})}3\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}},2c)\exp\left(-\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot n\delta^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{d}{2^{j-2}},2c)\right]^{2}\exp\left(-\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot n\delta^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=3\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{j}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},2c)\right]^{2}\exp\left(-n\varepsilon_{j}^{2}\right).

The last step used our definition of
\varepsilon_{j}
,
\delta
, and
C
.

Next, for
j=2
, note that
\mathbb{P}(A_{2}\cap A_{1}^{c})=\mathbb{P}(A_{2})
since
\mathbb{P}(A_{1})=0
. Set
\delta=\tfrac{d}{2(C+1)}=\tfrac{d}{c}
. Well note that
\Upsilon_{2}
is chosen from a maximal
\delta=\tfrac{d}{c}
-packing (hence covering) of
B(f_{R},d)\cap\mathcal{F}=B(\bar{f},d)\cap\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}
. Such a packing has cardinality bounded by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(d,c)\leq\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(d,2c)
, using Lemma
3.12
. Again applying Lemma
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\|\Upsilon_{j}-\bar{f}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>\delta(C+1))

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(d,2c)\exp(-\Lambda(C,\sigma,L)\cdot n\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=3\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},2c)\exp(-n\varepsilon_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 3\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{2}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},2c)\right]^{2}\exp(-n\varepsilon_{2}^{2}).

Part 2:
Deducing a bound on
\mathbb{P}(A_{J})
for any
1\leq J\leq J^{\ast}
.

We now sum over our bounds using the
\mathbb{P}(A_{J})\leq\sum_{j=2}^{J}\mathbb{P}(A_{j}\cap A_{j-1}^{c})
fact, noting that by Lemma
3.12
,

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{j}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},2c)\leq\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{J}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},2c).

Taking
a_{J}=\exp(-n\varepsilon_{J}^{2})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{J})

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{j=2}^{J}\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{j}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},2c)\right]^{2}\exp(-n\varepsilon_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 3\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{J}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},2c)\right]^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{J}\exp(-n\varepsilon_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 3\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\operatorname{loc}}(\tfrac{c\varepsilon_{J}}{\sqrt{\Lambda(\tfrac{c}{2}-1,\sigma,L)}},2c)\right]^{2}\cdot\frac{a_{J}}{1-a_{J}}.

Since

For the following parts, let

Bounding

.

. We can show

\displaystyle\kappa\varepsilon_{J}

\displaystyle<\|\bar{f}-f^{\ast}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}

Part 4:
Bounding
\mathbb{P}(\|\bar{f}-f^{\ast\ast}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}>\kappa^{\prime}x)

The last few lines used the definition of the event

Part 5:
Bounding
\mathbb{E}_{\vec{X},\vec{Y}}\|\bar{f}-f^{\ast\ast}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}.

We integrate the bound from Part 4:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\vec{X},\vec{Y}}\|\bar{f}-f^{\ast\ast}(\vec{X},\vec{Y})\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\|\bar{f}-f^{\ast\ast}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{P}_{X})}^{2}>x)\mathrm{d}x

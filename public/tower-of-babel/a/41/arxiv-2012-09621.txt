Weak coupling limit for the ground state energy of the 2D Fermi polaron
David Mitrouskas

Abstract
We analyze the ground state energy for N N fermions in a two-dimensional box interacting with an impurity particle via two-body point interactions. We allow for mass ratios M > 1.225 M>1.225 between the impurity mass and the mass of a fermion and consider arbitrarily large box sizes while keeping the Fermi energy fixed. Our main result shows that the ground state energy in the limit of weak coupling is given by the polaron energy. The polaron energy is an energy estimate based on trial states up to first order in particle-hole expansion, which was proposed by Chevy in the physics literature. For the proof we apply a Birman–Schwinger principle that was recently obtained by Griesemer and Linden. One main new ingredient is a suitable localization of the polaron energy.



1
Introduction and main result

It is a universal challenge in quamtum theory to understand the physics of few particles immersed into a complex enviroment in terms of properties of quasi-particles. A famous example of a quasi-particle is the Fröhlich polaron developed in a series of influential works by Landau, Pekar and Fröhlich
[
16
,
17
,
27
,
11
]
. They suggested to describe the motion of an electron through a polarizable crystal in terms of a polaron, that is, a quasi-particle composed of an electron dressed by a local deformation of the crystal. This picture leads to a drastic simplification as the complex many-body problem is replaced by a self-consistent non-linear one-body model, which is much more accessible to computations. While the Fröhlich polaron is certainly the most prominent example for a polaron model, the concept of quasi-particles and polarons has turned out very useful far beyond its original application in the theory of electrons moving through crystals. For instance the experimental realization of impurities immersed into ultracold atomic gases during the last two decades has triggered the invention and analysis of many new models such as the Fermi polaron
[
5
]
, the Bose polaron
[
15
]
and the angulon
[
31
]
. In the present work we are interested in the two-dimensional Fermi polaron which is a popular model in theoretical physics to describe strongly population imbalanced Fermi gases at low temperature confined to the two-dimensional plane. In case of extreme imbalance there is only a single particle interacting with a gas of non-interacting fermions via a two-body short range interaction.

We consider
N
identical fermions and an additional distinguished particle, called impurity particle, in a two-dimensional box
\Omega=[-L/2,L/2]^{2}
with periodic boundary conditions. The underlying Hilbert space is
L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes\mathscr{H}_{N}
where
\mathscr{H}_{N}=\bigwedge^{N}L^{2}(\Omega)
denotes the space of anti-symmetric
N
-particle wave functions. For a short-range potential, the Pauli principle suppresses the interaction among the fermions which is therefore neglected. The Hamiltonian of the system is formally described by

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{M}\Delta_{y}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Delta_{x_{i}}-g\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta(x_{i}-y),

(1.1)

where

represents the coordinate of the impurity,
\Delta
is the Laplace operator and

denotes the ratio between the mass of the impurity particle and the mass of a fermion. The interaction is given by a Dirac-delta-potantial

with coupling strength

. This model is known as the 2D Fermi polaron and has been analyzed to a great extent in the physics literature, see e.g.
[
5
,
7
,
28
,
6
,
29
,
4
,
25
,
30
,
26
]
. The Fermi polaron is of interest, among other reasons, because of the occurrence of a pairing mechanism somewhat analogous to the famous BCS–BEC crossover. In two space dimensions, one expects a transition of the ground state as a function of the coupling strength. While for weak coupling, the impurity particle is expected to be surrounded by a cloud of particle-hole excitations, in the strong coupling regime it is predicted that the impurity is closely bound by a single fermion forming a molecular state.

Here we provide a rigorous analysis of the ground state energy in the limit of weak coupling by which we confirm its asymptotic form conjectured in the physics literature. From the mathematical point of view, our work is a continuation of recent articles by Griesemer and Linden
[
19
,
13
,
14
]
in which they provide a definition of the self-adjoint Hamiltonian

1.1
The model

A possible approach to define the Fermi polaron is to start with a regularized version of the point interaction and then remove the regularization in a suitable sense

\displaystyle(a_{k}\Psi)^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n+1}\int_{\Omega}\textnormal{d}x_{n+1}\overline{\varphi_{k}(x_{n+1})}\Psi^{(n+1)}(x_{1},...,x_{n+1}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle(a_{k}^{*}\Psi)^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}\varphi_{k}(x_{j})\Psi^{(n-1)}(x_{1},...,x_{j-1},x_{j+1},...,x_{n})

(1.3)

In the physics literature the polaron energy is considered to be a good approximation in the weak coupling limit
E_{B}\nearrow 0
as well as in the large density limit

[

The two classes of trial states were investigated extensively in the physics literature leading to indications for the anticipated difference between the shape of the ground state in the weak and strong coupling limits (see, e.g., the literature quoted in the previous section). For this reason the Fermi polaron is also discussed in the context of the BCS–BEC crossover. Most results in the physics literature, however, are based on variational estimates using suitable classes of trial states. We remark that this can only justify upper bounds for the ground state energy, whereas here we provide a corresponding lower bound.

The Fermi polaron has been studied also in three dimensions. The problem of defining a semi bounded self-adjoint Hamiltonian in this case was solved in

Quantum models with

1.2
Main result

We are now ready to state our main result which provides an asymptotic estimate for the ground state energy
\min\sigma(H)
of the 2D Fermi polaron.

Theorem 1.2
.

Set
M>1.225
and for
L>0
and
\mu>0
, fix the number of particles
N(\mu)
by (
1.7
). Moreover, let the Hamiltonian
H
be the limit operator of
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as stated in Proposition
1.1
. Then the ground state energy
E(\mu,E_{B})=\min\sigma(H)
and the lowest solution
e_{\rm P}(\mu,E_{B})<0
of the polaron equation (
1.10
) satisfy the following property. There exist constants
c_{0},C>0
(possibly depending on
M
) such that

\displaystyle\big|E(\mu,E_{B})-E_{0}(\mu)-e_{\rm P}(\mu,E_{B})\big|\,

\displaystyle\leq C\,\frac{|e_{\rm P}(\mu,E_{B})|}{\log(\mu/|E_{B}|)}

(1.14)

for all
L>0
,
\mu>0
and
E_{B}<0
with
L^{2}|E_{B}|\geq 1
and
\mu/|E_{B}|\geq c_{0}
.

Remarks
.

a

1.3.
In Lemma
3.3
we show that

as

.

1.4.
The condition

characterizes the range of parameters in which the two-body binding energy

is at least of the order of the minimal kinetic excitation energy which equals

. In this sense our analysis is beyond the perturbative regime.

1.5.
Since the constant on the right side of (

) does not depend on

, we can directly infer a statement about the ground state energy in the thermodynamic limit,

\displaystyle\limsup_{L\to\infty}\bigg|\frac{E(\mu,E_{B})-E_{0}(\mu)}{e_{\rm P}(\mu,E_{B})}-1\bigg|\,

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\log(\mu/|E_{B}|)}

(1.15)

for all
\mu/|E_{B}|\geq c_{0}
.

1.6.
The condition

is related to the problem of stability (of second kind), that is, to find a uniform lower bound for the ground state energy in the thermodynamic limit

. While it is known that

is bounded from below for all

, it is unclear whether a uniform bound exists when

]
.

1.7.
The upper bound in (

) was proven in
[
14
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we give a brief sketch of the argument in Section

. The novel contribution of the present work is the derivation of the lower bound.

1.8.
A similar result was obtained in
[
20
]
for the case of an infinitely heavy impurity, formally corresponding to

. In this case the

The Birman–Schwinger operator

We show how to use Proposition

2.3

p\in\kappa\mathbb{Z}^{2}

(Vw)_{p}=(\langle\varphi_{p}|\otimes 1_{\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}})\,e^{iP_{\rm f}y}w
where

shall indicate to take the scalar product in the coordinate

with the plane wave
\varphi_{p}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. To see that the parameter

describes the total momentum, use

to verify

\displaystyle\big\langle w,P_{\rm tot}w\big\rangle=\sum_{p\in\kappa\mathbb{Z}^{2}}p\,\big\langle(Vw)_{p},(Vw)_{p}\big\rangle.

(2.11)

The map

is called Lee–Low–Pines transformation
[

and its inverse is given by
V^{*}(w_{p})=e^{-iP_{\rm f}y}(\varphi_{p}\otimes w_{p})
.

From this definition it is not difficult to check that
\phi(\lambda)
in (

) transforms into
V\phi(\lambda)V^{*}=\sum_{p\in\kappa\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\phi_{p}(\lambda)
where

\displaystyle\phi_{p}(\lambda)=G(p-P_{\rm f},T-\lambda)-H_{p}(\lambda)-X_{p}(\lambda)+P_{p}(\lambda)

(2.12)

is defined as an operator on
\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}
with

\displaystyle H_{p}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=a(\eta)\frac{1}{\frac{1}{M}(p-P_{\rm f})^{2}+T-\lambda}a^{*}(\eta),

(2.13)

\displaystyle X_{p}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=a(\eta)A^{*}_{p}(\lambda)+A_{p}(\lambda)a^{*}(\eta),

(2.14)

\displaystyle P_{p}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2},l^{2}>\mu}a_{l}^{*}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{M}(p-P_{\rm f}-k-l)^{2}+T+k^{2}+l^{2}-\lambda}a_{k},

(2.15)

and

\displaystyle\quad a(\eta)=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k^{2}\leq\mu}a_{k},\quad A_{p}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{k^{2}>\mu}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{M}(p-P_{\rm f}-k)^{2}+T+k^{2}-\lambda}a_{k}.

(2.16)

Note that the first summand in
\phi_{p}(\lambda)
defines an unbounded operator whereas the three other terms can be shown to be bounded operators. The domain of essential self-adjointness of
\phi_{p}(\lambda)
is the dense subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of states of the form
\varphi_{k_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\varphi_{k_{N(\mu)-1}}
with
k_{1},...,k_{N(\mu)-1}\in\kappa\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

3
Localization of the polaron energy

By proposition

the lower bound

is equivalent to

To prepare our first main statement we need to introduce a suitable orthogonal projector in the Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}
. For its definition let us give names to the subsets of the momentum lattice
\kappa\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that correspond to hole and particle momenta w.r.t. the Fermi sea,

Moreover for

\displaystyle\text{Ran}(\Pi_{\varepsilon})=\overline{\text{lin}}\big\{a^{*}_{l_{1}}...a_{l_{m-1}}^{*}a_{k_{1}}...a_{k_{m}}\left|\rm{FS}_{\mu}\right\rangle\,:\,m\geq 1,\ k_{1},...,k_{m}\in\Lambda_{\rm h},\ l_{1},....,l_{m-1}\in\Lambda_{{\rm p},\varepsilon}^{\leq}\big\}.

(3.3)

Here

stands for the closure in
\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}
of the linear hull of the subset

. For a better understanding of
\text{Ran}(\Pi_{\varepsilon})
and its orthogonal complement, let us recall that the set of all anti-symmetric products of
N(\mu)-1

\displaystyle D=\big\{a^{*}_{l_{1}}...a_{l_{m-1}}^{*}a_{k_{1}}...a_{k_{m}}\left|\rm{FS}_{\mu}\right\rangle\,:\,m\geq 1,\ k_{1},...,k_{m}\in\Lambda_{\rm h},\ l_{1},...,l_{m-1}\in\Lambda_{\rm p}\big\},

(3.4)

is a total set of the Hilbert space

, i.e.
\overline{\text{lin}}D=\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}
. A comparison with (

\Pi_{\varepsilon}
projects on all states in
\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}
that have particle modes occupied solely in the momentum lattice region

(this includes all states with zero particle modes occupied), whereas the range of
\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon}
consists of states that have at least one mode occupied in
\Lambda_{{\rm p},\varepsilon}^{>}=\Lambda_{\rm p}\setminus\Lambda_{{\rm p},\varepsilon}^{\leq}
.

In the next proposition we provide a lower bound for
\phi_{p}(\lambda)
in terms of two operators that act only on
\text{Ran}(\Pi_{\varepsilon})
and
\text{Ran}(\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon})
, respectively. The physical meaning of the two subspaces is the following: On
\text{Ran}(\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon})
it is not clear how to obtain a suitable
L

P_{p}(\lambda)
which is one of the main obstacles in the analysis. On this subspace we estimate the negative part of
P_{p}(\lambda)
in terms of
G(p-P_{\rm f},T-\lambda)
. This is closely connected to the problem of obtaining a lower bound of
H
uniformly in the system size
L\to\infty
. Such a bound, though necessary for the proof of Theorem
1.2
, is however not much related to the asymptotic form of
E(\mu,E_{B})-E_{0}(\mu)
. The latter will be determined on
\text{Ran}(\Pi_{\varepsilon})
on which the operator
P_{p}(\lambda)
is easily estimated with a suitable uniform bound. Hence on this subspace all of
G(p-P_{\rm f},T-\lambda)
is available (and needed) for the analysis of the correct energy asymptotics. This explains the motivation behind the following decomposition of
\phi_{p}(\lambda)
. Since the operator
G(p-P_{\rm f},T-\lambda)
is needed on both subspaces separately, it is an important step in our argument.

Proposition 3.1
.

There are constants

such that for all

,

,

and

\phi_{p}(\lambda)\geq\Phi_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)+\Psi_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)\,

\displaystyle=\,\Pi_{\varepsilon}\big(G(p-P_{\rm f},T-\lambda)\,-\,H_{p}(\lambda)-X_{p}(\lambda)-\varepsilon^{-1}\big)\Pi_{\varepsilon},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Psi_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)\,

\displaystyle=\,\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon}\big(G(p-P_{\rm f},T-\lambda)\,+\,P_{p}(\lambda)\,-K(\varepsilon,\widetilde{\mu})\big)\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon},

and

\displaystyle\Pi_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{M}(p-P_{\rm f}-l)^{2}+T+l^{2}-\lambda}\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{M}(p-P_{\rm f}-l)^{2}+T+l^{2}-\lambda}\Pi_{\varepsilon}\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon}=0.

(3.44)

From here the proof works the same way as for (

\displaystyle\Pi_{\varepsilon}\,P_{p}(\lambda)\,\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon}+\text{h.c.}\geq-\frac{C}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},

(3.45)

which completes the proof of the lemma and thus also the proof of Lemma

∎

4
Analysis of
\Phi_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)
: perturbed polaron equation

In this section we show that the condition
\Phi_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)\geq 0
leads to a perturbed polaron equation for
\lambda
and then provide a suitable estimate for the solution of this equation.

In order to obtain the presumably optimal asymptotics of the error in (

), we introduce another orthogonal projector
\Pi_{\varepsilon,1}
with

defined as the closed subspace of all states containing exactly one unoccupied momentum mode (a hole) in the lattice region

\Lambda_{{\rm h},\varepsilon}^{\leq}

Lemma 4.1

There are constants

\displaystyle\Pi_{\varepsilon}\,H_{p}(\lambda)\,\Pi_{\varepsilon}-\Pi_{\varepsilon,1}\,H_{p}(\lambda)\,\Pi_{\varepsilon,1}\,\geq\,C\varepsilon^{-2}\,\Pi_{\varepsilon,1}+C\varepsilon\log\widetilde{\mu}\,\big(\Pi_{\varepsilon,0}+\Pi_{\varepsilon,2+}\big).

\bullet

∎

The goal of the next lemma is to analyze the condition
pol(\lambda)-r\geq 0
for a given number
r\geq 0
. To see for which
\lambda
such a bound may hold, we use the fact that this operator is given by an expression of the form
K-V^{*}V
with

and

K
is self-adjoint and
K\geq c

\displaystyle K-V^{*}V\,

\displaystyle=\,(K-V^{*}V)K^{-1}(K-V^{*}V)+V^{*}(1-VK^{-1}V^{*})V

\displaystyle\geq\,V^{*}(1-VK^{-1}V^{*})V.

(4.23)

This is a key argument in the proof of the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any fixed
r>0
there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that for all

and
\mu/|E_{B}|\geq c_{0}

\displaystyle E_{0}(\mu)-\lambda-\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2}\leq\mu}\frac{1}{G(0,E_{0}(\mu)-\lambda-k^{2})-r}=0.

(4.24)

Remark 4.1.
We call (
4.24

Proof.

Since
T\geq E_{0}(\mu)-\mu
on
\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}
and
\lambda\leq E_{0}(\mu)+e_{\rm P}(\mu,E_{B})
, it follows by (
3.18
) that

exceeds the value of

for
\widetilde{\mu}
large enough. For such
\widetilde{\mu}
we can use (
4.23
) to find

\displaystyle pol(\lambda)-r\,\geq\,a(\eta)\frac{1}{T-\lambda}\,\mathcal{F}(T-\lambda,r)\,\frac{1}{T-\lambda}a^{*}(\eta)\!\upharpoonright\!\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}

(4.25)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(T-\lambda,r)=\left(T-\lambda-a^{*}(\eta)\frac{1}{G(0,T-\lambda)-r}a(\eta)\right)\!\upharpoonright\!\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)}.

(4.26)

From here it follows similarly as in the proof of

that

\displaystyle E_{0}(\mu)-\lambda-\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2}}\frac{1}{G(0,E_{0}(\mu)-k^{2}-\lambda)-r}\geq 0.

(4.27)

For convenience of the reader we provide the proof of the last statement in Appendix

∎

Next we prove the existence of a unique solution to the perturbed polaron equation (

For any fixed

.

2

5
Analysis of
\Psi_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)
: stability condition

On the subspace
\text{Ran}(\Pi^{\perp}_{\varepsilon})
it is not clear how to obtain a suitable
L
-independent bound for the operator
P_{p}(\lambda)
. A possible solution to this difficulty is to estimate its negative part in terms of
G(p-P_{\rm f},T-\lambda)
. Such a bound was derived in
[

,
13
]
in the context of the 2D Fermi polaron at zero density (there the model is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
instead of the box
\Omega
and the kinetic energy
E_{0}(\mu=0)
is zero). The strategy of our proof follows the one developed there, but several new obstacles need to be dealt with in the present case. The new obstacles are due to
\mu>0
and the fact that we have to work with momentum sums instead of integrals.

We write
P_{p}(\lambda)=P_{p}(\lambda)-\widetilde{P}_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)+\widetilde{P}_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)
where

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2},l^{2}>\mu/\varepsilon}a_{l}^{*}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{M}(p-P_{\rm f}-k-l)^{2}+T+k^{2}+l^{2}-\lambda}a_{k}.

(5.1)

The operator

Lemma 5.1
.

There are constants
c_{0},\varepsilon_{0},C>0
such that

\displaystyle P_{p}(\lambda)\,-\,\widetilde{P}_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)\,\geq-C\,\sqrt{\varepsilon^{-1}\log(\mu/|E_{B}|)}

(5.2)

on
\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}
for all
p\in\kappa\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
L^{2}|E_{B}|\geq 1
,
\mu/|E_{B}|\geq c_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
.

Proof.

Write

\displaystyle P_{p}(\lambda)\,-\,\widetilde{P}_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{\mu<k^{2},l^{2}\leq\mu/\varepsilon}a_{l}^{*}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{M}(p-P_{\rm f}-k-l)^{2}+T+k^{2}+l^{2}-\lambda}a_{k}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu<k^{2}\leq\mu/\varepsilon\\
l^{2}>\mu/\varepsilon\end{subarray}}a_{l}^{*}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{M}(p-P_{\rm f}-k-l)^{2}+T+k^{2}+l^{2}-\lambda}a_{k}+\text{h.c}.

(5.4)

We proceed as in the proof of (
3.20
) to obtain

(
5.3
)

\displaystyle\geq-C\bigg(f(\mu,E_{B})\frac{\mu}{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{f(\mu,E_{B})|e_{\rm P}(\mu,E_{B})|}\bigg).

(5.5)

Choosing

we get the desired bound for this line. The proof for the second line works in complete analogy.
∎

To a large extent this section is about the derivation of a lower bound for
\widetilde{P}_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)

\varepsilon
small enough, let the function

be given by

\displaystyle\beta(u,\varepsilon)=\min\Bigg\{1,\frac{(M+1-u)(M+2)\big(1-(1+\frac{M+1-u}{M(M+2)})\sqrt{\varepsilon}\big)}{(M+1-u)(1-2\sqrt{\varepsilon})+M(M+2)(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})}\Bigg\}

(5.6)

and set

\displaystyle\alpha(M,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{M(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})+1}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\beta(u,\varepsilon)(M(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon})+1-u)}\textnormal{d}u\right).

(5.7)

Proposition 5.2
.

There are constants

\Psi_{p}(\lambda,\varepsilon)\geq 0
is thus satisfied if

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon^{1/3})\,\frac{M}{M+1}-\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\,\alpha(M,\varepsilon)\,\geq\,0.

(5.61)

This is similar to the stability condition at zero density that was derived in
[
13
]
. There it was shown that the Fermi polaron defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is stable if

\displaystyle\frac{M}{M+1}-\alpha(M,0)\geq 0

(5.62)

which was proven to hold for all
M>1.225

[
13
, Theorem 1]
. Since
\alpha(M,\varepsilon)
depends continuously on
\varepsilon
, we can conclude that (
5.61
) holds for any given
M>1.225
if we choose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. This completes the proof of the corollary.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2

As a first step we replace
G(q,\tau)
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(q,\tau)=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k}\left(\frac{1}{mk^{2}-E_{B}}-\frac{\xi_{\mu}(k^{2})}{\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau}\right),

(6.1)

where

\displaystyle\xi_{\mu}(s)=\begin{cases}&\quad\quad\quad\quad 0\hskip 81.09052pt(s\leq\mu)\\
&\frac{1}{2}\cos\Big(\frac{\pi(s-\mu)\log\widetilde{\mu}}{\mu}\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\quad\quad(\mu\leq s\leq\mu+\mu/\log\widetilde{\mu})\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad 1\hskip 82.22844pt(s\geq\mu+\mu/\log\widetilde{\mu}).\end{cases}

(6.2)

Compared to
G(q,\tau)
we have replaced the characteristic function
\chi_{(\mu,\infty)}(k^{2})
with a smoother cutoff described by
\xi_{\mu}(k^{2})
. The error for this can be controlled by a crude estimate like

\displaystyle|G(q,\tau)-\widetilde{G}(q,\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2}\geq\mu}\frac{\chi_{(\mu,\mu+\mu/\log\widetilde{\mu})}(k^{2})}{\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau}\leq C\frac{\mu}{(\mu+\tau)\log\widetilde{\mu}},

(6.3)

which is easily justified by means of (
3.7
). Next we write
\widetilde{G}(q,\tau)=L^{-2}\sum_{k}g(k)
with

\displaystyle g(k)=\frac{1}{mk^{2}+|E_{B}|}-\frac{\xi_{\mu}(k^{2})}{\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau},

(6.4)

and apply Poisson’s summation formula (see e.g.
[
12
, Section 3.2]
) to find

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(q,\tau)-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(k)\textnormal{d}^{2}k=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\bigg(\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}\sum_{k\in\kappa\mathbb{Z}^{2}}g(k)-(2\pi)\widehat{g}(0)\bigg)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in L\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
z\neq 0\end{subarray}}\widehat{g}(z).

(6.5)

To compute
\widehat{g}(0)
we replace
\xi_{\mu}(s)
again with
\chi_{(\mu,\infty)}(k^{2})
and estimate the difference

\displaystyle\Bigg|\,(2\pi)\widehat{g}(0)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{mk^{2}+|E_{B}|}-\frac{\chi_{(\mu,\infty)}(k^{2})}{\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau}\right)\textnormal{d}^{2}k\,\Bigg|\leq C\frac{\mu}{(\mu+\tau)\log\widetilde{\mu}}.

(6.6)

The integral can be evaluated explicitly,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{mk^{2}+|E_{B}|}-\frac{\chi_{(\mu,\infty)}(k^{2})}{\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau}\right)\textnormal{d}^{2}k

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt=\frac{\pi}{m}\log\left(\frac{\frac{1}{M+1}q^{2}+\tau+m\mu}{|E_{B}|}\right)+\frac{\pi}{m}\log\left(1-\frac{F(q^{2},\tau)}{2}\right)

(6.7)

where

\displaystyle F(s,\tau)=\frac{\frac{1}{M}s+\tau+m\mu}{\frac{1}{M+1}s+\tau+m\mu}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4s\frac{\mu}{M^{2}}}{\big(\frac{1}{M}s+\tau+m\mu\big)^{2}}}\right).

(6.8)

The last expression is not larger than
1+1/M
such that for
M>1
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\log\left(1-\frac{F(q^{2},\tau)}{2}\right)\bigg|\leq C.

(6.9)

It follows that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\,(2\pi)\widehat{g}(0)-\frac{\pi}{m}\log\left(\frac{\frac{1}{M+1}q^{2}+\tau+m\mu}{|E_{B}|}\right)\Bigg|\leq C\bigg(1+\frac{\mu}{(\mu+\tau)\log\widetilde{\mu}}\bigg).

(6.10)

Next we need to estimate the right side in (
6.5
). To this end, write
g(k)=g_{1}(k)+g_{2}(k)
with

\displaystyle g_{1}(k)=\frac{1}{mk^{2}+|E_{B}|},\quad\quad g_{2}(k)=\frac{\xi_{\mu}(k^{2})}{\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau},

(6.11)

and use rotational symmetry, i.e.
\widehat{g}_{i}(z)=\widehat{g}_{i}(|z|e_{u})
,
i=1,2
, where
e_{u}
denotes the first unit vector in the
(k_{u},k_{v})
plane. We can then use integration by parts to compute the Fourier transform for
z\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\widehat{g}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=m^{-1}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}k_{u}e^{ik_{u}|z|}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}k_{v}\frac{1}{k^{2}+|E_{B}|/m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(i|z|)^{3}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}k_{u}\left(\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial k_{u}^{3}}e^{ik_{u}|z|}\right)\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}k_{v}\frac{1}{k^{2}+|E_{B}|/m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{im|z|^{3}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}k_{u}e^{ik_{u}|z|}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}k_{v}\frac{18k_{u}(k_{v}^{2}+|E_{B}|/m)-24k_{u}^{3}}{(k^{2}+|E_{B}|/m)^{4}}.

(6.12)

Of the last expression we estimate the absolute value to get

\displaystyle|\widehat{g}_{1}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m|z|^{3}}\int\textnormal{d}^{2}k\left(\frac{18|k|}{(k^{2}+|E_{B}|/m)^{3}}+\frac{24|k|^{3}}{(k^{2}+|E_{B}|/m)^{4}}\right)\leq\frac{C}{|z|^{3}|E_{B}|^{3/2}}.

(6.13)

The bound for
|\widehat{g}_{2}(z)|
works similarly but is slightly more cumbersome. We start again with

\displaystyle\widehat{g}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{im|z|^{3}|E_{B}|^{3/2}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}k_{u}e^{ik_{u}|z|}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\textnormal{d}k_{v}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial k_{u}^{3}}\left(\frac{\xi_{\mu}(k^{2})|E_{B}|^{3/2}}{\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau}\right)

(6.14)

for which we need to compute the different derivatives. A straightforward computation shows

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial k_{u}^{n}}\xi_{\mu}(k^{2})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{(\log\widetilde{\mu})^{n}}{\mu^{n/2}}\chi_{(\mu,\mu+\mu/\log\widetilde{\mu})}(k^{2}),\quad n\in\{1,2,3\}.

(6.15)

Abbreviating the denominator as
D(k)=\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau
it is not difficult to verify

\displaystyle\chi_{(\mu,\mu+\mu/\log\widetilde{\mu})}(k^{2})\bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{u}}\frac{1}{D(k)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}(k^{2}+\tau)}+\frac{\mu^{1/2}}{(k^{2}+\tau)^{2}}\bigg),

(6.16)

\displaystyle\chi_{(\mu,\mu+\mu/\log\widetilde{\mu})}(k^{2})\bigg|\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial k_{u}^{2}}\frac{1}{D(k)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{(k^{2}+\tau)^{2}}+\frac{\mu}{(k^{2}+\tau)^{3}}\bigg),

(6.17)

\displaystyle\chi_{(\mu,\mu+\mu/\log\widetilde{\mu})}(k^{2})\bigg|\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial k_{u}^{3}}\frac{1}{D(k)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{\mu^{1/2}(k^{2}+\tau)^{2}}+\frac{\mu^{1/2}}{(k^{2}+\tau)^{3}}+\frac{\mu^{3/2}}{(k^{2}+\tau)^{4}}\bigg).

(6.18)

To illustrate this for the first line, we compute

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{u}}\frac{1}{D(k)}\bigg|=\bigg|\frac{1}{D(k)^{2}}\bigg(\frac{2}{M}(k_{u}-q_{u})+2k_{u}\bigg)\bigg|\leq C\bigg(\frac{|k-q|}{D(k)^{2}}+\frac{|k|}{D(k)^{2}}\bigg)

(6.19)

and use
D(k)\geq\frac{1}{M}(k-q)^{2}
and
k^{2}\leq 2\mu
in combination with a balanced Cauchy-Schwarz estimate. This leads to

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{u}}\frac{1}{D(k)}\bigg|\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{D(k)^{3/2}}+\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{D(k)^{2}}\bigg)\leq C\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}D(k)}+\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{D(k)^{2}}\bigg)

(6.20)

from which the bound in (
6.16
) follows by
D(k)\geq k^{2}+\tau
. The other two lines are obtained in close analogy.

Summing up the different combinations we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial k_{u}^{3}}\bigg(\frac{\xi_{\mu}(k^{2})|E_{B}|^{3/2}}{\frac{1}{M}(p-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\frac{C(\log\widetilde{\mu})^{3}}{\widetilde{\mu}^{3/2}}\bigg(\chi_{(\mu,\mu+\mu/\log\widetilde{\mu})}(k^{2})\frac{1}{k^{2}+\tau}+\chi_{(\mu,\infty)}(k^{2})\sum_{j=2}^{4}\frac{\mu^{j-1}}{(k^{2}+\tau)^{j}}\bigg)

(6.21)

by which we can estimate the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bigg|\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial k_{u}^{3}}\bigg(\frac{\xi_{\mu}(k^{2})|E_{B}|^{3/2}}{\frac{1}{M}(q-k)^{2}+k^{2}+\tau}\bigg)\bigg|\textnormal{d}^{2}k

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(1+\frac{\mu}{(\mu+\tau)\log\widetilde{\mu}}\bigg)^{3}.

(6.22)

Together with (
6.13
) and (
6.14
), this gives

\displaystyle|\widehat{g}(z)|\leq\frac{C}{|z|^{3}|E_{B}|^{3/2}}\bigg(1+\frac{\mu}{(\mu+\tau)\log\widetilde{\mu}}\bigg)^{3}.

(6.23)

The remaining series can be bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in L\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
z\neq 0\end{subarray}}|z|^{-3}|E_{B}|^{-3/2}=(L|E_{B}|^{1/2})^{-3}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
z\neq 0\end{subarray}}|z|^{-3}\leq C

(6.24)

because of
L^{2}|E_{B}|\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
z\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|z|^{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\geq 1}\frac{4}{(n^{2}+m^{2})^{3/2}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{4}{n^{3}}\leq\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{8\pi}{s^{2}}+\frac{4}{s^{3}}\bigg)\textnormal{d}s\leq C

(6.25)

by the integral test of convergence.

We conclude that the absolute value of the right side in (
6.5
) is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in L\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
z\neq 0\end{subarray}}|\widehat{g}(z)|\leq C\bigg(1+\frac{\mu}{(\mu+\tau)\log\widetilde{\mu}}\bigg)^{3}.

(6.26)

Hence the proof of the lemma is complete.

Completing the proof of Lemma
4.2

It remains to analyze the condition
\mathcal{F}(T-\lambda,r)\geq 0
. For that we approximate
\mathcal{F}(T-\lambda,r)
by
\mathcal{F}^{(n)}(T-\lambda,r)
where the operator
\mathcal{F}^{(n)}(T-\lambda,r)
arises by replacing the
function
G(0,\tau)
in (
4.26
) by

\displaystyle G^{(n)}(0,\tau)=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2}\leq n}\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}-E_{B}}-\frac{\chi_{(\mu,\infty)}(k^{2})}{k^{2}+\tau}\right).

(B.1)

Note that
G^{(n)}(0,\tau)\to G(0,\tau)
as
n\to\infty
for every
\tau>-\mu
. Thus,
G^{(n)}(0,T-\lambda)\psi\to G_{\mu}(0,T-\lambda)\psi
as
n\to\infty
for every
\psi\in D
(recall that
D\subset\mathscr{H}_{N(\mu)-1}
is the set of all anti-symmetric product states, see (
3.4
)). Since
D
forms a total set of eigenstates of

G(0,T-\lambda)
on
\mathscr{H}_{N\mspace{-1mu}(\mspace{-1mu}\mu\mspace{-1mu})-1}
, its linear hull
\text{lin}D\subseteq\mathscr{H}_{N\mspace{-1mu}(\mspace{-1mu}\mu\mspace{-1mu})-1}
is a domain of essential self-adjointness for this
operator. Furthermore,
G^{(n)}(0,T-\lambda)\geq G^{(n)}(0,-\mu-e_{\rm P}(\mu,E_{B}))
and thus, by the convergence of
G^{(n)}(0,\tau)
and the fact that
G(0,-\mu-e_{\rm P}(\mu))\geq C\log\widetilde{\mu}
, it follows that there is a
c>0
such that
G^{(n)}(0,T-\lambda)-r>c
for
n
large enough. Hence as
n\to\infty
,

(G^{(n)}(0,T-\lambda)-r)^{-1}\to(G(0,T-\lambda)-r)^{-1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(n)}(T-\lambda,r)\to\mathcal{F}(T-\lambda,r)
strongly.

Using the pull-through formula (
2.6
), we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(n)}(T-\lambda,r)

\displaystyle=T-\lambda-\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2}\leq\mu}(G^{(n)}(0,T-k^{2}-\lambda)-r)^{-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2},l^{2}\leq\mu}\!\!a_{k}(G^{(n)}(0,T-k^{2}-l^{2}-\lambda)-r)^{-1}a_{l}^{*}

(B.2)

on
\mathscr{H}_{N\mspace{-1mu}(\mspace{-1mu}\mu\mspace{-1mu})}
. Assuming that the last term in (
B.2
), which we call
\mathcal{P}^{(n)}(T-\lambda,r)
in the following, is a positive operator on

\mathscr{H}_{N\mspace{-1mu}(\mspace{-1mu}\mu\mspace{-1mu})}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(n)}(T-\lambda,r)\geq E_{0}(\mu)-\lambda-\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{k^{2}\leq\mu}(G^{(n)}(0,E_{0}(\mu)-k^{2}-\lambda)-r)^{-1},

(B.3)

since
T\geq E_{0}(\mu)
on
\mathscr{H}_{N\mspace{-1mu}(\mspace{-1mu}\mu\mspace{-1mu})}
. In view of (
4.25
), this completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

It remains to show
\mathcal{P}^{(n)}(T-\lambda,r)\geq 0
. For
\psi\in\mathscr{H}_{N\mspace{-1mu}(\mspace{-1mu}\mu\mspace{-1mu})}
,

\displaystyle L^{2}\,\mbox{$\langle\psi,\mathcal{P}^{(n)}(T-\lambda,r)\psi\rangle$}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k^{2},l^{2}\leq\mu}\mbox{$\langle\psi,a_{k}\>\exp(-t\,[G^{(n)}(0,T-k^{2}-l^{2}-\lambda)-r)\>a_{l}^{*}\psi\rangle$}\,\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\>\exp(-t[L^{-2}\sum_{p^{2}\leq n}\tfrac{1}{p^{2}-E_{B}}\!-\!r])\,\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(t)\,\textnormal{d}t

(B.4)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(t)=\sum_{k^{2},l^{2}\leq\mu}\mbox{$\langle\psi,a_{k}\prod\limits_{\mu<q^{2}\leq n}\!\!\exp(t(q^{2}+T-k^{2}-l^{2}-\lambda)^{-1})\>\,a_{l}^{*}\psi\rangle$}.

(B.5)

We show that
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that the product in the definition of
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(t)
has
only finitely many factors, because
A_{n}=\{q\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\mathbb{Z}^{2}\>|\>\mu<q^{2}\leq n\}
is a finite set.
We consider the exponential series and obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(t)=\sum_{k^{2},l^{2}\leq\mu}\mbox{$\langle\psi,a_{k}\prod\limits_{q\in A_{n}}\!\!\left(\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\,\frac{1}{(q^{2}+T-k^{2}-l^{2}-\lambda)^{m}}\right)a_{l}^{*}\psi\rangle$}.

(B.6)

By the absolute convergence of the exponential series, we can rearrange the product of series to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(t)=\sum_{m:A_{n}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}}\>\>\sum_{k^{2},l^{2}\leq\mu}\mbox{$\langle\psi,a_{k}\prod\limits_{q\in A_{n}}\!\!\left(\frac{t^{m(q)}}{m(q)!}\,\frac{1}{(q^{2}+T-k^{2}-l^{2}-\lambda)^{m(q)}}\right)a_{l}^{*}\psi\rangle$},

(B.7)

where we sum over all
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-valued functions
m
on the finite set
A_{n}
. Note that the factor in parentheses indexed
by
q
is equal to
1
if
m(q)=0
. For all factors with
m(q)\neq 0

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{\tau}}=\frac{1}{c_{\tau}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!\textnormal{d}s\>e^{-as^{1/\tau}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad c_{\tau}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!\textnormal{d}s\>e^{-s^{1/\tau}}

(B.8)

for
a,\tau>0

m

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{2},l^{2}\leq\mu}\>\>\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in A_{n}\\

m(p)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\!\!e^{-(p^{2}+T-k^{2}-l^{2}-\lambda)s_{p}^{1/m(p)}}a_{l}^{*}\psi\rangle$}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in A_{n}\\
m(q)\neq 0\end{subarray}}}\left(\frac{t^{m(q)}}{m(q)!\,c_{m(q)}}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!\textnormal{d}s_{q}\right)\big|\hskip-0.56905pt\big|\sum\limits_{k^{2}\leq\mu}\prod\limits_{{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in A_{n}\\

(B.9)

This yields
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(t)\geq 0

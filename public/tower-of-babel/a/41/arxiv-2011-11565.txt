The ℋ \mathcal{H} -tautological ring
Carl Lian

Abstract
. We extend the theory of tautological classes on moduli spaces of stable curves to the more general setting of moduli spaces of admissible Galois covers of curves, introducing the so-called ℋ \mathcal{H} -tautological ring. The main new feature is the existence of restriction-corestriction morphisms remembering intermediate quotients of Galois covers, which are a rich source of new classes. In particular, our new framework includes classes of Harris-Mumford admissible covers on moduli spaces of curves, which are known in some (and speculatively many more) examples to lie outside the usual tautological ring. We give additive generators for the ℋ \mathcal{H} -tautological ring and show that their intersections may be algorithmically computed, building on work of Schmitt-van Zelm. As an application, we give a method for computing integrals of Harris-Mumford loci against tautological classes of complementary dimension, recovering and giving a mild generalization of a recent quasi-modularity result of the author for covers of elliptic curves.

Introduction

1.1.
Hurwitz loci on moduli spaces of curves

The cycle theory of the moduli space of stable curves
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is central to understanding its geometry. Moduli spaces of branched covers of curves provide a particularly rich source of algebraic cycles on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{g/h,d}
be a moduli space of branched covers (a
Hurwitz space
), parametrizing finite degree
d
morphisms
f:X\to Y
, where
X,Y
are smooth curves of genus
g,h
, respectively. Suppose further that
f
is simply branched over

b=(2g-2)-d(2h-2)

points. We then get morphisms

\textstyle{\mathcal{H}_{g/h,d}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{g/h,d}}
\scriptstyle{\delta_{g/h,d}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}_{g,b}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}_{h,b}}

remembering the source and target, marked with the ramification and branch points, respectively.

Via the Harris-Mumford space of admissible covers, one can compactify
\mathcal{H}_{g/h,d}
and extend the morphisms above:

\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g/h,d}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{g/h,d}}
\scriptstyle{\delta_{g/h,d}}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,b}}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,b}}

By to the Riemann Existence Theorem, the map
\delta_{g/h,d}
is flat and quasi-finite of degree given combinatorially by a
Hurwitz number
counting monodromy actions of the fundamental group of the target curve, punctured at the branch points, on the fibers of
f
.

On the other hand, the map
\phi_{g/h,d}
gives an interesting cycle of homological degree
3h-3+b
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,b}
upon pushing forward the fundamental class
1\in A_{*}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g/h,d})
; we refer to such cycles as
branched cover loci
or
Hurwitz loci
.

We note three reasons for a detailed study of such cycles:

(i)

Cycles
(\phi_{g/h,d})_{*}(1)
are interesting from the point of view of birational geometry. For example, Eisenbud, Harris, and Mumford prove
[
HM82
,
Har84
,
EH87
]
that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is of general type for
g\geq 24
by producing effective divisors

on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
for which
K_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}=A+E
, for some ample divisor
A
. When
g
is odd, they take
E
to be the branched cover locus
(\pi_{g/0,(g+1)/2})_{*}(1)
of curves of sub-generic gonality.

More recently, numerous examples of extremal classes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
have been constructed via branched cover loci, see, for instance,
[
CC14
,
CC15
,
CP16
,
CT16
,
Bla20
]
.

(ii)

Cycles
(\phi_{g/h,d})_{*}(1)
satisfy interesting numerological phenomena. For example, in the case
h=1
, we have the following:

Conjecture 1
.

[
L20
, Conjecture 1]

Fix an integer
g\geq 2
. We have:

\sum_{d\geq 1}[(\phi_{g/1,d})_{*}(1)]q^{d}\in A^{g-1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})\otimes\Qmod,

where
\Qmod
is the ring of quasimodular forms.

Conjecture
[
L20
]
is known to be true for
g=2,3

[
L20
, Theorem 1.2]
, with a number of variants, for example, allowing for marked points to be taken into account. We give a slight improvement on this result in the case
g=2
in Theorem
6.4
.

(iii)

Cycles
(\phi_{g/h,d})_{*}(1)
are known in some cases to be non-tautological, see, for example,
[
GP03
,
vZ18
]
. We defer a more detailed discussion of this phenomenon to the next section.

1.2.
The classical tautological ring

Both the Chow ring
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
and the cohomology ring
H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g.n})
are extremely complicated and a full understanding of these objects seems out of reach. However, Mumford
[
Mum83
]
initiated a study of certain
tautological classes
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
that appear in many natural geometric situations and are largely computable in practice.

By definition, the tautological rings
R^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\subset A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
form the smallest system of
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebras containing the
\psi
and
\kappa
classes and closed under all pushforwards by forgetful morphisms
\pi:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and boundary morphisms
\xi_{\Gamma}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, indexed by stable graphs
\Gamma
of genus
g
with
n
marked points.
1
1

1

In fact, one can make this definition without mentioning the
\psi
and
\kappa
classes, which will automatically be included, but we include them explicitly to highlight their fundamental importance.
Additive generators for the tautological ring may be given by
decorated boundary strata
, that is, pushforwards of polynomials in
\psi
and
\kappa
classes by boundary morphisms, see
[
GP03
, Appendix A]
, and indeed such classes may be algorithmically intersected in terms of the combinatorics of dual graphs.

Many cohomology classes on moduli spaces of curves arising in geometry turn out to be tautological. However, as first shown by Graber-Pandharipande
[
GP03
]
, Hurwitz loci, in general, are not. For example, as we review in §
3.6
, the
(3h-1)
-dimensional locus of stable curves of genus
2h
admitting an admissible double cover of a curve of genus
h
is non-tautological for
h
sufficiently large. Indeed, its pullback to the boundary stratum
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2h}
is a positive multiple of the diagonal class. However, if it were tautological, it would have a tautological Künneth decomoposition upon pullback, which is impossible because
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,1}
is known to have odd cohomology for
h
sufficiently large.

This example yields two insights: firstly, one would get a strictly larger theory by adding Hurwitz loci to the calculus of tautological classes, and secondly, doing so would require diagonal classes to be added as well. We will, in fact, go further: we develop this theory in the Chow rings of Hurwitz spaces themselves.

1.3.
Galois covers

The main drawback to the Harris-Mumford compactification of Hurwitz spaces by admissible covers is that in general, spaces of admissible covers fail to be smooth. In order to carry out intersection theory on them in a robust way, therefore, we must pass to a resolution of singularities.

We will instead work with
Galois covers
. Suppose that
f:X\to Y
is a degree
d
étale cover of (possibly non-proper) curves. Then, we have a principal
S_{d}
bundle

\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}:=(X\times_{Y}\cdots\times_{Y}X)-\Delta\to Y

given by taking the
d
-fold product over
X
and removing all diagonals. Moreover, the data of
f
can be recovered from the
S_{d}
-cover
\widetilde{X}\to Y
by defining
X=\widetilde{X}/S_{d-1}
.

If
f:X\to Y
is now assumed to be a possibly branched cover of smooth curves, one can carry out the same construction on the étale locus, then pass to the unique extension to proper curves, to produce a map
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\to Y
that is an
S_{d}
-cover, but no longer a principal
S_{d}
-bundle.

If, however,
f:X\to Y
is an admissible cover of nodal curves, the construction breaks down. On the other hand, the inverse construction is still valid: an
admissible
S_{d}
-cover

\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\to Y
gives rise to an admissible cover
f:\widetilde{X}/S_{d-1}\to Y
.

Thus, we have a map
\nu:\widetilde{H}_{g/h,d}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g/h,d}
, where
\widetilde{H}_{g/h,d}
parametrizes
S_{d}
-covers
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\to Y
that, upon taking the intermediate quotient by
S_{d-1}
, gives an admissible cover of the required type. The work of Abramovich-Corti-Vistoli in the language of
twisted stable maps
(where the scheme-theoretic
S_{d}
-cover
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\to Y
is replaced by a principal
S_{d}
-bundle
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
over a stacky curve
\mathcal{Y}
) implies that
\widetilde{H}_{g/h,d}
is smooth and
\nu
is a normalization, see
[
ACV03
, Proposition 4.2.2]
.

Therefore, we work in the following setting, following
[
SvZ18
]
: if
G
is a finite group, let
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}
be the moduli space of
pointed admissible
G
-covers
with source genus
g
and with
monodromy data
given by
\xi
, see Definition
3.4
. Then,
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}
is a smooth Deligne-Mumford stack, see Theorem
3.7
; it admits source and target maps

\displaystyle\phi:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,r}

\displaystyle\delta:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g^{\prime},b}

as in the Harris-Mumford setting.

1.4.
The
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring

We develop the theory of tautological classes on spaces of admissible
G
-covers
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}
in parallel with that of the classical theory.

As in the classical setting, we have natural notions of forgetful morphisms
\pi:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi+\{1\}}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}
and boundary morphisms
\xi_{(\Gamma,G)}:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{(\Gamma,G)}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}
, the latter indexed by
admissible
G
-graphs
. In addition, one can define
\psi
and
\kappa
classes on
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}
by pullback under
\phi
.

However, the main new feature of the theory of spaces of admissible
G
-covers is the existence of
restriction morphisms

\res^{G}_{G_{1}}:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G_{1},\xi^{\prime}}

restricting the
G
-action on a stable curve to that of a subgroup
G_{1}\subset G
, and
corestriction morphisms

\cores^{G}_{G/G_{1}}:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g_{1},G/G_{1},\xi^{\prime}}

corestricting the
G
-action on a stable curve
X
to that of
G/G_{1}
on the partial quotient
X/G_{1}
, where
G_{1}\subset G
is a normal subgroup.

Combining these operations gives a more general notion of
restriction-corestriction morphisms
, see Definition
3.22
.

For example, restriction maps
\res^{G}_{\{1\}}
recover source maps
\phi
, and corestriction maps
\cores^{G}_{\{1\}}
recover target maps
\delta
. The pre-composition

\phi_{g/h,d}\circ\nu:\widetilde{H}_{g/h,d}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,b}

of the source map on a Harris-Mumford space with its normalization by
S_{d}
-covers may also be regarded as the composition

\cores^{S_{d-1}}_{\{1\}}\circ\res^{S_{d}}_{S_{d-1}}.

Finally, as indicated in §
1.2
, our theory must also include diagonal morphisms

\Delta:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}

in the calculus, as pullbacks of restriction maps by boundary morphisms may include contributions from such classes.

The main definition of the paper (see also Definition
3.25
) is then:

Definition 1.1
.

The
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring
is the smallest system of
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebras

R^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g_{j},G_{j},\xi_{j}}\right)\subset A^{*}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g_{j},G_{j},\xi_{j}}\right),

ranging over all finite products of moduli spaces of admissible
G
-covers, that contains all
\psi
and
\kappa
classes and is closed under pushfoward by all (products of) boundary, forgetful, restriction-corestriction, and diagonal maps.

If
\gamma
is an arbitrary composition of tautological morphisms and
\theta
is a polynomial in the
\psi
and
\kappa
classes, then certainly
\gamma_{*}(\theta)
must be
\mathcal{H}
-tautological. Our main theorem (see also Theorem
5.1
) is that such classes additively generate the
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring.

Theorem 1.2
.

The classes
\gamma_{*}(\theta)
comprise a set of additive generators for the
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring. Moreover, the
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring is closed under bivariant pullback by the previously-described tautological morphisms.

In fact, one can take
\gamma
to be a composition of diagonal, restriction-corestriction, forgetful, and boundary morphisms, in that order, see Theorem
5.2
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is constructive. We give algorithms for expressing the bivariant pullback of any
\mathcal{H}
-tautological class by any tautological morphism. Many of the methods are analogous to those in the classical situation. For example, one can intersect boundary classes on spaces of admissible
G
-covers (see §
4.2
) in an essentially identical fashion to that of boundary classes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
(see §
2.4
or
[
GP03
, Appendix A]
), keeping track of the additional data of a group action.

However, we highlight here two aspects of the theory requiring new ideas.

(i)

Intersections of restriction-corestriction morphisms with boundary strata, see §
4.3
. This was initiated in the case of the source map
\phi=\res^{G}_{\{1\}}
by Schmitt-van Zelm
[
SvZ18
]
, where many examples of classes of Galois Hurwitz loci on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
determined by their integrals against classical tautological classes are computed.

We extend the results of Schmitt-van Zelm to the setting of restriction-corestriction morphisms, where the functorial intersections are, in general, non-reduced, owing to the fact that boundary strata in general lie in the branch locus of corestriction morphisms.

In particular, our methods allow for the integration of classical tautological classes against arbitrary (non-Galois) Hurwitz loci coming from Harris-Mumford spaces, which is already new. In §
6
, we employ our methods to re-compute the classes of
d
-elliptic loci in genus 2, as originally carried out in
[
L20
]
, and state a mild generalization of the quasimodularity observed therein, see Theorem
6.4
.

(ii)

Intersections of two restriction-corestriction morphisms, see §
4.6
. These methods are new, but because the intersection-theoretic formulas are quite complicated, with terms indexed by group-theoretic data that seem difficult to compute in practice, the results may perhaps be more profitably viewed as proofs of existence.

1.5.
Further directions

We briefly propose here four avenues for further investigation on the
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring.

(i)

Improvement and implementation of algorithms. SAGE code of Delecroix-Schmitt-van Zelm is available to perform computations in the classical tautological ring, see
[
DSvZ
]
. Their code has additional functionality for certain Galois Hurwitz loci on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

A next step would be extend this work to include functionality for the general
\mathcal{H}
-tautological calculus, particularly that of Harris-Mumford loci, which, as we explain in §
6
, can in fact can be dealt with computationally without making explicit reference to Galois covers.

As we indicated above, the results of §
4.6
are, at least as stated, likely too unwieldy to be implemented computationally; some theoretical speedups would thus be desirable here.

(ii)

Study of the structure of the
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring, following, for instance, the Faber conjectures for the classical tautological ring, see
[
Fab99
]
.

Two places to start would be the following questions:

•

What is the rank of
(R_{\mathcal{H}})_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi})
? In the classical situation, the answer is always 1, by a result of Graber-Vakil
[
GV01
]
. When
G=\{1\}
, that is,
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, the answer in the
\mathcal{H}
-tautological setting is again 1 assuming that zero-cycles of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
defined over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
are tautological (see
[
PS20
, Speculation 1.3]
), though we do not have an independent proof. In the general case, the situation is complicated by the fact that
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}
may be disconnected, but in principle the group of zero-dimensional
\mathcal{H}
-tautological classes could still have rank 1.

•

What is the largest integer
k
for which
R_{\mathcal{H}}^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{g})=0
? In the classical situation, the answer is
k=g-1
due to Looijenga and Faber, see
[
Loo95
,
Fab97
]
. However, when one allows
\mathcal{H}
-tautological classes, this becomes false when
g=12
due to van Zelm’s result that the bielliptic locus on
\mathcal{M}_{12}
is non-tautological, see
[
vZ18
, Theorem 2]
.

(iii)

Construction of non-trivial
\mathcal{H}
-tautological relations. For classical tautological classes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, the Pixton relations
[
Pix12
,
PPZ15
]
are conjecturally a complete set of relations; these can be pulled back to
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}
. However, establishing
\mathcal{H}
-tautological relations not arising in this way would require new tools. For example, the full Conjecture
1
is unlikely to be proven by (classical) “tautological methods,” as the classes appearing are not all tautological.

As a step in this direction, Clader
[
Cla17
]
has constructed relations on the moduli space of cyclic covers, pushing forward to (classical) tautological relations on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

(iv)

Study of the size of the
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring, relative to that (for example) of the classical tautological ring, or on the other hand the entire cohomology or Chow ring of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. In particular, it would be interesting now to attempt to construct cycles that are not
\mathcal{H}
-tautological.

1.6.
Conventions

We work over
\mathbb{C}
. All curves are assumed projective and connected with only nodes as singularities, unless otherwise indicated. The
genus
of a curve refers to its arithmetic genus. All moduli spaces are understood to be stacks, rather than coarse moduli spaces.

We will work throughout with rational Chow groups
A^{*}(\mathcal{X})
, where
\mathcal{X}
ranges over a system of products of Hurwitz spaces.

All group actions are left group actions, unless otherwise stated.

For sake of clarity of exposition, we will often describe constructions involving moduli spaces on the level of closed points, rather than functors; we invite the reader to generalize the discussion appropriately, for example, by replacing marked points on curves with sections of families of curves.

2.1.
The universal family

Definition 2.1
.

We define the forgetful morphism
\pi:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

Taken together,

We have the following easy result:

The commutative diagram

\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}

is Cartesian up to birational equivalence.

\psi

Let

2.3.
Stable graphs and boundary strata

The boundary of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g.n}
admits a stratification by substacks indexed by stable graphs and parametrized by products of moduli spaces of curves of smaller dimension.

Definition 2.6
.

A
graph

\Gamma
consists of the data of a vertex set
V(\Gamma)
, a half-edge set
H(\Gamma)
, a fixed-point free involution
\iota:H(\Gamma)\to H(\Gamma)
whose orbits form the edge set
E(\Gamma)
of
E
, and an assignment
a:H(\Gamma)\to V(\Gamma)
of a vertex to each half-edge.

A
stable graph
includes the additional data of a genus function
g:V(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and an ordered set
L(\Gamma)
of legs with an assignment
\zeta:L(\Gamma)\to V(\Gamma)
of a vertex to each leg. We denote the
valency
of a vertex, the sum of the number of half-edges and legs of
\Gamma

v
, by
n(v)
, and require that
\Gamma
satisfies the stability condition that
2g(v)-2+n(v)>0
for any
v\in V(\Gamma)
.

The
genus
of
\Gamma
is defined to be

g(\Gamma):=h^{1}(\Gamma)+\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}g(v),

and the number of elements

of

is referred to as the number of
marked points
of
\Gamma
.

Stable graphs of genus
g

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Given a stable graph
\Gamma

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}:=\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g(v),n(v)}

We then have a natural

\xi_{\Gamma}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

gluing together marked points corresponding to paired half-edges, whose image is the closure of the locus of stable curves with dual graph
\Gamma

The map
\xi_{\Gamma}
is finite and unramified; the degree onto its image is given by
\#\Aut(\Gamma)
, where the notion of a morphism (and hence an automorphism) of a stable graph is given below.

Definition 2.8

Suppose

2.6.
The tautological ring

Definition 2.15
.

The
tautological ring
is the smallest system of
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebras
R^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\subset A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
containing all
\psi
and
\kappa

Similarly, one can define the tautological

by the image of the tautological ring in Chow under the cycle class map.

Additive generators for the tautological ring are given by

, that is, pushforwards of monomials in
\psi
and
\kappa
classes by boundary morphisms, see
[

Computing intersection numbers of tautological classes amounts to computing to integrals of polynomials in

[

]
.

3.1.

Stacks of admissible covers of nodal curves were introduced by Harris-Mumford in
[

to compactify Hurwitz spaces of branched covers of smooth curves. Later work by Mochizuki
[

]
and Abramovich-Corti-Vistoli

clarified the notion of a family of admissible covers over an arbitrary base scheme. In addition, the moduli space

of stable maps to
BG
constructed by Abramovich-Corti-Vistoli gives a normalization of the Harris-Mumford space by way of twisted
G
-covers. Incarnations of this space of also appear in
[
JKK05
]
and
[

]

[

]

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a finite group and let

be an arbitrary base scheme. An

-cover
over

of connected nodal curves over

with a fiberwise action of

on

and distinct marked sections

satisfying the following properties:

•

[D,q_{1},\ldots,q_{b}]
is a stable curve over
S
,

•

if
s\in S
is a geometric point, then
f(x)\in Y_{s}
is a node if and only if

is,

•

f
, with the given
G
-action on
X
, is a principal
G
-bundle away from
q_{1},\ldots,q_{b}\in Y
and the nodes of
Y

•

in analytic local coordinates over a marked point of
D
, the map

is isomorphic to

\Spec(R[x^{\prime}])\to\Spec(R[x])\to\Spec(R),

where
e\geq 1
is an integer and
f^{*}(x)=(x^{\prime})^{e}
,

•

in analytic local coordinates over a node of
D
, the map

is isomorphic to

\Spec(R[x^{\prime},y^{\prime}]/(x^{\prime}y^{\prime}-r))\to\Spec(R[x,y]/(xy-r^{e}))\to\Spec(R),

where
e\geq 1
is an integer and
f^{*}(x)=(x^{\prime})^{e}
and

•

has cyclic stabilizer

Note that, if

By definition, the target of an admissible

G
-cover.

Definition 3.2

A

-cover, along with, for each

Given a choice of

by the coset

[

, Lemma 3.4]
, allowing us to define later the source morphism in Definition

We also record the monodromy of the

-cover

at each distinguished marked point. The stabilizer of the

-action at

Let

.

.

The monodromy data at the distinguished marked points of an admissible

We are now ready to define the moduli spaces of admissible

Definition 3.4

Let

The space

Definition 3.5

The

Theorem 3.7
.

[

3.5.
Restriction-corestriction maps

This is the main new feature of spaces of admissible
G
-covers not available in the classical case.

Suppose
X\to Y
is an admissible
G
-cover, so that
Y
is isomorphic to the scheme-theoretic quotient
X/G
. If
G_{1}\subset G
is a subgroup, one can
restrict
to the action of
G_{1}
, that is, remember the admissible
G_{1}
-cover
X\to X/G_{1}
. If
G_{1}\subset G
is normal, then we may also
corestrict
to the induced action of
G/G_{1}
on
X/G_{1}
, remembering the admissible
G/G_{1}
-cover
X/G_{1}\to Y=X/G
.

One must also keep track of the monodromy data in applying these operations. This is essentially done in
[
BR11
, §2.2.2]
, but in our setting with distinguished points in marked
G
-orbits, the definitions require a bit more care.

First, consider the case of restriction to the action of an arbitrary subgroup
G_{1}\subset G
. Upon such a restriction, the
G
-orbits of marked points on
X
break into
G_{1}
-orbits, and one must make a choice of distinguished elements
\{p_{ij}\}
of the
G_{1}
-orbits. This amounts, for
i=1,2,\ldots,b
, to choosing representatives
\langle t_{ij}\rangle
for the orbits of the
G_{1}
-action on
G/\langle h_{i}\rangle
.

The stabilizer
G_{ij}
of the
G
-action at the point
p_{ij}=t_{ij}\cdot p_{i1}
is the cyclic group generated by

t_{ij}h_{i}t_{ij}^{-1}\in G,

acting on
T_{p_{ij}}X\cong\mathbb{C}
by multiplication by
e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\cdot\#G_{ij}}
. (Note that this formula is independent of the choice of
t_{ij}
within a left coset of
\langle h_{i}\rangle
.)

On the other hand, the stabilizer at
p_{ij}
of the
G_{1}
-action is the cyclic group

G^{\prime}_{ij}=G_{ij}\cap G_{1}=\langle t_{ij}h_{i}^{r_{ij}}t_{ij}^{-1}\rangle,

where

r_{ij}=\frac{\#G_{ij}}{\#G^{\prime}_{ij}}.

Similarly to before,
t_{ij}h_{i}^{r_{ij}}t_{ij}^{-1}
acts on
T_{p_{ij}}X\cong\mathbb{C}
by multiplication by
e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\cdot\#G^{\prime}_{p_{ij}}}
.

Once we have chosen distinguished marked points
t_{ij}\cdot p_{i}
for the cover
X\to X/G_{1}
, in order to obtain a point of the stack of admissible
G_{1}
-covers, one needs in addition to order the
G_{1}
-orbits of marked points, or equivalently, the marked points on the quotient
X/G_{1}
.

Therefore, we make the following definition:

Definition 3.18
.

Let
G_{1}\subset G

G
-covers

, ranging over all

. The

\res^{G}_{G_{1}}:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G_{1},\xi^{\prime}}

\res^{G}_{G_{1}}([f:X\to X/G])=[f^{\prime}:X\to X/G_{1}],

with the new monodromy data

h^{\prime}_{ij}=t_{ij}h_{i}^{r_{ij}}t_{ij}^{-1}

at the distinguished marked point

Taking

Taking

G
-cover

to itself, but possibly changing the distinguished marked points in each

-orbit, conjugating the monodromy accordingly, and reordering the
G
-orbits. For general restriction maps, different relabeling data modify

3.6.
Diagonals and non-tautological cycles from
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}

Our goal is to construct a framework including classical tautological classes that also incorporates the cycles obtained from source maps
\phi
, and from restriction-corestriction morphisms more generally. As we explain here,
diagonal
morphisms also play an essential role in the theory. In fact, we have already seen diagonals appear naturally in Remark
3.15
and Definition
3.19
.

We now recall the example of Graber-Pandharipande
[
GP03
]
of a non-tautological cycle arising from admissible
G
-covers, further illustrating the role of diagonal maps.

Let
h\geq 1
be an integer and let
\overline{\mathcal{H}}=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{2h,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},(1)^{2}}
denote the moduli space of admissible
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-covers
f:X\to Y
, where
X,Y
have genus
2h,h
, respectively, and
f
is ramified over two marked points. We have that
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
is a smooth stack of dimension
3h-1
. We have, as usual, a source morphism

\phi:\overline{\mathcal{H}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2h,2}.

We will abusively refer to the map

\phi:\overline{\mathcal{H}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2h}

obtained by forgetting the two ramification points by the same name.

Let
\Gamma
be the stable graph of genus
2h
consisting of two genus
h
vertices connected by a single edge, with associated boundary map

\xi_{\Gamma}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2h}.

Lemma 3.23
.

[
GP03
, Lemma 1]

The class
\xi_{\Gamma}^{*}(\phi_{*}(1))
is a non-zero multiple of the class of the diagonal

\Delta:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h.1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,1}.

More explicitly, the only admissible
\mathbb{Z}/2
-cover
f:X\to Y
whose source lies in the boundary stratum
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}
has
Y=C\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
C
is a genus
h

X=C\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}\cup C
, where the two copies of
C
in
X
map isomorphically to that in
Y
, and the rational component of
X
has degree 2 over that of
Y
.

The existence of odd singular cohomology in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{h,1}
for sufficiently large
h
, as shown by Pikaart
[
Pik95
]
, implies that
\phi_{*}(1)\in H^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2h})
cannot have a tautological Künneth composition upon pullback by
\xi_{\Gamma}
. One may then conclude:

Proposition 3.24
.

[
GP03
, §2]

For all sufficiently large

, we have
\phi_{*}(1)\notin RH^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2h})
.

Similar constructions of non-tautological cycles, including those which already fail to be tautological in Chow, use the existence of odd middle cohomology in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,11}
, see
[
GP03
, Theorem 2]
and
[
vZ18
]
.

In our framework, the computation of Graber-Pandharipande shows that, in order for
\phi_{*}(1)
to be included in a
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebra of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2h}
containing all tautological classes, and more generally, in a system of such
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebras for all spaces of admissible
G
-covers, this system of
\mathbb{Q}
-subalgebras should also include classes coming from general diagonal morphisms

\Delta:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\to\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}\times\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}.

The target of this morphism is a product of isomorphic admissible
G
-cover spaces, and it will therefore be convenient for us to work with
arbitrary
products of such spaces.

3.7.
The
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring

We are now ready to make the main definition of this paper.

-subalgebras

G
-covers and each map

H_{1}
and
H_{2}

Let us spell out the last condition in more detail. Suppose that
(\ell,\ell^{\prime})
is a pair of half-edges comprising an edge and let

be the vertices to which
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
are attached, respectively. Let

be the (unpointed) admissible

-graph with the single vertex

with monodromy data

.

Then, under the restriction of the

-action on

to

(which is a subgroup of

because

), the monodromy datum

becomes
h_{\ell,j}=h_{\ell}^{r_{\ell,j}}
, where

Similarly, we define

. The balancing condition then requires that
h_{\ell,j}=h_{\ell^{\prime},j}^{-1}\in H_{j}
for

We may define a
morphism
(resp. automorphism) of augmented admissible
H
-graphs to be an
H
-equivariant morphism (resp. automorphism) of graphs compatible with the data of the leg orbit orderings, normal subgroups

, and monodromy data

.

The following explains how augmented admissible

-graphs arise when intersecting restriction morphisms.

Lemma 4.25
.

Suppose that

is a closed point of the functor
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,H_{1},\xi_{1}}\times_{\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,G,\xi}}\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{g,H_{2},\xi_{2}}

Each component

-curve, and we obtain monodromy data

Note that the construction of

Remark 4.31
.

The index set for the coproduct can in principle be enumerated by a finite computation. Indeed,

is uniformly bounded in terms of

It remains to compute the top Chern class of the excess bundle on the fiber product. We may do so on each component of the finite unramified cover

We have no combinatorial description of the normal bundles of restriction maps as in the case of boundary maps, but we may compute explicit formulas for the Chern classes of the spaces involved, see §
4.5

5.

5.1.

-tautological ring

Our main theorem is now immediate from the results of the previous section:

Theorem 5.1

Let

-tautological ring is closed under bivariant pullback by all of the aforementionoed tautological morphisms.

Proof.

All such classes are
\mathcal{H}
-tautological by definition. The results of the previous section allow one to compute the bivariant pullback of such a composition by another one, one tautological morphism at a time. At each step, we intersect basic
\mathcal{H}
-tautological morphisms

-tautological class in terms of a space

In particular, the intersection of any two classes of the form

∎

In fact, one can give a smaller set of additive generators for the
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring:

Proposition 5.2
.

The
\mathcal{H}
-tautological ring is generated additively by classes
\gamma_{*}(\theta)
as above, where

is taken to be the composition of diagonal, restriction-corestriction, forgetful, and boundary morphisms,

.

Proof.

This follows from the following claims, whose verifications we leave to the reader.

•

the composition of a boundary, forgetful, or restriction-corestriction map followed by a diagonal map is equal to a composition of a diagonal map followed by maps of the original type,

•

the composition of a boundary map followed by a forgetful map is equal to the composition of forgetful maps followed by a boundary map,

•

the composition of a boundary map followed by a restriction-corestriction map is equal to the composition of restriction-corestriction maps, diagonal maps, and a boundary map, in that order, and

•

the composition of a forgetful map followed by a restriction-corestriction map is equal to a restriction-corestriction map followed by forgetful maps.

∎

(i)

(v)

Combine the previous two steps to compute the Segre class of

Proof of Theorem
6.3

We will need the following lemma. We thank David Yang for providing the proof.

Lemma 6.8
.

We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}am+bn=d\\
a,b,m,n>0\end{subarray}}(mn-am)\min(a,b)=0.

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}am+bn=d\\
a,b,m,n>0\end{subarray}}(mn-bn)\min(a,b)=0.

Proof.

The two statements are clearly equivalent. We prove the first. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}am+bn=d\\
a,b,m,n>0\end{subarray}}m(n-1)\min(a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}am+bn=d\\
a,b,m,n>0\end{subarray}}m(n-a)(\min(a,b)-b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}am+bn=d\\
a,b,m,n>0\\
a<b\end{subarray}}m(n-a)(a-b).

On the index set in the last sum, we have an involution

(a,b,m,n)\mapsto(n,n+m,b-a,a)

on which the terms being summed become
(b-a)(a-n)(-m)
, that is, they change sign. The conclusion is now immediate.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.3
.

We have already obtained the intersection number with
\Delta_{01}
in §
6.3.1
. It remains to combine the above contributions to the intersection number with
\Delta_{00}
. We have the desired intersection number coming from covers of type
(\Delta_{00},\Delta_{01})
; it suffices to show now that the other three contributions cancel. Ignoring the common factors of

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 8\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}am+bn=d\\

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle 8\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}am+bn=d\\

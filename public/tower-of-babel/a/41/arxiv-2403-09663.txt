Exceptional Collections of Line Bundles for Smooth Toric Fano Surfaces and Threefolds
Reginald Anderson

Abstract
. The cellular resolution of the diagonal given by Bayer-Popescu-Sturmfels for unimodular projective toric varieties yields a full, strong exceptional collection of line bundles on unimodular projective toric surfaces. The Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev resolution of the diagonal yields a full, strong exceptional collection of line bundles for 16 of the 18 smooth toric Fano threefolds.



1.
Introduction

We work over an algebraically closed field
k=\overline{k}
of characteristic
0
. Consider the question of whether two varieties
X
and
Y
are isomorphic. This can be answered by either explicitly giving an isomorphism
X\cong Y
, or by showing that no such isomorphism exists. For affine varieties, this is equivalent to asking whether their affine coordinate rings
A(X)
and
A(Y)
are isomorphic. For projective varieties, the homogeneous coordinate rings
S(X)
and
S(Y)
are dependent upon an embedding into
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and thus are no longer isomorphism invariants. Though explicitly giving an isomorphism between varieties
X
and
Y
which are isomorphic can be difficult in general, Bondal-Orlov proved that if
X
is a smooth projective variety which is Fano or general type, the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves
D^{b}_{Coh}(X)
is an isomorphism invariant
[
BO01
]
.

Here, let
X
be a smooth projective toric Fano variety over
k=\overline{k}=\mathbb{C}
, and
D^{b}(X)=D^{b}_{Coh}(X)
its bounded derived category of coherent sheaves. We’ll need the following definitions, which hold in a general
k
-linear triangulated category,
\mathcal{D}
:

Definition 1.1
.

An object
E
of
\mathcal{D}
is
exceptional
if

Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(E,E[\ell])=\begin{cases}k&\ell=0,\\
0&\text{ else }.\end{cases}

Definition 1.2
.

An ordered collection
\mathcal{E}=\{E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{n}\}
of objects is
exceptional
if there are no Hom’s to the left, i.e.,

Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j}[\ell])=0

for
i>j
and all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 1.3
.

An exceptional collection
\mathcal{E}
is
strong
if the only Hom’s which appear in any direction are concentrated in degree 0, i.e.,

Hom_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j}[\ell])=0

for
\ell\neq 0
.

Definition 1.4
.

\mathcal{E}
is
full
if the smallest triangulated subcategory generated by
\mathcal{E}
is all of
\mathcal{D}
.

Since
X
is smooth, projective, and Fano,
D^{b}(X)
determines
X
. In the presence of a strong, full exceptional collection
\mathcal{E}
, call
G=\bigoplus_{i}E_{i}
a generator (or “tilting object”), and let
A=End(G)
. Bondal showed
[
Bon90
]
that there is an exact equivalence

D^{b}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}D^{b}(\text{mod}-A)

from
D^{b}(X)
to right modules over the endomorphism algebra
A
induced from taking
RHom(G,-)
as
k
-linear categories. That is, the presence of a strong, full exceptional collection allows for a nicest possible presentation of
D^{b}(X)
.

King conjectured in unpublished notes that any smooth projective toric variety has a strong, full exceptional collection of line bundles. King’s conjecture was proven false
[
HP06
]

[
Efi14
]
, and is currently checked on a case-by-case basis whether a given smooth projective toric variety has a strong, full exceptional collection of line bundles, and if so, how to construct such a collection. Here we construct a strong, full exceptional collection of line bundles for unimodular projective toric surfaces.

Bayer-Popescu-Sturmfels
[
BPS99
]
gave a cellular resolution of the diagonal for a proper subclass of smooth toric projective varieties which they called unimodular. This cellular resolution
(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{H}_{L}^{\epsilon}/L},\partial^{\epsilon})
also gives a resolution of the diagonal, modulo torsion from the irrelevant ideal of
X_{\Sigma}\times X_{\Sigma}
for smooth toric varieties
[
Andng
]
, and for global quotient toric D-M stacks of a smooth toric variety by a finite abelian group, which correspond to simplicial toric varieties
[
Andnga
]
. There is much recent work in the area of virtual resolutions of the diagonal and of toric subvariety of a smooth projective toric variety: Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev
[
HHL23
]
, Bruce-Cranton Heller-Sayrafi
[
BHS22
]
, Erman-Brown
[
BE23
]
, using the notion of virtual resolution due to Berkesch-Erman-Smith
[
Erm20
]
. This paper focuses on the following theorems.

Theorem 1.5
.

The cellular resolution
(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{H}_{L}^{\epsilon}/L},\partial^{\epsilon})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
yields a strong full, exceptional collection of line bundles for any unimodular projective toric surface.

Here, we give a classification of smooth projective toric surfaces which are unimodular in the sense of Bayer-Popescu-Sturmfels, and show that this classification agrees with the 5 smooth toric Fano surfaces given in Craw
[
Cra07
]
. For smooth projective toric Fano threefolds, we give the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

For 16 of the 18 smooth, projective toric Fano 3-folds, the Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev resolution of the diagonal yields a full, strong, exceptional collection of line bundles.

Theorem
1.6
agrees with Bondal’s observation
[
Bon06
]
that for all smooth toric Fano threefolds except for two, there exists a full strong exceptional collection of line bundles.

2.
Prior results

Here, we follow the conventions of
[
Andng
]
and
[
Andnga
]
, which follow the conventions of Bayer-Sturmfels
[
BS97
]
and Bayer-Popescu-Sturmfels
[
BPS99
]
to resolve the diagonal for a unimodular toric variety. Bayer-Popescu-Sturmfels give a cellular resolution of the Lawrence ideal
J_{L}
corresponding to a unimodular lattice
L
. The lattice
L
given by the image of
B
in the fundamental exact sequence
[
CLS11
]

(1)

0\rightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathbb{Z}^{|\Sigma(1)|}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}Cl(X_{\Sigma})\rightarrow 0,

describes the lattice of principal divisors of the projective toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
. Here
M
gives the lattice of characters for the dense open torus of
X_{\Sigma}
, and we conflate
B
as a matrix with the homomorphism it represents. We adopt Bayer-Popescu-Sturmfels’ notion of unimodularity for the lattice
L
and toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call
L
and
X_{\Sigma}

unimodular
when
B
has linearly independent columns and every maximal minor of
B
lies in the set
\{0,\pm 1\}
.

Throughout this paper, “unimodular” refers to this restrictive condition of Bayer-Popescu-Sturmfels. Unimodular toric varieties form a proper subclass of smooth toric varieties. Let
X_{\Sigma}
have homogeneous coordinate ring
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{\rho}\text{ }|\text{ }\rho\in\Sigma(1)]
graded by
Cl(X_{\Sigma})
via Equation
1
, and
X_{\Sigma}\times X_{\Sigma}
have homogeneous coordinate ring
S=R\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}R
. We use the lattice
L
to construct a hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{H}_{L}
by intersecting
\mathbb{R}L
, the real span of the lattice
L=\text{Im }(B)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, with all lattice translates of the coordinate hyperplanes
\{x_{i}=j\},1\leq i\leq n,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. This gives an infinite cellular complex
\mathbb{R}L\cap\mathcal{H}_{L}
, which we quotient by the action of the lattice
L
to yield the finite cellular complex
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}_{L}\cap L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{H}_{L}\cap L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{H}_{L}\cap L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{H}_{L}\cap L}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}}},
which supports the minimal
Cl(X_{\Sigma})\times Cl(X_{\Sigma})
-graded resolution of

\displaystyle J_{L}=\left<\textbf{x}^{\textbf{a}}\textbf{y}^{\textbf{b}}-\textbf{x}^{\textbf{b}}\textbf{y}^{\textbf{a}}\text{ }|\text{ }\textbf{a}-\textbf{b}\in L\right>=\left<\textbf{x}^{\textbf{u}}\textbf{y}^{\textbf{v}}-\textbf{x}^{\textbf{v}}\textbf{y}^{\textbf{u}}\text{ }|\text{ }\pi(\textbf{u})=\pi(\textbf{v})\text{ in }Cl(X)\right>\subset S

corresponding to
X_{\Sigma}\cong\Delta\subset X_{\Sigma}\times X_{\Sigma}
.

3.
Listing all complete unimodular toric surfaces

The condition that the matrix
B
with row vectors given by primitive ray generators
\{\rho_{i}\}=\Sigma(1)
has linearly independent columns and that all maximal minors lie in
\{0,\pm 1\}
is rather stringent.

Lemma 3.1
.

The list of complete unimodular toric surfaces is

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{2},Bl_{p}\mathbb{P}^{2},Bl_{p,q}\mathbb{P}^{2},Bl_{p,q,r}\mathbb{P}^{2}

either
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
\mathbb{P}^{2}
blown up at any of 0-3 torus invariant points.

Proof.

If
\Sigma\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a complete fan, then the support
|\Sigma|=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\sigma
is all of
N_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that the first ray is
e_{1}
, and that the second lives in the upper half plane. This leaves one of

\{-e_{1},-e_{1}+e_{2},e_{2},e_{1}+e_{2}\}

as a second choice of ray. Since for any cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we have
\sigma\cap(-\sigma)=\{0\}
, the second choice of ray must be one of

\{-e_{1}+e_{2},e_{2},e_{1}+e_{2}\}.

(i) If the second ray is
-e_{1}+e_{2}
, then we need to add in another ray from the lower half-plane to keep all cones strongly convex. We cannot choose
-e_{1}-e_{2}
and keep all maximal minors in
\{0,\pm 1\}
and are forced to choose
-e_{2}
to make all cones strongly convex. This gives
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. From here we can refine the fan iteratively by adding in (up to the action of
SL(2,\mathbb{Z}))

e_{1}-e_{2},-e_{1}+e_{2}
, and
-e_{1}
. These give
Bl_{p}\mathbb{P}^{2},Bl_{p,q}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and
Bl_{p,q,r}\mathbb{P}^{2}
(
\mathbb{P}^{2}
blown up at any of 1-3 torus invariant points), respectively.

(ii) If the second ray chosen is instead
e_{2}
, then to enforce strong convexity of all cones we must choose one of
-e_{1},-e_{2},
or
-e_{1}-e_{2}
, though
-e_{1}-e_{2}
gives
\mathbb{P}^{2}
which was already chosen up to isomorphism. Up to symmetry, it is equivalent to choose either
-e_{1}
or
-e_{2}
, and whichever is not chosen as a third ray would then be chosen as a fourth ray. This gives
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Adding in either of the rays
e_{1}-e_{2}
or
-e_{1}+e_{2}
would return to a previous isomorphism class of a fan given by the same number of rays from (i).

(iii) If the second ray is
e_{1}+e_{2}
, then both
e_{1}-e_{2}
and
-e_{1}+e_{2}
are forbidden by unimodularity to keep all maximal minors in
\{0,\pm 1\}
. One must eventually choose
-e_{1}
or
-e_{1}-e_{2}
, which returns to previously occuring isomorphism classes up to the action of
SL(2,\mathbb{Z})
. There are now no new isomorphism classes of unimodular complete toric varieties which arise. ∎

5.
Cellular resolutions

One guiding principle made apparent by the cellular resolution we use here, as well as the resolution of the diagonal due to Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev
[
HHL23
]
and
X_{A}
, the geometric realization of the classifying space
B(\mathcal{C}_{A})
in the notation of
[
FH22
]
is the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

For a smooth projective toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
, there exists a cellular resolution
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{H}_{L}/L}^{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
supported on a real torus
\mathbb{T}^{m}
.

Proof.

Up to isomorphism of the fan
\Sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists a maximal cone
\sigma\preceq\Sigma
which can be taken to be
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\left<e_{1},\dots e_{n}\right>
. Thus, the quotient
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}_{L}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{L}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{H}_{L}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{L}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{H}_{L}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{L}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{H}_{L}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{L}$}}}
hyperplane arrangement may be taken inside of a fundemental domain which is a Minkowski sum
\sum_{i=1}^{n}[0,e_{i}]=\left[0,1\right]^{n}
with opposite pairs of faces identified, creating the torus
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{M_{\mathbb{R}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{M}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{M_{\mathbb{R}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{M}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{M_{\mathbb{R}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{M}$}}{\raisebox{1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M_{\mathbb{R}}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.70833pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{M}$}}}
.∎

A second proof comes from the construction of the resolution of the diagonal in
[
HHL23
]
which is constructed on the same hyperplane arrangement, and our Theorem 5.1 above is proven explicitly for the resolution of the diagonal bimodule in
[
FH22
]
.

Since the resolution of the diagonal constructed in
[
HHL23
]
is minimal, we use the Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev resolution of the diagonal to investigate the following question:

Question
:
For which smooth toric Fano threefolds and fourfolds does the Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev resolution of the diagonal yield a full strong exceptional collection of line bundles?

6.19.
Discussion

The polytopes F.3D.0001 and F.3D.0000 correspond to II(d) and III(k) in Watanabe-Wanabe’s classification, respectively, which are
Z=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2))
and
W
, the blowup of
Z
along a line, respectively. That a numerical condition for
Z
and
W
fails for the presence of a full, strong exceptional collection of line bundles is given in
[
Bon06
]
.

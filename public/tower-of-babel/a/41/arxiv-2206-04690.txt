Gaussian upper bounds for heat kernels on graphs with unbounded geometry
Matthias Keller, Christian Rose

Abstract
We prove large-time Gaussian upper bounds for continuous-time heat kernels of Laplacians on graphs with unbounded geometry. Our estimates hold for centers of large balls satisfying a Sobolev inequality and volume doubling. Distances are measured with respect to an intrinsic metric with finite distance balls and finite jump size. The Gaussian decay is given by Davies’ function which is natural and sharp in the graph setting. Furthermore, we find a new polynomial correction term which does not blow up at zero. Although our main focus is on unbounded Laplacians, the results are new even for the normalized Laplacian. In the case of unbounded vertex degree or degenerating measure, the estimates are affected by new error terms reflecting the unboundedness of the geometry. Keywords: graph, heat kernel, Gaussian bound, unbounded geometry 2020 MSC: 39A12, 35K08, 60J74

Introduction

First upper bounds for fundamental solutions of parabolic equations go back to the seminal work by Nash, Moser, and Aronson on diverence form operators on

]
.

On weighted graphs with bounded geometry, bounds for continuous-time heat kernels have been studied since the seminal work of Davies and Pang,
[

,

]
. Among many other milestones over the last decades, we mention here the fundamental work of Delmotte on characterizations of upper and lower bounds for heat kernels,
[

]
, see also

[

,

,

,
BCK
,

,

,

.

Our focus lies on heat kernel upper bounds of Laplacians on graphs with unbounded geometry. The Davies–Gaffney–Grigor’yan lemma, i.e., an
\ell^{2}
-heat kernel bound, was proven for possibly unbounded graph Laplacians in
[
BHY17
]
by Bauer, Hua, and Yau in terms of intrinsic metrics. Folz,
[
Fol11
]
, derived off-diagonal Gaussian upper bounds in terms of intrinsic metrics from on-diagonal estimates via Grigor’yan’s two-point method. Such bounds have also been obtained for elliptic operators with possibly unbounded edge weights but bounded means on combinatorial graphs in
[
MO16
,
ADS16
]
.
In a remarkable article, Barlow and Chen,

[
BC16
]
, extended Delmotte’s work on normalized Laplacians, i.e., bounded Laplacians on graphs, to unbounded combinatorial geometry.

Of course, this leads to the question: “Why study graphs of unbounded geometry
at all?“ On the one hand, a general result which does not include any superfluous
assumptions is always desirable from a pure mathematical point of view. This includes the opportunity
to handle diffusion processes on graphs beyond constant speed in a unified framework.
Moreover, from the perspective of potential applications graphs with unbounded vertex degree are quite natural. Indeed, many real life networks (such as social networks, the internet, citation
networks, pandemic transmission, etc.) are distinguished by the fact that they have few vertices
with very high degree (hubs, super spreaders, etc.) while the majority of vertices have a rather
low degree.

In the present article we will prove Gaussian upper bounds for heat kernels of Laplacians on weighted graphs with unbounded geometry. We assume a Sobolev inequality and volume doubling only in large balls encoded via intrinsic metrics and obtain sharp Gaussian bounds for all large times. The exponential factor is given by the graph-specific Gaussian found by Davies
[
Dav93b
]
and includes the long-time asymptotics given by the bottom of the spectrum of the Laplacian. The sharpness can be seen from the correct long-time behavior in terms of the bottom of the spectrum and the sharp estimates for the heat kernel on the integers obtained by Pang. The unboundedness of the geometry enters the estimates for non-normalized Laplacians via a new correction function including means of the vertex degree and inverse of the measure. Moreover, we obtain a new polynomial correction term which is better than the corresponding term on manifolds.

A crucial ingredient are intrinsic metrics which are a powerful concept allowing to deal with unbounded Laplacians on graphs. They have already been used to prove a great variety of results,
[
BKH13
,
HKW13
,
Fol14a
,
Fol14b
,
BKW15
,
Kel15
,
HKS20
,
KLW21
]
. Originally they have been introduced in Sturm’s fundamental work on strongly local Dirichlet forms in
[
Stu94
]
and for general regular Dirichlet forms in
[
FLW14
]
.
While first ideas towards of intrinsic metrics on graphs can be found in
[
Dav93a
]
, where Davies already noticed that the combinatorial metric should be replaced in heat kernel bounds, they appear first explicitly in
[
Fol11
,
GHM12
]
.

Davies also observed that the general lack of chain rule for the gradient on graphs yields a Gaussian exponential which deviates from the one on manifolds,
[
Dav93b
]
. This disparity is also reflected by the short-time behavior of the heat kernel which is different from the well-established Varadhan asymptotics on manifolds,
[
KLVW15
]
. However, the long term behavior of Davies’ Gaussian corresponds asymptotically to the one on manifolds. Our results show that this is also true in the case of unbounded geometry.

Our bounds on the heat kernel depend on new geometric terms which neither appeared so far in the study of bounded Laplacians nor elliptic operators on combinatorial graphs.

1.1
The set-up

The terminology used here follows mostly
[
KLW21
]
. Let
X
be a countable set,
m\colon X\to(0,\infty)
a measure on
X
of full support, and
b\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
be a connected symmetric graph over
(X,m)
. We assume further that
b
is
locally finite
, i.e. the sets
\{y\in X\mid b(x,y)>0\}
are finite for all
x\in X
. The space of all real-valued continuous functions on
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(X)
. Furthermore,
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denotes the norm in the space
\ell^{p}(X,m)
,
p\in[1,\infty]
. For
f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and
x,y\in X
, we abbreviate

\nabla_{xy}f:=f(x)-f(y),

and let
\Delta\colon\mathcal{C}(X)\to\mathcal{C}(X)
be the Laplace operator given by

\Delta f(x)=\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\nabla_{xy}f,

where
f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
and
x\in X
and we interpret the right-hand side as divergence form operator.
By local finiteness,
\Delta
maps the compactly supported functions
\mathcal{C}_{c}(X)
into itself and therefore the restriction of
\Delta
to
\mathcal{C}(X)
is a symmetric operator on
\ell^{2}(X,m)
. By slight abuse of notation we also denote the Friedrichs realization of
\Delta
in
\ell^{2}(X,m)
by
\Delta
and set

\Lambda:=\inf\spec(\Delta).

The heat semigroup of
\Delta
acting on
\ell^{2}(X,m)
will be denoted by
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, i.e.,
P_{t}=\mathrm{e}^{-t\Delta}
,
t\geq 0
. By discreteness of
X
the heat semigroup has a kernel
p\colon[0,\infty)\times X\times X\to[0,\infty)
, called the
heat kernel
,
which satisfies for all
f\in\ell^{2}(X,m),x\in X,t\geq 0

P_{t}f(x)=\sum_{x\in X}m(x)p_{t}(x,y)f(y).

Furthermore,
u=P_{t}f
solves the heat equation for
f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)

\frac{d}{dt}u=-\Delta u,\hskip 10.00002ptu(0,\cdot)=f.

Our heat kernel estimates will be formulated in terms of an
intrinsic metric
with respect to
b
on
(X,m)
which is a pseudo-metric
\rho\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
such that

\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)\rho^{2}(x,y)\leq m(x),

for all
x\in X
.
In the following,
\rho
will always be a non-trivial intrinsic (pseudo-) metric. The jump size is given by

S:=\sup\{\rho(x,y)\colon x,y\in X,b(x,y)>0\}>0.

Example 1.1
.

For a given graph, the pseudo-metric,

\displaystyle\rho(x,y)=\inf_{x=x_{0}\sim\ldots\sim x_{k}=y}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{1}{\Deg(x_{j})}\wedge\frac{1}{\Deg(x_{j+1})}\right)^{1/2}\wedge S,

for
x,y\in X

always gives an intrinsic metric with bound on the jump size by
S
.
Note that the combinatorial distance is intrinsic if and only if the corresponding Laplacian is bounded, cf.
[
KLW21
, Lemma 11.22]
.

For
R\geq 0
and
x\in X
, we denote the distance balls with respect to
\rho
by

B(R):=B_{x}(R):=\{y\in X\colon\rho(x,y)\leq R\}.

A standing assumption on the intrinsic metric is the following.

Assumption 1.1
.

The distance balls with respect to the intrinsic metric are compact and the jump size is finite.

For
x\in X
and
f\in\mathcal{C}(X)
we define

|\nabla f|(x):=\Bigg(\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)(\nabla_{xy}f)^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The combinatorial interior of a set
A\subset X

will be denoted by

\displaystyle A^{\circ}=\{x\in A\colon b(x,y)=0\mbox{ for all }y\in X\setminus A\}.

The results presented in this article are obtained by assuming the classical Sobolev inequality and volume doubling assumptions in terms of intrinsic metrics. In contrast to the typical assumptions on manifolds we require these properties on annuli with positive inradius instead of small balls.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
x\in X
,
R_{2}\geq R_{1}\geq 0
,
n>2
, and
d>0
.

(i)

The
Sobolev inequality

S(n,R_{1},R_{2})
holds in
x
, if there is a constant
C_{S}>0
such that for all
R\in[R_{1},R_{2}]
,
u\in\mathcal{C}(X)
,
\supp u\subset B(R)^{\circ}
, we have

\frac{m(B(R))^{\frac{2}{n}}}{C_{S}R^{2}}\|u\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}^{2}\leq\||\nabla u|\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

We abbreviate
S(n,R_{1}):=S(n,R_{1},R_{1})
.

(ii)

The
volume doubling
property
V(d,R_{1},R_{2})
is satisfied in
x
if there exists
C_{D}>0
such that

m(B(r_{2}))\leq C_{D}\left(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\right)^{d}m(B(r_{1})),\hskip 20.00003ptR_{1}\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq R_{2}.

(iii)

The property
SV(R_{1},R_{2})=SV(d,n,R_{1},R_{2})
holds in
x
if the Sobolev inequality
S(n,R_{1},R_{2})
and volume doubling
V(d,R_{1},R_{2})
hold.

Remark 1.3
.

We can replace
V(d,R_{1},R_{2})
by the property
V^{\ast}(d,R_{1},R_{2})
:

m(B(2r))\leq C_{D}^{\ast}m(B(r)),\hskip 20.00003ptr\in[R_{1},R_{2}].

V(d,R_{1},R_{2})
implies
V^{\ast}(d,R_{1},R_{2})
and
V^{\ast}(d,R_{1},R_{2})
implies
V(d,R_{1},R_{2}/2)
, however, with different constants.
Due to this asymmetry the assumption
V(d,R_{1},R_{2})
is more natural.

1.2
Three special cases of the main result

Our main result is Theorem
6.1
which shows Gaussian upper heat kernel bounds for all Laplacians in all vertices satisfying
SV(R_{1},R_{2})
for

and a certain

In this section we present exemplarily special cases in terms of the measure

As initially observed by Davies,
[

]
, instead of the Gaussian
\mathrm{e}^{-r^{2}/4t}

,

is the jump size of the intrinsic metric.

Let
\deg(x):=\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)
,
x\in X
. Our first choice is the so-called normalized Laplacian, what refers to the choice

m=\deg
. The combinatorial distance
\rho_{\mathrm{c}}
is an intrinsic metric with jump size
1
for this choice of the measure. For this model we have the following.

Theorem 1.4

(Normalizing measure)
.

Let
m=\deg
and

d>0
,

,

R/2\geq r\geq 42
, and
Y\subset X
. Assume
that for all
x\in Y

The function

appears naturally in the graph setting,
[

,
Pan93
,
Del99

BHY15
,
BHY17

We have

i.e., the limit of the quotient is one, justifying the name Gaussian behavior.

Our results are sharp in a certain sense.
First of all, for the normalized Laplacian on
\mathbb{Z}

[

that the behavior of

is essentially exact. Secondly, the term

[

The exponential decay starts after time

[

Our estimates refine Davies’ a priori bound
[
Dav93b
]
and the Gaussian upper bounds obtained in
[
BC16
]
for the normalized Laplacian. Moreover, it refines the sharp Davies-Gaffney-Grigor’yan Lemma of
[

,
BHY17
]
even for unbounded Laplacians as can be seen below.

Next, we discuss the correction terms. Rather surprising is the polynomial correction term in time and space: it is easy to see that for
r\geq 0,t>0

\left(1\vee\sqrt{t^{2}+r^{2}}-t\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\leq\left(1\vee\frac{r^{2}}{t}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}.

The right-hand side of this inequality is the polynomial correction term appearing in similar estimates on manifolds, cf.
[
Stu95
,
Gri09
]
. Here, we obtain a better correction term on graphs which satisfies

\left(1\vee\sqrt{t^{2}+r^{2}}-t\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\to 1\vee r^{\frac{n}{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\to 0,

and

\left(1\vee\sqrt{t^{2}+r^{2}}-t\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\to 1,\hskip 10.00002ptt\to\infty.

As mentioned above, an upper bound for the heat kernel of the normalized Laplacian with unbounded combinatorial geometry for large times has been obtained by Barlow and Chen,
[
BC16
]
. There, the authors assume the Poincaré inequality and volume doubling for all balls with fixed center and radii in some given interval whose lower bound is large enough. Additionally, they assume that the volume of balls is comparable from above in terms of the measure of its center. Our result only uses the Sobolev inequality and volume doubling on large balls instead and gives an essentially optimal bound. Moreover, our estimates hold for all times larger than a certain threshold.

Anchored upper bounds for heat kernels of elliptic operators with possibly unbounded edge weights have been obtained in
[

]

However, their hypotheses are imposed on the underlying combinatorial graph.
The authors assume a weaker Sobolev inequality and Ahlfors regularity anchored at a fixed vertex for all large balls. In a subsequent work

1.3
Strategy and discussion of the proof

Our proof relies on a variant of Davies’ method,
[
Dav87
]
, which is a nowadays well-established strategy to prove upper heat kernel bounds. We combine this with a new
\ell^{2}
-mean value inequality, (MV
2
) for short, for unbounded graph Laplacians.

Davies
[
Dav87
]
showed that off-diagonal upper bounds are a consequence of on-diagonal upper bounds. To this end, for the semigroup
P
of an elliptic operator, he considers the sandwiched semigroup
P^{\omega}=e^{-\omega}Pe^{\omega}
for certain test functions
\omega.
As on-diagonal heat kernel bounds are equivalent to
\log
-Sobolev inequalities, it suffices to derive such inequalities for the sandwiched operator from the given one. This yields pointwise on-diagonal bounds for the kernel
P^{\omega}
and optimizing over
\omega
leads to the desired off-diagonal bounds. The approach reduces the problem to prove on-diagonal upper bounds for the sandwiched heat kernel which was pursued in a multitude of articles, including
[
Dav93b
,
Dav93a
,
Pan93
,
CKS87
,
Zhi13
,
ADS16
]
.

Our approach is based on a somewhat similar observation. We show that (MV
2
) for
P^{\omega}
on a space-time cylinder
I\times B
implies upper heat kernel bounds in terms of operator norms of
P^{\omega}
for bounded
\omega
. The integrated maximum principle for Lipschitz functions obtained in
[
BHY17
, Lemma 3.3]
then gives control on the norms of the semigroups. From there on we follow Davies’ idea from
[
Dav93b
]
and optimize the resulting bound with respect to the Lipschitz constants. This yields an upper heat kernel bound containing the graph-specific Gaussian term
\mathrm{e}^{-\zeta_{S}(\rho,t)}
and additional terms depending on the geometry of
I\times B
. This is then implemented in Section
5
.

Next, we derive (MV
2
) for the sandwiched operator depending on the geomety of the graph. The
different aspects of the unbounded geometry pose the main challenges in the proof of (MV
2
).
First, in contrast to
[
Del99
]
, the combinatorial geometry is not assumed to be bounded, and the Sobolev inequality and volume doubling only hold for radii larger than a given threshold. Secondly, due to the unbounded weighted vertex degree and inverse measure, the Laplacian is a priori neither bounded nor uniformly elliptic. Hence, we cannot apply results from
[
CKS87
,
BC16
,
MO16
]
. Moreover, we do not assume that the functions
D_{p}
and
M_{p}
are bounded.

In order to prove the (MV
2
) we adapt the classical Moser iteration scheme,
[
Mos64
]
, which was already applied to graphs in, e.g.,
[
Del99
]
or
[
ADS16
]
. That is, we iterate
\ell^{p}
-norms of (sub/super-) solutions of the sandwiched heat equation with
p\to\infty
on balls
B(R)
with radius shrinking from radius
R
to radius
R/2
.

Contrary to the continuum, the iteration procedure cannot be carried out indefinitely: if one iterates the radii, say along the sequence
R_{k}=(1+2^{-k})R/2
,
k\to\infty
, it is not clear that the number of vertices contained in
B(R_{k})
decreases in every step. In
[
Del99
]
and
[
ADS16
]
, this was resolved by case distinction. Hence, the geometric restrictions on the graph have to be assumed a priori on the whole domain of iteration. This gives no control on the first appearance of a geometric influence of the small-scale graph structure on the (MV
2
).

We resolve this problem by splitting the proof of (MV
2
) into two parts: First, we iterate subsolutions in space-time cylinders as long as balls for a specific sequence of radii shrink in each step. This yields a bound on the
\ell^{p}
-norm of subsolutions on the smallest ball attained by this procedure, where
p
depends on the maximal number of iteration steps. Second, starting from this particular
p
, we iterate in time to the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm of the solution while keeping the space fixed.

For the first part of the proof, we derive maximal inequalities in distance balls for subsolutions. The key are suitable cut-off functions whose existence is guaranteed by the properties of the intrinsic metric. The maximal inequalities together with the Sobolev inequality yield the initial iteration step, where the radius of the ball shrinks by jump size. Then we iterate this inequality in the interval
[R/2,R]
, giving the maximal value for
p
. This can be found in Section
2
.

In the second part, we iterate supersolutions instead of subsolutions in time on fixed space. To this end, we prove maximal inequalities for supersolutions, where the constants now depend on
D_{p}
and
M_{p}
. We use these maximal inequalities to obtain an
\ell^{\kappa}
-mean value inequality via iteration, where
\kappa
depends on
p
, independent of
SV(R_{1},R_{2})
. The proof of this procedure can be found in Section
3
.

Combining the first and second part in Section
4
yields the desired (MV
2
).

Finally, in Section
6
we obtain our main result Theorem
6.1
and derive the heat kernel bound from the (MV
2
) for the sandwiched operators via our abstract theorem obtained in Section
5
. The proofs of Theorems
1.4
,
1.5
, and
1.6
can be found there as well.

2.1

For a
\rho

\Delta_{\omega}v(x):=\mathrm{e}^{\omega(x)}\Delta(\mathrm{e}^{-\omega}v)(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pth(\omega):=\sup_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in X}\frac{b(x,y)}{m(x)}|\nabla_{xy}\mathrm{e}^{\omega}\nabla_{xy}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega}|.

In this paragraph,
v\geq 0
denotes a non-negative subsolution of
\partial_{t}+\Delta_{\omega}
on
[0,\infty)\times X
, i.e.,

\frac{d}{dt}v+\Delta_{\omega}v\leq 0.

The following lemma is a variant of
[
ADS16
, Lemma 2.2]
. Since we deal with different norms and operators, we give a complete proof for reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a closed non-empty interval,
B\subset X
finite,
\phi\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
with
0\leq\phi\leq\mathbf{1}_{B\setminus\partial_{i}B}
,
p\geq 1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi v_{t}^{p}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\phi|\nabla v_{t}^{p}|\|_{2}^{2}\leq 166p^{2}\left(h(\omega)+\||\nabla\phi|\|_{\infty}^{2}\right)\|\Eins_{B}v_{t}^{p}\|_{2}^{2}.

To simplify notation in the following we write for
f\in\ell^{1}(X,m)

\displaystyle\sum_{X}mf=\sum_{x\in X}m(x)f(x).

Proof.

We write
\nabla:=\nabla_{xy}
and
\psi:=\mathrm{e}^{\omega}
. (Recall that
(\nabla f)^{2}\in\mathcal{C}(X\times X)
given by
(\nabla f)^{2}(x,y)=(f(x)-f(y))
, which is not to be confused with
|\nabla f|^{2}\in\mathcal{C}(X)
with
|\nabla f|^{2}(x)=\sum_{y\in X}b(x,y)(\nabla f)^{2}(x,y)
.)
Use the subsolution property and non-negativity of
v
, the definition of
\Delta_{\omega}
, and Green’s formula to get

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2p}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{X}m\phi^{2}v_{t}^{2p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{X}m\phi^{2}v_{t}^{2p-1}\Delta_{\omega}v_{t}=\sum_{X}m\phi^{2}v_{t}^{2p-1}\psi\Delta(\psi^{-1}v_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in X}b(x,y)(\nabla\phi^{2}v_{t}^{2p-1}\psi)(\nabla\psi^{-1}v_{t}).

We conclude statement by applying the following
pointwise
estimate to the right-hand side of the inequality above. Specifically, the term
\mathbf{1}_{B}
enters via the observation that
\nabla\phi
and
\av(\phi^{2})
are supported on
B\times B
.

Claim
.

For any
x,y\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla(\phi^{2}v_{t}^{2p-1}\psi)\nabla(\psi^{-1}v_{t})\!\geq\!\frac{1}{2p}\av(\phi^{2})(\nabla v_{t}^{p})^{2}\!-\!(6+160p)\big(|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|\av(\phi^{2})\!+\!(\nabla\phi)^{2}\big)\av(v_{t}^{2p}),

where
\av(f):=\frac{1}{2}(f(x)+f(y))
.

Proof of the claim.

We have
\nabla(fg)=\av(f)\nabla g+\av(g)\nabla f
. Thus,

\displaystyle(\nabla\phi^{2}v_{t}^{2p-1}\psi)(\nabla\psi^{-1}v_{t})=\av(\phi^{2})T_{1}(x,y)+(\nabla\phi^{2})T_{2}(x,y),

where

T_{1}(x,y):=(\nabla v_{t}^{2p-1}\psi)(\nabla\psi^{-1}v_{t}),\hskip 10.00002ptT_{2}(x,y):=\av(v_{t}^{2p-1}\psi)(\nabla\psi^{-1}v_{t}).

We bound
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
from below and start with
T_{1}
.
Expand using the product rule and apply
\av(\psi)\av(\psi^{-1})=1-\frac{1}{4}\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}
and
\av(\psi)(\nabla\psi^{-1})=-\av(\psi^{-1})(\nabla\psi)
to get

\displaystyle T_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\av(\psi)\av(\psi^{-1})\nabla v_{t}^{2p-1}\nabla v_{t}+\av(v_{t})\av(v_{t}^{2p-1})\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\av(\psi^{-1})\av(v_{t}^{2p-1})\nabla\psi\nabla v_{t}+\av(\psi)\av(v_{t})\nabla v_{t}^{2p-1}\nabla\psi^{-1}

\displaystyle=\nabla v_{t}^{2p-1}\nabla v_{t}+\bigg(\av(v_{t})\av(v_{t}^{2p-1})-\frac{1}{4}\nabla v_{t}^{2p-1}\nabla v_{t}\bigg)\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\av(\psi)\nabla\psi^{-1}\bigg(\av(v_{t})\nabla v_{t}^{2p-1}-\av(v_{t}^{2p-1})\nabla v_{t}\bigg)=:S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}.

We bound
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
from below and start with
S_{1}
, where we just apply the elementary inequality (cf.
[
ADS16
, (B.1)]
)

(a^{2p-1}-b^{2p-1})(a-b)\geq\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}(a^{p}-b^{p})^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt,a,b\geq 0,\ p>1/2

to obtain

S_{1}\geq\frac{2p-1}{p^{2}}(\nabla v^{p})^{2}.

Now, we estimate
S_{2}
. Bound it by its negative modulus and apply the inequalities
\nabla v^{2p-1}\nabla v\leq 2\av(v^{2p})
and
\av(v^{\alpha})\av(v^{\beta})\leq\av(v^{\alpha+\beta})
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
as a consequence of Young’s inequality, to get

S_{2}\geq-\frac{3}{2}\av(v^{2p})|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|.

Last but not least, we estimate
S_{3}
from below. First, bound it by its negative modulus. Apply the elementary inequality (cf.
[
ADS16
, (B.2)]
)

|a^{2p-1}b-b^{2p-1}a|\leq\frac{p-1}{p}|a^{2p}-b^{2p}|,\hskip 10.00002pta,b\geq 0,\ p\geq 1,

to the second factor to obtain

\displaystyle|\av(v_{t})\nabla v_{t}^{2p-1}-\av(v_{t}^{2p-1})\nabla v_{t}|

\displaystyle=|v_{t}(x)^{2p-1}v_{t}(y)-v_{t}(y)^{2p-1}v_{t}(x)|\leq\frac{2(p-1)}{p}|\av(v_{t}^{p})\nabla v_{t}^{p}|.

Note that
\av(\psi)\av(\psi^{-1})=1+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|
implies

|\av(\psi)\nabla\psi^{-1}|=\sqrt{\av(\psi)\av(\psi^{-1})}\sqrt{|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|}=\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|}\sqrt{|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|}.

The above identity and Young’s inequality
ab\leq\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}/\epsilon+\epsilon b^{2})
,
\epsilon>0

|S_{3}|
yield

\displaystyle|S_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\av(\psi)\av(v_{t}^{p})|\nabla\psi^{-1}\nabla v_{t}^{p}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(1+\frac{1}{4}|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|\right)(\nabla v_{t}^{p})^{2}+2\epsilon|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|\av(v_{t}^{p})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{2(p-1)}{p}(\nabla v_{t}^{p})^{2}+\frac{2(p-1)}{p}|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|\left(\frac{1}{4\epsilon}(\nabla v_{t}^{p})^{2}+2\epsilon\av(v_{t}^{p})^{2}\right).

By applying
\av(v^{p})^{2}\leq 4\av(v^{2p})
,
(\nabla v^{p})^{2}\leq 2\av(v^{2p})
collecting all terms, and choosing
\epsilon=2p
we obtain

T_{1}(x,y)\geq S_{1}+S_{2}+S_{3}\geq\frac{1}{p}(\nabla v_{t}^{p})^{2}-(2+32p)|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|\av(v_{t}^{2p}),

since
3/2+2(p-1)/4p^{2}+32(p-1)\leq 2+32p
.

Now we bound
(\nabla\phi^{2})T_{2}
from below. Expand the factors of
T_{2}
by the product rule and bound the resulting summands from below by their negative moduli. Use the inequalities
\av(\psi v_{t}^{2p-1})\leq 2\av(\psi)\av(v_{t}^{2p-1})
and
\av(v^{\alpha})\av(v^{\beta})\leq\av(v^{\alpha+\beta})

to obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla\phi^{2})T_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(\nabla\phi^{2})\av(v_{t}^{2p-1}\psi)(\nabla\psi^{-1}v_{t})

\displaystyle\geq-2|\nabla\phi^{2}\av(\psi)\nabla\psi^{-1}|\av(v^{2p})-2\av(\psi)\av(\psi^{-1})|\nabla\phi^{2}\av(v^{2p-1})\nabla v|.

Apply
\nabla\phi^{2}=2\av(\phi)\nabla\phi
, the elementary inequality (cf.
[
ADS16
, (B.3)]
)

(a^{2p-1}+b^{2p-1})|a-b|\leq 4|a^{p}-b^{p}|(a^{p}+b^{p}),\hskip 10.00002pta,b\geq 0,\ p\geq\frac{1}{2},

and Young’s inequality to the modulus involved in the second summand to get

\displaystyle|\nabla\phi^{2}\av(v^{2p-1})\nabla v|

\displaystyle\leq 8\av(\phi)\av(v^{p})|\nabla\phi\nabla v^{p}|\leq\frac{4}{\delta}\av(\phi)^{2}(\nabla v^{p})^{2}+4\delta(\nabla\phi)^{2}\av(v^{p})^{2}.

Apply
\nabla\phi^{2}=2\av(\phi)\nabla\phi
,

|\av(\psi)\nabla\psi^{-1}|=\sqrt{-\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}}\sqrt{\av(\psi)\av(\psi^{-1})}
,
and Young’s inequality to the first summand to get

\displaystyle|\nabla\phi^{2}\av(\psi)\nabla\psi^{-1}|

\displaystyle=2|\av(\phi)\nabla\phi|\sqrt{-\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}}\sqrt{\av(\psi)\av(\psi^{-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\av(\phi)^{2}|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|+(\nabla\phi)^{2}\av(\psi)\av(\psi^{-1}).

Plugging in the above estimates, rearranging, and using

\displaystyle(\nabla\phi^{2})T_{2}(x,y)\geq-\frac{8}{\delta}\av(\phi)^{2}(\nabla v^{p})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-\left(\left(2+8\delta\right)(\nabla\phi)^{2}+|\nabla\psi\nabla\psi^{-1}|\left(\left(2+\frac{4}{\delta}\right)\av(\phi)^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}+2\delta\right)(\nabla\phi)^{2}\right)\right)\av(v^{2p}).

\av(\phi^{2})\leq 2\av(\phi)^{2}\leq 2\av(\phi^{2})

and choosing

\displaystyle\frac{m(B(R_{2}))^{\frac{2}{n}}}{C_{S}R_{2}^{2}}\left(\sum_{B(R_{1})}mw_{t}^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq\frac{m(B(R_{2}))^{\frac{2}{n}}}{C_{S}R_{2}^{2}}\left(\sum_{B(R_{2})}m(\phi w_{t})^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}|\nabla(\phi w_{t})|\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{R_{2}^{2}}\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}\phi w_{t}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{B(R_{2})}m|\nabla\phi|^{2}w_{t}^{2}+2\!\!\sum_{x\in B(R_{2})}m|\nabla w_{t}|^{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{1}{R_{2}^{2}}\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}w_{t}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}|\nabla w_{t}|\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{2}{(R_{2}-R_{1}-S)^{2}}+\frac{1}{R_{2}^{2}}\right)\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}w_{t}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\left[\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}|\nabla w_{t}|\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{(R_{2}-R_{1}-S)^{2}}\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}w_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\right].

where we use Proposition
2.4
applied to
\phi
in the fourth inequality.
Hence, we get

\frac{m(B(R_{2}))^{\frac{2}{n}}}{C_{S}R_{2}^{2}}\frac{1}{T_{3}-T_{2}}\int\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\sum_{B(R_{1})}m_{B(R_{1})}w_{t}^{2\alpha}\ \mathrm{d}t\\
\leq 3\cdot\frac{1}{m(B(R_{1}))}\frac{1}{T_{3}-T_{2}}\max_{t\in[T_{2},T_{3}]}\left\|\Eins_{B(R_{1})}w_{t}\right\|_{2}^{\frac{2}{n}}\\
\cdot\int\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\left(\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}|\nabla w_{t}|\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{(R_{2}-R_{1}-S)^{2}}\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}w_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t.

Now, let
w=v^{p}
. We observe

\|\Eins_{B(R_{1})}v_{t}^{p}\|_{2}\leq\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}v_{t}^{p}\|_{2}
and we apply Lemma
2.5
to this term as well as to
\|\Eins_{B(R_{2})}|\nabla v_{t}^{p}|\|_{2}^{2}
with
the choice of radii
R_{3}-S>R_{2}
. Collecting the remaining factors into the constant
C_{0}
yields the statement.
∎

The theorem below shows that the Moser iteration procedure delivers a weaker result than in the continuum setting. More precisely, instead of a bound for the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm of a subsolution in terms of its
\ell^{2}
-norm, it allows only to bound a certain
\ell^{p}
-norm of subsolutions. The value

Theorem 2.7

(Moser iteration in time and space)
.

Let
x\in X

T
,

,

For all non-negative
\Delta_{\omega}

\left(\frac{1}{2\delta(R/2)^{2}}\int\limits\limits_{T-\delta(R/2)^{2}}^{T+\delta(R/2)^{2}}\sum_{B(R/2)}m_{B(R/2)}v_{t}^{2\alpha^{K}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\alpha^{-K}}\\

By assumption on

.

Together with Lemma
2.1

B(\rho_{k+1})\subset B(\rho_{k}-S)\subset B(\rho_{k})^{\circ},\hskip 10.00002ptk\in\{0,\ldots,K\}.

Hence, we can iterate
K
times.
We apply Proposition
2.6
to

and

,

,

V(d,R/2,R)

for radii

Finally, this implies using

and the estimates on the three factors of

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\delta R^{2}}\left(\frac{\rho_{K}^{2}}{(R/2)^{2}}\frac{m(B(\rho_{K}))}{m(B(R/2))}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{K}}}\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}C_{0,k}^{\frac{1}{\alpha^{k+1}}}

Remark 2.8
.

One may wonder about the choice for the chosen radii used for the iteration procedure, since usually diadic iteration steps are used. In fact, our choice yields faster convergence of the error function
\Gamma
introduced in Section

, in the diadic case it is only

3
Moser iteration in time and fixed space

In this paragraph we let
(B,\mu)
be a discrete measure space
with finite measure and the norm will be abbreviated by

\|\cdot\|_{p}:=\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(B,\mu)}\hskip 20.00003ptp\in[1,\infty].

For
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
q
the Hölder conjugate of
p
, i.e.,

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
,
where
q=1
if
p=\infty
. Below, we will apply these results to
B=B(R)
and
\mu=m/m(B(R))
.

3.1
Maximal inequalities for supersolutions on fixed space

The following lemma can be seen as an interpolation lemma for the embedding operator between discrete
\ell^{p}
spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have for all
v\in\mathcal{C}(B)

\|v\|_{\infty}\leq\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}\|v\|_{q}.

Proof.

Hölder’s inequality yields for all
p\in[1,\infty]

\sup_{B}|v|\leq\sum_{B}\mu\frac{1}{\mu}|v|\leq\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}\|v\|_{q}.\hfill\qed

The lemma below gives a variant of the maximal inequality for supersolutions in contrast to one for subsolutions in Lemma
2.5
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,

T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq T_{3}<T_{4}
, and
v\geq 0
a bounded
\Delta_{\omega}
-supersolution on
[T_{1},T_{4}]\times B
. Then, for any
s\geq 1
, we have

\max_{[T_{2},T_{3}]}{\|v^{s}\|}_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{T_{4}-T_{3}}+s\|\Deg\|_{p}\right)\int\limits\limits_{T_{1}}^{T_{4}}{\|v_{\tau}^{s}\|}_{q}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Proof.

We have for any
t\in(T_{1},T_{4})

\frac{d}{dt}v_{t}^{s}=sv_{t}^{s-1}\frac{d}{dt}v_{t}\geq-sv_{t}^{s-1}\Delta_{\omega}v_{t}\geq-s\Deg v_{t}^{s}.

By continuity we can choose
t^{\ast}\in[T_{2},T_{3}]
with

\sum_{B}\mu v_{t^{\ast}}^{s}=\max_{t\in[T_{2},T_{3}]}\sum_{B}\mu v_{t}^{s}.

Therefore, for any
\tau\in[t^{\ast},T_{3}]
, we have by Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\sum_{B}\mu v_{\tau}^{s}-\sum_{B}\mu v_{t^{\ast}}^{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{B}\mu(v_{\tau}^{s}-v_{t^{\ast}}^{s})=\sum_{B}\mu\int\limits_{t^{\ast}}^{\tau}\frac{d}{dt}v_{t}^{s}\mathrm{d}t=\int\limits_{t^{\ast}}^{\tau}\sum_{B}\mu\frac{d}{dt}v_{t}^{s}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq-s\int\limits_{t^{\ast}}^{\tau}\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{t}^{s}\Deg\ \mathrm{d}t.

Integrating with respect to
\tau
from
t^{\ast}
to
T_{4}
, and setting
L:=T_{4}-t^{\ast}
yields

\displaystyle L\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{t^{\ast}}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits\limits_{t^{\ast}}^{T_{4}}\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{\tau}^{s}\ \mathrm{d}\tau+s\int\limits\limits_{t^{\ast}}^{T_{4}}\int\limits\limits_{t^{\ast}}^{\tau}\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{t}^{s}\Deg\ \mathrm{d}t\ \mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{4}}\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{\tau}^{s}\ \mathrm{d}\tau+sL\int\limits\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{4}}\!\!\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{t}^{s}\Deg\ \mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\left({\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}+s{L}\left(\sum_{B}\mu\Deg^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\int\limits\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{4}}\left(\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{\tau}^{sq}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathrm{d}\tau,

where the last step follows from Hölder’s inequality applied to both terms. This yields the claim after dividing by
L\geq T_{4}-T_{3}
.
∎

3.2
The iteration steps

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty]
and
T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq T_{3}<T_{4}
,
\beta\in(1,1+\tfrac{1}{q})
, and
v\geq 0
a bounded
\Delta_{\omega}
-supersolution on
[T_{1},T_{4}]\times B
. Then, we have, for all
s\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\int\limits\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\big\|v_{t}^{s\beta}\big\|_{q}\mathrm{d}t\leq\left[s\left(\frac{1\vee\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{T_{4}-T_{3}}+\|\Deg\|_{p}\right)\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}^{q}\right]^{\beta-1}\left(\int\limits\limits_{T_{1}}^{T_{4}}\left\|v_{t}^{s}\right\|_{q}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\beta}.

Proof.

We have to distinguish between the cases
p\in(1,\infty)
and
p=\infty
.

\mathbf{1^{\mathrm{st}}}
case:
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Let
\gamma:=\tfrac{q}{1-q(\beta-1)}
. We apply Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\tilde{p}=1/((\beta-1)q)
and
\tilde{q}=\gamma/q
, i.e.,
1/\tilde{p}+1/\tilde{q}=1
,

\displaystyle\int\limits\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\big\|v_{t}^{s\beta}\big\|_{q}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int\limits\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\big\|v_{t}^{s(\beta-1)q}v_{t}^{sq}\big\|_{1}^{\frac{1}{q}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\big\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|_{1}^{(\beta-1)}\big\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|_{\gamma}\mathrm{d}t\leq\max_{[T_{2},T_{3}]}\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|_{1}^{(\beta-1)}\int\limits\limits_{T_{1}}^{T_{4}}\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|_{\gamma}\mathrm{d}t.

We estimate the integrand of the second factor on the right hand side. We apply Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
3.1
, i.e.,
\|v_{t}^{s}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}\|v_{t}^{s}\|_{q}
,

\displaystyle\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{t}^{s(\gamma-q)}v_{t}^{sq}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\leq\big\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|_{\infty}^{(1-\frac{q}{\gamma})}\|v_{t}^{s}\|_{q}^{\frac{q}{\gamma}}\leq\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}^{(1-\frac{q}{\gamma})}\|v_{t}^{s}\|_{q}^{(1-\frac{q}{\gamma})+\frac{q}{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}^{q(\beta-1)}\|v_{t}^{s}\|_{q}

as
(1-\frac{q}{\gamma})=q(\beta-1)
. We conclude by estimating the first factor with
Lemma
3.2

\displaystyle\max_{[T_{2},T_{3}]}\|v^{s}\|_{1}\leq\left(\frac{\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{T_{4}-T_{3}}+s\|\Deg\|_{p}\right)\int\limits\limits_{T_{1}}^{T_{4}}\|v_{t}^{s}\|_{q}\mathrm{d}t.

\mathbf{2^{\mathrm{nd}}}
case:
p=\infty
.
The proof works for all
\beta>1
, but for simplicity we restrict ourselves to
\beta\in(1,2)
. We closely follow the arguments of the first case.
We expand
\beta=(\beta-1)+1
and obtain

\displaystyle\int\limits\limits_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}\big\|v_{t}^{s\beta}\big\|_{1}\mathrm{d}t\leq\max_{[T_{2},T_{3}]}\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|_{\infty}^{(\beta-1)}\int\limits\limits_{T_{1}}^{T_{4}}\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|_{1}\mathrm{d}t

Now, we obtain the statement as Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
give

\max_{[T_{2},T_{3}]}{\|v_{t}^{s}\big\|}_{\infty}\leq\max_{[T_{2},T_{3}]}{\|v_{t}^{s}\|}_{1}\leq\left(\frac{1}{T_{4}-T_{3}}+\|\Deg\|_{\infty}\right)\int\limits\limits_{T_{1}}^{T_{4}}{\|v_{t}^{s}\|}_{1}\mathrm{d}t.\hfill\qed

The Moser iteration in time will be applied after iteration in space and time cannot be continued due to discreteness of the space. In the next section we apply the theorem below with
B=B(R)
and
\mu=m_{B(R)}
for some
R\geq 0
.

Theorem 3.4

(Moser iteration in time)
.

Fix real numbers
\delta>0
,
T\geq 0
,
p\in(1,\infty]
,
\beta\in(1,1+\tfrac{1}{q})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
v\geq 0
a bounded
\Delta_{\omega}
-supersolution on
[(1-\delta)T,(1+\delta)T]\times B
.
Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{[(1-\delta/2)T,(1+\delta/2)T]\times B}\!\!v^{2}\leq G_{B}\left(\frac{1}{2\delta T}\int\limits\limits_{(1-\delta)T}^{(1+\delta)T}\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{t}^{2\beta^{k}q}\ \mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k}q}}\!\!\!,

where
G=G_{B,\mu}(\delta,R,T,r,\beta)
is given by

\displaystyle G=C_{\beta}\left[\left(1\vee\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\delta T\|\Deg\|_{p}\right)\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k}}},

and
C_{\beta}:=4^{((4+1/\ln\beta)+\beta/(\beta-1))/(\beta-1)}
.

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, denote

\rho_{k}:=\left(1+2^{-k}\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\delta T}{2}}.

Then, we have

\rho_{0}=\sqrt{\delta T},\hskip 20.00003pt\rho_{k}\to\sqrt{\frac{\delta T}{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptk\to\infty.

Lemma
3.3
yields for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with

T_{1}=T-\rho_{k}^{2},\hskip 10.00002ptT_{2}=T-\rho_{k+1}^{2},\hskip 10.00002ptT_{3}=T+\rho_{k+1}^{2},\hskip 10.00002ptT_{1}=T+\rho_{k}^{2},

and
s=2\beta^{k}
the inequality

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\rho_{k+1}^{2}}\int\limits\limits_{T-\rho_{k+1}^{2}}^{T+\rho_{k+1}^{2}}\|v_{t}^{2\beta^{k+1}}\|_{q}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k+1}}}\leq C_{p,k}^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k+1}}}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\rho_{k}^{2}}\int\limits\limits_{T-\rho_{k}^{2}}^{T+\rho_{k}^{2}}\|v_{t}^{2\beta^{k}}\|_{q}\mathrm{d}t\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k}}}

with

\displaystyle C_{p,k}=\frac{\rho_{k}^{2\beta}}{\rho_{k+1}^{2}}\left[2\beta^{k}\left(\frac{1\vee\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\rho_{k}^{2}-\rho_{k+1}^{2}}+\|\Deg\|_{p}\right)\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}^{q}\right]^{\beta-1}.

We will iterate this inequality. But first we estimate
C_{p,k}
.
By the definition of the radii
\rho_{k}=\left(1+2^{-k}\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\delta T}{2}}
, we have since
\beta<2

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{k}^{2\beta}}{\rho_{k+1}^{2}}=\left(\frac{\delta T}{2}\right)^{\beta-1}\frac{(1+2^{-k}(\sqrt{2}-1))^{2}}{(1+2^{-(k+1)}(\sqrt{2}-1))^{2}}\leq\left(\frac{\delta T}{2}\right)^{\beta-1}2^{2\beta}\leq 16(T\delta)^{\beta-1}

and

\rho_{k}^{2}-\rho_{k+1}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{6}2^{-k}\delta T.

These estimates yield, using
\beta<2
and hence
16\cdot 12\cdot 2^{2k(\beta-1)}\leq 4^{k+5}

\displaystyle C_{p,k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,k}^{\prime}:=4^{k+5}\left[\left(1\vee\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\delta T\|\Deg\|_{p}\right)\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}^{q}\right]^{\beta-1}.

Using
\delta/2\geq\rho_{k+1}^{2}
and Hölder’s inequality, we infer from iterating the above inequality for any
l\in\mathbb{N}

\left(\int\limits\limits_{(1-\delta/2)T}^{(1+\delta/2)T}{\|v_{t}^{2\beta^{k+l}}\|}_{1}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k+l}}}\\
\leq\left(2\rho_{k+l}^{2}\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k+l}}}\left(\frac{1}{2\rho_{k+l}^{2}}\int\limits\limits_{T-\rho_{k+l}^{2}}^{T+\rho_{k+l}^{2}}{\|v_{t}^{2\beta^{k+l}}\|}_{q}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k+l}}}\\
\leq\left(2\rho_{k+l}^{2}\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k+l}}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}(C_{p,k+j-1}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k+j}}}\left(\frac{1}{2\rho_{k}^{2}}\int\limits\limits_{T-\rho_{k}^{2}}^{T+\rho_{k}^{2}}{\|v_{t}^{2\beta^{k}}\|}_{q}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k}}}.

Letting
l\to\infty
, the left-hand side converges to the supremum of
v^{2}
. Since
\rho_{k}
is bounded and
\mu(B)<\infty
, the first factor on the right-hand side converge to 1. Applying Jensen’s inequality to the integral on the right-hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{[(1-\frac{\delta}{2})T,(1+\frac{\delta}{2})T]}\!\!\!\!v^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{2\rho_{k}^{2}}\int\limits\limits_{T-\rho_{k}^{2}}^{T+\rho_{k}^{2}}\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{t}^{2\beta^{k}q}\ \mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k}q}}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{2\delta T}\int\limits\limits_{(1-\delta)T}^{(1+\delta)T}\sum_{B}\mu\ v_{t}^{2\beta^{k}q}\ \mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k}q}}

as
2\rho_{k}^{2}\geq\delta T
and
[T-\rho_{k}^{2},T+\rho_{k}^{2}]\subset[(1-\delta)T,(1+\delta)T]
, where

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(C_{p,k+j-1}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{\beta^{k+j}}}

\displaystyle=4^{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{k+j}{\beta^{k+j}}+4\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta^{k+j}}}\left[\left(1\vee\mu(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\delta T\|\Deg\|_{p}\right)\|\mu^{-1}\|_{p}^{q}\right]^{\frac{\beta-1}{\beta^{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta^{j}}}\!\!.

Now the statement follows since
\beta>1
and using
k\beta^{-k}\leq 1/\ln\beta
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta^{-j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\beta^{-j}-1=\frac{1}{\beta-1}=\left(1-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{j}{\beta^{j}}=\left(1-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{j}{\beta^{j}},

and thus
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{k+j}{\beta^{k+j}}+4\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta^{k+j}}\leq((4+1/\ln\beta)+\beta/(\beta-1))/(\beta-1).

∎

4
\mathbf{\ell^{2}}
-mean value inequality for solutions

In case of a local Dirichlet form it suffices to prove subsolution estimates and the corresponding Moser steps to obtain an
\ell^{2}
-mean value inequality for solutions of the heat equation. On graphs, the iteration of subsolutions over space and time comes to a natural stop depending on the radius and the jump size. To obtain an
\ell^{2}
-mean value inequality for graphs, we iterate estimates for supersolutions in constant space afterwards. This iteration over time alone then yields error terms which capture the unbounded geometry of the graph. We encountered them in the previous section as the error term
G_{B}
and, next, we define them in the form in which they enter the main result, Theorem
6.1
, below.

We set for
\beta\in(1,1+1/q)

\theta(r):=\frac{1}{2\beta^{\kappa(r)}},\hskip 20.00003pt\kappa(r):=\left\lfloor\sqrt{\frac{r}{4S}}-2\right\rfloor.

Define the error-function
\Gamma_{x}(r):=\Gamma_{x}(r,p,n,\beta)\geq 0
by

\Gamma_{x}(r)=\left[\left(1+r^{2}D_{p}\left(r\right)\right)M_{p}\left(r\right)^{q}m\left(B\left(r\right)\right)^{q}\right]^{\theta(r)},

where
D_{p}(r)=D_{p}(x,R)
and
M_{p}(r)=M_{p}(x,r)
are the
p
-averages of
\mathrm{Deg}
and
m^{-1}
over
B(r)=B_{x}(r)
.
Indeed, the first factor including
D_{p}
and
M_{p}
captures the unboundedness of the geometry, while the second factor is a volume correction term. Note that in the setting we have in mind, the volumes of balls are growing polynomially, such that this second factor will be bounded as both
\theta
and
\eta
are exponentially decreasing.

Remark 4.1

(Properties of
\theta
and
\Gamma
)
.

It is not hard to see that we have

\theta(r)\asymp\beta^{-\sqrt{r/4S}}=e^{-\gamma\sqrt{r}}

with
\gamma=\ln\beta/\sqrt{4S}>0
.
The function
\Gamma_{x}
can easily be estimated if we have some additional control on the volume growth of distance balls, what is a natural assumption in the present setting.
If we assume
V(d,R_{1},R_{2})
in
x
, we get for all
r\in[R_{1},R_{2}]

\displaystyle m(B(r))^{q\theta(r)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left(\frac{m(B(R_{1}))}{R_{1}^{d}}\right)r^{d}\right)^{q\theta(r)}\lesssim\left(\frac{m(B(R_{1}))}{R_{1}^{d}}\right)^{q\theta(r)},

where we used volume doubling in the first line and boundedness of the function
r\mapsto r^{q\theta(r)}
in the second. This yields

\displaystyle\Gamma_{x}(r)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{r\in[R_{1},R_{2}]}\left[\left(1+r^{2}D_{p}\left(r\right)\right)M_{p}\left(r\right)^{q}\right]^{\theta(r)}\cdot\left(\frac{m(B(R_{1}))}{R_{1}^{d}}\right)^{q\theta(r)}.

The first term is clearly bounded in terms of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
if
[R_{1},R_{2}]
is compact. If
R_{2}=\infty
, we might restrict to
x\in X
satisfying

r^{2}D_{p}(r)M_{p}(r)^{q}\lesssim\exp\left(\exp\left(\gamma{\sqrt{r}}\right)\right)

on
[R_{1},\infty)
, what is satisfied, e.g., if
D_{p}
and
M_{p}
grow polynomially. Then we get

\displaystyle\Gamma_{x}(r)\leq C(x,R_{1}).

Theorem
2.7
and Theorem
3.4
applied to balls with the averaging measure imply the following
\ell^{2}
-mean value inequality.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
x\in X
,
d>0
,
n>2
,
p\in(1,\infty]
,
\alpha=1+\tfrac{2}{n}
,

\beta=1+\frac{1}{n\vee 2q},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{
and
}\hskip 10.00002ptR\geq 8S\left(\frac{\ln q}{\ln\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}+3\right)^{2}.

Assume
SV(R/2,R)
in

. Then, for

,

, and non-negative
\Delta_{\omega}
-solutions

\displaystyle\sup_{[T-\tau R^{2}/8,T+\tau R^{2}/8]\times B(R/2)}v^{2}\leq\frac{C_{d,n,\beta}\Gamma(R/2)^{2}(1+\tau R^{2}h(\omega))^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}{\tau^{\frac{n}{2}+1}R^{2}m(B(R))}\int\limits\limits_{T-\tau R^{2}}^{T+\tau R^{2}}\sum_{B(R)}m\ v_{t}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}t,

Clearly,

We obtain the statement since the function

5
Davies method with
\mathbf{\ell^{2}}
-mean value inequality

In this section we derive upper heat kernel bounds from an
\ell^{2}
mean value inequality for the sandwiched Laplacian.

For the semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of the Laplacian
\Delta
on
\ell^{2}(X,m)
and
\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(X)
, we let

P_{t}^{\omega}:=\mathrm{e}^{\omega}P_{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega}.

Then,
(P_{t}^{\omega})_{t\geq 0}
is also a semigroup of bounded operators on
\ell^{2}(X,m)
.
For
f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)
, the map
t\mapsto P_{t}^{\omega}f
solves the
\ell^{2}
-Cauchy problem for the operator
\mathrm{e}^{\omega}\Delta\mathrm{e}^{-\omega}
, i.e.,

\frac{d}{dt}P_{t}^{\omega}f=-\mathrm{e}^{\omega}\Delta\mathrm{e}^{-\omega}P_{t}^{\omega}f,

since
P_{t}f
is the solution of the
\ell^{2}
-Cauchy problem for
\Delta
.
For a bounded linear operator
A\colon\ell^{p}(X,m)\to\ell^{q}(X,m)
, we denote the operator norm by
\|A\|_{p,q}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
T\geq 0
. Assume that for
i\in\{1,2\}
there exist
x_{i}\in X
,
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
, and
\phi\colon\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\times\ell^{\infty}(X)\to(0,\infty)
such that for
f\in\ell^{2}(X),f\geq 0
, and
\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(X)

\phi(\omega,x_{i})^{2}(P_{T}^{\omega}f)(x_{i})^{2}\leq\int\limits_{a_{i}}^{b_{i}}\|P_{t}^{\omega}f\|_{2}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}t.

Then we have for the heat kernel of
\Delta

p_{2T}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\inf_{\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(X)}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\omega(x_{2})-\omega(x_{1})}}{\phi(\omega,x_{1})\phi(-\omega,x_{2})}\left(\int\limits_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int\limits_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}}\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(X)
.
One directly calculates

\displaystyle p_{2T}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\omega(x_{2})-\omega(x_{1})}\|\Eins_{\{x_{1}\}}P_{2T}^{\omega}\Eins_{\{x_{2}\}}\|_{1,\infty}.

In order to estimate the norm, observe that duality implies

\|P_{T}^{\omega}\Eins_{\{x_{2}\}}\|_{1,2}=\|\Eins_{\{x_{2}\}}P_{T}^{-\omega}\|_{2,\infty}.

The semigroup property implies

\displaystyle\|\Eins_{\{x_{1}\}}P_{2T}^{\omega}\Eins_{\{x_{2}\}}\|_{1,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Eins_{\{x_{1}\}}P_{T}^{\omega}\|_{2,\infty}\|P_{T}^{\omega}\Eins_{\{x_{2}\}}\|_{1,2}=\|\Eins_{\{x_{1}\}}P_{T}^{\omega}\|_{2,\infty}\|\Eins_{\{x_{2}\}}P_{T}^{-\omega}\|_{2,\infty},

so we are left to estimate the last two norms.
For
f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)
,
f\geq 0
,
and
\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(X)
the assumption readily gives

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},\omega)^{2}P_{T}^{\omega}f(x_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}t\ \|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Now for general
f\in\ell^{2}(X,m)
we decompose
f=f_{+}-f_{-}
, where
f_{\pm}=\max\{0,\pm f\}
. Since
P_{T}
is positivity preserving and
(c-d)^{2}\leq c^{2}+d^{2}
,
c,d\geq 0
, the estimate above yields

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},\omega)^{2}P_{T}^{\omega}f(x_{1})^{2}\leq\phi(x_{1},\omega)^{2}P_{T}^{\omega}f_{+}(x_{1})^{2}+\phi(x_{1},\omega)^{2}P_{T}^{\omega}f_{-}(x_{1})^{2}\leq\int\limits_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\|f\|_{2}^{2}.

Since
\|\Eins_{\{x_{1}\}}P_{T}^{\omega}f\|_{\infty}^{2}=P_{T}^{\omega}f(x_{1})^{2}
, we obtain

\|\Eins_{\{x_{1}\}}P_{T}^{\omega}\|_{2,\infty}\leq\phi(x_{1},\omega)^{-1}\left(\int\limits_{a_{1}}^{b_{1}}\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

An analogous bound holds if we replace
x_{1}
,
a_{1}
,
b_{1}
,
\omega
by
x_{2}
,
a_{2}
,
b_{2}
,
-\omega
which gives

\displaystyle\|\Eins_{\{x_{2}\}}P_{T}^{-\omega}\|_{2,\infty}\leq\phi(x_{2},-\omega)^{-1}\left(\int\limits_{a_{2}}^{b_{2}}\|P_{T}^{-\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\ \mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This finishes the proof since
\|P_{t}^{-\omega}\|_{2,2}=\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}
because
P_{t}^{-\omega}
is the adjoint
P_{t}^{\omega}
.
∎

The upper bound in Lemma
5.1
depends on the best choice of a function in a minimizaton problem. Davies,
[
Dav93b
]
following upon
[
Dav87
]
, chose a certain family of Lipschitz functions and minimized with respect to the Lipschitz constant. We will use this argument as well.

To this end, we need an integrated maximum principle for graphs with intrinsic metric from
[
BHY17
]
. We say that
\omega\in\mathcal{C}(X)
is
\kappa
-Lipschitz if
\omega
is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the intrinsic metric
\rho
with Lipschitz constant
\kappa
. Recall that

denotes the jump size of the intrinsic metric.

Lemma 5.2

(
[
BHY17
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Let
\kappa\geq 0
and

. For any
\kappa
-Lipschitz function

, the function

\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty),\hskip 10.00002pt\ t\mapsto\exp\left(2\Lambda t-\frac{2}{S^{2}}\left(\cosh\left(\kappa S\right)-1\right)t\right)\ \|\mathrm{e}^{\omega}P_{t}f\|_{2}^{2}

is non-increasing.

Note that the constant
2S^{-2}(\cosh(\kappa S)-1)
is in fact an upper bound for

defined in Section
2
if
\omega
is
\kappa
-Lipschitz as shown in the proof of Theorem
5.3
. Moreover, it is the reason for the specific form of the function

below.

The following theorem provides a heat kernel upper bound for a fixed time on subsets of a graph where an
\ell^{2}
-mean value inequality holds. To this end, recall the function
\zeta_{S}
defined for
r\geq 0
,

by

\zeta_{S}(r,t)=\frac{1}{S^{2}}\left(rS\arsinh\left(\frac{rS}{t}\right)+t-\sqrt{t^{2}+r^{2}S^{2}}\right).

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
T>0
,

Y\subset X
,

a,b\colon Y\to[0,\infty)
,
a\leq b
,
\phi\colon Y\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

Then we have
for all

\displaystyle p_{2T}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(b(x)-a(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}(b(y)-a(y))^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(\frac{b(x)+b(y)-2T}{2}\sigma(\rho(x,y),2T)\right)}{\phi\big(x,\sigma(\rho(x,y),2T)\big)\phi\big(y,\sigma(\rho(x,y),2T)\big)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\cdot\exp\big(-\Lambda(a(x)+a(y))-\zeta_{S}(\rho(x,y),2T)\big),

where

\sigma(r,t):=2S^{-2}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{r^{2}S^{2}}{t^{2}}}-1\right).

Proof.

For
\kappa\geq 0
, let

E_{\kappa}:=\{\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(X)\colon\omega\ \mbox{ is }\kappa\text{-Lipschitz}\}\subset\ell^{\infty}(X).

Lemma
5.1
implies for all

p_{2T}(x,y)\leq\inf_{\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(X)}I_{1}\cdot I_{2}\leq\inf_{\kappa>0}\inf_{\omega\in E_{\kappa}}I_{1}\cdot I_{2},

with

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\omega(y)-\omega(x)}}{\phi(x,h(\omega))\phi(y,h(-\omega))},\hskip 10.00002ptI_{2}:=\left(\int\limits_{a(x)}^{b(x)}\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\!\left(\int\limits_{a(y)}^{b(y)}\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\!.

To estimate

, we basically use Davies’ idea from
[
Dav93b
, Theorem 10]
. We choose

\nu:=\min\{\rho(x,\cdot),\rho(x,y)\}

and for
\kappa\geq 0

\omega:=-\kappa\nu.

We clearly have
\nu\in E_{1}
,
\omega\in E_{\kappa}
, and

\displaystyle\omega(y)-\omega(x)=-\kappa\rho(x,y).

With these choices we bound
h(\omega)
. First, a direct calculation gives

\displaystyle|\nabla_{xy}\mathrm{e}^{\omega}\nabla_{xy}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega}|=2(\cosh(\kappa\rho(x,y))-1).

Since the map

we get for

[

Plugging in these estimates and using that

\displaystyle h(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in X}\sum_{x\in X}\frac{b(x,y)}{m(x)}|\nabla_{xy}\mathrm{e}^{\omega}\nabla_{xy}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega}|=2\sup_{x\in X}\sum_{x\in X}\frac{b(x,y)}{m(x)}(\cosh(\kappa\rho(x,y))-1)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{x\in X}\sum_{x\in X}\frac{b(x,y)}{m(x)}\frac{\rho(x,y)^{2}}{S^{2}}(\cosh(\kappa S)-1)\leq 2S^{-2}(\cosh(\kappa S)-1)=:\gamma(\kappa S).

Using

and the monotonicity properties of

, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\omega(y)-\omega(x)}}{\phi(x,h(\omega))\phi(y,h(-\omega))}\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa\rho(x,y)}}{\phi(x,\gamma(\kappa S))\phi(y,\gamma(\kappa S))}.

Now, we estimate

. Let

with

\displaystyle=\|\mathrm{e}^{\omega}P_{t}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega}f\|_{2}^{2}=\Phi(t)\mathrm{e}^{-2\Lambda t+2S^{-2}\left(\cosh\left(\kappa S\right)-1\right)t}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2\Lambda t+\gamma(\kappa S)t}.

Since

, we get for
z\in\{x,y\}

\displaystyle\int\limits_{a(z)}^{b(z)}\|P_{t}^{\omega}\|_{2,2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{a(z)}^{b(z)}\mathrm{e}^{-2\Lambda t+\gamma(\kappa S)t}\mathrm{d}t\leq(b(z)-a(z))\mathrm{e}^{-2\Lambda a(z)+\gamma(\kappa S)b(z)}.

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq(b(x)-a(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}(b(y)-a(y))^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\Lambda(a(x)+a(y))+\frac{b(x)+b(y)}{2}\gamma(\kappa S)\right).

Now we put everything together.
Set

r:=\rho(x,y),\hskip 10.00002ptB:=b(x)+b(y),

F(\theta):=\inf_{\eta>0}f(\eta,\theta),\hskip 10.00002ptf(\eta,\theta):=\left(-\eta+\frac{1}{\theta}(\cosh(\eta)-1)\right).

The value
\eta_{\theta}=\arsinh(\theta)=:\kappa_{\theta}S
is the minimum of the function
\eta\mapsto f(\eta,\theta)
,
\theta>0
.
Plugging in the minimizer
\eta_{0}:=\eta_{\theta_{0}}
for the choice
\theta=\theta_{0}:=rS/2T
yields

\displaystyle\inf_{\kappa>0}\inf_{\omega\in E_{\kappa}}I_{1}\cdot I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\kappa>0}\!\frac{(b(x)-a(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}(b(y)-a(y))^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\phi(x,\gamma(\kappa S))\phi(y,\gamma(\kappa S))}\exp\left(\!\!-\Lambda(a(x)+a(y))\!+\!\left(\!\!-\kappa r+\frac{2T+B-2T}{2}\gamma(\kappa S)\right)\!\!\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\kappa>0}\!\frac{(b(x)-a(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}(b(y)-a(y))^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\phi(x,\gamma(\kappa S))\phi(y,\gamma(\kappa S))}\exp\left(\!\!-\Lambda(a(x)+a(y))\!+\!\frac{r}{S}f\left(\kappa S,\frac{rS}{2T}\!\right)\!+\!\frac{B-2T}{2}\gamma(\kappa S)\!\!\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(b(x)-a(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}(b(y)-a(y))^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\phi(x,\gamma(\eta_{0}))\phi(y,\gamma(\eta_{0}))}\exp\left(-\Lambda(a(x)+a(y))+\frac{r}{S}F\left(\frac{rS}{2T}\right)+\frac{B-2T}{2}\gamma(\eta_{0})\right).

We are left with simplifying the remaining terms. The hyperbolic Pythagorean theorem yields
\cosh(\arsinh(\theta))=\sqrt{1+\theta^{2}}
. Hence,

\gamma(\eta_{0})=2S^{-2}\left(\cosh\left(\arsinh\left(\frac{rS}{2T}\right)\right)-1\right)=2S^{-2}\left(\sqrt{1+\frac{r^{2}S^{2}}{4T^{2}}}-1\right)=\sigma(r,2T).

Moreover, as
\eta_{\theta}=\arsinh(\theta)
minimizes
f(\cdot,\theta)
, we get

\displaystyle F(\theta)=\frac{1}{\theta}\left(\sqrt{1+\theta^{2}}-1\right)-\arsinh\left(\theta\right).

Since

\frac{r}{S}F\left(\frac{rS}{2T}\right)=\frac{r}{S}\frac{2T}{rS}\left(\sqrt{1+{\frac{rS}{2T}}^{2}}-1\right)-\frac{r}{S}\arsinh\left(\frac{rS}{2T}\right)=-\zeta_{S}(r,2T),

the claim follows.
∎

Main result: Gaussian upper bounds

Recall that for
x\in X
the error function
\Gamma_{x}\geq 0
is defined by

\Gamma_{x}(r)=\left[\left(1+r^{2}D_{p}\left(r\right)\right)M_{p}\left(r\right)^{q}m\left(B\left(r\right)\right)^{q}\right]^{\theta(r)},

where
\theta(r)\asymp\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma\sqrt{r}}
. The following theorem is our main result.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
I
be an arbitrary index set,
x_{i}\in X
,
d_{i}>0
,
n_{i}>2
,
p_{i}\in(1,\infty]
,
q_{i}
the Hölder conjugate of
p_{i}
,

\alpha_{i}=1+\frac{2}{n_{i}},\hskip 10.00002pt\beta_{i}=1+\frac{1}{n_{i}\vee 2q_{i}},\hskip 10.00002ptR_{0,i}:=8S\left(\frac{\ln q_{i}}{\ln\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\beta_{i}}}+3\right)^{2},

and
R_{i}\geq 2r_{i}\geq 2R_{0,i}
. Assume
that for all
i\in I

x_{i}
satisfies

. Then for all
i,j\in I

and
t\geq 8\max\{r_{i}^{2},r_{j}^{2}\}
we have

p_{t}(x_{i},x_{j})\leq C\Gamma_{x_{i}}(\tau_{i})\Gamma_{x_{j}}(\tau_{j})\\
\cdot\frac{\left(1\vee S^{-2}\left(\sqrt{t^{2}+\rho_{ij}^{2}S^{2}}-t\right)\right)^{\frac{n_{ij}}{2}}}{\sqrt{m(B_{x_{i}}(\sqrt{t}\wedge R_{i}))m(B_{x_{j}}(\sqrt{t}\wedge R_{j}))}}\mathrm{e}^{-\Lambda(t-\frac{1}{2}(t\wedge R_{i}^{2}+t\wedge R_{j}^{2}))-\zeta_{S}\left(\rho_{ij},t\right)},

where

\rho_{ij}:=\rho(x_{i},x_{j}),\hskip 10.00002ptn_{ij}=\frac{n_{i}+n_{j}}{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\tau_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{t}{8}}\wedge\frac{R_{k}}{2},\hskip 10.00002ptk\in\{i,j\},

and

C=2^{3+n_{ij}+d_{i}+d_{j}}\mathrm{e}C_{D,i}C_{D,j}\sqrt{C_{d_{i},n_{i},\beta_{i}}C_{d_{j},n_{j},\beta_{j}}},

where
C_{D,k}
is the doubling constants of Definition
1.2
(ii), and
C_{d_{k},n_{k},\beta_{k}}
is given by Theorem
4.2
,
k\in\{i,j\}
.

Proof.

Let
i\in I
. Since
x_{i}
satisfies

SV(R/2,R)
for all
R\in[2r_{i},R_{i}]
.
If

f\geq 0

is a solution on
[0,\infty)\times X
. Hence, Theorem
4.2
implies for all
\delta\in(0,1]
,
T\geq\delta R^{2}
, and
\omega\in\ell^{\infty}(X)

\displaystyle P_{T}^{\omega}f(x_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(t,x)\in[T-\delta R^{2}/8,T+\delta R^{2}/8]\times B_{x_{i}}(R/2)}(P_{t}^{\omega}f)(x)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{i}\Gamma_{x_{i}}(R/2)^{2}(1+\delta R^{2}h(\omega))^{\frac{n}{2}+1}}{\delta^{\frac{n_{i}}{2}+1}R^{2}m(B_{x_{i}}(R))}\int\limits\limits_{T-\delta R^{2}}^{T+\delta R^{2}}\|\Eins_{B_{x_{i}}(R)}P_{t}^{\omega}f\|_{2}^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

where we
set
C_{i}:=C_{d_{i},n_{i},\beta_{i}}
.
For
T>0
, set

r_{i}^{\prime}:=\left(\sqrt{T}\wedge R_{i}\right)\vee 2r_{i}\in[2r_{i},R_{i}].

If
T\geq 4r_{i}^{2}
, then we have
T-\delta r_{i}^{\prime 2}\geq 0
. Set

Y=\{x_{i},x_{j}\}
and for
k\in\{i,j\}

a(x_{k})=T-\delta r_{k}^{\prime 2},\hskip 10.00002ptb(x_{k})=T+\delta r_{k}^{\prime 2},\hskip 10.00002ptr(x_{k})=r_{k}^{\prime},

and
\phi(x_{k},h(\omega))>0
given by

\phi(x_{k},h(\omega))^{-2}=\frac{C_{k}\Gamma_{x_{k}}(r_{k}^{\prime}/2)^{2}}{\delta^{\frac{n_{i}}{2}+1}r_{k}^{\prime 2}m(B_{x_{k}}(r_{k}^{\prime}))}(1+\delta r_{k}^{\prime 2}h(\omega))^{\frac{n}{2}+1}.

5.3

for

, and

\displaystyle p_{t}(x_{i},x_{j})=p_{2T}(x_{i},x_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(b(x_{i})-a(x_{i}))^{\frac{1}{2}}(b(x_{j})-a(x_{j}))^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(\frac{b(x_{i})+b(x_{j})-2T}{2}\sigma(\rho(x_{i},x_{j}),2T)\right)}{\phi\left(x_{i},\sigma(\rho(x_{i},x_{j}),2T)\right)\phi\left(x_{j},\sigma(\rho(x_{i},x_{j}),2T)\right)}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\cdot\exp\left(-\Lambda(a(x_{i})+a(x_{j}))-\zeta_{S}(\rho(x_{i},x_{j}),2T)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{\prime}\Gamma_{x_{i}}(r_{i}^{\prime}/2)\Gamma_{x_{j}}(r_{j}^{\prime}/2)\delta^{\frac{-n_{ij}}{2}}}{\sqrt{m(B_{x_{i}}(r_{i}^{\prime}))m(B_{x_{j}}(r_{j}^{\prime}))}}\left(1+\delta r_{i}^{\prime 2}\sigma(\rho_{ij},t)\right)^{\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+\delta r_{j}^{\prime 2}\sigma(\rho_{ij},t)\right)^{\frac{n}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}

Now, we estimate the last exponential term.

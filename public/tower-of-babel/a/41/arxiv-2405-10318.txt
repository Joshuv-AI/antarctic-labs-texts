Gauge theory of giant phonon magnetic moment in doped Dirac semimetals
Wenqin Chen, Xiao-Wei Zhang, Ying Su, Ting Cao, Di Xiao, Shi-Zeng Lin

Abstract
We present a quantum theory of phonon magnetic moment in doped Dirac semimetals. Our theory is based on an emergent gauge field approach to the electron-phonon coupling, applicable to both gapless and gapped systems. We find that the magnetic moment is directly proportional to the electrical Hall conductivity through the phonon Hall viscosity. Our theory is combined with the first-principles calculations, allowing us to quantitatively implement it to realistic materials. Magnetic moments are found to be on the order of Bohr magneton for certain phonon modes in graphene and Cd 3 ​ As 2 \text{Cd}_{3}\text{As}_{2} . Our results provide practical guidance for the dynamical generation of large magnetization in the topological quantum materials.

The mechanism of time-reversal symmetry (TRS) breaking is formulated in a topological Chern-Simons term, which appears as a phonon Hall viscosity modifying the phonon dynamics. We find that the phonon magnetic moment is directly linked to the Hall conductivity through the phonon Hall viscosity. Our results provide a theoretical framework to calculate the phonon magnetic moment from the basic properties of crystal structure and electronic transport. .— We start from a general model for Dirac semimetals with electron-phonon (

Phonon Hall viscosity
.— We move on to discuss the topological quantum field theory of the emergent gauge field. Integrating out the fermionic degrees of freedom in the system defined by Eq. (
2
), the quantum corrections from the Dirac fermions give rise to a Chern-Simons (CS) term in the effective action, if the gauge fields are (2+1)-dimensional
[
2
,
47
]
. For the EM gauge field
\bm{A}
, the CS term is
S_{\text{CS}}[\bm{A}]=\sigma_{xy}/2\int{d^{3}x\epsilon^{ijk}A_{i}\partial_{j}A_{k}}
. It describes a Hall conductivity response that reads
J_{i}\equiv-\delta S_{\text{CS}}[\bm{A}]/\delta A_{j}=\sigma_{xy}\epsilon_{ij}E_{j}
. The CS term can also be generalized to (3+1) dimesional Weyl semimetals, because the Weyl semimetals can be constructed by stacking Chern insulators in the momentum space
[
48
]
. Since the emergent gauge field
\bm{a}^{\nu}
couple to the Dirac fermions equivalently as
\bm{A}
, we write a CS term of the same form associated to
\bm{a}^{\nu}

S_{\text{CS}}[\bm{a}^{\nu}]=\frac{\sigma_{xy}}{2}\int{d^{3}x}\epsilon^{ijk}a^{\nu}_{i}\partial_{j}a_{k}^{\nu}.

(3)

The valley index
\chi
is dropped out because
S_{\text{CS}}[\bm{a}^{\nu}]
is quadratic in
\bm{a}^{\nu}
, which indicates the contributions from electrons at two valleys add up. We identify
S_{\text{CS}}[\bm{a}^{\nu}]
as the effective term describing the phonon Hall viscosity
[
2
,
49
]
. This response is universal in topologically nontrivial systems since it is directly linked to the Hall conductivity
\sigma_{xy}
. The coefficient of phonon Hall viscosity
\eta_{H}
is the antisymmetric part of a general viscosity tensor
\eta_{ijkl}

[
50
]
. It is dissipationless and exists only when TRS is broken.

As we show next, the phonon Hall viscosity will modify the phonon dynamics. For the sake of simplicity, here we restrict ourselves to a model of phonons defined by the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\text{ph}}=(\rho_{I}/2)[(\dot{Q}^{\nu}_{x})^{2}+(\dot{Q}^{\nu}_{y})^{2}-(\omega_{0}^{\nu}Q^{\nu}_{x})^{2}-(\omega_{0}^{\nu}Q^{\nu}_{y})^{2}]
, where
\rho_{I}
is the ionic mass density,
Q^{\nu}_{x,y}
are the linearly polarized phonon displacements, and
\omega_{0}^{\nu}
is the
\Gamma
-point(
\bm{q}=0
) frequency. This model describes doubly degenerate optical modes in the long-wavelength limit, but our theory applies generally to the modes that have the emergent gauge field description. We further assume
C_{4z}
rotation symmetry and thus the emergent gauge field is simply
\bm{a}^{\nu}=(g^{\nu}/ev_{F})\bm{Q}^{\nu}
. To switch on the phonon Hall viscosity
\eta_{H}
, an out-of-plane magnetic field
B
is applied to break the TRS. As a result, there is an additional phonon viscosity term in the phonon Lagrangian,

\mathcal{L}_{\eta}=\eta_{H}(Q^{\nu}_{y}\dot{Q}^{\nu}_{x}-Q^{\nu}_{x}\dot{Q}^{\nu}_{y}),

(4)

where
\eta_{H}=\sigma_{xy}(g^{\nu})^{2}/(2e^{2}v_{F}^{2})
. We solve the equations of motion in the circularly polarized basis
\{Q_{l/r}^{\nu}=(Q_{x}^{\nu}\pm iQ_{y}^{\nu})/\sqrt{2}\}
. The frequencies of the left-handed and right-handed polarized modes are
\omega^{\nu}_{l/r}=[(\omega^{\nu}_{0})^{2}+(\eta_{H}/\rho_{I})^{2}]^{1/2}\pm\eta_{H}/\rho_{I}
, respectively. We find a splitting of the phonon frequencies given by
\delta\omega^{\nu}=2\eta_{H}/\rho_{I}
that is proportional to
\eta_{H}
.

To obtain the magnetic field dependence of phonon frequencies, we now turn to compute the Hall conductivity. In Dirac semimetals, the Dirac-cone dispersion transforms into Landau levels (LLs) in a magnetic field. For a finite concentration
n_{F}
of carriers (electrons or holes), the Fermi level
\varepsilon_{F}
is between the
n
th and
(n+1)
th LL. Under relatively weak field, the LLs are filled to a large index (
|n|\gg
1) such that the Hall conductivity
response of the Dirac fermions is semiclassical. Hence we can use the Drude theory to calculate the Hall conductivity,
\sigma_{xy}=\sigma_{0}\omega_{c}\tau_{\text{tr}}/(1+\omega_{c}^{2}\tau_{\text{tr}}^{2})
, where
\sigma_{0}
is the dc conductivity,
\omega_{c}
is the cyclotron frequency, and
\tau_{\text{tr}}
is the transport lifetime. The field dependence enters through the cyclotron frequency given by
\omega_{c}=eB/m^{*}_{c}
(
m^{*}_{c}
is the cyclotron effective mass). As a result, we obtain the phonon frequencies as functions of
B

\omega^{\nu}_{l/r}=\omega^{\nu}_{0}\left[\sqrt{1+\left(\xi\frac{\widetilde{\mu}_{\text{tr}}B}{1+\widetilde{\mu}_{\text{tr}}^{2}B^{2}}\right)^{2}}\pm\xi\frac{\widetilde{\mu}_{\text{tr}}B}{1+\widetilde{\mu}_{\text{tr}}^{2}B^{2}}\right]

(5)

where
\xi={\sigma_{0}(g^{\nu})^{2}}/(2e^{2}v_{F}^{2}\rho_{I}\omega_{0}^{\nu})
and the carrier mobility
\widetilde{\mu}_{\text{tr}}=e\tau_{\text{tr}}/m_{c}^{*}
. In the quantum limit at large magnetic fields and low doping, one needs to go beyond the semiclassical approximation for
\sigma_{xy}
and calculate
\sigma_{xy}
quantum mechanically, i.e. using the Kubo formula

Giant phonon magnetic moment
.— The most important finding in Eq. (
5
) is that the frequencies of left-handed and right-handed polarized phonons split linearly with
B
when the field is weak (
B\ll\widetilde{\mu}_{\text{tr}}^{-1}
). This linear field dependence is identified as the phonon Zeeman effect
[
27
,
28
]

\hbar\omega^{\nu}_{l/r}=\hbar\omega^{\nu}_{0}\pm\bm{\mu}_{\text{ph}}\cdot\bm{B}
. We have

\mu_{\text{ph}}=\frac{(g^{\nu})^{2}}{v_{F}^{2}\rho_{I}B}\frac{\hbar}{2e^{2}}\sigma_{xy}

(6)

where
\sigma_{xy}(B\rightarrow 0)=\sigma_{0}\widetilde{\mu}_{\text{tr}}B
. This is our main result: the phonon magnetic moment
\mu_{\text{ph}}
is directly linked to the electrical Hall conductivity
\sigma_{xy}
through the phonon Hall viscosity
\eta_{H}
. Finally, we express
\mu_{\text{ph}}
in the unit of Bohr magneton as
\mu_{\text{ph}}=\sigma_{0}\tau_{\text{tr}}(ev_{F})^{-2}(g^{\nu})^{2}\rho_{I}^{-1}(m_{e}/m_{c}^{*})\mu_{B}
in the limit of
\omega_{c}\tau\ll 1
.

Conclusion and outlook.
— We have established a theoretical framework to calculate the phonon magnetic moment in doped Dirac semimetals by treating phonon as an emergent gauge field. We find that the phonon magnetic moment is directly proportional to the Hall conductivity, indicating that a significant enhancement can be achieved with high carrier concentration and carrier mobility. Our theory is combined with the first-principles calculations, allowing us to quantitatively implement it to realistic materials. Magnetic moments are found to be on the order of Bohr magneton for the optical modes in graphene and
\text{Cd}_{3}\text{As}_{2}
.
These modes are Raman active, and their magnetic moments can be measured by the phonon Zeeman splitting under magnetic fields using the Raman spectroscopy
[
69
,
70
,
3
]
. Our results also pave the way for subsequent extensions to the infrared-active modes. In future experimental investigations, our theory offers tangible direction to search for large phonon magnetic moments in the topological quantum materials.

Electron-phonon coupling

We start from the many-particle Hamiltonian of electrons and ions at equilibrium positions,
H=H_{\text{e}}+H_{\text{ion}}+H_{\text{ei}}
, where
H_{\text{ei}}=\sum_{i\bm{R}\alpha}V_{\text{ei}}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{R}-\bm{\tau}_{\alpha})
is the electron-ion interaction Hamiltonian. Here
\bm{R}
is the lattice vector for unit cells and
\bm{\tau}_{\alpha}
is the position of ion
\alpha
within a unit cell. If the ions vibrate,
H_{\text{ei}}
will change to
\sum_{i\bm{R}\alpha}V_{\text{ei}}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{R}-\bm{\tau}_{\alpha}+\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha})
, where
\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha}
is the atomic displacement of the ion
\alpha
in the unit cell
\bm{R}
from its equilibrium position. It is usually small compared to the lattice constant, allowing us to expand the potential to the first order of
\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha}
as
\sum_{i\bm{R}\alpha}V_{\text{ei}}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{R}-\bm{\tau}_{\alpha})+\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha}\cdot\bm{\nabla}V_{\text{ei}}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{R}-\bm{\tau}_{\alpha})+O(\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha}^{2})
. Thus the electron-phonon coupling Hamiltonian comes from the first-order correction,

H_{e\text{-ph}}=\sum_{i\bm{R}\alpha}\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha}\cdot\bm{\nabla}V_{\text{ei}}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{R}-\bm{\tau}_{\alpha})=\int d\bm{r}\rho(\bm{r})\sum_{\bm{R}\alpha}\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha}\cdot\bm{\nabla}V_{\text{ei}}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{R}-\bm{\tau}_{\alpha}),

(S1)

where
\rho(\bm{r})
is the density operator of electrons, and
\rho(\bm{r})=\sum_{i}\delta(\hat{\bm{r}}_{i}-\bm{r})
in the coordinate representation. Now we move to the second quantization. The recipe is using the second quantization form of density operator
\rho(\bm{r})=\psi^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\psi(\bm{r})
. The field operator projected to a specific basis, such as Bloch electronic basis, is
\psi^{\dagger}(\bm{r})=\sum_{n\bm{k}}\psi^{*}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r})c^{\dagger}_{n\bm{k}}
, where
n
is the energy band index and
\bm{k}
is electronic wavevector. The Bloch wavefunctions are given by
\psi_{n\bm{k}}(r)=u_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r})e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}
, where
u_{n\bm{k}}=\innerproduct{\boldsymbol{r}}{n\boldsymbol{k}}
is the periodic Bloch function within one unit cell. Under this representation, the electron-phonon coupling Hamiltonian becomes

H_{e\text{-ph}}=\sum_{nm}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\int d\bm{r}\psi^{*}_{n\bm{k}}(\bm{r})\sum_{\bm{R}\alpha}\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha}\cdot\bm{\nabla}V_{\text{ei}}(\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{R}-\bm{\tau}_{\alpha})\psi_{m\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{r})c^{\dagger}_{n\bm{k}}c_{m\bm{k}^{\prime}}.

(S2)

Now we expand the atomic displacement of the ion at
\bm{R}+\bm{\tau}_{\alpha}
in terms of the vibrational modes,

\delta\bm{\tau}_{\bm{R}\alpha}=\sum_{\bm{q},\nu}\sqrt{\frac{\overline{M}}{NM_{\alpha}}}e^{i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{R}}\ \bm{\xi}_{\alpha}^{(\nu)}(\bm{q})Q_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)},

(S3)

where
\bm{q}
is the phonon wavevector,
\nu
labels the normal modes,
\omega_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)}
is the frequency dispersion,
\bm{\xi}_{\alpha}^{(\nu)}(\bm{q})
is the normal mode eigenvector or polarization vector that solves the dynamic matrix problem,
\overline{M}=1/N_{\alpha}\sum_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}
is the average mass of
N_{\alpha}
atoms in a unit cell,
Q_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)}
refers to as the complex normal coordinate of the displacement projected onto the normal mode
\nu
. Since the left hand side of equation (
S3
) is real and the normal mode eigenvectors satisfy
\bm{\xi}_{\alpha}^{(\nu)}(\bm{q})^{*}=\bm{\xi}_{\alpha}^{(\nu)}(-\bm{q})
, we obtain the complex conjugation relation,
Q_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)*}=Q_{-\bm{q}}^{(\nu)}
. The quantization of the phonons is given by

Q_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\overline{M}\omega^{(\nu)}_{\bm{q}}}}[a^{(\nu)}_{\bm{q}}+a^{(\nu)\dagger}_{-\bm{q}}],

(S4)

where
M_{\alpha}
is the mass of the ion
\alpha
,
\omega_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)}
is the frequency of mode
\nu
, and
a^{\dagger}(a)
is the creation (annihilation) operator of phonons. We arrives at the Frölich Hamiltonian for the electron-phonon coupling,

\displaystyle H_{e\text{-ph}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{nm\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{q},\nu}g_{mn}^{(\nu)}(\bm{k},\bm{q})Q_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)}c^{\dagger}_{n\bm{k}+\frac{\bm{q}}{2}}c_{m\bm{k}-\frac{\bm{q}}{2}},

(S5)

where the electron-phonon coupling matrix element is given by

g_{mn}^{(\nu)}(\bm{k},\bm{q})=\bra{n\boldsymbol{k}+\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}}({\nabla}V_{\text{ei}})_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)}\ket{m\boldsymbol{k}-\frac{\boldsymbol{q}}{2}}_{\text{uc}}.

(S6)

We have defined
({\nabla}V_{\text{ei}})_{\bm{q}}^{(\nu)}=\sum_{\bm{R}\alpha}\sqrt{\overline{M}/M_{\alpha}}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{R})}\bm{\xi}_{\alpha}^{(\nu)}(\bm{q})\cdot\bm{\nabla}V_{\text{ei}}(\bm{r}-\bm{R}-\bm{\tau}_{\alpha})
. Bloch thereom has been used. Evaluating
g_{mn}^{(\nu)}(\bm{k},\bm{q})
is the essential part in the calculations of the electron-phonon coupling. Common approaches are the density functional perturbation theory and the frozen phonon approach, which we will employ. Other approaches such as the tight-binding method are useful for different purposes.

\text{Cd}_{3}\text{As}_{2}

In this section, we develop a phonon-modulated tight-binding model for
\text{Cd}_{3}\text{As}_{2}

5s
and As-
4p
near the
\Gamma

[
1

\ket{S_{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{2}},\ket{P_{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{3}{2}},\ket{S_{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}},\ket{P_{\frac{3}{2}}-\frac{3}{2}}

H(\bm{k})=\varepsilon_{0}(\bm{k})\hat{I}+M(\bm{k})\tau^{z}+Ak_{x}\sigma^{z}\tau^{x}+Ak_{y}\tau^{y},

(S7)

where
\varepsilon_{0}(\bm{k})=C_{0}+C_{1}k_{z}^{2}+C_{2}(k_{x}^{2}+k_{y}^{2})

C_{0,1,2}
,
M_{0,1,2}
, and
A
fitted from the DFT calculations, and
\bm{\sigma},\bm{\tau}
are Pauli matrices in spin and orbital space. Since spin-up and spin-down blocks are effectively decoupled in the model Hamiltonian, we can analyze them separately. In the following, we focus on the spin-up block that is based on
\ket{S_{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{2}},\ket{P_{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{3}{2}}

h=M(\bm{k})\tau^{z}+Ak_{x}\tau^{x}+Ak_{y}\tau^{y}.

(S8)

By making the substitution,

\displaystyle k_{x,y}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\frac{1}{a}\sin{(k_{x,y}a)},

(S9)

\displaystyle k_{x,y,z}^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\frac{2}{a^{2}}[1-\cos{(k_{x,y,z}a)}],

we regularize the Hamiltonian on a simple cubic lattice with lattice constant

h^{\text{latt}}=[m_{0}+m_{1}\cos{(k_za)}+m_{2}\cos{(k_xa)}+m_{2}\cos{(k_ya)}]\tau^{z}+t\sin{(k_xa)}\tau^{x}+t\sin{(k_ya)}\tau^{y},

(S10)

where
m_{0}=M_{0}+\frac{2M_{1}}{a^{2}}+\frac{4M_{2}}{a^{2}}

m_{1}=-\frac{2M_{1}}{a^{2}}

m_{2}=-\frac{2M_{2}}{a^{2}}

with the velocity vector
\bm{v}_{\chi}=\hbar^{-1}a(t,t,-\chi m_{1}\sin{(k_{z0}a)})

\text{Cd}_{3}\text{As}_{2}

crystal has a complex structure with 80 atoms per unit cell, its low-energy physics can be well described by this effective tight-binding model with Cd-
5s
and As-
4p
orbitals on the vertices of the cubic lattice. The real-space tight-binding Hamiltonian is given by
H=\sum_{j}(h_{j}^{\text{intra}}+h_{j}^{\text{inter}})
where

\displaystyle h_{j}^{\text{intra}}

\displaystyle=m_{0}(a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j}-b^{\dagger}_{j}b_{j})

(S11)

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{1}}{2}(a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j+a\hat{x}}-b^{\dagger}_{j}b_{j+a\hat{x}}+a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j+a\hat{y}}-b^{\dagger}_{j}b_{j+a\hat{y}})+h.c.

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{2}}{2}(a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j+a\hat{z}}-b^{\dagger}_{j}b_{j+a\hat{z}})+h.c.

\displaystyle h_{j}^{\text{inter}}

\displaystyle=-i\frac{t}{2}(a^{\dagger}_{j}b_{j+a\hat{x}}+b^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j+a\hat{x}})+h.c.

(S12)

\displaystyle-\frac{t}{2}(a^{\dagger}_{j}b_{j+a\hat{y}}+b^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j+a\hat{y}})+h.c..

In order to capture the in-plane doubly degenerate

The effect of phonons on the tight-binding Hamiltonian is to modify the hopping integrals. The correction has two types: the bond-length change, and the rotations between orbitals. The bond-length change is isotropic and exists for all orbitals
[
2
,
3
,
4

For our purpose of deriving the emergent gauge field that shifts the Dirac cones on the
\Gamma
-
Z

and
p

3
]

t_{sp}(a\hat{z}+\delta\bm{\tau})\approx\frac{\bm{n}\cdot(a\hat{z}\times\delta\bm{\tau})}{a}\frac{\partial t_{sp}(a\hat{x})}{\partial a},

(S13)

where
\bm{n}
is the normal vector to the plane and

p

can be related the phonon displacement using Eq. (

\delta h_{j}^{\Gamma_{8}}=-i\frac{t}{2}\frac{\beta Q_{x}^{E_{u}}}{a}[a^{\dagger}_{j}b_{j+a\hat{z}}+a^{\dagger}_{j}b_{j-a\hat{z}}+b^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j+a\hat{z}}+b^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j-a\hat{z}}]+h.c.

(S14)

III
Hall conductivity of
\text{Cd}_{3}\text{As}_{2}

We consider an external magnetic field applied along the
z
-axis of
\text{Cd}_{3}\text{As}_{2}
. The field couples into the Hamiltonian in two distinct ways,
{H}\to{H}(\bm{p}+e\bm{A})+{H}_{Z}
. The first part is the orbital effect, and the second part is the Zeeman effect. Thus, there are
two contributions to Hall conductivity. The orbital contribution is calculated in the semiclassical limit using the Drude model,

\sigma_{xy}^{\text{orbital}}=\sigma_{0}\frac{\omega_{c}\tau}{1+\omega_{c}^{2}\tau^{2}}.

(S18)

The Zeeman field breaks the time-reversal symmetry and splits the Dirac points into Weyl points in momentum space and also causes a shift in energy
[
5
,
6
]
. We consider the Hamiltonian in the Eq. (
S7
). The Zeeman term takes the form

H_{Z}=-(g_{s}\bm{J}_{s}+g_{p}\bm{J}_{p})\cdot\bm{B},

(S19)

where
g_{s}
and
g_{p}
are
g
-factors for the
s
and
p
orbitals. When
\bm{B}=B_{z}\hat{z}
, the total Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H=\epsilon_{0}(k)+\begin{bmatrix}M(k)-\frac{3}{2}g_{p}B_{z}&A(k)&0&0\\
A^{*}(k)&-M(k)-\frac{1}{2}g_{s}B_{z}&0&0\\
0&0&-M(k)+\frac{1}{2}g_{p}B_{z}&-A(k)\\
0&0&-A^{*}(k)&M(k)+\frac{3}{2}g_{p}B_{z}\end{bmatrix}.

(S20)

The details of solving this Hamiltonian can be found in Ref.
[
5
]
. For any magnitude of
B_{z}
, at least one pair of Weyl points exist; for small enough
B_{z}\ll|\frac{4M_{0}}{g_{s}-3g_{p}}|
, both pairs exist. By adopting the parameters
M_{0}=0.02\ \text{eV}
,
g_{s}=18.6
,
g_{p}=2
for
\text{Cd}_{3}\text{As}_{2}
, the critical value of
B_{z}
is calculated as
\approx 50\ T
. Thus, we consider the case where four Weyl nodes exist and given by

k_{z0}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{\pm(g_{s}-3g_{p})B_{z}/4-M_{0}}{M_{1}}}.

(S21)

The Weyl nodes are monopole sources of the Berry curvature that open a channel for intrinsic Hall conductivity. Hence, our calculations of Hall conductivity need to include a Zeeman contribution given by

\sigma_{xy}^{\text{Zeeman}}=\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi^{2}\hbar}\kappa_{z},

(S22)

where
\kappa_{z}
is the generalized distance between the four Weyl nodes, which is the
z
-component of the vector defined by

\bm{\kappa}=\sum_{i}C_{i}\bm{P}_{i}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{\xi^{i}}\bm{P}_{i}.

(S23)

Here,
i
labels four different nodes,
C_{i}
are their topological charges,
\bm{P}_{i}
are their momenta, and
\xi^{i}
are their chiralities. When the magnetic field is weak, the Zeeman-induced shift of momenta is small compared to the momenta of the Weyl nodes, allowing us to expand
\kappa_{z}
to the linear order in
B_{z}
as
\kappa_{z}=\sqrt{-M_{0}/M_{1}}(g_{s}-3g_{p})\mu_{B}B_{z}/M_{0}
, where we have restored the Bohr magneton
\mu_{B}
. For the parameters
M_{0}=0.02\ \text{eV}
,
M_{1}=-18.77\ \text{eV}\cdot\text{\AA}^{2}
,
g_{s}=18.6
,
g_{p}=2
, and
B_{z}=1\ T
, the Zeeman contribution to Hall conductivity is
\sigma_{xy}^{\text{Zeeman}}\approx(7.5\times 10^{-3}{\text{\AA}}^{-1})\frac{e^{2}}{h}
. For comparison, the orbital contribution at
B_{z}=1\ T
calculated using the parameters in the main text is
\sigma_{xy}^{\text{orbital}}\approx(2\ \text{\AA}^{-1})\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}
, which dominates over the Zeeman contribution.

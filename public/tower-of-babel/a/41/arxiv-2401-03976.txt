4D Chern-Simons and the pure spinor A ​ d ​ S 5 × S 5 AdS_{5}\times S^{5} superstring
Nathan Berkovits, Rodrigo S. Pitombo

Abstract
Four-dimensional Chern-Simons (4DCS) theory is useful for understanding integrable sigma-models and constructing new ones. In this paper, we show how to derive the complete pure spinor A ​ d ​ S 5 × S 5 AdS_{5}\times S^{5} superstring sigma-model from 4DCS theory with defects. The matter sector of this sigma model was previously derived by Costello and Yamazaki, and we propose here that the pure spinor ghosts come from gauge-fixing meromorphic transformations of 4DCS which lead to the usual pure spinor Lax connection including the ghost contribution.



I
Introduction

An important step in fully understanding the AdS/CFT duality in its original formulation
Maldacena 1999
is to study the behavior of superstrings propagating in the
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
background. In this context, the pure spinor formalism for the superstring
Berkovits 2000
plays an essential role since it can describe Type IIB backgrounds with Ramond-Ramond flux
Berkovits and Howe 2002
and its worldsheet action in an
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
background is quantizable
Berkovits 2005a
.
Sigma models that describe superstrings in this background are special because they are integrable (at least classically). This means that the equations of motion for these models are equivalent to the flatness of a Lax connection
\mathcal{L}(z)
:

d\mathcal{L}(u)+\mathcal{L}(u)\wedge\mathcal{L}(u)=0\;\;\;\;\forall u\in\mathds{C}

(1)

where
z
is called the spectral parameter. The Green-Schwarz
Bena et al. 2004
, pure spinor
Vallilo 2004
;
Berkovits 2005b
and the recently introduced B-RNS-GSS
Chandia and Gomide 2024
formalisms have this property. Moreover, integrable structures also appear in the field theory side of the duality
Minahan and Zarembo 2003
;
Beisert and Staudacher 2003
.

Discovering that a model has a Lax connection satisfying (
1
) usually involves guesswork. However, in a series of papers
Costello et al. 2018a
;
Costello et al. 2018b
;
Costello and Yamazaki 2019
Costello, Witten and Yamazaki have shown that one can use a four-dimensional version of Chern-Simons theory with defects to construct sigma models in such a way that their integrability is guaranteed, as the flatness equation is implied by one of the equations of motion. For our purposes, the theory lives in
\mathds{R}^{2}\times\mathds{CP}^{1}
and the spectral parameter on which
\mathcal{L}
depends is reinterpreted as a holomorphic coordinate on the Riemann sphere.

Using 4D Chern-Simons theory (4DCS), several integrable field theories have been shown to come from different defect configurations in the 4D manifold such as the principal chiral model, the WZW model, the Yang-Baxter model
Costello and Yamazaki 2019
;
Lacroix 2022
, and others
Fukushima et al. 2022
;
Fukushima et al. 2021
;
Liniado and Vicedo 2023
;
Caudrelier et al. 2021
;
Lacroix and Vicedo 2021
;
Vicedo 2021
. Thus, a natural question is whether one can construct the superstring sigma model on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
with this framework. In
Costello and Yamazaki 2019
Costello and Yamazaki constructed the matter sector (
20
) of the pure spinor superstring on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
, and some integrable deformations
Fukushima et al. 2020
;
Tian et al. 2021
as well as the Green-Schwarz sigma model
Costello and Stefański 2020
on
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
were also constructed using this framework. Nevertheless, none of the constructions included the ghost sector of the worldsheet action, which plays an essential role in the pure spinor formalism.

The purpose of this work is to show how the 4DCS framework with gauge supergroup
\mathrm{PSU}(2,2|4)
can be used to describe the complete
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
pure spinor superstring including the ghost sector. In the construction of
Costello and Yamazaki 2019
, there is some arbitrariness in the choice of pole structure for the Lax connection. In particular, one imposes by hand which components of
\mathcal{L}
have poles at given points, giving rise to the chiral and anti-chiral defects. Here, we propose that this arbitrary choice should be interpreted as a gauge-fixing condition. We then use a set of
\mathrm{PSU}(2,2|4)
-valued meromorphic gauge transformations to restrict the pole structure of
\mathcal{L}
.

Fixing the meromorphic gauge symmetry will lead to a set of 32 bosonic and 10 fermionic ghosts localized in two-dimensional subspaces of
\mathds{R}^{2}\times\mathds{CP}^{1}
. This characterizes the ghost system as a so-called “order defect”, and defects of this type were thoroughly analyzed in
Costello and Yamazaki 2019
. Adapting a cohomology argument of
Aisaka and Kazama 2003
to
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
, we argue that these ghosts should be equivalent to the usual pair of left and right-moving pure spinor bosonic ghosts, and the system composed by the 4DCS gauge field coupled to the ghosts yields the complete pure spinor
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
superstring.
Thus, 4DCS not only gives a complete description of the formalism but also furnishes a natural origin for the pure spinor ghosts in terms of gauge-fixing. Note that in
Berkovits 2009
, the 22 bosonic pure spinor ghosts in an
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
background were similarly derived from 32 bosonic and 10 fermionic ghosts by gauge-fixing
\mathrm{PSU}(2,2|4)
local symmetries.

The paper is organized as follows: In the following subsection, we review the pure spinor formalism in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. In section
II
we introduce 4D Chern-Simons and the defects that lead to coset sigma models. In section
III
we discuss gauge invariance and argue that for a specific gauge-fixing, the Faddeev-Popov procedure leads to a ghost system and BRST charge which are equivalent to those of the pure spinor formalism. And in section
IV
, we show how the 4D setup of the defects and ghosts leads to the 2D action and Lax connection of the pure spinor superstring in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
.

I.1
Review of the pure spinor formalism in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}

We are interested in describing superstrings propagating in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. The associated superspace can be described in terms of the supercoset

\frac{\mathrm{PSU}(2,2|4)}{\mathrm{SO}(4,1)\times\mathrm{SO}(5)}.

(2)

The
\mathfrak{psu}(2,2|4)
algebra is given by

\displaystyle\left\{Q_{\alpha},Q_{\beta}\right\}=\gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{a}P_{a},\quad\left\{\hat{Q}_{\hat{\alpha}},\hat{Q}_{\hat{\beta}}\right\}=\gamma^{a}_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}P_{a},

(3)

\displaystyle{\left[P_{a},\hat{Q}_{\hat{\beta}}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta\gamma_{a}\right)^{\alpha}_{\hat{\beta}}Q_{\alpha},}

(4)

\displaystyle{\left[P_{a},Q_{\alpha}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma_{a}\eta\right)_{\alpha}^{\hat{\beta}}\hat{Q}_{\hat{\beta}},}

(5)

\displaystyle\left[P_{a},P_{b}\right]=-L_{ab},

(6)

\displaystyle\left\{Q_{\alpha},\hat{Q}_{\hat{\beta}}\right\}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma^{ab}\eta\right)_{\alpha\hat{\beta}}L_{ab}.

(7)

and the non-vanishing supertraces are

\displaystyle\mathrm{sTr}\left(P_{a}P_{b}\right)=\eta_{ab},\quad\mathrm{sTr}\left(Q_{\alpha}\hat{Q}_{\hat{\beta}}\right)=-2\eta_{\alpha\hat{\beta}},

(8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{sTr}\left(L_{ab}L_{cd}\right)=\eta_{a[b}\eta_{c]d.}.

(9)

where
a=0,\ldots,9
are tangent space vector indices and
\alpha/\hat{\alpha}=1,\ldots,16
are ten-dimensional spinor indices.

This supercoset describes a “semi-symmetric space” because the superalgebra
\mathfrak{psu}(2,2|4)
has an automorphism
\rho
which induces a
\mathds{Z}_{4}
grading

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}

(10)

such that

\rho\cdot\mathfrak{g}^{(k)}=i^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{(k)}

(11)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}=\mathrm{span}(L_{ab})\cong\mathfrak{so}(4,1)\oplus\mathfrak{so}(5)

(12)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}=\mathrm{span}(Q_{\alpha})

(13)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(2)}=\mathrm{span}(P_{a})

(14)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}=\mathrm{span}(\hat{Q}_{\hat{\alpha}}).

(15)

Therefore, the superstring in this space can be described by the supergroup-valued degree of freedom

g(w,\bar{w})\in\mathrm{PSU}(2,2|4)

(16)

with the equivalence relation

g(w,\bar{w})\sim g(w,\bar{w})h(w,\bar{w})\;\;\;\;\;h\in\mathrm{SO}(4,1)\times\mathrm{SO}(5)

(17)

where
(w,\bar{w})
are the complex coordinates of the worldsheet.
The matter sector of the action is constructed out of the
\mathfrak{psu}(2,2|4)
-valued left-invariant currents

J:=g^{-1}dg

(18)

which can be decomposed in terms of the
\mathds{Z}_{4}
grading of
\mathfrak{psu}(2,2|4)
as

J=J^{(0)}+J^{(1)}+J^{(2)}+J^{(3)}.

(19)

In particular, the matter part is
Mazzucato 2012

S_{m}=\int d^{2}w\;\mathrm{sTr}\big(\frac{1}{2}J_{\bar{w}}^{(2)}J_{w}^{(2)}+\frac{3}{4}J_{w}^{(3)}J_{\bar{w}}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{4}J_{w}^{(1)}J_{\bar{w}}^{(3)}\big).

(20)

We also have ghosts which play a fundamental role in cancelling the conformal anomaly and constructing physical vertex operators. As in 10D flat space, they are bosonic fields with 10D Weyl spinor indices (
\lambda^{\alpha}
,
\hat{\lambda}^{\hat{\alpha}}
) that satisfy the pure spinor constraints

\displaystyle\lambda^{\alpha}\gamma^{a}_{\alpha\beta}\lambda^{\beta}=0

(21)

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}^{\hat{\alpha}}\gamma^{a}_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\hat{\lambda}^{\hat{\beta}}=0.

(22)

In the
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
model, they can be conveniently written in terms of
\mathfrak{g}
-valued ghosts as

\displaystyle\lambda:=\lambda^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(1)}

(23)

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}:=\hat{\lambda}^{\hat{\alpha}}\hat{Q}_{\hat{\alpha}}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(3)}.

(24)

From the structure constants in (
3
), it is direct to see that the pure spinor constraints can be rewritten as

\{\lambda,\lambda\}=\{\hat{\lambda},\hat{\lambda}\}=0.

(25)

The model also has anti-ghosts
(y,\hat{y})\in(\mathfrak{g}^{(3)},\mathfrak{g}^{(1)})
which can be used to construct the ghost-number zero objects

N=-\{y,\lambda\}\;\;\;\;\;\hat{N}=-\{\hat{y},\hat{\lambda}\}\;\;\in\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}

(26)

The ghost contribution to the action is then

S_{gh}=\int d^{2}w\;\mathrm{sTr}\big(y\nabla_{\bar{w}}^{(0)}\lambda+\hat{y}\nabla^{(0)}_{w}\hat{\lambda}-N\hat{N}\big),

(27)

where we defined the
\mathfrak{g}^{(0)}
-covariant derivative

\nabla^{(0)}=d+[J^{(0)},\cdot].

(28)

The complete action is just the sum

S=S_{m}+S_{gh.}.

(29)

This action is invariant under the BRST transformations

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;Q\cdot g=g(\lambda+\hat{\lambda})

(30)

\displaystyle Q\cdot y=-J^{(3)}_{w}\;\;\;\;Q\cdot\hat{y}=-J^{(1)}_{\bar{w}}

(31)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;Q\cdot\lambda=Q\cdot\hat{\lambda}=0

(32)

and the Noether procedure yields the BRST charge

\displaystyle Q=\oint dw\;\mathrm{sTr}\big(\lambda J^{(3)}_{\bar{w}}\big)-\oint d\bar{w}\;\mathrm{sTr}\big(\hat{\lambda}J^{(1)}_{w}\big).

(33)

In this sigma model, the equations of motion are equivalent to the flatness of the Lax connection

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{w}=J_{w}^{(0)}+\frac{1}{u}J_{w}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{u^{2}}J_{w}^{(2)}+\frac{1}{u^{3}}J_{w}^{(3)}-(1-\frac{1}{u^{4}})N

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bar{w}}=J_{\bar{w}}^{(0)}+uJ_{\bar{w}}^{(3)}+u^{2}J_{\bar{w}}^{(2)}+u^{3}J_{\bar{w}}^{(1)}-(1-u^{4})\hat{N}.

(35)

where
u
is the spectral parameter defined in (
1
).
Moreover, one can show
Mikhailov and Schafer-Nameki 2011
that, under BRST transformations, the Lax connection transforms as

Q\cdot\mathcal{L}=d\tilde{\lambda}+[\mathcal{L},\tilde{\lambda}]

(36)

where

\tilde{\lambda}=\frac{1}{u}\lambda+u\hat{\lambda}.

(37)

Note that (
36
) has exactly the form of the gauge transformation in Chern-Simons theory. Thus, it seems reasonable that the ghosts of the formalism can be reinterpreted as Faddeev-Popov ghosts in a gauge-fixing of 4D Chern-Simons theory. Moreover, from the form of (
37
) it is natural to expect that they are related to meromorphic gauge transformations. This will be shown below by starting with the 4DCS action of
Costello and Yamazaki 2019
for the
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
superstring and gauge-fixing the local symmetries.

II
4D Chern-Simons

Our starting point is the 4D Chern-Simons (4DCS) action given by
Costello et al. 2018b
;
Costello et al. 2018a
;
Costello and Yamazaki 2019

S=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\omega\wedge\mathrm{sTr}\big(A\wedge dA+\frac{1}{3}A\wedge\big[A\wedge A\big]\big)

(38)

with a gauge (super)group
G=\mathrm{PSU}(2,2|4)
and
\mathcal{M}=\Sigma\times\mathds{CP}^{1}

\Sigma

\Sigma=\mathds{R}^{2}
from now on. Throughout the paper, (

\mathds{CP}^{1}

(w,\bar{w})
are coordinates on
\mathds{R}^{2}

Moreover,

The equation of motion for
A

\displaystyle\omega\wedge F(A)=0

(40)

where the field-strength

(41)

III.1
Residual gauge transformations and ghosts

In archipelago gauge, the equation of motion (
40
) implies

\displaystyle\phi(u)\partial_{\bar{u}}\mathcal{L}=0

(52)

\displaystyle\phi(u)(d\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{L}\wedge\mathcal{L})=0.

(53)

where comparing with (
43
) we see that

\displaystyle\phi(u):=\frac{u^{3}}{(u^{4}-1)^{2}}.

(54)

The residual gauge transformations should be consistent with these equations and leave
A_{\bar{u}}
unchanged. Since (
52
) implies that
\mathcal{L}
only has poles up to third-order, the residual transformations are

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{L}=\nabla_{\Sigma}\tilde{\Lambda}

(55)

where

\nabla_{\Sigma}:=d_{\Sigma}+[\mathcal{L},\cdot],

(56)

and
\tilde{\Lambda}
should be defined such that
\delta\mathcal{L}
only has poles up to third order at
u=0
or
u=\infty
and should vanish at
u^{4}=1
to preserve the boundary condition (
50
). The precise form of these parameters will depend on the specific value of
\mathcal{L}
, but the general structure is

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}=\big(1/u-u^{3}\big)\Lambda_{1}(w,\bar{w})+\big(1/u^{2}-u^{2}\big)\Lambda_{2}(w,\bar{w})+\big(u-1/u^{3}\big)\Lambda_{3}(w,\bar{w})+\mathrm{higher}\;\mathrm{poles}

(57)

where
\Lambda_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}^{(k)}
. Here, (
\Lambda_{1}
,
\Lambda_{2}
,
\Lambda_{3}
) are independent parameters and the higher poles are determined in terms of (
\Lambda_{1}
,
\Lambda_{2}
,
\Lambda_{3}
) by the requirement that
\nabla_{\Sigma}\tilde{\Lambda}
only has poles up to third order. We therefore have 32 fermionic (
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{3}
) and 10 bosonic (
\Lambda_{2}
) gauge parameters. Since each fermionic/bosonic gauge parameter leads to a bosonic/fermionic ghost, a naive counting indicates that the ghost system obtained from gauge-fixing this symmetry is equivalent to
32-10=22
bosonic ghosts, where the fermionic ghosts have been interpreted as ghosts-for-ghosts which cancel 10 of the bosonic ghosts. This is precisely the number of degrees of freedom of a pair of pure spinors, and a similar derivation of 22 pure spinor bosonic ghosts from 32 bosonic and 10 fermionic ghosts was used in
Berkovits 2009
.

To further understand this, let’s use
(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3})
to impose gauge-fixing conditions on

. Let

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{k}u^{k}\mathcal{L}^{k}.

and

which leads to a pair of ghosts

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-1},\mathcal{L}_{\bar{w}}^{-3}]+[\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-2},\mathcal{L}_{\bar{w}}^{-2}]=0

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-2},\mathcal{L}_{\bar{w}}^{-3}]+[\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-3},\mathcal{L}_{\bar{w}}^{-2}]=0

\displaystyle L_{ab}[(\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{w})^{\alpha}(\mathcal{L}_{\bar{w}}^{-3})^{\hat{\beta}}(\gamma^{ab}\eta)_{\alpha\hat{\beta}}+(\mathcal{L}_{w}^{-2})^{a}\eta^{bc}\mathcal{N}^{I}_{c}\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{I}]=0

Assuming that

\mathcal{L}_{\bar{w}}^{-3}=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{I}=0.

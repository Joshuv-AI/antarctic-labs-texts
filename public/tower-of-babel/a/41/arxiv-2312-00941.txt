THE QUANTUM RAINBOW SCATTERING EFFECT ON A SINGLE CRYSTALLINE PLANE IN APPROXIMATION OF CONTINUOUS POTENTIAL
N.F. Shul’ga ∗,, V.D. Koriukina ∗∗,

Abstract
The differential scattering cross section for charged relativistic particles moving parallel close to the crystalline plane of atoms was obtained. The rainbow scattering effect in the approximation of continuous potential was demonstrated. The problem was considered on the basis of the eikonal approximation of quantum electrodynamics.

1
The problem formulation

In this work, we investigate the fast charged particles scattering on one crystalline plane of atoms. The plane is chosen so that inside it the particles are distributed with equal probability, thermal fluctuations occur in the transverse to the plane direction. This plane corresponds to a two-dimensional amorphous medium. This problem is a certain approximation for considering the movement of particles in a crystal close to one of its crystalline planes. For high-energy particles, this problem makes sense because the screened potential of atoms leads to a rapid decrease of the total plane potential with distancing from it, and, as a result, the particles scattering is mainly determined with the potential of the nearest plane. Let us consider the simplest version of the crystal orientation to the incident beam: let the particle incident parallel to the plane.

We note that, in contrast to scattering on crystal strings, when scattering on the specified plane, the Debye-Waller factor in the incoherent part is absent in the cross-section. Also, this cross-section differs from the scattering cross-section for a three-dimensional amorphous medium in containing a coherent part.

2
The differential scattering cross section

In the zero eikonal approximation of quantum electrodynamics, the differential scattering cross section is given by the formula
[
2
,
3
]

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\sigma}{d\vec{q}_{\perp}}=|a|^{2}

(1)

where the scattering amplitude
a
is defined as follows

\displaystyle a=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d^{2}\rho\ e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\vec{q}\vec{\rho}}\left[1-e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}{\chi}_{0}^{(N)}(\vec{\rho})}\right]

(2)

where
\vec{\rho}
are the coordinates transverse to the particles motion,
\vec{q}
is the momentum transmitted due to scattering, the
\chi_{0}^{(N)}
function for
N
particles in the target is defined in this way

\displaystyle\chi_{0}^{(N)}=-\frac{1}{v}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\ U^{(N)}(\vec{\rho},z)

(3)

where
v
is the incident particles velocity,
z
is the longitudinal coordinate relative to the particles movement,
U^{(N)}
is the total potential of
N
particles of the target.

Since the real potential is very complicated, as an approximation we average the differential scattering cross section over the particles distribution in the target according to the method used in the paper
[
6
]
. It can be shown that preserving only the terms in the scattering amplitude, which are linear in the function
\chi_{0}^{(1)}
, we can obtain the following formula for the cross section

\displaystyle a=\frac{-i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d^{2}\rho\ e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\left\{\vec{q}\vec{\rho}+N\bar{\chi}_{0}(\vec{\rho})\right\}}

(4)

where
\bar{\chi}_{0}=\bar{\chi}_{0}^{(1)}
and the bar corresponds to averaging.

Here a continuous potential idea
[
4
]
naturally appears.

If we assume that the function
\bar{\chi}_{0}
depends only on the coordinate
x
(we will demonstrate the admissibility of this statement later), it is possible, by integrating over
y
, to rewrite the scattering amplitude in this way

\displaystyle a=\frac{-i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\ e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\left\{q_{x}x+N\bar{\chi}_{0}\left(x\right)\right\}}\ 2\pi\delta(q_{y})

(5)

Since the square of the Dirac’s delta function is

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\ e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}q_{y}y}\right|^{2}=2\pi L_{y}\ \delta(q_{y})

(6)

where
L_{y}
is the size of the target in the
y
direction, then integrating the differential cross section by the transmitted momentum
q_{y}
, we come to the this expression for the differential scattering cross section

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dq_{x}}=\frac{L_{y}}{2\pi}\ |\tilde{a}|^{2}

(7)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{a}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\ e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\left\{q_{x}x+N\bar{\chi}_{0}\left(x\right)\right\}}

(8)

Analogically to the rainbow scattering on a string of atoms shown in
[
3
]
, we demonstrate the presence of this effect in scattering on a crystal plane. We note that the rainbow scattering effect occurs when the transmitted momentum corresponds to two or more values of impact parameter (from a quasi-classical point of view). In the paper
[
6
]
we demonstrated that such multiple correspondence exists for this crystalline plane. The rainbow scattering effect is mainly due to values which are close to the extremum of the dependence function of the transmitted momentum on the impact parameter. Since, from the standpoint of the quasi-classical approach, the transmitted momentum is determined by the derivative
\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}
, then the extremum of this dependence function corresponds to the impact parameter
x_{m}
for which
\partial_{x}^{2}\bar{\chi}_{0}\left(x_{m}\right)=
0. Expanding the underexponential expression in (
8
) near this extremum point
x=x_{m}+\Delta_{x}
, we obtain the following series

\displaystyle q_{x}x+N\bar{\chi}_{0}\left(x\right)\approx q_{x}x_{m}+N\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})+\Delta_{x}\left[q_{x}+N\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})\right]+N\frac{\Delta_{x}^{3}}{6}\partial_{x}^{3}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})

(9)

Then
\tilde{a}
is determined by the integral

\displaystyle\tilde{a}=e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\left\{q_{x}x_{m}+N\bar{\chi}_{0}\left(x_{m}\right)\right\}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\Delta_{x}\ exp\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\left\{\Delta_{x}\left[q_{x}+N\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})\right]+N\frac{\Delta_{x}^{3}}{6}\partial_{x}^{3}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})\right\}\right]

(10)

After integration we obtain the final expression for scattering near the extremum point

\displaystyle\tilde{a}=2\pi\ e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\left\{q_{x}x_{m}+N\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})\right\}}\left(\frac{N}{2\hbar}\partial_{x}^{3}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})\right)^{-1/3}\times

\displaystyle\times Ai\left[\frac{q_{x}+N\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})}{\hbar}\left(\frac{N}{2\hbar}\partial_{x}^{3}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})\right)^{-1/3}\right]

(11)

where
Ai
is the Airy function. So, the differential scattering cross section near the extremum has the following form

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dq_{x}}=2\pi L_{y}\ \left(\frac{N}{2\hbar}\partial_{x}^{3}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})\right)^{-2/3}Ai^{2}\left[\frac{q_{x}+N\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})}{\hbar}\left(\frac{N}{2\hbar}\partial_{x}^{3}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{m})\right)^{-1/3}\right]

(12)

The scattering cross section far from the extremum point in the quasi-classical approach can be expressed as follows
[
6
]

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dq_{x}}=L_{y}\left[\frac{1}{\left|N\partial_{x}^{2}\bar{\chi}_{0}^{(1)}\right|}+\frac{1}{\left|N\partial_{x}^{2}\bar{\chi}_{0}^{(2)}\right|}\right]

\displaystyle x_{i},i=1,2:q_{x}+N\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{i})=0

(13)

where
\bar{\chi}_{0}^{(i)}=\bar{\chi}_{0}(x_{i})
.

3
Functions related to the potential

In order to obtain a specific form of the differential cross section for our case, we need to find the functions contained in the cross section expression. Such functions are listed below.

The potential of an individual atom in a target with a nuclear charge
Z|e|
is considered to be a screened Coulomb potential with a screening radius
R

\displaystyle u(\vec{r})=\frac{Z|e|}{r}\ exp\left[-\frac{r}{R}\right]

(14)

The
\chi_{0}
function for this potential, mentioning that for ultrarelativistic particles
v\rightarrow c
, is

\displaystyle\chi_{0}=-\frac{e}{c}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dz\>u(r)=\pm 2Z\alpha\>K_{0}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)

(15)

where
\alpha
is a fine structure constant,
K_{0}
is a modified Bessel function of the second kind. The sign "
+
" corresponds to the function for electrons, "
-
" for positrons.

Let us obtain the averaged
\bar{\chi}_{0}
function using its Fourier components
\chi_{\mu}

\displaystyle\chi_{\mu}=\frac{\pm 2Z\alpha}{\mu^{2}+R^{-2}}

(16)

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}_{0}=\int du_{x}\ dy_{0}\ f(u_{x},y_{0})\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d^{2}\mu}{2\pi}\>e^{i{\mu}_{x}\left(x-{u}_{x}\right)+i{\mu}_{y}\left(y-{y}_{0}\right)}\chi_{\mu}

(17)

where
f
is the distribution function of atoms in the target, which in the
y
direction corresponds to the equally probable distribution of atoms and in the
x
direction is determined by thermal fluctuations:

\displaystyle f(u_{x},y_{0})=\ \frac{\exp\left[-\frac{u_{x}^{2}}{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}\right]}{\sqrt{2\pi\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\frac{1}{L_{y}},\ y_{0}\in\left[-\frac{L_{y}}{2},\frac{L_{y}}{2}\right]

(18)

After averaging, the
\bar{\chi}_{0}
function is

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}_{0}=\pm Z\alpha\ \frac{\pi R}{L_{y}}\ e^{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}\left\{e^{\frac{x}{R}}\>Erfc\left[\sqrt{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\right]+e^{-\frac{x}{R}}\>Erfc\left[\sqrt{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\right]\right\}

(19)

The fact that the
\bar{\chi}_{0}
function does not depend on
y
is in good agreement with the equally probable distribution of atoms in this direction. Derivatives of the
\bar{\chi}_{0}
function are also required to obtain the scattering cross section. The first derivative from a quasi-classical point of view determines the transmitted momentum
\left(q_{x}=-N\ \partial_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}\right)
and has the following form:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}=\frac{\pm\pi Z\alpha}{L_{y}}\ e^{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}\left\{e^{\frac{x}{R}}\>Erfc\left[\sqrt{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\right]-e^{-\frac{x}{R}}\>Erfc\left[\sqrt{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\right]\right\}

(20)

The second and third derivatives have the following expressions, respectively

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}=\frac{\pm\pi Z\alpha}{RL_{y}}\ e^{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}\left\{e^{\frac{x}{R}}\>Erfc\left[\sqrt{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\right]+\right.

\displaystyle\left.+e^{-\frac{x}{R}}\>Erfc\left[\sqrt{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\right]-\frac{2\sqrt{2}\ R}{\sqrt{\pi\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\ exp\left[-\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}-\frac{x^{2}}{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}\right]\right\}

(21)

\displaystyle\partial^{3}_{x}\bar{\chi}_{0}=\frac{\pm\pi Z\alpha}{R^{2}L_{y}}\ e^{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}\left\{e^{\frac{x}{R}}\>Erfc\left[\sqrt{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}+\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\right]-\right.

\displaystyle\left.-e^{-\frac{x}{R}}\>Erfc\left[\sqrt{\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}}-\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\right]+\frac{2\sqrt{2}\ R^{2}x}{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}\sqrt{\pi\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}}\ exp\left[-\frac{\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}{2R^{2}}-\frac{x^{2}}{2\overline{u_{x}^{2}}}\right]\right\}

(22)

Conclusions

In the present paper, we considered the problem of fast charged particles scattering on the crystal plane of atoms in the eikonal approximation, which allows us to excede the limits of the Born approximation. Extending our previous research
[
5
,
6
]
, we obtained the differential scattering cross section for the specified problem, avoiding the usage of an additional approximation (stationary phase method). Taking into account the configuration of the crystal plane made it possible to reduce the scattering problem to a one-dimensional one. It was also shown that near the extremum of the transmitted momentum dependence on the impact parameter, the scattering cross section has an approximation that indicates the presence of the rainbow scattering effect. We note that omitting
\chi_{0}^{2}
terms (which took place in (
4
)) leads us to approximation of continuous potential which does not count the incoherent scattering. The incoherent scattering in its turn is of particular interest since it may impact the scattering cross section.

Also, the differential scattering cross sections for various target parameters were obtained. These results demonstrate how the frequency of cross section oscillations near the rainbow point is related to the target thickness. The application regions of the eikonal approximation for the given problem are also specified.

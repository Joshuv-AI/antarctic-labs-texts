Efficient Federated Low Rank Matrix Completion
Ahmed Ali Abbasi and Namrata Vaswani

Abstract
In this work, we develop and analyze a Gradient Descent (GD) based solution, called Alternating GD and Minimization (AltGDmin), for efficiently solving the low rank matrix completion (LRMC) in a federated setting. LRMC involves recovering an n × q n\times q rank- r r matrix 𝑿 ⋆ {\bm{X}}^{\star} from a subset of its entries when r ≪ min ⁡ ( n , q ) r\ll\min(n,q) . Our theoretical guarantees (iteration and sample complexity bounds) imply that AltGDmin is the most communication-efficient solution in a federated setting, is one of the fastest, and has the second best sample complexity among all iterative solutions to LRMC. In addition, we also prove two important corollaries. (a) We provide a guarantee for AltGDmin for solving the noisy LRMC problem. (b) We show how our lemmas can be used to provide an improved sample complexity guarantee for AltMin, which is the fastest centralized solution.



I
Introduction

In this work, we develop and analyze a fast and communication-efficient solution for efficiently solving the low rank matrix completion (LRMC) problem in a federated setting. LRMC involves recovering/learning a low rank (LR) matrix from a small number of its observed entries. Our proposed algorithm, and the sample and iteration complexity guarantees that we prove for it, are also useful in centralized settings.
Federation means that (i) different subsets of the data are acquired at different distributed nodes; and (ii) all nodes can only communicate with the central node or “center”. Communication-efficiency is a key concern with all distributed algorithms, including federated ones. Privacy of the data is another concern in a federated setting. In this work, “privacy” means the following: the nodes’ raw data (here observed matrix entries) cannot be shared with the center; and the algorithm should be such that the center cannot recover any of the columns of the LR matrix.

I-C

Contributions and Novelty

We develop and analyze the AltGDmin algorithm for solving the LRMC problem. The design of AltGDmin is motivated by a federated setting.
To our best knowledge, our work provides the first theoretical guarantees for solving LRMC in a federated setting; see Theorem
2.1
. The sample and iteration complexity bounds that we prove are applicable in centralized settings as well. In addition, we also prove two important corollaries. (a) We provide a guarantee for AltGDmin for solving the noisy LRMC problem. (b) We also show how our lemmas can be used to provide an improved sample complexity guarantee for AltMin which is the fastest centralized solution for LRMC.

Using our results we can argue that, in a federated setting, AltGDmin is the most communication-efficient solution. It is also one of the two fastest private solutions and has the second smallest sample complexity. Our new corollary for AltMin (and smooth AltMin) proves that it also has the same sample complexity as that of AltGDmin. This discussion treats
\kappa,\mu
and
\gamma
(number of nodes) as numerical constants. We discuss these points in more detail in Sec.
III
and in Table
I
.

While AltGDmin has been studied for solving the LRCS problem, there are some important differences between the two problems that necessitate new analysis approaches. LRMC samples are row and column-wise local, while LRCS samples are global for each column. Because of this, LRMC requires assuming left and right singular vectors’ incoherence and proving the same for each new iterate of
{\bm{U}}
and
\bm{B}
. LRCS needed this only for
\bm{B}
. We need a new approach to show the incoherence of
{\bm{U}}
at each iteration since the update of
{\bm{U}}
in AltGDmin is different from that in all existing solutions to LRMC.
FactGD for LRMC
[
11
]
projects
{\bm{U}}
(and
\bm{B}
) onto the space of row (column) incoherent matrices after each GD step; this automatically ensures incoherence after each update. AltMin
[
3
]
updates
{\bm{U}}
by solving an LS problem (and not by GD), so the incoherence proof there is different too. ProjGD
[
7
]
implemented projected GD for
{\bm{X}}
itself (does not factorize it). There are other important proof differences too. We explain these in Sec.

.

II
Alternating GD and Minimization (AltGDMin) Algorithm and Guarantees

II-A

AltGDmin Algorithm

The goal is to minimize the following squared loss cost function

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\check{\bm{B}},\,\check{\bm{U}}\colon\check{\bm{U}}^{\intercal}\check{\bm{U}}=\bm{I}\end{subarray}}f(\check{\bm{U}},\check{\bm{B}}),\ f(\check{\bm{U}},\check{\bm{B}}):=\|(\bm{Y}-\check{\bm{U}}\check{\bm{B}})_{\Omega}\|_{F}^{2}

(2)

We impose the orthornormal columns constraint on
\check{\bm{U}}
as one way to ensure that the norm of
{\bm{U}}
does not keep increasing or decreasing continuously with algorithm iterations, while that of
\bm{B}
decreases or increases.

AltGDmin proceeds as follows. We initialize
{\bm{U}}
as explained below; this approach is adapted from that in FactGD
[
11
]
.
After the initialization, different from AltMin
[
3
]
, which used alternating exact minimization for both
{\bm{U}}
and
\bm{B}
, and different from FactGD
[
11
]
, which used GD for both
{\bm{U}}
and
\bm{B}
,
AltGDmin alternates between exact minimization over
\bm{B}
and a single GD step for
{\bm{U}}
. The GD step is followed by an orthonormalization (QR) step. The above simple changes help ensure that the AltGDmin iteration complexity is better than that of FactGD and comparable to that of AltMin. The use of exact minimization for one of the variables, along with not using the projection onto incoherent matrices step (replacing it by QR), helps ensure that AltGDmin provably converges with a nearly constant step size. Because of this, the AltGDmin iteration complexity is better than that of FactGD by a factor of
r
.

II-A
1
Initialization

As in most previous work
[
3
,
11
]
, the first step of our initialization is to compute the top
r
singular vectors of
\bm{Y}
; denote the matrix formed by these singular vectors by
{\bm{U}}^{(00)}
. This is followed by a step to make
{\bm{U}}^{(00)}
incoherent. We borrow this step from
[
11
]
. It involves projecting
{\bm{U}}^{(00)}
onto the space of row incoherent matrices,

{{\mathcal{U}}}:=\{\check{\bm{U}}:\|{\check{\bm{u}}}^{j}\|\leq\mu\sqrt{r/n}\}

i.e., computing
\Pi_{{\mathcal{U}}}({\bm{U}}^{(00)})=\min_{\check{\bm{U}}\in{\mathcal{U}}}\|\check{\bm{U}}-{\bm{U}}^{(00)}\|_{F}
. It is easy to see that this projection can be obtained in closed form as follows:

[\Pi_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\bm{M})]^{j}=\bm{m}^{j}\cdot\min\left(1,\frac{\mu\sqrt{r/n}}{\|\bm{m}^{j}\|}\right),\ \text{ for all }j\in[n]

In words, if a row of
\bm{M}
has
\ell_{2}
norm that is more than the threshold
\mu\sqrt{r/n}
, then one renormalizes the row so that its norm equals the threshold. If the norm is less than this threshold, then we do not change it. Clearly this is an order
nr
time operation.
The last step is computing a QR decomposition of
\Pi_{{\mathcal{U}}}({\bm{U}}^{(00)})
. This needs time of order
nr^{2}
.

II-A
2
AltGDmin iterations

We update
\bm{B}
and
{\bm{U}}
as follows for a total of
T
iterations.
The minimization over
\bm{B}
is a decoupled least squares (LS) problem since
f(\check{\bm{U}},\check{\bm{B}})
decouples as
f(\check{\bm{U}},\check{\bm{B}})=\sum_{k\in[q]}\|\bm{y}-\bm{S}_{k}\check{\bm{U}}\check{\bm{b}}_{k}\|^{2}
.
We update
\bm{b}_{k}
as

\displaystyle\bm{b}_{k}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\check{\bm{b}}}\|\bm{y}-\bm{S}_{k}{\bm{U}}\check{\bm{b}}\|^{2}=(\bm{S}_{k}{\bm{U}})^{\dagger}\bm{y}_{k},\ \text{ for all }k\in[q]

Let
{\bm{U}}_{k}:=\bm{S}_{k}{\bm{U}}
, then
\bm{b}_{k}:={\bm{U}}_{k}^{\dagger}\bm{y}_{k}
. Recall that
\bm{M}^{\dagger}:=(\bm{M}^{\intercal}\bm{M})^{-1}\bm{M}^{\intercal}
.
We emphasise here that we write things as above for notational ease in both explaining the idea and the proof. The time complexity for computing
\bm{b}_{k}
depends only on the sub-matrix of
{\bm{U}}_{k}
with nonzero rows. This is of size
|\Omega_{k}|\times r
. The time cost of computing
\bm{b}_{k}
is thus order
|\Omega_{k}|r^{2}
for a given
k
. Hence, the total cost for all nodes is
\sum_{k}|\Omega_{k}|r^{2}=|\Omega|r^{2}
.

We update
{\bm{U}}
by one GD step followed by orthnormalization using QR, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{U}}^{+}={\bm{U}}-\eta\nabla_{\bm{U}}f({\bm{U}},\bm{B})),\text{ and }{\bm{U}}^{+}=\mathrm{QR}(\tilde{\bm{U}}^{+})

The gradient is

\nabla_{\bm{U}}f({\bm{U}},\bm{B})=2(({\bm{U}}\bm{B})_{\Omega}-\bm{Y})\bm{B}^{\intercal}=2\sum_{k=1}^{q}(\bm{S}_{k}{\bm{U}}\bm{b}_{k}-\bm{y}_{k})\bm{b}_{k}^{\intercal}

For the gradient computation, the time cost is
\sum_{k}|\Omega_{k}|r=|\Omega|r
. The QR step needs time of order
nr^{2}
.

. Sample splitting (line 1) is assumed, as is common in most structured data recovery literature, e.g.,
[

,

]

, sample-splitting is assumed for obtaining provable guarantees for all iterative solutions for LRMC (and those for LRCS) in which one or both of the alternating steps is a minimization step.

3
Federated AltGDmin

In a federated setting, at each algorithm iteration,
t
, each node
\ell=1,2,\dots,\gamma
performs two operations i) updating
\bm{b}_{k}
by the LS solution, for all
k\in\bm{S}_{\ell}
; and ii) computation of the partial gradient
\sum_{k\in\bm{S}_{\ell}}[\nabla_{{\bm{U}}}f({\bm{U}},\bm{B})]_{k}=\sum_{k\in\bm{S}_{\ell}}({\bm{U}}_{k}\bm{b}_{k}-\bm{y})_{\Omega_{k}}\bm{b}_{k}^{\intercal}
.
Only the

partial gradient needs to be sent to the center. The center sums the received partial gradients, implements the GD step, and
computes the QR decomposition, and broadcasts the updated
{\bm{U}}^{+}
to all the nodes. This is used by the nodes in the next iteration.
The communication cost from nodes to center is equal to the number of nonzero entries in
\nabla_{{\bm{U}}}f({\bm{U}},\bm{B})

r\cdot\max(n,\sum_{k\in\bm{S}_{\ell}}|\Omega_{k}|)=r\cdot\min(n,(|\Omega|/\gamma))=nr
since we assume

is a necessary lower bound for any approach to work. The center to nodes communication cost is also
nr
. The computation cost is as explained above with
|\Omega|

|\Omega|/\gamma
.

The initialization can be federated using the power method applied to
\bm{Y}\bm{Y}^{\intercal}
. This costs order
\sum_{k\in\bm{S}_{\ell}}|\Omega_{k}|r
per power method iteration. The upstream (node to center) per-node communication cost is
\min(n,\sum_{k\in\bm{S}_{\ell}}|\Omega_{k}|)r=nr
and the downstream cost is also
nr
. The power method converges linearly and thus, for

accuracy,
\log(1/\epsilon_{svd})

Details of this federation as well as detailed time and communication cost discussion are provided in
[

II-B

AltGDmin Guarantee

We can prove the following for AltGDmin. Our theorem statements and proofs assume
n\leq q
for simplicity. Recall from above that the per-iteration per-node computation and communication complexities of AltGDmin are
\max(nr^{2},(|\Omega|/\gamma)r^{2})
and
\max(nr,\min(n,(|\Omega|/\gamma))r)=nr
. Here per-node takes the maximum of the node or center complexities.
We use this and the iteration complexity (expression for
T
) derived in the result below to provide an expression for its total per-node computation and communication complexities.

Let
p_{tot}:=2Tp
. Our proof of Theorem
2.1
given next obtains a lower bound on the required
p
in each algorithm iteration.

Theorem 2.1
.

Pick an
\epsilon<1
.
Assume that Assumption
1.1
holds and that, entries of
{\bm{X}}^{\star}
are observed independently with probability

satisfying
nqp_{tot}>C\kappa^{6}\mu^{2}qr^{2}\log q\log({1}/{\epsilon})
. Set
\eta=0.5/(p{\sigma_{\max}^{*2}})
and
T=C\kappa^{2}\log(1/\epsilon)
. Then, with probability (w.p.) at least
1-4T/n^{3}
,

\mathrm{SD}_{F}({\bm{U}}^{(T)},{\bm{U}}^{\star}{})\leq\epsilon\text{ and }\|{\bm{X}}^{(T)}-{\bm{X}}^{\star}\|_{F}\leq\epsilon\|{\bm{X}}^{\star}\|.

(3)

(recall that
{\bm{X}}^{(T)}={\bm{U}}^{(T)}\bm{B}^{(T)}
).

The total per-node computation complexity of federated AltGDmin is
C\kappa^{2}\log(1/\epsilon)\cdot\max(n,|\Omega|/\gamma)r^{2}
and its total per-node communication complexity is
C\kappa^{2}\log(1/\epsilon)\cdot nr
.

The proof is given in Sec.
IV
and
V
. Before this, we discuss this result and the novel ideas used in our proof in Sec.

.

Observe that

matrix entries, and we set the GD step size,
\eta
, and the total number of iterations,

{\bm{X}}^{\star}
with
\epsilon
accuracy. The number of iterations needed is order
\log(1/\epsilon)
. We can achieve any
\epsilon

.

We state Theorem

III
Discussion and Proof Main Ideas/Novelty

III-A

Discussion

The average (expected value of) the required sample complexity is
nqp
. For this discussion, we assume
n\approx q
,
\kappa\approx\mu
,
\max(\log(1/\epsilon),r)=\log(1/\epsilon)
. Our guarantee given in Theorem
2.1
shows that the AltGDmin sample complexity is
\kappa^{6}\mu^{2}nr^{2}\log n\log(1/\epsilon)
. This is worse than that of FactGD by a factor of
\kappa^{2}\log(1/\epsilon)
. However, the iteration complexity of AltGDmin is
T=\kappa^{2}\log(1/\epsilon)
while that of FactGD is
T=\kappa\mu r\log(1/\epsilon)
. Assuming
\kappa\approx\mu
, this means the AltGDmin iteration complexity is better by a factor of
r
. This is also demonstrated numerically in Sec.
VIII
.
Per iteration, both have the same communication cost, while the FactGD computation cost is lower than that of AltGDmin by a factor of
r
.
Consequently, the total AltGDmin communication cost is lower than that of FactGD by a factor of
r
, while the total computation costs are similar. We provide a summary in Table
I
.

The ProjGD sample complexity is worse than that of AltGDmin by a factor of
\log n\log(1/\epsilon)
. Moreover, the ProjGD communication cost per iteration is much higher than that of AltGDmin and FactGD. The same is true for its computation cost making it one of the slowest and most communication inefficient.

The original sample complexity of AltMin and Smooth AltMin is much higher than that of AltGDmin or FactGD (see Table
I
). However, the corollary that we prove for AltMin later in Sec
VII
shows that the AltMin sample complexity is
\kappa^{4}\mu^{2}nr^{2}\log n\log(1/\epsilon)
and its iteration complexity is
\log(1/\epsilon)
.

In summary, when compared with all other methods, AltGDmin has the lowest total communication cost. It also has one of the lowest total computation costs and the second lowest sample cost. Moreover as we argue in detail in
[
1
]
, AltGDmin is private and requires just one data exchange per algorithm iteration. Federated FactGD can be made private with two data exchanges per algorithm iteration. AltMin can be made private by solving the LS problem needed to update
{\bm{U}}
using GD rather than the closed form solution. This requires order
\log(1/\epsilon)
extra data exchanges per main algorithm iteration. AltMin-Private is much slower both in numerical experiments and using theoretical complexities; see Table
I
and Sec.
VIII
.

The reason that AltGDmin needs a factor of
\log(1/\epsilon)
in its sample complexity is because its proof uses sample-splitting. To our best knowledge, all guarantees for all solutions for LRMC or LRCS that factor
{\bm{X}}
as
{\bm{X}}={\bm{U}}\bm{B}
, and in which one (or both) of the two alternating steps is a minimization step, use sample-splitting
[
3
,
12
,
24
,
25
,
22
,
23
]
. Some of this cited work provides guarantees for LR phase retrieval which is a phaseless measurements’ generalization of LRCS; and hence any LRPR solution automatically solves LRCS. All three problems involve recovery from non-global measurements (no scalar measurement depends on the entire
{\bm{X}}^{\star}
). For such problems, properties such as restricted isometry property cannot be proved.

The reason that AltGDmin iteration and sample complexity need an extra factor of
\kappa^{2}
is due to the orthonormalization step (QR step) when updating
{\bm{U}}
. For any algorithm that updates one or both of
{\bm{U}},\bm{B}
by GD, such a step is one way to prevent the norm of the estimate
{\bm{U}}
from increasing/decreasing over iterations (while that of
\bm{B}
decreases/increases). The other way to ensure this is to add a norm balancing term in the cost function for the GD step for
{\bm{U}}
, as done in FactGD. However this would then also require a row norm clipping step for
{\bm{U}}
at each iteration, like in FactGD. Use of these two steps to replace the QR step can possibly help improve its dependence on
\kappa
. But it will also mean that we cannot borrow the overall proof approach developed for AltGDmin for LRCS in
[
23
]
that allows us to get a guarantee with a (nearly) constant step size, consequently, a (nearly) best iteration complexity of
\kappa^{2}\log(1/\epsilon)
.

III-B

Proof Novelty

1.

The most important difference between LRCS and LRMC is that the LRMC proofs require incoherence of each iterate of
{\bm{U}}
and of
\bm{B}
. When analyzing AltGDmin for LRCS in
[
22
,
23
]
, this was needed only for
\bm{B}
because LRCS measurements are column-wise global (matrix
\bm{A}_{k}
is dense and so each entry of
\bm{y}_{k}
depends on the entire column
\bm{x}^{\star}_{k}
). Our proof of incoherence of each updated
{\bm{U}}
needs a different approach than that used in all past work on LRMC as well because our algorithm is different. (i) ProjGD
[
7
]
does not use a factorized representation for
{\bm{X}}
and hence the gradient w.r.t.
{\bm{U}}
does not exist for it. (ii) We do not update
{\bm{U}}
by LS and hence we cannot use the approach used for AltMin or Smooth AltMin
[
3
,
12
]
. (iii) FactGD
[
11
]
uses row norm clipping to ensure incoherence at each algorithm iteration, while AltGDmin does not use this (except for initialization).
The details of our approach are explained below in the proof outlines, and in Lemma
4.6
(and proof) and its use in proving Theorem
2.1
.

As noted earlier, not using row norm clipping at each iteration is one reason why we can borrow the overall proof technique used for LRCS
[
23
]
in order to prove a correctness guarantee with a constant GD step size. The other reason is that we use minimization for updating
\bm{B}
(and not GD).

2.

Unlike LRCS, LRMC measurements are both row-wise and column-wise local. Consequently, it is not possible to get a tight column-wise bound (bound on
\|\bm{b}_{k}-{\bm{U}}^{\intercal}\bm{x}^{\star}_{k}\|
, and hence on
\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{x}^{\star}_{k}\|
, for each
k
) under the desired sample complexity. We can only bound
\|\bm{B}-{\bm{U}}^{\intercal}{\bm{X}}^{\star}\|_{F}
and
\|{\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{\star}\|_{F}
. This has implications on how we bound the gradient norm (both the gradient expected value and its deviation from it). This is one reason why the use of
\mathrm{SD}_{F}
(instead of
\mathrm{SD}_{2}
) in our bounding helps prove a better guarantee.

3.

We need to use the matrix Bernstein inequality
[
26
]
for bounding all of terms, instead of the concentration bounds used in
[
23
]
(sub-Gausian Hoeffding or sub-exponential Bernstein inequality followed by an epsilon-net argument).

4.

Our initialization guarantee is better than that of AltMin and Smooth Altmin
[
3
,
12
]
by a factor of
r^{3}
and
r
respectively, and comparable to that of FactGD.
The reason for this is that we adapt the row norm clipping idea of
[
11
]
for our initialization step.
This approach is better than the entry-wise clipping used in
[
3
]
.
Row norm clipping can be analyzed in terms of
\mathrm{SD}_{F}({\bm{U}}^{(0)},{\bm{U}}^{\star}{})
and this is the other reason we use
\mathrm{SD}_{F}
in our proofs (see Sec.
III-D
).

5.

All analysis of AltGDmin for LRCS has been for the noise-free case. In this work, we also provide an extension of our result to the more practical noisy case. Our proof explains a general strategy for doing this for any iterative algorithm.

6.

Lastly, we also show how our lemmas can be used to improve the sample complexity dependence of AltMin and Smoothed AltMin on
r
from
r^{4.5}
and
r^{3}
respectively to
r^{2}
.

25

(the latter used a similar approach for analyzing the AltMin algorithm for LR phase retrieval).

Corollary 6.1
.

Let

\epsilon_{noise}:=\max_{jk}\frac{\lvert w_{jk}\rvert}{\lvert x^{*}_{jk}\rvert}

In the setting of Theorem
2.1
, if

\epsilon_{noise}\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{r}\kappa^{3}},

then, by setting

, we can guarantee that

\ \text{ and }\ \|{\bm{X}}^{(T)}-{\bm{X}}^{\star}\|_{F}\leq\mathrm{SD}_{F}({\bm{U}}^{(T)},{\bm{U}}^{\star}{}){\sigma_{\max}^{*}}.

In general, for any
\epsilon

T=C\kappa^{2}\log(1/\epsilon)

\mathrm{SD}_{F}({\bm{U}}^{(T)},{\bm{U}}^{\star}{})\leq\max(\epsilon,\kappa^{2}\sqrt{r}\epsilon_{noise})

Proof.

Corollary
6.1
extends our noise-free case proof to the noisy case using the following simple ideas.
First we assume that the noise level is small enough so that accurate initialization is possible; this requires noise level,
\epsilon_{noise}\sqrt{r}

for our required value of

\delta^{(0)}

Next, at each iteration, we attempt to bound terms and prove incoherence for the updated

for a

. Details are given in Appendix
A

AltGDMin

AltGDMin (Fed.) has faster convergence because of low communication complexity and low time complexity at the center. The upstream communication complexity,
\min(\sum_{k\in\bm{S}_{\ell}}\nabla_{{\bm{U}}}f({\bm{U}},\bm{b}_{k}),nr)=nr

VIII-
5
FactGD

\nabla_{{\bm{U}}}f=\nabla_{{\bm{X}}}\bm{B}^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
and
\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}

nr

\bm{B}
is updated locally (GD iteration and normalizing) at the nodes but this requires two data exchanges with the center. This is because the gradient of

(\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\intercal}-{\bm{U}}^{\intercal}{\bm{U}})\bm{B}

\bm{B}\bm{B}^{\intercal}

O(nr+r^{2})

AltMin (Fed./NotPrvt.) is slower than AltGDMin (Fed.) because the
n

{\bm{U}}
-update LS problems are solved sequentially at the center with complexity

IX
Conclusions

In this work we developed and analyzed the Alternating GD and Minimization (AltGDmin) algorithm for solving the low rank matrix completion (LRMC) problem. The design of AltGDmin is motivated by a federated setting.
Using our results (sample and iteration complexity bounds) we argued that, in a federated setting, AltGDmin is the most communication-efficient solution. It is also one of the two fastest private solutions and has the second smallest sample complexity. In addition, we were able to use our lemmas to prove an improved sample complexity guarantee for AltMin, which is the fastest centralized solution for LRMC.

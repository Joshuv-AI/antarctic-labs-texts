Counterexamples in 4 4 -manifold topology
Daniel Kasprowski, Mark Powell, Arunima Ray

Abstract
. We illustrate the rich landscape of 4-manifold topology through the lens of counterexamples. We consider several of the most commonly studied equivalence relations on 4 4 -manifolds and how they are related to one another. We explain implications e.g. that h h -cobordant manifolds are stably homeomorphic, and we provide examples illustrating the failure of other potential implications. The information is conveniently organised in a flowchart and a table.

Next we discuss the various notions of stable equivalence.

Definition 2.1
.

The
4
-manifolds
M
and
N
are said to be
stably homeomorphic
if there are integers
s,t
such that
M\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{s}}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
and
N\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{t}}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
are homeomorphic.
They are said to be
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
-stably homeomorphic
if there are integers
s,t
such that
M\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{s}}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and
N\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{t}}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
are homeomorphic, for some choices of connected sum.

Definition 2.2
.

The smooth
4
-manifolds
M
and
N
are said to be
stably diffeomorphic
if there are integers
s,t
such that
M\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{s}}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
and
N\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{t}}(S^{2}\times S^{2})
are diffeomorphic. They are said to be
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
-stably diffeomorphic
if there are integers
s,t
such that
M\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{s}}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and
N\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{t}}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
are diffeomorphic, for some choices of connected sum.

Note that
S^{2}\times S^{2}
admits an orientation reversing self-diffeomorphism, so there is essentially only one choice of connected sum. On the other hand
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
does not admit any such diffeomorphism (nor homeomorphism), so for oriented manifolds there are two possible connected sums up to diffeomorphism/homeomorphism, usually denoted
M\#\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and
M\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
. Therefore, the definitions above say that
M
and
N
are
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
-stably diffeomorphic (resp. homeomorphic) if there are integers
s_{1},s_{2},t_{1},t_{2}
such that
M\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{s_{1}}}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{s_{2}}}\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
and
N\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{t_{1}}}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\,\,{\scalebox{1.2}{\#}^{t_{2}}}\overline{\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
are diffeomorphic (resp. homeomorphic). Note that for nonorientable manifolds, there is a unique connected sum
N\#\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. We remark that some authors require
s=t
in the definition of stable homeomorphism and diffeomorphism. Manifolds
M
and
N
which are stably homeomorphic or diffeomorphic have
s=t
in our definition exactly when
\chi(M)=\chi(N)
.

We emphasise that “stably” refers by default to connected sum with copies of
S^{2}\times S^{2}
, and only “
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
-stably” refers to connected sum with copies of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

To motivate
Definitions

2.1
and
2.2
, consider an alternative strategy to classify manifolds, based on Kreck’s modified surgery
[

and realised by Hambleton-Kreck for e.g. 4-manifolds with finite cyclic fundamental group in
[

: first classify manifolds up to stable homeomorphism, and then investigate the homeomorphism types within each stable class. In the latter step one attempts to prove that

This strategy in principle also applies to diffeomorphism classifications, but the cancellation step is much harder. Similarly, another approach to classification is to first classify manifolds up to
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
-stable equivalence, and then attempt to blow down extraneous
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
summands.

Definition 2.3
.

The
4
-manifolds
M
and

-cobordant
if there is a

(W;M,N)

smoothly

Associated with a homotopy equivalence
f\colon X\to Y
between CW complexes
X
and
Y

Whitehead torsion

\tau(f)\in\Wh(\pi_{1}(X))
, with values in the
Whitehead group
of
\pi_{1}(X)
, which we define next. Let
\GL(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])
be the stable general linear group, and let
\E(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])
be the subgroup of elementary matrices, i.e. consisting of products of the matrices that produce row and column operations. By definition

\K_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]):=\GL(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])/\E(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])

and
\Wh(\pi_{1}(X)):=\K_{1}(\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])/\{\pm(g)\mid g\in\pi_{1}(X)\}
. For example
\Wh(\{e\})=0
, essentially because of the Euclidean algorithm. See
[
33
]
for an accessible introduction to simple homotopy theory, including more examples of Whitehead groups and the definition of Whitehead torsion.

Definition 2.4
.

A homotopy equivalence
f\colon X\to Y
between CW complexes
X
and
Y
is a
simple homotopy equivalence
if its Whitehead torsion
\tau(f)
vanishes.

By Chapman’s theorem
[
32

the Whitehead torsion
\tau(f)
only depends on the homeomorphism type of
X
and
Y
. Hence Whitehead torsion is well-defined for homotopy equivalences between manifolds which are homeomorphic to CW complexes, e.g. smooth manifolds or closed manifolds of dimension
\neq 4
. It is an open question whether every topological
4

4

However, we can define the notion of simple homotopy equivalence of topological manifolds as follows.
Embed
M
in high-dimensional Euclidean space. By
[

]

D(M)
admitting a triangulation. The inclusion map
z_{M}\colon M\to D(M)
of the

Let
z_{M}^{-1}
denote the homotopy inverse of
z_{M}
.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a homotopy equivalence
f\colon M\to N
between topological manifolds (not necessarily of the same dimension) is a
simple homotopy equivalence
if the composition
z_{N}\circ f\circ z_{M}^{-1}\colon D(M)\to D(N)
is a simple homotopy equivalence.

The Whitehead torsion
\tau(W;M)
of an
h
-cobordism
(W;M,N)
is by definition the Whitehead torsion of the inclusion map
M\hookrightarrow W
. This also coincides with the Whitehead torsion of the relative chain complex
C_{*}(\widetilde{W},\widetilde{M})
, where
\widetilde{W}
and

are the universal covers.

The
4

An

For the remainder of this section, let

Definition 4.1

The

Note that

is necessarily also a homotopy equivalence in the definition above.

The

of

and

.

Note that

is necessarily a simple homotopy equivalence.

is a good group. Then every

5.2.

5.13.

The existence of these manifolds is the content of

*Theorem 1.2. We restate the theorem and give an original proof below.

Theorem 5.12
.

Let

The proof will use the simple surgery exact sequence.
The simple

(p+2)/2&p\text{ even}.\end{cases}

In both cases

since

.
To compute these

[
118
]
to obtain

L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p\times\mathbb{Z}])\cong L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p])\oplus L_{4}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}/p]).

Then

[

]

[

[

[

]

[

[

]
*p. 227 and Proposition 12.1 for general

The normal maps

•

be
\partial^{-1}(\widehat{\Omega}_{4}^{\Spin}(M))
.
By

*Lemma 2.2, there is a long exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(B)}

. There we will restrict to

a power of

5.14.
Simple homotopy equivalent,
h
-cobordant
4
-manifolds that are not
s
-cobordant

We construct arbitrarily large collections of closed, orientable, topological 4-manifolds that are simple homotopy equivalent and
h
-cobordant but not topologically
s
-cobordant. This will prove
Theorem

1.10
, which we restate below. We will employ the same scheme as in the previous subsection. The manifolds in each collection will be simple homotopy equivalent and
h
-cobordant to a fixed 4-manifold
L_{2^{r},1}\times S^{1}
, for some
r
. We will show that by making
r
large enough we can obtain a collection of 4-manifolds of any given size, with the following properties.

•

They are orientable and have nontrivial fundamental group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/{2^{r}}\times\mathbb{Z}
for some
r\geq 8
.

•

They are all simple homotopy equivalent and topologically
h
-cobordant to one another. As a result, they are homotopy equivalent, stably homeomorphic, and
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
-stably homeomorphic. They are are pairwise not topologically
s
-cobordant and therefore not homeomorphic.

•

Since they are homotopy equivalent they all have vanishing Euler characteristic, the same as
L_{2^{r},1}\times S^{1}
.

•

They are all stably homeomorphic and
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
-stably homeomorphic.

•

We do not know whether they are smoothable, and therefore the smooth questions are not applicable.

Theorem
1.10
.

For every
n\geq 1
, there is a collection
\{N_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of closed, orientable, topological 4-manifolds, that are all simple homotopy equivalent and
h
-cobordant to one another, but which are pairwise not
s
-cobordant.

Proof.

Let
M_{r}:=L_{2^{r},1}\times S^{1}
, for
r\geq 1
.
Let
\pi_{r}:=\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}\times\mathbb{Z}
, and let
G_{r}:=\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}
. The proof will again use the surgery sequence, both with the
h
and
s
decorations. We begin by investigating the individual terms.
Shaneson splitting
[
118
]
shows that

L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})\cong L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}G_{r})\oplus L_{4}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}G_{r})\text{ and }L_{5}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})\cong L_{5}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}G_{r})\oplus L_{4}^{p}(\mathbb{Z}G_{r}).

Then by
[
15
]
*Theorem 7, we know

L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}G_{r})=0=L_{5}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}G_{r}).

Here we use that
s=0
, in the notation of that theorem (this is a different
s
to the
s
-decoration of the
L
-groups).
By
[
15
]
*Theorem 1 we have that

L_{4}^{p}(\mathbb{Z}G_{r})=\mathbb{Z}^{m(r)}.

where
m(r):=2^{r-1}+1
.
By
[
15
]
*Theorem 2 and
[
30
]
*Theorem B:

L_{4}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}G_{r})=\mathbb{Z}^{m(r)}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n(r)}

where

n(r):=\lfloor 2(2^{r-2}+2)/3\rfloor-\lfloor r/2\rfloor-1.

Putting this all together we have:

L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{m(r)}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n(r)}\text{ and }L_{5}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{m(r)}.

The kernel of the forgetful map
L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})\to L_{5}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})
is the torsion summand

K:=\ker(L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})\to L_{5}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r}))\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n(r)},

since
L^{h}_{5}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})
is torsion free and the kernel of the map
L^{s}_{n}(R)\to L^{h}_{n}(R)
is 2-torsion, for every ring with involution
R
and every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by the Rothenberg exact sequence
[
118
]
*Proposition 4.1.
The elements in
K
act on the simple structure set of
M_{r}
, producing topological manifolds that are stably homeomorphic and simple homotopy equivalent to
M_{r}
.
We can compute the normal maps as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(M_{r}\times[0,1],M_{r}\times\{0,1\})

\displaystyle\cong H^{4}(M_{r}\times[0,1],M_{r}\times\{0,1\};\mathbb{Z})\oplus H^{2}(M_{r}\times[0,1],M_{r}\times\{0,1\};\mathbb{Z}/2)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2.

We have a direct sum because there is a 5-connected map
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{TOP}\to K(\mathbb{Z},4)\times K(\mathbb{Z}/2,2)

[
83
]
.
The
\mathbb{Z}
summand is detected by the ordinary signature, and in particular it maps to one of the multisignature summands in
\mathbb{Z}^{m(r)}\subseteq L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})
, under the surgery obstruction map. The torsion summand
\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
could map to the torsion elements in
L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})
. But at least a summand of
L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})
, one isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n(r)-2}
, acts nontrivially on the simple structure set. Note that
|(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{n(r)-2}|=2^{n(r)-2}
.

Still, it might be the case that some of the elements of the simple structure set
\mathcal{S}^{s}(M_{r})
obtained by this action of
L_{5}^{s}
are identified by the action of the simple homotopy self-equivalences
\hAut^{s}(M_{r})
. Note that
|\hAut^{s}(M_{r})|\leq|\hAut(M_{r})|
. We will show the following lemma.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
r\geq 8
. Then

2^{n(r)-2}/|\hAut(M_{r})|>1.

Moreover, for any
k
there exists an
r
with
2^{n(r)-2}/|\hAut(M_{r})|>k.

Every element
M_{r}(\kappa):=\kappa\cdot[\operatorname{Id}\colon M_{r}\to M_{r}]\in\mathcal{S}^{s}(M_{r})
arising from the action of an element
\kappa\in K
maps trivially to the homotopy structure set
\mathcal{S}^{h}(M_{r})
, by the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}^{s}(M_{r})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{5}^{h}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{S}^{h}(M_{r}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}

The
M_{r}(\kappa)
are all therefore
h
-cobordant to
M_{r}
. Since they arise from the action of the simple
L
-group
L_{5}^{s}(\mathbb{Z}\pi_{r})
, they are all simple homotopy equivalent and stably homeomorphic to one another.
By
Lemma

5.14
there is more than one orbit of
\{M_{r}(\kappa)\}_{\kappa\in K}/\hAut^{s}(M_{r})
, and these are not
s
-cobordant and therefore not homeomorphic manifolds.
Moreover, for a given
k
we can choose
r
so that there are at least
k
orbits, and therefore we find arbitrarily large collections.
∎

Now we prove
Lemma

5.14
.

Proof of
Lemma

5.14
.

As in
Section

5.13
, we use the braid from
[
64
]
. There we claimed that
\hAut(L_{p,q}\times S^{1})
is finite for any lens space
L_{p,q}
with
p
odd. Now we claim the same when
p=2^{r}
, and moreover in this case we compute an explicit upper bound, in terms of
r
, for the order of
\hAut(L_{p,q}\times S^{1})
.
The braid includes the exact sequence
[
64
]
*Corollary 2.13

(5.6)

\widehat{\Omega}_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r},M_{r})\to\hAut(M_{r})\to\hAut(B_{r}),

so we need upper bounds for the cardinalities of
\widehat{\Omega}_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r},M_{r})
and
\hAut(B_{r})
, where
\hAut(B_{r})
denotes the set of homotopy self-equivalences of the Postnikov 2-type
B_{r}
up to homotopy. The spin bordism group
\widehat{\Omega}_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r},M_{r})
also appeared in the proof of
Lemma

5.13
, and we refer the reader there for the definition.

First we compute
\hAut(B_{r})
. Since
\pi_{2}(M_{r})=0
, the Postnikov 2-type
B_{r}
is given by
B\pi_{r}
. Therefore,
\hAut(B_{r})\cong\Aut(\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}\times\mathbb{Z})
. We will now show that
|\hAut(B_{r})|=2^{2r}
. An arbitrary endomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}\times\mathbb{Z}
maps
(1,0)
to
(a,0)
and
(0,1)
to
(b,z)
, for some
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}
. For an automorphism, we must have that
z=\pm 1
and
a
must be a generator of
\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}
. Hence there are
2^{2r}=2\cdot 2^{r-1}\cdot 2^{r}
allowed choices for
z,a
and
b
.

To find an upper bound for
|\widehat{\Omega}_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r},M_{r})|
we use the exact sequence
[
64
]
*Lemma 2.2

(5.7)

\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r})\to\widehat{\Omega}_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r},M_{r})\to\widehat{\Omega}_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{4}^{\Spin}(B_{r}).

We investigate the bordism groups using the Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence.
The sequence we need, for
X\in\{M_{r},B_{r}\}
, is

E^{2}_{p,q}=H_{p}(X;\Omega_{q}^{\Spin})\Rightarrow\Omega_{p+q}^{\Spin}(X).

In the range of interest
0\leq q\leq 5
, we have:

\Omega_{q}^{\Spin}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\mathbb{Z},&\text{for }q=0,4,\\
\mathbb{Z}/2,&\text{for }q=1,2,\\
0,&\text{for }q=3,5.\\
\end{array}\right.

We also need the homology of
M_{r}
, which by the Künneth theorem with
\mathbb{Z}/2
-coefficients is as follows:

H_{k}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2,&\text{for }k=0,4,\\
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2},&\text{for }k=1,2,3,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Additionally
H_{1}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
. Since
B_{r}=B\pi_{r}
can be constructed from
M_{r}
by adding cells of dimension four and higher, for
A\in\{\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}\}
the induced map

H_{k}(M_{r};A)\to H_{k}(B_{r};A)

is an isomorphism for
k=0,1,2
and a surjection for
k=3
.
Finally we will need that
H_{5}(B_{r};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}
.

Now we use this homology information together with the spectral sequences to obtain an upper bound for the cardinality of the cokernel of the map
\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r})
from (
5.7
). The map
M_{r}\to B_{r}
induces maps between each page of the spectral sequences computing
\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(M_{r})
and
\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r})
.
The nonzero terms
E^{2}_{p,q}
on the
E^{2}
page with
p+q=5
are as follows:

H_{1}(-;\mathbb{Z}),H_{3}(-;\mathbb{Z}/2),H_{4}(-;\mathbb{Z}/2),\text{ and }H_{5}(-;\mathbb{Z}).

The maps
H_{1}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(B_{r};\mathbb{Z})
and
H_{3}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H_{3}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2)
are onto as explained above, so by naturality of the spectral sequence these terms do not contribute to the cokernel.
The mod 2 fundamental class in
H_{4}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
maps nontrivially to
H_{4}(B_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
, so possibly one
\mathbb{Z}/2
could contribute to the cokernel (whether or not it does so depends on differentials which we shall not take into account). The only other contribution to the cokernel comes from the term
H_{5}(B_{r};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}
.
As a result the cokernel of
\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r})

has at most
2^{r+1}
elements.

Next we find an upper bound on the size of

\ker\big(\widehat{\Omega}_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{4}^{\Spin}(B_{r})\big)
. We do this by considering the composition

\widehat{\Omega}_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{4}^{\Spin}(B_{r}).

Consider the Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence computing
\Omega_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})
. The nonzero terms

on the
E^{2}
page with
p+q=4
are

H_{0}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}),H_{2}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2),H_{3}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2),\text{ and }H_{4}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}).

The map
H_{0}(M_{r};\Omega_{4}^{\Spin})\cong\mathbb{Z}\to H_{0}(B_{r};\Omega_{4}^{\Spin})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is an isomorphism, as explained above.
The image of the inclusion
\widehat{\Omega}_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})
consists of elements with trivial image under the edge homomorphism
\Omega_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to H_{4}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z})\cong E^{2}_{p,0}
term on the
E^{2}
page, since the latter map is given by the mapping degree times the fundamental class
[M_{r}]
.

It follows that the kernel of
\widehat{\Omega}_{4}^{\Spin}(M_{r})\to\Omega_{4}^{\Spin}(B_{r})
is generated by elements coming from the terms
H_{2}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
and
H_{3}(M_{r};\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
, and so the kernel has at most
2^{4}
elements.
Thus by (
5.7
), we see that

|\widehat{\Omega}_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r},M_{r})|\leq 2^{r+1}\cdot 2^{4}=2^{r+5}.

It now follows from the sequence (
5.6
) that

|\hAut(M_{r})|\leq|\hAut(B_{r})|\cdot|\widehat{\Omega}_{5}^{\Spin}(B_{r},M_{r})|\leq 2^{2r}\cdot 2^{r+5}=2^{3r+5}.

An elementary calculation, recalling that
n(r)=\lfloor 2(2^{r-2}+2)/3\rfloor-\lfloor r/2\rfloor-1
, shows that for
r\geq 8
we have

n(r)-2-3r-5>0.

This implies that
2^{n(r)-2}/|\hAut(M_{r})|\geq 2^{n(r)-2}/2^{3r-5}=2^{n(r)-2-3r-5}>1
, as desired.
In addition, note that
n(r)-2-3r-5\to\infty
as
r\to\infty
. It follows that for a given
k
, there exists an
r
such that
2^{n(r)-2}/|\hAut(M_{r})|\geq 2^{n(r)-2-3r-5}>k
.
∎

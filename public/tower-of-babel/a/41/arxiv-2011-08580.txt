On the partial uniform ellipticity and complete conformal metrics with prescribed curvature functions on manifolds with boundary Thanks: The author was supported by NSFC Grant 11801587.
Rirong Yuan

Abstract
. We consider the problem of finding complete conformal metrics with prescribed curvature functions of the Einstein tensor and of more general modified Schouten tensors. To achieve this, we reveal an algebraic structure of a wide class of fully nonlinear equations. Our method is appropriate and delicate as shown by a topological obstruction. Finally, we discuss Hessian equations and Weingarten equations by confirming a key assumption.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we assume that
(M,g)
is a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
,
with smooth boundary
\partial M
,
\bar{M}=M\cup\partial M
, and with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
.
Let
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}
and

\mathrm{R}_{g}
denote the Ricci and scalar curvature of
g
, respectively.
The modified Schouten tensor of
(M,g)
is simply defined as

S_{g}^{\tau}=\frac{1}{n-2}(\mathrm{Ric}_{g}-\frac{\tau}{2(n-1)}\mathrm{R_{g}}g),

which includes the Schouten tensor and Einstein tensor as special cases.

In conformal geometry, it is interesting to find which function could arise as a curvature function of a conformal metric.
For the scalar curvature, according to the Yamabe problem proved by combining the work
of Trudinger, Aubin, Schoen
[
1
,
53
,
55
]
,
any closed Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
admits a conformal metric with constant scalar curvature. A fully nonlinear version of Yamabe problem, for the Schouten tensor
S_{g}^{1}
, on closed Riemannian manifolds was proposed in
[
58
]
, since then
it has drawn much attention in

[
4
,
8
,
18
,
26
,
28
,
42
,
34
,
32
,
35
,
50
,
59
,
60
]
.
The
\sigma_{k}
-curvature equations for modified Schouten tensor
S_{g}^{\tau}
, with proper restriction to
\tau
, was studied in
[
33
,
52
]
.
When the manifold has boundary, the Yamabe problem with certain boundary properties was studied by
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
, and further complemented by
[
15
,
16
,
36
,
37
,
5
,
48
,
49
]
(see
[
10
,
11
,
39
]
for a fully nonlinear version), in which the conformal metrics are not
complete.

However, the case is very different when the metric is complete.
A deep result of Aviles-McOwen
[
2
]
(see Theorem
5.8
), significantly extending a famous theorem of Loewner-Nirenberg
[
47
]
, states that any compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with smooth boundary admits a complete conformal metric with negative constant scalar curvature.
Also, it would be reasonable to study the fully nonlinear version. For
-\mathrm{Ric}_{g}
, Guan
[
21
]
and Gursky-Streets-Warren
[
31
]
studied certain fully nonlinear prescribed curvature problem and proved the existence of complete conformal metrics;
moreover, Gursky-Streets-Warren
obtained asymptotic behavior for complete conformal metric with
\sigma_{k}(-\mathrm{Ric}_{g})=1
, extending that of
[
47
]
from
\sigma_{1}
to
\sigma_{k}
.
For the modified Schouten tensor

S_{g}^{\tau}
with
\tau>n-1
, it was also considered by Li-Sheng
[
43
]
who proved some existence results.

In some problems from differential geometry and PDEs,
it seems interesting to
study prescribed curvature problem for the Einstein tensor

G_{g}=\mathrm{Ric}_{g}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{R}_{g}g,

which corresponds exactly to
critical
case
\tau=n-1
from the viewpoint of PDEs.
As described later, there is a topological obstruction to constructing complete conformal metrics with nonnegative Einstein tensor
on certain 3-manifolds, which indicates
that, in this
critical
case, the prescribed curvature problem is much more complicated.

The purpose of this paper is to settle the
critical
case left open: finding on
M

a complete metric
{g}_{\infty}
, that is conformal to
g
, with prescribed curvature

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(\lambda_{{g}_{\infty}}(G_{{g}_{\infty}}))=\psi\mbox{ in }M,

where
\lambda_{{g}_{\infty}}(G_{{g}_{\infty}})
denote eigenvalues of
G_{{g}_{\infty}}
with respect to
{g}_{\infty}
.
As in
[
6
]
,
f
is a smooth symmetric function of
n
real variables defined in an open symmetric convex cone

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

with vertex at the origin,
\partial\Gamma\neq\emptyset
,
\Gamma_{n}\subseteq\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{1},

where

\Gamma_{k}

is the
k
-th Gårding’s cone defined by

\Gamma_{k}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0,\mbox{ }\forall 1\leq j\leq k\right\},

where
\sigma_{k}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}
.
In addition,
f
satisfies

(1.2)

f\mbox{ is concave in }\Gamma,

(1.3)

\displaystyle f(t\lambda)=tf(\lambda)\mbox{ for any }\lambda\in\Gamma,\mbox{ }t>0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle f(\vec{\bf 1})=1,\mbox{ }f|_{\Gamma}>0,\mbox{ and }f|_{\partial\Gamma}\equiv 0,

where
\vec{\bf 1}:=(1,\cdots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Conditions (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) yield

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\geq nf(\lambda),\,

\displaystyle\forall\lambda\in\Gamma.

We assume that there is a metric
\underline{g}={e^{2\underline{u}}g}
with
\underline{u}\in C^{2}(\bar{M})
such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\underline{g}}(G_{\underline{g}})\in\Gamma\mbox{ in }\bar{M}.

We call such a metric an
admissible conformal
metric for simplicity.

We prove the following existence results.

In particular,

.

In the following theorem we obtain the asymptotic behavior and uniqueness of complete conformal metrics satisfying (

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

,

Remark 1.4
.

It is amazing that in Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
we do not impose the following condition corresponding the ellipticity of the equation

(1.7)

\displaystyle f_{i}(\lambda):=\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{i}}(\lambda)>0\mbox{ in }\Gamma,\ 1\leq i\leq n,

to be compared with fundamental work of Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
6

in which (
1.7
) was imposed as a crucial condition in the theory of fully nonlinear elliptic equations. Surprisingly, we can prove that a weaker condition than (
1.7
)
is satisfied by

obeying (
1.2
) and

(1.8)

\displaystyle\mbox{For each $\sigma<\sup_{\Gamma}f$ and }\lambda\in\Gamma,\mbox{ }\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}f(t\lambda)>\sigma.

Proposition 1.5
.

If

satisfies (
1.2
) and (
1.8
), then

(1.9)

\displaystyle f_{i}(\lambda)\geq 0,\mbox{ }\forall 1\leq i\leq n,\mbox{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)>0\mbox{ in }\Gamma.

Remark 1.6
.

It is a remarkable fact that the condition
\Gamma\neq\Gamma_{n}
imposed in
Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
is crucial and cannot be further dropped.

Here is a topological obstruction.
On a
3
-manifold
(M,g)
, the Einstein tensor connects closely
with the sectional curvature:
Fix
x\in M
,
let
\Sigma\subset T_{x}M
be a tangent
2
-plane,
K_{g}(\Sigma)
be the sectional curvature of
(M,g)
with respect to

\Sigma
, then (see
[
30
]
)

\displaystyle G_{g}(\vec{\bf n},\vec{\bf n})=-K_{g}(\Sigma),

where
\vec{\bf n}\in T_{x}M
is a unit normal vector to
\Sigma
.
Therefore, if Theorem
1.1
holds for
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
(so do both Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
), then for any conformal class
[g]

on
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times(0,1)
(not necessary the standard one, to be compared with that in
[
30
]
),
there would exist in
[g]
a complete metric with negative sectional curvature according to Theorem

1.2
,
which contradicts to the Cartan-Hadamard theorem.

This topological obstruction also motivated Gursky-Streets-Warren to prove that any 3-manifold with boundary admits a complete conformal metric satisfying a pinching condition.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
it suffices to solve the Dirichlet problem

(1.10)

\displaystyle f(\lambda(\Delta ug-\nabla^{2}u+\frac{1}{n-2}G_{g}+\frac{n-3}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}g+du\otimes du))=\frac{\psi e^{2u}}{n-2},

(1.11)

\displaystyle u|_{\partial M}=+\infty,

according to the formula under the conformal transform
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g

(see e.g.
[
3
]
),

(1.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-2}G_{\tilde{g}}=\frac{1}{n-2}G_{g}+\Delta ug-\nabla^{2}u+\frac{n-3}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}g+du\otimes du,\mbox{ if }n\geq 3,

where and hereafter
\nabla u
and
\nabla^{2}u
are gradient and Hessian of

with respect to
g
,

|\nabla u|^{2}=g(\nabla u,\nabla u)
,

is the Laplacian operator with
\Delta u=\mathrm{tr}_{g}(\nabla^{2}u)
,
and
\lambda(A)

(=\lambda_{g}(A))
denote the eigenvalues of
A
with respect to
g
.

The key issue is to derive interior estimates for equation (
1.10
).
However,
it is rather hard to achieve.
The topological obstruction
as described in Remark
1.6

indicates that when
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
, at least for

, the estimates for equation (
1.10
) have to fail, in which the equation is only of
(n-1)
-uniform ellipticity, as denoted later, according to
Proposition

proved below, to be compared with those in

[

,
31
,
30
,
43
]
where the corresponding curvature equations are clearly of fully uniform ellipticity,
thereby proving existence results.
It follows that
the difficulty and
obstruction arise primarily
from the lack of fully uniform ellipticity of the curvature equation. It is noteworthy that this is different from that of the
k
-Yamabe problem for Schouten tensor studied previously in
[
7
]
for

,

and in
[

]
for general

(see also
[

,

]
for other related work).

We start our strategy with partial uniform ellipticity.
Throughout this article, for simplicity, we call the function

f
is of
k
-uniform ellipticity
, if
f
satisfies (
1.9
) and
there exists a uniform positive constant
\theta
, such that for any
\lambda\in\Gamma
with the order
\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}
,

(1.13)

\displaystyle f_{i}(\lambda)\geq\theta\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\lambda),\mbox{ }\forall 1\leq i\leq k.

In particular,
n
-uniform ellipticity
is also called
fully uniform ellipticity
.
Accordingly, we have a similar notion of partial uniform ellipticity for a second order elliptic equation,
if its linearized operator satisfies a similar condition.

It was shown in
[
46
]
that
\sigma_{k}
in
\Gamma_{k}
is of
(n+1-k)
-uniform ellipticity, while their proof relies essentially on the specific properties of
\sigma_{k}
which can not adapt to more general functions.
The first object of our strategy is to settle the general context left open by Lin-Trudinger.
We observe that
a characterization (see Lemma
2.1
), extending that initially proposed in
[
62
]
,
for
f
satisfying (
1.2
) and (
1.8
)
leads naturally to bridging partial uniform ellipticity of
f
and
the count of negative component of a vector contained in
\Gamma
.

Definition 1.7
.

For the cone
\Gamma
as stated above, we define

\displaystyle\kappa_{\Gamma}=\max\left\{l:(-\alpha_{1},\cdots,-\alpha_{l},\alpha_{l+1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\Gamma,\mbox{ where }\alpha_{j}>0,\mbox{ }\forall j=1,\cdots,n\right\}.

Clearly,
\kappa_{\Gamma}

is well defined; in addition, one can see that

(1)

\kappa_{\Gamma}
is an integer with
0\leq\kappa_{\Gamma}\leq n-1
.

(2)

\kappa_{\Gamma}=n-k\mbox{ if }\Gamma=\Gamma_{k}
.

(3)

\kappa_{\Gamma}\geq 1
if and only if
\Gamma\neq\Gamma_{n}
.

(4)

\kappa_{\Gamma}=n-1\mbox{ if and only if $\Gamma$ is of type 2 in the sense of \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{CNS3}{}{}]}}.

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose
f
satisfies (

) and (
1.8

Then (

) holds and there is a universal positive constant

This theorem states that

-uniform ellipticity

Corollary 1.9
.

The

-uniform ellipticity asserted in Theorem

\displaystyle f_{i}(\lambda)\geq\vartheta_{\Gamma}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}(\lambda)\mbox{ if }\lambda_{i}\leq 0.

to study certain geometric PDEs from classical differential geometry and conformal geometry.

2.
The partial uniform ellipticity

Throughout this paper, we denote

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\sigma}:=

\displaystyle\{\lambda\in\Gamma:f(\lambda)>\sigma\},\mbox{ }\partial\Gamma^{\sigma}:=\{\lambda\in\Gamma:f(\lambda)=\sigma\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\infty}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:(\lambda^{\prime},c,\cdots,c)\in\Gamma\mbox{ for some }c>0\right\}.

Our approach is based on the results we proved in this section and Section

This section is devoted to the investigation of partial uniform ellipticity.
The following lemma is the starting point. The first two statements of this lemma was initially proposed by the author in

for

Lemma 2.1
.

For
f
satisfying (
1.2
), the following are equivalent each other.

(1)

f
further satisfies (
1.8
).

(2)

For each
\lambda
,
\mu\in\Gamma,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\mu_{i}>0.

(3)

f(\lambda+\mu)>f(\lambda)
for any
\lambda
,
\mu\in\Gamma
.

(4)

f(\lambda+c\vec{\bf 1})>f(c\vec{\bf 1})
,
\forall\lambda\in\Gamma
,
\forall c>0
.

Proof.

In the proof, we use the formula saying that for any
\lambda
,
\mu\in\Gamma
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle f(\lambda)\geq f(\mu)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)(\lambda_{i}-\mu_{i}),

which follows from the concavity of
f
.

\mathrm{(1)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(2)}
:
Fix
\lambda\in\Gamma
. The condition (
1.8
) implies that for any

\mu\in\Gamma
, there is
T\geq 1
(may depend on
\mu
) such that for each

,

f(t\mu)>f(\lambda)
. Together with (
2.1
),
one gets
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)(t\mu_{i}-\lambda_{i})>0
. Thus,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\lambda_{i}>0
(if one takes
\mu=\lambda
)
then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\mu_{i}>0
.

\mathrm{(2)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(3)}
:
The proof uses (
2.1
).

\mathrm{(4)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(1)}
:
Given
\sup_{\partial\Gamma}f<\sigma<\sup_{\Gamma}f
. Let

be the positive constant with

(2.2)

\displaystyle f(c_{\sigma}\vec{\bf 1})=\sigma.

For any
\lambda\in\Gamma
,

t\lambda-c_{\sigma}\vec{\bf 1}\in\Gamma
for
t>\frac{\sqrt{n}c_{\sigma}}{\mbox{dist}(\lambda,\partial\Gamma)}
.
Thus
f(t\lambda)>\sigma
for such

.
∎

As an interesting result, we obtain Proposition

Proof of Proposition

.

Fix

According to Lemma

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)\mu_{i}>0

By setting

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(\lambda)>0

Note that

f_{i}(\lambda)\geq 0\mbox{ for each}i.

∎

Suppose that

It follows from (

Therefore, Theorem

Assume

For the

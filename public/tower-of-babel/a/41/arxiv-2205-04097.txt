Thermal suppression of bubble nucleation at first-order phase transitions in the early Universe Preprint: HIP-2022-3/TH
Mudhahir Al Ajmi, Mark Hindmarsh

Abstract
One of the key observables in a gravitational wave power spectrum from a first order phase transition in the early Universe is the mean bubble spacing, which depends on the rate of nucleation of bubbles of the stable phase, as well as the bubble wall speed. When the bubbles expand as deflagrations, it is expected that the heating of the fluid in front of the phase boundary suppresses the nucleation rate. We quantify the effect, showing that it increases the mean bubble separation, and acts to enhance the gravitational wave signal by a factor of up to order 10. The effect is largest for small wall speeds and strong transitions.

I
Introduction

An early Universe cosmological first-order phase transition
Steinhardt 1982

can lead to interesting physical consequences such as matter-antimatter asymmetry
Kuzmin et al. 1985
,
primordial magnetic fields
Hogan 1983
;
Baym et al. 1996

and the production of a stochastic background of
gravitational waves
Witten 1984
;
Hogan 1986

The power spectrum of the gravitational waves contains information about the thermodynamic and transport properties of the
system at the time of the phase transition. If the transition happened at around the electroweak scale of 100 GeV, when the Universe was about
10^{-11}
seconds old,
the gravitational waves could be observable at planned space-based detectors like Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA)
Amaro-Seoane et al. 2017
,

Caprini et al. 2020
,
and the principal thermodynamic and transport properties could be measured over a wide region of the parameter space
Gowling and Hindmarsh 2021
.

The transformation of the metastable phase into the stable one is described by cosmological homogeneous nucleation theory

Steinhardt 1982
;
Linde 1983
(see
Hindmarsh et al. 2021
for a review).
Once the temperature has fallen below the critical temperature of the transition
T_{\text{c}}
,
quantum or thermal fluctuations produce small spherical bubbles of the stable phase,
which expand and merge, and eventually the whole Universe is converted to its stable phase.
The peak of the bubble nucleation rate defines the nucleation temperature
T_{\text{n}}
, and the time taken
to complete the transition can be expressed as a transition rate
\beta
.
Part of the potential energy of the supercooled metastable phase is converted into bulk fluid motion, which
sources gravitational waves long after the transition is completed
Hindmarsh et al. 2014
.
The kinetic energy of the bulk fluid motion is controlled by a fourth parameter
\alpha
, which is essentially the ratio of
the potential energy difference to the thermal energy. It quantifies the strength of the transition.
The kinetic energy of the bulk motion is also controlled by the equation of state of the fluid, most importantly
through the speeds of sound in the two phases
Giese et al. 2021
.
All quantities will be defined more precisely below.

At the end of the transition, the density of bubble nucleation sites
n_{*}
defines a mean bubble spacing
R_{*}=1/n_{*}^{1/3}
.
If bubble nucleation continues undisturbed in the metastable phase, and the bubbles expand at a constant speed
v_{\text{w}}
,
the mean bubble spacing is given by

R_{*}=(8\pi)^{1/3}v_{\text{w}}/\beta.

(1)

However, if the bubbles expand as deflagrations
Landau and Lifshitz 1987
;
Steinhardt 1982
;
Kurki-Suonio and Laine 1996
;
Espinosa et al. 2010
;
Hindmarsh and Hijazi 2019
, the fluid ahead of the advancing bubble wall is heated, out to the radius of a shock.
In this region the nucleation rate is reduced. Thus we expect fewer bubbles to be nucleated, and the mean bubble spacing should increase.
The effect acts to increase the peak wavelength of the gravitational wave power spectrum
Hindmarsh 2018
;
Hindmarsh et al. 2017
.
and also the peak power, which scales as
R_{*}
or
R_{*}^{2}
, depending on the strength of the transition

Caprini et al. 2020
.

The suppression of nucleation by the heating effect has been noted before
Moore and Rummukainen 2001
, where it was estimated that nucleation was suppressed everywhere between the wall and the shock. In this paper we quantify the effect more precisely, for transitions with strength parameters up to
\alpha\simeq 1
.
We find that the increase in the mean bubble spacing can increase the gravitational wave power
by a factor of up to O(10), partially
compensating the suppression due to the interactions between bubbles
Cutting et al. 2020
.

II
Bubble nucleation: standard calculation

First, we discuss the formation of bubbles in the plasma according to the standard treatment.
We will suppose that the transition rate is much faster than the Hubble rate
H
, so that
we can neglect cosmic expansion.
The bubble nucleation rate per unit volume has the form
Guth and Weinberg 1981
;
Linde 1983
;
Enqvist et al. 1992
;
Hindmarsh and Hijazi 2019

p(t)=p_{0}e^{-S(T(t))}.

(2)

where
S
is the action for the appearance of a bubble, which in a thermal transition is equal to the energy of a
critical bubble divided by the temperature. It is a function of time through its dependence on the temperature
T(t)
.
In the small supercooling or “thin wall” approximation
Linde 1983
;
Enqvist et al. 1992
,

S\simeq\frac{s_{0}}{|(1-\hat{T})]^{2}},

(3)

where
\hat{T}=T/T_{\text{c}}
,
T_{\text{c}}
is the critical temperature, and
s_{0}
is a constant computable from the
effective potential of the theory.
The nucleation rate is then zero precisely at the critical temperature, and increases very rapidly below it.

Once a bubble has nucleated, it grows at a constant speed
v_{\text{w}}
, determined by the friction between the wall and the plasma.
The increasing population of growing bubbles reduces the fraction of the Universe in the metastable phase.

Let
V
be the volume in the metastable phase, and
V_{\text{s}}
the volume in the stable phase, out of a total volume
V_{\text{tot}}
, such that

V_{\text{tot}}=V+V_{\text{s}}.

In the notation of Ref.
Enqvist et al. 1992
,

h=V/V_{\text{tot}}

(4)

denotes the fraction of the Universe in the metastable (high-temperature) phase.
First, we consider the reduction in the volume of the unbroken phase between times
t
and
t+dt
due to the growth of bubbles nucleated between earlier times
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime}+dt^{\prime}
:

d^{2}V(t,t^{\prime})=-dN_{\text{b}}(t^{\prime})4\pi{R}^{2}dR\frac{V(t)}{V(t^{\prime})},

(5)

where
dN_{\text{b}}
is the number of bubbles nucleated in that time interval, and
R
is the radius of those bubbles at time
t
. The factor
V(t)/V(t^{\prime})
takes into account the fact that only parts of the bubbles growing into the unbroken phase will change the volume of that phase.

The number of bubbles nucleated between
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime}+dt^{\prime}
is

dN_{\text{b}}=p(t^{\prime})V(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime},

(6)

where
p(t^{\prime})
is the bubble nucleation rate per unit volume, and
the factor
V(t^{\prime})
accounts for the fact that bubbles nucleate only
in the metastable phase.
Finally, we have

R=v_{\text{w}}(t-t^{\prime}),\;\;dR=v_{\text{w}}dt,

(7)

as the bubbles are assumed to grow with constant speed after nucleation.

The nucleation probability is non-zero only below the critical temperature
T_{\text{c}}
, which is reached at time
t_{\text{c}}
, so the change in the volume of the stable phase between
t
and
t+dt
is, in total,

dV(t)=-v_{\text{w}}V(t)dt\int_{t_{\text{c}}}^{t}dt^{\prime}p(t^{\prime})4\pi v_{\text{w}}^{2}(t-t^{\prime})^{2}.

(8)

Dividing by the total volume
V_{\text{tot}}
, we obtain a differential equation for
h
, the fraction remaining in the metastable phase:

\frac{dh}{dt}=-v_{\text{w}}h(t)\int_{t_{\text{c}}}^{t}dt^{\prime}p(t^{\prime})4\pi v_{\text{w}}^{2}(t-t^{\prime})^{2}.

(9)

It is straightforward to check that the solution to this equation, with the boundary condition
h(t)=1
for
t<t_{\text{c}}
, is

h(t)=\exp\left(-\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_{t_{\text{c}}}^{t}dt^{\prime}p(t^{\prime})v_{\text{w}}^{3}(t-t^{\prime})^{3}\right).

(10)

A saddle-point approximation to the integral is possible.
We define the transition rate parameter

\beta=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\ln p(t)\right|_{t_{f}},

(11)

where
t_{f}
will be specified later,
and write

p(t)\simeq p_{f}e^{\beta(t-t_{f})}

(12)

where
p_{f}=p_{0}\exp(-S(t_{f}))
.
The integral can be performed in the approximation
\beta(t_{\text{c}}-t_{f})\to-\infty
, giving

h(t)=\exp\left(-{8\pi}v_{\text{w}}^{3}\beta^{-4}p_{f}e^{\beta t}\right).

(13)

Choosing
t_{f}
to be the time at which
h(t_{f})=1/e
,
we have

h(t)=\exp\left(-e^{\beta(t-t_{f})}\right).

(14)

with

{8\pi}v_{\text{w}}^{3}\beta^{-4}p_{f}=1.

(15)

The time
t_{f}
is then found as the solution to

{8\pi}v_{\text{w}}^{3}\beta^{-4}p_{0}e^{-S(t_{f})}=1.

(16)

Its value does not play an essential role in the following.

To calculate the number of bubbles, we integrate the equation

\frac{dN_{\text{b}}}{dt^{\prime}}=p(t^{\prime})V(t^{\prime}),

(17)

or, in terms of the bubble density
n_{\text{b}}=N_{\text{b}}/V_{\text{tot}}
,

\frac{dn_{\text{b}}}{dt^{\prime}}=p(t^{\prime})h(t^{\prime}).

(18)

The final bubble density is then

n_{\text{b}}=\beta^{3}/8\pi v_{\text{w}}^{3}.

(19)

We define a mean bubble centre spacing
R_{*}
as

R_{*}=n_{\text{b}}^{-1/3}=(8\pi)^{1/3}(v_{\text{w}}/\beta).

(20)

Note that we have assumed that the bubble nucleation rate
is the same everywhere in the metastable phase. However,
the expanding bubble releases energy and heats up the fluid, as well as setting it in motion.
If this heating effect extends in front of the bubble wall, as it does for
deflagrations, we can see from (
3
) that the nucleation rate will be reduced.

III
Hydrodynamics of bubble growth by deflagrations

In order to calculate the suppression effect noted in the previous section, we need to calculate the temperature profile around an expanding bubble for deflagrations.

In the case of perfect fluid the plasma is locally in equilibrium. Therefore, the energy momentum tensor of the fluid can be written as:

T^{\text{f}}_{\mu\nu}=w\,u_{\mu}u_{\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}\,p,

(21)

where
w
is the enthalpy density and
p
is the pressure.
The four-velocity field of the plasma
u^{\mu}
is related to its
three-velocity
\mathbf{v}
by

u^{\mu}=\frac{(1,\mathbf{v})}{\sqrt{1-\mathbf{v}^{2}}}=(\gamma,\gamma\mathbf{v})\ .

(22)

The energy density,
e
, the enthalpy density
w
, the entropy density
s
are related to the pressure as follows:

e\equiv T\frac{\partial p}{\partial T}-p\ ,\quad w\equiv T\frac{\partial p}{\partial T}\ ,\quad s\equiv\frac{\partial p}{\partial T}\ .

(23)

Evidently,

w=e+p\ .

The motion of the fluid is governed by conservation of energy-momentum:

\partial^{\mu}T^{\text{f}}_{\mu\nu}=0\ .

(24)

The fluid around an expanding spherical bubble at time since nucleation
\Delta t
and radial distance
r
can be described by the purely radial fluid 3-velocity
v(r,\Delta t)
and enthalpy
w(r,\Delta t)
.
The partial differential equations can be solved numerically
Kurki-Suonio and Laine 1996
, and it is observed that
the fluid quickly settles down to a self-similar solution, depending only on a coordinate

\xi=r/\Delta t
. In this self-similar form,
the fluid equations become

\displaystyle\frac{dv}{d\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2v(1-v^{2})}{\xi(1-\xi v)}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{c_{\rm s}^{2}}-1\right)^{-1}\,,

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{dw}{d\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w\left(1+\frac{1}{c_{\rm s}^{2}}\right)\gamma^{2}\mu\frac{dv}{d\xi}\,.

(26)

Here,
c_{\rm s}^{2}=dp/de
is the speed of sound and

\mu=\frac{\xi-v}{1-\xi v}.

(27)

is the fluid velocity at
\xi
in a frame that is moving outward at speed
\xi
.

We now discuss the boundary conditions at the bubble wall.
The plasma is in the stable phase behind the wall, and in the metastable phase in front. We denote quantities evaluated just behind the wall with a subscript
-
, and in front with a subscript
+
, and quantities in the frame moving with the wall with a tilde.

In the frame moving with the wall, conservation of energy density and momentum density imply

\displaystyle w_{+}\tilde{\gamma}_{+}^{2}+p_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{-}\tilde{\gamma}_{-}^{2}+p_{-},

(28)

\displaystyle w_{+}\tilde{\gamma}_{+}^{2}\tilde{v}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{-}\tilde{\gamma}_{-}^{2}\tilde{v}_{-},

(29)

These equations may be rearranged to give

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\alpha_{+}}\left[\left(\frac{\tilde{v}_{-}}{2}+\frac{1}{6\tilde{v}_{-}}\right)\right.

(30)

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle\left.\sqrt{\left(\frac{\tilde{v}_{-}}{2}+\frac{1}{6\tilde{v}_{-}}\right)^{2}+\alpha_{+}^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\alpha_{+}-\frac{1}{3}}\right],

where

\alpha_{+}=\frac{4}{3}\frac{\theta_{+}-\theta_{-}}{w_{+}}\ ,

(31)

and
\theta=(e-3p)/4
.
In a deflagration, the fluid exits from the wall with the smaller of the wall speed and the sound speed in the stable phase,

\tilde{v}_{-}=\min(v_{\text{w}},c_{\text{s}}(T_{-}))

(32)

These equations can be straightforwardly solved numerically, once the speed of sound is known as a function of enthalpy density. In practice one wishes to apply the boundary condition at large radii, where
v=0
and
T=T_{\text{n}}
, the bubble nucleation temperature.
This means that one must “shoot” for this value from an initial guess for
\alpha_{+}
. The amount of energy available to be released by the transition is conveniently parametrised by

\alpha_{\text{n}}=\frac{4}{3}\frac{\theta_{\text{m}}(T_{\text{n}})-\theta_{\text{s}}(T_{\text{n}})}{w_{\text{m}}(t_{\text{n}})},

(33)

where the subscripts m and s denote quantities evaluated in the metastable and stable phases.

In this paper we use the simplest possible equation of state, the bag model, in which

{}p_{\text{m}}=\epsilon+a_{\text{m}}T^{4},\quad p_{\text{s}}=a_{\text{s}}T^{4}.

(34)

In this model
c_{\text{s}}^{2}=1/3
and
w\propto T^{4}
.

Small wall speed limit

In the limit of small wall speed, the fluid velocity should be small
(v(\xi)\ll\xi)
, and a simple approximation to the solution to the fluid equations is known.
The approximation is

v_{\text{ip}}(\xi)=v_{\text{max}}\frac{v_{\text{w}}^{2}}{\xi^{2}}\frac{c_{\text{s}}^{2}-\xi^{2}}{c_{\text{s}}^{2}-v_{\text{w}}^{2}},

(36)

where

v_{\text{max}}(v_{\text{w}},\alpha)
is
the maximum fluid speed in the bubble centre frame, reached just outside the wall.
For small
\alpha
and
v_{\text{w}}
not too close to the sound speed
Espinosa et al. 2010
;
Hindmarsh et al. 2021
,

v_{\text{max}}\simeq 3\alpha_{\text{n}}v_{\text{w}}\frac{1}{1-3v_{\text{w}}^{2}}.

(37)

This gives an analytic form for the enthalpy,

w(\xi)\simeq w_{\text{n}}\exp\left[2(1+c_{\text{s}}^{2})\frac{v_{\text{w}}^{2}}{c_{\text{s}}^{2}-v_{\text{w}}^{2}}v_{\text{max}}\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-\frac{1}{c_{\text{s}}}\right)\right]

(38)

where
w_{\text{n}}
is the enthalpy density at
\xi\geq c_{\text{s}}
, the enthalpy density at the nucleation temperature.

Because
w\propto T^{4}
, we have for small enthalpy differences
\Delta w/w=4\Delta T/T
.
Hence

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta T}{T_{\text{n}}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1+c_{\text{s}}^{2})\frac{v_{\text{w}}^{2}}{c_{\text{s}}^{2}-v_{\text{w}}^{2}}v_{\text{max}}\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-\frac{1}{c_{\text{s}}}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{3\alpha_{\text{n}}}{2}(1+c_{\text{s}}^{2})\frac{v_{\text{w}}^{3}}{c_{\text{s}}^{2}(1-3v_{\text{w}}^{2})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-\frac{1}{c_{\text{s}}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle{6\alpha_{\text{n}}}\frac{v_{\text{w}}^{3}}{(1-3v_{\text{w}}^{2})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-\sqrt{3}\right)

This relation is useful in the low velocity region when
v\lesssim 0.05
.

IV
Position-dependent bubble nucleation

In this section, we take into account the dependence of the bubble nucleation rate on the temperature. The temperature is raised closer to the critical temperature in front of a deflagration, and so we expect the nucleation rate to be suppressed around the expanding bubble.

The bubble nucleation rate per unit volume is now space-dependent as well as time dependent, due to the dependence of the bubble appearance action on the temperature:

p(t^{\prime},\textbf{x})=p_{0}e^{-S(t^{\prime},\textbf{x})}.

(40)

Let us write

T(t^{\prime},\textbf{x})=\bar{T}(t^{\prime})+\Delta T(t^{\prime},\textbf{x}),

(41)

where
\bar{T}
is the undisturbed temperature outside the shock surrounding each bubble. Hence

p(t^{\prime},\textbf{x})=p(t^{\prime})e^{-\Delta S(t^{\prime},\textbf{x})}

(42)

The rate of bubble nucleation over the remaining volume remaining in the metastable phase
V^{\prime}\equiv V(t^{\prime})
is

\frac{dN_{\text{b}}}{dt^{\prime}}=\int_{V^{\prime}}d^{3}x\,p(t^{\prime},\textbf{x}),

(43)

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{dN_{\text{b}}}{dt^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=p(t^{\prime})\left(V^{\prime}-\int_{V^{\prime}}d^{3}x(1-e^{-\Delta S})\right),

(44)

where we have rearranged the equation to bring out a term which acts to reduce the effective volume of the metastable phase.
Let us give the correction term the symbol

\Delta V_{\text{s,eff}}=\int_{V^{\prime}}d^{3}x(1-e^{-\Delta S}).

(45)

We can regard this quantity as an increase in the effective volume of the stable phase, where no bubbles can nucleate.

Let us first consider a single bubble nucleated at time
t_{1}
, with radius
R=v_{\text{w}}(t^{\prime}-t_{1})
. We can then write

\Delta V_{\text{s,eff}}=V_{\text{s}}f,

(46)

where

f=\frac{3}{v_{\text{w}}^{3}}\int_{v_{\text{w}}}^{v_{\text{sh}}}\xi^{2}d\xi(1-e^{-\Delta S})

(47)

is a constant factor giving the relative increase in the effective volume of the bubble,

v_{\text{sh}}
is the speed of the shock which surrounds the bubble, and the volume in the stable phase inside the bubble is
V_{\text{s}}=4\pi v_{\text{w}}^{3}(t^{\prime}-t_{1})^{3}/3
.
The effective volume in the stable phase is then

V_{\text{s,eff}}=(1+f)V_{\text{s}}.

(48)

The change in the bubble appearance action
\Delta S
can be expressed in terms of the transition rate parameter
\beta=-\partial S/\partial t
,

\Delta S\simeq\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}\Delta T=\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}\frac{\partial t}{\partial T}\Delta T=\frac{\beta}{H}\frac{\Delta T}{T}.

(49)

We neglect higher orders in
{\Delta T}/{T}
, which means that in the region of
(v_{\text{w}},\alpha_{\text{n}})
plane where
\Delta T/T\sim 1
,
our calculations can be expected to receive large corrections. These corrections will depend on the second and higher derivatives
of the action with respect to the logarithm of the temperature, parameters which are not part of the standard description of phase transitions. We leave more precise studies for future work, indicating where corrections are likely to be more important by the density of the blue shading in the
(v_{\text{w}},\alpha_{\text{n}})
plane.

With this approximation in mind, we may write

f=\frac{3}{v_{\text{w}}^{3}}\int_{v_{\text{w}}}^{v_{\text{sh}}}\xi^{2}(1-e^{-\tilde{\beta}\Delta T(\xi)/T_{\text{n}}})d\xi

(50)

where
\tilde{\beta}=\beta/H
,
T_{\text{n}}
is the nucleation temperature, and
\Delta T(\xi)=T(\xi)-T_{\text{n}}
.
We can rewrite the factor in terms of an effective wall speed
v_{\text{eff}}
,

1+f=\frac{v_{\text{eff}}^{3}}{v_{\text{w}}^{3}}.

(51)

We plot the ratio
v_{\text{eff}}/v_{\text{sh}}
on the top row of Fig.
3
. We see that for fast (
v_{\text{w}}\gtrsim c_{\text{s}}
) bubble walls in strong (
\alpha_{\text{n}}\gtrsim 0.3
) and rapid (
\tilde{\beta}\gtrsim 100
) transitions, the estimate
v_{\text{eff}}\simeq v_{\text{sh}}
is reasonably accurate. However, for slower walls in weak and slow transitions, the approximation fails.

Now let us consider the situation with many bubbles.
We label the bubbles by
i=1,\ldots,N_{\text{b}}
, and define a radial coordinate for each bubble
r_{i}
.
Before bubbles start overlapping, the effective volume in regions where bubble nucleation has stopped is

\displaystyle V_{\text{s,eff}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{b}}}\frac{4\pi}{3}v_{\text{w}}^{3}(t^{\prime}-t_{i})^{3}(1+f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{b}}}\frac{4\pi}{3}v_{\text{eff}}^{3}(t^{\prime}-t_{i})^{3}.

(52)

Conversely, the effective volume remaining where bubble nucleation can take place is

V_{\text{eff}}=V_{\text{tot}}-V_{\text{s,eff}}.

(53)

The rate of change of the fraction in the metastable phase is then modified to

\frac{dh}{dt}=-v_{\text{w}}h\frac{V_{\text{eff}}}{V}\int_{t_{\text{c}}}^{t}dt^{\prime}p(t^{\prime})4\pi v_{\text{w}}^{2}(t-t^{\prime})^{2}.

(54)

We take the ratio
V_{\text{eff}}/V
to be that from a single bubble,

\frac{V_{\text{eff}}}{V}=\frac{V_{\text{tot}}-V_{\text{s,eff}}}{V}\simeq\frac{(1+f)h-f}{h}.

(55)

This approximation neglects regions outside
the shells of radius
v_{\text{eff}}(t^{\prime}-t_{i})

but inside
v_{\text{sh}}(t^{\prime}-t_{i})
,
where the temperature in overlapping fluid shells has also reached a high enough value to suppress nucleation.
This extra volume where nucleation is suppressed will give a small positive correction to
v_{\text{eff}}
.

We are also neglecting interactions between the shell of one bubble and the wall of a neighbouring one, which tend to slow down the expansion of the wall
Cutting et al. 2020
. We discuss this effect in the conclusions section.

We see that bubbles stop nucleating once
h
drops below

h_{\text{x}}=\frac{f}{1+f}=1-\frac{v_{\text{w}}^{3}}{v_{\text{eff}}^{3}},

(56)

so that
h_{\text{x}}
is the fractional volume at which the symmetric phase is reheated enough to prevent further bubble nucleation.
We plot
h_{\text{x}}
as a function of
v_{\text{w}}
and
\alpha_{\text{n}}
, for three values of
\tilde{\beta}
, in the bottom row of Figure
3
.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper we have studied the suppression of bubble nucleation in cosmological phase transitions proceeding by deflagrations. In a deflagration,
some of the energy released by the transition goes into heating up the fluid in front of the bubble wall which, as a result, suppresses further bubble nucleation. In a detonation, on the other hand, the bubble wall is ahead of the shell of excess thermal energy, and the effect is absent.

We find that nucleation stops when a certain fraction of the volume in the metastable phase has been converted. The fraction can easily be computed from the solution of the relativistic hydrodynamic equations, in an expansion in the relative temperature fluctuation
\Delta T/T_{\text{n}}
. We solve the equations for a fluid with a bag model equation of state, and compute the first order effect. This is sufficient for transition strengths below around
0.3
, and wall speeds below the sound speed.

The suppression of nucleation results in a lower number of bubbles per unit volume, and therefore a larger mean distance between their centres. The effect results in a larger intensity of gravitational waves from the transition.

The region of higher temperature extends outward to a leading shock, which travels faster than the sound speed. For this reason it has sometimes been estimated that the region extends out to the shock speed
v_{\text{sh}}

Moore and Rummukainen 2001
or the sound speed (an estimate of the shock speed)
Caprini et al. 2020
. Here we have shown that the effect is more complicated.
The suppression can be expressed as the effective speed
v_{\text{eff}}
of expanding spherical volumes inside which nucleation stops, with
v_{\text{w}}<v_{\text{eff}}<v_{\text{sh}}
. We show that this approximation
v_{\text{eff}}\simeq v_{\text{sh}}
works well for fast walls in strong and rapid transitions, but not otherwise.

The more rapid the phase transition, as measured by the parameter
\tilde{\beta}
, the more sensitive the system is to the suppression effect. This is because
\tilde{\beta}
is equal to the logarithmic derivative of the nucleation probability with respect to the temperature. Increasing the phase transition strength parameter
\alpha_{\text{n}}
also increases the effect, as one would expect from the larger release of thermal energy. The effect also increases with decreasing wall speed
v_{\text{w}}
, as the heated volume is larger relative to the bubble size.

For example, for (
v_{\text{w}}
,
\alpha_{\text{n}}
,
\tilde{\beta}
) = (0.1,
3\times 10^{-2}
,
1000
),
the ratio
v_{\text{eff}}/v_{\text{sh}}\simeq 0.4
, and bubbles stop nucleating when only 5% of the universe has been converted to the stable phase. This has the effect of increasing the mean bubble spacing by a factor 4, and the gravitational wave intensity by a factor 5. We show the magnitude of both effects, as functions of
v_{\text{w}}
and
\alpha_{\text{n}}
, in contour plots in Fig.
7
.

Our results are derived from a numerical solution to an integral equation for the fraction remaining in the metastable phase as a function of time,
h(t)
. We have also shown that good numerical approximations exist, and that the suppression factors can be calculated from the solution of the relativistic hydrodynamic equations.

A further effect to consider for precise calculations of the gravitational wave power spectrum is the altered collision time distribution
Hindmarsh and Hijazi 2019
. In the standard calculation with exponentially growing nucleation rate per unit volume of metastable phase, the distribution of times between a segment of wall being nucleated, and colliding with another segment of wall, is distributed exponentially. If all bubbles are nucleated simultaneously, the distribution is a power times an exponential. As
h_{\text{x}}
is reduced from 1 to 0, we are effectively interpolating between these two situations, and we therefore expect the shape of the gravitational wave power spectrum to interpolate between the exponential and simultaneous
Hindmarsh and Hijazi 2019
as well.

Finally, in this paper we have assumed that the walls expand with a constant speed throughout the transition. On the other hand, when a bubble wall encounters the heated region surrounding another bubble, the pressure difference across it will be reduced, and the wall will slow down
Cutting et al. 2020
. If the nucleation has effectively stopped by the time the bubble walls start to slow, the number of bubbles nucleated per unit volume, and hence the mean bubble spacing
R_{*}
, will not be affected. The effect of the walls slowing will therefore be smaller for larger
h_{\text{x}}
, and hence larger
\tilde{\beta}
.
We therefore expect the slowing of the walls to be important only for lower values of
\tilde{\beta}
. We will explore the effect in more detail elsewhere.

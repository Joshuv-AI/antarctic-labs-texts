1.
Introduction

For a given Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
q
, the

L
-function
L(s,\chi)
is defined in the half-plane
\sigma>1
via the
equivalent definitions

L(s,\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\chi(n)n^{-s}=\prod_{p\text{~prime}}(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1}.

If
\chi
is nonprincipal, then

L(s,\chi)
extends to an entire function on the complex plane, and
if
\chi
is primitive, then
L(s,\chi)
satisfies
a simple functional equation relating its values at

s
and
1-s
. In this note, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
\theta>\frac{1}{3}
,
there are constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
that depend only on
\theta
such that
the following property holds.
Let
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
be distinct primitive Dirichlet characters modulo
q
.
Then, for any
T\geqslant c_{1}q^{\theta}
, the functions
L(s,\chi_{1})
and
L(s,\chi_{2})

have different zeros
(
counted with multiplicity
\,)
in the region

\mathcal{R}(q,T)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big\{s=\sigma+it\in\mathbb{C}:0<\sigma<1,~T<t<T+c_{2}q^{\theta}\log T\big\}.

In other words, the function

(1.1)

\frac{L(s,\chi_{1})}{L(s,\chi_{2})}+\frac{L(s,\chi_{2})}{L(s,\chi_{1})}

has at least one pole in
\mathcal{R}(q,T)
.
Moreover, if
q
is cubefree, then the same result holds for
any
\theta>\frac{1}{4}
.

Some related results have been found previously.
Fujii
[
6
, Thm.
1^{\prime}
]
showed that if
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are
distinct primitive characters to the same modulus
q
,
then a positive proportion of the nontrivial zeros
of
L(s,\chi_{1})L(s,\chi_{2})
are poles of the function (
1.1
).
Moreover, it follows from
[
6
, §3 Cor.
(i)
]
that

L(s,\chi_{1})
and
L(s,\chi_{2})
have at least
c_{3}\log T

such zeros in the region
\mathcal{R}(q,T)
for a positive proportion of

T\in(T_{1},T_{1}+T_{1}^{1/2+\varepsilon})
if

T_{1}\geqslant c_{4}
and
H>T_{1}^{1/2+\varepsilon}
, where
c_{3},c_{4}>0
are
constants that depend only
q
and
\varepsilon
;
this result also holds generally for distinct Dirichlet

L
-functions
associated to primitive Dirichlet characters whose
moduli may be different (see Fujii
[
6
, §4]
). Thus, Fujii’s method
produces more such zeros than Theorem
1.1
in short rectangles of the form

\{0<\sigma<1,~T_{1}<t<T_{1}+H\}
, but the constants are ineffective
in
q
and
\varepsilon
. By contrast, Theorem
1.1
produces fewer zeros in
\mathcal{R}(q,T)

on average, but the holds for all large
T
.

Using a variant of Hamburger’s theorem,
Raghunathan
[
14
, Thm. 3]
showed that for any pair of
distinct primitive characters
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
, both functions

\frac{L(s,\chi_{1})}{L(s,\chi_{2})}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{L(s,\chi_{2})}{L(s,\chi_{1})}

have infinitely many non-integer poles.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on a generalization of
a striking formula due to Landau
[
10
, Satz 1]
,
which asserts that for any fixed
x>1
one has

(1.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
1<\gamma\leqslant T\end{subarray}}x^{\rho}=-\frac{T}{2\pi}\Lambda(x)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(x)+O(\log T),

where the sum runs over the multiset of nontrivial zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma

of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
with
1<\gamma\leqslant T
,

\Lambda
is von Mangoldt function, and
\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the indicator
function of the integers:

\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}1&\quad\hbox{if $x\in\mathbb{Z}$},\\
0&\quad\hbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Gonek
[
9
, Thm. 1]
gave a uniform version of Landau’s
result (
1.2
) with an error term expressed in terms of both
variables
x
and
T
. Some stronger
versions of the Gonek-Landau theorem are known; see

[
4
,
7
,
8
]
.
The following theorem is an extension of
[
9
, Thm. 1]
,
which is proved using Gonek’s methods, covering the case of an
L
-function
attached to a primitive Dirichlet character.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q
.
Uniformly for
x\geqslant 2
and
T_{2}>T_{1}>1
, we have

(1.3)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
T_{1}<\gamma<T_{2}\end{subarray}}x^{\rho}=-\frac{T_{2}-T_{1}}{2\pi}\Lambda(x)\chi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(x)+O(E),

where the sum runs over the nontrivial zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma

of
L(s,\chi)
for which
T_{1}<\gamma<T_{2}

(
each zero being summed
according to its multiplicity
)
,

E\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=x\log x\,\log\log 2x+x\log x\,\min\Big\{\frac{T_{2}}{x},\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle}\Big\}+x\,\log\log 2x\,\log 2qT_{2},

and
\langle x\rangle
is the distance from
x
to the nearest
prime power other than
x
itself.
The implied constant in (
1.3
) is absolute.

We remark that similar results have been achieved covering
all functions in the Selberg class (including the Dirichlet
L
-functions
considered here); see, for example,
[
15
,
5
,
12
,
13
]
.
For our purposes, the error term provided
by Theorem
1.2
is convenient as the dependence on
the quantity
\langle x\rangle
is explicit.

Theorem
1.2
is proved in §
3
and
is used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(see §
4
).

2.
Preliminaries

Lemma 2.1
.

For
x\geqslant 2
,
T>1
, and
c\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1+\tfrac{1}{\log x}
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geqslant 2\\
n\neq x\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\min\Big\{T,\frac{1}{|\log(x/n)|}\Big\}\ll\log x\,\log\log 2x+\log x\,\min\Big\{\frac{T}{x},\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle}\Big\}.

Proof.

See Gonek
[
9
, Lem. 2]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\chi
be a primitive character modulo
q\geqslant 1
. Then the estimate

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma-t|<1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{s-\rho}+O\big(\log 2q|t|\big)

holds uniformly for
-1\leqslant\sigma\leqslant 2
and
|t|\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

See Montgomery and Vaughan
[
11
, Lems. 12.1 and 12.6]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
\theta>\frac{1}{3}
,
there are constants
c_{3},c_{4}>0
that depend only on
\theta
such that
the following property holds. If
\chi
is a nonprincipal character modulo
q\geqslant 3
,
then there exists a prime
p_{0}
such that

(2.1)

p_{0}\leqslant c_{3}q^{\theta}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\big|\chi(p_{0})-1\big|\geqslant\frac{c_{4}}{(\log q)^{2}}.

If
q
is cubefree, then the same result holds for
any
\theta>\frac{1}{4}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this when
q
is sufficiently large (depending on
\theta
).

The well known results of Burgess
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

state that the bound

(2.2)

\sum_{N<n\leqslant N+H}\chi(n)\mathop{\,\ll\,}\limits_{\varepsilon,r}H^{1-1/r}q^{(r+1)/(4r^{2})+\varepsilon}

holds for
N\in\mathbb{R}
,
H\geqslant 1
,
\varepsilon>0
, and any
integer
r\geqslant 2
, provided that
q
is cubefree or that
r\leqslant 3
;
the implied constant depends only on
\varepsilon
and
r
.
Applying (
2.2
)
with
N\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=0
,
H\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c_{3}q^{\theta}
,
\varepsilon\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{4}(\theta-\frac{1}{3})
,
and
r\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=3
, we have

(2.3)

\sum_{n\leqslant c_{3}q^{\theta}}\chi(n)\mathop{\,\ll\,}\limits_{\theta}q^{\theta-\frac{1}{12}(\theta-\frac{1}{3})},

where the implied constant depends only on
\theta
.

On the other hand, assume there is no prime
p_{0}
satisfying
(
2.1
). Then
\chi(p_{0})=1+O_{\theta}((\log q)^{-2})
holds
for all primes
p_{0}\leqslant c_{3}q^{\theta}
, and thus

\chi(n)=1+O_{\theta}((\log q)^{-1})
holds for all natural numbers

n\leqslant c_{3}q^{\theta}
since each
n
has at most
O_{\theta}(\log q)
prime factors. Consequently,

\sum_{n\leqslant c_{3}q^{\theta}}\chi(n)\mathop{\,\gg\,}\limits_{\theta}q^{\theta};

this is impossible in view of (
2.3
). Thus, the
assumption is erroneous, and the lemma follows for general
q
.

When
q
is cubefree, we apply
(
2.2
) with the choices
N\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=0
,

H\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=c_{3}q^{\theta}
, and

\varepsilon\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{8r}(\theta-\frac{1}{4})-\frac{1}{4r^{2}}
,
where the integer
r
is large enough so that
\varepsilon>0
.
In this case, we have

(2.4)

\sum_{n\leqslant c_{3}q^{\theta}}\chi(n)\mathop{\,\ll\,}\limits_{\theta}q^{\theta-\frac{7}{8r}(\theta-\frac{1}{4})},

and the argument can be completed as above.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Our proof closely parallels that of Gonek
[
9
, Thm. 1]
.

We can assume without loss of generality that neither
T_{1}
nor
T_{2}

is the ordinate of a zero of
L(s,\chi)
.
Put
c\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1+\tfrac{1}{\log x}
and denote by

\mathcal{C}
the oriented rectangle:

c+iT_{1}~~\longrightarrow~~c+iT_{2}~~\longrightarrow~~1-c+iT_{2}~~\longrightarrow~~1-c+iT_{1}~~\longrightarrow~~c+iT_{1}.

By Cauchy’s theorem we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
T_{1}<\gamma<T_{2}\end{subarray}}x^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\bigg(\intop\nolimits_{c+iT_{1}}^{c+iT_{2}}+\intop\nolimits_{c+iT_{2}}^{1-c+iT_{2}}+\intop\nolimits_{1-c+iT_{2}}^{1-c+iT_{1}}+\intop\nolimits_{1-c+iT_{1}}^{c+iT_{1}}\bigg)\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\,x^{s}\,ds

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}\quad\text{(say)}.

We estimate the four integrals separately.

In the half-plane
\sigma>1
we have

(3.1)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=-\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}},

and therefore

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(c+it,\chi)\,x^{c+it}\,dt=-\frac{x^{c}}{2\pi}\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^{c}}\intop\nolimits_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\Big(\frac{x}{n}\Big)^{it}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{T_{2}-T_{1}}{2\pi}\Lambda(x)\chi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(x)+O\bigg(x^{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geqslant 2\\
n\neq x\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{c}}\min\Big\{T_{2},\frac{1}{|\log(x/n)|}\Big\}\bigg).

Applying Lemma
2.1
and taking into account that

x^{c}\ll x
, we see that

(3.2)

I_{1}=-\frac{T_{2}-T_{1}}{2\pi}\Lambda(x)\chi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}(x)+O\bigg(\!x\log x\bigg(\!\log\log 2x+\min\Big\{\frac{T_{2}}{x},\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle}\Big\}\bigg)\!\bigg).

Next, using Lemma
2.2
we have

I_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\\
|\gamma-T_{2}|<1\end{subarray}}\intop\nolimits_{c+iT_{2}}^{1-c+iT_{2}}\frac{x^{s}\,ds}{s-\rho}+O\bigg(\log 2qT_{2}\,\intop\nolimits_{1-c}^{c}x^{\sigma}\,d\sigma\bigg),

where the sum runs over the zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma

of
L(s,\chi)
for which
|\gamma-T_{2}|<1
, each zero being summed
according to its multiplicity. Since
x^{c}\ll x
, the error term
is
O(x\log 2qT_{2}/\log x)
, and arguing as in
[
9
, p. 402]

we have for each zero
\rho
in the sum:

\intop\nolimits_{c+iT_{2}}^{1-c+iT_{2}}\frac{x^{s}\,ds}{s-\rho}\ll x\,\log\log 2x.

Since there are at most
O(\log 2qT_{2})
such zeros, we obtain that

(3.3)

I_{2}\ll x\,\log\log 2x\,\log 2qT_{2}.

Similarly,

(3.4)

I_{4}\ll x\,\log\log 2x\,\log 2qT_{1}.

Finally, we come to

(3.5)

I_{3}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1-c+it,\chi)\,x^{1-c+it}\,dt.

We recall the functional equation
(cf.
[
11
, Eq. (10.35)]
):

(3.6)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1-s,\overline{\chi})-\log\frac{q}{2\pi}-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(1-s)+\frac{\pi}{2}\cot\frac{\pi}{2}(s+\kappa)

where

\kappa=\kappa(\chi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{if $\chi(-1)=1$},\\
1&\quad\text{if $\chi(-1)=-1$}.\\
\end{cases}

Setting
s\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1-c+it
for some
t\in(T_{1},T_{2})
,
and using the estimate
[
11
, Eq. (C.17)]
for the digamma function
\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma
,
we derive that

\displaystyle-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1-c+it,\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(c-it,\overline{\chi})+\log\frac{q}{2\pi}+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(c-it)-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\cot\tfrac{\pi}{2}(1-c+\theta+it)

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(c-it,\overline{\chi})+\log\frac{q\,t}{2\pi}+O(t^{-1}),

where the implied constant is absolute. Inserting this estimate
into (
3.5
), and taking into account that

x^{1-c}\ll 1
, it follows that

I_{3}=\frac{x^{1-c}}{2\pi}\intop\nolimits_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(c-it,\overline{\chi})\,x^{it}\,dt+O\left(\frac{\log 2qT_{2}}{\log x}+\log T_{2}\right).

Using (
3.1
), the first term is

\displaystyle-\frac{x^{1-c}}{2\pi}\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^{c}}\intop\nolimits_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}(nx)^{it}\,dt

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{n\geqslant 2}\frac{\Lambda(n)|\chi(n)|}{n^{c}\log nx}

\displaystyle\ll-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(c)\frac{1}{\log x}\ll\frac{1}{(c-1)\log x}=1.

Therefore,

(3.7)

I_{3}\ll\log 2qT_{2}.

Combining (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.4
),
and (
3.7
), the theorem follows.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We prove only the first result. The extension of the
theorem for cubefree moduli is proved using a similar
argument.

In what follows, all constants
c_{j}
are positive and
depend only on
\theta
. Put

\theta^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{2}(\theta+\frac{1}{3})
,
so
\theta^{\prime}
depends only on
\theta
,
and
\theta>\theta^{\prime}>\frac{1}{3}
.

Applying Lemma
2.3
to the nonprincipal character

\chi\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\chi_{1}\overline{\chi}_{2}
, it follows that
there are constants
c_{3},c_{4}
and a prime
p_{0}\geqslant 3
such that

(4.1)

p_{0}\leqslant c_{3}q^{\theta^{\prime}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad|\chi_{1}(p_{0})-\chi_{2}(p_{0})|\geqslant\frac{c_{4}}{(\log q)^{2}}.

Let
T\geqslant c_{1}q^{\theta}
, where

c_{1}\geqslant 1
; note that
\log qT\ll\log T
.
Let
\Delta
be any real number such that
0<\Delta<T
.
For
j=1
or
2
, let
\mathcal{S}_{j}(T)
be the multiset of
zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
of
L(s,\chi_{j})
with
T<\gamma<T+\Delta
,
where the number of times each zero occurs in
\mathcal{S}_{j}(T)
is the same as
its multiplicity. Applying Theorem
1.2
with

x\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=p_{0}
,
T_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T
, and
T_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T+\Delta
, we have

(4.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{j}(T)\end{subarray}}p_{0}^{\rho}=-\frac{\Delta\log p_{0}}{2\pi}\chi_{j}(p_{0})+O(p_{0}\log T\,\log\log 2p_{0})\qquad(j=1\text{~or~}2)

(here we have used the fact that
\langle p_{0}\rangle\geqslant 1
and that
\log qT\ll\log T
).

Now suppose that
\mathcal{S}_{1}(T)
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}(T)
are equal (as multisets).
Taking the difference of the estimates in (
4.2
), we get that

\frac{\Delta\log p_{0}}{2\pi}\big(\chi_{1}(p_{0})-\chi_{2}(p_{0})\big)\ll p_{0}\log T\,\log\log 2p_{0}.

Using (
4.1
) and the bound

q^{\theta^{\prime}}(\log q)^{3}\ll_{\theta}q^{\theta}
, we derive that

\Delta\mathop{\,\ll\,}\limits_{\theta}q^{\theta^{\prime}}(\log q)^{2}\log T\,\frac{\log\log 2p_{0}}{\log p_{0}}\mathop{\,\ll\,}\limits_{\theta}\frac{q^{\theta}\log T}{\log q}.

But this is plainly impossible if we define

\Delta\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{c_{2}q^{\theta}\log T}{\log q}

for some suitably large choice of
c_{2}
. With this choice of
\Delta
,
the contradiction implies that either
\Delta\geqslant T
,
or else
\mathcal{S}_{1}(T)\neq\mathcal{S}_{2}(T)
.
However, for a sufficiently large constant
c_{1}
, we have

\frac{c_{2}q^{\theta}\log T}{\log q}<T\qquad(T\geqslant c_{1}q^{\theta}).

In other words,
\Delta<T
, and thus
\mathcal{S}_{1}(T)\neq\mathcal{S}_{2}(T)
.
The theorem is proved.

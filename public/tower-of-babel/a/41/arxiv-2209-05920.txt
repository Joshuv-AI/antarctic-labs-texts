Categorical and K-theoretic Donaldson-Thomas theory of ℂ 3 \mathbb{C}^{3} (part II)
Tudor Pădurariu and Yukinobu Toda

Abstract
. Quasi-BPS categories appear as summands in semiorthogonal decompositions of DT categories for Hilbert schemes of points in the three dimensional affine space and in the categorical Hall algebra of the two dimensional affine space. In this paper, we prove several properties of quasi-BPS categories analogous to BPS sheaves in cohomological DT theory. We first prove a categorical analogue of Davison’s support lemma, namely that complexes in the quasi-BPS categories for coprime length and weight are supported over the small diagonal in the symmetric product of the three dimensional affine space. The categorical support lemma is used to determine the torsion-free generator of the torus equivariant K-theory of the quasi-BPS category of coprime length and weight. We next construct a bialgebra structure on the torsion free equivariant K-theory of quasi-BPS categories for a fixed ratio of length and weight. We define the K-theoretic BPS space as the space of primitive elements with respect to the coproduct. We show that all localized equivariant K-theoretic BPS spaces are one dimensional, which is a K-theoretic analogue of the computation of (numerical) BPS invariants of the three dimensional affine space.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Quasi-BPS categories

In
[
PTa
,
Păda
,
Păd23
]
, we studied quasi-BPS categories
\mathbb{S}(d)_{w}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}

(length)
and
w\in\mathbb{Z}
(weight)
in relation to categorical Donaldson-Thomas (DT) theory and to categorical Hall algebras (of surfaces and of
quivers with potentials).
They are defined to be full subcategories
of the category of matrix factorizations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(d)_{w}:=\mathrm{MF}(\mathbb{M}(d)_{w},\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d})\subset\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}(d),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d}),

where
\mathbb{M}(d)_{w}
is a
twisted
noncommutative resolution first considered by Špenko–Van den Bergh
[
ŠdB17
]
,
see Subsection
2.6
for more details.
Here, the stack
\mathcal{X}(d)
and the regular function
\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d}

are given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}(d):=\operatorname{Hom}(V,V)^{\oplus 3}/GL(V),\ \mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d}(A,B,C)=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits A[B,C],

where
V
is a
d
-dimensional vector space.

There is also a
graded quasi-BPS category
\mathbb{S}^{\rm{gr}}(d)_{v}
, which is equivalent (via Koszul duality)
to a subcategory

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(d)_{v}\subset D^{b}(\mathscr{C}(d)),

where
\mathscr{C}(d)
is the derived moduli stack of zero-dimensional
sheaves on
\mathbb{C}^{2}

with length
d
. We will also consider
T
-equivariant versions of these categories, where

T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
is the Calabi-Yau torus of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{K}=K(BT)=\mathbb{Z}[q_{1}^{\pm 1},q_{2}^{\pm 1}]

and by
\mathbb{F}
the fractional field of
\mathbb{K}
.
The (
T
-equivariant or not) Grothendieck groups of
\mathbb{S}(d)_{w}
and
\mathbb{S}^{\mathrm{gr}}(d)_{w}\cong\mathbb{T}(d)_{w}
are isomorphic.

The purpose of this paper is to prove several
properties of quasi-BPS categories
analogous to BPS sheaves in cohomological DT theory,
and use these properties to
compute the
T
-equivariant K-theory of quasi-BPS categories.

1.2.
Semiorthogonal decompositions

We briefly review semiorthogonal decompositions
with summands given by quasi-BPS categories proved in
[
PTa
,
Păda
]
.

In our previous paper
[
PTa
]
,
we constructed a semiorthogonal decomposition of
the categorification
\mathcal{DT}(d)

of the Donaldson-Thomas invariant
\mathrm{DT}_{d}
,
which is a virtual count of zero-dimensional closed subschemes in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
The category
\mathcal{DT}(d)
is defined by

\mathcal{DT}\left(d\right):=\text{MF}\left(\text{NHilb}(d),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d}\right),

see
[
PTa
, Subsection 1.5]
.
Here
\text{NHilb}(d)
is the noncommutative Hilbert scheme of points

\text{NHilb}(d):=\left(V\oplus\text{Hom}(V,V)^{\oplus 3}\right)^{\text{ss}}/GL(V).

More precisely, in
[
PTa
, Theorem 1.1]
we showed that there is
a semiorthogonal decomposition

(1.3)

\mathcal{DT}(d)=\left\langle\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{S}(d_{i})_{v_{i}+d_{i}\left(\sum_{i>j}d_{j}-\sum_{j>i}d_{j}\right)}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\begin{array}[]{c}0\leqslant v_{1}/d_{1}<\cdots<v_{k}/d_{k}<1\\
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}=d\end{array}\right\rangle.

There is also a semiorthogonal decomposition of
D^{b}(\mathscr{C}(d))

in graded quasi-BPS categories,
see
[
Păda
, Theorem 1.1]
:

(1.4)

D^{b}\left(\mathscr{C}(d)\right)=\left\langle\boxtimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{T}(d_{i})_{v_{i}}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\begin{array}[]{c}v_{1}/d_{1}<\cdots<v_{k}/d_{k}\\
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}=d\end{array}\right\rangle.

1.3.
Numerical and cohomological BPS invariants

It is expected that, for any smooth Calabi-Yau threefold
X
, there are certain deformation invariant integers called
BPS invariants
which determine the Donaldson-Thomas and Gromov-Witten invariants of
X
, see
[
PT14
, Section 2 and a half]
.
Denote by
\Omega_{d}
the BPS invariants of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
for
d\geqslant 1
. Then

(1.5)

\Omega_{d}=-1\text{ for all }d\geqslant 1.

The wall-crossing formula for the DT invariants of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
, see
[
JS12
, Section 6.3]
,
[
Tod10
, Remark 5.14]
, says that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\geqslant 0}\mathrm{DT}_{d}q^{d}=\prod_{d\geqslant 1}(1-(-q)^{d})^{d\Omega_{d}}=\prod_{d\geqslant 1}\frac{1}{\left(1-(-q)^{d}\right)^{d}},

see
[
PTa
, Subsection 1.6]
for more details.

Davison–Meinhardt
[
DM20
]
constructed a perverse sheaf
\mathcal{B}PS_{d}
on
\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
,
called
BPS sheaf
,
whose Euler characteristic recovers the BPS invariant
\Omega_{d}
. Davison
[
Davb
, Theorem 5.1]
showed that

(1.7)

\mathcal{B}PS_{d}=\Delta_{*}\mathrm{IC}_{\mathbb{C}^{3}},

where
\Delta\colon\mathbb{C}^{3}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
is the small diagonal. Davison
[
Davb
, Lemma 4.1]
also proved restrictions on the support of BPS sheaves for tripled quivers with potentials and used
[
Davb
, Lemma 4.1]
to prove purity results about stacks of representations of preprojective algebras
[
Davb
, Theorem A]
.
We refer to (
1.7
) as Davison’s support lemma.
The (cohomological) BPS spaces are the cohomology of the BPS sheaf.

Properties (and computations in special cases) of BPS sheaves have applications in the study of Hodge theory of various cohomological DT spaces (in particular, in proving purity of the Borel-Moore homology of moduli of objects in K
3
categories
[
Davc
]
) and in the study of Cohomological Hall algebras
[
Dava
,
KV
]
.

Our point of view is that the semiorthogonal decomposition (
1.3
) may be
regarded as a categorification of the wall-crossing formula (
1.6
)
and the category
\mathbb{S}(d)_{w}
may be regarded as a categorical analogue of the
BPS invariant (
1.5
) or BPS sheaf (
1.7
).
In this paper, we
make the above heuristic more rigorous.
Let
(d,w)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
be integers with
\gcd(d,w)=1
.
We first prove a categorical analogue of (
1.7
), namely
any object of
\mathbb{S}(d)_{w}
is supported over the small diagonal
in
\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
.
Further, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define a K-theoretic analogue of the cohomological BPS space:

\mathrm{P}(nd)_{nw}\subset K_{T}\left(\mathbb{S}(nd)_{nw}\right)\cong K_{T}\left(\mathbb{T}(nd)_{nw}\right)

and show that
\mathrm{P}(nd)_{nw,\mathbb{F}}:=\mathrm{P}(nd)_{nw}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{F}
is a one dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-vector space,
compare with (
1.5
).

1.5.
Integral generator of K-theory of quasi-BPS categories

In
[
PTa
, Theorem 1.2]
, we showed that the localized (i.e. taking

)

Using Lemma

and Theorem

, we show that:

.

(Theorem

)

Let
(d,w)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(d,w)=1
.
The
\mathbb{K}
-module

with generator

.

For

A corollary of the Borel-Weyl-Bott theorem

For future reference, we state a result from
[

, Section 3.2]
. We continue with the notations from Subsection

Let

be the weight lattice of

We assume that

-representation, meaning that for any weight

Let

be the dominant Weyl-shifted conjugate of

For a multiset

\sigma_{J}:=\sum_{\beta\in J}\beta.

Let

is dominant or zero. We let

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

be a symmetric
G
-representation, and
let
\lambda
be an antidominant cocharacter of

.
Recall the fixed and attracting stacks and the corresponding maps

For a weight

\left(\bigoplus_{J}\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes\Gamma_{G}\left((\chi-\sigma_{J})^{+}\right)\left[|J|-\ell(J)\right],d\right)\xrightarrow{\sim}p_{\lambda*}q_{\lambda}^{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X^{\lambda}}\otimes\Gamma_{G^{\lambda}}(\chi)\right),

2.5.
Matrix factorizations

In the notations from Subsection
2.2.1
,
we denote by

\displaystyle\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}(d),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d})

The support of

[
Păd22
]
for details.
We sometimes write

We also consider equivariant and graded matrix factorizations for the regular function (

),

[

, Subsection 2.6.2]
for details. The group

acts on the linear maps corresponding to the edges

of the quiver

Consider the two dimensional subtorus

).
Then

and preserves

.

3.1
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider a pair
(d,w)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
and let
\mathcal{F}
be an object in
\mathbb{S}(d)_{w}
. Assume there exists
p\in\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{3})\setminus\Delta
such that the support of
\mathcal{F}
intersects
\pi^{-1}(p)
. Write
p=\sum_{i=1}^{l}p^{(i)}
,
p^{(i)}=d^{(i)}x^{(i)}
,
d^{(i)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
x^{(i)}\neq x^{(i^{\prime})}
for
i\neq i^{\prime}
and
l\geqslant 2
.
Then there exist non-zero objects

\mathcal{F}_{i}\in\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}_{p^{(i)}}(d^{(i)}),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d^{(i)}})_{w^{(i)}}

for
1\leqslant i\leqslant l
with

\frac{w^{(1)}}{d^{(1)}}=\cdots=\frac{w^{(l)}}{d^{(l)}}

and
w^{(1)}+\cdots+w^{(l)}=w
.
In particular, if
\gcd(d,w)=1
,
then any object in
\mathbb{S}(d)_{w}
is supported on

\pi^{-1}(\Delta)
. The same result holds for the categories
\mathbb{S}^{\bullet}_{\ast}(d)_{w}
introduced in Subsection
2.6.2
.

In Subsection
4.1
, we discuss divisibility properties of complexes supported on
\pi^{-1}(\Delta)
, see Lemma
4.1
. In Subsection
4.2
, we use Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
to
show that
K_{T}(\mathbb{S}(d)_{w})^{\prime}
is a free
\mathbb{K}
-module with generator
[\mathcal{E}_{d,w}]
when
\gcd(d,w)=1
.

(3.23)

\displaystyle\rho-\rho_{p}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j,a<b}(\beta_{i}^{(b)}-\beta_{j}^{(a)}),

the weight
\chi+\rho
satisfies (
3.21
). Conversely, if
\chi
is a

GL(V)
-dominant
T(d)
-weight satisfying (
3.21
), from
[
PTa
, Proposition 3.5]

it is written as

\displaystyle\chi+\rho=\sum_{i>j}c_{ij}(\beta_{i}-\beta_{j})

for
0\leqslant c_{ij}\leqslant 3/2
.
Therefore from (
3.23
), the weight
\chi+\rho_{p}
is written as (
3.22
).
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

The subcategory (
3.18
) for
k\geqslant 2

is right orthogonal to

\mathbb{S}_{p}(d)_{w}
for
w=w_{1}+\cdots+w_{k}
, where
w_{i}

is given in (
3.13
).

Proof.

Recall that
w_{i}=v_{i}+d_{i}(\sum_{i>j}d_{j}-\sum_{j>i}d_{j})
.
Choose weights
\psi_{i}\in\textbf{W}_{p_{i}}(d_{i})_{0}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
.
From the proof of semiorthogonality in
[
Păda
, Proposition 4.3]
,
it is enough to show that the
r
-invariant of the weight

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\psi_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}w_{i}\tau_{d_{i}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{p_{i}}+\rho_{p}

with respect to the polytope
\textbf{W}_{p}(d)

is bigger than
1/2
.
Here, the polytope
\textbf{W}_{p}(d)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\textbf{W}_{p}(d):=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{sum}[0,\beta]+\mathbb{R}\tau_{d},

where the sum is after all weights in
\operatorname{Ext}_{Q}^{1}(R,R)
.

Suppose the contrary, i.e. the weight (
3.24
) lies in

\textbf{W}_{p}(d)_{w}
. Let
\lambda
be the cocharacter
as in (
3.17
),
and write it as
\lambda=(\lambda^{(1)},\lambda^{(2)})
,
where
\lambda^{(j)}
is the cocharacter of
GL(V^{(j)})
.
We set
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{End}(V)

and
\mathfrak{g}^{(j)}=\mathrm{End}(V^{(j)})
.
The weight (
3.24
) is written as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\psi_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}\tau_{d_{i}}-\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda>0}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{(1)})^{\lambda^{(1)}>0}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{(2)})^{\lambda^{(2)}>0}.

By the assumption, the above weight
is an element of
\textbf{W}_{p}(d)_{w}
.
Then using an argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.5
, we have that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\psi_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}\tau_{d_{i}}-\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda>0}\in\textbf{W}(d)_{w}.

Note that we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda,\sum_{i=1}^{k}\psi_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}\tau_{d_{i}}-\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda>0}-w\tau_{d}\right\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(k+1-i)d_{i}\left(\frac{v_{i}}{d_{i}}-\frac{w}{d}\right)-\left\langle\lambda,\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda>0}\right\rangle.

For
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, define

\tilde{v}_{i}:=d_{i}\left(\frac{v_{i}}{d_{i}}-\frac{w}{d}\right).

Then
\tilde{v}_{1}+\cdots+\tilde{v}_{k}=0

and
\tilde{v}_{1}+\cdots+\tilde{v}_{l}<0
for
1\leqslant l<k
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(k+1-i)d_{i}\left(\frac{v_{i}}{d_{i}}-\frac{w}{d}\right)=\sum_{l=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}\tilde{v}_{i}\right)<0.

It follows that

(3.26)

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda,\sum_{i=1}^{k}\psi_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}\tau_{d_{i}}-\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda>0}-w\tau_{d}\right\rangle<-\left\langle\lambda,\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda>0}\right\rangle.

On the other hand, we claim that for any weight
\chi\in\textbf{W}(d)_{0}
, we have that

(3.27)

\langle\lambda,\chi\rangle\geqslant-\left\langle\lambda,\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda>0}\right\rangle.

We thus obtain a contradiction with (
3.26
) and (
3.25
).
To prove (
3.27
), write

\chi=\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}(\beta_{i}-\beta_{j})

with
0\leqslant c_{ij}\leqslant 3/2
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\lambda,\chi\rangle\geqslant\sum_{\langle\lambda,\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}\rangle<0}c_{ij}\langle\lambda,\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}\rangle\geqslant\frac{3}{2}\sum_{\langle\lambda,\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}\rangle<0}\langle\lambda,\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}\rangle=-\left\langle\lambda,\frac{3}{2}\mathfrak{g}^{\lambda>0}\right\rangle.

∎

4.
Integral generator of equivariant K-theory of quasi-BPS categories

Recall the graded quasi-BPS category
\mathbb{S}^{\rm{gr}}(d)_{v}
,
which is equivalent via Koszul duality to

\mathbb{T}(d)_{v}\subset D^{b}(\mathscr{C}(d)).

In
[
PTa
]
, we proved that
the monomials
[\mathcal{E}_{d_{1},v_{1}}]\ast\cdots\ast[\mathcal{E}_{d_{k},v_{k}}]

for
v_{i}/d_{i}=v/d

give a basis of the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space

K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{F}
.
In this section, we consider the torsion free
equivariant K-theory defined by

\displaystyle K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})^{\prime}:=K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})/(\mathbb{K}\text{-torsion}).

It is conjectured by Schiffmann–Vasserot
[
SV13
, Conjecture 7.13]
that
K_{T}(\mathscr{C}(d))
is torsion free as a
\mathbb{K}
-module,
so conjecturally
K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})^{\prime}
is isomorphic to

K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})
.
We compute
K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})^{\prime}
when
\gcd(d,v)=1
, and when

(d,v)=(2,0)
. Theorem
3.1
plays a key role
in the case of
\gcd(d,v)=1
.

4.1.
Sheaves supported over the small diagonal

Let

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathscr{C}(d)}[-1]:=\operatorname{Spec}\mathrm{Sym}\left(\mathbb{T}_{\mathscr{C}(d)}[1]\right)

be
the
(-1)
-shifted cotangent of
\mathscr{C}(d)
.
An object
\mathcal{E}\in D^{b}(\mathscr{C}(d))
has singular support
[
AG15
]
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}^{\rm{sg}}(\mathcal{E})\subset(\Omega_{\mathscr{C}(d)}[-1])^{\rm{cl}}.

We identify the right hand side with

\mathrm{Crit}(\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d})\subset\mathcal{X}(d)
,
which is isomorphic to the moduli stack of
zero-dimensional coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{C}^{3}

of length
d
.
Under the Koszul duality equivalence (
2.23
), we have by
[
Toda
, Proposition 2.3.9]
that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}^{\rm{sg}}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{Supp}(\Phi(\mathcal{E})).

In the following lemma, we show that the
K-theory class of an object in
D^{b}_{T}(\mathscr{C}(d))
with singular support
contained in
\pi^{-1}(\Delta)
has a certain divisibility property.
The proof is inspired by the proof of wheel conditions
in
[
Zha
, Theorem 2.9, Corollary 2.10]
,
[
Neg23
, Proposition 2.11]
:

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\mathcal{E}\in D^{b}_{T}(\mathscr{C}(d))
whose singular support is
contained in
\pi^{-1}(\Delta)
, the element

i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}]\in K_{T}(\mathcal{Y}(d))=\mathbb{K}[z_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,z_{d}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}

is divisible by

(4.1)

\displaystyle(q_{1}-1)^{d-1}(q_{2}-1)^{d-1}(q_{1}q_{2}-1)^{d-1}\in\mathbb{K}.

Proof.

By
[
PTa
, Lemma 4.9]
, it is enough to show that, for

\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{MF}_{T}(\mathcal{X}(d),\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W)
supported
on
\pi^{-1}(\Delta)
,
its image under the forget-the-potential map (
1.8
):

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Theta([\mathcal{F}]):=[\mathcal{F}^{0}]-[\mathcal{F}^{1}]\in K_{T}(\mathrm{MF}(\mathcal{X}(d),0))=\mathbb{K}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,z_{d}^{\pm d}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}

is divisible by
(q_{1}-1)^{d-1}(q_{2}-1)^{d-1}(q_{1}q_{2}-1)^{d-1}
.
We consider the morphism

\displaystyle h\colon\mathbb{C}^{d-1}\setminus\{0\}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Crit}(\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus 3}

\displaystyle(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d-1})

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(0,0,(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d-1},0)\right),

where
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d-1},0)
is the diagonal matrix.
Let
T=(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{2}
act on
\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
with weight
q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1}
and
let the maximal torus
T(d)\subset GL(d)
act on
\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
trivially.
Then the above morphism
h
is
T\times T(d)
-equivariant, so it induces a
morphism

\displaystyle h\colon(\mathbb{C}^{d-1}\setminus\{0\})/(T\times T(d))\to\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus 3}/(T\times T(d))\to\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus 3}/(T\times GL(d)).

By the localization sequence

\displaystyle K_{T\times T(d)}(\{0\})\to K_{T\times T(d)}(\mathbb{C}^{d-1})\to K_{T\times T(d)}(\mathbb{C}^{d-1}\setminus\{0\})\to 0,

we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle K_{T\times T(d)}(\mathbb{C}^{d-1}\setminus\{0\})\cong\mathbb{K}[z^{\pm 1}_{1},\ldots,z^{\pm 1}_{d}]/(1-q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1})^{d-1}.

Note that we have

\displaystyle\pi\circ h(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d-1})=\{(0,0,t_{1}),\ldots,(0,0,t_{d-1}),(0,0,0)\}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{3}),

in particular the image of
h
does not intersect
\pi^{-1}(\Delta)\subset\mathrm{Crit}(\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits W_{d})
.
We thus have that

h^{\ast}(\Theta([\mathcal{F}]))=0\text{ in }K_{T\times T(d)}(\mathbb{C}^{d-1}\setminus\{0\}).

Therefore the element (
4.2
) is divisible by
(q_{1}q_{2}-1)^{d-1}
.
By replacing
h
with

\displaystyle(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d-1})

\displaystyle\mapsto(0,(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d-1},0),0),

\displaystyle(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d-1})

\displaystyle\mapsto((t_{1},\ldots,t_{d-1},0),0,0),

the element (
4.2
) is also divisible by
(q_{1}-1)^{d-1}
,
(q_{2}-1)^{d-1}
, respectively.
∎

By combining the above lemma
with Theorem
3.1
, we obtain
the following:

Corollary 4.2
.

For any object
\mathcal{E}\in\mathbb{T}_{T}(d)_{v}
with

\gcd(d,v)=1
, the element

\displaystyle i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}]\in K_{T}(\mathcal{Y}(d))=\mathbb{K}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,z_{d}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}

is divisible by (
4.1
).
In particular, the element

i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{d,v}]

in (
2.39
) for
\gcd(d,v)=1

is divisible by (
4.1
).

Remark 4.3
.

It is not clear from the expression (
2.39
) that
i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{d,v}]
is divisible
by (
4.1
) when
\gcd(d,v)=1
.
Further, the condition
\gcd(d,v)=1
is necessary
for the above divisibility.
Indeed, for
d=2
, a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{2,0}]

\displaystyle=(1-q_{1}^{-1})(1-q_{2}^{-1})(1-q_{1}^{-1}-q_{2}^{-1}-q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1}+z_{1}^{-1}z_{2}+z_{1}z_{2}^{-1}),

\displaystyle i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{2,1}]

\displaystyle=(1-q_{1}^{-1})(1-q_{2}^{-1})(1-q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1})(z_{1}+z_{2}).

The element
i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{2,0}]

is not divisible by (
4.1
).
In particular, the singular support of
\mathcal{E}_{2,0}
is not included in

\pi^{-1}(\Delta)
.

4.2.
Integral generator for the coprime case

Recall that
K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})
is expected to be torsion
free as a
\mathbb{K}
-module.
We also expect that
K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})
is freely generated by

[\mathcal{E}_{d,v}]
if
\gcd(d,v)=1
.
The following theorem, which is an application of Corollary
4.2
,
gives evidence towards this expectation.

Theorem 4.4
.

Consider a pair
(d,v)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(d,v)=1
.
There is an isomorhism of
\mathbb{K}
-modules:

\displaystyle K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{E}_{d,v}].

Proof.

By the isomorphism (
2.31
),
the
\mathbb{K}
-module
K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})^{\prime}

is isomorphic to the image of

\displaystyle i_{\ast}\colon K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})\to\mathbb{K}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,z_{d}^{\pm}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}.

It is enough to show that the image of the
above morphism
is generated by
i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{d,v}]
as a
\mathbb{K}
-module.
By Corollary
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}(i_{\ast})\subset(q_{1}-1)^{d-1}(q_{2}-1)^{d-1}(q_{1}q_{2}-1)^{d-1}\mathbb{K}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,z_{d}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{d}}.

Moreover,
\mathrm{Im}(i_{\ast})\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{F}
is
generated by
i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{d,v}]
over
\mathbb{F}

by
[
PTa
, Theorem 4.12]
.
It is thus enough to show that
i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{d,v}]
is written as

\displaystyle i_{\ast}[\mathcal{E}_{d,v}]=(q_{1}-1)^{d-1}(q_{2}-1)^{d-1}(q_{1}q_{2}-1)^{d-1}\cdot E,

where
E
is not divisible by a non-unit element in
\mathbb{K}
.

By (
2.39
), we have that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}\left(\frac{z_{1}^{m_{1}}\ldots z_{d}^{m_{d}}}{(1-q^{-1}z_{1}^{-1}z_{2})\cdots(1-q^{-1}z_{d-1}^{-1}z_{d})}\cdot\prod_{j>i}\xi(z_{i}z_{j}^{-1})\right)=(q_{1}q_{2}-1)^{d-1}\cdot E.

By setting

(4.4)

f_{d}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}):=\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}(z_{i+1}-q_{1}^{-1}z_{i})(z_{i+1}-q_{2}^{-1}z_{i})\cdot\\
\prod_{j>i+1}(z_{j}-q_{1}^{-1}z_{i})(z_{j}-q_{2}^{-1}z_{i})(z_{j}-qz_{i}),

we can write (
4.3
) as

(4.5)

\displaystyle(-q)^{-\frac{1}{2}(d-1)(d-2)}(z_{1}\cdots z_{d})^{2-d}\cdot\mathrm{Sym}\left(\frac{z_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots z_{d-1}^{m_{d-1}}z_{d}^{m_{d}-1}f_{d}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})}{\prod_{j>i}(z_{j}-z_{i})}\right).

Plug in
z_{i}=q_{1}^{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
in the formula (
4.5
). The only non-zero term in the sum above corresponds to the identity permutation. The factors of this term not in
\mathbb{K}^{*}
divide

(4.6)

q_{1}^{a+1}-1,\,q_{1}^{a}-q_{2}^{-1},\text{ or }q_{1}^{a}-q_{2}\text{ for some }a\geqslant 1.

Next plug in
z_{i}=q_{2}^{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
in the formula (
4.5
). The only non-zero term in the sum of (
4.5
) corresponds to the identity permutation. The factors of this term not in
\mathbb{K}^{*}
divide

(4.7)

q_{2}^{a+1}-1,\,q_{2}^{a}-q_{1}^{-1},\text{ or }q_{2}^{a}-q_{1}\text{ for some }a\geqslant 1.

The only factors which divide terms in both sets (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) are
q_{1}-q_{2}
and
q_{1}q_{2}-1
. The factor
q_{1}q_{2}-1
appears with multiplicity
d-1
as it corresponds to
z_{i+1}-q_{2}^{-1}z_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1
.

It suffices to show that
q_{1}-q_{2}
does not divide (
4.5
).
We will be using computations from
[
Neg22
, Section 2]
. Note that
q_{1},q_{2},q
from our paper correspond to
q_{1}^{-1},q_{2}^{-1},q^{-1}
in loc. cit. Further, the weight
v\in\mathbb{Z}
corresponds to
k\in\mathbb{Z}
in loc. cit.
By
[
Neg22
, Equation (2.35)]
, the equality
P_{d,k}=E_{d,k}
for
\gcd(d,k)=1
in loc. cit., see
[
Neg22
, Equations (2.6) and (2.35)]
, and the isomorphism between shuffle algebras
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{F}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{F}}
, we can write

(q_{1}q_{2}-1)^{d-1}\cdot E=y+t\text{ in }\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\mathbb{K}\left[\frac{1}{1-q_{2}^{-1}},\frac{1}{1-q^{-1}}\right]

for
t
a
\mathbb{K}
-torsion element and

(4.8)

y:=\frac{(1-q_{2}^{-1})(1-q)^{v}}{(1-q_{2}^{-1})^{v}(1-q^{-1})}\cdot\mathrm{Sym}\left(\frac{z_{1}^{m_{1}}\ldots z_{d}^{m_{d}}}{(1-q_{2}z_{1}^{-1}z_{2})\cdots(1-q_{2}z_{d-1}^{-1}z_{d})}\cdot\prod_{j>i}\xi(z_{i}z_{j}^{-1})\right).

It suffices to show that
q_{1}-q_{2}
does not divide
y
.
By setting

(4.9)

g_{d}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}):=\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}(z_{i+1}-q_{1}^{-1}z_{i})(z_{i+1}-qz_{i})\cdot\\
\prod_{j>i+1}(z_{j}-q_{1}^{-1}z_{i})(z_{j}-q_{2}^{-1}z_{i})(z_{j}-qz_{i}),

we can write the
\mathrm{Sym}(-)
term of
y
as

(4.10)

\displaystyle(-q_{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}(d-1)(d-2)}(z_{1}\cdots z_{d})^{2-d}\cdot\mathrm{Sym}\left(\frac{z_{1}^{m_{1}}\ldots z_{d-1}^{m_{d-1}}z_{d}^{m_{d}-1}g_{d}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d})}{\prod_{j>i}(z_{j}-z_{i})}\right).

It suffices to show that
q_{1}-q_{2}
does not divide (
4.10
).
Let
z_{i}=q^{-i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
. The only non-zero term in the sum of (
4.10
) corresponds to the identity permutation. The factors of this term not in
\mathbb{K}^{*}
divide

q^{-a-1}-1,q^{-a}-q_{1}^{-1},q^{-a}-q_{1}\text{ for some }a\geqslant 1.

None of these polynomials is divisible by
q_{1}-q_{2}
, and the conclusion thus follows.
∎

4.3.
Integral generator for
K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(2)_{0})^{\prime}

The computation of
K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})^{\prime}

is more subtle when
\gcd(d,v)>1
,
since the monomials
[\mathcal{E}_{d_{1},v_{1}}]\ast\cdots\ast[\mathcal{E}_{d_{k},v_{k}}]

for
v_{i}/d_{i}=v/d

do not generate it over
\mathbb{K}
,
see Remark
4.8
. We need to find
other objects giving
\mathbb{K}
-basis of

K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(d)_{v})^{\prime}
.
Here we give a computation for
(d,v)=(2,0)
.

Let
V
be a two-dimensional vector space and let

\mathfrak{sl}\subset\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{End}(V)
be its
traceless part. Note that

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{sl}]=1+z_{1}^{-1}z_{2}+z_{1}z_{2}^{-1}\in K(BGL(2))=\mathbb{Z}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},z_{2}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{2}}.

The structure sheaf of the classical truncation
\mathscr{C}(2)^{\rm{cl}}

fits into the exact sequence

(4.11)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}(q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-2})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}(q_{1}^{-2}q_{2}^{-1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{sl}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}(q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B}}{{\to}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{C}(2)^{\rm{cl}}}\to 0.

Here over
(X,Y)\in\mathfrak{g}^{\oplus 2}
, the maps
A
,
B
are given by

\displaystyle A|_{(X,Y)}=(2X-\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits X\cdot I,2Y-\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits Y\cdot I),\ B|_{(X,Y)}(Z)=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\nolimits(Z[X,Y]).

We set
M_{1}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{C}(2)^{\rm{cl}}}(q_{1}q_{2})
.
We also define
a coherent sheaf
M_{2}
on
\mathscr{C}(2)^{\rm{cl}}
by the exact sequence

(4.12)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}(q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B^{\vee}}}{{\to}}\mathfrak{sl}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A^{\vee}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}(q_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}(q_{2})\to M_{2}\to 0.

The sequences (
4.11
), (
4.12
)
are the Eagon-Northcott complex and the Buchsbaum-Rim complex
associated with
A^{\vee}\colon\mathfrak{sl}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}(q_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}(2)}(q_{2})
,
respectively. In particular they are exact, see
[
Eis95
, Theorem A2.10]
.
From the exact sequences (
4.11
), (
4.12
),
we have
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathbb{T}(2)_{0}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

As a
\mathbb{K}
-module, we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(2)_{0})^{\prime}=\mathbb{K}[M_{1}]\oplus\mathbb{K}[M_{2}].

Proof.

It is enough to show that the image of

(4.14)

\displaystyle i_{\ast}\colon K_{T}(\mathbb{T}(2)_{0})\to\mathbb{K}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},z_{2}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{2}}

is generated by
i_{\ast}M_{1}
,
i_{\ast}M_{2}
and that
i_{\ast}M_{1},i_{\ast}M_{2}

are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}
.
Below we omit
i_{\ast}

from the notation
i_{\ast}M_{j}
.
From the exact sequences (
4.11
), (
4.12
), we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle[M_{1}]=q_{1}q_{2}+q_{1}^{-1}+q_{2}^{-1}-\mathfrak{sl},\ [M_{2}]=q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1}+q_{1}+q_{2}-\mathfrak{sl}.

A direct computation shows that

(4.16)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{E}_{1,0}\ast\mathcal{E}_{1,0}]

\displaystyle=1+q_{1}^{-1}+q_{2}^{-1}+q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-2}+q_{1}^{-2}q_{2}^{-1}+q_{1}^{-2}q_{2}^{-2}-2\mathfrak{sl}q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1}

\displaystyle=q_{1}^{-1}q_{2}^{-1}([M_{1}]+[M_{2}]),

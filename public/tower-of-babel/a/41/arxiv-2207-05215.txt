Scattering properties of charged black holes in nonlinear and Maxwell’s electrodynamics
Marco A. A. de Paula, Luiz C. S. Leite, Luís C. B. Crispino

Abstract
We investigate the scattering properties of a massless scalar field in the background of a charged Ayón-Beato-García regular black hole solution. Using a numerical approach, we compute the differential scattering cross section for arbitrary values of the scattering angle and of the incident wave frequency. We compare our results with those obtained via the classical geodesic scattering of massless particles, as well as with the semiclassical glory approximation, and show that they present an excellent agreement in the corresponding limits. We also show that Ayón-Beato-García and Reissner-Nordström black hole solutions present similar scattering properties, for low-to-moderate values of the black hole electric charge, for any value of the scattering angle.

Introduction

Scattering is one of the most common procedures in Physics to improve our knowledge about objects. In black hole (BH) scenarios, this procedure is a little more sophisticated due to the existence of a one-way membrane, called event horizon. Thus, for instance, we cannot investigate the interior of BHs, as we study the interior of the atomic nucleus. Nevertheless, it is possible to analyze how BHs absorb and scatter bosonic and fermionic fields, propagating in their vicinities. Typically, in order to do that, we choose a particular field with spin
s
, e.g., scalar (
s=0
), Dirac (
s=1/2
), electromagnetic (
s=1
) or gravitational (
s=2
) field, and study its absorption and scattering processes in a given BH spacetime. Since the late 1960s this has been done in several standard BHs scenarios (cf., e.g., Refs.
FHM1988
;
CDE2009
;
OCH2011
;
CDHO2014
;
CDHO2015
;
BC2016
;
LBC2017
and references therein).

Once there are several observational evidences that BHs are likely to be surrounded by matter
N2005
, the investigation of how they interact with their astrophysical environment becomes essential. Moreover, the scattering of light plays an important role in the analysis of BHs shadows. As an example, we may anticipate subtle properties of BHs shadows which, in principle, could be measured by the Event Horizon Telescope Consortium (EHTC), which recently unveiled the first shadow image of a supermassive BH
AAA2019L1
. We can, e.g., compute the shadows of distinct BH solutions and verify if they can be distinguishable through the EHTC data (cf., e.g., Ref.
SVX2020

In the last years, the absorption and scattering properties of regular BHs (RBHs) solutions (for a review about RBHs see, e.g., Ref.

Their key motivation was to find a finite self-energy for the electromagnetic field of a point charge, in contrast with the infinite result obtained via Maxwell’s theory. II.1
Geodesic Scattering

The classical Lagrangian,
\mathrm{L_{\rm{geo}}}
, associated with the geodesic motion can be written as

\mathrm{L_{\rm{geo}}}=\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu},

(6)

where the overdot denotes differentiation with respect to an affine parameter. Since the line element (
3

For null geodesics, the condition

is satisfied, and we can show that

\left(\dfrac{du}{d\varphi}\right)^{2}=\mathcal{T}(u)\equiv\dfrac{1}{b^{2}}-f(1/u)u^{2},

(8)

in which

, and

is the impact parameter. By solving Eq. (
8

1

\alpha
is, the stronger is the influence of the BH in the massless particle trajectory.

Glory Approximation

As a useful tool to anticipate wave interference effects of the scattering process near the backward direction, stands out the glory approximation. The differential SCS of a scalar wave in a spherically symmetric BH background can be written as

III.1
Scalar Field

The Klein-Gordon (KG) equation governs the propagation of a massless and chargeless test scalar field
\Phi
. In the background of curved spacetimes, it can be written as

\nabla_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}\Phi=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\Phi\right)=0.

(17)

Taking into account the spherical symmetry of the spacetime under consideration (cf. Eq. (
3
)), we can decompose the scalar field
\Phi
as

\Phi\equiv\sum_{l}^{\infty}C_{\omega l}\Phi_{\omega l}=\sum_{l}^{\infty}C_{\omega l}\frac{\Psi_{\omega l}(r)}{r}P_{l}(\cos\theta)e^{-i\omega t},

(18)

where
\omega
and
l
are the frequency and the angular momentum of the scalar field mode, respectively, and the constant coefficients
C_{\omega l}
are determined by the boundary conditions. The functions
P_{l}(\cos\theta)
are the Legendre polynomials and
\Psi_{\omega l}(r)
are radial functions. Using the tortoise coordinate
r_{\star}
, which can be obtained from the relation
dr=f(r)dr_{\star}
, we can show that
\Psi_{\omega l}(r)
satisfies

\frac{d^{2}}{dr_{\star}^{2}}\Psi_{\omega l}+\left(\omega^{2}-V_{\rm{eff}}(r)\right)\Psi_{\omega l}=0,

(19)

where the effective potential
V_{\rm{eff}}(r)
is

V_{\rm{eff}}(r)\equiv f(r)\left(\dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{df(r)}{dr}+\dfrac{l(l+1)}{r^{2}}\right).

(20)

Notice that the domain of the tortoise coordinate is
(-\infty,\infty)
, whereas the domain of the coordinate
r
is
[r_{+},\infty)
. In Fig.
6
we show the effective potential of the ABG RBH,
V^{\rm{ABG}}_{\rm{eff}}
, for different values of
l
. We see that the potential barrier grows as we increase
l
and also presents the following asymptotic limits:

\lim_{r_{\star}\rightarrow\pm\infty}V_{\rm{eff}}(r_{\star})=0.

(21)

III.2
Scattering Cross Section

For the BH scattering problem, we assume the following boundary conditions

\Psi_{\omega l}\sim\begin{cases}T_{\omega l}e^{-i\omega r_{\star}},&r_{\star}\rightarrow-\infty,\\
e^{-i\omega r_{\star}}+R_{\omega l}e^{i\omega r_{\star}},&r_{\star}\rightarrow\infty,\end{cases}

(22)

where
T_{\omega l}
and
R_{\omega l}
are complex coefficients. The solutions (
22
) are the so-called
in modes
, which correspond to plane waves purely incoming from the past null infinity. From the conservation of the flux, one can show that

|T_{\omega l}|^{2}+|R_{\omega l}|^{2}=1,

(23)

in which
|T_{\omega l}|^{2}
and
|R_{\omega l}|^{2}
are the transmission and reflection coefficients, respectively.

The differential SCS for static and spherically spacetimes is given by
FHM1988

\dfrac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=|h(\theta)|^{2},

(24)

where the scattering amplitude
h(\theta)
reads

h(\theta)=\dfrac{1}{2i\omega}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(2l+1)[e^{2i\delta_{l}(\omega)}-1]P_{l}(\cos\theta),

(25)

with the phase shifts
e^{2i\delta_{l}(\omega)}
defined as

e^{2i\delta_{l}(\omega)}\equiv(-1)^{l+1}R_{\omega l}.

(26)

In order to compute Eq. (
24
) via the partial-waves method, we need to obtain the coefficients
R_{\omega l}
. We have applied the stiffness switching integration method
WP2007
to solve Eq. (
19
) numerically from near the event horizon,
r_{h}
, up to some radial position far from the BH. Then we match the numerical solutions of Eq. (
19
) with the appropriated boundary conditions given by Eq. (
22
). We also adopt the convergence method developed in Refs.
YRW1954
;
DDL2006
to improve the results obtained from Eq. (
25
).

Final Remarks

We have investigated the scattering properties of a massless and chargeless test scalar field in the background of ABG RBHs. We computed the SCS numerically and compared our results with classical (geodesic) scattering and semiclassical glory approximation, showing that: (i) for small scattering angles, the behavior of the SCS is well described by classical results; (ii) for large scattering angles, the SCS can be approximated by the glory formula.

Regarding the influence of electric charge, we have shown that its contribution to the SCS of ABG BHs is more important as we consider higher values of the scattering angle. For small angles, the SCS diverges due to the behavior of the gravitational potential and, in this limit, the contribution of the BH’s charge to the SCS is less significant. Moreover, near the backward direction, the interference fringe widths become larger, as one enhances the value of
\alpha
. The latter can be understood with the help of semiclassical formula (
16
), which relates the interference fringe widths to
b_{g}^{-1}
, and we have shown that
b_{g}
decreases monotonically with the electric charge for ABG BHs.

We have found that the equality between the glory parameter
b_{g}
of ABG and RN BHs can be achieved for
\alpha^{\rm{RN}}\lesssim 0.8825
and
\alpha^{\rm{ABG}}\leq 1
. By considering pairs
(\alpha^{\rm{ABG}},\alpha^{\rm{RN}})
for which
b_{g}
coincide, we have shown that for small-to-moderate values of
\alpha
, the results for the SCSs of ABG and RN BHs are very similar in the whole scattering angle range. Therefore, the results presented along this paper, combined with those obtained in Ref.
PLC2020
, indicate that, from the point of view of the scattering and absorption properties of massless scalar waves, we may not be able to distinguish RBHs solutions from standard ones.

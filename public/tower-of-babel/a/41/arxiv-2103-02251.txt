Odd-frequency pairing inherent in Bogoliubov Fermi liquid
Tatsuya Miki, Shun-Ta Tamura, Shoma Iimura, and Shintaro Hoshino

Abstract
The disorder and interaction effects on Bogoliubov-Fermi surfaces with preserved inversion symmetry are studied for a low-energy effective model coupled to bosonic degrees of freedom. It is shown that the non-ideal Bogoliubov quasiparticles (bogolons) generically induce the odd-frequency pair amplitude which reflects a Cooper pairing at different time. The self-energy of bogolons is mainly contributed by the disorder effects in the low frequency limit as in the usual electron liquid. Depending on the choice of the parameters, there are two kinds of solutions: one is frequency-independent (but with sign function of frequency) and the other is proportional to the inverse of the frequency, which exist in both the normal and anomalous parts of the self-energy. These characteristic self-energy structures are clearly reflected in the single-particle spectrum. Since the bogolons are originally composed of electrons, the connection between the two is also sought using the concrete j = 3 / 2 j=3/2 fermion model, which reveals that the odd-frequency pairing of bogolons is mainly made of the electrons’ odd-frequency pairing.

In this paper, we demonstrate the emergence of the spatially uniform odd-frequency pair in the world not of electrons but of bogolons.
This odd-frequency pairing is induced by a non-ideality of bogolons, where the broken gauge-symmetry plays a crucial role as different from the electronic interactions.
As shown in the following, the induced odd-frequency pair is closely related to the energy structure of the usual Fermi-liquid self-energy existing in the diagonal Green function for electrons, but for bogolons it is reflected also in the off-diagonal part, i.e., pair amplitude and pair potential.

Below, we connect the two important concepts of superconductivity, i.e., the Bogoliubov Fermi surface and odd-frequency pairing, based on the concrete model.
As in the theories of superconductivity and of Fermi liquid, the degrees of freedom near the Fermi level are important for the low-temperature and low-energy properties.
Hence we also assume that the dominant contribution enters through the degrees of freedom near the Bogoliubov Fermi surface.
The non-interacting part of the Hamiltonian for bogolons near the Fermi level is written as

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}\xi_{\bm{k}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\alpha_{\bm{k}}.

(1)

In the following, we consider the inversion symmetric systems (
\xi_{-\bm{k}}=\xi_{\bm{k}}
) with a time-reversal symmetry breaking where the Fermi surface is topologically protected
Agterberg17
.
In order to have an intuition for the energy scales, we write the energy dispersion as
\xi_{\bm{k}}=\frac{\bm{k}^{2}}{2m_{b}}-\varepsilon_{{\rm F}b}

m_{b}
and
\varepsilon_{{\rm F}b}

\hbar=k_{\rm B}=1

As inferred from the original electronic system

m_{b}\sim\frac{\Delta}{\varepsilon_{{\rm F}e}}m_{e}

\varepsilon_{{\rm F}b}\sim\Delta

\varepsilon_{{\rm F}e}
are mass and Fermi energy for electrons, and

is the energy scale for the superconducting gap function. Here we have assumed the magnitude relation
\varepsilon_{{\rm F}e}>\Delta

The Fermi wavenumber is given by
k_{{\rm F}b}=\sqrt{2m_{b}\varepsilon_{{\rm F}b}}

Thus the Fermi velocity for bogolons is similar to that of electrons:
v_{{\rm F}b}=\frac{k_{{\rm F}b}}{m_{b}}\sim v_{{\rm F}e}

Note that the potential term proportional to
\alpha^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\alpha^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}}

Such pair potential may be generated with spontaneous symmetry breaking below the ordering temperature

for bogolons as shown in Fig.

For the superconducting state considered here, the time-reversal and gauge symmetries are already broken.
Nevertheless, in terms of bogolons as in Eq. (
1

This paradox at first sight is rationalized by considering the self-energy terms in which the symmetry of the system is reasonably reflected.
Then, we arrive at the important conclusion that the anomalous part can be finite in the presence of correlations, and its symmetry must be even-parity pairing.
Since the bogolons have no internal degrees of freedom at the Fermi level, and in order to satisfy the Pauli principle, the pairing state of bogolons must have odd relative time dependence.
Hence the odd-frequency pairing is reasonably realized.

We proceed along with the conventional microscopic approach for the Fermi liquid based on the weak-coupling limit

The total Hamiltonian is written as

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}\Big[\rho_{\bm{q}}u_{1}(\bm{k},\bm{q})\alpha^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\rho_{\bm{q}}u_{2}(\bm{k},\bm{q})\alpha^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\alpha^{\dagger}_{-\bm{k}}+{\rm H.c.}\Big],

The second term with

We also consider the correlation effects in

Among the various interactions, we take the simple model where the bogolons are coupled with bosons.
The interaction term is given by the replacement

\displaystyle u_{j}(\bm{k},\bm{q})\rho_{\bm{q}}\to\mathrm{i}g_{j}(\bm{k},\bm{q})\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{0,\bm{q}}}{2}}(b_{\bm{q}}-b_{-\bm{q}}^{\dagger})

Note that, in the above model, we have assumed that the Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces are stable as separated from the other bands located at higher energies.
Then, the disorders and interactions dominantly affect the bogolons located near the Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces.
Otherwise, the original assumption of the stable Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces must be reconsidered.

In order to show the presence of the pure odd-frequency pairing,
we use the simple weak-coupling perturbation theory, which is a minimal description for the Fermi liquid and is sufficient for generating anomalous Green functions.
The self-energy is contributed by the diagrams shown in Fig.
2

The resultant normal part of the self-energy is given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{1\bm{k}}{\rm sgn\,}\varepsilon_{n}+a_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{i}b_{\bm{k}}(\pi^{2}T^{2}-\varepsilon_{n}^{2}){\rm sgn\,}\varepsilon_{n},

(4)

where
\varepsilon_{n}=(2n+1)\pi T

The first term corresponds to a quasiparticle damping due to impurity scattering.
The second term represents a renormalization factor, and the third term is responsible for the damping in the usual Fermi liquid theory.
For bogolons, the anomalous self-energy is also present, whose diagrammatic contribution is very similar to the normal self-energy as shown in Fig.
2

The anomalous part is obtained as

\displaystyle S^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2\bm{k}}{\rm sgn\,}\varepsilon_{n}+c_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{i}d_{\bm{k}}(\pi^{2}T^{2}-\varepsilon_{n}^{2}){\rm sgn\,}\varepsilon_{n},

(5)

which is the same frequency dependence as the usual Fermi liquid.
Namely, the Fermi-liquid self-energy is originally odd in frequency and therefore it matches well with the requirement of the bogolon anomalous self-energy under the inversion symmetry.
This is the reason why the odd-frequency pair potential naturally appears for bogolons.
We emphasize that the
spatially uniform and purely odd-frequency pairing
is realized in the present setup, which is necessarily accompanied by the normal self-energies.

From the Hermiticity relation, it can be shown that

\Gamma_{1}
are real, while

As discussed in Ref.

In this case, the anomalous part

Hence the broken gauge symmetry and time-reversal symmetry are clearly reflected in the anomalous self-energy of bogolons which is odd in frequency.

The effect of the presence of the odd-frequency pairing is best visualized in the single-particle spectral functions.

Thus, the Bogoliubov Fermi liquid shows characteristic single-particle excitations at low energies, which can be much different from the usual electron liquids.
The above two different behaviors, the first- and second-kind solutions, are observed depending on the choice of parameters

While the observed behaviors are similar to the Fermi liquid, which implies weak disorder effects, the tuning of the system may make it clearer to detect the fingerprints of Bogoliubov-Fermi liquids.

To summarize, we have demonstrated that the non-ideal bogolons generate purely odd-frequency pair potential and pair amplitude near the Fermi surface, as different from the usual Fermi liquid of electrons.
The odd-frequency pair recovers the original symmetry of the electron system in terms of bogolons, where the broken gauge and time-reversal symmetries are not reflected in the level of the non-interacting Hamiltonian of bogolon.
The effect of the odd-frequency pairing is clearly seen in the single-particle spectral functions at low energies where the disorder effect is dominantly present.
The system with Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces is a suitable playground for studying the properties of odd-frequency pairing.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q}}\rho_{\bm{q}}u_{1}(\bm{k},\bm{q})\alpha^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle\rho_{\bm{q}}=\sum_{i}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\bm{R}_{i}},

(S2)

The connection to the original electrons is shown in SM C.
We have the relations

\displaystyle u_{1}(\bm{k},\bm{q})=u_{1}^{*}(\bm{k}+\bm{q},-\bm{q}),

(S4)

\displaystyle u_{2}(\bm{k},\bm{q})=-u_{2}(-\bm{k}-\bm{q},\bm{q}),

1. Born approximation

We consider the lowest-order contribution of the impurity potential.
Second order self-energies (Born approximation) are given as follows:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{1,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})+2\Sigma_{2,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}),

(S10)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=n_{\mathrm{imp}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}|u_{1}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})|^{2}G^{0}_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}),

(S11)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-2n_{\mathrm{imp}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}|u_{2}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})|^{2}G^{0}_{\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}),

(S12)

\displaystyle S^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-2n_{\mathrm{imp}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}u_{1}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})u_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})

\displaystyle\quad\times[G^{0}_{\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})-G^{0}_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})],

(S13)

which corresponds to the diagrams shown in Fig.
2
of the main text.
For the evaluation of the wavevector integral,
we replace the coupling constant by its averaged value with respect to
\bm{q}
.
Then we only have to evaluate the integration as follows:

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}G^{0}_{\bm{q}}(\pm\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\pm\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}-\xi_{\bm{q}}}

\displaystyle=\mp\mathrm{i}\pi D_{0}\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n},

(S14)

where
D_{0}
is a density of states of bogolon at Fermi level.
Then,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\pi n_{\mathrm{imp}}D_{0}\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n}\langle|u_{1}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})|^{2}\rangle_{\bm{q}},

(S15)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{i}\pi n_{\mathrm{imp}}D_{0}\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n}\langle|u_{2}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})|^{2}\rangle_{\bm{q}},

(S16)

\displaystyle S_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-4\mathrm{i}\pi n_{\mathrm{imp}}D_{0}\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n}\langle u_{1}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})u_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})\rangle_{\bm{q}},

(S17)

where
\langle\cdots\rangle_{\bm{q}}=\int\mathrm{d}\bm{q}\cdots/\int\mathrm{d}\bm{q}1
.
Hence, we obtain the self-energies

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{1,\bm{k}}\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n},

(S18)

\displaystyle S_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2,\bm{k}}\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n},

(S19)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1,\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\pi n_{\mathrm{imp}}D_{0}\big(\langle|u_{1}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})|^{2}\rangle_{\bm{q}}+4\langle|u_{2}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})|^{2}\rangle_{\bm{q}}\big),

(S20)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2,\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=4\pi n_{\mathrm{imp}}D_{0}\langle u_{1}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})u_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})\rangle_{\bm{q}}.

(S21)

One sees that the usual Born approximation result is reproduced if
u_{2}
, which is specific to bogolons, is set to zero.

As pointed out in the main text, the spectral function shows unphysical behavior for
|\Gamma_{2,\bm{k}}|>\Gamma_{1,\bm{k}}
.
However, we can show that the condition
|\Gamma_{2,\bm{k}}|<\Gamma_{1,\bm{k}}
is satisfied based on the expressions obtained above together with the magnitude relation between arithmetic and geometric means.

2. Self-consistent treatment

Figure S1:

Feynman diagrams relevant to the self-consistent Born approximation.

We also consider the self-energy with self-consistent treatment.
Namely, we consider the diagrams for the self-energy shown in Fig.
S1
.
The self-consistent equations are derived as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=n_{\mathrm{imp}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Big[|u_{1}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})|^{2}G_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle-4|u_{2}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})|^{2}G_{\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle-2u_{1}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})u_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})F_{\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle-2u_{1}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})u_{2}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})F^{\dagger}_{\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})\Big],

(S22)

\displaystyle S^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=n_{\mathrm{imp}}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Big[2u_{1}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})u_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})

\displaystyle\times\big(G_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})-G_{\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})\big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}u_{1}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})u_{1}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})\big(F^{\dagger}_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})-F^{\dagger}_{\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})\big)

\displaystyle+2u_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})u_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{q},\bm{k}-\bm{q})\big(F_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})-F_{\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})\big)\Big].

Obviously, these equations reduce to those in the Born approximation in the last subsection if one drops the self-energies in the right-hand side.
In order to search for the concrete solutions, we simplify the equation by replacing the coefficients by the wavevector independent ones, and then the self-energies are also
\bm{k}
-independent.
We have considered all the parameter space within this approximation, and always have found physical solutions.
Defining the energy-dependent functions
\Gamma_{1,2}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})
by
\Sigma(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{1}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})
and
S^{\dagger}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})
, we obtain the equations

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi n_{\mathrm{imp}}D_{0}}{\sqrt{-(\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{Re}\Gamma_{1}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}))^{2}+|\Gamma_{2}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})|^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\big(|u_{1}|^{2}+4|u_{2}|^{2}\big)(\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{Re}\Gamma_{1}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}))

\displaystyle-2u_{1}u_{2}^{\ast}\Gamma_{2}^{\ast}(i\varepsilon_{n})-2u_{1}^{\ast}u_{2}\Gamma_{2}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})\Big]

(S24)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi n_{\mathrm{imp}}D_{0}}{\sqrt{-(\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{Re}\Gamma_{1}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}))^{2}+|\Gamma_{2}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})|^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\Big[4u_{1}^{\ast}u_{2}^{\ast}(\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{Re}\Gamma_{1}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}))

\displaystyle-u_{1}^{\ast}u_{1}^{\ast}\Gamma_{2}(i\varepsilon_{n})-4u_{2}^{\ast}u_{2}^{\ast}\Gamma_{2}^{\ast}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})\Big].

(S25)

Performing a suitable transformation for the dimensionless expressions of the equations, we find that the equations are controlled by the complex parameter
u_{1}/|u_{2}|
.
We note that the solution is identical to the Born approximation discussed in the last subsection at high frequencies.

3. Single-particle spectral functions

From the normal and anomalous Green functions, we obtain several physical quantities of interest.
The first one is the single-particle spectral function and density of states defined by

\displaystyle A_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathrm{Im}\,G_{\bm{k}11}(\varepsilon+\mathrm{i}\eta),

(S31)

\displaystyle D(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}A_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon)\simeq D_{0}\int\mathrm{d}\xi_{\bm{k}}A_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon).

(S32)

The momentum distribution function is given by

\displaystyle n_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}\alpha_{\bm{k}}\rangle.

(S33)

In addition, the time-dependent pair amplitude can also be calculated.
Whereas the static quantity such as
\langle\alpha_{\bm{k}}\alpha_{-\bm{k}}\rangle
is zero due to the inversion symmetry, the pair amplitude is finite at different times.
Specifically we consider the spatially local quantity

\displaystyle F(\tau)

\displaystyle=T\sum_{n,\bm{k}}F_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}\tau}.

(S34)

The above quantities are graphically shown in the main text.

The real time dynamics is also obtained by performing the analytic continuation.
The retarded Green function is given by

\displaystyle G^{\rm R}(t)=-\mathrm{i}\theta(t)\langle\{\alpha_{\bm{k}}(t),\alpha_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}\}\rangle,

(S35)

where
\theta(t)
is the step function.
Here we have considered the real-time Heisenberg picture
A(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t\mathscr{H}}A\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t\mathscr{H}}
.
In the following, we summarize the above physical quantities for the solutions of both the first and second kinds.

(i) Solution of the first kind

We consider the self-energies

\displaystyle\Sigma(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{1}\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n},\ \ S^{\dagger}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=-\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n},

(S36)

with
\Gamma_{1}>|\Gamma_{2}|
. The density of states is

\displaystyle\frac{D(\varepsilon)}{D_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\varepsilon|+\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{1}}{2\sqrt{(|\varepsilon|+\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{1})^{2}+|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}}}+{\rm c.c.}

(S37)

The momentum distribution function is

\displaystyle n_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{2\pi\sqrt{|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}-\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}}}\ln\left(\frac{\Gamma_{1}-\sqrt{|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}-\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}}}{\Gamma_{1}+\sqrt{|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}-\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}

(S38)

at zero temperature.
The spatially local pair amplitude at
T=0
is

\displaystyle\frac{F^{\dagger}(\tau)}{\Gamma_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\varepsilon\left[\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\sqrt{(|\varepsilon|+\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{1})^{2}+|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}}}+{\rm c.c.}\right]\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon\tau}

(S39)

for
\tau>0
, which is numerically integrated.

The real time dependence of the retarded Green function (
t>0
) is given by

\displaystyle G^{\rm R}_{\bm{k}}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\left(1+\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}}\,t-\Gamma_{1}t}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}-|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}}\,t-\Gamma_{1}t}

(S40)

for
|\xi_{\bm{k}}|>|\Gamma_{2}|
and

\displaystyle G^{\rm R}_{\bm{k}}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{i}+\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}-\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-(\Gamma_{1}-\sqrt{|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}-\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}})t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathrm{i}-\frac{\xi_{\bm{k}}}{\sqrt{|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}-\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-(\Gamma_{1}+\sqrt{|\Gamma_{2}|^{2}-\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}})t}

(S41)

for
|\xi_{\bm{k}}|<|\Gamma_{2}|
.

(ii) Solution of the second kind

We summarize the results for the self-energies

\displaystyle\Sigma(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=|V|^{2}/\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n},\ \ S^{\dagger}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=V^{2}/\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}.

(S42)

The density of states is

\displaystyle\frac{D(\varepsilon)}{D_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}|V|\delta(\varepsilon)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}-|V|^{2}}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-2|V|^{2}}}\theta(\varepsilon-\sqrt{2}|V|).

(S43)

The momentum distribution function is

\displaystyle n_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{\bm{k}}-\xi_{\bm{k}}[1-2f(E_{\bm{k}})]}{2E_{\bm{k}}},

(S44)

where
E_{\bm{k}}=\sqrt{\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}+2|V|^{2}}
and
f(x)=1/(\mathrm{e}^{x/T}+1)
.
The spatially local pair amplitude is

\displaystyle\frac{F^{\dagger}(\tau)}{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi V}{2\sqrt{2}|V|}-V\int_{\sqrt{2}|V|}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\varepsilon\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon\tau}}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}-2|V|^{2}}}

(S45)

for
\tau>0
,
which is numerically integrated.
The real time dependence of the Green function is given by

\displaystyle G^{\rm R}_{\bm{k}}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2E_{\bm{k}}^{2}}\Big[2|V|^{2}+(\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}+|V|^{2}+\xi_{\bm{k}}E_{\bm{k}})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}E_{\bm{k}}t}

\displaystyle+(\xi_{\bm{k}}^{2}+|V|^{2}-\xi_{\bm{k}}E_{\bm{k}})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}E_{\bm{k}}t}\Big]

(S46)

for
t>0
.

1. Boson self-energy

We first derive the boson self-energy.
The Green function of boson is defined by

\displaystyle D_{\bm{q}}(\tau)=-\langle\mathcal{T}\phi_{\bm{q}}(\tau)\phi_{-\bm{q}}\rangle,

(S47)

\displaystyle\phi_{\bm{q}}=\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{0,\bm{q}}}{2}}(b_{\bm{q}}-b_{-\bm{q}}^{\dagger}).

(S48)

Fourier transformation is given by

\displaystyle D_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1/T}\mathrm{d}\tau D_{\bm{q}}(\tau)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}\tau},

(S49)

where
\omega_{n}=2n\pi T
is the bosonic Matsubara frequency.
The self-energy
\Pi_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})^{-1}

\displaystyle=D^{0}_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})^{-1}-\Pi_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}),

(S50)

with the zeroth-order Green function
D^{0}_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}/[(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})^{2}-\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}]
.
The diagrammatic contributions are illustrated in Fig.
S3
(a), and are then given as follows:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\Pi_{1,\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})+2\Pi_{2,\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}),

(S51)

\displaystyle\Pi_{1,\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=\frac{T}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{m}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{k}\,|g_{1}(\bm{k},-\bm{q})|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times G^{0}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m})G^{0}_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m}-\mathrm{i}\omega_{n}),

(S52)

\displaystyle\Pi_{2,\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})=-\frac{T}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{m}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{k}|g_{2}(\bm{k},-\bm{q})|^{2}G^{0}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m})

\displaystyle\quad\times[G^{0}_{-\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m}+\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})+G^{0}_{-\bm{k}+\bm{q}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m}-\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})],

(S53)

where
G^{0}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})
is a free particle Green function of bogolon.
First, we find an explicit form of
\Pi_{1,\bm{q}}(\mathrm{i}\omega_{n})
.
For the concrete calculation, we introduce the wavevector-averaged coupling constant
\overline{\Gamma}_{1,\bm{q}}\equiv\langle|g_{1}(\bm{k},-\bm{q})|^{2}\rangle_{\bm{k}}
.
Then we obtain the real part of retarded self-energy at low
\omega
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{1,\bm{q}}(\omega)=-\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{1,\bm{q}}m_{b}k_{\mathrm{F}b}}{(2\pi)^{2}}h_{1}\left(\frac{q}{2k_{\mathrm{F}b}}\right),

(S54)

where

\displaystyle h_{1}(x)\equiv 1+\frac{1-x^{2}}{2x}\ln\left|\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right|

(S55)

is the Lindhard function shown in Fig.
S3
(b).
We also get the imaginary part as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{1,\bm{q}}(\omega)=-\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{1,\bm{q}}m_{b}^{2}}{2\pi q}\omega\theta(2k_{\mathrm{F}b}-q).

(S56)

These are the same form as those for the usual electrons.
On the other hand, in order to calculate
\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{2,\bm{q}}(\omega)
, we introduce the wavevector-averaged coupling constant
\overline{\Gamma}_{2,\bm{q}}\equiv\langle|g_{2}(\bm{k},-\bm{q})|^{2}\rangle_{\bm{k}}
. The result of the calculation of
\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{2,\bm{q}}(\omega)
at low
\omega
is as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{2,\bm{q}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{2,\bm{q}}m_{b}k_{\mathrm{F}b}}{(2\pi)^{2}}h_{2,T}\left(\frac{q}{2k_{\mathrm{F}b}}\right),

(S57)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\Pi^{\mathrm{R}}_{2,\bm{q}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{2,\bm{q}}m_{b}^{2}}{2\pi q}\omega\theta\big((1+\sqrt{3})k_{\mathrm{F}b}-q\big),

(S58)

where

\displaystyle h_{2,T}(x)\equiv

\displaystyle\int_{\sqrt{1-\omega_{\mathrm{C}}/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}b}}}^{\sqrt{1+\omega_{\mathrm{C}}/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}b}}}\hskip-5.69054pt\mathrm{d}y\frac{y}{x}\tanh\left(\frac{y^{2}-1}{2T/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{F}b}}\right)\ln\left|\frac{y^{2}-1+2x^{2}+2xy}{y^{2}-1+2x^{2}-2xy}\right|,

(S59)

where the energy integration is performed within the cutoff frequency of bosons
\omega_{\mathrm{C}}
.
We have assumed that the dominant contributions enter at small
q
.
Note that
h_{2,T}(q/2k_{\mathrm{F}b})
diverges logarithmically in the infrared regime at zero temperature limit.
This corresponds to the Cooper instability intrinsic to Fermi surfaces.
However, this divergence is suppressed at finite
T
as shown in Fig.
S3
(b), in which we are interested.

For later discussion, we derive the explicit form of boson Green function. The Dyson equation is given as follows:

\displaystyle[D^{\mathrm{R}}_{\bm{q}}(\omega)]^{-1}=\frac{1}{\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}}\left(\omega^{2}-\omega_{\bm{q}}^{2}-2\mathrm{i}\omega\gamma_{\bm{q}}\right),

(S60)

where

\displaystyle\omega_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{0,\bm{q}}\sqrt{1-\eta_{\bm{q}}},

(S61)

\displaystyle\eta_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{1,\bm{q}}m_{b}k_{\mathrm{F}b}}{(2\pi)^{2}}h_{1}\left(\frac{q}{2k_{\mathrm{F}b}}\right)+2\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{2,\bm{q}}m_{b}k_{\mathrm{F}b}}{(2\pi)^{2}}h_{2,T}\left(\frac{q}{2k_{\mathrm{F}b}}\right),

(S62)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{1,\bm{q}}m_{b}^{2}\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}}{4\pi q}\theta(2k_{\mathrm{F}b}-q)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{2,\bm{q}}m_{b}^{2}\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}}{2\pi q}\theta\big((1+\sqrt{3})k_{\mathrm{F}b}-q\big).

(S63)

Therefore, we get the explicit form of the Green function as

\displaystyle D_{\bm{q}}^{\mathrm{R}}(\omega)=\frac{\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}}{2\omega_{\bm{q}}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega-\omega_{\bm{q}}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{\bm{q}}}-\frac{1}{\omega+\omega_{\bm{q}}+\mathrm{i}\gamma_{\bm{q}}}\right).

(S64)

which will be used to obtain the bogolon self-energies.

2. Bogolon normal self-energy

Next, we derive the bogolon normal self-energy given in Eq. (
S9
). The diagram of the second-order self-energy is shown in Fig.
2
(b) of the main text.
The corresponding self-energy
\Sigma_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{1,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})+2\Sigma_{2,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}),

(S65)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=-\frac{T}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{m}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{k}^{\prime}\,|g_{1}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times G^{0}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m})D_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m}),

(S66)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{2T}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{m}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{k}^{\prime}\,|g_{2}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\times G^{0}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m})D_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m}).

(S67)

We calculate
\Sigma_{1,\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})
at first.
For the evaluation of
\Sigma_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})
, we replace the Matsubara sums by the energy integral.
By using the spectral representation for the non-interacting Green function

\displaystyle G^{0,\mathrm{R}}(\bm{k},\varepsilon)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\varepsilon^{\prime}\frac{A_{\bm{k}}^{0}(\varepsilon^{\prime})}{\varepsilon+\mathrm{i}\eta-\varepsilon^{\prime}},

(S68)

the retarded self-energy has the form

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\mathrm{R}}_{1,\bm{k}}(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{k}^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\varepsilon^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\quad\times|g_{1}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}\frac{A_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}^{0}(\varepsilon^{\prime})\mathrm{Im}D^{\mathrm{R}}_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\omega)}{\omega+\varepsilon^{\prime}-\varepsilon-\mathrm{i}\eta}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2T}\right)+\coth\left(\frac{\omega}{2T}\right)\right].

(S69)

To evaluate the integral, we again introduce the wavevector-averaged coupling constant
\overline{\Gamma}_{3,\bm{k}}\equiv\langle|g_{1}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}\rangle_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Using the variable transformation
\bm{k}^{\prime}\to(q,\xi_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})
with
\bm{q}=\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\mathrm{R}}_{1,\bm{k}}(\varepsilon)=\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{3,\bm{k}}m_{b}}{(2\pi)^{3}k}\int_{0}^{k_{1}}\mathrm{d}q\,q\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega\int_{\xi_{|\bm{k}|-q}}^{\xi_{|\bm{k}|+q}}\mathrm{d}\varepsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\mathrm{Im}D^{\mathrm{R}}_{\bm{q}}(\omega)}{\omega+\varepsilon^{\prime}-\varepsilon-\mathrm{i}\eta}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2T}\right)+\coth\left(\frac{\omega}{2T}\right)\right],

(S70)

where
k_{1}=\mathrm{min}\{q_{\mathrm{C}},2k_{\mathrm{F}b}\}
, and
q_{\mathrm{C}}
is a wavevector cutoff of bosons.
We can approximate
\xi_{|\bm{k}|-q}
and
\xi_{|\bm{k}|+q}
by
-\infty
and
+\infty
, respectively
AGD_book
.
This procedure is checked for
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Fb}}/\omega_{\rm C}\gg 1
by comparing the result with the numerical calculation without using the extrapolation, regardless of the cutoff wavevector
k_{1}
.
Moreover, we use the relation
\mathrm{Re}D^{\mathrm{R}}_{\bm{q}}(\omega)=\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}/(\omega^{2}-\omega_{\bm{q}}^{2})
.
Then, the real part of
\Sigma_{1,\bm{k}}^{\mathrm{R}}(\varepsilon)
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\Sigma^{\mathrm{R}}_{1,\bm{k}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{3,\bm{k}}\zeta}{\omega^{*}}\varepsilon

(S71)

for
\varepsilon\ll\omega_{\mathrm{C}}
, where we have introduced the quantities

\displaystyle\omega^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k_{1}}\mathrm{d}q\,q\frac{\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}}{\omega_{\bm{q}}^{2}}}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k_{1}}\mathrm{d}q\,q\frac{\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}\gamma_{\bm{q}}}{\omega_{\bm{q}}^{4}}},

(S72)

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta}{\omega^{*}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{b}}{4\pi^{2}k_{\mathrm{F}b}}\int_{0}^{k_{1}}\mathrm{d}q\,q\frac{\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}}{\omega_{\bm{q}}^{2}}

(S73)

to make the notations simple.
With
\mathrm{Im}D^{\mathrm{R}}_{\bm{q}}(\omega)\simeq-2\omega_{0,\bm{q}}^{2}\gamma_{\bm{q}}\omega/\omega_{\bm{q}}^{4}
for
\omega\ll\gamma_{\bm{q}}
, the imaginary part of
\Sigma_{1,\bm{k}}^{\mathrm{R}}(\varepsilon)
is obtained as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\Sigma^{\mathrm{R}}_{1,\bm{k}}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\overline{\Gamma}_{3,\bm{k}}\zeta}{\omega^{*2}}(\pi^{2}T^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}).

(S74)

The other contribution can also be evaluated with a similar procedure.
We list the result below:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\mathrm{R}}_{2,\bm{k}}(\varepsilon)=-\frac{2\overline{\Gamma}_{4,\bm{k}}\zeta}{\omega^{*}}\varepsilon-\mathrm{i}\frac{2\overline{\Gamma}_{4,\bm{k}}\zeta}{\omega^{*2}}(\pi^{2}T^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}),

(S75)

where
\overline{\Gamma}_{4,\bm{k}}=\langle|g_{2}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}\rangle_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}
.

3. Bogolon anomalous self-energy

In this subsection, we derive the bogolon anomalous self-energy.
The diagram of the second-order self-energy is shown in Fig.
2
(c) of the main text. With inversion symmetry, the corresponding self-energy
S_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})
is given as follows:

\displaystyle S^{\dagger}_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})=-\frac{2T}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{m}\int\mathrm{d}\bm{k}^{\prime}\,g_{1}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})g_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\times G^{0}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m})[D_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}(-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m})-D_{\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{m})].

(S76)

The explicit form of the retarded self-energy
S_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger\mathrm{R}}(\varepsilon)
can be derived by a similar manner to the previous subsection.
The result is written as

\displaystyle S_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger\mathrm{R}}(\varepsilon)=-\frac{4\overline{\Gamma}_{5,\bm{k}}\zeta}{\omega^{*}}\varepsilon-\mathrm{i}\frac{4\overline{\Gamma}_{5,\bm{k}}\zeta}{\omega^{*2}}(\pi^{2}T^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}),

(S77)

where
\overline{\Gamma}_{5,\bm{k}}=\langle g_{1}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})g_{2}^{\ast}(\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})\rangle_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}
.

4. Analytic continuation

For the characterization of the frequency-dependent functional forms, it is suitable to see the physical quantities on the imaginary (or Matsubara) axis.
The results on the retarded bogolon self-energies obtained in the previous subsections can be analytically continued to the imaginary axis as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{k}}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=a_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{i}b_{\bm{k}}\left[\pi^{2}T^{2}+(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})^{2}\right]\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n},

(S78)

\displaystyle S_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})

\displaystyle=c_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}+\mathrm{i}d_{\bm{k}}\left[\pi^{2}T^{2}+(\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n})^{2}\right]\mathrm{sgn}\,\varepsilon_{n},

(S79)

where

\displaystyle a_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=-(\overline{\Gamma}_{3,\bm{k}}+4\overline{\Gamma}_{4,\bm{k}})\frac{\zeta}{\omega^{*}},

(S80)

\displaystyle b_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{\bm{k}}}{\omega^{\ast}},

(S81)

\displaystyle c_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=-4\overline{\Gamma}_{5,\bm{k}}\frac{\zeta}{\omega^{*}},

(S82)

\displaystyle d_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{\bm{k}}}{\omega^{\ast}},

(S83)

from which we can construct both the retarded and advanced Green functions.
Note that
a_{\bm{k}}
and
b_{\bm{k}}
are real, while
c_{\bm{k}}
and
d_{\bm{k}}
are complex.

The electron-electron interaction is also expected to lead to a similar effect discussed above.
The imaginary part should be calculated in a manner similar to Ref.
AGD_book
,
and then the real part may be reconstructed through the Kramers-Kronig relation
Miyake
;
Jacko
.

Homotopy type of stabilizers of circle-valued functions with non-isolated singularities on surfaces
Bohdan Feshchenko

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to the study of homotopy properties of stabilizers of smooth functions on surfaces, i.e., groups of diffeomorphisms of surfaces preserving a given function. For some class of smooth functions which is a generalization of the class of Morse-Bott functions on surfaces the homotopy type of a connected component of the identity map of the stabilizer are completely described.



1.
Introduction

The stabilizers and orbits of smooth functions on compact surfaces are special spaces of smooth maps which arise from the action of the group of diffeomorphisms on the space of smooth functions. These spaces naturally appear in problems of deformations of smooth functions on surfaces. A systematic study of their homotopy properties has been started by S. Maksymenko
[
9
]
.
We will give precise definitions of stabilizers and orbits below.

Let

be a smooth compact surface and
P
be either
\mathbb{R}
or

of

\xi:C^{\infty}(M,P)\times\mathcal{D}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M,P),\qquad\xi(f,h)=f\circ h.

For a smooth function

we denote by

\mathcal{S}(f)=\{h\in\mathcal{D}(M)\,|f\circ h=f\},\quad\mathcal{O}(f)=\{f\circ h\,|\,h\in\mathcal{D}(M)\}

the stabilizer and the orbit of

with respect to the action

. Endow strong Whitney topologies on
\mathcal{D}(M)
and

\mathcal{S}(f)
and
\mathcal{O}(f)

, and by

a connected component of

For circle-valued functions with isolated singularities on compact surfaces the homotopy type of

[

is contractible if

has at least one saddle point, otherwise

is homotopy equivalent to

.
For a generic function

is homotopy equivalent to

An algebraic structure of homotopy groups of orbits and other groups which “partially” controls the homotopy type of orbits is well understood for all compact oriented surfaces except

and less understood for some non-oriented surfaces, e.g., for functions on Klein bottle and projective plane. More details on these results can be found in
[
13
]
.
We will also note that recently homotopy properties of orbits were applied to some questions on persistent homology of Morse functions by J. Leygonie and D. Beers
[
7
]
.

Our goal is to generalize results on homotopy type of stabilizers and orbits to more general class
of circle-valued functions on surfaces whose critical points can be non-isolated. The natural class of such functions to consider are Morse-Bott functions.

Morse-Bott functions are the generalization of Morse functions by significantly weakening the conditions on the set of critical points – a critical set of a Morse-Bott function is a union of submanifolds and each connected component of such union is “non-degenerate in the normal direction”. General information about them in the context of Morse theory can be found
[
17
]
.
Such functions are not too rigid as Morse functions and they can “catch” some additional information on symmetries that manifolds may have, which is especially important when the dimension of a manifold is high.
Morse-Bott functions are very popular object to research and were studied by many specialists in a variety of problems in topology and its applications.
In particular
topological classification of Morse-Bott functions on oriented surfaces were studied by E. B. Batista et al.
[
2
]
and by J. Martínez-Alfaro et al.
[
16
]
. I. Gelbukh classified Morse-Bott functions with the only
2

[
4
]
. Topological properties foliations with Morse-Bott singularities of codimension-

were investigated by B. Scárdua and J. Seade

[

, and homotopy properties of diffeomorphisms preserving Morse-Bott foliations on lens spaces were studied by S. Maksymenko
[

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Class
\mathcal{F}(M,P)

Let
M
be a smooth compact surface and
f:M\to P
be a smooth function from
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
.
By definition of the class
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
the set of critical points
\Sigma_{f}
of
f
is a disjoint union of submanifolds of
M
. Since
M
is compact it follows that the cardinality of
\Sigma_{f}
is finite and each connected component of
\Sigma_{f}
is either a critical point or a critical circle.

Let
z
be a connected component of
\Sigma_{f}
being a critical point of
f
. By (3.a) of Definition
1.1
near
z
a function
f
can be represented by a homogeneous polynomial
f_{z}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
without multiple factors of
\deg\geq 2
. It is known that
0\in\mathbb{R}
is the only critical point of
f_{z}
, therefore a point
z
is an isolated critical point of
f
. The set of isolated critical points of
f
will be denoted by
\Sigma_{f}^{0}.
Due to result by P. T. Church and J. G. Timourian
[
3
]
and A. Prishlyak
[
18
]
the topological structure of level-sets of any smooth function on surface near an isolated critical point
can be realized by level-sets of a homogeneous polynomial without multiple factors.
So the class
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
contains functions with topologically generic isolated singularities. Also note that a point
z
can be a local extreme (local minimum or maximum) of
f
, and if
z
is not a local extreme then
z
is called a (generalized) saddle.

Denote by
\Sigma_{f}^{1}
a set of critical circles of
f
, i.e.,
\Sigma_{f}^{1}=\Sigma_{f}-\Sigma_{f}^{0}
.
Let
C\in\Sigma_{f}^{1}
be a critical circle of
f
and
p
be a point in
C
. By (3.b) of Definition
1.1
near
p
a function
f
can be represented as the polynomial
f_{C}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f_{C}(x,y)=\pm y^{n_{C}}
for some
n_{C}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}.
Note that the number
n_{C}
can be odd or even, and a point
p\in C
is a local extreme of
f
if and only if
n_{C}
is even.
A normal bundle
\nu C\cong\bigcup_{p\in C}T_{p}M/T_{p}C
over
C
can be oriented or non-oriented. Therefore
\nu C
is diffeomorphic to either a cylinder or a Möbius band. Due to Morse-Bott lemma (see
[
1
, Theorem 1]
), a Morse-Bott function
g
near each critical point
p
of a critical circle
C
has the form
g_{z}(x,y)=\pm y^{2}
. So the behavior of functions from
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
near critical circles is more general than for Morse-Bott functions.

Summing up the above a class
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
generalizes the class of Morse-Bott functions on
M
as well as the class of smooth functions with isolated singularities.
So the class
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
is “large” and consists of functions that have topologically generic singularities.

A leaf

Let

2.3.
Hamiltonian vector fields

Let
M
be a smooth and
oriented
surface.
It is well-known that
M
admits a symplectic form
\omega:TM\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e., a non-degenerate skew-symmetric
2

\omega
is non-degenerate follows that the map
\omega^{\flat}:TM\to T^{*}M
given by
\omega^{\flat}_{p}(X)=\omega_{p}(X,\cdot)
for each

is a bundle isomorphism. Let
f
be a smooth
P

M
and
df:TM\to TP
be its differential.
Since

is either
\mathbb{R}
or

it is well-known that

is a globally trivializable bundle; let
\zeta:TP\to P\times\mathbb{R}
be its trivialization isomorphism. Therefore a differential

induces a section
Df:TM\to\mathbb{R}
of
T^{*}M
defined as the composition

\textstyle{Df:TM\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{df}
\textstyle{TP\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\zeta}
\textstyle{P\times\mathbb{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R},}

where
p_{2}
is the projection.
A vector field
X_{f}=(\omega^{\flat})^{-1}(Df)
on
M
is called
a Hamiltonian vector field of

. It is know that
X_{f}
satisfies the following conditions:

•

X_{f}
is tangent to
\partial M
,

•

singular points of
X_{f}
correspond to critical points of
f
,

•

is constant along trajectories of
X_{f}
, i.e.,
X_{f}(f)=0.
In other words,
X_{f}
is tangent to level-sets of

.

Note that for a function
f
from
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
each point in a critical circle

is a singular point of

.

A vector field

on a surface

is said to be an

, if for every singular point

a germ of

is smoothly equivalent to a germ
(X_{g},0)
of a Hamiltonian vector field

for some homogeneous polynomial

without multiple factors of degree
\geq 2
with respect to the standard symplectic structure

on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, i.e.,

has the form
X_{g}(x,y)=-g^{\prime}_{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+g^{\prime}_{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}.

3.
Shifts along orbits of flows

3.1.
Shift-maps

Let
M
be a smooth compact surface,
F
be a vector field on
M

\partial M

\mathbf{F}:M\times\mathbb{R}\to M

\mathrm{Fix}(\mathbf{F})

Consider the map

\phi:C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\to C^{\infty}(M,M),\qquad\phi(\alpha)=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha},

where
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}(x)=\mathbf{F}(x,\alpha(x))
,

This map is called
a shift-map along trajectories of

, or simply, a shift-map. Shift-maps were studied in detail by S. Maksymenko in a series of papers
[
8
,
10

11

12
]
.
We present some definitions and results needed to us in §

Let

be a smooth map not necessary a diffeomorphism. A smooth function
\alpha:U\to\mathbb{R}
is called
a shift-function for

h=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
on

, and if

, then a function

is called

for

Let
\Gamma
be the following set of functions
\{\alpha\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\,|\,F\alpha>-1\}
. Note that the set
\Gamma
is open and convex in

. It is also known that a smooth map

is a diffeomorphism if and only if
\alpha\in\Gamma
.

3.2.
Group
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
and its homotopy type

A flow
\mathbf{F}
of a vector field
F
defines a foliation
\Delta_{\mathbf{F}}
on
M
: a leaf
\gamma
of
\Delta_{\mathbf{F}}
is an integral curve of a vector field
F
. Let
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{F})
be a group of
leaf-preserving
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms
of
M
, i.e.,
h\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
if
h(\gamma)\subset\gamma
for
\gamma\in\Delta_{\mathbf{F}}
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
a connected component of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{F})
containing
\mathrm{id}_{M}.

S. Maksymenko in
[
12
]
computed a homotopy type of the group
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
for a large class of flows of vector fields on manifolds satisfying certain topological conditions on regular and singular points.
For our purpose we restrict attention to some smaller class of vector fields
\mathcal{V}
on
M
for which the result mentioned above is true. And for the sake of completeness we also recall some definitions.

Let
z\in M
be a regular point of
F
, i.e.,
F(z)\neq 0.
The point
z
is called

if there exists a sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of real numbers such that
\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}|t_{i}|=\infty
and
\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\mathbf{F}_{t_{i}}(z)=z.
In fact every periodic orbit is recurrent.

Let
z
be a periodic point of
F
,
\gamma_{z}
be its orbit and
D
be an open arc passing through
z
and transversally intersecting
\gamma_{z}.
Define the germ of a
first return
(
Poincaré
)
map

R:(D,z)\to(D,z)
which maps each point
x\in D
to the first point
R(x)
at which the orbit
\gamma_{x}
first returns to
D
.

Definition 3.3
.

A vector filed
F
on a smooth compact surface
M
belongs to the class
\mathcal{V}
if
F
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

F
is tangent to
\partial M
and
\mathrm{Fix}(\mathbf{F})
is nowhere dense in
M
,

(b)

every non-periodic regular point of
F
is not recurrent,

(c)

for every periodic point
z
of
F
the germ at
z
of its first return map
R:(D,z)\to(D,z)
is periodic,

(d)

F
is an
H
-type.

The following lemma describes the homotopy type of the group
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
for vector fields from
\mathcal{V}.

Lemma 3.4

(Theorem 3.5,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
F
be a vector field from
\mathcal{V}
,
\mathbf{F}
be its flow, and
\phi|_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\to\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
be a shift-map given by
\phi(\alpha)=\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
.

(1)

If
\mathbf{F}
has at least one non-closed orbit then
\phi|_{\Gamma}
is a homeomorphism and the group
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
is contractible since
\Gamma
is convex.

(2)

If
\mathbf{F}
has no saddles then there exists a smooth function
\alpha\in\Gamma
such that
\phi|_{\Gamma}
can be represented as the composition

\textstyle{\phi|_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Gamma/\mathcal{A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F}),}

where
\mathcal{A}=\{n\alpha\,|\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a closed discrete subgroup of

, and

is a homeomorphism. In this case

is a

-covering map and

is homotopy equivalent to

Proof of Theorem

Note that the proof of Theorem

reduced to the case of an oriented double cover of

So we left details of the proof in this case to the reader and

Let

Proposition 4.1
.

For a function
f
from
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
the following holds true:

(1)

there exists a vector field
F
on
M
tangent to
\partial M
which satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

a point
z
is a fixed point of
F
if and only if

(ii)

f
is constant along trajectories of
F
, i.e.,
F(f)=0
,

(iii)

is an

corresponds to the type of critical point in a local representation of

(3.a Definition

)
;

(2)

the following equality holds
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
, where
\mathbf{F}
is a flow of
F
,

(3)

F
belongs to
\mathcal{V}.

Indeed,
let
F
be a vector field such in (1) of Proposition

. By (2)
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)
has the same homotopy type as
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
, and by (3)

. Therefore
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)
is contractible (or homotopy equivalent to

) when
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
is contractible (or homotopy equivalent to

).
Moreover a vector field

The proof is completed.

. A vector field

in general satisfies the only property (ii) of (1) of Proposition

. The property (i) is failed for

since

can have a critical circle, and near an isolated critical point of

does not have the behavior such in (iii). A main idea of construction of

near isolated critical points of

using a local representation of

and to replace
X_{f}
near critical circles of
f
to some vector field without singularities tangent to connected components of level-sets of

. We will describe this procedure below.

(A)
Near isolated critical points.
Let
z\in\Sigma_{f}^{0}
be an isolated critical point of
f
.
Then by (3.a) of Definition
1.1
there exist charts
U
near

and
V
near

such that

has a representation by a homogeneous polynomial without multiple factors

By decreasing

we can assume that

is an
f
-adapted neighborhood of

Define a vector field

on

to be a Hamiltonian vector field of

by the formula

(1)

F_{z}(x,y)=-(f_{z})^{\prime}_{y}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+(f_{z})^{\prime}_{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}

A vector field

has the same trajectories as
X_{f}
and
F_{z}(f)=0
on

i.e.,
F_{z}
is tangent to leaves of
f
on

For each regular leaf

and

is an
1

X_{f}
and
F_{z}
are colinear on

, i.e.,
(X_{f})_{p}=\lambda_{p}(F_{z})_{p}
for some non-zero number
\lambda_{p}
smoothly depending on

, but they can be differ on a direction. We can always redefine coordinate system on
U
(by transforming
(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)
) such that the function
\lambda
is positive-defined. In this case
X_{f}
and
F_{z}
are called codirectional.

So near an isolated critical point

we define a vector field
F_{z}
by (
1
) which is codirectiolal with

.

(B)
Near critical circles.
Let
C\in\Sigma_{f}^{1}
be a critical circle of
f
, and

be any point in
C

1.1
there exist charts

near

near

such that

is represented in the form

for some

Define a vector field

on

by the formula

(2)

F_{p}(x,y)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}.

Note that
F_{z}
has the same trajectories as

and

. Let

-adapted neighborhood of

of
f

. Since a normal bundle

So we can extend
F_{p}
defined on

to a vector field

on

.
By the same argument as in (A) we can always redefine coordinate system on

such that
F_{C}
and

So in some
f
-adapted neighborhood

F_{C}
such that
F_{C}
has no singularities on
Q
,
F_{C}
is tangent to leaves of
f
belonging to

, and
F_{C}
is codirectional with

on

Gluing all pieces together.
From now on assume that
f
has
n
isolated critical points and
m
critical circles,
n,m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. By (A) and (B) for each

and

we have

-adapted neighborhoods

of

and

of

, and the corresponding vector fields

defined on

and

respectively,

To obtain a vector field

on

such in (1) we need to use the partition of unity subordinating to some open cover of

.

We put

for

and
W_{n+j}=Q_{j}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,m,
and
will extend the family

to an open cover of

as follows.
Let
Y_{i}
be an
f
-adapted neighborhood of an element of

such that

,

. Since

compact it follows that the number of connected components of

is finite, say

of this complement is diffeomorphic to an open cylinder in

and contains no critical points of

,

Let also
Y_{r}
be an

\overline{Y_{r}}\subset Z_{r}
,

So we extend the family

by putting

Let

be a family of smooth functions on

•

\mu_{s}=1
on

,

•

\mu_{s}=0

•

•

\sum_{s=1}^{n+m+k}\mu_{s}(x)=1

It is easy to see that the family

of

Define a vector field

on

(3)

F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}F_{z_{i}}+\sum_{j=n+1}^{n+m}\mu_{j}F_{C_{j}}+\sum_{s=n+m+1}^{k}\mu_{s}X_{f}|_{W_{s}}.

We claim that

satisfies (i).
By formula (
3
) a vector field

has at least
n

. Since each

and
F_{C_{j}}
are codirectional with

and each
\mu_{s}
is non-negative and supported on

it follows that

By definition

and

,

have no singularities, so the only singularities of

It is easy to see that

Proof of (2)

Let
h\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F})
be a diffeomorphism. So here exists an isotopy
H_{t}:M\to M
,
t\in[0,1]
such that
H_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
,
H_{1}=h
and
H_{t}(\gamma)\subset\gamma
for
\gamma\in\Delta_{\mathbf{F}}
. We have
f(H_{t}(\gamma))=f(\gamma)
and therefore
f(H_{t}(x))=f(x)
for
t\in[0,1]
and
x\in M
. Therefore
H_{t}\in\mathcal{S}(f)
, which means that
h\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f).

From the other hand, let
h\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f).
For
h
there exists an isotopy
H_{t}:M\to M
such that
H_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
,
H_{1}=h
and
H_{t}\in\mathcal{S}(f)
for
t\in[0,1].
Then we have
H_{t}(f^{-1}(c))\subset f^{-1}(c)
for every
c\in P,

t\in[0,1].
We need to show that
H_{t}(\gamma)\subset\gamma
for all
\gamma\in\Delta_{\mathbf{F}}.
By definition of
F
a trajectory
\gamma
can be either non-closed or closed: closed trajectories corresponds to isolated critical points, critical circles and regular leafs of
f
, and non-closed trajectories corresponds to leaves of
f
which are complements to saddle points.

By chain rule we have

df_{p}=d(f\circ H_{t})_{p}=(df)_{H_{t}}\circ(dH_{t})_{p}
. Since
H_{t}
is a diffeomorphism for each
t\in[0,1]
, it follows that

is non-zero.
So if
z
is a regular (critical) point of
f
, then the arc
\{H_{t}(z)\,|\,t\in[0,1]\}
consists of the only regular (critical) points of
f
.
Therefore for each closed trajectories
\gamma\in\Delta_{\mathbf{F}}
we have
H_{t}(\gamma)\subset\gamma.

Moreover if
p
is an isolated critical point of
f
then
H_{t}(z)=z
, and so all singularities of

are fixed with respect to an isotopy
H_{t}
. This yields that for each non-closed trajectory
\gamma
of
F
we have
H_{t}(\gamma)\subset\gamma.
It shows that diffeomorphism
h
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(\mathbf{F}).

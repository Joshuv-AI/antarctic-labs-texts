Number of Equivalence classes of Rational Functions over Finite Fields
Xiang-dong Hou

Abstract
. Two rational functions f , g ∈ 𝔽 q ​ ( X ) f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X) are said to be equivalent if there exist ϕ , ψ ∈ 𝔽 q ​ ( X ) \phi,\psi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X) of degree one such that g = ϕ ∘ f ∘ ψ g=\phi\circ f\circ\psi . We give an explicit formula for the number of equivalence classes of rational functions of a given degree in 𝔽 q ​ ( X ) \mathbb{F}_{q}(X) . This result should provide guidance for the current and future work on classifications of low degree rational functions over finite fields. We also determine the number of equivalence classes of polynomials of a given degree in 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[X] .

1.
Introduction

For a nonconstant rational function
f(X)
over a field
\mathbb{F}
, written in the form
f(X)=P(X)/Q(X)
, where
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}[X]
,
Q\neq 0
, and
\text{gcd}(P,Q)=1
, we define
\deg f=\max\{\deg P,\deg Q\}
. Then
[\mathbb{F}(X):\mathbb{F}(f)]=\deg f
. By Lüroth theorem, every subfield
E\subset\mathbb{F}(X)
with
[\mathbb{F}(X):E]=d
is of the form
\mathbb{F}(f)
for some
f\in\mathbb{F}(X)
with
\deg f=d
. Let

(1.1)

G(\mathbb{F})=\{\phi\in\mathbb{F}(X):\deg\phi=1\}.

The group
(G(\mathbb{F}),\circ)
is isomorphic to the projective linear group
\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F})
and the Galois group
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}(X)/\mathbb{F})
of
\mathbb{F}(X)
over
\mathbb{F}
. For
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\cr c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F})
, its corresponding element in
G(\mathbb{F})
, denoted by
\phi_{A}
, is
\phi_{A}=(aX+b)/(cX+b)
. For
\phi\in G(\mathbb{F})
, its corresponding element in
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}(X)/\mathbb{F})
, denoted by
\sigma_{\phi}
, is the
\mathbb{F}
-automorphism of
\mathbb{F}(X)
defined by
\sigma_{\phi}(X)=\phi(X)
.

Two rational functions
f,g\in\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}
are said to be
equivalent
, denoted as
f\sim g
, if there exist
\phi,\psi\in G(\mathbb{F})
such that
g=\phi\circ f\circ\psi
. This happens if and only if
\mathbb{F}(g)=\sigma(\mathbb{F}(f))
for some
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}(X)/\mathbb{F})
.

The set
\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}
equipped with composition
\circ
is a monoid and
G(\mathbb{F})
is the group of units of
(\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F},\circ)
. In a parallel setting, one replaces
\mathbb{F}(X)
with
\mathbb{F}[X]
and
G(\mathbb{F})
with the affine linear group
\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F})=\{\phi\in\mathbb{F}[X]:\deg\phi=1\}
. Then
(\mathbb{F}[X]\setminus\mathbb{F},\circ)
is a submonoid of
(\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F},\circ)
and
\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F})
is its group of units. If two polynomials
f,g\in\mathbb{F}[X]\setminus\mathbb{F}
are equivalent as rational functions, i.e.,
g=\phi\circ f\circ\psi
for some
\phi,\psi\in G(\mathbb{F})
, then there are
\alpha,\beta\in\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F})
such that
g=\alpha\circ f\circ\beta
; see Lemma
8.1
. Factorizations in the monoids
(\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F},\circ)
and
(\mathbb{F}[X]\setminus\mathbb{F},\circ)
are difficult questions that have attracted much attention
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
9
,
10
,
18
,
19
]
. Factorizations in
(\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F},\circ)
are determined by the lattice
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F})
of the subfields of
\mathbb{F}(X)
and vice versa. The Galois group
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}(X)/\mathbb{F})
is an automorphism group of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F})
and the
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}(X)/\mathbb{F})
-orbits in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F})
correspond to the equivalence classes in
\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}
.

Many intrinsic properties of rational functions are preserved under equivalence. The degree of a rational function in
\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}
is invariant under equivalence. Equivalent rational functions in
\mathbb{F}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}
have isomorphic arithmetic monodromy groups. The number of ramification points and their ramification indices of a rational function are preserved under equivalence
[
16
]
.
When
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the finite field with
q
elements, there is another important invariant:
|f(\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}))|
, the number of values of
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
on the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In the theory and applications of finite fields, an important question is to understand the polynomials that permute
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the rational functions that permute
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
under the aforementioned equivalence. For classifications of low degree permutation polynomials of finite fields, see
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
14
,
17
]
. Permutation rational functions of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
of degree 3 and 4 were classified recently
[
5
,
8
,
13
]
. Equivalence of rational functions over finite fields also arises in other circumstances.
There is a construction of irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
using a rational function
R(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
; the number of irreducible polynomials produced by the construction depends only on the equivalence class of
R(X)

[
15
]
.
It is known that the equivalence classes of rational functions
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}(f)
is Galois are in one-to-one correspondence with the classes of conjugate subgroups of
\text{PGL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
; see
[
12
]
.

When
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there are only finitely many equivalence classes of rational functions in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
with a given degree
n
. We shall denote this number by
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
. Despite its obvious significance, this number was not known previously. The main contribution of the present paper is the determination of
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
for all
q
and
n
(Theorem
6.1
). For example, when
n=3
, we have

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)=\begin{cases}2(q+1)&\text{if}\ q\equiv 1,4\pmod{6},\cr 2q&\text{if}\ q\equiv 2,5\pmod{6},\cr 2q+1&\text{if}\ q\equiv 3\pmod{6}.\end{cases}

The classification of rational functions of degree
n\leq 2
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is straightforward; see Sections 7.1 and 7.2. When
n=3
and
q
is even, the classification was obtained recently by Mattarei and Pizzato
[
16
]
using the fact that such rational functions have at most two ramification points. The case
n=3
and
q
odd is still unsolved. (In this case, it was shown in
[
16
]
that
\mathfrak{N}(q,3)\leq 4q
.)
A complete classification of rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
appears to be out of reach. However, the determination of
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
is an important step towards understanding the equivalence classes of rational functions over finite fields, especially those with low degree.

Here is the outline of our approach. There is an action of
\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
on the set of subfields
F\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
with
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(X):F]=n
, and
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
is the number of orbits of this action. To compute
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
by Burnside’s lemma, it suffices to determine the number of such subfields of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
fixed by each member
A
of
\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{F}_{q})
. From there on, the computation becomes quite technical and depends on the canonical form of
A
.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we include some preliminaries and lay out the plan for computing
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
. The ingredients of the formula for
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
are computed in Sections 3 – 5 and the explicit formula for
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
is presented in Section 6. A discussion of low degree rational functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
ensued in Section 7. The last section is devoted to equivalence classes of polynomials over finite fields. The situation is much simpler compared with the case of rational functions. The number of equivalence classes are computed and, as concrete examples, polynomials of degree up to 5 are classified. Several counting lemmas used in the paper are gathered in the appendix.

2.1.
Rational functions and subfields

Let

(2.1)

\mathcal{R}_{q,n}=\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X):\deg f=n\}.

By Lemma A
Appendix: Counting Lemmas
,

|\mathcal{R}_{q,n}|=\begin{cases}q-1&\text{if}\ n=0,\cr q^{2n-1}(q^{2}-1)&\text{if}\ n>0.\end{cases}

For
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we define
f_{1}\sim f_{2}
if
f_{2}=\phi\circ f_{1}\circ\psi
for some
\phi,\psi\in G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and we define
f_{1}\overset{\rm L}{\sim}f_{2}
if there exists
\phi\in G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
f_{2}=\phi\circ f_{1}
. It is clear that

f_{1}\overset{\rm L}{\sim}f_{2}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathbb{F}_{q}(f_{1})=\mathbb{F}_{q}(f_{2})

and

f_{1}\sim f_{2}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \mathbb{F}_{q}(f_{2})=\sigma(\mathbb{F}_{q}(f_{1}))\ \text{\rm for some}\ \sigma\in\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Recall that
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
denotes the number of
\sim
equivalence classes in
\mathcal{R}_{q,n}
; this number is the main subject of our investigation.

For
f=P/Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
,
\text{gcd}(P,Q)=1
, let

\mathcal{S}(f)=\langle P,Q\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=\{aP+bQ:a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},

the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of
\{P,Q\}
. (Throughout this paper, an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span is denoted by
\langle\ \rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.) Then
f_{1}\overset{\rm L}{\sim}f_{2}

\Leftrightarrow

\mathcal{S}(f_{1})=\mathcal{S}(f_{2})
. By Lüroth theorem, every subfield
F\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
with
[\mathbb{F}_{q}(X):F]=n<\infty
is of the form
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(f)
, where
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
is of degree
n
. The number of such
F
is

\frac{|\mathcal{R}_{q,n}|}{|G(\mathbb{F}_{q})|}=\frac{q^{2n-1}(q^{2}-1)}{q(q^{2}-1)}=q^{2(n-1)}.

Denote the set of these fields by
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{q^{2(n-1)}}\}
(Figure
1
) and let
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)/\mathbb{F}_{q})
act on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Then
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
is precisely the number of orbits of this action.

. Therefore, a field

, where

if and only if
\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(X))=\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(cX))
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(X)
with
\deg f=n>0
and
1\neq c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
with
o(c)=d
, where
o(c)
denotes the multiplicative order of
c
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(X))=\mathbb{F}_{q}(f(cX))
if and only if

\mathcal{S}(f)=\langle X^{r_{1}}P_{1}(X^{d}),\,X^{r_{2}}Q_{1}(X^{d})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}},

where
P_{1},Q_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
are monic,
0\leq r_{1},r_{2}<d
,
\deg(X^{r_{2}}Q_{1}(X^{d}))<\deg(X^{r_{1}}P_{1}(X^{d}))=n
, and
\text{\rm gcd}(X^{r_{1}}P_{1},\,X^{r_{2}}Q_{1})=1
.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) Obvious.

(
\Rightarrow
) We may assume that
f=P/Q
, where
P,Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
are monic,
\deg P=n
,
\deg Q=m<n
,
\text{gcd}(P,Q)=1
, and the coefficient of
X^{m}
in
P
is 0. Let
n\equiv r_{1}\pmod{d}
and
m\equiv r_{2}\pmod{d}
, where
0\leq r_{1},r_{2}<d
. Such a pair
(P,Q)
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{S}(f)
. Since

\langle P(X),Q(X)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}=\mathcal{S}(f)=\mathcal{S}(f(cX))=\langle c^{-n}P(cX),c^{-m}Q(cX)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}},

we have

P(X)=c^{-n}P(cX),\quad Q(X)=c^{-m}Q(cX).

Thus the coefficient of
X^{i}
in
P(X)
is 0 for all
i
with
i\not\equiv n\pmod{d}
, whence
P(X)=x^{r_{1}}P_{1}(X^{d})
. In the same way,
Q(X)=X^{r_{2}}Q_{1}(X^{d})
. Since
\text{gcd}(P,Q)=1
, we have
\text{gcd}(X^{r_{1}}P_{1},X^{r_{2}}Q_{1})=1

5.1.
A useful lemma

Let
p=\text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Every
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
has a representation

(5.1)

f(X)=g_{p-1}(X^{p}-X)X^{p-1}+g_{p-2}(X^{p}-X)X^{p-2}+\cdots+g_{0}(X^{p}-X),

where
g_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. Define
\Delta f=f(X+1)-f(X)
. Then
\Delta^{p}f=0
, and for
0\leq i\leq p-1
,

\Delta^{i}f=g_{i}(X^{p}-X)i!+\sum_{j=i+1}^{p-1}g_{j}(X^{p}-X)\Delta^{i}X^{j}.

It follows that
g_{i}
in (
5.1
) are uniquely determined by
f
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
0\leq i\leq p-1
. Then
\Delta^{i}f=0
if and only if
g_{j}=0
for all
i\leq j\leq p-1
in (
5.1
).

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) Obvious.

(
\Rightarrow
) Assume the contrary. Let
j_{0}
be the largest
j
such that
g_{j}\neq 0
. Then
i\leq j_{0}\leq p-1
. We have

\displaystyle\Delta^{i}f\,

\displaystyle=g_{j_{0}}(X^{p}-X)\Delta^{i}X^{j_{0}}+\sum_{j<j_{0}}g_{j}(X^{p}-X)\Delta^{i}X^{j}

\displaystyle=g_{j_{0}}(X^{p}-X)\binom{j_{0}}{i}X^{j_{0}-i}+\sum_{j<j_{0}-i}h_{j}(X^{p}-X)X^{j}\kern 25.00003pt(h_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X])

\displaystyle\neq 0,

which is a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 6.1
.

(6.3)

(6.4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\;\text{\rm odd}\cr 1<d\,\mid\,\text{\rm gcd}(q+1,n)\end{subarray}}\phi(d)q^{2n/d-2}

(6.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q+1\cr d\,\nmid\,n\end{subarray}}\phi(d)\bigl((-1)^{\lfloor n/d\rfloor}(1+q)+q^{2\lfloor n/d\rfloor+1}(q-1)\bigr),

In (
6.2
) and (

),

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}(q,n)=\,

2^{\circ}

\displaystyle=\frac{q-1}{2}\mathfrak{A}(q,n).

3^{\circ}

\displaystyle\sum_{\{\alpha,\alpha^{q}\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}}\text{Fix}(A_{\{\alpha,\alpha^{q}\}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(q+1)(q^{2}+1)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q+1\cr d\,\nmid\,n\end{subarray}}\phi(d)\Bigl(\frac{1+(-1)^{\lfloor n/d\rfloor}}{2}(1+q)^{2}+q(q^{2\lfloor n/d\rfloor+2}-1)\Bigr)

q-1\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(q,3)\,

\displaystyle=\phi(3)\Bigl(1+\frac{2(q^{2}-1)}{q+1}\Bigr)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<d\,\mid\,q-1\cr d\,\nmid\,3\end{subarray}}\phi(d)

\displaystyle=2(1+2(q-1))+q-1-\phi(1)-\phi(3)

\displaystyle=2(2q-1)+q-1-3=5q-6.

If
q-1\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,

\mathfrak{A}(q,3)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<d\,\mid\,q-1\cr d\,\nmid\,3\end{subarray}}\phi(d)=q-1-\phi(1)=q-2.

Next, assume
q
is odd.

If
q-1\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(q,3)\,

\displaystyle=\phi(3)\Bigl(1+\frac{2(q^{2}-1)}{q+1}\Bigr)+\phi(2)\frac{q^{3}+1}{q+1}+\sum_{3<d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)

\displaystyle=2(1+2(q-1))+q^{2}-q+1+q-1-\phi(1)-\phi(2)-\phi(3)

\displaystyle=2(2q-1)+q^{2}-4=q^{2}+4q-6.

If
q-1\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,

\mathfrak{A}(q,3)=\phi(2)\frac{q^{3}+1}{q+1}+\sum_{3<d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)=q^{2}-q+1+q-1-\phi(1)-\phi(2)=q^{2}-2.

To summarize,

\mathfrak{A}(q,3)=\begin{cases}5q-6&\text{if}\ q\equiv 4\pmod{6},\cr q-2&\text{if}\ q\equiv 2\pmod{6},\cr q^{2}+4q-6&\text{if}\ q\equiv 1\pmod{6},\cr q^{2}-2&\text{if}\ q\equiv 3,5\pmod{6}.\end{cases}

2^{\circ}
Computing
\mathfrak{B}(q,3)
.

First assume
q
is even.

If
q+1\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(q,3)\,

\displaystyle=\phi(3)+\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q+1\cr d\,\nmid\,3\end{subarray}}\phi(d)\bigl[(1+q)+q(q-1)\bigr]

\displaystyle=2+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q+1\cr d\,\nmid\,3\end{subarray}}\phi(d)=2+q+1-\phi(1)-\phi(3)=q.

If
q+1\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,

\mathfrak{B}(q,3)=\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q+1\cr d\,\nmid\,3\end{subarray}}\phi(d)\bigl[(1+q)+q(q-1)\bigr]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q+1\cr d\,\nmid\,3\end{subarray}}\phi(d)=q+1-\phi(1)=q.

Next, assume
q
is odd.

If
q+1\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(q,3)\,

\displaystyle=\phi(3)+\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\Bigl[\phi(2)(-(1+q)+q^{3}(q-1))+\sum_{3<d\,\mid\,q+1}\phi(d)(1+q+q(q-1))\Bigr]

\displaystyle=2+\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\Bigl[q^{4}-q^{3}-q-1+(q^{2}+1)\sum_{3<d\,\mid\,q+1}\phi(d)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=2+\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\bigl[(q^{2}+1)(q^{2}-q-1)+(q^{2}+1)(q+1-\phi(1)-\phi(2)-\phi(3))\bigr]

\displaystyle=2+q^{2}-q-1+q+1-4=q^{2}-2.

If
q+1\not\equiv 0\pmod{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(q,3)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\Bigl[\phi(2)(-(1+q)+q^{3}(q-1))+\sum_{3<d\,\mid\,q+1}\phi(d)((1+q)+q(q-1))\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{2}+1}\bigl[(q^{2}+1)(q^{2}-q-1)+(q^{2}+1)(q+1-\phi(1)-\phi(2))\bigr]

\displaystyle=q^{2}-q-1+q+1-2=q^{2}-2.

To summarize,

\mathfrak{B}(q,3)=\begin{cases}q&\text{if $q$ is even},\cr q^{2}-2&\text{if $q$ is odd}.\end{cases}

3^{\circ}
Computing
\mathfrak{C}(q,3)
. We have

\mathfrak{C}(q,3)=\begin{cases}q(q-1)&\text{if}\ p=2,\cr q&\text{if}\ p=3,\cr 0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

4^{\circ}

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)

If
q\equiv 1\pmod{6}
,

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)=\frac{q^{3}}{q^{2}-1}+\frac{1}{2(q-1)}(q^{2}+4q-6)+\frac{1}{2(q+1)}(q^{2}-2)=2(q+1).

If
q\equiv 2\pmod{6}
,

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)=\frac{q^{3}}{q^{2}-1}+\frac{1}{2(q-1)}(q-2)+\frac{1}{2(q+1)}q+\frac{1}{q}q(q-1)=2q.

If
q\equiv 3\pmod{6}
, i.e.,
p=3
,

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)=\frac{q^{3}}{q^{2}-1}+\frac{1}{2(q-1)}(q^{2}-2)+\frac{1}{2(q+1)}(q^{2}-2)+\frac{1}{q}q=2q+1.

If
q\equiv 4\pmod{6}
,

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)=\frac{q^{3}}{q^{2}-1}+\frac{1}{2(q-1)}(5q-6)+\frac{1}{2(q+1)}q+\frac{1}{q}q(q-1)=2(q+1).

If
q\equiv 5\pmod{6}
,

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)=\frac{q^{3}}{q^{2}-1}+\frac{1}{2(q-1)}(q^{2}-2)+\frac{1}{2(q+1)}(q^{2}-2)=2q.

To summarize,

\mathfrak{N}(q,3)=\begin{cases}2(q+1)&\text{if}\ q\equiv 1,4\pmod{6},\cr 2q&\text{if}\ q\equiv 2,5\pmod{6},\cr 2q+1&\text{if}\ q\equiv 3\pmod{6}.\end{cases}

8.
Equivalence Classes of Polynomials

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
g=\phi\circ f\circ\psi
for some
\phi,\psi\in G(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
g=\alpha\circ f\circ\beta
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\text{\rm AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
\psi(X)=A(X)/B(X)
.

Case 1.
Assume that
B(X)=1
. Then
\psi=A\in\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Since
f\circ A=f(A(X))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
and
\phi\circ f\circ A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
, it follows that
\phi\in\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Case 2.
Assume that
B(X)\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
B(X)=X+d
and
A(X)=aX+b
. Let
f(X)=X^{n}+a_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{0}
. Then

f(\phi(X))=\frac{A(X)^{n}+a_{n-1}A(X)^{n-1}B(X)+\cdots+a_{0}B(X)^{n}}{B(X)^{n}}.

Let
\phi(X)=(sX+t)/(uX+v)
. Then

(8.1)

u\bigl(A(X)^{n}+a_{n-1}A(X)^{n-1}B(X)+\cdots+a_{0}B(X)^{n}\bigr)+vB(X)^{n}=1

and

g(X)=s\bigl(A(X)^{n}+a_{n-1}A(X)^{n-1}B(X)+\cdots+a_{0}B(X)^{n}\bigr)+tB(X)^{n}.

By (
8.1
),
u\neq 0
and

g(X)=su^{-1}(1-vB(X)^{n})+tB(X)^{n}=su^{-1}+(t-su^{-1}v)B(X)^{n}.

Hence we may assume
g(X)=X^{n}
. By (
8.1
) again,

\displaystyle uf\Bigl(\frac{A(X)}{B(X)}\Bigr)+v\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B(X)^{n}}=\Bigl(\frac{1}{X+d}\Bigr)^{n}=\Bigl(\frac{AX+b}{X+d}-a\Bigr)^{n}(b-ad)^{-n}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\frac{A(X)}{B(X)}-a\Bigr)^{n}(b-ad)^{-n}.

So
f(X)=u^{-1}(b-ad)^{-n}(X-a)^{n}-u^{-1}v
. Hence we may assume
f(X)=X^{n}
. Then
f=g
.
∎

Because of Lemma
8.1
, we define
two polynomials
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
to be
equivalent
if there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
g=\alpha\circ f\circ\beta
; the meaning of equivalence between
f
and
g
is the same whether they are treated as polynomials or as rational functions.

Let

\mathcal{P}_{q,n}=\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]:\deg f=n\}

and let
\mathfrak{M}(q,n)
denote the number of equivalence classes in
\mathcal{P}_{q,n}
. Compared with
\mathfrak{N}(q,n)
,
\mathfrak{M}(q,n)
is much easier to determine.

For
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, define
f\overset{L}{\sim}g
if there exists
\alpha\in\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
g=\alpha\circ f
. Let
[f]
denote the
\overset{L}{\sim}
equivalence class of
f
. Each
\overset{L}{\sim}
equivalence class has a unique representative
X^{n}+a_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}X
. Let
\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
act on the set of
\overset{L}{\sim}
equivalence classes in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
as follows: For
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\alpha\in\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
[f]^{\alpha}=[f\circ\alpha]
. Then
\mathfrak{M}(q,n)
is the number of
\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
-orbits in
\Omega_{n}:=\{[f]:f\in\mathcal{P}_{q,n}\}
. The information about the conjugacy classes of
\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
is given in Table
2
. For
\alpha\in\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{F}_{q})
, let

\text{Fix}(\alpha)
be the number of elements in
\Omega_{n}
fixed by
\alpha
. All we have to do is to determine
\text{Fix}(\alpha)
for each representative
\alpha
in Table
2
.

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
p=\text{\rm char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We have

\mathfrak{M}(q,n)=\frac{q^{n-2}}{q-1}+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{1<d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}+\begin{cases}q^{n/p-1}&\text{if}\ p\mid n,\cr q^{-1}&\text{if}\ n=1,\cr 0&\text{if $p\nmid n$ and $n>1$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Burnside’s lemma and (
8.2
) – (
8.4
),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(q,n)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q(q-1)}\text{Fix}(X)+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus\{1\}}\text{Fix}(aX)+\frac{1}{q}\text{Fix}(X+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{n-2}}{q-1}+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{1<d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}+\frac{1}{q}\text{Fix}(X+1),

where

\frac{1}{q}\text{Fix}(X+1)=\begin{cases}q^{n/p-1}&\text{if}\ p\mid n,\cr q^{-1}&\text{if}\ n=1,\cr 0&\text{if $p\nmid n$ and $n>1$}.\end{cases}

∎

In Theorem
8.2
, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{q^{n-2}}{q-1}+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{1<d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{q^{n-2}}{q-1}+\frac{1}{q-1}\Bigl(\sum_{d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}-q^{n-1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}+\frac{q^{n-2}-q^{n-1}}{q-1}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\Bigl(\sum_{d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)(q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}-1)+\sum_{d\,\mid\,q-1}\phi(d)\Bigr)-q^{n-2}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q-1\cr d<n\end{subarray}}\phi(d)(q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}-1)+1-q^{n-2}.

Hence

\mathfrak{M}(q,n)=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q-1\cr d<n\end{subarray}}\phi(d)(q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}-1)+\begin{cases}1-q^{n-2}+q^{n/p-1}&\text{if}\ p\mid n,\cr 1&\text{if}\ n=1,\cr 1-q^{n-2}&\text{if $p\nmid n$ and $n>1$}.\end{cases}

In the above, the sum

\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q-1\cr d<n\end{subarray}}\phi(d)(q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}-1)

can be made more explicit as follows: Write

\text{lcm}\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}=\prod_{\text{$r$ prime}}r^{\nu_{r}},\qquad\nu_{r}=\lfloor\log_{r}(n-1)\rfloor,

and

\text{gcd}(\text{lcm}\{1,2,\dots,n-1\},q-1)=\prod_{\text{$r$ prime}}r^{u_{r}}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\,\mid\,q-1\cr d<n\end{subarray}}\phi(d)(q^{\lceil n/d\rceil-1}-1)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{r}\leq u_{r}\cr\prod_{r}r^{e_{r}}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\phi\Bigl(\prod_{r}r^{e_{r}}\Bigr)(q^{\lceil n/\prod_{r}r^{e_{r}}\rceil-1}-1)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{r}\leq u_{r}\cr\prod_{r}r^{e_{r}}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\prod_{r}r^{e_{r}}\Bigr)\Bigl(\prod_{r:e_{r}>0}(1-r^{-1})\Bigr)(q^{\lceil n/\prod_{r}r^{e_{r}}\rceil-1}-1).

Appendix: Counting Lemmas

For
m,n\geq 0
, let

\displaystyle\alpha_{m,n}\,

\displaystyle=|\{(f,g):f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\ \text{monic},\ \deg f=m,\ \deg g=n,\ \text{gcd}(f,g)=1\}|,

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\,

\displaystyle=|\{(f,g):f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\ \text{monic},\ \deg f<n,\ \deg g=n,\ \text{gcd}(f,g)=1\}|.

Lemma A1.

We have

(A1)

\alpha_{m,n}=\begin{cases}q^{n}&\text{if}\ m=0,\cr q^{m+n}(1-q^{-1})&\text{if}\ m,n>0,\end{cases}

and

(A2)

\alpha_{n}=q^{2n-1},\quad n\geq 1.

Proof.

For (
A1
), we may assume that
n-m=d\geq 0
, and it suffices to show that

(A3)

\alpha_{m,m+d}=\begin{cases}q^{d}&\text{if}\ m=0,\cr q^{2m+d}(1-q^{-1})&\text{if}\ m>0,\end{cases}

The pairs
(f,g)
, where
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
are monic,
\deg f=m
and
\deg g=m+d
, are of the form
(hf_{1},hg_{1})
, where
h,f_{1},g_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
are monic,
\deg f_{1}=m-\deg h
,
\deg g_{1}=m+d-\deg h
, and
\text{gcd}(f_{1},g_{1})=1
. Hence

q^{2m+d}=\sum_{i\geq 0}q^{i}\alpha_{m-i,m+d-i},

whence

\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{2m+d}X^{m}=\Bigl(\sum_{i\geq 0}q^{i}X^{i}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}\alpha_{j,j+d}X^{j}\Bigr).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\alpha_{j,j+d}X^{j}\,

\displaystyle=(1-qX)\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{2m+d}X^{m}=q^{d}\Bigl(\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{2m}X^{m}-\sum_{m\geq 0}q^{2m+1}X^{m+1}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=q^{d}\Bigl(1+\sum_{m\geq 1}(q^{2m}-q^{2m-1})X^{m}\Bigr)=q^{d}\Bigl(1+\sum_{m\geq 1}q^{2m}(1-q^{-1})X^{m}\Bigr),

which is (
A3
) (with
j
in place of
m
).

For (
A2
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{m,n}=q^{n}+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}q^{m+n}(1-q^{-1})

\displaystyle=q^{n}+q^{n}(q-1)\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}q^{m}=q^{n}+q^{n}(q^{n-1}-1)

\displaystyle=q^{2n-1}.

∎

Lemma A2.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{q,n}=\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]:\deg f=n\}
. Then

|\mathcal{R}_{q,n}|=\begin{cases}q-1&\text{if}\ n=0,\cr q^{2n-1}(q^{2}-1)&\text{if}\ n>0.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
n>0
, we have

|\mathcal{R}_{q,n}|=(q-1)(2\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n,n})=(q-1)(2q^{2n-1}+q^{2n}(1-q^{-1}))=q^{2n-1}(q^{2}-1).

∎

For
m,n\geq 0
, let

\displaystyle\beta_{m,n}=

\displaystyle|\{(f,g):f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]\ \text{monic},\ \deg f=m,\ \deg g=n,\ f(0)\neq 0,\ \text{gcd}(f,g)=1\}|.

Lemma A3.

We have

\beta_{m,n}=\begin{cases}q^{m-n-1}(q-1)\displaystyle\frac{q^{2n+1}+1}{q+1}&\text{if}\ m>n\geq 0,\cr q^{n}&\text{if}\ m=0,\cr q^{n-m}(q-1)\displaystyle\frac{q^{2m}-1}{q+1}&\text{if}\ 1\leq m\leq n.\end{cases}

Proof.

We have

\alpha_{m,n}=\beta_{m,n}+\beta_{n,m-1}.

Therefore,

(A4)

\displaystyle\beta_{m,n}\,

\displaystyle=\alpha_{m,n}-\beta_{n,m-1}=\alpha_{m,n}-(\alpha_{n,m-1}-\beta_{m-1,n-1})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{m,n}-\alpha_{m-1,n}+\beta_{m-1,n-1}=c_{m,n}+\beta_{m-1,n-1},

where

\displaystyle c_{m,n}\,

\displaystyle=\alpha_{m,n}-\alpha_{m-1,n}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}q^{n}&\text{if}\ m=0,\cr q^{m-1}(q-1)&\text{if}\ m>0,\ n=0,\cr q^{n}(q-2)&\text{if}\ m=1,\ n>0,\cr q^{m+n-2}(q-1)^{2}&\text{if}\ m>1,\ n>0.\end{cases}

By (
A4
),

\beta_{m,n}=\sum_{i\geq 0}c_{m-i,n-i}.

When
m>n
,

\displaystyle\beta_{m,n}\,

\displaystyle=c_{m,n}+c_{m-1,n-1}+\cdots+c_{m-n,0}

\displaystyle=c_{m,n}+c_{m-1,n-1}+\cdots+c_{m-n+1,1}+q^{m-n-1}(q-1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}q^{m-n+2i-2}(q-1)^{2}+q^{m-n-1}(q-1)

\displaystyle=q^{m-n}(q-1)^{2}\,\frac{q^{2n}-1}{q^{2}-1}+q^{m-n-1}(q-1)

\displaystyle=q^{m-n-1}(q-1)\frac{q^{2n+1}-1}{q+1}.

When
m\leq n
,

\displaystyle\beta_{m,n}\,

\displaystyle=c_{m,n}+c_{m-1,n-1}+\cdots+c_{0,n-m}

\displaystyle=c_{m,n}+c_{m-1,n-1}+\cdots+c_{1,n-m+1}+q^{n-m}.

In the above, if
m=0
,

\beta_{0,n}=q^{n};

if
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\beta_{m,n}\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{m}q^{n-m+2i-2}(q-1)^{2}+q^{n-m+1}(q-2)+q^{n-m}

\displaystyle=q^{n-m+2}(q-1)^{2}\,\frac{q^{2(m-1)}-1}{q^{2}-1}+q^{n-m+1}(q-2)+q^{n-m}

\displaystyle=q^{n-m}(q-1)\frac{q^{2m}-1}{q+1}.

∎

Let
\overline{(\ )}=(\ )^{q}
be the Frobenius of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and for
g=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
, define
\bar{g}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\bar{a}_{i}X^{i}
.
For
0\neq g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
, define
\tilde{g}=X^{\deg g}\bar{g}(X^{-1})
; that is, for
g=a_{m}X^{m}+a_{m-1}X^{m-1}+\cdots+a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
,
a_{m}\neq 0
,

\tilde{g}=\bar{a}_{0}X^{m}+\bar{a}_{1}X^{m-1}+\cdots+\bar{a}_{m}.

Clearly,
\widetilde{g_{1}g_{2}}=\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}
,
\widetilde{X^{m}}=1
, and
\tilde{\tilde{g}}=g
if
g(0)\neq 0
. We say the
g
is
self-dual
if
\tilde{g}=cg
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
. In this case,
(\bar{a}_{0},\bar{a}_{m})=c(a_{m},a_{0})
, which implies that
a_{0}/a_{m}\in\mu_{q+1}
and
c=\bar{a}_{0}/a_{m}\in\mu_{q+1}
.

Define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}\,

\displaystyle=|\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]:\text{$g$ is monic, self-dual, $\deg g=i$}\}|,

\displaystyle\Theta_{i}\,

\displaystyle=|\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]:\text{$g$ is monic, $\deg g=i$, $\text{gcd}(g,\tilde{g})=1$}\}|,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i,j}\,

\displaystyle=|\{(g,h):\text{$g,h\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]$ monic, self-dual, $\text{gcd}(g,h)=1$}\}|.

Lemma A4.

We have

(A5)

\Lambda_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ i=0,\cr(q+1)q^{i-1}&\text{if}\ i>0,\end{cases}

\Theta_{i}=\frac{1}{1+q^{2}}\bigl[(-1)^{i}(1+q)+q^{2i+1}(q-1)\bigr].

Proof.

Every monic
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
has a unique representation
g=g_{1}h
, where
h=\text{gcd}(g,\tilde{g})
, which is monic and self-dual, and
g_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
is monic such that
\text{gcd}(g_{1},\tilde{g}_{1})=1
. Therefore,

\sum_{i=0}^{l}\Lambda_{i}\Theta_{l-i}=|\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]\ \text{monic of degree $l$}\}|=q^{2l},

that is,

(A6)

\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Lambda_{i}X^{i}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Theta_{j}X^{j}\Bigr)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}q^{2l}X^{l}=\frac{1}{1-q^{2}X}.

Clearly
\Lambda_{0}=1
. Assume
l\geq 1
. Let
g(X)=X^{l}+a_{l-1}X^{l-1}+\cdots+a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
, so
\tilde{g}(X)=\bar{a}_{0}X^{l}+\bar{a}_{1}X^{l-1}+\cdots+1
. Then
g
is self-dual if and only if

(A7)

\begin{array}[]{cccccccl}&\bar{a}_{0}&\kern-6.00006pt(&a_{0}&a_{1}&\dots&a_{l-1}&\kern-6.00006pt)\cr=&&\kern-6.00006pt(&1&\bar{a}_{l-1}&\dots&\bar{a}_{1}&\kern-6.00006pt).\end{array}

If
l-1
is even, to satisfy

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccl}&\bar{a}_{0}&\kern-6.00006pt(&a_{0}&a_{1}&\dots&a_{(l-1)/2}&a_{(l+1)/2}&\dots&a_{l-1}&\kern-6.00006pt)\cr=&&\kern-6.00006pt(&1&\bar{a}_{l-1}&\dots&\overline{a_{(l+1)/2}}&\overline{a_{(l-1)/2}}&\dots&\bar{a}_{1}&\kern-6.00006pt),\end{array}

we can choose
a_{0}\in\mu_{q+1}
, choose
a_{1},\dots,a_{(l-1)/2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
arbitrarily and let
a_{i}=\overline{a_{l-i}}/\bar{a}_{0}
for
(l+1)/2\leq i\leq l-1
. Hence
\Lambda_{l}=(q+1)(q^{2})^{(l-1)/2}=(q+1)q^{l-1}
. If
l-1
is odd, to satisfy

\begin{array}[]{cccccccccccl}&\bar{a}_{0}&\kern-6.00006pt(&a_{0}&a_{1}&\dots&a_{l/2-1}&a_{l/2}&a_{l/2+1}&\dots&a_{l-1}&\kern-6.00006pt)\cr=&&\kern-6.00006pt(&1&\bar{a}_{l-1}&\dots&\overline{a_{l/2+1}}&\overline{a_{l/2}}&\overline{a_{l/2-1}}&\dots&\bar{a}_{1}&\kern-6.00006pt),\end{array}

we can choose
a_{0}\in\mu_{q+1}
, choose
a_{1},\dots,a_{l/2-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
arbitrarily, choose
a_{l/2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
\bar{a}_{0}a_{l/2}=\overline{a_{l/2}}
and let
a_{i}=\overline{a_{l-i}}/\bar{a}_{0}
for
\l/2+1\leq i\leq l-1
. Since
a_{0}\in\mu_{q+1}
, the number of choices for
a_{l/2}
is
q
. Thus we also have
\Lambda_{l}=(q+1)q(q^{2})^{l/2-1}=(q+1)q^{l-1}
. Therefore,

\Lambda_{l}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ l=0,\cr(q+1)q^{l-1}&\text{if}\ l>0.\end{cases}

We then have

(A8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Lambda_{i}X^{i}\,

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(q+1)q^{i-1}X^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(q+1)q^{i-1}X^{i}+1-(q+1)q^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q+1}{q}\frac{1}{1-qX}-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1+X}{1-qX}.

By (
A6
) and (
A8
),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Theta_{j}X^{j}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1-qX}{1+X}\cdot\frac{1}{1-q^{2}X}=\frac{1+q}{1+q^{2}}\frac{1}{1+X}+\frac{q(q-1)}{1+q^{2}}\frac{1}{1-q^{2}X}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+q}{1+q^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}X^{j}+\frac{q(q-1)}{1+q^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{2j}X^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+q^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\bigl[(-1)^{j}(1+q)+q^{2j+1}(q-1)\bigr]X^{j}.

Hence

\Theta_{j}=\frac{1}{1+q^{2}}\bigl[(-1)^{j}(1+q)+q^{2j+1}(q-1)\bigr].

∎

Lemma A5.

For
i,j\geq 0
, we have

\Gamma_{i,i+j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ i=j=0,\cr q^{j-1}(q+1)&\text{if}\ i=0,\ j>0,\cr\displaystyle\frac{q(q+1)}{q^{2}+1}(q^{2i}-q^{2i-2}-(-1)^{i}2)&\text{if}\ i>0,\ j=0,\cr\displaystyle\frac{q^{j-1}(q+1)(q^{2}-1)}{q^{2}+1}(q^{2i}-(-1)^{i})&\text{if}\ i>0,\ j>0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Each ordered pair
(f,g)
, where
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
are monic and self-dual with
\deg f=i
and
\deg g=i+j
, has a unique representation
(f,g)=(f_{1}h,g_{1}h)
, where
f_{1},g_{1},h\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
are monic and self-dual and
\text{gcd}(f_{1},g_{1})=1
. Thus

\sum_{k}\Lambda_{k}\Gamma_{i-k,i+j-k}=\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i+j}.

Therefore,

(A9)

\Bigl(\sum_{k\geq 0}\Lambda_{k}X^{k}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{l\geq 0}\Gamma_{l,l+j}X^{l}\Bigr)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i+j}X^{i}.

When
j=0
, by (
A5
),

(A10)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i}X^{i}\,

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i\geq 1}(q+1)^{2}q^{2(i-1)}X^{i}

(8.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}(q+1)^{2}q^{2(i-1)}X^{i}+1-(q+1)^{2}q^{-2}

(8.6)

\displaystyle=(q+1)^{2}q^{-2}\frac{1}{1-q^{2}X}+1-(q+1)^{2}q^{-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+(2q+1)X}{1-q^{2}X}.

Combining (
A9
), (
A8
) and (
A10
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{l\geq 0}\Gamma_{l,l}X^{l}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1-qX}{1+X}\cdot\frac{1+(2q+1)X}{1-q^{2}X}

\displaystyle=\frac{2q+1}{q}-\frac{2q(q+1)}{q^{2}+1}\cdot\frac{1}{1+X}+\frac{(q-1)(q+1)^{2}}{q(q^{2}+1)}\cdot\frac{1}{1-q^{2}X}

\displaystyle=\frac{2q+1}{q}-\frac{2q(q+1)}{q^{2}+1}\sum_{l\geq 0}(-1)^{l}X^{l}+\frac{(q-1)(q+1)^{2}}{q(q^{2}+1)}\sum_{l\geq 0}q^{2l}X^{l}.

Hence

\Gamma_{l,l}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ l=0,\cr\displaystyle\frac{q(q+1)}{q^{2}+1}(q^{2l}-q^{2l-2}-(-1)^{l}2)&\text{if}\ l>0.\end{cases}

When
j>0
, by (
A5
),

(A11)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{i+j}X^{i}\,

\displaystyle=(q+1)q^{j-1}+\sum_{i\geq 1}(q+1)^{2}q^{2i+j-2}X^{i}

(8.7)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}(q+1)^{2}q^{2i+j-2}X^{i}+(q+1)q^{j-1}-(q+1)^{2}q^{j-2}

(8.8)

\displaystyle=(q+1)^{2}q^{j-2}\frac{1}{1-q^{2}X}-(q+1)q^{j-2}

\displaystyle=q^{j-1}(q+1)\frac{1+qX}{1-q^{2}X}.

Combining (
A9
), (
A8
) and (
A11
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{l\geq 0}\Gamma_{l,l+j}X^{l}\,

\displaystyle=q^{j-1}(q+1)\frac{1-qX}{1+X}\cdot\frac{1+qX}{1-q^{2}X}

\displaystyle=q^{j-1}(q+1)\Bigl(1+\frac{1-q^{2}}{1+q^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{1+X}-\frac{1-q^{2}}{1+q^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{1-q^{2}X}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=q^{j-1}(q+1)+\frac{q^{j-1}(q+1)(1-q^{2})}{1+q^{2}}\Bigl(\sum_{l\geq 0}(-1)^{l}X^{l}-\sum_{l\geq 0}q^{2l}X^{l}\Bigr).

Hence

\Gamma_{l,l+j}=\begin{cases}q^{j-1}(q+1)&\text{if}\ l=0,\cr\displaystyle\frac{q^{j-1}(q+1)(q^{2}-1)}{q^{2}+1}(q^{2l}-(-1)^{l})&\text{if}\ l>0.\end{cases}

.
∎

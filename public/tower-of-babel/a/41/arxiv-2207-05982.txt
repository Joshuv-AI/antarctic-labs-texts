On the representation of weakly maxitive monetary risk measures and their rate functions Thanks: I would like to thank Michael Kupper and Henri Comman for valuable comments, and an anonymous reviewer for his suggestions, which have helped to improve the presentation of the article.
José M. Zapata

Abstract
. The present paper provides a representation result for monetary risk measures (i.e., monotone translation invariant functionals) satisfying a weak maxitivity property. This result can be understood as a functional analytic generalization of Gärtner-Ellis large deviations theorem. In contrast to the classical Gärtner-Ellis theorem, the rate function is computed on an arbitrary set of continuous real-valued functions rather than the dual space. As an application of the main result, we establish a large deviation result for sequences of sublinear expectations on regular Hausdorff topological spaces. Key words: maxitive monetary risk measure, large deviations, rate function, Laplace principle. AMS Subject Classification: 46N30, 60F10, 91B05.

In the following we fix an arbitrarily given non-empty set

\psi_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}

\psi_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x)=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{H}}\{f(x)-\psi(f)\}

We want to establish sufficient conditions so that

We say that a point

(1.1)

\psi^{\ast}_{\mathcal{H}}(y)-f(y)>\psi^{\ast}_{\mathcal{H}}(x)-f(x)\quad\mbox{ for all }y\neq x.

The interpretation of the exposing condition (

Denote by

In the special case of the asymptotic entropy, the main result of this paper reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is exponentially tight.
Then:

(i)

For every closed set
C\subset E
, we have the upper bound

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in C)\leq-\underset{y\in C}{\inf}\psi_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x).

(ii)

For every open set
O\subset E
, we have the lower bound

-\underset{y\in O\cap\mathscr{E}}{\inf}\psi_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(X_{n}\in O).

(iii)

If, moreover,

(1.2)

\underset{x\in O}{\inf}\psi_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x)=\underset{x\in O\cap{\mathscr{E}}}{\inf}\psi_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x)\quad\mbox{for all }O\subset E\mbox{ open,}

then
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the LDP and LP with rate function
\psi_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}
.

Of great importance,
Gärtner-Ellis theorem provides the LDP for sequences of random variables with values on a topological vector space by testing the rate function on the dual space.
This well-known result turns out to be a particular instance of Theorem
1.1
above for the special case when
E
is a topological vector space and the particular choice
\mathcal{H}=E^{\ast}
; cf.
[
12
, Theorem 4.5.20]
.
One of the novelties of Theorem
1.1
is that it allows for different options for the testing set
\mathcal{H}
, instead of limiting ourselves to the dual space.
Moreover, it is well-known that Gärtner-Ellis theorem does not cover all cases in which a LDP exists; there are examples for which the LDP holds, but it does not follow from this basic result; see
[
12
, Remarks(d), p. 45]
and
[
10
]
.
In contrast, Theorem
1.1
above allows for arbitrary choices of
\mathcal{H}
, covering situations where Gärtner-Ellis theorem fails.
This is illustrated in Example
5.5
below, where we have a situation where a LDP is not covered by Gärtner-Ellis theorem, but it is captured by choosing a family
\mathcal{H}
of inverted v-shaped functions.

Whereas we have stated above our main result for the particular functional
\psi(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{nf(X_{n})}]
, it applies to very general functionals allowing to cover some non-standard setups as those in
[
2

11
,
14
,
16
,
22
,
28
,
31
]
.
For instance, in situations with model uncertainty, one may be interested in considering a set
\mathcal{P}
of probability measures rather than a single probability measure
\mathbb{P}
; see
[
11
,
28
]
and references therein.
This situation is covered by our main result by considering the robust asymptotic entropy
\psi_{\mathcal{P}}(f)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{Q}[e^{nf(X_{n})}]
. In particular, we extend to infinite dimensional spaces the version of Gärtner-Ellis theorem for sequences of sublinear expectations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
proven in
[
28

with the advantage that the rate function is now tested on arbitrary sets of continuous functions.

The present approach fully relies on topological and order properties and, in particular, it is not needed an underlying probability space.
We emphasize that the existing proofs of Gärtner-Ellis theorem are based on probability concepts such as the Radon-Nykodym derivative which are not needed here; cf.
[
12
, Theorem 4.5.20]

In line with
[
21

25
]
, the machinery is taken from maxitive integration.
More specifically, as in
[
21
]
we use the convex integral introduced by Cattaneo
[
5

, which is conceptually related to the idempotent integral in tropical mathematics
[
23
]
and can be obtained as a transformation of the Shilkret integral
[
27
]
.
In particular, we rely on the duality bounds for convex integrals and the convex integral representation of weakly maxitive monetary risk measures provided in
[
21
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section

In Section

In Section

Finally, in Section

3.
Integral representation of maxitive monetary risk measures

A
monetary risk measure
8
8

8

Here, we use the terminology of
[
15
]
up to a sign change.
is a function

\phi\colon B(E)\to[-\infty,\infty]

satisfying:

(N)

Normalization:
\phi(0)=0
,

(M)

Monotonicity:
\phi(f)\leq\phi(g)
whenever
f\leq g
,

(T)

Translation invariance:
\phi(f+c)=\phi(f)+c
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

We say that a monetary risk measure
\phi
is
maxitive
if it satifies

\phi(f\vee g)\leq\phi(f)\vee\phi(g)\quad\mbox{ for all }f,g\in B(E).

Due to (b2)–(b4) in Section
2
the convex integral (
2.1
) is a monetary risk measure which is maxitive if the corresponding concentration is maxitive.
In the following, we focus on the converse direction and analyze when a monetary risk measure can be represented as a convex integral.
Denote by
\bar{B}(E)
the set of all
f\in B(E)
such that
f
is bounded from above.
The following result was obtained in a slightly different setting in
[
5
, Corollary 6]
.
We provide a short proof in the present setting.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\phi\colon B(E)\to[-\infty,\infty]
be a maxitive monetary risk measure, and
J_{A}=\phi(-\infty 1_{A^{c}})
for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(A)
.
Then,
J
is a maxitive concentration, and

\phi(f)=\phi_{J}(f)

for all
f\in\bar{B}(E)
.

Proof.

For every function
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
that is bounded from above and every set
A\subset E
, we define

\bar{\phi}(f):=\underset{g\in B(E)\colon f\leq g}{\inf}\phi(g),\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\bar{J}_{A}:=\bar{\phi}(-\infty 1_{A^{c}}).

Inpection shows that
\bar{\phi}
is finitely maxitive and additively homogeneous in the sense of
[
5
]
.
Then, by
[
5
, Corollary 6]
, we have that

\bar{\phi}(f)=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+\bar{J}_{\{f\geq c\}}\}

for all
f
that are bounded from above.
In particular, for
f\in B(E)
, we have

{\phi}(f)=\bar{\phi}(f)=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+\bar{J}_{\{f\geq c\}}\}\\
=\underset{c\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{c+{J}_{\{f\geq c\}}\}=\phi_{J}(f).

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

Given a function
\phi\colon B(E)\to[-\infty,\infty]
, we define the sets

B_{\phi}(E):=\left\{f\in B(E)\colon\mbox{there exists }t>1\mbox{ such that }\phi(tf)<\infty\right\},

C_{\phi}(E):=\left\{f\in C(E)\colon\mbox{there exists }t>1\mbox{ such that }\phi(tf)<\infty\right\}.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\phi\colon B(E)\to[-\infty,\infty]
be a monetary risk measure.
If
f\in B_{\phi}(E)
, then

\underset{m\to\infty}{\lim}{\phi}\left(f1_{\{f\geq m\}}-\infty 1_{\{f<m\}}\right)=\underset{m\to\infty}{\lim}{\phi}\left(f1_{\{f>m\}}-\infty 1_{\{f\leq m\}}\right)=-\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in B_{\phi}(E)
, and take
t>1
such that
\phi(tf)<\infty
.
Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
and define
g=\exp(f-m)
.
Then, by translation invariance and monotonicity, we have

\displaystyle-m+\phi\left(f1_{\{f\geq m\}}-\infty 1_{\{f<m\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(-m+f1_{\{f\geq m\}}-\infty 1_{\{f<m\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(\log(g)1_{\{g\geq 1\}}-\infty 1_{\{g<1\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\phi\left(\log(g^{t})\right)

\displaystyle=\phi\left(t(f-m)\right)

\displaystyle=-mt+\phi\left(tf\right).

Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\underset{m\to\infty}{\lim}{\phi}\left(f1_{\{f>m\}}-\infty 1_{\{f\leq m\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{m\to\infty}{\lim}{\phi}\left(f1_{\{f\geq m\}}-\infty 1_{\{f<m\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{m\to\infty}{\lim}\Big(m(1-t)+\phi\left(tf\right)\Big)=-\infty,

where the latter limit is
-\infty
since
t>1
and
\phi\left(tf\right)<-\infty
. This completes the proof.

Proof.

We finally apply Theorem

Denote by

A function

A functional which satisfies the properties (1)-(4) is also called upper expectation in robust statistics
[

]

]

]

(a_{n}^{1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},(a_{n}^{2})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\ldots,(a_{n}^{N})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are
[0,\infty]
-valued sequences, then

(5.1)

\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{n}^{i}\leq\vee^{N}_{i=1}\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log a_{n}^{i},

(5.2)

\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{n}^{i}\leq\Big(\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\frac{1}{n}\log a_{n}^{1}\Big)\vee\Big(\vee_{i=2}^{N}\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log a_{n}^{i}\Big).

As a consequence, we have.

Lemma 5.3
.

\underline{\psi},\overline{\psi}
satisfy Assumption
4.1
.

Proof.

We prove that

\overline{\psi}(f)\leq\vee_{i=1}^{N}\overline{\psi}(g_{i})

for
f\in{U}(E)
, and
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}\in{L}(E)
with
f\leq\vee_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}
.
Indeed, as a consequence of Lemma
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}\left(e^{n(\vee_{i=1}^{N}g_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}e^{ng_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{E}_{n}\left(e^{ng_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\vee_{i=1}^{N}\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}\left(e^{ng_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\vee_{i=1}^{N}\overline{\psi}(g_{i}).

∎

Adopting the usual terminology of standard large deviations theory
[
12
]
, we introduce the following:

•

We say that the sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
exponentially tight
if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a compact set
K\subset E
such that

\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(K^{c})<-n.

Here,
\mu_{n}\colon\mathcal{B}(E)\to[0,1]
is the capacity associated with
\mathcal{E}_{n}
which is given by
\mu_{n}(A)=\mathcal{E}_{n}(1_{A})
.

•

We say that
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the
large deviation principle
(LDP) with rate function
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
if

\displaystyle-\underset{x\in{\rm int}(A)}{\inf}I(x)\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(A)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(A)\leq-\underset{x\in{\rm cl}(A)}{\inf}I(x)

for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(E)
.

•

We say that
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the
Laplace principle
(LP) with rate function
I\colon E\to[0,\infty]
if

(5.3)

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}(e^{nf})=\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}

for all
f\in C_{\overline{\psi}}(E)
.

Notice that the exponential tightness of
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is equivalent to the tightness of
\overline{J}
in the sense of Definition
2.5
.

We turn next to the main result of this section.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a distinguished nonempty set of continuous real-valued functions on
E
. Denote by
\mathscr{E}
the set of all
\mathcal{H}
-exposed points of
\overline{\psi}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}
which admit an exposing function
f\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\underline{\psi}(f)=\overline{\psi}(f)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathcal{E}_{n}(e^{nf})\quad\mbox{and}\quad f\in B_{\overline{\psi}}(E).

In view of Lemma
5.3
, we have the following result as a direct consequence of Theorem
4.7
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that the sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of sublinear expectations is exponentially tight.
Then:

(i)

For every closed set
C\subset E
, we have the upper bound

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(C)\leq-\underset{y\in C}{\inf}\overline{\psi}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x).

(ii)

For every open set
O\subset E
, we have the lower bound

-\underset{y\in O\cap\mathscr{E}}{\inf}\overline{\psi}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mu_{n}(O).

(iii)

If, moreover,

(5.4)

\underset{x\in O}{\inf}\overline{\psi}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x)=\underset{x\in O\cap{\mathscr{E}}}{\inf}\overline{\psi}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}(x)\quad\mbox{for all }O\subset E\mbox{ open, }

then
(\mathcal{E}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the LDP and LP with rate function
\overline{\psi}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\ast}
.

In the special case when
\mathcal{H}=E^{\ast}
is the dual space of a topological vector space
E
Theorem
5.4
above amounts to the well-known Gärtner-Ellis theorem; see
[
12

[

We have that Theorem

above is more flexible as it allows for arbitrary choices for

.
In the following example we show a case where the LDP and LP follow from Theorem

above, but it is not covered by Gärtner-Ellis theorem.

Example 5.5

For each

1/n

X_{n}

.

Equivalently,

Consider the lower/upper asymptotic entropies

\underline{\psi}(f)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\liminf}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{nf(X_{n})}],\quad\overline{\psi}(f)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{nf(X_{n})}].

For every

, define the compact set
K_{m}:=[-m,m]
.
Then,
\overline{J}_{K_{m}^{c}}=-m
. Hence,

is exponentially tight.
Consider

as in Gärtner-Ellis theorem

.
In that case, for every

\overline{\psi}^{\ast}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(x)=\underset{y\in\mathbb{R}}{\sup}\{yx-\overline{\psi}(y)\}=|x|.

Now, consider the set of continuous functions

\mathcal{H}_{2}:=\left\{f_{a}\colon a\geq 0\right\},

In contrast, by considering other types of exposing functions, for instance, inverted v-shaped functions as in this case, we can produce richer classes of exposing points and prove a LDP by means of Theorem

Although Gärtner-Ellis theorem does not capture the lower bound in the LDP, we may try other known methods to prove it.
Next, we briefly discuss some of these methods.
We note that, in this simple one-dimensional case, the limit representation
[

yields the rate function

, but we would still need to prove the existence of a LDP to apply this result.
Since

[
12

.
However, we need to verify that

Alternatively, as proven in

In contrast, our method leads to a family of inverted v-shaped functions for which is directly checked that

Remark 5.6
.

The main result of
[
28
]
is a version of Gärner Ellis theorem for sequences of sublinear expectations on a finite dimensional setting, which is proven by adapting the proof of the standard case.
This result is a particular instance of Theorem

for the special choices

As illustrated in Example

In addition, the authors of
[

]
consider sublinear expectations of the particular form

Also, among other restrictions it is assumed in

Appendix A
Pairs of monetary risk measures

As a consequence of the duality bounds provide in
[
21
]
(see Theorem
2.1
) and in line with
[
20
, Proposition 5.2]
we have the following.

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose that
\underline{\phi},\overline{\phi}
is a pair of monetary risk measures such that
\overline{\phi}
is weakly maxitive and
\underline{\phi}(f)\leq\overline{\phi}(f)
for all
f\in B(E)
.
If the pair
\underline{\phi},\overline{\phi}
satisfies the LDP with rate function
I(\cdot)
, then the pair
\underline{\phi},\overline{\phi}
satisfies the LP with rate function
I(\cdot)
.

Proof.

Suppose that the pair
\underline{\phi},\overline{\phi}
satisfies the LDP with rate function
I(\cdot)
, and fix
f\in C_{\overline{\phi}}(E)
. Since
\overline{\phi}
is weakly maxitive, due to Theorem
3.5
we have that
\phi_{\overline{J}}(f)=\overline{\phi}(f)
.
Then, by Theorem
2.1
, we have

(A.1)

\overline{\phi}(f)=\phi_{\overline{J}}(f)=\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}.

Now, given
x\in E
and
\delta>0
, since
f
is upper semicontinuous, there exists
U\in\mathcal{U}_{x}
such that
\inf_{y\in U}f(y)\geq f(x)-\delta
.
Then, by monotonicity and translation invariance

\displaystyle\underline{\phi}(f)

\displaystyle\geq\underline{\phi}(f1_{U}-\infty 1_{U^{c}})\geq f(x)-\delta+\underline{J}_{U}

\displaystyle\geq f(x)-\delta-\underset{y\in U}{\inf}I(y)\geq f(x)-\delta-I(x).

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
and taking the supremum over all
x\in E
also yields

\underline{\phi}(f)\geq\underset{x\in E}{\sup}\{f(x)-I(x)\}.

This along with (
A.1
) shows that the pair
\underline{\phi},\overline{\phi}
satisfies the LP with rate function
I(\cdot)
.
∎

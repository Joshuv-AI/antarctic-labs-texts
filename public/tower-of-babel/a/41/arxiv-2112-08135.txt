Multiple solutions for nonlinear boundary value problems of Kirchhoff type on a double phase setting
Alessio Fiscella, Greta Marino, Andrea Pinamonti, Simone Verzellesi

Abstract
. This paper deals with some classes of Kirchhoff type problems on a double phase setting and with nonlinear boundary conditions. Under general assumptions, we provide multiplicity results for such problems in the case when the perturbations exhibit a suitable behavior in the origin and at infinity, or when they do not necessarily satisfy the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition. To this aim, we combine variational methods, truncation arguments and topological tools.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study multiplicity results for some classes of double phase problems of Kirchhoff type with nonlinear boundary conditions. More precisely, we consider the problems

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-M\left[\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{p}}{p}+a(x)\frac{|\nabla u|^{q}}{q}\right)dx\right]\mbox{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)=h_{1}(x,u)\quad&&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\[4.73611pt]
&M\left[\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{p}}{p}+a(x)\frac{|\nabla u|^{q}}{q}\right)dx\right]\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nu=h_{2}(x,u)&&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

and

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-M\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)\Delta_{p}u-M\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}dx\right)\divergenz\left(a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)=h_{1}(x,u)\quad&&\text{in }\Omega,\\[4.30554pt]
&\left[M\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+M\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}dx\right)a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right]\cdot\nu=h_{2}(x,u)&&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
along the paper, and without further mentioning,

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N>1
, is a bounded domain with
C^{1,\alpha}
- boundary
\partial\Omega
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\nu(x)
is the outer unit normal of
\Omega
at the point
x\in\partial\Omega
,
1<p<q<N
, and

(1.3)

q<p^{*},\quad a\colon\overline{\Omega}\to[0,\infty)\text{ is in }L^{\infty}(\Omega),

with
p^{*}=Np/(N-p)
being the critical Sobolev exponent, see (
2.4
) for its definition.
We assume that
M\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a
continuous
function satisfying the following assumptions:

(M_{1})

there exists

\theta\geq 1

such that

tM(t)\leq\theta\mathscr{M}(t)

for all

t\in[0,\infty)
,

where

\mathscr{M}(t)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}M(\tau)\;d\tau
;

(M_{2})

for all

\tau>0

there exists

\kappa=\kappa(\tau)>0

such that

M(t)\geq\kappa

for all

t\geq\tau
.

Moreover,
h_{1}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
h_{2}\colon\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are
Carathéodory
functions whose properties will be specified in the sequel, case by case. A classical model for
M
verifying
(M_{1})
-
(M_{2})
due to Kirchhoff is given by
M(t)=a+bt^{\theta-1}
, where
a
,
b\geq 0
and
a+b>0
.

Problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are said of double phase type because of the presence of two different elliptic growths
p
and
q
. The study of double phase problems and related functionals originates from the seminal paper by Zhikov
[
28
]
, where he introduced the functional

(1.4)

u\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p}+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}\right)dx\quad\mbox{with }1<p<q,\quad a(\cdot)\geq 0,

in order to provide models for strongly anisotropic materials. Indeed, the weight coefficient
a(\cdot)
dictates the geometry of composites made of two different materials with distinct power hardening exponents
p
and
q
. From the mathematical point of view, (
1.4
) is a prototype of a functional whose integrands change their ellipticity according to the points where
a(\cdot)
vanishes or not. In this direction, Zhikov found other mathematical applications for (
1.4
) in the study of duality theory and of the Lavrentiev gap phenomenon, as shown in
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
. Furthermore, (
1.4
) falls into the class of functionals with non-standard growth conditions, according to Marcellini’s definition given in
[
22
,
23
]
. Following this line of research, Mingione et al. provide different regularity results for minimizers of (
1.4
), see
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
7
]
. In
[
5
]
, Colasuonno and Squassina analyze the eigenvalue problem with Dirichlet boundary condition of the double phase operator, explicitly appearing in (
1.1
), that is,

(1.5)

u\mapsto\mbox{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right),

whose energy functional is given by (
1.4
).

Starting from
[
5
]
, several authors studied existence and multiplicity results for nonlinear problems driven by (
1.5
), such as in
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
26
]
with the help of different variational techniques. In particular, in
[
14
]
Fiscella and Pinamonti provide existence and multiplicity results for Kirchhoff double phase problems but with Dirichlet boundary condition. That is, in
[
14
]
we have

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-M\left[\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{p}}{p}+a(x)\frac{|\nabla u|^{q}}{q}\right)dx\right]\mbox{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)=f(x,u)&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{in }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-M\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)\Delta_{p}u-M\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}dx\right)\mbox{div}\left(a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)=f(x,u)&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\mbox{in }\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where the nonlinear subcritical term

satisfies the classical Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz (AR) condition, crucial to prove the boundedness of the so-called Palais-Smale sequences.

The aim of the present paper is to provide different multiplicity results for some classes of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), that will heavily depend on the properties of the involved nonlinearities. More specifically, in the first part of the work, we will study existence of at least two constant sign solutions (more precisely, one nonnegative and one nonpositive), under the assumption that the nonlinearities satisfy some strong conditions such as the superlinearity at
\pm\infty
. These results are inspired by the truncation arguments used in
[
10
]
, where the authors studied existence of constant sign solutions in a double phase setting but without the Kirchhoff coefficient, namely, with
M\equiv 1
. However, the presence of the nonlocal Kirchhoff coefficient in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) makes the comparison analysis more intriguing than
[
10
]
. For this, our multiplicity result will depend on the value
M(0)
, beside the first eigenvalue of the Robin eigenvalue problem for the
p
-Laplacian, as required in
[
10
]
. Other results for existence of solutions with sign information in a double phase setting can be found, for example, in
[
16
,
25
,
15
]
.

In the second part of the article we will study multiplicity of solutions in the case when the nonlinearities on the right hand side do not necessarily satisfy the (AR) condition. In this direction, we can mention
[
17
,
18
]
where they consider problems (
1.1
) and (
1.6
) with
M\equiv 1
, namely without the Kirchhoff coefficient. In
[
18
]
, Ge et al. exploit the classical mountain pass theorem and the Krasnoselskii’s genus theory in order to prove existence and multiplicity results for (
1.6
), replacing the (AR) condition with

(1.7)

\mathcal{F}(x,t)\leq\mathcal{F}(x,s)+C_{0},\quad\mbox{where }\mathcal{F}(x,t)=tf(x,t)-qF(x,t)\mbox{ and }C_{0}>0

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
,
0<t<s
or
s<t<0
, where
F
is the primitive of
f
with respect the second variable. On the other hand, in
[
17
]
Gasiński and Winkert prove the existence of two solutions of (
1.1
) with
h_{1}(x,t)=f(x,t)-|t|^{p-2}t-a(x)|t|^{q-2}t
, with the following quasi-monotonic assumption for
f

(1.8)

t\mapsto tf(x,t)-qF(x,t)\mbox{ is nondecreasing in }\mathbb{R}^{+}\mbox{ and nonincreasing in }\mathbb{R}^{-},

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Because of the presence of a nonlocal Kirchhoff coefficient
M
in (
1.1
), similar hypothesis to (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) can not work, even if we assume a monotonic assumption for
t\mapsto\theta\mathscr{M}(t)-M(t)t
. This is a consequence of the fact that
M
in (
1.1
) does not depend on the norm of
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
, that is the functional space where we look for the solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). Indeed, as shown in detail in Section
2
,
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
is a suitable Musielak-Orlicz Sobolev space endowed with a norm of Luxemburg type. For this reason, we need different conditions for the nonlinearities in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) than in
[
17
,
18
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the main properties of Musielak-Orlicz Sobolev spaces
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)

and we state some technical lemmas concerning the Kirchhoff coefficient
M
. In Section
3
, for each problem we prove the existence of two constant sign solutions, more precisely one nonnegative and one nonpositive, combining the ideas of
[
10
,
14
]
. In order to show the results, we require some strong conditions on the nonlinearities, like the superlinearity at
\pm\infty
. We also point out that the proofs depend on the first eigenvalue of the Robin eigenvalue problem for the
p
-Laplacian.
In the last part of the paper, namely Section
4
, we show the existence of infinitely many solutions to a class of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). In particular, we introduce suitable assumptions for the nonlinearities in order to avoid the (AR) condition.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce the basic notation of our paper, the functional space where we find the solutions to (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and we present some technical lemmas for

For all

and

. The corresponding Sobolev space is denoted by

, endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{1,r}
.
On the boundary
\partial\Omega
of

we consider the
(N-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\sigma
and denote by

the boundary Lebesgue space with corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|_{r,\partial\Omega}

As matter of notations, for all

we set
t^{\pm}:=\max\{\pm t,0\}
and similarly we denote by

the positive and negative parts of a function

The function
\mathcal{H}\colon\Omega\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
defined as

\mathcal{H}(x,t):=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q}\quad\mbox{for a.e. }x\in\Omega\mbox{ and for all }t\in[0,\infty),

with

and

, is a generalized N-function (N stands for

), according to the definition in
[

,
24
]
, and satisfies the so-called

condition, that is,

\mathcal{H}(x,2t)\leq 2^{q}\mathcal{H}(x,t)\quad\mbox{for a.e. }x\in\Omega\mbox{ and for all }t\in[0,\infty).

Then, we introduce the
\mathcal{H}
-modular function
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}
given by

(2.1)

\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{H}(x,|u|)\;dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(|u|^{p}+a(x)|u|^{q}\right)dx.

Therefore, by
[
24
]
we can define the Musielak-Orlicz space

as

L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}\,\bigl|\,u\mbox{ is measurable and }\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)<\infty\right\},

endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\,\,\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

From
[
5
,
9
]
, the space

is a separable, uniformly convex, Banach space.
On the other hand, from
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
we have the following relation between the norm

and the
\mathcal{H}
-modular function.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that

,
(u_{j})_{j}\subset L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
, and

. Then,

(i)

If
u\neq 0
, then
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}=c
if and only if
\displaystyle\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\frac{u}{c}\right)=1
;

(ii)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}<1

(resp.=1,\,>1)
if and only if
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)<1

(resp.=1,\,>1)
;

(iii)

If
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}<1

(iv)

If

We also define the complete

-modular function

Hence, following the proof of

we can relate

Assume that

,

. Then,

\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}=c

\displaystyle\widehat{\varrho}_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\frac{u}{c}\right)=1

\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}<1

, under the assumptions that

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be such that

{A_{1}}

\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)M[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\geq{A_{1}}\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p},

We first prove (

Since

First suppose that

Suppose now that

\frac{\kappa}{2}\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u),&\mbox{if $\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{p}<1/4$ and $\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{q,a}\geq 1/4$},\end{cases}

with

given in

with

Combining (

Lemma 2.6

Let

{B_{1}}
,
{B_{2}}>0
such that

(2.16)

\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq{B_{1}}(1+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q}+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta})

and

(2.17)

\mathscr{M}(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p})+\mathscr{M}(\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q})+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq{B_{2}}(1+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q}+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}).

Proof.

Let us fix
u\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
such that
\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}>1
. We first prove (
2.16
).

If
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\geq q
, then
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\geq\frac{1}{q}\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\geq 1
, and so by (
2.6
) and Proposition
2.1
, we get

(2.18)

\begin{split}\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]\leq\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{M}(1)}{p^{\theta}}[\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]^{\theta}\leq\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{M}(1)}{p^{\theta}}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}.\end{split}

If conversely
\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\leq q
, then
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\leq\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\leq q
. Therefore

(2.19)

\begin{split}\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]\leq\displaystyle\mathcal{M},\end{split}

where
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\max_{t\in[0,q]}\mathscr{M}(t)\in(0,\infty)
by
(M_{2})
and the continuity of
M
.
Therefore, combining (
2.18
), (
2.19
) with Proposition
2.1
we obtain

\begin{split}\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)&\leq\mathcal{M}+\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{M}(1)}{p^{\theta}}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq\mathcal{M}+\displaystyle\mathscr{M}(1)\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q}.\end{split}

In order to prove (
2.17
) we observe that, since
\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}>1
, from Proposition
2.2
we have that
\widehat{\varrho}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q}
.
We distinguish four cases. If
\|\nabla u\|_{p}\geq 1
and
\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}\geq 1
, then

(2.20)

\begin{split}\mathscr{M}(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p})+\mathscr{M}(\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q})+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)&\leq\mathscr{M}(1)\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p\theta}+\mathscr{M}(1)\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q\theta}+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\\
&\leq 2\mathscr{M}(1)\widehat{\varrho}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)^{\theta}+\widehat{\varrho}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\\
&\leq\max\{1,2\mathscr{M}(1)\}(\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q}).\end{split}

If
\|\nabla u\|_{p}<1
and
\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}\geq 1
it holds that

(2.21)

\begin{split}\mathscr{M}(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p})+\mathscr{M}(\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q})+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)&\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}+\mathscr{M}(1)\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q\theta}+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\\
&\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}+\mathscr{M}(1)\widehat{\varrho}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)^{\theta}+\widehat{\varrho}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\\
&\leq\max\{1,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},\mathscr{M}(1)\}(1+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q}),\end{split}

with
\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}=\max_{t\in[0,1]}\mathscr{M}(t)
.
If
\|\nabla u\|_{p}\geq 1
and
\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}<1
the estimate is the same as above. Finally, when
\|\nabla u\|_{p}<1
and
\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}<1
, it holds that

(2.22)

\mathscr{M}(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p})+\mathscr{M}(\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q})+\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq 2\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}+\widehat{\varrho}_{\mathcal{H}}(u)\leq\max\{2\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},1\}(1+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q}).

Combining (
2.20
)-(
2.22
) we get (
2.17
).
∎

We now recall some basic properties on the spectrum of the negative
p
-Laplacian with Robin boundary condition. We refer to the paper of L
\hat{\text{e}}

[
19
]
for further details. The
p
-Laplacian eigenvalue problem with Robin boundary condition is given by

(2.23)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p}u&=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u\quad&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nu&=-\beta|u|^{p-2}u&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

with
\beta>0
.
It is well known that there exists a smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{1,p}>0
of (
2.23
) which is isolated, simple, and can be variationally characterized by

(2.24)

\lambda_{1,p}=\inf_{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),u\neq 0}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p}d\sigma}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}dx}.

Moreover, let
u_{1,p}
be the normalized (that is,
\|u_{1,p}\|_{p}=1
) positive eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1,p}
. It is known that
u_{1,p}\in\text{int}\left(C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})_{+}\right)
, where

\text{int}\left(C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})_{+}\right):=\left\{u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}):\,u(x)>0\text{ for all }x\in\overline{\Omega}\right\}.

We conclude this section with a result which will enable us to obtain the existence of infinitely many solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).
To this aim, let
X
be a Banach space. We recall that a functional
E\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the
Cerami condition

(C)
if every sequence
(u_{j})_{j}\subset X
such that

(2.25)

(E(u_{j}))_{j}\mbox{ is bounded and }(1+\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}})E^{\prime}(u_{j})\to 0\mbox{ in }X^{*}\mbox{ as }j\rightarrow\infty

admits a convergent subsequence in
X
; see for instance
[
3
]
. We say that
(u_{j})_{j}
is a Cerami sequence for
E
if it satisfies (
2.25
).

Let us now suppose that
X
is a reflexive and separable Banach space. It is well known that there exist
(e_{j})_{j}\subset X
and
(e^{*}_{j})_{j}\subset X^{*}
such that

(2.26)

X=\overline{\mathrm{span}\{e_{j}:\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}}\quad\text{as well as}\quad X^{*}=\overline{\mathrm{span}\{e_{j}^{*}:\,j\in\mathbb{N}\}}

and

(2.27)

\langle e_{i}^{*},e_{j}\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

For all
j\in\mathbb{N}
we set

(2.28)

X_{j}:=\mathrm{span}\{e_{j}\},\qquad Y_{j}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{j}X_{i},\qquad Z_{j}:=\bigoplus_{i=j}^{\infty}X_{i}.

We can then state the following result, given in
[
20
, Theorem 2.9]
, which is a variant of the classical Fountain theorem
[
27
, Theorem 3.6]
for functionals that satisfy the Cerami condition instead of the Palais-Smale condition.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
E\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfy the Cerami condition
(C)
and be such that
E(-u)=E(u)
. Moreover, suppose that for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
\rho_{j}>\gamma_{j}>0
such that

(i)

\displaystyle b_{j}:=\inf_{u\in Z_{j},\|u\|=\gamma_{j}}E(u)\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
,

(ii)

\displaystyle a_{j}:=\max_{u\in Y_{j},\|u\|=\rho_{j}}E(u)\leq 0
.

Then,
E
has a sequence of critical points
(u_{j})_{j}
such that
E(u_{j})\to\infty
.

3.
Constant sign solutions

In this section we prove the existence of constant-sign solutions to a class of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with superlinear nonlinearities. More specifically, we consider

(3.1)

\displaystyle h_{1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=(\zeta-\vartheta)|t|^{p-2}t-a(x)|t|^{q-2}t-f(x,t)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega,

\displaystyle h_{2}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\beta|t|^{p-2}t

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega,

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\beta>0
,
\zeta>\vartheta>0
are parameters to be further specified, and

is a Carathéodory function that satisfies suitable structure conditions stated below. Then problem (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-M\left[\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{p}}{p}+a(x)\frac{|\nabla u|^{q}}{q}\right)dx\right]\mbox{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\\
&\qquad=(\zeta-\vartheta)|u|^{p-2}u-a(x)|u|^{q-2}u-f(x,u)\quad&&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
&M\left[\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{p}}{p}+a(x)\frac{|\nabla u|^{q}}{q}\right)dx\right]\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nu=-\beta|u|^{p-2}u&&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

We assume that the nonlinearity
f\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
Carathéodory
function such that

(
f_{1}
)

f
is bounded on bounded subsets of
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
;

(
f_{2}
)

It holds

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{f(x,t)}{|t|^{q-2}t}=\infty\quad

\displaystyle\text{uniformly for a.e. }x\in\Omega;

(
f_{3}
)

It holds

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(x,t)}{|t|^{p-2}t}=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{uniformly for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

A classical model for
f
satisfying
(f_{1})
-
(f_{3})
is given by
f(x,t):=w(x)|t|^{k-2}t
, where
k>q
and
w\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\inf_{\Omega}w>0
.
We say that
u\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
3.2
) if it satisfies

\begin{split}&M\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\right]\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\;dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(\vartheta|u|^{p-2}u+a(x)|u|^{q-2}u\right)\varphi\;dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\left(\zeta|u|^{p-2}u-f(x,u)\right)\varphi\;dx-\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\varphi\;d\sigma\end{split}

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
.

Our existence result for problem (
3.2
) reads as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let (
1.3
),
(M_{1})
-
(M_{2})
, and
(f_{1})
-
(f_{3})
hold true. Moreover, assume that
\vartheta>0
and

(3.3)

\zeta>\vartheta+\max\{1,M(0)\}\lambda_{1,p},

where
\lambda_{1,p}
is the first eigenvalue of the Robin eigenvalue problem given in (
2.24
).
Then, there exist two nontrivial weak solutions
\widetilde{u}
,
\underline{u}\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
to problem (
3.2
), such that
\widetilde{u}\geq 0
and
\underline{u}\leq 0
.

Proof.

First of all we observe that hypothesis
(f_{2})
allows us to find a constant
A=A(\zeta)>1
such that

(3.4)

\displaystyle f(x,t)t\geq\zeta|t|^{q}\quad

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega\text{ and all }|t|\geq A.

We start with the existence of a nonnegative solution of (
3.2
). From (
3.4
) we can take a constant function
u_{0}\in(A,\infty)
and use the fact that
p<q
to achieve

(3.5)

\zeta u_{0}^{p-1}-f(x,u_{0})\leq 0\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

Moreover we consider the cut-off functions
b^{+}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
b_{\beta}^{+}\colon\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(3.6)

b^{+}(x,t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }t<0\\
\zeta t^{p-1}-f(x,t)&\text{if }0\leq t<u_{0}\\
\zeta u_{0}^{p-1}-f(x,u_{0})&\text{if }t\geq u_{0}\end{cases},\qquad b_{\beta}^{+}(x,t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }t<0\\
-\beta t^{p-1}&\text{if }0\leq t<u_{0}\\
-\beta u_{0}^{p-1}&\text{if }t\geq u_{0}\end{cases},

and set

(3.7)

B^{+}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}b^{+}(x,s)\;ds\quad\text{as well as}\quad B_{\beta}^{+}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}b_{\beta}^{+}(x,s)\;ds,\quad\text{and}\quad F(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)\;ds.

It is easy to verify that the functions in (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) are Carathéodory functions.
Moreover we consider the
C^{1}
-functional
J^{+}\colon W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

(3.8)

J^{+}(u)=\mathscr{M}\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\right]+\frac{\vartheta}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q,a}^{q}-\int_{\Omega}B^{+}(x,u)\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}B_{\beta}^{+}(x,u)\;d\sigma.

We aim to apply the direct methods of the calculus of variations to
J^{+}
. To this end, we first show that
J^{+}
is coercive. Indeed, let
u\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
be such that
\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}>1
. Using hypothesis
(M_{1})
and taking into account that
p<q
we have

\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]\geq\frac{1}{\theta}M[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\geq\frac{1}{q\theta}M[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u).

Furthermore, we see that, thanks to the truncations (
3.6
) and hypothesis
(f_{1})
, we have that the last two terms in (
3.8
) are bounded.
These facts together with (
2.8
) yield that
J^{+}
is coercive.

We then show that
J^{+}
is also (sequentially weakly) lower semicontinuous. Indeed, let us take
u\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
and
(u_{j})_{j}\subset W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
such that
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
.
By Propositions
2.1
-
2.3
and
[
4
, Theorem 4.9]
there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
(u_{j})_{j}
, such that, as

,

\begin{gathered}u_{j}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)\cap L^{q}_{a}(\Omega),\qquad\nabla u_{j}\rightharpoonup\nabla u\mbox{ in }\left[L^{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\right]^{N},\qquad\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})\to\ell,\\
u_{j}(x)\to u(x)\mbox{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega,\qquad u_{j}(x)\to u(x)\mbox{ for $\sigma$-a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega.\end{gathered}

We use the fact that
\mathscr{M}
is increasing and continuous,
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}
is sequentially weakly lower semicontinuous, and
B^{+}
and
B^{+}_{\beta}
are Carathéodory functions bounded from below to apply the Fatou’s lemma and achieve

\begin{split}J^{+}(u)&\leq\mathscr{M}\left[\lim_{j\to\infty}\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})\right]+\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{p}\|u_{j}\|^{p}_{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u_{j}\|^{q}_{q,a}\right)\\
&\qquad\quad-\int_{\Omega}\lim_{j\to\infty}B(x,u_{j})\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}\lim_{j\to\infty}B^{+}_{\beta}(x,u_{j})\;d\sigma\\
&\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})]+\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{p}\|u_{j}\|^{p}_{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u_{j}\|^{q}_{q,a}\right)-\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}B(x,u_{j})\;dx\\
&\qquad\quad-\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}B^{+}_{\beta}(x,u_{j})\;d\sigma\\
&\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}J^{+}(u_{j}).\end{split}

Therefore, there exists a function
\widetilde{u}\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
such that

(3.9)

J^{+}(\widetilde{u})=\inf\left\{J^{+}(u):\,u\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\right\}.

Let us verify that
\widetilde{u}
is not trivial. First of all, thanks to hypothesis
(f_{3})
, for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

(3.10)

F(x,t)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{p}|t|^{p}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega\text{ and for all }|t|\leq\delta.

We further define the function
h\colon[0,\infty)\to[M(0),\infty)
such that

(3.11)

h(t)=\begin{cases}\,M(0)&\text{ if }t=0\\
\displaystyle\max_{s\in(0,t]}\frac{\mathscr{M}(s)}{s}&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}.

It is easy to see that
h
is well-defined and continuous. These facts, together with (
3.3
), guarantee that there exists
t_{1}\in(0,1)
such that

(3.12)

\zeta>\vartheta+\max\{1,h(t_{1})\}\lambda_{1,p}.

Let
u_{1,p}
be the normalized eigenfunction, that is
\|u_{1,p}\|_{p}=1
, corresponding to
\lambda_{1,p}
. Since
u_{1,p}\in\text{int}\left(C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})_{+}\right)
we can choose

for all

, which implies that

tu_{1,p}\in(0,u_{0})\qquad\text{for all }x\in\overline{\Omega}\text{ and }t\in(0,t_{2}].

Moreover, let us choose

\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla(tu_{1,p}))\leq t_{1}\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,t_{3}]$}.

Taking (

) implies that

\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla(tu_{1,p}))]\leq h(t_{1})\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla(tu_{1,p}))

Then we use the truncations in (

\begin{split}J^{+}({t}u_{1,p})&=\mathscr{M}\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla({t}u_{1,p}))\right]+\frac{\vartheta}{p}\|{t}u_{1,p}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|{t}u_{1,p}\|_{q,a}^{q}\\

for every

. Since

-\overline{t}^{p}\left(\frac{\zeta-\vartheta-\max\left\{1,h(t_{1})\right\}\lambda_{1,p}-\varepsilon}{p}\right)+\overline{t}^{q}\left(\frac{h(t_{1})\|\nabla u_{1,p}\|_{q,a}^{q}+\|u_{1,p}\|_{q,a}^{q}}{q}\right)<0,

We now choose

\begin{split}&M\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla\widetilde{u})\right]\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\widetilde{u}+a(x)|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{q-2}\nabla\widetilde{u}\right)\cdot\nabla(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})^{+}\;dx\\

&\leq 0,\end{split}

where the last inequality holds by (

(3.20)

\begin{split}&M\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla\widetilde{u})\right]\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{p-2}\nabla\widetilde{u}+a(x)|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{q-2}\nabla\widetilde{u}\right)\cdot\nabla(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})^{+}\;dx\\
&=M\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla\widetilde{u})\right]\int_{\{\widetilde{u}>u_{0}\}}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{p}+a(x)|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{q}\;dx,\\
\end{split}

while on the other hand, exploiting the fact that
\widetilde{u}>u_{0}>1
, we get

(3.21)

\begin{split}0&\geq\int_{\Omega}(\vartheta\widetilde{u}^{p-1}+a(x)\widetilde{u}^{q-1})(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})^{+}dx\\
&=\int_{\{\widetilde{u}>u_{0}\}}\vartheta\widetilde{u}^{p-1}(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})+a(x)\widetilde{u}^{q-1}(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})\;dx\\
&\geq\int_{\{\widetilde{u}>u_{0}\}}\vartheta(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})^{p}+a(x)(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})^{q}\;dx.\end{split}

Gathering (
3.19
), (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), we see that

\begin{split}M\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla\widetilde{u})\right]&\int_{\{\widetilde{u}>u_{0}\}}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{p}+a(x)|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{q}\;dx+\int_{\{\widetilde{u}>u_{0}\}}\vartheta(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})^{p}a(x)(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})^{q}\;dx\leq 0,\end{split}

which gives
(\widetilde{u}-u_{0})^{+}=0
, and therefore
\widetilde{u}\leq u_{0}
. This fact in particular implies that
\widetilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
By definition of the truncations (
3.6
),
\widetilde{u}
turns out to be a weak solution to (
3.2
).

In order to show the existence of a nonpositive solution, we first fix the constant function
u_{1}\equiv-u_{0}
and use equation (
3.4
) and the fact that
p<q
to achieve

\zeta|u_{1}|^{p-2}u_{1}-f(x,u_{1})\geq 0\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

Then we consider the cut-off, Carathéodory functions
b^{-}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
b^{-}_{\beta}\colon\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(3.22)

b^{-}(x,t)=\begin{cases}\zeta|u_{1}|^{p-2}u_{1}-f(x,u_{1})&\text{if }t\leq u_{1}\\
\zeta|t|^{p-2}t-f(x,t)&\text{if }u_{1}<t\leq 0\\
0&\text{if }t>0\end{cases},\qquad b_{\beta}^{-}(x,t)=\begin{cases}-\beta|u_{1}|^{p-2}u_{1}&\text{if }t\leq u_{1}\\
-\beta|t|^{p-2}t&\text{if }u_{1}<t\leq 0\\
0&\text{if }t>0\end{cases},

set

(3.23)

B^{-}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}b^{-}(x,s)\;ds\quad\text{as well as}\quad B_{\beta}^{-}(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}b_{\beta}^{-}(x,s)\;ds,

and consider the
C^{1}
-functional
J^{-}\colon W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

J^{-}(u)=\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)]+\frac{\vartheta}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q,a}^{q}-\int_{\Omega}B^{-}(x,u)\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}B_{\beta}^{-}(x,u)\;d\sigma.

Reasoning in a similar fashion as before we find a global minimizer
\underline{u}\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
of
J^{-}
such that
\underline{u}\in[u_{1},0]
.
By definition of the truncations (
3.22
) we see that
\underline{u}
is a nonpositive weak solution of problem (
3.2
).
The proof is complete.
∎

Now, we consider problem (
1.2
) with
h_{1}
,
h_{2}
as in (
3.1
). Then we get

(3.24)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-M\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)\Delta_{p}u-M\left(\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q}dx\right)\divergenz\left(a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=(\zeta-\vartheta)|u|^{p-2}u-a(x)|u|^{q-2}u-f(x,u)\qquad&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
&\left[M(\|\nabla u\|^{p}_{p})|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+M(\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{q,a})a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right]\cdot\nu=\beta|u|^{p-2}u\qquad&&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

We say that
u\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
3.24
) if

\begin{split}&M\left(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\;dx+M\left(\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q}\right)\int_{\Omega}a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\;dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\left(\vartheta|u|^{p-2}u+a(x)|u|^{q-2}u\right)dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(\vartheta|u|^{p-2}u-f(x,u)\right)\varphi\;dx-\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}|u|^{p-2}u\varphi\;d\sigma\end{split}

holds for all
\varphi\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
. The existence result concerning problem (
3.24
) reads as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

In the same hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
, there exist two nontrivial weak solutions
\widetilde{u}
,
\underline{u}\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
to problem (
3.24
), such that
\widetilde{u}\geq 0
and
\underline{u}\leq 0
.

Proof.

As before, we start with the nonnegative solution, following the proof of Theorem
3.1
until (
3.7
). Then we consider the
C^{1}
-functional
\mathcal{J}^{+}\colon W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}^{+}(u)&=\frac{1}{p}\mathscr{M}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}\right)+\frac{1}{q}\mathscr{M}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q}\right)+\frac{\vartheta}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q,a}^{q}-\int_{\Omega}B^{+}(x,u)\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}B_{\beta}^{+}(x,u)\;d\sigma.\end{split}

Arguing as in the previous proof and thanks to (
2.9
) we have that
\mathcal{J}^{+}
is coercive and lower semicontinuous.
Therefore, there exists
\widetilde{u}\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
such that

\mathcal{J}^{+}(\widetilde{u})=\inf\{\mathcal{J}^{+}(u):\,u\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\}.

In order to show that
\widetilde{u}
is nontrivial, we let
u_{1,p}\in\text{int}\left(C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})_{+}\right)
be the normalized eigenfunction corresponding to the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1,p}
of problem (
2.23
), and choose
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
as in (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), respectively. Moreover, we choose
t_{3}\in(0,t_{2}]
such that

\|\nabla(tu_{1,p})\|_{p}^{p},\,\|\nabla(tu_{1,p})\|_{q,a}^{q}\leq t_{1}\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,t_{3}]$},

and finally
\overline{t}
and
\varepsilon>0
in order to satisfy (
3.16
). It follows that

\mathcal{J}^{+}(\widetilde{u})\leq\mathcal{J}^{+}(\overline{t}u_{1,p})<0=\mathcal{J}^{+}(0),

therefore
\widetilde{u}\not\equiv 0
. The proof that
0\leq\widetilde{u}\leq u_{0}
works as in the previous case, and thus
\widetilde{u}
is a weak, nonnegative, and bounded solution to (
3.24
).

In order to find the nonpositive solution we consider the functional
\mathcal{J}^{-}\colon W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}^{-}(u)&=\frac{1}{p}\mathscr{M}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}\right)+\frac{1}{q}\mathscr{M}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{q,a}^{q}\right)+\frac{\vartheta}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q,a}^{q}-\int_{\Omega}B^{-}(x,u)\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}B_{\beta}^{-}(x,u)\;d\sigma,\end{split}

with
B^{-}
and
B_{\beta}^{-}
given as in (
3.22
), (
3.23
), and reason as in the previous case. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We conclude this section pointing out that Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
generalize
[
10
, Theorem 4.2]
in a nonlocal Kirchhoff framework. In any case, assumption (
3.3
) is consistent with the constraint for
\zeta
requested in
[
10
]
, when
M\equiv 1
.

4.
Infinitely many solutions

In this section we prove the existence of infinitely many solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) when
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are symmetric. Throughout this section and differently from the previous one,
\partial\Omega
is assumed to be only Lipschitz. More precisely, we choose

(4.1)

\displaystyle h_{1}(x,t)

\displaystyle=f(x,t)-|t|^{p-2}t-a(x)|t|^{q-2}t\quad

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega,\,\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle h_{2}(x,t)

\displaystyle=g(x,t)

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega,\,\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

where
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are two
Carathéodory
functions that satisfy the following assumptions:

(h_{1})

there exist exponents
r_{1}\in(p,p^{*})
and
r_{2}\in(p,p_{*})
, and two constants
c_{1}
,
c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(x,t)|\leq c_{1}\left(1+|t|^{r_{1}-1}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega\text{ and all }t\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle|g(x,t)|\leq c_{2}\left(1+|t|^{r_{2}-1}\right)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega\text{ and all }t\in\mathbb{R};

(h_{2})

F(x,t)\geq 0
and
G(x,t)\geq 0
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and
x\in\partial\Omega
, respectively, and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
where

F(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}f(x,s)\;ds\quad\text{as well as}\quad G(x,t):=\int_{0}^{t}g(x,s)\;ds.

Moreover it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{|t|\to\infty}\frac{F(x,t)}{|t|^{q\theta}}=\infty\quad

\displaystyle\text{uniformly for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

(h_{3})

It holds that

\mathcal{F}(x,t):=\frac{1}{q\theta}f(x,t)t-F(x,t)\geq 0\quad\text{as well as}\quad\mathcal{G}(x,t):=\frac{1}{q\theta}g(x,t)t-G(x,t)\geq 0

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega
, respectively, and for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(h_{4})

There exist
t_{0}>0
,
d_{1}
,
d_{2}>0
,
s_{1}>N/p
and
s_{2}>(N-1)/(p-1)
such that

\displaystyle F(x,t)^{s_{1}}\leq d_{1}|t|^{ps_{1}}\mathcal{F}(x,t)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega,\,\text{ for all }|t|>t_{0},

\displaystyle G(x,t)^{s_{2}}\leq d_{2}|t|^{ps_{2}}\mathcal{G}(x,t)\quad

\displaystyle\text{for a.e. }x\in\partial\Omega,\,\text{ for all }|t|>t_{0},

(h_{5})

f(x,-t)=-f(x,t)
and
g(x,-t)=-g(x,t)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and a.e.
x\in\partial\Omega
, respectively, and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 4.1
.

When
\theta<p_{*}/q
, two simple model functions
f
and
g
satisfying
(h_{1})
–
(h_{5})
are given by
f(x,t)=w(x)|t|^{r_{1}-2}t
and
g(x,t)=z(x)|t|^{r_{2}-2}t
, where
w\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\inf_{\Omega}w>0
and
z\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
with
\inf_{\partial\Omega}z>0
, and with parameters
t_{0}>0
and

(4.2)

\begin{gathered}s_{1}\in\left(\frac{N}{p},\frac{q\theta}{q\theta-p}\right),\qquad r_{1}\in\left(q\theta,\frac{ps_{1}}{s_{1}-1}\right),\qquad d_{1}=\frac{1}{r_{1}^{s_{1}}}\cdot\frac{\|w\|_{\infty}^{s_{1}}}{\inf_{\Omega}w}\cdot\frac{r_{1}q\theta}{r_{1}-q\theta},\\
s_{2}\in\left(\frac{N-1}{p-1},\frac{q\theta}{q\theta-p}\right),\qquad r_{2}\in\left(q\theta,\frac{ps_{2}}{s_{2}-1}\right),\qquad d_{2}=\frac{1}{r_{2}^{s_{2}}}\cdot\frac{\|z\|_{\infty,\partial\Omega}^{s_{2}}}{\inf_{\partial\Omega}z}\cdot\frac{r_{2}q\theta}{r_{2}-q\theta}.\end{gathered}

We observe that such parameters in (
4.2
) exist since
\theta<p_{*}/q
. Moreover, it can be easily seen that this restriction on the choice of
\theta
is necessary if we want to have models of this form.

With the choice (
4.1
) we have that problem (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-M\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\right]\mbox{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)=f(x,u)-|u|^{p-2}u-a(x)|u|^{q-2}u\quad&&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
&M\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\right]\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nu=g(x,u)&&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

We say that
u\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
4.3
) if it satisfies

\begin{split}&M\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\right]\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u+a(x)|\nabla u|^{q-2}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\;dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(|u|^{p-2}u+a(x)|u|^{q-2}u\right)\varphi\;dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)\varphi\;dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}g(x,u)\varphi\;d\sigma\end{split}

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
.
We see that (
4.3
) is the Euler-Lagrange equation corresponding to the functional
I:W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
defined as

I(u):=\mathscr{M}\left[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\right]+\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q,a}^{q}-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u)\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}G(x,u)\;d\sigma.

Now, we are ready to state the existence result to (
4.3
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let (
1.3
),
(M_{1})
-
(M_{2})
, and
(h_{1})
-
(h_{5})
hold true.
Then, problem (
4.3
) has infinitely many weak solutions
(u_{j})_{j}
with unbounded energy.

Theorem
4.2
generalizes
[
17
, Theorem 5.9]
where problem (
4.3
) was considered with
M\equiv 1
, namely without Kirchhoff coefficient. Here, the function
f
satisfies the crucial assumption
(h_{4})
, different from the quasi-monotonic assumption (
1.8
) assumed in
[
17
]
. Indeed, (
1.8
) can not work for problem (
4.3
), even if
M
satisfies some monotonic condition. This is due to the fact that
M
in (
4.3
) depends on
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)
given in (
2.7
) and not on the seminorm
\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. Also, we do not have a proper equivalence between
\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)
and
\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, but only the following relation

\frac{1}{q}\min\{\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p},\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\}\leq\frac{1}{q}\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\leq\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\leq\frac{1}{p}\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u)\leq\frac{1}{p}\max\{\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{p},\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{q}\}

implied by Proposition
2.1
.

We aim to prove Theorem
4.2
by means of Theorem
2.7
. To this end, we first show that
I
satisfies the
(C)
condition and then that assumptions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
2.7
are satisfied. We start with the following preliminary result.

Lemma 4.3
.

Any Cerami sequence
(u_{j})_{j}
of
I
is bounded in
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{j})_{j}
be a sequence satisfying (
2.25
) with
E=I
.
Hence, there exist
C>0
and
\varepsilon_{j}>0
, with
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, such that

(4.4)

|\langle I^{\prime}(u_{j}),\varphi\rangle|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{j}\|\varphi\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}}{1+\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}}\quad\mbox{for all }\varphi\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }j\in\mathbb{N}

and

(4.5)

|I(u_{j})|\leq C\quad\mbox{for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

We claim that
(u_{j})_{j}
is bounded in
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
.

Arguing by contradiction, we assume that
\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty

and, without loss of generality, that
\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}>1
for
j
sufficiently large.
Set
v_{j}:=u_{j}/\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}
. It holds that
\|v_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}=1
, therefore there exists
v\in W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

(4.6)

\displaystyle v_{j}(x)\to v(x)\mbox{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega,\qquad v_{j}\to v\mbox{ in }L^{\nu_{1}}(\Omega)\cap L^{\nu_{2}}(\partial\Omega)

for all
\nu_{1}\in[1,p^{*})
and all
\nu_{2}\in[1,p_{*})
, exploiting the reflexivity of
W^{1,\mathcal{H}}(\Omega)
and Proposition
2.3
-(ii) and (iii).
We aim to show that
v=0
. To this end, we set
\Omega^{*}=\{x\in\Omega:\,v(x)\neq 0\}
and assume that
|\Omega^{*}|>0
. Since
\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, then

|u_{j}|=\big\|u_{j}\big\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}\cdot|v_{j}|\to\infty\quad\mbox{a.e. in }\Omega^{*}.

Taking into account
(h_{2})
, we get

\displaystyle\infty=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{F(x,u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{F(x,u_{j})}{|u_{j}|^{q\theta}}\cdot|v_{j}|^{q\theta}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{*}.

Then, Fatou’s lemma gives

(4.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}}\;dx=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,u_{j})|v_{j}|^{q\theta}}{|u_{j}|^{q\theta}}\;dx=\infty.

On the other hand, we use (
2.25
) together with the nonnegativity of
G
first and then (
2.16
) to achieve

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}F(x,u_{j})\;dx

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})]+\frac{1}{p}\|u_{j}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u_{j}\|_{q,a}^{q}+C

\displaystyle\leq{B_{1}}(1+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q}+\|u\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta})+C\quad\text{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

Dividing by
\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}
, passing to the limit superior as
j\to\infty
and recalling that
\theta\geq 1
, we have

(4.8)

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{q\theta}}\;dx<\infty,

which contradicts (
4.7
).
In conclusion,
\Omega^{*}
has zero measure, that is,
v=0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Take now
j\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}>1
. By hypothesis
(M_{1})
, inequality (
2.25
), together with (
2.8
) and the fact that
\theta\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle C\geq I(u_{j})=

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})]+\frac{1}{p}\|u_{j}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{q}\|u_{j}\|_{q,a}^{q}-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u_{j})\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}G(x,u_{j})\;d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta}M[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})]\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})+\frac{1}{q}\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(u_{j})-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u_{j})\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}G(x,u_{j})\;d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{{A_{1}}}{q\theta}\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u_{j})\;dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}G(x,u_{j})\;d\sigma.

Since
\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, from the previous inequality we have

(4.9)

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left[\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}}dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{G(x,u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}}d\sigma\right]\geq\frac{{A_{1}}}{q\theta}>0.

We aim to show that

(4.10)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\frac{F(x,u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}}dx=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{G(x,u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}}d\sigma=0,

which will eventually contradict (
4.9
). We first observe that, thanks to hypothesis
(M_{1})
together with (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle C\geq

\displaystyle I(u_{j})-\frac{1}{q\theta}\langle I^{\prime}(u_{j}),u_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})]-\frac{1}{q\theta}M[\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})]\varrho_{\mathcal{H}}(\nabla u_{j})+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q\theta}\right)\|u_{j}\|_{p}^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q\theta}\right)\|u_{j}\|_{q,a}^{q}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\left[F(x,u_{j})-\frac{1}{q\theta}f(x,u_{j})u_{j}\right]dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}\left[G(x,u_{j})-\frac{1}{q\theta}g(x,u_{j})u_{j}\right]d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{F}(x,u_{j})\;dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{G}(x,u_{j})\;d\sigma.

For all
a\geq 0
and
b>a
, we now set
\Omega_{j}(a,b):=\left\{x\in\Omega:\,a\leq|u_{j}(x)|<b\right\}
. Thanks to
(h_{1})
and (
4.6
) it follows that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{j}(0,t_{0})}\frac{F(x,u_{j})}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}}\;dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\int_{\Omega_{j}(0,t_{0})}\left(\frac{|u_{j}|}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}}+\frac{|u_{j}|^{r_{1}}}{{r_{1}}\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}\left(\frac{\|u_{j}\|_{1}}{\|u_{j}\|_{1,\mathcal{H}}^{p}}+\frac{1}{r_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{j}(0,t_{0})}|u_{j}|^{r_{1}-p}|v_{j}|^{p}\;dx\right)

Global well-posedness for the 2D incompressible heat conducting Navier-Stokes equations with temperature-dependent coefficients and vacuum
Wenchao Dong, Qingyan Li 1. School of Sciences, Chang’an University, Xi’an 710064, China;2. Center for Nonlinear Studies, School of Mathematics, Northwest University, Xi’an 710127, China

Abstract




Abstract:
We consider the initial boundary problem of 2D non-homogeneous incompressible heat conducting Navier-Stokes equations with vacuum, where the viscosity and heat conductivity depend on temperature in a power law of Chapman-Enskog. We derive the global existence of strong solution to the initial-boundary value problem, which is not trivial, especially for the nonisentropic system with vacuum. Significantly, our existence result holds for the cases that the viscosity and heat conductivity depend on
\theta
with possibly different power laws (i.e.,
\mu=\theta^{\alpha},\kappa=\theta^{\beta}
with constants
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
) with smallness assumptions only on
\alpha
and the measure of initial vacuum domain. In particular, the initial data can be arbitrarily large. Moreover, it is obtained that both velocity and temperature decay exponentially as time tends to infinity.

Keywords
: Navier-Stokes equations; Global strong solution; Large-time behavior; Temperature-dependent transport coefficients

Introduction

The non-homogeneous incompressible heat conducting Navier-Stokes equations (see
[
33
]
p.117,
[
37
]
p.23) can be stated as follows:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{t}+\dive(\rho u)=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+\dive(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P=\dive(2\mu D(u)),\\
c_{v}\big((\rho\theta)_{t}+\dive(\rho u\theta)\big)-2\mu|D(u)|^{2}=\dive(\kappa\nabla\theta),\\
\dive u=0.\end{array}\right.

The system is supplemented with the initial condition

\displaystyle(\rho,u,\theta)(x,0)=(\rho_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0})(x),~~x\in\Omega,

(1.5)

and the boundary condition

\displaystyle u=0,~~\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\mathbf{n}}=0,~~\text{on}~\partial\Omega,

(1.6)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded smooth domain,
\mathbf{n}
is the unit outward normal to
\partial\Omega
. Here
\rho,u,\theta
and
P
stand for the unknown density, velocity, absolute temperature and pressure of the fluid respectively.
D(u)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{T}\big)
is the deformation tensor. The coefficients
\mu
,
c_{v}
and
\kappa
denote the viscosity, specific heat at constant volume and heat conductivity respectively.

In the theory of gas dynamics, the time evolution of the particle distribution function for the charged particles in a dilute gas can be modeled by the Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system. It is well known that, if one derives the Navier-Stokes equations from the Boltzmann equation by applying the Chapman-Enskog expansion, the viscosity and heat conductivity coefficients are shown to be functions of absolute temperature in a power law. For details see
[
8
,
21
]

r^{-a}

means intermolecular distance, then
\mu
and
\kappa
are both proportional to a certain power of the temperature:

\displaystyle\mu,\kappa\varpropto\theta^{\frac{a+4}{2a}}.

For Maxwellian molecules (
a=4
) the dependence is linear, while for elastic spheres (
a=\infty
)
the dependence is like
\sqrt{\theta}
. According to
[
8
]
, the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\mu=c_{1}\theta^{b},~~~~\kappa=c_{2}\theta^{b},~~~~b\in[\frac{1}{2},\infty),

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are two positive constants. Based on this, in this paper, we mainly concentrate on the case that
c_{v}=1
and
\mu,\kappa
satisfy the following physical restrictions

\mu=\mu(\theta)=\theta^{\alpha},\ \ \kappa=\kappa(\theta)=\theta^{\beta},\ \ \forall~\alpha,\beta\geq 0.

(1.7)

Definition 1.1
.

(
Strong Solution

and

Theorem 1.1
.

For some positive constants

and
\underline{\theta}
, suppose that the initial data

satisfies

0\leq\rho_{0}\in W^{1,q},~~~~u_{0}\in H_{0,\sigma}^{1}\cap H^{2},~~~~\underline{\theta}\leq\theta_{0}\in H_{\mathbf{n}}^{2},

(1.13)

and the compatibility conditions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rr}-\dive(2\mu(\theta_{0})D(u_{0}))+\nabla P_{0}=\sqrt{\rho_{0}}g_{1},\\
-\dive(\kappa(\theta_{0})\nabla\theta_{0})-2\mu(\theta_{0})|D(u_{0})|^{2}=\sqrt{\rho_{0}}g_{2},\end{array}\right.

for some
P_{0}\in H^{1}
and

. Then there exist two positive constants

and

depending only on

and

, such that if

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\epsilon_{0}

(1.16)

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|P\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{0}e^{-\sigma_{1}t},

(1.19)

\displaystyle\|\theta-\frac{1}{\overline{\rho_{0}}|\Omega|}E_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho}\theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{0}e^{-\sigma_{2}t},

We now make some comments on the analysis in this paper. To extend the local strong solution whose existence is obtained by Lemma
4.1
globally in time, one needs to establish global a priori estimates on smooth solution to (
1
)-(
1.7
) in suitable higher norms. There is extremely strong nonlinearity and degeneracy caused by transport coefficients, both of which create great difficulty for the a priori estimates, especially for the second-order estimates. The main idea is to combine the bootstrap argument and time-weighted estimates successfully applied to the Navier-Stokes equations. It turns out that as in
[
22
]
, the key ingredient here is to obtain the time-independent bounds on
\theta(x,t)
. However, the methods applied in
[
22
,
51
]
rely crucially on the smallness of the initial data or
\mu,\kappa=const.
. Therefore, some new ideas are needed here. First of all, according to the regularity properties of Stokes system and the smallness of
\alpha
, using an inequality derived by Desjardins
[
15
]
(see Lemma
2.8
), we establish the a priori estimates of
\theta
in suitable norms. Here, the main obstacle comes from the estimate of
\theta
in vacuum, which strongly interacts on the velocity field. Motivated by
[
7
]
, we divide the integration region into two parts (see (
3.1
)) and get the estimates of the temperature from the condition (
1.17
). Then, by using the Poincaré’s inequality and carefully calculations, we derive the decay estimates of the solution
(u,\theta,P)
. Meanwhile, we obtain the higher-order estimates of the solution owing to
L^{2}
-theory of elliptic equations. Finally, applying these a priori estimates and the fact that the velocity is divergent free, we can extend the local strong solution globally in time.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In section
2
, we present some basic facts and inequalities which will be used later. Section
3
concerns some a priori estimates on smooth solution which are needed to extend the local solution to all time. Finally, with all a priori estimates at hand, the main result Theorem
1.1
is proved in section
4
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t):[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are nonnegative bounded measurable function,

c(t):[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative integrable function. If

f_{1}(t)\leq f_{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}c(s)f_{1}(s)ds,~~~\forall~t\in[0,T],

then it follows that

f_{1}(t)\leq f_{2}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}f_{2}(s)c(s)\exp\big(\int_{s}^{t}c(\tau)d\tau\big)ds,~~~\forall~t\in[0,T].

Moreover, if
f_{2}(t)
is a monotone increasing function over
[0,T]
, then we obtain the estimate

f_{1}(t)\leq f_{2}(t)\exp\big(\int_{0}^{t}c(s)ds\big),~~~\forall~t\in[0,T].

Lemma 2.3

Let

In this subsection, our main effort is focused on the time-weighted estimates of the solution, with the aim of obtaining the decay properties of

Firstly, collecting Lemmas

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{3}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C_{0}(T).

(3.55)

Lemma 3.7

Under the assumptions of Proposition

step 1
. Thanks to Poincaré’s inequality (see

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\rho}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\tilde{\rho}\frac{d^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{2\tilde{\rho}d^{2}}{\pi^{2}\underline{\theta}^{\alpha}}\|\theta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D(u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

3.6

which means that

e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\sqrt{\rho}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\sigma_{1}s}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq C_{0}.

step 2
. Since (
3.1
) and Lemma
2.3
, we can check that

Multiplying (

) by
e^{\sigma_{1}t}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big(e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\theta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D(u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\sqrt{\rho}\theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\big)e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\theta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D(u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma_{1}e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\theta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D(u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

\displaystyle e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\sigma_{1}s}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq C_{0}.

(3.59)

step 3
.
we deduce from (
3.1
) and Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big(e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\theta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}D(u_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|\theta_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+Ce^{\sigma_{1}t}\big(\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\theta_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+Ce^{\sigma_{1}t}\big(\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\|(\sqrt{\rho}\theta_{t},\nabla\theta_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This together with Grönwall’s inequality, (

\displaystyle e^{\sigma_{1}t}\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\sigma_{1}s}\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\leq C_{0}.

(3.60)

Therefore, we arrive at (

) from (

)-(

∎

Now we derive the following decay estimates on the temperature.

Lemma 3.8
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(e^{\sigma_{2}t}(\|\theta-\frac{1}{\overline{\rho_{0}}|\Omega|}E_{0}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho}\theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\big)+\int_{0}^{T}e^{\sigma_{2}t}\|(\nabla\theta,\theta_{t})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}dt\leq C_{0},

step 1

Multiplying the last inequality by

4
Global Well-Posedness

In this section, we start with the local existence of a strong solution which has been established in
[
9
,
22
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(Local Strong Solution)
.

Suppose that
(\rho_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0})
satisfies (
1.13
)-(
1.17
). Then there exist a
small time
T_{0}>0
and a unique strong solution
(\rho,u,\theta,P)
to the problem (
1
)-(
1.7
)
in
\Omega\times[0,T_{0}]
satisfying (
1.1
).

With all the a priori estimates in section
3
and Lemma
4.1
at hand, we are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
:

It follows from (
1.13
), (
3.1
) and Lemma
4.1
that there exists a
T_{1}\in(0,T_{0}]
such that (
3.1
) holds for
T=T_{1}
. Set

\displaystyle T^{*}\triangleq\big\{T~\big|~(\rho,u,\theta,P)~\text{is a strong solution on}~\Omega\times(0,T]~\text{and}~\eqref{60}~\text{holds}\big\}.

(4.1)

Then,
T^{*}\geq T_{1}>0
.

For any
T\in(T_{1},T^{*}]
with
T
finite, in view of Proposition
3.2
, we know that

\displaystyle\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,q}),\ \rho_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{q}),

which combining with the work by P.Lions
[
33
, Lemma 2.3, p.43]
yields

\displaystyle\rho\in C([0,T];W^{1,q}).

(4.2)

Moreover, since

\displaystyle u,\theta\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}),~~~~~~u_{t},\theta_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C([0,T];H^{2})}\leq C(T)\big(\|u\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{3})}+\|u_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}\big)

(see
[
17
, Theorem 4, p.304]
), one has

\displaystyle u,\theta\in C([0,T];H^{2}).

(4.3)

According to the equations
\eqref{1}_{2},\eqref{1}_{3}
and (
4.2
)-(
4.3
) (see
[
51
, p.21]
), we can see that

\displaystyle\rho u_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla u,\ \rho\theta_{t}+\rho\theta\cdot\nabla\theta\in C([0,T];L^{2}),

(4.4)

we get after using Stokes estimates (Lemma
2.5
) that

\displaystyle P\in C(0,T;H^{1}).

(4.5)

Now, we claim that

\displaystyle T^{*}=\infty.

(4.6)

Otherwise,
T^{*}<\infty
. Then by Lemmas
3.1
-
3.5
, the estimate (
3.2
) holds for
T=T^{*}
. It follows from (
4.2
)-(
4.5
) that

\displaystyle(\rho^{*},u^{*},\theta^{*},P^{*})(x)\triangleq(\rho,u,\theta,P)(x,T^{*})=\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}(\rho,u,\theta,P)(x,t)

satisfies

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho^{*}\in W^{1,q},~~~~u^{*}\in H_{0,\sigma}^{1}\cap H^{2},~~~~\underline{\theta}\leq\theta^{*}\in H_{\mathbf{n}}^{2},

\displaystyle\rho^{*}u^{*}_{t}+\rho^{*}u^{*}\cdot\nabla u^{*}\in L^{2},~~~~\rho^{*}\theta^{*}_{t}+\rho^{*}\theta^{*}\cdot\nabla\theta^{*}\in L^{2},~~~~P^{*}\in H^{1}.

And let

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}_{1}\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rho^{*}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\rho^{*}u^{*}_{t}+\rho^{*}u^{*}\cdot\nabla u^{*}),&\text{if}~x\in\{x|\rho^{*}(x)>0\}\\
0,&\text{if}~x\in\{x|\rho^{*}(x)=0\}\end{array}\right.,

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}_{2}\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rho^{*}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\rho^{*}\theta^{*}_{t}+\rho^{*}u^{*}\cdot\nabla\theta^{*}),&\text{if}~x\in\{x|\rho^{*}(x)>0\}\\
0,&\text{if}~x\in\{x|\rho^{*}(x)=0\}\end{array}\right..

Then, from Proposition
3.2
, we obtain that
\mathrm{g}_{1},\mathrm{g}_{2}\in L^{2}
and satisfy the compatibility conditions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rr}-\dive(2\mu(\theta^{*})D(u^{*}))+\nabla P^{*}=\sqrt{\rho^{*}}\mathrm{g}_{1},\\

Finally, to finish the proof of Theorem

and

Remark 5
.

It is well known that
\rho
is a constant along the streamline (flow map). Thus,
\rho
cannot converge to any constant as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
Indeed,
\forall\,x\in\Omega,t\in[0,\infty]
, we define
X(x,s;t)
as follows

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}X(x,s;t)=u(X(x,s;t),s),~~~\forall\,s\in[0,\infty],\\[5.69054pt]
X(x,t;t)=x.\end{array}\right.

Using the fact that
u\in L^{1}(0,\infty;W^{1.\infty})
and Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem (Lemma Appendix A.1. in
[
12
]
), we know that
X(x,s;t)
is well-defined. Besides, we obtain from (
1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\rho(X(x,s;t),s)=0,

which means for any
(x,s,t)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty]\times[0,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\rho(X(x,s;t),s)=\rho(X(x,t;t),t)=\rho(x,t).

Taking
s=0
, we get

\displaystyle\rho(x,t)=\rho_{0}(X(x,0;t)),~~~\forall~(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty].

We see that

\displaystyle\rho(x,\infty)\triangleq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\rho(x,t)=\rho_{0}(X(x,0;\infty)).

(4.9)

Therefore, the state of
\rho
at infinity time is determined by
X(x,0;\infty)
, which also depends on the velocity
u
. Consequently, in general it cannot converge to a constant unless
\rho_{0}\equiv const.
.

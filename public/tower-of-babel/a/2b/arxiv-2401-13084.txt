On hyperspaces of knots and planar simple closed curves
Paweł Krupski, Krzysztof Omiljanowski

Abstract
. G^{+}_{PL}
) if and only if
C
and
D
have the same isotopy type (PL-isotopy type).

The following theorem can be proved using an idea similar to the proof of Proposition
2.4
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Each orbit of
G^{+}_{PL}
on
\mathcal{K}_{T}
is dense in
\mathcal{K}_{T}
.

Proof.

Choose arbitrary
C\in\mathcal{K}_{T}
and
D\in\mathcal{K}_{P}
. Consider a basic neighborhood of
D
homeomorphic to
\langle\widetilde{U_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{U_{n}}\rangle\cap\mathcal{K}_{T}
of the form
\langle W_{1},\dots,W_{n}\rangle\cap\mathcal{K}_{T}
, where
W_{k}
is an open polyhedral cube homeomorphic to
\widetilde{U_{k}}
for each
k
, and the union

Q^{\prime}=W_{2}\cup\dots\cup W_{n}
is a polyhedron homeomorphic to an open cube
Q=(-M,M)^{3}
containing
C
. As in the proof of Proposition
2.4
, replacing sets
\widetilde{U_{k}}
with
W_{k}
, construct a PL-isotopic deformation
I:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that the homeomorphism
g:=I(\cdot,1):\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfies
g(C)\in\langle W_{1},\dots,W_{n}\rangle\cap\mathcal{K}_{T}
. So, we have shown that each polyhedral knot
D
is arbitrarily close to an element of the orbit of
C
. It follows that any element of the orbit of
D
is arbitrarily close to an element of the orbit of
C
.

∎

.

4.
Cantor manifolds and ANR’s

For definitions and basic properties of various types of infinite dimensions, in particular of the strong countable dimension and weak (strong) infinite dimension, the reader is referred to
[
4
]
.
An infinite-dimensional space
X
is called an
infinite-dimensional Cantor manifold
if no finite-dimensional closed subset separates
X
. If no weakly infinite-dimensional closed subset separates
X
, then
X
is
strongly infinite-dimensional Cantor manifold
.
The Hilbert cube
[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
and
\hat{c_{0}}=\{(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}:\lim_{j}x_{j}=0\}
are examples of strongly infinite-dimensional Cantor manifolds. The space
\sigma=\{(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}:\text{$x_{j}=0$ for all but finitely many $j$}\}
is an infinite-dimensional Cantor manifold.

Theorem 4.1
.

(1)

The hyperspaces
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\mathcal{K}_{T}
are strongly infinite-dimensional Cantor manifolds that contain closed copies of
\hat{c_{0}}
in each basic set.

(2)

The hyperspaces
\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\mathcal{K}_{P}
are infinite-dimensional Cantor manifolds that contain closed copies of
\sigma
in each basic set.

Proof.

First, construct a special embedding
f:[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}\to C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Fix a sequence
\frac{2\pi}{m}=\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}>\dots>0
converging to
0
and let
p_{j}
be the point of intersection of the ray from
0
through
e^{i\alpha_{j}}
with polygon
P
, for each
j\geq 1
.
Given a sequence
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
, let
f((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})
be the closure of
the union of segments

[e^{i\frac{2\pi k}{m}},e^{i\frac{2\pi(k+1)}{m}}],\quad[p_{2j-1},(1+\frac{x_{j}}{n})p_{2j}],\ \text{and}\ [(1+\frac{x_{j}}{n})p_{2j},p_{2j+1}]

for
k=1,\dots,m-1
,
j\geq 1
. Then
f((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\in\langle U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\rangle
and

f
is an embedding satisfying

f((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad(x_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\in\hat{c_{0}}

and

f((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\in\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad(x_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\in\sigma.

Also, the mapping
\widetilde{f}((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})=f((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\times\{0\}\in\langle\widetilde{U_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{U_{n}}\rangle
is an embedding satisfying

\widetilde{f}((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\in\mathcal{K}_{T}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad(x_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\in\hat{c_{0}}

and

\widetilde{f}((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})\in\mathcal{K}_{P}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad(x_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}\in\sigma.

Equivalently, the above properties mean that
f(\hat{c_{0}})=f([0,1]^{\mathbb{N}})\cap\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
f(\sigma)=f([0,1]^{\mathbb{N}})\cap\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\widetilde{f}(\hat{c_{0}})=\widetilde{f}([0,1]^{\mathbb{N}})\cap\mathcal{K}_{T}
,
\widetilde{f}(\sigma)=\widetilde{f}([0,1]^{\mathbb{N}})\cap\mathcal{K}_{P}
. Hence,
f(\hat{c_{0}})
,
f(\sigma)
,
\widetilde{f}(\hat{c_{0}})
, and
\widetilde{f}(\sigma)
are closed subsets of the neighborhoods
\langle U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}
or
\langle\widetilde{U_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{U_{n}}\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}
for respective hyperspaces
\mathcal{C}\in\{\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathcal{K}_{T},\mathcal{K}_{P}\}
.
Since the open base of contractible sets in
\mathcal{C}
, as in Theorem
2.5
, consists of homeomorphic images of
\langle U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}
or
\langle\widetilde{U_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{U_{n}}\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}
under induced homeomorphisms of the entire plane or the rings
\widetilde{U}
,
n=7,8,\dots
, each basic set contains a closed copy of
\hat{c_{0}}
or
\sigma
, respectively.

Let a closed subset
\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{C}\in\{\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathcal{K}_{P},\mathcal{K}_{T}\}
separate
\mathcal{C}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{V}\cup\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
are nonempty, open in
\mathcal{C}
, and disjoint. Since
\mathcal{C}
is connected by Propositions
2.2
and
2.4
,
\mathcal{Z}
is nonempty. We can also assume
\mathcal{Z}=\bd_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{V}=\bd_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
, where
\bd_{\mathcal{C}}
stands for the “boundary in
\mathcal{C}
” operator.

By Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
, there exists a homeomorphism
\mathfrak{h}
which maps
\langle U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}
or
\langle\widetilde{U_{1}},\dots,\widetilde{U_{n}}\rangle\cap\mathcal{C}
, depending on whether

\mathcal{C}\in\{\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal{C}\in\{\mathcal{K}_{P},\mathcal{K}_{T}\},

onto an open neighborhood
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathfrak{h}(P)\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{A}
either of the sets:

\mathfrak{h}(f(\hat{c_{0}}))
if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\mathfrak{h}(\widetilde{f}(\hat{c_{0}})
if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{K}_{T}
,

\mathfrak{h}(f(\sigma))
if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\mathfrak{h}(\widetilde{f}(\sigma))
if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{K}_{P}
.

For
\mathcal{C}\in\{\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathcal{K}_{T}\}
suppose that
\mathcal{Z}

is weakly infinite-dimensional and for
\mathcal{C}\in\{\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\mathcal{K}_{P}\}
suppose that
\mathcal{Z}

is finite-dimensional.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}\subset\cl_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{V}
, where
\cl_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{V}
is the closure (in
\mathcal{C}
) of
\mathcal{V}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}
is strongly infinite-dimensional in case (1), and
\mathcal{A}
is infinite-dimensional in case (2), it contains an element
A\in\mathcal{V}
.
By Fact
3.6
, Theorem
3.7
, and Remark
3.8
, there exist
B\in\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{V}^{\prime}

and
a PL-isotopy
I:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
I:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, respectively, such that
I(A,0)=A
,
I(A,1)=B
. Let
t_{0}=\max\{t:\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A},t)\subset\cl_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{V}\}
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the induced homotopy on
\mathcal{C}
. Notice that
t_{0}<1
. Since the set

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A},t_{0})
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{A}
, it is not contained in
\mathcal{Z}
.
Choose
A_{0}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A},t_{0})\cap\mathcal{V}
and let
d(A_{0},\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{V})=\inf\{d(A_{0},C):C\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{V}\}
, where
d
is the Hausdorff distance in
\mathcal{C}
.
Now, by continuity of
\mathcal{I}
, there is
t>t_{0}
such that
d(\mathcal{I}(A_{0},t),\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{V})>0
. Then
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A},t)
meets both the sets
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. Being a copy of
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A},t)
is connected, so it intersects
\mathcal{Z}
. This means that
\mathcal{Z}
separates
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{A},t)
, which contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{A}
is strongly infinite-dimensional Cantor manifold in case (1), and
\mathcal{A}
is infinite-dimensional Cantor manifold in case (2).

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Hyperspaces
\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\mathcal{K}_{P}
are
\sigma
-compact, strongly countable-dimensional ANR’s.

Proof.

K. Sakai proved in
[
15
]
that the Vietoris hyperspace
Pol(X)
of all connected compact polyhedra in a compact convex subset
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n>1
, is
\sigma
-compact, strongly countable-dimensional. Hence, the hyperspace
Pol(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of connected compact polyhedra in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
also is
\sigma
-compact and strongly countable-dimensional. It follows from Theorem
3.1
that
\mathcal{S}_{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\mathcal{K}_{P}
are
\sigma
-compact, and being such subspaces of respective
Pol(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, they are strongly countable-dimensional. Recall that both the hyperspaces are locally contractible (Theorem
2.5
). By a result of W. E. Haver
[
5
]
, each
\sigma
-compact, strongly countable-dimensional and locally contractible metric space is an ANR.

∎

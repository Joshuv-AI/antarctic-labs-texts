Eisenstein points on the Hilbert cuspidal eigenvariety
Adel Betina, Mladen Dimitrov and Sheng-Chi Shih

Abstract
. We present a comprehensive study of the geometry of Hilbert p p -adic eigenvarieties at parallel weight one intersection points of their cuspidal and Eisenstein loci. The Galois theoretic approach presents genuine difficulties due to the lack of good deformation theory for pseudo-characters irregular at p p and reflects the rich local geometry at such points. We believe that our geometric results lead to deeper insight into the arithmetic of Hilbert automorphic forms and we produce several applications in Iwasawa theory to support that.



Dedicated to the memory of Joël Bellaïche.

Theorem A
.

Assume that

(ii)

If

(iii)

If

is not factorial (hence not regular). In particular,

.

Let us now briefly describe the techniques involved in the proof.
Mazur’s deformation theory provides a powerful tool to analyze the completed local rings
\mathcal{T}
of eigenvarieties at classical points whose
Galois representation are irreducible and
p
-regular, as one can then define a trianguline deformation functor which is representable by a ring surjecting to
\mathcal{T}
and whose tangent space computes in terms of Galois cohomology. In the ordinary case, this further leads to modularity lifting

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{T}
theorems allowing precise control of
\mathcal{T}
even when the classicality theorems alluded to above are not available (see
[
52
]
and
[
8
, §7.6.3]
). Some of this can be salvaged for representations which are reducible but still regular at
p
(see
[
7
]
).
However, for reducible Galois representation which are irregular at
p
, these approaches are no more viable.
A tentative approach to palliate to this difficulty is to instead consider a well tailored deformation functor of an indecomposable representation having the same semi-simplification. This was successfully used in
[
15
]
for
\GL_{2}
over
\mathbf{Q}
and it generalizes well to the Hilbert modular setting in case (i) of the above Theorem. The reason is that when
F_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbf{Q}_{p}
the classes unramified away from
\mathfrak{p}
produced by
Ribet’s method lie on a specific line in the
d
-dimensional cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi^{-1})
. Beyond this case, there is no such canonical line and we have to resort to other methods.

Pseudo-characters and generalized matrix algebras have efficiently been used to study deformations of reducible representations (see for example the book of Bellaïche–Chenevier
[
8
]
) and our completed local rings
\mathcal{T}
carry such objects.
However, in the absence of an
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{T}
theorem, there is no direct description of the tangent space of
\mathcal{T}
in Galois theoretic and eventually linear algebra terms. A genuine difficulty is the translation of the ordinarity condition at places in
\Sigma_{p}^{\mathrm{irr}}
which is essential to shape the geometry. Ultimately our proof of cases (ii) and (iii) of Theorem
A
uses in a delicate mix of Galois theoretic and modular properties of (nearly)-ordinary cuspidal Hida families.
Our analysis of the geometry turns out to be closely related to the basic question in deformation theory whether a pseudo-character is the trace of a representation. In case (ii), we can show that the
\mathcal{T}
-valued pseudo-character does come from an actual representation, reflecting the fact that the extension group
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)
admits a ‘modular’ parametrization (see §
5.3
). In contrast, in case (iii),
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)
lacks canonical lines reflecting the non-existence of
\mathcal{T}
-valued Galois representation ultimately forcing
\mathcal{T}
to be singular.

Understand the geometry of
\mathcal{C}
at
f_{\phi_{1}}

\mathcal{C}_{\cusp}
, the study of the congruence ideal
between the Eisenstein and cuspidal families specializing to

For example, in case of Theorem

(ii), we show that the complete local ring of

\varLambda\times_{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}\varLambda\times_{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}\varLambda

Theorem B
.

(Theorem

One has
\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}\lsem X\rsem[1/p]=(\zeta_{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}})

The points of intersection between the Eisenstein components and the Hilbert cuspidal eigenvariety correspond to zeros of the Deligne–Ribet
p
-adic
L
-function. In his proof
[

of the Iwasawa Main Conjecture over totally real fields Wiles
had to treat these trivial zeros separately, as the cohomological classes constructed by Ribet’s method do not have the required local property at

Such difficulties seem to be inherent to the non-Gorenstein nature of the singularity, as Hida’s congruence ideal
\mathbf{C}^{0}
and Mazur’s module
\mathbf{C}^{1}
, forming the bridge between the analytic and arithmetic sides of Iwasawa’s Main Conjecture, differ.
Geometrically Theorem

is given by the order of vanishing of

Part of Theorem

who explicitly constructed cuspidal families congruent to

-adic ordinary modular forms in Hida theory (see

In this paper we show existence of a fundamental exact sequence for the space of Coleman families of parallel weight

-adic modular forms in full generality (Theorem

Arithmetic applications

The Langlands program is a web of deep and far-reaching conjectures introducing harmonic analysis on reductive groups to
shed light on problems in Number Theory. It namely relates geometric
p
-adic Galois representations to automorphic forms and further to motives, via the notion of an
L
-function. A most mysterious aspects of this relationship, made precise in the Bloch–Kato Conjecture, is the connection between the orders of vanishing and special values of
L
-functions on one side, and certain arithmetic invariants in the Galois cohomology of these
p
-adic representations on the other. A very efficient approach to establish instances of these conjectures consists to explore analogous questions for
p
-adic
L
-functions such as the Iwasawa Main Conjecture and Perrin-Riou’s
p
-adic Beilinson Conjecture.
These
p
-adic analogues are more amenable because automorphic forms and their
L
-functions can be
p
-adically deformed allowing a family approach to the original conjecture. As aforementioned, to set this strategy in motion one needs to first understand the local geometry of an eigenvariety.
For example, a special case of Theorem
A
, namely considering the infinitesimal deformation of
f_{\phi_{1}}
in the anti-parallel direction for
F
real quadratic in which the prime
p
is inert, allowed Darmon–Pozzi–Vonk
[
25
]
to relate some values of the Dedekind–Rademacher cocycle to global
p
-units in the narrow class field of
F
. Theorem
A
is a crucial input for the study
of diagonal cycles on Shimura curves in
[
4
]
with application to the arithmetic of elliptic curves over
F
.

We close the introduction by describing two new applications in Iwasawa theory.

1.
Twisted Coleman families and Hilbert eigenvarieties

Weight
1

p
-adic Hilbert modular forms is given in
Hida’s book
[

and a control theorem is established under the assumption that

Theorem 1.5
.

One has a Hecke equivariant exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}}
-modules

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbf{S}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathbf{M}^{{\dagger}}_{\mathcal{U}}\xrightarrow{\res}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{U}}[\mathscr{P}_{1}(p^{n})]^{(\phi_{p})}\to 0.

Proof.

Recall that for

Finally, (

\displaystyle\begin{split}0\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mu_{p^{n}},v)\times\mathcal{U},\omega^{\sw_{\mathcal{U}}^{\circ}}(-D))&\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mu_{p^{n}},v)\times\mathcal{U},\omega^{\sw_{\mathcal{U}}^{\circ}})\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{\mathfrak{c}}(\mu_{p^{n}},v)\times\mathcal{U},(\mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{1})_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{D\times\mathcal{U}}))=&\mathrm{H}^{0}(D\times\mathcal{U},\mathcal{O}_{D\times\mathcal{U}})\\

The desired short exact sequence (

\left(\log_{p}(\tilde{\sigma}\circ g^{-1}(\cdot))\right)_{\sigma\in\Sigma,g\in\Gal(H/F)}
of

\Hom\left((\mathcal{O}_{H}\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p})^{\times},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\right)
.
As
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{H}}^{\times}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})[\phi]=\{0\}
, it follows from (
57
) that
\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{H},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})[\phi^{-1}]\simeq\Hom\left((\mathcal{O}_{H}\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p})^{\times},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\right)[\phi^{-1}]
, yielding the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

The space
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)\simeq\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{H},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})[\phi^{-1}]
is
d
-dimensional with basis

([\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}])_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{F}}
, where
\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}
is the cocycle whose image in
\Hom\left((\mathcal{O}_{H}\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p})^{\times},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\right)
equals

(u\otimes v)\mapsto\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\Gal(H/F)}\phi(g)\log_{p}\left(\iota_{p}\circ\tilde{\sigma}\circ g^{-1}(u)\cdot v\right).

Moreover
[\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}]
is only ramified at the prime
of
F
determined by
\sigma
via the decomposition
\Sigma=\coprod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{p}}\Sigma_{\mathfrak{q}}
.

Since
(\phi-\mathbf{1})
is a basis of the coboundaries and
\phi(\tau)=-1\neq 1
, where
\tau\in\mathrm{G}_{F}
denotes a complex conjugation,
each
[\eta]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)
is represented by a unique cocycle
\eta
such that
\eta(\tau)=0
. We will fix
\tau
and
\eta
throughout this article and make no further comment.
The image of
\eta_{\mathbf{1}}=\log_{p}\circ\epsilon_{\cyc}\in\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{Q}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})
under the natural restrictions

\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{\mathbf{Q}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\hookrightarrow\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{H},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\xrightarrow{\eqref{eq:24}}\Hom\left((\mathcal{O}_{H}\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{p})^{\times},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\right)

corresponds to
\log_{p}\circ\mathrm{N}_{H/\mathbf{Q}}
. The following cohomological interpretation of
\mathscr{L}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)
will be
used in §
4.4
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\loc_{\mathfrak{p}}
denote the restriction map of
\mathrm{G}_{F}
to
\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(i)

There exists a unique
[\eta_{\phi}^{(\mathfrak{p})}]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)
unramified outside
\mathfrak{p}
and such that

\loc_{\mathfrak{p}}([\eta_{\phi}^{(\mathfrak{p})}-\eta_{\mathbf{1}}])\in\ker\left(\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\to\Hom(\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\right).

(ii)

Under the local Artin map, one has the following equality in
\Hom(F_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})
:

\loc_{\mathfrak{p}}([\eta_{\phi}^{(\mathfrak{p})}-\eta_{\mathbf{1}}])=\mathscr{L}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}.

(iii)

For all
\sigma\in\Sigma\backslash\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
\loc_{\mathfrak{p}}([\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}])\in\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}^{\times}\cdot\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(iv)

One always have
\mathscr{L}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)\neq 0
,
\mathscr{L}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi^{-1})\neq 0
and
\mathscr{L}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi)+\mathscr{L}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi^{-1})\neq 0
.

Proof.

(i) By Lemma
3.1
, any
[\eta]\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)
unramified outside
\mathfrak{p}
can be written as
[\eta]=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}h_{\sigma}[\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}]
for some
h_{\sigma}\in\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
. Moreover, the condition
\eta_{|\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\eta_{\mathbf{1}|\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
yields
h_{\sigma}=1
for all
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
as required.
(ii) We follows the proof of
[
15
, Prop. 2.5]
. Consider the exact sequence, analogous to (
57
),

(58)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{H},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{w|p}\Hom\left(H_{w_{0}}^{\times}\times\prod_{w\neq w_{0}}\mathcal{O}_{H_{w}}^{\times},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\right)\longrightarrow\Hom\left(\mathcal{O}_{H}\left[\tfrac{1}{w_{0}}\right]^{\times},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\right).

Let
e
be the exponent of the Hilbert class field of
H
and choose
x_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{H}[\tfrac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}]^{\times}
having valuation
e
at
w_{0}
and
0
at all other places of
H
.
Then
u_{\phi}:=e_{\phi}\cdot x_{0}
is a basis of the line
(\mathcal{O}_{H}[\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}}]^{\times}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})[\phi]
such that
\ord_{w_{0}}(u_{\phi})=e
, where
e_{\phi}:=\sum_{g\in\Gal(H/F)}\phi(g)g^{-1}
is the idempotent element projecting
x_{0}
to the
\phi
-component.
By the above discussion,
(\eta_{\phi}^{(\mathfrak{p})}-\eta_{\mathbf{1}})
, as an element of
\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{H},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})
, is given by

(59)

\displaystyle(\eta_{\phi}^{(\mathfrak{p})}-\eta_{\mathbf{1}})(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}-\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}\sum_{g\in\Gal(H/F)}\log_{p}(\tilde{\sigma}\circ g^{-1}\cdot).

Its evaluation at
x_{0}
vanishes by (
58
), and hence, we have

e\cdot(\eta_{\phi}^{(\mathfrak{p})}-\eta_{\mathbf{1}})(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})=-\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}\sum_{g\in\Gal(H/F)}\phi(g)\log_{p}(\tilde{\sigma}\circ g^{-1}(x_{0}))=-\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}\log_{p}(\tilde{\sigma}(u_{\phi}))=e\cdot\mathscr{L}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\phi).

(iii) For each
\sigma\in\Sigma\backslash\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we have seen in Lemma
3.1
that
\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
. A computation similar to
[
15
, (18)]

shows that

\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}(\Frob_{\mathfrak{p}})=-\sum_{g\in\Gal(H/F)}\phi(g)\log_{p}(\tilde{\sigma}\circ g^{-1}(x_{0})),

which is non-zero by the Baker–Brumer Theorem.
(iv) Set
x_{0}^{-}:=(\tau-1)\cdot x_{0}
. It is an eigenvector of
\tau
with eigenvalue
-1
. For any place
w
of
H
, let
||\cdot||_{w,p}
be the local norm of
H_{w}
defined by
[
32
, (1.8)]
. As the character
\phi
is a totally odd, one can verify that
e\mathscr{L}(\phi)=\log_{p}||e_{\phi}\cdot x_{0}^{-}||_{w_{0},p}
. By a similar argument of the proof of
[
15
, Prop. 2.5(1)]
and by Baker–Brumer’s theorem, to show the assertion, it suffices to show that the values
\{\log_{p}||g\cdot x_{0}^{-}||_{w_{0},p}\mid g\neq 1\in\Gal(H/F)/\{1,\tau\}\}
are linear independent over
\mathbf{Q}
. By essentially the same argument of the proof of
[
32
, Prop. 1.16]
, one can verify that if
\log_{p}\prod_{g}a_{g}||g\cdot x_{0}^{-}||_{w_{0},p}=0
for
a_{g}\in\mathbf{Q}
then
\prod_{g}(g\cdot x_{0}^{-})^{a_{g}}
is a root of unity, which contradicts to the definition of
x_{0}
. Here the product runs through non-identify representatives of
\Gal(H/F)/\{1,\tau\}
. Thus, one has
a_{g}=0
for all
g\neq 1\in\Gal(H/F)/\{1,\tau\}
and hence, the assertion follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

(i)

\dim_{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}\ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)\xrightarrow{\loc_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\right)=\max(d-d_{\mathfrak{p}}-1,0)
.

(ii)

If
\phi
is regular at all primes
\mathfrak{q}\mid p
,
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
, then
\dim_{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}\ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)\to\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{q}},\phi)\right)=d_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Proof.

For any
\mathfrak{q}\mid p
, we consider the inflation-restriction exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{q}},\phi^{\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{q}}})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}},\phi)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{q}},\phi).

By Lemma
3.1
,
\ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\right)
has a basis given by
\{\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma\backslash\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
As
\dim_{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})=1
and Proposition
3.2
(ii) implies that

\eta_{\phi}^{(\tilde{\sigma})}\notin\ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\right)
for any
\sigma\in\Sigma\backslash\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The assertion (i) follows. Again by Lemma
3.1
, we have
\dim_{\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}\ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi)\to\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{q}},\phi)\right)=d_{\mathfrak{p}}
. As
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{q}},\phi^{\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{q}}})=\{0\}
for all primes
\mathfrak{q}\mid p
,
\mathfrak{q}\neq\mathfrak{p}
(see
[
26
, §1.2]
), the assertion (ii) immediately follows.
∎

3.2.
Nearly-ordinary cuspidal deformation functor

Let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be finite order Hecke characters such that
\phi=\phi_{2}\phi_{1}^{-1}
totally odd and irregular at a
unique
prime above
p
, i.e.,
\Sigma_{p}^{\mathrm{irr}}=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. The goal of this subsection is to introduce a nearly-ordinary deformation functor and compute its tangent space. This will be used in §
4.2
and §
4.4
to prove Theorem
A
(i) and (iii), respectively.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be the category of Artinian local
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
-algebras
A
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{A}
and residue field
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
, where the morphisms are local homomorphisms of
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
-algebras inducing identity on the residue field. Fix a non-trivial cocycle
[\eta]\in\ker\left(\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\phi^{-1})\to\prod_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{p}\setminus\{\mathfrak{p}\}}\mathrm{H}^{1}(F_{\mathfrak{q}},\phi^{-1})\right)
, where we recall that
\Sigma_{p}
denotes the set of primes of
F
above
p
.
Fix a representative
\eta
of
[\eta]
with
\eta(\tau)=0
as in §
3.1
. As the centralizer of the image of
\rho=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\phi_{1}&\phi_{2}\eta\\
0&\phi_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
consists only of scalar matrices, the functor
\mathcal{D}_{\eta}
assigning to
A\in\mathcal{A}
the set of lifts
\rho_{A}:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\GL_{2}(A)
of
\rho
modulo strict equivalence is pro-representable by a
complete Noetherian local
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
-algebra
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}
(see
[
43
]
).

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\nord}_{\eta}
be the functor assigning to
A\in\mathcal{A}
the set of couples
(\rho_{A},\Fil_{A})
such that

(i)

\rho_{A}:\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\GL_{2}(A)
is a continuous representation lifting
\rho
,

(ii)

for
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{p}\setminus\{\mathfrak{p}\}
,
\rho_{A|\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}}
has a
\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}
-stable free rank
1
quotient (free as
A
-module), lifting
\phi_{1}
,

(iii)

\Fil_{A}\subset A^{2}
is a free direct factor over
A
of rank
1
which is
\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}
-stable,

modulo strict equivalence relation
(\rho_{A},\Fil_{A})\sim(M\rho_{A}M^{-1},M\cdot\Fil_{A})
for
M\in 1+\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathfrak{m}_{A})
.
Let
\mathcal{D}^{\ord}_{\eta}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\nord}_{\eta}
be the subfunctor of ordinary deformations, i.e., those for which the inertia subgroups act trivially on the free rank
1
quotients in (ii) and (iii) above.

Proposition 3.5
.

We fix a representative

\rho_{\univ}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mathrm{G}_{F}\to\GL_{2}(\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta})

of the universal deformation of
\rho
and denote by
(e_{1},e_{2})
the canonical basis of
(\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta})^{2}
.

(i)

The functor
\mathcal{D}^{\nord}_{\eta}
is pro-representable by a complete Noetherian local
\varLambda^{\nord}
-algebra
\mathcal{R}^{\nord}_{\rho_{\univ}}
a quotient of
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\lsem Y\rsem
by the ideal
\mathscr{J}^{\nord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y)$}}}
defined below in (
68
), together with its universal deformation represented by
\rho^{\nord}
the pushforward of
\rho_{\univ}
along
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\lsem Y\rsem\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{\nord}_{\rho_{\univ}}
, and the universal nearly-ordinary line at
\mathfrak{p}
given by
e_{1}+Ye_{2}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{D}^{\ord}_{\eta}
is pro-representable by a complete Noetherian local
\varLambda
-algebra
\mathcal{R}^{\ord}_{\eta}
a quotient of
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}
by the ideal
\mathscr{J}^{\ord}
defined below in (
67
), and which is independent from the choice of the representative
\rho_{\univ}
of the universal deformation of
\rho
.

(iii)

Changing the representative
\rho_{\univ}
with
\rho_{\univ}^{\prime}=M^{-1}\rho_{\univ}M
for
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\ker\left(\GL_{2}(\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta})\to\GL_{2}(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\right)
gives an isomorphism of
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}
-algebras

\mathcal{R}^{\nord}_{\rho_{\univ}}\simeq\mathcal{R}^{\nord}_{\rho^{\prime}_{\univ}}

such that
Y\mapsto M\cdot Y
, where
M\cdot Y=\frac{\gamma+\delta Y}{\alpha+\beta Y}
(the homography of
Y
under
M
).

(iv)

We have a
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
-linear injective map
\Hom_{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{R}^{\nord}_{\rho_{\univ}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}[\epsilon])\hookrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\ad\rho)\oplus\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
given by

\varphi\mapsto([\frac{\varphi(\rho^{\nord})\rho^{-1}-\mathbf{1}_{2}}{\epsilon}],\frac{\varphi(Y)}{\epsilon}).

Proof.

We show (i), (ii) and (iii) simultaneously. We recall that
\Sigma_{p}^{\mathrm{irr}}=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. We will analyze the local deformation conditions at the primes above
p
. We start with the local deformation conditions at
\mathfrak{p}
.
We have

(60)

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
-Y&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a(g)&b(g)\\
c(g)&d(g)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
Y&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a(g)+b(g)Y&b(g)\\
c(g)+(d(g)-a(g))Y-b(g)Y^{2}&d(g)-b(g)Y\end{pmatrix}

and this leads us to consider the ideals of
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\lsem Y\rsem
:

(61)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}^{\nord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y,\mathfrak{p})$}}}&=\left(c(g)+(d(g)-a(g))Y-b(g)Y^{2};g\in\mathrm{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)\\
\mathcal{I}^{\ord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y,\mathfrak{p})$}}}&=\left(d(g)-1-b(g)Y;g\in\mathrm{I}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)+\mathcal{I}^{\nord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y,\mathfrak{p})$}}}.\end{split}

We highlight that
[\eta]_{\mid\mathrm{I}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}}\neq 0\in\Hom(\mathrm{I}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})
, otherwise
\mathrm{res}_{\mathrm{G}_{H}}([\eta])\in\Hom(\mathrm{G}_{H},\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})[\phi^{-1}]
will be unramified everywhere and hence
[\eta]=0
by the finiteness of the ideal class group of
H
. Choosing any
g_{0}\in\mathrm{I}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}}
such that
\eta(g_{0})\neq 0
yields that
b(g_{0})\in(\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta})^{\times}
, hence

(62)

\begin{split}Y&\equiv\tfrac{d(g_{0})-1}{b(g_{0})}\mod\mathcal{I}^{\ord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y,\mathfrak{p})$}}}\text{ and }\\
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\lsem Y\rsem/\mathcal{I}^{\ord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y,\mathfrak{p})$}}}&\simeq\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}/(\text{\small{$c(g)+(d(g)-a(g))(\tfrac{d(g_{0})-1}{b(g_{0})})-b(g)\tfrac{(d(g_{0})-1)}{b^{2}(g_{0})}^{2}$}};g\in\mathrm{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}})\end{split}

This leads us to define the ideal

(63)

\mathscr{J}^{\ord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},\mathfrak{p})$}}}=(c(g)+(d(g)-a(g))(\tfrac{d(g_{0})-1}{b(g_{0})})-b(g)\tfrac{(d(g_{0})-1)}{b^{2}(g_{0})}^{2};g\in\mathrm{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}})

of
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}
. If we let now
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\ker\left(\GL_{2}(\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta})\to\GL_{2}(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})\right)
and
\rho^{\prime}_{\univ}(g)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a^{\prime}(g)&b^{\prime}(g)\\
c^{\prime}(g)&d^{\prime}(g)\end{smallmatrix}\right)=M\rho_{\univ}(g)M^{-1}
another representative of the universal deformation, then a direct calculation shows that

(64)

\mathcal{I}^{\nord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y,\mathfrak{p})$}}}=\mathcal{I}^{\nord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho^{\prime}_{\univ},Y^{\prime}=\frac{\gamma+\delta Y}{\alpha+\beta Y},\mathfrak{p})$}}}\text{ and }\mathcal{I}^{\ord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y,\mathfrak{p})$}}}=\mathcal{I}^{\ord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho^{\prime}_{\univ},Y^{\prime}=\frac{\gamma+\delta Y}{\alpha+\beta Y},\mathfrak{p})$}}}.

In particular, (
62
) and (
64
) show the equality of ideals
\mathscr{J}^{\ord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},\mathfrak{p})$}}}=\mathscr{J}^{\ord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho^{\prime}_{\univ},\mathfrak{p})$}}}
, i.e.,

(65)

\begin{split}\text{\small{$(c(g)+(d(g)-a(g))(\tfrac{d(g_{0})-1}{b(g_{0})})-b(g)\tfrac{(d(g_{0})-1)}{b^{2}(g_{0})}^{2};g\in\mathrm{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}})$}}=\text{\small{$(c^{\prime}(g)+(d^{\prime}(g)-a^{\prime}(g))(\tfrac{d^{\prime}(g_{0})-1}{b^{\prime}(g_{0})})-b^{\prime}(g)\tfrac{(d^{\prime}(g_{0})-1)}{b^{\prime}(g_{0})^{2}}^{2};g\in\mathrm{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}})$}}\end{split}

and hence, the ideal (
63
), now denoted by
\mathscr{J}^{\ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, defines a deformation condition in the sense of Ramakrishna.
Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{p}\backslash\{\mathfrak{p}\}
(so that
\phi_{\mid\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}}\neq\mathbf{1}
). Hence
\phi_{1}(g_{\mathfrak{p}})\neq\phi_{2}(g_{\mathfrak{p}})
for a certain
g_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, and by Hensel’s Lemma and
[
8
, Lem. .1.4.3]
there exists a matrix
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\GL_{2}(\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta})
with
\gamma\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}^{\univ}}
such that
M\rho_{\univ}(g_{\mathfrak{p}})M^{-1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}&0\\
0&\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. One has necessarily
M\rho_{\mid\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}}M^{-1}\equiv\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\phi_{1}&0\\
0&\phi_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mod\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}}
because
\mathrm{res}_{\mathfrak{q}}([\eta])=0
(hence
\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}\equiv\phi_{1}(\mathfrak{q})
and
\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}\equiv\phi_{2}(\mathfrak{q})
). In particular, this means that
b^{\prime}(g)\in\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}}
for all
g\in\mathrm{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, where
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a^{\prime}(g)&b^{\prime}(g)\\
c^{\prime}(g)&d^{\prime}(g)\end{smallmatrix}\right)=M\rho_{\univ}(g)M^{-1}
. We set then the ideals of
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}
:

(66)

\begin{split}\mathscr{J}^{\nord}_{\mathfrak{q}}&=\left(b^{\prime}(g);g\in\mathrm{G}_{F_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right)\\
\mathscr{J}^{\ord}_{\mathfrak{q}}&=\left(a^{\prime}(g)-1;g\in\mathrm{I}_{F_{\mathfrak{q}}}\right)+\mathscr{J}^{\nord}_{\mathfrak{q}}.\end{split}

Here the ideal
\mathscr{J}^{\nord}(\mathfrak{q})
(resp.
\mathscr{J}^{\ord}(\mathfrak{q})
) does not depend neither on the choice of
M
nor on the choice of
\rho_{\univ}
, because any other matrix
P
satisfying
P\rho_{\univ}(g_{\mathfrak{p}})P^{-1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}&0\\
0&\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
yields the equality
MP^{-1}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u_{1}&0\\
0&u_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\GL_{2}(\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta})
, and this will not affect the ideal
\mathscr{J}^{\nord}(\mathfrak{q})
(resp.
\mathscr{J}^{\ord}(\mathfrak{q})
). Therefore,
\mathcal{D}^{\ord}_{\eta}
is representable by

\mathcal{R}^{\ord}_{\eta}=\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}/\mathscr{J}^{\ord},

where

(67)

\mathscr{J}^{\ord}=\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\mid p}\mathscr{J}^{\ord}_{\mathfrak{q}},

and similarly,
\mathcal{D}^{\nord}_{\eta}
is representable by

\mathcal{R}^{\nord}_{\rho_{\univ}}=\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\lsem Y\rsem/\mathscr{J}^{\nord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y)$}}},

where

(68)

\mathscr{J}^{\nord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y)$}}}=\mathcal{I}^{\nord}_{\text{\tiny{$(\rho_{\univ},Y,\mathfrak{p})$}}}+\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{p}\backslash\{\mathfrak{p}\}}\mathscr{J}^{\nord}_{\mathfrak{q}}.

The universal nearly-ordinary deformation
\rho^{\nord}:G_{F}\to\GL_{2}(\mathcal{R}^{\nord}_{\rho_{\univ}})
is the pushforward of
\rho_{\univ}
along
\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\lsem Y\rsem\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{\nord}
. To see this, let
x
be a point of
\mathcal{D}_{\eta}^{\nord}(A)
which is then represented by a push-forward
\rho_{A}
of
\rho_{\univ}
along the homomorphism

\varphi_{A}:\mathcal{R}^{\univ}_{\eta}\to A
, and a
\mathfrak{p}
-nearly-ordinary line
F_{A}\subset A^{2}
. Other
\mathfrak{q}
-nearly-ordinary lines (when
\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{p}\backslash\{\mathfrak{p}\}
) are uniquely determined by
\rho_{A}
thanks to Hensel’s lemma, i.e., when a
\mathfrak{q}
-nearly-ordinary line exists it is necessarily unique.
The filtration
F_{A}
has a basis
e_{1}+ye_{2}
with

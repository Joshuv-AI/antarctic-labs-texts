Theoretical issues in the accurate computation of the electron-phonon interaction contribution to the total energy Preprint: APS/123-QED
Shilpa Paul, M. P. Gururajan, Amrita Bhattacharya, T. R. S. Prasanna

Abstract
The total energy is the most fundamental quantity in ab initio studies. To include electron-phonon interaction (EPI) contribution to the total energy, we have recast Allen’s equation, for the case of semiconductors and insulators. This equivalent expression can be computed using available software, leading to more accurate total energy. We calculate the total energies and their differences for carbon-diamond and carbon-hexagonal polytypes. Contrary to the current understanding, where per-atom quantities are computed from the primitive unit cell, our results, unexpectedly, show that the per-atom total energy (EPI included) depends on the unit cell size and violates the unit cell independence. For example, it differs for carbon-diamond by 1 eV/atom between the primitive cell and supercells. This norm-violating behaviour arises from the partial Fan-Migdal (FM) self-energy term present in Allen’s equation. Consequently, to determine the total energy differences between polytypes, supercells with identical number of atoms are necessary. A crucial inference of general validity is that any equation that contains a partial Fan-Midgal self-energy term violates the unit cell independence, contrary to the current understanding. Further theoretical studies are needed to establish if the total energy (EPI included) is an exception or can be reconciled with the unit cell independence.



The total energy is the most fundamental quantity in the
ab initio

studies of materials, which also lay the basis of the density functional
theory (DFT)
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
;
Kohn and Sham 1965
. In
ab initio
studies of
crystalline solids, the accepted norm is to determine the total energy and
other properties only for the primitive unit cell. This is because the
per-atomic total energy and other properties remain invariant,
whether calculated for the primitive unit cell (PC) or for any supercell
(SC). This is referred to as “unit cell independence” in the present
manuscript. Consequently, in
ab initio
studies, the
total energy is first minimized, using variational methods, with respect to
structural parameters for the PC. Only then are the other quantities, e.g.
band-structure, electronic, optical properties etc. computed for the so
obtained relaxed ground state structure.

The
ab initio
total energy is of prime interest in determining the
stable structure and energy differences and phase boundaries of polymorphs.
Thus, it is essential to determine the total energy as accurately as
possible. DFT studies
Hohenberg and Kohn 1964
;
Kohn and Sham 1965
have been successful
in determining the stable structure and energy differences between
polymorphs for many materials. However, for some materials, the DFT and
experimental results are at variance. Hence, additional contributions from
van der Waals (vdW) dispersion and zero-point vibrational energy (ZPVE) are
also included to obtain accurate total energy
Kawanishi and Mizoguchi 2016
;
Scalise et al. 2019
;
Ramakers et al. 2022
;
Cazorla and Gould 2019
;
van Setten et al. 2007
;
Bhat et al. 2018
.

We have recently proposed the inclusion of electron-phonon interaction
(EPI) contribution to the total energy
Ramasimha Varma et al. 2023
. Referring to our
work, Allen has formally derived the expression for including the same from
second-order perturbation theory
Allen 2022
. This equation
cannot be computed, as such, using available software. Hence, in earlier
studies, we have approximated Allen’s equation
Allen 2022
and
computed the EPI contribution to the total energy for C, Si, SiC and BN
polymorphs
Ramasimha Varma et al. 2023
;
Paul et al. 2022
. Our results show that the EPI
contribution is more important than the vdW and ZPVE contributions due to
its greater sensitivity to crystal structure
Ramasimha Varma et al. 2023
;
Paul et al. 2022
.

However, for accurate total energy studies, it is necessary to compute the
complete Allen’s equation (Eq. 4 of Ref.
Allen 2022
) for the
EPI contribution. In this paper, we recast this equation, for
the case of semiconductors and insulators. The recast (equivalent)
expression can be computed using existing software, leading to more
accurate total energy for all semiconductors and insulators.

However, our results raise serious theoretical issues. Allen’s equation

Allen 2022
for the EPI contribution to the total energy
contains a partial Fan-Migdal (FM) self-energy term, identical to that
present in the earliest EPI studies by Fröhlich

Fröhlich 1950
and Fan
Fan 1951
. Our computational results
on carbon polytypes show that the presence of the partial FM self-energy
term makes the
ab initio
total energy violate the unit cell
independence, a most surprising result. Due to this, use of supercells with
identical number of atoms are necessary to determine the total
energy differences between polytypes. Our results show that any equation
that contains a partial Fan-Midgal self-energy term violates the unit
cell independence. This is likely the first case where per-atom quantities
are not uniquely obtained from the primitive unit cell. Further theoretical
studies are needed to understand and resolve this issue.

In the Allen-Heine theory, EPI contribution to the eigenenergy arises from
the Debye-Waller (DW) and Fan-Migdal (FM) terms and, at 0 K, is given by

Allen and Heine 1976
;
Allen and Hui 1980

\Delta\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)=\Delta^{\textrm{DW}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)+\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)

(1)

The FM self-energy term includes contributions from both conduction and
valence bands
Allen and Heine 1976
;
Allen and Hui 1980
. It can also be written as a sum
of their separate contributions as
Nery et al. 2018

\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)=\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{occ}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)+\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{unocc}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)

(2)

These partial FM self-energy terms are given by
Nery et al. 2018

\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{unocc}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)=\frac{1}{N_{\textbf{q}}}\sum^{\textrm{BZ}}_{\textbf{q}j}\sum^{\textrm{unocc}}_{n^{\prime}}\frac{{|\langle\textbf{k}n|H_{j}^{(1)}|\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}n^{\prime}\rangle|}^{2}}{\omega-\epsilon_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}n^{\prime}}-\omega_{\textbf{q}j}+i\eta^{\prime}}

(3)

and

\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{occ}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)=\frac{1}{N_{\textbf{q}}}\sum^{\textrm{BZ}}_{\textbf{q}j}\sum^{\textrm{occ}}_{n^{\prime}}\frac{{|\langle\textbf{k}n|H_{j}^{(1)}|\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}n^{\prime}\rangle|}^{2}}{\omega-\epsilon_{\textbf{k}+\textbf{q}n^{\prime}}+\omega_{\textbf{q}j}+i\eta^{\prime}}

(4)

Because EPI alters all eigenenergies, the total energy is also altered

Ramasimha Varma et al. 2023
. Using second-order perturbation theory, Allen has derived
the expression for the EPI contribution to the total energy,
\Delta E_{\textrm{EP}}(V,0)
, as
Allen 2022

\Delta E_{\textrm{EP}}(V,0)=\\
\sum_{k}\left[\langle k|V^{(2)}|k\rangle+\sum_{Q}\frac{|\langle k|V^{(1)}|k+Q\rangle|^{2}}{\epsilon_{k}-\epsilon_{k+Q}}(1-f_{k+Q})\right]\;f_{k}

(5)

The terms in the square bracket in Eq.
5
differ from
the terms in the original expression for
\Delta\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)
(Eq.
1
) in the
Allen-Heine theory
Allen and Heine 1976
;
Allen and Hui 1980
, only by the presence of the
(
1-f_{k+Q}
) factor in the second term. The first
(DW) term is the same in both expressions. Due to the (
1-f_{k+Q}
)
factor, the existing computational packages cannot compute the quantity
inside the square bracket in Eq.
5
, but can compute

\Delta\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)
.

We describe below a method to accurately compute Eq.

5
based on the EPI implementation (TDepES) in the
ABINIT software package
Gonze et al. 2009
;
Gonze et al. 2016
;
Abi
.

The ABINIT module TDepES
Abi
(Temperature Dependence of the
Electronic Structure) calculates the EPI correction,
\Delta\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)
, for any eigenenergy,
\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}
.
The underlying theory has been discussed by Poncé
et al.

Poncé et al. 2015
;
Poncé et al. 2014a
. This module has been widely used to
obtain temperature dependent band-structures and band-gaps
Poncé et al. 2015
;
Poncé et al. 2014b
;
Poncé et al. 2014a
;

;

;

;

;

.

We note that the second term in Eq.
5
is
identical to that in the earliest EPI studies of Fröhlich (Eq.2.11)

Fröhlich 1950
and Fan (Eq.4)
Fan 1951
. The presence of
the (
1-f_{k+Q}
) factor is to ensure that the state
k+Q
is an empty
state to satisfy the Pauli principle
Fröhlich 1950
;
Fan 1951
.
For semiconductors and insulators, the Fermi-Dirac distribution function,

f_{k+Q}
and
f_{k}
, equals one for the occupied (valence) states and
equals zero for the unoccupied (conduction) states
Nery et al. 2018
. It
follows that for the second term, the contributions from the occupied bands
is zero due to the (
1-f_{k+Q}
) factor. Hence, the second term only
includes contributions from unoccupied bands. Alternately, by opening the
(
1-f_{k+Q}
) factor, it becomes
\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)-\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{occ}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)
. Thus, Eq.

5
can be rewritten as

\begin{split}\Delta E_{\textrm{EP}}(V,0)&=\sum^{\textrm{occ}}_{n,\textbf{k}}\left[\Delta^{\textrm{DW}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)+\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{unocc}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)\right]\\
&=\sum^{\textrm{occ}}_{n,\textbf{k}}\left[\Delta\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)-\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{occ}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)\right]\\
&=\Delta E^{\textrm{ep}}_{\textrm{bs}}(V,0)-\Delta E_{\textrm{occ}}^{\textrm{FM,tot}}\end{split}

(6)

The equivalent expression, Eq.
6
, shows that
the EPI contribution to the total energy comes from the EPI contribution to
the band-structure energy,
\Delta E^{\textrm{ep}}_{\textrm{bs}}(V,0)
,
corrected by the total FM-occupied contribution,
\Delta E_{\textrm{occ}}^{\textrm{FM,tot}}
.

To compute Eq.
6
, we first run
the TDepES module
Abi
with the optimized number of bands,
M=N_{\textrm{tot}}
, that includes conduction and valence bands
Poncé et al. 2015
.
This will give the EPI correction to the eigenenergy,

\Delta\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)
, the first term in Eq.

6
. We run the TDepES module a second time
with
M=N_{\textrm{val}}
to obtain the FM-occupied contribution,
\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{occ}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(0)
,

Nery et al. 2018
;
Poncé et al. 2014a
the second
term in Eq.
6
. The summation in Eq.

6
can be written as a weighted sum over all
the k-points in the Irreducible Brillouin Zone (IBZ). Thus, the EPI
contribution to the total energy, Eq.
6
, is
computed as

\Delta E_{\textrm{EP}}(V,0)=\\
2\sum_{n,\textbf{k}}^{\textrm{occ,IBZ}}w_{\textbf{k}}\Delta\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(V,0)-2\sum_{n,\textbf{k}}^{\textrm{occ,IBZ}}w_{\textbf{k}}\Delta^{\textrm{FM}}_{\textrm{occ}}\epsilon_{n\textbf{k}}(V,0)

(7)

Using Eq.
7
to include EPI contribution, a more
accurate
ab initio
total energy can be computed for all
semiconductors and insulators.

In conclusion, to include EPI contribution to the total energy, we have
recast Allen’s equation, for semiconductors and insulators. This equivalent
expression can be computed using available software, leading to more
accurate total energy for all semiconductors and
insulators. We have calculated the total energy (EPI included) and their
differences for the C-dia and C-hex polytypes. However, serious theoretical
issues are identified from our studies. They show that the total energy is
not uniquely determined by using the primitive unit cell and is dependent
on the unit cell size. This most surprising result is due to the partial FM
self-energy term present in Allen’s equation. Due to this, to obtain
reliable total energy differences between the C-dia and C-hex polytypes,
instead of primitive cells, it becomes necessary to use supercells with
identical number of atoms. We infer a hidden and non-obvious relation
between the occupied and unoccupied Fan-Midgal terms. A crucial inference
of general validity is that any equation that contains a partial Fan-Midgal
term violates the unit cell independence. This is likely the first case
where per-atom quantities are not uniquely obtained from the primitive unit
cell. Further theoretical studies are needed to establish if the
ab
initio
total energy (EPI included) is an exception or can be reconciled
with the unit cell independence and be uniquely determined from the
primitive unit cell.

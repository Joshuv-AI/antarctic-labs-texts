Abstract
In this paper, We present the geometry three dimensional Heisenberg group ( ℍ 3 , g ) (\mathbb{H}_{3},g) and its geodesics curves. After, we study the Killing magnetic curves and some geodesic Killing magnetic curves with its explicit formulas for such curves. Key words: Heisenberg group; geodesic Killing magnetic curves; Killing vector fields; Killing magnetic curves. MSC: 53A04, 53C25

Introduction

In physics, particularly in electromagnetism, the trajectory of charged
particles moving under the action of a magnetic fields makes an important
research topic. In geometry, this trajectory in any manifold is known as a
magnetic curve.

On a Riemannian manifold, the magnetic curve
\gamma
is modeled as a
solution of two order differential equation
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\varphi\left(\gamma^{\prime}\right)
known as

Lorentz
equation, where
\varphi
is
(1,1)
-tensor field that
present the
Lorentz
force associated to the magnetic fields
F
.
The magnetic curve generalise the geodesic curves in following way: When the
particles move under the absence of the magnetic fields so freely only under
the influence of gravity (i.e.
\varphi\equiv 0
), the
Lorentz

equation is exactly the geodesic equation
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=0.

The magnetic curve was studied intensively in different kind of manifolds in
Riemannian, Lorentzian and generally in pseudo-Riemannian concept. (See
[

]
,
[

,
[
7
]
,
[
18
]
)

Furthermore, if the magnetic fields
F
corresponds to a killing vector,
then the trajectory of particles moving under the action of
F
is called
Killing magnetic curve.

Interesting results on Killing magnetic curves are given in Euclidian
3-space (
[
9
]
), Minkowski 3-space (
[

]
),
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
(

(

2
Preliminaries

On Riemannian manifold
(M,g),
the magnetic curves is a trajectories of the
charged particles moving under the action of the magnetic fields
F
which
can be represented by a closed
2
-form

F(X,Y)=g(\varphi\left(X\right),Y)

(1)

where
X,Y
are vector fields on
(M,g)
and
\varphi
is skew-symmetric
(1,1)
-tensor field. that present the
Lorentz
force associated to
F
. For a regular curve
\gamma:I\subset\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(M,g)
,
\gamma
is called magnetic curve if

\nabla_{\mathbf{t}}\mathbf{t=}\varphi\left(\mathbf{t}\right)

(2)

known as the Lorentz equation, where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection
associated to
g\
and
\mathbf{t}=\gamma^{\prime}
is the speed vector of

\gamma.
The magnetic curve generalise the notion of geodesic curve under
arc-length parametrization.

From the property of magnetic curve
\gamma

\frac{d}{dt}g(\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})=0

\gamma
has a constant speed vector. In particular, if
\gamma
is
parameterized by the arc length, then
\gamma
is called
normal
magnetic curve
.

We call
K

a Killing vector field on
M
if it satisfy the
Killing equation

g(\nabla_{X}K,Y)+g(\nabla_{Y}K,X)=0

for every vector fields
X,Y
on
M
.

We define on M the cross product of two vector fields
X,Y
on
M
as

g(X\times Y,Z)=dvg(X,Y,Z)

(3)

for all vector fields
Z
on
M
and
dvg
denotes the volume form on
M.

Let
F_{K}=\emph{i}_{K}dvg
be the Killing magnetic field corresponding to
the Killing vector field
K
on
M
, where
\emph{i}
is inner product.
Then, the
(1,1)
-tensor fields corresponding to the Lorentz force of
F_{K}

is

\gamma(X)=K\times X.

(4)

Then, we can rewrite the Lorentz equation Eq.(
2
) as

\nabla_{\mathbf{t}}\mathbf{t=}K\times\mathbf{t}

(5)

and the it solution is called
Killing magnetic curves
corresponding
to the killing vector fields
K.

In the sequel, to simplify, we call this curve a
K
-magnetic curve.

3
Geometry structure of
\mathbb{H}_{3}

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{3}
is a quasi-abelian Lie group
diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and it has the standard representation in
GL(3,\mathbb{R})
as

\mathbb{H}_{3}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&x&z\\
0&0&y\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right)\mid\left(x,y,z\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\right\}

endowed with the multiplication

(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})=(x_{1}+x_{2},y_{1}+y_{2},z_{1}+z_{2}-x_{1}y_{2}).

The invariant Riemannian metric with respect to the left-translations
corresponding to that multiplication is denoted by

g=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}dx^{2}+dy^{2}+(xdy+dz)^{2}.

(6)

where
\lambda
is a strictly positive real number. All left-invariant
Riemannian metric on the
\mathbb{H}_{3}
is isometric to the metric
g
.

The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of the metric
g
with respect to the
left-invariant orthonormal basis

e_{1}=\partial y-x\partial z,\ \ e_{2}=\lambda\partial x,\ \ e_{3}=\partial z,

(7)

with dual basis

\omega^{1}=dy;\ \ \omega^{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda}dx;\ \ \omega^{3}=xdy+dz.

are given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}=0\\
\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}=\frac{\lambda}{2}e_{3}\\
\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{3}=-\frac{\lambda}{2}e_{2}\end{array}\begin{array}[]{l}\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}=-\frac{\lambda}{2}e_{3}\\
\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{2}=0\\
\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{3}=\frac{\lambda}{2}e_{1}\end{array}\begin{array}[]{l}\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{1}=-\frac{\lambda}{2}e_{2}\\
\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{2}=\frac{\lambda}{2}e_{1}\\
\nabla_{e_{3}}e_{3}=0\end{array}\right.

(8)

The Lie bracket of the base
\left(e_{i}\right)_{i=\overline{1,3}}
are
given by the following identities

[e_{1},e_{2}]=\lambda e_{3};\ \ \ [e_{1},e_{3}]=[e_{2},e_{3}]=0

(9)

The Lie algebra of Killing vector field of
(\mathbb{H}_{3},g)
is generated
by the following killing vectors

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial z,\ \ \mathbf{K}_{2}=\partial y,\ \ \mathbf{K}_{3}=\partial x-y\partial z\ and

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}y\partial x-x\partial y-\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{2}y^{2}-x^{2})\partial z,

and using the Eq.(
7
), we can rewrite the killing vectors in the base
\left(e_{i}\right)_{i=\overline{1,3}}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{3},\ \ \mathbf{K}_{2}=e_{1}+xe_{3},\ \ \ \ \mathbf{K}_{3}=\frac{1}{\lambda}e_{2}-ye_{3}\ and

(10)

\displaystyle\ \mathbf{K}_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-xe_{1}+\lambda ye_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\lambda^{2}y^{2}-3x^{2})e_{3}.

for more detail see (
[
3
]
).

On the other hand, for the contact form

\eta=xdy+dz=\omega^{3}

(11)

and the
(1,1)
-tensor
\varphi
given in base
\left(e_{i}\right)_{i=\overline{1,3}}
by

\varphi\left(e_{1}\right)=e_{2};\ \varphi\left(e_{2}\right)=-e_{1}\ and\ \varphi\left(e_{3}\right)=0

(12)

we have

\displaystyle\eta\left(e_{3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1;\ \varphi^{2}\left(X\right)=-X+\eta\left(X\right)e_{3}\ and

\displaystyle g(\varphi\left(X\right),\varphi\left(Y\right))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(X,Y)+\eta(X)\eta(Y)

for any
X,Y\in\chi(\mathbb{H}_{3})
and
\xi=e_{3}
, the Heisenberg
group
(\mathbb{H}_{3},\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
is an almost contact
manifold. Moreover, we have

d\eta(X,Y)=g(X,\varphi\left(Y\right))

(13)

then
(\mathbb{H}_{3},\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
is a contact manifold and the
fundamental
2
-form
d\eta
is closed and hence it defines a magnetic
field. For more detail see (
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
16
]
).

\begin{array}[]{l}i.\ \gamma_{G}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x\left(t\right)=c_{1}t+c_{2}\\

Numerical evaluation of dual norms via the MM algorithm
Mauro Bernardi, Marco Stefanucci, Antonio Canale

Abstract
We deal with the problem of numerically computing the dual norm, which is important to study sparsity-inducing regularizations ( Jenatton et al., 2011 ; Bach et al., 2012 ) . The dual norms find application in optimization and statistical learning, for example, in the design of working-set strategies, for characterizing dual gradient methods, for dual decompositions and in the definition of augmented Lagrangian functions. Nevertheless, the dual norm of some well-known sparsity-inducing regolarization methods are not analytically available. Examples are the overlap group ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm of Jenatton et al., 2011 and the elastic net norm of Zou and Hastie, 2005 . Therefore we resort to the Majorization-Minimization principle of Lange, 2016 to provide an efficient algorithm that leverages a reparametrization of the dual constrained optimization problem as unconstrained optimization with barrier. Extensive simulation experiments have been performed in order to verify the correctness of operation, and evaluate the performance of the proposed method. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of the algorithm in retrieving the dual norm even for large dimensions.

Introduction

In this paper we deal with the problem of computing the dual norm, which is important to
study sparsity-inducing regularizations
(
Jenatton et al., 2011
;
Bach et al., 2012
)
.
For any vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
the dual norm
\Omega^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})
of the norm
\Omega(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
(defined over the euclidean space
\mathcal{X}
) is defined as
\Omega^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})=\sup_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\big\{\mathbf{z}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}\,|\,\Omega(\mathbf{z})\leq 1\big\}
. The previous optimization problem can be equivalently rewritten as

\displaystyle\Omega^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})=\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}-\mathbf{z}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}

(1)

\displaystyle\upsilon(\mathbf{z})\geq 0,

(2)

where
\upsilon(\mathbf{z})=1-\Omega(\mathbf{z})\in(0,1)
. Moreover, the dual norm of
\Omega^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})
is
\Omega(\mathbf{x})
itself, and as a consequence, the formula above holds also if the roles of
\Omega(\mathbf{x})
and
\Omega^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})
are exchanged. The dual norms find application in several contexts: in the design of working-set strategies, for characterizing dual gradient methods, for dual decompositions and in the definition of augmented Lagrangian functions that arises in the context of optimization methods
(
Boyd et al., 2011
, see, e.g.)
. Two further interesting applications of dual norms are to verify if the irrepresentable condition holds in the context of model selection consistency of sparsity-inducing penalties,
(
Bühlmann and van de Geer, 2011
, see, e.g.)
and for computing optimality conditions of sparse regularized problems
(
Osborne et al., 2000
, see, e.g.)
. In the latter case, sparse regularized problems involve convex optimizations of the form

\max_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}h(\mathbf{x})+\lambda\Omega(\mathbf{x}),

(3)

for any
\lambda>0
, where
f:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex differentiable function and
\Omega:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
sparsity-inducing—typically nonsmooth and non-euclidean—norm. Simple calculations show that
\mathbf{x}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is optimal for (
3
) if and only if
\lambda^{-1}\nabla f(\mathbf{x}^{\star})\in\partial\Omega(\mathbf{x}^{\star})
where

\partial\Omega(\mathbf{x}^{\star})=\begin{cases}\{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\in\mathbb{R}^{p};\,\Omega^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$})\leq 1\};&\mathrm{if}\quad\mathbf{x}^{\star}=0\\
\{\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}\in\mathbb{R}^{p};\,\Omega^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$})\leq 1,\,\&\,\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}^{\star}=\Omega(\mathbf{x}^{\star})\};&\mathrm{if}\quad\mathbf{x}^{\star}\neq 0.\end{cases}

(4)

From previous conditions it is possible to derive interesting properties of the problem (
3
), as well as efficient algorithms for solving it.
Consider now the problem of checking the irrepresentable condition for (
3
) where, for example,
h(\mathbf{x})=\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|_{2}
and
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is a given design matrix and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is known. We need to calculate the dual norm
\Omega^{\ast}\big({\mathbf{A}}_{0}^{\intercal}{\mathbf{A}}_{1}({\mathbf{A}}_{1}^{\intercal}{\mathbf{A}}_{1})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{1}\big)
, where
\mathbf{A}_{0}
and
\mathbf{A}_{1}
are partitions of the design matrix that corresponds to the true zeros and non-zeros of
\mathbf{x}
, respectively, and
\mathbf{r}_{1}
is the the first derivative of the norm
\Omega(\mathbf{x})
,
\Omega^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})
, evaluated at the optimal solution of (
3
),
\mathbf{x}^{\star}
. The irrepresentable condition is important to study sparse-inducing norms for model selection purposes,
(see, e.g.
Zhao and Yu, 2006
;
Bühlmann and van de Geer, 2011
, for an overview)
.

Special cases of sparsity-inducing norms are, the
\ell_{1}
-norm
(
Tibshirani, 1996
)

\Omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})=\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}|x_{j}|
, the
\ell_{\infty}
-norm
\Omega_{\infty}(\mathbf{x})=\max_{j=1,\dots,p}|x_{j}|
, the
\ell_{2}
-norm
(
Hoerl and Kennard, 2000
)
,
\Omega_{2}(\mathbf{x})=\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}
, the Group
\ell_{2}
-norm
(
Yuan and Lin, 2006
)

\Omega_{G}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}\sqrt{w_{g}}\|\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2}
, where
\mathbf{x}=\{\mathbf{x}_{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{(G)}\}
and each
\mathbf{x}_{(g)}
for
1\leq g\leq G
represents a group from
\mathbf{x}
and
w_{g}
represents the number of weights in
\mathbf{x}_{g}
for
1\leq g\leq G
. In all those cases an analytical solution for the dual norm is available. In particular, the
\ell_{1}
- and
\ell_{\infty}
-norms are dual to each other, and the
\ell_{2}
-norm is self-dual (dual to itself). As concerns the Group
\ell_{2}
-norm
\Omega_{G}(\mathbf{x})
we have instead that
\Omega^{\ast}_{G}(\mathbf{x})=\max_{g=1,\dots,G}\frac{1}{\sqrt{w_{g}}}\|\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2}
. However, for the overlap group
\ell_{2}
-norm, introduced by
Jenatton et al., 2011
as a relaxation of the group
\ell_{2}
-norm that allows for groups to share the same components of the vector
\mathbf{x}
, an analytical solution for the dual norm does not exists. The overlap group LASSO norm finds application in many relevant applied contexts of statistical learning to induce sparse solutions,
(see
Jenatton et al., 2011
, for extensive examples)
. When an analytical solution to the dual norm does not exist we can retrieve optimization methods for solving problem (
1
)-(
2
). However, the computational cost of performing the constrained optimziation in (
1
)-(
2
) is especially high when
p
is large,
(
Jenatton et al., 2011
, see)
.

Previous considerations motivate our idea to provide an efficient algorithm for computing the dual norm for the case where an analytical solution does not exist or direct optimization of (
1
)-(
2
) is not feasible. The proposed algorithm relies on the Majorization-Minimization (MM, hereafter) principle of
Lange, 2013
;
Lange, 2016
for dealing with the constrained optimization problem with penalties or barriers.

2
Barrier methods for constrained optimization

Let us consider the constrained optimization problem

\Omega^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})=\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}-\mathbf{z}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x},\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\upsilon(\mathbf{z})\geq 0,

(5)

where
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\upsilon(\mathbf{z})=1-\Omega(\mathbf{z})\in(0,1)
.
Optimization (
5
) falls into the class of linear programming problems subject to nonlinear constraints, for which there exists lots of efficient solvers
(
Nocedal and Wright, 2006
, See, e.g.)
. However, the huge number of constraints may be a crucial issue that even the most sophisticated tools in the linear programming literature are not able to manage easily. Here, we adopt a different strategy, avoiding reparametrizations and transforming (
5
) onto an unconstrained problem
(
Luenberger and Ye, 2021
, see)
. Now, let us define the Lagrangian function

\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})\equiv\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{z})+\varrho\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{z}),

(6)

as the sum of the objective function
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{z})
and of the penalization term
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{z})
weighted by a positive penalization parameter
\varrho>0
. And, in particular,
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{z})
is chosen to be the negative of the logarithmic transformation of the constraint violation, namely:

\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{z})=-\log(\upsilon(\mathbf{z})).

(7)

Then, assuming that
\varrho
is big enough and requiring other technical but mild conditions

(
Nocedal and Wright, 2006
, Theorem 17.3, Page 507)
, the constrained problem (
5
) shares the
same solution of the penalized unrestricted optimization of the Lagrangian function,
that is
\widehat{\mathbf{z}}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{z}}\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})
. Therefore, the unconstrained optimization problem becomes

\displaystyle\Omega^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})

(8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{z})-\varrho\log(\upsilon(\mathbf{z})),

(9)

for
\varrho\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

2.1
Newton-Raphson solution

Newton-Raphson-type algorithms require the evaluation of the gradient and the hessian matrix of the objective function

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}^{\prime}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{x}-\varrho\frac{\upsilon^{\prime}(\mathbf{z})}{\upsilon(\mathbf{z})}

(10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=-\varrho\frac{\upsilon^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{z})}{\upsilon(\mathbf{z})}+\varrho\frac{\upsilon^{\prime}(\mathbf{z})\big(\upsilon^{\prime}(\mathbf{z})\big)^{\intercal}}{(\upsilon(\mathbf{z}))^{2}},

(11)

where
\upsilon^{\prime}(\mathbf{z})=-\Omega^{\prime}(\mathbf{z})
and
\upsilon^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{z})=-\Omega^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{z})
. In the following section, we provide some examples of calculation of the main ingredients of the Newton-Raphson algorithm (e.g., the first and second derivative of the norm,
\Omega^{\prime}(\mathbf{z})
and
\Omega^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{z})
) for the numerical optimization with barrier, in the special cases of the
\ell_{2}
and group
\ell_{2}
norms. For those norms, the dual solution is available analytically. For the
\ell_{1}
-norm,
\Omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})=\|\mathbf{x}\|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}|x_{j}|
which corresponds to the LASSO penalty
(
Tibshirani, 1996
, see)
, the dual solution exist
\Omega_{1}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})=\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\infty}
, for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. However, in such case the second derivative of the norm is not available since
\Omega_{1}(\mathbf{x})\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
and the Newton-Raphson algorithm fails.

2.1.1
Examples:
\ell_{2}
and group
\ell_{2}
norms

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, for the
\ell_{2}
-norm
\Omega_{2}(\mathbf{x})=\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}
which corresponds to the RIDGE penalty of
Hoerl and Kennard, 2000
, we have:

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}}

(12)

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{\intercal}}{\big(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}\big)^{3}}+\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}}\mathbf{I}_{p}.

(13)

For the Group
\ell_{2}
-norm which corresponds to the Group-LASSO penalty of
Yuan and Lin, 2006
, we have
\Omega_{G}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}\sqrt{w_{g}}\|\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2}
, where
\mathbf{x}=\{\mathbf{x}_{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{(G)}\}
and each
\mathbf{x}_{(g)}
for
1\leq g\leq G
represents a group from
\mathbf{x}
and
w_{g}
represents the number of weights in
\mathbf{x}_{g}
for
1\leq g\leq G
. Therefore:

\displaystyle\Omega_{G}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\sqrt{w_{1}}\mathbf{x}_{1}}{\|\mathbf{x}_{1}\|_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{\sqrt{w_{G}}\mathbf{x}_{G}}{\|\mathbf{x}_{G}\|_{2}}\end{bmatrix}

(14)

\displaystyle\Omega_{G}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{diag}\big(\sqrt{w_{1}}\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{1}),\dots,\sqrt{w_{G}}\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x}_{G})\big).

(15)

Both the
\ell_{2}
-norm
\Omega_{2}(\mathbf{x})
and the group
\ell_{2}
-norm
\Omega_{G}(\mathbf{x})
admit an analytical closed form expressions for the corresponding dual norms
\Omega_{2}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})
and
\Omega_{G}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})
, therefore optimization methods for finding the numerical solution of the optimization problem in equations (
1
)-(
2
) are not required for these norms. In the following section we introduce a Newton-Raphson-type algorithm for dealing with the problem of finding the dual norm of the group
\ell_{2}
-norm with overlapping groups. The analytical solution for the overlap-group
\ell_{2}
-norm is not available,
(
Jenatton et al., 2011
, see, e.g.)
.

2.1.2
Overlap group
\ell_{2}
-norm

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, then the overlap-group-LASSO norm
(
Jenatton et al., 2011
)
, is defined as

\Omega_{OG}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}\|\mathbf{w}_{g}\odot\mathbf{x}\|_{2},

(16)

where
\mathbf{w}_{g}=\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\odot\mathbf{s}_{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathbf{s}_{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

\mathbf{x}

g

\mathbf{x}_{g}=\mathbf{s}_{g}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}

\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{G\times p}

\mathbf{s}_{g}=\big(s_{1}^{(g)},\dots,s_{p}^{(g)}\big)^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

defined as
1

-th component of
\mathbf{x}

and
0
otherwise and
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=\big(\widetilde{w}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{w}_{p}\big)^{\intercal}
with general entry

\widetilde{w}_{l}=\big(\sum_{g=1}^{G}s^{(g)}_{l}\big)^{-1}
for
l=1,\dots,p
. The previous overlap-group
\ell_{2}
-norm can be easily rewritten as

\Omega_{OG}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{g=1}^{G}\|\mathbf{c}_{g}\odot\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2},

(17)

where
\mathbf{x}_{g}=(x_{g,1},\dots,x_{g,n_{g}})=\mathbf{s}_{g}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{g}}
has
g
-th entry corresponding to the elements of
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
that belong to the
g
-th group,
\mathbf{c}_{g}=\big(c_{g,1},\dots,c_{g,n_{g}})\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{g}}
with
n_{g}=\sum_{l=1}^{p}s_{l}^{(g)}
and
\mathbf{c}_{g}=\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}})\big]
where
\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{g})=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(\widetilde{w}_{1})&\cdots&\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\infty)}(\widetilde{w}_{p})\end{bmatrix}
denotes the element-wise indicator function, for
g=1,\dots,G
. When some of the groups overlap, the penalty
\Omega_{OG}(\mathbf{x})
is still a norm (if all covariates are in at least one group) whose ball has singularities when some
\mathbf{x}_{g}
are equal to zero, (which is not our case).

For the overlap-group
\ell_{2}
-norm defined in equation (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\Omega_{OG}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\widetilde{w}_{1}^{2}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{c}_{g}\odot\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
x_{p}\widetilde{w}_{p}^{2}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{c}_{g}\odot\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2}}\end{bmatrix},

(18)

and

\displaystyle\Omega_{G}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\Omega_{OG,(1,1)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})&\Omega_{OG,(1,2)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})&\cdots&\Omega_{OG,(1,p)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})\\
\Omega_{OG,(2,1)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})&\Omega_{OG,(2,2)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})&\cdots&\Omega_{OG,(2,p)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\Omega_{OG,(p,1)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})&\Omega_{OG,(p,2)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})&\cdots&\Omega_{OG,(p,p)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})\\
\end{bmatrix},

(19)

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{OG,(j,j)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{j}^{2}\Bigg(\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{c}_{g}\odot\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2}}-x_{j}^{2}\widetilde{w}_{j}^{2}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\big(\|\mathbf{c}_{g}\odot\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2}\big)^{3}}\Bigg)

(20)

\displaystyle\Omega_{OG,(j,k)}^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=x_{j}x_{k}\widetilde{w}_{j}^{2}\widetilde{w}_{k}^{2}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{\big(\|\mathbf{c}_{g}\odot\mathbf{x}_{g}\|_{2}\big)^{3}}.

(21)

Note that the hessian matrix is singular if and only if there exists a
j\in\{1,\dots,p\}
such that

does not belong to any group, e.g.,
g\notin j
for
g=1,\dots,G
.

3
MM algorithm for barrier methods

Now the challenge is to find an efficient way to optimize
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})
. Fortunately,

\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{z})
is concave, then it can be minorized by a linear function, making so possible to implement a minorization-maximization (MM)
scheme. Before proceeding let us briefly introduce the MM algorithm and its basic properties.

Suppose we want to minimise the objective function
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}\big(\mathbf{z}\big):\Re^{p+1}\to\Re
and denote with
\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}
the current iterate, the MM algorithm proceeds in two steps:

(i)

create a surrogate (majorizer) function
g\big(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}\big):\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies

(i.1)

g\big(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}\big)=\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}\big)
;

(i.2)

g\big(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}\big)\geq\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}\big(\mathbf{z}\big)
, for all
\mathbf{z}
;

(ii)

\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k+1)}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{z}\in\Re^{p+1}}g\big(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}\big)
.

Therefore,
g\big(\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}|\vartheta^{(k)}\big)\leq g\big(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}\big)
, with conditions
(i)
and
(ii)
entails the descent property, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}\big(\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}\big)\leq\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}\big)
. For further references we refer to
Lange, 2010
,
Hunter and Lange, 2004
,
Lange et al., 2014
and to
Wu and Lange, 2010
for a comparison between EM and MM.

One way of improving the barrier method is to change the barrier constant as the iterations proceed. This sounds vague, but matters simplify enormously if we view the construction of an adaptive barrier method from the perspective of the MM algorithm. Proposition
3.1
provide the analytic expression for the majorizer function

\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
, that is the basic ingredient to derive our MM update.

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider the Lagrangian functions
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})
as given in equation (
6
), and define

g(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})=-\mathbf{z}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}-\varrho\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\log(\upsilon(\mathbf{z}))+\varrho\upsilon^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})(\mathbf{z}-\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}),

(22)

where
\upsilon^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
is the gradient of the function
\upsilon(\mathbf{z})
evaluated at
\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}
defined in equation (
2
). Then
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
majorizes
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})
, which means that for any pair
(\mathbf{z},\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
we have
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
, and
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})=\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
if and only if
\mathbf{z}=\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}
.

Proof.

See
Lange, 2013
. The proof is reported here for the sake of completeness
Consider the following inequalities

\displaystyle-\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\log(\upsilon(\mathbf{z}))+\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\log(\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}))+\upsilon^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})(\mathbf{z}-\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})}{\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})}\big(\upsilon(\mathbf{z})-\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\big)+\upsilon^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})(\mathbf{z}-\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\upsilon(\mathbf{z})+\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})+\upsilon^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})(\mathbf{z}-\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0,

(23)

based on the concavity of the functions
\log(y)
and
\upsilon(\mathbf{z})
. Because equality holds throughout when
\mathbf{z}=\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}
, we have identified a novel function majorizing
0
and incorporating a barrier for
\upsilon(\mathbf{z})
. The significance of this discovery is that the surrogate function

g(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})=-\mathbf{z}^{\intercal}\mathbf{x}-\varrho\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\log(\upsilon(\mathbf{z}))+\varrho\upsilon^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})(\mathbf{z}-\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}),

(24)

majorizes
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})
up to an irrelevant additive constant. Minimization of the surrogate function drives
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})
downhill while keeping the inequality constraints inactive. In the limit, one or more of the inequality constraints may become active.
∎

As described by, for instance,
Lange, 2013
,
Lange, 2016
and
Sun et al., 2017
, if
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
is a
majorizer of the convex function
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})
, then the sequence
\{\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}\}
, defined through the recursion formula

\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k+1)}=\arg\max_{\mathbf{z}}\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
, converges to the global minimum of
\mathcal{L}_{\varrho}(\mathbf{z})
.
Unfortunately, in our case, the optimization step of the surrogate function
g(\mathbf{z}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})
in equation (
24
) does not admit a closed form expression, therefore, we must revert to the MM gradient algorithm. The Newton-Raphson’s update for a fixed length
\gamma>0
is

\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k+1)}=\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}-\gamma\big(d^{2}g(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\big)^{-1}dg(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}),

(25)

and requires the first and second differentials

\displaystyle dg(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=df(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})

(26)

\displaystyle d^{2}g(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}|\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})

\displaystyle=d^{2}f(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})-\varrho\upsilon^{\prime\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})+\frac{\varrho}{\upsilon(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})}\upsilon^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\big(\upsilon^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\big)^{\intercal},

(27)

where

4
Application

In this application we consider the dual norm of overlap group
\ell_{2}
-norm,
\Omega_{OG}(\mathbf{x})
introduced by
Bernardi et al., 2021
for kernel penalization in the functional regression context. The norm is defined as

\Omega^{c}_{\cal I}(u_{\mathcal{I}^{c}})=\sum_{b\in{\cal B}^{c}}\|c_{b}^{({\cal I}^{c})}\odot u_{\mathcal{I}^{c}}\|_{2},

(28)

where
{\cal B}\subset\{1,\dots,B,B+1\}
is the set of indices of the true non-zero groups and
{\cal I}\subset\{(m,l):m=1,\dots,M;\,l=1,\dots,L\}
is the set of the indices of the non-zero coefficients
\psi_{m,l}
,
c_{b}=\mathsf{vec}(\mathbf{S}_{b}\odot\mathbf{C})
,
where
\mathbf{S}_{b}
is the
M\times L
selection matrix with general entry
s_{m,l}^{(b)}
, defined as
1
if the parameter
\psi_{m,l}
belongs to group
b
and 0 otherwise and
\mathbf{C}
is the matrix with general element
c_{m,l}
defined as
c_{ml}=\big(\sum_{b=1}^{B+1}s_{m,l}^{(b)}\big)^{-1}
.

To check for the irrepresentable condition condition, we need to calculate the dual norm
\big(\Omega^{c}_{\cal B}\big)^{*}\big({\mathbf{Z}}_{0}^{\intercal}{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}({\mathbf{Z}}_{1}^{\intercal}{\mathbf{Z}}_{1})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{1}\big)
, where
\mathbf{r}_{1}
is the vector containing, for
(m,l)\in\cal I
, the elements

\psi_{m,l}c^{2}_{m,l}\sum_{b\in{\cal B},b:\psi_{b}\ni\psi_{m,l}}\|c_{b}\odot\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}\|_{2}^{-1}
.

5
Conclusion

In this paper we provide a new algorithm for solving numerically the dual norm optimization problem. The methods relies on the Majorization-Minimization principle of
Lange, 2016
combined with barrier methods that efficiently convert a constrained optimization problem into an unconstrained one. Extensive simulation experiments have been performed in order to verify the correctness of operation, and evaluate the performance of the proposed method. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of the algorithm in retrieving the dual norm even for large dimensions.

A Nested Matrix-Tensor Model for Noisy Multi-view Clustering
Mohamed El Amine Seddik, Mastane Achab, Henrique Goulart, Merouane Debbah

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a nested matrix-tensor model which extends the spiked rank-one tensor model of order three. This model is particularly motivated by a multi-view clustering problem in which multiple noisy observations of each data point are acquired, with potentially non-uniform variances along the views. In this case, data can be naturally represented by an order-three tensor where the views are stacked. Given such a tensor, we consider the estimation of the hidden clusters via performing a best rank-one tensor approximation. In order to study the theoretical performance of this approach, we characterize the behavior of this best rank-one approximation in terms of the alignments of the obtained component vectors with the hidden model parameter vectors, in the large-dimensional regime. In particular, we show that our theoretical results allow us to anticipate the exact accuracy of the proposed clustering approach. Furthermore, numerical experiments indicate that leveraging our tensor-based approach yields better accuracy compared to a naive unfolding-based algorithm which ignores the underlying low-rank tensor structure. Our analysis unveils unexpected and non-trivial phase transition phenomena depending on the model parameters, “interpolating” between the typical behavior observed for the spiked matrix and tensor models.

Introduction

Tensor methods have received growing attention
in recent years, especially from a statistical perspective, following the introduction of a statistical model for tensor PCA by
Richard & Montanari 2014
.
In machine learning, these methods are particularly attractive for addressing several unsupervised learning tasks which can be formulated as the extraction of some
low-rank structure
from a (potentially high-dimensional) tensor containing observations or functions thereof (such as high-order moments).
Among the many existing examples, we can mention learning latent variable models such as Dirichlet allocation, topic models, multi-view models and Gaussian mixtures
(
Anandkumar et al. 2014
;
Anandkumar et al. 2015
;
Ge et al. 2015
;
Hsu et al. 2012
;
Hsu & Kakade 2013
;
Janzamin et al. 2019
;
Khouja et al. 2022
;
Bakshi et al. 2022
;
Rahmani et al. 2020
)
; learning probability densities and non-Gaussian mixtures
(
Kargas & Sidiropoulos 2019
;
Singhal et al. 2023
;
Oseledets & Kharyuk 2021
)
; detecting communities from interaction data of (possibly multi-view or time-evolving) networks
(
Anandkumar et al. 2013
;
Huang et al. 2015
;
Gujral et al. 2020
;
Fernandes et al. 2021
)
; and high-order co-clustering
(
Papalexakis et al. 2012
)
.

Despite its simplicity, the statistical model of
Richard & Montanari 2014
, sometimes called a
rank-one spiked tensor model
, has raised many theoretical challenges. A significant amount of work has been done to understand the fundamental questions related to this model
(
Perry et al. 2020
;
Jagannath et al. 2020
;
Goulart et al. 2022
;
Auddy & Yuan 2022
;
Ben Arous et al. 2021
;
Seddik et al. 2021
)
, in particular involving statistical thresholds and the asymptotic performance of estimators in the large-dimensional limit.
However, the findings of these works have a somewhat limited practical impact due to the rank-one nature of that model, motivating the development and study of more sophisticated statistical models for the analysis of tensor methods.
In particular, phase transitions associated with multi-spiked tensor models of rank
r>1
have been considered by
Chen et al. 2021
;
Lesieur et al. 2017
.

In this work, we take another path towards bridging the gap between theory and practical applications, by proposing a statistical
nested matrix-tensor model
that generalizes the (third-order) rank-one spiked tensor model and is motivated by a problem that we call
noisy multi-view clustering
, which can be formulated as follows.
Let
{\mathbf{M}}={\bm{\mu}}{\bm{y}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{Z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
be a data matrix containing
n
observations of
p
-dimensional vectors centered around
\pm{\bm{\mu}}
(i.e., data are made of two classes), with
{\bm{y}}\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
holding their corresponding labels and
{\mathbf{Z}}
a Gaussian matrix modeling data dispersion.
Now, suppose that we are given
m
different noisy observations of
{\mathbf{M}}
with potentially different signal-to-noise ratios (SNR), denoted by:

\tilde{{\mathbf{X}}}_{k}={\bm{\mu}}{\bm{y}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{Z}}+\tilde{{\mathbf{W}}}_{k},\quad k=1,\ldots,m,

where
\tilde{\mathbf{W}}_{k}
is a
p\times n
matrix comprising independent Gaussian entries drawn from
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{k}^{2})
.
Assuming that the variances
\sigma_{k}^{2}
are known (or can be accurately estimated), one can build a tensor
{\bm{\mathsfit{X}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n\times m}
containing normalized slices
{\mathbf{X}}_{k}=h_{k}\tilde{{\mathbf{X}}}_{k}
, with
h_{k}:=1/\sigma_{k}
, so that:

\displaystyle{\bm{\mathsfit{X}}}=\left({\bm{\mu}}{\bm{y}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{Z}}\right)\otimes{\bm{h}}+{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}\,\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times n\times m},

(Nested Matrix-Tensor Model)

where the tensor
{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}
has independent standard Gaussian entries and
{\bm{h}}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

The above model can be seen as a more general version of the rank-one spiked model that incorporates a nested structure allowing for more flexible modeling (Specifically, when the variances of the elements in
{\mathbf{Z}}
tend to zero, one recovers the rank-one spiked model).
The common low-rank structure in the slices
{\mathbf{X}}_{k}
, which can be interpreted as different views of the data, encodes the latent clustering structure that can then be retrieved by using tensor methods applied on
{\bm{\mathsfit{X}}}
.

In particular, our results precisely quantify the asymptotic performance of a simple estimator of the vectors
{\bm{\mu}},{\bm{y}}
, and
{\bm{h}}
based on rank-one approximation of
{\bm{\mathsfit{X}}}
, in the large-dimensional limit where
p,n,m\to\infty
at the same rate.
This is achieved by resorting to the recently developed approach of
Goulart et al. 2022
and
Seddik et al. 2021
, which allows one to use tools from random matrix theory by inspecting
contractions
of the random tensor model in question.
Numerical results are given to illustrate the usefulness of such predictions even for moderately large values of
p
and
n
, and also to show the superiority of such a tensor-based approach in comparison with a naive spectral method that does not take the tensor structure of the model into account.
Quite interestingly, our results show that the performance of such a rank-one spectral estimator exhibits different phase transition behaviors depending on two parameters governing the SNR and the data dispersion, effectively “interpolating” between phase transition curves that are characteristic of matrix and tensor models.

Key contributions:
Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

1.

We introduce a nested matrix-tensor model that generalizes the (third-order) spiked tensor model, and we provide a random matrix analysis of its best rank-one tensor approximation in the high-dimensional regime.

2.

We provide an application of this model to the problem of clustering multi-view data and show that the developed theory allows the exact characterization of the asymptotic performance of a multi-view clustering approach. Further simulations suggest the superiority of the tensor-based clustering approach compared to a naive unfolding method that ignores the hidden rank-one structure.

Related work on tensor multi-view methods:

In multi-view machine learning
(
Xu et al. 2013
;
Zhao et al. 2017
;
Sun 2013
)
, one has to deal with data
coming from different sources or exhibiting various statistical or physical natures (e.g. documents composed of both text and images).
The main challenge consists in jointly leveraging both the agreement and the complementarity of the different views
(
Blum & Mitchell 1998
;
Dasgupta et al. 2001
;
Nigam & Ghani 2000
)
, e.g. via learning a shared latent subspace
(
White et al. 2012
)
for diverse tasks such as regression
(
Kakade & Foster 2007
)
or clustering
(
Chaudhuri et al. 2009
;
Gao et al. 2015
;
Cao et al. 2015
)
.
In this context, multi-view clustering algorithms using a low-rank tensor representation of the multi-view data have already been proposed: among others,
Xie et al. 2018
;
Wu et al. 2020
relied on tensor-SVD
(
Kilmer et al. 2013
)
while
Liu et al. 2013
favored a Tucker-type tensor decomposition.

However, the usual sense employed for the term “multi-view clustering” is not exactly the same that we adopt here, since in our problem all views essentially hold noisy measurements of the same quantities.
Hence, our work is perhaps closer in spirit to certain tensor-based clustering models comprising an additional diversity (e.g., temporal), such as those of
Papalexakis et al. 2012
or those reviewed in
Fernandes et al. 2021
.
Yet, it differs from this literature in that our additional diversity is quite specific (namely, it comes from the availability of multiple measurements for each individual in the sample) and, furthermore, we derive the exact asymptotic performance of our proposed tensor-based method in the large-dimensional limit.

\{1,\ldots,n\}
is denoted by
[n]

is denoted by
{\mathbb{S}}^{p-1}
.
The Dirac measure at some real value
x
is denoted by
\delta_{x}
. The support of a measure
\nu
is denoted by
\mathrm{Supp}(\nu)
. The inner product between two vectors
{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}
is denoted by
\langle{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}\rangle=\sum_{i}{u}_{i}{v}_{i}
. The imaginary part of a complex number
z
is denoted by
\Im[z]
. The set of eigenvalues of a matrix
{\mathbf{M}}
is denoted by
\mathrm{Sp}({\mathbf{M}})
. Almost sure convergence of a sequence of random variables is denoted by
\asto
. The arrow
\toind
denotes the convergence in distribution.

2.1
Tensor Notations and Contractions

In this section, we introduce the main tensor notations and definitions used throughout the paper, which we recommend following carefully for a clear understanding of its technical contents.

Three-order tensors:
The set of third-order tensors of size
n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}
is denoted
{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. The scalar
{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}
or
[{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}]_{ijk}
denotes the
(i,j,k)
entry of a tensor
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
.

Rank-one tensors:
A tensor
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
is said to be of rank-one if it can be represented as the outer product of three real-valued vectors
({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{2}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{3}}
. In this case, we write
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}={\bm{x}}\otimes{\bm{y}}\otimes{\bm{z}}
, where the outer product is defined such that
[{\bm{x}}\otimes{\bm{y}}\otimes{\bm{z}}]_{ijk}={x}_{i}{y}_{j}{z}_{k}
.

Tensor contractions:
The first mode contraction of a tensor
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
with a vector
{\bm{x}}
yields a matrix denoted
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{x}},\cdot,\cdot)
with entries
[{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{x}},\cdot,\cdot)]_{jk}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}{x}_{i}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}
. Similarly,
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}(\cdot,{\bm{y}},\cdot)
and
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}(\cdot,\cdot,{\bm{z}})
denote the second and third mode contractions of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
with vectors
{\bm{y}}
and
{\bm{z}}
respectively. We will sometimes denote these contractions by
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{x}})
,
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{y}})
, and
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{z}})
if there is no ambiguity.
The contraction of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
with two vectors
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}}
is a vector denoted
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},\cdot)
with entries
[{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},\cdot)]_{k}=\sum_{ij}{x}_{i}{y}_{j}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}
. Similarly, the contraction of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
with three vectors
{\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}}
is a scalar denoted
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})=\sum_{ijk}{x}_{i}{y}_{j}{z}_{k}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}
.

Tensor norms:
The Frobenius norm of a tensor
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
is denoted
\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}\|_{F}
with
\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{ijk}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}^{2}
. The spectral norm of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
is
\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}\|=\sup_{\|{\bm{u}}\|=\|{\bm{v}}\|=\|{\bm{w}}\|=1}|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})|
.

Best rank-one approximation:
A best rank-one approximation of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
corresponds to a rank-one tensor of the form
\lambda{\bm{u}}\otimes{\bm{v}}\otimes{\bm{w}}
, where
\lambda>0
and
{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}}
are unitary vectors, that minimizes the square loss
\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}-\lambda{\bm{u}}\otimes{\bm{v}}\otimes{\bm{w}}\|_{F}^{2}
. The latter generalizes to tensors the concept of singular value and vectors
(
Lim 2005
)
and the scalar
\lambda
coincides with the spectral norm of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
.
Such a best rank-one approximation can be computed via
tensor power iteration
which consists of iterating:

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}\leftarrow{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}(\cdot,{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})/\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}(\cdot,{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})\|,\quad{\bm{v}}\leftarrow{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},\cdot,{\bm{w}})/\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},\cdot,{\bm{w}})\|,\quad{\bm{w}}\leftarrow{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},\cdot)/\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},\cdot)\|,

starting from some appropriate initialization
(
Kofidis & Regalia 2002
;
Anandkumar et al. 2014
)
.

2.2
Random Matrix Theory

In this section, we recall some necessary tools from random matrix theory (RMT) which are at the core of our main results. Specifically, we will consider the
resolvent
formalism of
Hachem et al. 2007
which allows one to characterize the spectral behavior of large symmetric random matrices and the estimation of low-dimensional functionals of such matrices. Given a symmetric matrix
{\mathbf{S}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
, the resolvent of
{\mathbf{S}}
is defined as
{\mathbf{R}}(\xi)=\left({\mathbf{S}}-\xi{\bm{I}}_{n}\right)^{-1}
for some
\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Sp}({\mathbf{S}})
.

In essence, RMT focuses on describing the distribution of eigenvalues of large random matrices. Typically, under certain technical assumptions on some random matrix
{\mathbf{S}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
, the
empirical spectral measure
of
{\mathbf{S}}
, defined as
\hat{\nu}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}
, converges in the weak sense
(
Van Der Vaart & Wellner 1996
)
to some deterministic probability measure
\nu
as
n\to\infty
and RMT aims at describing such a
\nu
. To this end, one widely considered (so-called analytical) approach relies on the
Stieltjes transform

(
Widder 1938
)
. Given a probability measure
\nu
, the Stieltjes transform of
\nu
is defined as
g_{\nu}(\xi)=\int\frac{d\nu(\lambda)}{\lambda-\xi}
with
\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathrm{Supp}(\nu)
, and the inverse formula allows one to describe the density of
\nu
as
\nu(dx)=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\Im[g_{\nu}(x+i\varepsilon)]
(assuming it admits one).

The Stieltjes transform of the empirical spectral measure,
\hat{\nu}_{n}
, is closely related to the resolvent of
{\mathbf{S}}
through the normalized trace operator. In fact,
g_{\hat{\nu}_{n}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{n}\Tr{\mathbf{R}}(\xi)
and the point-wise
almost sure
convergence of
g_{\hat{\nu}_{n}}(\xi)
to some deterministic Stieltjes transform
g_{\nu}(\xi)
(where
\nu
is defined on
\mathbb{R}
) on the upper-half complex plane is equivalent to the weak convergence of
\hat{\nu}_{n}
to
\nu

(
Tao 2012
)
. Our analysis relies on estimating quantities involving
\frac{1}{n}\Tr{\mathbf{R}}(\xi)
, making the use of the resolvent approach a natural choice (see Appendix
A
for the derivation of our results).

3
Main Results

3.1
The Nested Matrix-Tensor Model

We start by defining our considered nested matrix-tensor model in a general form since it might have applications beyond the multi-view data model in Eq. (
Nested Matrix-Tensor Model
). Let
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
and further denote
n_{M}=n_{1}+n_{2}
and
n_{T}=n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
.
We consider the following statistical model:

{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}=\beta_{T}{\mathbf{M}}\otimes{\bm{z}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}\,\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}},\quad{\mathbf{M}}=\beta_{M}{\bm{x}}\otimes{\bm{y}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}\,\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}},

(1)

where we assume that
\|{\bm{x}}\|=\|{\bm{y}}\|=\|{\bm{z}}\|=1
and that the entries of
{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}
and
{\mathbf{Z}}
are independent Gaussian random variables, with
{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2})
and
{\mathsfit{Z}}_{ij}\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,\sigma_{M}^{2})
. For the sake of simplicity, we consider the unit variance case
\sigma_{T}=\sigma_{M}=1
in the remainder of the paper while we defer the general variance case to Appendix
A
.

Remark 1
(Spectral normalization)

Note that the normalization of
{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}
by
\sqrt{n_{T}}
(resp.
{\bm{Z}}
by
\sqrt{n_{M}}
) in Eq. (
1
) ensures that the spectral norm of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
is of order
O(1)
when the dimensions
n_{i}
grow to infinity. This follows from a standard concentration result
(
Seddik et al. 2021
, Lemma 4)
.

Best rank-one tensor estimator:
We consider the analysis of the best rank-one approximation of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
which corresponds to the following problem
(
Lim 2005
)
:

\argmin_{\lambda>0,\,\|{\bm{u}}\|\|{\bm{v}}\|\|{\bm{w}}\|=1}\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}-\lambda{\bm{u}}\otimes{\bm{v}}\otimes{\bm{w}}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\argmax_{\|{\bm{u}}\|\|{\bm{v}}\|\|{\bm{w}}\|=1}{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})\ .

(2)

In particular, the solution for the scalar
\lambda
in the left-hand problem coincides with the spectral norm of
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
, i.e.,
\lambda=\|{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}\|
.
Given a critical point
(\lambda,{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})
of that problem,
it holds that
(
Lim 2005
)
:

{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}(\cdot,{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})=\lambda{\bm{u}},\quad{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},\cdot,{\bm{w}})=\lambda{\bm{v}},\quad{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},\cdot)=\lambda{\bm{w}},\quad\lambda={\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}}).

(3)

In essence, for sufficiently large
\beta_{M}
and
\beta_{T}
, the triplet
({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})
will start to align with the signal components
({\bm{x}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{z}})
and our main goal is to quantify these alignments (i.e., the inner products
\langle{\bm{u}},{\bm{x}}\rangle
,
\langle{\bm{v}},{\bm{y}}\rangle
and
\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle
) in the large dimensional regime when
n_{i}\to\infty
. To this end, we need a typical set of assumptions that we formulate as follows (see
(
Goulart et al. 2022
;
Seddik et al. 2021
)
for similar assumptions in the case of spiked random tensors).

Assumption 1

There exists a sequence of critical points
(\lambda,{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})
satisfying Eq. (
3
) such that,
when
n_{i}\to\infty
with
\frac{n_{1}}{n_{T}}\to c_{1}>0,\frac{n_{2}}{n_{T}}\to c_{2}>0,\frac{n_{3}}{n_{T}}\to c_{3}>0
, we have the following:

\lambda\asto\bar{\lambda},\quad|\langle{\bm{u}},{\bm{x}}\rangle|\asto\alpha_{1},\quad|\langle{\bm{v}},{\bm{y}}\rangle|\asto\alpha_{2},\quad|\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle|\asto\alpha_{3}.

In the remainder of the paper, we refer to the quantities
(\lambda,\langle{\bm{u}},{\bm{x}}\rangle,\langle{\bm{v}},{\bm{y}}\rangle,\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle)
as
summary statistics
as per the formalism introduced by
Ben Arous et al. 2022
since the asymptotic limits of these scalar quantities fully describe the asymptotic behavior of the considered best rank-one tensor estimator applied to
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}
.

Remark 2
(On Assumption
1
)

The almost sure convergence of the summary statistics has been demonstrated in
(
Jagannath et al. 2020
)
in the case of the spiked tensor model. We believe similar arguments can be extended to our proposed nested matrix-tensor model to validate Assumption
1
.

3.2
Associated Random Matrix

As discussed in the previous section, our primary goal is to compute the asymptotic summary statistics
(\bar{\lambda},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
in terms of the model’s parameters, namely, the signal-to-noise ratios
(\beta_{M},\beta_{T})
and the dimension ratios
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})
.
To this end, we follow the approach developed by
Seddik et al. 2021
, who studied the
asymmetric
spiked tensor model, and where it has been shown that the estimation of
(\bar{\lambda},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
boils down to the analysis of the
block-wise contraction random matrix

{\bm{\Phi}}
in Eq.(
4
), which can be done by deploying tools from random matrix theory.

\displaystyle{\bm{\Phi}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{0}}_{n_{1}\times n_{1}}&{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{w}})&{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{v}})\\
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{w}})^{\top}&{\bm{0}}_{n_{2}\times n_{2}}&{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}})\\
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{v}})^{\top}&{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}})^{\top}&{\bm{0}}_{n_{3}\times n_{3}}\end{bmatrix}

(4)

Given the model in Eq. (
1
), it can be easily noticed that
{\bm{\Phi}}
decomposes as a sum of two matrices
{\mathbf{H}}+{\mathbf{L}}
where
{\mathbf{L}}
is a low-rank matrix related to the signal part in the nested matrix-tensor model (the expression of
{\mathbf{L}}
is provided in Eq. (
17
) in Appendix
A
), and
{\mathbf{H}}
corresponds to the noise part of the model, being given by:

{\mathbf{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\bm{0}}_{n_{1}\times n_{1}}&\frac{\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{w}})&\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{v}})\\
\frac{\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{w}})^{\top}&{\bm{0}}_{n_{2}\times n_{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}})\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{v}})^{\top}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}})^{\top}&{\bm{0}}_{n_{3}\times n_{3}}\end{bmatrix}.

(5)

Remark 3
(On the spectrum of
{\bm{\Phi}}
)

In terms of spectrum, we will see subsequently that the matrices
{\bm{\Phi}}
and
{\mathbf{H}}
share the same “bulk” of eigenvalues while the spectrum of
{\bm{\Phi}}
exhibits two isolated eigenvalues at positions
2\lambda
and
-\lambda
if
\beta_{M},\beta_{T}
are large enough. In fact, one can quickly check that, given the identities in Eq. (
3
), the scalars
2\lambda
and
-\lambda
are eigenvalues of
{\bm{\Phi}}
with respective multiplicities
1
and
2
, and respective eigenvectors
({\bm{u}}^{\top},{\bm{v}}^{\top},{\bm{w}}^{\top})^{\top}
for the eigenvalue
2\lambda

and
({\bm{u}}^{\top},{\bm{0}}^{\top},-{\bm{w}}^{\top})^{\top},({\bm{0}}^{\top},{\bm{v}}^{\top},-{\bm{w}}^{\top})^{\top}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
-\lambda
.

Theorem 1
(Limiting spectrum)

Under Assumption
1
, the empirical spectral measure of
{\mathbf{H}}
or
{\bm{\Phi}}
converges weakly almost surely to a deterministic distribution
\nu
whose Stieltjes transform is given by
g(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}g_{i}(\xi)
such that
\Im[g(\xi)]>0
for
\Im[\xi]>0
, and where
(g_{i}(\xi))_{i\in[3]}

5
Conclusion & Perspectives

We introduced the nested matrix-tensor model and provided a high-dimensional analysis of its best rank-one approximation, relying on random matrix theory. Our analysis has brought theoretical insights into the problem of muti-view clustering and demonstrates the ability of random matrix tools to assess the theoretical performance of the considered clustering method. This paves the way for an elaborated theoretical assessment and improvement of more sophisticated tensor-based methods. In particular, our present findings address only the case of binary clustering by considering the rank-one matrix model
{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{y}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{Z}}
which can be extended to higher ranks, thereby modeling a multi-class problem. Besides, such an extension would require the analysis of more sophisticated tensor methods (e.g. the block-term decomposition
(
De Lathauwer 2008
)
) which is more challenging compared to the present best rank-one estimator. Nevertheless, we believe our present work constitutes a fundamental basis for the development of more general results.

\langle{\bm{u}},{\bm{x}}\rangle\to\alpha_{1}
and (for similar reason) say
\langle{\bm{v}},{\bm{y}}\rangle\to\alpha_{2}
and
\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle\to\alpha_{3}
.
To maintain consistency, we will adhere to the convention of using
{x}_{i}
or
{u}_{i}
to represent the components of the first mode,
{y}_{j}
or
{v}_{j}
to represent the components of the second mode and
{z}_{k}
or
{w}_{k}
to represent the components of the third mode. In the remainder, if some quantity expresses as
a(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}b_{i}(n)
, the notation
a(n)\asymp b_{j}(n)
for some
j\in[r]
means that
b_{j}(n)
is the only contributing term of
a(n)
for estimating the quantities of interest (limiting spectrum, summary statistics) as
n\to\infty
:
this approximation is considered based on the results developed in
(
Seddik et al. 2021
)
which identified such contributing terms.

A.1
Proof of
Theorem

3

We start by expressing the expected value of
\lambda
via Eq. (
3
):

\mathbb{E}[\lambda]=\mathbb{E}[{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})]=\mathbb{E}[\beta_{T}({\mathbf{M}}\otimes{\bm{z}})({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}[{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})]\\
=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}[\beta_{T}\beta_{M}\langle{\bm{x}},{\bm{u}}\rangle\langle{\bm{y}},{\bm{v}}\rangle\langle{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}}\rangle]}_{\to\beta_{T}\beta_{M}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}}+\beta_{T}\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\bm{u}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Z}}{\bm{v}}\underbrace{\langle{\bm{z}},{\bm{w}}\rangle}_{\to\alpha_{3}}]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}[{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})]\ .

(8)

Then, let us rewrite the last term in Eq. (
8
) by using Stein’s lemma:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\mathbb{E}[{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{u}_{i}{v}_{j}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\sigma_{T}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\frac{\partial({u}_{i}{v}_{j}{w}_{k})}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}]\\
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\sigma_{T}^{2}\mathbb{E}[\frac{\partial{u}_{i}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}{v}_{j}{w}_{k}]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\sigma_{T}^{2}\mathbb{E}[{u}_{i}\frac{\partial{v}_{j}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}{w}_{k}]+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\sigma_{T}^{2}\mathbb{E}[{u}_{i}{v}_{j}\frac{\partial{w}_{k}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}]\ .

In order to compute the partial derivatives appearing in the equation above, we recall from Eq. (
3
) that

{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}(\cdot,{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})=\lambda{\bm{u}}\iff\forall i,\quad\lambda{u}_{i}=\sum_{jk}{v}_{j}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}\ ,

which implies:

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{u}_{i}+\lambda\frac{\partial{u}_{i}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}=\sum_{jk}\frac{\partial{v}_{j}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}+\sum_{jk}{v}_{j}\frac{\partial{w}_{k}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}+\sum_{jk}{v}_{j}{w}_{k}\underbrace{\frac{\partial{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\delta_{ia}\delta_{jb}\delta_{kc}}\\
=[{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{w}})\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}]_{i}+[{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{v}})\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}]_{i}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{v}_{b}{w}_{c}\delta_{ia}\ .

Equivalently, in a more compact vectorial form,

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{\bm{u}}+\lambda\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}={\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{w}})\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}+{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{v}})\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{v}_{b}{w}_{c}{\bm{e}}_{a}^{n_{1}}\ ,

(9)

where
{\bm{e}}_{a}^{n_{1}}
denotes the standard basis vector of dimension
n_{1}
with all coordinates equal to 0 except at position
a
, where it is equal to 1.

Similarly, one can show that

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{\bm{v}}+\lambda\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}={\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{w}})^{\top}\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}+{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}})\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{u}_{a}{w}_{c}{\bm{e}}_{b}^{n_{2}}\ ,

(10)

and

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{\bm{w}}+\lambda\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}={\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}})^{\top}\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}+{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{v}})^{\top}\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{u}_{a}{v}_{b}{\bm{e}}_{c}^{n_{3}}\ .

(11)

Let us now compute
\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}
. We have by Eq. (
3
),

\lambda={\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})=\sum_{ijk}{u}_{i}{v}_{j}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}\ ,

from which we deduce:

\frac{\partial\lambda}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}=\sum_{ijk}\frac{\partial{u}_{i}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{v}_{j}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}+\sum_{ijk}{u}_{i}\frac{\partial{v}_{j}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}+\sum_{ijk}{u}_{i}{v}_{j}\frac{\partial{w}_{k}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}+\sum_{ijk}\frac{{u}_{i}{v}_{j}{w}_{k}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\delta_{ia}\delta_{jb}\delta_{kc}\\
=\frac{{u}_{a}{v}_{b}{w}_{c}}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\ ,

(12)

where the last equality follows from observing that the three first sums in Eq. (
12
) are equal to zero.
Indeed, recalling that
\|{\bm{u}}\|^{2}
is constant equal to
1
, we have for the first sum:

\sum_{ijk}\frac{\partial{u}_{i}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}{v}_{j}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{T}}_{ijk}={\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}(\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}},{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}})=\lambda{\bm{u}}^{\top}\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{\partial\|{\bm{u}}\|^{2}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}=0\ ,

(13)

and the same holds for the two other sums.

By combining
Equations

9
,
10
,
11
and
12
, we have

\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}\\
\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}\\
\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}\end{bmatrix}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\underbrace{\left(\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}0&{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{w}})&{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{v}})\\
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{w}})^{\top}&0&{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}})\\
{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{v}})^{\top}&{\bm{\mathsfit{T}}}({\bm{u}})^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}-\lambda{\bm{I}}_{n_{T}}\right)^{-1}}_{R(\lambda)}\begin{bmatrix}{v}_{b}{w}_{c}({\bm{e}}_{a}^{n_{1}}-{u}_{a}{\bm{u}})\\
{u}_{a}{w}_{c}({\bm{e}}_{b}^{n_{2}}-{v}_{b}{\bm{v}})\\
{u}_{a}{v}_{b}({\bm{e}}_{c}^{n_{3}}-{w}_{c}{\bm{w}})\end{bmatrix}\ .

(14)

Similarly, one can also show that

\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ab}}\\
\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ab}}\\
\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ab}}\end{bmatrix}=-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\left({\bm{\Phi}}-\lambda{\bm{I}}_{n_{T}}\right)^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle{v}_{b}({\bm{e}}_{a}^{n_{1}}-{u}_{a}{\bm{u}})\\
\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle{u}_{a}({\bm{e}}_{b}^{n_{2}}-{v}_{b}{\bm{v}})\\
{u}_{a}{v}_{b}{\bm{z}}-\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle{u}_{a}{v}_{b}{\bm{w}}\end{bmatrix}\ .

(15)

By discarding asymptotically negligible terms in
{\bm{\Phi}}
, we consider the asymptotically equivalent matrix
{\mathbf{H}}\asymp{\bm{\Phi}}={\mathbf{H}}+{\mathbf{L}}
given by:

{\mathbf{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{w}})&\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{v}})\\
\frac{\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{w}})^{\top}&0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}})\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{v}})^{\top}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}})^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}\ ,

(16)

such that
{\mathbf{L}}={\bm{\Phi}}-{\mathbf{H}}
is equal to

\begin{bmatrix}0&\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle\beta_{T}\beta_{M}{\bm{x}}\otimes{\bm{y}}&\langle{\bm{v}},{\bm{y}}\rangle\beta_{T}\beta_{M}{\bm{x}}\otimes{\bm{z}}+\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}({\mathbf{Z}}{\bm{y}})\otimes{\bm{z}}\\
\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle\beta_{T}\beta_{M}{\bm{y}}\otimes{\bm{x}}&0&\langle{\bm{u}},{\bm{x}}\rangle\beta_{T}\beta_{M}{\bm{y}}\otimes{\bm{z}}+\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}({\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})\otimes{\bm{z}}\\
\langle{\bm{v}},{\bm{y}}\rangle\beta_{T}\beta_{M}{\bm{z}}\otimes{\bm{x}}+\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\bm{z}}\otimes({\mathbf{Z}}{\bm{y}})&\langle{\bm{u}},{\bm{x}}\rangle\beta_{T}\beta_{M}{\bm{z}}\otimes{\bm{y}}+\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\bm{z}}\otimes({\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})&0\end{bmatrix}\ .

(17)

For convenience, let us denote
\beta_{T}^{\prime}=\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle\beta_{T}

and the matrix-valued function
{\mathbf{G}}
:

{\mathbf{G}}(\xi)=({\mathbf{H}}-\xi{\bm{I}}_{n_{T}})^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}^{11}(\xi)&{\mathbf{G}}^{12}(\xi)&{\mathbf{G}}^{13}(\xi)\\
{\mathbf{G}}^{12}(\xi)^{\top}&{\mathbf{G}}^{22}(\xi)&{\mathbf{G}}^{23}(\xi)\\
{\mathbf{G}}^{13}(\xi)^{\top}&{\mathbf{G}}^{23}(\xi)^{\top}&{\mathbf{G}}^{33}(\xi)\end{bmatrix}\ ,

(18)

which implies that
{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}(\xi)=\xi{\mathbf{G}}(\xi)+{\bm{I}}_{n_{T}}
.

Plus, observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}

\displaystyle\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{11}{v}_{b}{w}_{c}{\bm{e}}_{a}^{n_{1}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{\partial{u}_{a}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{aa}{v}_{b}{w}_{c}\ ,

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}

\displaystyle\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{22}{u}_{a}{w}_{c}{\bm{e}}_{b}^{n_{2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{\partial{v}_{b}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\textnormal{G}}^{22}_{bb}{u}_{a}{w}_{c}\ ,

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}

\displaystyle\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\mathbf{G}}^{33}{u}_{a}{v}_{b}{\bm{e}}_{c}^{n_{3}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{\partial{w}_{c}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{abc}}\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\textnormal{G}}^{33}_{cc}{u}_{a}{v}_{b}\ ,

(21)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ab}}

\displaystyle\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\alpha_{3}{v}_{b}{\mathbf{G}}^{11}{\bm{e}}_{a}^{n_{1}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{\partial{u}_{a}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ab}}\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{aa}\alpha_{3}{v}_{b}\ ,

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\bm{v}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ab}}

\displaystyle\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\alpha_{3}{u}_{a}{\mathbf{G}}^{22}{\bm{e}}_{b}^{n_{2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{\partial{v}_{b}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ab}}\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\textnormal{G}}^{22}_{bb}\alpha_{3}{u}_{a}\ .

(23)

Let us now compute
\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{G}})]
.
First note that
\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})=\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{11}+\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{22}+\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{33}

decomposes as the sum of the traces of three blocks.

Block (1,1)

We have

({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{11}=\left[\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{w}})\right]{\mathbf{G}}^{12\top}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{v}}){\mathbf{G}}^{13\top}

(24)

and thus

({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{11}_{ii}=\left(\left[\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{w}})\right]{\mathbf{G}}^{12\top}\right)_{ii}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\left({\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{v}}){\mathbf{G}}^{13\top}\right)_{ii}\\
=\left(\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}{\mathbf{G}}^{12\top}\right)_{ii}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\left({\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{w}}){\mathbf{G}}^{12\top}\right)_{ii}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{jk}{v}_{j}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ik}^{13}\\
=\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{j}{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{jk}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{jk}{v}_{j}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ik}^{13}.

We deduce that

\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{11}]=\frac{1}{n_{T}}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}[({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{11}_{ii}]\\
=\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}[{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}]+\frac{1}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}]+\frac{1}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{v}_{j}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ik}^{13}]\ .

(25)

Once again, we will use Stein’s lemma to compute
\mathbb{E}[{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}]
. For that purpose,
we need the derivative of
{\mathbf{G}}
w.r.t.
{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}
:

{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}}=\xi{\mathbf{G}}+{\bm{I}}_{n_{T}}\Rightarrow\frac{\partial{\mathbf{H}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}{\mathbf{G}}+{\mathbf{H}}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{G}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}=\xi\frac{\partial{\mathbf{G}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}\Rightarrow({\mathbf{H}}-\xi{\bm{I}}_{n_{T}})\frac{\partial{\mathbf{G}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}=-\frac{\partial{\mathbf{H}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}{\mathbf{G}}\\
\Rightarrow\frac{\partial{\mathbf{G}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}=-{\mathbf{G}}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{H}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}{\mathbf{G}}.

In the last equation, observe that

\frac{\partial{\mathbf{H}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{\beta_{T}\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}\top}+\langle{\bm{z}},\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}\rangle\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}&0\\
\frac{\beta_{T}\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}\top}+\langle{\bm{z}},\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}\rangle\frac{\beta_{T}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}\\
\asymp\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{\beta_{T}\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}\top}&0\\
\frac{\beta_{T}\langle{\bm{w}},{\bm{z}}\rangle}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}\top}&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}\ .

Hence,

\frac{\partial{\mathbf{G}}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}^{12}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}\top}&{\mathbf{G}}^{11}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}\top}&0\\
{\mathbf{G}}^{22}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}\top}&{\mathbf{G}}^{12\top}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}\top}&0\\
{\mathbf{G}}^{23\top}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}\top}&{\mathbf{G}}^{13\top}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}\top}&0\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}\ ,

from which it follows that

\frac{\partial{\textnormal{G}}^{12}_{ij}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}[{\mathbf{G}}^{12}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{12}]_{ij}-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}[{\mathbf{G}}^{11}{\bm{e}}_{i}^{n_{1}}{\bm{e}}_{j}^{n_{2}\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{22}]_{ij}=-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\textnormal{G}}^{12}_{ij}{\textnormal{G}}^{12}_{ij}-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{ii}{\textnormal{G}}^{22}_{jj}\\
\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{ii}{\textnormal{G}}^{22}_{jj}\ ,

(26)

and finally by Stein’s lemma,

\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}[{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}]=\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sigma_{M}^{2}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\partial{\textnormal{G}}^{12}_{ij}}{\partial{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}}\right]\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime 2}}{n_{T}n_{M}}\sigma_{M}^{2}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}[{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{ii}{\textnormal{G}}^{22}_{jj}]\\
=-\beta_{T}^{\prime 2}\left(\frac{n_{T}}{n_{M}}\right)\sigma_{M}^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\underbrace{\frac{1}{n_{T}}\Tr({\mathbf{G}}^{11})}_{\rightarrow g_{1}(\xi)}\underbrace{\frac{1}{n_{T}}\Tr({\mathbf{G}}^{22})}_{\rightarrow g_{2}(\xi)}\right]\ .

(27)

For ease of notation throughout this proof, we will often omit the argument
\xi
and simply write
g_{i}
instead of
g_{i}(\xi)
.

Similarly, we can show that

\frac{\partial{\textnormal{G}}^{12}_{ij}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{w}_{k}{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{ii}{\textnormal{G}}^{22}_{jj}\ ,

(28)

and

\frac{\partial{\textnormal{G}}^{13}_{ik}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{v}_{j}{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{ii}{\textnormal{G}}^{33}_{kk}\ .

(29)

Hence,

\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{11}]\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime 2}n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sigma_{M}^{2}g_{1}g_{2}+\frac{\sigma_{T}^{2}}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{w}_{k}\frac{\partial{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}]+\frac{\sigma_{T}^{2}}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{v}_{j}\frac{\partial{\textnormal{G}}_{ik}^{13}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}]\\
\asymp-\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime 2}n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sigma_{M}^{2}g_{1}g_{2}-\frac{\sigma_{T}^{2}}{n_{T}^{2}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{w}_{k}^{2}{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{ii}{\textnormal{G}}^{22}_{jj}]-\frac{\sigma_{T}^{2}}{n_{T}^{2}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{v}_{j}^{2}{\textnormal{G}}^{11}_{ii}{\textnormal{G}}^{33}_{kk}]\\
\asymp-\left(\sigma_{T}^{2}+\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime 2}n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sigma_{M}^{2}\right)g_{1}g_{2}-\sigma_{T}^{2}g_{1}g_{3}\ ,

(30)

where
g_{3}(\xi)
denotes the limit of
\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{G}}^{33})]
.

Block (2,2)

We have

({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{22}=\left[\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{w}})^{\top}\right]{\mathbf{G}}^{12}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}}){\mathbf{G}}^{23\top}

(31)

and thus

({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{22}_{jj}=\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{i}{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ik}{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ik}{u}_{i}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{jk}^{23}.

We deduce that

\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{22}]=\frac{1}{n_{T}}\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}[({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{22}_{jj}]\\
=\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime}}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{M}}}\sum_{ij}\mathbb{E}[{\textnormal{Z}}_{ij}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}]+\frac{1}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{w}_{k}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ij}^{12}]+\frac{1}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{u}_{i}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{jk}^{23}]\ .

(32)

Then, we show that

\frac{\partial{\textnormal{G}}^{23}_{jk}}{\partial{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}}\asymp-\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{u}_{i}{\textnormal{G}}^{22}_{jj}{\textnormal{G}}^{33}_{kk}\ .

(33)

Hence,

\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{22}]\asymp-\left(\sigma_{T}^{2}+\frac{\beta_{T}^{\prime 2}n_{T}}{n_{M}}\sigma_{M}^{2}\right)g_{1}g_{2}-\sigma_{T}^{2}g_{2}g_{3}\ .

(34)

Block (3,3)

We have

({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{33}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{v}})^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{13}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}{\bm{\mathsfit{W}}}({\bm{u}})^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}^{23}

(35)

and thus

({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{33}_{kk}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ij}{v}_{j}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ik}^{13}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ij}{u}_{i}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{jk}^{23}.

We deduce that

\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{33}]=\frac{1}{n_{T}}\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}[({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{33}_{kk}]\\
=\frac{1}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{v}_{j}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{ik}^{13}]+\frac{1}{n_{T}\sqrt{n_{T}}}\sum_{ijk}\mathbb{E}[{u}_{i}{\mathsfit{W}}_{ijk}{\textnormal{G}}_{jk}^{23}]\ .

(36)

Hence,

\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{33}]\asymp-\sigma_{T}^{2}g_{1}g_{3}-\sigma_{T}^{2}g_{2}g_{3}\ .

(37)

System of equations for traces.

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{11}]

\displaystyle=\xi\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{G}}^{11})]+\underbrace{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{T}}}_{\to c_{1}}\ ,

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{T}}\mathbb{E}[\Tr({\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{G}})^{22}]

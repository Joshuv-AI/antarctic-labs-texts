Unique common fixed points of four generalized contractive mappings in ordered partial metric spaces
Talat Nazir, Sergei Silvestrov

Abstract
The existence and uniqueness of the common fixed point for generalized contractive mappings in order partial metric spaces is investigated. The existence of nonnegative solution of implicit nonlinear integral equations is also studied. Some examples demonstrating the validity of our main results are constructed. The presented results extend and unify various comparable results in the existing literature. MSC 2020: 47H09, 47H10, 54C60, 54H25

Introduction

Fixed point theory is a one of the powerful tool, noteworthy and stimulating
themes of nonlinear functional analysis that blends topology, analysis and
applied mathematics. In the fixed-point technique, the controllability
problem is converted to a fixed-point problem for an applicable nonlinear
operator in a function space. An essential part of this approach is to
guarantee the solvability of the equations for an invariant subset for this operator.

Definition 1

Let

be a nonempty set. A function

p:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is said to be a partial metric on
X

if for any

the following conditions hold true:

1)

p(x,x)=p(y,y)=p(x,y)
if and only if
x=y;

2)

p(x,x)\leq p(x,y);

3)

p(x,y)=p(y,x);

4)

p(x,z)\leq p(x,y)+p(y,z)-p(y,y)
.

The pair
(X,p)
is then called a partial metric space.

If

, then (
{1}
) and (
{2}
) imply that
x=y
. But
the converse does not always hold.

A trivial example of a partial metric space is the pair
(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},p)
, where the partial metric
p:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is
defined as

.

is a complete partial metric space. We assume the following that there exists a positive

(i)

(ii)

\int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa(t,s,v(s))ds\leq 2hv(t)\
for
each

.

(iii)

The control functions
\psi
and
\varphi
are connected
with relation that

\psi(a)+\phi(2a)\leq\psi(2a),

for every
a\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

Then integral equation (

) has a solution in

.

Proof

∎Define
(fx)(t)=F(t,x(t))
and
(gx)(t)=\int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa(t,s,x(s))ds.

Now

\displaystyle\psi(p(fx,gy))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi\left(\max\left(\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ \left(fx\right)(t),\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ \left(fy\right)(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi\left(\max\left(\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ F(t,x(t)),\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ \int\limits_{\Omega}\kappa(t,s,y(t))dt\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\psi\left(\max\left(\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ hx(t),\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ 2hy(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\psi\left(2h\max\left(\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ x(t),\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ y(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\psi\left(\frac{1}{2}\max\left(\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ x(t),\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ y(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi\left(\max\left(\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ x(t),\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ y(t)\right)\right)-\phi\left(\max\left(\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ x(t),\underset{t\in\Omega}{\sup}\ y(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi\left(p(x,y)\right)-\phi\left(p(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(M_{p}(x,y))-\varphi(M_{p}(x,y)).

Thus for every comparable elements
x,y\in X,

\psi(p(fx,gy))\leq\psi(M_{p}(x,y))-\varphi(M_{p}(x,y)),

is satisfies where

M_{p}(x,y)=\max\{p(x,y),p(fx,x),p(gy,y),\dfrac{p(fx,y)+p(gy,x)}{2}\}.

Now we can apply Theorem

by taking

and

Consistent with

. For

, define
\delta_{p}:CB^{p}(X)\times CB^{p}(X)\rightarrow[0,\infty)

\displaystyle p(x,A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{p(x,a):a\in A\},

\displaystyle\delta_{p}(A,B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{p(a,B):a\in A\},

\displaystyle H_{p}(A,B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\delta_{p}(A,B),\delta_{p}(B,A)\}.

It can be verified that

Lemma 2
(

)

Let
(X,p)
be a partial metric
space and

Proposition 1
(

)

Let
(X,p)
be a
partial metric space. For any

(i)

\delta_{p}(A,A)=\sup\{p(a,a):a\in A\};

(ii)

\delta_{p}(A,A)\leq\delta_{p}(A,B);

(iii)

\delta_{p}(A,B)=0

(iv)

\delta_{p}(A,B)\leq\delta_{p}(A,C)+\delta_{p}(C,B)-\inf\limits_{c\in C}p(c,c).

Proposition 2
(

)

Let
(X,p)
be a

Every

(

Definition 6

Let

\displaystyle\max\{p(x,y),p(f_{i}x,x),p(f_{i}y,y),p(f_{i}^{2}x,f_{i}x),

\displaystyle p(f_{i}^{2}y,y),p(f_{i}^{2}y,f_{i}y),\dfrac{p(f_{i}x,y)+p(f_{i}y,x)}{2}\}

-contraction mappings. Define

\displaystyle f_{1}(A)\bigcup f_{2}(A)\bigcup\cdot\cdot\cdot\bigcup f_{N}(A)

Definition 7

T
, assume that
U
and
V
are two fixed points of
T
with
H_{p}\left(U,V\right)
is not zero.
From (
11
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\psi(H_{p}(U,V))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(H_{p}(T\left(U\right),T\left(V\right)))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\psi\left(M_{T}\left(U,V\right)\right)-\phi\left(M_{T}\left(U,V\right)\right),

where

\displaystyle M_{T}\left(U,V\right)=\max\{H_{p}(U,V),H_{p}(U,T\left(U\right)),H_{p}(V,T\left(V\right)),

\displaystyle\frac{H_{p}(U,T\left(V\right))+H_{p}(V,T\left(U\right))}{2},H_{p}(T^{2}\left(U\right),U),H_{p}(T^{2}\left(U\right),V),H_{p}(T^{2}\left(U\right),T\left(V\right))\}

\displaystyle=\max\{H_{p}\left(U,V\right),H_{p}(U,U),H_{p}(V,V),

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\frac{H_{p}(U,V)+H_{p}(V,U)}{2},H_{p}\left(U,U\right),H_{p}(U,V),H_{p}(U,V)\}

\displaystyle=H_{p}(U,V),

that is,

\psi(H_{p}(U,V))\leq\psi\left(H_{p}\left(U,V\right)\right)-\phi\left(H_{p}\left(U,V\right)\right),

a contradiction. Thus

has a unique fixed point

∎

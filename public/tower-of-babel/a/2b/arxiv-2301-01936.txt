Clustering of large deviations in moving average processes: the long memory regime
Arijit Chakrabartylabel=e1]arijit.isi@gmail.com, Gennady Samorodnitsky t1 label=e2]gs18@cornell.edu

Abstract
We investigate how large deviations events cluster in the framework of an infinite moving average process with light-tailed noise and long memory. The long memory makes clusters larger, and the asymptotic behaviour of the size of the cluster turns out to be described by the first hitting time of a randomly shifted fractional Brownian motion with drift.

1
Introduction

We consider an infinite moving average process of the form

X_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}Z_{n-i}\,,\ n\geq 0\,,

(1.1)

where the noise variables
(Z_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{Z}})
are assumed to bef i.i.d. non-degenerate random variables. The noise distribution
F_{Z}
is
assumed have finite exponential moments:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tz}\,F_{Z}(dz)<\infty\text{ for all }t\in{\mathbb{R}}\,.

(1.2)

Furthermore, assuming that the noise is centred:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\,F_{Z}(dz)=0\,,

(1.3)

the series defining the process in (
1.1
) converges if and
only if the coefficients
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\ldots
satisfy

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}^{2}<\infty\,.

(1.4)

In this case
(X_{n})
is
a zero mean stationary ergodic
process. For
\varepsilon>0
we consider the sequence of large deviation
events

E_{j}(n,\varepsilon)=\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=j}^{n+j-1}X_{i}\geq\varepsilon\right\},\ j\geq 0.

(1.5)

By stationarity, each event
E_{j}(n,\varepsilon)
is equally rare,
and we are interested in the cluster of these events that occur given
that the event
E_{0}(n,\varepsilon)
occurs.

In
Chakrabarty and Samorodnitsky 2022
the short memory case was
considered. In this context, “short memory” corresponds to the case

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|<\infty\ \text{and}\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\not=0.

(1.6)

In this short memory case the conditional on
E_{0}(n,\varepsilon)
law of the
sequence
\bigl({\bf 1}(E_{j}(n,\varepsilon),\,j=1,2,\ldots)
converges
weakly, as
n\to\infty
, to the law of a sequence with a.s. finitely
many non-zero entries. the total number
D_{\varepsilon}
of the non-zero
entries turns out to scale as
\varepsilon^{-2}
, and
\varepsilon^{2}D_{\varepsilon}
has an
interesting weak limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
. We refer the reader to

Chakrabarty and Samorodnitsky 2022
for details, and a minor
technical condition required for the above statements.

In the present paper we are interested in the long memory case. For
the moving average processes (
1.1
) “long memory” refers to
the case when the coefficients
(a_{j})
satisfy the square summability
assumption (
1.4
) but not the absolute summability
assumption in (
1.6
). A typical assumption in this is

(a_{n})\ \text{is regularly varying with exponent}\ -\alpha,\ 1/2<\alpha<1;

(1.7)

see
Samorodnitsky 2016
. It turns out that, in this case (under
certain technical assumptions, an example of which is below), the
conditional on
E_{0}(n,\varepsilon)
law of the
sequence
\bigl({\bf 1}(E_{j}(n,\varepsilon),\,j=1,2,\ldots)
converges
weakly, as
n\to\infty
, to the degenerate probability measure

\delta_{(1,1,\ldots)}
. That is, once the event
E_{0}(n,\varepsilon)

occurs, the events
(E_{j}(n,\varepsilon))
become very likely. In order to
understand their structure we concentrate on the random variables

I_{n}(\varepsilon)=\inf\left\{j\geq 1:E_{j}(n,\varepsilon)\text{ does not
occur}\right\},\,n\geq 1

(1.8)

and establish a weak limit theorem for this sequence under a proper
scaling. Interestingly, the limit turns out to be the law of the first hitting
time of a randomly shifted fractional Brownian motion with drift.

The main result containing the above limit theorem and the technical
assumptions it requires are in Section
2
. The proof of
the main theorem requires a long sequence of preliminary results, all
of which are presented in that section. Finally, some useful facts
needed for the proofs are collected in Section
3
.

2
The assumptions and the main result

Our result on clustering of large deviation events in the long memory
case will require a number of assumptions that we state next. First of
all, we will replace the assumption of regular variation
(
1.7
) by the asymptotic power function assumption

a_{n}\sim n^{-\alpha},\ 1/2<\alpha<1,\ \text{and is eventually monotone}.

(2.1)

There is no doubt that the results of the paper hold under the more
general regular variation assumption as well. The extra generality
will, however, require making an already highly technical argument
even more so. The potentially resulting lack of clarity makes the
added generality less valuable. The same is true about the eventual
monotonicity assumption.

We will need additional assumptions on the distribution of the noise
variables. We will assume that some
\theta_{0}>0
,

\sup_{|\theta|\leq\theta_{0}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{2}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{(it+\theta)z}\,F_{Z}(dz)\right|dt<\infty\,.

(2.2)

Next, let

\sigma_{Z}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{2}\,F_{Z}(dz)

(2.3)

be the variance of the noise. Denote

\kappa=\text{the smallest integer}>\frac{4\alpha-1}{2-2\alpha}.

(2.4)

In other words,
\kappa=\bigl[(1+2\alpha)/(2-2\alpha)\bigr]
. We
assume that a generic noise variable
Z
satisfies

EZ^{i}=EG^{i}\ \text{for}\ 1\leq i\leq\kappa,

(2.5)

where
G\sim N(0,\sigma_{Z}^{2})
.

Remark 2.1
.

It is standard to verify that (
2.2
) implies that the noise
distribution has a twice continuously differentiable density

f_{Z}
. One the other hand, a sufficient condition for (
2.2
) is that
the noise distribution has a four times continuously differentiable density
f_{Z}
such that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\theta_{0}|x|}\left|\frac{d^{i}}{dx^{i}}f_{Z}(x)\right|dx<\infty\ \text{for}\ i=1,2,3,4.

The moment equality assumption (
2.5
) restricts how far
the the noise distribution can be from a normal distribution. Note
that in the range
1/2<\alpha<5/8
we have
\kappa=2
, in which case
the assumption is vacuous. Since
\kappa\geq 2
for all

\alpha\in(1/2,1)
, (
1.3
) is implied by
(
2.5
).

To state our main result, we need to introduce several key
quantities. Let

\beta=\frac{4-4\alpha}{3-2\alpha}\in(0,1)

(2.6)

and

H=3/2-\alpha\in(1/2,1).

(2.7)

We denote by
(B_{H}(t):t\geq 0)
the standard fractional Brownian motion
with Hurst index
H
, i.e. a zero mean Gaussian process with
continuous paths and covariance function

E\left(B_{H}(s)B_{H}(t)\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(s^{2H}+t^{2H}-|s-t|^{2H}\right),\,s,t\geq 0\,.

(2.8)

If
T_{0}
is a standard exponential random variable independent of the
fractional Brownian motion, then

\tau_{\varepsilon}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\,B_{H}(t)\leq(2C_{\alpha})^{-1/2}\varepsilon t^{2H}-(C_{\alpha}/2)^{1/2}\sigma_{Z}^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}T_{0}\right\},\,\varepsilon>0,

(2.9)

is an a.s. finite and strictly positive random variable. Here
\sigma_{Z}^{2}
is
the variance of the noise in (
2.3
) and

C_{\alpha}=\frac{\rm{B}(1-\alpha,2\alpha-1)}{(1-\alpha)(3-2\alpha)},

(2.10)

with
\rm{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
the standard Beta function.

We are now in a position to state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume the finite exponential moment condition (
1.2
), that
the coefficients satisfy the power-type condition (
2.1
), the
regularity condition (
2.2
) and the moment equality condition
(
2.5
). Then for every
\varepsilon>0
the first non-occurrence
times (
1.8
) satisfy

P\left(n^{-\beta}I_{n}(\varepsilon)\in\cdot\bigr|E_{0}(n,\varepsilon)\right)\Rightarrow P\left(\tau_{\varepsilon}\in\cdot\right),\,n\to\infty\,.

(2.11)

Remark 2.2
.

It is worthwhile to observe that the limit law obtained in Theorem

2.1
depends on the noise distribution only through its
variance
\sigma_{Z}^{2}
. This can be understood by noticing that in the
long memory case considered in this paper we have

{\rm Var}(X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n})\gg n
; see Lemma
2.1
below. Therefore, the
events
E_{j}(n,\varepsilon)
should be viewed as moderate deviation events,
not large deviation events. It has been observed in many
situations that moderate deviation events are influenced by the
Gaussian weak limit of the quantities of
interest. At the intuitive level, this explains why it is the variance
of the process that appears in the limit.

For comparison, in the short memory case (
1.6
), we have

{\rm Var}(X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n})\sim cn
for some
c>0
, the events
E_{j}(n,\varepsilon)

should be viewed as large deviation events, and their behaviour
depends on much more than just the variance of the noise. See

Chakrabarty and Samorodnitsky 2022
for details.

We start on the road to proving Theorem
2.1
by establishing
certain basic estimates that will be used throughout the paper. Denote

A_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j}a_{i},\,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,,

(2.12)

with the convention that a sum (or an integral) is zero if the lower
limit exceeds the upper limit (so that
A_{j}=0
for
j\leq-1
, for
example). Let

S_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}X_{i},\,n\geq 1\,,

(2.13)

and denote

\sigma_{n}^{2}={\rm Var}(S_{n}),\,n\geq 1\,.

(2.14)

In the sequel we use the following notation. We will denote by

\varphi_{Z}(t)=\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{tz}\,F_{Z}(dz)\right),\ t\in{\mathbb{R}}

(2.15)

the log-Laplace transform of a noise variable. We will frequently use
the obvious facts

\varphi\ \text{is convex and}\ \varphi_{Z}(x)\sim x^{2}\sigma_{Z}^{2}/2,\ x\to 0,

(2.16)

and

\varphi_{Z}^{\prime}\ \text{is continuous, nondecreasing and}\ \varphi_{Z}^{\prime}(x)=x\sigma_{Z}^{2}+O(x^{2}),\ x\to 0.

(2.17)

We will write

G_{\theta}
for the probability
measure obtained by exponentially tilting the law
F_{Z}
by

\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
. That is,

G_{\theta}(dz)=\bigl(Ee^{\theta Z}\bigr)^{-1}e^{\theta z}F_{Z}(dz).

(2.18)

It is clear that, as
\theta\to 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\,G_{\theta}(dz)\sim\theta\sigma_{Z}^{2},\ \left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\,G_{\theta}(dz)-\theta\sigma_{Z}^{2}\right|=O(\theta^{2})\ \text{and}\ =O(|\theta|^{3})\ \ \text{if}\ \ \kappa\geq 3,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|z|^{k}\,G_{\theta}(dz)\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}|z|^{k}\,F(dz),\,k=1,2,\ldots.

Lemma 2.1
.

Asymptotically we have

A_{j}\sim(1-\alpha)^{-1}j^{1-\alpha},\,j\to\infty

(2.20)

and

\sigma_{n}^{2}\sim C_{\alpha}\sigma_{Z}^{2}n^{3-2\alpha},\,n\to\infty\,.

(2.21)

Furthermore, for any
t>0
, as
n\to\infty
,

\sum_{i=0}^{[n^{\beta}t]}(A_{i}-A_{i-n})^{2}\sim K_{1}t^{3-2\alpha}n^{4-4\alpha}\,,

(2.22)

and

\sum_{i=n-[n^{\beta}t]+1}^{n}(A_{i}-A_{i-n})^{2}\sim\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+[n^{\beta}t]}(A_{i}-A_{i-n})^{2}\sim(1-\alpha)^{-2}n^{2-2\alpha+\beta}t\,,

(2.23)

with

K_{1}=(1-\alpha)^{-2}(3-2\alpha)^{-1}\,.

(2.24)

Finally, for any
t>0
, as
n\to\infty
,

\frac{\sigma_{Z}^{2}}{\sigma_{n}^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-n}\right)\left(A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]}-A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]-n}\right)=1-n^{1-2\alpha}t^{3-2\alpha}(1+o(1))\,.

(2.25)

Proof.

The claim (
2.20
) is, of course, an immediate consequence
of the assumption (
2.1
). For (
2.21
), first
note that

\displaystyle R_{n}=\rm Cov(X_{0},X_{n})

\displaystyle\sim\sigma_{Z}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-\alpha}(j+n)^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle\sim n^{1-2\alpha}\sigma_{Z}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\alpha}(1+x)^{-\alpha}\,dx

\displaystyle=C_{\alpha}\sigma_{Z}^{2}(1-\alpha)(3-2\alpha)n^{1-2\alpha}

as
n\to\infty
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=-(n-1)}^{n-1}(n-|i|)R_{|i|}\sim 2C_{\alpha}\sigma_{Z}^{2}(1-\alpha)(3-2\alpha)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(n-i)i^{1-2\alpha}

\displaystyle\sim 2C_{\alpha}\sigma_{Z}^{2}(1-\alpha)(3-2\alpha)n^{3-2\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)x^{1-2\alpha}\,dx=C_{\alpha}\sigma_{Z}^{2}n^{3-2\alpha}\,,

which is (
2.21
). Next, for a fixed
t>0
and large
n
,
by (
2.20
) and the fact that
\beta<1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{[n^{\beta}t]}(A_{i}-A_{i-n})^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{[n^{\beta}t]}A_{i}^{2}\sim(1-\alpha)^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{[n^{\beta}t]}i^{2-2\alpha}\sim K_{1}\left(n^{\beta}t\right)^{3-2\alpha},

proving (
2.22
). Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=n-[n^{\beta}t]+1}^{n}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-n}\right)^{2}\sim\sum_{i=n-[n^{\beta}t]+1}^{n}A_{n}^{2}\sim(1-\alpha)^{-2}n^{\beta+2-2\alpha}t\,,

showing the first equivalence in (
2.23
)
and the second equivalence can be shown in the same way.

For (
2.25
), we start by writing

S_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(A_{j}-A_{j-n})Z_{n-1-j}\,,n\geq 1\,,

(2.26)

so that

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{2}=\sigma_{Z}^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(A_{j}-A_{j-n})^{2}\,,n\geq 1\,.

(2.27)

Therefore, for large
n
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\sigma_{Z}^{2}}-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(A_{i}-A_{i-n})(A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]}-A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]-n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{[n^{\beta}t]-1}(A_{i}-A_{i-n})^{2}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]}-A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]-n}-A_{i}+A_{i-n}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Biggl[\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-[n^{\beta}t]}\right)^{2}

(2.28)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,+\sum_{i=n-[n^{\beta}t]}^{\infty}\left(A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]}-A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]-n}-A_{i}+A_{i-n}\right)^{2}\Biggr]\,.

By (
2.20
),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-[n^{\beta}t]}\right)^{2}\sim(1-\alpha)^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(i^{1-\alpha}-(i-[n^{\beta}t])_{+}^{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\sim n^{4-4\alpha}t^{3-2\alpha}(1-\alpha)^{-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[y^{1-\alpha}-(y-1)_{+}^{1-\alpha}\right]^{2}dy

as
n\to\infty
. By (
3.1
) with
H=3/2-\alpha
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[y^{1-\alpha}-(y-1)_{+}^{1-\alpha}\right]^{2}dy

(2.29)

\displaystyle=\left[\left(3-2\alpha\right)(1-\alpha)\right]^{-1}\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}\Gamma(2\alpha-1)\Gamma(2-\alpha)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\alpha}{3-2\alpha}\rm{B}\left(2\alpha-1,1-\alpha\right)=(1-\alpha)^{2}C_{\alpha},

so

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-[n^{\beta}t]}\right)^{2}\sim C_{\alpha}t^{3-2\alpha}n^{4-4\alpha},\,n\to\infty\,.

(2.30)

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-[n^{\beta}t]}\right)^{2}=O\left(n^{2\beta}\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}i^{-2\alpha}\right)=O\bigl(n^{2\beta+1-2\alpha}\bigr)=o\bigl(n^{4-4\alpha}\bigr),

(2.31)

we conclude also that

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-[n^{\beta}t]}\right)^{2}\sim C_{\alpha}t^{3-2\alpha}n^{4-4\alpha},\,n\to\infty\,.

(2.32)

It follows from (
2.31
) and (
2.32
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=n-[n^{\beta}t]}^{\infty}\left(A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]}-A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]-n}-A_{i}+A_{i-n}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left[-A_{j}+A_{j-[n^{\beta}t]}+\left(A_{j+n}-A_{j+n-[n^{\beta}t]}\right)\right]^{2}\sim C_{\alpha}t^{3-2\alpha}n^{4-4\alpha}\,.

In combination with (
2.28
) and (
2.30
) we
obtain

\frac{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{\sigma_{Z}^{2}}-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(A_{i}-A_{i-n})(A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]}A_{i+[n^{\beta}t]-n})\sim C_{\alpha}t^{3-2\alpha}n^{4-4\alpha}\,.

Dividing both sides by
\sigma_{Z}^{-2}\sigma_{n}^{2}
and appealing to (
2.21
), (
2.25
) follows.

We now consider certain large deviations of the partial sum
S_{n}
under a change
of measure. With an eye towards a subsequent application, we allow the
partial sum, given in the form (
2.26
), to be
“corrupted”. For
n\geq 1
and
t\geq 0
we define

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{[n^{\beta}t]}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-n}\right)Z_{n-i-1}\,,

(2.33)

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=n-[n^{\beta}t]}^{n-1}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-n}\right)Z_{n-i-1}\,,

(2.34)

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{3}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+[n^{\beta}t]}\left(A_{i}-A_{i-n}\right)Z_{n-i-1}\,.

(2.35)

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}>0
and denote

\bar{S}_{n}=S_{n}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\xi_{n}^{i}(t_{i}),\,n\geq 1\,.

(2.36)

Let
(\gamma_{n})
,
(\theta_{n})
and
(\eta_{n})
be real sequences satisfying

\gamma_{n}=o\left(n^{3/2-\alpha}\right),\ \theta_{n}=o\left(n^{-(1-\alpha)}\right),\ 1\ll\eta_{n}\ll n^{1/2}\,.

If
\tilde{S}_{n}
is a random variable with the law

P\left(\tilde{S}_{n}\in dx\right)=\left(E(e^{\theta_{n}\bar{S}_{n}})\right)^{-1}e^{\theta_{n}x}P\left(\bar{S}_{n}\in dx\right),\,n\geq 1\,,

(2.37)

then for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
h>0
,

P\left(\eta_{n}\sigma_{n}^{-1}\left(\tilde{S}_{n}-E(\tilde{S}_{n})+\gamma_{n}\right)\in[x,x+h]\right)\sim\eta_{n}^{-1}(2\pi)^{-1/2}h,\,n\to\infty.

(2.38)

Furthermore,

\sup_{n\geq 1,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\eta_{n}P\left(\eta_{n}\sigma_{n}^{-1}\tilde{S}_{n}\in[x,x+1]\right)<\infty\,.

(2.39)

Proof.

Let
(\tilde{Z}_{ni},\,n\geq 1,\,i\geq 0)
be a collection of
independent random variables such that the law of
\tilde{Z}_{ni}
is

G_{(A_{i}-A_{i-n})\theta_{n}}
in the notation of (
2.18
).
Then for large
n
,

\tilde{S}_{n}\overset{d}{=}A_{0}\tilde{Z}_{n0}+(A_{n}-A_{0})\tilde{Z}_{nn}+\sum_{i=[n^{\beta}t_{1}]+1}^{n-[n^{\beta}t_{2}]-1}A_{i}\tilde{Z}_{ni}+\sum_{i=n+[n^{\beta}t_{3}]+1}^{\infty}(A_{i}-A_{i-n})\tilde{Z}_{ni}\,.

(2.40)

The proof applies to (
2.40
) the bound
(
3.2
) in the appendix, with
n=\infty
. For any
z\in{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\left|P\left(\tilde{S}_{n}-E(\tilde{S}_{n})\leq z\sqrt{{\rm Var}(\tilde{S}_{n})}\right)-\Phi(z)\right|

(2.41)

\displaystyle\leq C_{u}\left({\rm Var}(\tilde{S}_{n})\right)^{-3/2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|A_{i}-A_{i-n}|^{3}E\left(|\tilde{Z}_{ni}-E\tilde{Z}_{ni}|^{3}\right),\,n\geq 1\,.

It is immediate from (
2.1
) that

\sup_{i\geq 0}\left|A_{i}-A_{i-n}\right|=O(n^{1-\alpha})\,,

(2.42)

so that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta_{n}\sup_{i\geq 0}\left|A_{i}-A_{i-n}\right|=0\,.

It follows from (
2.19
) that

E\tilde{Z}_{ni}\to 0,\ \ {\rm Var}(\tilde{Z}_{ni})\to\sigma_{Z}^{2},\ \ E\left(|\tilde{Z}_{ni}-E\tilde{Z}_{ni}|^{3}\right)\to\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|z^{3}|\,F_{Z}(dz)

(2.43)

uniformly in
i
as
n\to\infty
. Since it is an elementary
conclusion from Lemma
2.1
that for any
\kappa>1/\alpha
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|A_{i}-A_{i-n}|^{\kappa}=O\bigl(n^{\kappa+1-\kappa\alpha}\bigr),

(2.44)

it follows from (
2.41
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left|P\left(\tilde{S}_{n}-E(\tilde{S}_{n})\leq z\sqrt{{\rm Var}(\tilde{S}_{n})}\right)-\Phi(z)\right|

\displaystyle=O\left(n^{4-3\alpha}\left({\rm Var}(\tilde{S}_{n})\right)^{-3/2}\right)\,.

Using (
2.43
) again we see that

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\tilde{S}_{n})

\displaystyle\sim\sigma_{n}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\rm Var}\left(\xi_{n}^{i}(t_{i})\right)\sim C_{\alpha}\sigma^{2}_{Z}n^{3-2\alpha},

(2.45)

with the second equivalence following from various claims in Lemma
2.1
. Thus,

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left|P\left(\tilde{S}_{n}-E(\tilde{S}_{n})\leq z\sqrt{{\rm Var}(\tilde{S}_{n})}\right)-\Phi(z)\right|=O(n^{-1/2})=o\left(\eta_{n}^{-1}\right)\,.

(2.46)

Therefore, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
h>0
, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle P\left(\eta_{n}\sigma_{n}^{-1}\left(\tilde{S}_{n}-E(\tilde{S}_{n})+\gamma_{n}\right)\in[x,x+h]\right)

\displaystyle=o\left(\eta_{n}^{-1}\right)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\bf 1}\bigl[{\rm Var}(\tilde{S}_{n})^{-1/2}(x\eta_{n}^{-1}\sigma_{n}-\gamma_{n})\leq z

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\leq{\rm Var}(\tilde{S}_{n})^{-1/2}((x+h)\eta_{n}^{-1}\sigma_{n}-\gamma_{n})\bigr]\phi(z)\,dz,

where
\phi
is the standard normal density. The assumptions on

\gamma_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
along with (
2.45
)
imply that the integration interval shrinks towards the origin. Thus,
the integral above is asymptotically equivalent to

\eta_{n}^{-1}\phi(0)h
, and (
2.38
) follows. Boundedness
of
\phi
in the above integral establishes (
2.39
).

We now look more closely at the processes defined in (
2.33
),
(
2.34
) and (
2.35
). The next lemma describes the

The following integral evaluation follows from (2), (6) and (51) in

(3.1)

Next, we will need the following version of the Berry-Essen
theorem valid for independent not necessarily identically
distributed summands; see

Let

be independent zero mean random variables with

Assuming

\left|P\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\leq Bz\right)-\Phi(z)\right|\leq C_{u}AB^{-3},\,z\in{\mathbb{R}}\,,

with

a universal constant, and

normal CDF. The fact that the constant is universal means that

) remains valid for

as long the series in
the left hand side converges and

are finite.

The following generalization of the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma can be
proven in the same way as the original statement.
If

is a measurable function such that for some

,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{\theta x}|f(x)|dx<\infty\ \text{ for all }\theta\in[-\delta,\delta]\,,

then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{|\theta|\leq\delta}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{(\theta+it)x}f(x)\,dx\right|=0\,.

(3.3)

We will use a simple bound on the characteristic function

of
a random variable

with a finite third moment. Let

be an
independent copy of

and

. Using the bound

for

\displaystyle Ee^{itY}

\displaystyle\leq 1-t^{2}E(Y^{2})/2+|t|^{3}E|Y|^{3}/6

\displaystyle\leq 1-t^{2}{\rm Var}(X)+4|t|^{3}E|X|^{3}/3\,.

This implies that

|\phi(t)|\leq\left(1-t^{2}{\rm Var}(X)+4|t|^{3}E|X|^{3}/3\right)^{1/2},\,t\in{\mathbb{R}}\,.

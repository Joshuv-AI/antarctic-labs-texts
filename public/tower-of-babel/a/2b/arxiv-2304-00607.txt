The degree-three bounded cohomology of complex Lie groups of classical type
Carlos De la Cruz Mengual

Abstract
. We establish Monod’s isomorphism conjecture in degree-three bounded cohomology for every complex simple Lie group of classical type. Our main ingredient is a bounded-cohomological stability theorem with an optimal range in degree three that we bootstrap from previous stability results by the author and Hartnick. The bootstrapping procedure relies on the occurrence in our setting of a variant of the recently observed phenomenon of secondary stability in the sense of Galatius–Kupers–Randal-Williams.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of main result

This article is the third one in a series concerned with the explicit computation of the continuous bounded cohomology of Lie groups via stabilization techniques (see prequels
[
11
,
12
]
). The theory of continuous bounded cohomology was developed in the early 2000s by Burger and Monod
[
6
,
28
]
as a topological refinement of the celebrated notion for discrete groups (conceived by Johnson, Trauber, and Gromov
[
19
]
independently), with powerful applications in geometry,
dynamics and rigidity theory

[
8
,
14
,
28
]
.

Despite its power, the computation of continuous bounded cohomology remains a notorious challenge. In the setting of Lie groups, the problem can be reduced to the connected semisimple case, whose behavior is predicted by the prominent

Isomorphism Conjecture
.

For any connected semisimple Lie group
G
with finite center, the comparison map
c^{\ast}:{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{\ast}(G)\to\HH^{\ast}(G)
is an isomorphism in every degree.

Here we denote by
\HH^{\ast}(G)
the continuous group cohomology of
G
with coefficients on the trivial
G
-module
\mathbb{R}
, and by
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{\ast}(G)
the corresponding continuous bounded cohomology. The comparison map
c^{\ast}
is defined by forgetting the boundedness of a class. The isomorphism conjecture was formulated in this form by Monod in his ICM address
[
27
]
in 2006. The surjectivity question, posed earlier by Dupont
[
9
]
, has been answered affirmatively in a variety of contexts, e.g.
[
21
,
25
]
. We emphasize that the continuous cohomology of Lie groups is well understood in virtue of the van Est isomorphism (see e.g.
[
2
, Cor. XI.5.6]
or
[
33
]
) and classical results on the cohomology of compact symmetric spaces (see e.g.
[
18
,
26
]
).

Beyond the trivial degrees zero and one, the isomorphism conjecture has been established fully in degree two by Burger and Monod
[
5
]
. However, in degrees
\geq 3
, the conjecture has seen scarce progress in the last two decades. In degree three, it has been verified for the groups

\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})

[
7
,
30
]
and
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})

[
1
,
30
,
17
,
3
]
. In higher degrees, our lack of understanding of the conjecture is remarkable, only known to hold in degree four for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

[
20
]
.

The goal of this paper is to expose further evidence in favor of the isomorphism conjecture.

Theorem 1
.

The isomorphism conjecture holds in degree three for every connected simple complex Lie group
G
of classical type. In particular, we have
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(G)\cong\HH^{3}(G)\cong\mathbb{R}
.

This statement encompasses the four classical complex families
\mathbf{A}_{r},\,\mathbf{B}_{r},\,\mathbf{C}_{r},\,\mathbf{D}_{r}
, which consist of the connected Lie groups locally isomorphic to

(1.1)

\SL_{r+1}(\mathbb{C}),\quad\SO_{2r+1}(\mathbb{C}),\quad\Sp_{2r}(\mathbb{C}),\quad\SO_{2r}(\mathbb{C}).

respectively. In each case,
r
is the rank of the group, and all are simple Lie groups whenever
r\geq 1
, except for
\SO_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})
(abelian and with two simple factors, respectively).

Non-vanishing results for the continuous bounded cohomology of Lie groups find important applications in the study of representations of fundamental groups of manifolds, and in particular, of their moduli spaces and deformations. The case of surface group representations with Hermitian targets, studied extensively in existing literature, serves as the most prominent example of this claim (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
). The relevant cohomological input in this setting is the non-vanishing of
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{2}
for non-compact Lie groups of Hermitian type.

A bounded-cohomological approach has been also applied successfully in the study of 3-manifold group representations into
\PSL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
—a group with non-vanishing
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}
—and has been instrumental in proofs of interesting rigidity phenomena (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
13
]
). It is our hope that
Theorem 1
be the foundation of future investigations of representation varieties of 3-manifold groups into more general complex Lie groups.

1.2.
The role of bounded-cohomological stability

Each of the four sequences of groups indexed by
r
and listed in (
1.1
) can be organized as increasing chains of inclusions

(1.2)

S_{0}\overset{\iota_{0}}{\hookrightarrow}S_{1}\overset{\iota_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}S_{2}\overset{\iota_{2}}{\hookrightarrow}\cdots\hookrightarrow S_{r}\overset{\iota_{r}}{\hookrightarrow}S_{r+1}\hookrightarrow\cdots

where
S_{r}
is the group of rank
r
and the inclusions
\iota_{r}
are block embeddings in appropriate bases. The following theorem concerning the degree-three continuous bounded cohomology of the groups in these families plays a key role in the proof of
Theorem 1
.

Theorem 2
.

The embeddings
\iota_{r}
in each of the classical families induce the isomorphisms

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}(\mathbf{A}_{r})&&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{3}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&\cdots&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{r+1}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&\cdots\\
(\mathbf{B}_{r})&&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SO_{3}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SO_{5}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&\cdots&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SO_{2r+1}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&\cdots\\
(\mathbf{C}_{r})&&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\Sp_{2}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\Sp_{4}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&\cdots&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\Sp_{2r}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&\cdots\\
(\mathbf{D}_{r})&&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SO_{6}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SO_{8}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&\cdots&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SO_{2r}(\mathbb{C}))&\xleftarrow{\ \cong}&\cdots\\[5.0pt]
\end{array}

The property that the embeddings
\iota_{r}
in a chain of inclusions as (
1.2
) induce isomorphisms

(1.3)

{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{q}(S_{r_{0}})\xleftarrow{\ \cong}\ {\rm H}_{\rm b}^{q}(S_{r_{0}+1})\xleftarrow{\ \cong}\ {\rm H}_{\rm b}^{q}(S_{r_{0}+2})\xleftarrow{\ \cong}\ \cdots

for every degree
q\in\mathbb{N}
, starting from an index
r_{0}=r_{0}(q)
, is known as
bounded-cohomological stability
(abbrv.
bc-stability
). An assignment
q\mapsto r_{0}(q)
as above is called
bc-stability range
.

Theorem 2
settles the
optimal
(i.e. lowest possible) bc-stability range
r_{0}(3)
in degree
q=3
for the four complex classical families. In the case of the
\mathbf{A}_{r}
family,
Theorem 2
was proved by Bucher, Burger, and Iozzi
[
3
, Thm. 2]
, relying on work by Monod
[
30
]
. Establishing the statement for the remaining three families is the crux of this article.

The isomorphism conjecture, if true, would imply bc-stability for all classical families of Lie groups—for which continuous cohomology is known to stabilize. Our derivation of
Theorem 1
from
Theorem 2
is a sort of converse of this implication, based on the following principle. If a classical family
(S_{r})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
is bc-stable with range
r_{0}(q)
, then proving the degree-
q
isomorphism conjecture for all the groups
(S_{r}\mid r\geq r_{0}(q))
within the range of bc-stability is reduced to verifying it only for
S_{r_{0}(q)}
. The specific feature that favors the application of this principle in our setting is that the range
r_{0}(3)
established by
Theorem 2
is low enough to reach groups whose
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}
is well understood. Indeed, all the groups at the base of the respective isomorphism chains for the families
\mathbf{B}_{r},\mathbf{C}_{r},\mathbf{D}_{r}
are of type
\mathbf{A}_{r}
in disguise.

1.3.
A secondary stability phenomenon

To explain the structure of our proof of
Theorem 2
for the families
\mathbf{B}_{r},\mathbf{C}_{r},\mathbf{D}_{r}
, we revisit the argument in Bucher–Burger–Iozzi
[
3
]
for the
\mathbf{A}_{r}
case.

In
[
30
]
, Monod proved the bc-stability of the family
\mathbf{A}_{r}
, with an addition that ensures a few extra injections—instead of isomorphisms—beyond the regime of bc-stability. In degree three:

{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C}))\hookleftarrow{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{3}(\mathbb{C}))\hookleftarrow{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{4}(\mathbb{C}))\cong{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{5}(\mathbb{C}))\cong\cdots\cong{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{r+1}(\mathbb{C}))\cong\cdots\vskip-1.0pt

Since
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C}))\cong\mathbb{R}
(see
[
1
,
7
]
), one deduces that
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
must have
at most
dimension one for
n\geq 3
. That the dimension is
at least
one is the content of
[
3
]
, which exhibits a non-zero class in
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
based on a construction by Goncharov
[
17
]
.

In analogy to (
1.3
), we call
weak
bc-stability the property that the embeddings
\iota_{r}
in (
1.2
) induce injections starting from an index
r_{1}=r_{1}(q)
for every degree
q\in\mathbb{N}
. The (weak) bc-stability of the complex
\mathbf{B}_{r},\mathbf{C}_{r},\mathbf{D}_{r}
—among other classical families—was first proved by Hartnick and the author in
[
12
, Thm. A, Cor. B]
. In recent work, Kastenholz and Sroka
[
22
]
improved the range of weak bc-stability given in
[
12
]
for those families to
r_{1}(q)=2q+2
.

As opposed to Monod’s weak bc-stability result
[
30
]
for
\mathbf{A}_{r}
, the ranges established in the references
[
12
,
22
]
for the complex families
\mathbf{B}_{r},\mathbf{C}_{r},\mathbf{D}_{r}
do not yield a reduction to a group whose
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}
is understood. Thus, our first step towards
Theorem 2
is the following range improvement in degree three.

Key Lemma
.

T
he block embeddings
\iota_{r}
induce injections

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}&&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\OO_{3}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\OO_{5}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&\cdots&\hookleftarrow&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\OO_{2r+1}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&\cdots\\[2.0pt]
&&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\Sp_{2}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\Sp_{4}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&\cdots&\hookleftarrow&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\Sp_{2r}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&\cdots\\[2.0pt]
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\OO_{4}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\OO_{6}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\OO_{8}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&\cdots&\hookleftarrow&{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\OO_{2r}(\mathbb{C}))&\hookleftarrow&\cdots\end{array}

Remark 1.1
.

The omission of the determinant-one condition in the orthogonal groups in the
Key Lemma
is on purpose. Upgrading it to determinant-one groups is a technical point that we will treat later. We ask from the reader to ignore that at this stage, and assume for the sake of the introduction that the
Key Lemma
has been proven for the special orthogonal groups.

A remarkable aspect of the
Key Lemma
is the next fact that lies at the heart of its proof, given in Section
4
. We will prove that if
any
of the block embeddings
\iota_{r}
for the families
\mathbf{B}_{r},\mathbf{C}_{r},\mathbf{D}_{r}
induces an injection at the level of
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}
, then necessarily
all
the
\iota_{r}
’s up to the ranges produced by the
Key Lemma
must induce injections. In this sense, any arbitrary range (e.g.,
[
12
,
22
]
) is as good as any other, for either of them will allow us to bootstrap the optimal range
a posteriori
.

The proof of this intriguing fact relies on a principle reminiscent of
secondary stability
in the sense of Galatius–Kupers–Randal-Williams
[
15
]
. A subject of ongoing research in the realm of ordinary homology, secondary stability is the event in which “the failure of stability is itself stable”. In our case, failure of weak bc-stability is measured by the kernels of the maps induced by the embeddings
\iota_{r}
. We prove the stability of those kernels based on a parametrization via cross-ratios of configuration spaces of isotropic projective points, exposed in Section
3
.

1.4.
Non-triviality of
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}
and Gromov norms

In analogy with the argument for
\mathbf{A}_{r}
given above, it remains to show the non-triviality of
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}
for the relevant groups. We will prove:

Theorem 3
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let
S_{r}
be either
\SO_{2r+1}(\mathbb{C}),\,\Sp_{2r}(\mathbb{C})
, or
\SO_{2r}(\mathbb{C})
, and let
n=n(r)
be the dimension of the standard
S_{r}
-representation, so that
S_{r}<\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Then, the restriction

(1.4)

\res_{r}:{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\to{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(S_{r})

is an isomorphism, except if
S_{r}=\SO_{2r}(\mathbb{C})
and
r\in\{1,2\}
.

Since
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
is 1-dimensional for every
n\geq 2
, the injectivity of the map (
1.4
) implies the desired non-triviality statement. For groups of types
\mathbf{B}_{r}
and
\mathbf{C}_{r}
, this follows from the next proposition, which is of independent interest, for it also elucidates the norm structure of
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}
.

Proposition 4
.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, if
S_{r}
in
Theorem 3
is
\SO_{2r+1}(\mathbb{C})
or
\Sp_{2r}(\mathbb{C})
, then
\res_{r}
is isometric.

The proofs of
subsection 1.4
and Theorems
2
and
3
for the families
\mathbf{B}_{r}
and
\mathbf{C}_{r}
, are given in Section
5
. On the other hand,
subsection 1.4
does not hold in the
\mathbf{D}_{r}
case (see
subsection 6.4
). Thus, we give a separate argument for Theorems
2
and
3
for that family in Section
6
.

1.5.
Outlook

In Section
8
, we discuss the norm structure of
{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}
for the classical complex Lie groups, and provide a conjecture in the
\mathbf{D}_{r}
case, which remains open.

With little extra bookkeeping of the various types of Levi factors of parabolic subgroups, it should be possible to apply our ideas to prove the degree-three isomorphism conjecture for the real split Lie groups
\SO_{+}(r+1,r)
,
\Sp_{2r}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\SO_{+}(r,r)
. In general, we expect the situation in degree three for classical groups to be fully determined by what occurs in rank one.

Finally, an exciting and potentially fruitful research avenue is the further usage of secondary stability techniques in bounded cohomology. This could serve for optimizing existing stability ranges, and, eventually, lead to new computations of bounded cohomology in higher degrees.

2.
Notation and standing assumptions

2.1.
Notation on formed spaces

In the rest of this article, we will adhere to the notation from
[
12
, §2]
, which we summarize below. The notation will be slightly simplified, as we only consider symmetric or antisymmetric formed spaces over
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. An
\varepsilon
-symmetric formed space
is a pair
(V,\omega)
of a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V
and an
\varepsilon
-symmetric bilinear form
\omega:V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
.
We write
q(v)\coloneqq\omega(v,v)
for the quadratic form. The rank of
(V,\omega)
is the maximal dimension of a totally isotropic subspace of
V
. Any non-degenerate
\varepsilon
-symmetric space
(V,\omega)
of dimension
n
splits as the direct sum of formed spaces

(2.1)

V\cong\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}^{\oplus r}\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\oplus d},\vskip-2.0pt

where
r
is the rank of
(V,\omega)
,
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}
is the
\varepsilon
-hyperbolic plane,
\mathcal{E}
is the Euclidean line, and
d=n-2r\in\{0,1\}
. Furthermore, if
\varepsilon=-1
, then
d=0
. We denote by
(V_{r}^{\varepsilon,d},\omega_{r}^{\varepsilon,d})
the formed space on the right-hand side of (
2.1
). An ordered basis
\mathscr{B}_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}=\{e_{r},\ldots,e_{1},dh,f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}\}
of
V_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}
is an adapted basis of
(V_{r}^{\varepsilon,d},\omega_{r}^{\varepsilon,d})
if
\langle h\rangle\cong\mathcal{E}
and
\langle e_{i},f_{i}\rangle\cong\mathcal{H}
for every
i
. The matrix

J_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\varepsilon Q_{r}\\[-2.0pt]
0&1_{d}&0\\[-2.0pt]
Q_{r}&0&0\end{pmatrix}\in{\rm M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),

represents the form
\omega_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}
in the basis
\mathscr{B}_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}
, where
Q_{r}\in{\rm M}_{r}(\mathbb{C})
is the matrix with 1’s on its antidiagonal and zero elsewhere (the middle row and column are omitted if
d=0
).

Let
G_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}
be the automorphism group of
(V_{r}^{\varepsilon,d},\omega_{r}^{\varepsilon,d})
and
S_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}<G_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}
its determinant-one subgroup. Both
G_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}
and
S_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}
are complex Lie subgroups of
\GL(V_{r}^{\varepsilon,d})
. Concretely,

G_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}=\begin{cases}\OO_{2r+1}(\mathbb{C}),&(\varepsilon,d)=(+1,1)\\[-2.0pt]
\Sp_{2r}(\mathbb{C}),&(\varepsilon,d)=(-1,0)\\[-2.0pt]
\OO_{2r}(\mathbb{C}),&(\varepsilon,d)=(+1,0)\end{cases}

Observe that

(2.2)

G_{r}^{(+1,1)}\cong\{\pm I\}\times S_{r}^{(+1,1)}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad G_{r}^{(-1,0)}=S_{r}^{(-1,0)}.\vskip-2.0pt

For fixed
d
, the inclusions
\mathscr{B}_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{r+1}^{\varepsilon,d}
of adapted bases gives rise to the embeddings

(2.3)

G_{0}^{\varepsilon,d}\overset{\iota_{0}}{\hookrightarrow}G_{1}^{\varepsilon,d}\overset{\iota_{1}}{\hookrightarrow}G_{2}^{\varepsilon,d}\hookrightarrow\cdots\hookrightarrow G_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}\overset{\iota_{r}}{\hookrightarrow}G_{r+1}^{\varepsilon,d}\hookrightarrow\cdots\vskip-2.0pt

of automorphism groups and of their corresponding determinant-one subgroups

. We abuse notation and denote by

3.
Configuration spaces of isotropic points

3.1.
The variety of isotropic points

We will consider the following generalized flag variety.

Definition 3.1
.

For any
r\geq r_{1}
, we call the irreducible complex projective variety

\mathcal{P}_{r}=\mathcal{P}_{r}^{\varepsilon,d}\coloneqq\{[v]\in\mathbb{P}(V_{r})\mid q(v)=0\}\vskip-3.0pt

the
variety of isotropic points
of
V_{r}
. By Witt’s lemma
[
35
, Thm. 7.4]
, it is a compact homogeneous
G_{r}
-space when endowed with the Hausdorff topology of its set of
\mathbb{C}
-points, and the Lebesgue class is its unique
G_{r}
-invariant measure class. The assumption
r\geq r_{1}
excludes two pathological cases: the set
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is empty, and
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{(+1,0)}
is not irreducible as an algebraic set.

Let
Q_{r}
be the stabilizer of
[e_{r}]\in\mathcal{P}_{r}
, a maximal parabolic subgroup of
G_{r}
with Levi decomposition
Q_{r}=U_{r}\rtimes R_{r}
, where
U_{r}
is unipotent and
R_{r}
reductive. Represented in the basis
\mathscr{B}_{r}
, the reductive factor
R_{r}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\times}\times G_{r-1}
consists of the block-diagonal matrices

\begin{pmatrix}\lambda&0&0\\[-2.0pt]
0&g&0\\[-2.0pt]
0&0&\lambda^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

with
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
g\in G_{r-1}
. If
Q_{r}^{-}
denotes the stabilizer of
[f_{r}]\in\mathcal{P}_{r}
, then
Q_{r}\cap Q_{r}^{-}=R_{r}
.

We are interested in parametrizing generic orbits of the diagonal
G_{r}
-action on product spaces
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}
, for
k\in\{3,4,5\}
. Genericity is understood in the measure-theoretical sense, and therefore, the products
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{k}
will be regarded as objects in the category of
Lebesgue
G_{r}
-spaces
.

Definition 3.2
.

We call
Lebesgue space
a standard Borel space
X
endowed with the measure class of a Borel probability measure. If
X
admits a Borel action of a lcsc group
G
, and the measure class is
G
-invariant, we call
X
a
Lebesgue
G
-space
. Morphisms of Lebesgue (
G
-) spaces are equivalence classes (up to null sets) of measure-class preserving Borel (
G
-)maps.

Example 3.3
.

An algebraic
\mathbb{C}
-variety
X
with the regular action of an algebraic group
G
is a Lebesgue
G
-space if we endow
X
with its Lebesgue measure class. The Borel
\sigma
-algebra of the set of orbits
G\!\setminus\!X
with respect to the Hausdorff topology on
X
is standard (see
[
38
, Thm. 3.1.3]
), and hence
G\!\setminus\!X
is a Lebesgue space.

If
v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}
are all non-zero vectors in
V_{r}
, we will write
[v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}]
as a shorthand for the tuple
([v_{0}],\ldots,[v_{k}])
of points in
\mathbb{P}(V_{r})
. We will say the tuple is in general position if so are the vectors
v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}
(or any choices of lifts). We define the subsets

6.1.
The Goncharov–Bucher–Burger–Iozzi volume cocycle

The content of this subsection is extracted entirely from the references
[
17
,
3
]
. We abbreviate by
\mathbb{P}
the complex projective line
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, isomorphic to the boundary of the hyperbolic 3-space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. We let
\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}:\mathbb{P}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function that assigns to a 4-tuple
(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\in\mathbb{P}^{4}
the oriented volume of the ideal tetrahedron in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
with vertices
p_{i}
. Up to rescaling,
\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}
is the unique alternating,
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant, continuous bounded cocycle
\mathbb{P}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
; see
[
1
,
7
]
.

We may write
\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}
in terms of the Bloch–Wigner dilogarithm
\mathcal{D}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
(see
[
37
, §3]
) as

(6.1)

\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}=\mathcal{D}\,\circ\,\CR_{0},

where
\CR_{0}
is the classical cross-ratio on
\mathbb{P}
(see
subsection 3.2
and
subsection 3.2
). Under the identification
\mathbb{P}\cong\hat{\mathbb{C}}
from (
3.4
), the equality (
6.1
) corresponds to

\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}(\infty,0,1,z)=\mathcal{D}(z).
Thus, the cocycle equation for
\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}
implies that
\mathcal{D}
satisfies Spence–Abel functional equation (
4.4
), and the alternation of
\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}
translates into the symmetries

(6.2)

\mathcal{D}(z)=\mathcal{D}\left(1-\frac{1}{z}\right)=\mathcal{D}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)=-\mathcal{D}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)=-\mathcal{D}(1-z)=-\mathcal{D}\left(\frac{-z}{1-z}\right).\vskip 3.0pt

A complete flag in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a sequence
F^{0}\!\subset\!F^{1}\!\subset\!\cdots\!\subset\!F^{n-1}\!\subset\!F^{n}
of subspaces
F^{j}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
\dim(F^{j})=j
. The group
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
acts transitively on the variety
\mathbb{F}_{n}
of complete flags in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and the stabilizer of the standard flag
\{0\}\!\subset\!\langle e_{1}\rangle\!\subset\!\cdots\!\subset\!\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1}\rangle\!\subset\!\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the upper-triangular subgroup
P_{n}<\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, a minimal parabolic subgroup. This identifies
\mathbb{F}_{n}
with the Furstenberg boundary of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Now, a complete
affine
flag
(F,\vec{v})
is a pair that consists of a complete flag
F
, and a tuple of spanning vectors
\vec{v}=(v^{1},\ldots,v^{n})
in the sense that

F^{j}=F^{j-1}\oplus\langle v^{j}\rangle\quad\mbox{for }j=1,\ldots,n.\vskip-3.0pt

We write
\mathbb{F}_{n,\mathrm{aff}}
for the set of affine flags, which comes equipped with a projection
\mathbb{F}_{n,\mathrm{aff}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{n}
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\sigma_{3}
denote the collection of equivalence classes
[V;(v^{0},\ldots,v^{3})]
, where
V
is a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space,
v^{0},\ldots,v^{3}\in V
are vectors that span
V
. The equivalence
[V,(v^{j})]\sim[W,(w^{j})]
holds if there exists a linear isomorphism

\phi:V\xrightarrow{\cong\ }W\mbox{ with }\ \phi(v^{j})=w^{j}\ \ \mbox{for all }j\in\{0,1,2,3\}.

Note that
\sigma_{3}
is a set since every class
[V;(v^{j})]
admits a representative
[\mathbb{C}^{m};(\bar{v}^{j})]
for
0\leq m\leq 4
.

Now, for any
J=(j_{0},\ldots,j_{3})\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{4}
, we set
T_{J}:\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{aff}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{4}\to\sigma_{3}
as

(6.3)

T_{J}\big((F_{0},\vec{v}_{0}),\ldots,(F_{3},\vec{v}_{3})\big)\coloneqq\left[\frac{\langle F_{0}^{j_{0}+1},\ldots,F_{3}^{j_{3}+1}\rangle}{\langle F_{0}^{j_{0}},\ldots,F_{3}^{j_{3}}\rangle};\left(v_{0}^{j_{0}+1},\ldots,v_{3}^{j_{3}+1}\right)\right],

where each
(F_{i},\vec{v}_{i})\in\mathbb{F}_{n,\mathrm{aff}}
is such that
F_{i}=(F_{i}^{0}\subset\cdots\subset F_{i}^{n})
and
\vec{v}_{i}=(v_{i}^{1},\ldots,v_{i}^{n})
, and the vectors in the right-hand side of (
6.3
) are regarded as classes modulo
\langle F_{0}^{j_{0}},\ldots,F_{3}^{j_{3}}\rangle
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
, and let
\mathrm{Vol}:\sigma_{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be the map defined as

\mathrm{Vol}\left[\mathbb{C}^{m};(v^{0},\ldots,v^{3})\right]\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}[v^{0},\ldots,v^{3}]&\mbox{if }m=2\mbox{ and all }v^{i}\neq 0,\\[-3.0pt]
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We let
\tilde{B}_{n}^{J}\colon\mathbb{F}_{n,\mathrm{aff}}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
be the composition
\mathrm{Vol}\,\circ\,T_{J}
for any
J\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{4}
. This function is independent of the choices of spanning vectors, and thus, descends to a bounded Borel function
B_{n}^{J}:\mathbb{F}_{n}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, the sum

B_{n}\coloneqq\sum_{J}B_{n}^{J}\vskip-4.0pt

descends to an alternating,
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant, bounded strict Borel cocycle
B_{n}:\mathbb{F}_{n}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
(see
[
3
, Cor. 13]
). Observe that
B_{2}=\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}
, so by analogy, we will call
B_{n}
the (Goncharov–Bucher–Burger–Iozzi)
volume cocycle
. The
bounded Borel class

b_{n}\in{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\cong{\rm H}_{\rm b}^{3}(\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
, mentioned in
Theorem 5.1
above, is defined as
b_{n}\coloneqq[B_{n}]
.

Whilst
B_{n}
is not everywhere continuous, it is so in the following generic locus.

Definition 6.4
.

We say that a 4-tuple
(F_{0},F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{4}
is in
general position
if

\dim\langle F_{0}^{j_{0}},\ldots,F_{3}^{j_{3}}\rangle=j_{0}+\cdots+j_{3}

as long as the right-hand side is at most
n
. We will denote by

\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\{4\}}
the Zariski-open (hence Hausdorff-open and co-null) subset of
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{4}
of 4-tuples in general position.

Lemma 6.5
.

For any
n\geq 2
and
J=(j_{0},\ldots,j_{3})\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^{4}
, the restriction of
B_{n}^{J}
to the open subset
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\{4\}}
is continuous, and vanishes identically unless the equality

(6.4)

\|J\|_{1}=j_{0}+\ldots+j_{3}=n-2

holds. In particular, the volume cocycle
B_{n}:\mathbb{F}_{n}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous on
\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\{4\}}
.

Proof.

See discussion preceding
[
3
, Lem. 16]
. The restriction
B_{n}^{J}|_{\mathbb{F}_{n}^{\{4\}}}
reduces to a hyperbolic volume function
\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}
when (
6.4
) holds, and vanishes otherwise. This implies the continuity statement. Note that there are precisely
n(n^{2}-1)/6
tuples
J
that fulfill (
6.4
).
∎

6.2.
The Furstenberg boundary of
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})

The content of this subsection is classical, and can be found e.g. in
[
36
, §I.7]
. We continue to abbreviate
\mathbb{P}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
.
Observe first that
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})
acts on the variety of totally isotropic
projective lines
in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{4})
(i.e. 2-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{4}
) with two orbits,
O^{+}
and
O^{-}
. Respectively, these consist of elements

\displaystyle l^{+}_{a}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big\{[1,-t,a,at]^{\top}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{4})\ \mid t\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\big\}\in O^{+},

\displaystyle l^{-}_{a}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big\{[1,a,-t,at]^{\top}\in\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{4})\ \mid t\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\big\}\in O^{-},

with
a\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathbb{P}
, given in coordinates with respect to the basis
\mathscr{B}_{2}^{(+1,0)}=\{e_{1},e_{2},f_{2},e_{1}\}
. These lines satisfy the properties

\begin{cases}l^{+}_{a}\cap l_{b}^{+}\!\!\!\!&=l_{a}^{-}\cap l_{b}^{-}=\emptyset\quad\mbox{for }a\neq b,\\
l^{+}_{a}\cap l_{b}^{-}\!\!\!\!&=[1,b,-a,ab]^{\top}.\end{cases}

The
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})
-action on the product
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq O^{+}\times O^{-}
is transitive, and the stabilizer of the pair
(l_{0}^{+},l_{0}^{-})\in\mathcal{F}
equals the subgroup of upper-triangular orthogonal matrices. Thus, we identify the Furstenberg boundary of
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})
with
\mathcal{F}
.

There exists an exceptional isogeny
\theta:\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\twoheadrightarrow\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})
that descends to an isomorphism of Lebesgue
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-spaces,

(6.5)

\partial\theta:\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P}\to\mathcal{F},\quad\partial\theta(a,b)\coloneqq(l_{a}^{+},l_{b}^{-}),\ \ a,b\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}.

The following embedding of the Furstenberg boundary
\mathcal{F}
into the full flag variety of
\mathbb{C}^{4}
plays a key role in our proof of
section 6
.

Definition 6.6
.

Let
\rho:\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{F}_{4}
be the
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant embedding defined as

\displaystyle\rho(l^{+},l^{-})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(\{0\}\subset\ l^{+}\cap l^{-}\ \subset\ l^{+}\ \subset l^{+}+l^{-}\ \subset\ \mathbb{C}^{4}\big).

An analogous map can be obtained if we choose
l^{-}
instead of
l^{+}
as the 2-dimensional subspace.

Remark 6.7
.

The image of
\rho
is a null set. In particular,
\rho
does not preserve measure classes.

6.3.
Generic values of the volume cocycle of
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})

We introduce a notion of genericity for tuples of points in the Furstenberg boundary
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 6.8
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\{k+1\}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\partial\theta^{\,\times(k+1)}\big(\mathbb{P}^{\{k+1\}}\times\mathbb{P}^{\{k+1\}}\big)\subset\mathcal{F}^{k+1},

where the brackets in the upper index of
\mathbb{P}
refer to the generic subsets (
3.2
) in the classical setting. The product
\mathbb{P}^{\{k+1\}}\times\mathbb{P}^{\{k+1\}}
is regarded as a subset of
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P})^{k+1}
in the obvious way.

Lemma 6.9
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

The set
\mathcal{F}^{\{k+1\}}
is an
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})
-invariant, co-null Borel subset of
\mathcal{F}^{k+1}
, and the equality
\partial_{i}(\mathcal{F}^{\{k+1\}})=\mathcal{F}^{\{k\}}
holds for every
i\in\{0,\ldots,k+1\}
.

(ii)

The action
\SO_{4}(\mathbb{C})\curvearrowright\mathcal{F}^{\{3\}}
is transitive.

(iii)

The image
\rho^{\,\times 4}(\mathcal{F}^{\{4\}})
is contained in the set
\mathbb{F}_{4}^{\{4\}}
of 4-tuples in general position.

Proof.

Item (i) follows from the fact that
\partial\theta
is an isomorphism of Lebesgue
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-spaces, and
\mathbb{P}^{\{k+1\}}\times\mathbb{P}^{\{k+1\}}
is invariant, Borel and co-null in
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P})^{k+1}
. Item (ii) is a consequence of the transitivity of the action
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\curvearrowright\mathbb{P}^{\{3\}}
. For (iii), we let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{3})
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{3})\in\mathbb{P}^{\{4\}}
. The genericity of these tuples means that their entries are pairwise distinct when regarded under the identification
\mathbb{P}\cong\hat{\mathbb{C}}
. Set
l_{i}^{+}\coloneqq l_{a_{i}}^{+}
and
l_{i}^{-}\coloneqq l_{b_{i}}^{-}
, so that

\big((l_{0}^{+},l_{0}^{-}),\ldots,(l_{3}^{+},l_{3}^{-})\big)=(\partial\theta_{0})^{4}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})\in\mathcal{F}^{4}.

We claim that the image under
\rho^{\,\times 4}
of the tuple
((l_{0}^{+},l_{0}^{-}),\ldots,(l_{3}^{+},l_{3}^{-}))
is in general position. Indeed, if we set
p_{i}\coloneqq l_{i}^{+}\cap l_{i}^{-}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}
, then, for instance, for distinct
i,j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, we have

\displaystyle\dim(p_{i}+p_{j})

\displaystyle=1+1-\dim(l_{i}^{+}\cap l_{i}^{-}\cap l_{j}^{+}\cap l_{j}^{-})=2,

\displaystyle\dim(p_{i}+l_{j}^{+})

\displaystyle=1+2-\dim(l_{i}^{+}\cap l_{i}^{-}\cap l_{j}^{+})=3,

\displaystyle\dim(p_{i}+(l_{j}^{+}+l_{j}^{-}))

\displaystyle=1+3-\dim(l_{i}^{+}\cap l_{i}^{-}\cap(l_{j}^{+}+l_{j}^{-}))=4,

where we used that
l_{i}^{+}\cap l_{j}^{+}=l_{i}^{-}\cap l_{j}^{-}=\{0\}
and

l_{i}^{+}\cap l_{i}^{-}\cap(l_{j}^{+}+l_{j}^{-})\subset(l_{i}^{+}\cap l_{i}^{-}\cap l_{j}^{+})+(l_{i}^{+}\cap l_{i}^{-}\cap l_{j}^{-})=\{0\}.

The remaining verifications are completely analogous to the three given above.
∎

In the following lemma, we examine the values of the volume cocycle
B_{n}
over the image of the generic set
\mathcal{F}^{\{4\}}
via the inclusion
\rho^{\,\times 4}:\mathcal{F}^{4}\to\mathbb{F}_{4}^{4}
. For simplicity, in its proof we will write 4-tuples of indices
(j_{0},\ldots,j_{3})
as
j_{0}\cdots j_{3}
, omitting parentheses and commas.

Lemma 6.10
.

For any
(a,b)\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, let
F_{(a,b)}\coloneqq\rho\big(\partial\theta(a,b)\big)
and
F_{a}\coloneqq F_{(a,a)}
. Then for all
(a,b)\in(\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus\{0,1\})^{2}
, the equality

B_{4}(F_{\infty},F_{0},F_{1},F_{(a,b)})=2\left(\mathcal{D}(a)+\mathcal{D}(b)\right)

holds. In particular, the map

B_{4}\,\circ\,\rho^{\,\times 4}|_{\mathcal{F}^{\{4\}}}:\mathcal{F}^{\{4\}}\to\mathbb{R}

is non-zero.

Proof.

We claim the following values for the non-vanishing summands
B_{4}^{J}
of
B_{4}

(see
subsection 6.1
):

(6.6)

B_{4}^{J}(F_{\infty},F_{0},F_{1},F_{(a,b)})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phantom{-}\mathcal{D}(b)&\mbox{ if }J\in\{2000,0200,0020,0002\},\\
-\mathcal{D}(b/a)&\mbox{ if }J\in\{1100,0011\},\\
\phantom{-}\mathcal{D}\left(\frac{1-b}{1-a}\right)&\mbox{ if }J\in\{1010,0101\},\\[4.0pt]
-\mathcal{D}\left(\frac{a(1-b)}{b(1-a)}\right)&\mbox{ if }J\in\{1001,0110\}.\\
\end{array}\right.

First, if we let
\phi_{ab}\coloneqq e_{1}+b\,e_{2}-a\,f_{2}+ab\,f_{1}
for any
a,b\in\hat{\mathbb{C}}
, then

l_{a}^{+}=\langle e_{1}-a\,f_{2},\ e_{2}+a\,f_{1}\rangle,\quad l_{b}^{-}=\langle e_{1}+b\,e_{2},\ b\,f_{1}-f_{2}\rangle,\quad\mathrm{and}\quad l_{a}^{+}\cap l_{b}^{-}=\langle\phi_{ab}\rangle,

and therefore, for
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle F_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\big(\{0\}\subset\langle f_{1}\rangle\subset\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle\subset\langle f_{1},f_{2},e_{2}\rangle\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}\big),

\displaystyle F_{0}

\displaystyle=\big(\{0\}\subset\langle e_{1}\rangle\subset\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle\subset\langle e_{1},e_{2},f_{2}\rangle\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}\big),

\displaystyle F_{(a,b)}

\displaystyle=\big(\{0\}\subset\langle\phi_{ab}\rangle\subset\langle\phi_{ab},e_{1}-a\,f_{2}\rangle\subset\langle\phi_{ab},e_{1}-a\,f_{2},e_{1}+b\,e_{2}\rangle\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}\big).

Now, we have:

\displaystyle B_{4}^{2000}(F_{\infty},F_{0},F_{1},F_{(a,b)})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Vol}\ \bigg[\frac{\langle f_{1},f_{2},e_{2}\rangle+\langle e_{1}\rangle+\langle\phi_{11}\rangle+\langle\phi_{ab}\rangle}{\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle+\{0\}+\{0\}+\{0\}};\big(e_{2},e_{1},\phi_{11},\phi_{ab}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Vol}\ \big[V^{(+1,0)}_{2}/\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle;\big(e_{2},e_{1},\phi_{11},\phi_{ab}\big)\big]

\displaystyle\overset{(\ast)}{=}\mathrm{Vol}\left[\mathbb{C}^{2};\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1&1\\[-3.0pt]
1&0&1&b\end{pmatrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{P}}(\infty,0,1,b)=\mathcal{D}(b),\quad\mathrm{and}\quad

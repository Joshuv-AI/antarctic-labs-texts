Abstract
. In this paper we consider a semiclassical version of the fractional Klein-Gordon equation on the lattice ℏ ​ ℤ n . \hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}. Contrary to the Euclidean case that was considered in [ 2 ] , the discrete fractional Klein-Gordon equation is well-posed in ℓ 2 ​ ( ℏ ​ ℤ n ) . \ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right). However, we also recover the well-posedness results in the certain Sobolev spaces in the limit of the semiclassical parameter ℏ → 0 \hbar\to 0 .

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the fractional Klein-Gordon equation on the discrete lattice

\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x=\hbar k,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right\},

depending on a (small) discretization parameter
\hbar>0
, and the behaviour of its solutions as
\hbar\rightarrow 0.
The discrete fractional Laplacian on
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is denoted by
\left(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)^{\alpha}
and is defined by

\left(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)^{\alpha}u(k):=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a^{(\alpha)}_{j}u(k+j\hbar),\quad 0<\alpha<1,

(1.1)

where the expansion coefficient
a_{j}^{(\alpha)}
is given by

a_{j}^{(\alpha)}:=\int\limits_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{n}}\left|2\sin\left(\pi\theta\right)\right|^{2\alpha}e^{-2\pi ij\cdot\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta.

(1.2)

We refer to Section
2
for further details about the discrete fractional Laplacian.

The fractional Klein-Gordon equation with fractional Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}u(t,x)+(-\mathcal{L})^{\alpha}u(t,x)+m(x)u(t,x)=f(t,x),\quad\text{ with }(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
\left(-\mathcal{L}\right)^{\alpha}
is the usual fractional Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined as a pseudo-differential operator with symbol
|2\pi\xi|^{2\alpha}=\left[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}4\pi^{2}\xi_{j}^{2}\right]^{\alpha}
i.e.,

(-\mathcal{L})^{\alpha}u(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left\{\left|2\pi\xi\right|^{2\alpha}\mathcal{F}u(\xi)\right\}\left(x\right)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\left|2\pi\xi\right|^{2\alpha}\widehat{u}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,

(1.4)

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are the usual Fourier transform and inverse Fourier transform, respectively. The fractional Laplacian operator can also be understood as a positive power of the classical Laplacian. We refer to
[
12
]
and
[
20
]
for more details and other alternative definitions of the fractional Laplacian.

The nonlocal nature of fractional derivatives like Riemann–Liouville fractional derivative, Caputo fractional derivative, Riesz fractional derivative, etc., plays a vital role in modeling various problems of classical and quantum mechanics (see
[
16
,
14
]
). In the last few decades, considerable attention has been given to the solutions of fractional partial differential equations.

Recently, analytical and numerical solutions of the fractional Klein-Gordon equation have been studied by many authors like
[
1
,
30
,
10
,
14
,
13
]
.
There have been studies where the mass term depends on the position,
[
4
,
9
,
29
,
28
]
and in
[
15
,
16
]
the fractional Laplacian was introduced. Recently in
[
2
]
, Altybay et al. considered the case where the mass term is singular with zero source term and studied the well-posedness in the very weak sense. In the regular situation i.e., when the mass term
m
is a regular function, authors have proved that the Cauchy problem (
1.3
) has a unique solution
u\in C\left([0,T];H^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,T];L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right)
, where
H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a fractional Sobolev space. More precisely, they have obtained the following well-posedness result for
f\equiv 0
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be non-negative. Suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
u_{1}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. Then, there is a unique solution
u\in C\left([0,T];H^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right)\cap C^{1}\left([0,T];L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\right)
to (
1.3
), and it satisfies the estimate

\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\left(1+\|m\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)\left[\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\right],

(1.5)

where
\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\left\|(-\mathcal{L})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.

Furthermore in
[
6
]
, Chatzakou et al. also studied the Klein-Gordon equation with positive (left) Rockland operator on a general graded Lie group. This setting of Rockland operators on graded Lie groups allows one to consider both elliptic and sub-elliptic settings in
1.3
. In this paper we are interested in studying the discretization of the Klein-Gordon equation with the fractional Laplacian like the classical wave equation in
[
8
]
.

The semiclassical analogue of the fractional Klein-Gordon equation on the lattice
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is given by the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}u(t,k)+\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}u(t,k)+m(k)u(t,k)=f(t,k),\quad\text{ with }(t,k)\in(0,T]\times\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\
u(0,k)=u_{0}(k),\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}u(0,k)=u_{1}(k),\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

with non-negative mass term
m\in\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))
.
The well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1.6
) on the discrete lattice
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
varies from the results obtained in Theorem
1.1
for the Euclidean case
in the sense that (
1.6
) is always well-posed in
\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, while in the Euclidean case, it is well-posed in fractional Sobolev space. More precisely, we shall prove the following well-posedness result for the lattice case in Section
3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T>0
. Assume that
m\in\ell^{\infty}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))
, then the Cauchy problem (
1.6
) is well-posed in
\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. In particular, if
u_{0},u_{1}\in\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\right)
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))
, then for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have
u(t),\partial_{t}u(t)\in\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
. Moreover for each
\hbar>0
, it satisfies the estimate

\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}\lesssim\left(1+\|m\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\right)\times\\
\left[\left(\hbar^{-2\alpha}+\|m\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\right)\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))}\right],

(1.7)

for all
u_{0},u_{1}\in\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and
f\in L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))
. The constant depends on
T
but not on
\hbar
.

A natural question one may ask here is:

Why is there a difference in the well-posedness results between the Euclidean case and the lattice case?

In view of Theorem
1.2
, the dependence of RHS of estimate (
1.7
) on
\hbar
can answer this question immediately. Clearly, RHS may go to infinity as
\hbar\rightarrow 0
. This difference arises because of the difference in boundedness behavior of the discrete fractional Laplacian and the fractional Laplacian on
\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, respectively.

Further, we are also interested in approximating the continuous solution in Euclidean settings
by the discrete solution in the lattice settings. There are many ways to approximate but a large literature includes the numerical approaches or by minimizing the notion of distance between the continuous solution and the discrete one, see for example
[
17
,
18
,
7
]
and references therein. The nonlocality and singularity of the fractional Laplace operator in equation (
1.3
) are major obstacles in numerical approach. So, in this paper we are interested in approximating the continuous solution by the discrete solution in
\ell^{2}
-norm like we did for the classical wave equation in
[
8
]
. The following theorem shows that under the assumptions that the solutions in the Euclidean case exist, they can be approximated/recovered in the semiclassical limit as
\hbar\rightarrow 0
. We require a little additional Sobolev
regularity to ensure that the convergence results are global on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
u
and
v
be the solutions of the Cauchy problems (
1.6
) on
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and (
1.3
) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively. Assume that the Cauchy data
u_{0}\in H^{4\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
u_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for every
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\|v(t)-u(t)\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}+\left\|\partial_{t}v(t)-\partial_{t}u(t)\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\hbar\rightarrow 0.

(1.8)

Thus, when
\hbar\to 0
, for the above mentioned space, we actually recover the well-posedness
results on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the solution
u
in the above statement becomes restricted to the lattice
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

We note that the symbolic calculus of pseudo-difference operators on lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has been developed in
[
5
]
. The symbolic calculus on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be thought
of as a dual one to the calculus developed on the torus in
[
25
,
26
]
. The difference equations on lattice
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, including Schrödinger equations have been
studied (e.g. in
[
23
,
22
,
19
,
24
]
) by developing the analysis in terms of kernels.

Definition 1.4

(Symbol class
S^{m}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}\right)
)
.

Let
m\in(-\infty,\infty)
. We say that a function
\sigma
:
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
belongs to
S^{m}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}\right)
if
\sigma(k,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}\right)
for all
k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and for all multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
there exists a positive constant
C_{\alpha,\beta,\hbar}
such that

\left|D_{\theta}^{(\beta)}\Delta_{k}^{\alpha}\sigma(k,\theta)\right|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,\hbar}(1+|k|)^{m-|\alpha|},

(1.9)

for all
k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}
. We denote by
\mathrm{Op}(\sigma)
the pseudo-difference operator with symbol
\sigma
given by

\operatorname{Op}(\sigma)f(k):=\hbar^{n/2}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}e^{2\pi ik\cdot\theta}\sigma(k,\theta)\widehat{f}(\theta)\mathrm{d}\theta,\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}

(1.10)

where

\widehat{f}(\theta)=\hbar^{n/2}\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}f(k)e^{-2\pi ik\cdot\theta},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}.

(1.11)

This paper is arranged in the following four sections. In Section

, we develop basic Fourier analysis and discrete fractional Laplacian on lattice

.
In Section

, we establish the well-posedness result for the Cauchy problem (
1.6
). Finally, in

, we discuss the limiting behaviour of
solutions to (
1.6
) in the limit of the semiclassical parameter
\hbar\to 0
.

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first recall some facts concerning the Fourier analysis on the discrete lattice
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

The Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
on the lattice
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is the space of rapidly decreasing functions
u:\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, that is,
u\in\mathcal{S}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
if for any
L<\infty
there exists a constant
C_{u,L,\hbar}
such that

|u(k)|\leq C_{u,L,\hbar}(1+|k|)^{-L},\quad\text{ for all }k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},

(2.1)

where
|k|=\hbar\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.
A grid function
u:\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is in
\ell^{p}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}),

1\leq p<\infty
, if

\|u\|_{\ell^{p}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}:=\left(\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left|u(k)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

(2.2)

while
u\in\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
if

\|u\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}:=\sup_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left|u(k)\right|<\infty.

(2.3)

If
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
, then

\ell^{p}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})\subsetneq\ell^{q}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})\text{ and }\|u\|_{\ell^{q}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\lesssim\|u\|_{\ell^{p}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}.

(2.4)

Let
u\in\ell^{p}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and
v\in\ell^{q}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, with
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, then the Hölder’s inequality takes the form

\|uv\|_{\ell^{1}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\leq\|u\|_{\ell^{p}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\|v\|_{\ell^{q}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}.

(2.5)

The inner product in the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)
is given by

\left(u,v\right)_{\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}=\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u(k)\overline{v(k)},

(2.6)

where
\overline{v}
is the complex conjugate of
v
. Let
\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional torus, which we identify with periodic
\left[-\frac{1}{2\hbar},\frac{1}{2\hbar}\right]^{n}
.
The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}:\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})
is defined as

\mathcal{F}_{\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u(\theta):=\hbar^{n/2}\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u(k)e^{-2\pi ik\cdot\theta},\quad u\in\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}),

(2.7)

and the inverse Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})\to\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
is defined as

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}v(k):=\hbar^{n/2}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}v(\theta)e^{2\pi ik\cdot\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta,\quad v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}).

(2.8)

The Plancherel formula takes the form

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}|\widehat{u}(\theta)|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}\widehat{u}(\theta)\overline{\widehat{u}(\theta)}d\theta

(2.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}\hbar^{n}\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u(k)e^{-2\pi ik\cdot\theta}\sum_{m\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\overline{u(m)}e^{2\pi im\cdot\theta}d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar^{n}\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\sum_{m\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u(k)\overline{u(m)}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}e^{2\pi i(m-k)\cdot\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|u(k)|^{2},

and the Parseval’s identity

\left(u,v\right)_{\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}\widehat{u}(\theta)\overline{\widehat{v}(\theta)}\mathrm{d}\theta.

(2.10)

The Fourier inversion formula is given by

u(k)=\hbar^{n/2}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}\widehat{u}(\theta)e^{2\pi ik\cdot\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta,\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

(2.11)

Let
\alpha>0
and let
u(x)
be a complex-valued function. The fractional centered difference (FCD) operator is given by

\Delta_{c}^{\alpha}u(x):=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{j}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha/2-j+1)\Gamma(\alpha/2+j+1)}u(x+j\hbar).

(2.12)

Using the following relation from
[
21
, page 114]
:

|2\sin(\pi\theta)|^{2\alpha}=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{j}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha/2-j+1)\Gamma(\alpha/2+j+1)}e^{2\pi ij\cdot\theta},

(2.13)

we get

\frac{(-1)^{j}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha/2-j+1)\Gamma(\alpha/2+j+1)}=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}|2\sin(\pi\theta)|^{2\alpha}e^{-2\pi ij\cdot\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta,

(2.14)

where
|2\sin(\pi\theta)|^{2\alpha}=\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}4\sin^{2}\left(\pi\theta_{i}\right)\right]^{\alpha}
Since, the integral form of the above identity make sense for
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as well, therefore, the definition of the fractional centered difference can be extended for functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as well in the following way

\Delta_{c}^{\alpha}u(x):=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{j}^{(\alpha)}u(x+j\hbar),

(2.15)

where the generating function
a_{j}^{(\alpha)}
is given by

a_{j}^{(\alpha)}:=\int\limits_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{n}}|2\sin(\pi\theta)|^{2\alpha}e^{-2\pi ij\cdot\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta=\int\limits_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{n}}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}4\sin^{2}\left(\pi\theta_{i}\right)\right]^{\alpha}e^{-2\pi ij\cdot\theta}\mathrm{d}\theta.

(2.16)

Using the Fourier transform and the Fourier inversion formula, it is very easy to verify that

|2\sin(\pi\theta)|^{2\alpha}=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{j}^{(\alpha)}e^{2\pi ij\theta}.

(2.17)

For more details about the FCD, we refer to
[
21
,
27
]
.

The discrete fractional Laplacian on
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is defined using the FCD. The discrete fractional Laplacian on
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, denoted by
\left(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)^{\alpha}
, is defined by

\left(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar}\right)^{\alpha}u(k):=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a^{(\alpha)}_{j}u(k+j\hbar),\quad 0<\alpha<1,

(2.18)

where
a^{(\alpha)}_{j}
is given by (
2.16
).
For example, take

\alpha=1
, then it is very easy to check that

1)

for
j=0
, we have
a_{j}^{(1)}=2n
,

2)

for
j=v_{i}
, we have
a_{j}^{(1)}=-1
,

3)

for
j=-v_{i}
, we have
a_{j}^{(1)}=-1
,

4)

for
j\neq 0,\pm v_{i}
, we have
a_{j}^{(1)}=0
,

where
v_{i}
is the
i^{th}
basis vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, having all zeros except for
1
at the
i^{th}
component.
This gives

\displaystyle(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{1}u(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2nu(k)-\sum\limits_{j=\pm v_{i}}u(k+j\hbar)

(2.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2nu(k)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(u(k+v_{i}\hbar)+u(k-v_{i}\hbar)\right),

which is usual discrete Laplacian on
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. For more details about the discrete Laplacian on
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, one can refer to
[
8
]
.

The symbol of
(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}
defined by
\sigma_{(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}}(k,\theta)=e^{-2\pi ik\cdot\theta}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}e^{2\pi ik\cdot\theta}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}}(k,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi ik\cdot\theta}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}e^{2\pi ik\cdot\theta}

(2.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi ik\cdot\theta}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{j}^{(\alpha)}e^{2\pi i(k+j\hbar)\cdot\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}a_{j}^{(\alpha)}e^{2\pi ij\hbar\cdot\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|2\sin(\pi\hbar\theta)|^{2\alpha},

with
(k,\theta)\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}
, and it is independent of
k
.

Moreover, since the symbol
\left|2\sin\left(\pi\hbar\theta\right)\right|^{2\alpha}
is non-negative, therefore the discrete fractional Laplacian is a self-adjoint operator i.e.,

((-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}u,u)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=((-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u,(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=(u,(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}u)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}.

(2.21)

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we will study the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.6
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Considering the discrete inner product of (
1.6
) with
\partial_{t}u
on both sides and comparing the real part, we get

\operatorname{Re}\left(\left(\partial_{t}^{2}u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}+\left(\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}+\right.\\
\left.\left(m(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\right)=\operatorname{Re}\left(f(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})},

(3.1)

for all
t\in(0,T]
.
Using the self-adjointness of
(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}
and the techniques from
[
2
,
3
]
, it is easy to check that:

i)

\operatorname{Re}\left(\partial_{t}^{2}u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
,

ii)

\operatorname{Re}\left(\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left\|\hbar^{-\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}
,

iii)

\operatorname{Re}\left(m(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}.

The energy functional of the system (
1.6
) is defined by

E(t):=\left\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\left\|\hbar^{-\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\left\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}.

(3.2)

Then from (
3.1
), it follows that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sqrt{E(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{E(t)}}\partial_{t}E(t)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{E(t)}}\operatorname{Re}\left(f(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{E(t)}}\left|\left(f(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{E(t)}}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})},

and so upon integrating, we obtain

\sqrt{E(t)}\leq\sqrt{E(0)}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s.

(3.4)

Applying the Young’s inequality followed by the Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle E(0)+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s,

for all
t\in[0,T]
, with constant depending on
T
but not on
\hbar
.
Using Definition
1.1
and the Parseval’s identity, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\hbar^{-\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{0}(\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hbar^{-\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{0}(\cdot),\hbar^{-\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{0}(\cdot))_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar^{-2\alpha}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}\left|2\sin(\pi\hbar\theta)\right|^{2\alpha}|\widehat{u_{0}}(\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar^{-2\alpha}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}4\sin^{2}\left(\pi\hbar\theta_{i}\right)\right]^{\alpha}|\widehat{u_{0}}(\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hbar^{-2\alpha}(4n)^{\alpha}\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n}}|\widehat{u_{0}}(\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\hbar^{-2\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}.

Furthermore, the hypothesis that
m\in\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})
gives

\left\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u_{0}(\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}\lesssim\|m\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}.

(3.7)

Combining the inequalities (
3.5
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
) with equality (
3.2
), we get

\left\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}\\
\lesssim\left(\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\hbar^{-2\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\|m\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))}\right),

(3.8)

and

\left\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}\\
\lesssim\left(\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\hbar^{-2\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\|m\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))}\right),

(3.9)

for all
t\in(0,T]
, with constants independent of
\hbar
.

Observe that to prove estimate (
1.7
), it remains to estimate
\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}
. Applying the Fourier transform to (
1.6
) with respect to
k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\widehat{u}_{tt}(t,\theta)+\beta^{2}\widehat{u}(t,\theta)=\widehat{g}(t,\theta)+\widehat{f}(t,\theta),\quad\text{ with }(t,\theta)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n},\\
\widehat{u}(0,\theta)=\widehat{u}_{0}(\theta),\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n},\\
\widehat{u}_{t}(0,\theta)=\widehat{u}_{1}(\theta),\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(3.10)

where
\beta^{2}=\hbar^{-2\alpha}|2\sin(\pi\hbar\theta)|^{2\alpha}
and
g(t,k)=-m(k)u(t,k)
.
If we denote

v(t):=\widehat{u}(t,\theta),\quad f(t):=\widehat{f}(t,\theta),\quad g(t):=\widehat{g}(t,\theta),

(3.11)

and

v_{0}:=\widehat{u}_{0}(\theta),\quad v_{1}:=\widehat{u}_{1}(\theta),

(3.12)

then (
3.10
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v^{\prime\prime}(t)+\beta^{2}v(t)=g(t)+f(t),\quad\text{ with }t\in(0,T],\\
v(0)=v_{0},\quad v^{\prime}(0)=v_{1},\end{array}\right.

(3.13)

with
\beta\geq 0
. By solving the homogeneous part of (
3.13
), and using the Duhamel’s principle (see, e.g.
[
11
]
), we get

v(t)=\cos\left(\beta t\right)v_{0}+\frac{\sin\left(\beta t\right)}{\beta}v_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin\left(\beta(t-s)\right)}{\beta}\left(g(s)+f(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s.

(3.14)

Using the following inequalities:

|\cos(\beta t)|\leq 1\text{~for all~}t\in[0,1]\text{~and~}|\sin(\beta t)|\leq 1,

(3.15)

for large values of
\beta t
, while
|\sin(\beta t)|\leq\beta t\leq\beta T
for small values of
\beta t,
the equality (
3.14
) gives

|v(t)|\lesssim|v_{0}|+|v_{1}|+\int\limits_{0}^{t}|g(s)|ds+\int\limits_{0}^{t}|f(s)|ds.

(3.16)

Now taking the
L^{2}
-norm, we get

\|\widehat{u}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|\widehat{u}_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})}+\left\|\widehat{u}_{1}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\widehat{g}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\|\widehat{f}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s.

(3.17)

Using the Plancherel formula, we obtain

\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\mathrm{d}s,

(3.18)

for all
t\in(0,T]
, where

\displaystyle\|g(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\|m(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|m\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\left\|m^{\frac{1}{2}}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}.

(3.19)

Combining the inequalities (
3.9
), (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we get

\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\lesssim\left\|u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}+T\left\|m\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\left(\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\right.\\
\left.\hbar^{-2\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\|m\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))}\right)\\
+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))},

(3.20)

for all
t\in(0,T]
. Then from (
3.8
) and (
3.20
), it follows that

\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}+\left\|\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}\lesssim\left(1+\|m\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\right)\times\\
\left[\left(\hbar^{-2\alpha}+\|m\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\right)\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T];\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}))}\right],

(3.21)

for all
t\in(0,T]
, with constant depending on
T
but not on
\hbar
. This gives the required estimate (
1.7
) from which the
uniqueness follows immediately.
∎

4.
Limit
\hbar\to 0

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Consider two Cauchy problems:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}^{2}u(t,k)+\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}u(t,k)+m(k)u(t,k)=0,\quad(t,k)\in(0,T]\times\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\
u(0,k)=u_{0}(k),\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}u(0,k)=u_{1}(k),\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{2}_{t}v(t,x)+(-\mathcal{L})^{\alpha}v(t,x)+m(x)v(t,x)=0,\quad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}v(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{array}\right.

(4.2)

where
\left(-\mathcal{L}\right)^{\alpha}
is the usual fractional Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here the initial data of the Cauchy problem (
4.1
) is the evaluation of the initial data from (
4.2
) on the lattice
\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. From the equations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
),
denoting
w:=u-v
, we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial^{2}_{t}w(t,k)+\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}w(t,k)+m(k)w(t,k)=\left((-\mathcal{L})^{\alpha}-\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}\right)v(t,k),\\
w(0,k)=0,\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n},\\
\partial_{t}w(0,k)=0,\quad k\in\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

Since
w_{0}=w_{1}=0
, applying the Theorem
1.2
for the above Cauchy problem and using the estimate (
1.7
), we get

\left\|w(t)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}+\left\|\partial_{t}w(t)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}^{n})}\lesssim\\
(1+\left\|m\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})})\left\|\left((-\mathcal{L})^{\alpha}-\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}\right)v(t)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})},

(4.4)

for all
t\in[0,T].
Now we will estimate the term
\left\|\left((-\mathcal{L})^{\alpha}-\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}\right)v(t)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}.

Using the Plancherel formula, we have

\left\|\left((-\mathcal{L})^{\alpha}-\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}\right)v(t)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\left\|(|2\pi\theta|^{2\alpha}-\hbar^{-2\alpha}|2\sin(\pi\hbar\theta)|^{2\alpha})\widehat{v}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})}.

(4.5)

Since
|\cdot|^{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous for
0<\alpha<1
, we have the following inequality

\left||x|^{2\alpha}-|y|^{2\alpha}\right|\lesssim\left||x|^{2}-|y|^{2}\right|^{\alpha},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(4.6)

and
|x|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n}^{2}}
. Now, using the above inequality and the Taylor expansion for
\sin^{2}(\pi\hbar\theta_{j})
, we get

\displaystyle\left||2\pi\theta|^{2\alpha}-\hbar^{-2\alpha}|2\sin(\pi\hbar\theta)|^{2\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}4\pi^{2}\theta^{2}_{j}-\hbar^{-2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}4\sin^{2}(\pi\hbar\theta_{j})\right|^{\alpha}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left[4\pi^{2}\theta^{2}_{j}-\hbar^{-2}4\left(\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}\theta_{j}^{2}-\frac{\pi^{4}}{3}\hbar^{4}\theta_{j}^{4}\cos\left(2\xi_{j}\right)\right)\right]\right|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4\pi^{4}\hbar^{2}}{3}\right)^{\alpha}\left|\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}^{4}\cos(2\xi_{j})\right|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\hbar^{2\alpha}\left[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{4}_{j}\right]^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\hbar^{2\alpha}|\theta|^{4\alpha},\quad\theta\in\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n},

where
|\theta|^{4\alpha}=\left[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{2}_{j}\right]^{2\alpha}
and
\xi_{j}\in(0,\pi\hbar\theta_{j})
or
(\pi\hbar\theta_{j},0)
depending on the sign of
\theta_{j}
. Since
u_{0}\in H^{4\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
v\in H^{4\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by Theorem
1.1
. Now, combining the above estimate with (
4.5
), we get

\left\|\left((-\mathcal{L})^{\alpha}-\hbar^{-2\alpha}(-\mathcal{L}_{\hbar})^{\alpha}\right)v(t)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n})}\lesssim\hbar^{2\alpha}\||\theta|^{4\alpha}\widehat{v}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\hbar}^{n})},

(4.8)

Using (
4.4
) and (
4.8
), we get
\|w(t)\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}w(t)\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}^{2}\rightarrow 0
as
\hbar\rightarrow 0
. Hence
\|w(t)\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}\rightarrow 0
and
\left\|\partial_{t}w(t)\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\hbar\mathbb{Z}^{n}\right)}\rightarrow 0
as
\hbar\rightarrow 0
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

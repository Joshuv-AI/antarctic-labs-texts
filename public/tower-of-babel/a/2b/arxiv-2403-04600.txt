Equivalence of Constacyclic Codes with shift constants of different orders
Reza Dastbasteh, Farzad Padashnick, Pedro M. Crespo, Markus Grassl, Javad Sharafi

Abstract
. Let a a and b b be two non-zero elements of a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , where q > 2 q>2 . It has been shown that if a a and b b have the same multiplicative order in 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , then the families of a a -constacyclic and b b -constacyclic codes over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} are monomially equivalent. In this paper, we investigate the monomial equivalence of a a -constacyclic and b b -constacyclic codes when a a and b b have distinct multiplicative orders. We present novel conditions for establishing monomial equivalence in such constacyclic codes, surpassing previous methods of determining monomially equivalent constacyclic and cyclic codes. As an application, we use these results to search for new linear codes more systematically. In particular, we present more than 70 70 new record-breaking linear codes over various finite fields, as well as new binary quantum codes.



Keywords:

constacyclic code, cyclic code, monomial equivalence, isometric equivalence, new linear code.

1.
Introduction

Constacyclic codes are an important class of linear codes because of
their algebraic structure and efficient encoding and decoding
algorithms. Due to their similarities with cyclic codes, constacyclic
codes inherit many of the desirable properties of cyclic codes making
them well-suited for various practical applications. For instance, in

[
10
]
, the authors proposed a decoding algorithm for
constacyclic codes using a slight modification of that of cyclic
codes. Furthermore, as discussed in
[
13
, Section 1.2]
,
certain constacyclic codes can outperform cyclic codes by offering
larger minimum distances and producing Hamming codes that are not
equivalent to cyclic codes.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field, where
q=p^{m}
and
p
is a prime
number. We denote the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
by
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
n
be a positive integer. A linear
code
C
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
vector subspace of

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. The parameters of a linear code of length
n
, dimension

k
, and minimum (Hamming) distance
d
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is denoted by

[n,k,d]_{q}
.
We denote the multiplicative order of
a
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
by
{\rm{ord}}(a)
.
A linear code
C
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called

a
-constacyclic
if for each codeword
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in C
, we have
(ac_{n-1},c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-2})\in C
. The value
a
is called the
shift constant
of such a
constacyclic code. A constacyclic code with shift constant
a=1

is called
cyclic
.

The concept of equivalence of codes has been known for several
decades. It can be traced back to the early developments in coding
theory.
It involves considering two linear codes as equivalent if one can be
transformed into the other through certain operations, such as
permutation of coordinates, multiplication of coordinates by a nonzero
scalar, and applying a field automorphism to all codewords. This
concept allows us to study different codes that have the same
underlying structure and properties.

There have been several works on the classification and determination
of equivalent cyclic and constacyclic codes using algebraic properties
of these codes; for example, see
[
3
,
4
,
9
,
12
,
15
]
. In
[
3
]
, the authors proved that for
each
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
such that
{\rm{ord}}(a)={\rm{ord}}(b)
, there exists an
isometry between the class of
a
-constacyclic and
b
-constacyclic
codes. Therefore, the next natural step in studying equivalent
constacyclic codes is to consider codes with shift constants of
different orders.

In
[
5
]
, Jürgen Bierbrauer established that every

a
-constacyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is monomially
equivalent to a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, provided that

\gcd({\rm{ord}}(a),n)=1
. This result gives a relationship between
constacyclic codes and cyclic codes, and also imposes another
restriction on constacyclic codes, allowing us to study codes that
exhibit properties that may be different from cyclic codes.

This paper extends previous works on equivalence of codes by providing
new conditions for the monomial equivalence of two constacyclic codes,
or a pair of constacyclic and cyclic codes. Specifically, we introduce
a new condition for establishing the monomial equivalence of two
constacyclic codes with shift constants of different orders.
Furthermore, we prove that our criterion generalizes the mentioned
result of Bierbrauer in
[
5
]
, as it can detect monomially
equivalent
a
-constacyclic and cyclic codes of length
n
, even when

\gcd({\rm{ord}}(a),n)>1
. Our new results are applied to small finite
fields, where we classify families of monomially equivalent
constacyclic codes. We also use these results to search more
systematically for new linear codes. In particular, we construct many
new record-breaking linear codes over various finite fields, as well
as binary quantum codes.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper we assume that
n
is a positive integer such
that
\gcd(n,q)=1
. It is well-known that there is a one-to-one
correspondence between
a
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
and ideals of the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-a\rangle
. Thus
each
a
-constacyclic code
C
is generated by a unique monic
polynomial
f(x)
such that
f(x)\mid(x^{n}-a)
. The polynomial

f(x)
is called the
generator polynomial
of
C
. Therefore
to determine all
a
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
it
is essential to find the irreducible factorization of
x^{n}-a
.

Let
{\rm{ord}}(a)=t
and
\alpha
be a primitive
(tn)
-th root of unity in
a finite field extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\alpha^{n}=a
. Then all
different roots of
x^{n}-a
are in the form
\alpha^{s}
, where
s\in\Omega_{a}
and

(2.1)

\Omega_{a}=\{{kt+1}\colon 0\leq k\leq n-1\}.

Remark 2.1
.

In the numerical examples of constacyclic codes throughout this paper,
the
(tn)
-th root of unity
\alpha
is fixed as follows. First we
find the smallest integer
z
such that
tn\mid q^{z}-1
. Let
\gamma

be the primitive element in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{z}}
chosen by the computer
algebra system Magma
[
7
]
. Set
\alpha_{0}=\gamma^{(q^{z}-1)/(tn)}
. Then there exists an integer
1\leq i\leq tn-1
such that

\alpha_{0}^{in}=a
. Now select
\alpha=\alpha_{0}^{i}
.

For each
s\in\Omega_{a}
, the
q
-cyclotomic coset
of
s
modulo
tn
is defined by

Z(s)=\{(q^{i}s)\bmod{tn}\colon 0\leq i\leq r-1\},

where
r
is the smallest number such that
q^{r}s\equiv s\pmod{tn}
. It is straight forward to see that there exist elements

b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}\in\Omega_{a}
such that
\{Z(b_{i})\colon 1\leq i\leq m\}
forms a partition of
\Omega_{a}
. Thus one can find the following
irreducible factorization of
x^{n}-a
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
:

x^{n}-a=\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x),

where

f_{i}(x)=\prod_{\ell\in Z(b_{i})}(x-\alpha^{\ell}).

A common approach to represent an
a
-constacyclic code with generator
polynomial
g(x)
(the monic polynomial dividing
x^{n}-a
) is by its

defining set

A\subseteq\Omega_{a}
such that
s\in A
if and
only if
g(\alpha^{s})=0
. It is easy to see that the defining set of an

a
-constacyclic code is a unique (as
\alpha
is already fixed) union
of
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
nt
.

An
n\times n
matrix
M
is called a
monomial matrix
over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
if
M
has exactly one nonzero entry from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in each row
and each column. Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two linear codes of length

n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
G
be a generator matrix for
C_{1}
. Then
C_{1}

and
C_{2}
are called
monomially equivalent
if there exists a
monomial matrix
M
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
GM
is a generator matrix
for
C_{2}
.

Another commonly used notion of equivalence of
codes is based on isometries between linear codes. We denote the
(Hamming) distance between two vectors
u
and
v
by
d(u,v)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
C_{1}
and

C_{2}
are called
isometric equivalent
if there exists an

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism
\phi\colon C_{1}\rightarrow C_{2}
such that

d(u,v)=d(\phi(u),\phi(v))
for each
u,v\in C_{1}
.

The map
\phi
with the above properties is called an
isometry of
linear codes
. It was shown that the following connection holds
between monomial and isometric equivalence of codes.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
6
, Corollary 1]

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two linear codes over a finite field. Then

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are monomially equivalent if and only if they are
isometric equivalent.

Hence the concepts of monomial and isometric equivalence are identical
in the sense of the above theorem and from now on we mainly use the
term monomial equivalence for both the monomial and isometric
equivalence of codes.

For each
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
, the families of
a
-constacyclic and

b
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
will be called
monomially equivalent
if there exists a one-to-one correspondence
between them that is given by an isometry of linear codes. The
following result shows that constacyclic codes with shifts constants
of the same order are monomially equivalent.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
3
, Corollary 1]

Let
n
be a positive integer such that
\gcd(n,q)=1
. Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that
{\rm{ord}}(a)={\rm{ord}}(b)
. Then the families of

a
-constacyclic and
b
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

are monomially equivalent.

Another result which will be generalized in the paper is the following
statement which states a sufficient condition for monomial equivalence
of cyclic and constacyclic codes.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
5
, Theorem 15]

Let
n
be a positive integer such that
\gcd(n,q)=1
. Let

a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
such that
\gcd(n,{\rm{ord}}(a))=1
. Then the families of

a
-constacyclic and cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are
monomially equivalent.

3.
Equivalence of constacyclic codes with shift constants of different orders

In this section, we present novel results on the monomial equivalence
of constacyclic codes over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. To the best of
our knowledge, there has been no comprehensive discussion on the
monomial equivalence of constacyclic codes with shift constants of
different orders. Therefore, we introduce new criteria for
establishing monomial equivalence of constacyclic codes that go beyond
the result of Theorem
2.4
. Our result also generalizes that
of Theorem
2.5
by identifying monomially equivalent
constacyclic and cyclic codes that were not previously detectable
using existing methods. These results will be applied in Section

5
to identify new record-breaking linear codes.

For the rest of this section, we fix
\xi
to be a generator of the
multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\langle\xi\rangle.

Our main result of this section is given below which discusses
monomial equivalence of constacyclic codes with shift constants of
not necessarily the same order.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\xi^{i_{1}},\xi^{i_{2}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ast}
for some
0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq q-2
such that
i_{1}=i_{2}s
for some non-negative integer
s
. Let
n
be
a positive integer such that
\gcd(n,q)=1
and
\gcd(n,q-1)=m
. If

m\mid\gcd(i_{2}(s-1),q-1)
, then the families of

\xi^{i_{1}}
-constacyclic and
\xi^{i_{2}}
-constacyclic codes of
length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are monomially equivalent.

Proof.

Let
n=m\beta
for some positive integer
\beta
, where
\gcd(\beta,q-1)=1
. The fact that
m\mid\gcd(i_{2}(s-1),q-1)
implies that there
exist integers
\gamma
and
\theta
such that
m\gamma=i_{2}(s-1)
and
m\theta=q-1
. Since
\gcd(\beta,q-1)=1
, we have

\gcd(\beta,\theta)=1
. So there exists
1\leq\beta^{\prime}\leq\theta-1

such that
\beta\beta^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{\theta}
. Next, we show that
the map

\Phi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-\xi^{i_{1}}\rangle\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-\xi^{i_{2}}\rangle

defined by
\Phi(x)=\xi^{i}x
, where
i=\gamma\beta^{\prime}+\theta
, is an isometry of linear codes.
First we show that it is well-defined. It is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\Phi(x^{n}-\xi^{i_{1}})=\xi^{in}x^{n}-\xi^{i_{1}}=\xi^{in}(x^{n}-\xi^{i_{1}-in})\in\langle x^{n}-\xi^{i_{2}}\rangle.

Note that since
\beta\beta^{\prime}\equiv 1\pmod{\theta}
, there exists
an integer
\ell
such that
\beta\beta^{\prime}=1+\ell\theta
. Using the
facts that
i_{1}=i_{2}s
,
i=\gamma\beta^{\prime}+\theta
,
n=m\beta
,
m\gamma=i_{2}(s-1)
, and
m\theta=q-1
, we compute

(3.1)

\begin{split}i_{1}-in&=i_{1}-(\gamma\beta^{\prime}+\theta)m\beta=i_{2}s-\gamma m(1+\ell\theta)=i_{2}s-i_{2}(s-1)-(q-1)\gamma\ell\\
&\equiv i_{2}\pmod{q-1}.\end{split}

This shows that
\Phi(x^{n}-\xi^{i_{1}})\in\langle x^{n}-\xi^{i_{2}}\rangle
and therefore
\Phi
is well-defined. The map
\Phi
is an
evaluation map, so it is a ring homomorphism, and it preserves the
weight of vectors. Thus it is enough to show that
\Phi
is
surjective, and the fact that it is a map between finite sets implies
that
\Phi
is a ring isomorphism. Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-\xi^{i_{2}}\rangle
. Then
p^{\prime}(x)=p(\xi^{-i}x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/\langle x^{n}-\xi^{i_{1}}\rangle
and
\Phi(p^{\prime}(x))=p(x)
. This proves
that
\Phi
is surjective. Thus it is a ring isomorphism that
preserves the distance between each pair of vectors.
∎

Note that in Theorem
3.1
the condition
i_{1}=i_{2}s

implies that
{\rm{ord}}(\xi^{i_{1}})\mid{\rm{ord}}(\xi^{i_{2}})
. The next
corollary gives a special case of Theorem
3.1
which can
be easily applied to check the monomial equivalence of certain
constacyclic codes.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that
{\rm{ord}}(a)\mid{\rm{ord}}(b)
. Let
n
be
a positive integer such that
\gcd(n,q)=\gcd(n,q-1)=1
. Then the
families of
a
-constacyclic and
b
-constacyclic codes of length
n

over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are monomially equivalent.

Proof.

Let
a=\xi^{i_{1}}
and
b=\xi^{i_{2}}
for some
0\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq q-2
. Then
{\rm{ord}}(a)\mid{\rm{ord}}(b)
implies that there exists a
non-negative integer
s
such that
i_{1}=i_{2}s
. Now the proof follows
from Theorem
3.1
since
1\mid\gcd(i_{2}(s-1),q-1)
.
∎

Next, we provide an example of monomially equivalent constacyclic
codes that cannot be obtained by Theorem
2.4
. Nevertheless,
such cases can be justified using the outcome of Corollary

3.2
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
a=4
and
b=2
be elements of
\mathbb{F}_{5}
. We have
{\rm{ord}}(2)=4
and

{\rm{ord}}(4)=2
and hence
{\rm{ord}}(4)\mid{\rm{ord}}(2)
. By Corollary

3.2
we have that
2
-constacyclic and

4
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{5}
are monomially
equivalent for each
n
satisfying
\gcd(n,5)=\gcd(n,4)=1
.

Next, we provide another example of monomially equivalent constacyclic
codes that can be detected by Theorem
3.1
, but not by
Corollary
3.2
. This stresses that the result of
Corollary
3.2
is only a special case of Theorem

3.1
, although Corollary
3.2
can
be applied easier in practice.

Example 3.4
.

Let
a=6
and
b=5
be elements of
\mathbb{F}_{7}^{\ast}=\langle 5\rangle
. We
have
6=5^{3}
in
\mathbb{F}_{7}
. Let
n
be a positive integer such that
\gcd(n,7)=1
and
\gcd(n,6)=2
. Let
i_{1}=3
,
i_{2}=1
, and
s=3

(
a=5^{i_{1}}
,
b=5^{i_{2}}
, and
i_{1}=i_{2}s
). Note that
2\mid\gcd(i_{2}(3-1),6)=\gcd(2,6)
. Hence Theorem
3.1
shows
that
a
-constacyclic and
b
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over

\mathbb{F}_{7}
are monomially equivalent. On the other hand, Corollary

3.2
requires
\gcd(n,7)=\gcd(n,6)=1
.

Recall that in a cyclic group of order
m
generated by an element

a
, we have
{\rm{ord}}(a^{k})=\frac{m}{\gcd(m,k)}
for any
0\leq k\leq m-1
. Next we prove that Theorem
2.5
is a special case of
Theorem
3.1
.

Let
C
be an
a
-constacyclic code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
n
such
that
\gcd(n,q)=1
and
\gcd(n,{\rm{ord}}(a))=1
. Suppose that
a=\xi^{i}
for
some
0\leq i\leq q-2
. Since
\gcd(n,{\rm{ord}}(a))=1
and

{\rm{ord}}(a)=\frac{q-1}{\gcd(i,q-1)}
, we have
\gcd(n,\frac{q-1}{\gcd(i,q-1)})=1
. This implies that

\gcd(n,q-1)=\gcd(n,\gcd(i,q-1))
. Note also that the latter equality
implies that
\gcd(n,q-1)\mid\gcd(i,q-1)
. Now
\xi^{i}
and
\xi^{0}

satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3.1
. In other words,
putting
i_{2}=i
and
i_{1}=0
implies that
\gcd(n,q-1)\mid\gcd(i(0-1),q-1)=\gcd(i,q-1)
. So Theorem
3.1
results
that the families of
a
-constacyclic and cyclic codes of length
n

over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are monomially equivalent.

In the following example, we show that Theorem
3.1

offers a wider range of applicability than Theorem
2.5
in
determining monomially equivalent constacyclic and cyclic codes.

Example 3.5
.

In the field
\mathbb{F}_{5}
, we have
\mathbb{F}_{5}^{\ast}=\langle 2\rangle
. Let
n
be
a positive integer in the form
n=4k+2
such that
\gcd(n,5)=1
. We
show the families of
4
-constacyclic codes and cyclic codes of length

n
over
\mathbb{F}_{5}
are monomially equivalent. First note that

{\rm{ord}}(4)=2
and
\gcd(n,{\rm{ord}}(4))=2
. Therefore, we cannot apply
Theorem
2.5
in this case. Moreover, we have
\gcd(n,5-1)=2
. Applying the result of Theorem
3.1
to

2^{i_{2}}
and
2^{i_{1}}
for
i_{2}=2
and
i_{1}=0
implies that

4
-constacyclic and cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{5}
are
monomially equivalent.

This example can be generalized as follows.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
n
and
m
be positive integers such that
\gcd(n,q-1)=m
. Then the families of
\xi^{mr}
-constacyclic and cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are monomially equivalent, for any positive integer
r
.

Proof.

Let
i_{2}=mr
and
i_{1}=0
. Next we apply Theorem
3.1
to
\xi^{i_{2}}
and
\xi^{i_{1}}
. First note that
\gcd(n,q-1)=m
and
m\mid\gcd(i_{2}(0-1),q-1)
. Now all the conditions of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied and thus the result follows from it.
∎

By Corollary
3.6
, if
\gcd(n,q-1)=m
, then
\xi^{mr}
-constacyclic and cyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are monomially equivalent for any positive integer
r
. Suppose additionally that
\gcd(r,m)=1
and
m^{2}\mid q-1
. Then
m\mid\frac{q-1}{\gcd(mr,q-1)}
yielding
m\mid{\rm{ord}}(\xi^{mr})
. As
m\mid n
, we have
m\mid\gcd(n,{\rm{ord}}(\xi^{mr}))
implying that Theorem
2.5
cannot be applied to prove monomial equivalence when
\gcd(r,m)=1
and
m^{2}\mid q-1
.

4.
Classification of equivalent constacyclic codes over Small Finite Fields

In this section, we study the restriction of the results in the
previous section to constacyclic codes over small finite fields when

q\leq 7
. This illustrates our new results and
also helps to classify families of monomially equivalent constacyclic
codes over small finite fields. In the next section, in our computer
search for new linear codes, we will apply the results of this section
to speed up the search. In fact we give at least one new
record-breaking linear code over each of the finite fields discussed
in this section.

We use graphs to depict the monomial equivalence of constacyclic
codes. In particular, for each finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we use a graph in
which a vertex labeled with
a
corresponds to the family of

-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover, each edge

constacyclic codes are monomially equivalent. Such a graph will be
called the “equivalence graph” of constacyclic codes.
Recall that we only consider constacyclic codes of length

n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
when
\gcd(n,q)=1
.

The first nontrivial constacyclic codes appear over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
. By
Theorem
2.5
, if
\gcd(n,2)=1
, the families of
cyclic and
2
-constacyclic codes of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{3}

).

The next example shows that if
\gcd(n,2)\neq 1
, then cyclic and

2
-constacyclic codes of length

over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
are not necessarily

Example 4.1
.

Let
n=10
. Then
\gcd(10,3)=1
and
\gcd(10,2)=2
. The irreducible

Therefore, the number of cyclic codes of length

5.
New record-breaking codes

Finding linear codes with good parameters is a challenging task in
algebraic coding theory. Many works in the literature have made
efforts to develop systematic approaches for computer-based searches
of good codes. However, the computational complexity associated with
computations like the code’s minimum distance has significantly slowed
down the search process. Recently, advancements have been made by
employing the equivalence of linear cyclic and constacyclic codes to
design more efficient search algorithms

[
3
,
4
,
12
]
. As a result, dozens of new
linear codes and binary quantum codes have been discovered.

In this section, we utilize the results on the equivalence of
constacyclic codes to cut down the size of the search space for new
linear codes with good parameters. To begin, we employ the results
presented in Theorems
2.4
and
2.5
, as well as
Sections
3
and
4
, to identify all families of monomially
equivalent constacyclic codes with distinct shift constants. Once this
is done, we focus on a specific shift constant
a
and apply the
results discussed in
[
1
,
12
]
to pinpoint
monomially equivalent
a
-constacyclic codes. This helps to search
more systematically for new linear codes with good parameters.
In certain scenarios, calculating the actual minimum distance can be a
time-consuming process, sometimes taking several days or even
years. However, by identifying equivalent codes and narrowing down the
search space, the computation can be restricted to a smaller set of
codes.

In particular, by pruning the search space using these results we were
able to identify more than
70
new record-breaking linear codes over
various finite fields and binary quantum codes. In our search we
considered the following methods:

•

direct construction of constacyclic codes,

•

applying Construction X to two constacyclic (cyclic) codes with an auxiliary best-known linear code,

•

applying Construction XX to three constacyclic (cyclic) codes with two auxiliary best-known linear codes.

In our computations we represent the currently best-known linear code
of length
n
and dimension
k
by
{\rm{BKLC}}(n,k)
. The parameters of
such codes are available online
[
14
]
.

Before presenting our new codes, we first recall Construction X (see

[
16
, Chapter 18, §7]
). Let

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be linear codes over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameters
[n,k_{1},d_{1}]_{q}
and
[n,k_{2},d_{2}]_{q}
such that
C_{2}\subseteq C_{1}
. Then,
we can express
C_{1}
as union of
q^{k_{1}-k_{2}}
different cosets of

C_{2}
, namely
D_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq q^{k_{1}-k_{2}}
, with
D_{1}=C_{2}
.

Let
C_{3}
be another linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameters

[n_{3},k_{3},d_{3}]_{q}
such that
k_{3}=k_{1}-k_{2}
. We denote each element of

C_{3}
by
z_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq q^{k_{3}}
, with
z_{1}=0
.
Construction X
of

(C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
is defined by

(5.1)

E=\{(x_{i},z_{i})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n+n_{3}}\colon x_{i}\in D_{i},1\leq i\leq q^{k_{1}-k_{2}}\}.

The labeling of the cosets
D_{i}
and the vectors
z_{i}\in C_{3}
can
chosen such that the new code
E
is also a linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

and has parameters
[n+n_{3},k_{1},d\geq\min(d_{2},d_{1}+d_{3})]_{q}
.
To apply Construction X to linear codes
(C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
, we use the
built-in function in Magma
[
7
]
using the command

ConstructionX(C1, C2, C3)
. Our numerical results result in
several improvements of the parameters of codes in the online tables

[
14
]
.

Example 5.6
.

(1) Let
C
be an
\omega
-constacyclic code of length
111
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}

with defining set
Z(1)\cup Z(7)\cup Z(19)
. Our computations using
Magma
[
7
]
show that
C
is a
[111,57,25]_{4}
Hermitian
dual-containing linear code. Computing the minimum distance took
almost
70
CPU years, and it was completed in about
47
days using
up to
600
cores. This code has a
better minimum distance

than the previously best-known linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, which has
minimum distance
23
. Shortening the code
C
gives another new
code with parameters
[110,56,25]_{4}
. Applying the construction of

[
8
, Theorem 2]
gives a
record-breaking
binary
quantum code with parameters
[\![111,3,25]\!]_{2}
. Moreover,
applying the construction of
[
11
, Corollary 3]

to the Hermitian dual of
C
gives another
record-breaking

binary quantum code with parameters
[\![114,0,26]\!]_{2}
. The latter
code is in correspondence to a Hermitian self-dual code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}

with parameters
[114,57,26]_{4}
, which is indeed another
record-breaking
linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
.

The
\omega
-constacyclic code of length
111
over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
with
defining set
Z(1)\cup Z(7)\cup Z(19)\cup Z(37)
is the Hermitian
dual of the code
C
. It has parameters
[111,54,d]_{q}
with

d\in\{26,28\}
. Verifying that the minimum distance is indeed
28

is estimated to take about
33
CPU years; we have not (yet) carried
out this calculation, but the code
[111,54,28]_{4}
would also
improve the currently best parameters.

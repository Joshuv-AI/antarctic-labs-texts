Exploring quantum weight enumerators from the n n -qubit parallelized SWAP test
Fei Shi, Kaiyi Guo, Xiande Zhang, Qi Zhao

Abstract
Quantum weight enumerators play a crucial role in quantum error-correcting codes and multipartite entanglement. They can be used to investigate the existence of quantum error-correcting codes and k k -uniform states. In this work, we build the connection between quantum weight enumerators and the n n -qubit parallelized SWAP test. We discover that each shadow enumerator corresponds precisely to a probability in the n n -qubit parallelized SWAP test, providing a computable and operational meaning for the shadow enumerators. Due to the non-negativity of probabilities, we obtain an elegant proof for the shadow inequalities. Concurrently, we can also calculate the Shor-Laflamme enumerators and the Rains unitary enumerators from the n n -qubit parallelized SWAP test. For applications, we employ the n n -qubit parallelized SWAP test to determine the distances of quantum error-correcting codes, and the k k -uniformity of pure states. Our results indicate that quantum weight enumerators can be efficiently estimated on quantum computers, and opening a path to calculate the distances of quantum error-correcting codes.



Introduction

Shor and Laflamme introduced quantum weight enumerators for quantum error-correcting codes (QECCs) as a method to characterize them

. Rains also introduced two additional weight enumerators known as the unitary enumerators and the shadow enumerators

For example, they have been employed to investigate the existence of asymmetric quantum codes

However, effective methods to calculate these quantum weight enumerators and determine the distances of QECCs through quantum circuits are currently lacking.

Determination of the distances for QECCs
.
—

Given an unknown normalized projector
{\cal Q}
, we can determine the distance
\delta
of the corresponding QECC by using the
n
-qubit parallelized SWAP test. Firstly, we need to apply the
n
-qubit parallelized SWAP test of
{\cal Q}
and
{\cal Q}
to obtain the probability distribution.
Secondly, we need to calculate the purity
\mathop{\rm Tr}({\cal Q}^{2})
by using Proposition
2

{\cal Q}
is equal to
\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm Tr}({\cal Q}^{2})}
. Thirdly, we need to calculate the Rains unitary enumerators (we only consider the Rains unitary enumerators for simplicity) by using Proposition
4
. Finally, we need to check whether
\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm Tr}({\cal Q}^{2})}B_{\delta-1}^{\prime}=A_{\delta-1}^{\prime}
.

Next, we show an example. Given an unknown 5-qubit normalized projector
{\cal Q}_{5}
, we want to determine its distance. Assume that we obtain the probability distribution (shadow enumerators) from the
5
-qubit parallelized SWAP test of

and

Robustness of the Rains enumerators
—

When preparing a QECC in experiment, noise is inevitable, which leads to a certain distance between the prepared QECC and the target QECC. In this context, what kind of impact will this have on the Rains enumerator? Let
{\cal Q}
be the target QECC. In the
n
-qubit parallelized SWAP test, we need to prepare two copies of
{\cal Q}
. By the no-cloning theorem, we have to prepare two QECCs separately. Due to the noise, we may prepare
{\cal Q}^{\prime}
and
{\cal Q}^{\prime\prime}
, where the trace distance between

{\cal Q}^{\prime}
(
{\cal Q}^{\prime\prime}
) and
{\cal Q}
satisfies
D({\cal Q}^{\prime},{\cal Q})\leq\epsilon_{1}
(
D({\cal Q}^{\prime\prime},{\cal Q})\leq\epsilon_{2}
). However, if
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
are small enough, then the Rains enumerators will also be very close.

Proposition 5

If
D({\cal Q}^{\prime},{\cal Q})\leq\epsilon_{1}
and
D({\cal Q}^{\prime\prime},{\cal Q})\leq\epsilon_{2}
, then
|A^{\prime}_{j}({\cal Q}^{\prime},{\cal Q}^{\prime\prime})-A^{\prime}_{j}({\cal Q},{\cal Q})|\leq 4\binom{n}{j}(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})
and
|B^{\prime}_{j}({\cal Q}^{\prime},{\cal Q}^{\prime\prime})-B^{\prime}_{j}({\cal Q},{\cal Q})|\leq 4\binom{n}{j}(\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2})
.

The proof of Proposition
5
is given in Appendix. When measuring probabilities in the
n
-qubit parallelized SWAP test, some errors may occur, which will also have a certain impact on the Rains enumerators. We do not intend to discuss this issue in this work.

Conclusions.
—

We have built the connection between quantum weight enumerators and the
n
-qubit parallelized SWAP test. We have shown that the shadow enumerators correspond to the probabilities of measuring bitstrings on the
n
control qubits of the
n
-qubit parallelized SWAP test. Additionally, we have shown that both the Shor-Laflamme enumerators and the Rains unitary enumerators can be expressed as functions of these probabilities. Utilizing these functions, we have also introduced a technique to determine the distances of QECCs and the
k
-uniformity of pure states.
It is interesting to compute the quantum weight enumerators and the distance of quantum error-correcting codes by using our method in experiment.

\displaystyle\leq 2||{\cal Q}^{\prime}_{T}-{\cal Q}_{T}||_{1}\qquad

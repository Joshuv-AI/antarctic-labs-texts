Effective multiple equidistribution of translated measures
Michael Björklund, Alexander Gorodnik

Abstract
. We study the joint distributions of translated measures supported on leaves which are expanded by subgroups of diagonal matrices and generalize previous results of Kleinbock–Margulis, Dabbs–Kelly–Li, and Shi. More specifically, we establish quantitative estimates on higher-order correlations for measures with low regularities and derive error terms which only depend on the distances between translations.

1.
Introduction

Let us consider a jointly continuous action of a topological group
G
on a topological space
X
. Given a finite Borel measure
\sigma
on
X
, one is often interested in the asymptotic behaviour of integrals of the form

\sigma(\phi\circ g):=\int_{X}\phi(gx)\,d\sigma(x),\quad\hbox{with $\phi\in C_{b}(X)$,}

as
g\to\infty
in
G
, as well as in the asymptotic behaviour of integrals of the form

\sigma\big(\phi_{1}\circ g_{1}\cdots\phi_{r}\circ g_{r}\big):=\int_{X}\phi_{1}(g_{1}x)\cdots\phi_{r}(g_{r}x)\,d\sigma(x),\quad\hbox{with $\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r}\in C_{b}(X)$,}

as
g_{i}\to\infty
and
g_{i}g_{j}^{-1}\to\infty
for
i\neq j
in
G
. The latter integrals are often called
r
-correlations
. In this paper we will be interested in quantitative estimates on
r
-correlations for actions on homogeneous spaces.

Basic models for the type of results that we are after can be found in
[
9
,
11
]
which deals with equidistribution of closed horocycles on the modular surface.
More generally, one can consider the action of a partially hyperbolic
one-parameter subgroup
(g_{t})
in
\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
on the homogeneous space
X:=\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
, equipped with the (unique)
\operatorname{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant probability invariant measure
\mu
. Kleinbock and Margulis
[
6
]
showed that in this case there exists
\delta>0
such that for every compactly supported smooth probability measure
\sigma
on an unstable leaf of
(g_{t})
and for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
, there is a constant
C(\sigma,\phi)
, such that

\big|\sigma(\phi\circ g_{t})-\mu(\phi)\big|\leq C(\sigma,\phi)\,e^{-\delta t}

(1.1)

for all
t\geq 0
. In fact, the result of
[
6
]
applies more generally to partially hyperbolic flows
on homogeneous spaces of semisimple Lie groups. Subsequently, Dolgopyat
[
4
]
developed an inductive argument which allows one to deduce from estimates of the form (
1.1
),
quantitative estimates on higher-order correlations. In the above setting described above, this argument tells us that there exists
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that
for every
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\geq 0

\big|\sigma\big(\phi_{1}\circ g_{t_{1}}\cdots\phi_{r}\circ g_{t_{r}}\big)-\mu(\phi_{1})\cdots\mu(\phi_{r})\big|\leq C(\sigma,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r})\,e^{-\delta^{\prime}D_{r}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}

for all
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
,
where

D_{r}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}):=\min(t_{1},t_{2}-t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}-t_{r-1}).

The idea behind this argument in
[
6
]
goes back to Margulis’ thesis
[
8
]
,
and uses that the flow
(g_{t})
is non-expanding transversally to the unstable leaves,
so that one can ”thicken” the measure
\sigma
to reduce the original problem to mixing estimates for the flow
(g_{t})
with respect to the volume measure
\mu
.

The problem becomes significantly harder when the measure
\sigma
is supported on a proper submanifold of the unstable leaf. A particular instance of this problem was investigated by Kleinbock and Margulis in
[
7
]
. They consider the case when
\sigma
is a smooth measure, compactly supported on an orbit of the subgroup

U_{m,n}:=\left\{\left(\begin{tabular}[]{cc}$I_{m}$&$u$\\
$0$&$I_{n}$\end{tabular}\right):\,u\in\hbox{Mat}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{R})\right\},

(1.2)

which is translated by the multi-parameter flow

g_{\textbf{t}}:=\hbox{diag}\big(e^{t_{1}},\ldots,e^{t_{m}},e^{-t_{m+1}},\ldots,e^{-t_{m+n}}\big)

with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}=\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}t_{j}
.
In this case, the orbits of
U_{m,n}
are unstable manifolds of
g_{\textbf{t}}
only when
t_{1}=\cdots=t_{m}\to+\infty
and
t_{m+1}=\cdots=t_{m+n}\to+\infty
, but not in general.
The main result in
[
7
]
states that there exists

\delta>0
, which is independent of
\sigma
, such that

\big|\sigma(\phi\circ g_{\textbf{t}})-\mu(\phi)\big|\leq C(\sigma,\phi)\,e^{-\delta\lfloor\textbf{t}\rfloor}

(1.3)

for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
,
where

\lfloor\textbf{t}\rfloor:=\min(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m+n}).

This result was generalized to homogeneous spaces of semisimple groups by Dabbs, Kelly, Li
[
3
]
and by Shi
[
10
]
. Quantitative estimates on higher order correlations were also established in
[
10
]
: for every integer
r\geq 1
, there exists
\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}(r)>0
such that

\big|\sigma\big(\phi_{1}\circ g_{\textbf{t}_{1}}\cdots\phi_{r}\circ g_{\textbf{t}_{r}}\big)-\mu(\phi_{1})\cdots\mu(\phi_{r})\big|\leq C(\sigma,\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r})\,e^{-\delta^{\prime}D_{r}(\textbf{t}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{t}_{r})}

(1.4)

for all
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
,
where

D_{r}(\textbf{t}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{t}_{r}):=\min\big(\lfloor\textbf{t}_{1}\rfloor,\lfloor\textbf{t}_{2}-\textbf{t}_{1}\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor\textbf{t}_{r}-\textbf{t}_{r-1}\rfloor\big).

We stress that this estimate only provides non-trivial information when the vectors
\textbf{t}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{t}_{r}
are
completely ordered
with respect to the function
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
and
all
gaps with respect to this order go to infinity.
This condition is too restrictive for some of the applications that we have in mind.

In this paper, we generalize this result in two ways.
Firstly, we show that one can reduce the regularity assumptions on the measure
\sigma
. Secondly, we establish a favourable estimate which only depend on
\lfloor\textbf{t}_{1}\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor\textbf{t}_{1}\rfloor
and the pairwise Euclidean distances
\|\textbf{t}_{i}-\textbf{t}_{j}\|

for
i\neq j
.

To formulate our first main result, we need some notation.
Let
Y
be a compact orbit of
U_{m,n}
in
X
. Then
Y
can be considered as a
mn
-dimensional torus, and we denote by
m_{Y}
the probability invariant measure on
Y
.
We also write
\widehat{Y}
for the character group of
Y
. Given a Borel measure
\sigma
on
Y
, we write
\hat{\sigma}(\chi):=\int_{Y}\chi\,d\sigma
,
\chi\in\widehat{Y}
, for the corresponding Fourier coefficients.
We say that
\sigma
is a
Wiener measure
if

\|\sigma\|_{W}:={\sum}_{\chi\in\widehat{Y}}|\hat{\sigma}(\chi)|<\infty.

Note that every Wiener measure on
Y
is absolutely continuous with respect to
m_{Y}
, with a continuous (but possibly nowhere differentiable) density.
We denote by
S_{d}
the family of norms introduced in Section
3.2
below.
In particular, one can take them to be the
C^{k}
-norm on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(X)
for a fixed sufficiently large
k
.

{theorem}

For every
r\geq 1
, there exist
d_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
and
C_{r},\delta_{r}>0
such that
for every Wiener probability measure
\sigma
supported on a compact orbit of
U_{m,n}
in
X
,
\textbf{t}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{t}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+n}
, and
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X)
,

\big|\sigma\big(\phi_{1}\circ g_{\textbf{t}_{1}}\cdots\phi_{r}\circ g_{\textbf{t}_{r}}\big)-\mu(\phi_{1})\cdots\mu(\phi_{r})\big|\leq C_{r}\,\|\sigma\|_{W}S_{d_{r}}(\phi_{1})\cdots S_{d_{r}}(\phi_{r})\,e^{-\delta_{r}\Delta_{r}(\textbf{t}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{t}_{r})},

where

\Delta_{r}(\textbf{t}_{1},\ldots,\textbf{t}_{r}):=\min\big(\lfloor\textbf{t}_{i}\rfloor,\|\textbf{t}_{i}-\textbf{t}_{j}\|:\,1\leq i\neq j\leq r\big).

Let us now formulate a more general version of this result.
Let
G
be a connected semisimple Lie group without compact factors
and
P
a parabolic subgroup of
G
such that the projection of
P
to every simple factor of
G
is proper. Let
U
be the unipotent radical of
P
and
A
a maximal connected
Ad
-diagonalizable subgroup of
P
. We write
\mathfrak{a}
and

\mathfrak{u}
for the corresponding Lie algebras and
consider the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{a}
on
\mathfrak{u}
. Fix a norm on
\mathfrak{a}
and let
d
denote the corresponding invariant distance function on
A
.
Let
\Phi(\mathfrak{u})
denote the set of roots (characters of
\mathfrak{a}
)
arising in this action. Let
\mathfrak{a}_{U}^{*,+}
be the positive cone
in the dual
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
spanned by the characters in
\Phi(\mathfrak{u})
.
We use the usual identification
\mathfrak{a}^{*}\to\mathfrak{a}
defined by the Killing form:
\alpha\mapsto s_{\alpha}
given by
\left<s_{\alpha},a\right>=\alpha(a)
for
a\in\mathfrak{a}
, and denote by
\mathfrak{a}_{U}^{+}
the corresponding cone in
\mathfrak{a}
given by this identification. The cone

A_{U}^{+}:=\exp(\mathfrak{a}_{U}^{+})

was introduced in
[
10
]
and is called the expanding cone for
U
.
For
a\in A_{U}^{+}
, we denote by
\lfloor a\rfloor_{U}
the distance from
a
to the boundary of the cone
A_{U}^{+}
.
Let
\Gamma
be an irreducible lattice in
G
and
X:=G/\Gamma

equipped with the invariant probability measure
\mu
.

The main result of
[
10
]
is a generalization of the estimate (
1.4
) to compactly supported smooth measures on an orbit of
U
in
X
.
Let us additionally assume that
U
is abelian and
Y
is a compact orbit of
U
in
X
. Given a Borel measure
\sigma
on
U
, we define the Wiener norm as above. In this setting, we establish the following general version of Theorem
1
:

{theorem}

For every
r\geq 1
, there exist
d_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
and
C_{r},\delta_{r}>0
such that for
every Wiener probability measure
\sigma
supported on
Y
,

t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in A_{U}^{+}
, and
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{r}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X)
,

\big|\sigma\big(\phi_{1}\circ t_{1}\cdots\phi_{r}\circ t_{r}\big)-\mu(\phi_{1})\cdots\mu(\phi_{r})\big|\leq C_{r}\,\|\sigma\|_{W}S_{d_{r}}(\phi_{1})\cdots S_{d_{r}}(\phi_{r})\,e^{-\delta_{r}\Delta_{r}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})},

where

\Delta_{r}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}):=\min\big(\lfloor t_{i}\rfloor_{U},\,d(t_{i},t_{j}):\,1\leq i\neq j\leq r\big).

As we clarify below, our method does not rely on particular properties of the horospherical subgroup
U
and allows us to produce quantitative estimates on
\sigma(\phi\circ t_{1}\cdots\phi\circ t_{r})
once estimates on
\sigma(\phi\circ t)
are available (see Theorem
2
).

2.
General result

Let
G
be a real Lie group,
U
a closed connected subgroup of
G
, and
T
a closed connected abelian subgroup of
G
that normalizes
U
such that the adjoint action of
T
on the Lie algebra of
U
is proper and diagonalizable.
Let
X
be a standard Borel space equipped with measurable action of
G
.
Let
\mu
be a
G
-invariant probability measure on
X
, and let
\nu
be a
U
-invariant and
U
-ergodic measure on
X
. We assume that the measure
\nu
has discrete spectrum. This means that there is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\nu)
consisting of
U
-eigenfunctions, and allows us to introduce a Wiener norm on
L^{\infty}(\nu)
(see Section
3.1
below).

We shall further assume that certain equidistribution properties hold for
\nu,T
and a sub-algebra
\mathcal{A}
of bounded functions on
X
, which is equipped with a family of norms
S_{d}
(see Section
3.2
below).
For example,
\mathcal{A}
could be
\mathbb{C}+C_{c}^{\infty}(X)
and
S_{d}
could be the

C^{k}
-norms for a fixed sufficiently large
k
.

Finally, we make the following two assumptions regarding quantitative equidistribution on
X
in terms of a fixed suitable norm
S_{d_{o}}
:

•

There exist
T_{+}\subset T
,
D_{o}\geq 1
,
\delta_{o}\in(0,1)
,
and a function

\rho:T_{+}\rightarrow[1,\infty)

such that

\big|\nu(\varphi\circ t)-\mu(\varphi)\big|\leq D_{o}\,\rho(t)^{-\delta_{o}}\,S_{d_{o}}(\varphi),

(EQ1)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}
and
t\in T_{+}
.

•

There exist
C\geq 1
, and
0<c<1/2
such that

\big|\mu(\varphi_{1}\circ\exp(w)\cdot{\varphi}_{2})-\mu(\varphi_{1}){\mu(\varphi_{2})}\,\big|\leq C\max\big(1,\|w\|\big)^{-c}\,S_{d_{o}}(\varphi_{1})\,S_{d_{o}}(\varphi_{2})

(EQ2)

for all
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
and
w\in\hbox{Lie}(U)
.

We fix an invariant metric
d
on
T
.
Given
r\geq 2
and
\underline{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\in T_{+}^{r}
, we define

\rho_{r}(\underline{t}):=\min\big(\rho(t_{1}),\ldots,\rho(t_{r})\big)\quad\textrm{and}\quad m_{r}(\underline{t}):=\min_{i\neq j}\exp(d(t_{i},t_{j})),

(2.1)

and for
r\geq 1
, we set

\Delta_{r}(\underline{t}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\rho(t_{1})&\textrm{if $r=1$}\\
\min\big(\rho_{r}(\underline{t}),m_{r}(\underline{t})\big)&\textrm{if $r\geq 2$}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

Later on in our argument, we shall slightly modify the definition of
m_{r}
(see (
3.8
) below).

Our main theorem provides quantitative estimates on
r
-correlations in terms of
\Delta_{r}
:

{theorem}

For every
r\geq 1
, there exist

d_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
and
D_{r},\delta_{r}>0
such that

\Big|\nu\Big(\varphi_{o}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ t_{i}\Big)-\nu(\varphi_{o})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mu(\varphi_{i})\Big|\leq D_{r}\Delta_{r}(\underline{t})^{-\delta_{r}}\|\varphi_{o}\|_{W(\nu)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}S_{d_{r}}(\varphi_{i}),

for all
\underline{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\in T_{+}^{r}
,
\varphi_{o}\in W(\nu)
, and
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in\mathcal{A}
.

We note that Theorem
1
follows immediately from Theorem
2
.
Indeed, every Wiener measure on a torus has a continous density,
and the assumptions (
EQ1
) and (
EQ2
) can been verified
in this setting.
In particular, (
EQ1
) was established in
[
10
]
, and (
EQ2
) is the well-known exponential mixing estimate (see, for instance,
[
6
, 2.4.3]
).

In Section
8
below, we also work out the parameters
d_{r},D_{r},\delta_{r}
in Theorem
2
explicitly.

3.
Notations

3.1.
The Wiener algebra

We recall that the measure
\nu
is assumed to have discrete spectrum with respect to
U
, that is,
L^{2}(\nu)
has an orthonormal basis consisting of
U
-eigenfunctions: there exists a set
\Xi
of unitary characters on
\hbox{Lie}(U)
and an orthonormal basis
\{\psi_{\xi}\,:\,\xi\in\Xi\}
of
L^{2}(\nu)
such that

\psi_{\xi}\circ\exp(w)=\xi(w)\psi_{\xi},\quad\textrm{for all $\xi\in\Xi$ and $w\in\hbox{Lie}(U)$},

(3.1)

where the identity is interpreted in the
L^{2}(\nu)
-sense. Without loss of generality,
\psi_{1}=1
. Furthermore,
for every
\xi\in\Xi
,

|\psi_{\xi}|=1,\quad\textrm{$\nu$-almost everywhere},

(3.2)

by the
U
-ergodicity of
\nu
.

We denote by
B(X)
the
*
-algebra of bounded complex-valued measurable functions on
X
under pointwise multiplication.
The supremum norm on
B(X)
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
For
\varphi\in B(X)
, we set

\|\varphi\|_{W(\nu)}:=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}|\nu(\varphi\cdot\overline{\psi}_{\xi})|,

(3.3)

where
\big\{\psi_{\xi}:\xi\in\Xi\big\}
is a fixed orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\nu)
.

We define the
Wiener algebra

W(\nu):=\Big\{\varphi\in B(X)\,:\,\|\varphi\|_{W(\nu)}<\infty\Big\}.

For
\varphi\in W(\nu)
,

\varphi=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi}\nu(\varphi\cdot\overline{\psi}_{\xi})\psi_{\xi},

(3.4)

with convergence in the
L^{\infty}(\nu)
-sense, In particular,

\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\nu)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{W(\nu)},\quad\textrm{for all $\varphi\in W(\nu)$}.

(3.5)

3.2.
A family of norms on the space
X

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
G
-invariant sub-
*
-algebra of the algebra
B(X)
. We assume that
\mathcal{A}
is equipped with an increasing family of norms

S_{1}\leq S_{2}\leq\cdots\leq S_{d}\leq\cdots

satisfying the following assumptions for fixed
d_{o}
and for all
d
:

•

For all
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}
,

\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq S_{d_{o}}(\varphi).

(S1)

•

There exist
A\geq 1
and
a>0
such that

\big\|\varphi\circ\exp(w)-\varphi\big\|_{\infty}\leq A\,\|w\|^{a}\,S_{d_{o}}(\varphi)\quad\hbox{for all $\varphi\in\mathcal{A}$ and $w\in\hbox{Lie}(U)$.}

(S2)

•

There exist
B_{d}\geq 1
and
b_{d}>\max(1/2,a/4)
such that

S_{d}\big(\varphi\circ\exp(w)\big)\leq B_{d}\,\max\big(1,\|w\|\big)^{b_{d}}\,S_{d}(\varphi)\quad\hbox{for all $\varphi\in\mathcal{A}$ and $w\in\hbox{Lie}(U)$.}

(S3)

•

There exists
M_{d}\geq 1
such that

S_{d}(\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2})\leq M_{d}\,S_{d+d_{o}}(\varphi_{1})S_{d+d_{o}}(\varphi_{2}),\quad\textrm{for all $\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{A}$}.

(S4)

Since
\mathcal{A}
is a
G
-invariant subalgebra, for

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in\mathcal{A}
and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in G
, we also have
that the product
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ g_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
.
We further assume that the norms
S_{d}
are convex in the following strong sense:

•

For every
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in\mathcal{A}
,
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in G
,
and compactly supported complex finite measures
\omega
on
G^{r}
,

\phi:=\int_{G^{r}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ g_{i}\right)\,d\omega(g_{1},\ldots,g_{r})\in\mathcal{A},

(S5)

and

S_{d}(\phi)\leq\int_{G^{r}}S_{d}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ g_{i}\right)\,d\omega(g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}).

(S6)

We now provide several examples of norms for which our set-up applies
when

is a finite-volume homogeneous space of a Lie group

(ii)

with respect to invariant Riemannian metric

The third example is the Sobolev norms uses in

4.
Proof of the main theorems

We fix
r\geq 1
,
\varphi_{o}\in W(\nu)
,
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in\mathcal{A}
and

\underline{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\in T^{r}_{+}

We wish to estimate the expression

4.1.
Step I: expanding the function
\varphi_{o}

By (

where the series converges uniformly, and with the convention that

\displaystyle\nu\Big(\varphi_{o}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ t_{i}\Big)-\nu(\varphi_{o})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mu(\varphi_{i})=\,

\displaystyle\nu(\varphi_{o})\Big(\nu\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ t_{i}\Big)-\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mu(\varphi_{i})\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\xi\neq 1}\nu(\varphi_{o}\cdot\overline{\psi}_{\xi})\,\nu_{\xi}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ t_{i}\Big),

(4.1)

where we used the notation

\nu_{\xi}(\eta):=\nu(\psi_{\xi}\cdot\eta),\quad\textrm{for $\eta\in B(X)$}.

(4.2)

Let us now define

D_{d,r}(\underline{t};1):=\sup\left\{\Big|\nu\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ t_{i}\Big)-\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mu(\varphi_{i})\Big|\,:\,\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in\mathcal{A},\enskip S_{d}(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,S_{d}(\varphi_{r})\leq 1\right\},

(4.3)

and, for

D_{d,r}(\underline{t};\xi):=\sup\left\{\Big|\nu_{\xi}\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ t_{i}\Big)\Big|\,:\,\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in\mathcal{A},\enskip S_{d}(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,S_{d}(\varphi_{r})\leq 1\right\}.

(4.4)

Finally, we set

(4.5)

Then it follows from (

\Big|\nu\Big(\varphi_{o}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ t_{i}\Big)-\nu(\varphi_{o})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mu(\varphi_{i})\Big|\leq E_{d,r}(\underline{t})\,\|\varphi_{o}\|_{W(\nu)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}S_{d}(\varphi_{i}).

(4.6)

Our goal from now on will be to estimate the quantity
E_{d,r}(\underline{t})
.
This will be established through an elaborate induction scheme, so it will be convenient to define

\widetilde{E}_{d,r-1}(\underline{t}):=\max\Big\{E_{d,p}(t_{i_{1}},\ldots,t_{i_{p}})\,:\,1\leq p<r,\enskip\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,r\}\Big\}.

(4.7)

4.2.
Step II: An upper bound on

(base of induction)

In this section, we prove the Theorem

The assumption (

D_{d_{o},1}(t;1)\leq D_{o}\,\rho(t)^{-\delta_{o}},\quad\textrm{for all $t\in T_{+}$}.

In view of (

To do this we shall exploit the following

{lemma}

For all
\xi\in\Xi

\exp(w)_{*}\nu_{\xi}=\xi(-w)\nu_{\xi}.

Proof.

Indeed, it follows from (

\displaystyle\exp(w)_{*}\nu_{\xi}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\nu_{\xi}(\varphi\circ\exp(w))=\nu(\psi_{\xi}\cdot\varphi\circ\exp(w))=\nu(\psi_{\xi}\circ\exp(-w)\cdot\psi)

\displaystyle=\xi(-w)\nu(\psi_{\xi}\cdot\varphi)=\xi(-w)\nu_{\xi}(\varphi).

∎

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}
,

\xi\neq 1
.
Using Lemma
4.2
, we conclude that for every

\displaystyle\nu_{\xi}(\varphi\circ t)

\displaystyle=\xi(sw)\nu_{\xi}(\varphi\circ t\circ\exp(sw))=\xi(sw)\nu_{\xi}(\varphi\circ\exp(s\Ad(t)w)\circ t)

\displaystyle=\xi(sw)\nu_{\xi}\big(\varphi\circ\exp(s\Ad(t)w)\circ t-\mu(\phi)\big),

Using that

\displaystyle|\nu_{\xi}(\phi_{L}\circ t)|

\displaystyle\leq\nu(|\phi_{L}|\circ t)\leq\nu(|\phi_{L}|^{2}\circ t)^{1/2}

The required bounds on the two terms in (

{lemma}

Let

\displaystyle\big|\nu(|\phi_{L}|^{2}\circ t)-\mu(|\phi_{L}|^{2})\big|

\displaystyle\big|\nu_{\xi}(\varphi\circ t)|=\big|\nu_{\xi}(\phi_{L}\circ t)|\leq\Big(4\sqrt{C}\big(L\|\Ad(t)w\|\big)^{-c/2}+\sqrt{D_{o}B_{d_{o}}^{\prime}}\big(L\|\Ad(t)w\|\big)^{b_{2d_{o}}}\rho(t)^{-\delta_{o}/2}\Big)S_{2d_{o}}(\varphi),

{lemma}

\beta_{p+1}<\beta_{1}\,\theta^{(q+1)/r}<\beta_{1}\,\theta^{q/r}\leq\beta_{p}.

\beta_{r}\leq\beta_{1}\,\theta=\gamma_{r}<\cdots<\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{0}=\beta_{1}.

1\leq p\leq r-1

\beta_{p+1}<\gamma_{q+1}<\gamma_{q}\leq\beta_{p},

8.
Explicit version of the main theorem

Let us now present a version of Theorem
2
with explicit parameters.
Here we additionally assume that the estimates (
S3
) and (
S4
) hold
for

B_{d}=L_{1}^{d}
and
b_{d}=\ell d
with
L_{1},\ell\geq 1

and

M_{d}=L_{2}^{d}
with
L_{2}\geq 1

respectively.
Such bounds can be verified for the Sobolev norms (see Section
3.2
). Then we prove:

{theorem}

There exist
H_{1},H_{2}>0
and
\lambda>1

such that
for all
r\geq 1
,
\varphi_{o}\in W(\nu)
,
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\underline{t}\in T_{+}^{r}
satisfying
\Delta_{r}(\underline{t})>H_{2}^{(r-1)!r!(r+1)!\,\lambda^{r}}
,

\Big|\nu\Big(\varphi_{o}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\varphi_{i}\circ t_{i}\Big)-\nu(\varphi_{o})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mu(\varphi_{i})\Big|\leq rH_{1}\,\Delta_{r}(\underline{t})^{-\frac{1}{(r!)^{2}(r+1)!\,\lambda^{r}}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{r}S_{(r+1)d_{o}}(\varphi_{i}),

Proof.

We recall from the proof of Theorem
8
that the parameters
\delta_{r}
,
D_{r}
, and
\delta_{r}
are determined by the following relations:

\displaystyle d_{r}

\displaystyle=d_{r-1}+d_{o}\quad\hbox{with $d_{1}=2d_{o}$},

\displaystyle D_{r}

\displaystyle=2P_{1}P_{d_{r-1}}^{rb_{d_{r-1}+d_{o}}}D_{r-1}^{1/2}+rQ,\quad\hbox{where $P_{d}:=\big(M_{d}B_{d+d_{o}}^{2}+2B_{d}^{2}\big)^{1/2b_{d+d_{o}}}$,}

\displaystyle\delta_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}\delta_{r-1}}{r(\frac{2c_{1}}{r}+2rb_{d_{r-1}+d_{o}})}.

It is clear from the recursive formulas that

d_{r}=(r+1)d_{o}

and

\delta_{r}=\frac{c_{1}\delta_{r-1}}{r(\frac{2c_{1}}{r}+2\ell r(r+1))}\geq\frac{1}{(r!)^{2}(r+1)!\,\lambda^{r}}

for an explicit
\lambda>1
, which depends only on
a
,
b
,
c
, and
\ell
.
However, with this choice,
D_{r}
grows super-exponentially fast in
r
. We modify the proof of Theorem
2
choosing
\theta
differently to get rid of the
P_{d_{r-1}}^{rb_{d_{r-1}}}
-factor. This will then imply that the constant grows linearly. Unfortunately, this type of argument only applies to those
\underline{t}\in T_{+}^{r}
such that
\Delta_{r}(\underline{t})
is sufficiently large depending on
r
.

We assume that the inductive assumption (
4.32
) holds and choose

\theta:=P_{d_{r-1}}\Delta_{r}(\underline{t})^{-\varepsilon_{r}},

where
\varepsilon_{r}
is given by (
4.33
), and
P_{d_{r-1}}
is defined in Proposition

4.4
. We note that

P_{d_{r-1}}=\big(M_{rd_{o}}B_{(r+1)d_{o}}^{2}+2B_{rd_{o}}^{2}\big)^{1/(2(r+1)d_{o})}=\big(L_{2}^{rd_{o}}L_{1}^{2(r+1)d_{o}}+2L_{1}^{2rd_{o}}\big)^{1/(2(r+1)d_{o})}\leq L_{1}(L_{2}+2).

Since
P_{d_{r-1}}\geq 1
, we have
M_{r}(\underline{t})^{-1}\leq\theta
, and we note that

\theta<1
provided that

\Delta_{r}(\underline{t})>P_{d_{r-1}}^{1/\varepsilon_{r}}.

(8.1)

Hence, if
\underline{t}
satisfies (
8.1
), then, just as in the previous proof, it follows from (
4.28
) combined with (
4.32
) that

E_{d+d_{o},r}(\underline{t})\leq D_{r}\Delta_{r}(\underline{t})^{-\delta_{r}},

where

D_{r}:=2P_{1}D_{r-1}^{1/2}+rQ_{r}\quad\hbox{with $Q_{r}:=QP_{d_{r-1}}^{c_{1}/r}$, }

(8.2)

and
\delta_{r}
as above.
Then

{1}/{\varepsilon_{r}}=\frac{\frac{2c_{1}}{r}+2rb_{d_{r-1}+d_{o}}}{\delta_{r-1}}\leq\left(\frac{2c_{1}}{r}+2\ell r(r+1)\right)((r-1)!)^{2}r!\lambda^{r-1}\leq\gamma\,(r-1)!r!(r+1)!\lambda^{r},

r

D_{r}\leq H_{1}r

for all

This finishes the proof.
∎

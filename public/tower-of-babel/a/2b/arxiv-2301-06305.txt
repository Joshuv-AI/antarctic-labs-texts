1.
Introduction

For a Borel subset
B
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C},
let
M_{0}(B)
denote the set of finite complex-valued Borel measures that are compactly supported in
B
and let
M_{0}^{+}(B)
be the set of positive measures in
M_{0}(B).
The support of
\nu\in M_{0}(\mathbb{C}),

\text{spt}(\nu),
is the smallest closed set that has full
|\nu|
measure. For a Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{C},

\nu_{A}
denotes
\nu
restricted to
A.

For a compact subset
K\subset\mathbb{C}
and
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K),
the functions in

\mbox{Rat}(K):=\{q:\mbox{$q$ is a rational function
with poles off $K$}\}

are members of
L^{\infty}(\mu)
. We let
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
be the weak-star closure of
\mbox{Rat}(K)
in
L^{\infty}(\mu).

C(K)
denotes the space of all continuous functions on
K
while
R(K)
denotes the uniform closure in
C(K)
of
\mbox{Rat}(K).
Let
R(K)^{\perp}
be the subset of measures
\nu\in M_{0}(K)
such that
\int fd\nu=0
for each
f\in R(K).
Then
R(K)^{\perp}\cap L^{1}(\mu)
is the space of weak-star continuous annihilators of
R^{\infty}(K,\mu).

There exists a Borel partition
\{\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1}\}
of
K
such that

\displaystyle\ R^{\infty}(K,\mu)=L^{\infty}(\mu|_{\Delta_{0}})\oplus R^{\infty}(K,\mu|_{\Delta_{1}})

(1.1)

where
R^{\infty}(K,\mu|_{\Delta_{1}})
contains no nontrivial
L^{\infty}
summands (see
[
3
, Proposition 1.16 on page 281]
).
Call
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
pure if
\Delta_{0}=\emptyset
in (
1.1
). Because of (
1.1
), we shall assume that
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
is pure in this paper.

The
envelope

E
with respect to
K
and
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K)
is the set of points
\lambda\in K
such that there exists
\mu_{\lambda}\in M_{0}(K)
that is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
such that
\mu_{\lambda}(\{\lambda\})=0
and
\int f(z)d\mu_{\lambda}(z)=f(\lambda)
for each
f\in R(K).
For
\lambda\in E
and
f\in R^{\infty}(K,,\mu),
set
\rho(f)(\lambda)=\int fd\mu_{\lambda}.
Clearly
\rho(f)(\lambda)
is independent of the particular
\mu_{\lambda}
chosen. We thus have a map
\rho
called Chaumat’s map for
K
and
\mu,
which associates
to each function in
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
a point function on
E.
Chaumat’s Theorem
[
2
]
states the following: The map
\rho
is an isometric isomorphism and a weak-star homeomorphism from
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
onto
R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}),
where
{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}
is the area measure (Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
)
{\mathfrak{m}}
restricted to
E
(also see
[
3
, Chaumat’s Theorem on page 288]
) and
\overline{B}
denotes the closure of the set
B\subset\mathbb{C}
.

Our main theorem below proves the conjecture
\diamond
posed by J. Dudziak in
[
6
, on page 386]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be a compact subset and
\mu\in M_{0}^{+}(K).
Suppose that
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
is pure,
E
is the envelope for
K
and
\mu,
and
\rho
is the Chaumat’s map for
K
and
\mu.
If
f\in R^{\infty}(K,\mu),
then
f
is invertible in
R^{\infty}(K,\mu)
if and only if there exists
\epsilon_{f}>0
such that

\ |\rho(f)(z)|\geq\epsilon_{f},~{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}-a.a..

For
\mathcal{H}
a complex separable Hilbert space,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the space of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}.
The spectrum and essential spectrum of an operator
A\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
are denoted
\sigma(A)
and
\sigma_{e}(A)
, respectively. An operator
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is called
subnormal
if there exists a complex separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
containing
\mathcal{H}
and a normal operator
N\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{K})
such that
N\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{H}
and
S=N|_{\mathcal{H}}.
Such an
N
is called a
minimal normal extension
(mne) of

if the smallest closed subspace of
\mathcal{K}
containing
\mathcal{H}
and reducing
N
is
\mathcal{K}
itself. Any two mnes of

are unitarily equivalent in a manner that fixes
S.

The spectrum
\sigma(S)
is the union of
\sigma(N)
and some collection of bounded components of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(N).
The book
[
3
]
is a good reference for basic information for subnormal operators.

For a subnormal operator

with

let
\mu
be the scalar-valued spectral measure (svsm) for

Since
\text{spt}(\mu)=\sigma(N)\subset\sigma(S),
we see that
\text{Rat}(\sigma(S))\subset L^{\infty}(\mu).
Therefore, for

the normal operator
f(N)
is well defined and

The operator

defines a functional calculus for

We assume that

is pure. Let
E
be the envelope for
\sigma(S)
and
\mu
and let
\rho
be the Chaumat’s map from
R^{\infty}(\sigma(S),\mu)
to

Define

the closure of
\rho(f)(E).
We define
cl(f,\sigma_{e}(S)),
the cluster set of

on
\sigma_{e}(S),
to be the subset of
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that there exists a sequence
\{z_{n}\}\subset E
and a

and

Combining Theorem

with

.

If

define the analytic capacity of

\ \gamma(B)=\sup|f^{\prime}(\infty)|,

where the supremum is taken over all those functions

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\},

The analytic capacity of a general subset

\mathbb{C}
is given by:

\ \gamma(F)=\sup\{\gamma(B):B\subset F\text{ compact}\}.

\displaystyle\ {\mathfrak{m}}(F)\leq 4\pi\gamma(F)^{2}.

For

\ \mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}(\nu)(z)=\int_{|w-z|>\epsilon}\dfrac{1}{w-z}d\nu(w).

The (principal value) Cauchy transform

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{C}(\nu)(z)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}(\nu)(z)\

(2.2)

for all

If

\displaystyle\ \tilde{\nu}(\lambda):=\int\frac{d|\nu|}{|z-\lambda|}<\infty,

Throughout this paper, the Cauchy transform of a measure always means the principal value of the transform.
In the sense of distributions,

\displaystyle\ \bar{\partial}\mathcal{C}(\nu)=-\pi\nu.

(2.4)

The maximal Cauchy transform is defined by

\ \mathcal{C}_{*}(\nu)(z)=\sup_{\epsilon>0}|\mathcal{C}_{\epsilon}(\nu)(z)|.

A related capacity,
\gamma_{+},
is defined for subsets

\ \gamma_{+}(F)=\sup\|\mu\|,

3.
Modified Vitushkin scheme of Paramonov and some lemmas

If a compact subset
F
is contained in
\mathbb{D}(a,\delta)
and
f
is bounded and analytic on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus F
with
f(\infty)=0,
we consider the Laurent expansion of
f
for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{D}(a,\delta),

\ f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{c_{n}(f,a)}{(z-a)^{n}}.

As
c_{1}(f,a)
does not depend on the choice of
a
, we define

c_{1}(f)=c_{1}(f,a).
The coefficient
c_{2}(f,a)
does depend on
a
. However, if
c_{1}(f)=0
, then
c_{2}(f,a)
does not depend on
a
, and in this case, we define
c_{2}(f)=c_{2}(f,a).

Let
\varphi
be a smooth function with compact support. Vitushkin’s localization operator

T_{\varphi}
is defined by

\ (T_{\varphi}f)(\lambda)=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int\dfrac{f(z)-f(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\bar{\partial}\varphi(z)d{\mathfrak{m}}(z),

where
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{C})
. Clearly,
(T_{\varphi}f)(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\mathcal{C}(\varphi\bar{\partial}f{\mathfrak{m}})(z).

Consequently, in the sense of distributions,

\ \bar{\partial}(T_{\varphi}f)(z)=\varphi(z)\bar{\partial}f(z).

Therefore,
T_{\varphi}f
is analytic outside of
\text{supp}(\bar{\partial}f)\cap\text{supp}(\varphi).
If
\text{supp}(\varphi)\subset\mathbb{D}(a,\delta),
then

\displaystyle(T_{\varphi}f)(\infty)=0,~c_{1}(T_{\varphi}f)=-\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int f(z)\bar{\partial}\varphi(z)d{\mathfrak{m}}(z),

\displaystyle c_{2}(T_{\varphi}f,a)=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\int(z-a)f(z)\bar{\partial}\varphi(z)d{\mathfrak{m}}(z).

The following is the estimate of the norm of
T_{\varphi}f.

\displaystyle\ \|T_{\varphi}f\|_{\infty}\leq 4\|f\|_{\infty}\delta\|\bar{\partial}\varphi\|.

(3.1)

See
[
8
, VIII.7.1]
for the details of
T_{\varphi}.

Let
\delta>0
. We say that

\{\varphi_{ij},S_{ij},\delta\}
is a smooth partition of unity subordinated to
\{2S_{ij}\},

if the following assumptions hold:

(i)
S_{ij}
is a square with vertices
(i\delta,j\delta),~((i+1)\delta,j\delta),~(i\delta,(j+1)\delta),
and
((i+1)\delta,(j+1)\delta);

(ii)
c_{ij}
is the center of
S_{ij}
and
\varphi_{ij}
is a
C^{\infty}
smooth function supported in
\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},\delta)\subset 2S_{ij}
and with values in
[0,1]
;

(iii)

\ \|\bar{\partial}\varphi_{ij}\|\leq\frac{C_{13}}{\delta},~\sum\varphi_{ij}=1.

(See
[
8
, VIII.7]
for details).

Let
f\in C(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})
(or
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})
) with compact support. Define
f_{ij}=T_{\varphi_{ij}}f,
then
f_{ij}\neq 0
for only finite many
(i,j).
Clearly,

\ f(z)=\sum_{f_{ij}\neq 0}f_{ij}(z).

The standard Vitushkin approximation scheme requires us to construct functions
a_{ij}
such that
f_{ij}-a_{ij}
has triple zeros at
\infty
, which requires us to estimate both
c_{1}(a_{ij})
and
c_{2}(a_{ij},c_{ij})
(see
[
8
, section 7 on page 209]
).

The main idea of P. V. Paramonov
[
9
]
is that one does not actually need to estimate
each coefficient

. It suffices to estimate
the sum of coefficients
\sum_{j\in J_{il}}c_{2}(a_{ij},c_{ij})
for a special non-intersecting partition
\{J_{il}\}
of
J_{i}=\{j_{min}\leq j\leq j_{max}\},
where
j_{min}=\min\{j:~f_{ij}\neq 0\}
and
j_{max}=\max\{j:~f_{ij}\neq 0\}.

We now state the modified Vitushkin approximation scheme by P. V. Paramonov.
We fix a bounded Borel subset
F\subset\mathbb{C}.
Let

\displaystyle\ \alpha_{ij}=\gamma(\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},k_{1}\delta)\cap F),

(3.2)

where
k_{1}\geq 3
is a fixed integer.
Let

and
m_{2}=\max\{i,j,~\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},k_{1}\delta)\cap F\neq\emptyset\}.

Note both
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are finite.
Set
min_{i}=\min\{j:~\alpha_{ij}\neq 0\}
and
max_{i}=\max\{j:~\alpha_{ij}\neq 0\}
. Let
J_{i}=\{j:~min_{i}\leq j\leq max_{i}\}
. We will also fix a constant
C_{0}>1.

Definition 3.1
.

For
j_{1}+1\in J_{i},
let

\displaystyle\ J=\{j:~j_{1}+1\leq j\leq j_{1}+s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}\}.

(3.3)

Choose positive integers

s_{2}
, and
s_{3}

\displaystyle\ \delta\leq\sum_{j=j_{1}+1}^{j_{1}+s_{1}}\alpha_{ij}<\delta+k_{1}\delta,

(3.4)

\ 6k_{1}+4C_{0}<s_{2}\leq 6k_{1}+4C_{0}+1,

\displaystyle\ \delta\leq\sum_{j=j_{1}+s_{1}+s_{2}+1}^{j_{1}+s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}}\alpha_{ij}<\delta+k_{1}\delta

(3.5)

hold. With this choice, we say that

is a complete group.

We now present a detailed description of the procedure of partitioning

into

into (finitely many) non-intersecting groups

,

Starting from the lowest index

(going upwards and without jumps in

of indices in

Definition 3.2

(3.6)

The following lemma is straightforward.

4.
The algebra
H^{\infty}(\mathcal{D})

For a bounded measurable subset
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{C},
define

\ L^{\infty}({\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathcal{D}})=\{f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}):~f(z)=0,~z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{D}\}.

Definition 4.1
.

The subset
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
is
\gamma
-open
if
there exists a subset
\mathcal{Q}
with
{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathcal{Q})=0
such that for
\lambda\in\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle\ \lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)\setminus\mathcal{D})}{\delta}=0.

(4.1)

If
\gamma(\mathcal{Q})=0,
then
\mathcal{D}
is called

\gamma
-open
.

Clearly, by (
2.1
), if
\mathcal{D}
is strong

\mathcal{D}
is
\gamma
-open.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded measurable subset. Assume that
\mathcal{D}
is
\gamma
-open. Let

If

[

The aim of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3

\mathcal{D}

\gamma

C_{f}>0
(depending on

,
\delta>0
, a smooth non-negative function
\varphi

\left\|\frac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C_{25}}{\delta},

\displaystyle\ \left|\int(z-\lambda)^{n}f(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathcal{D}}(z)\right|\leq C_{f}\delta^{n}\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,k_{1}\delta)\setminus\mathcal{D}),

(4.2)

where

is a given integer and

\{f_{n}\},
where

5.

For a compact subset

Definition 5.1
.

The envelope

for

and
\mu
is the set of points
\lambda\in K
such that there exists
\mu_{\lambda}\in M_{0}(K)
that is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
such that

The elementary properties of

are listed below.

Proposition 5.2
.

(a)

is the set of weak-star continuous homomorphisms on

(see
[
3
, Proposition VI.2.5]

(b)

is a nonempty Borel set with area density one at each of its points (see
[
3
, Proposition VI.2.8]

(c)
\text{int}(E)=\text{int}(\overline{E})
(see
[
3
, Proposition VI.3.9]
).

(d)
\overline{E}
is the union of

[
3

For

\rho(f)(\lambda)=\int fd\mu_{\lambda}.
Clearly

[

]
states as the following: The map

(5.4)

6.
A criterion for functions in
R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E})

In this section, we prove the following criterion.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}({\mathfrak{m}}_{E})
be given with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathfrak{m}}_{E})}\leq 1
.
Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)
f\in R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}).

(2) There exists a constant
C_{f}>0
(only depending on
f
) and an integer
k_{1}\geq 2
so that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
\delta>0
, and for all choices of a smooth non-negative function
\varphi
with support in
\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)
satisfying
\varphi\leq 1
and
\left\|\frac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C_{45}}{\delta},
we have

\ \left|\int(z-\lambda)^{n}f(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}(z)\right|\leq C_{f}\delta^{n}\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,k_{1}\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R})

for all integers
n\geq 0.

(3)
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{R}).

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): For
f\in R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}),
we find
\{f_{n}\}\subset R(\overline{E})
such that
\|f_{n}\|\leq C_{f}
and
f_{n}(z)\rightarrow f(z),~{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}-a.a.
(see
[
3
, Proposition VI.22.3]
).
Using
[
8
, Theorem VIII.8.1]
, for a smooth
\varphi
with support in
\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta),
we get

\ \begin{aligned} \ &\left|\int f_{n}(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}(z)\right|\\
\ \leq&\left|\int_{\overline{E}}f_{n}(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}(z)\right|+\left|\int_{\overline{E}\setminus E}f_{n}(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}(z)\right|\\
\ \leq&C_{46}C_{f}\delta\left\|\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}\left(\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,k_{1}\delta)\setminus\overline{E})+\sqrt{{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,k_{1}\delta)\cap(\overline{E}\setminus E))}\right).\end{aligned}

From Corollary
5.9
, we get
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\mathcal{R}}={\mathfrak{m}}_{E}.
Using (
2.1
), we have

\ \sqrt{{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},k_{1}\delta)\cap(\overline{E}\setminus E))}\leq\sqrt{{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},k_{1}\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R})}\leq\sqrt{4\pi}\gamma(\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},k_{1}\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R}).

Hence, since
\mathcal{R}\subset\overline{E},

\ \left|\int f_{n}(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}(z)\right|\leq C_{46}C_{f}(1+\sqrt{4\pi})\delta\left\|\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}\gamma(\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},k_{1}\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R}).

Taking weak-star limits for
n\rightarrow\infty
and replacing
\varphi
by
(z-\lambda)^{n}\varphi,
we prove (2).

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3) follows from Theorem
4.3
since
\mathcal{R}
is
\gamma
-open from Corollary
5.7
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1): Let
f
be a bounded and analytic function outside of a compact subset
E_{f}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{R}
with
\|f\|\leq 1.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
\delta>0
, and a smooth function
\varphi
with support in
\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)
, we see that
\frac{T_{\varphi}f}{\|T_{\varphi}f\|}
is analytic off
\text{supp}(\varphi)\cap E_{f}.
Using (
3.1
), we have the following calculation.

\ \begin{aligned} \ |(T_{\varphi}f)^{\prime}(\infty)|=&\dfrac{1}{\pi}\left|\int f(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}(z)\right|\\
\ \leq&\|T_{\varphi}f\|\gamma(\text{supp}(\varphi)\cap E_{f})\\
\ \leq&C_{47}\delta\left\|\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R}).\ \end{aligned}

Since, by (
2.1
),

\ \begin{aligned} \ \left|\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathcal{R}}f(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}(z)\right|\leq&C_{48}\left\|\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R})\\
\ \leq&C_{49}\delta\left\|\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R}),\ \end{aligned}

we get

\ \left|\int f(z)\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}(z)\right|\leq C_{50}\delta\left\|\dfrac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}\gamma(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R}).

Therefore, the assumption of Theorem
4.3
holds
if we replace
\varphi
by
(z-\lambda)^{n}\varphi
above.
We now use the notation in the proof of Theorem
4.3
for
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{R}.
Applying Lemma
2.7
, we get
\eta_{ij}^{0}\in M_{0}^{+}(\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},k_{1}\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R})
for
\Lambda=\{g_{n}\},
a dense subset of
R(\overline{E})^{\perp}\cap L^{1}({\mathfrak{m}}_{E})
, such that
\|\eta_{ij}^{0}\|\geq\frac{1}{2C_{5}}\alpha_{ij},
where
\alpha_{ij}=\gamma(\mathbb{D}(c_{ij},k_{1}\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R}).
By (
2.6
),
\int{\mathcal{C}}(\eta_{ij}^{0})g_{n}d{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}=0
for
n\geq 1
since
{\mathcal{C}}(g_{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{E})(z)=0,~\eta_{ij}^{0}-a.a.
by Lemma
2.4
. Hence, using the Hahn-Banach Theorem, we see that
{\mathcal{C}}(\eta_{ij}^{0})\in R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}).
Set
\eta_{ij}=\frac{\eta_{ij}^{0}}{\|\eta_{ij}^{0}\|}\alpha_{ij}.
Then
\eta_{ij}
satisfies (
4.5
) and
f_{\delta}\in R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}),
where
f_{\delta}
is defined as in (
4.6
). Applying Lemma
4.4
(3), we see that
f\in R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}).
Thus,
H^{\infty}(\mathcal{R})\subset R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E})
since those functions

above are weak-star dense in

∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\eta\in M_{0}^{+}(\mathbb{C})
and
\|{\mathcal{C}}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})}\leq 1.
Suppose that

\displaystyle\ \eta(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta))\leq

\displaystyle C_{52}\left|\int{\mathcal{C}}\eta(z)\dfrac{\partial\phi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{52}\left|\int{\mathcal{C}}\eta(z)\dfrac{\partial\phi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}d{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}(z)\right|+C_{52}\left\|\dfrac{\partial\phi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,2\delta)\setminus\mathcal{R})

where

).

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,

, a smooth non-negative function
\varphi
with support in

and
\left\|\frac{\partial\varphi(z)}{\partial\bar{z}}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C_{45}}{\delta},

\ \begin{aligned} \ &\left|\int(z-\lambda)^{n}{\mathcal{C}}(w\eta)\bar{\partial}\varphi d{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}\right|\\
\ \leq&\left|\int(z-\lambda)^{n}{\mathcal{C}}(w\eta)\bar{\partial}\varphi d{\mathfrak{m}}\right|+\delta^{n}\|{\mathcal{C}}(w\eta)\|\|\bar{\partial}\varphi\|{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbb{D}(\lambda,\delta)\setminus E)\\

.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\eta\in M_{0}^{+}(\mathbb{C})
and
\|{\mathcal{C}}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})}\leq 1.
If
{\mathcal{C}}\eta\in R^{\infty}(\overline{E},{\mathfrak{m}}_{E}),

\eta(\mathcal{R})=0.

Suppose that there exists a compact subset

such that
\eta(B_{0})>0.

Then from Corollary
2.4
, we see that
\gamma(B_{0})>0.

By Proposition
2.2
(1)-(3), there exists
B_{1}\subset B_{0}
with
\eta(B_{1})>0
and
N_{2}(\eta_{B_{1}})<\infty.
Without loss of generality, we assume

Using Proposition
2.2
(4) and Theorem
2.1
(1), we have
\eta_{B_{1}}(B_{2})\leq C_{54}\gamma(B_{2})
for

Therefore, applying Lemma
2.6
, we may assume
{\mathcal{C}}_{*}(g_{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{E})\in L^{\infty}(\eta_{B_{1}}).

Using Proposition
2.2
(4), we find

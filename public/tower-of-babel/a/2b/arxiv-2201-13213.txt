Fermi surface segmentation in the helical state of a Rashba superconductor
Alireza Akbari 1,2 and Peter Thalmeier 1

Abstract
We investigate the quasiparticle excitations in the FFLO- type helical state of a superconductor with inversion-symmetry breaking and strong Rashba spin-orbit coupling. We restrict to a state with single finite momentum of Cooper pairs in the helical phase that is determined by minimization of the condensation energy. We derive the dependence of quasiparticle dispersions on the Rashba coupling strength and external field. It leads to a peculiar momentum-space segmentation of the corresponding Rashba Fermi surface sheets which has not yet been observed experimentally. We show that it may be directly visualized by the method of quasiparticle interference that identifies the critical points of the segmented sheets and can map their evolution with field strength, bias voltage and Rashba coupling. We also indicate a strategy how to determine the finite Cooper-pair momentum from experimental quantities. This investigation has the potential for a more detailed microscopic understanding of the helical superconducting state under the influence of Rashba spin-orbit coupling.



I
Introduction

In a superconductor (SC) with small orbital pair breaking a new state may become stable at larger fields where
the conduction electrons are not bound in BCS pairs
(-{\bf k}\uparrow,{\bf k}\downarrow)
but rather in pairs
(-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\uparrow,{\bf k}+{\bf q}\downarrow)
with finite center-of-mass momentum
2{\bf q}
characterized by a gap function
\Delta({\bf r})=\Delta_{\bf q}\exp(i{\bf q}{\bf r})
. This Fulde-Ferrell-Larkin-Ovchinnikov (FFLO) state
Fulde and Ferrell 1964
;
Larkin and Ovchinnikov 1964
is well studied theoretically, both in superconductors of various dimensionality
Shimahara 1994
;
Shimahara 1998
as well as in condensed quantum gases
Sheehy and Radzihovsky 2007
;
Sheehy 2015
.
Convincing evidence for the experimental realization of this state at low temperatures and high fields is, however, rather scarce which may be due to the sensitivity to impurities
Takada 1970
;
Matsuda and Shimahara 2007
;
Wang et al. 2007
and orbital pair breaking
Gruenberg and Gunther 1966
;
Adachi and Ikeda 2003
. There are unconventional heavy fermion superconductors
Matsuda and Shimahara 2007
and organic superconductors
Lortz et al. 2007
;
Mayaffre et al. 2014
as well as Fe-pnictides
Burger et al. 2013
;
Zocco et al. 2013

where its appearance has been suggested. The existence of the FFLO phase in these cases is mostly infered from thermodynamic anomalies
Bianchi et al. 2003
in the low-temperature high-field sector of the phase diagram or from NMR experiments
Kumagai et al. 2011
and they may be used to map out the FFLO phase boundaries.

However, such experiments do not address the microscopic nature of this state deep inside the FFLO-type phase. The latter is stabilized by a tradeoff between the loss of condensation energy due to the kinetic energy of pairs with center of mass (CM) momentum and gain in Zeeman energy due to population imbalance of spin states
Combescot 2007
;
Zwicknagl and Wosnitza 2011
. This tradeoff depends on the momentum position on the Fermi surface (FS) and therefore generally the latter is segmented into regions where the pairs are still stable with finite
2{\bf q}
(paired region) and where they are unstable (unpaired region).
The relative size of these FS segments depends on the field strength with the paired region vanishing above the critical field of FFLO phase. This microscopic structure of the FFLO state has not been probed in practice due to lack of suitable techniques. It was proposed in Ref.
Akbari and Thalmeier 2016

that STM-based quasiparticle interference (QPI) method is a promising candidate for this purpose. However as a feasibility study only the inversion symmetric superconductor was investigated in this work.

In reality inversion symmetry at the superconductor surface is broken and some of the promising SC materials have layered structure with broken 2D inversion symmetry in the layers or even have bulk non-centrosymmetric structure with complete lack of inversion symmetry. Then Rashba-type spin orbit coupling exists and will greatly modify both the FFLO- type states as well as QPI spectral features. In particular the Fermi surface will be doubled into two Rashba Fermi surfaces with different spin texture. This important case is therefore worthy of a separate theoretical investigation presented in this work. There is an important distinction, however, to the common FFLO case where the Zeeman term leads to different Fermi sphere radii of up and down spin electrons, whereas under the presence of a dominating Rashba coupling the two Rashba band
(\lambda=\pm 1)
Fermi spheres are shifted perpendicular to the field by a certain amount proportional to the field strength. This leads immediately to stable Cooper pairs with finite momentum
2{\bf q}
that grows with field strength characterized by an isotropic gap function
\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}\exp(i{\bf q}{\bf r})
.
This commonly called ‘helical’ state
Kaur et al. 2005
is therefore of the FFLO type but has a somewhat different composition of the condensation energy than in the original Zeeman dominated FFLO case.

Some aspects of the helical state including Rashba coupling and Zeeman term have been studied before, concerning mostly critical field curves
Agterberg and Kaur 2007
;
Loder et al. 2013
;
Nakamura and Yanase 2015
;
Zwicknagl et al. 2017
.
Here we focus on the microscopic consequences of the Rashba coupling and its image in the QPI spectrum
Akbari and Thalmeier 2013a
. As a prerequesite we derive the quasiparticle excitation spectrum in the paired and unpaired segments of momentum space whose size depend on the field strength. The corresponding QPI spectrum is created by scattering of quasiparticles from randomly distributed dilute impurities at the surface. We consider normal charge as well as Ising-type magnetic impurities. Our momentum-resolved QPI analysis has a twofold aim: Both the Rashba-doubling of Fermi surface sheets as well as their segmentation in the helical state due to the appearance of unpaired states may be investigated as function of field strength, bias voltage, Rashba coupling and chemical potential. In this way one may get a more microscopic understanding of the peculiar helical superconducting state. In particular we show that it is possible to obtain a direct experimental measure of the pair momentum
2{\bf q}
by analyzing the characteristic wave vectors of the QPI image. The Rashba case with its helical phase is more amenable to such QPI analysis because the latter may appear already at small fields whereas the conventional FFLO phase requires generally very high fields.

The model for the Rashba superconductor is introduced in Sec.
II
and the Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitations are
derived in Sec.
III
following a method introduced by Cui et al in Ref.
Cui et al. 2006
for the inversion-symmetric case without Rashba term (see also Ref.
Akbari and Thalmeier 2016
). In Sec.
IV
we calculate the corresponding Green’s functions and quasiparticle DOS for the helical phase. In the main part of Sec.
V
we derive the QPI spectrum in Born approximation using a model for impurity scattering that contains both normal and magnetic scattering, transformed to the Rashba band states. Finally Sec.
VI
we discuss the numerical results in detail and Sec.
VII
presents the summary and conclusion.

II
Model definition

Here we introduce the commonly used bandstructure model including the Rashba coupling originating from inversion-symmetry breaking. We use the periodic form in view of the later QPI calculations but sometimes discuss the features of Rashba bands in the
parabolic approximation for convenience. Subsequently a minimal model for the superconducting s-wave state introduced in Ref.
Kaur et al. 2005
will be briefly described and the Hamiltonian for the helical phase discussed.

II.1
Normal state Rashba bands and states

The 2D Rashba Hamiltonian in an external field is given by
Kaur et al. 2005

\displaystyle H_{0}=\sum_{\bf k}\Psi^{\dagger}_{\bf k}h_{0{\bf k}}\Psi_{\bf k};\;\;\;h_{0{\bf k}}=\xi_{\bf k}\sigma_{0}+(\alpha{\bf g}_{\bf k}+{\bf b})\cdot{\bm{\sigma}},

(1)

in the spin representation. Here
\Psi^{\dagger}_{\bf k}=(a^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\uparrow},a^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\downarrow})
are conduction electron spinors and

\varepsilon_{\bf k}=-2t(\cos k_{x}+\cos k_{y})
with
-\pi\leq k_{x},k_{y}\leq\pi
is the periodic tight binding (TB) conduction band dispersion which is more suitable for the later treatment of QPI spectrum. Here
t>0
is the hopping element leading to a conduction band half-width
D_{c}=4t
and
\xi_{\bf k}=\varepsilon_{\bf k}-\mu_{\rm TB}
. The chemical potential
\mu_{\rm TB}
in the periodic band model therefore lies in the interval
-D_{c}\leq\mu_{\rm TB}\leq D_{c}
and is referenced to the
band center

\varepsilon_{\bf k}=0
. It is necessary to map this to the 2D parabolic band model for
\mu_{\rm TB}\leq 0
with
\varepsilon_{\bf k}=\varepsilon_{0}+{\bf k}^{2}/2m
.
Here
\varepsilon_{0}=-D_{c}
is the bottom of the band and
m=2/D_{c}
its effective mass. The chemical potential referenced to the
band bottom
is then given by
\mu=\mu_{\rm TB}-\varepsilon_{0}\geq 0
.
Furthermore
{\bf b}=\mu_{B}{\bf B}
is the Zeeman energy scale given by the applied magnetic field
B
. The inversion symmetry breaking Rashba spin-orbit coupling is odd under inversion with
{\bf g}_{-{\bf k}}=-{\bf g}_{{\bf k}}
, explicitly
{\bf g}^{P}_{\bf k}=(k_{y},-k_{x},0)/k_{F}=(\sin\theta_{\bf k},-\cos\theta_{\bf k},0)
in the parabolic band model where
\theta_{\bf k}
is the azimuthal angle of
{\bf k}
counted from the
k_{x}
- axis where the Fermi wave number is
k_{F}=(2m\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
v_{F}=k_{F}/m
the Fermi velocity. To stay consistent with the tight binding model dispersion we will take the periodic form

\displaystyle{\bf g}^{\rm TB}_{\bf k}=(\sin k_{y},-\sin k_{x},0),

(2)

where both forms are normalized, i.e.
|g^{P}_{\bf k}|=1
and
|g^{\rm TB}_{\bf k}|_{\rm max}=\sqrt{2}
. Equivalence in the limit of small wave vectors
k_{x},k_{y}\ll\pi
demands that the Rashba coupling constants in the two models are then related by
\alpha_{P}=k_{F}\alpha_{\rm TB}
.
We suppress indices TB, P in the following and rely on the context. Diagonalization of the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) leads to

\displaystyle H_{0}=\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\varepsilon_{{\bf k}\lambda}c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}c_{{\bf k}\lambda};\;\;\;

(3)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{{\bf k}\lambda}({\bf b})=\xi_{{\bf k}}+\lambda|\alpha{\bf g}_{{\bf k}}+{\bf b}|\equiv\xi_{\bf k}+\lambda\zeta_{\bf k}^{+},

where
\varepsilon_{{\bf k}\lambda}({\bf b})
denotes the Rashba- split and Zeeman- shifted bands (refered to
\mu
) which have eigenfunctions corresponding to helicities
\lambda=\pm 1
. Here we introduce the auxiliary functions
\zeta_{\bf k}^{\pm}=|\alpha{\bf g}_{\bf k}\pm{\bf b}|
.
In zero field the two Rashba bands are given by

\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda}=\xi_{{\bf k}}+\lambda|\alpha{\bf g}_{{\bf k}}|=\frac{1}{2m}(k+\lambda k_{0})^{2}-\tilde{\mu},

(4)

where
k_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\alpha|}{\mu}k_{F}
and
\tilde{\mu}=\mu(1+\frac{1}{4}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\mu^{2}})
. This describes two parabolic dispersions shifted by
k_{0}
. The ensuing two Fermi spheres have radii given approximately by
k_{F}^{\lambda}=k_{F}-\lambda k_{0}=k_{F}(1-\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{|\alpha|}{\mu})
for moderate Rashba coupling
|\alpha|\ll\mu
. Then their relative difference
(k_{F}^{-}-k_{F}^{+})/k_{F}=|\alpha|/\mu
is a direct measure for the strength of the Rashba coupling.
The operators
\Phi_{\bf k}^{\dagger}=(c_{{\bf k}+}^{\dagger}c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}-})

(\lambda=\pm)
creating the helical eigenstates
|{\bf k}\lambda\rangle=c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}|0\rangle
are obtained
Thalmeier and Akbari 2020
from

\displaystyle\Phi^{\dagger}_{\bf k}=\Psi^{\dagger}_{\bf k}S_{\bf k};\;\;\;S_{\bf k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}1&ie^{-i\theta_{\bf k}}\cr ie^{i\theta_{\bf k}}&1\end{matrix}\right],

(5)

where
\theta_{\bf k}=-\tan^{-1}(g_{{\bf k}x}/g_{{\bf k}y})=\tan^{-1}(\sin k_{y}/\sin k_{x})\rightarrow\tan^{-1}(k_{y}/k_{x})
where the second and last expression correspond to TB and parabolic models, respectively. For finite but small in-plane field
{\bf b}=b(\cos\phi,\sin\phi,0)

(b\ll\alpha)
, where
\phi
defines the field angle with respect to planar axes
the Rashba dispersions of Eq. (
3
) in the parabolic model can be written explicitly as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{{\bf k}\lambda}({\bf b})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}(k+\lambda k_{0})^{2}-\tilde{\mu}+\lambda s_{\alpha}b\sin(\theta_{\bf k}-\phi),

(6)

where we defined
s_{\alpha}={\rm sign}(\alpha)
. This leads to Rashba Fermi sheets with a radius
given by

\displaystyle k_{F}^{\lambda}(\theta_{\bf k},\phi)=k^{\lambda}_{F}-\frac{1}{2\mu}k_{F}\lambda s_{\alpha}b\sin(\theta_{\bf k}-\phi),

(7)

where
we assumed the physical hierarchy of energy scales according to
(b<|\alpha|<\mu<2D_{c})
. An example for the geometry
of Rashba Fermi surface sheets is shown in Fig.
1
. The effect of the field on the two Rashba sheets may be easily
understood by considering the relative change compared to the zero-field value
k^{\lambda}_{F}
as function of the angle
\theta_{\bf k}
.
For momentum (anti-)parallel to the field with
\theta_{\bf k}=\phi+\pi,\phi
there is no change and
k_{F}^{\lambda}(\theta_{\bf k},\phi)=k^{\lambda}_{F}
. For perpendicular case with

\theta_{\bf k}=\phi+\pi/2,\phi+3\pi/2
,
we have

k_{F}^{\lambda}(\theta_{\bf k},\phi)=k^{\lambda}_{F}\mp\frac{1}{2}k_{F}\lambda s_{\alpha}(b/\mu)
.
Thus the two Rashba sheets are shifted
perpendicular
to the field in opposite directions by the amount

\displaystyle q_{s}=\frac{b}{2\mu}k_{F}=\frac{m\mu_{B}B}{k_{F}}=\frac{b}{v_{F}}.

(8)

While the splitting of Rashba sheets is a measure for the coupling
|\alpha|
their shifting perpendicular to
B
is a determined by field
strength alone. These basic Rashba characteristics are shown in Fig.
1
(a) as it results from the splitted and shifted dispersions in Figs.
1
(b,c).

II.2
Superconducting state with finite momentum pairing

In this work we do not discuss the possible mechanisms behind the superconducting gap formation in non-centrosymmetric compounds without inversion symmetry, for an excellent review see Ref.
Sigrist 2009
. In these materials with Rashba spin-oribit coupling phonons
Wiendlocha et al. 2016
as well as spin-fluctuations
Yanase and Sigrist 2008
;
Takimoto and Thalmeier 2009
;
Mukherjee and Takimoto 2012
may be the driving mechanism for Cooper pair formation. In any case it is important to realize
that, independent of the mechanism the gap function contains spin- singlet as well as triplet components due to
the inversion symmetry breaking presented by the Rashba term.

In addition here we consider the possibility of a common overall momentum
2{\bf q}
of Cooper pairs due
to the pairbreaking effect of the external field in conjunction with Rashba spin-orbit coupling. One should expect
that the size of
{\bf q}
is correlated with the shift of the Rashba FS sheets
Agterberg and Kaur 2007
perpendicular to the field as given
by Eq. (
8
). The real value of
q
should be evaluated by the minimization of the condensation
energy in the helical SC phase as is demonstrated in Sec.
III
.
As mentioned in the introduction more general pairs with multiple
{\bf q}_{i}
, in particular the
‘stripe phase’
Kaur et al. 2005
with
({\bf q},-{\bf q})
will not be considered here. Of the many possible choices of gap functions we use the minimal model introduced by Kaur et al
Kaur et al. 2005
which reduces to
the spin-singlet form in the limit
\alpha=0
. In the helical basis it is characterized by two gap functions
\Delta^{\bf k}_{{\bf q}\lambda}
for the two Rashba sheets. The resulting mean field pair Hamiltonian in helicity representation is described by

\displaystyle H_{\rm MF}=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\varepsilon_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}c_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}

(9)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}[\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}c^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}+\Delta^{*}_{{\bf q}\lambda}c_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}c_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}].

With the gap equation for the isotropic state of the FFLO phase given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}=-\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}{{}^{\prime}}V_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\langle c_{-{\bf k}^{\prime}+{\bf q}\lambda^{\prime}}c_{{\bf k}^{\prime}+{\bf q}\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle,

(10)

where the prime indicates that the summation over
{\bf k}^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}
runs only over the paired momentum regions with
positve
quasiparticle energies in Eq. (
21
). For the singlet case (in the limit
\alpha=0
) considered the pairing interaction in the helical basis in the limit
|{\bf b}/\alpha|\ll 1
takes the form
Kaur et al. 2005

\displaystyle\hat{V}=-\frac{V_{0}}{2}(\sigma_{0}-\sigma_{x})=-\frac{V_{0}}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{array}\right].

Inserting this two-band pairing interaction into the gap equation [Eq. (
10
)] leads to the condition
\Delta^{\bf k}_{{\bf q}-}=-\Delta^{\bf k}_{{\bf q}+}
. The opposite sign of the two gaps is enforced by the opposite spin texture on the two Rashba bands.
To keep the parameter set for our investigation at a minimum we restrict to the simple case of
isotropic
(
\Delta_{{\bf q}-}=-\Delta_{{\bf q}+}
) gaps without
k
- dependence. Then the total BCS Hamiltonian including the mean field energy constant is given by

\displaystyle H_{\rm BCS}=H_{\rm MF}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\frac{\Delta^{2}_{{\bf q}\lambda}}{V_{0}}.

(14)

Introducing the Nambu spinors
\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=(c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda},c_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda})
we may write
H_{\rm MF}=\hat{H}_{\rm MF}+E_{0}
where

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm MF}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\psi^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\hat{h}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\psi_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda};\;\;\;E_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\varepsilon_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda},

(15)

and the Hamilton matrix in Nambu space is given by

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\varepsilon_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}&-\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}\\
-\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}^{*}&-\varepsilon_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}\end{array}\right].

(16)

Using the symmetries
\xi_{\bf k}=\xi_{-{\bf k}}
and
{\bf g}_{\bf k}=-{\bf g}_{-{\bf k}}
the diagonal elements are obtained as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}({\bf b})

\displaystyle=\xi_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}+\lambda|\alpha{\bf g}_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}+{\bf b}|=\xi_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}+\lambda\zeta^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}};

(17)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}({\bf b})

\displaystyle=\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}+\lambda|\alpha{\bf g}_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}-{\bf b}|=\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}+\lambda\zeta^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}},

here we defined the auxiliary Rashba functions

\zeta^{\pm}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}=|\alpha{\bf g}_{{\bf k}\pm{\bf q}}\pm{\bf b}|
where both signs on the right are taken simultaneously
+
or
-
.
We also introduce symmetric (s) and antisymmetric (a) combinations explicitly given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}+\varepsilon_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda})

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\xi_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}+\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}})+\lambda\frac{1}{2}(\zeta^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}+\zeta^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}})\equiv\xi^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}+\lambda\zeta^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}},

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}-\varepsilon_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\xi_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}-\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}})+\lambda\frac{1}{2}(\zeta^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}-\zeta^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}})\equiv\xi^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}+\lambda\zeta^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}},

They fulfil the even/odd symmetry relations
\varepsilon^{s}_{-{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
and

\varepsilon^{a}_{-{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=-\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
, respectively. Here we defined
\xi^{s,a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}=\frac{1}{2}(\xi_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}\pm\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}})
and
\zeta^{s,a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}=\frac{1}{2}(\zeta^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}\pm\zeta^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}})
.
In the formal limit of no Rashba coupling (
\alpha=0
) this simplifies to
\zeta^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}=|b|
and
\zeta^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}=0
.
In this case the two Rashba bands
\epsilon_{{\bf k}\lambda}
(Eq. (
3
)) become the Zeeman split bands with effective spin
index
\lambda
.
Now we can split the diagonal matrix elements in the Hamiltionian into symmetric and antisymmetric parts and using
the symmetry relations we arrive at

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\tau_{0}+\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}&-\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}\\
-\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}^{*}&-\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\end{array}\right].

(19)

We note that in the following we
will also use the property
\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=0
which is due the antisymmetry of
\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
. Hereby the summation over
k

III
Bogoliubov transformation for paired and depaired states

The first part in the
k
- symmetrized Hamiltionian in Eq. (
19
) is already diagonal.
The second part can now be diagonalized by a Bogoliubov transformation to quasiparticle states created
by
\alpha_{{\bf k}\lambda},\beta_{{\bf k}\lambda}
with the corresponding Hamiltonian expressed as

\displaystyle H_{\rm MF}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\bigl[|E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|\alpha^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\alpha_{\bf k}+|E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|\beta^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\beta_{\bf k}\bigr]

(20)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}{{}^{\prime}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}-E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda};\;\;E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}>0\\
\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}+\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda};\;\;E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0\\
\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}-\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda};\;\;E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0\end{array}\right\}.\quad

Here the quasiparticle energies are given by
(\tau=\pm,\bar{\tau}=\mp)
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}&=E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}+\tau\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=E^{\bar{\tau}}_{-{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda},\\
E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}&=[\varepsilon^{s2}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}+\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}=E_{-{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}.\end{aligned}

(21)

When for a given
{\bf k},\lambda
both
E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}>0

(\tau=\pm)
one has a stable Cooper pair state with pair momentum
2{\bf q}
for this wave vector
{\bf k}
and band
\lambda
. If, on the other hand
E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0
or
E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0
the pair state is broken and only unpaired quasiparticle states at exist at the wave vectors
{\bf k}+{\bf q}
,
-{\bf k}+{\bf q}
. Note the remarkable fact that although for these wave vectors
|E^{\pm}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|
are normal quasiparticle excitations their energy nevertheless contains the gap size
\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}
determined by the paired states. This is because in the coherent helical ground state the unpaired electrons and holes also experience the pairing molecular field sustained by the paired electrons, even though they do not contribute to it.
The mean field energy constant (last term in Eq. (
20
)) in the two cases is different because of the additional condensation energy in the paired state.

Therefore the corresponding Bogoliubov transformations for the two cases are also different:
For the paired states it is given by
Cui et al. 2006
:

E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}>0:\left[\begin{array}[]{c}c_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}\\
c^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{*}_{{\bf k}\lambda}&v_{{\bf k}\lambda}\\
-v^{*}_{{\bf k}\lambda}&u_{{\bf k}\lambda}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{{\bf k}\lambda}\\
\beta^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}\end{array}\right],

(22)

whereas for the depaired states it may be written as
Cui et al. 2006

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0:\left[\begin{array}[]{c}c_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}\\
c^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{*}_{{\bf k}\lambda}&v_{{\bf k}\lambda}\\
-v^{*}_{{\bf k}\lambda}&u_{{\bf k}\lambda}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}\\
\beta^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}\lambda}\end{array}\right];\;\;\;\\
&E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0:\left[\begin{array}[]{c}c_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}\\
c^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}\lambda}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{*}_{{\bf k}\lambda}&v_{{\bf k}\lambda}\\
-v^{*}_{{\bf k}\lambda}&u_{{\bf k}\lambda}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{{\bf k}\lambda}\\
\beta_{{\bf k}\lambda}\end{array}\right].\end{aligned}

Explicitly the transformation coefficients are given by

\displaystyle u_{{\bf k}\lambda}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigl(1+\frac{\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}}{E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}}\bigr);\;\;\;v_{{\bf k}\lambda}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}}{E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}}\bigr).

(24)

These coefficients fulfil the wellknown relations

\displaystyle u_{{\bf k}\lambda}^{2}-v^{2}_{{\bf k}\lambda}=\frac{\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}}{E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}};\;\;2u_{{\bf k}\lambda}v_{{\bf k}\lambda}=\frac{\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}}{E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}}.

(25)

Note the important fact that only the
symmetrized
Rashba band energies
\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
appear in the transformation coefficients
u_{{\bf k}\lambda},v_{{\bf k}\lambda}
. However, both
momentum- symmetric
\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
and anti- symmetric
\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
contribute to the superconducting quasiparticle energies
E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
in Eq. (

The total BCS Hamiltionian, including the constant energy in Eq. (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H_{\rm BCS}=&H_{\rm MF}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}{{}^{\prime}}\frac{|\Delta|^{2}_{{\bf q}\lambda}}{V_{0}}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}(|E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|\alpha^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\alpha_{\bf k}+|E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|\beta^{\dagger}_{\bf k}\beta_{\bf k})\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}{{}^{\prime}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}-E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}+\frac{|\Delta|^{2}_{{\bf q}\lambda}}{V_{0}}\\
\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}+\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}+\frac{|\Delta|^{2}_{{\bf q}\lambda}}{V_{0}}\\
\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}-\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}+\frac{|\Delta|^{2}_{{\bf q}\lambda}}{V_{0}}\end{array}\right\}.\end{aligned}

Here the second term
\langle H_{\rm BCS}\rangle
is equal the total ground state energy
E_{G}({\bf q},\Delta_{{\bf q}\pm})
of the helical FFLO-type state. As in Eq. (
20
) the sum extends over the upper value for paired states with both
E^{\pm}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}>0
whereas the lower
values correspond a sum only over to the unpaired states with
E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0
or
E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0
, respectively. These conditional sums are indicated by the prime. The helical SC ground state energy may be rewritten explicitly as (see also Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle E_{G}({\bf q},\Delta_{{\bf q}\pm})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda}\Bigl[N\bigl(\frac{|\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}|^{2}}{V_{0}}\bigr)-\sum_{\bf k}(E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}-\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})

(27)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt+\sum_{\bf k}[E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\Theta(-E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})+E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\Theta(-E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})]\Bigr].

This energy functional should be minimized with respect to
q
and
\Delta_{{\bf q}\pm}
for Rashba coupling
\alpha
and field strength
b
.
It contains the possibilities of the helical
({\bf q}\neq 0,|\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}|>0)
, BCS
({\bf q}=0,|\Delta_{0\lambda}|>0)
and unpolarized normal
(b=0,{\bf q}=0,\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}=0)
states. For the latter the ground state energy is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E^{0}_{G}=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}(\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda}-|\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda}|)=\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}f_{{\bf k}\lambda}\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda},\\
\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda}=&\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}({\bf q}=0,b=0)=\xi_{\bf k}+\lambda|\alpha{\bf g}_{\bf k}|,\end{aligned}

(28)

where
f_{\bf k}=\Theta(-\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda})
is the zero temperature Fermi function for the unpolarized Rashba-split bands
\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda}
(cf. Eq. (
4
)). The minimization problem is greatly simplified by the equal size of the gaps
|\Delta_{{\bf q}\pm}|=\Delta_{\bf q}
in the model defined by Eq. (
II.2
). Although strictly this holds only for
q
=0 we will also keep this minimization constraint for the helical case. The pairing potential strength
V_{0}
in Eqs. (
II.2
,
28
) is related to the gap size
\Delta_{0}
by the simplified single gap equation obtained from Eq. (
10
)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V_{0}}=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\frac{1}{2E_{{\bf k}\lambda}}\Theta(\varepsilon_{c}-|\varepsilon_{{\bf k}\lambda}|),

(29)

where the BCS zero-field quasiparticle energy is
E_{{\bf k}\lambda}=[\varepsilon^{02}_{{\bf k}\lambda}+\Delta_{0}^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Here
\xi_{c}
is an effective cutoff
of the pairing potential
(\Delta_{0}<\xi_{c}<2D_{c})
. In the following calculations the gap size
\Delta_{0}
is used directly as a fixed input parameter, then the cutoff may be absorbed in an effective coupling constant
V_{0}
by deleting the
\Theta
- function.

For finding the ground state by numerical minimization it is useful to subtract the normal state energy from the ground state energy in Eq. (
27
) to obtain the superconducting condensation energy
E_{c}=E_{G}-E^{0}_{G}
according to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &E_{c}({\bf q},\Delta_{{\bf q}\pm})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda}\Bigg[N\bigl(\frac{|\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}|^{2}}{V_{0}}\bigr)-\sum_{\bf k}\Big[(E_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}-|\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda}|)\\
&\!+(\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\!-\!\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda})+[E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\Theta(-E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})+E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\Theta(-E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})]\Big]\Bigg].\end{aligned}

(30)

Note that the asymmetric
\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
Rashba energies of Eq. (
18
) enter only in the unpaired quasiparticle contribution (last term).
Using the pure singlet gap constraint
\Delta_{{\bf q}\pm}=\pm\Delta_{\bf q}
the minimization of

\Delta_{{\bf q}}
and

\alpha

{\bf q}(b,\alpha)

IV
Green’s functions in the Rashba-FFLO state and quasiparticle DOS

The Green’s functions in the FFLO- type superconducing state are needed for the calculation of quasiparticle DOS and interference spectra. Using Eq. (
19
)
we obtain:

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{{\bf q}\lambda}({\bf k},i\omega_{n})=(i\omega_{n}-\hat{h}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})^{-1}

(31)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(i\omega_{n}-E^{+}_{{\bf q}{\bf k}\lambda})(i\omega_{n}+E^{-}_{{\bf q}{\bf k}\lambda})}\times

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}i\omega_{n}+\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}-\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}&-\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}\\[5.69046pt]
-\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}^{*}&i\omega_{n}-\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}-\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\end{array}\right].

The normal and anomalous Green’s functions elements
G^{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}_{{\bf q}\lambda}({\bf k},i\omega_{n})
satisfy the following symmetry relations:

\displaystyle G^{11}_{{\bf q}\lambda}(-{\bf k},i\omega_{n})=-G^{22}_{{\bf q}\lambda}({\bf k},-i\omega_{n}),\;\;\;

(32)

and likewise

\displaystyle G^{12*}_{{\bf q}\lambda}(-{\bf k},i\omega_{n})=G^{21}_{{\bf q}\lambda}({\bf k},-i\omega_{n}).\;\;\;

(33)

The spectral function corresponding to the above Green’s function is obtained as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} A^{\lambda}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}(\omega)&=-\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}[{\rm tr}[\hat{G}_{{\bf q}\lambda}({\bf k},\omega+i\eta)]]_{\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\\
&=\sum_{\tau}\delta(\omega-E_{{\bf q}{\bf k}\lambda}^{\tau}).\end{aligned}

(34)

Now using the symmetry relation
E^{\tau}_{-{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=E^{\bar{\tau}}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}
(with
\tau=\pm
and
\bar{\tau}=\mp
)
one can define symmetrized spectral function according to

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{\lambda}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}=\frac{1}{2}[A^{\lambda}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}(\omega)+A^{\lambda}_{-{\bf k}{\bf q}}(\omega)]=\frac{1}{2}[A^{\lambda}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}(\omega)+A^{\lambda}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}(-\omega)].

(35)

Using Eq. (
34
) they may be obtained for paired as well as unpaired regions as

\bar{A}^{\lambda}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\tau}[\delta(\omega-|E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|)+\delta(\omega+|E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|].

(36)

This result agrees with the expression that may be directly infered from the quasiparticle Hamiltonian of Eq. (
20
).
Summation over quasiparticle momenta
k
then leads to the quasiparticle DOS,
\rho_{{\bf q}\lambda}(\omega)
,
for Rashba band
\lambda
in the helical state with pair momentum
2{\bf q}
according to

\rho_{{\bf q}\lambda}(\omega>0)=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{{\bf k}}[\delta(\omega-|E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|)+\delta(\omega-|E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|)].

(37)

This presentation for the DOS is perfectly adequate for its numerical evaluation and will in fact be used later. However, to elucidate
the distinction between conduction bands without spin-orbit coupling
(\alpha=0)
treated previously
Cui et al. 2006
;
Akbari and Thalmeier 2016
and the present Rashba-split
bands it is illuminating to evaluate this expression partly analytically, except for a remaining momentum angle integration.
For that purpose we can simplify the expressions in Eq. (
18
) when
q/k_{F}\ll 1
and
{\bf k}\simeq k_{F}\hat{{\bf k}}
is close to the Fermi surface. Then
\xi_{\bf k}^{s}\simeq\xi_{\bf k}
and
\xi^{a}_{\bf k}\simeq qv_{F}\cos(\theta_{\bf k}-\theta_{\bf q})
with
v_{F}=k_{F}/m
and the orthogonal pair momentum and field directions defined by
\theta_{\bf q}=\frac{\pi}{2}

({\bf q}=\mbox{q}\hat{{\bf y}})
and
{\bf b}=\mbox{b}\hat{{\bf x}}
.
Furthermore this leads to
{\bf g}_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}}\simeq{\bf g}_{\bf k}\simeq(k_{F}^{y},-k_{F}^{x},0)/k_{F}=(\sin\theta_{\bf k},-\cos\theta_{\bf k},0)
.
Using these approximations we get
(\xi_{\bf k}={\bf k}^{2}/2m-\mu)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}\equiv\varepsilon_{{\bf k}\lambda}=\xi_{\bf k}+\lambda|\alpha{\bf g}_{\bf k}|=\xi_{\bf k}+\lambda|\alpha|,

(38)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}^{a}=(v_{F}q+\lambda b)\sin\theta_{\bf k}.

In this approximation the superconducting quasiparticle energies (Eq.
21
) then simplify to

E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=[(\xi_{{\bf k}}+\lambda|\alpha|)^{2}+\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tau(v_{F}q+\lambda b)\sin\theta_{\bf k}.

(39)

The quasiparticle DOS
\rho(\omega)
may be evaluated
Cui et al. 2006
;
Akbari and Thalmeier 2016
as

\displaystyle\rho_{{\bf q}}(\omega>0)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{\lambda}\rho^{n}_{\lambda}(0)\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!d\theta\int_{0}^{\hbar\omega_{c}}\!d\varepsilon

(40)

\displaystyle\Big[\delta(\omega-|E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|)+|\delta(\omega-|E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}|)\Big],\quad\quad

where
\rho^{n}_{\lambda}(0)=\rho_{n}(0)=m/2\pi
is the normal state DOS equal for both Rashba bands. With the angle-independent
bare Rashba dispersion
\varepsilon^{0}_{{\bf k}\lambda}=\xi_{{\bf k}}+\lambda|\alpha|
denoted by
\varepsilon
we can write

E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=[\varepsilon^{2}+\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tau(v_{F}q+\lambda b)\sin\theta_{\bf k}.

(41)

Introducing now
\hat{E}^{\tau}_{\theta q\lambda}=\omega-\tau(v_{F}q+\lambda b)\sin\theta
, the
\varepsilon
- integration leads to the partial radial DOS at angle
\theta=\theta_{\bf k}

\hat{\rho}_{{\bf q}\lambda}(\omega,\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{|\hat{E}^{+}_{\theta q\lambda}|}{[|\hat{E}^{+}_{\theta q\lambda}|^{2}-\Delta^{2}_{q\lambda}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{|\hat{E}^{-}_{\theta q\lambda}|}{[|\hat{E}^{-}_{\theta q\lambda}|^{2}-\Delta^{2}_{q\lambda}]^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Bigr),

(42)

and the total DOS is then given by

\rho_{\bf q}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\lambda}\rho^{n}_{\lambda}(0)\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\hat{\rho}_{{\bf q}\lambda}(\omega,\theta).

(43)

Which has four contributions due to two quasiparticle branches for each of the two Rashba split bands characterized
by
(\tau,\lambda)=(\pm,\pm)
. They have the same form and are determined by their different energies
\hat{E}^{\tau}_{\theta q\lambda}
which
are explicitly given by

\displaystyle\hat{E}^{\pm}_{\theta q+}=\omega\mp(v_{F}q+b)\sin\theta;\;\;\hat{E}^{\pm}_{\theta q-}=\omega\mp(v_{F}q-b)\sin\theta.

(44)

Note that because of the helical spin polarization of Rashba states the Zeeman contribution for a fixed field direction is now also proportional to
\sin\theta
since the spins are locked with respect to crystal axes for
|\alpha|\gg|{\bf b}|
. This is an essential difference to the inversion symmetric case without Rashba spin-orbit coupling where they can align parallel to the

- field
Cui et al. 2006
;
Akbari and Thalmeier 2016
and therefore no dependence on the momentum angle
\theta
appears in this case.

V
The quasiparticle interference spectrum

Now we turn to the main object of this work, the calculation of the quasiparticle interferenc spectrum in the helical phase
which should show in a very straightforward manner the effect of the sofar hypothetical momentum-space segmentation
of quasiparticles into paired and unpaired regions determined by field
b
and Cooper pair momentum
2{\bf q}
. This
effect contains the microscopic essence of the helical superconducting state. For this purpose it is also necessary to
define a simple model for the surface-impurity scattering of quasiparticles and transform it to the basis of helical Rashba
band states.

V.1
The normal and magnetic impurity scattering

We consider the two most frequent cases of normal charge (c) impurities and magnetic moment (m) impurities being responsible for electron scattering at the surface of the Rashba FFLO- type superconductor.
In the normal state, using spin representation of conduction electrons the isotropic scattering from impurities
located at random sites
{\bf R}_{i}
is described by

\displaystyle U_{c}({\bf r}-{\bf R}_{i})=U_{0}\sigma_{0}\delta({\bf r}-{\bf R}_{i});\;\;\;

(45)

\displaystyle U_{m}({\bf r}-{\bf R}_{i})=U_{1}\sigma_{z}\delta({\bf r}-{\bf R}_{i})

for the charge and exchange scattering, respectively. In the latter case we assumed an Ising-type classical local moment
\langle S_{z}(i)\rangle
at site
i
oriented along z-direction by a uniaxial potential, i.e.
U_{1}=\frac{1}{2}J_{\rm ex}\langle S_{z}(i)\rangle
where
J_{\rm ex}
is the on-site exchange constant.
This leads to a Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\rm imp}=\sum_{i{\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime}\sigma}U_{\sigma}a_{{\bf k}^{\prime}\sigma}^{\dagger}a_{{\bf k}\sigma}e^{i({\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}){\bf R}_{i}};\;\;\;U_{\sigma}=U_{0}+\sigma U_{1}

(46)

in spin representation describing the scattering by random impurities at the surface where
\tilde{{\bf q}}={\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}
is the momentum transfer. It has to be transformed to the helical eigenstates of the Rashba bands
defined by Eq. (
5
). Furthermore in the superconducting state we must
use appropriate scattering matrices in Nambu (particle-hole) space according to the replacement
(U_{0},U_{1})\rightarrow(U_{0}\tau_{z},U_{1}\tau_{0})
where
\tau_{z},\tau_{0}
are corresponding Pauli and unit matrices, respectively
Akbari and Thalmeier 2013b
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle H_{\rm imp}=\sum_{i{\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\!\!\Big[

\displaystyle U_{0}\tilde{V}^{0}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})\tau_{z}

(47)

\displaystyle+U_{1}\tilde{V}^{1}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})\tau_{0}\Big]c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}\lambda}c_{{\bf k}\lambda^{\prime}}e^{i({\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}){\bf R}_{i}}.

Here the momentum dependent scattering form factors
\tilde{V}^{0,1}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})
are introduced by the transformation to helical eigenstates
|{\bf k}\lambda\rangle
of each Rashba bands according to Eq. (
5
). They are obtained from the transformation matrix in this equation according to

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{0}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})=\sum_{\sigma}S^{*}_{\sigma\lambda}({\bf k}^{\prime})S_{\sigma\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k});\;\;\;

(48)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{1}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})=\sum_{\sigma}\sigma S^{*}_{\sigma\lambda}({\bf k}^{\prime})S_{\sigma\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}).

Explicitly we obtain in helicity space
(\lambda\lambda^{\prime})
effective momentum- dependent c,m scattering potentials, given, respectively by

\displaystyle\{\tilde{V}^{0}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})\}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1+e^{i(\theta_{\bf k}-\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}})}&i(e^{-i\theta_{\bf k}}-e^{-i\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}})\cr i(e^{i\theta_{\bf k}}-e^{i\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}})&1+e^{-i(\theta_{\bf k}-\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}})}\end{matrix}\right];\;\;

(49)

\displaystyle\{\tilde{V}^{1}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})\}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1-e^{i(\theta_{\bf k}-\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}})}&i(e^{-i\theta_{\bf k}}+e^{-i\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}})\cr-i(e^{i\theta_{\bf k}}+e^{i\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}})&-(1-e^{-i(\theta_{\bf k}-\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}))}\end{matrix}\right].

These scattering matrices are Hermitean fulfilling the relations
\tilde{V}^{\kappa*}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime})=\tilde{V}^{\kappa}_{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda}({\bf k}^{\prime},{\bf k})

(\kappa=0,1)
.
Since we use the periodic TB band model for the QPI calculation in the next section we also must use the periodic form
of the phase angle
\theta_{\bf k}=\tan^{-1}(\sin k_{y}/\sin k_{x})
in the above expressions appropriate for the TB model. Note that one has to be careful to pick the right branches so that the polar angle covers the whole interval
[0,2\pi]
. This is guaranteed if we define
\theta^{0}_{\bf k}=\tan^{-1}(|\sin(k_{y})/\sin(k_{x})|)
and choose
\theta_{\bf k}
in the whole BZ
-\pi\leq k_{x},k_{y}\leq\pi
in counterclockwise fashion in the four quadrants (I-IV)
(\pm k_{x}>0,\pm k_{y}>0)
according to

\displaystyle\mbox{(I)}:\;\theta_{\bf k}=\theta^{0}_{\bf k};\;\quad\quad\quad\mbox{(II)}:\;\theta_{\bf k}=\pi-\theta^{0}_{\bf k};\;\;\;

(50)

\displaystyle\mbox{(III)}:\;\theta_{\bf k}=\theta^{0}_{\bf k}+\pi;\;\;\;\mbox{(IV)}:\;\theta_{\bf k}=2\pi-\theta^{0}_{\bf k}.

In the helicity representation the scattering matrix includes non-diagonal inter-band terms
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
even though we started from a scattering potential diagonal in spin quantum numbers. Both momentum dependence and interband features of the scattering play a role in the QPI spectrum.

V.2
QPI spectrum in Born approximation

The Fourier component of the surface charge modulation corresponding to momentum transfer
\tilde{{\bf q}}={\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}
(not to be confused with Cooper pair momentum
2{\bf q}
) and bias voltage
\omega=eV
induced by the scattering from random impurites is given by (per impurity site)
Akbari and Thalmeier 2013b
;
Akbari and Thalmeier 2013a

\displaystyle\delta N(\tilde{{\bf q}},\omega)=-\frac{1}{\pi}{\rm Im}{\Big[}\tilde{\Lambda}(\tilde{{\bf q}},i\omega_{n}){\Big]}_{i\omega_{n}\rightarrow\omega+i\delta},

(51)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}(\tilde{{\bf q}},i\omega_{n})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{\Big[}\tau_{z}\hat{G}_{\lambda}({\bf k},i\omega_{n})\hat{t}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime},i\omega_{n})\hat{G}_{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}^{\prime},i\omega_{n}){\Big]}_{11},

where
\hat{t}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})(i\omega_{n})
is the scattering t- matrix due to the impurity scattering potential (Eq. (
47
)) and the index
(11)
projects out the electron part of the Nambu matrix. Since the effective scattering in
H_{\rm imp}
is
momentum dependent due to helical transformation we treat it only in Born approximation (BA) for weak scattering. As a matter of experience the QPI spectra in momentum space do not strongly depend on this simplification
Akbari and Thalmeier 2013b
. In the Born case the t- matrix is frequency independent and simply given by

\hat{t}^{c}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})=U_{0}\tilde{V}^{0}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})\tau_{z};\quad\hat{t}^{m}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})=U_{1}\tilde{V}^{1}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})\tau_{0},

(52)

in the normal (charge) and exchange (magnetic) scattering (c,m) cases, respectively. Inserting this in Eq. (
51
), using the explicit
FFLO- type Green’s function (Eq. (
31
)) and defining
\tilde{\Lambda}_{(0,1)}(\tilde{{\bf q}},i\omega_{n})=U_{(0,1)}\Lambda_{0,1}(\tilde{{\bf q}},i\omega_{n})
we obtain the final result of QPI spectrum function (suppressing the pair momentum index
q
everywhere)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}_{\kappa}(\tilde{{\bf q}},i\omega_{n})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}\tilde{V}^{\kappa}_{(1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})\Bigl[\frac{(i\omega_{n}+\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}\lambda}-\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}\lambda})(i\omega_{n}+\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}}-\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})-(-1)^{\kappa}\Delta_{\lambda}\Delta_{\lambda^{\prime}}}{(i\omega_{n}-E^{+}_{{\bf k}\lambda})(i\omega_{n}+E^{-}_{{\bf k}\lambda})(i\omega_{n}-E^{+}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})(i\omega_{n}+E^{-}_{{\bf k}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}})}\Bigr]

(53)

for the two cases of normal
(c,\kappa=0)
and magnetic
(m,\kappa=1)
scattering, respectively, whereby the sign constraint
\Delta_{+}=-\Delta_{-}=\Delta(q,b)
for the gap functions has to be kept. In this sum we are using the BA scattering matrix from Eq. (
49
) the quasiparticle energies from Eq. (
21
) and the (anti-) symmetrized normal state dispersions from Eq. (
18
). The value of the SC gap is obtained from the minimization procedure of Eq. (
30
). Note that only the real part of the scattering matrix
\tilde{V}^{\kappa}_{(1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})={\rm Re}\tilde{V}^{\kappa}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})
which, due to the hermiticity of Eq. (
49
), is symmetric under exchange of all indices enters the expression for
\tilde{\Lambda}_{\kappa}(\tilde{{\bf q}},i\omega_{n})
. Likewise the imaginary part
\tilde{V}^{\kappa}_{(2)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})={\rm Im}\tilde{V}^{\kappa}_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})
must be antisymmetric under this exchange and beacuse the expression in parentheses in Eq. (
53
) is symmetric the summation over it gives zero. The real symmetric scattering matrix elements in Eq. (
53
) in the charge
(\kappa=0)
and magnetic
(\kappa=1)

\displaystyle\{\tilde{V}^{0}_{(1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf k}{\bf k}^{\prime})\}\!=\!\!\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}1+\cos(\theta_{\bf k}-\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}})&\sin\theta_{\bf k}-\sin\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\cr-\sin\theta_{\bf k}+\sin\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}&1\!+\!\cos(\theta_{\bf k}-\theta_{{\bf k}^{\prime}})\end{matrix}\right];

(54)

The difference between the two is due to the different influence of helical spin texture in the two scattering mechanisms.

VII
Conclusion and Outlook

In this work we investigated microscopic features of helical phase in Rashba superconductors with isotropic and equal magnitude of the gap function on the two Rashba bands. The latter have helical spin texture enforced by the strong Rashba spin-orbit coupling. In a magnetic field they are shifted perpendicular to the field by an amount proportional to its size.
Therefore Cooper pairing in a state with non-vanishing pair momentum 2
q
will be favored.

Using the approximations for large Rashba coupling we derived the condensation energy as function of
q
. Minimization leads to the dependence of pair momentum and gap size on the applied field. At the same time we computed the quasiparticle energies in the helical state. Their most interesting aspect is a segmentation of momentum space into regions where Cooper pairs are stable and gapped Bogoliubov excitations exist and other regions spread around the direction of the overall pair momentum where pair breaking due to large kinetic energy destroys the Cooper pairs and leads to normal low- energy quasiparticles with corresponding Fermi surface sheets. These are present despite the fact that the gap
\Delta^{\bf k}_{{\bf q}\lambda}=\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}
is nodeless in
k
- space and real space.

This basic microscopic structure of the helical state, a coherent superposition of paired and unpaired states with associated peculiar evolution of Fermi surface (surfaces of constant energy) topology as function of field and frequency has sofar not been investigated experimentally. In this work we have shown that the technique of quasiparticle interference is well suited to address this central property of Rashba superconductors with finite momentum Cooper pairing. It is able to monitor the apperance of the segmented Fermi surface sheets of unpaired quasiparticles as function of field strength and bias voltage until they evolve
into those of the normal state Rashba sheets for large values of these tuning parameters. Due to the helical frozen spin texture the QPI images obtained for charge and magnetic impurity scattering on the surface show considerable difference and are complementary in the intensity distribution.
Furthermore following some of the characteristic wave vectors of the segments one may derive an estimate for the size of
the pair momentum 2q which is not accessible by other experimental means.

The FFLO-type helical phase in the Rashba superconductor is more amenable to such QPI investigations because
it appears already for small fields and does not require the extremely large fields of the genuine FFLO phase in the
inversion symmetric superonductors. It may also occur more frequently since there is a considerable number of inversion-symmetry breaking (non-centrosymmetric) superconductors known by now. In particular such QPI investigations for the
helical phase should be possible in layered superonductors with strong 2D character which has been assumed in our analysis.

Appendix B
The proof of vanishing charge current

Without a Rashba coupling it is known that the total charge current in the helical state vanishes even though the pairs have finite momentum. This is due to the fact that the current is the pair-momentum derivative of the total energy which must vanish in the ground state
Cui et al. 2006
. Here we show that this still holds for the case of finite Rashba coupling. The charge current operator is commonly given in terms of Bloch operators creating spin
\sigma_{z}
eigenstates
Cui et al. 2006
. After a unitary transformation to helical states
(\lambda=\pm
) in the Rashba system we obtain (in units of e):

{\bf J}^{c}_{\bf q}=\!\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\bf k}[({\bf k}+{\bf q})c^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}+}c_{{\bf k}+{\bf q}+}-({\bf k}-{\bf q})c^{\dagger}_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}-}c_{-{\bf k}+{\bf q}-}].

(62)

Transforming to Bogliubov quasiparticle states with Eqs. (
22
,
III
) we obtain for the y-component (
q_{y}=q,k_{y}=k
) of the current;

\displaystyle\langle J^{c}_{q}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\Big[2q|v_{{\bf k}\lambda}|^{2}\theta_{H}(E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})\theta_{H}(E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})

(63)

\displaystyle+(q+k)\theta_{H}(-E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})+(q-k)\theta_{H}(-E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda})\Big].

Now we consider again the total ground state energy Eq. (
III
), using an equivalent form for the paired term
according to Eq. (
57
) and the relation
\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\varepsilon^{a}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}=0
:

\langle H_{\rm BCS}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}2\varepsilon^{s}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}v^{2}_{{\bf k}\lambda}-\frac{|\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}|^{2}}{V_{0}};\;\;\;\;\;\;\;E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}>0\\
\varepsilon_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}(b)+\frac{|\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}|^{2}}{V_{0}};\;\;\;\;\;\;\quad E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0\\
\varepsilon_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}\lambda}(-b)+\frac{|\Delta_{{\bf q}\lambda}|^{2}}{V_{0}};\;\;\;\;\;E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0\end{array}\right\}.\\

(64)

Then, using similar small-q approximation as in Sec.
IV
, we arrive at the identity

\frac{\partial\langle H_{\rm BCS}\rangle}{\partial q}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf k}\lambda}\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}2qv^{2}_{{\bf k}\lambda};\;\;\;E^{\tau}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}>0\\
q+k;\;\;\;E^{+}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0\\
q-k;\;\;\;E^{-}_{{\bf k}{\bf q}\lambda}<0\end{array}\right\}=\langle J^{c}_{q}\rangle.

(65)

Therefore, similar as in the inversion symmetric case the charge current is the momentum gradient of the total energy, cf. Eq.(
63
). We conclude that also in the presence of the Rashba coupling we have vanishing charge current
\langle J^{c}_{q}\rangle=0
in the ground state. This situation may be different for the spin current which is already nonzero in the zero field phase
of the Rashba superconductor
Vorontsov et al. 2008
.

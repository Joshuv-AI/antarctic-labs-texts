A set of q \displaystyle q -coherent states for the Rogers-Szegő oscillator
Zouhaïr Mouayn, Othmane El Moize

Abstract
We discuss a model of a q \displaystyle q -harmonic oscillator based on Rogers-Szegő functions. We combine these functions with a class of q \displaystyle q -analogs of complex Hermite polynomials to construct a new set of coherent states depending on a nonnegative integer parameter m \displaystyle m . Our construction leads to a new q \displaystyle q -deformation of the m \displaystyle m -true-polyanalytic Bargmann transform whose range defines a generalization of the Arik-Coon space. We also give an explicit formula for the reproducing kernel of this space. The obtained results may be exploited to define a q \displaystyle q -deformation of the Ginibre- m \displaystyle m -type process on the complex plane.

Introduction

Coherent states (CS) are a type of quantum states which were first introduced
by Schrödinger
[
1
]
when he described certain states of the harmonic oscillator (HO). Later,
Glauber
[
2
]
used these states for his quantum mechanical description of coherent
laser light, and coined the term CS. Like canonical CS, they satisfy
a set of relevant properties and they too have found wide applications in different branches of physics such as quantum optics, statistical
mechanics, nuclear physics and condensed matter physics
[
3
]
. One of many definitions of CS is a special superposition with the form

\Psi_{z}:=\left(e^{z\bar{z}}\right)^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\bar{z}^{j}}{\sqrt{j!}}\phi_{j},

(1.1)

where the
\displaystyle\phi_{j}
’s span a Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}
usually called Fock space.

The terminology of generalized CS (GCS) was first appeared and studied in
[
4
,
5
]
in connection with states discussed in
[
6
]
. These states are usually associated with potential algebras other than the
oscillator one
[
3
,
7
,
8
]
. An important example is provided by the
\displaystyle q
-deformed CS (
\displaystyle q
-CS for brevity) related to deformations of boson
operators
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.
\displaystyle q
-CS are usually constructed in a way that
they reduce to their standard counterparts as
\displaystyle q\rightarrow 1
and are
special case of a more general class, the so-called
\displaystyle f
-deformed CS
[
12
]
. Among
\displaystyle q
-CS, there are those associated with the relation

a_{q}a_{q}^{\ast}-qa_{q}^{\ast}a_{q}=1

(1.2)

with
\displaystyle 0<q<1
, where
\displaystyle a_{q}
are often termed maths-type
\displaystyle q
-bosons operators
[
13
,
14
]
because the
basic
numbers and special functions attached
to them have been extensively studied in mathematics
[
15
]
. As a consequence, the corresponding wavefunctions of these
\displaystyle q
-boson operators were found in terms of many orthogonal
\displaystyle q
-polynomials
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

The
\displaystyle q
-CS may be defined
[
9
]
through a
\displaystyle q
-analog of the expansion (
1.1
) by a superposition of a set of number states
\displaystyle\phi_{j}^{(q)}
spaning a Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{q}
which stands for a
\displaystyle q
-analog of the Fock space
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}
as :

\Psi_{z}^{q}:=\left(e_{q}(z\bar{z})\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\bar{z}^{j}}{\sqrt{[j]_{q}!}}\phi_{j}^{(q)},

(1.3)

for
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C}_{q}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C},\>(1-q)\zeta\bar{\zeta}<1\}
where
\displaystyle[j]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{j}}{1-q}\rightarrow j
as
\displaystyle q\rightarrow 1
,

[j]_{q}!=\frac{(q;q)_{j}}{(1-q)^{j}}

(1.4)

denotes the
\displaystyle q
-factorial of
\displaystyle j
and

e_{q}(\xi):=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\xi^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}=\frac{1}{((1-q)\xi;q)_{\infty}},\quad|\xi|<\frac{1}{1-q}

(1.5)

being a
\displaystyle q
-exponential function satisfying
\displaystyle e_{q}(\xi)\to e^{\xi}
as
\displaystyle q\to 1
. For details on basic notations of
\displaystyle q
-calculus we refer to
[

,
20
,
29

In this paper, we adopt the

(1.6)

(1.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle P_{n}(x;a|q)={}_{2}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}q^{-n},0\\

Here,

[

\displaystyle z^{j}/\sqrt{[j]_{q}!}

\displaystyle q

Here, we introduce an explicit realization of

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle q

2
A CS formalism

Here, we adopt the prototypical model of CS presented in (
[
19
]
, pp.72-77) and described as follows. Let
\displaystyle X
be a set equipped with a measure
\displaystyle d\mu
and
\displaystyle L^{2}(X)
the Hilbert space of
\displaystyle d\mu
-square integrable functions

\displaystyle f(x)
on
\displaystyle X
. Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}\subset L^{2}(X)
be a closed subspace with an orthonormal basis
\displaystyle\{\Phi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
such that

\mathcal{N}(x):=\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}|\Phi_{j}(x)|^{2}<+\infty,\qquad x\in X.

(2.1)

Let
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}
be another (functional) Hilbert space with the same dimension as
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}
and
\displaystyle\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

is a given orthonormal basis of
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}
. Then, consider the family of states
\displaystyle\{\Psi_{x}\}_{x\in X}
in
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}
, through the following linear superpositions

\Psi_{x}:=(\mathcal{N}(x))^{-1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\Phi_{j}(x)\phi_{j}.

(2.2)

These CS obey the normalization condition

\langle\Psi_{x}|\Psi_{x}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}=1

(2.3)

and the following resolution of the identity operator on
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}

\textbf{1}_{\mathscr{H}}=\int_{X}|\Psi_{x}\rangle\langle\Psi_{x}|\mathcal{N}(x)d\mu(x)

(2.4)

which is expressed in terms of Dirac’s bra-ket notation
\displaystyle|\Psi_{x}\rangle\langle\Psi_{x}|
meaning the rank one operator defined by
\displaystyle\varphi\mapsto\langle\Psi_{x}|\varphi\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}\cdot\Psi_{x}
,
\displaystyle\varphi\in\mathscr{H}
. The choice of the Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}
defines in fact a quantization of the space
\displaystyle X
by
the coherent states in (
2.2
), via the inclusion map
\displaystyle X\ni x\mapsto\Psi_{x}\in\mathscr{H}
and the property
(
2.4
) is crucial in setting the bridge between the classical and the quantum worlds. The CST associated with the set
\displaystyle\Psi_{x}
is the map
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:\mathscr{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{2}
defined for every
\displaystyle x\in X
by

\mathcal{B}[\phi](x):=\left(\mathcal{N}(x)\right)^{1/2}\langle\phi,\Psi_{x}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}.

(2.5)

From the resolution of the identity (
2.4
) and for
\displaystyle\phi,\,\psi\in\mathscr{H}
, we have
\displaystyle\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\mathscr{H}}=\langle\mathcal{B}[\phi],\mathcal{B}[\psi]\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}

meaning that
\displaystyle\mathcal{B}
is an isometric map.

The formula (
2.2
) can be considered as a generalization of the series expansion of the
canonical CS in (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
being an orthonormal basis of the Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathscr{H}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, consisting of eigenstates of the quantum HO given by

\phi_{j}(\xi)=\left(\sqrt{\pi}2^{j}j!\right)^{-1/2}H_{j}(\xi)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}

(2.6)

where

H_{j}(\xi):=j!\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!(j-2k)!}(2\xi)^{j-2k}

(2.7)

is the Hermite polynomial of degree
\displaystyle j
(
[
20
]
, p.59). Here, the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}
is the Bargmann-Fock space
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}(\mathbb{C})
of entire complex-valued functions which are
\displaystyle\pi^{-1}e^{-z\bar{z}}d\lambda
-square integrable,
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(z)=e^{z\bar{z}}
,
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C}
and
\displaystyle d\lambda
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In this case, the associated CST

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathfrak{F}(\mathbb{C})
, defined for any function
\displaystyle f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by

\mathcal{B}_{0}[f](z):=\pi^{-\tfrac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{2}+\sqrt{2}\xi z-\tfrac{1}{2}z^{2}}f(\xi)d\xi,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}

(2.8)

turns out to be the well known Bargmann transform (
[
30
]
, p.12).

3
The Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{q}

We observe that the coefficients

\Phi_{j}^{q}(z)=\frac{z^{j}}{\sqrt{[j]_{q}!}},\quad j=0,1,2,\ldots,

(3.1)

occurring in the number state expansion of
\displaystyle\Psi_{z}^{q}
in (
1.3
) constitute a particular case of the complex-valued functions
\displaystyle\Phi_{j}^{q,m}(z)
in (
1.7
). Indeed, for
\displaystyle m=0
we have that
\displaystyle\Phi_{j}^{q,0}(z)=\Phi_{j}^{q}(z)
. For
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
, these coefficients are a slight modification of the
\displaystyle 2D
complex
orthogonal polynomials (
[
21
]
, p.6783) :

H_{m,j}(z,\zeta|q):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m\wedge j}\begin{bmatrix}m\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}\begin{bmatrix}j\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k}{2}}(q;q)_{k}z^{m-k}\zeta^{j-k},\ z,\zeta\in\mathbb{C}.

(3.2)

Precisely,

\Phi_{j}^{q,m}(z):=\frac{H_{m,j}(\sqrt{1-q}z,\sqrt{1-q}\bar{z}|q)}{\sqrt{q^{mj}(q;q)_{j}(q;q)_{m}}}.

(3.3)

Note, also, that by using (
1.4
), one can check that
\displaystyle\Phi_{j}^{q,m}(z)\rightarrow\left(m!j!\right)^{-1/2}H_{m,j}(z,\bar{z})
as
\displaystyle q\to 1
, where

H_{m,j}(z,\zeta)=\sum_{k=0}^{m\wedge j}(-1)^{k}k!\binom{m}{k}\binom{j}{k}z^{m-k}\zeta^{j-k}

(3.4)

denote the
\displaystyle 2D
complex Hermite polynomials introduced by Itô
[
22
]
in the context of complex Markov processes.

Furthermore, we observe that the functions
\displaystyle\Phi_{j}^{q,m}(z)
constitute an orthonormal system in the Hilbert space
\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{q}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{q,m},d\mu_{q}(z))
of square integrable functions on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{q,m}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C},\>(1-q)\zeta\bar{\zeta}<q^{m}\}
with respect to the measure

d\mu_{q}(z)=\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\otimes\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{q^{l}(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{l}}\delta(r-\frac{q^{\tfrac{1}{2}l}}{\sqrt{1-q}}),

(3.5)

\displaystyle z=re^{i\theta},\ r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\displaystyle\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and
\displaystyle\delta(r-\bullet)
is the Dirac mass function at the point
\displaystyle\bullet
. Indeed, we may assume that
\displaystyle m\geq j
because of the symmetry
\displaystyle H_{r,s}(z,w|q)=H_{s,r}(w,z|q)
, then we use the expression (
1.7
) to obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle\Phi_{j}^{q,m},\Phi_{k}^{q,m}\rangle_{\mathfrak{F}_{q}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}_{q,m}}\Phi_{j}^{q,m}(z)\overline{\Phi_{k}^{q,m}}(z)d\mu_{q}(z)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(-1)^{j+k}\frac{(q;q)_{m}q^{\binom{j}{2}}\sqrt{1-q}^{m-j}}{(q;q)_{m-j}(q;q)_{m}\sqrt{q^{mj}(q;q)_{j}}}\frac{(q;q)_{m}q^{\binom{k}{2}}\sqrt{1-q}^{m-k}}{(q;q)_{m-k}\sqrt{q^{mk}(q;q)_{k}}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i\theta(j-k)}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{l}(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{l}}P_{j}(r^{2},q^{m-j}|q)P_{k}(r^{2},q^{m-k}|q)r^{2m-j-k}\delta(r-\frac{q^{\tfrac{l}{2}}}{\sqrt{1-q}})

=\frac{(-1)^{j+k}(q;q)_{m}q^{\binom{j}{2}+\binom{k}{2}-mj}}{(q;q)_{j}(q;q)_{m-j}(q;q)_{m-k}}\delta_{j,k}\;\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{l(1+m-j)}(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{l}}P_{j}(q^{l},q^{m-j}|q)P_{k}(q^{l},q^{m-j}|q)=\delta_{j,k}.

(3.6)

Here, we have used the orthogonality relations of Wall polynomials (
[
20
]
, p.107):

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\tau q)^{l}}{(q;q)_{l}}P_{s}(q^{l};\tau|q)P_{n}(q^{l};\tau|q)=\frac{(\tau q)^{n}}{(\tau q;q)_{\infty}}\frac{(q;q)_{n}}{(\tau q;q)_{n}}\ \delta_{s,n},\quad 0<\tau<q^{-1}

(3.7)

for parameters
\displaystyle\tau=q^{m-j}
,
\displaystyle s=j
and
\displaystyle n=k
.

Finally, we denote by
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}_{q}(\mathbb{C})
the completed space of holomorphic functions on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{q}=\mathbb{C}_{q,0}
,
equipped with the scalar product

\langle\varphi,\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{C}_{q}}{\varphi(z)}\overline{\phi(z)}d\mu_{q}(z).

(3.8)

The element
\displaystyle\Phi_{j}^{q,0}(z)=([j]_{q}!)^{-1/2}z^{j}
form an orthonormal
basis of the space
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{2}_{q}(\mathbb{C})
which coïncides
with Arik-Coon space
[
9
]
whose reproducing kernel is given by

K_{q}(z,w):=e_{q}(z\bar{w}),\qquad z,w\in\mathbb{C}_{q}.

(3.9)

Note that by letting
\displaystyle q\rightarrow 1
, the measure
\displaystyle d\mu_{q}
reduces to the Gaussian measure
\displaystyle\pi^{-1}e^{-z\bar{z}}d\lambda
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}
.

4
A Rogers-Szegő Hamiltonian

Following
[
31
]
, we consider a
\displaystyle q
-deformed creation and annihilation operators as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle B^{\ast}_{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\kappa x}}{i\sqrt{1-q}}\left(e^{i\kappa x}-q^{3/4}e^{i\kappa\partial_{x}}\right)

(4.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle B_{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{-e^{-i\kappa x}}{i\sqrt{1-q}}\left(e^{-i\kappa x}-q^{1/4}e^{i\kappa\partial_{x}}\right).

(4.2)

Here
\displaystyle\kappa
is a deformation parameter related to a finite-difference method with
respect to
\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle q=e^{-2\kappa^{2}},\>\kappa>0
. Note that for
\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{C}
the operator
\displaystyle e^{a\partial_{x}}
acts on a function
\displaystyle f(x)
as
\displaystyle e^{a\partial_{x}}[f](x)=f(x+a).
Moreover, it is not difficult to verify that operators (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) satisfy the
\displaystyle q
-commutation relation

[B_{q},B^{\ast}_{q}]=B_{q}B^{\ast}_{q}-qB^{\ast}_{q}B_{q}=1.

(4.3)

Similarly to the case of the quantum HO, the
\displaystyle q
-deformed HO is described by the Hamiltonian

H_{q}=\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega\left(B_{q}B^{\ast}_{q}+B^{\ast}_{q}B_{q}\right)

(4.4)

where
\displaystyle\omega
is the oscillator frequency and
\displaystyle\hbar
denotes the Planck’s constant. Explicitly,

H_{q}=\frac{1}{2(q-1)}\hbar\omega\left(-2+(q^{1/4}+q^{5/4})e^{i\kappa x}e^{i\kappa\partial_{x}}+(q^{-1/4}+q^{3/4})e^{-i\kappa x}e^{i\kappa\partial_{x}}+(q^{3/2}+q^{1/2})e^{2i\kappa\partial_{x}}\right).

(4.5)

For the sake of simplicity we will take
\displaystyle\hbar=\omega=1
. The eigenstates of the Hamiltonian in (
4.5
) are given by

\varphi_{j}^{RS}(x):=\frac{(i\sqrt{q})^{j}}{\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{(q;q)_{j}}}H_{j}\left(-e^{2i\kappa x};q\right)e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}},\;x\in\mathbb{R}

(4.6)

in terms of the Rogers–Szegő polynomials (
[
29
]
, p.455)

H_{n}(\xi;q):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\right]_{q}(q^{-1/2}\xi)^{k},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{C}.

(4.7)

It was proved (
[
31
]
, p.612) that the functions
\displaystyle\varphi_{j}^{RS}
satisfy the orthonormality relations on the full real line, i.e.,

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{j}^{RS}(x)\varphi_{k}^{RS}(x)dx=\delta_{jk}.

(4.8)

Furthermore, each of these functions can also be reproduced by
iterating
\displaystyle j
times the action of the
\displaystyle q
-creation operator
\displaystyle B_{q}^{*}
on the Gaussian function as

\varphi_{j}^{RS}(x)=\left(B_{q}^{\ast}\right)^{j}\,\left(\pi^{\frac{-1}{4}}e^{\tfrac{-1}{2}x^{2}}\right)

(4.9)

and one can check that the operators
\displaystyle B_{q}^{\ast}
and
\displaystyle B_{q}
act on them as

B^{\ast}_{q}\varphi_{j}^{RS}(x)=\varphi_{j+1}^{RS}(x)\quad,\qquad B_{q}\varphi_{j}^{RS}(x)=[j]_{q}\varphi_{j-1}^{RS}(x).

(4.10)

Then, it follows that the Hamiltonian (
4.4
) is diagonal on these states and has the eigenvalues

\varepsilon_{j}^{q}=\frac{1}{2}\left([j+1]_{q}+[j]_{q}\right)

(4.11)

as
\displaystyle q
-deformed energy levels.

Finally, let us mention that in the limit
\displaystyle q\to 1
(
\displaystyle\kappa\to 0
), the operators (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) reduce to the well-known non relativistic HO creation and annihilation operators. Also, from (
[
31
]
, p.614) one has that

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}\varphi_{j}^{RS}(x)=\left(\sqrt{\pi}2^{j}j!\right)^{-1/2}H_{j}(x)e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}}

(4.12)

where
\displaystyle H_{j}(\cdot)
is the Hermite polynomial (
2.7
), meaning that the
\displaystyle\varphi_{j}^{RS}
’s stand for
\displaystyle q
-analogs of the HO wave functions which justify our choice in (
4.6
).

5
A new set of G
\displaystyle q
-CS

For
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle q\in]0,1[
, we define a new set of G
\displaystyle q
-CS by the following superposition of the
\displaystyle\varphi_{j}^{RS}
’s:

\Psi_{z}^{q,m}:=(\mathcal{N}_{q,m}(z\bar{z}))^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\overline{\Phi_{j}^{q,m}(z)}\varphi_{j}^{RS}

(5.1)

where

\mathcal{N}_{q,m}(z\bar{z})=\frac{q^{-m}(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{m}}{(q^{-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{\infty}}

(5.2)

is the normalization factor ensuring
\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{z}^{q,m}|\Psi_{z}^{q,m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1

Using the normalization factor (

We now establish (see Appendix A for the proof) that the wave function of the CS (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Psi_{z}^{q,m}(\xi)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\left(\frac{q^{m}\,e^{-\xi^{2}}(q^{-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{\infty}}{\sqrt{\pi}(q;q)_{m}(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{m}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\frac{q^{-m/4}e^{im\kappa\xi}}{(iz\sqrt{\tfrac{1-q}{q^{m-1}}},-iz\sqrt{\tfrac{1-q}{q^{m}}}e^{2i\kappa\xi};q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle Q_{m}\left(\frac{ie^{-i\kappa\xi}q^{1/4}-ie^{i\kappa\xi}q^{-1/4}}{2};zq^{-1/4}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-q}{q^{m-1}}e^{i\kappa\xi}},\bar{z}q^{1/4}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-q}{q^{m-1}}e^{-i\kappa\xi}}\>\;|q\right),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{q}^{m}(\alpha;\beta)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m}\lambda)^{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}\,\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{k}}{(\alpha q^{1-m};q)_{k}}\,\frac{(q^{j+1})^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{l}}{(\beta q^{1-m};q)_{l}}\,\frac{(q^{j+1})^{l}}{(q;q)_{l}}

(5.49)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k,l\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{k}}{(\alpha q^{1-m};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{l}}{(\beta q^{1-m};q)_{l}}\,\frac{q^{l}}{(q;q)_{l}}\,\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m+k+l}\lambda)^{j}}{(q;q)_{j}}.

(5.50)

Now, by applying the
\displaystyle q
-binomial theorem

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(a;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}\xi^{n}=\frac{(a\xi;q)_{\infty}}{(\xi;q)_{\infty}},\quad|\xi|<1

(5.51)

for
\displaystyle\xi=q^{-m+k+l}\lambda
and
\displaystyle a=0
, the last sum reads

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{q}^{m}(\alpha;\beta)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k,l\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{k}}{(\alpha q^{1-m};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{l}}{(\beta q^{1-m};q)_{l}}\,\frac{q^{l}}{(q;q)_{l}}\,\frac{1}{(q^{-m+k+l}\lambda;q)_{\infty}}.

(5.52)

By applying the identity (
5.31
) to the factor
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{-m+k+l}\lambda;q)_{\infty}}
, Eq.(
5.52
) can be rewritten as

\mathsf{S}_{q}^{m}(\alpha;\beta)=\frac{1}{(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{k}}{(\alpha q^{1-m};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{l}(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{k+l}}{(\beta q^{1-m};q)_{l}}\,\frac{q^{l}}{(q;q)_{l}}.

(5.53)

Next, by writing
\displaystyle(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{l+k}=(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{k}(q^{k-m}\lambda;q)_{l}
, Eq.(
5.53
) transforms to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{q}^{m}(\alpha;\beta)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m},q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{k}}{(\alpha q^{1-m};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m},q^{k-m}\lambda;q)_{l}}{(\beta q^{1-m};q)_{l}}\,\frac{q^{l}}{(q;q)_{l}}

(5.54)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m},q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{k}}{(\alpha q^{1-m};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-m},q^{k-m}\lambda\\
\beta q^{1-m}\end{array}\Big|q;q\right),

in terms of the
\displaystyle{}_{2}\phi_{1}
series. The latter one satisfies the identity (
[
15
]
, p.10):

{}_{2}\phi_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-n},b\\
c\end{array}\Big|q;q\right)=\frac{(b^{-1}c;q)_{n}}{(c;q)_{n}}b^{n},\quad n=0,1,2,....

(5.58)

Setting
\displaystyle n=m
,
\displaystyle b=q^{k-m}\lambda
and
\displaystyle c=\beta q^{1-m}
in (
5.58
) allows us to rewrite (
5.54
) as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{q}^{m}(\alpha;\beta)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m},q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{k}}{(\alpha q^{1-m};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\,\frac{(q^{1-k}\frac{\beta}{\lambda};q)_{m}}{(q^{1-m}\beta;q)_{m}}(q^{k-m}\lambda)^{m}.

(5.59)

Furthermore, applying the identity

(aq^{-n};q)_{k}=\frac{(a^{-1}q;q)_{n}}{(a^{-1}q^{1-k};q)_{n}}(a;q)_{k}q^{-nk},\quad a\neq 0

(5.60)

to
\displaystyle(q^{1-k}\frac{\beta}{\lambda};q)_{m}
, leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{q}^{m}(\alpha;\beta)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(q^{-m}\lambda)^{m}(q\frac{{w}}{{z}};q)_{m}}{(q^{1-m}\beta;q)_{m}(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m},q^{-m}\lambda,\frac{{z}}{{w}};q)_{k}}{(q^{1-m}\alpha,q^{-m}\frac{{z}}{{w}};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}.

(5.61)

We now summarize the above calculations as follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle K_{q,m}(z,w)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{q^{-m}(q^{1-m}\alpha;q)_{m}(q\frac{{w}}{{z}};q)_{m}}{(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{\infty}(q;q)_{m}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(q^{-m},q^{-m}\lambda,\frac{{z}}{{w}};q)_{k}}{(q^{1-m}\alpha,q^{-m}\frac{{z}}{{w}};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}

(5.62)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(q^{1-m}\alpha;q)_{m}(q\frac{{w}}{{z}};q)_{m}}{q^{m}(q^{-m}\lambda;q)_{\infty}(q;q)_{m}}\,{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-m},q^{-m}\lambda,\frac{{z}}{{w}}\\
q^{1-m}\alpha,q^{-m}\frac{z}{w}\end{array}\Big|q;q\right).

By making appeal to the finite Heine transformation (
[
39
]
, p.2):

{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-n},\xi,\beta\\
\gamma,q^{1-n}/\tau\end{array}\Big|q;q\right)=\frac{(\xi\,\tau;q)_{n}}{(\tau;q)_{n}}\;{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-n},\gamma/\beta,\xi\\
\gamma,\xi\,\tau\end{array}\Big|q;\beta\,\tau q^{n}\right)

(5.66)

for the parameters
\displaystyle\xi=z/w,\;\beta=q^{-m}\lambda,\;\gamma=q^{1-m}\alpha,\;\tau=qw/z
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055ptK_{q,m}(z,w)=\frac{(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{m}}{q^{m}(q^{-m}(1-q)z\bar{w};q)_{\infty}}{}_{3}\phi_{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q^{-m},\frac{{z}}{{w}},q\frac{\bar{z}}{\bar{w}}\\
q,q^{-m+1}(1-q)z\bar{z}\end{array}\Big|q;q(1-q)w\bar{w}\right).

For the limit of
\displaystyle K_{q,m}(z,w)
as
\displaystyle q\to 1
, we may use (
1.5
) to see that

\lim_{q\to 1}\frac{(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{m}}{q^{m}(q^{-m}(1-q)z\bar{w};q)_{\infty}}=\lim_{q\to 1}q^{-m}(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{m}\;e_{q}(q^{-m}z\bar{w})=\mathrm{exp}\left(z\bar{w}\right).

(5.70)

By another side, we observe that
\displaystyle(q^{-m};q)_{k}=0,\forall k>m
, meaning that
\displaystyle{}_{3}\phi_{2}
series in (
5
) terminates as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(q^{-m},z/w,q\bar{z}/\bar{w};q)_{k}}{(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z},q;q)_{k}}\,\frac{(q(1-q)w\bar{w})^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}.

(5.71)

Thus, from the identity

\begin{bmatrix}s\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}:=(-1)^{k}q^{ks-\binom{k}{2}}\frac{(q^{-{s}};q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k}},\>s\in\mathbb{C}

(5.72)

together with (
1.4
) we, successively, have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 1}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(q^{-m},z/w,q\bar{z}/\bar{w};q)_{k}}{(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z},q;q)_{k}}\,\frac{(q(1-q)w\bar{w})^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}\lim_{q\to 1}\left(\frac{(q^{-m};q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\frac{(z/w,q\bar{z}/\bar{w};q)_{k}}{(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{k}}\,\frac{(1-q)^{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\,q^{k}(w\bar{w})^{k}\right)

(5.73)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}\lim_{q\to 1}\left(\begin{bmatrix}m\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}(-1)^{k}q^{\binom{k}{2}-mk}\frac{(z/w,q\bar{z}/\bar{w};q)_{k}}{(q^{1-m}(1-q)z\bar{z};q)_{k}}\,\frac{q^{k}(w\bar{w)}^{k}}{[k]_{q}!}\right)

(5.74)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\binom{m}{k}\frac{|z-w|^{2k}}{k!}.

(5.75)

Finally, by noticing that the last sum in (
5.73
) is the evaluation of the Laguerre polynomial
\displaystyle L_{m}^{(0)}
at
\displaystyle|z-w|^{2}
(
[
20
]
, p.108), the proof is completed.
\displaystyle\square

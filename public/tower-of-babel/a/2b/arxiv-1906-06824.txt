Maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules over a noncommutative 2-dimensional singularity
X.-S. Qin, Y.-H. Wang and J.J. Zhang

Abstract
. We study properties of graded maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules over an ℕ {\mathbb{N}} -graded locally finite, Auslander Gorenstein, and Cohen-Macaulay algebra of dimension two. As a consequence, we extend a part of the McKay correspondence in dimension two to a more general setting.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 16E65, 16S38, 14A22

0.
Introduction

A noncommutative version of the McKay correspondence in dimension
two was developed in
[
CKWZ1
,
CKWZ2
,
CKWZ3
]
. One of the main
ingredients was the study of the invariant subrings of connected
graded, noetherian, Artin-Schelter regular algebras of global
dimension two under natural actions of quantum binary polyhedral
groups. The McKay quivers
[
CKWZ3
, Definition 2.9]
of these
quantum binary polyhedral groups are twisted versions of

\widetilde{A}\widetilde{D}\widetilde{E}
graphs where the details
can be found in
[
CKWZ1
, Proposition 7.1]
. It was proved in

[
CKWZ3
, Theorem B]
that the McKay quiver is isomorphic to the
Gabriel quiver
[
CKWZ3
, Definition 2.8]
of the smash product
algebra corresponding to the action.

The noncommutative singularities (or equivalently, their associated
algebras) studied in
[
CKWZ1
,
CKWZ2
,
CKWZ3
]
are usually far from
commutative and do not satisfy a polynomial identity. For these
noncommutative singularities we introduced the concept of a
noncommutative quasi-resolution
[
QWZ
, Definition 0.5]
which
generalizes Van den Bergh’s noncommutative crepant resolution

[
VdB1
,
VdB2
]
. The smash product constructions used in

[
CKWZ1
,
CKWZ2
,
CKWZ3
]
are examples of noncommutative
quasi-resolutions. Recently Reyes-Rogalski proved that the Gabriel
quivers of non-connected,
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded, Artin-Schelter regular
algebras of global dimension two are twisted versions
(which are called
pretzeled quivers
in this paper) of the

\widetilde{A}\widetilde{D}\widetilde{E}
graphs.

Recent study of the invariant theory of (non-connected graded)
preprojective algebras under finite group actions initiated by
Weispfenning
[
We
]
suggests that one should extend the
noncommutative McKay correspondence to a larger class of not
necessarily connected, graded algebras. The aim of this short
paper is to supply a small piece of the puzzle in this slightly
more general version of the noncommutative McKay correspondence.
Let
\Bbbk
be a base field and let MCM (respectively, CM)
stand for “maximal Cohen-Macaulay” (respectively,
“Cohen-Macaulay”). We summarize the main results as follows.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
A
be a noetherian
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded locally finite
algebra of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension two. Suppose that

(a)

A
has a balanced dualizing complex,

(b)

A
is Auslander Gorenstein and CM, and

(c)

A
has a noncommutative quasi-resolution
B
.

Then

(1)

A
is of finite Cohen-Macaulay type in the graded sense.

(2)

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of
indecomposable MCM graded right
A
-modules up to degree shifts
and isomorphisms and the set of graded simple right
B
-modules
up to degree shifts and isomorphisms.

(3)

Let
\{M_{1},\cdots,M_{d}\}
be a complete list of the
indecomposable MCM graded right
A
-modules up to degree shifts
and isomorphisms. Then, for some choice of integers

w_{1},\cdots,w_{d}
,
C:=\operatorname{End}_{A}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}M_{i}(w_{i}))

is an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded noncommutative quasi-resolution of
A
.
As a consequence,
C
is graded Morita equivalent to
B
.

(4)

A
is a noncommutative graded isolated singularity.

One should compare Theorem
0.1
with results in

[
He
, Section 3]
in the commutative case and

[
Jo
, Theorem 2.5]
,
[
CKWZ3
, Corollary 4.5 and Theorem 5.7]

in the noncommutative case.

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
A
satisfy the hypotheses in
Theorem
0.1
. Further assume that the noncommutative
quasi-resolution
B
in Theorem
0.1
(c) is standard in the
sense of Definition
1.3
. Then the following holds.

(1)

The Gabriel quiver of
B
is a pretzeled quiver of a finite
union of graphs of
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{D}\widetilde{E}
type.

(2)

The standard noncommutative quasi-resolution
B
is unique
up to isomorphism.

The above theorem confirms that a generalized version of the
noncommutative McKay correspondence should still be within the
framework of
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{D}\widetilde{E}
diagrams.
Since the Gabriel quiver
{\mathcal{G}}(B)
of
B
is defined by
using simple modules over
B
, by the correspondence given in
Theorem
0.1
(3) and the uniqueness in Theorem

0.2
(2),
{\mathcal{G}}(B)
(if it exists) is also an
invariant of MCM modules over
A
. The proof of Theorem

0.2
follows from Theorem
0.1
and results of
Reyes-Rogalski when one relates the results in
[
RR1
,
RR2
]
with
the concept of noncommutative quasi-resolutions.

Terminology used in the above theorems will be explained in
later sections. The proofs of Theorems
0.1
and

0.2
will be given in Section 3.

1.1.
Generalized Artin-Schelter regular algebras

In this subsection, we review the definition of a generalized
Artin-Schelter (AS) regular algebra.

Definition 1.1
.

[
RR1
, Definition 1.4]

Let
A
be a locally finite graded algebra and
J:=J(A)
be the
graded Jacobson radical, that is, the intersection of all graded
maximal right ideals of
A
. Write
S=A/J
. We say that
A
is

generalized AS Gorenstein of dimension
d
if
A
has
graded injective dimension
d
and there is a graded invertible

(S,S)
-bimodule
V
such that

(E1.1.1)

{\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(S,A)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}V,&\mbox{if $i=d$},\\
0,&\mbox{if $i\neq d$},\end{array}\right.

as
(S,S)
-bimodules.
If further
A
has graded global dimension
d
, then
A
is called

generalized AS regular of dimension
d
.

Definition 1.2
.

[
RR2
, Section 3]

Let
A
be a locally finite
\mathbb{N}
-graded
\Bbbk
-algebra. If the
finite-dimensional semisimple algebra
S:=A/J(A)\cong A_{0}/J(A_{0})

is isomorphic to a product
\Bbbk^{\oplus d}
of finitely many copies of
the base field
\Bbbk
, then we say that
A
is
elementary
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
A
be a graded algebra. We say
A
is
standard

if
A_{0}
is
\Bbbk^{\oplus d}
for some positive integer
d

and
A
is generated by
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
.

A very nice result proven by Reyes-Rogalski is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
RR2
]

Let
A
be an
{\mathbb{N}}
-graded generalized AS
regular algebra. Suppose that

(a)

A
is standard,

(b)

A
is noetherian,

(c)

{\rm gldim}\,A=2
.

Then
A
is isomorphic to the algebra
A_{2}(Q,\tau)

described in
[
RR2
, Definition 7.5]
where
Q

is a quiver whose arrows all have weight 1 and whose
spectral radius
\rho(Q)
is 2.

Proof.

We combine some results of Reyes-Rogalski.
By
[
RR1
, Theorem 1.5]
,
A
is twisted
Calabi-Yau in the sense of
[
RR1
, Definition 1.2]
.
By
[
RR2
, p.37]
, every locally finite elementary
graded twisted Calabi-Yau algebra of global dimension 2
is isomorphic to
A_{2}(Q,\tau)
. Since
A
is standard,
the weight of every arrow in
Q
is 1. Since
A
is
noetherian, by
[
RR2
, Theorem 7.8(2)]
,
\rho(Q)=2
.
∎

1.2.
Noncommutative quasi-resolutions

In this subsection we review some definitions about
noncommutative quasi-resolutions from
[
QWZ
]
. We assume
that all algebras are noetherian in this subsection.
First we recall the definition of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension.

Definition 1.5
.

[
KL
, Definition 2.1]

Let
A
be an algebra and
M
a right
A
-module.

(1)

The
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
(or
{\rm GKdim}\,
)
of
A
is defined to be

{\rm GKdim}\,A=\sup_{V}\{\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\log_{n}(\dim V^{n})\mid V\subseteq A\}

where
V
ranges over all finite dimensional
\Bbbk
-subspaces of
A
.

(2)

The
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
(or
{\rm GKdim}\,
)
of
M
is defined to be

{\rm GKdim}\,M=\sup_{V,W}\{\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\log_{n}(\dim WV^{n})\mid V\subseteq A,W\subseteq M\}

where
V
and
W
range over all finite dimensional
\Bbbk
-subspaces
of
A
and
M
respectively.

If
M
is a finitely generated graded module over a locally finite
graded algebra
A
, then its
{\rm GKdim}\,
can be computed by
[
Zh
, (E7)]

{\rm GKdim}\,M=\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\log_{n}\sum\limits_{j\leq n}\dim(M_{j}).

By
[
KL
, Theorem 6.14]
,
{\rm GKdim}\,
is exact for modules over a
locally finite graded algebra.

For simplicity, we always work with the dimension function
{\rm GKdim}\,
in
this paper. Next we specialize some definitions in
[
QWZ
]
to the

{\rm GKdim}\,
case and omit the prefix “
{\rm GKdim}\,
” in some cases.

Definition 1.6
.

[
QWZ
, Definition 1.5]

Let
n\geq 0
. Let
A
and
B
be two locally finite graded algebras.

(1)

Two
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right
A
-modules
X,Y
are called

n
-isomorphic
, denoted by
X\cong_{n}Y
, if there
exist a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right
A
-module
P
and homogeneous
homomorphisms of degree zero
f:X\to P
and
g:Y\to P
such
that both the kernel and cokernel of
f
and
g
are in

\operatorname{GrMod}_{n}A
.

(2)

Two
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
(B,A)
-bimodules
X,Y
are called

n
-isomorphic
, denoted by
X\cong_{n}Y
, if there
exist a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
(B,A)
-bimodule
P
and homogeneous
bimodule homomorphisms with degree zero
f:X\to P

and
g:Y\to P
such that both the kernel and cokernel of

f
and
g
are in
\operatorname{GrMod}_{n}A
when viewed as graded right

A
-modules.

Definition 1.7
.

[
Le
, Definitions 1.2 and 2.1]

Let
A
be an algebra and
M
a right
A
-module.

(1)

The
grade number
of
M
is defined to be

j_{A}(M):=\inf\{i|{\rm Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,A)\neq 0\}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}.

If no confusion can arise, we write
j(M)
for
j_{A}(M)
. Note that

j_{A}(0)=+\infty
.

(2)

We say
M
satisfies the
Auslander condition
if for any
q\geq 0,

j_{A}(N)\geq q
for all left
A
-submodules
N
of
{\rm Ext}_{A}^{q}(M,A)
.

(3)

We say
A
is
Auslander-Gorenstein
(respectively,

Auslander regular
) of dimension

if

\operatorname{injdim}A_{A}=\operatorname{injdim}{{}_{A}A}=n<\infty
(respectively,
{\rm gldim}\,A=n<\infty
)
and every finitely generated left and right

-module satisfies
the Auslander condition.

A graded version of an Auslander-Gorenstein (respectively, Auslander
regular) algebra is defined similarly.

Definition 1.8
.

[
ASZ
, Definition 0.4]

Let
A
be a locally finite graded algebra. We say
A
is
graded
Cohen-Macaulay
(or,
graded CM
in short) if
{\rm GKdim}\,(A)=d\in\mathbb{N},

and

j(M)+{\rm GKdim}\,(M)={\rm GKdim}\,(A)

for every graded finitely generated nonzero left (or right)
A
-module
M
.

Let
A
be a locally finite
\mathbb{N}
-graded algebra and
n
a nonnegative
integer. Let
\operatorname{GrMod}_{n}A
denote the full subcategory of
\operatorname{GrMod}A

consisting of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded right
A
-modules

with
{\rm GKdim}\,(M)\leq n
.
Since

is exact over a noetherian locally finite

-graded

,

\operatorname{QGr}_{n}A:=\frac{\operatorname{GrMod}A}{\operatorname{GrMod}_{n}A}\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{qgr}_{n}A:=\frac{\operatorname{grmod}A}{\operatorname{grmod}_{n}A}.

We denote the natural and exact projection functor by

(E1.8.1)

\pi:\operatorname{GrMod}A\longrightarrow\operatorname{QGr}_{n}A.

For

we write
\mathcal{M}
for the object

in
\operatorname{QGr}_{n}A

{\rm Hom}_{\operatorname{QGr}_{n}A}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=\lim_{\longrightarrow}{\rm Hom}_{A}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})

for

\in\operatorname{GrMod}A

is a graded submodule of

of

is no more than

, and
where the direct limit runs over all the pairs

with these

is also defined from

(E1.8.3)

\operatorname{grmod}A\longrightarrow\operatorname{qgr}_{n}A.

It follows from
[
QWZ
, (E1.10.1)]
that the functor
\pi

in (
E1.8.1
) has a right adjoint functor

(E1.8.4)

\omega:\operatorname{QGr}_{n}A\longrightarrow\operatorname{GrMod}A.

By

, Proposition 2.10(2)]
, when

is
(n+2)
-pure
in the sense of Definition

(6) (in the next
section) over an Auslander Gorenstein and CM algebra

then

agrees with the Gabber closure
defined in
[
QWZ
, Definition 2.8]
.

We will use
\operatorname{qgr}_{0}A
in section 2,
which will be denoted by
\operatorname{qgr}A
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a category consisting of (
\mathbb{N}
-)graded,
locally finite, noetherian algebras with finite
{\rm GKdim}\,

[
QWZ
, Example 3.1]
. Our definition of a noncommutative
quasi-resolution will be made inside the category

.

Definition 1.9
.

[
QWZ
, Definition 0.5]

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
{\rm GKdim}\,(A)=d\geq 2
.
If there are a graded Auslander-regular and
{\rm CM}

and two

-graded bimodules
{}_{B}M_{A}

,

-graded bimodules, then the triple

(or

.

If
A\in\mathcal{A}
with

, then by

[

, Theorem 4.2 and Lemma 8.2]

Lemma 1.10
.

If
A
is noetherian, graded, locally finite, Auslander

is generalized AS regular.

Proof.

By the Auslander and CM properties, for every
finite dimensional graded right

{\rm Ext}^{i}_{A}(M,A)=\begin{cases}0&i\neq d:={\rm gldim}\,A,\\
N&i=d\end{cases}

By the double-Ext spectral sequence

,

induces a bijection from the set of graded

∎

2.
Preparations

In this section we will give the relation between
Gabriel quivers, pretzeled quivers of graphs, and
noncommutative quasi-resolutions (NQRs).

2.1.
Gabriel Quivers

Let
Q
be a quiver that has finitely many vertices and arrows.
If we label the vertices of
Q
by integers from
1
to
d
,
then the adjacency matrix of
Q
is a square
d\times d
-matrix
over
{\mathbb{N}}
. It is clear that there is a one-to-one
correspondence between

(E2.0.1)

\{{\text{quivers with vertices labeled $\{1,2,\cdots,d\}$}}\}\Longleftrightarrow\{{\text{$d\times d$-matrices over ${\mathbb{N}}$.}}\}

For this reason, the adjacency matrix of
Q
is also denoted by

Q
if no confusion occurs. The opposite quiver of
Q
is
obtained by changing the direction of each arrow in
Q
. Hence
the adjacency matrix of the opposite quiver of
Q
is the
transpose of the adjacency matrix of
Q
. We denote the opposite
quiver of
Q
by
Q^{op}
.

Next we review the definition of a Gabriel quiver. Suppose that

A
is locally finite graded and elementary with

S=A/J(A)=\Bbbk^{\oplus d}
. One can lift (not necessarily uniquely) the

d
primitive orthogonal idempotents of
S
to an orthogonal
family of primitive idempotents with
1=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{n}
in

A_{0}
, see
[
Lam
, Corollary 21.32]
. Notice that

A=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}A
, so every
e_{i}A
is a graded
projective right
A
-module, which is an indecomposable module
since
e_{i}
is a primitive idempotent. Then we get
d
distinct
simple right
A
-modules
S_{i}:=e_{i}S=e_{i}A/e_{i}J(A)
, and every
simple graded right module is isomorphic to a shift of one of
the
S_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq d
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be an elementary, locally finite, graded algebra such that

A/J(A)=\Bbbk^{\oplus d}
. The
Gabriel quiver

\mathcal{G}(A)
of
A
is defined by

\bullet

vertices: graded simple right
A
-modules
S_{1},\ldots,S_{d}

corresponding to individual projection to
\Bbbk
.

\bullet

arrows:
S_{i}\xrightarrow{q_{ij}}S_{j}
if
q_{ij}=\dim_{\Bbbk}{\rm Ext}_{A}^{1}(S_{j},S_{i})_{-1}
where
{\rm Ext}_{A}^{1}(S_{j},S_{i})

has a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading.

Under the identification in (
E2.0.1
),
\mathcal{G}(A)=(q_{ij})_{d\times d}
where
q_{ij}
is defined above.

Note that if
A
in the above definition is Koszul in the sense of

[
MV
, Definition 1.5]
, then
{\rm Ext}_{A}^{1}(S_{j},S_{i})
is
concentrated in degree
-1
. In this case,

q_{ij}=\dim_{\Bbbk}{\rm Ext}_{A}^{1}(S_{j},S_{i})_{-1}=\dim_{\Bbbk}{\rm Ext}_{A}^{1}(S_{j},S_{i}).

Remark 2.2
.

Suppose
A_{0}=\Bbbk^{\oplus d}
.

(1)

Let
P_{i}=e_{i}A
. Then
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{d}\}
is a complete
list of indecomposable graded projective right
A
-modules
up to degree shifts and isomorphisms.

(2)

It is easy to see that

q_{ij}=\dim_{\Bbbk}{\rm Hom}_{A}(P_{i},P_{j})_{1}

for all
1\leq i,j\leq d
.

2.2.
Twists of a quiver and Pretzelizations

Let
Q=(q_{ij})_{d\times d}
be a quiver with
d
vertices (or a

d\times d
-matrix over
{\mathbb{N}}
). A
graph
is a class
of special quivers
Q
with
q_{ij}=q_{ji}
for all
i,j
.
Then a quiver
Q
is a graph if and only if
Q=Q^{op}
. A graph is also
called a
symmetric quiver
. The example given in (
E2.6.1
)
is a graph, and the example given in (
E2.6.2
) is not a graph in
this paper.

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of the vertex set
\{1,\cdots,d\}

We first collect some definitions from the literature. In the
next definition we only consider graded right modules. The
same definition can be made for graded left modules. Since

Definition 2.8

(2)

We say

be a semisimple Hopf algebra acting on
R
homogeneously
and inner-faithfully. Assume that the homological determinant
of the
H
-action
[
RRZ
, Definition 3.7]
is trivial.
Let
A=R^{H}
. Suppose that
\Bbbk

is algebraically closed and that the conditions in

[
QWZ
, Example 8.5]
hold. Then
A
has a standard NQR.
As a consequence, the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
A
is a
pretzelization of a graph of
\widetilde{A}\widetilde{D}\widetilde{E}
type.

Proof.

Let
B=A\#H
as in
[
QWZ
, Example 8.5]
.
By
[
QWZ
, Example 8.5]
,
B
is a NQR of
A
. It is
easy to check that
B_{0}
is semisimple and
B
is generated
by
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
. Write
B=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}P_{i}^{\oplus u_{i}}

where
u_{i}\geq 1
and
\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{d}\}
is a complete list
of indecomposable projective right
B
-modules which are
direct summands of
B
. Let
C=\operatorname{End}_{B}(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d}P_{i})
.
Then
C
is graded Morita equivalent to
B
. As a consequence,

C
is a NQR of
A
. By working with the minimal
projective resolution of the graded simples, one can
shows that
C
is generated by
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
. Since

\Bbbk
is algebraically closed, it forces that
C_{0}=\Bbbk^{\oplus d}
. This means that
C
is standard.

The consequence follows from Theorem
0.2
(1)
and Definition
3.2
.
∎

0.1

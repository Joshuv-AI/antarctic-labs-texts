Abstract

New copulas, based on perturbation theory, are introduced to clarify a
symmetrization
procedure for asymmetric copulas. We give also some properties of the
symmetrized
copula. Finally, we examine families of copulas with a prescribed symmetrized one. By the way, we study topologically, the set of all symmetric copulas and give some of its classical and new properties.

1.
Preliminaries

Definition 1.1
.

A copula
\displaystyle C
is a bifunction on
\displaystyle{\tenBb I}^{2}
into
\displaystyle{\tenBb I}
which satisfies the following conditions for all
\displaystyle u,v,u_{1},v_{1},u_{2},v_{2}
in
\displaystyle{\tenBb I}

(1)

Border conditions:
\displaystyle C(0,v)=C(u,0)=0.

(2)

Uniform margins:
\displaystyle C(1,v)=v
and
\displaystyle C(u,1)=u.

(3)

the
\displaystyle C-
volume property:
\displaystyle V_{C}(R)=C(u_{2},v_{2})-C(u_{2},v_{1})-C(u_{1},v_{2})+C(u_{1},v_{1})\geq 0
for all rectangle
\displaystyle R=[u_{1},v_{1}]\times[u_{2},v_{2}]\subset{\tenBb I}^{2}
with
\displaystyle u_{1}<u_{2}
and
\displaystyle u_{2}<v_{2}
.

Any element of
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is framed between Fréchet-Hoeftding bounds
\displaystyle W
and
\displaystyle M
given by
\displaystyle W(u,v)=\max(u+v-1,0)
and
\displaystyle M(u,v)=\min(u,v)
. Precisely, we have

\forall(x,y)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}:\quad W(x,y)\leq C(x,y)\leq M(x,y).

These bounds (
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle W
) are also copulas but in higher dimensions, say for multivariate copulas,
\displaystyle W
is not a copula.

Statistically speaking, Fréchet-Hoeftding bounds
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle W
model respectively the co-monotonicity and anti-monotonicity of empirical variables
\displaystyle X
and
\displaystyle Y
. The copula
\displaystyle\Pi:\ (x,y)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}\mapsto xy
characterizes the total independence between the two variables.

On the other hand, some derived copulas from a given one will serve as efficient tool to treat the asymmetry questions mainly those of
transpose copula
,
survival
one.

Definition 1.2
.

The survival copula of a given copula
\displaystyle C

is the function
\displaystyle\widehat{C}
defined by the formula

\forall u,v\in{\tenBb I}:\quad\widehat{C}(u,v)=u+v-1+C(1-u,1-v)

we denote by
\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}
(
[
8
, pages,159-182]
) the difference of two probabilities
\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}=P((X_{1}-X_{2})(Y_{1}-Y_{2})>0)-P((X_{1}-X_{2})(Y_{1}-Y_{2})<0)

for a pair of continuous random vectors
\displaystyle(X_{1},X_{2})
and
\displaystyle(Y_{1},Y_{2})
. If the corresponding copulas are
\displaystyle C_{1}
and
\displaystyle C_{2}
, then we have

{\mathcal{Q}}(C_{1},C_{2})=4\int_{{\tenBb I}^{2}}C_{1}(u,v)~~dC_{2}(u,v)-1

The usual four measures of concordance may be defined in terms of the concordance function
\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Kendall’s tau of
\displaystyle C
is defined by

\tau(C)={\mathcal{Q}}(C,C)

Spearman’s rho by

\rho(C)=3{\mathcal{Q}}(C,\Pi)

Gini’s gamma by

\gamma(C)={\mathcal{Q}}(C,M)+{\mathcal{Q}}(C,W).

Blomqvist’s Beta by

\beta(C)=4C\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)-1.

2.
Asymmetry and radial asymmetry of bivariate copulas

Definition 2.4
.

A bivariate copula
\displaystyle C
is said:

•

Symmetric if it satisfies

\forall u,v\in{\tenBb I}:\quad C(u,v)=C(v,u).

•

Radial symmetric if it satisfies

\widehat{C}=C.

We denote
\displaystyle C^{T}
the transpose of
\displaystyle C
defined by
\displaystyle C^{T}(u,v)=C(v,u)
.

Example 2.5
.

(1)

The bounds
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle W
of Fréchet-Hoeffding and the independence copula
\displaystyle\Pi
are symmetric and also radial symmetric.

(2)

Let
\displaystyle\alpha
and
\displaystyle\beta
be in
\displaystyle(0,1)
. The Marshall Olkin family of copulas.

C_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)=min(u^{1-\alpha}v,uv^{1-\beta})

is asymmetric if
\displaystyle\alpha\neq\beta
.

Proposition 2.6
.

The subsets
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
are both convex and closed in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
with respect to the uniform convergence.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle(C_{n})
be a sequence of symmetric copulas which converges uniformly to a copula
\displaystyle C
. We show that
\displaystyle C
is also symmetric. For all u,v in
\displaystyle{\tenBb I}
, we have:

C(v,u)=\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}C_{n}(v,u)=\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}C_{n}(u,v)=C(u,v)

Let now
\displaystyle(C_{n})
be a sequence in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
which converges uniformly to a copula
\displaystyle C
.

C(u,v)=\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}C_{n}(u,v)=\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\widehat{C_{n}}(u,v)=\widehat{C}(u,v)

If
\displaystyle C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
and
\displaystyle\lambda
is a real number in
\displaystyle{\tenBb I}
, then easily
\displaystyle\lambda C_{1}+(1-\lambda)C_{2}
belongs to
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

If
\displaystyle C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{R}
and
\displaystyle\lambda
is a real number in
\displaystyle{\tenBb I}
, then
\displaystyle\lambda C_{1}+(1-\lambda)C_{2}\in\mathcal{R}
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

•

The projection of any bivariate copula
\displaystyle C
respect to
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
is the symmetric copula
\displaystyle C_{S}=\frac{C+C^{T}}{2}
.

•

The projection of any bivariate copula
\displaystyle C
respect to
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}
is the radial symmetric copula
\displaystyle C_{R}=\frac{C+\widehat{C}}{2}
.

Proof.

We show that
\displaystyle\frac{C+C^{T}}{2}
is the closest symmetric copula to
\displaystyle C
.

Let
\displaystyle S
be any symmetric copula. Since

\forall(u,v)\in{\tenBb I}^{2},~~|C(u,v)-S(u,v)|=|C^{T}(v,u)-S^{T}(v,u)|=|C^{T}(v,u)-S(v,u)|.

, we have
\displaystyle\|C-S\|=\|S-C^{T}\|.

And this equality gives

\|C-C^{T}\|\leq\|C-S\|+\|S-C^{T}\|\leq 2\|C-S\|

so

\|\frac{C-C^{T}}{2}\|\leq\|C-S\|

A similar proof can be given to the second point.
∎

.

A measures
\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle C

3.
New copulas and inverse problem

As mentioned above, the sequel is devoted to the inverse problem as explained at the end of the last section. To do so, we begin by defining a new copula based on perturbation theory:

Theorem 3.10
.

For all
\displaystyle(u,v)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}
, we put:
\displaystyle P(u,v)=(u-v)\min(u,v)\min(1-u,1-v)
. Then, the mapping

C(u,v)=\Pi(u,v)+P(u,v)=uv+(u-v)\min(u,v)\min(1-u,1-v)

defines a copula.

The mapping
\displaystyle C
is a natural perturbation of independence copula
\displaystyle\Pi
. One may se
[
15
]
for more detail on perturbation theory of copulas. The novelty in this new copula is, for the best of our knowledge, its asymmetry, contrary to the most known perturbed copulas in the literature.

Let us prove that
\displaystyle C
is actually a copula

of Theorem (
3.12
).

\displaystyle P
satisfies for all
\displaystyle u,v\in{\tenBb I}

P(v,u)=-P(u,v),P(u,0)=P(u,1)=P(0,v)=P(1,v)=0

Thus the bifunction
\displaystyle C=\Pi+P
satisfies the copula boundary conditions.

Let
\displaystyle R=[u_{1},u_{2}]\times[v_{1},v_{2}]
be a rectangle in
\displaystyle{\tenBb I}^{2}
.
We denote

\Delta=\{(x,y)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}~~y=x\},~~\Delta^{-}=\{(x,y)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}~~y\leq x\}~~\text{and}~~\Delta^{+}=\{(x,y)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}~~y\geq x\}.

For geometrical considerations, it suffices to discuss the following cases:

•

If
\displaystyle(v_{1},v_{2})
and
\displaystyle(u_{1},u_{2})
are both in the mean diagonal
\displaystyle\Delta
, meaning
\displaystyle u_{1}=v_{1}
and
\displaystyle u_{2}=v_{2}
then clearly
\displaystyle V_{P}(R)=0
holds

•

If
\displaystyle R\subset\Delta^{-}

\displaystyle V_{P}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{2}(1-u_{2})(u_{2}-v_{2})+v_{1}(1-u_{1})(u_{1}-v_{1})-v_{2}(1-u_{1})(u_{1}-v_{2})-v_{1}(1-u_{2})(u_{2}-v_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v_{2}-v_{1})(u_{2}-u_{1})(1-u_{2}-u_{1}+v_{1}+v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\Pi}(R)(1-u_{2}-u_{1}+v_{1}+v_{2})

Thus
\displaystyle V_{C}(R)=V_{\Pi}(R)(2-u_{2}-u_{1}+v_{1}+v_{2})\geq 0

•

If
\displaystyle R\subset\Delta^{+}
then

\displaystyle V_{P}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{2}(1-v_{2})(u_{2}-v_{2})+u_{1}(1-v_{1})(u_{1}-v_{1})-u_{2}(1-v_{1})(u_{1}-v_{2})-u_{1}(1-v_{2})(u_{2}-v_{1})

It remains to prove that for all
\displaystyle u,v\in{\tenBb I}
we have
\displaystyle C(u,v)\in{\tenBb I}
.
The partial derivatives of
\displaystyle C
can be written as
follows:

(u,v)\in\Delta^{-}\begin{cases}\frac{\partial C}{\partial u}(u,v)=v(2-2u+v)\\
\\
\frac{\partial C}{\partial v}(u,v)=2u-2uv+v^{2}\end{cases}

and

(u,v)\in\Delta^{+}\begin{cases}\frac{\partial C}{\partial u}(u,v)=2-2u-2v+2uv+v^{2}\\
\\
\frac{\partial C}{\partial v}(u,v)=1-2u-2v+2uv+u^{2}\end{cases}

Thus the extremal values of
\displaystyle C
are taken in the borders of
\displaystyle\Delta^{-}
and
\displaystyle\Delta^{+}
.
∎

\displaystyle M

Theorem 3.12
.

For all
\displaystyle(u,v)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}
, we put:
\displaystyle Q(u,v)=(u+v-1)\min(1-u,v)\min(u,1-v)
. Then, the mapping

C(u,v)=\Pi(u,v)+Q(u,v)=uv+(u+v-1)\min(1-u,v)\min(u,1-v)

defines a copula.

Proof.

\displaystyle Q
satisfies for all
\displaystyle u,v\in{\tenBb I}

Q(1-u,1-v)=-Q(u,v),Q(u,0)=Q(u,1)=Q(0,v)=Q(1,v)=0

We denote

\Omega=\{(x,y)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}~~y=1-x\},~~,\Omega^{-}=\{(x,y)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}~~y\leq 1-x\},~\text{and}~\Omega^{+}=\{(x,y)\in{\tenBb I}^{2}~~y\geq 1-x\}

We have to show that the independence perturbed copula
\displaystyle C=\Pi+Q
is a copula.

The bifunction
\displaystyle C
satisfies the border conditions.

Let
\displaystyle R=[u_{1},u_{2}]\times[v_{1},v_{2}]
be a rectangle in
\displaystyle{\tenBb I}^{2}
. As above, the geometrical considerations lead to discuss only the following cases:

•

If
\displaystyle(u_{1},v_{2})
and
\displaystyle(u_{2},v_{1})
are in
\displaystyle\Omega
, then

V_{Q}(R)=Q(u_{1},v_{1})+Q(u_{2},v_{2})-Q(u_{1},1-u_{1})-Q(u_{2},1-u_{2})=0

•

If
\displaystyle R\subset\Omega^{-}

\displaystyle V_{Q}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{2}u_{2}(u_{2}+v_{2}-1)+u_{1}v_{1}(u_{1}+v_{1}-1)-u_{1}v_{2}(u_{1}+v_{2}-1)-u_{2}v_{1}(u_{2}+v_{1}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V_{\Pi}(R)\left(u_{1}+u_{2}+v_{1}+v_{2}-1\right)

Thus
\displaystyle V_{C}(R)=V_{\Pi}(R)(u_{1}+u_{2}+v_{1}+v_{2})\geq 0

•

If
\displaystyle R\subset\Omega^{+}
. Let
\displaystyle R^{\prime}
be the rectangle
\displaystyle[1-u_{2},1-u_{1}]\times[1-v_{2},1-v_{1}]
then

\displaystyle V_{Q}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-V_{Q}(R^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-V_{\Pi}(R^{\prime})\left(1-u_{1}+1-u_{2}+1-v_{1}+1-v_{2}-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-V_{\Pi}(R)\left(3-u_{1}-u_{2}-v_{1}-v_{2}\right)

Thus
\displaystyle V_{C}(R)=V_{\Pi}(R)(u_{1}+u_{2}+v_{1}+v_{2}-2)\geq 0
because
\displaystyle u_{1}+v_{1}\geq 1
and
\displaystyle u_{2}+v_{2}\geq 1

It remains to prove that for all
\displaystyle u,v\in{\tenBb I}
we have
\displaystyle C(u,v)\in{\tenBb I}
.
The partial derivatives of
\displaystyle C
are given by

(u,v)\in\Omega^{-}\begin{cases}\frac{\partial C}{\partial u}(u,v)=v(2u+v)\\
\\
\frac{\partial C}{\partial v}(u,v)=u(u+2v)\end{cases}

and

(u,v)\in\Omega^{+}\begin{cases}\frac{\partial C}{\partial u}(u,v)=2-2u-2v+2uv+v^{2}\\
\\
\frac{\partial C}{\partial v}(u,v)=2-2u-2v+2uv+u^{2}\end{cases}

Thus the extremal values of
\displaystyle C
are taken on the borders of
\displaystyle\Omega^{-}
and
\displaystyle\Omega^{+}
.

∎

As just done for a symmetric copula, given a radial symmetric copula
\displaystyle R
. Does it exist a copula
\displaystyle C
other than
\displaystyle R
itself such that
\displaystyle C_{R}=R
? We try to answer for the same tree copulas as done above.

Proposition 3.13
.

(1)

\displaystyle M
is the only copula
\displaystyle C
satisfying
\displaystyle C_{R}=M
.

(2)

\displaystyle W
is the only copula
\displaystyle C
satisfying
\displaystyle C_{R}=W
.

(3)

There are many copulas
\displaystyle C
satisfying
\displaystyle C_{R}=\Pi
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
\displaystyle C
be a copula such
\displaystyle C_{R}=M
. Meaning
\displaystyle C-M=M-\widehat{C}
.

\displaystyle C-M
is non positive bifunction and equal to a non negative one
\displaystyle M-\widehat{C}
thus
\displaystyle C=M

(2)

Let
\displaystyle C
be a copula such
\displaystyle C_{R}=W
. Meaning
\displaystyle C-W=W-\widehat{C}
.

\displaystyle C-W
is non negative bifunction and equal to a non positive one
\displaystyle W-\widehat{C}
thus
\displaystyle C=W

∎

Remark 3.14
.

For any given
\displaystyle\theta\in[-1,1]
,

•

The bifunction
\displaystyle~~\Pi+\theta P
is a copula whose the symmetrized is
\displaystyle\Pi
.

•

The bifunction
\displaystyle~~\Pi+\theta Q
is a copula whose the radial symmetrized is
\displaystyle\Pi
.

4.
Conclusion

The notion of symmetrized and radial symmetrized copula will be probably a key to study the phenomena which are not symmetric by approximating with a symmetric distribution. We have cited some properties preserved by the symmetrization procedure and we hope to investigate statistically the loss of such operation and characterize copulas for which the symmetrized one is archimedean.

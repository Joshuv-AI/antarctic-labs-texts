The first step in proving Theorem

[

Such a “codimension estimate” was first established by Naff
[
21
]
for mean curvature flow in Euclidean spaces. His argument relies on the maximum principle but requires very careful accounting of first order terms. Our proof is inspired by Naff’s but requires some effort to overcome the bad ambient curvature terms as well as the possible presence of points where the mean curvature is zero (at which the conormal subspace is not even defined). The codimension estimate is also a crucial ingredient in our “cylindrical estimate”, in that it is needed to establish a suitable “Poincaré-type inequality” (cf.
[
18
, Proposition 3.2]
), which is the key ingredient in a Huisken–Stampacchia iteration argument. The cylindrical estimate allows us to obtain pointwise estimates for the gradient and Hessian of the second fundamental form using the maximum principle. These estimates imply that the flow becomes either uniformly convex or quantitatively cylindrical with respect to a codimension one subspace in regions of high curvature. Once they are in place, we are able to apply the surgery apparatus developed by Huisken–Sinestrari
[
15
]
, as extended by Nguyen
[
22
]
to the high codimension setting.

Taking the conormal projection yields

\displaystyle(\nabla_{u}\nabla_{v}{\mathbf{A}}{})^{\hat{\perp}}={}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{T}}{}(u)\otimes\big({\mathrm{T}}{}(v,{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}})+\nabla^{\top}_{v}{h}\big)+\hat{\nabla}_{u}\hat{\nabla}_{v}{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}+\nabla_{u}\hat{\mathbf{T}}{}(v)\otimes{h}+\hat{\mathbf{T}}{}(v)\otimes\nabla^{\top}_{u}{h}\mskip 1.5mu,

and hence

(2.22)

\displaystyle(\Delta{\mathbf{A}}{})^{\hat{\perp}}={}

\displaystyle\tr_{{g}^{\top}}\big(\hat{\mathbf{T}}{}(\mskip 1.5mu\cdot\mskip 1.5mu)\otimes{\mathrm{T}}{}(\mskip 1.5mu\cdot\mskip 1.5mu,{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}})+2\hat{\mathbf{T}}{}(\mskip 1.5mu\cdot\mskip 1.5mu)\otimes\nabla^{\top}_{\cdot}{h}\big)+\dvg\hat{\mathbf{T}}{}\otimes{h}+\hat{\Delta}{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}\mskip 1.5mu.

2.4.
Mean curvature flow

Now consider a family
X:M^{n}\times I\to N_{K}^{n+\ell}
of immersed submanifolds
X(\cdot,t):M^{n}\to N_{K}^{n+\ell}
of
N_{K}^{n+\ell}
which evolve by mean curvature flow. That is,

\partial_{t}X={\mathbf{H}}{}\mskip 1.5mu,

where
\partial_{t}X\doteqdot dX(\partial_{t})
, and
\partial_{t}
, the
canonical vector field
, is defined via its action on functions
f\in C^{\infty}(M\times I)
by

\partial_{t}|_{(x_{0},t_{0})}f\doteqdot\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}f(x_{0},t_{0}+t)\mskip 1.5mu.

The tangent bundle to
M\times I
splits as
T(M\times I)=TM\oplus\mathbb{R}\partial_{t}
, where we conflate
TM
with the
spatial tangent bundle
,
\{\xi\in T(M\times I):dt(\xi)=0\}
. Here,
dt
is the one-form dual to
\partial_{t}
, or, equivalently, the differential of the
time projection

(p,t)\mapsto t
from
M\times I
to
I
.

We make use of the
time-dependent connections
of Andrews–Baker
[
4
]
, which extend the tangential and normal covariant derivatives to allow differentiation in space-time directions
\xi\in TM\oplus\mathbb{R}\partial_{t}
. That is,

\nabla^{\top}_{\xi}V\doteqdot\big({}^{X}\!D_{\xi}\big[dX(V)\big]\big)^{\top}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\nabla^{\perp}_{\xi}N\doteqdot\big({}^{X}\!D_{\xi}N\big)^{\perp},

where
{}^{X}\!D
is the pullback of
D
to
X^{\ast}TN
. Observe that
\nabla^{\top}_{\xi}
and
\nabla_{\xi}^{\perp}
coincide with
\nabla^{\top}
and
\nabla^{\perp}
, respectively, when
\xi\in TM
, while

(2.23)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\top}_{t}V=[\partial_{t},V]-{\mathbf{L}}{}(V,{\mathbf{H}}{})\mskip 1.5mu,

where
[\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,]
denotes the Lie bracket and
\nabla_{t}\doteqdot\nabla_{\partial_{t}}
.

The main advantage of working with the time-dependent connection (as opposed to the more commonly used Lie derivative) is that the tensors
{g}^{\top}
and
{g}^{\perp}
are
\partial_{t}
-parallel:

\nabla^{\top}_{t}{g}^{\top}=0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\nabla^{\perp}_{t}{g}^{\perp}=0\,.

In order to exploit this, we first derive space-time analogues of the Codazzi–Gauss–Mainardi–Ricci–Weingarten equations (following
[
4
]
).

Observe that

{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{t},u)\doteqdot({}^{X}\!D_{t}[dX(u)])^{\perp}=\nabla^{\perp}_{u}{\mathbf{H}}{}

and hence

{g}^{\top}({\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{t},\nu),u)={g}^{\perp}(\nabla^{\perp}_{u}{\mathbf{H}}{},\nu)\mskip 1.5mu.

Thus, proceeding as in the “stationary” case, we obtain the “temporal” Gauss equation

(2.24)

0=\Rm^{\top}(\partial_{t},u,v,w)+{g}^{\perp}(\nabla^{\perp}_{w}{\mathbf{H}}{},{\mathbf{A}}{}(u,v))-{g}^{\perp}(\nabla^{\perp}_{v}{\mathbf{H}}{},{\mathbf{A}}{}(u,w))\mskip 1.5mu,

the temporal Codazzi–Mainardi equation

(2.25)

\nabla_{t}{\mathbf{A}}{}(u,v)=\nabla^{\perp}_{u}\nabla^{\perp}_{v}{\mathbf{H}}{}+{\mathbf{A}}{}({\mathbf{L}}{}(u,{\mathbf{H}}{}),v)+{g}^{\top}(u,v){\mathbf{H}}{},

and the temporal Ricci equation

(2.26)

0=\Rm^{\perp}(\partial_{t},u,\mu,\nu)+{g}^{\perp}(\nabla^{\perp}_{{\mathbf{L}}{}(u,\nu)}{\mathbf{H}}{},\mu)-{g}^{\perp}(\nabla^{\perp}_{{\mathbf{L}}{}(u,\mu)}{\mathbf{H}}{},\nu)\mskip 1.5mu.

The Gauss and Ricci equations allow us to interchange space-time covariant derivatives. The Codazzi equation provides an evolution equation for
{\mathbf{A}}{}
.

Of course, the space-time connections
\nabla^{\top}
and
\nabla^{\perp}
also exhibit “spatial” Codazzi–Gauss–Mainardi–Ricci–Weingarten equations when restricted to the spatial tangent bundle.

Next, we derive a useful parabolic analogue of the Jacobi equation for minimal surfaces. It is not needed in the sequel, but it does provide some insight into the subsequent evolution equations.

Proposition 2.1

(Jacobi equation)
.

Let
\{X_{\varepsilon}:M\times I\to N_{K}\}_{\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})}
be a
1
-parameter family of immersed mean curvature flows with
X_{0}=X
. The normal component
\sigma\doteqdot\left(\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_{\varepsilon=0}X_{\varepsilon}\right)^{\perp}
of the variation field satisfies the
Jacobi equation

\displaystyle(\nabla_{t}^{\perp}-\Delta^{\perp})\sigma={}

\displaystyle\tr_{{g}^{\top}}\big({\mathbf{A}}{}(\,\cdot\,,{\mathbf{L}}{}(\,\cdot\,,\sigma))\big)+nK\sigma\mskip 1.5mu.

Proof.

Denote by
\tau
the tangential component of the variation field. Fix
(p,t)
and consider a normal coordinate neighbourhood
(U,\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})
for
M
about
p
with respect to
g_{t}
. Choosing
U
smaller if necessary, we may identify
\tau
with a section of
TU
. Using (
2.5
), we compute at
(p,t)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}_{i}\nabla^{\perp}_{j}\sigma={}

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}_{i}({}^{X}\!D_{j}\sigma)^{\perp}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},({}^{X}\!D_{j}\sigma)^{\top})+({}^{X}\!D_{i}({}^{X}\!D_{j}\sigma))^{\perp}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{j},\sigma))+({}^{X}\!D_{i}({}^{X}\!D_{j}(\partial_{\varepsilon}X-\tau)))^{\perp}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{j},\sigma))+\big({}^{X}\!D_{i}({}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}X)-{}^{X}\!D_{i}(\nabla^{\top}_{j}\tau+{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{j},\tau))\big)^{\perp}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{j},\sigma))+({}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}({}^{X}\!D_{i}\partial_{j}X)+{}^{X}\!\Rm(\partial_{\varepsilon},\partial_{i})\partial_{j})^{\perp}

\displaystyle-{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},\nabla_{j}\tau)-\nabla_{i}{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{j},\tau)-{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{j},\nabla^{\top}_{i}\tau)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{j},\sigma))+({}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}(dX(\nabla^{\top}_{i}\partial_{j})+{\mathbf{A}}{}_{ij})+\Rm(\sigma+\tau,\partial_{i}X)\partial_{j}X)^{\perp}

\displaystyle-{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},\nabla_{j}\tau)-\nabla_{i}{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{j},\tau)-{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{j},\nabla^{\top}_{i}\tau)\mskip 1.5mu.

Note that

\displaystyle{}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}[dX(\nabla_{i}\partial_{j})]={}

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}dX(\partial_{k})+\Gamma_{ij}{}^{k}{}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}[dX(\partial_{k})]\mskip 1.5mu,

and hence

({}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}[dX(\nabla^{\top}_{i}\partial_{j})])^{\perp}=0

at
(p,t)
. Since, by the Codazzi equation,

(\Rm(\tau,\partial_{i}X)\partial_{j}X)^{\perp}=0=\nabla_{i}{\mathbf{A}}{}(\tau,\partial_{j})-\nabla_{\tau}{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},\partial_{j}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}_{i}\nabla^{\perp}_{j}\sigma={}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{j},\sigma))-(\Rm(\partial_{i}X,\sigma)\partial_{j}X)^{\perp}

\displaystyle+({}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{A}}{}_{ij}))^{\perp}-{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{i},\nabla^{\top}_{j}\tau)-{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{j},\nabla^{\top}_{i}\tau)-\nabla_{\tau}{\mathbf{A}}{}(\partial_{j},\partial_{j})\mskip 1.5mu.

On the other hand, the mean curvature flow equation yields

\displaystyle{}^{X}\!D_{t}\sigma={}

\displaystyle{}^{X}\!D_{t}[dX(\partial_{\varepsilon})-\tau]

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}[dX(\partial_{t})]-{}^{X}\!D_{t}\tau

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle{}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{H}}{}-{}^{X}\!D_{t}\tau\mskip 1.5mu.

Since

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}g_{ij}={}

\displaystyle\left\langle{}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}\partial_{i}X,\partial_{j}X\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{i}X,{}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}\partial_{j}X\right\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\langle{}^{X}\!D_{i}(\tau+\sigma),\partial_{j}X\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{i}X,{}^{X}\!D_{j}(\tau+\sigma)\right\rangle

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle g(\nabla^{\top}_{i}\tau-{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{i},\sigma),\partial_{j})+g(\partial_{i},\nabla^{\top}_{j}\tau-{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{j},\sigma))\mskip 1.5mu,

and hence

\displaystyle\partial_{\varepsilon}g^{ij}={}

\displaystyle-g^{ip}g^{jq}\partial_{\varepsilon}g_{pq}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-g^{ip}g^{jq}\left(g(\nabla^{\top}_{p}\tau-{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{p},\sigma),\partial_{q})\right)+g\left(\partial_{p},\nabla^{\top}_{q}\tau-{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{q},\sigma))\right)\mskip 1.5mu,

we obtain

\displaystyle{}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{H}}{}={}

\displaystyle g^{ij}{}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{A}}{}_{ij})-2g^{ip}g^{jq}g(\nabla^{\top}_{p}\tau-{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{p},\sigma),\partial_{q}){\mathbf{A}}{}_{ij}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle g^{ij}\left({}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}({\mathbf{A}}{}_{ij})-2{\mathbf{A}}{}(\nabla^{\top}_{i}\tau-{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{i},\sigma),\partial_{j})\right)

and hence

\nabla_{t}^{\perp}\sigma=\left(g^{ij}\left({}^{X}\!D_{\varepsilon}{\mathbf{A}}{}_{ij}-2{\mathbf{A}}{}(\nabla^{\top}_{i}\tau-{\mathbf{L}}{}(\partial_{i},\sigma),\partial_{j})\right)-{}^{X}\!D_{t}\tau\right)^{\perp}\mskip 1.5mu.

Finally, since

{}^{X}\!D_{t}\tau-{}^{X}\!D_{\tau}{\mathbf{H}}{}=dX[\partial_{t},\tau]=\frac{\partial\tau^{i}}{\partial t}dX(\partial_{i})

has no normal component, we conclude that

\displaystyle(\nabla_{t}^{\perp}-\Delta^{\perp})\sigma={}

\displaystyle\tr_{{g}^{\top}}\Big({\mathbf{A}}{}(\,\cdot\,,{\mathbf{L}}{}(\,\cdot\,,\sigma))+(\Rm(\,\cdot\,,\sigma)\,\cdot\,)^{\perp}\Big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\tr_{{g}^{\top}}\big({\mathbf{A}}{}(\,\cdot\,,{\mathbf{L}}{}(\,\cdot\,,\sigma))\big)+nK\sigma

as desired.
∎

(\nabla_{t}^{\perp}-\Delta^{\perp}){\mathbf{H}}{}=\tr_{{g}^{\top}}\big({\mathbf{A}}{}(\,\cdot\,,{\mathbf{L}}{}(\,\cdot\,,{\mathbf{H}}{}))\big)+nK{\mathbf{H}}{}\mskip 1.5mu.

It follows that

Now consider points where

such that

(2.39)

|F|^{2}+|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{5}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|+K^{3}\geq\gamma W^{3}

at points in
U_{\alpha,\eta}
with
{\mathbf{H}}{}\neq 0
, where

W\doteqdot|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+K\mskip 1.5mu.

In fact, we will show that (
2.39
) holds as an algebraic inequality. If we write
\lambda_{p}
for the eigenvalues of
h
, this inequality can be rewritten as

\sum_{p,q}(\lambda_{p}\lambda_{q}+K)^{2}(\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{q})^{2}+\tr(\lambda)^{5}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|+K^{3}\geq\gamma(\tr(\lambda)^{2}+K)^{3},

where

(2.40)

|\lambda|^{2}\doteqdot\sum_{p}\lambda_{p}^{2}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\tr(\lambda)\doteqdot\sum_{p}\lambda_{p}\mskip 1.5mu.

Thus, if (
2.39
) is false (as an algebraic inequality), then we can find a sequence of vector-valued symmetric bilinear forms
{\mathbf{A}}{}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\odot\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
with nonvanishing trace such that the conditions
\tr(\lambda^{i})>0
,

(2.41)

0\leq|\lambda^{i}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{i}|^{2}-\left(\tfrac{1}{n-1}+\eta\right)\tr(\lambda^{i})^{2},

and

(2.42)

|\lambda^{i}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{i}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n-2+\alpha}\tr(\lambda^{i})^{2}-2(2-\alpha)K\leq 0

hold for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and yet

(2.43)

\frac{\sum_{p,q}(\lambda_{p}^{i}\lambda_{q}^{i}+K)^{2}(\lambda_{p}^{i}-\lambda_{q}^{i})^{2}+\tr(\lambda^{i})^{5}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{i}|+K^{3}}{(\tr(\lambda^{i})^{2}+K)^{3}}\to 0

as
i\to\infty
. It follows that
\tr(\lambda^{i})^{2}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
, and hence, as a consequence of (
2.42
),

\tr(\lambda^{i})^{-2}|\lambda^{i}|^{2}+\tr(\lambda^{i})^{-2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{i}|^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{n-2+\alpha}+2(2-\alpha)K\tr(\lambda^{i})^{-2}\mskip 1.5mu.

So (passing to a subseqence if necessary) we may extract limits

\tr(\lambda^{i})^{-1}\lambda^{i}\to\lambda^{\infty}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\tr(\lambda^{i})^{-1}{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{i}\to{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{\infty}.

Passing to the limit in (
2.43
) we find that

\sum_{p,q}(\lambda_{p}^{\infty}\lambda_{q}^{\infty})^{2}(\lambda_{p}^{\infty}-\lambda_{q}^{\infty})^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{\infty}|=0,

and hence
{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{\infty}=0
and
\lambda^{\infty}=\tfrac{1}{m}\sum_{p=1}^{m}e_{p}
for some
1\leq m\leq n
, where
\{e_{p}\}_{p=1}^{n}
is the standard basis for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Passing to limits in (
2.41
) and (
2.42
), and inserting
{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}_{\infty}=0
and
|\lambda^{\infty}|^{2}=m^{-1}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{n-1}+\eta\leq\frac{1}{m}\leq\frac{1}{n-2+\alpha}.

It follows that
n-2<m<n-1
, which is absurd. We conclude that our initial assumption was false; that is, (
2.39
) holds in
U_{\alpha,\eta}
at points where
{\mathbf{H}}{}\neq 0
. Recalling (
2.38
) then yields

\gamma W^{3}\leq K^{3}+|E|^{2}+CW^{\frac{5}{2}}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|\mskip 1.5mu,

where
C=C(n,\alpha)
. Applying the hypothesis (
2.37
) and Young’s inequality then yields

\gamma W^{3}\leq K^{3}+|E|^{2}

with
\gamma
taking a smaller value and depending now also on
\delta
and
\Lambda
.

On the other hand, wherever
{\mathbf{H}}{}=0
,

W^{3}=K^{3}\leq|E|^{2}+K^{3}\mskip 1.5mu.

We conclude that

\gamma W^{3}\leq|E|^{2}+K^{3}

at all points of
U_{\alpha,\eta}
, where
\gamma=\gamma(n,\alpha,\eta,\delta,\Lambda)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\gamma\!\int\!u^{2}Wd\mu\leq{}

\displaystyle\int\frac{u^{2}}{W^{2}}\left(|E|^{2}+K^{3}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\int u^{2}\left(\frac{|E|^{2}}{W^{2}}+K\right)d\mu\mskip 1.5mu.

We now estimate, using Simons’ equation and the divergence theorem,

\displaystyle\int\frac{u^{2}}{W^{2}}|E|^{2}d\mu={}

\displaystyle\int\frac{u^{2}}{W^{2}}E\ast\nabla^{2}{\mathbf{A}}{}\,d\mu

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int\frac{u^{2}}{W^{2}}\!\left(\frac{\nabla u}{u}\ast E+\frac{\nabla W}{W}\ast E+\nabla E\right)\!\ast\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\int\frac{u^{2}}{W^{2}}\left(W^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}+W^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla W|+W|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|\right)|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C\int u^{2}\left(\frac{|\nabla u|}{u}+\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|}{W^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|}{W^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\mu\mskip 1.5mu,

where
C
depends only on
n
,
\alpha
and
\eta
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.1.
Preserving quadratic pinching

If
X_{0}:M^{n}\to S^{n+\ell}_{K}
satisfies (
1.2
), then we can find
\alpha\in(\alpha_{n},1)
such that

(3.1)

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-2+\alpha}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-2(2-\alpha)K\leq 0\mskip 1.5mu,

where

(3.2)

\alpha_{n}\doteqdot\max\{2-\tfrac{n}{4},0\}=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{4}&\text{if}\;\;n=5\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if}\;\;n=6\\
\frac{1}{4}&\text{if}\;\;n=7\\
0&\text{if}\;\;n\geq 8\mskip 1.5mu.\end{cases}

We will show that this is preserved under mean curvature flow. In fact, we will prove (more generally) that the condition

(3.3)

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-m+\alpha}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-2(m-\alpha)K\leq 0

is preserved for any integer
m\geq 1
and any
\alpha\in[0,1)
so long as
m-\alpha\leq\frac{n}{4}
.

Proposition 3.1

(Quadratic pinching is preserved)
.

Let
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to S^{n+\ell}_{K}
be a solution to mean curvature flow such that (
3.1
) holds on
M^{n}\times\{0\}
for some integer
m\geq 1
and some
\alpha\in[0,1)
. If
m-\alpha\leq\frac{n}{4}
, then (
3.1
) holds on
M^{n}\times\{t\}
for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

Given positive numbers
a
and
b
, consider the function

Q\doteqdot\frac{1}{2}\left(|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-bK\right)\,.

By (
2.30
) and (
2.28
),

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)Q={}

\displaystyle|{\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}\wedge\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+|{\left\langle{\mathbf{A}}{},{\mathbf{A}}{}\right\rangle^{\top}}|^{2}-a|{\mathbf{L}}{}(\,\cdot\,,{\mathbf{H}}{})|^{2}+nK\big(|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)-2nK|\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)\mskip 1.5mu.

At a point where
{\mathbf{H}}{}\neq 0
, decomposing
{\mathbf{A}}{}
into its irreducible components yields
[
4
]

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}=|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\mskip 1.5mu,

|{\mathbf{L}}{}(\cdot,{\mathbf{H}}{})|^{2}=|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}H^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{4}\mskip 1.5mu,

|{\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}\wedge\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}=2|({\mathbf{N}}\otimes\mathring{{h}})\wedge{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}\wedge{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\mskip 1.5mu,

and

\displaystyle|{\left\langle{\mathbf{A}}{},{\mathbf{A}}{}\right\rangle^{\top}}|^{2}={}

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle{\mathbf{N}}\otimes\mathring{{h}}+\tfrac{1}{n}H{\mathbf{N}}g^{\top}+{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}},{\mathbf{N}}\otimes\mathring{{h}}+\tfrac{1}{n}H{\mathbf{N}}g^{\top}+{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}\big)^{2}+2\big|\big\langle{\mathbf{N}}\otimes\mathring{{h}},{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}\big\rangle^{\top}\big|^{2}+|{\big\langle{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}\big\rangle^{\!\top}}|^{2}\mskip 1.5mu.

Applying the estimates
[
4
, p. 372]

(3.4)

|{\mathbf{N}}\otimes\mathring{{h}}\wedge{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\big|\big\langle\mathring{{h}},{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}\big\rangle^{\top}\big|^{2}\leq 2|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}

and
[
2
, Proposition 3]
(cf.
[
11
, Lemma 1]
)

(3.5)

|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}\wedge{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+|{\big\langle{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}},{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}\big\rangle^{\!\top}}|^{2}\leq\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{4}

now yields

\displaystyle|{\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}\wedge\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+|{\left\langle{\mathbf{A}}{},{\mathbf{A}}{}\right\rangle^{\top}}|^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle 4|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{4}+\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 3|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{4}+\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}\big)|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}H^{2}

and hence

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)Q={}

\displaystyle|{\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}\wedge\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+|{\left\langle{\mathbf{A}}{},{\mathbf{A}}{}\right\rangle^{\top}}|^{2}-a\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}\big)H^{2}+nK\big(2Q+bK\big)-2nK|\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 3|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{4}-\tfrac{1}{n}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}H^{2}-2nK\big(|\mathring{h}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+nK\big)\big(2Q+bK\big)-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\big(3|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}-bK\big)|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}

\displaystyle+bK\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+nK\big)-2nK\big(|\mathring{h}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+2Q\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+nK\big)-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)\mskip 1.5mu.

Rewriting

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}=2Q+aH^{2}+bK\mskip 1.5mu,

we obtain

\displaystyle bK\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+nK\big)={}

\displaystyle bK(2Q+aH^{2}+(b+n)K)

and

\displaystyle-2nK\big(|\mathring{h}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\big)={}

\displaystyle-2nK\big(2Q+\big(a-\tfrac{1}{n}\big)H^{2}+bK\big)\mskip 1.5mu,

and hence

\displaystyle bK\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+nK\big)-{}

\displaystyle 2nK\big(|\mathring{h}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-2K(2n-b)Q+(a(b-2n)+2)KH^{2}+b(b-n)K^{2}.

Similarly, using

\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}=\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-2Q-bK\big)

we find

\displaystyle 3|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}-bK={}

\displaystyle 3|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-2Q-bK\big)-bK

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\big(3-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\big(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{2}{an-1}Q-\big(1-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)bK\mskip 1.5mu.

If
a\leq\tfrac{4}{3n}
, then the first term on the right is nonpositive. Discarding this term and putting things back together, we arrive at

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)Q\leq{}

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{4}-\big(1-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)bK|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+b(b-n)K^{2}

\displaystyle+(a(b-2n)+2)KH^{2}+2Q\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{an-1}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+(b-n)K\big)

\displaystyle-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)\mskip 1.5mu.

Observe that if

a=\frac{1}{n-m+\alpha}\;\;\text{and}\;\;b=2(m-\alpha)

for some
m
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
m-\alpha\leq\frac{n}{4}
(which ensures that
a\leq\tfrac{4}{3n}
), then

a(b-2n)+2=0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\big(1-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)b=2(b-n).

We arrive at

\displaystyle(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{4}-\big(1-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)bK|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+b(b-n)K^{2}={}

\displaystyle(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{1}{an-1}\big)|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{4}-2(b-n)K|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}

\displaystyle+b(b-n)K^{2}.

The discriminant of the quadratic form on the right is
3(b-n)(\tfrac{n}{3}-m+\alpha)
. Since
m-\alpha\leq\tfrac{n}{4}
and
b=2(m-\alpha)
, this is strictly negative. Thus,

(3.6)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)Q\leq{}

\displaystyle 2Q\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{an-1}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+(b-n)K\big)-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)\mskip 1.5mu.

Since
n\geq 1+\frac{2(m-\alpha)}{3}
, the gradient terms can be discarded using the Kato inequality.

On the other hand, wherever
{\mathbf{H}}{}=0
,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)Q={}

\displaystyle|\mathring{{\mathbf{A}}{}}\wedge\mathring{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+\big|\big\langle\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{},\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}\big\rangle^{\top}\big|^{2}-nK|\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)\mskip 1.5mu.

So
[
2
, Proposition 3]
yields

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)Q\leq{}

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{3}{2}|\mathring{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}-nK\big)|\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 3|\mathring{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}Q+\big(\tfrac{3}{2}b-n\big)K|\mathring{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-a|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)\mskip 1.5mu.

Since
b=2(m-\alpha)<\frac{n}{2}
, the Kato inequality then yields

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)Q\leq{}

\displaystyle 3|\mathring{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}Q\mskip 1.5mu.

We conclude that

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)Q\leq{}

\displaystyle fQ

everywhere for some locally bounded function
f
, at which point we may conclude that non-positivity of
Q
is preserved.
∎

We can use the preservation of pinching to obtain an estimate for the trace-free second fundamental form which improves at large times, using the maximum principle.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X:M\times[0,T)\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
be a solution to mean curvature flow with initial condition in satisfying (
3.3
) with
m-\alpha<\frac{n}{4}
. There is a constant
C=C(n,m-\alpha)
such that

(3.7)

\frac{|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}}{|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+K}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}\mskip 1.5mu.

Proof.

Set

g\doteqdot\frac{1}{2}\big(|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)\;\;\text{and}\;\;W\doteqdot\frac{1}{2}\big(bK+a|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\big)\mskip 1.5mu,

where

a=\frac{4}{3n}\;\;\text{and}\;\;b=\frac{n}{2}.

Applying (
2.28
) and (
2.30
) yields (cf. Proposition
3.1
), wherever
{\mathbf{H}}{}\neq 0
,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)g\leq{}

\displaystyle\big(3|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}\big)|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-2nK\big(|\mathring{h}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+2g\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+nK\big)-\big(|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)\mskip 1.5mu.

Note that

|\mathring{h}|^{2}+|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}=2g

so that

3|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}=(3-\tfrac{1}{an-1})|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+(\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{1}{an-1})|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+\tfrac{2}{an-1}g\mskip 1.5mu.

The first three terms are non-positive. The remaining term is also non-positive by the Kato inequality. Thus,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)g\leq{}

\displaystyle-4nKg+2g\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{an-1}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}+nK\big)

(3.8)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle 2g\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{an-1}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}-nK\big)\mskip 1.5mu.

Since

\displaystyle-(\partial_{t}-\Delta)W\leq-2W\big(|\mathring{{h}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{an-1}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{n}H^{2}-(n-b)K\big)-2\gamma|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\mskip 1.5mu,

where

2\gamma=1-\tfrac{n+2}{3}a>0\mskip 1.5mu,

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{g}{W}}{\frac{g}{W}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)g}{g}-\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)W}{W}+2\left\langle\nabla\log\frac{g}{W},\nabla\log W\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle-2bK-2\gamma\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{W}+2\left\langle\nabla\log\frac{g}{W},\nabla\log W\right\rangle

wherever
{\mathbf{H}}{}\neq 0
.

On the other hand, wherever
{\mathbf{H}}{}=0
,

\displaystyle-(\partial_{t}-\Delta)W\leq{}

\displaystyle-3|\mathring{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}W

and

(3.9)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)g\leq{}

\displaystyle\big(3|\mathring{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}-2K\big)g\mskip 1.5mu,

and hence, at such points,

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{g}{W}}{\frac{g}{W}}\leq{}

\displaystyle-2K+2\left\langle\nabla\log\frac{g}{W},\nabla\log W\right\rangle.

Since
b\geq 1
we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{g}{W}}{\frac{g}{W}}\leq{}

\displaystyle-2K+2\left\langle\nabla\log\frac{g}{W},\nabla\log W\right\rangle

everywhere. The maximum principle now implies the claim.
∎

The codimension estimate

Next, we obtain an estimate for

In fact, the estimate holds under the condition (

[

.

Proposition 3.4

(Codimension estimate (cf.

Claim 3.6

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{2(n-1)}{n(n+2)}(1-s_{2})-\tfrac{an-1}{n}\big)H^{2}|\hat{\mathbf{T}}{}|^{2}+\big(\tfrac{2(n-1)}{n(n+2)}(1-s_{1})-\tfrac{an-1}{n}\big)|\nabla H|^{2}

3.3.
The cylindrical estimate

Next, we use the codimension estimate (Proposition
3.4
) and the Poincaré-type inequality (Proposition
2.3
) to obtain a cylindrical estimate. The argument follows the Stampacchia iteration procedure developed by Huisken
[
13
]
.

Given
n\geq 5
and
\ell\geq 2
, it will be convenient to state the estimate in terms of the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
of
n
-dimensional submanifolds of
S_{K}^{n+\ell}
satisfying

–

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n-2+\alpha}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-2(2-\alpha)K
, (with
\alpha\geq\alpha_{n}
, where
\alpha_{n}
is defined by (
3.2
)),

–

\mu(M)\leq VK^{-\frac{n}{2}}
, and

–

\max_{M}|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\leq\Theta K
.

Of course, every
n
-dimensional submanifold of
S_{K}^{n+\ell}
satisfying (
1.2
) lies in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
for some
\alpha\geq\alpha_{n}
,
V<\infty
, and
\Theta<\infty
.

Proposition 3.7

(Cylindrical estimate)
.

Let
X:M\times[0,T)\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
be a solution to mean curvature flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
and set
\eta_{0}\doteqdot\frac{1}{n-2+\alpha_{n}}-\frac{1}{n-1}
. For every
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
there exists
C_{\eta}=C_{\eta}(n,\alpha,V,\Theta,\eta)<\infty
so that

(3.19)

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n-1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\leq\eta|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+C_{\eta}K\mskip 1.5mu\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}\quad\text{in}\quad M^{n}\times[0,T)\,.

Proof.

Given
\eta>0
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
, consider the function

g_{\eta,\sigma}\doteqdot\frac{1}{2}\big[|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\big(\tfrac{1}{n-1}+\eta\big)|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big]W^{\sigma-1},

where

W\doteqdot\frac{1}{2}\big(bK+a|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\big)

for

a=\tfrac{1}{n-2+\alpha_{n}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;b=2(2-\alpha_{n}).

Computing as in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we obtain, for
\eta\leq\eta_{0}\doteqdot\frac{1}{n-2+\alpha_{n}}-\frac{1}{n-1}
,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)g_{\eta,\sigma}}{g_{\eta,\sigma}}\leq{}

\displaystyle-2K+\sigma n|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}+2(1-\sigma)\left\langle\frac{\nabla g_{\eta,\sigma}}{g_{\eta,\sigma}},\frac{\nabla W}{W}\right\rangle\mskip 1.5mu.

Now consider, for
k\geq 0
and
p\geq 3
,

v_{k}\doteqdot(\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma}-k)_{+}^{\frac{p}{2}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;V_{k}(t)\doteqdot\spt v_{k}(\cdot,t)\mskip 1.5mu.

By (
3.20
),

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)v_{k}^{2}}{v_{k}^{2}}={}

\displaystyle p\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)g_{\eta,\sigma}+2Kg_{\eta,\sigma}}{(\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma}-k)_{+}}-4\big(1-\tfrac{1}{p}\big)\frac{|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}}{v_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\left(\sigma np|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-2pK-2\gamma p\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{W}\right)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma}}{(\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma}-kK^{\sigma})_{+}}+4\frac{|\nabla v_{k}|}{v_{k}}\frac{|\nabla W|}{W}

\displaystyle-4\big(1-\tfrac{1}{p}\big)\frac{|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}}{v_{k}^{2}}

and hence, for
p
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)v_{k}^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle-2pKv_{k}^{2}+\sigma np\mskip 1.5mu\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma,+}(\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma,+}-k)_{+}^{p-1}|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\gamma pv_{k}^{2}\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{W}-2|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}\mskip 1.5mu.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int v_{k}^{2}\,d\mu+2\int|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}\,d\mu+\int|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}v_{k}^{2}\,d\mu\leq{}

\displaystyle-2pK\int v_{k}^{2}\mskip 1.5mud\mu-\gamma p\int v_{k}^{2}\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{W}\mskip 1.5mud\mu

(3.21)

\displaystyle+c\sigma p\int_{V_{k}}(\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma})^{p}W\mskip 1.5mud\mu\mskip 1.5mu,

where
c=c(n)
. In particular, when
k=0
, the Poincaré inequality (Proposition
2.3
) yields

(3.22)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int v_{0}^{2}\,d\mu\leq{}

\displaystyle-2pK\int v_{0}^{2}\mskip 1.5mud\mu

and hence

(3.23)

\displaystyle\int v_{0}^{2}\mskip 1.5mud\mu\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-2pKt}\mskip 1.5mu,

so long as
p\geq\ell^{-1}
and
\sigma\leq\ell p^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, where
\ell=\ell(n,\alpha,\eta)
and
C=C(n,K,\alpha,V,\Theta,\sigma,p)
.

The
L^{2}
-estimate (
3.23
) can be bootstrapped to an
L^{\infty}
-estimate using (
3.21
) by applying Huisken–Stampacchia iteration. Indeed, using (
3.23
) we may estimate, for
p\geq\ell
,
\sigma\leq\ell^{-1}p^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,

\displaystyle V_{k}\leq{}

\displaystyle k^{-p}\int_{V_{k}}(\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma})^{p}d\mu\leq Ck^{-p}\mskip 1.5mu,

where
C=C(n,K,\alpha,V,\Theta,\sigma,p)
, so that

\displaystyle V_{k}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{n}}{n+1}K^{-\frac{n}{2}}

so long as
k\geq k_{0}=k_{0}(n,K,\alpha,V,\Theta,\sigma,p)
. This enables us to apply the Sobolev inequality
[
12
]
(cf.
[
20
]
), which yields, at each time,

\frac{1}{c_{\mathrm{S}}}\Bigg(\int_{V_{k}}v_{k}^{2^{*}}d\mu\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}\leq\int_{V_{k}}\big(|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}+v_{k}^{2}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\big)d\mu\mskip 1.5mu,

where
2^{*}\doteqdot\tfrac{2n}{n-2}
and
c_{\mathrm{S}}
depends only on
n
, and consequently

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int v_{k}^{2}\,d\mu+\frac{1}{c_{\mathrm{S}}}\Bigg(\int_{V_{k}}v_{k}^{2^{*}}d\mu\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle c\mskip 1.5mu\sigma p\int_{V_{k}}(\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma})^{p}\big(|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+K\big)\mskip 1.5mud\mu

so long as
p\geq\ell
,
\sigma\leq\ell^{-1}p^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and
k\geq k_{0}
. Assuming further that
k_{0}\geq\max g_{\eta,\sigma}(\cdot,0)
(which can be achieved with dependence on
n
,
K
,
\alpha
,
V
,
\Theta
,
\sigma
and
p
only), integrating now gives

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T)}\int_{M}v_{k}^{2}(\cdot,t)\,d\mu+\int_{0}^{T}\!\bigg(\int_{M}v_{k}^{2q}\,d\mu\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,dt\leq C\sigma p\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{V_{k}}(\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma})^{p}\big(|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+K\big)\mskip 1.5mu\,d\mu\,dt,

where
C\doteqdot c_{\mathrm{S}}c
depends only on
n
. Applying the Hölder, interpolation and Young inequalities, exactly as in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
, now yields
C=C(n,\ell,\alpha,V,\Theta,\sigma,p)<\infty
and
\gamma=\gamma(n)>0
such that

\displaystyle A(h)\leq\frac{C}{(h-k)^{p}}A(k)^{\gamma}

for all
h>k>k_{0}
, where

A(k)\doteqdot\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{V_{k}}\,d\mu_{t}\mskip 1.5mudt\mskip 1.5mu,

so long as
p\geq\ell^{-1}
and
\sigma\leq\ell p^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, where
\ell=\ell(n,\alpha,\eta)
. Fixing
p\doteqdot\ell^{-1}
and
\sigma\doteqdot\ell p^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, Stampacchia’s lemma
[
27
, Lemma 4.1]
now yields

\displaystyle A(k_{0}+d)=0\,,

where

\displaystyle d^{p}\doteqdot 2^{p\gamma/(\gamma-1)}CA(k_{0})^{\gamma-1}\,.

Estimating via (
3.23
)

\displaystyle A(k)\leq k_{0}^{-p}\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-5.69054pt\int v_{k_{0}}^{2}\,d\mu\,dt\leq C(n,K,\alpha,V,\Theta,\eta)\mskip 1.5mu,

we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{2Kt}g_{\eta,\sigma}\leq C(n,K,\alpha,V,\Theta,\eta)\,.

Young’s inequality then yields

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n-1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\leq 2\eta|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+C(n,K,\alpha,V,\Theta,\eta)\mskip 1.5mu\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}\,.

The theorem now follows from the scaling covariance of the estimate.
∎

3.4.
The gradient estimate

Next, we derive a suitable analogue of the “gradient estimate”
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
. We need the following doubling estimates for solutions with initial data in the class
\mathcal{C}^{n,\ell}_{K}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
X:M\times[0,T)\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
be a maximal solution to mean curvature flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
. Defining
\Lambda_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}
by

(3.25)

\Lambda_{0}/2\doteqdot\Theta\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mathrm{e}^{2n\lambda_{0}}\doteqdot 1+\frac{n}{n+3\Lambda_{0}},

we have

(3.26)

\mathrm{e}^{2nKT}\geq 1+\frac{2n}{3\Lambda_{0}}\,,

and, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.27)

\max_{M\times\{\lambda_{0}K^{-1}\}}|\nabla^{k}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}\leq\Lambda_{k}K^{k+1}\mskip 1.5mu,

where
\Lambda_{k}
depends only on
n
,
k
and
\Theta
.

Proof.

Since

\max_{M\times\{0\}}|{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}\leq\Lambda_{0}K/2\,,

a straightforward
ode
comparison argument applied to the inequality

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}\leq(3|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}+2nK)|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}

yields

\max_{M\times\{t\}}|{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}\leq\frac{nK}{\left(3+\frac{2n}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-2nKt}-3}\,.

We immediately obtain (
3.26
) and

(3.28)

\displaystyle|{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}(\mskip 1.5mu\cdot\mskip 1.5mu,t)\leq\Lambda_{0}K\;\;\text{for all}\;\;t\leq\lambda_{0}K^{-1}\,.

The claim (
3.27
) now follows from the Bernstein estimates (Proposition
2.2
).
∎

Modifying an argument of Huisken
[
13
, Theorem 6.1]
and Huisken–Sinestrari
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
, we can now obtain a pointwise estimate for the gradient of the second fundamental form which holds up to the singular time.

Proposition 3.9

(Gradient estimate (cf.
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
))
.

Let
X:M\times[0,T)\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
,
n\geq 5
, be a solution to mean curvature flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
. There exist
c=c(n,\ell,\alpha,\Theta)<\infty
,
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(n)>0
and, for every
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
,
C_{\eta}=C_{\eta}(n,\alpha,V,\Theta,\eta)<\infty
such that

(3.29)

\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+K}+c\left(|{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\right)\leq\left(\eta+\frac{1}{n-1}\right)|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+C_{\eta}K\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}

in
M\times[\lambda_{0}K^{-1},T)
, where
\lambda_{0}
is defined by (
3.25
).

Note that the conclusion is not vacuous since, by Proposition
3.8
, the maximal existence time of a solution with initial data in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
is at least
\frac{1}{2nK}\log\left(1+\frac{2n}{3\Lambda_{0}}\right)>\lambda_{0}K^{-1}
.

Setting
\eta=\eta_{0}
, say, yields the cruder estimate

(3.30)

|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\leq C(|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{4}+K^{2})\mskip 1.5mu,

where
C=C(n,\alpha,V,\Theta)
.

Proof of Proposition
3.9
.

We proceed as in
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
. By (
2.34
),

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle-2|\nabla^{2}{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}+c\left(|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}+K\right)|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\,.

We will control the bad term using the good term in the evolution equation for
|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}
and the Kato inequality (
2.9
).

By the cylindrical estimate, given any
\eta>0
we can find
C_{\eta}=C_{\eta}(n,\alpha,V,\Theta,\eta)>2
such that

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n-1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\leq\eta|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+C_{\eta}K\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}\,,

and hence

G_{\eta}\doteqdot 2C_{\eta}K\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}+\left(\eta+\tfrac{1}{n-1}\right)|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\geq C_{\eta}K\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}>0\,.

By the initial pinching condition,

G_{0}\doteqdot 4(2-\alpha_{n})K+\tfrac{1}{n-2+\alpha_{n}}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\geq 2(2-\alpha_{n})K>0\,.

Arguing as in (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), but keeping part of the gradient terms using the Kato inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)G_{\eta}\geq{}

\displaystyle-2n|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}(2C_{\eta}K\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}-G_{\eta})-4C_{\eta}K^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}+\beta|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle-2n(|{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+K)G_{\eta}+\beta|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\mskip 1.5mu,

where
\beta\doteqdot\frac{3}{n+2}-\frac{1}{n-1}=\frac{2n-5}{(n+2)(n-1)}
, so long as
\eta\leq\left(1-\frac{3}{n+2}\right)\beta
.

Similarly,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)G_{0}\geq{}

\displaystyle-2n(|{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+K)G_{0}\,.

We seek a bound for the ratio
\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}
. So suppose that
\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}
attains a (parabolic) interior local maximum at
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Then, at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{k}\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}=2\frac{\left\langle\nabla_{k}\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{},\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}\right\rangle}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}-\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\left(\frac{\nabla_{k}G_{\eta}}{G_{\eta}}+\frac{\nabla_{k}G_{0}}{G_{0}}\right)

and hence

\displaystyle 4\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\left\langle\frac{\nabla G_{\eta}}{G_{\eta}},\frac{\nabla G_{0}}{G_{0}}\right\rangle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\left|\frac{\nabla G_{\eta}}{G_{\eta}}+\frac{\nabla G_{0}}{G_{0}}\right|^{2}\leq 4\frac{|\nabla^{2}{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\,.

Furthermore, at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,

\displaystyle 0\leq(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}={}

\displaystyle\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}-\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\left(\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)G_{\eta}}{G_{\eta}}+\frac{(\partial_{t}-\Delta)G_{0}}{G_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\left\langle\nabla\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}},\nabla(G_{\eta}G_{0})\right\rangle+2\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\left\langle\frac{\nabla G_{\eta}}{G_{\eta}},\frac{\nabla G_{0}}{G_{0}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\left((c+4n)(|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}+K)-\beta\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}}\right)

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{(c+4n)\beta^{-1}(|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}+K)}{4(2-\alpha_{n})K+\frac{1}{n-2+\alpha_{n}}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}.

Since the solution is uniformly pinched,

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{n-2+\alpha}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+2(2-\alpha)K\,,

we obtain, at
(x_{0},t_{0})
,

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{\eta}G_{0}}\leq{}

\displaystyle C\,,

where
C
depends only on
n
,
k
and
\alpha
.

On the other hand, since
G_{0}>C_{0}K\doteqdot 2(2-\alpha_{n})K
and
G_{\eta}>C_{\eta}K\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}
, if no interior local parabolic maxima are attained, then, by Proposition
3.8
, we have for any
t\geq\lambda_{0}K^{-1}

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{M}\times\{t\}}\frac{|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{G_{0}G_{\eta}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{M}\times\{\lambda_{0}K^{-1}\}}\frac{|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{G_{0}G_{\eta}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{M}\times\{\lambda_{0}K^{-1}\}}\frac{|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{C_{0}C_{\eta}K^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda_{1}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda_{0}}}{C_{0}C_{\eta}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}\mathrm{e}^{2\lambda_{0}}\,.

The theorem follows.
∎

3.5.
Higher order estimates

The gradient estimate can be used to bound the first order terms which arise in the evolution equation for
\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}
. A straightforward maximum principle argument exploiting this observation yields an analogous estimate for
\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}
.

Proposition 3.10

(Hessian estimate (cf.
[
13
,
15
]
))
.

Let
X:M\times[0,T)\to S_{K}^{n+1}
,
n\geq 5
, be a solution to mean curvature flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
. There exists
C=C(n,\alpha,V,\Theta)
such that

(3.31)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}\leq C(|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{6}+K^{3})\;\;\text{in}\;\;M\times[\lambda_{0}K^{-1},T)\,.

Proof.

We proceed as in
[
15
, Theorem 6.3]
. Set
W\doteqdot|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+K
. By (
2.35
),

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}\leq c\left(W|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{4}\right)-2|\nabla^{3}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}\,,

where
c
depends only on
n
and
k
. Recalling (
2.28
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{c}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}\left[W|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{4}\right]-2\frac{|\nabla^{3}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle\!-5\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}\left[|{{\mathbf{L}}{}(\cdot,{\mathbf{H}}{})}|^{2}+nK|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{35}{4}\frac{|\nabla^{2}{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}\frac{|\nabla W|^{2}}{W}+\frac{5}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}\left\langle\nabla|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2},\nabla W\right\rangle.

We can use the good third order term on the first line to absorb the ultimate term, since

\displaystyle\frac{5}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}\left\langle\nabla|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2},\nabla W\right\rangle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{10}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}|\nabla^{3}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}||\nabla^{2}{\mathbf{A}}{}||\nabla W|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{W^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{|\nabla^{3}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{2}}+25\frac{|\nabla^{2}{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}|\nabla W|^{2}}{W^{4}}\right).

Estimating

\frac{|\nabla W|^{2}}{W}\leq 4|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}

then yields

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{c}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}\left[W|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}+|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{4}\right]-\frac{|\nabla^{3}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}+100\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}.

Restricting to
t\geq\lambda_{0}K^{-1}
and estimating the first order terms using Proposition
3.9
(and Young’s inequality) then yields

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle c_{1}\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{3}{2}}}+C_{1}K^{2}\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}\mathrm{e}^{-2Kt}

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{4}W^{2}+C_{1}K^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}-\frac{|\nabla^{3}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}\,,

where
c_{1}
depends only on
n
,
k
,
\alpha
and
\Theta
, and
C_{1}
depends also on
V
.

Similar arguments yield

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{c_{2}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}W^{3}+C_{2}K^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}-\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{3}{2}}}

and

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\frac{|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}\leq{}

\displaystyle c\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{9}{2}}}\left(W^{2}+|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\right)-\frac{|\nabla^{2}{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{c_{3}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}W^{3}+C_{3}K^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}}{W^{\frac{9}{2}}}-\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}\,,

where
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
depend only on
n
,
\alpha
, and
\Theta
, and
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
depend also on
V
.

Setting

f\doteqdot\frac{|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}+c_{1}\frac{|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{3}{2}}}+C_{1}K^{2}\frac{|\nabla{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}}{W^{\frac{7}{2}}}

and estimating
W\geq K
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)f\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{4}W^{2}+C_{1}K^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}+c_{1}\frac{c_{2}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}W^{3}+C_{2}K^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle+C_{1}K^{2}\frac{c_{3}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}W^{3}+C_{3}K^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}}{W^{\frac{9}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{(c_{1}+c_{1}c_{2}+c_{3}C_{1})|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}W^{3}+(C_{1}+c_{1}C_{2}+C_{1}C_{3})K^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}}{W^{\frac{5}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle(c_{1}+c_{1}c_{2}+c_{3}C_{1})|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}W^{\frac{1}{2}}+(C_{1}+c_{1}C_{2}+C_{1}C_{3})K^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}

\displaystyle\doteqdot{}

\displaystyle c_{4}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}W^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{4}K^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}\,.

On the other hand, the function
G
defined by

G^{2}\doteqdot 2(2-\alpha_{n})K+\tfrac{1}{n-2+\alpha_{n}}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}

satisfies

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)G\geq c_{5}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}W^{\frac{1}{2}}\mskip 1.5mu,

where
c_{5}
depends only on
n
and
\alpha
. Thus,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)\left(f-\frac{c_{4}}{c_{5}}G+\frac{C_{4}}{4}K^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}\right)\leq{}

\displaystyle 0\,.

The maximum principle and Proposition
3.8
then yield

\displaystyle\max_{M\times\{t\}}\left(f-\frac{c_{4}}{c_{5}}G\right)\leq{}

\displaystyle\max_{M\times\{\lambda_{0}K^{-1}\}}\left(f-\frac{c_{4}}{c_{5}}G\right)+\frac{C_{4}}{4}K^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-4\lambda_{0}}-\mathrm{e}^{-4Kt}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle C_{5}K^{\frac{1}{2}}

for all
t\geq\lambda_{0}K^{-1}
, where
C_{5}
depends only on
n
,
\alpha
,
V
, and
\Theta
. We conclude that

|\nabla^{2}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|^{2}\leq cW^{3}+CK^{\frac{1}{2}}W^{\frac{5}{2}}\;\;\text{in}\;\;M\times[\lambda_{0}K^{-1},T)\,,

where
c
and
C
depend only on
n
,
\alpha
,
V
, and
\Theta
. The claim now follows from Young’s inequality.
∎

Applying the Hessian estimate in conjunction with the the rough evolution equation

(\nabla_{t}-\Delta){\mathbf{A}}{}={\mathbf{A}}{}\ast{\mathbf{A}}{}\ast{\mathbf{A}}{}+K\ast{\mathbf{A}}{}

for
{\mathbf{A}}{}
yields an analogous bound for
\nabla_{t}{\mathbf{A}}{}
, and hence, in particular, for the time derivative of
{\mathbf{H}}{}
. Thus, in high curvature regions, we obtain the following a priori bounds for
\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}
and
\nabla_{t}^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
X:M^{n}\times[0,T)\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
,
n\geq 2
, be a solution to mean curvature flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
. There exist
h_{\sharp}=h_{\sharp}(n,\alpha,V,\Theta)
and
c_{\sharp}=c_{\sharp}(n,\alpha,V,\Theta)
such that

(3.32)

|{\mathbf{H}}{}|(x,t)\geq h_{\sharp}\sqrt{K}\;\implies\;\frac{|\nabla^{\perp}{\mathbf{H}}{}|}{|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}}(x,t)\leq c_{\sharp}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\frac{|\nabla^{\perp}_{t}{\mathbf{H}}{}|}{|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{3}}(x,t)\leq\frac{c_{\sharp}^{2}}{2}.

3.6.
Neck detection

The cylindrical and gradient estimates can be used to show that regions of very high curvature which are not pinched in the sense of Andrews and Baker must form high quality ‘neck’ regions.

Let
X:M\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+\ell+1}

(\varepsilon,k,L)
-

\varepsilon
-cylindrical and
(\varepsilon,k)

[

Lemma 3.13

(Neck detection (cf.
[
15
, Lemma 7.4]
))
.

Let

be a solution to mean curvature flow with initial condition in the class

then

\Lambda_{r_{0},k,\varepsilon}(x_{0},t_{0})\leq\varepsilon r_{0}^{-(k+1)}

for each

, where

,

and, for each

\Lambda_{r,k,\varepsilon}(x,t)\doteqdot\max_{\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon^{-1}r}(x,t)\times(t-10^{4}r^{2},t]}|\nabla^{k}{{\mathbf{A}}{}}|\,.

Proof.

The result can be obtained by

[

, Lemma 7.4]
(cf.
[
9
, Theorem 7.13]
,
[

, Lemma 4.16]
and
[

).
∎

By the Gauss equation and the arguments of
[

, §3]
(cf.
[

), necks of sufficiently high quality can be integrated (after pulling up to the tangent space) to obtain “almost hypersurface” necks (in the tangent space), which can be replaced by a pair of “convex caps” in a controlled way.

3.7.
Hypersurface detection

The codimension estimate can be used to show that, after pulling up to the tangent space, regions of high curvature almost lie in some
(n+1)
-dimensional affine subspace.

Definition 3.14
.

(\varepsilon,k)

The following lemma, combined with the Gauss equation, shows that points of sufficiently large curvature have almost hypersurface neighbourhoods (of ‘size’

Lemma 3.15

(Hypersurface detection (cf.
[
22
, Lemma 5.8]
))
.

Let
X:M\times[0,T)\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
be a solution to mean curvature flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}^{n,\ell}_{K}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
. Given
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{100}
, there exist
h_{\sharp}=h_{\sharp}(n,\ell,\alpha,V,\Theta,\varepsilon)<\infty
,

and
\theta_{\sharp}=\theta_{\sharp}(n,\ell,\alpha,V,\Theta)>0
with the following property. If

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{H}}{}|(x_{0},t_{0})\geq h_{\sharp}\sqrt{K}\mskip 1.5mu,

then

\sup_{\mathcal{B}_{L_{\sharp}r_{0}}(x_{0},t_{0})\times(t_{0}-\theta_{\sharp}r_{0}^{2},t_{0}]}|\nabla^{k}{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|\leq\varepsilon r_{0}^{-(k+1)}

.

Proof.

The proof is again very similar to that of
[
15
, Lemma 7.4]
.
∎

Note that almost hypersurface regions which satisfy our pinching condition do indeed lie close to a genuine “hypersurface”. This can be proved by an argument similar to
[

, Proposition 2.4]
(cf.
[

). We do not require this here, however. Indeed, we only need bounds for

and

4.
The key estimates for surgically modified flows

We need to show that suitable versions of the key estimates still hold in the presence of surgeries. In the following definition, surgery is performed on the middle third of a neck of size
r
in the obvious way:

(i)

Scale by
r^{-1}
and precompose with
\exp_{r^{-1}X(p)}^{-1}
to obtain a neck in
T_{r^{-1}X(p)}S_{r^{2}K}^{n+\ell}
.

(ii)

Perform surgery on the middle third of this neck in
T_{r^{-1}X(p)}S_{r^{2}K}^{n+\ell}
as described in
[
22
, Section 3]
(cf.
[
15
, Section 3]
).

(iii)

Re-embed in
S_{K}^{n+\ell}
by composing with
\exp_{r^{-1}X(p)}
and scaling by
r
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
surgically modified (mean curvature) flow
in
S_{K}^{n+\ell}
with
neck parameters

(\varepsilon,k,L)
,
surgery parameters

(\tau,B)
, and
surgery scale

r
is a finite sequence
\{X_{i}:M_{i}^{n}\times[T_{i},T_{i+1}]\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}\}_{i=1}^{N-1}
of smooth mean curvature flows
X_{i}:M_{i}^{n}\times[T_{i},T_{i+1}]\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
for which the
(i+1)
-st initial datum
X_{i+1}(\mskip 1.5mu\cdot\mskip 1.5mu,T_{i+1}):M_{i+1}\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
is obtained from the
i
-th final datum
X_{i}(\mskip 1.5mu\cdot\mskip 1.5mu,T_{i+1}):M_{i}\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}
by performing finitely many
(\tau,B)
-standard surgeries, in the sense of
[
15
, Section 3]
, on the middle thirds of
(\varepsilon,k,L)
-necks with mean curvature satisfying
\frac{n-1}{10r}\leq H\leq\frac{10(n-1)}{r}
, and then discarding finitely many connected components that are diffeomorphic either to
S^{n}
or to
S^{1}\times S^{n-1}
.

4.1.
Preserving quadratic pinching

For a suitable range of neck and surgery parameters, and surgery scales, the surgery procedure of
[
22
]
(cf.
[
15
, Section 3]
) preserves the quadratic pinching condition (
1.2
).

In the statement of the following proposition (Proposition
4.2
) and henceforth, when we refer to a surgically modified flow, it is taken for granted that the neck and surgery parameters, and the surgery scale, are fixed within a suitable range (which we progressively refine).

Proposition 4.2

(Quadratic pinching for surgically modified flows)
.

Every surgically modified flow
\{X_{i}:M_{i}^{n}\times[T_{i},T_{i+1}]\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}\}_{i=1}^{N-1}
,
n\geq 5
, with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
(with
\alpha>\alpha_{n}
when
n=5,6,7
) satisfies (
3.1
) for all
t\in[T_{1},T_{N}]
.

Proof.

Since
|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{n-1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}
on a hypersurface cylinder and
|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{n}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}
on a hypersurface cap, we can ensure, for a suitable choice of neck and surgery parameters and surgery scales, that

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-2+\overline{\alpha}}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}<2(2-\overline{\alpha})K

on regions modified or added by surgery, where
\overline{\alpha}\doteqdot\frac{\alpha+10}{11}\in(\alpha,1)
, say. Indeed, this follows from
[
22
, Corollary 3.20]
and the Gauss equation since the surgery scale may be taken arbitrarily small. We can now proceed as in the proof of Proposition
3.4
in the time intervals
(T_{i},T_{i+1})
.
∎

4.2.
The codimension estimate

Similar reasoning shows that the codimension estimate holds for surgically modified flows for a suitable range of neck and surgery parameters, and surgery scales.

Proposition 4.3

(Codimension estimate for surgically modified flows)
.

Let
\{X_{i}:M_{i}^{n}\times[T_{i},T_{i+1}]\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}\}_{i=1}^{N-1}
,
n\geq 5
, be a surgically modified flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
(with
\alpha>\alpha_{n}
when
n=5,6,7
). There exist
\delta=\delta(n,\alpha,V,\Theta)>0
and
C=C(n,\alpha,\Theta)<\infty
such that

|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\leq CK^{\delta}(|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+K)^{1-\delta}\mskip 1.5mu\;\;\text{wherever}\;\;{\mathbf{H}}{}\neq 0\mskip 1.5mu.

Proof.

As in Proposition
3.4
, we seek a bound for

f_{\sigma}\doteqdot\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{2}|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}}{W}W^{\sigma}&\text{if}\;\;{\mathbf{H}}{}\neq 0\\
0&\text{if}\;\;{\mathbf{H}}{}=0\end{cases}

for some
\sigma\in(0,1)
.

The key observation is that, for a suitable range of neck and surgery parameters and surgery scales,
f_{\sigma}
is pointwise nonincreasing on regions modified during surgery, provided
\sigma
is small enough relative to the surgery parameters. This follows from
[
22
, Corollary 3.20]
. In addition, since
|{\hat{{\mathbf{A}}{}}}|^{2}\equiv 0
on a hypersurface cylinder or cap, we have that
f_{\sigma}\equiv 0
on regions added during surgery. The claim follows since, recalling the proof of Proposition
3.4
, for
\sigma
small depending on
n
and
m-\alpha
we have

f_{\sigma}\leq\max\left\{\max_{M\times\{T_{i}\}}f_{\sigma},CK^{\sigma}\right\}\mskip 1.5mu.

in each of the time intervals
(T_{i},T_{i+1})
, where
C=C(n,m-\alpha)
.
∎

4.3.
The cylindrical estimate

Similar reasoning yields a cylindrical estimate for surgically modified flows.

Proposition 4.4

(Cylindrical estimate for surgically modified flows (Cf.
[
15
, Theorem 5.3]
))
.

Let
\{X_{i}:M_{i}^{n}\times[T_{i},T_{i+1}]\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}\}_{i=1}^{N-1}
,
n\geq 5
, be a surgically modified flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
(with
\alpha>\alpha_{n}
when
n=5,6,7
). For every
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
there exists
C_{\eta}=C_{\eta}(n,\ell,\alpha,V,\Theta,\eta)<\infty
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n-1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\leq\eta|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}+C_{\eta}K\quad\text{in}\quad M_{i}\times[T_{i},T_{i+1}]

for all
i
.

Proof.

We can arrange, for suitable neck and surgery parameters, that
(g_{\sigma,\eta})_{+}
is pointwise non-increasing in regions modified by surgery, and zero in regions added by surgery. This follows from
[
22
, Corollary 3.20]
and the Gauss equation. Proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
3.7
, we obtain analogues of the inequalities (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) on each time interval
(T_{i},T_{i+1})
, with

replaced by
(g_{\sigma,\eta}-k)_{+}^{\frac{p}{2}}

is pointwise non-increasing across surgery times, and the total volume of the solution decreases under surgery, the analogue of (
3.21
) can be integrated from

to

to obtain an analogue of (
3.24
). The remainder of the proof of the cylindrical estimate then applies unmodified.
∎

4.4.
The gradient estimate

Since the derivatives of the second fundamental form are zero on round Euclidean cylinders and spherical caps, the derivative estimates also pass to surgically modified flows.

Proposition 4.5

(Gradient estimate for surgically modified flows (cf.
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
))
.

Let
\{X_{i}:M_{i}^{n}\times[T_{i},T_{i+1}]\to S_{K}^{n+\ell}\}_{i=1}^{N-1}
,

, be a surgically modified flow with initial condition in the class
\mathcal{C}_{K}^{n,\ell}(\alpha,V,\Theta)
(with

when

(4.2)

\displaystyle|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\leq C(|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{4}+K^{2})

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Proposition
3.9
, but with the exponential decay term discarded and fixed
\eta=\beta

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{n-1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}

|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{n}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{n-1}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\beta}{2}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}

\displaystyle G_{\beta}\doteqdot{}

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{1}{n-1}+\beta\right)|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}+2C_{\beta}K\geq\tfrac{\beta}{2}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}

\displaystyle G_{0}\doteqdot{}

\displaystyle\tfrac{3}{n+2}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}-|{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}+2C_{0}K\geq\tfrac{3}{2}\beta|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{2}.

Furthermore, since
|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\equiv 0
on a hypersurface cylinder or cap, we can ensure, for a suitable range of neck and surgery parameters, and surgery scales, that, on regions modified or added by surgery,
|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}\leq\mu_{0}|{\mathbf{H}}{}|^{4}

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla{\mathbf{A}}{}|^{2}}{G_{0}G_{\beta}}\leq\frac{4\mu_{0}}{3\beta^{2}}\,.

\beta
sufficiently small, we may now proceed as in the proof of Proposition
3.9
in the time intervals
(T_{i},T_{i+1})
.
∎

An eigenvalue problem for prescribed curvature equations
Taehun Lee

Abstract
. We study an eigenvalue problem for prescribed σ k \sigma_{k} -curvature equations of star-shaped, k k -convex, closed hypersurfaces. We establish the existence of a unique eigenvalue and its associated hypersurface, which is also unique, provided that the given data is even. Moreover, we show that the hypersurface must be strictly convex. A crucial aspect of our proof involves deriving uniform estimates in p p for L p L_{p} -type prescribed curvature equations.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth, closed hypersurface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. We use
\nu(X)
and
\kappa(X)
to denote the outward unit normal and principal curvatures of the hypersurface
\Sigma
at point
X
, respectively.
This paper concerns the following eigenvalue problem for prescribed curvature equations:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle{X},{\nu(X)}\right\rangle^{k}\sigma_{k}(\kappa(X))=\lambda\psi(\nu(X))\quad\text{for any }X\in\Sigma,

where
\sigma_{k}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
represents the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree
k
and
\psi
is a smooth, positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The curvature
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
includes the mean curvature, scalar curvature, and Gauss curvature for cases when
k=1,2
, and
n
, respectively.

Equation (
1.1
) is connected to singularity models for an anisotropic flow governed by powers of curvature
\sigma_{k}
, see
[
4
,
12
,
13
,
24
,
31
]
. Indeed, for
p\not=1
, if
X
denotes a parametrization of the evolving hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=-\operatorname{sign}(1-p)(f(\nu)\sigma_{k}(\kappa))^{\frac{1}{1-p}}\nu,

then its self-similar solutions fulfill

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle{X},{\nu(X)}\right\rangle^{p-1}\sigma_{k}(\kappa(X))=\lambda/f(\nu(X))

for some constant
\lambda>0
.
Therefore, (
1.1
) corresponds to (
1.2
) with
p=k+1
and the anisotropic function
f=1/\psi
. In particular, for the case of
k=1
and
p=2
, equation (
1.2
) represents a self-similar solution of the (anisotropic) inverse mean curvature flow
[
10
,
12
]
. Note that (
1.2
) remains invariant under dilation of the hypersurface if and only if
p=k+1
, and the constant
\lambda
in (
1.2
) can be absorbed by scaling when
p\not=k+1
.

In the case of
k=n
, equation (
1.2
) becomes the celebrated
L_{p}
Minkowski problem initiated by Lutwak
[
28
]
, where the admissible solutions are convex. This is a central problem in convex geometry, and there is a vast literature on the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem; see for instance
[
3
,
11
,
22
,
29
,
34
]
and the references therein. In terms of the support function
u:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
defined by
u(x)=\left\langle{x},{\nu^{-1}(x)}\right\rangle
for a strictly convex hypersurface
\Sigma
, equation (
1.2
) can be transformed into

(1.3)

\displaystyle\det(\nabla^{2}u+uI)=\lambda^{-1}u^{p-1}f\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
\nabla
denotes the covariant derivative with respect to an orthonormal frame on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
I
is the identity matrix. The existence results for (
1.3
) can be found in
[
11
]
for
p>-n-1
and
[
22
]
for
p<-n-1
. In particular, Chou and Wang
[
11
]
completely resolved the eigenvalue problem in this case (
p=n+1
in (
1.3
) or
k=n
in (
1.1
)) by obtaining a unique pair
(u,\lambda)
, where
u
is the support function of a strictly convex, closed hypersurface that is positive on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and
\lambda>0
is the eigenvalue.

On the other hand, when
p=1
, equation (
1.2
) reduces to the well-known prescribed curvature equation. In
[
14
]
, Guan and Guan demonstrated the existence of a strictly convex, closed hypersurface satisfying (
1.2
), under the condition that
f
is even, that is,
f(x)=f(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

We note that an equation analogous to (
1.2
) arises in the context of curvature measures, which can be expressed as follows:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\kappa(X))=\frac{\left\langle{X},{\nu(X)}\right\rangle}{|X|^{n+1}}f\left(\frac{X}{|X|}\right).

Given certain conditions on
f
, Guan, Lin, and Ma
[
17
]
demonstrated the existence of convex solutions to this equation. Moreover, Guan, Li, and Li
[
15
]
proved the existence of a unique admissible solution (which is
k
-convex and star-shaped, see
Definition

1.1
below) for a general
f
. The
L^{p}
dual generalization of this problem, in the measure form of (
1.4
), has been further investigated in
[
2
,
30
]
.

In this paper we are interested in the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) for all
1\leq k<n
, assuming that
\psi
is an even function, that is,
\psi(x)=\psi(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
To describe an admissible hypersurface
\Sigma
of the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
), it is necessary to define the notions of a
k
-convex and a star-shaped hypersurface.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
C^{2}
regular hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

(i)

A hypersurface
\Sigma
is said to be
k
-convex
if its principal curvatures
\kappa(X)=(\kappa_{1}(X),\ldots,\kappa_{n}(X))
lie in
\Gamma_{k}
for all
X\in\Sigma
. Here,
\Gamma_{k}
represents the G
\mathring{a}
rding cone, defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\sigma_{i}(\lambda)>0,i=1,\ldots,k\right\}.

(ii)

A hypersurface
\Sigma
is called
star-shaped
if
\left\langle{X},{\nu(X)}\right\rangle>0
for all
X\in\Sigma
.

We say that a hypersurface
\Sigma
is
admissible
if
\Sigma
is a
k
-convex, star-shaped hypersurface. The number
\lambda
is referred to as the eigenvalue of (
1.1
) if there exists an admissible hypersurface
\Sigma
that satisfies (
1.1
) for the given
\lambda
. We note that (
1.1
) is ensured to be elliptic at all admissible hypersurfaces.

We now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
1\leq k<n
, and let
\psi
be a smooth, positive, even function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then, there exists a unique eigenvalue
\lambda>0
for (
1.1
). Moreover, the corresponding admissible hypersurface
\Sigma
is strictly convex and unique up to dilation.

Remark 1.3
.

(i)

From the uniqueness, it can be deduced that the hypersurface
\Sigma
is origin-symmetric.

(ii)

The evenness of
\psi
will be used in establishing the existence of an eigenvalue and hypersurface.
The uniqueness of the eigenvalue and hypersurface does not require the evenness of
\psi
. See the proof of
Theorem

1.2
in
Section

5
.

(iii)

As previously noted, the case when
k=n
was resolved for general
f
in
[
11
]
.

We emphasize that, unlike the Laplace operator which possesses an infinite number of eigenvalues, our problem (
1.1
) has only one eigenvalue. Furthermore, any origin-symmetric admissible solution to (
1.1
) must be strictly convex.

The proof of the main theorem relies on a compactness argument by deriving uniform estimates in
p
for (
1.2
) with
k+1<p<k+1+\varepsilon
. Additionally, in the same range of
p
, the existence and uniqueness of (
1.2
) are required, where the existence follows from the work of Guan, Ren, and Wang
[
20
]
(see
Proposition

2.1
below). We will prove the uniqueness in
Proposition

2.2
. Combining existence and uniqueness, we summarize the result as follows.
Here,
f
is not necessarily even. An admissible solution to (
1.2
) is defined in the same manner, imposing conditions that the hypersurface is
k
-convex and star-shaped.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
p>k+1
with
1\leq k\leq n
, and let
f
be a smooth, positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then, there exists a unique admissible solution to (
1.2
) for any
\lambda>0
. Moreover, the solution must be strictly convex.

We note that the quantity
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
corresponds to an operator in Hessian quotient equation when expressed as a support function. Indeed, if
\Sigma
is strictly convex, then in terms of the support function
u
of
\Sigma
, (
1.2
) becomes

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\det(\nabla^{2}u+uI)}{\sigma_{n-k}(\nabla^{2}u+uI)}=\frac{fu^{p-1}}{\lambda}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n},

as described in (
2.9
) below. When
k=n
, we can see that this equation reduces to the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem (
1.3
), as mentioned earlier.

Another important equation that includes (
1.3
) is the equation for the
L_{p}
Christoffel–Minkowski problem, which is formulated as

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\nabla^{2}u+uI)=\frac{fu^{p-1}}{\lambda}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n}.

See Bryan–Ivaki–Scheuer
[
5
]
, Guan–Lin–Ma
[
16
]
, Guan–Ma
[
18
]
, and Guan–Ma–Zhou
[
19
]
for the case
p=1
; Guan–Xia
[
21
]
for
1<p<k+1
; Hu–Ma–Shen
[
23
]
for
p\geq k+1
.

When
p=k+1
, a key distinction between equations (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) is that (
1.5
) permits cone singularities with the origin as the vertex of the cone for some non-symmetric
f
(see
[
12
]
for instance), whereas (
1.6
) guarantees that the origin is always located inside
\Sigma
, leading to a smooth solution
[
23
]
.

The fully non-linear equations in the Hessian quotient class have been investigated over a domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by Caffarelli, Nirenberg, and Spruck
[
6
]
, Krylov
[
26
]
, Trudinger
[
35
,
36
]
, and Bao, Chen, Guan, and Ji
[
1
]
. See also
[
25
]
for a crucial application of the Hessian quotient in the convexity estimate of the mean convex mean curvature flow. Recently, Chen and Xu
[
9
]
addressed the quotient of mixed Hessians of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{k}(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{1},\ldots,\bar{\mu}-\mu_{n})}{\sigma_{l}(\bar{\mu}-\mu_{1},\ldots,\bar{\mu}-\mu_{n})}=\frac{fu^{p-1}}{\lambda}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
\mu_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\mu_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
\nabla^{2}u+uI
,
\bar{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}
,

0\leq l<k\leq n
, and
p\geq k-l+1
.

Finally, we conclude the introduction by mentioning the eigenvalue problems associated with the Monge–Ampère equation over a convex domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which can be found in
[
27
,
33
,
37
]
. These problems may be viewed as the Euclidean counterpart of the eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) with
k=n
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect relevant information, such as properties of
\sigma_{k}
, radial functions for star-shaped hypersurfaces, and support functions in convex geometry. In addition, the proof of
Theorem

1.4
is presented. In Section
3
, we derive estimates for volume and diameter when

. Section
4
dedicated to establishing curvature estimates. Finally, the proof of
Theorem

1.2
is provided in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

We refer to
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
for general background on
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
. Recall that

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\kappa)=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{k}}.

With an abuse of notation, we often denote by
\sigma_{k}(A)
the
k^{\text{th}}
elementary symmetric polynomial with respect to the eigenvalues of a symmetric two-tensor
A
.

The elementary symmetric sum
\sigma_{k}
satisfies the following property, known as the Maclaurin inequality:

(2.1)

\displaystyle c_{1}\sigma_{1}\geq c_{2}\sigma_{2}^{1/2}\geq\cdots\geq c_{n}\sigma_{n}^{1/n}

for some dimensional constant
c_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.
It is also well-known that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\kappa_{i}}(\kappa)>0\quad\text{for all }\kappa\in\Gamma_{k}\text{ and }i=1,\ldots,n

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}\left(\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n}\right)=\frac{\sigma_{n-k}(\kappa_{1}^{-1},\ldots,\kappa_{n}^{-1})}{\sigma_{n}(\kappa_{1}^{-1},\ldots,\kappa_{n}^{-1})}.

We recall some basic properties of star-shaped hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
; for details, see
[
32
]
. For a star-shaped hypersurface, the position vector
X
can be expressed as
X=\rho(x)x
for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
using the radial function
\rho:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, the induced metric
g_{ij}
is given as

(2.4)

\displaystyle g_{ij}[\rho]=\rho^{2}\delta_{ij}+\nabla_{i}\rho\nabla_{j}\rho.

The unit outward normal is given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{\rho x-\nabla\rho}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}}},

and the relation between the radial function
\rho
and the support function
u=\left\langle{X},{\nu}\right\rangle
is

(2.6)

\displaystyle u=\frac{\rho^{2}}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}}}.

The second fundamental form can be computed as

(2.7)

\displaystyle h_{ij}[\rho]=\left(\rho^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\rho^{2}\delta_{ij}+2\nabla_{i}\rho\nabla_{j}\rho-\rho\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\rho\right).

For a moment, let us consider
\Sigma
as a strictly convex, closed hypersurface in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
. Let
\nu(x)
be the unit outer normal vector to
\Sigma
at
x\in\Sigma
. The Gauss map
\nu
is then a diffeomorphism from
\Sigma
onto
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The support function
u:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
of
\Sigma
is defined as

\displaystyle u(x)=\sup\{\left\langle{x},{y}\right\rangle:y\in\Sigma\}=\left\langle{x},{\nu^{-1}(x)}\right\rangle,\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

One can verify that the second fundamental form
h
is given by the expression

\displaystyle h_{ij}[u]=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u+u\delta_{ij}.

Using this and the Ricci identity, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{l}h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{l}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u+(\nabla_{l}u)\delta_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{j}u+\overline{R}_{lijm}\nabla_{m}u+(\nabla_{l}u)\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{j}u+(\delta_{lj}\delta_{im}-\delta_{lm}\delta_{ij})\nabla_{m}u+(\nabla_{l}u)\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{l}\nabla_{j}u+(\nabla_{i}u)\delta_{lj}=\nabla_{i}h_{lj}

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}\nabla_{l}h_{ij}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}h_{lj}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}h_{lj}+\overline{R}_{kilm}h_{mj}+\overline{R}_{kijm}h_{lm}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}h_{kl}+(\delta_{kl}\delta_{im}-\delta_{km}\delta_{il})h_{mj}+(\delta_{kj}\delta_{im}-\delta_{km}\delta_{ij})h_{lm}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}h_{kl}+\delta_{kl}h_{ij}-h_{kj}\delta_{il}+\delta_{kj}h_{il}-h_{kl}\delta_{ij},

where
\overline{R}_{ijkl}
is the Riemannian curvature tensor on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.
Hence, it follows that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Delta h_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\operatorname{tr}(h)+nh_{ij}-\operatorname{tr}(h)\delta_{ij}.

The eigenvalues of
h
correspond to the reciprocals of the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}=\kappa_{i}[u]
so that

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\nabla^{2}u+uI)=\sigma_{k}(\kappa_{1}[u]^{-1},\ldots,\kappa_{n}[u]^{-1}).

By using (
2.3
), equation (
1.2
) can be reformulated as

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\det(\nabla^{2}u+uI)}{\sigma_{n-k}(\nabla^{2}u+uI)}=\frac{fu^{p-1}}{\lambda}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n}.

In light of equation (
1.3
), equation (
2.9
) migh be referred to as an
L_{p}
-type curvature equation.
Since
\sigma_{k}^{1/k}
is concave operator, it is natural to define
F(A)=(\sigma_{n}(A)/\sigma_{n-k}(A))^{1/k}
. We then rewrite (
2.9
) as

\displaystyle F(\nabla^{2}u+uI)=(f\lambda^{-1})^{1/k}u^{\frac{p-1}{k}}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n}.

We conclude this section by establishing the existence and uniqueness results for (
1.2
) when
k>p+1
. The following two propositions prove
Theorem

1.4
. As mentioned in the introduction, the existence result is a consequence of Theorem 1.5 in
[
20
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p>k+1
, and let
f
be a smooth positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then there exists a strictly convex hypersurface
\Sigma
satisfying (
1.2
).

Proof.

It suffices to show the following (i) barrier condition and (ii) convexity property in
[
20
]
: (i) there is a constant
r>1
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{f}\left(X,\frac{X}{|X|}\right)\leq\frac{\sigma_{k}(1,1,\ldots,1)}{r^{k}}\quad\text{for }|X|=r,

where
\tilde{f}(X,\nu)=\frac{\lambda\psi(\nu)}{\left\langle{X},{\nu}\right\rangle^{p-1}}
; (ii)
\tilde{f}^{-1/k}(X,\nu)
is locally convex in
X
for any fixed
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

The former holds since
\tilde{f}(X,X/|X|)=\lambda\psi(\nu)r^{1-p}
and
1-p<-k
, and the latter follows from
\tilde{f}^{-1/k}(X,\nu)=(\lambda\psi(\nu))^{-1/k}\left\langle{X},{\nu}\right\rangle^{\frac{p-1}{k}}
and
\frac{p-1}{k}>1
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
p>k+1
. If
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
are two admissible solutions of (
1.2
), then
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{i}
,
u_{i}
, and
\nu_{i}
(
i=1,2
) be the radial function, support function, and outward unit normal of
\Sigma_{i}
, respectively. Note that
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are positive functions. Suppose that
v=\log(\rho_{1}/\rho_{2})
attains its maximum at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then, at
x_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{j}v

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{j}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{\nabla_{j}\rho_{2}}{\rho_{2}},

\displaystyle 0\geq\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}v

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{\nabla_{i}\rho_{1}\nabla_{j}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\rho_{2}}{\rho_{2}}+\frac{\nabla_{i}\rho_{2}\nabla_{j}\rho_{2}}{\rho_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\rho_{2}}{\rho_{2}}.

Then it follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.7
) that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\frac{g_{ij}[\rho_{1}]}{\rho_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}+\frac{\nabla_{i}\rho_{1}\nabla_{j}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}^{2}}=\delta_{ij}+\frac{\nabla_{i}\rho_{2}\nabla_{j}\rho_{2}}{\rho_{2}^{2}}=\frac{g_{ij}[\rho_{2}]}{\rho_{2}^{2}}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{h_{ij}[\rho_{1}]}{\rho_{1}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+|\nabla\rho_{1}|^{2}/\rho_{1}^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\delta_{ij}+2\frac{\nabla_{i}\rho_{1}\nabla_{j}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\rho_{1}}{\rho_{1}}\right)\geq\frac{h_{ij}[\rho_{2}]}{\rho_{2}}.

Similarly, by utilizing homogeneity, we can obtain from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{u_{1}}{\rho_{1}}=\frac{u_{2}}{\rho_{2}}.

Since the principal curvatures are the eigenvalue of
h_{ij}
with respect to
g_{ij}
, it follows from (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) that

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\kappa_{i}[\rho_{1}]\geq\rho_{2}\kappa_{i}[\rho_{2}]\quad\text{for all }i=1,2,\dots,n.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{1}^{k}\sigma_{k}(\kappa[\rho_{1}])\geq\rho_{2}^{k}\sigma_{k}(\kappa[\rho_{2}]).

Using (
1.2
) and (
2.12
), we get

\displaystyle\lambda u_{1}^{1-p}=f(\nu_{1})\sigma_{k}(\kappa[\rho_{1}])\geq f(\nu_{2})\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{k}\sigma_{k}(\kappa[\rho_{2}])=\lambda\left(\frac{u_{2}}{u_{1}}\right)^{k}u_{2}^{1-p}.

This implies that
\rho_{1}^{k+1-p}\geq\rho_{2}^{k+1-p}
. Since
p>k+1
, we have
\rho_{1}\leq\rho_{2}
at
x_{0}
. Consequently,
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}v\leq 1
. Similarly, we obtain
\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}v\geq 1
, which proves that
v\equiv 1
and, therefore,
\rho_{1}\equiv\rho_{2}
.
∎

3.
Volume and diameter estimates for
p>k+1

In this section, we provide estimates for volume and diameter, which will be used to extract a sequence after suitable scaling in a subsequent section. According to
Theorem

1.4
, there exists a unique, strictly convex solution
u=u_{p}
to (
1.2
) with
\lambda=1
after appropriate dilation, provided
p>k+1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
p>k+1
. If
u
is the solution to equation (
1.2
) with
\lambda=1
, then for the volume
V(\Sigma)
of the region bounded by the associated hypersurface
\Sigma
, it holds that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f}\leq\left(\frac{V(\Sigma)}{V(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{p-k-1}{n+1}}\leq\frac{1}{\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f}.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be the radius of the ball centered at the origin such that
V(B_{\rho})=V(\Sigma)
.
We first observe

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\leq\rho\leq\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u.

If
u
achieves its minimum at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then
\nabla^{2}u(x_{0})\geq 0
and consequently,
h_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}u+u\delta_{ij}\geq u\delta_{ij}
at
x_{0}
. Since the eigenvalues of
h_{ij}
are the principal radii, we deduce
\kappa_{i}\leq u^{-1}
and
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)\leq u^{-k}
at the point
x_{0}
. It follows from (
1.2
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f(x_{0})}=u(x_{0})^{p-1}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)(x_{0})\leq u(x_{0})^{p-k-1}\leq\rho^{p-k-1},

and hence, the first inequality in (
3.1
) is derived from

\displaystyle V(\Sigma)=V(B_{\rho})=\rho^{n+1}V(B_{1}).

The second inequality in (
3.1
) can be obtained through similar arguments, by considering the maximum point of
u
rather than the minimum point.
∎

The next lemma is valid for a more extensive range of
p
; however, the range
p>k+1
suffices for proving the main theorem.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p>1-k/n>0
. If
u
is the solution to (
1.2
) with
\lambda=1
and
\Sigma
is the associated hypersurface, then there exists a constant
C
that depends only on
n
,
k
,
p
, and
\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u\leq C\left[V(\Sigma)\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{*}}},

where
p_{*}=1+\frac{(p-1)n}{k}>0
. Here, the constant
C
is finite for all
p>1-k/n
and increases in
p

Remark 3.3
.

Due to the monotone increasing property of the constant in equation (
3.2
), it uniformly holds for
p\in(k+1,k+1+\epsilon)
, where
\epsilon>0
is any number.

Proof.

Suppose that
u
attains its maximum at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Due to the convexity of
\Sigma
, the hypersurface
\Sigma
encloses the line segment joining
u(x_{0})x_{0}
and the origin. Thus we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle u(x)\geq\max\{\left\langle{u(x_{0})x_{0}},{x}\right\rangle,0\}=u(x_{0})\max\{\left\langle{x_{0}},{x}\right\rangle,0\}.

Applying the Maclaurin inequality (
2.1
), we deduce that
c_{k}\sigma_{k}^{1/k}\geq c_{n}\sigma_{n}^{1/n}
. This leads to

\displaystyle\frac{cu}{K}=\frac{cu}{\sigma_{n}}\geq\frac{u}{\sigma_{k}^{n/k}}=u^{1+\frac{(p-1)n}{k}}f^{\frac{n}{k}}=u^{p_{*}}f^{\frac{n}{k}}

for some dimensional constant
c>0
.
Hence,

(3.4)

\displaystyle c(n+1)V(\Sigma)=c\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{u}{K}\geq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u^{p_{*}}f^{\frac{n}{k}}\geq\left(\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f\right)^{\frac{n}{k}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u^{p_{*}}.

Observe that
p_{*}=1+\frac{(p-1)n}{k}=\frac{pn-(n-k)}{k}>0
. From (
3.3
), we derive

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u^{p_{*}}\geq u(x_{0})^{p_{*}}\int_{\left\langle{x_{0}},{x}\right\rangle\geq 0}\left\langle{x_{0}},{x}\right\rangle^{p_{*}},

where the last integral is invariant under
x_{0}
due to symmetry and is a positive constant that decreases in
p_{*}
. The conclusion then follows from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).
∎

The
C^{1}
estimate is derived from the convexity property.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u
be a smooth, strictly convex solution to (
1.1
). Then

\displaystyle|\nabla u|\leq\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u.

Proof.

Since
|X|^{2}=u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla u|\leq\sqrt{u^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}}=|X|\leq\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|X|=\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u

which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Uniform positive lower bound of curvature for
0<p-k-1<\varepsilon

The purpose of this section is to establish that the sum of the reciprocals of the principal curvatures remains bounded by the same constant for
k+1<p<k+1+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon>0
is any number.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
p>k+1
, and let
u
be the solution of the equation (
1.2
) with
\lambda=1
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}\leq\frac{2n(p-1)}{k}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{p-1}{k}}\left\lVert f^{\frac{1}{k}}\right\rVert_{C^{1,1}}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle W:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\kappa_{i}}=\operatorname{tr}(h)=\Delta u+nu.

We will apply the maximum principle to
W
. By recalling equation (
2.8
) and taking semi-positive definite matrix
F^{ij}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial h_{ij}}
to both sides of the equation, we obtain

(4.1)

\displaystyle F^{ij}\Delta h_{ij}=F^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}W+nF^{ij}h_{ij}-W\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}.

Here and henceforth, we employ the Einstein summation convention, in which repeated upper and lower indices are automatically summed.

Since
F
is homogeneous of degree one, we obtain

\displaystyle F^{ij}h_{ij}=F=u^{\frac{p-1}{k}}f^{\frac{1}{k}}.

It is known
[
35
]
(see also
[
18
]
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{ii}}\left(\frac{\sigma_{n}/\binom{n}{n}}{\sigma_{n-k}/\binom{n}{n-k}}\right)^{1/k}\geq n\quad\text{so}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}\geq n\left[\binom{n}{k}\right]^{-1/k}\geq 1.

By taking the second derivatives to equation (
1.2
) and utilizing the concavity of
F
, we obtain the inequality:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Delta(u^{\frac{p-1}{k}}f^{\frac{1}{k}})=\Delta F=F^{ij,lm}\nabla_{q}h_{ij}\nabla^{q}h_{lm}+F^{ij}\Delta h_{ij}\leq F^{ij}\Delta h_{ij}.

As
p>k+1
, we have
a:=\frac{p-1}{k}>1
. Note that
\Delta u=W-nu\geq-nu
. Since

\displaystyle\Delta u^{a}=au^{a-1}\Delta u+a(a-1)u^{a-2}|\nabla u|^{2}\geq au^{a-1}\Delta u=-nau^{a},

we obtain through direct computation

\displaystyle\Delta(u^{a}f^{\frac{1}{k}})

\displaystyle=f^{\frac{1}{k}}\Delta u^{a}+2\nabla u^{a}\nabla f^{\frac{1}{k}}+u^{a}\Delta f^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\geq-2au^{a-1}|\nabla u||\nabla f^{\frac{1}{k}}|+u^{a}(\Delta f^{\frac{1}{k}}-naf^{\frac{1}{k}}).

Hence, combining with (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle F^{ij}\Delta h_{ij}\geq-2au^{a-1}|\nabla u||\nabla f^{\frac{1}{k}}|+u^{a}(\Delta f^{\frac{1}{k}}-naf^{\frac{1}{k}}).

Thus, (
4.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle F^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}W

\displaystyle=F^{ij}\Delta h_{ij}-nF^{ij}h_{ij}+W\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{ii}

\displaystyle\geq-2au^{a-1}|\nabla u||\nabla f^{\frac{1}{k}}|+u^{a}(\Delta f^{\frac{1}{k}}-n(a+1)f^{\frac{1}{k}})+W.

Using
Lemma

3.4
, we deduce at the maximum point of
W
,

\displaystyle W\leq 2au^{a-1}|\nabla u||\nabla f^{\frac{1}{k}}|-u^{a}(\Delta f^{\frac{1}{k}}-n(a+1)f^{\frac{1}{k}})\leq 2na\lVert{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}^{a}\lVert{f^{\frac{1}{k}}}\rVert_{C^{1,1}},

which completes our proof.
∎

5.
Proof of
Theorem

1.2

We first prove the existence of a strictly convex solution to (
1.1
).
Suppose
0<p-k-1<1
, and let
u_{p}
be the unique positive solution to (
1.2
) with
\lambda=1
. This is possible due to
Theorem

1.4
. Let
\Sigma_{p}
be the associated hypersurface whose support function is
u_{p}
. By the uniqueness of
u_{p}
and the evenness of
\psi
, we see that
\Sigma_{p}
is origin-symmetric.

Recalling
p_{*}
in
Lemma

3.2
, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{*}}-\frac{1}{n+1}=\frac{k}{np-n+k}-\frac{1}{n+1}=\frac{n(k+1-p)}{(np-n+k)(n+1)}.

By
Lemma

3.1
and
Lemma

3.2
, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle u_{p}\leq C\left[V(\Sigma_{p})\right]^{\frac{1}{p_{*}}}=C\left[V(\Sigma_{p})\right]^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\left[V(\Sigma_{p})^{\frac{p-k-1}{n+1}}\right]^{-\frac{n}{(np-n+k)}}\leq C\left[V(\Sigma_{p})\right]^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

Here we may assume the constant
C
is uniform in
p
since we restrict the upper bound of
p
.

Define the volume normalized solution as

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{p}=\frac{u_{p}}{[V(\Sigma_{p})]^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}

and denote by
\tilde{\Sigma}_{p}
the associated hypersurface of
\tilde{u}_{p}
.
Then
\tilde{u}_{p}
solves

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}(\kappa)}=[V(\Sigma_{p})]^{\frac{p-k-1}{n+1}}u^{p-1}f=:\frac{u^{p-1}f}{\lambda_{p}}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Moreover, it follows from the definition of
\tilde{u}_{p}
, (
5.1
), and
Lemma

3.1
that

(5.3)

\displaystyle V(\tilde{\Sigma}_{p})=1,\quad 0<\tilde{u}_{p}\leq C,\quad\text{and}\quad 1/C\leq\lambda_{p}\leq C.

We can assume that, by considering a subsequence,
\tilde{u}_{p}
and
\lambda_{p}
converge uniformly to a function
u_{0}
and a number
\lambda_{0}
, respectively, as
p
approaches
k+1
from above. Note that

\displaystyle 0\leq u_{0}\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad 1/C\leq\lambda_{0}\leq C.

Furthermore, it follows from the Blaschke selection theorem that
u_{0}
is the support function of some convex hypersurface, say
\Sigma_{0}
. Since
\Sigma_{p}
are origin-symmetric, so is
\Sigma_{0}
. Thus, if
u_{0}(x_{0})=0
at a point
x_{0}
, then we have
u_{0}(-x_{0})=0
, which contradicts to
V(\Sigma_{0})=1
. Therefore,
u_{0}>0
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Our purpose is now to show that the pair
(\Sigma_{0},\lambda_{0})
is the desired solution to (
1.1
), which can be done by using
Lemma

4.1
. Indeed, let
\tilde{u}_{p_{j}}
be a sequence such that
\tilde{u}_{p_{j}}\rightarrow u_{0}
uniformly. Since
u_{0}>0
, we may assume that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{p_{j}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{0}>0\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Applying
Lemma

4.1
to
u_{p_{j}}
and using (
5.1
), we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\tilde{\kappa}_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{V(\Sigma_{p})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}}{\kappa_{i}}\leq CV(\Sigma_{p})^{\frac{p-1}{k(n+1)}-\frac{1}{n+1}}=C\lambda_{p}^{-\frac{1}{k}}\leq C,

where
\{\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\}
are the principal curvatures of
\tilde{u}_{p_{j}}
and the last constant
C
does not depend on
p
. Combining (
5.2
), (
5.3
), (
5.4
), and (
5.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\max_{i}\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\leq\frac{\sigma_{k}(\tilde{\kappa})}{\left(\min_{i}\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\right)^{k-1}}\leq\frac{u^{1-p}}{\lambda_{p}f\left(\min_{i}\tilde{\kappa}_{i}\right)^{k-1}}\leq C.

Therefore,
\tilde{u}_{p_{j}}
solves the following uniformly elliptic equation

\displaystyle\frac{\det(\nabla^{2}u+uI)}{\sigma_{n-k}(\nabla^{2}u+uI)}=\frac{u^{p-1}f}{\lambda_{p}}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{S}^{n},

and by the standard elliptic theory, we have
C^{3,\alpha}
estimates:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{u}_{p_{j}}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C.

Passing to a subsequence, we finally see that
u_{0}
with
\lambda_{0}
satisfies (
1.1
).

Lastly, we prove uniqueness. Assume that (
1.1
) has two distinct
k
-convex solutions, namely
\left(\Sigma_{1},\lambda_{1}\right)
and
\left(\Sigma_{2},\lambda_{2}\right)
. Notice that since (
1.1
) is scale invariant, any solution multiplied by a constant continues to be a solution. By appropriately scaling
\Sigma_{2}
, we can ensure that
\Sigma_{2}
is enclosed within
\Sigma_{1}
and touches
\Sigma_{1}
at a point
X_{0}
. We then observe that, at the point

,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}\psi(\nu_{\Sigma_{1}}(X_{0}))}{\left\langle{X_{0}},{\nu_{\Sigma_{1}}(X_{0})}\right\rangle^{k}}=\sigma_{k}(\kappa[\Sigma_{1}])\leq\sigma_{k}(\kappa[\Sigma_{2}])=\frac{\lambda_{2}\psi(\nu_{\Sigma_{2}}(X_{0}))}{\left\langle{X_{0}},{\nu_{\Sigma_{2}}(X_{0})}\right\rangle^{k}},

where we used the monotonicity (

) of
\sigma_{k}
with respect to
\kappa
. Since
\nu_{\Sigma_{1}}(X_{0})=\nu_{\Sigma_{2}}(X_{0})
, we conclude that
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
. Changing the role of
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, we also get
\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{1}
. Therefore,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
.

Suppose we have two different solutions,
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, of equation (

) with the same

\Sigma_{2}
is not a constant multiple of
\Sigma_{1}
, we can multiply
\Sigma_{2}
by an appropriate constant so that, after rotation if necessary, parts of

and

can be represented as graphs of

and

, respectively, over the same domain

on

and

in

.

On the other hand, it is known that
\sigma_{k}(\kappa[\Sigma_{i}])=G(DU_{i},D^{2}U_{i})

, as shown in

]

Limit of nonlinear Signorini problems for incompressible materials
Edoardo Mainini, Danilo Percivale, Robertus van der Putten

Abstract
. The paper is devoted to the linearization of the non linear Signorini functional in the incompressible case. The limit functional, in the sense of Γ \Gamma -convergence, may coincide with the expected one only in some particular cases.

Linear elasticity; Finite elasticity;

1.
Introduction

In linear elastostatics, the classical Signorini problem
[
23
]
requires to find the equilibrium of an elastic body subject to external forces and resting on a rigid support
E\!\subset\!\partial\Omega
in its reference configuration
\Omega
. Precisely, if
\Omega
is subject to suitable volume and surface forces
{\mathbf{f}}:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
{\mathbf{g}}:\partial\Omega\!\setminus\!E\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u})\,:=\,\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{u}\ d{\bf x}+\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus E}\mathbf{g}\cdot\mathbf{u}\ d{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

is the load potential and
\mathbf{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the displacement field, then, by assuming that
\mathcal{H}^{2}(E)\!>\!0
, the variational formulation of the Signorini problem consists in finding a minimizer of the functional

(1.2)

\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{u}):=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}\big({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u})\big)\,d{\bf x}-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u})

among all
\mathbf{u}
in the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}\geq 0

\,\mathcal{H}^{2}
-​ a.e. on
E
where
\mathbf{n}
is the inward unit vector normal to
\partial\Omega
and
\mathcal{H}^{2}
is the two-dimensional Hausdorff measure. As usually happens, here

\mathbb{E}
denotes the linear strain tensor,
\mathbb{C}
represents the classical linear elasticity tensor and

\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbb{E})\,:=\,\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}^{T}\mathbb{C}({\bf x})\,\mathbb{E}

is the corresponding strain energy density (see
[
8
]
).
A classical result (see
[
6
]
) states that a minimizer exists if
\mathcal{L}({\bf v})\!\leq\!0\,
for
every infinitesimal rigid displacement
{\bf v}
such that
\,{\bf v}\cdot\mathbf{n}\!\geq\!0

\,\mathcal{H}^{2}
-​ a.e. on
E
and
\mathcal{L}({\bf v})\!=\!0
if and only if
{\bf v}\cdot\mathbf{n}\equiv 0

\,\mathcal{H}^{2}
-​ a.e. on
E
.
More recently a proper generalization of the latter formulation has been given by assuming that the set
E
has positive Sobolev capacity
and accordingly modifying the obstacle condition by requiring
{\bf v}\cdot\mathbf{n}\geq 0
on
E
up to a set of null capacity (shortly, q.e. on
E
): the existence of minimizers for this general setting was proved in
[
2
, Theorem 4.5]
.
Although the original formulation given in
[
23
]
may look different from the generalized notion exploited in
[
2
]
, it can be shown (see Remark
2.1
) that if the set
E\!\subset\!\partial\Omega
is regular in an appropriate sense then the two frameworks coincide.

In the recent paper
[
16
]
it has been shown that, under sharp conditions on
\mathcal{L}
, there exists a sequence of (suitably rescaled) functionals
\mathcal{G}_{h}
of finite elasticity such that
\inf\mathcal{G}_{h}
converges to the minimum of
\mathcal{E}
and that in addition there are examples in which this convergence fails.
The aim of this paper is to show that at least in the planar obstacle case, namely when
E\subset\partial\Omega\cap\{{\bf x}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{3}=0\}\not\equiv\emptyset
(being
(\mathbf{e}_{i}),

i=1,2,3,
the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
) a similar result holds also for incompressible energy densities.
More in detail, denoting by
\mathbf{y}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
the deformation field and by
h>0
an adimensional parameter, we introduce
a strain energy density
\mathcal{W}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty]
, which is frame indifferent and minimized at the identity, and the family of energy functionals

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}(\mathbf{y}):=h^{-2}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{W}^{I}\big({\bf x},\nabla\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})\big)\,d{\bf x}-h^{-1}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})

where
\mathcal{L}
is defined as in (
1.1
) and
\mathcal{W}^{I}
is the incompressible strain energy density (coinciding with
\mathcal{W}
if
\det\nabla\mathbf{y}\equiv 1
and set equal to
+\infty
otherwise).
We define

\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}(\mathbf{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}(\mathbf{y})&\ \ \hbox{if }\ {\mathbf{y}}\in\mathcal{A}\\
\ +\infty&\ \ \hbox{otherwise in $H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
E
, the portion of the elastic body that is sensitive to the obstacle, is such that
\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits E>0
, and where
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(E)
denotes the class of admissible deformations (i.e., those
\mathbf{y}\in H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{3}\geq 0
quasi-everywhere on
E
, i.e., up to sets of null capacity).
Moreover we have to assume that

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})\leq 0

for every rigid deformation
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{A}
. Indeed, if there exists a rigid deformation
\overline{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathcal{L}(\overline{\mathbf{y}}-{\bf x})>0
, then

\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}(\overline{\mathbf{y}})=-h^{-1}\mathcal{L}(\overline{\mathbf{y}}-\mathbf{x})\to-\infty\ \text{as}\ h\to 0^{+}.

Therefore, since a rigid deformation
\mathbf{y}({\bf x})=\mathbf{R}{\bf x}+\mathbf{c}
with
\mathbf{R}\!\in\!SO(3),\ \mathbf{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},
belongs to
\mathcal{A}
whenever
c_{3}\geq-\left(\mathbf{R}{\bf x}\right)_{3}
on
E
, we have to assume

(1.3)

\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x}+\mathbf{c})\leq 0\quad\mbox{for every $\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)$ and every $\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ s.t. $c_{3}\geq-\left(\mathbf{R}{\bf x}\right)_{3}$ on $E$.}

In the main result of this paper (Theorem
2.8
below) we show that if
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the necessary condition (
1.3
) together with
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{e}_{3})<0
and

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}\left(\,(\mathbf{R}{\bf x}-{\bf x})_{1}\,\mathbf{e}_{1}+(\mathbf{R}{\bf x}-{\bf x})_{2}\,\mathbf{e}_{2}\,\right)\leq 0\quad\forall\,\mathbf{R}\in SO(3),

and if
\mathcal{W}
satisfies some standard assumptions to be detailed in the next section, then

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\inf_{H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}=\min_{H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\mathcal{G}^{I},

where, with the notation
D^{2}
for Hessian of
\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\cdot)
, the limit functional is characterized as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}^{I}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\max_{\mathbf{R}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{L},E}}{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u})&\ \ \hbox{if }\ \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{3}\geq 0\ \hbox{quasi-everywhere on}\ E\\
\ \ +\infty&\ \ \hbox{otherwise in $H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})$ }\end{array}\right.

\mathcal{Q}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{F}^{T}\,D^{2}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}&\hbox{if }\;{\rm Tr}\,\mathbf{F}=0\\
+\infty&\hbox{otherwise,}\\
\end{array}\right.

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}\,=\,\left\{\,\mathbf{R}\in SO(3):\ \mathcal{L}\left((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I})\,{\bf x}\right)\,-\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in E}\big(\mathbf{(}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{x})_{3}\big)\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{e}_{3})=0\,\right\}.

In general,
\mathcal{G}^{I}
differs from the expected limit functional
\mathcal{E}^{I}
, defined by replacing
\mathcal{Q}
with
\mathcal{Q}^{I}
in (
1.2
).
However, under the hypotheses on
E
and
\mathcal{L}
that were previously detailed, it has been proven in
[
16
]
(see Lemma
2.6
below) that either

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}\equiv\{\mathbf{I}\}
or

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}\,=\,\left\{\mathbf{R}\in SO(3):\mathbf{R}\mathbf{e}_{3}=\mathbf{e}_{3}\right\}.

If
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}\!\equiv\!\{\mathbf{I}\}
then clearly

{\mathcal{G}}^{I}\!\equiv\!\mathcal{E}^{I}
, hence in this case we recover the minimum of the Signorini problem in linearized elasticity as the limit of the nonlinear energies
\inf\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}
.
In particular, if
\Omega
is
contained in the upper half-space,
E=\partial\Omega\cap\{x_{3}=0\}
, if
\mathbf{f}=f\mathbf{e}_{3},\ \mathbf{g}=g\mathbf{e}_{3}
and
\mathcal{L}

satisfies conditions (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{e}_{3})<0,
then since
\mathcal{L}({\bf v})=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}{\bf v})
for every
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}
we get
\min\mathcal{G}^{I}\!=\!\min\mathcal{E}^{I}
hence
\inf\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}\to\min\mathcal{E}^{I}
in this case.

Plan of the paper.
In section
2
we rigorously provide assumptions on the obstacle, on the external forces and the global energy functionals. Then, we state the main variational convergence result. In Section
3
we give the proof of the main result after having
proved suitable technical lemmas. With respect to the analysis from
[
16
]
about the compressible case, some care is needed in constructing the suitable upper and lower bounds while simultaneously taking care of the obstacle condition and the incompressibility constraint.

2.
Notation and main results

In the following,
\Omega
will denote the reference configuration of an elastic body.
\Omega
is always assumed to be a nonempty, bounded, connected, Lipschitz open set in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

2.1.
The obstacle

Notations
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
will be used to represent generic points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, with components referred to the canonical basis

(\mathbf{e}_{i})
,
i=1,2,3
.
In the Signorini problem, the elastic body rests on a frictionless rigid support
E\subset\partial\Omega
. The set
E
will be assumed to be planar, i.e., contained in
\{x_{3}=0\}
, and of positive capacity, according to the next definition.

For every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
we define the Sobolev capacity of
K
by setting, see
[
1
, Section 2.2]
,

\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits K\!=\!\inf\left\{\|w\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}^{2}:w\!\in\!C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,w\geq 1\,\text{on}\,K\right\}.

If
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is open we define

\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits G:=\sup\{\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits K:K\ \text{compact},\ K\subset G\},

and for a generic set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits F:=\inf\{\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits G:G\ \text{open},\ F\subset G\}.

A property is said to hold quasi-everywhere (q.e. for short) if it holds true outside a set of zero capacity.
We introduce (see
[
2
]
) a canonical representative of a set
F
, called the
essential part
of
F
and denoted by
F_{ess}
, which coincides with
F
whenever
F
is a smooth closed manifold or the closure of an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
:
for every set
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
we define

F_{ess}\ :=\ \bigcap\,\{\,C:\ C\hbox{ is closed and }\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits(F\!\setminus\!C)=0\,\}.

As shown in
[
2
]
, we have

F_{ess}\ \text{ is a closed subset of}\ \overline{F},\qquad\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits(F\!\setminus\!F_{ess})=0,\qquad\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits F=0\ \text{ if and only if}\ F_{ess}=\emptyset.

Moreover, if
\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits F>0
and
{\bf x}\in F_{ess}
, then
\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits(F\cap B_{r}({\bf x}))>0
for every
r>0
.

Throughout the paper we will assume that the set
E
giving the obstacle condition satisfies

(2.1)

E\subset\partial\Omega\cap\{x_{3}=0\}\not\equiv\emptyset\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits E>0.

We recall that every function in
H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
has a precise representative defined quasi-everywhere on the whole
\overline{\Omega}
.
Indeed, if
\mathbf{u}\!\in\!H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
\mathbf{v}\!\in\!H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
is a Sobolev extension of
\mathbf{u}
, it is well known (see
[
1
, Proposition 6.1.3]
) that the limit

\displaystyle{\mathbf{v}}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\ :=\ \lim_{r\downarrow 0}\ \frac{1}{|B_{r}(\mathbf{x})|}\,\int_{B_{r}(\mathbf{x})}\!\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{\xi})\,d\mathbf{\xi}

exists for q.e.
{\bf x}\!\in\!{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.
The function
{\mathbf{v}}^{*}
is called the precise representative of
\mathbf{v}
and is a quasicontinuous function in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, meaning that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists an open set
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
such that
\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits V<\varepsilon
and

{\mathbf{v}}^{*}
is continuous in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\!\setminus\!V
.
It has been proven in
[
16
]
that if
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ \mathbf{v}_{2}
are two distinct Sobolev extensions of
\mathbf{u}
then

\mathbf{v}_{1}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\ =\ \mathbf{v}_{2}^{*}(\mathbf{x})

for q.e.
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Therefore if
\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
we may define its precise representative for quasi-every
\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega}
by

(2.2)

{\mathbf{u}}^{*}(\mathbf{x})=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\ \frac{1}{|B_{r}(\mathbf{x})|}\,\int_{B_{r}(\mathbf{x})}\!\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{\xi})\,d\mathbf{\xi},\qquad\text{q.e. }\mathbf{x}\in\overline{\Omega},

where
\mathbf{v}
is any Sobolev extension of
\mathbf{u}
.
The function
{\mathbf{u}}^{*}
is
pointwise quasi-everywhere defined by (
2.2
) and
is
quasicontinuous
on
\overline{\Omega}
i.e., for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a relatively open set
V\subset\overline{\Omega}
such that
\mathop{{\rm cap}}\nolimits V<\varepsilon
and

{\mathbf{u}}^{*}
is continuous in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus V
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
then its negative part
w^{-}
is in
H^{1}(\Omega)
too.
Moreover, both
(w^{-})^{*}
and
(w^{*})^{-}
are quasicontinuous in
\overline{\Omega}
and
(w^{-})^{*}=(w^{*})^{-}=w^{-}
a.e. in
\Omega
. Then, by
[
11
]
,
(w^{-})^{*}=(w^{*})^{-}
q.e. in
\overline{\Omega}
.
Therefore the condition
(w^{-})^{*}=0
q.e. in
E_{ess}
is equivalent to
w^{*}\geq 0
q.e. in
E_{ess}
.
In particular, as it was pointed out in in
[
16
]
, Theorem 2.1 of
[
5
]
entails that if
E_{ess}\subset\partial\Omega
is Ahlfors
2
-regular and
\mathcal{H}^{2}(E_{ess})>0
, then the condition
w\geq 0\
q.e. on
E_{ess}
is equivalent to
w\geq 0\ \ \mathcal{H}^{2}
a.e. on
E_{ess}
so the classical framework of

[
2
,
23
,
12
]

is equivalent to ours in this case.

2.2.
The elastic energy density

Let
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
denote the set of
3\times 3
real matrices, endowed with the Euclidean norm
|\mathbf{F}|=\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\mathbf{F}^{T}\mathbf{F})}
. We let
{\mathrm{sym}\mathbf{F}}:=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{F}^{T}+\mathbf{F})
.
SO(3)
will denote the special orthogonal group.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}^{3}
and
{\mathcal{B}}^{3}
denote respectively the

\sigma\mbox{-algebras}

of Lebesgue measurable and Borel measurable subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

and let
\mathcal{W}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty]
be
{\mathcal{L}}^{3}\!\times\!{\mathcal{B}}^{9}
- measurable satisfying the following standard assumptions, see also
[
19
,
20
]
:

(2.3)

{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{R}\mathbf{F})={\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})\quad\ \forall\ \mathbf{\!}\mathbf{R}\!\in\!SO(3)\ \ \forall\ \mathbf{F}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\quad\qquad\ \mbox{for a.e. ${\bf x}\!\in\!\Omega$},

(2.4)

\min_{\mathbf{F}}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})={\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})=0\quad\mbox{for a.e. ${\bf x}\in\Omega$}

and as far as it concerns the regularity of
\mathcal{W}
, we assume that there exist an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
SO(3)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}
, an increasing function
\omega:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\omega(t)=0
and a constant
K>0

such that for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\Omega

(2.5)

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\vskip 2.84544pt\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\cdot)\in C^{2}(\mathcal{U}),\;\;\;\vskip 2.84544pt|D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})|\leq K\;\;\hbox{and}\\
&|D^{2}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})-D^{2}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{G})|\leq\omega(|\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{G}|),\quad\forall\;\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G}\in\mathcal{U}.\end{array}

Moreover we assume that there exists
C>0
such that for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\Omega

(2.6)

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})\geq C(d(\mathbf{F},SO(3)))^{2}\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\end{array}

where
d(\,\cdot\,,SO(3))
denotes the Euclidean distance function from the set of rotations.
In order to consider incompressible elasticity models, starting from a function
\mathcal{W}
as above we introduce the incompressible strain energy density by letting, for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\Omega
,

(2.7)

{\mathcal{W}}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})&\hbox{if}\ \ \det\mathbf{F}=1\\
+\infty&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We recall some basic properties that follow from the above assumptions, see
[
19
,
20
]
. For a.e.
{\bf x}\in\Omega
there holds

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}h^{-2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I}+h\mathbf{F})=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{F}^{T}D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}=\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm sym}\mathbf{F}\,D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\ {\rm sym}\mathbf{F}\qquad\ \forall\ \mathbf{F}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},

hinting to the linear elastic energy density as limit of the nonlinear energies, with
\mathbb{C}({\bf x})=D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})
, and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{F}^{T}D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}

\displaystyle\geq C|\mathrm{sym}\mathbf{F}|^{2}\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{F}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},

where
C
is the constant in (
2.6
).
Taking (
2.5
) into account we have, for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\Omega
,

(2.8)

\left|\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I}+h\mathbf{F})-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\,{\rm sym}\mathbf{F}\,D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\ {\rm sym}\mathbf{F}\right|\leq h^{2}\omega(h|\mathbf{F}|)|\mathbf{F}|^{2}

for every
\mathbf{F}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}
and every
h>0
such that
\mathbf{I}+h\mathbf{F}\in\mathcal{U}
.

We mention a class of energy densities
{\mathcal{W}}
(the so called Yeoh materials
[
24
,
25
]
) fulfilling the assumptions above (
2.3
)–(
2.6
) and for which the main result of the present paper (see Theorem
2.8
below) applies
(for simplicity, we consider the homogeneous case):

{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbf{F}):=\sum_{k=1}^{3}c_{k}(|\mathbf{F}|^{2}-3)^{k}

with
c_{k}>0
.
It is easy to check that with this choice the energy density satisfies all assumptions from (
2.3
) to (
2.5
) while inequality (
2.6
) has been proven in
[
17
]
. It is worth noticing that when material constants are suitably chosen then also the classical Ogden-type energies may fulfill the assumptions from (
2.3
) to (
2.6
) and we refer to
[
17
]
for all details.

2.3.
External forces

We introduce a body force field
\mathbf{f}\in L^{6/5}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and a surface force field
\mathbf{g}\in L^{4/3}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. From now on,
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{g}
will always be understood to satisfy these summability assumptions. The load functional is the following linear functional

\mathcal{L}({\bf v}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf x}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\bf v}\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}({\bf x}),\qquad{\bf v}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We note that since
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
and the Sobolev trace embedding
H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
imply that
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded functional over
H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
and throughout the paper we denote its norm with
\|\mathcal{L}\|_{*}
.

For every
\mathbf{R}\!\in\!SO(3)
we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{R}}:=\{\mathbf{c}:c_{3}\geq-\min_{{\bf x}\in E_{ess}}(\mathbf{R}{\bf x})_{3}\}

and we assume the following
geometrical compatibility between load and obstacle

(2.9)

\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x}+\mathbf{c})\leq 0\qquad\forall\mathbf{R}\in SO(3),\ \forall\mathbf{c}\,\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathbf{R}}

together with

(2.10)

\mathcal{L}\left((\mathbf{R}{\bf x}-{\bf x}\right)_{\alpha}\mathbf{e}_{\alpha})\leq 0\quad\forall\,\mathbf{R}\in SO(3),

the summation convention over the repeated index
\alpha=1,2
being understood all along this paper.
It can be shown that condition (
2.9
) is equivalent to (see
[
16
, Remark 3.4]
)

(2.11)

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{e}_{3})\leq 0=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{e}_{1})=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{e}_{2}),\qquad\quad\Phi(\mathbf{R},E,\mathcal{L})\leq 0\quad\forall\,\mathbf{R}\!\in\!SO(3),

where we have set

\Phi(\mathbf{R},E,\mathcal{L}):=\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x})\,-\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{e}_{3})\min_{{\bf x}\in E_{ess}}(\mathbf{R}{\bf x})_{3}

and from now on we will use (
2.11
) in place of (
2.9
). It has been shown in
[
16
]
that conditions (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) are compatible and that they do not imply each other, see also Remark
3.3
below.
We next state three auxiliary lemmas, in order to better clarify the role
of conditions (
2.10
) and (
2.11
). Proofs may be found in
[
16
]
. In the following,
\wedge
denotes the cross product.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that (
2.10
) holds. Then,

(2.12)

2.4.
Energy functionals

If
E
fulfils (
2.1
)
the incompressible Signorini problem in linear elasticity
can be described as the minimization of the functional

\mathcal{E}^{I}:H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}^{I}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u})&\hbox{if}\ \mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}\\
\ \!\!+\infty&\hbox{otherwise in}\ H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{array}\right.

where
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}):=\!\mathop{{\rm sym}}\nolimits\nabla\mathbf{u}
,
\;\;\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{F}^{T}\mathbb{C}({\bf x})\,\mathbf{F}
,
\;\;\mathbb{C}({\bf x}):=D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})
,

{\mathcal{Q}}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})\qquad\hbox{if}\;\;{\rm Tr}\,\mathbf{F}=0\\
&\\
&+\infty\ \hbox{otherwise}\\
\end{array}\right.

and where
\mathcal{A}
is defined by

\mathcal{A}\ :=\ \left\{\,\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3}):\ \,u_{3}^{*}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0\ \,\hbox{q.e.}\ \mathbf{x}\!\in\!E\,\right\}.

The meaning of such constraint is that the deformed configuration of
E
, namely
\{\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x})\!:=\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}),\,\mathbf{x}\!\in\!E\}
, is constrained to remain in
\{y_{3}\geq 0\}
.

For every
\mathbf{y}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
we introduce the set

(2.14)

\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{y})\,:=\,\mathop{{\rm argmin}}\nolimits\left\{\,\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{R}|^{2}\,d{\bf x}:\ \mathbf{R}\in SO(3)\right\}.

Thus,
due to the rigidity inequality of
[
7
]
, there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega)>0
such that for every
\mathbf{y}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
and every
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{y})

(2.15)

\int_{\Omega}\Big(d\big(\nabla\mathbf{y},SO(3)\big)\Big)^{2}\,d{\bf x}\,\geq\,C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{R}|^{2}\,d{\bf x},

where
d\big(\mathbf{F},SO(3)\big):=\min\{|\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{R}|:\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)\}
. The rigidity inequality is a crucial tool for obtaining linear elasticity as limit of finite elasticity via
\Gamma
-convergence, as seen in
[
4
]
and subsequent works
[
9
,
13
,
15
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

Let us introduce nonlinear elastic energies. If
(h_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,1)
is a vanishing sequence,
the rescaled finite elasticity functionals
\mathcal{G}_{j}:H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}
are defined by

\mathcal{G}_{j}(\mathbf{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}&\displaystyle h_{j}^{-2}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\nabla\mathbf{y})\,d{\bf x}-h_{j}^{-1}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})\ \qquad\hbox{if}\ \mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{A}\\
&\\
&+\infty\ \qquad\hbox{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

The related rescaled incompressible finite elasticity functionals
\mathcal{G}_{j}^{I}:H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}
are defined by

\mathcal{G}_{j}^{I}(\mathbf{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}&\displaystyle h_{j}^{-2}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{W}^{I}({\bf x},\nabla\mathbf{y})\,d{\bf x}-h_{j}^{-1}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})\ \qquad\hbox{if}\ \mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{A},\\
&\\
&+\infty\ \qquad\hbox{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

It will be shown (see Lemma
3.1
below) that
\inf_{H^{1}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\mathcal{G}_{j}^{I}>-\infty
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
(\mathbf{y}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\!\subset\!H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
is said a
minimizing sequence of the sequence of functionals

\mathcal{G}_{j}^{I}
if

(2.16)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\left(\,\mathcal{G}_{j}^{I}(\mathbf{y}_{j})\,-\inf_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}\mathcal{G}_{j}^{I}\right)=0.

The main focus of the paper is to investigate if
\mathcal{G}_{j}^{I}(\mathbf{y}_{j})
converges to a minimum of (
2.16
) whenever
(\mathbf{y}_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a minimizing sequence of
\mathcal{G}_{j}^{I}
.
To this end we introduce
the functionals
\mathcal{I}^{I},\ \widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{I},\ \mathcal{G}^{I}:H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
defined by

\mathcal{I}^{I}(\mathbf{u}):=\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u})+\tfrac{1}{2}b_{\alpha}(\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{3}+\mathbf{e}_{3}\otimes\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}))\,d{\bf x},

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}^{I}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{I}(\mathbf{u})-\max_{\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u})&\hbox{if}\ \mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}\\
\ \!\!+\infty&\hbox{otherwise in}\ H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}^{I}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\max_{\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u})&\hbox{if}\ \mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}\\
\ \!\!+\infty&\hbox{otherwise in}\ H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}
is defined by (
2.13
).

Remark 2.7
.

It is worth noticing that
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{I}\leq\mathcal{G}^{I}\leq\mathcal{E}^{I}
, since
\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L},E}
, and it is straightforward to check that
\mathcal{I}^{I},\ \widetilde{\mathcal{G}}^{I},\ \mathcal{G}^{I}
are all continuous with respect to the strong convergence in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Lemma 3.4

\displaystyle\sup_{{\bf x}\in\overline{\Omega}}|(\nabla\mathbf{z}(t,{\bf x})-\mathbf{I})|\leq 3(\exp(t\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}})-1)\qquad\forall\ t\geq 0,

Since
{\bf v}\in C_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},{\mathbb{R}}^{3})

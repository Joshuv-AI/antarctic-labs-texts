No black hole bomb for 𝑫 D -dimensional non-extremal Reissner-Nordstrom black holes against charged massive scalar perturbation
Jia-Hui Huang

Abstract
The superradiant stability of asymptotically flat D D -dimensional non-extremal Reissner-Nordstrom black holes under charged massive scalar perturbation is analytically studied. In previous works, it proved that there are no black hole bombs for five and six-dimensional non-extremal Reissner-Nordstrom black holes against charged massive scalar perturbation. In this work, we extend the previous discussions to the D D -dimensional case ( D ≥ 7 D\geq 7 ) and find that the same conclusion holds in arbitrary higher dimensional case.

Introduction

When a charged rotating black hole is scattered off by a charged bosonic field, the electromagnetic or (and) the rotational energy of the black hole may be extracted by the external field under certain conditions. This phenomenon is called superradiance

\Omega_{H}
is the angular velocity of the black hole horizon and

D

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we present the description of the model and the asymptotic analysis of boundary conditions. In Section 3, the effective potential of the radial equation of motion is given, and the asymptotic behaviors of the effective potential at the horizon and spatial infinity are discussed.
In Section 4, we give a brief description of the proof of our main result that there is no potential well outside the black hole horizon for the superradiant bound modes. The details of the proof are in the appendix. The final Section is devoted to the summary.

2
Model description

In this section, we present the model in which we are interested, i.e. a
D
-dimensional non-extremal RN black hole against charged massive scalar perturbation.
The metric of the
D
-dimensional non-extremal RN black hole

;

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}.

(2)

The function

reads

\displaystyle f(r)=1-\frac{2m}{r^{D-3}}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2(D-3)}},

(3)

where the parameters
m
and

are related with the ADM mass
M
and electric charge
Q
of the RN black hole,

\displaystyle m=\frac{8\pi}{(D-2)Vol(S^{D-2})}M,~~q=\frac{8\pi}{\sqrt{2(D-2)(D-3)}Vol(S^{D-2})}Q.

(4)

Here
Vol(S^{D-2})=2\pi^{\frac{D-1}{2}}/\Gamma(\frac{D-1}{2})
is the volume of unit (
D-2
)-sphere.

d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}
is the common line element of a (
D-2
)-dimensional unit sphere
S^{D-2}
and can be written as

\displaystyle d\Omega_{D-2}^{2}=d\theta_{D-2}^{2}+\sum^{D-3}_{i=1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{D-2}\sin^{2}(\theta_{j})d\theta_{i}^{2},

(5)

where the ranges of the angular coordinates are taken as
\theta_{i}\in[0,\pi](i=2,..,D-2),\theta_{1}\in[0,2\pi]
.
The inner and outer horizons of this RN black hole are

\displaystyle r_{\pm}=(m\pm\sqrt{m^{2}-q^{2}})^{1/(D-3)}.

(6)

The event horizon of the black hole is located at
r_{h}=r_{+}
. We introduce two symbols
u,v
, defined as
u=r_{+}^{D-3},v=r_{-}^{D-3}
.
It is obvious that we have the following two equalities:

\displaystyle u+v=2m,~uv=q^{2}.

(7)

The electromagnetic field outside the black hole horizon is described
by the following 1-form vector

\displaystyle A=-\sqrt{\frac{D-2}{2(D-3)}}\frac{q}{r^{D-3}}dt=-c_{D}\frac{q}{r^{D-3}}dt.

(8)

The equation of motion for a charged massive scalar perturbation in this
D
-dimensional non-extremal black hole background is governed by the covariant Klein-Gordon equantion

\displaystyle(D_{\nu}D^{\nu}-\mu^{2})\phi=0,

(9)

where
D_{\nu}=\nabla_{\nu}-ieA_{\nu}
is the covariant derivative and
\mu,~e
are the mass and charge of the scalar field respectively.
Since the RN black hole is stationary, the solution of the above equation with definite angular frequency can be written as

\displaystyle\phi(t,r,\theta_{i})=e^{-i\omega t}R(r)\Theta(\theta_{i}).

(10)

The angular eigenfunctions
\Theta(\theta_{i})
are
(D-2)
-dimensional scalar spherical harmonics and the corresponding eigenvalues are given by
-l(l+D-3),(l=0,1,2,..)
Chodos:1983zi
;
Higuchi:1986wu
;
Rubin1984
;
Achour:2015zpa
;
Lindblom:2017maa
.

The radial equation of motion is described by

\displaystyle\Delta\frac{d}{dr}(\Delta\frac{dR}{dr})+UR=0,

(11)

where

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{D-2}f(r),

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\omega+eA_{t})^{2}r^{2(D-2)}-l(l+D-3)r^{D-4}\Delta-\mu^{2}r^{D-2}\Delta.

(12)

In order to analyze the physical boundary conditions needed here at the horizon and spatial infinity, we define the tortoise coordinate

by
dy=\frac{r^{D-2}}{\Delta}dr
and a new radial function
\tilde{R}=r^{\frac{D-2}{2}}R
, then the radial equation (
11
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\tilde{R}}{dy^{2}}+\tilde{U}\tilde{R}=0,

(13)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{U}=\frac{U}{r^{2(D-2)}}-\frac{(D-2)f(r)[(D-4)f(r)+2rf^{\prime}(r)]}{4r^{2}}

(14)

The asymptotic behaviors of
\tilde{U}
at the spatial infinity and the outer horizon are

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\tilde{U}=\omega^{2}-\mu^{2},~~\lim_{r\rightarrow r_{h}}\tilde{U}=(\omega-c_{D}\frac{eq}{r_{h}^{D-3}})^{2}=(\omega-e\Phi_{h})^{2},

(15)

where
\Phi_{h}
is the electric potential of the outer horizon of the RN black hole. Since we just consider a classical black hole and are interested in the black hole bomb mechanism,
we need purely ingoing wave condition at the horizon and bound state condition at spatial infinity, which leads to the following two conditions

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle e\Phi_{h}=c_{D}\frac{eq}{r_{+}^{D-3}},

(16)

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\mu.

(17)

The first inequality is the superradiance condition and the second inequality gives the bound state condition.

where

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4r^{2}(r^{2D-6}-2mr^{D-3}+q^{2})^{2},

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 2(2m^{2}-q^{2}(2\lambda_{l}+3(D-4)(D-2)+2))r^{2D-6}-4mq^{2}(D-4)(D-2)r^{D-3}

\displaystyle q^{4}(D-4)(D-2),

\displaystyle V^{\prime}(r)=0

5
Summary

In this work, superradiant stability of
D
-dimensional (
D\geq 7
) non-extremal RN black hole under charged massive scalar perturbation is studied analytically. Based on
the asymptotic analysis of the effective potential
V(r)
experienced by the scalar perturbation, it is known that there is at least one maximum for the effective potential outside the black hole event horizon. Then, we analyze the numerator of the derivative of the effective potential
C(z+r_{h})
, which is a polynomial of
z=r-r_{h}
. We find that

\displaystyle b_{0}<0,~~b_{k}>0~(4D-9\leq k\leq 6D-18).

(38)

We also proves that

\displaystyle\text{sign}(b_{p+1})\geq\text{sign}(b_{p})~~(1\leq p\leq 4D-10).

(39)

So the sign change in the following sequence of the real coefficients of
C(z+r_{h})

\displaystyle b_{6D-18},b_{6D-19},...,b_{p+1},b_{p},..,b_{1},b_{0},

(40)

is always 1. Then according to Descartes’ rule of signs, we know there is at most 1 positive root for the equation
C(z+r_{h})=0
(i.e.
V^{\prime}(r)=0
when
r>r_{h}
). Thus, there is only one maximum for the effective potential and there is no potential well outside the horizon for the superradiant bound modes. Namely, there is no black hole bomb for
D
-dimensional non-extremal RN black hole against charged massive scalar perturbation.

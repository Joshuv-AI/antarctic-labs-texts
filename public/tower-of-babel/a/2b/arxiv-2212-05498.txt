On graph classes with minor-universal elements
Agelos Georgakopoulos

Abstract
A graph U U is universal for a graph class 𝒞 ∋ U \mathcal{C}\ni U , if every G ∈ 𝒞 G\in\mathcal{C} is a minor of U U . We prove the existence or absence of universal graphs in several natural graph classes, including graphs component-wise embeddable into a surface, and graphs forbidding K 5 K_{5} , or K 3 , 3 K_{3,3} , or K ∞ K_{\infty} as a minor. We prove the existence of uncountably many minor-closed classes of countable graphs that (do and) do not have a universal element. Some of our results and questions may be of interest to the finite graph theorist. In particular, one of our side-results is that every K 5 K_{5} -minor-free graph is a minor of a K 5 K_{5} -minor-free graph of maximum degree 22.



Keywords:
Universal graph, infinite graph, minor-closed, excluded minor, graphs in surfaces,

05C99, 05C83, 05C10.

Introduction

Given a binary relation
\prec
between graphs —typically the (induced) subgraph or minor relation— we say that a graph
U
is
\prec
-universal for a graph class
\mathcal{C}\ni U
, if
G\prec U
holds for every
G\in\mathcal{C}
. (It is often, but not always, the case that
\mathcal{C}
is closed under
\prec
.)

The study of universal graphs is one of the oldest topics of infinite graph theory. The best known instance is the
Rado graph
[
22
]
, an induced-subgraph-universal countable graph. Diestel, Halin & Vogler
[
2
]
proved that there is no subgraph-universal graph in the class of countable graphs with no
K_{n}
minor for
5\leq n\leq\aleph_{0}
, but there is one for
n\leq 4
.

Pach
[
20
]
proved that there is no subgraph-universal planar graph. Huynh et al.
[
10
]
recently strengthened this by showing that if
G
contains every countable planar graph as a subgraph then it must have the infinite complete graph
K_{\infty}
as a minor. Lehner
[
17
]
strengthened this further by showing that the same conclusion holds if
G
contains every countable planar graph as a topological minor.

For more background about universal graphs we refer to e.g.
[
9
,
12
,
14
,
15
]
and the survey
[
13
]
.

This paper studies
<
-universal graphs, where
<
stands for the minor relation, with an emphasis on interactions with the finite graph minor theory. Despite Pach’s result and its aforementioned strengthenings, Diestel and Kühn proved

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

There is a
<
-universal countable planar graph.

Theorem
1.1
provides motivation as well an important tool for this paper. Other natural minor-closed classes known so far to have minor-universal elements are forests, series-parallel graphs
[
2
]
, outer-planar graphs
[
3
]
, and graphs with accumulation-free embeddings in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
15
]
. Diestel & Kühn
[
3
]
asked

Question 1.1
.

Which minor-closed graph classes have a countable
<
-universal element?

They specifically discussed the special cases of Question
1.1
for the classes
Forb(K_{5})
and
Forb(K_{\infty})
. This paper settles these two cases of Question
1.1
, as well as many more. Our first result is easy to explain, and it adapts a well-known idea (
[
13
, Proposition 3.2.]
):

Theorem 1.2
.

The class of countable
K_{\infty}
-minor-free graphs has no
<
-universal element.

Proof.

Suppose
U
is a universal
K_{\infty}
-minor-free graph. Add a new vertex
v^{*}
, and join it with an edge to each vertex of
U
to obtain a new graph

. Notice that
C(U)
is still
K_{\infty}
-minor-free, and therefore we can find

as a minor

. Let

be the branch set of such a minor corresponding to

. Thus

is a minor of

, hence the latter is still universal. Repeat the argument to find a minor of

every branch set of which contains one of

be the new branch set corresponding to

. Repeat ad infinitum to define

, and notice that each

sends an edge to all

form the branch sets of a

∎

We remark that the class of

-minor-free graphs had attracted a lot of attention, and alternative characterizations are known

A second look at the above proof reveals that the choice of the forbidden minor

that is a minor of

Corollary 1.3

Let

-universal element. ∎

Examples of such

include the ray, the half- and full-grid, and any graph which is a disjoint union of infinitely many copies of a fixed graph

be the ray, we thus strengthen a result of

Theorem 1.4

Let

embeds in

The other aforementioned question of Diestel and Kühn has a positive answer:

Theorem 1.5

The class of countable graphs with no

-universal element. So does the class of countable

This is the most difficult result of this paper. We prove it in Section

Theorem

provides the simplest proof I am aware of for the fact that there are uncountably many minor-twin classes of countable graphs: for if

was a countable sequence representing them all, then

-minor-free graph.

Note that for every graph

To summarize, we have provided the following response to Question

which perhaps answers it to the extent possible:

Theorem 1.6

There are uncountably many minor-closed classes of countable graphs that do, and uncountably many that do not have a

-universal element.

One can still propose refined versions of the question, e.g. asking for sufficient conditions for the existence of a
<
-universal element. The following question of this form is motivated by the above discussion on graphs on surfaces and Corollary

:

Problem

Let

be finite connected graphs. Must

have a

-universal element?

We will discuss this problem further in Section

As a consequence of our proof of Theorem

) the following corollary, which as far as I am aware is new even for finite graphs.

Corollary 1.7
.

Every

-minor-free graph is a minor of a
K_{5}
-minor-free graph with maximum degree 22. Every

-minor-free graph is a minor of a

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Graphs

We follow the terminology of Diestel
[
5
]
. We use
V(G)
to denote the set of vertices, and
E(G)
the set of edges of a graph
G
. For
S\subseteq V(G)
, the subgraph
G[S]
of
G\
induced
by
S
has vertex set
S
and contains all edges of
G\
with both endvertices in
S
.

The
degree

d(v)=d_{G}(v)
of a vertex
v
in a graph
G
, is the number of edges of
G\
incident with
v
. The
maximum degree

\Delta(G)
of
G\
is
\sup_{v\in V(G)}d(v)
. The
closed neighbourhood
of
v\in V(G)
is the subgraph of
G\
induced by
v
and all its
neighbours
, i.e. the vertices sharing an edge with
v
.

We write
G\dot{\cup}H
for the disjoint union of two graphs
G,H
, and
\omega\cdot G
for the disjoint union of countably infinitely many copies of
G
.

A
ray
is a one-way infinite path.

2.2
Embeddings

A
surface
is a 2-manifold without boundary. An
embedding
of a countable graph
G\
into a surface
\Sigma
is a map
f:G\to\Sigma
from the 1-complex obtained from
G\
when identifying each edge with the interval
[0,1]
to
\Sigma
such that the restriction of
f
to each finite subgraph of
G
is an embedding in the topological sense, i.e. a homeomorphism onto its image. (The reason why we restrict to finite subgraphs here is that the 1-complex topology of
G\
is not metrizable when
G\
is not locally finite, and so such
G\
cannot have an embedding into a metrizable space
\Sigma
. For example, a star with infinitely many leaves admits an embedding into
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in our sense but it does not admit a topological embedding.)

A
face
of an embedding
f:G\to\Sigma
is a component of
\Sigma\backslash f(G)
. A
facial walk
is a (finite, or two-way infinite) sequence
\ldots v_{0}e_{0}v_{1}e_{1}\ldots
of vertices and edges of
G\
whose
f
-images appear along the boundary of a face of
f
in that order.

The
(orientable) genus

\gamma(G)
of a graph
G\
is the smallest genus
\gamma(\Sigma)
of an orientable surface
\Sigma
into which
G\
embeds.

2.3
Minors

Let
G,H
be graphs. An
H
-
minor
of
G
is a collection of disjoint connected subgraphs
B_{v},v\in V(H)
of
G
, called
branch sets
,
and edges
E_{uv},uv\in E(H)
of
G\
such that each
E_{uv}
has one end-vertex in
B_{u}
and one in
B_{v}
. We write
H<G
to express that
G
has an
H
minor.

We say that
G
and
H
are
minor-twins
, if both
G<H
and
H<G
hold. Any two finite minor-twins are isomorphic, but in the infinite case it is often helpful to think in terms of equivalence classes of minor-twins instead of isolated graphs.

Given a set
X
of graphs, we write
\mathrm{Forb}(X)
for the class of graphs
H
such that no element of
X
is a minor of
H
. For a minor-closed graph class
\mathcal{C}
, we write
\mathrm{Ex}(\mathcal{C})
for the set of graphs
G
such that
G\not\in\mathcal{C}
but
H\in\mathcal{C}
for every
H<G
such that
G\not<H
. If
\mathcal{C}
consists of finite graphs, then
\mathrm{Forb}(\mathrm{Ex}(\mathcal{C}))=\mathcal{C}
holds by the definitions. For infinite graphs this will fail if there is an infinite descending sequence
G_{1}>G_{2}>\ldots
(consisting of graphs not in
\mathcal{C}
) which as far as I know is open
[
28
]
.

3
Uncountably many classes with no
<
-universal element

The aim of this section is to prove

Proposition 3.1
.

There are uncountably many classes of countable graphs that have no
<
-universal element.

To begin with, recall that Theorem
1.2
implies that there are uncountably many minor-twin classes of countable graphs.

As we will see, one can find uncountably many minor-twin classes among much more restricted classes of graphs using the following result of Matthiesen.

The
tree-order

\leq
on the set of vertices of a rooted tree
(T,r)
is defined by setting
x\leq y
whenever
x
lies on the unique path in
T
from its root
r
to
y
. Given two rooted trees
(T,r),(T^{\prime},r^{\prime})
, we write
(T,r)<_{\leq}(T^{\prime},r^{\prime})
if there is an embedding
t:(T,r)\to(T^{\prime},r^{\prime})
that respects the tree-order when restricted to
V(T)
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
18
]
)
.

There are uncountably many equivalence classes of locally finite rooted trees with respect to the order-preserving topological minor relation
<_{\leq}
. This remains true in the class of rooted trees every vertex of which has at most two successors.

Theorem
3.2
provides another way for proving that there are uncountably many minor-twin classes of countable graphs: let
\mathcal{T}
be an uncountable family of rooted trees no two of which are in the same equivalence class of
<_{\leq}
as provided by Theorem
3.2
, and attach a triangle to the root of each member of
\mathcal{T}
. The resulting family of (unrooted) graphs contains no pair of minor-twins. We leave the details to the reader as a warm-up exercise before the following proof.

3.1
.

Let

, and every vertex of each

Our plan is to associate to each

as a minor, such that

has no
<
-universal element by Corollary

. Then, for every even

Once (

To see the latter claim, note first that as all our graphs have maximum degree 3, we can find a topological

Universal elements for embeddable graphs

As mentioned in the introduction, C. Thomassen observed that for every closed surface
\Sigma
other than
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the class of countable graphs that embed in
\Sigma
has no
<
-universal element. We repeat the argument verbatim from
[
3
, § 6]
:

Indeed any such universal graph
U
would contain a cycle
C
whose deletion reduces the Euler genus of
U
. Then every minor of
U
can be embedded in a simpler surface than
\Sigma
after the deletion of at most
|C|
vertices. This however will not be the case for every graph embeddable in
\Sigma
.

Nevertheless, we will prove

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed orientable surface, and let
\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}
be the class of countable graphs
G
such that every component of
G\
embeds in
\Sigma
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{\Sigma}
has a
<
-universal element.

The following will allow us to restrict our attention to locally finite graphs in the proof of Theorem
4.1
. Moreover, it allows us to choose our universal graphs to be locally finite if we want to. A graph is
sub-cubic
if it has maximum degree 3.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a countable graph embeddable in a closed surface
\Sigma
. There is a sub-cubic graph

embeddable in
\Sigma
such that
G<G^{\prime}
.

A proof of the special case of Lemma

]
; our proof is simpler.

The idea is to obtain

If a countable graph

around the new vertices

Starting with

We say that a minor-closed graph class

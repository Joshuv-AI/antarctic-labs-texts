The clique number of the exact distance t t -power graph: complexity and eigenvalue bounds
Aida Abiad, Afrouz Jabal Ameli, Luuk Reijnders

Abstract
The exact distance t t -power of a graph G G , G [ ♯ ​ t ] G^{[\sharp t]} , is a graph which has the same vertex set as G G , with two vertices adjacent in G [ ♯ ​ t ] G^{[\sharp t]} if and only if they are at distance exactly t t in the original graph G G . We study the clique number of this graph, also known as the t t -equidistant number. We show that it is NP-hard to determine the t t -equidistant number of a graph, and that in fact, it is NP-hard to approximate it within a constant factor. We also investigate how the t t -equidistant number relates to another distance-based graph parameter; the t t -independence number. In particular, we show how large the gap between both parameters can be. The hardness results motivate deriving eigenvalue bounds, which compare well against a known general bound. In addition, the tightness of the proposed eigenvalue bounds is studied.



Keywords:
Exact distance graph power; Clique number; Complexity; Eigenvalue bounds

We investigate the complexity of the (

First, we will show that it is NP-hard to determine

We begin this section by exploiting some properties of a well-studied family of graphs known as split graphs. Such properties will be useful in particular for our reduction when

Definition 1

(Split graph)
.

A graph

such that

is a clique and

Split graphs were defined and characterized by Földes and Hammer
[
8
,

]
. These graphs were also introduced independently by Tyshkevich and Chernyak
[

]

Lemma 2
.

Whether or not a graph

is split can be determined in polynomial time. Furthermore if
G
is split, we can find a partition of

into a clique and independent set in polynomial time.

Proof.

Let
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\ldots\geq d_{n}
be the degree sequence of

and let
v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{n}
be a labeling of vertices such that
v_{i}
has degree
d_{i}
. Hammer and Simeone
[
9
]
showed that a graph is a split iff for largest index

such that

, we have:

k(k-1)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}d_{i}=0.

Furthermore the authors showed that if
G
is split, then
(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}v_{i},\bigcup_{j=k+1}^{n}v_{j})
is a partition of
G
into a clique and an independent set. Note that the above-mentioned process can clearly be done in polynomial time.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. For any fixed integer
t\geq 1
finding a maximum
t
-equidistant number of

is an NP-hard problem.

Proof.

If
G
is split, then determining the clique number of

can be done in polynomial time. Let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be a partition of the vertices of
G
such that
V_{1}
is a clique and
V_{2}
is an independent set. Note that we can obtain such a partition (if it exists) in polynomial time using Lemma
2
. Note that as
V_{2}
is an independent set of nodes any clique of

can contain at most one vertex in
V_{2}
. Furthermore, we already know
V_{1}
is a clique. Hence if
V_{1}\cup\{v\}
is a clique for any

then this is the maximum clique and otherwise

itself is the maximum clique of

.

Now assume
G
is not split. We will give a reduction of determining the clique number of
G
to determining the
t
-equidistant number of a graph
H
. We split into two cases,
t
even and
t
odd.

•

t
is odd:
Construct a graph
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
from
G
by replacing every edge
e\in E
by a path of length
t
, that is add
t-1
additional vertices into every edge. We henceforth call the vertices in
V
original vertices, and the vertices in
V^{\prime}\setminus V
dummy vertices. Note that the distance of original vertices

and

is
d
in
G
then their distance is
k\times t
in

.

We now want to show that

has a clique of size

if and only if

has a

-equidistant set of size

. The first implication follows directly from the definition of

. For the second, note that:

(i)

If

is a

-equidistant set in

, then

is a clique in
G
.

(ii)

If
U\subset V^{\prime}
is a
t
-equidistant set in

, then

either consists entirely of original vertices, or entirely of dummy vertices. This is due to the fact that the distance of a dummy vertex from an original vertex can not be a multiple of
t
.

Meaning we only need to consider the additional case when

is a
t
-equidistant set in

. For

or
2

.

Now, consider a

-equidistant set in

, for which
U\subset V^{\prime}\setminus V
and
\absolutevalue{U}=3
. Consider two vertices

. By construction, they must lie on two distinct paths corresponding to two distinct edges in

that share an endpoint, say
(v_{1},v_{2}),(v_{1},v_{3})\in E

t=d(u_{1},u_{2})=d_{H}(u_{1},v_{1})+d_{H}(u_{2},v_{1})\implies d_{H}(u_{1},v_{1})\neq d_{H}(u_{2},v_{1}).

This is because

is odd. Hence, for the third vertex

it cannot be on a path corresponding to an edge incident to

, as it could never be at distance

from both

and

in this case. Instead, it must be on the path corresponding to the edge

. For this to occur,

Finally, assume in the above that

, however, just as

, this vertex must lie on the path corresponding to the edge

. But then

Thus
\omega(G)={\rm eq}_{t}(H)

by computing

.

•

t
is even:
Just as in the odd case, construct a graph
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
from
G
by replacing every edge

by a path of length
t
, that is add
t-1
additional vertices into every edge. However, now we additionally connect all the ”central” dummy vertices, i.e. the vertices in the center of the paths of length

has a clique of size

if and only if

has a

-equidistant set of size

(i)

is a

is a clique in

.

(ii)

Any

(iii)

For any clique

These are true because:

(i)

Follows directly from the construction of

(ii)

If

(iii)

By assumption,

From assertions (i) and (ii) we find

. Assertion (iii) tells us we are always in the latter case. Indeed, let

. Now, any vertex in

to some central dummy vertex in

, and hence at distance

is a

-equidistant set in

. This means we can exactly determine

Now since
\omega
is NP-hard,

must be NP-hard as well.

Theorem 4

For every fixed integer

, there exists no constant approximation for computing the

Proof.

For this purpose we show that if such an approximation algorithm exist then there exists a constant approximation for the maximum clique problem where

Now to prove use we use a similar argument to the proof of Theorem

Let

is not split. Assume there exists a

for some constant

Now we use the same reduction as discussed in the proof of Theorem

•

t

Note that our reduction constructs an instance

of

is polynomial in terms of size of

Therefore given a

•

Again our reduction constructs an instance

.
Now we can use the

We now provide a proof for the NP-hardness of

Theorem 5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. Finding the equidistant number of
G
is an NP-hard problem.

Proof.

We will give a reduction of determining the clique number of
G
to determining the equidistant number of a graph
H
.
Let
G=(V,E)
with
\absolutevalue{V}=n
, and let

be the complete graph on

H=G\nabla K_{n}
, the join of

and

. That is,

has vertex set
V\cup U
and edge set
E\cup F\cup\{(v,u):v\in V,u\in U\}
.
First we show
\omega(H)\geq{\rm eq}_{t}(H)
for all
t
, which implies
{\rm eq}(H)=\omega(H)
. For any
v\in V
we have
U\cup\{v\}
is a clique in
H
of size
n+1
, hence
\omega(H)\geq n+1
. On the other hand, for
t>1
we have that any
t
-equidistant set can contain at most 1 vertex in

. Hence
{\rm eq}_{t}(H)\leq n+1
, and thus
{\rm eq}(H)=\omega(H)
.
Finally, we will show
\omega(H)=\omega(G)+n
. Clearly if we have a clique
C
of size
m
in
G
, then
C\cup U
will be a clique of size
m+n
in
H
, hence
\omega(H)\geq\omega(G)+n
. On the other hand, if

is a maximal clique of size
m^{\prime}
in

, then

because all vertices in

are adjacent to all vertices in

. Thus

will be a clique in

of size

, and hence

.
Thus, we have found

3
The relation between the equidistant number and the
t
-independence number

A set
U
of vertices of a graph
G
is called a
t
-independent set
if
d(u,v)>t
for all
u,v\in U
. The
t
-independence number
of a graph
G
, denoted
\alpha_{t}
, is the size of the largest
t
-independent set of
G
. Clearly, the
t
-equidistant number is closely related to the
(t-1)
-independence number, but with the underlying set having stricter requirements. The following lemmas make this connection more precise.

The following lemmas investigate the relation between the above parameters.

Lemma 6
.

[
26
]

For any graph
G
with clique number
\omega(G)
we have
\omega(G)={\rm eq}_{1}(G)\leq{\rm eq}(G)\leq\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)\}
. For graphs
with diameter two we have the equality
{\rm eq}(G)=\max\{\omega(G),\alpha(G)\}
.

A straightforward extension of Lemma
6
, using
\alpha_{t}(G)
, is as follows.

Lemma 7
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then, at least one of the following holds

1.

{\rm eq}(G)={\rm eq}_{i}(G)
, for some
i\in[1,t]
,

2.

{\rm eq}(G)\leq\alpha_{t}(G)
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then for
t,t^{*}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
with
t<t^{*}
we have
\alpha_{t}(G)\geq{\rm eq}_{t^{*}}(G)
.

Proof.

If
U
is a
t^{*}
-equidistant set, then all its vertices are at pairwise distance at least
t
, meaning it is also a
t
-independent set. Hence
\alpha_{t}(G)\geq{\rm eq}_{t^{*}}(G)
.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
G
be a graph. If
t\geq{\rm diam}(G)
, then
\alpha_{t-1}(G)={\rm eq}_{t}(G)
.

Proof.

The case
t>{\rm diam}(G)
is trivial, as in this case no two vertices are far enough apart to form a
(t-1)
-independent set or
t
-equidistant set, and hence
\alpha_{t-1}(G)=1={\rm eq}_{t}(G)
.

Next is
t={\rm diam}(G)
.
\alpha_{t-1}(G)\geq{\rm eq}_{t}(G)
follows from Lemma
8
. To prove the converse, let
U
be a
(t-1)
-independent set. Then for all
u,v\in U
we have

, additionally, since
t={\rm diam}(G)
, we have
d(u,v)\leq{\rm diam}(G)=t
. Thus
d(u,v)=t
for all
u,v\in U
, and thus
U
is a
t
-equidistant set. Since this holds for arbitrary
U
, we conclude
\alpha_{t-1}(G)\leq{\rm eq}_{t}(G)
, and thus
\alpha_{t-1}(G)={\rm eq}_{t}(G)
.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then there exists a
t^{*}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
such that for all
t<t^{*}
we have
\alpha_{t}(G)\geq{\rm eq}(G)
.

Proof.

By definition, there is a
t^{*}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
such that
{\rm eq}(G)={\rm eq}_{t^{*}}(G)
. The desired inequality follows from applying Lemma
8
.
∎

First, we define the main quantity we are interested in:

Definition 11
.

Let

Theorem 12

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{AE(k,t)}{k}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\max\left\{\frac{\alpha_{t-1}(G)}{k}:G\text{ is of order }k\right\}.

Proof.

The inequality

follows trivially from
\alpha_{t-1}-{\rm eq}_{t}\leq\alpha_{t-1}
.

For the other inequality, fix some

such that

\alpha_{t-1}(G(n,t))=\max\left\{\alpha_{t-1}(G):G\text{ is of order }n\right\}.

Next, define the graphs

Furthermore, note that

Now, fix an

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{AE(k,t)}{k}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\alpha_{t-1}(G(n,t))}{n+t}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\alpha_{t-1}(G(n,t))}{n}

Let

It is of interest to investigate how well these bounds induced from the different parameters perform in practice, especially in view of the results from Section
3
which tell us the gap between

can grow very large. For this reason, it is worth considering an alternative and more direct approach to derive eigenvalue bounds. Instead of using interlacing on the adjacency matrix (as we did to derive Propositions

This makes sense, as the adjacency eigenvalue interlacing may not fully capture the properties of an equidistant set, whereas the distance matrix might. While eigenvalue interlacing using the adjacency matrix is a widely used tool, the distance matrix is much less studied when it comes to using eigenvalue interlacing for bounding graph parameters.

Theorem 24
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
D
be its distance matrix, with eigenvalues
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
. Then the
t
-equidistant number satisfies:

{\rm eq}_{t}(G)\leq\absolutevalue{\left\{i : \tilde{\lambda}_i \leq-t\right\}}+1.

(8)

Proof.

Assume
{\rm eq}_{t}\geq 2
, else the result is trivial. Let
U
be a
t
-equidistant set of size
{\rm eq}_{t}
. Then, we can label the vertices in such a way that
D
has principal
{\rm eq}_{t}\times{\rm eq}_{t}
submatrix:

B=t(J-I),

where
J
is the all
1
matrix and
I
the identity matrix, both of appropriate size.
B
has spectrum
\{(t({\rm eq}_{t}-1))^{[1]},(-t)^{[{\rm eq}_{t}-1]}\}
. Cauchy interlacing then tells us:

\tilde{\lambda}_{n-{\rm eq}_{t}+i}\leq-t\text{ for }i\in[2,{\rm eq}_{t}].

Hence, we must have at least
{\rm eq}_{t}-1
eigenvalues less than
-t
, meaning

\displaystyle{\rm eq}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\absolutevalue{\left\{i : \tilde{\lambda}_i \leq-t\right\}}+1.\qed

Using quotient interlacing on the distance matrix, we can obtain two more bounds on the
t
-equidistant number for the socalled transmission-regular graphs. A graph is called
d
-transmission-regular
if its distance matrix has constant row sum
d
.

Theorem 25
.

Let
G
be a
d
-transmission regular graph on
n
vertices, and let
D
be its distance matrix, with eigenvalues
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
. Then the
t
-equidistant number satisfies:

\displaystyle{\rm eq}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tilde{\lambda}_{2}+t)n}{tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}}

\displaystyle\text{ if }tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}>0,

(9a)

\displaystyle{\rm eq}_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\tilde{\lambda}_{n}+t)n}{tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{n}}

\displaystyle\text{ if }tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{n}<0.

(9b)

Proof.

Let
U
be a
t
-equidistant set of size
r={\rm eq}_{t}
. Now partition the rows and columns of
D
into
U
and
V\setminus U
. Let
S
be the normalized characteristic matrix of this partition. Then the quotient matrix
B
will be:

\displaystyle B=S^{\top}DS=\begin{pmatrix}t(r-1)&\frac{1}{r}\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in V\setminus U}d_{uv}\\
\frac{1}{n-r}\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in V\setminus U}d_{uv}&\frac{1}{n-r}\sum_{u\in V\setminus U}\sum_{v\in V\setminus U}d_{uv}\end{pmatrix}.

Now using that
D
has constant row sum
d
, we find:

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in V\setminus U}d_{uv}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V}\sum_{v\in V}d_{uv}-\left(\sum_{u\in V\setminus U}\sum_{v\in V}d_{uv}+\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in U}d_{uv}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V}d-\left(\sum_{u\in V\setminus U}d+\sum_{u\in U}t(r-1)\right)=nd-((n-r)d+tr(r-1))

\displaystyle=rd-tr(r-1),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in V\setminus U}\sum_{v\in V\setminus U}d_{uv}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in V}\sum_{v\in V}d_{uv}-\left(\sum_{u\in U}\sum_{v\in V}d_{uv}+\sum_{u\in V\setminus U}\sum_{v\in U}d_{uv}\right)

\displaystyle=nd-(rd+rd-tr(r-1))=nd-2rd+tr(r-1).

Hence

\displaystyle B=\begin{pmatrix}t(r-1)&d-t(r-1)\\
\frac{rd-tr(r-1)}{n-r}&\frac{nd-2rd+tr(r-1)}{n-r}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
B
has eigenvalues
\mu_{1}=d
and

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\tr(B)-d=t(r-1)+\frac{nd-2rd+tr(r-1)}{n-r}-d

\displaystyle=\frac{(t(r-1))(n-r)+nd-2rd+tr(r-1)-d(n-r)}{n-r}

\displaystyle=\frac{tn(r-1)-rd}{n-r}=\frac{r(tn-d)-tn}{n-r}.

Now we find by interlacing that
\tilde{\lambda}_{2}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
. If
tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}>0
, then:

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{2}\geq\frac{r(tn-d)-tn}{n-r}

\displaystyle\iff\tilde{\lambda}_{2}(n-r)\geq r(tn-d)-tn

\displaystyle\iff(\tilde{\lambda}_{2}+t)n\geq r(tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{2})

\displaystyle\iff\frac{(\tilde{\lambda}_{2}+t)n}{tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{2}}\geq r.

Similarly, if
tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{n}<0
then:

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\leq\frac{r(tn-d)-tn}{n-r}

\displaystyle\iff\tilde{\lambda}_{n}(n-r)\leq r(tn-d)-tn

\displaystyle\iff(\tilde{\lambda}_{n}+t)n\leq r(tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{n})

\displaystyle\iff\frac{(\tilde{\lambda}_{n}+t)n}{tn-d+\tilde{\lambda}_{n}}\geq r.\qed

Computational experiments suggest that the bound from (

4.2
Bounds on eq

Using the bound on
{\rm eq}_{t}
from Theorem
24
, we can obtain a bound on
{\rm eq}
, captured in the following corollary.

Corollary 26
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
D
be its distance matrix, with eigenvalues
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
. Then the equidistant number satisfies:

{\rm eq}(G)\leq\absolutevalue{\left\{i : \tilde{\lambda}_i \leq-1\right\}}+1.

(10)

Proof.

By Theorem
24
we have

{\rm eq}_{t}(G)\leq\absolutevalue{\left\{i : \tilde{\lambda}_i \leq-t\right\}}+1\leq\absolutevalue{\left\{i : \tilde{\lambda}_i \leq-1\right\}}+1

for all
t
. Hence,
{\rm eq}(G)
will also be bounded by the above.
∎

An interesting consequence of Theorem
24
, is that if we know the bound from Theorem
24
is tight for some
t^{*}
, then we can reduce the number of values for
t
that need to be considered when determining
{\rm eq}
. This is illustrated in the following result.

Corollary 27
.

Let
G
be a graph, and let
D
be its distance matrix, with eigenvalues
\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
. Then, if there exists a
t^{*}\in[1,{\rm diam}(G)]
such that

{\rm eq}_{t^{*}}=\absolutevalue{\left\{i : \tilde{\lambda}_i \leq-t^*\right\}}+1,

then

{\rm eq}(G)=\max\{{\rm eq}_{t}(G):1\leq t\leq t^{*}\}.

Proof.

For
t>t^{*}
, we have

{\rm eq}_{t}\leq\absolutevalue{\left\{i : \tilde{\lambda}_i \leq-t\right\}}+1\leq\absolutevalue{\left\{i : \tilde{\lambda}_i \leq-t^*\right\}}+1={\rm eq}_{t^{*}}.

Hence

\displaystyle{\rm eq}(G)

\displaystyle=\max\{{\rm eq}_{t}(G):1\leq t\leq{\rm diam}(G)\}=\max\{{\rm eq}_{t}(G):1\leq t\leq t^{*}\}.\qed

For obtaining the next bound on
{\rm eq}
we will use Lemma
6
combined with a bound on
\alpha
and a bound on
\omega
.

Theorem 28

(Ratio bound, unpublished, see e.g.
[
14
]
)
.

Let
G
be a regular graph with
n
vertices and adjacency eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
. Then the independence number of
G
satisfies:

\alpha(G)\leq n\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}}.

For bounding
\omega
we will use the earlier stated result by Haemers (Proposition
20
).

Corollary 29
.

Let
G
be a regular graph with adjacency eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
. Then the equidistant number of
G
satisfies:

{\rm eq}(G)\leq n\cdot\max\left\{\frac{-\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{n}},\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{n-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}\right\}.

(11)

Proof.

Use Lemma
6
, and then bound
\alpha
and
\omega
with Theorem
28
and Proposition
20
, respectively.
∎

5.1
MILP for the Inertial-type bound (Proposition
15
)

Below is an MILP implementation by Abiad et al.
[
2
]
for the Inertial-type bound on
\alpha_{t}
, which can also be used for finding the best polynomial in Proposition
15
.

Let
G=(V,E)
have adjacency matrix
A
having distinct eigenvalues
\theta_{0}>\cdots>\theta_{d}
with multiplicities
\mathbf{m}=(m_{0},\ldots,m_{d})
. Let
u\in V
, and let
p(x)=a_{t}x^{t}+\cdots+a_{0}
,
\mathbf{b}=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{d})\in\{0,1\}^{d+1}
,
M\in\mathbb{R}
large, and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
small, then we have the following MILP:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \text{variables: }&(a_{0}\ldots,a_{t}),(b_{0},\ldots,b_{d})\\
\text{parameters: }&t,M,\varepsilon\\
\text{input: }&\text{For a regular graph }G\text{, its adjacency matrix }A,\\
&\text{ its spectrum }\{\theta_{0}^{m_{0}},\ldots,\theta_{d}^{m_{d}}\},\text{ a vertex }u\in V\\
\text{output: }&(a_{0}\ldots,a_{t})\text{, the coefficients of a polynomial }p\\
\ \\
\text{minimize}\ \ &\mathbf{m}^{\top}\mathbf{b}\\
\text{subject to}\ \ &\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}(A^{i})_{vv}\geq 0,\ \ v\in V\setminus\{u\}\\
&\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}(A^{i})_{uu}=0\\
&\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}\theta_{j}^{i}-Mb_{j}+\varepsilon\leq 0,\ j=0,\ldots,d\\
&\mathbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{d+1}\end{aligned}}

(12)

Implementing such an MILP for all
u\in V
will find the best
p
for
G
. Note that when the graph is walk-regular, this MILP only needs to be run once.

5.2
LP for the Ratio-type bound (Proposition
16
)

The following LP can be used to compute the optimal polynomial of Proposition
16
.

Let
G=(V,E)
have adjacency matrix
A
and distinct eigenvalues
\theta_{0}>\cdots>\theta_{d}
. Then, for some
u\in V
and
l\in[1,d]
we have the LP:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \text{variables: }&(a_{0}\ldots,a_{t})\\
\text{parameters: }&t\\
\text{input: }&\text{For a graph }G\text{, its adjacency matrix }A,\\
&\text{The spectrum of }A:\{\theta_{0},\ldots,\theta_{d}\}.\\
&\text{A vertex }u\in V.\text{ An }l\in[1,d].\\
\text{output: }&(a_{0}\ldots,a_{t})\text{, the coefficients of a polynomial }p\\
\ \\
\text{maximize}\ \ &\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}\theta_{0}^{i}-\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}\theta_{l}^{i}\\
\text{subject to}\ \ &\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}((A^{i})_{vv}-(A^{i})_{uu})\leq 0,\ v\in V\setminus\{u\}\\
&\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}((A^{i})_{uu}-\theta_{l}^{i})=1\\
&\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}(\theta_{0}^{i}-\theta_{j}^{i})>0,\ \ j\in[1,d]\\
&\sum_{i=0}^{t}a_{i}(\theta_{j}^{i}-\theta_{l}^{i})\geq 0,\ \ j\in[1,d]\end{aligned}}

(13)

Solving this for all
u\in V
and
l\in[1,d]
yields the best polynomial.

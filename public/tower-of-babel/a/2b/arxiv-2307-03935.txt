Abstract

1

Introduction

Exchanges provide a medium for buyers and sellers to come together and trade at a common price based on each agents’ perspective. With the advent of electronic trading, we witness the emergence of market makers who provide transparent two-sided markets at all times. They play a critical role to ensure a healthy flow of liquidity for the price discovery process and enable a seamless trading experience.

Indeed, through the years, a key problem of exchanges is to attract and reward these LPs. The objective is to establish a mechanism to encourage a consistent of liquidity through rebates and incentives. For instance, NYSE establishes designated Market Makers (DMMs) where they are specially appointed and obliged to maintain quotes and facilitate transactions for an allocated set of stocks. Deutsche Boerse has also implemented the Xetra Liquidity Provider Programme to reward LPs based on a set of parameters. Especially for margin trading which relies more on liquidity depth to absorb delinquent traders, exchanges have to strike a fine balance between tighter spreads and deeper depths. As such, to incentivise LPs, it is critical to understand their considerations in quoting buy and sell orders to the book (BIS, n.d.):

Revenue: Bid ask spread, Delta market value from position

Costs: Funding Maker fees

They primarily profit by arbitraging the differences in spread and thus compete on the order execution and turnover to build more volume. In fact, spread behaviour is a crucial focus for all market participants:

* Takers: Tighter spreads improve the price discovery mechanism as takers can execute orders closer to the fair price and institutional investors can put on larger blocks of trade without suffering from excessive slippage.

* Makers (LPs): These providers constantly face a dilemma between turnover and spreads as many attempt to identify the optimal spread management. For instance, lower spread means that the makers’ quotes are more likely to get hit, resulting in higher turnover rates. However, this will also entail a lower income per trading cycle since the profit potential is lower each time.

Therefore, the paper will begin by unravelling the LP rewards scheme on dYdX, in particular the spread management aspects. I will empirically analyse the historical order book data from Tardis and trading data (eg. volume, number of trades, average trade size, average open interest) for the corresponding interval via the dYdX API from mid-April. Subsequently, by modelling the different changes in spreads, it will provide an understanding on the change in parameters for each LP. The aim of this paper would be to:

Enhance the market efficiency by ensuring sufficient liquidity in the orderbook and tightening the maxSpread parameter, while being aligned with current liquidity provision strategies by LPs

2

Deconstructing the Spread

The bid-ask spread (quoted spread)can be defined as:

QuotedSpread=Ask-Bid

(1)

This is the simplest form of measurement taken directly from quotes at the top of the orderbook. However, the majority of trades usually occur beyond this point given varying transaction sizes.

Prior empirical research by Demsetz (1968) provides the foundation for identifying the determinants of the spread based on the NYSE - inventory, transaction, information costs and level of competition based on the number of market makers providing liquidity. This was further expounded by Copeland and Galai (1983) in emphasising the variables of volatility and trading activities which are extrinsic risks faced by liquidity providers. More recent studies on the NYSE has also revealed that volume exerts statistically significant inverse relationship with spreads while the impact of price on spreads returns mixed results depending on the sectors as it incurs different variations in the market making costs. Furthermore, greater volatility induces more risks for makers, who may widen spreads to price in the uncertainties. As a result, different exchanges have curated Market Maker Programmes which actively encourage volume and spreads for designated market Makers. For instance, the Xetra Liquidity Provider service agreement includes volatility within its scores, where the achieved BBO presence is doubled in times of volatility, on which the EUR index exceeds a range of 3%.

Therefore, the spread can be represented with a simplified equation where:

Spread=f(l,v,p)

(2)

l = liquidity, v = volatility, p = price impact

Therefore, it is commonly expected that the long tail markets will have a wider spread, reflecting greater adverse selection. Hence, compensating LPs for assuming delta risk in being exposed to these price movements.

5

Backtesting on CPI, BOE, PPI Releases

To backtest these results, 2 particular periods were observed - 10th May (CPI Release), 11th May (PPI Release), which have been associated with periods of heightened volatility. Minute by minute orderbook data was collected for all the markets for 5 hours through the release (between 5bps and 30bps of maxSpread) and the historical volumes within the intervals were retrieved by taking the difference between the rows of
usd\_volume
. These data were retrieved from the endpoints on dYdX API and compared against the bid and ask depths. The percentage of intervals where depths on both sides were insufficient to cover 80% volume traded at the interval is calculated. This is a tight parameter as this assumes volume to be one-sided in the market. The 80% was based off the longshort ratios on dYdX based off coinglass.

E.g. If CELO-USD returns 20%, it suggests that there was insufficient liquidity on either side of the orderbook to meet the volume traded for 20% of the period. An initial condition of 1% was set at the threshold, and any markets which return
\geq 5\%
(which means either bid or ask depths are lesser than volume traded) will have their maxSpreads widened and re-tested again. In this case, since CELO-USD’s suggested maxSpread was 30bps, this will be compared against 40bps to test for any difference in significance for results.

The results of these can be found in the Appendix and these formed the basis for the recommendation in the next section.

Discussion

Markets exhibited a distinct segmentation in behavior based on liquidity provision and volume. After all, higher volumes and tighter spreads are correlated.

* Unincentivised markets : eg. UMA, ZRX.

BBO spreads
consistently\geq 40bps

(Consistent with Binance which does not list UMA-USDT perps). While orders can be largely fulfilled within the 30bp maxSpread, it can remain status quo due to the lack of trading activities and compensate the risk by LP ss for providing liquidity in these markets.

* Low volume markets : eg. ADA, CRV, CELO

Empirically, the liquidity within the maxSpread of 30bps and 40bps, for both bid and ask depths have been rather consistently the same and all orders can be absorbed within 30bps each side, with leftover buffer. Therefore, 30bps will be suggested.

* Mid volume markets : eg. SOL, MATIC, AVAX, LINK

Given that these are more mature altcoin markets with higher volumes and interest, spreads can be tightened. It is noteworthy to recognise that majority, if not all of these orders can be absorbed within 20bps of each side. Furthermore, even in occasions with anomalous trading volumes, the amount of depth at 40bps still remains insufficient to cover it.

* High volume markets (i.e. BTC, ETH) - Less than 10 bps would be ideal given the maturity of BTC and ETH markets. Historical data has also revealed the consistent tight spreads around the mid price, of $1m in depth on each side. However, given the relatively higher trading volumes on dYdX and the need to reduce slippage, 15bps is suggested to provide more buffer as seen from the post-CPI release. Nonetheless, I will contend in Section 7 that this part of the formula can be removed.

7.2

Suggestion 2: Increase the makerVolume weightage for non-BTC/ETH markets to 0.85, from 0.65

The present LP rewards formula heavily emphasises on depths and spreads given the 2 components (bid, spread and Qmin) scores, with just 0.65 for maker volume. Especially with the consistently low volumes and wide spreads, the system can be easily gamed by LPs who provider wider and passive orders which hardly get hit. This drives the scenario of a constant re-firing of quotes at the maxSpread to be eligible for these rewards as prices change.

Likewise, a concerning issue is the ratio of volumes for these markets relative to other CEXes. Altcoins vs BTC/ETH on dYdX presents an extremely disoriented picture where there is an overt amount of trading activities in the majors. The lack of volume proves concerning and this can be seen from the relatively wide quotes away from the BBO, causing traders to suffer from worse slippages, deteriorating the experience. Suggestion 3: Modify the existing LP Reward Allocation for non BTC/ETH markets

Currently, 80% of the rewards are distributed amongst the remaining markets in the exchange. Based on daily trading volume and rewards distributed to each market, Therefore, long tail markets may be disincentivised.

7.4

Suggestion 4: Assigning DMMs for each market with a rewards and penalty function per epoch

Designated Market Makers (DMMs) are a common implementation in traditional exchanges where they are appointed by the exchange to provide liquidity in certain markets based on a set of criteria. This may include a minimum BBO presence, minimum passive volume share and passive volume ratio. For instance, these DMMs will be required to provide orders for a percentage of their trading time within the BBO while hitting a required share of their passively executed volume which remains in the order book. A similar programme can be implemented for the different markets.

The main rationale would be that every LP has a particular focus on certain markets based on the products they are proficient in. Especially in v4, where permissionless market listings become possible, this provides an avenue for LPs who specialize in these new markets to actively provide liquidity. This phenomenon can already be observed on dYdX where the top 3 to 4 accounts are primarily dominated by a group of different LPs which record disproportionately higher makerVolume and rewards:

Therefore, a trial can be established in the altcoin markets to model the likely improvement in liquidity. A suggestion can be seen as follows:

7.4.1

Voting Mechanism

Each LP can submit their intended functioning parameters based on the aforementioned metrics, while staking a certain amount as part of this voting process. Based on an equally weighted formula, the LP with the highest score will be the DMM for that market and will be obliged to provide liquidity at the pre defined parameters.

The amount of stake possible can be based on the following:

Staked Requirement = score x liquidity_constant

Penalty = penalty_fraction x staked_amount

Reward = reward_fraction x staked_amount

This opens up a new mechanism for specific LPs to provide liquidity to their preferred products without overtly spreading across multiple markets. As a result, improving the market efficiency given the increased competition through a pragmatic way of distributing the rewards.

7.5

Suggestion 5: Reward Multiplier for Volatile Periods

Volatile periods can be defined as periods of time where prices fluctuate, presenting much uncertainty due to asymmetric information from a mixture of informed and uninformed traders.

In markets with high volatility / low volume, investors and LPs will want to place orders far away from trading levels to benefit from violent movements. This results in a power law distribution where the tails of the order book are particularly fatter and the spreads are noticeably wider. LPs can also simply withdraw their liquidity during that short period of time, which does not really affect their uptime score. Hence, LPs with a high uptime during the volatile periods should be adequately rewarded based on a multiplier to further distil the sticky behaviour regardless of market environments.

An initial constant of 2 is suggested to the makerVolume scores on volatile days However, the impacts of these on the overall LP rewards are beyond the scope of this paper and can be revisited in the future should the historical data be readily available.

8

Conclusion

Ultimately, the LP incentives schemes plays a critical role in supporting vibrant markets on dYdX. The parameters have to be carefully calibrated to seek a fine balance between makers (profitability vs risk) and takers (slippages), which can be further seen from the perspective of the operating spreads and depths permissible. The ideal state of the exchange would be when LPs instinctively compete at the BBO, ensuring a healthy flow of liquidity and this can be effectively witnessed in mature markets (BTC/ETH) given the high volumes, tighter spreads and lower inherent risks.

Through multiple series of backtesting on historical and more recent data using different metrics, the adjusted maxSpreads have been suggested in Section 6, directing incentives to a tighter active range in majority of the markets. At the same time, alternatives have been explored to improve the LP rewards mechanism based on the recent end of epoch data. The following is recommended as the immediate steps:

1. Revise the LP rewards scheme for BTC/ETH markets to a volume parameter.

2. Increase the makerVolume weightage for non-BTC/ETH markets from 0.65 to 0.85, following the path taken by BTC/ETH last year.

The impacts on liquidity dynamics will then have to be monitored before adjusting the rewards allocations and introducing a reward multiplier for periods of volatility.

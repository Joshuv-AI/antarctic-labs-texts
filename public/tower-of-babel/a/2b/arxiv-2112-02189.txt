Homogeneous codimension one foliations on reducible symmetric spaces of noncompact type
Ivan Solonenko

Abstract
. We extend the classification of homogeneous codimension-one foliations on irreducible Riemannian symmetric spaces of noncompact type obtained by Berndt and Tamaru in [ 4 ] to the reducible case, thus completing it for all noncompact symmetric spaces.



1.
Introduction

A foliation
\mathcal{{F}}
with connected properly embedded leaves on a connected Riemannian manifold
M
is called homogeneous if the subgroup of isometries of
M
preserving the leaves of
\mathcal{{F}}
acts transitively on each leaf. Given a connected Lie group
G
acting properly and isometrically on
M
without singular orbits, the orbit foliation of
G
is homogeneous, and, by design, every homogeneous foliation arises in this way. We say that
\mathcal{{F}}
is polar (resp., hyperpolar) if it is the orbit foliation of a polar (resp., hyperpolar) action on
M
. Perhaps the easiest class of homogeneous foliations consists of foliations of codimension one. These correspond to cohomogeneity-one actions and are always hyperpolar. Thus, homogeneous codimension-one foliations are an interesting object of study in the intersection of the theories of polar actions and Riemannian foliations.

In
[
4
]
, Berndt and Tamaru invented two new methods for constructing homogeneous codimension-one foliations on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. One of them produces such a foliation – denoted by
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell}
– for every one-dimensional linear subspace
\ell\subseteq\mathfrak{{a}}
, where
\mathfrak{{a}}
is a maximal abelian subspace in the
(-1)
-eigenspace of a Cartan involution of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{{g}}
of the isometry group of
M
. The other method produces a foliation
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{i}}
for each of the simple restricted roots
\upalpha_{i}\in\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
. These two types of foliations are parametrized by
\mathbb{{P}}\mathfrak{{a}}\simeq\mathbb{R}P^{r-1}
(here
r=\mathrm{rank}\hskip 1.00006pt(M)=\dim(\mathfrak{{a}})
) and
\hskip-0.8pt\left\{\hskip 0.29999pt{\upalpha_{1},\ldots,\upalpha_{r}}\hskip 0.29999pt\right\}
, respectively. Restricting to the case when
M
is irreducible, the authors proved that every homogeneous codimension-one foliation on it arises in one of these two ways – up to isometric congruence. Moreover, if we let
\mathrm{DD}_{M}
denote the Dynkin diagram of
M
, then its automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
acts naturally on
\hskip-0.8pt\left\{\hskip 0.29999pt{\upalpha_{1},\ldots,\upalpha_{r}}\hskip 0.29999pt\right\}
and
\mathfrak{{a}}
, and two foliations
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell^{\prime}}
(resp.,
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{i}}
and
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{j}}
) are congruent if and only if
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
(resp.,
\upalpha_{i}
and
\upalpha_{j}
) lie in the same
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
-orbit (whereas the foliations
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{i}}
are never congruent). This gives the complete classification of homogeneous codimension-one foliations on irreducible symmetric spaces of noncompact type and shows that their moduli space is isomorphic to

(\mathbb{R}P^{r-1}\sqcup\hskip-0.8pt\left\{\hskip 0.29999pt{1,\ldots,r}\hskip 0.29999pt\right\})/\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD_{M}}).

In particular, if
M
is of rank one and thus isometric to a hyperbolic space over
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},
or
\mathbb{O}
, it has only two homogeneous codimension-one foliations up to congruence:
\mathcal{{F}}_{\mathfrak{{a}}}
and
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{1}}
.

In their subsequent paper
[
3
]
with Díaz-Ramos, Berndt and Tamaru came up with a geometric construction that allows one to produce homogeneous hyperpolar foliations on any symmetric space of noncompact type from the foliations
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{1}}
on hyperbolic spaces and foliations by parallel affine subspaces on the Euclidean spaces. They went on to show that every homogeneous hyperpolar foliation on
M
arises via this construction up to congruence. Notably, they do not impose the irreducibility assumption on
M
. On the other hand, it is not generally known when two foliations obtained by this construction are congruent to each other. When applied to foliations of codimension one, their method produces precisely the foliations
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell}\,(\ell\subseteq\mathfrak{{a}})
and
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{i}}\,(1\leqslant i\leqslant r)
defined above. Altogether, this implies that on any symmetric space of noncompact type, any homogeneous codimension-one foliation is congruent to either some
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell}
or some
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{i}}
. Hence, to complete the classification of such foliations, one just needs to tell when two foliations of the form
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell}
(resp.,
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{i}}
) are congruent to each other.

In this paper, we accomplish this by showing that the classification in the irreducible case works verbatim in the situation when
M
is reducible:

Main Theorem
.

Let
M
be a symmetric space of noncompact type and rank
r
. Then, given one-dimensional linear subspaces
\ell,\ell^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{{a}}
(resp., simple roots
\upalpha_{i},\upalpha_{j}\in\hskip-0.8pt\left\{\hskip 0.29999pt{\upalpha_{1},\ldots,\upalpha_{r}}\hskip 0.29999pt\right\}
), the foliations
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell}
and
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell^{\prime}}
(resp.,
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{i}}
and
\mathcal{{F}}_{\upalpha_{j}}
) are isometrically congruent if and only if
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
(resp.,
\upalpha_{i}
and
\upalpha_{j}
) differ by some
P\in\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
. Consequently, the moduli space of homogeneous codimension-one foliations on
M
is isomorphic to

(\mathbb{R}P^{r-1}\sqcup\hskip-0.8pt\left\{\hskip 0.29999pt{1,\ldots,r}\hskip 0.29999pt\right\})/\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD_{M}}).

One noteworthy caveat is that we make the routine assumption that the Riemannian metric of
M
is normalized in such a way that it agrees with the Killing form of the Lie algebra of the isometry group. When
M
is irreducible, this assumption is relatively benign, for it amounts to simply rescaling the Riemannian metric by a constant factor. It poses a nontrivial restriction, however, when
M
is reducible, because different de Rham factors of
M
may carry different normalization constants in general. Since the authors of
[
3
]
make this assumption as well, it remains an open question how much of the classification remains valid in the reducible case when the Riemannian metric is normalized in a different way.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review some rudiments of the theories of symmetric spaces of noncompact type and parabolic subgroups that will be necessary in the rest of paper. We also prove a somewhat folklore result about the isometry group of a Riemannian manifold decomposed as a Riemannian product. In Section
3
we first introduce the results of Berndt, Tamaru, and Díaz-Ramos and then prove the Main Theorem. The paper is concluded by Section
4
, in which we give an alternative – more algebraic – proof of the uniqueness result for foliations of the type
\mathcal{{F}}_{\ell}
.

2.
Prerequisites

The purpose of this section is to establish the notation and terminology necessary to understand the classification results of Berndt, Tamaru, and Díaz-Ramos as well as the rest of the paper. We also prove a useful result pertaining to the isometry group of a Riemannian manifold decomposed as a Riemannian product. We follow the notation established in
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
and refer to the same articles as well as
[
9
,
7
,
10
]
for more detailed expositions.

2.1.
Polar and codimension-one foliations

Let
M
be a connected Riemannian manifold, and let
\mathcal{{F}}
be a foliation on
M
with connected properly embedded leaves. Then we call
\mathcal{{F}}

homogeneous
if the group
I(M,\mathcal{{F}})=\hskip-0.8pt\left\{\hskip 0.29999pt{\upvarphi\in I(M)\mid\upvarphi(F)=F\hskip 3.50006pt\text{for every leaf}\hskip 3.50006ptF\in\mathcal{{F}}}\hskip 0.29999pt\right\}
acts transitively on each leaf of
\mathcal{{F}}
. Since the leaves are properly embedded, it follows that
I(M,\mathcal{{F}})
is a closed Lie subgroup of
I(M)
. Moreover, its identity component
I^{0}(M,\mathcal{{F}})
also acts transitively on each leaf because the leaves are connected. Conversely, given a connected Lie group
G
acting properly and isometrically on
M
without singular orbits, the leaves of the action form a homogeneous foliation. Note that the leaves of a homogeneous foliation are equidistant from each other.

Assume that
M
is complete. We say that a homogeneous foliations is
polar
if it is the orbit foliation of a polar action on
M
. In other words,
\mathcal{{F}}
is polar if there exists a complete immersed submanifold
\Upsigma\subseteq M
, called a section, that intersects all the leaves of
\mathcal{{F}}
and does so orthogonally. If exist, all sections are isometrically congruent to each other and necessarily totally geodesic. Many authors usually assume sections to be embedded or even properly embedded. A homogeneous polar foliation is called
hyperpolar
if its sections are flat. Homogeneous foliations of codimension one turn out to be automatically hyperpolar. Indeed, given such a foliation
\mathcal{{F}}
, its sections should be just geodesics normal to its leaves. One can show that any normal geodesic intersects all the leaves, does so orthogonally, and its image is an immersed submanifold, hence a section.

2.2.
The Iwasawa and horospherical decompositions

Let
M
be a symmetric space of noncompact type. We denote
G=I^{0}(M),\widetilde{G}=I(M),
and
\mathfrak{{g}}=\mathrm{Lie}\hskip 0.59998pt(G)
. Pick any point
o\in M
and write
\widetilde{K}=\widetilde{G}_{o}
and
K=G_{o}=\widetilde{K}^{0}
for the isotropy groups at
o
and
\mathfrak{{k}}=\mathrm{Lie}\hskip 0.59998pt(K)
for the isotropy Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{{g}}
. We have the corresponding Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{{g}}=\mathfrak{{k}}\oplus\mathfrak{{p}}
and the Cartan involution
\uptheta\in\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathfrak{{g}})
that has
\mathfrak{{k}}
and
\mathfrak{{p}}
as its
(+1)
- and
(-1)
-eigenspaces, respectively. We identify
\mathfrak{{p}}\cong T_{o}M
in the usual way. If we denote the Killing form of
\mathfrak{{g}}
by
B
, then
B_{\uptheta}(X,Y)=-B(X,\uptheta Y)
is a
\widetilde{K}
-invariant inner product on
\mathfrak{{g}}
. The form
B_{\uptheta}
coincides with
B
on
\mathfrak{{p}}
and equals
-B
on
\mathfrak{{k}}
. The Cartan decomposition is orthogonal with respect to both
B
and
B_{\uptheta}
. We stick with
B_{\uptheta}
as our default inner product on
\mathfrak{{g}}
. Whenever
U\subset V
are subspaces of
\mathfrak{{g}}
, we write
V\ominus U
for the orthogonal complement of
U
in
V
. We make a standard but pivotal assumption that the Riemannian metric of
M
at
o
coincides with the restriction of
B_{\uptheta}
(hence of
B
) to
\mathfrak{{p}}\cong T_{o}M
. We will sometimes refer to this for brevity as “the Riemannian metric on
M
comes from the Killing form”.

Pick a maximal abelian subspace
\mathfrak{{a}}
in
\mathfrak{{p}}
and write
r=\dim(\mathfrak{{a}})=\mathrm{rank}\hskip 1.00006pt(M)
. The connected Lie subgroup
A\subset G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{{a}}
is closed and abelian and its orbit
A\hskip-1.2pt\cdot o\simeq\mathbb{{E}}^{r}
is a maximal flat in
M
. The restriction of
B_{\uptheta}
to
\mathfrak{{a}}
is an inner product and thus gives an isomorphism
\mathfrak{{a}}\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 6.3941pt\hskip-6.3941pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\phantom{\sim}}$}\hskip-6.3941pt\hskip-4.76384pt\raisebox{0.64444pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\!\sim\,$}}\hskip-4.76384pt\hskip 6.3941pt}}\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
and the corresponding inner product on
\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
. We have the restricted root system
\Upsigma\subset\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
, which gives rise to the restricted root space decomposition
\mathfrak{{g}}=\mathfrak{{g}}_{0}\oplus\bigoplus_{\upalpha\in\Upsigma}\mathfrak{{g}}_{\upalpha}
. Here
\mathfrak{{g}}_{0}=\mathfrak{{k}}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{{a}}
, where
\mathfrak{{k}}_{0}=Z_{\mathfrak{{k}}}(\mathfrak{{a}})=N_{\mathfrak{{k}}}(\mathfrak{{a}})
. The summands of the restricted root space decomposition are pairwise orthogonal with respect to
B_{\uptheta}
. Note that
\uptheta\mathfrak{{g}}_{\upalpha}=\mathfrak{{g}}_{-\upalpha}
. Finally, we make a choice of positive roots
\Upsigma^{+}\subset\Upsigma
and write
\Uplambda=\hskip-0.8pt\left\{\hskip 0.29999pt{\upalpha_{1},\ldots,\upalpha_{r}}\hskip 0.29999pt\right\}\subseteq\Upsigma^{+}
for the set of simple roots. The sum
\mathfrak{{n}}=\bigoplus_{\upalpha\in\Upsigma^{+}}\mathfrak{{g}}_{\upalpha}
is a nilpotent subalgebra of
\mathfrak{{g}}
. We have a vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{{g}}=\mathfrak{{k}}\oplus\mathfrak{{a}}\oplus\mathfrak{{n}}
called the Iwasawa decomposition. The connected Lie subgroup of
G
corresponding to
\mathfrak{{n}}
is closed and will be denoted by
N
. The multiplication induces a diffeomorphism
KAN\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 6.3941pt\hskip-6.3941pt\hbox{$\xrightarrow{\phantom{\sim}}$}\hskip-6.3941pt\hskip-4.76384pt\raisebox{0.64444pt}{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\!\sim\,$}}\hskip-4.76384pt\hskip 6.3941pt}}G
called the global Iwasawa decomposition. Since
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
, the subgroups
A
and
N
are simply connected. Their product
S=AN
is a simply connected closed solvable subgroup of
G
(with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{{s}}=\mathfrak{{a}}\oplus\mathfrak{{n}}
) that acts simply transitively on
M
. In particular,
M
can be realized, albeit not uniquely, as a simply connected solvable Lie group with a suitable left-invariant Riemannian metric. Every root
\upalpha
can be carried to a vector
H_{\upalpha}\in\mathfrak{{a}}
along the isomorphism between
\mathfrak{{a}}
and
\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
. On the other hand, the basis
\upalpha_{1},\ldots,\upalpha_{r}
for
\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
gives the dual basis for
\mathfrak{{a}}
, which we denote by
H^{\upalpha_{1}},\ldots,H^{\upalpha_{r}}
. By definition,
\langle\hskip 1.00006pt{H^{\upalpha_{i}}}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt{H_{\upalpha_{j}}}\hskip 1.00006pt\rangle\hskip 0.20004pt=\upalpha_{j}(H^{\upalpha_{i}})=\updelta_{ij}
.

A linear endomorphism
T\in\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{{a}}^{*})
is called an automorphism of
\Upsigma
if it maps
\Upsigma
onto itself and preserves the integers
n_{\upalpha\upbeta}=\frac{2\langle\hskip 0.81949pt{\upalpha}\hskip 0.81949pt|\hskip 0.81949pt{\upbeta}\hskip 0.81949pt\rangle\hskip 0.16391pt}{||\upbeta||^{2}}
, i.e.
n_{F(\upalpha)F(\upbeta)}=n_{\upalpha\upbeta}
for all
\upalpha,\upbeta\in\Upsigma
. Such
T
is necessarily a linear automorphism and, since the inner product on
\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
comes from the Killing form
B
, in fact an orthogonal transformation of
\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
. The group
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)
is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{O}(\mathfrak{{a}}^{*})
and it contains the Weyl group
\mathrm{{W}}(\Upsigma)
as a normal subgroup. A choice of positive roots allows to split the short exact sequence
\mathrm{{W}}(\Upsigma)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)/\mathrm{{W}}(\Upsigma)
as a semidirect product. Indeed, having fixed
\Upsigma^{+}
, note that
\Uplambda
gives rise to a Dynkin diagram, which we denote by
\mathrm{DD}_{M}
. We treat it as a vertex-weighted graph (with some edges oriented and doubled/tripled and some vertices ”marked” in case they correspond to a simple root whose double is also root) where each vertex is assigned the multiplicity of the corresponding simple root, i.e. the dimension of the corresponding root subspace. We will be working with automorphisms of
\mathrm{DD}_{M}
, which we require by definition to preserve the multiplicities of the vertices. Any such automorphism
P\in\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
gives rise to a linear automorphism of
\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}
– denoted by the same letter – simply by permuting the basis vectors in
\Uplambda
. One can easily see that
P\in\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)
and the map
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})\to\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)
is an injective group homomorphism. Moreover, its image and
\mathrm{{W}}(\Upsigma)
do not intersect and their product is the whole automorphism group of
\Upsigma
, hence
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)\simeq\mathrm{{W}}(\Upsigma)\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
. Note that there may be elements in
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
interchanging some connected components of
\mathrm{DD}_{M}
– precisely when
M
has isometric de Rham factors or, equivalently, when
\mathfrak{{g}}
has isomorphic simple ideals (this is one of the several places where the assumption on the Riemannian metric of
M
plays an important role).

Now, we have a compact group
N_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})
and its representation
\uppsi\colon N_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})\to\mathrm{O}(\mathfrak{{a}}^{*}),k\mapsto({\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathrm{Ad}\hskip 0.59998pt(k)\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\mathfrak{{a}}}}^{*})^{-1}
. One readily sees that
\mathrm{Ker}\hskip 0.39993pt(\uppsi)=Z_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})
and
\hskip 0.80002pt\mathrm{Im}\hskip 0.39993pt(\uppsi)\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)
, which implies that
\mathrm{Ad}\hskip 0.59998pt(k)
permutes the restricted root subspaces:
\mathrm{Ad}\hskip 0.59998pt(k)\mathfrak{{g}}_{\upalpha}=\mathfrak{{g}}_{\uppsi(k)\upalpha}
. Since the Riemannian metric on
M
comes from the Killing form
B
, one can show that the image of
\uppsi
equals the whole automorphism group of
\Upsigma
, hence
N_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})/Z_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})\cong\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)
. We can also give a similar description of the subgroups
\mathrm{{W}}(\Upsigma)
and
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
of
\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\Upsigma)
. Namely, consider the normalizers
N_{K}(\mathfrak{{a}})
and
N_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{n}})
. These are both subgroups of
N_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})
, and we have
N_{K}(\mathfrak{{a}})\cap Z_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})=Z_{K}(\mathfrak{{a}})
and
N_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{n}})\supseteq Z_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})
. It turns out that
\uppsi(N_{K}(\mathfrak{{a}}))=\mathrm{{W}}(\Upsigma)
and
\uppsi(N_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{n}}))=\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
, so
N_{K}(\mathfrak{{a}})/Z_{K}(\mathfrak{{a}})\cong\mathrm{{W}}(\Upsigma)
and
N_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{n}})/Z_{\widetilde{K}}(\mathfrak{{a}})\cong\mathrm{Aut}\hskip 0.59998pt(\mathrm{DD}_{M})
. Note that all of these subgroups of
\widetilde{K}
share the same Lie algebra
\mathfrak{{k}}_{0}
.

[

]
, we will need some basic facts from the theory of parabolic subgroups, which we now briefly introduce. Let
\Upphi
be any subset of
\Uplambda
. Write
\Upsigma_{\Upphi}
for the root subsystem of
\Upsigma

\Upsigma_{\Upphi}
. We start by dividing

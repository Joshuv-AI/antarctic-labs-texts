Non-acyclic Reidemeister torsions of manifolds of odd dimension


Abstract
Given an oriented closed C ∞ C^{\infty} -manifold M M of odd dimension and a unitary representation ρ : π 1 ​ ( M ) → GL n ​ ( 𝔽 ) \rho:\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}) , we define a Reidemeister torsion, even if the cohomology associated with ρ \rho is not acyclic. As corollaries, we introduce some topological invariants of M M , which include non-acyclic extensions of abelian torsions and the Alexander polynomials of links. Furthermore, we propose a volume form of the SU ⁡ ( n ) {\rm SU}(n) -character varieties of M M . Moreover, we compute the Reidemeister torsions of some representations of 3-manifolds and give a comparison of the works of Farber and Turaev.

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field with involution
\bar{}:{\mathbb{F}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}
, and let
C^{*}
be a bounded chain complex consisting of finite dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}
-vector spaces.
Roughly speaking, a combinatorial (Reidemeister) torsion of
C^{*}
is an alternating product of determinants of
\delta^{i}
’s (see §
2
for the definition).
For a closed oriented
C^{\infty}
-manifold
X
,
if the cellular complex
C^{*}_{\rho}(X)
of
X
with local coefficients over
{\mathbb{F}}
is acyclic and
satisfies certain conditions, the torsion of
C^{*}_{\rho}(X)
is a topological invariant and studied from many aspects;
see, e.g.,
[
Tur
,
Nic
]
for its applications and developments.
Meanwhile, if the complex
C_{*}^{\rho}(X)
is not acyclic, with a choice of the basis of cohomology
H^{*}_{\rho}(X)
,
we can define a (non-acyclic) torsion in
{\mathbb{F}}
that depends on the choice.
By the parity of Poincaré duality, the existing approaches of the non-acyclic torsion
are different according to the parity of
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(X)
; see
[
Söz
,
Wit
]
and the references therein for even cases of
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(X)
,
and see
[
PY
]
for knot cases.
Let us focus on non-acyclic torsions with
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits X=2m+1
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Inspired by analytic approaches to the torsions as in the Ray-Singer norm,
Farber and Turaev
[
Far
,
FT
]
define a torsion on the determinant line of the cohomology of any flat vector bundle over
X
, and a
Poincaré-Reidemeister scalar product
from their torsion; see Appendix
B
.
In the definition, they assume
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{C}}
, and also use the duality from the twisted intersection forms on homology.
However, the definition of the twisted intersection form had ambiguous aspects, and the above determinant line is not quantitatively investigated; furthermore, few non-trivial examples of computing the scalar products are known; see, e.g.,
[
Far
,
FT
,
NS
]
.

In this paper, from a cohomological viewpoint with a Poincaré duality,
we will define combinatorially a non-acyclic torsion with respect to a
representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
with
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits X=2m+1
, where
\rho
is required to admit a bilinear form satisfying a certain condition; see Theorem
3.1
. The point of the definition is that
{\mathbb{F}}
may be generally a field of characteristic zero, and the torsion is independent of the choice of the basis of

H^{*}_{\rho}(X)
; see Theorem
3.1
. Furthermore, we show (Appendix
B
) that the torsion is a (square-root) modification of the Poincaré-Reidemeister scalar product.
However, because the non-acyclic torsion is defined in a quotient group of the form
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\{x\bar{x}\mathrm{det}(\rho(y))\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times},y\in\pi_{1}(X)\}
, the quotient group may be trivial in some cases; see Section
5
for some computations of the group.
To obtain non-trivial torsions, we shall restrict ourselves to cases in which the quotient group is not trivial.

In such non-trivial cases,
we suggest applications from the non-acyclic torsions to some topological invariants of a closed
C^{\infty}
-manifold
X
of odd dimension; see Section
6
.
When
\rho
is trivial, our torsion can be expressed in terms of the ordinary cohomology; see Sections
6.1
and
6.2
.
For non-trivial cases of
\rho
, we see that the torsion above produces abelian Reidemeister torsions in non-acyclic cases (Section
6.3
).
As an application, we propose a generalization of the Alexander polynomial of links in Section
6.4
.
As another application, we ensure that the torsion generates a volume form on the
{\rm SU}(n)
-character variety of
X
, as an analogy to the volume form of the flat moduli spaces of closed surfaces
[
Wit
]
(see Section
4
).

Finally, in Section
8
, we focus on the case of
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits X=3
and provide examples of computing the torsions of 3-manifolds.
In the 3-dimensional case,
we can compute the Poincaré duality because some procedures for computing the cup product are known
[
S86
,
Tro
]
; in doing so, we obtain non-trivial torsions in some cases.
In addition, we argue further refinements of the torsions; see Propositions
7.2
and
7.3
.
Although this paper suggests quantitative definitions and research of non-acyclic torsions, their applications and potential properties need to be developed in future studies.

Conventional terminology.

Throughout this paper,
{\mathbb{F}}
and
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}={\mathbb{F}}\setminus\{0\}
denote a commutative field and the multiplicative group, respectively. Furthermore, we denote by
\bar{}:{\mathbb{F}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}
an involution on
{\mathbb{F}}
(possibly,
\ \bar{}=\mathrm{id}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
).
By
X
, we mean an oriented closed connected
C^{\infty}
-manifold of dimension
2m+1
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Review: Refined Reidemeister torsions of
{\rm GL}_{n}
-representations

Let us briefly review algebraic torsions. Let
V
be a
d
-dimensional vector space over a field
{\mathbb{F}}
.
For two bases of
V
, say
{\mathbf{b}}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}\},{\mathbf{c}}=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{d}\}\subset V

We obtain the transition matrix
P\in{\rm GL}(d;{\mathbb{F}})
that satisfies
b_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}P_{(j,i)}c_{j}
, where

is the
(j,i)
-th entry of

.
In what follows, we write
[{\mathbf{b}}/{\mathbf{c}}]\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
for the determinant of
P
.
Take a cochain complex

C^{*}:0\longrightarrow C^{0}\overset{\delta^{0}}{\longrightarrow}C^{1}\overset{\delta^{1}}{\longrightarrow}\cdots\xrightarrow{\ \delta^{k-2}\ }C^{k-1}\xrightarrow{\ \delta^{k-1}\ }C^{k}\longrightarrow 0,

(1)

such that each
C^{i}
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}
, and choose a basis
{\mathbf{c}}_{i}
of
C^{i}
for each
i
.
We employ the common notations
Z^{i}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(\delta^{i})
,
B^{i}=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits(\delta^{i-1})
, and
H^{i}=Z^{i}/B^{i}
.
Considering the two exact sequences

0\longrightarrow Z^{i}\longrightarrow C^{i}\overset{\delta^{i}}{\longrightarrow}B^{i+1}\longrightarrow 0,\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt0\longrightarrow B^{i}\longrightarrow Z^{i}{\longrightarrow}H^{i}\longrightarrow 0

over
{\mathbb{F}}
, we choose sections
\mathfrak{s}_{i}:B^{i+1}\rightarrow C^{i}
and

\mathfrak{s}_{i}^{\prime}:H^{i}\rightarrow Z^{i}
.
For
i\leq k
, let us choose a basis
{\mathbf{b}}_{i}
of
B^{i}
, and fix its lift to
C^{i-1}

\widetilde{{\mathbf{b}}}_{i}
denote the lift, that is,
\widetilde{{\mathbf{b}}}_{i+1}=\mathfrak{s}_{i}({\mathbf{b}}_{i+1})
.
Similarly, we select a basis
{\mathbf{h}}_{i}
of

Then, under the identifications, the (ordered) union

Denote by
{\mathbf{c}}

({\mathbf{c}}_{0}\cup{\mathbf{c}}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup{\mathbf{c}}_{k})

To summarize,

\tau(C^{*},{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{h}}):=\prod_{i=0}^{k}[({\mathbf{b}}_{i}\cup\widetilde{{\mathbf{h}}}_{i}\cup\widetilde{{\mathbf{b}}}_{i+1})/{\mathbf{c}}_{i}]^{(-1)^{i+1}}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}.

It is known (see
[
Tur
]

\tau(C^{*},{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{h}})

and

\mathfrak{s}_{i}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{i}^{\prime}
.
However, the torsion depends on the choices of
\mathbf{c}_{i}
and
\mathbf{h}_{i}
.
More precisely, if we select other such bases,
\mathbf{c}_{i}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{h}_{i}^{\prime}
, the following holds
[
Tur
, Remark 1.4.1]

\tau(C^{*},{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{h}})=\tau(C^{*},{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})\prod_{i=0}^{k}([{\mathbf{c}}_{i}/{\mathbf{c}}^{\prime}_{i}][{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{\prime}/{\mathbf{h}}_{i}])^{(-1)^{i+1}}\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}.

Next, let us review Reidemeister torsions. Let
Y
be a connected finite CW-complex. Take a
{\rm GL}_{n}
-representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(Y)\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
, and regard
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
as a left
{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(Y)]
-module. Let
\widetilde{Y}
be the universal covering space of
Y

C_{*}(\widetilde{Y};{\mathbb{Z}})
be the cellular complex associated with the CW-complex. This
C_{*}(\widetilde{Y};{\mathbb{Z}})
can be viewed as a left free
{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(Y)]
-module by covering transformations.
Choose a basis
{\mathbf{c}}_{i}
of the free
{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(Y)]
-module
C_{i}(\widetilde{Y};{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Then, we have the chain complex of the local system
C_{*}(\widetilde{Y};{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(Y)]}\mathbb{F}^{n}
.
Dually, the cochain complex of the local system is defined by

C^{*}_{\rho}(Y;\mathbb{F}^{n}):=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(Y)]\textrm{-mod}}(C_{*}(\widetilde{Y};{\mathbb{Z}}),\mathbb{F}^{n}).

As a special case, if
n=1
and
\rho
is trivial, we use the notation
C^{*}
instead of
C_{\rho}^{*}
.
For the standard basis
{\mathbf{f}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, the tensor product
{\mathbf{c}}_{i}\otimes{\mathbf{f}}
is a basis of the vector space
C_{i}(\widetilde{Y};{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{1}(Y)]}\mathbb{F}^{n}
.
Let
{\mathbf{c}}_{i}^{*}=({\mathbf{c}}\otimes{\mathbf{f}})^{*}
be the dual basis of
C^{i}_{\rho}(Y;\mathbb{F}^{n})
. Let
{\mathbf{c}}_{Y}
denote the union
{\mathbf{c}}_{0}^{*}\cup{\mathbf{c}}_{1}^{*}\cup\cdots\cup{\mathbf{c}}_{{\rm dim}Y}^{*}
.
Now, let us consider the subgroups

\begin{split}\mathrm{det}(\rho(\pi_{1}(Y)))&:=\{\mathrm{det}(\rho(x))\mid x\in\pi_{1}(Y)\}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{\times},\\
\pm\mathrm{det}(\rho(\pi_{1}(Y)))&:=\{\varepsilon\mathrm{det}(\rho(x))\mid x\in\pi_{1}(Y),\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}\}\subset{\mathbb{F}}^{\times},\end{split}

and the quotient group
\mathbb{F}^{\times}/\pm\mathrm{det}(\rho(\pi_{1}(Y)))
.
Furthermore, with a choice of basis
\mathbf{h}_{i}
of the cohomology
H^{i}_{\rho}(Y;\mathbb{F}^{n})
,
the Reidemeister torsion of
(
Y,\rho
) is defined as

\tau(C^{*}_{\rho}(Y;\mathbb{F}^{n}),{\mathbf{c}}_{Y},{\mathbf{h}})\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}/\pm\mathrm{det}(\rho(\pi_{1}(Y))).

As is well known
[
Mil
,
Tur
]
, the torsion modulo
\pm\mathrm{det}(\rho(\pi_{1}(Y)))
does not depend on the choice of
{\mathbf{c}}_{Y}
.

In addition, let us review the sign-refined torsions by Turaev
[
Tur
,
Dub
]
, and state Theorem
2.1
. Let
H^{*}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
be the ordinary cohomology over
{\mathbb{R}}
. Choosing an orientation of
\oplus_{i\geq 0}H^{i}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
, we choose a basis
\mathbf{h}_{i}^{{\mathbb{R}}}\subset H^{i}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
so that the sequence
(\mathbf{h}_{0}^{{\mathbb{R}}},\mathbf{h}_{1}^{{\mathbb{R}}},\dots)
is a positive basis in the oriented vector space
H^{*}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
. Consider

\tilde{\tau}(C^{*}(Y;\mathbb{R}),\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h}^{{\mathbb{R}}}):=(-1)^{N(Y)}\tau(C^{*}(Y;\mathbb{R}),\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h}^{{\mathbb{R}}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\times},

where

N(Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{{\rm dim}(Y)}\bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathrm{dim}H^{{\rm dim}(Y)-j}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathrm{dim}C^{{\rm dim}(Y)-j}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})\bigr)\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}.

(3)

Then,
the refined torsion
is defined to be

\tau^{0}_{\rho}(Y,\mathbf{h}):=\mathrm{sign}\bigl(\tilde{\tau}(C^{*}(Y;\mathbb{R}),\mathbf{c},\mathbf{h}^{{\mathbb{R}}})\bigr)^{n}\cdot\tau(C^{*}_{\rho}(Y;\mathbb{F}^{n}),\mathbf{c}_{Y},\mathbf{h})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(\pi_{1}(Y)).

(4)

If
n
is even, then the sign is +1, that is,
\tau^{0}_{\rho}(Y,\mathbf{h})=\tau(C^{*}_{\rho}(Y;\mathbb{F}^{n}),\mathbf{c}_{Y},\mathbf{h})

Theorem 2.1

(see
[
Tur
, Chapter 18]
or
[
Dub
, Chapter 2]
)
.

The sign-refined torsion
\tau^{0}_{\rho}(Y,\mathbf{h})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(\pi_{1}(Y))
is independent of the order of the oriented cells of
Y
and the choice of
\mathbf{h}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
; however, it does depend on the choice of
\mathbf{h}
. Furthermore, the torsion is invariant under simple homotopy equivalences that preserve the homology orientation.

As a corollary, let us consider the situation in which

is an oriented closed
C^{\infty}
-manifold.
Recall that any two triangulations of an oriented closed
C^{\infty}
-manifold

are simple homotopy equivalent (see, e.g.,
[

and
\mathbf{h}
.

3
Non-acyclic torsions of closed manifolds of odd dimension

Since Theorem
2.1
and (
2
) imply that the refined torsion (
4
) depends on
\mathbf{h}
,
it is reasonable to find some torsions, which depend only on the representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(Y)\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
. Previous studies have investigated torsions where
Y
is an oriented closed manifold of even dimension; see, e.g.,

[
Söz
,
Wit
]
.
In contrast, this paper addresses non-trivial torsions in the case
\mathrm{dim}X=2m+1
in terms of Poincaré duality.

To this end, we prepare some cup products as follows.
Fix a bilinear form
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}
satisfying the
\rho
-invariance
, that is,

\psi(\rho(g)\cdot v,\rho(g)\cdot w)=\psi(v,w)
holds for any
g\in\pi_{1}(X)
and
v,w\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Furthermore,
suppose that
\psi
is either hermitian or anti-hermitian.
Using the cup product
\smile
, let us consider the composite map

H^{i}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\otimes H^{2m+1-i}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\smile}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2m+1}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\otimes{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\xrightarrow{\ \psi_{*}\circ\cap[X]\ }{\mathbb{F}},

(5)

where
\cap[X]
is the cap-product with the fundamental class
[X]\in H_{2m+1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
, and
the last map
\psi_{*}
is the coupling with
\psi
.
In addition, we define
N({\mathbb{F}})
to be
\{y\bar{y}\mid y\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}\}
as a multiplicative subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
,
and we consider the quotient group

{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\langle N({\mathbb{F}}),\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(\rho(\pi_{1}(X)))\rangle={\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\{x\bar{x}\mathrm{det}(\rho(g))\mid x\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times},g\in\pi_{1}(X)\}.

Then, the topological invariance of the refined torsion is shown as follows:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}
be a (anti-)hermitian
\rho
-invariant bilinear form.
Take a basis
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}
of
H^{j}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})
for
j\leq m.

Suppose the non-degeneracy of (
5
), which defines uniquely

{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\rm dual}\subset H^{2m+1-j}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})
as the dual basis of
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}
.
Then, the refined torsion

\tau^{0}_{\rho}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{0},\mathbf{h}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{h}}_{m},\mathbf{h}_{m}^{\rm dual},\mathbf{h}_{m-1}^{\rm dual},\dots,{\mathbf{h}}_{0}^{\rm dual}\})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\langle N({\mathbb{F}}),\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(\rho(\pi_{1}(X)))\rangle

(6)

is independent of the choice of the bases
{\mathbf{h}}_{0},\dots,{\mathbf{h}}_{m}
.

Proof.

Likewise the proof of Theorem
2.1
, it suffices to show that the torsion is independent of the choice of
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}
and the order of
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}
.
The independence of the order is obvious since any change in the order of
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}
is parallel to that of
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\rm dual}
.
To show the remaining independence of
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}
, we choose another ordered basis
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\prime}
of
H^{j}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})
.
Since
\psi
is (anti) hermitian , we can easily check that
[{\mathbf{h}}_{j}/{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\prime}]=\overline{[{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\rm dual}/({\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\prime})^{\rm dual}]}^{-1}
.
Thus, by (
2
), the torsion (
6
) is equal to

\tau^{0}_{\rho}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{0}^{\prime},\dots,{\mathbf{h}}_{m}^{\prime},(\mathbf{h}_{m}^{\prime})^{\rm dual},\dots,({\mathbf{h}}_{0}^{\prime})^{\rm dual}\})\prod_{j=0}^{m}([{\mathbf{h}}_{j}/{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\prime}]\overline{[{\mathbf{h}}_{j}/{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\prime}]})^{(-1)^{j+1}}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}.

(7)

Thus, the torsion in the quotient group
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})
is independent of the choice of the bases
{\mathbf{h}}_{0},\dots,{\mathbf{h}}_{m}
as required.
∎

5
).
We will give such a condition as follows:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}
be a
\rho
-invariant bilinear form.
If
\psi
is non-degenerate and the field
{\mathbb{F}}
is of characteristic zero, then there is an isomorphism
D:H^{i}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\cong H_{\rho}^{2m+1-i}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})

Although this proposition may be known (see, e.g.,
[

]
), we defer the proof into Appendix
A
.
Here, we note that
D(ax)=\bar{a}D(x)
for
a\in{\mathbb{F}}
and
x\in H^{i}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n}).

Furthermore, we will show that the refined torsion in Theorem

]
; see Appendix

, and
\psi^{\prime}
be the Killing form that sends
(X,Y)
to
\mathrm{Tr}(XY)
, where the involution is the identity. From the viewpoint of a stratification of
R_{G}(X)
as a real algebraic variety,
there is an open dense set
R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}\subset R_{G}(X)
that has a
C^{\infty}
-manifold structure; see, e.g.,
[
Kar
]
.
As a classical result, recall that, for
f\in R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}
, the first cohomology
H^{1}_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}(X;\mathfrak{g})
can be identified with
the tangent space of
R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}
, as in
[
Sic
,
Kar
]
.
Since the Killing form
\psi^{\prime}
is non-degenerate, the pairing (
5
) is non-degenerate by Proposition
3.2
.
Thus, the correspondence

H^{2}_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}(X;\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits(H^{1}_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}(X;\mathfrak{g}),{\mathbb{R}});\ \ w\longmapsto(v\mapsto\psi^{\prime}((v\smile w)\cap[X]))

(9)

is an isomorphism, and therefore, the second one
H^{2}_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}(X;\mathfrak{g})
can be identified with
the cotangent space of
R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}
. Let
d
be the dimension
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(H^{2}_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}(X;\mathfrak{g}))
.
Thus, for a basis
{\mathbf{h}}_{2}
of
H^{2}_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}(X;\mathfrak{g})
, the
d
-fold exterior product (i.e., the determinant bundle) of
{\mathbf{h}}_{2}
is a
d
-form of the manifold
R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}
.
Let
{\mathbf{h}}_{2}^{\otimes}
denote the
d
-form, and
\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm dual}
be the dual basis of
\mathbf{h}_{2}
by (
9
).
From (
7
), the
d
-form defined to be

\tau_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}^{0}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm dual},\mathbf{h}_{2}\})^{1/2}\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\otimes}\in\bigwedge^{d}H^{2}_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}(X;\mathfrak{g})\setminus\{0\}

(10)

is independent of the choices of
\mathbf{h}_{2}
. In fact, if we have another
\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\prime}
, by (
2
) and (
7
), we see

\begin{split}\tau_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}^{0}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm dual},\mathbf{h}_{2}\})^{1/2}\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\otimes}&=\bigr([\mathbf{h}_{2}/\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\prime}]^{2}\tau_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}^{0}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm dual},\mathbf{h}_{2}\})\bigr)^{1/2}[\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\prime}/\mathbf{h}_{2}]\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\otimes}\\
&=\bigr([\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm dual}/\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm{}^{\prime}dual}][\mathbf{h}_{2}/\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\prime}]\tau_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}^{0}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm dual},\mathbf{h}_{2}\})\bigr)^{1/2}\mathbf{h}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}\otimes}\\
&=\tau_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}^{0}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm{}^{\prime}dual},\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\prime}\})^{1/2}\mathbf{h}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}\otimes}.\end{split}

In summary, the correspondence

R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}\longrightarrow\wedge^{d}H^{2}_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}(X;\mathfrak{g})\setminus\{0\};f\longmapsto\tau_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}^{0}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm dual},\mathbf{h}_{2}\})^{1/2}\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\otimes}

can be regarded as a volume form of the variety
R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}
.
Thus, it is reasonable to define the volume of the
{\rm SU}(n)
-character variety as follows:

Definition 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits(X)=3
and
G={\rm SU}(n)
.
We define
the volume of
R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}
to be the integral of
\tau_{\rho^{\prime}\circ f}^{0}(X,\{\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\rm dual},\mathbf{h}_{2}\})^{1/2}\mathbf{h}_{2}^{\otimes}
on
R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}
.

In §
8.3
, we give examples of computing the volume, where
X
is a torus bundle.

Finally, we discuss a comparison with the preprint
[
MP
]
. Here, starting from the same assumption in Definition
4.2
, the authors define a volume of the variety
R_{G}(X)^{\rm o}
using another approach (see Theorems 3.5 and 3.6 in
[
MP
]
) and examine some relations to
[
Wit
]
.
However, their definition requires a certain condition; but Definition
4.2
does not.
It is seemingly interesting to check whether their volume and our volume in the condition are equal or not.

5
Some computations of the quotient groups
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})

Since the torsion (
6
) lies in the quotient group
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})
in the case
\mathrm{Im}(\rho)\subset\mathrm{SL}_{n}({\mathbb{F}})
, we provide some computations of the groups of some fields
{\mathbb{F}}
.

Example 5.1
.

(i)

If the equation
x\bar{x}=1
implies
x=1\in{\mathbb{F}}
, then
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})
is trivial.
For example, if
\bar{x}=x
for any
x\in{\mathbb{F}}
and
{\mathbb{F}}
is quadratically closed,
then
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})=0.

(ii)

Let
q
be a prime power, and
{\mathbb{F}}
the finite field of order
q
. Suppose the involution is
\mathrm{id}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
. Since
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
is the cyclic group of order
q-1
,
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2
if
q
is odd, and

{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})\cong 0
if
q
is even.

(iii)

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be
{\mathbb{C}}
, and the involution be the complex conjugacy.
Then, the map
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}\rightarrow S^{1}
that sends
x
to
x/|x|
induces the isomorphism
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{C}})\cong S^{1}=U(1)
.

Next, we focus on the case where

{\mathbb{F}}
is the fraction field of a unique factorization domain
A
satisfying
\overline{A}\subset A
.
Let us choose a complete representative set of all prime elements of
A
and denote the set by
\mathfrak{P}_{A}
.
Notice that
p\in A
is prime if and only if
\bar{p}
is prime.
Without loss of generality, we may suppose that every
p\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}
satisfies either
\bar{p}\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}
or

\bar{p}=a_{p}p
for some
a_{p}\in A^{\times}
.
Let
\mathfrak{P}_{A}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}
be the subset
\{p\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}\mid p^{-1}\bar{p}\in A^{\times}\}
which is the invariant subset of the conjugation; we can choose a subset
\mathfrak{cP}_{A}\subset\mathfrak{P}_{A}

such that
\mathfrak{P}_{A}=\mathfrak{P}_{A}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}\sqcup\mathfrak{cP}_{A}\sqcup\overline{\mathfrak{cP}_{A}}.

Then, we have the isomorphism

\bigoplus_{p\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}}{\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus\bigoplus_{q\in\mathfrak{cP}_{A}}{\mathbb{Z}}\cong{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\langle N({\mathbb{F}}),A^{\times}\rangle,

(11)

which sends
((a_{p})_{p},(b_{q})_{q})
to
\prod_{p}p^{a_{p}}\prod_{q}q^{b_{q}}
.
We also have a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow A^{\times}/\{a\bar{a}\mid a\in A^{\times}\}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\langle N({\mathbb{F}}),A^{\times}\rangle\rightarrow 0,

(12)

where the first (resp. second) map is obtained by the inclusion
A\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}
(resp. the projection).
In summary, from (
11
) and (
12
), we can sometimes determine the group
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})
as follows:

Example 5.2
.

Suppose that the involution on
{\mathbb{F}}
is the identity. Then,
\mathfrak{cP}_{A}
is obviously empty, and
p=\bar{p}
for any
p\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}
; thus, the sequence (
12
) splits. Hence,

{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})={\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/({\mathbb{F}}^{\times})^{2}\cong(A^{\times}/(A^{\times})^{2})\oplus\bigoplus_{p\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}}{\mathbb{Z}}/2.

For example, if
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Q}}
and
A={\mathbb{Z}}
, then
{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{Q}})\cong\bigoplus_{p\in\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{Z}})}{\mathbb{Z}}/2
.
Furthermore, if
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{R}}
, then
{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{R}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2
.

Example 5.3
.

As mentioned in Section
4
, let
{\mathbb{F}}
be the fraction field
{\mathbb{Q}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Here, the involution of
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{F}}
is defined by
f(t_{1}^{-1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{-1})
.
Notice that
{\mathbb{F}}
is the fraction field of
A={\mathbb{Q}}[t^{\pm 1}_{1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{\pm 1}]
as a unique factorization domain.
We say
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{F}}
to be
reciprocal
if
\bar{f}=rt_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{n_{\ell}}f
for some

r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
and
n_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then, the subset
\mathfrak{P}_{A}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}
is the set consisting of reciprocal irreducible polynomials in
{\mathbb{Q}}[t^{\pm 1}_{1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{\pm}]
,
and any
p\in\mathfrak{cP}_{A}
is not reciprocal.
It is not so hard to check that the group
A^{\times}/\{a\bar{a}\mid a\in A^{\times}\}
is isomorphic to

{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/({\mathbb{Q}}^{\times})^{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
is generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell}
;
the exact sequence (
12
) turns out to be

0\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}/({\mathbb{Q}}^{\times})^{2}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})^{\times}/N({\mathbb{Q}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell}))\rightarrow

(13)

\hskip 23.49976pt\rightarrow\bigoplus_{p\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}}{\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus\bigoplus_{q\in\mathfrak{cP}_{A}}{\mathbb{Z}}\longrightarrow 0.

Here, let us notice that
(g-\bar{g})^{2}=-(g-\bar{g})\overline{(g-\bar{g})}=-1\neq 1
hold in
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})
for any
g\in A
such that
g\neq\bar{g}
;
Thus,
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})
contains many elements of order 4, unless
\ell=1
.
In particular, the exact sequence does not split.

We proceed with the computation for the simplest case of
\ell=1
.
Let
\mathfrak{sP}_{A}
be the set of symmetric irreducible polynomials in
{\mathbb{Q}}[t^{\pm 1}]
.
Since
{\mathbb{Q}}[t]
is a Euclidean domain,
any polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Q}}[t^{\pm 1}]
ensures some
n,m,u,s\in{\mathbb{Z}},r\in{\mathbb{Q}},a_{i}\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}^{{\mathbb{Z}}/2}
and
b_{j}\in\mathfrak{P}_{A}
such that

f=r(1-t)^{u}(1+t)^{s}a_{1}\cdots a_{n}b_{1}\cdots b_{m},\hskip 23.49976pt\textrm{and}\hskip 23.49976pt\overline{a_{i}}=a_{i}.

Since

(1-t)^{2}=-t(1-t)(1-\bar{t}),\hskip 23.49976pt\textrm{and}\hskip 23.49976pt(1+t)^{2}=t(1+t)(1+\bar{t}),

the subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)^{\times}/N({\mathbb{Q}}(t))
generated by
-1,t\pm 1,t
is isomorphic to

{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/4
, where the summand
{\mathbb{Z}}
is generated by
t+1
and
{\mathbb{Z}}/4
is done by
(t-1)(1+t^{-1})
.
In conclusion, we obtain from (
13
) an isomorphism

{\mathbb{Q}}(t)^{\times}/N({\mathbb{Q}}(t))\cong{\mathbb{Z}}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/4\oplus\bigoplus_{p\in\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{Z}})\setminus\{0\}}{\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus\bigoplus_{p\in\mathfrak{sP}_{A}\setminus\{t\pm 1\}}{\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus\bigoplus_{q\in\mathfrak{cP}_{A}}{\mathbb{Z}}.

Example 5.4
.

Suppose that
{\mathbb{F}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a number field and the involution is the complex conjugacy satisfying
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}
be the integral closure of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Dirichlet’s unit theorem implies that the first term in (
12
) is computed as

\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\times}/\{a\bar{a}\mid a\in\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\times}\}\cong({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})^{r_{1}-1}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{r_{2}}\oplus(\{a\in{\mathbb{F}}\mid a\bar{a}=1\}/({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})),

where
r_{1}
is the number of real embeddings and
r_{2}
is the number of conjugate pairs of complex embeddings of
{\mathbb{F}}
, and the latter quotient by
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
is defined by the involution.
Meanwhile, recall that the ideal class group
C(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}})
is trivial if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
is a UFD.
Thus, if
C(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}})
is trivial, then we can
compute the group
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})
by (
11
) and (
12
).

However, conversely, if
C(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}})\neq 1
, it seems hard to obtain a similar isomorphism to (
11
) because of the following reasons.
Hasse’s local-global principle implies that the computation of
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/N({\mathbb{F}})
is derived from
those of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/(N({\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}))
, where
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}})
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the local field.
However, even if the residue field of
\mathfrak{p}
is of characteristic two, the computation of
{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}/(N({\mathbb{F}}_{\mathfrak{p}}))
is difficult.
Furthermore, if
{\mathbb{Q}}\subset{\mathbb{F}}
is not an abelian extension, in general,
it is also difficult to determine all prime ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
even using the class field theory.

6
Some topological invariants from refined torsions

As corollaries of Theorem
3.1
, we introduce some topological invariants in terms of refined torsions.
For example, we show that the theorem produces extended definitions of
abelian Reidemeister torsions and (multivariable) Alexander polynomials of links; see Sections
6.3
and
6.4
.
Before those, we also discuss some torsions with trivial coefficients, which are shown to be not so interesting (Sections
6.1
and
6.2
).

6.1
Torsions from the trivial coefficients over a field
{\mathbb{F}}

We begin by examining the case where
\rho
with
n=1
is trivial and
\psi
is the canonical multiplication of
{\mathbb{F}}
.
Then,
H^{j}(X;{\mathbb{F}})
is the ordinary cohomology of
X
, and the ordinary cup product in (
5
) is widely known to be non-degenerate.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
X
be an oriented closed manifold of dimension
2m+1
.
We define the torsion of
X
to be the refined torsion (
6
) in
{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/({\mathbb{F}}^{\times})^{2}
.

Example 6.2
.

Suppose that
X
admits a CW-complex structure that
all boundary maps of the cellular complex are zero.
Then,
\mathbf{h}_{j}=\mathbf{c}_{j}
, and
\mathbf{b}_{j}=\emptyset
by definitions.
Thus, to compute the refined torsion, we may only find a dual basis
\mathbf{h}_{j}^{\rm dual}
by computing the cup product
and
[\mathbf{h}_{j}^{\rm dual}/\mathbf{c}_{2m+1-j}]\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
.
In particular, the refined torsion is a weaker invariant than the cup product structure of
X
in coefficients
{\mathbb{F}}
.

However, we give two examples.
Let
X
be the torus
(S^{1})^{2m+1}
. Consider the CW-structure of
S^{1}
consisting of a
1
-cell and a
0
-cell and the product CW-structure of
X
. Then, by an easy computation of the cross product, we can check that
\mathbf{h}_{j}^{\rm dual}=\mathbf{c}_{2m+1-j}
.
Hence, the torsion is trivially 1.

On the other hand, let
{\mathbb{F}}
be the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
X
be the 3-dimensional lens space
L(p,q)
, where
p
is an odd prime and
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is relatively prime to
p
.
Then, by the genus-one Heegaard decomposition, we have a CW-structure of
X
such that
the
k
-cell is single with
0\leq k\leq 3
, and that the boundary map is zero.
Then, by the resulting computation of the cup product (see, e.g.,
[
Nos
, Example 5.1]
),
\mathbf{h}_{1}^{\rm dual}=q\mathbf{c}_{2}
and

\mathbf{h}_{0}^{\rm dual}=\mathbf{c}_{3}
. In conclusion, the refined torsion is
q\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}/({\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times})^{2}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2.

In particular, the torsions distinguish between
L(7,1)
and
L(7,2)
.
However, as seen above, the torsion in this subsection is a weak topological invariant.

6.2
Torsions from the trivial coefficients over
{\mathbb{Q}}

We focus on the rational case
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Q}}
and suggest a
modification of Definition
6.1
.
For a finitely generated abelian group
A
, we denote by
\mathrm{Free}(A)
the free submodule of
A
.
Notice that the inclusion
\iota:{\mathbb{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}
induces an isomorphism

\iota_{*}:H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\cong H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})
, and the cup product

\smile:\mathrm{Free}(H^{j}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}))\times\mathrm{Free}(H^{2m+1-j}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}))\longrightarrow H^{2m+1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}

is unimodular, that is, the determinant of
\smile
is
+1
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Choose a basis
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}
of
\mathrm{Free}(H^{j}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}))
for
j\leq m
.
Let
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}^{\rm dual}\subset H^{2m+1-j}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
be the dual basis of
{\mathbf{h}}_{j}
.
The refined torsion
\tau^{0}_{\rho}(X,{\mathbf{h}})\in{\mathbb{Q}}
in
(
4
) is a topological invariant of
X
.

Since the proof is almost the same as the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we omit the details.
However, if
X
is a rational homology sphere or if
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits X=3
, then the torsion is not entirely interesting as follows:

Proposition 6.4
.

(i)

If
H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H^{*}(S^{2m+1};{\mathbb{Q}})
, then
the torsion
\tau^{0}_{\rho}(X,{\mathbf{h}})
is equal to
\prod_{i=1}^{2m}|H^{i}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})|^{(-1)^{i}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(ii)

If
m=1
, then the torsion
\tau^{0}_{\rho}(X,{\mathbf{h}})
equals
\varepsilon_{X}|H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})|^{-1}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
for some
\varepsilon_{X}\in\{\pm 1\}
.

Proof.

By Morse theory, we can choose a CW-complex on
X
such that
the
0
-th and
(2m+1)
-th cells are single.
Then, the coboundary maps
\delta^{0}
and
\delta^{2m}
in the cellular complex
C^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
are zero.
Consider the subcomplex
E^{*}
defined by
C^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
if
1\leq*\leq 2m
and by zero otherwise.
If
H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H^{*}(S^{2m+1};{\mathbb{Q}})
, then
E^{*}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
is acyclic, and

\tau^{0}_{\rho}(X,{\mathbf{h}})
is equal to the torsion of
E^{*}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
;
Theorem 1.4 in
[
Tur
]
immediately implies that the torsion of
E^{*}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
is equal to
\prod_{i=1}^{2m}|H^{i}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})|^{(-1)^{i}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. Hence, we complete the proof of (i).

Next, consider the case
m=1
, i.e.,
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits X=3.

Then, the subcomplex is presented as
0\rightarrow C^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta^{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}C^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow 0.

Suppose that the elementary divisors of
\delta^{1}
are
e_{1},\dots,e_{k},0,\dots,0
for some
e_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that
|H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})|=|H_{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})/\mathrm{Free}(H_{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}}))|=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{k}.

The elementary divisor theory implies that
\delta^{1}
is the diagonal matrix
e_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus e_{k}\oplus 0\oplus\cdots\oplus 0
up to the choice of the base of
C^{2}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
. Since
Z^{1}=H^{1}
, we can verify that the torsion of the diagonal matrix is
equal to
\varepsilon_{X}(e_{1}\cdots e_{k})^{-1}
. In summary,
the required torsion is equal to
\varepsilon_{X}|H^{1}(X;{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})|^{-1}
as claimed in (ii). ∎

6.3
Non-acyclic abelian Reidemeister torsions

The purpose of this subsection is to show that the torsions in Theorem
3.1
in abelian coefficients yield
an extended definition of the abelian Reidemeister torsions including non-acyclic cases.

For this purpose, let us first review abelian Reidemeister torsions from
[
Tur
, Chapter II]
.
Choose the decomposition

\mu:H_{1}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})\cong\mathrm{Free}(H_{1}(X))\oplus\mathrm{Tor}(H_{1}(X))

and a generating set
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell}\}
of
\mathrm{Free}(H_{1}(X))
.
Let
\rho_{\rm ab}
be the composition of the abelianization of
\pi_{1}(X)
with
\mu
and the map
\mathrm{Free}(H_{1}(X))\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{F}})
that sends
t_{i}
to
t_{i}
itself, where
{\mathbb{F}}
is the field
{\mathbb{Q}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})
as in Example
5.3
,
which is a local coefficient via
\rho_{\rm ab}
.
If the complex
C^{*}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}})
is acyclic,
then the torsion of
C^{*}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}})
is called
the maximal abelian torsion
,
and it is well-studied together with its relationship to the Alexander polynomial and Milnor torsions; see
[
Mil
,
Tur
]
.

On the other hand, concerning the torsion parts, it is known (see
[
Tur
, Theorem 12.5]
) that the group ring
{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathrm{Tor}(H_{1}(X))]
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is
isomorphic to a direct sum of some cyclotomic fields
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathbb{F}}_{m}
,
where
{\mathbb{F}}_{i}={\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{i})\subset{\mathbb{C}}
for some root of unity
\mu_{i}
. Let
p_{i}:{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathrm{Tor}(H_{1}(X))]\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{i}
be the projection.
Then, the composition of the abelianization of
\pi_{1}(X)
and
p_{i}
gives a representation
\rho_{i}:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{i}^{\times}={\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{F}}_{i})
.

We will explain the above purpose in detail.
For
f=f(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{F}}
, we define the involution
\bar{f}
as
f(t_{1}^{-1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{-1})
, and
similarly, the involution on
{\mathbb{Q}}[\mathrm{Tor}(H_{1}(X))]
by the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear extension of the map
g\mapsto-g
on
\mathrm{Tor}(H_{1}(X))
.
Define
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}\times{\mathbb{F}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}
and

\psi_{i}:{\mathbb{F}}_{i}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{i}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{i}
by
(a,b)\mapsto a\bar{b}
, which satisfy the non-degeneracy in Proposition
3.2
.
Therefore, as a result of Theorem
3.1
, even if
C^{*}_{\rho}(X;{\mathbb{F}})
is not acyclic, we can define the torsion

\tau_{\rho_{\rm ab}}(X)\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\langle N({\mathbb{F}}),t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{\pm 1}\rangle,

(14)

similar to (
6
).
On the other hand, for the torsion subgroup of

,
we can define the torsion to be the tuple

\bigl(\tau_{\rho_{1}}(X,\mathbf{h}),\dots,\tau_{\rho_{m}}(X,\mathbf{h})\bigr)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{1}^{\times}/\langle\mu_{1},N({\mathbb{F}}_{1})\rangle\times\cdots\times{\mathbb{F}}_{m}^{\times}/\langle\mu_{m},N({\mathbb{F}}_{m})\rangle,

as in
[
Tur
, Section II. 13]
.
In conclusion, these torsions in non-acyclic cases can be interpreted as an extension of the abelian Reidemeister torsions.

6.4
Extension of the Alexander polynomial of links

Next, we define torsions of any link, of which the Alexander polynomial is zero, as a generalization of the Alexander polynomials of links.

For this, let us review the Alexander polynomial; see, e.g.,
[
Tur
, §II. 15]
.
Let
L\subset S^{3}
be a link of
\ell
-components. Fix an abelianization
\mu:\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
and
a generating set
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell}\}
of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
.
Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be the fractional field
{\mathbb{Q}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})
. Since
\mu
is regarded as a representation
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L)\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{\pm 1}])
that sends
g
to
\mu(g)
,
we have the homology
H_{*}(S^{3}\setminus L;{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{\pm 1}])
of local systems.
Then,
the (multivariable) Alexander polynomial

\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{\pm 1}]/\{\pm t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}\mid(k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\}

is defined to be
the order of
H_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L;{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{\pm 1}])
, that is, the minimal polynomial that
annihilates
H_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L;{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{\ell}^{\pm 1}])
.
In the case
t=t_{1}=\cdots=t_{\ell}
, the one variable polynomial
\Delta_{L}(t,\dots,t)\in{\mathbb{Q}}(t)/\{t^{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is also called
the Alexander polynomial
.
It is known that the polynomial
\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})
is not zero if and only if the homology
H_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L;{\mathbb{F}})
vanishes,
and
\Delta_{L}(t,\dots,t)
is not zero if and only if the homology
H_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L;{\mathbb{Q}}(t))
vanishes.
If
\ell=1
, that is,
L
is any knot, then
\Delta_{L}(t)\neq 0
is well known.

We also review (
15
) below, which is shown by Turaev.
Let
M_{L}
be the closed 3-manifold obtained by the zero-surgery on
L
. Then, the abelianization
\mu
induces an abelianization
\pi_{1}(M_{L})\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}
, which is considered as a representation

\mu_{\rm ab}:\pi_{1}(M_{L})\rightarrow{\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{F}})={\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{Q}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})).

Thus,
{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{Q}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})
can be regarded as local coefficients of
M_{L}.
It is known (see
[
Tur
, Corollary 15.2 and Theorem 17.2]
) that, if the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})
is not zero, then

\begin{split}\tau_{\mu_{\rm ab}}^{0}(M_{L})=\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})&\prod_{j:1\leq j\leq\ell}(t_{j}-1)\\
&\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\{rt_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}\mid(k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell},r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}\},\end{split}

(15)

and that the same equality with
t=t_{1}=\cdots=t_{\ell}
holds if
\Delta_{L}(t,\dots,t)\neq 0
.

In summary, it seems sensible to extend the Alexander polynomial from the viewpoint of refined torsions.

Definition 6.5
.

Suppose
\mu>1
.
Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be
{\mathbb{Q}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})
as above.
If
\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\ell})
is zero, we define
an (extended) Alexander polynomial

of
L
to be the torsion

\tau^{0}_{\mu_{\rm ab}}(M_{L})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\langle N({\mathbb{F}}),\{t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}\mid(k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\ell}\}\rangle,

(16)

where
\tau^{0}_{\mu_{\rm ab}}
is defined in (
14
).

Recall that the quotient group in (
16
) is computed in Example
5.3
.
However, as a future problem, it remains to establish a procedure for computing the torsions of links with Alexander polynomial zero.

In the preceding sections, we defined the torsions modulo the subgroup

Throughout this section, we suppose a closed 3-manifold

Definition 7.1

As in Theorem

Proposition 7.2
.

Suppose that the triple
(\rho,\psi,\Sigma)

H^{\rho}_{0}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})

By the non-degeneracy, let us choose a normalized orthonormal basis

of

with respect to (
17

and the dual basis
{\mathbf{h}}_{1}^{\rm dual}
of

Then, the refined torsion

\tau^{0}_{\rho}(X,\{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\mathbf{h}_{1}^{\rm dual}\})\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}/\pm\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(\pi_{1}(X))

(18)

modulo
\pm\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(\pi_{1}(X))

{\mathbf{h}}_{1}

Proof.

Consider another orthonormal basis

∎

However, in general, there is no clarity on whether the existence of such a 2-class

is admissible; see §

Proposition 7.3
.

Assume that the inclusion

\displaystyle=(1-x_{1},1-x_{2},1-x_{3},\dots,1-x_{g})^{\rm transpose}.

(22)

D:\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{*}(X;({\mathbb{F}}^{n})^{*})
.
In addition, define

\displaystyle\mu^{\bullet}:\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{*}(X;({\mathbb{F}}^{n})^{*})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n}\oplus({\mathbb{F}}^{n})^{*});

\displaystyle(v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{m},w_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{m})

\displaystyle\longmapsto v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{m}\wedge w_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{m}.

As in Section 4.7 in
[
Far
]
and Section 9 in
[
FT
]
, the
Poincaré-Reidemeister (scalar) product
is defined to be a hermitian product by setting

{\rm PR}:\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\times\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{*}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{\times};(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\frac{\mu^{\bullet}(\alpha\otimes D(\beta))}{\tau_{\rho\oplus\bar{\rho}}(X)}.

(33)

However, we will show that this product is trivial in the sense of Lemma
B.1
.
For this, let
H^{\rm half}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})
be the graded vector space
\oplus_{j=0}^{m}H^{j}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})
.
Similarly, the Poincaré duality induces an isomorphism

D:\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{\rm half}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits(\oplus_{j=m+1}^{2m+1}H^{j}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})).

Then, by definition, for any
\mathbf{h}^{\rm half}\in H^{\rm half}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})
, we can easily show

{\rm PR}(\tau_{\rho}^{0}(X,\mathbf{h}^{\prime})\mathbf{h}^{\prime},\tau_{\rho}^{0}(X,\mathbf{h}^{\prime})\mathbf{h}^{\prime})=1\in{\mathbb{F}},

where
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}=\mathbf{h}^{\rm half}\otimes D(\mathbf{h}^{\rm half})^{-1}
.
Hence, by Lemma
B.1
, the bilinear isomorphism class of
{\rm PR}
is trivial, as required.

We shall consider a modification of the PR product, and provide a comparison with the refined torsion. To be precise, we define
a half PR product
by the hermitian product

{\rm PR}^{\rm half}:\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{\rm half}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\times\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H^{\rm half}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{\times};(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\frac{\mu^{\bullet}(\alpha\otimes D(\beta))}{\tau_{\rho}(X)}.

(34)

By definition, we can easily check that, for any
\mathbf{h}^{\rm half}\in H^{\rm half}(X;{\mathbb{F}}^{n})
, the value
{\rm PR}^{\rm half}(\mathbf{h}^{\rm half},\mathbf{h}^{\rm half})

is equal to the inverse of the refined torsion in (
6
).
In conclusion, it follows from Lemma
B.1
that the bilinear form associated with the refined torsion in (
6
) is equal to the half PR product (
34
), as a modification of the original
{\rm PR}
product.

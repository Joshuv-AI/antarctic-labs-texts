1.
Introduction

The theory of monomial ideals is one of the most well-studied topics in the area of combinatorial commutative algebra.
Since any monomial ideal can be deduced into a
squarefree
monomial ideal by polarization (see
[
9
, Section 1.6]
),
Stanley–Reisner rings and Stanley–Reisner ideals play a crucial role.
The goal of the present paper is the investigation of some algebraic invariants on Stanley–Reisner rings (or ideals).

For the terminologies used throughout the present paper, see Section
2
.
One typical class of Stanley–Reisner ideals is the edge ideals
I(G)
of graphs
G
, which coincide with the Stanley–Reisner ideals of the independent complexes
\Delta(G)
of
G
.
Recently, the invariants on the Stanley–Reisner rings
\Bbbk[\Delta(G)]
of
\Delta(G)
are intensively investigated (
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
, and so on).
What we would like to do in the present paper is to generalize the previous studies on the edge ideals and initiate a new study for more general squarefree monomial ideals than edge ideals.

For the investigation of the invariants on Stanley–Reisner rings of simplicial complexes
\Delta
,
we focus on the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)
and the Cohen–Macaulay type
\operatorname{type}(\Delta)
.
The first main result of the present paper is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complex of dimension
d-1
satisfying
\Delta=\operatorname{core}(\Delta)
.
Then we have

\displaystyle d\leq\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)\cdot\operatorname{type}(\Delta).

(1.1)

We can also see in the following proposition that a stronger inequality holds for the simplicial complexes
\Delta
under some stronger assumption on
\Bbbk[\Delta]
:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complex of dimension
d-1
satisfying
\Delta=\operatorname{core}(\Delta)
.
Assume that
\Delta
satisfies one of the following:

(1)

\Bbbk[\Delta]
has a
1
-linear resolution; or

(2)

a(\Delta)=0
, where
a(\Delta)
denotes the
a
-invariant of
\Bbbk[\Delta]
.

Then the inequality

\displaystyle d\leq\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)+\operatorname{type}(\Delta)-1

(1.2)

holds.

Note that the inequality (
1.2
) implies (
1.1
) since
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)\geq 1
and
\operatorname{type}(\Delta)\geq 1
.
In Section
3
, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.2
.

If an inequality appears among the invariants, then it is quite natural to think of whether that is best possible or not.
The following theorem, which is the second main result, shows that the inequality (
1.1
) is best possible:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
d,r,t
be integers with
r,t\geq 2
and assume that the inequalities
r\leq d\leq rt
hold.
Then there exists a graph
G
having no isolated vertex such that
\Delta(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay and

\dim(\Delta(G))=d-1,\;\;\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(G))=r\text{ and }\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G))=t.

We note that an isolated vertex of
G
corresponds to a vertex in
V(G)\setminus\operatorname{core}(V(G))
, so the condition
\Delta=\operatorname{core}(\Delta)
is equivalent to what
G
has no isolated vertex.
Moreover, the inequality
r\leq d
naturally holds (see Remark
2.1
).

In Section
4
, we give a proof of Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Notation and terminologies

In this section, we collect the notation used in the present paper.
Please consult, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 5]
or
[
9
]
, for the detailed information.

Let
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
be a finite set.
We call a family
\Delta
of subsets of
V
an
(abstract) simplicial complex
on the vertex set
V

if it satisfies that

(i)

\{v_{i}\}\in\Delta
for any
v_{i}\in V
; and

(ii)

if
F\in\Delta
and
F^{\prime}\subset F
, then
F^{\prime}\in\Delta
.

Note that
\emptyset
belongs to
\Delta
for any simplicial complex
\Delta
.
Let
\dim(\Delta)
denote the dimension of
\Delta
, i.e.,
\dim(\Delta)=\max\{\dim(F):F\in\Delta\}
.

Throughout this section, let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex of dimension
d-1
on the vertex
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
.

2.1.
Terminologies on simplicial complexes

Given
F\in\Delta
, let

\displaystyle\Delta\setminus F=\{G\in\Delta:G\subset V\setminus F\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{star}(F)=\{G\in\Delta:F\cup G\in\Delta\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\operatorname{link}(F)=\operatorname{star}(F)\setminus F.

When
F=\{v\}
, we use the notation
\Delta\setminus v
instead of
\Delta\setminus\{v\}
, and so on.

Given a new vertex
v
, let
\Delta*v
be a new simplicial complex on the vertex set
V\sqcup\{v\}
consisting of
\{F,F\cup\{v\}:F\in\Delta\}
.
We call
\Delta*v
a
cone
of
\Delta
. Note that
\operatorname{star}_{\Delta*v}(v)=\Delta*v
.

For
W\subset V
, let
\Delta_{W}=\{F\in\Delta:F\subset W\}
.
Let
\operatorname{core}(V)=\{v\in V:\operatorname{star}(v)\neq\Delta\}
and let
\operatorname{core}(\Delta)=\Delta_{\operatorname{core}(V)}
.
In the sequel, we often assume that
\Delta=\operatorname{core}(\Delta)
, which is equivalent to
\operatorname{core}(V)=V
.

We recall the definition of vertex decomposable simplicial complexes.
A simplicial complex
\Delta
on the vertex set
V
is called
vertex decomposable
(
[
1
]
) if
\Delta
is a simplex
or if there is a vertex
v\in V
, called a
shedding vertex
, such that

•

\Delta\setminus v
and
\operatorname{link}(v)
are vertex decomposable; and

•

no face of
\operatorname{link}(v)
is a facet of
\Delta\setminus v
.

It is well known that for a pure simplicial complex
\Delta
, the vertex decomposability of
\Delta
implies Cohen–Macaulayness of
\Delta
.
(Here, we say that
\Delta
is
pure
if all facets of
\Delta
have the same dimension.)

Let
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
be two simplicial complexes on disjoint vertex sets of dimension
d-1
.
Let
R_{1}\in\Delta_{1}
and
R_{2}\in\Delta_{2}
be ridges, which are faces of dimension
d-2
, respectively.
We define a new simplicial complex, called the
ridge sum of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
,
by identifying
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
in the union
\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}
of simplicial complexes.
The terminology of ridge sums was introduced in
[
15
]
and similar notions appear in
[
5
]
.

We say that a
(d-1)
-dimensional pure simplicial complex
\Delta
is
strongly connected
if for any two facets
F,F^{\prime}
of
\Delta
,
there exists a sequence of facets
F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{p}
of
\Delta
such that
F_{0}=F
,
F_{p}=F^{\prime}
and
\dim(F_{i-1}\cap F_{i})=d-2
for each
1\leq i\leq p
.

2.2.
Terminologies on Stanley–Reisner rings

We refer the reader to
[
2
]
for the introduction to the theory of Stanley–Reisner rings.

Let
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring with
n(=|V|)
variables over a field
\Bbbk
.
We denote by
I_{\Delta}
(resp.
\Bbbk[\Delta]
) the
Stanley–Reisner ideal
(resp.
Stanley–Reisner ring
) of
\Delta

over a field
\Bbbk
, i.e.,

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}x_{i_{j}}:\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{\ell}}\}\not\in\Delta\right)\subset S\text{ and }\Bbbk[\Delta]=S/I_{\Delta}.

The invariants of
\Delta
or
\Bbbk[\Delta]
or
I_{\Delta}
discussed in the present paper are listed below:

•

Let
\dim(\Bbbk[\Delta])
denote the Krull dimension of
\Bbbk[\Delta]
. Then
\dim(\Bbbk[\Delta])=\dim(\Delta)+1
.

•

We say that
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay over
\Bbbk
if
\Bbbk[\Delta]
is Cohen–Macaulay.
In the sequel, we often omit “over
\Bbbk
”. By Reisner’s criterion (
[
2
, Corollary 5.3.9]
),
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if

\widetilde{H}_{i}(\operatorname{link}(F))=0
for all
F\in\Delta
and
i<\dim(\operatorname{link}(F))
,
where
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma)
denotes the
i
-th reduced simplicial homology of a simplicial complex
\Gamma
with values in
\Bbbk
.

•

Let
\omega_{\Delta}
denote the canonical module of
\Bbbk[\Delta]
.

•

On the Tor modules, we use the notation
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(\Delta)
instead of
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk[\Delta])
.

•

Let
\beta_{ij}(\Delta)=\dim_{\Bbbk}(\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{S}(\Delta)_{i+j})
for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\dim_{\Bbbk}
stands for the dimension as a
\Bbbk
-vector space,
and let
\beta_{i}(\Delta)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta_{ij}(\Delta)
. We call
\beta_{i}(\Delta)
the
i
-th graded Betti number
of
\Bbbk[\Delta]
.
Note that by Auslander–Buchsbaum formula (see
[
2
, Theorem 1.3.3]
), we see that
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if the projective dimension of
\Bbbk[\Delta]
is
n-d
,
which is equivalent to
\beta_{i}(\Delta)=0
for any
i>n-d
and
\beta_{n-d}(\Delta)\neq 0
(see
[
2
, Corollary 1.3.2]
).

•

Let
\operatorname{reg}(\Bbbk[\Delta])
denote the
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of
\Bbbk[\Delta]
, i.e.,

\operatorname{reg}(\Bbbk[\Delta])=\max\{j-i:\beta_{ij}(\Delta)\neq 0\}
. We use the notation
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)
instead of
\operatorname{reg}(\Bbbk[\Delta])
.

•

Assume that
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay over
\Bbbk
.
Let
\operatorname{type}(\Bbbk[\Delta])
denote the
Cohen–Macaulay type
of
\Bbbk[\Delta]
. Namely,
\operatorname{type}(\Bbbk[\Delta])=\beta_{c}(\Delta)
, where
c=n-\dim(\Bbbk[\Delta])
.
Similarly to the above, we use the notation
\operatorname{type}(\Delta)
instead of
\operatorname{type}(\Bbbk[\Delta])
.

•

Moreover, when
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay over
\Bbbk
, let
a(\Bbbk[\Delta])=a(\Delta)
denote the
a
-invariant
(see
[
8
]
) of
\Bbbk[\Delta]
.
Namely, we have
a(\Delta)=-\min\{j:(\omega_{\Delta})_{j}\neq 0\}
.
It is well known that

\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)=a(\Delta)+\dim(\Bbbk[\Delta]).

•

Let
\operatorname{Indeg}(I_{\Delta})
denote the maximal degree of the minimal system of generators of
I_{\Delta}
.
Let

\operatorname{MNF}(\Delta)=\{G\subset V:G\not\in\Delta,\;G^{\prime}\in\Delta\text{ for any }G^{\prime}\subsetneq G\}.

Namely,
\operatorname{MNF}(\Delta)
is the set of
minimal non-faces
of
\Delta
. Note that
\operatorname{Indeg}(I_{\Delta})=\max\{|G|:G\in\operatorname{MNF}(\Delta)\}
.

•

We say that
\Bbbk[\Delta]
has a
p
-linear resolution
if
\beta_{ij}(\Delta)=0
unless
j-i=p
.
In particular,
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)=p
in this case.
Note that if
\Bbbk[\Delta]
has a
p
-linear resolution, then
\operatorname{Indeg}(I_{\Delta})=p+1
, but the converse is not true in general.

Remark 2.1
.

It is known that
a(\Delta)=\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)-\dim(\Bbbk[\Delta])\leq 0
(see
[
16
, Corollary B.4.1]
).
Thus, the inequality
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)\leq\dim(\Delta)+1
always holds for any Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complex
\Delta
.

Remark 2.2
.

Any Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complex
\Delta
is always strongly connected.
In fact, if a
(d-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta
is not strongly connected, then there should exist two facets
F,F^{\prime}
such that

\dim(F\cap F^{\prime})<d-2
and
\operatorname{link}(F\cap F^{\prime})
is disconnected. When
\Delta
is pure, we have
\dim(\operatorname{link}(F\cap F^{\prime}))=d-2-\dim(F\cap F^{\prime})>0
.
Hence,
\operatorname{link}(F\cap F^{\prime})
is not Cohen–Macaulay by Reisner’s criterion (see also
[
2
, Exercise 5.1.26]
).
On the other hand,
\operatorname{link}(G)
must be Cohen–Macaulay for any
G\in\Delta
if
\Delta
is Cohen–Macaulay.

2.3.
Terminologies on graphs

Throughout the present paper, we only treat finite simple graphs (i.e., a finite graph with no loops and no multiple edges), and we omit “finite simple”.

Let
G
be a graph on the vertex set
V(G)
with the edge set
E(G)
.
We call a subset
W
of
V(G)
an
independent set
if
\{v,w\}\not\in E(G)
for any
v,w\in W
.
Then we can associate an abstract simplicial complex
\Delta(G)
on
V(G)
as follows:

\Delta(G)=\{W\subset V(G):W\text{ is an independent set of }G\},

which is called an
independent complex
of
G
.

•

For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, let
N_{G}(v)=\{w\in V(G):\{v,w\}\in E(G)\}
and let
N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
.

•

For a subset
S\subset V(G)
, we denote by
G|_{S}
the subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
S
with the edge set
\{\{v,w\}:v,w\in S\}\cap E(G)
.
We use the notation
G\setminus S
instead of
G|_{V(G)\setminus S}
.

•

Let
M\subset E(G)
be a subset of the edge set. We say that
M
is an
induced matching
of
G
if
M
satisfies that

–

e\cap e^{\prime}=\emptyset
for any
e,e^{\prime}\in M
with
e\neq e^{\prime}
; and

–

there is no
e^{\prime\prime}\in E(G)
such that
e\cap e^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
e^{\prime}\cap e^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
.

Moreover, let
\operatorname{im}(G)
denote the maximal cardinality among induced matchings of
G
, called the
induced matching number
of
G
.

•

For a subset
C\subset V(G)
, we say that
C
is a
vertex cover
of
G
if
C\cap e\neq\emptyset
for any
e\in E(G)
.

Let
V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and consider the polynomial ring
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
The
edge ideal

I(G)
of a graph
G
is defined by
I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}:\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E(G))\subset S
.
Edge ideals are very well studied around the area of combinatorial commutative algebra.
We notice that the edge ideal of
G
coincides with the Stanley–Reisner ideal of
\Delta(G)
.

3.
Proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.2

The goal of this section is to give proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Proposition
1.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complex of dimension
d-1
satisfying
\Delta=\operatorname{core}(\Delta)
. Let

\displaystyle X(\Delta)=\{F:\widetilde{H}_{\dim(\operatorname{link}(F))}(\operatorname{link}(F))\neq 0\}.

We define
M(\Delta)
by setting the set of minimal faces of
X(\Delta)
with respect to inclusion.

We claim the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Work with the same notation as above. Then we have the following:

\displaystyle(1)\quad\bigcup_{F\in M(\Delta)}\operatorname{star}(F)=\Delta;

\displaystyle(2)\quad d-|F|\leq\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)\text{ for each }F\in M(\Delta);

\displaystyle(3)\quad|M(\Delta)|\leq\operatorname{type}(\Delta).

Proof.

(1) It is enough to show that
H\in\bigcup_{F\in M(\Delta)}\operatorname{star}(F)
for each facet
H
of
\Delta
.
Given any facet
H\in\Delta
, since
\operatorname{link}(H)=\{\emptyset\}
satisfies that
\dim_{\Bbbk}(\widetilde{H}_{-1}(\operatorname{link}(H))=1
,
we see that
H\in X(\Delta)
. Therefore,
H\in\operatorname{star}(F)
for some
F\in M(\Delta)
.

(2) By
[
2
, Exercise 5.6.6]
, we see that

\displaystyle(\omega_{\Delta})_{j}\neq 0\;\Longrightarrow\;j\geq|F|\text{ for }F\in X(\Delta).

(3.1)

Hence, for each
F\in M(\Delta)
. we have

-a(\Delta)=\min\{j:(\omega_{\Delta})_{j}\neq 0\}\leq|F|.

Therefore, we obtain that
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)=a(\Delta)+d\geq d-|F|
.

(3) We see from (
3.1
) that the elements of
X(\Delta)
correspond to the non-vanishing squarefree
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-degree components of
\omega_{\Delta}
,
where
n
is the number of vertices of
\Delta
. Since
\operatorname{type}(\Delta)
is equal to the number of minimal generators of
\omega_{\Delta}
and each
F
in
M(\Delta)
should correspond to a minimal generator of
\omega_{\Delta}
, we obtain that
|M(\Delta)|\leq\operatorname{type}(\Delta)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By our assumption
\Delta=\operatorname{core}(\Delta)
, there are facets
H,H^{\prime}
of
\Delta
with
H\cap H^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
Moreover, the Cohen–Macaulayness of
\Delta
implies that
\Delta
is strongly connected (see Remark
2.2
).
Hence, there exists a sequence of facets
F_{1},\ldots,F_{p}
such that

F_{1}=H
,
F_{p}=H^{\prime}
and
\dim(F_{i}\cap F_{i+1})=d-2
for each
1\leq i\leq p-1
.
By Lemma
3.1
(1), there exists a sequence of elements
S_{1},\ldots,S_{q}\in M(\Delta)
such that

F_{i_{j-1}+1},\ldots,F_{i_{j}}\in\operatorname{star}(S_{j})
for each
1\leq j\leq q
, where
1=i_{0}<i_{1}<\cdots<i_{q}=p
.
Then we may assume that

\operatorname{star}(S_{i})
and
\operatorname{star}(S_{i+1})
contain a common facet for each
i
.

(3.2)

In fact, by choice of a sequence
S_{1},\ldots,S_{q}
,
\operatorname{star}(S_{i})
and
\operatorname{star}(S_{i+1})
contain a common face
H
with
\dim(H)=d-2
.
Since there are at least two facets containing
H
, i.e.,
\operatorname{link}(H)
consists of at least two vertices, we have
H\in X(\Delta)
.
Hence, there is
T\in M(\Delta)
with
T\subset H
. If either
T=S_{i}
or
T=S_{i+1}
holds,
then we see that
\operatorname{star}(S_{i})
and
\operatorname{star}(S_{i+1})
contain a common facet. Even if
T\neq S_{i}
and
T\neq S_{i+1}
,
we may add
T
between
S_{i}
and
S_{i+1}
, i.e., we replace the sequence by
S_{1},\ldots,S_{i},T,S_{i+1},\ldots,S_{q}
.
Then
\operatorname{star}(S_{i})
and
\operatorname{star}(T)
contain a common facet, and so do
\operatorname{star}(T)
and
\operatorname{star}(S_{i+1})
.

Since we may also assume that
S_{1},\ldots,S_{q}
are all distinct,
we can take a required sequence
S_{1},\ldots,S_{q}
satisfying (
3.2
).
Then we can see that

\displaystyle d-\left|\bigcap_{i=1}^{j}S_{i}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j}(d-|S_{i}|)\text{ for each }1\leq j\leq q

(3.3)

by induction. The case
j=1
is trivial. Let
j>1
and assume that (
3.3
) holds for
j-1
.
Take a common facet
G
of
\operatorname{star}(S_{j-1})
and
\operatorname{star}(S_{j})
.
Then we have
|(\bigcap_{i=1}^{j-1}S_{i})\cup S_{j}|\leq|S_{j-1}\cup S_{j}|\leq|G|=d
.
Thus, by the hypothesis of induction, we conclude that

\displaystyle d-\left|\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{j-1}S_{i}\right)\cap S_{j}\right|

\displaystyle=d-\left(\left|\bigcap_{i=1}^{j-1}S_{i}\right|+|S_{j}|-\left|\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{j-1}S_{i}\right)\cup S_{j}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}(d-|S_{i}|)-|S_{j}|+d=\sum_{i=1}^{j}(d-|S_{i}|).

Here, we have that
S_{1}\subset H
and
S_{q}\subset H^{\prime}
. Hence,
S_{1}\cap S_{q}\subset H\cap H^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
In particular,
\bigcap_{i=1}^{q}S_{i}=\emptyset
holds. Therefore, by applying (
3.3
) with
j=q
, we obtain that

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=d-\left|\bigcap_{i=1}^{q}S_{i}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}(d-|S_{i}|)\leq\max\{d-|S_{i}|:1\leq i\leq q\}\cdot|M(\Delta)|

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)\cdot\operatorname{type}(\Delta)

by Lemma
3.1
(2) and (3), as desired.
∎

Next, we prove Proposition
1.2
.
Here, we know that for a simplicial complex
\Delta
,
\operatorname{Indeg}(I_{\Delta})=2
holds if and only if there is a graph
G
such that
\Delta=\Delta(G)
.
Since
\operatorname{Indeg}(I)=2
holds if
S/I
has a
1
-linear resolution and

contains no linear polynomial,
we may discuss the edge ideals of graphs in the case (1).

The following theorem is used for the proof of Proposition
1.2
(1).

Theorem 3.2

(
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices all of which are non-isolated, and let
d-1=\dim(\Delta(G))
.
Suppose that
\Delta(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay. Then we have
n-d\geq d
.

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

(1) Let
n
be the number of vertices of
G
and let
c=n-d
, where
d-1=\dim(\Delta(G))
.
Here, Betti numbers of homogeneous Cohen–Macaulay
\Bbbk
-algebras with
p
-linear resolutions over the polynomial ring
are completely determined by Eisenbud–Goto (
[
6
, Proposition 1.7 (c)]
) and
we know that
\operatorname{type}(\Delta)=\beta_{c}(\Delta)=\binom{c+p-1}{p}
for a Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complex
whose Stanley–Reisner ring has a
p
-linear resolution. From this, we obtain that
\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G))=c
.
Since
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(G))=1
, it follows from Proposition
3.2
that
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(G))+\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G))-1=c\geq d
, as required.

(2) Let
\Delta
be a Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complex of dimension
d-1
satisfying
\Delta=\operatorname{core}(\Delta)
.
If
a(\Delta)=0
, since
a(\Delta)=\operatorname{reg}(\Delta)-\dim(\Bbbk[\Delta])
, the inequality (
1.2
) trivially holds.
Note that if
\Delta
is Gorenstein and
\Delta=\operatorname{core}(\Delta)
, then
a(\Delta)=0
(see
[
2
, Exercise 5.6.8]
).
Thus, the inequality (
1.2
) holds, as required.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

The goal of this section is to construct examples of simplicial complexes
\Delta
such that
the triple
(\dim(\Delta),\operatorname{reg}(\Delta),\operatorname{type}(\Delta))
satisfies the required inequalities.
We construct such simplicial complexes as independent complexes.

Before the construction of examples,
let us recall two constructions of graphs from a given graph
G
;
whiskered graph
W(G)
and
S
-suspension
G^{S}
.
Let
G
be a graph on the vertex set
V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
with the edge set
E(G)
.

4.1.
Whiskered graphs

We define the
whiskered graph
of
G
, denoted by
W(G)
, by setting

\begin{split}V(W(G))=V(G)\sqcup\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{d}\}\;\text{ and }\;E(W(G))=E(G)\sqcup\{\{v_{i},w_{i}\}:i=1,\ldots,d\},\end{split}

(4.1)

where
w_{1},\ldots,w_{d}
are new vertices.

Lemma 4.1

(cf.
[
3
]
,
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

For any graph
G
with
d
vertices,
\Delta(W(G))
is pure and vertex decomposable.
In particular,
\Delta(W(G))
is a Cohen–Macaulay simplicial complex of dimension
d-1
.

4.2.
S
-suspensions

Given an independent set
S
of
G
, we define the
S
-suspension
of
G
, denoted by
G^{S}
, by setting

V(G^{S})=V(G)\sqcup\{w\}\text{ and }E(G^{S})=E(G)\sqcup\{\{v,w\}:v\not\in S\},

where
w
is a new vertex.
Note that
S\sqcup\{w\}
becomes an independent set of
G^{S}
. Hence, in the language of the independent complex,

\Delta(G^{S})
is the ridge sum of
\Delta(G)
and a new simplex
S\sqcup\{w\}
along the ridge
S
.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
11
, Lemma 1.2]
,
[
5
, Theorem 2.9]
,
[
12
, Lemma 1.5]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
S
be an independent set of
G
.

(1)

Assume that
\Delta(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay. If
|S|=\dim(\Delta(G))
, then
\Delta(G^{S})
is also Cohen–Macaulay of the same dimension as
\Delta(G)
.

(2)

Assume that
|S|=\dim(\Delta(G))
. Then
\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G^{S}))=\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G))+1
.

(3)

Assume that
G
has no isolated vertex. Then
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(G^{S}))=\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(G))
.

Proof.

The statement (1) (resp. (3)) is a direct consequence of
[
11
, Lemma 1.2]
(resp.
[
12
, Lemma 1.5]
).
The statement (2) follows from
[
5
, Theorem 2.9]
.
∎

4.3.
Whiskered graphs of complete multi-partite graphs

For the construction of our desired graph, we consider the whiskered graphs of complete multi-partite graphs,
which play the essential role in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}
be integers with
r_{1}\geq\cdots\geq r_{t}\geq 1
. Consider
G=W(K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}})
. Then

\dim(\Delta(G))=\sum_{i=1}^{t}r_{i}-1,\;\;\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(G))=r_{1}\;\text{ and }\;\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G))=t.

For the proof of Proposition
4.3
, we recall a result from
[
4
]
.
Let
G
be a graph on the vertex set
V(G)
with
2d
vertices all of which are non-isolated vertices. We consider the condition that

\begin{split}&V(G)=X\sqcup Y,\;\text{where }\\
&\text{$X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}$ is a minimal vertex cover of $G$, and }\\
&\text{$Y=\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}\}$ is a maximal independent set of $G$, such that }\\
&\{\{x_{i},y_{i}\}:i=1,\ldots,n\}\subset E(G).\end{split}

(4.2)

Consider the graph satisfying (
4.2
). Let
E_{i}=\{k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:\{x_{k},y_{i}\}\in E(G)\}\setminus\{i\}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
,
and let
O_{[d]}(G)
be the graph on the vertex set
V(O_{[d]}(G))=V(G)
with the edge set

\displaystyle E(O_{[d]}(G))=\left(E(G)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{d}\{\{x_{k},y_{i}\}:k\in E_{i}\}\right)\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{d}\{\{x_{k},x_{i}\}:k\in E_{i}\}.

Proposition 4.4

(
[
4
, Corollary 4.4]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with
2d
vertices all of which are non-isolated vertices and let
\dim(\Delta(G))=d-1
.
Assume that
\Delta(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay and
G
satisfies (
4.2
). Then

\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G))=\upsilon(O_{[n]}(G)|_{X}),

where
\upsilon(H)
denotes the number of minimal vertex covers of a graph
H
.

Before giving the proof of Proposition
4.3
, we fix the notation on
K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}}
.
Let
V_{i}=\{x_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,x_{r_{i}}^{(i)}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,t
and let
V(K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}})=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{t}V_{i}
.
Let
G=W(K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}})
, let
V(G)=\{x^{(i)}_{j},y^{(i)}_{j}:1\leq i\leq t,1\leq j\leq r_{i}\}
, and let

\displaystyle E(G)=E(K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}})\sqcup\{\{x_{j}^{(i)},y_{j}^{(i)}\}:1\leq i\leq t,1\leq j\leq r_{i}\}.

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

First,
\dim(\Delta(G))=\sum_{i=1}^{t}r_{i}-1
directly follows from Lemma
4.1
.

Next, we show that
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(G))=r_{1}
.
Here, it follows from
[
14
, Theorem 2.4]
that
\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(H))=\operatorname{im}(H)

if a graph
H
contains no induced cycle of length
5
and
\Delta(H)
is vertex decomposable.
By the structure of
G
, we see that
G
contains no induced cycle of length
5
.
Moreover, Lemma
4.1
implies that
\Delta(G)
is vertex decomposable.
Since we see that
\{\{x_{j}^{(i)},y_{j}^{(i)}\}:j=1,\ldots,r_{1}\}
forms an induced matching of
G
for each
i
and those are maximal ones,
we conclude that
\operatorname{im}(G)=r_{1}
.

Finally, we show that
\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G))=t
. Here, we can check that
G
satisfies (
4.2
)
by setting
X=V(K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}})
and
Y=V(G)\setminus X
. Under this setting, we see that
O_{[d]}(G)=E(K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}})
,
i.e., all vertices in
Y
become isolated in
O_{[d]}(G)
.
Hence, we may count the number of minimal vertex covers of
K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}}
. Let
V=V(K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}})
.
Then
C\subset V
is a minimal vertex cover if and only if
C=V\setminus V_{i}
for some
i
.
In fact, if there are
i
and
i^{\prime}
with
i\neq i^{\prime}
such that
V_{i}\setminus C\neq\emptyset
and
V_{i^{\prime}}\setminus C\neq\emptyset
,
since we consider a complete multi-partite graph, there must be an edge between
V_{i}\setminus C
and
V_{i^{\prime}}\setminus C
, a contradiction.
Therefore, one has
\upsilon(O_{[d]}(G|_{X}))=\upsilon(O_{[d]}(K_{r_{1},\ldots,r_{t}}))=t
, so we conclude that
\operatorname{type}(\Delta(G))=t
by Proposition
4.4
, as required.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
d,r,t
be integers with
r,t\geq 2
and
r\leq d\leq rt
.

Write

, where
0\leq q<r

(resp.

) is the quotient (resp. the remainder) of

divided by

.
Note that we have

and

Let
G=W(K_{\underbrace{r,\ldots,r}_{p},q})
if

and let
G=W(K_{\underbrace{r,\ldots,r}_{p}})
if

.
Note that
|V(K_{\underbrace{r,\ldots,r}_{p},q})|=pr+q=d
.
Thus, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that
\Delta(G)
is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension

.
Moreover, by Proposition
4.3
, we know that

\operatorname{reg}(\Delta(G))=r\text{ and }\operatorname{type}(\Delta(H))=p+1.

If

∎

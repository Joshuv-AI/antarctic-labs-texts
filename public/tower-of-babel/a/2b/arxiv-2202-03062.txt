Perfect State Transfer on semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups
Majid Arezoomand

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the problem on the existence of perfect state transfer (PST for short) on semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups (which are not necessarily regular), i.e on the graphs having semiregular and abelian subgroups of automorphisms with two orbits of equal size. We stablish a characterization of semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups having PST. As a result, we give a characterization of Cayley graphs over groups with an abelian subgroup of index 2 2 having PST, which improves the earlier results on Cayley graphs over abelian groups, dihedral groups and dicyclic group and determines Cayley graphs over generalized dihedral groups and generalized dicyclic groups having PST.



Keywords: Perfect state transfer, Semi-Cayley graph, Eigenvalue of a graph

Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C25, 81P45, 15A18.

Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a simple undirected graph with adjacency matrix
A
. The continuous-time quantum walk on
\Gamma
is defined through the time-dependent unitary matrix

\displaystyle H(t)=\exp(-{\bf i}tA)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-{\bf i})^{k}t^{k}A^{k}}{k!},~~~0<t\in\mathbb{R},~{\bf i}=\sqrt{-1}.

This concept first introduced by Farhi and Gutman by a motivation of the Schrödinger equation. They used it as
a paradigm to design efficient quantum algorithms
[
9
]
.

The significance of the study of quantum state transfer lies in its applications to the theory
of Quantum Information and Computation. In fact, one of the cruical ingredients in most of quantum information
processing protocols is the transfer of a quantum state from one location to another location. Quantum spin network is an example of
physical systems that can serve as a quantum channel. By considering the networks as graphs, in algebraic graph theory, one of the main questions is to find a characterization of graphs having perfect state transfer. We say that a graph
\Gamma
has a
perfect state transfer
(PST for short) from the vertex
u
to the vertex
v
at time
t
if the

(u,v)
-entry of
H(t)
, denoted by
H_{u,v}(t)
, has absolute value
1
. If
|H_{u,u}(t)|=1
then we say that
\Gamma
is periodic
at
u
with period
t
.
\Gamma
is called periodic if it is periodic at all vertices with period
t
.

Recently, the problem of characterization of graphs having PST has been one of the interesting topics in algebraic graph theory. For a survey on this topic and its related question, up to 2011, we refer the reader to
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
Godsil has showed that if perfect state transfer
occurs in a graph, then the square of its spectral radius is either an integer or lies in a quadratic
extension of the rationales
[
12
]
. Moreover, existence of PST has been obtained for some
particular classes of graphs, for example, distance-regular graphs
[
7
,
8
]
,
Hadamard diagonalizable graphs
[
15
]
and complete bipartite
graphs
[
17
]
. Also some authors studied the PST problem of Cayley graphs due to their nice algebraic structures. A characterization of Cayley graphs over abelian group, dihedral groups with conjugate-closed Cayley set, dihedral groups with non-conjugate-closed Cayley set, dicyclic groups and also over semidihedral groups are given in
[
18
,
6
,
5
,
3
,
16
]
, respectively.
Despite many results on PST on graphs, there are no known constructions of PST on
non-abelian Cayley graphs in literature.

The purpose of this paper is two-fold: First we present an explicit general method to find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups which facilitates the computations of PST. Many families of well-known graphs are examples of semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups, such as bicirculants (including I-graphs, the generalized Petersen graphs, rose window graphs, Tabačjn graphs,
n
-gonal
n
-cone graphs),
n
-sunlet graphs, and the join graph of two Cayley graphs over the same group, also all Cayley graphs over groups having a subgroup of index
2
. So we provide a way to characterize
a large family of graphs having PST.
Secondly, a vital
step for making further progress on existence of PST on Cayley graphs over non-abelian groups. Since any Cayley graph over a group

G
having subgroup
H
of index
2
is a semi-Cayley graph over
H
, the main results of
[
18
,
6
,
5
,
3
,
16
]
are direct consequences of our results.
Also as a result, we investigate the existence of PST on Cayley graphs over generalized dihedral groups and generalized dicyclic groups.

The rest of the current work is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present some comments and notations used in the next sections. In Section
3
, we give exact formula for the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups. Finally,the existence of PST on semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups is explored in Section
4
and as a result we improve some earlier results. Also, we characterize all Cayley graphs over generalized dihedral groups and generalized dicyclic groups having PST.

2
Preliminaries and notations

Here we recall some of the standard facts and notation used in this paper. Our notation for
representations and characters of finite groups is due to the notation introduced in
[
14
]
. Throughout this paper, graphs are finite, simple and undirected, and also groups are
finite.

2.1
Spectrum of a graph and PST

Let
\Gamma
be an undirected graph with adjacency matrix
A
and
H(t)=\exp(-{\bf i}tA)
. The eigenvalues of
A
are called
(adjacency) eigenvalues
of
\Gamma
.
\Gamma
is called
integral
if all of its eigenvalues are integers.
Let
\Gamma
has
m
vertices and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{m}
be the eigenvalues of
\Gamma
,
v_{i}

is a column eigenvector of
\lambda_{i}
and
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Then, since
A
is
a real symmetric matrix, by the Spectral Decomposition Theorem
[
19
, Theorem 3.4]
,

H(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\exp(-{\bf i}t\lambda_{i})E_{i},

where
E_{i}=v_{i}.v_{i}^{*}
, where
v_{i}^{*}
is the conjugate transpose of
v_{i}
. So
\Gamma
has a PST from the vertex
u
to the vertex
v
if and only if the the absolute value of the
(u,v)
-entry of
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\exp(-{\bf i}t\lambda_{i})E_{i}
is equal to
1
.

2.2
Cayley graphs and semi-Cayley graphs

Given a group
G
and an inverse-closed subset of
G
, the Cayley graph of
G
with respect to the Cayley set
S
, denoted by
{\rm Cay}(G,S)
, is a graph
with vertex set
G
and edge-set
\{\{g,sg\}\mid s\in S\}
. Clearly
{\rm Cay}(G,S)
is a vertex-transitive
|S|
-regular graph.

As a generalization of Cayley graphs, see
[
1
, page 2]
, semi-Cayley graphs are introduced. Let
G
be a finite group,
R,L
and
S
be subsets of
G
such that
R
and
L
are inverse-closed subsets not containing the identity element of
G
. The
semi-Cayley graph
over
G
with respect to
R
,
L
and
S
, denoted by
{\rm SC}(G,R,L,S)
is
an undirected graph with vertex set
\{(g,0),(g,1)\mid g\in G\}
and edge set consists of three sets:

\displaystyle\{\{(x,0),(y,0)\}\mid yx^{-1}\in R\}\ \ \ (\textrm{right edges}),

\displaystyle\{\{(x,1),(y,1)\}\mid yx^{-1}\in L\}\ \ \ (\textrm{left edges}),

\displaystyle\{\{(x,0),(y,1)\}\mid yx^{-1}\in S\}\ \ \ (\textrm{spoke edges}).

Clearly
{\rm SC}(G,R,L,S)
is a regular graph if and only if
|R|=|L|
. By the following result, every Cayley graph over a group having a subgroup of index
2
is a semi-Cayley graph. Note that the converse is not true, since as an example the Petersen graph is a semi-Cayley graph over the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
and it not a Cayley graph. We will use this fact frequently without referring it.

Lemma 2.1
.

(see
[
1
, Lemma 8]
)
Let
\Gamma={\rm Cay}(G,T)
, and
H
be a subgroup of
G
of index
2
with left transversal
\{1,x\}
. Then
\Gamma\cong{\rm SC}(H,R,L,S)
, where
R=H\cap T
,
L=H\cap xTx^{-1}
and
S=H\cap xT
.

2.3
Irreducible characters of finite abelian groups

In this paper, we need irreducible characters of finite abelian groups. For the definitions and basic facts about representations and characters of finite groups, not defined here, we refer the reader to
[
14
]
. Let
G
be an abelian
group of order
n
. It is well-known that every finite abelian group is a direct product of cyclic groups. Let
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
be
a cyclic group of order
r
. Then we may assume that
G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\langle g_{2}\rangle\times\ldots\times\langle g_{k}\rangle

where
k\geq 1
and
n=n_{1}n_{2}\ldots n_{k}
and
\langle g_{l}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{n_{l}}
. Let
{\rm Irr}(G)
be the set of all inequivalent complex irreducible characters of
G
. Then, by
[
14
, Theorem 9.8]
,

{\rm Irr}(G)=\{\chi_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}\mid 0\leq j_{l}\leq n_{l},l=1,\ldots,k\}
, where

\chi_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C},~~~(g_{1}^{i_{1}},\ldots,g_{k}^{i_{k}})\mapsto\exp(\frac{2\pi{\bf i}j_{1}i_{1}}{n_{1}})\ldots\exp(\frac{2\pi{\bf i}j_{k}i_{k}}{n_{k}}).

The character
\chi_{0,\ldots,0}
is the trivial character and in the rest of paper, we denote it by
\chi_{1}
. It is clear
that for every
g\in G
and
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
, we have
|\chi(g)|=1
. For a character of
G
and a subset
X
of
G
, we denote

\sum_{x\in X}\chi(x)
with
\chi(X)
and if
X=\emptyset
then we put
\chi(X)=0
.

3
Spectra of semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups

Let
\Gamma={\rm SC}(G,R,L,S)
be a semi-Cayley graph over an abelian group
G
. Let
G=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}
and

{\rm Irr}(G)=\{\chi_{1}=1,\ldots,\chi_{n}\}

be the set of all inequivalent irreducible characters of
G
. For
1\leq i\leq n
, we define the matrix

\displaystyle A_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\chi_{i}(R)&\chi_{i}(S)\\
\overline{\chi_{i}(S)}&\chi_{i}(L)\end{bmatrix},

where
\overline{\chi_{i}(S)}
is the complex conjugate of
\chi_{i}(S)
. Note that since
R
and
L
are inverse-closed,
\chi_{i}(R)
and
\chi_{i}(L)
are real numbers by
[
14
, Proposition 13.9]
. Then eigenvalues of
A_{i}
are

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\chi_{i}(R)+\chi_{i}(L)+\sqrt{(\chi_{i}(R)-\chi_{i}(L))^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}\big),

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{-}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\chi_{i}(R)+\chi_{i}(L)-\sqrt{(\chi_{i}(R)-\chi_{i}(L))^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}\big).

Let
u_{i}^{+}=(a_{i}^{+},b_{i}^{+})
and
u_{i}^{-}=(a_{i}^{-},b_{i}^{-})
be an eigenvector of
A_{i}
corresponding to
\lambda_{i}^{+}
and
\lambda_{i}^{-}
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle b_{i}^{+}\chi_{i}(S)=\frac{-x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}}{2}a_{i}^{+},~~~a_{i}^{+}\overline{\chi_{i}(S)}=\frac{x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}}{2}b_{i}^{+},

(1)

and

\displaystyle b_{i}^{-}\chi_{i}(S)=\frac{-x_{i}-\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}}{2}a_{i}^{-},~~~a_{i}^{-}\overline{\chi_{i}(S)}=\frac{x_{i}-\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}}{2}b_{i}^{-}

(2)

where
x_{i}=\chi_{i}(R)-\chi_{i}(L)\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\chi_{i}(S)=0
, then we may assume that
\lambda_{i}^{+}=\chi_{i}(R)
,
\lambda_{i}^{-}=\chi_{i}(L)
,
(a_{i}^{+},b_{i}^{+})=(1,0)
and
(a_{i}^{-},b_{i}^{-})=(0,1)
. Let
c_{i}^{+}=\frac{|a_{i}^{+}|^{2}}{|a_{i}^{+}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{+}|^{2}}
,
c_{i}^{-}=\frac{|a_{i}^{-}|^{2}}{|a_{i}^{-}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{-}|^{2}}
,
d_{i}^{+}=\frac{|b_{i}^{+}|^{2}}{|a_{i}^{+}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{+}|^{2}}
,
d_{i}^{-}=\frac{|b_{i}^{-}|^{2}}{|a_{i}^{-}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{-}|^{2}}
,
e_{i}^{+}=\frac{a_{i}^{+}\overline{b_{i}^{+}}}{|a_{i}^{+}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{+}|^{2}}
and
e_{i}^{-}=\frac{a_{i}^{-}\overline{b_{i}^{-}}}{|a_{i}^{-}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{-}|^{2}}
. If
\chi_{i}(S)\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle c_{i}^{+}=\frac{|x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}|^{2}}{|x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}|^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}},

\displaystyle c_{i}^{-}=\frac{|x_{i}-\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}|^{2}}{|x_{i}-\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}|^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}},

\displaystyle d_{i}^{+}=\frac{4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}{|x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}|^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}},

\displaystyle d_{i}^{-}=\frac{4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}{|x_{i}-\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}|^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}},

\displaystyle e_{i}^{+}=2\chi_{i}(S)\frac{x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}}{|x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}|^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}},

\displaystyle e_{i}^{-}=-2\chi_{i}(S)\frac{x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}}{|x_{i}+\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}}|^{2}+4|\chi_{i}(S)|^{2}},.

Furthermore, by
[
1
, Lemma 11]
,

v_{i}^{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n(|a_{i}^{+}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{+}|^{2})}}(a_{i}^{+}\chi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1}),\ldots,a_{i}^{+}\chi_{i}(g_{n}^{-1}),b_{i}^{+}\chi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1}),\ldots,b_{i}^{+}\chi_{i}(g_{n}^{-1}))^{T}

and

v_{i}^{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n(|a_{i}^{-}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{-}|^{2})}}(a_{i}^{-}\chi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1}),\ldots,a_{i}^{-}\chi_{i}(g_{n}^{-1}),b_{i}^{-}\chi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1}),\ldots,b_{i}^{-}\chi_{i}(g_{n}^{-1}))^{T}

, where
x^{T}
is the transpose of vector
x
, are eigenvectors of
\Gamma
corresponding to
\lambda_{i}^{+}
and
\lambda_{i}^{-}
, respectively, and
\{v_{i}^{+},v_{i}^{-}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
. Let
E_{i}^{+}=v_{i}^{+}.(v_{i}^{+})^{*}
and
E_{i}^{-}=v_{i}^{-}.(v_{i}^{-})^{*}
. Then we have

\displaystyle E_{i}^{+}=\frac{1}{n(|a_{i}^{+}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{+}|^{2})}\begin{bmatrix}|a_{i}^{+}|^{2}B&a_{i}^{+}\overline{b_{i}^{+}}B\\
b_{i}^{+}\overline{a_{i}^{+}}B&|b_{i}^{+}|^{2}B\end{bmatrix},~~~~E_{i}^{-}=\frac{1}{n(|a_{i}^{-}|^{2}+|b_{i}^{-}|^{2})}\begin{bmatrix}|a_{i}^{-}|^{2}B&a_{i}^{-}\overline{b_{i}^{-}}B\\
b_{i}^{-}\overline{a_{i}^{-}}B&|b_{i}^{-}|^{2}B\end{bmatrix},

where

. We keep these notations throughout the paper.

4
PST on semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups

Let us first give some relations between PST on Cayley graphs and semi-Cayley graphs.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma={\rm SC}(G,R,L,\emptyset)
. Then
\Gamma
has a PST from
(g,r)
to
(h,s)
if and only if
r=s=0
and
{\rm Cay}(G,R)
has a PST from
g
to
h
, or
r=s=1
and
{\rm Cay}(G,L)
has a PST from
g
to
h
.

Proof.
Let
A
,
B
and
C
be the adjacency matrices of
\Gamma
,
{\rm Cay}(G,R)
and
{\rm Cay}(G,L)
, respectively. Then

A=\begin{bmatrix}B&0\\
0&C\end{bmatrix}
, which implies that
H_{A}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}H_{B}(t)&0\\
0&H_{C}(t)\end{bmatrix}
. Now the result is straightforward.

\hfill\blacksquare

The proof of the following lemma is similar to
[
4
, Theorem 1]
. We give it for completeness.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X={\rm Cay}(G,R)
and
\Gamma={\rm SC}(G,R,R,S)
, where
G
is an abelian group. Let
A
,
B
and
C
be the adjacency matrices of
\Gamma

X
and
{\rm Cay}(G,S)

H_{A}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}H_{B}(t)D_{1}&CD_{2}\\
C^{T}D_{2}&H_{B}(t)D_{1}\end{bmatrix},

where
D_{1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-{\bf i}t)^{2k}}{(2k)!}(CC^{T})^{k}=\cos(t\sqrt{CC^{T}})
and
D_{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-{\bf i}t)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}(CC^{T})^{k}=-{\bf i}(CC^{T})^{\frac{-1}{2}}\sin(t\sqrt{CC^{T}})
where the second equality for
D_{2}
holds if

\det(C)\neq 0
. Furthermore, if

has a PST from
g
to

at time
t

(1)

\Gamma

(g,0)
to
(h,0)

(g,1)

(h,1)

(2)

\Gamma

(g,0)
to
(h,1)

t\in(2\mathbb{Z}+1)\frac{\pi}{2}
and
S=\{1\}

Proof.
Suppose that
G=\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{n}\}
and consider the ordering

(g_{1},0),\ldots,(g_{n},0),(g_{1},1),\ldots,(g_{n},1)

for the vertices of
\Gamma
. Then
A=I_{2}\otimes B+(P\otimes C+Q\otimes C^{T})
, where

is the

and

1&0\end{bmatrix}
. Since
G

CC^{T}=C^{T}C
,

and

. Also we have
P^{2}=Q^{2}=0_{2}

, where

(I_{2}\otimes B)D=P\otimes(BC)+Q\otimes(BC^{T})=P\otimes(CB)+Q\otimes(C^{T}B)=D(I_{2}\otimes B),

This implies that

H_{A}(t)=\exp(-{\bf i}tA)=\begin{bmatrix}\exp(-{\bf i}tB)D_{1}&CD_{2}\\
C^{T}D_{2}&\exp(-{\bf i}tB)D_{1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}H_{B}(t)D_{1}&CD_{2}\\
C^{T}D_{2}&H_{B}(t)D_{1}\end{bmatrix},

where
D_{1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-{\bf i}t)^{2k}}{(2k)!}(CC^{T})^{k}=\cos(t\sqrt{CC^{T}})

D_{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-{\bf i}t)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}(CC^{T})^{k}=-{\bf i}(CC^{T})^{\frac{-1}{2}}\sin(t\sqrt{CC^{T}})
where the second equality for
D_{2}
holds if

\det(C)\neq 0
.

\hfill\blacksquare

Now we are ready to give a characterization of
{\rm SC}(G,R,L,S)
having PST, where
G
is abelian and

. For this purpose, we need notation of the

-adic exponential valuation of rational numbers which is a mapping defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\psi_{2}:\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\},\\
&\psi_{2}(0)=\infty,\\
&\psi_{2}(2^{l}\frac{a}{b})=l,~~~~where~a,b,l\in\mathbb{Z}~and~2\not|ab.\\
\end{split}

We assume that

and

has the following properties. For

(2)

and the equality holds if
\psi_{2}(\beta)\neq\psi_{2}(\beta^{\prime}).

Theorem 1

r=1,s=0
then for each
j
we have
\chi_{j}(a)=\frac{|\chi_{j}(S)|}{\chi_{j}(S)}\exp(-{\bf i}(\lambda_{1}^{+}-\lambda_{j}^{+})t)
or
\chi_{j}(a)=\frac{|\chi_{j}(S)|}{\overline{\chi_{j}(S)}}\exp(-{\bf i}(\lambda_{1}^{+}-\lambda_{j}^{+})t)
, respectively. Since the complex conjugate of any irreducible character of
G
is an irreducible character of
G
and the corresponding
eigenvalues of an irreducible character and its complex conjugate are the same, since
R
is inverse-closed, we conclude that in both cases
\exp(-{\bf i}(\lambda_{1}^{+}-\lambda_{j}^{+})t)
is a real number, which means that it is
1
or
-1
. Hence
(\lambda_{1}^{+}-\lambda_{j}^{+})T\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}
. Since
T\in\mathbb{Q}
and
\lambda_{1}^{+}=|R|+|S|\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\lambda_{j}^{+}\in Q
. This means that
\Gamma
is integral and
\chi_{j}(R)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
j
. The converse direction is clear by Lemma
4.4
.

\hfill\blacksquare

Let us recall generalized dihedral and generalized dicyclic groups as follows: Given an abelian group

Since
{\rm SC}(G,R,L,G)
is the join graph of
{\rm Cay}(G,R)
and
{\rm Cay}(G,L)
, the following result gives a characterization of the join of two Cayley graphs over the same abelian group.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\Gamma={\rm SC}(G,R,L,G)
, where
G
is an abelian group of order
n
, and
{\rm Irr}(G)=\{\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{n}\}
. Then
\Gamma
has a PST between vertices
u=(g,r)
and
v=(h,s)
at time
t
if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

g=h,r=s=0
,
(\lambda_{1}^{+}-\chi_{i}(R))t\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
for all
i
and

t\sqrt{(|R|-|L|)^{2}+4n^{2}}\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

g=h,r=s=1
,
(\lambda_{1}^{-}-\chi_{i}(L))t\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
for all
i
and

t\sqrt{(|R|-|L|)^{2}+4n^{2}}\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

g\neq h,r=s=0
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\exp({\bf i}\lambda_{i}^{+}t)=0
and
t\sqrt{(|R|-|L|)^{2}+4n^{2}}\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
.

(4)

g\neq h,r=s=1
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\exp({\bf i}\lambda_{i}^{-}t)=0
and

t\sqrt{(|R|-|L|)^{2}+4n^{2}}\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
,

where
\lambda_{1}^{+}=\frac{|R|+|L|+|\sqrt{(|R|-|L|)^{2}+4n^{2}}}{2}
,
\lambda_{1}^{-}=\frac{|R|+|L|-|\sqrt{(|R|-|L|)^{2}+4n^{2}}}{2}

,
\lambda_{i}^{+}=\chi_{i}(R)
and
\lambda_{i}^{-}=\chi_{i}(L)
,
i=2,\ldots,n
are eigenavlues of
\Gamma
.

Proof.
We have
X=\{i\mid\chi_{i}(S)=0\}=\{2,\ldots,n\}
since
\chi(G)=0
for all
1\neq\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
. This menas that for all
i\neq 1
, we may assume that
\lambda_{i}^{+}=\chi_{i}(R)
and
\lambda_{i}^{-}=\chi_{i}(L)
. Then, by Lemma
4.4
and Theorem
2
,
\Gamma
has a PST from
u=(g,r)
to
v=(h,s)
if and only if
r=s=0
,
\chi_{i}(a)=\exp(-{\bf i}(\lambda_{1}^{+}-\lambda_{i}^{+})t)
for all
i
and
t\sqrt{(|R|-|L|)^{2}+4n^{2}}\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
or
r=s=1
,
\chi_{i}(a)=\exp(-{\bf i}(\lambda_{1}^{-}-\lambda_{i}^{-})t)
for all
i
and
t\sqrt{(|R|-|L|)^{2}+4n^{2}}\in\pi\mathbb{Z}
where
a=g^{-1}h
. Putting
a=1
, we get
the first and second parts. Now let
a\neq 1
. Then by the column orthogonality relations
[
14
, Theorem 16.4]
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(a)=0
, which proves the last two parts.

\hfill\blacksquare

Example 4.10

has no PST by Corollary
4.7

is even, by considering the character

4.4
implies that
\Gamma
has no PST and is not periodic.

Example 4.11
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
\Gamma
be the
n
-gonal
n
-cone, the join of an
n
-cycle with the empty graph with
n

Then
\Gamma={\rm SC}(G,R,L,S)
, where
G=\langle a\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{n}
,
R=\{a,a^{-1}\}
,
L=\emptyset
and

. Then,
by Corllary
4.9
,
\lambda_{1}^{+}=1+\sqrt{1+n^{2}}
,
\lambda_{1}^{-}=1-\sqrt{1+n^{2}}

\lambda_{i}^{+}=2Re(\chi_{i}(a))=2\cos(\frac{2\pi i}{n})
and
\lambda_{i}^{-}=0
. If
n
is odd, then
\Gamma
has no PST by Corollary
4.7
.
Let
n=2m
. Consider the irreducible character
\chi_{m}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with the rule
\chi_{m}(a^{k})=(-1)^{k}
and let

\Gamma
has a PST from
(g,r)
to
(h,s)
. Then Corollary
4.9
implies that
g=h,r=s=0

occurs if

Example 4.12
.

\Gamma={\rm Cay}(G,T)

5
Concluding remarks

The perfect state transfer (PST) in quantum walks, where the underlying graphs are semi-Cayley graphs over abelian groups were studied. We derive the spectral decomposition of any semi-Cayley graph over an abelian group
G
in terms of the irreducible characters of
G
. Since every Cayley graph over a group
G
having an abelian subgroup
H
is a semi-Cayley graph over
H
,
our method for investigating of PST can be applied to any Cayley graph over a finite group having an abelian subgroup of index
2
. One can apply our method to the quasi-abelian semi-Cayley graphs.

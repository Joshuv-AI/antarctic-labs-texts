Abstract
. We investigate the bounded composition operators induced by linear fractional self-maps of the right half-plane ℂ + \mathbb{C}_{+} on the Hardy space H 2 ​ ( ℂ + ) . H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}). 1.
Introduction

All along this paper,
\mathbb{C}
denotes the complex plane,
\mathbb{U}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
is the unit disk and
\mathbb{C}_{+}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}(z)>0\}
is the right half-plane.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{S}
be a space of functions defined on
\Omega.
A composition operator
C_{\phi}
on
\mathcal{S}
is an operator acting by composition to the right with a chosen
self-map
\phi
of
\Omega
, i.e.,

\displaystyle C_{\phi}f=f\circ\phi,\quad f\in\mathcal{S}.

The self-map
\phi
is called the
symbol
of the composition operator
C_{\phi}.

It is well-known that if
\Omega=\mathbb{U}
and
\phi
is an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{U}
then
C_{\phi}
is bounded on the Hardy space of the unit disk
H^{2}(\mathbb{U})
(see
[
1
]
).
If
\Omega=\mathbb{C}_{+},
Elliot and Jury established a boundedness criterion for composition operators on the Hardy space of the right half-plane
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
in terms of angular derivative (see
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
). As a consequence of this caracterization, we have that the only linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{C}_{+}

inducing bounded composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
are those of the following form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\phi(w)=aw+b,\quad\text{where}\ a>0\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0.

Let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
denote the space of all bounded linear operators on a separable complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}.
A
conjugation
on
\mathcal{H}
is a conjugate-linear
operator satisfying
C^{2}=I
and
\left\langle Cx,Cy\right\rangle=\left\langle y,x\right\rangle
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
. An operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is called
cohyponormal
if
\left\|Tx\right\|\leq\left\|T^{*}x\right\|
for all
x\in\mathcal{H},

normal
if
TT^{*}=T^{*}T,

self-adjoint
if
T=T^{*},

unitary
if
TT^{*}=I=T^{*}T
and
complex symmetric
(or
C
-symmetric
) if there is a conjugation
C
on
\mathcal{H},
for which
CT^{*}C=T.
In
[
6
]
, Noor and Severiano studied the composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
induced by linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{C}_{+}.
They completely characterize the symbols that induce complex symmetric composition operators (see next theorem) and provide a new prove to characterize normal, self-adjoint and unitary composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Theorem 1.1
.

[
6
, Theorems 2 and 6]
Let
\phi
be as in (
1.1
). Then

(1)

C_{\phi}
is normal on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
a=1
or
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.

(2)

C_{\phi}
is self-adjoint on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
a=1
and
b\geq 0.

(3)

C_{\phi}
is unitary on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
a=1
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.

(4)

C_{\phi}
is complex symmetric on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
C_{\phi}
is normal on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Noor and Severiano
[
6
]
study the complex symmetry of composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. The key of this study is to analyze the cyclic behavior of the bounded composition operators induced by linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
, which allowed them to characterize the operators that are complex symmetric. Despite this characterization, they did not exhibit the conjugations for the cases that these operators are complex symmetric.

In the present paper, we investigate the bounded composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
induced by linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{C}_{+}.
In Theorem
3.1
, we completely characterize the cohyponormal composition operators induced by linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{C}_{+}.

As a consequence of this characterization we obtain a new proof of
[
6
, Theorem 6]
.
For complex symmetry of composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),
our main results are Theorems
4.1
and
4.5
, which provide concrete examples of conjugations for which linear fractional operators are complex symmetric.

2.
preliminaries

2.1.
The Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})

The Hardy space of the right half-plane is the normed space

H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}):=\left\{f\in H(\mathbb{C}_{+}):\left\|f\right\|=\left(\sup_{0<x<\infty}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x+iy)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}<\infty\right\},

and the norm
\left\|\;\cdot\;\right\|
is induced by an inner product, which makes
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
a Hilbert space. For each
u\in\mathbb{C}_{+},
the function

\displaystyle K_{u}(w)=\frac{1}{\overline{u}+w},\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}

is called
reproducing kernel
for
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
at
u.
This kernel function has the fundamental property:

\langle f,K_{u}\rangle=f(u)\textrm{ for each }f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

If
\phi
is an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that
C_{\phi}
is bounded, then a simple computation gives

(2.1)

\displaystyle C_{\phi}^{*}K_{u}=K_{\phi(u)}

for each
u\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Recall that the classical Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{U})
of the unit disk is given by

H^{2}(\mathbb{U}):=\left\{f\in H(\mathbb{U}):\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}=\left(\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta\right)^{1/2}<\infty\right\}.

The space
H^{2}(\mathbb{U})
is a Hilbert space when endowed with the norm
\left\|\;\cdot\>\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{U}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{+}
be the mapping defined by
z\mapsto(1-z)/(1+z),
then
\gamma
is a self-inverse bijection. The operator
V:H^{2}(\mathbb{U})\longrightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left(Vf\right)(w)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+w}f\left(\frac{1-w}{1+w}\right),\quad f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{U}),w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}

is an isometric isomorphism whose inverse
V^{-1}:H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\longrightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{U})
is given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left(V^{-1}g\right)(z)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+z}g\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right),\quad g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),z\in\mathbb{U}

see
[
4
, p. 489]
.

2.2.
Linear fractional composition operators

We say that a bounded composition operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is a
linear fractional composition operator
if its inducing symbol has the form

(2.4)

\displaystyle\phi(w)=aw+b,\ \text{where}\ a>0\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0.

The map
\phi
is classified according to the value of
a:

•

\phi
is
parabolic
if
a=1
(and necessarily
b\neq 0
) and it is a

parabolic automorphism
if additionally
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.

•

\phi
is
hyperbolic
if
a\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
and it is a
hyperbolic automorphism
if additionally
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.

In general, there is not a formula for adjoints of compositions operators. However, when the operator is a linear fractional composition operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, then a such formula exists (see
[
6
, Proposition 1]
) as follows:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\phi
be as in (
2.4
). Then the adjoint of
C_{\phi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is given by

\displaystyle C_{\phi}^{*}=a^{-1}C_{\psi},

where
\psi(w)=a^{-1}w+a^{-1}\overline{b}.

2.3.
Complex symmetry

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
be separable Hilbert spaces. In
[
3
, page 1291]
, Garcia and Putinar showed that if
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is
C
-symmetric, then there is a natural way to construct complex symmetric operators on
\mathcal{H}_{1},
just consider
T_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
with
T_{1}=U^{-1}TU
for some isometric isomorphism
U:\mathcal{H}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}.
We present this fact in the following result, which allow us directly conclude whether a given mapping is a conjugation or a complex symmetric operator:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
be separable Hilbert spaces. If
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is
C
-symmetric and
T_{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
with
T_{1}=U^{-1}TU
for some isometric isomorphism
U:\mathcal{H}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
, then

(a)

U_{1}^{-1}CU_{1}
is a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}_{1},
for any isometric isomorphism
U_{1}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}.

(b)

T_{1}
is
U^{-1}CU
-symmetric.

3.
Cohyponormality

In this section, we completely characterize the linear fractional composition operators that are cohyponormal on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
Since
C_{\phi}
is cohyponormal, when
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a=1
or
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0
(Theorem
1.1
), then it is enough to consider the case
a\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)>0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\phi
be the self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
defined by
\phi(w)=aw+b.

(a)

If
a\in(0,1)
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)>0,
then
C_{\phi}
is not cohyponormal.

(b)

If
a\in(1,\infty)
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)>0,
then
C_{\phi}
is cohyponormal.

Proof.

(a) First observe that
u=b(1-a)^{-1}
is a fixed point of
\phi
in
\mathbb{C}_{+}.
By (
2.1
), it follows that
K_{u}
is an eigenvector for
C_{\phi}^{*}.
Now, suppose that
C_{\phi}
is cohyponormal. Since
C_{\phi}
is cohyponormal,
\mathrm{Ker}(C_{\phi}^{*}-\overline{\lambda}I)\subset\mathrm{Ker}(C_{\phi}-\lambda I)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.
Thus
K_{u}
is an eigenvector for
C_{\phi}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
1.
Then a simple computation gives

\phi(w)=w
for all
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+},
which implies

a=1
and
b=0.
This contradiction shows that
C_{\phi}
is not cohyponormal.

(b) Let
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and
a\in(1,\infty).
Considering the change of variables
u=ax+\mathrm{Re}(b)
and
v=ay+\mathrm{Im}(b),
we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\|C_{\phi}f\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<x<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(ax+\mathrm{Re}(b)+i(ay+\mathrm{Im}(b))|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathrm{Re}(b)<u<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a}|f(u+iv)|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{a^{-1}\mathrm{Re}(b)<u<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a}|f(u+iv)|^{2}dv

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{a^{-1}\mathrm{Re}(b)<u<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(u+iv)|^{2}dv.

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.1
we have
C_{\phi}^{*}f=a^{-1}C_{\psi}f.
Thus the change of variables
r=a^{-1}x+a^{-1}\mathrm{Re}(b)
and
s=a^{-1}y-a^{-1}\mathrm{Im}(b)
gives

(3.2)

\displaystyle\left\|C_{\phi}^{*}f\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=a^{-1}\sup_{0<x<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(a^{-1}(x+iy)+a^{-1}\overline{b}|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=a^{-1}\sup_{0<x<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(a^{-1}x+a^{-1}\mathrm{Re}(b)+i(a^{-1}y-a^{-1}\mathrm{Im}(b))|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=a^{-1}\sup_{a^{-1}\mathrm{Re}(b)<r<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}a|f(r+is)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\sup_{a^{-1}\mathrm{Re}(b)<r<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(r+is)|^{2}ds.

From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we have
\left\|C_{\phi}f\right\|\leq\|C_{\phi}^{*}f\|,
for all
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

∎

Combining Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\phi
be the self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
defined by
\phi(w)=aw+b.

Then
C_{\phi}
is cohyponormal on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
a\geq 1
or
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.

Corollary 3.3
.

For
a>0
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0,
let
\phi
be the self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
defined by
\phi(w)=aw+b.
Then
C_{\phi}
is complex symmetric on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
if and only if
a=1
or
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.

Proof.

Suppose that
C_{\phi}
is complex symmetric. If
a\in(0,1),
then by Theorem
3.1

aC_{\phi}^{*}=C_{\psi}
is cohyponormal, where
\psi(w)=a^{-1}w+a^{-1}\overline{b}.
Since
C_{\psi}
is simultaneously cohyponormal and complex symmetric, then
C_{\psi}
is normal. By Theorem
1.1
, we obtain
a^{-1}=1
or
\mathrm{Re}(a^{-1}\overline{b})=0.
Since
a\in(0,1),
we have
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.
Similarly, if
a\geq 1
then
C_{\phi}
is simultaneously cohyponormal and complex symmetric, and hence
C_{\phi}
is normal. Therefore,
a=1
or
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.
Conversely, since normal operators are complex symmetric, it follows from Theorem
1.1
that
C_{\phi}
is complex symmetric whenever
a=1
or
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.

∎

4.
Complex Symmetry

In this section, we deal with the problem of exhibit conjugations for the linear fractional composition operators that are complex symmetric.

4.1.
Parabolic self-maps of
\mathbb{C}_{+}

Here we explicit a concrete conjugation
C
for which the compositions operators induced by parabolic symbols are
C
-symmetric.

Let
J:H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\longrightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
be the mapping defined by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left(Jf\right)(w)=\overline{f\left(\overline{w}\right)},\quad f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

It is easy to check that
J
is a conjugation.

Next result shows that the composition operators induced by parabolic symbols are
J
-symmetric.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
Then
C_{\phi}
is
J
-symmetric if and only if
\phi
is a parabolic symbol.

Proof.

Suppose that
C_{\phi}
is
J
-symmetric. Then
JC_{\phi}^{*}K_{u}=C_{\phi}JK_{u}
for each
u\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.
Since
JK_{u}=K_{\overline{u}},
it follows that

(4.2)

\displaystyle K_{\overline{u}}(\phi(w))=K_{\overline{\phi(u)}}(w),\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

If
u=1
in (
4.2
), then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\phi(w)}=\frac{1}{\phi(1)+w},

and hence
\phi(w)=w+\overline{\phi(1)}-1,
for all
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Conversely, suppose that
\phi
is a parabolic symbol, that is
\phi(w)=w+b
with
\mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0.
Then
(C_{\phi}^{*}f)(w)=f(w+\overline{b})
, for each
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.
Hence

\displaystyle\left(JC_{\phi}Jf\right)(w)=\overline{C_{\phi}Jf(\overline{w})}=\overline{Jf(\phi(\overline{w}))}=f(w+\overline{b})=(C_{\phi}^{*}f)(w),

which shows that
C_{\phi}
is
J
-symmetric. ∎

4.2.
Hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{C}_{+}

By Theorem
1.1
, among the hyperbolic symbols only the automorphisms induce complex symmetric composition operators. Here, we exhibit a concrete conjugation
C
for which these operators are
C
-symmetric.

Narayan
et al.

[
5
]
showed that if
c\in(-1,1),

\Psi_{c}(z):=\frac{\sqrt{1-|c|^{2}}}{1-cz},\Phi_{c}(z):=\frac{c-z}{1-cz}

and
J_{\mathbb{U}}
is the conjugation defined on
H^{2}(\mathbb{U})
by
(J_{\mathbb{U}}f)(z)=\overline{f(\overline{z})}
then
J_{\mathbb{U}}T_{\Psi_{c}}C_{\Phi_{c}}
is a conjugation on
H^{2}(\mathbb{U}).
By Lemma
2.2
, the mapping

(4.3)

\displaystyle W_{c}:=VJ_{\mathbb{U}}T_{\Psi_{c}}C_{\Phi_{c}}V^{-1}

is a conjugation on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
In the following result we determine an expression for
W_{c}
when
c=0.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
c=0
in (
4.3
) then

(4.4)

\displaystyle(W_{0}f)(w)=\frac{1}{w}\overline{f\left(\frac{1}{\overline{w}}\right)},\quad f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Proof.

If
c=0
then
\Psi_{0}\equiv 1
and
\Phi_{0}(z)=-z
for each
z\in\mathbb{U}.
Hence

T_{\Psi_{0}}C_{\Phi_{0}}=C_{\Phi_{0}}.
Now observe that for each
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+},
we have

\displaystyle(W_{0}f)(w)

\displaystyle=(VJ_{\mathbb{U}}C_{\Phi_{0}}V^{-1}f)(w)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+w}J_{\mathbb{U}}C_{\Phi_{0}}V^{-1}f\left(\gamma^{-1}(w)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+w}\overline{C_{\Phi_{0}}V^{-1}f\left(\overline{\gamma^{-1}(w)}\right)}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+w}\overline{V^{-1}f\left(-\overline{\gamma^{-1}(w)}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+w}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1-\gamma^{-1}(w)}\overline{f\left(\gamma(-\overline{\gamma^{-1}(w)})\right)}.

Since

\displaystyle 1-\gamma^{-1}(w)=1-\frac{1-w}{1+w}=\frac{1+w-1+w}{1+w}=\frac{2w}{1+w}

and

\displaystyle\tau(-\overline{\gamma^{-1}(w)})=\frac{1+\overline{\gamma^{-1}(w)}}{1-\overline{\gamma^{-1}(w)}}=\frac{1+\frac{1-\overline{w}}{1+\overline{w}}}{1-\frac{1-\overline{w}}{1+\overline{w}}}=\frac{1+\overline{w}+1-\overline{w}}{1+\overline{w}-1+\overline{w}}=\frac{1}{\overline{w}}

we obtain

\displaystyle(W_{0}f)(w)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1+w}\frac{\sqrt{2}(1+w)}{2w}\overline{f\left(\frac{1}{\overline{w}}\right)}=\frac{1}{w}\overline{f\left(\frac{1}{\overline{w}}\right)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the next proposition, we completely describe the
W_{0}
-symmetric operators
C_{\phi}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
Then
C_{\phi}
is
W_{0}
-symmetric if and only if
\phi(w)=aw
with
a>0.

Proof.

Suppose that
C_{\phi}
is
W_{0}
-symmetric. Thus
W_{0}C_{\phi}^{*}K_{u}=C_{\phi}W_{0}K_{u}
for each
u\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.
Since
(W_{0}K_{u})(w)=\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)\overline{K_{u}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{w}}\right)},
we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)\overline{K_{\phi(u)}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{w}}\right)}=\left(\frac{1}{\phi(w)}\right)\overline{K_{u}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{\phi(w)}}\right)},\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

If
u=1
in (
4.5
), then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(1)w+1}=\frac{1}{\phi(w)+1}

and hence
\phi(w)=\phi(1)w.
Since
C_{\phi}
is bounded,
\phi(1)>0
(see (
1.1
)). Conversely, suppose that
\phi(w)=aw
for some
a>0.
If
\psi(w)=a^{-1}w
then
C_{\phi}^{*}=a^{-1}C_{\psi}
(see Proposition
2.1
). Hence, for each
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
we obtain

\displaystyle\left(W_{0}C_{\phi}W_{0}f\right)(w)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)\overline{\left(C_{\phi}W_{0}f\right)\left(\frac{1}{\overline{w}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)\overline{W_{0}f\left(\frac{a}{\overline{w}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{w}\right)\overline{\left(\frac{\overline{w}}{a}\right)\overline{f\left(\frac{w}{a}\right)}}

\displaystyle=\left(C_{\phi}^{*}f\right)(w).

Hence
W_{0}C_{\phi}W_{0}=C_{\phi}^{*}.

∎

Let
\phi
be a hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{C}_{+},
that is,
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.
A simple computation shows that
\phi
can be written as

(4.6)

\displaystyle\phi=\tau\circ\varphi\circ\tau^{-1},

where
\varphi(w)=aw
and
\tau(w)=w+(a-1)^{-1}b.
In particular, (
4.6
) implies

C_{\phi}=C_{\tau}^{-1}C_{\varphi}C_{\tau}.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)=0.
If
\tau(w)=w+(a-1)^{-1}b
and
W_{a,b}:=C_{\tau}^{-1}W_{0}C_{\tau},
then

(a)

W_{a,b}
is a conjugation.

(b)

C_{\phi}
is
W_{a,b}
-symmetric on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Proof.

(a) Since
C_{\tau}
is unitary and
W_{0}
is a conjugation, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that
W_{a,b}=C_{\tau}^{-1}W_{0}C_{\tau}
is a conjugation.

(b) By Theorem
4.3
, if
\varphi(w)=aw
then
C_{\varphi}
is
W_{0}
-symmetric. Hence

\displaystyle W_{a,b}C_{\phi}W_{a,b}

\displaystyle=C_{\tau}^{-1}W_{0}C_{\tau}(C_{\tau}^{-1}C_{\varphi}C_{\tau})C_{\tau}^{-1}W_{0}C_{\tau}

\displaystyle=C_{\tau}^{-1}(W_{0}C_{\varphi}W_{0})C_{\tau}

\displaystyle=C_{\tau}^{-1}C_{\varphi}^{*}C_{\tau}

\displaystyle=C_{\tau}^{-1}(a^{-1}C_{\varphi^{-1}})C_{\tau}

\displaystyle=a^{-1}C_{\psi},

where
\psi=(\tau\circ\varphi\circ\tau^{-1})^{-1}.
A straightforward computation gives
\psi(w)=a^{-1}w+a^{-1}\overline{b}.
Since, by Proposition
2.1
,

C_{\phi}^{*}=a^{-1}C_{\psi}
, we get
W_{a,b}C_{\phi}W_{a,b}=C_{\phi}^{*}.

∎

Since
\tau(w)=w+ir
with
r\in\mathbb{R}
induces a unitary operator, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that
J_{r}:=C_{\tau}^{-1}W_{0}C_{\tau}
is a conjugation on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
Using this notation,
W_{a,b}
becomes
J_{(a-1)^{-1}\mathrm{Im}(b)}.
Now we completely characterize the composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
that are
J_{r}
-symmetric.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\phi
be an analytic self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
such that
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
Then
C_{\phi}
is
J_{r}
-symmetric for some
r\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if
\phi
is a hyperbolic automorphism.

Proof.

Supoose that
C_{\phi}
is
J_{r}
-symmetric. Then
W_{0}C_{\tau}C_{\phi}C_{\tau}^{-1}W_{0}=(C_{\tau}C_{\phi}C_{\tau}^{-1})^{*},
which shows that the composition operator
C_{\tau^{-1}\circ\phi\circ\tau}=C_{\tau}C_{\phi}C_{\tau}^{-1}
is
W_{0}
-symmetric. By Theorem
4.3
, there is
a>0
such that
(\tau^{-1}\circ\phi\circ\tau)(w)=aw
for each
w\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.
Now a simple computation shows that
\phi(w)=aw+i(1-a)r.
Conversely, in view of the Corollary
4.4
, each hyperbolic automorphism is
J_{(a-1)^{-1}\mathrm{Im}(b)}
-symmetric for convenient
a
and
b.

∎

5.
Adjoint

Inspired by Proposition
2.1
we provide a new formula for adjoints of composition operators induced by linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{C}^{+}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\phi(w)=aw+b
with
a>0
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0.
If
\varphi(w)=a^{-1}w
and
J
is the conjugation defined in (
4.1
), then
U_{a}:=a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi}J
is an isometric conjugate-linear operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
and

(5.1)

\displaystyle U_{a}C_{\phi}U_{a}=C_{\phi}^{*}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
U_{a}
is a conjugate-linear operator. By Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle(a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi})^{*}=a^{-1/2}aC_{\varphi^{-1}}=a^{1/2}C_{\varphi^{-1}},

moreover

a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi}a^{1/2}C_{\varphi^{-1}}=I=a^{1/2}C_{\varphi^{-1}}a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi}.

These equalities imply that
(a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi})^{*}=(a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi})^{-1},
and hence
a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi}
is unitary. Now observe that if
f,g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{+})
then

\displaystyle\left\langle U_{a}f,U_{a}g\right\rangle=\langle(a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi})Jf,(a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi})Jg\rangle=\left\langle Jf,Jg\right\rangle=\left\langle g,f\right\rangle

which implies that
U_{a}
is an isometry. To show (
5.1
), first note that for each
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{+}),
we have

\displaystyle U_{a}C_{\phi}U_{a}f(w)

\displaystyle=a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi}JC_{\phi}a^{-1/2}C_{\varphi}Jf(w)=a^{-1}JC_{\phi}C_{\varphi}Jf(a^{-1}w)

\displaystyle=\overline{a^{-1}C_{\phi}C_{\varphi}Jf(a^{-1}\overline{w})}=\overline{a^{-1}C_{\varphi}Jf(a(a^{-1}\overline{w})+b)}

\displaystyle=\overline{a^{-1}Jf(a^{-1}\overline{w}+a^{-1}b)}=a^{-1}f(a^{-1}w+a^{-1}\overline{b}).

By Proposition
2.1
, we conclude that
U_{a}C_{\phi}U_{a}=C_{\phi}^{*}
.
∎

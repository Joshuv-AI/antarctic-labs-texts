High order numerical methods based on quadratic spline collocation method and averaged L1 scheme for the variable-order time fractional mobile/immobile diffusion equation
Xiao Ye, Jun Liu, Bingyin Zhang, Hongfei Fu, Yue Liu

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the variable-order time fractional mobile/immobile diffusion (TF-MID) equation in two-dimensional spatial domain, where the fractional order α ⁡ ( t ) \alpha(t) satisfies 0 < α ∗ ≤ α ⁡ ( t ) ≤ α ∗ < 1 0<\alpha_{*}\leq\alpha(t)\leq\alpha^{*}<1 . We combine the quadratic spline collocation (QSC) method and the L ​ 1 + L1^{+} formula to propose a QSC- L ​ 1 + L1^{+} scheme. It can be proved that, the QSC- L ​ 1 + L1^{+} scheme is unconditionally stable and convergent with 𝒪 ⁡ ( τ min ⁡ { 3 − α ∗ − α ⁡ ( 0 ) , 2 } + Δ ​ x 2 + Δ ​ y 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\tau^{\min{\{3-\alpha^{*}-\alpha(0),2\}}}+\Delta x^{2}+\Delta y^{2}) , where τ \tau , Δ ​ x \Delta x and Δ ​ y \Delta y are the temporal and spatial step sizes, respectively. With some proper assumptions on α ⁡ ( t ) \alpha(t) , the QSC- L ​ 1 + L1^{+} scheme has second temporal convergence order even on the uniform mesh, without any restrictions on the solution of the equation. We further construct a novel alternating direction implicit (ADI) framework to develop an ADI-QSC- L ​ 1 + L1^{+} scheme, which has the same unconditionally stability and convergence orders. In addition, a fast implementation for the ADI-QSC- L ​ 1 + L1^{+} scheme based on the exponential-sum-approximation (ESA) technique is proposed. Moreover, we also introduce the optimal QSC method to improve the spatial convergence to fourth-order. Numerical experiments are attached to support the theoretical analysis, and to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed schemes.

Introduction

Over the past several decades, the fractional partial differential equations (FPDEs) have attracted more and more attention as a tool for

diffusion processes and viscoelastic behavior and so on

Recent studies showed that the variable order fractional PDEs are more powerful tools for modeling many multiphysics phenomena, where

As an important class of variable-order FPDEs, the variable-order time fractional mobile/immobile diffusion (TF-MID) equation
describes the transport characteristics of particle in the fluid, and provides a more realistic model for
solute diffusion transport in heterogeneous porous media
[
39
,
44
]

In recent years, various numerical methods for FPDEs have been proposed, such as

Since the QSC method employs smoother basis functions, and needs less degrees of freedom than some classical methods for the same number of grid points, then the QSC method results in algebraic systems of relatively smaller scale. The QSC method, as well as its optimal version, have been widely applied for various kinds of integer-order PDEs

,

,

(2.2)

u(x,y,t)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt(x,y,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T],

(2.3)

[0,T]

\displaystyle 0<\alpha_{*}\leq\alpha(t)\leq\alpha^{*}<1,\ t\in[0,T],\ \ \lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}(\alpha(t)-\alpha(0))\ln t\text{ exists }.

The variable-order Caputo fractional

\displaystyle{}^{C}_{0}D_{t}^{\alpha(t)}u(x,y,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{1-\alpha(t)}(t-s)\partial_{s}u(x,y,s)ds,

\displaystyle\omega_{1-\beta}(t):=\frac{t^{-\beta}}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}.

The QSC method for space discretization

2.3

We average model (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}u_{t}(x,y,t)dt+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha(t)}u(x,y,t)dt=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\kappa\mathcal{L}u(x,y,t)dt+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}f(x,y,t)dt.

(2.18)

It can be verified for the first term on the left hand side of (
2.18

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}u_{t}(x,y,t)dt=\frac{u^{n}(x,y)-u^{n-1}(x,y)}{\tau}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t}u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y).

(2.19)

For the first term on right hand side of (
2.18

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\kappa\mathcal{L}u(x,y,t)dt=\kappa\mathcal{L}u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)+r_{3,n},

(2.20)

where

\displaystyle r_{3,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\frac{\kappa}{2}\mathcal{L}\big[u\left(x,y,t\right)-u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\frac{\kappa}{2}\mathcal{L}\big[u\left(x,y,t\right)-\Pi u(x,y,t)\big]=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\frac{\kappa}{2}\mathcal{L}\theta u\left(x,y,t\right),

and satisfies

\displaystyle|r_{3,n}|\leq\frac{\kappa}{2\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}|\mathcal{L}\theta u\left(x,y,t\right)|dt\leq C_{3}\tau\left(t_{n}^{1-\alpha(0)}-t_{n-1}^{1-\alpha(0)}\right)

Similarly, for the second term on the right hand side of (
2.18

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}f(x,y,t)dt=f^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)+r_{4,n},

\displaystyle r_{4,n}=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\frac{1}{2}f_{tt}\left(x,y,\rho_{1}\right)\left(t-t_{n}\right)\left(t-t_{n-1}\right)dt,\hskip 10.00002pt\rho_{1}\in(t_{n-1},t_{n}),

\displaystyle|r_{4,n}|\leq\frac{C_{4}}{2\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left(t-t_{n}\right)\left(t-t_{n-1}\right)dt=\mathcal{O}\left(\tau^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t}u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)+\bar{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t}}^{\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)=\kappa\mathcal{L}u^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)+f^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)+R^{n},

In order to find numerical solution of model (
2.1

with the form (

\displaystyle=\kappa\sum_{i=0}^{M_{x}+1}\sum_{j=0}^{M_{y}+1}c_{ij}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\left[\phi_{i}^{\prime\prime}(x)\phi_{j}(y)+\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(y)\right]+f^{n-\frac{1}{2}}(x,y),\hskip 10.00002pt(x,y)\in\Omega,\ 1\leq n\leq N,

with the initial condition

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{M_{x}+1}\sum_{j=0}^{M_{y}+1}c_{ij}^{0}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(y)=u^{0}(x,y),\hskip 10.00002pt(x,y)\in\bar{\Omega},

(2.25)

and the boundary condition

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{M_{x}+1}\sum_{j=0}^{M_{y}+1}c_{ij}^{n}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(y)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt(x,y)\in\partial\Omega,\ 1\leq n\leq N.

for

3
Numerical analysis of the QSC-
L1^{+}
scheme

Before the numerical analysis, we need some definitions of the inner products and norms.
Define
\mathcal{M}_{h}=\big\{u,\ u=\{u_{i,j},\ (i,j)\in\bar{\Lambda}\}\big\}
as the spatial grid function space with respect to the partition
\triangle
,
and
\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{h}=\{u\in\mathcal{M}_{h},\ u_{ij}=0\ \text{for}\ (i,j)\in\partial{\Lambda}\}
.
For any
u,v\in\mathring{\mathcal{M}}_{h}
, we define the discrete inner product

\displaystyle(u,v):=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i=0}^{M_{x}+1}\sum_{j=0}^{M_{y}+1}u_{ij}v_{ij},\hskip 10.00002pt\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{x}u,\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{x}v\right):=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i=1}^{M_{x}+1}\sum_{j=0}^{M_{y}+1}\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{x}u_{ij}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{x}v_{ij}\right),

\displaystyle\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{y}u,\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{y}v\right):=\Delta x\Delta y\sum_{i=0}^{M_{x}+1}\sum_{j=1}^{M_{y}+1}\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{y}u_{ij}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{y}v_{ij}\right).

Thus, the corresponding discrete norms can be obtained as

\displaystyle\|u\|:=\sqrt{\left(u,u\right)},\hskip 10.00002pt\left|u\right|_{1x}:=\sqrt{\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{x}u,\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{x}u\right)}\ ,\hskip 10.00002pt\left|u\right|_{1y}:=\sqrt{\left(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{y}u,\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{y}u\right)}\ .

Recalling that

\displaystyle\bar{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{t}}^{\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}v^{n-\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{n-k+1}^{(n)}\left(v^{k}-v^{k-1}\right),

we can reformulate scheme (
2.27
) as

\displaystyle(1+\tau a_{1}^{(n)})\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}c_{ij}^{n}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}c_{ij}^{n-1}\right)+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{n-k+1}^{(n)}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}c_{ij}^{k}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}c_{ij}^{k-1}\right)=\tau\kappa(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{y}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x})c_{ij}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\tau f_{ij}^{n-\frac{1}{2}},

(3.1)

for
(i,j)\in\bar{\Lambda}
and
1\leq n\leq N
. For convenience, we define the coefficients of (
3.1
) uniformly as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &b_{1}^{(n)}=1+\tau a_{1}^{(n)},\\
&b_{n-k+1}^{(n)}=\tau a_{n-k+1}^{(n)},\quad k=1,\ 2,\ \cdots,\ n-1,\end{aligned}\right.

then the QSC-
L1^{+}
scheme (
2.27
) can be further rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{n-k+1}^{(n)}\left(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}c_{ij}^{k}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}c_{ij}^{k-1}\right)=\tau\kappa\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{y}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\right)c_{ij}^{n-\frac{1}{2}}+\tau f_{ij}^{n-\frac{1}{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt(i,j)\in\bar{\Lambda},\ 1\leq n\leq N,

(3.2)

Based on Lemma
2.2
, the new coefficients
\left\{b_{n-k+1}^{(n)},k=1,2,\cdots,n\right\}
in scheme (
3.2
) satisfy the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

At time instant
t=t_{n}
, if
\tau\leq 1
, the coefficients
\left\{b_{n-k+1}^{(n)},k=1,2,\cdots,n\right\}
in scheme (
3.2
) satisfy

\displaystyle b_{1}^{(n)}>b_{2}^{(n)}>\cdots>b_{n}^{(n)}>0.

Proof.

According to the definition of
a_{n-k+1}^{(n)}
, we have

\displaystyle a_{1}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)dsdt=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\omega_{3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(\tau)=\frac{\tau^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)},

and

\displaystyle a_{2}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-2}}^{t_{n-1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)dsdt=\frac{\tau^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}\left(2^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}-2\right).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle b_{1}^{(n)}-b_{2}^{(n)}=1+\tau(a_{1}^{(n)}-a_{2}^{(n)})=1+\frac{\tau^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}\left(3-2^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\right).

When
\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\in\left[2-\frac{ln3}{ln2},1\right)
, we can get
3-2^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\geq 0
, which verifies that
b_{1}^{(n)}>b_{2}^{(n)}
.
When
\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\in\left(0,2-\frac{ln3}{ln2}\right)
, we have
-1<3-2^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}<0
. Since
\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)\geq 1
, we
have
\frac{\tau^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}\leq 1
, with
\tau\leq 1
, which leads to
b_{1}^{(n)}>b_{2}^{(n)}
.
The monotonicity of series
\big\{b_{k}^{(n)},\ k=2,3,\cdots,n\big\}
can be found in Lemma
2.2
.
\blacksquare

Lemma
3.1
means that all the coefficients of the QSC-
L1^{+}
scheme (
3.2
) are monotonic, and this property plays an important role in the following numerical analysis.

3.1

To proceed with the analysis of stability, we need some auxiliary lemmas. We first investigate some properties of the coefficients in the QSC-

Lemma 3.2

Suppose that
\alpha^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
, and
\alpha^{\prime}(t)
is uniformly bounded for
0\leq t\leq T
, then for any fixed
n
with
2\leq n\leq N
, we have

b_{n-k}^{(n)}\leq\left(1+C_{5}\tau\right)b_{n-k}^{(n-1)},\hskip 10.00002ptk=1,\cdots,n-1,

where
C_{5}
is a positive constant.

Proof.

The proof is generally divided into two parts. In the first part, we consider the case
k=1,\cdots,n-2
, and in the second part, we consider the case
k=n-1
.

(I) For
k=1,\cdots,n-2
, we have
t_{k+1}\leq t_{n-1}
, and

\displaystyle b_{n-k}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\tau a_{n-k}^{(n)}=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)dsdt

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\left[\omega_{3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-t_{k}\right)-\omega_{3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-t_{k}\right)-\omega_{3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-t_{k+1}\right)+\omega_{3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-t_{k+1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\tau\Gamma(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n})}\left[(n-k)^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}-2(n-k-1)^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}+(n-k-2)^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\right].

Then the quotient of
b_{n-k}^{(n)}
and
b_{n-k}^{(n-1)}
can be simplified as

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-k}^{(n)}}{b_{n-k}^{(n-1)}}=\cdot\frac{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}\right)}{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}\cdot\frac{t_{n-k}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}-2t_{n-k-1}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}+t_{n-k-2}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{t_{n-k}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}-2t_{n-k-1}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}+t_{n-k-2}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}}:=A^{(n)}\cdot B^{(n)}.

We first investigate the quantity
A^{(n)}
. Notice the fact that
0<\alpha(t)\leq\alpha^{*}<1
, and
\Gamma(x)
is a increasing and
differentiable function on the interval
[3-\alpha^{*},3]
. Denote
\Gamma_{*}=\min_{3-\alpha^{*}\leq s\leq 3}\left|\Gamma(s)\right|

and
\Gamma_{*}^{\prime}=\max_{3-\alpha^{*}\leq s\leq 3}|\Gamma^{\prime}(s)|
. Using Taylor expansion, we have

\displaystyle A^{(n)}=\frac{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)+\Gamma^{\prime}\left(\xi_{n}\right)\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}\right)}{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}=1+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}\left(\xi_{n}\right)}{\Gamma\left(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}\cdot\alpha^{\prime}(\eta_{n})\cdot\tau\leq 1+\tau\left\|\alpha^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\cdot\frac{\Gamma_{*}^{\prime}}{\Gamma_{*}}\leq 1+C_{6}\tau,

where
\xi_{n}\in(3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1},3-\tilde{\alpha}_{n})
and
\eta_{n}\in(t_{n-1},t_{n})
.
Next, for the quantity
B^{(n)}
, it can be verified that

\displaystyle B^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Big[t_{n-k}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}-t_{n-k-1}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\Big]-\left[t_{n-k-1}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}-t_{n-k-2}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\right]}{\Big[t_{n-k}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}-t_{n-k-1}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}\Big]-\Big[t_{n-k-1}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}-t_{n-k-2}^{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}\Big]}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)\left(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}}{{\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}\right)\left(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}\right)}}\cdot\frac{\int_{t_{n-k-1}}^{t_{n-k}}\int_{x-\tau}^{x}s^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}dsdx}{\int_{t_{n-k-1}}^{t_{n-k}}\int_{x-\tau}^{x}s^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}dsdx}:=B_{1}^{(n)}\cdot B_{2}^{(n)}.

For the term
B_{1}^{(n)}
in (
3.4
), since
\alpha^{\prime}(t)
is bounded
and
0<\alpha(t)\leq\alpha^{*}<1
,
we can obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle B_{1}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)\left(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}}{{\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}\right)\left(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}\right)}}=\left(1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}\right)\cdot\left(1+\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{\tau\left\|\alpha^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}}{2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}\right)\cdot\left(1+\frac{\tau\left\|\alpha^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}}{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}\right)\leq 1+C_{7}\tau.

For the term
B_{2}^{(n)}
in (
3.4
), we define a continuous auxiliary function

\displaystyle h_{1}(z)=s^{-z},\hskip 10.00002pts>0.

Next, we discuss
B_{2}^{(n)}
separately according to the value of
s
.
If
s\leq 1
, since the function
h_{1}(z)
is increasing and
\alpha(t)
is decreasing, we can get
h_{1}(\tilde{\alpha}_{n})\leq h_{1}(\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1})
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle B_{2}^{(n)}=\frac{\int_{t_{n-k-1}}^{t_{n-k}}\int_{x-\tau}^{x}s^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}dsdx}{\int_{t_{n-k-1}}^{t_{n-k}}\int_{x-\tau}^{x}s^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}dsdx}\leq\frac{\int_{t_{n-k-1}}^{t_{n-k}}\int_{x-\tau}^{x}s^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}dsdx}{\int_{t_{n-k-1}}^{t_{n-k}}\int_{x-\tau}^{x}s^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n-1}}dsdx}=1.

(3.6)

If
s>1
, we take the derivative of
h_{1}(z)
,

\displaystyle h_{1}^{\prime}(z)=-\ln s\cdot s^{-z}.

Since
h_{1}^{\prime}(z)

Lemma 3.3

5
Acceleration techniques

Computational efficiency of the numerical schemes for FPDEs has always been concerned.
In this section, we consider two kinds of techniques to accelerate the implementation of numerical schemes.
One is the fast computation based on the ESA technique along the time direction, the other is the optimal QSC method from the view of space direction.

5.1
Fast computation in time direction

We can see that the time discretization of Caputo fractional differential operator involves the numerical solutions at all previous time levels, the computation is extremely
expensive for long-time simulations.
In order to reduce the computational cost, we employ ESA technique to accelerate the evaluation of the

L1^{+}
scheme for variable-order FPDEs. The main purpose is to approximate the singular kernel
t^{-\beta}
of the Caputo fractional differential operator
on the interval
[\tau,T]
efficiently.

Lemma 5.1
(
[
41
]
)

At any time instant
t_{n}
, for
\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\in[\alpha_{*},\alpha^{*}]\subset(0,1)
,

t\in\left[t_{n-1},t_{n}\right]
,
s\in\left[0,t_{n-2}\right]
and the expected accuracy
0<\epsilon\leq 1/e
,
if we choose constants
h
,
\overline{N}
and
\underline{N}
as

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\log 3+\alpha^{*}\log(\cos 1)^{-1}+\log\epsilon^{-1}},\hskip 10.00002pt\underline{N}=\left\lceil\frac{1}{h}\frac{1}{\alpha_{*}}(\log\epsilon+\log\Gamma(1+\alpha^{*}))\right\rceil,

\displaystyle\overline{N}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{h}\left(\log\frac{T}{\Delta t}+\log\log\epsilon^{-1}+\log\alpha_{*}+2^{-1}\right)\right\rfloor,

then the quantity
\left(\frac{t-s}{T}\right)^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}
can be approximated by

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{t-s}{T}\right)^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}-\sum_{r=\underline{N}+1}^{\overline{N}}\varpi^{(n,r)}e^{\frac{-\lambda^{(r)}\left({t-s}\right)}{T}}\right|\leq\left(\frac{t-s}{T}\right)^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\epsilon,

where the quadrature exponents and weights are given by

\displaystyle\lambda^{(r)}=e^{rh},\hskip 10.00002pt\varpi^{(n,r)}=\frac{he^{\tilde{\alpha}_{n}rh}}{\Gamma\left(\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}.

Now for any
v\in\mathfrak{T}
, the non-local term
\bar{\delta}_{t}^{\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}v(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}})
with
n\geq 3
defined in (
2.11
) for the QSC-
L1^{+}
scheme
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bar{\delta}_{t}^{\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}v\left(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)&=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n-2}}\partial_{t}\Pi v(s)\ \omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)dsdt+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{t_{n-2}}^{t}\partial_{t}\Pi v(s)\ \omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)dsdt\\
&:=I_{\tau}^{t_{0},t_{n-2}}\left(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)+I_{\tau}^{t_{n-2},t}\left(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right).\end{aligned}

(5.1)

First, for the local term in (
5.1
), it can be computed directly as

\displaystyle I_{\tau}^{t_{n-2},t}\left(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\sum_{k=n-1}^{n}a_{n-k+1}^{(n)}\left(v^{k}-v^{k-1}\right),

(5.2)

where the coefficients

and

Second, based on the definition of
\omega_{1-\beta}(t)
for the singular kernel, we get the non-local term as

\displaystyle I_{\tau}^{t_{0},t_{n-2}}\left(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\tau\Gamma(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n})}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}\partial_{t}\Pi v(s)\left(\frac{t-s}{T}\right)^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}dsdt.

According to Lemma
5.1
, the term
(\frac{t-s}{T})^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}
in the integral can be approximated. Then we have

\displaystyle I_{\tau}^{t_{0},t_{n-2}}\left(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{T^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\tau\Gamma(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n})}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n-2}}\partial_{t}\Pi v(s)\sum_{r=\underline{N}+1}^{\overline{N}}\varpi^{(n,r)}e^{-\lambda^{(r)}\frac{t-s}{T}}dsdt

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{T^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\tau\Gamma(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n})}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t_{n-2}}\partial_{t}\Pi v(s)\sum_{r=\underline{N}+1}^{\overline{N}}\varpi^{(n,r)}e^{-\lambda^{(r)}\frac{t-t_{n-2}}{T}}e^{-\lambda^{(r)}\frac{t_{n-2}-s}{T}}dsdt

where

\displaystyle b^{(n,r)}=\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}e^{-\lambda^{(r)}\frac{t-t_{n-2}}{T}}dt,\hskip 10.00002ptV^{(n,r)}=\int_{0}^{t_{n-2}}\partial_{t}\Pi v(s)e^{-\lambda^{(r)}\frac{t_{n-2}-s}{T}}ds,\hskip 10.00002ptr=\underline{N}+1,\underline{N}+2,\cdots,\overline{N}.

\displaystyle V^{(n,r)}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda^{(r)}\frac{\tau}{T}}V^{(n-1,r)}+\frac{T}{\lambda^{(r)}\tau}\left(1-e^{-\lambda^{(r)}\frac{\tau}{T}}\right)\left(v^{n-2}-v^{n-3}\right).

\displaystyle\Big[\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}-\gamma_{n}\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{x}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}x}\big)\Big]\Big[\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}-\gamma_{n}\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{y}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}y}\big)\Big]\tilde{c}_{ij}^{n}

(5.10)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}\tilde{c}_{ij}^{n-1}+\gamma_{n}\Big[\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{x}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}x}\big)\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}+\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{y}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}y}\big)\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\Big]\tilde{c}_{ij}^{n-1}

\displaystyle+2\gamma_{n}^{2}\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{x}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}x}\big)\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{y}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}y}\big)\tilde{c}_{ij}^{n-1}-\gamma_{n}^{2}\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{x}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}x}\big)\big(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{y}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}y}\big)\tilde{c}_{ij}^{n-2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\gamma_{n}T^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\tau\kappa\Gamma(1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n})}\sum_{r=\underline{N}+1}^{\overline{N}}\varpi^{(n,r)}b^{(n,r)}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{x}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{y}\tilde{V}_{ij}^{(n,r)}+\frac{2\gamma_{n}}{\kappa}f_{ij}^{n-\frac{1}{2}},\ (i,j)\in\bar{\Lambda}.

The optimal ADI-QSC-F
L1^{+}
scheme can achieve fourth-order accuracy in space, which means that we can get a desired accuracy with much less mesh grids.

Conclusions

In this paper, we develop the QSC-
L1^{+}
scheme for the variable-order TF-MID equation in two dimensional space domain.
The scheme is proved to be unconditionally stable and convergent with accuracy
\mathcal{O}\big(\tau^{\min{\{3-\alpha^{*}-\alpha(0),2\}}}+\Delta{x}^{2}+\Delta{y}^{2}\big)
,
for proper assumptions on
\alpha(t)
.
Based on the QSC-
L1^{+}
scheme, we design a novel ADI framework to achieve the ADI-QSC-
L1^{+}
scheme, and we also analyze
its unconditional stability and convergence.
The numerical experiments show that the results fit well with the theoretical analysis, even
\alpha(t)
do not satisfy the restrictions.
Then we employ the fast evaluation based on ESA technique to obtain the ADI-QSC-F
L1^{+}
scheme, which can reduce the computation cost greatly.
Furthermore, the optimal QSC method is also applied to get the optimal ADI-QSC-F
L1^{+}
scheme, the numerical results
show that the higher order schemes lead to much better computational efficiency.

Appendix A
Estimate of
r_{1,n}

Based on the definition of
r_{1,n}
, we have

\displaystyle r_{1,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha(t)}v(t)dt-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{}_{0}^{C}D_{t}^{\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}v(t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\omega_{1-\alpha(t)}(t-s)-\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)\right]\partial_{s}v(s)dsdt.

We can obtain from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\left|r_{1,n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Q_{0}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\big[\omega_{1-\alpha(t)}(t-s)-\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)\big]dsdt\right|

\displaystyle=Q_{0}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left[\frac{t^{1-\alpha(t)}}{\Gamma\left(2-\alpha(t)\right)}-\frac{t^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\Gamma\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}\right]dt\right|

\displaystyle=Q_{0}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\frac{\Gamma\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)t^{1-\alpha(t)}-\Gamma(2-\alpha(t))t^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}}{\Gamma(2-\alpha(t))\Gamma\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)}dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq Q_{0}Q_{1}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\Gamma\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)\left(t^{1-\alpha(t)}-t^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\right)dt\right|+Q_{0}Q_{1}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}t^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\big[\Gamma\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)-\Gamma(2-\alpha(t))\big]dt\right|,

where
\Gamma(x)
is bounded when
x\in(1,2)
.
By Taylor’s expansion, we can get

\displaystyle\left.t^{1-\alpha(\xi)}\right|_{\xi=t}=

\displaystyle t^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}-t^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(\ln t)\alpha^{\prime}(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}})\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[t^{1-\alpha(\eta_{1})}(\ln t)^{2}\left(\alpha^{\prime}\left(\eta_{1}\right)\right)^{2}-t^{1-\alpha(\eta_{1})}(\ln t)\alpha^{\prime\prime}(\eta_{1})\right]\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2},

and

\displaystyle\Gamma(2-\alpha(t))=

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)-\Gamma^{\prime}\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right){\alpha}^{\prime}(t_{n-\frac{1}{2}})\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left[-\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\left(2-\alpha\left(\eta_{2}\right)\right)\left(\alpha^{\prime}\left(\eta_{2}\right)\right)^{2}+\Gamma^{\prime}\left(2-\alpha\left(\eta_{2}\right)\right)\alpha^{\prime\prime}\left(\eta_{2}\right)\right]\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2},

where
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are both between
t
and
t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left|r_{1,n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Q_{0}Q_{1}Q_{2}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left[t^{1-\alpha(t)}-t^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\right]dt\right|+Q_{0}Q_{1}Q_{3}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\big[\Gamma(2-\alpha(t))-\Gamma\left(2-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\right)\big]dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq Q_{0}Q_{1}Q_{2}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left[Q_{4}\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)+Q_{5}\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}\right]dt\right|

\displaystyle+Q_{0}Q_{1}Q_{3}\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left[Q_{6}\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)+Q_{7}\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}\right]dt\right|,

Since

\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)dt=0
and

\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\left(t-t_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}dt=\tau^{3}
,
we can get
\left|r_{1,n}\right|=\mathcal{O}\left(\tau^{2}\right)
.

Appendix B
Estimate of
r_{2,n}

Similar to the routine in Refs.
[
36
,
47
]
, we can get the following proof.
By exchanging the order of integration and using integration by parts on each subinterval,
we can get

\displaystyle r_{2,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\left[\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)\partial_{s}\theta v(s)dsdt-\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}(t-s)\partial_{s}\theta v(s)dsdt\right]

(B.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{n}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\theta v\left(s\right)ds-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{n-1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-s\right)\theta v\left(s\right)ds.

(I) For
1\leq n\leq 3
, we have from (
2.8
) and (
B.1
) that

\displaystyle\left|r_{2,n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left[\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-s\right)-\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\right]\left|\theta v\left(s\right)\right|ds+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\left|\theta v\left(s\right)\right|ds

(B.2)

\displaystyle\leq Q_{8}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(t_{k}^{1-\alpha(0)}-t_{k-1}^{1-\alpha(0)}\right)\tau^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}+Q_{9}\left(t_{n}^{1-\alpha(0)}-t_{n-1}^{1-\alpha(0)}\right)\tau^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}=Q_{10}t_{n}^{1-\alpha(0)}\tau^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\leq Q_{11}t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-\alpha(0)}\tau^{2}.

(II) For
n\geq 4
, we set
n_{0}=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil
so that
\frac{n}{2}\leq n_{0}\leq\frac{n}{2}+1
and
n\geq n_{0}+2
.
According to (
B.1

r_{2,n}=r_{2,n}^{1}+r_{2,n}^{2}+r_{2,n}^{3}
, where

\displaystyle r_{2,n}^{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\left[\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)-\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-s\right)\right]\theta v\left(s\right)ds,

(B.3)

\displaystyle r_{2,n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{n_{0}}}^{t_{n_{0}+1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\theta v\left(s\right)ds,

\displaystyle r_{2,n}^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n-1}\left[\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\theta v\left(s\right)ds-\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-s\right)\theta v\left(s\right)ds\right].

Since
0\leq\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-s\right)-\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\leq Q_{12}\tau\left(t_{n-1}-t_{n_{0}}\right)^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-1}\leq Q_{13}\tau t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-1}
for
t_{0}\leq s\leq t_{n_{0}}
,
we can get from (
2.8
) that

\displaystyle\left|r_{2,n}^{1}\right|\leq Q_{14}\tau^{2}t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\left(t_{k}^{1-\alpha(0)}-t_{k-1}^{1-\alpha(0)}\right)=Q_{14}\tau^{2}t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-1}t_{n_{0}}^{1-\alpha(0)}\leq Q_{15}t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-\alpha(0)}\tau^{2}.

(B.4)

Similarly, we can obtain

\displaystyle\left|r_{2,n}^{2}\right|\leq Q_{16}\tau\left(t_{n_{0}+1}^{1-\alpha(0)}-t_{n_{0}}^{1-\alpha(0)}\right)\left(t_{n}-t_{n_{0}+1}\right)^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\leq Q_{17}\tau^{2}t_{n_{0}}^{-\alpha(0)}t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\leq Q_{18}t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-\alpha(0)}\tau^{2}.

(B.5)

According to that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\left(s-t_{k}\right)\left(s-t_{k+1}\right)ds=\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-s\right)\left(s-t_{k-1}\right)\left(s-t_{k}\right)ds,

we can rewrite
r_{2,n}^{3}
as
r_{2,n}^{3}=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n-1}\left(\eta_{n}^{k}-\widetilde{\eta}_{n}^{k}\right)
, where

\displaystyle\eta_{n}^{k}=\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\left[\theta v\left(s\right)-\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}v\left(t_{k}\right)}{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)\left(s-t_{k+1}\right)\right]ds,

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{k}=\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-s\right)\left[\theta v\left(s\right)-\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}v\left(t_{k}\right)}{2}\left(s-t_{k-1}\right)\left(s-t_{k}\right)\right]ds.

When
n_{0}+1\leq k\leq n-1
and
t_{k}\leq s\leq t_{k+1}
,
\theta v\left(s\right)=\frac{\partial_{t}^{2}v\left(\hat{\rho}_{k}\right)}{2}\left(s-t_{k}\right)\left(s-t_{k+1}\right)
,
where
\hat{\rho}_{k}\in\left(t_{k},t_{k+1}\right)
.
Thus, there exists
\hat{\zeta}_{k}\in\left(t_{k},\hat{\rho}_{k}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{n}^{k}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)\left|\partial_{t}^{3}v\left(\hat{\zeta}_{k}\right)\right|\left(\hat{\rho}_{k}-t_{k}\right)\left(s-t_{k}\right)\left(t_{k+1}-s\right)ds\leq Q_{19}\tau^{3}t_{k}^{-1-\alpha(0)}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n}-s\right)ds.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{k}\right|\leq Q_{20}\tau^{3}t_{k-1}^{-1-\alpha(0)}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\omega_{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\left(t_{n-1}-s\right)ds,\hskip 9.24994ptn_{0}+1\leq k\leq n-1.

Based on that
t_{k}\geq t_{k-1}\geq t_{n_{0}}\geq\frac{1}{2}t_{n}
when
n_{0}+1\leq k\leq n-1
, we can get

\displaystyle\left|r_{2,n}^{3}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n-1}\left|\eta_{n}^{k}-\tilde{\eta}_{n}^{k}\right|\leq Q_{21}\tau^{2}t_{n}^{-1-\alpha(0)}\left[\left(t_{n}-t_{n_{0}+1}\right)^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}+\left(t_{n-1}-t_{n_{0}}\right)^{1-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}}\right]\leq Q_{22}t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-\alpha(0)}\tau^{2}.

(B.6)

By (
B.4
), (
B.5
) and (

\left|r_{2,n}\right|\leq Q_{23}t_{n}^{-\tilde{\alpha}_{n}-\alpha(0)}\tau^{2}
for

The proof is completed.

On the chromatic edge stability index of graphs
Saieed Akbari, Arash Beikmohammadi, Boštjan Brešar, Tanja Dravec, Mohammad Mahdi Habibollahi, Nazanin Movarraei

Abstract
Given a non-trivial graph G G , the minimum cardinality of a set of edges F F in G G such that χ ′ ​ ( G ∖ F ) < χ ′ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\prime}(G\setminus F)<\chi^{\prime}(G) is called the chromatic edge stability index of G G , denoted by e ​ s χ ′ ​ ( G ) es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G) , and such a (smallest) set F F is called a (minimum) mitigating set. While 1 ≤ e ​ s χ ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ 1\leq es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor holds for any graph G G , we investigate the graphs with extremal and near-extremal values of e ​ s χ ′ ​ ( G ) es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G) . The graphs G G with e ​ s χ ′ ​ ( G ) = ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor are classified, and the graphs G G with e ​ s χ ′ ​ ( G ) = ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ − 1 es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1 and χ ′ ​ ( G ) = Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 \chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1 are characterized. We establish that the odd cycles and K 2 K_{2} are exactly the regular connected graphs with the chromatic edge stability index 1 1 ; on the other hand, we prove that it is NP-hard to verify whether a graph G G has e ​ s χ ′ ​ ( G ) = 1 es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=1 . We also prove that every minimum mitigating set of an r r -regular graph G G , where r ≠ 4 r\neq 4 , with e ​ s χ ′ ​ ( G ) = 2 es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=2 is a matching. Furthermore, we propose a conjecture that for every graph G G there exists a minimum mitigating set, which is a matching, and prove that the conjecture holds for graphs G G with e ​ s χ ′ ​ ( G ) ∈ { 1 , 2 , ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ − 1 , ⌊ n / 2 ⌋ } es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\in\{1,2,\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\} , and for bipartite graphs.

Introduction

Throughout this paper all graphs are finite and simple, that is, with no loops and multiple edges, and moreover, with at least one edge.

.
We let

For any

in the proper edge coloring

For any

, let

denote the set of colors appearing in
v
.
The

is the set of neighbors of

, whereas the

of
v
is
N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
. The
degree
of a vertex

in

is denoted by
d_{G}(v)=|N_{G}(v)|
. A
k

is a graph in which every vertex has degree
k
. A graph

if it is
k

.
The subgraph of
G

will be denoted by

. The

. In this paper

One of the most celebrated results in graph theory due to Vizing
[
16
]
states that the chromatic index of an arbitrary simple graph lies between the maximum degree
\Delta(G)
and
\Delta(G)+1
. Graphs with
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)
are said to be of
Class
1
, while graphs with
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1
are said to be of
Class
2
. Holyer proved that determining whether a graph is of Class 1 (or 2) is in general an NP-complete problem
[
9
]
, which in part explains the large number of investigations of the chromatic index and related properties; see a recent survey on edge colorings
[
6
]
.

Vizing in a follow-up
[
17
]
investigated the effect of edge removal in a graph of Class 2, and called such a graph
critical
if after removing any edge the chromatic index drops. It is easy to see that the only critical Class 1 graphs are the stars. It is also easy to see that every Class 2 graph
G
contains a subgraph
H
such that
H
is a critical Class 2 graph and
\Delta(H)=\Delta(G)
. Vizing later proposed two conjectures for critical Class 2 graphs, notably, in
[
18
]
he conjectured that every such graph admits a
2
-factor, and in
[
19
]
that the independence number of a critical graph is at most half of its order. In spite of many attempts and partial results, the conjectures are still unresolved; see a recent study
[
11
]
and the references therein.

In this paper, we propose a different perspective on the study of the effect of edge removal with respect to the chromatic index. A similar study with respect to the chromatic number was initiated by Staton
[
15
]
in 1980, and received a considerable attention in recent years
[
2
,
5
,
13
]
. The question considered in these papers is, how many edges need to be removed from a graph so that its chromatic number drops. To the best of our knowledge, an analogous question for the chromatic index has not yet been studied. (The only exception is a very recent paper
[
12
]
in which the concept was mentioned in a more general context, and some initial results were obtained.)
We find this surprising and want to initiate the investigation of this problem, which we formally define as follows.

The
chromatic edge stability index
,
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)
, of a graph
G
is the minimum number of edges of
G
such that their deletion results in a graph
H
with
\chi^{\prime}(H)=\chi^{\prime}(G)-1
. For a set
F\subseteq E(G)
we denote by
G\setminus F
the graph
G
without the edges of the set
F
. If
F=\{e\}
, where
e\in E(G)
, we simply write
G\setminus e
instead of
G\setminus\{e\}
.
A
mitigating set
is a set of edges
F
in
G
such that
\chi^{\prime}(G\setminus F)<\chi^{\prime}(G)
.

A
matching

M
is a set of edges in
G
such that every two distinct edges
e
and
f
in
M
are not adjacent. We say that a matching
M
with
|M|=k
is a
k
-matching
. A
perfect matching
of

is a matching
M
of
G
that covers all vertices of
G
. The cardinality of a maximum matching of a graph
G
is the
matching number
of
G
and denoted by
\alpha^{\prime}(G)
.

Let
c:E(G)\to\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{\chi^{\prime}(G)}\}
be a proper
\chi^{\prime}(G)
-edge coloring of
G
. Suppose that

C
is a color class. Then, obviously
\chi^{\prime}(G\setminus C)<\chi^{\prime}(G)
, and since
C
is a matching, we infer
|C|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{|V(G)|}{2}\right\rfloor
. This yields the upper bound in the following basic result (the lower bound is trivial).

Lemma 1.1
.

If
G
is a graph of order
n
, then
1\leq es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

The following two questions will be studied in this paper. What are the graphs that achieve the extremal or near extremal values of the chromatic edge stability index in view of the inequalities in Lemma
1.1
? The second question is concerned with the structure of minimum mitigating sets. Notably, it is not clear if there is always such a proper edge coloring of
G
for which there is a minimum mitigating set obtained in this way (by deletion of edges of a smallest color class). We suspect the answer is affirmative, and propose this as the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

For every graph
G
there exists a minimum mitigating set which is a matching.

In Section

we establish the notation, present several preliminary results from the literature, and study the chromatic edge stability index in some simple graph families. In Section

In Section

Preliminary results

In this section, we establish the notation, and present the chromatic edge stability index in some simple families of graphs, such as cycles, paths, complete graphs and complete bipartite graphs. We also present several useful preliminary results from the literature concerning edge colorings.

Lemma 2.11

If

es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor.

Proof.

First, let

\chi^{\prime}(K_{n})=n-1

3
Graphs with extreme values of
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)

In this section, we first consider the graphs with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
and then continue with investigating the graphs with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=1
. The results yield that Conjecture
1
holds for both classes.

Our first result shows that complete graphs
K_{n}
(with odd
n
) are the only graphs of Class 2 with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
G
is a Class
2
graph of order
n
, then
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
if and only if
n
is odd and
G
is isomorphic to
K_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a Class 2 graph of order
n
.
We first prove that
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)<\frac{n}{2}
, if
n
is even. Therefore, let
G
be a Class 2 graph of even order. By contradiction suppose that
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
. Then by Remark
2.1
every color class of any proper
\chi^{\prime}(G)
-edge coloring contains exactly
\frac{n}{2}
edges. Thus
|E(G)|=(\Delta(G)+1)\frac{n}{2}
, which is a contradiction, since
|E(G)|\leq\frac{\Delta(G)n}{2}
holds in every graph
G
.
We may thus assume that
n
is odd.

Now, let
c
be a proper
\chi^{\prime}(G)
-edge coloring of
G
with
\Delta(G)+1
colors and suppose that
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then by Remark
2.1
every color class of
c
contains exactly
\frac{n-1}{2}
edges. Thus
|E(G)|=(\Delta(G)+1)\frac{n-1}{2}
. We note that
|E(G)|\leq\frac{n\Delta(G)}{2}
, which implies
\Delta(G)\geq n-1
, and hence
G
is isomorphic to
K_{n}
. The reverse direction follows from Lemma
2.11
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
G
is a Class
1
graph of even order
n
, then
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
if and only if
G
is a regular graph.

Proof.

Let
G
be a Class 1 graph of even order
n
, and suppose that
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
. If
c
is a proper
\chi^{\prime}(G)
-edge coloring of
G
with
\Delta(G)
colors, then each color class of
c
must have exactly
\frac{n}{2}

|E(G)|=\Delta(G)\frac{n}{2}
, and

is a
\Delta(G)
-regular graph.

Conversely, let
G
be a
\Delta(G)
-regular graph of Class 1. In order to reduce the chromatic index (to
\Delta(G)-1
), one must remove a set of edges

from

G
. Hence
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
, and by Lemma

es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
, which completes the proof.
∎

Let

.

If
G

First, let

\chi^{\prime}(G)

(1)

\Delta(G)-1

Next, we focus our attention on the graphs

\chi^{\prime}(G)

Proof.

Let
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=1

\chi^{\prime}(G\setminus e^{\prime})=\chi^{\prime}(G)-1

-edge coloring of

and

is a proper

-edge coloring of the edges of

which contains a singleton color class. The reverse direction follows from Remark

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

If

is a graph of Class
1
with

, then

contains at most two vertices of degree

, and if there are two vertices of degree

, then they are adjacent.

Proof.

Let

. If

contains more than two vertices of degree

or two non adjacent vertices of degree

, then
\Delta(G\setminus e)=\Delta(G)
for any

, and so
\chi^{\prime}(G\setminus e)\geq\Delta(G\setminus e)=\Delta(G)=\chi^{\prime}(G)
. Hence,

, a contradiction.
∎

Next, we characterize graphs

with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=1
among all connected regular graphs, showing that there are just two such families.

Theorem 3.8
.

If

is a connected regular graph, then

if and only if

is isomorphic to

or

with odd

Proof.

Let

be a connected regular graph of order
n
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=1
. Hence it follows from Lemma
3.6
that there exists a proper
\chi^{\prime}(G)
-edge coloring
c
of the edges of

with a singleton color class

. Suppose first that
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+1
. Hence
|C_{1}|+\cdots+|C_{\Delta(G)+1}|=\frac{n\Delta(G)}{2}
and
|C_{i}|\leq\frac{n}{2}
for any

. If

is even, (since

) the equality holds if and only if there exists

such that

for any

. So, we have
\Delta(G)-1
disjoint perfect matchings. Since

is

, a contradiction.
If

the only regular graph

, which completes the proof, since the converse is clear.
∎

In the rest of this section, we focus on the computational complexity of determining whether a given graph achieves one of the extremal values.

For a regular graph

of even order Theorem

implies that
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
if and only if

is of Class
1
.
Note that

about NP-completeness of determining whether a

-regular graph is of Class 1. We derive the following result.

Given a graph

, even if

is restricted to

-regular graphs.

We prove a similar result for graphs with the chromatic edge stability index equal to

Lemma 3.10
.

Let

be an
r
-regular graph of order

be obtained from

if and only if

Proof.

We start by proving the following claim.

Claim 3.11
.

\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G)+1
.

Proof.
Since
\Delta(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G)+1
and exactly two vertices of

have degree
\Delta(G^{\prime})
,
Core(G^{\prime})
is a forest. Hence by Lemma

G^{\prime}
is of Class 1 and
\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G)+1

(\square)

Suppose first that

. Hence there exists

such that

. Hence

and hence

For the converse, suppose that

. Since by Claim

\chi^{\prime}(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G)+1
,
F=\{xy\}
is a minimum mitigating set of

and hence

.
∎

By Lemma

, we infer that the existence of a polynomial algorithm to determine whether a graph

has

would imply that one can determine by polynomial algorithm whether a regular graph is of Class 1. We again invoke the result of Holyer, Theorem

, and derive the following.

Corollary 3.12

Given a graph

\Delta(G)=4
and

.

In this section, we prove that Conjecture

If

-regular graph, where

If

or

Now, let

be a graph of Class 2. Assume that there exists a set of edges

such that
\chi^{\prime}(G\setminus S)=\chi^{\prime}(G)-1=r

Case 1.

, is an

-matching and each of the colors

and

Remark 4.2

For every odd integer

The reverse direction follows from Theorem

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
G
be a graph of Class
2
of odd order
n
which is not connected. Then
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-3}{2}
if and only if
G
is isomorphic to
K_{n-2}\cup K_{2}
or
K_{n-2}\cup\overline{K_{2}}
, for

or
K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{1}
or
K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}
, where

is a positive integer.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
, it is not hard to see that all graphs given in the statement of the lemma are of Class 2, have odd order and chromatic edge stability index equal to
\frac{n-3}{2}
.

For the reverse direction, suppose that
G
has
k
components
H_{1},\ldots,H_{k}
. For each
H_{i}
,
i\in[k]
, either
\chi^{\prime}(H_{i})\leq\Delta(G)
or
\chi^{\prime}(H_{i})=\Delta(G)+1
and by Theorem
3.1
,
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(H_{i})\leq\frac{|V(H_{i})|-1}{2}
. So for every
i\in[k]
, there exists
M_{i}\subset E(H_{i})
such that
\chi^{\prime}(H_{i}\setminus M_{i})\leq\Delta(G)
and
|M_{i}|\leq\frac{|V(H_{i})|-1}{2}
, and we may assume that
M_{i}
is a minimum such set (in particular, if
\chi^{\prime}(H_{i})\leq\Delta(G)
, then

). Clearly,

is a mitigating set and

. So,
k\leq 3
and now we consider two cases:

Case 1.

k=3
.
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-3}{2}\leq|M|=|M_{1}|+|M_{2}|+|M_{3}|\leq\frac{|V(H_{1})|-1}{2}+\frac{|V(H_{2})|-1}{2}+\frac{|V(H_{3})|-1}{2}=\frac{n-3}{2}
. So
|M_{1}|=\frac{|V(H_{1})|-1}{2}
and
|M_{2}|=\frac{|V(H_{2})|-1}{2}
and
|M_{3}|=\frac{|V(H_{3})|-1}{2}
. If
\chi^{\prime}(H_{i})\leq\Delta(G)
, for

, then
|M_{i}|=\frac{|V(H_{i})|-1}{2}=0
and hence
|V(H_{i})|=1
. If
\chi^{\prime}(H_{i})=\Delta(G)+1
, for

, then Theorem
3.1
yields that

. So
H_{i}\cong K_{1}
or
H_{i}\cong K_{\Delta(G)+1}
. Since
G
is of Class 2, it is isomorphic to
K_{n-2}\cup\overline{K_{2}}
or
K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{1}
or
K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}
, where
m
is a positive integer, as desired.

Case 2.

k=2
. If
|M_{1}|\leq\frac{|V(H_{1})|-3}{2}
or
|M_{2}|\leq\frac{|V(H_{2})|-3}{2}
, then
|M|\leq\frac{n-4}{2}<\frac{n-3}{2}=es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)
, a contradiction. So
|M_{i}|=\frac{|V(H_{i})|-2}{2}
or
|M_{i}|=\frac{|V(H_{i})|-1}{2}

. With no loss of generality suppose that

and
|M_{2}|=\frac{|V(H_{2})|-2}{2}
. By Corollary
5.7
, there is no connected graph of even order of Class 2 with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{|V(H_{2})|-2}{2}
. So,
\chi^{\prime}(H_{2})\leq\Delta(G)
and then
|M_{2}|=\frac{|V(H_{2})|-2}{2}=0
and hence
|V(H_{2})|=2
. Since
G
is of Class 2,
H_{1}
is of Class 2 and by Theorem
3.1
,
H_{1}
is a complete graph of odd order. Hence,

is isomorphic to
K_{n-2}\cup K_{2}
, as desired.
∎

For edge

denote

by

.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
G
be a graph of Class
2
of odd order
n
. Then
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-3}{2}
if and only if
G
is isomorphic to
K_{n}^{-}
or
K_{n-2}\cup K_{2}
or
K_{n-2}\cup\overline{K_{2}}
or
\overline{K_{2}\cup\ldots\cup K_{2}\cup K_{1,2}}
or an
(n-3)
-regular graph, for
n\geq 5
or
K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{1}
or
K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}
, where
m
is a positive integer.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
and Lemmas
5.8
,
5.9
and
5.12
, it is not hard to see that all graphs given in the statement of the theorem are of Class 2, have odd order with chromatic edge stability index equal to
\frac{n-3}{2}
.

If
G
is not connected, then by Lemma
5.12
, it is isomorphic to
K_{n-2}\cup K_{2}
or
K_{n-2}\cup\overline{K_{2}}
, for
n\geq 5
or
K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{1}
or
K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}
, where
m
is a positive integer, as desired.

Now, suppose that
G
is connected and let
H=Core(G)
.
It follows from Lemma
5.3
that
\frac{n-3}{2}=es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(H)\leq\frac{a}{2}
, where
a=|V(H)|
which implies
a\geq n-3
. Now, we consider four cases:

Case 1.

a=n-3
. Since
\alpha^{\prime}(H)\geq\frac{n-3}{2}
, there exists a perfect matching
M
in
H
. If we choose an arbitrary set
F
of
\alpha^{\prime}(H)-1
edges from

, then
Core(G\setminus F)
is a forest and hence by Lemma
2.8
,
\chi^{\prime}(G\setminus F)=\Delta(G\setminus F)=\Delta(G)<\chi^{\prime}(G)
. Therefore,
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(H)-1=\frac{n-3}{2}-1
, a contradiction.

Case 2.

a=n-2
. Let
V(H)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-2}\}
, and let
M
be a maximum matching of
H
. Since
\alpha^{\prime}(H)=\frac{n-3}{2}
, we have
|V(H)\setminus V(M)|=1
, and let
v_{1}\in V(H)\setminus V(M)
. Assume that there exists an edge
v_{2}v_{3}\in M
such that
v_{1}v_{2}\notin E(H)
or
v_{1}v_{3}\notin E(H)
. Then
Core(G\setminus(M\setminus\{v_{2}v_{3}\}))
is a forest of order 3 and by Lemma
2.8
, the graph
G\setminus(M\setminus\{v_{2}v_{3}\})
is of Class 1. Hence,
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)<\frac{n-3}{2}
, a contradiction. Therefore
d_{H}(v_{1})=a-1
. Let
M^{\prime}=(M\setminus\{v_{2}v_{3}\})\cup\{v_{1}v_{2}\}
which is obviously another maximum matching of
H
and by the same method
d_{H}(v_{3})=a-1
. Now, by the same manner we infer that
H\cong K_{n-2}
.

Now, let
V(G)\setminus V(H)=\{x,y\}
. Since
G
is connected, there is at least one edge between
\{x,y\}
and
V(H)
. Hence there exists
w\in V(H)
such that
d_{G}(w)\geq n-2
. Since all vertices of
H
have the same degree in

, then
d_{G}(v)\geq n-2
, for any
v\in V(H)
. Suppose that there exists
w\in V(H)
that is adjacent to both

and

. Therefore
d_{G}(v)=n-1
for all

. Since

,

and

are not adjacent. Thus
G\cong K_{n}^{-}
.

Therefore, it remains to consider the case when
\Delta(G)=n-2
and each vertex
v\in V(H)
is adjacent to exactly one vertex from
\{x,y\}
. By Remark
5.1
, there exists a proper edge coloring
c^{\prime}
of

with
\Delta(H)+1=n-2
colors such that for each

,
c_{i}\notin c^{\prime}(v_{i})
, and
c_{j}\in c^{\prime}(v_{i})
for any
j\neq i
. Now, we extend this coloring to a proper edge coloring
c
of
E(G)
as follows. If
e\in E(H)
, then define
c(e)=c^{\prime}(e)
. If
v_{i}\in V(H)
is adjacent to
x
in
G
, then define
c(xv_{i})=c_{i}
, and if
v_{i}\in V(H)
is adjacent to
y
in
G
, then define
c(yv_{i})=c_{i}
. Since
\chi^{\prime}(G)>\Delta(G)
,

. Indeed, if
xy\notin E(G)
,
c
is a proper edge coloring of
G
with
\Delta(G)
colors. Hence

is a graph with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=1
. Since
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-3}{2}
we deduce that

and so

. Hence

, a contradiction.

Case 3.

a=n
. Then
G
is a connected
r
-regular graph. We start by showing that
\alpha^{\prime}(G)=\frac{n-1}{2}
. By contradiction assume that
\alpha^{\prime}(G)\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
. Let
M
be a matching of size
\alpha^{\prime}(G)
. Let

. Hence

is a mitigating set of size at most

.

Now, we show that
r\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
. Let
V(G)\setminus V(M)=\{u\}
. If

, then there exists

such that

and

|L_{c^{2}}|=|L_{c}|+1=\frac{r}{2}+1

M\cup N^{\prime}_{c^{2}}
is a mitigating set of size
\frac{n-5}{2}
, a contradiction. Otherwise, there are exactly two vertices
a,b\in N(u)\setminus\{w\}
such that
f_{c^{2}}(a)=f_{c^{2}}(b)=c_{3}
and for any
d\in V(G)\setminus N[u]
, different from
v
, we have
f_{c^{2}}(d)\neq c_{3}
, while for all
d\in V(G)
,
f_{c^{2}}(d)\neq c_{2}
. So,
P_{v}(c_{2},c_{3})
ends in
a
or
b
or
w
. Let
c^{3}
be a proper
r
-edge coloring of
G\setminus(M\cup T)
by replacing
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
in
P_{v}(c_{2},c_{3})
of
c^{2}
. Now, there is exactly one vertex
h\in N(u)
such that
f_{c^{3}}(h)=c_{2}
. Hence,
|L_{c^{3}}|=|L_{c}|+1=\frac{r}{2}+1
and
M\cup(T\setminus L^{\prime}_{c^{3}})
is a mitigating set of size
\frac{r}{2}\leq\frac{n-5}{2}
, a contradiction.

Subcase 3.2.
In this case
G[V(G)\setminus N[u]]
is an independent set. Clearly,
Core(G\setminus T)
is a forest and so by Lemma
2.8
,
T
is a mitigating set (where
T
is again the set of all edges incident with
u
). By the same method, there is
N\subseteq T
,
|N|\leq\frac{r}{2}
such that
N
is a mitigating set of size
\frac{r}{2}\leq\frac{n-5}{2}
, a contradiction.

Case 4.

a=n-1
. We show that
|\alpha^{\prime}(Core(G))|=\frac{n-1}{2}
. Let
M
be a maximum matching of
Core(G)
. By Lemma
2.8
,
M
is a mitigating set. So
|M|\geq\frac{n-3}{2}=es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)
. To get a contradiction, assume that
|M|=\frac{n-3}{2}
. Let
V(Core(G))\setminus V(M)=\{a,b\}
. Clearly,
a
is not adjacent to
b
so for every
cd\in M
there are at most 3 edges in
G[a,b,c,d]
, because otherwise
M
is not maximum matching of
Core(G)
. Hence,
Core(G\setminus(M\setminus cd))
is a unicyclic graph or a tree, and is not a disjoint union of cycles. Since
G
is connected, by Lemma
2.9
,
M\setminus cd
is a mitigating set of size at most
\frac{n-5}{2}
, a contradiction.

Thus
G
has a matching say
M_{1}
, which saturates
Core(G)
. Let
G_{1}=G\setminus M_{1}
. By Lemma
5.4
,
G_{1}
is of Class 2 and
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G_{1})\geq es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-3}{2}
.
Since
G_{1}
is not a complete graph, then by Theorem
3.1
,
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-3}{2}
. If
G_{1}
is connected and
|Core(G_{1})|=n-1
, then similar to the previous proof,
G_{1}
has a matching
M_{2}
which saturates
Core(G_{1})
. By repeating this procedure in
l
steps, we find a Class 2 regular graph
G_{l}
such that
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G_{l})=\frac{n-3}{2}
or a Class 2 graph
G_{l}
which is not connected such that
|Core(G_{l})|=n-1
and
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G_{l})=\frac{n-3}{2}
. By Case 3 and Lemma
5.12
,
G_{l}
is an
(n-3)
-regular graph, for

or
G_{l}\cong K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}
or
G_{l}\cong K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{1}
, where
m
is a positive integer. Note that
G_{i-1}=G_{i}\cup M_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,l
, where
G_{0}=G
. If
G_{l}
is an
(n-3)
-regular graph, then
l=1
and so
G\cong\overline{K_{2}\cup\ldots\cup K_{2}\cup K_{1,2}}
. If
G_{l}\cong K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{1}
, then by Lemma
5.10
,
G_{l-1}
is of Class 1, a contradiction. If
G_{l}\cong K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}\cup K_{2m+1}
, then by Lemma
5.11
,
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G_{l-1})<\frac{n-3}{2}
, a contradiction.
∎

We say that a graph
G
of order
n
is

if it has

vertices of the same degree.

Corollary 5.14
.

Let
G
be a connected non-regular and not almost regular graph of Class
2
of odd order
n
. Then
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-3}{2}
if and only if
G
is isomorphic to
K_{n}^{-}
, for

.

In our next result we prove that Conjecture
1
holds also for graphs
G
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
.

Theorem 5.15
.

If
G
is a graph of order
n
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1=k
, then there exists a
k
-matching mitigating set.

Proof.

First, we prove that the result holds for a graph
G
of Class 2. Suppose that there does not exist a
k
-matching mitigating set in
G
.
If
n
is even, then
|C_{i}|=\frac{n}{2}
, for each color class
C_{i}
. So
|E(G)|=\frac{n}{2}(\Delta(G)+1)
, a contradiction. If
n
is odd, then
|C_{i}|=\frac{n-1}{2}
, for each color class
C_{i}
. So
|E(G)|=\frac{n-1}{2}(\Delta(G)+1)=\frac{(n-1)\Delta(G)+(n-1)}{2}
. This is possible only if
G
is isomorphic to
K_{n}
, which is a contradiction as
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(K_{n})=\frac{n-1}{2}
by Lemma
2.11
.

Now, let
G
be of Class 1. Let
c
be a proper edge coloring of
G
using
\chi^{\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)
colors. Suppose that there does not exist a
k
-matching mitigating set in
G
. If
n
is even, then
|C_{i}|=\frac{n}{2}
, for each color class
C_{i}
. Hence
|E(G)|=\frac{n}{2}\Delta(G)
, which implies that
G
is a regular graph. By Corollary
3.3
,
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
, a contradiction. If
n
is odd, then
|C_{i}|=\frac{n-1}{2}
, for each color class
C_{i}
. Hence
|E(G)|=\frac{n-1}{2}\Delta(G)
. Then it follows from Theorem
3.5
that
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\frac{n-1}{2}
, a contradiction.
∎

6
The chromatic edge stability index in bipartite graphs

In this section we show that Conjecture
1
holds for bipartite graphs.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=k
. Then there exists a
k
-matching mitigating set.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
k
.
For
k=1
, the assertion is trivial. Assume that the result holds for
k
and
G
is a bipartite graph with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=k+1
and
M
is a mitigating set such that
|M|=k+1
. We choose an arbitrary edge
xy\in M
. By assumption, there exists a
k
-matching mitigating set
M^{\prime}
of
G\setminus xy
. If
\{x,y\}\cap V(M^{\prime})=\varnothing
, then
M^{\prime}\cup\{xy\}
is a
(k+1)
-matching mitigating set of
G
, as desired.
Otherwise, let
M^{\prime\prime}=M^{\prime}\cup\{xy\}
and by Theorem
2.4
(Kőnig’s theorem), there is a proper
(\Delta(G)-1)
-edge coloring
c
of
G\setminus M^{\prime\prime}
. Now, we consider two cases:

Case 1.

\{x,y\}\cap V(M^{\prime})=\{x\}
. Assume that
xz\in M^{\prime}
. Suppose
x
is in the partite set
A
,
and
y
,
z
are in the partite set
B
. It is clear that there exists a color
c_{1}
such that
c_{1}\notin c(x)
.
Let
P
be the longest alternating path in
G
starting at
x
, traverse
xy
and whose edges are consecutively contained in
M^{\prime\prime}
and
C_{1}
(
C_{1}
is the related color class to
c_{1}
).
Assume that the last vertex of
P
is
u
. If
u\in B
, then the set of all edges of
P
in
C_{1}
union
M^{\prime\prime}\setminus E(P)
, forms a mitigating set for
G
of size
k
, a contradiction. To see this, color all edges of
E(P)\cap M^{\prime\prime}
by
c_{1}
and for each edge of
E(G)\setminus(M^{\prime\prime}\cup E(P))
keep its color in
c
. Now, if
u\in A
, then apply the same method to obtain a mitigating set, which is a
(k+1)
-matching of
G
.

Case 2.

\{x,y\}\cap V(M^{\prime})=\{x,y\}
. Since
G\setminus M^{\prime\prime}
has a proper
(\Delta(G)-1)
-edge coloring, we have
\Delta(G\setminus M^{\prime\prime})\leq\Delta(G)-1
. Note that
d_{G\setminus M^{\prime\prime}}(x)\leq\Delta(G)-2
and
d_{G\setminus M^{\prime\prime}}(y)\leq\Delta(G)-2
. So
\Delta(G\setminus M^{\prime})\leq\Delta(G)-1
. Thus by Theorem
2.4
,
M^{\prime}
is a mitigating set of
G
of size
k
, a contradiction.

∎

7
Concluding remarks

The main purpose of this paper is to introduce the new invariant measuring the effect of edge removal on proper edge colorings, which we call the chromatic edge stability index. In doing so, we encounter a natural problem, which we posed as Conjecture
1
, and which claims that there exists a smallest edge set
F
,
F\subset E(G)
, whose removal results in a graph with smaller chromatic index than
G
, such that
F
is a matching. Several results of this paper confirm the truth of the conjecture in some classes of graphs. Lemma
3.6
and Theorem
4.4
imply that Conjecture
1
holds for graphs
G
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\in\{1,2\}
. Since each color class of a proper
\chi^{\prime}(G)
-edge coloring of a graph
G
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
has exactly
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
edges, Conjecture
1
holds also for a graph
G
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, and Theorem
5.15
confirms the conjecture when
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
. Thus the remaining problem is to resolve the conjecture for a (non-bipartite) graph
G
of order
n
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)\in\{3,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-2\}
.

In addition, we obtained characterizations of graphs
G
with a fixed value of
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)
for some specific extremal and near-extremal values. Since not all of these characterizations are complete, we propose several problems, which would fill the missing parts of the descriptions.

Problem 7.1
.

Characterize the class of connected (regular) graphs
G
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=2
.

Since we did not obtain a structural characterization of the connected graphs with the chromatic edge stability index
1
, we can probably not expect a complete structural characterization of Problem
7.1
, which seems even harder. However, if we restrict to regular graphs, it might be possible to find a nice description of such graphs, similarly as for regular graphs with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=1
in Theorem
3.8
.

The following problem arises from Section
5
, addressing the Class
1
graphs
G
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
for which no description was found.

Problem 7.2
.

Characterize the connected Class
1
graphs
G
of order
n
with
es_{\chi^{\prime}}(G)=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor-1
.

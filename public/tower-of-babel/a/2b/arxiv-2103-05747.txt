Asymptotic posterior normality of the generalized extreme value distribution
Likun Zhang, Benjamin A. Shaby

Abstract
The univariate generalized extreme value (GEV) distribution is the most commonly used tool for analyzing the properties of rare events. The ever greater utilization of Bayesian methods for extreme value analysis warrants detailed theoretical investigation, which has thus far been underdeveloped. Even the most basic asymptotic results are difficult to obtain because the GEV fails to satisfy standard regularity conditions. Here, we prove that the posterior distribution of the GEV parameter vector, given n n independent and identically distributed samples, converges in distribution to a trivariate normal distribution. The proof necessitates analyzing integrals of the GEV likelihood function over the entire parameter space, which requires considerable care because the support of the GEV density depends on the parameters in complicated ways.



Keywords
non-regular parametric families; posterior consistency

1
Introduction

The family of generalized extreme value (GEV) distributions is a standard tool for studying the tail behavior of variables in fields ranging from finance to climate science, and Bayesian inference for the GEV family has become increasingly popular
(
Coles and Tawn 1996
;
Martins and Stedinger 2000
;
Northrop and Attalides 2016
, e.g.)
. Although the GEV was introduced almost a century ago
(
Fisher and Tippett 1928
)
, classical asymptotic properties that hold for common parametric families such as the exponential families still remain to be confirmed under the GEV, which fails to meet standard regularity conditions. In this paper, we elucidate an important large-sample property of Bayesian inference based on independent GEV samples, and show that the posterior distribution, paired with a sufficiently regular prior, can be well-approximated by a normal distribution as the sample size
n
increases.

Consider a sequence of independent samples
\mathbf{y}_{n}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
from a generic parametric distribution
P_{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
, for
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\Phi\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
, with density
p(y\,|\,\boldsymbol{\phi})
in regard to a dominating measure
\mathcal{P}
on some measurable space
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})
. Under common regularity conditions
(
Fisher 1922
;
Cramér 1946
;
Wald 1949
, e.g.)
, the asymptotic normality of the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE)
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
, which maximizes the joint log-likelihood
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\phi})
or solves its associated score equations
\nabla L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\boldsymbol{0}
, is well-established. Analogous results hold for Bayesian estimates. For parametric families that satisfy common regularity conditions, the information contained in the samples
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}
overwhelms the prior density
\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and the posterior density

\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\phi})\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})\Big/C_{n},\quad C_{n}=\int_{\Phi}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\phi})\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})d\boldsymbol{\phi},

(1.1)

will concentrate at
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
in the form of a normal density
(
Bernstein 1927
;
Von Mises 1931
)
.

However, as a result of the non-regularity of the GEV, frequentist asymptotic results have only recently been established, and Bayesian asymptotic behavior remains unexplored. Treating
\boldsymbol{Y}_{n}
as a sequence of maxima over finitely-sized blocks that will become GEV distributed as the block size goes to infinity,
Dombry 2015
proved the existence and strong consistency of the
local
MLE that maximizes the GEV log-likelihood in a pre-specified closed neighborhood of the true parameter
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
, for
\xi_{0}>-1
.
Bücher and Segers 2018
and
Dombry and Ferreira 2019
established the asymptotic normality of the local MLE when
\xi_{0}>-1/2
. Under a simpler setting where
\boldsymbol{Y}_{n}
consists of independent samples from the GEV distribution,
Bücher and Segers 2017
proved a
O_{p}(1/\sqrt{n})
rate of convergence of the local MLE to
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
and showed the central limit relations hold uniformly over the pre-specified compact set for
\xi_{0}>-1/2
.

However, interesting Bayesian quantities often require integrals over the entire parameter space. Therefore, theoretical analysis requires moving away from the vicinity of
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}
and examining the likelihood function globally.
Zhang and Shaby 2021
explored the global properties of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\theta})
and showed that the local MLE defined in
Bücher and Segers 2017
actually gives the global maximum for the likelihood function when
n
is sufficiently large. They showed that, informally, the likelihood function becomes highly peaked around the local MLE, and that it decays quickly as
||\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}||\rightarrow\infty
. We will use this result to approximate the normalizing constant
C_{n}
defined in (
1.1
).

If
\mathbf{y}_{n}
is sampled from a GEV distribution, the elucidation of the aforementioned asymptotic properties is not trivial. The GEV
cumulative distribution function
can be parameterized by
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\tau,\mu,\xi)
:

P_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(y)=\begin{cases}\exp\left\{-\left[1+\xi\left(\frac{y-\mu}{\tau}\right)\right]^{-1/\xi}\right\},&\xi\neq 0,\\
\exp\left\{-\exp\left[-\frac{y-\mu}{\tau}\right]\right\},&\xi=0,\end{cases}

for
1+\xi(y-\mu)/\tau>0
when
\xi\neq 0
, with scale parameter
\tau>0
, location parameter
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and shape parameter
\xi\in\mathbb{R}

(
Mises 1936
;
Jenkinson 1955
)
. Since the support of the GEV distribution depends on
\boldsymbol{\theta}
, it is especially challenging not only to establish the existence and consistency of its MLE, but also to derive the asymptotic normality of the posterior. For the remainder of the paper, we use
\boldsymbol{\phi}
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}
to denote the parameter vectors of a generic family of distributions and the GEV family, respectively.

In our main result on posterior asymptotic normality, we assume a class of prior distributions that is general enough to accommodate most cases of interest. This assumption is not critical, however, as other classes of priors would yield identical results, after slight modification of the proofs. Practical recommendations for choices of prior distributions is a nuanced topic that is well beyond the scope of the current work.

2
Main results

Our main result is establishing the asymptotic normality of the posterior distribution, given i.i.d. samples from the GEV distribution and a sufficiently regular prior distribution.

Many versions of sufficient conditions for asymptotic posterior normality have appeared in the literature, including
Le Cam 1958
,
Freedman 1963
,
Walker 1969
,
Bickel and Yahav 1969
,
Chao 1970
and
van der Vaart 2000
. While these conditions have different restrictions on the sample space
\mathcal{X}
, the parameter space
\Phi
, and
p(y\,|\,\boldsymbol{\phi})
, they mostly require the support
\{y:p(y\,|\,\boldsymbol{\phi})>0\}
to be independent of
\boldsymbol{\phi}
, as is the case for the regularity conditions imposed by
Cramér 1946
and
Wald 1949
for MLE. The GEV distribution clearly violates this assumption.

Our results remain valid under any fairly regular prior distribution. To make this concrete, we define one of many possible classes of priors that is compatible with the theory we present below.

Condition 1
.

Let
\pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})
be any continuous proper prior density function with support on
\Theta=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\tau,\mu,\xi):\tau>0,\mu\in\mathbb{R},\xi>-1/2\}
or an improper prior density function that satisfies
\pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})=g(\xi)/\tau
, where
g
is bounded on any interval
[-1/2,c]
,
c>-1/2
, and is regularly varying at infinity with index
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
; that is,
g(t\xi)/g(\xi)\rightarrow t^{\alpha}
as
\xi\rightarrow\infty
.

We are now ready to state the following main result:

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots
are independently sampled from a GEV distribution
P_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}}
with
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}\in\Theta=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}:\tau>0,\xi>-1/2\}
, and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}
is the local MLE based on
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}
. Let
L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n})
be the Hessian of the log-likelihood
L_{n}
at
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}
and
\Sigma_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n})=\{-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n})\}^{-1}
. Paired with a prior
\pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})
that satisfies Condition
1
,
the posterior distribution of
\Sigma_{n}^{-1/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n})(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n})
converges in distribution to a trivariate standard normal distribution. That is,

\mathcal{P}\left(\left.\Sigma_{n}^{-1/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n})(\boldsymbol{\theta}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n})\leq\boldsymbol{a}\,\right\rvert\boldsymbol{Y}_{n}\right)\rightarrow\Psi(\boldsymbol{a}),\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

(2.1)

P_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}}
-almost surely for all
\boldsymbol{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\Psi(\cdot)
is the distribution function of the trivariate standard normal distribution.

Again, Condition
1
is sufficient but not necessary for asymptotic posterior normality, and other large classes of priors will also be sufficient. We impose this condition on the priors because it simplifies the proof and it is fairly common for any model with location and scale parameters. An important special case is the form
\pi(\tau,\mu,\xi)\propto 1/\tau
, which is invariant under reparametrization. Under the GEV model, the corresponding maximal data information (MDI)
(
Zellner 1971
)
, and Jeffreys
(
Jeffreys 1961
)
priors can both be factorized into
g(\xi)/\tau

(
Northrop and Attalides 2016
)
, although
g(\xi)
increases without limit at
-1/2
and
\infty
for the Jeffreys prior and consequently the Jeffreys prior is not permissible for any sample size
n
. Hence the tail heaviness of
g(\xi)
needs to be controlled to ensure posterior propriety and asymptotic normality. In
Zhang and Shaby 2024
, the performances of the ruled-based reference priors
(
Berger et al. 2009
)
, MDI priors and the Beta priors were examined extensively, and recommendations were also provided on the choice of prior for the GEV model according to the use case and the tail-heaviness of the observations.

For the priors that satisfy Condition
1
, it is possible to study the properties of
C_{n}
defined in (
1.1
), and then verify non-standard sets of sufficient conditions for asymptotic posterior normality which admit non-regular families of densities and cope with challenges posed by the support. Examples of such results include
Dawid 1970
, who restricts
\Phi\subseteq\mathbb{R}
;
Heyde and Johnstone 1979
, who focus on stochastic processes with
\Phi\subseteq\mathbb{R}
;
Chen 1985
, who extends
\Phi
to be in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
; and
Ghosal et al. 1995
, whose posterior convergence is more general and includes non-normal limits. In this paper, we will work with the conditions proposed by
Chen 1985
due to their generality and simplicity.

In Section
3
, we describe the conditions in
Chen 1985
and explain heuristically their respective roles in the deduction of asymptotic posterior normality, and compare them with other sets of conditions in the literature. In Section
4
, we utilize the asymptotic properties of the likelihood function from
Zhang and Shaby 2021
to prove the first two conditions of
Chen 1985
. We then verify the third and last condition, and carefully prove that the contribution to the integral of
C_{n}
outside any pre-specified compact neighborhood
is negligible
asymptotically. In Section
6
, we summarize the ancillary results that we derived in the process of checking
Chen 1985
’s conditions, and discuss how the asymptotic posterior normality might be used to derive ruled-based noninformative priors for the family of GEV distributions.

3
Sufficient conditions for asymptotic posterior normality

Chen 1985
established conditions that lead to asymptotic posterior normality for any generic family of distributions parameterized by
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\Phi\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Denote
B_{r}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\prime}\in\Phi:||\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{\phi}||<r\}
as a neighborhood of
\boldsymbol{\phi}
, and
S(y)=\{\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\Theta:p(y\,|\,\boldsymbol{\phi})>0\}
as the set of plausible
\boldsymbol{\phi}
values under which
y
can be observed. Given a sequence of samples
\boldsymbol{Y}_{n}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
, define

\Omega_{n}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}S(Y_{i}).

Then the domain of the integral in (
1.1
) can be reduced to
\Omega_{n}
. Recall
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\phi})
, and we see
\Omega_{n}
is also the domain for the log-likelihood.

Most sufficient conditions in the literature for asymptotic posterior normality either require independence of the support from the parameters or have technical formulations that are difficult to verify.
For example, Theorem 10.1 in
van der Vaart 2000
asks for the existence of a sequence of uniformly consistent test functions, which is difficult to verify due to the non-regularity of the GEV
. The conditions in
Chen 1985
are appealing because there are only three basic conditions that provide ample operational flexibility.

Lemma 3.1

(Modified from
Chen 1985
)
.

Suppose with probability
1
for each
n>N
(
N>0
), there exists a strict local maximum point
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
for
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
such that the gradient
L^{\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
is a zero vector and the Hessian matrix
L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
is negative definite. Also suppose the prior density
\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})
is positive and continuous at
\boldsymbol{\phi}=\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}
, and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
tends to
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}
almost surely as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The three basic conditions presented below will ensure that the posterior converges in distribution to a trivariate normal distribution as
n
grows to infinity:

(C1)

The largest eigenvalue of
\Sigma_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})=\{-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}^{-1}
converges almost surely to
0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

(C2)

For any
\epsilon>0
, there almost surely exists
N_{\epsilon}>0
and
r>0
such that, for all
n>\max\{N,N_{\epsilon}\}
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in B_{r}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
,
L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
exists and satisfies

I-A(\epsilon)\leq L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})\{L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}^{-1}\leq I+A(\epsilon),

in which
I
is the
k\times k
identity matrix,
A(\epsilon)
is a
k\times k
positive semi-definite matrix whose largest eigenvalue tends to
0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
and
k
is the dimension of the space
\Phi
. Also, the Loewner order, i.e.,
A\leq B
if and only if
B-A
is positive semidefinite, is used here.

(C3)

The posterior distribution
\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
asymptotically concentrates around
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
. That is, for any
r>0
,

\int_{\Omega_{n}\setminus B_{r}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})}\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})d\boldsymbol{\phi}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{a.s.},\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

More specifically, under
(C1)
and
(C2)
,
(C3)
is the necessary and sufficient condition that (
2.1
) holds.

The original statement of
Chen 1985
calculates the gradient and Hessian of the posterior
\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
and approximates the posterior distribution by
N(\boldsymbol{m}_{n},\Sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{m}_{n}))
, where
\boldsymbol{m}_{n}
is the posterior local mode that solves
L^{\prime}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})+\{\log\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})\}^{\prime}=0
. However, his proof can easily be modified to apply to the MLE
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
, given that
\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})
is continuous at
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
is strongly consistent; see Appendix
A
for the modified proof.

We caution that the seemingly short list of conditions in Lemma
3.1
implicitly relies on several further assumptions, which are trivially satisfied for many continuous parametric families and appear explicitly in other works. For example, assumption (C1) in
Dawid 1970
requires
\Omega_{n}
to contain
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}
in its interior.
Chen 1985
essentially assumes this condition when he applies a Taylor expansion to approximate
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
near the mode
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
. Also, most sets of conditions assume the identifiability of the parameter; that is, different values of
\boldsymbol{\phi}
imply different distributions, which induces positive Kullback-Leibler divergence and the consistency of the MLE.
Chen 1985
circumvents this condition by assuming the existence of local MLE and the negative definiteness of the Hessian at the local MLE. Fortunately, the GEV family possesses both the required identifiability and a consistent local MLE.

Now we provide an overview of the role of each condition and explain heuristically how the asymptotic posterior normality is attained. Firstly,
(C1)
implies that the posterior density
\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
is highly peaked around
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
. This condition is also required by (A3) in
Heyde and Johnstone 1979
. The term
-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
is often referred to as the observed Fisher information matrix. If
-n^{-1}L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
tends to
I(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0})
almost surely, which is the case for many parametric families, then
(C1)
holds automatically.

Secondly, the main function of
(C2)
is to make sure
L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
behaves sufficiently smoothly for values of
\boldsymbol{\phi}
near
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
. This condition can also be found in (B4) of
Walker 1969
, (C11) of
Dawid 1970
and (A5) of
Heyde and Johnstone 1979
, while (IH1) in
Ghosal et al. 1995
is weaker and only assumes Lipschitz continuity of
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
in a pre-specified compact set. Expanding a Taylor series only to the first order under
(C2)
, we can use the Lagrange’s form of the remainder to obtain
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})=L_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})+(\boldsymbol{\phi}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})^{T}L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi}^{+}_{n})(\boldsymbol{\phi}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})/2
for some
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{n}^{+}
lying between
\boldsymbol{\phi}
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
. When
\boldsymbol{\phi}
is near
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
,
L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi}^{+}_{n})\approx L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
, and thus the posterior can be approximated as

\begin{split}\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})&=\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\exp\{\log\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})-\log\pi(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}\exp\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})-L_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}\\
&\approx c(\mathbf{y}_{n})\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\phi}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})^{T}\{-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}(\boldsymbol{\phi}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\right\},\end{split}

(3.1)

in which the constant
c(\mathbf{y}_{n})
becomes independent of the prior, as
(C1)
ensures that the observed information
-L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
dominates the prior information
-\pi^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
, i.e.,
|\pi^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})|/|L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})|\rightarrow 0
when
n\rightarrow\infty
. The approximation in (
3.1
) already confirms that
\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
behaves like a Gaussian kernel inside a small neighborhood of
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
.

Thirdly,
(C3)
concerns the global properties of
L_{n}
, which are needed to validate each approximation leading to (
3.1
) when moving away from
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
. In fact, under
(C1)
and
(C2)
, the consistency of the posterior, i.e.
(C3)
, is sufficient and necessary for that purpose; see Lemma
A.1
of Appendix
A
. In essence,
(C3)
is equivalent to the assertion that
\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})
converges in distribution to a distribution degenerate at
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}
, given the strong consistency of the local MLE. It is worth noting that the existence of a sequence of uniformly consistent tests required by
van der Vaart 2000
is used in effect to ensure
(C3)
of
Chen 1985
; see the proof of Theorem 10.1 therein. Moreover, the need for
(C3)
to assure the consistency of the Bayes estimators has also been stressed by
Freedman 1963
and
Diaconis and Freedman 1986
. This property holds in many regular parametric families under mild conditions, but it is again difficult to verify for the family of GEV distributions. In comparison, Equation (5) in
Walker 1969
, (C7) in
Dawid 1970
, (A4) in
Heyde and Johnstone 1979
and (IH2) in
Ghosal et al. 1995
all require uniform-boundedness of the tail behaviors in similar ways, and are stricter than
(C3)
. For example, Equation (5) in
Walker 1969
states

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{P}\left[\sup_{\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\Omega_{n}\setminus B_{r}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})}n^{-1}\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})-L_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}<-k(r)\right]=1,

(3.2)

where
k(r)
is a positive constant depending on
r
. Equation (
3.2
) is equivalent to (C3.1) in
Chen 1985
, which is sufficient for
(C3)
under
(C1)
and
(C2)
. Furthermore, (
3.2
) implies that there is no likelihood value of
\boldsymbol{\phi}
not near
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}
that is larger than that of the local MLE
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
. Meanwhile for the GEV likelihood,
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
is almost surely the unique maximum point in
B_{r}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
for all sufficiently large
n

(
Dombry 2015
, Proposition 2)
. Therefore, if (
3.2
) holds for the GEV family, the local maximizer
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}
is also the unique global maximizer.

Although
Zhang and Shaby 2021
established the global optimality of
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}
for the GEV, they did not verify (
3.2
) because it essentially requires global Hölder continuity of
n^{-1}L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
. They only showed the weaker property that
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})<L_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n})
for
\boldsymbol{\theta}\neq\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}
. However, we will use results contained therein to validate
(C1)
and
(C2)
, and then study the integral in
(C3)
directly.

4
Proof of asymptotic posterior normality for the GEV distributions

4.1
Steepness and smoothness

Since the score equations of the GEV log-likelihood only have roots if
\xi_{0}>-1
and second-order asymptotic properties needed for
(C2)
only hold if
\xi_{0}>-1/2
, we confine the parameter space to be
\Theta=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}:\tau>0,\xi>-1/2\}
. Following the notation in
Zhang and Shaby 2021
, we define
\beta=\beta(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\mu-\tau/\xi
and re-parameterize the GEV log-likelihood using the one-to-one mapping from
\boldsymbol{\theta}=(\tau,\mu,\xi)
to
\boldsymbol{\eta}=(\tau,\beta,\xi)
. Under the
\boldsymbol{\eta}
-parameterization, the log-likelihood function can be written as

L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=-n\log\tau+(\xi+1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log W_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta}),\quad\xi\neq 0,

(4.1)

where

W_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=1+\xi\left(\frac{Y_{i}-\mu}{\tau}\right)=\frac{\xi}{\tau}(Y_{i}-\beta).

(4.2)

The case
\xi=0
can also be unified in this formulation by continuity, as
W_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\rightarrow\exp\{-(Y_{i}-\mu)/\tau\}
as
\xi\rightarrow 0
. The advantage of re-parameterizing from
(\tau,\mu,\xi)
to
(\tau,\beta,\xi)
in this way is that it helps delineate the domain of the log-likelihood concisely:

\Omega_{n}=\{\boldsymbol{\eta}:\tau>0,\xi>-1/2\text{ and }\xi(Y_{i}-\beta)>0,\;i=1,\ldots,n\}.

Consequently,
\Omega_{n}
, which is also the domain of integral in (
1.1
), becomes two rectangular boxes on each side of the
\tau
-
\beta
plane at
\xi=0
. Since the determinant of Jacobian for the transformation from
\boldsymbol{\theta}
to
\boldsymbol{\eta}
is an identity matrix, proving the asymptotic posterior normality under
\boldsymbol{\theta}
-parameterization and
\boldsymbol{\eta}
-parameterization are equivalent. Additionally, applying the substitution
\beta=\mu-\tau/\xi
simplifies the integral in (
1.1
):

\int_{\Theta\cap\{\xi>0\}}\pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\exp\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\}d\boldsymbol{\theta}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{Y_{(1)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta})\exp\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\}d\tau d\beta d\xi,

and

\int_{\Theta\cap\{-1/2<\xi<0\}}\pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\exp\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\}d\boldsymbol{\theta}=\int_{-1/2}^{0}\int_{Y_{(n)}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta})\exp\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\}d\tau d\beta d\xi,

in which
Y_{(1)}
and
Y_{(n)}
denote the sample minimum and maximum, respectively. Note that the prior
\pi(\cdot)
defined by Condition
1
does not depend on
\mu
or
\beta
.

Appendix G in
Zhang and Shaby 2021
lists the exact form of the Hessian matrix of
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
, in which every element can be written as linear combinations of sums of the form

\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{-k-a/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\log^{b}W_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta}),

where
k,b=0,1,2
,
a=0,1
. For
(C1)
, we need to evaluate the Hessian
L^{\prime\prime}_{n}
at the local MLE, whose limiting form is given in the following result.

Lemma 4.1

(Proposition 4 in
Zhang and Shaby 2021
)
.

Suppose
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots
are independently sampled from
P_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}}
and let
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}=(\hat{\tau}_{n},\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{\xi}_{n})
, or equivalently
\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n}=(\hat{\tau}_{n},\hat{\mu}_{n}-\hat{\tau}_{n}/\hat{\xi}_{n},\hat{\xi}_{n})
, be the local MLE of
L_{n}
that is strongly consistent. Then for constants

6
Discussion

In this paper, we formally derive the asymptotic posterior normality for the family of GEV distributions under a large class of priors. Proposition
4.6
demonstrates that the posterior approximation can be easily obtained if the parameter space
\Theta
is compact, as is generally the case
(
van der Vaart 2000
, Chapter 10)
. The bulk of the technical difficulty lies in the evaluation of the integral of
\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\eta})\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta})
outside the neighborhood of
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}
, which is closely related to the elliptic integral equivalent to the Carlson
R
-function; see Lemma
C.1
. An interesting side result from the asymptotic posterior normality is that the lower bound in (
4.4
) is attained asymptotically. Therefore, it provides a novel way of estimating an integral that would be difficult to calculate otherwise.

Another major implication from the asymptotic posterior normality is the simplification of the derivation of the reference prior suggested by
Berger et al. 2009
. This class of rule-based priors attempts to maximize the information from the sample so that in a formal sense the prior has a minimal effect on posterior inference. The reference prior formulation is attractive in part because it overcomes the paradoxes of
Stein 1956
and
Dawid et al. 1973
, which may occur with flat priors and Jeffreys priors. In the general multivariate case, computing reference priors requires multiple applications of a difficult sequence of integrals. Under asymptotic posterior normality, however, the procedure can be streamlined into a rote algorithm requiring only the Fisher information matrix.

Appendix A
Lower bound of the normalizing constant

Lemma A.1
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
3.1
, conditions
(C1)
and
(C2)
together imply that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})|\Sigma_{n}|^{1/2}\leq(2\pi)^{-k/2}\qquad\text{a.s.},

(A.1)

where
\Sigma_{n}=\{-L_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}^{-1}
. The equality holds if and only if
(C3)
is true.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
, and let
n>\max\{N,N_{\epsilon}\}
and
r>0
as given in
(C2)
. Since
\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})
is positive and continuous at
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0}
, there exists
0<r_{0}<r
and
m(r_{0}),\;M(r_{0})>0
such that, for any
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{2}\in B_{r_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0})
,
m(r_{0})\leq\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{1})/\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{2})\leq M(r_{0})
. Also, both
m(r_{0})
and
M(r_{0})
tend to 1 as
r_{0}\rightarrow 0
.

By the strong consistency of
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
, there almost surely exists
N^{\prime}>0
such that for any
n>N^{\prime}
,
B_{r_{0}/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\subset B_{r_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0})\subset B_{r}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{0})
. Then, for
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in B_{r_{0}/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})
, a simple Taylor expansion produces

\begin{split}\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})&=\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\exp\{\log\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})-\log\pi(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}\exp\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})-L_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}\\
&=\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\{\pi(\boldsymbol{\phi})/\pi(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{\phi}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})^{T}L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{n}^{+})(\boldsymbol{\phi}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})\right\},\end{split}

where

L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})(I-A(\epsilon))\leq L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{n}^{+})\leq L^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})(I+A(\epsilon)),

for some
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{n}^{+}
lying between
\boldsymbol{\phi}
and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n}
. Now consider the posterior probability

P_{n}=\int_{B_{r_{0}/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})}\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\phi})d\boldsymbol{\phi}.

It can be bounded above by

P_{n}^{+}=\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})M(r_{0})|\Sigma_{n}|^{1/2}|I-A(\epsilon)|^{-1/2}\int_{|\boldsymbol{z}|<s_{n}}\exp(-\boldsymbol{z}^{t}\boldsymbol{z}/2)d\boldsymbol{z},

and bounded below by

P_{n}^{-}=\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})m(r_{0})|\Sigma_{n}|^{1/2}|I+A(\epsilon)|^{-1/2}\int_{|\boldsymbol{z}|<t_{n}}\exp(-\boldsymbol{z}^{t}\boldsymbol{z}/2)d\boldsymbol{z},

where
s_{n}=r_{0}\{1-\underline{a}(\epsilon)\}/(2\underline{\sigma}_{n})
and
t_{n}=r_{0}(1+\bar{a}(\epsilon))/(2\bar{\sigma}_{n})
, with
\underline{a}(\epsilon)
and
\underline{\sigma}_{n}
being the smallest eigenvalues of
A(\epsilon)
and
\Sigma_{n}
, and
\bar{a}(\epsilon)
and
\bar{\sigma}_{n}
being the largest eigenvalues of
A(\epsilon)
and
\Sigma_{n}
, respectively.

Because of
(C1)
, both
s_{n}
and
t_{n}
tend to
\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We can then establish

\begin{split}(2\pi)^{k/2}m(r_{0})&|I+A(\epsilon)|^{-1/2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})|\Sigma_{n}|^{1/2}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P_{n}\\
&\leq(2\pi)^{k/2}M(r_{0})|I-A(\epsilon)|^{-1/2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})|\Sigma_{n}|^{1/2}\end{split}

As
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
,
r_{0}\rightarrow 0
, and thus
M(r_{0})\rightarrow 1
. Moreover,
|I\pm A(\epsilon)|^{1/2}\rightarrow 1
,
m(r_{0})\rightarrow 1
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.
Because
P_{n}\leq 1
for all

, we can now conclude

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\pi_{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{n})|\Sigma_{n}|^{1/2}\leq(2\pi)^{-k/2},

where the equality holds if and only if
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P_{n}=1
.
∎

Lemma B.3
.

For all upper semi-continuous functions
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty)
that are upper-bounded,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{n}[f]\leq P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}[f]\qquad\text{a.s.}

Proof.

This is analogous to Lemma 3 in
Dombry 2015
, in which
(Y_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is treated as coming from a distribution in the domain of attraction of a GEV. Since a GEV distribution
P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}
is in its own domain of attraction, the block size sequence
m(n)
is 1 in our setting. Therefore, the condition
\lim_{m(n)\rightarrow\infty}m(n)=+\infty
is violated.

However, the proof in our simpler setting is similar. Define the empirical distribution function

\mathbb{F}_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{i}\leq y\}},\qquad y\in\mathbb{R},

and the corresponding empirical quantile function

\mathbb{F}^{-1}_{n}(u):=\inf\{y\in\mathbb{R}:\mathbb{F}_{n}(y)\geq u\},\qquad u\in(0,1).

We can show that
\mathbb{F}^{-1}_{n}(u)=Y_{(\lceil nu\rceil-1)}
, where
\lceil\cdot\rceil
is the ceiling function. Since the distribution function
P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}
is differentiable at
P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}^{-1}(u)
for any
u\in(0,1)
, we have

Y_{(\lceil nu\rceil-1)}\rightarrow P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}^{-1}(u)\qquad\text{a.s.}

By the upper semi-continuity of
f
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(\mathbb{F}^{-1}_{n}(u))\leq f(P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}^{-1}(u)).

(B.3)

Meanwhile, we use the relation
\mathbb{P}_{n}[f]=\int_{0}^{1}f(\mathbb{F}^{-1}_{n}(u))du
, and
P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}[f]=\int_{0}^{1}f(P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}^{-1}(u))du
. Because
f
is upper-bounded, we can apply Fatou’s lemma to derive

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{1}f(\mathbb{F}^{-1}_{n}(u))du\leq\int_{0}^{1}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(\mathbb{F}^{-1}_{n}(u))du\leq\int_{0}^{1}f(P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}^{-1}(u))du,

with the last inequality due to (
B.3
).
∎

Lemma B.4

If
\xi_{0}\neq 0

(B.4)

C.2
respectively. Furthermore, let
z_{n}=(n-1)\log\{n(\xi_{0}+r_{1})\}/\{n(\xi_{0}+r_{1})\}
, and we have

\begin{split}\int_{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}^{\infty}\xi^{-n+\frac{3}{2}}&(n\xi)^{-(n-1)/(n\xi)}d\xi\leq\int_{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}^{\infty}\xi^{-n+\frac{3}{2}}\{n(\xi_{0}+r_{1})\}^{-(n-1)/(n\xi)}d\xi\\
&=\{(\xi_{0}+r_{1})z_{n}\}^{-n+5/2}\gamma(n-5/2,z_{n})\\
&<\frac{7}{\sqrt{n-5/2}}\{(\xi_{0}+r_{1})z_{n}\}^{-n+5/2}\exp\{-z_{n}+(n-5/2)\log z_{n}\}\\
&=\frac{7}{\sqrt{n-5/2}}(\xi_{0}+r_{1})^{-n+5/2}\exp\{-z_{n}\},\end{split}

(C.3)

where the penultimate inequality follows from Lemma
C.3
. Combining the previous two inequalities and the fact that
\Gamma(n)\leq n^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\exp\{-n+1\}
,

\begin{split}C_{n}^{(2)}&\leq\frac{140\Gamma(n)n^{-n+1}(\xi_{0}+r_{1})^{-n+5/2}}{\prod_{j=2}^{n}\delta_{j}}\sqrt{\frac{n}{(n-1)(n-5/2)}}\exp\{-z_{n}\}\\
&\leq\frac{140(\xi_{0}+r_{1})^{-n+5/2}}{\prod_{j=2}^{n}\delta_{j}}\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{n-5/2}\exp\left\{-z_{n}-n+1\right\}.\end{split}

Finally, we utilize Lemma
4.5
to deduce

\begin{split}\frac{C_{n}^{(2)}}{C_{n}}\leq&M_{1}\frac{n^{7/2}}{n-5/2}\left(1+\frac{r_{1}}{\xi_{0}}\right)^{-n+5/2}\left(\frac{\hat{\xi}_{n}}{\xi_{0}}\right)^{n}\times\left\{(Y_{(1)}-\hat{\beta}_{n})\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{Y_{(j)}-\hat{\beta}_{n}}{\delta_{j}}\right\}\times\\
&\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{1/\hat{\xi}_{n}}_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})\right\}\times\exp\left\{-z_{n}\right\},\end{split}

(C.4)

where

M_{1}=\frac{280e\xi^{5/2}_{0}|I(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0})|^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0})}.

Now it suffices to prove that the right-hand side of (
C.4
) converges almost surely to
0
. Lemma
C.4
gives

(Y_{(1)}-\hat{\beta}_{n})\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{Y_{(j)}-\hat{\beta}_{n}}{\delta_{j}}<\frac{\tau_{0}\exp(4n/\xi_{0})}{2^{n}\xi_{0}}\qquad\text{a.s.},

and Lemma
4.1
ensures

\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{1/\hat{\xi}_{n}}_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})=\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\hat{\xi}_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log W_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})\right\}\sim\exp(n\gamma)\qquad\text{a.s.}

The dominating term on the right-hand side of (
C.4
) is

\exp\left[-n\left\{\log\left(1+\frac{r_{1}}{\xi_{0}}\right)-\log\frac{\hat{\xi}_{n}}{\xi_{0}}-\frac{4}{\xi_{0}}+\log 2-\gamma\right\}\right].

Since
\hat{\xi}_{n}\rightarrow\xi_{0}
almost surely and
r_{1}
is predetermined to satisfy
\log(1+r_{1}/\xi_{0})>4/\xi_{0}-\log 2+\gamma
, this quantity must converge almost surely to
0
, which immediately proves that
C_{n}^{(2)}/C_{n}\rightarrow 0
almost surely for when
g(\xi)
is uniform.

If
g
is regularly varying at infinity with index
\alpha
, then
g(\xi)=\mathcal{L}(\xi)\xi^{\alpha}
with
\mathcal{L}(\xi)
being a slowly varying function at infinity. The inequality in (
C.3
) can be modified using Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

\begin{split}\int_{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}^{\infty}&g(\xi)\xi^{-n+\frac{3}{2}}(n\xi)^{-(n-1)/(n\xi)}d\xi=\int_{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}(\xi)\xi^{-n+\frac{3}{2}+\alpha}(n\xi)^{-(n-1)/(n\xi)}d\xi\\
&\leq\left\{\int_{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}^{\infty}\mathcal{L}^{2}(\xi)\xi^{-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}d\xi\right\}^{1/2}\cdot\left\{\int_{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}^{\infty}\xi^{-2n+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}5}+2\alpha}(n\xi)^{-2(n-1)/(n\xi)}d\xi\right\}^{1/2}.\end{split}

(C.5)

From the Karamata’s Theorem
(
Resnick 2008
, p. 17)
, we know the first term in the last line is a finite constant because
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\xi)\xi^{-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}\in RV_{-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}2}}
, while the second term can be relaxed in the same way as (
C.3
):

\begin{split}\left\{\int_{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}^{\infty}\right.&\left.\xi^{-2n+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}5}+2\alpha}(n\xi)^{-2(n-1)/(n\xi)}d\xi\right\}^{1/2}\leq\left[\int_{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}^{\infty}\xi^{-2n+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}5}+2\alpha}\{n(\xi_{0}+r_{1})\}^{-2(n-1)/(n\xi)}d\xi\right]^{1/2}\\
&=\{2(\xi_{0}+r_{1})z_{n}\}^{-n+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3}+\alpha}\gamma^{1/2}(2n-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}6}-2\alpha,2z_{n})\\
&<\{2(\xi_{0}+r_{1})z_{n}\}^{-n+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3}+\alpha}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{7}}{(2n-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}6}-2\alpha)^{1/4}}\exp\{-z_{n}+(n-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3}-\alpha)\log(2z_{n})\}\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{7}}{(2n-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}6}-2\alpha)^{1/4}}(\xi_{0}+r_{1})^{-n+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}3}+\alpha}\exp\{-z_{n}\},\end{split}

where
z_{n}
is defined in (
C.3
) and the penultimate inequality again follows from Lemma
C.3
. Plug this result back into (
C.5
), and we see that the crucial dominating terms in (
C.3
) are retained when
g
is regularly varying at infinity with index
\alpha
. Therefore, the remaining proof can be passed through and we have
C_{n}^{(2)}/C_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow 0
.

∎

Lemma C.1
.

Suppose
\delta_{j}>0
,
j=1,\ldots,n
, and
\min_{j}\delta_{j}<\max_{j}\delta_{j}
. Then for any
\xi>0
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+\delta_{j}s)^{-1-\xi}ds<\frac{B(n,n\xi)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}}.

(C.6)

Proof.

Integral of the form (
C.6
) is an elliptic integral which is equivalent to the Carlson
R
-function, the Dirichlet average of the power series
x^{n}
. More specifically,

R_{-n}(\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{\delta})=\frac{1}{B(n,c-n)}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+\delta_{j}s)^{-b_{i}}ds,

where
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is the beta function,
\boldsymbol{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
, and
c=\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}>n
. For the technical details of the Carlson
R
-function and its equivalence to the integrals of the form (
C.6
), see Chapter 6 in
Carlson 1977
.

Carlson 1966
provides useful inequalities for the hypergeometric
R
-function. In particular, when
n<c
,

R_{-n}(\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{\delta})<K_{-n}(\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{\delta}):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}^{-nb_{i}/c}.

In our case,
b_{j}=1+\xi
,
c=n+n\xi
and
nb_{i}/c=1
, showing that (
C.6
) holds.
∎

Lemma C.2
.

Suppose
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 2}
is a positive sequence that converges to
b>0
. Then there exists
N>0
such that for any
n>N
and
\xi>b_{n}
,

B\left(n-1,\frac{n-1}{n\xi}\right)<20\sqrt{\frac{n\xi}{n-1}}(n\xi)^{-\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}.

Proof.

By Stirling approximation,
\Gamma(x)=\frac{1}{x}\sqrt{2\pi x}\left(\frac{x}{e}\right)^{x}e^{\theta(x)/(12x)}
, where
\theta(x)\in(0,1)
and
x>0
. Therefore

\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{n\xi}\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\pi n\xi}{n-1}}\left(\frac{n-1}{n\xi}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}\exp\left\{-\frac{n-1}{n\xi}+1\right\}.

Since
\{b_{n}\}_{n\geq 2}
has a positive limit, there exists
N>0
such that
1/(n\xi)<1/(nb_{n})<1
for any
n>N
and
\xi>b_{n}
. Apply the Stirling approximation again, and we have

\begin{split}\frac{\Gamma(n-1)}{\Gamma\left(n-1+\frac{n-1}{n\xi}\right)}&\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1+\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}{n-1}}\left(\frac{n-1}{n-1+\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}\right)^{n-1+\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}(n-1)^{-\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}\exp\left\{\frac{n-1}{n\xi}+1\right\}\\
&<\sqrt{2}\left(1-\frac{\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}{n-1+\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}\right)^{n-1+\frac{1}{\xi\log n}}(n-1)^{-\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}\exp\left\{\frac{n-1}{n\xi}+1\right\}\\
&<\sqrt{2}(n-1)^{-\frac{n-1}{n\xi}}\exp\left\{\frac{n-1}{n\xi}+1\right\}.\end{split}

The last line holds by noticing that
(1-1/x)^{x}<1
for any
x>1
.

Since
2e^{2}\sqrt{\pi}<20
, the desired upper bound can be obtained by combining the previous two inequalities.

∎

Lemma C.3
.

Suppose
\{z_{n}\}_{n\geq 2}
and
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 2}
are
two positive sequences that satisfy
z_{n}=o(n)
and
a_{n}=O(n)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Then
z_{n}/a_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. There exists
N>0
such that for any
n>N
,

\gamma\left(a_{n},z_{n}\right)<\frac{7}{\sqrt{a_{n}}}\exp\{-z_{n}+a_{n}\log z_{n}\},

where
\gamma(a,z):=\int_{0}^{z}t^{a-1}e^{-t}dt
is the incomplete gamma function.

Proof.

We use results from
Temme 1996
concerning the uniform expansion of the incomplete gamma function
\gamma(a,z)
as
a\rightarrow\infty
. Denote
\lambda=z/a
and
\eta^{2}/2=\lambda-1-\log\lambda
, in which
\text{sgn}(\eta)=\text{sgn}(\lambda-1)
. It follows that

\frac{\gamma(a,z)}{\Gamma(a)}=\frac{1}{2}\text{erfc}(-\eta\sqrt{a/2})-S_{a}(\eta),

(C.7)

where the residual term
S_{a}(\eta)
is small and dominated by the error function
\text{erfc}(\cdot)
, which is defined by

\text{erf}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{z}e^{-t^{2}}dt,\;\;\text{erfc}(z)=1-\text{erf}z.

In our case,
a_{n}=O(n)
,
a_{n}\gg z_{n}
. It is clear that
\lambda_{n}=z_{n}/a_{n}<1
except for finite terms. By the uniformity of the expansion (
C.7
), there exists
N_{1}>0
such that for any
n>N_{1}
,

\begin{split}\frac{\gamma\left(a_{n},z_{n}\right)}{\Gamma(a_{n})}&<\text{erfc}(-\eta_{n}\sqrt{a_{n}/2})=\text{erfc}(\sqrt{a_{n}(\lambda_{n}-1-\log\lambda_{n})})\\
&\leq\exp\left\{-a_{n}(\lambda_{n}-1+\log a_{n}-\log z_{n})\right\}.\end{split}

(C.8)

The last inequality utilizes the fact that
\text{erfc}(z)\leq\exp(-z^{2})
for
z>0
.

By Stirling’s approximation,

\Gamma(a_{n})\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{a_{n}}}\exp\{a_{n}(-1+\log a_{n})+1\}.

Plug this bound into (
C.8
), and we get

\gamma\left(a_{n},z_{n}\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{a_{n}}}\exp\left\{-a_{n}(\lambda_{n}-\log z_{n})+1\right\},

which gives the desired upper bound because
\sqrt{2\pi}e<7
.
∎

Lemma C.4
.

Let
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle iid}}{{\sim}}P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}
, and
\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n}
is the local MLE of
L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
that is strongly consistent. Then

\mathcal{P}\left(\exists N\text{ such that for }n>N,\quad\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{Y_{(j)}-\hat{\beta}_{n}}{Y_{(j)}-Y_{(1)}}<\frac{\tau_{0}\exp(4n/\xi_{0})}{2^{n}\xi_{0}(Y_{(1)}-\hat{\beta}_{n})}\right)=1.

(C.9)

Proof.

At first, one may think right away that the product term in the brackets tends to
1
given that
Y_{(1)}\rightarrow\beta_{0}
and
\hat{\beta}_{n}\rightarrow\beta_{0}
almost surely. However, this is not as trivial as it seems because of the different rates of these two convergences and the asymptotic spacings between the order statistics.

(I)
First we prove that

\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\xi_{0}\left(Y_{(j)}-Y_{(1)}\right)}{\tau_{0}}\geq\exp\left(n\xi_{0}\gamma-\frac{4n}{\xi_{0}}\right)\qquad\text{a.s.}

(C.10)

Denote
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})=(P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}(Y_{1}),\ldots,P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}(Y_{n}))
. Then
U_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, can be regarded as a sequence of independent uniform random variables. Since

P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}}(y)=\exp\left[-\left\{\frac{\xi_{0}}{\tau_{0}}(y-\beta_{0})\right\}^{-1/\xi_{0}}\right],

we can write

\begin{split}Y_{(j)}-Y_{(1)}&=(Y_{(j)}-\beta_{0})-(Y_{(1)}-\beta_{0})\\
&=\frac{\tau_{0}}{\xi_{0}}\left\{\left(-\log U_{(j)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-\left(-\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}\right\},\end{split}

(C.11)

Meanwhile, recall that
|\log x-\log y|\leq|x-y|/\min(x,y)
. We have

\begin{split}\log&\left(-\log U_{(j)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-\log\left\{\left(-\log U_{(j)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-\left(-\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}\right\}\leq\\
&\frac{\left(-\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}}{\left(-\log U_{(j)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-\left(-\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}}=\frac{1}{\left(\log U_{(j)}/\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-1}.\end{split}

(C.12)

Combine (
C.11
) and (
C.12
) to obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\log\frac{\xi_{0}\left(Y_{(j)}-Y_{(1)}\right)}{\tau_{0}}&=\sum_{j=2}^{n}\log\left\{\left(-\log U_{(j)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-\left(-\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}\right\}\\
&\geq\sum_{j=2}^{n}\log\left(-\log U_{(j)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(\log U_{(j)}/\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-1}.\end{split}

(C.13)

Next we further relax the lower bound in (
C.13
). On one hand,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\log\left(-\log U_{(j)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(-\log U_{i}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-\frac{\log\left(-\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}}{n}\\
&\rightarrow E\log(-\log U)^{-\xi_{0}}-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\left(\log n\right)^{-\xi_{0}}}{n}=\xi_{0}\gamma\qquad\text{a.s.}\end{split}

(C.14)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
C.13
), we begin with Theorem 2 of
Chung 1949
,

\mathcal{P}\left(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbb{F}_{n}(t)-F(t)|}{\sqrt{2^{-1}n\log_{2}n}}=1\right)=1

for any continuous distribution function
F
with
\mathbb{F}_{n}
as its empirical distribution function. Equivalently, when
n
is very large, the classic location of
U_{(j)}
is at
r/n
, and uniformly

\left|U_{(j)}-\frac{j}{n}\right|\leq\frac{\log_{2}n}{2n},\;j=1,\ldots,n.

Therefore, there almost surely exists
N_{1}>0
such that for all
n>N_{1}
,

1\leq\frac{\log U_{(j)}}{\log U_{(1)}}\leq 1-\frac{\log j}{2\log n},\;j=2,\ldots,n.

It follows that

\begin{split}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(\log U_{(j)}/\log U_{(1)}\right)^{-\xi_{0}}-1}&\leq\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\{1-\log j/(2\log n)\}^{-\xi_{0}}-1}\\
&\leq\frac{2\log n}{\xi_{0}}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\log j},\end{split}

(C.15)

where the last inequality holds because
(1-x)^{-\xi_{0}}-1\geq\xi_{0}x
for
x\in(0,1)
and
\xi_{0}>0
.

Plugging (
C.14
) and (
C.15
) back into (
C.13
) shows that there almost surely exists
N_{2}>0
such that for all
n>N_{2}
,

\sum_{j=2}^{n}\log\frac{\xi_{0}\left(Y_{(j)}-Y_{(1)}\right)}{\tau_{0}}\geq n\xi_{0}\gamma-\frac{2\log n}{\xi_{0}}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1}{\log j}.

Since
\sum_{j=2}^{n}1/\log j\leq\int_{2}^{n+1}(1/\log x)dx=\text{li}(n+1)-\text{li}(2)<2n/\log n
, where
\text{li}(\cdot)
is the logarithmic integral function, it follows that

\sum_{j=2}^{n}\log\frac{\xi_{0}\left(Y_{(j)}-Y_{(1)}\right)}{\tau_{0}}\geq n\xi_{0}\gamma-\frac{4n}{\xi_{0}},

which is exactly (
C.10
) after applying the exponential function on both sides of the previous inequality.

(II)
Now we prove (
C.9
). Lemma
4.1
ensures

\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\xi_{0}(Y_{i}-\hat{\beta}_{n})}{\tau_{0}}\right\}^{1/n}=\exp\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\frac{\xi_{0}(Y_{i}-\hat{\beta}_{n})}{\tau_{0}}\right\}\rightarrow\exp(\xi_{0}\gamma)\qquad\text{a.s.}

(C.16)

Thus there almost surely exists
N_{3}>0
such that for all
n>N_{3}
,

\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\xi_{0}(Y_{(j)}-\hat{\beta}_{n})}{\tau_{0}}<\left\{\frac{\exp(\xi_{0}\gamma)}{2}\right\}^{n}\frac{\tau_{0}}{\xi_{0}(Y_{(1)}-\hat{\beta}_{n})}.

To sum up, we combine the previous inequality with (
C.10
) to conclude that there almost surely exists
N=\max\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\}
such that for all
n>N
,

\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{Y_{(j)}-\hat{\beta}_{n}}{Y_{(j)}-Y_{(1)}}<\frac{\tau_{0}\exp(4n/\xi_{0})}{2^{n}\xi_{0}(Y_{(1)}-\hat{\beta}_{n})},

which ends the proof of this lemma.
∎

C.2
Proof that
C_{n}^{(3)}
is negligible

For
C_{n}^{(3)}
, we utilize the moments of a generalized Pareto distribution. Suppose
X\sim\text{GP}(\kappa,\tau)
with density

f(x)=\frac{1}{\tau}\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{\tau}x\right)^{-1-1/\kappa},\quad x>0.

The moment generating function is

M_{X}(t)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(t\tau)^{m}}{\prod_{i=0}^{m}(1-i\kappa)}.

Then

EX^{m}=M_{X}^{(m)}(0)=\frac{m!\tau^{m}}{\prod_{i=0}^{m}(1-i\kappa)},\text{ where }m\kappa<1.

(C.17)

Proof of
C_{n}^{(3)}
being negligible.

Fix
r_{2}>1
. Denote
\tilde{\beta}=\beta_{0}-r_{2}
,
\tilde{\tau}=\tau_{0}+r_{3}
and
\tilde{\delta}_{i}=Y_{i}-\tilde{\beta}
. Since
Y_{(1)}>\beta_{0}
, we know
Y_{(j)}-\tilde{\beta}>1
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. Similarly as in (
C.1
), we apply substitutions
t=\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\}\tau^{1/\xi}
and
s=(\tilde{\beta}-\beta)^{-1}
to get

\begin{split}C_{n}^{(3)}&=\int_{\Omega_{n}^{3}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\eta})\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta})d\boldsymbol{\eta}\\
&=\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\beta_{0}-r_{2}}g(\xi)\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}+r_{3}}\tau^{n/\xi-1}\exp\left\{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}W^{-1/\xi}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}d\tau d\beta d\xi\\
&=\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\tilde{\beta}}\frac{g(\xi)\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\xi^{-1}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}^{n}}\gamma\left(n,(\tau_{0}+r_{3})^{1/\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right)d\beta d\xi\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\xi)\Gamma(n)\xi^{-n+1}\frac{s^{n-2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\tilde{\delta}_{i}s)^{-{1}/{\xi}-1}}{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1+\tilde{\delta}_{i}s)^{-1/\xi}\right\}^{n}}dsd\xi.\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\xi)\Gamma(n)\xi^{-n+1}\frac{s^{n-2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\tilde{\delta}_{i}s)^{-{1}/{(\xi\log n)}-1}}{n^{n-n/\log n}}dsd\xi.\end{split}

(C.18)

The penultimate line is due to
\gamma(n,\cdot)\leq\Gamma(n)
. The last line is obtained similarly to (
C.2
) while raising both sides of the inequality by
n-n/\log n
.

By
Mitrinović 1964
,

\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\tilde{\delta}_{i}s)\geq(1+\bar{\delta}_{n}s)^{n},\text{ where }\tilde{\delta}_{i}>0,\;\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{i}=\bar{\delta}_{n}^{n}.

Thus, (
C.17
) with parameters
(\kappa,\tau)
such that
1/\kappa=n-1+n/(\xi\log n)
and
\kappa/\tau=\bar{\delta}_{n}
gives

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}&s^{n-2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\tilde{\delta}_{i}s)^{-{1}/{(\xi\log n)}-1}ds\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-2}(1+\bar{\delta}_{n}s)^{-n-n/(\xi\log n)}ds\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(n-1)}{(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{j})^{1-1/n}}\cdot\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}\{n-1+n/(\xi\log n)-i\}}.\end{split}

Plug this result back to the right-hand side of (
C.18
) while letting
M_{2}>0
be the upper bound of
g(\xi)
in
(-1/2,\xi_{0}+r_{1})
to obtain

\begin{split}C_{n}^{(3)}&\leq\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)e^{n}}{n^{n}(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{j})^{1-1/n}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\frac{g(\xi)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1+n\xi i/\log n)}d\xi\\
&\leq\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)e^{n}}{n^{n}(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{j})^{1-1/n}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\frac{M_{2}}{(1+n\Gamma^{1/(n-1)}(n)\xi/\log n)^{n-1}}d\xi\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)e^{n}}{n^{n}(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{j})^{1-1/n}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{n}\frac{M_{2}}{(n-2)\Gamma^{1/(n-1)}(n)}\times\\
&\qquad\left[1-\frac{1}{(1+n\Gamma^{1/(n-1)}(n)(\xi_{0}+r)/\log n)^{n-1}}\right]\\
&\sim\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)e^{n}}{n^{n}(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{j})^{1-1/n}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{n}\frac{M_{2}e}{n(n-2)}.\end{split}

The third-to-last line is obtained by applying the inequality from
Mitrinović 1964
again, and the last line is due to the Stirling’s approximation.

Now we use Lemma
4.5
to deduce

\begin{split}\frac{C_{n}^{(3)}}{C_{n}}\leq&M_{3}\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)e^{2n+1}\log^{n}n}{n^{2n+1}(n-2)}\tau_{0}^{n}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})\right\}^{1+1/\hat{\xi}_{n}}\big/\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{j}\right)^{1-1/n},\end{split}

(C.19)

where
M_{3}=(2\pi)^{-3/2}|I(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0})|^{1/2}\pi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0})M_{2}
. Stirling’s approximation ensures

\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)e^{2n+1}}{n^{2n+1}(n-2)}\rightarrow 0.

Lemma
4.1
gives

\prod_{i=1}^{n}W^{1+1/\hat{\xi}_{n}}_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})=\exp\left\{\left(1+\frac{1}{\hat{\xi}_{n}}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log W_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})\right\}\sim\exp\{n\gamma(1+\xi_{0})\}

(C.20)

almost surely.
Meanwhile, since
Y_{(j)}-\tilde{\beta}>1
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\tilde{\delta}_{j}/n\rightarrow\infty
in contrast to (
C.16
). More specifically,

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{j}\geq\prod_{j=\lceil\log n\rceil}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{(j)}\geq\prod_{j=\lceil\log n\rceil}^{n}(Y_{(j)}-\beta_{0})\geq(Y_{(\lceil\log n\rceil)}-\beta_{0})^{n-\log n},

in which
\delta_{(j)}=Y_{(j)}-\beta_{0}+r_{2}
. Similar to (
C.11
) in Lemma
C.4
, we know

Y_{(\lceil\log n\rceil)}-\beta_{0}=\frac{\tau_{0}}{\xi_{0}}\left\{-\log U_{(\lceil\log n\rceil)}\right\}^{-\xi_{0}}\geq\frac{\tau_{0}}{\xi_{0}}\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)^{\xi_{0}}.

Combine the previous results to get

\begin{split}\tau_{0}^{n}\log^{n}n\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})\right\}^{1+1/\hat{\xi}_{n}}\big/&\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\delta}_{j}\right)^{1-1/n}\leq\exp[n\{-\xi_{0}\log n/2-\xi_{0}\log n(\log\log n)/n+\\
&\xi_{0}\log^{2}n/n+(1+\xi_{0})\log\log n+\log\tau_{0}+\gamma(1+\xi_{0})\}].\end{split}

This upper bound converges to zero because
-\xi_{0}\log n/2
is the dominating term in the curly brackets.

Therefore, the upper bound in (
C.19
) converges to zero almost surely, which means
C_{n}^{(3)}/C_{n}\rightarrow 0
almost surely.
∎

C.3
Proof that
C_{n}^{(4)}
is negligible

Proof.

Similarly as in (
C.1
), we obtain

\begin{split}C_{n}^{(4)}&=\int_{\Omega_{n}^{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\eta})\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta})d\boldsymbol{\eta}\\
&=\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{Y_{(1)}}g(\xi)\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\int_{\tau_{0}+r_{3}}^{\infty}\tau^{n/\xi-1}\exp\left\{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}W^{-1/\xi}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}d\tau d\beta d\xi\\
&=\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{Y_{(1)}}\frac{g(\xi)\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{\xi^{-1}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}^{n}}\Gamma\left(n,(\tau_{0}+r_{3})^{1/\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right)d\beta d\xi.\end{split}

By the inequality of harmonic and geometric means,

\begin{split}\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})&=\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}^{1/n}\times\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}^{(n-1)(\xi+1)/n}\\
&\leq\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}\times\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}^{(n-1)(\xi+1)}.\end{split}

Plug this inequality back into the right-hand side of the previous display and then apply the substitution
q=(\tau_{0}+r_{3})^{1/\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=(\tau_{0}+r_{3})^{1/\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\xi(Y_{i}-\beta)\}^{-1/\xi}
to obtain

\displaystyle C_{n}^{(4)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{Y_{(1)}}\frac{g(\xi)\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}^{(n-1)\xi-1}}{\xi^{-1}n^{(n-1)\xi+n}}\Gamma\left(n,(\tau_{0}+r_{3})^{1/\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})d\beta d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\frac{g(\xi)(\tau_{0}+r_{3})^{-(n-1)}}{\xi^{-1}n^{(n-1)\xi+n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{(n-1)\xi-1}\Gamma\left(n,q\right)dqd\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\frac{g(\xi)(\tau_{0}+r_{3})^{-(n-1)}}{n^{(n-1)\xi+n}}\cdot\frac{\Gamma((n-1)\xi+n)}{(n-1)}d\xi.

The last equality utilizes the fact that
\int_{0}^{\infty}q^{a-1}\Gamma(b,q)dq=\Gamma(a+b)/a
for
a,b>0
. By Stirling’s approximation,
\Gamma(x)\leq 3\sqrt{x}(x/e)^{x}
for large
x
(e.g.,
x>20
). Therefore for
\xi\in(0,\xi_{0}+r_{1})
,

\begin{split}\frac{\Gamma((n-1)\xi+n)}{n^{(n-1)\xi+n}}&\leq 3\sqrt{(n-1)\xi+n}\left\{\frac{(n-1)\xi+n}{ne}\right\}^{(n-1)\xi+n}\\
&<3\sqrt{n(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)}\left(\frac{\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1}{e}\right)^{(n-1)\xi+n},\end{split}

and it follows that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\frac{g(\xi)\Gamma((n-1)\xi+n)}{n^{(n-1)\xi+n}}d\xi&<3M_{2}\sqrt{n(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)}\int_{0}^{\xi_{0}+r_{1}}\left(\frac{\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1}{e}\right)^{(n-1)\xi+n}d\xi\\
&<\frac{3M_{2}\sqrt{n(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)}[(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)/e]^{(n-1)(\xi_{0}+r_{1})+n}}{\log[(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)/e]},\end{split}

where
M_{2}
is the upper bound of
g(\xi)
in
(-1/2,\xi_{0}+r_{1})
.

Hence,
C_{n}^{(4)}
can be further bounded by

C_{n}^{(4)}\leq M_{4}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n-1}\left(\frac{\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1}{e}\right)^{(n-1)(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)}(\tau_{0}+r_{3})^{-n},

where

M_{4}=3M_{2}(\tau_{0}+r_{3})\frac{(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)^{3/2}/e}{\log[(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)/e]}.

Combining this result with Lemma
4.5
and denoting
M_{5}=2M_{4}|I(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0})|^{1/2}/\{(2\pi)^{3/2}\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0})\}
, we get

\frac{C_{n}^{(4)}}{C_{n}}\leq\frac{M_{5}n^{2}e^{n}}{n-1}\left(\frac{\tau_{0}+r_{3}}{\hat{\tau}_{n}}\right)^{-n}\left(\frac{\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1}{e}\right)^{(n-1)(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})\right\}^{1+1/\hat{\xi}_{n}}.

(C.21)

Similar to the proof in Section
C.1
, we now prove the right-hand side of (
C.21
) converges almost surely to
0
. From (
C.20
), we see that the dominating term on the right-hand side of (
C.21
) is

\exp\left[n\left\{1+\log\frac{\hat{\tau}_{n}}{\tau_{0}}-\log\left(1+\frac{r_{3}}{\tau_{0}}\right)+(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)\log\left(\frac{\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1}{e}\right)+(1+\xi_{0})\gamma\right\}\right]

Since
\hat{\tau}_{n}\rightarrow\tau_{0}
almost surely and
r_{3}
is predetermined to satisfy
\log\left(1+r_{3}/\tau_{0}\right)>(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)\log\{(\xi_{0}+r_{1}+1)/e\}+(1+\xi_{0})\gamma+1
, this dominating term converges almost surely to
0
and thus
C_{n}^{(4)}/C_{n}\rightarrow 0
almost surely.
∎

C.4
Proof that
C_{n}^{(5)}
is negligible

Proof.

Denote
\Delta_{j}=\Delta_{j}(n)=Y_{(n)}-Y_{(j)}
. Similarly to (
C.1
), we apply the substitutions
t=\tau^{1/\xi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{\xi(Y_{i}-\beta)\}^{-1/\xi}
and
s=(\beta-Y_{(n)})^{-1}
sequentially:

\begin{split}C_{n}^{(5)}&=\int_{\Omega_{n}^{5}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(Y_{i}\,|\,\boldsymbol{\eta})\pi(\boldsymbol{\eta})d\boldsymbol{\eta}\\
&=\int_{-1/2}^{0}\int_{Y_{(n)}}^{\infty}\frac{g(\xi)\prod_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}{(-\xi)^{-1}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\right\}^{n}}\Gamma(n)d\beta d\xi\\
&=\int_{-1/2}^{0}g(\xi)\Gamma(n)(-\xi)^{-n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{n-2}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+\Delta_{j}s)^{-{1}/{\xi}-1}}{\left\{1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+\Delta_{j}s)^{-1/\xi}\right\}^{n}}dsd\xi\\
&\leq\int_{-1/2}^{0}g(\xi)\Gamma(n)(-\xi)^{-n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{n-2}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+\Delta_{j}s)^{-{1}/{(2\xi)}-1}}{\left\{1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+\Delta_{j}s)^{-1/\xi}\right\}^{n}}dsd\xi\\
&\leq\int_{-1/2}^{0}g(\xi)\Gamma(n)n^{-n}(-\xi)^{-n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-2}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+\Delta_{j}s)^{{1}/{(2\xi)}-1}dsd\xi.\\
\end{split}

(C.22)

The penultimate line is obtained due to
\xi<0
and
(1+\Delta_{j}s)^{1/\xi}<1
,
j=1,\ldots,n-1
. The last line is obtained via applying (
C.2
) to the numerator of the inner integrand. By
Mitrinović 1964
,

\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+\Delta_{j}s)\geq(1+\bar{\Delta}_{n}s)^{n-1},\text{ where }\Delta_{j}>0,\;\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Delta_{j}=\bar{\Delta}_{n}^{n-1}.

Thus, (
C.17
) with parameters
(\xi,\tau)
such that
1/\kappa=n-2-(n-1)/(2\xi)
and
\kappa/\tau=\bar{\Delta}_{n}
gives

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}&s^{n-2}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(1+\Delta_{j}s)^{{1}/{(2\xi)}-1}ds\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-2}(1+\bar{\Delta}_{n}s)^{(n-1)\{{1}/{(2\xi)}-1\}}ds\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(n-1)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Delta_{j}}\cdot\frac{1}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}\{-(n-1)/(2\xi)+n-2-i\}}.\end{split}

Plug this result back to the right-hand side of (
C.22
), and recall that
M_{2}
is the upper bound of
g(\xi)
in
(-1/2,\xi_{0}+r_{1})
:

\begin{split}C_{n}^{(5)}&\leq\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)n^{-n}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Delta_{j}}\int_{-1/2}^{0}\frac{g(\xi)}{\prod_{i=0}^{n-2}\{(n-1)/2-(n-2-i)\xi\}}d\xi\\
&\leq\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)n^{-n}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Delta_{j}}\left(\frac{2}{n-1}\right)^{n-1}\int_{-1/2}^{0}\frac{M_{2}}{\{1-2\Gamma^{1/(n-1)}(n-1)\xi/(n-1)\}^{n-1}}d\xi\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)n^{-n}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Delta_{j}}\cdot\frac{2^{n-1}M_{2}}{(n-1)^{n}}\cdot\frac{1-(1+\Gamma^{1/(n-1)}(n-1)/(n-1))^{-n+2}}{2\Gamma^{1/(n-1)}(n-1)/(n-1)}\\
&\sim\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)n^{-n}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Delta_{j}}\cdot\frac{2^{n-2}M_{2}}{(n-1)^{n}}\end{split}

The third-to-last line is obtained by applying the inequality from
Mitrinović 1964
again, and the last line is due to the Stirling’s approximation.

Once more we use Lemma
4.5
to deduce

\begin{split}\frac{C_{n}^{(5)}}{C_{n}}\leq&M_{3}\frac{\Gamma(n-1)\Gamma(n)2^{n-2}e^{n}}{n^{n}(n-1)^{n}}\tau_{0}^{n}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_{n})\right\}^{1+1/\hat{\xi}_{n}}\big/\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\Delta_{j}\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{a.s.},\end{split}

(C.23)

where
M_{3}=(2\pi)^{-3/2}|I(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0})|^{1/2}\pi^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0})M_{2}
.
∎

Appendix D
Outline of proof for negative and zero shapes

D.1
The case
\xi_{0}\in(-1/2,0)

The regions we consider when
\xi_{0}\in(-1/2,0)
are

\begin{split}\Omega_{n}^{1}&=\{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Omega_{n}\setminus B_{r}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{0}):\xi_{0}-r_{1}<\xi<0,Y_{(n)}<\beta<\beta_{0}+r_{2},\tau<\tau_{0}+r_{3}\},\\
\Omega_{n}^{2}&=\{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Omega_{n}:-1/2<\xi<\xi_{0}-r_{1}\},\\
\Omega_{n}^{3}&=\{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Omega_{n}:\xi_{0}-r_{1}<\xi<0,\beta>\beta_{0}+r_{2},\tau<\tau_{0}+r_{3}\},\\
\Omega_{n}^{4}&=\{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Omega_{n}:\xi_{0}-r_{1}<\xi<0,\tau>\tau_{0}+r_{3}\},\;\Omega_{n}^{5}=\{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Omega_{n}:\xi>0\},\end{split}

and
r_{1},\;r_{2},\;r_{3}>r
are pre-specified constants such that

\begin{split}\log\left(1-\frac{r_{1}}{\xi_{0}}\right)&>-\frac{4}{\xi_{0}}-\log 2+\gamma,\quad r_{2}>1,\\
\log\left(1+\frac{r_{3}}{\tau_{0}}\right)&>(r_{1}+1-\xi_{0})\log\frac{r_{1}+1-\xi_{0}}{e}+(1-\xi_{0})\gamma+1.\end{split}

Again, we denote the contributions to the numerator in (
4.3
) from integrals over these sub-regions as
C_{n}^{(1)},\ldots,C_{n}^{(5)}
. Because Lemma
B.1
–
B.4
hold for
\xi_{0}\neq 0
, the proof for
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C_{n}^{(1)}/C_{n}=0
(i.e. Proposition
4.6
) when
\xi_{0}\in(-1/2,0)
remains the same as in Appendix
B
. To show
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C_{n}^{(k)}/C_{n}=0
,
k=2,\ldots,5
almost surely, we simply need to make fine adjustments to the proofs in Appendix
C.1
–
C.4
respectively.

D.2
The case
\xi_{0}=0

When
\xi_{0}=0
,
\hat{\xi}_{n}=O_{p}(n^{-1/2})
is arbitrarily small and
\hat{\xi}_{n}
can be either positive or negative,
which greatly complicates the behavior of
L_{n}
when
\xi
is around
0
. However, when
\xi_{0}=0
, the log-likelihood
L_{n}
is highly peaked in the rectangular box
\{|\tau-\tau_{0}|<1,|\mu-\mu_{0}|<1,\xi\in[-\exp(\gamma)/\log n,\exp(\gamma)/\log\log n]\}
(note that the width of the range of
\xi
is arbitrarily small for large
n
)
(
Zhang and Shaby 2021
, Appendix F)
.
Therefore it is easier to work with the
\boldsymbol{\theta}
-parameterization
. We can show by following the proof in Appendix
C.1
and
C.4
that the contributions to the numerator in (
4.3
) from integrals over

\begin{split}\Omega_{n}^{2}&=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Omega_{n}:\xi>\xi_{0}+r_{1}=r_{1}\},\;\Omega_{n}^{5}=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Omega_{n}:-1/2<\xi<\xi_{0}-r_{1}=-r_{1}\},\end{split}

are negligible for any fixed constant
r_{1}\in(0,1/2)
.

In the meantime, we can dissect
\Omega_{n}\cap\{\xi\in[-r_{1},r_{1}]\}
as follows:

\begin{split}\Omega_{n}^{1}&=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Omega_{n}\setminus B_{r}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}):-r_{1}<\xi<r_{1},\mu_{0}-r_{2}<\mu<\mu_{0}+r_{2},0<\tau<\tau_{0}+1\},\\
\Omega_{n}^{3}&=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Omega_{n}:-r_{1}<\xi<r_{1},|\mu-\mu_{0}|>r_{2},\tau<\tau_{0}+1\},\\
\Omega_{n}^{4}&=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Omega_{n}:-r_{1}<\xi<r_{1},\tau>\tau_{0}+1\},\end{split}

where
r_{2}>\max\{\tau_{0}\gamma+\zeta,2(\tau_{0}+1)\}
with
\zeta>\tau_{0}\gamma+\gamma+1
. To prove
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C_{n}^{(1)}/C_{n}=0
, we show that
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}P_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}}[l_{B(\boldsymbol{\theta},\epsilon)}]=-\infty

and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B(\boldsymbol{\theta},\epsilon)\cap\Omega_{n}}\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})d\boldsymbol{\theta}=0
for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
on the boundary of
\Omega_{n}^{1}
with
\tau=0
, similarly to Lemma
B.1

and
B.4
. Combining this result and Lemma 6 in
Dombry 2015
again establishes
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C_{n}^{(1)}/C_{n}=0
in the same way as the proof for Proposition
4.6
. To show
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C_{n}^{(4)}/C_{n}=0
, one can slightly alter the proof in Appendix
C.3
.
Lastly, the proof of
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}C_{n}^{(3)}/C_{n}=0
is given in Lemma
D.1
.

Lemma D.1
.

Suppose
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots
are independently sampled from
P_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}}
with
\xi_{0}=0
. Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega_{n}^{3}}\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})d\boldsymbol{\theta}=0
almost surely.

Proof.

When
-1/2<-r_{1}<\xi<r_{1}
, note that
(1+a\xi)^{1/\xi}
is a decreasing function of
\xi
for any fixed
a\in\mathbb{R}
for which in
1+a\xi>0
. Then we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\theta})&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{1+\frac{\xi(Y_{i}-\mu)}{\tau}\right\}^{-1/\xi}\geq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{1-\frac{Y_{i}-\mu}{2\tau}\right\}^{2}\\
&=1-\frac{\bar{Y}_{n}-\mu}{\tau}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(Y_{i}-\mu)^{2}}{4\tau^{2}}\geq 1-\frac{\bar{Y}_{n}-\mu}{\tau}+\frac{(\bar{Y}_{n}-\mu)^{2}}{4\tau^{2}},\end{split}

(D.1)

in which
\bar{Y}_{n}
is the sample mean which tends to
\mu_{0}+\tau_{0}\gamma
almost surely as
n\rightarrow\infty
, and the last inequality uses the convexity of the quadratic function.

Meanwhile,
r_{2}>\max\{\tau_{0}\gamma+\zeta,2(\tau_{0}+1)\}
ensures that
(\bar{Y}_{n}-\mu)/\tau\in(-\infty,0)\cup(2,\infty)
for all large
n
and all
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\Omega_{n}^{3}
. The concavity of the logarithm function gives

\begin{split}n^{-1}&\left(-1-\frac{1}{\xi}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log W_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\leq\left(-1-\frac{1}{\xi}\right)\log\left\{1+\frac{\xi(\bar{Y}_{n}-\mu)}{\tau}\right\}\\
&\leq\lim_{\xi\nearrow 0}\left(-1-\frac{1}{\xi}\right)\log\left\{1+\frac{\xi(\bar{Y}_{n}-\mu)}{\tau}\right\}=-\frac{\bar{Y}_{n}-\mu}{\tau},\end{split}

(D.2)

in which we also utilized the fact that
(-1-1/\xi)\log(1+\xi a)
is an increasing function of
\xi
in
\{\xi:1+a\xi>0,\;a\in(-\infty,0)\cup(2,\infty)\}
.

Combining (
D.1
) and (
D.2
), we have

\begin{split}n^{-1}L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})&=-\log\tau+n^{-1}\left(-1-\frac{1}{\xi}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log W_{i}(\boldsymbol{\theta})-n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}^{-1/\xi}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\\
&=-\log\tau-1-\frac{(\bar{Y}_{n}-\mu)^{2}}{4\tau^{2}}<-\log\tau-1-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau^{2}}.\end{split}

The last inequality of the last display again exploits the fact that
r_{2}>\max\{\tau_{0}\gamma+\zeta,2(\tau_{0}+1)\}
and
\bar{Y}_{n}
tends to
\mu_{0}+\tau_{0}\gamma
almost surely. Consequently,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{n}^{3}}\pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\exp\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\}d\boldsymbol{\theta}&<\int_{\Omega_{n}^{3}}g(\xi)\tau^{-n-1}\exp\left\{-\frac{n\zeta^{2}}{4\tau^{2}}-n\right\}d\boldsymbol{\theta}\\
&<\frac{\Pi_{g}}{2}\left(\frac{4}{n\zeta}\right)^{(n-1)/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)e^{-n},\end{split}

where
\Pi_{g}
again denotes the upper bound of
g(\xi)
in
\xi\in(-r_{1},r_{1})
. Similarly to the proof of Lemma
B.4
, we can easily show that

\int_{\Omega_{n}^{3}}\pi_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})d\boldsymbol{\theta}=\int_{\Omega_{n}^{3}}\pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\exp\{L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\}d\boldsymbol{\theta}/C_{n}\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
almost surely on account of Lemma
4.5
and the fact that
\zeta>\tau_{0}\gamma+\gamma+1
.

∎

The c c -differential spectrum of x ↦ x p n + 1 2 x\mapsto x^{\frac{p^{n}+1}{2}} in finite fields of odd characteristics
Constanza Riera, Pantelimon Stănică, Haode Yan

Abstract
In the paper, we concentrate on the map x ↦ x p n + 1 2 x\mapsto x^{\frac{p^{n}+1}{2}} on 𝔽 p n {\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}} and using combinatorial and number theory techniques, we compute its detailed c c -differential spectrum for all values of c ≠ 1 c\neq 1 (the spectrum for c = 1 c=1 is known).

The absolute trace

Since the case of

]

We include here the

-differential uniformity for this function, and we give in the proof a complete characterization of the conditions on

[
11
]
, who treated the
(-1)
-spectrum for the function

We will use the following result (see
[
4
]
) about the cyclotomic numbers: Let
S_{i,j}=\{x\neq 0,-1:\eta(x+1)=i,\eta(x)=j\}
, where
i,j\in\{\pm 1\}
. Then, if
\eta(-1)=1
(which happens if
p^{n}\equiv 1\mod 4
), then
|S_{1,1}|=\frac{p^{n}-5}{4}
and
|S_{-1,1}|=|S_{1,-1}|=|S_{-1,-1}|=\frac{p^{n}-1}{4}
. If
\eta(-1)=-1
(which happens if
p^{n}\equiv 3\mod 4
), then
|S_{1,-1}|=\frac{p^{n}+1}{4}
and
|S_{1,1}|=|S_{-1,1}|=|S_{-1,-1}|=\frac{p^{n}-3}{4}
.

We start with the easy case of
c=0
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
F(x)=x^{d}
, where
d=\frac{p^{n}+1}{2}
(for
p
odd). If
c=0
, then the function is PcN if
\eta(-1)=1
, and APcN if
\eta(-1)=-1
, with spectrum
\left\{{}_{c}{\omega}_{0}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{2},_{c}{\omega}_{1}=1,_{c}{\omega}_{2}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{2}\right\}
.

Proof.

Let
d=\frac{p^{n}+1}{2}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{2}+1
. We look at the equation

F(x+1)-cF(x)=b,

and so,

\displaystyle(x+1)^{\frac{p^{n}+1}{2}}-cx^{\frac{p^{n}+1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(x+1)^{\frac{p^{n}-1}{2}+1}-cx^{\frac{p^{n}-1}{2}+1}

\displaystyle=\eta(x+1)(x+1)-c\eta(x)x=b.

(1)

When
c=0
, Equation (
1
) becomes
\eta(x+1)(x+1)=b
.

•

If
x=-1
, then,
b=0
.

•

Let
\eta(x+1)=1
. Then, the equation becomes
x+1=b
, so
x=b-1
. This solution is valid if and only if
\eta(b)=1
.

•

Let
\eta(x+1)=-1
. Then, the equation becomes
-x-1=b
, so
x=-b-1
. This solution is valid if and only if
\eta(-b)=-1
.

Therefore, if
\eta(-1)=1
, then we have a unique solution for every
b
. The function is then 0-PN (that is, a permutation), so its spectrum is, naturally,

\{{{}_{0}}\omega_{1}=p^{n}\}.

(2)

If
\eta(-1)=-1
, then we have a single solution for
b=0
, two solutions for
b
such that
\eta(b)=1
, and no solutions otherwise. The function is then 0-APN, with spectrum

\left\{{}_{0}{\omega}_{0}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{2},\,_{0}{\omega}_{1}=1,\,_{0}{\omega}_{2}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{2}\right\}.

(3)

The claim is therefore shown.
∎

We continue with the more complicated case of
c=-1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F(x)=x^{d}
, where
d=\frac{p^{n}+1}{2}
(for
p
odd), over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}},n\geq 1
. If
c=-1
, then the spectrum is
\left\{{}_{c}{\omega_{0}}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{2},\,_{c}{\omega_{1}}=\frac{p^{n}-3}{2},\,_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+3}{4}}}=2\right\}
if
\eta(-1)=1
, and
\left\{{}_{c}{\omega_{0}}=\frac{3p^{n}-5}{4},\,_{c}{\omega_{2}}=\frac{p^{n}-3}{4},\,_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+1}{4}}}=1,\,_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+5}{4}}}=1\right\}
, if
\eta(-1)=-1
.

Proof.

When
c=-1
, Equation (
1
) becomes

\eta(x+1)(x+1)+\eta(x)x=b.

(4)

Assuming
\eta(-1)=1
, we get the solutions:

•

for
b=1
,
x=0
and all elements
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that
\eta(x+1)=1=-\eta(x)
(that is,
|S_{1,-1}|=\frac{p^{n}-2+\eta(-1)}{4}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{4}
);

•

for
b=-1
,
x=-1
and all elements
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that
\eta(x+1)=-1=-\eta(x)
(that is,
|S_{-1,1}|=\frac{p^{n}-\eta(-1)}{4}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{4}
);

•

one solution if
\eta(b+1)=\eta(b-1)
, and no solution otherwise.

We get then, for
b=\pm 1
,
\frac{p^{n}+3}{4}
solutions for (
4
).

The spectrum is therefore

{}_{c}{\omega_{0}}

\displaystyle=|\{b\neq\pm 1:\eta(b+1)\neq\eta(b-1)\}|

{}_{c}{\omega_{1}}

\displaystyle=|\{b\neq\pm 1:\eta(b+1)=\eta(b-1)\}|

{}_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+3}{4}}}

\displaystyle=|\{b=\pm 1\}|=2.

The
c
-differential uniformity is then
\frac{p^{n}+3}{4}
, achieved only in two points.
We want to compute the cardinality of
T_{i,j}=\{b\neq\pm 1:\eta(b-1)=i,\eta(b+1)=j\}
,
i,j\in\{\pm 1\}
. We have that

\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}(1+i\eta(b-1))(1+j\eta(b+1))=4|T_{i,j}|+2+j\eta(2)+i\eta(-2).

On the other hand,

\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}(1+i\eta(b-1))(1+j\eta(b+1))=p^{n}+ij\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\eta((b-1)(b+1)).

We know
[
7
]
that if
f(x)=a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}
is a polynomial in a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of odd characteristic,
a_{2}\neq 0,d=a_{1}^{2}-4a_{0}a_{2}
, and
\eta
is the quadratic character on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, then the Jacobsthal sum of
f
is

\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\eta(f(x))=\begin{cases}-\eta(a_{2})&\text{ if }d\neq 0\\
(q-1)\eta(a_{2})&\text{ if }d=0.\end{cases}

(5)

To compute
\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\eta((b-1)(b+1))
, we apply (
5
) and
take
f(x)=x^{2}-1
,
d=1
, and so, the Jacobsthal sum becomes

\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}\eta(x^{2}-1)=-\eta(1)=-1
. Therefore

\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}}(1+i\eta(b-1))(1+j\eta(b+1))=p^{n}-ij,

so

|T_{i,j}|=\frac{1}{4}\left(p^{n}-ij-2-j\eta(2)-i\eta(-2)\right).

If
\eta(-1)=1
, then
|T_{1,1}|=\frac{1}{4}(p^{n}-3-2\eta(2))
,
|T_{1,-1}|=\frac{1}{4}(p^{n}-1)=|T_{-1,1}|
, and
|T_{-1,-1}|=\frac{1}{4}(p^{n}-3+2\eta(2))
(recall that
2
is a quadratic residue if and only if
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{8}
, or,
p\equiv\pm 3\pmod{8}
and
n
even).

Thus,

{}_{c}{\omega_{0}}

\displaystyle=|\{b\neq\pm 1:\eta(b+1)\neq\eta(b-1)\}|=|T_{1,-1}|+|T_{-1,1}|=\frac{p^{n}-1}{2},

{}_{c}{\omega_{1}}

\displaystyle=|\{b\neq\pm 1:\eta(b+1)=\eta(b-1)\}|=|T_{1,1}|+|T_{-1,-1}|=\frac{p^{n}-3}{2},

and thus, the spectrum becomes

\left\{{}_{c}{\omega_{0}}=\frac{p^{n}-1}{2},\,_{c}{\omega_{1}}=\frac{p^{n}-3}{2},\,_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+3}{4}}}=2\right\}.

(6)

For
\eta(-1)=-1
, we get, for
b=1
,
x=0
,
x=-1
, and all elements
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that
\eta(x+1)=1=-\eta(x)
(that is,
|S_{1,-1}|=\frac{p^{n}-2+\eta(-1)}{4}=\frac{p^{n}-3}{4}
); for
b=-1
, all elements
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
such that
\eta(x+1)=-1=-\eta(x)
(that is,
|S_{-1,1}|=\frac{p^{n}-\eta(-1)}{4}=\frac{p^{n}+1}{4}
); two solutions if
\eta(b+1)=\eta(b-1)=\eta(2)
, and no solution, otherwise.
The spectrum is then:

{}_{c}{\omega_{2}}

\displaystyle=|\{b\neq\pm 1:\eta(b+1)=\eta(b-1)=\eta(2)\}|

{}_{c}{\omega_{0}}

\displaystyle=|\{b\neq\pm 1:b\notin c_{\omega_{2}}\}|

{}_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+5}{4}}}

\displaystyle=|\{b=1\}|=1

{}_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+1}{4}}}

\displaystyle=|\{b=-1\}|=1.

If
\eta(-1)=-1
, then
|T_{1,1}|=\frac{1}{4}(p^{n}-3)=|T_{-1,-1}|
,
|T_{1,-1}|=\frac{1}{4}(p^{n}-1+2\eta(2))
and
|T_{1,1}|=\frac{1}{4}(p^{n}-1-2\eta(2))
. Then:

{}_{c}{\omega_{2}}

\displaystyle=|\{b\neq\pm 1:\eta(b+1)=\eta(b-1)=\eta(2)\}|=\frac{1}{4}(p^{n}-3),

{}_{c}{\omega_{0}}

\displaystyle=|\{b\neq\pm 1:b\notin c_{\omega_{2}}\}|=p^{n}-2-\frac{1}{4}(p^{n}-3)=\frac{3p^{n}-5}{4}.

The
c
-differential uniformity is then
\frac{p^{n}+5}{4}
, achieved only in one point, and the spectrum is then

\left\{{}_{c}{\omega_{0}}=\frac{3p^{n}-5}{4},\,_{c}{\omega_{2}}=\frac{p^{n}-3}{4},\,_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+1}{4}}}=1,\,_{c}{\omega_{\frac{p^{n}+5}{4}}}=1\right\}.

(7)

The theorem is therefore shown.

3
Conclusions

In this paper we concentrate on the map
x\mapsto x^{\frac{p^{n}+1}{2}}
on a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{n}}
,
p
being an odd prime and
n
a positive integer, and compute the complete
c
-differential spectrum, by using Weil sums and elliptic curve methods. Perhaps the methods we used can be applied to finding the spectrum of other interesting maps, with potential low differential or
c
-differential uniformity.

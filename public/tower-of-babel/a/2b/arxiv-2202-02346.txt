On the T-equivariant cohomology of Hessenberg varieties
Daniel Sánchez Argáez, Felipe Zaldívar

Abstract
. For an endomorphism s : V → V s:V\rightarrow V of a finite dimensional complex vector space and an action of a torus T T on the full flag variety GL n ⁡ ( ℂ ) / B \GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B , we give a description of its fixed point set when s s is semisimple or regular nilpotent. We also compute the one dimensional orbits of this action on the Hessenberg subvariety Hess ⁡ ( s , h ) ⊆ GL n ⁡ ( ℂ ) / B \hes(s,h)\subseteq\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B for any Hessenberg function h h . For the action of the one dimensional torus S S and a regular nilpotent endomorphism N : V → V N:V\rightarrow V , we give a new computation of the equivariant cohomology of the Hessenberg variety Hess ⁡ ( N , h ) \hes(N,h) for any Hessenberg function using determinantal conditions.

4.
One dimensional orbits in a Hessenberg variety. The nilpotent case

In this section we obtain the one-dimensional orbits from a
T
-action on the Hessenberg variety
\hes(N,h)
when
N
is a regular nilpotent operator. Thus,
N
is a matrix whose Jordan form has one block with corresponding eigenvalue equal to zero. We obtain an analogue description as in the diagonal case in Theorem
3.4
. Here,
T
is an
n
-torus acting on flag variety
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B
by left multiplication. First, we determine which fixed points in
(\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B)^{T}
belong to
\hes(N,h)
. Next, we do same for the one dimensional orbits of
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B
.

By Proposition
2.2
, the
T
-fixed points
[w]\in\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B
correspond to
w\in S_{n}
. For
[w]\in(\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B)^{T}
with column vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
,
Nv_{w^{-1}(i+1)}=e_{i}
for
all
1\leq i<n
, where
e_{i}
are the canonical base, and for
i=1
,
Nv_{w^{-1}(1)}=Ne_{1}=\overline{0}
, the null vector. By Theorem
2.5
, a

T
-fixed point
[w]
belongs to
\hes(N,h)
if and only if its column vectors vanish in the ideal
I_{N,H_{h}}
. This is equivalent to
w^{-1}(i)\leq h(w^{-1}(i+1))
for all
1\leq i<n
. This last claim follows from the fact that
Nv_{w^{-1}(i+1)}
and
v_{w^{-1}(i+1)}
are linearly independent since

\tilde{d}=d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(i+1)},\ldots,v_{h({w^{-1}(i+1)})},Nv_{{w^{-1}(i+1)}},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}=0

if and only if
e_{i}=v_{w^{-1}(i)}
belongs to

(4.1)

B_{w^{-1}(i+1),r}=\left\{v_{1},\ldots v_{h(w^{-1}(i+1))},Nv_{w^{-1}(i+1)},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n}\right\}

for all
r>h(w^{-1}(i+1))
, and (
4.1
) is equivalent to
w^{-1}(i)\leq h(w^{-1}(i+1))
.

Now, we compute the one dimensional orbits of
\hes(N,h)
. From Proposition
3.1
and Equation (
3.2
) the one dimensional orbits of
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B
are
O_{w,s_{j,k}w}
with
w\in S_{n}
and the transposition
s_{j,k}
. For
w\in S_{n}
with
[w]\in\GL_{n}/B
the corresponding flag, in (
3.1
) we defined

(4.2)

O_{w,s_{j,k}w}=\left\{[G_{jk}(c)w]\in\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B:c\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\right\}

with
j<k
such that
w^{-1}(j)>w^{-1}(k)
.
To determine which of the orbits (
4.2
) belong to
\hes(N,h)
, first note that
[w]
must belong to
\hes(N,h)
and if that is the case,
O_{w,s_{j,k}}\subset\hes(N,h)
if every flag
[G_{j,k}(c)w]
of
O_{w,s_{j,k}w}
vanish in the ideal
I_{N,H_{h}}
and the proof of this last claim starts by noting that

(4.3)

Nv_{w^{-1}(k)}=\begin{cases}e_{k-1}+ce_{j-1}&\text{ if }j,k\neq 1,\\
e_{k-1}&\text{ if }j=1,\;\text{note that $k>j=1$}\end{cases}

and for
j,k\neq 1
in (
4.3
),
[G_{j,k}(c)w]
vanish in
I_{N,H_{h}}
if and only if for all
r>h(w^{-1}(k))
we have the first equality in (
4.4
), where the next equalities follow from a direct computation:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=\tilde{d}=d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},Nv_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}\\
&=d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},e_{k-1}+ce_{j-1}+\lambda_{k}v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}\\
&=d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},e_{k-1},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}\\
&\quad+d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},ce_{j-1},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}.\end{aligned}

We observe now the right-hand side of the last equality in (
4.4
) is zero if and only if for all
r>h(w^{-1}(k))
both determinants are simultaneously zero, that is

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},e_{k-1},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}\\
&=d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},ce_{j-1},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}.\end{aligned}

Indeed, by Lemma
2.4
there is
r^{\prime}>h(w^{-1}(k))
such that the two determinants in the last equality in (
4.4
) are nonzero and cancel each other if and only if
w^{-1}(k-1)
and
w^{-1}(j-1)
are greater than
h(w^{-1}(k))
.
Now, for
r=w^{-1}(k-1)
we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&\neq d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},e_{k-1},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}\\
\end{aligned}

and hence

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &0=d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},ce_{j-1},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}.\end{aligned}

Similarly, for

, Equation
4.7

is nonzero and Equation

is zero. Thus, both determinants cannot be nonzero simultaneously proving (
4.5
).
Lastly, the vanishing of both summands in (

) holds if and only if

w^{-1}(k-1)\leq h(w^{-1}(k))\qquad\text{ and }\qquad w^{-1}(j-1)\leq h(w^{-1}(k)).

Next, for the case

in (

) equality (

) holds

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=d_{(1,\ldots,n),(v_{1},\ldots v_{w^{-1}(k)},\ldots,v_{h(w^{-1}(k))},e_{k-1},\ldots\widehat{v_{r}},\ldots v_{n})}\end{aligned}

if and only if

.

These arguments characterize when an open orbit

is contained in
\hes(N,h)
. To determine when the

we need to characterize when

. For this, observe that

is the permutation matrix that interchanges the rows

and

of

. In other words,

interchanges the columns

and

of

. For the column

this is equivalent to show that

. To check this, observe that for

,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (s_{jk}w)^{-1}(j)&=(w^{-1}s_{jk})(j)=w^{-1}(k)\leq h((s_{jk}w)^{-1}(j+1))=h(w^{-1}(j+1))\\
\end{aligned}

and for
k=j+1
, we have

.
Similarly, for the column

,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (s_{jk}w)^{-1}(k)&=(w^{-1}s_{jk})(k)=w^{-1}(j)\leq h((s_{jk}w)^{-1}(k+1))=h(w^{-1}(k+1)).\\
\end{aligned}

Hence,
\overline{O_{w,s_{j,k}w}}\subset\hes(N,h)
if and only if

(1)

[w]\in\hes(N,h)^{T}

(2)

(3)

w^{-1}(k-1)\leq h(w^{-1}(k)
and
w^{-1}(j-1)\leq h(w^{-1}(k))

and we have proved the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T
be an
n
-torus acting on
\GL_{n}({\mathbb{C}})/B
by left multiplication. Let

be a Hessenberg variety with

(2)

.

(3)

and
w^{-1}(j-1)\leq h(w^{-1}(k))
.

5.

In this section, we consider the action by left multiplication of the one dimensional torus

First, consider the action of the torus on

for all

E. Insko

-equivariant cohomology class of any

-equivariant cohomology ring of

(Insko, Theorem 4.14)

Abstract
Following the success of the so-called algebraic approach to the study of decision constraint satisfaction problems (CSPs), exact optimization of valued CSPs, and most recently promise CSPs, we propose an algebraic framework for valued promise CSPs. To every valued promise CSP we associate an algebraic object, its so-called valued minion. Our main result shows that the existence of a homomorphism between the associated valued minions implies a polynomial-time reduction between the original CSPs. We also show that this general reduction theorem includes important inapproximability results, for instance, the inapproximability of almost solvable systems of linear equations beyond the random assignment threshold.

2.3
Promise CSP

The Promise CSP over a pair of structures
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})

\mathbf{A}

\mathbf{B}
.
4
4

4

This is the decision version. The

2.5
Minion homomorphisms and reductions

A minion homomorphism between function minions is a mapping of
N
-ary functions in the first minion to
N
-ary functions in the second minion that preserves taking minors.
This concept does not depend on concrete functions in the minion, it only depends on the mappings
f\mapsto f^{(\pi)}
.
We therefore first introduce an abstraction of function minions that carries exactly this information.

Definition 2.16

(Minion)
.

An (abstract)
minion

\mathscr{M}
consists of a collection of sets
(\mathscr{M}^{(N)})_{N\in\mathrm{FinSet}}
, together with a
minor map

{\mathscr{M}}^{(\pi)}:{\mathscr{M}}^{(N)}\to{\mathscr{M}}^{(N^{\prime})}
for every function
\pi:N\to N^{\prime}

{\mathscr{M}}^{(\textnormal{id}_{N})}=\textnormal{id}_{{\mathscr{M}}^{(N)}}
for all finite sets
N
and
{\mathscr{M}}^{(\pi)}\circ{\mathscr{M}}^{(\pi^{\prime})}=\mathscr{M}^{(\pi\circ\pi^{\prime})}
whenever such a composition is well-defined. When the minion is clear,
we write
f^{(\pi)}
for
{\mathscr{M}}^{(\pi)}(f)
.

A minion
\mathscr{M}
is
nontrivial
if
\mathscr{M}^{(N)}
is nonempty for every (equivalently some)
nonempty
N
, and it is
locally finite
if
\mathscr{M}^{(N)}
is finite for every
N
.

The most natural choice of morphisms between minions is minion homomorphisms defined as follows.

9

9

In the language of category theory, a minion is simply a functor from the category of finite sets to the category of sets (a minion corresponds to the functor

,
\pi\mapsto\mathscr{M}^{(\pi)}
) and minion homomorphisms are natural transformations. Note that the projection minion from

Definition 2.17

(Minion homomorphism)
.

Let

and

A

,

, and

.

The reduction theorem discussed above is the following.

Theorem 2.18

(Reductions via minion homomorphism)

Let

to

\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})\leq\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})

10

In fact, Theorem 1 even holds with a log-space reduction, but that will not concern us.

Proposition 2.20

(Between MCs)

Let

(From PCSP to MC)

(\mathrm{proj}^{D_{u}}_{h(u)})^{(\pi)}=\mathrm{proj}^{D_{u}}_{\pi(h(u))}=\mathrm{proj}^{D_{u}}_{h(v)}
and the constraints are satisfied because of (C).
On the other hand, if
(f_{x})_{x\in X}
corresponds to a satisfying assignment of the
PCSP
, then
f_{u}^{(\pi)}=f_{v}
by (M) and each

is a polymorphism by (S).
∎

3
Valued Promise CSP

The generalization of PCSP to the valued setting is obtained by replacing relations by valued relations, that is, mappings
A^{Z}\to\mathbb{Q}\cup\{-\infty\}
, and suitably adjusting the concepts. The crisp PCSPs can be modelled as Valued PCSPs with
\{-\infty,0\}
-valued relations.

This section covers the basics up to a valued and improved version of canonical formulas. A generalization of minion homomorphisms and the main reduction theorem are given in
Section

4
and examples of valued homomorphisms are shown in
Section

5
.

3.1
Preliminaries

We denote by
\mathbb{Q}^{+}
(
\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
) the set of positive (nonnegative) rational numbers and by
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
the set of rational numbers together with an additional symbol
-\infty
. We naturally extend the operations and order, leaving
0\cdot-\infty
undefined.

We will work with probability distributions on finite sets with rational probabilities, so we can formally regard a probability distribution on
N
as a function
\mu:N\to\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
such that
\sum_{n\in N}\mu(n)=1
. We denote by
\Delta N
the set of probability distributions on
N
. The
support
of a probability distribution
\mu\in\Delta N
is the set
\mathrm{Supp}(\mu)=\{n\in N\mid\mu(n)>0\}
.

If
f:N\to N^{\prime}
and
\mu\in\Delta N
, we define
f(\mu)\in\Delta N^{\prime}
in the natural way
(f(\mu))(n^{\prime})=\sum_{n;f(n)=n^{\prime}}\mu(n)
, that is,
n^{\prime}
can be sampled according to
f(\mu)
by sampling
n
according to
\mu
and computing
n^{\prime}=f(n)
.
We also use the notation
F(\mu)
when
\mu\in\Delta N
and
F
is a probability distribution on a set of mappings
N\to N^{\prime}
, i.e., to sample
F(\mu)
we independently sample
n\sim\mu
,
f\sim F
and compute
f(n)
.

Given
\mu\in\Delta N
and a function
f:N\to\mathbb{Q}
, we denote by
\mathbb{E}_{n\sim\mu}f(n)
the expected value of
f(n)
when
n
is sampled according to
\mu
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{n\sim\mu}f(n)=\sum_{n\in N}\mu(n)f(n)
.

A basic tool for some of the proofs is Farkas’ lemma
[
51
]
. The following formulation will be convenient for us. In the statement, juxtaposition denotes the standard matrix multiplication,
T
is used for the transposition, and
\mathbf{x}\geq 0
means that all the components are nonnegative.

Theorem 3.1

(Farkas’ lemma)
.

Let
I,J
be finite sets,
F\in\mathbb{Q}^{I\times J}
, and
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Q}^{I}
. The following are equivalent.

(i)

\exists\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0})^{J}\ F\mathbf{y}\leq\mathbf{q}
.

(ii)

\forall\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0})^{I}\ (F^{T}\mathbf{x}\geq 0\implies\mathbf{q}^{T}\mathbf{x}\geq 0).

Definition 3.3

(Payoff formula)
.

Let
\Sigma

\tau

where

The

Given additionally a valued

-structure

, or

where summands

We allow empty formulas

Note that the convention that

Definition 3.4

(Valued PCSP)
.

A

is a quadruple
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
where

•

\mathbf{A}
,
\mathbf{B}
are valued relational structures in the same signature
\Sigma
, and

•

are the

and

such that

\exists h\ \Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(h)\geq c\,w(\Phi)

\exists h\ \Phi^{\mathbf{B}}(h)\geq s\,w(\Phi)
for every payoff
\Sigma
-formula

Given a valued promise template

, the
Promise Constraint
Satisfaction Problem over

, denoted by

Input

\tau
-sorted set

\Sigma

\Phi
over

Output

if
\exists h\ \Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(h)\geq c\,w(\Phi)

\forall h\ \Phi^{\mathbf{B}}(h)<s\,w(\Phi)
.
11
11

11

The promise is that we are in one of the two cases, i.e., not in the case that
\forall h\ \Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(h)<c\,w(\Phi)
and
\exists h\ \Phi^{\mathbf{B}}(h)\geq sw(\Phi)
.

Let us start the discussion about this generalization of PCSPs by giving examples of problems included in this framework.

First observe that Valued PCSPs indeed generalize crisp PCSPs: for a crisp template
(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
we define a valued promise template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},0,0)
by setting
\phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{a})=0
if
\mathbf{a}\in\phi^{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}
and
\phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{a})=-\infty
otherwise for all
\phi\in\sigma
,
\mathbf{a}\in A^{\textnormal{ar}(\phi)}
, and similarly for
\mathbf{B}^{\prime}
. Clearly,
\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
is equivalent to
\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},0,0)
.

Another natural valued promise template associated to a crisp template
(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
is
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
, where
\phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{a})=1
if
\mathbf{a}\in\phi^{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}
,
\phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{a})=0
otherwise, and

are the completeness and soundness parameters. PCSPs over such templates include e.g. approximation problems for MaxCSPs, such as the following concrete problems.

Example 3.5

(
\textnormal{3LIN2}(c,s)
)
.

Given a weighted system of linear equations over

with exactly 3 variables in each equation, accept if there exists an assignment that satisfies a
c
-fraction of the equations (taking weights into account), and reject if there is no assignment that satisfies an

-fraction of the equations.

This problem is
\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A},c,s)

and the signature consists (as in

We denote this PCSP as well as the template by

Note that

Example 3.8

We fix disjoint sets

Example 3.9

We now discuss several possible variations and modifications of the definition of valued PCSPs, ordered by the significance of the difference they would cause.

First, we have decided for the maximization version of the definition. The corresponding minimization problem can be obtained by multiplying all payoff functions as well as
c
and
s
by
-1
(cf.
Example

3.10
), so results for our version can be easily transferred to the minimization version and vice versa.

Second, note that by shifting (and/or scaling) the payoff functions in
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
and modifying
c
and
s
in the same way, we get an equivalent problem. It would therefore be possible to fix
c,s
, e.g., to
c=s=0
and define a template just as a pair
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
. Our choice here was inspired by a more natural formulation of problems such as
\textnormal{3LIN2}(c,s)
.

Third, a natural version of the definition is to require
\Phi
to be

normalized
, that is,
w(\Phi)=1
. An instance can then be regarded as a
probability distribution on constraints;
\Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(h)
can be interpreted as
the expected value of
\phi^{\mathbf{A}}(h\mathbf{x}_{i})
when constraint

\phi(\mathbf{x}_{i})
is selected according to this distribution. Note that an
equivalent normalized instance can be obtained by dividing all the weights by
w(\Phi)
, unless
w(\Phi)=0
, i.e., all the weights are zero. Therefore this alternative formulation differs from our formulation only very slightly.

Fourth, a more substantial change would be to require that all the weights be equal, say to 1. We regard the presented version as slightly more natural. Note however that it is often the case that positive (algorithmic) results work even for the weighted version and negative (hardness) results already for the non-weighted one, by emulating weights via repeated constraints.

Fifth, the most substantial change would be to not fix
c,s
in advance and
rather make them part of the instance. An important intermediate choice is to fix

s/c
: a template would be a triple
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\kappa)
, an instance would include

c
(not
s
), and
yes
and
no
would be defined in the same way as in the
definition with
s=\kappa c
. Such a framework includes constant factor
approximation problems for MaxCSP; for
\kappa=1
and
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{B}
this framework
essentially coincides with general-valued
CSPs
[
30
,
43
]
. In fact, the
algebraic framework discovered for
\kappa=1
and general
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
by

[
39
]
was among the starting points for this work. We give basics of this framework in
Appendix

C
.

3.3
Polymorphisms

A natural generalization of an
N
-ary operation from
A
to
B
to the valued world consists of a probability distribution on
N
and a probability distribution on a set
\mathcal{F}
of (normal)
N
-ary operations from
A
to
B
. In our situation
\mathcal{F}
will be the set of all
N
-ary polymorphisms of the pair of feasibility structures corresponding to a pair
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
of valued structures. We therefore denote

\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})=\mathrm{Pol}(\textnormal{feas}(\mathbf{A}),\textnormal{feas}(\mathbf{B}))

and introduce the following concept.

Definition 3.11

(Weighting)
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a minion and
N
a finite set. An
N
-ary weighting of
\mathscr{M}
is a pair

\Omega=(\Omega^{\mathrm{in}},\Omega^{\mathrm{out}})\quad\textnormal{ where }\quad\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}\in\Delta N,\ \Omega^{\mathrm{out}}\in\Delta\mathscr{M}^{(N)}.

Relation-matrix pairs for valued structures are introduced in an analogous fashion as in the crisp case. Given such a pair and a weighting
\Omega
of
\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
we have two naturally associated rationals: the expected payoff in
\mathbf{A}
of the
n
-th column, when
n
is selected according to
\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}
; and the expected payoff in
\mathbf{B}
of the tuple obtained by applying
f
to the rows of the matrix, when
f
is selected according to
\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}
.

Definition 3.12

(Relation-matrix pairs, input and output payoffs)
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
be a pair of valued
\Sigma
-structures,
\mathscr{M}=\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, and
N
a finite set. We define

\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbf{A},N)=\{(\phi,M)\mid\phi\in\sigma,M\in A^{\textnormal{ar}(\phi)\times N},\ \forall n\in N\ \mathrm{col}_{n}(M)\in\textnormal{feas}(\phi^{\mathbf{A}})\}.

For an
N
-ary weighting
\Omega
of
\mathscr{M}
and
(\phi,M)\in\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbf{A},N)
we define

\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi,M]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\,\phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{col}_{n}(M))\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}[\phi,M]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{f\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}}\,\phi^{\mathbf{B}}(f\,\mathrm{rows}(M)).

For an
N
-ary weighting
\Omega
of
\mathscr{M}
and functions
\alpha:N\to\mathbb{Q}
,
\beta:\mathscr{M}^{(N)}\to\mathbb{Q}
we define

\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\alpha]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\,\alpha(n)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}[\beta]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{f\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}}\,\beta(f).

For a weighting
\Omega
, each relation-matrix pair thus gives us a point
(\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi,M],\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}[\phi,M])
in the plane
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. We call
\Omega
a
\kappa
-polymorphism if all these points lie on or above the line with slope
\kappa
going through
(c,s)
.

Definition 3.13

(Polymorphisms)
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
be a pair of valued
\Sigma
-structures,
\mathscr{M}=\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, and
c,s\in\mathbb{Q}
.

•

Let
\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
. An
N
-ary weighting
\Omega
of
\mathscr{M}
is
a
\kappa
-polymorphism
of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
if

\forall(\phi,M)\in\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbf{A},N)\ \ \Omega^{\mathrm{out}}[\phi,M]-s\geq\kappa(\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi,M]-c).

•

An
N
-ary weighting
\Omega
of
\mathscr{M}
is
a
polymorphism
of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
if it is a
\kappa
-polymorphism for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
.

•

A finite family
(\Omega_{j})_{j\in J}
of weightings of
\mathscr{M}
of arities
\mathcal{N}=(N_{j})_{j\in J}
is an
\mathcal{N}
-ary
plurimorphism
of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
if there exists
\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
such that every
\Omega_{j}
is a
\kappa
-polymorphism.

We will denote by
\kappa\mbox{-}\mathrm{Pol}^{(N)}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
,
\mathrm{Pol}^{(N)}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
, and
\mathrm{Plu}^{(\mathcal{N})}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
the sets of all
N
-ary
\kappa
-polymorphism,
N
-ary polymorphisms, and
\mathcal{N}
-ary plurimorphisms, respectively,
and by
\kappa\mbox{-}\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
,
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
, and
\mathrm{Plu}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
the collections of the corresponding morphisms indexed by their arities.

We remark that one could also define “
\infty
-polymorphisms” corresponding to vertical lines: the inequality would be
0\geq\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi,M]-c
, so having such a polymorphism is equivalent to
\mathbf{A}\leq c
. This would remove some (weak) assumptions in the following, but would cause some other inconveniences.

For a polymorphism
\Omega
, all points in
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
determined by relation-matrix pairs lie above or on a line going through
(c,s)
with a nonnegative slope
\kappa
. In particular, these points avoid the region
R=\{(x,y)\mid x\geq c,y<s\}
and so does any convex combination of these points (since half-planes are convex). It is easy to see that, conversely, if the convex hull of these points avoids
R
, then
\Omega
is a polymorphism. This is phrased more generally for plurimorphisms in item
(iii)
of the following proposition, in the language of probabilities. It is also geometrically clear that it is enough to require that the convex hulls of two points
avoid
R
, leading to item
(ii)
.

Proposition 3.14

(Alternative definitions of plurimorphisms)
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
be a pair of valued
\Sigma
-structures,
\mathscr{M}=\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
,
c,s\in\mathbb{Q}
, and
(\Omega_{j})_{j\in J}
a finite family of weightings of
\mathscr{M}
of arities
(N_{j})_{j\in J}
. The following are equivalent.

(i)

(\Omega_{j})_{j\in J}
is a plurimorphism of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
.

(ii)

Each pair
(\Omega_{j},\Omega_{j^{\prime}})
with
j,j^{\prime}\in J
is a plurimorphism of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
.

(iii)

For every probability distribution

\mu\in\Delta\{(j,\phi,M)\mid j\in J,(\phi,M)\in\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbf{A},N_{j})\}

We first state the items in the proposition in a geometric way using the following notation.

•

.

•

H_{\kappa}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}\mid y-s\geq\kappa(x-c)\}
for
\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
.

•

P[j,\phi,M]=(\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}_{j}[\phi,M],\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}_{j}[\phi,M])\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
for

,
(\phi,M)\in\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbf{A},N_{j})

The geometric translation is as follows. We also add an additional item
(iv)
that is equivalent to
(iii)
with

required to have at most two-element support.

(i)

There exists
\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
such that all the points

lie in

.

(ii)

For every

\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
such that all the points

and

lie in

.

(iii)

The convex hull of all the points

does not intersect
R
.

(iv)

For all pairs of points
P[j,\phi,M]
,
P[j^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},M^{\prime}]

R
.

The implications from
(i)
to

and from
(iii)
to

are trivial. Note that for every

, the halfplane

is convex and disjoint from

(i)
implies
(iii)
and

implies
(iv)

implies

Assume
(iv)
, in particular no point

lies in

P_{x}\geq c
implies
P_{y}\geq s
. Assume also that there is some point with

(thus

) as otherwise
\kappa=0
proves
(i)

Let
\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
be the minimum value such that all the points

that lie in

also lie in

, which exists since we have a finite number of points at least one of which is in
H_{\infty}
, and the sets

cover
H_{\infty}
and get inclusion-wise larger as

increases. Let

witnesses that

cannot be further decreased, that is,

and

We claim that every point

, which gives

. The claim holds when this point is in

and

and, by

, it intersects it at

. Therefore the line segment, in particular the point
(P^{\prime}_{x},P^{\prime}_{y})
, is on or above the line connecting
(P_{x},P_{y})
and
(c,s)
— the line

We remark that the notion of
(c,s)

In the crisp case, we observed that polymorphisms give us a way to combine solutions in
\mathbf{A}
to obtain solutions in

. Item

with

can be used to show a valued version of this fact: if

is at least

.
We formalize this in the result below, which can be seen as a valued version of

. For simplicity, we only formulate one implication and restrict to polymorphisms (rather than plurimorphisms).

Proposition 3.15

(Polymorphisms and payoffs)
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
be a pair of valued
\Sigma
-structures,
N
a finite set,
c,s\in\mathbb{Q}
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}
, and
\Omega
an
N
-ary
\kappa
-polymorphism of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
.

Then for every finite set
X
, every payoff
\Sigma
-formula
\Phi
over
X
, and every
M\in A^{X\times N}
such that every column is in
\textnormal{feas}(\Phi^{\mathbf{A}})
we have that

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{f\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}}\ \Phi^{\mathbf{B}}(f\mathrm{rows}(M))-s\,w(\Phi)\geq\kappa\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\ \Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{col}_{n}(M))-c\,w(\Phi)\right).

In particular,

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\ \Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{col}_{n}(M))\geq c\,w(\Phi)\implies\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{f\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}}\ \Phi^{\mathbf{B}}(f\mathrm{rows}(M))\geq s\,w(\Phi).

Proof.

Consider
M
and
\Phi=\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}\phi_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i})
as in the statement.
For
i\in I
we define a matrix
M_{i}^{\prime}
as in the proof of
Proposition

2.10
, that is,
M_{i}^{\prime}\in A^{\textnormal{ar}(\phi_{i})\times N}
has
M(\mathbf{x}_{i}(z),n)
at position
(z,n)
. Recall that
\mathrm{col}_{n}(M^{\prime}_{i})=\mathrm{col}_{n}(M)\mathbf{x}_{i}
and
\mathrm{rows}(M^{\prime}_{i})=\mathrm{rows}(M)\mathbf{x}_{i}
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\ \Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{col}_{n}(M))

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}\phi_{i}^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{col}_{n}(M)\mathbf{x}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\phi_{i}^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{col}_{n}(M)\mathbf{x}_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\phi_{i}^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{col}_{n}(M^{\prime}_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi_{i},M_{i}^{\prime}]

and similarly

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{f\in\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}}\Phi^{\mathbf{B}}(f\mathrm{rows}(M))=\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}[\phi_{i},M_{i}^{\prime}].

The inequality now follows from
\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}[\phi_{i},M_{i}^{\prime}]-s\geq\kappa(\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi_{i},M_{i}^{\prime}]-c)
.

We remark that an alternative proof of the second part is to normalize
\Phi
and apply item
(iii)
of
Proposition

3.14
to the probability distribution on
\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbf{A},N)
sampled by taking the relation-matrix pair
(\phi_{i},M_{i}^{\prime})
with probability
w_{i}
.
∎

Proposition 3.16

\displaystyle\forall\rho^{\mathrm{in}},\rho^{\mathrm{out}}\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}\ \ \forall(\Omega_{j})_{j\in J}\textnormal{ where $\Omega_{j}$ is an $N_{j}$-ary weighting of $\mathscr{M}$}

\displaystyle\Big(\big(\forall j\ \forall(\phi,M)\!\in\!\mathrm{Mat}(\mathbf{A},N_{j})\ \rho^{\mathrm{out}}(\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}_{j}[\phi,M]\!-\!s)\!\geq\!\rho^{\mathrm{in}}(\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}_{j}[\phi,M]\!-\!c)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\ \wedge\ \rho^{\mathrm{in}}\sum_{j\in J}\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}_{j}[\alpha_{j}]\geq 0\Big)

\displaystyle\implies\rho^{\mathrm{out}}\sum_{j\in J}\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}_{j}[\beta_{j}]\geq 0.

Noting that the right-hand side of the implication is always satisfied when
\rho^{\mathrm{out}}=0
, we can equivalently write (with
\kappa=\rho^{\mathrm{in}}/\rho^{\mathrm{out}}
)

\displaystyle\forall\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{+}_{0}

\displaystyle\ \forall(\Omega_{j})_{j\in J}\textnormal{ where $\Omega_{j}$ is an $N_{j}$-ary weighting of $\mathscr{M}$}

\displaystyle(\forall j\ \Omega_{j}\in\kappa\mbox{-}\mathrm{Pol}^{(N_{j})}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}))

\displaystyle\implies\left(\kappa\sum_{j\in J}\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}_{j}[\alpha_{j}]\geq 0\ \implies\sum_{j\in J}\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}_{j}[\beta_{j}]\geq 0\right),

which is exactly item
(i)
.
∎

4
Valued minion homomorphisms and reductions

We define a valued minion as an abstraction of a set of plurimorphisms. As discussed in the last paragraph of

Definition 4.1

(Minor of a weighting)
.

Let

Definition 4.2

(Valued minion)
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a minion. A

\mathscr{M}
is a collection
\mathbb{M}=(\mathbb{M}^{(\mathcal{N})})
indexed by finite families of finite sets

such that

1.

elements of
\mathbb{M}^{(\mathcal{N})}
are families

where each

-ary weighting of
\mathscr{M}
;

2.

\mathbb{M}
is closed under taking minors in the following sense:
if
(\Omega_{j})_{j\in J}\in\mathbb{M}^{(\mathcal{N})}

\Omega^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}

(given by some

if so is

Observe that a minor of a

-polymorphism of a valued promise template is again a
\kappa
-polymorphism. It follows that the collection of all plurimorphisms of a valued promise template is a valued minion over the minion of polymorphisms of its feasibility template.

Some polymorphisms of the feasibility template have zero probability in any
\kappa
-polymorphism of the valued template; they play no role in this sense. In the definition of valued minion homomorphisms, it is advantageous to restrict ourselves to polymorphisms that play some role,

for which we need the following definition.

Definition 4.3
.

Let

be a valued minion over an abstract minion

. The

of

Definition 4.4

(Valued minion homomorphisms)
.

Let
\mathbb{M}

\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
be valued minions over minions
\mathscr{M}
and

take their sum, and thus obtain a payoff formula

that

to

for each minor condition

.

and analogously for

.

Properties in item

\displaystyle\Psi^{\mathbf{A}}(h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U}\Phi_{u}^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{proj}^{D_{u}}_{h(u)})\geq\sum_{u\in U}(\gamma\alpha_{u}(h(u))+\delta^{\mathrm{in}}_{u}+cw(\Phi_{u}))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{u\in U}\gamma\alpha_{u}(h(u))+cw(\Phi)\geq cw(\Phi)=cw(\Psi).

On the other hand, if

Assume now that there are no formulas satisfying item

in

5
Examples of homomorphisms

Examples of valued minion homomorphisms of course include minion homomorphisms for crisp templates. More precisely, if
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},0,0)
and
(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime},0,0)
are templates such that all symbols in all four structures are interpreted as
(-\infty,0)
-valued relations and
\xi
is a minion homomorphism from
\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
to
\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
, then the probability distribution
\Xi
that assigns probability one to
\xi
is a valued minion homomorphism from
\mathrm{Plu}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},0,0)
to
\mathrm{Plu}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime},0,0)
. Notice that, in this case, the support of
\mathrm{Plu}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},0,0)
coincides with
\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
.

A crisp template can be in fact represented in many equivalent ways. This is discussed in the following example.

Example 5.1
.

If a valued promise template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
has perfect completeness and soundness, that is,
\mathbf{A}\leq c
and
\mathbf{B}\leq s
, then
\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
is clearly equivalent to
\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
, where
A=A^{\prime}
,
B=B^{\prime}
and relational symbols are interpreted as

\phi^{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}=\{\mathbf{a}\in A^{\textnormal{ar}(\phi)}\mid\phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{a})=c\},\quad\phi^{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}}=\{\mathbf{b}\in B^{\textnormal{ar}(\phi)}\mid\phi^{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{b})=s\}.

Many formally different valued templates
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
determine the same crisp template
(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
, for instance,

\textnormal{3LIN2}(1,1)
from
Example

3.5
and
3LIN2
from
Example

2.6
considered
(-\infty,0)
-valued with
c=s=0
(as above). Observe that all such templates
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
have the same
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathrm{Plu}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s))
(equal to
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
) and the same plurimorphisms when restricted to the support. Therefore, the reduction between such two versions of the same problem is covered by our reduction theorem as witnessed by the valued minion homomorphism
\Xi
that assigns probability one to the identity on the support.

We remark that this would not be the case if in
Definition

4.4
the minions
\mathscr{M}
,
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
were not restricted to the supports
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathbb{M})
,
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime})
. For instance, there is even no minion homomorphism from
\mathrm{PolFeas}(\textnormal{3LIN2}(1,1))
(which consists of all Boolean functions) to
\mathrm{PolFeas}(\textnormal{3LIN2})
(which consists of parity functions depending on odd number of coordinates), since e.g. every minion homomorphism maps commutative binary operations (which the first minion has) to commutative binary operations (which the second minion does not have).

This issue will be absent in all the other situations in this section. For convenience we will define the probability distribution
\Xi
on all the minion homomorphisms
\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
. The
\Xi
from the official definition is obtained by restricting the minion homomorphisms to the supports.

Recall that the reduction theorem fully explains hardness for crisp CSPs (but not for crisp PCSPs
[
4
]
).
In the remainder of this section we discuss two types of situations in which a reduction is explained by the reduction theorem in the valued setting.

5.1
Gadget reductions

We start with a very simple example of a gadget reduction.

Example 5.2

(
\textnormal{3LIN2}(c,s)\leq 5\textnormal{LIN2}(c,s)
,
c\geq s
)
.

Rename relational symbols for
\textnormal{3LIN2}(c,s)
(see
Example

2.6
) to
\phi^{\prime}_{0}
,
\phi^{\prime}_{1}
to distinguish them from relational symbols for
5\textnormal{LIN2}(c,s)
.
The reduction is: replace every constraint
\phi^{\prime}_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
in the input payoff formula by
\phi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{3},x_{3})
.

In the example, the gadget for
\phi^{\prime}_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is the payoff formula
\phi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{3},x_{3})
. More generally, one can use more complex gadgets, possibly including additional variables. Such reductions are covered by the reduction theorem via particularly strong valued minion homomorphisms: assigning probability one to the inclusion, see
Appendix

A
.

Sometimes a reduction can be obtained by replacing constraints by gadgets and merging some of the additional variables, cf.
[
11
]
. The following is an example
that can be explained by the reduction theorem.

Example 5.3

(
\textnormal{3LIN2}(c,s)\leq 4\textnormal{LIN2}(c,s)
,
c\geq s
)
.

We replace every constraint
\phi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
by
\phi_{i}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},z)
, where
z
is a fresh variable common to all the constraints. The reduction works since if an assignment for the new instance assigns 0 to
z
, then forgetting
z
gives an assignment for the original instance with the same payoff; and if
z
is assigned
1
, then we additionally flip the values
0\leftrightarrow 1
.

We verify that this reduction is explained by a valued minion homomorphism.
Let
\Xi
assign probability one to a single
\xi
defined as follows. For
f:\{0,1\}^{N}\to\{0,1\}

we define
\xi(f)=f
if
f(0,0,\dots,0)=0
and
\xi(f)=t\circ f
otherwise, where
t:\{0,1\}\to\{0,1\}
is the transposition.
We claim that

\Xi
is a valued minion homomorphism from
\mathrm{Plu}(4\textnormal{LIN2}(c,s))
to
\mathrm{Plu}(\textnormal{3LIN2}(c,s))
; it even maps
\kappa
-polymorphisms to
\kappa
-polymorphisms for each
\kappa
.

Proof.

Consider any relation-matrix pair
(\phi^{\prime}_{i},M^{\prime})
for
\textnormal{3LIN2}(c,s)
and the relation-matrix pair
(\phi_{i},M)
for
4\textnormal{LIN2}(c,s)
where
M
is
M^{\prime}
padded with a constant zero row.
The point
(\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi_{i},M],\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}[\phi_{i},M])

coincides with
(\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi^{\prime}_{i},M^{\prime}],\Xi(\Omega^{\mathrm{out}})[\phi^{\prime}_{i},M^{\prime}])
, so
\Xi
indeed maps
\kappa
-polymorphisms to
\kappa
-polymorphisms. To see this, observe first that
\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi_{i},M]=\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}[\phi^{\prime}_{i},M^{\prime}]
since adding trailing zeroes does not affect the parity of a tuple. For
\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}
, consider
f:\{0,1\}^{N}\to\{0,1\}
. If
f(0,\ldots,0)=0
, we have that
\phi^{\prime}_{i}(\xi(f)\mathrm{rows}(M^{\prime}))=\phi^{\prime}_{i}(f\mathrm{rows}(M^{\prime}))=\phi_{i}(f\mathrm{rows}(M))
. On the other hand, if
f(0,\ldots,0)=1
, we have
\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\xi(f)\mathrm{rows}(M^{\prime}))=1-\phi_{i}^{\prime}(f\mathrm{rows}(M^{\prime}))=\phi_{i}(f\mathrm{rows}(M))
. Noticing that
\Xi(\Omega^{\mathrm{out}})[\phi^{\prime}_{i},M^{\prime}]=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{f\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{out}}}\phi^{\prime}_{i}(\xi(f)\mathrm{rows}(M^{\prime}))
completes the proof.
∎

A systematic investigation of gadget-like reductions is a task that we leave for future work.

5.2
Homomorphisms to Gap Label Cover

Recall from
Example

3.7
that for every
1\geq\epsilon>0
, there exist
D,E
such that
\textnormal{GLC}_{D,E}(1,\epsilon)
is NP-hard. This result is a starting point for many inapproximability results, including those in an influential paper by Håstad
[
35
]
.

The following proposition gives a sufficient condition for a reduction from the Gap Label Cover, in particular, it isolates the core of Håstad’s results. The reduction and its correctness is simple (similar to the reduction from MC to PCSP in
Proposition

2.24
) and not needed in this paper, so we leave it to the reader.

The statement uses instances over the set of variables
A^{D}\cup A^{E}
. Note that assignments

exactly correspond to pairs of functions
(f_{D}:A^{D}\to A^{\prime},f_{E}:A^{E}\to A^{\prime})
and we write such assignments in this way.

Proposition 5.4

(Reductions from GLC)
.

Let
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},c,s)
be a valued promise template,
\mathscr{M}=\mathrm{PolFeas}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
,
D
and

finite disjoint sets, and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that there exist

•

mappings
\Lambda_{D}:\mathscr{M}^{(D)}\to\Delta D
and
\Lambda_{E}:\mathscr{M}^{(E)}\to\Delta E
,

•

for every

\Phi_{\pi}
over the set of variables

, and
a linear nondecreasing function

with

such that for every

1.

\Phi^{\mathbf{A}}_{\pi}(\mathrm{proj}^{D}_{d},\mathrm{proj}^{E}_{\pi(d)})\geq c
for every

, and

\gamma_{\pi}(\Phi^{\mathbf{B}}_{\pi}(f_{D},f_{E}))\leq\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\sim\Lambda_{D}(f_{D})\\
e\sim\Lambda_{E}(f_{E})\end{subarray}}\pi(d,e)

,

Then

.

Example 5.5

(
\textnormal{3LIN2}(1-\delta,1/2+\delta)
)
.

The first inapproximability result in
[

satisfies the conditions of

The mapping

(and similarly

[

Details are worked out in

.

We will show in

that the reduction in

Note that

Proposition 5.6

(Simplified homomorphisms to GLC)
.

Let

,

(i)

(ii)

For every

,

\mathbf{e}\in E^{N}

Proof.

The direction from

is straightforward:
we use item
(iii)
in
Proposition

3.14

and
\mathbf{e}

For the opposite direction, we note that if

Theorem 5.7

(Reductions from GLC via homomorphisms)
.

Under the assumptions of

(2)

In order to verify that this value is at least

6
Conclusion

Our main result,
Theorem

4.5
, shows that computational complexity is determined by symmetries in the vast framework of valued PCSPs. We see this result as a step towards the general goal of providing uniform descriptions of algorithms, tractability boundaries, and reductions.

Crisp non-promise CSPs already include many important combinatorial problems. Valued PCSPs generalize this framework in two directions: towards approximation (promises) and optimization (values). A further vast enlargement in the combinatorial direction would be provided by incorporating interesting classes of infinite structures. In fact,
[
54
,
22
]
already contribute to this project in the constant factor setting.

There are however many basic questions and theory-building tasks left open already for finite-domain valued PCSPs:
to characterize gadget reductions (or versions of definability) in terms of polymorphisms or plurimorphisms;
to characterize plurimorphism valued minions of templates;
to clarify whether plurimorphisms are necessary to determine computational complexity or enough information is provided already by polymorphisms;
to develop methods for proving nonexistence of homomorphisms;
to revisit the valued CSP dichotomy without fixed threshold
[
43
]
and Raghavendra’s result on unique games hardness of approximation for all MaxCSPs
[
49
]
;
among others.
An interesting special case for a full complexity classification is the valued non-promise CSPs with fixed threshold.

The most exciting (and likely challenging) research goal to us is to improve the reduction theorem so that it explains the PCP theorem
[
31
]
(hardness of Gap Label Cover) or even the Unique Games Conjecture
[
40
]
(hardness of Unique Games), or some special cases such as the 2-to-2 Conjecture (now theorem
[
41
]
). Crucially, both Gap Label Cover and Unique Games are within our framework, and we can now thus at least specify the aim: to weaken the concept of valued minion homomorphism so that, e.g., plurimorphisms of
\textnormal{GLC}_{D,E}(1,\epsilon)
homomorphically map to the projection minion. A reason for cautious optimism is the recent “Baby PCP” paper
[
9
]
that contributes to this effort in the crisp setting.

Appendix A
Definability

The starting point of the algebraic approach to the CSP was the discovery
[
38
]
that so-called primitive positive (pp-) definability of a CSP template in another template, which entails a simple gadget reduction between the corresponding CSPs, can be characterized in terms of polymorphisms.

This fact can be generalized to crisp PCSPs
[
48
,
23
]
.
The purpose of this appendix is to show how this first step generalizes to valued PCSPs. We again start by discussing the crisp setting and then move on to the valued one.

A.1
Crisp case

Informally, a pp-definition of a relation in a structure
\mathbf{A}
is a definition that uses relations in
\mathbf{A}
, the equality relation, conjunction, and existential quantification. For example, if the signature of
\mathbf{A}
contains a binary symbol
\phi
and a ternary symbol
\phi^{\prime}
, we may pp-define a ternary relation
\psi
by the formula

\psi(x,y,z)\quad\equiv\quad\exists u,v\ \phi(y,x)\wedge\phi(y,v)\wedge\phi^{\prime}(v,z,u)\wedge(u=v)\wedge(x=z).

The equalities can be eliminated by merging variables unless they are between free variables. In the above example, we can define the same relation by the formula

(x=z)\wedge\left(\exists u\ \phi(y,x)\wedge\phi(y,u)\wedge\phi^{\prime}(u,x,u)\right).

A convenient way to formally deal with existential quantification and equalities between free variables is to define a pp-definition of a relation
\psi
as a conjunctive formula
\Phi
over a set of variables
X
together with a mapping
\iota:\textnormal{ar}(\psi)\to X
which describes how the coordinates of
\psi
are matched to the free variables of
\Phi
. In the above example, let
\textnormal{ar}(\psi)=\{1,2,3\}
and take

X=\{x,y,u\},\quad\Phi=\phi(y,x)\wedge\phi(y,u)\wedge\phi^{\prime}(u,x,u),\quad\iota(1)=x,\iota(2)=y,\iota(3)=x.

The relation defined in
\mathbf{A}
by
\Phi
and
\iota
is

\psi=\{\mathbf{a}\iota\mid\exists\mathbf{a}\in A^{X}\ \Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{a})\}.

We say that a CSP template
(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{A}^{\prime})
is pp-definable from a CSP template
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A})
, where
A=A^{\prime}
, if each relation in
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is pp-definable from
\mathbf{A}
. Crucially, this happens if, and only if, every polymorphism of
(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A})
is a also a polymorphism of
(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{A}^{\prime})
. Equivalently, we have a particularly simple minion homomorphism
\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A})\to\mathrm{Pol}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{A}^{\prime})
, the inclusion. We remark that the polynomial reduction from
\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{A}^{\prime})
to
\textnormal{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A})
can then be directly done by replacing conjuncts of the input formula by their pp-definitions (gadgets)
\Phi,\iota
.

Proposition

A.2
proves a generalization of this fact to promise templates. The appropriate generalization of pp-definability is as follows: a PCSP template

is pp-definable from

if for each symbol
\psi
in the signature of
(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},\mathbf{B}^{\prime})
there is
\Phi
and
\iota
such that the relation defined in
\mathbf{A}
by
\Phi,\iota
contains
\psi^{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}}
, and the relation defined in
\mathbf{B}
by
\Phi,\iota
is contained in
\psi^{\mathbf{B}^{\prime}}
.

The formula

assuming the inclusion of polymorphism minions, is a canonical formula from

Appendix C
Constant factor valued PCSPs

In this section we discuss the constant factor approximation version of the theory from
[
39
]
, which was among the starting points of this work. The difference is that instead of fixing
c
(completeness) and
s
(soundness), we fix a constant factor
\kappa
, we let

be a part of the input, and define the soundness as

. We also simplify the accepting inequality
\Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(h)\geq cw(\Phi)
to
\Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(h)\geq c
because

is a part of the input anyway, and adjust the rejecting inequality accordingly.

The proofs are quite similar (and somewhat simpler) to the proofs in the previous section, therefore they are quite brief.
Moreover, we abuse the terminology and name the concepts in the same way as in the main part of the paper. If the need arises in the future to simultaneously use both, then perhaps the adjective “constant factor” could be added, e.g., one could define a
constant factor valued promise template
.

•

\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle=\kappa\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{n\sim\Omega^{\mathrm{in}}}\ \Phi^{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{col}_{n}(M)).\qed

Proposition C.5

(Characterization of templates)
.

Let

(ii)

Proof.

Definition C.6

Let

\mathbb{M}=(\mathbb{M}^{(N)})_{N\in\mathrm{FinSet}}

Definition C.7

(Valued minion homomorphisms)
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
,
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
be valued minions over minions
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}

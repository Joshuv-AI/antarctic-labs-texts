On the reducibility of scalar generalized Verma modules associated to two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras Thanks: * Corresponding author.
Zhanqiang Bai and Zhaojun Wang*

Abstract
. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} be a simple complex Lie algebra of classical type. A generalized Verma module induced from a one-dimensional representation of a parabolic subalgebra of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is called a scalar generalized Verma module of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . In this article, we use Gelfand-Kirillov dimension to determine the reducibility of scalar generalized Verma modules of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} associated to two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras of non-maximal type. Such a module exists only when 𝔤 = 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( n , ℂ ) \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C}) or 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 ​ ( 2 ​ n , ℂ ) \mathfrak{so}(2n,\mathbb{C}) for classical types. We find that the reducible points of these modules can be drawn in a two-dimensional complex plane.

1.
Introduction

The study of highest weight modules of Lie algebras plays an important role in representation theory. The Gelfand–Kirillov dimension is an important invariant to measure the size of an infinite-dimensional module, which was first introduced by Gelfand and Kirillov
[
12
]
. In this article,
we will use this invariant to study the reducibility problem of some generalized Verma modules. To state our problem and results, we need some notations.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie
algebra and
U(\mathfrak{g})
be its universal enveloping algebra.
Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra and denote by
\Delta
the root system associated to
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. We choose a positive root system

\Delta^{+}\subset\Delta
and a simple system
\Pi\subset\Delta^{+}
. Let
\rho
be the half sum of positive roots in
\Delta
. Let

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bar{\mathfrak{n}}
be the triangular decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
with nilradical
\mathfrak{n}
and its opposite nilradical
\bar{\mathfrak{n}}
. We choose a subset
I\subset\Pi
and it generates a subsystem

\Delta_{I}\subset\Delta
.
Let
\mathfrak{q}_{I}
be the standard parabolic subalgebra corresponding to
I
with Levi decomposition
\mathfrak{q}_{I}=\mathfrak{l}_{I}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{I}
. So when
I=\emptyset
, we have
\mathfrak{q}_{\emptyset}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{b}
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}_{I}=\mathfrak{l}_{I}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{I}
and
F(\lambda)
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{l}_{I}
-module with the highest weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. It can be viewed as a

\mathfrak{q}_{I}
-module by letting
\mathfrak{u}_{I}
act on it trivially. The
generalized Verma module
(GVM)
M_{I}(\lambda)
is defined by

M_{I}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{q}_{I})}F(\lambda).

In particular,
M(\lambda)=M_{\emptyset}(\lambda)
is called a
Verma module
.
The irreducible quotient of
M(\lambda)
is denoted by
L(\lambda)
. It is also the irreducible quotient of
M_{I}(\lambda)
. When
\dim(F(\lambda))=1
,
M_{I}(\lambda)
is called a
generalized Verma
module of scalar type
or
scalar generalized Verma module
. When
F(\lambda)
is infinite-dimensional, the reducibility problem also attracts
considerable attention in the literatures (see for example
[
11
,
16
,
17
,
20
]
).

The reducibility of generalized Verma modules is very important in representation theory. It is closely related to many other research areas (see for example
[
3
,
9
,
19
,
20
]
). In general, the standard method is to apply Jantzen’s criterion
[
15
]
to determine the reducibility of generalized Verma
modules.
But in general, this criterion is not very easy to use in application. For example, Enright-Wolf
[
10
]
studied the reducibility problem for scalar generalized Verma modules associated
to many maximal two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras. Kubo
[
18
]
found some simplified reducibility criteria to solve the reducibility problem for scalar generalized Verma modules associated to exceptional simple Lie algebras and some maximal parabolic subalgebras. By using Kubo’s simplification criteria, He
[
13
]
gave the reducibility for all scalar generalized Verma modules of Hermitian symmetric pairs. Then He-Kubo-Zierau
[
14
]
found the reducibility for all scalar generalized Verma modules associated to maximal parabolic subalgebras. Bai-Xiao
[
3
]
found the reducibility for all generalized Verma modules of Hermitian symmetric pairs. Xiao-Zhang
[
21
]
found some refinements of Jantzen’s simplicity criteria and used them to study the reducibility of generalized Verma modules.
All of the above results are based on Jantzen’s criterion
[
15
]
.

Recently Bai-Xiao
[
2
]
found that a scalar generalized Verma module
M_{I}(\lambda)
is reducible if and only if the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of its irreducible quotient
L(\lambda)
is strictly less than
\dim(\mathfrak{u}_{I})
. By using this result, they also obtained the same result with He
[
13
]
.

In general, it is not easy to compute the
Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions of explicit modules. Bai-Xie
[
2
]
and Bai-Xiao-Xie
[
4
]
found some simple algorithms using Robinson-Schensted insertion algorithm to compute the Gelfand-Kirillov dimensions of highest weight modules for all classical Lie algebras. By using these algorithms, Bai-Jiang
[
1
]
gave a new proof for
the reducibility of the scalar generalized Verma modules associated to maximal parabolic subalgebras.

In this article, we will use Gelfand-Kirillov dimension to study the reducibility problem for scalar generalized Verma modules associated
to two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras. The definition of
k
-step nilpotent lie algebras is given in the following:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{u}
be any nonzero Lie algebra. Put
\mathfrak{u}_{0}=\mathfrak{u}
,
\mathfrak{u}_{1}=[\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{u}]
, and
\mathfrak{u}_{k}=[\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{u}_{k-1}]

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we call
\mathfrak{u}_{k}
the
k
-th step of
\mathfrak{u}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}
is called nilpotent if
\mathfrak{u}_{k}=0
for some
k
, and it is called
k
-step nilpotent if
\mathfrak{u}_{k-1}\neq 0
and
\mathfrak{u}_{k}=0
.

If the niradical
\mathfrak{u}
of a parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
is
k
-step nilpotent then we say that
\mathfrak{q}
is a
k
-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebra
.

From Kubo
[
18
, Proposition 3.1.4]
, we find that most results on the reducibility of scalar generalized Verma modules are associated to maximal two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras, for example, see
[
2
,
3
,
13
,
14
,
10
]
. When the parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}
of a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is two-step nilpotent and not a maximal parabolic subalgebra, this case can only happen in type
A
and
D
when
\mathfrak{g}
is a classical Lie algebra.

Suppose
\mathfrak{q}_{I}\subset\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
is two-step nilpotent and not a maximal parabolic subalgebra. Then
\Pi\backslash I

contains two simple roots
\alpha_{p}
and
\alpha_{q}
by
[
18
, Proposition 3.1.4]
. Let
\xi_{i}
be the fundamental weight associated to the
i
-th simple root
\alpha_{i}\in\Pi
. If
M_{I}(\lambda)
is of scalar type, we know that

\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}
for some
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. Our criterion is
given as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Let
M_{I}(\lambda)
be a scalar generalized Verma module of
\mathfrak{g}
with highest weight
\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}
, where
\xi_{p}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{p}=e_{p}-e_{p+1}
,
\xi_{q}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{q}=e_{q}-e_{q+1}
. We write
q-p=k
,
(k\geq 1)
,
m=\min\{p,n-q\}
,
h=\max\{p,n-q\}
. Then
M_{I}(\lambda)
is reducible if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

When
z=z_{1}=z_{2}
, we have the following.

(a)

z\notin\mathbb{Z}
,

m\geq 1
and
z\in(\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})\bigcap(-\frac{k+m}{2},+\infty)
.

(b)

z\in\mathbb{Z}
. We have

(i)

If
m\geq k-1
, we have

z\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\lceil{\frac{m}{2}}\rceil-\lfloor{\frac{k-1}{2}}\rfloor+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if $k$ is even}\\
-\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor-\lfloor{\frac{k-1}{2}}\rfloor+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if $k$ is odd}\\
\end{array}\right..

(ii)

If
0<m<k-1
, we have

z\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\max\{\lceil{\frac{k+m}{2}}\rceil,h\}+1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if $h<k$ }\\
-k+1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if $h\geq k$}\\
\end{array}\right..

(iii)

If
m=0
, we have

z\in-\min\{h,k\}+1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(2)

When
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
, we have the following.

(a)

If
n-q=0
, we have

z_{1}\in 1-\min\{p,n-p\}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}.

(b)

If
n-q\geq 1
, we have the following.

(i)

z_{2}\in 1-\min\{q-p,n-q\}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
, or

(ii)

z_{1}\in 1-\min\{p,q-p\}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, or

(iii)

(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
, and

z_{1}+z_{2}\in-q+p-\min\{p,n-q\}+\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{q}_{I}\subset\mathfrak{so}(2n,\mathbb{C})
is two-step nilpotent and not a maximal parabolic subalgebra. Then
\Pi\backslash I

contains two simple roots
\alpha_{p}
and
\alpha_{q}
(
p,q\in\{1,n-1,n\}
) by
[
18
, Proposition 3.1.4]
. Let
\xi_{i}
be the fundamental weight associated to the
i
-th simple root
\alpha_{i}\in\Pi
. If
M_{I}(\lambda)
is of scalar type, we know that

\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}
for some
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. Our criterion is
given as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(2n,\mathbb{C})
.
M_{I}(\lambda)
is a scalar generalized Verma module with highest weight
\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}

(p,q\in\{1,n-1,n\})
, where
\xi_{p}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{p}=e_{p}-e_{p+1}
,
\xi_{q}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{q}=e_{q}-e_{q+1}
. Then
M_{I}(\lambda)
is reducible if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

If
p=1
and
q=n-1
or
n
, we have

(a)

z_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, or

(b)

z_{1}+z_{2}\in-n+2+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, or

(c)

z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\textnormal{~and~}z_{2}\in\begin{cases}-n+3+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n~\textnormal{is odd}\\
-n+4+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n~\textnormal{is even}\end{cases}
.

(2)

If
p=n-1
and
q=n
, we have

(a)

z_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, or

(b)

z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
, or

(c)

z_{1}+z_{2}\in\begin{cases}-n+1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n~\textnormal{is odd}\\
-n+2+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n~\textnormal{is even}\end{cases}
.

The paper is organized as follows. The necessary preliminaries for two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras and Gelfand-Kirillov dimension are given in Sections
2
and
3
. In Section
4
, we give the reducibility of scalar generalized Verma modules for type
A_{n-1}
. In Section

2.
Two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie
algebra and let
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

be a fixed Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. There is a simple characterization of
k
-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras in the following.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
18
, Proposition 3.1.4]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex simple Lie algebra with highest root
\gamma
, and
\mathfrak{q}_{S}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
be the parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
that is parameterized by
S
with
S=\{\alpha_{i_{1}},\cdots,\alpha_{i_{r}}\}\subset\Pi
. Then
\mathfrak{u}
is k-step nilpotent if and only if
k=m_{i_{1}}+m_{i_{2}}+\cdots+m_{i_{r}}
, where
m_{i_{j}}

are the
multiplicities
of
\alpha_{i_{j}}
in
\gamma
.

From
[
8
]
, we can find the explicit expression of highest root
\gamma
. For type
B
and
C
, all two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras are maximal ones.
For type
A
, a two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
corresponds to the subsets
I=\Pi\setminus\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\}

(
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\in\Pi
). For type
D
, a two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{q}
is maximal or it corresponds to the subsets
I=\Pi\setminus\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\}

(
i,j\in\{1,n-1,n\}
).

In this section, we give the explicit values of
\dim(\mathfrak{u})
. The results are as follows.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n-1}
, for
I=\Pi\setminus\{\alpha_{p},\alpha_{p+1}\}
, we will have

\Pi=\{e_{1}-e_{2},e_{2}-e_{3},\cdots,e_{n-1}-e_{n}\}
.

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})=\{e_{i}-e_{j}|1\leq i<j\leq p~\text{or}~p+2\leq i<j\leq n\}.

So
\dim(\mathfrak{u})=(p+1)(n-p)-1=p(n-p)+(n-p-1)
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A_{n-1}
, for
I=\Pi\setminus\{\alpha_{p},\alpha_{q}\},q-p>1
, we will have

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})=\{e_{i}-e_{j}|1\leq i<j\leq p~\text{or}~p+1\leq i<j\leq q~\text{or}~q+1\leq i<j\leq n\}.

So
\dim(\mathfrak{u})=q(n-q)+p(q-p)
.

(3)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
D_{n}
, for
I=\Pi\setminus\{\alpha_{p},\alpha_{q}\}
, we will have

\Pi=\{e_{1}-e_{2},e_{2}-e_{3},\cdots,e_{n-1}-e_{n},e_{n-1}+e_{n}\}
.

\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})
=
\begin{cases}\{e_{i}-e_{j}|2\leq i<j\leq n-1\}\cup\{e_{i}+e_{n}|2\leq i\leq n-1\}&\text{if}~p=1,q=n-1\\
\{e_{i}-e_{j}|2\leq i<j\leq n\}&\text{if}~p=1,q=n\\
\{e_{i}-e_{j}|1\leq i<j\leq n-1\}&\text{if}~p=n-1,q=n\end{cases}
.

Then we can have
\dim(\mathfrak{u})=\frac{1}{2}(n^{2}+n-2)
.

3.
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension

In this section, we recall the algorithms of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of highest wight modules found by Bai-Xie
[
2
]
and Bai-Xiao-Xie
[
4
]
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
U(\mathfrak{g})
-module. Fix a finite dimensional generating subspace
M_{0}
of
M
. Let
U_{n}(\mathfrak{g})
be the standard filtration of
U(\mathfrak{g})
. Set
M_{n}=U_{n}(\mathfrak{g})\cdot M_{0}
and

\text{gr}(M)=\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\text{gr}_{n}M,

where
\text{gr}_{n}M=M_{n}/{M_{n-1}}
. Thus
\text{gr}(M)
is a graded module of
\text{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g}))\simeq S(\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension
of
M
is defined by

\operatorname{GKdim}M=\varlimsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\dim(U_{n}(\mathfrak{g})M_{0})}{\log n}.

It is easy to see that the above definition is independent of the choice of
M_{0}
.

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie
algebra. Let
\{\xi_{1},..,\xi_{n}\}\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be the fundamental weights. For any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we have

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(\lambda+\xi_{i})\leq\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(\lambda).

In particular, if
M_{I}(\lambda)
is reducible, then
M_{I}(\lambda+\xi_{i})
is also reducible.

Proof.

See the proof in
[
2
, Lemma 4.4]
.
∎

The following Lemma is very useful in our proof.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

A scalar generalized Verma module
M_{I}(\lambda)
is irreducible if and only if
\emph{GKdim}\>L(\lambda)=\dim(\mathfrak{u})
.

For a totally ordered set
\Gamma
, we denote by
\mathrm{Seq}_{n}(\Gamma)
the set of sequences
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
of length
n
with
x_{i}\in\Gamma
. In our paper, we usually take
\Gamma
to be
\mathbb{Z}
or a coset of
\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
p(x)
be the shape of the Young tableau
P(x)
obtained by applying Robinson-Schensted algorithm (
[
5
]
) to
x\in\mathrm{Seq}_{n}(\Gamma)
.

For a Young diagram
P
, use
(k,l)
to denote the box in the
k
-th row and the
l
-th column.
We say the box
(k,l)
is
even
(resp.
odd
) if
k+l
is even (resp. odd). Let
p_{i}^{ev}
(resp.
p_{i}^{odd}
) be the numbers of even (resp. odd) boxes in the
i
-th row of the Young diagram
P
.
One can easily check that

p_{i}^{ev}=\begin{cases}\left\lceil\frac{p_{i}}{2}\right\rceil&\text{ if }i\text{ is odd},\\
\left\lfloor\frac{p_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor&\text{ if }i\text{ is even},\end{cases}\quad p_{i}^{odd}=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{p_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor&\text{ if }i\text{ is odd},\\
\left\lceil\frac{p_{i}}{2}\right\rceil&\text{ if }i\text{ is even}.\end{cases}

(3.4)

Here for
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lfloor a\rfloor
is the largest integer
n
such that
n\leq a
, and
\lceil a\rceil
is the smallest integer
n
such that
n\geq a
. For convenience, we set

p^{ev}=(p_{1}^{ev},p_{2}^{ev},\cdots)\quad\mbox{and}\quad p^{odd}=(p_{1}^{odd},p_{2}^{odd},\cdots).

For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathrm{Seq}_{n}(\Gamma)
, set

\displaystyle{x}^{-}=

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n},-x_{n},-x_{n-1},\cdots,-x_{2},-x_{1}).

Proposition 3.5

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
\lambda+\rho=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
be integral. Then

{\rm GKdim}\>L(\lambda)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}-\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}(i-1)p(\lambda+\rho)_{i}&\textnormal{if}\>\Delta=A_{n-1}\\
n^{2}-n-\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}(i-1)p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})_{i}^{ev}&\textnormal{if}\>\Delta=D_{n}\end{array}\right..

When
\lambda
is non-integral, we need some more notations before we give the algorithm of GK dimension.

We define three functions
F_{A}
,
F_{D}
as

\displaystyle F_{A}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}(k-1)p_{k},

\displaystyle F_{D}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}(k-1)p_{k}^{ev},

where
p(x)=(p_{1},p_{2},\cdots)
is the shape of the Young tableau
P(x)
.

Definition 3.6
.

Fix
\lambda+\rho=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
, we define
[\lambda]
to be the set of maximal subsequence
x
of
\lambda+\rho
such that any two entries of
x
has an integral difference.

For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(2n,\mathbb{C})
, we define
[\lambda]
to be the set of maximal subsequence
x
of
\lambda+\rho
such that any two entries of
x
have an integral difference or sum. In this case, we set
[\lambda]_{1}
(resp.
[\lambda]_{2}
) to be the subset of
[\lambda]
consisting of sequences with all entries belonging to
\mathbb{Z}
(resp.
\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
).
Since there is at most one element in
[\lambda]_{1}
and
[\lambda]_{2}
, we denote them by
(\lambda+\rho)_{(0)}
and
(\lambda+\rho)_{(\frac{1}{2})}
.
We set
[\lambda]_{1,2}=[\lambda]_{1}\cup[\lambda]_{2},\quad[\lambda]_{3}=[\lambda]\setminus[\lambda]_{1,2}
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}
and
x=(\lambda_{i_{1}},\lambda_{i_{2}},\cdots\lambda_{i_{r}})\in[\lambda]_{3}
. Let
y=(\lambda_{j_{1}},\lambda_{j_{2}},\cdots,\lambda_{j_{p}})
be the maximal subsequence of
x
such that
j_{1}=i_{1}
and the difference of any two entries of
y
is an integer. Let
z=(\lambda_{k_{1}},\lambda_{k_{2}},\cdots,\lambda_{k_{q}})
be the subsequence obtained by deleting
y
from
x
, which is possible empty.
Define

\tilde{x}=(\lambda_{j_{1}},\lambda_{j_{2}},\cdots,\lambda_{j_{p}},-\lambda_{k_{q}},-\lambda_{k_{q-1}},\cdots,-\lambda_{k_{1}}).

Proposition 3.8

(
[
5
, Theorem 4.6]
and
[
4
, Theorem 5.7]
)
.

The GK dimension of
L(\lambda)
can be computed as follows.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
,

\operatorname{GKdim}L(\lambda)=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}-\sum_{x\in[\lambda]}F_{A}(x).

(2)

If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(2n,\mathbb{C})
,

\operatorname{GKdim}L(\lambda)=n^{2}-n-F_{D}((\lambda+\rho)_{(0)}^{-})-F_{D}((\lambda+\rho)_{(\frac{1}{2})}^{-})-\sum_{x\in[\lambda]_{3}}F_{A}(\tilde{x}).

Remark 3.9
.

From Proposition
3.8
, we know that the GK dimension of a highest weight module
L(\lambda)
only depends on the shape of some Young tableaux associated with

4.
Reducibility of scalar generalized Verma modules for type
A_{n-1}

First we recall a useful result which will be used in our proof.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
or
[
7
, Theorem 5.5]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(\mathfrak{n},\mathbb{C})
.
Suppose the parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
corresponds to two simple roots
\{\alpha_{p},\alpha_{q}\}
. A simple module
L(\lambda)
(
\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}
is integral) has
\operatorname{GKdim}L(\lambda)=\dim(\mathfrak{u})
if and only if
P(\lambda+\rho)
has the same shape with the Young diagram consisting of three columns
\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}=\{p,q-p,n-q\}
.

In the following, we will give the reducible points for type
A
. Since we’re talking about two-step nilpotent parabolic subalgebras, we need to compute the
GK
dimension of the irreducible quotient
L(\lambda)
of the scalar generalized Verma module

M_{I}(\lambda)
(with
\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}
and
\xi_{p}
being the fundamental weight corresponding to the simple root
\alpha_{p}
,
\xi_{q}
being the fundamental weight corresponding to the simple root
\alpha_{q}
).

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(\mathfrak{n},\mathbb{C})
, and
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-1},\alpha_{p+1},\dots,\alpha_{q-1},\alpha_{q+1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}\}
,
\alpha_{p}=e_{p}-e_{p+1}
,
\alpha_{q}=e_{q}-e_{q+1}
.

We have

\xi_{p}=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{p}-\frac{p}{n}(e_{1}+\cdots+e_{n}),

\xi_{q}=e_{1}+\cdots+e_{q}-\frac{q}{n}(e_{1}+\cdots+e_{n}).

Denote
t=\frac{n-p}{n}
,
s=\frac{n-q}{n}
, then we have

\xi_{p}=(\underbrace{t,\cdots,t}_{p},\underbrace{t-1,\cdots,t-1}_{n-p})
,
\xi_{q}=(\underbrace{s,\cdots,s}_{q},\underbrace{s-1,\cdots,s-1}_{n-q})
.

In
[
9
]
, we know that
2\rho=(n-1,n-3,\dots,-n+3,-n+1)
, thus

\rho=(\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n-3}{2},\dots,\frac{-n+3}{2},\frac{-n+1}{2}),

\begin{split}\lambda+\rho=(&\underbrace{z_{1}t+z_{2}s+\frac{n-1}{2},\dots,z_{1}t+z_{2}s+\frac{n-2p+1}{2}}_{p},\\
&\underbrace{(t-1)z_{1}+z_{2}s+\frac{n-2p-1}{2},\dots,(t-1)z_{1}+z_{2}s+\frac{n-2q+1}{2}}_{q-p},\\
&\underbrace{(t-1)z_{1}+(s-1)z_{2}+\frac{n-2q-1}{2},\dots,(t-1)z_{1}+(s-1)z_{2}+\frac{-n+1}{2}}_{n-q}).\end{split}

We denote
\lambda+\rho=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
.

Since there are two parameters, we will divide our arguments into the following two cases.

(1)

z_{1}=z_{2}
;

(2)

z_{1}\neq z_{2}
.

Suppose
M_{I}(\lambda)
is our scalar generalized Verma module with highest weight
\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}
, where
\xi_{p}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{p}=e_{p}-e_{p+1}
,
\xi_{q}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{q}=e_{q}-e_{q+1}
.
By Lemma
3.2
, if
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a reducible for
M_{I}(\lambda)
, then
(z_{1}+k,z_{2}+d)
is also a reducible for any
k,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
When
z_{1}
or
z_{2}
takes the minimal value, we will call
(z_{1},z_{2})

a boundary reducible point
of
M_{I}(\lambda)
. When
z_{1}=z_{2}
, this boundary reducible point is also called
a first reducible point
of
M_{I}(\lambda)
since it is minimal in the diagonal line. See

4.1.
Reducibility when

When

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
.
M_{I}(\lambda)
is a scalar generalized Verma module with highest weight
\lambda=z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q})
, where
\xi_{p}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{p}=e_{p}-e_{p+1}
,
\xi_{q}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{q}=e_{q}-e_{q+1}
. We write
q-p=k
,
(k\geq 1)
,
m=\min\{p,n-q\}
,
h=\max\{p,n-q\}
. Then
M_{I}(\lambda)
is reducible if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

z\notin\mathbb{Z}
,

m\geq 1
and
z\in(\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})\bigcap(-\frac{k+m}{2},+\infty)
.

(2)

z\in\mathbb{Z}
. We have

(a)

If
m\geq k-1
, we have

z\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\lceil{\frac{m}{2}}\rceil-\lfloor{\frac{k-1}{2}}\rfloor+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if $k$ is even}\\
-\lfloor{\frac{m}{2}}\rfloor-\lfloor{\frac{k-1}{2}}\rfloor+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if $k$ is odd}\\
\end{array}\right..

(b)

If
0<m<k-1
, we have

z\in\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\max\{\lceil{\frac{k+m}{2}}\rceil,h\}+1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if $h<k$ }\\
-k+1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if $h\geq k$}\\
\end{array}\right..

(c)

If
m=0
, we have

z\in-\min\{h,k\}+1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

\begin{split}\lambda+\rho=(&\underbrace{(s+t)z+\frac{n-1}{2},\dots,(s+t)z+\frac{n-2p+1}{2}}_{p},\\
&\underbrace{(s+t-1)z+\frac{n-2p-1}{2},\dots,(s+t-1)z+\frac{n-2q+1}{2}}_{q-p},\\
&\underbrace{(s+t-2)z+\frac{n-2q-1}{2},\dots,(s+t-2)z+\frac{-n+1}{2}}_{n-q}).\end{split}

We have
\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{p+1}=z+1
,
\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{q+1}=2z+1+q-p
and
\lambda_{q}-\lambda_{q+1}=z+1
.

Step 1.
Suppose
z\notin\mathbb{Z}
.

Step 1.1
When
z\notin\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
,
by Proposition
3.8
we know that
\lambda+\rho
corresponds to three Young tableaux and these three Young tableaux have only one column. The numbers of boxes in these three columns are
p,q-p
and
n-q
. Then we have

\text{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))=\begin{cases}p(n-p)+(n-q)&\text{if}~q-p=1\\
q(n-q)+p(q-p)&\text{if}~q-p>1\end{cases}.

So
\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))=\dim(\mathfrak{u})
and
M_{I}(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))
is irreducible .

Step 1.2.
When
z\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
, equivalently
\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{q+1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, by Proposition
3.8
we know that
\lambda+\rho
corresponds to two Young tableaux. One of these Young tableaux has one column and the number of boxes in this column is
q-p
. The other Young tableau may have two columns. We denote it by
P_{2}
.

Recall that we denote
m=\min\{p,n-q\}
,
h=\max\{p,n-q\}
. Then we have the follows.

Step 1.2.1.

When
m=0
(equivalently
n-q=0
), the Young tableau
P_{2}
has one column and the number of boxes in this column is
p
, so
M_{I}(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))
is irreducible.

Step 1.2.2.

When
m\geq 1
, the Young tableau
P_{2}
may have two columns.

Firstly,
when
z>-\frac{k+1}{2}=-\frac{q-p+1}{2}
, equivalently
\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{q+1}
, the Young tableau
P_{2}
has one column and the number of boxes in this column is
n-q+p
. So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))
is reducible.

Then when
-\frac{k+1}{2}\geq z>-\frac{k+m}{2}
, equivalently
\lambda_{p}\leq\lambda_{q+1}
,
\lambda_{p}>\lambda_{n}
(if
m=n-q
) or
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{q+1}
(if
m=p
), the Young tableau
P_{2}
has two columns and the numbers of boxes in these columns are
p+r
and
n-q-r
, where
0<r<n-q
. So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))
is reducible.

When
z=-\frac{k+m}{2}
, the Young tableau
P_{2}
has two columns and the numbers of boxes in these columns are
p
and
n-q
, so
M_{I}(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))
is irreducible.

Thus when
z\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
M_{I}(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))
is reducible if and only if
z\in(\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})\bigcap(-\frac{k+m}{2},+\infty)
.

Step 2.
When
z\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\lambda+\rho
corresponds to one Young tableau.

According to Lemma
3.2
we need to find the first reducible point for

When
z>-1

z+1>0

M_{I}(z(\xi_{p}+\xi_{q}))
is reducible in this case.

When
z\leq-1

z+1\leq 0
, the Young tableau may consist of two or three columns. Let’s talk about the reducibility in a
case-by-case way.

Step 2.1.
First we consider the case when
m=0
that is
n-q=0

Step 2.1.1.
When
k=1
, we will have the follows.

If
z=-1
, we will have

(

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(n,\mathbb{C})
.
M_{I}(\lambda)
is a scalar generalized Verma module with highest weight
\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}
(
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
), where
\xi_{p}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{p}=e_{p}-e_{p+1}
,
\xi_{q}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{q}=e_{q}-e_{q+1}
. We write
m=\min\{p,n-q\}
,
k=q-p
. Then
M_{I}(\lambda)
is reducible if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

If
n-q=0
, we have
z_{1}\in 1-\min\{p,n-p\}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

If
n-q\geq 1
, we have

(a)

z_{1},z_{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
z_{1}+z_{2}\in-k-m+\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, or

(b)

z_{1}\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
z_{2}\in 1-\min\{k,n-q\}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, or

(c)

z_{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
z_{1}\in 1-\min\{p,k\}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, or

(d)

(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, and

(i)

z_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
z_{2}>-1
, or

(ii)

z_{1}>-1
,
z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, or

(iii)

z_{1}\leq-1,z_{2}\leq-1
, and

(A)

z_{1}+z_{2}>-k-m
, or

(B)

z_{1}>-\min\{k,p\}
,
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
, or

(C)

z_{2}>-\min\{k,n-q\}
,
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
.

Proof.

\begin{split}\lambda+\rho=(&\underbrace{z_{1}t+z_{2}s+\frac{n-1}{2},\dots,z_{1}t+z_{2}s+\frac{n-2p+1}{2}}_{p},\\
&\underbrace{(t-1)z_{1}+z_{2}s+\frac{n-2p-1}{2},\dots,(t-1)z_{1}+z_{2}s+\frac{n-2q+1}{2}}_{q-p},\\
&\underbrace{(t-1)z_{1}+(s-1)z_{2}+\frac{n-2q-1}{2},\dots,(t-1)z_{1}+(s-1)z_{2}+\frac{-n+1}{2}}_{n-q}).\end{split}

We have
\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{p+1}=z_{1}+1
,
\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{q+1}=z_{1}+z_{2}+1+q-p
and
\lambda_{q}-\lambda_{q+1}=z_{2}+1
.

, and

.

5.
Reducibility of scalar generalized Verma modules for type
D_{n}

In this section, we write
n=2k
if
n
is an even integer and
n=2k+1
if
n
is an odd integer.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}(2n,\mathbb{C})
with
n\geq 4
.
M_{I}(\lambda)
is a scalar generalized Verma module with highest weight
\lambda=z_{1}\xi_{p}+z_{2}\xi_{q}

(p,q\in\{1,n-1,n\})
, where
\xi_{p}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{p}=e_{p}-e_{p+1}
,
\xi_{q}
is the fundamental weight corresponding to
\alpha_{q}=e_{q}-e_{q+1}
. Then
M_{I}(\lambda)
is reducible if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

If
p=1
and
q=n-1
or
n
, one of the following holds:

(a)

z_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
.

(b)

z_{1}+z_{2}\in-n+2+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\textnormal{~if}~z_{1},z_{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}~\textnormal{or}~z_{1}=-1
.

(c)

z\in\lfloor-\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+\frac{3}{2}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\textnormal{~if}~z_{1}=z_{2}=z\notin\mathbb{Z}
.

(d)

z_{2}\in\begin{cases}-n+3+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n=2k+1\\
-n+4+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n=2k\end{cases}\textnormal{if}~z_{1}\in\mathbb{C},~\textnormal{or}~z_{1}=z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
p=n-1
and
q=n
, one of the following holds:

(a)

z_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
.

(b)

z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
.

(c)

z_{1}=z_{2}\in\begin{cases}-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n=2k+1\\
-\frac{n}{2}+1+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n=2k\end{cases}
.

(d)

z_{1}+z_{2}\in\begin{cases}-n+1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n=2k+1\\
-n+2+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\textnormal{if}~n=2k\end{cases}
.

Proof.

\mathbf{Step~1.}
Firstly, we consider the case
p=1,q=n-1
.

Now we have
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l})=\{\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-2},\alpha_{n}\}
, where
\alpha_{i}=e_{i}-e_{i+1}

(1\leq i\leq n-1)
and
\alpha_{n}=e_{n-1}+e_{n}
.
Correspondingly we have

\xi_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)
and

\xi_{n-1}=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2},\cdots,\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})
.

Since

\rho=(n-1,n-2,\dots,1,0)
, we have

\lambda+\rho=(z_{1}+\frac{1}{2}z_{2}+n-1,\frac{1}{2}z_{2}+n-2,\cdots,\frac{1}{2}z_{2}+1,-\frac{1}{2}z_{2}).

Step 1.1.
When
z_{1}=z_{2}
, denote
z=z_{1}=z_{2}
, then we can write

\lambda+\rho=(\frac{3}{2}z+n-1,\frac{1}{2}z+n-2,\cdots,\frac{1}{2}z+1,-\frac{1}{2}z)
.

(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(\frac{3}{2}z+n-1,\cdots,-\frac{1}{2}z,\frac{1}{2}z,\cdots,-(\frac{3}{2}z+n-1))
.

Step 1.1.1.
When
z\in\mathbb{Z}
, equivalently
\lambda+\rho
is integral, we have the following.

(1)

Suppose
n
is odd. Then we have the follows.

(a)

When
z\geq 0
, we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(1,\underbrace{0,1,0,1,0,1,\cdots,0,1}_{2n-2}).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))&=n^{2}-n-(2+4+\cdots+2n-2)\\
&=n^{2}-n-(n^{2}-n)=0<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible.

(b)

If
z=-1
,
(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(n-\frac{5}{2},n-\frac{5}{2},\cdots,\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2},\cdots,-n+\frac{5}{2},-n+\frac{5}{2})
.

When
n=3
, we will have
p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,1).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))=n^{2}-n-1=5=\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is irreducible.

When
n\geq 4
, we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(1,1,1,1,\underbrace{1,0,1,0,1,\cdots,0}_{2n-8}).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+3+4+6+\cdots+2n-6)\\
&=4n-10<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible.

(c)

If
z=-n+3
,
(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(-\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{7}{2},\cdots,\frac{1}{2}n-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{3}{2},\cdots,\frac{1}{2}n-\frac{7}{2})
.

And we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,\underbrace{1,1,1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-3},0,1).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+3+4+\cdots+n-3+n-1)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-2<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible.

(d)

If
z=-n+2
,
(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(-\frac{1}{2}n+2,\cdots,\frac{1}{2}n-1,-\frac{1}{2}n+1,\cdots,\frac{1}{2}n-2)
.

And we will have
p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,\underbrace{1,1,1,1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-2}).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+3+4+\cdots+n-2)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-1=\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is irreducible.

Therefore
z=-n+3
is the first reducible point when
n
is odd.

(2)

Suppose
n
is even. Then we have the follows.

(a)

When
z\geq-1
, it is the same with the case for
n
being odd.

So we have
\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))<\dim(\mathfrak{u})
, and
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible.

(b)

If
z=-2
,
(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(n-4,n-3,\cdots,1,-1,\cdots,-n+3,-n+4)
.

When
n=4
, we will have
p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,1,1).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))=n^{2}-n-(1+2)=9=\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is irreducible.

When
n\geq 4
, we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,\underbrace{1,1,1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-4},0,1,0,1).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+\cdots+n-4+n-2+n)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-4<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible.

(c)

If
z=-n+4
,

(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(-\frac{1}{2}n+5,\cdots,\frac{1}{2}n-2,-\frac{1}{2}n+2,\cdots,\frac{1}{2}n-5)
.

And we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,\underbrace{1,1,1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-4},0,1,0,1).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-4<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible.

(d)

If
z=-n+3
,
(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(-\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{7}{2},\cdots,\frac{1}{2}n-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{3}{2},\cdots,\frac{1}{2}n-\frac{7}{2})
.

And we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,\underbrace{1,1,1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-3},1).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-1=\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is irreducible.

Thus
when
n
is even, the first reducible point is:

z=

\begin{cases}-1\qquad&\text{if}~n=4\\
-n+4\qquad&\text{if}~n\geq 6\end{cases}
.

Therefore, when
z\in\mathbb{Z}
, the reducible points are:

z\in

\begin{cases}-n+3+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\text{if}~n=2k+1\\
\begin{cases}-1+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\text{if}~n=4\\
-n+4+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}&\text{if}~n\geq 6\end{cases}&\text{if}~n=2k\end{cases}
.

Step 1.1.2.
When
z\notin\mathbb{Z}
, we have the following.

(1)

When
z\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
, we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-}_{(0)})^{ev}=p((\lambda+\rho)^{-}_{(\frac{1}{2})})^{ev}=0
and
\lambda+\rho\in[\lambda]_{3}
.

Denote
x:=\lambda+\rho
. Thus
\tilde{x}=(\frac{3}{2}z+n-1,-\frac{1}{2}z,-\frac{1}{2}z-1,-\frac{1}{2}z-2,\cdots,-\frac{1}{2}z-n+2)
. Then we have the following.

(a)

Suppose
n
is odd. Then we have the follows.

(i)

When
z>-\frac{1}{2}(n-1)
, we will have

p(\widetilde{x})=(1,\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-1}).

Then by Proposition
3.8
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+3+4+\cdots+n-1)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible.

(ii)

If
z=-\frac{1}{2}n
, we will have

p(\widetilde{x})=(2,\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-2}).

Then by Proposition
3.8
,

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-1=\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is irreducible.

Therefore
z=-\frac{n}{2}+1
is the first reducible point for this case.

(b)

Suppose
n
is even. Then we have the follows.

(i)

When
z>-\frac{1}{2}(n-1)
, we will have

p(\widetilde{x})=(1,\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-1}).

Then by Proposition
3.8
,

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible.

(ii)

If
z=-\frac{1}{2}(n-1)
, we will have

p(\widetilde{x})=(2,\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-2}).

Then by Proposition
3.8
,

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-1=\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is irreducible.

Therefore
z=-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3}{2}
is the first reducible point for this case.

(2)

When
z\notin\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-}_{(0)})^{ev}=p((\lambda+\rho)^{-}_{(\frac{1}{2})})^{ev}=0
and

x_{1}=(\frac{1}{2}z+n-2,\frac{1}{2}z+n-3\cdots,\frac{1}{2}z+1,-\frac{1}{2}z)
,
x_{2}=(\frac{3}{2}z+n-1)\in[\lambda]_{3}
.

So
p(\widetilde{x_{1}})=(1,\underbrace{1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-2})
and
p(\widetilde{x_{2}})=(1).

Then by Proposition
3.8
,

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}n-1=\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is irreducible.

Therefore, when
z\notin\mathbb{Z}
,

M_{I}(z(\xi_{1}+\xi_{n-1}))
is reducible if and only if

z\in\lfloor-\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+\frac{3}{2}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Step 1.2.
When
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
, we have

(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(z_{1}+\frac{1}{2}z_{2}+n-1,\cdots,-\frac{1}{2}z_{2},\frac{1}{2}z_{2},\cdots,-z_{1}-\frac{1}{2}z_{2}-n+1).

Step 1.2.1.
When
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, equivalently
\lambda+\rho
is integral, we have the following.

(1)

When
z_{1}>-1,z_{2}>-1
, we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(\underbrace{1,0,1,0,\cdots,1,0}_{2n-2},1).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})=n^{2}-n-(n^{2}-n)=0<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})
is reducible.

(2)

When
z_{1}>-1,z_{2}=-1
, we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(1,1,\underbrace{1,0,1,0,\cdots,1,0}_{2n-4}).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+4+\cdots+2n-4)\\
&=n^{2}-n-(n^{2}-3n+3)=2n-3<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})
is reducible.

(3)

When
z_{1}>-1,z_{2}<-1
, we will have

\begin{cases}p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(\underbrace{1,1,1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-1},0,1)\quad&\text{if}~n=2k\\
p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(\underbrace{1,1,1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-1},1)\qquad&\text{if}~n=2k+1\end{cases}
.

Then by Proposition
3.5
,
when
n=2k
, we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+3+4+\cdots+n-2+n)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n-1<\dim(\mathfrak{u}),\end{split}

and when
n=2k+1
, we have

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})
is reducible.

(4)

When
z_{1}\leq-1,z_{2}>-1
, we will have

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(1,1,\underbrace{1,0,1,0,\cdots,1,0}_{2n-6},1)

or
p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,1,\underbrace{1,0,1,0,\cdots,1,0}_{2n-6},1).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+4+6+\cdots+2n-6+2n-4)\\
&=2n-3<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So
M_{I}(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})
is reducible.

(5)

When
z_{1}\leq-1,z_{2}\leq-1
, we have the follows.

(a)

When
z_{1}=-1,z_{2}<-1
, we will have the following.

(i)

If
z_{2}=-2
, we will have

(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(n-3,n-3,n-4,\cdots,0,1,-1,0,\cdots,4-n,3-n,3-n)
.

So we have

\begin{cases}p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,1)&\text{if}~n=3\\
p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,1,1)&\text{if}~n=4\\
p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(1,1,1,1,\underbrace{1,0,1,0\cdots,1}_{2n-9})&\text{if}~n\geq 5\end{cases}
.

Then by Proposition
3.5
,
when
n=3
, we have
\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})=3^{2}-3-1=5=\dim(\mathfrak{u})
, and
when
n=4
, we have
\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})=4^{2}-4-(1+2)=9=\dim(\mathfrak{u})
, and
when
n\geq 5
, we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})&=n^{2}-n-(1+2+3+4+6+\cdots+2n-6)\\
&=4n-10<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).\end{split}

So we can get
\begin{cases}M_{I}(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})~\text{is}~\text{irreducible}&~\text{if}~n=3,4\\
M_{I}(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})~\text{is}~\text{reducible}&~\text{if}~n\geq 5\end{cases}
.

(ii)

If
z_{2}=-n+3
, we will have

(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(\frac{1}{2}n-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}n-\frac{1}{2},\cdots,-\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{5}{2},\frac{1}{2}n-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{3}{2},\cdots,-\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{1}{2})
.

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(\underbrace{1,1,1,1,\cdots,1,1}_{n}).

Then by Proposition
3.5
,

\mathrm{GKdim}\>L(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})=\frac{1}{2}n^{2}-\frac{1}{2}n<\dim(\mathfrak{u}).

So
M_{I}(z_{1}\xi_{1}+z_{2}\xi_{n-1})
is reducible.

(iii)

If
z_{2}=-n+2
, we will have

(\lambda+\rho)^{-}=(\frac{1}{2}n-1,\frac{1}{2}n-1,\cdots,-\frac{1}{2}n+2,\frac{1}{2}n-1,-\frac{1}{2}n+1,\cdots,-\frac{1}{2}n+1)
.

p((\lambda+\rho)^{-})^{ev}=(2,\underbrace{1,1,1,\cdots,1}_{n-2}).

Then by Proposition
3.5

Remark 5.4
.

From Proposition
3.8
, we find that for a given parabolic subalgebra

\operatorname{GKdim}L(\lambda)=\dim(\mathfrak{u})
if and only if for any

, the function

or
F_{D}(x)
takes the minimal value, equivalently,
\operatorname{GKdim}L(x)
is maximal in the corresponding parabolic category
\mathcal{O}
where

. Some more results about this characterization can be found in

6
]

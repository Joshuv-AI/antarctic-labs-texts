Tight closure of powers of parameter ideals in hypersurface rings and their tight Hilbert polynomials Thanks: The first author is supported by the Senior Research Fellowship of HRDG, CSIR, Government of India
Saipriya Dubey, Vivek Mukundan and Jugal Verma

Abstract
. In this paper we find the tight closure of powers of parameter ideals of certain diagonal hypersurface rings. In many cases the associated graded ring with respect to tight closure filtration turns out to be Cohen-Macaulay. This helps us find the tight Hilbert polynomial in these diagonal hypersurfaces. We determine the tight Hilbert polynomial in the following cases: (1) F F -pure diagonal hypersurfaces where number of variables is equal to the degree of defining equation, (2) diagonal hypersurface rings where characteristic of the ring is one less than the degree of defining equation and (3) quartic diagonal hypersurface in four variables.

Primary: 13A35. Secondary: 13D40

1.
Introduction

The objective of this paper is to determine the tight Hilbert polynomial of ideals generated by homogeneous system of parameters in diagonal hypersurface rings in prime characteristic. The tight Hilbert polynomial was introduced by K. Goel, V. Mukundan and J. K. Verma in
[
8
]
. Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a
d
-dimensional analytically unramified local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
and
I
be an
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal. Let
I^{*}
denote the tight closure of
I
(refer Definition
2.5
). Let
H_{I}^{*}(n)=\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{n})^{*}}\right)
where
\ell
denotes the length. Then for large
n,

H_{I}^{*}(n)
is given by a polynomial
P_{I}^{*}(n)
written as

P_{I}^{*}(n)=e_{0}^{*}(I)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-e_{1}^{*}(I)\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}^{*}(I),

where
e_{i}^{*}(I)\in\mathbb{Z}.
Recall that
R
is called an
F
-rational local ring if the ideals of the principal class, that is, ideals
J
generated by height
J
elements, are tightly closed. It is proved
[
8
]
that in a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
R
,
e_{1}^{*}(I)=0
if and only if
R
is
F
-rational. This indicates that the other tight Hilbert coefficients might play an important role in detecting other
F
-singularities. They also computed the tight Hilbert polynomial for two-dimensional diagonal hypersurfaces
K[X,Y,Z]/(X^{r}+Y^{r}+Z^{r}),
where
K
is a field of prime characteristic
p
and
(p,r)=1.
Since there are only a handful of examples of tight Hilbert polynomials known in the literature, this paper is devoted to computation of the tight Hilbert polynomial of parameter ideals
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
of diagonal hypersurfaces in higher dimensions, i.e.
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}]/(X_{1}^{r}+X_{2}^{r}+\cdots+X_{d+1}^{r})
with the unique homogeneous maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}=(I,x_{d+1})
in some special cases. (Throughout, the lower case letters denote the images of the corresponding variables.)

The tight Hilbert coefficients
e_{i}^{*}(I)
can be computed via the tight closure of the powers of the paramter ideals
(I^{n})^{*}
. In general, computing the tight closure of the powers can be quite hard. In a hypersurface ring, the tight closure of a homogeneous system of parameters can be computed using the
strong vanishing theorem
[
14
, Theorem 6.4]
. This result computes the tight closure of the homogeneous system of parameters when the characteristic is “large enough” compared to the degree of the defining ideal of the hypersurface. It is interesting to note that the tight closure of
I
equals
I+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}
in this case. Much of this work has been a result of searching for ideals
I
whose tight closure of the powers
(I^{n})^{*}
has a similar description, such as
I^{n}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}
for some
k
, as that appearing in the strong vanishing theorem.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains basic definitions, notations and results which will provide all the tools necessary in the later sections. In Section
3
, we find the Hilbert polynomial of the filtration
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},
where
I_{0}=R
and
I_{n}=I^{n}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+t}
for
n\geq 1
that occur (in the subsequent sections) as the tight closure filtration of ideals in diagonal hypersurface rings .
In Section
4
, we determine the tight Hilbert polynomial in the ring
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}]/(f),
where degree of
f
is
d+1
and the characteristic
p>(d-1)(d+1)-d.
Here, the associated graded ring of the tight closure filtration
\{(I^{n})^{*}\}_{n\geq 0}
is given by
\operatorname{gr}_{I}^{*}(R)=\bigoplus\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{(I^{n})^{*}}{(I^{n+1})^{*}}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T=\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}],

R=T/(f)
where
f\in T
is a square-free, monic in
X_{d+1}
homogeneous polynomial of degree
d+1
and
p>(d-1)(d+1)-d.
Let
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(I,x_{d+1}).
Then
(I^{n})^{*}=I^{n}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+d-1}
and for
n\geq 1,

\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{n})^{*}}\right)=(d+1)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}.

Moreover
\operatorname{gr}^{*}_{I}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

One of the important ingredient of the proof is the use of
[
15
]
which gives bounds on the tight closure of
I
using the betti numbers appearing in a resolution of
R/I
. Here we use the Eagon-Northcott complex which resolves
R/I^{n}
as a means of constructing the tight closure of the powers
I^{n}
.

Recall that, a Noetherian local ring
R
of prime characteristic
p
is said to be
F
-pure if the Frobenius homomorphism
F:M\rightarrow M\otimes_{R}F(R)
is injective for all
R
-modules
M
where
F(R)
is the image of
R
under the frobenius endomorphism which takes
r\in R
to
r^{p}.
We know that the test elements play a vital role in the theory of tight closure. In
F
-pure rings, the “test ideal” turns out to be a radical ideal
[
7
, Proposition 2.5]
. Exploiting this property of
F
-pure rings, in Theorem
4.3
we determine the tight Hilbert polynomial in all diagonal hypersurface
F
-pure rings when the number of variables is equal to the degree of the defining equation of the hypersurface. Along with results on tight Hilbert polynomial of a minimal reduction of the maximal homogeneous ideals, we also present the result on F-pure rings.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{t+2}]/(X_{1}^{r}+X_{2}^{r}+\cdots+X_{t+2}^{r}),

I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t+1})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(I,x_{t+2}),
where
p>tr-(t+1).
Then

(1)

\operatorname{gr}_{I}^{*}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)
The tight Hilbert polynomial
P_{I}^{*}(n)
is given by

P_{I}^{*}(n)=\begin{cases}r\binom{n+t}{t+1},&\text{ if }r\leq t+1,\\
r\binom{n+t}{t+1}-\binom{r-t}{2}\binom{n+t-1}{t}+\binom{r-t}{3}\binom{n+t-2}{t-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{j}\binom{r-t}{j+1}\binom{n+t-j}{t+1-j},&\text{ if }r\geq t+2.\\
\end{cases}

If
r\geq t+2,

e_{j}^{*}(I)=0
for
r-t\leq j\leq t+1.

(3)
If
r=t+2
and
R
is F-pure, then for all
n\geq 1
,

\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{n})^{*}}\right)=(t+2)\binom{n+t}{t+1}-\binom{n+t-1}{t}.

In Section
5
, we explore the tight Hilbert polynomial in the diagonal hypersurface rings where the characteristic of the ring is one less than the degree of the defining equation.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
R=\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}]}{(X_{1}^{p+1}+X_{2}^{p+1}+\cdots+X_{d+1}^{p+1})},

I=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(I,x_{d+1}).
Then

(1)
(I^{n})^{*}=I^{n}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}
for all
n\geq 1.

(2)
P_{I}^{*}(n)=(p+1)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-\binom{p}{2}\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{j}\binom{p}{j+1}\binom{n+d-j-1}{d-j},
where
j=\min\{p-1,d\}.

Moreover,
e_{i}^{*}(I)=0
for
p\leq i\leq d
and
\operatorname{gr}^{*}_{I}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Finally in Section
6
, we determine the tight Hilbert polynomial for quartic diagonal hypersurfaces in four variables.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
R=\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[X,Y,Z,W]}{(X^{4}+Y^{4}+Z^{4}+W^{4})},

I=(x,y,z)
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(I,w).
Then for
n\geq 1,

P_{I}^{*}(n)=\begin{cases}4\binom{n+2}{3}-3\binom{n+1}{2}+n,&\text{ if }p=3,\\
4\binom{n+2}{3}-\binom{n+1}{2},&\text{ if }p\geq 5.\end{cases}

Moreover
\operatorname{gr}^{*}_{I}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

2.
Preliminaries

We first set up notation, recall certain basic concepts and results needed in subsequent sections.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a local ring and
I
be an
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal of
R.
A sequence of ideals
{\mathcal{F}}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

is called an
I
-filtration
if for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z},

(1)

I_{n}=R
for all
n\leq 0,\;\;

(2)

I_{n}\supseteq I_{n+1},\;\;

(3)

I_{m}I_{n}\subseteq I_{m+n}\;\;
and
(4)

I^{n}\subseteq I_{n}.

The
I
-filtration
{\mathcal{F}}
is called
I
-admissible
if there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
I_{n}\subseteq I^{n-r}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}.

We associate three blowup algebras with an
I
-admissible filtration
{\mathcal{F}}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. The Rees algebra of
{\mathcal{F}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{F}})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}t^{n}
, the extended Rees algebra of
{\mathcal{F}}
is denoted by
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}({\mathcal{F}})=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}I_{n}t^{n}
and the associated graded ring of
{\mathcal{F}}
is denoted by
\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathcal{F}}}(R)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{n}/I_{n+1}.
If
{\mathcal{F}}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\geq 0},
we denote the blow up algebras by
\mathcal{R}(I),\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(I)
and
\operatorname{gr}_{I}(R)
respectively.
Let
I
be an
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal. The
Hilbert function of
{\mathcal{F}}
is defined by
H_{{\mathcal{F}}}(n)=\ell(R/I_{n}).
If
{\mathcal{F}}
is an
I
-admissible filtration, then for sufficiently large
n,

H_{{\mathcal{F}}}(n)
coincides with a polynomial
P_{{\mathcal{F}}}(n)
of degree
d
for large
n,
where
d=\dim R.
There exist integers
e_{0}({\mathcal{F}}),e_{1}({\mathcal{F}}),\ldots,e_{d}({\mathcal{F}})
so that

P_{{\mathcal{F}}}(n)=e_{0}({\mathcal{F}})\binom{n+d-1}{d}-e_{1}({\mathcal{F}})\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}({\mathcal{F}})

and it is called the
Hilbert polynomial of
{\mathcal{F}}.
The uniquely determined integers
e_{i}({\mathcal{F}})
are called the
Hilbert coefficients of
{\mathcal{F}}.
The coefficient
e_{0}({\mathcal{F}})
is a positive integer and it is called the
multiplicity of
{\mathcal{F}}
. The coefficient
e_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
is called as the
Chern number of
{\mathcal{F}}.

For
{\mathcal{F}}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},

e(I):=e_{0}({\mathcal{F}})=e_{0}(I)
(resp.
e_{1}(I):=e_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
) is called the
multiplicity
(resp. the
Chern number
) of
I.

The
reduction number
of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to a minimal reduction
J\subseteq I
is defined as
r_{J}(\mathcal{F})=\min\{m|JI_{n}=I_{n+1}\text{ for all }n\geq m\}
and the reduction number of
\mathcal{F}
is

r(\mathcal{F})=\min\{r_{J}(\mathcal{F})|J\text{ is a minimal reduction of }\mathcal{F}\}.

The
postulation number
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as

\eta(\mathcal{F})=\min\{n|P_{\mathcal{F}}(m)=H_{\mathcal{F}}(m)\text{ for all }m>n\}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
I
be an ideal of
R.
An element
x\in R
is
integral over an ideal
I
if

x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\dots+a_{n}=0

where
a_{i}\in I^{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,n.

The
integral closure
\overline{I}
of
I
is the ideal of all
x\in R
which are integral over
I.

A Noetherian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
is said to be
analytically unramified
if its completion with respect to the
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic topology is reduced.

D. Rees characterised
[
18
, Theorem 1.4]
analytically unramified local rings in terms of the normal filtration

\{\overline{I^{n}}\}
of any
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal
I.

Theorem 2.3

(
D. Rees).

A Noetherian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
is analytically unramified if and only if the
normal filtration
\{\overline{I^{n}}\}
of any
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal
I
is
I
-admissible.

Let the normal filtration of
I
be given by
\mathcal{N}=\{\overline{I^{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.
Rees’s Theorem implies that if

is analytically unramified then
{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{N})=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{I^{n}}t^{n}
is a finite module over

Therefore the normal Hilbert function

coincides with a polynomial
\overline{P}_{I}(n)
of degree

for large

and is known as the

I
.
We write

\overline{P}_{I}(n)=\overline{e}_{0}(I)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-\overline{e}_{1}(I)\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}\overline{e}_{d}(I).

Here
\overline{e}_{0}(I)=e(I),
the multiplicity of
I
,
\overline{e}_{1}(I)
is known as the
normal Chern number of
I
and the coefficients
\overline{e}_{i}(I)
for

I.
In this case, we denote the blow up algebras
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{N}),

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(\mathcal{N})
and
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{N}}(R)

\overline{\mathcal{R}}(I),

\overline{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(I)

We now introduce the filtration
\{(I^{n})^{*}\}
of tight closure of powers an ideal

Definition 2.4
.

For a ring

the subset of

Let

.

An element

such that

for all sufficiently large

An element

for

in

The

.

[

3.
Hilbert polynomial for the filtration
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},

where

and

for

In the subsequent sections, we will show that in most of the cases we consider, the tight closure of powers of a linear system of parameters in diagonal hypersurfaces takes a special form. Therefore computing the Hilbert polynomial in such cases becomes crucial.

Let

Then we calculate the Hilbert polynomial

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T=K[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}]
and

where
K
is an infinite field and

is a homogeneous square-free polynomial of degree

Let

be a minimal reduction of

Let

where

for all

and for some fixed

Then

(1)

\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)
If
r\leq t+1
then
I_{n}=I^{n}
for all
n
and

(3)
If

H(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R),\lambda)=\frac{(t+1)+{\lambda}+\cdots+{\lambda}^{r-t-1}}{(1-{\lambda})^{d}},

e_{j}(\mathcal{F})=\binom{r-t}{j+1}\text{ for }1\leq j\leq r-t-1\text{ and }e_{j}(\mathcal{F})=0\text{ for }r-t\leq j\leq d.

Also
\eta(\mathcal{F})=r-t-d-1
and
r(\mathcal{F})=r-t-1.

Proof.

We note that
\mathcal{F}
is an
I
-admissible filtration. Since

is square-free in
T,

R_{{\mathfrak{m}}}
is reduced and analytically unramified. Clearly
I_{n}\subseteq\overline{I^{n}}={\mathfrak{m}}^{n}
which implies that

\mathcal{R^{\prime}}(I)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R^{\prime}}}(I).

As

is analytically unramified,
\overline{\mathcal{R^{\prime}}}(I)
is a finite
\mathcal{R^{\prime}}(I)
-module so that
\mathcal{R^{\prime}}(\mathcal{F})
is a finite
\mathcal{R^{\prime}}(I)
-module making
\mathcal{F}
an
I
-admissible filtration.

(1) Since

is Cohen-Macaulay, by Valabrega-Valla Theorem
[

, Proposition 3.5]
, it is enough to show that

for

Notice that

for
n\geq 1
as

is Cohen-Macaulay. This implies that for all

Therefore
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2) Let

We will show that
r({\mathfrak{m}})=r-1.
The Hilbert series of

is given by

H(R,{\lambda})=\frac{1-{\lambda}^{r}}{(1-{\lambda})^{d+1}}=\frac{1+{\lambda}+\cdots+{\lambda}^{r-1}}{(1-{\lambda})^{d}}.

Since
\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\eta({\mathfrak{m}})=r-1-d,
we have
r({\mathfrak{m}})=r-1

[
17
, Corollary 5.7]
.

Let

Since

for

This implies that
\mathcal{F}
is the
I
-adic filtration and for

H_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=r\binom{n+d-1}{d}.

(3) Let

Apply induction on

If
d=0
then
R=\frac{K[X_{1}]}{(X_{1}^{r})},

I=(0),

{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1})
and
\mathcal{F}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
where
I_{0}=R
and
I_{n}={\mathfrak{m}}^{n+t}
for
n\geq 1.
In this case
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)=\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+1}}\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+t}}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+t+1}}.
This implies that

\displaystyle H(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R),\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\ell([\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)]_{n}){\lambda}^{n}

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+1}}\right)+\ell\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+1}}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+2}}\right){\lambda}+\cdots+\ell\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{r-1}}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{r}}\right){\lambda}^{r-1-t}.

Note that
\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+1}}\cong\frac{K[X_{1}]}{(X_{1}^{t+1})}
since

Therefore
\ell\left(\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+1}}\right)=t+1.
If
1\leq i\leq r-1-t
then

\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+i}}\cong\frac{K[X_{1}]}{(X_{1}^{t+i})}\text{ and }\frac{R}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+i+1}}\cong\frac{K[X_{1}]}{(X_{1}^{t+i+1})}.

This implies that
\ell\left(\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+i}}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+i+1}}\right)=1
for
1\leq i\leq r-t-1.
Therefore

H(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R),\lambda)=(t+1)+{\lambda}+\cdots+{\lambda}^{r-t-1}.

Let
d\geq 1.
After a linear change of coordinates, we may assume that
f
is monic in
X_{d+1}.
Since
f
is monic in
X_{d+1},

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
is a minimal reduction of
{\mathfrak{m}}.
Thus we may assume that
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}).
Clearly

is a nonzerodivisor on
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
so that

\frac{\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)}{x_{1}^{*}\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)}\cong\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}/(x_{1})}(R/(x_{1})).

Let

J=IS
and
\Gamma=\{J_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
where

and

for

where

is the unique maximal homogeneous ideal in the

-dimensional ring

Notice that the filtration

\mathcal{F}/(x_{1})=\{L_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
in

Indeed for

and

Also

and

By induction hypothesis,

Since

is a nonzerodivisor of

it follows that

Let

Then by

Let

Therefore for

Since
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal{F}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay,
\eta(\mathcal{F})=r-t-d-1
and

∎

4.
The Tight Hilbert polynomial for diagonal hypersurfaces

The purpose of this section is to find the tight Hilbert polynomial in certain diagonal hypersurface rings. As evidenced in
[
8
]
, this will enable us to detect
F
-rationality of such rings. The first proposition helps us to compute the tight Hilbert polynomial of a system of parameters generated by test elements in excellent reduced Cohen-Macaulay rings. In fact, it also provides information about the various blow up algebras associated with the tight closure filtration in this case. It is an easy consequence of
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be
d
-dimensional excellent reduced Cohen-Macaulay local ring. Let
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
be test elements and
Q=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
be
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary.Then

(1)
(Q^{n})^{*}=Q(Q^{n-1})^{*}
for all
n\geq 2.

(2)
e_{0}(Q)-e_{1}^{*}(Q)=\ell(R/Q^{*}).

(3)
\ell(R/(Q^{n})^{*})=e_{0}(Q)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-e_{1}^{*}(Q)\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}
for all
n\geq 1.

(4)

G^{*}(Q)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(Q^{n})^{*}/(Q^{n+1})^{*}
and
\mathcal{R}^{*}(Q)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(Q^{n})^{*}t^{n}
are Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

By
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
,
(Q^{n})^{*}=Q^{n-1}Q^{*}.
This implies that

(Q^{n})^{*}\subseteq Q((Q^{n-2})^{*}Q^{*})^{*}=Q(Q^{n-1})^{*}\subseteq(Q^{n})^{*}\text{ for all }n\geq 2.

This proves (1). The remaining parts follow by Huneke-Ooishi Theorem
[
13
]
.
∎

In the next theorem, we demonstrate the use of Strong Vanishing Theorem for computing the tight Hilbert polynomial. It is well known that the Strong Vanishing Theorem helps in computation of the tight closure of an ideal generated by system of parameters for rings with “large” prime characteristic. However to find the tight Hilbert polynomial, we need to compute the tight closure of powers of such ideals. For this, we use Theorem
2.10
which ensures a bound for tight closure of
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideals once we have its projective resolution. This resolution in our case will be given by the Eagon-Northcott complex.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
T=\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}],

R=T/(f)
where
f\in T
is a square-free, monic in
X_{d+1}
homogeneous ploynomial of degree
d+1
and
p>(d-1)(d+1)-d.
Let
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(I,x_{d+1}).
Then
(I^{n})^{*}=I^{n}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+d-1}
and for
n\geq 1,

\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{n})^{*}}\right)=(d+1)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}.

Moreover
\operatorname{gr}^{*}_{I}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

Since
p>(d-1)(d+1)-d,
it follows from Theorem
2.8
that Strong Vanishing holds for
R.
Therefore we have
I^{*}=I+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}.
Using the fact that the Eagon-Northcott complex resolves
I^{n}
and that the differentials
d_{i},i\geq 2
of this complex is linear, it follows from Theorem
2.10
that
(I^{n})^{*}=I^{n}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+d-1}.
We claim that
r^{*}(I)\leq 1.
Indeed, for
n\geq 1,

I(I^{n})^{*}=I^{n+1}+I{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+d-1}=I^{n+1}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+d}
where the last equality follows as
r({\mathfrak{m}})=d.

Therefore by Huneke-Ooishi Theorem (Proposition
4.1
) we have for
n\geq 1,

\displaystyle\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{n})^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle=e_{0}(I)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-e_{1}^{*}(I)\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}

\displaystyle=(d+1)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-e_{1}^{*}(I)\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}

Put
n=1
to obtain
\ell\left(\frac{R}{I+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}}\right)=(d+1)-e_{1}^{*}(I).
Since

\frac{R}{I+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}}\cong\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}]/(f)}{\left((X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d})+(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1})^{d}+(f)\right)/(f)}\cong\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{d+1}]}{({X_{d+1}})^{d}},

it follows that
e_{1}^{*}(I)=1.

∎

Due to
[
11
, Proposition 5.21 (c)]
, many diagonal hypersurface rings are
F
-pure, namely,
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}]/(X_{1}^{d+1}+X_{2}^{d+1}+\cdots+X_{d+1}^{d+1}),
where
p\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}d+1).
In such cases, the test ideal turns out to be a radical ideal by Proposition
2.7
.Using this, we find the tight Hilbert polynomial in
F
-pure diagonal hypersurface rings where the number of variables is equal to the degree of the defining equation.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{d+1}]/(X_{1}^{d+1}+X_{2}^{d+1}+\cdots+X_{d+1}^{d+1})
be F-pure,
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(I,x_{d+1})
. Then for all

\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{n})^{*}}\right)=(d+1)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}.

Moreover
\operatorname{gr}^{*}_{I}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

Observe that, using Theorem
2.6
,
x_{1}^{d},\ldots,x_{d+1}^{d}\in\tau(R).
Since

is F-pure, taking radicals on both sides, Proposition
2.7
yields

Therefore by Proposition
4.1
it follows that for
n\geq 1,

\ell\left(\frac{R}{(I^{n})^{*}}\right)=(d+1)\binom{n+d-1}{d}-e_{1}^{*}(I)\binom{n+d-2}{d-1},

where
e_{1}^{*}(I)=\ell\left(\frac{I^{*}}{I}\right).
Using Briançon-Skoda Theorem

which implies that

We claim that
I^{*}=I+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}.
Since
I:\tau(R)=I^{*}

[
14
, Corollary 4.2]
, it is enough to show that

If possible, let

then

a contradiction. Hence the claim. Since

\frac{R}{I+{\mathfrak{m}}^{d}}\cong\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d+1}]}{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d},X_{d+1}^{d})}\cong\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{d+1}]}{(X_{d+1}^{d})},

it follows that

∎

As promised in section
3
, we now give a class of rings where the tight closure filtration turns out to be the filtration
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},
where
I_{0}=R
and
I_{n}=I^{n}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+t}
for
n\geq 1.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

(1)

\operatorname{gr}_{I}^{*}(R)

(2)

∎

5.
The Tight Hilbert polynomial for degree
p+1
hypersurface ring of characteristic
p

The goal of this section is to compute the tight Hilbert polynomial of linear system of parameters in diagonal hypersurface rings where the characteristic of the ring is one less than the degree of the defining equation. In order to find the tight Hilbert polynomial, we calculate the initial ideals of certain ideals in
K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}].

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A=K[X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{m}]
be a polynomial ring over field
K
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

for some

Let

If

denotes the graded reverse lex ordering with

then

\operatorname{in}_{>}(J)=(X_{1}^{N},(X_{2}^{q},X_{3}^{q},\ldots,X_{m}^{q})^{k}).

Proof.

It suffices to show that

G=\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}^{N},\left[\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}X_{j}^{qr_{j}}\right]X_{m}^{q[k-(r_{2}+r_{3}+\cdots+r_{m-1})]}\text{, }r_{j}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,k\}\text{ for }2\leq j\leq m-1\Bigg\}

.
For

consider the
S
-polynomial of

and

\displaystyle S(f,g)

\displaystyle=\frac{X_{1}^{N}\left[\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}X_{j}^{qr_{j}}\right]X_{m}^{q[k-(r_{2}+r_{3}+\cdots+r_{m-1})]}}{X_{1}^{N}}f-\frac{X_{1}^{N}\left[\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}X_{j}^{qr_{j}}\right]X_{m}^{q[k-(r_{2}+r_{3}+\cdots+r_{m-1})]}}{\left[\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}X_{j}^{qr_{j}}\right]X_{m}^{q[k-(r_{2}+r_{3}+\cdots+r_{m-1})]}}g

\displaystyle=\left[\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}X_{j}^{qr_{j}}\right]X_{m}^{q[k-(r_{2}+r_{3}+\cdots+r_{m-1})]}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{m}X_{i}^{N}\right).

By division algorithm, it follows that
\overline{S(f,g)}^{G}=0
for

and

where

Now for

and
\overline{s}=(s_{2},s_{3},\ldots,s_{m-1})

we have

-polynomial,

\displaystyle S\left(\left[\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}X_{j}^{qr_{j}}\right]X_{m}^{q[k-(r_{2}+r_{3}+\cdots+r_{m-1})]},\left[\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}X_{j}^{qs_{j}}\right]X_{m}^{q[k-(s_{2}+s_{3}+\cdots+s_{m-1})]}\right)=0.

It follows that

is a Gröbner basis of

and hence
\operatorname{in}_{>}(J)=(X_{1}^{N},(X_{2}^{q},X_{3}^{q},\ldots,X_{m}^{q})^{k}).

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

First we will show that

Clearly,

(x_{d+1}^{p+1})^{p^{e}}=-(x_{1}^{p+1})^{p^{e}}-(x_{2}^{p+1})^{p^{e}}-\cdots-(x_{d}^{p+1})^{p^{e}}\in I^{[p^{e+1}]}.

Therefore
\underbrace{(x_{d+1}^{2})^{p^{e}}\cdots(x_{d+1}^{2})^{p^{e}}\,}_{\text{$m$ times}}\in I^{[p^{e+1}]}

Since

it follows that

(x_{d+1}^{2})^{p^{e+1}}=\underbrace{(x_{d+1}^{2})^{p^{e}}\cdots(x_{d+1}^{2})^{p^{e}}\,}_{\text{$p$ times}}\in I^{[p^{e+1}]}.

Hence
x_{d+1}^{2}\in I^{*}.

It is enough to show that

As

is a test element by Theorem

Note that

Hence

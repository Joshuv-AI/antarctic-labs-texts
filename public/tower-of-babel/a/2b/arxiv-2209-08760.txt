On the range of the X X -ray transform of symmetric tensors compactly supported in the plane
Kamran Sadiq, Alexandru Tamasan

Abstract
. We find the necessary and sufficient conditions on the Fourier coefficients of a function g g on the torus to be in the range of the X X -ray transform of a symmetric tensor of compact support in the plane.

Primary 44A12, 35J56; Secondary 45E05

1.
Introduction

We revisit the range characterization of the
X
-ray transform of a real valued symmetric
m
- tensors of compact support in the Euclidean plane. Via a re-parametrization of lines, the
m=0
case is the classical Radon transform
[
37
]
, for which the necessary and sufficient constraints have been long established independently by Gelfand and Graev
[
11
]
, Helgason
[
13
]
, and Ludwig
[
24
]
; we refer to the result as
GGHL characterization
. Models which account for the attenuation have also been considered in the homogeneous case
[
21
]
, and in the non-homogeneous case in the breakthrough works
[
1
,
30
,
31
]
, and subsequently
[
29
,
4
,
3
,
26
]
. Finding applications in noise reduction
[
43
,
44
]
, completion of the data
[
45
,
12
,
16
,
22
]
, CT-hardware failure diagnosis
[
34
]
, the range characterization problem in the
0
-tensor case continues to stimulate the interests of both mathematicians and practitioners
[
6
,
7
,
17
,
18
,
38
,
25
,
23
]
. In particular in
[
38
]
the authors gave a range characterization in terms of the Bukhgeim-Hilbert transform, the Hilbert-like transform associated with A-analytic maps in the sense of Bukhgeim
[
5
]
. The latter result was extended to
1
-and
2
-tensors in
[
39
,
41
]
, and to an arbitrary order in
[
32
]
.

For tensors of order
m\geq 1
, the non-injectivity of the
X
-ray transform makes the range characterization problem more interesting. In the Euclidean plane, the GGHL-characterization was extended to arbitrary symmetric
m
-tensors in
[
33
]
.
For a survey of results on tomography of tensors in the Euclidean plane we refer to
[
8
]
.

The systematic study of tensor tomography in non-Euclidean spaces originated in
[
42
]
. On simple Riemannian surfaces, the range characterization of the geodesic
X
-ray of compactly supported
0
and
1
has been established in terms of the scattering relation in
[
36
]
, and the results were extended to symmetric tensors of arbitrary order in
[
2
]
, and to attenuating media in
[
20
]
; see
[
35
]
for a comprehensive survey.

The connection between the Euclidean version of the characterization in
[
36
]
and the GGHL characterization was established in
[
25
]
.

In
[
40
]
the authors considered the lines parametrized by points on the torus and gave a range characterization for compactly supported functions in terms of the Fourier coefficients on the Fourier lattice of the torus. This novel point of view allowed to establish the missing connection between the result in
[
38
]
and the classical GGHL characterization. Although
Xf
is a function on the torus, this problem differs from the one in
[
15
]
, where for a given direction (of rational slope) the integration takes place over a finite union of parallel segments in the unit disc.

In here we extend the result in
[
40
]
to symmetric tensors of an arbitrary order. The method of proof is based on some explicit mapping properties of the Bukhgeim-Hilbert transform. Apart from the symmetry constraints due to the double parameterization of the lines, of specific interest are the moment conditions.
The thrust of this work are the constraints (
2.17
) and (
2.33
) replacing the generalized moment conditions in
[
33
]
, and the sufficiency part in Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
for tensors of finite smoothness.

This work concerns real valued tensors. For a complex valued tensor
{\bf f}
, since
\displaystyle\re(X{\bf f})=X(\re({\bf f}))
and
\displaystyle\im(X{\bf f})=X(\im({\bf f}))
, Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
apply separately to
\re({\bf f})
and to
\im({\bf f})
.

All the details establishing notation and the statement of the main results are in Section
2

3
we briefly recall existing results on
A

In Section
4

To improve the readability of the work, some of the claims are proven in the appendix.

2.
Preliminaries and statement of main results

Let
{\bf f}=(f_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}})
, with
i_{1},...,i_{m}\in\{1,2\}
be a real valued symmetric
m
-tensor, with integrable components of compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By scaling and translating, we may assume that all the components have compact support inside the the unit disc
\Omega=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}.
The boundary
\varGamma
of
\Omega
is the unit circle, but we keep this notation to differentiate from the set
{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}
of directions. The symmetry refers to the components
f_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}}
being invariant under any transposition of indexes.

For
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}:=\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\theta}\otimes\boldsymbol{\theta}\otimes\cdots\otimes\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{m}\in\left({{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\right)^{m}
and
z\in\Omega
, let

\langle{\bf f}(z),\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}\rangle
denote the pairing

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle{\bf f}(z),\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}\rangle=f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}(z)\theta^{i_{1}}\cdot\theta^{i_{2}}\cdots\theta^{i_{m}},

where the summation convention is understood over all repeated indexes
\displaystyle(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m})\in\{1,2\}^{m}
.

The
X
-ray transform of
{\bf f}
is given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle X{\bf f}(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle{\bf f}(z+t\boldsymbol{\theta}),\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}\rangle dt,\quad(z,\boldsymbol{\theta})\in\Omega\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}.

Following directly from its definition

(2.3)

\displaystyle X{\bf f}(x,-\boldsymbol{\theta})=(-1)^{m}X{\bf f}(x,\boldsymbol{\theta}),

so that
\boldsymbol{\theta}\mapsto X{\bf f}(x,\boldsymbol{\theta})
is an even function for
{\bf f}
of an even order
m
, and an odd function for
{\bf f}
of an odd order
m
.

Lines
\displaystyle L_{(\beta,\theta)}:=\{e^{i\beta}+se^{i\theta}:\;s\in\mathbb{R}\}
intersecting
\overline{\Omega}
are parametrized in coordinates
\displaystyle\{(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta}):\beta,\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]\}
on the torus
\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}
, and
then

(2.4)

\displaystyle X{\bf f}(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\langle{\bf f}(e^{i\beta}+se^{i\theta}),\boldsymbol{\theta}^{m}\rangle ds

is also understood as a function on the torus.

Since
\displaystyle L_{(\beta,\theta)}=L_{(2\theta-\beta-\pi,\theta)}=L_{(\beta,\theta+\pi)}=L_{(2\theta-\beta-\pi,\theta+\pi)},
the set of lines intersecting
\overline{\Omega}
are quadruply covered when
(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})
ranges over the entire torus
\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}
. Moreover, the following symmetries are satisfied,

(2.5)

\displaystyle X{\bf f}(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})=(-1)^{m}X{\bf f}(e^{i\beta},e^{i(\theta+\pi)}),\text{ and }

(2.6)

\displaystyle X{\bf f}(e^{\mathrm{i}\beta},e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})=(-1)^{m}X{\bf f}(e^{i(2\theta-\beta-\pi)},e^{i(\theta+\pi)}),\text{ for }(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})\in\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}};

see Figure

If
{\bf f}=(f_{i_{1}i_{2}...i_{m}})

i_{1},...,i_{m}\in\{1,2\}

may not be integrable on the torus. However, if either

(2.7)

\displaystyle\textnormal{supp }f_{i_{1}...i_{m}}\subset\Omega,\mbox{ or }f_{i_{1}...i_{m}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\mbox{ for some }p>2,

We consider the partition of the torus into three parts: the “outflux” part

(2.8)

\varGamma_{+}:=\left\{(e^{i\beta},e^{i(\beta+\alpha)})\in\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}:\,\beta\in(-\pi,\pi],\;|\alpha|<\frac{\pi}{2}\right\},

the “influx” part

(2.9)

\varGamma_{-}:=\left\{(e^{i\beta},e^{i(\beta+\alpha)})\in\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}:\,\beta\in(-\pi,\pi],\;\frac{\pi}{2}<|\alpha|\leq\pi\right\},

and the (Lebesgue negligible) variety
\varGamma_{0}:=(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})\setminus(\varGamma_{+}\cup\varGamma_{-})
parameterizing the tangent lines to the circle; see Figure

Our result gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a function
g\in L^{1}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
to satisfy

(2.10)

g=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}X{\bf f},&\mbox{ on }\varGamma_{+},\\
-X{\bf f},&\mbox{ on }\varGamma_{-}.\end{array}\right.

The characterization is in terms of the Fourier coefficients

(2.11)

\displaystyle g_{n,k}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}g(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})e^{-in\theta}e^{-ik\beta}d\theta d\beta,\;n,k\in\mathbb{Z}

on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
.

The two indexes play a different role. Throughout, the first index is the Fourier mode in the angular variable on
{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}
, and we call it an
angular mode
. The second index is the mode in the boundary variable on
\varGamma
, and we call it a
boundary mode
.

The change of parity in the order of the tensor propagates to the statements of the results. For the sake of clarity we separate the two cases.

2.1.
The case of an even order
m
-tensor

If
m
is even, it is easy to check that
g
in (
2.10
) satisfy the symmetry relation

(2.12)

\displaystyle g(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})=g(e^{i(2\theta-\beta-\pi)},e^{i(\theta+\pi)}),\text{ for a.e. }(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})\in\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}.

Motivated by this relation, let
L^{1}_{\text{sym}}(\Omega\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
denote the space of integrable functions
g
on the torus satisfying the symmetry in (
2.12
). Since

\left(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta}\right)
and
\left(e^{i(2\theta-\beta-\pi)},e^{(\theta+\pi)}\right)
are either both in
\varGamma_{+}
, or both in
\varGamma_{-}
, we can consider the spaces
L^{1}_{\text{sym}}(\varGamma_{\pm})
of integrable functions on the half-tori satisfying (
2.12
). Clearly,
\displaystyle g\in L^{1}_{sym}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
if and only if its restrictions
\displaystyle g|_{\varGamma_{\pm}}\in L^{1}_{\text{sym}}(\varGamma_{\pm})
.

Moreover, since
g
in (
2.10
) is odd with respect to the angular variable:

(2.13)

\displaystyle g(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})=-g(e^{i\beta},e^{i(\theta+\pi)}),

let us consider the subspace
L^{1}_{\text{sym,odd}}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
of functions in
L^{1}_{\text{sym}}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
, which, in addition to satisfying (
2.12
), they also satisfy (
2.13
).

In the statements below we use the notations in
[
42
]
:

\displaystyle L^{1}(\mathbf{S}^{m};\Omega)=\left\{{\bf f}=(f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}})\in\mathbf{S}^{m}(\Omega):f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}\in L^{1}(\Omega)\right\}

for the space of real valued, symmetric tensor fields of order
m
with integrable components.
Similarly,

C^{\mu}(\mathbf{S}^{m};\Omega)
,
0<\mu<1
, denotes the tensor fields of order
m
with locally Hölder continuous components, and we use the notation
\langle n\rangle=(1+|n|^{2})^{1/2}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Range characterization for even order tensors)
.

(i) Let
{\bf f}\in L^{1}(\mathbf{S}^{m};\Omega)
be a real valued, integrable symmetric tensor field of even order
m=2q,\,q\geq 0
, satisfying (
2.7
), and
g\in L^{1}_{\emph{sym,odd}}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
, with

g=X{\bf f}\text{ on }\varGamma_{+}(\mbox{ and }g=-X{\bf f}\mbox{ on }\varGamma_{-}).

Then the Fourier coefficients
\{g_{n,k}\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
g
satisfy the following conditions:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\text{Oddness}:

\displaystyle\quad g_{n,k}=0,

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all \emph{even} }n\in\mathbb{Z}\text{, and all }k\in\mathbb{Z};

(2.15)

\displaystyle\text{Conjugacy}:

\displaystyle\quad g_{-n,-k}=\overline{g_{n,k}},

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all}\;n,k\in\mathbb{Z};

(2.16)

\displaystyle\text{Symmetry}:

\displaystyle\quad g_{n,k}=(-1)^{n+k}g_{n+2k,-k},

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all}\;n,k\in\mathbb{Z};

(2.17)

\displaystyle\text{Moments}:

\displaystyle\quad g_{n,k}=(-1)^{k}g_{n+2k,-k},

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all \emph{odd} }n\leq-(2q+1)\text{, and all }k\leq 0.

(ii) Let
\{g_{n,k}\}
be given for all
odd

n\leq-1
, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq-1\\
n=\,\emph{odd}\end{subarray}}\langle n\rangle^{2}\sum_{k=-{\infty}}^{\infty}\lvert g_{n,k}\rvert<{\infty},\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=-{\infty}}^{\infty}\langle k\rangle^{1+\mu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq-1\\
n=\,\emph{odd}\end{subarray}}\lvert g_{n,k}\rvert<{\infty},

for some
\mu>1/2
, and let

(2.19)

\displaystyle g_{-n}:=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}g_{-n,k}\,e^{ik\beta},\;\emph{odd}n\leq-1,

be defined on
\varGamma
.

If
\{g_{n,k}\}
satisfy (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), then there exists a real valued

{\bf f}\in L^{1}(\mathbf{S}^{m};\Omega)

such that the mapping

(2.20)

\displaystyle(\Gamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})\ni(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})\longmapsto 2\re\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq-1\\
n=\,\emph{odd}\end{subarray}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{n,k}e^{in\theta}e^{ik\beta}\right\}

defines a function in
L^{1}_{\emph{sym,odd}}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
, which coincides with
X{\bf f}
on
\varGamma_{+}
(and with
-X{\bf f}
on
\varGamma_{-}
).

For
q\geq 1
,
{\bf f}
is uniquely determined by an element in the class

(2.21)

\displaystyle\Psi_{g}^{\text{even}}:=

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\psi_{-1},\psi_{-3},\cdots\psi_{-(2q-1)}\right)\in\left(W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})\right)^{q}:\psi_{-(2j-1)}\big\lvert_{\varGamma}=g_{-(2j-1)},\;1\leq j\leq q\right\}.

Moreover if
\psi_{-1},\psi_{-3},...,\psi_{-2q+1}\in C^{1,\mu}(\overline{\Omega})
, then
{\bf f}\in C^{\mu}(\mathbf{S}^{m};\Omega)
.

If
q=0
, the class is empty and
{\bf f}
is uniquely determined by the data.

2.14
) and (
2.15
) are not intrinsic to the

-ray transform. The symmetry constraints (

\varGamma_{+}
, and they are shared by any function on the torus satisfying the symmetry (

); see Lemma

in the appendix.

.

\displaystyle(\Gamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})\ni(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})\longmapsto 2\re\left\{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(n,k)\in R\cup G\end{subarray}}g_{n,k}e^{in\theta}e^{ik\beta}\right\}

is precisely

on

We formulate next the odd-order tensor case.

2.2.
The case of odd order
m
-tensors

If
m
is odd, it is easy to check that
g
in (
2.10
) obeys the skew-symmetry relation

(2.28)

\displaystyle g(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})=-g(e^{i(2\theta-\beta-\pi)},e^{i(\theta+\pi)}),\text{ for a.e. }(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})\in\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}.

This motivates to work in the space
L^{1}_{\text{skew}}(\Omega\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
of integrable functions
g
on the torus satisfying the skew-symmetry in (
2.28
). Since
\left(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta}\right)
and
\left(e^{i(2\theta-\beta-\pi)},e^{(\theta+\pi)}\right)
are either both in
\varGamma_{+}
, or both in
\varGamma_{-}
, we can consider the spaces
L^{1}_{\text{skew}}(\varGamma_{\pm})
of integrable functions on the half-tori
\varGamma_{\pm}
satisfying (
2.28
). Clearly,
\displaystyle g\in L^{1}_{skew}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
if and only if its restrictions
\displaystyle g|_{\varGamma_{\pm}}\in L^{1}_{\text{skew}}(\varGamma_{\pm})
.

Moreover, since
g
in (
2.10
) is even with respect to the angular variable:

(2.29)

\displaystyle g(e^{i\beta},e^{i\theta})=g(e^{i\beta},e^{i(\theta+\pi)}),

we further consider the subspace
L^{1}_{\text{skew,even}}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
of functions in
L^{1}_{\text{skew}}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
, which, in addition to satisfying (
2.28
), satisfy (
2.29
).

Our result gives necessary and sufficient conditions for a function
g\in L^{1}_{\text{skew,even}}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
to coincide with
X{\bf f}
on
\varGamma_{+}
(and implicitly with
-X{\bf f}
on
\varGamma_{-}
), for some symmetric tensor
{\bf f}
of odd order
m
.

Theorem 2.2

(Range characterization for odd order tensors)
.

(i) Let
{\bf f}\in L^{1}(\mathbf{S}^{m};\Omega)
be a real valued, integrable symmetric tensor field of odd order
m=2q+1,\,q\geq 0
, satisfying (
2.7
), and
g\in L^{1}_{\emph{skew,even}}(\varGamma\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}})
, with

g=X{\bf f}\text{ on }\varGamma_{+}.

Then the Fourier coefficients
\{g_{n,k}\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
g
satisfy the following conditions:

(2.30)

\displaystyle\text{Evenness}:

\displaystyle\quad g_{n,k}=0,

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all \emph{odd} }n\in\mathbb{Z}\text{, and all }k\in\mathbb{Z};

(2.31)

\displaystyle\text{Conjugacy}:

\displaystyle\quad g_{-n,-k}=\overline{g_{n,k}},

\displaystyle\quad\text{for all}\;n,k\in\mathbb{Z};

(2.32)

Lemma A.1
.

Let
m,k\geq 0
be integers with
0\leq k\leq m
, and let
Q_{m.k}(t)=\left(t+1\right)^{m-k}\left(t-1\right)^{k}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\{Q_{m,k}(t)\}_{k=0}^{m}
form an basis in the space of polynomials of degree
m
.

Proof.

Given
h_{0},h_{1},\cdots,h_{m}
, we show that there are unique
g_{0},g_{1},\cdots,g_{m}
such that

(A.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}g_{k}(t-1)^{k}(t+1)^{m-k}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}h_{k}t^{k}.

We argue by induction in
m
. For
m=1
,
g_{0}=(h_{1}+h_{0})/2
and
g_{1}=(h_{1}-h_{0})/2
.

Assume next that
\displaystyle\{Q_{m,k}(t)\}_{k=0}^{m-1}
form a basis for polynomials of degree
m-1
. To simplify notation, let
a_{k}:={m\choose k}=\frac{m!}{k!(m-k)!}
, for
0\leq k\leq m
. Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}=2^{m}
.

The left hand side of (
A.1
) rewrites

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}

\displaystyle g_{k}(t-1)^{k}(t+1)^{m-k}=(t+1)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}g_{k}(t-1)^{k}(t+1)^{m-1-k}+g_{m}(t-1)^{m}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\text{induction hypothesis}}}(t+1)\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\gamma_{k}t^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}a_{k}g_{m}t^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\gamma_{k}t^{k+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\gamma_{k}t^{k}+\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}a_{k}g_{m}t^{k}

\displaystyle=\left(\gamma_{0}+(-1)^{m}a_{0}g_{m}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left(\gamma_{k-1}+\gamma_{k}+(-1)^{m-k}a_{k}g_{m}\right)t^{k}+\left(\gamma_{m-1}+a_{m}g_{m}\right)t^{m}.

The identity (
A.1
) yields that
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m-1},g_{m}
solve the
(m+1)\times(m+1)
linear system:

(A.2)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&0&\cdots&0&0&(-1)^{m}a_{0}\\
1&1&0&0&\cdots&0&0&(-1)^{m-1}a_{1}\\
0&1&1&0&\cdots&0&0&(-1)^{m-2}a_{2}\\
0&0&1&1&\cdots&0&0&(-1)^{m-3}a_{3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\cdots&1&1&-a_{m-1}\\
0&0&0&0&\cdots&0&1&a_{m}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\gamma_{0}\\
\gamma_{1}\\
\gamma_{2}\\
\gamma_{3}\\
\vdots\\
\gamma_{m-1}\\
g_{m}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}h_{0}\\
h_{1}\\
h_{2}\\
h_{3}\\
\vdots\\
h_{m-1}\\
h_{m}\end{bmatrix}.

The determinant of the matrix above is calculated by expanding it along the last column: Since all the
m\times m
- cofactor matrices have determinant 1, the
(m+1)\times(m+1)
determinant is
\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{k}=2^{m}>0
.

In addition to
g_{m}
being determined, the determination of
\gamma_{0},...,\gamma_{m-1}
together with the induction hypothesis uniquely determine
g_{0},...g_{m-1}

, the unknowns

are to be determined recursively for

, and thus the problem is reduced to the

case.

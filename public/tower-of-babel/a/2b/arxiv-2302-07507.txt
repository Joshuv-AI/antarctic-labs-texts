Abstract
. In this study, we investigate the existence, uniqueness, and maximal regularity estimates of solutions to homogeneous initial value problems involving time-measurable pseudo-differential operators within the framework of weighted mixed norm Lebesgue spaces. The class of temporal weights in our regularity estimates contains Muckenhoupt’s class, and the initial data is in weighted Besov spaces with variable order.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the homogeneous initial value problem described by the following equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
denotes a time-measurable pseudo-differential operator defined as

\displaystyle\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\psi(t,\cdot)\mathcal{F}[u(t,\cdot)]\right](x),

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
represent the
d
-dimensional Fourier transform and its inverse on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.
Specifically, they are defined by

\mathcal{F}[f](\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\xi\cdot x}f(x)\mathrm{d}x,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{F}^{-1}[f](x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

The symbol
\psi
associated with the operator
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
is required to fulfill two conditions:

•

We say that a symbol
\psi(t,\xi)
or the operator
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
satisfies
an ellipticity condition
(with (
\gamma
,
\kappa
))
if there exists a
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and
\kappa\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\Re[-\psi(t,\xi)]\geq\kappa|\xi|^{\gamma},\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.2)

where
\Re[z]
denotes the real part of the complex number
z
.

•

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that a symbol
\psi(t,\xi)
or the operator
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
has
a
n
-times regular upper bound
(with (
\gamma
,
M

\gamma
and
M
such that

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)|\leq M|\xi|^{\gamma-|\alpha|},\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in(0,\infty)\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}),

(1.3)

for any (
d
-dimensional) multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq n
.

The parameter
\gamma
in conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) denotes the

of the operator
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
, which is consistently set within the range
(0,\infty)
throughout this paper.

Our objective is to determine a suitable value for

in (

) that allows us to establish a well-posedness result and develop a maximal regularity theory for equation (
1.1
) within the framework of
L_{q}((0,T),\mu(\mathrm{d}t);L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))
.
Here,
\mu
denotes a nonnegative Borel measure, and
w
refers to a weight function belonging to Muckenhoupt’s

Definition 1.1
.

For

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},r>0}\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}w(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}w(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p-1}

Despite significant advancements, to the authors’ knowledge, results elucidating the solvability of equation (

This gap can be attributed to two principal challenges.
Firstly, the absence of trace theorems for non-power-type temporal weights, denoted as
\mu(\mathrm{d}t)\neq t^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}t
at the initial moment, leaves the optimal initial data space not characterized.
Secondly, the solution operator, defined by
u_{0}\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(t,0,x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y

L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to

\displaystyle K(t,s,x-y)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\cdot)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right](x-y).

This transformation complicates the application of the weighted
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})

1.4

Furthermore, while an

L_{p}

We overcome the difficulties by leveraging the Littlewood-Paley theory and the Laplace transform.
Initially, we prove spatial mean oscillation estimations for each Littlewood-Paley projection of a solution, as delineated in Theorem
4.2
.
This approach establishes a framework for weighted
L_{p}

Within this framework, integrating the solution along time variable yields the Laplace transform of a measure
\mu(\mathrm{d}t)

\mu
will be our temporal weight class.
We note that the temporal weight is beyond the
A_{p}(\mathbb{R})

The application of the Laplace transform to

These methodologies are underpinned by pointwise upper bound estimates for each Littlewood-Paley projection of the fundamental solution, as specified in Theorem
5.1

The novelty of our results is twofold.

(1)

Optimal initial data space
: Our findings are particularly significant when the temporal measure is defined as
\mu(\mathrm{d}t)=w(t)\mathrm{d}t

A question arises regarding whether the generalized Besov space proposed in this study is equivalent to the trace space.
We affirmatively address this question, offering a comprehensive explanation and references in Remark

(2)

Distinctiveness from inhomogeneous problems
: Our results extend beyond the scope of existing work on trace theorems and inhomogeneous problem solutions.

2.
Main result

To state the main result, we need the following definitions among sequences and functions.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a sequence

\|\bm{r}\|_{d}:=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\bm{r}(j+1)-\bm{r}(j)|<\infty.

Definition 2.2

Let

Given that we are dealing with a general weight, the interpretation of a solution to (

Nevertheless, it remains feasible to approximate the solution

The inherent lack of smoothness in the symbol

This broader class is denoted by

Herein, we provide a detailed mathematical definition to articulate this concept rigorously.

f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}:=\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{q\bm{r}(j)}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

(iii)

((Homogeneous) Weighted Bessel potential space)
We use
\dot{H}_{p,q}^{\tilde{\bm{r}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
to denote the space of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{p,q}^{\tilde{\bm{r}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}:=\left\|\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|2^{\tilde{\bm{r}}(j)}\Delta_{j}f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}<\infty.

(iv)

((Homogeneous) Weighted Besov space)

\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\tilde{\bm{r}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
denotes the space of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\tilde{\bm{r}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}:=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{q\tilde{\bm{r}}(j)}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that if
\bm{r}(j)=sj
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
, then the space
H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
is equivalent to
the classical weighted Bessel potential space
H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
whose norm is given by

\|f\|_{H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}:=\|(1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

(see Corollary
B.4
).

We can relate a constant to each
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to characterize sufficient smoothness of our symbols.
For each
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define

R_{p,d}^{w}:=\sup\left\{p_{0}\in(1,2]:w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}

(2.1)

and say that
R_{p,d}^{w}
is
the regularity constant of the weight
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Due to the reverse Hölder property of Muckenhoupt’s class,
R_{p,d}^{w}
is well-defined,
i.e.

R_{p,d}^{w}\in(1,2]
.
We assume that
\psi
has a
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor+2\right)
-times regular upper bound with
(\gamma,M)
.

Our main result is the following sufficient condition for the existence and uniqueness of a solution in weighted
L_{p}
-spaces with variable smoothness.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\mu
be a nonnegative Borel measure on
(0,\infty)
, and

\bm{r}:\mathbb{Z}\to(-\infty,\infty)
,
\bm{\mu}:\mathbb{Z}\to(-\infty,\infty)
be sequences having a controlled difference.
Suppose that
\psi
is a symbol satisfying the ellipticity condition with
(\gamma,\kappa)
and having the
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor+2\right)
-times regular upper bound with
(\gamma,M)

(
see (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (
2.1
)
)
.
Additionally, assume that the Laplace transform of
\mu
is controlled by a sequence
\bm{\mu}
in a
\gamma
-dyadic way with parameter
a\in(0,\infty)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(2^{\gamma j}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-2^{\gamma j}t\right)\mu(\mathrm{d}t)\leq N_{\mathcal{L}_{\mu}}\cdot 2^{j\gamma a}2^{-\bm{\mu}(j)},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.2)

Then for any
u_{0}\in B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}-\frac{\bm{\mu}}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
, there exists a unique solution

u\in L_{q}\left((0,T),t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right);H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)\right)

to (
1.1
).
Moreover, if
u\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then the following a priori estimates hold:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right)\leq N(1+\mu_{a,T,\kappa,\gamma,q})\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}-\frac{\bm{\mu}}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

(2.3)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\||\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,\cdot)|+|\Delta^{\gamma/2}u(t,\cdot)|\right\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}-\bm{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\|u_{0}\|^{q}_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\bm{r}-\frac{\bm{\mu}}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

(2.4)

where
[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K
,
N=N(a,\|\bm{r}\|_{d},\|\bm{\mu}\|_{d},N_{\mathcal{L}_{\mu}},d,\gamma,\kappa,K,M,p,q)
, and

\displaystyle\mu_{a,T,\kappa,\gamma,q}=\int_{0}^{T}t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right).

The proof of this main theorem will be given in Section
4
.
We finish the section by presenting the definition of our solution.

Definition 2.6

(Solution)
.

Assume that all parameters are given as in Theorem
2.5
.
We say that
u\in L_{q}\left((0,T),t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right);H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)\right)
is a solution to (
1.1
) if
there exists a sequence
u_{n}\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
u_{n}(0,\cdot)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}u_{n}(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u_{n}(t,x),\quad\forall(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u_{n}(0,\cdot)\to u_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}-\frac{\bm{\mu}}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),\\
u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in}\quad L_{q}\left((0,T),t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right);H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)\right)\end{gathered}

(2.5)

as
n\to\infty
.
Due to this definition, we have (
2.3
) for any solution

u\in L_{q}\left((0,T),t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right);H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)\right)

to (
1.1
) with the corresponding
u_{0}\in B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}-\frac{\bm{\mu}}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
.

Remark 2.7
.

Since our equation is linear, by using
a priori
estimate we may consider that our solution is a strong solution in
L_{q}\left((0,T),t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right);H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)\right)
.
Indeed, due to (
2.4
), for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\partial_{t}(u_{n}-u_{m})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}-\bm{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)(u_{n}-u_{m})(t,\cdot)\right\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}-\bm{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle\leq N\|u_{n}(0,\cdot)-u_{m}(0,\cdot)\|^{q}_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}-\frac{\bm{\mu}}{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Thus defining
\partial_{t}u
and
\psi(t,-i\nabla)u
as the limits of
\partial_{t}u_{n}
and
\psi(t,-i\nabla)u_{n}
in

L_{q}\left((0,T),t^{a}\mu\left(\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t\right);H_{p}^{\bm{r}-\bm{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)\right),

respectively, we understand that our solution
u
is a strong solution.
However, the limits
\partial_{t}u
and
\psi(t,-i\nabla)u
are not well-defined in general, which means that they could be dependent on the choice of a sequence
u_{n}
approximating to
u
.
To make the limits
\partial_{t}u
and
\psi(t,-i\nabla)u
uniquely determined, we need the condition
\bm{r}-\bm{\gamma}\geq 0
and another condition on a measure
\mu
, which will be specified in the next remark.

Remark 2.8
.

To show that our solution becomes the classical weak solution, we need an extra condition on a measure
\mu
.
For simplicity, we may assume that the scaling constant
\frac{(1\wedge q)\kappa}{16^{\gamma}}
is 1.
Additionally, assume that the measure
\mu(\mathrm{d}t)
has a density
\mu(t)
and
t^{-a}(1/\mu)(t)
is locally in
L_{q/(q-1)}
,
i.e.

\mu(\mathrm{d}t)=\mu(t)\mathrm{d}t
and

\int_{0}^{T_{0}}t^{-\frac{aq}{q-1}}(\mu(t))^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}\mathrm{d}t<\infty
for all
T_{0}\in(0,T)
.
Let
u
be a solution to (
1.1
). Then there exists a
u_{n}\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
so that (
2.5
) holds.
Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{n}(t,x)\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{n}(t,x)\overline{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x,

(2.6)

where

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\overline{\psi(t,\cdot)}\mathcal{F}[u(t,\cdot)]\right](x)

and
\overline{\psi(t,\xi)}
denotes the complex conjugate of
\psi(t,\xi)
.
Moreover, applying Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{n}(t,x)\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\|u_{n}-u\|_{L_{q}((0,T),t^{a}\mu\,\mathrm{d}t;L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}t^{-\frac{aq}{q-1}}(\mu(t))^{-\frac{q}{q-1}}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}},

where
T_{0}
is a positive number so that
\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)=0
for all
t\in[T_{0},T)
.
Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{n}(t,x)\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{n}(t,x)\overline{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\overline{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Finally, taking the limit in (
2.6
), we show that our solution becomes a classical weak solution,
i.e.

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)\overline{\psi}(t,-i\nabla)\varphi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

(2.7)

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 2.9
.

We want to emphasize that (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) can be distinguished.
In other words, there could be different two measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
so that
\bm{\mu_{1}}(j)=\bm{\mu_{2}}(j)
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
but there exists a
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\bm{\mu_{1}}(k)\neq\bm{\mu_{2}}(k)
.

Let
\mu_{1}(\mathrm{d}t):=|t|^{b_{1}}\mathrm{d}t
and
\mu_{2}(\mathrm{d}t):=\left(|t|^{b_{1}}+|t|^{b_{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t
.
Since both
|t|^{b_{1}}
and
|t|^{b_{1}}+|t|^{b_{2}}
are in
A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, by Lemma
3.1
-
(ii)
and
(iii)
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}((0,2^{-j\gamma}))\simeq 2^{-j\gamma(b_{1}+1)}\implies\bm{\mu_{1}}(j)=j\gamma(a+b_{1}+1)\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mu_{2}((0,2^{-j\gamma}))=2^{-j\gamma(b_{1}+1)}+2^{-j\gamma(b_{2}+1)}.

If
-1<b_{1}<b_{2}<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{2}((0,2^{-j\gamma}))\lesssim\begin{cases}&2^{-j\gamma(b_{1}+1)}\quad\text{if}~j\in\mathbb{N},\\
&2^{-j\gamma(b_{2}+1)}\quad\text{if $j$ is a non-positive integer}.\end{cases}

Therefore if we put

\displaystyle\bm{\mu_{2}}(j):=\begin{cases}&j\gamma(a+b_{1}+1)\quad\text{if}~j\in\mathbb{N},\\
&j\gamma(a+b_{2}+1)\quad\text{if $j$ is a non-positive integer},\end{cases}

then
\bm{\mu_{1}}(j)=\bm{\mu_{2}}(j)
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, but,
\bm{\mu_{1}}\neq\bm{\mu_{2}}
.

3.
Examples and Applications

In this section, we present some sufficient conditions for
(\mu,\bm{\mu})
satisfying (
2.2
) and corresponding well-posedness results.
We also present applications of our results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be a nonnegative Borel measure defined on
(0,\infty)
and
\mu^{-a_{0}}(\mathrm{d}t):=t^{-a_{0}}\mu(\mathrm{d}t)
.
Suppose that
a_{0}
is a nonnegative constant that the Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}
is well-defined.

(i)

If

\displaystyle\inf_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(2^{\gamma(j+1)})}{\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(2^{\gamma j})}>0,

(3.1)

then
\bm{\mu}(j):=j\gamma a-\log_{2}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(2^{\gamma j})\right)
is a sequence having the controlled difference, and
(\mu^{-a_{0}},\bm{\mu})
satisfies (
2.2
) for
a_{0}\in[0,\infty)
.

(ii)

Suppose that for any
k\in(1,\infty)
, there exist positive constants
b_{k}
and
B_{k}
such that

0<b_{k}\leq\frac{\mu((0,\theta))}{\mu((0,k\theta))}\leq B_{k}<1,\quad\forall\theta\in(0,\infty).

(3.2)

Then
\bm{\mu}(j):=(j\gamma(a-a_{0})-\log_{2}(\mu(0,2^{-j\gamma})))
is a sequence having the controlled difference, and
(\mu^{-a_{0}},\bm{\mu})
satisfies (
2.2
) for
a_{0}\in[0,-\log_{2}(B_{2}))
.

(iii)

If
w^{\prime}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then
w^{\prime}
satisfies (
3.2
).

(iv)

Let
\delta(t)
be a nonnegative locally integrable function,
\eta(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\delta(s)\mathrm{d}s
,
\eta^{-1}(t):=\inf\{s>0:\eta(s)\geq t\}
and
\mu(\mathrm{d}t):=1_{[0,T^{\prime}]}(t)/(\delta(\eta^{-1}))\mathrm{d}t
.
Suppose that there exist positive constants
\beta,t_{0},N_{0}
such that

\left|\left\{t\in[0,t_{0}]:h\leq\eta(t)<4h\right\}\right|\leq N_{0}h^{1/\beta}.

(3.3)

Then
\bm{\mu}(j):=j\gamma(a-a_{0}-1)-\frac{j\gamma}{\beta}
is a sequence having the controlled difference, and
(\mu^{-a_{0}},\bm{\mu})
satisfies (
2.2
) for
a_{0}\in[0,1/\beta)
.

Proof.

(i)
Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(2^{\gamma j})=2^{\log_{2}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(2^{\gamma j})\right)}=2^{j\gamma a}2^{-j\gamma a+\log_{2}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(2^{\gamma j})\right)}=2^{j\gamma a}2^{-\bm{\mu}(j)},

(
2.2
) holds with
N_{\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}}=1
.
The sequence
\bm{\mu}
has a controlled difference due to (
3.1
)
and the fact that
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(2^{\gamma j})
is non-increasing as
j
increases.

(ii)
Choose

\displaystyle a_{0}\in\left[0,-\log_{2}B_{2}\right).

(3.4)

We claim that

\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(\lambda)\simeq\lambda^{a_{0}}\mu((0,1/\lambda))\quad\forall\lambda\in(0,\infty).

(3.5)

Indeed, by (
3.2
) and the ratio test,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\mu^{-a_{0}}(dt)\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\mu(dt)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{n-1}}^{2^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\mu(dt)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2^{n-1}}\mu((0,2^{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\mu((0,1))\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2^{n-1}}b^{-n}_{2}<\infty.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\mu^{-a_{0}}(\mathrm{d}t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{2^{-n-1}}^{2^{-n}}t^{-a_{0}}\mathrm{d}t

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{(n+1)a_{0}}\mu((0,2^{-n}))\leq\mu((0,1))\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{(n+1)a_{0}}B_{2}^{n}.

Due to the choice of
a_{0}
in (
3.4
), it is obvious that
2^{a_{0}}B_{2}<1
and the last term in (
3.7
) converges.
Therefore combining (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(1)\simeq\mu((0,1)).

(3.8)

Next, we use the scaling property.
For
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, define the measures as

\mu_{1/\lambda}(\mathrm{d}t):=\mu\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)

and

\mu^{-a_{0}}_{1/\lambda}(\mathrm{d}t):=\mu^{-a_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\mathrm{d}t\right).

Then for any
k\in(1,\infty)
,

0<b_{k}\leq\frac{\mu_{1/\lambda}((0,\theta))}{\mu_{1/\lambda}((0,k\theta))}\leq B_{k}<1,\quad\forall\theta\in(0,\infty).

Thus applying (
3.8
) with
\mu_{1/\lambda}
instead of
\mu(\mathrm{d}t)
, we prove the claim that

\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(\lambda)=\lambda^{a_{0}}\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}_{1/\lambda}}(1)\simeq\lambda^{a_{0}}\mu_{1/\lambda}((0,1))=\lambda^{a_{0}}\mu((0,1/\lambda)).

Therefore, for all
a\in[0,\infty)
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(2^{j\gamma})\simeq 2^{j\gamma a_{0}}\mu((0,2^{-j\gamma}))=2^{j\gamma a}\cdot 2^{-\left(j\gamma(a-a_{0})-\log_{2}(\mu((0,2^{-j\gamma})))\right)}

In other words,
the Laplace transform of
\mu^{-a_{0}}
with
a_{0}\in\left[0,-\log_{2}B_{2}\right)
is controlled by a sequence

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}(j)=\left(j\gamma(a-a_{0})-\log_{2}(\mu((0,2^{-j\gamma})))\right)

(3.9)

in a
\gamma
-dyadic way with a parameter
a
.

(iii)

Since
w^{\prime}\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists
\nu\in(1,\infty)
such that
w^{\prime}\in A_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})
. By
[
14
, Proposition 7.1.5 and Lemma 7.2.1]
,

b_{k}:=\frac{1}{k^{\nu}[w^{\prime}]_{A_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}}\leq\frac{\mu((0,\theta))}{\mu((0,k\theta))}\leq\left(1-\frac{(1-k^{-1})^{\nu}}{[w^{\prime}]_{A_{\nu}(\mathbb{R})}}\right)=:B_{k},\quad\forall k\in(1,\infty),\,\theta\in(0,\infty).

(iv)
It suffices to prove that for any
T^{\prime}>0
, there exists a constant
N_{T^{\prime}}>0
such that

\eta(t)\geq N_{T^{\prime}}t^{\beta},\quad\forall t\in[0,T^{\prime}].

(3.10)

Indeed, if we have (
3.10
), then for
a_{0}\in[0,1/\beta)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{-a_{0}}}(2^{j\gamma})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}\mathrm{e}^{-2^{j\gamma}t}\frac{t^{-a_{0}}}{\delta(\eta^{-1}(t))}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\eta^{-1}(T^{\prime})}\mathrm{e}^{-2^{j\gamma}\eta(s)}(\eta(s))^{-a_{0}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq N(N_{0},\beta,T^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2^{j\gamma}s^{\beta}}}{s^{a_{0}\beta}}\mathrm{d}s=N2^{j\gamma a}2^{-\bm{\mu}(j)}.

Since
T^{\prime}
is finite, it suffices to prove only that (
3.10
) holds near zero. Note that (
3.3
) can be rewritten as

\left|\left\{t\in[0,t_{0}]:h\leq\eta(t)<4h\right\}\right|=|[0,t_{0}]\cap[\eta^{-1}(h),\eta^{-1}(4h))|\leq N_{0}h^{1/\beta},\quad\forall h>0.

Observe that for
\eta^{-1}(h)\leq t_{0}

|[0,t_{0}]\cap[\eta^{-1}(h/4),\eta^{-1}(h))|=|[\eta^{-1}(h/4),\eta^{-1}(h))|.

Then we have

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}(h)

\displaystyle=\eta^{-1}(h)-\eta^{-1}(h/4)+\eta^{-1}(h/4)-\eta^{-1}(h/4^{2})+\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta^{-1}(4^{-k}h)-\eta^{-1}(4^{-1-k}h)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{0}(4^{-1-k}h)^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle=N(N_{0},\beta)h^{1/\beta}.

In a word,
\eta^{-1}(h)\leq N(N_{0},\beta)h^{1/\beta}
for all
\eta^{-1}(h)\leq t_{0}
. Since
\eta(\eta^{-1}(t))=t
for all
t>0
, we put
\eta(\eta^{-1}(h))
instead of
h
and take
t=\eta^{-1}(h)
to obtain

u(t,x):=\mathcal{T}_{t,0}u_{0}(x)
becomes a classical strong solution to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

Therefore, roughly speaking, it is sufficient to show boundedness of
\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{\varepsilon}u_{0}
in appropriate spaces for our
a priori
estimates.
More precisely, due to the definitions of the Besov and Sobolev spaces, we have to estimate their Littlewood-Paley projections.
We recall that
\Psi
is a function in the Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}[\Psi]
is nonnegative, supported in an annulus
\{\frac{1}{2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
, and
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)=1
for
\xi\not=0
.
Then we define the Littlewood-Paley projection operators
\Delta_{j}
and
S_{0}
as
\mathcal{F}[\Delta_{j}f](\xi)=\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)\mathcal{F}[f](\xi)
,
S_{0}f=\sum_{j\leq 0}\Delta_{j}f
, respectively.
We denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t,s}^{\varepsilon,j}f(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{j}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{t,s}^{\varepsilon,\leq 0}f(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S_{0}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y,

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y):=(\Delta_{j}P_{\varepsilon})(t,s,x-y):=\Delta_{j}\left[P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,\cdot)\right](x-y).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\psi(t,-i\nabla)\mathcal{T}_{t,s}^{j}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{j}\psi(t,-i\nabla)p(t,s,x-y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y

and

\displaystyle\psi(t,-i\nabla)\mathcal{T}_{t,s}^{\leq 0}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S_{0}\psi(t,-i\nabla)p(t,s,x-y)f(y)\mathrm{d}y.

Next we recall Hardy-Littlewood’s maximal function and Fefferman-Stein’s sharp (maximal) function.
For a locally integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}f(x)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|f(y)|\mathrm{d}y:=\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x_{0})|}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|f(y)|\mathrm{d}y

and

\displaystyle f^{\sharp}(x):=\mathbb{M}^{\sharp}f(x)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|f(y_{0})-f(y_{1})|\mathrm{d}y_{0}\mathrm{d}y_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{|B_{r}(x_{0})|^{2}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|f(y_{0})-f(y_{1})|\mathrm{d}y_{0}\mathrm{d}y_{1},

where the supremum is taken over all balls
B_{r}(x_{0})
containing
x
with
r\in(0,\infty)
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Moreover, for a function
f(t,x)
defined on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we use the notation

\mathbb{M}_{x}^{\sharp}\big(f(t,x)\big)
or
\mathbb{M}_{x}^{\sharp}\big(f(t,\cdot)\big)(x)
to denote the sharp function with respect to the variable
x
after fixing
t
.
We recall a weighted version of the Hardy-Littlewood Theorem and Fefferman-Stein Theorem which play an important role in our main estimate.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that
[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K
for a positive constant
K
.
Then there exists a positive constant
N=N(d,K,p)
such that for any
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbb{M}f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

and

\displaystyle\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\big\|\mathbb{M}_{x}^{\sharp}f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

This weighted version of the Hardy-Littlewood Theorem and the Fefferman-Stein Theorem is very well-known. For instance, see
[
7
, Theorems 2.2 and 2.3]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
p_{0}\in(1,2]
be a constant so that
p_{0}\leq R_{p,d}^{w}
and

\left\lfloor\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor.

Suppose that
\psi
is a symbol satisfying the ellipticity condition with
(\gamma,\kappa)
and having the
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor+2\right)
-times regular upper bound with
(\gamma,M)
.
Then there exist positive constants
N=N(d,\delta,\varepsilon,\gamma,\kappa,M
,
N^{\prime}=N^{\prime}(d,p_{0},\varepsilon,\gamma,\kappa,M)
,
N^{\prime\prime}=N^{\prime\prime}(d,p_{0},\delta,\gamma,\kappa,M)
, and
N^{\prime\prime\prime}=N^{\prime\prime\prime}(d,p_{0},\gamma,\kappa,M)
such that for all

,

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{M}^{\sharp}_{x}\left(\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{\varepsilon,j}f\right)(x)\leq N2^{j\varepsilon\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa t2^{j\gamma}\times\frac{p_{0}(1-\delta)}{2^{\gamma}}}\left(\mathbb{M}\left(|f|^{p_{0}}\right)(x)\right)^{1/p_{0}},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1),\\

The proof of Proposition
4.2
is given in Sections
5
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Suppose that
\psi
is a symbol satisfying the ellipticity condition with
(\gamma,\kappa)
and having the
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor+2\right)
-times regular upper bound with
(\gamma,M)
.

,

,

, and

,

, and

where the dependence of constants
N
,
N^{\prime}
,
N^{\prime\prime}
, and
N^{\prime\prime\prime}
is given similarly to those in Proposition
4.2
with additional dependence to
p
and an upper bound of the
A_{p}
semi-norm of

.

Proof.

First, we claim that there exists a
p_{0}\in(1,2]
such that

p_{0}\leq R_{p,d}^{w}\wedge p
,
w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\left\lfloor\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor
.
Recall that

\displaystyle R_{p,d}^{w}:=\sup\left\{p_{0}\in(1,2]:w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}.

It is well-known that
R_{p,d}^{w}>1
and

w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\forall p_{0}\in\left(1,R_{p,d}^{w}\right)

due to the reverse Hölder inequality (
e.g.
see
[
4
, Remark 2.2]
).
Note that
\left\lfloor\frac{d}{p}\right\rfloor
is left-continuous and piecewise-constant with respect to
p
.
Therefore, there exists a
p_{0}\in\left(1,R_{p,d}^{w}\right)
such that
\left\lfloor\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor

and
w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It only remains to show that
p_{0}\in\left(1,R_{p,d}^{w}\right)
above is less than or equal to
p
.
If
p\geq 2
, then it is obvious since
p_{0}<2
. Thus we only consider the case
p\in(1,2)
.
Recall that
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined only for
p>1
(
A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-class is not introduced in this paper (see Definition (
1.1
)).
Thus any
p_{0}\in(1,2)
such that

is obviously less than to
p
.

Next we apply the weighted version of Hardy-Littlewood Theorem and Fefferman-Stein Theorem.
Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and choose a
p_{0}
in the claim, i.e.
p_{0}\in\left(1,R_{p,d}^{w}\wedge p\right)
,
w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

\left\lfloor\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor
.
Then by Proposition
4.2
, we have

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{M}^{\sharp}_{x}\left(\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{\varepsilon,j}f\right)(x)\leq N2^{j\varepsilon\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa t2^{j\gamma}\times\frac{p_{0}(1-\delta)}{2^{\gamma}}}\left(\mathbb{M}\left(|f|^{p_{0}}\right)(x)\right)^{1/p_{0}},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1),\\
\mathbb{M}^{\sharp}_{x}\left(\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{\varepsilon,\leq 0}f\right)(x)\leq N^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{M}\left(|f|^{p_{0}}\right)(x)\right)^{1/p_{0}},\\
\mathbb{M}^{\sharp}_{x}\left(\psi(t,-i\nabla)\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{j}f\right)(x)\leq N^{\prime\prime}2^{j\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa t2^{j\gamma}\times\frac{p_{0}(1-\delta)}{2^{\gamma}}}\left(\mathbb{M}\left(|f|^{p_{0}}\right)(x)\right)^{1/p_{0}},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1),\\
\mathbb{M}^{\sharp}_{x}\left(\psi(t,-i\nabla)\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{\leq 0}f\right)(x)\leq N^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(\mathbb{M}\left(|f|^{p_{0}}\right)(x)\right)^{1/p_{0}}.\end{gathered}

Moreover, recalling
\frac{p}{p_{0}}\in(1,\infty)
and applying Proposition
4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{\varepsilon,j}f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N2^{j\varepsilon\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa t2^{j\gamma}\times\frac{p_{0}(1-\delta)}{2^{\gamma}}}\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{\varepsilon,\leq 0}f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N^{\prime}\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

\displaystyle\big\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{j}f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N^{\prime\prime}2^{j\gamma}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa t2^{j\gamma}\times\frac{p_{0}(1-\delta)}{2^{\gamma}}}\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\big\|\psi(t,-i\nabla)\mathcal{T}_{t,0}^{\leq 0}f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N^{\prime\prime\prime}\big\|f\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Since
p_{0}\in(1,2)
, the corollary is proved.

Lemma 5.7

Appendix A
Weighted multiplier and Littlewood-Paley theorem

Proposition A.1

(Weighted Mikhlin multiplier theorem)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Suppose that

\sup_{R>0}\left(R^{2|\alpha|-d}\int_{R<|\xi|<2R}|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\pi(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2}\leq N^{*},\quad\forall|\alpha|\leq d.

(A.1)

Then there exists a constant
N=N(d,p,K,N^{*})
such that

\|\mathbb{T}_{\pi}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

where
[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K
and

\mathbb{T}_{\pi}f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\pi\mathcal{F}[f]](x).

Proof.

By
[
14
, Theorem 6.2.7]
, the operator
\mathbb{T}_{\pi}:L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded for all
q\in(1,\infty)
. It is well-known that there exists
r>1
such that
w\in A_{p/r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Applying
[
11
, Corollaries 6.10, 6.11, and Remark 6.14]
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{T}_{\pi}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

where
N=N(d,p,K,N^{*})
. The proposition is proved.
∎

Proposition A.2

(Weighted Littlewood-Paley theorem)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|Sf\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,p)[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\max(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p-1})}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

(A.2)

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,p)[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{\max(1/2,1/(p^{\prime}-1))}{p-1}}\|Sf\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

(A.3)

where

\displaystyle Sf(x):=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\Delta_{j}f(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(A.4)

Proof.

This result is already proved in several literature
[
33
,
46
]
. They did not, however, reveal the growth of implicit constants relative to
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-seminorm.
These optimum implicit constants could be obtained from recent general theories.
The first inequality is proved in
[
35
,
49
]
for various types of Littlewood-Paley operators.
We show that
Sf
is one of such Littlewood-Paley operators.
The second inequality follows from the first inequality and the duality of
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
, which will be shown in the last part of the proof.

For
\alpha\in(0,1]
, let
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
be a family of functions
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
supported in
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|\leq 1\}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad|\phi(x)-\phi(x^{\prime})|\leq|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha},\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then we define a maximal operator over the family
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
as

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(f)(x,t):=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}|\phi_{t}\ast f(x)|,\quad\phi_{t}(y)=t^{-d}\phi(t^{-1}y).

(A.5)

Using (
A.5
), we construct intrinsic square functions as follows:

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\biggl(\int_{\Gamma(x)}\big|A_{\alpha}(f)(y,t)\big|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t}{t^{d+1}}\biggr)^{1/2},

\displaystyle g_{\alpha}(f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\biggl(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|A_{\alpha}(f)(x,t)\big|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\biggr)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha}(f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\biggl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|A_{\alpha}(f)(x,2^{j})\big|^{2}\biggr)^{1/2},

where
\Gamma(x)
denotes the conic area
\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}:|x-y|\leq t\}
.
In
[
49
]
, Wilson showed pointwise equivalences among
G_{\alpha}
,
g_{\alpha}
and
\sigma_{\alpha}
,
i.e.

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(f)(x)\approx g_{\alpha}(f)(x)\approx\sigma_{\alpha}(f)(x),

(A.6)

where the implicit constants depend only on
\alpha
and
d
.
Moreover, Lerner
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
proved

\displaystyle\|G_{\alpha}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq C(\alpha,d,p)[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\max(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p-1})}.

(A.7)

It should be remarked that
[
35
, Theorem 1.1]
covers a broad class of operators of Littlewood-Paley type.
However, this result does not give (
A.2
) directly since the Littlewood-Paley operator considered in this paper is not an integral form (see (
A.4
)).
Our Littlewood-Paley operator is given as the summation of
\Delta_{j}f=\Psi_{j}\ast f
over
\mathbb{Z}
and the symbol of
\Delta_{j}
is supported in
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:2^{j-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{j+1}\}
.
Note that
\Psi_{j}
is globally defined due to the uncertainty principle, while elements in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
are supported in
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|\leq 1\}
.
To fill this gap, we need a new family
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
introduced in
[
49
]
.
For
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\varepsilon>0
, let
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
be a family of functions
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\begin{gathered}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad|\phi(x)|\leq(1+|x|)^{-d-\varepsilon},\\
|\phi(x)-\phi(x^{\prime})|\leq|x-x^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\bigl((1+|x|)^{-d-\varepsilon}+(1+|x^{\prime}|)^{-d-\varepsilon}\bigr),\quad\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{gathered}

Then we can define
\widetilde{A}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
on the basis of all functions in the above class as in (
A.5
),
i.e.

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)(x,t):=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}|\phi_{t}\ast f(x)|,\quad\phi_{t}(y)=t^{-d}\phi(t^{-1}y).

(A.8)

We can also define
\widetilde{G}_{\alpha,\varepsilon},\widetilde{g}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{\sigma}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
similarly to
G_{\alpha}
,
g_{\alpha}
and
\sigma_{\alpha}
. Likewise, they become equivalent,
i.e.

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)(x)\approx\widetilde{g}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)(x)\approx\widetilde{\sigma}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)(x).

(A.9)

Here, the implicit constants depend only on
\alpha,\varepsilon
and
d
. Since every Schwartz function is contained in
\mathcal{C}_{1,1}
, it follows that
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{1,1}
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle|\Delta_{j}f(x)|\leq\widetilde{A}_{1,1}(f)(x,2^{j}),

which yields

\displaystyle Sf(x)\leq C(d)\widetilde{\sigma}_{1,1}(f)(x)\approx C(d)\widetilde{G}_{1,1}(f)(x).

(A.10)

It is also known in
[
49
, Theorem 2]
, for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
0<\alpha^{\prime}\leq\alpha
, there is a constant
C=C(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\varepsilon,d)
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(f)(x)\leq CG_{\alpha^{\prime}}(f)(x).

(A.11)

Thus finally by (
A.7
), (
A.9
), (
A.10
) and (
A.11
), we have

\displaystyle\big\|Sf\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq C(d)\big\|\widetilde{G}_{1,1}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

(A.12)

\displaystyle\leq C(d)\big\|G_{1}(f)\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq C(d,p)[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\max(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p-1})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

For the converse inequality (
A.3
), we recall that the topological dual space of
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
is
L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bar{w}\,\mathrm{d}x)
where

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,\quad\bar{w}(x):=(w(x))^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

For a more detail, see
[
4
, Theorem A.1]
.
Then by the almost orthogonality of
\Delta_{j}
, the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, Hölder’s inequality, and (
A.12
), for all
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
and
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{j}f(x)(\Delta_{j-1}+\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j+1})g(x)\mathrm{d}x

(A.13)

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Sf(x)Sg(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq 3\|Sf\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\|Sg\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bar{w}\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq N(d,p)[\bar{w}]_{A_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\max(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}-1})}\|Sf\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\|g\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bar{w}\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Combining (
A.12
) and (
A.13
), we have

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}=\sup_{\|g\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bar{w}\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq N(d,p)[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{\max(1/2,1/(p^{\prime}-1))}{p-1}}\|Sf\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

The proposition is proved.
∎

Appendix B
Properties of function spaces

Lemma B.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose that

[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K.

Then there exists a positive constant
N=N(d,p,K)
such that

\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x).

Proof.

By the definition of
S_{0}
, there exists a
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

S_{0}f(x)=\Phi\ast f(x)=\int_{B_{1}(0)}f(x-y)\Phi(y)\mathrm{d}y+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{2^{k}}(0)\setminus\overline{B_{2^{k-1}}(0)}}f(x-y)\Phi(y)\mathrm{d}y.

Since
\Phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists
N=N(d,\Phi)
such that

|\Phi(y)|+|y|^{d+1}|\Phi(y)|\leq N,\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Therefore
|S_{0}f(x)|\leq N(d,\Phi)\mathbb{M}f(x)
and it leads to the first part of the inequality,
i.e.

\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)

due to the weighted Hardy-Littlewood Theorem.
Next recall

\Delta_{j}f=f\ast\Psi_{j}=f\ast 2^{jd}\Psi(2^{j}\cdot),

and we show that
\Delta_{j}
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator to obtain

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

(B.1)

In other words, we have to show that
f\to\Delta_{j}f
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Psi_{j}(x-y)
is a standard kernel.
It is obvious that

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}[\Psi_{j}](\xi)|=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)|=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}[\Psi](\xi)|,

which implies that
f\to\Delta_{j}f
becomes a bounded operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
due to the Plancherel theorem.
More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}[\Psi](\xi)|\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

(B.2)

where
\Psi_{j}=2^{jd}\Psi(2^{j}\cdot)
and
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Next we show that
\Psi_{j}(x-y)
is a standard kernel.
Observe that there exists a
N=N(d,\Psi)
such that

\displaystyle|x|^{d}|\Psi_{j}(x)|+|x|^{d+1}|\nabla\Psi_{j}(x)|=|2^{j}x|^{d}|\Psi(2^{j}x)|+|2^{j}x|^{d+1}|\nabla\Psi(2^{j}x)|\leq N,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(B.3)

Moreover, by the fundamental theorem of calculus,

|\Psi_{j}(x)-\Psi_{j}(y)|\leq|x-y|\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla\Psi_{j}(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)|\mathrm{d}\theta.

If
2|x-y|\leq\max(|x|,|y|)
, then for
\theta\in[0,1]
,

|\theta x+(1-\theta)y|\geq\max(|x|,|y|)-|x-y|\geq\frac{\max(|x|,|y|)}{2}\geq\frac{|x|+|y|}{4}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\Psi_{j}(x)-\Psi_{j}(y)|\leq|x-y|\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla\Psi_{j}(\theta x+(1-\theta)y)|d\theta\leq\frac{N4^{d+1}|x-y|}{(|x|+|y|)^{d+1}}.

(B.4)

Due to (
B.3
) and (
B.4
),
\Psi_{j}(x-y)
becomes a standard kernel with constant
1
and
N
.
Especially,
N
can be chosen uniformly for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In total, the
L_{2}
-bounds are given uniformly for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
in (
B.2
) and
\Psi_{j}(x-y)
becomes a standard kernel with same parameters for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore applying
[
14
, Theorem 7.4.6]
, we have (
B.1
). The lemma is proved.
∎

Remark B.2
.

An operator
T:L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is linearizable if there exist a Banach space
B
and a
B
-valued linear operator
U:L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};B)
such that

|Tf(x)|=\|Uf(x)\|_{B},\quad f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Hence any linear operator is linearizable.

Let
\{T_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of linearizable operators and
K\in(0,\infty)
.
Assume that there exist a
r\in(1,\infty)
and
C(K)\in(0,\infty)
such that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|T_{j}f(x)|^{r}w(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq C(K)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{r}w(x)\mathrm{d}x

(B.5)

for all
[w]_{A_{r}}\leq K
and
f\in L_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
.
Then for all
1<p,q<\infty
, there exist
K^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
and
C
such that

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|T_{j}f_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq C\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|f_{j}|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x)}

(B.6)

for all
[w^{\prime}]_{A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K^{\prime}
and
f_{j}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x)
. The above statement can be found, for instance, in
[
13
, p. 521, Remarks 6.5]
with a modification to dependence of
A_{p}
-norms.
In particular, since
\Delta_{j}
is linear and satisfies the weighted inequality for any
r>1
due to Lemma
B.1
, we have (
B.6
)
with
T_{j}=\Delta_{j}
and it will be used in the proof of the next proposition.

For
\bm{r}:\mathbb{Z}\to(0,\infty)
, we denote

\pi_{\bm{r}}:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}~\text{and}~\pi^{\bm{r}}:=S_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}.

Proposition B.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty)
, and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose that two sequences
\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}:\mathbb{Z}\to(-\infty,\infty)
satisfy

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(|\bm{r}^{\prime}(j+1)-\bm{r}^{\prime}(j)|+|\bm{r}(j+1)-\bm{r}(j)|\right)=:C_{0}<\infty.

(B.7)

Then for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\begin{gathered}\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|f\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\quad\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\\
\|\pi_{\bm{r}}f\|_{\dot{H}_{p}^{\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{p}^{\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\quad\|\pi_{\bm{r}}f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,q}^{\bm{r}+\bm{r}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\end{gathered}

where the equivalences depend only on
p
,
q
,
d
,
C_{0}
and
[w]_{A_{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
In particular, we have

\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|f\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Proof.

Due to the existence of
S_{0}
, the proof of the inhomogeneous case become more difficult.
Even the case
\bm{r}^{\prime}\neq\bm{0}
is quite similar to the case
\bm{r}^{\prime}=\bm{0}
, where

\bm{0}(j)=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus we only prove the inhomogeneous case with the assumption
\bm{r}^{\prime}=\bm{0}
.

First, by the definition of
\pi^{\bm{r}}
and the triangle inequality, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

(B.8)

For the converse inequality of (
B.8
), we use Proposition
A.1
. One can easily check that

\Pi_{0}(\xi):=\frac{\mathcal{F}[\Phi](\xi)}{\mathcal{F}[\Phi](\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)}

is infinitely differentiable,
\Pi_{0}(\xi)=1
if
|\xi|\leq 1
and
\Pi_{0}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\geq 2
, where

\mathcal{F}[\Phi](\xi)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi).

This certainly implies that
\Pi_{0}
satisfies (
A.1
). Hence by Proposition
A.1
,

\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}=\|\mathbb{T}_{\Pi_{0}}\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

where

\mathbb{T}_{\Pi_{0}}f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\Pi_{0}\mathcal{F}[f]](x).

This also yields

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}=\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f-S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

and thus

\displaystyle\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

(B.9)

Moreover, Proposition
A.2
implies

\displaystyle\Big\|S\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},

(B.10)

where the implicit constant depends only on
p
,
d
and
[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
Therefore by (
B.9
) and (
B.10
),

\displaystyle\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+\Big\|S\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Next we claim

\displaystyle\Big\|S\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

(B.11)

Put
T_{j}:=\Delta_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
and

\begin{gathered}f_{0}:=2^{\bm{r}(1)}\Delta_{1}f,\quad f_{1}:=2^{\bm{r}(1)}\Delta_{1}f+2^{\bm{r}(2)}\Delta_{2}f\\
f_{j}:=(2^{\bm{r}(j-1)}\Delta_{j-1}+2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}+2^{\bm{r}(j+1)}\Delta_{j+1})f\quad\text{if }j\geq 2,\quad f_{j}:=0\quad\text{if }j<0.\end{gathered}

Considering the almost orthogonal property of
\Delta_{j}
and Remark
B.2
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|S\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|T_{j}f_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big\|\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

For the converse of (
B.11
), we make use of the Khintchine inequality (
e.g.
see
[
16
]
),

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\big|2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\approx\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big|^{p}\Big)^{1/p},\quad p\in(0,\infty)

(B.12)

where the implicit constants depend only on
p
and
\{X_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables with the Rademacher distribution. Let
\Pi_{1}
be a Fourier multiplier defined by

\Pi_{1}(\xi):=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)}{\mathcal{F}[\Phi](\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)}.

Recall the notation

\mathbb{T}_{\Pi_{1}}f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\Pi_{1}\mathcal{F}[f]](x)

and

\displaystyle\pi^{\bm{r}}:=S_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}.

Then the right-hand side of (
B.12
) equals to

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left|\mathbb{T}_{\Pi_{1}}(\pi^{\bm{r}}f)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

It is easy to check that
\Pi_{1}
satisfies (
A.1
), that is,
\Pi_{1}
is a weighted Mikhlin multiplier.
Thus it follows that

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle\leq N\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

(B.13)

\displaystyle=N\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big\|\mathbb{T}_{\Pi_{1}}(\pi^{\bm{r}}f)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq N[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\max(1,(p-1)^{-1})}\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

By (
B.11
), (
B.12
) and (
B.13
), we conclude that

\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|f\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Similarly, we compute
\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}
.
Recall the definition first:

\displaystyle\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle=\|S_{0}\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\|\Delta_{j}\pi^{\bm{r}}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle=\|S_{0}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\|2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}.

Due to the almost orthogonal property again, it is obvious that

\begin{gathered}S_{0}\pi^{\bm{r}}f=S_{0}(S_{0}+2^{\bm{r}(1)}\Delta_{1})f,\\
\Delta_{1}\pi^{\bm{r}}f=\Delta_{1}(S_{0}+2^{\bm{r}(1)}\Delta_{1}+2^{\bm{r}(2)}\Delta_{2})f,\\
\Delta_{j}\pi^{\bm{r}}f=\Delta_{j}(2^{\bm{r}(j-1)}\Delta_{j-1}+2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}+2^{\bm{r}(j+1)}\Delta_{j+1})f,\quad j\geq 2.\end{gathered}

Moreover, setting

\begin{gathered}\Pi_{2}:=\frac{\mathcal{F}[\Phi]+\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-1}\cdot)}{\mathcal{F}[\Phi]+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\cdot)},\\
\Pi_{3}:=\frac{2^{\bm{r}(1)}(\mathcal{F}[\Phi]+\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-1}\cdot)+\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-2}\cdot))}{\mathcal{F}[\Phi]+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\cdot)},\\
\Pi_{j+2}:=\frac{2^{\bm{r}(j)}(\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j+1}\cdot)+\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\cdot)+\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j-1}\cdot))}{\mathcal{F}[\Phi]+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\cdot)},\quad j\geq 2,\end{gathered}

we have

\begin{gathered}S_{0}f=\mathbb{T}_{\Pi_{2}}S_{0}\pi^{\bm{r}}f,\quad 2^{\bm{r}(1)}\Delta_{1}f=\mathbb{T}_{\Pi_{3}}\Delta_{1}\pi^{\bm{r}}f,\\
2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f=\mathbb{T}_{\Pi_{j+2}}\Delta_{j}\pi^{\bm{r}}f,\quad j\geq 2.\end{gathered}

Therefore, by Lemma
B.1
, and Proposition
A.1
, we obtain

\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

since all
L_{p}
-bounds can be chosen uniformly for all
j
due to the assumption on
\bm{r}
(
B.7
).
The proposition is proved.
∎

Corollary B.4
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, put
\bm{r}(j)=sj
.
Then

H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)=H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)

whose norm is given by

\|f\|_{H_{p}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}:=\|(1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Proof.

Recall the notation

\pi^{\bm{r}}:=S_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}.

By Proposition
B.3
, it follows that
\|f\|_{H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}
.
Thus it is sufficient to show

\displaystyle\|\pi^{\bm{r}}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\approx\|(1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

Let
\Psi
be the Schwartz function appearing in the definition of
\Delta_{j}
.
Define

\mathcal{F}[\Phi](\xi)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi).

and

\Pi(\xi):=\frac{\mathcal{F}[\Phi](\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{sj}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}}.

Then
\Pi
is a weighted Mikhlin multiplier in Proposition
A.1
.
Thus we have

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{\bm{r}(j)}\Delta_{j}f\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}=\Big\|\mathbb{T}_{\Pi}\Bigl((1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\Bigr)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|(1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

For the converse direction, it suffices to observe that

\widetilde{\Pi}(\xi)=\frac{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}}{\mathcal{F}[\Phi](\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{sj}\mathcal{F}[\Psi](2^{-j}\xi)}

becomes a weighted Mikhlin multiplier as well. The corollary is proved.
∎

Definition B.5
.

Let
q\in(0,\infty)
,
\bm{r}:\mathbb{Z}\to(-\infty,\infty)
, and
B
be a Banach lattice.

(i)

We denote by
B(\ell_{2}^{\bm{r}})
the set of all
B
-valued sequences
x=(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots)
such that

\|x\|_{B(\ell_{2}^{\bm{r}})}:=\|x_{0}\|_{B}+\left\|\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{2\bm{r}(j)}|x_{j}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\right\|_{B}<\infty,\quad|x|:=x\vee(-x).

(ii)

We denote by
\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}(B)
the set of all
B
-valued sequences
x=(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots)
such that

\|x\|_{\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}(B)}:=\|x_{0}\|_{B}+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{q\bm{r}(j)}\|x_{j}\|_{B}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}<\infty.

In particular, we use the simpler notation
\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}=\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R})
.

Definition B.6
.

Let
X
and
Y
be quasi-Banach spaces. We say that
X
is a retract of
Y
if there exist linear transformation
\mathfrak{I}:X\to Y
and
\mathfrak{P}:Y\to X
such that
\mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{I}
is an identity operator in
X
.

Lemma B.7
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty)
, and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Suppose that

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\bm{r}(j+1)-\bm{r}(j)|=:C_{0}<\infty.

(B.14)

(i)

Then
H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
is a retract of
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)(\ell_{2}^{\bm{r}})
.

(ii)

Then
B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
is a retract of
\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}(L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))
.

Proof.

We suggest a unified method to prove both (i) and (ii) simultaneously.
Let

(X,Y)=\left(H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)(\ell_{2}^{\bm{r}})\right)

or

(X,Y)=\left(B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}(L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))\right).

Consider two mappings:

f\mapsto\mathfrak{I}(f):=(S_{0}f,\Delta_{1}f,\Delta_{2}f,\cdots)

and

\bm{f}=(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots)\mapsto\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f}):=S_{0}(f_{0}+f_{1})+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=-1}^{1}\Delta_{j}f_{j+l}.

By using the fact that
f=S_{0}(f)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{j}f
,
it is each to check that
\mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{I}
is an identity operator in
X
. Moreover,
due to the definitions of the function spaces,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{X}=\|\mathfrak{I}(f)\|_{Y}.

(B.15)

In other words, the target space of the mapping
f\mapsto\mathfrak{I}(f)
is
Y

and this implies that
\mathfrak{I}
is a linear transformation from
X
to
Y
since the linearity of the mapping is obvious.

Now, it only remains to show that
\mathfrak{P}
is a linear transformation from
Y
to
X
.
Since the linearity is trivial as before, it is sufficient to show that the target space of the mapping

\bm{f}=(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots)\mapsto\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})
is
X
.
By the almost orthogonality of the Littlewood-Paley projections,

\begin{gathered}S_{0}\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})=S_{0}f_{0}+S_{0}f_{1}+S_{0}\Delta_{1}f_{2},\\
\Delta_{1}\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})=\Delta_{1}(S_{0}+\Delta_{1})f_{0}+\Delta_{1}f_{1}+\Delta_{1}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})f_{2}+\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}f_{3},\end{gathered}

(B.16)

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})

(B.17)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{i}\Delta_{i-1}f_{i-2}+\Delta_{i}(\Delta_{i-1}+\Delta_{i})f_{i-1}+\Delta_{i}f_{i}+\Delta_{i}(\Delta_{i}+\Delta_{i+1})f_{i+1}+\Delta_{i}\Delta_{i+1}f_{i+2}

\displaystyle=:T_{i}^{1}f_{i-2}+T_{i}^{2}f_{i-1}+T_{i}^{3}f_{i}+T_{i}^{4}f_{i+1}+T_{i}^{5}f_{i+2},\quad i\geq 2.

The remaining part of the proof becomes slightly different depending on
Y=L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)(\ell_{2}^{\bm{r}})
or
Y=\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}(L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))
.

(i)

Due to (
B.16
),

\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}2^{2\bm{r}(i)}|\Delta_{i}\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})|^{2}\leq N\sum_{k=1}^{5}\mathcal{I}_{k},

where

\mathcal{I}_{k}=\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}|T_{i}^{k}(2^{\bm{r}(i)}f_{i+k-3})|^{2},\quad k=1,2,3,4,5.

By Lemma
B.1
, the sequence of linear operators
\{T_{j}^{k}\}_{j=2}^{\infty}
satisfies (
B.5
) for all
k=1,2,3,4,5
. Using Remark
B.2
and (
B.14
), we have

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}2^{2\bm{r}(i)}|\Delta_{i}\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|\bm{f}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)(\ell_{2}^{\bm{r}})}.

This implies that
\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})\in H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
.

(ii)

By Lemma
B.1
,

\displaystyle\|S_{0}\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\sum_{j=0}^{2}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

(B.18)

and

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{i}\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\sum_{j=-2}^{2}\|f_{i+j}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)},\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{N},

(B.19)

where
N
is independent of
i
and
f_{i}=0
if
i<0
.
Moreover, due to (
B.14
),

2^{\bm{r}(i)}\|\Delta_{i}\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\sum_{j=-2}^{2}2^{\bm{r}(i+j)}\|f_{i+j}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}

(B.20)

and the constant
N
is independent of
i
.
Finally, recalling the definition of
B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
and combining all (
B.18
), (
B.19
), and (
B.20
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})\|_{B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq N\|\bm{f}\|_{\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}(L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))},\quad q\in(0,\infty),

where
N
is independent of
\bm{f}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{P}
is a linear transformation from
Y
to
X
.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Proposition B.8
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\bm{r}:\mathbb{Z}\to(-\infty,\infty)
be a sequence satisfying

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\bm{r}(j+1)-\bm{r}(j)|<\infty.

(i)

The space
X
is a quasi-Banach space

(ii)

The closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under the quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
is
X
,

where the space
X
indicates
H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
or
B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)
.

Proof.

We put

(X,Y)=\left(H_{p}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x)(\ell_{2}^{\bm{r}})\right)

or

(X,Y)=\left(B_{p,q}^{\bm{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x),\ell_{q}^{\bm{r}}(L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w\,\mathrm{d}x))\right)

as in the previous lemma.

(i)

All properties of a quasi-Banach space is obvious except completeness.
Thus we only prove the completeness. Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a Cauchy sequence in
X
.
It is obvious that
Y
is a Banach space due to the completeness of
L_{p}
-spaces with general measures.
Therefore, using the equivalence of the norms in (
B.15
), one can find a
\bm{f}\in Y
such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}(f_{n})=(S_{0}f_{n},\Delta_{1}f_{n},\cdots)\to\bm{f}\quad\text{ in }Y.

By Lemma
B.7
,
\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})\in X
and

\|f_{n}-\mathfrak{P}(\bm{f})\|_{X}\leq N\|\mathfrak{I}(f_{n})-\bm{f}\|_{Y}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. Therefore,
X
is complete.

(ii)

With the help of Proposition
B.3
, it is sufficient to consider the case that the order is
\bm{0}
.
Then
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is dense in
X
due to
[
45
, Theorem 2.4]
.
Finally, based on the typical truncation and diagonal arguments, the result is obtained.

The proposition is proved.
∎

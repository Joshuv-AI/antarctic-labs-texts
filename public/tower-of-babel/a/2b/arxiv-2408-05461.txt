Dynamical behaviour of a Soap Film Bridge driven by an Electrostatic Force
Lina Sophie Schmitz

Abstract
. We study a free boundary problem describing a tubular soap film bridge driven by an electrostatic force. To realize the electrostatic force, the soap film bridge is placed inside an outer metal cylinder and a voltage difference between this cylinder and the soap film is applied. Our model consists of a quasilinear parabolic equation for the evolution of the film coupled with an elliptic equation for the electrostatic potential in the unknown domain between outer cylinder and soap film bridge. For the rotationally symmetric case, we prove local well-posedness of this free boundary problem by recasting it as a single quasilinear parabolic equation with a non-local source term. Moreover, for large applied voltages, we show that solutions do not exist globally for a wide class of initial values.

1.2.
Main Results and Strategy

We describe our results on existence and non-existence of local and global solutions. The first result reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Local Well-Posedness)

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
,
\lambda\geq 0
,
\sigma>0
and
u_{0}\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
with
1>u_{0}(z)>-1
for
z\in(-1,1)
. Then, there exists a unique maximal solution
(u,\psi_{u})
to the coupled free boundary problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
)
on the maximal interval of existence
\big[0,T_{max}\big)
where

in the sense that

\displaystyle u\in C^{1}\big([0,T_{max}),L_{q}(-1,1)\big)\cap C\big([0,T_{max}),W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\big)

solves (
1.1
) and
\psi_{u(t)}\in W^{2}_{2}\big(\Omega(u(t))\big)
solves (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) for each
t\in[0,T_{max})
.

Here, functions in
W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
satisfy a Dirichlet boundary condition and we use the notation
\psi_{u}
instead of
\psi
, which reason is explained below. To prove Theorem
1.1
we adapt
[
8
]
with smaller changes according to
[
19
]
. Our proof is based on semigroup theory (more precisely: its time-dependent counterpart
[
3
]
) and Banach’s fixed point theorem, and strongly relies on the quasilinear theory
[
2
,
3
]
. To apply this theory, we want to recast the free boundary problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) as a single parabolic equation for the film deflection
u
only,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\sigma\,\partial_{z}\mathrm{arctan}(\sigma\partial_{z}u)&=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{u+1}+\lambda\,g(u)\,,\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad u(t,\pm 1)&=0\,,\qquad-1<u<1\,,\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad u(0,z)&=u_{0}\,,\qquad z\in(-1,1)\,,\end{cases}

with
-1<u_{0}<1
and electrostatic force

(1.4)

\displaystyle g(u):=(1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}u)^{2})^{3/2}\,\big|\partial_{r}\psi_{u}(z,u+1)\big|^{2}\,.

This single parabolic equation is exactly equivalent to equation (
1.1
). The difference is now that we view the electrostatic force as a non-local map
\left[u\mapsto g(u)\right]
between suitable function spaces. More precisely, for fixed time, this map should first take the function
u
to the electrostatic potential
\psi_{u}
, which solves the elliptic equation (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) on the
u
-dependent domain
\Omega(u)
, and then do further manipulations with
\psi_{u}
resulting in (
1.4
). Note that the electrostatic potential occurs no longer as an equal unknown which justifies the notation
\psi_{u}
. Of course, it is not clear on which function spaces the map
[u\mapsto g(u)]
is meaningful, and indeed our main work to prove Theorem
1.1
is to establish Lipschitz continuity of
g
between fractional Sobolev spaces. To this end, we rely on elliptic regularity for non-smooth convex domains from
[
18
]
for which we include a detailed proof in the Appendix
6
. With Lipschitz continuity of
g
at hand, Theorem
1.1
follows from a refinement of a fixed point argument based on
[
2
,
3
]
.

Remark

We point out that the regularity of
[u\mapsto g(u)]
, which we provide in Section
3
, will be slightly different to the one usually required for local well-posedness results in the quasilinear theory, see for example
[
2
, Theorem 12.1]
or also
[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
. This is due to the corners of
\Omega(u)
and it is the reason for performing the whole fixed point argument by hand.

To state our second result precisely, we require the set

(1.5)

\displaystyle S(\kappa):=\Big\{v\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\,\Big|\,\|v\|_{W^{2}_{q}(-1,1)}\leq 1/\kappa\,,\ -1+\kappa\leq v(z)\leq 1-\kappa\Big\}\,,

for
\kappa>0
and fixed
q>2
, which consists of film deflections which neither pinch-off nor touch the outer metal cylinder.

Corollary 1.2

(Global Existence Criterion)

If for each
\tau>0
, there exists
\kappa(\tau)\in(0,1)
such that the unique maximal solution
u
from Theorem
1.1
satisfies
u(t)\in S(\kappa(\tau))
for all
t\in[0,T_{max})\cap[0,\tau]
, then
u
exists globally, that is
T_{max}=\infty
.

Corollary
1.2
implies that if

, then the soap film pinches-off, touches the outer metal cylinder or the

-norm of

blows up. While the first two cases possess a direct physical interpretation, the norm blow-up corresponds more likely to a limitation of the model.

Finally, as our third contribution, we study the dynamical behaviour of the film deflection
u
for large applied voltages
\lambda
. In this case, a dominance of the electrostatic force is expected resulting in non-existence of global solutions. Indeed, we can prove this non-existence under the additional assumption that
\sigma
is not too small which a-priori excludes the possibility of pinch-off for many initial film deflections as we shall see below.
More precisely, we need to know that the stationary version of (
1.1
) with
\lambda=0
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&-\sigma\partial_{z}\mathrm{arctan}(\sigma\partial_{z}u)=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{u+1}\,,\\
&u(\pm 1)=0\,,\quad-1<u<1\,,\end{cases}

possesses at least one solution. Since (
1.6
) is the well-known minimal surface equation for a surface of revolution, it can be found in many textbooks, see for example
[

, p. 282]
, that there exists a threshold value
\sigma_{crit}\approx 1.2
such that (

) has at least one solution for
\sigma\geq\sigma_{crit}
and no solution for
\sigma<\sigma_{crit}
.

Moreover, all stationary solutions have the shape of a (translated) catenoid, i.e.

(1.7)

\displaystyle u_{cat}(z):=\frac{\mathrm{cosh}(cz)}{\mathrm{cosh}(c)\ }-1\,,\qquad z\in(-1,1)\,,

for
c>0
with
\sigma=\frac{\mathrm{cosh}(c)}{c}
, and we may always assume, for fixed
\sigma\geq\sigma_{crit}
, that we have picked the smallest of such catenoids.

Theorem 1.3

(Non-Existence of Global Solutions)

Let
\sigma\geq\sigma_{crit}
. There exists
\lambda_{crit}(\sigma)>0
such that for each
\lambda>\lambda_{crit}
and each initial condition
u_{0}\geq u_{cat}
, the corresponding solution
(u,\psi_{u})
to
(
1.1
)-(
1.3
) has a finite maximal time of existence
T_{max}(u_{0})<\infty
.

To outline the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we fix
\sigma\geq\sigma_{crit}
and a solution

to (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) with initial value

. Then, the parabolic comparison principle guarantees that

implies

, so that pinch-off of the soap film bridge is excluded. This makes it possible to consider the functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t):=-\int_{-1}^{1}\ln\big(u(t,z)+1\big)\,\mathrm{d}z\,,\qquad t\in[0,T_{max})\,,\qquad T_{max}=T_{max}(u_{0})\,,

while we aim at showing

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq-C<0\,,\qquad t\in[0,T_{max})\,,

for

The question of non-existence of global solutions
for large

. In particular, a related result is contained in

Recall that in our case the right-hand side of (

will be controlled by the restriction

3.
Elliptic Subproblem

In this section, we prove Lipschitz continuity of the map
[v\mapsto g(v)]
, where
g(v)
denotes the electrostatic force from (
1.4
), and
v\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
with
q>2
and
-1<v(z)<1
is a time-independent film deflection. The key step in the analysis of
[v\mapsto g(v)]
is the investigation of
[v\mapsto\psi_{v}]
with
\psi_{v}
being the solution to the elliptic subproblem (
1.2
)-(
1.3
).

As in
[
20
]
, we note that this elliptic equation has a unique weak solution
\psi_{v}\in W^{1}_{2}\big(\Omega(v)\big)
by Lax-Milgram Theorem, but that this regularity is not sufficient to define the electrostatic force
g(v)
as it contains the square of the trace of the derivative of
\psi_{v}
. In addition, the Lax-Milgram Theorem provides no information on the dependency of
\psi_{v}
on
v
.
To make the dependency of
\psi_{v}
on
v
accessible, we transform the domain
\Omega(v)
, on whose closure
\psi_{v}
is defined, to an
v
-independent reference domain. More precisely, for a given film deflection
v\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
with
-1>v(z)>1
and

, we transform the domain
\Omega(v)
to the fixed rectangle

\Omega=(-1,1)\times\big(1,2)

via
T_{v}:\overline{\Omega(v)}\rightarrow\overline{\Omega}
defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle T_{v}(z,r):=\left(z,\frac{r-2v(z)}{1-v(z)}\right)\,,\qquad(z,r)\in\overline{\Omega(v)}\,.

Due to the chain rule as well as transformation results for Sobolev functions
[

, Lemma 2.3.2]
, we get that the electrostatic potential
\psi_{v}
solves (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) weakly or strongly on

if and only if

is a weak or strong solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L_{v}\phi_{v}&=0\qquad\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\ \ \ \,\phi_{v}&=\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{ln}(r)}{\mathrm{ln}(2)}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,,\end{cases}

where the transformed

is given by

\displaystyle L_{v}w

\displaystyle:=\sigma^{2}(1-v)\partial_{z}^{2}w-2\sigma^{2}\,\partial_{z}v\,(2-r)\partial_{r}\partial_{z}w

\displaystyle\quad\ \ +\frac{1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}v)^{2}(2-r)^{2}}{1-v}\,\partial_{r}^{2}w

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad\ \ +\left[-\sigma^{2}(2-r)\Big(\partial_{z}^{2}v+\frac{2(\partial_{z}v)^{2}}{1-v}\Big)+\frac{1}{2v+(1-v)r}\right]\partial_{r}w\,.

In divergence form this operator reads

(3.4)

\displaystyle L_{v}w=\mathrm{div}\left(A(v)\nabla w\right)+d(v)\cdot\nabla w

with

\displaystyle A(v)=[a_{ij}(v)]_{i,j=1}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{2}(1-v)&-\sigma^{2}\,\partial_{z}v\,(2-r)\\
-\sigma^{2}\,\partial_{z}v\,(2-r)&\displaystyle\frac{1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}v)^{2}(2-r)^{2}}{1-v}\end{pmatrix}\,,

\displaystyle d(v)=\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}(v)\\
d_{2}(v)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2v+(1-v)r}\end{pmatrix}\,.

To prevent the operator

3.1.
Solution Theory

The aim of this subsection is threefold: We present the weak and strong solution theory for the problem

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L_{v}\Phi_{v}&=F\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\ \ \ \Phi_{v}&=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\end{cases}

with

in

or

respectively, which is closely related to the transformed problem (

),
we derive a-priori estimates for

holding uniformly on

Before we start, let us note that the transformed operator

Lemma 3.1

There exists a constant
\alpha=\alpha(\kappa)>0
such that

\displaystyle\alpha|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi^{T}A(v)\xi\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}|\xi|^{2}\,,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,,\quad(z,r)\in\Omega\,,\quad v\in S(\kappa)\,.

Proof

The real eigenvalues

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\big(A(v)\big)=\mu_{+}+\mu_{-}\,,\qquad\det\big(A(v)\big)=\mu_{+}\mu_{-}.

Computing the expressions for the trace and the determinant explicitly, we easily see that there exists

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha(\kappa)}\geq\mathrm{tr}\big(A(v)\big)\geq\mu_{+}\geq\mu_{-}\geq\frac{\det\big(A(v)\big)}{\mathrm{tr}\big(A(v)\big)}\geq\alpha(\kappa)>0

for all

∎

Weak Solutions.

We consider weak solutions to (
3.5
) and corresponding a-priori estimates.
Though existence and uniqueness results for solutions are usually supplemented by a-priori estimates, see
[
12
, Corollary 8.7, Lemma 9.17]
and
[
10
, Theorem 6.2.6]
, we have to repeat the arguments to include the
v
-dependency.

Lemma 3.2

For each
v\in S(\kappa)
and each
F\in W^{-1}_{2,D}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique weak solution
\Phi_{v}\in W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega)
to (
3.5
), i.e. to the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L_{v}\Phi_{v}&=F\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\ \ \ \Phi_{v}&=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, there exists
C_{1}(\kappa)>0
(independent of
F
,
\Phi_{v}
and
v
) such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{v}\|_{W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}(\kappa)\|F\|_{W^{-1}_{2,D}(\Omega)}\,.

Proof
.
The existence of a unique weak solution to problem (
3.5
) is a consequence of
[
12
, Theorem 8.3]
. So it remains to prove (
3.6
):

(i)
As a first step, we show the existence of
C(\kappa)>0
with

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{v}\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\,\big(\|\Phi_{v}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{W_{2,D}^{-1}(\Omega)}\big)

for each
v\in S(\kappa)
and
F\in W^{-1}_{2,D}(\Omega)
. To this end, we test the weak formulation of (
3.5
) with
\Phi=\Phi_{v}
resulting in

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Phi^{T}A(v)\nabla\Phi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)=\int_{\Omega}\big(d(v)\cdot\nabla\Phi\big)\Phi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)-\langle F,\Phi\rangle_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Combining now the uniform ellipticity of
-L_{v}
with
v
-independent ellipticity constant
\alpha(\kappa)>0
from Lemma
3.1
with Friedrich’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\left(\Big|\int_{\Omega}\big(d(v)\cdot\nabla\Phi\big)\Phi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)\Big|+\|F\|_{W_{2,D}^{-1}(\Omega)}\|\Phi\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\right)

for some
C(\kappa)>0
. Finally, the fact that
\|d(v)\|_{\infty}
is uniformly bounded on
S(\kappa)
together with Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\big(\|\Phi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|F\|_{W_{2,D}^{-1}(\Omega)}\|\Phi\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\big)

for some new
C(\kappa)>0
, which is obviously equivalent to (
3.7
).

(ii)
Next, the
L_{2}
-norm of
\Phi_{v}
on the right-hand side of (
3.7
) has to be eliminated. However, for this we can proceed by contradiction as in
[
12
, Lemma 9.17]
, and we refer to
[
35
, Lemma 3.2]
for details.
∎

Regularity Step: Strong Solutions.
We establish that
\Phi_{v}
is a strong solution to (
3.5
) if the right-hand side
F
is more regular. Since
\Omega
is a rectangle, i.e. a domain with corners, this result does not follow from standard elliptic regularity theory,
but from Theorem
6.1
.

Lemma 3.3

For each
v\in S(\kappa)
and each
F\in L_{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique strong solution
\Phi_{v}\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega)
to (
3.5
), i.e to the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L_{v}\Phi_{v}&=F\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\ \ \ \Phi_{v}&=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, there exists
C_{2}(\kappa)>0
(independent of
F
,
\Phi_{v}
and
v
) such that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{v}\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}(\kappa)\|F\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Proof
. By Lemma
3.2
, we find
\Phi_{v}\in W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega)
being the unique weak solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathrm{div}\big(A(v)\nabla w\big)&=F-d(v)\cdot\nabla\Phi_{v}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\ \,w&=0\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega\,.\end{cases}

From the facts that
W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)
is a Banach algebra and
W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)\hookrightarrow C([-1,1])
, we deduce that the coefficients of
-L_{v}
satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\|a_{ij}(v)\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|d_{i}(v)\|_{\infty}\leq C(\kappa)\,,\qquad v\in S(\kappa)\,.

Because the ellipticity constant
\alpha(\kappa)>0
of
-L_{v}
is independent of
v\in S(\kappa)
, see Lemma
3.1
, we deduce from Theorem
6.1

that
\Phi_{v}
belongs to
W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega)
and that there exists
C(\kappa)>0
with

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{v}\|_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\kappa)\big(\|\Phi_{v}\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\big)\,.

Since
\|F\|_{W^{-1}_{2,D}(\Omega)}\leq\|F\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}
, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{v}\|_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}(\kappa)\|F\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,

for some
C_{2}(\kappa)>0
, and the proof is complete.
∎

In summary, the previous two Lemmata
3.2
and
3.3
ensure unique weak and strong solvability of (
3.5
). More precisely, for
v\in S(\kappa)
, the operator

(3.8)

\displaystyle L_{D}(v)\Phi:=L_{v}\Phi\,,\qquad\Phi\in W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega)

satisfies

L_{D}(v)\in\mathcal{L}_{is}(W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega),W^{-1}_{2,D}(\Omega))\cap\mathcal{L}_{is}(W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega),L_{2}(\Omega)),

and its inverse
L_{D}(v)^{-1}
is uniformly bounded for
v\in S(\kappa)
.

From a solution to (
3.5
) one easily obtains a solution to the transformed electrostatic problem:
Noting that
f_{v}:=L_{v}\frac{\ln(r)}{\ln(2)}
belongs to
L_{2}(\Omega)
one finds that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\phi_{v}:=-L_{D}(v)^{-1}f_{v}+\frac{\ln(r)}{\ln(2)}\in W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)

is the unique strong solution to the transformed electrostatic problem (
3.2
). Thanks to

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|f_{v}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\kappa)\,,\qquad v\in S(\kappa)\,,

and the uniform estimates on
L_{D}(v)^{-1}
, the function
\phi_{v}
also satisfies a uniform estimate

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{v}\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\kappa)\,,\qquad v\in S(\kappa)\,.

Remark 3.4

We briefly comment on the regularity of the original electrostatic potential
\psi_{v}=\phi_{v}\circ T_{v}
solving

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r}\psi_{v})+\partial_{z}^{2}\psi_{v}&=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega(v)\,,\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\psi_{v}&=h_{v}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega(v)\,,\end{cases}

where we set
\sigma=1
in this remark, and
h_{v}
is given by (
1.3
).
Due to the corners of
\Omega(v)
, one might expect the regularity
\psi_{v}\in W^{2}_{2}(\Omega(v))\cap C^{\infty}\big(\overline{\Omega(v)}\setminus\{(\pm 1,1),(\pm 1,2)\}\big)
to be optimal in general. However, one can show that
\psi_{v}
is smooth up to the
boundary in
(\pm 1,2)
. In addition, if
v\in W^{3}_{\infty}(-1,1)
with
v(\pm 1)=v_{z}(\pm 1)=v_{zz}(\pm 1)=0
, then
\psi_{v}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega(v)})
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, i.e.
\psi_{v}
is a classical solution. This follows from the Schwarz reflection principle
[
12
, Exercise 2.4]
and Schauder Theory, see
[
12
, Lemma 6.18]
.

\displaystyle\|L_{D}(v)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(W_{2,D}^{\theta-1}(\Omega),W_{2,D}^{\theta+1}(\Omega))}\leq C_{3}(\kappa)\,,\qquad v\in S(\kappa)\,.

.

3.2.
Regularity of the Electrostatic Force

In this subsection, we prove Lipschitz continuity and analyticity of the electrostatic force
[v\mapsto g(v)]
for which we adapt
[
8
]
.

For convenience, we recall the notation

\displaystyle S(\kappa)=\Big\{v\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\,\Big|\,\|v\|_{W^{2}_{q}(-1,1)}\leq 1/\kappa\,,\ -1+\kappa\leq v(z)\leq 1-\kappa\Big\}

for
\kappa>0
and
q>2
while
q^{\prime}
denotes the dual exponent of
q
.

The desired Lipschitz continuity is proven in several steps. First, we derive continuity properties of
[v\mapsto L_{v}]
where
L_{v}
is defined in (
3.3
). Subsequently, we establish continuity of
[v\mapsto\phi_{v}]
, and finally, we transfer the continuity properties to
[v\mapsto g(v)]
.
The regularity of
[v\mapsto L_{v}]
follows as in
[
8
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Lemma 3.6

Given
\xi\in[0,1/q^{\prime})
and
\alpha\in(\xi,1)
, there exists
C_{4}(\kappa)
such that

\displaystyle\|L_{v}-L_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2}_{2}(\Omega),W_{2,D}^{-\alpha}(\Omega))}\leq C_{4}(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}

for all
v,w\in S(\kappa)
.

Proof
.
Let
v,w\in S(\kappa)
and
\Phi\in W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)
. Then,

L_{v}\Phi\in L_{2}(\Omega)\subset W_{2,D}^{-\alpha}(\Omega)
where the critical term
-\sigma^{2}(2-r)\,\partial_{z}^{2}v\,\partial_{r}\Phi
of
L_{v}\Phi
belongs to
L_{2}(\Omega)
thanks to Hölder’s inequality and the embedding
W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L_{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(\Omega)
.
For
\psi\in W^{\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)
, the definition of
L_{v}
in non-divergence form yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big[(L_{v}-L_{w})\Phi\big]\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}\int_{\Omega}[w-v]\,\partial_{z}^{2}\Phi\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)

\displaystyle\quad\ \ -2\sigma^{2}\int_{\Omega}(2-r)\,[\partial_{z}v-\partial_{z}w]\,\partial_{z}\partial_{r}\Phi\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)

\displaystyle\quad\ \ +\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}v)^{2}(2-r)^{2}}{1-v}-\frac{1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}w)^{2}(2-r)^{2}}{1-w}\bigg)\,\partial_{r}^{2}\Phi\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)

\displaystyle\quad\ \ -\sigma^{2}\int_{\Omega}(2-r)\,[\partial_{z}^{2}v-\partial_{z}^{2}w]\,\partial_{r}\Phi\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)

\displaystyle\quad\ \ -2\sigma^{2}\int_{\Omega}(2-r)\,\bigg(\frac{(\partial_{z}v)^{2}}{1-v}-\frac{(\partial_{z}w)^{2}}{1-w}\bigg)\,\partial_{r}\Phi\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)

\displaystyle\quad\ \ +\int_{\Omega}\bigg(\frac{1}{2v+(1-v)r}-\frac{1}{2w+(1-w)r}\bigg)\,\partial_{r}\Phi\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)

\displaystyle=:I+II+\dots+VI\,.

We point out that
IV
is the critical term since it contains the already mentioned second weak derivatives of
v
(and
w
). Therefore, we will only treat
IV
in detail. The other five terms can be estimated by using Hölder’s inequality, the embedding
W^{2-\xi}_{q}(-1,1)\hookrightarrow C^{1}([-1,1])
as well as the definition of
S(\kappa)
, which results in

\displaystyle|I|+|II|+|III|

\displaystyle+|V|+|VI|

\displaystyle\leq C\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\|\Phi\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{W^{\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)}\,.

For
IV
, a simple application of Hölder’s inequality combined with Sobolev’s embedding theorem only yields existence of the integral but not the desired estimate. Instead, we argue as follows: Due to Fubini
’s Theorem and the fact that
\partial_{z}^{2}\in\mathcal{\mathcal{L}}\big(W^{2-\xi}_{q}(-1,1),W^{-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)\big)
by
[
14
, Theorem 1.4.4.6]
(as
1-\xi\neq 1/q
), we find

\displaystyle|IV|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}\bigg|\int_{\Omega}(2-r)\,[\partial_{z}^{2}v-\partial_{z}^{2}w]\,\partial_{r}\Phi\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)\ \bigg|

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}\bigg|\int_{-1}^{1}[\partial_{z}^{2}v-\partial_{z}^{2}w](z)\bigg(\int_{1}^{2}(2-r)\partial_{r}\Phi(z,r)\psi(z,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}\|\partial_{z}^{2}v-\partial_{z}^{2}w\|_{W^{-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)}\bigg\|\int_{1}^{2}(2-r)\partial_{r}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,r)\psi(\,\cdot\,,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg\|_{W^{\xi}_{q^{\prime}}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\sigma^{2}\|v-w\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q}(-1,1)}\bigg\|\int_{1}^{2}(2-r)\partial_{r}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,r)\psi(\,\cdot\,,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg\|_{W^{\xi}_{q^{\prime}}(-1,1)}\,.

Here, we also used the fact that
W_{q^{\prime},D}^{\xi}(-1,1)=W^{\xi}_{q^{\prime}}(-1,1)
due to the choice
\xi<1/q^{\prime}
so that the dual space of
W^{\xi}_{q^{\prime}}(-1,1)
coincides with
W^{-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)
.
Next, we apply the real interpolation method to obtain

\displaystyle\bigg\|\int_{1}^{2}(2-r)\partial_{r}\Phi(\,\cdot\,,r)\psi(\,\cdot\,,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg\|_{W^{\xi}_{q^{\prime}}(-1,1)}\leq C\big\|(2-r)\partial_{r}\Phi\,\psi\big\|_{W^{\xi}_{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\,,

see
[
35
, Lemma A.2]
for details, from which we deduce further that

\displaystyle|IV|\leq C\,\sigma^{2}\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\big\|(2-r)\partial_{r}\Phi\,\psi\big\|_{W^{\xi}_{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\,.

Finally, the Multiplication Theorem
2.1
ensures

\displaystyle W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)\cdot W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)\cdot W^{\alpha}_{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{\xi}_{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\,,

and we arrive at

\displaystyle|IV|

\displaystyle\leq C\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\|2-r\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\|\partial_{r}\Phi\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{W^{\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\|\Phi\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\|\psi\|_{W^{\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)}\,.

Summing up the estimates for
I
to
VI
, we have shown that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}\big[(L_{v}-L_{w})\Phi\big]\,\psi\,\mathrm{d}(z,r)\Big|\leq C_{4}(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\,\|\Phi\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\,\|\psi\|_{W^{\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)}\,.

Taking the supremum over
\psi\in W^{\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)
with
\|\psi\|_{W_{2,D}^{\alpha}(\Omega)}\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\big\|(L_{v}-L_{w})\Phi\big\|_{W^{-\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)}\leq C_{4}(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\|\Phi\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\,,

and thus

\displaystyle\|L_{v}-L_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2}_{2}(\Omega),W^{-\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega))}\leq C_{4}(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}

as claimed.
∎

Next, we study the dependence of
\phi_{v}
on
v
. The result is the analogue to
[
8
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Lemma 3.7

Let
\xi\in[0,1/q^{\prime})
and
\alpha\in(\xi,1)
with
\alpha\neq 1/2
be given. Then, there exists
C_{5}(\kappa)
such that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{v}-\phi_{w}\|_{W_{2,D}^{2-\alpha}(\Omega)}\leq C_{5}(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\,,\qquad v,w\in S(\kappa)\,.

Proof
.
Let us recall from (
3.9
) that

\displaystyle\phi_{v}=-L_{D}(v)^{-1}f_{v}+\frac{\ln(r)}{\ln(2)}\,,\qquad f_{v}=L_{v}\frac{\ln(r)}{\ln(2)}\,.

First, we deduce from Lemma
3.6
that

\displaystyle\|f_{v}-f_{w}\|_{W_{2,D}^{-\alpha}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{v}-L_{w}\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2}_{2}(\Omega),W_{2,D}^{-\alpha}(\Omega))}\bigg\|\frac{\ln(r)}{\ln(2)}\bigg\|_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\,.

Next, we write

\phi_{v}-\phi_{w}=-L_{D}(v)^{-1}(f_{v}-f_{w})+\big(L_{D}(w)^{-1}-L_{D}(v)^{-1}\big)f_{w}\,.

Then, a combination of (
3.12
) with Proposition
3.5
(for
\theta=1-\alpha\neq 1/2
and
\theta=1
) as well as Lemma
3.6
yields

\displaystyle\|\phi_{v}-\phi_{w}\|_{W_{2,D}^{2-\alpha}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{D}(v)^{-1}(f_{v}-f_{w})\|_{W_{2,D}^{2-\alpha}(\Omega)}+\|(L_{D}(v)^{-1}-L_{D}(w)^{-1})f_{w}\|_{W_{2,D}^{2-\alpha}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{D}(v)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(W_{2,D}^{-\alpha}(\Omega),W^{2-\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega))}\|f_{v}-f_{w}\|_{W^{-\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ +\|L_{D}(v)^{-1}(L_{w}-L_{v})L_{D}(w)^{-1}f_{w}\|_{W^{2-\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}+\|L_{D}(v)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{-\alpha}_{2,D},W^{2-\alpha}_{2,D}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ \times\|L_{w}-L_{v}\|_{\mathcal{L}(W_{2}^{2}(\Omega),W_{2,D}^{-\alpha}(\Omega))}\|L_{D}(w)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{2}(\Omega),W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega))}\|f_{w}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\big(1+\|f_{w}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\big)\,.

Finally, estimate (
3.10
) ensures that the
L_{2}
-norm of
f_{w}
is uniformly bounded on
S(\kappa)
, and the assertion follows.

∎

Having established continuity properties of
[v\mapsto\phi_{v}]
, we turn to the main issue of this section and provide Lipschitz continuity of the electrostatic force
[v\mapsto g(v)]
. The result is an adaptation of
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
:

Proposition 3.8

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
,
\kappa\in(0,1)
and
\lambda,\sigma>0
. For
\xi\in[0,1/2)
and
\nu\in[0,1/2-\xi)
, the map

[v\mapsto g(v)]\,:\quad S(\kappa)\rightarrow W^{\nu}_{2,D}(-1,1)

is bounded, and there exists a constant
C_{6}(\kappa)>0
such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|g(v)

\displaystyle-g(w)\|_{W_{2,D}^{\nu}(-1,1)}\leq\,C_{6}(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q,D}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}

as well as

(3.14)

\displaystyle\bigg\|\frac{1}{v+1}-\frac{1}{w+1}\bigg\|_{W_{2,D}^{\nu}(-1,1)}\leq C_{6}(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W_{q,D}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\,,\qquad v,w\in S(\kappa)\,.

Proof
.
(i)
As a first step, we express the electrostatic force

g(v)=\big(1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}v)^{2}\big)^{3/2}\,\big|\partial_{r}\psi_{v}\big(z,v+1\big)\big|^{2}

defined in (
1.4
) in terms of the transformed electrostatic potential
\phi_{v}
. To this end, we recall from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\psi_{v}(z,r)=\phi_{v}\big(T_{v}(z,r)\big)=\phi_{v}\Big(z,\,\frac{r-2v(z)}{1-v(z)}\Big)\,,\qquad(z,r)\in\Omega(v)\,,

and consequently

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\psi_{v}\big(z,v(z)+1\big)=\frac{\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(z,1)}{1-v(z)}\,,\qquad z\in(-1,1)\,.

This yields

(3.15)

\displaystyle g(v)=\big(1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}v)^{2}\big)^{3/2}\,\frac{|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)|^{2}}{(1-v)^{2}}\,,\qquad v\in S(\kappa)\,.

Moreover, as the second preliminary observation, we note that

(3.16)

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)\big\|_{W^{1/2}_{2}(-1,1)}\leq C(\kappa)\,,\qquad v\in S(\kappa)\,.

Indeed, since
\phi_{v}
belongs to
W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)
, the trace theorem
[
14
, Theorem 1.5.1.2]
yields

\|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}(-1,1)}\leq C\,\|\phi_{v}\|_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)}\,,\qquad v\in S(\kappa)\,,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
v
. In combination with the fact that
\phi_{v}
is uniformly bounded on
S(\kappa)
due to (
3.11
), estimate (
3.16
) then follows.

(ii)
We deduce from the representation of
g
in (
3.15
) and

\displaystyle W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)\cdot W^{1/2}_{2}(-1,1)\cdot W^{1/2}_{2}(-1,1)\hookrightarrow W^{\nu}_{2}(-1,1)\,,

due to the Multiplication Theorem
2.1
, that

\displaystyle\|g(v)\|_{W^{\nu}_{2,D}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\left\|\frac{\big(1+\sigma^{2}(\partial_{z}v)^{2}\big)^{3/2}}{(1-v)^{2}}\right\|_{W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)}\,\big\|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)\big\|_{W_{2}^{1/2}(-1,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)

for
v\in S(\kappa)
. Here, the last inequality follows from (
3.16
). Consequently,
g
maps
S(\kappa)
to
W^{\nu}_{2,D}(-1,1)
and is bounded.

(iii)
We present the main part of the proof. Namely, we derive the stated Lipschitz continuity of
g
based on (
3.15
). To this end, we write

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle g(v)-g(w)\|_{W^{\nu}_{2,D}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})^{3/2}}{(1-w)^{2}}\,\Big(|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)|^{2}-|\partial_{r}\phi_{w}(\,\cdot\,,1)|^{2}\Big)\right\|_{W_{2,D}^{\nu}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left\|(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})^{3/2}\,\left(\frac{1}{(1-w)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(1-v)^{2}}\right)\,|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)|^{2}\right\|_{W^{\nu}_{2,D}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\left\|\Big((1+\sigma^{2}v_{z}^{2})^{3/2}-(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})^{3/2}\Big)\,\frac{1}{(1-v)^{2}}\,|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)|^{2}\right\|_{W^{\nu}_{2,D}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle=:I+II+III\,,

and estimate each part separately:

For
I
:
We let
\alpha\in(\xi,1/2-\nu)
, and write

\displaystyle I=\left\|\frac{(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})^{3/2}}{(1-w)^{2}}\,\Big(\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)+\partial_{r}\phi_{w}(\,\cdot\,,1)\Big)\Big(\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)-\partial_{r}\phi_{w}(\,\cdot\,,1)\Big)\right\|_{W_{2,D}^{\nu}(-1,1)}\,.

From

\displaystyle W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)\cdot W^{1/2}_{2}(-1,1)\cdot W_{2}^{1/2-\alpha}(-1,1)\hookrightarrow W^{\nu}_{2}(-1,1),

which holds thanks to the Multiplication Theorem

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})^{3/2}}{(1-w)^{2}}\right\|_{W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)}\,\big\|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)+\partial_{r}\phi_{w}(\,\cdot\,,1)\big\|_{W^{1/2}_{2}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt\times\big\|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)-\partial_{r}\phi_{w}(\,\cdot\,,1)\big\|_{W^{1/2-\alpha}_{2}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\,\big\|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)-\partial_{r}\phi_{w}(\,\cdot\,,1)\big\|_{W^{1/2-\alpha}_{2}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\,\|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}-\partial_{r}\phi_{w}\|_{W^{1-\alpha}_{2}(\Omega)}\,,\qquad v,w\in S(\kappa)\,.

For

\displaystyle\leq\big\|(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})^{3/2}\big\|_{W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)}\,\left\|\frac{1}{(1-w)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(1-v)^{2}}\right\|_{W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)}\,\|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)\|^{2}_{W^{1/2}_{2}(-1,1)}

where we use that

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\,\|v-w\|_{W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)}

For

\displaystyle(1+\sigma^{2}v_{z}^{2})^{3/2}-(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})^{3/2}

\displaystyle\quad\ \ +(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})\left((1+\sigma^{2}v_{z}^{2})^{1/2}-(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{r(v,w)(v_{z}+w_{z})}{(1-v)^{2}}\,(v_{z}-w_{z})\,|\partial_{r}\phi_{v}(\,\cdot\,,1)|^{2}\right\|_{W^{\nu}_{2,D}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\|v_{z}-w_{z}\|_{W^{1-\xi}_{q}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\kappa)\|v-w\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q}(-1,1)}

using

\displaystyle W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)\cdot W^{1-\xi}_{q}(-1,1)\cdot W^{1/2}_{2}(-1,1)\cdot W^{1/2}_{2}(-1,1)\hookrightarrow W^{\nu}_{2,D}(-1,1)

due to the Multiplication Theorem
2.1
. .
Combining the estimates for

(iv)

4.
Coupled System

We turn to the proof of the first two main results, Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
, i.e. well-posedness as well as the global existence criterion for the coupled free boundary problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
). As already mentioned, we reinterpret the system (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) as the following single quasilinear parabolic equation for the film deflection

(4.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\sigma\partial_{z}\mathrm{arctan}(\sigma\partial_{z}u)=G(u)

with Lipschitz continuous and non-local right-hand side
[u\mapsto G(u)]
given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle G(u):=-\frac{1}{u+1}+\lambda g(u)\,.

Our proof follows
[
8
]
.

Before we start, we introduce some notations: Let
q\in(2,\infty)
and
\xi\in(0,1/q^{\prime})
where
q^{\prime}
denotes the dual exponent of
q
. For
\kappa\in(0,1)
, we put

\displaystyle Z(\kappa):=\big\{v\in W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)\,

\displaystyle\big|\,\|v\|_{W_{q}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}\leq 1/\kappa\,,\ -1+\kappa\leq v(z)\leq 1-\kappa\,\big\}

and define

\displaystyle B(v)w:=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(1+\sigma^{2}v_{z}^{2})}w_{zz}\,,\qquad w\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\,,

for
v\in Z(\kappa)
, where the connection between
[v\mapsto B(v)]
and (
4.1
) is given via

B(u)u=-\frac{\sigma^{2}u_{zz}}{(1+\sigma^{2}u_{z}^{2})}=-\sigma\partial_{z}\arctan(\sigma\partial_{z}u)\,,\qquad u\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\,,

which is the second order operator occuring on the left-hand side of (
4.1
).
Furthermore, the choice of
\xi
and Sobolev’s embedding theorem ensure that
-\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{2}}{(1+\sigma^{2}v_{z}^{2})}\in C\big([-1,1]\big)
so that each
B(v)
is uniformly elliptic.

In the next two lemmata, we establish properties of
[v\mapsto B(v)]
. More precisely, we show that
[v\mapsto B(v)]
is globally Lipschitz continuous on
Z(\kappa)
and that each
-B(v)
generates an analytic semigroup satisfying uniform estimates for
v\in Z(\kappa)
.

Lemma 4.1

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
,
\kappa\in(0,1)
and
\xi\in(0,1/q^{\prime})
.
Then, there exists a constant
l(\kappa)
such that

\displaystyle\|B(w)-B(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1),L_{q}(-1,1))}\leq l(\kappa)\|w-v\|_{W_{q,D}^{2-\xi}(-1,1)}

for
v,w\in Z(\kappa)
.

Proof
.
The statement follows from

\displaystyle\|B(w)

\displaystyle-B(v)\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1),L_{q}(-1,1))}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{2}\,\left\|\frac{1}{(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})}-\frac{1}{(1+\sigma^{2}v_{z}^{2})}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{4}\left\|\frac{1}{(1+\sigma^{2}w_{z}^{2})(1+\sigma^{2}v_{z}^{2})}\right\|_{\infty}\,\|w_{z}+v_{z}\|_{\infty}\,\|w_{z}-v_{z}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq l(\kappa)\|w-v\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)}\,,

where we made use of
Z(\kappa)
being continuously embedded and bounded in
C^{1}\big([-1,1]\big)
due to Sobolev’s embedding theorem.
∎

Lemma 4.2

Let
q\in(2,\infty)
,
\kappa\in(0,1)
and
\xi\in(0,1/q^{\prime})
. Moreover, let
\omega>0
be fixed.
Then, there is a constant
k:=k(\kappa)\geq 1
such that for each
v\in Z(\kappa)
one has

\displaystyle B(v)\in\mathcal{H}\big(W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1),L_{q}(-1,1),k,\omega\big)\,.

Proof
.
In
[
3
, Remark I.1.2.1 (a)]
a criterion for
B(v)
to belong to one of the quantitative versions of
\mathcal{H}\big(W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1),L_{q}(-1,1)\big)
, introduced in (
2.1
), is given which reads as follows:

Assume that there are constants
C_{i}(\kappa)>0
for
i=8,9
such that for all
v\in Z(\kappa)
one has:

(i)

\big\|B(v)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1),L_{q}(-1,1))}\leq C_{8}(\kappa)
,

(ii)

[\mathrm{Re}\mu\geq\omega]\in\rho(-B(v))
and

\displaystyle\big\|[\mu+B(v)]^{-1}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{q}(-1,1))}\leq\frac{C_{8}(\kappa)}{|\mu|}\,,\qquad\mathrm{Re}\mu\geq\omega\,,

(iii)

\big\|[\omega+B(v)]^{-1}\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(L_{q}(-1,1),W_{q,D}^{2}(-1,1))}\leq C_{9}(\kappa)
.

Then, the assertion of the lemma follows from
[
3
, Remark I.1.2.1 (a)]
.

Thus, we only have to check (i)–(iii). We first define
V:=(1+\sigma^{2}v_{z}^{2})/\sigma^{2}
so that
B(v)w=-1/V\,w_{zz}
. Then,

(4.3)

\displaystyle 1/\sigma^{2}\leq V\leq C_{10}(\kappa)\,,\qquad v\in Z(\kappa)\,,

and the differential operator
B(v)
satisfies

\displaystyle\big\|B(v)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1),L_{q}(-1,1))}\leq\sigma^{2}\,,\qquad v\in Z(\kappa)\,,

which is condition (i).

Next, we check condition (ii).
To this end, we note that
B(v)
is uniformly elliptic. Consequently, for
f\in L_{q}(-1,1)
, the equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}B(v)u&=f\,,\\
u(\pm 1)&=0\end{cases}

is uniquely solvable in
W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
with
B(v)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}\big(L_{q}(-1,1),W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\big)
due to
[
12
, Theorem 9.15, Lemma 9.17]
.
It follows from the Theorem of Rellich-Kondrachov that
B(v)^{-1}\in\mathcal{L}\big(L_{q}(-1,1)\big)
is compact, and
[
16
, Theorem 6.29]
implies that the spectrum
\sigma(-B(v))
consists only of eigenvalues.
Now we fix an eigenvalue
\mu
of
-B(v)
and a corresponding eigenfunction
\varphi\in W^{2}_{q,D}\big((-1,1),\mathbb{C}\big)
. Testing

\mu\varphi-\frac{1}{V}\partial_{z}^{2}\varphi=0

with
V\,\overline{\varphi}\in W^{2}_{q^{\prime},D}\big((-1,1),\mathbb{C}\big)
and using integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{-\int_{-1}^{1}|\partial_{z}\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z}{\int_{-1}^{1}V\,|\varphi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z}<0

so that

\displaystyle\big[\mathrm{Re}\mu>0]\subset\rho(-B(v))\,,\qquad v\in Z(\kappa)\,.

Next, let
u\in W_{q,D}^{2}(-1,1)
be the unique solution to

\displaystyle[\mu+B(v)]u=f\,,\qquad f\in L_{q}\big((-1,1),\mathbb{C}\big)\,,

for
\mu>0
. Testing this equation with
V\,|u|^{q-2}\overline{u}\in L_{q^{\prime}}\big((-1,1),\mathbb{C}\big)
yields – along the lines of the proof of
[
25
, Proposition 2.4.2]
– the resolvent estimate (ii).

Finally, we turn to condition (iii). For
v\in Z(\kappa)
and
u\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)
, we find

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{W^{1}_{q}(-1,1)}^{q}+C_{10}(\kappa)^{q}\big\|B(v)u\big\|_{L_{q}(-1,1)}^{q}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)}^{q}+C\,\|u\|_{L_{q}(-1,1)}^{q}+C_{10}(\kappa)^{q}\big\|[\omega+B(v)]u\big\|_{L_{q}(-1,1)}^{q}

thanks to (
4.3
), the triangle inequality and Ehrling’s lemma. Rearranging (
4.4
), we deduce from (ii) the existence of a constant
C_{9}(\kappa)>0
with

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)}\leq C_{9}(\kappa)\big\|[\omega+B(v)]u\big\|_{L_{q}(-1,1)}\,,\quad v\in Z(\kappa)\,,\quad u\in W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\,,

which is equivalent to condition (iii). Now everything is proven.

∎

If
v
now depends on

, then
-B(v)
generates a parabolic evolution operator (instead of an analytic semigroup), which satisfies regularity estimates holding uniformly on

. The corresponding result is

Proposition 4.3

[

Proof of Theorem

It suffices to show the existence of a unique local solution

(i)

Choice of a complete metric space

\kappa>0

[

(4.5)

\displaystyle\big\|U_{B(v)}(t,s)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1))}+\big(t-s)^{1-\mu}\big\|U_{B(v)}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(W^{2\mu}_{q,D}(-1,1),W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1))}\leq C_{11}(\kappa)

for each

and

\tilde{\kappa}:=\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{C_{11}(\kappa)}\leq\kappa

and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\tau}(\kappa,\tilde{\kappa}):=\bigg\{v:\,

\displaystyle[0,\tau]\rightarrow W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\,\bigg|\,

\displaystyle\|v(t)-v(s)\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)}\leq|t-s|^{\rho}\,,\ \ v(t)\in S(\tilde{\kappa})\cap Z(\kappa)\,,\ \ s,t\in[0,\tau]\bigg\}

(ii)

Definition of the map
\Lambda

G(v(t))=\frac{-1}{1+v(t)}+\lambda g(v)(t)\,,\qquad v\in\mathcal{V}_{\tau}(\kappa,\tilde{\kappa})\,,\qquad t\in[0,\tau]\,,

for the right-hand side of (

due to Corollary

\displaystyle\Lambda(v)(t):=U_{B(v)}(t,0)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}U_{B(v)}(t,s)G(v(s))\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\qquad t\in[0,\tau]\,,

defines for each
v\in\mathcal{V}_{\tau}(\kappa,\tilde{\kappa})
the unique solution

\Lambda(v)\in C^{1}\big([0,\tau],L_{q}(-1,1)\big)\cap C\big([0,\tau],W^{2}_{q,D}(-1,1)\big)

to the linear problem

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+B(v)u=G(v)\,,\qquad u(0)=u_{0}\,.

It remains to adjust
\tau\in(0,1]
such that the map
\Lambda
possesses further properties:

(iii)

\Lambda
is a self-mapping
: It follows from
[
3
, Theorem II.5.3.1]
(with

and

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(v)(t)

\displaystyle-\Lambda(v)(s)\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{12}(\kappa)\,|t-s|^{2\rho}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{W_{q,D}^{2-\xi+4\rho}(-1,1)}+\|G(v(t))\|_{L_{\infty}((0,t),L_{q}(-1,1))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{13}(\kappa)\left(\frac{1}{2\kappa}+C_{7}(\tilde{\kappa})\right)\,\tau^{\rho}\,|t-s|^{\rho}\qquad v\in\mathcal{V}_{\tau}(\kappa,\tilde{\kappa})\,,\ s,t\in[0,\tau]\,,

where we additionally used the choice of
\kappa
and Corollary
3.9

smaller, if necessary, we find, for arbitrary

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(v)(t)-\Lambda(v)(s)\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)}\leq|t-s|^{\rho}\,.

Next, the triangle inequality and (

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(v)(t)\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Lambda(v)(t)-\Lambda(v)(0)\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)}+\|u_{0}\|_{W^{2-\xi}_{q,D}(-1,1)}

while (

\displaystyle\Lambda(v)(t)\geq-1+2\kappa-C\,\tau^{\rho}\,.

Appendix: Elliptic Regularity on Convex Domains

The goal of this appendix is to give a new and detailed proof of an elliptic regularity result on bounded, convex domains from
[
18
, Theorem 3.10.1]
which has been used in Lemma
3.3
:

Theorem 6.1

Suppose that

•

\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is convex, open and bounded,

•

A\in\big[W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})\big]^{n\times n}
, where
q>n
, is symmetric,

•

there is
\alpha>0
with

\displaystyle\xi^{T}A(x)\xi\geq\alpha|\xi|^{2}\,,\qquad x\in\overline{\Omega}_{0}\,,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.

Then, for each
F\in L_{2}(\Omega_{0})
, the problem

(6.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\ -\mathrm{div}\big(A(x)\nabla\phi\big)&=F\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{0}\,,\\
\ \ \qquad\qquad\quad\phi&=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{0}\end{cases}

has a unique solution
\phi\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega_{0})
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C
depending only on
q
,
n
,
\Omega_{0}
, the
W^{1}_{q}
-norm of the coefficients of
A
and the ellipticity constant
\alpha
such that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C\,\|F\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,.

We note that, thanks to the Riesz Representation Theorem, problem (
6.1
) has a unique weak solution
\phi\in W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega_{0})
.

The difficult part is to improve its regularity because such an improvement of regularity is usually derived for bounded
C^{2}
-domains, see for example
[
10
, Theorem 6.3.4]
.
But convex domains as assumed in Theorem
6.1
are merely Lipschitz domains
[
14
, Corollary 1.2.2.3]
, for which, in general, an improvement of regularity may even fail.

In
[
14
, Theorem 3.2.1.2]
the special case
q=\infty
of Theorem
6.1
is proven. The argument is based on an approximation of
\Omega_{0}
from the inside by a sequence of smooth convex domains
(\Omega_{m})
on which the elliptic problem possesses a sequence of unique
W^{2}_{2}
-solutions
(\phi_{m})
.
Furthermore, using the convexity of the domains, a remarkable
W^{2}_{2}
-a-priori estimate for
\phi_{m}
independent of

is derived, which allows the author to extract a subsequence of
(\phi_{m})
converging to a
W^{2}_{2}
- solution to the original problem on
\Omega_{0}
. We derive an improved
W^{2}_{2}
-a-priori-estimate to combine the idea of domain approximation from
[
14
]
with an approximation of the coefficients of
A(\,\cdot\,)
to reduce the assumption from
q=\infty
to
q>n
and thus to give a new proof of Theorem
6.1
.

An important ingredient in our proof is Sobolev’s embedding theorem on convex domains as it allows to characterize the embedding constant solely by the volume and diameter of the domain.

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L_{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\|v\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\,,\qquad v\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)\,.

Proof

This is a special case of
[
27
]
.
∎

A direct consequence of Sobolev’s embedding theorem on convex domains is the following:

\delta>0
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
depending only on
q,n,

\delta
,
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
and
|\Omega|
such that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L_{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\delta\|v\|_{W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\,\|v\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
v\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof
.
For fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,q-n)
, we deduce from

\displaystyle 2<\frac{2q}{q-2}<\frac{2(q-\varepsilon)}{(q-\varepsilon)-2}

and Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{L_{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(\Omega)}\leq\|v\|^{2(1-\theta)}_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,\|v\|^{2\theta}_{L_{\frac{2(q-\varepsilon)}{(q-\varepsilon)-2}}(\Omega)}

for suitable
\theta=\theta(q,n)\in(0,1)
and all
v\in W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)
. Now an application of Proposition
6.2
together with the weighted Young’s inequality completes the proof of (
6.3
).
∎

We can now turn to the improved a-priori estimate on smooth convex domains (here that means:
\partial\Omega\in C^{2}
). The starting point is the following result from
[
14
]
:

Proposition 6.4

Let
\Omega
be a convex, bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a
C^{2}
-boundary, and
A
a symmetric
n\times n
-matrix with each eigenvalue larger than
\alpha>0
. Then, there exists a constant
C_{18}
depending only on the diameter of
\Omega
and
\alpha
such that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{18}\,\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,,\qquad\phi\in W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega)\,.

Proof
.
This follows from the first step in the proof of
[
14
, Lemma 3.1.3.2]
by carefully analysing the appearing constants.
∎

Next, we aim at extending the improved a-priori estimate (
6.4
) to elliptic operators with variable coefficients. Our result is a generalization of the a-priori estimate in
[
14
, Lemma 3.1.3.2, Theorem 3.1.3.1]
. As in
[
14
, Lemma 3.1.3.2]
, we treat variable coefficient operators locally as a perturbation of constant coefficient operators.
Our new ingredient is the Sobolev’s embedding theorem for convex domains. It allows us to formulate an a-priori estimate in which the constant does not depend on the
W^{1}_{\infty}
-norm of the coefficient matrix
A(\,\cdot\,)
as in
[
14
]
, but only on the
W^{1}_{q}
-norm of this matrix for
q>n
.

Proposition 6.5

Suppose that

•

\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is convex, open and bounded,

•

q>n
,

•

A\in\big[C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{0})\big]^{n\times n}
is symmetric,

•

there is
\alpha>0
with

\displaystyle\xi^{T}A(x)\xi\geq\alpha|\xi|^{2}\,,\qquad x\in\overline{\Omega}_{0}\,,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,.

Then, there exists a constant
C
depending only on the
W^{1}_{q}
-norm of the coefficients of
A
, the ellipticity constant
\alpha
and
\Omega_{0}
such that for each convex and open
\Omega\subset\Omega_{0}
with
C^{2}
-boundary and
|\Omega|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\Omega_{0}|
the estimate

(6.5)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,,\qquad\phi\in W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)\cap W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega)\,,

holds.

Proof
.
(i)

Local Estimate:

Near fixed
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}_{0}
, our first goal is to prove a local version of (
6.5
). To this end, we interpret the operator locally as a perturbation of the constant coefficient operator

-\mathrm{div}\big(A(x_{0})\nabla\phi\big)
treated in Proposition
6.4
. Assume that
\phi\in W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega)
with support contained in
\mathbb{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega

(where
r>0
will be determined later). Writing
A(x_{0})=[a_{ij}(x_{0})]
and
A(x)=[a_{ij}(x)]
we deduce from

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\big(A(x_{0})\nabla\phi\big)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\big(A(x)\nabla\phi\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\big(a_{ij}(x_{0})-a_{ij}(x)\big)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{i}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}\phi

that

\displaystyle\big|\mathrm{div}\big(A(x_{0})\nabla\phi\big)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\big(A(x)\nabla\phi\big)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|a_{ij}(x_{0})-a_{ij}(x)|^{2}\,|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\phi|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\partial_{i}a_{ij}(x)|^{2}\,|\partial_{j}\phi|^{2}\Big)\,.

Integrating with respect to
x\in\Omega
and using that
W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})\hookrightarrow C^{s}(\overline{\Omega}_{0})
with
s=1-n/q>0
, we get

\displaystyle\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(x_{0})\nabla\phi\big)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(n,\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},\Omega_{0}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\big)\bigg(r^{2s}\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{i}a_{ij}|^{2}\,|\partial_{j}\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)\,.

Applying Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{q-2}{q}=1
together with Corollary
6.3
to the second term in (
6.6
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{i}a_{ij}|^{2}\,|\partial_{j}\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|a_{ij}\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega)}^{2}\,\|\partial_{j}\phi\|_{L_{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0}\big)\Big(\delta\,\|\partial_{j}\phi\|_{W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+C(\delta)\,\|\partial_{j}\phi\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\Big)

for each
\delta>0
. Plugging this back into (
6.6
) yields

\displaystyle\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(x_{0})\nabla\phi\big)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

(6.7)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},n,q,\Omega_{0}\big)\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Big((r^{2s}+\delta)\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)}+C(\delta)\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\,.

Now we infer from Proposition
6.4
, the triangle inequality and (
6.7
) that there exists a constant
C_{19}=C_{19}\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0}\big)
with

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,C_{18}^{2}\,\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Big(\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(x_{0})\nabla\phi\big)-\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{19}\,\Big(\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+(r^{2s}+\delta)\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{W_{2}^{2}(\Omega)}+C(\delta)\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\,.

Choosing
r
and
\delta>0
with

(r^{2s}+\delta)\leq\frac{1}{2\,C_{19}}\,,

we arrive at

(6.8)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0}\big)\,\Big(\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)

for all
\phi\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega)
with support contained in
\mathbb{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega
.
Finally, it follows from Friedrich’s inequality, Gauss’s theorem and the weighted Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega_{0})\big)\|\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\,C\big(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega_{0})\big)\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi^{T}A(x)\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\mathrm{diam}(\Omega_{0}),\alpha\big)\,\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi\|_{W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\,,

so that we can eliminate the
W^{1}_{2}
-norm of
\phi
on the right-hand side of (
6.8
) and get

(6.9)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\,\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
\phi\in W^{1}_{2,D}(\Omega)
with support contained in
\mathbb{B}(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega
where
C
and also
r
depend only on
q
,
n
,
\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})}
, the ellipticity constant
\alpha
, and
\Omega_{0}
(but not on
\Omega
).

(ii)

Global Estimate:

We aim for a global version of (
6.9
). To this end, note that
\overline{\Omega}_{0}
is compact so that there are
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in\overline{\Omega}_{0}
with

\overline{\Omega}_{0}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{B}(x_{i},r).

Let
\{\theta_{i}\,|\,i=1,\dots,m\}
be a smooth partition of unity on
\overline{\Omega}_{0}
subordinated to
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{B}(x_{i},r)
. For
\phi\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega)
,
it follows that
\theta_{i}\phi\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega)
with support in
\mathbb{B}(x_{i},r)\cap\Omega
and

(6.10)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},n,q,\Omega_{0},\alpha\big)\,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla(\theta_{i}\phi)\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

thanks to (
6.9
). For the right-hand side, we compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\big(A(x)\nabla(\theta_{i}\phi)\big)

\displaystyle=\theta_{i}\,\mathrm{div}\big(A(x)\nabla\phi\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big((\partial_{j}\phi)\big[A(x)\nabla\theta_{i}\big]_{j}+(\partial_{j}\theta_{i})\big[A(x)\nabla\phi\big]_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle+\phi\,\mathrm{div}\big(A(x)\nabla\theta_{i}\big)

\displaystyle=:I+II+III\,.

Since

\displaystyle\|\theta_{i}\|_{C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}_{0})}\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0},\alpha\big)

for each
i=1,\dots,m
, we find

(6.11)

\displaystyle\|I\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0},\alpha\big)\,\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

as well as

\displaystyle\|II\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 2n^{2}\|\theta_{i}\|_{C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}_{0})}\,\|A\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}_{0})}\,\|\nabla\phi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

(6.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0},\alpha\big)\,\|\nabla\phi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\,,

where we used that
W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega}_{0})
. For
III
, we compute further

III=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\phi\,\partial_{j}a_{jk}\,\partial_{k}\theta_{i}+a_{jk}\,\phi\,\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\theta_{i}\,,

and hence

\displaystyle\|III\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0},\alpha\big)\,\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\Big(\|\phi\,\partial_{j}a_{jk}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|\phi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\Big)\,,

where we applied the embedding
W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega}_{0})
once more. Since

\displaystyle\|\phi\,\partial_{j}a_{jk}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}(\partial_{j}a_{jk})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\,\|\phi\|_{L_{\frac{2q}{q-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0}\big)\|\phi\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

due to Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev’s embedding theorem
6.2
, we arrive at

(6.13)

\displaystyle\|III\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0},\alpha\big)\|\phi\|_{W^{1}_{2}(\Omega)}\,.

Plugging the estimates for
I
to
III
in (
6.11
)-(
6.13
) back into (
6.10
), we find that

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\big(\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})},q,n,\Omega_{0},\alpha\big)\,\Big(\big\|\mathrm{div}\big(A(\,\cdot\,)\nabla\phi\big)\big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|\phi\|_{W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)

for
\phi\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega)
. Eventually, we can apply the same steps which lead to (
6.9
) to eliminate the
W^{1}_{2}
-norm of
\phi
on the right-hand side.
∎

Based on the improved a-priori estimate (
6.5
), we can complete the proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

(i)

Approximation of the domain:

Suppose first that
A\in\big[C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{0})\big]^{n\times n}
. Then, we can follow the lines of the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 3.2.1.2]
with
[
14
, Equation (3.2.1.3)]
replaced by the improved a-priori estimate from Proposition
6.5
. Hence, problem (
6.1
) has a unique solution
\phi\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega_{0})

which additionially satisfies the estimate

(6.14)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C\big(q,n,\Omega_{0},\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega)},\alpha\big)\,\|F\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,.

(ii)

Approximation of the coefficients:

Now we treat the general case
A\in\big[W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})\big]^{n\times n}
with
q>n
. Recall that
\Omega_{0}
has a Lipschitz boundary so that we find a sequence
(A^{(m)})\subset\big[C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{0})\big]^{n\times n}

such that each
A^{(m)}(x)
is symmetric and
A^{(m)}\rightarrow A
in
\big[W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})\big]^{n\times n}
. Moreover, we may assume that

\sup_{m}\|A^{(m)}\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})}\leq 2\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})}\,.

It remains to arrange that the
(A^{(m)})
have a common ellipticity constant. To this end, note that
q>n
implies that

\displaystyle\big|\xi^{T}A(x)\xi-\xi^{T}A^{(m)}(x)\xi\big|

\displaystyle\leq|\xi^{T}|\,\|A-A^{(m)}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}_{0})}|\xi|

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|A-A^{(m)}\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega_{0})}\rightarrow 0

for
m\rightarrow\infty
and for each
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\xi|=1
and
x\in\overline{\Omega}_{0}
. Hence, we may assume without loss of generality that

\displaystyle\big|\xi^{T}A(x)\xi-\xi^{T}A^{(m)}(x)\xi\big|\leq\alpha/2\,,\qquad|\xi|=1\,,\ x\in\overline{\Omega}_{0}\,,\ m\in\mathbb{N}\,,

which immediately implies that each
A^{(m)}
is uniformly elliptic with a common ellipticity constant
\alpha/2
.

Now it follows from part (i) that there exists a unique solution
\phi_{m}\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega_{0})
to the problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\ -\mathrm{div}\big(A^{(m)}(x)\nabla\phi_{m}\big)&=F\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{0}\,,\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\phi_{m}&=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{0}\end{cases}

with

(6.15)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{m}\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega_{0})}\leq C\big(q,n,\Omega_{0},\|A\|_{W^{1}_{q}(\Omega)},\alpha\big)\,\|F\|_{L_{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,,\qquad m\in\mathbb{N}\,,

due to (
6.14
). Hence, we find a subsequence
(\phi_{m})
and
\phi\in W^{2}_{2,D}(\Omega_{0})
with
\phi_{m}\rightarrow\phi
in
W^{1}_{2}(\Omega_{0})
and
\phi_{m}\rightharpoonup\phi
in
W^{2}_{2}(\Omega_{0})
. Letting
m\rightarrow\infty
in the weak formulation

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{0}}\nabla\phi_{m}^{T}A^{(m)}(x)\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega_{0}}F\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,,\qquad\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega_{0})\,,

we see that
\phi
is a solution to (
6.1
). It is unique due to the Riesz Representation Theorem. Finally, estimate (
6.2
) follows from (
6.15
) and the weak lower semi-continuity of
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{W^{2}_{2}(\Omega_{0})}
.
∎

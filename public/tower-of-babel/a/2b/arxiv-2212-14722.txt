Abstract
We describe the class, in the Grothendieck group of stacks, of the stack of twisted G G -covers of genus 0 0 curves, in terms of the loci corresponding to covers over smooth bases.

Introduction

Moduli stacks of twisted stable maps have been introduced in the framework of Gromov–Witten theory
for orbifolds and for Deligne–Mumford stacks,
independently by Chen–Ruan
[
16
]
and by Abramovich–Graber–Vistoli
[
2
,
3
]
. In the case where the target is the classifying stack
{\mathcal{B}G}
of a finite group
G
,
a twisted stable map is called a twisted
G
-cover
[
1
]
.
The moduli stack
{\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{g,n}(G)}
of balanced twisted stable maps to
{\mathcal{B}G}
is isomorphic to
the stack of admissible
G
-covers, which has been extensively studied due to its several applications
(see, e.g.,
[
1
,
23
,
31
,
32
,
44
]
).

The present
work originates from
[
13
,
14
,
15
]

where the authors studied the classification problem for the connected components
of moduli spaces of Galois covers of smooth curves. The results obtained there give a complete description
of such components for any genus of the base curves when the Galois group is the dihedral group,
in the general case one only has a complete classification when the genus of the base curves is large enough.
In this article, we consider the compactifications of moduli spaces of Galois covers of curves
given by admissible covers, restricting our attention to the case where the genus of the base curves is
0
.
The aim is to investigate how the boundary can be used to obtain information about
the geometry of the moduli spaces.
Our main result (Theorem
6.4
) gives a description of the class of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)

in the Grothendieck group of stacks, i.e., of the
motivic class
of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)
.
This description is obtained by stratifying the moduli stack as the disjoint union of locally closed
substacks
\mathcal{B}^{\rm bal}(G,\tilde{\tau})
, which correspond to the
G
-covers
of a given combinatorial structure
\tilde{\tau}
, where
\tilde{\tau}
is a gerby tree (cf. Definition
5.4
).
The main technical step is Proposition
6.3
, where we show how these strata are
obtained by gluing together the loci in
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)
corresponding to covers over smooth bases.
In order to keep track of the actions of the symmetric groups on the marked points, these
gluing constructions correspond to two basic operations, the
Day convolution

(Definition
3.2
) and the
composition
(Definition
3.7
).
As a result, we obtain a formula (
6.7
) that
expresses the motivic class of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)

recursively in terms of
the loci
\mathcal{B}_{k}
, for
k\leq n
, corresponding to covers over smooth bases
(cf. Remark
6.5
).
Here we follow the strategy of
[
27
]
(where the authors compute the Betti numbers of the moduli spaces
of stable maps to projective spaces), further extended in
[
8
]
to the case of
moduli spaces of stable maps to Grassmannians.
Over the field of complex numbers, by applying the Poincaré (or Serre) characteristic

[
10
,
27
]
,
our formula allows to compute the Betti numbers of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)
in terms of
the cohomology of
\mathcal{B}_{k}
, as a representation of the symmetric group
\boldsymbol{S}_{k}
,
k=3,\dots,n
,
and the decomposition of
\mathcal{B}_{k}
as union of the open and closed substacks

\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}:=\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,\boldsymbol{c}}(G)\cap\mathcal{B}_{k}
,
where
\boldsymbol{c}=(c_{1},\dots,c_{k})
varies among the sequences of
k
conjugacy classes of
G
,
and
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,\boldsymbol{c}}(G)
is the full sub-category of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,k}(G)
whose objects are the twisted
G
-covers
whose evaluation at the marked points is equal to
\boldsymbol{c}
(cf. Definition
2.9
).
Notice that
\mathcal{B}_{\boldsymbol{c}}
is determined by
\mathcal{B}_{k}
, as a stack over

\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{B}G})^{k}
with respect to the evaluation morphism.
We will address the problem of computing effectively these invariants
of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
in future works.

The structure of the article is as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the definition and collect
basic properties of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)
. In Section
3
, we define the Day convolution and the composition, and
we prove their main properties that will be used. In Section
4
, following the definition of the Grothendieck group of
stacks from
[
22
]
, we introduce the Grothendieck group
of
\mathsf{S}
-modules, over which there is an induced composition
operation.
In Section
5
, we define the notion of gerby tree
\tilde{\tau}
(following
[
4
]
) and we show that the locus
in
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)
of covers of a fixed combinatorial type
\tilde{\tau}
is locally closed.
In Section
6
, we prove our main results, Proposition
6.3
and Theorem
6.4
.
In the last Section
7
, we show how these results can be used to compute the Hodge–Grothendieck
characteristic of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)
in terms of
\mathcal{B}_{k}
for
k=3,\dots,n
.

,

For all (enriched) categories

The stack of twisted
\boldsymbol{G}
-covers

In this section, we recall the definition of the stack of balanced twisted
G
-covers of

-pointed genus
0

\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)
, following
[
1
]
,
to which we refer for further details and properties of this stack.

Let
\Bbbk
be a fixed algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
. By an algebraic
\Bbbk
-stack we mean an algebraic stack of finite type over
\Bbbk
which has affine stabilizers.
The
2

\mathsf{AlgSt}_{\Bbbk}
, while
\mathsf{St}_{\Bbbk}
denotes the
2

Let

be a finite group. Our aim is to study the class of

in the Grothendieck ring of algebraic
\Bbbk
-stacks

.

Roughly speaking, the objects of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{bal}}_{0,n}(G)
are twisted stable maps from twisted curves to
{\mathcal{B}G}
, the classifying stack of
G
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
twisted curve
over a
\Bbbk
-scheme
T
is a morphism
\mathcal{C}\to T
, where

(1)

\mathcal{C}
is a Deligne–Mumford stack;

(2)

\mathcal{C}\to T
is étale locally isomorphic to
[U/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{r}]\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, and
U
is

•

either
\operatorname{Spec}(A[z])
, with
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{r}
acting as
(\zeta,z)\mapsto\zeta\cdot z
,

•

or
\operatorname{Spec}(A[z,w]/(zw-t))
, for some
t\in A
, with
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{r}
acting as
(\zeta,(z,w))\mapsto(\zeta\cdot z,\zeta^{a}\cdot w)
,
for some
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

Notice that in the second case the fiber over
t=0
has a node in
U
, which is locally smoothable if and only if
a\equiv-1
(mod
r
).
A twisted curve is called
balanced
if at every node it is presented as above with
a\equiv-1
(mod
r
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let

be a finite set. A
twisted nodal

-pointed curve
over a
\Bbbk
-scheme

is a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\,T,}

(2)

(3)

is an étale gerbes (see

(4)

the morphism

Remark 2.3
.

In the previous definition, one should think of

as the marked points of the twisted curve, indeed we will refer to

as the

If

[

We refer to
[

and

for more details on twisted curves and their moduli.

.

For any finite set

2.1
The coarse moduli space of the stack of cyclotomic gerbes

For lack of a suitable reference, we show here that the coarse moduli space of
{\overline{\mathcal{I}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{B}G})}
,
which will be denoted

in the article, can be identified with the set of conjugacy classes of

To this aim, by

we have that
{\overline{\mathcal{I}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}}({\mathcal{B}G})}
is isomorphic to the

{{\mathcal{I}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}}({\mathcal{B}G})}
with respect to
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}
, hence its coarse moduli space is isomorphic to that of

{{\mathcal{I}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}}({\mathcal{B}G})}
.
By

, an object of

and

An arrow from

to

is an arrow

such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r})_{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r})_{T^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}_{T}(\xi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}_{T^{\prime}}(\xi^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\,T^{\prime},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{\prime}}

where

is induced by

and
{\rm Id}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{r}}
,

\operatorname{Aut}_{T}(\xi)\leftarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{T^{\prime}}(\xi^{\prime})
is the map induced by

(it associates any

3
The category of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{S}}
-modules over a base

In order to study the class of

In this section, we develop a formalism that is needed when we express

and

One complication arises here because the marked points

Furthermore, we shall also take into account the stacks

An approach towards this direction can be recovered from the theory of colored operads, where many of the tools that we
use originated.
The connection with this theory has been already highlighted in
[
43

Here we adopt the point of view of
[
36

according to which an operad is a monoid for a certain tensor product

(the so called “composition”, which in our context is defined in Definition

in a particular category of functors. However, we can not use directly the results therein for two reasons:
firstly, the category of stacks is a

-category, hence we need to work in the context of enriched categories;
secondly, as mentioned above, the gluing maps are more complicated in our setting due to the local monodromies.
Let us recall the main definitions and results, which we adapt for our purposes.
Since these results are effortlessly reworked versions of well-known facts in the non-enriched case, we do not present complete proofs, but we rather indicate which classical results they are adapted from.

We consider categories enriched over a fixed cosmos

is a strict monoidal category, i.e., the associator and the unitors are identity morphisms. Unless otherwise stated, all categorical constructions (for instance, coends and Kan extensions) will be carried out in the
\mathscr{V}
-enriched setting. Such level of generality is needed because

and

are
2
-categories, i.e., they are enriched over the cosmos

of small categories. A reference for all the tools of enriched category theory that we will use is

Our guiding example is given by

Proposition 3.4

Lemma 5.6

Proof.

Let

By definition,

is connected and

The right-hand side of the previous equation is equal to

6

In this section, we prove our main result, Theorem

corresponding to stable maps with smooth domain. To this aim we need some preliminary definitions and results.
First of all, following
[
8

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic
\Bbbk
-stack. A locally closed decomposition of
\mathcal{X}
is a morphism

f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
of algebraic
\Bbbk
-stacks that satisfies the following conditions:

•

the restriction of
f
to each connected component of
\mathcal{Y}
is a locally closed immersion;

•

f
is bijective on closed points.

Let us recall that a locally closed immersion is a morphism that can be factored as
j\circ i
, where
i
is a closed immersion and
j
is an open immersion.

In the following, we will use the next result, which is well known, but we provide an elementary proof for completeness.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
be a locally closed decomposition. Let
\mathcal{Y}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{Y}_{m}

be the connected components of
\mathcal{Y}
. Then the following equality holds true in
K_{0}(\mathsf{AlgSt}_{\Bbbk})
:

\{\mathcal{X}\}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\{f(\mathcal{Y}_{k})\}.

Proof.

By definition,
f(\mathcal{Y}_{1}),\dots,f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})
are locally closed substacks of
\mathcal{X}
such that

\mathcal{X}=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{m}f(\mathcal{Y}_{k}).

We proceed by induction on
m
. Let us assume
m>1
, since the case
m=1
is trivial.
Now, for
\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}
being the closure of
f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})
, using the properties of
K_{0}(\mathsf{AlgSt}_{\Bbbk})
and the fact that

f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})
is open in
\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}
, we have the following equalities:

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{X}\}

\displaystyle=\{\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\}+\{\mathcal{X}\setminus\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\}

\displaystyle=\{{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\}+\{\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\setminus f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})\}+\{\mathcal{X}\setminus\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\}.

Now we observe that

\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\setminus f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{m-1}\bigl(\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\setminus f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})\bigr)\cap f(\mathcal{Y}_{k})=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\cap f(\mathcal{Y}_{k})

and

\mathcal{X}\setminus\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{m-1}\bigl(\mathcal{X}\setminus\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\bigr)\cap f(\mathcal{Y}_{k}),

which are locally closed decompositions of the corresponding right-hand sides. By induction, we deduce that

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{X}\}

\displaystyle=\{{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bigl\{\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\cap f(\mathcal{Y}_{k})\bigr\}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\bigl\{\bigl(\mathcal{X}\setminus\overline{f(\mathcal{Y}_{m})}\bigr)\cap f(\mathcal{Y}_{k})\bigr\}

\otimes
, respectively.

The Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathbf{M})
is the quotient of the free abelian group on the isomorphism classes
[M]

of objects
M
in
\mathbf{M}
by the subgroup generated by elements of the form

[M]-[M^{\prime}]-[M^{\prime\prime}]
whenever
M\cong M^{\prime}\oplus M^{\prime\prime}
.
The operation
\otimes
induces a ring structure on
K_{0}(\mathbf{M})
.

The following result holds true over any field of characteristic
0
(see
[
28
, Theorem 4]
and
[
30
]
).

Theorem 7.3
.

There exists a unique ring homomorphism
\overline{h}\colon K_{0}({\rm Var})\to K_{0}(\mathbf{M})

such that
\overline{h}([X])=[(X,[\Delta_{X}])]
for
X
smooth and projective.

Let us recall for later use that
\overline{h}([X])
coincides with the class in
K_{0}({\rm Hot}^{b}(\mathbf{M}))

of the weight complex
W(X)
of
X

[
28
, Sections 2.1 and 3.2]
.

{\rm Hot}^{b}(\mathbf{M})
denotes the category of bounded chain complexes in

\mathbf{M}
up to homotopy, and one uses the identification of
K_{0}({\rm Hot}^{b}(\mathbf{M}))
with
K_{0}(\mathbf{M})

to get a class in
K_{0}(\mathbf{M})
.

The cohomology of a motive is defined as follows. For any object
M=(X,p)
of
\mathbf{M}

H^{i}(M):={\rm Im}\bigl(p_{*}\colon H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\bigr),

where
p_{*}
denotes the action induced on cohomology by
p
,

p_{*}(\alpha):=({\rm pr}_{2})_{*}(p\cdot{\rm pr}_{1}^{*}(\alpha))
, where
{\rm pr}_{i}\colon X\times X\to X

is the projection to the
i
-th component.
Since
p_{*}
is a morphism in
{\rm MHS}
,
H^{i}(M)
is an object of
{\rm MHS}
,
and following
[
41
, Appendix A-3]
we define

\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{M}}(M):=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\bigl[H^{i}(M)\bigr]\in K_{0}({\rm MHS}).

Notice that
\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{M}}
is additive on the objects of
\mathbf{M}
, therefore
it induces a group homomorphism

\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{M}}\colon\ K_{0}(\mathbf{M})\to K_{0}({\rm MHS}).

It is proved in
[
41
, Appendix A]
that the HG-characteristic lifts to motives, i.e.,

\mathfrak{e}=\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{M}}\circ\overline{h}.

It follows from this that
\mathfrak{e}([X/G])=\mathfrak{e}_{\mathbf{M}}\bigl(\overline{h}([X/G])\bigr)
.

Corollary 5.3 in
[
18
]
implies that there is an isomorphism of weight complexes,

W(X/G)\cong W(X)^{G}
, where
W(X)^{G}=\bigl(W(X),\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}[g]\bigr)

is the image of
W(X)
under the projector
\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}[g]
.
Here
W(X)
has to be considered as an object of the category of functors from
G
to
{\rm Hot}^{b}(\mathbf{M})
.

Let us now apply the functor
H^{n}
to
W(X/G)
. We obtain a complex of

\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces whose
i
-th cohomology group is
denoted
R^{i}H^{n}(W(X/G))
, and it coincides with

{\rm gr}_{n}^{W}H_{\rm c}^{i+n}(X/G,\mathbb{Q})

[
28
, Section 3.1]
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle R^{i}H^{n}(W(X/G))

\displaystyle=R^{i}H^{n}\bigl(W(X)^{G}\bigr)=R^{i}(H^{n}(W(X)))^{G}

\displaystyle=\bigl(R^{i}H^{n}(W(X))\bigr)^{G}=\bigl({\rm gr}_{n}^{W}H_{\rm c}^{i+n}(X,\mathbb{Q})\bigr)^{G},

where we have used the fact that
H^{n}(X,p)={\rm Im}(p_{*}\colon H^{n}(X)\to H^{n}(X))
for any motive
(X,p)
,
by definition, and that taking
G
-invariants is exact for vector spaces over
\mathbb{Q}
.

The claim follows because the Grothendieck group of mixed Hodge structures
is isomorphic to the one of pure Hodge structures (
{\rm HS}
) via the homomorphism
K_{0}({\rm MHS})\to K_{0}({\rm HS})

defined by sending
[(V,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})]
to
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl[{\rm gr}_{i}^{W}V\bigr]

[
42
, Corollary 3.9]
.

7.2
Application: the abelian case

In this section, we consider the forgetful morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{0,n}(G)\to\mathcal{M}_{0,n}

that sends any twisted
G
-cover
(\mathcal{C}\to C,\Sigma,f\colon\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}G})

of an
n
-pointed curve of genus
0

to
(C,|\Sigma_{1}|,\dots,|\Sigma_{n}|)=:(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})

(we adopt the notation of Definition
2.4
).
As in the previous section
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{0,n}(G)
and
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
denote coarse moduli spaces.
The morphism
\pi
is finite and étale (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Lemma 5.2.2]
), and its fiber
over
(C,p_{1},\dots,p_{n})
can be identified with the quotient set

\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(C\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\},p_{0}),G)/G,

where
p_{0}\in C\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
is a base point and
G
acts by conjugation
(see, e.g.,
[
12
,
25
]
).

Let us fix a geometric basis of
\pi_{1}(C\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\},p_{0})
,
i.e., loops
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
based at
p_{0}
, that go near
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}

along given paths, turn around the marked points and go back to
p_{0}
along the same paths.
Furthermore,
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
intersect each other only at
p_{0}
,
their homotopy classes
[\gamma_{1}],\dots,[\gamma_{n}]
generate the fundamental group

\pi_{1}(C\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\},p_{0})
and are subject only to the relation

[\gamma_{1}]\cdots[\gamma_{n}]=1
.

Using this geometric basis, we can identify the set of morphisms
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(C\setminus\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\},\allowbreak p_{0}),G)

with the set
{\rm HV}(G,n)
of
G
-Hurwitz vectors in
G
of genus
0
(we use the same notation as in

[
15
, Definition 2.1]
),

{\rm HV}(G,n):=\bigl\{(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in G^{n}\mid g_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot g_{n}=1\bigr\},

the action by
G
corresponds to the simultaneous conjugation of
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
.
The Artin’s braid group
Br_{n}
is generated by the so-called elementary braid
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}

and it acts on
{\rm HV}(G,n)
as follows:

(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\cdot\sigma_{i}=\bigl(g_{1},\dots,g_{i-1},g_{i}g_{i+1}g_{i}^{-1},g_{i},g_{i+2},\dots,g_{n}\bigr).

Notice that this action commutes with the
G
-action, hence it induces an action on
{\rm HV}(G,n)/G
.

One can use this action to describe the connected components of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{0,n}(G)
.
In particular, when
G
is abelian, by
[
12
, Proposition 2.1]
, we have that

\pi\colon\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{0,n}(G)\to\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
is a trivial bundle, hence
\mathcal{B}^{\mathrm{sm}}_{0,n}(G)\cong\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times{\rm HV}(G,n)

and the evaluation maps correspond to the projections
{\rm HV}(G,n)\to G
.
Therefore, we obtain an expression of
[\mathcal{B}]\in K_{0}^{\mathsf{S}}({\rm Var}/B)
in terms of
the classes
[\mathcal{M}_{0,n}]
, for all
n
.
Using the known results about the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{0,n}

(e.g.,
[
26
,
34
,
39
]
), one can determine the Betti numbers of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
for every
n
, hence those of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
using the results of the previous section.

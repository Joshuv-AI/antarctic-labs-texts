Joyce structures and their twistor spaces
Tom Bridgeland

Abstract
. Joyce structures are a class of geometric structures which first arose in relation to holomorphic generating functions for Donaldson-Thomas invariants. They can be thought of as non-linear analogues of Frobenius structures, or as special classes of complex hyperkähler manifolds. We give a detailed introduction to Joyce structures, with particular focus on the geometry of the associated twistor space. We also prove several new results.



1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to give an introduction to a class of geometric structures known as Joyce structures. These structures have appeared in several contexts recently, including integrable systems
[
13
,
20
,
21
]
and topological string theory
[
1
,
2
]
. A Joyce structure on a complex manifold
M
involves a one-parameter family of flat and symplectic non-linear connections on the tangent bundle
T_{M}
, and gives rise to a complex hyperkähler structure on the total space
X=T_{M}
. The precise definition first appeared in
[
12
]
, but the essential features are standard in twistor theory (see e.g.
[
19
]
), and go back to work of Plebański
[
40
]
.

Joyce structures take their name from a line of research initiated in
[
32
]
which aims to encode the Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants
[
33
,
36
]
of a three-dimensional Calabi-Yau (CY
3
) category in a geometric structure on its space of stability conditions
[
9
,
10
]
. From this point of view a Joyce structure should be thought of as a non-linear analogue of a Frobenius structure
[
17
,
18
]
, in which the linear structure group
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
has been replaced by the group of Poisson automorphisms of a complex torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. The wall-crossing formula shows that the DT invariants can be viewed as the Stokes data for an isomonodromic family of irregular connections on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
taking values in this group. This perspective is the subject of
[
12
]
and is summarised in
[
15
, Appendix A]
.

Relations between the wall-crossing formula in DT theory and real hyperkähler manifolds were first discovered in the celebrated work of Gaiotto, Moore and Neitzke
[
25
,
26
]
. The connection with complex hyperkähler manifolds is somewhat different and was made in
[
15
]
. In physical terms the two stories are related by the conformal limit
[
1
,
2
,
24
]
.

The geometry of a Joyce structure is often clearer when viewed through the lens of the associated twistor space
p\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Each fibre of the map

is the leaf space of a half-dimensional foliation on
X=T_{M}
. There are essentially three distinct fibres,
Z_{0}
,
Z_{1}
and
Z_{\infty}
, of which
Z_{0}
is naturally identified with
M
. It is intriguing to note that in simple examples associated to the DT theory of a quiver, the fibre
Z_{0}=M
is a quotient of the space of stability conditions, whereas
Z_{1}
is closely related to the cluster Poisson variety. The fibre
Z_{\infty}
remains rather mysterious.

An interesting class of examples of Joyce structures was constructed in
[
16
]
. The base
M
parameterises pairs consisting of a Riemann surface of some fixed genus
g
equipped with a quadratic differential with simple zeroes. The extension to spaces of quadratic differentials with poles of fixed orders will appear in
[
41
]
. These Joyce structures are expected to arise from the DT theory of the CY
3
categories considered in
[
14
,
28
]
, and relate in physics
[
8
]
to supersymmetric gauge theories of class
S[A_{1}]
. We give explicit descriptions of the two simplest examples of this type, which are naturally associated to the DT theory of the A
1
and A
2

Conventions

We work throughout in the category of complex manifolds and holomorphic maps. All symplectic forms, metrics, bundles, connections, sections etc. are holomorphic. The holomorphic tangent bundle of a complex manifold
M
is denoted
T_{M}
, and the derivative of a map of complex manifolds
f\colon M\to N
is denoted
f_{*}\colon T_{M}\to f^{*}(T_{N})
. The map
f
is called étale if
f_{*}
is an isomorphism. We use the symbol
\mathcal{L}
to denote the Lie derivative.

2.
Pre-Joyce structures

Recall
[
17
,
18
]
that a Frobenius structure on a complex manifold
M
consists of a pencil of flat, torsion-free connections on the tangent bundle of
M
with certain additional properties (the existence of the identity and Euler vector fields, potentiality of the multiplication, etc). In a similar way, a Joyce structure on
M
consists of a pencil of flat, symplectic
non-linear
connections on the tangent bundle of
M
, again admitting certain additional symmetries. In this section we focus on the pencil of connections, which we refer to as a pre-Joyce structure, leaving discussion of the required symmetries to the next section.

2.1.
Non-linear connections

We begin by briefly summarising some basic facts about non-linear connections in the sense of Ehresmann (see e.g.
[
34
, Chapter 3]
). Recall that we are always working in the category of complex manifolds, so all connections will be holomorphic.

Let
\pi\colon X\to M
be a holomorphic submersion of complex manifolds. We denote the fibres by
X_{m}=\pi^{-1}(m)
. The derivative of
\pi
gives rise to a short exact sequence of bundles

(1)

0\longrightarrow T_{X/M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi^{*}(T_{M})\longrightarrow 0.

Definition 2.1
.

A non-linear
connection on the map
\pi
is a map of bundles
h\colon\pi^{*}(T_{M})\to T_{X}
satisfying
\pi_{*}\circ h=1
.

Writing
H=\operatorname{im}(h)
and
V=T_{X/M}
, the tangent bundle of
X
decomposes as a direct sum
T_{X}=H\oplus V
. We call tangent vectors and vector fields horizontal or vertical if they lie in
H
or
V
respectively. Note that a vector field
u\in H^{0}(M,T_{M})
can be lifted to a horizontal vector field
h(u)\in H^{0}(X,T_{X})
by composing the pullback
\pi^{*}(u)\in H^{0}(X,\pi^{*}(T_{M}))
with the map
h
.

Consider a smooth path
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
. Given a point
x\in X_{\gamma(0)}
we can look for a lifted path
\alpha\colon[0,\delta]\to X
satisfying
\alpha_{*}(\frac{d}{dt})=h(\gamma_{*}(\frac{d}{dt}))
and
\alpha(0)=x
. Since we have not assumed that
\pi
is proper, such a lift will exist only for small enough
\delta>0
. For
t\in[0,\delta]
we call
\alpha(t)\in X_{\gamma(t)}
the time
t
parallel transport of the point
x
along the path
\gamma
. Given a point
x_{0}\in X_{\gamma(0)}
we can find a
\delta>0
and open subsets
U_{t}\subset X_{\gamma(t)}
with
x_{0}\in U_{0}
, such that time
t
parallel transport along
\gamma
defines an isomorphism
\operatorname{PT}_{\gamma}(t)\colon U_{0}\to U_{t}
for each
t\in[0,\delta]
.

Given complex manifolds
M,N
there is a connection on the projection map
\pi_{M}\colon M\times N\to M
induced by the canonical splitting
T_{M\times N}=\pi_{M}^{*}(T_{M})\oplus\pi_{N}^{*}(T_{N})
. The connection
h
is called flat if it is locally isomorphic to a connection of this form. More precisely:

Definition 2.2
.

The connection
h
is flat if the following equivalent conditions hold:

(i)

for every
x\in X
there are local co-ordinates
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
on
X
at
x
, and
(y_{1},\cdots,y_{d})
on
M
at

, such that
x_{i}=\pi^{*}(y_{i})
and
h(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}})=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d
,

(ii)

the sub-bundle
H=\operatorname{im}(h)\subset T_{X}
is closed under Lie bracket:
[H,H]\subset H
.

Suppose given a relative symplectic form
\Omega_{\pi}\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}\,\,T^{*}_{X/M})
on the map
\pi
. It restricts to a symplectic form
\Omega_{m}\in H^{0}(X_{m},\wedge^{2}\,\,T^{*}_{X_{m}})
on each fibre
X_{m}
.
We say that the connection
h
preserves
\Omega_{\pi}
if for any path
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to M
the partially-defined parallel transport maps
\operatorname{PT}_{\gamma}(t)\colon X_{\gamma(0)}\to X_{\gamma(t)}
take

to
\Omega_{\gamma(t)}
. This is equivalent to the statement that for any horizontal vector field

on

the Lie derivative
\mathcal{L}_{u}(\Omega_{\pi})=0
.

Using the decomposition
T_{X}=H\oplus V
the relative form

can be lifted uniquely to a form
\Omega\in H^{0}(X,\wedge^{2}\,T^{*}_{X})
satisfying
\ker(\Omega)=H
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(i)

The connection
h
preserves
\Omega_{\pi}
precisely if
i_{v_{1}}i_{v_{2}}(d\Omega)=0
for any two vertical vector fields
v_{1},v_{2}\in H^{0}(X,T_{X/M})
.

(ii)

If the connection

is flat then it preserves
\Omega_{\pi}
precisely if

.

Proof.

Part (i) is
[
27
, Theorem 4]
. For part (ii), note that one implication follows from (i), so let us assume that

is flat and preserves
\Omega_{\pi}
and prove that

.

Take three vector fields
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}
on

and consider the expression defining
d\Omega(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
. We can assume that each
u_{i}
is either horizontal or vertical. Note that both horizontal and vertical vector fields are closed under Lie bracket, and
i_{h}(\Omega)=0
for any horizontal vector field

. Thus
d\Omega(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=0
as soon as two of the
u_{i}
are horizontal. In the remaining cases two of the

are vertical, and the claim follows from part (i).∎

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts freely and properly on
X
preserving the map
\pi
. Then

is a complex manifold and the quotient map

is étale. There is an induced submersion

and a factorisation

. A connection

will be called

on

Definition 2.4
.

A pre-Joyce structure
(\omega,h)
on a complex manifold
M
consists of

(i)

a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega
on
M
,

(ii)

a non-linear connection
h
on the tangent bundle
\pi\colon X=T_{M}\to M
,

such that for each
\epsilon\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
the connection
h_{\epsilon}=h+\epsilon^{-1}v
is flat and symplectic.

Take a local co-ordinate system
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
on
M
which is Darboux, in the sense that

(4)

\omega=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot dz_{p}\wedge dz_{q},

with
\omega_{pq}
a constant skew-symmetric matrix. We denote by
\eta_{pq}
the inverse matrix.

There are associated linear co-ordinates
(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n})
on the tangent spaces
T_{M,m}
obtained by writing a tangent vector in the form
\sum_{i}\theta_{i}\cdot{\partial}/{\partial z_{i}}
. We thus get induced local co-ordinates
(z_{i},\theta_{j})
on the total space
X=T_{M}
. In these co-ordinates

(5)

v_{i}:=v\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\Big)=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{i}}.

The fact that the connection
h
is flat and symplectic ensures that we can write

(6)

h_{i}:=h\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\Big)=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}+\sum_{p,q}\eta_{pq}\cdot\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{p}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{q}},

for locally-defined functions
W_{i}=W_{i}(z,\theta)

W_{i}
is only well-defined up to the addition of functions

vanishes along the zero section

W_{i}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},0,\cdots,0)=0.

The connection

is flat precisely if
\big[h_{i}+\epsilon^{-1}v_{i},h_{j}+\epsilon^{-1}v_{j}\big]=0
for all

. A short calculation shows that this holds for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
precisely if

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{k}}\Big(\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}-\frac{\partial W_{j}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Big)=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{k}}\Big(\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}-\frac{\partial W_{j}}{\partial z_{i}}-\sum_{p,q}\eta_{pq}\cdot\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{p}}\cdot\frac{\partial W_{j}}{\partial\theta_{q}}\Big)=0.

for all
1\leqslant i,j,k\leqslant n
.

Remark 2.5
.

So as to be able to include certain interesting examples it is sometimes useful to weaken the axioms of a (pre-) Joyce structure to allow the connection
h\colon\pi^{*}(T_{M})\to T_{X}
to have poles. In precise terms this means that
h
should be defined by a bundle map
h\colon\pi^{*}(T_{M})\to T_{X}(D)
satisfying

(10)

(\pi_{*}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}(D))\circ h=1_{\pi^{*}(T_{M})}\otimes s_{D},

where
D\subset X
is an effective divisor, and
s_{D}\colon\mathscr{O}_{X}\to\mathscr{O}_{X}(D)
is the canonical inclusion. When expressed in terms of local co-ordinates as above, this just means that the functions
W_{i}(z,\theta)
are meromorphic. We will refer to the resulting structures as meromorphic (pre-) Joyce structures.

Definition 2.6
.

A complex hyperkähler structure
(g,I,J,K)
on a complex manifold

consists of

(i)

a metric

g(R(u_{1}),R(u_{2}))=g(u_{1},u_{2}),\qquad\nabla(R)=0,\qquad R\in\{I,J,K\}.

(24)

\displaystyle\Omega_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot dz_{p}\wedge dz_{q},

(25)

\displaystyle 2i\Omega_{I}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{p,q}\bigg(\frac{\partial W_{p}}{\partial\theta_{q}}-\frac{\partial W_{q}}{\partial\theta_{p}}\bigg)\cdot dz_{p}\wedge dz_{q}-\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot d\theta_{p}\wedge dz_{q},

(26)

\displaystyle\Omega_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot d\theta_{p}\wedge d\theta_{q}+\sum_{p,q}\frac{\partial W_{q}}{\partial\theta_{p}}\cdot d\theta_{p}\wedge dz_{q}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{p,q,r,s}\eta_{rs}\cdot\frac{\partial W_{p}}{\partial\theta_{r}}\frac{\partial W_{q}}{\partial\theta_{s}}\cdot dz_{p}\wedge dz_{q}.

Note that by (
8
) the first term in (
25
) is independent of the co-ordinates
\theta_{k}
and hence descends to
M
. In the case of a Joyce structure the identity (
47
) shows that this term vanishes.

3.
Joyce structures

A Joyce structure on a complex manifold
M
is a pre-Joyce structure with certain additional symmetries. These symmetries are controlled by a special kind of integral affine structure on
M
which we call a period structure. After introducing the necessary definitions we derive some consequences of the extra symmetries, both for the associated complex hyperkähler structure on
X=T_{M}
, and for the local generating functions
W_{i}
.

3.1.
Period structures

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a holomorphic vector bundle on a complex manifold
M
. By a lattice in
\mathcal{H}
we mean a locally-constant subsheaf of abelian groups
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that the multiplication map
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{O}_{M}\to\mathcal{H}
is an isomorphism. There is an induced flat (linear) connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{H}
whose flat sections are
\mathbb{C}
-linear combinations of the sections of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A period structure
(T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}},\nabla,Z)
on a complex manifold
M
consists of

(P1)

a lattice
T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset T_{M}
whose associated flat connection is denoted
\nabla
,

(P2)

a vector field
Z\in\Gamma(M,T_{M})
satisfying
\nabla(Z)=\operatorname{id}
.

Given a point
p\in M
, a basis of the free abelian group
T_{M,p}^{\mathbb{Z}}
extends uniquely to a basis of
\nabla
-flat sections
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n}
of
T_{M}
over a contractible open neighbourhood
p\in U\subset M
. Writing the vector field
Z
in the form
Z=\sum_{i}z_{i}\cdot\phi_{i}
then defines holomorphic functions
z_{i}\colon U\to\mathbb{C}
. Given a local co-ordinate system
(u_{1},\cdots,u_{n})
on
M
, condition (P2) shows that

(27)

\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}=\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}}(Z)=\sum_{i}\frac{\partial z_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}\cdot\phi_{i},

for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
, from which it follows that
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
is also a local co-ordinate system. Applying (
27
) with
u_{j}=z_{j}
then shows that
\phi_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
. This implies in particular that the connection
\nabla
is torsion-free.

Recall
[
35
]
that an integral affine structure on a complex manifold
M
is a lattice
T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset T_{M}
whose associated flat connection
\nabla
is torsion-free . A local co-ordinate system
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
is then called integral affine if the tangent vectors
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
lie in the lattice
T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. Such co-ordinate systems are uniquely defined up to affine transformations of the form
z_{i}\mapsto\sum_{j}a_{ij}z_{j}+v_{i}
with
(a_{ij})\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and
(v_{i})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Given a period structure on a complex manifold
M
we obtain an integral affine structure by forgetting the vector field
Z
. A system of integral affine co-ordinates
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
will be called integral linear if
Z=\sum_{i}z_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}
. Such co-ordinate systems are uniquely defined up to linear transformations of the form
z_{i}\mapsto\sum_{j}a_{ij}z_{j}
with
(a_{ij})\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Thus a period structure can be thought of as an integral
linear
structure.

Definition 3.2
.

A period structure will be called homogeneous if the vector field
Z
generates an action of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{C}^{*}
on
M
.

We can use the connection
\nabla
on
T_{M}
to lift the vector field
Z
on
M
to a horizontal vector field
E
on
X=T_{M}
. If we take a system of integral linear co-ordinates
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
on
M
, and associated co-ordinates
(z_{i},\theta_{j})
on
X=T_{M}
as in Section
2.2
, then

(28)

Z=\sum_{i}z_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}},\qquad E=\sum_{i}z_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}.

In what follows we refer to both
Z
and
E
as Euler vector fields. This will hopefully not cause confusion since they live on different spaces.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}},\nabla,Z)
be a homogeneous period structure on a complex manifold
M
. Then the lifted Euler vector field
E
generates an action of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
on
X=T_{M}
. This action is obtained by combining the
derivative of the action map
a\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\times M\to M
, with a rescaling of weight
-1
on the linear fibres of the projection
\pi\colon T_{M}\to M
.

Proof.

Take local co-ordinates
(z_{i})
on
M
and
(z_{i},\theta_{j})
on
X
as above. The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
M
is given by
t\cdot(z_{i})=(tz_{i})
. The derivative of the action map
a\colon\mathbb{C}^{*}\times M\to M
defines a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
X
given by
t\cdot(z_{i},\theta_{j})=(tz_{i},t\theta_{j})
. Composing with the weight
-1
rescaling action on the fibres gives the action
t\cdot(z_{i},\theta_{j})=(tz_{i},\theta_{j})
whose generating vector field is
E
.
∎

3.2.
Joyce structures

We define a Joyce structure by combining a pre-Joyce structure with a compatible period structure. Given a symplectic form
\omega\colon T_{M}\times T_{M}\to\mathscr{O}_{M}
there is an induced pairing
\eta\colon T_{M}^{*}\times T_{M}^{*}\to\mathscr{O}_{M}
defined by the condition that the induced maps
\omega^{\flat}\colon T_{M}\to T_{M}^{*}
and
\eta^{\flat}\colon T_{M}^{*}\to T_{M}
are mutually inverse. We refer to
\eta
as the inverse of
\omega
.

Definition 3.4
.

A Joyce structure on a complex manifold
M
consists of

(a)

a period structure
(T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}},Z,\nabla)
on
M
,

(b)

a pre-Joyce structure
(\omega,h)
on
M
,

satisfying the following compatibility conditions:

(J1)

if
\eta
is the inverse of
\omega
then
(2\pi i)^{-1}\eta
takes integral values on the dual lattice
(T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}\subset T_{M}^{*}
,

(J2)

the connection
h
is invariant under translations by the lattice
(2\pi i)\,T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset T_{M}
,

(J3)

if
E
is the
\nabla
-horizontal lift of the vector field
Z
, then for any vector field
u
on
M

(29)

h([Z,u])=[E,h(u)],

(J4)

the connection
h
is invariant under the action of the involution
-1\colon X\to X
which acts by multiplication by
-1
on the fibres of
\pi\colon X=T_{M}\to M
.

We say that a Joyce structure is homogeneous if the underlying period structure is.

Given a period structure on a complex manifold
M
we introduce the quotient

(30)

X^{\#}=T_{M}^{\#}=T_{M}/(2\pi i)\,T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}.

Axiom (J2) is the statement that the connection
h
descends to a connection on the
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
-bundle over
M
given by the projection
\pi\colon X^{\#}\to M
.

Remark 3.5
.

In the context of DT theory, in which
M
is a space of numerical stability conditions on a CY
3
category, the period structure
(T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}},Z,\nabla)
and symplectic form
\omega
are well-known and immediate: the integral linear co-ordinates are given by central charges, and the symplectic form is induced by the inverse of the Euler form. The extra content of the Joyce structure is then just the non-linear connection
h
, which however is required to satisfy a complicated system of symmetry and curvature conditions.

3.3.
Associated hyperkähler structures

Given a Joyce structure on a complex manifold
M
, the associated complex hyperkähler structure
(g,I,J,K)
on
X=T_{M}
defined in Section
2.3
has certain extra symmetry properties. For example, axiom (J2) ensures that it is invariant under the translations by the lattice
(2\pi i)\,T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset T_{M}
, and hence descends to the quotient manifold
X^{\#}
introduced above. The other axioms give the following result.

Lemma 3.6
.

There are identities

(31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{E}(I)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{E}(J\pm iK)

\displaystyle=\pm(J\pm iK),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{E}(g)

\displaystyle=g.

(32)

\displaystyle(-1)^{*}(I)

\displaystyle=I,

\displaystyle(-1)^{*}(J\pm iK)

\displaystyle=-(J\pm iK),

\displaystyle(-1)^{*}(g)

\displaystyle=-g.

Proof.

We will evaluate both sides of each identity on vector fields of the form
h(u)
and
v(u)
, where
u
is an arbitrary vector field on
M
.
For the identities (
31
) note first that we have

(33)

\mathcal{L}_{E}(h(u))=h(\mathcal{L}_{Z}(u)),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{E}(v(u))=v(\mathcal{L}_{Z}(u)+u)

The first of these equations is axiom (J3), and the second follows from Lemma
3.3
.

If
u
is a vector field on
M
then using (
21
) we have

(34)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{E}(J-iK))\,h(u)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{E}((J-iK)\,h(u))-(J-iK)\,\mathcal{L}_{E}(h(u))=0,

(35)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\mathcal{L}_{E}(J-iK))\,v(u)&=\mathcal{L}_{E}((J-iK)\,v(u))-(J-iK)\,\mathcal{L}_{E}(v(u))\\
&=2h(\mathcal{L}_{Z}(u))-2h(\mathcal{L}_{Z}(u)+u)=-2h(u),\end{split}

and it follows that
\mathcal{L}_{E}(J-iK)=-(J-iK)
. The claim
\mathcal{L}_{E}(J+iK)=J+iK
follows in the same way.
The statement that
\mathcal{L}_{E}(I)=0
holds because by (
33
)

(36)

(\mathcal{L}_{E}(I))\,h(u)=\mathcal{L}_{E}(I(h(u)))-I(\mathcal{L}_{E}(h(u)))=\mathcal{L}_{E}(ih(u))-i\mathcal{L}_{E}(h(u))=0,

with an analogous identity for
v(u)
.

For the statement on the metric note first that (J1) implies that

\mathcal{L}_{Z}(\omega)=2\omega
.
Then using the definition (
14
) of the metric
g
we have

(37)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{E}(g)(h(u_{1}),v(u_{2}))&=E\cdot g(h(u_{1}),v(u_{2}))-g(\mathcal{L}_{E}(h(u_{1})),v(u_{2}))-g(h(u_{1}),\mathcal{L}_{E}(v(u_{2})))\\
&=E\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{*}(\omega(u_{1},u_{2}))-\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{*}(\omega(\mathcal{L}_{Z}(u_{1}),u_{2}))-\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{*}(\omega(u_{1},\mathcal{L}_{Z}(u_{2})+u_{2}))\\
&=\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{Z}(\omega)(u_{1},u_{2}))-\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{*}(\omega(u_{1},u_{2}))=\tfrac{1}{2}\pi^{*}(\omega(u_{1},u_{2}))\\
&=g(h(u_{1}),v(u_{2})),\end{split}

where we used the fact that
\pi_{*}(E_{x})=Z_{\pi(x)}
for all
x\in X
to write
E\cdot\pi^{*}(f)=\pi^{*}(Z\cdot f)
for any function
f
on
M
.
An easier argument gives

(38)

\mathcal{L}_{E}(g)(h(u_{1}),h(u_{2}))=0=\mathcal{L}_{E}(g)(v(u_{1}),v(u_{2})),

so we find that
\mathcal{L}_{E}(g)=g
as required.

The identities (
32
) follow from the definitions (
13
) and (
14
) together with

(39)

(-1)^{*}(h(u))=h(u),\qquad(-1)^{*}(v(u))=-v(u).

The first of these equations is axiom (J4), and the second is immediate from the definition of the involution
-1
.
∎

3.4.
Plebański function

Consider a local system of integral linear co-ordinates
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
on
M
. Taking associated co-ordinates
(z_{i},\theta_{j})
on
X=T_{M}

we can express the connections
h_{\epsilon}
using locally-defined functions
W_{i}=W_{i}(z,\theta)
as in Section
2.2
. As before we fix the integration constants by imposing the condition that
W_{i}
vanishes on the zero section
M\subset X=T_{M}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

There is a unique locally-defined function
W=W(z,\theta)
which vanishes on the zero section
M\subset X=T_{M}
and satisfies
W_{i}=\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta_{i}}
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. It also satisfies the relations

(40)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{i}\partial z_{j}}-\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{j}\partial z_{i}}=\sum_{p,q}\eta_{pq}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{i}\partial\theta_{p}}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{j}\partial\theta_{q}}

(41)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{i}\partial\theta_{j}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},\theta_{1}+2\pi ik_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}+2\pi ik_{n})=\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{i}\partial\theta_{j}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}),

(42)

\displaystyle W(\lambda z_{1},\cdots,\lambda z_{n},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n})=\lambda^{-1}\cdot W(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}),

(43)

\displaystyle W(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},-\theta_{1},\cdots,-\theta_{n})=-W(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}),

for all
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n
, where
(k_{1},\cdots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Proof.

Using the expressions (
28
) the axioms (J2) – (J4) become the conditions

(44)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},\theta_{1}+2\pi ik_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}+2\pi ik_{n})=\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}),

(45)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}(\lambda z_{1},\cdots,\lambda z_{n},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n})=\lambda^{-1}\cdot\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}),

(46)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},-\theta_{1},\cdots,-\theta_{n})=-\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}).

Note that (
46
) implies that both sides vanish along the zero section
M\subset X=T_{M}
where all
\theta_{k}=0
. It then follows that for all
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n

(47)

\frac{\partial W_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}-\frac{\partial W_{j}}{\partial\theta_{i}}=0.

Indeed, (
8
) shows that this expression is independent of the co-ordinates
\theta_{k}
, and so if it vanishes on the zero section it must be identically zero. It follows that there is a locally-defined function
W=W(z,\theta)
such that
W_{i}=\partial W/\partial\theta_{i}
. We again fix the integration constants by assuming that

W
vanishes on the zero section
M\subset X=T_{M}
. In view of (
7
) we therefore have

(48)

W(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},0,\cdots,0)=0=\frac{\partial W}{\partial\theta_{i}}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n},0,\cdots,0).

The equations (
40
) then follow from (
9
). Indeed (
9
) shows that the difference of the two sides is independent of the co-ordinates
\theta_{k}
, but by (
46
) and (
48
) both sides vanish along the zero section.
Similarly, we deduce (
42
) from (
45
), and (
43
) from (
46
).
∎

The function
W
is called the Plebański function, and the partial differential equations (
40
) are known as Plebański’s second heavenly equations.

4.
Twistor space

In this section we define the twistor space
p\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
associated to a pre-Joyce structure on a complex manifold
M
. It is defined as the space of leaves of a foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X
, where as before
X=T_{M}
denotes the total space of the tangent bundle of
M
. The construction coincides with the Penrose twistor space construction
[
39
]
applied to the complex hyperkähler manifold on
X
.
We explain some special properties enjoyed by the twistor space of a Joyce structure, and derive an equation describing the twistor lines. In the final part we use this equation to revisit the analogy between Joyce and Frobenius structures which was the main topic of
[
12
]
.

4.1.
Construction of the twistor space

Let

M
be a complex manifold equipped with a pre-Joyce structure, and set
X=T_{M}
. For each point
(\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
there is a bundle map

(49)

\epsilon_{0}v+\epsilon_{1}h\colon\pi^{*}(T_{M})\to T_{X},

whose image, which depends only on the corresponding point
\epsilon=[\epsilon_{0}:\epsilon_{1}]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, is a half-rank sub-bundle
H(\epsilon)\subset T_{X}
. The definition of a pre-Joyce structure ensures that this sub-bundle is closed under Lie bracket,
and hence defines a foliation on
X
. The twistor space
Z
defined below will have a map
p\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose fibre over the point
\epsilon\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the space of leaves of this foliation.

To give a global definition of the twistor space
Z
we first recall that the tangent bundle of the product
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X
has a canonical direct sum decomposition

(50)

T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X}=\pi_{1}^{*}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})\oplus\pi_{2}^{*}(X),

where
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
denote the projections from
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X
onto the two factors. There is a half-rank sub-bundle
H\subset\pi_{2}^{*}(T_{X})
which when restricted to a fibre
\pi_{1}^{-1}(\epsilon)
is the sub-bundle
H(\epsilon)\subset T_{X}
. Composing with the canonical inclusion
\pi_{2}^{*}(T_{X})\subset T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X}
we can view
H
as a sub-bundle of
T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X}
and this is easily seen to be closed under Lie bracket. The twistor space
Z
is then defined to be the space of leaves of the associated foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X
.

We denote by
q\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X\to Z
the quotient map. There is an induced projection
p\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
which satisfies
p\circ q=\pi_{1}
. We denote by
Z_{\epsilon}=p^{-1}(\epsilon)\subset Z
the twistor fibre over
\epsilon\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and
q_{\epsilon}\colon X\to Z_{\epsilon}
the induced quotient map. For each
\epsilon\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
there is a commutative diagram

(51)

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{\epsilon}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{1}}
\textstyle{Z_{\epsilon}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{\{\epsilon\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}^{1}}

in which the horizontal arrows are the obvious closed embeddings.

Each point
x\in X
determines a section of the map
p\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}

(52)

\sigma_{x}\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\to Z,\qquad\epsilon\mapsto q(\epsilon,x),

which we refer to as a twistor line.

Recall the complex hyperkähler structure
(g,I,J,K)
on
X
and the associated closed 2-forms
\Omega_{\pm}
and
\Omega_{I}
defined by (
19
). As in the proof of Theorem
2.7
, an easy calculation using (
13
) shows that

(53)

H(\epsilon)=\operatorname{im}\left(\epsilon_{0}v+\epsilon_{1}h\right)=\ker\left(\epsilon_{0}^{2}(J+iK)+2i\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{1}I+\epsilon_{1}^{2}(J-iK)\right).

Since the right-hand side is precisely the kernel of the closed 2-form

(54)

\epsilon_{0}^{2}\,\Omega_{+}+2i\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{1}\,\Omega_{I}+\epsilon_{1}^{2}\,\Omega_{-},

this 2-form descends along the map
q_{\epsilon}\colon X\to Z_{\epsilon}
, and defines a symplectic form
\Omega_{\epsilon}
on
Z_{\epsilon}
.

In more global terms, the formula (
54
) defines a twisted relative 2-form on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X
, namely a section of the bundle
\pi_{1}^{*}(\mathscr{O}(2))\otimes\pi_{2}^{*}(\wedge^{2}\,T_{X}^{*})
. This descends along the quotient map
q\colon X\to Z
to give a twisted relative symplectic form on the twistor space
p\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. By definition, this is the section
\Omega
of the bundle
p^{*}(\mathscr{O}(2))\otimes\wedge^{2}\,T_{Z/\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{*}
uniquely defined by the condition

(55)

q^{*}(\Omega)=\epsilon_{0}^{2}\,\Omega_{+}+2i\epsilon_{0}\epsilon_{1}\Omega_{I}+\epsilon_{1}^{2}\,\Omega_{-}.

From now on we will take the usual embedding
\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
with affine co-ordinate
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}/\epsilon_{0}
, and write
\mathbb{P}^{1}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
with
\infty=[0:1]
.
When
\epsilon\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
, the twistor fibre
Z_{\epsilon}
is the space of leaves of the foliation on
X
associated to the integrable distribution
\operatorname{im}(h_{\epsilon})\subset T_{X}
, where as before we write
h_{\epsilon}=h+\epsilon^{-1}v
. On the other hand the twistor fibre
Z_{0}
is the space of leaves of the foliation on
X
associated to the vertical sub-bundle
\operatorname{im}(v)=\ker(\pi_{*})
, and is therefore identified with
M
.

Restricting
\Omega
to a twistor fibre
Z_{\epsilon}
gives a complex symplectic form
\Omega_{\epsilon}
, well-defined up to multiplication by a nonzero constant. When
\epsilon\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\cup\{\infty\}
we fix this scale by taking

(56)

q_{\epsilon}^{*}(\Omega_{\epsilon})=\epsilon^{-2}\,\Omega_{+}+2i\epsilon^{-1}\,\Omega_{I}+\Omega_{-},

whereas for
\epsilon=0
we take
\Omega_{0}=\omega
. We then have relations

(57)

q_{0}^{*}(\Omega_{0})=\Omega_{+},\qquad q_{\infty}^{*}(\Omega_{\infty})=\Omega_{-}.

Remark 4.1
.

Unfortunately, to obtain a well-behaved twistor space
p\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
we cannot in general just take the space of leaves of the foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times X
. Rather, we should consider the holonomy groupoid, which leads to the analytic analogue of a Deligne-Mumford stack
[
38
]
. We will completely ignore these subtleties here, and essentially pretend that
Z
is a complex manifold. In fact, for what we do here, nothing useful would be gained by a more abstract point of view, because we are only really using the twistor space as a useful and suggestive shorthand. All statements we make about the space
Z
can be easily translated into statements only involving objects on
X
. For example, a symplectic form on the twistor fibre
Z_{\epsilon}
is nothing but a closed 2-form on
X
whose kernel coincides with the sub-bundle
H(\epsilon)\subset T_{X}
. Similarly, an étale map from the twistor fibre

5.3.
Another Plebański function

In Section
3.4
we described a Joyce structure in local co-ordinates using the Plebański function
W=W(z,\theta)
. For the sake of completeness we briefly discuss here an alternative generating function, also introduced by Plebański
[
40
]
. In the literature (see e.g.
[
19
]
) the function
W
is called the Plebański function of the second kind, whereas the function
U
introduced below is the Plebański function of the first kind.

Consider a Joyce structure on a complex manifold
M
. We can introduce
local co-ordinates on
X=T_{M}
by combining local Darboux co-ordinates
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
on the twistor fibre
Z_{0}=M
as in Section
2.2
, with the pullback of local Darboux co-ordinates
(\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n})
on the twistor fibre
Z_{\infty}
. Then by definition

(78)

\Omega_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot dz_{p}\wedge dz_{q},\qquad\Omega_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot d\phi_{p}\wedge d\phi_{q}.

To find an expression for
\Omega_{I}
in the co-ordinate system
(z_{i},\phi_{j})
, note that since
Z_{\infty}
is the quotient of
X
by the distribution spanned by the vector fields
h_{i}
of (
6
), we can write

(79)

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\bigg|_{\phi}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\bigg|_{\theta}+\sum_{p,q}\eta_{pq}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{i}\partial\theta_{p}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{q}},

where the subscripts indicate which variables are being held fixed. Then

(80)

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{r}}\bigg|_{\phi}\Big(\sum_{k}\omega_{ks}\theta_{k}\Big)=\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{r}\partial\theta_{s}},

and the symmetry of the right-hand side ensures that there is a locally-defined function
U=U(z_{i},\phi_{j})
on
X
satisfying the equations

(81)

\frac{\partial U}{\partial z_{s}}=\sum_{k}\omega_{ks}\theta_{k},\qquad\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial z_{r}\partial z_{s}}=\frac{\partial^{2}W}{\partial\theta_{r}\partial\theta_{s}}.

The formula (
25
) then becomes

(82)

2i\Omega_{I}=-d\Big(\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\theta_{p}\,dz_{q}\Big)=-\sum_{p,q}\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial\phi_{p}\partial z_{q}}\cdot d\phi_{p}\wedge dz_{q}.

After restricting to a fibre of
\pi\colon X\to M
the formula (
26
) shows that

(83)

\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot d\theta_{p}\wedge d\theta_{q}=\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot d\phi_{p}\wedge d\phi_{q},

which implies that

(84)

\sum_{p,q}\omega_{pq}\cdot\frac{\partial\theta_{p}}{\partial\phi_{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial\theta_{q}}{\partial\phi_{j}}=\omega_{ij}.

Using (
81
) we then obtain the relations

(85)

\sum_{r,s}\eta_{rs}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial\phi_{i}\partial z_{r}}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial\phi_{j}\partial z_{s}}=-\omega_{ij}

which are known as Plebański’s first heavenly equations.

6.
Hamiltonian systems

In this section we show how to use a Joyce structure to define a time-dependent Hamiltonian system. The construction depends on two additional pieces of data: a cotangent bundle structure on
Z_{0}
, and a Lagrangian submanifold
R\subset Z_{\infty}
. We give some motivation in the next section, where, in the particular case of Joyce structures of class
S[A_{1}]
, we relate our Hamiltonian systems to isomonodromy connections.

6.1.
Hamiltonian systems

We begin with the following definition.

Definition 6.1
.

A time-dependent Hamiltonian system consists of the following data:

(i)

a submersion
f\colon Y\to B
with a relative symplectic form
\Omega\in H^{0}(Y,\wedge^{2}\,T^{*}_{Y/B})
,

(ii)

a flat, symplectic connection
k
on
f
,

(iii)

a section
\varpi\in H^{0}(Y,f^{*}(T_{B}^{*}))
.

For each vector field
u\in H^{0}(B,T_{B})
there is an associated function

(86)

H_{u}=(f^{*}(u),\varpi)\colon Y\to\mathbb{C}.

There is then a pencil
k_{\epsilon}
of symplectic connections on
f
defined by

(87)

k_{\epsilon}(u)=k(u)+\epsilon^{-1}\cdot\Omega^{\sharp}(dH_{u}).

The system is called strongly-integrable if these connections are all flat.

These definitions become more familiar when expressed in local co-ordinates. Take co-ordinates
t_{i}
on the base
B
, which we can think of as times, and
k
-flat Darboux co-ordinates
(q_{i},p_{i})
on the fibres of
f
, so that
\Omega=\sum_{i}dq_{i}\wedge dp_{i}
. We can then write
\varpi=\sum_{i}H_{i}\,dt_{i}
and view the functions
H_{i}\colon Y\to\mathbb{C}
as time-dependent Hamiltonians. The connection
k_{\epsilon}
is then given by the flows

(88)

k_{\epsilon}\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}\Big)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\cdot\sum_{j}\Big(\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial p_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{j}}-\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial q_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}\Big).

The condition that the system is strongly-integrable is that for all
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n

(89)

\sum_{r,s}\Big(\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial q_{r}}\cdot\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial p_{s}}-\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial q_{s}}\cdot\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial p_{r}}\Big)=0,\qquad\frac{\partial H_{i}}{\partial t_{j}}=\frac{\partial H_{j}}{\partial t_{i}}.

For a nice exposition of Definition
6.1
see
[
5
, Section 5]
. Note that Boalch works in the real
C^{\infty}
setting, whereas we assume, as elsewhere in the paper, that all structures are holomorphic. Note also that Boalch assumes that
Y=M\times B
is a global product, with
M
a fixed symplectic manifold, and
k
the canonical connection on the projection
f\colon M\times B\to B
. We can always reduce to this case by passing to an open subset of
Y
.

6.2.
Hamiltonian systems from Joyce structures

Let
M
be a complex manifold with a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega
. By a cotangent bundle structure on

we mean the data of a complex manifold

and an open embedding

, such that
\omega
is the restriction of the canonical symplectic form on
T_{B}^{*}
. We denote by
\rho\colon M\to B

The Liouville 1-form on

restricts to a 1-form

on the open subset

Given local co-ordinates

on

(90)

\omega=\sum_{i}dt_{i}\wedge ds_{i},\qquad\beta=\sum_{i}s_{i}\cdot\rho^{*}(dt_{i}),\qquad\lambda=\sum_{i}s_{i}\,dt_{i}.

Let

(ii)

a Lagrangian submanifold

Set

Given the above data there is a strongly-integrable time-dependent Hamiltonian system on the map

To make condition (iii) more explicit, take a co-ordinates system

on

Each moduli space parameterises the indicated objects, and the maps
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
and
\pi_{i}
are the obvious projections. The map
\alpha
is the above-mentioned extension of the spectral correspondence, and the map
\beta_{\epsilon}
is defined by the rule

(100)

\beta_{\epsilon}(C,E,\nabla,\Phi)=(C,-\det(\Phi),E,\nabla-\epsilon^{-1}\Phi).

An important point is that
\alpha
is birational, and
\beta_{\epsilon}
is generically étale.

Given a point of
M=\mathcal{M}(C,Q)
, an anti-invariant line bundle with connection
(L,\partial)
on the spectral curve
\Sigma
has an associated holonomy representation
H_{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})^{-}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
. This determines
(L,\partial)
up to an action of the group of 2-torsion line bundles on
C
. We therefore obtain an étale map from
\mathcal{M}(C,Q,L,\partial)
to the space
X^{\#}
. The isomonodromy connection on the map
\pi_{1}
is a flat symplectic connection whose leaves consist of connections
(E,\nabla_{\epsilon})
with fixed monodromy. Pulling this connection through (
99
) gives a family of non-linear symplectic connections
h_{\epsilon}
on the projection
\pi\colon X^{\#}\to M
.
This gives rise to a meromorphic Joyce structure on
M
, with the poles arising because

\alpha
is only birational rather than an isomorphism, and
\beta_{\epsilon}
is only generically étale.

7.2.
Twistor space

Consider the twistor space
p\colon Z^{\#}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
associated to the Joyce structure of Section
7.1
. It could be an interesting problem to try to give a direct moduli-theoretic construction of this space. For now we can at least describe the fibre
Z_{1}
up to an étale cover. Note that since these Joyce structures are meromorphic, the connections
h_{\epsilon}
are only well-defined on the projection
\pi\colon X^{0}\to M
of an open subset
X^{0}\subset X=T_{M}
. We define the twistor fibres as the spaces of leaves of the resulting foliations of
X^{0}
.

Choose a reference surface
S_{g}
of genus
g
, set
G=\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and define

(101)

\operatorname{MCG}(g)=\pi_{0}(\operatorname{Diff}^{+}(S_{g})),\qquad\mathcal{X}(g)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm grp}(\pi_{1}(S_{g}),G)/G.

Then the mapping class group
\operatorname{MCG}(g)
acts on the character stack
\mathcal{X}(g)
in the usual way, and sending a quadruple
(C,E,\nabla)
to the monodromy of the connection
\nabla
defines a map

(102)

\mu\colon\mathcal{M}(C,E,\nabla)/J^{2}(C)\to\mathcal{X}(g)/\operatorname{MCG}(g),

whose fibres are by definition the horizontal leaves of the isomonodromy connection.
Transferring this map across the diagrams (
99
), and passing to the leaf space yields an étale map

(103)

\mu\colon Z_{\epsilon}^{\#}\to\mathcal{X}(g)/\operatorname{MCG}(g).

It would be interesting to test the speculations of Section
4.3
in this context. It has been suggested that the appropriate Darboux co-ordinates to consider on the character variety
\mathcal{X}(g)
are the Bonahon-Thurston shear co-ordinates
[
7
,
22
]
.
Let us instead consider spaces of quadratic differentials with poles of fixed orders. The corresponding Joyce structures will appear in
[
41
]
.
It is natural to expect that there is a similar map to (
103
) in which
\mathcal{X}(g)
is replaced with a space of framed local systems
[
23
]
. It is then expected that the preferred Darboux co-ordinates associated to a general half-plane
H(\varphi)
are the Fock-Goncharov co-ordinates for the WKB triangulation determined by the horizontal trajectories of the quadratic differential
e^{-2\pi i\varphi}\cdot Q
.
It follows from the work of Gaiotto, Moore and Neitzke
[
26
]
, and the results of
[
14
]
, that as
\varphi
varies these satisfy the jumps (
76
) determined by the DT theory of the corresponding category. The asymptotic property (
69
) should follow from existing results in exact WKB analysis. The whole story has been treated in full detail
[
13
]
in the special case discussed in Section
9
below.

7.3.
Hamiltonian systems

Let us consider the Hamiltonian system of Theorem
6.2
in the case of Joyce structures of Section
7.1
.
Recall that a crucial feature of the construction of these Joyce structures is the isomonodromy connection on the map

(104)

\pi_{1}\colon\mathcal{M}(C,E,\nabla_{1})\to\mathcal{M}(C),

which is both flat and symplectic.
To construct a Hamiltonian system we need a whole one-parameter family of such connections. In the notation of Section
6
, the isomonodromy connection is
k_{1}
, but we also need the connection
k_{\infty}
before we can write (
87
). This issue is often a little hidden in the literature because in many examples
there is a natural choice for the reference connection
k_{\infty}
which is then taken without further comment.
It is discussed explicitly in
[
29
]
, and is also mentioned for example in
[
6
, Remark 7.1]
.

One way to try to define a Hamiltonian system on the map (
104
) is to choose for each bundle
E
a distinguished ‘reference’ connection
\nabla_{\infty}
. Then we can define a Higgs field
\Phi=\nabla_{\infty}-\nabla_{1}
and for
\epsilon\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
a connection
\nabla_{\epsilon}=\nabla_{\infty}-\epsilon^{-1}\Phi
. We can then define
k_{\epsilon}
to be the isomonodromy connection for the family of connections
(E,\nabla_{\epsilon})
. Many interesting examples of isomonodromic systems in the literature involve bundles with meromorphic connections on a genus 0 curve. Since the generic such bundle
E
is trivial, it is then natural to take
\nabla_{\infty}=d
. But for bundles with connection on higher genus curves there is no such canonical choice.

Consider the meromorphic Joyce structures of Section
7.1
. Note that the base
M=\mathcal{M}(C,Q)
has a natural cotangent bundle structure, with
B=\mathcal{M}(C)
being the moduli space of curves of genus
g
, and
\rho\colon M\to B
the obvious projection
\rho\colon\mathcal{M}(C,Q)\to\mathcal{M}(C)
. Indeed, the tangent spaces to
\mathcal{M}(C)
are the vector spaces
\mathcal{T}_{C}\mathcal{M}(C)=H^{1}(C,T_{C})
, and Serre duality gives
H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}^{\otimes 2})=H^{1}(C,T_{C})^{*}
. Thus
T_{B}^{*}
parameterises pairs
(C,Q)
of a curve
C
together with a quadratic differential
Q\in H^{0}(C,\omega_{C}^{\otimes 2})
, and
M\subset T_{B}^{*}
is the open subset where
Q
has simple zeroes.

Let us choose a Lagrangian
R\subset\mathcal{X}(g)/\operatorname{MCG}(g)
. Pulling back by the étale map gives a Lagrangian in
Z_{\infty}
which we also denote by
R
. The subspace of the quotient
\mathcal{X}(g)/\operatorname{MCG}(g)
consisting of monodromy representations of connections on a fixed bundle
E
is known to be Lagrangian. Then for a generic bundle
E
we can expect these two Lagrangians to meet in a finite set of points, and so locally on the moduli of bundles we can define
\nabla_{\infty}
by insisting that its monodromy lies in
R
.

Using the natural cotangent bundle structure
\rho\colon M\to B
and the Lagrangian
R\subset Z_{\infty}
we can now apply the construction of Section
6.2
to these examples. We obtain a diagram

(105)

\textstyle{Y=q_{\infty}^{-1}(R)\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(C,E,\nabla,\Phi)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(C,E,\nabla_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(C)}

where the map
\beta_{1}
is defined by setting
\nabla_{1}=\nabla-\Phi
.

The isomonodromy connection defines a flat connection
h_{1}
on the map
\pi_{1}
. The Hamiltonian system of Theorem
6.2
defines a whole pencil of flat connections
k_{\epsilon}
on the map
f
. The transversality assumption ensures that the map
\beta_{1}\circ i
is étale, and the pullback of the connection
h_{1}
then coincides with
k_{1}
. Thus by choosing the Lagrangian
R\subset Z_{\infty}
and using it to define reference connections, we have upgraded the isomonodromy connection to a Hamiltonian system.

8.
The doubled A
1
example

In this section we discuss a simple and rather degenerate example of a pre-Joyce structure which does not quite satisfy the conditions to be a Joyce structure. It is related to the
DT theory of the A
1
quiver.

8.1.
Pre-Joyce structure

We take
M=\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}
with co-ordinates
(z,z^{\vee})
, and consider the total space of the tangent bundle
X=T_{M}
with corresponding co-ordinates
(z,z^{\vee},\theta,\theta^{\vee})
as in Section
2.2
.
We define a pre-Joyce structure on
M
by taking the symplectic form

(106)

\omega=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\cdot dz\wedge dz^{\vee},

and the pencil of connections
h+\epsilon^{-1}v
defined by

(107)

h_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\bigg)=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\frac{\theta}{2\pi iz}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\vee}},\qquad h_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\vee}}\bigg)=\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\vee}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta^{\vee}}.

The corresponding Plebański function is

(108)

W(z,z^{\vee},\theta,\theta^{\vee})=-\frac{\theta^{3}}{6(2\pi i)^{2}z}.

The formulae (
24
) - (
26
) become

(109)

\displaystyle\Omega_{+}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\cdot dz\wedge dz^{\vee},\qquad 2i\Omega_{I}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\big(d\theta\wedge dz^{\vee}-d\theta^{\vee}\wedge dz\big),

(110)

\displaystyle\Omega_{-}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\cdot d\theta\wedge d\theta^{\vee}-\frac{\theta}{(2\pi i)^{2}z}\cdot d\theta\wedge dz.

The above pre-Joyce structure was extracted from the DT theory of the A
1
quiver in
[
12
, Section 8]
by first applying a doubling procedure and then solving the resulting Riemann-Hilbert problem. The doubling procedure is required because the Euler form of the category is degenerate, and in fact identically zero.

8.2.
Period structure

We can define a period structure on
M
by declaring the co-ordinates
(z,z^{\vee})
to be integral linear. The resulting lattice
T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset T_{M}
is spanned by integral linear combinations of the vector fields
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\vee}}
. This is a homogeneous period structure, since the vector field

(111)

Z=z\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+z^{\vee}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\vee}}

generates the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action
t\cdot(z,z^{\vee})=(tz,tz^{\vee})
. The inverse
\eta\colon T_{M}^{*}\times T_{M}^{*}\to\mathscr{O}_{M}
of the symplectic form
\omega
satisfies
\eta(dz^{\vee},dz)=2\pi i
.

Combining the above pre-Joyce structure and period structure does not quite yield a Joyce structure. The problem is with axiom (J2): the connection (
107
) is not invariant under translations by the lattice
(2\pi i)\,T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}
since it depends on
\theta
rather than its exponential. This problem seems to be related to the doubling procedure referred to above.

8.3.
Alternative co-ordinates

There is another integral affine structure on
M
with integral affine co-ordinates

(112)

v=z,\qquad v^{\vee}=z^{\vee}-\frac{z}{2\pi i}\bigg(\log\Big(\frac{z}{2\pi i}\Big)-1\bigg).

The associated co-ordinates
(v,v^{\vee},\phi,\phi^{\vee})
on
X=T_{M}
are

(113)

\phi=\theta,\qquad\phi^{\vee}=\theta^{\vee}-\frac{\theta}{2\pi i}\cdot\log\Big(\frac{z}{2\pi i}\Big).

In this co-ordinate system the connection
h_{\epsilon}
takes the form

(114)

h_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\bigg)=\frac{\partial}{\partial v}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi},\qquad h_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\vee}}\bigg)=\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\vee}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi^{\vee}},

and hence the corresponding Plebański function
W(v,v^{\vee},\phi,\phi^{\vee})=0
.

Note however that since

(115)

Z=v\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial v}+\Big(v^{\vee}-\frac{v}{2\pi i}\Big)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\vee}},

we cannot combine this integral affine structure with the vector field
Z
to form a period structure.

8.4.
First Plebański function

It follows from (
114
) that the fibre co-ordinates
(\phi,\phi^{\vee})
are preserved by the connection
h=h_{\infty}
. By (
26
) they satisfy

(116)

\Omega_{-}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\cdot d\phi\wedge d\phi^{\vee}.

They therefore descend to Darboux co-ordinates on the twistor fibre
Z_{\infty}
.
The corresponding first Plebański function defined as in Section
5.3
is

(117)

U(z,z^{\vee},\phi,\phi^{\vee})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\bigg(z^{\vee}\phi-\phi^{\vee}z+\frac{\phi z}{2\pi i}-\frac{\phi z}{2\pi i}\cdot\log\Big(\frac{z}{2\pi i}\Big)\bigg).

It satisfies

(118)

\frac{\partial U}{\partial z^{\vee}}=\frac{\theta}{2\pi i},\qquad\frac{\partial U}{\partial z}=\frac{-\theta^{\vee}}{2\pi i}.

The Jacobian matrix has entries

(119)

\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial z\partial\phi}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log\Big(\frac{z}{2\pi i}\Big),\qquad\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial z^{\vee}\partial\phi}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}=-\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial z\partial\phi^{\vee}},\qquad\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial z^{\vee}\partial\phi^{\vee}}=0,

and has determinant
-1/(2\pi i)^{2}
as required by the first Plebański equation (
85
).

Remark 8.1
.

In this case the Jacobian matrix (
119
) is skew-symmetric. This means we can find
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}(z,z^{\vee})
such that

(120)

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial z}=\frac{\partial U}{\partial\phi},\qquad\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial z^{\vee}}=-\frac{\partial U}{\partial\phi^{\vee}}.

Then
\mathcal{F}
is closely related to the prepotential of
[
12
, Section 7]
. Explicitly we have

(121)

\mathcal{F}(z,z^{\vee})=\frac{zz^{\vee}}{2\pi i}-\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}z^{2}\log\Big(\frac{z}{2\pi i}\Big)-\frac{3}{4}z^{2}\bigg).

9.
The A
2
example

Here we consider a very interesting Joyce structure which is perhaps the simplest non-trivial example. It is related to the DT theory of the A
2
quiver, and is an example of a Joyce structure of class
S[A_{1}]
. For more details on this example we refer the reader to
[
13
]
.

9.1.
Period structure

The base of the Joyce structure is

(122)

M=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:4a^{3}+27b^{2}\neq 0\}.

Associated to a pair
(a,b)\in M
is a quadratic differential

(123)

Q_{0}(x)\,dx^{\otimes 2}=(x^{3}+ax+b)\,dx^{\otimes 2}

on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
which has a single pole of order 7 at
x=\infty
and simple zeroes. There is an associated double cover
\Sigma\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
which is the projectivization of the affine elliptic curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+ax+b
.

Take a basis of cycles
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\subset H_{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})
with intersection
\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}=1
. We shall need the periods and quasi-periods of the elliptic curve
\Sigma
. They are given by

(124)

\omega_{i}=\int_{\gamma_{i}}\frac{dx}{2y},\qquad\eta_{i}=-\int_{\gamma_{i}}\frac{x\,dx}{2y},

and satisfy the Legendre relation
\omega_{2}\eta_{1}-\omega_{1}\eta_{2}=2\pi i
.

There are local co-ordinates on
M
given by

(125)

z_{i}=\int_{\gamma_{i}}y\,dx=\int_{\gamma_{i}}\sqrt{x^{3}+ax+b\,}\,dx,

and relations

(126)

\frac{\partial}{\partial a}=-\eta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-\eta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}},\qquad\frac{\partial}{\partial b}=\omega_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+\omega_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}.

We define a period structure on
M
by declaring
(z_{1},z_{2})
to be the integral linear co-ordinates. We take the symplectic form

(127)

\omega=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}dz_{1}\wedge dz_{2}=da\wedge db.

Note that the inverse satisfies

(128)

\eta\big(dz_{1},dz_{2}\big)=2\pi i

and so axiom (J1) of Definition
3.4
is satisfied.

The Euler vector field is

(129)

Z=z_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}+z_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}=\frac{4a}{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}+\frac{6b}{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial b}.

Note that
Z
does not generate a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action on
M
. In fact the moduli space of quadratic differentials of the form (
123
) is the quotient
M/\mu_{5}
, where the group
\mu_{5}\subset\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts via
\zeta\cdot(a,b)=(\zeta^{4}a,\zeta^{6}b)
. The vector field
Z
generates an action of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
on this quotient.

9.2.
Joyce structure

We have local co-ordinates
(z_{i},\theta_{j})
on the tangent bundle
X=T_{M}
. We consider the quotient
X^{\#}=T_{M}/(2\pi i)\,T_{M}^{\mathbb{Z}}
. The fibre over a point
(a,b)\in M
is the cohomology group
H^{1}(\Sigma,\mathbb{C}^{*})\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
. We introduce some alternative co-ordinates on
X^{\#}
by the relation

(130)

\theta_{i}=-\int_{\gamma_{i}}\bigg(\frac{p}{x-q}+r\bigg)\frac{dx}{2y},

where
p^{2}=q^{3}+aq+b
and
r\in\mathbb{C}
.
The integral is well-defined up to multiples of
2\pi i
. The numbers
\xi_{i}=\exp(\theta_{i})
are the holonomies of a connection on the line bundle on
\Sigma
with divisor
(q,p)-\infty
.

In terms of the fibre co-ordinates
(\theta_{a},\theta_{b})
associated to the co-ordinates
(a,b)
we then have

(131)

\theta_{i}=-\eta_{i}\theta_{a}+\omega_{i}\theta_{b}.

The functions
(\theta_{a},\theta_{b})
are given explicitly by

(132)

\theta_{a}=-\frac{1}{4}\int^{(q,p)}_{(q,-p)}\frac{dx}{y},\qquad\theta_{b}=\frac{1}{4}\int^{(q,p)}_{(q,-p)}\frac{xdx}{y}-r.

The Joyce structure on
X=T_{M}
is obtained by taking the connection
h_{\epsilon}
given by

(133)

\displaystyle h_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2p}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial q}-\frac{q}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial a}-\frac{r}{p}\frac{\partial}{\partial q}-\frac{r^{2}(3q^{2}+a)-qpr}{2p^{3}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigg),

(134)

\displaystyle h_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial b}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial b}+\frac{r}{2p^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigg).

This is the isomonodromy connection for a
pencil of connections
\nabla-\epsilon^{-1}\Phi
on the trivial rank 2 bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with

(135)

\nabla=d-\begin{pmatrix}r&0\\
0&-r\end{pmatrix}\frac{dx}{2p},\qquad\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}p&x^{2}+xq+q^{2}+a\\
x-q&-p\end{pmatrix}dx.

Equivalently we can consider the deformed cubic oscillator

(136)

y^{\prime\prime}(x)=Q(x)y(x),\qquad Q(x)=\epsilon^{-2}Q_{0}(x)+\epsilon^{-1}Q_{1}(x)+Q_{2}(x),

where the terms in the potential are

(137)

Q_{1}(x)=\frac{p}{x-q}+r,\qquad Q_{2}(x)=\frac{3}{4(x-q)^{2}}+\frac{r}{2p(x-q)}+\frac{r^{2}}{4p^{2}}.

There is a rational expression for the Plebański function

(138)

W=\frac{1}{4(4a^{3}+27b^{2})p}\Big(2apr^{3}-(6aq^{2}-9bq+4a^{2})r^{2}-3p(3b-2aq)r-2ap^{2}\Big),

although note that this does not satisfy the second of the normalisation conditions (
48
). In other words, the uniquely-defined Plebański function of Lemma
3.4
differs from the above expression by a function of the form
\sum_{i}a_{i}(z)\cdot\theta_{i}
.

9.3.
Further properties

In the co-ordinates
(a,b,q,r)
the Euler vector field
E
is

(139)

E=\frac{4a}{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}+\frac{6b}{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial b}+\frac{2q}{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial q}+\frac{r}{5}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}.

The expressions (
126
) and (
131
) and a short calculation using (
132
) gives

(140)

2i\Omega_{I}=-da\wedge d\theta_{b}+db\wedge d\theta_{a}=dq\wedge dp+da\wedge dr.

The twistor fibre
Z_{\infty}
is the space of leaves of the foliation defined by the connection
h=h_{\infty}
. The functions

(141)

\phi_{1}=q+\frac{ar}{p},\qquad\phi_{2}=\frac{r}{2p},

descend to
Z_{\infty}

because they are constant along the flows (
133
) and (
134
) with
\epsilon=\infty
. Moreover, if we restrict to a fibre
F
of the projection
\pi\colon X=T_{M}\to M
by fixing
(a,b)
, then

(142)

-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\cdot d\theta_{1}\wedge d\theta_{2}\big|_{F}=d\theta_{a}\wedge d\theta_{b}\big|_{F}=-\frac{dr}{2p}\wedge dq\big|_{F}=d\phi_{1}\wedge d\phi_{2}\big|_{F}.

Thus we have

(143)

\Omega_{\infty}=d\phi_{1}\wedge d\phi_{2}.

The first Plebański function
U\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
of Appendix
5.3
is given by

(144)

U=\frac{1}{2}\int_{(q,-p)}^{(q,p)}(x^{3}+ax+b)^{1/2}\,dx,

because a simple calculation using the flows (
133
) and (
134
) shows that

(145)

\frac{\partial U}{\partial b}\bigg|_{\phi}=h\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial b}\Big)U=-\theta_{a},\qquad\frac{\partial U}{\partial a}\bigg|_{\phi}=h\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\Big)U=\theta_{b}.

Since the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action rescales
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
with weights
-\tfrac{2}{5}
and
\tfrac{2}{5}
respectively, we have

(146)

i_{E}(\Omega_{\infty})=i_{E}(d\phi_{1}\wedge d\phi_{2})=\frac{2}{5}d(\phi_{1}\phi_{2}).

The Joyce function
F
of Section
5.1
is given by

(147)

F=\frac{1}{5}(1-2\phi_{1}\phi_{2}),

where for the constant normalisation we used the limiting behaviour of
q,p,r
along the zero section
M\subset T_{M}
as discussed in
[
13
, Section 4.3]
.

The distinguished fixed point of
Z_{\infty}
is defined by
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=(0,\infty)
. It is an isolated fixed point. The linear Joyce connection on
M
is the one whose flat co-ordinates are
(a,b)
, and the negative and positive weight spaces
V_{-}
and
V_{+}
of Lemma
5.4
are spanned by
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial b}
respectively. The Joyce metric is

(148)

g=\frac{1}{5}\cdot(da\otimes db+db\otimes da).

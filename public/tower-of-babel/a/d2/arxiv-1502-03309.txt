Following the notations given in

, letting
\mathbb{V}
be the hyperplane,

\mathbb{V}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3};\;x+y+z=0\}

and the root system
R=\{\pm(e_{1}-e_{2}),\;\pm(e_{1}-e_{3}),\;\pm(e_{2}-e_{3})\}

C
the corresponding fundamental Weyl chamber,

C=\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3});\quad\lambda_{3}<\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{1}\}.

The Weyl group is isomorphic to the symmetric group
S_{3}
. The Dunkl operators are given by

T_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+k\sum_{1\leq j\neq i\leq 3}\frac{1-s_{i,j}}{x_{i}-x_{j}},\qquad i=1,2,3

where
k

s_{i,j}
acts on functions of vaiables
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
by interchanging the variables
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
.
The Dunkl kernel
E_{k}(.,y)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, characterized by being the unique solution of the following eigenvalue problem

T_{i}(E_{k}(.,y))(x)=y_{i}E_{k}(x,y);\qquad E(0,y)=0,\quad i=1,2,3.

Let
J_{k}
the generalized Bessel function associated with
R
and
k
, given by

\displaystyle J_{k}(x,y)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{\sigma\in G}E_{k}(\sigma.x,y).

(1.1)

The functions
J_{k}
are related to the ordinary modified Bessel functions
\mathcal{J}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}
by (see
[
1
]

\displaystyle J_{k}(\mu,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(3k)}{V(\lambda)^{2k-1}\Gamma(k)^{3}}\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}e^{\frac{(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-2\mu_{3})(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})}{2}}\mathcal{J}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})}{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})W_{k}(\mu,\lambda)d\nu_{1}d\nu_{2},

for all
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})\in\mathbb{V}\cap C
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
where

\displaystyle V(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})

\displaystyle W_{k}(\nu,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big((\lambda_{1}-\nu_{1})(\lambda_{1}-\nu_{2})(\lambda_{2}-\nu_{2})(\nu_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\nu_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\nu_{2}-\lambda_{3})\Big)^{k-1}.

Recall here that

\mathcal{J}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}(z)=\frac{\Gamma(2k)}{2^{2k-1}\Gamma(k)^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{zt}(1-t^{2})^{k-1}dt;\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}.

In the next section we shall use this fact to construct an integral formula for
E_{k}
. The following theorem is the main result of this article.

Theorem 1
.

The Dunkl kernel of type
A_{2}
has the following integral formula

\displaystyle E_{k}(\mu,\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(3k)}{V(\lambda)^{2k}\Gamma(k)^{3}}\int_{\lambda_{2}}^{\lambda_{1}}\int_{\lambda_{3}}^{\lambda_{2}}\bigg\{3(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})\mathcal{J}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})}{2}\right)

(1.2)

\displaystyle-6\Big(\nu_{1}\nu_{2}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{2}(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\Big)\mathcal{J}_{k-\frac{1}{2}}^{\prime}\left(\frac{(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})}{2}\right)\bigg\}

\displaystyle(\lambda_{3}-\nu_{1})(\lambda_{3}-\nu_{2})e^{\frac{(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}-2\mu_{3})(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})}{2}}W_{k}(\nu,\lambda)d\nu_{1}d\nu_{2},

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{V}\cap C
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Uncertainty principles associated with the short time quaternion coupled fractional Fourier transform
Bivek Gupta, Amit K. Verma, Ravi P. Agarwal

Abstract
In this paper, we extend the coupled fractional Fourier transform of a complex valued functions to that of the quaternion valued functions on ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} and call it the quaternion coupled fractional Fourier transform (QCFrFT). We obtain the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality for QCFrFT and obtain the associated Rènyi uncertainty principle. We also define the short time quaternion coupled fractional Fourier transform (STQCFrFT) and explore its important properties followed by the Lieb’s and entropy uncertainty principles.



Keywords
: Quaternion Coupled Fractional Fourier Transform; Short Time Quaternion Coupled Fractional Fourier Transform; Lieb’s Uncertainty Principle

Preliminaries

The quaternion algebra
\mathbb{H}=\{s=s_{0}+is_{1}+js_{2}+ks_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\},
where
i,j
and
k
are the imaginary units satisfying the Hamilton’s multiplication rule
i^{2}=j^{2}=k^{2}=-1,~ij=-ji=k,~jk=-kj=i,~ki=-ik=j
. For a quaternion
s=s_{0}+is_{1}+js_{2}+ks_{3},
we define the following terms

•

Sc(s)=s_{0},
called the scalar part of
s
which satisfies the cyclic multiplicative symmetry (
[
15
]
), i.e.,
Sc(qrs)=Sc(sqr)=Sc(rsq),~\forall~q,r,s\in\mathbb{H}.

•

the quaternion conjugate
\bar{s}=s_{0}-is_{1}-js_{2}-ks_{3},
which satisfies
\overline{rs}=\bar{r}\bar{s},~\overline{r+s}=\bar{s}+\bar{s},~\bar{\bar{s}}=s,~\forall~r,s\in\mathbb{H}.

•

the modulus
|s|=\sqrt{s\bar{s}}=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{3}s_{l}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},
which satisfies
|rs|=|r||s|,~\forall~r,s\in\mathbb{H}.

A
\mathbb{H}-
valued function
g
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is of the form
g(\boldsymbol{t})=g_{0}(\boldsymbol{t})+ig_{1}(\boldsymbol{t})+jg_{2}(\boldsymbol{t})+kg_{3}(\boldsymbol{t}),~\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
g_{0},g_{1},g_{2}
and
g_{3}
are real valued. The
L^{q}-
norm,
1\leq q<\infty,
of

is defined by

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g(\boldsymbol{t})|^{q}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(2)

and the collection of all measurable function with
\mathbb{H}-
valued having finite
L^{q}-
norm is a Banach space denoted by
L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the set of all essentially bounded measurable
\mathbb{H}-
valued functions with norm

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\mbox{ess~sup}_{\boldsymbol{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g(\boldsymbol{t})|.

(3)

Moreover, the
\mathbb{H}-
valued inner product

\displaystyle(g,h)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{h(\boldsymbol{t})}d\boldsymbol{t},

(4)

with symmetric real scalar part
\langle g,h\rangle=Sc\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(\boldsymbol{t})\overline{h(\boldsymbol{t})}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)

turns

to a Hilbert space, where the norm in (

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sqrt{\langle g,g\rangle}=\sqrt{(g,g)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g(\boldsymbol{t})|^{2}d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(5)

3
Quaternion Coupled Fractional Fourier transform (QCFrFT)

In this section we give a definition of two sided QCFrFT and study its important properties.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}),\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1},~\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. The QCFrFT of
f(\boldsymbol{x})\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),~\boldsymbol{x}=(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},
is defined by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})d\boldsymbol{x},~\boldsymbol{\omega}=(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}

(6)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})=\tilde{d}(\gamma_{1})e^{-i\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})(|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2})-\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\right\}}

(7)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})=\tilde{d}(\gamma_{2})e^{-j\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{2})(|\boldsymbol{x}_{2}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}|^{2})-\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\cdot M_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\right\}},

(8)

with
\gamma_{1}=\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2},~\delta_{1}=\frac{\alpha_{1}-\beta_{1}}{2},~\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})=\frac{\cos\gamma_{1}}{2},~\tilde{b}(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})=\frac{\cos\delta_{1}}{\sin\gamma_{1}},~\tilde{c}(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})=\frac{\sin\delta_{1}}{\sin\gamma_{1}},~\tilde{d}(\gamma_{1})=\frac{ie^{-i\gamma_{1}}}{2\pi\sin\gamma_{1}},

M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})&\tilde{c}(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})\\
-\tilde{c}(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})&\tilde{b}(\gamma_{1},\delta_{1})\end{pmatrix}

and

\gamma_{2}=\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2},~\delta_{2}=\frac{\alpha_{2}-\beta_{2}}{2},~\tilde{a}(\gamma_{2})=\frac{\cos\gamma_{2}}{2},~\tilde{b}(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})=\frac{\cos\delta_{2}}{\sin\gamma_{2}},~\tilde{c}(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})=\frac{\sin\delta_{2}}{\sin\gamma_{2}},~\tilde{d}(\gamma_{2})=\frac{je^{-j\gamma_{2}}}{2\pi\sin\gamma_{2}},

M_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{b}(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})&\tilde{c}(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})\\
-\tilde{c}(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})&\tilde{b}(\gamma_{2},\delta_{2})\end{pmatrix}.

The corresponding inversion formula is given by

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\overline{\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})}(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\overline{\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})}d\boldsymbol{\omega}

(9)

Remark 1
.

For
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\beta},
the kernels
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})
are the tensor product of two one-dimensional FrFT kernel. Thus, the QCFrFT reduces to the two sided QFrFT of the function
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).
Moreover, if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}),
the QCFrFT reduces to the two sided QFT of the function
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).

3.1
QCFrFT in terms of QFT

We now obtain an important relation between QCFrFT and QFT.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})f(\boldsymbol{x})\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})e^{-i\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{i\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}}\tilde{f}(\boldsymbol{x})\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{j\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\cdot M_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}\right\}\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})e^{-j\tilde{a}(\gamma_{2})|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}|^{2}},

where
\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})=\frac{ie^{-i\gamma_{1}}}{\sin\gamma_{1}},

\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})=\frac{je^{-j\gamma_{2}}}{\sin\gamma_{2}}
and

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(\boldsymbol{x})=e^{-i\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}|^{2}}f(\boldsymbol{x})e^{-j\tilde{a}(\gamma_{2})|\boldsymbol{x}_{2}|^{2}}.

(10)

Thus,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f)(\boldsymbol{\omega})

\displaystyle=\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})e^{-i\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\right)(-M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},-M_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})e^{-j\tilde{a}(\gamma_{2})|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}|^{2}}

(11)

where

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-i\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}}\tilde{f}(\boldsymbol{x})\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-j\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}}d\boldsymbol{x}

(12)

is the quaternion Fourier transform of the function in
\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Example of QCFrFT:

With the assumption that
\boldsymbol{\alpha},~\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies the conditions in definition
3.1
. We see that the QCFrFT
\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}
of the function

f(\boldsymbol{x})=e^{i\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}|^{2}}e^{-A|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}|^{2}}e^{-B|\boldsymbol{x}_{2}|^{2}}e^{j\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})|\boldsymbol{x}_{2}|^{2}},~A,B>0
is given as

\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\frac{1}{4AB}\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})e^{-i\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{|M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}}{A}+\frac{|M_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}|^{2}}{B}\right)}\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})e^{-j\tilde{a}(\gamma_{2})|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}|^{2}},

where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are the matrices given in definition
3.1
.

We now obtain the following important inequality, called the H-Y inequality, based on the relation (
11
) among QCFrFT and the QFT.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and
1\leq p\leq 2,

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1,
then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},

(13)

where
A_{p}=\left(\frac{p^{\frac{1}{p}}}{q^{\frac{1}{q}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Using relation (
11
), we get

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle=|\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})||\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})|\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\right)(-M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},-M_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})||\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})|}{\left(|\det(-M_{1})||\det(-M_{2})|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

Applying the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality (
[
23
]
) for the QFT, yields

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\|_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})||\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})|A_{p}^{4}}{\left(|\det(-M_{1})||\det(-M_{2})|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle=\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}A_{p}^{4}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{p}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

By virtue of (
10
) we obtain (
13
). This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

•

For
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\beta},
equation (

) reduces to the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality for the QFrFT of the function

•

For

In what follows we obtain the Parseval’s formula associated with the QCFrFT.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
f,g\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),
then

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f,\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}g\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle.

(14)

In particular,

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=\|\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(15)

Proof.

Applying Parseval’s formula for the QFT, we get

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{g}\rangle

\displaystyle=Sc\left[|\det(-M_{1})||\det(-M_{2})|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{f}\right)(-M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},-M_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})\overline{\left(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{H}}\tilde{g}\right)(-M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1},-M_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right].

Using relation (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{|\det(-M_{1})||\det(-M_{2})|}{|\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}Sc\left[\frac{1}{\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})}e^{i\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}}\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\overline{\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}g\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\frac{1}{\overline{\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})}}e^{-i\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}}\right]d\boldsymbol{\omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\det(-M_{1})||\det(-M_{2})|}{|\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{2})|^{2}|\tilde{d}_{0}(\gamma_{1})|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}Sc\left[\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\overline{\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}g\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})}\right]d\boldsymbol{\omega}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f,\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}g\rangle.

Again, using equation (
10
), it can be shown that
\langle\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle.
Hence (
14
) follows. With
f=g,
in (
14
), we have (
15
).

This finishes the proof.

3.2
Rènyi entropy uncertainty principle

The Rènyi entropy UPs for the proposed QCFrFT is obtained in this subsection. Similar findings for the complex FrFT can be seen in
[
13
]
. These UPs for the QPFT and two sided quaternion QPFT have recently been discovered in
[
27
]
and
[
14
]
,
[
7
]
respectively. We recall the following.

Definition 3.2
.

[
11
,
13
]

If
P
is a probability density function on
\mathbb{R}^{n},
then the Rènyi entropy of
P
is defined by

\displaystyle H_{s}(P)=\frac{1}{1-s}\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[P(\boldsymbol{x})]^{s}d\boldsymbol{x}\right),~s>0,s\neq 1.

(16)

If
s\rightarrow 1,
then (
16
) results in the Shannon entropy given by

\displaystyle E(P)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P(\boldsymbol{x})\log[P(\boldsymbol{x})]d\boldsymbol{x}.

(17)

In what follows, we obtain the Rènyi entropy UP for QCFrFT.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),

\frac{1}{2}<\alpha_{0}<1
and
\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{0}}=2,
then

\displaystyle H_{\alpha_{0}}(|f|^{2})+H_{\beta_{0}}\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\right)\geq\frac{2}{\alpha_{0}-1}\log(2\alpha_{0})+\frac{2}{\beta_{0}-1}\log(2\beta_{0})+2\log\left(\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|\right).

Proof.

By inequality (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}d\boldsymbol{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(18)

Putting
p=2\alpha_{0}
and
q=2\beta_{0},
in equation (
18

(19)

Since

(20)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{0}}\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{2\alpha_{0}}d\boldsymbol{x}\right)+\frac{1}{1-\beta_{0}}\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2\beta_{0}}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right).

4
Short time quaternion coupled fractional Fourier transform

The two sided short time quaternion coupled fractional Fourier transform (STQCFrFT) is defined and its properties are examined in this section .

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}),\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1},~\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}\notin 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. The STQCFrFT of a function
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
with respect to
g\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),
called a quaternion window function (QWF), is defined by

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})f(\boldsymbol{x})\overline{g(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{t})}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})d\boldsymbol{x},~(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4},

(23)

where
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})
are given respectively by (
7
) and (
8
).

It is to be noted that if the kernels
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})
both lies together to the left or to the right of
f(\boldsymbol{x})\overline{g(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{t})}
in the integral in (
23
), then we have the left or right sided STQCFrFT. But our study mainly focus on the two sided STQCFrFT.

Remark 4
.

For
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\beta},
the kernels
\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})
are the tensor product of two one-dimensional FrFT. Thus, the STQCFrFT reduces to the STQFrFT of the function
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).
Moreover, if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}),
the STQCFrFT reduces to the STQFT of the function
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).

We mention below a lemma that will be used in proving some basic properties of the STQCFrFT.

Let

\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})
and
\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1}+\boldsymbol{k}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})=e^{-i\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})\left(|\boldsymbol{k}_{1}|+2\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\right)-\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\right\}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}),

(24)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2}+\boldsymbol{k}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2})=e^{-j\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{2})\left(|\boldsymbol{k}_{2}|+2\boldsymbol{x}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{2}\right)-\boldsymbol{k}_{2}\cdot M_{2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}\right\}}\mathcal{K}^{j}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{2},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{2}).

(25)

From the definition of

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1}+\boldsymbol{k}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})

\displaystyle=\tilde{d}(\gamma_{1})e^{-i\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})\left(|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}+\boldsymbol{k}_{1}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}\right)-(\boldsymbol{x}_{1}+\boldsymbol{k}_{1})\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\right\}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{d}(\gamma_{1})e^{-i\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})\left(|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{k}_{1}|^{2}+2\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{1}+|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}\right)-\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}-\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\right\}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{d}(\gamma_{1})e^{-i\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})\left(|\boldsymbol{x}_{1}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}|^{2}\right)-\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\right\}}e^{-i\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})\left(|\boldsymbol{k}_{1}|^{2}+2\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\right)-\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\right\}},

\displaystyle\mbox{i.e.,}~\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1}+\boldsymbol{k}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1})

\displaystyle=e^{-i\left\{\tilde{a}(\gamma_{1})\left(|\boldsymbol{k}_{1}|+2\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\right)-\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\cdot M_{1}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}\right\}}\mathcal{K}^{i}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}(\boldsymbol{x}_{1},\boldsymbol{\omega}_{1}).

Lemma 4.5

Proof.

Using the definition

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H}}\{f(\cdot)\overline{g(\cdot-\boldsymbol{t})}\}\right)(\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(32)

Using Hausdorff-Young inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq A_{p}^{4}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|f(\boldsymbol{x})\overline{g(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{t})}\right|^{p}d\boldsymbol{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=A_{p}^{4}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}|\tilde{g}(\boldsymbol{t}-\boldsymbol{x})|^{p}d\boldsymbol{x}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},~\tilde{g}(\boldsymbol{x})=g(-\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=A_{p}^{4}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left\{\left(|f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right)(\boldsymbol{t})\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\leq A_{p}^{4q}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{2-q}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{2-q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left\{\left(|f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right)(\boldsymbol{t})\right\}^{\frac{q}{p}}d\boldsymbol{t}.

This implies

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq A_{p}^{4q}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{2-q}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{2-q}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left\{\left(|f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right)(\boldsymbol{t})\right\}^{\frac{q}{p}}d\boldsymbol{t}\right]^{\frac{p}{q}\cdot\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=A_{p}^{4}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left\||f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{\frac{q}{p}}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(33)

We observe that, if
l=\frac{q}{p},~k=\frac{2}{p},
then
k\geq 1
and
1+\frac{1}{l}=\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{k}.

Since
|\tilde{g}|^{p},~|f|^{p}\in L^{k}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),
by Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\||f|^{p}\star|\tilde{g}|^{p}\right\|^{\frac{1}{p}}_{L^{\frac{q}{p}}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq A_{k}^{8}A_{l^{\prime}}^{4}\|f\|^{p}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|\tilde{g}\|^{p}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(34)

Thus, by virtue of (
4.1
) and (
34
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left(A_{p}^{2}A_{k}^{\frac{4}{p}}A_{l^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{p}}\right)^{2}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},

(35)

where
A_{r}=\left(\frac{r^{\frac{1}{r}}}{r^{\prime\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1.

Now, we have

\displaystyle A_{p}^{2}A_{k}^{\frac{4}{p}}A_{l^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{\frac{1}{p}}}{q\frac{1}{q}}\cdot\frac{k}{{k^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{k^{\prime}p}}}\cdot\frac{{l^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{pl^{\prime}}}}{\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}},~\mbox{since}~k=\frac{2}{q},~l=\frac{q}{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{q^{\frac{2}{q}}}\cdot\frac{{l^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{pl^{\prime}}}}{{k^{\prime}}^{\frac{2}{k^{\prime}p}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{q^{\frac{2}{q}}}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{q-p}{pq}},~\mbox{since}~k^{\prime}=2l^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}.

(36)

Thus using equation (
4.1
) in (
35
), we get

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{2}{q}-1}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{4}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

This completes the proof.

4.2
Lieb’s uncertainty principle

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable. A function
G\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
\epsilon-
concentrated,
\epsilon\geq 0,
on
\Omega
if

\|\chi_{\Omega^{c}}G\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\epsilon\|G\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\chi_{\Omega}
takes the value
1
on
\Omega
and
0
otherwise.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
0\neq f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and
g
be a QWF. If
\epsilon\geq 0
and
\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f
is
\epsilon-
concentrated on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4},
then

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{8}{q-2}},~q>2

(37)

where
|\Omega|
denoted the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{S}^{\wedge_{1},\wedge_{2}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f
is
\epsilon-
concentrated on
\Omega,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Omega^{c}}\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq\epsilon^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

This gives

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\geq(1-\epsilon^{2})\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(38)

By Holder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(\chi_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right)^{\frac{q}{q-2}}d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\}^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}\right\}^{\frac{2}{q}}

\displaystyle=|\Omega|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\left\|\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{q}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

Using, the Lieb’s inequality (
31
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{S}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathbb{H},g}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq|\Omega|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{4}{q}-2}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{4}{q}-2}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{8}{q}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(39)

From equation (
38
) and equation (
39
), we get

\displaystyle|\Omega|^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{4}{q}-2}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{\frac{4}{q}-2}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{\frac{8}{q}}\geq(1-\epsilon^{2}).

This gives

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\left(\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|\right)^{2\left(1-\frac{2}{q}\right)\frac{q}{q-2}}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{8}{q-2}},~\mbox{since}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1

\displaystyle\mbox{i.e.,}~|\Omega|\geq\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}(1-\epsilon^{2})^{\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\frac{q}{2}\right)^{\frac{8}{q-2}}.

This proves (
37
).
∎

4.3
Entropy uncertainty principle

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
f\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and
g
be a QWF with
\|f\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=1,
then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})\geq 2\left[2+\log\left(\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|\right)\right],

(41)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\right)d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle I(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},q)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{q}d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}.

(42)

Then using (
42
) in (
30
), we get

\displaystyle I(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},2)=1.

(43)

Also, from (
31
) and (
43
), it can be shown that

\displaystyle I(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},q)\leq\left(\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|\right)^{2-q}\left(\frac{2}{q}\right)^{4}.

(44)

For
s>0,
define

\displaystyle R(s)=\frac{I(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},2)-I(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},2+2s)}{s}.

Then

\displaystyle R(s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{s}\left\{1-\left(\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|\right)^{-2s}\left(\frac{1}{1+s}\right)^{4}\right\}.

(45)

Assume that
\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta})<\infty,
otherwise (
41
) is obvious.

Now from the inequality
1+s\log a\leq a^{s},~s>0,
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{1}{s}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\left(1-\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2s}\right)\leq-\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\right).

(46)

Since,
-\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\log\left(\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\right)
is integrable, using Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem in (
46
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}R(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}\left\{\frac{1}{s}\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2}\left(1-\left|\left(\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{H},g}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}f\right)(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{\omega})\right|^{2s}\right)\right\}d\boldsymbol{t}d\boldsymbol{\omega}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{S}(f,g,\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}).

(47)

Again from (
45
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0+}R(s)\geq 2\left[2+\log\left(\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}}{2}\right)\right|\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}}{2}\right)\right|\right)\right].

(48)

Thus, by virtue of (
4.3
) and (
48
), equation (
41
) follows. This finishes the proof.
∎

Conclusions

In this paper, we have defined the two sided QCFrFT and obtained the Parseval’s formula and the sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality based on which we obtained its Rènyi entropy UP. Including the basic properties like boundedness, translation, etc of the newly proposed STQCFrFT, we also obtain the inner product relation followed by the reconstruction formula. Not only that with the aid of sharp Hausdorff-Young inequality for the QCFrFT we also obtained the Lieb’s and entropy UPs for the proposed STQCFrFT.

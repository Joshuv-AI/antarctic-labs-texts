Estimates for solutions of homogeneous time-delay systems: Comparison of Lyapunov–Krasovskii and Lyapunov–Razumikhin techniques
G. Portilla a , I.V. Alexandrova b , S. Mondié a and A.P. Zhabko b

Abstract
In this contribution, the estimates for the response of time delay systems with nonlinear homogeneous right-hand side of degree strictly greater than one are constructed. The existing results obtained via the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach are reminded. Their proofs, revisited in the appendix, lead to explicit expressions of the involved constants. Based on a recently introduced Lyapunov–Krasovskii functional and known estimates of the domain of attraction, we present new estimates of the system response. We compare both approaches and discuss the illustrative examples.

homogeneous time-delay systems, estimates for solutions, Lyapunov–Krasovskii functionals

Introduction

Stability analysis via the linear approximation, when it is nonsingular, is usually the first choice method for studying nonlinear systems
(
Halanay (1966)
)
.
This is true in particular for systems with delays that have received outstanding attention in the past decades
(
Gu et al. (2003)
)
.
When the linear approximation is zero, it is possible to perform the analysis by the first homogeneous approximation. This strategy has lead to significant contributions to the design of controllers
(
Hermes (1991)
)
, and to robust stability analysis
(
Rosier (1992)
)
.

Authors usually favour the Lyapunov–Razumikhin framework for studying the homogeneous time delay systems
(
Efimov, Perruquetti & Richard (2014)
)
. In particular, delay-independent stability of homogeneous systems is addressed in
Aleksandrov & Zhabko (2012)
;
Aleksandrov et al. (2014)
;
Efimov et al. (2016)
. The approach was applied to the estimation of the system response in
Aleksandrov & Zhabko (2012)
and of the attraction region in
Aleksandrov et al. (2014)
. It indeed appears natural to use the Lyapunov function of the corresponding delay-free system when applying the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach to the homogeneous delay system. This strategy has established a remarkable result: if the delay-free system is asymptotically stable, then the trivial solution of the homogeneous delay system is asymptotically stable for all delays when the homogeneity degree is strictly greater than one
(
Aleksandrov & Zhabko (2012)
)
.

A Lyapunov–Krasovskii functional for systems of homogeneity degree strictly greater than one was recently introduced in

Aleksandrov et al. (2015)
;
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
. It is built from the homogeneous delay free system
Lyapunov function, following ideas of the so-called complete type
functionals approach
(
Kharitonov (2013)
)
. It has been successfully used to present
necessary and sufficient conditions for local stability and to give estimates of the
region of attraction
(
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
)
.

The same case of homogeneity degree strictly greater than one is addressed in this contribution.
Our aim is to find estimates of the solutions via the Lyapunov–Krasovskii functional introduced in
Aleksandrov et al. (2015)
;
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
and to compare them with those obtained via the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach
(
Aleksandrov & Zhabko (2012)
;
Aleksandrov, Aleksandrova & Zhabko (2016)
)
.
Comparison of the domains of attraction obtained via both approaches
(
Aleksandrov et al. (2014)
;
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
)
is also presented. It is worth clarifying that the previously mentioned results concern local asymptotic stability, hence so do the presented results. For global asymptotic stability, stronger conditions, as those presented in
Efimov, Perruquetti & Richard (2014)
, are required. Existing results derived via the Razumikhin theorem are revisited in the appendix. In contrast with the original works
(
Aleksandrov & Zhabko (2012)
;
Aleksandrov et al. (2014)
;
Aleksandrov, Aleksandrova & Zhabko (2016)
)
, explicit formulae for all the involved constants are given, thus allowing the comparison.

To the best of our knowledge, few studies address direct practical comparison of the Lyapunov–Krasovskii and the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approaches. This work may be considered as a case study in this direction. Moreover, it is often assumed that the Lyapunov–Krasovskii approach makes possible the presentation of quantitative estimates of the convergence rate, whereas the Lyapunov–Razumikhin method only gives qualitative bounds
(
Efimov, Polyakov et al. (2014)
)
. Our analysis shows that for homogeneous time delay systems this does not hold. The structure of the estimates obtained via both approaches is the same, and they both depend on the Lyapunov function of the original system with zero delay. This common feature allows us to compare the involved constants directly. Furthermore, since the Lyapunov–Krasovskii functionals in use are those corresponding to the necessary and sufficient stability conditions, the comparison is expected to be revealing, in line with the linear case where the use of exact, prescribed derivative, functionals may lead to obtention of tighter estimates. The conclusion is quite surprising: although the estimate of the domain of attraction may be less conservative for the Lyapunov–Krasovskii approach,
the estimate of the response obtained via Lyapunov–Razumikhin one turns out to be much closer to the system response.

The contribution is organised as follows. In Section II, basic definitions and properties of homogeneous systems are
introduced. We also present the existing estimates of the domain of attraction and of convergence rate obtained via the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach. Estimates of the solutions using the Lyapunov–Krasovskii approach are presented in Section III. Section IV is devoted to an illustrative example. The paper ends with discussion in Section V, where we compare the results of both approaches.
The proof for the estimates obtained via the Razumikhin approach is given in the appendix.

2
Previous results

In this paper, we study a homogeneous time delay system of the form

\dot{x}(t)=f(x(t),x(t-h)),

(1)

where
x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

h>0
is a constant delay, the vector function
f(x,y)
is continuously differentiable and homogeneous of degree
\mu>1
, i.e.

f(cx,cy)=c^{\mu}f(x,y)\quad\forall c>0,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The initial condition is

x(\theta)=\varphi(\theta),\quad\varphi\in C([-h,0],\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Consider also the delay free system

\dot{z}(t)=f(z(t),z(t)).

(2)

In the sequel, system (
2
) is assumed to be asymptotically stable.

2.1
Properties of homogeneous systems

A homogeneous function
f(x,y)
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, admits a bound of the form
(
Zubov (1964)
)

\displaystyle\|f(x,y)\|\leq m\left(\|x\|^{\mu}+\|y\|^{\mu}\right),\quad\text{where}

(3)

\displaystyle m\geq\max_{\|x\|^{\mu}+\|y\|^{\mu}=1}\|f(x,y)\|>0.

Its derivative consists of the homogeneous functions of degree
\mu-1,
hence there exist constants
m_{1},m_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial f(x,y)}{\partial x}\right\|\leq m_{1}\left(\|x\|^{\mu-1}+\|y\|^{\mu-1}\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial f(x,y)}{\partial y}\right\|\leq m_{2}\left(\|x\|^{\mu-1}+\|y\|^{\mu-1}\right).

(5)

Since the delay free system (
2
) is asymptotically stable,
it is possible to find a twice continuously differentiable positive definite Lyapunov function
V(x)

(
Zubov (1964)
;
Rosier (1992)
)
which is homogeneous of degree
\gamma\geq 2,
and thus admits bounds of the form

k_{0}\|x\|^{\gamma}\leq V(x)\leq k_{1}\|x\|^{\gamma},

(6)

where
k_{0},k_{1}>0.
Furthermore, the time derivative of
V(x)
along the solutions of system (
2
) satisfies the equation

\left(\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x}\right)^{T}f(x,x)\leq-\mathrm{w}\|x\|^{\gamma+\mu-1},\quad\mathrm{w}>0.

(7)

Since the components of
\dfrac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x}
and
\dfrac{\partial^{2}V(x)}{\partial x^{2}}
are the homogeneous functions of degrees
\gamma-1
and
\gamma-2\geq 0,
respectively, there exist constants
k_{2},k_{3}>0
such that

\left\|\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x}\right\|\leq k_{2}\|x\|^{\gamma-1},\ \ \left\|\frac{\partial^{2}V(x)}{\partial x^{2}}\right\|\leq k_{3}\|x\|^{\gamma-2}.

(8)

In the considerations below, an arbitrary Lyapunov function
V(x)
which possesses all the above mentioned properties in a neighborhood
\|x\|\leq H,

H>0,
may be used.

Theorem 1
.

(
Aleksandrov et al. (2014)
)
.
Let
\mu>1.
If system (
2
) is asymptotically stable, then the trivial solution of the delay system (
1
) is also asymptotically stable for any delay
h\geq 0.

2.2
A review on existing estimates via Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach

Here, we recall the estimates of the domain of attraction and of the system response obtained via the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach in
Aleksandrov & Zhabko (2012)
;
Aleksandrov et al. (2014)
;
Aleksandrov, Aleksandrova & Zhabko (2016)
, where the Lyapunov function of the delay free system (
2
) is used. For the sake of completeness and for comparison purposes, we include the detailed proofs into the appendix, where we provide explicit formulae for all involved constants.

Given
\alpha>1,
introduce the Razumikhin condition

V(x(\xi))<\alpha V(x(t)),\quad\forall\,\xi\in[t-2h,t],\quad\forall\,t\geq h.

(9)

It is shown in
Aleksandrov et al. (2014)
that there exist
\delta>0
and
k_{5}=k_{5}(\delta)>0
such that the time derivative of
V(x)
satisfies

\frac{dV(x(t))}{dt}\leq-k_{5}\|x(t)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}

(10)

along the solutions of system (
1
) which obey the Razumikhin condition (
9
) and
\|x_{t}\|_{h}\leq\delta.
Define the values

\displaystyle\kappa=\left(\frac{k_{0}}{k_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}},\quad K=\Bigl(1+(\mu-1)mh(\kappa\delta)^{\mu-1}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}.

Theorem 2
.

(
Aleksandrov et al. (2014)
)
.
Let
\Delta
be the root of the equation

\Delta+mh\Delta^{\mu}=\dfrac{\kappa\delta}{K}.

(11)

If system (
2
) is asymptotically stable, then the set of initial functions satisfying
\|\varphi\|_{h}<\Delta

is contained in the attraction region of the trivial solution of system (
1
).

Remark 1
.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
2
that if
\|\varphi\|_{h}<\Delta,
then
\|x(t,\varphi)\|<\delta
for any
t\geq 0.

Theorem 3
.

(
Aleksandrov & Zhabko (2012)
;
Aleksandrov, Aleksandrova & Zhabko (2016)
)
.
If system (
2
) is asymptotically stable, then there exist
\tilde{c}_{1},

\tilde{c}_{2}>0
such that the solutions of system (
1
) with
\|\varphi\|_{h}<\Delta,
where
\Delta
is the root of equation (
11
), admit an estimate of the form

\|x(t,\varphi)\|\leq\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}\|\varphi\|_{h}}{(1+\tilde{c}_{2}\|\varphi\|_{h}^{\mu-1}t)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}},\quad t\geq 0.

(12)

The constants
k_{5},

\delta,

\tilde{c}_{1},

\tilde{c}_{2}
are specified in the Appendix.

3
Estimates via Lyapunov–Krasovskii approach

The purpose of this section is to present the estimates for solutions via the Lyapunov–Krasovskii approach.

3.1
General Case

We use the functional introduced in
Aleksandrov et al. (2015)
;
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
:

\displaystyle v(\varphi)=V(\varphi(0))+\left(\left.\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x}\right|_{x=\varphi(0)}\right)^{T}\int_{-h}^{0}f(\varphi(0),\varphi(\theta))d\theta

(13)

\displaystyle+\int_{-h}^{0}(\mathrm{w_{1}}+(h+\theta)\mathrm{w_{2}})\|\varphi(\theta)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}d\theta.

Here,
\mathrm{w}_{1},\mathrm{w}_{2}>0
are such that
\mathrm{w}_{0}=\mathrm{w}-\mathrm{w}_{1}-h\mathrm{w}_{2}>0
. It is well known that the Lyapunov functional (
13
) is a measure of the states of the system, and that its lower and upper bounds, which we present below, are crucial to find the domain of attraction and the estimates of the solutions.

Lemma 1
.

(
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
)
There exist
a_{1},a_{2}>0
such that functional (
13
) admits a lower bound of the form

v(\varphi)\geq a_{1}\|\varphi(0)\|^{\gamma}+a_{2}\int_{-h}^{0}\|\varphi(\theta)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}d\theta

(14)

in the neighbourhood
\|\varphi\|_{h}\leq\delta.
Here,
\delta\in(0,H_{1}),

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{1}&=\left(\frac{k_{0}}{hk_{2}m(1+\chi^{-2\mu})}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}},\\
a_{1}&=k_{0}-hk_{2}m(1+\chi^{-2\mu})\delta^{\mu-1},\\
a_{2}&=\mathrm{w}_{1}-k_{2}m\chi^{2(\gamma-1)}.\end{split}

The constant
\chi>0
is chosen in such a way that
a_{2}>0.

Lemma 2
.

There exist
b_{1},b_{2}>0
such that functional (
13
) admits an upper bound of the form

v(\varphi)\leq b\left(\|\varphi(0)\|^{\gamma}+\int_{-h}^{0}\|\varphi(\theta)\|^{\gamma}d\theta\right),\quad\text{if}\quad\|\varphi\|_{h}\leq\delta.

(15)

Here,
b=\max\{b_{1},b_{2}\},\,

b_{1}=k_{1}+2hmk_{2}\delta^{\mu-1},

\,b_{2}=(mk_{2}+\mathrm{w}_{1}+h\mathrm{w}_{2})\delta^{\mu-1}.

Proof.

We estimate each summand of the functional. First,

\displaystyle I_{1}(\varphi)=V(\varphi(0))\leq k_{1}\|\varphi(0)\|^{\gamma},

\displaystyle I_{3}(\varphi)=\int_{-h}^{0}(\mathrm{w_{1}}+(h+\theta)\mathrm{w_{2}})\|\varphi(\theta)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}d\theta\leq(\mathrm{w}_{1}+h\mathrm{w}_{2})\int_{-h}^{0}\|\varphi(\theta)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}d\theta.

Second, it follows from (
8
) and (
3
) that

\displaystyle I_{2}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\left(\left.\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x}\right|_{x=\varphi(0)}\right)^{T}\int_{-h}^{0}f(\varphi(0),\varphi(\theta))d\theta

\displaystyle\leq hk_{2}m\|\varphi(0)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}+k_{2}m\|\varphi(0)\|^{\gamma-1}\!\!\!\int_{-h}^{0}\!\!\|\varphi(\theta)\|^{\mu}d\theta.

Using the inequality
d^{p}g^{q}\leq d^{p+q}+g^{p+q},

\,p,q\geq 1,

\,d,g\geq 0
, we have

I_{2}(\varphi)\leq 2hk_{2}m\|\varphi(0)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}+k_{2}m\int_{-h}^{0}\|\varphi(\theta)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}d\theta.

Combining all summands together, we obtain

v(\varphi)\leq(k_{1}+2hmk_{2}\|\varphi(0)\|^{\mu-1})\|\varphi(0)\|^{\gamma}+(mk_{2}+\mathrm{w}_{1}+h\mathrm{w}_{2})\int_{-h}^{0}\|\varphi(\theta)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}d\theta.

Taking into account that
\|\varphi\|_{h}\leq\delta
, we arrive at (
15
).
∎

It follows from the proof of Lemma
2
that
v(\varphi)
also admits an upper bound of the form

\displaystyle v(\varphi)\leq k_{1}\|\varphi(0)\|^{\gamma}+\beta\|\varphi\|_{h}^{\gamma+\mu-1},

(16)

where
\beta=(2k_{2}m+\mathrm{w}_{1}+h\mathrm{w}_{2})h.

Now, we present the time derivative of the functional (
13
) along the solutions of system (
1
). Notice that the positive constants
\mathrm{w}_{0},\mathrm{w}_{1},\mathrm{w}_{2}
play an essential role in achieving the above lower bound, and also in establishing below the negativity of the derivative, in line with complete type functionals for linear case
(
Kharitonov (2013)
)
.

Lemma 3
.

(
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
)

The time derivative of functional
v(\varphi)
along the solutions of system (
1
) satisfies

\frac{dv(x_{t})}{dt}\leq-c\left(\|x(t)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}+\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}d\theta\right),

(17)

if
\|x_{t}\|_{h}\leq\delta.
Here,
c=\min\{c_{1},c_{2}\},\,

c_{1}=\mathrm{w}_{0}-4hL\delta^{\mu-1},

\,c_{2}=\mathrm{w}_{2}-2L\delta^{\mu-1},

\,L=mm_{1}k_{2}+m^{2}k_{3},
and
\delta\in(0,H_{2}),
where

H_{2}=\left(\min\left\{\frac{\mathrm{w}_{0}}{4hL},\frac{\mathrm{w}_{1}}{2hL},\frac{\mathrm{w}_{2}}{2L}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}.

Lemmas
1
and
2
allow proving the following result on the estimate of the domain of attraction
(
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
)
.

Theorem 4
.

(
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
)

Let
\Delta
be a positive root of equation

k_{1}\Delta^{\gamma}+\beta\Delta^{\gamma+\mu-1}=a_{1}\delta^{\gamma}.

(18)

If system (
2
) is asymptotically stable, then the set of initial functions
\|\varphi\|_{h}<\Delta

is the estimate of the attraction region of the trivial solution of (
1
).

Remark 2
.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
4
that if
\|\varphi\|_{h}<\Delta,
then
\|x(t,\varphi)\|<\delta
for any
t\geq 0
.

Next, we connect the functional
v(x_{t})
to its time derivative with the help of the above bounds, using the following technical result.

Lemma 4
.

Let
p
and
q
be such that
p>q\geq 1
. Then, the following inequality is satisfied

\left(\|x(t)\|^{q}+\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{q}d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq L_{1}\left(\|x(t)\|^{p}+\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{p}d\theta\right),

(19)

where
L_{1}=\bigl(2\max\{1,h\}\bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}-1}
.

Proof.

Given
d,g\geq 0
and
l\geq 1
, the following inequality holds by convexity:

(d+g)^{l}\leq 2^{l-1}(d^{l}+g^{l}).

Applying this inequality to the left-hand side of (
19
),
we get

\left(\|x(t)\|^{q}+\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{q}d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq 2^{\frac{p}{q}-1}\left(\|x(t)\|^{p}+\left(\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{q}d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\right).

(20)

We use Hölder’s inequality to end the proof:

\left(\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{q}d\theta\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\leq h^{\frac{p-q}{q}}\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{p}d\theta.

Now, the result follows from (
20
).
∎

Lemma 5
.

The following inequality is satisfied

\frac{dv(x_{t})}{dt}\leq-L_{2}v(x_{t})^{\frac{\gamma+\mu-1}{\gamma}},\quad t\geq 0,

(21)

along the solutions with
\|x_{t}\|_{h}\leq\delta<\min\{H_{1},H_{2}\}.
Here,

L_{2}=\frac{c}{b^{\frac{\gamma+\mu-1}{\gamma}}L_{1}},

where
b
comes from the upper bound (
15
),
c
comes from the bound for the derivative (
17
) and
L_{1}
comes from Lemma 1 with
p=\gamma+\mu-1
and
q=\gamma
.

Proof.

Combining (
17
) with Lemma
4
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{dv(x_{t})}{dt}\leq-c\left(\|x(t)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}+\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{\gamma+\mu-1}d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{c}{L_{1}b^{\frac{\gamma+\mu-1}{\gamma}}}\left[b\left(\|x(t)\|^{\gamma}+\int_{-h}^{0}\|x(t+\theta)\|^{\gamma}d\theta\right)\right]^{\frac{\gamma+\mu-1}{\gamma}}.

The lemma now follows from the upper bound (
15
).
∎

By using the comparison lemma
(
Khalil (1996)
)
, we are finally able to present estimates for the solutions.

Theorem 5
.

Let system (
2
) be asymptotically stable. The solutions of system (
1
) with initial functions satisfying
\|\varphi\|_{h}<\Delta,
where
\Delta
is defined in (
18
), admit an estimate of the form

\|x(t,\varphi)\|\leq\hat{c}_{1}\|\varphi\|_{h}\left[1+\hat{c}_{2}\|\varphi\|_{h}^{\mu-1}t\right]^{-\frac{1}{\mu-1}},\quad t\geq 0,

(22)

where

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{k_{1}+\beta\Delta^{\mu-1}}{a_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}=\frac{\delta}{\Delta},

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{b}\left(\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}\right)\left(\frac{k_{1}+\beta\Delta^{\mu-1}}{2b\max\{1,h\}}\right)^{\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Since
v(x_{t})
fulfils the differential inequality (
21
), we take
t_{0}=0
and consider the comparison equation

\frac{du(t)}{dt}=-L_{2}u^{\frac{\gamma+\mu-1}{\gamma}}(t)

(23)

with initial condition

u(0)=u_{0}=(k_{1}+\beta\Delta^{\mu-1})\|\varphi\|_{h}^{\gamma}.

Such choice of
u_{0}
implies
v(\varphi)<u_{0}
due to (
16
), if
\|\varphi\|_{h}<\Delta.
The solution of equation (
23
) can be easily obtained by using the method of separation of variables:

u(t)=u_{0}\left[1+L_{2}\left(\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}\right)u_{0}^{\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}}t\right]^{-\frac{\gamma}{\mu-1}}.

Hence, using the comparison lemma and bound (
14
), we get

a_{1}\|x(t,\varphi)\|^{\gamma}\leq v(x_{t})\leq u_{0}\left[1+L_{2}\left(\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}\right)u_{0}^{\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}}t\right]^{-\frac{\gamma}{\mu-1}}.

The result follows from the previous inequality.
∎

Remark 3
.

The structure of the estimates (
12
) and (
22
) is the same, and it is similar to that of a counterpart for delay free homogeneous systems
(
Zubov (1964)
)
.

3.2
Scalar Case

In this section, we present less conservative estimates for the solutions of
the scalar equation of the form

\dot{x}(t)=\alpha_{1}x^{\mu}(t)+\alpha_{2}x^{\mu}(t-h),

(24)

where the constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
h\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\mu>1
is an odd entire number. Assume that
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}<0
which implies the asymptotic stability of the trivial solution of (
24
). Take
\mathrm{w}=-2(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})>0,

V(x)=x^{2},
and use the following modification of functional (
13
) presented in
Zhabko & Alexandrova (2021)
:

v(\varphi)=\left(\varphi(0)+\alpha_{2}\int_{-h}^{0}\varphi^{\mu}(\theta)d\theta\right)^{2}+\int_{-h}^{0}(\mathrm{w_{1}}+(h+\theta)\mathrm{w_{2}})\varphi^{\mu+1}(\theta)d\theta.

(25)

Here,
\mathrm{w}_{1},\mathrm{w}_{2}>0
are such that
\mathrm{w}_{0}=\mathrm{w}-\mathrm{w}_{1}-h\mathrm{w}_{2}>0
. Bounds (
14
), (
15
) and (
17
) for functional (
25
) take the form

\displaystyle v(\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq a_{1}\varphi^{2}(0)+a_{2}\int_{-h}^{0}\varphi^{\mu+1}(\theta)d\theta,\quad|\varphi|_{h}\leq\delta,

\displaystyle v(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq b\left(\varphi^{2}(0)+\int_{-h}^{0}\varphi^{2}(\theta)d\theta\right),\quad|\varphi|_{h}\leq\delta,

\displaystyle\frac{dv(x_{t})}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq-c\left(x^{\mu+1}(t)+\int_{-h}^{0}x^{\mu+1}(t+\theta)d\theta\right),\quad|x_{t}|_{h}\leq\delta.

Here,
\delta<\min\{H_{1},H_{2}\},
and

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{w}_{1}\chi^{2}}{|\alpha_{2}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}},\quad H_{2}=\left(\min\left\{\dfrac{\mathrm{w}_{0}}{hL},\dfrac{\mathrm{w}_{2}}{L}\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}},\quad 0<\chi<\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{h|\alpha_{2}|}},

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=|\alpha_{2}||\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}|,\quad a_{1}=1-\chi^{2}h|\alpha_{2}|>0,\quad a_{2}=\mathrm{w}_{1}-|\alpha_{2}|\chi^{-2}\delta^{\mu-1}>0,

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\max\{1+|\alpha_{2}|h;\bigl(|\alpha_{2}|(1+|\alpha_{2}|h)\delta^{\mu-1}+\mathrm{w}_{1}+h\mathrm{w}_{2}\bigr)\delta^{\mu-1}\},

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\min\{\mathrm{w}_{0}-hL\delta^{\mu-1};\mathrm{w}_{2}-L\delta^{\mu-1}\}.

The functional admits also an upper bound

\displaystyle v(\varphi)\leq\varphi^{2}(0)+\beta|\varphi|_{h}^{\mu+1},

where
\beta=(2|\alpha_{2}|+\alpha_{2}^{2}h\delta^{\mu-1}+\mathrm{w}_{1}+h\mathrm{w}_{2})h
.
As in Theorem

\Delta
is a positive root of equation

(26)

then the set of initial functions
|\varphi|_{h}<\Delta

is the estimate of the region of attraction of the trivial solution of (

). Now, we present another
technical lemma, which is less conservative than Lemma

.

Lemma 6
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an entire number. Then,

\left(x^{2}(t)+\int_{-h}^{0}x^{2}(t+\theta)d\theta\right)^{k}\leq L_{1}\left(x^{2k}(t)+\int_{-h}^{0}x^{2k}(t+\theta)d\theta\right),

(27)

where
L_{1}=2^{k-2}(1+h)^{k-1}
.

Proof.

The proof is carried out by mathematical induction. It is easy to verify (
27
) for
k=2
taking into account that

\left(\int_{-h}^{0}x^{2}(t+\theta)d\theta\right)^{2}\leq h\int_{-h}^{0}x^{4}(t+\theta)d\theta.

Assume that (
27
) holds for a
k\geq 2,
then

\left(x^{2}(t)+\int_{-h}^{0}x^{2}(t+\theta)d\theta\right)^{k+1}\leq 2^{k-2}(1+h)^{k-1}\\
\times\left(x^{2k}(t)+\int_{-h}^{0}\!\!x^{2k}(t+\theta)d\theta\right)\!\!\left(x^{2}(t)+\int_{-h}^{0}\!\!x^{2}(t+\theta)d\theta\right).

Using
d^{p}g^{q}\leq d^{p+q}+g^{p+q}
in the right-hand side of this inequality, we get

\left(x^{2}(t)+\int_{-h}^{0}x^{2}(t+\theta)d\theta\right)^{k+1}\leq 2^{k-1}(1+h)^{k}\left(x^{2(k+1)}(t)+\int_{-h}^{0}x^{2(k+1)}(t+\theta)d\theta\right),

and the lemma is proved.
∎

Using Lemma
6
with
k=(\mu+1)/2\in\mathbb{Z}
instead of Lemma
4
and repeating the steps of the previous section, we arrive at the following estimates for solutions of (
24
).

Theorem 6
.

If
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}<0,
then
the solutions of equation (
24
) with initial functions satisfying
|\varphi|_{h}<\Delta,
where
\Delta
is a positive root of (

|x(t,\varphi)|\leq\hat{c}_{1}|\varphi|_{h}\left[1+\hat{c}_{2}|\varphi|_{h}^{\mu-1}t\right]^{-\frac{1}{\mu-1}},

(28)

where

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1+\beta\Delta^{\mu-1}}{a_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\delta}{\Delta},

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c\left(\mu-1\right)}{b}\left(\frac{1+\beta\Delta^{\mu-1}}{2b(1+h)}\right)^{\frac{\mu-1}{2}}.

Remark 4

The reduction of conservatism in Theorem

5
Discussion

Unexpectedly, we obtain that the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach gives much better results for homogeneous systems. Although in the first example, tuning some parameters of the Lyapunov–Krasovskii approach, we are able to achieve a less conservative bound for the region of attraction, as well as the closer estimate at the beginning of the response, this is not the case in general. Using the same value for
\delta
in both approaches,
let us compare the estimates for the solutions (
12
) and (
22
).
To this end, compare the values
\hat{c}_{1}
and
\hat{c}_{2}
with
A
and
B
(see the proof of Theorem
3
in the Appendix) that precede
\tilde{c}_{1}
and
\tilde{c}_{2}.

While
\hat{c}_{1}
and
A
admit the same expression
\delta/\Delta,
the values
\hat{c}_{2}
and
B
can be presented in the form

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{bL_{1}}\left(\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}\right)\left(\frac{a_{1}}{b}\right)^{\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\delta}{\Delta}\right)^{\mu-1},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}\right)k_{0}^{\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\delta}{\Delta}\right)^{\mu-1}.

As the values
\delta
and
\Delta
are usually rather small in practice, conditions (
33
) and (
34
) become insignificant, and one can often take
\rho=\overline{d}-\varepsilon,
where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small.
In this case, the constant

B
is close to

\widetilde{B}=\frac{k_{5}}{k_{1}}\left(\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}\right)\left(\frac{k_{0}}{k_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\mu-1}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\delta}{\Delta}\right)^{\mu-1}.

Clearly, the values
k_{5},

k_{1},
and
k_{0}
have a similar meaning for the Lyapunov function as the values
c,

b,
and
a_{1}
for the Lyapunov–Krasovskii functional.
The analysis of how the constants are built gives the expressions

\displaystyle a_{1}=k_{0}-\textup{const}\cdot\delta^{\mu-1},\quad b=\max\{k_{1}+\textup{const}\cdot\delta^{\mu-1},\textup{const}\cdot\delta^{\mu-1}\},

\displaystyle c=\min\{\textup{w}_{0}-\textup{const}\cdot\delta^{\mu-1},\textup{w}_{2}-\textup{const}\cdot\delta^{\mu-1}\},\quad k_{5}=\textup{w}-\textup{const}\cdot\delta^{\mu-1},

where “
const
” means a different (positive) constant in each case. One can notice that
a_{1}<k_{0},

b>k_{1}.
Although it is not easy to compare the constants involved in the expressions for
c
and
k_{5}
directly,
\textup{w}>\textup{w}_{0}
and
\textup{w}>\textup{w}_{2}
imply that
k_{5}>c
for a sufficiently small
\delta.
Hence, if
\delta
is sufficiently small, then

\frac{k_{5}}{k_{1}}>\frac{c}{b},\quad\frac{k_{0}}{k_{1}}>\frac{a_{1}}{b}.

In addition,
L_{1}>1.

We arrive at the following conclusions:

\star

First,
\hat{c}_{2}<\widetilde{B}\approx B<\tilde{c}_{2}
at least when
\delta
is rather small. Second,
\hat{c}_{1}<A<\tilde{c}_{1},
if
\Delta
for the Lyapunov–Krasovskii approach is larger than those for the Lyapunov–Razumikhin one. If this is the case, then we are able to get a closer estimate at the beginning of the system response via the Lyapunov–Krasovskii method as in Example 1, see Figure
2
. However, this is not the case in general. Moreover, since the constant in the denominator is in fact the one accounting for the rate of convergence, we conclude that the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach gives in general a tighter bound, see Figures
1
and
3
.

\star

The sources of conservatism for the Lyapunov–Krasovskii approach are
(a)
the multiplier
L_{1}>1
appearing due to the fact that the degrees of the upper bound for the functional and the bound for its derivative along the solutions are different, and
(b)
the constants
c,

b,
and
a_{1}
for the Lyapunov–Krasovskii functional in comparison with
k_{5},

k_{1},
and
k_{0}
for the Lyapunov function.

\star

The sources of conservatism for the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach are
(c)
additional conditions (
33
) and (
34
) on
\rho
appearing due to the fact that the solution of the comparison equation should satisfy the Razumikhin condition, and
(d)
the fact that the bound is first obtained for
t\geq h
only, and that it is necessary to supplement it with the bound for
t\in[0,h].

\star

For the scalar equation (
24
), the Lyapunov–Krasovskii bound can be slightly improved (compare (
28
) to (
22
)) by introducing a less conservative multiplier
L_{1}>1.

\star

Assuming that
V(x),
and hence the constants
k_{j},

j=0,\ldots,5,
depend on
\mathrm{w}
linearly, what is natural in view of (
7
), we obtain that the Lyapunov–Razumikhin bounds do not depend on
\mathrm{w}.
At the same time, the Lyapunov–Krasovskii ones depend on the relation between
\mathrm{w}_{0},

\mathrm{w}_{1}
and
\mathrm{w}_{2},
hence these parameters may serve for optimization purposes.

The sources of conservatism for the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach turn out to be insignificant. Conditions (
33
) and (
34
) hold in the example because
\delta
is small. As for
(d)
, the same bound obtained for
t\geq h
holds for all
t\geq 0
in general. We conclude that the Lyapunov function of the delay free system works rather well, and better than the Lyapunov–Krasovskii functional, in the estimation of the convergence rate of a homogeneous time delay system solutions. This unexpected conclusion may be proceeding from the delay-independent stability property of the systems under consideration.
We also notice that slightly different assumptions on the right-hand sides are exploited in Sections
2.2
and
3
: differentiability of
f(x,y)
with respect to
y
in the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approach and with respect to
x
in the Lyapunov–Krasovskii one.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we obtain estimates of the response of nonlinear homogeneous systems with delay and right-hand side homogeneity degree strictly greater than one. The problem is addressed via the Lyapunov–Krasovskii and the Lyapunov–Razumikhin approaches. In both approaches, the results are based on the same Lyapunov function of the corresponding delay free system. This fact allows us to compare the approaches directly, in the light of some illustrative examples.

Furthermore, since
1-Bh\Delta^{\mu-1}>0
due to condition (
34
), we have

\displaystyle\|x(t,\varphi)\|\leq\frac{A\|\varphi\|_{h}}{(1-B\|\varphi\|_{h}^{\mu-1}h+B\|\varphi\|_{h}^{\mu-1}t)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}}\leq\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}\|\varphi\|_{h}}{(1+\tilde{c}_{2}\|\varphi\|_{h}^{\mu-1}t)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}},

if
t\geq h,
where

\tilde{c}_{1}=\frac{A}{(1-Bh\Delta^{\mu-1})^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}},\quad\tilde{c}_{2}=\frac{B}{1-Bh\Delta^{\mu-1}}.

It remains to obtain the estimate for
t\in[0,h]
. According to Lemma
7
,

\|x(t,\varphi)\|\leq A_{1}\|\varphi\|_{h},\quad t\in[0,h],

where
A_{1}=K(1+mh\Delta^{\mu-1}).

Notice that

\dfrac{(1+\tilde{c}_{2}\Delta^{\mu-1}h)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}A_{1}}{\tilde{c}_{1}}=\kappa\leq 1.

Hence,

\|x(t,\varphi)\|\leq\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}\|\varphi\|_{h}}{(1+\tilde{c}_{2}\Delta^{\mu-1}h)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}}\leq\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}\|\varphi\|_{h}}{(1+\tilde{c}_{2}\|\varphi\|_{h}^{\mu-1}t)^{\frac{1}{\mu-1}}},\quad t\in[0,h].

Therefore, the required bound (
12
) holds for all
t\geq 0,
and the proof is complete.

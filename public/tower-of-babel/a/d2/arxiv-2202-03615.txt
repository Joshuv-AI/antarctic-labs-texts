Third-order k k -Jacobsthal matrix sequence: Another way of demonstrating their properties
GAMALIEL CERDA-MORALES

Abstract
. Recently, Cerda-Morales [ 6 ] introduced commutative matrices derived from the third-order Jacobsthal matrix sequence and the third-order Jacobsthal–Lucas matrix sequence. In the present work, through the identification of certain special matrices, we can identify other forms of demonstration and also the description of commutative matrix properties for negative indices. A new generalization of this sequence is used for our purpose.

The third-order Jacobsthal and modified third-order Jacobsthal sequences
\left(J_{n}^{(3)}\right)_{n\geq 0}
and
\left(K_{n}^{(3)}\right)_{n\geq 0}
, are defined for
n\geq 0
by

J_{n+3}^{(3)}=J_{n+2}^{(3)}+J_{n+1}^{(3)}+2J_{n}^{(3)}\ \textrm{and}\ K_{n+3}^{(3)}=K_{n+2}^{(3)}+K_{n+1}^{(3)}+2K_{n}^{(3)},

in which
J_{0}^{(3)}=0
,
J_{1}^{(3)}=J_{2}^{(3)}=1
and
K_{0}^{(3)}=3K_{1}^{(3)}=K_{2}^{(3)}=3
, respectively.

The Binet formulae are

(1)

J_{n}^{(3)}=\frac{1}{7}2^{n+1}-\frac{3+2i\sqrt{3}}{21}\omega_{1}^{n}-\frac{3-2i\sqrt{3}}{21}\omega_{2}^{n}=\frac{1}{7}\left(2^{n+1}-Z_{n}\right)

and

(2)

K_{n}^{(3)}=2^{n}+\omega_{1}^{n}+\omega_{2}^{n}=2^{n}+Y_{n},

where

Z_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}2&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 0\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\\
-3&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 1\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\\
1&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 2\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\end{array}\right.\ \textrm{and}\ Y_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}2&\textrm{if}&n\equiv 0\kern-5.0pt\pmod{3}\\
-1&\textrm{if}&n\not\equiv 0\kern-6.00006pt\pmod{3}\end{array}\right.

In
[
3

\left(J_{n}^{(3)}\right)
and
\left(K_{n}^{(3)}\right)

r\geq 0
,
n\geq 0

(3)

J_{r(n+3)}^{(3)}=K_{r}^{(3)}J_{r(n+2)}^{(3)}-\left(2^{r}Y_{r}+1\right)J_{r(n+1)}^{(3)}+2^{r}J_{rn}^{(3)},

The Binet formulae are

J_{rn}^{(3)}=\frac{1}{7}\left(2^{rn+1}-Z_{rn}\right)\ \textrm{and}\ K_{rn}^{(3)}=2^{rn}+Y_{rn},

Corollary 1.7
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we obtain:

(5)

\det\left[\left(\emph{\textrm{M}}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\right]=k^{n},

(6)

\det\left[\emph{\textrm{N}}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\emph{\textrm{M}}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\right]=(k+1)^{2}(k^{2}+k+2)k^{n-1}.

Proof.

We note that
\det\left[\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right]=k
. By mathematical induction, let us write:

\displaystyle\det\left[\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n+1}\right]

\displaystyle=\det\left[\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\cdot\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right]

\displaystyle=\det\left[\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\right]\cdot\det\left[\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right]

\displaystyle=k^{n}\cdot k=k^{n+1}.

Similarly, let us admit that
\det\left[\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\right]=(k+1)^{2}(k^{2}+k+2)k^{n-1}
. Thus, we can see that

\displaystyle\det\left[\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n+1}\right]

\displaystyle=\det\left[\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\cdot\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right]

\displaystyle=\det\left[\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\right]\cdot\det\left[\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right]

\displaystyle=(k+1)^{2}(k^{2}+k+2)k^{n-1}\cdot k

\displaystyle=(k+1)^{2}(k^{2}+k+2)k^{n}.

∎

2.
Other demonstrations for the commutative properties

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we define

\textrm{J}_{n}(k)=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}

and

\textrm{j}_{n}(k)=\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n},

are two matrices of order 3, with real coefficients.

In this section, we will discuss other simpler and more immediate ways to demonstrate the matrix properties of the matrices previously defined.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
m,\ n\geq 1
, the following results hold:

(7)

\emph{\textrm{J}}_{m+n}(k)=\emph{\textrm{J}}_{m}(k)\cdot\emph{\textrm{J}}_{n}(k)=\emph{\textrm{J}}_{n}(k)\cdot\emph{\textrm{J}}_{m}(k),

(8)

\emph{\textrm{j}}_{m}(k)\cdot\emph{\textrm{j}}_{n}(k)=\emph{\textrm{j}}_{n}(k)\cdot\emph{\textrm{j}}_{m}(k),

(9)

\emph{\textrm{J}}_{m}(k)\cdot\emph{\textrm{j}}_{n}(k)=\emph{\textrm{j}}_{n}(k)\cdot\emph{\textrm{J}}_{m}(k),

Proof.

For the first item, we see that the following commutative properties occur

\displaystyle\textrm{J}_{m+n}(k)=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m+n}

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m}\cdot\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\cdot\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\textrm{J}_{n+m}(k).

The second equation of this theorem, let us also see that

\displaystyle\textrm{j}_{m}(k)\cdot\textrm{j}_{n}(k)

\displaystyle=\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m}\cdot\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\cdot\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle=\textrm{j}_{n}(k)\cdot\textrm{j}_{m}(k)

and we record the commutative property of
\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\cdot\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}=\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\cdot\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}
. To conclude, let us easily see that

\textrm{J}_{m}(k)\cdot\textrm{j}_{n}(k)=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m}\cdot\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}=\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\cdot\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m}=\textrm{j}_{n}(k)\cdot\textrm{J}_{m}(k).

∎

Proof.

Let us consider that

\textrm{j}_{m+n}(k)=\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m+n}=\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\cdot\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{m}=\textrm{j}_{n}(k)\textrm{J}_{m}(k).

In addition, we can write immediately that

\textrm{j}_{m+n}(k)=\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\textrm{J}_{m+n}(k)=\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\textrm{J}_{n}(k)\cdot\textrm{J}_{m}(k)=\textrm{J}_{n}(k)\cdot\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\textrm{J}_{m}(k)=\textrm{J}_{n}(k)\cdot\textrm{j}_{m}(k)

since, we know the commutativity of the matrix product
\textrm{J}_{1}(k)\cdot\textrm{j}_{0}(k)=\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\cdot\textrm{J}_{1}(k)
. The proof is completed.
∎

3.
Matrix sequence properties for negative indices

Now, we will develop the study of certain properties determined by the following inverse third-order
k
-Jacobsthal matrix indicated by
\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}
. We can immediately determine some particular cases

\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}\end{array}\right],

and

\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}\\
\frac{1-k}{k^{2}}&\frac{1-k+k^{2}}{k^{2}}&\frac{1-k}{k^{2}}\end{array}\right].

We have observed that the elements of the type
J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)
, for a positive integer
n\geq 0
can be determined directly from the recurrence relation indicated by

J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)=\frac{1-k}{k}J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+\frac{1-k}{k}J_{-(n-2)}^{(3)}(k)+\frac{1}{k}J_{-(n-3)}^{(3)}(k).

From these preliminary examples, we will state the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\emph{\textrm{J}}_{-n}(k)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-n}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle\emph{\textrm{J}}_{-n}(k)

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}=\left[\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-1}\right]^{n}=\left[\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}\right]^{-1},

where
T_{-n}^{(3)}(k)=(k-1)T_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+kJ_{-n}^{(3)}(k)
.

Proof.

The result holds for
n=1
:

\displaystyle\textrm{J}_{-1}(k)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{0}^{(3)}(k)&(k-1)J_{-1}^{(3)}(k)+kJ_{-2}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-1}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-1}^{(3)}(k)&(k-1)J_{-2}^{(3)}(k)+kJ_{-3}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-2}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-2}^{(3)}(k)&(k-1)J_{-3}^{(3)}(k)+kJ_{-4}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-3}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right].

By mathematical induction, we assume that

\textrm{J}_{-n}(k)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-n}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right],

with
T_{-n}^{(3)}(k)=(k-1)J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+kJ_{-n}^{(3)}(k)
. Next, consider the following matrix power:

\displaystyle\textrm{J}_{-(n+1)}(k)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}\end{array}\right]^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}\end{array}\right]^{n}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}\end{array}\right].

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\textrm{J}_{-(n+1)}(k)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-n}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+4)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right],

and using the following relation

J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)=(k-1)J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)+(k-1)J_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)+kJ_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k),

note that
T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)=J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)
. In this way, we will write

\displaystyle\textrm{J}_{-n}(k)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}&\frac{1-k}{k}\end{array}\right]^{n}=\left[\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-1}\right]^{n}.

∎

In the following theorem, we will reduce the Binet formula corresponding to the terms of negative indices and not discussed in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we obtain

(14)

J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)=\frac{1}{k^{2}+k+1}\left[k\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{n}+\frac{B\omega_{1}^{n}-A\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\right],

where
A=\omega_{1}k-1
,
B=\omega_{2}k-1
and
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
are roots of the polynomial
x^{2}+x+1
.

Proof.

We know that
\omega_{1}\omega_{2}=1
. In this way, we will make the following substitutions

\displaystyle J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}+k+1}\left[k^{-n+1}-\frac{A\omega_{1}^{-n}-B\omega_{2}^{-n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}+k+1}\left[k\cdot k^{-n}-\frac{\frac{A}{\omega_{1}^{n}}-\frac{B}{\omega_{2}^{n}}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}+k+1}\left[k\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{n}+\frac{B\omega_{1}^{n}-A\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\right],

using the Binet formula
J_{n}^{(3)}(k)=\frac{1}{k^{2}+k+1}\left[k^{n+1}-\frac{A\omega_{1}^{n}-B\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\right]
.
∎

Let us consider the following equation
\textrm{j}_{n}(k)=2\textrm{J}_{n+1}(k)+(1-k)\textrm{J}_{n}(k)+2\textrm{J}_{n-1}(k)
from Proposition
2.2
. We will reduce the corresponding identity to the terms of negative indices.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we obtain that

j_{-n}^{(3)}(k)=2J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)+2J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k).

Proof.

We can observe that

\displaystyle(k^{2}+k+1)

\displaystyle\cdot\left[2J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)+2J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\right]

\displaystyle=2k\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{n-1}+2\frac{B\omega_{1}^{n-1}-A\omega_{2}^{n-1}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ +(1-k)k\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{n}+(1-k)\frac{B\omega_{1}^{n}-A\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ +2k\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{n+1}+2\frac{B\omega_{1}^{n+1}-A\omega_{2}^{n+1}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}

\displaystyle=(k^{2}+k+2)\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{1}}+(1-k)+2\omega_{1}\right)\frac{B\omega_{1}^{n}-A\omega_{2}^{n}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}.

On the other hand, we can directly verify that
\omega_{2}=\frac{1}{\omega_{1}}
and
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}=-1
. Finally, we deduce
\frac{1}{\omega_{1}}+(1-k)+2\omega_{1}=-(k+1)
and

2J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+(1-k)J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)+2J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)=j_{-n}^{(3)}(k).

∎

Now, we see the following theorem that allows determining the generating matrices for the family of matrices
\{\textrm{j}_{-n}(k)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, which we have preliminarily defined by

\textrm{j}_{-n}(k)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)&t_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&kj_{-n}^{(3)}(k)\\
j_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&t_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kj_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
j_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&t_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)&kj_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right],

where
t_{-n}^{(3)}(k)=(k-1)j_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)+kj_{-n}^{(3)}(k)
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we obtain that

(15)

\emph{\textrm{j}}_{-n}(k)=\left(\emph{\textrm{J}}_{1}(k)\right)^{-n}\cdot\emph{\textrm{j}}_{0}(k)=\emph{\textrm{j}}_{0}(k)\cdot\left(\emph{\textrm{J}}_{1}(k)\right)^{-n},

(16)

\left(\emph{\textrm{j}}_{n}(k)\right)^{-1}=\left(\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\right)^{-1}\cdot\textrm{j}_{-n}(k)\cdot\left(\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

(
15
: From the previous theorem we will consider

\displaystyle\textrm{j}_{-n}(k)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}j_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)&t_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&kj_{-n}^{(3)}(k)\\
j_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&t_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kj_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
j_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&t_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)&kj_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=2\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{-(n-2)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-n}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ +(1-k)\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{-(n-1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-n}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ +2\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{-n}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+1)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)\\
J_{-(n+2)}^{(3)}(k)&T_{-(n+4)}^{(3)}(k)&kJ_{-(n+3)}^{(3)}(k)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=2\textrm{J}_{-(n-1)}(k)+(1-k)\textrm{J}_{-n}(k)+2\textrm{J}_{-(n+1)}(k).

On the other hand, we know that
\textrm{J}_{-n}(k)=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}
. In this way, we will make the corresponding substitutions to determine that

\displaystyle\textrm{j}_{-n}(k)

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}\cdot\left[2\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}+(1-k)\textrm{I}+2\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}k-1&2k&2k\\
2&1-k&2\\
\frac{2}{k}&\frac{2}{k}&-\frac{1}{k}(k^{2}+k-2)\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}\cdot\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\cdot\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}=\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\cdot\textrm{J}_{-n}(k).

(
16
): From Eq. (
15
) and
\textrm{j}_{n}(k)=\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\cdot\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{n}
, we know that

\displaystyle\left(\textrm{j}_{n}(k)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}\cdot\left(\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\right)^{-1}\cdot\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\left(\textrm{M}_{k,1}^{(3)}\right)^{-n}\cdot\left(\textrm{N}_{k,0}^{(3)}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\right)^{-1}\cdot\textrm{j}_{-n}(k)\cdot\left(\textrm{j}_{0}(k)\right)^{-1}.

The proof is completed.
∎

Exponentially localised interface eigenmodes in finite chains of resonators
Habib Ammari, Silvio Barandun, Bryn Davies, Erik Orvehed Hiltunen, Thea Kosche, Ping Liu

Abstract
. This paper studies wave localisation in chains of finitely many resonators. There is an extensive theory predicting the existence of localised modes induced by defects in infinitely periodic systems. This work extends these principles to finite-sized systems. We consider finite systems of subwavelength resonators arranged in dimers that have a geometric defect in the structure. This is a classical wave analogue of the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model. We prove the existence of a spectral gap for defectless finite dimer structures and find a direct relationship between eigenvalues being within the spectral gap and the localisation of their associated eigenmode. Then we show the existence and uniqueness of an eigenvalue in the gap in the defect structure, proving the existence of a unique localised interface mode. To the best of our knowledge, our method, based on Chebyshev polynomials, is the first to characterise quantitatively the localised interface modes in systems of finitely many resonators.



Keywords.
Finite Hermitian resonator systems, subwavelength resonances, interface eigenmodes, capacitance matrix, topological protection, Chebyshev polynomials, robust wave localisation

AMS Subject classifications.

34L40, 34L20, 35B34, 15A18, 15B05.

1.
Introduction

Wave localisation at subwavelength scales has many important applications in nanophotonics and nanophononics
[
9
,
21
,
19
]
. Here,
subwavelength
means that the incident wavelengths are much larger than the size of the building blocks of the structure. When these relatively small building blocks are locally resonant (which, in the case studied here, will be due to large material contrasts) this allows for waves to be localised at subwavelength scales, thereby beating traditional diffraction limits
[
5
,
4
,
7
]
. This principle has unlocked a wealth of novel nanotechnologies
[
22
,
13
]
.

In this paper, we consider wave localisation at the interface between two systems of
finitely many
subwavelength resonators. These resonators are arranged in pairs or dimers, such that the model we consider shares many of the features of the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) model in quantum mechanics
[
25
]
. We prove the existence of exponentially localised interface eigenmodes in this finite structure. These interface modes have been subject to numerous studies in the setting of infinite structures. A particular focus has been put on studying the topological properties of infinite periodic structures and then introducing carefully designed interfaces so as to create so-called
topologically protected
eigenmodes
[
8
]
. These modes have significant implications for applications since they are expected to be robust with respect to imperfections in the design. These concepts have been widely studied in a variety of settings, most notably in quantum mechanics for the Schrödinger operator
[
17
,
16
]
and more recently, for related continuous models of classical wave systems in
[
20
,
27
,
24
,
12
,
26
]
. In finite-sized systems, these eigenmodes have been observed both experimentally and numerically; see, for instance,
[
2
,
8
,
30
,
10
]
and references therein.

The present work considers the far less-explored but more realistic physical setting of interface modes in finite dimer structures. As far as we know, it is the first work to deal with the existence of interface modes in finite structures. It provides a one-to-one correspondence between the position of the eigenfrequency in the spectrum of the corresponding infinite periodic structure (
i.e.
, in the asymptotic spectral bulk, its boundary or in the asymptotic spectral gap; see
Definition

4.1
) and the behaviour (localised versus delocalised) of the corresponding eigenmode. Our results hold for any finite and large enough system of dimer structure with defect satisfying a mild condition on the size of the resonators. Furthermore, we show that the eigenfrequencies lying in the gap converge exponentially as the size of the structure goes to infinity and provide an explicit and simple formula for the limit. We also show that the Hermitian nature of the system together with the position of the interface eigenfrequency in the gap yields a very strong stability property when the geometry of the system is perturbed.

Our approach is based on the capacitance matrix, which in general is a powerful tool for characterising the subwavelength eigenfrequencies of a system of high-contrast resonators
[
6
,
18
,
2
]
. In essence, the capacitance formulation provides a discrete approximation to the continuous spectral problem of the differential model, valid in the high-contrast asymptotic limit. This approximation is based solely on first principles and provides a natural starting point for both theoretical analysis and numerical simulation of wave localisation in the subwavelength regime
[
7
,
3
,
4
]
. In the case of
our one-dimensional, finite system of dimer resonators with a geometric defect, the capacitance matrix is a perturbed tridiagonal block
2
-Toeplitz matrix; see (
2.1
). Based on some properties of the Chebyshev polynomials, we prove existence and uniqueness of an eigenfrequency in the gap for the finite dimer structures with a geometric defect and show exponential localisation of the corresponding eigenmode. Our proof does not rely on any perturbation argument neither on any
a prior
assumption on the band gaps of the two dimer systems in the structure.

The paper is organised as follows. In
Section

2
, we introduce the capacitance matrix approximation of a finite, dimer system with a geometric defect in order to approximate its eigenfrequencies and associated eigenmodes. In
Section

3
, we first write the characteristic polynomial of the capacitance matrix associated to a defectless, finite dimer system in terms of Chebyshev polynomials. Then, we characterise the structure of the eigenvectors of the capacitance matrix associated with the perturbed dimer structure.
Section

4
is devoted to the derivation of a direct relationship between an eigenvalue being within the spectral gap and the localisation of its corresponding eigenvector. In
Section

5
, we show the existence and uniqueness of an eigenvalue of the capacitance matrix lying in the asymptotic spectral gap and consequently the existence of a unique localised interface eigenvector. Furthermore, we show that this eigenvalue converges exponentially fast to a value in the gap as the system size increases. In
Section

6
, we analyse the robustness of the interface localisation with respect to imperfections in the structure design. The paper ends with some concluding remarks in
Section

7
. In
Appendix

A
, we recall some basic definitions and results on pseudo-spectra of normal matrices.
Appendix

B
is devoted to the discussion of the topological origin of the robustness of the interface eigenmodes.

In this work, we consider the one-dimensional wave equation propagating in a heterogeneous medium with space-dependent material parameters:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\omega^{2}}{\kappa(x)}u(x)+\frac{\dd}{\dd x}\left(\frac{1}{\rho(x)}\frac{\dd}{\dd x}u(x)\right)=0,\qquad x\in\R.

The material parameters

\displaystyle\kappa(x)=\begin{cases}\kappa_{b},&x\in D,\\
\kappa,&x\in\R\setminus D,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho(x)=\begin{cases}\rho_{b},&x\in D,\\
\rho,&x\in\R\setminus D,\end{cases}

where the constants
\rho_{b},\rho,\kappa,\kappa_{b}\in\R_{>0}
. The wave speeds inside the set
D
of resonators and inside the background medium
\R\setminus D

v_{b}
and
v
, the wave numbers respectively by

and

, and the contrast between the densities of the resonators and the background medium by

:

(2.2)

\displaystyle v_{b}:=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{b}}{\rho_{b}}},\qquad v:=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{\rho}},\qquad k_{b}:=\frac{\omega}{v_{b}},\qquad k:=\frac{\omega}{v},\qquad\delta:=\frac{\rho_{b}}{\rho}.

For these step-wise defined material parameters, the wave problem determined by (
2.1
) reduces to the following system of coupled one-dimensional Helmholtz equations:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\dd{{}^{2}}}{\dd x^{2}}u(x)+\frac{\omega^{2}}{v^{2}}u(x)=0,&x\in\R\setminus D,\\
\frac{\dd{{}^{2}}}{\dd x^{2}}u(x)+\frac{\omega^{2}}{v_{b}^{2}}u(x)=0,&x\in D,\\
u|_{\iR}(x^{\iLR}_{{i}})-u|_{\iL}(x^{\iLR}_{{i}})=0,&1\leqslant i\leqslant N,\\
\left.\frac{\dd u}{\dd x}\right|_{\iR}(x^{\iL}_{{i}})-\delta\left.\frac{\dd u}{\dd x}\right|_{\iL}(x^{\iL}_{{i}})=0,&1\leqslant i\leqslant N,\\
\delta\left.\frac{\dd u}{\dd x}\right|_{\iR}(x^{\iR}_{{i}})-\left.\frac{\dd u}{\dd x}\right|_{\iL}(x^{\iR}_{{i}})=0,&1\leqslant i\leqslant N,\\
\big(\frac{\dd}{\dd|x|}-\mathrm{i}k\big)u=0&\text{for }x\in(-\infty,x^{\iL}_{1})\cup(x^{\iR}_{N},+\infty),\\
\end{cases}

where for a one-dimensional function

\displaystyle w|_{\iL}(x)\coloneqq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\to 0\\
s>0\end{subarray}}w(x-s)\quad\mbox{and}\quad w|_{\iR}(x)\coloneqq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\to 0\\
s>0\end{subarray}}w(x+s)

if the limits exist.

We are interested in the high-contrast regime characterised by the contrast parameter
\delta
being small. In this case, there exist resonant modes of the system at subwavelength frequencies, for which the size of the resonators is smaller than the wavelength in the background medium.

Definition 2.1
.

A resonance
\omega(\delta)\in\C
for which there exists a non-trivial solution to (
2.3
) is called

in the high-contrast regime if

\displaystyle\omega(\delta)\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\delta\to 0.

One consequence of this asymptotic ansatz is that it lends itself to characterisation using asymptotic analysis
[
6
]
. This limit recovers subwavelength resonances, while keeping the size of the resonators fixed.

In
[
18
]
, an asymptotic analysis in the subwavelength limit was performed on the system of one-dimensional subwavelength resonators considered here. It was shown that the leading-order behavior of the resonances is given by the eigenpairs of the

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{s_{1}}&-\frac{1}{s_{1}}\\
-\frac{1}{s_{1}}&\frac{1}{s_{1}}+\frac{1}{s_{2}}&-\frac{1}{s_{2}}\\
&-\frac{1}{s_{2}}&\frac{1}{s_{2}}+\frac{1}{s_{3}}&-\frac{1}{s_{3}}\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&-\frac{1}{s_{N-2}}&\frac{1}{s_{N-2}}+\frac{1}{s_{N-1}}&-\frac{1}{s_{N-1}}\\
&&&&-\frac{1}{s_{N-1}}&\frac{1}{s_{N-1}}\end{pmatrix}.

This is a modified version of the conventional capacitance matrix that is often used to characterise many-body low-frequency resonance problems. We summaries the findings of
[

]
here below.

.

Consider a system of
N
subwavelength resonators with size

and spacings

for

are simple. Then, the

4.
Asymptotic spectral gap and localised interface modes

In this section we will prove the existence of a spectral gap for a defectless structure of dimers. Furthermore, we will demonstrate that a direct relationship exists between an eigenvalue being within the spectral gap and the localisation of its corresponding eigenvector.

4.1.
Asymptotic spectral gap

We first show the existence of a spectral gap for the capacitance matrix of an unperturbed structure of dimers.

By physical considerations we assume
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}<0

\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}

5.
Existence, uniqueness, and convergence of the eigenvalue in the gap

5.1.

5.2.
Uniqueness

We now show the uniqueness of the eigenvalue in the band gap. We will use the following result about the monotonicity of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
k\in\N
, then

\displaystyle\frac{U_{k-1}(x)}{U_{k}(x)}

is strictly decreasing for
x\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,+\infty)
for any
k\in\N
.

Proof.

Considering the derivative of
\dfrac{U_{k-1}(x)}{U_{k}(x)}
, we have

(5.8)

\left(\frac{U_{k-1}(x)}{U_{k}(x)}\right)^{\prime}=\frac{U_{k}(x)U_{k-1}^{\prime}(x)-U_{k}^{\prime}(x)U_{k-1}(x)}{U_{k}^{2}(x)}.

A well-known property of Chebyshev polynomials is that

U_{k}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{(k+1)T_{k+1}(x)-xU_{k}(x)}{x^{2}-1},

where
T_{k}(x)
are the Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind. Thus, to demonstrate the negativity of (
5.8
) for
|x|>1
, we only need to show that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle>U_{k}(x)[kT_{k}(x)-xU_{k-1}(x)]-[(k+1)T_{k+1}(x)-xU_{k}(x)]U_{k-1}(x)

(5.9)

\displaystyle=U_{k}(x)kT_{k}(x)-(k+1)T_{k+1}(x)U_{k-1}(x).

It is also well-known that

(5.10)

T_{\ell}(x)U_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\left(U_{\ell+n}(x)+U_{n-\ell}(x)\right),&\text{ if }n\geqslant\ell-1,\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(U_{\ell+n}(x)-U_{\ell-n-2}(x)\right),&\text{ if }n\leqslant\ell-2.\end{cases}

So that (
5.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}(U_{2k}(x)+U_{0}(x))-\frac{k+1}{2}(U_{2k}(x)-U_{0}(x))=-\frac{U_{2k}(x)-(2k+1)}{2}.

By
U_{2k}(x)>2k+1,k>0,x\in(-\infty,-1)\cup(1,+\infty)
and the proof is complete.
∎

6.
Stability analysis

Interface modes of SSH-like structures are well known to be stable,
i.e.
, perturbations of the system affect them only slightly. In this section we show that perturbation in the geometry have limited effect on both the resonant frequencies and the associated modes and we quantify this effect.

To this end we consider a system of
N=4m+1
resonators as shown in
Figure

2
but the spacings
s_{i}
are now perturbed:

(6.1)

\displaystyle s_{i}=\begin{cases}s_{1}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i},&1\leqslant i\leqslant 2m,\text{ $i$ odd},\\
s_{2}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i},&1\leqslant i\leqslant 2m,\text{ $i$ even},\\
s_{1}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i},&2m+1\leqslant i\leqslant 4m,\text{ $i$ even},\\
s_{2}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i},&2m+1\leqslant i\leqslant 4m,\text{ $i$ odd}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, we denote

(6.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}=\begin{cases}-\frac{\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i}}{s_{1}(s_{1}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i})},&1\leqslant i\leqslant 2m,\text{ $i$ odd},\\
-\frac{\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i}}{s_{2}(s_{2}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i})},&1\leqslant i\leqslant 2m,\text{ $i$ even},\\
-\frac{\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i}}{s_{1}(s_{1}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i})},&2m+1\leqslant i\leqslant 4m,\text{ $i$ even},\\
-\frac{\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i}}{s_{2}(s_{2}+\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i})},&2m+1\leqslant i\leqslant 4m,\text{ $i$ odd}.\end{cases}

The following proposition handles stability of the eigenvalues and is a direct application of the well-known Weyl theorem.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
be the capacitance matrix associated to the structure described in (
6.1
) and let

(6.3)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\coloneqq\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N-2}|\varepsilon_{i}|+|\varepsilon_{i+1}|.

Then, the eigenvalues
\widehat{\lambda}_{k}
(sorted increasingly) satisfy

(6.4)

\displaystyle|\widehat{\lambda}_{k}-\lambda_{k}|\leqslant 2\varepsilon,\quad 1\leqslant k\leqslant N,

where
\lambda_{k}
are the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Weyl’s theorem states the same result but with the bound of (
6.4
) replaced by
\|\widehat{\mathcal{C}}-\mathcal{C}\|
. However, for a tridiagonal matrix
M
with
\bm{1}
in its kernel,
\|M\|\leqslant 2\max_{i}|M_{i(i-1)}|+|M_{i(i+1)}|
by the Gershgorin circle theorem so we obtain the result.
∎

Applying
Proposition

6.1
to our system of dimers where the perturbations
|\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{i}|\leqslant\eta
are in the interval
(-\eta,\eta)
for some
\eta>0
, we obtain that the eigenvalue perturbation is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{2\eta}{s_{1}(s_{1}-\eta)}+\frac{2\eta}{s_{2}(s_{2}-\eta)}=2\eta\left(\frac{1}{s_{1}^{2}}+\frac{1}{s_{2}^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\eta^{2})\quad\text{as }\eta\to 0.

In order to analyse the stability of the eigenvectors, we will use the
Davis–Kahan theorem
[
14
]
, which needs some preliminary introduction. Let
E
and
F
be
d\times r
matrices with orthonormal columns such that
\text{span}(E)=\mathcal{E}
and
\text{span}(F)=\mathcal{F}
. The
canonical angles
between
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are defined as

\displaystyle\theta_{j}=\cos^{-1}\sigma_{j}\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant r,

where
\sigma_{j}
are the
r

E^{T}F
. We denote by

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})=\diag(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{r})

the canonical angles’ matrix.

The Davis–Kahan theorem states the following.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
S
and
\widetilde{S}
be two
d\times d
symmetric matrices with eigenvalues

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\lambda_{d},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}\geqslant\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\widetilde{\lambda}_{d},

respectively. Fix
1\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant d
and let
V
and
\widetilde{V}
be the matrices having as columns the eigenvectors corresponding to
\lambda_{j}
and
\widetilde{\lambda}_{j}
for
r\leqslant j\leqslant s
. Let
\text{span}(V)=\mathcal{V}
and
\text{span}(\widetilde{V})=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}
. Define

\displaystyle\updelta\coloneqq\inf\{|\lambda-\widetilde{\lambda}|:\lambda\in[\lambda_{s},\lambda_{r}],\widetilde{\lambda}\in(-\infty,\widetilde{\lambda}_{s+1}]\cup[\widetilde{\lambda}_{r-1},\infty)\}.

If
\updelta>0
, then

\displaystyle\|\sin\Theta(\mathcal{V},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{\|S-\widetilde{S}\|_{2}}{\updelta},

where
\sin\Theta(\mathcal{V},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})_{ii}=\sin(\Theta(\mathcal{V},\widetilde{\mathcal{V}})_{ii})
.

As a direct consequence of Theorem
6.2

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{s_{1}}-\frac{1}{s_{2}}\right)
in (
6.3

\bm{v}
and

and

and

(6.5)

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}-\widehat{\bm{v}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\sqrt{2}\varepsilon}{\updelta}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\sqrt{2}\varepsilon}{\updelta_{0}-2\varepsilon},

where
\updelta\coloneqq\min\{|\lambda_{i}-\widehat{\lambda}_{i+1}|,|\lambda_{i}-\widehat{\lambda}_{i-1}|\}
and
\updelta_{0}=\min\{|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1}|,|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}|\}

a priori

7.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have quantitatively characterised interface eigenmodes in finite, dimer systems of subwavelength resonators with a geometric defect and proved the exponential decay of their associated eigenmodes. Our characterisation is based on (broken) translation invariance properties of the associated capacitance matrix together with properties of Chebyshev polynomials. The
3
-term recurrence relation satisfied by the Chebyshev polynomials is shown to be useful for analysing spectra of tridiagonal (perturbed)
2
-Toeplitz matrices.

Following this line of research, it would be very interesting to generalise the results obtained in this paper to extended (known also as multi-band) SSH models, exhibiting exponentially localised interface eigenmodes with corresponding eigenfrequencies inside the multiple subwavelength band gaps of the structure
[
23
,
28
]
. Another highly interesting direction would be to extend the current results to finite dimer systems of three-dimensional subwavelength resonators. In the case of three-dimensional systems of subwavelength resonators, the main difficulty occurs from the long-range interactions between the resonators, which lead to a slow decay of the off-diagonal terms of the corresponding capacitance matrix. Nevertheless, in view of the recent results on
k
-banded approximations of three-dimensional capacitance matrices
[
1
]
together with the convergence spectral results as the size of the structure goes to infinity in
[
3
,
7
]
, it may be possible to prove existence and uniqueness of localised eigenmodes in finite chains of subwavelength resonators in three dimensions, as numerically shown in
[
8
]
.
Another very interesting problem is to relate the localisation effect at the interface to the statistics of the eigenvalues of the capacitance matrix under random perturbations in the parameters of the system. This would extend the well-known Thouless localisation criterion
[
15
]
to interface eigenmodes.

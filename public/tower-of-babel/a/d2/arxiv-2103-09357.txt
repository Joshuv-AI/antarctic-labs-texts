Abstract
. In this paper we prove a new abstract stability result for perturbed saddle-point problems based on a norm fitting technique. We derive the stability condition according to Babuška’s theory from a small inf-sup condition, similar to the famous Ladyzhenskaya-Babuška-Brezzi (LBB) condition, and the other standard assumptions in Brezzi’s theory, in a combined abstract norm. The construction suggests to form the latter from individual fitted norms that are composed from proper seminorms. This abstract framework not only allows for simpler (shorter) proofs of many stability results but also guides the design of parameter-robust norm-equivalent preconditioners. These benefits are demonstrated on mixed variational formulations of generalized Poisson, Stokes, vector Laplace and Biot’s equations.

Saddle-point problems/systems are of a two-by-two block form and characterized by an operator/matrix
of the form

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B_{1}^{T}\\
B_{2}&-C\end{pmatrix}

where
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle C

\displaystyle B_{1}^{T}

operator/matrix
\displaystyle B_{1}
. We consider the symmetric case in this paper where
\displaystyle A^{T}=A
,
\displaystyle C^{T}=C
, and

\displaystyle B_{1}=B_{2}=B
. Problems in which
\displaystyle C\neq 0
are often referred to as
perturbed saddle-point problems

In
[
63
]
a technique has been proposed to determine norms for parameter-dependent
SPPs providing necessary and sufficient conditions
for their well-posedness and leading to robust estimates of the solution in terms of the data.
A drawback of this approach, however,
is that these conditions are often hard to verify in practice
as the operators inducing the norms are
defined only implicitly.

More general SPPs in which
\displaystyle A
(and
\displaystyle C
) are allowed to be nonsymmetric and
\displaystyle B_{1}\neq B_{2}

have also been studied by many authors, see, e.g.,
[
52
,
17
,
12
]
,
and the references therein.
Their analysis, in general is more complicated and is mostly done following the
monolithic
approach, i.e., imposing
conditions on
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
rather than on
\displaystyle A
,
\displaystyle B_{1}
,
\displaystyle B_{2}
, and
\displaystyle C
separately.

Our work is motivated by the stability analysis of variational problems occurring in

[
20
]

which originates from the early works of Terzaghi and Biot

,

Various formulations of Biot’s consolidation model have been considered and analyzed since it had been introduced

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

Although they typically relate more than two physical fields, or quantities
of interest (except for the two-field formulation of Biot’s model),
the variational problems arising from the above-mentioned formulations–subject to a proper grouping or rather
aggregation of variables–result in symmetric two-by-two block systems of saddle point form characterized by a self-adjoint
operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
.

The abstract framework presented in the next section of this paper applies to such saddle-point operators. After introducing
some notation, we recall the classical stability results of Babuška and Brezzi for classical (unperturbed) SPPs.
Next, we focus on perturbed (symmetric) SPPs, initially summarizing some of the additional conditions which, together
with the Ladyzhenskaya-Babuška-Brezzi (LBB) condition (small inf-sup condition), imply the necessary and sufficient
stability condition of Babuška (big inf-sup condition). Our main theoretical result then follows in Section
2.3

where we propose a generalization of the classical Brezzi conditions for the analysis of perturbed SPPs with
\displaystyle C\neq 0
.
These new conditions imply the Babuška condition. The fitted norms on which they are based provide a constructive tool for
designing norm-equivalent preconditioners.

This paper does not discuss discretizations and discrete variants of inf-sup conditions. However, the proposed framework
directly translates to discrete settings where it also allows for shorter and simpler proofs of the well-posedness of
discrete models and error-estimates for stable discretizations.

2.
Abstract framework

2.1.
Notation and problem formulation

Consider two Hilbert spaces
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle Q
equipped with the norms

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{V}
and
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
induced by the scalar products

\displaystyle(\cdot,\cdot)_{V}
and
\displaystyle(\cdot,\cdot)_{Q}
, respectively.
We denote their product space by
\displaystyle Y:=V\times Q
and endow it
with the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Y}
defined by

(2.1)

\|y\|_{Y}^{2}=(y,y)_{Y}=(v,v)_{V}+(q,q)_{Q}=\|v\|_{V}^{2}+\|q\|_{Q}^{2}\qquad\forall y=(v;q):=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
q\end{pmatrix}\in Y.

Next, we introduce an abstract
bilinear form
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\cdot\,;\cdot),(\cdot\,;\cdot))

on
\displaystyle Y\times Y
defined by

(2.2)

\mathcal{A}((u;p),(v;q)):=a(u,v)+b(v,p)+b(u,q)-c(p,q)

for some symmetric positive semidefinite (SPSD)
bilinear forms
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
on

\displaystyle V\times V
,
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\displaystyle Q\times Q
, i.e.,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle a(u,v)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=a(v,u)\qquad\forall u,v\in V,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle a(v,v)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq 0\qquad\forall v\in V,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle c(p,q)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=c(q,p)\qquad\forall p,q\in Q,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle c(q,q)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq 0\qquad\forall q\in Q.

and a bilinear form

\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\displaystyle V\times Q
.

We assume that
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
are
continuous with respect to the norms
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{V}
and
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
, i.e.,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle a(u,v)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}_{a}\|u\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}\qquad\forall u,v\in V,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle b(v,q)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}_{b}\|v\|_{V}\|q\|_{Q}\qquad\forall v\in V,\forall q\in Q,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle c(p,q)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}_{c}\|p\|_{Q}\|q\|_{Q}\qquad\forall p,q\in Q.

Then each of these bilinear forms defines a bounded linear operator as follows:

(2.10a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle A:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}:\,\,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle Au,v\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V}=a(u,v),\qquad\forall u,v\in V,

(2.10b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle C:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}:\,\,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle Cp,q\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}=c(p,q),\qquad\forall p,q\in Q,

(2.10c)

\displaystyle\displaystyle B:V\rightarrow Q^{\prime}:\,\,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle Bv,q\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}=b(v,q),\qquad\forall v\in V,\forall q\in Q,

(2.10d)

\displaystyle\displaystyle B^{T}:Q\rightarrow V^{\prime}:\,\,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle v,B^{T}q\rangle_{V\times V^{\prime}}=b(v,q),\qquad\forall v\in V,\forall q\in Q.

Here,
\displaystyle V^{\prime}
and
\displaystyle Q^{\prime}
denote the dual spaces of
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle Q
and

\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the corresponding duality pairings.

Associated with the bilinear form
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
defined in (
2.2
) we consider the following abstract perturbed saddle-point problem

(2.11)

\mathcal{A}((u;p),(v;q))=\mathcal{F}((v;q))\qquad\forall v\in V,\forall q\in Q

which can also be written as

\mathcal{A}(x,y)=\mathcal{F}(y)\qquad\forall y\in Y,

thereby using the definitions
\displaystyle x=(u;p)
and
\displaystyle y=(v;q)
, or, in operator form

(2.12)

\mathcal{A}x=\mathcal{F}

where

(2.13)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}:\,\,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}x,y\rangle_{Y^{\prime}\times Y}=\mathcal{A}(x,y),\qquad\forall x,y\in Y

and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\in Y^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\mathcal{F}(y)=\langle\mathcal{F},y\rangle_{Y^{\prime}\times Y}

for all
\displaystyle y\in Y.

The operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
can also be represented in block form by

(2.14)

\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B^{T}\\
B&-C\end{pmatrix}.

Problem (
2.11
) (and (
2.12
)) is called a perturbed saddle-point problem (in operator form)
when
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)\not\equiv 0
and a classical saddle-point problem in
the case
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)\equiv 0
.

2.2.
Babuška’s and Brezzi’s conditions for stability of saddle-point problems

As is well-known from
[
5
]
, the abstract variational problem (
2.11
) is well-posed
under the following necessary and sufficient conditions (i) and (ii) given in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(Babuška
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\in Y^{\prime}
be a bounded linear functional.
Then the saddle-point problem (
2.11
) is well-posed if and only if there exist positive
constants
\displaystyle\bar{C}
and
\displaystyle\alpha

for which
the conditions

(2.15)

\mathcal{A}(x,y)\leq\bar{C}\|x\|_{Y}\|y\|_{Y}\qquad\forall x,y\in Y,

(2.16)

\inf_{x\in Y}\sup_{y\in Y}\frac{\mathcal{A}(x,y)}{\|x\|_{Y}\|y\|_{Y}}\geq\underline{\alpha}>0

hold.
The solution
\displaystyle x
then
satisfies the stability estimate

\|x\|_{Y}\leq\frac{1}{\underline{\alpha}}\sup_{y\in Y}\frac{\mathcal{F}(y)}{\|y\|_{Y}}=:\frac{1}{\underline{\alpha}}\|\mathcal{F}\|_{Y^{\prime}}.

Remark 2.2
.

Estimate (
2.15
) ensures continuity, that is, boundedness of
the operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
from above,
whereas (
2.16
) is a stability condition, sometimes
referred to as Babuška condition, which grants boundedness of
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
from below.

Using the operator notations introduced in (
2.14
), the conditions (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) can be rewritten as

(2.17)

\underline{\alpha}\|y\|_{Y}\leq\|\mathcal{A}y\|_{Y^{\prime}}\leq\bar{C}\|y\|_{Y}~~\hbox{for all}~~y\in Y.

In
[
63
]
, the condition (
2.17
) is characterized by two equivalent conditions as stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(Zulehner
[
63
]
)
.

If there are constants
\displaystyle\underline{\gamma}_{v},\bar{\gamma}_{v},\underline{\gamma}_{q},\bar{\gamma}_{q}>0
such that

(2.18)

\underline{\gamma}_{v}\|v\|_{V}^{2}\leq a(v,v)+\left(\sup_{q\in Q}\frac{b(v,q)}{\|q\|_{Q}}\right)^{2}\leq\bar{\gamma}_{v}\|v\|_{V}^{2}\quad\forall v\in V,

and

(2.19)

\underline{\gamma}_{q}\|q\|_{Q}^{2}\leq c(q,q)+\left(\sup_{v\in V}\frac{b(v,q)}{\|v\|_{V}}\right)^{2}\leq\bar{\gamma}_{q}\|q\|_{Q}^{2}\quad\forall q\in Q,

then (
2.17
)
is satisfied with constants
\displaystyle\underline{\alpha},\bar{C}>0
that depend only on
\displaystyle\underline{\gamma}_{v},\bar{\gamma}_{v},\underline{\gamma}_{q},\bar{\gamma}_{q}
.
And, vice versa, if the estimates (
2.17
) are satisfied with constants
\displaystyle\underline{\alpha},\bar{C}>0
, then the estimates (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) are satisfied with constants
\displaystyle\underline{\gamma}_{v},\bar{\gamma}_{v},\underline{\gamma}_{q},\bar{\gamma}_{q}>0
that depend only on
\displaystyle\underline{\alpha},\bar{C}>0
.

Remark 2.4
.

The two conditions (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), entangling the bilinear forms
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot),b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
, are equivalent to the two conditions (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) which entangle the bilinear forms
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
and the bilinear forms
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
, respectively.
However, verifying the two big conditions (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) is sometimes difficult or even impractical. Our aim is to propose a framework untangling the bilinear forms
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot),b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
and impose Brezzi-type conditions, in particular a small coercivity condition on
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
and a small inf-sup condition on
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
.

For the classical saddle-point problem, i.e.,
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)\equiv 0
, the following theorem which we formulate
under the conditions that
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric positive semidefinite
and

(2.20)

{\rm Ker}(B^{T}):=\{q\in Q:b(v,q)=0\,\,\forall v\in V\}=\emptyset

has been proven in
[
16
]
, see
also
[
17
,
12
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(Brezzi
[
16
]
)
.

Assume that the bilinear forms
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
are continuous on
\displaystyle V\times V
and on

\displaystyle V\times Q
, respectively,
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric positive semidefinite, and also that

(2.21)

a(v,v)\geq\underline{C}_{a}\|v\|_{V}^{2}\qquad\forall v\in{\rm Ker}(B),

(2.22)

\inf_{q\in Q}\sup_{v\in V}\frac{b(v,q)}{\|v\|_{V}\|q\|_{Q}}\geq\beta>0,

hold. Then the classical saddle-point problem (problem (
2.11
) with
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)\equiv 0
) is well-posed.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that if
\displaystyle{\rm Ker}(B^{T})\neq\emptyset
, the statement of the above theorem (Theorem
2.5
) remains valid if
we identify any two elements
\displaystyle q_{1}
,
\displaystyle q_{2}
for which
\displaystyle q_{0}:=q_{1}-q_{2}
is an element of
\displaystyle{\rm{Ker}}(B^{T})
, i.e.,
replacing the space
\displaystyle Q
with the quotient space
\displaystyle Q/{\rm{Ker}}(B^{T})
and also the norm

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
with
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q/{\rm{Ker}}(B^{T})}
, the latter being defined by

\|q\|_{Q/{\rm{Ker}}(B^{T})}=\inf_{q_{0}\in{\rm Ker}(B^{T})}\|q+q_{0}\|_{Q}.

In this case, the solution
\displaystyle p
is only unique up to an arbitrary element
\displaystyle p_{0}\in{\rm Ker}(B^{T})
.

For the classical saddle-point problem Brezzi’s stability condition (
2.22
)
and the continuity of
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
imply Babuška’s stability condition (
2.16
),
see
[
21
]
, where it has also been shown that from (
2.16
)
it follows (
2.22
) and the inf-sup condition for
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
in the kernel of
\displaystyle B
, the latter
being equivalent to the coercivity estimate (
2.21
) if
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric positive semidefinite.

Obviously, the stability condition (
2.16
) directly applies to perturbed saddle-point problems,
a reason why they can be studied using Babuška’s theory.
However, conditions (
2.21
) and (
2.22
) together with the continuity of
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)

and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
in general are not sufficient to guarantee the stability condition (
2.16
)
when
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)\not\equiv 0
.
Additional conditions to ensure (
2.16
) have been studied, for example in
[
17
,
14
,
12
]
.

In
[
17
]
it has been shown that a condition on the kernel of
\displaystyle B^{T}
can be used as an additional assumption
to ensure well-posedness of the perturbed saddle-point problem, that is, in particular,
for Babuška’s inf-sup condition (
2.16
) to hold.
This condition is expressed in terms of the following auxiliary problem

(2.23)

\epsilon(p_{0},q)_{Q}+c(p_{0},q)=-c(p^{\perp},q),\qquad\forall q\in{\rm Ker}(B^{T})

and requires the following general assumption:

Assumption 1
.

There exists a
\displaystyle\gamma_{0}>0
such that for every
\displaystyle p^{\perp}\in({\rm Ker}(B^{T}))^{\perp}

and for every
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
it holds that the norm of the solution
\displaystyle p_{0}\in{\rm Ker}(B^{T})
of (
2.23
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\|p_{0}\|_{Q}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{0}}\|p^{\perp}\|_{Q}
.

The theorem then reads as follows:

Theorem 2.7

(Brezzi and Fortin
[
17
]
)
.

Assume that
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
are
continuous bilinear forms on
\displaystyle V\times V
, on
\displaystyle V\times Q
, and on

\displaystyle Q\times Q
, respectively. Further assume that
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
and

\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
are symmetric positive semidefinite. Finally,
let (
2.21
), (
2.22
) (conditions (i) and (ii)
from Theorem
2.5
) and Assumption
1
be satisfied. Then for every
\displaystyle f\in V^{\prime}
and every
\displaystyle g\in{\rm Im}(B)
problem (
2.11
)
with
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
as defined in (
2.2
) and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(y)=\langle f,v\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V}+\langle g,q\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}

has a unique solution

\displaystyle x=(u;p)
in
\displaystyle Y=V\times Q/M
where

M={\rm Ker}(B^{T})\cap{\rm Ker}(C).

Moreover, the estimate

\|u\|_{V}+\|p\|_{Q/{\rm Ker}(B^{T})}\leq K(\|f\|_{V^{\prime}}+\|g\|_{Q^{\prime}})

holds with a constant
\displaystyle K
only depending on
\displaystyle\bar{C}_{a}
,
\displaystyle\bar{C}_{c}
,
\displaystyle\underline{C}_{a}
,

\displaystyle\beta
and
\displaystyle\gamma_{0}
.

Remark 2.8
.

The result in
[
17
]
is more general than Theorem
2.7
in that it applies also to non-symmetric but positive semidefinite
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
. We are
considering only the case of symmetric positive semidefinite
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
in this paper.

In order to ensure the boundedness (continuity) of the symmetric positive semidefinite bilinear form
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)

with respect to the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
, and more generally the boundedness of
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot)

with respect to the combined norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Y}=(\|\cdot\|_{V}^{2}+\|\cdot\|_{Q}^{2})^{1/2}
, it is
natural to include the contribution of
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
in the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
, e.g., by
defining
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
via

(2.24)

\|q\|^{2}_{Q}=|q|_{Q}^{2}+t^{2}c(q,q),\qquad\forall q\in Q,

for a proper seminorm or norm
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{Q}
and a parameter
\displaystyle t\in[0,1]
.

As it has been shown in
[
14
]
the stability of the perturbed saddle-point problem
then can be proven under Brezzi’s conditions for the classical saddle-point problem and the additional condition

(2.25)

\inf_{u\in V}\sup_{(v;q)\in V\times Q}\frac{a(u,v)+b(u,q)}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v;q)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}\geq\gamma>0

where
\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
is defined by

(2.26)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v;q)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}:=\|v\|_{V}^{2}+|q|_{Q}^{2}+t^{2}c(q,q),\qquad t\in[0,1],

and provides a specific choice of
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Y}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Y}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
.
The corresponding theorem then reads as:

Theorem 2.9

(Braess
[
14
]
)
.

Assume that the classical saddle-point problem with
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
being SPSD and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)\equiv 0

is stable, i.e., Brezzi’s conditions (

) and
(

) are fulfilled. If in addition condition (

) holds with

and we choose
\displaystyle t>0

\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)\not\equiv 0

then the perturbed saddle-point problem (
2.11
) is stable under the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Y}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}

and the stability constant
\displaystyle\underline{\alpha}

) depends only on
\displaystyle\beta
,
\displaystyle\underline{C}_{a}
and

.

Remark 2.10
.

Note that as it can easily be seen if
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric positive semidefinite,
condition (
2.25
) is equivalent to the condition that there exists a constant

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}>0
such that

(2.27)

\frac{a(u,u)}{\|u\|_{V}}+\sup_{q\in Q}\frac{b(u,q)}{|q|_{Q}+tc(q,q)}\geq\gamma^{\prime}\|u\|_{V}\qquad\forall u\in V.

Moreover, as shown in
[
14
]
, then (
2.27
) is also equivalent to
the condition that there exists a constant
\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime\prime}>0
such that

(2.28)

\sup_{(v;q)\in Y}\frac{\mathcal{A}((u;0),(v;q))}{{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(v;q)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}}\geq\gamma^{\prime\prime}\|u\|_{V}\qquad\forall u\in V.

Since (
2.25
) is an inf-sup condtion for
\displaystyle(a(\cdot,\cdot)+b(\cdot,\cdot))
which can be interpreted as a big
inf-sup condition on
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
for
\displaystyle p=0
under the specific norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Y}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
,
see (
2.28
), Theorem
2.9
still does not provide us with the desired stability result
in terms of conditions on
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
,
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
separately. On the other hand,
Theorem
2.7
requires the solution of the auxiliary problem (
2.23
) on
\displaystyle{\rm Ker}(B^{T})

for which one has to verify Assumption
1

which, in some situations, is a difficult task.

Our aim is to avoid the latter and still impose Brezzi-type conditions,
in particular a small inf-sup condition on
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
. In the next section, we will prove a theorem (Theorem
2.14
) which ensures the stability of the perturbed saddle-point problem (12) under conditions which are equivalent to the conditions in Brezzi’s theorem (Theorem
2.5
) when the perturbation term vanishes.
Moreover, our approach provides a framework suited for finding norms in which stability can
be shown and allows for simplifying and shortening proofs based on the result of Babuška.

2.3.
A new framework for the stability analysis of perturbed saddle-point problems

The key idea for studying and verifying the stability of perturbed saddle-point problems we follow in this
paper is to construct proper norms as part of an abstract framework which applies to a variational formulation of
various multiphysics models. As we have already observed in the previous subsection, a norm-splitting
of the form (
2.24
) is quite natural if the symmetric positive semidefinite perturbation form

\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
is not identical to zero. For fixed
\displaystyle t>0
, the norm defined in (
2.24
) is
equivalent to the norm defined by

(2.29)

\|q\|_{Q}^{2}:=|q|_{Q}^{2}+c(q,q)=:\langle\bar{Q}q,q\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}.

Note that the assumption that
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
is a full norm induced by an inner product
under which
\displaystyle Q
is a Hilbert space implies that the seminorm
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{Q}
corresponds to an SPSD
bilinear form
\displaystyle d(\cdot,\cdot):Q\times Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, i.e.,
\displaystyle|q|_{Q}^{2}=d(q,q)
. Consequently, the form

\displaystyle c(p,q)+d(p,q)
is symmetric positive definite (SPD) and defines a linear operator
\displaystyle\bar{Q}:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
by
\displaystyle\langle\bar{Q}p,q\rangle:=c(p,q)+d(p,q)
.

Now we introduce the following splitting of the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{V}
defined by

(2.30)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|v\|^{2}_{V}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=|v|^{2}_{V}+|v|^{2}_{b}

where
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{V}
is a proper seminorm, which is a norm on
\displaystyle{\rm Ker}(B)
satisfying

|v|_{V}^{2}\eqsim a(v,v)\qquad\forall v\in{\rm Ker}(B)

and
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{b}
is defined by

(2.31)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|v|^{2}_{b}:=\langle Bv,\bar{Q}^{-1}Bv\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}=\|Bv\|_{Q^{\prime}}^{2}.

Here,
\displaystyle\bar{Q}^{-1}:Q^{\prime}\rightarrow Q
is an isometric isomorphism (Riesz isomorphism) since
\displaystyle\bar{Q}
is an
isometric isomorphism, i.e.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|Bv\|_{Q^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|\bar{Q}^{-1}Bv\|_{Q}^{2}=(\bar{Q}^{-1}Bv,\bar{Q}^{-1}Bv)_{Q}=\langle\bar{Q}\bar{Q}^{-1}Bv,\bar{Q}^{-1}Bv\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}=\langle Bv,\bar{Q}^{-1}Bv\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}.

Remark 2.11
.

Note that both
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{V}
and
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{b}
can be seminorms as long as they add up to
a full norm.
Likewise, only the sum of the seminorms
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{Q}
and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
has to define a norm.
In some particular situations, it is also useful to identify certain of the involved seminorms with

\displaystyle 0
, in which case the corresponding splitting becomes a trivial splitting.
The splitting (
2.30
) is closely related to a Schur complement type operator, corresponding to the
modified (regularized) bilinear form resulting from
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\cdot;\cdot),(\cdot;\cdot))
by replacing

\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
with
\displaystyle(\cdot,\cdot)_{Q}
.

In order to present our main theoretical result, we give the following definition.

Definition 2.12
.

For two Hilbert spaces
\displaystyle V
and
\displaystyle Q
, a norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{V}
on
\displaystyle V
and a norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
on
\displaystyle Q

are called fitted
if they satisfy the splittings (
2.29
) and (
2.30
), respectively, where
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{Q}

is a seminorm on
\displaystyle Q
and
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{V}
and
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{b}
are seminorms on
\displaystyle V,

the latter defined according to (
2.31
).

Remark 2.13
.

Note that the norm fitting can also be performed by first fixing the full norm on
\displaystyle V
(instead of the full norm on
\displaystyle Q
as described above).
Exploiting the structure of the problem, in the latter case one uses the following norm splittings

\begin{array}[]{l}\|v\|_{V}:=|v|_{V}^{2}+a(v,v)=:\langle\bar{V}v,v\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V},\\
\|q\|_{Q}:=|q|_{Q}^{2}+|q|_{b}^{2},\end{array}

where
\displaystyle\bar{V}:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is a linear operator,
\displaystyle|q|_{Q}^{2}
is equivalent to
\displaystyle c(q,q)
and
\displaystyle|q|_{b}^{2}=:\langle B^{T}q,\bar{V}^{-1}B^{T}q\rangle_{V^{\prime}\times V}
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{V}
and
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Q}
be fitted norms according to Definition
2.12
,
which immediately implies the continuity of
\displaystyle b(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)

in these norms with
\displaystyle\bar{C}_{b}=1
and

\displaystyle\bar{C}_{c}=1
, cf. (
2.8
)–(
2.9
).
Consider the bilinear form
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\cdot;\cdot),(\cdot;\cdot))

defined in (
2.2
) where
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous,
i.e., (
2.7
) holds, and
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
and

\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
are symmetric positive semidefinite.
Assume, further, that
\displaystyle a(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the coercivity estimate

(2.32)

a(v,v)\geq\underline{C}_{a}|v|_{V}^{2},\qquad\forall v\in V,

and that there exists a constant

\displaystyle\underline{\beta}>0
such that

(2.33)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
v\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{b(v,q)}{\|v\|_{V}}\geq\underline{\beta}|q|_{Q},\qquad\forall q\in Q.

Then the bilinear form
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\cdot;\cdot),(\cdot;\cdot))
is continuous and inf-sup stable under the combined norm

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{Y}
defined in (

), i.e., the conditions (

Before presenting the proof of Theorem

, we show an auxiliary result and make some remarks.

Lemma 2.15
.

The inf-sup condition:
there exists a constant

\displaystyle\underline{\beta}>0
such that

(2.34)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in V\\
v\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{b(v,q)}{\|v\|_{V}}\geq\underline{\beta}|q|_{Q},\qquad\forall q\in Q,

is equivalent to the condition: for any
\displaystyle q\in Q
, there exists
\displaystyle v\in V
, such that

(2.35)

b(v,q)=|q|_{Q}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|v\|_{V}\leq\underline{\beta}^{-1}|q|_{Q}.

Proof.

Obviously, (
2.35
) implies (
2.34
). Hence, it remains to prove that (
2.34
) implies (
2.35
).
Let
\displaystyle K_{Q}=\{q\in Q:|q|_{Q}=0\}
and define
\displaystyle\displaystyle\|q\|_{Q/K_{Q}}:=|q|_{Q}
which is a norm on the quotient space
\displaystyle Q/K_{Q}
.
For any
\displaystyle q\in Q
, where the class in
\displaystyle Q/K_{Q}
which
\displaystyle q
belongs to is also denoted by
\displaystyle q
, there exists
\displaystyle f\in(Q/K_{Q})^{\prime}
s.t.
\displaystyle f(q)=\|q\|_{Q/K_{Q}}^{2}
and
\displaystyle\|f\|_{(Q/K_{Q})^{\prime}}=\|q\|_{Q/K_{Q}}
. Since
\displaystyle B
is onto, we can find
\displaystyle v
s.t.
\displaystyle Bv=f
and by the open mapping theorem, we can find
\displaystyle v
with
\displaystyle\|v\|_{V}\leq\underline{\beta}^{-1}\|f\|_{(Q/K_{Q})^{\prime}}=\underline{\beta}^{-1}\|q\|_{Q/K_{Q}}=\underline{\beta}^{-1}|q|_{Q}
and
\displaystyle b(v,q)=\langle Bv,q\rangle=f(q)=\|q\|_{Q/K_{Q}}^{2}=|q|_{Q}^{2}
.
∎

Remark 2.16

.

Demonstrating (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(({{w}};{{r}}),(v;q))=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{C}_{a}\|{{w}}\|_{V}\|v\|_{V}+\|v\|_{V}\|{{r}}\|_{Q}+\|{{w}}\|_{V}\|q\|_{Q}+\|{{r}}\|_{Q}\|q\|_{Q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{C}(\|{{w}}\|_{V}+\|{{r}}\|_{Q})(\|v\|_{V}+\|q\|_{Q})\leq 2\bar{C}\|({{w}};{{r}})\|_{Y}\|(v;q)\|_{Y}

with
\displaystyle\bar{C}:=\max\{\bar{C}_{a},1\}
.

In order to prove (
2.16
) for a positive constant
\displaystyle\delta
, which will be selected later,
and for a given arbitrary pair
\displaystyle({{w}},{{r}})\in V\times Q
, we choose

(2.40)

where, by Lemma

\displaystyle{{w}}_{0}\in V
can be chosen such that

(2.41a)

\displaystyle\displaystyle b({{w}}_{0},{{r}})=

\displaystyle\displaystyle|{{r}}|_{Q}^{2},

(2.41b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|{{w}}_{0}\|_{V}\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\underline{\beta}^{-1}|{{r}}|_{Q},

and

(2.42)

q:=-\delta{{r}}+{{r}}_{0}

where

(2.43)

{{r}}_{0}:=\bar{Q}^{-1}B{{w}}.

Note that the existence of an element
\displaystyle{{w}}_{0}
satisfying (

Then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|v\|_{V}\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\delta{{w}}\|_{V}+\|{{w}}_{0}\|_{V}\leq\delta\|{{w}}\|_{V}+\underline{\beta}^{-1}|{{r}}|_{Q}\leq\delta\|{{w}}\|_{V}+\underline{\beta}^{-1}\|{{r}}\|_{Q},

and, consequently,

Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle v
and
\displaystyle q

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(({{w}};{{r}}),(v;q))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=a({{w}},\delta{{w}}+{{w}}_{0})+b(\delta{{w}}+{{w}}_{0},{{r}})-b({{w}},\delta{{r}}-{{r}}_{0})+c({{r}},\delta{{r}}-{{r}}_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\|{{w}}\|_{V}^{2}+\|{{r}}\|_{Q}^{2}\right)={\frac{1}{4}}\|({{w}};{{r}})\|_{Y}^{2}.

3.1.
Generalized Poisson and generalized Stokes equations

Example 3.1
.

The first example, see
[
13
]
, is the following mixed variational problem resulting from a weak formulation of a
generalized Poisson equation: find
\displaystyle(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}({\rm div},\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\bm{u},\bm{v})+(p,{\rm div}\bm{v})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\rm div},\Omega),

\displaystyle\displaystyle({\rm div}\bm{u},q)-t(p,q)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-(f,q),\qquad\forall q\in L^{2}(\Omega),

where
\displaystyle t\geq 0
is a parameter.

The bilinear forms generating
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\cdot;\cdot),(\cdot;\cdot))

are given by

a(\bm{u},\bm{v}):=(\bm{u},\bm{v}),\quad b(\bm{v},q):=({\rm div}\bm{v},q),\quad c(p,q)=t(p,q),\quad\forall\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\bm{V},\forall p,q\in Q,

where

\displaystyle Q:=L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\bm{V}:=\bm{H}({\rm div},\Omega)
. Using the norm fitting technique, we define
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{Q}
,

\displaystyle|\cdot|_{V}
by

|q|_{Q}^{2}:=(q,q)\quad\forall q\in Q\quad\text{and}\quad|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}:=(\bm{v},\bm{v})\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}.

Obviously,
\displaystyle B:={\rm div}:\bm{V}\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
, and (
2.29
) and
(
2.30
) take the form

\|q\|_{Q}^{2}=|q|_{Q}^{2}+c(q,q)=(q,q)+t(q,q)=((1+t)q,q)=\langle(1+t)Iq,q\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q},

\|\bm{v}\|_{V}^{2}=|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}+|\bm{v}|_{b}^{2}=(\bm{v},\bm{v})+\langle{\rm div}\bm{v},\frac{1}{(1+t)}I{\rm div}\bm{v}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}=(\bm{v},\bm{v})+\frac{1}{(1+t)}({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v}),

respectively.
Since
\displaystyle a(\bm{v},\bm{v})=(\bm{v},\bm{v})=|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}
for all
\displaystyle\bm{v}\in\bm{V}
, condition
(
2.32
) in Theorem
2.14
is satisfied with
\displaystyle\underline{C}_{a}=1
.

Finally, we have to verify condition (
2.33
) in Theorem
2.14
, namely

\sup_{\bm{v}\in V}\frac{({\rm div}\bm{v},q)}{(\|\bm{v}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{(1+t)}\|{\rm div}\bm{v}\|^{2})^{1/2}}\geq\underline{\beta}|q|_{Q}=:\underline{\beta}\|q\|,\qquad\forall q\in Q

which follows directly from the classical inf-sup condition (
3.1
) on the spaces
\displaystyle(\bm{V},Q)
since
\displaystyle t\geq 0
.

As a result, we obtain that the preconditioner

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\begin{bmatrix}(I-(1+t)^{-1}\nabla\rm div)^{-1}&\\
&((1+t)I)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}

is norm-equivalent for the combined norm, cf.
[
47
]
. To solve the
\displaystyle\bm{H}(\rm div)
subproblem, one can use various
preconditioners, see, e.g.,
[

,
38
]
, multigrid, see, e.g.,
[

,
2
]
,
and domain decomposition methods
[

]
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that in this example as well as the ones which follow, the perturbation term
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)
, due to the presence of various parameters,
can dominate the problem. In this situation stability often cannot be proven using Theorem

because

\displaystyle\|q\|_{Q}
has to bound
\displaystyle c(q,q)^{\frac{1}{2}}
which for dominating perturbation conflicts satisfying the classical LBB condition (
2.22
) uniformly.
A way to overcome this problem is to work either with the Braess inf-sup condition, see Theorem
2.9
,
or with the Babuška inf-sup condition, see Theorem
2.1
,
or, alternatively use the simpler to verify inf-sup condition provided in Theorem
2.14
.

Example 3.3
.

The second example we consider is taken from
[

]
.
Its mixed variational formulation reads: find
\displaystyle(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times(H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega))

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\nabla\bm{u},\nabla\bm{v})-(p,{\rm div}\bm{v})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(\bm{f},\bm{v}),\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\displaystyle-({\rm div}\bm{u},q)-(\kappa\nabla p,\nabla q)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(g,q),\qquad\forall q\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

where
\displaystyle\kappa\geq 0
is a parameter.

The bilinear forms defining
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\cdot;\cdot),(\cdot;\cdot))

here are given by

In this example, we set

\displaystyle Q:=H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
,
\displaystyle\bm{V}:=\bm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, and
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{Q}
,

\displaystyle|\cdot|_{V}
to be

|q|_{Q}^{2}:=(q,q)\quad\forall q\in Q\quad\text{and}\quad|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}:=(\nabla\bm{v},\nabla\bm{v})\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}.

Then the operator
\displaystyle B
is defined by
\displaystyle B:\bm{V}\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
,
\displaystyle B:=-{\rm div}
and the norm splittings (
2.29
) and (
2.30
) are given by

(3.5)

\|q\|_{Q}^{2}=|q|_{Q}^{2}+c(q,q)=(q,q)+(\kappa\nabla q,\nabla q)=\langle\bar{Q}q,q\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}

and

\|\bm{v}\|_{V}^{2}=|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}+|\bm{v}|_{b}^{2}=(\nabla\bm{v},\nabla\bm{v})+\langle B\bm{v},\bar{Q}^{-1}B\bm{v}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}.

Condition (
2.32
) is automatically satisfied with
\displaystyle\underline{C}_{a}=1
. To show
(
2.33

3.5
) we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rl}\langle B\bm{v},\bar{Q}^{-1}B\bm{v}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}&=\|B\bm{v}\|_{Q^{\prime}}^{2}=\left(\sup_{q\neq 0}\frac{b(\bm{v},q)}{\|q\|_{Q}}\right)^{2}=\left(\sup_{q\neq 0}\frac{({\rm div}\bm{v},q)}{\|q\|_{Q}}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\sup_{q\neq 0}\frac{\|{\rm div}\bm{v}\|\|q\|}{\|q\|_{Q}}\right)^{2}\\
&\leq({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v}).\end{array}

Thus,

(3.6)

\|\bm{v}\|_{V}^{2}=(\nabla\bm{v},\nabla\bm{v})+\langle B\bm{v},\bar{Q}^{-1}B\bm{v}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}\leq(\nabla\bm{v},\nabla\bm{v})+({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v})\leq 2(\nabla\bm{v},\nabla\bm{v})\leq 2\|\bm{v}\|_{1}^{2}.

Now, we choose
\displaystyle\bm{v}_{0}
such that
\displaystyle-{\rm div}\bm{v}_{0}=q
and hereby obtain from the Stokes
inf-sup condition (
3.2

\displaystyle\|\bm{v}_{0}\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{s}}\|q\|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{v}\in V}\frac{b(\bm{v},q)}{\|\bm{v}\|_{V}}\geq\frac{b(\bm{v}_{0},q)}{\|\bm{v}_{0}\|_{V}}=\frac{\|q\|^{2}}{\|\bm{v}_{0}\|_{V}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\|q\|^{2}}{\|\bm{v}_{0}\|_{1}}\geq\frac{\beta_{s}}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\|q\|^{2}}{\|q\|}=:\underline{\beta}\|q\|=\underline{\beta}|q|_{Q},\qquad\forall q\in Q.

The induced norm-equivalent preconditioner in this example reads as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\begin{bmatrix}(-\Delta-\nabla(I-{\rm div}\kappa\nabla)^{-1}{\rm div})^{-1}&\\
&(I-{\rm div}\kappa\nabla)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\eqsim\begin{bmatrix}-\Delta^{-1}&\\
&(I-{\rm div}\kappa\nabla)^{-1}\end{bmatrix},

where the equivalence is due to (
3.6

3.2.
Stokes Darcy problem

Example 3.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\Omega=\Omega_{S}\cup\Omega_{D}
and
\displaystyle\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{S}\cap\partial\Omega_{D}
. We assume that
\displaystyle\Gamma_{S}^{D}\cup\Gamma_{S}^{N}\cup\Gamma
forms a disjoint decomposition of
\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{S}
and, similarly,
\displaystyle\Gamma_{D}^{D}\cup\Gamma_{D}^{N}\cup\Gamma
is a partition of
\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{D}
. Denote

H^{1}_{\Gamma_{i}^{D}}(\Omega_{i})=\left\{w\in H^{1}(\Omega_{i}):w|_{\Gamma_{i}^{D}}=0\right\},~~i=S~\hbox{or}~D.

The classical formulation of Stokes Darcy problem follows
[
22
]
: Find
\displaystyle\left(\bm{u},p_{S},p_{D}\right)\in\bm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{S}^{D}}(\Omega_{S})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{S})\times H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}^{D}}(\Omega_{D})
such that

\displaystyle\left(2\mu\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{u}\right),\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}\right)\right)_{\Omega_{S}}+\beta_{\tau}\left(\bm{\tau}\cdot\bm{u},\bm{\tau}\cdot\bm{v}\right)_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle-\left(p_{S},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{S}}+\left(p_{D},\bm{n}\cdot\bm{v}\right)_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{f}_{S},\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{S}},

\displaystyle\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{S}^{D}}(\Omega_{S}),

\displaystyle-\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{u},q_{S}\right)_{\Omega_{S}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\forall q_{S}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{S}),

\displaystyle\left(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{u},q_{D}\right)_{\Gamma}-\left(\kappa\nabla p_{D},\nabla q_{D}\right)_{\Omega_{D}}

\displaystyle=\left(f_{D},q_{D}\right)_{\Omega_{D}},

\displaystyle\forall q_{D}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}^{D}}(\Omega_{D}),

where
\displaystyle\bm{n}:=\bm{n}_{S}
is the outer normal of the Stokes domain and
\displaystyle\bm{\tau}:=\bm{I}-(\bm{n}\otimes\bm{n})
is the projection onto the tangent bundle of the interface
\displaystyle\Gamma
.

The bilinear forms defining
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\cdot;\cdot),(\cdot;\cdot))

here are given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle a(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\left(2\mu\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{u}\right),\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}\right)\right)_{\Omega_{S}}+\beta_{\tau}\left(\bm{\tau}\cdot\bm{u},\bm{\tau}\cdot\bm{v}\right)_{\Gamma},\quad\forall\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\bm{V},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad b(\bm{v},\bm{q})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=-\left(q_{S},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}\right)_{\Omega_{S}}+\left(q_{D},\bm{n}\cdot\bm{v}\right)_{\Gamma},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V},\forall\bm{q}\in\bm{Q},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad c(\bm{p},\bm{q})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\left(\kappa\nabla p_{D},\nabla q_{D}\right)_{\Omega_{D}},\quad\forall\bm{p},\bm{q}\in\bm{Q},

where
\displaystyle\bm{V}=\bm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{S}^{D}}(\Omega_{S}),\bm{Q}=L^{2}(\Omega_{S})\times H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}^{D}}(\Omega_{D})
and
\displaystyle\bm{p}=(p_{S},p_{D}),\bm{q}=(q_{S},q_{D})
.
We fix
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{Q}
,
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{V}
to be

|\bm{q}|_{Q}^{2}:=(2\mu)^{-1}(q_{S},q_{S})_{\Omega_{S}}+(2\mu)^{-1}\|q_{D}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}^{2},\quad\forall\bm{q}\in\bm{Q},

|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}:=\left(2\mu\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}\right),\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}\right)\right)_{\Omega_{S}}+\beta_{\tau}\left(\bm{\tau}\cdot\bm{v},\bm{\tau}\cdot\bm{v}\right)_{\Gamma},\quad\forall\bm{u}\in\bm{V}.

Noting that
\displaystyle a(\bm{v},\bm{v})=|\bm{v}|^{2}_{\bm{V}}
for all
\displaystyle\bm{v}\in\bm{V}
, that is,
(
2.32
) is satisfied with
\displaystyle\underline{C}_{a}=1
. In addition, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{q}\|_{Q}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=|\bm{q}|_{Q}^{2}+c(\bm{q},\bm{q})=\langle\bar{Q}\bm{q},\bm{q}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q},~\forall\bm{q}\in\bm{Q},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=|\bm{v}|_{\bm{V}}^{2}+\langle B\bm{v},\bar{Q}^{-1}B\bm{v}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}=|\bm{v}|_{\bm{V}}^{2}+|\bm{v}|_{b}^{2},~\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}.

The continuity of
\displaystyle B
is shown by the following calculation, utilizing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and a trace inequality:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\langle B\bm{v},\bm{q}\right\rangle_{\bm{Q}^{\prime}\times\bm{Q}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{v},p_{S}\right)_{\Omega_{S}}+\left(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{v},q_{D}\right)_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla\cdot\bm{v}\|_{\Omega_{S}}\|q_{S}\|_{\Omega_{S}}+\|\bm{n}\cdot\bm{v}\|_{\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}\|q_{D}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}\right)\|_{\Omega_{S}}\left(\|q_{S}\|_{\Omega_{S}}+\|q_{D}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(2\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}\right)\|_{\Omega_{S}}(2\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|q_{S}\|_{\Omega_{S}}^{2}+\|q_{D}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|\bm{v}|_{\bm{V}}\left|q\right|_{\bm{Q}}\leq|\bm{v}|_{V}\|q\|_{\bm{Q}}

Furthermore, by (
2.31
), we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\bm{v}|_{b}=\|B\bm{v}\|_{\bm{Q}^{\prime}}=\sup_{\bm{q}\in\bm{Q}}\frac{\left\langle B\bm{v},\bm{q}\right\rangle_{\bm{Q}^{\prime}\times\bm{Q}}}{\|q\|_{\bm{Q}}}\leq|\bm{v}|_{\bm{V}},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V},

and
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\bm{V}}
is equivalent to
\displaystyle|\cdot|_{\bm{V}}
, namely

(3.9)

\|\bm{v}\|_{\bm{V}}\eqsim|\bm{v}|_{\bm{V}},\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}.

Now we show that (
2.33
) is satisfied. By (
3.9
), it suffices to show the following inf-sup condition of
\displaystyle B
:
there exists a constant
\displaystyle\underline{\beta}>0
such that

(3.10)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{v}\in\bm{V}\\
\bm{v}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{b(\bm{v},\bm{q})}{|\bm{v}|_{\bm{V}}}\geq\underline{\beta}|\bm{q}|_{\bm{Q}},\quad\forall\bm{q}\in\bm{Q}.

For any given
\displaystyle\bm{q}=\left(q_{S},q_{D}\right)
, let
\displaystyle\bm{v}^{S}\in

\displaystyle\bm{H}^{1}\left(\Omega_{S}\right)
be constructed, using the Stokes inf-sup condition (
3.2
), such that

(3.11)

\bm{v}^{S}|_{\Gamma}=0,\quad\nabla\cdot\bm{v}^{S}=-q_{S},\quad\|\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}^{S}\right)\|_{\Omega_{S}}\leq\beta_{s}\|q_{S}\|_{\Omega_{S}}.

On the other hand, let
\displaystyle\phi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
be the Riesz representative of
\displaystyle\left.q_{D}\right|_{\Gamma}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
. We then define
\displaystyle\bm{v}^{D}\in\bm{H}^{1}\left(\Omega_{S}\right)
as the bounded extension that satisfies

(3.12)

\bm{v}^{D}|_{\Gamma}=\phi\bm{n},\quad\nabla\cdot\bm{v}^{D}=0,\quad\|\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}^{D}\right)\|_{\Omega_{S}}\leq\beta_{0}\|\phi\|_{\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}=\beta_{0}\|q_{D}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}.

We now set the test function
\displaystyle\bm{v}_{0}:=(2\mu)^{-1}\left(\bm{v}^{S}+\bm{v}^{D}\right)
. Noting that
\displaystyle\bm{\tau}\cdot\bm{v}_{0}=0
on
\displaystyle\Gamma
, this function satisfies

\displaystyle b(\bm{v}_{0},\bm{q})

\displaystyle=-(2\mu)^{-1}\left(\nabla\cdot\bm{v}^{S},q_{S}\right)_{\Omega_{D}}+(2\mu)^{-1}\left(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{v}^{D},q_{D}\right)_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=(2\mu)^{-1}\|q_{S}\|_{\Omega_{S}}^{2}+(2\mu)^{-1}\|q_{D}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}^{2}=|\bm{q}|_{\bm{Q}}^{2},

\displaystyle|\bm{v}_{0}|_{\bm{V}}

\displaystyle=(2\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bm{\epsilon}\left((2\mu)^{-1}\left(\bm{v}^{S}+\bm{v}^{D}\right)\right)\|_{\Omega_{S}}

\displaystyle\leq(2\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}^{S}\right)\|_{\Omega_{S}}+\|\bm{\epsilon}\left(\bm{v}^{D}\right)\|_{\Omega_{S}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(2\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|q_{S}\|_{\Omega_{S}}+\|q_{D}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}\right)=|\bm{q}|_{\bm{Q}}.

Hence, condition (
3.10
) is fulfilled.

.

find
\displaystyle\bm{p}\in\bm{H}_{0}({\rm curl},\Omega),\bm{u}\in\bm{H}_{0}({\rm div},\Omega)
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\alpha\bm{p},\bm{q})-(\bm{u},{\rm curl}\bm{q})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\bm{q}\in\bm{H}_{0}({\rm curl},\Omega),

\displaystyle\displaystyle-({\rm curl}\bm{p},\bm{v})-({\rm div}\bm{u},{\rm div}\bm{v})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(f,\bm{v}),\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}({\rm div},\Omega),

The bilinear forms that define

are

\displaystyle\displaystyle a(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=({\rm div}\bm{u},{\rm div}\bm{v}),\qquad\forall\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\bm{V},

\displaystyle\displaystyle b(\bm{v},\bm{p})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=({\rm curl}\bm{p},\bm{v}),\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V},\forall\bm{p}\in\bm{Q},

\displaystyle\displaystyle c(\bm{p},\bm{q})

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=(\alpha\bm{p},\bm{q}),\qquad\forall\bm{p},\bm{q}\in\bm{Q},

where
\displaystyle\bm{V}=\bm{H}_{0}({\rm div},\Omega),\bm{Q}=\bm{H}_{0}({\rm curl},\Omega)

\displaystyle|\cdot|_{Q}
and

\displaystyle|\cdot|_{V}
to be

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\bm{q}|_{Q}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=((\alpha+1){\rm curl}\bm{q},{\rm curl}\bm{q}),\qquad\forall\bm{q}\in\bm{Q},

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v}),\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}.

As in the previous examples,
\displaystyle a(\bm{v},\bm{v})=|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}
for all
\displaystyle\bm{v}\in\bm{V}

(
2.32
) is satisfied with
\displaystyle\underline{C}_{a}=1

\displaystyle B:={\rm curl^{*}}:\bm{V}\rightarrow\bm{Q}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{q}\|_{Q}^{2}

Remark 3.6
.

Consider
\displaystyle\bm{H}({\rm div}_{0},\Omega)=\{\bm{v}\in\bm{H}({\rm div},\Omega):{\rm div}\bm{v}=0\}
.
Then for any
\displaystyle\alpha>0
and
\displaystyle\bm{v}\in\bm{H}_{0}({\rm curl},\Omega)\cap\bm{H}({\rm div}_{0},\Omega)
, we have

((\alpha I+(\alpha+1){\rm curl^{*}curl})\bm{v},\bm{v})\leq(c_{P}\alpha+(\alpha+1))({\rm curl^{*}curl}\bm{v},\bm{v})\leq c_{1}((\alpha+1){\rm curl^{*}curl}\bm{v},\bm{v}),

where

\displaystyle c_{P}
denotes the Poincaré constant for the
\displaystyle\rm curl
operator and
\displaystyle c_{1}=c_{P}+1
. Hence,

((\alpha I+(\alpha+1){\rm curl^{*}curl})^{-1}\bm{f},\bm{f})\geq c_{1}^{-1}((\alpha+1)^{-1}({\rm curl^{*}curl})^{-1}\bm{f},\bm{f}),

for any
\displaystyle\bm{f}\in(\alpha I+(\alpha+1){\rm curl^{*}curl})\left(\bm{H}_{0}({\rm curl},\Omega)\cap\bm{H}({\rm div}_{0},\Omega)\right)
.
Now, by using the Helmholtz decomposition
\displaystyle\bm{v}={\rm curl}\bm{w}+\nabla z
of
\displaystyle\bm{v}
and choosing
\displaystyle\bm{f}={\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v}
we obtain

(3.16)

c_{1}^{-1}((\alpha+1)^{-1}{\rm curl}\bm{w},{\rm curl}\bm{w})\leq\left((\alpha I+(\alpha+1){\rm curl^{*}curl})^{-1}{\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v},{\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v}\right).

On the other hand, the Poincare’s inequality for vector Laplacian (see Theorem 2.2 in
[
3
]
) implies

(3.17)

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\nabla z,\nabla z)\leq c_{P_{v}}\left(({\rm div}(\nabla z),{\rm div}(\nabla z))+({\rm curl}(\nabla z),{\rm curl}(\nabla z))\right)=c_{P_{v}}({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v}),

where
\displaystyle c_{P_{v}}
denotes the Poincaré constant for vector Laplacian.
By multiplying (
3.17
) with
\displaystyle c_{1}^{-1}(\alpha+1)^{-1}
we obtain

(3.18)

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{1}^{-1}(\alpha+1)^{-1}(\nabla z,\nabla z)\leq c_{1}^{-1}(\alpha+1)^{-1}c_{P_{v}}({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v})\leq c_{1}^{-1}c_{P_{v}}({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v}).

Combining (
3.16
) and (
3.18
) and noting that
\displaystyle(\bm{v},\bm{v})=({\rm curl}\bm{w},{\rm curl}\bm{w})+(\nabla z,\nabla z)
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{1}^{-1}(\alpha+1)^{-1}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}\leq c_{1}^{-1}c_{P_{v}}\|{\rm div}\bm{v}\|^{2}+\left((\alpha I+(\alpha+1){\rm curl^{*}curl})^{-1}{\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v},{\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v}\right).

Next we add
\displaystyle c_{1}^{-1}\|{\rm div}\bm{v}\|^{2}
to both sides and get

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{1}^{-1}\big((\alpha+1)^{-1}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}+\|{\rm div}\bm{v}\|^{2}\big)\leq c_{1}^{-1}(c_{P_{v}}+1)\|{\rm div}\bm{v}\|^{2}+\left((\alpha I+(\alpha+1){\rm curl^{*}curl})^{-1}{\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v},{\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v}\right).

By multiplying with
\displaystyle c_{1}
and setting
\displaystyle c_{2}:=\max\left\{c_{1},(c_{P_{v}}+1)\right\}
it follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\alpha+1)^{-1}\|\bm{v}\|^{2}+({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v})\leq c_{2}\|\bm{v}\|_{V}^{2}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{V}^{2}=|\bm{v}|_{V}^{2}+\langle B\bm{v},\bar{Q}^{-1}B\bm{v}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v})+\left((\alpha I+{\rm curl^{*}}(\alpha+1){\rm curl})^{-1}{\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v},{\rm curl^{*}}\bm{v}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v})+\left((\alpha+1)^{-1}\bm{v},\bm{v}\right).

Therefore we conclude that
\displaystyle\|\bm{v}\|_{V}^{2}
and
\displaystyle({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v})+((\alpha+1)^{-1}\bm{v},\bm{v})
are equivalent.

The corresponding norm-equivalent preconditioner is then given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{B}:=\begin{bmatrix}((\alpha+1)^{-1}I-\nabla{\rm div})^{-1}&\\
&(\alpha I+(\alpha+1){\rm curl}^{*}{\rm curl})^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

To solve the
\displaystyle\bm{H}(\rm curl)
subproblem,
one can use the preconditioner proposed in
[
30
]
,
the multigrid method in
[
2
]
, or domain decomposition methods
[
59
]
.

Example 3.7

,

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=(\varepsilon(\bm{v}),\varepsilon(\bm{v}))+\lambda({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v}),\qquad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V},

\displaystyle B:\bm{V}\rightarrow Q^{\prime}

\displaystyle B:=-\alpha{\rm div}
. Therefore, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rl}\|\bm{v}\|_{V}^{2}&=(\varepsilon(\bm{v}),\varepsilon(\bm{v}))+\lambda({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v})+\langle B\bm{v},\bar{Q}^{-1}B\bm{v}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}\\
&\leq(\varepsilon(\bm{v}),\varepsilon(\bm{v}))+\left(\lambda+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\eta}\right)({\rm div}\bm{v},{\rm div}\bm{v})\leq\left(1+\lambda+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\eta}\right)\|\bm{v}\|_{1}^{2}.\end{array}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{b(\bar{\bm{v}}_{0},\bm{q})}{\|\bar{\bm{v}}_{0}\|_{V}}\geq\frac{|\bm{q}|_{Q}^{2}}{\sqrt{2\max\{\beta_{s}^{-2},\beta_{d}^{-2}\}}|\bm{q}|_{Q}}=:\underline{\beta}|\bm{q}|_{Q},\qquad\forall\bm{q}\in\bm{Q},

where we have used (
3.26
) and (
3.27
).

From our findings we conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}\left(\begin{bmatrix}-2\mu{\rm div}\varepsilon&\\
&(\tau\kappa)^{-1}I\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}-\nabla&\\
&\nabla\end{bmatrix}\mathcal{C}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\rm{div}&\\
&-\rm{div}\end{bmatrix}\right)^{-1}\\
&\mathcal{C}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eqsim\begin{bmatrix}(-2\mu{\rm div}\varepsilon)^{-1}&&\\
&\tau\kappa(I-\nabla{\rm div})^{-1}&\\
&&\mathcal{C}^{-1}\end{bmatrix},

provides a norm-equivalent preconditioner,
where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{C}:=\begin{bmatrix}(\lambda^{-1}+(2\mu)^{-1})I&\alpha\lambda^{-1}I\\
\alpha\lambda^{-1}I&(c_{0}+\alpha^{2}\lambda^{-1}+\tau\kappa)I\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{C}^{-1}=\eta\begin{bmatrix}2\mu(\alpha^{2}+\lambda(c_{0}+\tau\kappa))I&-2\mu\alpha I\\
-2\mu\alpha I&(\lambda+2\mu)I\end{bmatrix},

with
\displaystyle\eta=1/(\alpha^{2}+(\lambda+2\mu)(c_{0}+\tau\kappa))
.

Remark 3.15
.

Noting that for any
\displaystyle\epsilon\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\bar{\bm{v}}\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\bar{\bm{v}}|_{V}^{2}+\langle B\bar{\bm{v}},\bar{Q}^{-1}B\bar{\bm{v}}\rangle_{Q^{\prime}\times Q}=a(\bar{\bm{v}},\bar{\bm{v}})+(R_{p}+\frac{1}{1+\lambda_{\mu}}+\alpha_{p})^{-1}(\text{Div}\bar{\bm{v}},\text{Div}\bar{\bm{v}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+(R_{p}+\frac{1}{1+\lambda_{\mu}}+\alpha_{p})^{-1}({\rm div}\bm{v}+{\rm div}\bm{z},{\rm div}\bm{v}+{\rm div}\bm{z})

4.
Conclusion

In this paper we have presented a new framework for the stability analysis of perturbed saddle-point problems in variational formulation
in a Hilbert space setting. Our aproach is constructive and is based on a specific norm fitting technique. The main theoretical
result (Theorem
2.14
) is a generalization of the classical splitting theorem (Theorem
2.5
) of Brezzi and
allows to conclude the necessary stability condition (big inf-sup condition) according to Babuška’s theory (Theorem
2.1
)
from conditions similar to those on which Brezzi’s theorem is based also in presence of a symmetric positive semidefinite perturbation
term
\displaystyle c(\cdot,\cdot)\not\equiv 0
.

As demonstrated on mixed formulations of generalized Poisson, Stokes, vector Laplacian, and Biot’s equations,
the new
norm fitting technique guids the process of defining proper parameter-dependent norms and allows for simple and short proofs of the
stability of perturbed saddle-point problems.
Although the examples in the present paper are continuous (infinite-dimensional) models, the abstract framework suggests that the
proposed technique is also quite useful when studying the stability of various mixed (finite element) discretizations and
has a wide range of applications.

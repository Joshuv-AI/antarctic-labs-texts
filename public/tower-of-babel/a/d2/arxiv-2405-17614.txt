Separation theorems for bounded convex sets of bounded operators
Mikaël Pichot, Erik Séguin

Abstract
. We establish new metric characterizations for the norm (respectively, ultraweak) closure of the convex hull of a bounded set in an arbitrary C ∗ C^{*} -algebra (respectively, von Neumann algebra), and provide applications of these results to the majorization theory.



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra, let
x\in M
, and let
X\subset M
be a bounded set.
We prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}
, where
\operatorname{conv}X
denotes the convex hull of
X
and the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology;

(2)

for every
y\in M
, there exists
z\in X
such that

\|x-z\|_{2}\leq\|y-z\|_{2}.

Here by
\|\cdot\|_{2}
we mean the 2-norm associated with a faithful normal state.

This result provides a metric characterization for the ultraweakly closed convex hull of a bounded set in
M
. It can be viewed as a separation theorem for bounded convex sets and can be used in some cases as a substitute to the Hahn-Banach theorem. In the present paper, we are mainly interested in applications to the majorization theory. In the most classical case of matrix majorization, we prove the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be arbitrary
n\times n
complex matrices. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

there exists unitary matrices
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
and positive numbers
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}=1,\qquad A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}U_{i}BU_{i}^{*};

(2)

for every complex matrix
C
, there exists a unitary matrix
U
such that

\|A-UBU^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|C-UBU^{*}\|_{2}.

Here by
\|\cdot\|_{2}
we mean the Frobenius norm.

We refer to
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
, and
[
11
]
for introductions to the majorization theory (see also §
3
below for the basic definitions). When
A
and
B
are Hermitian matrices, Conditions (1) and (2) are equivalent to the well-known majorization relation
A\prec B
between self-adjoint matrices (see Def.
3.3
). More general majorization results, for arbitrary elements in von Neumann algebras and
C^{*}
-algebras, will be established in this paper.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a separation lemma for bounded sets in Hilbert spaces (Lemma
2.1
). Although we are mainly discussing majorization in the present paper, we mention that in
[
15
]
, a related separation lemma (for general, not necessarily bounded sets) was used as an intermediate step in the proof of an Ulam-type stability characterization of amenability for groups, in terms of positive definite maps with values in
B(\mathcal{H})
.

In §
4
we generalize Theorem
1.1
to arbitrary von Neumann algebras, and use this generalization to prove (in §
5
) the following separation result for the norm closed convex hull of a bounded set in an arbitrary
C^{*}
-algebra.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra,
{X\subset A}
be a bounded set, and
{x\in A}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

{x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
, where the closure is with respect to the norm topology;

(2)

for every state
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}(A)}
and
{y\in A}
, there exists
{z\in X}
such that

\|{x-z}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|{y-z}\|_{2,\psi}.

The majorization theory in von Neumann algebras (see
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
) and
C^{*}
-algebras (see
[
17
,
14
,
13
,
12
]
) is well-studied.
For self-adjoint elements in a
C^{*}
-algebra, Theorem 1.1 in
[
13
]

provides a general majorization criterion in terms of lower semi-continuous traces. At the cost of testing against all states on
A
, we obtain the following characterization of the norm closure of the convex hull of the unitary orbit of a non-necessarily self-adjoint operator in a general
C^{*}
-algebra.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and let
x,y\in A
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(A)\}}
, where
U(A)
denotes the unitary group of
A
and the closure is with respect to the norm topology;

(2)

for every state
\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}(A)
and
z\in A
, there exists
u\in U(A)
such that

\|x-uyu^{*}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|z-uyu^{*}\|_{2,\psi}.

We conclude this paper with some remarks on submajorization (§
6
).

2.
A lemma

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a (real or complex) Hilbert space.
We begin with a separation lemma which provides a metric characterization of the norm closure of a bounded convex set in
\mathcal{H}
. We do not assume that
\mathcal{H}
is separable.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathcal{H}
be a bounded set, let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be a vector, and let
\Omega\subset\mathcal{H}
be a dense set. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\xi\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}
, where the closure is with respect to the norm topology;

(2)

for every
\eta\in\Omega
, there exists
\zeta\in X
such that

\|\xi-\zeta\|\leq\|\eta-\zeta\|.

Proof.

Lemma 2.2 in
[
15
]
shows that (1) implies (2); we establish the
converse here. Suppose that
{\xi\notin{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}}
and (2) holds.

Let
\eta_{0}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\xi
onto
{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
. Since
\Omega
is dense, there exists a sequence
(\eta_{n})\subset\Omega
such that
\eta_{n}\to\eta_{0}
.
We may assume that
\eta_{n}\neq\xi
for every
n
. In this case, the median (real) hyperplane
M_{n}
for
[\xi,\eta_{n}]
(thus,
M_{n}
consists of all vectors equidistant to
\xi
and
\eta_{n}
) divides
\mathcal{H}
into two disjoint open half-spaces. We call
H_{n}
the open half-space associated with
M_{n}
which contains
\eta_{n}
.

Since
\eta_{0}
is the orthogonal projection of
\xi
onto
{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
, the latter set is included in
H_{0}
. We claim that if
{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
is a bounded set, it must be included in
H_{n}
for every
n
sufficiently large.

Note that there exists a ball
B_{0}
of center
[\xi,\eta_{0}]\cap M_{0}
in
M_{0}
such that
{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
is included in
B_{0}\times\ell
, where
\ell
denotes the span of
\xi-\eta_{0}
. For every
\varepsilon>0
and every
n
sufficiently large,
M_{n}\cap B_{0}\times\ell
is included in the
\varepsilon
-neighbourhood of
B_{0}
. We let
\varepsilon<\frac{1}{4}\|\xi-\eta_{0}\|
, and find an
N
such that for every
n\geq N
,
{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}\subset H_{n}
.

In particular, there exists
n\geq 0
such that

\|\eta_{n}-\zeta\|<\|\xi-\zeta\|

for every
\zeta\in{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
.

Since
\eta_{n}\in\Omega
, we obtain for
{\zeta\in X}
as in (2)

\|\xi-\zeta\|\leq\|\eta_{n}-\zeta\|<\|\xi-\zeta\|

which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

It is clear from the proof that the second condition may be replaced with

(2’)

the closure of the set

\Omega_{\xi}:=\{\eta\in\Omega:\ \exists\zeta\in X:\|\xi-\zeta\|\leq\|\eta-\zeta\|\}

contains the boundary of
\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}
.

The equivalence between (1) and (2) under the condition that
\Omega=\mathcal{H}
in (2) is a particular case of
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
. In particular, it follows, in the setting of Lemma
2.1
, that
\Omega_{\xi}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
if and only if
\Omega_{\xi}=\Omega
.

We next show that the assumption that
X
is bounded in Lemma
2.1
is important. The following counterexample shows the result fails in general for unbounded convex sets.

Proposition 2.3
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

there exists a non-empty closed convex set
X\subset\mathcal{H}
, a dense set
\Omega\subset\mathcal{H}
, and an element
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
for which the equivalence in Lemma
2.1
fails;

(2)

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}\geq 2
.

Proof.

We first prove no counterexample to Lemma
2.1
can exist in dimension one. Suppose
X
is a non-empty subset of
\mathbb{R}=B(\mathbb{R})
. Then
\operatorname{conv}X
is an interval in
\mathbb{R}
, and for every dense set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
,
\xi\in{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
if and only if
\forall\eta\in\Omega
,
\exists\zeta\in X
such that

|\xi-\zeta|\leq|\eta-\zeta|.

Indeed, if
\xi\not\in{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
, then by density we may choose an
\eta
which lies in the non-empty open interval between
\xi
and
{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
. Then it is clear that for every
\zeta\in X
,
\eta
is closer to
\zeta
than
\xi
is. Conversely, if
\xi\in{\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
and
\eta
is an element in
\Omega
, then regardless of the relative position of
\xi
and
\eta
, it is not difficult to prove that there will always exist some
\zeta
in
X
which is closer (although not necessarily strictly) to
\xi
than to
\eta
.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space of finite real dimension
n\geq 2
, which we shall identify with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for convenience.
We consider the closed convex set

X=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{H}:x_{1}\leq 0\}

let
\xi=(1,0,\ldots,0)
, and let
\Omega:=\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}\xi
. Clearly
\xi\not\in X
and
\Omega
is a dense set. We claim that Condition (2) in Lemma
2.1
holds. Indeed, let
\eta\in\Omega
. Then, since
\eta\not\in\mathbb{R}\xi
, the median (real) hyperplane for
[\eta,\xi]
intersects the boundary hyperplane

\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{H}:x_{1}=0\}.

Therefore, there exists an element
\zeta\in X
such that

\|\xi-\zeta\|<\|\eta-\zeta\|.

This proves that (2) holds.

A similar argument clearly works in infinite dimension.
∎

3.
Separation theorems and majorization

In this section we let
M
be a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra and fix a faithful normal state
\psi
on
M
. We write
\|x\|_{2}:=\sqrt{\psi(x^{*}x)}
for the 2-norm associated with the state
\psi
, and view
M
as a dense subset of
L^{2}(M,\psi)
.
A direct application of Lemma
2.1
gives:

Theorem 3.1

(Separation theorem in
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebras)
.

Let
X\subset M
be a bounded set and
{x\in M}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology;

(2)

for every
y\in M
, there exists
z\in X
such that

\|x-z\|_{2}\leq\|y-z\|_{2}.

Proof.

Since
X
is bounded and
M
is dense in the
2
-norm topology, it follows by Lemma
2.1
that

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}

if and only if for every
y\in M
, there exists
z\in X
such that

\|x-z\|_{2}\leq\|y-z\|_{2}

where the closure is relative to the
2
-norm. Since the
2
-norm topology coincides with the ultrastrong topology on bounded sets, this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

It is not difficult to extend Theorem
3.1
to direct sums of
\sigma
-finite algebras. Namely, let
S
be a set, let
M=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in S}M_{\alpha}
be a direct sum of
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebras, and for every

\alpha\in S
, let
\psi_{\alpha}
be a faithful normal state on
M_{\alpha}
. If
X\subset M
is a bounded set and
x\in M
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\,X}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology;

(2)

for every
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in S
and
y\in M
, there exists
z\in X
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x-z\|_{2,\psi_{\alpha_{i}}}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|y-z\|_{2,\psi_{\alpha_{i}}}^{2}.

We shall in fact establish a completely general statement, valid for all von Neumann algebras, in §
4
below. The particular case of direct sums of
\sigma
-finite algebras is interesting on its own, as it is sufficient for some applications, including to double duals of separable
C^{*}
-algebras (the fact that double duals of separable
C^{*}
-algebras are direct sums of
\sigma
-finite algebras is well-known to experts—for example, Elliott uses it in
[
3
, Lemma 3.4]
; we refer to
[
16
]
for more details).

As mentioned in the introduction, Theorem
3.1
has applications in the majorization theory.
We first recall the following definition (see
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
).

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose
x,y\in M
are self-adjoint, and
\psi
is tracial and factorial. We say that
x
is majorized by
y
, and write
x\prec y
, if the following two conditions hold:

(a)

\psi(x)=\psi(y)

(b)

\psi((x-r)_{+})\leq\psi((y-r)_{+})
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

Here
(x-r)_{+}
denotes the element
obtained from
x
by functional calculus with the function
x\mapsto(x-r)_{+}:=\max(x-r,0)
.

If
M
is a finite factor,
x,y\in M
are self-adjoint elements, and
\psi
is tracial, then Condition (1) in Theorem
3.4
is well-known to be equivalent to
x\prec y
,
by the results of Hiai and Nakamura:

Theorem 3.4

(see Theorem 2.1 in
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose
x,y\in M
are self-adjoint, and
\psi
is tracial and factorial. The following are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology;

(2)

x\prec y
.

Our next result provides a metric characterization of the ultraweak closure of the convex hull of the unitary orbit of an arbitrary operator in an arbitrary
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra
M
. (In fact, the assumption that
M
is
\sigma
-finite can be dropped, see §
4
.) Condition (2) can be interpreted as a majorization condition
x\prec y
for arbitrary elements
x,y
in
M
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
x,y\in M
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology;

(2)

for every
z\in M
, there exists
u\in U(M)
such that

\|x-uyu^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|z-uyu^{*}\|_{2}.

In the finite dimensional case, Theorem
3.5
provides a tracial majorization criterion for general (not necessarily Hermitian)
n\times n
real or complex matrices (Cor.
1.2
). To the best of our knowledge, this criterion is new even for matrix algebras.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Consider the bounded set

X=\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}

It follows by Theorem
3.1
that

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}}

if and only if for every
z\in M
, there exists
u\in U(M)
such that

\|x-uyu^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|z-uyu^{*}\|_{2}.\qed

We remark that a direct application of Lemma 2.2 in
[
15
]
(as opposed to Lemma
2.1
in the present paper) would only establish an equivalence between the following two conditions:

(1)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology; and,

(2’)

for every
z\in L^{2}(M,\psi)
, there exists
u\in U(M)
such that

\|x-uyu^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|z-uyu^{*}\|_{2}.

This equivalence already implies the proposed characterization for matrices, since
L^{2}(M,\psi)=M
if
M=M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

It also follows that (2) in Theorem
3.5
is equivalent to (2’) for every
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra.

On the other hand, the full generality of Lemma
2.1
is not required to establish
Theorem
3.5
. This is because a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra
M
is “projection closed” in the following sense.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space. A pair
X\subset Y
of subsets of
\mathcal{H}
is
projection closed
if for every
y\in Y
, the orthogonal projection of
y
onto the closure of the convex hull of
X
belongs to
Y
.

For example, if
Y
is a finite dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, then the pair
{X\subset Y}
is projection closed for every subset
{X\subset Y}
. Furthermore, it is easy to check that Lemma
2.1
may be replaced by the following result (setting
Y=M\subset\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(M,\psi)
), which is a direct modification of Lemma 2.2 in
[
15
]
, in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
{X\subset Y}
be a projection closed pair in
\mathcal{H}
and let
{\xi\in Y}
be a vector. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\xi\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}
, where the closure is with respect to the norm topology;

(2)

for every
\eta\in Y
, there exists
\zeta\in X
such that

\|\xi-\zeta\|\leq\|\eta-\zeta\|.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of Lemma 2.2 in
[
15
]
.
∎

Finally, we mention that for self-adjoint elements, Theorem
3.5
can be combined with the Hiai-Nakamura theorem
[
6
, Theorem 6.4]
that the ultraweak closure and the norm closure of the convex hulls of unitary orbits must coincide. This gives the following result.

Theorem 3.8
.

If
x,y\in M
are self-adjoint elements, where
M
is a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra, then the following are equivalent:

(1’)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the operator norm topology;

(2)

for every
z\in M
, there exists
u\in U(M)
such that

\|x-uyu^{*}\|_{2}\leq\|z-uyu^{*}\|_{2}.

It seems to be an open question whether or not the ultraweak and operator norm closures of
\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}
coincide in general (see
[
6
, p. 36]
) for an arbitrary element
y
in a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra
M
.

4.
Separation theorem in general von Neumann algebras

In this section we let
M
be a general (not necessarily
\sigma
-finite) von Neumann algebra. If
A
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}(A)}
is a state, then we let
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\psi}}
denote the positive semi-definite sesquilinear form on
A
defined by

\langle x,y\rangle_{\psi}=\psi(y^{*}x)

and let
{\|\cdot\|_{2,\psi}}
denote the induced semi-norm. Furthermore, if
{F\subset\mathop{\mathrm{St}}(A)}
is a finite set of states, then we let
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{F}}
denote the positive semi-definite sesquilinear form on
A
defined by

\langle x,y\rangle_{F}=\sum_{\psi\,\in\,F}\langle x,y\rangle_{\psi}

and let
{\|\cdot\|_{2,F}}
denote the induced semi-norm.

Theorem 4.1

(Separation theorem in general von Neumann algebras)
.

Let
{X\subset M}
be a bounded set and
{x\in M}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

{x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology

(2)

for every normal state
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
and
{y\in M}
, there exists
{z\in X}
such that

\|{x-z}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|{y-z}\|_{2,\psi}

(3)

there exists a separating set
{W\subset\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
of normal states such that for every finite subset
{F\subset W}
and
{y\in M}
, there exists
{z\in X}
such that

\|x-z\|_{2,F}\leq\|y-z\|_{2,F}

Proof.

Suppose first that
{(2)}
holds and let
{F\subset\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
be a finite set of normal states. Let
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
be the normal state defined by

\psi=\frac{1}{|F|}\sum_{\varphi\,\in\,F}\varphi

Then
{\|\cdot\|_{2,\psi}=|F|^{-1/2}\,\|\cdot\|_{2,F}}
, which yields the implication
{(2)\Rightarrow(3)}
. Now suppose that
{(3)}
holds. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the collection of all finite subsets of
W
, let
{F\in\mathcal{F}}
be a finite set of normal states, and let

N_{F}=\{y\in M:\|y\|_{2,F}=0\}

Let
{\mathcal{H}_{F}}
denote the completion of
{M/N_{F}}
to a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product induced on the quotient space by
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{F}}
. Let
{\pi_{F}:M\to\mathcal{H}_{F}}
be the canonical map; then
{(3)}
implies that for every
{y\in M}
, there exists
{z\in X}
such that

\|\pi_{F}(x)-\pi_{F}(z)\|\leq\|\pi_{F}(y)-\pi_{F}(z)\|

As
{\pi_{F}(M)}
is dense in
{\mathcal{H}_{F}}
, it follows by Lemma
2.1
that

\pi_{F}(x)\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}(\pi_{F}(X))}=\overline{\pi_{F}(\operatorname{conv}X)}

where the closure is with respect to the norm topology; thus for every
{F\in\mathcal{F}}
and every
{\varepsilon>0}
there exists
{z_{F,\varepsilon}\in\operatorname{conv}X}
such that

\|x-z_{F,\varepsilon}\|_{2,F}=\|\pi_{F}(x)-\pi_{F}(z_{F,\varepsilon})\|<\varepsilon

Define a direction on
{\mathcal{F}\times(0,\infty)}
by letting
{(E,\delta)\leq(F,\varepsilon)}
if
{E\subset F}
and
{\varepsilon\leq\delta}
. Let
{\psi\in W}
be a normal state and
{\varepsilon>0}
be a positive real number; then

(E,\delta)\geq(\{\psi\},\varepsilon)\implies\|x-z_{E,\delta}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|x-z_{E,\delta}\|_{2,E}<\delta\leq\varepsilon

and so
{\|x-z_{E,\delta}\|_{2,\psi}\to 0}
for all
{\psi\in W}
. As
W
is separating, the topology induced by
{\{\|\cdot\|_{2,\psi}:\psi\in W\}}
agrees with the ultrastrong topology on bounded sets; it therefore follows that
{(z_{E,\delta})\to x}
ultrastrongly, hence ultraweakly, which proves
{(3)\Rightarrow(1)}
. Now suppose that
{(1)}
holds. Let
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
be a normal state, let
{F=\{\psi\}}
, and let
{\mathcal{H}_{F}}
and
{\pi_{F}}
be as above; then
{\pi_{F}(x)\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}(\pi_{F}(X))}}
, where the closure is with respect to the norm topology, and thus it follows by Lemma
2.1
that for every element
{y\in M}
, there exists
{z\in X}
such that

\|{x-z}\|_{2,\psi}=\|\pi_{F}(x)-\pi_{F}(z)\|\leq\|\pi_{F}(y)-\pi_{F}(z)\|=\|{y-z}\|_{2,\psi}

thereby proving the implication
{(1)\Rightarrow(2)}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

It is natural to ask whether the conditions in Theorem
4.1
are equivalent to the following condition:

(4)

there exists a separating set
{W\subset\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
of normal states such that for every
{\psi\in W}
and
{y\in M}
, there exists
{z\in X}
such that

\|{x-z}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|{y-z}\|_{2,\psi}

The following counterexample demonstrates that this is not so: let
{M=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}}
, let
{a=(1,0)}
, let
{b=(0,1)}
, let
{X=\{a,b\}}
, let
{x=(1,1)}
, and let
{\varphi,\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
be the normal states defined by

\varphi(y,z)=y,\qquad\qquad\psi(y,z)=z

Then
{W=\{\varphi,\psi\}}
is a separating set of normal states and

\|x-a\|_{2,\varphi}=0,\qquad\qquad\|x-b\|_{2,\psi}=0

which implies that
{(4)}
holds. However, it is clear that
{x\notin\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
, and thus
{(1)}
does not hold.

5.
Separation theorem in general
C^{*}
-algebras

We use a standard double dual argument to deduce the following result from Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra,
{X\subset A}
be a bounded set, and
{x\in A}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

{x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
, where the closure is with respect to the norm topology

(2)

for every state
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}(A)}
and
{y\in A}
, there exists
{z\in X}
such that

\|{x-z}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|{y-z}\|_{2,\psi}

Proof.

Suppose first that
{(2)}
holds. Let
{M=A^{**}}
, let
{\iota:A\hookrightarrow M}
be the canonical embedding, let
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
be a normal state, and let
{y\in M}
be an arbitrary element; then Kaplansky’s density theorem implies that there exists a bounded net
{(y_{\alpha})_{\alpha\,\in\,\mathcal{I}}}
in
A
such that
{(\iota(y_{\alpha}))\to y}
ultrastrongly. For every
{\alpha\,\in\,\mathcal{I}}
, let
{z_{\alpha}\in X}
be an element such that
{\|x-z_{\alpha}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|y_{\alpha}-z_{\alpha}\|_{2,\psi}}
. As
{\iota(X)}
is bounded, it follows by ultraweak compactness that
{(z_{\alpha})}
admits a subnet
{(z_{\beta})_{\beta\,\in\,\mathcal{J}}}
such that
{(\iota(z_{\beta}))\to z}
ultraweakly for some
{z\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}(\iota(X))}}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology. Let
{\kappa=\sup\{\|\iota(z_{\gamma})\|_{2,\psi}:\gamma\in\mathcal{J}\}}
; then

\displaystyle|\langle\iota(x-y_{\beta}),\iota(z_{\beta})\rangle_{\psi}-\langle\iota(x)-y,z\rangle_{\psi}|

\displaystyle=|\langle\iota(x)-y,\iota(z_{\beta})-z\rangle_{\psi}-\langle\iota(y_{\beta})-y,\iota(z_{\beta})\rangle_{\psi}|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle\iota(x)-y,\iota(z_{\beta})-z\rangle_{\psi}|+\kappa\,\|\iota(y_{\beta})-y\|_{2,\psi}\to 0

whence
{\operatorname{Re}\,\langle\iota(x)-\iota(y_{\beta}),\iota(z_{\beta})\rangle_{\psi}\to\operatorname{Re}\,\langle\iota(x)-y,z\rangle_{\psi}}
. This implies that

\operatorname{Re}\,\langle\iota(x)-y,z\rangle_{\psi}\geq\textstyle\frac{1}{2}\,(\|\iota(x)\|_{2,\psi}^{2}-\|y\|_{2,\psi}^{2})

and therefore

\|\iota(x)-z\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|{y-z}\|_{2,\psi}

It then follows by Proposition
4.1
that
{\iota(x)\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}(\iota(X))}}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology, and thus
{x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}X}}
, where the closure is with respect to the weak topology; the implication
{(2)\Rightarrow(1)}
then follows by Mazur’s theorem. The implication
{(1)\Rightarrow(2)}
follows immediately by passing to the double dual and applying Proposition
4.1
.
∎

We obtain the following majorization result as an immediate consequence of the above theorem.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and let
x,y\in A
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(A)\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the norm topology;

(2)

for every state
\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}(A)
and
z\in A
, there exists
u\in U(A)
such that

\|x-uyu^{*}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|z-uyu^{*}\|_{2,\psi}.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the bounded set

X=\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(A)\}

and apply Theorem
5.1
.
∎

6.
Remarks on submajorization

The following characterizations are variations on our previous results in the context of the submajorization theory (see
[
4
,
17
,
13
]
) which can be obtained by using the same techniques.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
x,y\in M
. Consider the following conditions:

(4)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyu^{*}:\|u\|\leq 1\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology;

(5)

for every normal state
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
and every
z\in M
, there exists
u\in M
with
\|u\|\leq 1
such that

\|x-uyu^{*}\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|z-uyu^{*}\|_{2,\psi};

(6)

x\in\overline{\operatorname{conv}\{uyv:u,v\in U(M)\}}
, where the closure is with respect to the ultraweak topology;

(7)

for every normal state
{\psi\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}_{\sigma}(M)}
and every
z\in M
, there exist
u,v\in U(M)
such that

\|x-uyv\|_{2,\psi}\leq\|z-uyv\|_{2,\psi}.

Then (4)
\ssi
(5)
\impl
(6)
\ssi
(7).

Proof.

The equivalences (4)
\ssi
(5) and (6)
\ssi
(7) are direct consequences of Theorem
4.1
and (4)
\impl
(6) follows by the following remark: if
M
is a von Neumann algebra and
y\in M
, then

\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}}\subset\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:\|u\|\leq 1\}}\subset\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyv^{*}:u,v\in U(M)\}}

where the closures are with respect to the ultraweak topology. Namely, it follows by the Kadison-Pedersen strengthening of the Russo-Dye theorem (see
[
7
, Theorem 1]
), that if
u\in M
is an element such that
\|u\|<1-2n^{-1}
for some integer
n
greater than 2, then there exist
n
unitary elements
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
in
M
such that
u=n^{-1}(u_{1}+\cdots+u_{n})
. This implies that:

\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:\|u\|<1\}\subset\mathrm{conv}\{uyv^{*}:u,v\in U(M)\}.

The inclusion
\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:\|u\|\leq 1\}}\subset\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyv^{*}:u,v\in U(M)\}}
follows. The first inclusion is obvious.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

We note that the inclusions

\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}}\subset\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:\|u\|\leq 1\}}\subset\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyv^{*}:u,v\in U(M)\}}

are strict in general. Namely, if
x\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:\|u\|\leq 1\}}
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, then
tx\in\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:\|u\|\leq 1\}}
. Furthermore, when
y=1_{M}
, the set
\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyv^{*}:u,v\in U(M)\}}
is the closed unit ball of
M
, every element in
\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:\|u\|\leq 1\}}
is positive, and the set
\overline{\mathrm{conv}\{uyu^{*}:u\in U(M)\}}
is reduced to
{\{1_{M}\}}
. In particular, the inclusions may be strict.

Analogous results also hold in the setting of
C^{*}
-algebras by using the norm topology rather than the ultraweak topology.

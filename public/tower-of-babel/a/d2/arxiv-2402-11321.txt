Introduction

Let
X
be a mean zero Gaussian random variable in a separable Hilbert space
{\mathbb{H}}
with covariance operator
\Sigma:{\mathbb{H}}\mapsto{\mathbb{H}},

defined as

\Sigma u:={\mathbb{E}}\langle X,u\rangle X,u\in{\mathbb{H}}.
In this paper, we study the problem of estimation of functionals of the form
\tau_{f}(\Sigma):={\rm tr}(f(\Sigma))

for smooth functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}=[0,+\infty)\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
based on i.i.d. observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
of r.v.
X\sim N(0,\Sigma).
1
1

1

Here and in what follows
{\rm tr}(A)
denotes the trace of operator
A
in
{\mathbb{H}}.

In what follows,
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
will be called the
trace functional
generated by a test function
f.

Note that covariance operator
\Sigma
is a self-adjoint positively semidefinite compact operator in
{\mathbb{H}}

(moreover, a trace class operator). Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq 0
be the eigenvalues of
\Sigma

(repeated with their multiplicities). Let us define the spectral measure of
\Sigma
as

\displaystyle\mu(B)=\mu_{\Sigma}(B):=\sum_{j\geq 1}I_{B}(\lambda_{j})

for Borel sets
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

Then

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\Sigma)={\rm tr}(f(\Sigma))=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}fd\mu_{\Sigma}=\sum_{j\geq 1}f(\lambda_{j}).

(1.1)

Note that
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
is well defined under any assumptions
on
f
that guarantee the absolute convergence of the series in the right hand side of (
1.1
) (for instance, for any continuous function
f
such
|f(x)|\lesssim x
for all
x\geq 0
in a neighborhood of
0
). If
{\mathbb{H}}
is infinite-dimensional and
f
is continuous, then the condition that
f(0)=0
is necessary for the convergence of the series
\sum_{j\geq 1}f(\lambda_{j}).
On the other hand, if
{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}})=d<\infty,
then

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\Sigma)=f(0)d+\tau_{\bar{f}}(\Sigma),

where
\bar{f}(x):=f(x)-f(0).
Thus, in this case, estimation of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
could be reduced to estimation of
\tau_{\bar{f}}(\Sigma)
with
\bar{f}(0)=0.

In what follows, we assume without loss of generality that
f(0)=0.

Clearly,
\tau_{f}(U\Sigma U^{-1})=\tau_{f}(\Sigma)

for any orthogonal transformation
U:{\mathbb{H}}\mapsto{\mathbb{H}},
so, trace functionals are important examples of orthogonally invariant functionals
of covariance operator
\Sigma
that depend only on the eigenvalues of
\Sigma
(arranged in decreasing order), but not on their eigenvectors.
The values of trace functionals for a properly chosen collection of test functions
f
could provide relevant information about the distribution of eigenvalues
of
\Sigma
(more precisely, about its spectral measure
\mu_{\Sigma}
), which makes estimation of trace functionals an important problem in statistical analysis of spectral properties of covariance operators. For instance, it is of interest to develop estimators
\hat{\mu}_{n}
of
\mu_{\Sigma}
for which

\displaystyle\|\hat{\mu}_{n}-\mu_{\Sigma}\|_{\mathcal{F}}:=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}\Bigl|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}fd\hat{\mu}_{n}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}fd\mu_{\Sigma}\Bigr|

converges to zero sufficiently fast for proper classes
{\mathcal{F}}
of smooth functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}.

A straightforward approach to estimation of the trace functional
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
is to use the plug-in estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})

based on the sample covariance operator
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}:{\mathbb{H}}\mapsto{\mathbb{H}},
defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}u:=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle X_{j},u\rangle X_{j},u\in{\mathbb{H}}.

The statistic

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})=\sum_{j\geq 1}f(\lambda_{j}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})),

where
\lambda_{1}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\geq\lambda_{2}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\geq\dots
are the eigenvalues of
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}
repeated with their multiplicities,
is often called
a linear spectral statistic.
The study of asymptotic properties of linear spectral statistics of high-dimensional covariance matrices
(as well as some other important random matrix ensembles) has been one of the central lines of research in asymptotic theory of random matrices.
This problem was studied in a high-dimensional framework where
{\mathbb{H}}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and the dimension
d
of the model
grows with the sample size
n
so that
\frac{d}{n}\to\gamma
as
n\to\infty,
where
\gamma\in(0,+\infty).
In this setting, it is natural
to
normalize
the spectral measure
\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}}
and define
\hat{\nu}_{n}:=d^{-1}\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}},
representing
the empirical distribution of the eigenvalues of sample covariance. In random matrix theory, the problem of weak convergence of sequences of such normalized random spectral measures
\{\hat{\nu}_{n}\}
to a nonrandom limit probability measure has been studied in great detail since the pathbreaking paper by Marchenko and Pastur
[
27
]
. In particular, it follows from the results of
[
27
]
that, in the case when
\Sigma=I_{d},
the
weak limit of sequence
\{\hat{\nu}_{n}\}
is a probability measure
\nu_{\gamma}
called
the Marchenko-Pastur Law
and defined as

\displaystyle\nu_{\gamma}(A):=\begin{cases}\tilde{\nu}_{\gamma}(A)&{\rm for}\ \gamma\in(0,1]\\
(1-\gamma^{-1})I_{A}(0)+\tilde{\nu}_{\gamma}(A)&{\rm for}\ \gamma>1,\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{\gamma}(dx):=\frac{1}{2\pi\gamma}\frac{\sqrt{(x-a)(b-x)}}{x}I_{[a,b]}(x)dx

with
a:=(1-\sqrt{\gamma})^{2},b:=(1+\sqrt{\gamma})^{2}.
More generally, if
\Sigma=\Sigma^{(n)}
is an arbitrary covariance
with normalized spectral measure
\mu_{n}:=d^{-1}\mu_{\Sigma^{(n)}}
such that sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
converges weakly
to a probability measure
\mu,
then the weak limit of normalized spectral measures
\hat{\nu}_{n}
is a probability measure

\nu_{\mu,\gamma},
depending only
\mu
and on
\gamma,
that coincides with the so called free multiplicative convolution

\mu\boxtimes\nu_{\gamma}
of measure
\mu
and Marchenko-Pastur Law
\nu_{\gamma}
(see
[
3
]
).
Finally, fluctuations of random variables
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}fd\hat{\nu}_{n}
have been studied in detail, in particular,
it was shown that the finite dimensional distributions of stochastic process

\displaystyle d\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}}fd\hat{\nu}_{n}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f\nu_{\mu_{n},\gamma_{n}}\Bigr)={\rm tr}(f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}))-d\int_{\mathbb{R}}fd\nu_{\mu_{n},\gamma_{n}},

where
\gamma_{n}:=\frac{d}{n}\to\gamma,
converge weakly to the finite-dimensional distributions of a non-degenerate Gaussian process
as
n\to\infty
in classes of analytic and smooth functions
f
(see
[
4
]
, Chapter 9 and references therein).
All these asymptotic results have been proved not only in the case of Gaussian models, but also for more general models.

Since normalized trace functional
d^{-1}{\rm tr}(f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}))
provides an estimator of
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}fd_{\nu_{\mu,\gamma}}

(or
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}fd_{\nu_{\mu_{n},\gamma_{n}}}
) rather than of
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}fd\mu
(or
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}fd\mu_{n}
), the statistical inference about the spectrum of
\Sigma

requires a solution of a rather difficult inverse problem of recovery of normalized spectral measure
\mu_{n}
of
\Sigma=\Sigma^{(n)}
based
on the observations of eigenvalues of the sample covariance (which could be viewed as a multiplicative free deconvolution problem),
see
[
10
,
6
,
1
]
.

Concentration bounds for linear spectral statistics in various random matrix models have been also extensively studied in the
literature, see
[
13
,
3
,
9
,
14
,
28
,
2
]
and references therein.
More recent results with deep connections to free probability could be found in
[
5
]
.

Among simple examples of trace functionals well studied in the literature is the log determinant of covariance

\log{\rm det}(\Sigma)={\rm tr}(\log(\Sigma)).
If
{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}})=d=d_{n}\leq n,
then it is known that

\displaystyle\frac{\log{\rm det}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-a_{n,d}-\log{\rm det}(\Sigma)}{b_{n,d}}

converges in distribution to a standard normal random variable for some explicit sequences of constants
a_{n,d},b_{n,d}
(see
[
8
]
and references therein).
This means that
\log{\rm det}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-a_{n,d}
is an asymptotically normal estimator of
\log{\rm det}(\Sigma)

with
a_{n,d}
providing an explicit bias correction for the plug-in estimator
\log{\rm det}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

The convergence rate of this estimator is typically of the order
\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.
In this case, the problem is relatively simple
since

\displaystyle\log{\rm det}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\log{\rm det}(\Sigma)=\log{\rm det}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{Z}),

where
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{Z}
is the sample covariance based on i.i.d. standard normal observations
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{n}\sim N(0,I_{d}).

In the general case, the plug-in estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
also suffers from a large bias (even in the case when
d=o(n)
),
which has to be reduced in order to improve the convergence rate. In the 80s-90s, Girko (see
[
11
,
12
]
and references therein)
developed asymptotically normal estimators of several special trace functionals of high-dimensional covariance (including the Stieltjes transform of its spectral function). His approach was based on constructing a functional
g_{f}
(defined implicitly by certain equations called
G
-equations) such that
g_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
provides a better estimation of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
than the naive plug-in estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

The proof of asymptotic normality of these estimators relied on the martingale central limit theorem, the centering and normalizing parameters
of the resulting CLT were hard to interpret and the estimators were not asymptotically efficient.

We will study the problem of estimation of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
and
\mu_{\Sigma}
in a dimension-free framework with complexity of statistical estimation characterized by the effective rank of
\Sigma:

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma):=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{2}}{\|\Sigma\|}=\frac{{\rm tr}(\Sigma)}{\|\Sigma\|}.

Note that

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq{\rm rank}(\Sigma)\leq{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}}).

If
{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}})=d<\infty
and the spectrum of covariance operator
\Sigma
is bounded from above and bounded away
from
0
by numerical constants, then
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\asymp d.
On the other hand, if many eigenvalues of
\Sigma
are close to
0,

{\bf r}(\Sigma)
could be much smaller than
d.
Note also that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
is always finite (even when
{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}})=\infty
).

It is known (see
[
22
]
) that the operator norm
\|\Sigma\|
and the effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
characterize the size of operator
norm error
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|
of the sample covariance operator
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}.

This is true not only for covariance operators in Hilbert spaces, but also in a more general case of separable Banach spaces.
Namely, it was proved in
[
22
]
that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\asymp\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}\Bigr)

(1.2)

and that for all
t>0
with probability at least
1-e^{-t}

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\Bigr|\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}\vee\|\Sigma\|\frac{t}{n}.

(1.3)

Moreover, in
[
21
,
23
]
, the effective rank was used as a complexity parameter in the
problems of estimation of bilinear forms of unknown covariance operators and linear forms of their eigenvectors.

In
[
16
,
17
,
24
,
19
]
, a problem of estimation of Hölder smooth
functionals
g(\Sigma)
of unknown covariance
\Sigma
was studied in several high-dimensional and infinite-dimensional Gaussian models.
The space of operators was equipped with the operator norm and the Hölder
C^{s}
-norms of the functionals were defined in terms of operator norms
of Fréchet derivatives of the orders up to
s
viewed as multilinear forms on the space of operators. In particular, functionals of the form

g(\Sigma)={\rm tr}(f(\Sigma)B),
where
f:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
is a smooth function in the real line and
B
is an operator
from a unit nuclear norm ball, are Hölder smooth in this sense. The final results obtained in
[
19
]
showed that the maximum
over the Hölder
C^{s}
-ball
\{g:\|g\|_{C^{s}}\leq 1\}
of radius
1
of the minimax
L_{2}
-errors of estimation of
g(\Sigma)

in the class of covariance operators
\Sigma
with
\|\Sigma\|\lesssim 1
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq r
is of the order

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}})^{s}.
This result implies that there is a phase transition between parametric
n^{-1/2}

error rate and slower rates depending on the degree
s
of smoothness of the functional.
More precisely, under the assumptions
r\leq n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
s\geq\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
the optimal
error rate is
n^{-1/2}
and, if
r\geq n^{\alpha}
and
s<\frac{1}{1-\alpha},
the optimal rate is slower than
n^{-1/2}

(for some functionals in the Hölder
C^{s}
-ball).
The estimation method used in
[
19
]
is based on the bias reduction via linear aggregation of several plug-in estimators
of
g(\Sigma)
with different sample sizes (see
[
15
]
) and the coefficients of linear combination are chosen in such a way that the biases of plug-in estimators
almost cancel out. These results could not be applied to trace functionals
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
since such functionals typically do not belong to the Hölder

C^{s}
-ball of radius
1
(for instance, it is not hard to check that the operator norm of the first derivative of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
is of the order
of nuclear norm
\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{1},
which could be as large as
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
). However, we will show in this paper that the estimation method used
in
[
19
]
still yields estimators of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
with optimal error rates if function
f
is sufficiently smooth
(although the error rates of efficient estimators could be slower in this case and the threshold on the smoothness of
f
for which they hold
could be larger).

Assuming that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n,
our goal is to construct estimators of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
with nearly optimal error rates and, moreover, to achieve asymptotically efficient estimation if
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
is small comparing with
n.
Our analysis is primarily based on perturbation theory
for operator functions. In particular, we use the first order Taylor expansion

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})=\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle+R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)

of the linear spectral statistic
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
with the linear term
\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle
and
the remainder term
R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)
to study the concentration properties of
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})

and to show that, under the condition
{\bf r}(\Sigma)=o(n),
the linear term is typically dominant in the resulting concentration
bounds.
Since
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
is a biased estimator of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
and the bias could have a substantial impact on the error rate
when
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
is relatively large, we will study in what follows estimators
\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
based on linear aggregation of several plug-in estimators
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}})
with different sample sizes
n_{j},j=1,\dots,m,m\geq 2:

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}).

It is possible to choose the coefficients
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
of the linear combination and the sample sizes
n_{1},\dots,n_{m}

in such a way that the biases of plug-in estimators
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}})
almost cancel out, resulting in a reduced bias of estimator

\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)|\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|^{m+1}{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

The proof of this bound relies on deep results in perturbation theory for trace functionals, namely, on the sharp bounds on the remainder of higher order Taylor expansions of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)
(see
[
31
]
).
Using concentration properties of plug-in estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}),
we study the concentration of estimator
\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
around its expectation and use the resulting concentration inequalities
along with the bound on the bias to get, under the assumptions that
\|\Sigma\|\lesssim 1,

\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\lesssim 1,

\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\lesssim 1

and
\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\lesssim 1,
the error rates of estimator
\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
in the
L_{p}
-norms of the order

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}+{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}{n}}+\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}+{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

In the case when
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq r\lesssim n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
f
is sufficiently smooth (so,
m
is sufficiently large),
the upper bound on the error rate is

\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}},
which is minimax optimal in the set of covariances
\Sigma
with effective rank bounded by
r.

We also construct a symmetrized (jackknife) version
\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of estimator
\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})

and obtain bounds on the accuracy of approximation of
\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})

by the first order linear term
\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle
of Taylor expansion of plug-in estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

These bounds allow us to study the accuracy of normal approximation of estimator
\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
and to show
its asymptotic efficiency with the
L_{2}
-error rate
(\sqrt{2}+o(1))\frac{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

The construction of the estimators and precise statement of the results are given in Section
2
, where we also introduce the corresponding estimators
\hat{\mu}_{n,m}
and
\check{\mu}_{n,m}
of the spectral measure
\mu_{\Sigma},
obtain bounds on

\|\hat{\mu}_{n,m}-\mu_{\Sigma}\|_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\|\check{\mu}_{n,m}-\mu_{\Sigma}\|_{\mathcal{F}}
and provide a Gaussian approximation
for a stochastic process
\sqrt{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}fd(\check{\mu}_{n,m}-\mu_{\Sigma}),f\in{\mathcal{F}}

over certain classes
{\mathcal{F}}
of smooth functions. In Section
3
, we provide concentration bounds for plug-in estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})

and for the remainder of its first order Taylor expansion around
\Sigma
as well as the normal approximation for the first order linear term

of Taylor expansion of
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}),
whereas the uniform versions of these
results over classes
{\mathcal{F}}
of smooth functions are provided in Section

. In Section

, we use
the results from the operator theory on higher order Taylor expansions of traces of operator functions to obtain a decomposition of the bias of plug-in estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}),

The proofs of the main results are given in Section

.

In the rest of this section, we introduce some notations used throughout the paper (some of them have already been used above).

For nonnegative real variables
A,B,
the notation
A\lesssim B
means that there exists a numerical constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB,

the notation
A\gtrsim B
means that
B\lesssim A
and the notation
A\asymp B
means that
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B.

The signs
\lesssim,\gtrsim
and
\asymp
will be provided with subscripts to indicate the dependence of the constants on certain
parameters, say,
A\lesssim_{\gamma,\beta}B
means that there exists
C_{\gamma,\beta}>0
such that
A\leq C_{\gamma,\beta}B.

The norm notation
\|\cdot\|
(without any subscripts) is used for the norm of the underlying Hilbert space
{\mathbb{H}},
for the operator
norms of linear operators acting in
{\mathbb{H}}
and, occasionally, for other norms (if there is no ambiguity). If there is an ambiguity, the norms
will be provided with subscripts.

The notation
\|A\|_{p}
for
p\geq 1
means the Schatten
p
-norm of operator
A
in Hilbert space

{\mathbb{H}}:

\|A\|_{p}:={\rm tr}^{1/p}(|A|^{p}),
where
|A|:=\sqrt{A^{\ast}A}.
In particular,
\|A\|_{1}
is the nuclear norm,
\|A\|_{2}
is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm and
\|A\|_{\infty}=\|A\|

is the operator norm of
A.
For
p\geq 1,
the Schatten class
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
consists of all linear operators
A
in
{\mathbb{H}}
with

\|A\|_{p}<\infty.
In particular,
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
is the space of all Hilbert–Schmidt operators. It is equipped with the Hilbert–Schmidt inner
product
\langle A,B\rangle={\rm tr}(A^{\ast}B)
(for which we use the same notation as for the inner product of Hilbert space
{\mathbb{H}}
).
The notation
A\preceq B
for self-adjoint operators
A,B
means that
B-A
is positively
semidefinite.
For vectors
x,y\in{\mathbb{H}},

x\otimes y
is the tensor product of
x
and
y.
It is the operator in
{\mathbb{H}}
defined as
follows:
(x\otimes y)u=x\langle y,u\rangle,u\in{\mathbb{H}}.

In what follows,
C^{k}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})
denotes the space of all functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
that are
k
times differentiable
in
(0,\infty)
with all the derivatives
f^{(j)},j=0,\dots,k
being continuous functions in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.
Such functions can be extended to
k
times
continuously differentiable functions in
{\mathbb{R}},
and we will use such an extension whenever it is needed.
We will use the notations

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{\infty}}:=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|f(x)|\ {\rm and}\ \|f\|_{\rm Lip}:=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}.

Similar norms defined for functions on a subset
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}
will be denoted by
\|f\|_{L_{\infty}(B)}
and
\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}(B)}.

We will use below Orlicz norms of random variables on a probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{A}},{\mathbb{P}}).

Given a convex increasing function
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with
\psi(0)=0,
define the
\psi
-norm
of a random variable
\xi:\Omega\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
as

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\psi}:=\inf\Bigl\{c>0:{\mathbb{E}}\psi\Bigl(\frac{|\xi|}{c}\Bigr)\leq 1\Bigr\}.

Denote also
L_{\psi}({\mathbb{P}}):=\{\xi:\|\xi\|_{\psi}<\infty\}.
Sometimes, it is convenient to use the notations
\|\cdot\|_{L_{\psi}}

or
\|\cdot\|_{L_{\psi}({\mathbb{P}})}
for the
\psi
-norm. If
\psi(u):=u^{p},u\geq 0,p\geq 1,
then the
\psi
-norm coincides with the
L_{p}
-norm
and
L_{\psi}({\mathbb{P}})=L_{p}({\mathbb{P}}).
One can also consider functions
\psi_{\alpha}(u)=e^{u^{\alpha}}-1,u\geq 0,\alpha\geq 1.

The corresponding
\psi_{\alpha}
-norms describe various types of exponential decay of the tails of r.v.
\xi.
In particular, random variables
in the space
L_{\psi_{1}}
have subexponential tails and random variables in the space
L_{\psi_{2}}
have subgaussian tails.
It is also well known that, for all
\alpha\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\psi_{\alpha}}\asymp\sup_{p\geq 1}p^{-1/\alpha}\|\xi\|_{L_{p}}.

(1.4)

Thus, one can define the equivalent norm to the
\psi_{\alpha}
-norm by the right hand side of (
1.4
),
characterizing the growth rate of the
L_{p}
-norms.
Note also that, for
\alpha\in(0,1),
the function
\psi_{\alpha}
is not convex and
\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{\alpha}}

is not a norm. However, two sided bound (
1.4
) still holds in this case and the right hand side of (
1.4
)
is still a norm. It will be convenient for our purposes to use it as an alternative definition of the
\psi_{\alpha}
-norm that holds in the whole
range of
\alpha>0.
It will be also convenient to extend the definition of
L_{p}
- and
\psi_{\alpha}
-norms to arbitrary functions

\xi:\Omega\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}.
For instance, the
\psi_{\alpha}
-norm of such non-measurable random variable
\xi
could be defined as

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{\psi_{\alpha}}^{\ast}:=\inf\Bigl\{\|\bar{\xi}\|_{\psi_{\alpha}}:|\bar{\xi}|\geq|\xi|,\bar{\xi}\ {\rm is\ a\ random\ variable}\Bigr\}.

Similarly, we can define the outer expectation of nonnegative function
\xi:{\Omega}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}

as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{\ast}\xi:=\inf\Bigl\{{\mathbb{E}}\bar{\xi}:\bar{\xi}\geq\xi,\bar{\xi}\ {\rm is\ a\ random\ variable}\Bigr\}.

In particular, such notations are useful when we deal with expectations (or Orlicz norms) of the sup-norm
of a stochastic process, since it allows as to avoid measurability issues. This approach is common in empirical
processes literature.
In what follows, we drop
∗
sign from outer expectations and outer norms even in the case when they are applied to non-measurable
random variables.

We will also use in what follows Wasserstein type distances between random variables (or, more precisely, between their distributions).
Namely, for random variables
\xi
and
\eta,
define

\displaystyle W_{p}(\xi,\eta):=\inf\Bigl\{\|\bar{\xi}-\bar{\eta}\|_{L_{p}}:\bar{\xi}\overset{d}{=}\xi,\bar{\eta}\overset{d}{=}\eta\Bigr\},p\geq 1,

where the infimum is taken over all random variables
\bar{\xi},\bar{\eta}
defined on probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{A}},{\mathbb{P}})

satisfying the constraints
\bar{\xi}\overset{d}{=}\xi,\bar{\eta}\overset{d}{=}\eta.
Similarly, for two stochastic processes

\xi,\eta:T\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},
define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{T,p}(\xi,\eta):=\inf\Bigl\{\Bigl\|\sup_{t\in T}|\bar{\xi}(t)-\bar{\eta}(t)|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}:\bar{\xi}\overset{f.d.d.}{=}\xi,\bar{\eta}\overset{f.d.d.}{=}\eta\Bigr\},p\geq 1,

where the infimum is taken over all stochastic processes
\bar{\xi}(t),t\in T
and
\bar{\eta}(t),t\in T
defined on probability
space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{A}},{\mathbb{P}})
such that

\bar{\xi}
has the same finite dimensional distributions as
\xi
and
\bar{\eta}
has the same finite dimensional distributions as
\eta.

Replacing the
L_{p}
-norm in the above definitions by the
\psi_{\alpha}
-norm, one can define the Wasserstein
\psi_{\alpha}
-distances

W_{p}
and
{\mathcal{W}}_{T,p}
that will be also used in what follows.

2
Main results

Let
G_{\Sigma}(f),f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be a centered Gaussian process with covariance function

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}G_{\Sigma}(f)G_{\Sigma}(g):={\rm tr}(\Sigma^{2}f^{\prime}(\Sigma)g^{\prime}(\Sigma)),f,g\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}).

We start this section with the following simple result (which is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.12
in Section
3
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
with
f(0)=0
and
\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}<\infty.

Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)-\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n}.

If, in addition,
\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}<\infty,
this implies that

\displaystyle\|\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{r(\Sigma^{2})}{n}}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n},

and, moreover,

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{1/2}}\Bigl(\sqrt{n/2}(\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)),G_{\Sigma}(f)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}{n}}+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}\subset C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be the class of all functions
f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
such that
f(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq m+1}\|f^{(j)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1.

Consider the following stochastic process

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{n}(f):=\sqrt{n/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}fd(\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}}-\mu_{\Sigma})=\sqrt{n/2}(\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)),f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{1}.

The next proposition provides bounds on the sup-norm of
\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}}-\mu_{\Sigma}
over the class
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
as well
as the approximation of stochastic process
\tilde{G}_{n}
by the Gaussian process
G_{\Sigma}
in the Wasserstein

{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\psi_{1/2}}
-distance (it follows from Proposition
4.9
of Section
4
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
\|\Sigma\|\lesssim 1
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then

\displaystyle\Big\|\|\hat{\mu}_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}}-\mu_{\Sigma}\|_{{\mathcal{F}}_{1}}\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}{n}}+\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n}

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\psi_{1/2}}(\tilde{G}_{n},G_{\Sigma})\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}{n}}+\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

Note that the “remainder terms” of the order
\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n}
in the first two bounds of Proposition
2.1

(
\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}
in the last bound) and similar terms in the bounds of Proposition
2.2
could be
prohibitively large. For instance, to make the remainder term negligible and to achieve the overall error rate of the order
\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}

(for which there exists a minimax lower bound, see Proposition
2.5
) for the plug-in estimator

\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}),
the condition
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n^{1/3}
should be satisfied. Even stronger conditions on the effective rank
are needed to deduce the asymptotic normality of properly normalized linear spectral statistic

\frac{\sqrt{n}(\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))}{\sqrt{2}\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}

from the last bound of Proposition
2.1
and to establish the asymptotic efficiency of
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

We will show that the remainder terms in the bounds of this type could be substantially improved for certain estimators of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)

with reduced bias, provided that function
f
is sufficiently smooth.
To construct such estimators of trace functional
\tau_{f}(\Sigma),
we will use a method of bias reduction based on linear aggregation of plug-in estimators with different
samples sizes
[
15
]
. The sample sizes of plug-in estimators and the coefficients of their linear combination are chosen to ensure that the biases of plug-in estimators
almost cancel out.
Namely, given
m\geq 2,
let
n_{1},\dots,n_{m}
be the sample sizes of plug-in estimators. Denote
\vec{n}:=(n_{1},\dots,n_{m}).
Assume that, for some
c>1,

n/c\leq n_{1}<\dots<n_{m}\leq n.
Let

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\hat{T}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}),

where the coefficients
C_{1},\dots C_{m}
provide the unique solution of the following system of
m
linear
equations:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}=1

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{C_{j}}{n_{j}^{l}}=0,l=1,\dots,m-1.

(2.3)

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle C_{j}:=\prod_{i\neq j}\frac{n_{j}}{n_{j}-n_{i}},j=1,\dots,m.

We will also assume that the following assumption holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}|C_{j}|\lesssim_{m}1.

(2.4)

For this, it is necessary that
n_{j+1}-n_{j}\asymp n.
A possible choice of the sample sizes is
n_{j}=q^{j-m}n,

j=1,\dots,m
for some
q>1.

The following signed measure

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{n}=\hat{\mu}_{n,m}=\hat{\mu}_{n,\vec{n}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}}

will be used as an estimator of spectral measure
\mu_{\Sigma}.
Clearly,

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}fd\hat{\mu}_{n,\vec{n}}.

In what follows, we assume that
\|\Sigma\|
is bounded from above by a numerical constant whereas the effective
rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)
could be large.
We state the main results of the paper in a somewhat simplified form in terms of
\psi_{1/2}
-norms of the errors
and the corresponding Wasserstein distances used for Gaussian approximation.
Since
\|\xi\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim p^{2}\|\xi\|_{\psi_{1/2}},
these results also imply the bounds
on the
L_{p}
-norms for arbitrary
p\geq 1.

More detailed and somewhat more complicated versions
of the main theorems including the bounds on the
L_{p}
-norms (and Wasserstein
W_{p}
distances) with more precise and more explicit
dependence on the value of
p
and on
\|\Sigma\|
will be stated and proved in Section
6
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
for some
m\geq 2,

f(0)=0,

\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}}\lesssim 1
and
\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\lesssim 1.

Suppose also
that
\|\Sigma\|\lesssim 1
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.
Then

\displaystyle\|\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim_{m}\frac{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}+{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that in the case when the function
f^{\prime}
is uniformly bounded from above and bounded away from zero by numerical constants,
we have

\displaystyle\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}=\Bigl(\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}^{2}f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\asymp\|\Sigma\|_{2}=\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})},

where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq 0
are the eigenvalues of
\Sigma.

Thus, the upper bound of Theorem
2.1
could be rewritten as

\displaystyle\|\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim_{m}\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}{n}}+\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}+{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

(2.5)

In particular, it follows from this result that the upper bound on the error rate is of the order
\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}},
provided that

{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n^{m/(m+2)}
(in Proposition
2.3
below, we show a minimax bound of the order
\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}

in the class of covariance operators with
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq r
).

Remark 2.2
.

To better understand how this bound could depend on the rate of decay of the eigenvalues of
\Sigma
and on the degree of smoothness of function
f,
assume that
{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}})=d\lesssim n.
If the eigenvalues of
\Sigma
are uniformly
bounded from above and bounded away from zero by numerical constants (so that
\lambda_{k}\asymp 1,k=1,\dots,d
),
then
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\asymp d
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})\asymp d
implying that

\displaystyle\|\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim_{m}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+d\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

(2.6)

Therefore, if
d\lesssim n^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
m+1\geq\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha},
then the first term in
the right hand side of bound (
2.6
) is dominant and the bound becomes

\displaystyle\|\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim_{m}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}},

otherwise the second term is dominant and the error rate is slower than
\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

Faster error rates are possible when the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
decay with certain rate. Assume, for instance, that, for some
\beta>0,

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\asymp k^{-\beta},k=1,\dots,d.

In this case

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma)\asymp\sum_{k=1}^{d}k^{-\beta}\asymp\begin{cases}d^{1-\beta}&{\rm for}\ \beta<1\\
\log d&{\rm for}\ \beta=1\\
1&{\rm for}\ \beta>1.\end{cases}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})\asymp\sum_{k=1}^{d}k^{-2\beta}\asymp\begin{cases}d^{1-2\beta}&{\rm for}\ \beta<1/2\\
\log d&{\rm for}\ \beta=1/2\\
1&{\rm for}\ \beta>1/2.\end{cases}

Therefore, we can easily get from bound (
2.5
) that, for
m\geq 2,

\displaystyle\|\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim_{m}\begin{cases}\frac{d^{1/2-\beta}}{n^{1/2}}+\frac{d^{(1-\beta)(m+3)/2}}{n^{(m+1)/2}}&{\rm for}\ \beta<1/2\\
\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{n}}+\frac{d^{(m+3)/4}}{n^{(m+1)/2}}&{\rm for}\ \beta=1/2\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{d^{(1-\beta)(m+3)/2}}{n^{(m+1)/2}}&{\rm for}\ \beta\in(1/2,1)\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}&{\rm for}\ \beta\geq 1.\\
\end{cases}

Comparing the last bound with (
2.6
), one can see that, in this case, consistent estimation could be possible
even when
d
is much larger than
n
(depending, of course, on the values of
\beta
and
m
).

Remark 2.3
.

Even faster rates could be possible and could be easily derived from the bound of Theorem
2.1
when
f^{\prime}(\lambda)\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0.

Remark 2.4
.

It is not hard to check that the bound of Theorem
2.1
and bound (
2.5
) also
hold for any function
f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
such that
f(0)=0
with constants in the inequalities
\lesssim
depending on
m
and on function
f
through

\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}([0,A])}
and
\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}([0,A])}
for
A=2\|\Sigma\|.

Although, at the moment, we could not prove minimax optimality of the error rate of Theorem
2.1
for a given function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},
we will provide some partial results in this direction.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}(a,r):=\{\Sigma:\|\Sigma\|\leq a,{\bf r}(\Sigma)\leq r\},\ a>0,r\geq 1.

We start with the following rather simple proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f
be a continuously differentiable function in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with
f(0)=0
and suppose that there exist numbers

0\leq\gamma_{1}<\gamma_{2}\leq 1
such that
|f^{\prime}(x)|\geq\lambda>0
for all
x\in[\gamma_{1}a,\gamma_{2}a]
and
for some constant
\lambda>0.
Then

\displaystyle\inf_{T_{n,f}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(T_{n,f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))^{2}\gtrsim_{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\lambda}\ a\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}},

where the infimum is taken over all the estimators
T_{n,f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma).

We will also describe a somewhat artificial class of functional estimation problems where the bound of Theorem
2.1
yields error rates for which some minimax optimality claims could be made. Namely, consider a problem of estimation of a “piecewise trace functional”
g:D_{g}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
defined on a set
of covariance operators

D_{g}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}B(\Sigma_{j},\delta),

where
N\geq 1,

\Sigma_{j},j=1,\dots,N
are given covariance operators such that
\|\Sigma_{i}-\Sigma_{j}\|\geq 4\delta,i\neq j

and
B(\Sigma_{j},\delta):=\{\Sigma:\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{j}\|<\delta\},j=1,\dots,N
are operator norm balls with centers
\Sigma_{j}
of radius
\delta>0,

and given by the following formula

\displaystyle g(\Sigma):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}I_{B(\Sigma_{j},\delta)}(\Sigma)\tau_{f_{j}}(\Sigma),\Sigma\in D_{g}

(2.7)

where, for all
j=1,\dots,N,

\displaystyle f_{j}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{m}:=\{f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}):f(0)=0,\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1,\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1,\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1\}.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}
be the class of all such “piecewise trace functionals”
g
for a given
\delta>0,
an arbitrary
N\geq 1,

arbitrary
\Sigma_{1},\dots,\Sigma_{N}
and arbitrary functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{m}.

For a functional
g:D_{g}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
with representation (
2.7
), consider the following
estimator
\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of
g(\Sigma)
based on i.i.d. observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\sim N(0,\Sigma):

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{N}I_{B(\Sigma_{j},\delta)}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\hat{T}_{f_{j},m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that
a\lesssim 1,

r\leq n
and
\delta\geq C\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}
for a sufficiently large numerical constant
C>0.

Then

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap D_{g}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,a}\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}+r\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}+r\exp\{-n(\delta\wedge\delta^{2})/2\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,a}\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}+r\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}+(\delta^{-1}\vee\delta^{-2})\frac{r}{n}.

On the other hand, the following minimax lower bounds for estimation of functionals from class
{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}.
hold.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
a\asymp 1
and
r\leq n.
If
\delta\gtrsim n^{-1/2},
then

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}}\inf_{T_{n,g}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap D_{g}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(T_{n,g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}\gtrsim_{m,a}\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}},

where the infimum is taken over all the estimators
T_{n,g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of
g(\Sigma).

Moreover, if
\delta=c\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}
with a sufficiently small numerical constant
c>0,

then

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}}\inf_{T_{n,g}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap D_{g}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(T_{n,g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}\gtrsim_{m,a}\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}+r\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

Remark 2.5
.

Despite the fact that propositions
2.4
and
2.5
shed some light on minimax rates
of estimation of piecewise trace functionals, they do not provide a definitive solution of this problem. To do this, one would have
to prove minimax lower bounds for larger values of
\delta\geq C\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}
and/or develop estimators and prove upper
bounds for smaller values of
\delta\leq c\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}
(and also to understand minimax rates for intermediate values
of
\delta\in[c\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}},C\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}]
).

The next result provides a bound on the estimation error of spectral measure
\mu_{\Sigma}
by the measure
\hat{\mu}_{n,m}
in the sup-norm over the class
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
of smooth functions.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that
\|\Sigma\|\lesssim 1
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then, for all
m\geq 2,

\displaystyle\Big\|\|\hat{\mu}_{n,m}-\mu_{\Sigma}\|_{{\mathcal{F}}_{m}}\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim_{m}\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}{n}}+\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}+{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

(2.8)

We will now turn to another estimator of trace functional which is also based on an approach discussed in
[
15
]
and used in
[
19
]
.
Namely, denote by
{\mathcal{A}}_{\rm sym}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all random variables of the form
\psi(X_{1}\otimes X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\otimes X_{n}),

where
\psi
is a symmetric Borel function of
n
variables with values in the space
L_{\rm sa}({\mathbb{H}})
of bounded self-adjoint operators in
{\mathbb{H}}.

Denote

\displaystyle\check{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\check{T}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):={\mathbb{E}}(\hat{T}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})|{\mathcal{A}}_{\rm sym}).

Thus, estimator
\check{T}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
is the symmetrization of statistic
\hat{T}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}),

which could be easily expressed in terms of
U
-statistics and interpreted as a jackknife estimator. Indeed, if
h(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
is a symmetric
kernel of order
k\leq n
on
L_{\rm sa}({\mathbb{H}}),
then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(h(X_{1}\otimes X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\otimes X_{k})|{\mathcal{A}}_{\rm sym})=\frac{1}{{n\choose m}}\sum_{i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}}h(X_{i_{1}}\otimes X_{i_{1}},\dots,X_{i_{k}}\otimes X_{i_{k}}).

Since plug-in estimator
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{i}})
is a symmetric function of
n_{i}
variables
X_{1}\otimes X_{1},\dots,X_{n_{i}}\otimes X_{n_{i}},

we have

\displaystyle\check{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}U_{n}f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}).

We can also define a signed measure

\displaystyle\check{\mu}_{n}=\check{\mu}_{n,m}=\check{\mu}_{n,\vec{n}}:={\mathbb{E}}(\hat{\mu}_{n}|{\mathcal{A}}_{\rm sym})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}U_{n}\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}}}

as a symmetrization of
\hat{\mu}_{n}
and we have

\displaystyle\check{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}fd\check{\mu}_{n}.

Theorem 2.3
.

Under the conditions of theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, the claims of these theorems hold also for estimator
\check{T}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})

and for signed measure
\check{\mu}_{n,m}.

In addition to this, estimator
\check{T}_{f,\vec{n}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
could be approximated by a linear form
\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle

of
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,
which provides a way to establish normal approximation for this estimator and (under proper assumptions) to establish its asymptotic
efficiency. Namely, the following result holds.

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose
f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
for some
m\geq 2,

f(0)=0,

\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\lesssim 1
and
\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\lesssim 1.

Suppose also
that
\|\Sigma\|\lesssim 1
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.
Then

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)-\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{\psi_{1/2}}}\lesssim_{m}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}+{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{1/2}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}\Bigl(\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\Bigr),G_{\Sigma}(f)\Bigr)\lesssim_{m}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}+{\bf r}(\Sigma)\sqrt{n}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

Finally, we provide a result on Gaussian approximation of stochastic process

\displaystyle\check{G}_{n}(f):=\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}\Bigl(\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\Bigr),f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{m}.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that
\|\Sigma\|\lesssim 1
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then,
for all
m\geq 2,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{m},\psi_{1/2}}(\check{G}_{n},G_{\Sigma})\lesssim_{m}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}+{\bf r}(\Sigma)\sqrt{n}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that
\frac{G_{\Sigma}(f)}{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}=:Z\sim N(0,1).
Thus, the second
bound of Theorem
2.4
implies that

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{1/2}}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{n}(\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))}{\sqrt{2}\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}},Z\Bigr)\lesssim_{m}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\sqrt{n}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}\Bigr).

As in Remark
2.1
, assume that function
f^{\prime}
is uniformly bounded from above and bounded away from zero by numerical constants.
Then, we get

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{1/2}}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{n}(\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))}{\sqrt{2}\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}},Z\Bigr)\lesssim_{m}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}+\sqrt{n}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}\Bigr).

Finally, assume that
{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}})=d<\infty
and also that
\lambda_{j}\asymp 1,j=1,\dots,d.
In this case,
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\asymp d
and

{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})\asymp d,
and the bound simplifies as follows:

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{1/2}}\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt{n}(\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))}{\sqrt{2}\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}},Z\Bigr)\lesssim_{m}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}+\sqrt{nd}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

Under the assumption
d=o(n^{m/(m+2)})
as
n\to\infty,
this proves the convergence of r.v.

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{n}(\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))}{\sqrt{2}\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}\overset{d}{\to}Z

in distribution to a standard normal r.v. as well as the convergence to
1
of properly normalized mean squared error of estimator

\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}):

\displaystyle\frac{n{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}(\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))^{2}}{2\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}^{2}}\to 1\ {\rm as}\ n\to\infty.

In particular, the convergence holds if
d\leq n^{\alpha}

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
m+1>\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}.
Theorem
2.5

implies that, in this case, the convergence of stochastic processes
\frac{\check{G}_{n}(f)}{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}},f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{m}

to the process
\frac{G_{\Sigma}(f)}{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}},f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
also holds.

The following local minimax lower bound shows the asymptotic efficiency of estimator
\check{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}).

Its proof easily follows from Theorem 2.3 in
[
20
]
(whose proof is based on van Trees inequality).

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\Sigma_{0}
be a finite rank covariance operator:
{\rm rank}(\Sigma_{0})=r.
Moreover, suppose that
\Sigma_{0}
is invertible in the finite-dimensional subspace
L:={\rm Im}(\Sigma_{0})
of dimension
r.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{L}
be the set of all covariance operators
\Sigma
with
{\rm Im}(\Sigma)\subset L

(and, hence, of rank
\leq r
).
Let
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be a function such that
\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}}<\infty
and
\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma_{0})\|_{2}>0.
Then, there exists
a constant
D>0
such that, for all

\delta\leq\frac{1}{2\|\Sigma_{0}^{-1}\|}\wedge 1,

\displaystyle\inf_{T_{n}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{L},\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|_{2}\leq\delta}\frac{n{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}(T_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))^{2}}{2\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 1-D\|\Sigma_{0}\|^{2}\|\Sigma_{0}^{-1}\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\delta}{\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma_{0})\|_{2}}+\|\Sigma_{0}\|^{2}\|\Sigma_{0}^{-1}\|^{2}\delta+\frac{\|\Sigma_{0}\|^{2}}{\delta^{2}n}\Bigr),

where the infimum is taken over all estimators
T_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
based on i.i.d. observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\sim N(0,\Sigma).

The bound of Proposition
2.6
implies the following version of Hájek-LeCam local asymptotic minimax bound:

\displaystyle\lim_{c\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{T_{n}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{L},\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{0}\|_{2}\leq\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}}\frac{n{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}(T_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))^{2}}{2\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}^{2}}\geq 1.

3
Concentration and normal approximation bounds

Our goal in this section is to study concentration and normal approximation properties of the linear term
\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle

and the remainder
R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)
of the first order Taylor expansion of trace functional
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}),
which would yield
concentration inequalities for
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
itself. There are many results on concentration inequalities
for linear spectral statistics and normalized spectral measures of random matrices available in the literature (see
[
13
,
3
,
9
,
14
,
28
,
2
,
5
]
and references therein), but our aim will be to express concentration properties in terms of effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma).

Consider an operator function
f(A),
where
f:{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
and
A:{\mathbb{H}}\mapsto{\mathbb{H}}
is a self-adjoint operator.
If
{\mathbb{H}}
is finite-dimensional, the spectral representation of
A
could be written as
A=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)}\lambda P_{\lambda},

where
\sigma(A)
is the spectrum of
A
and
P_{\lambda}
is the orthogonal projection on the eigen-space of
A,
corresponding to its eigenvalue

\lambda\in\sigma(A).
The following formula for the derivative of
f(A)
in the direction of a self-adjoint operator
H
is well known (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
, Theorem V.3.3):

\displaystyle(Df)(A)[H]=\frac{d}{dt}f(A+tH)_{|_{t=0}}=\sum_{\lambda,\mu\in\sigma(A)}f^{[1]}(\lambda,\mu)P_{\lambda}HP_{\mu},

(3.1)

where

\displaystyle f^{[1]}(\lambda,\mu):=\begin{cases}\frac{f(\lambda)-f(\mu)}{\lambda-\mu}&\lambda\neq\mu\\
f^{\prime}(\lambda)&\lambda=\mu\end{cases}

is the first order divided difference of function
f.

Formula (
3.1
) holds for any function
f
continuously differentiable in an open interval that contains
\sigma(A)
and, moreover,
in this case, the operator function
A\mapsto f(A)
is Fréchet differentiable at
A.
The following formula for the derivative of trace functional
\tau_{f}
is an immediate
consequence of (
3.1
):

\displaystyle(D\tau_{f})(A)[H]=\frac{d}{dt}\tau_{f}(A+tH)_{|_{t=0}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)}f^{\prime}(\lambda){\rm tr}(P_{\lambda}HP_{\lambda})=\langle f^{\prime}(A),H\rangle.

Similar differentiation formulas hold in the infinite-dimensional case, although more smoothness of function
f
could be required (for instance, that
f
belongs to the Besov class
B^{1}_{\infty,1}({\mathbb{R}})
) and, in the case of operator
A
with continuous spectrum,
the double discrete sum in (
3.1
) should be replaced by a double operator integral with respect to the resolution of identity of operator
A

(see, e.g.,
[
29
,
30
]
and references therein).

Let

\displaystyle R_{f}(A;H):=\tau_{f}(A+H)-\tau_{f}(A)-\langle f^{\prime}(A),H\rangle

be the remainder of the first order Taylor expansion of functional
\tau_{f}(A).

In the finite dimensional case, it is easy to show the following bound on
R_{f}(A;H).

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}<\infty.

Then

\displaystyle|R_{f}(A;H)|\leq\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}}{2}\|H\|_{2}^{2}.

proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tau_{f}(A+tH)=\langle f^{\prime}(A+tH),H\rangle,t\in[0,1].

Therefore,

\displaystyle R_{f}(A;H)=\tau_{f}(A+H)-\tau_{f}(A)-\langle f^{\prime}(A),H\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}\langle f^{\prime}(A+tH)-f^{\prime}(A),H\rangle dt,

implying the bound

\displaystyle|R_{f}(A;H)|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|f^{\prime}(A+tH)-f^{\prime}(A)\|_{2}dt\|H\|_{2}.

It is well known that, if
f^{\prime}
is Lipschitz, then the operator function
A\mapsto f^{\prime}(A)
is also Lipschitz with respect
to the Hilbert–Schmidt norm (see, e.g.
[
32
]
and references therein), which implies that

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}(A+tH)-f^{\prime}(A)\|_{2}\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{{\rm Lip}}t\|H\|_{2},t\in[0,1].

The claim of the proposition now easily follows.

∎

Clearly, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})

\displaystyle=\tau_{f}(\Sigma)+\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle+R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma),

which implies that

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})=\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle+R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma).

Thus, the study of concentration and normal approximation properties of plug-in estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
reduces to the study of concentration and normal approximation
of the linear term
\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle
and concentration of the remainder
R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma).

In what follows, we will obtain concentration and normal approximation bounds with explicit dependence on the effective rank
{\bf r}(\Sigma)

and some other relevant quantities.

First, we deal with concentration properties of random variables

\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle.

For this, we will review several well know facts.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\{\xi_{k}\}
be independent centered random variables with
\sup_{k\geq 1}\|\xi_{k}\|_{\psi_{1}}<\infty
and let
\{a_{k}\}

be a sequence of real numbers with
\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}^{2}<\infty.
Then, the series
\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\xi_{k}
converges
a.s. and, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\xi_{k}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sup_{k\geq 1}\|\xi_{k}\|_{\psi_{1}}\Bigl(\Bigl(\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\sqrt{p}\vee\sup_{k\geq 1}|a_{k}|p\Bigr).

proof.

The result easily follows from Bernstein’s inequality for sums of independent subexponential random variables (see
[
36
]
, Theorem 2.8.1) by expressing the
p
-th moments in terms of the tail probabilities and bounding the integrals.

∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
B
be a bounded self-adjoint operator in
{\mathbb{H}}.
Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,B\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma^{1/2}B\Sigma^{1/2}\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\|\Sigma^{1/2}B\Sigma^{1/2}\|\frac{p}{n}.

proof.

It is enough to prove the inequality in the case when covariance operator
\Sigma
is of finite rank
N
(the general case easily follows by a finite rank approximation
and passing to the limit as
N\to\infty
). Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{N}
be the orthonormal eigenvectors of operator
\Sigma^{1/2}B\Sigma^{1/2}
(acting
in
{\rm Im}(\Sigma)
) corresponding
to its eigenvalues
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N}.
Clearly, we can represent
X=\Sigma^{1/2}Z,

Z=\sum_{j=1}^{N}g_{j}\phi_{j},
where
\{g_{j}\}
are i.i.d. standard normal random variables.
We can also represent
X_{i}=\Sigma^{1/2}Z_{i},i=1,\dots,n,

Z_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}g_{ij}\phi_{j},i=1,\dots,n
with
\{g_{ij}\}
being i.i.d. standard normal.
Then, it follows that

\displaystyle\langle BX,X\rangle=\langle\Sigma^{1/2}B\Sigma^{1/2}Z,Z\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mu_{j}g_{j}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,B\rangle

\displaystyle=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle BX_{i},X_{i}\rangle-{\mathbb{E}}\langle BX,X\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{N}n^{-1}\mu_{j}(g_{i,j}^{2}-1).

Since
\|g_{i,j}^{2}-1\|_{\psi_{1}}\lesssim 1,

we can apply the bound of Proposition
3.2
to get

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\langle\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,B\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mu_{j}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|\mu_{j}|\frac{p}{n},

implying the claim.

∎

In particular, we will use the following immediate corollary of Proposition
3.3
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}).

Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\frac{p}{n}.

Another immediate consequence of Proposition
3.3
is the following concentration bound for
{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}).

Proposition 3.5
.

For all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\rm tr}(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{n}.

Next we look at normal approximation in Wasserstein
W_{2}
-distances of random variables

\displaystyle G_{n}(f):=\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle,f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}).

Recall that
G_{\Sigma}(f),f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}G_{\Sigma}(f)G_{\Sigma}(g):={\rm tr}(\Sigma^{2}f^{\prime}(\Sigma)g^{\prime}(\Sigma)),f,g\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}).

Proposition 3.6
.

For all
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
and all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle W_{p}(G_{n}(f),G_{\Sigma}(f))\lesssim\frac{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}\sqrt{p}\vee\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|p}{\sqrt{n}}.

proof.

We will use the construction from the proof of Proposition
3.3
with
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots
being the orthonormal eigenvectors of covariance operator
\Sigma

corresponding to its eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots.
Then
X=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sqrt{\lambda}_{k}g_{k}\phi_{k}
with i.i.d. standard normal r.v.
\{g_{k}\}
and

X_{j}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sqrt{\lambda}_{k}g_{j,k}\phi_{k},j=1,\dots,n
with i.i.d. standard normal r.v.
\{g_{j,k}\}.

The following representation holds for stochastic process
G_{n}(f),f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}):

\displaystyle G_{n}(f)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i,k}^{2}-1).

On the other hand, Gaussian process
G_{\Sigma}(f)
could be represented as

\displaystyle G_{\Sigma}(f):=\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})\eta_{k},

where
\{\eta_{k}\}
are also i.i.d. standard normal r.v. Note that the series representing processes
G_{n}(f)
and
G_{\Sigma}(f)
converge a.s.
and also in the
\psi_{1}
-norm (see Proposition
3.2
).
By Theorem 2.1 in
[
33
]
, for i.i.d.
g_{i}\sim N(0,1)
and
\eta\sim N(0,1),

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i}^{2}-1),\eta\Bigr)\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{k\geq 1}W_{\psi_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i,k}^{2}-1),\eta_{k}\Bigr)\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

and, moreover, random vectors
(g_{1,k},\dots,g_{n,k},\eta_{k}),k\geq 1
are independent. Therefore, one can construct independent random variables

(\bar{\xi}_{k},\bar{\eta}_{k}),k\geq 1,
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{k}\overset{d}{=}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(g_{i}^{2}-1),\ \bar{\eta}_{k}\overset{d}{=}\eta_{k}

and

\displaystyle\sup_{k\geq 1}\|\bar{\xi}_{k}-\bar{\eta}_{k}\|_{\psi_{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

Let
\zeta_{k}:=\bar{\xi}_{k}-\bar{\eta}_{k},k\geq 1.

Then,
\{\zeta_{k}\}
are independent centered r.v. with
\sup_{k\geq 1}\|\zeta_{k}\|_{\psi_{1}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

Also, stochastic process

\displaystyle\bar{G}_{n}(f):=\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})\bar{\xi}_{k},f\in{\mathcal{F}}

has the same finite dimensional distributions as
G_{n}(f),f\in{\mathcal{F}}
and stochastic process

\displaystyle\bar{G}_{\Sigma}(f):=\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})\bar{\eta}_{k},f\in{\mathcal{F}}

has the same finite dimensional distributions as
G_{\Sigma}(f),f\in{\mathcal{F}}.

By Proposition
3.2
, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle W_{p}(G_{n}(f),G_{\Sigma}(f))\leq\|\bar{G}_{n}(f)-\bar{G}_{\Sigma}(f)\|_{L_{p}}=\Bigl\|\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})\zeta_{k}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\Bigl(\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}^{2}(f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k}))^{2}\Bigr)\sqrt{p}\vee\sup_{k\geq 1}|\lambda_{k}||f^{\prime}(\lambda_{k})|p\Bigr),

which completes the proof.

∎

Note that
\frac{G_{\Sigma}(f)}{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}
is a standard normal random variable. Thus, the bound of Proposition
3.6
implies that

\displaystyle W_{p}\Bigl(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}},Z\Bigr)\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{n}}\vee\frac{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|}{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}}\frac{p}{\sqrt{n}}\lesssim\frac{p}{\sqrt{n}},

yielding that

\displaystyle W_{\psi_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{G_{n}(f)}{\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}},Z\Bigr)\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}},

where
Z
is a standard normal random variable.

Several bounds for sample covariance operators will be useful in the study of concentration properties of the remainder

R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)
of the first order Taylor expansion as well as for other purposes. Its proof is based
on

Proposition 3.7
.

The following bound holds:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}.

proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\otimes X-\Sigma\|_{2}^{2}}{n}\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\otimes X\|_{2}^{2}}{n}=\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|^{4}}{n}.

It follows from the Gaussian concentration inequality that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/4}(\|X\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|)^{4}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{1/2},

which implies that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/4}\|X\|^{4}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|+C\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\leq{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|X\|^{2}+C\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}=\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}+C\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}.

The claim now easily follows.

∎

The following result could be viewed as an extension of the bound of Proposition
3.7
to Schatten
p
-norms for
p\geq 2.

Its proof is based on non-commutative Khintchine inequality
[
25
,
26
]
(these inequalities were first used to obtain
bounds for the sample covariance in
[
34
]
).

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then, for all
p\geq 2,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma\|{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{1/p}(\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}\vee\sqrt{p\log n})\Bigl(1\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1/4-1/2p}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1/2-1/p}\Bigr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\|\Sigma\|{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{1/p}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\Bigr).

Remark 3.1
.

If
{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}})=d<\infty,

\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{p}\leq d^{1/p}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|.
In this case, it easily follows from (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim_{p}d^{1/p}\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}.

Proposition
3.8
shows that, in the bounds of this type, the factor
d^{1/p}
can be replaced by
{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{1/p}
(at least, for
p\geq 2
).

proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
{\rm dim}({\mathbb{H}})<\infty
(otherwise, we can use a finite-dimensional approximation).

By symmetrization inequality, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{p}^{p}\leq 2^{p}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\Bigr\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{2^{p}}{n^{p/2}}{\mathbb{E}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}\Bigl\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\Bigr\|_{p}^{p},

where
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}
are i.i.d. Rademacher random variables independent of
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}.

By non-commutative Khintchine inequality
[
25
,
26
]
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\Bigl\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\Bigr\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim C\sqrt{p}\Bigl\|\Bigl(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigr\|_{p}

\displaystyle=C\sqrt{p}\Bigl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})^{2}\Bigr\|_{p/2}^{1/2},

which implies

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}\Bigl\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\Bigr\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim C^{p}(\sqrt{p})^{p}\Bigl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\Bigr\|_{p/2}^{p/2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{2^{p}C^{p}(\sqrt{p})^{p}}{n^{p/2}}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\Bigr\|_{p/2}^{p/2}

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{2C\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}\Bigl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\Bigr\|_{p/2}^{p/2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\preceq\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}\hat{\Sigma}_{n},

which implies that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}(X_{j}\otimes X_{j})\Bigr\|_{p/2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|_{p/2}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}\Bigl({\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{p/2})\Bigr)^{2/p}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|{\bf r}(\Sigma_{n})^{2/p}=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1-2/p}\Bigl({\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\Bigr)^{2/p}.

This yields the following bounds:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\frac{\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{n}}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|^{p}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{p/2-1}{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})

\displaystyle=2C\frac{\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl\|\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2-1/p}{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})^{1/p}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\frac{\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl\|\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{3p}}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2-1/p}\Bigr\|_{L_{3p}}\Bigl\|{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})^{1/p}\Bigr\|_{L_{3p}}

\displaystyle=2C\frac{\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl\|\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{3p}}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{3p}}^{1/2-1/p}\Bigl\|{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\Bigr\|_{L_{3}}^{1/p}.

(3.2)

Using Gaussian concentration inequality for
\|X\|,
we get

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|+{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\Bigl|\|X_{j}\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|\Bigr|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|+{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p\log n}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\Bigl|\|X_{j}\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|\Bigr|^{p\log n}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|+\Bigl({\mathbb{E}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Bigl|\|X_{j}\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|\Bigr|^{p\log n}\Bigr)^{1/p\log n}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|+n^{1/p\log n}{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p\log n}\Bigl|\|X\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|X\|\Bigr|^{p\log n}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}+e^{1/p}C\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\sqrt{p\log n}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\|X_{j}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}(\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}+\sqrt{p\log n}).

(3.3)

As a consequence of (
1.2
), (
1.3
), we have under the assumption
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n
that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\leq\|\Sigma\|+\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(1\vee\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\Bigr).

(3.4)

Finally, using the bound of Proposition
3.5
, we get

\displaystyle\Bigl\|{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm tr}(\Sigma)+\Bigl\|{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\rm tr}(\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|{\bf r}(\Sigma)+\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{n}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Sigma\|{\bf r}(\Sigma)+\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{n}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\Bigl({\bf r}(\Sigma)\vee\frac{p}{n}\Bigr).

(3.5)

Using bounds (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) with
3p
instead of
p
and bound (
3
) with

p=3,
we easily get from (
3
) that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{1/p}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{1/p}(\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}\vee\sqrt{p\log n})\Bigl(1\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1/4-1/2p}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{1/2-1/p}\Bigr)\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}},

which completes the proof.
∎

We are interested in concentration
inequalities for the remainder
R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)
of the first order Taylor expansion around its expectation.
Namely, we will prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
with
f^{\prime}
being Lipschitz in
{\mathbb{R}}
and
f(0)=0.

Suppose
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\frac{p}{n}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr).

(3.6)

For the proof, we need several auxiliary statements.

Let
g:S\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a locally Lipschitz function on a metric space
(S,d).
Its local Lipschitz constant
(Lg)(x)
at point
x\in S
is defined as

\displaystyle(Lg)(x):=\inf_{U\ni x}\sup_{x_{1},x_{2}\in U}\frac{|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|}{d(x_{1},x_{2})},

where the infimum is taken over all balls centered at
x.
In the case when
S={\mathbb{R}}^{N},
it is equipped with the standard Euclidean norm and
f
is continuously differentiable,

(Lg)(x)
coincides with the Euclidean norm of the gradient
(\nabla g)(x).
The next statement is a well known form of Gaussian concentration inequality.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be a standard normal random variable in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.
For any locally Lipschitz function
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}

and for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|g({\mathcal{Z}})-{\mathbb{E}}g({\mathcal{Z}})\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\sqrt{p}\|(Lg)({\mathcal{Z}})\|_{L_{p}}.

The following simple lemma will be useful.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
u_{i},v_{i}\in{\mathbb{H}},i=1,\dots,n,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\otimes v_{i}\Bigr\|_{2}\leq\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|u_{i}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}v_{j}\otimes v_{j}\Bigr\|^{1/2}.

proof.

Observe that, for an orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}:j\geq 1\}
of
{\mathbb{H}},

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\otimes v_{i}\Bigr\|_{2}=\Bigl\|\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\otimes e_{i}\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}\otimes v_{j}\Bigr)\Bigr\|_{2}\leq\Bigl\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\otimes e_{i}\Bigr\|_{2}\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}\otimes v_{j}\Bigr\|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|u_{i}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\sup_{\|s\|\leq 1,\|t\|\leq 1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle e_{j},s\rangle\langle v_{j},t\rangle\leq\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|u_{i}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\sup_{\|s\|\leq 1}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle e_{j},s\rangle^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\sup_{\|t\|\leq 1}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle v_{j},t\rangle^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|u_{i}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}v_{j}\otimes v_{j}\Bigr\|^{1/2}.

∎

The next proposition provides a simple bound on the local Lipschitz constant
(L\hat{\Sigma}_{n})(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of the function

{\mathbb{H}}\times\dots\times{\mathbb{H}}\ni(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\mapsto\hat{\Sigma}_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{2}.

Proposition 3.10
.

The following bound holds:

\displaystyle(L\hat{\Sigma}_{n})(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\leq\frac{2\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

proof.

Let
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime}=\hat{\Sigma}_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime\prime}=\hat{\Sigma}_{n}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})

for
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in{\mathbb{H}}.
Then

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\otimes x_{j}-n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}\otimes y_{j}\Bigr\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\|n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}-y_{j})\otimes x_{j}\Bigr\|_{2}+\Bigl\|n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}\otimes(x_{j}-y_{j})\Bigr\|_{2}.

The bound of Lemma
3.1
implies that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}-y_{j})\otimes x_{j}\Bigr\|_{2}\leq n^{-1/2}\Bigl\|n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\otimes x_{j}\Bigr\|^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\leq\frac{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}\otimes(x_{j}-y_{j})\Bigr\|_{2}\leq\frac{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime}\|^{1/2}+\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

If now we let
x_{i}\to X_{i}
and
y_{i}\to X_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n,
we have that
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime}\|^{1/2}\to\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}

and
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\|^{1/2}\to\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2},
implying that

\displaystyle(L\hat{\Sigma}_{n})(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\leq\frac{2\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

∎

In the proofs of concentration inequalities for smooth functionals of sample covariance
\hat{\Sigma}_{n},
it is convenient to assume that covariance operator
\Sigma
is of finite rank

In this case, we can write

where

and

We can also write

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}({\mathcal{Z}}):=\hat{\Sigma}_{n}(\Sigma^{1/2}Z_{1},\dots,\Sigma^{1/2}Z_{n}).

Then, it follows from the bound of Proposition

that

\displaystyle(L\tilde{\Sigma}_{n})({\mathcal{Z}})\leq\frac{2\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

(3.7)

The next proposition provides a concentration bound for Schatten norm errors

of sample covariance
for all

Proposition 3.11
.

Suppose
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then, for all
p\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{r}-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{r}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\Bigr).

proof.

Assume that
\Sigma
is of finite rank
N
and represent
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}
as a function of
{\mathcal{Z}}:

\hat{\Sigma}_{n}=\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}({\mathcal{Z}}).

Since, for
r\geq 2,

\displaystyle|\|A\|_{r}-\|B\|_{r}|\leq\|A-B\|_{r}\leq\|A-B\|_{2},

3.7

\displaystyle(L\|\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{r})({\mathcal{Z}})\leq\frac{2\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

3.9

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{r}-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{r}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\frac{\sqrt{p}}{\sqrt{n}}\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}.

(3.8)

Also, using bound (

\displaystyle\Bigl|\|A\|^{1/2}-\|B\|^{1/2}\Bigr|\leq\frac{\|A-B\|}{\|A\|^{1/2}+\|B\|^{1/2}}\leq\frac{\|A-B\|_{2}}{\|A\|^{1/2}+\|B\|^{1/2}}

\displaystyle(L\|\tilde{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2})({\mathcal{Z}})\leq\frac{\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

\displaystyle\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{1/2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}.

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Sigma\|+{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|+\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Sigma\|+C\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\vee 1\Bigr).

(3.15)

On the other hand, the bounds of Propositions
3.7
and Proposition
3.11
(for
r=2
)
imply that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{2}+\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{2}-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\Bigr).

(3.16)

Thus, we can use bounds (
3
), (
3
) and (
3
) to conclude that

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}\|f^{\prime}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}-{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}\|f^{\prime}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{3/2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{3/2}\Bigr).

Combining the last bound with (
3.12
), we get

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|^{1/2}\|f^{\prime}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{3/2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\vee\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{3/2}\Bigr).

Together with (
3
), this yields bound (
3.6
) in the case of covariance
\Sigma
of finite rank.

In the general case, let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq 0
be the eigenvalues of
\Sigma
(repeated with their multiplicities) and let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots
be an orthonormal basis of corresponding
eigenvectors. Then,
X=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}g_{k}\phi_{k},

\{g_{k}\}
being i.i.d. standard normal r.v. Denote
X^{(N)}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}g_{k}\phi_{k}
and
let

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(N)}:={\mathbb{E}}(X^{(N)}\otimes X^{(N)})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{k}(\phi_{k}\otimes\phi_{k}).

Define
X_{j}^{(N)}:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}g_{j,k}\phi_{k},j=1,\dots,n,
where
\{g_{j,k}\}
are i.i.d. standard normal r.v. and let
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}
be the sample covariance
based on
X_{1}^{(N)},\dots,X_{n}^{(N)}.
Then,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)\vee\frac{p}{n}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr),

(3.17)

and it remains to pass in the above inequality to the limit as
N\to\infty.

Observe that
\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|=\lambda_{1}=\|\Sigma\|
and

\displaystyle{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{1}}\to\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{1}}={\bf r}(\Sigma)\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

We will prove that

\displaystyle R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})\to R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty\ {\rm a.s.}

(3.18)

Indeed, since
f(0)=0,

f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
and
\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}<\infty,

we have

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(x)|\leq|f^{\prime}(0)|+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}x\ {\rm and}\ |f(x)|\leq|f^{\prime}(0)|x+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm LIp}\frac{x^{2}}{2},x\geq 0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\tau_{f}(\Sigma)-\tau_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)})|\leq\sum_{k>N}|f(\lambda_{k})|\leq|f^{\prime}(0)|\sum_{k>N}\lambda_{k}+\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}}{2}\sum_{k>N}\lambda_{k}^{2}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

(3.19)

Note also that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|X-X^{(N)}\|\leq{\mathbb{E}}^{1/2}\|X-X^{N}\|^{2}=(\sum_{k>N}\lambda_{k})^{1/2}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

By Gaussian concentration, it is easy to conclude that
X^{(N)}\to X
a.s. and, similarly,
X_{j}^{(N)}\to X_{j}
a.s. for
j=1,\dots,n
as
N\to\infty.

This easily implies that
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|\to 0
as
N\to\infty,
and, since
{\rm rank}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})\leq n,{\rm rank}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\leq n,

the convergence also holds in Hilbert-Schmidt and nuclear norms. Note also that

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|\leq n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}\otimes X_{j}\|=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}=:A

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}\|\leq n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}^{(N)}\|^{2}\leq n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|X_{j}\|^{2}=A.

Since
f
is Lipschitz on
[0,A],
it can be extended to a Lipschitz function
\bar{f}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
with preservation of
its Lipschitz constant (that might depend on
A
). Since
\sigma(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\subset[0,A]
and
\sigma(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})\subset[0,A],

we have
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})=\bar{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
and
f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})=\bar{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}).

Therefore, taking also into account that
{\rm rank}(f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}))\leq n
and
{\rm rank}(f(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}))\leq n,
we get

\displaystyle|\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})|=|{\rm tr}(\bar{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}))-{\rm tr}(\bar{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}))|\leq\|\bar{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-\bar{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2n}\|\bar{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)})-\bar{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2n}\|\bar{f}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|_{2}=\sqrt{2n}\|f\|_{\rm Lip([0,A])}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|_{2}\to 0\ {\rm a.s.}

(3.20)

as
N\to\infty.

Finally, note that

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}(\Sigma^{(N)})-f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma^{(N)}-\Sigma\|_{2}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty

and

\displaystyle\|(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})-(\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)\|_{2}\leq\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|_{2}+\|\Sigma^{(N)}-\Sigma\|_{2}\to 0\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty,

implying that

\displaystyle\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma^{(N)}),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\rangle\to\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty\ {\rm a.s.}

(3.21)

It follows from (
3.19
), (
3
) and (
3.21
) that (
3.18
) holds.

Next we show that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})\to{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)\ {\rm as}\ N\to\infty.

(3.22)

For this, note that, in view of Proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle|R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})|\leq\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}}{2}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|_{2}^{2}.

Using bound (
3
) with
p=4,
we get

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}|R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}^{2}}{4}\sup_{N\geq 1}{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|_{2}^{4}=\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}^{2}}{4}\sup_{N\geq 1}\Bigl\|\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)}\|_{2}\Bigr\|_{L_{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}^{2}\|\Sigma\|^{4}\sup_{N\geq 1}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})^{4}}{n^{2}}\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}^{2}\|\Sigma\|^{4}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{4}}{n^{2}}<\infty.

Thus, one can pass to the limit as
N\to\infty
under the expectation to prove (
3.22
).

Finally, observe that, by bound (
3
) for all
p^{\prime}>p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\sup_{N\geq 1}\Bigl\|R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})\Bigr\|_{L_{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\sup_{N\geq 1}\|\Sigma^{(N)}\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{(N)})}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{p^{\prime}}{n}}\vee\frac{p^{\prime}}{n}\Bigr)\vee\frac{p^{\prime}}{n}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p^{\prime}}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{p^{\prime}}{n}}\vee\frac{p^{\prime}}{n}\Bigr)\vee\frac{p^{\prime}}{n}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p^{\prime}}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)<\infty.

Since also

\displaystyle R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma^{(N)},\hat{\Sigma}_{n}^{(N)}-\Sigma^{(N)})\to R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)

(3.23)

as
N\to\infty
a.s., it is possible to pass to the limit as
N\to\infty
in bound (
3
) to complete the proof.
∎

The next corollary is immediate.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
f\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be such that
f(0)=0,

\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}<\infty
and
\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}<\infty.

Suppose that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then, for all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\Bigl(\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\frac{p}{n}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\rm Lip}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\vee\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\frac{p}{n}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr).

proof.

Using the first order Taylor expansion, we get

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})=\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle+R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma).

It remains to use bounds of Proposition
3.4
and Theorem
3.1
and to observe that

\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|_{2}\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|_{2}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|\sqrt{{\bf r}(\Sigma^{2})}
and
\|\Sigma f^{\prime}(\Sigma)\|\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|.

∎

of Section

4
Bounds on sup-norms of empirical spectral processes

Our next goal is to develop bounds on the sup-norms of empirical spectral processes, such as

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-{\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}fd\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}}-{\mathbb{E}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}fd\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}},f\in{\mathcal{F}}

over a class
{\mathcal{F}}
of sufficiently smooth functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying the condition
f(0)=0.

This problem could be reduced to bounding the sup-norms of the linear process

\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle,f\in{\mathcal{F}}
and the centered remainder process

R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma),f\in{\mathcal{F}}.

Consider the following pseudometric: for
f,g\in C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),

\displaystyle\tau(f,g):=\|f^{\prime}-g^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}|f^{\prime}(x)-g^{\prime}(x)|,

and denote

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}=\rho_{\tau}({\mathcal{F}}):=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}.

We will use standard notations
N_{\tau}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)
for the
\varepsilon
-covering number of
{\mathcal{F}}
with respect to pseudometric
\tau
and

\displaystyle H_{\tau}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon):=\log N_{\tau}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon),\varepsilon>0

be the corresponding
\varepsilon
-entropy of
{\mathcal{F}}.
Similar notations will be used for other pseudometrics.

The following result for the process
\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle,f\in{\mathcal{F}}
holds.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be a class of functions
f
satisfying the condition
f(0)=0.

Suppose
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n
and let
\delta\in[0,\rho_{\tau}/2].
For all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}|\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\int_{\delta}^{\rho_{\tau}}H_{\tau}^{1/2}({\mathcal{F}};\varepsilon)d\varepsilon+\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{n}\int_{\delta}^{\rho_{\tau}}H_{\tau}({\mathcal{F}};\varepsilon)d\varepsilon+\delta\|\Sigma\|\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\vee\frac{p}{\sqrt{n}}\Bigr).

In particular, for
\delta=0,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}|\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{\tau}}H_{\tau}^{1/2}({\mathcal{F}};\varepsilon)d\varepsilon+\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{n}\int_{0}^{\rho_{\tau}}H_{\tau}({\mathcal{F}};\varepsilon)d\varepsilon.

A consequence is the following statement.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be a class of functions
f
such that
f(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq m+1}\|f^{(j)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1.

Suppose that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then, for
m\geq 2,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}|\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim(\|\Sigma\|+1)^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+(\|\Sigma\|+1)\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{n}

and, for
m=1,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}|\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}\lesssim(\|\Sigma\|+1)^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+(\|\Sigma\|+1)\|\Sigma\|\log n\frac{p}{n}.

We also have the following result on approximation of stochastic process

\displaystyle G_{n}(f)=\sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}\langle f^{\prime}(\Sigma),\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\rangle,f\in{\mathcal{F}}

by the Gaussian process
G_{\Sigma}(f),f\in{\mathcal{F}}
in Wasserstein distance
{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{F}},p}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be a class of functions
f
satisfying the condition
f(0)=0.

Suppose
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n
and let
\delta\in[0,\rho_{\tau}/2].
For all
p\geq 1,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{F}},p}(G_{n},G_{\Sigma})\lesssim\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\int_{\delta}^{\rho_{\tau}}H_{\tau}^{1/2}({\mathcal{F}};\varepsilon)d\varepsilon+\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{\delta}^{\rho_{\tau}}H_{\tau}({\mathcal{F}};\varepsilon)d\varepsilon+\delta\|\Sigma\|{\bf r}(\Sigma)\frac{p}{\sqrt{n}}.

In particular, for
\delta=0,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{F}},p}(G_{n},G_{\Sigma})\lesssim\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{\tau}}H_{\tau}^{1/2}({\mathcal{F}};\varepsilon)d\varepsilon+\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{\tau}}H_{\tau}({\mathcal{F}};\varepsilon)d\varepsilon.

In particular, the following statement is a consequence of Proposition
4.3
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be a class of functions
f
such that
f(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq m+1}\|f^{(j)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1.

Suppose also that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then, for
m\geq 2,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{F}},p}(G_{n},G_{\Sigma})\lesssim(\|\Sigma\|+1)^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+(\|\Sigma\|+1)\|\Sigma\|\frac{p}{\sqrt{n}}.

On the other hand, for
m=1,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathcal{F}},p}(G_{n},G_{\Sigma})\lesssim(\|\Sigma\|+1)^{1/2}\|\Sigma\|_{2}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+(\|\Sigma\|+1)\|\Sigma\|\log n\frac{p}{\sqrt{n}}.

For
f,g:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}},
denote

\displaystyle d(f,g):=\|f^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}|f^{\prime\prime}(x)-g^{\prime\prime}(x)|.

Let

\displaystyle\rho_{d}=\rho_{d}({\mathcal{F}}):=\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}.

For the remainder process
R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma),f\in{\mathcal{F}},

the following result will be proved.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be a class of functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying the condition
f(0)=0.

Suppose
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n
and let
\delta\in[0,\rho_{d}/2].
Then, for all

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}|R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\int_{\delta}^{\rho_{d}}(H_{d}^{1/2}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)+1)d\varepsilon+\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\frac{p}{n}\int_{\delta}^{\rho_{d}}(H_{d}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)+1)d\varepsilon

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\int_{\delta}^{\rho_{d}}(H_{d}^{2}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)+1)d\varepsilon\Bigr)+\delta\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n}\vee\frac{p}{n}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr).

In particular, for
\delta=0,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}|R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}\int_{0}^{\rho_{d}}(H_{d}^{1/2}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)+1)d\varepsilon+\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\frac{p}{n}\int_{0}^{\rho_{d}}(H_{d}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)+1)d\varepsilon

\displaystyle+\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\int_{0}^{\rho_{d}}(H_{d}^{2}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)+1)d\varepsilon\Bigr).

In particular, Proposition
4.5
implies the following statement.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}\subset C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
be a class of functions
f
such that
f(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\max_{2\leq j\leq m+1}\|f^{(j)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1.

Suppose that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

Then,
for
m\geq 4,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}|R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)-{\mathbb{E}}R_{f}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)|\Bigr\|_{L_{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl((\|\Sigma\|+1)^{1/2}\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p}{n}}+(\|\Sigma\|+1)\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{\sqrt{n}}\vee 1\Bigr)\frac{p}{n}+(\|\Sigma\|+1)^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n}\vee 1\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr);

(4.1)

for

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Sigma\|^{2}\Bigl(\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)^{2}}{n}\vee\frac{p}{n}\vee\Bigl(\frac{p}{n}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigr)e^{-n/2p}

5
Bounding the bias of estimator
\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})

Our goal in this section is to prove the following result concerning the bias of estimator
\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma).

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
for some
m\geq 2,

f(0)=0
and
\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}<\infty.
Then

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f,m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)|\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|^{m+1}{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

For the proof, we need several deep results on higher-order Taylor expansions for functional
\tau_{f},
obtained in
[
31
]
.
Assume that function
f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+})
is extended to a function
f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}})

(such an extension always exists).
For self-adjoint operators
A,H,
denote by

\displaystyle S_{f}^{(m)}(A,H):=f(A+H)-f(A)-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}f(A+tH)|_{t=0}

the remainder of the
m
-th order Taylor expansion of operator function
f(A).

Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{k}
denote the class of functions
f\in C^{k}({\mathbb{R}})

such that the Fourier transform
\widetilde{f^{(k)}}
of the
k
-th derivative of
f

is integrable:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{k}}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|\widetilde{f^{(k)}}(t)|dt<\infty.

It was proved in
[
31
]
(see Theorems 1.1, 2.1) that, for all
f\in\bigcap_{k=0}^{m+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{k}
and all perturbations
H\in{\mathcal{S}}_{m+1},

there exists
a function
\eta_{m+1}=\eta_{m+1,f,A,H}\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
S_{f}^{(m)}(A,H)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
and the following higher order Lifshits-Krein spectral
shift formula holds

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{f}^{(m)}(A,H))=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f^{(m+1)}(\lambda)\eta_{m+1}(\lambda)d\lambda

with

\displaystyle\|\eta_{m+1}\|_{L_{1}}\lesssim_{m}\|H\|_{m+1}^{m+1}.

This result immediately provides a bound on the remainder of Taylor expansion
of order
m
for trace functional
\tau_{f}.
Namely, defining

\displaystyle R_{f}^{(m)}(A,H):=\tau_{f}(A+H)-\tau_{f}(A)-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\tau_{f}(A+tH)|_{t=0},

we get

\displaystyle|R_{f}^{(m)}(A,H)|\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|H\|_{m+1}^{m+1}.

(5.1)

It was also shown in
[
31
]
that, for all
t\in[0,1],

\frac{d^{m+1}}{dt^{m+1}}f(A+tH)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Bigl|{\rm tr}\Bigl(\frac{d^{m+1}}{dt^{m+1}}f(A+tH)\Bigr)\Bigr|\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|H\|_{m+1}^{m+1}.

The proof of these results relied on representations of higher order derivatives of
f(A)
as multiple
operator integrals (see
[
29
,
30
]
)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}f(A+tH)|_{t=0}=(D^{k}f)(A)[H,\dots,H],

where

\displaystyle(D^{k}f)(A)[H_{1},\dots,H_{k}]=\sum_{\pi\in S_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dots\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{[k]}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k+1})E_{A}(ds_{1})H_{\pi(1)}dE_{A}(ds_{2})H_{\pi(2)}\dots H_{\pi(k)}dE_{A}(ds_{k+1}),

(5.2)

S_{k}
being the set of all permutations of
1,\dots,k.

Here
f^{[k]}
is the
k
-th order divided difference of function
f,
defined for
k=0
as
f^{[0]}=f
and for
k\geq 1
as

\displaystyle f^{[k]}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k+1}):=\begin{cases}\frac{f^{[k-1]}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1},s_{k})-f^{[k-1]}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1},s_{k+1})}{s_{k}-s_{k+1}}&s_{k}\neq s_{k+1}\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f^{[k-1]}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k-1},t)|_{t=s_{k}}&s_{k}=s_{k+1}\end{cases}

and
E_{A}
a projection valued measure called the resolution of identity of self-adjoint operator
A.

Such integrals reduce to countable sums in the case when
A=\Sigma
is a covariance operator (or another operator with discrete spectrum).
Using the technique of multiple operator integrals, it was possible to prove that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Bigl|{\rm tr}\Bigl(\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}f(A+tH)\Bigr)\Bigr|\lesssim\|f^{(k)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|H\|_{k}^{k}

(under the assumption that
f\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{k}{\mathcal{W}}_{j}
).
Since
(D^{k}f)(A)[H_{1},\dots,H_{k}]
is a
k
-linear form and

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\Bigl(\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}f(A+tH)|_{t=0}\Bigr)={\rm tr}((D^{k}f)(A)[H,\dots,H]),

it easily follows that
{\rm tr}((D^{k}f)(A)[H_{1},\dots,H_{k}])
is a bounded
k
-linear form on space
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
(and, for that matter, on all the spaces
{\mathcal{S}}_{p}
for
p\leq k
).

These results allow us to obtain representation formulas for the bias of estimator
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
of
\tau_{f}(\Sigma)

slightly modifying (and specializing) Lemma 3.1 in
[
19
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f\in\bigcap_{k=0}^{m+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{k}
for some
m\geq 2
with
f(0)=0
and
\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}<\infty.
Suppose that
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.
Then, the following representation holds

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{\beta_{l,m}(\Sigma,f)}{n^{l}}+R_{n},

where coefficients
\beta_{l,m}(\Sigma,f)
do not depend on
n
and

\displaystyle|R_{n}|\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|^{m+1}{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

(5.3)

proof.

We use the
m
-th order Taylor expansion for the trace functional
\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})
around
\Sigma:

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})=\tau_{f}(\Sigma)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{{\rm tr}((D^{j}f)(\Sigma)[\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,\dots,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma])}{j!}+R_{f}^{(m)}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma),

which implies that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}{\rm tr}((D^{j}f)(\Sigma)[\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,\dots,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma])}{j!}+R_{n},

where
R_{n}={\mathbb{E}}R_{f}^{(m)}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma).
Since
{\rm tr}((D^{j}f)(\Sigma)[\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,\dots,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma])
are bounded
j
-linear forms
on the space
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}
of nuclear operators and
\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}_{1},
one can repeat the proof of Lemma 3.1 in
[
19
]

to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}{\rm tr}((D^{j}f)(\Sigma)[\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma,\dots,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma])}{j!}=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{\beta_{l,m}(\Sigma,f)}{n^{l}}

with coefficients
\beta_{l,m}(\Sigma,f)
not depending on
n.
We can now use bound (
5.1
) for
R_{f}^{(m)}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)

and the bound of Proposition
3.8
for
p=m+1
to show that, under the assumption
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n,

\displaystyle|R_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}|R_{f}^{(m)}(\Sigma,\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma)|\lesssim\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}{\mathbb{E}}\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|_{m+1}^{m+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|^{m+1}{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

∎

We are ready to provide the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

proof.

We will first assume that
f\in\bigcap_{k=0}^{m+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{k}.

We have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}({\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{j}})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)).

Using Proposition
5.1
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{\beta_{l,m}(\Sigma,f)}{n_{j}^{l}}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}R_{n_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{C_{j}}{n_{j}^{l}}\beta_{l,m}(\Sigma,f)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}R_{n_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}R_{n_{j}}

since

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{C_{j}}{n_{j}^{l}}=0,l=1,\dots,m-1.

Hence

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|C_{j}||R_{n_{j}}|\lesssim_{m}\max_{1\leq j\leq m}|R_{n_{j}}|.

By bound (
5.3
),

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq m}|R_{n_{j}}|\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|^{m+1}{\bf r}(\Sigma)\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n_{j}}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n_{j}}{n_{j}}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

Under the condition that
n_{j}\asymp_{m}n
for all
j=1,\dots,m,
this yields the bound

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)|\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|^{m+1}{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

(5.4)

Next, we extend the bound to all functions
f\in C^{m+1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
with
f(0)=0
and
\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}<\infty.

To this end, we will construct a sequence
\{f_{k}\}
of functions in
{\mathbb{R}}

such that
f_{k}\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{m+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{j}
and
f_{k}
converges to
f
pointwise as
k\to\infty.

Let
p
be the
(m+2)
-fold convolution of uniform densities in
[-1,1].

It is supported in the interval
[-m-2,m+2],

p(0)>0

and its characteristic function is

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}(t)=\Bigl(\frac{\sin(t)}{t}\Bigr)^{m+3},t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

It is easy to see that
p
is
m+1
times continuously differentiable and the functions

\displaystyle\widetilde{p^{(j)}}(t)=(-it)^{j}\widetilde{p}(t),t\in{\mathbb{R}}

are integrable for all
j=0,\dots,m+1.
Thus,
p\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{m+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{j}.

Let

\displaystyle p_{k}(x):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{k}}p\Bigl(\frac{x}{\sigma_{k}}\Bigr),x\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\sigma_{k}\in(0,1),\sigma_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty.

Clearly,
p_{k}\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{m+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{j}
for all
k\geq 1.

Define

\displaystyle f_{k}(x):=(f\ast p_{k})(x)-\frac{(f\ast p_{k})(0)}{p(0)}p(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Clearly,
f_{k}(0)=0.
Also, by continuity of
f,
we easily get that

(f\ast p_{k})(x)\to f(x)
as
k\to\infty
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

In particular,
(f\ast p_{k})(0)\to f(0)=0
as
k\to\infty.
Therefore,

f_{k}(x)\to f(x)
as
k\to\infty.

By simple properties of convolution, we have

\displaystyle f_{k}^{(j)}(x)=(f\ast p_{k}^{(j)})(x)-\frac{(f\ast p_{k})(0)}{p(0)}p^{(j)}(x),j=0,\dots,m+1,

which easily implies that

\displaystyle\widetilde{f_{k}^{(j)}}(t)=\widetilde{f}(t)\widetilde{p_{k}^{(j)}}(t)-\frac{(f\ast p_{k})(0)}{p(0)}\widetilde{p^{(j)}}(t),j=0,\dots,m+1.

Since,
p,p_{k}\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{m+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{j}
and
f\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
(implying that
\tilde{f}
is uniformly bounded),
it follows that
f_{k}\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{m+1}{\mathcal{W}}_{j}
for all
k\geq 1.

Thus, by (
5.4
),

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f_{k}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f_{k}}(\Sigma)|\lesssim_{m}\|f_{k}^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\|\Sigma\|^{m+1}{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

(5.5)

Note also that

\displaystyle f_{k}^{(j)}(x):=(f^{(j)}\ast p_{k})(x)-\frac{(f\ast p_{k})(0)}{p(0)}p^{(j)}(x),x\in{\mathbb{R}},j=0,\dots,m+1.

Using the Taylor expansion of order
m
of function
f
and the fact that
f(0)=0,
it is easy to check that

\displaystyle|f(-y)|\leq\frac{|f^{\prime}(0)|}{1!}|y|+\dots+\frac{|f^{(m)}(0)|}{m!}|y|^{m}+\frac{\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}}{(m+1)!}|y|^{m+1}.

Since

\displaystyle(f\ast p_{k})(0)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(-y)p_{k}(y)dy,

it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle|(f\ast p_{k})(0)|\lesssim\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{|f^{(l)}(0)|}{l!}\sigma_{k}^{l}+\frac{\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}}{(m+1)!}\sigma_{k}^{m+1}.

(5.6)

This yields the bound

\displaystyle\|f_{k}^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f^{(m+1)}\ast p_{k}\|_{L_{\infty}}+\frac{|(f\ast p_{k})(0)|}{p(0)}\|p^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}+(|f^{\prime}(0)|\vee\dots\vee|f^{(m)}(0)|)\sigma_{k}

(5.7)

and we can rewrite (
5.5
) as follows:

\displaystyle|{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f_{k}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f_{k}}(\Sigma)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}(\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}+(|f^{\prime}(0)|\vee\dots\vee|f^{(m)}(0)|)\sigma_{k})\|\Sigma\|^{m+1}{\bf r}(\Sigma)\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{{\bf r}(\Sigma)}{n}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}.

(5.8)

It remains to justify passing to the limit as

Note that, similarly to (

) and (
5

\displaystyle|f_{k}^{(j)}(0)|\lesssim|f^{(j)}(0)|+(|f^{(j+1)}(0)|\vee\dots\vee|f^{(m)}(0)|\vee\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}})\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f^{(j)}(0)|\vee|f^{(j+1)}(0)|\vee\dots\vee|f^{(m)}(0)|\vee\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}.

Therefore, by the

-th order Taylor expansion, for all

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}(x+x^{2}+\dots+x^{m+1})\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}}(dx)={\mathbb{E}}({\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})+\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\dots+\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}\|_{m+1}^{m+1})<\infty,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f_{k}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})={\mathbb{E}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f_{k}d\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}}\to{\mathbb{E}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}fd\mu_{\hat{\Sigma}_{n}}={\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})

as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f_{k}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f_{k}}(\Sigma)\to{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\ {\rm as}\ k\to\infty.

) as

f\psi_{k}\in C^{m+1}

and
\|(f\psi_{k})^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq C\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}
with a constant
C>0
that depends only on
\psi
and on
m.
Since
f\psi_{k}
are continuous functions with bounded
supports, we have
f\psi_{k}\in L_{1}({\mathbb{R}}),k\geq 1.
Thus, the bound of Theorem
5.1
holds for functions
f\psi_{k}
(and
\|(f\psi_{k})^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}

could be replaced by
\|f^{(m+1)}\|_{L_{\infty}}
up to a constant that depends only on
m
). To finish the proof, it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\tau_{f\psi_{k}}(\Sigma)\to\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\ {\rm and}\ {\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f\psi_{k}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})\to{\mathbb{E}}\tau_{f}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n})

(5.9)

as
k\to\infty
for all
n\geq 1.
This would imply that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f\psi_{k}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f\psi_{k}}(\Sigma)\to{\mathbb{E}}\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma)\ {\rm as}\ k\to\infty,

and the bound of Theorem
5.1
would follow.
It is easy to check that, for a nonnegative function
f,
the convergence (
5.9
) follows by the monotone convergence theorem.
In the general case, it is enough use the representation
f=f_{+}-f_{-},
where
f_{+}:=f\vee 0,f_{-}=-(f\wedge 0).

∎

We start with the proof of Proposition
2.3
.

proof.

In what follows, we will deal only with random variables
X\sim N(0,\Sigma)
taking values in a fixed subspace
L\subset{\mathbb{H}}
with

{\rm dim}(L)=d=[r].
In this case,
{\rm Im}(\Sigma)\subset L
and, assuming that
\|\Sigma\|\leq a,
we have
\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r).

Moreover, we can view
\Sigma
as a covariance operator in space
L
and, choosing an orthonormal basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
of
L,
we can
identify
L
with
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and view
\Sigma
as a
d\times d
covariance matrix.

We will use a version of the two hypotheses method (see
[
35
]
, Section 2.3).
Consider two covariance operators in
L:

\Sigma_{0}:=\gamma_{1}aI_{d}
and
\Sigma_{1}:=(1+\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{dn}})\gamma_{1}aI_{d},

where
c_{1}=\frac{\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}}.

It is immediate that
\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{1}\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r).

By a simple computation, the following bound
on the Kullback-Leibler distance between the distributions of i.i.d. observations

X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
sampled from
N(0,\Sigma_{0})
and from
N(0,\Sigma_{1})
holds:

\displaystyle K(N(0,\Sigma_{0})^{\otimes n}\|N(0,\Sigma_{1})^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=nK(N(0,\Sigma_{0})\|N(0,\Sigma_{1}))=n\Bigl({\rm tr}(\Sigma_{1}^{-1}\Sigma_{0})-d+\log\frac{{\rm det}(\Sigma_{1})}{{\rm det}(\Sigma_{0})}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=nd\Bigl(\frac{1}{1+c_{1}/\sqrt{nd}}-1+\log(1+c_{1}/\sqrt{nd})\Bigr)\lesssim c_{1}^{2}.

This bound easily implies that minimax error of testing the hypotheses
H_{0}:\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}
against
the alternative
H_{1}:\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}
is bounded away from zero by a constant depending only on
c_{1}

(see
[
35
]
, Theorem 2.2).
Note also that
\tau_{f}(\Sigma_{0})=f(\gamma_{1}a)d
and
\tau_{f}(\Sigma_{1})=f(\gamma_{1}a(1+c_{1}/\sqrt{nd}))d.

Without loss of generality, assume that
f^{\prime}(x)\geq\lambda,x\in[\gamma_{1}a,\gamma_{2}a].

Then,

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\Sigma_{1})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma_{0})=d\int_{\gamma_{1}a}^{\gamma_{1}a(1+c_{1}/\sqrt{nd})}f^{\prime}(x)dx\geq d\lambda\gamma_{1}ac_{1}/\sqrt{nd}=\lambda\gamma_{1}c_{1}a\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}.

By a standard argument used in the two hypotheses method, this easily implies

\displaystyle\inf_{T_{n,f}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(T_{n,f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{T_{n,f}}\max_{\Sigma\in\{\Sigma_{0},\Sigma_{1}\}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(T_{n,f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f}(\Sigma))^{2}\gtrsim\lambda\gamma_{1}c_{1}a\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}},

completing the proof.

∎

Next, we provide the proof of Proposition
2.4
.

proof.

First note that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}I(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|<\delta)+{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}I(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}I(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|<\delta)+{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{2}I(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ +|g(\Sigma)|{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|<\delta\}.

(7.1)

Next, observe that, for
\Sigma\in D_{g},

\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{j}\|<\delta
for some (in fact, unique) value of
j.

If
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|<\delta,
we have
\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma_{j}\|<2\delta,

which also holds for the unique
value of
j.
Thus, we have that
g(\Sigma)=\tau_{f_{j}}(\Sigma)
and
\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})=\hat{T}_{f_{j}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}),

implying that, for
\Sigma
satisfying
\|\Sigma-\Sigma_{j}\|<\delta,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}I(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|<\delta)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f_{j}}(\Sigma))^{2}I(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|<\delta)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f_{j}}(\Sigma))^{2}.

Therefore, for all
\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap D_{g},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}I(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|<\delta)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq N}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\hat{T}_{f}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\tau_{f_{j}}(\Sigma))^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,a}\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}+r\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1},

(7.2)

where we used the bound of Theorem
2.1
.

On the other hand note that, for all
\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r),

\displaystyle|g(\Sigma)|\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|\tau_{f_{j}}(\Sigma)|\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\|f_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}{\rm tr}(\Sigma)\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\|f_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}ar\lesssim_{a}r

(7.3)

and

\displaystyle|\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})|\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|\hat{T}_{f_{j}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})|=\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\Bigl|\sum_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}\tau_{f_{j}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{i}})\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}|C_{i}|\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|\tau_{f_{j}}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{i}})|\lesssim_{m}\max_{1\leq j\leq N}\|f_{j}^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{i}})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\max_{1\leq i\leq m}{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{i}}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{2}I(\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta)\leq{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/4}\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{4}{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\|\max_{1\leq i\leq m}{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{i}})\|_{L_{4}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta\}\lesssim_{m}\|\max_{1\leq i\leq m}{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{i}})\|_{L_{4}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\Bigl({\rm tr}(\Sigma)+\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\|{\rm tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{n_{i}})-{\rm tr}(\Sigma)\|_{L_{4}}\Bigr){\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\|\Sigma\|{\bf r}(\Sigma){\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta\},

(7.4)

where we used the bound of Proposition
3.5
and the conditions that
n_{i}\asymp n,i=1,\dots,m
and
{\bf r}(\Sigma)\lesssim n.

It follows from bounds (
7
), (
7
), (
7.3
) and (
7
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap D_{g}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m,a}\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}+r\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}+r\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)}{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta\}.

It remains to use bounds (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) to get, under the conditions
\delta\geq C\sqrt{\frac{r}{n}}

for a sufficiently large numerical constant
C>0,

a\lesssim 1
and
r\leq n,
that

\displaystyle\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)}{\mathbb{P}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}\{\|\hat{\Sigma}_{n}-\Sigma\|\geq\delta\}\leq\exp\{-n(\delta\wedge\delta^{2})/2\},

which implies the claim.

∎

We now turn to the proof of Proposition
2.5
.

proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
2.3
, we assume that random variable
X\sim N(0,\Sigma)
takes values in a fixed subspace

L\subset{\mathbb{H}}
with
{\rm dim}(L)=d=[r]
and use a representation of
\Sigma
as a
d\times d
covariance matrix.

To prove the first bound of Proposition
2.5
, one has to construct functionals from the class
{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}

for which a minimax lower bound of order
\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}
holds.
This is done for the functional
g(\Sigma):={\rm tr}(\Sigma)I_{B(\Sigma_{0},\delta)}(\Sigma)

for any
\delta\geq\frac{2c_{1}}{\sqrt{nd}}
and the proof of the minimax lower bound is based
on the two hypotheses method (exactly as in the proof of the lower bound of Proposition
2.3
).

A more difficult argument is needed to construct functionals
g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}
for which a minimax lower bound of the order
d\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1}
holds. Note that it is enough to prove this bound only for a sufficiently large
d,
say,
d\geq d_{0}
(otherwise, the
first term
\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}
will be dominant up to a constant that depends only on
d_{0}
).

First, we construct a sufficiently
large set
\Sigma_{\omega},\omega\in B\subset\{-1,1\}^{d(d+1)/2}
of “well separated”
d\times d
covariance matrices. Namely,
let
A_{\omega}:=(\omega_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}
be a symmetric matrix with entries
\omega_{ij}=\pm 1,
and define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\omega}:=I_{d}+\varepsilon\Bigl(2I_{d}+\frac{2A_{\omega}}{\sqrt{d}}\Bigr),\omega=(\omega_{ij}:i\leq j)\in\{-1,1\}^{d(d+1)/2},

with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
to be chosen later. Assuming that
\omega_{ij},i\leq j
are independent Rademacher random variables,
A_{\omega}

is a symmetric
d\times d
Bernoulli random matrix. It is well known that, with a high probability,
\|A_{\omega}\|\leq c^{\prime}\sqrt{d}
for some
constant
c^{\prime}\geq 2.
Moreover, by Wigner theorem,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}{\rm tr}\Bigl(\Bigl(2I_{d}+\frac{2A_{\omega}}{\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)^{m+1}\Bigr)\overset{{\mathbb{P}}}{\longrightarrow}\int_{-2}^{2}(2+x)^{m+1}\mu_{\rm sc}(dx)=:c_{m}>0\ {\rm as}\ d\to\infty,

where
\mu_{\rm sc}(dx)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{4-x^{2}}dx
is Wigner’s semicircle law in
[-2,2].
It follows that there exists a sequence
\delta_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
and a number
\bar{d}\geq 1
such that
with probability at least
1/2
for all
d\geq\bar{d}

\displaystyle\|A_{\omega}\|\leq c^{\prime}\sqrt{d}

(7.5)

and

\displaystyle\Bigl|\frac{1}{d}{\rm tr}\Bigl(\Bigl(2I_{d}+\frac{2A_{\omega}}{\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)^{m+1}\Bigr)-c_{m}\Bigr|\leq\delta_{d}.

(7.6)

In other words, for all
d\geq\bar{d},
there exists a subset
B_{d}^{\prime}\subset\{-1,1\}^{d(d+1)/2}
such that
{\rm card}(B_{d}^{\prime})\geq 2^{d(d+1)/2-1}
and, for all
\omega\in B_{d}^{\prime},

bounds (
7.5
) and (
7.6
) hold. In what follows, we assume that
(c^{\prime}-1)\varepsilon<1/4.
This implies that, for all
\omega\in B_{d}^{\prime},

\Sigma_{\omega}
is a
covariance operator with
\|\Sigma_{\omega}\|\lesssim 1
and
\|\Sigma_{\omega}^{-1}\|\lesssim 1.

We can now use standard arguments from the proof of Varshamov-Gilbert lemma to show that there exists
B\subset B_{d}^{\prime}

such that
{\rm card}(B)\geq 2^{d^{2}/8}
and

\displaystyle h(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\sum_{i\leq j}I(\omega_{ij}\neq\omega_{ij}^{\prime})\geq d^{2}/8,\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in B,\omega\neq\omega^{\prime}

(see the proof of Proposition 5.1 in
[
19
]
for a similar argument with more details).

Note that, for all
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in B,\omega\neq\omega^{\prime},

\displaystyle\|\Sigma_{\omega}-\Sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\frac{2\varepsilon}{\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)^{2}\|A_{\omega}-A_{\omega^{\prime}}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\sum_{i,j}(\omega_{ij}-\omega_{ij}^{\prime})^{2}\geq\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\sum_{i\leq j}(\omega_{ij}-\omega_{ij}^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{16\varepsilon^{2}}{d}h(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\geq\frac{16\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{d^{2}}{8}=2\varepsilon^{2}d

and

\displaystyle\|\Sigma_{\omega}-\Sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}\|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\|\Sigma_{\omega}-\Sigma_{\omega^{\prime}}\|_{2}\geq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon.

We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. random variables sampled from
N(0,\Sigma_{\omega}),\omega\in B.

Suppose that
\varepsilon\leq c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}
for a small enough constant
c_{1}>0.

Then

\displaystyle\inf_{\hat{\omega}}\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\omega}}\|\Sigma_{\hat{\omega}}-\Sigma_{\omega}\|_{2}^{2}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{2}d,

where the infimum is taken over all the estimators
\hat{\omega}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of parameter
\omega
based on
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}.

The proof relies on a well known approach to minimax lower bounds based on many hypotheses
(see
[
35
]
, Section 2.6).

In what follows,
\varepsilon:=c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}
for a small enough constant
c_{1}>0

(as in Lemma
7.1
) and we also assume that
(c^{\prime}-1)\varepsilon<1/4.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
f\in C^{m+2}({\mathbb{R}})
be a function such that
f(0)=0,

f(1)=\dots=f^{(m)}(1)=0
and
f^{(m+1)}(1)\neq 0.

Suppose also that
\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1,

\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1
and
\|f^{(m+2)}\|_{L_{\infty}}\leq 1.
Then, for all
d\geq\bar{d}

and all
\omega\in B,

\displaystyle|\tau_{f}(\Sigma_{\omega})-c_{m}(f)\varepsilon^{m+1}d|\lesssim_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon),

where

\displaystyle c_{m}(f):=c_{m}\frac{f^{(m+1)}(1)}{(m+1)!}.

proof.

Clearly,

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\Sigma_{\omega})={\rm tr}(f(\Sigma_{\omega}))={\rm tr}\Bigl(f\Bigl(I_{d}+\varepsilon\Bigl(2I_{d}+\frac{2A_{\omega}}{\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr).

Note that

\displaystyle f(1+\varepsilon(2+x))=\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}\frac{f^{(j)}(1)}{j!}\varepsilon^{j}(2+x)^{j}+R(x)=\frac{f^{(m+1)}(1)}{(m+1)!}\varepsilon^{m+1}(2+x)^{m+1}+R(x),

where the following bound holds for the remainder
R(x):

\displaystyle|R(x)|\leq\frac{\|f^{(m+2)}\|_{L_{\infty}}}{(m+2)!}\varepsilon^{m+2}|2+x|^{m+2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\tau_{f}(\Sigma_{\omega})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}f\Bigl(1+\varepsilon\Bigl(2+\lambda_{k}\Bigl(\frac{2A_{\omega}}{\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr)=\frac{f^{(m+1)}(1)}{(m+1)!}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\frac{1}{d}{\rm tr}\Bigl(2I_{d}+\frac{2A_{\omega}}{\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)^{m+1}

\displaystyle=c_{m}\frac{f^{(m+1)}(1)}{(m+1)!}\varepsilon^{m+1}d+\rho=c_{m}(f)\varepsilon^{m+1}d+\rho

where

\displaystyle|\rho|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|f^{(m+1)}(1)|}{(m+1)!}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\delta_{d}+\frac{\|f^{(m+2)}\|_{L_{\infty}}}{(m+2)!}\varepsilon^{m+2}d\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\Bigl|2+\lambda_{k}\Bigl(\frac{2A_{\omega}}{\sqrt{d}}\Bigr)\Bigr|^{m+2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|f^{(m+1)}(1)|}{(m+1)!}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\delta_{d}+2^{m+2}(1+c^{\prime})^{m+2}\frac{\|f^{(m+2)}\|_{L_{\infty}}}{(m+2)!}\varepsilon^{m+2}d

\displaystyle\lesssim_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon).

∎

Assume now that
f\in C^{m+2}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
7.2
and
f^{(m+1)}(1)=b>0,

b\asymp 1.

Let
\delta:=\varepsilon/4
and define

\displaystyle g_{ij}(\Sigma):=\sum_{\omega\in B}I_{B(\Sigma_{\omega},\delta)}(\Sigma)\tau_{\omega_{ij}f}(\Sigma),\Sigma\in D_{g_{ij}},1\leq i\leq j\leq d.

Clearly,
g_{ij}\in{\mathcal{G}_{\delta}},i\leq j
and

\displaystyle g_{ij}(\Sigma_{\omega})=\tau_{\omega_{ij}f}(\Sigma_{\omega})=\omega_{ij}\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d+\rho_{ij},i\leq j,

(7.7)

where
\bar{c}_{m}=\frac{c_{m}b}{(m+1)!}
and

\displaystyle\max_{i\leq j}|\rho_{ij}|\lesssim_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon).

(7.8)

Suppose now that

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}}\inf_{T_{n,g}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap D_{g}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(T_{n,g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}<\Delta.

Then, for all
i\leq j,
there exist estimators
\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of functionals
g_{ij}(\Sigma)
such that

\displaystyle\max_{i\leq j}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap D_{g_{ij}}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g_{ij}(\Sigma))^{2}<\Delta,

which implies that

\displaystyle\max_{i\leq j}\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\omega}}^{1/2}(\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g_{ij}(\Sigma_{\omega}))^{2}<\Delta.

(7.9)

Denote
\tilde{\omega}_{ij}:={\rm sign}(\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}))

and let

\displaystyle\hat{\omega}\in{\rm Argmin}_{\omega\in B}\sum_{i,j}(\tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\omega_{ij})^{2}.

Then, for all
\omega\in B,

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{i,j}(\hat{\omega}_{ij}-\omega_{ij})^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\leq\Bigl(\sum_{i,j}(\hat{\omega}_{ij}-\tilde{\omega}_{ij})^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}+\Bigl(\sum_{i,j}(\tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\omega_{ij})^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\leq 2\Bigl(\sum_{i,j}(\tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\omega_{ij})^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\Sigma_{\hat{\omega}}-\Sigma_{\omega}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\sum_{i,j}(\hat{\omega}_{ij}-\omega_{ij})^{2}\leq\frac{16\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\sum_{i,j}(\tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\omega_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{16\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{i,j}(\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij})^{2}

(7.10)

Note that

\displaystyle|\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij}|\leq 2|\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij}|.

(7.11)

Indeed, suppose
\tilde{\omega}_{ij}=+1
and
\omega_{ij}=-1.
Then,

\displaystyle|\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij}|=2\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d.

On the other hand,
\tilde{\omega}_{ij}=+1
implies that
\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\geq 0
and

\displaystyle|\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij}|=\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})+\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\geq\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d=\frac{1}{2}|\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij}|,

which implies (
7.11
). Similar argument holds when
\tilde{\omega}_{ij}=-1,\omega_{ij}=+1.

Note also that, by (
7.7
) and (
7.8
),

\displaystyle|\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij}|\leq|\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g_{ij}(\Sigma_{\omega})|+|g_{ij}(\Sigma_{\omega})-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij}|

\displaystyle\leq|\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g_{ij}(\Sigma_{\omega})|+c_{m}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{m+1}d(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon).

Using the last bound along with (
7
) and (
7.11
), we get

\displaystyle\|\Sigma_{\hat{\omega}}-\Sigma_{\omega}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{16\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{i,j}(\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \tilde{\omega}_{ij}-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{6}\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{i,j}(\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-\bar{c}_{m}\varepsilon^{m+1}d\ \omega_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{7}\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{i,j}(\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g_{ij}(\Sigma_{\omega}))^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{7}\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}(c_{m}^{\prime})^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}d^{2}(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{7}\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{i,j}(\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g_{ij}(\Sigma_{\omega}))^{2}+c_{m}^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{2}d(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon)^{2},

where
c_{m}^{\prime\prime}:=\frac{2^{7}(c_{m}^{\prime})^{2}}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\omega}}\|\Sigma_{\hat{\omega}}-\Sigma_{\omega}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{7}\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{i,j}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\omega}}(\tilde{T}_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g_{ij}(\Sigma_{\omega}))^{2}+c_{m}^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{2}d(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon)^{2},

and, by bound (
7.9
),

\displaystyle\max_{\omega\in B}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma_{\omega}}\|\Sigma_{\hat{\omega}}-\Sigma_{\omega}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2^{7}\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}\Delta^{2}+c_{m}^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{2}d(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon)^{2}.

By Lemma
7.1
, we get that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}d\leq C^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{d}\frac{1}{\bar{c}_{m}^{2}\varepsilon^{2(m+1)}}\Delta^{2}+c_{m}^{\prime\prime}\varepsilon^{2}d(\delta_{d}+\varepsilon)^{2}\Bigr)

with a sufficiently large constant
C^{\prime}>0.
Since also
\delta_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty,
we can choose
d_{0}\geq\bar{d}
so that, for all

d\geq d_{0},

\delta_{d}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{8C^{\prime}c_{m}^{\prime\prime}}.
If, in addition,
\varepsilon^{2}=c_{1}^{2}\frac{d}{n}\leq\frac{1}{8C^{\prime}c_{m}^{\prime\prime}}
(which holds
if constant
c_{1}
is small enough), we can conclude
that
\Delta\gtrsim\varepsilon^{m+1}d
for all
d\geq d_{0},

which implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{g\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\delta}}\inf_{T_{n,g}}\sup_{\Sigma\in{\mathcal{S}}(a,r)\cap D_{g}}{\mathbb{E}}_{\Sigma}^{1/2}(T_{n,g}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})-g(\Sigma))^{2}\gtrsim d\Bigl(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\Bigr)^{m+1},

completing the proof.

∎

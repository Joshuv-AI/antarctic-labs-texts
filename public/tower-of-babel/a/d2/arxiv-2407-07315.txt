CosmoCLIP: Generalizing Large Vision-Language Models for Astronomical Imaging
Raza Imam, Mohammed T. Alam †, Umaima Rahman, Mohsen Guizani, Fakhri Karray

Abstract
Existing vision-text contrastive learning models enhance representation transferability and support zero-shot prediction by matching paired image and caption embeddings while pushing unrelated pairs apart. However, astronomical image-label datasets are significantly smaller compared to general image and label datasets available from the internet. We introduce CosmoCLIP , an astronomical image-text contrastive learning framework precisely fine-tuned on the pre-trained CLIP [ 1 ] model using SpaceNet and BLIP-based descriptive captions. SpaceNet, attained via FLARE [ 2 ] , constitutes ∼ \sim 13k optimally distributed images, while BLIP acts as a rich knowledge extractor . The rich semantics derived from this SpaceNet and BLIP descriptions, when learned contrastively , enable CosmoCLIP to achieve superior generalization across various in-domain and out-of-domain tasks. Our results demonstrate that CosmoCLIP is a straightforward yet powerful framework, significantly outperforming CLIP in zero-shot classification and image-text retrieval tasks.

Method: CosmoCLIP

This section provides technical details about
CosmoCLIP
, as shown in Figure
1
.
CosmoCLIP
includes three main components: (1) vision and text encoders, which extract embeddings; (2) knowledge extraction, which creates the context similarity matrix; and (3) context similarity training, which fine-tunes the pre-trained VLM.

2.1
Vision-Text Encodings

CosmoCLIP
consists of two encoders designed to process text and visual inputs simultaneously.
The visual encoder, denoted as
\mathcal{E}_{v}
, maps a visual input
X_{img}
to a fixed-length embeddings
\boldsymbol{f}_{v}
. A projection head
\mathcal{V}
then maps
\boldsymbol{f}_{v}
to
\boldsymbol{F}_{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as:

\boldsymbol{f}_{v}=\mathcal{E}_{v}(X_{img})\hskip 28.45274pt\Rightarrow\hskip 28.45274pt\boldsymbol{F}_{v}=\mathcal{V}(\boldsymbol{f}_{v})

(1)

The text encoder,
\mathcal{E}_{t}
, processes text input, generating a latent textual embeddings
\boldsymbol{f}_{t}
from the class labels
y
, followed by projection of raw embeddings
\boldsymbol{f}_{t}
to
\boldsymbol{F}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as:

\boldsymbol{f}_{t}=\mathcal{E}_{t}(X_{img})\hskip 28.45274pt\Rightarrow\hskip 28.45274pt\boldsymbol{F}_{t}=\mathcal{T}(\boldsymbol{f}_{t})

(2)

where
\mathcal{T}
is the projection head, while
\boldsymbol{F}_{v}
and
\boldsymbol{F}_{t}
are feature representations for images and texts.

2.2
Knowledge Extraction

Knowledge extraction in
CosmoCLIP
involves generating high-quality image-text pairs using a large-scale captioning model, such as BLIP, denoted as
G_{caption}
. Given an input image
X_{img}
, the captioner generates a caption
L_{img}
:

L_{img}=G_{caption}(X_{img})

(3)

By applying
G_{caption}
to a large dataset, we obtain
N
paired image-text samples:

(X_{img}^{i},L_{img}^{i})=(X_{img}^{i},G_{caption}(X_{img}^{i}))~\text{$\forall$ $i$}=\{1,\ldots N\}

(4)

These pairs are used to construct a context similarity matrix, which is then utilized for context similarity training to fine-tune the pre-trained model.

2.3
Context Similarity Training

Context similarity training in
CosmoCLIP
aims to align image and text embeddings in a shared space, enhancing their mutual understanding. Following the Eq.
1
and
2
, the image and text feature representations (
\boldsymbol{F}_{v}
and
\boldsymbol{F}_{t}
) are projected into a joint embedding space using learned projection matrices
W_{v}
and
W_{t}
to achieve normalized embeddings as:

\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}_{v}}=\frac{\boldsymbol{F}_{v}\cdot W_{v}}{\|\boldsymbol{F}_{v}\cdot W_{v}\|_{2}},\quad\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}_{t}}=\frac{\boldsymbol{F}_{t}\cdot W_{t}}{\|\boldsymbol{F}_{t}\cdot W_{t}\|_{2}}

(5)

Next, the scaled pairwise cosine similarities between image and text embeddings are computed:

\hat{y}=\exp(\tau)\cdot(\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}_{v}}\cdot\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}_{t}}^{\top})

(6)

The symmetric loss function is then calculated using cross-entropy (CE) loss
[
21
]
, applied along both axes of the similarity matrix:

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\text{CE}(\hat{y},y,\text{axis}=0)+\text{CE}(\hat{y},y,\text{axis}=1)\right)

(7)

For zero-shot inference, each text feature with class labels
y\in{1,2,\cdots,M}
is paired with the image feature. The prediction probability for class
y_{i}
given input
X_{img}
is expressed as:

p(y_{i}|{X_{img}})=\frac{\hat{y}_{i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{C}\hat{y}_{j}}

(8)

where
\hat{y}
denotes the cosine similarity, and
\tau
is the softmax temperature parameter.

Experimentation Details

Datasets

For fine-tuning the CLIP, we use our curated SpaceNet dataset
[
2
]
, which is
in-domain
and contains about 12,900 samples across eight fine-grained classes: Planet, Asteroid, Nebula, Comet, Star, Black Hole, Galaxy, and Constellation. SpaceNet, representing an
optimal

[
2

For testing
out-of-domain

[

[

out-of-domain
datasets reflect real-world noisy distributions.

Implementation Details

For fine-tuning, we initialized an ImageNet pre-trained CLIP-ViT-B/16 variant which includes a 12-layer Vision Transformer (ViT) for image encoding, producing 768-dimensional embeddings, and a 12-layer, 512-dimensional text encoder. This variant processes images at a resolution of 224
\times
224 pixels and handles text sequences up to 77 tokens.
We implemented a linear projection head with 512 output dimensions and a learnable temperature parameter
\tau
starting at 1.00. The other settings are: learning rate of 2e-5, batch size of 32, 10 epochs, and train:validation:test split of 80:10:10. We ran the entire setup on a single NVIDIA A100 40GB GPU. Additionally, following Eq.
4
, we generated our textual descriptions via Image captioning models including LLaVa
[
20
]
and BLIP
[
19
]
.

4
Conclusion

We present
CosmoCLIP
, an astronomical image-text contrastive learning framework, fine-tuned on the pre-trained CLIP model
[
1
]
using the optimally distributed SpaceNet dataset from the FLARE framework
[
2
]
and descriptive captions generated by BLIP captioning model.
CosmoCLIP
surpasses the baseline CLIP, achieving state-of-the-art performance across zero-shot classification and image-text retrieval tasks by a significant margin. With its rich feature semantics,
CosmoCLIP
is poised to become a foundational model for the astronomical domain, capable of handling a wide range of downstream tasks. In future, we plan to extend
CosmoCLIP
’s capabilities to encompass video analysis, further broadening its applicability and impact in the astronomical domain.

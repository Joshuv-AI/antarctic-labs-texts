BEHAVIOUR OF THE NORMALIZED DEPTH FUNCTION
ANTONINO FICARRA, JÜRGEN HERZOG, TAKAYUKI HIBI

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a squarefree monomial ideal,
K
a field. The
k
th squarefree power
I^{[k]}
of
I
is the monomial ideal of
S
generated by all squarefree monomials belonging to
I^{k}
. The biggest integer
k
such that
I^{[k]}\neq(0)
is called the monomial grade of
I
and it is denoted by
\nu(I)
. Let
d_{k}
be the minimum degree of the monomials belonging to
I^{[k]}
. Then,
\text{depth}(S/I^{[k]})\geq d_{k}-1
for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(I)
. The normalized depth function of
I
is defined as
g_{I}(k)=\text{depth}(S/I^{[k]})-(d_{k}-1)
,
1\leq k\leq\nu(I)
. It is expected that
g_{I}(k)
is a non-increasing function for any
I
. In this article we study the behaviour of
g_{I}(k)
under various operations on monomial ideals. Our main result characterizes all cochordal graphs
G
such that for the edge ideal
I(G)
of
G
we have
g_{I(G)}(1)=1
. They are precisely all cochordal graphs
G
whose complementary graph
G^{c}
is connected and has a cut vertex. As a far-reaching application, for given integers
1\leq s<m
we construct a graph
G
such that
\nu(I(G))=m
and
g_{I(G)}(k)=0
if and only if
k=s+1,\dots,m
. Finally, we show that any non-increasing function of non-negative integers is the normalized depth function of some squarefree monomial ideal.

1. Introduction

The study of the algebraic properties of the powers of a homogeneous ideal
I
of a polynomial ring
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
K
a field, is a classical topic in Commutative Algebra. Many of the known invariants of
I
behave asymptotically well, that is, stabilize or show a regular behaviour for sufficiently high powers of
I
.
In the last two decades the study of the depth function
f_{I}(k)=\textup{depth}(S/I^{k})
of a homogeneous ideal
I
has attracted a lot of interest. A classical result of Brodmann
[
4
]
assures that
f_{I}(k)
is constant for
k\gg 0
. On the other hand, the initial behaviour of
f_{I}(k)
remained quite elusive for a long time. It was conjectured in
[
14
]
that for any bounded convergent function
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
there exists a suitable homogeneous ideal
I
such that
\varphi=f_{I}
. Many years later, this conjecture was settled in the affirmative by H.T. Hà, H. Nguyen, N. Trung and T. Trung in
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Recently, the study of the depth function of squarefree powers of squarefree monomial ideals was initiated in
[
8
]
, see also
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
17
,
18
]
. Let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal and
G(I)
be its unique minimal set of monomial generators. The
k
th squarefree power
of
I
, denoted by
I^{[k]}
, is the monomial ideal generated by the squarefree monomials of
I^{k}
. Thus
u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}
,
u_{i}\in G(I)
,
i=1,\dots,k
, belongs to
G(I^{[k]})
if and only if
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k}
is a regular sequence. Let
\nu(I)
be the
monomial grade
of
I
,
i.e.
, the biggest length of a monomial regular sequence contained in
I
. Then
I^{[k]}
is non-zero if and only if
k\leq\nu(I)
.

Our motivation for studying such powers also comes from graph theory. Let
G
be a finite simple graph on vertex set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
,
i.e.
,
G
has no loops or multiple or directed edges. Furthermore, all the graphs we consider in this article do not have isolated vertices. The
edge ideal

I(G)
, associated to
G
, is the ideal of
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
generated by all squarefree monomials
x_{i}x_{j}
,
i\neq j
, such that
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. A
matching

M
of
G
is a set of edges of
G
such that no two distinct edges of
M
have common vertices. If
|M|=k
, then
M
is called a
k
-matching
. We denote by
\nu(G)
the
matching number
of
G
, that is the biggest size of a matching of
G
. Then, if
u_{p}=x_{i_{p}}x_{j_{p}}\in I(G)
,
p=1,\dots,k
, we have that
u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}\in G(I(G)^{[k]})
if and only if
M=\big\{\{i_{p},j_{p}\}:p=1,\dots,k\big\}
is a
k
-matching of
G
. In particular,
\nu(I(G))=\nu(G)
.

Again, let
I\subset S
be a squarefree monomial ideal. We always let
S
to be the smallest polynomial ring that contains
G(I)
. Our main object of study is the
normalized depth function
of
I
. For
1\leq k\leq\nu(I)
, we denote by
d_{k}=\textup{indeg}(I^{[k]})
the
initial degree
of
I^{[k]}
,
i.e.
, the minimum degree of a monomial generator of
I^{[k]}
. Then, for all
k\geq 0
such that
I^{[k]}\neq(0)
, we have
\textup{depth}(S/I^{[k]})\geq d_{k}-1

[
8
, Proposition 1.1(b)]
. The normalized depth function of
I
is defined as

g_{I}(k)=\textup{depth}(S/I^{[k]})-(d_{k}-1),\ \ \ \ \ k=1,\dots,\nu(I).

In contrast to the behaviour of the depth function of ordinary powers, a quite different situation is expected. Indeed, it was predicted in
[
8
]
that the following is true:

Conjecture.

For any squarefree monomial ideal,
g_{I}(k)
is a non-increasing function.

At present, this conjecture is widely open. In this article, we investigate the behaviour of the normalized depth function under some general operations and for a large class of edge ideals.

Let us discuss now the outlines of the article.

In Section 2, we discuss the behaviour of the normalized depth function with respect to two standard operations on monomial ideals: products and sums. In Theorem
2.1
, we show that the normalized depth function is additive if we take products of monomial ideals
I_{1}\subset S_{1}
,
I_{2}\subset S_{2}
of polynomial rings
S_{1},S_{2}
in disjoint sets of variables. That is
g_{I_{1}I_{2}}(k)=g_{I_{1}}(k)+g_{I_{2}}(k)
. Hence, in Corollary
2.2
we deduce that
g_{I_{1}I_{2}}
is non-increasing if both
g_{I_{1}}
,
g_{I_{2}}
are non-increasing. Then, we analyze the relationship between
g_{I}
and
g_{(I,x)}
where
x
is a variable not dividing any monomial generator of
I
. Under mild hypotheses, the precise relationship is obtained in Proposition
2.4
. Its proof depends on the concept of
Betti splitting

[
9
]
and a criterion of Bolognini (Proposition
2.3
). Next, if
g_{I}
is non-increasing, then
g_{(I,x)}
is non-increasing, too (Corollary
2.5
).

Section 3 contains our main two results. We focus our attention on the class of cochordal graphs. Recall that a graph
G
is called
cochordal
if its
complementary graph

G^{c}
is
chordal
, that is,
G^{c}
does not contain induced cycles of length greater than three. In 1990
[
10
]
, Fröberg proved that
I(G)
has a linear resolution if and only if
G
is cochordal. This result has been further refined by Herzog, Hibi and Zheng
[
15
]
by showing that
G
is cochordal if and only if all ordinary powers
I(G)^{k}
have linear quotients. It was noted in
[
8
, Corollary 3.2]
that all the squarefree powers
I(G)^{[k]}
have linear quotients,
k=1,\dots,\nu(G)
, if
G
is cochordal. Furthermore, in
[
8
, Corollary 2.2]
all graphs
G
such that
g_{I(G)}(1)=0
have been classified. In Theorem
3.1
we classify all cochordal graphs
G
such that
g_{I(G)}(1)=1
. They are precisely all cochordal graphs
G
such that
G^{c}
is connected with a cut vertex. Moreover, if
G
is such a graph, the normalized depth function is
g_{I(G)}(1)=1
and
g_{I(G)}(k)=0
for
k=2,\dots,\nu(G)
. The proof of this theorem relies upon Hochster’s formula and a criterion obtained in
[
8
]
(Proposition
3.5
). An indispensable tool is the notion of
special
k
-matching
(Definition
3.7
), see also Example
3.6
. A far-reaching application of Theorem
3.1
is given in Theorem
3.10
. Note that the conjecture on the non-increasingness of
g_{I}
would also imply that if
g_{I}(k)=0
then
g_{I}(k+1)=0
, too, for any
k<\nu(I)
. Hence, it is natural to consider the following problem, which was raised in
[
8
]
.

Problem.

For given integers
1\leq s<m
, find a finite simple graph
G
with
\nu(G)=m
such that
g_{I(G)}(k)>0
for
k=1,\dots,s
and
g_{I(G)}(k)=0
for
k=s+1,\dots,m
.

In Theorem
3.10
we solve the above problem. A variation of Proposition
2.4
(Lemma
3.11
) is required for its proof. In particular, for the graph
G
we construct to solve the above problem, we have
g_{I(G)}(k)=s-(k-1)
for
k=1,\dots,s
and
g_{I(G)}(k)=0
for
k=s+1,\dots,m
,
m=\nu(I(G))
.

In Section 4, we show that any non-increasing sequence of non-negative integers can be the normalized depth function of some squarefree monomial ideal (Theorem
4.1
). On the other hand, it is an open question if any non-increasing function can be the normalized depth function of an edge ideal.

Macaulay2

]
and CoCoA

1
]
which have been invaluable tools to make our experiments.

2. The behaviour of the normalized depth function with respect to some operations on monomial ideals

In this section we analyze the behaviour of the normalized depth function with respect to some operations on monomial ideals.

Our first result shows that the normalized depth function is additive with respect to the product of monomial ideals in disjoint sets of variables.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
S_{1}=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
S_{2}=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
be polynomial rings in disjoint sets of variables and let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
. Let
I_{1}\subset S_{1}
,
I_{2}\subset S_{2}
be squarefree monomial ideals. Then,
\nu(I_{1}I_{2})=\min\{\nu(I_{1}),\nu(I_{2})\}
and for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(I_{1}I_{2})
,

g_{I_{1}I_{2}}(k)=g_{I_{1}}(k)+g_{I_{2}}(k).

Proof.

Obviously,
\nu(I_{1}I_{2})=\min\{\nu(I_{1}),\nu(I_{2})\}
. Let
1\leq k\leq\nu(I_{1}I_{2})
. Note that
(I_{1}I_{2})^{[k]}=I_{1}^{[k]}I_{2}^{[k]}
, and moreover
I_{1}^{[k]}\subset S_{1}
and
I_{2}^{[k]}\subset S_{2}
. By
[
16
, Corollary 3.2]
,

\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S/(I_{1}I_{2})^{[k]})=\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S_{1}/I_{1}^{[k]})+\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S_{2}/I_{2}^{[k]})-1.

Then

n+m-\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S/(I_{1}I_{2})^{[k]})=n-\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S_{1}/I_{1}^{[k]})+m-\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S_{2}/I_{2}^{[k]})+1.

Let
d_{k}=\textup{indeg}(I_{1}^{[k]})
,
\delta_{k}=\textup{indeg}(I_{2}^{[k]})
. Then
\textup{indeg}((I_{1}I_{2})^{[k]})=d_{k}+\delta_{k}
. Therefore, by the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula

\textup{depth}(S/(I_{1}I_{2})^{[k]})-(d_{k}+\delta_{k}-1)=\textup{depth}(S_{1}/I_{1}^{[k]})+\textup{depth}(S_{2}/I_{2}^{[k]})+1-(d_{k}+\delta_{k}-1),

and hence
g_{I_{1}I_{2}}(k)=g_{I_{1}}(k)+g_{I_{2}}(k)
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of the previous theorem, suppose
g_{I_{1}}
and
g_{I_{2}}
are non-increasing functions, then
g_{I_{1}I_{2}}
is a non-increasing function too.

Let
I\subset S^{\prime}=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a squarefree monomial ideal. Now we examine the relationship between
g_{I}
and
g_{J}
, where
J=(I,x)\subset S=S^{\prime}[x]=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x]
.

For the proof of the next result we recall the concept of
Betti splitting

[
9
]
.
Let
I
,
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
be monomial ideals of
S
such that
G(I)
is the disjoint union of
G(I_{1})
and
G(I_{2})
. We say that
I=I_{1}+I_{2}
is a
Betti splitting
if

\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(I_{1})+\beta_{i,j}(I_{2})+\beta_{i-1,j}(I_{1}\cap I_{2})\ \ \ \textup{for all}\ i,j.

(1)

In particular, by
[
9
, Corollary 2.2(a)]
,

\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(I)=\max\big\{\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(I_{1}),\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(I_{2}),\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(I_{1}\cap I_{2})+1\big\}.

(2)

The following criterion is due to Bolognini.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
Let
I
,
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
be monomial ideals of
S
such that
G(I)
is the disjoint union of
G(I_{1})
and
G(I_{2})
. Suppose that
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are componentwise linear. Then
I=I_{1}+I_{2}
is a Betti splitting.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
S^{\prime}=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
S=S^{\prime}[x]=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x]
be polynomial rings and let
I\subset S^{\prime}
be a squarefree monomial ideal all of whose squarefree powers are componentwise linear. Let
J=(I,x)
and
d_{k}=\textup{indeg}(I^{[k]})
for
1\leq k\leq\nu(I)
. Furthermore, set
g_{I}(0)=g_{I}(\nu(I)+1)=+\infty
and
d_{0}=0
. Then
\nu(J)=\nu(I)+1
and for all
1\leq k\leq\nu(J)
,

g_{J}(k)=\min\{g_{I}(k)+d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1,g_{I}(k-1)\}.

(3)

Proof.

Firstly we verify our formula in the cases
k=1
and
k=\nu(J)
.

When
k=1
, then
J^{[1]}=J=I+(x)
and
\textup{depth}(S/J)=\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I)
. Since
\textup{indeg}(J)=\min\{d_{1},1\}=1
, we get that

g_{J}(1)=\textup{depth}(S/J)=\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I)=\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I)-(d_{1}-1)+(d_{1}-1)=g_{I}(1)+(d_{1}-1).

This agrees with formula (
3
), since
d_{0}=0
and
g_{I}(0)=+\infty
.

When
k=\nu(J)
, then
J^{[\nu(J)]}=xI^{[\nu(I)]}
. In this case,
\textup{indeg}(J^{[\nu(J)]})=\textup{indeg}(I^{\nu(I)})+1=d_{\nu(I)}+1
and
\textup{depth}(S/J^{[\nu(J)]})=\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I^{[\nu(I)]})+1
. Hence

g_{J}(\nu(J))=\textup{depth}(S/J^{[\nu(J)]})-d_{\nu(I)}=\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I^{[\nu(I)]})+1-d_{\nu(I)}=g_{I}(\nu(I)),

and since
g_{I}(\nu(I)+1)=+\infty
, this agrees with (
3
).

Now let
1<k<\nu(J)
. Note that
J^{[k]}=I^{[k]}+xI^{[k-1]}
. By our hypothesis both ideals
I^{[k]}
and
I^{[k-1]}
are componentwise linear. Thus
xI^{[k-1]}
is componentwise linear too, and by Proposition
2.3
,
J^{[k]}=I^{[k]}+xI^{[k-1]}
is a Betti splitting. Hence by equation (
2
)

\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(J^{[k]})=\max\big\{\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(I^{[k]}),\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(xI^{[k-1]}),\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(I^{[k]}\cap xI^{[k-1]})+1\big\}.

Note that
I^{[k]}\subset I^{[k-1]}
and since
x
does not divide any of the minimal generators of
I
, we obtain that
I^{[k]}\cap xI^{[k-1]}=xI^{[k]}
. Since
\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S/xI^{[k]})=\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k]})
, we have

\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S/J^{[k]})=\max\big\{\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k]})+1,\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k-1]})\big\}.

Applying the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula we get

\textup{depth}(S/J^{[k]})=\min\big\{\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k]}),\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k-1]})+1\big\}.

Note that

\textup{indeg}(J^{[k]})=\min\{\textup{indeg}(I^{[k]}),\textup{indeg}(I^{[k-1]})+1\}=\min\{d_{k},d_{k-1}+1\}=d_{k-1}+1

because
d_{k}>d_{k-1}
. Hence

\displaystyle g_{J}(k)\

\displaystyle=\ \textup{depth}(S/J^{[k]})-d_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\ \min\{\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k]})-d_{k-1},\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k-1]})-d_{k-1}+1\}

\displaystyle=\ \min\{\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k]})-(d_{k}-1)+d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1,\textup{depth}(S^{\prime}/I^{[k-1]})-(d_{k-1}-1)\}

\displaystyle=\ \min\{g_{I}(k)+d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1,g_{I}(k-1)\},

as desired.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

With the assumptions and notation of the previous proposition, it follows that
g_{J}
is a non-increasing function if
g_{I}
is non-increasing.

Proof.

By hypothesis
g_{I}(k+1)-g_{I}(k)\leq 0
for all
k=1,\dots,\nu(I)-1
. We shall prove that
g_{J}(k+1)-g_{J}(k)\leq 0
for all
k=1,\dots,\nu(I)
. Recall that
\nu(J)=\nu(I)+1
.

Let
k=1
. From Proposition
2.4
we have
g_{J}(2)=\min\{g_{I}(2)+d_{2}-d_{1}-1,g_{I}(1)\}
and
g_{J}(1)=g_{I}(1)+(d_{1}-1)
. If
g_{J}(2)=g_{I}(1)
, then
g_{J}(2)-g_{J}(1)=-(d_{1}-1)\leq 0
. Otherwise, if
g_{J}(2)=g_{I}(2)+d_{2}-d_{1}-1
, then
g_{I}(1)\geq g_{I}(2)+d_{2}-d_{1}-1
and

\displaystyle g_{J}(2)-g_{J}(1)\

\displaystyle=\ g_{I}(2)+d_{2}-d_{1}-1-(g_{I}(1)+(d_{1}-1))

\displaystyle=\ g_{I}(2)+d_{2}-2d_{1}-g_{I}(1)

\displaystyle\leq\ g_{I}(2)+d_{2}-2d_{1}-(g_{I}(2)+d_{2}-d_{1}-1)

\displaystyle=\ 1-d_{1}\leq 0,

since
d_{1}\geq 1
.

Let
k\in\{2,\dots,\nu(I)-1\}
. From Proposition
2.4
we have

\displaystyle g_{J}(k+1)\

\displaystyle=\ \min\{g_{I}(k+1)+d_{k+1}-d_{k}-1,g_{I}(k)\},

\displaystyle g_{J}(k)\

\displaystyle=\ \min\{g_{I}(k)+d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1,g_{I}(k-1)\}.

We distinguish the four possible cases.

Case 1.
Assume
g_{J}(k+1)=g_{I}(k+1)+d_{k+1}-d_{k}-1
and
g_{J}(k)=g_{I}(k)+d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1
. Then
g_{J}(k+1)\leq g_{I}(k)
and

\displaystyle g_{J}(k+1)-g_{J}(k)\

\displaystyle=\ g_{J}(k+1)-g_{I}(k)-(d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\ g_{I}(k)-g_{I}(k)-(d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1)

\displaystyle=\ -(d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1)\leq 0

because
d_{k}\geq d_{k-1}+1
.

Case 2.
Assume
g_{J}(k+1)=g_{I}(k+1)+d_{k+1}-d_{k}-1
and
g_{J}(k)=g_{I}(k-1)
. Then
g_{J}(k+1)\leq g_{I}(k)
and
g_{J}(k+1)-g_{J}(k)\leq g_{I}(k)-g_{I}(k-1)\leq 0
by our hypothesis.

Case 3.
Assume
g_{J}(k+1)=g_{I}(k)
and
g_{J}(k)=g_{I}(k)+d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1
. Then we have
g_{J}(k+1)-g_{J}(k)=-(d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1)\leq 0
as observed before.

Case 4.
Assume
g_{J}(k+1)=g_{I}(k)
and
g_{J}(k)=g_{I}(k-1)
. Then
g_{J}(k+1)-g_{J}(k)=g_{I}(k)-g_{I}(k-1)\leq 0
by our hypothesis.

Finally, let
k=\nu(I)
, then
g_{J}(k+1)=g_{J}(\nu(J))=g_{I}(\nu(I))=g_{I}(k)
. Whereas,
g_{J}(k)=\min\{g_{I}(k)+d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1,g_{I}(k-1)\}
. If
g_{J}(k)=g_{I}(k)+d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1
, then
g_{J}(k+1)-g_{J}(k)=-(d_{k}-d_{k-1}-1)\leq 0
, as noted before. Otherwise, if
g_{J}(k)=g_{I}(k-1)
, then
g_{J}(k+1)-g_{J}(k)=g_{I}(k)-g_{I}(k-1)\leq 0
, because by our hypothesis
g_{I}
is a non-increasing function. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

In Proposition
2.4
we assumed that all squarefree powers of
I
are componentwise linear in order to guarantee that for all
2\leq k\leq\nu(I)
,
J^{[k]}=I^{[k]}+xI^{[k-1]}
is a Betti splitting. However this hypothesis is not required because it was proved in the meanwhile that
J^{[k]}=I^{[k]}+xI^{[k-1]}
is always a Betti splitting
[
5
, Lemma 1.4]
.

3. The normalized depth function of cochordal graphs

In this section we examine the normalized depth function of cochordal graphs.

Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)=[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
and edge set
E(G)
. We always assume that
G
has no isolated vertices. Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
. The
edge ideal

I(G)
, associated to
G
, is the ideal of
S
generated by the set of all monomials
x_{i}x_{j}
,
i\neq j
, for which
i
is
adjacent
to
j
,
i.e.
,
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
.

The graph
G
is called
connected
if for any
i
and
j
there is a
path
between
i
and
j
, that is, a sequence of vertices
i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{p}
such that
i_{0}=i
,
i_{p}=j
and

\ell=s-1
. Set
y_{i}=x_{n+2i-1}x_{n+2i}
,
i=1,\dots,\ell
. Let
G_{0}
be any graph on vertex set
[n]
,
n
big enough, whose complementary graph satisfies condition (iii) of Theorem
3.1
, and with matching number
\nu(I(G_{0}))=m-\ell
. Let
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}]
and
J=(I(G_{0}),y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{\ell})
. Then
\nu(J)=m
. We claim that
J^{[k]}
has a linear resolution,
k=1,\dots,m
. For
\ell=1
,
J^{[k]}=I(G_{0})^{[k]}+y_{1}I(G_{0})^{[k-1]}
is a Betti splitting, because
I(G_{0})^{[k]}
,
y_{1}I(G_{0})^{[k-1]}
have linear resolutions. Note that
I(G_{0})^{[k]}\cap y_{1}I(G_{0})^{[k-1]}=y_{1}I(G_{0})^{[k]}
has again a linear resolution and it is equigenerated in degree
2k+1
. Since
J^{[k]}
is equigenerated in degree
2k
, applying formula (
1
) in our situation, we see that
J^{[k]}
has again a linear resolution. For
\ell>1
, we set
L=(I(G_{0}),y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell-1})
. Then
J=(L,y_{\ell})
. By induction
L^{[k]}
has a linear resolution,
k=1,\dots,\nu(L)
. Thus repeating the same argument as in the case
\ell=1
, it follows that
J^{[k]}
has a linear resolution, for all
k=1,\dots,\nu(J)
.

Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1},\dots,x_{n+2\ell}]
. Let
G
be the graph on vertex set
[n+2\ell]
,
\ell=s-1
, whose edge set is

E(G_{0})\cup\big\{\{n+1,n+2\},\{n+3,n+4\},\dots,\{n+2s-3,n+2s-2\}\big\}.

Note that
\nu(I(G))=m
. We claim that
g_{I(G)}(k)=s-(k-1)
for
k=1,\dots,s
and
g_{I(G)}(k)=0
for
k=s+1,\dots,m
. This will conclude our proof.

For
s=2
, our claim follows from Lemma
3.11
.
Let
s>2
,
L=(I(G_{0}),y_{1},\dots,y_{s-2})
and
G^{\prime}=G-\{n+2s-3,n+2s-2\}
. Note that
\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S/I(G)^{[k]})=\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(R/J^{[k]})
. Since
J^{[k]}=L^{[k]}+y_{\ell}L^{[k-1]}
is a Betti splitting, formula (
2
) yields

\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(R/J^{[k]})=\max\big\{\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(R/L^{[k]})+1,\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(R/L^{[k-1]})\big\}.

Thus

\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S/I(G)^{[k]})=\max\big\{\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S/I(G^{\prime})^{[k]})+1,\textup{proj}\phantom{.}\!\textup{dim}(S/I(G^{\prime})^{[k-1]})\big\}.

Arguing as in Proposition
2.4
, we have

g_{I(G)}(k)=\min\big\{g_{I(G^{\prime})}(k)+1,g_{I(G^{\prime})}(k-1)\big\}.

By induction on
s
, we may assume that
g_{I(G^{\prime})}(k)=(s-1)-(k-1)
for
k=1,\dots,s
and
g_{I(G^{\prime})}(k)=0
for
k=s,\dots,m-1
. An easy calculation shows that
g_{I(G)}(k)=s-(k-1)
for
k=1,\dots,s
and
g_{I(G)}(k)=0
for
k=s+1,\dots,m
, as desired.
∎

4. Construction of non-increasing normalized depth functions

We conclude this article with the following result which shows that any non-increasing function can be the normalized depth function of a suitable squarefree monomial ideal.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\dots\geq a_{m}
be a non-increasing sequence of non-negative integers. Then, there exists a squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
n
large enough, such that
\nu(I)=m
and
g_{I}(k)=a_{k}
for
k=1,\dots,m
.

For the proof of this result we need the following lemmata.

Lemma 4.2
.

Given positive integers
s<m
, there exists a squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
n
large enough, such that
\nu(I)=m
,
g_{I}(k)=1
for
k=1,\dots,s
and
g_{I}(k)=0
for
k=s+1,\dots,m
.

Proof.

Let
G
be any graph on
[n]
,
n
large enough, with matching number
\nu(G)=m-s
such that
G^{c}
is disconnected and chordal. Let
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1},\dots,x_{n+s}]
and
I=(I(G),x_{n+1},\dots,x_{n+s})
. We claim that
\nu(I)=m
,
g_{I}(k)=1
for
k=1,\dots,s
and
g_{I}(k)=0
for
k=s+1,\dots,m
. This will conclude our proof.

By
[
8
, Corollary 2.6]
,
g_{I(G)}(k)=0
for
k=1,\dots,m-s
. We prove our statement by induction on
s
. For
s=1
, since
\textup{indeg}(I(G))=2
and
I(G)^{[k]}
has a linear resolution for all
k=1,\dots,m-s
, by Proposition
2.4
we get that
\nu((I(G),x_{n+1}))=(m-s)+1
,
g_{(I(G),x_{n+1})}(1)=1
and
g_{(I(G),x_{n+1})}(k)=0
for
k=2,\dots,(m-s)+1
. Suppose our claim is true up to
s-1
and set
L=(I(G),x_{n+1},\dots,x_{n+(s-1)})
. Then
L^{[k]}
has a linear resolution, for
k=1,\dots,m-1
, and
I=(L,x_{n+s})
. Moreover,
\textup{indeg}(L)=1
,
\nu(L)=(m-s)+(s-1)=m-1
,
g_{L}(k)=1
for
k=1,\dots,s-1
and
g_{L}(k)=0
for
k=s,\dots,m-1
. Applying again Proposition
2.4
, our statement follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Given a positive integer
m
, there exists a squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
n
large enough, such that
\nu(I)=m
and
g_{I}(k)=1
for
k=1,\dots,m
.

Proof.

By the previous lemma, we can find
L\subset K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
n
large enough, such that
\nu(L)=m+1
,
g_{L}(k)=1
for
k=1,\dots,m
,
g_{L}(m+1)=0
. Let
G
be any graph on
[p]
with matching
\nu(G)=m
such that
G^{c}
is disconnected. Let
I(G)=(y_{i}y_{j}:\{i,j\}\in E(G))
be its edge ideal and
S=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{p}]
. We claim that
I=L\cdot I(G)
verifies
\nu(I)=m
and
g_{I}(k)=1
for
k=1,\dots,m
. This follows at once by Theorem
2.1
and
[
8
, Corollary 2.6]
.
∎

Finally, we are in the position to prove our last result.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Any vector
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m})
, with
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\dots\geq a_{m}\geq 0
integers, can be written uniquely as a sum of vectors of type
(1,1,\dots,1,0,0,\dots,0)
and type
(1,1,\dots,1)
. Combining Theorem
2.1
with Lemmata
4.2
,
4.3
, the result follows.
∎

Transit cosmological models with observational constraints in f ⁡ ( Q , T ) f(Q,T) gravity


Abstract
This cosmological model is a study of modified f ⁡ ( Q , T ) f(Q,T) theory of gravity which was recently proposed by Xu et al. (Eur. Phys. J. C 79 , 708 (2019)). In this theory of gravity, the action contains an arbitrary function f ⁡ ( Q , T ) f(Q,T) where Q Q is non-metricity and T T is the trace of energy-momentum tensor for matter fluid. In our research, we have taken the function f ⁡ ( Q , T ) f(Q,T) quadratic in Q Q and linear in T T as f ⁡ ( Q , T ) = α ​ Q + β ​ Q 2 + γ ​ T f(Q,T)=\alpha Q+\beta Q^{2}+\gamma T where α \alpha , β \beta and γ \gamma are model parameters, motivated by f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity. We have obtained the various cosmological parameters in Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-walker (FLRW) Universe viz. Hubble parameter H H , deceleration parameter q q etc. in terms of scale-factor as well as in terms of redshift z z by constraining energy-conservation law. For observational constraints on the model, we have obtained the best-fit values of model parameters using the available data sets like Hubble data sets H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) , Joint Light Curve Analysis (JLA) data sets and union 2.1 2.1 compilation of SNe Ia data sets by applying R 2 R^{2} -test formula. We have calculated the present values of various observational parameters viz. H 0 H_{0} , q 0 q_{0} , t 0 t_{0} and statefinder parameters ( s , r ) (s,r) , these values are very close to the standard cosmological models. Also, we have observed that the deceleration parameter q ⁡ ( z ) q(z) shows signature-flipping (transition) point within the range 0.423 ≤ z t ≤ 0.668 0.423\leq z_{t}\leq 0.668 through which it changes its phase from decelerated to accelerated expanding universe with equation of state (EoS) − 1.071 ≤ ω − 0.96 -1.071\leq\omega-0.96 for 0 ≤ z ≤ 3 0\leq z\leq 3 .

2
Some Preliminary definitions and Cosmological Field Equations

The Einstein-Hilbert action for
f(Q,T)
gravity is defined in
[
21
]
as

I=\int{\left(\frac{1}{16\pi}f(Q,T)+L_{m}\right)d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}}

(1)

where
f(Q,T)
is an arbitrary function of the non-metricity
Q
and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor
T
, and
L_{m}
is the Lagrangian for
matter source and
g=\text{det}(g_{ij})
read as the determinant of the metric tensor
g_{ij}
and

Q\equiv-g^{ij}(L^{\alpha}_{\beta i}L^{\beta}_{j\alpha}-L^{\alpha}_{\beta\alpha}L^{\beta}_{ij})

(2)

where
L^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}
is known as deformation tensor defined by

L^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\lambda}(\nabla_{\gamma}g_{\beta\lambda}+\nabla_{\beta}g_{\lambda\gamma}-\nabla_{\lambda}g_{\beta\gamma})

(3)

The non-metricity
Q
and the energy-momentum tensor are defined respectively as

Q_{\alpha}={Q_{\alpha}^{i}}_{i},~~~~T_{ij}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta(\sqrt{-g}L_{m})}{\delta g^{ij}}

(4)

and

\theta_{ij}=g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{ij}}

(5)

By varying the action
(1)
with respect to the metric components, we obtain the following field equations:

-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\nabla_{\alpha}(f_{Q}\sqrt{-g}P^{\alpha}_{ij})-\frac{1}{2}fg_{ij}+f_{T}(T_{ij}+\theta_{ij})-f_{Q}(P_{i\alpha\beta}Q_{j}^{\alpha\beta}-2{Q^{\alpha\beta}}_{i}P_{\alpha\beta j})=8\pi T_{ij}

(6)

where
P^{\alpha}_{ij}
is defined as the supper-potential of the model mentioned as in
[
21
]
.

Now, let us suppose the universe is isotropic and spatially flat and hence, it can be described by an FLRW metric given by

ds^{2}=-N^{2}(t)dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2})

(7)

where
a(t)
is called scale factor which describes evolution of the universe. The increasing value of
a(t)
reveals the expansion and decreasing
value indicates the collapse of the universe and its rate is measured by Hubble function defined as
H=\frac{\dot{a}}{a}
. The non-metricity
Q
for
FLRW metric is derived as
Q=6H^{2}
for lapse function
N(t)=1

[
21
]
. Let us suppose that our universe is filled with perfect fluid and hence,
the energy momentum tensor for perfect fluid is defined by
T_{ij}=(\rho+p)u_{i}u_{j}+pg_{ij}
and therefore, the
\theta_{ij}
is defined and obtained as

\theta^{i}_{j}=\delta^{i}_{j}p-2T^{i}_{j}=\text{diag}(2\rho+p,-p,-p,-p)

(8)

Let us introduce the following notations
F\equiv f_{Q}
and
8\pi\tilde{G}\equiv f_{T}
for simplicity of field equations. For the FLRW metric
(7)
,
the derived field equation
(6)
gives the following equations:

8\pi\rho=\frac{f}{2}-6FH^{2}-\frac{2\tilde{G}}{1+\tilde{G}}(\dot{F}H+F\dot{H})

(9)

8\pi p=-\frac{f}{2}+6FH^{2}+2(\dot{F}H+F\dot{H})

(10)

To obtain the evolution function for Hubble parameter, we have combined the Eqs.
(9)
and
(10)
as

\dot{H}+\frac{\dot{F}}{F}H=\frac{4\pi}{F}(1+\tilde{G})(\rho+p)

(11)

In generalized
f(Q,T)
gravity the energy-conservation law is derived in
[
21
]
as

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=\frac{\tilde{G}}{16\pi(1+\tilde{G})(1+2\tilde{G})}\left[\dot{S}-\frac{(3\tilde{G}+2)\dot{\tilde{G}}}{(1+\tilde{G})\tilde{G}}S+6HS\right]

(12)

where
S=2(\dot{F}H+F\dot{H})
.

3
Cosmological Solutions of the Field Equations

Let us choose such a solution of the field equations
(9)
&
(10)
so that
\tilde{G}\neq 0
and

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=0

(13)

Therefore, the Eq.
(12)
becomes

\dot{S}-\frac{(3\tilde{G}+2)\dot{\tilde{G}}}{(1+\tilde{G})\tilde{G}}S+6HS=0

(14)

Here, we have taken
f(Q,T)=\alpha Q+\beta Q^{2}+\gamma T
which is obtained by replacing
R
by
Q
in
f(R,T)
-gravity in which some authors
have studied the form
f(R,T)=R+\alpha R^{2}+\beta T
with
R
as Ricci scalar curvature and
T
is the trace of energy momentum tensor
T_{ij}
and here,
factor
R^{2}
is added to explain the late time acceleration in expanding universe and this study motivated us to investigate the above form of
f(Q,T)
.
Now, we obtain
F=f_{Q}=\alpha+2\beta Q
and
8\pi\tilde{G}=f_{T}=\gamma
and also,
\dot{\tilde{G}}=0
and using this in Eq.
(14)
, we get

\dot{S}+6HS=0

(15)

Integrating Eq.
(15)
, we obtain the Hubble parameter
H
as

H=\frac{1}{6}\left[\frac{1}{\beta}\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\frac{12\beta k}{a^{6}}}-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(16)

where
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
are model parameters and
k
is an integrating constant.

The various observational data sets are available in terms of redshift
z
and it can be easily compared with theoretical studies in terms of redshift.
Hence, the various parameters need to derive in terms of redshift
z
and hence, we are used the relationship between the scale-factor and redshift give as

\frac{a_{0}}{a}=1+z

(17)

where
a_{0}
is the present value of scale factor and as a standard convention, we will take
a_{0}=1
throughout the study. Using Eq. (
17
) in (
16
),
we get

H(z)=\frac{1}{6}\left[\frac{1}{\beta}\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+12\beta k(1+z)^{6}}-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(18)

Now, the deceleration parameter
q(t)
is defined as
q(t)=-1-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}
and calculated as

q(t)=-1+\frac{18\beta k}{a^{6}[\alpha^{2}+\frac{12\beta k}{a^{6}}-\alpha\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\frac{12\beta k}{a^{6}}}]}

(19)

This in terms of redshift obtained as

q(z)=-1+\frac{18\beta k(1+z)^{6}}{[\alpha^{2}+12\beta k(1+z)^{6}-\alpha\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+12\beta k(1+z)^{6}}]}

(20)

Now, from Eqs.
(9)
&
(10)
we have calculated the energy density
\rho
and isotropic pressure
p
respectively as

\rho=-\frac{3\alpha}{2(4\pi+\gamma)}H^{2}-\frac{27\beta}{(4\pi+\gamma)}H^{4}-\frac{\gamma k}{4(4\pi+\gamma)(8\pi+\gamma)a^{6}}

(21)

p=\frac{\alpha}{2(4\pi+\gamma)}H^{2}+\frac{27\beta}{4\pi+\gamma}H^{4}-\frac{k(16\pi+\gamma)}{4(4\pi+\gamma)(8\pi+\gamma)a^{6}}

(22)

From Eq.
(16)
one can see that for
\alpha=0
, we can find
H\propto\frac{1}{a^{3/2}}
,
H^{2}\propto\frac{1}{a^{3}}
and
H^{4}\propto\frac{1}{a^{6}}
and
hence,
\rho\propto\frac{1}{a^{6}}
which shows the existence of stiff matter in the universe. And in late time universe it converted into ordinary matter
and dark energy.

1.
Introduction

A commutative Banach algebra is a Banach space
(X,\,\|\,\cdot\,\|_{X})

which is a commutative algebra, i.e., with an
associative, distributive and commutative multiplication
satisfying
\|fg\|_{X}\leq\|f\|_{X}\|g\|_{X}

for all
f,g\in X
.
There are many examples of commutative Banach algebras.
For this paper the Fourier algebra
A({\mathbf{R}})
is an important
example, i.e., the set of all functions on
{\mathbf{R}}

which are the Fourier transforms of functions
in
L^{1}({\mathbf{R}})
, is a commutative
Banach algebra with pointwise multiplication, denoted
by
\mathcal{F}L^{1}(\mathbf{R})
elsewhere.
In addition, the Wiener algebra
A({\mathbf{T}})
,
which corresponds to a periodic version
of
A({\mathbf{R}})
, and
the Fourier-Beurling algebra

{\mathcal{F}}L_{s}^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(s\geq 0)
,
which can be regarded as a generalization of
A({\mathbf{R}})
,
are commutative Banach algebras
(see Section
2.3
for the definition of

{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
).
As is well known, those function spaces play important roles
in various fields such as commutative Banach algebras,
operating functions, and spectral synthesis.
We refer the reader to Kahane
[
19
]
,
Katznelson
[
20
]
, Reiter
[
30
]
,
Reiter-Stegeman
[
31
]

and Rudin
[
32
]

for more details.

On the other hand, modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

form a family of function spaces
introduced by Feichtinger
[
9
]

(see Definition
2.1
).
In some sense, they behave like the Besov spaces
B^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
But they appear to be better suited for the description of
problems in the area of time-frequency analysis
and are often a good substitute for the usual
spaces
L^{p}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
or
B^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(see
[
14
]
,
[
17
]

for more details).
It is important for our considerations that
M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

is a commutative Banach algebra
if
s>n(1-\frac{1}{q})
, or
q=1
and
s\geq 0

(cf.
[
13
]
, Theorem 10),
and that it appears naturally in the study
of certain partial differential equations.
For example, we can solve the nonlinear Schrödinger equations

iu_{t}+\Delta u=|u|^{2k}u

in
M^{2,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
for all

k\in{\mathbf{N}}
by using
the algebraic properties of
M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(see
[
1
]
,
[
37
]
).
Furthermore, as a commutative Banach algebra,

M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

satisfies many interesting properties similar to

A({\mathbf{R}})
,
A({\mathbf{T}})
and also

{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(cf.
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,

[
21
]
,

[
22
]
,
[
23
]
,

[
29
]
).
Therefore, it is of interest to further clarify the
properties of
M_{s}^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

In this paper we study several properties of
M_{s}^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
as a commutative Banach algebra. In particular, we will establish the validity of
the Wiener-Lévy theorem for
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
and see that the sets of spectral synthesis for

{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

coincide with those for
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
independently from
p
(given the general restrictions).
Moreover, we will consider the inclusion relation between
the modulation space
M^{p,1}_{0}({\mathbf{R}})
and the
Fourier Segal algebra
{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
.

The organization of this paper is as follows. After a preliminary section devoted to the definition and basic properties of

In particular we will show

The
Fourier transform
of

is defined by

{\mathcal{F}}f(\xi)=f^{\wedge}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx.

Similarly, if

then the inverse Fourier transform of

is defined by

{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}h(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\widehat{h}(-x)
.

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}=(2\pi)^{-n}\langle\widehat{f},\widehat{g}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}

and

,

Moreover,

M({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
denotes the sets of

\mu

.
Here

2.1.
Short-time Fourier transform

For
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
the short-time Fourier transform

V_{\phi}
of
f
with respect to the window

\phi
is defined by the duality expression

V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)=\langle f,M_{\xi}T_{x}\phi\rangle=\langle f(t),\phi(t-x)e^{it\xi}\rangle=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}f(t)\overline{\phi(t-x)}e^{-it\xi}dt,

where the translation operator
T_{x}
and the modulation operator
M_{\xi}

are defined by
(T_{x}h)(t)=h(t-x)
and
(M_{\xi}h)(t)=e^{it\xi}h(t)

It is known that

(see
[
17
, Lemma 11.2.3]
)
and

(1)

\displaystyle V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}e^{-ix\xi}V_{\widehat{\phi}}\widehat{f}(\xi,-x)=(2\pi)^{-n}e^{-ix\xi}(f*M_{\xi}\phi^{*})(x),

where
\phi^{*}(x)=\overline{\phi(-x)}

(see
[
17
, Lemma 3.1.1]
).
We also note that
if

then

(see
[
17
, Theorem 11.2.5]
).
Moreover, we have Moyals Equation:

\langle V_{\phi}f,V_{\psi}g\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})}=(2\pi)^{n}\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}

for all

(see
[
17
, Theorem 3.2.1]
).

2.2.
Modulation spaces

Definition 2.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,

and
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\setminus\{0\}

The modulation space
M_{s}^{p,q}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})=M_{s}^{p,q}

consists of all
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that the norm

\|f\|_{M_{s}^{p,q}}=\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{sq}\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}|V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)|^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}d\xi\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

p=\infty
or
q=\infty
).
We simply write
M^{p,q}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

instead of
M^{p,q}_{0}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

We collect basic properties of
M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

in the following lemma:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p,p_{1},p_{2},q,q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
and
s,s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathbf{R}}
.
Then

(i)

The space
M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

is a Banach space, and different windows define equivalent norms.

(ii)

(
Density and duality
)

If
p,q<\infty
, then
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

is dense in

and

(M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}))^{\prime}=M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(iii)

If
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
,
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
and

s_{1}\geq s_{2}
, then
M^{p_{1},q_{1}}_{s_{1}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow M^{p_{2},q_{2}}_{s_{2}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

(iv)

If
s>\frac{n}{q^{\prime}}
, or
q=1
and
s\geq 0
, then

M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\subset C({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and

M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

are multiplication algebra, i.e., we have

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}\leq c\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}\|g\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}},\quad f,g\in M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(v)

Remark 2.4

Let

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1\leq p,q<\infty
.
Suppose that

s>{n}/{q^{\prime}}
,
or
q=1
and
s\geq 0
.
Then we have

\|fg\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\infty,q}_{s}}\|g\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}},\quad f,g\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}).

If

f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and
g\in M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
then
fg\in M^{1,\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})(\hookrightarrow M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}))
.

Proof..

We start the proof by observing that the pointwise relationships
for modulation spaces correspond to convolution relations for
their (inverse) Fourier transforms
in
W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{q}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

The claim made is thus equivalent to the statement that (writing

f,g
for
\widehat{f}
,
\widehat{g}
respectively):

(2)

\|f\ast g\|_{W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{q}_{s})}\lesssim\|f\|_{W({\mathcal{F}}L^{\infty},\ell^{q}_{s})}\|g\|_{W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{q}_{s})}

which results by the coordinate-wise convolution result established in

[
12
]
and the fact that

{\mathcal{F}}L^{p}\ast{\mathcal{F}}L^{\infty}\subset{\mathcal{F}}L^{p}\quad\mbox{because}\quad L^{p}\cdot L^{\infty}\subseteq L^{p},

as well as the fact that
(\ell^{q}_{s}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n}),\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{q}_{s}})

is a Banach algebra with respect to the natural convolution, because the weight
function
x\mapsto\langle x\rangle^{s}
is weakly subadditive for any

s\geq 0
and consequently
\ell^{1}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})\cap\ell^{q}_{s}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})
is a Banach
algebra with respect to convolution. This observation have been
made explicit in
[
4
]
. But for
s>\frac{n}{q^{\prime}}
, it follows
from the Hölder inequality that

\ell^{q}_{s}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\ell^{1}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})
(with corresponding
continuous embedding), and thus

(\ell^{q}_{s}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n}),\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{q}_{s}})
is Banach algebra
with respect to (discrete) convolution.

In a similar way we use the local convolution relation

{\mathcal{F}}L^{p}\ast{\mathcal{F}}L^{p^{\prime}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}L^{\infty}\quad\mbox{due to the relation}\quad L^{p}\cdot L^{p^{\prime}}\subseteq L^{\infty},

combined with the global condition

\ell^{p}_{s}\ast\ell^{\,p^{\prime}}_{-s}\subseteq\ell^{\infty}_{-s}

using the submultiplicativity condition (writing
x=(x-y)+y
):

\langle{x+y}\rangle^{-s}\leq\langle x\rangle^{-s}\langle y\rangle^{s}.

∎

Let us also observe another consequence of the reasoning used in the

multiplier algebra of Sobolev algebras. We formulate it as another
lemma. Let us shortly recall that the classical Sobolev spaces

(H^{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}})

so that one has
\ell^{2}_{s}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\ell^{1}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})
for

via
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and consequently by the Hausdorff-Young
version for Wiener amalgams (see
[

]
) one has the following chain
of continuous embeddings:

H^{s}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(W(L^{2},\ell^{1}))\hookrightarrow W({\mathcal{F}}L^{1},\ell^{2})\hookrightarrow W(C_{0},\ell^{2}).

Lemma 2.6
.

For
s>{n}/{2}
we have
M^{\infty,2}_{s}\cdot H^{s}\subseteq H^{s}
, meaning
that
M^{\infty,2}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is a subset of the pointwise multiplier algebra of the Sobolev algebra

Proof..

We have already noted that
s>{n}/{2}
implies
H^{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow W(C_{0},\ell^{2})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

The following reasoning implies the classical fact that it is a

as Sobolev algebra).

We have to verify that the corresponding convolution relation
is valid on the Fourier transform side. In fact, we may invoke
the main result of
[
12
]
:

(3)

W({\mathcal{F}}L^{\infty},\ell^{2}_{s})*W({\mathcal{F}}L^{2},\ell^{2}_{s})\subset W(L^{2},\ell^{2}_{s}),

using again that
\ell^{2}_{s}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})=\ell^{2}_{s}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})\cap\ell^{1}({\mathbf{Z}}^{n})

is a Banach algebra with respect to convolution, and

{\mathcal{F}}L^{\infty}*{\mathcal{F}}L^{2}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}L^{2}=L^{2},\quad\mbox{due to the fact that}\quad L^{\infty}\cdot L^{2}\subseteq L^{2}.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For
1\leq p\leq 2
we have the chain of continuous embeddings

W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{p})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}L^{p}\hookrightarrow W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{p^{\prime}})

or by taking inverse Fourier transforms

M^{p,p}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{p}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow M^{p,p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}).

In particular, we have

M^{2,2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})=L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Proof..

We start with the observation that the classical Hausdorff-Young
estimate implies for
1\leq p\leq 2
, with
1/p^{\prime}+1/p=1
:

{\mathcal{F}}L^{p}\hookrightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}\quad\mbox{or equivalently}\quad L^{p}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}L^{p^{\prime}}

and consequently

W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{p})\hookrightarrow W(L^{p^{\prime}},\ell^{p})\hookrightarrow W(L^{p},\ell^{p})=L^{p},

which implies:

W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{p})={\mathcal{F}}(W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{p}))\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\quad\mbox{for}\,\,1\leq p\leq 2.

Thus the first continuous embeddings is verified.
For the second inclusion recall that

[
13
]
) (see above, due to the obvious fact that
p\leq 2\leq p^{\prime}
) implies

{\mathcal{F}}L^{p}={\mathcal{F}}(W(L^{p},\ell^{p}))\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}(W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p^{\prime}},\ell^{p}))\hookrightarrow_{HY}W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{p^{\prime}}).

∎

2.3.
Fourier-Beurling algebra

For
s\geq 0
the Fourier-Beurling algebra

{\mathcal{F}}L_{s}^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})={\mathcal{F}}L_{s}^{1}
is
the set of all

f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that the norm

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L_{s}^{1}}=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|d\xi

is finite.
It is well-known that
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is a multiplication algebra,
because Beurling algebras are Banach convolution
algebras (
[
31
]
). We also recall the following result
(e.g.,
[
31
, Proposition 1.6.14]
):

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
f\in{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f-\phi f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}<\varepsilon
.

For the inclusion relation between
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, we have the following
(cf.
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
).

Lemma 2.9
.

For
1\leq p\leq 2
and
s\geq 0
one has

M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Proof..

Let
f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

be such that

{\rm supp~}\varphi\subset[-1,1]^{n}

and
\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{n}}\varphi(\xi-k)=1
.
Since
{\rm supp~}\varphi(\cdot-k)\subset k+[-1,1]^{n}
,
it follows from
the Minkowski inequality for integral,
the Hölder inequality and
the Hausdorff-Young inequality that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{n}}\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}|\varphi(\cdot-k)\widehat{f}(\cdot)|\Big\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{n}}\langle k\rangle^{s}\|\varphi(\cdot-k)\widehat{f}(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{n}}\langle k\rangle^{s}\|\varphi(\cdot-k)\widehat{f}(\cdot)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{n}}\langle k\rangle^{s}\|\varphi(D-k)f\|_{L^{p}}=\|f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}},

which yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

For
s\geq 0
we define the space
({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
by

({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}=\{f\in{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})~|~{\rm supp~}f~{\rm is~compact}\}.

Then we have

({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}\hookrightarrow M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

for
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof..

Let
f\in{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and

{\rm supp~}f
be compact.
Then for
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
with

\phi(x)=1
on
{\rm supp~}f
, we have by Lemma
2.5

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}=\|f\phi\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{s}}\|\phi\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}.

Moreover, for
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

with
\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{n}}\varphi(\xi-k)=1
,
we have that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{s}}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{n}}\langle k\rangle^{s}\|\varphi(D-k)f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}^{n}}\langle k\rangle^{s}\|\varphi(\cdot-k)\widehat{f}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}},

which implies the desired result.
∎

3.
Approximate units

In this section, we prove the following result,
which corresponds to the
M^{p,q}_{s}
-version of
Bhimani-Ratnakumar
[
3
, Proposition 3.14]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
1\leq p,q<\infty

and
s>{n}/{q^{\prime}}
, or

q=1
and
s\geq 0
. Then for any
f\in M_{s}^{p,q}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists

\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f-\phi f\|_{M_{s}^{p,q}}<\varepsilon.

We remark that Bhimani

[
2
, Proposition 4.8]

considers the case
q=1
and
s=0
.

3.1.
Technical lemmas

To prove Theorem
3.1
we prepare a lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s\geq 0
and

\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
be such that

\psi(0)=1
. For
0<\lambda<1
,
we define
\psi_{\lambda}(x)=\psi(\lambda x)
.
Then, for any
g\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and

\varepsilon>0
, there exists
0<\lambda_{0}<1
such that

\|(1-\psi_{\lambda})g\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon\quad\forall 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}.

Proof..

Let
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\setminus\{0\}
.
We first note that

\displaystyle V_{\phi}((1-\psi_{\lambda})g)(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}e^{-ix\xi}V_{\widehat{\phi}}\big(\widehat{g}-(2\pi)^{-n}(\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}*\widehat{g})\big)(\xi,-x)

by (
1
) in Section
2.1
.
Moreover, since

\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}(\eta)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\widehat{\psi}(\frac{\eta}{\lambda})

and

1=\psi(0)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}(\eta)d\eta
,
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{g}(t)-(2\pi)^{-n}(\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}*\widehat{g})(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\lambda)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}(\widehat{g}(t)-\widehat{g}(t-\eta))\widehat{\psi}\Big(\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big)d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}(\widehat{g}\big(t)-(T_{\lambda\eta}\widehat{g})(t)\big)\widehat{\psi}(\eta)d\eta

and thus

V_{\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{g}-(2\pi)^{-n}(\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}*\widehat{g}))(\xi,-x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}V_{\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{g}-T_{\lambda\eta}\widehat{g})(\xi,-x)\widehat{\psi}(\eta)d\eta.

Therefore we obtain by the Fubini Theorem that

\displaystyle\|(1-\psi_{\lambda})g\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\approx\iint_{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}|V_{\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{g}-(2\pi)^{-n}(\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}*\widehat{g}))(\xi,-x)|dxd\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}V_{\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{g}-T_{\lambda\eta}\widehat{g})(\xi,-x)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n}_{(x,\xi)})}|\widehat{\psi}(\eta)|d\eta.

On the other hand, since
0<\lambda<1
,
s\geq 0
and

V_{\widehat{\phi}}(T_{\lambda\eta}\widehat{g})(\xi,-x)=e^{-i\lambda\eta(-x)}(V_{\widehat{\phi}}\widehat{g})(\xi-\lambda\eta,-x),

we have

\displaystyle\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}V_{\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{g}-T_{\lambda\eta}\widehat{g})(\xi,-x)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n}_{(x,\xi)})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\iint_{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}|V_{\widehat{\phi}}\widehat{g}(\xi,-x)|dxd\xi+\langle\lambda\eta\rangle^{s}\iint_{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}\langle\xi-\lambda\eta\rangle^{s}|V_{\widehat{\phi}}\widehat{g}(\xi-\lambda\eta,-x)|dxd\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle\eta\rangle^{s}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}V_{\widehat{\phi}}\widehat{g}(\xi,-x)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n}_{(x,\xi)})}.

We note that since
\widehat{\phi},\widehat{g}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
we have

V_{\widehat{\phi}}\widehat{g}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
,
and also

\displaystyle V_{\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{g}-T_{\lambda\eta}\widehat{g})(\xi,-x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}(\widehat{g}(t)-\widehat{g}(t-\lambda\eta))\overline{\widehat{\phi}(t-\xi)}e^{ixt}dt\to 0\quad(\lambda\to 0)

for all
\eta,\xi,x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
Thus it follows from
the Lebesgue convergence theorem that

\displaystyle\|(1-\psi_{\lambda})g\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}\lesssim\iiint_{{\mathbf{R}}^{3n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}|V_{\widehat{\phi}}(\widehat{g}-T_{\lambda\eta}\widehat{g})(\xi,-x)||\widehat{\psi}(\eta)|dxd\xi d\eta\to 0

as
\lambda\to 0
,
which implies the desired result.

∎

3.2.
The proof of Theorem
3.1

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
Since
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is dense in
M_{s}^{p,q}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
there exists
g\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f-g\|_{M_{s}^{p,q}}<\varepsilon
.
Moreover by Lemma
3.2
,
there exist
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and

such that

for all

,

(v)

that

4.
Closed ideals in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

In this section, we consider the closed ideals
in
M_{s}^{p,1}({\bf R}^{n})
,
which play an important role in
Section
6
.
Throughout this section,
X
stands for
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\geq 0
)
or
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(s\geq 0)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
I
be a linear subspace of
X
.
Then
I
is called an
ideal
in
X

if
fg\in I
whenever
f\in X
and
g\in I
.
Moreover, if an ideal
I
in
X
is a closed subset of

X
, then
I
is called a
closed ideal
in
X
.
For a subset
S
of
X
,
the set
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}I_{\lambda}

is called the ideal generated by
S
, where

\{I_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

denoted the set of all ideals in
X
containing
S
.

It is easy to see that the closed ideal generated by
S

coincides with the closure in
X
of the set

\Big\{\sum^{n}_{j=1}f_{j}g_{j}~\Big|~f_{j}\in X,\ g_{j}\in S,\ n\in{\mathbf{N}}\Big\}.

Definition 4.2
.

For a closed ideal
I
of
X
the zero-set of
I

is defined by
\displaystyle Z(I)=\bigcap_{f\in I}f^{-1}(\{0\})

with
f^{-1}(\{0\})=\{x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{2}~|~f(x)=0\}
.

We note that
x\in Z(I)
if and only if

f(x)=0
for all
f\in I
.
Moreover,
Z(I)
is a closed subset of
X

whenever
I
is a closed ideal in
X
.
In fact, if
f\in X
, then

f
is continuous on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
and thus

f^{-1}(\{0\})
is a closed subset of
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.

The following lemma seems to be known to many people,
but for the reader’s convenience, we give the
proof. We will write
f|_{E}=0
if
f(x)=0
for all
x\in E
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
Then

I(E)=\{f\in X~|~f|_{E}=0\}

is a closed ideal in
X
with
E=Z(I(E))
.

Proof..

We give the proof only for the case
X=M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
;
the other case is similar.
It is clear that
I(E)
is an ideal in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
To see that
I(E)
is closed,
let
f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
\{f_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=1}\subset I(E)

and

\|f_{n}-f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\to 0

(n\to\infty)
.
Since

\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f_{n}-f\|_{M^{\infty,1}}\lesssim\|f_{n}-f\|_{M^{p,1}}\lesssim\|f_{n}-f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}},

we see that

\{f_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=1}
converges pointwise to
f
on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
Since
f_{n}|_{E}=0
, we have
f|_{E}=0
, and thus
f\in I(E)
.
Hence
I(E)
is closed.
Next we prove
E=Z(I(E))
.
Since
E\subset Z(I(E))
is clear, we
show
Z(I(E))\subset E
.
Suppose

x_{0}\not\in E
.
Since
E
is closed
and
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\subset M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
there exists

f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

f(x_{0})=1
and
f|_{E}=0
.
Then
f\in I(E)
and
f(x_{0})\not=0
.
Thus
x_{0}\not\in Z(I(E))
,
which implies the desired result. ∎

We also prepare the following lemma,
which also characterises
closed ideals in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
We will call a subspace
M
of
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

is modulation invariant if

M_{\eta}f(x)=e^{ix\eta}f(x)\in M
whenever

f\in M
and
\eta\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
and
s\geq 0
.

Then for any closed ideal
I
in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, one has
:

(i)

If
g\in M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
satisfies

(4)

\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)\overline{V_{\psi}g(x,\xi)}dxd\xi=0\quad{\rm for~all~}f\in I

for any pair
\phi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

with
\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\not=0
, if and only if

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\Big[V_{\phi}f(x,\cdot)*V_{\overline{\psi}}\overline{g}(x,\cdot)\Big](\xi)dx=0\quad{\rm for~all~}f\in I,\xi\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

for any pair
\phi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
with
\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\not=0
.

(ii)
Every closed ideal in

M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

coincides with a closed modulation invariant
subspace of
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Proof..

We first assume that (
5
) holds.
Then we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\Big[V_{\phi}f(x,\cdot)*V_{\overline{\psi}}\overline{g}(x,\cdot)\Big](\xi)dx=(2\pi)^{n}\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\langle M_{-\xi}f,g\rangle

for all
f\in I
,

\xi\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

and
\phi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

with
\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\not=0
,
and thus
\langle f,g\rangle=0
.
Hence

\iint_{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}V_{\phi}f(x,\xi)\overline{V_{\psi}g(x,\xi)}dxd\xi=(2\pi)^{-n}\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\langle f,g\rangle=0,

which gives
(
4
).

Next we assume that (
4
) holds.
We note that Lemma
2.5
implies that

f\overline{g}\in M^{1,\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

for all
f\in I
.
Then for all
h\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
\phi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

with
\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\not=0
,
we have

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\langle f\overline{g},h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\langle f\overline{h},g\rangle=(2\pi)^{-n}\langle V_{\phi}(f\overline{h}),V_{\psi}g\rangle.

On the other hand, since
f\in I
and
I
is a closed ideal in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
we see that
f\overline{h}\in I
.
Therefore, (
4
) implies that

\langle V_{\phi}(f\overline{h}),V_{\psi}g\rangle=0,

and thus
\langle f\overline{g},h\rangle=0
.
By the duality, we obtain

f\overline{g}=0
.
Hence, we obtain for
x,\xi\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=V_{\phi\overline{\psi}}(f\overline{g})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}V_{\phi}f(x,\xi-\eta)V_{\overline{\psi}}\overline{g}(x,\eta)d\eta

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}V_{\phi}(M_{-\xi}f)(x,-\eta)\overline{V_{\psi}g(x,-\eta)}d\eta,

and thus

\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\langle M_{-\xi}f,g\rangle=0
,
which implies
(
5
).

Finally, we give the characterization of closed
ideals in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Let
I
be a closed ideal in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
We first note that if
g\in M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

satisfies (
5
),
then

(6)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}[V_{\phi}f(x,\cdot)*V_{\overline{\psi}}\overline{g}(x,\cdot)](\xi)dx=\langle V_{\phi}(M_{-\xi}f),V_{\psi}g\rangle

holds for all
f\in I
,
\xi\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
and

\phi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

with
\langle\psi,\phi\rangle\not=0
.
Suppose that
I
is not modulation invariant.
Then there exist
f_{0}\in I
and
\eta_{0}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

such that
M_{-\eta_{0}}f_{0}\not\in I
.
On the other hand, we note that

(M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}))^{\prime}=M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
i.e., for any
\ell\in(M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}))^{\prime}

and
\phi,\psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

with
\langle\psi,\phi\rangle=0
, there
exists
g\in M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that

\ell(f)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}\langle\psi,\phi\rangle}\langle V_{\phi}f,V_{\psi}g\rangle.

Therefore, the Hahn-Banach theorem
and the duality imply that there exists

g_{0}\in M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that

\langle f,g_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}\langle\psi,\phi\rangle}\langle V_{\phi}f,V_{\psi}g_{0}\rangle=0

for all
f\in I
, and

\displaystyle\langle M_{-\eta}f,g_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}\langle\psi,\phi\rangle}\langle V_{\phi}(M_{-\eta}f_{0}),V_{\psi}g_{0}\rangle=1.

The above two equalities imply that
g_{0}
satisfies (
5
)
but not (
6
), which yields a contradiction.

Conversely, we assume that
I
is a closed
modulation invariant subspace
of
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
If
I
is not an ideal in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
then there exist
f_{0}\in I

and
h_{0}\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

f_{0}\overline{h_{0}}\not\in I
.
The Hahn-Banach theorem and the duality imply that
there exists
g_{0}\in M^{p^{\prime},\infty}_{-s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that

(7)

\displaystyle\langle f,g_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}\langle\psi,\phi\overline{\phi}\rangle}\langle V_{\phi\overline{\phi}}f,V_{\psi}g_{0}\rangle=0

for all
f\in I
, and

(8)

\displaystyle\langle f_{0}\overline{h_{0}},g_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}\langle\psi,\phi\overline{\phi}\rangle}\langle V_{\phi\overline{\phi}}(f_{0}\overline{h_{0}}),V_{\psi}g_{0}\rangle=1.

We note that

V_{\phi\overline{\phi}}(f_{0}\overline{h_{0}})(x,\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n}[V_{\phi}f_{0}(x,\cdot)*V_{\overline{\phi}}\overline{h_{0}}(x,\cdot)](\xi)

and thus

\displaystyle\langle V_{\phi\overline{\phi}}(f_{0}\overline{h_{0}}),V_{\psi}g_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}[(V_{\phi}f_{0}(x,\cdot)*V_{\overline{\phi}}\overline{h_{0}}(x,\cdot))*V_{\overline{\psi}}\overline{g_{0}}(x,\cdot)](0)dx

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}[(V_{\phi}f_{0}(x,\cdot)*V_{\overline{\psi}}\overline{g_{0}}(x,\cdot))*V_{\overline{\phi}}\overline{h_{0}}(x,\cdot)](0)dx.

Moreover
for all
x,\xi\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
,
we have by the Parseval identity that

\displaystyle[V_{\phi}f_{0}(x,\cdot)*V_{\overline{\psi}}\overline{g_{0}}(x,\cdot)](\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}V_{\phi}(M_{-\xi}f_{0})(x,\eta)\overline{V_{\psi}g_{0}(x,\eta)}d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}{\mathcal{F}}_{y\to\eta}[(M_{-\xi}f_{0})(y)\cdot\overline{\phi(y-x)}](\eta)\overline{{\mathcal{F}}_{y\to\eta}[g_{0}(y)\overline{\psi(y-x)}](\eta)}d\eta

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}(M_{-\xi}f_{0})(y)\cdot\overline{\phi(y-x)}\overline{g_{0}(y)\overline{\psi(y-x)}}dy

\displaystyle=\langle M_{-\xi}f_{0}\cdot T_{x}(\overline{\phi}\psi),g_{0}\rangle.

Since
I
is modulation invariant, we have

M_{-\xi}f_{0}\in I
, and thus

M_{-\xi}f_{0}\cdot T_{x}(\overline{\phi}\psi)\in I

by
T_{x}(\overline{\phi}\psi)\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Therefore we have by (
7
)

[V_{\phi}f_{0}(x,\cdot)*V_{\overline{\psi}}\overline{g_{0}}(x,\cdot)](\xi)=0.

Hence

\langle V_{\phi\overline{\phi}}(f_{0}\overline{h_{0}}),V_{\psi}g_{0}\rangle=0
,
which contradicts
(
8
).

∎

5.
Wiener-Lévy
theorem for
M_{s}^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

In this section, we consider the Wiener-Lévy theorem
for
M_{s}^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(cf.
[
31
, Theorem 1.3.1]
).
More precisely, we show the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\geq 0
,

f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and
a compact subset
K\subset{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
Suppose that
F
is an analytic function on a neighborhood of

f(K)=\{f(x)~|~x\in K\}
in
{\mathbf{C}}
.
Then there exists
g\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

g(x)=F(f(x))
for all
x\in K
.

To prove Theorem
5.1
,
we prepare the following lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
s\geq 0
,

f,h\in M^{1,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
x_{0}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
. We set

H^{\lambda}_{x_{0}}(x)=\Big(f\Big(x_{0}+\frac{x}{\lambda}\Big)-f(x_{0})\Big)h(x),\quad\mbox{for}\,\,\lambda>0.

Then we have

(9)

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\|H^{\lambda}_{x_{0}}\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}=0.

Proof..

With
g_{0}(t)=e^{-\frac{|t|^{2}}{2}}
we have by (
1
) in Section
2.1
for
\lambda>1

\displaystyle V_{g_{0}}(H^{\lambda}_{x_{0}})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}e^{ix\xi}V_{\widehat{g_{0}}}(H^{\lambda}_{x_{0}})^{\wedge}(\xi,-x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\big((H^{\lambda}_{x_{0}})^{\wedge}*M_{-x}(\widehat{g_{0}})^{*}\big)(\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\big((H^{\lambda}_{x_{0}})^{\wedge}*M_{-x}\widehat{g_{0}}\big)(\xi).

We note that

\displaystyle\Big(f\Big(x_{0}+\frac{\cdot}{\lambda}\Big)\Big)^{\wedge}(\eta)=(T_{\lambda x_{0}}D_{\lambda}f)^{\wedge}(\eta)=\lambda^{n}e^{i\lambda x_{0}\eta}\widehat{f}(\lambda\eta)

with
D_{\lambda}f(\eta)=f(\frac{\eta}{\lambda})
.
Moreover, since
f,\widehat{f}\in M^{1,1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
we have
by the Fourier inversion formula that

f(x_{0})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\lambda^{n}e^{i\lambda x_{0}\eta}\widehat{f}(\lambda\eta)d\eta.

Thus, by putting
H(x,\xi)=[\widehat{h}*M_{-x}g_{0}](\xi)
,
we see
that

\displaystyle\big((H^{\lambda}_{x_{0}})^{\wedge}*M_{-x}g_{0}\big)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\Big[\Big(f\Big(x_{0}+\frac{\cdot}{\lambda}\Big)\Big)^{\wedge}*\widehat{h}*M_{-x}g_{0}\Big](\xi)-f(x_{0})[\widehat{h}*M_{-x}g_{0}](\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\lambda^{n}e^{i\lambda x_{0}\eta}\widehat{f}(\lambda\eta)\Big(H(x,\xi-\eta)-H(x,\xi)\Big)d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}e^{ix_{0}\eta}\widehat{f}(\eta)\Big(H\Big(x,\xi-\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big)-H(x,\xi)\Big)d\eta.

Therefore, we have by the Fubini Theorem that

\displaystyle\|H^{\lambda}_{x_{0}}\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\eta)|\Big(\iint_{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\Big|H\Big(x,\xi-\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big)-H(x,\xi)\Big|dxd\xi\Big)d\eta.

Moreover, by the submultiplicity of

\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}
for
s\geq 0
,
one has

\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\lesssim\Big\langle\xi-\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s}\Big\langle\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big\rangle^{s}\quad and\quad\Big\langle\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big\rangle\approx 1+\Big|\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big|\leq 1+|\eta|\approx\langle\eta\rangle

for large
\lambda
,
and thus

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(\eta)|\iint_{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\Big|H\Big(x,\xi-\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big)-H(x,\xi)\Big|dxd\xi\lesssim\langle\eta\rangle^{s}|\widehat{f}(\eta)|\|h\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}.

Assume now that (
9
)
is not valid, i.e.,
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0

such that for some sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=1}

with
\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=\infty
,
one has

\|H^{\lambda_{n}}_{x_{0}}\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}\geq\varepsilon_{0}
.
But this is not possible since the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem
implies that
\|H^{\lambda_{n}}_{x_{0}}\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}\to 0

(n\to\infty)
.
This implies (
9
).
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Given
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\geq 0
,
f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,

\tau\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
x_{0}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
For
\lambda>0
, we define

G^{\lambda}_{x_{0}}(x)=\Big(f\Big(x_{0}+\frac{x}{\lambda}\Big)-f(x_{0})\Big)\tau(x).

Then we have

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\|G^{\lambda}_{x_{0}}\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}=0.

Proof..

Suppose that
\lambda>0
and
{\rm supp~}\tau\subset B_{\lambda}(0)
.
Take
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that
\psi(x)=1
on
B_{2}(x_{0})
.
Since
\psi(x_{0})=1
and

\psi(x_{0}+\frac{x}{\lambda})=1
on
{\rm supp~}\tau
,
we see that

\Big(f\Big(x_{0}+\frac{x}{\lambda}\Big)-f(x_{0})\Big)\tau(x)=\Big((f\psi)\Big(x_{0}+\frac{x}{\lambda}\Big)-(f\psi)(x_{0})\Big)\tau(x)

for all
x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
On the other hand,
Lemma
2.5
implies that

\|f\psi\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{s}}\|\psi\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\|\psi\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}},

and thus
f\psi\in M^{1,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Hence, it follows from Lemma
5.2
that

\displaystyle\|G^{\lambda}_{x_{0}}\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\lesssim\|G^{\lambda}_{x_{0}}\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}=\Big\|\Big((f\psi)\Big(x_{0}+\frac{\cdot}{\lambda}\Big)-(f\psi)(x_{0})\Big)\tau\Big\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}\to 0

as
\lambda\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
f
,
K
and
F
be as in Theorem
5.1
,
and
x_{0}\in K
.
Then there exists
g_{x_{0}}\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that

g_{x_{0}}(x)=F(f(x))

on some neighborhood of
x_{0}
.

Proof..

Set
z_{0}=f(x_{0})
. Since

F
is analytic at
z_{0}
, there exists

0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
such that

F(z)
has the power series expansion

F(z)=F(z_{0})+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}c_{j}(z-z_{0})^{j}\quad(|z-z_{0}|<\varepsilon_{0}).

Let
\tau\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
be a function
which is
1
near the origin.
Then it follows from Lemma
5.3

that there exists
\lambda_{x_{0}}>0

such that

\|(f(x_{0}+\frac{\cdot}{\lambda_{x_{0}}})-f(x_{0}))\tau\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon_{0}/c
,
where the constant
c
is as
in Lemma
2.2

(iv)
.
Thus we see that

b_{x_{0}}(x)=F(f(x_{0}))\tau(x)+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}c_{j}\Big(\Big(f\Big(x_{0}+\frac{x}{\lambda_{x_{0}}}\Big)-f(x_{0})\Big)\tau(x)\Big)^{j}

converges in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Hence

\displaystyle g_{x_{0}}(x)

\displaystyle=b_{x_{0}}(\lambda_{x_{0}}(x-x_{0}))

\displaystyle=F(f(x_{0}))\tau_{x_{0}}(x)+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}c_{j}\big(\big(f(x)-f(x_{0})\big)\tau_{x_{0}}(x)\big)^{j}

is in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
where we denote
\tau_{x_{0}}(x)=\tau(\lambda_{x_{0}}(x-x_{0}))
.
Moreover, since
g_{x_{0}}(x)=F(f(x))

in some neighborhood of
x_{0}
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
5.1

by extending the local result above to the compact set
K
.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We first note that
by Lemma
5.4
,
we have for each
x_{0}\in K
,
there exist
\tau_{x_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and a neighborhood
U_{x_{0}}
of
x_{0}

such that
g_{x_{0}}
defined by

g_{x_{0}}(x)=F(f(x_{0}))\tau_{x_{0}}(x)+\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}c_{j}\big((f(x)-f(x_{0}))\tau_{x_{0}}(x)\big)^{j}

satisfies
g_{x_{0}}(x)=F(f(x))
on
U_{x_{0}}
.
Since

K\subset\bigcup_{x_{0}\in K}U_{x_{0}}

and
K
is compact,
there exist

\{x_{j}\}^{N}_{j=1}\subset K

such that the
corresponding neighborhoods
\{U_{x_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{N}

cover
K
.
Now we set

h_{1}(x)=\tau_{x_{1}}(x)
and

h_{j}(x)=\tau_{x_{j}}(x)(1-\tau_{x_{1}}(x))\cdots(1-\tau_{x_{j-1}}(x))\quad(j=2,3,\cdots,N).

Since
\tau_{x_{j}}(x)=1
and
g_{x_{j}}(x)=F(f(x))
on
U_{x_{j}}

(j=1,\cdots,N)
, it is easily see that

\sum^{N}_{j=1}h_{j}(x)=1
on
K
.
Therefore
g\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

defined by

g(x)=\sum^{N}_{j=1}h_{j}(x)g_{x_{j}}(x)

satisfies

\displaystyle g(x)=\sum^{N}_{j=1}h_{j}(x)F(f(x))=F(f(x))\sum^{N}_{j=1}h_{j}(x)=F(f(x)).

for all
x\in K
, which yields the desired result.
∎

Under a mild extra condition on
f
respectively

F
, one can extend the local result to the
following global result.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\geq 0
,

f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and

F
be analytic on an open neighborhood of

f({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\cup\{0\}
with
F(0)=0
.
Then there exists
g\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that
g(x)=F(f(x))
.

Proof..

Since
F
is analytic on a neighborhood of
0
with
F(0)=0
,
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

F(z)
has the power series representation

F(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}c_{j}z^{j}\quad(|z|<\varepsilon_{0}).

It follows from Theorem
3.1

that for any
\varepsilon
with
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,
there exists
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f-\phi f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|f-\phi f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon/c,

where the constant
c
is as
in Lemma
2.2

(iv)
.
Now we set

g_{0}(x)=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}c_{j}\big(f(x)-\phi(x)f(x)\big)^{j}.

Then we have
g_{0}\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and

g_{0}(x)=F(f(x)-\phi(x)f(x))=F(f(x))

(x\not\in{\rm supp~}\phi)
.
On the other hand, let
\tau_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
be
such that
\tau_{0}(x)=1

on
{\rm supp~}\phi
.
Then it follow from Theorem
5.1

that there exists exists
g_{1}\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that

g_{1}(x)=F(f(x))
on
{\rm supp~}\tau_{0}
.
Now we set

g(x)=(1-\tau_{0}(x))g_{0}(x)+\tau_{0}(x)g_{1}(x).

We note that
g\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
If
x\in{\rm supp~}\phi
, then

\tau_{0}(x)=1
and
g_{1}(x)=F(f(x))
,
and thus
g(x)=F(f(x))
.
Moreover, if
x\in{\rm supp~}\tau_{0}\setminus{\rm supp~}\phi
,
then
g_{0}(x)=F(f(x)-\phi(x)f(x))=F(f(x))
and
g_{1}(x)=F(f(x))
, and thus

g(x)=F(f(x))
. Finally, if
x\not\in{\rm supp~}\tau_{0}
, then

\tau_{0}(x)=0
and
g_{0}(x)=F(f(x))
, and thus

g(x)=F(f(x))
.
Hence we obtain the desired result.
∎

As an application of Theorem
5.1
,
we obtain the following version of
Wiener’s general Tauberian theorem for
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(cf.
[
31
, Theorem 1.4.1]
,

[
20
, Ch VIII. 6.4]
).

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\geq 0
,

f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
I
be a closed ideal in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
If
I
is generated by one function
f
in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
then we have:
I=M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
if and only if

f(x)\not=0

(x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Proof..

Suppose that
I=M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Since
I
is the closed ideal generated by
f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
we see that
I
is equal to the closure in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

of the set

\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda_{j}\phi_{j}f~\Big|~\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbf{C}},\ \phi_{j}\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}),\ N\in{\mathbf{N}}\Big\}.

If
f(x_{0})=0
for some
x_{0}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
,
then we have
g(x_{0})=0
for all
g\in I
.
Since
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\subset M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
this contradicts
I=M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Conversely, suppose that

f(x)\not=0
for all
x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
Let
K
be a compact subset of
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

and
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

be such that
{\rm supp~}\phi\subset K
.
Since

F(z)=\frac{1}{z}
is analytic on
{\mathbf{C}}\setminus\{0\}

and

f(x)\not=0
for all
x\in K
,
it follows from Theorem
5.1

that
there exists
g\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that
fg=1
on
K
.
Since
f\in I
and
I
is an ideal in

M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, we have

\phi=\phi g\cdot f\in I
.
Moreover, since

C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

is dense in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(cf. Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
3.1
),
we obtain
I=M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

By Lemma
4.4
,
the ideal

I
in Theorem
5.6

is a closed modulation invariant subspace of

M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Thus
I
is equal to the closure in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

of the set

A=\Big\{\sum^{N}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}e^{i\eta_{j}x}f(x)~|~\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbf{C}},\ \eta_{j}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n},\ N\in{\mathbf{N}}\Big\}.

As a corollary of Theorem
5.6
,
we also obtain
a variant of
Wiener’s approximation theorem
for the Wiener amalgam space

W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(cf.
[
31
, Theorem 1.4.8]
).

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\geq 0
and

W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

be the Wiener amalgam space
consisting of all
\widehat{f}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that
f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

with the norm

\|\widehat{f}\|_{W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})}=\|f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}
.
Then for any
\widehat{f}\in W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, we have that

f(x)\not=0
for all
x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
if and only if
the set

{\mathcal{F}}(A)=\Big\{\sum^{N}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta_{j})~|~\lambda_{j}\in{\mathbf{C}},\ \eta_{j}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n},\ N\in{\mathbf{N}}\Big\}

is dense in
W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Proof..

Let
\widehat{f}\in W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
We first assume that
f(x)\not=0
for all
x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
Then Remark
5.7
implies
that for any
g\in W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and
\varepsilon>0
,
there exist

\{\lambda_{j}\}^{N}_{j=1}\subset{\mathbf{C}}
and
\{\eta_{j}\}^{N}_{j=1}\subset{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

such that

\Big\|\widehat{g}-\sum^{N}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta_{j})\Big\|_{W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})}=\Big\|g-\sum^{N}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}e^{in_{j}x}f(x)\Big\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon

holds.
Therefore,
{\mathcal{F}}(A)
is dense in
W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Conversely, we assume that
{\mathcal{F}}(A)
is dense in
W({\mathcal{F}}L^{p},\ell^{1}_{s})({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then the set
A
in Remark
5.7

is dense in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Hence Theorem
5.6
implies that

f(x)\not=0
for all
x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

The same principles apply for the case of e.g.
finitely many functions

f_{1},\cdots,f_{\ell}

without common zero.

6.
Set of spectral synthesis
for
M_{s}^{p,1}({\bf R}^{n})

In this section, we consider the set of spectral synthesis
for
M_{s}^{p,1}({\bf R}^{n})
.
Throughout this section,
X
stands for
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\geq 0
)
or
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(s\geq 0)
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
E
be a closed subset of
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

and
I(E)
be the set defined in Lemma

4.3
.
Define
J(E)
by the closed ideal in
X

generated by

J_{0}(E)=\{f\in X~|~f(x)=0~{\rm in~a~neighborhood~of}~E\}.

Then
E
is called a
set of spectral synthesis
for
X

if
I(E)=J(E)
.

Remark 6.2
.

We can easily see that
E
is a set of spectral synthesis
for
X
if and only if

x+E
is a set of spectral synthesis
for
X
for some/any
x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.

In the following, we shall prove the following.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
,
s\geq 0
and
K
be a compact subset of
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
.
Then
K
is a set of spectral synthesis for
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
if and only
if
K
is a set of spectral synthesis for
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

6.1.
Technical lemmas

To prove Theorem
6.3
,
we use
the “ideal theory for Segal algebras”
developed in
Reiter
[
30
, Ch.6, §2]

(see also
[
5
]
,
[
31
]
).
In the following, we denote by
({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
the space defined in Lemma
2.10
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
I
be a closed ideal in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then
I
is the closure of
I\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Proof..

Since
I\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}\subset I
and
I
is closed in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
the closure of
I\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is contained in
I
.
We next show that
I
is contained in the
closure of
I\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Let
f\in I
.
Then we have by Lemma
2.8

that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that
\|f-\phi f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}<\varepsilon
holds.
Since
I
is an ideal in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, we have

\phi f\in I\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
.
Hence we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
and
s\geq 0
.
Suppose that
I
and
I^{\prime}
are closed ideals in

{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then we have the following:

(i)

I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is a
closed ideal in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

(ii)

If
I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})=I^{\prime}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
then we have
I=I^{\prime}
.

Proof..

(i)
We first prove that
I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is an
ideal in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Let
f\in I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and
g\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Since
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

is a multiplication algebra, we see
fg\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Moreover, since
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
I
is an ideal in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
we have
fg\in I
.
Hence we
fg\in I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

for any
f\in I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and

g\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
which implies the desired result.
We next prove
I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is closed
in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Let
f
be in the closure of
I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
in

M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then, there exists
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f_{n}-f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\to 0

(n\to\infty)
.
We note that

M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
and thus

\|f_{n}-f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}\to 0

(n\to\infty)
.
Since

I
is closed in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is complete,
we obtain
f\in I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
which implies the desired result.

(ii)

Since

I\cap M^{p,1}_{s}\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}=I^{\prime}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}

and
({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}\hookrightarrow M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
we have
I\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}=I^{\prime}\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
.
Thus it follows from Lemma
6.4

that
I=I^{\prime}
, which implies the desired result.

∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
and
s\geq 0
.
Then for any closed ideal
I_{M}
in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
the ideal
I_{F}
in

{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

defined by the closure of
I_{M}
in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

satisfies

I_{M}=I_{F}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Proof..

Let
I_{M}
be a closed ideal in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and define
I_{F}^{\prime}
by the closure of

I_{M}\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}

in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then
I_{F}^{\prime}
is a closed ideal in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
To see
I_{F}^{\prime}
is an ideal in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
let
f\in I_{F}^{\prime}
and
g\in{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then there exists
\{f_{n}\}^{\infty}_{n=1}\subset I_{M}\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}

such that

\|f-f_{n}\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}\to 0

(n\to\infty)
.
Since
f_{n}\in({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
, there exists
\psi_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that
\psi_{n}(x)=1
on
{\rm supp~}f_{n}
.
Then we have
\psi_{n}g\in({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}\hookrightarrow M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Therefore,

\psi_{n}g\cdot f_{n}\in I_{M}
,
and thus

f_{n}g=f_{n}\cdot\psi_{n}g\in I_{M}\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}
.
Moreover, since

\displaystyle\|fg-f_{n}g\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}\lesssim\|f-f_{n}\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}\|g\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}\to 0\quad(n\to\infty),

we have
fg\in I_{F}^{\prime}
.
Hence,
I_{F}^{\prime}
is an ideal in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Next, we prove that

I_{F}

is equal to
I_{F}^{\prime}
.
Let
f\in I_{F}
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
g\in I_{M}
such that

\|f-g\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}<\varepsilon
.
On the other hand, Theorem
3.1
implies that
there exists
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that

\|g-\phi g\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon
.
Hence we have
\phi g\in I_{M}\subset M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Hence we obtain
\phi g\in I_{M}\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}

and

\displaystyle\|f-\phi g\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}+\|\phi g-g\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-g\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}+\|\phi g-g\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\lesssim\varepsilon,

which yields
I_{F}\subset I_{F}^{\prime}
.
The reverse inclusion is clear.

Finally, we prove
I_{M}=I_{F}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Since
I_{M}\subset I_{F}
and
I_{M}\subset M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
we see that

I_{M}=I_{M}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\subset I_{F}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
On the other hand, let
f\in I_{F}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and
\varepsilon>0
.
By Theorem
3.1
, there exists
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that

\|f-\phi f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon
.
Now we take

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
with

\varphi(x)=1
on
{\rm supp~}\phi
.
Since
f\in I_{F}=I_{F}^{\prime}
,
there exists
h\in I_{M}\cap({\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s})_{c}

such that

\|f-h\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{\|\varphi\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\|\phi\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}}
.
Then we have
\phi h\in I_{M}
.
Since
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow M^{\infty,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

(see the proof of Lemma
2.10
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\|f-\phi h\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-\phi f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}+\|\varphi\phi(f-h)\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-\phi f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}+\|\varphi\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\|\phi(f-h)\|_{M^{\infty,1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-\phi f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}+\|\varphi\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\|\phi(f-h)\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f-\phi f\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}+\|\varphi\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\|\phi\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}\|f-h\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}.

Therefore
f
is in the closure of
I_{M}
in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Since
I_{M}
is closed in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
we get the desired result.

∎

Remark 6.7
.

Let
I_{M}
and
I_{M}^{\prime}
be closed ideals in

M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, and

I_{F}
be the closure of
I_{M}
in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
If the closure of
I_{M}^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

is equal to
I_{F}
, then
Proposition
6.6
implies that
I_{M}=I_{M}^{\prime}
.

Combining Lemma
6.5

and Proposition
6.6
,
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 6.8

(The ideal theory for Segal algebras)
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
,
s\geq 0
,

{\mathcal{I}}_{F}
be the set of all closed ideals in

{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
and
{\mathcal{I}}_{M}
be the set of all closed ideals in

M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Define the map
\iota:{\mathcal{I}}_{F}\to{\mathcal{I}}_{M}
by

\iota(I_{F})=I_{F}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then

\iota
is bijective.
More precisely, we have

\iota^{-1}(I_{M})=\overline{I_{M}}^{\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}}\qquad(I_{M}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{M})

and

\iota\big(\overline{I_{M}}^{\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}}\big)=\overline{I_{M}}^{\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}}\cap M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})=I_{M},

where
\overline{I_{M}}^{\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}}}

denotes the closure of
I_{M}
in

{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

6.2.
The proof of Theorem
6.3

For a closed subset
K
of
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
,
we set
I_{F}(K)=\{f\in{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})~|~f|_{K}=0\}

and
I_{M}(K)=\{f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})~|~f|_{K}=0\}
.
Moreover, we define
J_{F}(K)
by the
closure
of

\{f\in{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})~|~f(x)=0{\rm~in~a~neighborhood~of~}K\}

in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
and

J_{M}(K)
by the
closure
of

\{f\in M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})~|~f(x)=0{\rm~in~a~neighborhood~of~}K\}

in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
We note that

J_{F}(K)
is the smallest closed ideal
I
in
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that
\bigcap_{f\in I}f^{-1}(\{0\})=K

(cf.
[
31
, Proposition 2.4.5]
).
Similarly,

J_{M}(K)
is the smallest closed ideal
I
in
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that
\bigcap_{f\in I}f^{-1}(\{0\})=K

(cf. Theorems
5.1
and
5.5
).
Thus Proposition
6.6
implies
that
I_{F}(K)=J_{M}(K)
if and only if

I_{F}(K)=J_{F}(K)
.
Therefore,
K
is a set of spectral synthesis for
M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

if and only if
K
is a set of spectral synthesis for
{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

7.1.
Technical Lemma

Lemma 7.2
.

For any
t_{0}\in{\bf R}^{n}
and
R>0
,
there exist
\psi^{(1)},\psi^{(2)}\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

\psi=\psi^{(1)}*\psi^{(2)}

satisfies

\psi(x)=1
on
B_{R}(t_{0})

and

{\rm supp~}\psi\subset B_{5R}(t_{0})
.

Proof..

Let
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})
be such that

g(x)\geq 0

(x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,

g(x)=1
on
B_{R}(0)

and

{\rm supp~}g\subset B_{2R}(0)
.
Define
\psi^{(1)},\psi^{(2)}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
by

\psi^{(1)}(x)=g\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)\quad{\rm and}\quad\psi^{(2)}(x)=\frac{2^{n}}{\|g\|_{L^{1}}}g(2(x-t_{0})),

respectively, and set
\psi=\psi^{(1)}*\psi^{(2)}
.
Then
\psi
satisfies the desired conditions.
Indeed, we note that

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\psi^{(1)}(x-y)\psi^{(2)}(y)dy=\frac{2^{n}}{\|g\|_{L^{1}}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}g\Big(\frac{x-y}{2}\Big)g(2(y-t_{0}))dy.

Moreover, if

x\in B_{R}(t_{0})
and

|2(y-t_{0})|\leq 2R
, then
we have

\frac{|x-y|}{2}\leq\frac{|x-t_{0}|}{2}+\frac{|t_{0}-y|}{2}\leq R,

and thus
g(\frac{x-y}{2})=1
.
Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle=(\psi^{(1)}*\psi^{(2)})(x)=\frac{2^{n}}{\|g\|_{1}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}g(2(y-t_{0}))dy=1

on
B_{R}(t_{0})
.
Also we have

{\rm supp~}\psi^{(1)}\subset\{x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}~|~|x|\leq 4R\}

and

{\rm supp~}\psi^{(2)}\subset\{x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}~|~|x-t_{0}|\leq R\},

and thus we see

{\rm supp~}\psi\subset{\rm supp~}\psi^{(1)}+{\rm supp~}\psi^{(2)}\subset\{x\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}~|~|x-t_{0}|\leq 5R\}.

∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let

\psi^{(1)},\psi^{(2)}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

be such that

{\rm supp~}\psi^{(j)}\subset B_{R}(0)

for some
R>0

(j=1,2)
.
Define
\psi=\psi^{(1)}*\psi^{(2)}
.
Then we have for
0\leq s<1
and
\vartheta\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\big|\widehat{\psi}\left(\xi-\vartheta\right)-\widehat{\psi}(\xi)\big|d\xi\leq C_{\psi}|\vartheta|^{s}\Big(\max_{|t|\leq R}|e^{i\vartheta t}-1|\Big)^{1-s}.

Proof..

We first note that
since
\widehat{\psi}=\widehat{\psi^{(1)}}\cdot\widehat{\psi^{(2)}}
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}(\xi-\vartheta)-\widehat{\psi}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\big(\widehat{\psi^{(1)}}(\xi-\vartheta)-\widehat{\psi^{(1)}}(\xi)\big)\widehat{\psi^{(2)}}(\xi-\vartheta)+\widehat{\psi^{(1)}}(\xi)\big(\widehat{\psi^{(2)}}(\xi-\vartheta)-\widehat{\psi^{(2)}}(\xi)\big).

Then it follows from
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
and the Plancherel theorem
that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\big|\widehat{\psi}(\xi-\vartheta)-\widehat{\psi}(\xi)\big|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\big|\widehat{\psi^{(1)}}(\xi-\vartheta)-\widehat{\psi^{(1)}}(\xi)\big|^{2}d\xi\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\big|\widehat{\psi^{(2)}}(\xi-\vartheta)\big|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\big|\widehat{\psi^{(2)}}(\xi-\vartheta)-\widehat{\psi^{(2)}}(\xi)\big|^{2}d\xi\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}\big|\widehat{\psi^{(1)}}(\xi-\vartheta)\big|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|{\mathcal{F}}_{x\to\xi}[(e^{i\vartheta x}-1)\psi^{(1)}(x)](\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}\langle\vartheta\rangle^{s}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{\psi^{(2)}}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}

\displaystyle\quad+\|{\mathcal{F}}_{x\to\xi}[(e^{i\vartheta x}-1)\psi^{(2)}(x)](\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}\langle\vartheta\rangle^{s}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{\psi^{(1)}}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}.

We note that

|e^{i\vartheta x}-1|\leq\min\{2,|\vartheta x|\}
,
and
for
j=1,2
,
it follows from the Plancherel Theorem that

\|{\mathcal{F}}_{x\to\xi}[(e^{i\vartheta x}-1)\psi^{(j)}(x)](\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\|(e^{i\vartheta x}-1)\psi^{(j)}(x)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{x})}.

Moreover, since
{\rm supp~}\psi^{(j)}\subset B_{R}(0)

(j=1,2)
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{F}}_{x\to\xi}[(e^{i\vartheta x}-1)\psi^{(j)}(x)](\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}(1+|\vartheta|^{s})

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\max_{|x|\leq R}|e^{i\vartheta x}-1|\Big)^{1-s}\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\big(|e^{i\vartheta x}-1|^{s}|\psi^{(j)}(x)|\big)^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|\vartheta|^{s})

\displaystyle\leq|\vartheta|^{s}\Big(\max_{|x|\leq R}|e^{i\vartheta x}-1|\Big)^{1-s}\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\big(|x|^{s}|\psi^{(j)}(x)|\big)^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2^{s}|\vartheta|^{s}\Big(\max_{|x|\leq R}|e^{i\vartheta x}-1|\Big)^{1-s}\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}|\psi^{(j)}(x)|^{2}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which yields the desired inequality.

∎

Now we prepare a lemma,
which corresponds to a weighted version of
Bhimani-Ratnakumar

[
3
, Proposition 3.14]
.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
0\leq s<1
,
f\in M^{1,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
x_{0}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

(i)

\|(f-f(x_{0}))\phi\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon

(ii)

\phi(x)=1
in some neighborhood of
x_{0}
.

Proof..

Take
\psi=\psi^{(1)}*\psi^{(2)}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

as in Lemma
7.2

with
t_{0}=0
and
R=1
,
i.e.,

\psi(x)=1
on
B_{1}(0)

and

{\rm supp~}\psi\subset B_{5}(0)
.
Define
\psi_{\lambda}(x)=\psi(\lambda x)
and

h^{\lambda}(x)=(f(x)-f(x_{0}))\psi_{\lambda}(x-x_{0})

for
\lambda>0
.
We note that if

\lambda>5

and
x\in{\rm supp~}h^{\lambda}
,
then

\psi(x-x_{0})=1
.
Thus

h^{\lambda}(x)=h^{\lambda}(x)\psi(x-x_{0})
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
x_{0}=0

(see Remark
6.2
).
Let
g_{0}(t)=e^{-\frac{|t|^{2}}{2}}

(t\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n})

and
\lambda>5
.
By (
1
) in Section
2.1
,
we have

\displaystyle\|h^{\lambda}\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}

\displaystyle=\|\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}V_{g_{0}}h^{\lambda}(x,\xi)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{x})}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\|\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}V_{\widehat{g_{0}}}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\xi,-x)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{x})}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}.

Since
\widehat{g_{0}}=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}g_{0}
,
g_{0}^{*}=g_{0}
and

h^{\lambda}(x)=h^{\lambda}(x)\psi(x)
,
we obtain by (
1
) in Section
2.1

\displaystyle V_{\widehat{g_{0}}}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\xi,-x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}V_{g_{0}}(\widehat{h^{\lambda}}*\widehat{\psi})(\xi,-x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{ix\xi}(\widehat{h^{\lambda}}*\widehat{\psi}*M_{-x}g_{0})(\xi).

Thus by the Fubini Theorem and the Minkowski inequality for integral that

\displaystyle\|h^{\lambda}\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n}\|\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}V_{\widehat{g_{0}}}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\xi,-x)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{x})}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}

\displaystyle\approx\|\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}(\widehat{h^{\lambda}}*\widehat{\psi}*M_{-x}g_{0})(\xi)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\xi)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}(\widehat{\psi}*M_{-x}g_{0})(\xi)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{\xi})}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}_{x})}

\displaystyle=\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}}\|\psi\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}.

Since

\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\zeta)=(2\pi)^{-n}(\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}*\widehat{f})(\zeta)-f(0)\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}(\zeta)

and

\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\widehat{\psi}(\frac{\zeta}{\lambda})
,
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{h^{\lambda}}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\eta)\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}(\zeta-\eta)d\eta-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\eta)d\eta\Big)\widehat{\psi_{\lambda}}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\lambda)^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\eta)\Big(\widehat{\psi}\Big(\frac{\zeta-\eta}{\lambda}\Big)-\widehat{\psi}\Big(\frac{\zeta}{\lambda}\Big)\Big)d\eta

and thus we obtain by Lemma
7.3

choosing
\vartheta=\frac{\xi}{\lambda}

and
\lambda>5

that

\displaystyle\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{\lambda^{n}}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\langle\zeta\rangle^{s}\Big|\widehat{\psi}\Big(\frac{\zeta-\eta}{\lambda}\Big)-\widehat{\psi}\Big(\frac{\zeta}{\lambda}\Big)\Big|d\zeta\Big)|\widehat{f}(\eta)|d\eta

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{s}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\Big|\widehat{\psi}\Big(\xi-\frac{\eta}{\lambda}\Big)-\widehat{\psi}(\xi)\Big|d\xi\Big)|\widehat{f}(\eta)|d\eta

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbf{R}}^{n}}|\eta|^{s}\left(\max_{|t|\leq 4}|e^{i\frac{\eta}{\lambda}t}-1|\right)^{1-s}|\widehat{f}(\eta)|d\eta.

We note that
M^{1,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
and also

\left(\max_{|t|\leq 4}|e^{i\frac{\eta}{\lambda}t}-1|\right)^{1-s}\leq 2
and

\left(\max_{|t|\leq 4}|e^{i\frac{\eta}{\lambda}t}-1|\right)^{1-s}\to 0

as
\lambda\to\infty
.
Therefore the Lebesgue convergence theorem yields
that

\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}\widehat{h^{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}\to 0

(\lambda\to\infty)
.
Therefore,
for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

\lambda_{0}>0
such that

\|h^{\lambda_{0}}\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon
.
Hence,
by putting
\phi(x)=\psi_{\lambda_{0}}(x)
,
we have the desired result.

∎

Next we prepare a lemma,
which corresponds to
a weighted version of
Bhimani
[
2
, Proposition 4.7]
.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
0\leq s<1
,

,
x_{0}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}

and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists
\tau\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
such that

(i)

\|(f-f(x_{0}))\tau\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon

(ii)

\tau(x)=1
in some neighborhood of
x_{0}
.

Proof..

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

be such that
\psi(x)=1
on some neighborhood of

x_{0}
, and set

h(x)=(f(x)-f(x_{0}))\psi(x)
.
We note that
h(x_{0})=0
.
Since
h\in M^{1,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,
Lemma
7.4

implies that there exists
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

such that

\|(h-h(x_{0}))\phi\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}<\varepsilon

and
\phi=1
on some neighborhood of
x_{0}
.
Now we define

\tau=\psi\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Then we see that
\tau=1
on some neighborhood of
x_{0}
,
and we have by
M^{1,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow M^{p,1}_{s}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

\displaystyle\|(f-f(x_{0}))\tau\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}=\|(h-h(x_{0}))\phi\|_{M^{p,1}_{s}}\lesssim\|(h-h(x_{0}))\phi\|_{M^{1,1}_{s}}.

Hence we obtain the desired result.

∎

The proof of Theorem

We first note that

8.
Inclusion relation between
M^{p,1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}

In this
section, we consider the inclusion relation between
the modulation space

M^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}})
and the Fourier
Segal algebra
{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
.
Here,
{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
is the space defined by
the norm

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{1}}.

We note that
since

{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
is the Fourier image of
Segal algebra
A_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
which is defined by
the norm

\|f\|_{A_{p}}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{p}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}}

(see
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
39
]
for more details),

{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
is a (abstract) Segal algebra
in
A({\mathbf{R}})(={\mathcal{F}}L^{1}_{0}({\mathbf{R}}))

in the following sense.

(i)

{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
is a commutative Banach algebra with
pointwise multiplication and the norm

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}}
.

(ii)

{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
is a dense subset
of the Fourier algebra
A({\mathbf{R}})
.

(iii)

{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
is isometrically invariant under modulation operators:

\|M_{\xi}f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}}=\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}},\quad\forall\xi\in{\mathbf{R}}.

Moreover,
{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
has similar properties to
A({\mathbf{R}})
(see
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
).
On the other hand,
Lemmas
2.2
,
2.7

and
2.9
imply
that

M^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
for
1\leq p\leq 2
.
Therefore, it is natural to ask whether
M^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}})
is a proper subset of
{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
.

Theorem 8.1
.

For
1\leq p\leq 2
we have the proper, dense inclusion

M^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}})\subsetneq{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})
.

We remark that Losert
[
27
, Theorem 2]

implies that Theorem
8.1

holds for the case
p=1
.

8.1.
Technical lemma

To prove Theorem
8.1
,
we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
{\mathbf{R}}
.
Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a discrete measure
\mu\in M({\mathbf{R}})

and
m,N\in{\mathbf{N}}
for which the following conditions hold.

(i)

\|\mu\|_{M(\mathbf{R}{})}=1
and
\|\widehat{\mu}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\varepsilon
.

(ii)

{\rm supp~}\mu
consists of
2^{m}
different points and is expressed as

{\rm supp~}\mu=\Big\{x\in{\mathbf{R}}~\Big|~x=\sum^{m}_{j=1}\alpha_{j}N_{j},\ \alpha_{j}=0,1,\ N_{j}=2^{j-1}N\Big\}.

(iii)

The sets

\{-x+K~|~x\in{\rm supp~\mu}\}
are mutually disjoint.

Proof..

Our proof is based on the argument
due to Kahane
[
19
, p.34-36]
,
which is also
known as the Rudin-Shapiro method.
Let
\varepsilon>0

and take
m\in{\mathbf{N}}

such that
2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{m}{2}}<\varepsilon
.
We choose
N\in{\mathbf{N}}
such that
the sets

\Big\{-\sum^{m}_{j=1}\alpha_{j}N_{j}+K~\Big|~\alpha_{j}=0,1,\ N_{j}=2^{j-1}N\Big\}

are mutually disjoint for different
choices of
\alpha_{j}\in\{0,1\}
,
and then define
\mu_{j},\nu_{j}\in M({\mathbf{R}})

(j=1,2,\cdots,m)

by

\mu_{0}=\nu_{0}=\delta_{0}

and

\mu_{j}=\mu_{j-1}+\nu_{j-1}*\delta_{N_{j}},\quad\nu_{j}=\mu_{j-1}-\nu_{j-1}*\delta_{N_{j}}\quad(j=1,2,\cdots,m).

We note that the set {
\sum^{m}_{j=1}\alpha_{j}N_{j}~|~\alpha_{j}=0,1\}

consists of
2^{m}
different points, and thus we obtain

\|\mu_{m}\|_{M({\mathbf{R}})}=2^{m}
.
Moreover, it follows from

(\mu_{j-1}\pm\mu_{j-1}*\delta_{N_{j}})^{\wedge}(\xi)=\widehat{\mu_{j-1}}(\xi)\pm\widehat{\nu_{j-1}}(\xi)e^{-i\xi N_{j}}\quad(j=1,2,\cdots,m)

that

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mu_{m}}(\xi)|^{2}+|\widehat{\nu_{m}}(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle=2(|\widehat{\mu_{m-1}}(\xi)|^{2}+|\widehat{\nu_{m-1}}(\xi)|^{2})

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=2^{m}(|\widehat{\mu_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}+|\widehat{\nu_{0}}(\xi)|^{2})

\displaystyle=2^{m+1}.

This gives

|\widehat{\mu_{m}}(\xi)|\leq(|\widehat{\mu_{m}}(\xi)|^{2}+|\widehat{\nu_{m}}(\xi)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{m+1}{2}}.

Now we set

\mu=2^{-m}\mu_{m}
.
Then we can easily see
\|\mu\|_{M({\mathbf{R}})}=1

and

\|\widehat{\mu}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{-m}2^{\frac{m+1}{2}}=2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{m}{2}}<\varepsilon.

Hence, we obtain the desired result.

∎

.

\mu
is also expressed as

\mu=2^{-m}\sum_{\alpha_{j}=0,1}C_{(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{2m})}\delta_{\alpha_{1}N_{1}+\alpha_{2}N_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{2m}N_{2m}},

where each term

or

For the sake of simplicity, we simply write

with
{\rm supp~}\mu=\{\ell(j)\in{\mathbf{N}}~|~j=1,2,\cdots,2^{m}\}
,
where
a_{j}=2^{-m}
or
a_{j}=-2^{-m}
.

.

Let
1\leq p<2
and choose

such that

2^{\frac{1}{2}-r(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}<\varepsilon

In the same way as above, we define

8.2.
The proof of Theorem
8.1

(i)
We first consider the case

As mentioned at the beginning of Section

Then Lemma

that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\mu\in M({\mathbf{R}})

and

It follows from Remark

\mu=\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}a_{j}\delta_{\ell(j)}

with

or

Moreover, since

we see that for any

for some

such that

Furthermore, we have by Remark

\nu=\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}b_{j}\delta_{N(j)}\in M({\mathbf{R}})

or

Now we define
f
by

\widehat{f}=\mu*(\widehat{\nu}\phi)
.

Step 1.

Firstly, we estimate

Let

be such that

\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}}\varphi(\xi-k)=1
,

{\rm supp~}\varphi\subset[-1,1]

\varphi(\xi)=1

Since

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)=\Big(\Big(\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}a_{j}\delta_{\ell(j)}\Big)*(\widehat{\nu}\phi)\Big)(\xi)=\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}a_{j}(\widehat{\nu}\phi)(\xi-\ell(j)),

we have

\varphi(\xi-\ell(k))\widehat{f}(\xi)=a_{k}(\widehat{\nu}\phi)(\xi-\ell(k))

and

\displaystyle\varphi(D-k)f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{k}}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}}(\widehat{\nu}\phi)(\xi-\ell(k))e^{ix\xi}d\xi=a_{k}e^{ix\ell(k)}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\widehat{\nu}\phi)(x).

Therefore, we obtain

\|\varphi(D-k)f\|_{L^{p}}=|a_{k}|\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\widehat{\nu}\phi)\|_{L^{p}}=|a_{k}|\|\nu*({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi)\|_{L^{p}}.

On the other hand,
since
\nu=\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}b_{j}\delta_{N(j)}
,
we have

\displaystyle\|\nu*({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi)\|_{L^{p}}=\Big\|\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}b_{j}({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi)(\cdot-N(j))\Big\|_{L^{p}}.

Moreover, since
\{-N(j)+K_{\eta}\}
are mutually disjoint
and

\Big|A+\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}B_{j}\Big|^{p}\geq\frac{1}{2^{p}}|A|^{p}-\Big|\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}B_{j}\Big|^{p}\geq\frac{1}{2^{p}}|A|^{p}-2^{rp}\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}|B_{j}|^{p},

we obtain that

\displaystyle\Big|\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}b_{j}({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi)(x-N(j))\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}b_{j}\Big((\chi_{K_{\eta}}\cdot({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi))(x-N(j))+(\chi_{{\mathbf{R}}\setminus K_{\eta}}\cdot({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi))(x-N(j))\Big|^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{p}}\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}|b_{j}|^{p}|(\chi_{K_{\eta}}\cdot({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi))(x-N(j))|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad-2^{rp}\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}|b_{j}|^{p}|(\chi_{{\mathbf{R}}\setminus K_{\eta}}\cdot({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi))(x-N(j))|^{p}.

Since
\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}|b_{j}|^{p}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nu*({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi)\|_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}|b_{j}|^{p}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{p}}\int_{\mathbf{R}}|(\chi_{K_{\eta}}\cdot({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi))(x-N(j))|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\quad-2^{rp}\int_{\mathbf{R}}|(\chi_{{\mathbf{R}}\setminus K_{\eta}}\cdot({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi))(x-N(j))|^{p}dx\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum^{2^{r}}_{j=1}|b_{j}|^{p}\Big(\frac{1}{2^{p}}\int_{K_{\eta}}|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi(x)|^{p}dx-2^{rp}\int_{{\mathbf{R}}\setminus K_{\eta}}|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi(x)|^{p}dx\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{p}}\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{p}}^{p}-(1+2^{rp})\eta.

Putting
\eta=\frac{1}{2^{p+1}(1+2^{rp})}\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{p}}^{p}
,
we obtain

\|\nu*({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi)\|_{L^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{p}}.

Hence we have by
\|\mu\|_{M({\mathbf{R}})}=\sum^{2^{m}}_{k=1}|a_{k}|=1

that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,1}}=\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}}\|\phi(D-k)f\|_{L^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}|\Big)\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{1}}=\frac{1}{2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{1}}.

Step 2.

Secondly, we show that
\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}
.
Since
\widehat{f}=\mu*(\widehat{\nu}\phi)
,

\|\widehat{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\varepsilon

and
\mu=\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}a_{j}\delta_{\ell(j)}

, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}|a_{j}|\|(\widehat{\nu}\phi)(\cdot-\ell(j))\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\widehat{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}|a_{j}|<\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{1}}.

Step 3.

Thirdly, we show that
\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{p}}
.
We note that

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\mu*(\widehat{\nu}\phi))(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Big(\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}a_{j}(\widehat{\nu}\phi)(\cdot-\ell(j))\Big)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}a_{j}\Big(\int_{\mathbf{R}}\widehat{\nu}(\xi-\ell(j))\phi(\xi-\ell(j))e^{ix\xi}d\xi\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum^{2^{m}}_{j=1}a_{j}e^{ix\ell(j)}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\widehat{\nu}\phi)(x)

\displaystyle=(\widehat{\nu}(-x))((\mu*({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi))(x)).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mu*({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi)\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\widehat{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nu\|_{M({\mathbf{R}})}\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{p}}.

Step 4.
We note that if
M^{p,1}({\mathbf{R}})={\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})

set-theoretically, then the closed graph theorem implies that the norms
on both spaces are equivalent.
Therefore we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|f\|_{M^{p,1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}\hskip-2.27626ptA_{p}({\mathbf{R}})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}+\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\varepsilon(\|\phi\|_{L^{p}}+\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\phi\|_{L^{1}}).

Since
\phi\not=0
, this is impossible.
Hence we obtain the desired result.

(ii)
Finally, we consider the case

.
Set

(k\in{\mathbf{N}})

and define

where

,

(if

(\xi\in{\mathbf{R}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbf{Z}}}\|\varphi(\cdot-k)\widehat{f}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\sum_{k=e^{10}}^{\infty}\frac{2}{k(\log_{e}k)^{2}}<\infty

and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}}\approx\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}}=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=e^{10}}\frac{|I_{k}|}{k^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=e^{10}}\frac{2}{k^{2}(\log_{e}k)^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

.
Hence we obtain

.

Classification of generalized Alexander quandles
Akihiro Higashitani, Seiichi Kamada, Jin Kosaka, Hirotake Kurihara

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to provide a new characterization of isomorphism classes of generalized Alexander quandles in terms of the underlying groups and their automorphisms. This extends the previous result [ 4 , Theorem 1.4] . Additionally, we compute the number of generalized Alexander quandles up to quandle isomorphism arising from groups up to order 127 127 and their group automorphisms.



1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation

Quandles
[
6
,
7
]
are algebraic systems equipped with three axioms that correspond to Reidemeister moves in knot theory.
According to Joyce, any quandle can be expressed by a disjoint union of homogeneous quandles (
[
6
, Theorem 7.2]
).
Here, we say that a quandle
Q
is
homogeneous
if the automorphism group of
Q
acts transitively on
Q
.
Furthermore, it has been shown that any homogeneous quandle can be constructed by a so-called
quandle triplet

(G,~H,~\psi)
(
[
6
, Theorem 7.1]
).

The primary focus of this paper is a special kind of homogeneous quandles called
generalized Alexander quandles
, which are quandles arising from groups.
Alexander quandles, which are generalized Alexander quandles arising from abelian groups, have been extensively studied in the context of knot theory.
Finite Alexander quandles have been classified up to quandle isomorphism by Nelson
[
8
]
.

Recently, the first named and fourth named authors established a kind of characterization theorem of finite generalized Alexander quandles of finite groups under certain assumptions
(P1)
and
(P2)
(detailed in Section
3
.)
The main objective of this paper is to extend the result of
[
4
]
.

1.2
Main results

The following is the main theorem of this paper:

Theorem 1.1

( Main Result )
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be groups with their identity elements
e
and
e^{\prime}
, respectively.
Let
\psi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G),~\psi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G^{\prime})
and let

Q=(Q(G,\psi),*),~Q^{\prime}=(Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}),*^{\prime}),~P=P(Q)\text{ and }P^{\prime}=P(Q^{\prime}).

Then the following conditions
(1)
and
(2)
are equivalent:

(1)

Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are isomorphic as quandles.

(2)

There exists a group isomorphism
h:P\rightarrow P^{\prime}
such that the following two conditions
(A)
and
(B)
are satisfied:

(A)

h\circ\psi|_{P}=\psi^{\prime}|_{P^{\prime}}\circ h
;

(B)

there exist representatives
A\subset G
and
A^{\prime}\subset G^{\prime}
of
G/P
and
G^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
, respectively, and a bijection
k:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
between
A
and
A^{\prime}
such that
h(e*a)=e^{\prime}*^{\prime}k(a)
holds for any
a\in A
.

The notation used in this paper will be explained in Sections
2
and
3
.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in Section
4
.

Remark 1.1
.

The statement of Theorem
1.1
does not assume the finiteness of groups.

By
[
4
]
, generalized Alexander quandles of finite groups up to order
15
are completely classified.
An observation of those of order
16
is also made, but the main result of
[
4
]
is not applicable for two cases.
By Theorem
1.1
, we can completely classify not only all generalized Alexander quandles of finite groups of order
16
, but also those with orders at most
127
(with some additional exceptional treatments).
See Section
5
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we prepare notation and recall notions on quandles and groups used throughout the paper.

2.1
Definition of quandles

First, let us recall a definition of quandle.
A non-empty set
Q
equipped with a binary operation
*
satisfying the following three axioms is called a
quandle
:

(Q1)

x*x=x
for any
x\in Q
;

(Q2)

for any
x\in Q
, the map defined by
s_{x}:Q\ni y\mapsto y*x\in Q
is bijective;

(Q3)

for any
x,y,z\in Q
, we have
(x*y)*z=(x*z)*(y*z)
.

The map appearing in (Q2) is called a
point symmetry
.

2.2
Homogeneous quandles and generalized Alexander quandles

Let
G
be a group and let
\psi
be an automorphism of
G
.
Moreover, we take a subgroup
H
of
G
satisfying
\psi(h)=h
for any
h\in H
.
We define a binary operation
*
on the set of left cosets
G/H
defined by
xH*yH:=y\psi(y^{-1}x)H
.
Then this binary operation becomes well-defined and
(G/H,*)
gives us a quandle.
The triplet
(G,H,\psi)
providing a new quandle
(G/H,*)
is called a
quandle triplet
, and we denote this quandle by
Q(G,H,\psi)
.
We notice that for any group
G
with its identity element
e
and any automorphism
\psi
of
G
,
the triplet
(G,\{e\},\psi)
always becomes a quandle triplet.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and let
\psi
be its automorphism.
Considering a binary operation
*
on
G
defined by
x*y:=y\psi(y^{-1}x)
, we obtain a quandle
(G,*)
, denoted by
Q(G,\psi)
or
(Q(G,\psi),*)
.
We call this quandle
Q(G,\psi)
a
generalized Alexander quandle
(or a
principal quandle
, see
[
2
]
).

In the case where
G
is abelian, we call
Q(G,\psi)
an
Alexander quandle
,
which has been studied especially in the context of knot theory.
Generalized Alexander quandles can be regarded as a generalization of it.

2.3
Notation and terminologies on quandles and groups

In what follows, we use the notation
(Q,*)
for quandles if we would like to emphasize the equipped binary operation; otherwise, we simply write
Q
.

Let
(Q,*)
and
(Q^{\prime},*^{\prime})
be quandles.
A map
f:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
is said to be a
quandle homomorphism
if
f(x*y)=f(x)*^{\prime}f(y)
holds for any
x,y\in Q
.
In addition to this, if
f
is bijective, then we call
f
a
quandle isomorphism
.
If there exists a quandle isomorphism between quandles
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
, then we say that
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are isomorphic as quandles, which we denote by
Q\cong Q^{\prime}
.

•

Let
{\mathrm{Aut}}(Q)
be the group consisting of all quandle automorphisms on
Q
, which we call the
automorphism group
of
Q
.
Note that the point symmetry
s_{x}
for each
x\in Q
belongs to
{\mathrm{Aut}}(Q)
.

•

Let
{\mathrm{Inn}}(Q)
be the subgroup of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(Q)
generated by
\{s_{x}:x\in Q\}
, which we call the
inner automorphism group
of
Q
.
More explicitly, we have
{\mathrm{Inn}}(Q)=\{s_{x_{n}}^{\pm 1}\circ s_{x_{n-1}}^{\pm 1}\circ\cdots\circ s_{x_{1}}^{\pm 1}:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in Q\}
.
In the case where
Q
is finite, we have
{\mathrm{Inn}}(Q)=\{{\mathrm{id}}_{Q},s_{x_{n}}\circ s_{x_{n-1}}\circ\cdots\circ s_{x_{1}}:n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in Q\}
.

We say that a quandle
Q
is
homogeneous

if the automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}(Q)
of
Q
acts on
Q
transitively.
According to
[
6
, Section 7]
, the following one-to-one correspondence is known:

\displaystyle\{\text{homogeneous quandles}\}/\cong\;\;\overset{1:1}{\longleftrightarrow}\;\;\{Q(G,H,\psi):(G,H,\psi)\text{ is a quandle triplet}\}/\cong,

where
/\cong
stands for that we consider this correspondence up to quandle isomorphism.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
be homogeneous quandles which are isomorphic.
Then, for any
x\in Q
and
x^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}
, we can always find a quandle isomorphism
f:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
with
f(x)=x^{\prime}
.
In fact, even if
f(x)=x^{\prime\prime}\neq x^{\prime}
, since
{\mathrm{Aut}}(Q^{\prime})
acts on
Q^{\prime}
transitively,
we can find
g\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(Q^{\prime})
with
g(x^{\prime\prime})=x^{\prime}
, so we may take an isomorphism
g\circ f
between
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
instead of
f
.

Therefore, for any generalized Alexander quandles
Q(G,\psi)
and
Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
which are isomorphic,
we may assume the existence of a quandle isomorphism
f:Q(G,\psi)\rightarrow Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
with
f(e)=e^{\prime}
,
where
G
and
G^{\prime}
are groups with their identity elements
e
and
e^{\prime}
, respectively.

We also collect some terminologies and notation concerning with groups. Let
G
be a group.

•

We denote the automorphism group of
G
by
{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
. Remark that this notation is the same as that for quandles.

•

Given
\psi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
, let
{\mathrm{Fix}}(\psi,G)=\{g\in G:\psi(g)=g\}
.

•

Let
N,H
be groups and let
\sigma:H\rightarrow{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
,
h\mapsto\sigma_{h}
be a group homomorphism.
The (outer)
semidirect product

N\rtimes_{\sigma}H
of
N
and
H
with respect to
\sigma
is a group
which is the direct product
N\times H
as a set equipped with the operation defined by
(n_{1},h_{1})(n_{2},h_{2})=(n_{1}\sigma_{h_{1}}(n_{2}),h_{1}h_{2})
.

2.4
Problem

Given a group
G
, let
{\mathcal{Q}}(G)
denote the set of all generalized Alexander quandles
Q(G,\psi)
with
\psi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
up to quandle isomorphism. Namely,

{\mathcal{Q}}(G)=\{Q(G,\psi):\psi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)\}/\cong.

Problem 2.1

(
[
5
, Problem 3.5]
)
.

Determine
{\mathcal{Q}}(G)
for a given group
G
.

This problem has some certain partial answers in terms of the automorphism group of groups.
For example,
[
8
, Corollary 2.2]
claims that
{\mathcal{Q}}(G)
can be characterized in terms of just numerical information in the case where
G
is finite and abelian.
Moreover, it is proved in
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
that if
G
is a finite simple group, then

{\mathcal{Q}}(G)
one-to-one corresponds to the conjugacy classes of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
.
Using this, we can also get this one-to-one correspondence in the case of symmetric groups (
[
4
, Corollary 1.3]
), though it is not simple.
Theorem
1.1
asserts a new kind of complete solution of Problem
2.1
.

3
Previous results on generalized Alexander quandles

In this section, we recall some results on generalized Alexander quandles, especially from
[
4
]
.

Let
G
be a group,
\psi
an automorphism of
G
, and
Q=Q(G,\psi)
the generalized Alexander quandle.
We define
P=P(Q)
as the orbit of the identity element
e
in
Q
under the action of
{\mathrm{Inn}}(Q)
, i.e.,

P=P(Q)=\{s_{x_{n}}^{\pm 1}\circ s_{x_{n-1}}^{\pm 1}\circ\cdots\circ s_{x_{1}}^{\pm 1}(e):n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in Q\}.

Then
P
enjoys a special property. In particular, the following statements hold:

Proposition 3.1

(
[
4
, Propositions 3.2 and 3.3]
)
.

The subset
P
of
G
is a subquandle of
Q
and a normal subgroup of
G
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
4
, Theorem 3.10]
,
[
9
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be groups with their identity elements
e
and
e^{\prime}
, respectively.
Let
\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G),~\psi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G^{\prime})
and let

Q=Q(G,\psi),~Q^{\prime}=Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}),~P=P(Q)\text{ and }P^{\prime}=P(Q^{\prime}).

Assume that there is a quandle isomorphism
f:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
with
f(e)=e^{\prime}
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

f\circ\psi=\psi^{\prime}\circ f
;

(ii)

f|_{P}:P\rightarrow P^{\prime}
is a group isomorphism;

(iii)

f|_{P}:Q(P,\psi|_{P})\rightarrow Q(P^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}|_{P^{\prime}})
is a quandle isomorphism;

(iv)

f(xP)=f(x)P^{\prime}
holds for any
x\in G
.

Remark 3.1
.

In
[
4
, Theorem 3.10]
, the finiteness of groups is assumed, but Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
3.1
do not need this assumption.
In fact, even in the case of infinite groups, we see that
Proposition
3.1
and the statements of (i), (iii) and (iv) of Theorem
3.1
hold by almost the same proofs with those given in
[
4
]
.
Moreover, the statement (ii) for (non-necessarily finite) groups is nothing but
[
9
, Lemma 2.4]
.

The following proposition is useful for study of generalized Alexander quandles up to isomorphism. It asserts that
a conjugacy class of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
determines an element of
{\mathcal{Q}}(G)
.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
4
, Proposition 3.5]
)
.

Let
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
and let
Q=Q(G,\psi)
and
Q^{\prime}=Q(G,\psi^{\prime})
.
If
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
are conjugate in
{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
, then
Q\cong Q^{\prime}
holds.

Now, we recall the main result of
[
4
]
. For this, we have to introduce more notions.
Given a group
G
and its automorphism
\psi
, let
Q=Q(G,\psi)
and
P=P(Q)
. We can consider a generalized Alexander quandle
Q(P,\psi|_{P})
.
Let
P^{2}
be
P(Q(P,\psi|_{P}))
.
By Proposition
3.1
,
P^{2}
is a normal subgroup of
P
.

We consider the following two properties (P1) and (P2).

(P1)

P^{2}
is a normal subgroup of
G
.

(P2)

P^{2}=\{s_{p}(e):p\in P\}
holds.

These two properties are independent each other and become invariants on generalized Alexander quandles (see
[
4
, Proposition 3.16]
).

Theorem 3.2

(
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be finite groups,
\psi\in\mathrm{Aut}(G)
,
\psi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Aut}(G^{\prime})
and let

Q=(Q(G,\psi),*),~Q^{\prime}=(Q(G^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}),*^{\prime}),~P=P(Q)\text{ and }P^{\prime}=P(Q^{\prime}).

Assume that
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
satisfy (P1) and (P2). Then the following conditions (1) and (2) are equivalent:

(1)

Q\cong Q^{\prime}
.

(2)

The following three conditions are satisfied:

(i)

|G|=|G^{\prime}|
;

(ii)

|{\mathrm{Fix}}(\psi,G)|=|{\mathrm{Fix}}(\psi^{\prime},G^{\prime})|
;

(iii)

there exists a group isomorphism
h:P\rightarrow P^{\prime}
such that the following
(A)
and
(B)
are satisfied:

(A)

h\circ\psi|_{P}=\psi^{\prime}|_{P^{\prime}}\circ h
;

(B)

for any
a\in G
, there exists
a^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
such that
h(e*a)=e^{\prime}*^{\prime}a^{\prime}
.

Note that the finiteness of the groups
G
and
G^{\prime}
is necessary in this theorem since we have to take the orders of the groups into account,
while Theorem
1.1
does require neither the finiteness condition on
G
and
G^{\prime}
nor the conditions (P1) and (P2) on
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

This section is devoted to proving Theorem
1.1
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2):

Let
f
be a quandle isomorphism
f:Q\rightarrow Q^{\prime}
with
f(e)=e^{\prime}
. (See Remark
2.1
.)
Let
h
be the restriction of
f
into
P
, i.e.,
h=f|_{P}:P\rightarrow P^{\prime}
.
Then, by Theorem
3.1
(i) and (ii), we see that the condition (A) is satisfied.

Take an arbitrary representative
A\subset G
of
G/P
, let
A^{\prime}=f(A)

and let
k
be the restriction of
f
into
A
, i.e.,
k=f|_{A}:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
.
Then it follows from the bijectivity of
f
(i.e.,
k
) together with Theorem
3.1
(iv) that
A^{\prime}
also becomes a representative of
G^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
.
Since

is an element of

h(e*a)=f(e*a)=f(e)*^{\prime}f(a)=e^{\prime}*^{\prime}k(a)
holds for any
a\in A
.
Hence, (B) is also satisfied.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1):

Let
h:P\rightarrow P^{\prime}
and

be a group isomorphism and a bijection between representatives

and
A^{\prime}

and

.
Since

Now, we define a map

by setting

f(x):=h(a_{x}p_{x}a_{x}^{-1})k(a_{x})\in G^{\prime}

for any

.
The bijectivity of

follows from that of

and

.
In what follows, we prove that

Since

and

Thus, we have the following:

\displaystyle f(x*y)

\displaystyle=h(a_{x*y}p_{x*y}a_{x*y}^{-1})k(a_{x*y})

\displaystyle=h(a_{y}p_{y}a_{y}^{-1})k(a_{y})\psi^{\prime}(k(a_{y})^{-1}h(a_{y}p_{y}^{-1}a_{y}^{-1})h(a_{x}p_{x}a_{x}^{-1})k(a_{x}))

\displaystyle=h(a_{x}\psi(a_{x}^{-1}))^{-1}~k(a_{x})=h(\psi(a_{x})a_{x}^{-1})~k(a_{x}).

Thus, we have

f(x)*^{\prime}f(y)=h(a_{y}p_{y}a_{y}^{-1})~h(a_{y}~\psi(a_{y}^{-1}))~h\circ\psi(a_{y}p_{y}^{-1}a_{y}^{-1}a_{x}p_{x}a_{x}^{-1})~h(\psi(a_{x})a_{x}^{-1})~k(a_{x})=f(x*y).

Therefore, we conclude that
f
is a quandle isomorphism, as required.

5
Classification of generalized Alexander quandles arising from finite groups with small order

In this section, we apply Theorem
1.1
to obtain the complete classification of
generalized Alexander quandles arising from finite groups with small orders.

Given a positive integer
n
, let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathrm{GAQ}}(n):

\displaystyle=\{Q(G,\psi):G\text{ is a group of order }n,\;\psi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)\}/\cong.

What we will work in this section is precisely to determine
|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathrm{GAQ}}(n)|
for
n\leq 127
.

5.1
The case
n\leq 15

In
[
4
, Section 6]
, classification of generalized Alexander quandles arising from finite groups
G
is discussed.
For example, the cases where
|G|=p,2p
or
p^{2}
are investigated theoretically, where
p
is a prime.
Moreover, it follows from
[
4
, Corollary 1.7]
that any generalized Alexander quandles arising from cyclic groups of order
2n

can be realized as those arising from dihedral groups.
By additional case-by-case discussions, we can eventually get the complete classification of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathrm{GAQ}}(n)
for
n\leq 15
.

5.2
The case
n=16

Most parts of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathrm{GAQ}}(16)
are also discussed in
[
4
, Section 6]
,
although two cases
\{Q_{1},Q_{1}^{\prime}\}
and
\{Q_{2},Q_{2}^{\prime}\}
are remained to be open.
In this subsection, we figure these cases out by using Theorem
1.1
.

Let
Q_{8}
be the quaternion group

\langle\overline{e},\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}:\overline{e}^{2}=e,\mathbf{i}^{2}=\mathbf{j}^{2}=\mathbf{k}^{2}=\mathbf{i}\mathbf{j}\mathbf{k}=\overline{e}\rangle
.
We denote the elements

e
,
\mathbf{i}
,
\mathbf{j}
,
\mathbf{k}
,

\overline{e}
,
\overline{e}\mathbf{i}
,
\overline{e}\mathbf{j}
,

\overline{e}\mathbf{k}
by

1,\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k},-1,-\mathbf{i},-\mathbf{j},-\mathbf{k}
, respectively, so that

Q_{8}=\{1,\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k},-1,-\mathbf{i},-\mathbf{j},-\mathbf{k}\}
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
be the group of order
2
.
Let
\sigma:\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Aut}}(Q_{8})
be defined by
i\mapsto\sigma_{i}
, where

\sigma_{\bar{0}}={\mathrm{id}}_{Q_{8}}
and
\sigma_{\bar{1}}:(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})\mapsto(\mathbf{i},-\mathbf{j},-\mathbf{k})
.
Then
\sigma_{i}
can be written as the inner automorphism
\sigma_{i}(a)=\mathbf{i}^{i}a\mathbf{i}^{-i}
for
a\in Q_{8}
.
Note that
\mathbf{i}^{i}a\mathbf{i}^{-i}
is well-defined on
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
We define the groups
G
and
G^{\prime}
as follows:

G=Q_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;G^{\prime}=Q_{8}\rtimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\psi
be the group automorphism of
Q_{8}

defined by
\psi(\mathbf{i})=\mathbf{j}
and
\psi(\mathbf{j})=\mathbf{k}
,
i.e.,
\psi
cyclically permutes
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k}
.
Moreover, we take the following maps
\psi_{1}
on
G
and
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
on
G^{\prime}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(a,i)=(\psi(a),i)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a,i)=(\psi(a\mathbf{i}^{i})\mathbf{i}^{-i},i).

Note that we have

\psi(a\mathbf{i}^{i})\mathbf{i}^{-i}=\begin{cases}\psi(a)&\text{ if }i=\bar{0},\\
\psi(a)\mathbf{k}&\text{ if }i=\bar{1}.\end{cases}

Obviously,
\psi_{1}
becomes a group automorphism of
G
of order
3
.
Now, we check that
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
becomes a group automorphism of
G^{\prime}
of order
3
.
Note that the injectivity of
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
is straightforward. So
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
is bijective.
For checking that
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
is a group homomorphism, we see that

\psi_{1}^{\prime}((a_{1},i_{1})\cdot(a_{2},i_{2}))=\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a_{1}\sigma_{i_{1}}(a_{2}),i_{1}+i_{2})=(\psi(a_{1}\sigma_{i_{1}}(a_{2})\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}+i_{2}})\mathbf{i}^{-(i_{1}+i_{2})},i_{1}+i_{2}).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a_{1},i_{1})\cdot\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a_{2},i_{2})

\displaystyle=(\psi(a_{1}\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}})\mathbf{i}^{-i_{1}},i_{1})\cdot(\psi(a_{2}\mathbf{i}^{i_{2}})\mathbf{i}^{-i_{2}},i_{2})

\displaystyle=(\psi(a_{1}\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}})\mathbf{i}^{-i_{1}}\sigma_{i_{1}}(\psi(a_{2}\mathbf{i}^{i_{2}})\mathbf{i}^{-i_{2}}),i_{1}+i_{2})

\displaystyle=(\psi(a_{1}\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}})\mathbf{i}^{-i_{1}}\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}}\psi(a_{2}\mathbf{i}^{i_{2}})\mathbf{i}^{-i_{2}}\mathbf{i}^{-i_{1}},i_{1}+i_{2})

\displaystyle=(\psi(a_{1}\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}})\psi(a_{2}\mathbf{i}^{i_{2}})\mathbf{i}^{-(i_{1}+i_{2})},i_{1}+i_{2})

\displaystyle=(\psi(a_{1}\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}}a_{2}\mathbf{i}^{i_{2}})\mathbf{i}^{-(i_{1}+i_{2})},i_{1}+i_{2})

\displaystyle=(\psi(a_{1}\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}}a_{2}\mathbf{i}^{-i_{1}}\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}+i_{2}})\mathbf{i}^{-(i_{1}+i_{2})},i_{1}+i_{2})

\displaystyle=(\psi(a_{1}\sigma_{i_{1}}(a_{2})\mathbf{i}^{i_{1}+i_{2}})\mathbf{i}^{-(i_{1}+i_{2})},i_{1}+i_{2}).

These imply
\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a_{1},i_{1})\cdot\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a_{2},i_{2})=\psi_{1}^{\prime}((a_{1},i_{1})\cdot(a_{2},i_{2}))
.
We can also check that the order of
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
is
3
. In fact,

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{\prime 3}(a,i)=\psi_{1}^{\prime}\circ\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\psi(a\mathbf{i}^{i})\mathbf{i}^{-i},i)=\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\psi^{2}(a\mathbf{i}^{i})\mathbf{i}^{-i},i)=(\psi^{3}(a\mathbf{i}^{i})\mathbf{i}^{-i},i)=(a\mathbf{i}^{i}\mathbf{i}^{-i},i)=(a,i).

Let
Q_{1}=(Q(G,\psi_{1}),*)
and
Q_{1}^{\prime}=(Q(G^{\prime},\psi_{1}^{\prime}),*^{\prime})
be the generalized Alexander quandles.

For
Q_{1}
, since
(1,\bar{0})*(a,i)=(a,i)\psi_{1}(a^{-1},i)=(a,i)(\psi(a^{-1}),i)=(a\psi(a^{-1}),\bar{0})
,
we have
P(Q_{1})\subset Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}
.
Moreover, since we know
\mathbf{j}\psi(\mathbf{j}^{-1})=-\mathbf{i}
and
\mathbf{k}\psi(\mathbf{k}^{-1})=-\mathbf{j}

and
Q_{8}
is generated by
-\mathbf{i}
and
-\mathbf{j}
, we have
P(Q_{1})=Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}
.
In a similar way, we can also obtain
P(Q_{1}^{\prime})=Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}
.
Note that
P(Q_{1}^{\prime})
is not only
Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}
as a set, but has the group structure
Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}
as a direct product.
Thus both of the coset decompositions of
G/P(Q_{1})
and
G^{\prime}/P(Q_{1}^{\prime})
are
Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}\sqcup Q_{8}\times\{\bar{1}\}
.
Since neither of these two quandles
Q_{1}
and
Q_{1}^{\prime}
satisfy (P2),
we cannot use Theorem
3.2
to determine whether
Q_{1}
and
Q_{1}^{\prime}
are isomorphic. In the following, we use Theorem
1.1
to show that
Q_{1}
and
Q_{1}^{\prime}
are isomorphic.

Now we construct the two maps
h
and
k
in Theorem
1.1
for
Q_{1}
and
Q_{1}^{\prime}
.
Since
P(Q_{1})=P(Q_{1}^{\prime})=Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}
as sets,
we can define the map
h\colon P(Q_{1})\to P(Q_{1}^{\prime})
to be the identity map.
Obviously, the condition (A) of Theorem
1.1
can be verified.
We take
A=\{(1,\bar{0}),(1,\bar{1})\}
and
A^{\prime}=\{(1,\bar{0}),(-\mathbf{i},\bar{1})\}

as the coset representatives of
G/P(Q_{1})
and
G^{\prime}/P(Q_{1}^{\prime})
,
respectively.
Then we define the map
k\colon A\to A^{\prime}
by

k(1,\bar{0})=(1,\bar{0})
and
k(1,\bar{1})=(-\mathbf{i},\bar{1})
.
Now we check the condition (B) of Theorem
1.1
.
For
(1,\bar{0})\in A
, we have

h((1,\bar{0})*(1,\bar{0}))=h(1,\bar{0})=(1,\bar{0})=(1,\bar{0})*^{\prime}k((1,\bar{0})).

For
(1,\bar{1})\in A
, we have

h((1,\bar{0})*(1,\bar{1}))=h((1,\bar{0}))=(1,\bar{0})

and

\displaystyle(1,\bar{0})*^{\prime}k(1,\bar{1})=(1,\bar{0})*^{\prime}(-\mathbf{i},\bar{1})=(-\mathbf{i},\bar{1})\psi_{1}^{\prime}((-\mathbf{i},\bar{1})^{-1})=(-\mathbf{i},\bar{1})(\mathbf{i},\bar{1})=(-\mathbf{i}^{2},\bar{0})=(1,\bar{0}).

Hence the condition (B) of Theorem
1.1
is also verified.
Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
, we have
Q_{1}\cong Q_{1}^{\prime}
.

Let
\tau\colon Q_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to Q_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
be the map defined by
\tau(a,i)=((-1)^{i}a,i)
.
We can check that
\tau
is
both of group automorphisms of
G
and
G^{\prime}
.
Let
\psi_{2}=\tau\circ\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}^{\prime}=\tau\circ\psi_{1}^{\prime}
.
Then both of the orders
\psi_{2}
and
\psi_{2}^{\prime}
are
6
.

Let
Q_{2}
and
Q_{2}^{\prime}
be the generalized Alexander quandles

(Q(G,\psi_{2}),*)
and
(Q(G^{\prime},\psi_{2}^{\prime}),*^{\prime})
, respectively.

We construct
h
and
k
in Theorem
1.1
to show

Q_{2}\cong Q_{2}^{\prime}
.

We can check
P(Q_{2})=P(Q_{2}^{\prime})=Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}
.
Let
h:P(Q_{2})\to P(Q_{2}^{\prime})
be the identity map on
Q_{8}\times\{\bar{0}\}
. The condition (A) of Theorem
1.1
is satisfied.
We take
A=\{(1,\bar{0}),(1,\bar{1})\}
and
A^{\prime}=\{(1,\bar{0}),(\mathbf{i},\bar{1})\}

as the coset representatives of
G/P(Q_{2})
and
G^{\prime}/P(Q_{2}^{\prime})
,
respectively.
Then we define the map
k\colon A\to A^{\prime}
by

k(1,\bar{0})=(1,\bar{0})
and
k(1,\bar{1})=(\mathbf{i},\bar{1})
.
Now we verify the condition (B) of Theorem
1.1
.
For
(1,\bar{0})\in A
, we have

h((1,\bar{0})*(1,\bar{0}))=h(1,\bar{0})=(1,\bar{0})=(1,\bar{0})*^{\prime}k((1,\bar{0})).

For
(1,\bar{1})\in A
, we have

h((1,\bar{0})*(1,\bar{1}))=h((1,\bar{1})\psi_{2}((1,\bar{1})^{-1}))=h((1,\bar{1})\psi_{2}((1,\bar{1})))=h((-1,\bar{1}+\bar{1}))=(-1,\bar{0})

and

\displaystyle(1,\bar{0})*^{\prime}k(1,\bar{1})=(1,\bar{0})*^{\prime}(\mathbf{i},\bar{1})=(\mathbf{i},\bar{1})\psi_{2}^{\prime}((\mathbf{i},\bar{1})^{-1})=(\mathbf{i},\bar{1})(\mathbf{i},\bar{1})=(-1,\bar{0}).

Hence the condition (B) of Theorem
1.1
is satisfied.
Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
, we have
Q_{2}\cong Q_{2}^{\prime}
.

Remark 5.1
.

For a quandle
Q=(Q,*)
and a positive integer
n
, we can construct a quandle
Q^{(n)}=(Q,*^{(n)})
by
x*^{(n)}y=s_{y}^{n}(x)
where
s_{y}
is the point symmetry at
y
for the quandle
(Q,*)
.
If two qundles
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are isomorphic, then
Q^{(n)}
and
Q^{\prime(n)}
are isomorphic (see
[
5
, Section 4]
).
For a generalized Alexander quandle
Q=Q(G,\psi)
, we have
Q^{(n)}=Q(G,\psi^{n})
.
For the quandles
Q_{1}
,
Q_{1}^{\prime}
,
Q_{2}
and
Q_{2}^{\prime}
above, we see that
Q_{2}^{(2)}\cong Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}^{\prime(2)}\cong Q_{1}^{\prime}
.
Thus, there is no need to verify
Q_{1}\cong Q_{1}^{\prime}
once
Q_{2}\cong Q_{2}^{\prime}
is verified. However, for the sake of clarity of argument, we verified
Q_{1}\cong Q_{1}^{\prime}
at first.

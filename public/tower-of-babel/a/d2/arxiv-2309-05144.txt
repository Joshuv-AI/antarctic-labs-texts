Geometry of entanglement and separability in Hilbert subspaces of dimension up to three
Rotem Liss, Tal Mor, Andreas Winter

Abstract
We present a complete classification of the geometry of the mutually complementary sets of entangled and separable states in three-dimensional Hilbert subspaces of bipartite and multipartite quantum systems. Our analysis begins by finding the geometric structure of the pure product states in a given three-dimensional Hilbert subspace, which determines all the possible separable and entangled mixed states over the same subspace. In bipartite systems, we characterise the 14 14 possible qualitatively different geometric shapes for the set of separable states in any three-dimensional Hilbert subspace ( 5 5 classes which also appear in two-dimensional subspaces and were found and analysed by Boyer, Liss and Mor [ Phys. Rev. A 95:032308, 2017 Boyer et al. 2017 ], and 9 9 novel classes which appear only in three-dimensional subspaces), describe their geometries, and provide figures illustrating them. We also generalise these results to characterise the sets of fully separable states (and hence the complementary sets of somewhat entangled states) in three-dimensional subspaces of multipartite systems. Our results show which geometrical forms quantum entanglement can and cannot take in low-dimensional subspaces.

Introduction

In the 89 years since the discovery of quantum entanglement
Einstein et al. 1935

and the realisation that it marks the main departure of quantum physics from any
form of classical explanation
Schrödinger 1935a
;
Schrödinger 1935b
;
Schrödinger 1935c

this quantum effect has been promoted
from a counter-intuitive foundational phenomenon

;
Ekert 1991
;

Indeed, quantum entanglement remains to this day the most studied
and most crucial feature separating non-classical information processing
from its classical counterpart
Horodecki et al. 2009
.

When classifying quantum entanglement, a major problem is deciding whether
a specific quantum state presents quantum entanglement (is “entangled”) or
does not present quantum entanglement (is “separable” or “non-entangled”).
It is well known that this general problem
is computationally hard (NP-hard)
Gurvits 2003
;
Gurvits 2004
;
Gharibian 2010
:
its hardness results from the fact that quantum separability
is a bilinear condition which, if it were easy to solve, would allow the
encoding of quadratic constraints into otherwise convex (semidefinite) problems,
which is known to give rise to computationally hard problems
Beigi and Shor 2007
;
Harrow and Montanaro 2013
.
However, the known hardness results only apply to the most general quantum states,
and in fact, it requires the consideration of highly mixed quantum
states whose density matrices have maximal ranks.
For example, any pure state (i.e., a rank-one quantum state)
is known to be entangled if and only if its partial trace (reduced state)
is not pure, a condition easy to check.
Formally, a pure state
\rho^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}

is separable if and only if its partial trace

\rho^{\mathrm{A}}\triangleq\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathrm{B}}\rho^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}
is pure,
which happens if and only if
\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{\mathrm{A}})^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{\mathrm{A}}=1
.

For quantum states whose rank is higher but still relatively small
(compared to the dimension of the full Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}}\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{B}}
),
analysing the reduced state is not enough, but separability
can still be efficiently detected in some cases.
First of all, if
\operatorname{rank}\rho^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}<\max\left\{\operatorname{rank}\rho^{\mathrm{A}},\operatorname{rank}\rho^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}

then the state
\rho^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}
is necessarily entangled
and in fact has distillable entanglement
Horodecki et al. 2003
.
If equality holds (
\operatorname{rank}\rho^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}=\max\left\{\operatorname{rank}\rho^{\mathrm{A}},\operatorname{rank}\rho^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}
),
or alternatively if
(\operatorname{rank}\rho^{\mathrm{A}})(\operatorname{rank}\rho^{\mathrm{B}})\leq 6
,
then the state
\rho^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}
is separable if and only if
it has a “positive partial transpose” (PPT), namely, if applying
the partial transpose operator
(\cdot)^{\mathrm{T}^{\mathrm{B}}}

to the density matrix
\rho^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}

results in a positive semidefinite matrix
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 2000
.
These results cover all bipartite density matrices of rank up to three.
If

the result of Chen and Djoković

shows that

\rho^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}
is separable if and only if it is PPT
and its support includes a non-zero product state.
Furthermore, for all ranks bounded away from the maximum
(concretely, for all ranks

Proof 4.1

If
\dim S_{\text{sep}}\leq 2
, then applying Theorem
1

to a two-dimensional subspace of
S
which includes
S_{\text{sep}}
proves that
the set
{\cal D}^{S_{\text{sep}}}_{\text{sep}}
of pure and mixed separable states over
S_{\text{sep}}

belongs to one of the five classes described in Theorem
1
.
All the other pure and mixed states over
S
(that are not states over
S_{\text{sep}}
)
must be entangled. Therefore,
{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}={\cal D}^{S_{\text{sep}}}_{\text{sep}}
indeed
belongs to one of the five classes described in Theorem
1
.

If
\dim S_{\text{sep}}=3
, then
S_{\text{sep}}=S
,
in which case we divide our proof into cases
according to the dimensions
(\dim A_{\text{sep}},\dim B_{\text{sep}})
:

4.1

(1,3) and (3,1)

The case (1,3) means that
\dim A_{\text{sep}}=1
, so
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}=|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}=|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
. Therefore,

\begin{split}S&={\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}\\
&=\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right\}\otimes{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}=\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right\}\otimes B_{\text{sep}},\end{split}

(15)

which means that all pure and mixed states over
S
are product states:

{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}={\cal D}(S)
, as needed.

The proof for the case (3,1) is symmetric,
so
S=A_{\text{sep}}\otimes\left\{|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}
and
{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}={\cal D}(S)
,
as needed.

4.2

(3,3)

In this case, all three states
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
are linearly independent, and so are
|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
.
Let
\left\{|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|3\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right\}

and
\left\{|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|3\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}

be orthonormal bases of
A_{\text{sep}}
and
B_{\text{sep}}
, respectively.
We can thus apply a local invertible map

X\otimes Y
, where
X
is an invertible matrix over
A_{\text{sep}}
mapping

|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\mapsto|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
,

|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\mapsto|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
, and

|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\mapsto|3\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
,
while
Y
is an invertible matrix over
B_{\text{sep}}
mapping

|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\mapsto|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
,

|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\mapsto|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
, and

|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\mapsto|3\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

(both maps exist, because there always exists an invertible matrix
mapping a given set of linearly independent states
into another given set of linearly independent states).

Since
S={\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}
,
all elements of
S
are superpositions

u|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+v|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+w|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
.
Under the above map, they are mapped to the respective superpositions

u|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+v|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+w|3\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|3\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
.

Since any local invertible map preserves separability,
we notice that each state in
S
is a product state if and only if
the state to which it is mapped is a product state.
However, since
u|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+v|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+w|3\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|3\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
is in its Schmidt form
and has a Schmidt number of
3
or less,
it is a product state if and only if two of its coefficients are zero
(otherwise, it is entangled).
Therefore, the only product states in
S

are the spanning states
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
.

This finding implies that the set of separable pure and mixed states over
S

includes exactly all mixtures of these three product states, so:

{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}=\operatorname{conv}\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}|^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes|\beta_{1}\rangle\!\langle\beta_{1}|^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{2}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}|^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes|\beta_{2}\rangle\!\langle\beta_{2}|^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{3}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}|^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes|\beta_{3}\rangle\!\langle\beta_{3}|^{\mathrm{B}}\right\},

(16)

as needed.

4.3

(2,3) and (3,2)

In the case (2,3) we can assume,
without loss of generality, that
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\neq|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
,
so
|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}=a|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}+b|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}

On the other hand,
|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

Using again local invertible linear maps, we can map

|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\mapsto|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}

|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\mapsto|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
,

|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\mapsto|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\mapsto|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\mapsto|3\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\mapsto a|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}+b|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}

\begin{split}u|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+v|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+w|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}&\mapsto u|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+v|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+wa|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|3\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+wb|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|3\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\\

v|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+wb|3\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

(i)

u=v=0
,
u=w=0
, and
v=w=0
. Therefore,
the only product states in
S
are the spanning states

|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
.
This implies, identically to case (3,3), that the only separable states

S
are the mixtures of these three product states, so:

{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}=\operatorname{conv}\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}|^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes|\beta_{1}\rangle\!\langle\beta_{1}|^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{2}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}|^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes|\beta_{2}\rangle\!\langle\beta_{2}|^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{3}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}|^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes|\beta_{3}\rangle\!\langle\beta_{3}|^{\mathrm{B}}\right\},

(18)

as needed.

(ii)

In this case, the resulting states

Therefore, the product states in

which are exactly all states in the Hilbert subspace

{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}=\operatorname{conv}\left[\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}|^{\mathrm{A}}\otimes|\beta_{1}\rangle\!\langle\beta_{1}|^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}\cup{\cal D}\left(\left\{|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right\}\otimes{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}\right)\right],

(19)

as needed.

The proof for the case (3,2) is symmetric.

4.4

(2,2)

We can assume, without loss of generality,

|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\neq|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}

and
|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\neq|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}=a|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}+b|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}

|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\mapsto|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

(i)

|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}=|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}

\displaystyle{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{conv}\left[{\cal D}(S_{1})\cup{\cal D}(S_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{conv}\left[{\cal D}\left(\left\{|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right\}\otimes{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}\right)\cup{\cal D}\left({\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right\}\otimes\left\{|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}\right)\right]

(ii)

are of the form:

\begin{split}u|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+v|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+w|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}&\mapsto x|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+y|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\\
&+z{\textstyle\frac{ad}{bc}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+z|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\\
&\propto x^{2}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+xy|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\\
&+xz{\textstyle\frac{ad}{bc}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+xz|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\\
&=x^{2}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+z^{2}{\textstyle\frac{ad}{bc}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\\
&+xz{\textstyle\frac{ad}{bc}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+xz|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\\
&=\left(x|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}+z|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right)\left(x|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+z{\textstyle\frac{ad}{bc}}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right).\end{split}

(25)

We can thus see that
S
includes an infinite number of product states,
all of the form
\gamma\left(x|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}+z|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right)\left(x|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+z\frac{ad}{bc}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right)

for some
x,z\in\mathbb{C}
and a normalisation factor
\gamma
.
Accordingly, we can define an invertible linear map

L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A_{\text{sep}}\mapsto B_{\text{sep}}
mapping:

\displaystyle L|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},

(26)

\displaystyle L|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ad}{bc}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},

(27)

and we conclude that the set of separable states in
S
is exactly:

\left\{|\Psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}\triangleq\gamma|\psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\left(L|\psi\rangle\right)^{\mathrm{B}}~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~|\psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\in A_{\text{sep}}~,~\gamma\in\mathbb{C}~,~\left\||\Psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}\right\|=1\right\}.

(28)

Thus, the separable pure and mixed states over
S

are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations) of the states
in Eq. (
28
). Formally:

{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}=\operatorname{conv}\left\{|\Psi\rangle\!\langle\Psi|^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~|\Psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}=\gamma|\psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\left(L|\psi\rangle\right)^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\in A_{\text{sep}}~,~\gamma\in\mathbb{C}~,~\left\||\Psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}\mathrm{B}}\right\|=1\right\},

(29)

as needed.

\begin{split}|\psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\psi\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}&=\frac{|1\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}+e^{i\varphi}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}}{\sqrt{2}}\otimes\frac{|1\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+e^{i\varphi}|2\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}}{\sqrt{2}}\\

6
Generalisation to multipartite systems

We can now generalise our result regarding bipartite systems
(Theorem
4.0
) to all multipartite systems.
In
Boyer et al. 2017
, the generalised classification
of
fully separable
states in two-dimensional subspaces
of multipartite systems (Theorem 11 in
Boyer et al. 2017
)
turned out to be completely identical to the classification of
separable states in two-dimensional subspaces of bipartite systems
(Theorem 9 in
Boyer et al. 2017
, stated in our paper as Theorem
1
).
However, in our paper, we will be able to find several differences
between the bipartite case and the multipartite case:
most notably, some bipartite classes disappear for multipartite systems.

Similarly to Section
2
,
we begin our analysis with a rank-3 quantum mixed state

\rho\triangleq\rho^{\mathrm{A}_{1}\cdots\mathrm{A}_{k}}
on a
k
-partite system

{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{1}}\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{k}}

(where
k\geq 3
).
As before, the support of
\rho
is the three-dimensional
subspace
S=\operatorname{supp}\rho
which is spanned by the eigenstates of
\rho
,
and we define:

\begin{split}S_{\text{sep}}&\triangleq{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\psi\rangle\in S~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~|\psi\rangle\text{ is a product state}\right\}\\
&={\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\psi\rangle\in S~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~\exists|\phi^{(1)}\rangle\in{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{1}}~,~\ldots~,~|\phi^{(k)}\rangle\in{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{k}}~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~|\psi\rangle=|\phi^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\phi^{(k)}\rangle\right\}.\end{split}

(44)

Since the cases
\dim S_{\text{sep}}\in\{0,1,2\}
were analysed in
Boyer et al. 2017
,
we focus again on the case
\dim S_{\text{sep}}=3
, where
S_{\text{sep}}=S

and it is spanned by three linearly independent product states:

S={\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{(k)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{3}^{(k)}\rangle\right\}.

(45)

We can now define the set of fully separable states we are going to analyse:

{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}\triangleq\{\rho\in{\cal D}(S)~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~\rho\text{ is fully separable}\},

(46)

where
{\cal D}(S)
is (as before) the set of all density matrices
over the Hilbert space
S
, and a state
\rho
is said to be “fully separable”
if it is a mixture of product states

|\phi^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\phi^{(k)}\rangle
.

Similarly to Theorem
4.0
, our analysis will depend on
the dimensions of the
k
local subspaces
A^{(j)}_{\text{sep}}
(for
1\leq j\leq k
),
which are defined as follows:

A^{(j)}_{\text{sep}}\triangleq{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}^{(j)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(j)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(j)}\rangle\right\},

(47)

and each of them can be
1
-,
2
-, or
3
-dimensional.

We would now like to point out that whenever the dimension of
A^{(j)}_{\text{sep}}

is
1
, it does not contribute any genuine entanglement and can be removed
from our analysis. Indeed, if
\dim A^{(j)}_{\text{sep}}=1
, then necessarily

|\alpha_{1}^{(j)}\rangle=|\alpha_{2}^{(j)}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{(j)}\rangle
,
in which case all states in
S
are actually tensor products of
a
(k-1)
-partite state with a constant state
|\alpha_{1}^{(j)}\rangle
.
This state can be trivially absorbed into another system without affecting
the analysis of entanglement and separability.
Thus, the state is
genuinely
only
(k-1)
-partite, not
k
-partite.
(A similar observation regarding the bipartite case of Theorem
4.0

is that systems corresponding to its cases (1,3) and (3,1)
are actually not bipartite at all, but “monopartite”.)
We can therefore assume, without loss of generality, that

\dim A^{(j)}_{\text{sep}}\in\{2,3\}
for all
1\leq j\leq k
,
thus focusing on
genuinely
multipartite systems with
k\geq 3
subsystems.

For the analysis, we shall also use the following Lemma,
which (informally) says that three quantum states that are either

linearly independent
or
linearly dependent but in general position

become linearly independent when we take their tensor product
with another (non-trivial) Hilbert space:

Lemma 6.1

In a bipartite Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}}\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{B}}
,
if three quantum states
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\in{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}}
are either
linearly independent

or
linearly dependent but in general position

(that is, any two of them are linearly independent),
then for any three quantum states
|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\in{\cal H}_{\mathrm{B}}

that are not all identical (up to normalisation and global phase),
the three states
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\in{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}}\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{B}}

are
linearly independent
.

Proof 6.2

Assume by contradiction that the three states

|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}

are linearly dependent. They must be in general position,
because any two of them cannot be equal
(otherwise, two of
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
would be equal).
Therefore, without loss of generality,

|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\in{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right\}
,
so there are
a,b\in{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{0\}
such that:

|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}=a|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+b|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}.

(48)

This equation means, in particular, that
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\in{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}}
cannot be linearly independent,
so they must be linearly dependent and, by assumption, in general position;
this observation implies that
|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\in{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right\}
,
so there are
x,y\in{{\mathbb{C}}}\setminus\{0\}
such that:

|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}=x|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}+y|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}},

(49)

and substituting this in Eq. (
48
), we find:

\left(x|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}+y|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\right)|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}=a|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}+b|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},

(50)

or equivalently,

|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\left(x|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}-a|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right)=|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\left(b|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}-y|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\right).

(51)

Because we know that
|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}\neq|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}
,
this necessarily implies
x|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}-a|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}=0

and
b|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}-y|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}=0
.
Because we know that
a,b,x,y\neq 0
, it holds that:

\frac{a}{x}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}=|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}=\frac{b}{y}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},

(52)

which contradicts the assumption that the three quantum states

|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}},|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}\in{\cal H}_{\mathrm{B}}

are not all identical (up to normalisation and global phase).
Thus, the three bipartite states mentioned above,

|\alpha_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{1}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}~,~|\alpha_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\beta_{3}\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
,
must be linearly independent, as we wanted.

For the full analysis, we use the following notations:
the set of integer numbers from
1
to
k

is denoted by
[k]
(formally,
[k]\triangleq\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
);
and for any subset
L\subseteq[k]
and
1\leq i\leq 3
, the product of all
states
|\alpha_{i}^{(j)}\rangle
for all
j\in L
is denoted
|\alpha_{i}^{(L)}\rangle

(formally,
|\alpha_{i}^{(L)}\rangle\triangleq\bigotimes_{j\in L}|\alpha_{i}^{(j)}\rangle
).
Similarly, for all
L\subseteq[k]
we define the generalised local set

A^{(L)}_{\text{sep}}
as the span of all states
|\alpha_{i}^{(L)}\rangle
:

A^{(L)}_{\text{sep}}\triangleq{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}^{(L)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(L)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(L)}\rangle\right\}.

(53)

We can now present the Theorem which classifies
all three-dimensional subspaces of genuinely multipartite
systems (
k
-partite systems with
k\geq 3
, where none of the local subsystems
is one-dimensional) into two general cases,
corresponding to Fig.
2
(a triangle) and
Fig.
3
(a spherical cone):

Theorem 6.2

Given a multipartite Hilbert space

{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{1}}\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{k}}

and any three-dimensional subspace

S\subseteq{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{1}}\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{k}}
,
let
S_{\text{sep}}
be the subspace spanned by all product states in
S

(that is,
S_{\text{sep}}\triangleq{\operatorname{span}}\{|\psi\rangle\in S~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~|\psi\rangle\text{ is a product state}\}
).
We would like to characterise the set of all fully separable states
(both pure and mixed) over
S
, a set we denote by
{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}

and formally define as follows:

{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}\triangleq\{\rho\in{\cal D}(S)~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~\rho\text{ is fully separable}\}.

(54)

If
\dim S_{\text{sep}}\leq 2
, then
{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}

belongs to one of the five classes described in Theorem 11 of
Boyer et al. 2017

(or, equivalently, to one of the five multipartite generalisations
of the classes described in Theorem
1
).

If
\dim S_{\text{sep}}=3
(so
S_{\text{sep}}=S
),
then using the notations of Eqs. (
45
)
and (
47
):

\begin{split}S&={\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{(k)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{3}^{(k)}\rangle\right\},\\
A^{(j)}_{\text{sep}}&\triangleq{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{1}^{(j)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(j)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(j)}\rangle\right\},\end{split}

(55)

and assuming, without loss of generality (as explained above),
that
\dim A^{(j)}_{\text{sep}}\in\{2,3\}
for all
1\leq j\leq k
,
one of the two following cases occurs:

(i)

Triangle:

The fully separable pure and mixed states over
S

are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations)
of
|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|
,

|\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}^{(k)}|
,
and
|\alpha_{3}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{3}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}^{(k)}|
;
all the other pure and mixed states over
S
are entangled.
Formally:

\begin{split}{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}=\operatorname{conv}&\left\{|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|~,~|\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}^{(k)}|~,\right.\\
&~~\left.|\alpha_{3}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{3}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}^{(k)}|\right\}.\end{split}

(56)

This case is a generalisation of the bipartite cases (3,3), (2,3)-i,
and (3,2)-i in Theorem
4.0
,
and it is illustrated in Fig.
2
.

(ii)

Spherical cone:

There exists a subsystem
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{A}_{\ell}}

(namely, there exists an index
1\leq\ell\leq k
)
such that out of the three states

|\alpha_{1}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle
,
two states are equal (without loss of generality,
we can assume
|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle
),
and the fully separable pure and mixed states over
S

are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations) of

|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|

with any pure or mixed state over the space

{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle\right\}
;
all the other pure and mixed states over
S
are entangled.
Formally, in case
|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle
:

\begin{split}{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}=&\operatorname{conv}\left[\left\{|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|\right\}\right.\\
&\left.~~~~\cup~{\cal D}\left({\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle\right\}\right)\right],\end{split}

(57)

and symmetric results are obtained
in case
|\alpha_{1}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle

or
|\alpha_{1}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle
.
This case is a generalisation of the bipartite case (3,2)-ii
(and its symmetric case (2,3)-ii) in Theorem
4.0
,
and it is illustrated in Fig.
3
.

Remark 6.3

We point out that there is
no
generalisation of neither bipartite cases
(2,2)-i and (2,2)-ii of Theorem
4.0
.
Thus, both cases (2,2)-i and (2,2)-ii
(illustrated in Figs.
4

and
5
, respectively) exist in bipartite systems
but do not exist in (genuinely) multipartite systems.

Proof 6.4

If
\dim S_{\text{sep}}\leq 2
, then applying Theorem 11 of
Boyer et al. 2017

to a two-dimensional subspace of
S
which includes
S_{\text{sep}}
proves that
the set
{\cal D}^{S_{\text{sep}}}_{\text{sep}}
of pure and mixed separable states over
S_{\text{sep}}

belongs to one of the five classes described in
Boyer et al. 2017

for multipartite systems.
All the other pure and mixed states over
S
(that are not states over
S_{\text{sep}}
)
must be entangled (that is, they cannot be fully separable).
Therefore,
{\cal D}^{S}_{\text{sep}}={\cal D}^{S_{\text{sep}}}_{\text{sep}}
indeed
belongs to one of the five classes described in
Boyer et al. 2017

for two-dimensional subspaces of multipartite systems,
which are direct generalisations of the classes
described in
Boyer et al. 2017
(or in our Theorem
1
)
for two-dimensional subspaces of bipartite systems.

If
\dim S_{\text{sep}}=3
, then
S_{\text{sep}}=S
,
in which case the proof typically applies Theorem
4.0
to
S

under specific bipartite
partitions
(or
cuts
)
and analyses entanglement and separability with respect to these partitions.
For our
k
-partite system

{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{1}}\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{k}}
and the set of indexes

[k]\triangleq\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, a bipartite partition is a pair
(X,Y)

of two disjoint sets of indexes
X,Y\subseteq[k]
such that
X\cup Y=[k]

and
X\cap Y=\emptyset
. A state is said to be
entangled (or separable)
with respect to the partition
(X,Y)
if merging most subsystems and leaving
only the two large quantum subsystems
{\cal H}_{X}\otimes{\cal H}_{Y}\triangleq\left(\bigotimes_{j\in X}{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{j}}\right)\otimes\left(\bigotimes_{j\in Y}{\cal H}_{\mathrm{A}_{j}}\right)

(reordering the quantum subsystems) results in
an entangled (or separable) bipartite state.
In particular, if a state is fully separable, it is separable with respect
to all possible bipartite partitions;
therefore, to prove that a multipartite state is entangled
(i.e., not fully separable), it is sufficient to
find a bipartite partition such that the state is entangled with respect to it.

We can now analyse the entanglement class of
S
in three different situations,
given the dimensions of the
k
local subspaces
A^{(1)}_{\text{sep}},A^{(2)}_{\text{sep}},\ldots,A^{(k)}_{\text{sep}}
(all dimensions are in
\{2,3\}
)
defined in Eq. (
47
):

Situation 1: There are at least
two
local subspaces
of dimension
\mathbf{3}
:

Formally, in this case, there exist
\ell,m\in[k]
(
\ell\neq m
)
such that
\dim A^{(\ell)}_{\text{sep}}=\dim A^{(m)}_{\text{sep}}=3
.
We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell\})
:
the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces
are
\dim A^{(\ell)}_{\text{sep}}=3

and
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}\geq\dim A^{(m)}_{\text{sep}}=3

(according to Lemma
6.1
), so the relevant case is (3,3).

Therefore, applying Theorem
4.0
(case (3,3))
to
S
under this partition
implies that we are in case
(i)
(a triangle):
the fully separable pure and mixed states over
S

are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations)
of
|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|
,

|\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}^{(k)}|
,
and
|\alpha_{3}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{3}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}^{(k)}|
,
and all the other pure and mixed states over
S
are entangled.

Situation 2: There is only
one
local subspace
of dimension
\mathbf{3}
:

Formally, in this case, there exists
\ell\in[k]

such that
\dim A^{(\ell)}_{\text{sep}}=3
,
and for any other
m\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
it holds that

\dim A^{(m)}_{\text{sep}}=2
. We divide into two cases:

(i):

There is a subsystem
m\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
such that
the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
are linearly dependent but in general position.
In this case, if we apply Lemma
6.1

we get that the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle
are linearly independent,
so
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
.

We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell\})
:
the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces are

\dim A^{(\ell)}_{\text{sep}}=\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
,
so the relevant case is (3,3).
Therefore, applying Theorem
4.0
(case (3,3))
to
S
under this partition
implies that we are in case
(i)
(a triangle),
identically to Situation 1 above.

(ii):

For all subsystems
m\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
,
two of the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
are equal to one another. Formally, for each

m\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
there exists a two-element subset

I_{m}\triangleq\{i_{m},j_{m}\}\subset\{1,2,3\}
such that

|\alpha_{i_{m}}^{(m)}\rangle=|\alpha_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\rangle
.
We divide into two subcases:

(a):

If there exist two subsets
I_{m}
and
I_{n}

which are different from one another (formally, if there exist

m,n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
such that
I_{m}\neq I_{n}
),
then the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(m)}\rangle|\alpha_{1}^{(n)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle|\alpha_{2}^{(n)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle|\alpha_{3}^{(n)}\rangle
must be linearly independent.
Therefore, according to Lemma
6.1
,
the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle

are, too, linearly independent,
so
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
.

We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell\})
:
the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces are

\dim A^{(\ell)}_{\text{sep}}=\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
,
so the relevant case is (3,3).
Therefore, applying Theorem
4.0
(case (3,3))
to
S
under this partition
implies that we are in case
(i)
(a triangle),
identically to the two cases above.

(b):

If all subsets
I_{n}
are identical to each other
for all
n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
,
then we can assume, without loss of generality,
I_{n}=\{2,3\}
.
Then, for all
n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
it holds that

|\alpha_{2}^{(n)}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{(n)}\rangle
, which in particular
implies
|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle
.
Therefore,
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=2
.

We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell\})
:
the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces
are
\dim A^{(\ell)}_{\text{sep}}=3

and
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=2

(where
|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle

are
not
in general position),
so the relevant case is (3,2)-ii.

Therefore, applying Theorem
4.0
(case (3,2)-ii)
to
S
under this partition
implies that we are in case
(ii)
(a spherical cone):
the fully separable pure and mixed states over
S

are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations) of

|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|

with any pure or mixed state over the space

{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle\right\}
,
and all the other pure and mixed states over
S
are entangled.

Situation 3:
All
local subspaces are of dimension
\mathbf{2}
:

Formally, in this case, for all
j\in[k]

it holds that
\dim A^{(j)}_{\text{sep}}=2
. We divide into three cases:

(i):

There are
two
subsystems
\ell,m\in[k]
such that
the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle
are linearly dependent but in general position

and
the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
are linearly dependent but in general position.
In this case, if we apply Lemma
6.1

we get that the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle
are linearly independent,
so
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
.

We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell\})
:
the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces are

\dim A^{(\ell)}_{\text{sep}}=2

(where the spanning states are in general position)
and
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
,
so the relevant case is (2,3)-i.
Therefore, applying Theorem
4.0
(case (2,3)-i)
to
S
under this partition
implies that we are in case
(i)

(a triangle), identically to three of the cases above.

(ii):

There is exactly
one
subsystem
\ell\in[k]
such that
the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle
are linearly dependent but in general position.
Thus, for all subsystems
m\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
,
two of the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
are equal to one another; formally, for each

m\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
there exists a two-element subset

I_{m}\triangleq\{i_{m},j_{m}\}\subset\{1,2,3\}
such that

|\alpha_{i_{m}}^{(m)}\rangle=|\alpha_{j_{m}}^{(m)}\rangle
.
We divide into two subcases:

(a):

If there exist two subsets
I_{m}
and
I_{n}

which are different from one another (formally, if there exist

m,n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
such that
I_{m}\neq I_{n}
),
then the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(m)}\rangle|\alpha_{1}^{(n)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle|\alpha_{2}^{(n)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle|\alpha_{3}^{(n)}\rangle
must be linearly independent.
Therefore, according to Lemma
6.1
,
the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle

are, too, linearly independent,
so
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
.

We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell\})
:
the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces are

\dim A^{(\ell)}_{\text{sep}}=2

(where the spanning states are in general position)
and
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
,
so the relevant case is (2,3)-i.
Therefore, applying Theorem
4.0
(case (2,3)-i)
to
S
under this partition
implies that we are in case
(i)

(a triangle), identically to four of the cases above.

(b):

If all subsets
I_{n}
are identical to each other
for all
n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}
,
then we can assume, without loss of generality,
I_{n}=\{2,3\}
.
Let us choose an arbitrary
m\in[k]\setminus\{\ell\}

(so
|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
),
then, because
|\alpha_{1}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle
are in general position,
the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{1}^{(m)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
are linearly independent
according to Lemma
6.1
.
This implies that
\dim A^{(\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
.
On the other hand, for all
n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\}
it holds that

|\alpha_{2}^{(n)}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{(n)}\rangle
, which in particular
implies
|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle
.
Therefore,
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=2
.

We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell,m\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})
:
the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces are

\dim A^{(\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=3

and
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=2

(where
|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle

are
not
in general position),
so the relevant case is (3,2)-ii.

Therefore, applying Theorem
4.0
(case (3,2)-ii)
to
S
under this partition
implies that we are in case
(ii)
(a spherical cone):
the fully separable pure and mixed states over
S

are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations) of

|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|

with any pure or mixed state over the space

{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\{\ell,m\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\{\ell,m\})}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle\right\}={\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle\right\}

(this equality holds because
|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
, and it proves that all those states
are indeed fully separable),
and all the other pure and mixed states over
S
are entangled.

(iii):

There are
no
subsystems
\ell\in[k]
such that
the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle
are in general position.
Thus, for all subsystems
\ell\in[k]
,
two of the three states
|\alpha_{1}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle

are equal to one another; formally, for each

\ell\in[k]
there exists a two-element subset

I_{\ell}\triangleq\{i_{\ell},j_{\ell}\}\subset\{1,2,3\}
such that

|\alpha_{i_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}\rangle=|\alpha_{j_{\ell}}^{(\ell)}\rangle
.

The sets
I_{\ell}
cannot be all identical to one another
(for all
\ell\in[k]
), because then we would get
\dim S_{\text{sep}}=2
.
Therefore, there must exist
\ell,m\in[k]
such that
I_{\ell}\neq I_{m}
.
This means that the three states

|\alpha_{1}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{1}^{(m)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
are linearly independent,
which means that
\dim A^{(\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
.
We now divide into two subcases:

(a):

If there exist two subsystems
n,o\in[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\}

such that
I_{n}\neq I_{o}
, then the three states

|\alpha_{1}^{(n)}\rangle|\alpha_{1}^{(o)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{2}^{(n)}\rangle|\alpha_{2}^{(o)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(n)}\rangle|\alpha_{3}^{(o)}\rangle
are linearly independent,
which means that
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
.

We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell,m\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})
:
the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces are

\dim A^{(\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=3
,
so the relevant case is (3,3).
Therefore, applying Theorem
4.0
(case (3,3))
to
S
under this partition
implies that we are in case
(i)

(a triangle), identically to five of the cases above.

(b):

If all subsets
I_{n}
are identical to each other
for all
n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\}
,
then we can assume, without loss of generality,
I_{n}=\{2,3\}
.
Then, for all
n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\}
it holds that

|\alpha_{2}^{(n)}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{(n)}\rangle
, which in particular

|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle
.
Therefore,
\dim A^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=2

We can therefore analyse the entanglement with respect to
the bipartite partition
(\{\ell,m\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})

the dimensions of the relevant local subspaces are

\dim A^{(\{\ell,m\})}_{\text{sep}}=3

(case (3,2)-ii)
to

with respect to the bipartite partition

(\{\ell,m\}~,~[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})

(notice that these states are not necessarily fully separable!)
are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations) of

|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|

with any pure or mixed state over the space

{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\{\ell,m\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\{\ell,m\})}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle\right\}={\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle\right\}
,
and all the other pure and mixed states over
S

are entangled with respect to this partition
(and, thus, certainly not fully separable).

Now we divide into two subsubcases:

i.:

If
I_{n}
is equal to either

or

(for all
n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\}
),
then without loss of generality, we can assume

.
Therefore,
I_{m}=I_{n}=\{2,3\}
,
which means that
|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
.

The partition-specific result we reached above
thus means that we are in case
(ii)
(a spherical cone):
the fully separable pure and mixed states over
S

are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations) of

|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|

with any pure or mixed state over the space

{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\{\ell,m\})}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\{\ell,m\})}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle\right\}={\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell\})}\rangle\right\}

(this equality holds because
|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle=|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle
, and it proves that all those states
are indeed fully separable),
and all the other pure and mixed states over

If
I_{\ell}\neq I_{n}\neq I_{m}

(for all
n\in[k]\setminus\{\ell,m\}
),
then in particular
I_{\ell}\neq\{2,3\}
and
I_{m}\neq\{2,3\}
,
which means that both
|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle

and
|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle

are linearly independent.

Therefore, we can reconsider the meaning of
the partition-specific result we reached above,
which says that the only states over
S

that

|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|

with any pure or mixed state over the local qubit space

{\operatorname{span}}\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle~,~|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle\right\}\otimes\left\{|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle\right\}

and all the other pure and mixed states over

are entangled.

could
be entangled
(that is, not fully separable) are the non-trivial superpositions

\left(a|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle+b|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle\right)\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle

are

entangled, since both
|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle

and
|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle,|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle

(which does not affect entanglement and separability) can easily map

|\alpha_{2}^{(\ell)}\rangle\mapsto|1^{(\ell)}\rangle
,

|\alpha_{3}^{(\ell)}\rangle\mapsto|2^{(\ell)}\rangle
,

|\alpha_{2}^{(m)}\rangle\mapsto|1^{(m)}\rangle
, and

|\alpha_{3}^{(m)}\rangle\mapsto|2^{(m)}\rangle
.

\left(a|1^{(\ell)}\rangle|1^{(m)}\rangle+b|2^{(\ell)}\rangle|2^{(m)}\rangle\right)\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{([k]\setminus\{\ell,m\})}\rangle

which is separable if and only if
a=0

.

The remaining states are only the three states

|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle
,

|\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{(k)}\rangle

|\alpha_{3}^{(1)}\rangle\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{3}^{(k)}\rangle

Thus, our analysis implies that we are in case
(i)

(a triangle): the fully separable pure and mixed states over
S

are exactly all mixtures (convex combinations)
of
|\alpha_{1}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{1}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{1}^{(k)}|
,

|\alpha_{2}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{2}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{2}^{(k)}|
,
and
|\alpha_{3}^{(1)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}^{(1)}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\alpha_{3}^{(k)}\rangle\!\langle\alpha_{3}^{(k)}|
,
and all the other pure and mixed states over
S
are entangled.

7
Discussion

We have presented a full classification of entanglement and separability
in three-dimensional Hilbert subspaces of bipartite systems,
as well as a generalisation to multipartite systems.
This is a general classification that is independent of entanglement measures
and applies to all three-dimensional subspaces of any possible Hilbert space.
While our results are not aimed at deciding whether a single state is entangled
or separable (because that problem is easily solvable for rank-
3
states
using the partial transpose criterion (PPT)
Horodecki et al. 2000
,
and in some cases also using alternative criteria
Wootters 1998
),
they are aimed at understanding the possible structures of entanglement and
separability in Hilbert spaces and their internal relations.

Our results generalise the findings of Boyer and two of the present authors
in
Boyer et al. 2017
, which applied to two-dimensional subspaces.
In addition to the expected classes in three dimensions (mostly generalisations
or combinations of the classes from
Boyer et al. 2017
),
we suggested an easy classification for each class
using the dimensions of local subspaces (along with information on whether
the spanning states of the subspaces are in general position, if applicable),
and we also found a few interesting classes that do not exist in two dimensions.

Our most interesting novel class, which is not similar to anything found in
two dimensions
Boyer et al. 2017
, is named (2,2)-ii in Theorem
4.0

and is described in Fig.
5
: it does not simply consist of
a finite number of product states or a complete Bloch sphere created by
degeneration of local eigenstates, like other classes,
but it includes all states of the form

|\psi\rangle^{\mathrm{A}}|\psi\rangle^{\mathrm{B}}
(up to local invertible operations)—that is,
all product states in the symmetric subspace (the triplet subspace),
as described in Section
5
.
The relation between the foundational phenomenon of the symmetric
subspace, appearing naturally here, and the possible classes of entanglement
in three dimensions and higher dimensions, is an intriguing topic
for future research.

Other possible directions for future research include extending our results
to four-dimensional subspaces
and higher-dimensional subspaces (where we may encounter more physical phenomena
not appearing here, including bound entanglement)
and finding more ways to utilise the geometric figures we suggested
in Section
5
for representing the sets of separable states
and their geometric features.
It would also be interesting to know how the number of possible
classes of entanglement and separability (5 classes for two-dimensional
subspaces in
Boyer et al. 2017

and 14 classes for three-dimensional subspaces here) increases with the
dimension of the subspace, and in particular, whether the number of classes
is always finite.

One observation we can make based on Theorems
1

and
4.0
is that for
2
- and
3
-dimensional subspaces, the number of
(pure) product states is either at most the dimension of the subspace
(
2
or
3
, respectively) or infinite
(cardinality of the continuum). It would be particularly interesting
to investigate the maximum finite number of pure product states in general
subspaces of arbitrary given dimension.

We have also generalised the results to multipartite systems. We point out that,
unlike the two-dimensional subspaces discussed in
Boyer et al. 2017
where the bipartite
and multipartite cases are essentially equivalent, in three-dimensional
subspaces we found substantive differences between bipartite and multipartite
systems: most importantly, some of the classes for set of separable states
existing in bipartite systems completely disappear for (genuinely) multipartite
systems—including the interesting symmetric class (2,2)-ii mentioned above.
It could be interesting to find out why these classes disappear
in the multipartite case,
whether their disappearance hints at a foundational phenomenon
of quantum entanglement,
and whether similar differences between bipartite and multipartite
appear in higher dimensions, too.

Abstract
. For linearized Navier-Stokes equations, we consider an inverse source problem of determining a spatially varying divergence-free factor. We prove the global Lipschitz stability by interior data over a time interval and velocity field at t 0 > 0 t_{0}>0 over the spatial domain. The key are Carleman estimates for the Navier-Stokes equations and the operator rot.



1.
Introduction

Let
T>0
, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary

\partial\Omega
and
A
,
B
be sufficiently smooth as specified below.
By
a^{T}
we denote the transpose of a vector
a
under consideration.
We set

\partial_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},\quad j=1,2,3,\quad\partial_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad\nabla=(\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\partial_{3}),\quad\nabla_{x,t}=(\nabla,\partial_{t})

and

\mbox{div}\thinspace v=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}v_{j}\quad\mbox{for $v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})^{T}$}.

We consider

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}v(x,t)-\Delta v+(A(x,t)\cdot\nabla)v+(v\cdot\nabla)B(x,t)+\nabla p=F(x,t),\\
&\mbox{div}\thinspace v=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$},\quad v=0\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$}.\end{array}\right.

In this article, we discuss the following inverse source problem when a
subdomain
\omega\subset\Omega
and
t_{0}\in(0,T)
are given.

Inverse source problem
.

We assume that a source term
F
satisfies some conditions stated below.
Then determine
F
by
v
in
\omega\times(0,T)
and
v(\cdot,t_{0})

in
\Omega
.

In particular, we aim at the global Lipschitz stability for the inverse
problem.

We notice that in our inverse source problem, we do not assume any
data of the pressure field
p
, because the observation of
p
may
be more difficult than the velocity field
v
.
If suitable data of
p
are available, then the inverse problem
is easily solved in the same way as Imanuvilov and Yamamoto

[
13
]
, and we omit the details.

We introduce some notations. We set

Q:=\Omega\times(0,T).

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})\in(\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\})^{3}

and
|\gamma|=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3}
, and

\partial_{x}^{\gamma}=\partial_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}\partial_{2}^{\gamma_{2}}\partial_{3}^{\gamma_{3}}\\
=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{\gamma_{1}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{\gamma_{2}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\right)^{\gamma_{3}}
.
We further set

H^{2,1}(Q)=\{v\in L^{2}(Q);\thinspace\partial_{x}^{\gamma}v\in L^{2}(Q),\thinspace|\gamma|\leq 2,\thinspace\partial_{t}v\in L^{2}(Q)\},

H:=\overline{\{v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)^{3}:\,\mbox{div}\thinspace v=0\,\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}\}^{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

and

V:=(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))^{3}\cap H.

In this article, for simplicity, we assume some smoothness for

v,p
, and not pursue the minimum regularity.
More precisely, we assume

A\in C^{2}([0,T];H^{1}(\Omega)),\quad B\in C^{2}([0,T];H^{2}(\Omega)).

For the solution
v,p
, we assume the regularity:

[formula]

\partial_{t}^{k}v\in L^{2}(0,T;V)\cap H^{2,1}(Q),\quad k=0,1,2,\quad p\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)).

Let
\omega\subset\Omega
be an arbitrarily chosen non-empty subdomain.
For an arbitrarily fixed constant
M>0
, we define an admissible set of
unknown
F
by

\mathcal{F}:=\{F\in H^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(\Omega));\,\mbox{div}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})=0\,\,\mbox{in $\Omega$},

[formula]

|\partial_{t}^{k}F(x,t)|\leq M|F(x,t_{0})|\,\,\mbox{for
$(x,t)\in Q$ and $k=1,2$},\quad F|_{\omega}=F|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\}

We are ready to state our main result.

Theorem 1.

There exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
\mathcal{F}
, such that

\|F\|_{H^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(\|v\|_{H^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\omega))}+\|v(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)})

for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 1 asserts the global Lipschitz stability over
\Omega
for the
inverse problem. Theorem 1 immediately implies the uniqueness within

\mathcal{F}
.
In general we can prove the uniqueness within more generous
admissible set than
F
defined by (1.3). For better uniqueness,
we need different arguments, especially another type of Carleman estimate,
and we omit the details.

In Choulli, Imanuvilov, Puel and Yamamoto
[
5
]
, a similar
stability estimate for the same type of the inverse source pronlem was proved.
Theorem 1 provides the global Lipschitz stability under conditions on

R
and
\omega
which are more natural and easier to be verified.

The condition

[formula]

\mbox{div}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}

is essential for the uniqueness in determining
F
without data of
p
,
as the following example
shows.

Obstruction to the uniqueness:

We consider a simple case

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\partial_{t}v(x,t)-\Delta v+\nabla p=f(x),\\
&\mbox{div}\thinspace v=0,\quad(x,t)\in Q:=\Omega\times I,\\
&v(x,t_{0})=0\quad\mbox{for $x\in\Omega$},\quad\mbox{supp $v\subset\Omega\times(0,T)$}.\end{array}\right.

Here
f
is an
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued smooth function.
It is trivial that
(v,p)=(0,0)
satisfies (1.5) with
f=0
.
Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then
(v,p):=(0,\,\psi)

satisfies (1.5) with
f:=\nabla\psi
. In other words, by the
appearance of the pressure field
p
in the Navier-Stokes equations,
there is no possibility for the uniqueness in
determining
f
given by a scalar potential.

We notice that if
F(x,t_{0})
is given by a vector potential:

F(x,t_{0})=\mbox{rot}\thinspace q(x),\quad x\in\Omega

with smooth
q
, then (1.4) holds automatically.

Next we examine the condition

[formula]

|\partial_{t}^{k}F(x,t)|\leq M|F(x,t_{0})|\quad\mbox{for
$(x,t)\in Q$ and $k=1,2$}.

Examples
.

(i)
Let
R(x,t)
be a smooth
3\times 3
matrix such that
det
R(x,t_{0})\neq 0
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
. Then (1.6) is satisfied.

(ii)
Let
r(x,t)=(r_{1}(x,t),r_{2}(x,t),r_{3}(x,t))^{T}
and

f(x)=(f_{1}(x),\,f_{2}(x),\,0)^{T}
be smooth functions satisfying

\mbox{rot}\thinspace f(x)=(-\partial_{3}f_{2}(x),\,\partial_{3}f_{1}(x),\,\partial_{1}f_{2}(x)-\partial_{2}f_{1}(x))^{T}=0,\quad\mbox{rot}\thinspace r(\cdot,t_{0})=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}.

Then
F(x,t):=r(x,t)\times f(x)
satisfies (1.4).
Indeed, as is directly seen,

\mbox{div}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})=\mbox{div}\thinspace(r(x,t_{0})\times f(x))=f(x)\cdot\mbox{rot}\thinspace r(x,t_{0})-r(x,t_{0})\cdot\mbox{rot}\thinspace f(x)=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$.}

If we assume
r_{3}(x,t_{0})\neq 0
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, then (1.6) is
satisfied. indeed, by

r\times f=(-r_{3}f_{2},\,r_{3}f_{1},\,r_{1}f_{2}-r_{2}f_{1})^{T}\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

we have

|\partial_{t}^{k}(r\times f)(x,t)|\leq C(|f_{1}(x)|+|f_{2}(x)|),\quad(x,t)\in Q,\,k=1,2

and

|r(x,t_{0})\times f(x)|\geq|r_{3}(x,t_{0})|(|f_{1}(x)|+|f_{2}(x)|)\geq C(|f_{1}(x)|+|f_{2}(x)|),\quad x\in\Omega

by
r_{3}(x,t_{0})\neq 0
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

2.
Key Carleman estimates

For a non-empty suddomain
\omega_{0}
with
\overline{\omega_{0}}\subset\omega
,
we know (e.g., Imanuvilov
[
10
]
) that there exists
\eta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying

[formula]

\eta|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\eta>0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$},\quad|\nabla\eta|>0\quad\mbox{on $\overline{\Omega\setminus\omega_{0}}$}.

Henceforth we set

t_{0}=\frac{T}{2}.

Let
\ell\in C^{\infty}[0,T]
satisfy

\ell(t)=\ell(T-t)
for
0\leq t\leq T
, and

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\ell(t)>0,\quad 0<t<T,\\
&\ell(t)=t,\quad 0\leq t\leq\frac{T}{4},\\
&\ell(t_{0})>\ell(t),\quad t\in(0,T)\setminus\{t_{0}\}.\end{array}\right.

We set

Q_{\omega}:=\omega\times(0,T),

recall that
Q=\Omega\times(0,T)
, and

[formula]

\varphi(x,t):=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}}{\ell^{8}(t)},\quad\alpha(x,t):=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}-e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}}{\ell^{8}(t)},\quad(x,t)\in Q.

Here we fix a sufficiently large constant
\lambda>0
.

Hencforth
C>0
,
C_{j}>
denote generic constants which are dependent on

\Omega
,
T
,
\lambda
, but independent of
s
.
A Carleman estimate for the Navier-Stokes equations is stated as:

Lemma 1.

Let
F\in L^{2}(0,T;H)
,
v(\cdot,t)\in V
,
0\leq t\leq T
and

v\in L^{2}(0,T;V)\cap H^{2,1}(Q)
satisfy (1.1). Let

m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then there exist constants

C>0
and
s_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{Q}(s^{m}\varphi^{m}|\nabla v|^{2}+s^{m+1}\varphi^{m+1}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace v|^{2}+s^{m+2}\varphi^{m+2}|v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}s^{m}\varphi^{m}|F|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C\int_{Q_{\omega}}(s^{m+1}\varphi^{m+1}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace v|^{2}+s^{m+2}\varphi^{m+2}|v|^{2}+s^{m+1}\varphi^{m+1}|\nabla v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

for all
s\geq s_{0}
.

For the case of
m=0
, the proof is found e.g., Theorem 2 in
Choulli, Imanuvilov, Puel and Yamamoto
[
5
]
.
In Section 5, for completeness we derive the lemma with general
m\in\mathbb{N}

from the case
m=0
.

Next let
\psi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfy

[formula]

\psi>c_{0}\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$},\quad\psi=c_{0}\quad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$},\quad|\nabla\psi|>0\quad\mbox{on $\overline{\Omega\setminus\omega}$},

where
c_{0}
is a given constant.
With sufficiently large fixed
\lambda>0
, we set

\varphi_{0}(x):=e^{\lambda\psi(x)},\quad x\in\Omega.

Then we show

Lemma 2.

Let
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfy
w|_{\omega}=0
, and

[formula]

\mbox{rot}\thinspace w=g,\quad\mbox{div}\thinspace w=h\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}.

Then there exist constants
C>0
and
s_{1}>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{s}|\nabla w|^{2}+s|w|^{2}\right)e^{2s\varphi_{0}(x)}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}(|g|^{2}+|h|^{2})e^{2s\varphi_{0}(x)}dx

for all
s\geq s_{1}
.

This is a Carleman estimate for stationary Maxwell’s equations and we
can refer to Vogelsang
[
20
]
. Here we prove by means of
a Carleman estimate for
-\Delta
, not by
[
20
]
.

Proof of Lemma 2.

Setting
\widetilde{\psi}(x)=\psi(x)-c_{0}
,
x\in\Omega
, we see that

\widetilde{\psi}>0
in
\Omega
,
\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
and

|\nabla\widetilde{\psi}|>0
on
\overline{\Omega\setminus\omega}
.
We set
\widetilde{\varphi_{0}}(x):=e^{\lambda\widetilde{\psi}(x)}=e^{-\lambda c_{0}}\varphi_{0}(x)
,
x\in\Omega
.

Since

\mbox{rot}\thinspace\mbox{rot}\thinspace w=-\Delta w+\nabla(\mbox{div}\thinspace w)

by
w\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, we see

[formula]

-\Delta w=\mbox{rot}\thinspace g-\nabla h\quad\mbox{in $\Omega$}

by (2.5). In terms of
w|_{\partial\Omega}=0
and
w|_{\omega}=0
, we
apply an
H^{-1}
-Carleman esimate (e.g., Theorem 2.2 in Imanuvilov and
Puel
[
12
]
) to (2.6), and we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{s}|\nabla w|^{2}+s|w|^{2}\right)e^{2s\widetilde{\varphi_{0}}(x)}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}(|g|^{2}+|h|^{2})e^{2s\widetilde{\varphi_{0}}(x)}dx

for all
s\geq\widetilde{s_{1}}
: some constant.
Setting
\widetilde{s}:=se^{\lambda c_{0}}
, we have

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\widetilde{s}}|\nabla w|^{2}+\widetilde{s}|w|^{2}\right)e^{2\widetilde{s}\widetilde{\varphi_{0}}(x)}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}(|g|^{2}+|h|^{2})e^{2\widetilde{s}\widetilde{\varphi_{0}}(x)}dx

for
\widetilde{s}\geq\widetilde{s_{1}}
.
By
\widetilde{s}\widetilde{\varphi_{0}}(x)=se^{\lambda c_{0}}e^{-\lambda c_{0}}\varphi_{0}(x)=s\varphi_{0}(x)
for
x\in\Omega
and
se^{\lambda c_{0}}\geq s
, we see

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{e^{-\lambda c_{0}}}{s}|\nabla w|^{2}+s|w|^{2}\right)e^{2s\varphi_{0}(x)}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}(|g|^{2}+|h|^{2})e^{2s\varphi_{0}(x)}dx

for
s\geq\widetilde{s_{1}}e^{-\lambda c_{0}}
. Setting
s_{1}:=\widetilde{s_{1}}e^{-\lambda c_{0}}

and replacing
C
by
Ce^{\lambda c_{0}}
, we complete the proof of
Lemma 2.
\blacksquare

We conclude this section with a lemma which constitutes a technical part of
the proof of Theorem 1, but the proof is elementary.

Lemma 3.

Let
g\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Then

\int_{Q}\varphi(x,t)|g(x)|e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt\leq C\int_{\Omega}|g(x)|e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx,

where
\alpha
and
\varphi
are defined by (2.3).

Proof of Lemma 3.

We set

[formula]

\widehat{\varphi}(t)=\frac{1}{\ell^{8}(t)},\quad 0<t<T.

Then, as can be directly verified, fixing

\lambda>0
, we can choose a constant
C>0
such that

[formula]

C^{-1}\varphi(x,t)\leq\widehat{\varphi}(t)\leq C\varphi(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q.

By (2.8), it is sufficient to prove

\int_{Q}\widehat{\varphi}(t)|g(x)|e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt\leq C\int_{\Omega}|g(x)|e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx.

First

\int_{Q}\widehat{\varphi}(t)|g(x)|e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt=\int_{\Omega}|g(x)|e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}\left(\int^{T}_{0}\widehat{\varphi}(t)e^{2s(\alpha(x,t)-\alpha(x,t_{0}))}dt\right)dx.

Since
\widehat{\varphi}(t)\geq 0
for
0<t<T
, we have

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\widehat{\varphi}(t)e^{2s(\alpha(x,t)-\alpha(x,t_{0}))}dt\leq\int^{T}_{0}\widehat{\varphi}(t)\exp\left(-2sC\frac{\ell^{8}(t_{0})-\ell^{8}(t)}{\ell^{8}(t_{0})\ell^{8}(t)}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\widehat{\varphi}(t)\exp\left(-\mu_{1}s\frac{\ell^{8}(t_{0})-\ell^{8}(t)}{\ell^{8}(t)}\right)dt,

with some constant
\mu_{1}>0
.
We fix
\varepsilon>0
. Hence

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\widehat{\varphi}(t)e^{2s(\alpha(x,t)-\alpha(x,t_{0}))}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\int^{T}_{T-\varepsilon}+\int^{T-\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{1}{\ell^{8}(t)}\exp\left(-\mu_{1}s\frac{\ell^{8}(t_{0})-\ell^{8}(t)}{\ell^{8}(t)}\right)dt.

Here we have

\int^{\varepsilon}_{0}\frac{1}{\ell^{8}(t)}\exp\left(-\mu_{1}s\frac{\ell^{8}(t_{0})-\ell^{8}(t)}{\ell^{8}(t)}\right)dt\leq\int^{\varepsilon}_{0}\frac{1}{\ell^{8}(t)}e^{-\frac{C}{\ell^{8}(t)}}dt<\infty

for
s\geq 1
for example.
Moreover

\int^{T-\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\ell^{8}(t)}\exp\left(-\mu_{1}s\frac{\ell^{8}(t_{0})-\ell^{8}(t)}{\ell^{8}(t)}\right)dt\leq\int^{T-\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\ell^{8}(t)}dt<\infty.

Thus the proof of Lemma 3 is complete.

3.
Proof of Theorem 1

With loss of generality, we can assume that
t_{0}=\frac{T}{2}
.
Indeed, for arbitrary
t_{0}\in(0,T)
, we choose small
\delta>0

such that
0<t_{0}-\delta<t_{0}+\delta<T
. Then, in place of
(0,T)
,
we can continue the proof over the time interval
(t_{0}-\delta,\,t_{0}+\delta)
.
We set

D:=\|v\|_{H^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\omega))}+\|v(\cdot,t_{0})\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Applying Lemma 1 with
m=1
to
\partial_{t}v
and
\partial_{t}^{2}v
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}s^{2}\varphi^{2}(|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v|^{2}+|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}^{2}v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}s\varphi\sum_{k=1}^{2}|\partial_{t}^{k}F|^{2}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle CD^{2}\int_{Q_{\omega}}s^{3}\varphi^{3}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt

for all
s\geq s_{0}
.
Since
\int_{Q}s^{3}\varphi^{3}e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C
and

\sum_{k=1}^{2}|\partial_{t}^{k}F(x,t)|^{2}\leq M|F(x,t_{0})|
for

(x,t)\in Q
, we obtain

[formula]

\int_{Q}s\varphi^{2}(|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v|^{2}+|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}^{2}v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q}\varphi|F(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt+CD^{2}.

By (1.1), we have

\displaystyle F(x,t_{0})=\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})-\Delta v(x,t_{0})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(A(x,t_{0})\cdot\nabla)v(x,t_{0})+(v(x,t_{0})\cdot\nabla)B(x,t_{0})+\nabla p(x,t_{0}).

Since
\mbox{rot}\thinspace\nabla=0
, we obtain

[formula]

\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})=\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})+\mbox{rot}\thinspace a(x),\quad x\in\Omega,

where

a(x):=-\Delta v(x,t_{0})+(A(x,t_{0})\cdot\nabla)v(x,t_{0})+(v(x,t_{0})\cdot\nabla)B(x,t_{0}),\quad x\in\Omega.

Hence, using that
\alpha(x,0)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\alpha(x,t)=0

for
x\in\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx=\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\partial_{t}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v(x,t)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dx\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int^{t_{0}}_{0}\int_{\Omega}((\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v\cdot\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}^{2}v)+|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v(x,t)|^{2}s(\partial_{t}\alpha))e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt.

Henceforth we write
\ell^{\prime}(t)=\frac{d\ell}{dt}(t)
.
Since

\partial_{t}\alpha(x,t)=\frac{-8}{\ell^{8}(t)}\frac{\ell^{\prime}(t)}{\ell(t)}(e^{\lambda\eta(x)}-e^{\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}})

and

\left|\frac{\ell^{\prime}(t)}{\ell(t)}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\ell^{8}(t)},\quad 0<t<T,

we can verify that
|\partial_{t}\alpha(x,t)|\leq C\varphi(x,t)^{2}
for

(x,t)\in Q
. Hence, applying
|ab|\leq\frac{1}{2}(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2})
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{Q}(|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v||\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}^{2}v|+s\varphi^{2}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}(|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v|^{2}+|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}^{2}v|^{2}+s\varphi^{2}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v|^{2})e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt

for all large
s>0
. Since
\varphi>0
on
\overline{Q}
, choosing
s>0
large,
we can estimate

|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v|^{2}\leq Cs\varphi^{2}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v|^{2}

in
Q
. Therefore, application of (3.1) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{Q}(|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}^{2}v|^{2}+s\varphi^{2}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v(x,t)|^{2})e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{Q}\varphi(x,t)|F(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t)}dxdt+CD^{2}

for all large
s>0
.
Applying Lemma 3 to the first term on the right-hand side, we have

\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace\partial_{t}v(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|F(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx+CD^{2}.

Therefore (3.2) yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}|F(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx+CD^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace a(x)|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\Omega}|F(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx+CD^{2}

for all large
s>0
. Applying Lemma 2 to
F(x,t_{0})
in view of

\mbox{div}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})=0
for
x\in\Omega
and
F|_{\partial\Omega}=F|_{\omega}=0
, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}s|F(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\mbox{rot}\thinspace F(x,t_{0})|^{2}e^{2s\alpha(x,t_{0})}dx.

for all large
s>0
. Choosing
s>0
large, we can complete the proof of
Theorem 1.

\blacksquare

4.
Concluding Remarks

4.1. Nonlinear case.

In this article, we mainly consider linearized Navier-Stokes equations.
For the original Navier-Stokes equations

[formula]

\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+(v\cdot v)v+\nabla p=F,\quad\mbox{div}\thinspace v=0\quad\mbox{in $Q$},

assuming suitable smoothness of
v,p
and taking the difference

v_{1}-v_{2}
where
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are solutions to (4.1) respectively
with
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, we can reduce the inverse source problem to
the one for the linearized Navier-Stokes equations. We omit the details.

4.2. Case of
t_{0}=0
.

We do not assume any initial value but we are given
v(\cdot,t_{0})
with

t_{0}>0
. In other words, our inverse problem does nor correspond
to an initial boundary value problem for the Navier-Stokes equations.
The formulation of inverse problems with
t_{0}>0
is standard for
parabolic equations (e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
,

[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,

[
11
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
21
]
).

On the other hand, for the case of
t_{0}=0
, inverse problems of
the type discussed here for equations of parabolic type including
the Navier-Stokes equations, are open,
and even the uniqueness is unknown in general.
In some special cases, we can prove the uniqueness (e.g., Theorem 4.7 in

[
18
]
).

4.3. Arbitrariness of observation time interval.

We consider the inverse problem in the time interval
(0,T)
, when
the Navier-Stokes equations (1.1) hold.
As is seen by the proof, in view of the parabolicity of the equations,
it is not necessary to take data over the same time interval, but sufficient
over a smaller time interval
I
such that
\overline{I}\subset(0,T)
.
Thus for the inverse problem, it suffices that we are concerned with
the smoothness of the solution
v,p
for

t>0
, and not to exploit the regularity at
t=0
which is more
delicate for
t>0
.

4.4. Choices of the weight functions of Carleman estimates.

Our key is a Carleman estimate. We have two kinds of Carleman estimates
according to the weight functions:

•

Regular weight function:

[formula]

\varphi(x,t):=e^{\lambda(d(x)-\beta(t-t_{0})^{2})},\quad(x,t)\in Q.

•

Singular weight function:

[formula]

\varphi(x,t)=\frac{e^{\lambda\eta(x)}-e^{2\lambda\|\eta\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}}}{h(t)},\quad(x,t)\in Q,

where
\lim_{t\to 0}h(t)=\lim_{t\to T}h(t)=0
.

Here
d,\eta\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
are chosen suitably.

The Carleman estimate with the weight (4.3) was proved firstly by
Imanuvilov
[
10
]
for a parabolic equation.
As for related inverse problems for the Navier-Stokes equations,
Choulli, Imanuvilov, Puel and Yamamoto
[
5
]
,
Fan, Di Cristo, Jiang and Nakamura
[
6
]
,
Fan, Jiang and Nakamura
[
7
]
used Carleman estimates with
(4.3), while Bellassoued, Imanuvilov and Yamamoto
[
2
]
,
Imanuvilov, Lorenzi and Yamamoto
[
11
]
and the
current article rely on (4.2).

The Carleman estimate with (4.2) is relevant for considering
inverse problems locally in
x
in the case where we are not given
the boundary condition on the whole
\partial\Omega
, while the Carleman
estimate with (4.3) is more convenient for deriving the global
stability in inverse problems.

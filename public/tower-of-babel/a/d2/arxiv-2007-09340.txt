Determinisability of one-clock timed automata
Lorenzo Clemente, Sławomir Lasota, Radosław Piórkowski

Abstract
The deterministic membership problem for timed automata asks whether the timed language recognised by a nondeterministic timed automaton can be recognised by a deterministic timed automaton. We show that the problem is decidable when the input automaton is a one-clock nondeterministic timed automaton without epsilon transitions and the number of clocks of the deterministic timed automaton is fixed. We show that the problem in all the other cases is undecidable, i.e., when either 1) the input nondeterministic timed automaton has two clocks or more, or 2) it uses epsilon transitions, or 3) the number of clocks of the output deterministic automaton is not fixed.

Introduction

Nondeterministic timed automata (
nta
) are one of the most widespread model of real-time reactive systems.
They are an extension of finite automata
with real-valued clocks which can be reset and compared by inequality constraints.
The nonemptiness problem for
nta
is decidable and in fact
PSpace
-complete,
as shown by Alur and Dill in their landmark paper
[
3
]
.
As a testimony to the importance of the model,
the authors received the 2016 Church Award
[
1
]

for the invention of timed automata.
This paved the way to the automatic verification of timed systems,
leading to mature tools such as UPPAAL
[
9
]
,
UPPAAL Tiga (timed games)
[
16
]
,
and PRISM (probabilistic timed automata)
[
33
]
.
The reachability problem is still a very active research area to these days

[
22
,
30
,
2
,
26
,
27
,
29
]
,
as well as expressive generalisations thereof,
such as the binary reachability problem

[
20
,
21
,
32
,
24
]
.

Deterministic timed automata (
dta
) form a strict subclass of
nta
where the next configuration is uniquely determined from the current one and the timed input symbol.
The class of
dta
enjoys stronger properties than
nta
,
such as decidable universality and inclusion problems
and closure under complementation
[
3
]
.
Moreover, the more restrictive nature of
dta
is necessary in several applications of timed automata,
such as test generation
[
37
]
,
fault diagnosis
[
13
]
,
and learning
[
46
,
42
]
,
winning conditions in timed games

[
5
,
31
,
14
]
,
and in a notion of recognisability of timed languages
[
35
]
.
For these reasons,
and for the more general quest of understanding the nature of the expressive power of nondeterminism in timed automata,
many researchers have focused on defining determinisable classes of timed automata,
such as strongly non-zeno
nta

[
6
]
,
event-clock
nta

[
4
]
,
and
nta
with integer-resets
[
41
]
.
The classes above are not exhaustive,
in the sense that there are
nta
recognising deterministic timed languages
not falling into any of the classes above.

Another remarkable subclass of
nta
is obtained by requiring the presence of just one clock (without epsilon transitions).
The resulting class of
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
is incomparable with
dta
:
For instance,
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
are not closed under complement (unlike
dta
)
and there are very simple
dta
languages which are not recognisable by any
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
.
Nonetheless,
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
, like
dta
, have decidable inclusion, equivalence, and universality problems
[
38
,
34
]
,
albeit the complexity is non-primitive recursive
[
34
, Corollary 4.2]

(see also
[
39
, Theorem 7.2]
for an analogous lower bound for the satisfiability problem of metric temporal logic).
Moreover, the non-emptiness problem for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
is
NLogSpace
-complete
(vs.
PSpace
-complete for unrestricted
nta
and
dta
, already with two clocks
[
22
]
),
and computing the binary reachability relation is simpler when there is only one clock than in the general case

[
18
]
.

The deterministic membership problem.

The
dta
membership problem
asks, given an
nta
,
whether there exists a
dta
recognising the same language.
There are two natural variants of this problem,
which are obtained by restricting the resources available to the sought
dta
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a bound on the number of clocks,
and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be a bound on the maximal absolute value of numerical constants.
The
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}

and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
membership problems

are the restriction of the problem above
where the
dta
is required to have at most
k
clocks,
resp., at most
k
clocks and absolute value of maximal constant bounded by
m
.
Notice that we do not bound the number of control locations of the
dta
,
which makes the problem non-trivial.

Since regular languages are deterministic,
the
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
membership problem can be seen as a quantitative generalisation of the regularity problem.
For instance, the
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{0}}
membership problem is exactly the regularity problem
since a timed automaton with no clocks is the same as a finite automaton.
We remark that the regularity problem is usually undecidable
for nondeterministic models of computation generalising finite automata,
e.g., context-free grammars/pushdown automata
[
40
, Theorem 6.6.6]
,
labelled Petri nets under reachability semantics
[
45
]
, Parikh automata
[
15
]
, etc.
One way to obtain decidability is to either restrict the input model to be deterministic
(e.g.,
[
44
,
45
,
8
]
),
or to consider finer notions of equivalence,
such as bisimulation (e.g.,
[
28
]
).

This negative situation is generally confirmed for timed automata.
For every number of clocks
k\in\mathbb{N}
and maximal constant
m
,
the
dta
,
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
, and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
membership problems are known to be undecidable
when the input
nta
has
\geq 2
clocks,
and for 1-clock
nta
with epsilon transitions
[
23
,
43
]
.
To the best of our knowledge,
the deterministic membership problem was not studied before when the input automaton is
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
without epsilon transitions.

Contributions.

We complete the study of the decidability border for the deterministic membership problem
initiated in
[
23
,
43
]
.
Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

The
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
membership and the
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
membership problems
are decidable for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
languages.

Our decidability result contrasts starkly
with the abdundance of undecidability results for the regularity problem.
We establish decidability by showing that if a
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k,m}}
recognises a
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
language,
then in fact it recognises a
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
language
and moreover there is a computable bound on the number of control locations of the deterministic acceptor (c.f.
\cref
thm:k-DTA-char).
This provides a decision procedure
since there are finitely many
dta
once the number of clocks, the maximal constant, and the number of control locations are fixed.

In our technical analysis we find it convenient to introduce the so called

always resetting
subclass of
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k}}
. These automata are required to reset at least one clock at every transition
and are thus of expressive power intermediate between
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k-1}}
and
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k}}
.
Always resetting
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{2}}
are strictly more expressive than
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
: For instance, the language of timed words of the form

(a,t_{0})(a,t_{1})(a,t_{2})
s.t.
t_{2}-t_{0}>2
and
t_{2}-t_{1}<1
can be recognised by an always resetting
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{2}}

but by no
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
.
Despite their increased expressive power,
always resetting
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{2}}

still have a decidable universality problem (the well-quasi order approach of
[
38
]
goes through),
which is not the case for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{2}}
.
Thanks to this restricted form,
we are able to provide in
\cref
thm:k-DTA-char an elegant characterisation of those
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
languages
which are recognised by an always resetting
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
.

We complement the decidability result above by showing that the problem becomes undecidable
if we do not restrict the number of clocks of the
dta
.

Theorem 2
.

The
dta
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{\textnormal{\_},m}}
(
m>0
) membership problems are undecidable for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
without epsilon transitions.

Finally, by refining the analysis of
[
23
]
,
we show that the
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
membership problems for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}

are non-primitive recursive.

Theorem 3
.

The
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
membership problems are
HyperAckermann
-hard for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
.

Related research.

Many works addressed the construction of a
dta
equivalent to a given
nta
(see
[
10
]
and references therein),
however since the general problem is undecidable,
one has to either sacrifice termination,
or consider deterministic under/over-approximations.
In a related line of work,
we have shown that the
deterministic separability problem
is decidable for the full class of
nta
, when the number of clocks of the separator is given in the input
[
19
]
.
This contrasts with undecidability of the corresponding membership problem.
Decidability of the deterministic separability problem when the number of clocks of the separator is not provided remains a challenging open problem.

2
Preliminaries

Timed words and languages.

Fix a finite alphabet
\Sigma
.
Let
{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
denote reals and nonnegative reals
1
1

1

Equivalently, nonnegative rationals may be considered
in place of reals.
, respectively.
A
timed word
over
\Sigma
is any sequence of the form

\displaystyle w\ =\ (a_{1},t_{1})\,\dots\,(a_{n},t_{n})\ \in\ (\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0})^{*}

(1)

which is
monotonic
,
in the sense that the timestamps
t_{i}
’s satisfy
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\dots\leq t_{n}
.
Let
\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)
be the set of all timed words over
\Sigma
,
and let
\mathbb{T}_{\geq{t}}(\Sigma)
be, for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
,
the set of timed words with
t_{1}\geq t
. A
timed language
is a subset of
\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)
.

The concatenation
w\cdot v
of two timed words
w
and
v

is defined only when the first time-stamp of
v
is greater or equal than the last timestamp of
w
.
Using this partial operation, we define,
for a timed word
w\in\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)
and a timed language
L\subseteq\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)
, the left quotient

w^{-1}L:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)\;\middle|\;w\cdot v\in L\right\}
.
Clearly
w^{-1}L\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\geq{t_{n}}}(\Sigma)
.

Clock constraints and regions.

Let
\mathtt{X}=\left\{\mathtt{x}_{1},\dots,\mathtt{x}_{k}\right\}
be a finite set of clocks.
A
clock valuation
is a function
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{\mathtt{X}}

assigning a non-negative real number
\mu(\mathtt{x})
to every clock
\mathtt{x}\in\mathtt{X}
.
A
clock constraint
is a quantifier-free formula of the form

\displaystyle\varphi,\psi\ ::\equiv\ \mathbf{true}\ |\ \mathbf{false}\ |\ \mathtt{x}_{i}-\mathtt{x}_{j}\sim z\ |\ \mathtt{x}_{i}\sim z\ |\ \neg\varphi\ |\ \varphi\land\psi\ |\ \varphi\lor\psi,

where “
\sim
” is a comparison operator in
\left\{=,<,\leq,>,\geq\right\}

and
z\in\mathbb{Z}
.
A clock valuation
\mu
satisfies a constraint
\varphi
, written
\mu\models\varphi
,
if interpreting each clock
\mathtt{x}_{i}
by
\mu(\mathtt{x}_{i})
makes
\varphi
a tautology.
An
k,m
-region
is a non-empty set of valuations
\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket

satisfied by a constraint
\varphi
with
k
clocks and absolute value of maximal constant bounded by
m
,
which is minimal w.r.t. set inclusion.
For instance, the clock constraint

1<\mathtt{x}_{1}<2\;\wedge\;4<\mathtt{x}_{2}<5\;\wedge\;\mathtt{x}_{2}-\mathtt{x}_{1}<3

defines a
2,5
-region consisting of an open triangle with nodes
(1,4)
,
(2,4)
and
(2,5)
.

Timed automata.

A (nondeterministic)
timed automaton
is a tuple
A=\left(\Sigma,\mathtt{L},\mathtt{X},\mathtt{I},\mathtt{F},\Delta\right)
,
where
\Sigma
is a finite input alphabet,

\mathtt{L}
is a finite set of control locations,

\mathtt{X}
is a finite set of clocks,

\mathtt{I},\mathtt{F}\subseteq\mathtt{L}
are the subsets of initial, resp., final, control locations,
and
\Delta
is a finite set of transition rules of the form

\displaystyle\left(p,a,\varphi,\mathtt{Y},q\right)

(2)

with
p,q\in\mathtt{L}
control locations,
a\in\Sigma
,
\varphi
a clock constraint to be tested,
and
\mathtt{Y}\subseteq\mathtt{X}
the set of clocks to be reset.
We write
nta
for the class of all nondeterministic timed automata,
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k}}
when the number
k
of clocks is fixed,

{\textsf{\sc nta}_{\textnormal{\_},m}}
when the bound
m
on constants is fixed,
and
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k,m}}
when both
k
and
m
are fixed.

An
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{\textnormal{\_},m}}

A
is
always resetting
if every transition rule as in (
2
) resets some clock
\mathtt{Y}\neq\emptyset
,
and
greedily resetting

if, for every clock
\mathtt{x}
,
whenever
\varphi
implies that
\mathtt{x}
belongs to
\left\{0,\ldots,m\right\}\cup(m,\infty)
, then
\mathtt{x}\in\mathtt{Y}
.

Reset-point semantics.

A
configuration
of an
nta

A
is a tuple
\left(p,\mu,t_{0}\right)

consisting of a control location
p\in\mathtt{L}
,
a reset-point assignment
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{\mathtt{X}}
, and a “now” timestamp
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

satisfying
\mu(\mathtt{x})\leq t_{0}
for all clocks
\mathtt{x}\in\mathtt{X}
.
Intuitively,
t_{0}
is the last timestamp seen in the input
and, for every clock
\mathtt{x}
,

\mu(\mathtt{x})
stores the timestamp of the last reset of
\mathtt{x}
.
A configuration is
initial
if
p
is so,
t_{0}=0
, and
\mu(\mathtt{x})=0
for all clocks
\mathtt{x}
,
and it is
final
if
p
is so
(without any further restriction on
\mu
or
t_{0}
).
For a set of clocks
\mathtt{Y}\subseteq\mathtt{X}
and a timestamp
u\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, let
\mu[\mathtt{Y}\mapsto u]

be the assignment which is
u
on
\mathtt{Y}
and agrees with
\mu
on
\mathtt{X}\setminus\mathtt{Y}
.
An assignment
\mu
together with
t_{0}
induces a clock valuation
{t_{0}}-{\mu}
defined as

({t_{0}}-{\mu})(\mathtt{x})=t_{0}-\mu(\mathtt{x})
for all clocks
\mathtt{x}\in\mathtt{X}
.
Clock assignments and valuations have the same type
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{\mathtt{X}}
,
however we find it technically convenient to store assignments in configurations
and use the derived valuations to interpret the clock constraints.
Such reset-point semantics based on reset-point assignments
has already appeared in the literature on timed automata
[
25
]

and it is the foundation of the related model of timed-register automata
[
12
]
.

Every transition rule (
2
)
induces a
transition
between configurations
\left(p,\mu,t_{0}\right)\xrightarrow{a,t}\left(q,\nu,t\right)

labelled by
(a,t)\in\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
whenever
t\geq t_{0}
,
{t}-{\mu}\models\varphi
, and
\nu=\mu[\mathtt{Y}\mapsto t]
.
The
timed transition system
induced by
A

is
\left(\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket,\xrightarrow{},F\right)
,
where
\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket
is the set of configurations,

{\xrightarrow{}}\subseteq\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket\times\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\times\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket

is as defined above, and

F\subseteq\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket
is the set of final configurations.
Since there is no danger of confusion,
we use
\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket
to denote either the timed transition system above, or its domain.
A
run
of
A

over
a timed word
w
as in (
1
)

starting
in configuration
\left(p,\mu,t_{0}\right)

and
ending
in configuration
\left(q,\nu,t_{n}\right)

is a path
\rho
in
\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket
of the form

\rho=\left(p,\mu,t_{0}\right)\xrightarrow{a_{1},t_{1}}\ \dots\xrightarrow{a_{n},t_{n}}\ \left(q,\nu,t_{n}\right)
. The run
\rho
is accepting if its last configuration satisfies
\left(q,\nu,t_{n}\right)\in F
.
The language
recognised
by configuration
(p,\mu,t_{0})
is defined as:

\displaystyle L_{\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket}(p,\mu,t_{0})=\left\{w\in\mathbb{T}(\Sigma)\;\middle|\;\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket\text{ has an accepting run over }w\text{ starting in }\left(p,\mu,t_{0}\right)\right\}.

Clearly
L_{\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket}(p,\mu,t_{0})\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\geq{t_{0}}}(\Sigma)
.
We write
L_{A}(c)
instead of
L_{\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket}(c)
.
The language recognised by the automaton
A
is

L(A)=\bigcup_{c\text{ initial}}L_{A}(c)
.
A configuration is
reachable
if it is the ending configuration in a run starting in an initial configuration.
In an always resetting
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{\textnormal{\_},m}}
,
every reachable configuration
(p,\mu,t_{0})
satisfies
t_{0}\in\mu(\mathtt{X})
,
and in a greedily resetting one,
1)
(p,\mu,t_{0})
has
m
-bounded span
,
in the sense that
\mu(\mathtt{X})\subseteq(t_{0}-m,t_{0}]
,
and moreover 2) any two clocks
\mathtt{x},\mathtt{y}
with integer difference

\mu(\mathtt{x})-\mu(\mathtt{y})\in\mathbb{Z}
are actually equal
\mu(\mathtt{x})=\mu(\mathtt{y})
.
Condition 2) follows from the fact that if
\mathtt{x},\mathtt{y}
have integer difference and
\mathtt{y}
was reset last,
then
\mathtt{x}
was itself an integer when this happened,
and in fact they were both reset together in a greedily resetting automaton.

Deterministic timed automata.

A timed automaton
A
is
deterministic
if it has exactly one initial location
and, for every two rules

\left(p,a,\varphi,\mathtt{Y},q\right),\left(p,a^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},\mathtt{Y}^{\prime},q^{\prime}\right)\in\Delta
,
if
a=a^{\prime}
and
\left\llbracket\varphi\land\varphi^{\prime}\right\rrbracket\neq\emptyset
then
\mathtt{Y}=\mathtt{Y}^{\prime}
and
q=q^{\prime}
.
Hence
A
has at most one run over every timed word
w
.
A
dta
can be easily transformed to a
total
one, where for every location
p\in\mathtt{L}
and
a\in\Sigma
,
the sets defined by clock constraints

\left\{\left\llbracket\varphi\right\rrbracket\;\middle|\;\exists\mathtt{Y},q\cdot\left(p,a,\varphi,\mathtt{Y},q\right)\in\Delta\right\}

are a partition of
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{\mathtt{X}}
.
Thus, a total
dta
has exactly one run over every timed word
w
.
We write
dta
for the class of deterministic timed automata,
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
,
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{\textnormal{\_},m}}
, and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
for the respective subclasses thereof.
A timed language is called
nta
language,
dta
language, etc.,
if it is recognised by a timed automaton of the respective type.

Example 4
.

Let
\Sigma=\left\{a\right\}
be a unary alphabet.
As an example of a timed language
L
recognised by a
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
,
but not by any
dta
,
consider the set of non-negative timed words of the form

(a,t_{1})\cdots(a,t_{n})

where
t_{n}-t_{i}=1
for some
1\leq i<n
.
The language
L
is recognised by the
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}

A=\left(\Sigma,\mathtt{L},\mathtt{X},\mathtt{I},\mathtt{F},\Delta\right)

with a single clock
\mathtt{X}=\left\{\mathtt{x}\right\}
and three locations
\mathtt{L}=\left\{p,q,r\right\}
,
of which
\mathtt{I}=\left\{p\right\}
is initial and
\mathtt{F}=\left\{r\right\}
is final, and transition rules

\displaystyle\left(p,a,\mathbf{true},\emptyset,p\right)\qquad\left(p,a,\mathbf{true},\left\{\mathtt{x}\right\},q\right)\qquad\left(q,a,\mathtt{x}<1,\emptyset,q\right)\qquad\left(q,a,\mathtt{x}=1,\emptyset,r\right).

Intuitively, in
p
the automaton waits until it guesses that the next input will be
(a,t_{i})
,
at which point it moves to
q
by resetting the clock (and subsequently reading
a
).
From
q
, the automaton can accept by going to
r
only if exactly one time unit elapsed since
(a,t_{i})
was read.
The language
L
is not recognised by any
dta
since, intuitively, any deterministic acceptor needs to store unboundedly many timestamps
t_{i}
’s.

Deterministic membership problems.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a subclass of
nta
.
We are interested in the following decision problem.

\mathcal{X}
membership problem
.

Input:
A timed automaton
A\in

nta
.

Output:
Does there exist a
B\in\mathcal{X}
s.t.
L(A)=L(B)
?

In the rest of the paper, we study the decidability status of the
\mathcal{X}
membership problem
where
\mathcal{X}
ranges over
dta
,
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
(for every fixed number of clocks
k
),

{\textsf{\sc dta}_{\textnormal{\_},m}}
(for every maximal constant
m
),
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}

\Cref

Timed automorphisms and invariance

A fundamental tool in this paper is invariance properties of timed languages recognised by
nta
with respect
to permutations of
{\mathbb{R}}
preserving integer differences.
In this section we establish these properties.
A
timed automorphism
is a monotone bijection
\pi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}

s.t. for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\pi(x+1)=\pi(x)+1
.
For instance, if
\pi(3.4)=4.5
, then necessarily
\pi(5.4)=6.5
and
\pi(-3.6)=-2.5
.
Timed automorphisms
\pi
are extended point-wise to
timed words
\pi((a_{1},t_{1})\dots(a_{n},t_{n}))=(a_{0},\pi(t_{1}))\dots(a_{n},\pi(t_{n}))
,
configurations
\pi(p,\mu,t_{0})=(p,\pi{\circ}\mu,\pi(t_{0}))
,
transitions

\pi(c\xrightarrow{a,t}c^{\prime})=\pi(c)\xrightarrow{a,\pi(t)}\pi(c^{\prime})
,
and sets
X
thereof
\pi(X)=\left\{\pi(x)\;\middle|\;x\in X\right\}
.

Remark 5
.

A timed automorphism
\pi
can in general take a nonnegative real
t\geq 0
to a negative one.
Whenever we write
\pi(x)
,
we always implicitly assume that
\pi
is defined on
x
.

Let
S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
.
An
S
-timed automorphism
is a timed automorphism s.t.
\pi(t)=t
for all
t\in S
.
Let
\Pi_{S}
denote the set of all
S
-timed automorphisms, and let
\Pi=\Pi_{\emptyset}
.
A set
X
is
S
-invariant

if
\pi(X)=X
for every
\pi\in\Pi_{S}
;
equivalently,
for every
\pi\in\Pi_{S}
,

x\in X
if, and only if
\pi(x)\in X
.
A set
X
is
invariant
if it is
S
-invariant with
S=\emptyset
.
The following three facts express some basic invariance properties.

Fact 6
.

The timed transition system
\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket
is invariant.

By unrolling the definition of invariance in the previous fact,
we obtain that the set of configurations is invariant,
the set of transitions
{\xrightarrow{}}
is invariant,
and that the set of final configurations
F
is invariant.

Fact 7

(Invariance of the language semantics)
.

The function
c\mapsto L_{A}(c)
from
\left\llbracket A\right\rrbracket
to languages is invariant,
i.e., for all timed permutations
\pi
,

L_{A}(\pi(c))=\pi(L_{A}(c))
.

Fact 8

(Invariance of the language of a configuration)
.

The language
L_{A}(p,\mu,t_{0})
is

(\mu(\mathtt{X})\cup\left\{t_{0}\right\})
-invariant. Moreover, if
A
is always resetting,
then
L_{A}(p,\mu,t_{0})
is

\mu(\mathtt{X})
-invariant.

Since timed automorphisms preserve integer differences,
only the fractional parts of elements of
S\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
matter
for
S
-invariance, and hence it makes sense to restrict to subsets of the half-open interval
[0,1)
.
Let
\mathsf{fract}({S})=\left\{\mathsf{fract}({x})\;\middle|\;x\in S\right\}\subseteq[0,1)
stand for the set of fractional parts of elements of
S
.
The following lemma shows that,
modulo the irrelevant integer parts,
there is always the least set
S
witnessing
S
-invariance.

Lemma 9
.

For finite subsets
S,S^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
,
if a timed language
L
is both
S
-invariant and
S^{\prime}
-invariant, then it is also
S^{\prime\prime}
-invariant where
S^{\prime\prime}=\mathsf{fract}({S})\cap\mathsf{fract}({S^{\prime}})
.

The
S
-orbit
of an element
x\in X

(which can be an arbitrary object on which the action of
timed automorphisms is defined)
is the set

\text{\sc orbit}_{S}(x)=\left\{\pi(x)\in X\;\middle|\;\pi\in\Pi_{S}\right\}

of all elements
\pi(x)
which can be obtained by applying some
S
-automorphism to
x
.
The
orbit
of
x
is just its
S
-orbit with
S=\emptyset
,
written
\text{\sc orbit}(x)
.
Clearly
x
and
x^{\prime}
have the same
S
-orbit
if, and only if,
\pi(x)=x^{\prime}
for some

\pi\in\Pi_{S}
.
For greedily resetting
nta
,
orbits of single configurations are in bijective correspondence with bounded regions.

Fact 10
.

Assume
A
is a greedily resetting
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k,m}}
.
Two reachable configurations
(p,\mu,t_{0})
and
(p,\mu^{\prime},t_{0}^{\prime})
of
A
with the same control location
p

have the same orbit
if, and only if,
the corresponding clock valuations
{t_{0}}-{\mu}
and

{t_{0}^{\prime}}-{\mu^{\prime}}

belong to the same
k,m
-region.

The
S
-closure
of a set
Y
, written
\Pi_{S}(Y)=\bigcup_{x\in Y}\text{\sc orbit}_{S}(x)
,
is the union of the
S
-orbits of all its elements.
The following fact characterises invariance in term of closures.

Fact 11
.

A set
Y
is
S
-invariant if, and only if,
\Pi_{S}(Y)=Y
.

Proof.

Only if direction follows by the definition of
S
-invariance. For the converse direction observe that

\Pi_{S}(X)=X
implies
\pi(X)\subseteq X
for every
\pi\in\Pi_{S}
.
The opposite inclusion follows by closure of
S
-timed automorphisms under inverse:

\pi^{-1}(X)\subseteq X
, hence
X\subseteq\pi(X)
.
∎

{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}

{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}

In this section we prove

establishing decidability of the
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
membership problems for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
.
Both results are shown using the following key characterisation of

{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
languages.
In particular, this characterisation provides a small bound on the number of control locations of a
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
equivalent to a given
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
(if any exists).

Lemma 12
.

Let
A
be a
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1,m}}
with
n
control locations,
and let

.
The following conditions are equivalent:

1.

L(A)=L(B)
for some always resetting
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}

B
.

For every timed word

, there is

of size at most
k
s.t. the last timestamp of

is in

and

is
S
-invariant.

3.

L(A)=L(B)
for some always resetting
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}

with at most

(

stands for the number of

-regions).

The proof of
\Cref
thm:kDTA:memb builds on
\Cref
thm:k-DTA-char
and on the following fact:

Lemma 13
.

The
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
membership problems are both decidable for
dta
languages.

Proof.

We reduce to a deterministic separability problem.
Recall that a language
S

separates
two languages

if

and

.
It has recently been shown that the

and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}

are decidable for

[

, Theorem 1.1]
,
and thus, in particular, for
dta
.
To solve the membership problem,
given a

A
, the procedure computes a

recognising the complement of

and checks whether

and
A^{\prime}
are
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
separable (resp.,
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
separable)
by using the result above.
It is a simple set-theoretic observation that

is a
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
language if, and only if, the languages

and

are separated
by some
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}

{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}

∎

Proof of
\Cref
thm:kDTA:memb.

We solve both problems in essentially the same way.
Given a
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1,m}}

,
the decision procedure enumerates all always resetting

with at most

and checks whether

(which is decidable by
[

]
).
If no such

the

is not an always resetting

the procedure therefore answers negatively.
Otherwise, in case when such a
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k+1}}

is found, then
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
membership
(resp.
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k,m}}
membership) test is performed on

\Cref

∎

(Complexity)
.

The implication
3

Every left quotient

It thus remains to prove the implication

Assuming Point

, we
are going to define an always resetting

We start from the timed transition system

Macro-configurations.

Configurations of the

We use the notation

Let

.
We define a deterministic timed transition system
\mathcal{X}
consisting of

\mathcal{X}
be the smallest set of macro-configurations and transitions such that

•

\mathcal{X}

Claim 15
.

L_{A}(X)=L_{\mathcal{X}}(X)
for every

. In particular
L(A)=L_{\mathcal{X}}(X_{0})

For a macro-configuration

Pre-states.

By assumption (Point 2),

-invariant for some

In this section we complete the decidability status of the deterministic membership problem

In
\cref

{\textsf{\sc dta}_{m}}
embership problem for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
(c.f.
\cref

and in
\cref

-hardness of the
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{k}}
membership problem for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
(c.f.
\cref

5.1
Undecidability of
dta
and
{\textsf{\sc dta}_{\textnormal{\_},m}}
membership for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}

It has been shown in
[
23
, Theorem 1]
that it is undecidable
whether a
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k}}
timed language can be recognised by some
dta
, for any fixed
k\geq 2
.
This was obtained by a reduction from the
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{k}}
universality problem,
which is undecidable for any fixed
k\geq 2
.
While the universality problem becomes decidable for
k=1
,
we show in this section that, as announced in
\cref
thm:undecidability,
the
dta
membership problem remains undecidable for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
.

Since the universality problem for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
is decidable,
we need to reduce from another (undecidable) problem.
Our candidate is the finiteness problem of lossy counter machines,
which is undecidable
[
36
, Theorem 13]
.
A
k
-counters lossy counter machine
(
k
-
LCM
) is a tuple
M=\left(C,Q,q_{0},\Delta\right)
,
where
C=\left\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\right\}
is a set of
k
counters,

Q
is a finite set of control locations,

q_{0}\in Q
is the initial control location,
and
\Delta
is a finite set of instructions of the form
\left(p,\mathtt{op},q\right)
,
where
\mathtt{op}
is one of
c\,\texttt{++}
,
c\,\texttt{--}
, and
c\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\texttt{=}}}0
.
A configuration of an
LCM

M
is a pair
\left(p,u\right)
,
where
p\in Q
is a control location,
and
u\in\mathbb{N}^{C}
is a counter valuation.
For two counter valuations
u,v\in\mathbb{N}^{C}
,
we write
u\leq v
if
u(c)\leq v(c)
for every counter
c\in C
.
The semantics of an
LCM

M
is given by a (potentially infinite) transition system over the configurations of
M

s.t. there is a transition
\left(p,u\right)\xrightarrow{\delta}\left(q,v\right)
,
for

\delta=\left(p,\mathtt{op},q\right)\in\Delta
, whenever

1)

\mathtt{op}=c\,\texttt{++}
and
v\leq u[c\mapsto u(c)+1]
, or

2)

\mathtt{op}=c\,\texttt{--}
and
v\leq u[c\mapsto u(c)-1]
, or

3)

\mathtt{op}=c\stackrel{{\scriptstyle?}}{{\texttt{=}}}0
and
u(c)=0
and
v\leq u
.

The
finiteness problem
(a.k.a. space boundedness) for an
LCM

M

asks to decide whether the reachability set

\text{Reach}(M)=\left\{\left(p,u\right)\;\middle|\;\left(q_{0},u_{0}\right)\xrightarrow{}^{*}\left(p,u\right)\right\}

is finite, where
u_{0}
is the constantly
0
counter valuation.

Theorem 28

(
[
36
, Theorem 13]
)
.

The
4
-
LCM
finiteness problem is undecidable.

We use the following encoding of
LCM
runs
as timed words over the alphabet
\Sigma=Q\cup\Delta\cup C

(c.f.
[
34
, Definition 4.6]
for a similar encoding).
We interpret a counter valuation
u\in\mathbb{N}^{C}

as the word over
\Sigma

\displaystyle u\;=\;\underbrace{c_{1}c_{1}\cdots c_{1}}_{u(c_{1})\text{ letters}}\ \underbrace{c_{2}c_{2}\cdots c_{2}}_{u(c_{2})\text{ letters}}\ \underbrace{c_{3}c_{3}\cdots c_{3}}_{u(c_{3})\text{ letters}}\ \underbrace{c_{4}c_{4}\cdots c_{4}}_{u(c_{4})\text{ letters}}.

With this interpretation, we encode an
LCM
run

\pi\;=\;\left(p_{0},u_{0}\right)\xrightarrow{\delta_{1}}\left(p_{1},u_{1}\right)\xrightarrow{\delta_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\delta_{n}}\left(p_{n},u_{n}\right)

as the following timed word,
called the
reversal-encoding
of
\pi

\displaystyle p_{n}\delta_{n}u_{n}\quad\cdots\quad p_{1}\delta_{1}u_{1}\quad p_{0}u_{0},

s.t.
p_{n}
occurs at time 0,
for every
1\leq i<n
,
p_{i}
occurs exactly after one time unit since
p_{i+1}
,
and if a “unit” of counter
c_{1}
did not disappear due to lossiness when going from
u_{i}
to
u_{i+1}
,
then the timestamps of the corresponding occurrences of letter
c_{1}
in
u_{i}
and
u_{i+1}
are also at distance one
(and similarly for the other counters).
Under the encoding above, we can build a
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}

A
recognising the complement of the
set of reversal-encodings of the runs of
M

(
[
34
]
for more details about the construction of

Intuitively, when reading the reversal-encoding of a run of
M
, the counters are allowed to spontaneously increase.
Therefore, the only kind of error that
A
must verify is that some counter spontaneously decreases.
This can be done by guessing an occurrence of letter (say)
c_{1}
in the current configuration
which does not have a corresponding occurrence in the next configuration after exactly one time unit.
This check can be performed by an
nta
with one clock.

Lemma 29

All the lower bounds in this section

The reduction is a suitable adaptation, generalization, and simplification

A timed language

L\rhd\left\{\$\right\}\rhd M\ =\ \left\{v(\$,t)(a_{1},t_{1}+t)\dots(a_{n},t_{n}+t)\in\mathbb{T}(\Sigma^{\prime})\;\middle|\;v\in L,(a_{1},t_{1})\dots(a_{n},t_{n})\in M\right\}.

(restate = lemEasyUndec, name = )
.

Let

and let

be a class of timed languages that

We immediately obtain

First, the lemma is applicable by taking as

the classes of languages recognised by

since this class contains all timeless timed languages,

6
Conclusions

We have shown decidability and undecidability results
for several variants of the deterministic membership problem for timed automata.
Regarding undecidability, we have extended the previously known results

[
23
,
43
]

by proving that the
dta
membership problem is undecidable already for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}
(
\cref
thm:undecidability),
and, over a unary input alphabet, it is undecidable for
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1,m}}
with
m\geq 6
(
\cref
remark:compression).
We leave open the question of what is the minimal
m
guaranteeing undecidability.
Regarding decidability, we have shown that when the resources available to the deterministic automaton are fixed
(either just the number of clocks
k
, or both clocks
k
and maximal constant
m
),
then the respective deterministic membership problem is decidable (
\cref
thm:kDTA:memb)
and
HyperAckermann
-hard (
\cref
thm:hardness).

Our deterministic membership algorithm is based on a characterisation of
{\textsf{\sc nta}_{1}}

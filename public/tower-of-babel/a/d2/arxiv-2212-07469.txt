Learning threshold neurons via the “edge of stability”
Kwangjun Ahn, Sébastien Bubeck, Sinho Chewi, Yin Tat Lee, Felipe Suarez, Yi Zhang

Abstract
Existing analyses of neural network training often operate under the unrealistic assumption of an extremely small learning rate. This lies in stark contrast to practical wisdom and empirical studies, such as the work of J. Cohen et al. (ICLR 2021), which exhibit startling new phenomena (the “edge of stability” or “unstable convergence”) and potential benefits for generalization in the large learning rate regime. Despite a flurry of recent works on this topic, however, the latter effect is still poorly understood. In this paper, we take a step towards understanding genuinely non-convex training dynamics with large learning rates by performing a detailed analysis of gradient descent for simplified models of two-layer neural networks. For these models, we provably establish the edge of stability phenomenon and discover a sharp phase transition for the step size below which the neural network fails to learn “threshold-like” neurons (i.e., neurons with a non-zero first-layer bias). This elucidates one possible mechanism by which the edge of stability can in fact lead to better generalization, as threshold neurons are basic building blocks with useful inductive bias for many tasks.

Introduction

How much do we understand about the training dynamics of neural networks?
We begin with a simple and canonical learning task which indicates that the answer is still “far too little”.

Motivating example:

1.1
Main scope of this work

In this work, we do not aim to understand the sparse coding problem (
1
) in its full generality.
Instead, we pursue the more modest goal of shedding light on the following question.

Q.
What is the role of a large step size in learning the bias for the ReLU network (
2
)?

As discussed above, the dynamics of the simple ReLU network (
2
) is a microcosm of emergent phenomena beyond the convex optimization regime.
In fact, there is a recent growing body of work
(
Cohen et al. 2021
;
Arora et al. 2022
;
Ahn et al. 2022
;
Lyu et al. 2022
;
Ma et al. 2022
;
Wang et al. 2022b
;
Chen and Bruna 2023
;
Damian et al. 2023
;
Zhu et al. 2023
)
on training with large learning rates,
which largely aims at explaining a striking empirical observation
called the “
edge of stability (EoS)

The edge of stability (EoS) phenomenon is a set of distinctive behaviors observed recently by
Cohen et al. 2021
when training neural networks with gradient descent (GD).
Here we briefly summarize the salient features of the EoS and
defer a discussion of prior work to
Subsection 1.3
.
Recall that if we use GD to optimize an
L

\eta
, then the well-known descent lemma from convex optimization ensures monotonic decrease in the loss so long as
L<2/\eta
.
In contrast, when
L>2/\eta
, it is easy to see on simple convex quadratic examples that GD can be unstable (or divergent).
The main observation of
Cohen et al. 2021
is that when training neural networks
1

with constant step size
\eta>0
, the largest eigenvalue of the Hessian at the current iterate (dubbed the “sharpness”) initially increases during training (“progressive sharpening”) and saturates near or above

A surprising message of the present work is that
the answer to our main question is intimately related to the EoS

Indeed,

Consequently, we first set out to thoroughly understand the workings of the EoS phenomena through a simple example.
Specifically, we consider a single-neuron linear neural network in dimension

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}\ni(x,y)\mapsto\ell(xy)\,,\qquad\text{where $\ell$ is convex, even, and Lipschitz}\,.

We then use the newfound insights gleaned from the analysis of (

To the best of our knowledge, we provide the first explanation of the mechanism by which a large learning rate can be

Learning threshold neurons with the mean model.

The connection between the single-neuron example and the ReLU network (
2
) can already be anticipated via a comparison of the dynamics:
(i)
for the single neuron example,

(ii)

1.3
Related work

Edge of stability.
Our work is motivated by the extensive empirical study of
Cohen et al. 2021
, which identified the EoS phenomenon. Subsequently, there has been a flurry of works aiming at developing a theoretical understanding of the EoS, which we briefly summarize here.

Properties of the loss landscape.
The works
(
Ahn et al. 2022
;
Ma et al. 2022
)
study the properties of the loss landscape that lead to the EoS. Namely,
Ahn et al. 2022
argue that the existence of forward-invariant subsets near the minimizers allows GD to convergence even in the unstable regime.
They also explore various characteristics of EoS in terms of loss and iterates. Also,
Ma et al. 2022
empirically show that the loss landscape of neural networks exhibits subquadratic growth locally around the minimizers. They prove that for a one-dimensional loss, subquadratic growth implies that GD finds a
2
-periodic trajectory.

Limiting dynamics.
Other works characterize the limiting dynamics of the EoS in various regimes.
(
Arora et al. 2022
;
Lyu et al. 2022
)
show that (normalized) GD tracks a “sharpness reduction flow” near the manifold of minimizers. The recent work of
Damian et al. 2023
obtains a different predicted dynamics based on self-stabilization of the GD trajectory. Also,
Ma et al. 2022
describes a quasi-static heuristic for the overall trajectory of GD when one component of the iterate is oscillating.

Simple models and beyond.
Closely related to our own approach, there are prior works which carefully study simple models.
Chen and Bruna 2023
prove global convergence of GD for the two-dimensional function
(x,y)\mapsto{(xy-1)}^{2}
and a single-neuron student-teacher setting; note that unlike our results, they do not study the limiting sharpness.
Wang et al. 2022b
study progressive sharpening for a neural network model. Also, the recent and concurrent work of
Zhu et al. 2023
studies the two-dimensional loss
(x,y)\mapsto(x^{2}y^{2}-1)^{2}
; to our knowledge, their work is the first to asymptotically and rigorously show that the limiting sharpness of GD is
2/\eta
in a simple setting, at least when initialized locally. In comparison, in
Section 2
, we perform a global analysis of the limiting sharpness of GD for
(x,y)\mapsto\ell(xy)
for a class of convex, even, and Lipschitz losses
\ell
, and in doing so we clearly delineate the “gradient flow regime” from the “EoS regime”.

Effect of learning rate on learning.
Recently, several works have sought to understand how the choice of learning rate affects the learning process, in terms of the properties of the resulting minima
(
Jastrzebski et al. 2018
;
Wu et al. 2018
;
Mulayoff et al. 2021
;
Nacson et al. 2022
)
and the behavior of optimization dynamics
(
Xing et al. 2018
;
Jastrzebski et al. 2019
;
Jastrzebski et al. 2020
;
Lewkowycz et al. 2020
;
Jastrzebski et al. 2021
)
.

Li et al. 2019
demonstrate for a synthethic data distribution and a two-layer ReLU network model that choosing a larger step size for SGD helps with generalization.
Subsequent works have shown similar phenomena for regression
(
Nakkiran 2020
;
Wu et al. 2021
;
Ba et al. 2022
)
, kernel ridge regression
Beugnot et al. 2022
, and linear diagonal networks
Nacson et al. 2022
.
However, the large step sizes considered in these work still fall under the scope of descent lemma, and most prior works do not theoretically investigate the effect of large step size in the EoS regime.
A notable exception is the work of
Wang et al. 2022a
, which studies the impact of learning rates greater than
2/\text{smoothness}
for a matrix factorization problem.
Also, the recent work of
Andriushchenko et al. 2023
seeks to explain the generalization benefit of SGD in the large step size regime by relying on a heuristic SDE model for the case of linear diagonal networks.
Despite this similarity, their main scope is quite different from ours, as we
(i)
focus on GD instead of SGD and
(ii)
establish a direct and detailed analysis of the GD dynamics for a model of the motivating sparse coding example.

2.2
Two different regimes for GD depending on the step size

Before stating rigorous results, in this section we begin by giving an intuitive understanding of the GD dynamics.
It turns out that for a given initialization
(x_{0},y_{0})
, there are two different regimes for the GD dynamics depending on the step size
\eta
.
Namely, there exists a threshold on the step size such that
(i)
below the threshold, GD remains close to the gradient flow for all time, and
(ii)
above the threshold, GD enters the edge of stability and diverges away from the gradient flow. See
Figure 8
.

First, recall that the GD dynamics are symmetric in
x,y
and that the lines
y=\pm x
are invariant.
Hence, we may assume without loss of generality that

\displaystyle y_{0}>x_{0}>0\,,\quad y_{t}>\lvert x_{t}\rvert~~\text{for all}~t\geq 1\,,\quad\text{and GD converges to $(0,y_{\infty})$ for $y_{\infty}>0$}\,.

From the expression (
11
) for the Hessian of
f
and our normalization
\ell^{\prime\prime}(0)=1
, it follows that the sharpness (the largest eigenvalue of loss Hessian) reached by GD in this example is precisely
y_{\infty}^{2}
.

Initially, in both regimes, the GD dynamics tracks the continuous-time gradient flow.
Our first observation is that the gradient flow admits a conserved quantity, thereby allowing us to predict the dynamics in this initial phase.

Lemma 1

(conserved quantity)
.

Along the gradient flow for
f
, the quantity
y^{2}-x^{2}
is conserved.

Proof.

Differentiating
y_{t}^{2}-x_{t}^{2}
with respect to
t
gives
2y_{t}\,(-\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,x_{t})-2x_{t}\,(-\ell(x_{t}y_{t})\,y_{t})=0
.
∎

1
implies that the gradient flow converges to
(0,y^{\mathrm{GF}}_{\infty})=(0,\sqrt{y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}})
.
For GD with step size
\eta>0
, the quantity
y^{2}-x^{2}
is no longer conserved, but we show in
6
that it is
approximately
conserved until the GD iterate lies close to the
y
-axis.
Hence, GD initialized at
(x_{0},y_{0})
also reaches the
y
-axis approximately at the point
(x_{t_{0}},y_{t_{0}})\approx(0,\sqrt{y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}})
.

At this point, GD either approximately converges to the gradient flow solution
(0,\sqrt{y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}})
or diverges away from it, depending on whether or not
y_{t_{0}}^{2}>2/\eta
.
To see this, for
\lvert x_{t_{0}}y_{t_{0}}\rvert\ll 1
, we can Taylor expand
\ell^{\prime}
near zero to obtain the approximate dynamics for
x
(recalling
\ell^{\prime\prime}(0)=1
),

\displaystyle\begin{split}x_{t_{0}+1}&\approx x_{t_{0}}-\eta x_{t_{0}}y_{t_{0}}^{2}=(1-\eta y_{t_{0}}^{2})\,x_{t_{0}}\,.\end{split}

(5)

From (
5
), we deduce the following conclusions.

(i)

If
y_{t_{0}}^{2}<2/\eta
, then
\lvert 1-\eta y_{t_{0}}^{2}\rvert<1
.
Since
y_{t}
is decreasing, it implies that
\lvert 1-\eta y_{t}^{2}\rvert<1
for all
t\geq t_{0}
, and so
\lvert x_{t}\rvert
converges to zero exponentially fast.

(ii)

On the other hand, if
y_{t_{0}}^{2}>2/\eta
, then
\lvert 1-\eta y_{t_{0}}^{2}\rvert>1
, i.e., the magnitude of
x_{t_{0}}
increases in the next iteration, and hence GD cannot stabilize.
In fact, in the approximate dynamics,
x_{{t_{0}}+1}
has the opposite sign as
x_{t_{0}}
, i.e.,
x_{t_{0}}
jumps across the
y
-axis.
One can show that the “bouncing” of the
x

y_{t}^{2}
has decreased past
2/\eta
, at which point we are in the previous case and GD approximately converges to
(0,2/\eta)
.

This reasoning, combined with the expression for the Hessian of

, shows that

\displaystyle\mathsf{sharpness}(0,y_{\infty})\coloneqq\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\nabla^{2}f(0,y_{\infty})\bigr)

\displaystyle\approx\min\bigl\{y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2},\,2/\eta\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\min\{\text{gradient flow sharpness},\,\text{EoS prediction}\}\,.

Accordingly, we refer to the case
y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}<2/\eta
as the
gradient flow regime
, and the case
y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}>2/\eta
as the
EoS regime
.

See
Figure 5
and
Figure 8
for illustrations of these two regimes; see also
Figure 9
for detailed illustrations of the EoS regime.
In the subsequent sections, we aim to make the above reasoning rigorous. For example, instead of the approximate dynamics (
5
), we consider the original GD dynamics and justify the Taylor approximation. Also, in the EoS regime, rather than loosely asserting that
\lvert x_{t}\rvert\searrow 0
exponentially fast and hence the dynamics stabilizes “quickly” once
y_{t}^{2}<2/\eta
, we track precisely how long this convergence takes so that we can bound the gap between the limiting sharpness and the prediction
2/\eta
.

Gradient flow regime.

Our first rigorous result is that when

for some constant
\delta\in(0,2)
, then the limiting sharpness of GD with step size
\eta
is
y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}+O(1)=\nicefrac{{(2-\delta)}}{{\eta}}+O(1)
, which is precisely the sharpness attained by the gradient flow up to a controlled error term.

In fact, our theorem is slightly more general, as it covers initializations in which
\delta
can scale mildly with
\eta
. The precise statement is as follows.

Theorem 1

(gradient flow regime; see
Subsection C.2
)
.

Suppose we run GD with step size
\eta>0
on the objective
f
, where
f(x,y)\coloneqq\ell(xy)
, and
\ell
satisfies Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A2)
.
Let
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfy
\tilde{y}>\tilde{x}>0
with
\tilde{y}^{2}-\tilde{x}^{2}=1
.
Suppose we initialize GD at
(x_{0},y_{0})\coloneqq(\frac{2-\delta}{\eta})^{1/2}\,(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, where
\delta\in(0,2)
and
\eta\lesssim\delta^{1/2}\wedge(2-\delta)
.
Then, GD converges to
(0,y_{\infty})
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{2-\delta}{\eta}-O(2-\delta)-O\Bigl(\frac{\eta}{\min\{\delta,2-\delta\}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\nabla^{2}f(0,y_{\infty})\bigr)\leq\frac{2-\delta}{\eta}+O\Bigl(\frac{\eta}{2-\delta}\Bigr)\,,

where the implied constants depend on
\tilde{x}
,
\tilde{y}
, and
\ell
, but not on
\delta
,
\eta
.

The proof of
Theorem 1
is based on a two-stage analysis.
In the first stage, we use Lemma
6
on the approximate conservation of
y^{2}-x^{2}
along GD in order to show that GD lands near the
y
-axis with
y_{t_{0}}^{2}\approx\nicefrac{{(2-\delta)}}{{\eta}}
. In the second stage, we use the assumptions on
\ell
in order to control the rate of convergence of
\lvert x_{t}\rvert
to
0
, which is subsequently used to control the final deviation of
y_{\infty}^{2}
from
\nicefrac{{(2-\delta)}}{{\eta}}
.

EoS regime.

Our next result states that when
y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}>2/\eta
, then the limiting sharpness of GD is close to the EoS prediction of
2/\eta
, up to an error term which depends on the exponent
\beta
in
(A2)
.

Theorem 2

(EoS; see
Subsection C.4
)
.

Suppose we run GD on
f
with step size
\eta>0
, where
f(x,y)\coloneqq\ell(xy)
, and
\ell
satisfies
(A1)
and
(A2)
.
Let
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfy
\tilde{y}>\tilde{x}>0
with
\tilde{y}^{2}-\tilde{x}^{2}=1
.
Suppose we initialize GD at
(x_{0},y_{0})\coloneqq\sqrt{\nicefrac{{(2+\delta)}}{{\eta}}}\,(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, where
\delta>0
is a constant.
Also, assume that for all
t\geq 1
such that
y_{t}^{2}>2/\eta
, we have
x_{t}\neq 0
.
Then, GD converges to
(0,y_{\infty})
satisfying

\displaystyle 2/\eta-O(\eta^{1/(\beta-1)})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\nabla^{2}f(0,y_{\infty})\bigr)\leq 2/\eta\,,

where the implied constants depend on
\tilde{x}
,
\tilde{y}
,
\delta\wedge 1
, and
\ell
, but not on
\eta
.

Remarks on the assumptions.
The initialization in our results is such that both
y_{0}
and
y_{0}-x_{0}
are on the same scale, i.e.,
y_{0},y_{0}-x_{0}=\Theta(1/\sqrt{\eta})
. This rules out extreme initializations such as
y_{0}\approx x_{0}
, which are problematic because they lie too close to the invariant line
y=x
. Since our aim in this work is not to explore every edge case, we focus on this setting for simplicity. Moreover, we imposed the assumption that the iterates of GD do not exactly hit the
y
-axis before crossing
y^{2}=2/\eta
. This is necessary because if
x_{t}=0
for some iteration
t
, then
(x_{t^{\prime}},y_{t^{\prime}})=(x_{t},y_{t})
for all
t^{\prime}>t
, and hence the limiting sharpness may not be close to
2/\eta
. This assumption holds generically, e.g., if we perturb each iterate of GD with a vanishing amount of noise from a continuous distribution, and we conjecture that for any
\eta>0
, the assumption holds for all but a measure zero set of initializations.

When
\beta=+\infty
, which is the case for the Huber loss in
1
, the limiting sharpness is
2/\eta+O(1)
.
When
\beta=2
, which is the case for the logistic and square root losses in
1
, the limiting sharpness is
2/\eta+O(\eta)
.
Numerical experiments show that
our error bound of
O(\eta^{1/(\beta-1)})
is sharp
; see
Figure 10
below.

We make a few remark about the proof. As we outline the proof in
Subsection C.3
, in turns out in order to bound the gap
2/\eta-y_{\infty}^{2}
, the proof requires a control of the size
\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}y_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert
, where
{\swabfamily t}
is the first iteration such that
y_{{\swabfamily t}}^{2}
crosses
2/\eta
. However, controlling the size of
\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}y_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert
is surprisingly delicate as it requires a fine-grained understanding of the bouncing phase.
The insight that guides the proof is the observation that during the bouncing phase, the GD iterates lie close to a certain envelope (
Figure 8
).

As a by-product of our analysis, we obtain a rigorous version of the quasi-static principle from which can more accurately track the sharpness gap and convergence rate (see
Subsection C.5
).

Theorem 1
,
Theorem 2
, and

3
Understanding the bias evolution of the ReLU network

In this section, we use the insights from
Section 2
to answer our main question, namely understanding the role of a large step size in learning threshold neurons for the ReLU network (
2
).
Based on the observed dynamics (
Figure 2
), we can make our question more concrete as follows.

Q.
What is the role of a large step size during the “
initial phase
” of training in which
(i)
the bias
b
rapidly decreases and
(ii)
the sum of weights
a^{-}+a^{+}
oscillates?

3.1
Approximating the initial phase of GD with the “mean model”

Deferring details to
Appendix D
, the GD dynamics for the ReLU network (
2
) in the initial phase are well-approximated by

\displaystyle\text{GD dynamics on }(a^{-},a^{+},b)\mapsto\ell_{\rm sym}(d\,(a^{-}+a^{+})\,g(b))\,,

(6)

where
\ell_{\rm sym}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\log(1+\exp(-s))+\log(1+\exp(+s)))
and
g(b)\coloneqq\E_{z\sim\NN(0,1)}\ReLU(z+b)
is the ‘smoothed’ ReLU.
The GD dynamics can be compactly written in terms of the parameter
A_{t}\coloneqq d\,(a^{-}_{t}+a^{+}_{t})
.

\displaystyle A_{t+1}

\displaystyle=A_{t}-2d^{2}\eta\,\ell_{\rm sym}^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,g(b_{t})\,,\qquad b_{t+1}=b_{t}-\eta\,\ell_{\rm sym}^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,A_{t}g^{\prime}(b_{t})\,.

(7)

Figure 7:
The ‘smoothed’ ReLU
g(b)

We call these dynamics
the mean model
.

Figure 13
shows that the mean model closely captures the GD dynamics for the ReLU network (
2
), and we henceforth focus on analyzing the mean model.

The main advantage of the representation (
7
) is that it makes apparent the connection to the single-neuron example that we studied in
Section 2
.
More specifically, (
7
) can be interpreted as the “rescaled” GD dynamics on the objective
(A,b)\mapsto\ell_{\rm sym}(Ag(b))
,
where the step size for the
A
-dynamics is multiplied by
2d^{2}
.
Due to this resemblance, we can apply the techniques from
Section 2
.

3.2
Two different regimes for the mean model

Throughout the section, we use the shorthand
\ell\coloneqq\ell_{\rm sym}
, and focus on initializing wiht
a_{0}^{\pm}=\Theta(1/d)
,
a^{-}+a^{+}\neq 0
, and
b_{0}=0
.
This implies
A_{0}=\Theta(1)
.
We also note the following fact for later use.

Lemma 2

(formula for the smoothed ReLU; see
Subsection E.1
)
.

The smoothed ReLU function
g
can be expressed in terms of the PDF
\varphi
and the CDF
\varPhi
of the standard Gaussian distribution as
g(b)=\varphi(b)+b\,\varPhi(b)
.
In particular,
g^{\prime}=\varPhi
.

Note also that
b_{t}
is monotonically decreasing. This is because
\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,A_{t}g^{\prime}(b_{t})\geq 0
since
\ell^{\prime}
is an odd function and
g(b),g^{\prime}(b)>0
for any
b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Following
Subsection 2.2
, we begin with the continuous-time dynamics of the mean model:

\displaystyle\dot{A}

\displaystyle=-2d^{2}\,\ell^{\prime}(Ag(b))\,g(b)\,,\qquad\dot{b}=-\ell^{\prime}(Ag(b))\,Ag^{\prime}(b)\,.

(8)

Lemma 3

(conserved quantity; see
Subsection E.1
)
.

Let
\kappa:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as
\kappa(b)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{b}g/g^{\prime}
. Along the gradient flow (
8
), the quantity
\frac{1}{2}A^{2}-2d^{2}\kappa(b)
is conserved.

Based on
3
, if we initialize the continuous-time dynamics (
8
) at
(A_{0},0)
and if
A_{t}\to 0
, then the limiting value of the bias
b_{\infty}^{\rm GF}
satisfies
\kappa(b_{\infty}^{\rm GF})=-\frac{1}{4d^{2}}\,A_{0}^{2}
, which implies that
b_{\infty}^{\rm GF}=-\Theta(\frac{1}{d^{2}})
; indeed, this holds since
\kappa^{\prime}(0)=g(0)/g^{\prime}(0)>0
, so there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
such that
c_{0}b\leq\kappa(b)\leq c_{1}b
for all
-1\leq b\leq 0
.
Since the mean model (
7
) tracks the continuous-time dynamics (
8
) until it reaches the
b
-axis, the mean model initialized at
(A_{0},0)
also approximately reaches
(A_{t_{0}},b_{t_{0}})\approx(0,-\Theta(\frac{1}{d^{2}}))\approx(0,0)
in high dimension
d\gg 1
.
In other words,
the continuous-time dynamics (
8
) fails to learn threshold neurons
.

Once the mean model reaches the
b
-axis,
we again identify two different regimes depending on the step size.
A Taylor expansion of
\ell^{\prime}
around the origin yields the following approximate dynamics (here
\ell^{\prime\prime}(0)=1/4
):
A_{t_{0}+1}\approx A_{t_{0}}-\frac{\eta d^{2}}{2}\,A_{t_{0}}\,{g(b_{t_{0}})}^{2}=A_{t_{0}}\,\bigl(1-\frac{\eta d^{2}}{2}\,{g(b_{t_{0}})}^{2}\bigr)
.
We conclude that the condition which now dictates whether we have bouncing or convergence is
\frac{1}{2}\,d^{2}g(b_{t_{0}})^{2}>2/\eta
.

(i)

Gradient flow regime:
If
2/\eta>d^{2}g(0)^{2}/2=d^{2}/(4\pi)
(since
g(0)^{2}={1}/{(2\pi)}
), i.e., the step size
\eta
is
below
the threshold
8\pi/d^{2}
, then the final bias of the mean model
b_{\infty}^{\rm MM}
satisfies
b_{\infty}^{\rm MM}\approx b_{\infty}^{\rm GF}\approx 0
.
In other words,
the mean model fails to learn threshold neurons
.

(ii)

EoS regime:
If
2/\eta<d^{2}/(4\pi)
, i.e., the step size
\eta
is
above
the threshold
8\pi/d^{2}
, then
\frac{1}{2}\,d^{2}g^{2}(b_{\infty}^{\rm MM})<2/\eta
, i.e.,
b_{\infty}^{\rm MM}<g^{-1}(2/\sqrt{\eta d^{2}})
.
For instance, if
\eta=\frac{10\pi}{d^{2}}
, then
b_{\infty}^{\rm MM}<-0.087
.
In other words,
the mean model successfully learns threshold neurons
.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we present the first explanation for the emergence of threshold neuron (i.e., ReLU neurons with negative bias) in models such as the sparse coding model (
1
) through a novel connection with the “edge of stability” (EoS) phenomenon.
Along the way, we obtain a detailed and rigorous understanding of the dynamics of GD in the EoS regime for a simple class of loss functions, thereby shedding light on the impact of large learning rates in non-convex optimization.

Our approach is largely inspired by the recent paradigm of “
physics-style
” approaches to understanding deep learning based on simplified models and controlled experiments (c.f.
(
Zhang et al. 2022
;
von Oswald et al. 2023
;
Abernethy et al. 2023
;
Allen-Zhu and Li 2023
;
Li et al. 2023
;
Ahn et al. 2023a
;
Ahn et al. 2023b
)
).
We found such physics-style approach quite effective to understand deep learning, especially given the complexity of modern deep neural networks. We hope that our work inspires further research on understanding the working mechanisms of deep learning.

Many interesting questions remain, and we conclude with some directions for future research.

•

Extending the analysis of EoS to richer models.

Although the analysis we present in this work is restricted to simple models, the underlying principles can potentially be applied to more general settings.
In this direction, it would be interesting to study models which capture the impact of the depth of the neural network on the EoS phenomenon.
Notably, a follow-up work by
Song and Yun 2023
uses bifurcation theory to extend our results to more complex models.

•

The interplay between the EoS and the choice of optimization algorithm.

As discussed in
Subsection 2.3
, the bouncing phase of the EoS substantially slows down the convergence of GD (see
Figure 10
). Investigating how different optimization algorithm (e.g., SGD, or GD with momentum) interact with the EoS phenomenon could potentially lead to practical speed-ups or improved generalization.
Notably, a follow up work by
Dai et al. 2023
studies the working mechanisms of a popular modern optimization technique called
sharpness-aware minimization

(
Foret et al. 2021
)
based on our sparse coding problem.

•

An end-to-end analysis of the sparse coding model.
Finally, we have left open the motivating question of analyzing how two-layer ReLU networks learn to solve the sparse coding model (
1
).
Despite the apparent simplicity of the problem, its analysis has thus far remained out of reach, and we believe that a resolution to this question would constitute compelling and substantial progress towards understanding neural network learning.
We are hopeful that the insights in this paper provide the first step towards this goal.

Appendix C
Proofs for the single-neuron linear network

We start by describing basic and relevant properties of the model and the assumptions on
\ell
.

Basic properties.

If
\ell
is minimized at
0
, then the
global minimizers
of
f
are the
x

y
-axes. The

of
f
are given by:

\displaystyle\nabla f(x,y)

\displaystyle=\ell^{\prime}(xy)\,\begin{bmatrix}y\\
x\end{bmatrix}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}f(x,y)

\displaystyle=\ell^{\prime\prime}(xy)\,\begin{bmatrix}y\\
x\end{bmatrix}^{\otimes 2}+\ell^{\prime}(xy)\,\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\,.

(11)

This results in GD
iterates

x_{t},y_{t}
, for step size
\eta>0
and iteration

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,y_{t}\,,

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=y_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,x_{t}\,.

(12)

Lemma 4

(invariant lines)
.

Assume that
\ell
is even, so that
\ell^{\prime}
is odd.
Then, the lines

are invariant for gradient descent on
f
.

Proof.

If
y_{t}=\pm x_{t}
, then

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=y_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,x_{t}=\pm x_{t}\mp\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}^{2})\,x_{t}\,,

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,y_{t}=x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}^{2})\,x_{t}\,,

and hence
y_{t+1}=\pm x_{t+1}
. Note that the iterates
{(x_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
are the iterates of GD with step size
\eta
on the one-dimensional loss function
x\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\,\ell(x^{2})
.
∎

We focus instead on initializing away from these two lines. We now state our assumptions on
\ell
.

We gather together some elementary properties of
\ell
.

Lemma 5

(properties of
\ell
)
.

Suppose that Assumption
(A1)
holds.

1.

\ell
is minimized at the origin and
\ell^{\prime}(0)=0
.

2.

Suppose that
\ell
is four times continuously differentiable near the origin. If Assumption
(A2)
holds, then
\ell^{(4)}(0)\leq 0
. Conversely, if
\ell^{(4)}(0)<0
, then Assumption
(A2)
holds for
\beta=2
.

Proof.

The first statement is straightforward. The second statement follows from Taylor expansion: for
s\neq 0
near the origin,

\displaystyle\frac{\ell^{\prime}(s)}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell^{\prime}(0)+\ell^{\prime\prime}(0)\,s+\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)\,\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r}{s}=1+\int_{0}^{s}\bigl(1-\frac{r}{s}\bigr)\,\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,.

(13)

Since
\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}
is odd, then Assumption
(A2)
and (

) imply that
\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}
is non-positive on
(0,\varepsilon)
for some

, which in turn implies

. Conversely, if

and some

.
∎

.

with different values of
\beta
.

•

Rescaled and symmetrized logistic loss.

\ell_{\rm rsym}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\,\ell_{\rm logi}(-2s)+\frac{1}{2}\,\ell_{\rm logi}(+2s)
.

Note
\ell_{\rm rsym}^{\prime}(s)=\tanh(s)
, thus
\ell_{\rm rsym}^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1
and
\ell_{\rm rsym}^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1-\tfrac{1}{4}\,|s|^{2}
, for
\lvert s\rvert<\tfrac{1}{4}
.

•

Square root loss.

\ell_{\rm sqrt}(s)\coloneqq\sqrt{1+s^{2}}
.

Note
\ell_{\rm sqrt}^{\prime}(s)=\frac{s}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}
, thus
\ell_{\rm sqrt}^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1
and
\ell_{\rm sqrt}^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1-\tfrac{2}{5}\,|s|^{2}
, for
\lvert s\rvert<\tfrac{2}{5}
.

•

Huber loss.

\ell_{\rm Hub}(s)\coloneqq\frac{s^{2}}{2}\one\{s\in[-1,1]\}+\bigl(\lvert s\rvert-\frac{1}{2}\bigr)\one\{s\notin[-1,1]\}
.

Note
\ell_{\rm Hub}^{\prime}(s)=s\one\{s\in[-1,1]\}+\sign(s)\one\{s\notin[-1,1]\}
, thus
\ell_{\rm Hub}^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1
, i.e., we have Assumption
(A2)
with
\beta=+\infty
.

•

Higher-order.

For
\beta>1
let
c_{\beta}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\beta+1}\,\bigl(\frac{\beta}{\beta+1}\bigr)^{\beta}

and
r_{\beta}\coloneqq\frac{\beta+1}{\beta}
.
We define
\ell_{\beta}
implicitly via its derivative

\ell_{\beta}^{\prime}(s)\coloneqq s\,\bigl(1-\,c_{\beta}\,|s|^{\beta}\bigr)\one\{s^{2}<r_{\beta}^{2}\}+\sign(s)\one\{s^{2}\geq r_{\beta}^{2}\}\,.

By definition,
\ell_{\beta}^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1
and
\ell_{\beta}^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1-c_{\ell}\,\lvert s\rvert^{\beta}
, where
c_{\ell}=c_{\beta}\wedge r_{\beta}
.

We now prove our main results from
Subsection 2.3
in order.

C.1
Approximate conservation along GD

We begin by stating and proving the approximate conservation of
y^{2}-x^{2}
for the GD dynamics.

Lemma 6

(approximately conserved quantity)
.

Let
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be such that
\tilde{y}>\tilde{x}>0
with
\tilde{y}^{2}-\tilde{x}^{2}=1
.
Suppose that we run GD on
f
with step size
\eta
with initial point
(x_{0},y_{0})\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{\eta}}\,(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, for some
\gamma>0
. Then, there exists
t_{0}=O(\frac{1}{\eta})
such that
\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}{\lvert x_{t}\rvert}\leq O(\sqrt{(\gamma^{-1}\vee\gamma)\,\eta})
and

y_{t_{0}}^{2}-x_{t_{0}}^{2}=\bigl(1-O(\eta)\bigr)\,(y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2})\,,

where the implied constant depends on
\tilde{x}
,
\tilde{y}
, and
\ell
.

Proof.

Let
D_{t}\coloneqq y_{t}^{2}-x_{t}^{2}
and note that

\displaystyle D_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\bigl(y_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,x_{t}\bigr)^{2}-\bigl(x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,y_{t}\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-\eta^{2}\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})^{2}\bigr)\,D_{t}\,.

(14)

Since
\ell
is
1
-Lipschitz, then
D_{t+1}=(1-O(\eta^{2}))\,D_{t}
.

This shows that for
t\lesssim 1/\eta^{2}
, we have
y_{t}^{2}-x_{t}^{2}=D_{t}\gtrsim D_{0}=y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}\asymp\gamma/\eta
.
Since
\ell^{\prime\prime}(0)=1
, there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
such that
\ell^{\prime}(\lvert xy\rvert)\geq\ell^{\prime}(c_{0})\geq c_{1}
whenever
\lvert xy\rvert\geq c_{0}
.
Hence, for all
t\geq 1
such that
t\lesssim 1/\eta^{2}
,
x_{t}>0
, and
\lvert x_{t}y_{t}\rvert\geq c_{0}
, we have
y_{t}^{2}\gtrsim\gamma/\eta
and

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,y_{t}=x_{t}-\Theta(\eta y_{t})=x_{t}-\Theta\bigl(\sqrt{\gamma\eta}\bigr)\,.

(15)

Since
x_{0}\asymp\sqrt{\gamma/\eta}
, this shows that after at most
O(1/\eta)
iterations, we must have either
x_{t}<0
or
\lvert x_{t}y_{t}\rvert\leq c_{0}
for the first time.
In the first case, (
15
) shows that
\lvert x_{t}\rvert\lesssim\sqrt{\gamma\eta}
. In the second case, since
y_{t}^{2}\gtrsim\gamma/\eta
, we have
\lvert x_{t}\rvert\lesssim\sqrt{\eta/\gamma}
.
Let
t_{0}
denote the iteration at which this occurs.

Next, for iterations
t\geq t_{0}
, we use the dynamics (
15
) for
x
and the fact that
\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})
has the same sign as
x_{t}
to conclude that there are two possibilities: either
x_{t+1}
has the same sign as
x_{t}
, in which case
\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert\leq\lvert x_{t}\rvert
, or
x_{t+1}
has the opposite sign as
x_{t}
, in which case
\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert\leq\eta\,\lvert\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\rvert\,y_{t}\leq\eta y_{t}\leq O(\sqrt{\gamma\eta})
.
This implies
\sup_{t\geq t_{0}}{\lvert x_{t}\rvert}\leq O(\sqrt{(\gamma^{-1}\vee\gamma)\,\eta})
as asserted.
∎

C.2
Gradient flow regime

In this section, we prove
Theorem 1
.
From
6
, there exists an iteration
t_{0}
such that
\lvert x_{t_{0}}\rvert\lesssim\sqrt{\eta/(2-\delta)}
and

\displaystyle\frac{2-\delta}{\eta}-O(2-\delta)

\displaystyle\leq y_{t_{0}}^{2}\leq\frac{2-\delta}{\eta}+x_{t_{0}}^{2}\leq\frac{2-\delta}{\eta}+O\bigl(\frac{\eta}{2-\delta}\bigr)\,.

In particular,
C\coloneqq\lvert x_{t_{0}}y_{t_{0}}\rvert\lesssim 1
.

We prove by induction the following facts: for
t\geq t_{0}
,

1.

\lvert x_{t}y_{t}\rvert\leq C
.

2.

\lvert x_{t}\rvert\leq\lvert x_{t_{0}}\rvert\exp(-\Omega(\alpha\,(t-t_{0})))
, where
\alpha\coloneqq\min\{\delta,2-\delta\}
.

Suppose that these conditions hold up to iteration
t\geq t_{0}
.
By Assumption
(A2)
, we have
\lvert\ell^{\prime}(s)\rvert\leq\lvert s\rvert
for all
s\neq 0
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=y_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,x_{t}\geq(1-\eta x_{t}^{2})\,y_{t}

(16)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\Bigl(-O\bigl(\frac{\eta^{2}}{2-\delta}\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\alpha\,(t-t_{0}))\bigr)\Bigr)\,y_{t}

(17)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\Bigl(-O\bigl(\frac{\eta^{2}}{2-\delta}\bigr)\sum_{s=t_{0}}^{t}\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\alpha\,(s-t_{0}))\bigr)\Bigr)\,y_{t_{0}}\geq\exp\Bigl(-O\bigl(\frac{\eta^{2}}{\alpha\,(2-\delta)}\bigr)\Bigr)\,y_{t_{0}}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle y_{t+1}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2-\delta}{\eta}-O(2-\delta)-O\bigl(\frac{\eta}{\alpha}\bigr)\,.

(19)

In particular,
\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{2-\delta}{\eta}\leq y_{t}^{2}\leq\frac{2-\delta/2}{\eta}
throughout.
In order for these assertions to hold, we require
\eta^{2}\lesssim\alpha\,(2-\delta)
, i.e.,
\eta\lesssim\min\{\sqrt{\delta},2-\delta\}
.

Next, we would like to show that
t\mapsto\lvert x_{t}\rvert
is decaying exponentially fast. Since

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,y_{t}\rvert=\bigl\lvert\,\lvert x_{t}\rvert-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(\lvert x_{t}\rvert\,y_{t})\,y_{t}\,\bigr\rvert\,,

it suffices to consider the case when
x_{t}>0
. Assumption
(A2)
implies that

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\eta y_{t}^{2})\,x_{t}\geq-\bigl(1-\frac{\delta}{2}\bigr)\,x_{t}\,.

For the upper bound, we split into two cases. We begin by observing that since
\ell
is twice continuously differentiable near the origin with
\ell^{\prime\prime}(0)=1
, there is a constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such that
\lvert s\rvert<\varepsilon_{0}
implies
\lvert\ell^{\prime}(s)\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2}\,\lvert s\rvert
.
If
s_{t}\coloneqq x_{t}y_{t}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, then

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(1-\frac{\eta}{2}\,y_{t}^{2}\bigr)\,x_{t}\leq\bigl(1-\frac{2-\delta}{4}\bigr)\,x_{t}\,.

Otherwise, if
s_{t}\geq\varepsilon_{0}
, then

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle\leq x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{0})\,y_{t}\leq x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{0})\,\frac{y_{t}^{2}}{s_{t}}\leq x_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{0})\,\frac{2-\delta}{2C\eta}\,x_{t}\leq\bigl(1-\Omega(2-\delta)\bigr)\,x_{t}\,.

Combining these inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert x_{t}\rvert\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\alpha)\bigr)\,.

This verifies the second statement in the induction.
The first statement follows because both
t\mapsto\lvert x_{t}\rvert
and
t\mapsto y_{t}
are decreasing.

This shows in particular that
\lvert x_{t}\rvert\searrow 0
, i.e., we have global convergence.
To conclude the proof, observe that (
19
) gives a bound on the final sharpness.

Remark 1
.

The proof also gives us estimates on the convergence rate.
Namely, from Lemma
6
, the initial phase in which we approach the
y
-axis takes
O(\frac{1}{\eta})
iterations.
For the convergence phase, in order to achieve
\varepsilon
error, we need
\lvert x_{t}\rvert\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon\eta}}{\sqrt{2-\delta}}
; hence, the convergence phase needs only
O(\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
iterations. Note that the rate of convergence in the latter phase does not depend on the step size
\eta
.

C.3
EoS regime: proof outline

We give a brief outline of the proof of
Theorem 2
:
As before,
6
shows that GD reaches the
y
-axis approximately at
(0,\sqrt{y_{0}^{2}-x_{0}^{2}})
.
At this point,
x
starts bouncing while
y
steadily decreases, and we argue that unless
x_{t}=0
or
y_{t}^{2}\leq 2/\eta
, the GD dynamics cannot stabilize (see
7
).

To bound the gap
2/\eta-y_{\infty}^{2}
, we look at the first iteration
{\swabfamily t}
such that
y_{{\swabfamily t}}^{2}
crosses
2/\eta
.
By making use of Assumption
(A2)
, we simultaneously control both the convergence rate of
\lvert x_{t}\rvert
to zero and the decrease in
y_{t}^{2}
in order to prove that

\displaystyle y_{\infty}^{2}\geq\frac{2}{\eta}-O(\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}y_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert)\,,

(20)

see
1
.
Therefore, to establish
Theorem 2
, we must bound
\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}y_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert
at iteration
{\swabfamily t}
.

Controlling the size of
\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}y_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert

The insight that guides the proof is the observation that during the bouncing phase, the GD iterates lie close to a certain envelope (
Figure 8
).
This envelope is predicted by the quasi-static heuristic as described in
Ma et al. 2022
.
Namely, suppose that after one iteration of GD, we have perfect bouncing:
x_{t+1}=-x_{t}
.
Substituting this into the GD dynamics, we obtain the equation

\displaystyle\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,y_{t}=2x_{t}\,.

(21)

According to Assumption
(A2)
, we have
\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})=x_{t}y_{t}\,(1-\Omega(\lvert x_{t}y_{t}\rvert^{\beta}))
,
Together with (
21
), if
y_{t}^{2}=(2+\delta_{t})/\eta\geq 2/\eta
, where
\delta_{t}
is sufficiently small, it suggests that

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t}y_{t}\rvert\lesssim\delta_{t}^{1/\beta}\,.

(22)

The quasi-static prediction (
22
) fails when
\delta_{t}
is too small. Nevertheless, we show that it remains accurate as long as
\delta_{t}\gtrsim\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}
, and consequently we obtain
\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}y_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert\lesssim\eta^{1/(\beta-1)}
.
Combined with (
20
), it yields
Theorem 2
.

C.4
EoS regime: crossing the threshold and the convergence phase

In this section, we prove
Theorem 2
. We first show that
y_{t}^{2}
must cross
\nicefrac{{2}}{{\eta}}
in order for GD to converge, and we bound the size of the jump across
\nicefrac{{2}}{{\eta}}
once this happens.

Throughout this section and the next, we use the following notation:

•

s_{t}\coloneqq x_{t}y_{t}
;

•

r_{t}\coloneqq\ell^{\prime}(s_{t})/s_{t}
.

In this notation, we can write the GD equations as

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\eta r_{t}y_{t}^{2})\,x_{t}\,,

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\eta r_{t}x_{t}^{2})\,y_{t}\,.

We also make a remark regarding
Assumption
(A2)
. If
\beta<+\infty
, then Assumption
(A2)
is equivalent to the following seemingly strongly assumption: for all
r>0
, there exists a constant
c(r)>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\ell^{\prime}(s)}{s}

\displaystyle\leq 1-c(r)\,\lvert s\rvert^{\beta}\,,\qquad\text{for all}~0<\lvert s\rvert\leq r\,.

(A2
+
)

Indeed, Assumption
(A2)
states that (
A2+
) holds for
some

r>0
.
To verify that (
A2+
) holds for some larger

, we can split into two cases. If
\lvert s\rvert\leq r
, then
\ell^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1-c\,\lvert s\rvert^{\beta}
. Otherwise, if
\lvert s\rvert>r
, then
\ell^{\prime}(r)/r<1
and the
1
-Lipschitzness of
\ell^{\prime}
imply that
\ell^{\prime}(s)/s<1
for
r\leq\lvert s\rvert\leq r^{\prime}
, and hence
\ell^{\prime}(s)/s\leq 1-c^{\prime}\,|s|^{\beta}
, for a sufficiently small constant

; thus we can take
c(r^{\prime})=c\wedge c^{\prime}
.
Later, we will invoke (
A2+
) with
r
chosen to be a universal constant, so that

can also be thought of as universal.

We begin with the following result about the limiting value of

.

Lemma 7

(threshold crossing)
.

Let
(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfy
\tilde{y}>\tilde{x}>0
with
\tilde{y}^{2}-\tilde{x}^{2}=1
.
Suppose we initialize GD with step size
\eta
with initial point
(x_{0},y_{0})\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{2+\delta}{\eta}}\,(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, where
\delta>0
is a constant. Then either
x_{t}=0
for some
t
or

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}y_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\eta}\,.

Proof.

Assume throughout that
x_{t}\neq 0
for all
t
.
Recall the dynamics for
y
:

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=y_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{t}y_{t})\,x_{t}\,.

By assumption
\ell^{\prime}(s)/s\to 1
as
s\to 0
, and
\ell^{\prime}
is increasing, so this equation implies that if
\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\lvert x_{t}\rvert}>0

must eventually cross
2/\eta
.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that there exists
\varepsilon>0
with
y_{t}^{2}>(2+\varepsilon)/\eta

. Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
be such that

\varepsilon^{\prime}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}
.
Then, there exists
\delta>0
such
\lvert x_{t}\rvert\leq\delta

, hence

\displaystyle\frac{|x_{t+1}|}{|x_{t}|}=\lvert 1-\eta r_{t}y_{t}^{2}\rvert>|(2+\varepsilon)\,(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})-1|>1\,.

The above means that
\lvert x_{t}\rvert
increases until it exceeds
\delta

\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\lvert x_{t}\rvert}\geq\delta
. This is our desired contradiction and it implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}y_{t}^{2}\leq 2/\eta
.
∎

Lemma 8

(initial gap)
.

Suppose that at some iteration

, we have

\displaystyle y_{{\swabfamily t}+1}^{2}

\displaystyle<\frac{2}{\eta}\leq y_{{\swabfamily t}}^{2}\,.

\displaystyle y_{{\swabfamily t}+1}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{\eta}-2\eta s_{\swabfamily t}^{2}\,.

We can bound

\displaystyle y_{{\swabfamily t}+1}^{2}

\displaystyle=y_{\swabfamily t}^{2}-2\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(x_{\swabfamily t}y_{\swabfamily t})\,x_{\swabfamily t}y_{\swabfamily t}+\eta^{2}\,{\ell^{\prime}(x_{\swabfamily t}y_{\swabfamily t})}^{2}\,x_{\swabfamily t}^{2}\geq y_{\swabfamily t}^{2}-2\eta\,\lvert x_{\swabfamily t}y_{\swabfamily t}\rvert^{2}\,,

where we used the fact that
\lvert\ell^{\prime}(s)\rvert\leq\lvert s\rvert

,
∎

.

We now provide an analysis of the convergence phase, i.e., after

Proposition 1

(convergence phase)
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\eta}-O(\lvert s_{\swabfamily t}\rvert)\leq y_{\infty}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{\eta}\,.

Write

First, by the same argument as in the proof of

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert x_{t}\rvert\exp\bigl(-\Omega(\rho_{t})\bigr)\,.

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle\rho_{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{t}+4\eta\sum_{s=t}^{t^{\prime}-1}x_{s}^{2}\leq q+O(\eta\,\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert)+O(\eta\,\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert^{2})\sum_{s=1}^{t^{\prime}-1}\exp\bigl(-\Omega(q\,(s-t))\bigr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\eta}\,\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert\lesssim\eta\,\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert\,.

Let us take stock of what we have established thus far.

•

According to Lemma

,
\lvert s_{t}\rvert
is bounded for all

by a constant.

•

Then, from
7
and
8
, we must have either

, or

.

•

In the latter case,
1

.

Note also that the analyses thus far have not made use of Assumption

, i.e., we have established the

.
Moreover, for all

, the asymptotic

The reader who is satisfied with this result can then skip ahead to subsequent sections.
The remainder of this section and the next section are devoted to substantial refinements of the analysis.

To see where improvements are possible, note that both

1
rely on the size of

at the crossing.
Our crude bound of

Another weakness of our proof is that it provides nearly no information about the dynamics during the bouncing phase, which constitutes an incomplete understanding of the EoS phenomenon.
In particular, we experimentally observe that during the bouncing phase, the iterates lie very close to the quasi-static envelope (

).
In the next section, we will rigorously prove all of these observations.

Before doing so, however, we show that
1

, which could be of interest in its own right.
It shows that even if the convergence phase begins with a large value of

, the limiting sharpness can be much closer to

than what

The following proposition combined with Lemma

From now on, we assume
\beta<+\infty
.

Proposition 2

(convergence phase; refined)
.

Suppose that
y_{{\swabfamily t}}^{2}<2/\eta\leq y_{{\swabfamily t}-1}^{2}
.
Then, GD converges to
(0,y_{\infty})
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\eta}\geq y_{\infty}^{2}\geq\frac{2}{\eta}-O(\eta\,\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert^{2})-\begin{cases}O(\eta^{1/(\beta-1)})\,,&\beta>2\,,\\
O(\eta\log(\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert/\eta))\,,&\beta=2\,,\\
O(\eta\,\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert^{2-\beta})\,,&\beta<2\,.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
y_{t}^{2}=(2-\rho_{t})/\eta
as before.
We quantify the decrease of
|x_{t}|
in terms of
\rho_{t}
and conversely the increase of
\rho_{t}
in terms of
|x_{t}|
by tracking the half-life of
|x_{t}|
, i.e., the number of iterations it takes
|x_{t}|
to halve. We call these epochs: at the
i
-th epoch, we have

2^{-(i+1)}\sqrt{\eta}<|x_{t}|\leq 2^{-i}\sqrt{\eta}\,.

Let
i_{0}
be the index of the first epoch, i.e.,
i_{0}=\lfloor\log_{2}(\sqrt{\eta}/\lvert x_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert)\rfloor
.
Due to Lemma
6
, we know that
i_{0}\geq-O(1)
.
From (
23
),
|x_{t}|
is monotonically decreasing and consequently
|s_{t}|
is decreasing as well. Also, our bound on the limiting sharpness implies that
y_{t}^{2}>1/\eta
for all
t
, provided that
\eta
is sufficiently small.

Let us now compute the dynamics of
\rho_{t}
and
|x_{t}|
. At epoch
i
,
|x_{t}|>2^{-(i+1)}\sqrt{\eta}
hence
|s_{t}|>2^{-(i+1)}
. Assumption (
A2+
) with
r=\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert\lesssim 1
implies that

\frac{\ell^{\prime}(s_{t})}{s_{t}}\leq 1-c\,2^{-\beta\,(i+1)}\,,

(25)

where
c=c(\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert)
. This allows to refine (
23
) on the decrease of
|x_{t}|
to

\displaystyle\frac{|x_{t+1}|}{|x_{t}|}

\displaystyle=\eta r_{t}y_{t}^{2}-1\leq(2-\rho_{t})\,(1-c\,2^{-\beta\,(i+1)})-1\leq 1-\rho_{t}-c\,2^{-\beta\,(i+1)}\,,

(26)

where the first inequality follows from (
25
) and the second from
\rho_{t}=2-\eta y_{t}^{2}<1
.
In turn, this inequality shows that the
i
-th phase only requires
O(2^{\beta i})
iterations.

Hence, if
t(i)
denotes the start of the
i
-th epoch, then (
24
) shows that

\displaystyle\rho_{t(i+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{t(i)}+4\eta^{2}\cdot 2^{-2i}\cdot O(2^{\beta i})\leq\rho_{t(i)}+O(\eta^{2}\,2^{(\beta-2)\,i})\,.

Summing this up, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{t(i)}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{{\swabfamily t}}+\eta^{2}\times\begin{cases}O(2^{(\beta-2)\,i})\,,&\beta>2\,,\\
O(i-i_{0})\,,&\beta=2\,,\\
O(2^{(\beta-2)\,i_{0}})=O(\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert^{2-\beta})\,,&\beta<2\,.\end{cases}

In the case of
\beta<2
, the final sharpness satisfies
2/\eta-O(\rho_{{\swabfamily t}}/\eta)-O(\eta\,\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert^{2-\beta})\leq y_{\infty}^{2}\leq 2/\eta
.

In the other two cases, suppose that we use this argument until epoch
i_{\star}
such that
2^{-i_{\star}}\asymp\eta^{\gamma}
.
Then, we have
\lvert x_{t(i_{\star})}\rvert\asymp\eta^{\gamma+1/2}
,
\lvert s_{t(i_{\star})}\rvert\asymp\eta^{\gamma}
, and by using the argument from
1
from iteration
t(i_{\star})
onward we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\rho_{t(i_{\star})}+\rho_{\infty}-\rho_{t(i_{\star})}\leq\rho_{{\swabfamily t}}+O(\eta^{\gamma+1})+\eta^{2}\times\begin{cases}O(2^{(\beta-2)\,i_{\star}})=O(\eta^{-\gamma\,(\beta-2)})\,,&\beta>2\,,\\
O(i_{\star}-i_{0})\,,&\beta=2\,.\end{cases}

We optimize over the choice of
\gamma
, obtaining
\gamma=1/(\beta-1)
and thus

\displaystyle\rho_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{{\swabfamily t}}+\begin{cases}O(\eta^{1+1/(\beta-1)})\,,&\beta>2\,,\\
O(\eta^{2}\log(\lvert s_{{\swabfamily t}}\rvert/\eta))\,,&\beta=2\,.\end{cases}

By collecting together the three cases and using
8
to bound
\rho_{{\swabfamily t}}
, we finish the proof.
∎

Using the crude bound
\lvert s_{t_{0}}\rvert\lesssim 1
from Lemma
6
, it yields

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\eta}

\displaystyle\geq y_{\infty}^{2}\geq\frac{2}{\eta}-O(\eta)-\begin{cases}O(\eta^{1/(\beta-1)})\,,&\beta>2\,,\\
O(\eta\log(1/\eta))\,,&\beta=2\,,\\
O(\eta)\,,&\beta<2\,,\end{cases}

which is optimal for
\beta>2
.

C.5
EoS regime: quasi-static analysis

We supplement Assumption
(A2)
with a corresponding lower bound on
\ell^{\prime}(s)/s
:

\displaystyle\text{There exists}~C>0~\text{such that}\qquad\frac{\ell^{\prime}(s)}{s}\geq 1-C\,\lvert s\rvert^{\beta}\qquad\text{for all}~s\neq 0\,.

(
A3
)

Under these assumptions, we prove the following result which is also of interest as it provides detailed information for the bouncing phase of the EoS.

Theorem 5

(quasi-static principle)
.

Suppose we run GD on
f
with step size
\eta>0
, where
f(x,y)\coloneqq\ell(xy)
and
\ell
satisfies Assumptions
(A1)
,
(A2)
, and (
A3
).
Write
y_{t}^{2}\coloneqq(2+\delta_{t})/\eta
and suppose that at some iteration
t_{0}
, we have
\lvert x_{t_{0}}y_{t_{0}}\rvert\asymp\delta_{t_{0}}^{1/\beta}
and
\delta_{t_{0}}\lesssim 1
.
Then, for all
t\geq t_{0}
with
\delta_{t}\gtrsim\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t}y_{t}\rvert

\displaystyle\asymp\delta_{t}^{1/\beta}\,,

where all implied constants depend on
\ell
but not on
\eta
.

In this section, we show that the GD iterates lie close to the quasi-static trajectory and give the full proof of
Theorem 2
. Recall from (
21
) that the quasi-static analysis predicts

\displaystyle\eta r_{t}y_{t}^{2}\approx 2\,,

(27)

and that during the bouncing phase, this closely agrees with experimental observations (
Figure 8
).
We consider the phase where
y_{t}^{2}
has not yet crossed the threshold
2/\eta
and we write
y_{t}^{2}\coloneqq(2+\delta_{t})/\eta
, thinking of
\delta_{t}
as small.
Then, (
27
) can be written
(2+\delta_{t})\,r_{t}\approx 2
.
If we have the behavior
\ell^{\prime}(s)/s=1-\Theta(\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta})
near the origin, then
r_{t}\approx 1-\Theta(\delta_{t})
implies that

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}\approx\delta_{t}\,.

(28)

Our goal is to rigorously establish (
28
). However, we first make two observations.
First, in order to establish
Theorem 2
, we only need to prove an upper bound on
\lvert s_{t}\rvert
, which only requires Assumption
(A2)
(to prove a lower bound on
\lvert s_{t}\rvert
, we need a corresponding lower bound on
\ell^{\prime}(s)/s
).
Second, even if we relax (
28
) to read
\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}\lesssim\delta_{t}
, this fails to hold when
\delta_{t}
is too small, because the error terms (the deviation of the dynamics from the quasi-static trajectory) begin to dominate.
With this in mind, we shall instead prove
\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}\lesssim\delta_{t}+C^{\prime}\,\eta^{\gamma}
, where the added
\eta^{\gamma}
handles the error terms and the exponent
\gamma>0
emerges from the proof.

Proposition 3

(quasi-static analysis; upper bound)
.

For all
t
such that
0\leq\delta_{t-1}\lesssim 1/(\beta\vee 1)
(for a sufficiently small implied constant), it holds that

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\,(\delta_{t}+C^{\prime}\,\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)})\,,

where
C,C^{\prime}>0
are constants which may depend on the problem parameters but not on
\eta
.

We first show that
Theorem 2
now follows.

Proof of
Theorem 2
.

As previously noted, the
\beta=+\infty
case is handled by the arguments of the previous section, so we focus on
\beta<+\infty
.
From
7
, we either have
y_{t}^{2}\to 2/\eta
and
\lvert x_{t}\rvert\to 0
, in which case we are done, or there is an iteration
{\swabfamily t}
such that
y_{\swabfamily t}^{2}<2/\eta\leq y_{{\swabfamily t}-1}^{2}
.
From
3
, since
\delta_{t-1}\geq 0
and
\delta_{t}\leq 0
, it follows that
\lvert s_{\swabfamily t}\rvert^{\beta}\lesssim\eta^{1/(\beta-1)}
.
The theorem now follows, either from
1
or from the refined
2
.
∎

We now prove
3
.
In the proof, we use asymptotic notation
O(\cdot)
,
\lesssim
, etc. in order to hide constants that depend on
\ell
(including
\beta
), but not on
\delta_{t}
and
\eta
.
However, the proof also involves choosing parameters
C,C^{\prime}>0
, and we keep the dependence on these parameters explicit for clarity.

Proof of
3
.

The proof goes by induction; namely, if
\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}\leq C\,(\delta_{t}+C^{\prime}\eta^{\gamma})
and
\delta_{t}\geq 0
at some iteration
t
, we prove that the same holds one iteration later, where the constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
as well as the exponent
\gamma>0
are chosen later in the proof.

For the base case, observe that the approximate conservation lemma (
6
) gives
\lvert s_{t}\rvert\lesssim 1
, and
\delta_{t}\gtrsim 1/(\beta\vee 1)
at the beginning of the induction, so the bound is satisfied initially if we choose
C
sufficiently large enough.

Throughout, we also write
\hat{\delta}_{t}\coloneqq\delta_{t}+C^{\prime}\eta^{\gamma}
as a convenient shorthand.
The strategy is to prove the following two statements:

1.

If
\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}=C_{t}\hat{\delta}_{t}
for some
C_{t}>\frac{C}{2}
, then
\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert^{\beta}\leq C_{t+1}\hat{\delta}_{t+1}
for some
C_{t+1}\leq C_{t}
.

2.

If
\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}=C_{t}\hat{\delta}_{t}
for some
C_{t}\leq\frac{C}{2}
, then
\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert^{\beta}\leq C\hat{\delta}_{t+1}
.

Proof of 1.

The dynamics for
x
give

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert 1-\eta y_{t}^{2}r_{t}\rvert\,\lvert x_{t}\rvert\,.

By Assumption (
A2+
) and
\lvert s_{t}\rvert\lesssim 1
,

\displaystyle r_{t}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\Omega(\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta})=1-\Omega(C\hat{\delta}_{t})

and hence

\displaystyle\eta y_{t}^{2}r_{t}

\displaystyle=(2+\delta_{t})\,\bigl(1-\Omega(C\hat{\delta}_{t})\bigr)=2-\Omega(C\hat{\delta}_{t})

for large
C
.
Also,
\ell^{\prime\prime}(0)=1
and a similar argument as in the proof of
Theorem 1
yields the reverse inequality
\eta y_{t}^{2}r_{t}\gtrsim 1
.
We conclude that

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle=\bigl(1-\Omega(C\hat{\delta}_{t})\bigr)\,\lvert x_{t}\rvert

and hence

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(1-\Omega(C\hat{\delta}_{t})\bigr)\,\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}=C_{t}\,\bigl(1-\Omega(C\hat{\delta}_{t})\bigr)\,\hat{\delta}_{t}\,.

Since we need a bound in terms of
\hat{\delta}_{t+1}
, we use the dynamics of
y
,

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\eta x_{t}^{2}r_{t})\,y_{t}\geq(1-\eta x_{t}^{2})\,y_{t}\,,

\displaystyle y_{t+1}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-2\eta x_{t}^{2})\,y_{t}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\delta_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\eta y_{t+1}^{2}-2\geq\delta_{t}-2\eta^{2}s_{t}^{2}\geq\delta_{t}-2\eta^{2}\,{(C\hat{\delta}_{t})}^{2/\beta}\,.

(29)

Substituting this in,

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C_{t}\,\bigl(1-\Omega(C\hat{\delta}_{t})\bigr)\,\bigl(\hat{\delta}_{t+1}+2\eta^{2}\,{(C\hat{\delta}_{t})}^{2/\beta}\bigr)

(30)

\displaystyle=C_{t}\hat{\delta}_{t+1}-\Omega(C^{2}\hat{\delta}_{t}\hat{\delta}_{t+1})+2C\eta^{2}\,{(C\hat{\delta}_{t})}^{2/\beta}\,.

(31)

Let us show that

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}_{t+1}\geq\frac{3}{4}\,\hat{\delta}_{t}\,.

(32)

From (
29
), we have
\hat{\delta}_{t+1}\geq\hat{\delta}_{t}-2\eta^{2}\,{(C\hat{\delta}_{t})}^{2/\beta}
, so we want to prove that
\eta^{2}\,{(C\hat{\delta}_{t})}^{2/\beta}\leq\hat{\delta}_{t}/8
.
If
\beta\leq 2
this is obvious by taking
\eta
small, and if
\beta>2
then this is equivalent to
C^{2/\beta}\eta^{2}\lesssim\hat{\delta}_{t}^{1-2/\beta}
.
It suffices to have
C^{2/\beta}\eta^{2}\lesssim{(C^{\prime})}^{1-2/\beta}\,\eta^{\gamma\,(1-2/\beta)}
, which is achieved by taking
C^{\prime}
large relative to
C
and by taking
\gamma\leq 2/(1-2/\beta)
; this constraint on
\gamma
will be satisfied by our eventual choice of
\gamma=\beta/(\beta-1)
.

Returning to (
31
), in order to finish the proof and in light of (
32
), we want to show that
C^{2}\hat{\delta}_{t}^{2}\gtrsim C^{1+2/\beta}\eta^{2}\hat{\delta}^{2/\beta}_{t}
.
Rearranging, it suffices to have
\hat{\delta}^{2-2/\beta}_{t}\gtrsim C^{2/\beta-1}\eta^{2}
, or
\hat{\delta}_{t}^{1-1/\beta}\gtrsim C^{1/\beta-1/2}\eta
.
Since by definition
\hat{\delta}_{t}\geq C^{\prime}\eta^{\gamma}
, by choosing
C^{\prime}
large it suffices to have
\gamma\leq 1/(1-1/\beta)=\beta/(\beta-1)
, which leads to our choice of
\gamma
.

Proof of 2.
Using the simple bound
\eta y_{t}^{2}r_{t}\leq 2+\delta_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta_{t})\,\lvert s_{t}\rvert\,,

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\beta\delta_{t})\,\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}=C_{t}\exp(\beta\delta_{t})\,\hat{\delta}_{t}\leq\frac{4}{3}\,C_{t}\exp(\beta\delta_{t})\,\hat{\delta}_{t+1}

where we used (
32
).
If
\exp(\beta\delta_{t})\leq 4/3
, which holds if
\delta_{t}\lesssim 1/\beta
, then from
C_{t}\leq C/2
we obtain
\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert^{\beta}\leq C\hat{\delta}_{t+1}
as desired.
∎

By following the same proof outline but reversing the inequalities, we can also show a corresponding lower bound on
\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta}
, as long as
\delta_{t}\gtrsim\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}
.
Although this is not needed to establish
Theorem 2
, it is of interest in its own right, as it shows (together with
3
) that the iterates of GD do in fact track the quasi-static trajectory.

Proposition 4

(quasi-static analysis; lower bound)
.

Suppose additionally that (
A3
) holds and that
\beta<+\infty
.
Also, suppose that at some iteration
t_{0}
, we have
\delta_{t_{0}}\lesssim 1
and that

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq c\,\delta_{t}^{1/\beta}

(33)

holds at iteration
t=t_{0}
, where
c
is a sufficiently small constant (depending on the problem parameters but not on
\eta
).
Then, (
33
) also holds for all iterations
t\geq t_{0}
such that
\delta_{t}\gtrsim\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}
.

Proof.

The proof mirrors that of
3
. Let
\delta_{t}\gtrsim\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}
for a sufficiently large implied constant.
We prove the following two statements:

1.

If
\lvert s_{t}\rvert=c_{t}\,\delta_{t}^{1/\beta}
for some
c_{t}<2c
, then
\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert\geq c_{t+1}\,\delta_{t+1}^{1/\beta}
for some
c_{t+1}\geq c_{t}
.

2.

If
\lvert s_{t}\rvert=c_{t}\,\delta_{t}^{1/\beta}
for some
c_{t}\geq 2c
, then
\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert\geq c\,\delta_{t+1}^{1/\beta}
.

Throughout the proof, due to
3
, we also have
\lvert s_{t}\rvert\lesssim\delta_{t}^{1/\beta}
.

Proof of 1.

The dynamics for
x
give

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert 1-\eta y_{t}^{2}r_{t}\rvert\,\lvert x_{t}\rvert\,.

By Assumption (
A3
),

\displaystyle r_{t}

\displaystyle\geq 1-O(\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta})\geq 1-O(c\,\delta_{t})\,.

If
c
is sufficiently small, then

\displaystyle\eta y_{t}^{2}r_{t}

\displaystyle\geq(2+\delta_{t})\,\bigl(1-O(c\,\delta_{t})\bigr)\geq 2+\Omega(\delta_{t})\,.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\lvert x_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\bigl(1+\Omega(\delta_{t})\bigr)\,\lvert x_{t}\rvert\,.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\eta x_{t}^{2})\,y_{t}\geq\bigl(1-O(\eta^{2}s_{t}^{2})\bigr)\,y_{t}\geq\bigl(1-O(\eta^{2}\delta_{t}^{2/\beta})\bigr)\,y_{t}

(34)

and hence

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert\geq\bigl(1+\Omega(\delta_{t})\bigr)\,\bigl(1-O(\eta^{2}\delta_{t}^{2/\beta})\bigr)\,\lvert s_{t}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq c_{t}\,\bigl(1+\Omega(\delta_{t})-O(\eta^{2}\delta_{t}^{2/\beta})\bigr)\,\delta_{t}^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle\geq c_{t}\,\bigl(1+\Omega(\delta_{t})-O(\eta^{2}\delta_{t}^{2/\beta})\bigr)\,\delta_{t+1}^{1/\beta}\,.

To conclude, we must prove that
\eta^{2}\delta_{t}^{2/\beta}\lesssim\delta_{t}
, but since
\delta_{t}\gtrsim\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}
(with sufficiently large implied constant), then this holds, as was checked in the proof of
3
.

Proof of 2.

Using Assumption (
A3
),

\displaystyle 1-O(\delta_{t})

\displaystyle\leq 1-O(\lvert s_{t}\rvert^{\beta})\leq r_{t}\leq 1\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle 2-O(\delta_{t})\leq(2+\delta_{t})\,\bigl(1-O(\delta_{t})\bigr)\leq\eta y_{t}^{2}r_{t}\leq 2+\delta_{t}

and

\displaystyle-1+O(\delta_{t})\geq 1-\eta y_{t}^{2}r_{t}\geq-1-\delta_{t}\,.

Together with the dynamics for
x
and (
34
),

\displaystyle\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\bigl(1-O(\delta_{t})\bigr)\,\bigl(1-O(\eta^{2}\delta_{t}^{2/\beta})\bigr)\,\lvert s_{t}\rvert\geq c_{t}\,\bigl(1-O(\delta_{t})\bigr)\,\bigl(1-O(\eta^{2}\delta_{t}^{2/\beta})\bigr)\,\delta_{t+1}^{1/\beta}\,.

Since
c_{t}\geq 2c
, if
\delta_{t}
and
\eta
are sufficiently small it implies
\lvert s_{t+1}\rvert\geq c\,\delta_{t+1}^{1/\beta}
.
∎

Convergence rate estimates.
Our analysis also provides estimates for the convergence rate of GD in both regimes. Namely, in the gradient flow regime, we show that GD converges in
O(1/\eta)
iterations, whereas in the EoS regime, GD typically spends
\Omega(1/\eta^{(\beta/(\beta-1))\vee 2})
iterations (
\Omega(\log(1/\eta)/\eta^{2})
iterations when
\beta=2
) in the bouncing phase (
Figure 10
). Hence, the existence of the bouncing phase dramatically slows down the convergence of GD.

Remark 2
.

Suppose that at iteration
t_{0}
, we have
\delta_{t_{0}}\asymp 1
.
Then, the assumption of
4
is that
\lvert s_{t_{0}}\rvert\gtrsim 1
.
If this is not satisfied, i.e.,
\lvert s_{t_{0}}\rvert\ll 1
, then the first claim in the proof of
4
shows that
\lvert s_{t_{0}+1}\rvert\geq(1+\Omega(\delta_{t}))\,\lvert s_{t_{0}}\rvert=(1+\Omega(1))\,\lvert s_{t_{0}}\rvert
.
Therefore, after
t^{\prime}=O(\log(1/\lvert s_{t_{0}}\rvert))
iterations, we obtain
\lvert s_{t_{0}+t^{\prime}}\rvert\gtrsim 1
and then
4
applies thereafter.

Remark 3
.

From the quasi-static analysis, we can also derive bounds on the length of the bouncing phase.
Namely, suppose that
t_{0}
is such that
\delta_{t_{0}}\asymp 1
and for all
t\geq t_{0}
, we have
\lvert s_{t}\rvert=\delta_{t}^{1/\beta}
.
If
\delta_{t_{0}}
is sufficiently small so that
r_{t}\gtrsim 1
for all
t\geq t_{0}
, then the equation for
y
yields

\displaystyle\delta_{t+1}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{t}-\Theta(\eta^{2}s_{t}^{2})=\delta_{t}-\Theta(\eta^{2}\delta_{t}^{2/\beta})\,.

We declare the
k
-th phase to consist of iterations
t
such that
2^{-k}\leq\delta_{t}\leq 2^{-(k-1)}
.
During this phase,
\delta_{t+1}\leq\delta_{t}-\Theta(\eta^{2}\,2^{-2k/\beta})
, so the number of iterations in phase
k
is
\asymp 2^{k\,(2/\beta-1)}/\eta^{2}
.
We sum over the phases until
\delta_{t}\asymp\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}
, since after this point the quasi-static analysis fails and
y_{t}^{2}
crosses over
2/\eta
shortly afterwards.
This yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}\lesssim 2^{-k}\lesssim 1\end{subarray}}2^{k\,(2/\beta-1)}\asymp\begin{cases}1/\eta^{2}\,,&\beta>2\,,\\
\log(1/\eta)/\eta^{2}\,,&\beta=2\,,\\
1/\eta^{\beta/(\beta-1)}\,,&\beta<2\,.\end{cases}

The time spent in the bouncing phase increases dramatically as
\beta\searrow 1
.

Appendix D
Deferred derivations of mean model

Figure 13:
Under the same setting as
Figure 1
, we compare the mean model with the GD dynamics of the ReLU network. The mean model is plotted with black dashed line. Note that
the mean model tracks the GD dynamics quite well during the initial phase of training.

In this section, we provide the details for the derivations of the mean model in
Subsection 3.1
.
Recall

f(\bm{x};a^{-},a^{+},b)=a^{-}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ReLU\bigl(-\bm{x}[i]+b\bigr)+a^{+}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ReLU\bigl(+\bm{x}[i]+b\bigr)\,,

where
\bm{x}=\lambda y\bm{e}_{j}+\bm{\xi}
.
We first approximate

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ReLU\bigl(\pm\bm{x}[i]+b\bigr)\approx\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ReLU\bigl(\pm\bm{\xi}[i]+b\bigr)\,.

In other words, we can ignore the contribution of the signal
\lambda y\bm{e}_{j}
.
This approximation holds because
(i)
initially, the bias
b
is not yet negative enough to threshold out the noise, and hence the summation
\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ReLU(\pm\bm{\xi}[i]+b)
is of size
O(d)
, and
(ii)
the difference between the left- and right-hand sides above is simply
\ReLU(\pm\lambda y\pm\bm{\xi}[j]+b)-\ReLU(\pm\bm{\xi}[j]+b)
, which is of size
O(1)
and hence negligible compared to the full summation.

Next, letting
g(b)\coloneqq\E_{z\sim\NN(0,1)}\ReLU(z+b)
be the ‘smoothed’ ReLU (see
Figure 7
), concentration of measure implies the following two facts:

•

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ReLU\bigl(\pm\bm{\xi}[i]+b\bigr)\,\approx\,d\E_{\xi\sim\NN(0,1)}\ReLU(\xi+b)\,\eqqcolon\,d\,g(b)
and

•

\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbbm{1}}{\left\{\pm\bm{x}[i]+b\geq 0\right\}}\approx d\E_{\xi\sim\NN(0,1)}{\mathbbm{1}}{\left\{\xi+b\geq 0\right\}}=d\,g^{\prime}(b)
.

Indeed, the summations above are sums of
d
i.i.d. non-negative random variables, and hence its mean is
\Omega(d)
(as long as
b\geq-O(1)
) and its standard deviation is
O(\sqrt{d})
.
Now, using these approximations, one can rewrite the GD dynamics on the population loss
\E[\ell_{\rm logi}(yf(\bm{x};a^{-},a^{+},b))]
.

Using these approximations, the output of the ReLU network (
2
) can be written as

f(\bm{x};a^{-},a^{+},b)\approx d\,(a^{-}+a^{+})\,g(b)\,,

which in turn leads to an approximation of the GD dynamics on the population loss
(a^{-},a^{+},b)\mapsto\E[\ell_{\rm logi}(yf(\bm{x};a^{-},a^{+},b))]
:

\displaystyle a^{\pm}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=a^{\pm}_{t}-\eta\E\Bigl[\ell_{\rm logi}^{\prime}\bigl(y\underbrace{f(\bm{x};a_{t}^{-},a_{t}^{+},b_{t})}_{\approx\,d\,(a_{t}^{-}+a_{t}^{+})\,g(b_{t})}\bigr)\times\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ReLU\bigl(\pm\bm{x}[i]+b_{t}\bigr)}_{\approx\,d\,g(b_{t})}\Bigr]

(35)

\displaystyle\approx a^{\pm}_{t}-\eta\,\ell_{\rm sym}^{\prime}\bigl(d\,(a^{-}_{t}+a^{+}_{t})\,g(b_{t})\bigr)\,d\,g(b_{t})\,,

(36)

\displaystyle b_{t+1}

\displaystyle=b_{t}-\eta\E\Bigl[\ell_{\rm logi}^{\prime}(y\underbrace{f(\bm{x};a_{t}^{-},a_{t}^{+},b_{t})}_{\approx\,d\,(a_{t}^{-}+a_{t}^{+})\,g(b_{t})}\bigr)\times\Bigl({a^{-}_{t}\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbbm{1}}{\left\{-\bm{x}[i]+b_{t}\geq 0\right\}}}_{\approx\,d\,g^{\prime}(b_{t})}}+{a^{+}_{t}\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{d}{\mathbbm{1}}{\left\{+\bm{x}[i]+b_{t}\geq 0\right\}}}_{\approx\,d\,g^{\prime}(b_{t})}}\Bigr)\Bigr]

(37)

\displaystyle\approx b_{t}-\eta\,\ell_{\rm sym}^{\prime}\bigl(d\,(a^{-}_{t}+a^{+}_{t})\,g(b_{t})\bigr)\,d\,(a^{-}_{t}+a^{+}_{t})\,g^{\prime}(b_{t})\,,

(38)

where
\ell_{\rm sym}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\log(1+\exp(-s))+\log(1+\exp(+s)))
is the symmetrized logistic loss.
Hence we arrive at the following dynamics on
a^{\pm}
and
b
that we call the mean model:

\displaystyle\begin{split}a^{\pm}_{t+1}&=a^{\pm}_{t}-\eta\,\ell_{\rm sym}^{\prime}\bigl(d\,(a^{-}_{t}+a^{+}_{t})\,g(b_{t})\bigr)\,d\,g(b_{t})\,,\\
b_{t+1}&=b_{t}-\eta\,\ell_{\rm sym}^{\prime}\bigl(d\,(a^{-}_{t}+a^{+}_{t})\,g(b_{t})\big)\,d\,(a^{+}_{t}+a^{-}_{t})\,g^{\prime}(b_{t})\,.\end{split}

(39)

Now, we can write the above dynamics more compactly in terms of the parameter
A_{t}\coloneqq d\,(a^{-}_{t}+a^{+}_{t})
.

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{t+1}&=A_{t}-2d^{2}\eta\,\ell_{\rm sym}^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,g(b_{t})\,,\\
b_{t+1}&=b_{t}-\eta\,\ell_{\rm sym}^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,A_{t}g^{\prime}(b_{t})\,.\end{split}

(40)

Proofs for the mean model

In this section, we prove the main theorems for the mean model.
We first recall the mean model for the reader’s convenience.

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{t+1}&=A_{t}-2d^{2}\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,g(b_{t})\,,\\
b_{t+1}&=b_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,A_{t}g^{\prime}(b_{t})\,.\end{split}

(41)

Deferred proofs

In this section, we collect together deferred proofs from
Subsection 3.2
.

Proof of
2
.

By definition,
g(b)=\int_{-b}^{\infty}(\xi+b)\,\varphi(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi=\int_{-b}^{\infty}\xi\,\varphi(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi+b\,\varPhi(b)
.
Recalling
\varphi^{\prime}(\xi)=-\xi\,\varphi(\xi)
, the first term equals
\varphi(b)
. Moreover,
g^{\prime}(b)=-b\,\varphi(b)+\varPhi(b)+b\,\varphi(b)=\varPhi(b)
.
∎

.

\partial_{t}(\frac{1}{2}\,A^{2})=A\dot{A}=-2d^{2}\,\ell^{\prime}(Ag(b))\,Ag(b)

E.2
Gradient flow regime

Proof of
Theorem 3
.

The following proof is analogous to the proof of
Theorem 1
.
We first list several facts we use in the proof:

(i)

|g^{\prime}(b)|=|\varPhi(b)|\leq 1
for all
b\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

\ell^{\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\exp(s)-1}{\exp(s)+1}
. Hence,

|\ell^{\prime}(s)|\leq\frac{1}{2}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and we have

\displaystyle\frac{\ell^{\prime}(s)}{s}\geq\frac{1}{8}\times\begin{cases}1\,,&\text{if}~\lvert s\rvert\leq 2\,,\\
2/\lvert s\rvert\,,&\text{if}~\lvert s\rvert>2\,.\end{cases}

(iii)

\ell^{\prime\prime}(0)=1/4
.

(iv)

\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)=-\frac{\exp(s)\,(\exp(s)-1)}{{(\exp(s)+1)}^{3}}
. Hence,
\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)<0
for
s>0
and
\ell^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)>0
for
s<0
.
In particular,
\lvert\ell^{\prime}(s)\rvert\leq\frac{1}{4}\,\lvert s\rvert
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Throughout the proof, we assume that
A_{0}>0
without loss of generality.
We prove by induction the following claim: for
t\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\gamma\coloneqq\frac{1}{200}\min\Bigl\{\delta,\,8-\delta,\,\frac{8-\delta}{A_{0}}\Bigr\}\,,

(42)

it holds that
\lvert A_{t}\rvert\leq A_{0}\exp(-\gamma t)
.
This clearly holds at initialization.

Suppose that the claim holds up to iteration
t
.
Using the bounds on
|g^{\prime}|
and
|\ell^{\prime}|
, it follows that

\displaystyle b_{t+1}

\displaystyle\geq b_{t}-\lvert\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\rvert\,\lvert A_{t}\rvert\,g^{\prime}(b_{t})\geq b_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\,\eta\,\lvert A_{t}\rvert

\displaystyle\geq b_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\,\eta A_{0}\exp(-\gamma t)\geq\cdots\geq b_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\,\eta A_{0}\sum_{s=0}^{t}\exp(-\gamma s)\geq-\frac{\eta A_{0}}{\gamma}\,.

In particular,
b_{t}\geq-1
and
g(b_{t})>0.08
, since
\eta\leq\frac{\gamma}{A_{0}}
.
Also, the bound shows that if the claim holds for all
t
, then we obtain the desired conclusion.

It remains to establish the inductive claim; assume that it holds up to iteration
t
.
For the dynamics of
A
, by symmetry we may suppose that
A_{t}>0
.
From
\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\leq A_{t}g(b_{t})/4
and
g(b_{t})\leq g(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle A_{t+1}=A_{t}-2\eta d^{2}\,\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,g(b_{t})

\displaystyle\geq\Bigl(1-\frac{\eta d^{2}}{2}\,{g(b_{t})}^{2}\Bigr)\,A_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\Bigl(1-\frac{\eta d^{2}}{2}\,{g(0)}^{2}\Bigr)\,A_{t}=-\Bigl(1-\frac{\delta}{4}\Bigr)\,A_{t}\,.

This shows that
A_{t+1}\geq-(1-\gamma)\,A_{t}
.
Next, we show that
A_{t+1}\leq(1-\gamma)\,A_{t}
. First, if
A_{t}g(b_{t})\leq 2
,

\displaystyle A_{t+1}

\displaystyle=A_{t}-2\eta d^{2}\,\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,g(b_{t})\leq A_{t}-\frac{1}{4}\,\eta d^{2}\,A_{t}\,{g(b_{t})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(1-\frac{(8-\delta)\,\pi}{4}\,{g(b_{t})}^{2}\Bigr)\,A_{t}\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{(8-\delta)}{4}\,\pi\cdot 0.08^{2}\Bigr)\,A_{t}\leq(1-\gamma)\,A_{t}\,,

since we have
g(b_{t})>0.08
. Next, if
A_{t}g(b_{t})\geq 2
, then

\displaystyle A_{t+1}

\displaystyle=A_{t}-2\eta d^{2}\,\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,g(b_{t})\leq A_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\,\eta d^{2}\,g(b_{t})=\Bigl(1-\frac{(8-\delta)\,\pi}{2}\,\frac{g(b_{t})}{A_{t}}\Bigr)\,A_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{(8-\delta)\,\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{0.08}{A_{0}}\Bigr)\,A_{t}\leq(1-\gamma)\,A_{t}\,.

This shows that
\lvert A_{t+1}\rvert\leq(1-\gamma)\,\lvert A_{t}\rvert
for the case
A_{t}>0
.
A similar conclusion is obtained for the case
A_{t}<0
.
The induction is complete.
∎

E.3
EoS regime

Proof of
Theorem 4
.

The following proof is analogous to the proof of
7
.
Assume throughout that
A_{t}\neq 0
for all
t
.
Recall the dynamics for
b
:

\displaystyle b_{t+1}

\displaystyle=b_{t}-\eta\,\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))\,A_{t}g^{\prime}(b_{t})\,.

Since
\ell^{\prime}(s)/s\to 1/4
as
s\to 0
, and
\ell^{\prime}
is increasing, this equation implies that if
\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\lvert A_{t}\rvert}>0
then
b_{t}
must keep decreasing until
\frac{1}{2}\,d^{2}{g(b_{t})}^{2}<2/\eta
.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that there exists
\varepsilon>0
with
\frac{1}{2}\,d^{2}g(b_{t})^{2}>(2+\varepsilon)/\eta
, for all
t
. Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
be such that
1-(2+\varepsilon)\,(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})<-1
, i.e.,
\varepsilon^{\prime}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2+\varepsilon}
.
Then, there exists
\delta>0
such
\lvert A_{t}\rvert\leq\delta
implies
\ell^{\prime}(A_{t}g(b_{t}))/(A_{t}g(b_{t}))>\frac{1}{4}\,(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})
, hence

\displaystyle\frac{|A_{t+1}|}{|A_{t}|}=\Bigl\lvert 1-4\cdot\frac{1}{4}\,(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})\cdot\frac{1}{2}\,\eta d^{2}\,{g(b_{t})}^{2}\Bigr\rvert>|(2+\varepsilon)\,(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})-1|>1\,.

The above means that
\lvert A_{t}\rvert
increases until it exceeds
\delta
, i.e.,
\liminf_{t\to\infty}{\lvert A_{t}\rvert}\geq\delta
. This is our desired contradiction and it implies that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\,d^{2}g(b_{t})^{2}\leq 2/\eta
.
∎

Remark 4
.

A straightforward calculation yields that when
(a_{\star}^{-},a_{\star}^{+},b_{\star})
is a global minimizer (i.e.,
a_{\star}^{-}+a_{\star}^{+}=0
), then
\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\nabla^{2}f(a_{\star}^{-},a_{\star}^{+},b_{\star})\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\,d^{2}\,{g(b_{\star})}^{2}
.
The mean model initialized at
(A_{0},0)
approximately reaches
(0,0)
whose sharpness is
d^{2}\,{g(0)}^{2}/2=d^{2}/4\pi
.
Hence, the bias learning regime
2/\eta<d^{2}/(4\pi)
precisely corresponds to the EoS regime,
2/\eta<\lambda_{\max}\bigl(\nabla^{2}f(a_{\star}^{-},a_{\star}^{+},b_{\star})\bigr)
.

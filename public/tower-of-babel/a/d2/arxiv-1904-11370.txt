NEW INTEGRAL TRANSFORM: SHEHU TRANSFORM A GENERALIZATION OF SUMUDU AND LAPLACE TRANSFORM FOR SOLVING DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS
SHEHU MAITAMA ∗ , WEIDONG ZHAO

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a Laplace-type integral transform called the Shehu transform which is a generalization of the Laplace and the Sumudu integral transforms for solving differential equations in the time domain. The proposed integral transform is successfully derived from the classical Fourier integral transform and is applied to both ordinary and partial differential equations to show its simplicity, efficiency, and the high accuracy.

\digamma[f(t)]=f(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-i\omega t\right)f(t)dt.

(1.1)

The Fourier transform have many applications in physics and

is similar with the Fourier transform and is defined as:

\pounds[f(t)]=F(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-st\right)f(t)dt.

The Laplace transform is highly efficient for solving some class

in

the Laplace transform, and the Fourier transform is that the
Laplace transform can be defined for both stable and unstable

system. In mathematical literature, the discrete-time equivalent

15
]
converts a

The multiplicative version of the two-sided Laplace transform

M[f(s);s]=f^{*}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}f(x)dx.

{\mathbb{N}^{+}}[f(t)](s,u)=R(s,u)=\frac{1}{u}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)f(t)dt,\,\,s\,>0,\,\,u>0,

Due to the rapid development in the physical science and
engineering models, there are many other integral transforms in

result, many authors became highly interested to come up with the
alternative approach for solving many real-life problems. In 2016,
Atangana and Alkaltani introduced a new double integral equation

Motivated by the above-mentioned researches, in this paper we
proposed a Laplace-type integral transform called Shehu transform

Laplace-type integral transform converges to Laplace transform

s=1

proposed integral transform is successfully applied to both
ordinary and partial differential equations. All the results
obtained in the applications section can easily be verified using
the Laplace or Fourier integral transforms. Throughout this paper,
the Shehu transform is denoted by an operator

2.
Main result

Definition 1
.

The Shehu transform of the function
v(t)
of exponential order is defined over the set of functions,

A=\left\{v(t):\exists\,\,N,\,\eta_{1},\,\eta_{2}>0,\,\,\left|v(t)\right|<N\exp\left(\frac{\left|t\right|}{\eta_{i}}\right),\,\,\textnormal{if}\,\,t\in(-1)^{i}\times\left[0,\infty\right)\right\}
,

by the following integral

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[v(t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V(s,u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)v(t)dt

(2.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\alpha}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)v(t)dt;\,\,\,\,s\,>0,\,\,u>0.

It converges if the limit of the integral exists, and diverges if
not.

The inverse Shehu transform is given by

{\mathbb{S}^{-1}}\left[V(s,u)\right]=v(t),\,\,\,for\,\,\,t\geq 0.

(2.2)

Equivalently

v(t)={\mathbb{S}^{-1}}\left[V(s,u)\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\alpha-i\infty}^{\alpha+i\infty}\frac{1}{u}\exp\left(\frac{st}{u}\right)V(s,u)\,ds,

(2.3)

where
s
and
u
are the Shehu transform variables, and
\alpha

is a real constant and the integral in Equ.(
2.3
) is taken
along
s=\alpha
in the complex plane
s=x+iy
.

Theorem 1
.

The sufficient condition for the existence of Shehu
transform
.
If the function
v(t)
is piecewise continues in every finite
interval
0\leq t\leq\beta
and of exponential order
\alpha
for

t>\beta
. Then its Shehu transform
V(s,u)
exists.

Proof.
For any positive number
\beta
, we algebraically
deduce

\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)v(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\beta}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)v(t)dt+\int_{\beta}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)v(t)dt.

(2.4)

Since the function
v(t)
is piecewise continues in every finite
interval
0\leq t\leq\beta
, then the first integral on the right
hand side exists. Besides, the second integral on the right hand
side exists, since the function
v(t)
is of exponential order

\alpha
for
t>\beta
. To verify this claim, we consider the
following case

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\beta}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)v(t)dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\beta}^{\infty}\left|\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)v(t)\right|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)\left|v(t)\right|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\beta}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)N\exp\left(\alpha t\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\int_{\beta}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{uN}{(s-\alpha u)}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{uN}{s-\alpha u}.

The proof is complete.
\Box

Property 1
.

Linearity property of Shehu transform
. Let the functions
\alpha v(t)
and
\beta w(t)
be in set A, then
\left(\alpha v(t)+\beta w(t)\right)\in
A, where
\alpha
and
\beta
are nonzero arbitrary constants, and

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\alpha v(t)+\beta w(t)\right]=\alpha{\mathbb{S}}\left[v(t)\right]+\beta{\mathbb{S}}\left[w(t)\right].

(2.5)

Proof.
Using the Definition 1 of Shehu transform, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[\alpha v(t)+\beta w(t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)(\alpha v(t)+\beta w(t))dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)(\alpha v(t))\,dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)(\beta w(t))dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)v(t)dt+\beta\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)w(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha u\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-st\right)v(ut)dt+\beta u\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-st\right)w(ut)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha{\mathbb{S}}\left[v(t)\right]+\beta{\mathbb{S}}\left[w(t)\right].

The proof is complete.
\Box

Property 2
.

Change of scale property of Shehu transform.
Let the function
v(\beta t)
be in set A, where
\beta
is an arbitrary constant. Then

{\mathbb{S}}\left[v(\beta t)\right]=\frac{u}{\beta}V\left(\frac{s}{\beta},u\right).

(2.6)

Proof.
Using the Definition 1 of Shehu transform, we
deduce

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[v(\beta t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)v(\beta t)dt

(2.7)

Substituting
\eta=\beta t
which implies
t=\frac{\eta}{\beta}

and
dt=\frac{d\eta}{\beta}
in Equ.(
2.7
) yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[v(\beta t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-s\eta}{u\beta}\right)v(\eta)d\eta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u\beta}\right)v\left(t\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{\beta}\right)v(ut)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u}{\beta}V\left(\frac{s}{\beta},u\right).

The proof is complete.
\Box

Theorem 2
.

Derivative of Shehu transform.

If the function
v^{(n)}(t)
is
the
nth
derivative of the function
v(t)\in A
with respect to

{}^{\prime}t^{\prime}
, then its Shehu transform is defined by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[v^{(n)}(t)\right]=\frac{s^{n}}{u^{n}}V(s,u)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{s}{u}\right)^{n-(k+1)}v^{(k)}(0).

(2.8)

When n=1,  2,  and 3 in Equ. (
2.8
) above, we obtain
the following derivatives with respect to
t
.

{\mathbb{S}}\left[v^{\prime}(t)\right]=\frac{s}{u}V(s,u)-v(0).

(2.9)

{\mathbb{S}}\left[v^{\prime\prime}(t)\right]=\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}V(s,u)-\frac{s}{u}v(0)-v^{\prime}(0).

(2.10)

{\mathbb{S}}\left[v^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)\right]=\frac{s^{3}}{u^{3}}V(s,u)-\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}v(0)-\frac{s}{u}v^{\prime}(0)-v^{\prime\prime}(0).

(2.11)

Proof.
Now suppose Equ. (
2.8
) is true for
n=k
.
Then using Equ. (
2.9
) and the induction hypothesis, we
deduce

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[(v^{(k)}(t))^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s}{u}{\mathbb{S}}\left[v^{(k)}(t)\right]-v^{(k)}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s}{u}\left[\frac{s^{k}}{u^{k}}{\mathbb{S}}\left[v(t)\right]-\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{s}{u}\right)^{k-(i+1)}v^{(i)}(0)\right]-v^{(k)}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{s}{u}\right)^{k+1}{\mathbb{S}}\left[v(t)\right]-\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\frac{s}{u}\right)^{k-i}v^{(i)}(0),

which implies that Equ. (
2.8
) holds for
n=k+1
. By
induction hypothesis the proof is complete.
\Box

The following important properties are obtain using the Leibniz’s
rule

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\partial v(x,t)}{\partial x}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)\frac{\partial v(x,t)}{\partial x}\,dt=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)v(x,t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[V(x,s,u)\right]\Rightarrow{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\partial v(x,t)}{\partial x}\right]=\frac{d}{dx}\left[V(x,s,u)\right],

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}v(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)\frac{\partial^{2}v(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}\,dt=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)v(x,t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\left[V(x,s,u)\right]\Rightarrow{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}v(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}\right]=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\left[V(x,s,u)\right],

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\partial^{n}v(x,t)}{\partial x^{n}}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)\frac{\partial^{n}v(x,t)}{\partial x^{n}}\,dt=\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)v(x,t)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial x^{n}}\left[V(x,s,u)\right]\Rightarrow{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\partial^{n}v(x,t)}{\partial x^{n}}\right]=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left[V(x,s,u)\right].

3.
Some useful results of Shehu transform

Property 3
.

Let the function
v(t)=1
be in set A. Then its Shehu transform is
given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[1\right]=\frac{u}{s}.

(3.1)

Proof.
Using Equ.(
2.1
), we deduce

{\mathbb{S}}\left[1\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt=-\frac{u}{s}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}=\frac{u}{s}.

This ends the proof.
\Box

Property 4
.

Let the function
v(t)=t
be in set A. Then its Shehu transform is
given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[t\right]=\frac{u^{2}}{s^{2}}.

(3.2)

Proof.
Using the Definition 1 of the Shehu transform and
integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[t\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt=\frac{u}{s}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[t\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}+\frac{u}{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{u^{2}}{s^{2}}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}=\frac{u^{2}}{s^{2}}.

Thus the proof ends.
\Box

Property 5
.

Let the function
v(t)=\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\,\,\,n=0,1,2..
be in set
A. Then its Shehu transform is given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\right]=\left(\frac{u}{s}\right)^{n+1}.

(3.3)

Proof.
From the Definition 1 of the Shehu
transform and integration by parts, we deduce

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[t^{n}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt=\frac{u}{s}n\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n-1}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{2}}{s^{2}}n(n-1)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n-2}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u^{3}}{s^{3}}n(n-1)(n-2)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n-3}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{4}}{s^{4}}n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n-4}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u^{5}}{s^{5}}n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)(n-4)\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n-5}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)dt=\cdots=n!\left(\frac{u}{s}\right)^{n+1}.

The proof is completed.
\Box

Property 6
.

Let the function

v(t)=\frac{t^{n}}{\Gamma(n+1)}\,\,\,n=0,1,2,\cdots
be in set A.
Then its Shehu transform is given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{t^{n}}{\Gamma(n+1)}\right]=\left(\frac{u}{s}\right)^{n+1}.

(3.4)

The proof of property 6 follows immediately from the previous
property 5.
\Box

Property 7
.

Let the function
v(t)=\exp(\alpha t)
be in A. Then its Shehu
transform is given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\exp(\alpha t)\right]=\frac{u}{s-\alpha u}.

(3.5)

Proof.
Using Equ.(
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[\exp(\alpha t)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{u}{s-\alpha u}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}=\frac{u}{s-\alpha u}.

This ends the proof.
\Box

Property 8
.

Let the function
v(t)=t\exp(\alpha t)
be in set A. Then its
Shehu transform is given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[t\exp(\alpha t)\right]=\frac{u^{2}}{(s-\alpha u)^{2}}.

(3.6)

Proof.
Using the Definition 1 of the Shehu
transform and integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[t\exp(\alpha t)\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}t\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{u}{s-\alpha u}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[t\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}

\displaystyle+\frac{u}{s-\alpha u}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{u^{2}}{(s-\alpha u)^{2}}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}=\frac{u^{2}}{(s-\alpha u)^{2}}.

The proof is complete.
\Box

Property 9
.

Let the function
v(t)=\frac{t^{n}\exp(\alpha t)}{n!}\,\,\,n=0,1,2,...
be in set A. Then its Shehu transform is
given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{t^{n}\exp(\alpha t)}{n!}\right]=\frac{u^{n+1}}{(s-\alpha u)^{n+1}}.

(3.7)

Proof.
Using the Definition 1 of the Shehu
transform and integration by parts, we deduce

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[t^{n}\exp(\alpha t)\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{un}{(s-\alpha u)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n-1}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u^{2}n(n-1)}{(s-\alpha u)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n-2}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)dt=\cdots=\frac{n!}{(s-\alpha u)^{n+1}}.

Thus the proof is complete.
\Box

Property 10
.

Let the function
v(t)=\frac{t^{n}}{\Gamma(n+1)}\exp(\alpha t)\,\,\,n=0,1,2,...
be in set A. Then its Shehu transform is
given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{t^{n}\exp(\alpha t)}{\Gamma(n+1)}\right]=\frac{u^{n+1}}{(s-\alpha u)^{n+1}}.

(3.8)

The proof of Property 10 follows as a direct consequence of
Property 9.
\Box

Property 11
.

Let the function
v(t)=sin(\alpha t)
be in set A. Then its Shehu
transform is given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\sin(\alpha t)\right]=\frac{\alpha u^{2}}{s^{2}+\alpha^{2}u^{2}}.

(3.9)

Proof.
Using the Definition 1 of the Shehu transform and
integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[\sin(\alpha t)\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)\sin(\alpha t)dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{u}{s}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)\sin(\alpha t)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}+\frac{u\alpha}{s}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)\cos(\alpha t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha u^{2}}{s^{2}}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)\cos(\alpha t)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}-\frac{\alpha^{2}u^{2}}{s^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)\sin(\alpha t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha u^{2}}{s^{2}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}u^{2}}{s^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{st}{u}\right)\sin(\alpha t)dt.

Simplifying the required integrals complete the proof of Property
11.

\displaystyle\frac{u^{2}\alpha}{(s-\beta u)^{2}}-\frac{u^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(s-\beta u)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\beta u)}{u}t\right)\sin(\alpha t)dt.

Property 17
.

Let the function
\frac{\exp\left(\beta t\right)-\exp\left(\alpha t\right)}{\beta-\alpha}
be in set A. Then its Shehu transform is
given by

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\exp\left(\alpha t\right)}{\beta-\alpha}\right]=\frac{u^{2}}{(s-\alpha u)(s-\beta u)}.

(3.15)

Proof.
Using the definition of Shehu transform, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\exp\left(\alpha t\right)}{\beta-\alpha}\right]=\frac{u}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)\left(\exp(\beta t)-\exp\left(\alpha t\right)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{(\beta u-s)}{u}t}dt-\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{(\alpha u-s)t}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{(\beta-\alpha)(\beta u-s)}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\beta u)t}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}

\displaystyle-\frac{u}{(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha u-s)}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\beta u)t}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{u}{(\beta-\alpha)(\beta u-s)}+\frac{u}{(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha u-s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-u(\alpha u-s)+u(\beta u-s)}{(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha u-s)(\beta u-s)}=\frac{u^{2}}{(s-\alpha u)(s-\beta u)}.

The proof is complete.
\Box

Property 18
.

Let the function
\frac{\beta\exp\left(\beta t\right)-\alpha\exp(\alpha t)}{\beta-\alpha}
be in set A. Then its Shehu
transform is given by:

{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\beta\exp\left(\beta t\right)-\alpha\exp\left(\alpha t\right)}{\beta-\alpha}\right]=\frac{us}{(s-\alpha u)(s-\beta u)}.

(3.16)

Proof:

Using the definition of Shehu transform, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}\left[\frac{\beta\exp\left(\beta t\right)-\alpha\exp\left(\alpha t\right)}{\beta-\alpha}\right]=\frac{1}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-st}{u}\right)\left(\beta\exp\left(\beta t\right)-\alpha\exp\left(\alpha t\right)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{(\beta u-s)}{u}t\right)dt-\frac{\alpha}{\beta-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(\frac{(\alpha u-s)t}{u}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{u\beta}{(\beta-\alpha)(\beta u-s)}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\beta u)t}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}

\displaystyle-\frac{u\alpha}{(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha u-s)}\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\alpha u)t}{u}\right)\right]^{\gamma}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{u\beta}{(\beta-\alpha)(\beta u-s)}+\frac{u\alpha}{(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha u-s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-u\beta(\alpha u-s)+u\alpha(\beta u-s)}{(\beta-\alpha)(\alpha u-s)(\beta u-s)}=\frac{us}{(s-\alpha u)(s-\beta u)}.

This ends the proof.
\Box

4.
Applications

In this section, the applications of the proposed transform are
presented. The simplicity, efficiency and high accuracy of the
Shehu transform are clearly illustrated.

Example 1
.

Consider the following first order ordinary differential equation

\frac{dv(t)}{dt}+v(t)=0,

(4.1)

subject to the initial condition

v(0)=1.

(4.2)

Applying the Shehu transform on both sides of Equ. (
4.1
),
we get

\frac{s}{u}V(s,u)-v(0)+V(s,u)=0.

(4.3)

Substituting the given initial condition and simplifying, we
deduce

V(s,u)=\frac{u}{s+u}

(4.4)

Taking the inverse Shehu transform of Equ. (
4.4
), yields

v(t)=\exp(-t).

(4.5)

Example 2
.

Consider the following second order ordinary differential equation

\frac{d^{2}v(t)}{dt^{2}}+\frac{dv(t)}{dt}=1

(4.6)

subject to the initial conditions

v(0)=0,\,\,\,\,\frac{dv(0)}{dt}=0.

(4.7)

Applying the Shehu transform on both sides of Equ. (
4.6
),
we obtain

\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}V(s,u)-\frac{s}{u}v(0)-v^{\prime}(0)+\frac{s}{u}V(s,u)-v(0)=\frac{u}{s}.

(4.8)

Substituting the given initial conditions and simplifying, we
deduce

V(s,u)=-\frac{u}{s}+\frac{u^{2}}{s^{2}}+\frac{u}{s+u}.

(4.9)

Taking the inverse Shehu transform of Equ. (
4.9
), we get

v(t)=-1+t+\exp(-t).

(4.10)

Example 3
.

Consider the following second nonhomogeneous order ordinary
differential equation

\frac{d^{2}v(t)}{dt^{2}}-3\frac{dv(t)}{dt}+2v(t)=\exp(3t)

(4.11)

subject to the initial conditions

v(0)=1,\,\,\,\,\frac{dv(0)}{dt}=0.

(4.12)

Applying the Shehu transform on both sides of Equ. (
4.11
),
yields

\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}V(s,u)-\frac{s}{u}v(0)-v^{\prime}(0)-3\left(\frac{s}{u}V(s,u)-v(0)\right)+2V(s,u)=\frac{u}{s-3u}.

(4.13)

Substituting the given initial conditions and simplifying, we
obtain

V(s,u)=\frac{5}{2}\frac{u}{(s-u)}-2\frac{u}{s-2u}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{u}{(s-3u)}.

(4.14)

Taking the inverse Shehu transform of Equ. (
4.14
), we get

v(t)=\frac{5}{2}\exp(t)-2\exp(2t)+\frac{1}{2}\exp(3t).

(4.15)

Example 4
.

Consider the following ordinary differential equation

\frac{d^{2}v(t)}{dt^{2}}+2\frac{dv(t)}{dt}+5v(t)=\exp(-t)\sin(t)

(4.16)

subject to the initial conditions

v(0)=0,\,\,\,\,\frac{dv(0)}{dt}=1.

(4.17)

Applying the Shehu transform on both sides of Equ. (
4.16
),
we get

\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}V(s,u)-\frac{s}{u}v(0)-v^{\prime}(0)+2\left(\frac{s}{u}V(s,u)-\frac{s}{u}v(0)\right)+5V(s,u)=\frac{u^{2}}{(s+u)^{2}+u^{2}}

(4.18)

Substituting the given initial conditions and simplifying, we get

V(s,u)=\frac{1}{3}\frac{u^{2}}{((s+u)^{2}+u^{2})}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{u^{2}}{((s+u)^{2}+(2u)^{2})}

(4.19)

Taking the inverse Shehu transform of Equ. (
4.19
), we get

v(t)=\frac{1}{3}\exp(-t)\sin(t)+\frac{2}{3}\exp(-t)\sin(2t).

(4.20)

Example 5
.

Consider the following homogeneous partial differential equation

\frac{\partial v(x,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial^{2}v(x,t)}{\partial x^{2}}

(4.21)

subject to the boundary and initial conditions

v(0,t)=0,\,\,\,v(1,t)=0,\,\,\,\,v(x,0)=3sin(2\pi x).

(4.22)

Applying the Shehu transform on both sides of Equ. (
4.21
),
we get

\frac{s}{u}V(x,s,u)-v(x,0)=\frac{d^{2}V(x,s,u)}{dx^{2}}.

(4.23)

Substituting the given initial condition and simplifying, we get

\frac{d^{2}V(x,s,u)}{dx^{2}}-\frac{s}{u}V(x,s,u)=-3sin(2\pi x).

(4.24)

The general solution of Equ. (
4.24
) can be written as

V(x,s,u)=V_{h}(x,s,u)+V_{p}(x,s,u),

(4.25)

where
V_{h}(x,s,u)
is the solution of the homogeneous part which
is given by

V_{h}(x,s,u)=\alpha_{1}\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{s}{u}}x\right)+\alpha_{2}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{s}{u}}x\right),

(4.26)

and
V_{p}(x,s,u)
is the solution of the nonhomogeneous part which
is given by

V_{p}(x,s,u)=\beta_{1}sin(2\pi x)+\beta_{2}cos(2\pi x).

(4.27)

Applying the boundary conditions on Equ. (
4.26
), we get

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=0\,\,\,\,and\,\,\,\,\alpha_{1}\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{s}{u}}\right)+\alpha_{2}\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{s}{u}}\right)=0\Rightarrow V_{h}(x,s,u)=0,

since
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=0
.

Using the method of undetermined coefficients on the
nonhomogeneous part, we get

V_{p}(x,s,u)=\frac{3u}{s+4\pi^{2}u}\sin(2\pi x),

(4.28)

Since,
\beta_{1}=\frac{3u}{s+4\pi^{2}u},
and
\beta_{2}=0
.

Then Equ. (
4.25
) will become

V(x,s,u)=\frac{3u}{s+4\pi^{2}u}\sin(2\pi x),

(4.29)

Taking the inverse Shehu transform of Equ. (
4.29
),
we get

v(x,t)=3\exp(-4\pi^{2}t)\sin(2\pi x).

(4.30)

5.
Conclusion

We introduced an efficient Laplace-type integral transform called
the Shehu transform for solving both ordinary and partial
differential equations. We presented its existence and inverse
transform. We presented some useful properties and their
applications for solving ordinary and partial differential
equations. We provide a comprehensive list of the Laplace
transform, Sumudu transform, and the natural transform in table 1
to show their mutual relationship with the Shehu transform.
Finally, based on the mathematical formulations, simplicity and
the findings of the proposed integral transform, we conclude that
it is highly efficient because of the following advantages:

•

It is a generalization of the Laplace and the Sumudu integral transforms.

•

Its visualization is easier than the Sumudu transform, the natural transform, and the Elzaki transform.

•

The Laplace-type integral transform become Laplace transform when the variable
u=1
and the Yang integral transform when the variable
s=1
.

•

It can easily be applied directly to some class of ordinary and the partial differential equations as demonstrated in the application section.

•

For advanced research in physical science and engineering, the proposed integral transform can be considered a stepping-stone to the Sumudu transform, the natural transform, the Elzaki transform, and the Laplace transform.

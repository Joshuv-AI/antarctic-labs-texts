Abstract
The stochastic gradient descent method with momentum (SGDM) is a common approach for solving large-scale and stochastic optimization problems. Despite its popularity, the convergence behavior of SGDM remains less understood in nonconvex scenarios. This is primarily due to the absence of a sufficient descent property and challenges in simultaneously controlling the momentum and stochastic errors in an almost sure sense. To address these challenges, we investigate the behavior of SGDM over specific time windows, rather than examining the descent of consecutive iterates as in traditional studies. This time window-based approach simplifies the convergence analysis and enables us to establish the iterate convergence result for SGDM under the Łojasiewicz property. We further provide local convergence rates which depend on the underlying Łojasiewicz exponent and the utilized step size schemes.



Keywords:
Stochastic gradient descent, momentum, Łojasiewicz inequality, iterate convergence, convergence rates

1.2
Contributions

The analysis of
\mathsf{SGDM}
is challenging due to the inherent entanglement of stochastic errors and the momentum mechanism, which complicates the derivation of convergence properties. Even in the deterministic setting, momentum methods typically do not monotonically decrease the objective function across iterations. This effect is amplified in
\mathsf{SGDM}
, where individual trajectories can exhibit vastly different behavior. To address these challenges, we employ a twofold strategy:

Time window-based analysis.
We leverage time window techniques (see
Section

3.2
)
to effectively estimate the aggregation of stochastic errors (
Lemma

3.2
) and to provide iterate bounds for
\mathsf{SGDM}
(
Lemma

3.3
) in an almost sure sense.

Auxiliary iterates and merit function.
In
Section

3.3
, we employ an auxiliary iterate sequence
\{z^{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, coupled with a carefully designed merit function
\mathcal{M}
. This strategic pairing allows disentangling the momentum term from the main dynamics of
\mathsf{SGDM}
. Together with the time window techniques, this enables us to establish an approximate descent-type property (
Lemma

3.4
) for
\mathsf{SGDM}
.

These tools allow us to show novel convergence results for momentum methods in the stochastic setting. Our key contributions are summarized as follows:

•

In the nonconvex setting, we show the convergence of the function and gradient values for the generic stochastic momentum mechanism —
\mathsf{SGDM}
. In particular, we prove that
\{f(\bm{x}^{k})\}_{k}
converges and
\|\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k})\|\to 0
almost surely (
Theorem

4.1
).

•

Leveraging the Łojasiewicz inequality — a mild assumption on the local geometry of
f
— we establish the almost sure convergence of the iterates generated by
\mathsf{SGDM}
to a stationary point of
f
. This seems to be the first iterate convergence guarantee for stochastic momentum methods in a nonconvex setting with momentum parameters being chosen freely from
\lambda\in[0,1)
and
\nu\geq 0
1
1

1

In practice, the momentum parameter is a fixed number, typically,
\lambda=0.9
rather than
\lambda_{k}\to 0
or
1
for
\mathsf{SHB}
, see,
[
43
,
39
]
and
[
17
, Chapter 8.3.2]
.
. We prove that the iterates generated by
\mathsf{SGDM}
converge to some stationary point almost surely without requiring the ubiquitous “a priori bounded iterate” assumption,
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|\bm{x}^{k}\|<\infty
a.s.
2
2

2

This assumption seems prevalent in the study of stochastic algorithms under the Łojasiewicz inequality
[
44
,
25
,
12
]
.
, or convexity of
f
. Instead we work with the weaker condition
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|\bm{x}^{k}\|<\infty
(a.s.) which is standard in
deterministic
Łojasiewicz inequality-based analyses
[
2
,
3
]
. This extends the theoretical guarantees for
\mathsf{SGDM}
to a wider spectrum of optimization problems.

•

Beyond iterate convergence, we further provide the asymptotic convergence rates of
\mathsf{SGDM}
for general step sizes (
Theorem

5.1
) and for polynomial step sizes (
Corollary

5.1
) — depending on the underlying Łojasiewicz exponent. The obtained rates improve the existing results in the convex
[
41
,
27
]
and nonconvex setting
[
44
]
(for
\mathsf{SGD}
) and can match the rates in the strongly convex setting.

Modeling the Stochastic Process and Assumptions

We assume that there is a sufficiently rich probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\}_{k},\mathbb{P})
that allows us to model the stochastic components of
\mathsf{SGDM}
in a unified way. We will use bold letters
\bm{x}^{k}
,
\bm{g}^{k}
, etc
.
to represent random variables while lowercase letters are typically reserved for realizations of a random variable,
x^{k}=\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)
, or deterministic parameters. Hence, each approximation of
\nabla f(x^{k})
is understood as a realization of a random vector
\bm{g}^{k}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
\lambda\in[0,1)
,
\nu\geq 0
,
\mathsf{SGDM}
generates a stochastic process
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
via

\left[\begin{aligned} \tilde{\bm{x}}^{k}&=\bm{x}^{k}+\nu(\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1}),\\
\bm{g}^{k}&=\nabla f(\tilde{\bm{x}}^{k})-\bm{e}^{k},\\
\bm{x}^{k+1}&=\bm{x}^{k}-\alpha_{k}\bm{g}^{k}+\lambda(\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1}),\end{aligned}\right.

(4)

where
\bm{e}^{k}
represents the stochastic errors and
\bm{x}^{1}=\bm{x}^{0}=x^{0}
are deterministic initial points. We work with the natural filtration
{\cal F}_{k}:=\sigma(\bm{x}^{0},\bm{x}^{1},\ldots,\bm{x}^{k})
(i.e., each
\bm{x}^{k}
is
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-measurable). Next, we introduce our main assumptions on the stochastic errors.

Assumption 2.1
.

Given the probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},\{{\cal F}_{k}\}_{k},\mathbb{P})
, the errors
\{\bm{e}^{k}\}_{k}
are assumed to satisfy
\mathbb{E}[\bm{e}^{k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{e}^{k}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]\leq\sigma^{2}
(a.s.) for all
k
.

This assumption is standard in the analysis of stochastic methods
[
7
,
8
,
11
,
32
]
. Next, we impose the following assumption on the objective function
f
.

Assumption 2.2
.

The function
f
is bounded from below by some
\bar{f}\in\mathbb{R}
and the gradient
\nabla f
is Lipschitz continuous with parameter
{\sf L}>0
.

This assumption is ubiquitous among studies on the convergence of first-order methods, see,
[
5
,
6
,
23
,
8
]
.
Below, we formally introduce the Łojasiewicz inequality, a mild assumption on the local geometry of the objective function. It plays a key role in establishing the iterate convergence of
\mathsf{SGDM}
.

Assumption 2.3
.

The function
f
satisfies the Łojasiewicz inequality on
\mathrm{crit}(f)
, i.e., for every
x^{*}\in\mathrm{crit}(f)
, there are
\eta\in(0,\infty]
and a neighborhood
U(x^{*})
of
x^{*}

such that

\hskip-8.61108pt\|\nabla{f}(x)\|\geq{\sf C}_{f}|f(x)-f(x^{*})|^{\theta}\quad\forall~x\in U(x^{*})\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:0<|f(x)-f(x^{*})|<\eta\},

where
{\sf C}_{f}={\sf C}_{f}(x^{*})>0
and
\theta=\theta(x^{*})\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
denotes the Łojasiewicz exponent of
f
at
x^{*}
.

One main feature of the Łojasiewicz inequality is that it holds universally for subanalytic and semialgebraic functions
[
31
,
20
,
2
]
. Moreover, a broad class of problems arising in practice and applications satisfy this property, see
[
4
, Section 4]
and
[
6
, Section 5]
.

Time Window Techniques

In this section, we introduce a time window-based analysis technique, which is tailored to facilitate the iterate convergence analysis of stochastic methods. We start with a toy example to motivate why such tool is needed in the nonconvex stochastic setting. We formally define the time window in
Section

3.2
. The time window introduces a new “scaling”, which is then used to derive an approximate descent-type property for
\mathsf{SGDM}
in
Section

3.3
.

3.1
Limitations of Classical Approaches

Conventional convergence analyses under the Łojasiewicz inequality are typically based on a verification of the
finite length property
, i.e.,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|<\infty
,

[
2
,
4
,
6
,
23
,
25
,
34
]
. This readily implies that
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence and thus, the iterates
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
have to converge. However, we will now illustrate that this property generally does
not hold
for stochastic methods.

Let us consider the two-dimensional case and let the objective function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and the stochastic error
\bm{e}^{k}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be given by

\displaystyle f(x)=f(x_{1},x_{2}):=\sin(x_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{e}^{k}:=\begin{cases}[0,1]^{\top}&\text{w.p. }50\%,\\
[0,-1]^{\top}&\text{w.p. }50\%.\\
\end{cases}

The function
f
is Lipschitz smooth and analytic (thus, the Łojasiewicz inequality holds,
[
31
]
), and we have
\mathbb{E}[\bm{e}^{k}]=0
,
\mathbb{E}[\|\bm{e}^{k}\|^{2}]=1
, and
\langle\nabla f(x),\bm{e}^{k}\rangle=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
k\geq 1
. By the update rule of
\mathsf{SGD}
, i.e.,
\bm{x}^{k+1}=\bm{x}^{k}-\alpha_{k}(\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k})-\bm{e}^{k})
, we obtain

\|\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|=\alpha_{k}\|\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k})-\bm{e}^{k}\|=\alpha_{k}\sqrt{\|\nabla f(\bm{x}^{k})\|^{2}+\|\bm{e}^{k}\|^{2}}\geq\alpha_{k}\|\bm{e}^{k}\|=\alpha_{k}.

Under the usual condition
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty
,
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
38
]
, it then follows
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\bm{x}^{k+1}-\bm{x}^{k}\|=\infty
a.s.. Consequently, we can not mimic the conventional, deterministic analysis route to prove iterate convergence of stochastic methods in the nonconvex case.

Discussion.
The finite length of
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
is not necessary for convergence. In fact, it is sufficient to show that
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence. To achieve this, we introduce an infinite and increasing subsequence
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{N}
and define
\Gamma_{k}:=\{t\in\mathbb{N}:\gamma_{k}<t\leq\gamma_{k+1}\}
. Our goal is to verify that

{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\|x^{\gamma_{k+1}}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad{\max}_{t\in\Gamma_{k}}\|x^{t}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|\to 0\quad\text{as\quad$k\to\infty$}.

Hence, for any given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
, it holds that
{\sum}_{t=k}^{\infty}\|x^{\gamma_{t+1}}-x^{\gamma_{t}}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
and
{\max}_{t\in\Gamma_{k}}\|x^{t}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
. Furthermore, for all
n>m\geq\gamma_{k_{0}}
, there are integers
k_{2}\geq k_{1}\geq k_{0}
such that
m\in\Gamma_{k_{1}}
and
n\in\Gamma_{k_{2}}
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\|x^{n}-x^{m}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{n}-x^{\gamma_{k_{2}}}\|+\|x^{\gamma_{k_{2}}}-x^{\gamma_{k_{1}}}\|+\|x^{m}-x^{\gamma_{k_{1}}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{t\in\Gamma_{k_{1}}}\|x^{t}-x^{\gamma_{k_{1}}}\|+\max_{t\in\Gamma_{k_{2}}}\|x^{t}-x^{\gamma_{k_{2}}}\|+{\sum}_{t=k_{1}}^{\infty}\|x^{\gamma_{t+1}}-x^{\gamma_{t}}\|<\varepsilon,

which indicates that
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
is a converging Cauchy sequence. The specific construction of
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k}
is tied to “time windows” and will be presented in the following subsection. The proof strategy sketched here will be formally explored in
Section

4
.

\alpha_{k}\leq(1-\delta){\sf T}\leq{\sf T}
for all
k\geq K
. This implies
\varpi(k,{\sf T})=\sup\{n\geq k:\Delta_{k,n}\leq{\sf T}\}
for all
k\geq K
and
\Delta_{\gamma_{k},\gamma_{k+1}}\leq{\sf T}
for all
k
with
\gamma_{k}\geq K
. By the optimality of
\gamma_{k+1}=\varpi(\gamma_{k},{\sf T})
, we can further infer
\Delta_{\gamma_{k},\gamma_{k+1}}\geq{\sf T}-\alpha_{\gamma_{k+1}}\geq\delta{\sf T}
for all

and
\gamma_{k}
satisfying
\gamma_{k}\geq K
. Here, let us also refer to the proof of
[

, Lemma 6.2]
.

The time window-based formalism will allow us to control certain aggregated error terms that are related to the stochastic errors
\{\bm{e}^{k}\}_{k}

Stochastic Error Estimates.
We consider step sizes

of the form

\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}_{++}\;\text{is non-increasing},\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty,\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}<\infty,

for some non-decreasing sequence
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}_{++}
. The condition (

) can be satisfied by, for instance, polynomial step sizes

,

, with

Based on the time window
{\sf T}
and the indices
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k}
, we introduce the aggregated error
\bm{s}_{k}
and the associated event

\bm{s}_{k}:=\max_{t\in\Gamma_{k}}\Big\|{\sum}_{i=\gamma_{k}}^{t-1}\alpha_{i}\bm{e}^{i}\Big\|\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\,\beta_{\gamma_{k}}^{2}\bm{s}_{k}^{2}(\omega)<\infty\right\},

(6)

where
\Gamma_{k}=\{t\in\mathbb{N}:\gamma_{k}<t\leq\gamma_{k+1}\}
. Next, we provide an almost sure bound for the aggregated error terms
\{\bm{s}_{k}\}_{k}
by showing that the event
\mathcal{S}
occurs with probability 1. The proof of
Lemma

3.2
is based on
[
44
, Lemma 6.1]
and is presented in
Section

A.1
.

Lemma 3.2

(Error estimate)
.

Let
Assumption

2.1
hold and suppose that
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfies the condition (
5
). It then holds that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S})=1
.

Note that
Lemma

3.2
provides an upper bound for the aggregated errors and implies
\bm{s}_{k}=o(\beta_{\gamma_{k}}^{-1})
a.s.. Since
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
is assumed to be non-decreasing, we can further infer
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bm{s}_{k}^{2}<\infty
a.s..

3.3
Iterate Bounds and Descent-type Property

To study the convergence of
\mathsf{SGDM}
, we introduce the following auxiliary sequence
\{\bm{z}^{k}\}_{k}
:

\bm{z}^{k}:=\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\bm{x}^{k}-\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}\bm{x}^{k-1}\quad\text{for all}\;k\geq 1.

(7)

Auxiliary sequences and interpolations of this form are frequently used in the analysis of momentum methods, see, e.g.,
[
15
,
29
]
. Moreover, the definition (
7
) directly implies:

\bm{z}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k}=\frac{\lambda}{1-\lambda}(\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{x}^{k-1})\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{z}^{k+1}=\bm{z}^{k}-\frac{\alpha_{k}}{1-\lambda}\bm{g}^{k}\quad\text{for all}\;k\geq 1.

(8)

We further define the stochastic process
\{\bm{d}_{k}\}_{k}
:

\bm{d}_{k}=\max\{{\max}_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}\|\bm{x}^{\ell}-\bm{x}^{\gamma_{k}}\|,\;{\max}_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}\|\bm{z}^{\ell}-\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k}}\|\}.

(9)

Next, we provide several almost sure iterate bounds for terms involving the sequences
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
,
\{\bm{z}^{k}\}_{k}
, and
\{\bm{s}^{k}\}_{k}
. The proof of
Lemma

3.3
is deferred to
subsection

A.2
.

Lemma 3.3

(Iterate bounds)
.

Let
Assumptions

2.1
and
2.2
hold and let
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{SGDM}
with
\lambda\in[0,1)
,
\nu\geq 0
, and
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfying (
5
). For every time window
{\sf T}\in(0,\frac{(1-\lambda)^{2}}{20{\sf L}(1+2\nu)}]
and its associated time indices
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k}
, there exists
K_{\sf T}\geq 1
such that the following bounds hold almost surely for all
k\geq K_{\sf T}
:

\bm{d}_{k}^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\|\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k}}-\bm{x}^{\gamma_{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{15}{(1-\lambda)^{2}}[{\sf T}^{2}\|\nabla f(\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2}+\bm{s}_{k}^{2}],\quad\text{and}

(10)

\|\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k+1}}-\bm{x}^{\gamma_{k+1}}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1+\lambda}{2}\|\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k}}-\bm{x}^{\gamma_{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{8}{(1-\lambda)^{3}}[{\sf T}^{2}\|\nabla f(\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2}+4\bm{s}_{k}^{2}].

(11)

In order to establish a descent-type property for
\mathsf{SGDM}
, we introduce a merit function
\mathcal{M}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
. Setting
\zeta:=\frac{3{\sf L}}{1-\lambda}
,
\mathcal{M}
is defined via:

\mathcal{M}(x,z):=f(z)+\zeta\|z-x\|^{2}\quad\implies\quad\nabla\mathcal{M}(x,z)=\begin{bmatrix}2\zeta(x-z)\\
\nabla f(z)+2\zeta(z-x)\end{bmatrix}.

(12)

Similar merit functions — typically of the form “
f(x^{k})+\zeta\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}
” — have been employed in the analysis of momentum-type methods
[
40
,
47
,
34
,
18
]
. In our setting, analogous usage of “
f(x^{k})
” in
\mathcal{M}
causes additional complications. Thus, our merit function is based on “
f(z^{k})
” instead of “
f(x^{k})
”. Below, we list several important bounds involving
\nabla\mathcal{M}(x,z)
:

\displaystyle 4\zeta^{2}\|x-z\|^{2}\leq\|\nabla\mathcal{M}(x,z)\|^{2},\quad\|\nabla f(z)\|^{2}\leq 2\|\nabla\mathcal{M}(x,z)\|^{2},

(13)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{M}(x,z)\|^{2}\leq 12\zeta^{2}\|z-x\|^{2}+2\|\nabla f(z)\|^{2}.

The latter inequalities follow from

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{M}(x,z)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=4\zeta^{2}\|x-z\|^{2}+\|\nabla f(z)+2\zeta(z-x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=8\zeta^{2}\|x-z\|^{2}+\|\nabla f(z)\|^{2}+4\zeta\langle\nabla f(z),z-x\rangle,

and
-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\|\nabla f(z)\|^{2}-\frac{8\zeta^{2}}{\varepsilon}\|z-x\|^{2}\leq 4\zeta\langle\nabla f(z),z-x\rangle\leq\|\nabla f(z)\|^{2}+4\zeta^{2}\|z-x\|^{2}
for
\varepsilon\in\{1,2\}
.
We now present an approximate, time window-based descent property for
\mathsf{SGDM}
. The proof can be found in
Section

A.3
.

Lemma 3.4

(Approximate descent)
.

Suppose
Assumptions

2.1
and
2.2
hold and
\mathsf{SGDM}
is run with
\lambda\in[0,1)
,
\nu\geq 0
, and
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
fulfilling (
5
). For any time window
{\sf T}\in(0,\frac{(1-\lambda)^{3}}{50{\sf L}(1+2\nu)^{2}}]
and its associated time indices
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k}
, there is

such that

\mathcal{M}(\bm{x}^{\gamma_{k+1}},\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k+1}})+\frac{{\sf L}}{12}\bm{d}_{k}^{2}+\frac{{\sf T}\|\nabla\mathcal{M}(\bm{x}^{\gamma_{k}},\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2}}{100(1-\lambda)}\leq\mathcal{M}(\bm{x}^{\gamma_{k}},\bm{z}^{\gamma_{k}})+\frac{8\bm{s}_{k}^{2}}{(1-\lambda){\sf T}}.

a.s. for all

.

Such choice of

,
3.3
and

4.1
Global Convergence

We first present a basic global convergence result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
Assumptions

2.1
and
2.2
hold and let
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{SGDM}
with
\lambda\in[0,1)
,
\nu\geq 0
, and
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
satisfying

\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}_{++}\;\text{is non-increasing},\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty,\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}<\infty.

(14)

Then, the following statements are valid:

(a)

It holds that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\bm{d}_{k}=0
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\bm{x}^{k}-\bm{z}^{k}\|=0
,
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla{f}(\bm{x}^{k})\|=0
, and
\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla{f}(\bm{z}^{k})\|=0
a.s..

(b)

\{f(\bm{x}^{k})\}_{k}
and
\{f(\bm{z}^{k})\}_{k}
converge to some
\bm{f}^{*}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
a.s..

Proof.

Proof
Set
\beta_{k}=1
in (
5
) and (
6
). By
Lemma

3.2
, we have
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S})=1
, where
\mathcal{S}=\{\omega\in\Omega:{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\;\bm{s}_{k}^{2}(\omega)<\infty\}
. We fix an arbitrary sample
\omega\in\mathcal{S}
and consider
x^{k}\equiv\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)
,
z^{k}\equiv\bm{z}^{k}(\omega)
,
s_{k}\equiv\bm{s}_{k}(\omega)
,
d_{k}\equiv\bm{d}_{k}(\omega)
, etc.. By
Lemma

3.4
, there is
K_{\sf T}\geq 1
such that for all
k\geq K_{\sf T}

\mathcal{M}(x^{\gamma_{k+1}},z^{\gamma_{k+1}})+u_{k+1}+\frac{{\sf L}}{12}d_{k}^{2}+\frac{{\sf T}\|\nabla\mathcal{M}(x^{\gamma_{k}},z^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2}}{100(1-\lambda)}\leq\mathcal{M}(x^{\gamma_{k}},z^{\gamma_{k}})+u_{k},

(15)

where
u_{k}:=\frac{8}{(1-\lambda){\sf T}}{\sum}_{i=k}^{\infty}s_{i}^{2}
. Since
f
is bounded from below, we have
\mathcal{M}(x^{\gamma_{k}},z^{\gamma_{k}})+u_{k}\downarrow f^{*}
for some
f^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
. Telescoping (
15
) and using
Eq.

13
, this yields

\|\nabla f(z^{\gamma_{k}})\|\to 0,\quad d_{k}\to 0,\quad\text{and}\quad{\max}_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}\|z^{\ell}-x^{\ell}\|\to 0\quad\text{as\quad$k\to\infty$}.

(16)

Here, the last result follows from (
8
) and the subsequent relations:

(1-\lambda)\|z^{m}-x^{m}\|={\lambda}\|x^{m}-x^{m-1}\|\leq{\lambda}[\|x^{m}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|+\|x^{m-1}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|]\leq{2\lambda}d_{k}\to 0,\quad\forall~m\in\Gamma_{k}.

Based on (
16
) and applying the Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla f
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\max}_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}\|\nabla f(x^{\ell})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f(x^{\gamma_{k}})\|+{\max}_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}{\sf L}\|x^{\ell}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|\leq\|\nabla f(x^{\gamma_{k}})\|+{\sf L}d_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f(z^{\gamma_{k}})\|+{\sf L}\|z^{\gamma_{k}}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|+{\sf L}d_{k}\to 0\quad\text{as\quad$k\to\infty$.}

Therefore, we can infer
\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|,\|\nabla f(z^{k})\|\to 0
as
\|x^{k}-z^{k}\|\to 0
.

Since
\{\mathcal{M}(x^{\gamma_{k}},z^{\gamma_{k}})+u_{k}\}_{k}
converges to
f^{*}
and it holds that
u_{k},\|z^{\gamma_{k}}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, we conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}f(z^{\gamma_{k}})=f^{*}
. By
Assumption

2.2
, we have

\displaystyle|f(y_{1})-f(y_{2})|\leq\max\{\|\nabla f(y_{1})\|,\|\nabla f(y_{2})\|\}\cdot\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|+\tfrac{{\sf L}}{2}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2{\sf L}}\max\{\|\nabla f(y_{1})\|^{2},\|\nabla f(y_{2})\|^{2}\}+{\sf L}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2},\quad\text{for all}\;\;y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Substituting
y_{1}=x^{\gamma_{k}}
,
y_{2}=z^{\gamma_{k}}
in (
17
) and using
\|z^{\gamma_{k}}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|\to 0
, we obtain

|f(x^{\gamma_{k}})-f(z^{\gamma_{k}})|\leq\tfrac{1}{2{\sf L}}\max\{\|\nabla f(x^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2},\|\nabla f(z^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2}\}+{\sf L}\|x^{\gamma_{k}}-z^{\gamma_{k}}\|^{2}\to 0.

Furthermore, substituting
y_{1}=x^{\gamma_{k}}
,
y_{2}=x^{\ell}
in (
17
), it follows

\max_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}|f(x^{\ell})-f(x^{\gamma_{k}})|\leq\frac{1}{2{\sf L}}\max_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}\cup\{\gamma_{k}\}}\|\nabla f(x^{\ell})\|^{2}+{\sf L}\cdot\max_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}\|x^{\ell}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|^{2}\to 0,\;\;k\to\infty.

This implies
\max_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}|f(x^{\ell})-f^{*}|\leq|f(x^{\gamma_{k}})-f^{*}|+\max_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}|f(x^{\ell})-f(x^{\gamma_{k}})|\to 0
and thus, we have
f(x^{k})\to f^{*}
as
k\to\infty
. Noting
\|x^{k}-z^{k}\|\to 0
, we also conclude that
\lim_{k\to\infty}f(z^{k})=f^{*}
.
∎

4.2
Iterate Convergence under the Łojasiewicz Inequality

In this subsection, we establish iterate convergence results, i.e., the stochastic process
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
generated by
\mathsf{SGDM}
is shown to converge to a
\mathrm{crit}(f)
-valued mapping
\bm{x}^{*}:\Omega\to\mathrm{crit}(f)
almost surely. This type of convergence is typically interpreted as a
last-iterate convergence
result, previously studied in the (strongly) convex setting,
[
14
,
41
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose
Assumptions

2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
hold and let
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
be generated by
\mathsf{SGDM}
with
\lambda\in[0,1),\;\nu\geq 0
, and non-increasing step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}_{++}
satisfying:

{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}\Big({\sum}_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\Big)^{2r}<\infty\quad\text{for some $r>\frac{1}{2}$}.

(18)

Then, the event

\mathcal{I}:=\{\omega\in\Omega:{\lim}_{k\to\infty}\,\|\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)\|=\infty\;\text{or}\;\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)\to x^{*}\in\mathrm{crit}(f)\}
occurs w.p.
1
.

Remark 4.1
.

We continue with several remarks on
Theorem

4.2
.

•

No a priori boundedness.
The statement
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{I})=1
can be interpreted as follows: If the stochastic process
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
, generated by
\mathsf{SGDM}
, does not diverge to infinity a.s., then it must converge to some stationary points a.s.. The case
\|\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)\|\to\infty
can be ruled out if
\{\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)\}_{k}
has at least one accumulation point. Alternatively, we may define the event

\mathcal{X}:=\{\omega\in\Omega:{\liminf}_{k\to\infty}\;\|\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)\|<\infty\}.

The iterates
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
then converge a.s
.
on
\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, if
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{X})=1
, then
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
converges a.s
.
to some stationary points of
f
. The condition
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{X})=1
is significantly weaker than the a priori (a.s.) boundedness of
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
required in other Łojasiewicz inequality-based convergence analyses of stochastic methods,
[
44
,
25
]
.

•

Step sizes requirements.
Condition (
18
) appeared in a similar form in
[
44
, Corollary 2.2]
requiring
r>1
. (
18
) holds, e.g., for polynomial step sizes of the form
\alpha_{k}\sim k^{-\gamma}
,
\gamma\in(\frac{2}{3},1]
. Hence, under proper polynomial step sizes, iterate convergence of
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
is ensured by
Theorem

4.2
.

where the last inequality uses
{\sum}_{i=1}^{\gamma_{k}}\alpha_{i}\geq\sum_{i=K}^{k-1}\Delta_{\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i+1}}+{\sum}_{i=1}^{\gamma_{K}-1}\alpha_{i}\geq\delta{\sf T}(k-K)
. Hence, by the definition of
\{u_{k}\}_{k}
, we conclude that

{\sum}_{k=t}^{\infty}u_{k}^{\vartheta}\to 0\quad\text{as $t$ tends to infinity.}

(21)

Due to
x^{\ell_{k}}\to x^{*}
,
\max_{\ell\in\Gamma_{k}}\|x^{\ell}-x^{\gamma_{k}}\|\to 0
, and
\bigcup_{k}\Gamma_{k}=\mathbb{N}
, there is a subsequence of
\{x^{\gamma_{k}}\}_{k}
converging to
x^{*}
. Moreover, utilizing
Lemma

3.4
,
f(z^{k})\to f(x^{*})
,
\|x^{k}-z^{k}\|\to 0
, and
u_{k}\to 0
, it holds that
\mathcal{M}(x^{\gamma_{k}},z^{\gamma_{k}})+u_{k}\downarrow f(x^{*})
and
\Psi_{k}\downarrow 0
. Hence, for any given
\rho>0
with
\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x^{*})\subseteq U(x^{*})
, there is
t\geq K
such that
|\mathcal{M}(x^{\gamma_{t}},z^{\gamma_{t}})-f(x^{*})|<1
,
z^{\gamma_{k}},x^{\gamma_{k}}\in V(x^{*})
(cf
.
(
20
)), and

\max\{\|x^{\gamma_{t}}-x^{*}\|,\|z^{\gamma_{t}}-x^{*}\|\}+{\sf M}\Psi_{t}+3(1+2\nu){\sf C}{\sf T}{\sum}_{i=t}^{\infty}u_{i}^{\vartheta}<\rho,

(22)

where
{\sf M}:=\frac{150(1+2\nu)}{(1-\lambda)^{3}}
. We now show that the following statements hold for all
k\geq t
:

(a)

x^{\gamma_{k}},z^{\gamma_{k}}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x^{*})
and
|f(x^{\gamma_{k}})-f^{*}|<\min\{1,\eta\}
,
|f(z^{\gamma_{k}})-f^{*}|<\min\{1,\eta\}
.

(b)

\sum_{i=t}^{k}d_{i}\leq{\sf M}[\Psi_{t}-\Psi_{k+1}]+3(1+2\nu){\sf C}{\sf T}\sum_{i=t}^{k}u_{i}^{\vartheta}.

We prove these statements by induction. By
Lemma

4.3
and the definition of the index
t
, (a) and (b) hold for
k=t
. Assume there is
m>t
such that the statements (a) and (b) are valid for
k=m
. Let us consider
k=m+1
. From (
20
), it follows
\max\{|f(x^{\gamma_{m+1}})-f^{*}|,|f(z^{\gamma_{m+1}})-f^{*}|\}<\min\{1,\eta\}
. Next, we show that
x^{\gamma_{m+1}},z^{\gamma_{m+1}}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x^{*})
. Using the triangle inequality and statement (b) for
k=m
,

\displaystyle\|x^{\gamma_{m+1}}-x^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{\gamma_{m+1}}-x^{\gamma_{m}}\|+\|x^{\gamma_{m}}-x^{\gamma_{t}}\|+\|x^{\gamma_{t}}-x^{*}\|\leq\|x^{\gamma_{t}}-x^{*}\|+{\sum}_{i=t}^{m}d_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\|x^{\gamma_{t}}-x^{*}\|+{\sf M}[\Psi_{t}-\Psi_{m+1}]+3(1+2\nu){\sf C}{\sf T}{\sum}_{i=t}^{m}u_{i}^{\vartheta}<\rho,

where the last inequality follows from (
22
) and
\Psi_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\geq t
. Repeating this step for
z^{\gamma_{m+1}}
, we can also show
z^{\gamma_{m+1}}\in\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(x^{*})
. This proves (a) for
k={m+1}
, implying that
x^{\gamma_{m+1}},z^{\gamma_{m+1}}\in U(x^{*})
and
\max\{|f(x^{\gamma_{m+1}})-f^{*}|,|f(z^{\gamma_{m+1}})-f^{*}|\}<\min\{1,\eta\}
. Hence,
Lemma

4.3
is applicable for
k=m+1
, i.e., we have

d_{m+1}\leq{\sf M}[\Psi_{m+1}-\Psi_{m+2}]+3(1+2\nu){\sf C}{\sf T}u_{m+1}^{\vartheta}.

Combining this inequality with the bound in (b) for
k=m
, we can infer
{\sum}_{i=t}^{m+1}d_{i}\leq{\sf M}[\Psi_{t}-\Psi_{m+2}]+3(1+2\nu){\sf C}{\sf T}{\sum}_{i=t}^{m+1}u_{i}^{\vartheta}
, i.e.,
(b) is also valid for
k=m+1
.
Therefore, both (a) and (b) hold for all
k\geq t
. Statement (b) and (
22
) then imply

{\sum}_{k=t}^{\infty}d_{k}\leq{\sf M}\Psi_{t}+3(1+2\nu){\sf C}{\sf T}{\sum}_{i=t}^{\infty}u_{i}^{\vartheta}<\rho<\infty.

According to the definition (
9
) of
d_{k}
and following the discussions in
Section

3.1
, this summability condition ensures that
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
is a Cauchy sequence and hence, the whole sequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
converges to the stationary point
x^{*}
. Recalling
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})=1
, this result holds in an almost sure sense.
\square

This subsection presents the proof of
Corollary

5.1
. W.l.o.g., we assume

, i.e.,
\alpha_{k}=\alpha/k^{\gamma}
. Clearly, we have

Part (a).
When
\gamma\in(\frac{2}{3},1)

\varepsilon\in(0,3\gamma-2)
and set

. By the integral test, we have
\Delta_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}=\Theta(k^{1-\gamma})
. Since
r<\frac{2\gamma-1}{2(1-\gamma)}
, it follows that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}^{2}\Delta_{k}^{2r}=\mathcal{O}(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}1/k^{2\gamma-2(1-\gamma)r})<\infty
.
Hence,
Theorem

5.1
(a) is applicable. As a result,
\{\bm{x}^{k}\}_{k}
converges a.s. to some
\bm{x}^{*}:\Omega\to\mathrm{crit}(f)
on
\mathcal{X}
and there is
\mathcal{R}\in\mathcal{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R})=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{X})
such that the realizations
x^{k}\equiv\bm{x}^{k}(\omega)
,
x^{*}\equiv\bm{x}^{*}(\omega)
,
\omega\in\mathcal{R}
, satisfy

\max\{|f(x^{k})-f^{*}|,\|f(x^{k})\|^{2}\}=\mathcal{O}(1/k^{(1-\gamma)\psi(\theta)})\quad\text{and}\quad\|x^{k}-x^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(1/k^{(1-\gamma)\phi(\theta)}),

where
f^{*}:=f(x^{*})
,
\psi(\theta)=\min\{2r,\frac{1}{2\theta-1}\}
, and
\phi(\theta)=\min\{r-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}\}
. Next, we compare
(1-\gamma)\psi(\theta)
with
\Psi(\gamma,\theta)=\min\{2\gamma-1,\tfrac{1-\gamma}{2\theta-1}\}
. Noting
\tfrac{1-\gamma}{2\theta-1}\geq\Psi(\gamma,\theta)
and

2r(1-\gamma)=2\gamma-1-\varepsilon/2>\Psi(\gamma,\theta)-\varepsilon,\quad\text{we have}\quad(1-\gamma)\psi(\theta)>\Psi(\gamma,\theta)-\varepsilon.

and

Similarly, we can derive
\|x^{k}-x^{*}\|=o(1/k^{\Phi(\gamma,\theta)-\varepsilon})

(1-\gamma)\phi(\theta)>\Phi(\gamma,\theta)-\varepsilon
, where
\Phi(\gamma,\theta)=\min\{\frac{3}{2}\gamma-1,\tfrac{(1-\gamma)(1-\theta)}{2\theta-1}\}
.

Part (b).

\gamma=1

\varepsilon>0

{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\;\alpha_{k}^{2}\cdot g(\Delta_{k})^{2}\leq\alpha^{2-2p}\exp(1)\cdot{\sum}_{k=1}^{\infty}\;1/(k\cdot\log(k)^{2p})<\infty.

Theorem

5.1
(b) and
\frac{1}{g(\Delta_{k})}\leq\frac{\alpha^{p}[1+\log(k)]^{p}}{\sqrt{k}}=o({\log(k)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2}}}/{\sqrt{k}})

that

|f(y_{1})-f(y_{2})|\leq\tfrac{1}{2{\sf L}}\max\{\|\nabla f(y_{1})\|^{2},\|\nabla f(y_{2})\|^{2}\}+{\sf L}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}.

(57)

Substituting
y_{1}=x^{\gamma_{k}}
and
y_{2}=z^{\gamma_{k}}
in (
57
) and using (
51
), this yields

|f(x^{\gamma_{k}})-f(z^{\gamma_{k}})|\leq\tfrac{1}{2{\sf L}}\max\{\|\nabla f(x^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2},\|\nabla f(z^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2}\}+{\sf L}\|x^{\gamma_{k}}-z^{\gamma_{k}}\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(k^{-\psi(\theta)}),

which, along with (
56
), further implies

|f(x^{\gamma_{k}})-f^{*}|\leq|f(z^{\gamma_{k}})-f^{*}|+|f(x^{\gamma_{k}})-f(z^{\gamma_{k}})|=\mathcal{O}(k^{-\psi(\theta)}).

(58)

As discussed before, for every integer
\ell\geq 1
, there is
k\geq 1
such that
\ell\in\Gamma_{k}
. Replacing
y_{1}=x^{\ell}
and
y_{2}=x^{\gamma_{k}}
in (
57
), this yields

|f(x^{\ell})-f(x^{\gamma_{k}})|\leq\tfrac{1}{2{\sf L}}\max\{\|\nabla f(x^{\ell})\|^{2},\|\nabla f(x^{\gamma_{k}})\|^{2}\}+{\sf L}d_{k}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(k^{-\psi(\theta)}),

where we applied (
52
), (
53
). Combining this with (
58
) gives
|f(x^{\ell})-f^{*}|\leq|f(x^{\ell})-f(x^{\gamma_{k}})|+|f(x^{\gamma_{k}})-f^{*}|=\mathcal{O}(k^{-\psi(\theta)})
. Using (
54
) and
\Delta_{\ell}\leq\Delta_{\gamma_{k+1}}
, we have

|f(x^{\ell})-f^{*}|=\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{\ell}^{-\psi(\theta)}).

Step 5:

Rates for
\{x^{k}\}_{k}
.
In this step, we will work with the adjusted Łojasiewicz exponent
\vartheta\in[\theta,1)
such that
\vartheta>\frac{1}{2r}
(which ensures the summability of
\{u_{k}^{\vartheta}\}_{k}
). Summing the recursion in
Lemma

4.3
from
k=m
to
n
leads to

{\sum}_{k=m}^{n}d_{k}\leq{\sf M}\Psi_{m}+3(1+2\nu){\sf C}{\sf T}{\sum}_{k=m}^{n}u_{k}^{\vartheta},

where
\Psi_{k}=\frac{1}{{\sf C}(1-\vartheta)}[\mathcal{M}(x^{\gamma_{k}},z^{\gamma_{k}})-f(x^{*})+u_{k}]^{1-\vartheta}=\frac{1}{{\sf C}(1-\vartheta)}y_{k}^{1-\vartheta}=\mathcal{O}(k^{-(1-\vartheta)\psi(\vartheta)})
and
{\sf M}=\frac{150(1+2\nu)}{(1-\lambda)^{3}}
. Letting
n\to\infty
and noting
u_{k}=\mathcal{O}(k^{-2r})
(by (
48
)), we have

{\sum}_{k=m}^{\infty}d_{k}=\mathcal{O}\Big(m^{-(1-\vartheta)\psi(\vartheta)}+{\sum}_{k=m}^{\infty}k^{-2r\vartheta}\Big)=\mathcal{O}(m^{-(1-\vartheta)\psi(\vartheta)}+m^{1-2r\vartheta}).

(59)

Since
\vartheta>0
can be selected freely from
(\tfrac{1}{2r},1)\cap[\theta,1)
, to achieve the best rate given
r>\frac{1}{2}
and
\theta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, we solve the following constrained optimization problem:

{\max}_{\vartheta\in[\theta,1)}\;\varphi(\vartheta):=\min\{2r\vartheta-1,2r(1-\vartheta),\tfrac{1-\vartheta}{2\vartheta-1}\}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\vartheta>\tfrac{1}{2r}.

(60)

Observe that
\phi(\vartheta)=2r\vartheta-1
if
\frac{1}{2r}<\vartheta\leq\frac{1+2r}{4r}
and
\phi(\vartheta)=\frac{1-\vartheta}{2\vartheta-1}
if
\frac{1+2r}{4r}<\vartheta<1
. Clearly,
\varphi(\vartheta)
increases in
(\frac{1}{2r},\frac{1+2r}{4r})
and decreases in
(\frac{1+2r}{4r},1)
. Hence, in the case
\theta\geq\frac{1+2r}{4r}
,
\varphi
attains the maximum at
\theta
; in the case
\frac{1}{2}\leq\theta<\frac{1+2r}{4r}
, the maximum is attained at
\frac{1+2r}{4r}
. Consequently, the solution
\vartheta^{*}
of (
60
) is given by

\vartheta^{*}=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1+2r}{4r}&\text{if}\;\frac{1}{2}\leq\theta<\frac{1+2r}{4r},\\
\theta&\text{if}\;\frac{1+2r}{4r}\leq\theta<1,\end{cases}\quad\implies\quad\varphi(\vartheta^{*})=\begin{cases}r-\tfrac{1}{2}&\text{if}\;\frac{1}{2}\leq\theta<\frac{1+2r}{4r},\\
\tfrac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}&\text{if}\;\frac{1+2r}{4r}\leq\theta<1,\end{cases}

i.e.,
\varphi(\vartheta^{*})=\phi(\theta):=\min\{r-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}\}
, and (
59
) reduces to
{\sum}_{k=m}^{\infty}d_{k}=\mathcal{O}(m^{-\phi(\theta)})
.
Recalling
x^{k}\to x^{*}\in\mathrm{crit}(f)
, we have
x^{\gamma_{k}}\to x^{*}
as
k\to\infty
and

\|x^{\gamma_{m}}-x^{*}\|\leq{\sum}_{k=m}^{\infty}\|x^{\gamma_{k}}-x^{\gamma_{k+1}}\|\leq{\sum}_{k=m}^{\infty}d_{k}=\mathcal{O}(m^{-\phi(\theta)}).

Analogous to our previous steps, for any index
\ell\geq 1,
there is
m\geq 1
such that
\ell\in\Gamma_{m}
. Hence, it holds that

\|x^{\ell}-x^{*}\|\leq\|x^{\ell}-x^{\gamma_{m}}\|+\|x^{\gamma_{m}}-x^{*}\|\leq d_{m}+{\sum}_{k=m}^{\infty}d_{k}=\mathcal{O}(m^{-\phi(\theta)}).

Finally, the relation
\Delta_{\ell}\leq\Delta_{\gamma_{m+1}}\leq\bar{\sf D}m
(by (
54
)) yields
\|x^{\ell}-x^{*}\|=\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{\ell}^{-\phi(\theta)})
.

Part (b).

Rates under
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
,
g(x)=\frac{\exp(rx)}{x^{p}}
and
r>0
,
p\geq 0
.
Substituting
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
in (
43
) and noting that
g(x)=\frac{\exp(rx)}{x^{p}}
and
u_{k}=o(g(\Delta_{k})^{-2})
by (
44
), we have

y_{k+1}\leq(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})y_{k}+\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}^{2p}\cdot\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}),\quad\text{where}\quad{\sf C}_{\lambda}=\tfrac{{\sf C}^{2}{\sf T}}{200(1-\lambda)}.

(61)

If
{\sf C}_{\lambda}\geq 1
, we have
y_{k+1}\leq\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}^{2p}\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})=g(\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})^{-2}
. Next, we consider
{\sf C}_{\lambda}<1
.
Since
\alpha_{k}\to 0
, there is
\tilde{k}\geq 1
such that
\alpha_{k}\leq{\sf T}
for all
k\geq\tilde{k}
.
By
Lemma

3.1
, we further have for all
k\geq i\geq\max\{\bar{k},\tilde{k},K_{\delta}\}
that

\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}-\Delta_{\gamma_{i}}={\sum}_{j=\gamma_{i}+1}^{\gamma_{k}}\;\alpha_{j}\leq\alpha_{\gamma_{k}}+\Delta_{\gamma_{i},\gamma_{k}}\leq{\sf T}+{\sf T}(k-i).

(62)

Dividing (
61
) by
(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k+1}
gives

\frac{y_{k+1}}{(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k+1}}\leq\frac{y_{k}}{(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k}}+\frac{\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}^{2p}\cdot\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})}{(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k+1}}.

Summing this estimate for
i=t,\dots,k
,
t\geq\max\{\bar{k},\tilde{k},K_{\delta}\}
, and multiplying
(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k+1}
back on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle y_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k-t+1}y_{t}+{\sum}_{i=t}^{k}\Delta_{\gamma_{i}}^{2p}\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{i}})(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k-i}

\displaystyle\leq(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k-t+1}y_{t}+\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}^{2p}\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}){\sum}_{i=t}^{k}\exp(2r(\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}-\Delta_{\gamma_{i}}))(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k-i}

\displaystyle\leq(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})^{k-t+1}y_{t}+\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}^{2p}\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}){\sum}_{i=t}^{k}\exp(2r{\sf T})[\exp(2r{\sf T})(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})]^{k-i},

where the second line uses
\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}\geq\Delta_{\gamma_{i}}
and the last line is due to (
62
). The condition
r<{\sf C}^{2}/400
implies
\exp(2r{\sf T})(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})<1
and hence, we have
(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})<\exp(-2r{\sf T})
and
{\sum}_{i=t}^{k}\exp(2r{\sf T})[\exp(2r{\sf T})(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})]^{k-i}\leq\exp(2r{\sf T})/[1-\exp(2r{\sf T})(1-{\sf C}_{\lambda})]=:\bar{\sf C}_{\lambda}
. Thus, due to (
62
), it follows

\displaystyle y_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-2r{\sf T}(k-t+1))y_{t}+\bar{\sf C}_{\lambda}g(\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})\cdot\exp(2r\Delta_{\gamma_{t}})y_{t}+\bar{\sf C}_{\lambda}g(\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})^{-2}.

Since
\exp(2r\Delta_{\gamma_{t}})y_{t}
and
\bar{\sf C}_{\lambda}
are fixed, this yields

y_{k+1}=\mathcal{O}(g(\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})^{-2})
. By (
62
), we have
\Delta_{\gamma_{k+1}}-\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}\leq 2{\sf T}
, which implies
\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})\leq\exp(4r{\sf T})\exp(-2r\Delta_{\gamma_{k+1}})
. Consequently, due to the monotonicity of
\{\Delta_{\gamma_{k}}\}_{k}
, we can infer

y_{k+1}=\mathcal{O}(g(\Delta_{\gamma_{k+1}})^{-2})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_{k}=\mathcal{O}(g(\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})^{-2}).

(63)

Based on (
63
) and
u_{k}=o(g(\Delta_{\gamma_{k}})^{-2})

. We will omit further details.

Valuations and nonzero torsion in module of differentials
Sarasij Maitra, Vivek Mukundan

Abstract
. Let ( R , 𝔪 R , 𝕜 ) (R,\mathfrak{m}_{R},\mathbb{k}) be a one-dimensional complete local reduced 𝕜 \mathbb{k} -algebra over a field of characteristic zero. Berger conjectured that R R is regular if and only if the universally finite module of differentials Ω R \Omega_{R} is torsion free. When R R is a domain, we prove the conjecture in several cases. Our techniques are primarily reliant on making use of the valuation semi-group of R R . First, we establish a method of verifying the conjecture by analyzing the valuation semi-group of R R and orders of units of the integral closure of R R . We also prove the conjecture in the case when certain monomials are missing from the monomial support of the defining ideal of R R . These monomials are based on the smallest power of 𝔪 R \mathfrak{m}_{R} that is contained within the conductor ideal. This also generalizes a previous result of [ CnGW98 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a (affine or projective) curve over a field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero. The classification of circumstances when
X
is smooth is of importance to both algebraists and geometers. Though there are other smoothness characterizations, the one involving the differential sheaf
\Omega_{X,x}
, where
x
is a closed point on
X
, has eluded researchers. Consider the completion of the stalk of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
at
x
and denote this by
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}
. R.W. Berger conjectured that
X
is smooth at
x
if and only if the sheaf of differentials
\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}\otimes\Omega_{X,x}
is torsion free.

Let
R=\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}\cong\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket/I
with
I\subseteq(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{2}
. For a curve, the differential sheaf mentioned above is
R\otimes\Omega_{X,x}
where
\Omega_{X,x}
is the module of Kähler differentials and
R\otimes\Omega_{X,x}
is the universally finite module of differentials, henceforth denoted by
\Omega_{R}
.
For the purposes of this article, we can define the universally finite module of differentials
\Omega_{R}
to be the module whose presentation is as follows:

R^{\mu(I)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{J}}}{{\to}}R^{n}\to\Omega_{R}\to 0

where
\mu(I)
is the minimal set of generators of
I
and
\mathcal{J}
is the Jacobian matrix on a generating set for
I
, considered as a matrix over
R
. For further details, we refer the reader to the excellent resource
[
Kun86
]
.
One of the first pieces of evidence on the smoothness of the curve
X
via the torsion freeness of its differential sheaf was studied by E. Kunz (
[
Kun61
]
) when the ring is of characteristic
p
and the base field is imperfect. Berger (
[
Ber63
]
) in 1963 conjectured the following for perfect fields:

Conjecture A

(R. W. Berger
[
Ber63
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a perfect field and let
R
be a reduced one-dimensional analytic
\mathbb{k}
-algebra. Then
R
is regular if and only if the torsion submodule
\displaystyle\tau(\Omega_{R})
of
\Omega_{R}
vanishes.

It follows from the above presentation that if
R
is regular (i.e.,
n=1
), then
\Omega_{R}
is free. Hence it is the converse direction that is of interest. Thus we assume
n\geqslant 2
throughout this article.

A direct generalization of this conjecture for higher dimensional surfaces does not hold (see for example
[
Mat67
, Example Pg 160]
,
[
Sch70
, Satz 9.4]
). But with added constraints on
R
(such as Cohen-Macaulayness of
R
), a generalized version does exist due to R. Hübl and D. Arapura independently (
[
H9̈0
,
Ara89
]
).
There have been numerous instances in which
A
has been established by a variety of different approaches (see
[
Sch70
]
,
[
HK71
]
,
[
Her78
]

[
BG80
]
,
[
Ulr81
]

[
Koc83
]
,
[
HW84
]
,
[
HW86
]
,
[
Ber88
]
,
[
Poh89
]
,
[
Güt90
]
,
[
Iso91
]
,
[

]
,
[
CnGW98
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
).
An excellent exposition on the earlier ones among the above listed results can be found in
[
Ber94
]
. An Artinian variant of the conjecture, named the Artin-Berger conjecture, was explored in
[
CnGW98
]
recovering some of the earlier cases.

Recently, the authors of this article along with C. Huneke
[
HMM21
]
demonstrated a new approach for explicitly constructing torsion in
\Omega_{R}
, where
R
is assumed to be a domain with residue field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic
0
and embedding dimension
n
. In that article, we provided a strategy for first constructing an over-ring
S
such that
R\subseteq S\subseteq\overline{R}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
, and embedding dimension of
S
is
n+s
. Here
\overline{R}
denotes the integral closure of
R
in its field of fractions
\operatorname{Quot(R)}
. We then demonstrated that constructing “sufficiently many” torsion elements in
\Omega_{S}
enables us to pull back a torsion element to
\Omega_{R}
. In
Proposition

3.3
of this article, we show that

\lambda(\Omega_{S})\geqslant ns+{s\choose 2}

(where
\lambda(M)
denotes the length of a module
M
), which is one less than the required number of torsion elements meeting the above sufficiency criteria. This settles
[
HMM21
, Remark 4.10]
.

Identifying
R
within
\overline{R}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
allows us to write
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
where
\alpha_{i}
’s are units in
\overline{R}
. The valuation semi-group of
R
, namely
v(R)
, is critical for our methods throughout this article.
In fact, the precise valuations
a_{i}
and the valuation of the conductor ideal,
\mathfrak{C}_{R}=R:_{\text{Quot}(R)}\overline{R}
, are critical in our work of constructing torsion elements. Moreover, the “order” of the unit
\alpha_{d}
in the description of
x_{d}=\alpha_{d}t^{a_{d}}
also plays a role. Given
\alpha_{d}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u_{di}t^{i}
, a unit in
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
, its order is defined to be

o(\alpha_{d})=v(\alpha_{d}-u_{d0})=\min_{i\geqslant 1}\{i~|~u_{ji}\neq 0\}.

We state our first result here which is quite technical, but we illustrate with examples that the conditions are often satisfied (see
Theorem

4.4
and
Theorem

4.6
).

Theorem B
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket\subseteq\overline{R}=k[[t]]
be as above with
\mathfrak{C}_{R}=(t^{c_{R}})\overline{R}
and
\mathbb{k}
, an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
Then
\tau(\Omega_{R})
is nonzero in the following cases:

(1)

the valuations
a_{n},a_{n-1}
satisfy
a_{n}+a_{n-1}\geqslant c_{R}
.

(2)

there exist
i,d
such that
\displaystyle o(\alpha_{d})+a_{i}\geqslant c_{R}
,
\displaystyle o(\alpha_{d})<\infty
and any one of the following statements holds.

(a)

o(\alpha_{d})\leqslant o(\alpha_{i})<\infty
,

(b)

d\geqslant i
.

It is possible to get hold of a torsion element by mapping
\Omega_{R}
to a rank one torsion free module (see
Section

2.2
). However, proving that it is non-zero is the most challenging part. Our discussion in
Proposition

4.7
provides an approach towards achieving this goal, by going modulo a monomial ideal. We implement this method and obtain our second main result. This is the broadest class where we establish the truth of Berger’s Conjecture (see
Theorem

4.9
).

Theorem C
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket/I
be a one-dimensional local domain where
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed of characteristic zero and
I\subseteq(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{2}
. Let
x_{i}
denote the image of
X_{i}
in
R
and let the conductor be
\mathfrak{C}_{R}
.
Suppose
\mathfrak{m}^{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{C}_{R}
and
x_{1}^{N-1},x_{1}^{N-2}x_{2}
,
N\geqslant 2
, are not in the monomial support ideal of
I
. Then the torsion submodule
\tau(\Omega_{R})
is nonzero.

The monomial support ideal of
I
is the same as
\operatorname{Mono}(I)
appearing in
[
PUV07
]
. The theorem recovers the main theorem of
[
CnGW98
]
. We also present other interesting examples that satisfy the hypothesis of the above theorem.

This raises the question if condition (2) in
C
can be relaxed. So far our methods have been unable to resolve this. Getting a complete answer even in the case when
\mathfrak{C}_{R}=\mathfrak{m}^{N}
for some large enough
N
would be interesting. The methods used in this article are innovative, in that, they explicitly compute torsion elements in
\Omega_{R}
, whereas most of the previous approaches to the
A
have been unable to do so.

2.
Setting and Preliminaries

Throughout this article we will assume that
\mathbb{k}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
, and
(R,\mathfrak{m}_{R},\mathbb{k})
is a one-dimensional complete equicharacteristic local
\mathbb{k}
-algebra which is a domain with embedding dimension
n
(
\mu_{R}(\mathfrak{m}_{R})=n
). Choosing
t
to be a uniformizing parameter of the integral closure
\overline{R}
of
R
, we may assume that
\overline{R}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
. As a result, we may write
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
where
\alpha_{i}
’s are units in
\overline{R}
and
a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{n}
. Using the technique as in the first paragraph of the proof of
[
HMM21
, Theorem 3.1]
, we can alternatively write
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{a_{1}},\alpha_{2}t^{a_{2}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
. We briefly explain the process. First we multiply by a nonzero element of
\mathbb{k}
to assume that the constant term of
\alpha_{1}
is
1
. Let
s=\beta t
where
\beta^{a_{1}}=\alpha_{1}
. Such a
\beta
exists due to Hensel’s Lemma
[
Eis95
, THeorem 7.3]
. The characterisitic of
\mathbb{k}
being zero is important here. Notice that under this change of variables,
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{s}\rrbracket
and
s=\beta t=t+\beta_{1}t^{2}+\beta_{2}t^{3}+\cdots
where
\beta=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\beta_{i}t^{i}
. Notice that
s^{a_{1}}=(\beta t)^{a_{1}}=\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}}
. Also
\alpha_{i}t^{a_{i}}=\alpha_{i}^{\prime}s^{a_{i}}
. Then
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket\cong\mathbb{k}\llbracket{s^{a_{1}},\alpha_{2}^{\prime}s^{a_{2}},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{\prime}s^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
.

We also define the valuation
v
on
\overline{R}
given by
v(p(t))=a
if
p(t)=t^{a}\alpha
where
\alpha
is a unit in
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
(also called
\operatorname{ord}(-)
, see for example
[
SH06
, Example 6.7.5]
). Let
v(R)
denote the valuation semi-group of
R
.

The conductor
\mathfrak{C}_{R}
is the largest common ideal of
R
and its
integral closure,
\overline{R}
. Since
\displaystyle\overline{R}=k\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
, we have that
\mathfrak{C}_{R}=(t^{i})_{i\geqslant c_{R}}\overline{R}
where
c_{R}
is the least integer such that
t^{c_{R}-1}\not\in R
, and
t^{c_{R}+i}\in R
for all
i\geqslant 0
. The number
c_{R}
is characterized as the least valuation in
\mathfrak{C}_{R}
. It is clear from this discussion that there cannot be any element
r\in R
, such that
v(r)=c_{R}-1
. Since
\displaystyle\overline{R}
is finitely generated over
R
(
[
SH06
, Theorem 4.3.4]
),
\mathfrak{C}_{R}\neq 0
, and it is never all of
R
unless
R
is regular.

We define an epimorphism

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow R

\displaystyle\Phi(X_{i})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}t^{a_{i}}\text{ for }1\leqslant i\leqslant n.

We denote the kernel of
\Phi
by
I=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})
and hence have the natural isomorphism
R\cong\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket/I
. Since
\operatorname{edim}R=n
,
I
is contained in
\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}
where
\mathfrak{m}_{R}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
, the maximal ideal of
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket
. Such rings are called
analytic
\mathbb{k}
-algebras
. We will interchangeably use
\alpha_{i}t^{a_{i}}
for
x_{i}
, the images of
X_{i}
in the quotient
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket/I
.

2.1.
Universally Finite Module of Differentials

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be an analytic one-dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-algebra as above, which is a domain. Let
I=(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})
where
f_{j}\in P=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}}\rrbracket,n\geqslant 2
. We assume that
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{P}^{2}
where
\mathfrak{m}_{P}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. Then the
universally finite module of differentials over
\mathbb{k}
, denoted by
\Omega_{R}
, has a (minimal) presentation given as follows:

R^{m}\xrightarrow{\left[\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right]}R^{n}\to\Omega_{R}\to 0

where
\left[\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right]
is the Jacobian matrix of
I
, with entries in
R
.

We refer the reader to the excellent resource
[
Kun86
]
for more information. Let
\displaystyle\tau(\Omega_{R})
denote the torsion submodule of
\Omega_{R}
.

2.2.
Computing Torsion

It is well-known that
\operatorname{rank}_{R}(\Omega_{R})=\operatorname{rank}_{R}(\Omega_{\overline{R}})=1
(
R
and
\bar{R}
have the same fraction field) and
\Omega_{\overline{R}}
is torsion-free. Let
f:\Omega_{R}\to\Omega_{\overline{R}}
be the natural map induced by the inclusion
R\hookrightarrow\overline{R}
. Recall that the torsion submodule of
\Omega_{R}
is the kernel of the homomorphism
\Omega_{R}\rightarrow\Omega_{R}\otimes Q
where
Q=\text{Quot}(R)
. Since
\Omega_{\overline{R}}
is torsion-free, we have
\Omega_{\overline{R}}\hookrightarrow\Omega_{R}\otimes Q
. Thus
\ker f
is the torsion submodule of
\Omega_{R}
and hence
\tau(\Omega_{R})=\ker f
. The map
f
acts as follows:

f(dx_{i})=\frac{dx_{i}}{dt}dt.

Since
\Omega_{\overline{R}}
is isomorphic to
\overline{R}
,
\Omega_{R}
surjects to an
R
-submodule
\sum_{i=1}^{n}R\frac{dx_{i}}{dt}
of
\overline{R}=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
.
Thus the torsion submodule,
\tau(\Omega_{R})
, consists of the column vectors
\begin{bmatrix}r_{1}\ldots r_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{t}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\frac{dx_{i}}{dt}=0
. Evidently,
\tau(\Omega_{R})
is nonzero precisely when the tuples
\begin{bmatrix}r_{1}\ldots r_{n}\end{bmatrix}^{t}
are not in the image of the presentation matrix (Jacobian matrix of
I
) of
\Omega_{R}
, all entries written in terms of the uniformizing parameter
t
.

One of the main issues while constructing torsion is to check whether the column vectors
[r_{1},\dots,r_{n}]^{t}
is nonzero in
\Omega_{R}
. One technique is to show that this column vector is nonzero in
\Omega_{R/J}
for some suitable ideal
J
in
R
which will then imply that it is nonzero in
\Omega_{R}
. Though we have a more general theorem for this purpose in
Proposition

4.7
, a version which we use in some of the results in the results in the sequel is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
CnGW98
, Proposition 2.6]

Suppose
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{R}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then the torsion
\tau(\Omega_{R/\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}})
has
{n\choose 2}

\mathbb{k}
-linearly independent torsion elements
\overline{x_{i}}d\overline{x_{j}}
for
i<j
. Here
\overline{x_{i}}
denotes the image of
x_{i}
in
R/\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}
.

We often avoid using the
\overline{\cdot}
for notational convenience.

2.3.
Quasi-homogeneous rings

These rings form an important class which satisfies the Berger conjecture. A ring
R
is said to be quasi-homogeneous, if there is a surjection
\Omega_{R}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{m}_{R}
. Kunz and Ruppert in
[
KR77
]
showed that such rings are completions of graded rings.
Scheja (
[
Sch70
]
) showed that the torsion submodule of the module of differentials of such rings are always nonzero. In fact, he showed that
\mu(\tau(\Omega_{R}))\geqslant{\operatorname{edim}R\choose 2}
when
R
is quasi-homogeneous.

2.4.
The extension
R[\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{R}}{x_{1}}]

The basic facts about the finite birational extension
S=R[\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{R}}{x_{1}}]
of
R
are discussed in detail in
[
HMM21
, Section 4]
.

We write
\mathfrak{C}_{R}/{x_{1}}
to denote the set of elements of the form
{c}/{x_{1}}
where
c\in\mathfrak{C}_{R}
. Using
[
HK71
, Proposition 2.9]
and
[
HMM21
, Lemma 4.1]
, we get that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{R}/x_{1}
is generated as an
R
-submodule of
\overline{R}
(in fact, it is the ideal
(t^{c-a_{1}})\overline{R}
) by
t^{b_{i}}
with
b_{1}<\cdots<b_{s}
where
b_{i}\in[c-a_{1},c-1]\setminus v(R)
. For further details, see
[
HMM21
, Notation 4.2]
. It turns out that
\mathfrak{C}_{R}/x_{1}
is the conductor of the ring
S
. In other words the conductor
\mathfrak{C}_{S}
equals the ideal
(t^{c-a_{1}})\overline{R}
. A complete description of the ring
S
is as follows:

Theorem 2.3
.

[
HMM21
, Thoerem 4.6]

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{R}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}
. Set
P=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},...,X_{n}}\rrbracket
. Choose
S,b_{1},\dots,b_{s}
as described above. Then there exists a presentation of
S
as follows:

S=R\Big[\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{R}}{x_{1}}\Big]=\frac{\mathbb{k}\llbracket{X_{1},\dots,X_{n},T_{1},\dots,T_{s}}\rrbracket}{\ker\Phi+\left(X_{i}T_{j}-g_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}),T_{k}T_{l}-h_{kl}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\right)_{{1\leqslant i\leqslant n,1\leqslant j\leqslant s\atop 1\leqslant k\leqslant l\leqslant s}}}

where

g_{ij}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}),h_{kl}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})^{2}P
for all
i,j,k,l
.

Moreover,
g_{ij}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),h_{kl}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathfrak{C}_{R}
. In particular, when
\mathfrak{C}_{R}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}
, it follows that
\operatorname{edim}S=n+s
.

3.
Finer computations of Torsion elements in
\Omega_{S}

One of the main results in this section is the existence of torsion elements in
\Omega_{S}
. We show that we can in fact construct
ns+{s\choose 2}

\mathbb{k}
-linearly independent torsion elements in
\Omega_{S}
which completes
[
HMM21
, Remark 4.10]
.

The following theorem establishes that once
ns+{s\choose 2}+1
torsion elements are constructed with entries in
\mathfrak{m}_{S}
, then we can pull back a nonzero torsion element from
\Omega_{S}
to
\Omega_{R}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
HMM21
, Theorem 4.9]

Let
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{\alpha_{1}t^{a_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
with conductor
\mathfrak{C}_{R}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{R}^{2}
. Construct
S=R\left[\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{R}}{x_{1}}\right]=R[T_{1},\dots,T_{s}]
as in
Theorem

2.3
. Let
\tau(\Omega_{R}),\tau(\Omega_{S})
represent the torsion submodules of
\Omega_{R},\Omega_{S}
respectively. If
\lambda(\tau(\Omega_{S}))\geqslant ns+{s\choose 2}+1
and all these torsion elements have non-units in the last
s
rows (corresponding to
dT_{1},\dots,dT_{s}
), then a
\mathbb{k}
-linear combination of these torsion elements can be pulled back to a nonzero torsion element in
\tau(\Omega_{R})
. In particular,
\tau(\Omega_{R})\neq 0
.

Remark 3.2
.

An obvious consequence of this theorem is that if
\lambda(\tau(\Omega_{S}))\geqslant ns+{s\choose 2}+k,k\geqslant 1
and all these torsions have non-units in the last
s
rows, then we can pull back
k
torsion elements to
\Omega_{R}
, each of which are
\mathbb{k}
-linear combinations of the

torsion elements. Notice that these
k
torsion elements are
\mathbb{k}
-linearly independent in
\Omega_{R}
as they are
\mathbb{k}
-linearly independent in
\Omega_{S}
. Thus
\lambda(\tau(\Omega_{R}))\geqslant k
.

It is easy to see that we can construct
{s\choose 2}

\Gamma_{ij}:=b_{j}T_{j}dT_{i}-b_{i}T_{i}dT_{j}
. This can be clearly seen as

in

. Now

If

is a monomial

to begin with, then we can construct the torsion element

. This is clearly nonzero in
\Omega_{S}
. But often

are not of this form and hence the need for the following theorem.

4.

In this section, we provide partial answers to Berger’s conjecture by carefully observing the value semi-group of

as well as order of the units

\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket

We begin by stating a useful test of checking the nonvanishing of torsion in
\Omega_{R}
. First notice that units in
\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
are power series of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u_{i}t^{i}

Notation 4.1
.

Let
\alpha_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u_{ji}t^{i}\in\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t}\rrbracket
be a unit. We denote

\displaystyle o(\alpha_{j})=v(\alpha_{j}-u_{j0})=\min_{i\geqslant 1}\{i~|~u_{ji}\neq 0\},

where

denotes the order valuation (also called
\operatorname{ord}(-)
, see for example
[
SH06
, Example 6.7.5]
). If
\alpha_{j}=u_{j0}\in\mathbb{k}
, then notice that
o(\alpha_{j})=\infty
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let the conductor of
R
be
\mathfrak{C}_{R}=(t^{c_{R}})\overline{R}
.
Suppose there exists
i,d
such that
\displaystyle o(\alpha_{d})+a_{i}\geqslant c_{R}
and
\displaystyle o(\alpha_{d})<\infty
. Then the torsion
\tau(\Omega_{R})
is nonzero if any one of the following statements holds.

(1)

o(\alpha_{d})\leqslant o(\alpha_{i})<\infty
,

(2)

d\geqslant i
.

Proof.

As in
[
HMM21
, Theorem 3.1]
, we write
R=\mathbb{k}\llbracket{t^{a_{1}},\alpha_{2}t^{a_{2}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
with conductor
\mathfrak{C}_{R}=(t^{c_{R}})\overline{R}
.
Notice that the
o(\alpha_{i})
remain the same by applying the above change of variables. For
(1)
, observe that
o(\alpha_{i})+a_{i}\geqslant o(\alpha_{d})+a_{i}\geqslant c_{R}
. Now

\displaystyle x_{i}=\alpha_{i}t^{a_{i}}=\alpha_{i0}t^{a_{i}}+t^{o(\alpha_{i})+a_{i}}b

where
b\in\overline{R}
. Since
o(\alpha_{i})+a_{i}\geqslant c_{R}
,
t^{o(\alpha_{i})+a_{i}}b\in\mathfrak{C}_{R}\subseteq R
. Thus,
t^{a_{i}}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{i0}}(x_{i}-t^{o(\alpha_{i})+a_{i}}b)\in R
. Thus, we get that
R=k\llbracket{t^{a_{1}},\alpha_{2}t^{a_{2}},\dots,t^{a_{i}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
. The proof is now complete using
[
HMM21
, Remark 3.3]
.

For
(2)
, first observe that by our setup,
o(\alpha_{d})+a_{d}\geqslant o(\alpha_{d})+a_{i}\geqslant c_{R}
. Apply the same argument as above to see that
R=k\llbracket{t^{a_{1}},\alpha_{2}t^{a_{2}},\dots,t^{a_{d}},\dots,\alpha_{n}t^{a_{n}}}\rrbracket
. This finishes the proof.

∎

Corollary 4.11

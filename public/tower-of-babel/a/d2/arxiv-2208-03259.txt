The spin Gromov–Witten/Hurwitz correspondence for P 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}
A. Giacchetto, R. Kramer, D. Lewański, A. Sauvaget

Abstract
. We study the spin Gromov–Witten (GW) theory of P 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} . Using the standard torus action on P 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} , we prove that the associated equivariant potential can be expressed by means of operator formalism and satisfies the 2-BKP hierarchy. As a consequence of this result, we prove the spin analogue of the GW/Hurwitz correspondence of Okounkov–Pandharipande for P 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} , which was conjectured by J. Lee. Finally, we prove that this correspondence for a general target spin curve follows from a conjectural degeneration formula for spin GW invariants that holds in virtual dimension 0.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Gromov–Witten theory of spin curves

The Gromov–Witten (GW) theory of Kähler surfaces has seen significant development over the last twenty years. Initially focused on rational and ruled surfaces of geometric genus
p_{g}=0
, it later extended to surfaces with positive
p_{g}
, a class that includes Calabi–Yau surfaces, most elliptic surfaces and most surfaces of general type
[
IP04
,
LP07
,
KL07
,
KL11
,
KL11a
,
Lee13
]
.

Let
X
be such an algebraic surface with a homology class
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, and a holomorphic
2
-form
\eta
. If the zero locus
B
of
\eta
is smooth and connected, then
(B,N_{B/X})
is a
spin curve
, i.e. a pair consisting of a curve and a line bundle
\vartheta
such that
\vartheta^{2}\cong\omega_{B}
(a theta characteristic). One of the striking ideas that emerged is the reduction of the GW theory of surfaces of positive geometric genus to the GW theory of a spin curve associated to it via the so-called localisation by cosections that we now recall (see
[
KL07
,
LP07
]
for surfaces with smooth canonical divisor, and
[
KL11a
, conjecture 1.2]
for a lift of that assumption).

The 2-form
\eta
induces a morphism from the obstruction sheaf of the moduli of stable maps to
X
to its structure sheaf (a cosection)
[
KL13
]
:

(1.1)

\sigma_{\eta}\colon\mathcal{O}b_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)}.

The locus
Z(\sigma_{\eta})
where
\sigma_{\eta}
is not surjective is the locus of stable maps to
X
whose image lies in the degeneracy locus of
\eta
. This locus is empty if
\beta
is not a multiple of the canonical class. Applying the general theory of localisation by cosections, Kiem and Li showed that the virtual fundamental cycle of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
can be represented as a cycle supported on
Z(\sigma_{\eta})
. In other words, they constructed a
localised
virtual cycle on the locus
Z(\sigma_{\eta})
such that

(1.2)

\iota_{*}\bigl[Z(\sigma_{\eta})\bigr]^{\textup{loc},\eta}=\bigl[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\bigr]^{\textup{vir}},

where
\iota\colon Z(\sigma_{\eta})\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is the embedding morphism. In particular, the GW invariants of
X
vanish unless
\beta=d\cdot K_{X}
. This approach is an algebro-geometric analogue of what was first discovered by Lee and Parker in symplectic geometry
[
LP07
]
.

If the vanishing locus of
\eta
is smooth and
\beta=d\cdot K_{X}
, then we have the canonical identification:

(1.3)

Z(\sigma_{\eta})\simeq\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(B,d),

and we write
\bigl[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(B,d)\bigr]^{\textup{loc},\vartheta}=\bigl[Z(\sigma_{\eta})\bigr]^{\textup{loc},\eta}
, where
\vartheta=N_{B/X}
. If
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,d[B])
is proper, then the GW theory of
X
can be reduced to the localised GW theory of
B
in the following way:

(1.4)

\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,d[B])]^{{\rm vir}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}\ev_{i}^{\ast}({\gamma_{i}})=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(B,d)]^{{\rm loc},\vartheta}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}\ev_{i}^{\ast}(\gamma_{i}\cdot[B]).

Here, each
k_{i}
is a non-negative integer and the
\gamma_{i}
are classes in
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})
(see
[
KL13
]
). The cycle
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(B,d)]^{{\rm loc},\vartheta}
only depends on the spin curve, therefore we refer to the invariants in the right-hand side of
equation

1.4
as the
spin GW invariants
of
(B,\vartheta)
. The deformation invariance proved in
[
KL13
]
implies that these invariants only depend on the genus of
B
and the
parity
(or Arf invariant) of
\vartheta
, i.e. the parity of
h^{0}(B,\vartheta)
.

The structure of these invariants has been a topic of research since the result of Lee–Parker
[
LP07
]
, and first conjectures were made by Maulik–Pandharipande
[
MP08
]
. A recurring idea since these pioneering works, is that they should have a similar structure to the classical GW invariants of curves as described by Okounkov–Pandharipande in their trilogy
[
OP06
,
OP06a
,
OP06b
]
. The most concrete proposal in this direction is Lee’s conjecture, which states that spin (
stationary
) GW invariants may be recovered by so-called spin Hurwitz numbers (see
[
Lee20
]
and
B
below). However, several steps in the general program of Okounkov–Pandharipande are significantly more difficult for spin curves, as we will indicate in the rest of this introduction.

1.2.
Equivariant spin Gromov–Witten theory of
\mathbb{P}^{1}

In general, one cannot relate the localised and the classical virtual cycles. However, for the special case
(B,\vartheta)=(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1))
, the following relation holds in
A_{g-1+d+n}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d))
(see
[
KL11
, corollary 2.4]

(1.5)

\bigl[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)\bigr]^{{\rm loc},\mathcal{O}(-1)}=c_{g-1+d}\left(R^{1}\pi_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(-1)\right)\cdot\bigl[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)\bigr]^{\rm vir},

where
\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)
is the universal curve and
f\colon\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the universal morphism. We exploit this relation and the standard torus action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, to compute the spin GW theory of
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1))
using the localisation formula of Graber–Pandharipande
[
GP99
]

(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1))

(1.6)

\Braket{\tau_{k_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}(\gamma_{n})}_{g,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1)}\coloneqq\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)]^{{\rm loc},\mathcal{O}(-1)}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i}^{k_{i}}\ev_{i}^{\ast}(\gamma_{i})

with
\gamma_{i}\in H^{*}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
. Define the equivariant tau-function by:

(1.7)

\tau(x,x^{\star};u,q)\coloneqq\sum_{g,d}(-8u)^{g-1}(-4q)^{d}\Braket{\exp\left(2\sum_{k\geq 0}\bigl(x_{k}\tau_{k}(\bm{0})+x_{k}^{\star}\tau_{k}(\bm{\infty})\bigr)\right)}_{g,d}^{\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1)}

where
\bm{0}
and
\bm{\infty}
are the the equivariant Poincaré duals of
0
and
\infty
. Our main result is an expression of this function in the operator formalism.

Theorem A
.

The equivariant tau-function of
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1))
can be represented in the neutral fermion Fock space as a single vacuum expectation value:

(1.8)

\tau(x,x^{\star};u,q)=\Braket{e^{\sum_{i}x_{i}\mathsf{B}_{i}}e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{q}{u}\Bigr)^{H}e^{\alpha^{B}_{-1}}e^{\sum_{j}x_{j}^{\star}\mathsf{B}^{\star}_{j}}}.

The formalism of the neutral fermion Fock space will be recalled in
section

2.1
, and the definition of the operators involved in this theorem are defined in
equation

2.9
and
equation

4.15
.

The classes
\bm{0}
and
\bm{\infty}
form a basis of the equivariant cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as a module over the equivariant cohomology of the point. Therefore, after taking the non-equivariant limit of
\tau
, this theorem allows us to compute the full spin GW theory of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Remark 1.1
.

The spin GW theory of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
plays a distinguished role in the GW theory of surfaces. Indeed, if
\tilde{X}\to X
is a blow-up at a smooth point of
X
, then the spin GW invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
are the classical GW invariants of
\tilde{X}
with the curve class given by a multiple of the exceptional divisor. Therefore,
A
provides the first case of an all-genera expression of a GW potential of a target of dimension greater than
1
.

1.3.
Spin GW/H correspondence

The proof of
A
involves an alternative enumerative theory associated to a spin curve.
Spin Hurwitz numbers
enumerate branched covers of a base Riemann surface endowed with a spin structure. Each cover is weighted by a sign determined by its parity
[
EOP08
]
. More precisely, if
f\colon C\to B
is a branched cover with odd ramifications over a spin curve
(B,\vartheta)
, and therefore even ramification divisor
R_{f}

(1.9)

N_{f,\vartheta}\coloneqq f^{\ast}\vartheta\otimes\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{1}{2}R_{f})

is a well-defined spin structure on
C
. Spin Hurwitz numbers are then defined as

(1.10)

H_{d}(B,\vartheta;\mu^{1},\dotsc,\mu^{n})\coloneqq\sum_{[f\colon C\to B]}\frac{(-1)^{p(N_{f,\vartheta})}}{|\mathord{\mathrm{Aut}}(f)|},\qquad p(N_{f,\vartheta})\equiv h^{0}(C,N_{f,\vartheta})\pmod{2},

where the ramification locus of
f
is given by the ramification profiles
\mu^{i}
—odd partitions of the degree
d
—over
n
points fixed on the base. Like spin GW invariants, these numbers only depend on the genus of
B
and the parity of
\vartheta
(see e.g.
[
Gun16
]
).

1.4.
Algorithmic computation, closed formulae, and 2-BKP

As an application, our methods provide an algorithm to explicitly compute any spin GW invariant of the Riemann sphere. The first closed formulae in this direction, for disconnected invariants without degree-zero components (denoted here with a
\emptyset

[

\Braket{\tau_{k_{1}}\cdots\tau_{k_{n}}}_{\emptyset,g,d}^{\bullet,\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1)}=\frac{2^{d}}{(d!)^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(-2)^{-k_{i}}k_{i}!\bigl[z_{i}^{2k_{i}+1}\bigr]\mathcal{U}_{d}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}).

One main advantage of
A
, the expression of equivariant spin GW invariants in the Fock space formalism, is that this formalism is very well-adapted to the theory of integrable hierarchies. Integrable hierarchies are infinite collections of compatible partial differential equations for a single function in infinitely many variables. A famous example is the Korteweg–de Vries (KdV) hierarchy, a special case of the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili (KP) hierarchy, which by the Kontsevich–Witten theorem
[
Kon92
,
Wit91
]
describes intersection theory on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, i.e. GW theory of a point. Okounkov–Pandharipande
[
OP06a
]
applied their expression for the equivariant GW theory of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to prove it satisfies another hierarchy, called the 2D Toda lattice hierarchy. This is equivalent to two coupled KP hierarchies, labelled by another discrete lattice parameter.

Our expression in the neutral fermion Fock space allows for a similar result. Because we work with the neutral fermions, we obtain a B-type version of the hierarchy, known as the
2
-BKP hierarchy. As its name suggests, this hierarchy governs two coupled B-type KP hierarchies. The lattice parameter, however, is not available in this context.

Theorem F
.

After a triangular linear change of variables
\{x_{i},x_{i}^{\star}\}\mapsto\{t_{i},s_{i}\}
, the equivariant tau-function
\tau
satisfies the
2
-BKP hierarchy.

For some background on the 2-BKP hierarchy, see
section

2.3
.

1.5.
Strategy of proof of
A

The spin Hurwitz numbers play a central role in the proof of
A
, as we will follow a parallel strategy to
[
OP06a
]
to compute the equivariant spin GW theory of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In the ordinary (i.e. non-spin) setting, Okounkov and Pandharipande establish the bridge between GW theory and Hurwitz numbers by virtual localisation on the space of stable maps to equivariant
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The integrals appearing in the localisation formula match with the right-hand side of the celebrated ELSV formula
[
ELSV01
]
:

(1.19)

h_{g;\mu}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mu_{i}^{\mu_{i}}}{\mu_{i}!}\right)\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\frac{\Lambda(-1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\mu_{i}\psi_{i})}.

The left-hand side of this equation is an ordinary Hurwitz number with one fixed ramification profile
\mu
and simple ramification elsewhere. From this match, they obtain an expression for GW invariants which is
quadratic
in the Hurwitz numbers. Exploiting the Fock space representation of ordinary Hurwitz numbers, they proceed to the ordinary analogue of
A
, which in turn establishes the integrability of the equivariant GW theory with respect to the
2
D Toda lattice hierarchy of PDEs and the ordinary GW/H correspondence for
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by taking the non-equivariant limit.

To implement this plan in the spin setting, a desired ingredient is an ELSV-type formula for the simplest instance of completed cycles spin Hurwitz numbers, which needs to be compatible with the virtual localisation of the localised fundamental class. The
(r+1)
-completed cycles spin Hurwitz numbers are obtained by imposing that all ramifications except the one over zero are given by completed cycles of the same type:

(1.20)

h^{+,r}_{g;\mu}\coloneqq\frac{|\mathord{\mathrm{Aut}}{(\mu)}|}{b!}H_{d}\bigl(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1);\mu,(\bar{c}_{r+1})^{b}\bigr),

where the Riemann–Hurwitz theorem imposes
rb=2g-2+\ell(\mu)+d
.

Spin Hurwitz numbers do not allow even-order ramification index. Hence, simple ramifications must be skipped as base case, and the first natural oddly ramified instance is given by the
3
-completed cycles. The presence of completed cycles for even the simplest case is in contrast to the non-spin case, which features simple ramifications. This complicates the operator formalism considerably, and new strategies must be adopted (especially in
section

4
) to achieve the desired results. An ELSV-type formula for
3
-completed cycles spin Hurwitz numbers appears in
[
GKL21
, equation (9.24)]
and features a double Hodge class as integrand
†
†

†

(r+1)
-completed cycles spin Hurwitz number for any even
r
. Here we only recall the one for
r=2

[

(1.21)

h^{+,2}_{g;\mu}=2^{g-1+n}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mu_{i}^{\frac{\mu_{i}-1}{2}}}{\bigl(\frac{\mu_{i}-1}{2}\bigr)!}\right)\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\frac{\Lambda(2)\Lambda(-1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\mu_{i}\psi_{i})}.

The virtual localisation analysis of the equivariant spin GW invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with its unique spin structure
\mathcal{O}(-1))
leaves some freedom in lifting the equivariant structure to
\mathcal{O}(-1)
: the weights of the representation at the fibres are only restricted to differ by
1

We use polynomiality of the localised class to make a (non-standard but symmetric) choice of weights, which shows that the double Hodge class in the above ELSV-type formula is exactly what is needed for the match: one Hodge class arises from the virtual fundamental class, the other from the top Chern class appearing in
equation

1.5
.

Using this ELSV-type formula to write the spin GW invariants as a quadratic expression in spin Hurwitz numbers, we can then re-express them as a product of vacuum expectation values in the neutral fermion Fock space. That shape can then be manipulated to obtain a single vacuum expectation value, which reduces to spin Hurwitz numbers in the non-equivariant limit to establish
C
.

The main technical step in the fermion analysis concerns the extension of the vacuum expectation value of the Hodge integrals to arbitrary complex
\mu_{i}
, and the calculation of the commutators of the required operators. In the ordinary case, Okounkov–Pandharipande
[
OP06
]
employed a sophisticated analysis involving hypergeometric functions to obtain these results. We rather follow the alternative route followed by Oblomkov–Okounkov–Pandharipande
[
OOP20
]
, by finding an ODE for a certain dressing operator.

2.1.
Neutral fermion Fock space

Definition 2.1
.

Let

(2.2)

\{\varphi_{k},\varphi_{l}\}=(-1)^{k}\delta_{k+l}.

Definition 2.2

Consider the subspace

of

We write

With the dual construction, (i.e. considering the unique graded highest-weight right module) we define the dual Fock space

and

Lemma 2.3
.

The vacuum expectation values of quadratic expressions in the

\braket{\varphi_{k}\varphi_{l}}=(-1)^{k}\delta_{k+l}u[l],\qquad\text{where}\qquad u[l]=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $l>0$},\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if $l=0$},\\

Definition 2.4

Let

\pi

2.2.
Spin Hurwitz numbers

As explained in the introduction, spin Hurwitz numbers are weighted counts of covers of a curve with a spin structure, with a sign given by the parity
[
EOP08
]
. Spin structures can be pulled back along branched covers, as long as all ramifications are odd: in that case the ramification divisor is even.

Definition 2.9
.

Let

be a spin curve and

It is a spin structure on
C
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(B,\vartheta)

where the sum is over all isomorphism classes of connected ramified covers with ramification profile
\mu^{i}

and unramified anywhere else. As usual, when dealing with disconnected covers, we add a superscript
\bullet
.

For partitions

of arbitrary size, one can extend the definition as follows: if at least one of the
\mu^{i}
has size greater than

H_{d}(B,\vartheta;\mu^{1},\dotsc,\mu^{n})\coloneqq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m_{1}(\widehat{\mu^{i}})}{{m_{1}(\mu^{i})}}\right)H_{d}\bigl(B,\vartheta;\widehat{\mu^{1}},\dotsc,\widehat{\mu^{n}}\bigr),

where

.

For any positive integer

\mathfrak{b}_{2\infty}
.
This hierarchy has many similar properties to the KP hierarchy, and in particular can be studied via the neutral fermion formalism. Its tau-functions are related to an orthogonal Grassmannian, and in the neutral fermionic Fock space they can be expressed as

(2.34)

\tau_{M}(t)=\Braket{\Gamma_{+}^{B}(t)M},\qquad\quad\Gamma_{+}^{B}(t)=\exp\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{2k+1}\alpha^{B}_{2k+1}\bigg),

with
M\in\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\infty}\coloneqq\set{e^{g_{1}}\cdots e^{g_{N}}}{g_{i}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\infty}}
. In other words, BKP tau-functions are the bosonic analogue through
theorem

2.6
of elements in the orbit
\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\infty}\ket{0}
.

Like the KP hierarchy, the BKP hierarchy also has extensions to multiple components. Tau-functions of
2
-BKP are of the form
[
Tak06
, page 5]
(or
[
Orl03
, equation (4.9)]
for the hypergeometric case),

(2.35)

\tau_{M}(t,s)=\Braket{\Gamma^{B}_{+}(t)M\Gamma_{-}^{B}(s)},\qquad\quad\Gamma_{\pm}^{B}(t)=\exp\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{2k+1}\alpha^{B}_{\pm(2k+1)}\bigg).

Their Hirota bilinear equation is given by
[
Tak06
, equation (2.1)]

(2.36)

\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}e^{\xi(t^{\prime}-t,z)}\tau(t^{\prime}-2[z^{-1}],s^{\prime})\tau(t+2[z^{-1}],s)=\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi iz}e^{\xi(s^{\prime}-s,z)}\tau(t^{\prime},s^{\prime}-2[z^{-1}])\tau(t,s+2[z^{-1}]),

where

(2.37)

\xi(t,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{2k+1}z^{2k+1},\quad[z^{-1}]=\Big(z^{-1},\frac{z^{-3}}{3},\frac{z^{-5}}{5},\dotsc\Big).

Schur functions are tau-functions for KP, and 2-KP tau-functions can be written as bilinear combinations of Schur functions. For 2-BKP, this role is taken by Schur
Q
-functions, see
[
Orl03
, proposition 1]
for the hypergeometric case. Moreover, using that the algebra
\mathfrak{b}_{\infty}
can be embedded in
\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}
in two independent ways
\iota_{1}
,
\iota_{2}
, for each
M\in\widehat{\mathrm{B}}_{\infty}
, one finds that
[
JM83
, equation (6.7)]
(indicating by a superscript whether the
\tau
function is of
KP
or
BKP
)

(2.38)

\tau^{B}_{M}(t_{1},t_{3},\dotsc)^{2}=\tau^{A}_{\iota_{1}(M)+\iota_{2}(M)}(t_{1},0,t_{3},0,t_{5},\dotsc).

This can be extended to 2-(B)KP as in the proof of
[
Lee20
, theorem 1.1]
.

One essential difference between 2-KP and 2-BKP is the grading or charge. The charge of the fermions in the A case allows for an extra lattice (
\mathbb{Z}
-valued) parameter
N
, which promotes 2-KP tau-functions to 2D Toda lattice
\tau_{N}
functions. In the neutral, B-type, case, there is only a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
grading, and moreover
\tau_{0}=\tau_{1}

[
JM83
, page 972]
. Therefore, there is no natural extension from 2-BKP to some kind of “2D B-Toda lattice”.

3.
Localisation formula for spin Gromov–Witten invariants

The goal of this section is to apply the virtual localisation formula of Graber–Pandharipande
[
GP99
]
to express spin GW invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in terms of integrals over the fixed points of the torus action, that is integrals over the moduli space of stable curves. The resulting formula will express spin GW invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in terms of double Hodge integrals arising as vertex terms.

3.1.
The torus action

We write
V=\mathbb{C}^{2}
. We consider the torus action on
V
with weights
(0,1)
, i.e.

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\times V

\displaystyle\longrightarrow V

\displaystyle\bigl(\xi,(x_{1},x_{2})\bigr)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\bigl(x_{1},\xi\cdot x_{2}\bigr).

We denote by
\mathbb{P}^{1}
the projectivisation of
V
. The torus action on
V
induces an action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and we denote by
0
and
\infty
the fixed points of this action. A lifting of the
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-k)
is determined by the weights
[\alpha,\alpha+k]
of the representations at the fibres
\mathcal{O}(-k)_{|0}
and
\mathcal{O}(-k)_{|\infty}
for some integer
\alpha
.

We write
H_{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}^{*}({\rm pt})=\mathbb{Q}[t]
for the equivariant cohomology ring of the point, where
t
is the first Chern class of the standard representation. The equivariant cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is then the module over
\mathbb{Q}[t]
given by:

(3.2)

H_{\mathbb{C}^{\ast}}^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\mathbb{Q}[h,t]/(h^{2}+th),

where
h
denotes the equivariant first Chern class of
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Then the equivariant Poincaré dual of
0
and
\infty
are given in this basis by

(3.3)

\mathbf{0}=t\cdot 1+h,\qquad\bm{\infty}=h.

Let
g,n,m,
and
d
be non-negative integers. The torus action on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
provides a canonical torus action on the moduli space of stable maps. Thus, we will work in the equivariant cohomology of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+m}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)
.

In the following, we will denote the generating series of the connected enumerative invariants of interest with a superscript
\circ
.

Definition 3.1
.

Define the connected, degree
d
, equivariant
(n+m)
-point function for spin GW invariants of genus
g
of
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1))
as:

(3.4)

\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{g,d}(z,w)\coloneqq(-1)^{g-1+d}2^{3g-3+n+m+2d}\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+m}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)]^{{\rm loc},\mathcal{O}(-1)}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z_{i}\ev_{i}^{\ast}(\mathbf{0})}{1-z_{i}\psi_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{w_{j}\ev_{n+j}^{\ast}(\bm{\infty})}{1-w_{j}\psi_{n+j}}.

The unstable terms are defined as:

(3.5)

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}()=\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{1,0}()=0\\
\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(z_{1})=\frac{1}{2tz_{1}},\qquad\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(w_{1})=-\frac{1}{2tw_{1}}\\
\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{z_{1}z_{2}}{z_{1}+z_{2}},\qquad\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(z_{1},w_{1})=0,\qquad\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(w_{1},w_{2})=\frac{w_{1}w_{2}}{w_{1}+w_{2}}.\end{gathered}

Define the corresponding genus generating series and its disconnected counterpart as:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{d}(z,w;u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}u^{g-1}\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{g,d}(z,w),

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{d}(z,w;u)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{P}\frac{1}{|\mathord{\mathrm{Aut}}(P)|}\prod_{k=1}^{\ell(P)}\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{d_{k}}\bigl(z_{I^{(k)}},w_{J^{(k)}};u\bigr),

where the
P
-sum is over all vectors
(I^{(k)},J^{(k)},d_{k})_{k=1}^{\ell}
such that
\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{\ell}I^{(k)}=\{1,\dotsc,n\}
,
\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{\ell}J^{(k)}=\{1,\dotsc,m\}
, and
\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}{d_{k}}=d
.

The restriction of the function
\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}
at
t=0
recovers the generating series of spin GW invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

(3.7)

\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{g,d}(z,w)\big|_{t=0}=(-1)^{g-1+d}2^{3g-3+n+m+2d}\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+m}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)]^{{\rm loc},\mathcal{O}(-1)}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z_{i}\ev_{i}^{\ast}([{{\rm pt}}])}{1-z_{i}\psi_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{w_{j}\ev_{n+j}^{\ast}([{{\rm pt}}])}{1-w_{j}\psi_{n+j}}.

3.2.
Applying the localisation formula

We describe here the computation of the generating function
\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{d}(z,w;u)
using the virtual localisation formula of Graber–Pandharipande
[
GP99
]
. We recall that the fixed loci of the
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-action on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+m}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)
are indexed by decorated graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{g,n,m,d}
, that is the set of tuples

(3.8)

\bigl(\Gamma,V(\Gamma)=V_{0}\sqcup V_{\infty},\underline{d}=(d_{e})_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\bigr),

where:

•

\Gamma
is a pre-stable graph. For each vertex
v
, we denote by
g(v),n(v),
and
{\rm val}(v)
the genus, number of half-edges, and valency (number of adjacent edges) of
v
.

•

The genus of
\Gamma
is
g
, i.e:

(3.9)

h_{1}(\Gamma)+\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}g(v)=g,

where
h_{1}(\Gamma)
is the first Betti number of the graph and
g(v)
are the evaluations of the genus function at every vertex.

•

The partition of
V(\Gamma)
makes
\Gamma
bipartite. Moreover, there are
n
legs attached to vertices in
V_{0}
, and
m
legs attached to vertices in
V_{\infty}
.

•

The degrees
d_{e}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
satisfy:

(3.10)

\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}d_{e}=d,

where
E(\Gamma)
is the set of edges of
\Gamma
.

The fixed locus
X_{\Gamma,\underline{d}}
associated to this datum is then isomorphic to

(3.11)

X_{\Gamma,\underline{d}}\cong\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g(v),n(v)}\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\iota_{(\Gamma,\underline{d})}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d),

with the convention that if
g(v)=0
and
n(v)=1
, or
2
(unstable vertices) then
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g(v),n(v)}
is a point. By
[
GP99
]
we have:

(3.12)

\bigl[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)\bigr]^{\rm vir}=\sum_{\Gamma,\underline{d}}\iota_{(\Gamma,\underline{d}),\ast}\beta(\Gamma,\underline{d})\in A_{*}\bigl(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{1},d)\bigr)[t,t^{-1}],

where the (Poincaré dual of the) class
\beta(\Gamma,\underline{d})
is given by

(3.13)

\begin{split}\beta(\Gamma,\underline{d})=\frac{1}{|\mathord{\mathrm{Aut}}(\Gamma,\underline{d})|\prod_{e\in E(\Gamma)}d_{e}}&\prod_{v\in V_{0}}t^{g(v)-1}\Lambda\Bigl(-\frac{1}{t}\Bigr)\left(\prod_{e\mapsto v}t^{-d_{e}}\frac{d_{e}^{d_{e}}}{d_{e}!}\frac{td_{e}}{t-d_{e}\psi_{e}}\right)\\
\times&\prod_{v\in V_{\infty}}(-t)^{g(v)-1}\Lambda\Bigl(\frac{1}{t}\Bigr)\left(\prod_{e\mapsto v}(-t)^{-d_{e}}\frac{d_{e}^{d_{e}}}{d_{e}!}\frac{td_{e}}{t+d_{e}\psi_{e}}\right).\end{split}

The convention for the contribution of unstable vertices will be given further. Moreover, along a fixed locus
(\Gamma,\underline{d})
, the different cohomology classes restrict as follows.

•

For all
i\leq n
, the class
\ev_{i}^{\ast}(\mathbf{0})=t
if the leg indexed by
i
is adjacent to a vertex in
V_{0}
, and vanishes otherwise. Conversely, if
j\geq n+1
, then
\ev_{j}^{\ast}(\bm{\infty})=-t
if the leg indexed by
j
is adjacent to a vertex in
V_{\infty}
, and vanishes otherwise.

•

Let
k
be a positive integer, and consider
R^{1}\pi_{*}f^{*}\mathcal{O}(-k)
, the first derived pushforward of
\mathcal{O}(-k)
. It is a vector bundle of rank
g+kd-1
. To localise this class, we may choose a lifting of the torus action to the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(-k)
. The lifting depends only on the choice of the weights of the
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-action on the fibres of the line bundle at
0
and
\infty
. As explained before, these weights are of the form
[\alpha,\alpha+k]
for any choice of integer
\alpha
. Then, given such a lifting, the class
c_{\mathrm{top}}\left(R^{1}\pi_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(-k)\right)
restricts to a fixed locus of the
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
-action as:

(3.14)

\begin{split}\iota^{*}_{(\Gamma,\underline{d})}c_{\mathrm{top}}\left(R^{1}\pi_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(-k)\right)=&\prod_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{kd_{e}-1}\frac{(\ell+kd_{e})(-\alpha t)+\ell(\alpha-k)t}{d_{e}}\\
&\times\prod_{\mathclap{v\in V_{0}}}\;\;(k\alpha t)^{{\rm val}(v)-1}(\alpha t)^{g(v)}\Lambda\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha t}\right)\\
&\times\prod_{\mathclap{v\in V_{\infty}}}\;\;(k(\alpha+k)t)^{{\rm val}(v)-1}((\alpha+k)t)^{g(v)}\Lambda\left(-\frac{1}{(\alpha+k)t}\right).\end{split}

A common choice to compute such integrals is
\alpha=-k
or
0
. Indeed, under such choices, the graphs with vertices of valency greater than
1
over
\infty
or
0
give a trivial contribution. We make a different choice here as it does not provide a symmetric role to both sides of the equation, but it should provide interesting results on the structure of the function
\mathbb{G}
.

We are now ready to apply the virtual localisation formula to compute the generating function
\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{d}(z,w;u)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
d\geq 0
,

(3.15)

\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{d}(z,w;u)=\sum_{\mu\in\OP_{d}}\frac{2^{\ell(\mu)}}{\mathfrak{z}_{\mu}}\frac{u^{-d}}{t^{n}(-t)^{m}}\prod_{k=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\biggl(\frac{u}{t}\frac{(\frac{u\mu_{k}}{t})^{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}}{\mu_{k}^{\prime}!}\biggr)\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mu,tz;\tfrac{u}{t})\prod_{k=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\biggl(-\frac{u}{t}\frac{(-\frac{u\mu_{k}}{t})^{\mu_{k}^{\prime}}}{\mu_{k}^{\prime}!}\biggr)\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mu,-tw;-\tfrac{u}{t}),

where
\mathfrak{z}_{\mu}=|\mathord{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mu)|\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}\mu_{i}
is the order of the stabiliser of any permutation of cycle type
\mu
under conjugation.

Proof.

Let us start from the connected generating series
\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{g,d}(z,w)
. Expressing the localised cycle through
equation

1.5
and applying
equation

3.12
and the projection formula, we obtain

\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{g,d}(z,w)=(-1)^{g-1+d}2^{3g-3+n+m+2d}\sum_{\Gamma,\underline{d}}\int_{X_{\Gamma,\underline{d}}}\beta(\Gamma,\underline{d})\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{z_{i}\iota^{*}_{(\Gamma,\underline{d})}\ev_{i}^{\ast}(\mathbf{0})}{1-z_{i}\psi_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{w_{j}\iota^{*}_{(\Gamma,\underline{d})}\ev_{n+j}^{\ast}(\bm{\infty})}{1-w_{j}\psi_{n+j}}\iota^{*}_{(\Gamma,\underline{d})}c_{\mathrm{top}}\left(R^{1}\pi_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(-1)\right).

For
c_{\mathrm{top}}(R^{1}\pi_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(-1))
, we use
equation

3.14
with the choices
k=1
and
\alpha=-1/2
, which yields a more symmetric formula. Note that this choice of
\alpha
is not integral, and thus does not correspond to a lifting of the torus action to
\mathcal{O}(-1)
. However, the class
c_{\mathrm{top}}(R^{1}\pi_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(-1))
depends polynomially on
\alpha
for each fixed locus, thus one may extend the expression of spin GW invariants to all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. For
k=1
and
\alpha=-1/2
,
equation

3.14
specialises to

\begin{split}\iota^{*}_{(\Gamma,\underline{d})}c_{g-1+d}\left(R^{1}\pi_{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathcal{O}(-1)\right)=&\;\hphantom{\times}\prod_{\mathclap{e\in E(\Gamma)}}\;\;\prod_{\ell=1}^{d_{e}-1}\frac{(d_{e}-2\ell)t}{2d_{e}}\\
&\;\times\prod_{\mathclap{v\in V_{0}}}\;\;\Bigl(-\frac{t}{2}\Bigr)^{g(v)+{\rm val}(v)-1}\Lambda\Bigl(\frac{2}{t}\Bigr)\\
&\;\times\prod_{\mathclap{v\in V_{\infty}}}\;\;\Bigl(\frac{t}{2}\Bigr)^{g(v)+{\rm val}(v)-1}\Lambda\Bigl(-\frac{2}{t}\Bigr).\end{split}

This choice of
\alpha
imposes that all graphs with at least one edge of even degree do not contribute, as some factor of the form
(d_{e}-2\ell)
vanishes. Combining the above equation with
equation

3.13
for
\beta(\Gamma,\underline{d})
and the pullbacks of the evaluations over
0
and
\infty
, we obtain

(3.16)

\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{g,d}(z,w)=(-1)^{g-1+d}2^{3g-3+n+m+2d}\sum_{\Gamma,\underline{d}}\frac{1}{|\mathord{\mathrm{Aut}}(\Gamma,\underline{d})|\prod_{e}d_{e}}\\
\times\prod_{v\in V_{0}}\biggl(\cont(v)\prod_{e\mapsto v}\cont(e)\biggr)\prod_{v\in V_{\infty}}\biggl(\cont(v)\prod_{e\mapsto v}\cont(e)\biggr).

The contributions of
(\Gamma,\underline{d})
are computed as follows.

•

The contribution of an half-edge
e
incident to
v\in V_{0}
is

\cont(e)=\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\biggr)^{d_{e}-1}\frac{d_{e}^{d^{\prime}_{e}}}{d^{\prime}_{e}!}t^{-d^{\prime}_{e}-1}.

The contribution of an half-edge
e
incident to
v\in V_{\infty}
is obtained by
t\mapsto-t
.

•

The contribution of a stable vertex
v\in V_{0}
is

\begin{split}\cont(v)&=\Bigl(-\frac{t}{2}\Bigr)^{{\rm val}(v)-1}\frac{(-t)^{g(v)}(t/2)^{g(v)}}{t^{3g(v)-2+n(v)}}\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g(v),n(v)}}\Lambda(2)\Lambda(-1)\prod_{e\mapsto v}\frac{d_{e}}{1-d_{e}\psi_{e}}\prod_{i\mapsto v}\frac{tz_{i}}{1-tz_{i}\psi_{i}}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{g(v)-1+{\rm val}(v)}}{t^{g(v)-1+n(v)-{\rm val}(v)}2^{3g(v)-3+n(v)+{\rm val}(v)}}2^{2g(v)-2+n(v)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g(v),n(v)}}\Lambda(2)\Lambda(-1)\prod_{e\mapsto v}\frac{d_{e}}{1-d_{e}\psi_{e}}\prod_{i\mapsto v}\frac{tz_{i}}{1-tz_{i}\psi_{i}}\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{g(v)-1+{\rm val}(v)}}{2^{3g(v)-3+n(v)+{\rm val}(v)}t^{g(v)-1+n(v)-{\rm val}(v)}}\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{g(v)}(d_{e},tz_{i}).\end{split}

The contribution of a stable vertex
v\in V_{\infty}
is obtained by
t\mapsto-t
.

•

The contributions at unstable vertices are given by the same expressions, if we use our convention of
\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{0}(z_{1})
and
\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{0}(z_{1},z_{2})
given in
equation

2.32
.

We can check the above formula for the unstable
1
-point and
2
-point functions, as given in
equation

3.5
. The
1
-point functions with a single insertion over
0
or
\infty
receive a contribution from a single graph respectively of the form

z_{1}
0
,
w_{1}
0
.

Vertices over
0
are depicted in white and vertices over
\infty
are depicted in black. In these cases, the right-hand side of
equation

3.16
gives

\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(z_{1})=\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{0}(tz_{1})=\frac{1}{2tz_{1}},\qquad\qquad\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(w_{1})=\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{0}(-tw_{1})=-\frac{1}{2tw_{1}},

which agree with
equation

3.5
. As for the
2
-point functions, there is no graph contributing to the mixed
2
-point function
\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(z_{1},w_{1})
since we are in degree zero. On the other hand, the graphs contributing to the
2
-point functions with two insertions over
0
or
\infty
receive a contribution from a single graph respectively of the form

z_{1}
z_{2}
0
,
w_{1}
w_{2}
0
.

As before, the right-hand side of
equation

3.16
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{0}(tz_{1},tz_{2})=\frac{z_{1}z_{2}}{z_{1}+z_{2}},

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{0,0}(w_{1},w_{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{0}(-tw_{1},-tw_{2})=\frac{w_{1}w_{2}}{w_{1}+w_{2}}.

which agree with
equation

3.5
.

We now take all these contributions together, and make the genus generating series on both sides. For this, we use the following global constraints:

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\bigl(g(v)-1\bigr)=g-1-|E(\Gamma)|,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}{\rm val}(v)=2|E(\Gamma)|,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\bigl(n(v)-{\rm val}(v)\bigr)=n+m,

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}d_{e}=d.

Collecting the powers of
2
from all half-edges and vertices, we find

-\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\bigl(3g(v)-3+n(v)+{\rm val}(v)\bigr)-2\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\bigl(d_{e}-1\bigr)=-(3g-3+n+m+2d)+|E(\Gamma)|.

In particular, the powers of
2
cancel out with the global prefactor
2^{3g-3+n+m+2d}
, except for a factor
2^{|E(\Gamma)|}
. Similarly for the powers of
-1
that are not paired with the equivariant parameter
t
:

\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\bigl(g(v)-1+{\rm val}(v)\bigr)=g-1+|E(\Gamma)|\equiv g-1+d\pmod{2}.

In the last equation, we used the fact that
|E(\Gamma)|\equiv d\pmod{2}
, since the edge degrees are all odd. In particular, all powers of
-1
cancel out with the global prefactor
(-1)^{g-1+d}
.

To conclude, let us analyse the powers of
t
. For each vertex
v
over
0
we can pair
-(g(v)-1)
powers of
t
with the genus parameter
u
, leaving out a total of
-\sum_{v\in V_{0}}(n(v)-{\rm val}(v))=-n
. Similarly for vertices over
\infty
, exchanging
t\mapsto-t
. Collecting all together, we have

\mathbb{G}^{\circ}_{d}(z,w;u)=\sum_{\Gamma,\underline{d}}\frac{2^{|E(\Gamma)|}}{|\mathord{\mathrm{Aut}}(\Gamma,\underline{d})|\prod_{e}d_{e}}\frac{u^{|E(\Gamma)|}}{t^{n}(-t)^{m}}\\
\prod_{v\in V_{0}}\mathbb{H}^{\circ}\bigl(\{d_{e}\}_{e\mapsto v},\{tz_{i}\}_{i\mapsto v};\tfrac{u}{t}\bigr)\prod_{e\mapsto v}\frac{1}{t}\frac{(\frac{1}{t}d_{e})^{d^{\prime}_{e}}}{d^{\prime}_{e}!}\\
\prod_{v\in V_{\infty}}\mathbb{H}^{\circ}\bigl(\{d_{e}\}_{e\mapsto v},\{-tw_{j}\}_{j\mapsto v};-\tfrac{u}{t}\bigr)\prod_{e\mapsto v}\frac{1}{-t}\frac{(-\frac{1}{t}d_{e})^{d^{\prime}_{e}}}{d^{\prime}_{e}!}.

We can distribute the factor
u^{|E(\Gamma)|}
to the half-edges using the relation

|E(\Gamma)|=2\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\bigl(d_{e}^{\prime}+1\bigr)-d.

To change from connected to disconnected counts, we may gather all double Hodge functions over
0
together, and similarly for
\infty
. Then the sum collapses to graphs with one vertex over
0
and one over
\infty
, and the only remaining information is the splitting of
d
, which is encoded in an odd partition
\mu
. The factor
|\mathord{\mathrm{Aut}}(\Gamma,\underline{d})|\prod_{e\in E(\Gamma)}d_{e}
collapses to
\mathfrak{z}_{\mu}
, and we simply note that the number of edges coincides with the length of the partition
\mu
.
∎

4.
Double Hodge integrals in operator formalism

Employing Fock space techniques in Hurwitz theory dates back to the foundational paper by Okounkov
[
Oko00
]
, where the Toda equation for Hurwitz numbers was proved for the first time. Working in Hurwitz theory via Fock space formalism has the significant advantage of providing an explicit representation of operators for computations. In the spirit of
[
OP06a
, section 2]
, this section aims to transfer the operator formalism from the Hurwitz side to the GW side, albeit in the spin setting. The methods for computing the commutation relation needed are analogous to those developed in
[
OOP20
]
.

More precisely, the main goal is to express the double Hodge integrals as correlators in the Fock space:

(4.1)

u^{n}\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n};u)=\Braket{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{B}(z_{i},uz_{i})}

for some specific operators
\mathcal{B}
. To this end, we start from the spin ELSV formula
2.30
which equates the double Hodge integrals with spin Hurwitz numbers for
z_{i}=\mu_{i}
odd positive integers. For these numbers, we know an expression as correlators, specifically as correlators of a product of the
\mathcal{B}
-operators. We need to extend the definition of the
\mathcal{B}
-operators from odd integral parameters to complex ones, and show that the associated correlators are rational functions of the parameters. This will allow us to conclude
equation

4.1
via a density argument.

4.1.
The definition of
\mathcal{B}
-operators

Definition 4.1
.

Define the operators

(4.2)

\mathcal{B}(z,uz)=e^{u\frac{z^{3}}{12}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j\geq k^{\prime}+1}\frac{\Gamma(z^{\prime}+1)\Gamma(z+2j)}{\Gamma(z+k+1)\Gamma(z^{\prime}+j+1)}(uz)^{j}[w^{2j-1}]\mathcal{S}(w)^{z}\varsigma(w)^{k}\mathcal{E}^{B}_{k}(w),

where we recall the notation
z^{\prime}=\frac{z-1}{2}
, the functions
\mathcal{S}(z)=2\frac{\sinh(\frac{z}{2})}{z}
,
\varsigma(z)=2\sinh\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)
, and the B-Okounkov–Pandharipande operators
\mathcal{E}^{B}_{k}
defined in
2.12
.

The main motivation for the above definition is the following result expressing the specialisation of
\mathcal{B}
to positive odd integer values of
z
in terms of bosons and the first completed cut-and-join operator (recall
definition

2.5
for the definition of such operators).

Proposition 4.2
.

For
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\textup{odd}}
, the operator
\mathcal{B}(\mu,u\mu)
is the conjugation of
\alpha^{B}_{-\mu}
by
e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}
, up to the non-polynomial part:

(4.3)

\mathcal{B}(\mu,u\mu)=\frac{\mu^{\prime}!}{(u\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}}}e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}\alpha^{B}_{-\mu}e^{-u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}e^{-\alpha^{B}_{1}}.

The proof is obtained by expanding both sides on the basis
E_{i,j}^{B}
from
definition

2.4
. Such expansion for the left hand-side was computed in
[
GKL21
, proposition 7.8]
, and we recall it here for the reader’s convenience. For this convention for of the basis elements
E^{B}_{j,k}
, we refer to
[
Gia21
, section 12.1]
.

Lemma 4.3

(
[
GKL21
]
)
.

Let
\Delta
be the backward difference operator, and denote
Q(l,\mu)=\frac{(l+\mu)^{3}-l^{3}}{3\mu}
. Then

(4.4)

\frac{\mu^{\prime}!}{(u\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}}}e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}\alpha^{B}_{-\mu}e^{-u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}e^{-\alpha^{B}_{1}}=\\
=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(u\mu)^{s}}{(\mu^{\prime}+1)_{s}}\Biggl(\sum_{j\geq 0}\sum_{l\geq\frac{j+1-\mu}{2}}(-1)^{l}\frac{\Delta^{j}}{j!}Q(l,\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}+s}E^{B}_{l+\mu-j,l}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta^{\mu-1}}{\mu!}Q(l,\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}+s}\big|_{l=0}{\mathrm{Id}}\Biggr).

To expand the left-hand side, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

The backwards difference operator acts on monomials as:

(4.5)

\frac{\Delta^{j}}{j!}\frac{l^{a}}{a!}=[w^{a}]\frac{\varsigma(w)^{j}}{j!}e^{w(l-\frac{j}{2})}.

Moreover, the extraction of powers of
l
from the polynomial
Q
yields

(4.6)

a![l^{a}]Q(l,\mu)^{p}=[w^{-a}]\left(\sum_{m=0}^{p}\binom{p}{m}\frac{(2p-2m)!}{w^{2p-2m}}\frac{\mu^{2m}}{12^{m}}\right)e^{w\frac{\mu}{2}}.

Proof.

We may expand the left-hand side of
equation

4.5
as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{j}}{j!}\frac{l^{a}}{a!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{j}{i}(-1)^{i}\frac{(l-i)^{a}}{a!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{j}{i}(-1)^{i}\sum_{k=0}^{a}\frac{1}{(a-k)!}(j-i)^{a-k}\frac{1}{k!}(l-j)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{a}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\binom{j}{i}(-1)^{i}\frac{1}{(a-k)!}(j-i)^{a-k}[w^{k}]e^{w(l-j)}.

The
i
-sum can be expressed as a Stirling number of the second kind
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{j}
, thanks to the identity
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{j}=\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{i}(t-i)^{n}\binom{j}{i}
. Thus:

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{j}}{j!}\frac{l^{a}}{a!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{a}\frac{1}{(a-k)!}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a-k}{j}[w^{k}]e^{w(l-j)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{a}[w^{a-k}](e^{w}-1)^{j}[w^{k}]e^{w(l-j)}

\displaystyle=[w^{a}]\frac{\varsigma(w)^{j}}{j!}e^{w(l-\frac{j}{2})}.

The second equality follows from the identity
\frac{1}{n!}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{j}=\frac{1}{j!}[w^{n}](e^{w}-1)^{j}
. For
equation

4.6
, we find

\begin{split}a![l^{a}]Q(l,\mu)^{p}&=a![l^{a}]\Big(l^{2}+\mu l+\frac{\mu^{2}}{3}\Big)^{p}\\
&=a![l^{a}]\Big(\big(l+\frac{\mu}{2}\big)^{2}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{12}\Big)^{p}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{p}\binom{p}{m}\frac{\mu^{2}m}{12^{m}}a![l^{a}]\Big(l+\frac{\mu}{2}\Big)^{2p-2m}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{p}\binom{p}{m}\frac{(2p-2m)!}{(2p-2m-a)!}\frac{\mu^{2m}}{12^{m}}\Big(\frac{\mu}{2}\Big)^{2p-2m-a}\\
&=[w^{-a}]\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{p}\binom{p}{m}\frac{(2p-2m)!}{w^{2p-2m}}\frac{\mu^{2m}}{12^{m}}\Big)e^{w\frac{\mu}{2}}.\hfill\qed\end{split}

Proof of
proposition

4.2
.

Starting from the left-hand side of
equation

4.4
, we find

\frac{\mu^{\prime}!}{(u\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}}}e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}\alpha^{B}_{-\mu}e^{-u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}e^{-\alpha^{B}_{1}}=\\
=\sum_{s\geq-\mu^{\prime}}\frac{(u\mu)^{s}}{(\mu^{\prime}+1)_{s}}\Bigg(\sum_{k=-\mu}^{2s-1}\sum_{l\geq k^{\prime}+1}(-1)^{l}\frac{\Delta^{\mu+k}}{(\mu+k)!}Q(l,\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}+s}E^{B}_{l-k,l}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta^{\mu-1}}{\mu!}Q(l,\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}+s}\big|_{l=0}{\mathrm{Id}}\Bigg).

We can now expand the difference operator using
lemma

4.4
to get

\frac{\Delta^{\mu+k}}{(\mu+k)!}Q(l,\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}+s}=\sum_{m=0}^{\mu^{\prime}+s}\binom{\mu^{\prime}+s}{m}\frac{(\mu+2(s-m)-1)!}{(\mu+k)!}\frac{\mu^{2m}}{12^{m}}[w^{2(s-m)-1}]\mathcal{S}(w)^{\mu}\varsigma(w)^{k}e^{w(l-\frac{k}{2})}.

Similarly for the contribution of the identity:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta^{\mu-1}}{\mu!}Q(l,\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}+s}\big|_{l=0}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu^{\prime}+s}\binom{\mu^{\prime}+s}{m}\frac{(\mu+2(s-m)-1)!}{\mu!}\frac{\mu^{2m}}{12^{m}}[w^{2(s-m)-1}]\mathcal{S}(w)^{\mu}\frac{e^{\frac{w}{2}}}{\varsigma(w)}.

Notice that we are extracting the coefficient of an odd power of
w
from
\mathcal{S}(w)^{\mu}\varsigma(w)^{-1}e^{\frac{w}{2}}
, and
\mathcal{S}(w)^{\mu}\varsigma(w)^{-1}
is an odd function. In particular, we can substitute
e^{\frac{w}{2}}
with its even part,
\cosh(w/2)
, since it would not change the extracted coefficient. Thus, we find

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta^{\mu-1}}{\mu!}Q(l,\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}+s}\big|_{l=0}=\\
=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\mu^{\prime}+s}\binom{\mu^{\prime}+s}{m}\frac{(\mu+2(s-m)-1)!}{\mu!}\frac{\mu^{2m}}{12^{m}}[w^{2(s-m)-1}]\mathcal{S}(w)^{\mu}\frac{\cosh(\tfrac{w}{2})}{\varsigma(w)}.

In particular, we get the right correction for
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{B}(w)
. All together, exchanging the
s
- and
k
-sums, we find

\frac{\mu^{\prime}!}{(u\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}}}e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}\alpha^{B}_{-\mu}e^{-u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}e^{-\alpha^{B}_{1}}=\sum_{k\geq-\mu}\sum_{s\geq k^{\prime}+1}\frac{(u\mu)^{s}}{(\mu^{\prime}+1)_{s}}\\
\times\sum_{m=0}^{\mu^{\prime}+s}\binom{\mu^{\prime}+s}{m}\frac{(\mu+2(s-m)-1)!}{(\mu+k)!}\frac{\mu^{2m}}{12^{m}}[w^{2(s-m)-1}]\mathcal{S}(w)^{\mu}\varsigma(w)^{k}\mathcal{E}^{B}_{k}(w).

Notice that the factorials simplify as

\frac{1}{(\mu^{\prime}+1)_{s}}\binom{\mu^{\prime}+s}{m}\frac{(\mu+2(s-m)-1)!}{(\mu+k)!}=\frac{\mu^{\prime}!}{(\mu+k)!}\frac{1}{m!}\frac{(\mu+2(s-m)-1)!}{(\mu^{\prime}+s-m)!}.

Exchanging the
s
- and
m
-sums, we should get bounds
m\geq 0
and
s\geq\max\set{m-\mu^{\prime},k^{\prime}+1}
. However, the summands for
s<m+k^{\prime}+1-\frac{\delta_{k,0}}{2}
vanish, due to the vanishing order of zeros of
\varsigma(w)^{k}\mathcal{E}^{B}_{k}(w)
. Hence, for
k\neq 0
, we can replace the lower bound for
s
by
m+k^{\prime}+1
in order to get

\begin{split}&\frac{\mu^{\prime}!}{(u\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}}}e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}\alpha^{B}_{-\mu}e^{-u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}e^{-\alpha^{B}_{1}}=\\
&\qquad\quad=\sum_{k\geq-\mu}\frac{\mu^{\prime}!}{(\mu+k)!}\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\mu^{2m}}{12^{m}}\sum_{s\geq m+k^{\prime}+1}\frac{(\mu+2(s-m)-1)!}{(\mu^{\prime}+s-m)!}(u\mu)^{s}[w^{2(s-m)-1}]\mathcal{S}(w)^{\mu}\varsigma(w)^{k}\mathcal{E}^{B}_{k}(w)\\
&\qquad\quad=e^{u\frac{\mu^{3}}{12}}\sum_{k\geq-\mu}\sum_{j\geq k^{\prime}+1}\frac{\mu^{\prime}!}{(\mu+k)!}\frac{(\mu+2j-1)!}{(\mu^{\prime}+j)!}(u\mu)^{t}[w^{2j-1}]\mathcal{S}(w)^{\mu}\varsigma(w)^{k}\mathcal{E}^{B}_{k}(w).\end{split}

We can now see that the above expression coincides with the definition of
\mathcal{B}
for
z=\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{\textup{odd}}_{+}
. Indeed, we have the factor
\frac{\Gamma(\mu^{\prime}+1)}{\Gamma(\mu+k+1)}
, which vanishes for
k<-\mu
(and has no poles, since
\mu
is assumed to be positive). On the other hand, by Legendre duplication formula, we find

\frac{\Gamma(\mu+2j)}{\Gamma(\mu^{\prime}+j+1)}=\frac{2^{\mu+2j-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(\mu^{\prime}+j+\tfrac{1}{2}),

which has no poles for
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{\textup{odd}}_{+}
and
j
integer. Thus, the summation in the definition of
\mathcal{B}
for
z=\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{\textup{odd}}_{+}
reduces to
k\geq-\mu
.
∎

4.2.
The commutation relation

We can now compute the commutator of two
\mathcal{B}
-operators. Consider the doubly infinite series

(4.7)

\delta(z,-w)=\frac{1}{w}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(-\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k}\in\mathbb{Q}(\!(z,w)\!),

which is a formal
\delta
-function at
z+w=0
in the sense that
(z+w)\delta(z,-w)=0
, or equivalently as the discontinuity of the rational function
\frac{1}{(z+w)}
along the divisor
z+w=0
.
The same series appears in the A-case
[
OP06a
, theorem 1]
.

Proposition 4.5

(Commutation relation)
.

(4.8)

\bigl[\mathcal{B}(z,uz),\mathcal{B}(w,uw)\bigr]=uzw\delta(z,-w).

Before giving a proof of the above commutation relation, let us explore its main consequences. Firstly, the correlator
\Braket{\mathcal{B}(z_{1},uz_{1})\cdots\mathcal{B}(z_{n},uz_{n})}
is a rational function with at most simple poles at
z_{i}=0
and simple poles along the antidiagonals
z_{i}+z_{j}=0
.

Corollary 4.6
.

We have

(4.9)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(z_{i}+z_{j})\Braket{\mathcal{B}(z_{1},uz_{1})\cdots\mathcal{B}(z_{n},uz_{n})}\in\mathbb{Q}[u^{\pm}]\llbracket z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\rrbracket.

Proof.

By
proposition

4.5
, the correlator, once multiplied with the product of
z_{i}+z_{j}
, must be symmetric in the
z_{i}
. It is also a Laurent series in
z_{1}
(and hence the other
z_{i}
) with at most a simple pole, as can be seen immediately from
definition

4.1
.
∎

Thanks to this regularity result, we can prove the main result of the section.

Theorem 4.7
.

The generating series of double Hodge integrals can be expressed as the following vacuum expectation value:

(4.10)

u^{n}\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n};u)=\Braket{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{B}(z_{i},uz_{i})}.

Proof.

Recall the spin ELSV formula (
theorem

2.14
) and the expression of spin Hurwitz numbers as vacuum expectation values (
proposition

2.12
):

\displaystyle h^{\bullet,+,2}_{g;\mu}

\displaystyle=2^{g-1+n}\bigg(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mu_{i}^{\mu_{i}^{\prime}}}{\mu_{i}^{\prime}!}\bigg)\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bullet}_{g,n}}\frac{\Lambda(2)\Lambda(-1)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\mu_{i}\psi_{i})},

\displaystyle h^{\bullet,+,2}_{g;\mu}

\displaystyle=2^{1-g}[u^{b}]\Braket{\prod_{i=1}^{n}e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}\frac{\alpha^{B}_{-\mu_{i}}}{\mu_{i}}e^{-u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}e^{-\alpha^{B}_{1}}}.

Here
b=g-1+\frac{|\mu|+n}{2}
is given by the Riemann–Hurwitz formula. On the other hand, from
proposition

4.2
we find

\frac{(u\mu)^{\mu^{\prime}}}{\mu^{\prime}!}\mathcal{B}(\mu,u\mu)=e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}\alpha^{B}_{-\mu_{i}}e^{-u\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}e^{-\alpha^{B}_{1}}.

Putting this together, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mu;u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}u^{g-1}2^{2g-2+n}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\bullet}_{g,n}}\Lambda(2)\Lambda(-1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mu_{i}}{1-\mu_{i}\psi_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}u^{g-1}\biggl(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}\biggr)\biggl(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mu_{i}^{\mu_{i}^{\prime}}}{\mu_{i}^{\prime}!}\biggr)^{-1}\bigl[v^{g-1+\frac{|\mu|+n}{2}}\bigr]\Braket{\prod_{i=1}^{n}e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}e^{v\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}\frac{\alpha^{B}_{-\mu_{i}}}{\mu_{i}}e^{-v\frac{\mathcal{F}^{B}_{3}}{3}}e^{-\alpha^{B}_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}u^{g-1}[v^{g-1+n}]\Braket{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i},v\mu_{i})}

\displaystyle=u^{-n}\Braket{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i},u\mu_{i})}.

Since both
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mu;u)
and
\Braket{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{B}(\mu_{i},u\mu_{i})}
are rational functions and they agree on a Zariski dense set, they coincide for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
∎

The rest of the section is dedicated to the proof of the commutation relation between
\mathcal{B}
-operators (
proposition

4.5
). We will pursue a similar approach to the A-case in
[
OOP20
]
. Recall from that paper the algebra

(4.11)

\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{Z}\braket{S^{\pm},H^{\pm}}/([H,S]-S).

We can see
\mathfrak{h}
as a subalgebra of the bi-infinite general linear algebra
\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}
, with the identification
H=\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
S=\alpha_{-1}
. Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
be its central extension induced from the one of
\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{\infty}
. In fact
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
can be described explicitly as the Lie subalgebra spanned by the Okounkov–Pandharipande operators
\mathcal{E}_{m,k}\coloneqq[z^{k}]\mathcal{E}_{m}(z)
.

Let us now define the Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{B}
to be the subalgebra of
\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{\infty}
spanned by
\mathcal{E}_{m,k}^{B}\coloneqq[z^{k}]\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m}(z)
.

Proposition 4.8
.

(1)

If
m+k
is even, then
\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m,k}=0
.

(2)

The map
p\colon\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{B}\colon\mathcal{E}_{m,k}\mapsto\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m,k}
is a surjective Lie algebra morphism, with right inverse
\iota\colon\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{B}\to\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}\colon\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m,k}\mapsto\mathcal{E}_{m,k}
for odd
m+k
.

Proof.

The first point follows immediately from the parity relations of
proposition

2.8
. For the second point, it is clear that
p\circ\iota={\mathrm{Id}}_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{B}}
, and this implies surjectivity of
p
. To show these are Lie algebra morphisms, we compare commutators. From
[
OP06
, equation (2.17)]
and the commutation relations of
proposition

2.8
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathcal{E}_{m}(z),\mathcal{E}_{n}(w)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\varsigma\big(\det\begin{bmatrix}m&z\\
n&w\end{bmatrix}\big)\mathcal{E}_{m+n}(z+w);

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m}(z),\mathcal{E}^{B}_{n}(w)\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varsigma\big(\det\begin{bmatrix}m&z\\
n&w\end{bmatrix}\big)\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m+n}(z+w)+\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2}\varsigma\big(\det\begin{bmatrix}m&z\\
n&-w\end{bmatrix}\big)\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m+n}(z-w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varsigma\big(\det\begin{bmatrix}m&z\\
n&w\end{bmatrix}\big)\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m+n}(z+w)+\frac{(-1)^{m+1}}{2}\varsigma\big(\det\begin{bmatrix}m&-z\\
n&w\end{bmatrix}\big)\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m+n}(w-z).

Because the second is the symmetrisation or antisymmetrisation of the first in
w
, depending on the parity of
n
(or in
z
, depending on the parity of
m
, or both) we find that if both
m+k
and
n+l
are odd, then

\bigl[p(\mathcal{E}_{m,k}),p(\mathcal{E}_{n,l})\bigr]=p\big([\mathcal{E}_{m,k},\mathcal{E}_{n,l}]\big),

because the (anti)symmetrisation preserves this coefficient. If either or both of
m+k
and
n+l
are even, the left-hand side of the above equation vanishes by definition of
\mathcal{E}^{B}_{m,k}
or of
\mathcal{E}^{B}_{n,l}
, and the right-hand side vanishes because the (anti)symmetrisation kills that coefficient. Similarly for
\iota
.
∎

By this proposition we can perform all of our calculations in
\mathfrak{h}
, then push them forward to
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
and eventually to
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{B}
along
p
. The computation of the commutator between
\mathcal{B}(z,uz)
operators is achieved by computing the commutator between their leading order in the
u
-variable, and showing that there exists a dressing operator
W
relating
\mathcal{B}(z,uz)
and their leading order in
u
by conjugation. The following lemma defines the dressing operator
W
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Consider the operators in
\mathfrak{h}
given by

(4.12)

D=S^{-1}+H,\qquad\quad\tilde{D}=S^{-1}+X,\qquad\quad E=\frac{1}{3}\big(X^{3}+S^{-1}X^{2}+XS^{-1}X+X^{2}S^{-1}\big).

where
X=-\frac{1}{2}\{H,\frac{Z}{1-Z}\}
and
Z=\frac{t}{2u}S^{2}
. Then

(4.13)

\frac{d\tilde{D}}{du}+\frac{1}{t}\bigl[E,\tilde{D}\bigr]=0,

and the unique solution
W
of the linear differential equation

(4.14)

\frac{dW}{du}=\frac{1}{t}WE

such that
W|_{u=0}=1
is upper unitriangular and satisfies
W^{-1}DW=\tilde{D}
.

Proof.

We start with the proof of
equation

4.13
. We simply have
\frac{d\tilde{D}}{du}=\frac{1}{2u}\{H,\frac{Z}{(1-Z)^{2}}\}
. On the other hand, direct computation shows that

\bigl[E,\tilde{D}\bigr]=\frac{1}{3}\Bigl(2[X^{2},S^{-2}]+X[S^{-1},X]S^{-1}-S^{-1}[X,S^{-1}]X\Bigr).

The commutator between
X
and
S^{-1}
can be simplified, using the commutation relation
[H,S^{-1}]=-S^{-1}
and the fact that
S^{-1}
commutes with any expression in
Z
:

\begin{split}[X,S^{-1}]=-\frac{1}{2}\bigg[\Big\{H,\frac{Z}{1-Z}\Big\},S^{-1}\bigg]=-\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\big[H,S^{-1}\big],\frac{Z}{1-Z}\Big\}=S^{-1}\frac{Z}{1-Z}.\end{split}

Thus, we find
[E,\tilde{D}]=\frac{1}{3}(2[X^{2},S^{-2}]-\{X,S^{-2}\frac{Z}{1-Z}\})=\frac{1}{3}(2[X^{2},S^{-2}]-\frac{t}{2u}\{X,\frac{1}{1-Z}\})
. A lengthy but straightforward computation shows that

(1)

[X^{2},S^{-2}]=\frac{t}{u}\{X,\frac{1}{1-Z}\}

(2)

\{X,\frac{1}{1-Z}\}=-\{H,\frac{Z}{(1-Z)^{2}}\}

Thus, we conclude as

\frac{1}{t}[E,\tilde{D}]=\frac{1}{3t}\bigg(-\frac{2t}{u}\Big\{H,\frac{Z}{(1-Z)^{2}}\Big\}+\frac{t}{2u}\Big\{H,\frac{Z}{(1-Z)^{2}}\Big\}\bigg)=-\frac{1}{2u}\Big\{H,\frac{Z}{(1-Z)^{2}}\Big\}=-\frac{d\tilde{D}}{du},

hence proving
equation

4.13
. As
equation

4.14
has no singularity at
u=0
, there does indeed exist a unique solution such that
W|_{u=0}=1
. This solution is upper unitriangular because
E
is strictly upper-triangular. On the other hand, by direct computation,

\frac{d}{du}\big(W\tilde{D}W^{-1}D^{-1}\big)=W\Bigl(\frac{1}{t}[E,\tilde{D}]+\frac{d\tilde{D}}{du}\Bigr)W^{-1}D^{-1}=0.

Together with
\tilde{D}|_{u=0}=S^{-1}+H=D
and
W|_{u=0}=1
, we get
W^{-1}DW=\tilde{D}
for all
u
.
∎

Define the operators

(4.15)

Theorem 4.10

Let

Lemma 4.11
.

As elements of
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}^{B}
,

(4.18)

\bigl[\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}(z),\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}(w)\bigr]=\frac{zw}{u}\delta(z,-w).

Proof.

We may set
t=1
for the proof by homogeneity. By definition,

\begin{split}\bigl[\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}(z),\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}(w)\bigr]&=\frac{1}{u^{2}}\bigg[\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(uz)^{k}}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}}\alpha^{B}_{2k-1},\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(uw)^{l}}{(w^{\prime}+1)_{l}}\alpha^{B}_{2l-1}\bigg]\\
&=\frac{1}{u^{2}}\sum_{k,l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(uz)^{k}}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}}\frac{(uw)^{l}}{(w^{\prime}+1)_{l}}(k-\tfrac{1}{2})\delta_{k+l-1}\\
&=\frac{w}{u}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(k-\tfrac{1}{2})\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}}\frac{1}{(w^{\prime}+1)_{1-k}}.\end{split}

Let us split this sum in positive
k
and non-negative
k
:
[\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}(z),\widetilde{\mathsf{B}}(w)]=C_{+}+C_{-}
. Then

\begin{split}\Big(1+\frac{z}{w}\Big)C_{+}&=\Big(1+\frac{z}{w}\Big)\frac{w}{u}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(k-\tfrac{1}{2})\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}(w^{\prime}+1)_{1-k}}\\
&=\frac{w}{u}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k}\frac{k-\tfrac{1}{2}}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}(w^{\prime}+1)_{1-k}}+\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k+1}\frac{k-\tfrac{1}{2}}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}(w^{\prime}+1)_{1-k}}\bigg)\\
&=\frac{w}{u}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k}\Big(\frac{1}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k-1}(w^{\prime}+1)_{1-k}}-\frac{z}{2(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}(w^{\prime}+1)_{1-k}}\Big)\\
&\qquad+\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k+1}\Big(-\frac{1}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}(w^{\prime}+1)_{-k}}+\frac{w}{2(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}(w^{\prime}+1)_{1-k}}\Big)\bigg)\\
&=\frac{w}{u}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k}\frac{1}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k-1}(w^{\prime}+1)_{1-k}}-\Big(\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k+1}\frac{1}{(z^{\prime}+1)_{k}(w^{\prime}+1)_{-k}}\bigg)\\
&=\frac{z}{u}.\end{split}

So we obtain

C_{+}=\frac{z}{u(1+z/w)}=\frac{z}{u}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(-\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k}.

Similar computations show that

C_{-}=-\frac{w}{u(1+w/z)}=-\frac{w}{u}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{0}\Big(-\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{l}=\frac{z}{u}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-1}\Big(-\frac{z}{w}\Big)^{k}.\qed

6.
The 2-BKP hierarchy

In this last section, we discuss the integrability property of the equivariant tau-function, as well as the constraints given by the divisor and string equations.

6.1.
Divisor and string equations

Proposition 6.1

(Divisor equation)
.

We have

(6.1)

[z_{0}]\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{g,d}(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{n},w_{1},\dots,w_{m})=2\left(\frac{1}{24}+d+t\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\right)\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{g,d}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},w_{1},\dots,w_{m}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of
[
OP06a
, proposition 12]
. Employing the neutral fermion description one obtains

[z_{0}]\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{g,d}(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{n},w_{1},\dots,w_{m})=[z_{0}]\Braket{\mathsf{B}(z_{0})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{B}(z_{i})e^{\alpha_{1}^{B}}\mathcal{P}_{d}e^{\alpha_{-1}^{B}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathsf{B}(w_{j})^{\star}}.

From
definition

4.1
it follows that
[z]\mathsf{B}(z)=2(\alpha^{B}_{1}+\frac{1}{24}+\cdots)
, where the dots represent summands which are killed by the covacuum. Hence:

[z]\Bra{\mathsf{B}(z)}=2\Bra{\left(\alpha^{B}_{1}+\frac{1}{24}\right)}=2\Bra{\left(\alpha^{B}_{1}+\frac{1}{24}+H\right)},

where the energy operator
H
can be added as it also gets annihilated by the covacuum. Notice that the following commutation holds:
[\alpha^{B}_{1}+H,\mathsf{B}(z)]=tz\mathsf{B}(z)
. This allows us to move
\alpha^{B}_{1}+H
to the right of the
\mathsf{B}
-operators in the vacuum expectation value, generating a factor of
t\sum_{i}z_{i}
in the process. Moreover, by employing the commutation relations
[H,\alpha^{B}_{1}]=-\alpha^{B}_{1}
and
H\mathcal{P}_{d}=d\mathcal{P}_{d}
, one obtains that

(\alpha^{B}_{1}+H)e^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{d}=de^{\alpha^{B}_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{d}.

This generates the last factor of
d
in
equation

6.1
.
∎

Proposition 6.2

(String equation)
.

We have

(6.2)

(-1)^{g-1+d}2^{3g-3+n+m+2d}\Braket{e^{\tau_{0}(1)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\mathbf{0})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\tau_{l_{j}}(\bm{\infty})}_{g,d}^{\bullet,\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1)}=\\
=\Biggl[\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}^{l_{j}+1}\Biggr]e^{\sum_{i}z_{i}+\sum_{j}w_{j}}\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{g,d}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},w_{1},\dots,w_{m}).

Proof.

The proof is the same as in
[
OP06a
, proposition 13]
, we repeat here some steps for reader’s convenience. Recall that in the localised equivariant cohomology of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
the identity class satisfies the relation
1=\frac{\mathbf{0}-\bm{\infty}}{t}
, which can be used as

\ev_{0}^{*}(1)=\frac{\ev_{0}^{*}(\mathbf{0})-\ev_{0}^{*}(\bm{\infty})}{t}.

By the divisor equation above we compute:

\begin{split}&(-1)^{g-1+d}2^{3g-3+n+m+2d}\Braket{\tau_{0}(1)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\mathbf{0})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\tau_{l_{j}}(\bm{\infty})}_{g,d}^{\bullet,\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1)}=\\
&\quad=(-1)^{g-1+d}2^{3g-3+n+m+2d}\Braket{\frac{\tau_{0}(\mathbf{0})-\tau_{0}(\bm{\infty})}{t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{k_{i}}(\mathbf{0})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\tau_{l_{j}}(\bm{\infty})}_{g,d}^{\bullet,\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}(-1)}\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{2t}\Biggl[z_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}^{l_{j}+1}\Biggr]\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{g,d}(z_{0},z,w)-\frac{1}{2t}\Biggl[w_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}^{l_{j}+1}\Biggr]\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{g,d}(z,w_{0},w)\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{2t}\Biggl[\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}^{l_{j}+1}\Biggr]\Bigg(2\Biggl(\frac{1}{24}+d+t\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\Biggr)-2\Biggl(\frac{1}{24}+d-t\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}\Biggr)\Bigg)\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{g,d}(z,w)\\
&\quad=\Biggl[\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{k_{i}+1}\prod_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}^{l_{j}+1}\Biggr]\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}\Bigg)\mathbb{G}^{\bullet}_{g,d}(z,w).\end{split}

Iterating this concludes the proof.
∎

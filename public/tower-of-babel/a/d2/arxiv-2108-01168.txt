1
Introduction

A digraph
D
is
semicomplete
if every two different vertices in
D
are joined by at least one arc.
A
tournament
is a semicomplete digraph without symmetric arcs.
The class of tournaments is one of the most well-studied classes of digraphs with many deeply and important results and applications, and provide a useful class of digraphs which allows of to have a first approach to solve very difficult problems.
As a consequence, several authors defined some new classes of digraphs which are generalizations of tournaments, commonly called nearly tournaments.
For instance, the class of
local in-tournaments
(
local out-tournaments
) was introduced by Bang-Jensen in
[
8
]
, as a digraph
D
such that for every
x\in V(D)
, the induced subdigraph of
N^{-}(x)
(respectively
N^{+}(x)
) is a tournament. A
local tournament
is a digraph which is both local in-tournament and local out-tournament. Several results related with local tournaments have been proved. For a deeply study of local tournaments, see
[
5
]
.

Other digraphs related with nearly tournaments are the multipartite tournaments. An
r
-partite tournament
(
r\geq 2
) is a digraph
D
such that
V(D)
can be partitioned into
r
disjoint independent sets, and every two vertices in different classes are joined by an asymmetric arc. Multipartite tournaments were considered by Moon in
[
34
]
, and several authors studied such class of digraphs. For a deeply study of multipartite tournaments, see
[
5
]
.

A digraph
D
is
r
-transitive
(
r\geq 2
) if for every
\{u,v\}\subseteq V(D)
, whenever there exists a
uv
-path with length
r
, we have that
(u,v)\in A(D)
. When

, an

-transitive digraph is called

. The class of

-transitive digraphs is one of the most studied nearly tournaments, due their particularly nice structure, and several results related with such digraphs arose. For instance, see
[
5
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
29
]
and
[
38
]
.

A digraph

is

if for every
\{u,v\}\subseteq V(D)
, whenever there exists a
uv
-path with length
2
, we have that

and

are joined by an arc.
Quasi-transitive digraphs were introduced by Ghouila-Houri in
[
27
]
as a consequence of their relation with comparability graphs, and it is one of the most studied class of digraphs, probably the main reason is the characterization theorem showed by Bang-Jensen and J. Huang in
[
7
]
.
Bang-Jensen
[

introduced the family of

-quasi-transitive digraphs in the context of strong arc-locally semicomplete digraphs. A digraph

if for every

, whenever there exists a

-path with length

) if for every

, whenever there exists a

, we have that

are joined by an arc. This class were introduced by Galeana-Sánchez and Hernández-Cruz

The concept of

was introduced by von Neumann and Morgenstern in
[

]
as a subset

, such that for every pair of different vertices in

[

]
,
[

]

[

Theorem 1.1

(König

)

Every transitive digraph has a kernel.

Theorem 1.2

(Duchet
[

]
)

If

]

]
,
[

]
,
[

]

[

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
D
be an
H
-colored
r

Proof.

First, we will show that every cycle in

(k,H)

is a closed walk and

For every

-walk in

with length at most

(b)), contradicting the choice of

In order to present our proof more compact, we will show the following claims.

Claim 2.

(x_{q+2},u_{1})\in A(D)

(x_{q+2},u_{1})\notin A(D)

is an
r

. Notice that

.
Since every
u_{i}u_{i+1}
-geodesic has length at most
2
, then
(x_{q},x_{q+1},x_{q+2},x_{q+3})
has at least two consecutive vertices in
C
, say
u_{t}
and
u_{t+1}
. It follows from Lemma
2.1
(c) that
(u_{t},u_{t+1})
is a symmetric arc in
C_{H}^{k-1}(D)
, which is no possible by the choice of

There exists
t\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
such that
x_{q+1}=u_{t}
and
x_{q+3}=u_{t+1}

First, we will show that
(x_{q},x_{q+1},x_{q+2})
has no two consecutive vertices of
C
…(*). Proceeding by contradiction, suppose that
(x_{q},x_{q+1},x_{q+2})

3.7
Local tournaments

The following lemma will be useful in what follows.

Lemma 3.21
.

[
8
]

Every pair of vertices in each strong component of a local in-tournament (out-tournament) lie on a cycle.

As a consequence of the previous lemma, we have the following result in
H
-colored local in-tournaments.

Theorem 3.22
.

Let

be an
H
-colored local in-tournament. If every cycle in

has

-length at most

(

(k,H)
-kernel.

Proof.

First, we will show that every cycle in

has a symmetric arc, then, by applying Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
2.3
, we will conclude that
D
has a
(k,H)
-kernel.

Let
C=(u_{0},\ldots,u_{n})
be a cycle in
C_{H}^{k-1}(D)
, and for every
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
, consider a
u_{i}u_{i+1}
-path with
H
-length at most
k-1
, say
T_{i}
(indices are taken modulo
n
). Clearly,
C^{\prime}=\cup_{i=0}^{n-1}T_{i}
is a closed walk in

containing

and

, which implies that

and

lie in the same strong component of

. It follows from Lemma

that there exists a cycle in

, such that

. Suppose that

). By hypothesis, we conclude that

Therefore, we have that every cycle in

∎

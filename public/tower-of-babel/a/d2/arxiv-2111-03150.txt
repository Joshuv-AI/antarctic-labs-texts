Conditions for Eliminating Cusps in One-phase free-boundary problems with degeneracy
Sean McCurdy

Abstract
. In this paper, we continue the study local minimizers of a degenerate version of the Alt-Caffarelli functional. Specifically, we consider local minimizers of the functional J Q ( u , Ω ) := ∫ Ω | ∇ u | 2 + Q ( x ) 2 χ { u > 0 } d x J_{Q}(u,\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+Q(x)^{2}\chi_{\{u>0\}}dx where Q ⁡ ( x ) = d ​ i ​ s ​ t ​ ( x , Γ ) γ Q(x)=dist(x,\Gamma)^{\gamma} for γ > 0 \gamma>0 and Γ \Gamma a submanifold of dimension 0 ≤ k ≤ n − 1 0\leq k\leq n-1 . Previously, it was shown that on Γ \Gamma , the free boundary ∂ { u > 0 } \partial\{u>0\} may be decomposed into a rectifiable set 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , which satisfies effective estimates, and a cusp set Σ \Sigma . In this note, we prove that under mild assumptions, in the case n = 2 n=2 and Γ \Gamma a line, the cusp set Σ \Sigma does not exist. Building upon the work of Arama and Leoni [ AL12 ] , our results apply to the physical case of a variational formulation of the Stokes’ wave and provide a complete characterization of the singular portion of the free boundary in complete generality in this context.

Definition 4.1

Lemma 4.2
.

(Increase Estimate)
Let

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla u_{t}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(R)}\leq\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(R)}(1+t+t^{2}).

Proof.

Thus, the following estimate gives the desired estimate.

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{1}u+t\partial_{2}u)^{2}+(\partial_{2}u)^{2}

Lemma 4.3
.

(Decrease Estimate)

Thus

\displaystyle(y_{2}^{2\gamma}-y_{1}^{2\gamma})

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{1}}^{y_{2}}2\gamma y^{2\gamma-1}dy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{y_{1}}^{y_{2}}y^{2\gamma}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\gamma+1}(y_{2}^{2\gamma+1}-y_{1}^{2\gamma+1}).

If
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
, then by assumption
(ii.)
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in[0,2\gamma]
. We note that for
y\in[0,2\gamma]
,
2\gamma y^{2\gamma-1}\geq y^{2\gamma}
. Thus, for
y_{1},y_{2}\in[0,2\gamma]
, the previous estimate holds.

Therefore, we may sum over all the intervals
(y_{2i-1},y_{2i})\in\Omega_{x}
and obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i}-a(y_{2i}^{2\gamma}-y_{2i-1}^{2\gamma})\leq-a\int_{\Omega_{x}}y^{2\gamma}dy.

Integrating with respect to
x\in[a,b]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\int_{F_{-t}(\Omega)}Q^{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{b}-a\int_{\Omega_{x}}y^{2\gamma}dy

\displaystyle\leq-a\int_{\Omega}Q^{2}(x)dx.

∎

6.

for all sufficiently small

\displaystyle:=I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t).

in

. Therefore, by the assumption of local minimality, for all

We shall prove the main theorem by showing that for all sufficiently small

By Lemma
4.2
, and Corollary

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq(1+t+t^{2})I_{1}(0)

\displaystyle\leq I_{1}(0)+2(t+t^{2})NC^{2}(2,\gamma).

To obtain the necessary decrease estimate, we decompose

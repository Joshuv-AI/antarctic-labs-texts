Integrability, geometry and wave solutions of some Kairat equations
Zh. Myrzakulova, S. Manukure, R. Myrzakulov, G. Nugmanova

Abstract
In this paper, we study some Kairat equations. The relation between the motion of curves and Kairat equations is established. The geometrical equivalence between the Kairat-I equation and the Kairat-II equation is proved. We also proves that these equations is gauge equivalent to each other. Three types traveling wave solutions of the Kairat-II equation as well as its some integrals of motion are found. The techniques used in this paper can be adopted to study other integrable spin systems and nonlinear models. In particular, using these methods we study some Zhanbota equations. Key words :integrable systems, Lax pair, soliton, Heisenberg ferromagnet equation, Kairat-I equation, gauge equivalence, Zhanbota-IV equation, geometrical equivalence, Kairat-II equation, travelling wave solutions, integrals of motion; Zhanbota-III equation.

The Kairat-I equation

The Kairat-I equation has the following form

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{xt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u_{xt}}{u_{xxt}}(2u_{t}{\bf S}_{x}-{\bf S}_{t})+2(4u_{x}u_{t}-u_{xxt}){\bf S}+2u_{xt}{\bf S}\wedge{\bf S}_{x},

(2.1)

\displaystyle u_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}{\bf S}^{2}_{x},

(2.2)

where
{\bf S}(x,t)
is the unit spin vector that is
{\bf S}=(S_{1},S_{2},S_{3})
and
{\bf S}^{2}=S_{1}^{2}+S_{2}^{2}+S_{3}^{2}=1
,
u(x,t)
is a real function (potential). The Kairat-I equation is integrable. The corresponding Lax representation of the Kairat-I equation reads as

\displaystyle\Psi_{x}=U_{1}\Psi,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}=V_{1}\Psi,

(2.4)

where
U_{1}
and

are

S
,

and their finite-order derivatives with respect to
x
and
t
,
\Psi(x,t)
is a
2\times 2
matrix function. The matrices
U_{1}
and
V_{1}
have the following forms

\displaystyle U_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{u_{xxt}}(2u_{t}S_{x}-S_{t}),

(2.5)

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{1-4\lambda}\left(\frac{u_{t}+2u_{xxt}}{u_{xxt}}(2u_{t}S_{x}-S_{t})-u_{xt}S+\frac{1}{2}u_{x}[S,S_{x}]\right),

(2.6)

where

\displaystyle S=\begin{pmatrix}S_{3}&S^{-}\\
S^{+}&-S_{3}\end{pmatrix},\quad S^{2}=I,\quad S^{\pm}=S_{1}\pm iS_{2},\quad S^{+}S^{-}+S_{3}^{2}=1.

(2.7)

The compatibility condition of the linear equations (

)-(
2.4

U_{1t}-V_{1x}+[U_{1},V_{1}]=0

\displaystyle S_{xt}

\displaystyle\frac{u_{xt}}{u_{xxt}}(2u_{t}S_{x}-S_{t})+2(4u_{x}u_{t}-u_{xxt})S+u_{xt}[S,S_{x}],

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}tr(S^{2}_{x}).

)-(
2.2

\displaystyle U_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{-1}U_{2}g-g^{-1}g_{x},

(5.2)

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{-1}V_{2}g-g^{-1}g_{t}.

This results proves the gauge equivalence between the Kairat-I equation (

)-(

Travelling solutions

In this section we are going to present exact three travelling wave solutions of the Kairat-II equation (4.1): elliptical, soliton and rational solutions. Let us find the travelling wave solution of the K-IIE (4.1). To do that instead of the independent variables
(x,t)
, let us introduce the new independent variable
y=ax+bt+c
, where
a,b,c
are real constants. So we have
u(x,t)=u(y)

\displaystyle u^{{}^{\prime}}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial y},\quad u_{t}=bu^{{}^{\prime}},\quad u_{x}=au^{{}^{\prime}},\quad u_{xx}=a^{2}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}},\quad u_{xt}=abu^{{}^{\prime\prime}},\quad u_{xxxt}=a^{3}bu^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}.

(6.1)

After some simple algebraic manipulations of the following forms of the K-II equation (4.1), we obtain:

\displaystyle abu^{{}^{\prime\prime}}-6a^{2}bu^{{}^{\prime}}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}+a^{3}bu^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}=0,

(6.2)

\displaystyle u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}-3a(u^{{}^{\prime}2})^{{}^{\prime}}+a^{2}u^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}}=0,

(6.3)

\displaystyle u^{{}^{\prime}}-3au^{{}^{\prime}2}+a^{2}u^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}+n=0,

(6.4)

\displaystyle 2u^{{}^{\prime}}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}-6au^{{}^{\prime}2}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}+2a^{2}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}}u^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}+2nu^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=0,

(6.5)

\displaystyle(u^{{}^{\prime}2})^{{}^{\prime}}-2a(u^{{}^{\prime}3})^{{}^{\prime}}+a^{2}(u^{{}^{\prime\prime}2})^{{}^{\prime}}+2nu^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=0.

(6.6)

Finally we get the following ordinary differential equation

\displaystyle u^{{}^{\prime}2}-2au^{{}^{\prime}3}+a^{2}u^{{}^{\prime\prime}2}+2nu^{{}^{\prime}}+m=0,

(6.7)

where
m
and
n
are real integration constants. Let
u^{{}^{\prime}}(y)=f(y)
, where
f(y)
is a real function. Then the last equation takes the form

\displaystyle a^{2}f^{{}^{\prime}2}-2af^{3}+f^{2}+2nf+m=0.

(6.8)

Our next step is
f(\zeta)=\mu+kh(\zeta)
, where
\mu,k
are some real constants and
h(y)
is a real function of
y
. Then step by step we obtain following equations for the functions
h(y)
:

\displaystyle a^{2}k^{2}h^{{}^{\prime}2}-2a(\mu+kh)^{3}+(\mu+kh)^{2}+2n(\mu+kh)+m=0,

(6.9)

\displaystyle a^{2}k^{2}h^{{}^{\prime}2}-2a(\mu^{3}+3\mu^{2}kh+3\mu k^{2}h^{2}+k^{3}h^{3})+(\mu^{2}+2\mu kh+k^{2}h^{2})+2n(\mu+kh)+m=0,

(6.10)

\displaystyle a^{2}k^{2}h^{{}^{\prime}2}=2ak^{3}h^{3}+(6a\mu k^{2}-k^{2})h^{2}+(6a\mu^{2}k-2\mu k-2nk)h+(2a\mu^{3}-\mu^{2}-2n\mu-m)=0,

(6.11)

\displaystyle a^{2}k^{2}h^{{}^{\prime}2}=2ak^{3}h^{3}+k^{2}(6a\mu-1)h^{2}+k(6a\mu^{2}-2\mu-2n)h+(2a\mu^{3}-\mu^{2}-2n\mu-m)=0,

(6.12)

\displaystyle h^{{}^{\prime}2}=\frac{2k}{a}h^{3}+a^{-2}(6a\mu-1)h^{2}+\frac{(6a\mu^{2}-2\mu-2n)}{a^{2}k}h+\frac{(2a\mu^{3}-\mu^{2}-2n\mu-m)}{a^{2}k^{2}}=0.

(6.13)

6.1
Elliptic solution

Now we introduce two constants
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
as:

\displaystyle g_{2}=-\frac{(6a\mu^{2}-2\mu-2n)}{a^{2}k},\quad g_{3}=-\frac{(2a\mu^{3}-\mu^{2}-2n\mu-m)}{a^{2}k^{2}}.

(6.14)

Then the unknown integration constants

\displaystyle n=a^{2}kg_{2}+3a\mu^{2}-\mu,\quad m=g_{3}a^{2}k^{2}+2a\mu^{3}-\mu^{2}-2n\mu.

Now we assume that constants

\displaystyle k=2a,\quad\mu=\frac{1}{6a}.

(6.16)

Then the equation (

\displaystyle\wp^{\prime 2}(y)=4\wp^{3}(y)-g_{2}\wp(y)-g_{3}.

Thus we obtain

Rational solution

Conclusion

The goal of this paper is to attract more attention to some Kairat equations. These Kairat equations are integrable are intimately related to the very known equations.
In the present paper, we provided a geometrical description some of integrable Kairat equations. It is done in two

Introduction

In this work, we study the Keller-Segel model with logistic sources in dimension two

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla c\right)+u\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}udx\right),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},~t\geq 0,\\
-\Delta c=u,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},~t\geq 0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.\end{array}\right.

Here the initial mass is defined as
m_{0}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{0}(x)dx.
This model is developed to describe the biological phenomenon chemotaxis
[
8
,
10
]
by introducing nonlocal terms in the logistic growth factor. The first equation states the random (Brownian) diffusion of the cells with a bias directed by the chemoattractant concentration. The chemoattractant
c
is directly released by the cells, diffuses on the substrate and can be expressed

\displaystyle c(x,t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(y,t)\log|x-y|dy.

(1.4)

The model at hand supposes that the cells production with logistic sources. Here
M_{0}
can be viewed as the mass capacity
[
12
]
and sometimes also called Malthusian parameter, induces an exponential growth for low density populations
[
9
]
. The nonlocal term
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}udx
describes the influence of the total mass of the species in the growth of the population, which is a competitive term limiting such growth
[
9
]
.

As pointed out in
[
6
,
12
]
, in the absence of the reaction term
u\left(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}udx\right)
, (
1
) has the property that the total mass is conserved
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{0}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x,t)dx
and the solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta u-\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla c\right),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},~t\geq 0,\\
-\Delta c=u,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},~t\geq 0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.\end{array}\right.

exists globally when the initial mass
m_{0}<8\pi,
while the solution becomes a singular measure for
m_{0}>8\pi.
A natural problem is that does the nonlocal reaction change this effect?

To analyze this issue, we consider the total mass

\displaystyle m(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u(x,t)dx

(1.8)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{dm(t)}{dt}=m(t)\left(M_{0}-m(t)\right)

(1.9)

and we obtain

\displaystyle m(t)=\frac{M_{0}}{1+\overline{C}e^{-M_{0}t}}.

(1.10)

where

\displaystyle\overline{C}=\frac{M_{0}-m_{0}}{m_{0}}.

(1.11)

We find that the mass increases in time when
m_{0}<M_{0}
and versus when
m_{0}>M_{0},
in both cases it converges to
M_{0}
as
t\to\infty.

The aim of this paper is to explore the influence of
M_{0},m_{0}
on the dynamics of solutions when the nonlocal reaction is present.

In order to put our system in perspective, we recall some related cases. Actually, chemotaxis models with local sources (where
u(M_{0}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}udx)
is replaced by
a_{0}-u(a_{1}-a_{2}u^{\gamma})
have been studied recently, which describes the situation where the influence of the nonlocal terms is neglected, and the global existence results have been obtained for different parameters
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}
, see
[
7
,
9
,
11
,
15
]
and the references therein. Logistic growth described by nonlocal terms has been used in the context of chemotaxis, which suggests a growth coefficient rate in a competitive system modelling cancer cells behavior. For instance, the nonlocal term in
[
13
]
is given in the form
\mu_{1}u\left(1-\int_{\Omega}k_{1}(x,y)u(y)dy\right)
where

13

To illustrate our results, we observe that the striking feature of (

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u_{\varepsilon}-K)_{+}^{p+1}dx+K(2p-1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u_{\varepsilon}-K)_{+}^{p}dx+K^{2}p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(u_{\varepsilon}-K)_{+}^{p-1}dx

Moreover,

\displaystyle\leq\int_{K\leq u_{\varepsilon}\leq K+1}1dx+\int_{u_{\varepsilon}>K+1}(u_{\varepsilon}-K)_{+}^{p}dx

Lemma 2.5

(The Second Moment)
.

Assume
\int_{R^{2}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx<\infty
and

. Let

\displaystyle m_{2}(t):=\int_{R^{2}}|x|^{2}u(x,t)dx=m(t)\left[4t-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\left(\frac{m_{0}}{M_{0}}e^{M_{0}t}+\frac{M_{0}-m_{0}}{M_{0}}\right)+\frac{\int_{R^{2}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx}{m_{0}}\right]

where

We notice that the second moment evolution is more complicated than in the classical system corresponding to mass conservation where the time derivative of the second moment is a constant. An easy consequence of the previous lemma is the following blow-up result.

Theorem 2.6

(Finite time blow-up for
M_{0}>8\pi
)
.

Assume
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx<\infty
. If

\displaystyle\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{p}=\infty,~~\mbox{for any}~~p>1.

(2.74)

Proof.

Our proof hinges on the second moment evolution. Firstly for
M_{0}>8\pi,
the second moment
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u(x,t)dx
can be estimated that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u(x,t)dx\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(4-\frac{M_{0}}{2\pi}\right)M_{0}t+\frac{M_{0}}{m_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx-\frac{M_{0}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{m_{0}}{M_{0}}&\mbox{for}~m_{0}\leq M_{0},\\[5.69054pt]
\left(4-\frac{M_{0}}{2\pi}\right)m_{0}t+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u_{0}(x)dx,&\mbox{for}~m_{0}>M_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Consider
M_{0}>8\pi
, it enables us to get that the second moment of the weak solution will become negative after some time and contradicts the non-negativity of
u

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x,t)dx=\int_{|x|\leq R}u(x,t)dx+\int_{|x|>R}u(x,t)dx\leq CR^{2(p-1)/p}||u||_{L^{p}}+\frac{1}{R^{2}}m_{2}(t),~for~all~p>1.

(2.77)

Choosing

\displaystyle||u||_{L^{1}}\leq C||u||_{L^{p}}^{\frac{2}{a+2}}m_{2}(t)^{\frac{a}{a+2}},

(2.78)

which induces that

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T^{*}}||u||_{L^{p}}\geq\lim_{t\to T^{*}}\frac{||u(x,t)||_{L^{1}}^{\frac{a+2}{2}}}{Cm_{2}(t)^{\frac{a}{2}}}=\infty.

\displaystyle\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}>1~~~\mbox{and}~~~M(0,r)<\mu\bar{M}_{\lambda_{0}}(r),~\forall r>0.

(4.15)

In view of (
1.10
) and
m_{0}<M_{0}
, we also have

\displaystyle m_{0}<m(t)<M_{0}

(4.16)

for any
0<t<\infty
. Then we look for a supersolution to (
4.1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{N}(t,r)=\min\left(1,\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}\right)}\right)

(4.17)

where

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=\lambda_{0}+\frac{M_{0}}{\mu\pi}\left(1-\mu\right)\left(\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}-1\right)t.

(4.18)

Furthermore, we may define
R(t)
as

\displaystyle 1=\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)R(t)^{-2}\right)}

(4.19)

which gives rise to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r\geq R(t),&\bar{N}=1,\\
r\leq R(t),&\bar{N}=\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}\right)}.\end{array}\right.

Now we claim that
\bar{N}(t,r)
is a supersolution to (
4.1
). Denote the operator
L

\displaystyle L(\bar{N}):=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{N}-r\left(\frac{\bar{N}^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{m(t)}{2\pi r}\bar{N}^{\prime}\bar{N}.

(4.22)

For
r\leq R(t)
, with the fact (
4.16
) we derive that

\displaystyle L(\bar{N})=L\left(\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{N}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(-\lambda^{\prime}(t)+\frac{8\lambda(t)r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(1-\mu\frac{m(t)}{M_{0}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\bar{N}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(-\lambda^{\prime}(t)+\frac{8\lambda(t)R(t)^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)R(t)^{-2}}\left(1-\mu\frac{m(t)}{M_{0}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{N}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(-\lambda^{\prime}(t)+\frac{M_{0}}{\mu\pi}\left(1-\mu\frac{m(t)}{M_{0}}\right)\left(\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\bar{N}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(-\lambda^{\prime}(t)+\frac{M_{0}}{\mu\pi}\left(1-\mu\right)\left(\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle=0.

(4.23)

Here the last line holds by the choice (
4.18
) of
\lambda(t)
. We also find that for
r>R(t)
,
L(\bar{N})=L(1)=0
. Hence recalling
M(0,r)\leq M(0,\infty)=1
and (
4.15
) respectively, the comparison principle implies that

\displaystyle M(t,r)\leq\min\left(1,\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}\right)}\right)=\bar{N}(t,r)

(4.24)

for all
0\leq t<\infty
and
0\leq r<\infty.
Moreover, by (
4.18
) we find

\displaystyle\lambda(t)\to\infty,

(4.25)

then
R(t)\to\infty
for
t
large enough. Therefore, for any given interval
r\in(0,R)
we have

\displaystyle M(t,r)\leq\mu\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)R^{-2}\right)}\to 0~~\mbox{as}~~t\to\infty.

(4.26)

Thus completes the proof.
\Box

4.2.2
Infinite time blow-up for
M_{0}>8\pi

Next we prove a refined blow-up result (with a weaker condition than the second moment condition) which is close enough to exhibit the shape of the chemotactic collapse solution.

Lemma 4.2

(Blow-up)
.

Assume
M_{0}<m_{0}
and
\bar{M}_{\lambda_{0}}(r)
is defined as (
4.11
) for some
\lambda_{0}>0
. If

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}<1,\quad M(0,r)>\bar{M}_{\lambda_{0}}(r)

(4.27)

for any
r>0
and there is no finite time blow-up, then the solution to (
4.1
) blows up in infinite time. Furthermore, the solution has a mass concentration at the origin, that’s

\displaystyle M(t,r(t))>\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}

(4.28)

for
r(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

Since
\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}<1
, We firstly choose
1<\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}<\mu_{1}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}<1~~~\mbox{and}~~~M(0,r)>\mu_{1}\bar{M}_{\lambda_{0}}(r)>\mu_{0}\bar{M}_{\lambda_{0}}(r),~\forall r>0.

(4.29)

Combining
M_{0}<m_{0}
with (
1.10
) also leads to

\displaystyle M_{0}<m(t)<m_{0}.

(4.30)

We consider the function

\displaystyle\underline{N}(t,r)=\max\left(\mu_{1}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda_{0}r^{-2}\right)},\mu_{0}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}\right)}\right),

(4.31)

where
\lambda(t)=\lambda_{0}e^{At}
for some
A<0,
we will prove that
\underline{N}
is a subsolution as it is the maximum of two subsolutions.

Firstly we denote
N_{1}:=\mu_{1}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda_{0}r^{-2}\right)}
and compute

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}N_{1}-r\left(\frac{N_{1}^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{m(t)}{2\pi r}N_{1}^{\prime}N_{1}

\displaystyle=N_{1}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda_{0}r^{-2}}\frac{8\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{0}+r^{2}}\left(1-\mu_{1}\frac{m(t)}{M_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq N_{1}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda_{0}r^{-2}}\frac{8\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{0}+r^{2}}\left(1-\mu_{1}\right)

\displaystyle<0.

(4.32)

Then it follows from (
4.29
) that
N_{1}
is a subsolution to (
4.1
). Secondly, we have to prove that
\mu_{0}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}\right)}
is a subsolution in the interval
0<r\leq R(t)
where it achieves the maximum, i.e.

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda_{0}R(t)^{-2}\right)}=\mu_{0}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)R(t)^{-2}\right)}.

(4.33)

By the definition of
R(t)
, there exists a constant
R_{0}
such that
r\leq R(t)\leq R_{0}
with
\mu_{1}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda_{0}R_{0}^{-2}\right)}=\mu_{0}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}},
then using (
4.30
) and a direct calculation provide

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\underline{N}-r\left(\frac{\underline{N}^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{m(t)}{2\pi r}\underline{N}^{\prime}\underline{N}

\displaystyle=\underline{N}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(-\lambda^{\prime}(t)+\frac{8\lambda(t)}{\lambda(t)+r^{2}}\left(1-\mu_{0}\frac{m(t)}{M_{0}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underline{N}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(-\lambda^{\prime}(t)+\frac{8\lambda(t)}{\lambda(t)+r^{2}}\left(1-\mu_{0}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\underline{N}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(-\lambda^{\prime}(t)+\frac{8\lambda(t)R_{0}^{-2}}{\lambda_{0}R_{0}^{-2}+1}\left(1-\mu_{0}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\underline{N}\frac{r^{-2}}{1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}}\left(-\lambda^{\prime}(t)+\frac{8\mu_{0}R_{0}^{-2}\lambda(t)}{\mu_{1}}\left(1-\mu_{0}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=0

(4.34)

by choosing

\displaystyle\lambda(t)=\lambda_{0}e^{-\frac{8\mu_{0}R_{0}^{-2}}{\mu_{1}}(\mu_{0}-1)t}=\lambda_{0}e^{At}.

(4.35)

We notice that
\underline{N}=\mu_{1}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda_{0}r^{-2}\right)}
for
r>R(t)
. Now keeping (
4.29
) in mind, the comparison principle gives

\displaystyle M(t,r)\geq\underline{N}(t,r)\geq\mu_{0}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda(t)r^{-2}\right)}=\mu_{0}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda_{0}e^{At}r^{-2}\right)}.

(4.36)

Therefore, at
r=e^{\frac{A}{3}t}
we find

\displaystyle M(t,r)\geq\mu_{0}\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}\left(1+\lambda_{0}e^{\frac{A}{3}t}\right)}

(4.37)

and the subsequent mass concentration at
r=0

\displaystyle M(t,r(t))>\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}

(4.38)

for
r(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty.
This is our desired estimate.
\Box

) as follows.

.

Assume that the initial data
\rho(0,r)
is radially symmetric,
\bar{\rho}_{\lambda_{0}}(r)
is defined as (
4.12
) for some

M_{0}<8\pi

\displaystyle\rho(0,r)<\bar{\rho}_{\lambda_{0}}(r)

(4.39)

for any

then the radially symmetric solution of (
4.1
) vanishes in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

\displaystyle\rho(0,r)>\bar{\rho}_{\lambda_{0}}(r)

\displaystyle 2\pi\int_{0}^{r(t)}\rho(t,\sigma)\sigma d\sigma>\frac{8\pi}{M_{0}}

(4.41)

for

as

By using (

), we transfer Theorem
4.3
to the original system (
1
).

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
u_{0}(r)
is the radially symmetric initial data to (
1
).

If
m_{0}<M_{0}<8\pi

Appendix

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon},u\in L_{+}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u_{\varepsilon}dx<\infty,\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}udx<\infty,

(4.45)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{\varepsilon}|\log u_{\varepsilon}|dx<\infty,

(4.46)

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u~~(\varepsilon\to 0)~~\mbox{weakly~~in~~}L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

(4.47)

Then

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x)|\log u(x)|dx<\infty,

(4.48)

and

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x)\log u(x)dx\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\log u_{\varepsilon}(x)dx.

(4.49)

Proof.

In the following,
C,C_{1},C_{2}
stand for finite positive constants that are independent of
\varepsilon
. We will give the proof step by step.

(i)

We firstly show that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1+|x|^{\beta})u_{\varepsilon}(x)dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1+|x|^{\beta})u(x)dx,\quad\mbox{for~~any}~~0<\beta<2.

(4.50)

In fact, for any
R>1,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1+|x|^{\beta})(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x))dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1+|x|^{\beta})1_{\{|x|\leq R\}}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x))dx+\int_{|x|>R}(1+|x|^{\beta})u_{\varepsilon}(x)dx+\int_{|x|>R}(1+|x|^{\beta})u(x)dx\right|.

We deal with the last two terms as follows.

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>R}(1+|x|^{\beta})u_{\varepsilon}(x)dx+\int_{|x|>R}(1+|x|^{\beta})u(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(\int_{|x|>R}|x|^{\beta}u_{\varepsilon}(x)dx+\int_{|x|>R}|x|^{\beta}u(x)dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{R^{2-\beta}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x)dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u(x)dx\right)\leq\frac{C}{R^{2-\beta}}.

Since, by weak convergence,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1+|x|^{\beta})1_{\{|x|\leq R\}}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x))dx\right|\to 0,\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

It follows that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left|(1+|x|^{\beta})(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x))\right|\leq\frac{C}{R^{2-\beta}},\quad\forall R>1.

Letting
R\to\infty
leads to (
4.50
).

(ii)

Let’s prove

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x)|\log u(x)|dx<\infty.

(4.51)

Actually, by convexity of
y\mapsto y\log y,y\geq 0,
one has

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}\log u_{\varepsilon}\geq u\log u+(1+\log u)(u_{\varepsilon}-u).

From this we have, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle 1_{\{1\leq u(x)\leq m\}}u_{\varepsilon}\log u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1_{\{1\leq u(x)\leq m\}}u\log u+1_{\{1\leq u(x)\leq m\}}(1+\log u)(u_{\varepsilon}-u).

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{1\leq u(x)\leq m\}}u\log udx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{1\leq u(x)\leq m\}}(1+\log u)(u_{\varepsilon}-u)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{1\leq u(x)\leq m\}}u_{\varepsilon}\log u_{\varepsilon}dx

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{\varepsilon}|\log u_{\varepsilon}|dx=:C_{1}(<\infty).

Since, by weak convergence,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{1\leq u(x)\leq m\}}(1+\log u)(u_{\varepsilon}-u)dx=0,

it follows by passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{1\leq u(x)\leq m\}}u\log udx\leq C_{1}.

Then letting
m\to\infty
gives by Levi monotone convergence

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{u(x)\geq 1\}}u\log udx\leq C_{1}.

This together with (
2.30
) and the boundedness of
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|x|^{2}u(x)dx
yields

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x)|\log u(x)|dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{u(x)<1\}}u(x)|\log u(x)|dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}1_{\{u(x)\geq 1\}}u(x)|\log u(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{-|x|^{2}}dx+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x)|x|^{2}dx+C_{1}<\infty.

Hence we close the proof of (
4.51
).

(iii)

Next, to prove (
4.49
), we consider the truncation

\displaystyle g_{m}(x)=u(x)\wedge m+\frac{1}{m}e^{-|x|}.

By convexity we have

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}\log u_{\varepsilon}\geq g_{m}(x)\log g_{m}(x)+(1+\log g_{m}(x))(u_{\varepsilon}-g_{m}),

i.e.

\displaystyle g_{m}(x)\log g_{m}(x)\leq u_{\varepsilon}\log u_{\varepsilon}+(1+\log g_{m}(x))(g_{m}-u_{\varepsilon}).

(4.52)

We firstly use (
4.51
) to check the condition of
g_{m}\log g_{m}
for using Lebesgue dominated convergence, i.e.

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}g_{m}|\log g_{m}|dx\leq C_{2}.

(4.53)

By convexity of
y\mapsto y\log y
on
y\geq 0
one has

\displaystyle(a+b)\log(a+b)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}2a+\frac{1}{2}2b\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{2}2a+\frac{1}{2}2b\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}2a\log 2a+\frac{1}{2}2b\log 2b,~~a,b\geq 0.

(4.54)

We also have

\displaystyle(a+b)\log\frac{1}{a+b}\leq a\log\frac{1}{a}+b\log\frac{1}{b},~~a,b\geq 0.

(4.55)

Now we use (
Appendix
) to see that if
g_{m}\geq 1,
then, because
m\geq 4,
we must have
u(x)>1/2,
so that

\displaystyle g_{m}(x)|\log g_{m}(x)|=g_{m}(x)\log g_{m}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(u(x)\wedge m)\log(2u(x)\wedge m)+\frac{1}{m}e^{-|x|}\log\left(\frac{2}{m}e^{-|x|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle u(x)\log(2u(x))+\frac{1}{m}e^{-|x|}\frac{2}{m}e^{-|x|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle u(x)+u(x)|\log u(x)|+e^{-2|x|}.

While if
g_{m}(x)<1,
then
u(x)<1,
then by (
4.55
) we have

\displaystyle g_{m}(x)|\log g_{m}(x)|=\left(u(x)+\frac{1}{m}e^{-|x|}\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{u(x)+\frac{1}{m}e^{-|x|}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle u(x)|\log u(x)|+\frac{1}{m}e^{-|x|}\log(me^{|x|})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle u(x)|\log u(x)|+\frac{2}{\sqrt{m}}e^{-|x|/2},

where we used
\log y=2\log\sqrt{y}\leq 2\sqrt{y}
for
y\geq 0.
So we have proved an
L^{1}
control

\displaystyle g_{m}(x)|\log g_{m}(x)|\leq u(x)+u(x)|\log u(x)|+e^{-|x|/2}+e^{-2|x|}.

(4.56)

The aim next is to deal with the second term in the right hand side of (
4.52
). Using

\displaystyle u(x)\wedge m=u(x)-(u(x)-m)_{+},

we handle

\displaystyle(1+\log g_{m}(x))(g_{m}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\log g_{m}(x))(u(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x))-(1+\log g_{m}(x))(u(x)-m)_{+}+(1+\log g_{m}(x))\frac{1}{m}e^{-|x|}.

(4.57)

By considering
g_{m}(x)\geq 1
and
g_{m}(x)<1
we have

\displaystyle|1+\log g_{m}(x)|\leq 1+\left(\log(m+1)\vee(|x|+\log m)\right)\leq 2(1+|x|)\log(m+1).

Therefore we have

\displaystyle|1+\log g_{m}(x)|\frac{1}{m}e^{-|x|}\leq 2\frac{\log(m+1)}{m}(1+|x|)e^{-|x|},

(4.58)

and by (
4.50
),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1+\log g_{m}(x))(u(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x))=0.

(4.59)

Moreover we have

\displaystyle-(1+\log g_{m}(x))(u(x)-m)_{+}\leq 0.

(4.60)

In fact the case
u(x)\leq m
is obvious. For the case
u(x)>m
we have
g_{m}(x)>m>1,
so that
\log g_{m}(x)>0.
So (
4.60
) holds true.

From (
4.52
), collecting (
Appendix
)-(
4.60
) we have by taking lower limit
\varepsilon\to 0
that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}g_{m}(x)\log g_{m}(x)dx\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\log u_{\varepsilon}(x)dx+2\frac{\log(m+1)}{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(1+|x|)e^{-|x|}dx,\quad\forall m\geq 4.

(4.61)

Finally by continuity one has

\displaystyle g_{m}(x)\log g_{m}(x)\to u(x)\log u(x)\quad(m\to\infty).

(4.62)

Then it follows (
4.56
) and Lebesgue dominated convergence and (
4.61
) that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u(x)\log u(x)dx=\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}g_{m}(x)\log g_{m}(x)dx\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\log u_{\varepsilon}(x)dx.

(4.63)

Thus we close the proof.
\Box

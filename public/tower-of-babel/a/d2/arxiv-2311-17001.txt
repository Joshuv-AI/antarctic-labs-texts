2.3
Our Approach: Nice edges and Gap edges.

As described in the previous part, identifying robust characterizations of

Towards establishing the above, it is useful to adopt the probabilistic view point of pseudo-distributions

. A first step towards addressing issues (i) and (ii) outlined above is to use a (standard) alternative rounding scheme

3.2
Lasserre/SoS Hierarchy

The Lasserre/SoS hierarchy
[
Sho87
,
Las01
]
is a sequence of strengthening of the basic SDP which enforce constraints on subsets of variables of increasing sizes. Formally, given a basic SDP with variable set
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
, the
R
-round Lasserre relaxation of the SDP optimizes the objective over the set of variables
\{X_{S,\alpha}\}_{|S|\leq R,\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{S}}
where
X_{S,\alpha}
is meant to be the indicator of the event that the subset
S
gets assigned label
\alpha
. The variables
X_{S,\alpha}
are associated with the so called degree-
R
pseudo-distribution
\mu:=\{\mu_{S}\}_{S:|S|\leq R}
where
\mu_{S}
is a probability distribution over labelings for the variables in
S
. While the various local distributions
\{\mu_{S}\}
may be not be globally consistent, the hierarchy ensures that they are consistent upto
R
-sized subset of variables by enforcing constraints among the
\mu_{S}
variables. As is standard, we shall use the “widetilde” notation to denote various quantities defined with respect to the pseudo-distribution – for e.g., we use
\widetilde{\Pr}
notation to denote the pseudo-probability and so on. Our analysis will not require the full strength of the hierarchy, and will essentially rely on a few well-known properties of degree-
2
pseudo-distributions which we formally describe below. For interested readers, we refer them to
[
Lau09
]
for a more exhaustive treatment of the topic.

Degree-
2
SoS distributions
The degree-
2
SoS lifting of an SDP is the SDP itself with additional degree-
2
Lasserre constraints. Hence, a degree-
2
pseudo-distribution
\mu
can be equivalently expressed as a vector solution comprising of a set of unit vectors
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}\cup\{u_{\emptyset}\}
where
u_{\emptyset}
is the “one vector” of the solution satisfying
\|u_{\emptyset}\|^{2}=1
. In particular, in an integral assignment, the
v_{i}
’s are intended to be
\pm 1
indicators. A useful feature of the vector representation is that due to the Lasserre constraints, linear algebraic functions of these vectors express several useful parameters of the pseudo-distribution. We list some of the key such properties below.

Proposition 3.2

Every degree-
2
pseudo-distribution can be equivalently identified with a vector solution

\displaystyle\lesssim C^{*}_{0}\sqrt{\alpha_{e}\nu_{e}}\sqrt{\log(d)\log\frac{1}{\delta}},

where the penultimate inequality is due
\nu_{e}\leq\mu^{\prime}_{1}/A_{d,\delta}
in the setting of the lemma, and the last inequality is using
\mu^{\prime}_{1}=\max(\mu^{\prime}_{1},1-\mu^{\prime}_{1})=\alpha_{e}
.

\square

5.2
When the biases are greater than
1/2

Using Lemma
5
as a black box, we can prove an identical variant of the lemma for the case where all the biases are greater than
1/2
. We state and prove the lemma formally below.

{lem}

[]
Fix a hyperedge
e=(1,2,\ldots,d)
and let
(\mu^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\prime}_{d})
be the corresponding set of biases such that
1\geq\mu^{\prime}_{d}\geq\mu^{\prime}_{d-1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu^{\prime}_{1}\geq 1/2
and
1-\mu^{\prime}_{1}\geq\max(A_{d,\delta}\nu_{e},\delta^{C^{*}_{0}})
, where
A_{d,\delta}:=16C^{*}_{0}C^{*}_{1}\log d\log(1/\delta)
. Then

\Pr_{e\sim E}\left[e\mbox{ is cut}\right]\lesssim C^{*}_{0}\sqrt{\alpha_{e}\nu_{e}\log(1/\delta)\log(d)},

where
C^{*}_{1}=O(C^{*}_{0}\log(1/\delta))
.

Let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}
be the ensemble of Gaussians associated with vertices
1,\ldots,d\in e
. Let us consider a new family of Gaussian random variables
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}=(\hat{g}_{1},\ldots,\hat{g}_{d})
such that
\hat{g}_{i}:=-g_{i}
for every
i\in[d]
. Furthermore, for every
i\in[d]
, define
\hat{\mu}_{i}=1-\mu^{\prime}_{i}
and
\hat{t}_{i}=\Phi^{-1}(\hat{\mu}_{i})
. Since
\phi(\cdot)
is symmetric around the origin, we have
\hat{t}_{i}=-t_{i}
for every
i\in[d]
. Consequently, this gives us a one-to-one correspondence between cut events with respect to
\mathcal{G}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}
respectively i.e.,

\left\{\exists i,j\in e:g_{i}\leq t_{i}\ \ \&\ \ g_{j}>t_{j}\right\}\iff\left\{\exists i,j\in e:\hat{g}_{i}\leq\hat{t}_{i}\ \ \&\ \ \hat{g}_{j}>\hat{t}_{j}\right\}

Furthermore, we can conclude the following.

•

We have
0\leq\hat{\mu}_{d}\leq\cdots\leq\hat{\mu}_{1}\leq 1/2
and
\hat{\mu}_{1}\geq\max\{\delta^{C^{*}_{0}},A_{d,\delta}\nu_{e}\}
.

•

Furthermore
\max_{i\in e}\min(\hat{\mu}_{i},1-\hat{\mu}_{i})=\max_{i\in e}\min(\mu^{\prime}_{i},1-\mu^{\prime}_{i})=\alpha_{e}
.

Therefore the Gaussians
(\hat{g}_{i})_{i\in e}
, and the biases
(\hat{\mu}_{i})_{i\in e}
together satisfy all the requirements for Lemma
5
. Hence, using the one-to-one correspondence established above and invoking Lemma

we get that

\displaystyle\Pr_{(g_{i})_{i\in e}}\left[\left\{\exists i,j\in e:g_{i}\leq t_{i}\ \ \&\ \ g_{j}>t_{j}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr_{(\hat{g}_{i})_{i\in e}}\left[\left\{\exists i,j\in e:\hat{g}_{i}\leq t^{\prime}_{i}\ \ \&\ \ \hat{g}_{j}>\hat{t}_{j}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C^{*}_{0}\sqrt{\alpha_{e}\nu_{e}\log(1/\delta)\log(d)}.

\square

\eta^{H}_{\rm max}=\min_{\{e^{\circ}\}}\max_{u\in V}\sum_{e:u\in e^{\circ}}\frac{\log|e^{\circ}|}{|e^{\circ}|}

Then towards bounding the first term, we can use the following observation implicit in the proof of Theorem 6.3

Lemma 9.1

We can bound

\frac{\sum_{e\in E}\sqrt{\hat{\alpha}_{e}\nu_{e}\log(d)}}{\sum_{v\in V}W(v)}\leq\sqrt{\eta^{H}_{\rm max}\phi^{*}\log(d)}

In particular, if

Appendix A
Facts from Information Theory

As in
[
RT12
]
, the proof of Lemma
6.1
uses elementary information theory. For completeness, we introduce the (specific instantiations) of quantities and related properties used in the proof. We again point out that all the logarithms used in this section will be base
2
. Given a
0/1
random variable
X
with bias
\mu
, the entropy of
X
, denoted by
H(X)
, is defined as
H(X)=\mu\log(1/\mu)+(1-\mu)\log(1/(1-\mu))
. For a pair of random variables
X,Y
, the mutual information between
X
and
Y
is denoted by
I(X;Y)
and is defined as
I(X;Y)=H(X)-H(X|Y)
where
H(X|Y)
is the conditional entropy of
X
given
Y
. We now list the facts used by the proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Fact A.1

For any
X\sim{\sf Bernoulli}(\mu)
such that
\mu\leq 1/2
we have

H(X)\leq 2\mu\log\frac{1}{\mu}.

Using
\log(1+\mu)\leq\mu
we get that

\displaystyle H(X)=\mu\log\frac{1}{\mu}+(1-\mu)\log\frac{1}{1-\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu\log\frac{1}{\mu}+(1-\mu)\log\left(1+\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu\log\frac{1}{\mu}+\mu\leq 2\mu\log\frac{1}{\mu}.

\square

Fact A.2

For any pair of boolean random variables
X,Y
we have
I(X;Y)\leq 1
.

Using the fact that the entropy of boolean random variable lies between
[0,1]
(for e.g., see Example 2.1.1
[
Cov99
]
), we have
I(X;Y)=H(X)-H(X|Y)\leq H(X)\leq 1
.

\square

Fact A.3

For any pair of Gaussian random variables
(g_{i},g_{j})\sim N(0,\Sigma)
we have
I(g_{i};g_{j})=-\frac{1}{2}\log|\Sigma|
.

Lemma A.4
(Data Processing Inequality)

Let
(X,Y)
and
(X^{\prime},Y")
be pairs of random variables such that
X
is completely determined by
X^{\prime}
and
Y
is completely determined by
Y^{\prime}
. Then
I(X;Y)\leq I(X^{\prime};Y^{\prime})

Appendix B
Integrality Gap

Here we show that the integralilty gap of the lifted SDP is still
\Omega(d)
for up to near-linear levels of SoS, as stated formally in the following proposition.

{reprop}

[]
For every
d\geq 2
, there exists a
\delta=\delta(d)
such that for
R=\Omega(n/\log(n))
, the
R
-level SoS lifting of the basic SDP for the
\delta
-HSSE problem has integrality gap
\Omega(d)
.

The gap instance is simple: it is just a
d
-uniform random hypegraph with a linear number of hyperedges. Formally, our construction is as follows.

Construction
. Given
d,n\geq 2
, set
\delta=1/d
,
k=\delta n
and
r=Cn\log(1/\delta)
for some large constant
C
.
Let
H=(V,E)
be a random hypergraph on
n
-vertices where
E=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
such that each
e_{i}
is a uniformly random
d
-sized subset of
V
.

Soundness.

We first note that for any constant
c>0
, we have
(\delta n-c)/(n-c)\leq\delta
.
Fix a subset
S\subseteq V
of size
k
. Then for any fixed
i\in[r]
,

\Pr_{H}\left[e_{i}\in\partial_{H}(S)\right]\geq 1-\delta^{d}-(1-\delta)^{d}\geq 1-e^{-1}-\delta^{d}\geq 1/4

where the first inequality is using
d=1/\delta
. Therefore,
{\mathsf{E}}|\partial_{H}(S)|\geq r/4
. Therefore, using Chernoff bound over the randomness of the choice of the hyperedges, we have that

\Pr_{H}\left[|\partial_{H}(S)|\geq\frac{r}{8}\right]\geq 1-e^{-r/64}

and hence taking a union bound over all
\binom{n}{\delta n}\leq e^{n\log(1/\delta)}
subsets of size
k
we have that

\Pr_{H}\left[~\forall\ S\in\binom{[n]}{k}:|\partial_{H}(S)|\geq\frac{r}{8}\right]\geq 1-\exp\left(n\log(1/\delta)-r/64\right)\geq 1-e^{-Cr/2}

for large constant
C
.

Completeness.

Consider the distribution over subsets described by the following process

•

Choose a random
k=\delta n=n/d
sized subset
S
and set
X_{i}=\mathbbm{1}(i\in S)
for every
i\in V
.

The above is a mixture over integer solutions, and therefore admits a valid degree
R
-pseudo-distribution. Fix a
R
-sized subset
S
, and let
E_{S}
denote the set of hyperedges incident on
S
i.e,

E_{S}:=\left\{e\in E|e\cap S\neq\emptyset\right\}

Now it is easy to see that

\Pr_{e=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\sim\binom{[n]}{d}}\left[e\in E_{S}\right]\leq\sum_{j\in[d]}\Pr\left[i_{j}\in S\right]\leq\frac{dR}{n}

and hence the expected number of hyperedges incident on
S
is at most
dRr/n\lesssim dR\log(1/\delta)
(since
r=O(n\log(1/\delta))
by our choice of parameters). Hence using Chernoff bound we have that

\Pr\left[|E_{S}|\geq dR\log(1/\delta)\sqrt{\log n}\right]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\log(n)\log(1/\delta)dR}{4}\right).

Taking a union bound over all
\binom{n}{R}\leq e^{R\log n}
choices of
R
-sized subsets we get that

\displaystyle\Pr\left[~\forall\ S\in\binom{n}{R}:|E_{S}|\leq dR\log(1/\delta)\sqrt{\log(n)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(R\log n-\frac{\log(n)\log(1/\delta)dR}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\log(n)\log(1/\delta)dR}{8}\right)

Now choose an
R^{\prime}
-sized subset
T
with
R^{\prime}\leq R-1
and and a conditioning
X_{T}\leftarrow\alpha
. Suppose the conditioning fixes
r^{\prime}
out of
R^{\prime}
variables to
1
. Then then the degree
R-R^{\prime}
distribution on the variables
V\setminus T
conditioned on
X_{T}\leftarrow\alpha
corresponds to the following.

•

Choose a uniformly random
(n/d-r^{\prime})
-sized subset
S
from
V\setminus T
and set
X_{i}=\mathbbm{1}(i\in S)
.

In particular, for any
i\in V\setminus T
we have that

\Pr_{X_{i}\sim\mu_{i}|X_{T}\leftarrow\alpha}[X_{i}=1]=\frac{\frac{n}{d}-r^{\prime}}{n-R^{\prime}}\leq\frac{2}{d}

as long as
R^{\prime}\leq n/2
. From the above, we can conclude that for any pair of variables
i,j\in V\setminus T

\Pr_{\mu_{ij}|X_{T}\leftarrow\alpha}\left[X_{i}\neq X_{j}\right]\leq\Pr_{\mu_{i}|X_{T}\leftarrow\alpha}\left[X_{i}=1\right]+\Pr_{\mu_{j}|X_{T}\leftarrow\alpha}\left[X_{j}=1\right]=\frac{4}{d}

(46)

Therefore, in summary, for any
R
-sized subset
T
, and a conditioning of the variables in
T
we have the following.

1.

The number of hyperedges incident on
T
is at most
dR\log(1/\delta)\sqrt{\log(n)}
for which the contribution to the objective value is at most
1
.

2.

For any hyperedge not intersecting with
T
, from (
46
) we know that the contribution to the objective is at most
4/d
.

Hence, the total value of the objective for any conditioning on any
R
-sized subset is at most

dR\log(1/\delta)\sqrt{\log(n)}+\frac{4r}{d}\leq\frac{8r}{d}

which completes the soundness analysis.

\square

Appendix C
Reduction from
SSVE
to
Hyper-SSE

We introduce some additional notation introduced in this section. For a graph
G=(V,E)
, and a subset of vertices
S\subset V
, we define the symmetric vertex boundary as
\partial^{\rm sym}_{G}(S)=\partial^{V}_{G}(S)\cup\partial^{V}_{G}(S^{c})
. Furthermore, the internal boundary of the set
S
is defined as
\partial^{V}_{\rm int}(S)=\partial^{V}_{G}(S^{c})
. The symmetric vertex expansion of
S
, denoted by
\Phi^{V}_{G}
is defined as

\Phi^{V}_{G}(S)=\frac{w(\partial^{\rm sym}_{G}(S))}{w(S)}

Lemma C.1

Given a vertex weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
with vertex weights
w:V\to\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}
, there exists an efficient procedure to construct a vertex weighted graph
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},w^{\prime})
for which the following properties holds:

•

Completeness
: If there exists a subset
S\subset V
such that
w(S)=\delta
and
\phi^{V}_{G}(S)\leq\varepsilon
, then there exist a set
S^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}
such that
w^{\prime}(S^{\prime})=\delta
and
\Phi^{V^{\prime}}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})\leq 2\varepsilon
.

•

Soundness
: If there exists a subset
S^{\prime}\subset V
such that and
w^{\prime}(S^{\prime})=\delta
and
\Phi^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S)\leq\varepsilon
, then there exists a set
S\subset V
such that
(1-\varepsilon)\delta\leq w(S)\leq\delta
and
\phi^{V}_{G}(S)\leq\varepsilon/(1-\varepsilon)
.

Furthermore, the maximum degree of the graph
G^{\prime}
is exactly the maximum degree of graph
G
.

Given the graph
G=(V,E,w)
we construct a new bipartite graph
G^{\prime}=(V_{L},V_{R},w^{\prime})
as follows. Here we assign
V_{L}=V
and
V_{R}=E
i.e., the left vertex set is the set of vertices in
G
and the right vertex set is the edge-set in
E
. Furthermore, for every vertex set
u\in V
and every edge
e=(u,v)\in E
we add an edge
(u,e)\in E
. For every
u\in V_{L}
we assign
w^{\prime}(u)=w(u)
and for every vertex
e\in V_{R}
we assign
w^{\prime}(0)
. This completes the description of the graph
G^{\prime}
. For brevity, we shall denote
V^{\prime}=V_{L}\cup V_{R}
. Now we analyze the completeness and soundness of the reduction.

Completeness
. Suppose there exists a subset
S\subset V
such that
\phi^{V}_{G}(S)\leq\varepsilon
and
w(S)=\delta
. Construct the set
S^{\prime}
by including all the vertices in
S
and all the edges incident on
S
i.e.,

S^{\prime}=S\cup\Big\{(u,v)\in V_{R}\Big|u\in S\vee v\in S\}

Since the edge vertices
e\in V_{R}
have weight
0
, we have
w^{\prime}(S)=w(S)=\delta
. Now we bound the symmetric vertex expansion of
S^{\prime}
. Fix a vertex
v\in\partial^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S)
. Since if
v\in V_{R}
, we have
w^{\prime}(v)=0
so it does not contribute anything to the weight of the symmetric boundary. Therefore we may assume that
v\in V_{L}=V
. Then we claim that
v\notin S^{\prime}
. Otherwise if
v\in S^{\prime}
, we must have
v\in S
(since
S^{\prime}\cap V=S
). Furthermore, if
v\in S
, then for every
e=(v,v^{\prime})\in E
we have
e\in S^{\prime}
(from the construction of
G^{\prime}
) and hence
N_{G^{\prime}}(v)\subset S^{\prime}
which contradicts
v\in\partial^{\rm sym}_{V^{\prime}}(S)
. Therefore we must have
v^{\prime}\notin S
. But then there exists edge-vertex
e=(u,v)\in S^{\prime}
for some
u\in S^{\prime}
. This implies that
v\in\partial^{V}_{G}(S)
. Hence, we have

\Phi^{V^{\prime}}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})=\frac{w^{\prime}\left(\partial^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})\right)}{w^{\prime}(S^{\prime})}=\frac{w\left(\partial^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime}\cap V)\right)}{w(S)}\leq\frac{w\left(\partial^{V}_{G}(S)\right)}{w(S)}=\phi^{V}_{G}(S)\leq\varepsilon.

Soundness
. Fix a subset
S^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}
such that
w^{\prime}(S^{\prime})=\delta
and
\Phi^{V^{\prime}}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})\leq\varepsilon
. Let
S^{\prime\prime}\overset{\rm def}{=}S^{\prime}\setminus\partial^{\rm int}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})
, and define
S=S^{\prime\prime}\cap V
. We claim that
\partial^{V}(S)\subset\partial^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})
. To see this, fix a vertex
v\in\partial^{V}_{G}(S)
. Then there exists
u\in S\cap N_{G}(v)
. Now since
u\in S
we also have
u\in S^{c}
. Now we consider the following cases. Now for contradiction, suppose
v\notin\partial^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})
, then this combined with the fact that
v\notin S
implies that
v\notin S^{\prime}
. Now we consider two cases:

Case (i)
. Suppose
(u,v)\notin S^{\prime}
. Then
u\in\partial^{\rm int}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})
which contradicts the fact that
u\in S
.

Case (ii)
. Suppose
(u,v)\in S^{\prime}
. Then
v\in\partial^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})
, contradicting that
v\notin\partial^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})
.

Therefore, the above establishes that
\partial^{V}_{G}(S)\subseteq\partial^{{\rm sym}}_{G^{\prime}}\cap V
. Furthermore, note that we have

w(S)=w^{\prime}(S^{\prime\prime})=w^{\prime}(S^{\prime})\setminus w\left(\partial^{\rm int}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})\right)\geq w^{\prime}(S^{\prime})-\varepsilon w(S^{\prime})\geq(1-\varepsilon)w^{\prime}(S^{\prime})=(1-\varepsilon)w(S^{\prime})

and since
S\subset S^{\prime}\cap V
we have
w(S)\cap w(S^{\prime}\cap V)
. Therefore, combining the above observations we get that

\phi^{V}_{G}(S)=\frac{w\left(\partial^{V}_{G}(S)\right)}{w(S)}\leq\frac{w\left(\partial^{\rm sym}_{G^{\prime}}(S^{\prime})\right)}{(1-\varepsilon)w^{\prime}(S^{\prime})}=\frac{\Phi^{\rm sym}_{G}(S)}{1-\varepsilon}.

\square

Lemma C.2

.

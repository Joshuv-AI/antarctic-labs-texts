Precise upper deviation estimates for the maximum of a branching random walk
Lianghui Luo

Abstract
We consider the precise upper large deviations estimates for the maximal displacement of a branching random walk. In addition, we obtain a description of the extremal process of the branching random walk conditioned on this large deviations event. This introduces a family of point measure playing a role similar to the decoration measures introduced in [ 9 ] for branching Brownian motion.



Keywords:
Branching random walk; Maximal displacement; Large deviation; Extremal process.

1
Introduction

We consider a branching random walk on the real line, which can be described as follows. At time 0, one particle is located at 0. At each time
n+1
, each particle alive at time
n
dies and produces its children. The displacement of the children with respect to the position of their parent is distributed as a point process
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathbb{R}
, and is independent of other branching events in the process.

For each particle
u
, we write
V(u)
for its position and
|u|
for its generation. Let
M_{n}
be the maximal position occupied by a particle at time
n
. The asymptotic property of
M_{n}
as
n
go to infinity has been well studied. Hammersley
[
17
]
, Kingman
[
20
]
and Biggins
[
7
]
obtained, under increasingly general conditions,the first order speed of
M_{n}
and the existence of an explicit constant
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
, depending on the reproduction law, satisfying

\frac{M_{n}}{n}\to x^{*},\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,\quad\text{almost surely on the non-extinction set}.

The asymptotic fluctuations of
M_{n}-x^{*}n
were studied by Hu and Shi
[
19
]
, Addario-Berry and Reed
[
1
]
, and Bramson and Zeitouni
[
10
]
. Under mild conditions, they showed that there exists
\theta^{*}>0
such that
M_{n}-x^{*}n
fluctuates almost surely between
-\frac{3}{2\theta^{*}}\log n
and
-\frac{1}{2\theta^{*}}\log n
, and
M_{n}-\mathrm{Med}(M_{n})
is tight, where
\mathrm{Med}(M_{n})=x^{*}n-\frac{3}{2\theta^{*}}\log n+O(1)
. Finally, Aïdékon
[
2
]
proved that
M_{n}-x^{*}n+\frac{3}{2\theta^{*}}\log n
converges in law to a randomly shifted Gumbel variable. For more background about branching random walk, we refer to the lecture notes of Shi
[
26
]
.

In this article, we take interest in the upper deviation probability of
M_{n}
. Under the assumption that the displacements of the children of an individual are independent of one another and of their number, Gantert and Höfelsauer
[
15
]
proved that for
x>x^{*}
, the upper large deviation probability
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)
decays exponentially if the displacement satisfies an exponential moment condition. We also mention that when the number of children is non-random, precise estimates on the right tail of
M_{n}
have been obtained in
[
11
]
. Without the independence between displacement and offspring, under some strong conditions, Rouault
[
25
]
showed the precise estimation of the probability
\mathbb{P}(\exists|u|=n,\ \text{s.t.}\ V(u)\in[nx-\delta,nx+\delta])
for
x>x^{*}
and
\delta>0
. For the lower deviation probability estimates of maximum, see
[
18
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
13
]
and
[
16
]
. For a branching random walk in which displacements have semi-exponential tails, Dyszewski et al.
[
14
]
proved that the law of
M_{n}/n^{\alpha}
satisfies a large deviation principle, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
depending on the tail distribution of the displacement. When the displacement has regularly varying tails, Bhattacharya
[
6
]
showed that there exists a sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that
M_{n}/\gamma_{n}
satisfies a large deviation principle.

In the context of branching Brownian motion, which is a continuous-time analogue of the branching random walk, in which particles move according to i.i.d Brownian motion while giving birth to children at an exponential rate, the precise estimation of the upper deviation probability of the maximum were studied in
[
12
]
.

Inspired by
[
15
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
5
]
, in this article, we want to obtain an analogous result for the branching random walk, i.e. computing the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)
for
x>x^{*}
, under close to optimal conditions. Before showing the main results, we introduce some necessary notation.
Consider a branching random walk started from one particle at initial time. With previous notation,
(V(u):|u|=n)
are the positions of particles alive at generation
n
.
Define

Z_{n}:=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V(u)}

(1.1)

the counting measure formed by the positions of the particles of the branching random walk at time
n
. For
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, we write
Z_{n}(A)
for the number of particles located in the set
A
at time
n
. We recall that
M_{n}=\max_{|u|=n}V(u)
is the maximal displacement at time
n
, with the usual convention
M_{n}=-\infty
if
\{|u|=n\}=\varnothing.

We denote by

\psi(\theta):=\log\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\right)

the log-Laplace transformation of the reproduction law of the branching random walk. We refer to the branching random walk as supercritical, critical or subcritical respectively when
\psi(0)>0
,
\psi(0)=0
or
\psi(0)<0
, i.e. the mean number of the children is larger than, equal to or smaller than 1.
To avoid that the initial particle produces 0 particle almost surely, we always assume that

\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})=0)<1

in this article.

Additionally, we assume that there exists some
\theta>0
satisfying
\psi(\theta)<\infty
, and we define

\psi^{*}(x):=\sup_{\theta\geq 0}\{\theta x-\psi(\theta)\}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

the Legendre transformation of
\psi
, which is non-decreasing and convex on
\mathbb{R}
. It is known from
[
7
]
that in the supercritical case, almost surely on the non-extinction set,

\frac{M_{n}}{n}\to x^{*}:=\inf_{\theta>0}\frac{\psi(\theta)}{\theta}=\sup\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\psi^{*}(x)<0\},\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Let us state the upper deviation estimates of
M_{n}
, which is a slight refinement of
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Assume that there exists some
\theta>0
satisfying
\psi(\theta)<\infty
. If
\psi(0)>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)=-\psi^{*}(x),\quad\text{for any }x>x^{*}.

If
\psi(0)\leq 0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)=-\psi^{*}(x),\quad\text{for any }x>\frac{\mathbb{E}(\sum_{|u|=1}V(u))}{\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}.

Remark 1.1
.

This proposition shows that
M_{n}
satisfies a large deviation principle as soon as the intensity measure of the reproduction law of the branching random walk admits an exponential moment. In particular, no extra condition on the reproduction law is needed.

In order to obtain the precise estimation of the probability
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)
, we need some extra assumptions.
For the offspring law of the branching random walk, we assume that

\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2)>0,

(1.2)

which implies that there is indeed some branching in the branching process we consider. We remark that if (
1.2
) does not hold, large deviation estimates of
M_{n}
build down to large deviations for an usual random walk, obtained by Bahadur and Rao
[
4
]
and Petrov
[
24
]
, see forthcoming Remark
1.2
.
We assume that there exists
\theta>0
such that

\psi(\theta)<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)>\psi(\theta),

(1.3)

where we denote by

\psi^{\prime}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}V(u)e^{\theta V(u)-\psi(\theta)}\right),

which is assumed to be well-defined. Observe that if
\psi
is finite in a neighbourhood of
\theta
, then by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem,
\psi^{\prime}(\theta)
corresponds to the derivative of
\psi
at
\theta
, justifying the notation.
And we assume that

\quad\sigma^{2}:=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}(V(u)-\psi^{\prime}(\theta))^{2}e^{\theta V(u)}\right)\in(0,\infty).

(1.4)

Moreover, we add a mild
L\log L
-type integrable condition

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\log_{+}\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\right)<\infty,

(1.5)

where
\log_{+}(x):=\log(\max\{x,1\}),
for
x\geq 0
.
Finally, we assume that the branching random walk is non-lattice, i.e. for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{P}(V(u)\in a+b\mathbb{Z},\forall|u|=1)<1,

(1.6)

where
a+b\mathbb{Z}:=\{a+bn:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
.

The next theorem gives the precise asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+y)
as
n\to\infty
, uniformly in
y=O(\sqrt{n})
. It extends the results showed in
[
12
]
for branching Brownian motion to the branching random walk.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). There exists an explicit constant
C(\theta)\in(0,1)
defined in (
3.8
) such that for any positive sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfying
a_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|y|\leq a_{n}}\left|\sqrt{n}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}e^{\theta y}e^{n(\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\psi(\theta))}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+y)-\frac{C(\theta)}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma\theta}\right|=0.

(1.7)

In particular, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}{\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}([n\psi^{\prime}(\theta),\infty)))}=C(\theta).

Remark 1.2
.

If assumption (
1.2
) does not hold, the conclusions in Theorem
1.2
remain valid with
C(\theta)=1
. Indeed if
\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})\leq 1)=1
, we have

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+y)=(\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})))^{n}\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+y),

where
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a random walk started from
0
and whose step distribution is such that for any non-negative measurable function
h
,
\mathbb{E}(h(S_{1}))=(\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})))^{-1}\mathbb{E}(\sum_{|u|=1}h(V(u)))
. Therefore by the large deviations principle of
[
24
, Theorem 2]
, we obtain (
1.7
) with
C(\theta)=1
. In other words, we have

C(\theta)<1\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\mathbb{P}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2)>0.

With similar strategies as in
[
5
]
and
[
25
]
, we use the spine decomposition theorem, time reversal and the local limit theorem to prove Theorem
1.2
. As a by-product, we obtain the joint limit distribution of the extremal process
\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V(u)-M_{n}}
and
M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)
conditionally on the event
\{M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). There exists a point process
D(\theta)
such that
(\mathcal{E}_{n},M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))
conditionally on the event
\{M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\}
converges in law to
(D(\theta),\mathbf{e})
, where
\mathbf{e}
is an exponential random variable of parameter
\theta
independent of
D(\theta)
.

Remark 1.3
.

In particular, if assumption (
1.2
) does not hold, then
\mathcal{E}_{n}=\delta_{0}
on
\{M_{n}>-\infty\}
, which implies that
D(\theta)=\delta_{0}
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.3
, it is sufficient to demonstrate that for any non-negative continuous function
\phi
with compact support and
x\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}(e^{-\langle\mathcal{E}_{n},\phi\rangle}1_{\{M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq x\}}|M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))\to e^{-\theta x}\mathbb{E}(e^{-\langle D(\theta),\phi\rangle})

which is shown in Section
4.1
. The point process
D(\theta)
obtained in Theorem
1.3
plays an analogous role to the decorations obtained in
[
9
, Propositon 7.5]
in the context of branching Brownian motion.

We also take interest in some properties of
D(\theta)
. Note that we have not assumed that the branching random walk is supercritical in Theorem
1.3
. If the branching random walk is subcritical (critical), we prove that the measure
D(\theta)
is finite (infinite) respectively. More precisely,
\mathbb{P}(D(\theta)(\mathbb{R})<\infty)=1
if and only if
\psi(0)<1
.

Besides, under some additional conditions, we show that
D(\theta)
is continuous in distribution with respect to
\theta
, i.e. for
\theta_{0}>0
such that
\psi^{\prime}(\theta_{0})>x^{*}
and any non-negative continuous function
\phi
with compact support, we have

\lim_{\theta\to\theta_{0}}\mathbb{E}(e^{-\langle D(\theta),\phi\rangle})=\mathbb{E}(e^{-\langle D(\theta_{0}),\phi\rangle}).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce Ulam-Harris-Neveu notation for trees and the many-to-one formula. Moreover, we introduce the spine decomposition, construct an auxiliary point process
D_{n}^{\theta}
by time-reversal along the spine and show that the limit of
D_{n}^{\theta}
exists. In Section
3
, we study the asymptotic properties of
\mathbb{E}(e^{-\langle\mathcal{E}_{n},\phi\rangle}1_{\{M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+y\}})
, and we see that
D(\theta)
is actually the limit of
D_{n}^{\theta}
biased by a random variable. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
1.1
. In Section
5
, we discuss the number of atoms of
D(\theta)
as the branching random walk is subcritical or critical, and we prove the continuity of
D(\theta)
with respect to
\theta
under additional integrability conditions.

In the proof of this paper, we denote by
C
positive constant that may change from line to line.

2
Spine decomposition from the tip of the branching random walk

In this section, we introduce the many-to-one formula and spine decomposition theorem, and we construct an auxiliary and show the relationship between the extremal process and the auxiliary process. At the end, we give a random walk estimate by local limit theorem.

2.1
Ulam-Harris-Neveu notation

We introduce the Ulam-Harris-Neveu notation for plane trees. Define the set of finite sequence of positive integer

\mathbb{T}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{N}^{n},

with the usual convention
\mathbb{N}^{0}:=\{\varnothing\}
. For
u\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we write
u=(u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(n)})
and

•

we denote by
|u|
the generation of
u\in\mathbb{T}
, i.e. if
u\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, then
|u|:=n
.

•

for
k\leq|u|
, we use
u_{k}=(u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(k)})
to express the ancestor at generation
k
of
u
. In particular,
u_{0}:=\varnothing
.

•

we denote by
{\ddagger}\varnothing,u{\ddagger}:=\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{|u|}\}
the set of vertices(including
\varnothing
and
u
) in the unique shortest path connecting the root
\varnothing
to
u
.

Let
\{Z^{(u)}:=(z_{k}^{(u)}:k\geq 1):u\in\mathbb{T}\}
be i.i.d random non-increasing sequences with
z_{k}^{(u)}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
and be such that
\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{z_{i}^{(u)}}
is distributed as the point process
\mathcal{L}
, with the convention that
\delta_{-\infty}:=0
.
A branching random walk
Z:=\{V(u):u\in\mathbb{T}\}
can be defined as follows: we set
V(\varnothing):=0
and

V(u):=\sum_{i=1}^{|u|}z_{u^{(i)}}^{(u_{i-1})},\quad\text{if }u\neq\varnothing,

with the convention that
-\infty+x=-\infty
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We treat particles
u
such that
V(u)=-\infty
as dead particles, which do not contribute to the branching random walk.

We end this section with many-to-one formula, which is helpful for our proof.

Lemma 2.1

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
26
]
)
.

Assume that
\theta>0
is such that
\psi(\theta)<\infty
. For any
n\geq 1
and any measurable function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}g(V(u_{1}),\ldots,V(u_{n})\right)=\mathbb{E}(e^{-\theta S_{n}+n\psi(\theta)}g(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})),

where
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a random walk started from 0 with step distribution satisfying that for any nonnegative measurable function
h
,

\mathbb{E}(h(S_{1}))=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}h(V(u))e^{\theta V(u)-\psi(\theta)}\right).

In the above lemma, the sum over
|u|=n
is taken over all live particles in the branching random walk alive at time
n
.

2.2
Spine decomposition: a change of measure

Lyons
[
21
]
introduced the spine decomposition theorem for the branching random walk as an alternative description of the law of the branching random biased by its additive martingale. This method is an extension of the spine decomposition obtained by Lyons et al.
[
22
]
for Galton-Watson processes and Chauvin and Rouault
[
12
]
for branching Brownian motion.

Let
(\mathscr{F}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the natural filtration of the branching random walk
Z
, i.e.

\mathscr{F}_{n}:=\sigma(V(u):|u|\leq n),

with
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}:=\sigma(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
.
As
\psi(\theta)<\infty
, consider an additive martingale
W_{n}^{\theta}
, defined by

W_{n}^{\theta}:=\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\psi(\theta)}.

According to Kolmogorov’s extension theorem, there exists a probability
\mathbb{P}_{Q}
on
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
satisfying for any
n\geq 0
,
A\in\mathscr{F}_{n}
,

\mathbb{P}_{Q}(A)=\mathbb{E}(W_{n}^{\theta}1_{A}).

The spine decomposition consists in an alternative description of the law
\mathbb{P}_{Q}
constructed as a branching random walk with a spine.
Let a random pair
(\hat{\mathcal{L}},\xi)
be such that for any non-negative measurable function
f
,

\mathbb{E}(f(\hat{\mathcal{L}},\xi))=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{\theta V(k)-\psi(\theta)}f(Z_{1},k)\right).

A branching random walk
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
with a spine
(\xi_{n}:n\geq 0)
can be described as follows. At time 0, one particle
\xi_{0}:=\varnothing
locates at position
V(\xi_{0})=0
. At each time
n\geq 1
, all particles die, while giving birth independently to sets of new particles. The displacements (with respect to their parent) of children of normal particle
z
are distributed as
(V(i):i\geq 1)
. The displacements (with respect to their parent) of children of spine particle
\xi_{n-1}
are distributed as
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
; the particle
\xi_{n}
is chosen among the children
y
of
\xi_{n-1}
with probability proportional to
e^{\theta V(y)}
. Let us denote by
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
be the law of the branching random walk with a spine. The spine decomposition theorem corresponds in the identification of the law of the size-biased branching random walk and the law of the branching random walk with a spine.

Theorem 2.2

(Lyons
[
21
]
)
.

For any
n\geq 0
, the law of
(V(u):|u|\leq n)
is identical under
\mathbb{P}_{Q}
and
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
. Moreover, for any
|u|=n
,

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}(u=\xi_{n}|\mathscr{F}_{n})=\frac{e^{\theta V(u)-n\psi(\theta)}}{W_{n}^{\theta}},\quad\text{a.s.}.

(2.1)

Next, we introduce some notation that is helpful for constructing an auxiliary point process
D_{n}^{\theta}
. Suppose that
\{((b_{k}(i):i\geq 1),w^{(k)})\}_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. copies of
((\hat{\ell}_{i}:i\geq 1),\xi)
, where
(\hat{\ell}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is the ranked sequence of atoms in
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
, which is non-increasing sequence converging to
-\infty
. Let
\{(V^{(i,k)}(u):u\in\mathbb{T}):k\geq 1,i\geq 1\}
be i.i.d copies of the branching random walk
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
, independent of
\{((b_{k}(i):i\geq 1),w^{(k)})\}_{k\geq 1}
. For
n\geq 0
, we define an auxiliary point process

D^{\theta}_{n}:=\delta_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i\neq w^{(k)}}\sum_{|u|=k-1}\delta_{b_{k}(i)+V^{(i,k)}(u)-S_{k}},

(2.2)

with the convention
D^{\theta}_{0}:=\delta_{0}
, where

S_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}b_{k}(w^{(k)}).

Recall that
\mathcal{E}_{n}=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V(u)-M_{n}}
. By decomposing the branching random walk with spine along the path
{\ddagger}\varnothing,\xi_{n}{\ddagger}
and reversing the spine and the displacement (with respect to their parent) of the children of the spine particles before
n
th generation, we show the relationship between
(\mathcal{E}_{n},M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))
and
(D_{n}^{\theta},S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\theta>0
such that
\psi(\theta)<\infty
and
\psi^{\prime}(\theta)<\infty
. For any non-negative measurable functions
F
,
f
and
n\geq 1
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}(F(\mathcal{E}_{n})f(M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}})\\
=&e^{n(\psi(\theta)-\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}F(D_{n}^{\theta})f(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta}(\{0\})}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right).\end{split}

(2.3)

Proof.

We have

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)}1_{\{M_{n}=V(u)\}}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)}\right)=e^{n\psi(\theta)}<\infty,

which implies that
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{n}(\{0\})<\infty)=1.

By definition, on
\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}
, we have
\mathcal{E}_{n}(\{0\})\geq 1
.
For all non-negative measurable functions
F
and
f
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{E}_{n})f(M_{n}-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}}]\\
=&\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\{0\})}\sum_{u\in N_{n}}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{u})f(M_{n}-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}}\right]\\
=&\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\frac{1}{W_{n}^{\theta}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\{0\})}\sum_{u\in N_{n}}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{u})f(V(u)-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)\right],\end{split}

where
N_{n}:=\{|u|=n:V(u)=M_{n}\}
, and
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{u}:=\sum_{|v|=n}\delta_{V(v)-V(u)}
is the branching random walk seen form the particle
u
.
Using the spine decomposition theorem, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{Q}\left[\frac{1}{W_{n}^{\theta}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\{0\})}\sum_{u\in N_{n}}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{u})f(V(u)-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)\right]\\
=&\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{n}(\{0\})}\sum_{u\in N_{n}}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{u})e^{-\theta V(u)+n\psi(\theta)}1_{\{\xi_{n}=u\}}f(V(u)-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)\right]\\
=&\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}}(\{0\})}e^{-\theta V(\xi_{n})+n\psi(\theta)}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}})f(V(\xi_{n})-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)1_{\{\xi_{n}\in N_{n}\}}\right]\\
=&\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}}(\{0\})}e^{-\theta V(\xi_{n})+n\psi(\theta)}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}})f(V(\xi_{n})-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)1_{\{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right].\end{split}

Next, we construct a mapping from
\mathbb{N}^{n}
to the atoms of
D_{n}^{\theta}
. Let
w^{(n)}_{k}:=(w^{(n)},\ldots,w^{(n-k+1)}),1\leq k\leq n
.
For
u\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, define
r(u):=\sup\{k\geq 0:u_{k}=w^{(n)}_{k}\}
and

V^{(n)}(u):=S_{n}-S_{n-r(u)}+b_{n-r(u)}(u^{(r(u)+1)})+V^{(u^{(r(u)+1)},n-r(u))}(u|_{n-r(u)-1}),

with the convention
V^{(n)}(w_{n}^{(n)})=S_{n}
, where
u|_{k}
is defined as the last
k
items of
u
, i.e.

u|_{k}:=(u^{(|u|-k+1)},\ldots,u^{(|u|)}).

Then for any
|u|=n
, we have

1)

If
u=w^{(n)}_{n}
,
V^{(n)}(u)-V^{(n)}(w^{(n)}_{n})=0
;

2)

If
u\neq w^{(n)}_{n}
,
V^{(n)}(u)-V^{(n)}(w^{(n)}_{n})=b_{n-r(u)}(u^{(r(u)+1)})+V^{(u^{(r(u)+1)},n-r(u))}(u|_{n-r(u)-1})-\sum_{i=1}^{n-r(u)}b_{i}(w^{(i)}),

which implies that

D_{n}^{\theta}=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V^{(n)}(u)-V^{(n)}(w^{(n)}_{n})}.

Observe that
(((b_{1}(i):i\geq 1),w^{(1)}),\ldots,((b_{n}(i):i\geq 1),w^{(n)}))
is distributed as
(((b_{n}(i):i\geq 1),w^{(n)}),\ldots,((b_{1}(i):i\geq 1),w^{(1)}))
. Therefore, we deduce that
((V^{(n)}(u):|u|=n),w_{n}^{(n)})
is distributed as
((\bar{V}^{(n)}(u):|u|=n),\bar{w}_{n})
, where

\begin{split}\bar{V}^{(n)}(u):&=\sum_{i=n-\bar{r}(u)+1}^{n}b_{n-i+1}(w^{(n-i+1)})+b_{\bar{r}(u)+1}(u^{(\bar{r}(u)+1)})+V^{(u^{(\bar{r}(u)+1)},n-\bar{r}(u))}(u|_{n-\bar{r}(u)-1})\\
&=S_{\bar{r}(u)}+b_{\bar{r}(u)+1}(u^{(\bar{r}(u)+1)})+V^{(u^{(\bar{r}(u)+1)},n-\bar{r}(u))}(u|_{n-\bar{r}(u)-1}),\end{split}

\bar{w}_{n}:=(w^{(1)},\ldots,w^{(n)})
and
\bar{r}(u):=r(u,\bar{w}_{n})
.
By its definition, we observe that
((\bar{V}^{(n)}(u):|u|=n),\bar{V}^{(n)}(\bar{w}_{n}))
under
\mathbb{P}
is distributed as
((V(u):|u|=n),V(\xi_{n}))
under
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
.
Thus

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}}(\{0\})}e^{-\theta V(\xi_{n})+n\psi(\theta)}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}})f(V(\xi_{n})-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)1_{\{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)\\
=&\mathbb{E}\left(e^{-\theta S_{n}+n\psi(\theta)}F(D_{n}^{\theta})f(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta}(\{0\})}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)\\
=&e^{n(\psi(\theta)-\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{-\theta S_{n}+n\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)}F(D_{n}^{\theta})f(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta}(\{0\})}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right),\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

Define the increasing limit of
D^{\theta}_{n}
as
n\to\infty
by

D_{\infty}^{\theta}:=\delta_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i\neq w^{(k)}}\sum_{|u|=k-1}\delta_{b_{k}(i)+V^{(i,k)}(u)-S_{k}}.

Using the lemma below, we will prove that
D_{\infty}^{\theta}
is a well-defined point process.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\theta>0
such that (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) hold. For
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}(\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\frac{\psi(\theta)}{\theta}))
, we define

A_{n,\varepsilon}:=\left\{\max_{\ell\geq n}\frac{\max_{k\geq 1}M^{(k,\ell)}-S_{\ell}}{\ell}<-\varepsilon\right\},

where
M^{(k,\ell)}:=b_{\ell}(k)+\max_{|u|=\ell-1}\{V^{(k,\ell)}(u)\}
.
Then as
n\to\infty
,
\mathbb{P}(A_{n,\varepsilon}^{c})\to 0.

Proof.

We denote by
\mathcal{Y}
the sigma-field generated by
(b_{\ell}(k),w^{(\ell)},\ell\geq 1,k\geq 1)
. By the independence of
\mathcal{Y}
and
(V^{(i,k)}(u):u\in\mathbb{T},i\geq 1,k\geq 0)
, for
\ell\geq n
, we have that almost surely

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(A_{n,\varepsilon}^{c}|\mathcal{Y})\leq&\sum_{\ell\geq n}\mathbb{E}(1_{\{\max_{k\geq 1}M^{(k,\ell)}-S_{\ell}\geq-\varepsilon\ell\}}|\mathcal{Y})\\
\leq&\sum_{\ell\geq n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)-\theta S_{\ell}+\theta\varepsilon\ell}\mathbb{E}(e^{\theta\max_{|u|=\ell-1}V^{(k,\ell)}(u)}).\end{split}

Using that
\mathbb{E}(e^{\theta\max_{|u|=\ell-1}V^{(k,\ell)}(u)})\leq\mathbb{E}(\sum_{|u|=\ell-1}e^{\theta V(u)})
, we have

\mathbb{P}(A_{n,\varepsilon}^{c}|\mathcal{Y})\leq\sum_{\ell\geq n}e^{(\ell-1)\psi(\theta)+\varepsilon\theta\ell}e^{-\theta S_{\ell}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)}.

(2.4)

Observe that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)}\geq e^{\varepsilon\theta\ell}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\log_{+}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)}\right)\geq\varepsilon\theta\ell\right).

By assumption (
1.5
) and the definition of
b_{\ell}(k)
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\log_{+}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)}\right)\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\log_{+}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\right)\right)<\infty,

which implies that

\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)}\geq e^{\varepsilon\theta\ell}\right)=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(\log_{+}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)}\right)\geq\varepsilon\theta\ell\right)<\infty.

By Borel-Cantelli Lemma, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)}<e^{\varepsilon\theta\ell}

(2.5)

almost surely for
\ell
large enough.
On the other hand, by the strong law of large numbers, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n}}{n}=\mathbb{E}(S_{1})=\psi^{\prime}(\theta),\quad\text{a.s.},

which, by assumption that
\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)>\psi(\theta)
, implies that almost surely,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\psi(\theta)n-\theta S_{n}+2\varepsilon n}{n}=\psi(\theta)-\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+2\varepsilon<0.

(2.6)

Combine (
2.4
) with (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we deduce that almost surely,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{n,\varepsilon}^{c}|\mathcal{Y})=0,

which, by dominated convergence theorem, yields that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{n,\varepsilon}^{c})=0.

∎

Now, we prove that
D_{\infty}^{\theta}
is a well-defined Radon measure.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\theta>0
such that (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) hold. For any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

D_{\infty}^{\theta}((y,\infty))<\infty
almost surely, and
D_{n}^{\theta}
converges to
D_{\infty}^{\theta}
almost surely for the topology of the vague convergence.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2}(\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\frac{\psi(\theta)}{\theta}))
.
Recall that
M^{(k,\ell)}=b_{\ell}(k)+\max_{|u|=\ell-1}V^{(k,\ell)}(u)
. Observe that

A_{n,\varepsilon}\subset\{\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{\ell\geq n}\{\max_{k\geq 1}M^{(k,\ell)}-S_{\ell}\}=-\infty\},

which, by Lemma
2.4
, implies that

\mathbb{P}(\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{\ell\geq n}\{\max_{k\geq 1}M^{(k,\ell)}-S_{\ell}\}=-\infty)=1.

(2.7)

Fix
y\in\mathbb{R}
, by (
2.7
), in order to prove
\mathbb{P}(D_{\infty}^{\theta}((y,\infty))<\infty)=1
, it is sufficient to prove that for any
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}(D_{n}^{\theta}((y,\infty))<\infty)=1.

Observe that for any
n\geq 1
, by the independence between
(V^{(k,\ell)}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})_{k\geq 1,\ell\geq 1}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, which is defined in Lemma
2.4
, we have

\mathbb{P}(D_{n}^{\theta}((y,\infty))<\infty|\mathcal{Y})=\mathbb{P}\left.\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{|u|=k-1}1_{\{}y_{k,\ell}+V^{(k,\ell)}(u)-y_{\ell}>y\}<\infty\right)\right|_{y_{k,\ell}=b_{\ell}(k),y_{\ell}=S_{\ell}}.

For any
y_{k,\ell},y_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
, by Markov inequality and many-to-one formula, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{|u|=k-1}1_{\{}y_{k,\ell}+V^{(k,\ell)}(u)-y_{\ell}>y\}\right)\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{(k-1)\psi(\theta)}e^{\theta(y_{k,\ell}-y_{\ell}-y)}.

According to the assumption that
\psi(\theta)<\infty
, we know that for any
\ell\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{\theta b_{\ell}(k)}<\infty\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)-\psi(\theta)}1_{\{\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}<\infty\}}\right)=1,

(2.8)

which implies that almost surely

\mathbb{P}(D_{n}^{\theta}((y,\infty))<\infty|\mathcal{Y})=1.

Thus, we obtain that
\mathbb{P}(D_{n}^{\theta}((y,\infty))<\infty)=1.

For any
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, where
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is the set of continuous function with compact support on
\mathbb{R}
, by (
2.7
), there exists a random time
N
satisfying for any
n>N
,

\max_{\ell\geq n}\{\max_{k\geq 1}M^{(k,\ell)}-S_{\ell}\}<\text{supp}(\phi)

where
\text{supp}(\phi)
is the closure of
\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|\phi(x)|>0\}.

Therefore
D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)=D_{N}^{\theta}(\phi)=D_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)
for any
n>N
, which yields

\lim_{n\to\infty}D_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)=D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi),\quad\text{a.s.,}

where
\mu(\phi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
for a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

2.3
Applications of local limit theorem of a non-lattice random walk

In this section, we make use of the local limit theorem in
[
27
]
to give estimates on the law of a non-lattice random walk, which is important for studying the asymptotic behavior of
D_{n}^{\theta}
and
S_{n}
in the Section 3.

Lemma 2.6
.

Consider a non-lattice random walk
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
S_{0}=0
. Assume that
S_{1}
has mean 0 and finite variance
\sigma^{2}
.
Then for any non-negative direct Riemann integrable function
g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
,
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|y|\leq\gamma_{n}}\left|\sqrt{n}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+y))-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|=0,

where
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a non-negative sequence such that
\gamma_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})
.

Proof.

For any
M>0
,
h>0
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+s))\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{y\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(y)\sqrt{n}\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\in[kh-s,(k+1)h-s)).

According to Corollary 1 in
[
27
]
, for
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
h>0
, for
n
large enough, uniformly in
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\begin{split}\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+s))&\leq\sum_{-\frac{M}{h}\leq k\leq\frac{M}{h}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}he^{-\frac{\left(\frac{(2k+1)h}{2}-s\right)^{2}}{2n\sigma^{2}}}\\
&+\sum_{k\leq-\frac{M}{h}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}h\\
&+\sum_{k\geq\frac{M}{h}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}h+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)h\varepsilon_{1}.\end{split}

(2.9)

In particular, by (
2.9
), we have

\sup_{n\geq 1}\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+s))\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}+\varepsilon_{1}\right)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)<\infty,

for
h
small enough as
g
is direct Riemann integrable.

On the other hand, we know that for any
M>0
,
h>0
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+s))\geq\sum_{-\frac{M}{h}\leq k\leq\frac{M}{h}}\inf_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)\sqrt{n}\mathbb{P}(S_{n}\in[kh-s,(k+1)h-s))

Similarly to above, for all
\varepsilon_{1}>0
and
h>0
, for
n
large enough, uniformly in
s\in\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}\sqrt{n}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+s))\geq&\sum_{-\frac{M}{h}\leq k\leq\frac{M}{h}}\inf_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\delta_{n}e^{-\frac{\left(\frac{(2k+1)h}{2}-s\right)^{2}}{2n\sigma^{2}}}\\
\quad\quad&-\sum_{-\frac{M}{h}\leq k\leq\frac{M}{h}}\inf_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)h\varepsilon_{1}.\end{split}

(2.10)

Using that
\gamma_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup\left\{\left|e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}e^{-\frac{\left(\frac{(2k+1)h}{2}-y\right)^{2}}{2n\sigma^{2}}}-1\right|:|y|\leq\gamma_{n},-\frac{M}{h}\leq k\leq\frac{M}{h}\right\}=0,

which, combined with (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), yields that for
h>0
,

\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|y|\leq\gamma_{n}}\left|\sqrt{n}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+y))-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\left|\sum_{-\frac{M}{h}\leq k\leq\frac{M}{h}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)h-\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|+C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)h\varepsilon_{1}\\
&+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{-\frac{M}{h}\leq k\leq\frac{M}{h}}\inf_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)h\right|\\
&+C\sum_{k\geq\frac{M}{h}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)h+C\sum_{k\leq-\frac{M}{h}}\sup_{x\in[kh,(k+1)h)}g(x)h.\end{split}

As
g
is directly Riemann integral, letting
h\to 0+
, we have

\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|y|\leq\gamma_{n}}\left|\sqrt{n}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+y))-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq&C\left(\int_{M}^{\infty}g(x)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{-\infty}^{-M}g(x)\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x\right).\end{split}

Letting
M\to\infty
and
\varepsilon_{1}\to 0+
, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|y|\leq\gamma_{n}}\left|\sqrt{n}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}\mathbb{E}(g(S_{n}+y))-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)\mathrm{d}x\right|=0.\qed

3
Limit estimates for the auxiliary point process

The main aim of this section is to prove the forthcoming Proposition
3.1
, which is an essential tool for the proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). Then for any non-negative function
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, as
n\to\infty
, we have

\sqrt{2\pi n}\theta\sigma e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}e^{\theta y}e^{n(\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\psi(\theta))}\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+y\}})\to\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right]

uniformly in
|y|\leq a_{n}
, where
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is the sequence defined in Theorem
1.2
.

This proposition will allow us to study the asymptotic behavior of the joint law of the extremal process
\mathcal{E}_{n}
and the maximal displacement
M_{n}
.

Let
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a non-negative integer sequence, satisfying

c_{n}<n,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}c_{n}=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{c_{n}^{2}}{n}<\infty.

(3.1)

For
\varepsilon>0
, recall that

A_{c_{n},\varepsilon}:=\left\{\max_{\ell\geq c_{n}}\frac{\max_{k\geq 1}\{b_{\ell}(k)+\max_{|u|=\ell-1}V^{(k,\ell)}(u)\}-S_{\ell}}{\ell}<-\varepsilon\right\}.

(3.2)

According to Lemma
2.3
, for any non-negative function
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}})\\
=&e^{n(\psi(\theta)-\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{D^{\theta}_{n}(\{0\})}e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}e^{-D^{\theta}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{S_{n}-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n\geq y\}}1_{\{D^{\theta}_{n}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right].\end{split}

Set

h_{n}^{\theta}(\phi):=\frac{1}{D^{\theta}_{n}(\{0\})}e^{-D^{\theta}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{D^{\theta}_{n}((0,\infty))=0\}},

(3.3)

and let
x_{\phi}:=\inf\text{supp}(\phi)
. For
n
such that
-\varepsilon c_{n}<x_{\phi}
, we observe that
h^{\theta}_{n}(\phi)=h^{\theta}_{c_{n}}(\phi)
on
A_{c_{n},\varepsilon}
, therefore

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{D^{\theta}_{n}(\{0\})}e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}e^{-D^{\theta}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{S_{n}-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n\geq y\}}1_{\{D^{\theta}_{n}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right]\\
=&\mathbb{E}[h_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}1_{\{S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}}1_{A_{c_{n},\varepsilon}}]+\mathbb{E}[h_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}1_{\{S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}}1_{A_{c_{n},\varepsilon}^{c}}]\\
=&I_{1}(n,y)+I_{2}(n,y,\varepsilon)+I_{3}(n,y,\varepsilon),\end{split}

(3.4)

where

\begin{split}&I_{1}(n,y):=\mathbb{E}[h_{c_{n}}^{\theta}(\phi)e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}1_{\{S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}}],\\
&I_{2}(n,y,\varepsilon):=-\mathbb{E}[h_{c_{n}}^{\theta}(\phi)e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}1_{\{S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}}1_{A_{c_{n},\varepsilon}^{c}}],\\
&I_{3}(n,y,\varepsilon):=\mathbb{E}[h_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}1_{\{S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}}1_{A_{c_{n},\varepsilon}^{c}}].\end{split}

Using Lemma
2.6
, it is straightforward to get an equivalent of
I_{1}(n,y)
and the decay rate of
I_{2}(n,y,\varepsilon)
and
I_{3}(n,y,\varepsilon)
with respect to
n
. We prove these two results in turn in the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and (
1.6
).
Then for any non-negative function
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{|y|\leq a_{n}}\left|\sqrt{n}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}e^{\theta y}I_{1}(n,y)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma\theta}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)\right|=0,

where
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is the sequence defined in Theorem
1.2
.

Proof.

Observe that, by Markov property,

e^{\theta y}I_{1}(n,y)=\mathbb{E}(h_{c_{n}}^{\theta}(\phi)g_{n}(S_{c_{n}}-c_{n}\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-y)),

where for
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we write

g_{n}(s):=\mathbb{E}(e^{-\theta(s+S_{n-c_{n}}-(n-c_{n})\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}1_{\{S_{n-c_{n}}-(n-c_{n})\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+s\geq 0\}}).

By the assumptions on
(c_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, it is straightforward to see that as
n\to\infty
,

\frac{n}{c_{n}}\to\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{S_{c_{n}}-c_{n}\psi^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n-c_{n}}}\to 0,\quad\text{a.s.,}

by strong law of large numbers. Moreover, as
a_{n}=O(\sqrt{n-c_{n}})
,
by Lemma
2.6
, we get that as
n\to\infty
,

\sup_{|y|\leq a_{n}}\left|\sqrt{n}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}g_{n}(S_{c_{n}}-c_{n}\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-y)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma\theta}\right|\to 0,\quad\text{a.s.}

By Lemma
2.5
and the monotonicity of
D_{n}^{\theta}
, we can get

\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{c_{n}}^{\theta}(\phi)=h_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi),\quad\text{a.s.},

where
h_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi):=\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}
.

\begin{split}&\sup_{|y|\leq a_{n}}\left|\sqrt{n}e^{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}n}}e^{\theta y}I_{1}(n,\theta)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma\theta}\mathbb{E}(h_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi))\right|\\

\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}}|M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))\to e^{-\theta y}\frac{\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}})}{\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}})}.

By Proposition
3.1
, for any non-negative
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
y\geq 0
, as
n\to\infty
, we have

\sqrt{2\pi}\theta\sigma\sqrt{n}e^{n(\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\psi(\theta))}\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}})\to e^{-\theta y}\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}).

Therefore, using Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
1.2
, we obtain that as
n\to\infty
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}}|M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))&=\frac{\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\geq y\}})}{\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}\\
&\to e^{-\theta y}\frac{\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}})}{\mathbb{E}(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}})},\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

4.2
Proof of Proposition
1.1

To prove Proposition
1.1
, we need to estimate the survival probability of a subcritical Galton-Watson process. A Galton-Watson process
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is defined as follows: we set
Y_{0}:=1
and for
n\geq 0

Y_{n+1}:=\sum_{k=1}^{Y_{n}}\xi_{k,n},

where
(\xi_{k,n})_{k,n\geq 0}
are i.i.d nonnegative integer-valued random variables.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider a Galton-Watson process
(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0}
defined as above. Assume that
m:=\mathbb{E}(Y_{1})\in(0,1),
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}>0)=\log m.

Proof.

For
L>0
, we construct a new Galton-Watson process
(Y^{(L)}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
by removing the offspring of each particle which produces more than
L
children. More precisely,
(Y^{(L)}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is defined as follows: we set
Y_{0}^{(L)}:=1
and

Y^{(L)}_{n+1}:=\sum_{k=1}^{Y^{(L)}_{n}}\xi_{k,n}1_{\{\xi_{k,n}\leq L\}},\quad n\geq 1.

By induction, we get
Y^{(L)}_{n}\leq Y_{n}
for any
n\geq 0
.
Let
m^{(L)}:=\mathbb{E}(Y^{(L)}_{1})=\mathbb{E}(Y_{1}1_{\{Y_{1}\leq L\}})
. By monotone convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{L\to\infty}m^{(L)}=m\in(0,1).

As
Y^{(L)}_{1}=Y_{1}1_{\{Y_{1}\leq L\}}\leq L
, we have
\mathbb{E}(Y^{(L)}_{1}\log_{+}(Y^{(L)}_{1}))<\infty
. According to
[
3
, P40]
, as
L
large enough, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Y^{(L)}_{n}>0)=\log m^{(L)}.

Therefore

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}>0)\geq\lim_{L\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Y^{(L)}_{n}>0)=\log m.

(4.2)

On the other hand, by the Markov inequality, we have

\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}>0)\leq\mathbb{E}(Y_{n}).

Note that
\mathbb{E}(Y_{n})=m^{n}
. Thus, letting
n\to\infty
, we get

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Y_{n}>0)\leq\log m,

which, combined with (
4.2
), finishes the proof.
∎

With Lemma
4.1
, we can prove Proposition
1.1
. Here we mainly use the strategy showed in
[
8
, Theorem 2]
to construct a truncated branching random walk that controls the lower bound of the probability
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)
. For
u,v\in\mathbb{T}
, we denote by
uv:=(u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(|u|)},v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{(|v|)})
the concatenation of
u
and
v
.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

For the upper bound, observe that for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\{M_{n}\geq nx\}=\{Z_{n}([nx,\infty))\geq 1\}.
By Markov inequality,

\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}([nx,\infty))\geq 1)\leq\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}([nx,\infty)))=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}1_{\{V(u)\geq nx\}}\right).

Thus, for any
\theta\geq 0
, by many-to-one formula, we have

\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}([nx,\infty)))\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-nx\theta}\right)=e^{-n(\theta x-\psi(\theta))}.

Optimizing the above equation with respect to
\theta
, it implies

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)\leq e^{-n\psi^{*}(x)}.

Next, we bound
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)
from below. If
\psi^{*}(x)=\infty
, then
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)=0
completes the proof. We therefore assume that
\psi^{*}(x)<\infty
. We know that
\psi^{*}(x)>0
because
\psi^{*}
is convex on
\mathbb{R}
and
x>x^{*}
. Similarly to
[
8
, Theorem 2]
, fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we construct a branching random walk
Z^{(k)}
as follows. The first generation of
Z^{(k)}
will consist only of those particles in the
k
th generation of the branching random walk
Z
with positions to the right of
kx
. The second generation is formed by applying the same procedure to the branching random walks initiated by each of these particles and so on. More precisely, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\geq 1
, we denote by

A_{0}^{k}:=\{\varnothing\}\text{ and }A_{i}^{k}:=\{uy\in\mathbb{T}:u\in A_{i-1}^{k},|y|=k,V(uy)-V(u)\geq kx\}

the collections of particles in each generation of the new branching random walk
Z^{(k)}
, which yields that
Z_{i}^{(k)}(\mathbb{R})=\#A_{i}^{k}
. It is obvious to see that
(Z_{n}^{(k)}(\mathbb{R}))_{n\geq 0}
is a Galton-Watson process with
Z_{0}^{(k)}(\mathbb{R})=1
and

\mathbb{E}(Z^{(k)}_{1}(\mathbb{R}))=\mathbb{E}(Z_{k}([kx,\infty))).
By the independence of the branching events, we know that

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)&\geq\mathbb{P}(M_{n-k\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}\geq(n-k\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor)x)\mathbb{P}(Z^{(k)}_{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}(\mathbb{R})>0)\\
&\geq\inf_{1\leq i\leq k}\mathbb{P}(M_{i}\geq ix)\mathbb{P}(Z^{(k)}_{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}(\mathbb{R})>0),\end{split}

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
means the integer part of
x
.
As
\psi(0)>0
, according to
[
8
, Theorem 1]
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}([nx,\infty)))=-\psi^{*}(x)\in(-\infty,0),

(4.3)

which implies that for
k
large enough,
\mathbb{E}(Z^{(k)}_{1}(\mathbb{R}))\in(0,1).

By Lemma
4.1
, we get

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}{n}\frac{1}{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}\log\mathbb{P}(Z^{(k)}_{\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor}(\mathbb{R})>0)=\frac{1}{k}\log\mathbb{E}(Z_{k}([kx,\infty))),

where the first inequality follows from
\inf_{1\leq i\leq k}\mathbb{P}(M_{i}\geq ix)>0
. By (
4.3
), letting
k\to\infty
, we get

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)\geq-\psi^{*}(x),

which completes the proof of lower bound in the case that
\psi(0)>0
.

On the other hand, if
\psi(0)\leq 0
, we can denote by
\mu
the intensity measure of
Z_{1}
defined by
\mu(A):=\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(A))
for any
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R})
. By many-to-one formula, we have

\mu^{*n}(A)=\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}(A)),

where
\mu^{*n}
means the
n
-fold convolution of
\mu
.
As
\mu(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))=e^{\psi(0)}<\infty
,
\mu/\mu(\mathbb{R})
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
. By Cramér’s theorem, we know that for any
x>\frac{1}{\mu(\mathbb{R})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}s\mu(\mathrm{d}s)=\frac{\mathbb{E}(\sum_{|u|=1}V(u))}{\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{E}(Z_{n}([nx,\infty)))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{\mu^{*n}([nx,\infty))}{\mu(\mathbb{R})^{n}}+\log\mu(\mathbb{R})=-\psi^{*}(x)\in(-\infty,0).

With the same arguments as in the case that
\psi(0)>0
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(M_{n}\geq nx)\geq-\psi^{*}(x),\quad\text{for any }x>\frac{\mathbb{E}(\sum_{|u|=1}V(u))}{\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))},

which completes the proof of lower bound.
∎

5
Some extra properties of the extremal process of conditioned branching random walk

In Section
5.1
, we will discuss when
D(\theta)
is finite. We give an alternative description of
D(\theta)
in Section
5.2
. Finally, we prove the weak continuity of
D(\theta)
in Section
5.3
.

5.1
Subcritical branching random walk

Recall that we do not assume the branching random walk
Z
to be supercritical. Under some extra conditions, we prove that
D(\theta)
is finite (infinite) if
Z
is subcritical (critical) and satisfies a
L\log L
condition (second moment condition).

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume there exists
\theta>0
such that
\psi(\theta)<\infty
and
(\ref{as3})
. If
Z
is subcritical and satisfies
\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})\log_{+}Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))<\infty
, then
\mathbb{P}(D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})<\infty)=1.

Proof.

Because
Z
is subcritical, then we know that
\psi(0)<0
, which implies
\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))\in(0,1)
. According to the definition of
Z_{n}
in (
1.1
), we know that
(Z_{n}(\mathbb{R}))_{n\geq 0}
is a subcritical GW process with
Z_{0}(\mathbb{R})=1
. By Lemma
4.1
, as
n\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{n}\log\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0)\to\log\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))\in(-\infty,0).

Recall the definitions of
(b_{k}(\ell):\ell\geq 1)_{k\geq 1}
and
(V^{(i,k)}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})_{i\geq 1,k\geq 1}
. Let
Z^{(i,k)}
be the number of particles of the branching random walk
(V^{(i,k)}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
at time
k-1
, i.e.

Z^{(i,k)}:=\sum_{|u|=k-1}1_{\{V^{(i,k)}(u)\in\mathbb{R}\}}.

Define

B_{k}:=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\delta_{b_{k}(\ell)}

the counting measure formed by
(b_{k}(\ell):\ell\geq 1)
.
Then

\mathbb{P}(D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})=\infty)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k\geq 2}\sum_{i=1}^{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})}Z^{(i,k)}=\infty\right).

Observe that there exists
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
, for
k
large enough, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})}Z^{(i,k)}>0\right)&=\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})}Z^{(i,k)}>0,B_{k}(\mathbb{R})>e^{ck}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})}Z^{(i,k)}>0,B_{k}(\mathbb{R})\leq e^{ck}\right)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}(\log_{+}B_{k}(\mathbb{R})>ck)+e^{ck}\mathbb{P}(Z_{k-1}>0)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}(\log_{+}B_{k}(\mathbb{R})>ck)+Ce^{ck}e^{k(1-\varepsilon_{1})\log\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R}))},\end{split}

where
c
is a positive constant in
(0,-(1-\varepsilon_{1})\log\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})))
. Because
x\log(1+y)\leq x\log(1+x)+y\log(1+y)
for any
x,y\geq 0
, we get

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\log_{+}Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})\right)\leq\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})\log_{+}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})))+\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\log_{+}(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)})\right)<\infty,

which implies that
\mathbb{E}(\log_{+}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R})))<\infty
. We therefore know that

\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})}Z^{(i,k)}>0\right)<\infty.

Thus, by the Borel-Cantelli Lemma, we conclude that almost surely for
k
large enough,

\sum_{i=1}^{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})}Z^{(i,k)}=0.

Because
\mathbb{E}Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})<\infty
, we have
Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})<\infty
almost surely,
which implies that for any
i,k\geq 1
,

B_{k}(\mathbb{R})<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad Z^{(i,k)}<\infty,\quad\text{a.s.}

Thus, we know that for any
n>0
, almost surely

\sum_{k=2}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})}Z^{(i,k)}<\infty.

Thus, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})}Z^{(i,k)}=\infty\right)=0,

which proves that

\mathbb{P}(D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})<\infty)=1.\qed

Proposition 5.2
.

Assume that there exists
\theta>0
satisfying
\psi(\theta)<\infty
. If
Z
is critical and satisfies
\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})^{2})<\infty
, then
\mathbb{P}(D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})=\infty)=1
.

Proof.

For
k\geq 1
, we define

C_{k}:=\{B_{k}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2\text{ and there exists }i\neq w^{(k)}\text{ such that }Z^{(i,k)}>0\},

where
Z^{(i,k)}
is defined in Proposition
5.1
. Let

s_{k}:=(w^{(k)}-1)1_{\{w^{(k)}>1\}}+2\times 1_{\{w^{(k)}=1\}}.

Observe that

\mathbb{P}(C_{k})\geq\mathbb{P}(B_{k}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2,Z^{(s_{k},k)}>0).

Since that
(B_{k}(\mathbb{R}),w^{(k)})
and
(Z^{(i,k)})_{i\geq 1}
are independent and
(Z^{(i,k)})_{i\geq 1}
are identical distributed, we have

\mathbb{P}(B_{k}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2,Z^{(s_{k},k)}>0)=\mathbb{P}(B_{k}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2)\mathbb{P}(Z^{(s_{k},k)}>0)=\mathbb{P}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2)\mathbb{P}(Z_{k-1}(\mathbb{R})>0),

where the last equation follows from that
(B_{k}(\mathbb{R}))_{k\geq 1}
are identical distributed and
Z^{(s_{k},k)}
is distributed as
Z_{k-1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Because
Z
is critical and
\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})^{2})<\infty
, by
[
3
, P20]
, we have

n\mathbb{P}(Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0)\to\frac{2}{\mathbb{E}(Z_{1}(\mathbb{R})^{2})},\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Thus, there exists some positive constant
C
such that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(C_{k})\geq C\mathbb{P}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k},

which, by that
\mathbb{P}(B_{1}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2)>0
, yields

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(C_{k})=\infty.

Because
(B_{k}(\mathbb{R}),w^{(k)},Z^{(i,k)},i\geq 1)_{k\geq 1}
are independent, we obtain that
(C_{k})_{k\geq 1}
are independent. By Borel-Cantelli Lemma, we have almost surely, infinitely many
k
such that
B_{k}(\mathbb{R})\geq 2
and there exists
i\neq w^{(k)}
satisfying
Z^{(i,k)}>0
. Observe that

D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{1\leq i\leq B_{k}(\mathbb{R}),i\neq w^{(k)}}Z^{(i,k)},

we therefore have

\mathbb{P}(D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\mathbb{R})=\infty)=1.\qed

5.2
Extremal process as a conditioned point measure

In this section, we give an alternative description of
D(\theta)
. We define a total order
<
in
\mathbb{T}
called the lexicographical order. For
u,v\in\mathbb{T}
, define
r(u,v):=\sup\{k\geq 0:u_{k}=v_{k}\}
the generation of the most recent common ancestor of
u
and
v
. Then we say that
u<v

if one of conditions below is satisfied:

1)

r(u,v)=|u|
and
|u|<|v|
.

2)

r(u,v)<|u|
and
u^{(r(u,v)+1)}<v^{(r(u,v)+1)}
.

When we see the branching random walk from the minimal particle located at the maximal position at time
n
for the lexicographical order, we can get the next lemma, which is similar to Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\theta>0
such that
\psi(\theta)<\infty
and
\psi^{\prime}(\theta)<\infty
. For any non-negative measurable function F, f and
n\geq 1
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}(F(\mathcal{E}_{n})f(M_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}})\\
=&e^{n(\psi(\theta)-\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}\mathbb{E}(e^{-\theta(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}F(D_{n}^{\theta})f(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{n}^{\theta}=0\}}),\end{split}

where

\bar{D}_{n}^{\theta}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i<w^{(k)}}\sum_{|u|=k-1}1_{\{b_{k}(i)+V^{(i,k)}(u)=S_{k}\}}.

Proof.

Define
m_{n}
to be the minimal element of
N_{n}
for the lexicographical order. By spine decomposition theorem, with similar arguments as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}[F(\mathcal{E}_{n})f(M_{n}-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}}]\\
=&\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}F(\mathcal{E}_{n})1_{\{u=m_{n}\}}f(M_{n}-\psi^{\prime}(\theta)n)1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}}\right)\\
=&\tilde{\mathbb{E}}(e^{-\theta V(\xi_{n})+n\psi(\theta)}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}})1_{\{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{\{\sum_{|u|=n,u<\xi_{n}}1_{\{V(u)=V(\xi_{n})\}}=0\}}f(V(\xi_{n})-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))).\end{split}

Observe that

\begin{split}&\sum_{|u|=n,u<\xi_{n}}1_{\{V(x)=V(\xi_{n})\}}\\
=&\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\xi^{(k)}-1}\sum_{|u|=n-k}1_{\{V(\xi_{k-1}i)-V(\xi_{k-1})+V(\xi_{k-1}iu)-V(\xi_{k-1}i)=V(\xi_{n})-V(\xi_{k-1})\}}.\end{split}

With the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we have that
(D_{n}^{\theta},\bar{D}_{n}^{\theta},S_{n})
under
\mathbb{P}
is distributed as

(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}},\sum_{|u|=n,u<\xi_{n}}1_{\{V(u)=V(\xi_{n})\}},V(\xi_{n}))

under
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}
.
Therefore

\begin{split}&\tilde{\mathbb{E}}(e^{-\theta V(\xi_{n})+n\psi(\theta)}F(\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}})1_{\{\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\xi_{n}}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{\{\sum_{|u|=n,u<\xi_{n}}1_{\{V(u)=V(\xi_{n})\}}=0\}}f(V(\xi_{n})-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta)))\\
=&\mathbb{E}(e^{-\theta S_{n}+n\psi(\theta)}F(D_{n}^{\theta})f(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{\{\bar{D}_{n}^{\theta}=0\}})\\
=&e^{n(\psi(\theta)-\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta))}\mathbb{E}(e^{-\theta S_{n}+n\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)}F(D_{n}^{\theta})f(S_{n}-n\psi^{\prime}(\theta))1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{\{\bar{D}_{n}^{\theta}=0\}}),\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

With the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma

, we can also prove

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)=\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{\{\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta}=0\}}).

By the proof of Theorems

and

, we get

C(\theta)=\mathbb{P}(D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta}=0)

and

is distributed as

5.3
Continuity of the extremal process

In this section, we prove that
D(\theta)
in Theorem
1.3
is continuous in distribution with respect to
\theta
under some stronger conditions.
First, we introduce an important lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Fix
\theta_{0}>0
. If there exists
\delta\in(0,\theta_{0})
satisfying for any
\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
,
\theta
satisfies assumptions (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\left(\log_{+}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\right)\right)^{1+\delta}\right]<\infty.

There exists
\varepsilon>0
s.t. as
n\to\infty
, we have

\sup_{\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]}\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c})\to 0,

where

A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon):=\left\{\max_{\ell\geq n}\frac{\max_{k\geq 1}\{b_{\ell}(k)+\max_{|u|=\ell-1}\{V^{(k,\ell)}(u)\}\}-S_{\ell}}{\ell}<-\varepsilon\right\}

and we should emphasize that
b_{\ell}(k)
and
S_{\ell}
depend on
\theta
even if we do not give them a subscript of
\theta
.

Proof.

With the same arguments as in Lemma
2.4
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c})\\
\leq&\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{\ell\geq n}\frac{\max_{k\geq 1}\{b_{\ell}(k)+\max_{|u|=\ell-1}\{V^{(k,\ell)}(u)\}\}-S_{\ell}}{\ell}\geq-\varepsilon\right)\\
\leq&e^{\psi(\theta)}\sum_{\ell\geq n}e^{-\ell(\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\psi(\theta)-c-2\varepsilon\theta)}+\sum_{\ell\geq n}\mathbb{P}\left(\log_{+}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{\theta b_{1}(k)}\right)>c\ell\right)+\sum_{\ell\geq n}\mathbb{P}(S_{\ell}<(\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\varepsilon)\ell),\end{split}

where
c
is a positive constant whose value is fixed later.
Observe that
\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\psi(\theta)
is increasing on
[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
by convexity and is positive, then

c_{1}:=\inf_{\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]}\{\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\psi(\theta)\}=(\theta_{0}-\delta)\psi^{\prime}(\theta_{0}-\delta)-\psi(\theta_{0}-\delta)\in(0,\infty).

With the same arguments as above, we can get

c_{2}:=\inf_{\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]}\{\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\frac{\psi(\theta)}{\theta}\}\in[\frac{c_{1}}{\theta_{0}+\delta},\frac{(\theta_{0}+\delta)\psi^{\prime}(\theta_{0}+\delta)-\psi(\theta_{0}+\delta)}{\theta_{0}-\delta}]\subset(0,\infty).

Meanwhile, using the continuity of
\psi^{\prime\prime}
on
[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
and that
\psi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)>0
for any
\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
, we know that

c_{3}:=\sup_{\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\theta)\in(0,\infty).

For
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,\frac{\delta}{2})
, by mean-valued theorem, for any
\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\frac{\delta}{2},\theta_{0}+\frac{\delta}{2}]
, we have

\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\frac{\psi(\theta)-\psi(\theta-\varepsilon_{1})}{\varepsilon_{1}}=\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\psi^{\prime}(\theta(\varepsilon_{1}))=(\theta-\theta(\varepsilon_{1}))\psi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\varepsilon_{1}})\leq\varepsilon_{1}c_{3},

where
\theta(\varepsilon_{1})
is a constant in
(\theta-\varepsilon_{1},\theta)
and
\theta_{\varepsilon_{1}}
is a constant in
(\theta(\varepsilon_{1}),\theta)
.
By many-to-one formula and the spine decomposition theorem, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}(S_{\ell}<(\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\varepsilon)\ell)\leq\mathbb{E}(e^{\varepsilon_{1}(\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\varepsilon)\ell-\varepsilon_{1}S_{\ell}})&=e^{\varepsilon_{1}(\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\varepsilon)\ell}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=\ell}e^{(\theta-\varepsilon_{1})V(u)-\ell\psi(\theta)}\right)\\
&=e^{-\ell(\psi(\theta)-\psi(\theta-\varepsilon_{1})-\varepsilon_{1}(\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\varepsilon))}.\end{split}

Set

c=\frac{1}{2}c_{1},\quad\varepsilon_{1}=\min\{\frac{1}{8}\frac{c_{2}}{c_{3}},\frac{\delta}{2}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon=\frac{1}{6}c_{2},

for any
\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
, we have

\theta\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\psi(\theta)-c-2\theta\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{6}c_{2}(\theta_{0}-\delta)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi(\theta)-\psi(\theta-\varepsilon_{1})-\varepsilon_{1}(\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-\varepsilon)\geq\frac{1}{24}\varepsilon_{1}c_{2}.

Observe that

\mathbb{P}\left(\log_{+}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{\theta b_{1}(k)}\right)>c\ell\right)\leq\frac{1}{c^{1+\delta}\ell^{1+\delta}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\left(\log_{+}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\right)\right)^{1+\delta}\right).

Let
g(\theta):=\mathbb{E}(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}(\log_{+}(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}))^{1+\delta}),\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
. By dominated convergence theorem, it is straightforward to see that
g
is continuous on
[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
, which yields

c_{4}:=\sup_{\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]}g(\theta)\in(0,\infty).

Besides, by the continuity of
\psi
on
[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
, we know that

c_{5}:=\sup_{\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]}\psi(\theta)\in(0,\infty).

In final, for
n
large enough, for any
\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
, we have

\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c})\leq e^{c_{5}}\sum_{\ell\geq n}e^{-\frac{1}{6}c_{2}(\theta_{0}-\delta)\ell}+\sum_{\ell\geq n}\frac{c_{4}}{c^{1+\delta}\ell^{1+\delta}}+\sum_{\ell\geq n}e^{-\frac{1}{24}\varepsilon_{1}c_{2}\ell},

which concludes that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]}\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c})\to 0.\qed

Now we use the Lemma
5.4
to give the proof of continuity.

Proposition 5.5
.

Consider a non-lattice branching random walk and fix
\theta_{0}>0
. If there exists
\delta\in(0,\theta_{0})
satisfying for any
\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
,
\theta
satisfies assumptions (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\left(\log_{+}\left(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\right)\right)^{1+\delta}\right]<\infty,

then
(D_{\infty}^{\theta}|D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta}=0)
is continuous in distribution at
\theta_{0}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove that for any non-negative function
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
,

\lim_{\theta\to\theta_{0}}\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta}=0\}})=\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}=0\}}).

For
\varepsilon>0
,
\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
, let
A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)
be defined as in Lemma
5.4
.
Observe that there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
s.t.
-\varepsilon N_{1}<\inf\text{supp}(\phi)
, then for
\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
and
n\geq N_{1}
, by Remark
5.1
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta}=0\}})\\
=&\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)\\
=&\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c}}\right]\\
=&\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c}}\right)\\
&+\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}1_{A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c}}\right),\end{split}

which yields that

\begin{split}&|\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta}=0\}})-\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}=0\}})|\\
\leq&2\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c})+\left|\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)-\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}=0\}})\right|.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)=\mathbb{E}(e^{\theta M_{n}-n\psi(\theta)}e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}}).

Observe that
\psi
is continuous on
[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]
, by dominated convergence theorem, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{\theta\to\theta_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\theta M_{n}-n\psi(\theta)}e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}}\right)&=\mathbb{E}\left(e^{\theta_{0}M_{n}-n\psi(\theta_{0})}e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(\phi)}1_{\{Z_{n}(\mathbb{R})>0\}}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta_{0}}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{n}^{\theta_{0}}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta_{0}}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right),\end{split}

which implies that

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{\theta\to\theta_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{n}^{\theta}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{n}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{n}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)&=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}(\{0\})}e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}((0,\infty))=0\}}\right)\\
&=\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}=0\}}).\end{split}

On the other hand, by Lemma
5.4
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\theta\in[\theta_{0}-\delta,\theta_{0}+\delta]}\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(\theta,\varepsilon)^{c})=0.

Thus, we have

\lim_{\theta\to\theta_{0}}\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta}=0\}})=\mathbb{E}(e^{-D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}(\phi)}1_{\{D_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}((0,\infty))=0,\bar{D}_{\infty}^{\theta_{0}}=0\}}),

which completes the proof.
∎

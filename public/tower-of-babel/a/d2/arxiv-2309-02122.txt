A Novel Holographic Framework Preserving Reflection Positivity in dS d Spacetime
Jean-Pierre Gazeau 1, Mariano A. del Olmo 2, Hamed Pejhan 3

Abstract
This manuscript introduces a novel holographic correspondence in d d -dimensional de Sitter (dS d ) spacetime, connecting bulk dS d scalar unitary irreducible representations (UIRs) with their counterparts at the dS d boundary ℐ ± {\cal{I}}^{\pm} , all while preserving reflection positivity. The proposed approach, with potential applicability to diverse dS d UIRs, is rooted in the geometry of the complex dS d spacetime and leverages the inherent properties of the (global) dS d plane waves, as defined within their designated tube domains.

IV
Plane waves: generating orthonormal bases for the scalar principal UIRs in the bulk of dS
d

The scalar principal waves do not display square integrability under the Klein-Gordon inner product. Nevertheless, they do give rise to the Klein-Gordon square-integrable, strictly speaking, orthonormal bases of the carrier Hilbert spaces of the scalar principal UIRs.

To illustrate this point, we invoke a set of bounded global coordinates appropriate for describing a bounded version of dS
d
spacetime. These intrinsic coordinates, known as conformal coordinates, can be expressed as:

\displaystyle x=\big(R\tan\rho,R(\cos\rho)^{-1}\mathbf{u}\big)\,,

(7)

where
-\frac{\pi}{2}<\rho<\frac{\pi}{2}
and
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{1}
(
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}_{1}
representing the unit
(d-1)
-dimensional sphere). Notably, the coordinate
\rho
serves as a timelike component and plays a crucial role as a conformal time parameter.

The expression

can be expressed using the conformal coordinates as:

\displaystyle\frac{x\cdot\xi}{R}=\frac{\xi^{0}e^{\mathrm{i}\rho}}{2\mathrm{i}\cos\rho}\big(1+r^{2}-2r(\textbf{u}\cdot\textbf{v})\big)\,,

(8)

where, above, we have defined
\xi\equiv(\xi^{0},\boldsymbol{\xi})\in C^{+}
, with
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{d})\equiv\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|\textbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,
\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|^{2}\equiv\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}
and
\textbf{v}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{d-1}
, and finally
r=\mathrm{i}e^{-\mathrm{i}\rho}
. Note that, since
\xi\in C^{+}
, we naturally have
\xi^{0}>0
and
\xi^{0}=\|\boldsymbol{\xi}\|
.

Then, by employing the generating function associated with Gegenbauer polynomials
C_{n}^{-\tau}(x)
(as outlined in, for instance, Ref.
Hua
), it becomes readily evident that:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{R}\right)^{\tau}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\xi^{0}e^{\mathrm{i}\rho}}{2\mathrm{i}\cos\rho}\right)^{\tau}\big(1+r^{2}-2r(\textbf{u}\cdot\textbf{v})\big)^{\tau}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\xi^{0}e^{\mathrm{i}\rho}}{2\mathrm{i}\cos\rho}\right)^{\tau}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}\;C_{n}^{-\tau}(\textbf{u}\cdot\textbf{v})\,.

(9)

Recall that
\real(\tau)<0
. This expansion lacks validity in the context of functions. This is because the generating function for the Gegenbauer polynomials (as clearly demonstrated above) exhibits convergence only when
|r|<1
. However, in our current scenario, we have
|r|=|\mathrm{i}e^{-\mathrm{i}\rho}|=1

\rho

\rho\mapsto\rho-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\;(\varepsilon>0)

{\cal{T}}^{-}

We also have the following auxiliary relation

\displaystyle C_{n}^{-\tau}(\textbf{u}\cdot\textbf{v})=\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{d}{2}-1\big)}{\Gamma(-\tau)}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}c_{k}\;C_{n-2k}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}(\textbf{u}\cdot\textbf{v})\,,

(10)

where:

\displaystyle c_{k}=\frac{\big(n-2k+\frac{d}{2}-1\big)\;\Gamma\big(k-\tau-\frac{d}{2}+1\big)\;\Gamma\big(n-k-\tau\big)}{k!\;\Gamma\big(-\tau-\frac{d}{2}+1\big)\;\Gamma\big(n-k+\frac{d}{2}\big)}\,,

(11)

and:

\displaystyle C_{L=n-2k}^{\frac{d}{2}-1}(\textbf{u}\cdot\textbf{v})=

\displaystyle\;\frac{2\,\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\big(L+\frac{d}{2}-1\big)\;\Gamma\big(\frac{d}{2}-1\big)}

\displaystyle\quad\times\;\sum_{l}{\cal Y}_{Ll}(\mathbf{u})\,{\cal Y}_{Ll}^{\ast}(\mathbf{v})\,,

(12)

in which
{\cal Y}_{Ll}
stands for the hyperspherical harmonics of degree
L

\mathbb{S}_{1}^{d-1}

\mathbf{v},\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{S}_{1}^{d-1}

Pursuing the above procedure, we arrive at the following pivotal expansion:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{R}\right)^{\tau}=2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\,(\xi^{0})^{\tau}\sum_{Ll}\Phi_{Ll}^{\tau}(x)\;{\cal Y}_{Ll}^{\ast}(\mathbf{v})\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\Phi_{Ll}^{\tau}(x)=\frac{\mathrm{i}^{L-\tau}\;e^{-\mathrm{i}(L-\tau)\rho}}{(2\cos\rho)^{\tau}}\;\frac{\Gamma(L-\tau)}{\Gamma\big(L+\frac{d}{2}\big)\;\Gamma(-\tau)}\;

\displaystyle\quad\times\;_{2}F_{1}\big(L-\tau\,,\,-\tau-{\textstyle{\frac{d}{2}}}+1\,;\,L+{\textstyle{\frac{d}{2}}}\,;\,-e^{-2\mathrm{i}\rho}\big)\;{\cal Y}_{Ll}(\mathbf{u})\,.

The following statements hold for any
\tau

First

\displaystyle\big\langle\Phi_{Ll}^{\tau}(x),\Phi_{L^{\prime}l^{\prime}}^{\tau}(x)\big\rangle_{\texttt{KG}}=\delta_{LL^{\prime}}\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\,.

(15)

The Klein-Gordon inner product

\displaystyle\big\langle\Phi(x),\Phi^{\prime}(x)\big\rangle_{\texttt{KG}}

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{i}\,\int_{\Sigma}\Big(\Phi^{\ast}(x){\partial}_{\gamma}\Phi^{\prime}(x)-\Phi^{\prime}(x){\partial}_{\gamma}\Phi^{\ast}(x)\Big)\;\mathrm{d}\sigma^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\big\langle\Phi(x),\Phi^{\prime}(x)\big\rangle_{\texttt{KG}}

\displaystyle\quad=\mathrm{i}\,\mathfrak{c}_{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{1}^{d-1},\rho=0}\Phi^{\ast}(\rho,\mathbf{u})\;\overset{\leftrightarrow}{\partial}_{\rho}\;\Phi^{\prime}(\rho,\mathbf{u})\;\mathrm{d}\mu(\mathbf{u})\,,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}_{\tau}=2^{2\real(\tau)}\,e^{\pi\imaginary(\tau)}\;\big|\Gamma(-\tau)\big|^{2}\,.

(17)

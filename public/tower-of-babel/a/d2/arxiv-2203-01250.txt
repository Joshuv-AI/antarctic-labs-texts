Abstract
. We consider first order local minimization problems of the form min ∫ ℝ N f ( u , ∇ u ) \min\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u,\nabla u) under a mass constraint ∫ ℝ N u = m \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u=m . We prove that the minimal energy function H ⁡ ( m ) H(m) is always concave, and that relevant rescalings of the energy, depending on a small parameter ε \varepsilon , Γ \Gamma -converge towards the H H -mass, defined for atomic measures ∑ i m i ​ δ x i \sum_{i}m_{i}\delta_{x_{i}} as ∑ i H ⁡ ( m i ) \sum_{i}H(m_{i}) . We also consider Lagrangians depending on ε \varepsilon , as well as space-inhomogeneous Lagrangians and H H -masses. 1.2.

Our first two assumptions are rather standard and guarantee the sequential lower semicontinuity of the functionals

Remarks and applications

We start with two situations where the expected

Vanishing parts do not contribute to energy.

By assumption

is continuous on

. Hence, we have proved

for every

3.

3.2.

We will first establish a lower bound for the minimal energy along vanishing sequences defined on varying subsets of

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{1}_{\mathrm{uloc}}({\Omega})}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\Omega\cap(x+[0,1)^{N})}\lvert u\rvert<+\infty.

It will be convenient to first extend our Sobolev functions to a neighbourhood
\Omega_{\delta}
of
\Omega
where for every

and every set

We will need to consider sufficiently regular domains for which we have an extension operator

on

with

\int_{\Omega_{n}}\lvert\nabla u_{n}\rvert^{p}\leq K_{1}\int_{\Omega_{n}}u_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

We deduce from Markov’s inequality, and

\displaystyle\nu^{2}_{n}([\eta,+\infty))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\int_{\{u_{n}\geq\eta\}}u_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\int_{\{u_{n}\geq\eta\}}u_{n}^{1-r}u_{n}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\theta_{n}\eta^{r-1}}\int_{\Omega_{n}}u_{n}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\theta_{n}\eta^{r-1}}\bigl(\lVert\nabla u_{n}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{n})}+\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{n})}\bigr)^{r\alpha}\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{L^{1}_{\mathrm{uloc}}({\Omega}_{n})}^{r(1-\alpha)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\eta^{r-1}}\bigl(1+\theta_{n}^{p-1}\bigr)\lVert u_{n}\rVert_{L^{1}_{\mathrm{uloc}}({\Omega}_{n})}^{r(1-\alpha)},

where in the last inequality, we have used the identity
\alpha r=p
and
3.19

depend only on

and

Since

is vanishing and

is bounded, the last term in the previous inequality goes to zero as

and it follows that
\nu_{n}^{2}\xrightharpoonup{\mathscr{C}_{b}^{\prime}}\delta_{0}
.

Step 3:
\nu_{n}^{3}\xrightharpoonup{\mathscr{C}_{b}^{\prime}}\delta_{0}
.
Fix

and
\eta>0
. One has by

\displaystyle\nu_{n}^{3}([M,+\infty))=\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\int_{\{\lvert\nabla u_{n}\rvert\geq M\}}u_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\int_{\{u_{n}<\eta\}\cap\{\lvert\nabla u_{n}\rvert\geq M\}}u_{n}+\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\int_{\{u_{n}>\eta\}}u_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta}{\theta_{n}}\lbm^{N}(\{\lvert\nabla u_{n}\rvert\geq M\})+\nu_{n}^{2}([\eta,+\infty))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta}{\theta_{n}}\frac{1}{M^{p}}\int_{\Omega_{n}}\lvert\nabla u_{n}\rvert^{p}+\nu_{n}^{2}([\eta,+\infty))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\eta K_{1}}{M^{p}}+\nu_{n}^{2}([\eta,+\infty)).

By the previous step, we know that

, hence taking the superior limit as

then

we get

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\nu_{n}^{3}([M,+\infty))=0
.
Since this is true for every

.

Step 4: conclusion.
By the previous steps, we deduce that
\nu_{n}\xrightharpoonup{\mathscr{C}_{b}^{\prime}}\delta_{(x_{0},0,0)}
as

. We define

as the lower semicontinuous envelope of

and weak convergence of

, we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega_{n}}f(\Phi_{n},u_{n},\nabla u_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\{u_{n}>0\}}\frac{f(\Phi_{n},u_{n},\nabla u_{n})}{u_{n}}u_{n}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,u,\xi)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\nu_{n}(x,u,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,u,\xi)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\delta_{(x_{0},0,0)}=f^{\prime}_{-}(x_{0},0^{+},0),

3.3.
Existence of optimal profiles

For the existence of an optimal profile in
1.2
, we need a criterion that rules out splitting and vanishing of minimizing sequences:

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
H:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a concave function. Then
H
is subadditive, and if for some
0<\theta<m
one has
H(m)=H(m-\theta)+H(\theta)
, then
H
is linear on
(0,m)
.

Proof.

By concavity,
t\mapsto\frac{H(t)}{t}
is non-increasing. Hence,

\displaystyle H(m)=\theta\frac{H(m)}{m}+(m-\theta)\frac{H(m)}{m}\leq\theta\frac{H(\theta)}{\theta}+(m-\theta)\frac{H(m-\theta)}{m-\theta}.

But, by assumption, the last inequality is an equality which means that
\frac{H(m)}{m}=\frac{H(\theta)}{\theta}=\frac{H(m-\theta)}{m-\theta}
. In particular, the monotone function
t\mapsto\frac{H(t)}{t}
must be constant on
[\theta,m]
, i.e.
H
must be linear on
[\theta,m]
. By concavity this is only possible if
H
is linear on
[0,m]
.
∎

We can now state and prove our existence result:

Proposition 3.12
.

Assume that
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\ni(u,\xi)\mapsto f(u,\xi)\in[0,+\infty]
satisfies
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
and
6
. Let

(resp.

). If the cost function

, defined in
1.3
, is not linear on
[0,m]
(resp.
[m,0]
), then the minimization problem in
1.3
admits a solution

, i.e.
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u=m
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(u,\nabla u)=H_{f}(m)
, such that
u\geq 0
(resp.
u\leq 0
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

We consider the case
m\geq 0
, the case
m<0
can then be deduced by considering
\tilde{f}(u,\xi)=f(-u,-\xi)
. We assume without loss of generality that
H_{f}
is finite on
(0,+\infty)

1.1

2.1
, the admissible class in

can be reduced to non-negative functions. In particular, if

in the following situation:

,

. We obtain

with

5.

5.1.
Scale-invariant Lagrangians and necessity of the slope assumption

Our assumption

is not very standard, but we need a condition of this type in order to get
\Gamma
-convergence of the rescaled energies

towards

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lvert v\rvert^{p^{\star}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p^{\star}}}\leq C\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall v\in L^{p^{\star}}\cap W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

to the function
8
8

8

Actually, we apply it to

is a suitable approximation of

and take

.

Hence, for every

, and even

Existence, uniqueness and properties of optimal profiles.

It is well-known that optimal profiles in the classical Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequalities do exist ; besides, up to rescalings and translations they are unique, radially decreasing and compactly supported. Existence of radially decreasing solutions is a consequence of Pólya–Szegö inequality (see for instance
[
BZ88
]
), compactness of the support follows for example from the compact support principle of Pucci, Serrin and Zou
[
PSZ99
]
or Pohozaev-type identities, and uniqueness from the work of Serrin and Tang
[
ST00
]
for instance. All these techniques may be adapted to our case for exponents
s\in[0,1)
, but we did not find a comprehensive reference, even more so when
s\in(-p^{\prime},0)
, except for
p=2
and
s<0
which is treated in
[
Dub98
]
. For this reason and for self-containedness, we provide a sketch of proof for the existence, symmetry, compactness of the support for
s\in(-p^{\prime},1])
of optimal profiles in
5.4
. We are only able to justify uniqueness for
s\in(0,1]
.

1. Existence of an optimal profile which is radially symmetric.
The radially symmetric decreasing rearrangement
u^{*}
of an admissible function
u\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfies
\lVert\nabla u^{*}\rVert_{p}\leq\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{p}
by the Pólya–Szegö inequality, and we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lvert u^{*}\rvert^{s}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\lvert u\rvert^{s}
by equimeasurability. Hence,

\mathcal{E}_{f}(u^{*})\leq\mathcal{E}_{f}(u).

Thus we can restrict the minimization to radially symmetric non-increasing functions. Take a minimizing sequence
(u_{n})_{n}
in this class:
u_{n}(\cdot)=v_{n}(\lvert\cdot\rvert)
with
v_{n}:[0,+\infty)
non-increasing,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{n}=m\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

(u_{n})_{n}

gives a uniform integrable decay in

at infinity ; when

so that the size of the support of

(a ball of radius

We deduce by lower semicontinuity of

converges in

to a minimizer

with mass

, i.e.
\mathcal{E}_{f}(u)=H_{f}(m)
and

. In particular,

2. Euler-Lagrange equation and compactness of the support.
Let
u(\cdot)=v(\lvert\cdot\rvert)
be a global minimizer of

with mass
m>0
, with

non-increasing. Let also

be the support of

if

and satisfies

(5.10)

\langle\delta\mathcal{E}_{f}(u)\,,\,w\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(p(\nabla u)^{p-1}\cdot\nabla w+su^{s-1}w)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=0.

In other words,

-p\Delta_{p}u+su^{s-1}=\lambda,\quad\text{in }B(0,R),

where

is the Lagrange multiplier associated to the mass constraint. One can see that

Indeed, let

be a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{f}
of mass

=\langle\lambda\,,\,\frac{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}m}u_{m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\frac{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}m}\langle\lambda\,,\,u_{m}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\frac{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}m}(\lambda m)=\lambda.

In particular,

is unique.

From the Euler-Lagrange equation, we also get that

is smooth in

by a bootstrap argument ; hence,

solves the Euler-Lagrange equation in the classical sense. In terms of the profile

-(pv^{\prime}(r)^{p-1}r^{N-1})^{\prime}+sv(r)^{s-1}r^{N-1}=\lambda r^{N-1},\quad\forall r\in(0,R).

For every

, integrating

(0,r)
yields

(5.13)

pv^{\prime}(r)^{p-1}r^{N-1}=\int_{0}^{r}(sv(\rho)^{s-1}-\lambda)\rho^{N-1}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho.

The LHS in

(0,R)

and

since the integrand goes to

, which is a contradiction. When

is bounded. In any case, we have thus proved that

3. Uniqueness when
s>0
.
We justify that there is a unique minimizer of the energy with mass
m
. Once we know that

does not vanish on
(0,R)
, the case of equality in Pólya–Szegö inequality (see
[

. Then, applying
[
ST00
, Theorem 1]
yields the uniqueness of radial solution to
\Delta_{p}u+f(u)=0
for the non-linearity
f(t)=\lambda-st^{s-1}
, thus we get that

is unique.

We now prove that
v^{\prime}<0
on
(0,R)
. Since the LHS in (

) is non-positive, the non-decreasing function

, which tends to

, must be negative near

for some

would be maximal and its derivative would vanish at

), this means that
g(r)=sv(r)^{s-1}-\lambda=0
so that

on
[0,r]
, but also that

integrates to

on

. Hence,

on

, a contradiction.

Conclusion and range of
\alpha
-masses obtained in this way.

To summarize, we have shown that if
-p^{\prime}<s\leq 1
then

is non-trivial. The converse is true. Indeed, if

then, by the preceding, there exists a radial decreasing minimizer

with

non-increasing and compactly supported on
[0,R]
,

. But, by the Young inequality,
\mathcal{E}_{f}(u)\geq c\int_{R/2}^{R}v(r)^{s/p^{\prime}}\lvert v^{\prime}(r)\rvert r^{N-1}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\geq c_{R}\int_{0}^{v(R/2)}t^{s/p^{\prime}}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t
with

, the latter being finite if and only if

.

Since
\alpha
, in

, is a monotone function of

, one may easily compute the range of parameters

that we obtain. If

and

are fixed,

5.4.
Branched transport approximation:

-masses of normal
1
-currents

Branched Transport is a variant of classical optimal transport (see
[

]
and Section 4.4.2 therein for a brief presentation of branched transport, and

]
for a vast exposition) where the transport energy concentrates on a network, i.e. a

\min\Big\{\mathbf{M}_{1}^{H}(w)\;:\;\mathrm{div}(w)=\mu^{-}-\mu^{+}\Big\},

where
\mu^{\pm}
are probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is mass-concave, and the

-mass

is this time defined for finite vector measures

whose distributional divergence is also a finite measure; in the language of currents, it is called a
1
-dimensional normal current. Any such measure may be decomposed into a
1
-rectifiable part
\theta\xi\cdot\hdm^{1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=6.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=4.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits\Sigma

and
\xi(x)
is a unit tangent vector to
\Sigma

\hdm^{1}
-a.e.

, and a
1
-diffuse part

satisfying
\lvert w^{\perp}\rvert(A)=0
for every
1
-rectifiable set

w=\theta\xi\cdot\hdm^{1}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=6.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=4.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits M+w^{\perp}.

The

-mass is then defined by:

(5.14)

\mathbf{M}_{1}^{H}(w)\coloneqq\int_{\Sigma}H(x,\theta(x))\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\hdm^{1}(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H^{\prime}(x,0)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\lvert w^{\perp}\rvert.

In the case

with

, a family of approximations of these functional has been introduced in
[

]
:

(5.15)

\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(w)=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varepsilon^{\gamma_{1}}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}+\varepsilon^{-\gamma_{2}}\lvert v\rvert^{\beta}&\text{if $w=v\lbm^{d}$, $v\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})$,}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

[

]

-mass

The functionals

The same slicing method would allow to extend our

for Lagrangians

5.5.
A Cahn-Hilliard model for droplets

Following the works
[

]
in the one-dimensional case and
[
Dub98
]
in higher dimension, we consider functionals on

of the form:

(5.17)

\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varepsilon^{-\rho}(W(u)+\varepsilon\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2})&if $\mu=u\lbm^{N},u\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R}_{+})$,\\

where

is a Borel function satisfying

for some exponent

[

]

Replacing

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varepsilon^{-N(1-s)}W(u)+\varepsilon^{N+2}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left([\varepsilon^{Ns}W(\varepsilon^{-N}\varepsilon^{N}u)]+\lvert\varepsilon^{N+1}\nabla u\rvert^{2}\right)\varepsilon^{-N}

where

Therefore if we take

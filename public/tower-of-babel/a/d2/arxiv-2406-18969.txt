Asymptotics of quantized barycenters of lattice polytopes with applications to algebraic geometry
Chenzi Jin, Yanir A. Rubinstein

Abstract
This article addresses a combinatorial problem with applications to algebraic geometry. To a convex lattice polytope P P and each of its integer dilations k ​ P kP one may associate the barycenter of its lattice points. This sequence of k k -quantized barycenters converge to the (classical) barycenter of the polytope considered as a convex body. A basic question arises: is there a complete asymptotic expansion for this sequence? If so, what are its terms? This article initiates the study of this question. First, we establish the existence of such an expansion as well as determine the first two terms. Second, for Delzant lattice polytopes we use toric algebra to determine all terms using mixed volumes of virtual rooftop polytopes, or alternatively in terms of higher Donaldson–Futaki invariants. Third, for reflexive polytopes we show the quantized barycenters are colinear to first order, and actually colinear in the case of polygons. The proofs use Ehrhart theory, convexity arguments, and toric algebra. As applications we derive the complete asymptotic expansion of the Fujita–Odaka stability thresholds δ k \delta_{k} on arbitrary polarizations on (possibly singular) toric varieties. In fact, we show they are rational functions of k k for sufficiently large k k . This gives the first general result on Tian’s stabilization problem for δ k \delta_{k} -invariants for (possibly singular) toric Fanos: δ k \delta_{k} stabilize in k k if and only if they are all equal to 1 1 , and when smooth if and only if asymptotically Chow semistable (a condition stronger than existence of a Kähler–Einstein metric). We also relate the asymptotic expansions to higher Donaldson–Futaki invariants of test configurations motivated by Ehrhart theory, and unify in passing previous results of Donaldson, Futaki, Ono, Sano, and Rubinstein–Tian–Zhang on existence of canonical Kähler metrics, obstructions, and stability thresholds.

Introduction

1.1
A combinatorial problem

This article initiates the study of a combinatorial problem:

what is the asymptotic behavior of the quantized (or discrete) barycenters of lattice polytopes?

To state this more precisely, let

M\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}

denote a lattice and
P\subset M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

The

\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P):=\frac{1}{k|kP\cap M|}\sum_{u\in kP\cap M}u,\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

considered as a convex body in

If so, what are the coefficients

The relation between stability thresholds and barycenters has a rich and long history.
Let
\Omega\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
be a Kähler class.
In 1983 Futaki introduced the eponymous invariant

In the case

Thus, Futaki’s obstruction can be rephrased geometrically as

Some thirty years later, Blum–Jonsson showed that Mabuchi’s theorem

\delta^{-1}=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\big[\langle\mathrm{Bc}(P_{X,\Omega}),v_{i}\rangle+b_{i}\big],

where

are the primitive generators of the one-dimensional cones of the fan determining

P_{X,\Omega}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}\left\{u\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,\left\langle u,v_{i}\right\rangle+b_{i}\geq 0\right\}

It was then realized by Rubinstein–Tian–Zhang that Blum–Jonsson’s formula in

, Corollary 7.1]
,

\delta_{k}^{-1}=\max_{i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}}\big[\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P_{X,-K_{X}}),v_{i}\rangle+1\big],

(1)

and that similarly

[

The formula (

In an appendix it is shown that (

Thus, the study of asymptotics of

for lattice polytopes is essentially equivalent to

\delta_{k}
for (possibly singular) toric varieties.
We employ this link in both directions in this article.
For general

as well as
determines

for general (possibly

Restricting to toric manifolds we then use (as well as add some
entries to) the well-developed
dictionary between Riemann–Roch theory and Ehrhart theory to answer

1.1
completely for
Delzant lattice polytopes

P
,
determining all

in terms of mixed volumes of virtual polytopes.
Thirdly, restricting to Gorenstein Fano varieties amounts to working with
the sub-class of reflexive polytopes for which we refine the general expansion
and show that
\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P)
are colinear, to first order, with
\mathrm{Bc}(P)
.
This is not true for general

Finally, for toric del Pezzos we show how the combinatorial and algebraic
approach give two completely different proofs of the same

or

are actually colinear with

that either convexity arguments or Ehrhart theory allows to replace the otherwise mysterious Ehrhart polynomial
expressions by completely explicit formulas involving the lattice-normalized measure or intersection numbers.

In a forthcoming article, the third in the series,
we unify the study of Tian’s and Fujita–Odaka’s invariants

2.3
Results for reflexive polytopes

Another subclass of lattice polytopes is that of reflexive polytopes.

Definition 2.9
.

[

A lattice polytope is called

Remark 2.10
.

We are not aware of a relationship between being reflexive and being Delzant. For instance, the square with vertices

and
(0,\pm 1)

Our third main result is the specialization of Theorem

quantized barycenters are, to first order,

colinear
,
and have a completely explicit expression.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let

be a reflexive polytope. Then

(recall Problem

\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P)=\mathrm{Bc}(P)\left(1+\frac{1}{2k}\right)+O(k^{-2}).

We give two completely different proofs of the two-dimensional part

Asymptotics of quantized barycenters of Delzant lattice polytopes

In this section, we express the asympototic expansion of the quantized barycenters in terms of mixed volumes of virtual polytopes using toric geometry. Here we restrict ourselves to Delzant lattice polytopes, namely the lattice polytopes that determines a smooth fan, i.e., the primitive generators of any
n
-dimensional cone form a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis. Recall Definition

Lemma 4.1
.

For divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
with associated virtual polytopes
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
,

V\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{n!}D_{1}\cdots D_{n}.

Proof.

Since any divisor can be written as the difference of two ample divisors, by multi-linearity we may assume
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
are ample. In that case the corresponding virtual polytopes
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
are polytopes. For
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}>0
, the divisor
k_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}D_{n}
is ample with associated polytope
k_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}P_{n}
. Therefore

\mathrm{Vol}\left(k_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}P_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{n!}\left(k_{1}D_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}D_{n}\right)^{n}.

Both sides are polynomials in
k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}
. In particular, the coefficients of
k_{1}\cdots k_{n}
in both sides of the equation should be equal, i.e.,

n!V\left(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\right)=D_{1}\cdots D_{n}.

∎

Definition 4.2

Define the Todd class

\mathrm{Td}(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}u_{j,0}^{(k)}+\sum_{l=1}^{kN_{j}-1}u_{j,l}^{(k)}+\frac{1}{2}u_{j,kN_{j}}^{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}u_{j,0}^{(k)}+\sum_{l=1}^{kN_{j}-1}\left(u_{j,0}^{(k)}+l\cdot w_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{j,0}^{(k)}+kN_{j}\cdot w_{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=k\sum_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}\left(u_{j,0}^{(k)}+\frac{kN_{j}}{2}w_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=k\sum_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}m_{j}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=k^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}m_{j}^{(1)}.

Combining these two equations and (
23
), we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{Bc}(kP\cap M)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k\#(kP\cap M)}\sum_{u\in kP\cap M}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\#(\partial(iP)\cap M)\right)}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{u\in\partial(kP)\cap M}u

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+1)(2k+1)}{6\left(1+\frac{k(k+1)}{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}m_{j}^{(1)},

and

\mathrm{Bc}(P)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P)=\frac{2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}m_{j}^{(1)}}{3\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}}.

Therefore

\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P)=\frac{(k+1)(2k+1)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}}{4+2k(k+1)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}}\mathrm{Bc}(P).

Finally, by Definition
2.1
,
N_{j}=\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}(F_{j}^{(1)})
(The line segment
F_{j}^{(1)}
has
N_{j}
fundamental parallelotopes). Then

\sum_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}=\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}\left(\partial P\right).

(28)

This concludes the proof.
∎

7
Asymptotics of
\delta_{k}
-invariants

In this section we study the asymptotics of
\delta_{k}
-invariant using the results of the previous sections.

Theorem 7.1
.

If
\delta_{k}(-K_{X})=1
for sufficiently large
k
, then
\delta_{k}(-K_{X})=1
for all
k
.

Proof.

By (
36
),
\delta_{k}(-K_{X})=1
if and only if
\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P)=0
, where
P
is the polytope associated to
X
. By Corollary
3.3
,
\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P)=0
for all
k
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.12
.

By Proposition
3.1
, (
34
) and (
36
),

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}(\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P),v_{i}\rangle+b_{i})

\displaystyle=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\left(\langle\mathrm{Bc}(P),v_{i}\rangle+b_{i}+\frac{\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}(\partial P)}{2\mathrm{Vol}(P)}\langle(\widetilde{\mathrm{Bc}}(\partial P)-\mathrm{Bc}(P)),v_{i}\rangle k^{-1}+O(k^{-2})\right)

\displaystyle=\delta^{-1}+\frac{\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}(\partial P)}{2\mathrm{Vol}(P)}\max_{i\in I}\langle\widetilde{\mathrm{Bc}}(\partial P)-\mathrm{Bc}(P),v_{i}\rangle k^{-1}+O(k^{-2}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\delta_{k}-\delta

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta^{2}(\delta^{-1}-\delta_{k}^{-1})}{\delta\delta_{k}^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\delta^{2}(\delta^{-1}-\delta_{k}^{-1})}{1-\delta(\delta^{-1}-\delta_{k}^{-1})}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{1}{\delta_{k}}\right)+O(k^{-2})

\displaystyle=-\delta^{2}\frac{\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}(\partial P)}{2\mathrm{Vol}(P)}\max_{i\in I}\langle\widetilde{\mathrm{Bc}}(\partial P)-\mathrm{Bc}(P),v_{i}\rangle k^{-1}+O(k^{-2}).

Since
\delta_{k}
is the minimum of finitely many rational functions, for sufficiently large
k
, there is a rational function that dominates the rest. Indeed, consider the asymptotic expansion and take the one whose coefficient dominates in the lexicographic order. Therefore
\delta_{k}
is a rational function of
k
when
k
is sufficiently large.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Recall

\delta_{k}=\min_{i=1,\ldots,d}\frac{1}{\left\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P),v_{i}\right\rangle+b_{i}}.

Since
\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P)
has an asymptotic expansion by Corollary
3.3
, for each
i=1,\ldots,d
,

\frac{1}{\left\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P),v_{i}\right\rangle+b_{i}}

has an asymptotic expansion. However,
\argmin_{i=1,\ldots,d}\left(\left\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P),v_{i}\right\rangle+b_{i}\right)^{-1}
is not necessarily constant for all
k
. Therefore we can only deduce that there is an expansion that works for sufficiently large
k
, but not necessarily all
k
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
X
be Fano. Then

\delta_{k}(-K_{X})=\delta(-K_{X})-\frac{1}{2k}\delta(-K_{X})(1-\delta(-K_{X}))+O(k^{-2}).

Proof.

By Theorem
5.5
,

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P),v_{i}\rangle+1

\displaystyle=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\langle\mathrm{Bc}(P),v_{i}\rangle\left(1+\frac{1}{2k}\right)+1+O(k^{-2})

\displaystyle=\delta^{-1}+\frac{1}{2k}\left(\delta^{-1}-1\right)+O(k^{-2}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\delta_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{1+\frac{1}{2k}(1-\delta)+O(k^{-2})}

\displaystyle=\delta-\frac{1}{2k}\delta(1-\delta)+O(k^{-2}).

∎

Lemma 7.4
.

For a smooth del Pezzo surface
X
,

h^{0}(X,-K_{X})=1+K_{X}^{2}.

In particular, if
X
is toric with associated polytope
P
, then

\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}\left(\partial P\right)=K_{X}^{2}.

Proof.

Since
-K_{X}
is ample, by Kodaira Vanishing Theorem

\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{X}))=h^{0}(X,-K_{X})
and by Riemann–Roch
[
24
, p. 472]

\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{X}))=\chi({\mathcal{O}}_{X})+K_{X}^{2}=h^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})-h^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})+h^{2}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})+K_{X}^{2}=1-h^{0,1}_{\bar{\partial}}+h^{0,2}_{\bar{\partial}}+K_{X}^{2}=1+K_{X}^{2}
.

By (
23
), (
27
) and (
28
),

h^{0}(X,-K_{X})=\#(P\cap M)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}=1+\widetilde{\mathrm{Vol}}\left(\partial P\right).

∎

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth toric del Pezzo surface. Then

\delta_{k}(-K_{X})=\left(1+\frac{(k+1)(2k+1)K_{X}^{2}}{4+2k(k+1)K_{X}^{2}}\left((\delta(-K_{X}))^{-1}-1\right)\right)^{-1},

(29)

In particular,

\frac{\delta_{k}(-K_{X})}{\delta(-K_{X})}=1-\frac{1}{2k}(1-\delta(-K_{X}))+O\left(k^{-2}\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
6.1
,

\displaystyle\delta_{k}(-K_{X})^{-1}-1

\displaystyle=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}(P),v_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+1)(2k+1)K_{X}^{2}}{4+2k(k+1)K_{X}^{2}}\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\langle\mathrm{Bc}(P),v_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+1)(2k+1)K_{X}^{2}}{4+2k(k+1)K_{X}^{2}}\left(\delta(-K_{X})^{-1}-1\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle\delta_{k}(-K_{X})

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{(k+1)(2k+1)K_{X}^{2}}{4+2k(k+1)K_{X}^{2}}\left((\delta(-K_{X}))^{-1}-1\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1+\frac{3}{2k}+O(k^{-2})}{1+\frac{1}{k}+O(k^{-2})}\left((\delta(-K_{X}))^{-1}-1\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left((\delta(-K_{X}))^{-1}+\frac{1}{2k}\left((\delta(-K_{X}))^{-1}-1\right)O(k^{-2})\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{2k}(1-\delta(-K_{X}))+O\left(k^{-2}\right)\right)\delta(-K_{X}),

as claimed.
∎

8
Relation to test configurations

In this section, we explain how the coefficients of the asymptotic expansion relate to the higher Donaldson–Futaki invariants.

Definition 8.1
.

•

A test configuration for
(X,L)
is a triple
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}},\sigma)
, where
\sigma
is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the pair
({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}})
, with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant projective morphism
\pi:{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
{\mathcal{L}}
is
\pi
-relatively very ample, i.e.,
\pi
lifts to
{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{r}_{\mathbb{C}^{1}}=\Proj\mathbb{C}[t][Z_{0},\ldots,Z_{r}]
which pulls
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
back to
{\mathcal{L}}
, and each fiber over
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, denoted by
({\mathcal{X}}_{t},{\mathcal{L}}_{t})
, is isomorphic to
(X,L)
.

•

We say that a test configuration is a product configuration if
{\mathcal{X}}\cong X\times\mathbb{C}
, and a trivial configuration if the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action on
{\mathcal{X}}\cong X\times\mathbb{C}
is trivial on the first factor.

•

The
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
{\mathcal{L}}
induces a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
H^{0}({\mathcal{X}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{k})
. Let
w_{k}
denote the sum of the weights of this action.

•

Define the Donaldson–Futaki coefficients to be the coefficients of the expansion

\frac{w_{k}}{kH^{0}\left({\mathcal{X}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{k}\right)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}DF_{i}\left({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}},\sigma\right)k^{-i}.

8.1
Construction of the rooftop test configuration

We construct a product test configuration

({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}},\sigma_{q(v)})

(30)

as follows.
Recall the pair
(\overline{{\mathcal{X}}},\overline{{\mathcal{L}}})
constructed in (
7
). The projection
M_{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
corresponds to an equivariant map
\pi:\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
(see Figure
2
). The subfan consisting of cones generated by
(v_{1},0),\ldots,(v_{d},0),(0,1)
(i.e., the cones on or above the hyperplane
M_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
) is the product of the fan of
X
and the fan of
\mathbb{C}
, and hence corresponds to the toric manifold
\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{C})\cong X\times\mathbb{C}
. Similarly, the subfan consisting of cones generated by
(v_{1},0),\ldots,(v_{d},0),(v,-1)
(i.e., the cones on or below the hyperplane
M_{\mathbb{R}}\times\{0\}
) corresponds to
\pi^{-1}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\{0\})\cong X\times\mathbb{C}
. Let
{\mathcal{X}}
denote the latter open submanifold and
{\mathcal{L}}
the restriction of
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}
over
{\mathcal{X}}
. Note that this product structure implies that each fiber of
\pi
is a copy of
(X,L)
.

The lattice point
(0,-1)\in N\times{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{*},(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n+1})
induces a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action expressed on the torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n+1}=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
(the first factor is the open-orbit
in
X
considered as a toric variety) as

\sigma(t):z\mapsto\sigma(t).z:=\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},t^{-1}z_{n+1}\right),\quad t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}.

By definition of a toric variety (both
X
and

are such) it is enough to specify the action on the open orbit and it will extend uniquely to

. Of course, it is not obvious what the action does at

as

blows up; this shows in passing that what we construct is not a trivial, but rather a product, test configuration (this test configuration is trivial if and only if
\overline{{\mathcal{X}}}=X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, which is precisely when

).
We can lift it to a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the line bundle
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}
by (recall

defined in (

\sigma_{q(v)}(t):\left(z,f\right)\mapsto\left(\sigma(t).z,t^{-q}f\right).

More precisely, for a section
s_{u^{\prime}}\in H^{0}(\overline{{\mathcal{X}}},\overline{{\mathcal{L}}})
given by

under the standard local trivialization over

\displaystyle\sigma_{q(v)}(t)\left(z,z^{u^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},t^{-1}z_{n+1}\right),t^{-q}z_{1}^{u^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{u^{\prime}_{n}}z_{n+1}^{u^{\prime}_{n+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},t^{-1}z_{n+1}\right),t^{u^{\prime}_{n+1}-q}z_{1}^{u^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{u^{\prime}_{n}}\left(t^{-1}z_{n+1}\right)^{u^{\prime}_{n+1}}\right)

Thus,

\sigma_{q(v)}(t)\left(s_{u^{\prime}}\right)=t^{u^{\prime}_{n+1}-q}s_{u^{\prime}}\circ\sigma_{t},

i.e., a section
s_{u^{\prime}}
has weight

.

to be the product test configuration (

8.2
Quantized barycenters and higher Donaldson–Futaki

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\left({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}},\sigma_{v}\right)
be the product test configuration (
30
) constructed in §

. Then

\left\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}\left(P\right),v\right\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}DF_{i}\left({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{L}},\sigma_{q(v)}\right)k^{-i}.

Proof.

Consider the lattice points on the facet

\left\{\left(u,\left\langle u,v\right\rangle+q\right)\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,u\in P\cap k^{-1}M\right\}.

They correspond to the torus invariant sections of
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}^{k}
that do not vanish along
{\mathcal{X}}_{0}={\mathcal{D}}_{d+2}

[
17
, p. 61]
.
For
u\in P\cap k^{-1}M
, let
s_{u}
denote the section of
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}^{k}
corresponding to
(u,\langle u,v\rangle+q)
, which has weight
k\langle u,v\rangle
. It follows that the section
s_{u}|_{{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{k}}
on
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{k}
also has weight

. Since

spans

\frac{w_{k}}{kH^{0}\left({\mathcal{X}}_{0},{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{k}\right)}=\frac{1}{kE_{P}\left(k\right)}\sum_{u\in P\cap k^{-1}M}k\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathrm{Bc}_{k}\left(P\right),v\right\rangle.

This concludes the proof.
∎

4.7
, we obtain an expression for the higher Donaldson–Futaki invariants of this test configuration.

.

On a result of Blum–Jonsson and Rubinstein–Tian–Zhang

Blum–Jonsson computed explicitly the
\delta
-invariant of toric
varieties
[
5
, Corollary 7.16]
using a valuative approach.
In the Fano case, Rubinstein–Tian–Zhang gave an alternative proof of their result
and also extended it to the
\delta_{k}
-invariants
by using an analytic approach with balanced metrics and coercivity
arguments
[
43
, Corollary 7.1]
.
The goal of this appendix is to give an alternative proof of the
Rubinstein–Tian–Zhang result and simultaneously extend it to any polarization, by showing that (as expected) the original valuative proof
of Blum–Jonsson for
\delta
adapts to handle also
\delta_{k}
.

We follow the valuative reformulation of the
\delta
-invariant due to Blum–Jonsson.
Let
(X,L)
be a polarized variety.
A
valuation
on
X
means a real valuation
w:\mathbb{C}(X)^{*}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
that is trivial on
\mathbb{C}
. We denote
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
the space of all valuations on
X
.
A
prime divisor

E

over

X
, meaning that
E
is a prime divisor on some birational model
Y
of
X
, defines a valuation
\mathrm{ord}_{E}:\mathbb{C}(X)^{*}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
in
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
as vanishing order at the generic point of
E
.
For any prime divisor
E
over
X
, the
log discrepancy
of
\mathrm{ord}_{E}
is defined by
A_{X}(\mathrm{ord}_{E}):=1+\mathrm{ord}_{E}(K_{Y/X})
.
We assume that
X
has klt singularities.
As showed in
[
7
]
, the log discrepancy can be extended to a lower semicontinuous function
A_{X}:\mathrm{Val}_{X}\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
that is homogeneous of order
1
, i.e.,
A_{X}(tw)=tA_{X}(w)
for
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Definition A.1
.

A
filtration

\mathcal{F}R_{\bullet}
of the graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
R=\oplus^{\infty}_{k=0}R_{k}=\oplus^{\infty}_{k=0}H^{0}(X,kL)
consists of a family of subspace
\{\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{k}\}_{\lambda}
of
R_{k}
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, satisfying:

(i)

(decreasing)
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{k}\subset\mathcal{F}^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{k}
if
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}
;

(ii)

(left-continuous)
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{k}=\cap_{\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda}\mathcal{F}^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{k}
;

(iii)

(multiplicative)
\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{k}\cdot\mathcal{F}^{\lambda^{\prime}}R_{k^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{F}^{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}}R_{k+k^{\prime}}

for any
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
;

(iv)

(linearly bounded)

\mathcal{F}^{0}R_{k}=R_{k}
and

\mathcal{F}^{\lambda}R_{k}=0
for
\lambda>>0
.

Any valuation

induces a filtration

{\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{\lambda}R_{k}:=\{s\in R_{k}~:~v(s)\geq\lambda\}

The jumping numbers of the filtration

a_{k,j}:=\inf\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}~:~{\rm codim}{\mathcal{F}}^{t}R_{k}\geq j\},\quad 1\leq j\leq N_{k}.

(32)

S_{k}(w):=\frac{1}{kN_{k}}\sum_{j}a_{k,j}

(33)

where
N_{k}:=h^{0}(X,kL)
.
The
expected vanishing order
is

S(w):=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(L)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Vol}(L;w\geq\lambda)d\lambda

\mathrm{Vol}(L;w\geq\lambda):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n!}{k^{n}}\dim{\mathcal{F}}_{w}^{\lambda k}R_{k},

\delta_{k}
- and
\delta

\delta_{k}(L)=\inf_{w}\frac{A_{X}(w)}{S_{k}(w)},\quad\delta(L)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\delta_{k}(L)=\inf_{w}\frac{A_{X}(w)}{S(w)},

w

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{b}}_{\lambda,k}({\mathcal{F}}):=\mathfrak{b}(|{\mathcal{F}}^{\lambda}R_{k}|),

The Schwarzschild Black Hole from Perturbation Theory to all Orders
Poul H. Damgaard, Kanghoon Lee

Abstract
Applying the quantum field theoretic perturbiner approach to Einstein gravity, we compute the metric of a Schwarzschild black hole order by order in perturbation theory. Using recursion, this calculation can be carried out in de Donder gauge to all orders in Newton’s constant. The result is a geometric series which is convergent outside a disk of finite radius, and it agrees within its region of convergence with the known de Donder gauge metric of a Schwarzschild black hole. It thus provides a first all-order perturbative computation in Einstein gravity with a matter source, and this series converges to the known non-perturbative expression in the expected range of convergence.

II
The metric

The Schwarzschild metric in harmonic gauge is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=-N\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\Bigg[F\delta^{ij}+\frac{G^{2}M^{2}}{N}\frac{x^{i}x^{j}}{r^{4}}\Bigg]\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\,,

(1)

where
r^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}
, and

N(r)=\frac{r-GM}{r+GM}\,,\qquad F(r)=\frac{(r+GM)^{2}}{r^{2}}\,.

(2)

We choose to work with Landau-Lifshitz variables and expand perturbatively around flat Minkowski
space as follows:

\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}\equiv\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}-h^{\mu\nu}\,.

(3)

With this choice of variables, the fluctuation field
h^{\mu\nu}
reads

h^{00}=-1+\frac{\left(r+GM\right)^{3}}{r^{2}\left(r-GM\right)}~~,~~~h^{ij}=\frac{G^{2}M^{2}x^{i}x^{j}}{r^{4}}\,.

(4)

Expanding
h^{00}
as a Laurent series around 1 we get

\displaystyle h^{00}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\left(1-\frac{GM}{r}\right)^{-1}-8-\frac{4GM}{r}-\frac{G^{2}M^{2}}{r^{2}}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4GM}{r}+\frac{7G^{2}M^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{8G^{3}M^{3}}{r^{3}}\ldots

(6)

Except for the few low-order corrections
h^{00}
is thus a geometric series in
GM/r
with fixed
coefficient 8, while
h^{ij}
truncates at second order.
The fluctuation field
h^{\mu\nu}
satisfies the de Donder gauge
condition
\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu})=\partial_{\mu}h^{\mu\nu}=0

which is equivalent to the harmonic gauge condition in these variables.

III
The Perturbiner method

We now set up the post-Minkowskian perturbative expansion for the Schwarzschild black hole metric
as defined in eq. (
3
).
We do this by generalizing the usual perturbiner method for scattering amplitudes to solutions of the perturbative Einstein equations. Specifically, we wish to generate the perturbative vacuum solution of Einstein gravity for a point-like source at the origin, corresponding to a Schwarzschild black hole.

The action is thus given by

S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\bigg[\frac{1}{2\kappa^{2}}\sqrt{-g}R+\frac{1}{2}j_{\mu\nu}(x)g^{\mu\nu}(x)\bigg]\,,

(7)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar and the source
j_{\mu\nu}(x)
is

j_{\mu\nu}(x)=Mv_{\mu}v_{\nu}\delta^{3}(\boldsymbol{x})\,,\qquad v_{\mu}=(-1,0,0,0)\,.

(8)

Here
\boldsymbol{x}
denotes the position 3-vector.
Let us consider the one-point function of
h^{\mu\nu}_{x}\equiv h^{\mu\nu}(x)
in the presence of the source

j_{\mu\nu}
,

\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{x}=\left\langle 0|h^{\mu\nu}_{x}|0\right\rangle_{j}=\frac{\delta W[j]}{\delta j_{x}^{\mu\nu}}\,,

(9)

where
W[j_{\mu\nu}]
is the generating functional

e^{W[j_{\mu\nu}]}\equiv\int\mathcal{D}h^{\mu\nu}\exp\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}S[h^{\mu\nu},j_{\mu\nu}]\right]\,.

(10)

Note that
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{x}
satisfies the classical equations of motion at tree level.
Since we are considering a time-independent solution, we restrict
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}(x)=\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}(\boldsymbol{x})
.

Expanding the generating functional in powers of
j_{\mu\nu}
,
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{x}
is given
in terms of connected correlation functions,

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{\boldsymbol{x}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}}\!\!\!\!\big\langle 0\big|T\big[h_{x}^{\mu\nu}h_{y_{1}}^{\kappa_{1}\lambda_{1}}\cdots h_{y_{n}}^{\kappa_{n}\lambda_{n}}\big]\big|0\big\rangle_{c}

(11)

\displaystyle\times\frac{ij_{y_{1}}^{\kappa_{1}\lambda_{1}}}{\hbar}\cdots\frac{ij_{y_{n}}^{\kappa_{n}\lambda_{n}}}{\hbar}\,,

where

\int_{x,y\cdots}=\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\mathrm{d}^{d}y\cdots\text{ and }\int_{p,q,\cdots}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}p}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{d}q}{(2\pi)^{d}}\cdots\,.

(12)

Hereafter, we will ignore the position of indices while keeping the summation convention for repeated indices.

Next, we substitute
j_{\mu\nu}
as defined in (
8
). It is useful to represent the delta function as

j^{\mu\nu}_{\boldsymbol{x}}=Mv^{\mu}v^{\nu}\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}e^{-i\boldsymbol{\ell}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

(13)

Then
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{x}
reduces to

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}J^{\mu\nu}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}\cdots\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}e^{-i\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12\cdots n}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\,,

(14)

where

J^{\mu\nu}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}\cdots\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}=\left(\frac{iM}{\hbar}\right)^{n}\big\langle 0\big|T\big[\tilde{h}_{-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12\cdots n}}^{\mu\nu}\tilde{h}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}^{00}\cdots\tilde{h}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}^{00}\big]\big|0\big\rangle_{c}\,,

(15)

and
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12\cdots n}=\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}+\cdots+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}
. Here
\tilde{h}_{\ell}^{\mu\nu}
is the Fourier transform of
h_{x}^{\mu\nu}
.
It is convenient to shift the loop momentum
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}\to-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12\cdots n}
so that

\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}e^{i\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}J^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}\,,

(16)

where

J^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}J^{\mu\nu}_{-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12\cdots n}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}\cdots\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}\,.

(17)

We denote the number of loop momenta of an off-shell current by its ‘rank’. For instance, the rank of
J^{\mu\nu}_{\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{n}}
is
n
. We then expand

\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}
according to this rank,
i.e.

\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G^{n}\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}~~,~~~\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}\equiv\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}J^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{{\ell}}}e^{i\boldsymbol{\ell}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

(18)

We now proceed to organize the perturbative expansion of the Einstein-Hilbert action. Because
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}
satisfies the classical equations of motion at tree level, we substitute (
3
)
into (
7
) and expand the equation of motion in powers of
G
,

\Box\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}=-2j^{\mu\nu}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tau^{\mu\nu}_{[n]}~,

(19)

where the
\tau^{\mu\nu}_{[n]}
’s arise in the well-known manner from gravitational self-interactions (and they will be given in
very compact forms below). This expansion is by construction also ordering the number of fields
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}
so that
\tau^{\mu\nu}_{[n]}
contains
(n+1)
fields.
The off-shell recursion relations are now obtained by substituting the perturbiner expansion.
The initial condition of the recursion is given by the rank-1 current,

\Delta\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}=-2j^{\mu\nu}=-2Mv^{\mu}v^{\nu}\int_{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\,,

(20)

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian.
Substituting the perturbiner expansion (
18
) for
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}

this provides us with the initial condition for the off-shell recursion relation

J^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=\frac{16\pi M}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{2}}v^{\mu}v^{\nu}\,,

(21)

or

J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=\frac{16\pi M}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{2}}\,,\qquad J^{0i}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=0\,,\qquad J^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=0\,.

(22)

We now proceed to rank-2 level. According to the initial condition in (
22
), the only nonvanishing component of
\mathsf{h}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}^{\mu\nu}
is
\mathsf{h}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}^{00}
.
Inserting the equations of motion to this order, we get the off-shell recursion relations at rank-2 from the
perturbiner expansion,

\displaystyle J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}|^{2}}\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell_{2}}}\bigg[\frac{5}{4}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}|^{2}-\frac{7}{8}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12}\cdot\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}\bigg]J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12}}J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle J^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}|^{2}}\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell_{2}}}\bigg[\frac{\ell^{(i}_{12}\ell^{j)}_{2}}{4}-\frac{\delta^{ij}\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12}\cdot\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}}{8}\bigg]J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12}}J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}}\,,

which shows how
J^{ij}
-components build up from the recursion. Performing the integrals, we get

\displaystyle J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=\frac{14\pi^{2}M^{2}}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|},~~J^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=\pi^{2}M^{2}\left[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|}-\frac{\ell^{i}\ell^{j}}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{3}}\right]\,.

(24)

Computational tedium would seem to prevent us from proceeding to rank-3 level (and beyond). However,
we do need the explicit evaluation for rank-3 in order to establish an all-order result by induction. This
is the first level at which we need to regularize the integrals and we choose to work with dimensional
regularization by going to
d=3-2\epsilon
dimensions at intermediate steps.
We first find
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}}
from the explicit forms of
\tau^{\mu\nu}_{[1]}
and

\tau^{\mu\nu}_{[2]}
by using the expansion of
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}
in (
18
). This gives

\displaystyle J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M^{3}\pi^{\epsilon+\frac{3}{2}}}{2^{-4\epsilon-1}|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{2\epsilon}}\frac{(\epsilon(2\epsilon+5)+6)\Gamma[1-2\epsilon]}{\Gamma\left[\frac{5}{2}-2\epsilon\right]}\Gamma[\epsilon]

(25)

\displaystyle J^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M^{3}\pi^{\epsilon+\frac{3}{2}}}{4^{-2\epsilon-1}|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{2\epsilon}}\frac{\delta^{ij}\epsilon\Gamma[3-2\epsilon]\Gamma[\epsilon-1]}{(2\epsilon-1)\Gamma[\frac{5}{2}-2\epsilon]}\,.

(26)

Fourier-transforming all of these results according to (
18
) and going to
d=3
dimensions, we obtain

\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}=\frac{4M}{r}v^{\mu}v^{\nu}\,,

(27)

so that

\mathsf{h}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}=\frac{4M}{r}\,,\qquad\mathsf{h}^{0i}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}=\mathsf{h}^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}}=0\,.

(28)

at rank-1 level. At rank-2,

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}e^{i\boldsymbol{\ell}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=\frac{7M^{2}}{r^{2}}\,,

(29)

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}e^{i\boldsymbol{\ell}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}J^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=\frac{M^{2}x^{i}x^{j}}{r^{4}}\,.

while at rank-3 we get

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}}=\frac{8M^{3}}{r^{3}}\,,\qquad\mathsf{h}^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}}=0\,.

(30)

These results match the metric of (
3
) to
{\mathcal{O}}(G^{3})
.

IV
Iterated loop integrals

As an intermediate step towards extending the above analysis to all orders we now demonstrate that
the loop integrals to any order are iterated one-loop bubble integrals, and hence solvable recursively.
We use the fact that a generic one-loop bubble integral is of the form

\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\frac{\ell^{\rho_{1}}\cdots\ell^{\rho_{m}}}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{\alpha_{1}}|\boldsymbol{\ell}+\boldsymbol{p}|^{\alpha_{2}}}=\frac{M^{\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{m}}[\boldsymbol{p}]}{|\boldsymbol{p}|^{\beta}}\,,

(31)

and apply it recursively to integrals of the perturbiner method,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}=

\displaystyle\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}}I^{\mu\nu,\kappa_{1}\lambda_{1},\kappa_{2}\lambda_{2},\cdots,\kappa_{i}\lambda_{i}}[\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}]

(32)

\displaystyle\quad\times

\displaystyle J^{\kappa_{1}\lambda_{1}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{1})}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12\cdots n}}J^{\kappa_{2}\lambda_{2}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{2})}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}}\cdots J^{\kappa_{i}\lambda_{i}}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(N_{i})}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}}\,,

where
n=N_{1}+N_{2}+\cdots N_{i}
. For off-shell currents of a single propagator,

J^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(m)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}=\frac{N^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(m)}}[\boldsymbol{\ell}]}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{\alpha_{m}}}\,,

(33)

we will now show that
\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}
in (
32
) again contains a single propagator by using (
33
) and (
31
).
For currents (
33
), we thus have

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}\frac{N^{\mu\nu}[\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}]}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12\cdots n}|^{\alpha_{1}}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}}\,,

(34)

where the numerator is polynomial in the arguments.
We now perform the
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}
-integration to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{3},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}\frac{N_{2}^{\mu\nu}[\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\boldsymbol{\ell}_{3}\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}]}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{13\cdots n}|^{\beta_{2}}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{3}|^{\alpha_{3}}\cdots|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}}\,,

(35)

with a new numerator
N_{2}^{\mu\nu}
.
Since the structure of the integrand is the same as before integration, we may repeat the previous procedure and perform the
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{3}
integral to get

\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{4},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}\frac{N_{3}^{\mu\nu}[\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1},\boldsymbol{\ell}_{4},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}]}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{14\cdots n}|^{\beta_{3}}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{4}|^{\alpha_{4}}\cdots|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}|^{\alpha_{n}}}\,,

(36)

with numerator
N_{3}^{\mu\nu}
.
This continues until all loop integrals are exhausted,
leading to

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{\mu\nu}_{n}[\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}]}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}|^{\beta_{n}}}\,,

(37)

where
N_{n}^{\mu\nu}
follows recursively in the manner indicated.

V
Perturbative solution to all orders

So far we have exploited off-shell recursion relations and iterated momentum integrals.
Our third step towards deriving an all-order result is to encode the fact that the metric and its inverse
expanded around flat Minkowski space are related by a geometric series which again is of iterative form.
We implement this by first considering
\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}
and its inverse
\mathfrak{g}_{\mu\nu}
as
independent fields, introducing an auxiliary field
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mu\nu}
whose value is fixed to be
the inverse of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}
:
\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\rho}\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\rho\nu}=\delta^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
.
We thus introduce perturbations of both
\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mu\nu}

Gomez:2021shh
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}-\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\tilde{\mathsf{h}}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(38)

which we expand in Newton’s constant
G
,

\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}G^{n}\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{\mu\nu}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}G^{n}\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}\,,

(39)

with the constraint

\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}=\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{\mu\rho}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n-m)}}\mathsf{h}^{\rho\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(m)}}\,.

(40)

We also introduce off-shell currents for
\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}

\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}e^{i\boldsymbol{\ell}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}}\tilde{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}\,,

(41)

where
\tilde{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}
satifies the following recursion relation:

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{p}}=J^{\mu\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{p}}+\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\tilde{J}^{\mu\rho}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n-m)}|\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{\ell}}J^{\rho\nu}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(m)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}\,.

(42)

We will now show that the expansion of
\mathsf{h}^{\mu\nu}
is

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{8M^{n}}{r^{n}}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\mathsf{h}^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad\text{for}~n\geq 3\,,

(43)

while
\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{00}
and
\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{ij}
are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{00}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\frac{(n+1)^{2}M^{n}}{r^{n}}\,,

(44)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathsf{h}}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{M^{2n}x^{i}x^{j}}{r^{2n+2}}\,.

As a first step, we will show by induction that the corresponding off-shell currents are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{(n+1)^{2}M^{n}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma[\frac{d-n}{2}]}{2^{n-d}\Gamma[\frac{n}{2}]}\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{d-n}}\,,

(45)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{M^{2n}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}2^{d-2n}\Gamma[\frac{d-2n}{2}]}{2\Gamma[n+1]|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{d-2n}}\Bigg[\delta^{ij}{-}\frac{(d{+}2n)\ell^{i}\ell^{j}}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{2}}\Bigg]\,,

\displaystyle J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{8M^{n}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\left[\frac{d-n}{2}\right]}{2^{n-d}\Gamma[\frac{n}{2}]}\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{d-n}}\,,\qquad\text{for~}n\geq 3

We begin by expressing the 00-component of the Einstein tensor in terms of the new variables.
From this we find that
\mathsf{h}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}
(
n\geq 4
) satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta\mathsf{h}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\partial_{i}\Bigg[\mathsf{h}^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}\partial_{j}\mathsf{h}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n-2)}}+\tilde{\mathsf{h}}{}^{kl}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n-2)}}\partial^{i}\mathsf{h}^{kl}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}-\tilde{\mathsf{h}}{}^{kl}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n-4)}}\partial_{j}\mathsf{h}^{kl}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}\mathsf{h}^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}

\displaystyle+\ell^{j}_{12}J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n-2)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12}}\Big)J^{ij}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}}\,,

where

\displaystyle X^{i}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}}\ell^{i}_{2}\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\tilde{J}^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n-m)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12}}J^{00}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(m)}|\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}}\,,

(49)

\displaystyle Y^{i}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{2}}\ell^{i}_{2}\tilde{J}^{kl}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(n-2)}|-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{12}}J^{kl}_{{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}|\boldsymbol{\ell_{2}}}\,.

\displaystyle=0\,,

(51)

To summarize this part, we have shown to all orders in perturbation theory that

is convergent for
GM/r<1
and sums to the correct expression in that region while the
expansion for

truncates at order
G^{2}
, in agreement with eq. (

).

As is well known, the harmonic gauge condition does not determine uniquely the standard harmonic-gauge metric (
4
) due to a residual choice of coordinate freedom within the harmonic gauge itself
Fromholz:2013hka
. This prompts the question as to how such a residual freedom should manifest itself within our recursive solution that seems to uniquely provide the standard form (
4
). The issue is resolved by noticing that the perturbative solution requires regularization of the pertinent loop integrals beyond the first few leading orders. Indeed, up to order
G^{2}
there is no need for regularizing the integrals and there is correspondingly no additional coordinate freedom of the harmonic-gauge metric
Fromholz:2013hka
. At order
G^{3}
we need to regularize the loop integrals. Simultaneously, we encounter constant numerators in the loop integrands. Those terms lead to (unregularized) integrals that are scale-free and they are set to zero in dimensional regularization. Other equally valid regularization schemes may set those integrals to other constant values, and thus introduce new scheme-dependent constants in the metric. By iteration, such constants will enter at all higher orders as well. This is in agreement with the arbitrariness stemming from residual coordinate freedom in this gauge.

VI
Conclusion

Applying the perturbiner method to Einstein gravity we have shown how
to derive the harmonic-gauge metric of a Schwarzschild black hole to all orders in perturbation theory.
This is the first example of a post-Minkowskian expansion in general relativity that has been
carried through to all orders and which therefore teaches us about the convergence of such
expansions. We find that the series is convergent within the expected range based on the closed expression of eq. (
4
). What makes this all-order result possible in spite of the
overwhelming complexity of perturbative gravity is the iterative systematics of the
perturbiner expansion for gravitational equations of motion, combined with recursive structures
of the involved loop integrals. In addition, complications arising from the
perturbative expansion of the Einstein-Hilbert action can be controlled by a doubling
of degrees of freedom at intermediate steps. This highlights the fact that also the weak-field
expansion of the action contains iterative structures, here inherited from the geometric series relation between metric and inverse metric, an observation made earlier in ref.
Cheung:2017kzx
. The perturbiner approach used here is an efficient
recursive technique for solving classical equations of motion. The resulting expressions resemble closely the classical parts of the loop expansion of perturbative gravity, and we have a few times used the language of the loop expansion even though all
integrations here arise only from the fact that the Green function technique works well through its Fourier transform.
The way classical contributions can arise in General Relativity from the loop expansion was clearly explained in
ref.
Holstein:2004dn
.
In a broader perspective, one may hope that the perturbiner approach to post-Minkowskian gravity
Cho:2021nim
;
Lee:2022aiu
;
Cho:2022faq
;
Adamo:2023cfp
;
Lee:2023zuu
;
Cho:2023kux
could lead to related simplifications for the general relativistic two-body problem.

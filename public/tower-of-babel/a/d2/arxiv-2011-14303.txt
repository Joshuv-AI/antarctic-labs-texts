A Probabilistic Higher-order Fixpoint Logic
Yo Mitani, Naoki Kobayashi, Takeshi Tsukada

Abstract
. We introduce PHFL, a probabilistic extension of higher-order fixpoint logic, which can also be regarded as a higher-order extension of probabilistic temporal logics such as PCTL and the μ p \mu^{p} -calculus. We show that PHFL is strictly more expressive than the μ p \mu^{p} -calculus, and that the PHFL model-checking problem for finite Markov chains is undecidable even for the μ \mu -only, order- 1 1 fragment of PHFL. Furthermore the full PHFL is far more expressive: we give a translation from Lubarsky’s μ \mu -arithmetic to PHFL, which implies that PHFL model checking is Π 1 1 \Pi^{1}_{1} -hard and Σ 1 1 \Sigma^{1}_{1} -hard. As a positive result, we characterize a decidable fragment of the PHFL model-checking problems using a novel type system.

1.
Introduction

Temporal logics such as CTL and CTL* have been playing important roles
in system verification.
Among the most expressive temporal logics is the
higher-order fixpoint logic
(
HFL
for short)
proposed by Viswanathan and Viswanathan
[
VV04
]
, which is a higher-order extension of the
modal
\mu
-calculus

[
Koz83
]
.
HFL is known to be strictly more expressive than the modal
\mu
-calculus but the model-checking problem against finite models is still decidable.

In view of the increasing importance of probabilistic systems,
temporal logics for probabilistic systems (such as PCTL
[
HJ94
]
)
and their model-checking problems have been studied and applied to verification and analysis of probabilistic systems and randomized distributed algorithms
[
KNP11
]
.
Recently Castro et al.
[
CKP15
]
have proposed a probabilistic extension of the modal
\mu
-calculus, called the
\mu^{p}
-calculus
.
They showed that the
\mu^{p}
-calculus is strictly more expressive than PCTL and
that the model-checking problem for the
\mu^{p}
-calculus belongs to NP
\cap
co-NP.

In the present paper, we introduce PHFL,
a probabilistic higher-order fixpoint logic, and studies the model checking problem.
PHFL can be regarded as a probabilistic extension of HFL and
as a higher-order extension of the
\mu^{p}
-calculus.
PHFL strictly subsumes the
\mu^{p}
-calculus
[
CKP15
]
,
which coincides with order-
0
PHFL.

We prove that
PHFL model checking for
finite Markov chains is undecidable even for the order-1 fragment of PHFL without fixpoint alternations,
by giving a reduction from the
value problem
of probabilistic automata
[
Rab63
,
Paz71
]
.
In the presence of fixpoint alternations (i.e., with both least and greatest fixpoint operators),
PHFL model checking is even harder: the order-1 PHFL model-checking problem is

\Pi^{1}_{1}
-hard and
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-hard.
The proof is by a reduction from the validity checking problem for
\mu
-arithmetic
[
Lub89
]

to PHFL model checking.
This may be surprising, because both
order-0 PHFL model checking (i.e.
\mu^{p}
-calculus model checking)
for finite Markov chains
[
CKP15
]
and
HFL model checking for finite state systems
[
VV04
]
are decidable.
The combination of probabilities and higher-order predicates suddenly makes the model-checking problem
highly undecidable.

As a positive result, we identify a decidable subclass of PHFL model-checking problems.
To characterize the subclass, we introduce a type system for PHFL formulas,
which is parameterized by a Markov chain
M
.
We show that the model-checking problem
M\models\varphi
is decidable provided that
\varphi
is typable in the type system for
M
, by giving a decision procedure using the decidability of existential theories of reals.
The decidable subclass is reasonably expressive: the problem of computing termination probabilities of
recursive Markov chains

[
EY09
]

can be reduced to the subclass.

The rest of this article is organized as follows.
Section
2
introduces PHFL and shows that it is strictly more expressive than the
\mu^{p}
-calculus.
Section
3
proves undecidability of the model-checking problem for

\mu
-only and order-
1
PHFL.
Section
4
proves that the PHFL model-checking problem is both
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-hard and
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-hard.
Section
5
introduces a decidable subclass of PHFL model-checking problems, and shows that
the subclass is reasonably large.
Section
6
discusses related work, and
Section
7
concludes the paper.
A preliminary summary of this article has been published
in Proceedings of FSCD 2020
[
MKT20
]
. This article contains details omitted in
the preliminary summary, and also significantly extends the decidable fragment of PHFL
in Section
5
.

2.
PHFL: Probabilistic Higher-order Fixpoint Logic

This section introduces PHFL, a probabilistic extension of HFL
[
VV04
]
.
PHFL is a logic used for describing properties of Markov chains.
We define its syntax and semantics and show that it is more expressive than
the
\mu^{p}
-calculus
[
CKP15
]
.

2.1.
Markov Chains

We first recall the standard notion of Markov chains. Our definitions follow those in
[
CKP15
]
.

Definition
\thethm
.

Let
\mathit{AP}
be a set of atomic propositions.
A
Markov chain
over
\mathit{AP}
is a tuple
(S,P,\rho_{\mathit{AP}},s_{\text{in}})
, where:

•

S
is a finite set of states,

•

P:S\times S\to[0,1]
satisfying
\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}P(s,s^{\prime})=1

for every
s\in S
, describes transition probabilities,

•

\rho_{\mathit{AP}}:\mathit{AP}\to 2^{S}
is a labeling function, and

•

s_{\text{in}}\in S
is an initial state.

For a Markov chain
M=(S,P,\rho_{\mathit{AP}},s_{\text{in}})
, its
embedded Kripke structure
is
K=(S,R,\rho_{\mathit{AP}},s_{\text{in}})
where

R\subseteq S\times S
is a relation such that
R=\{(s,s^{\prime})|P(s,s^{\prime})>0\}
.

Intuitively,
P(s,s^{\prime})
denotes the probability that the state
s
transits
to the state
s^{\prime}
, and
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(p)
gives the set of states where
p
is true.
Throughout the paper, we assume that the set
\mathit{AP}
of atomic propositions
is closed under negations, in the sense that for any
p\in\mathit{AP}
,
there exists
\overline{p}\in\mathit{AP}
such that
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(\overline{p})=S\setminus\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(p)
.

Given a Markov chain
M
, we often write

S_{M},P_{M},\rho_{\mathit{AP},M},s_{\text{in},M}
for its components; we omit
the subscript
M
when it is clear from the context.

2.2.
Syntax of PHFL Formulas

As in HFL
[
VV04
,
KLB17
]
, we need the notion of types to
define the syntax of PHFL formulas.

The set of types, ranged over by
\tau
, is given by:

\tau::=\mathit{Prop}\,|\,\tau_{1}\rightarrow\tau_{2}.

The type
\mathit{Prop}
describes
quantitative
propositions, whose values
range over
[0,1]
. Intuitively, the value of a quantitative proposition represents the
probability

that the proposition holds. The type
\tau_{1}\to\tau_{2}
is for functions from
\tau_{1}
to
\tau_{2}
.
For example,
(\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop})\to\mathit{Prop}
represents the type of (higher-order, quantitative) predicates
on unary predicates.

Remark
\thethm
.

In the
\mu^{p}{}
-calculus
[
CKP15
]

and the previous version of this paper
[
MKT20
]
, two kinds of propositions were considered:

quantitative
propositions,
which take values in
[0,1]
, and

qualitative
propositions, which take truth values.
In the present paper, we consider only quantitative propositions for the sake of simplicity,
and regard qualitative propositions as a special case of the former
by treating
0
and
1
as the truth values “false” and “true” respectively.

We assume a countably infinite set

\mathit{Var}
of variables, ranged over by
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
.
The set of PHFL (pre-)formulas, ranged over by
\phi
, is given by:

\phi::=p\,|\,X\,|\,\phi_{1}\lor\phi_{2}\,|\,\phi_{1}\land\phi_{2}\,|\,[\phi]_{J}\,|\,\square\phi\,|\,\lozenge\phi\,|\,\mathbin{\bigcirc}\phi\,|\,\mu X.\phi\,|\,\nu X.\phi\,|\,\lambda X.\phi\,|\,\phi_{1}\,\phi_{2}.

Here,

p
ranges over the set
\mathit{AP}
of atomic propositions
(of the underlying Markov chains; we thus assume that
\mathit{AP}
is closed under negations).
The subscript
J
of
[\phi]_{J}

is either “
>r
” or “
\geq r
” for some rational number
r\in[0,1]
.
We often identify
J
with an interval:
for example, “
>r
” is regarded as
(r,1]=\{\,x\mid r<x\leq 1\,\}
.
Given a quantitative proposition
\phi
, the formula
[\phi]_{>r}
(resp.
[\phi]_{\geq r}
) is a qualitative formula, which is true just if the probability that
\phi
holds is greater than
r
(resp. no less than
r
).
We exclude trivial bounds “
>1
” and “
\geq 0
”;
note that
[\phi]_{>1}
and
[\phi]_{\geq 0}
are equivalent
to false and true respectively.
The formulas
\square\phi
,
\lozenge\phi
, and

\mathbin{\bigcirc}\phi
respectively mean the minimum, maximum, and average
probabilities that
\phi
holds after a one-step transition.
The formulas
\mu X.\phi
and
\nu X.\phi
respectively
denote the least and greatest fixpoints
of
\lambda X.\phi
.
Note that
\phi
may denote higher-order predicates, as in HFL
[
VV04
]
(but unlike in the modal
\mu
-calculus and
its probabilistic variants
[
CKP15
,
MS13
,
MM97
]
,
where fixpoints are restricted to propositions).
We have also
\lambda
-abstractions and applications,
which are used for manipulating higher-order predicates.
The prefixes

\mu X
,
\nu X
and
\lambda X
bind the variable
X
.
As usual,
we identify formulas up to the renaming of bound variables and
implicitly apply
\alpha
-conversions. We write

[\phi_{1}/X]
for the capture-avoiding substitution of
\phi_{1}
for

X
, and
[\phi_{1}/X]\phi_{2}
for the formula obtained by
applying the substitution
[\phi_{1}/X]
to
\phi_{2}
.

In order to exclude out ill-formed formulas like
(p_{1}\lor p_{2})(\phi)
,
we restrict the shape of
formulas using a simple type system.
A
type environment
is a map from a finite set of variables to the set of types.
A
type judgment
is of the form
\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau
.
The typing rules are shown in Figure
1
.
A formula
\phi
is
well-typed

if
\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau
is derivable for some
\Gamma
and
\tau
.
Henceforth, we consider only well-typed formulas.

Example 2.1
.

For a proposition
p\in\mathit{AP}
, the formula
\phi=(\mu F.\lambda X.X\lor F(\mathbin{\bigcirc}X))p

is
a well-typed formula of type
\mathit{Prop}
.
By unfolding the fixpoint formula (i.e., replacing
\mu X.\phi
with

[\mu X.\phi/X]\phi
, which will be justified by the semantics introduced later)
and applying
\beta
-reductions, we obtain:

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\equiv(\lambda X.X\lor(\mu F.\lambda X.X\lor F(\mathbin{\bigcirc}X))(\mathbin{\bigcirc}X)){p}

\displaystyle\equiv{p}\lor(\mu F.\lambda X.X\lor F(\mathbin{\bigcirc}X))(\mathbin{\bigcirc}{p})

\displaystyle\equiv{p}\lor\mathbin{\bigcirc}{p}\lor(\mu F.\lambda X.X\lor F(\mathbin{\bigcirc}X))(\mathbin{\bigcirc}\mathbin{\bigcirc}{p})

\displaystyle\equiv{p}\lor\mathbin{\bigcirc}{p}\lor\mathbin{\bigcirc}\mathbin{\bigcirc}{p}\lor\cdots

Thus, intuitively,
the formula represents
the function that maps the current state
s
to the value
\sup_{k\geq 0}q_{k}
where
q_{k}
is the probability
that a
k
-step transition sequence
starting from the state
s
ends in a state satisfying
p
. ∎

.

Following the definition of HFL by Kobayashi et al.
[
KLB17

,
we have excluded out negations.
By a transformation similar to that in

]

and our assumption that the set of atomic propositions is closed under negations,
any closed ground-type formula of PHFL extended with

can be transformed to an equivalent negation-free formula
(as long as the occurrences of negations are restricted
as in the original HFL

so that fixpoint operators are applied to only monotonic functions). ∎

We define the
order
of a type
\tau
by:

\mathit{order}(\mathit{Prop})=0\qquad\mathit{order}(\tau_{1}\rightarrow\tau_{2})=\max(\mathit{order}(\tau_{1})+1,\mathit{order}(\tau_{2})).

The order of a formula
\phi
such that
\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau
is
the largest order of types used in the derivation of
\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau
.
The
order-
k
PHFL
is the fragment of PHFL consisting of formulas of order up to
k
.
Order-
0
PHFL coincides with the
\mu^{p}
-calculus
[

.

2.3.
Semantics

We first give the semantics of types.
We write
\leq_{\mathbb{R}}
for the natural order over the set
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers,
and often omit the subscript when there is no danger of confusion.
For a map
f
, we write
\mathit{dom}(f)
for the domain of
f
.

Definition 2.3

(Semantics of Types)
.

Let
M
be a Markov chain.
For each
\tau
, we define
a partially ordered set
\llbracket\tau\rrbracket_{M}=(D_{\tau},\sqsubseteq_{\tau})

inductively by:

\begin{array}[]{l}D_{\mathit{Prop}}=S_{M}\to[0,1]\qquad f\sqsubseteq_{\mathit{Prop}}g\,\overset{\text{def}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\,\forall s\in S_{M}.f(s)\leq g(s)\\
D_{\tau_{1}\rightarrow\tau_{2}}=\{f\in D_{\tau_{1}}\rightarrow D_{\tau_{2}}\mid\forall x,y\in D_{\tau_{1}}.x\sqsubseteq_{\tau_{1}}y\implies f(x)\sqsubseteq_{\tau_{2}}f(y)\}\\
f\sqsubseteq_{\tau_{1}\rightarrow\tau_{2}}g\,\overset{\text{def}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\,\forall x\in D_{\tau_{1}}.f(x)\sqsubseteq_{\tau_{2}}g(x).\end{array}

For a type environment
\Gamma
, we write

\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket_{M}
for the set of maps
f
such that

\mathit{dom}(f)=\mathit{dom}(\Gamma)
and
f(x)\in D_{\Gamma(x)}

for every
x\in\mathit{dom}(\Gamma)
.

We omit the subscript
M
below.
Note that
\llbracket\tau\rrbracket
forms a complete lattice for each
\tau
.
We write
\bot_{\tau}
for the least element of
\llbracket\tau\rrbracket
, and
for a set
V\subseteq D_{\tau}
, we write
\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\tau}}}V

(
\textstyle{\bigsqcap_{\tau}}V
, resp.) for the least
upper
bound (greatest lower bound, resp.)
of
V
with respect to
\sqsubseteq_{\tau}
; we often omit the subscript
\tau
if it is clear from the context.
Note also that for every functional type
\tau_{1}\to\tau_{2}
,
every element of
D_{\tau_{1}\to\tau_{2}}
is monotonic.
Thus, for every type
\tau
and every function
f\in D_{\tau\rightarrow\tau}
,
there exist
the least and greatest fixed points of
f
, which we write
\mathit{LFP}(f)

and
\mathit{GFP}(f)
respectively. They are given by:

\mathit{LFP}(f)=\textstyle{\bigsqcap_{\tau}}\{x\in D_{\tau}\mid f\,x\sqsubseteq_{\tau}x\}\qquad\mathit{GFP}(f)=\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\tau}}}\{x\in D_{\tau}\mid x\sqsubseteq_{\tau}f\,x\}.

We now define the semantics of formulas.
Since the meaning of a formula depends on its type environment,
we actually define the semantics
\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau\rrbracket_{M}

for each type judgment
\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau
.
Here, the subscript
M
denotes the underlying Markov chain, which is often omitted.

Definition 2.4

(Semantics of Type Judgement)
.

Let
M
be a Markov chain and assume that
\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau
is derivable.
Then its semantics
\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau\rrbracket_{M}\in\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket\to\llbracket\tau\rrbracket
is defined by induction on
the (unique) derivation of
{\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau}
by:

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash p:{\mathit{Prop}}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda s\in S_{M}.\text{if $s\in\rho_{\mathit{AP},M}(p)$ then $1$ else $0$}

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash X:\tau\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(X)

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi_{1}\land\phi_{2}:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda s\in S_{M}.\min_{i\in\{1,2\}}\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi_{i}:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)(s)

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi_{1}\lor\phi_{2}:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda s\in S_{M}.\max_{i\in\{1,2\}}\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi_{i}:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)(s)

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash[\phi]_{J}:{\mathit{Prop}}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda s\in S_{M}.\text{if $\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)(s)\in J$ then $1$ else $0$}

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\square\phi:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda s\in S_{M}.\min_{s^{\prime}:P_{M}(s,s^{\prime})>0}\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\lozenge\phi:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda s\in S_{M}.\max_{s^{\prime}:P_{M}(s,s^{\prime})>0}\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\mathbin{\bigcirc}\phi:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda s\in S_{M}.\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S_{M}}\big(P_{M}(s,s^{\prime})\cdot\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi:\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)(s^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\mu X.\phi:\tau\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathit{LFP}(\lambda v\in D_{\tau}.\llbracket\Gamma,X:\tau\vdash\phi:\tau\rrbracket_{M}(\rho[X\mapsto v]))

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\nu X.\phi:\tau\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathit{GFP}(\lambda v\in D_{\tau}.\llbracket\Gamma,X:\tau\vdash\phi:\tau\rrbracket_{M}(\rho[X\mapsto v]))

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\lambda X.\phi:\tau_{1}\to\tau_{2}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda v\in D_{\tau_{1}}.\llbracket\Gamma,X:\tau_{1}\vdash\phi:\tau_{2}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho[X\mapsto v])

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi_{1}\,\phi_{2}:\tau\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi_{1}:\tau_{2}\to\tau\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)\,(\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi_{2}:\tau_{2}\rrbracket_{M}(\rho))

In the last equality,
\tau_{2}
is uniquely determined from
\Gamma
and
\phi_{2}
.
In the definitions of the semantics of
\square\phi
and
\lozenge\phi
,
the set
\{s^{\prime}\in S_{M}|P(s,s^{\prime})>0\}
is non-empty and finite, because

\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S_{M}}P(s,s^{\prime})=1
and
S_{M}
is finite
by the definition of Markov chains. Thus
the max/min operations are well-defined.
We also note that
\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau\rrbracket
is a monotone function from
\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
to
\llbracket\tau\rrbracket
(where
\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket
is ordered
by the component-wise ordering; note also Remark
2.5
below).
This ensures the well-definedness of the semantics of
\mu X.\phi
,

\nu X.\phi
, and
\lambda X.\phi
.

Remark 2.5
.

Recall that in a formula
[\phi]_{J}
, we allow the predicate
J
to be
“
>r
” or “
\geq r
” (where
r\in[0,1]
), but neither “
<r
”
nor “
\leq r
”.
Allowing “
<r
” would break the monotonicity of the semantics of a formula.
For example,
\llbracket\emptyset\vdash\lambda X.[X]_{<1}:\mathit{Prop}\to{\mathit{Prop}}\rrbracket=\lambda v\in D_{\mathit{Prop}}.\lambda s\in S.(\mbox{if $v(s)<1$ then $1$ else $0$})

is not monotonic. ∎

We often omit
M
, the type of the formula, and the type environment,
and just write

\llbracket\phi\rrbracket
or
\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi\rrbracket
for
\llbracket\Gamma\vdash\phi:\tau\rrbracket_{M}
when
there is no danger of confusion.
For a Markov chain

M=(S,P,\rho_{\mathit{AP}},s_{\text{in}})
and
a closed PHFL formula
\phi
of type
{\mathit{Prop}}
,
we write
M\models\phi
if

\llbracket\phi\rrbracket(s_{\text{in}})=1
.

Example 2.6
.

Recall the PHFL formula
\phi=\psi\,{p}
where

\psi=\mu F.\lambda X.X\lor F(\mathbin{\bigcirc}X)
in Example
2.1
.
We have:

\displaystyle\llbracket\psi\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\mathit{LFP}\Big(\lambda v\in D_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}.\lambda x\in D_{\mathit{Prop}}.\lambda s\in S.

\displaystyle\hphantom{\lambda v \in D_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}. \lambda x\in D_{\mathit{Prop}}}\max\Big(x\,s,v\,(\lambda s^{\prime}\in S.\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}}P(s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime})\cdot(xs^{\prime\prime}))\,s\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\lambda v.\lambda x.\lambda s.\max\Big(x\,s,v\,(\lambda s^{\prime}\in S.\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}}P(s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime})\cdot(xs^{\prime\prime}))\,s\Big)\Big)^{n+1}(\bot_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}})

\displaystyle=\lambda x.\lambda s.\max_{0\leq k\leq n}\sum_{s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{k}\in S^{k+1},s_{0}=s}\big(x(s_{k})\cdot\prod_{0\leq j\leq k-1}P(s_{j},s_{j+1})\big)

for every
n\geq 0
.
Thus, we have:

\llbracket\psi\rrbracket\geq\lambda x.\lambda s\in S.\sup_{k\geq 0}\sum_{s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{k}\in S^{k+1},s_{0}=s}\big(x(s_{k})\cdot\prod_{0\leq j\leq k-1}P(s_{j},s_{j+1})\big).

Actually, the equality holds, because
the righthand side is a fixpoint of

\lambda v\in D_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}.\lambda x\in D_{\mathit{Prop}}.\lambda s\in S.\max(x\,s,v(\lambda s\in S.\sum_{s^{\prime}}P(s,s^{\prime})\cdot(xs^{\prime}))).

The semantics of
\phi
is, therefore, given by

\llbracket\phi\rrbracket=\lambda s\in S.\sup_{k\geq 0}\sum_{s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{k}\in S^{k+1},s_{0}=s,s_{k}\in\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(p)}\prod_{0\leq j\leq k-1}P(s_{j},s_{j+1}).

2.4.
Expressive Power

PHFL obviously subsumes the
\mu^{p}
-calculus
[
CKP15
]
, which coincides with order-
0
PHFL.
Hence PHFL also subsumes PCTL
[
HJ94
]
, since the
\mu^{p}
-calculus subsumes PCTL
[
CKP15
]
.

PHFL is
strictly
more expressive than the
\mu^{p}
-calculus.

Theorem 2.6
.

Order-1 PHFL is strictly more expressive than the
\mu^{p}
-calculus, i.e.,
there exists an order-1 PHFL proposition
\phi

such that
\phi
is not equivalent to any
\mu^{p}
-formula.

Proof 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the set of Markov chains

M=(S,P,\rho_{\mathit{AP}},s_{\text{in}})
that satisfy the following conditions.

•

S=\{s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{n}\}
for a positive integer
n
,

•

P(s_{i},s_{i+1})=1
(
0\leq i\leq n-1
),
P(s_{n},s_{n})=1
and
P(s_{i},s_{j})=0
otherwise.

•

There are three atomic propositions
a,b,c
with
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(a)\cup\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(b)=\{s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1}\}
,

\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(a)\cap\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(b)=\emptyset
and
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(c)=\{s_{n}\}
.

•

The initial state is
s_{\text{in}}=s_{0}
.

Let
\phi
be the order-1 PHFL formula of type
{\mathit{Prop}}
:

(\mu F.\lambda X.a\land\lozenge(X\lor F(b\land\lozenge X)))(b\land\lozenge c).

Note that, for
M\in\mathcal{M}
,
M\models\phi
holds just if

n
is even,
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(a)=\{s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\}

and
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(b)=\{s_{\frac{n}{2}},s_{\frac{n}{2}+1},\dots,s_{n-1}\}
.

We show that there is no
\mu^{p}
-formula equivalent to
\phi
.
Suppose that a
\mu^{p}
-formula
\phi^{\prime}

were
equivalent to
\phi
,
which would imply that
M\models\phi
if and only if
M\models\phi^{\prime}
for
any
M\in\mathcal{M}
.
For
M\in\mathcal{M}
, let us write
K_{M}
for the embedded Kripke structure
of
M
.
Since all the transitions in
{M}
are deterministic,
there exists a modal
\mu
-calculus formula
\phi^{\prime\prime}
such that

M\models\phi^{\prime}
if and only if
K_{M}\models\phi^{\prime\prime}

(note that
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is obtained by replacing
\mathbin{\bigcirc}
with

\lozenge
, and replacing
[\phi_{1}]_{J}
with

true
if
J
is “
\geq 0
” and with
\phi_{1}
otherwise).
That would imply that
K_{M}\models\phi^{\prime\prime}

for
M\in\mathcal{M}
,
just if

n
is even and

\rho_{\mathit{AP}}
satisfies
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(a)=\{s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\}

and
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(b)=\{s_{\frac{n}{2}},s_{\frac{n}{2}+1},\dots,s_{n-1}\}
.
But then
\phi^{\prime\prime}
would describe
the non-regular language
\{a^{m}b^{m}\mid m\geq 1\}
,
which contradicts the fact that the modal
\mu
-calculus can express
only regular properties.

For non-probabilistic logics,

-calculus

.
The above proof can be easily adapted to show that fact.

3.
Undecidability of PHFL Model Checking

In this section we prove the undecidability of
the following problem.

Definition 3.1

(PHFL Model Checking)
.

The
PHFL model-checking problem
for finite Markov chains
is the problem of
deciding
whether
M\models\phi
, given
a (finite) Markov chain
M
and a closed PHFL formula
\phi
of type
{\mathit{Prop}}
as input.

We prove that the problem is undecidable
even for the order-1 fragment of PHFL without fixpoint alternations,
by a reduction from
the undecidability of the value-1 problem
[
GO10
]
for
probabilistic automata
[
Rab63
]
.
In contrast to the undecidability of PHFL model checking,
the corresponding model-checking problems are
decidable

for the full fragments of
the
\mu^{p}
-calculus
[
CKP15
]
and
(non-probabilistic) HFL
[
VV04
]
,
with fixpoint alternations.
Thus, the combination of
probabilities and higher-order predicates introduces a new difficulty.

In Section
3.1
,
we review the definition of probabilistic automata and the value-1 problem.
Section
3.2
shows
the reduction from the value-1 problem
to the PHFL model-checking problem.

3.1.
Probabilistic Automata

We review probabilistic automata
[
Rab63
]
and
the undecidability of the value-1 problem.
Our definition follows
[
Fij17
]
.

Definition 3.2

(Probabilistic Automata)
.

A
probabilistic automaton

A
is a quintuple

(Q,\Sigma,q_{I},\Delta,F)
where

•

Q
is a finite set of states,

•

\Sigma
is a finite set of input symbols,

•

q_{I}\in Q
is an initial state,

•

\Delta:Q\times\Sigma\to D(Q)
, where
D(Q):=\{\,f\colon Q\to[0,1]\mid\sum_{q\in Q}f(q)=1\,\}

is the set of probabilistic distributions over the set
Q
,
represents transition probabilities, and

•

F\subseteq Q
is a set of accepting states.

For a word
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}\in\Sigma^{n}
,
the probability that
w
is accepted by
A=(Q,\Sigma,q_{I},\Delta,F)
,
written
A(w)
, is defined by:

A(w):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{0},\dots,q_{n-1}\in Q,q_{n}\in F\\
\mathit{s.t.}\ q_{0}=q_{I}\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\Delta(q_{i-1},w_{i})(q_{i}).

The
value
of a probabilistic automaton
A
, denoted by

\text{val}(A)
, is defined by

\text{val}(A):=\sup_{w\in\Sigma^{\ast}}A(w).

The problem of deciding whether
\text{val}(A)=1
,
called the
value-1 problem
, is known to be undecidable.

Theorem 3.2

(Undecidability of The Value-1 Problem
[
GO10
]
)
.

Given
a probabilistic automaton
A
,
whether

\text{val}(A)=1
is undecidable.

3.2.
The Undecidability Result

Let
A=(Q,\Sigma,q_{I},\Delta,F)
be
a probabilistic automaton, where
\Sigma=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{|\Sigma|}\}

with
|\Sigma|>0
.
We shall construct
a Markov chain
M_{A}
and a PHFL formula
\phi_{A}
, so that
\text{val}(A)=1
if and only if

M_{A}\models\phi_{A}
. The undecidability of PHFL model checking then
follows immediately from Theorem
2
.

We first construct the Markov chain
M_{A}
.
The set
\mathit{AP}
of atomic propositions is
\{\,p_{c}\mid c\in\Sigma\,\}\uplus\{\,p_{F}\,\}
.
The Markov chain
M_{A}=(S,P,\rho_{\mathit{AP}},s_{\text{in}})
is defined as follows.

•

The set
S
of states is
Q\uplus(Q\times\Sigma)
.

•

The transition probability
P
is given by:

\displaystyle P((q,c),q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\Delta(q,c)(q^{\prime})

(
c\in\Sigma
and
q,q^{\prime}\in Q
)

\displaystyle P(q,(q,c))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}

(
c\in\Sigma
and
q\in Q
)

\displaystyle P(s,s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=0

(otherwise)

The first transition (from
(q,c)
to
q^{\prime}
) is used to simulate
the transition of
A
from
q
to
q^{\prime}
for the input symbol
c
.
The second transition (from
q
to
(q,c)
) is used to
choose the next input symbol to be supplied to the automaton; the probability is not important, and replacing
1/|\Sigma|
with any non-zero probability does not affect the arguments below.

•

\rho_{\mathit{AP}}
is defined by:

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(p_{c})

\displaystyle=\{\,(q,c)\mid q\in Q\,\}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(p_{F})

\displaystyle=\{\,q\mid q\in F\,\}.

•

The initial state is
s_{\text{in}}=q_{I}
.

Intuitively, the Markov chain
M_{A}
simulates the behavior of

A
. The atomic proposition
p_{c}
means that
A
is currently reading the symbol

c
, and
p_{F}
means that
A
is in a final state.

Based on this intuition, we now construct the PHFL formula
\phi_{A}
.
For each
c\in\Sigma
, we define a formula
f_{c}

of type
\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}
by:

f_{c}:=\lambda X.\lozenge({p_{c}}\land\bigcirc X).

Intuitively
f_{c}(\phi)
denotes the probability that the automaton
transits to a state satisfying
\phi
given
c
as the next input.
Given a word
w=w_{1}w_{2}\dots w_{n}\in\Sigma^{*}
, we define the
formula
g_{w}
by

g_{w}:=f_{w_{1}}(f_{w_{2}}(\dots(f_{w_{n}}{p_{F}})\dots)).

We write
A_{q}
for the automaton obtained from
A
by replacing the initial
state with
q
. The following lemma states that
g_{w}
represents
the probability that
w
is accepted by the automaton from the current state
q
.

Lemma 3.3
.

A_{q}(w)=\llbracket g_{w}\rrbracket_{M_{A}}(q)
for every
q\in Q
.

Proof 3.4
.

Let
A=(Q,\Sigma,q_{I},\Delta,F)
.
The proof proceeds by induction on the length
|w|
of
w
.

•

Case where
|w|=0
, i.e.,
w=\epsilon
:
By the definition of
A_{q}
,

A_{q}(\epsilon)=1
if
q\in F
and
0
otherwise.
We have the required result, as
g_{\epsilon}={p_{F}}
.

•

Case where
|w|>0
:
Let
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}=w_{1}w^{\prime}
.
We have:

\displaystyle A_{q}(w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{0},\dots,q_{n-1}\in Q,q_{n}\in F\;\mathit{s.t.}\ q_{0}=q\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\Delta(q_{i-1},w_{i})(q_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q}\Delta(q,w_{1})(q^{\prime})\cdot(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}\dots,q_{n-1}\in Q,q_{n}\in F\;\mathit{s.t.}\ q_{1}=q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{2\leq i\leq n}\Delta(q_{i-1},w_{i})(q_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q}\Delta(q,w_{1})(q^{\prime})\cdot A_{q^{\prime}}(w^{\prime}).

Since
g_{w}=f_{w_{1}}(g_{w^{\prime}})\equiv\lozenge({p_{w_{1}}}\land\bigcirc g_{w^{\prime}})
,
we have:

\displaystyle\llbracket g_{w}\rrbracket_{M_{A}}(q)

\displaystyle=\max_{c\in\Sigma}\llbracket{p_{w_{1}}}\land\bigcirc g_{w^{\prime}}\rrbracket(q,c)

\displaystyle=\llbracket\bigcirc g_{w^{\prime}}\rrbracket(q,w_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{q^{\prime}\in Q}\Delta(q,w_{1})(q^{\prime})\cdot\llbracket g_{w^{\prime}}\rrbracket(q^{\prime})

By the induction hypothesis, we have
A_{q^{\prime}}(w^{\prime})=\llbracket g_{w^{\prime}}\rrbracket(q^{\prime})
,
which implies the the required result.

Using Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

\text{val}(A)=\sup_{n\in\omega}\llbracket\bigvee_{w\in\Sigma^{\leq n}}g_{w}\rrbracket_{M_{A}}(q_{I})
,
where
\Sigma^{\leq n}
is the set of words of length up to
n
.
This can be expressed by using the least fixpoint operator.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\theta_{A}
be the formula of type
\mathit{Prop}\rightarrow\mathit{Prop}
defined by:

\theta_{A}:=\mu F.\big(\lambda X.X\lor\bigvee_{c\in\Sigma}F\,(f_{c}\,X)\big).

Then
\text{val}(A)=\llbracket\theta_{A}\,{p_{F}}\rrbracket_{M_{A}}(q_{I})
.
Therefore
M_{A}\models\phi_{A}
if and only if
\text{val}(A)=1
,
for
\phi_{A}:=[\theta_{A}\,{p_{F}}]_{\geq 1}
.

Proof 3.5
.

Let

\xi:=\lambda F.\lambda X.X\vee\bigvee_{c\in\Sigma}F\,(f_{c}\,X).

Then, we have

\llbracket\theta_{A}\rrbracket_{M}\quad=\quad\llbracket\mu F.\xi\,F\rrbracket_{M}\quad=\quad\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}}}\{\llbracket\xi^{n}(\bot)\rrbracket\mid n\in\omega\}

where
\bot:=\lambda Z.\mu U.U
is the formula of type
\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}
,
and
\xi^{n}(x)
denotes
n
-times applications of
\xi
to
x
.
In fact,
\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}}}\{\llbracket\xi^{n}(\bot)\rrbracket\mid n\in\omega\}

is a fixpoint of
\llbracket\xi\rrbracket
, because:

\displaystyle\llbracket\xi\rrbracket(\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}}}\{\llbracket\xi^{n}(\bot)\rrbracket\mid n\in\omega\})

\displaystyle=\lambda x\in D_{\mathit{Prop}}.x\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}}}}\big(\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup}}_{c\in\Sigma}(\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}}}\{\llbracket\xi^{n}(\bot)\rrbracket\mid n\in\omega\})(\llbracket f_{c}\rrbracket\;x)\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda x\in D_{\mathit{Prop}}.x\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}}}}\big(\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup}}_{c\in\Sigma}(\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}}}}\{\llbracket\xi^{n}(\bot)\rrbracket(\llbracket f_{c}\rrbracket\;x)\mid n\in\omega\})\big)

\displaystyle=\lambda x\in D_{\mathit{Prop}}.\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}}}}\{x\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}}}}\big(\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup}}_{c\in\Sigma}\llbracket\xi^{n}(\bot)\rrbracket(\llbracket f_{c}\rrbracket\;x)\big)\mid n\in\omega\}

\displaystyle=\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}}}\{\llbracket\xi^{n+1}(\bot)\rrbracket\mid n\in\omega\}.

Since
\llbracket\xi\rrbracket
is monotonic and

\llbracket\bot\rrbracket
is the least element, we also have:

\llbracket\mu F.\xi\,F\rrbracket_{M}=\llbracket\xi^{n}(\mu F.\xi\,F)\rrbracket\sqsupseteq\llbracket\xi^{n}(\bot)\rrbracket

for any
n\in\omega

hence also

\llbracket\mu F.\xi\,F\rrbracket_{M}\sqsupseteq\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}}}}\{\llbracket\xi^{n}(\bot)\rrbracket\mid n\in\omega\}.

Thus, we have the equality.

By a straightforward induction on
n
, we also have:

\llbracket\xi^{n+1}(\bot)\,{p_{F}}\rrbracket_{M}=\llbracket\bigvee_{w\in\Sigma^{\leq n}}g_{w}\rrbracket_{M}
.
Therefore, by using also Lemma
3.3
, we obtain:

\text{val}(A)\quad=\quad\sup_{n}(\llbracket\bigvee_{w\in\Sigma^{\leq n}}g_{w}\rrbracket_{M_{A}}(q_{I}))\quad=\quad\sup_{n}(\llbracket\xi^{n+1}(\bot){p_{F}}\rrbracket(q_{I}))\quad=\quad\llbracket\theta_{A}\,{p_{F}}\rrbracket_{M_{A}}(q_{I}),

which implies the required result.

The following is an immediate corollary of
Theorems
2
and
3
.

Corollary 3.6

(Undecidability of PHFL Model-Checking Problem)
.

There is no algorithm that, given a Markov chain
M
and a closed order-1
formula
\phi
of type
{\mathit{Prop}}
, decides whether
M\models\phi
.

4.
Hardness of the PHFL Model-Checking Problem

In the previous section, we have seen that
PHFL model checking is undecidable
even for the fragment of PHFL without fixpoint alternations.
In this section,
we give a lower bound of the hardness of the PHFL model-checking problem
in the presence of fixpoint alternations.
The following theorem states the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.0
.

The order-1 PHFL model-checking problem is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-hard and
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-hard.

Note that
\Pi^{1}_{1}
and
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
, defined in terms of the second-order arithmetic, contain very hard problems.
For example, those classes contain
the problem of deciding whether a given first-order Peano arithmetic formula is true.

We prove this theorem by reducing
the validity checking problem of the
\mu
-arithmetic
[
Lub89
]

to the PHFL model-checking problem.
Even the validity checking problem of a higher-order extension of the
\mu
-arithmetic
can be reduced to the PHFL model-checking problem. The key in the proof
is a representation of natural numbers as quantitative propositions such that all
the operations on natural numbers in the
\mu
-arithmetic are expressible in PHFL.

This section is structured as follows.
Section
4.1
reviews
the basic notions of the
\mu
-arithmetic.
Section
4.2
describes the reduction
and proves the theorem above.

4.1.
Higher-Order Fixpoint Arithmetic

The
\mu
-arithmetic
[
Lub89
]
is a first-order arithmetic with fixpoint operators.
This section briefly reviews its higher-order extension, studied by Kobayashi et al.
[
KTW18
]
.

As in PHFL, we first define the types of
\mu
-arithmetic formulas.
The set of
types
, ranged over by
A
, is given by:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle::=N\,|\,T

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle::=\Omega\,|\,A\to T.

The type
N
is for natural numbers,

\Omega
for (qualitative) propositions,
and
A\to T
for functions.
We do not allow functions to return values of type
N
.
We define the order of types of the
\mu
-arithmetic similarly to the PHFL types,
by:
\mathit{order}(N)=\mathit{order}(\Omega)=0

and
\mathit{order}(A\to T)=\max(\mathit{order}(A)+1,\mathit{order}(T))
.

Assume a countably infinite set
\mathit{Var}
of variables ranged over by
X
.
The set of formulas, ranged over by
\varphi
, is given by the following grammar.

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle::=X\,|\,Z\,|\,Ss

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle::=s\,|\,s_{1}\leq s_{2}\,|\,\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}\,|\,\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2}\,|\,\lambda X.\varphi\,|\,\varphi_{1}\,\varphi_{2}\,|\,\mu X.\varphi\,|\,\nu X.\varphi.

Here,
Z
and
S
respectively denote the constant
0

and the successor function on natural numbers.

\Gamma,X\mathbin{:}A\vdash_{\mu}X:A

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}Z:N

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}s:N

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}S\,s:N

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}s:N

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}t:N

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}s\leq t:\Omega

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}:\Omega

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{2}:\Omega

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}:\Omega

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}:\Omega

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{2}:\Omega

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2}:\Omega

\Gamma,X:A\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:T

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\lambda X.\varphi:A\rightarrow T

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}:A\rightarrow T

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{2}:A

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi\,\varphi_{2}:T

\Gamma,X:T\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:T

\Gamma,X:T\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:T

\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\nu x.\varphi:T

Definition 4.1

(Semantics of Types)
.

The semantics of a type
A

A

(1)

The semantics of
N

:

\displaystyle D_{N}

\displaystyle D_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{\Longleftrightarrow}p\leq q

(2)

The semantics of
A\to T
:

\displaystyle D_{A\to T}

\displaystyle=\{\,f:D_{A}\to D_{T}\mid\forall u,v\in D_{A}.u\sqsubseteq_{A}v\implies f(u)\sqsubseteq_{T}f(v)\,\}

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}X:A\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\theta(X)

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}Z:N\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=0

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}Ss:N\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}s:N\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)+1

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}s\leq t:\Omega\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{(if $\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}s:N\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)\leq\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}t:N\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)$)}\\
0&\mbox{(if $\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}s:N\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)>\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}t:N\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)$)}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2}:\Omega\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}:\Omega\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)\mathbin{\textstyle{\bigsqcup_{\Omega}}}\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{2}:\Omega\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2}:\Omega\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}:\Omega\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)\textstyle{\bigsqcap_{\Omega}}\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{2}:\Omega\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\lambda X.\varphi:A\to T\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\lambda v\in\llbracket A\rrbracket_{\mu}.\llbracket\Gamma,X:A\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:T\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta[X\mapsto v])

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}\,\varphi_{2}:T\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{1}:A\to T\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)\,(\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi_{2}:A\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta))

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\mu X.\varphi:T\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\mathit{LFP}(\lambda v\in D_{T}.\llbracket\Gamma,X:T\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:T\rrbracket(\theta[X\mapsto v]))

\displaystyle\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\nu X.\varphi:T\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

\displaystyle:=\mathit{GFP}(\lambda v\in D_{T}.\llbracket\Gamma,X:T\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:T\rrbracket(\theta[X\mapsto v]))

As in the case of PHFL, we write
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)
for

\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:A\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)

and
just
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mu}
for
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mu}(\emptyset)

when there is no confusion.

Example 4.3
.

Let
\varphi=\mu F.\lambda X.(X=100\lor F(S(S\,X)))
where

100
is an abbreviation of the term

\underbrace{S(S(\dots S}_{100}\,Z)\dots)
.
The semantics
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mu}
is a function
f:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,1\}

where
f(n)=1
just if

n
is an even number no greater than
100
.

The
validity checking problem
of the higher-order fixpoint arithmetic is the problem
of,
given a closed formula
\varphi
of type
\Omega
,
deciding whether
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mu}=1
.
The following result is probably folklore,
which follows from the well-known fact that
the
fair termination problem for programs
is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-complete (see, e.g., Harel
[
Har86
]
),
and the fact that the fair termination of a program can be reduced to the validity of
a first-order fixpoint arithmetic formula (see, e.g.,

[
KTW18
]
for the reduction).

Theorem 4.3
.

The validity checking problem of the first-order fixpoint arithmetic is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-hard
and
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-hard.

Remark 4.4
.

As for an upper bound,
Lubarsky
[
Lub89
]
has shown that predicates on natural numbers definable by
\mu
-arithmetic formulas
belong to
\Delta^{1}_{2}
.
One can prove that the validity problem for the
\mu
-arithmetic is
\Delta^{1}_{2}
as well.

4.2.
Hardness of PHFL Model Checking

We give a reduction from the validity checking problem of the higher-order fixpoint arithmetic
to the PHFL model-checking problem.
The main theorem of this section (Theorem
4
) is an immediate consequence of this reduction and Theorem
5
.

Given a formula
\varphi
of the higher-order fixpoint arithmetic, we need to effectively construct a pair

(\phi,M)
of a formula
of PHFL and a Markov chain such that
\varphi
is true if and only if
M\models\phi
.
The Markov chain
M
is independent of the formula
\varphi
.
We first define the Markov chain and then explain the intuition of the translation of formulas.

The Markov chain
M=(S,P,\rho_{\mathit{AP}},s_{\text{in}})
is shown in Figure
3
.
It is defined as follows.

•

The set of states is
S=\{s_{0},s^{\prime}_{0},s_{1},s_{1}^{\prime}\}
.

•

The transition probability satisfies
P(s_{0},s_{1})=P(s_{0},s^{\prime}_{0})=P(s^{\prime}_{0},s_{0})=P(s^{\prime}_{0},s_{1}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}
,

P(s_{1},s_{0})=P(s_{1}^{\prime},s^{\prime}_{0})=1

and
P(s_{i},s_{j})=0
for all other pairs of states.

•

There are four atomic propositions
p_{0},p_{0}^{\prime},p_{1}
, and
p_{1}^{\prime}
,
representing each state (e.g.
\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(p_{0})=\{s_{0}\}
).

•

The initial state
s_{\text{in}}
is
s_{0}
.

Figure 3.
The Markov Chain for Reduction from Higher-order Fixpoint Arithmetic to PHFL.

For notational convenience, we write
v\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket_{M}
as a tuple
(v(s_{0}),v(s^{\prime}_{0}),v(s_{1}),v(s_{1}^{\prime}))
.

As mentioned at the beginning of this section, the key of the reduction is the representation of natural numbers, as well as operations on natural numbers.
We encode a propositional formula
\varphi

into a quantitative propositional formula
\phi
such that

\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{M}=(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mu},\_,\_,\_)
,
and encode a natural number
n
into a quantitative propositional formula

\psi
such that

\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{M}=(\frac{1}{2^{n}},1-\frac{1}{2^{n}},\_,\_)
.
Here,
\_
denotes a “don’t care” value.
We implement primitives on natural numbers
Z
,
S
and
\leq
, as follows.

The constant
Z
can be represented by
{p_{0}}
.
Indeed,
\llbracket{p_{0}}\rrbracket_{M}=(1,0,0,0)=(1/2^{0},1-(1/2^{0}),0,0)
as expected.

Assuming that
\phi
represents

n
(i.e.
\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{M}=(1/2^{n},1-(1/2^{n}),\_,\_)
), the successor
n+1
can be represented by

\phi^{\prime}\quad:=\quad\bigcirc((\bigcirc\phi\land(p_{1}\lor p_{1}^{\prime}))\lor p_{0}).

Indeed, we have:

\displaystyle\llbracket\bigcirc\phi\rrbracket_{M}

\displaystyle=(\_,\_,\frac{1}{2^{n}},1-\frac{1}{2^{n}})

\displaystyle\llbracket\bigcirc\phi\land(p_{1}\lor p_{1}^{\prime})\rrbracket_{M}

\displaystyle=(0,0,\frac{1}{2^{n}},1-\frac{1}{2^{n}})

\displaystyle\llbracket(\bigcirc\phi\land(p_{1}\lor p_{1}^{\prime}))\lor p_{0}\rrbracket_{M}

\displaystyle=(1,0,\frac{1}{2^{n}},1-\frac{1}{2^{n}})

\displaystyle\llbracket\bigcirc((\bigcirc\phi\land(p_{1}\lor p_{1}^{\prime}))\lor p_{0})\rrbracket_{M}

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{2^{n}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\times(1-\frac{1}{2^{n}}),\_,\_)

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2^{n+1}},1-\frac{1}{2^{n+1}},\_,\_).

It remains to encode
\leq
.
We use the fact that, for any natural numbers
n
and
m
,

n\leq m\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{2^{n}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{m}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{2^{n}}+(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}})\geq 1.

The
s_{0}^{\prime}
-component of the representation of a natural number plays an important role
below.
Assume that
\phi
and
\chi
represent
n
and
m
respectively.
Then we have

\llbracket\bigcirc\phi\land p_{1}\rrbracket_{M}=(0,0,\frac{1}{2^{n}},0)\qquad\llbracket\chi\land p_{0}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{M}=(0,1-\frac{1}{2^{m}},0,0)

and thus

\llbracket(\bigcirc\phi\land p_{1})\lor(\chi\land p_{0}^{\prime})\rrbracket_{M}=(0,1-\frac{1}{2^{m}},\frac{1}{2^{n}},0).

Therefore

\llbracket\bigcirc((\bigcirc\phi\land p_{1})\lor(\chi\land p_{0}^{\prime}))\rrbracket_{M}=(\frac{1}{2}\times\big(\frac{1}{2^{n}}+(1-\frac{1}{2^{m}})\big),\_,\_,\_).

Thus,
n\leq m
if and only if the
s_{0}
-component of the above formula is
\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
In other words,
n\leq m
just if:

\llbracket[\bigcirc((\bigcirc\phi\land p_{1})\lor(\chi\land p_{0}^{\prime}))]_{\frac{1}{2}}\rrbracket_{M}=(1,\_,\_,\_).

Let us formalize the above argument.
We first give the translation of types:

\displaystyle\textit{tr}(N)

\displaystyle=\mathit{Prop}

\displaystyle\textit{tr}(\Omega)

\displaystyle={\mathit{Prop}}

\displaystyle\textit{tr}(A\to T)

\displaystyle=\textit{tr}(A)\to\textit{tr}(T).

The translation can be naturally extended to type environments.
Following the above discussion, the translation of formulas of type
N
is given by

\textit{tr}(Z)={p_{0}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\textit{tr}(S\,s)=\bigcirc((\bigcirc\textit{tr}(s)\land(p_{1}\lor p_{1}^{\prime}))\lor p_{0}).

The comparison operator can be translated as follows:

\textit{tr}(s\leq t)=[\bigcirc((\bigcirc\textit{tr}(s)\land p_{1})\lor(\textit{tr}(t)\land p_{0}^{\prime}))]_{\geq\frac{1}{2}}.

The translation of other connectives is straightforward:

\displaystyle\textit{tr}(\varphi_{1}\land\varphi_{2})=\textit{tr}(\varphi_{1})\land\textit{tr}(\varphi_{2})\qquad\textit{tr}(\varphi_{1}\lor\varphi_{2})=\textit{tr}(\varphi_{1})\lor\textit{tr}(\varphi_{2})\qquad\textit{tr}(\lambda X.\varphi)=\lambda X.\textit{tr}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\textit{tr}(X)=X\qquad\textit{tr}(\varphi_{1}\,\varphi_{2})=\textit{tr}(\varphi_{1})\,\textit{tr}(\varphi_{2})\qquad\textit{tr}(\mu X.\varphi)=\mu X.\textit{tr}(\varphi)\qquad\textit{tr}(\nu X.\varphi)=\nu X.\textit{tr}(\varphi).

The following lemma states that the translation preserves types.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:A
, then
\textit{tr}(\Gamma)\vdash\textit{tr}(\varphi):\textit{tr}(A)
.

Proof 4.6
.

This follows by straightforward induction on the derivation of
\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:A
.

We prove the correctness of the translation.
For each type
A
of the higher-order fixpoint arithmetic, we define a relation

({\sim_{A}})\subseteq\llbracket A\rrbracket_{\mu}\times\llbracket\textit{tr}(A)\rrbracket_{M}
by induction on
A
as follows:

\displaystyle n\sim_{N}(r_{0},r_{0}^{\prime},r_{1},r_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\text{def}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\quad r_{0}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\mbox{ and }r_{0}^{\prime}=1-\frac{1}{2^{n}}

\displaystyle b\sim_{\Omega}(r_{0},r_{0}^{\prime},r_{1},r_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\text{def}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\quad b=r_{0}

\displaystyle f\sim_{A\to T}g

\displaystyle\quad\overset{\text{def}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\quad\forall x\in\llbracket A\rrbracket_{\mu}.\forall y\in\llbracket\textit{tr}(A)\rrbracket_{M}.\;x\sim_{A}y\implies f\,x\sim_{T}g\,y.

This relation can be naturally extended to the interpretations of type environments: given a type environment
\Gamma
of the
\mu
-arithmetic,
the relation
({\sim_{\Gamma}})\subseteq\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket_{\mu}\times\llbracket\textit{tr}(\Gamma)\rrbracket_{M}
is defined by

\theta\sim_{\Gamma}\rho\quad\overset{\text{def}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\quad\forall X\in\mathit{dom}(\Gamma).\;\theta(X)\sim_{\Gamma(X)}\rho(X).

The following theorem states the correspondence between
the source and the target of the translation.
A proof is provided in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:A
be a formula of the higher-order fixpoint arithmetic.
Assume
\theta\in\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket_{\mu}
and
\rho\in\llbracket\textit{tr}(\Gamma)\rrbracket
.
If

\theta\sim_{\Gamma}\rho
,
then

\llbracket\Gamma\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:A\rrbracket_{\mu}(\theta)\sim_{A}\llbracket\textit{tr}(\Gamma)\vdash\textit{tr}(\varphi):\textit{tr}(A)\rrbracket_{M}(\rho)
.

Corollary 4.7
.

The validity problem of the order-
k
fixpoint arithmetic
is reducible to the order-
k

PHFL model-checking problem.

Proof 4.8
.

Assume
\emptyset\vdash_{\mu}\varphi:\Omega
.
By Theorem
6
,
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mu}\sim_{\Omega}\llbracket\textit{tr}(\varphi)\rrbracket_{M}
.
Therefore,
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mu}=1
if and only if
\llbracket\textit{tr}(\varphi)\rrbracket_{M}(s_{0})=1
, i.e.
M\models\textit{tr}(\varphi)
.
The mapping
\varphi\mapsto(\textit{tr}(\varphi),M)
is obviously effective, and preserves the order.

Theorem
4
is an immediate consequence of Theorem
5
and Corollary
4.7
.

5.
Decidable Subclass of Order-1 PHFL Model Checking

As we have seen in Section
3
,
PHFL model checking is undecidable, even for order 1.
In this section, we identify a decidable subclass of the
order-1 PHFL model-checking problems
(i.e., a set of pairs
(\phi,M)
such that whether
M{\models}\phi
is decidable).
We identify the subclass by using a type system: we define a type system
\mathcal{T}_{M}

for
PHFL
formulas, parameterized by
M
, so that if
\phi
is a
proposition well-typed in
\mathcal{T}_{M}
, then
M{\models}\phi
is decidable.

This section is structured as follows.
In Section
5.1
, we introduce the type system
\mathcal{T}_{M}
,
and prove that the semantics of any order-1 well-typed formula is an affine function.
Section
5.2
introduces a matrix representation of affine functions
and shows the decidability of
M{\models}\phi
by appealing to the decidability
of the first-order theory of reals
[
Tar51
]
. Section
5.3
shows
that the restricted fragment is reasonably expressive, by giving an encoding of the
termination problem for recursive Markov chains into the restricted fragment of PHFL model checking.

5.1.
Type-based Restriction of Order-1 PHFL

We first explain the idea of the restriction imposed by our type system.
By definition, the semantics of a (closed) order-
1
PHFL formula
\phi

of type
\mathit{Prop}\to\mathit{Prop}
with respect to the Markov chain
M

is a map
f_{\phi}
from the set of functions
S\to[0,1]
to the same set,
where
S
is the set of states of
M
.
Thus, if
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}\}
is fixed,
f_{\phi}

can be regarded as a function from
[0,1]^{n}
to
[0,1]^{n}
.
Now, if the function
f_{\phi}
were affine,
i.e., if
there are functions
f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n}
such that

f_{\phi}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n})=(f_{1}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}),\dots,f_{n}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}))
,
where
f_{i}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n})=c_{i,0}+c_{i,1}r_{1}+\dots+c_{i,n}r_{n}

for some real numbers
c_{i,j}
,
then the function
f_{\phi}
would be representable by a finite number of reals
c_{i,j}
.
The semantics of a fixpoint formula would then be
given as a solution of a fixpoint equation on the coefficients,
which is solvable by appealing to the decidability of first-order theories of reals
[
Tar51
]
.

Based on the observation above, we introduce a type system to restrict the formulas
so that the semantics of every well-typed order-
1
formula is affine.
The conjunction
\phi_{1}\land\phi_{2}
is one of the problematic logical connectives
that may make the semantics of an order-
1
formula non-affine:
recall that the
\min
operator was used to define the semantics of conjunction.
We require that for every subformula of the form
\phi_{1}\land\phi_{2}
and for each state
s\in S
,
one of the values
\llbracket\phi_{1}\rrbracket(s)
and
\llbracket\phi_{2}\rrbracket(s)
is the constant
0
or
1
.
We can then remove the
\min
operator,
since we have
\min(0,x)=0
and
\min(1,x)=x

for every
x\in[0,1]
.

We parameterize the type system by the Markov chain
M
,
since it often depends on
M

whether the semantics of an order-
1
formula is affine. For example,
the semantics of
(p\land\phi_{1})\lor(q\land\phi_{2})
is affine
if the semantics of
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are affine
and if

p
and
q
cannot be simultaneously true (i.e., if
\rho_{\mathit{AP},M}(p)\cap\rho_{\mathit{AP},M}(q)=\emptyset
). Without the parameterization, the resulting type system would
be too conservative.

The discussion above motivates us to refine the type
\mathit{Prop}
of propositions
to
\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
where
T,U\subseteq S
and
T\cap U=\emptyset
.
Intuitively, the type
\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
describes propositions
\phi\in\mathit{Prop}

such that
\llbracket\phi\rrbracket(s)=0
for all
s\in T
and

\llbracket\phi\rrbracket(s)=1
for all
s\in U
;
there is no guarantee on the value of
\llbracket\phi\rrbracket(s)

for
s\in S\setminus(T\cup U)
.
The syntax of
refined types
is given by:

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle::=\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}\,|\,\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}\to\kappa

where
T
and
U
range over the set of subsets of
S

satisfying
T\cap U=\emptyset
.
Note that each type
\kappa
can be expressed as

\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{2},U_{2}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}

where
k\geq 0
.

We define the translation from the set of refined types to the set of types in PHFL by

\displaystyle\textit{tr}(\mathit{Prop}^{T,U})

\displaystyle=\mathit{Prop}

\displaystyle\textit{tr}(\kappa_{1}\to\kappa_{2})

\displaystyle=\textit{tr}(\kappa_{1})\to\textit{tr}(\kappa_{2})

and the translation of type environment
\mathcal{K}
by
(\textit{tr}(\mathcal{K}))(x)=\textit{tr}(\mathcal{K}(x))
.
The semantics of refined types is defined as follows.
As explained above, the values of function types are restricted to affine functions.

Definition 5.1
.

For each refined type
\kappa
, we define the subset
\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket\subseteq\llbracket\textit{tr}(\kappa)\rrbracket
as follows.

\begin{array}[]{l}\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}\rrbracket=\{v\in D_{\mathit{Prop}}\mid\forall s\in T.v(s)=0,\forall s\in U.v(s)=1\}\\
\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}\rrbracket=\\
\qquad\{f\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{k}\to\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket\mid\\
\qquad\qquad f\mbox{ is affine on $\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\rrbracket$, and}\\
\qquad\qquad\forall v_{1}\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket,\dots,v_{k}\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\rrbracket.f\,v_{1}\,\cdots\,v_{k}\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}\rrbracket\}\end{array}

In the definition above, by “
f
is affine on
D_{1}\times\dots\times D_{k}
”, we mean that, for each state
s_{\ell}\in S
,
there exist some coefficients
c^{\ell}_{0},c^{\ell}_{1,1},\ldots,c^{\ell}_{k,n}
such that, for every
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in D_{1}\times\dots\times D_{k}
,

f\,v_{1}\,\cdots\,v_{k}\,s_{\ell}=c^{\ell}_{0}+\sum_{i\in\{1,\ldots,k\},j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}c^{\ell}_{i,j}v_{i}(s_{j}).

Remark 5.2
.

Note that
\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
is not closed under various operations.
For example,
the greatest lower bound of affine functions
\lambda(x,y).x
and

\lambda(x,y).y\in[0,1]^{2}\to[0,1]
is
\lambda(x,y).\min(x,y)
, which is not affine.
This means that the conjunction does not preserve affinity, as mentioned above.
A similar observation applies to fixpoints: for a monotone function
h
on
\llbracket\textit{tr}(\kappa)\rrbracket
, even if
h\,x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
for every
x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
, it is not necessarily the case that
\mathit{LFP}(h)\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
.
For example, let
S=\{s\}
,
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}\to\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\{s\}}
,
and
h(f)=\lambda v.\lambda s.\max(f(v)(s),v(s)^{2})
. For any
f\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
and
v\in\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}
,

h(f)(v)(s)=\max(f(v)(s),v(s)^{2})=\max(1,v(s)^{2})=1
, hence
h(f)\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
.
However,
\mathit{LFP}(h)=\lambda v.\lambda s.v(s)^{2}\not\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
.

We restrict PHFL formulas by a type system parameterized by a Markov chain
M
.
We consider a type judgment of the form:

\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\kappa
.
Here,
\mathcal{K}
is a type environment of the form

X_{1}\mathbin{:}\kappa_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\mathbin{:}\kappa_{k}
; it is for fixpoint variables, i.e.,
those bound by
\mu
or
\nu
. The other type environment
\Delta

is of the form
Y_{1}\mathbin{:}\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}},\ldots,Y_{m}\mathbin{:}\mathit{Prop}^{T_{\ell},U_{\ell}}
;
it is for variables bound by
\lambda
. We require that the domains of

\mathcal{K}
and
\Delta
are disjoint.
The intended meaning of the judgment

\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\kappa
,
where
\Delta=Y_{1}\mathbin{:}\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}},\ldots,Y_{\ell}\mathbin{:}\mathit{Prop}^{T_{\ell},U_{\ell}}

and
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{\ell+1},U_{\ell+1}}\to\cdots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{\ell+m},U_{\ell+m}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}

is as follows. Assume: (i) each fixpoint variable
X
is bound
to an affine function as described by
\mathcal{K}(X)
,
(ii) each
Y_{i}\;(1\leq i\leq\ell+m)
is bound to a value
(x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,n})

described by
\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}
. Then
the value of
\phi\,Y_{\ell+1}\,\cdots\,Y_{\ell+m}
is an affine function
on
x_{i,j}
. Note that the value of
\phi\,Y_{\ell+1}\,\cdots\,Y_{\ell+m}

need not be affine on the values of fixpoint variables.
Below
\Delta
is treated as a
sequence
of type bindings, while

\mathcal{K}
is treated as a
set
.

\displaystyle\frac{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}\\
T^{\prime}\subseteq T\quad\quad U^{\prime}\subseteq U\end{array}}{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\mathit{Prop}^{T^{\prime},U^{\prime}}\end{array}}

(
T-WeakTU
)

\displaystyle\frac{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\kappa\end{array}}{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\mathcal{K};X\mathbin{:}\mathit{Prop}^{T,U},\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\kappa\end{array}}

(

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\kappa
be a refined type and
\gamma
be an ordinal number.
For every increasing chain

of elements in

\bigsqcup_{\alpha<\gamma}f_{\alpha}
in
\llbracket\textit{tr}(\kappa)\rrbracket

\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket

\bigsqcap_{\alpha<\gamma}x_{\alpha}
is in
\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
.

Proof 5.9
.

We prove the former.
Assume
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
.

We first give an alternative characterization of affinity.
For each
i\leq k
, given
v_{i}\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}\rrbracket
and
r\in[0,1]
, we define
r\cdot v_{i}
by
(r\cdot v_{i})(s)=r(v_{i}(s))
.
Note that
r\cdot v_{i}
may not be a member of
\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}\rrbracket
, but
r\cdot v_{i}+(1-r)\cdot v_{i}^{\prime}\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}\rrbracket
for every
v_{i},v_{i}^{\prime}\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}\rrbracket
and
r\in[0,1]
(here the sum is the point-wise sum on reals).
Then
f\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{k}\to\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket
is affine on
\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\rrbracket
if and only if, for every
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k}),(v^{\prime}_{1},\dots,v^{\prime}_{k})\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\rrbracket

f(r\cdot v_{1}+(1-r)\cdot v_{1}^{\prime})\dots(r\cdot v_{k}+(1-r)\cdot v_{k}^{\prime})=r(f\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k})+(1-r)(f\,v_{1}^{\prime}\,\dots\,v_{k}^{\prime}).

Let
(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\gamma}
be an increasing chain and
f=\bigsqcup_{\alpha<\gamma}f_{\alpha}
.
Then

can be characterized in terms of the limits in real numbers as

y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket
.
Since
\lim_{\alpha<\gamma}
commutes with linear operations,
for every
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
,

(v^{\prime}_{1},\dots,v^{\prime}_{k})\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle f\,(r\cdot v_{1}+(1-r)\cdot v^{\prime}_{1})\dots(r\cdot v_{k}+(1-r)\cdot v^{\prime}_{k})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha<\gamma}(f_{\alpha}\,(r\cdot v_{1}+(1-r)\cdot v^{\prime}_{1})\dots(r\cdot v_{k}+(1-r)\cdot v^{\prime}_{k})

\displaystyle=\lim_{\alpha<\gamma}\big(r(f_{\alpha}\,v_{1}\dots v_{k})+(1-r)(f_{\alpha}\,v^{\prime}_{1}\,\dots\,v^{\prime}_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=r\big(\lim_{\alpha<\gamma}(f_{\alpha}\,v_{1}\,\dots v_{k})\big)+(1-r)\big(\lim_{\alpha<\gamma}(f_{\alpha}\,v^{\prime}_{1}\,\dots\,v^{\prime}_{k})\big)

\displaystyle=r(f\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k})+(1-r)(f\,v_{1}^{\prime}\,\dots\,v_{k}^{\prime}).

The latter is the dual of the former, and can be proved in the same manner,

f\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}\sqsubseteq_{\mathit{Prop}}g\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}
for every
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\rrbracket
.
For interpretations
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in\llbracket\mathcal{K}\rrbracket
, we write
\rho_{0}\precsim_{\mathcal{K}}\rho_{1}
if
\rho_{0}(X)\precsim_{\mathcal{K}(X)}\rho_{1}(X)
for every
X\in\mathit{dom}(\mathcal{K})
.
We write
f\sim_{\kappa}g
for
f\preceq_{\kappa}g\land g\preceq_{\kappa}f
, and analogously for interpretations. The results in the rest of this subsection
allows us to compute the semantics of a formula of type

\kappa
up to
\sim_{\kappa}
.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
be a refined type and
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
be monotone functions on
\textit{tr}(\kappa)
such that
x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
implies
h_{0}\,x,h_{1}\,x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
and that
x_{0}\precsim_{\kappa}x_{1}
implies
h_{0}\,x_{0}\precsim_{\kappa}h_{1}\,x_{1}
.

•

If
U=\emptyset
, then
\mathit{LFP}(h_{0})\precsim_{\kappa}\mathit{LFP}(h_{1})
.

•

If
T=\emptyset
, then
\mathit{GFP}(h_{0})\precsim_{\kappa}\mathit{GFP}(h_{1})
.

Here
\mathit{LFP}
and
\mathit{GFP}
are taken in
\llbracket\textit{tr}(\kappa)\rrbracket
.

Proof 5.14
.

We prove the former; the proof of the latter is analogous.
As discussed in the proof of Lemma
5.10
,

\mathit{LFP}(h_{0})=h_{0}^{\alpha}(\bot)
and
\mathit{LFP}(h_{1})=h_{1}^{\alpha}(\bot)
for some sufficiently large ordinal
\alpha
.
We prove
h_{0}^{\alpha}(\bot)\precsim_{\kappa}h_{1}^{\alpha}(\bot)
by induction on
\alpha
.
Trivially
h_{0}^{\alpha}(\bot)=\bot\precsim_{\kappa}\bot=h_{1}^{\alpha}(\bot)
.
For
\alpha=\beta+1
, since
h_{0}^{\beta}(\bot)\precsim_{\kappa}h_{1}^{\beta}(\bot)
by the induction hypothesis,
h_{0}(h_{0}^{\beta}(\bot))\precsim_{\kappa}h_{1}(h_{1}^{\beta}(\bot))
follows from the assumption.
If
\alpha
is a limit cardinal, then
h_{i}^{\alpha}=\bigsqcup_{\beta<\alpha}h_{i}^{\beta}(\bot)
.
For every
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\rrbracket
, we have
h_{i}^{\alpha}(\bot)(v_{1})\dots(v_{k})=\bigsqcup_{\beta<\alpha}(h_{i}^{\beta}(\bot)(v_{1})\dots(v_{k}))
by definition.
Since
h_{0}^{\beta}(\bot)(v_{1})\dots(v_{k})\precsim_{\kappa}h_{1}^{\beta}(\bot)(v_{1})\dots(v_{k})
holds for every
\beta<\alpha
by the induction hypothesis, we have
h_{0}^{\alpha}(\bot)(v_{1})\dots(v_{k})\precsim_{\kappa}h_{1}^{\alpha}(\bot)(v_{1})\dots(v_{k})
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
\phi
be a PHFL formula such that
\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\kappa
.
Then, for every
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\llbracket\mathcal{K}\rrbracket
such that
\rho\precsim_{\mathcal{K}}\rho^{\prime}
, we have
\llbracket\lambda\Delta.\phi\rrbracket(\rho)\precsim_{\Delta\to\kappa}\llbracket\lambda\Delta.\phi\rrbracket(\rho^{\prime})
.

Proof 5.16
.

By induction on the structure of derivation
\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\kappa
.
The most cases are easy.
For fixpoints, we appeal to Lemma
5.13
.
The details are given in Appendix
A
.

The above lemma shows that the semantic domain for
\kappa
can be regarded as
\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
modulo
\sim_{\kappa}
.
Furthermore the least and greatest fixpoints can be characterized in terms of the preorder
\precsim_{\kappa}
.

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
\kappa
be a refined type and
h
be a monotone function on
\textit{tr}(\kappa)
such that
x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
implies
h\,x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
and that
x\precsim_{\kappa}x^{\prime}
implies
h\,x\precsim_{\kappa}h\,x^{\prime}
.
If
\mathit{LFP}(h)\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
, then
\mathit{LFP}(h)
is a least element in
\{x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket\mid h\,x\precsim_{\kappa}x\}
with respect to
\precsim_{\kappa}
.
Similarly, if
\mathit{GFP}(h)\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
, then
\mathit{GFP}(h)
is a greatest postfixpoint
of
h
w.r.t
\precsim_{\kappa}
.

Proof 5.18
.

Assume
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{k},U_{k}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
.
Let
\bot_{\kappa}
be the least element of
\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
, which is defined by:
for every
s\in S_{M}
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\bot_{\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}}(s)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $s\in U$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}\\
\bot_{\kappa}\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}=\begin{cases}\bot_{\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}}&\mbox{if $v_{i}\sqsupseteq\bot_{\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}}$ for every $i$}\\
\lambda s.0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{array}

For an ordinal number
\alpha
, we define
x_{\alpha}
by
x_{0}=\bot_{\kappa}
,

x_{\beta+1}=h(x_{\beta})
and
x_{\alpha}=\bigsqcup_{\beta<\alpha}x_{\beta}
if
\alpha
is a limit ordinal. By the monotonicity of
h
and Lemma
5.8
,
the sequence
\{x_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
is well-defined and forms an increasing chain.
Thus, there exists an ordinal
\alpha
such that
x_{\alpha}
is a fixpoint of
h
,
and in particular, an element of

\{x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket\mid h\,x\precsim_{\kappa}x\}
.
It follows by straightforward induction on
\alpha
that
x_{\alpha}
is also a least element
of
\{x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket\mid h\,x\precsim_{\kappa}x\}
.
Now, by the assumption that
\mathit{LFP}(h)\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
,

\mathit{LFP}(h)
is also an element of

\{x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket\mid h\,x\precsim_{\kappa}x\}
,
and since
\mathit{LFP}(h)\sqsubseteq{}x_{\alpha}
,

\mathit{LFP}(h)
is also a least element of
\{x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket\mid h\,x\precsim_{\kappa}x\}
.
The proof for
\mathit{GFP}(h)
is analogous.

If
\mathcal{K};\emptyset\vdash_{M}\mu X.\phi:\kappa
and
\rho\in\llbracket\mathcal{K}\rrbracket
, then
h=\llbracket\lambda X.\phi\rrbracket(\rho)
satisfies the condition of the lemma above
(recall Lemma
5.15
).
Hence
\llbracket\mu X.\phi\rrbracket(\rho)
is
\sim_{\kappa}
-equivalent to every least
fixpoint of
\llbracket\lambda X.\phi\rrbracket(\rho)
with respect to
\precsim_{\kappa}
;
in other words, to compute
\llbracket\mu X.\phi\rrbracket(\rho)
up to

\sim_{\kappa}
, it suffices to compute a least fixpoint of

\llbracket\lambda X.\phi\rrbracket(\rho)
in the quotient set
\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket/\sim_{\kappa}
.
A similar statement holds for
\nu X.\phi
.

5.2.
Decidability

This subsection gives a description of the interpretation of a PHFL formula using the first-order theory of reals,
and obtains the decidability of the restricted fragment of PHFL model checking.
To this end, we need to represent an element in
\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
as a tuple of reals.
Each element of
\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}\rrbracket
can be naturally written as an
n
-tuple of
[0,1]
where
n
is the number of states in
M
.
What remains is a representation of functions
\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}\rrbracket
.

The key result is Lemma
5.15
, which allows us to identify
x,y\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
if
x\sim_{\kappa}y
.
Let
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
.)
Since
x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
is affine on
\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\rrbracket
, the
\sim_{\kappa}
-equivalence class of
x
contains an affine function on
[0,1]^{m}
, i.e.,

x\sim_{\kappa}f
holds for some

f\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}\,s=c_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in S}c_{s,i,s^{\prime}}v_{i}(s^{\prime}).

We can use the tuple of coefficients
(c_{s},(c_{s,i,s^{\prime}})_{i\leq k,s^{\prime}\in S})_{s\in S}
as a representation of
x
.

Henceforth, we assume the set
S_{M}
of states of
M
is
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
We define the
affine semantics

\mathop{(\!|}\mathit{Prop}^{m}\to\mathit{Prop}\mathop{|\!)}
of type
\mathit{Prop}^{m}\to\mathit{Prop}
by

\displaystyle\mathop{(\!|}\mathit{Prop}^{m}\to\mathit{Prop}\mathop{|\!)}

\displaystyle=\{(c_{i,j,k})_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},j\in\{0,\ldots,m\},k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}}\in[0,1]^{n(m+1)(n+1)}\mid

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{i,j,k}\leq 1\mbox{ for every $i=1,\dots,n$.}\}

The tuple
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}

represents
the function
f\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{m}\to\mathit{Prop}\rrbracket
such that, for each
i\in S_{M}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

f\,g_{1}\,\cdots\,g_{m}\,i=c_{i,0,0}+\Sigma_{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\},k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}c_{i,j,k}\cdot g_{j}(k).

We write
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}^{\dagger}
for the function
f
above.
The
affine semantics

\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}
of refined type
\kappa
is defined by

\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}=\{(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}\in\mathop{(\!|}\textit{tr}(\kappa)\mathop{|\!)}\mid(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}^{\dagger}\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket\}.

Lemma 5.19
.

For every
x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
, there exists
\mathbf{c}=(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}
such that
x\sim_{\kappa}\mathbf{c}^{\dagger}
.

Proof 5.20
.

Let
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
.
Since
x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
, it is affine on
\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\rrbracket
, i.e. for every
(v_{1},\dots,v_{m})\in\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\rrbracket\times\dots\times\llbracket\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\rrbracket
and
i\in S_{M}
,

x\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{m}\,i=c_{i,0,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{i,j,k}v_{j}(k).

We can assume without loss of generality that
c_{i,j,k}=0
if
k\in T_{j}
since
v_{j}(k)=0
in this case.
Let
c_{i,0,k}=c_{i,j,0}=0
for
j,k\geq 0
.
Then
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}\in\mathop{(\!|}\mathit{Prop}^{m}\to\mathit{Prop}\mathop{|\!)}
and
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}^{\dagger}\sim_{\kappa}x
.
Therefore
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}
since the latter implies
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}^{\dagger}\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
.

Remark 5.21
.

For
x\in\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket
,
\mathbf{c}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}
such that
x\sim_{\kappa}\mathbf{c}^{\dagger}
is not necessarily unique.
The representation becomes unique if one imposes the following conditions.
Assume that
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
.

•

c_{i,j,0}=c_{i,0,k}=0
for
j,k>0
. Note that these coefficients are not used in
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}^{\dagger}
.

•

c_{i,j,k}=0
if
k\in T_{j}
. If
k\in T_{j}
, then
v_{j}(k)=0
and thus
c_{i,j,k}
does not affect the value of
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}^{\dagger}
.
So we can assume without loss of generality that
c_{i,j,k}=0
.

•

c_{i,j,k}=0
if
k\in U_{j}
. If
k\in U_{j}
, then
v_{j}(k)=1
.
So, by adding
c_{i,j,k}
to the constant part
c_{i,0,0}
if necessarily, we obtain another representation that belongs to the same
\sim_{\kappa}
-equivalence class and that
c_{i,j,k}=0
.

A representation
\mathbf{c}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}
is
canonical
if it satisfies the above conditions.
It is not difficult to see that each
\sim_{\kappa}
-equivalence class has exactly one canonical representation.

The second and third conditions are convenient for computing the affine semantics of
\phi_{1}\lor\phi_{2}
and

\phi_{1}\land\phi_{2}
. Suppose
\emptyset;\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi_{i}:\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
and

T_{1}\cup U_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup U_{2}=S_{M}
.
If
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}
and
(c^{\prime}_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}
are canonical representations for the (affine) semantics
of
\lambda\Delta.\phi_{1}
and
\lambda\Delta.\phi_{2}
respectively, then
the semantics
(c^{\prime\prime}_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k})
of
\lambda\Delta.\phi_{1}\land\phi_{2}

is also obtained pointwise by:

c^{\prime\prime}_{i,j,k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\min(c_{i,0,0},c^{\prime}_{i,0,0})&\mbox{if $j=k=0$}\\
c^{\prime}_{i,j,k}&\mbox{if $i\in U_{1}$}\\
c_{i,j,k}&\mbox{if $i\in U_{2}\setminus U_{1}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

As stated before,
we will describe the affine semantics of a well-typed PHFL formula using the first-order theory of reals.
Before doing so, however, we show an example of directly computing the affine semantics.

.

Let

be the Markov chain

(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}

(c_{i,j,k})_{j,k}

In the example above, we have directly computed the affine semantics.
In general, however, we describe the affine semantics
(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}
by using logical formulas,
to deal with arbitrary alternations of fixpoint operators.
All the operations and properties on
\llbracket\kappa\rrbracket

required for computing the affine semantics
are definable by using the first-order theory of reals.
Assume
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
and
let
\mathbf{c}=(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}
.
For example, the value
\mathbf{c}^{\dagger}\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{m}\,i
can be represented as a term

c_{i,0,0}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{i,j,k}v_{j}(k).

Then, for
\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}
, the relation
\mathbf{c}\precsim_{\kappa}\mathbf{d}
is written as

\displaystyle\forall v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in[0,1]^{n}.

\displaystyle\Big(\bigwedge_{j=1,\dots,m}v_{j}\in\mathop{(\!|}\mathit{Prop}^{T_{j},U_{j}}\mathop{|\!)}\Big)\Rightarrow\bigwedge_{i=1,\dots,n}\Big[\big(\mathbf{c}^{\dagger}\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}\,i\big)\leq\big(\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}\,i\big)\Big]

and
\mathbf{c}^{\dagger}\sim_{\kappa}\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}
as

\mathbf{c}^{\dagger}\precsim_{\kappa}\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}~~\wedge~~\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}\precsim_{\kappa}\mathbf{c}^{\dagger}.

The meets and joins are also describable: for example,
\mathbf{c}_{1}^{\dagger}\sqcap\mathbf{c}_{2}^{\dagger}\sim_{\kappa}\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\forall v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\in[0,1]^{n}.\Big(\bigwedge_{j=1,\dots,m}v_{j}\in\mathop{(\!|}\mathit{Prop}^{T_{j},U_{j}}\mathop{|\!)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\Rightarrow\bigwedge_{i=1,\dots,n}\Big[\big(\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}\,i\big)=\max\Big((\mathbf{c}_{1}^{\dagger}\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}\,i),~(\mathbf{c}_{2}^{\dagger}\,v_{1}\,\dots\,v_{k}\,i)\Big)\Big];

note that
\max
as well as
\min
is an operation definable in the first-order theory of reals.

Lemma 5.23
.

Suppose
\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\kappa
and
\mathcal{K}=\{X_{1}:\kappa_{1},\dots,X_{N}:\kappa_{N}\}
.
Then one can effectively construct a formula
\Phi
of the first-order real arithmetic such that, for every
\mathbf{c}_{\ell}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa_{\ell}\mathop{|\!)}
(
\ell=1,\dots,N
) and
\mathbf{d}\in\mathop{(\!|}\Delta\to\kappa\mathop{|\!)}
,

\Phi(\mathbf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{N},\mathbf{d})~~\Leftrightarrow~~\llbracket\lambda\Delta.\phi\rrbracket(\{X_{\ell}\mapsto\mathbf{c}_{\ell}^{\dagger}\mid 1\leq\ell\leq N\})\sim_{\Delta\to\kappa}\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}.

Proof 5.24
.

By induction on the structure of derivation
\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi:\kappa
.
The most cases are easy.
For example, consider the case that the last rule is (
T-Conj
).
Then
\phi=\phi_{1}\wedge\phi_{2}
and
\mathcal{K};\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi_{i}:\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}
for
i=1,2
.
By the induction hypothesis, we have predicates
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}
representing
\llbracket\lambda\Delta.\phi_{1}\rrbracket
and
\llbracket\lambda\Delta.\phi_{2}\rrbracket
.
Then
\Phi(\mathbf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{N},\mathbf{d})
is defined as

\displaystyle\exists\mathbf{d}_{1}\in\mathop{(\!|}\Delta\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\mathop{|\!)}.~\exists\mathbf{d}_{2}\in\mathop{(\!|}\Delta\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{2},U_{2}}\mathop{|\!)}.

\displaystyle\quad\Phi_{1}(\mathbf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{N},\mathbf{d}_{1})~~\wedge~~\Phi_{2}(\mathbf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{N},\mathbf{d}_{2})~~\wedge~~\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}\sim_{\kappa}\mathbf{d}_{1}^{\dagger}\sqcap\mathbf{d}_{2}^{\dagger}.

Since
\llbracket\lambda\Delta.\phi_{i}\rrbracket(\{X_{\ell}\mapsto\mathbf{c}_{\ell}^{\dagger}\mid 1\leq\ell\leq N\})\in\llbracket\Delta\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}\rrbracket
by Theorem
7
, there exists a
\mathbf{d}_{i}\in\mathop{(\!|}\Delta\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{i},U_{i}}\mathop{|\!)}
such that
\Phi_{i}(\mathbf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{N},\mathbf{d}_{i})
.

The only non-trivial cases are fixpoints.
Consider the case that the last rule is (
T-Mu
).
Then
\phi=\nu X.\phi_{0}
and
\mathcal{K},X:\kappa;\Delta\vdash_{M}\phi_{0}:\kappa
.
By the induction hypothesis, we have
\Phi_{0}
representing the semantics of
\phi_{0}
.
Then
\Phi(\mathbf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{N},\mathbf{d})
is defined as

\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{N},\mathbf{d},\mathbf{d})~~\wedge~~\forall\mathbf{d}^{\prime}\in\mathop{(\!|}\kappa\mathop{|\!)}.\Phi_{0}(\mathbf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{c}_{N},\mathbf{d}^{\prime},\mathbf{d}^{\prime})\Rightarrow\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}\precsim_{\kappa}\mathbf{d}^{\prime\dagger}.

The first condition says that
\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}
is a fixpoint of
\llbracket\lambda X.\phi_{0}\rrbracket
modulo
\sim_{\kappa}
.
The second condition says that
\mathbf{d}^{\dagger}
is a least element in the set of fixpoints
\mathbf{d}^{\prime\dagger}
of
\llbracket\lambda X.\phi_{0}\rrbracket
modulo
\sim_{\kappa}
.
Correctness of the above formula follows from Corollary
5.17
.

Theorem 5.24
.

Given a closed formula
\phi
and a Markov chain
M
such that

\emptyset;\emptyset\vdash_{M}\phi:\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
,
it is decidable whether
M\models\phi
.

Proof 5.25
.

Assume that
s_{\text{in},M}=1
.
By Lemma
5.23
, one can effectively construct a formula
\Phi
of the first-order real arithmetic such that
\Phi(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
is valid if and only if
\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{M}(\emptyset)(i)=r_{i}
for every
i=1,\dots,n
.
Hence
M\models\phi
if and only if
\exists r_{2},\dots,r_{n}.\Phi(1,r_{2},\dots,r_{n})
.
The validity of this formula is decidable
[
Tar51
]
.

Remark 5.26
.

The formula obtained in the proof above
contains both universal and existential quantifiers in general;
hence the complexity is doubly exponential time in general
[
DH88
]
.
However, if
\phi
is of the form
[\phi^{\prime}]_{\geq r}
where

\phi^{\prime}
contains no occurrences of
\nu
and
[\cdot]_{J}
,
then we can express
\Phi

using only existential quantifiers, and appeal
to a decision algorithm for the existential theory of the reals, whose complexity
is PSPACE
[
Can88
]
.
In fact, to deal with
\mu
-formulas in the restricted fragment,
it suffices to consider only inequalities of the form

\mathit{LFP}(h)\preceq_{\kappa}x
, which can be represented by
an existential formula
\exists x^{\prime}.(x^{\prime}\precsim_{\kappa}x\land h\,x^{\prime}\precsim_{\kappa}x^{\prime})
.
Note that
\precsim
is definable by a quantifier-free formula, as stated in the lemma below.
Note also that the semantics of conjunctions and disjunctions can be expressed without using
quantifiers, as discussed in Remark
5.21
.

Lemma 5.27
.

Let
\kappa=\mathit{Prop}^{T_{1},U_{1}}\to\dots\to\mathit{Prop}^{T_{m},U_{m}}\to\mathit{Prop}^{T,U}
.
For

, we write

•

if

•

.
Then,

if and only if:

\bigwedge_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}\bigwedge_{J\in\mathcal{J}(\kappa)}\left(c_{i,0,0}+\sum_{(j,k)\in J}c_{i,j,k}\leq c^{\prime}_{i,0,0}+\sum_{(j,k)\in J}c^{\prime}_{i,j,k}\right).

Proof 5.28
.

If:

Suppose

.
We regard
(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})=(g_{j}(k))_{j\in\{1,\dots,m\},k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}
as an

We also identify

where

if

and
h_{j}(k)=0

Let

Hence

\displaystyle(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}^{\dagger}\,g_{1}\,\dots\,g_{m}\,i

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}r_{\ell}(c_{i,0,0}+\sum_{(j,k)\in J_{\ell}}c_{i,j,k})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell}r_{\ell}(c^{\prime}_{i,0,0}+\sum_{(j,k)\in J_{\ell}}c^{\prime}_{i,j,k})

\displaystyle=(c_{i,j,k})_{i,j,k}^{\dagger}\,g_{1}\,\dots\,g_{m}\,i.

Only if:

For each

5.3.
Expressivity

We have already seen in Example
5.3
and Lemma
5.6

that the decidable fragment of the order-1 PHFL model checking problem
strictly subsumes
\mu^{p}{}
-calculus model checking.
To provide a further evidence of the expressivity of the decidable fragment,
in this subsection, we show that the termination problem for
recursive Markov chains (or,
probabilistic pushdown systems)
[
EY09
,
BEKK13
]
can be encoded into the decidable fragment of
order-1 PHFL model checking.
Since recursive Markov chains are known to be equivalent to order-1
probabilistic higher-order recursion schemes (PHORS)
[
KLG20
]
,
we encode below the termination problem
for the latter into the PHFL model checking problem.

We first recall the definition of PHORS, specialized for order 1.

Definition 5.29
.

An order-1 PHORS
\mathcal{G}
is a triple
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{R},t)
,
where:

•

\mathcal{X}
is a finite map from (order-1) variables to their arities;

•

\mathcal{R}
is a finite set of rules of the form:

X\,y_{1}\,\cdots,y_{\mathcal{X}(X)}\to t_{L}\oplus_{p}t_{R},

where
t_{L},t_{R}
range over the set of
(\mathcal{X},\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{\mathcal{X}(X)}\})
-terms.
Here, the set of
(\mathcal{X},V)
-terms, ranged over by
t
, is
defined by the grammar:

t::=\mathtt{e}\mid y\mid X\,t_{1}\,\cdots,t_{\mathcal{X}(X)},

where
y
and
X
range over
V
and
\mathit{dom}(\mathcal{X})
respectively,
and
\mathtt{e}
is a special symbol denoting termination.

•

t
is a
(\mathcal{X},\emptyset)
-term.

For
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}},t_{\mathcal{G}})
,
the reduction relation
t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi,p}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}t^{\prime}
on terms (where

\pi\in\{L,R\}^{*}
and
p\in[0,1]
) is defined by:

\displaystyle\frac{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\end{array}}{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon,1}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}t\end{array}}

\displaystyle\frac{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi,q}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}X\,t_{1}\,\cdots\,t_{k}\quad\quad X\,y_{1}\,\cdots\,y_{k}\to t_{L}\oplus_{p}t_{R}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}\end{array}}{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi L,q\cdot p}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}[t_{1}/y_{1},\ldots,t_{k}/y_{k}]t_{L}\end{array}}

\displaystyle\frac{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi,q}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}X\,t_{1}\,\cdots\,t_{k}\quad\quad X\,y_{1}\,\cdots\,y_{k}\to t_{L}\oplus_{p}t_{R}\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}}\end{array}}{\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi R,q\cdot(1-p)}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}[t_{1}/y_{1},\ldots,t_{k}/y_{k}]t_{R}\end{array}}

Note that the reduction is deterministic:
for
\pi\in\{L,R\}^{*}
, there exists at most one
(p,t^{\prime})
such that

t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi,p}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}t^{\prime}
.
The
termination probability
of
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{G}},t_{\mathcal{G}})
,
written
\mathit{ProbT}(\mathcal{G})
, is defined by:

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathit{ProbT}(\mathcal{G},t,\pi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p&\mbox{if $t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi,p}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathtt{e}$}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\\
\mathit{ProbT}(\mathcal{G})=\sum_{\pi\in\{L,R\}^{*}}\mathit{ProbT}(\mathcal{G},t_{\mathcal{G}},\pi).\end{array}

The following example has been taken from
[
KLG20
]
.

Example 5.30
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the order-1 PHORS
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{R},X_{0})
,
where:

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{X}=\{X_{0}\mapsto 0,F\mapsto 1\}\\
\mathcal{R}=\{X_{0}\ =\ X_{1}\,\mathtt{e}\oplus_{1}X_{0},\quad X_{1}\,y\ =\ y\oplus_{p}X_{1}(X_{1}\,y)\}.\end{array}

Then, we have
X_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L,1}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}X_{1}\,\mathtt{e}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L,p}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathtt{e}
and

X_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L,1}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}X_{1}\,\mathtt{e}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R,1-p}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}X_{1}(X_{1}\,\mathtt{e})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L,p}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}X_{1}\,\mathtt{e}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L,p}}{{\longrightarrow}}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathtt{e}
.
The termination probability

\mathit{ProbT}(\mathcal{G})
is
1
if
p\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\frac{p}{1-p}
if
p<\frac{1}{2}
. ∎

We now encode an order-1 PHORS

\mathcal{G}
into a Markov chain
M_{\mathcal{G}}
and a closed order-1 PHFL formula
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}

such that
\emptyset;\emptyset\vdash_{M}\phi_{\mathcal{G}}:\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}

and
\mathit{ProbT}(\mathcal{G})
coincides with the probability
that
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
holds at the initial state of
M_{\mathcal{G}}
(i.e.,

\mathit{ProbT}(\mathcal{G}=\llbracket\phi_{\mathcal{G}}\rrbracket(\emptyset)(s_{\text{in}})
).
We first give a construction of
M
. Let
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}

be the set
\{p,1-p\mid\oplus_{p}\mbox{ occurs in }\mathcal{G}\}
. We assume
p_{1}<p_{2}<\cdots<p_{m}
.
We define the Markov chain
M_{\mathcal{G}}=(S,P,\rho_{\mathit{AP}},s_{\text{in}})
as follows.

•

S=\{s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{m+1}\}
,

•

P
satisfies
P(s_{0},s_{1})=p_{1}
,
P(s_{0},s_{i})=p_{i}-p_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq m
,

P(s_{0},s_{m+1})=1-p_{m}
,
P(s_{i},s_{0})=1
for
1\leq i\leq m+1
and
P(s_{i},s_{j})=0
otherwise,

•

\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(P_{i})=\{s_{i}\}
for each
0\leq i\leq m+1
, and

•

s_{\text{in}}=s_{0}
.

Note that
\llbracket\mathbin{\bigcirc}(P_{1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{i})\rrbracket(\emptyset)(s_{0})=p_{i}

and
\llbracket\mathbin{\bigcirc}(P_{i+1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{m+1})\rrbracket(\emptyset)(s_{0})=1-p_{i}
.
Now for each term of
t
of PHORS
\mathcal{G}
, we construct
a formula
\left<t\right>
, so that the termination probability
of
t
coincides with the probability that
\phi_{t}
holds at
s_{0}
.
The translation is given by:

\begin{array}[]{l}\left<\mathtt{e}\right>=\text{true}\qquad\left<y\right>=y\qquad\left<X_{i}\,t_{1}\,\cdots\,t_{k}\right>=X_{i}\,\left<t_{1}\right>\,\cdots\,\left<t_{k}\right>.\end{array}

For
each rule

X_{i}\,y_{i}\,\cdots\,y_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}=t_{L}\oplus_{p_{i}}t_{R}
of
\mathcal{R}
,
we construct the following equality on PHFL formulas:

X_{i}=\lambda y_{1},\ldots,y_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}.\mathbin{\bigcirc}(((P_{1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{i})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{L}\right>)\lor((P_{i+1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{m+1})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{R}\right>)).

We thus obtain a system of mutually recursive equations

\{X_{1}=\phi_{1},\cdots,X_{k}=\phi_{k}\}
, whose least solution
\theta
(which
maps each
X_{i}
to a formula
\psi_{i}
that satisfies
\psi_{i}=\theta\phi_{i}
)
can be represented by using the least fixpoint operators in an obvious manner.
We then let
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
be
\theta\left<t_{\mathcal{G}}\right>
.

Example 5.31
.

Recall
\mathcal{G}
in Example
5.30
. Assuming
p<\frac{1}{2}
,

M_{\mathcal{G}}
is:

•

S=\{s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4}\}
.

•

P(s_{0},s_{1})=p
,
P(s_{0},s_{2})=1-2p
,
P(s_{0},s_{3})=p
,
P(s_{0},s_{4})=0
,

P(s_{i},s_{0})=1
for
i\in\{1,2,3,4\}
and
P(s_{i},s_{j})=0
for
i,j>0
.

•

\rho_{\mathit{AP}}(P_{i})=\{s_{i}\}
for each
i\in\{0,\dots,4\}
, and

•

s_{\text{in}}=s_{0}
.

The rules of
\mathcal{G}
are translated to the following equations:

\begin{array}[]{l}X_{0}=\mathbin{\bigcirc}(((P_{1}\lor P_{2}\lor P_{3})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}(X_{1}\,\mathtt{e}))\lor(P_{4}\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}X_{0}))\\
X_{1}=\lambda y.\mathbin{\bigcirc}((P_{1}\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}y)\lor((P_{2}\lor P_{3}\lor P_{4})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}(X_{1}(X_{1}\,y))))\end{array}

Thus,
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
is given as:

\mu X_{0}.\mathbin{\bigcirc}(((P_{1}\lor P_{2}\lor P_{3})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}(\psi_{1}\,\mathtt{e}))\lor(P_{4}\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}X_{0})),

where
\psi_{1}
is:

\mu X_{1}.\lambda y.\mathbin{\bigcirc}((P_{1}\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}y)\lor((P_{2}\lor P_{3}\lor P_{4})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}(X_{1}(X_{1}\,y)))).

Actually,
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
can be simplified to
\psi_{1}\,\mathtt{e}
in this case.
∎

To see the correctness of the above encoding,
recall that

\llbracket\mathbin{\bigcirc}(P_{1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{i})\rrbracket(\emptyset)(s_{0})=p_{i}

and
\llbracket\mathbin{\bigcirc}(P_{i+1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{m+1})\rrbracket(\emptyset)(s_{0})=1-p_{i}
.
Thus, by the equality on
X_{i}
above,
the probability that
\theta(X_{i}\,\left<t_{1}\right>\,\cdots,\left<t_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}\right>)
holds
at
s_{0}
is equivalent to

p_{i}\cdot q_{L}+(1-p_{i})\cdot q_{R}
, where
q_{L}
and
q_{R}

are respectively the probabilities that

\theta(\left<[t_{1}/y_{1},\ldots,t_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}/y_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}]t_{L}\right>)

and

\theta(\left<[t_{1}/y_{1},\ldots,t_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}/y_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}]t_{R}\right>)

hold at
s_{0}
.
Thus,
the formula
\theta(X_{i}\,\left<t_{1}\right>\,\cdots,\left<t_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}\right>)

mimics the termination probability of
X_{i}\,\left<t_{1}\right>\,\cdots,\left<t_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}\right>
,
which is equivalent to
p_{i}\cdot q^{\prime}_{L}+(1-p_{i})\cdot q^{\prime}_{R}
, where
q^{\prime}_{L}
and
q^{\prime}_{R}

are respectively the termination probabilities of

[t_{1}/y_{1},\ldots,t_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}/y_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}]t_{L}

and

[t_{1}/y_{1},\ldots,t_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}/y_{\mathcal{X}(X_{i})}]t_{R}
.
We omit a formal proof of correctness of the encoding.

We now check that
\phi_{\mathcal{G}}
belongs to the restricted fragment.
To this end, it suffices to check that, for each rule

X\,y_{1}\,\cdots\,y_{k}\to t_{L}\oplus_{p}t_{R}\in\mathcal{R}
, the type judgment:

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{X}};\Delta\vdash_{M_{\mathcal{G}}}\mathbin{\bigcirc}(((P_{1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{i})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{L}\right>)\lor((P_{i+1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{m+1})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{R}\right>)):\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}

holds for
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{X}}=\{X\mathbin{:}(\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset})^{\mathcal{X}(X)}\to\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}\mid X\in\mathit{dom}(\mathcal{X})\}

and
\Delta=y_{1}\mathbin{:}\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset},\ldots,y_{k}\mathbin{:}\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}
.

By a straightforward induction on the structure of
t_{L}
, we have:

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{X}};\Delta\vdash_{M_{\mathcal{G}}}\left<t_{L}\right>:\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}
,
which implies

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{X}};\Delta\vdash_{M_{\mathcal{G}}}\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{L}\right>:\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}
.
We also have:

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{X}};\Delta\vdash_{M_{\mathcal{G}}}P_{1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{i}:\mathit{Prop}^{\{P_{0},P_{i+1},\ldots,P_{m+1}\},\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{i}\}}.

Thus, by using
T-And
, we have:

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{X}};\Delta\vdash_{M_{\mathcal{G}}}(P_{1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{i})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{L}\right>:\mathit{Prop}^{\{P_{0},P_{i+1},\ldots,P_{m+1}\},\emptyset}.

Similarly, we have:

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{X}};\Delta\vdash_{M_{\mathcal{G}}}(P_{i+1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{m+1})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{R}\right>:\mathit{Prop}^{\{P_{0},P_{1},\ldots,P_{i}\},\emptyset}.

Thus, by using
T-Or
and
T-Avg
, we obtain

\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{X}};\Delta\vdash_{M_{\mathcal{G}}}\mathbin{\bigcirc}(((P_{1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{i})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{L}\right>)\lor((P_{i+1}\lor\cdots\lor P_{m+1})\land\mathbin{\bigcirc}\left<t_{R}\right>)):\mathit{Prop}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}

as required.

6.
Related Work

As mentioned in Section
1
, PHFL can be regarded as a probabilistic extension of the higher-order fixpoint logic,
and as a higher-order extension of the
\mu^{p}
-calculus.
We thus compare our work with previous studies on
(non-probabilistic) higher-order fixpoint logic
and those on (non-higher-order) probabilistic logics.
As already mentioned, for (non-probabilistic) HFL, model checking of
finite-state systems is known to be
decidable
[
VV04
]
, and
k
-EXPTIME
complete
[
ALS07
]
. This is in a sharp
contrast with our result that PHFL model checking is highly
undecidable (both
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-hard and
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-hard) even at
order 1.

As for studies on probabilistic logics,
besides the
\mu^{p}
-calculus, there are other probabilistic extensions of
the modal
\mu
-calculus
[
MM97
,
HK97
,
MS13
]
.
Łukasiewicz
\mu
-calculus introduced by Mio and Simpson
[
MS13
]

is among the most expressive ones, which has, in addition to

\land
and
\lor
,
another kind of conjunction (
\odot
) and disjunction (
\oplus
),
called Łukasiewicz operations.
To our knowledge, ours is
the first
higher-order
and probabilistic extension of
the modal
\mu
-calculus.
The decidable fragment of PHFL studied in
Section
5
is strictly more expressive than the
\mu^{p}
-calculus.
We conjecture that the expressive power of PHFL is
incomparable to that of Łukasiewicz
\mu
-calculus.
On the one hand, the property defined by the PHFL formula in the proof of
Theorem
1
cannot be expressed in
Łukasiewicz
\mu
-calculus, hence PHFL is not subsumed by
Łukasiewicz
\mu
-calculus. On the other hand,
Łukasiewicz operations do not seem expressible in PHFL.
It would be interesting to study a higher-order extension of
Łukasiewicz
\mu
-calculus (in other words, an extension of PHFL with
Łukasiewicz operations).

Recently, Kobayashi et al.
[
KLG19
]
introduced
PHORS, a probabilistic extension of higher-order recursion schemes (HORS),
which can also be viewed as a higher-order extension of recursive Markov chains
(or probabilistic pushdown systems), and proved that the almost sure termination
problem is undecidable. Although the problem setting is quite different (in our
work, the
logic
is higher-order whereas the
system
to be verified is higher-order
in their work), our encoding of the
\mu
-arithmetic in Section
4

has been partially inspired by
their undecidability proof; they also
represented a natural number
n
as the probability
\frac{1}{2^{n}}
.

In Section
3
, we have used the undecidability
of the value-1 problem for probabilistic automata to prove the undecidability
of PHFL model checking.
Fijalkow et al.
[
FGKO15
]

studied a decidable subclass of probabilistic automata called leaktight automata.
The idea of their restriction appears to be quite different from our type-based
restriction of the PHFL model checking problem.

7.
Conclusion

We have introduced PHFL, a probabilistic logic which can be regarded as both a probabilistic extension of HFL and
a higher-order extension of the probabilistic logic
\mu^{p}
-calculus.
We have shown that the model-checking problem for PHFL for a finite Markov chain is undecidable
for the
\mu
-only and order-
1
fragment.
We have also shown that the model-checking problem for
the full order-
1
fragment of
PHFL is
\Pi^{1}_{1}
-hard and
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-hard.
As positive results,
we have introduced a decidable subclass of the PHFL model-checking problem,
and showed that the termination problem of Recursive Markov Chains can be encoded
in the subclass.

Finding an upper bound of the hardness of the PHFL model-checking problem is
left for future work. It is also left for future work to find a larger decidable class of
PHFL model-checking problems.

A.1.
Proof of Theorem
6

The following lemma states that the relation
\sim
is preserved by
the least upper bound operation.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
I
be a set and
T
be a type of
\mu
-arithmetic.
Assume families
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
and
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of elements of
\llbracket T\rrbracket_{\mu}
and
\llbracket\textit{tr}(T)\rrbracket
, respectively, and
suppose that
v_{i}\sim_{T}u_{i}
for every
i\in I
.
Then
\bigsqcup_{i\in I}v_{i}\sim_{T}\bigsqcup_{i\in I}u_{i}
.

Proof A.2
.

By induction on the structure of
T
.
The base case
T=\Omega
is obvious.
Assume that
T=A\to T^{\prime}
.

Let
x\in\llbracket A\rrbracket_{\mu}
and
y\in\llbracket\textit{tr}(A)\rrbracket
and assume that
x\sim_{A}y
.
For each
i\in I
, since
v_{i}\sim_{T}u_{i}
, we have
v_{i}\,x\sim_{T^{\prime}}u_{i}\,y
.
By the induction hypothesis,

\bigsqcup_{i\in I}(v_{i}\,x)\sim_{T^{\prime}}\bigsqcup_{i\in I}(u_{i}\,y).

Since the order on functions are component-wise, we have

\left(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}v_{i}\right)\,x=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}(v_{i}\,x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}u_{i}\right)\,y=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}(u_{i}\,y).

So

\left(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}v_{i}\right)\,x\sim_{T^{\prime}}\left(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}u_{i}\right)\,y.

Since
x\sim_{A}y
is arbitrary, we have:

\left(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}v_{i}\right)\sim_{A\to T^{\prime}}\left(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}u_{i}\right).

We are now ready to prove Theorem
6
.

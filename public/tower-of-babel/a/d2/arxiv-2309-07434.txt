Exact and local compression of quantum bipartite states
Kohtaro Kato

Abstract
We study exact local compression of a quantum bipartite state; that is, applying local quantum operations to reduce the dimensions of the Hilbert spaces while perfectly preserving the correlation. We provide a closed-form expression for the minimal achievable dimensions, formulated as a minimization of the Schmidt rank of a particular pure state constructed from the given state. Numerically tractable upper and lower bounds on this rank are also obtained. As an application, we consider the exact compression of quantum channels. This method enables the analysis of a post-processing step that reduces the output dimensions while retaining the information contained in the original channel’s output.



I
Introduction

Quantum data compression is one of the most fundamental quantum information processing. Its concept is analogous to Shannon’s classical data compression and aims to reduce the dimensions of the storage of quantum states while minimizing information loss.
Various approaches to quantum compression have been proposed, primarily focusing on achieving approximately or asymptotically accurate representations of quantum states
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. These protocols have yielded valuable insights into the trade-offs between compression efficiency, fidelity, and additional resources.

Nevertheless, there remains room to investigate
exact
compression of quantum states, where the compressed representation fully retains the information content of the original state.
This compression type has gained interest in information theory and the condition is known as the sufficiency of statistics
[
6
,
7
]
.
Classical statistics are
sufficient
for a given statistical model if they are as informative as the original model.
It is well known that the minimal random variable for describing a statistical model (a family of probability distributions) is given as the minimal sufficient statistics associated with it
[
8
]
.
The concept of sufficiency has been extended to quantum systems
[
9
,
10
]
, where the concept of sufficient statistics is replaced by that of sufficient subalgebras.

In this work, we study the local and exact compressions of bipartite quantum states. In other words, we consider quantum operations in each subsystem (local operations) with a smaller output Hilbert space that can be reverted by another quantum operation. This type of data compression is an exact and noiseless one-shot quantum data compression of general mixed state sources without side information or entanglement assistance. Here, it is crucial to consider mixed state source, since pure state source cannot be compressed less than the Schmidt rank.

An asymptotic scenario of local compressions of mixed state sources has been studied in Refs.
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
for classical-quantum states and extended for fully quantum states in Ref.
[
5
]
. The asymptotically optimal rate in Ref.
[
5
]
is given by the entropy of the state restricted on the subalgebra defined via the Koashi-Imoto decomposition
[
15
,
16
]
. Similarly, one can check that the minimal dimension of (one-shot) exact local compression is also given by a subspace defined via the Koashi-Imoto decomposition. However, the explicit calculation of the Koashi-Imoto decomposition is highly complicated
[
15
,
17
]
, and thus no closed formula for the optimal rate has been obtained so far.

As a result, we show a closed formula to calculate the minimal dimension of the output Hilbert spaces. The formula is obtained by minimizing the Schmidt rank (i.e., the rank of the reduced matrix) over unitarily related pure states, calculated from the input state. As a corollary, we provide additional tractable lower and upper bounds for the minimal dimensions. Our result is based on the recent development of quantum sufficiency
[
18
]
and operator algebra quantum error correction
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
, which formulates the set of operators preserved by a given quantum channel.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section
II
provides relevant background concepts. We provide an informal sketch of our strategy for the proof and summary of the main results in Section
III
. Section
IV
provides a new characterization of the minimal sufficient subalgebra in our setting, which plays an important role in validation. Section
V
discusses the validation of the main results. Section
VI
presents applications of the main results. Finally, Section
VII
concludes the paper by summarizing our findings and outlining potential directions for future research.

II-B

Exact local compression of bipartite states

We are interested in the quantum bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
in the finite-dimensional Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{AB}={\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
.
We assume without loss of generality that
\rho_{A},\rho_{B}>0
(by restricting each Hilbert space to the support of the reduced state).

Definition 1
.

We say a CPTP-map
{\mathcal{E}}_{B\to{\tilde{B}}}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{B})\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{B}})
is an exact local compression of
\rho_{AB}
on
B
, if there exists a CPTP-map
{\mathcal{R}}_{{\tilde{B}}\to B}
satisfying

{\mathcal{R}}_{{\tilde{B}}\to B}\circ{\mathcal{E}}_{B\to{\tilde{B}}}(\rho_{AB})=\rho_{AB}.

(4)

We say that compression
{\mathcal{E}}_{B\to{\tilde{B}}}
is nontrivial if
d_{\tilde{B}}<d_{B}
. We define the exact compression
{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to{\tilde{A}}}
on
A
similarly.

This study aims to calculate the minimal dimensions
d_{{\tilde{A}}},d_{{\tilde{B}}}
of the exact local compressions. It is sufficient to consider only the compressions on
B
(or
A
) because of the symmetry of the problem.

II-C

Koashi-Imoto decomposition

In Ref.
[
15
]
, Koashi and Imoto analyzed the structure of quantum operations that remain a set of classically labeled quantum states
\{\rho^{x}_{B}\}_{x\in\chi}
unchanged. They found that for any set
\{\rho_{B}^{x}\}
, there is a unique decomposition of
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
that completely characterizes CPTP-maps preserving
\{\rho_{B}^{x}\}
. This idea was generalized to a fully quantum setup in Ref.
[
16
]
. It has been shown that for any (finite-dimensional) quantum bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
, there is a unique decomposition called
the Koashi-Imoto decomposition
.

Definition 2
.

Consider a direct sum decomposition

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{B}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{i}{{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{L}}}\otimes{{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{R}}}

(5)

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i}p_{i}\rho_{AB_{i}^{L}}\otimes\omega_{B_{i}^{R}}\,,

(6)

where
\{p_{i}\}
is a probability distribution and
\rho_{AB_{i}^{L}}
and
\omega_{B_{i}^{R}}
are the states on
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{L}}}
and
{{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{R}}}
, respectively.
A decomposition in Eqs. (
5
)-(
6
) is said to be the Koashi-Imoto decomposition if for any CPTP-map
{\mathcal{E}}_{B}
satisfying

{\mathcal{E}}_{B}(\rho_{AB})=\rho_{AB}\,,

any
{\mathcal{E}}_{B}
’s Stinespring dilation isometry

defined by

{\mathcal{E}}_{B}(\cdot)={\rm tr}_{E}\left(V_{B\to BE}\cdot(V_{B\to BE})^{\dagger}\right)

is decomposed into

V_{B\to BE}=\bigoplus_{i}I_{B_{i}^{L}}\otimes V_{B_{i}^{R}\to B_{i}^{R}E}

satisfying

{\rm tr}_{E}\left(V_{B_{i}^{R}\to B_{i}^{R}E}\>\omega_{B_{i}^{R}}(V_{B_{i}^{R}\to B_{i}^{R}E})^{\dagger}\right)=\omega_{B_{i}^{R}}\,,\quad\forall i.

The Koashi-Imoto decomposition can be considered as a special case of the factorization theorem in Refs.
[
23

24
]

[

25
,
26
]
.

The factorization theorem
[
23

24
]
further demonstrates that, if Eq. (

is a CPTP map. This implies that minimal compression is achieved when

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{{\tilde{B}}}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{i}{{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{L}}}

\displaystyle\rho_{A{\tilde{B}}}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{i}p_{i}\rho_{AB_{i}^{L}}\,.

II-D

Minimal sufficient subalgebra

Here we introduce another characterization of the Koashi-Imoto decomposition based on the theory of quantum sufficiency. Consider a set of density matrices on
B

{\mathcal{S}}:=\left\{\mu_{B}=\frac{{\rm tr}_{A}\left(M_{A}\rho_{AB}\right)}{{\rm tr}\left(M_{A}\rho_{A}\right)}\;|\;0\leq M_{A}\leq I_{A}\right\}\,,

(8)

dominated by
\rho_{B}
(i.e.,
\rho_{B}\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
\forall\mu_{B}\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
{\rm supp}(\mu_{B})\subset{\rm supp}(\rho_{B})
). In terms of
{\mathcal{S}}
, Eq. (
4
) is rephrased as
[
16
,
27
]

{\mathcal{R}}_{{\tilde{B}}\to B}\circ{\mathcal{E}}_{B\to{\tilde{B}}}(\mu_{B})=\mu_{B}\,,\forall\mu_{B}\in{\mathcal{S}}.

(9)

This kind of condition has been extensively studied in the theory of sufficiency
[
9
,
10
]
, and it is known that the map
{\mathcal{R}}_{{\tilde{B}}\to B}
can always be selected as the Petz recovery map
{\mathcal{R}}^{\rho,{\mathcal{E}}}_{{\tilde{B}}\to B}
with respect to
\rho_{B}
.

The theory of sufficiency states that the minimal dimension of
{\tilde{B}}
is associated with the
minimal sufficient subalgebra

{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{B})
of
{\mathcal{S}}
. Because
{\mathcal{M}}^{S}_{B}
is a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra, a decomposition

{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\cong\bigoplus_{i}{{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{L}}}\otimes{{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{R}}}

(10)

exists such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i}{\mathcal{B}}({{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{L}}})\otimes I_{B_{i}^{R}}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}\cong

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i}I_{B_{i}^{L}}\otimes{\mathcal{B}}({{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{R}}})\,,

where
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}:=\{X_{B}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{B})|[X_{B},Y_{B}]=0\,,\;\forall Y_{B}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}\}
is the commutant of
{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}
.
The sufficiency theorem
[
10
]
states that Eq. (
9
) implies
{\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{B}}
must support algebra isomorphic to
{\mathcal{M}}^{S}_{B}
.
Therefore, the minimal Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{B}}
must be:

{\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{B}}\cong\bigoplus_{i}{{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{i}^{L}}}\,.

As discussed in Sec.
II-C
, decomposition (
10
) must match that of the Koashi-Imoto decomposition (
5
): an algorithm can be used to calculate the Koshi-Imoto decomposition
[
15
,
17
]
; however, these algorithms do not have an explicit formula to calculate the decomposition or the dimension
d_{\tilde{B}}
. For a similar reason, calculating the optimal compression rate in the asymptotic results in Ref.
[
5
]
is a non-trivial task.

III
Main result

III-A

Sketch of the proof

In this section, we provide a sketch of the strategy to obtain our main result. The explicit proof is given in Sec.
IV
and Sec.
V
, and readers who are interested in the statement of the main theorem can skip this subsection.

Our main goal is to obtain the optimal dimension (
7
) from a given bipartite density matrix
\rho_{AB}
, without explicitly calculating the Koashi-Imoto decomposition. The main proof consists of two steps. First, we characterize
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}
as the largest subalgebra of the fixed-point algebra of a certain CPTP-map
{\mathcal{T}}_{B}
that is invariant under modular transformation
\Delta_{\rho_{B}}^{t}(\cdot)=\rho_{B}^{it}\cdot\rho_{B}^{-it}
(Sec.
IV
).

To obtain the characterization, notice that the Koashi-Imoto decomposition (
6
) implies that

J_{AB}=\bigoplus_{i}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{B_{i}^{L}}\rho_{AB_{i}^{L}}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{B_{i}^{L}}\otimes I_{B_{i}^{R}}\,.

This is the Choi-Jamiołkowski representation of a unital CP-map
\Omega_{A\to B}^{\dagger}
. A recent result in quantum sufficiency
[
18
]
shows that the minimal sufficient subalgebra
{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}
is generated by
\Delta_{\rho_{B}}^{t}\circ\Omega^{\dagger}_{A\to B}(M_{A})
for
M_{A}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{A}),t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Although it is still hard to specify the explicit algebra structure of
{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}
, one can characterize the commutant
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}
as the set of operators commuting with these generators.
Since the generators of
{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}
form the images of unital CP-maps
\Delta^{t}_{\rho_{B}}\circ\Omega^{\dagger}_{A\to B}
, a standard theorem in operator-algebra quantum error correction
[
20
]
on the commutant of a unital CP-map provides an interpretation of
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}
as the intersection of the correctable algebras of CPTP-maps parameterized by
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Further application of another result in error correction
[
21
]
implies that
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}
is the largest subalgebra of the fixed points of
{\mathcal{T}}_{B}:={\mathcal{R}}^{\tau,\Omega_{E\to B}}_{E\to B}\circ\Omega_{B\to E}
, where
\Omega_{B\to E}:=(\Omega_{B\to A})^{c}
, that is invariant under
\Delta_{\rho_{B}}^{t}
.

In the second (Sec.
V
), we obtain the Choi state of the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{B}
on
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}
by using this characterization. A conditional expectation is an idempotent positive linear map onto a subalgebra, and thus it is a projection as a superoperator (an operator acting on the vector space of operators). The projection onto the fixed point algebra
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{T}}_{B})
is obtained as the projection with eigenvalue 1 in the spectral decomposition of the superoperator representation of
{\mathcal{T}}_{B}
. The invariance under the modular transformation for all
t
is equivalent to the invariance under the adjoint action
{\rm ad}_{\rho_{B}}
of the generator of
\Delta_{\rho_{B}}^{t}
, from which we can remove the dependence on
t
. Hence, the projection onto
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}
is obtained from the superoperators corresponding to
{\mathcal{T}}_{B}
(
13
) and
{\rm ad}_{\rho_{B}}
(
14
).

The Choi state of
\mathbb{E}_{B}
is obtained from the superoperator projection via the reshuffling
[
29
]
. Its canonical purification consists of the direct sum of four maximally entangled states among four copies of
B_{i}^{R},B_{i}^{L}
(Fig.
1
in Sec.
V
). Simply calculating the rank of
B
provides
\sum_{i}d_{B_{i}^{R}}d_{B_{i}^{L}}=d_{B}
, counting contributions from both the entanglement between
B_{i}^{R}
and its copy and the entanglement between
B_{i}^{L}
and its copy.
We then extract the optimal dimension (
7
) by only removing the entanglement between
B_{i}^{R}
and its copy via an optimization over bipartite unitaries. This final step enables us to count the contribution only from
B_{i}^{L}
.

III-B

The statement of the main result

S(B{\bar{B}})_{UCU^{\dagger}}=H(\{p_{i}\})+\sum_{i}p_{i}\left(\log d_{B^{R}_{i}}+S(B^{L}_{i}{\bar{B}})_{\Phi_{i}}\right)\,,

(57)

where
H(\cdot)
denotes the Shannon entropy. Only the final term depends on
U_{{\bar{B}}{\bar{B}}_{1}}
.
The minimum in Eq. (
18
) is achieved when every
\Phi_{i,B^{L}_{i}{\bar{B}}}
is in a pure state (the minimum value is the entanglement of the purification
[
32
]
of
C_{BB_{1}}
).
The corresponding state is given as

\displaystyle|{\tilde{C}}\rangle_{BB_{1}{\bar{B}}{\bar{B}_{1}}}=\sum_{i}\sqrt{p_{i}}|\Psi_{i}\rrangle_{B_{i}^{R}{B_{1i}^{R}}}|\Psi_{i}\rrangle_{B_{i}^{L}{\bar{B}}}|\Psi_{i}\rrangle_{B_{1i}^{L}{\bar{B}}_{1}}\,,

(58)

where
|\Psi_{i}\rrangle_{B_{i}^{L}{\bar{B}}}
(and
|\Psi_{i}\rrangle_{B_{1i}^{L}{\bar{B}}_{1}}

B_{i}^{L}
and
{\bar{B}}
(
B_{i}^{R}
and
{\bar{B}}_{1}
)(Fig.

It is clear that

\displaystyle{\rm SchR}(BB_{1}{\bar{B}}_{1}:{\bar{B}})_{\tilde{C}}

\displaystyle={\rm rank}({\tilde{C}}_{\bar{B}})=\sum_{i}{d_{B^{L}_{i}}}

(59)

\displaystyle{\rm SchR}(B{\bar{B}}:B_{1}{\bar{B}}_{1})_{\tilde{C}}

\displaystyle={\rm rank}({\tilde{C}}_{{\bar{B}}{\bar{B}}_{1}})=\sum_{i}{d_{B^{R}_{i}}}

(60)

By comparing

and decomposition (

from which the theorem holds.

Note that it might be true that the minimal achievable dimension can be calculated by minimization:

\min_{U_{BB_{1}}}{\rm rank}\left({\rm tr}_{B_{1}}\left(U_{BB_{1}}C_{BB_{1}}U_{BB_{1}}^{\dagger}\right)\right)\,.

(61)

This expression does not need a purification, however, we do not know an easily implementable algorithm to perform the minimization in Eq. (

Application examples

VI-A

Classical case

The condition for the classical case is well known. However, here we reproduce it using our results to check for consistency.
Let us consider the classical bipartite state

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{a,b}p(a,b)|a,b\rangle\langle a,b|

such that
\rho_{A},\rho_{B}>0
without loss of generality. Then, the Choi matrix
J_{AB}
is

J_{AB}=\sum_{a,b}p(a|b)|a,b\rangle\langle a,b|

and obtain
\omega_{a,b}=p(a|b)
and
K_{a,b}=\sqrt{p(a|b)}|a\rangle\langle b|
. The unital CP map in Eq. (
12
) can be written as

\Omega^{\dagger}_{A\to B}(\cdot)=\sum_{a,b}p(a|b)\langle a|\cdot|a\rangle|b\rangle\langle b|\,.

Then, we introduce an equivalence relation between basis labels
|b\rangle
as

b\sim b^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow p(a|b)=p(a|b^{\prime})\,,\quad\forall a\,.

This equivalence relation induces a disjoint decomposition of the label set
{\mathcal{B}}=\{b\}
as
{\mathcal{B}}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{B}}^{i}
and the Hilbert space as
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}=\bigoplus_{i}{\mathcal{H}}_{B^{i}}
, where
{\mathcal{H}}_{B^{i}}:={\rm span}\{|b\rangle\>|\>b\in{\mathcal{B}}^{i}\}
. We define
I_{B^{i}}:=\sum_{b\in{\mathcal{B}}^{i}}|b\rangle\langle b|
. A simple calculation shows that

{\rm Im}{\Omega}^{\dagger}_{A\to B}=\left\{\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}I_{B^{i}}\,\middle|\>c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}\,,

and therefore,

{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{T}})=\left({\rm Im}\Omega^{\dagger}_{A\to B}\right)^{\prime}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}|i\rangle\langle i|\otimes M_{B^{i}}(\mathbb{C})\,

from Theorem
2
:
As
\rho_{B}
is classical and does not have an off-diagonal term between
b\neq b^{\prime}
,
\Delta_{\rho_{B}}^{t}({\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{T}}))\subset{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{T}})
is true for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence,
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}={\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{T}})
and the minimal sufficient subalgebra of
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
become

{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}|i\rangle\langle i|\otimes I_{B^{i}}\,

that the verification of

C_{BB_{1}}=\frac{1}{d_{B}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}|i\rangle\langle i|_{B_{i}^{L}}\otimes|{\mathcal{I}}\rrangle\llangle{\mathcal{I}}|_{B_{i}^{R}B_{1i}^{R}}\otimes|i\rangle\langle i|_{B_{1i}^{L}}\,.

and

d_{\tilde{B}}^{\min}=m={\rm rank}(C_{BB_{1}})\,.

(62)

is simple (
d_{B_{i}^{L}}=1
in this case).
This is consistent with minimal sufficient statistics
[
7
]
.

VI-B

Pure bipartite states

For a pure state,
\rho_{AB}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|_{AB}
restricted to
\rho_{A},\rho_{B}>0
implies
d_{A}=d_{B}={\rm SchR}(A)_{\psi}
.

J_{AB}=|{\mathcal{I}}\rrangle\llangle{\mathcal{I}}|_{AB}

(63)

and thus
\Omega_{A\to B}^{\dagger}={\rm id}_{A\to B}
. This implies that

{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{T}})=\mathbb{C}I_{B}

(64)

according to Theorem
2
. This is also evident that
\Omega_{B\to E}
must be a completely depolarized channel. Therefore,
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}I
and
d_{\tilde{B}}^{\min}={\rm SchR}(A:B)_{\psi}=d_{B}
; that is, further compression is impossible.

VI-C

Exact compression of quantum channel

For a given CPTP-map
{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to B}
,
{\mathcal{F}}_{B\to{\tilde{B}}}
is an exact compression if there is a post-processing CPTP-map
{\mathcal{R}}_{{\tilde{B}}\to B}
that satisfies

\displaystyle{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}_{A\to{\tilde{B}}}

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{F}}_{B\to{\tilde{B}}}\circ{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to B}\,,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to B}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{R}}_{{\tilde{B}}\to B}\circ{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}_{A\to{\tilde{B}}}\,.

Again, the exact compression is non-trivial if
d_{\tilde{B}}<d_{B}
.
Consider the (normalized) Choi state of
{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to B}

\rho_{AB}=({\rm id}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{E}}_{{\bar{A}}\to B})(|\Psi\rrangle\llangle\Psi|_{A{\bar{A}}})\,.

(65)

Then, the problem is reduced to the exact compression of
\rho_{AB}
on
B
.

For these Choi states,
J_{AB}
is further reduced to

\displaystyle J_{AB}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}(\tau_{A})^{-1/2}({\rm id}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{E}})\left(|\Psi\rrangle\llangle\Psi|_{A{\bar{A}}}\right){\mathcal{E}}(\tau_{A})^{-1/2}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}(I_{A})^{-1/2}({\rm id}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{E}})\left(|{\mathcal{I}}\rrangle\llangle{\mathcal{I}}|_{A{\bar{A}}}\right){\mathcal{E}}(I_{A})^{-1/2}\,,

which is the Choi operator of
({\mathcal{R}}^{\tau,{\mathcal{E}}}_{B\to A})^{\dagger}
; that means,
\Omega_{A\to B}^{\dagger}=({\mathcal{R}}^{\tau,{\mathcal{E}}}_{B\to A})^{\dagger}
.
Therefore,
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}
is the largest subalgebra of
({\rm Im}({\mathcal{R}}^{\tau,{\mathcal{E}}}_{B\to A})^{\dagger})^{\prime}
invariant under
{\rm ad}_{{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to B}(\tau_{A})}
.

VI-C
1
Unital channels

Consider the case in which
{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to B}
is unital, that is,
{\mathcal{E}}(I_{A})=I_{B}
(
A\cong B
). Then
\Delta_{\rho_{B}}^{t}={\rm id}_{B}
and
\Omega_{B\to A}={\mathcal{R}}^{\tau,{\mathcal{E}}}_{B\to A}={\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger}_{B\to A}
are true. Because the modular invariance is trivial,
({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}={\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{T}})
and

({\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S})^{\prime}=({\rm Im}{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to B})^{\prime}\,,\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{B}^{S}={\rm Alg}\left({\rm Im}{\mathcal{E}}_{A\to B}\right)\,.

(66)

from Theorem
2
.

An example of a unital channel is a
G
-twirling operation of a finite group
G
.
Let
U(g)
be a unitary representation of
G
in
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
.
G
-twirling
{\mathcal{G}}(\cdot)
is defined as follows:

{\mathcal{G}}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}U(g)\cdot U(g^{-1})\,.

(67)

In general,
U(g)
is not irreducible and the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
can be decomposed as

{\mathcal{H}}_{B}=\bigoplus_{\mu:irrep}{\mathcal{H}}_{B^{L}_{\mu}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B^{R}_{\mu}}\,,

(68)

where the irreducible decomposition of

U(g)=\bigoplus_{\mu:irrep}U_{\mu}(g)\otimes I_{B^{R}_{\mu}},

(69)

correspondingly.

and
{\rm dim}{\mathcal{H}}_{B^{R}_{\mu}}

\mu

(70)

VII
Conclusion

We studied the task of exact local compression of quantum bipartite states, where the goal is to locally reduce the dimensions of each Hilbert space without losing any information. We demonstrated a closed formula that allows us to calculate the minimum achievable dimensions for this compression task.

Furthermore, we derived simple upper and lower bounds for the rank of the matrix, which offers a numerically tractable approach to estimate the minimal dimensions. These bounds are in general not tight; however, it is still useful to check, for example, the scaling of the optimal compressed dimension. Future studies could focus on exploring better (analytical and numerical) expressions for the optimal dimension. It would also be interesting to investigate the relationship between the obtained dimension and other known one-shot entropic quantities.

The ability to compress quantum states and channels while maintaining their informational content could have more implications for zero-error quantum information processing. Theorem
2
suggests that non-trivial compression is impossible for generic bipartite states or quantum channels. The asymptotic result
[
5
]
also relies on the non-trivial Koashi-Imoto decomposition, so considering multiple copies is not helpful. However, it would be possible that for structured states or channels such as tensor network states or covariant channels, exact local compression can be generally non-trivial. The exact local compression on many-body states are also related to renormalization group flow as discussed in Refs.
[
27
,
28
]

Practically, it would also be interesting to investigate
approximate
one-shot scenario, which has a better potential for applications in quantum information processing (such as quantum information bottleneck problem
[
33
]
) but has not been investigated in this paper. The asymptotic result
[
5
]
implies that asymptotic compression with vanishing global error also requires non-trivial Koashi-Imoto decomposition, thus the vanishing error condition may still be too restrictive (see e.g., Ref.
[
34
]
for a possible modification). One possible way to obtain approximate compression is considering “smoothed” variant of the minimal dimension obtained in this paper. We leave studying such modification as a future problem.

Appendix A
Operator-algebra quantum error correction

The theory of operator-algebra quantum error correction introduced in Refs.
[
19
,
20
]
provides the most general framework of quantum error correction in the sense that it is a generalization of both standard quantum error correction
[
35
,
36
]
and operator quantum error correction
[
37
,
38
]
. Standard quantum error correction discusses the condition for a subspace that is left protected from noise (a CPTP-map), and operator quantum error correction discusses that for a noiseless subsystem. Operator-algebra quantum error correction includes both of these frameworks and provides a characterization of the set of observables whose information is perfectly protected from a given noise.

Formally, the
correctable algebra

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{E}}
of a CPTP-map
{\mathcal{E}}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}})
on a code subspace
P_{C}{\mathcal{H}}
is a subalgebra of
{\mathcal{B}}(P_{C}{\mathcal{H}})
defined as:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{X\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})\;|\;[P_{C}E_{a}^{\dagger}E_{b}P_{C},X]=0\;\forall a,b\right\}\,,

(71)

where
\{E_{a}\}
are the Kraus operators of
{\mathcal{E}}
.
P_{C}
is the projection onto
C
, and it is set to be
P_{C}=I
in the main text and in the following. Eq. (
71
) is a generalization of the Knill-Laflamme condition
[
36
]
, and it has been shown that the condition is equivalent to the existence of
recovery map

{\mathcal{R}}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}})\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})
such that
[
19
,
20
]

({\mathcal{R}}\circ{\mathcal{E}})^{\dagger}(X)=X\,,\forall X\in{\mathcal{A}}\,.

(72)

This is also equivalent to

{\rm tr}\left(X\rho\right)={\rm tr}\left(X{\mathcal{R}}\circ{\mathcal{E}}(\rho)\right)\,,\forall\rho\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})\,,\forall X\in{\mathcal{A}}\,,

(73)

i.e., the information of all
X\in{\mathcal{A}}
on any state is perfectly recoverable after the noise is performed.
The recovery map can be chosen to be the Petz recovery map with respect to the completely mixed state
\tau
, and the following characterization is known.

Lemma 3
.

[
21
]
For any CPTP-map
{\mathcal{E}}
, it holds that

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{F}}\left({\mathcal{R}}^{\tau,{\mathcal{E}}}\circ{\mathcal{E}}\right)={\mathcal{F}}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger}\circ({\mathcal{R}}^{\tau,{\mathcal{E}}})^{\dagger}\right)\,.

(74)

We emphasize that one should not confuse the minimal sufficient subalgebra and the correctable algebra. Although both algebras have recovery maps, the former characterizes the information needed to keep a given set of states under arbitrary noises, while the latter characterizes the information protected from a given noise.

In the standard quantum error correction, the no-cloning theorem
[
39
]
induces a complementary relation: if one can perfectly recover a state from the noise, then one cannot learn any information about the state from the output of the complimentary channel of the noise. In operator-algebra quantum error correction, it is possible to learn some classical information from the complementary channel without violating the no-cloning theorem.

For a Kraus representation
\{E_{a}\}
of
{\mathcal{E}}
, a Kraus representation
\{F_{a}\}
of the complementary channel
{\mathcal{E}}^{c}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{E})
is given by

F_{a}:=\sum_{b}|\phi_{b}\rangle\langle a|E_{b}\,,

(75)

where
\{|\phi_{b}\rangle\}
and
\{|a\rangle\}
are orthonormal bases of
{\mathcal{H}}_{E}
and
{\mathcal{K}}
, respectively. The correctable algebra of
{\mathcal{E}}^{c}
is then generated by operators commuting with

F_{a}^{\dagger}F_{b}=\sum_{c,d}E_{c}^{\dagger}|a\rangle\langle\phi_{c}|\phi_{d}\rangle\langle b|E_{d}={\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger}(|a\rangle\langle b|),,

(76)

thus the following lemma holds.

Lemma 2
.

[
20
]

For any CPTP-map
{\mathcal{E}}
, let
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{E}}^{c}}
be the correctable algebra of the complementary map
{\mathcal{E}}^{c}
. Then, it holds that

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{E}}^{c}}=({\rm Im}{\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger})^{\prime}\,.

(77)

This is the complementary relation for operator-algebra quantum error correction.

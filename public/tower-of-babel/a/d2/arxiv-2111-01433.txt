Blow-up of solutions to semilinear wave equations with a time-dependent strong damping
Ahmad Z. Fino, Mohamed Ali Hamza

Abstract

We also study the local existence in time of mild solution in the energy space H 1 ​ ( ℝ n ) × L 2 ​ ( ℝ n ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) .

1
Introduction

The aim of the paper is to establish a blow-up result for local in time solutions to the Cauchy
problem for the following semilinear strong damped wave equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\,\,\displaystyle{u_{tt}-\Delta u-\frac{b_{0}}{(1+t)^{\beta}}\Delta u_{t}=|u|^{p}}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}},\,t>0,}\\
\\
\displaystyle{u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\;\;u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}},}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
n\geq 1
,
p>1
,
b_{0}
is a positive constant, and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
b_{0}=1
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p\in(1,\infty)&\hbox{for}\,\,n=1,2,\\
\\
p\in(1,\frac{n}{n-2}]&\hbox{for}\,\,n\geq 3,\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

and the initial data are in the energy space

(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.3)

Here after,
\|\cdotp\|_{q}
and
\|\cdotp\|_{H^{1}}

(1\leq q\leq\infty)
stand for the usual
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-norm and

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-norm, respectively.

In this paper, we study the blow-up result of solution of (
1.1
). Before
going on, it is necessary to mention that the
case
b_{0}=0
in (
1.1
) is the classical semilinear wave equation for which we have the Strauss conjecture. More precisely, this case is characterized by a critical power, denoted by
p_{S}
, which is the positive solution of the following quadratic equation

(n-1)p^{2}-(n+1)p-2=0,

and is given by

p_{S}=p_{S}(n):=\frac{n+1+\sqrt{n^{2}+10n-7}}{2(n-1)}.

(1.4)

More precisely, if
p\leq p_{S}
then there is no global solution for (
1.1
) under suitable sign assumptions for the initial data,
and for
p>p_{S}
a global solution exists for small initial data; see e.g.
[
7
,
10
,
12
,
14
]
among many other references.

A slightly less sharp blow-up result under much weaker assumptions was obtained by Kato
[
8
]
with a much easier proof. In particular, Kato pointed out the role of the exponent
{(n+1)}/{(n-1)}<p_{S}(n),
for
n\geq 2,
in order to have more general initial data, but still with compact support.

We take the opportunity to mention here that the test function method, introduced by
[
13
]
and used by
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
, plays a similar role as of Kato’s method to prove blow-up results. In fact, the test function is effective in the case of parabolic equations which means that it provide us exactly the critical exponent
p_{c}
, while in the case of hyperbolic equations (cf.
[
9
]
) we get the so-called Kato’s exponent
p^{*}
i.e. we obtain a blow-up result for
p\leq p^{*}<p_{c}
. This is one of the weakness of the test function method but in general it can be applied to a more general equation and system.

When
\beta=0
, and
b_{0}=1
, problem (
1.1
) is reduced to

\displaystyle{u_{tt}-\Delta u-\Delta u_{t}=|u|^{p}},\ \ \ \ \displaystyle{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}},t>0,}

(1.5)

which is called the viscoelastic damping case. D’Ambrosio and Lucente
[
3
, Theorem 4.2]
proved that the solution of (
1.5
) blows-up in finite time when
1<p\leq(n+1)/(n-1)_{+}
, where
(\cdotp)_{+}:=\max\{0,\cdotp\}
, by applying the test function method. Similar result has been obtained recently by Fino
[
4
]
in the case of an exterior domain. On the other hand, D’Abbicco-Reissig
[
2
]
proved that there exists a global solution for (
1.5
) when
p>1+\frac{3}{n-1}

(n\geq 2)
for sufficiently small initial data. Therefore, the exact value of the critical exponent is still an open question.

When
\beta\neq 0
, and
b_{0}=1
, we give an intuitive observation for understanding the influence of the damping term (
-\frac{1}{{(1+t)}^{\beta}}\Delta u_{t}
) by scaling argument. Let
u(t,x)
be a solution of the linear strong damped wave equation

u_{tt}(t,x)-\Delta u(t,x)-\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\beta}}\Delta u_{t}(t,x)=0.

(1.6)

When
\beta\geq-1
, we put

v(t,x)=u(\lambda(1+t),\lambda x),\qquad\lambda(t+1)=s,\,\,\lambda x=y,

(1.7)

with a parameter
\lambda>0
, we have

v_{ss}(s,y)-\Delta v(s,y)-\frac{1}{\lambda^{\beta+1}s^{\beta}}\Delta v_{s}(s,y)=0.

Thus, when
\beta=-1
we notice that the equation (
1.6
) is invariant, while when
\beta>-1
, letting
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain the wave equation without damping

v_{ss}(s,y)-\Delta v(s,y)=0.

We note that
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
is corresponding to
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

On the other hand, when
\beta<-1
, we put

v(t,x)=u(\lambda^{\frac{2}{1-\beta}}(1+t),\lambda x),\qquad\lambda(t+1)=s,\,\,\lambda x=y,

with a parameter
\lambda>0
, we have

v_{ss}(s,y)-\frac{1}{s^{\beta}}\Delta v_{s}(s,y)-\lambda^{\frac{2(\beta+1)}{1-\beta}}\Delta v(s,y)=0.

In this case, letting
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain the hyperbolic equation

v_{ss}(s,y)-\frac{1}{s^{\beta}}\Delta v_{s}(s,y)=0.

In this paper, our goal is to generalize Kato’s exponent and give sufficient conditions for finite time blow-up of a new type of class of equations (
1.1
) for
b_{0}>0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Let us mention that our blow-up results and initial conditions are similar to that of Kato.

This paper is organized as follows. We start in Section
2
by introducing the mild solution of (
1.1
).
Then, we state the main theorem of our work. In Section
3
,
we study the local existence of the solutions of equation (
1.1
) in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Finally, in Section
4
, we prove the blow-up theorem (Theorem
1
).

2
Main results

This section is aimed to state our main results. For that purpose, we first start by giving the definition of the mild solution of (
1.1
).

Definition 1
.

(Mild solution)

Let
(u_{0},u_{1})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We say that a function

u\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

is a mild solution of (
1.1
) if and
u
has the initial data
u(0)=u_{0}
,
u_{t}(0)=u_{1}
and satisfies
the integral equation

u(t,x)=R(t,0)(u_{0},u_{1})+\int_{0}^{t}S(t,s)|u(s)|^{p}\,ds

(2.8)

in the sense of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where the operators
R
and
S
are defined below. Moreover, if
T>0
can be arbitrary chosen, then
u
is called a global mild solution (
1.1
).

Here is the statement of our main theorem in this paper.

Theorem 1

(
Blow-up
)
.

We assume that

(u_{0},u_{1})\in\big(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big)\times\big(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\big)

satisfying the following condition:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx>0.

(2.9)

If

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1<p\leq\frac{n+1}{(n-1)_{+}}&\text{if}\,~\beta\geq-1,\\
&\\
1<p\leq\frac{n(1-\beta)+2}{(n(1-\beta)-2)_{+}}&\text{if}\,~\beta\leq-1,\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

where
(\cdotp)_{+}:=\max\{0,\cdotp\}
, then the mild solution of (
1.1
) blows-up in finite time.

Remark 1
.

We stress that
the exponent
\frac{n+1}{(n-1)_{+}}
appearing in (
2.10
) was introduced first in
[
8
]

to prove the nonexistence of global
solutions to the semilinear wave equation with the nonlinearity
|u|^{p},
for small initial data with compact support.

Remark 2
.

Theorem 1 asserts that if
\beta\geq-1
, then the critical exponent for
p
, is greater than or equal to
\frac{n+1}{(n-1)_{+}}
.
Therefore, the
blow-up region obtained in the present work in the case

\beta\geq-1
constitute somehow an extension to the results related to the blow-up region of the solution of the equation (
1.5
) obtained in
[
3
]
.

Remark 3
.

It is interesting to recall that thanks to the transformation (
1.7
)
the asymptotic behavior of the solution to (
1.6
) in the case
\beta\geq-1
, is given by the free wave equation.
Unfortunatly, the time-dependent damping term (
-\frac{1}{{(1+t)}^{\beta}}\Delta u_{t}
)
makes the problem parabolic and loses the property that the speed of propagation is finite.
For this reason, we obtain the blow-up region given by (
2.10
) which is included in
(1,p_{S}(n)]
.
We think it is likely possible to
extend the blow-up region to a larger interval
(1,p_{c}]
where

\frac{n+1}{(n-1)_{+}}<p_{c}\leq p_{S}(n)
at least for
\beta
large enough.

(\psi_{2}^{\eta})_{tt}=\eta\psi_{2}^{\eta-1}(\psi_{2})_{tt}+\eta(\eta-1)\psi_{2}^{\eta-2}|(\psi_{2})_{t}|^{2},
the inequality (
4.24
) becomes

I_{1}\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{1}^{\ell}\psi_{2}^{\eta-p^{\prime}}|(\psi_{2})_{tt}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{1}^{\ell}\psi_{2}^{\eta-2p^{\prime}}|(\psi_{2})_{t}|^{2p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt.

(4.25)

Using and proceeding similarly as for (
4.25
) and using the identity
\Delta(\psi_{1}^{\ell})=\ell\psi_{1}^{\ell-1}\Delta\psi_{1}+\ell(\ell-1)\psi_{1}^{\ell-2}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2},
we easily deduce

I_{2}\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta}\psi_{1}^{\ell-p^{\prime}}|\Delta\psi_{1}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta}\psi_{1}^{\ell-2p^{\prime}}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt.

(4.26)

In the same way, thanks to
\Delta\psi_{t}=\Delta(\psi_{1}^{\ell})(\psi^{\eta}_{2})_{t}
, we write

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\beta}}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|\psi^{1/p}\psi^{-1/p}\,|\Delta(\psi_{1}^{\ell})|\,|(\psi^{\eta}_{2})_{t}|\,dx\,dt

(4.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+C\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\beta p^{\prime}}}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{1}^{-\ell/(p-1)}|\Delta(\psi_{1}^{\ell})|^{p^{\prime}}\,\psi_{2}^{-\eta/(p-1)}\,|(\psi^{\eta}_{2})_{t}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt.

Clearly,

\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\beta p^{\prime}}}\leq C\,T^{-\beta p^{\prime}},\qquad\forall\,t\in\left(\frac{T}{2},T\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+C\,T^{-\beta p^{\prime}}\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta-p^{\prime}}|(\psi_{2})_{t}|^{p^{\prime}}\psi_{1}^{\ell-p^{\prime}}|\Delta\psi_{1}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt

(4.28)

\displaystyle\,\,+\,C\,T^{-\beta p^{\prime}}\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta-p^{\prime}}|(\psi_{2})_{t}|^{p^{\prime}}\psi_{1}^{\ell-2p^{\prime}}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt.

In the same manner,

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\beta+1}}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|\psi^{1/p}\psi^{-1/p}\,\,\psi^{\eta}_{2}\,|\Delta(\psi_{1}^{\ell})|\,dx\,dt

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+C\,\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{(\beta+1)p^{\prime}}}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta}\psi_{1}^{\ell-p^{\prime}}|\Delta\psi_{1}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\,\,+\,C\,\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{(\beta+1)p^{\prime}}}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta}\psi_{1}^{\ell-2p^{\prime}}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt.

Finally, it remains only to control the term
I_{5}
. Note
by exploiting the identities

\Delta\psi(0,x)=\Delta(\psi_{1}^{\ell})=\ell\psi_{1}^{\ell-1}\Delta\psi_{1}+\ell(\ell-1)\psi_{1}^{\ell-2}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\psi_{t}(0,x)=\eta\psi_{1}^{\ell}(\psi_{2})_{t}(0),

we infer

I_{5}\leq C\,\int_{\Omega(T)}|u_{0}|\left(\psi_{1}^{\ell-1}|\Delta\psi_{1}|+\psi_{1}^{\ell-2}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2}+|(\psi_{2})_{t}(0)|\psi_{1}^{\ell}\right)\,dx.

(4.30)

Plugging (
4
) together with (
4.25
)-(
4.30
) and choosing
\varepsilon
small enough, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega(T)}u_{1}(x)\psi(0,x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{1}^{\ell}\psi_{2}^{\eta-p^{\prime}}|(\psi_{2})_{tt}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{1}^{\ell}\psi_{2}^{\eta-2p^{\prime}}|(\psi_{2})_{t}|^{2p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta}\psi_{1}^{\ell-p^{\prime}}|\Delta\psi_{1}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta}\psi_{1}^{\ell-2p^{\prime}}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\,T^{-\beta p^{\prime}}\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta-p^{\prime}}|(\psi_{2})_{t}|^{p^{\prime}}\psi_{1}^{\ell-p^{\prime}}|\Delta\psi_{1}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\,T^{-\beta p^{\prime}}\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta-p^{\prime}}|(\psi_{2})_{t}|^{p^{\prime}}\psi_{1}^{\ell-2p^{\prime}}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\,\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\frac{(\beta+1)p}{p-1}}}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta}\psi_{1}^{\ell-p^{\prime}}|\Delta\psi_{1}|^{p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt+C\,\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\frac{(\beta+1)p}{p-1}}}\int_{\Omega(T)}\psi_{2}^{\eta}\psi_{1}^{\ell-2p^{\prime}}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2p^{\prime}}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\,\int_{\Omega(T)}|u_{0}|\left(\psi_{1}^{\ell-1}|\Delta\psi_{1}|+\psi_{1}^{\ell-2}|\nabla\psi_{1}|^{2}+|(\psi_{2})_{t}(0)|\psi_{1}^{\ell}\right)\,dx.

(4.31)

By neglecting the first term in the left-hand side and
taking into account the expresion of
\psi
given by (
4.21
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega(T)}u_{1}(x)\psi^{\ell}_{1}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\;T^{-2p^{\prime}+1+nd}+C\;T^{-2dp^{\prime}+1+nd}+C\;T^{-\beta p^{\prime}-p^{\prime}-2dp^{\prime}+1+nd}

(4.32)

\displaystyle+\,C\;T^{-2dp^{\prime}+nd}\int_{0}^{T}(1+t)^{-\frac{(\beta+1)\,p}{p-1}}\,dt+\,C\left(T^{-2d}+T^{-1}\right)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{0}(x)|\,dx.

Now, we distinguish two cases:

I. First case:
\beta\geq-1
.

In this case, we choose
d=1
.

Subcritical case
(p<\frac{n+1}{(n-1)_{+}})
.

Note that, as

\int_{0}^{T}(1+t)^{-\frac{(\beta+1)\,p}{p-1}}\,dt\leq C\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T^{1-\frac{(\beta+1)\,p}{p-1}}&\text{if}~\beta\,p<-1,\\
\ln(T)&\text{if}~\beta\,p=-1,\\
1&\text{if}~\beta\,p>-1,\\
\end{array}\right.

(4.33)

we have
\int_{0}^{T}(1+t)^{-\frac{(\beta+1)\,p}{p-1}}\,dt\leq CT
, for all
T>1
. Then
(
4.32
) implies

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega(T)}u_{1}(x)\psi^{\ell}_{1}(x)\,dx\leq C\;T^{-2p^{\prime}+1+n}+C\;T^{-(\beta+1)p^{\prime}-2p^{\prime}+1+n}+\,C\left(T^{-2}+T^{-1}\right)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{0}(x)|\,dx,

(4.34)

for all
T>1
. We use the fact
\beta\geq-1
, to conclude that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega(T)}u_{1}(x)\psi^{\ell}_{1}(x)\,dx\leq C\;T^{-2p^{\prime}+1+n}+\,C\left(T^{-2}+T^{-1}\right)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{0}(x)|\,dx,\quad\forall\,T>1.

(4.35)

Note that, we can easily see that

-2p^{\prime}+1+n<0,

if

p<\frac{n+1}{(n-1)_{+}}.

Letting
T\rightarrow\infty
, and using the Lebesgue
dominated convergence theorem, we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)\,dx\leq 0.

This contradicts our assumption (
2.9
).

Critical case
(p=\frac{n+1}{n-1})
, when
n\geq 2
.

Let
n\geq 2
, and

p=\frac{n+1}{n-1}.

From the subcritical case (
4.35
), we can see easily that we have

u\in L^{p}((0,\infty);L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

(4.36)

On the other hand, by applying Hölder’s inequality instead of Young’s inequality, we get

\int_{\Omega(T)}u_{1}(x)\psi^{\ell}_{1}(x)\,dx\leq C\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T^{d})}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\,C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Delta(T^{d})}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\,C\left(T^{-2}+T^{-1}\right)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{0}(x)|\,dx.

Letting
T\longrightarrow\infty
and taking into consideration (
4.36
) we get a contradiction.

II. Case of
\beta<-1
.

In this case, we choose
d=\frac{1-\beta}{2}
.

Note that, as
p\beta<\beta<-1
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}(1+t)^{-\frac{(\beta+1)\,p}{p-1}}\,dt\leq CT^{1-{(\beta+1)\,p}}.

(4.37)

Therfore, (
4.32
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T)}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega(T)}u_{1}(x)\psi^{\ell}_{1}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\;T^{-2p^{\prime}+1+nd}+C\;T^{-2dp^{\prime}+1+nd}

\displaystyle\,+\,C\;T^{-2dp^{\prime}+nd+1-(\beta+1)\,p^{\prime}}+\,C\left(T^{-2d}+T^{-1}\right)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{0}(x)|\,dx.

Now, we distinguish two subcases:

Subcritical case
p<\frac{n(1-\beta)+2}{n(1-\beta)-2}
:

As
\beta<-1
, we have

-2dp^{\prime}+1+nd<-(\beta+1)p^{\prime}-2dp^{\prime}+1+nd<0\quad\hbox{and}\quad-2p^{\prime}+1+nd<0,,

where we have used

p<\frac{n(1-\beta)+2}{n(1-\beta)-2},

for all
n\geq 1
. So, letting
T\longrightarrow\infty
, we get a contradiction.

Critical case
p=\frac{n(1-\beta)+2}{n(1-\beta)-2}
:

Let
n\geq 1
, and

p=\frac{n(1-\beta)+2}{n(1-\beta)-2}.

(4.38)

From the subcritical case, we can see easily that we have

u\in L^{p}((0,\infty);L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})).

(4.39)

On the other hand, by applying Hölder’s inequality instead of Young’s inequality, we get

\int_{\Omega(T)}u_{1}(x)\psi(0,x)\,dx\leq C\int_{\frac{T}{2}}^{T}\int_{\Omega(T^{d})}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\,C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Delta(T^{d})}|u|^{p}\psi\,dx\,dt+\,C\left(T^{-2}+T^{-1}\right)\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u_{0}(x)|\,dx.

Letting
T\longrightarrow\infty
and taking into consideration (
4.39
) we get a contradiction.
\hfill\blacksquare

Electron-Photon Exchange-Correlation Approximation for QEDFT
I-Te Lu, Michael Ruggenthaler, Nicolas Tancogne-Dejean, Simone Latini, Markus Penz, Angel Rubio

Abstract
Quantum-electrodynamical density-functional theory (QEDFT) provides a promising avenue for exploring complex light-matter interactions in optical cavities for real materials. Similar to conventional density-functional theory, the Kohn-Sham formulation of QEDFT needs approximations for the generally unknown exchange-correlation functional. In addition to the usual electron-electron exchange-correlation potential, an approximation for the electron-photon exchange-correlation potential is needed. A recent electron-photon exchange functional [ C. Schäfer et al., Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 118 , e2110464118 (2021) ], derived from the equation of motion of the non-relativistic Pauli-Fierz Hamiltonian, shows robust performance in one-dimensional systems across weak- and strong-coupling regimes. Yet, its performance in reproducing electron densities in higher dimensions remains unexplored. Here we consider this QEDFT functional approximation from one to three-dimensional finite systems and across weak to strong light-matter couplings. The electron-photon exchange approximation provides excellent results in the ultra-strong-coupling regime. However, to ensure accuracy also in the weak-coupling regime across higher dimensions, we introduce a computationally efficient renormalization factor for the electron-photon exchange functional, which accounts for part of the electron-photon correlation contribution. These findings extend the applicability of photon-exchange-based functionals to realistic cavity-matter systems, fostering the field of cavity QED (quantum electrodynamics) materials engineering.

To address this, we propose the inclusion of a renormalization factor in the electron-photon functional.
We focus on three finite one-electron systems coupled to the photon vacuum of a perfect cavity—a one-dimensional

Emphasizing the electron-photon interaction, our findings provide insights into the performance of the proposed

We note that the electron-photon functional is not limited to but can be beyond the long-wavelength approximation and that the extension to the time-dependent case for driven cavities will require developing functionals depending on the time-dependent current operator, which we will show in follow-up work.

II.3

The

IV
Conclusion and outlook

In summary, we have explored the reliability of
px
and
pxLDA
approximations, which are based on expressing the quantum fluctuations of the photons by the quantum-matter fluctuations, across various dimensional systems, coupling parameters, and frequency regimes.
While designed for ultra-strong coupling, these non-perturbative approximations can achieve accuracy in weak-coupling situations by incorporating renormalization factors that take electron-photon correlation contributions into account.
We demonstrated the efficiency of these methods in recovering both qualitative and quantitative exact results.
Moreover, we established connections between changes in optical spectra and modifications in the ground state of strongly coupled light-matter systems.
Accessing both static and time-dependent observables, such as changes in ground-state densities and optical spectra, within a unified theoretical framework opens up a lot of intriguing possibilities to explore in more detail the modifications of chemical and material equilibrium properties as uncovered by seminal experimental investigations in polaritonic chemistry and materials science
Ebbesen 2016
;
Sanvitto and Kéna-Cohen 2016
;
Frisk Kockum et al. 2019
;
Garcia-Vidal et al. 2021
;
Schlawin et al. 2022
;
Ebbesen et al. 2023
.
This underscores the potential of
ab initio
QED methods, particularly
QEDFT
, for future applications to the control of chemical and materials processes in cavities of tailored quantum environments.
Our study demonstrates that even simple approximate functionals yield qualitatively correct results and can be systematically improved, not only qualitatively but also quantitatively, through comparative analysis.

Here we concentrated on simple finite systems to thoroughly examine the reliability of various approximate functionals.
Unlike the often-employed unitarily-equivalent length-form of the
PF
Hamiltonian
Craig and Thirunamachandran 1998
;
Schäfer et al. 2020
;
Mandal et al. 2023
;
Ruggenthaler et al. 2023
, the presented approximations, grounded in the velocity gauge, offer direct applicability to extended systems, setting the stage for their application to solids.
The velocity gauge provides a direct connection to full minimal-coupling considerations, with a complete minimal-coupling form of the
px
potential already established in the literature
Schäfer et al. 2021
.
Exploring this approach for the full minimal-coupling
PF
Hamiltonian within the Maxwell-Pauli-Kohn-Sham framework
Jestädt et al. 2019
and considering functionals for chiral cavities
Hübener et al. 2021
;
Riso et al. 2023
represent promising future directions.

Beyond ground-state scenarios, the development of non-adiabatic (current-)density functionals is crucial.
Leveraging the local-force equation, similar to (electron-only) time-dependent
DFT
, offers a viable strategy
Tchenkoue et al. 2019
;
Tancogne-Dejean et al. 2024
.
Future research will also involve extensive benchmarking of existing approximate
QEDFT
functionals.
While benchmarks for standard electronic-structure methods are well-established, reliable reference results for polaritonic systems, especially in realistic contexts, are still in progress.
This challenge extends beyond single molecules and atoms to collectively-coupled systems
Feist et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2022
;
Sidler et al. 2022
;
Campos-Gonzalez-Angulo et al. 2023
;
Mandal et al. 2023
;
Ruggenthaler et al. 2023
, where the non-perturbative interplay between electronic and ro-vibrational degrees of freedom in large ensembles presents intriguing possibilities
Schlawin et al. 2022
;
Simpkins et al. 2023
;
Hirai et al. 2023
.
Exploring these interactions could unveil novel avenues for electronic-structure methods by bridging disparate energy and length scales.

APPENDIX A
Derivation for the Pauli-Fierz Hamiltonian with the dressed photon modes

After the expansion of the kinetic energy term in Eq. (
1
), the PF Hamiltonian can be written as the sum of 1) the Hamiltonian for the matter
\hat{H}_{\rm{M}}
, 2) the Hamiltonian for the photon system
\hat{H}_{\gamma}
, and 3) the interaction between the two systems
\frac{1}{c}\hat{\mathbf{A}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{\rm{p}}
with the paramagnetic current operator
\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{\rm{p}}=\sum_{l=1}^{N_{e}}(-i\nabla_{l})
:

\hat{H}_{\rm{PF}}=\hat{H}_{\rm{M}}+\frac{1}{c}\hat{\mathbf{A}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{\rm{p}}+\hat{H}_{\gamma},

where the Hamiltonian for the matter system is defined as

\hat{H}_{\rm{M}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{e}}\nabla_{l}^{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{N_{e}}v_{\rm{ext}}(\mathbf{r}_{l})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l\neq k}^{N_{e}}w(\mathbf{r}_{l},\mathbf{r}_{k}),

while that for the photon system is

\hat{H}_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\omega_{\alpha}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{a}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{N_{e}}{2c^{2}}\hat{\mathbf{A}}^{2},

where only in this appendix we overload the notation
\hat{H}_{\gamma}
for many modes, compared to the one in the main text.
Here we introduce a pair of harmonic coordinates for the bare photons:

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\alpha}}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}+\hat{a}_{\alpha}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{\alpha}=i\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}-\hat{a}_{\alpha}\right),

and rewrite
\hat{H}_{\gamma}
in terms of the harmonic coordinates as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\left(\hat{p}_{\alpha}^{2}+\hat{q}_{\alpha}\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=1}^{M_{p}}W_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\hat{q}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)

(22)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{\mathbf{P}}^{\intercal}\hat{\mathbf{P}}+\hat{\mathbf{Q}}^{\intercal}\mathbf{W}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}\right),

where we introduce a few notations to simplify the Hamiltonian:
\hat{\mathbf{P}}=(\hat{p}_{1},...,\hat{p}_{M_{p}})^{\intercal}
(
\intercal
means transpose),
\hat{\mathbf{Q}}=(\hat{q}_{1},...,\hat{q}_{M_{p}})^{\intercal}
, and
W_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}=\omega_{\alpha}^{2}\delta_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}+N_{e}\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha^{\prime}}\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
.
The matrix
\mathbf{W}
is real and symmetric, and can be diagonalized using an orthonormal matrix
\mathbf{U}
, such that
\mathbf{\tilde{\Omega}}=\mathbf{U}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{U^{T}}
with eigenvalues
\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}^{2}
, where
\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}
is the dressed frequency for the
\alpha
-th photon mode.
Next, we use a pair of transformed harmonic coordinates
\hat{\tilde{\mathbf{P}}}=\mathbf{U}\hat{\mathbf{P}}
and
\hat{\tilde{\mathbf{Q}}}=\mathbf{U}\hat{\mathbf{Q}}
, that is,
\hat{\tilde{p}}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{M_{p}}U_{\alpha\beta}\hat{p}_{\beta}
and
\hat{\tilde{q}}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{M_{p}}U_{\alpha\beta}\hat{q}_{\beta}
, respectively.
The Hamiltonian for the photon system
\hat{H}_{\gamma}
[Eq. (
22
)] becomes, with the help of the identity
\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{U}^{\intercal}\mathbf{U}
,

\hat{H}_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\left(\hat{\tilde{p}}_{\alpha}^{2}+\tilde{\omega}^{2}_{\alpha}\hat{\tilde{q}}_{\alpha}^{2}\right)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}\left(\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{\tilde{a}}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

where in the second equality we define the annihilation
\hat{\tilde{a}}_{\alpha}
and creation operator
\hat{\tilde{a}}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}
for the dressed photons as

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{a}}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}}}\left(\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}\hat{\tilde{q}}_{\alpha}+i\hat{\tilde{p}}_{\alpha}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\tilde{a}}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}}}\left(\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}\hat{\tilde{q}}_{\alpha}-i\hat{\tilde{p}}_{\alpha}\right).

Since we now use the dressed photons instead of the bare photons, the vector potential operator
\hat{\mathbf{A}}
needs to be rewritten in terms of the dressed photons as well:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{A}}

\displaystyle=c\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\lambda_{\alpha}\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\alpha}\hat{q}_{\alpha}=c\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\lambda_{\alpha}\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\alpha}\sum_{\beta=1}^{M_{p}}U_{\beta\alpha}\hat{\tilde{q}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=c\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\left[\sum_{\beta=1}^{M_{p}}U_{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\beta}\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\beta}\right]\hat{\tilde{q}}_{\alpha}=c\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\hat{\tilde{q}}_{\alpha}=\hat{\tilde{\mathbf{A}}},

where we define the coupling
\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}
and polarization
\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}
for each dressed photon mode using the relation:

\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{M_{p}}U_{\alpha\beta}\lambda_{\beta}\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\beta}.

(23)

The polarizations for each bare and each dressed photon mode,
\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}
, are normalized, i.e.,
|\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\alpha}|=|\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}|=1
.
Using this property, one can obtain the coupling
\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}
for the
\alpha
-th dressed photon mode.
If we assume that all the photon modes have the same coupling parameter, then the polarization for each dressed photon mode becomes
\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{M_{p}}U_{\alpha\beta}\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}_{\beta}
, which reproduces the results in Ref.
Faisal 1987
.
However, if the coupling parameters are different, then one should use Eq. (
23
) to get the correct polarization instead.
Note that the vector potential operator does not transform via the unitary matrix but expresses itself in terms of the dressed photon modes.
Therefore, the PF Hamiltonian [Eq. (
1
)] in terms of the dressed photon modes becomes Eq. (
2
).

APPENDIX B
Electron-photon exchange potential for one electron coupled to one photon mode

Here we focus on the
px

For many cavity-modes cases, we can add similar
px

\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}
and dressed photon frequency
\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}

v_{\rm{px}}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}v_{\rm{px},\alpha}(\mathbf{r})

\alpha

Assume that the wave function of the ground state
\psi_{0}(\mathbf{r})=\rho^{1/2}(\mathbf{r})

13

\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{\rm{p}}

\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\rm{p}}(\mathbf{r})
, and
N_{e}=1

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}v_{\rm{px}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\tilde{\lambda}}^{2}}{\tilde{\omega}^{2}}\nabla\cdot\left[\frac{(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}\cdot\nabla)\langle(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{\rm{p}})\hat{\mathbf{j}}_{\rm{p}}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\psi_{0}}}{\rho(\mathbf{r})}\right]

APPENDIX C
Electron-photon exchange energy within the local density approximation

Here we derive the
px

18
)] from two approaches, the reduced density matrix (RDM) and the virial relation.

C.1
Derivation from the reduced density matrix

We follow a similar strategy to derive the
px
energy within the local-density approximation as the pxLDA force derived in Ref.
Schäfer et al. 2021
.
First, we define the 1-particle and 2-particle RDM for the Slater-determinant
\Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}_{3},...,\mathbf{r}_{N_{e}})=\Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\underline{\mathbf{r}})=\Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\underline{\underline{\mathbf{r}}})
, where we use
\underline{\mathbf{r}}=(\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}_{3},...,\mathbf{r}_{N_{e}})
and
\underline{\underline{\mathbf{r}}}=(\mathbf{r}_{3},\mathbf{r}_{4},...,\mathbf{r}_{N_{e}})
.
The 1-particle RDM (1RDM) is defined as

\rho_{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1})=N_{e}\int d\underline{\mathbf{r}}\ \Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\underline{\mathbf{r}})\Phi^{*}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1},\underline{\mathbf{r}}),

while the 2-particle RDM (2RDM) is defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{(2)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2};\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{e}(N_{e}-1)}{2}\times\int d\underline{\underline{\mathbf{r}}}\ \Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\underline{\underline{\mathbf{r}}})\Phi^{*}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2},\underline{\underline{\mathbf{r}}}).

Using the above formula for the 1RDM and 2RDM (with the closed-shell assumption), we can write the expectation value of the current-current correlation in Eq. (
17
) as

\displaystyle\innerproduct{(\tilde{\pol}_{\alpha}\cdot\hbJp)\Phi}{(\tilde{\pol}_{\alpha}\cdot\hbJp)\Phi}=N_{e}\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}\int d\underline{\mathbf{r}}\ \left[(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{1})\Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\underline{\mathbf{r}})\right]^{*}\left[(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{1})\Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\underline{\mathbf{r}})\right]

\displaystyle+N_{e}(N_{e}-1)\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}\int d\mathbf{r}_{2}\int d\underline{\underline{\mathbf{r}}}\ \left[(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{2})\Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\underline{\underline{\mathbf{r}}})\right]^{*}\left[(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{1})\Phi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\underline{\underline{\mathbf{r}}})\right]

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{1^{\prime}})(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{1})\rho_{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1})\Big|_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1}=\mathbf{r}_{1}}+2\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}\int d\mathbf{r}_{2}(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{2^{\prime}})(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{1})\rho_{(2)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2};\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2})\Big|_{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1}=\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2}=\mathbf{r}_{2}}.

For closed-shell Slater-determinant states, which we assume here and below, the 2RDM can be written in terms of the 1RDM as

\displaystyle\rho_{(2)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2};\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\Big[\rho_{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1})\rho_{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2})\rho_{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1})\Big].

The 1RDM for the

HEG
, which we assume here, is given as

\rho_{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1})=\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}|<k_{\rm{F}}}d\mathbf{k}\ e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1})},

where
k_{\rm{F}}(\mathbf{r})=2\pi[\rho(\mathbf{r})/2V_{d}]^{1/d}
.
Within the
HEG

\nabla_{1}\rho_{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1})\Big|_{\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1}}=\frac{2i}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}|<k_{\rm{F}}}d\mathbf{k}\ \mathbf{k}=0.

Therefore, the expectation value within LDA becomes

\displaystyle\langle{(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{J}}_{\rm{p}})^{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}|<k_{\rm{F}}}d\mathbf{k}\ (\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{k})^{2}

\displaystyle-\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}|<{\rm{min}}(k_{\rm{F}},k^{\prime}_{\rm{F}})}d\mathbf{k}\ (\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{k})^{2}.

For the HEG,
k^{\prime}_{\rm{F}}=k_{\rm{F}}

For an inhomogeneous medium, min(

) approaches zero because

can possibly get small.
Therefore, we propose the following formula for the px energy within the LDA approximation:

E_{\rm{pxLDA}}=\int d\mathbf{r}\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\frac{-\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}^{2}}{2\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}^{2}}\right)\left[\frac{2\kappa}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}|<k_{\rm{F}}}d\mathbf{k}\ (\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{k})^{2}\right],

\kappa\in[0,1]

\kappa=1

\kappa=1

One can recover other scenarios by including the factor
\kappa

The square bracket in the formula for the pxLDA energy can be evaluated in the polar coordinates in
d

\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{|\mathbf{k}|<k_{\rm{F}}}d\mathbf{k}\ (\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{k})^{2}=\frac{2V_{d}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{k_{\rm{F}}^{d+2}(\mathbf{r})}{d+2}.

E_{\rm{pxLDA}}=\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\frac{-\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{2}}{\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}^{2}}\right)\frac{2\pi^{2}}{d+2}\left(\frac{1}{2V_{d}}\right)^{\frac{2}{d}}\int d\mathbf{r}\ \rho^{\frac{2+d}{d}}(\mathbf{r}).

(24)

Note that if we take the above isotropic pxLDA potential into the KS equations, we would lose the information of the polarization direction of those photon modes when solving the KS equations.
However, if we take the pxLDA potential obtained from the force balance equation to compute the density, then we compute the pxLDA energy using Eq. (
24

C.2
Derivation from the virial relation

The electron-photon exchange force (and its LDA version) is

\mathbf{F}_{\rm{px(LDA)}}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{2}}{\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}^{2}}(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{f}_{\alpha,\rm{px(LDA)}}(\mathbf{r}),

where
\mathbf{f}_{\alpha,\rm{pxLDA}}(\mathbf{r})
[Eq. (
15

\mathbf{f}_{\alpha,\rm{pxLDA}}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{d+2}\rho(\mathbf{r})\left[\frac{\rho(\mathbf{r})}{2V_{d}}\right]^{\frac{2}{d}}\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}.

The pxLDA energy can be obtained using the virial relation (with a factor of
1/2
due to the photon-energy counter term) as

\displaystyle E_{\rm{pxLDA}}=\frac{1}{2}\int d\mathbf{r}\ \mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{F}_{\rm{pxLDA}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{d+2}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{2}}{\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}^{2}}\int d\mathbf{r}\ (\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{r})(\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla)\left[\rho(\mathbf{r})\left(\frac{\rho(\mathbf{r})}{2V_{d}}\right)^{\frac{2}{d}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\frac{-\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{2}}{\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}^{2}}\right)\frac{2\pi^{2}}{d+2}\left(\frac{1}{2V_{d}}\right)^{\frac{2}{d}}\int d\mathbf{r}\ \rho^{\frac{2+d}{d}}(\mathbf{r}),

where we use integration by parts in the last line.
The px energy obtained from the virial relation is the same as the one obtained from the RDM approach.

Here is a side note regarding the total
px
force (and its LDA version): it satisfies the zero-force condition, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}d\mathbf{r}\ \mathbf{F}_{\rm{px}}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{M_{p}}\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{\alpha}^{2}}{\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}d\mathbf{r}\ (\tilde{\bm{\mathbf{{\varepsilon}}}}_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{f}_{\alpha,\rm{px}}(\mathbf{r})

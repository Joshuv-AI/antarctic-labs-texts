1.
Introduction

In
[
6
]
, E. Breuillard proved what can be considered as a non-abelian version of Lehmer’s problem about the Mahler measure of an algebraic number. He showed that if a set
F\subset\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
of invertible matrices with algebraic entries generates a group that is not virtually solvable, then its arithmetic height (a measure of its arithmetic complexity) is bounded below by an absolute constant
\epsilon_{d}>0
independent of
F
. Some consequences of Breuillard’s theorem include the existence of a lower bound for the exponential growth of a non-virtually solvable group of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, a strong version of the classical Tits alternative about existence of free subgroups in linear groups, lower bounds in the girth of finite groups of Lie type and very recently some results about the geometry of arithmetic locally symmetric spaces
[
11
]
. See
[
7
]
for a discussion of some of these applications.

In this article, we will provide a more elementary proof of Breuillard’s theorem that avoids the use of Bilu’s equidistribution theorem
[
5
]
, results of Zhang
[
15
]
about small points on algebraic tori, or results about the geometry of Bruhat–Tits buildings. The key idea in our proof relies on the existence of
word maps
in compact Lie groups whose image lies close to the identity element. These words are known as
almost laws
after Andreas Thom
[
13
]
; See Section
2.2
.

1.1.
Definitions

Let
K\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
be a number field and let
V_{K}
be the set of absolute values on
K
up to equivalence, which are either archimedean (corresponding to real and complex embeddings of
K
in
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
) or non-archimedean (corresponding to prime ideals in the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of
K
). Let
K_{v}
be the corresponding completion of
K
and define
n_{v}:=[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
, if
v|p
or
n_{v}:=[K_{v}:\mathbb{R}]
if
\mathbb{R}\subseteq K_{v}
. For a vector
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d})
in
K_{v}^{d}
, we define
\|x\|_{v}:=\max_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|_{v}
if
v
is non-archimedean and
\|x\|_{v}:=\sqrt{|x_{1}|_{v}^{2}+\dots+|x_{d}|_{v}^{2}}
if
\nu
is archimedean.

For a matrix
A
in
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(K)
, let
\|A\|_{v}
be the operator norm. The

of
A
is defined as

h(A):=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{v\in V_{K}}n_{v}\log^{+}\|A\|_{v},

where
\log^{+}(x)=\log\max(|x|,1)
for a real number
x
. For a finite set
F
of matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(K)
, the height of
F
is defined as

h(F):=\max_{A\in F}h(A),

and the
normalized height
is defined by

\widehat{h}(F):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(F^{n})}{n},

where
F^{n}
consists of all products
n
elements in
F
.
The limit above exists because
h(F^{n})
is sub-additive, which also implies
h(F)\geq\widehat{h}(F)
.

Our main result is a new proof of the following theorem due to Breuillard
[
6
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any positive integer

, there exists
\epsilon_{d}>0
such that for any symmetric finite set
F\subset\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, either

(1)

F
generates a virtually solvable group, or

(2)

\widehat{h}(F)>\epsilon_{d}
.

Remark 1.2
.

In section
4
we give a brief discussion on the possible estimates one can get of
\epsilon_{d}
by using our methods and ideas of E. Breuillard and A. Thom.

.

In
[

]
, Breuillard proved this theorem without the assumption that

is symmetric

1.2.
Idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1

We illustrate the idea of the proof in a very particular case. Assume
d=2
,
F\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and assume that all the entries of elements of
F
are algebraic integers. In this case, the only contribution to the height comes from archimedean places. Let
w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
be an almost law for
\mathrm{SU}(2)
(see Section
2.2
for the definition). Suppose for simplicity, there exists
A,B\in F
such that
\mathrm{Tr}(w(A,B)-Id)\neq 0
(see Lemma
3.1
), and let

(1)

Q=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-Id)),

where
k=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
,
A_{i}=\sigma_{i}(A)
and
B_{i}=\sigma_{i}(B)
, and
\sigma_{i}:K\to\mathbb{C}
are the different embeddings of
K
in
\mathbb{C}
.

By construction,
Q
is a nonzero integer and therefore
|Q|\geq 1
. Let
\delta>0
be small enough and let
S
be the set of
i
’s such that either
\|A_{i}\|
or
\|B_{i}\|
is greater than
e^{\delta}
. There are two cases: If
|S|\geq{k/2}
, then one of the heights
h(A)
or
h(B)
must be greater than
\frac{1}{2}\delta
and so
h(F)\geq\frac{1}{2}\delta
and we are done. If
S\leq k/2
we have for
i\notin S
,
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
are close to a pair of elements of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
and then for such
i

\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-Id)
is smaller than say
e^{-10}
by choosing the almost law appropriately and
\delta
small enough.

Therefore from
|Q|\geq 1
, we obtain

\left|\prod_{i\in S}\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-Id)\right|\geq\left|\prod_{i\notin S}\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-Id)\right|^{-1}\geq e^{10{k/2}}

and this implies that
the height of

must be bounded below by a constant

independent of

. Thus
h(F)\geq\frac{1}{|w|}h(F^{|w|})\geq\frac{1}{|w|}h(w(A,B))\geq\frac{c}{|w|}
, where

is the length of

.

2.1.
Heights

For a number field

and an absolute value

on

of

on

so that its restriction to

. With such normalization, we have the following product formula:

Theorem 2.1

(Product formula)
.

Let
K
be a number field and
V_{K}
be the set of equivalent classes of absolute values on
K
. Then, for every
x\in K
,

\prod_{v\in V_{K}}|x|_{v}^{n_{v}}=1.

Let
k
be a local field of characteristic
0
. Let
\|\cdot\|_{k}
be the standard norm on

as in the introduction. We use the same notation for the operator norm on the space
M_{d}(k)
of
d
by
d
matrices with entries in
k
. Following
[
6
]
, we define in below the quantities
\Lambda_{k}(F)
,
E_{k}(F)
and
R_{k}(F)
for a bounded set
F\subset M_{d}(k)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
F
be a bounded subset of matrices in

. We set

(1)

the

of

as

\|F\|_{k}=\sup_{g\in F}\|g\|_{k},

(2)

the

of

(3)

the

of

and

(4)

the

For simplicity, when

, we drop

in the notation and denote the above quantities using

With the above definition, we can reformulate the normalized height for a finite set

as follows (see

\widehat{h}(F)=\frac{1}{[k:Q]}\sum_{v\in V_{K}}n_{v}\log^{+}R_{v}(F)

Lemma 2.3

(
[

, Proposition 2.7]
)
.

\Lambda_{k}(F)\leq R_{k}(F)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
|\cdot|_{v}
be a non-archimedean place on a field

and equip

with the operator norm

induced by the norm

Let

2.2.
Almost laws

Given an element
w
in a free group
\mathbb{F}_{n}=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
of rank
n
, there is a natural
word map
associated to any group
G

w\colon\prod_{n}G\to G,

defined as follows:
Express
w
as a reduced word with alphabet
\{x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}\}
, and then for any
(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})\in\prod_{n}G
, substitute each
x_{i}
by
g_{i}
and
x_{i}^{-1}
by
g_{i}^{-1}
.
For example, if
w=[x_{1},x_{2}]=x_{1}x_{2}x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}^{-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then
w(A,B)=[A,B]
.

Definition 2.5
.

For a group
G
, a
law
is a nontrivial element
w\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
such that the image of
w_{G}
is the identity element
1_{G}
. Given
\epsilon>0
and a metric
d
on
G
, an
\epsilon
-almost law
is a nontrivial element
w\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
such that the image of
G
lies in an
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
1_{G}
.

For instance, the word
w=[x_{1},x_{2}]\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
is a law in a group
G
if and only if
G
is abelian. In general, groups obeying a law have rather special properties, for instance they contain no nonabelian free subgroups, and the stable commutator length vanishes
[
9
]
. Our proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the following result due to A. Thom, which has also been attributed to E. Lindestrauss; see
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(A. Thom
[
13
]
)
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group and
d_{G}
a bi-invariant metric in
G
. For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists an
\epsilon
-almost law
w_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
on
G
, that is, for all
A,B\in G
we have

d_{G}(w_{\epsilon}(A,B),1_{G})<\epsilon.

In
\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, an almost law on the compact subgroup
\mathrm{U}(d)
extends to a neighborhood. In below we denote the identity matrix as
I_{d}
, and let
d_{\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})}(X,Y)\vcentcolon=\|X-Y\|
, where
\|A\|
is the operator norm with respect to the Euclidean metric for any
A\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
. Note that the restriction of
d_{\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})}
to
\mathrm{U}(d)
is bi-invariant.

Corollary 2.7
.

For every
d>0
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a nontrivial element
w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
\delta=\delta(d,\epsilon)>0
such that for any
A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying
\|A\|,\|A^{-1}\|,\|B\|,\|B^{-1}\|<e^{\delta}
we have

d_{\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})}(w(A,B),I_{d})<\epsilon.

Moreover,
w
can be taken as any
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
-almost law on
\mathrm{U}(d)
, and
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{8|w_{\epsilon/2}|}
.

Proof.

Let
w=w_{\epsilon/2}
be an
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
-almost law as in Theorem
2.6
for
\mathrm{U}(d)
with respect to the restricted metric.
Then for any
A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, by the singular value decomposition we have
A=P_{A}\Lambda_{A}Q_{A}
and
B=P_{B}\Lambda_{B}Q_{B}
where
P_{A},Q_{A},P_{B},Q_{B}\in\mathrm{U}(d)
and
\Lambda_{A},\Lambda_{B}\in\Delta_{d}
.
For
\|A\|,\|A^{-1}\|,\|B\|,\|B^{-1}\|<e^{\delta}
, all diagonal entries of
\Lambda_{A},\Lambda_{B}
lie in
(e^{-\delta},e^{\delta})
.

Let
A^{\prime}=P_{A}Q_{A}
and
B^{\prime}=P_{B}Q_{B}
, which lie in
\mathrm{U}(d)
. Note that
\|A-A^{\prime}\|=\|\Lambda_{A}-I_{d}\|\leq e^{\delta}-1
and
\|A^{-1}-A^{\prime-1}\|=\|\Lambda_{A}^{-1}-1\|\leq e^{\delta}-1
, and similarly for
B
.
By inserting intermediate words replacing one
A^{\pm 1}
(resp.
B^{\pm 1}
) by
A^{\prime\pm 1}
(resp.
B^{\prime\pm 1}
) at a time in the word

, we obtain from the triangle inequality that

\|w_{\epsilon/2}(A,B)-w_{\epsilon/2}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\|\leq|w_{\epsilon/2}|\cdot e^{\delta(|w_{\epsilon/2}|-1)}(e^{\delta}-1).

For
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{8|w_{\epsilon/2}|}<1
, we have
e^{\delta(|w_{\epsilon/2}|-1)}<e^{\epsilon/8}<2
and
e^{\delta}-1<2\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{4|w_{\epsilon/2}|}
.
Hence

\|w_{\epsilon/2}(A,B)-w_{\epsilon/2}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\|\leq|w_{\epsilon/2}|e^{\delta(|w_{\epsilon/2}|-1)}(e^{\delta}-1)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4}\cdot e^{\frac{\epsilon}{8}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2},

and

\|w_{\epsilon/2}(A,B)-I_{d}\|\leq\|w_{\epsilon/2}(A,B)-w_{\epsilon/2}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\|+\|w_{\epsilon/2}(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})-I_{d}\|\leq\epsilon

using the almost law.
∎

Proof of Theorem

We need to prove the uniform lower bound
\widehat{h}(F)\geq\epsilon_{d}
whenever

To make use of this assumption on

Lemma 3.1
.

Given any non-trivial word

(only depending on

) such that for any set

Let

\mathrm{Tr}(w(X,Y)-I_{d})\neq 0

As

Let

Assume that

. We claim that

is trivial. Indeed, by Borel’s theorem

. As

as we desired.
∎

.
Let

\epsilon_{2}=\frac{\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{\log\frac{2d}{\epsilon}+\frac{|w|\delta^{2}}{16d\log\frac{1}{c}}},\quad\text{and}\quad\epsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\epsilon_{2}\log\frac{2d}{\epsilon}\right),

where
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{8|w|}
as in Corollary
2.7
and
0<c<1
is the constant (only depending on
d
) from Proposition
3.5
below, which can be chosen as
c=\frac{1}{2d}
.
Note that
\epsilon_{2}<\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}/\log\frac{2d}{\epsilon}<1
and thus
\epsilon_{1}>0
.
Let

(3)

\epsilon_{d}\vcentcolon=\frac{\epsilon_{2}\delta^{2}}{32nd\log\frac{1}{c}}=\frac{\delta^{2}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{32nd\log\frac{2d}{\epsilon}\log\frac{1}{c}+2n|w|\delta^{2}},

which is at least at the order of
\frac{1}{n(d^{2}|w|^{2}+|w|)}
as
d\to\infty
by setting
\epsilon\in(0,1)
independent of
d
.
The constants
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
are chosen so that

\epsilon_{d}=\frac{\epsilon_{2}\delta^{2}}{32nd\log\frac{1}{c}}=\frac{-\epsilon_{1}+(1-\epsilon_{2})\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\epsilon_{2}\log 2d}{n|w|}=\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{n|w|},

which are the lower bounds of the normalized height in the analysis below.

We consider the two possibilities in the following two subsections.

3.1.
Case 1:
|Q|\geq e^{-\epsilon_{1}k}

In this case, we will use the estimate

(4)

\widehat{h}(F)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{v\in V_{K}}n_{v}\log^{+}R_{v}(F)\geq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log^{+}R_{i}(F),

where
R_{i}(F)=R(\sigma_{i}(F))
and we simply ignore the non-archimedean places.

Partition the index set
I\vcentcolon=\{1,2\cdots,k\}
as
I=I_{S}\sqcup I_{M}\sqcup I_{L}
, where

I_{S}\vcentcolon=\{i:|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-I_{d})|<\epsilon\},\quad I_{L}\vcentcolon=\{i:|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-I_{d})|>2d\},

and
I_{M}=I\setminus(I_{S}\sqcup I_{L})
. Note that
I_{S}\cap I_{L}=\emptyset
since
\epsilon<1
.

We further consider two subcases depending on the size of
I_{S}
.

3.1.1.
Case 1a:
|I_{S}|>(1-\epsilon_{2})k

In this case, we have

\prod_{i\notin I_{S}}|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-I_{d})|=\frac{|Q|}{\left|\prod_{i\in I_{S}}\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-I_{d})\right|}\geq e^{-\epsilon_{1}k}\cdot\epsilon^{-|I_{S}|}.

Taking
\log
on both sides, we have

\sum_{i\notin I_{S}}\log|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-I_{d})|\geq-\epsilon_{1}k+|I_{S}|\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\geq-\epsilon_{1}k+(1-\epsilon_{2})k\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}.

As
|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-I_{d})|\leq 2d
for all
i\in I_{M}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{L}}\log|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-I_{d})|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\notin I_{S}}\log|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i})-I_{d})|-|I_{M}|\log 2d

(5)

\displaystyle\geq-\epsilon_{1}k+(1-\epsilon_{2})k\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}-|I_{M}|\log 2d.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
X\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, if
|\mathrm{Tr}(X-I_{d})|>d
, then the spectral radius
\Lambda(X)\geq\frac{1}{d}|\mathrm{Tr}(X-I_{d})|-1
.
Moreover, if
|\mathrm{Tr}(X-I_{d})|>2d
, then
\Lambda(X)\geq\frac{1}{2d}|\mathrm{Tr}(X-I_{d})|
.

Proof.

Let
r=\frac{1}{d}|\mathrm{Tr}(X-I_{d})|
and let
\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{d}\in\mathbb{C}
be the eigenvalues of
X
.
Then for the average
\bar{\lambda}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}
we have
r=|\bar{\lambda}-1|
.

It follows that some
\lambda_{i}
lies outside the open disk
D
of radius
r
around
1\in\mathbb{C}
, since otherwise their average
\bar{\lambda}
lies in
D
by convexity, contradicting
r=|\bar{\lambda}-1|
.

Since
r>1
by our assumption, the disk
D
contains the open disk
B
of radius
r-1
around
0\in\mathbb{C}
.
Thus
\lambda_{i}\notin B
and
\Lambda(X)\geq|\lambda_{i}|\geq r-1
.

In the case
r>2
, we further have
\Lambda(X)\geq r-1\geq r-\frac{r}{2}=\frac{r}{2}
.
∎

Applying Lemma
3.3
to the left-hand side of Equation (
3.1.1
) we obtain

\sum_{i\in I_{L}}\log(2d\Lambda(w(A_{i},B_{i})))\geq-\epsilon_{1}k+(1-\epsilon_{2})k\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}-|I_{M}|\log 2d,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{L}}\log(\Lambda(w(A_{i},B_{i})))

\displaystyle\geq-\epsilon_{1}k+(1-\epsilon_{2})k\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}-(|I_{M}|+|I_{L}|)\log 2d

\displaystyle>-\epsilon_{1}k+(1-\epsilon_{2})k\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\epsilon_{2}k\log 2d

since
|I_{M}|+|I_{L}|=|I|-|I_{S}|<\epsilon_{2}k
.

Combining this with Equation (
4
) and Lemma
2.3
, as
w(A,B)\in F^{n|w|}
, we conclude

\displaystyle n|w|\cdot\widehat{h}(F)=\widehat{h}(F^{n|w|})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in I_{L}}\log(R_{i}(w(A,B)))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\in I_{L}}\log(\Lambda(w(A_{i},B_{i})))

\displaystyle\geq-\epsilon_{1}+(1-\epsilon_{2})\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\epsilon_{2}\log 2d

\displaystyle=n|w|\epsilon_{d}.

Thus
\widehat{h}(F)\geq\epsilon_{d}
as desired in this situation.

3.1.2.
Case 1b:
|I_{S}|\leq(1-\epsilon_{2})k

Recall that for any
i\notin I_{S}
, we have
|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A_{i},B_{i}))-I_{d}|>\epsilon
.
Since trace is conjugate-invariant, we know that
|\mathrm{Tr}(w(CA_{i}C^{-1},CB_{i}C^{-1}))-I_{d}|>\epsilon
for all
C\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
.
Thus by Corollary
2.7
, for any
i\notin I_{S}
and any
C\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, at least one of the four matrices
CA_{i}^{\pm 1}C^{-1},CB_{i}^{\pm 1}C^{-1}
has norm no less than
e^{\delta}
. Thus we have
E(T_{i})\geq e^{\delta}
for
T_{i}\vcentcolon=\{A_{i}^{\pm 1},B_{i}^{\pm 1}\}
.

In particular, this implies that for one element among
\{A^{\pm 1},B^{\pm 1}\}
, let’s say
A
, we have
\|A_{i}\|\geq e^{\delta}
for
\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{2}k
different
i
’s and that implies the lower bound
h(F^{n_{w}})\geq h(A)\geq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{2}\delta
, which implies

h(F)\geq\frac{\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{2}\delta}{n_{w}}.

As we want to obtain a bound for the normalized height and not only for the height, we need a way to compare them, to do this we will make use of the following two propositions, which are due to Breuillard. Recall that our
A,B
lie in
\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
6
, Proposition 2.9]
)
.

For any
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and a finite subset
F\subset\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, we have

E(F^{M})\geq E(F)^{\sqrt{\frac{M}{4d}}}

Proposition 3.5

(
[
6
, Lemma 2.1(b)]
)
.

There are uniform constants
c=c(d)
and
N(d)
such that for any finite subset
F\subset\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
there is
q\in[1,N(d)]
with

\Lambda(F^{qM})\geq c^{q}E(F^{M})^{q},

The latter proposition can be deduced from an inequality of Bochi
[
3
, Thereom B]
, which was also recently proved by Breuillard in
[
8
]
with a good choice of
c=c(d)
(at the cost of increasing
N(d)
); See
[
8
, Theorem 5]
. The inequality of Breuillard together with
[
8
, Lemma 1]
implies that one can take
c=\frac{1}{2d}
.

These propositions imply that for every
i\notin I_{S}
, there is some
q
with

\Lambda(T_{i}^{qM})\geq c^{q}E(T_{i}^{M})^{q}\geq c^{q}E(T_{i})^{q\sqrt{\frac{M}{4d}}}\geq c^{q}e^{q\delta\sqrt{\frac{M}{4d}}}.

Note that for
T\vcentcolon=\{A^{\pm 1},B^{\pm 1}\}
, we have
T\subset F^{n}
and thus
T_{i}^{qM}\subset(\sigma_{i}F)^{nqM}
.
Therefore, using the estimate above, we obtain

R_{i}(F)^{nqM}=R_{i}(F^{nqM})=R((\sigma_{i}F)^{nqM})\geq R(T_{i}^{qM})\geq\Lambda(T_{i}^{qM})\geq c^{q}e^{q\delta\sqrt{\frac{M}{4d}}}.

That is,

\log R_{i}(F)\geq\frac{1}{nM}\left(\log c+\delta\sqrt{\frac{M}{4d}}\right).

Here
\log(c)<0
, so the right hand side is maximized to
\frac{\delta^{2}}{16nd\log(1/c)}
when
M=16d\log^{2}c/\delta^{2}
.
As
M
is an integer, we should take
M=\left\lfloor 16d\log^{2}c/\delta^{2}\right\rfloor
or
\left\lceil 16d\log^{2}c/\delta^{2}\right\rceil
.
Since
16d\log^{2}c/\delta^{2}
is sufficiently large, in either case we get

\log R_{i}(F)\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{32nd\log\frac{1}{c}}.

Hence by Equation (
4
), we have

\widehat{h}(F)\geq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i\notin I_{S}}\log^{+}R_{i}(F)\geq\frac{\epsilon_{2}\delta^{2}}{32nd\log\frac{1}{c}}=\epsilon_{d}.

3.2.
Case 2:
|Q|<e^{-\epsilon_{1}k}

In this case, for
\alpha\vcentcolon=\mathrm{Tr}(w(A,B)-I_{d})
, we can apply the product formula Theorem
2.1
so that

\prod_{v\in V_{K}^{f}}|\alpha|^{n_{v}}_{v}=\frac{1}{|Q|}>e^{\epsilon_{1}k},

where
V_{K}^{f}
denotes the non-archimedean places of
K
.

Since
|\cdot|_{v}
is an ultrametric for all
v\in V_{K}^{f}
, by Lemma
2.4
we have

|\alpha|_{v}\leq\max\{|\mathrm{Tr}(w(A,B))|_{v},|\mathrm{Tr}(I_{d})|_{v}\}\leq\max\{R_{v}(w(A,B)),1\}.

Hence for all
v\in V_{K}^{f}
we have

\log|\alpha|_{v}\leq\log^{+}R_{v}(w(A,B))

Therefore, this inequality and the product formula together imply

\displaystyle\widehat{h}(F)=\frac{1}{n|w|}\widehat{h}(F^{n|w|})

\displaystyle\geq\widehat{h}(w(A,B))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n|w|k}\sum_{v\in V_{K}^{f}}\log^{+}R_{v}(w(A,B))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n|w|k}\sum_{v\in V_{K}^{f}}\log|\alpha|_{v}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n|w|k}(\epsilon_{1}k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{n|w|}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{d}.

4.
Remarks about explicit estimates of the height gap
\epsilon_{d}

One can construct examples showing that the height gap
\epsilon_{d}\leq\frac{c}{d}
for some
c>0
independent of
d
. A natural question is to determined the actual order of
\epsilon_{d}
and Breuillard has asked the following:

Question 4.1
.

Is
\epsilon_{d}\geq\frac{1}{cd^{c}}
for some
c>0
?

We will explain how our method might lead to obtain some explicit bounds for this constant.

From equation (
3
), we have that

\epsilon_{d}\geq\frac{\delta^{2}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}}{32n_{w}d\log\frac{2d}{\epsilon}\log\frac{1}{c}+2n_{w}|w|\delta^{2}},

where
c
is the constant appearing in Proposition
3.5
and
\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{8|w|}
. Recently Breuillard proved that
c
can be taken to
\frac{1}{2d}
by
[
8
, Theorem 5 and Lemma 1]
. Therefore by taking
\epsilon=1/4
, we see that
\epsilon_{d}
is at least at the order of

\frac{1}{n_{w}(d^{2}|w|^{2}+|w|)},

where
|w|
is the word length of a
\frac{1}{4}
-almost law
w
on
\mathrm{U}(d)
and the constant
n_{w}
comes from the escape of the hypersurface
V
defined by

\mathrm{Tr}(w(X,Y)-\text{Id})=0

as in Lemma
3.2
.

One can obtain a bound of the order of
10^{10^{d}}
for
|w|
by considering the almost laws described by Thom in
[
13
]
. It is nonetheless quite likely that a much shorter almost law
w
exists. Thom and Breuillard pointed out to us the fact that Kozma and Thom showed in
[
12
]
that for the symmetric group
S_{d}
(which in some ways behave similarly to
\mathrm{SU}(d)
when
d
is large), there exists a law of order
e^{C\log^{4}(d)\log\log(d)}
, which assuming Babai’s conjecture could be improved to be of the order
e^{C\log(d)\log\log(d)}
, which is quite close to be polynomial.

The constant
n_{w}
when computed from the generalized Bezout’s Theorem as in
[
10
]
seems to be quite large. It was suggested by Breuillard that there seems to be another way of estimating
n_{w}
by considering an appropriate finite quotient of the group generated by
F
(for example by moding out a prime ideal of the ring where
F
is defined ), and then showing that in this finite quotient one can escape from the hypersurface
V
quickly. The advantage is that there are various results about the diameter of simple groups. Michael Larsen suggested the use of a strong approximation theorem by Weisfeiler
[
14
]
to find the appropriate quotient.

To implement this idea, one must make sure the reduction of
V
to this finite group of Lie type is a proper subset. The dimension of the variety
V
is bounded by
d|w|
and so the reduction of
V
can be proved to be a proper subset via the Schwartz–Zippel lemma, or via the Lang–Weil estimates, provided that the finite quotient is large enough (the prime ideal giving rise to the finite quotient must have covolume larger than the degree of
V
, at least).

Assuming Babai’s conjecture about the diameter of finite groups one then might expect that
n_{w}
could be taken of the order
(|d\log(|w|))^{O(1)}
and using results towards Babai conjecture this can possibly be taken to be of the order
e^{(|d\log(|w|))^{O(1)}}
, see
[
2
]
.

Signs of the Second Coefficients of Hecke Polynomials
Erick Ross, Hui Xue

Abstract
. Let T m ​ ( N , k , χ ) T_{m}(N,k,\chi) be the m m -th Hecke operator of level N N , weight k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , and nebentypus χ \chi , where N N is coprime to m m . We first show that for any given m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 , the second coefficient of the characteristic polynomial of T m ​ ( N , k , χ ) T_{m}(N,k,\chi) is nonvanishing for all but finitely many triples ( N , k , χ ) (N,k,\chi) . Furthermore, for χ \chi trivial and any fixed m m , we determine the sign of the second coefficient for all but finitely many pairs ( N , k ) (N,k) . Finally, for χ \chi trivial and m = 3 , 4 m=3,4 , we compute the sign of the second coefficient for all pairs ( N , k ) (N,k) .

Let

and

Several interesting questions about these Hecke operators

[
11

or

Now, let us write the characteristic polynomial for

To ease notation we will simply write

Using this notation, the Generalized Lehmer Conjecture concerns the nonvanishing of the first coefficient

of

2.
Second coefficients in terms of traces

Following
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
, we first derive a formula for
a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)
in terms of traces of Hecke operators.

Lemma 2.1
.

For convenience of notation, let
T_{m}
denote
T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
. Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\Tr T_{m}\right)^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}\chi(d)d^{k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}\right].

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1}\ldots\lambda_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
T_{m}
. By the definition of characteristic polynomial, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\lambda_{i}\right)^{2}-\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\lambda_{i}^{2}\right]

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m})^{2}-\Tr T_{m}^{2}\right].

On the other hand, recall the following formula
[
3
, Theorem 10.2.9]
for Hecke operators:

T_{m}^{2}=\sum_{d\mid m}\chi(d)d^{k-1}T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}.

Thus,

(2.5)

\displaystyle a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m})^{2}-\Tr T_{m}^{2}\right]

(2.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m})^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}\chi(d)d^{k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}\right],

as desired.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Recall that
\psi(N)=N\prod_{p|N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)
, and let
\omega(N)
denote the number of distinct prime divisors of
N
. Define

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(N):=\frac{2^{\omega(N)}\sqrt{N}}{\psi(N)},\qquad\theta_{2}(N):=\frac{(2^{\omega(N)})^{2}}{\psi(N)},\qquad\theta_{3}(N):=\frac{2^{\omega(N)}}{\psi(N)}.

Then each
\theta_{i}(N)\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

In particular, we have the bounds given in the following table.

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr\displaystyle N\geq&1&43&571&8,\!800&150,\!000&2,\!700,\!000&63,\!000,\!000\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle\theta_{1}(N)\leq&1.00&0.465&0.257&0.133&0.0607&0.0265&0.0106\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle\theta_{2}(N)\leq&1.34&0.445&0.149&0.0424&0.00941&0.00189&0.000314\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle\theta_{3}(N)\leq&1.00&0.0556&0.00926&0.00133&0.000147&0.000015&0.000015\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Proof.

Note that every prime other than
2,3,5,7
is
\geq 8
. Thus
\omega(N)\leq 4+\log_{8}(N)
and so
2^{\omega(N)}\leq 2^{4+\log_{8}(N)}\leq 16\cdot N^{1/3}
. Since
\psi(N)\geq N
, it is clear that

\theta_{1}(N)=\frac{2^{\omega(N)}\sqrt{N}}{\psi(N)},\ \ \ \theta_{2}(N)=\frac{(2^{\omega(N)})^{2}}{\psi(N)},\ \ \ \theta_{3}(N)=\frac{2^{\omega(N)}}{\psi(N)}\ \ \longrightarrow 0\ \text{ as }\ N\longrightarrow\infty.

To prove the specific numerical bounds given above, we will first show that

(3.1)

\theta_{1}(N)\leq 0.0106,\ \theta_{2}(N)\leq 0.000314,\ \theta_{3}(N)\leq 0.000015

for all
N\geq 584,\!000,\!000
. Then we will verify each of the claimed bounds in the table by exhaustive computer check over all
N<584,\!000,\!000
.

Let
p_{n}
denote the
n
-th prime number, and let
P_{n}:=p_{1}\cdots p_{n}
.
For all
N
with
\omega(N)\geq 9
, we show that
\theta_{i}(N)\leq\theta_{i}(P_{9})
.
For such
N
, let
N=q_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots q_{m}^{e_{m}}
be its prime factorization. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(N)}{\sqrt{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q_{1}+1)q_{1}^{e_{1}-1}\cdots(q_{m}+1)q_{m}^{e_{m}-1}}{q_{1}^{e_{1}/2}\cdots q_{m}^{e_{m}/2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(q_{1}+1)\cdots(q_{m}+1)}{q_{1}^{1/2}\cdots q_{m}^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(p_{1}+1)\cdots(p_{m}+1)}{p_{1}^{1/2}\cdots p_{m}^{1/2}}\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(\text{since $\frac{x+1}{x^{1/2}}$ is increasing for $x\geq 1$}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi(P_{9})}{\sqrt{P_{9}}}\frac{(p_{10}+1)\cdots(p_{m}+1)}{p_{10}^{1/2}\cdots p_{m}^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\psi(P_{9})}{\sqrt{P_{9}}}2^{m-9}.

This means that

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(N)=\frac{2^{m}\sqrt{N}}{\psi(N)}\leq\frac{2^{m}\sqrt{P_{9}}}{2^{m-9}\psi(P_{9})}=\theta_{1}(P_{9})\leq 0.0106
.

By an identical argument,

\displaystyle\psi(N)\geq 4^{m-9}\psi(P_{9})
,
which means that

\displaystyle\theta_{2}(N)\leq\theta_{2}(P_{9})\leq 0.000314

and

\displaystyle\theta_{3}(N)\leq\theta_{3}(P_{9})\leq 0.000015
.
This verifies the three bounds in (
3.1
) for all
N
with
\omega(N)\geq 9
.

For
N
with
\omega(N)\leq 8
and
N\geq 584,\!000,\!000
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\omega(N)}\sqrt{N}}{\psi(N)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{8}\sqrt{N}}{N}

\displaystyle\leq 0.00915,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\theta_{2}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2^{\omega(N)})^{2}}{\psi(N)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{16}}{N}

\displaystyle\leq 0.000314,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\theta_{3}(N)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\omega(N)}}{\psi(N)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{8}}{N}

\displaystyle\leq 0.000015.

This verifies the three bounds in (
3.1
) for all
N
with
N\geq 584,\!000,\!000
.

Then via exhaustive computer check over all

Next, we bound the inner summation for
A_{2,m}
in (
2.9

Lemma 3.2
.

For
t,n,m
given in (
2.9
),

\left\lvert U_{k-1}(t,m)\cdot\mu(t,n,m)\right\lvert\leq 2\psi(n)2^{\omega(N)}m^{(k-1)/2}.

Proof.

Recall that
m\geq 1
,
t\in\ZZ
such that
4m-t^{2}>0
, and
n\geq 1
such that
n^{2}\,|\,(t^{2}-4m)
and
\frac{t^{2}-4m}{n^{2}}\equiv 0,1\,\pmod{4}
. And recall that
U_{k-1}(t,m)=\frac{\rho^{k-1}-\bar{\rho}^{k-1}}{\rho-\bar{\rho}}
where
\rho,\bar{\rho}
are the two roots of the polynomial
X^{2}-tX+m
. Finally, recall that
\displaystyle\mu(t,n,m)=\frac{\psi(N)}{\psi(N/N_{n})}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{c\!\!\mod N}\chi(c)
, where
N_{n}=\gcd(N,n)
, and the primed summation runs through all elements
c
of
(\ZZ/N\ZZ)^{\times}
which lift to solutions of
c^{2}-tc+m\equiv 0\,\pmod{NN_{n}}
.

So,

(3.5)

\left\lvert U_{k-1}(t,m)\cdot\mu(t,n,m)\right\lvert=\left\lvert\frac{\rho^{k-1}-\bar{\rho}^{k-1}}{\rho-\bar{\rho}}\right\lvert\cdot\frac{\psi(N)}{\psi(N/N_{n})}\cdot\left\lvert\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{c\!\!\!\mod N}\chi(c)\right\lvert.

We give bounds on each of these three factors.

First, since

\left\lvert\rho\right\lvert=\sqrt{m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left\lvert\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\lvert=\sqrt{4m-t^{2}},

we have

(3.6)

\left\lvert\frac{\rho^{k-1}-\bar{\rho}^{k-1}}{\rho-\bar{\rho}}\right\lvert\leq\frac{\left\lvert\rho^{k-1}\right\lvert+\left\lvert\bar{\rho}^{k-1}\right\lvert}{\left\lvert\rho-\bar{\rho}\right\lvert}=\frac{2m^{(k-1)/2}}{\sqrt{4m-t^{2}}}.

Second, note that for every prime
p\mid N
, we will either have
p\mid N_{n}
or
p\mid N/N_{n}
. Thus

(3.7)

\displaystyle\psi(N)

\displaystyle=N\prod_{p|N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq N_{n}\prod_{p|N_{n}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\cdot N/N_{n}\prod_{p|N/N_{n}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\psi(N_{n})\psi(N/N_{n}),

which yields

(3.10)

\frac{\psi(N)}{\psi(N/N_{n})}\leq\psi(N_{n})\leq\psi(n),

where the second inequality comes from the fact that
N_{n}\mid n
.

Third, note for every term
c
in the sum of (
3.5
),
c^{2}-tc+m\equiv 0\pmod{NN_{n}}
means that
c^{2}-tc+m\equiv 0\pmod{N}
as well. By
[
12
, Lemma 2]
, the congruence
x^{2}-tx+m\equiv 0\pmod{N}
has at most
2^{\omega(N)}\sqrt{4m-t^{2}}
solutions. Thus

(3.11)

\left\lvert\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{c\!\!\!\mod N}\chi(c)\right\lvert\leq 2^{\omega(N)}\sqrt{4m-t^{2}}.

Combining the bounds (
3.6
), (
3.10
), and (
3.11
), we obtain

\left\lvert U_{k-1}(t,m)\cdot\mu(t,n,m)\right\lvert\leq 2\psi(n)2^{\omega(N)}m^{(k-1)/2},

which completes the proof.
∎

\sum_{\tau}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\Sigma(N,m,d)\right\lvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid N\\

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid N\\

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid N\\
(\tau,N/\tau)\mid h\end{subarray}}\phi(\gcd(\tau,N/\tau))

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\mid h}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\mid N\\
(\tau,N/\tau)=\delta\end{subarray}}\phi(\delta)

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\mid h}\phi(\delta)\cdot\#\{\tau\mid N\ \colon\ \gcd(\tau,N/\tau)=\delta\}.

In the case of
d\neq\sqrt{m}
, we have
h\neq 0
, and so using Lemma
3.4
below, we have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\Sigma(N,m,d)\right\lvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\delta\mid h}\phi(\delta)\cdot\#\{\tau|N\ \colon\ \gcd(\tau,N/\tau)=\delta\}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\delta\mid h}\phi(\delta)\cdot 2^{\omega(N)}

(3.19)

\displaystyle=h\cdot 2^{\omega(N)}.

Here, we used the well-known formula

\sum_{\delta\mid h}\phi(\delta)=h
.

In the general case, write
N=DM^{2}
where
D
is squarefree. Then for any
\delta
such that
\gcd(\tau,N/\tau)=\delta
for some
\tau
, note
\delta\mid\tau
and
\delta\mid N/\tau
imply
\delta^{2}\mid N
, which means that
\delta\mid M
.
Hence by Lemma
3.4
again,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\Sigma(N,m,d)\right\lvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\delta\mid h}\phi(\delta)\cdot\#\{\tau\mid N\ \colon\ (\tau,N/\tau)=\delta\}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\delta\mid M}\phi(\delta)\cdot\#\{\tau\mid N\ \colon\ (\tau,N/\tau)=\delta\}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\delta\mid M}\phi(\delta)\cdot 2^{\omega(N)}

(3.23)

\displaystyle=M\cdot 2^{\omega(N)}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{N}\cdot 2^{\omega(N)},

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
N
and
\delta
be positive integers. Then

\#\{\tau\mid N\ \colon\ \gcd(\tau,N/\tau)=\delta\}\leq 2^{\omega(N)}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\delta\mid N
(otherwise, the inequality holds trivially).
Consider the possible
\tau
that would yield
\gcd(\tau,N/\tau)=\delta
.
For each prime
p\mid N
,
let
v_{p}(\cdot)
denote
p
-adic valuation, and let
r_{p}:=v_{p}(\delta)
. Note that
\gcd(\tau,N/\tau)=\delta
precisely for
\tau
such that
v_{p}(\gcd(\tau,N/\tau))=r_{p}
for each
p\mid N
. On the other hand,
v_{p}(\gcd(\tau,N/\tau))=r_{p}
means that either
v_{p}(\tau)=r_{p}
and
v_{p}(N/\tau)\geq r_{p}
, or
v_{p}(N/\tau)=r_{p}
and
v_{p}(\tau)\geq r_{p}
. This yields only two possible values for
v_{p}(\tau)
:
r_{p}
and
v_{p}(N)-r_{p}
. Thus, since there are at most two possible options for
v_{p}(\tau)
for each prime
p\mid N
, we have that
\#\{\tau\mid N\ \colon\ \gcd(\tau,N/\tau)=\delta\}\leq 2^{\omega(N)}
.
∎

Recall what big

In other words, for any fixed value of

notation with respect to

, and for any fixed value of

, this can be interpreted as big

notation with respect to

Lemma 4.1

Next, we prove Theorems

and
1.2
in the case when

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
m\geq 1
be fixed and not a perfect square. Suppose that

, and
\chi

By Lemma

\displaystyle a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\Tr T_{m})^{2}-\sum_{d\mid m}\chi(d)d^{k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}\right].

\displaystyle~~\chi(d)d^{k-1}\Tr T_{m^{2}/d^{2}}

\displaystyle a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)

\displaystyle a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)

(N,k,\chi)

Discussion

We first make some observations about the proofs of Propositions
4.3
and
4.4
. One of the last steps in these two proofs was to show that for any given
(m,N,\chi)
, the
a_{2}
coefficient is nonvanishing for sufficiently large
k
. It is worth noting that is also what one would expect from the well-known Skolem-Mahler-Lech theorem
[
4
, Section 2.1]
. For any fixed
(m,N,\chi)
,
\Tr T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
satisfies a linear recurrence in
k
; this fact follows from the Eichler-Selberg trace formula (
2.7
). So by Lemma
2.1
,
a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)
also satisfies a linear recurrence in
k
. Thus by Skolem-Mahler-Lech, one would expect that for any given
(m,N,\chi)
,
a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)
only vanishes for finitely many
k
.

Now, one might ask if the same result holds including
m
as well, i.e. if
a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)
only vanishes for finitely many
(m,N,k,\chi)
. It turns out this is not true for a trivial reason. When
\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)<2
, the characteristic polynomial for
T_{m}(N,k,\chi)
will have degree less than
2
. Thus the second coefficient will neccessarily be
0
for all
m
in this case.

So one might instead ask if
a_{2}(m,N,k,\chi)
vanishes only for finitely many
(m,N,k,\chi)
when we restrict to the case of
\dim S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\geq 2
. It turns out this is not true either. For example,
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(23))
has dimension
2
. And the two Hecke eigenforms for
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(23))
both happen to have
43
rd Fourier coefficient vanish
[
7
]
. Thus for any
m
with exactly one factor of
43
, these two eigenforms will both have
m
-th Fourier coefficient vanish. So for such
m
,
T_{m}(23,2)
will have two eigenvalues of
0
, and hence will have characteristic polynomial
x^{2}
. This gives an infinite family of vanishing
a_{2}
coefficients.

One could also ask if the result still holds if we allow
N
not coprime to
m
. In fact, this appears to not be true either. For example, it appears that for
j\geq 2
, all the eigenvalues of
T_{3}(3^{j},4)
vanish. This would give an infinite family of
N
for which
a_{2}(3,N,4)
vanishes. One can find many other similar examples.

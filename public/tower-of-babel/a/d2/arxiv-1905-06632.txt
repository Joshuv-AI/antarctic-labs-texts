The direct image of generalized divisors and the Norm map between compactified Jacobians
Raffaele Carbone

Abstract
. Given a finite, flat morphism between embeddable noetherian schemes of pure dimension 1, we define the notion of direct and inverse image for generalized divisors and generalized line bundles. In the case when we deal with (possibly reducible, non-reduced) projective curves over a field and the codomain curve is smooth, we introduce the compactified Jacobians parametrizing torsion-free rank-1 sheaves and we study the Norm and inverse image maps between compactified Jacobians. Finally, we introduce and study the Prym stack defined as the kernel of the Norm map.

Introduction

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
be a finite, flat morphism between noetherian curves. The first aim of this paper is to define and study the direct and inverse image for generalized divisors and generalized line bundles on
X
and
Y
. Moreover, in the case when
X
and
Y
are projective curves over a field
k
, our aim is to define and study the direct and inverse image for families of generalized divisors, and the notion of Norm and inverse image for torsion-free rank-1 sheaves.

Recall that the notion of Cartier divisor has been generalized by Hartshorne in his papers
[
Har94
]
and
[
Har07
]
, in order to include as effective divisors any codimension-one subscheme without embedded points, defined possibly by more than a single equation. A
generalized divisor
on a curve
X
of pure dimension 1 is a nondegenerate fractional ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules; generalized divisors up to linear equivalence are equivalent to
generalized line bundles
, i.e. pure coherent sheaves which are locally free of rank 1 at each generic point. Generalized line bundles can be generalized further by considering
torsion-free sheaves “of rank 1”
, with different notions of rank.

The notions of direct and inverse image for Cartier divisors are well-known; these notions are compatible with linear equivalence, giving rise to analogous well-known maps for line bundles, called the Norm and the inverse image maps between the Jacobian schemes (see for example
[
Gro67
, §21]
and
[
HP12
]
). To the best of our knowledge, the same notions for generalized divisors, generalized line bundles and torsion-free rank-1 sheaves have not been discussed so far.

In the first part of the paper we collect and review in a coherent framework definitions and results about the Norm map, generalized divisors, torsion-free rank-1 sheaves; we also recall the construction and the properties of the Fitting ideal, a construction used in algebraic geometry to produce a direct image of a morphism of schemes. In the second part, basing upon geometric intuitions, we propose definitions of the direct and inverse image for generalized divisors and generalized line bundles, and we study their properties. Then, restricting to the case when
X
and
Y
are projective curves over a field, we consider the same notions for families of effective generalized divisors, parametrized by the Hilbert scheme. In particular, the direct image between Hilbert schemes can be defined only if the curve
Y
is smooth. In the third part of the paper, under the condition that
Y
is smooth, we study the Norm and the inverse image maps between the compactified Jacobians parametrizing torsion-free rank-1 sheaves on
X
and
Y
; assuming that
X
is reduced with locally planar singularities, we also study the fibers of the Norm map.

The structure of this paper is the following. In Section
1
we review in details the definition of direct and inverse image for Cartier divisors and the definition of Norm map for line bundles. In Section
2
we review and compare different classes of coherent sheaves with well-defined rank and degree; in particular we distinguish among line bundles, generalized line bundles, torsion-free rank-1 sheaves and torsion-free sheaves of polarized rank 1. The moduli stacks corresponding to such classes are introduced and compared. In Section
3
we review the theory of generalized divisors on a curve and its connection with the theory of generalized line bundles, via the Abel map; we also introduce the geometric objects that parametrize well-behaved families of generalized divisors on a fixed curve. In Section
4
we recall briefly the notion of Fitting ideal associated to any coherent sheaf. In Section
5
we introduce and discuss in details the notion of direct and inverse image for generalized divisors and generalized line bundles. In Section
6
we study the notion of direct and inverse image for families of effective generalized divisors. In Section
7
we define the Norm map and the inverse image map between compactified Jacobians, and their relation with the generalized Abel map. Finally, in Section
8
we study the fibers of the Norm map and we introduce the Prym stack as the fiber over the trivial sheaf.

The motivation of this work comes from the Hitchin fibration for the moduli space of
G
-Higgs bundles on a fixed smooth and projective curve
C
, for
G
varying among classical groups such as GL, SL, PGL, SO, Sp. When
G=
GL, the Hitchin fibres are compactified Jacobians of certain (possibly singular) covers of
C
, called spectral curves, associated to the fibre. For others
G
, a description of the generic Hitchin fibres (i.e. those for which the singular locus of the spectral curve is empty or as small as possible) is given with the help of a Prym variety, i.e. the locus in
J(X)
of bundles whose Norm with respect to a given finite, flat morphism is zero. The study of the Norm map between compactified Jacobians allows us to extend this kind of description to the remaining Hitchin fibres.

1.1.
Direct and inverse image of Cartier divisors

We recall now the definitions of direct and inverse image for Cartier divisors. For any curve
X
, denote with
\mathcal{K}_{X}
the sheaf of total quotient rings of the curve. Recall that the set of
Cartier divisors
on

\mathcal{K}_{X}^{*}/\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}

\operatorname{CDiv}(X)=\Gamma(X,\mathcal{K}_{X}^{*}/\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}).

Although the group operation on
\mathcal{K}_{X}^{*}/\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}

\operatorname{CDiv}(X)

\operatorname{Prin}(X)

\Gamma(X,\mathcal{K}_{X}^{*})\longrightarrow\Gamma(X,\mathcal{K}_{X}^{*}/\mathcal{O}_{X}^{*}).

It is well known

\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{X}
that are also invertible
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
[
Gro67

is the sub
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module of

equal to
\mathcal{O}_{X|U_{i}}\cdot f_{i}
on any
U_{i}
.

1.2.
Norm of line bundles

We come now to the definition of the Norm map for invertible

Definition/Lemma 1.5

If

2.
Review of Jacobian stacks

In this section, we first review and compare different classes of coherent sheaves with well-defined rank and degree. The aim is to compare the different meanings for a (torsion-free) sheaf to be “of rank 1”. In particular, moving from the simplest case of line bundles, we distinguish among generalized line bundles, torsion-free rank-1 sheaves and torsion-free sheaves of polarized rank 1. Then, we introduce and compare the corresponding moduli stacks, enlarging the Jacobian scheme.

2.1.
Rank and degree of coherent sheaves

Let
X
be any Noetherian scheme of pure dimension
1
. In this subsection, we recall and compare different classes of coherent sheaves with well-defined rank and degree. We start by recalling the following definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

The
support
of
\mathcal{F}
is the closed set

\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{F})=\{x\in X|\mathcal{F}_{x}\neq 0\}\subseteq X.

The
dimension
of
\mathcal{F}
is the dimension of its support and is denoted
\dim(\mathcal{F})
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
torsion subsheaf
of
\mathcal{F}
is the maximal subsheaf
T(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathcal{F}
of dimension 0. If
\mathcal{F}=T(\mathcal{F})
, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
torsion sheaf
. If
T(\mathcal{F})=0
we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
torsion-free
.

Equivalently,
\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free if
\dim(\mathcal{E})=1
for any non-trivial coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, i.e.
\mathcal{F}
is pure of dimension 1. For any sheaf
\mathcal{F}
, the quotient sheaf
\mathcal{F}/T(\mathcal{F})
is either zero or torsion-free.

A first class of coherent sheaves with well-defined rank, after vector bundles, is given by generalized vector bundles.

Definition 2.3
.

\mathcal{F}
is a
generalized vector bundle
if it is torsion-free and there exists a positive integer
r
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\xi}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\xi}^{\oplus r}
for any generic point
\xi
of
X
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\xi}
denotes the stalk of
\mathcal{F}
at
\xi
. The integer
r
is called the
rank
of the generalized vector bundle
\mathcal{F}
. A generalized vector bundle of rank
1
is also called a
generalized line bundle
.

Clearly, a vector bundle of rank
r
is also a generalized vector bundle of the same rank.

Remark 2.4
.

Generalized line bundles were introduced in
[
BE00
]
in order to study limit linear series on a ribbon. As pointed out in
[
EG95
]
, generalized line bundles are strictly related to generalized divisors introduced indipendently by Hartshone in
[
Har86
]
(followed by
[
Har94
]
and
[
Har07
]
). See also Section
3
for details. The generalization to any rank

is due to
[
Sav19
]
.

We introduce now the notion of rank for any coherent sheaf. Denote with

the irreducible components of

and with

the generic point of each irreducible component

. Denote with

for each

Definition 2.5
.

The

is defined as the positive integer:

\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i}):=\ell_{\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi_{i}}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi_{i}}).

where
\ell_{\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi_{i}}}
denotes the length as
\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi_{i}}
-module.

Definition 2.6
.

(Rank and multirank of a coherent sheaf)
The
rank
of
\mathcal{F}
on
X_{i}
is defined as the rational number

\operatorname{rk}_{X_{i}}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{\ell_{\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi_{i}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi_{i}})}{\ell_{\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi_{i}}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi_{i}})}.

The
multirank
of
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is the
n
-uple
{\underline{r}}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
where
r_{i}
is the rank of
\mathcal{F}
at
X_{i}
. The sheaf
\mathcal{F}
has
rank

r
on
X
if it has rank
r
at each
X_{i}
.

Note that, since length of modules is additive in short exact sequences, the rank and multirank of coherent sheaves are additive in short exact sequences too.

Proposition 2.7
.

A generalized vector bundle of rank
r
on
X
has rank
r
as a coherent sheaf. If
X
is reduced, any torsion-free sheaf of rank
r
is a generalized vector bundle of the same rank.

Proof.

The first statement follows from
2.6
and the fact that isomorphic modules have the same length. For the second assertion, note that on an integral curve
X
there exists for any torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
an open dense subset
U\subset X
such that
\mathcal{F}_{|U}
is locally free, hence the stalk of
\mathcal{F}
at the generic point of
X
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi}
-module of rank
\operatorname{rk}_{X}(\mathcal{F})
. If
X
is reducible, the assertion follows by considering any irreducible component.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

There are in literature other notions for the rank of a coherent sheaf on a irreducible component. A classical notion is the
reduced rank
of
\mathcal{F}
on
X_{i}
:

\operatorname{\textbf{rank}}_{X_{i}}(\mathcal{F})=\dim_{\kappa(\xi_{i})}(\mathcal{F}_{\xi_{i}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi}}\kappa(\xi_{i})).

This definition computes the rank of the sheaf restricted to its reduced support; it agrees with the other definitions for generalized vector bundles. However, there are cases when the reduced rank of a torsion-free sheaf differs from its rank, as it happens for quasi-locally free sheaves on a ribbon (see
[
Sav19
, §1.4]
). They also provide examples of torsion-free sheaves which are not generalized vector bundles, even if their rank is well defined.

The definition of
\operatorname{rk}_{H}(\mathcal{F})
comes at least from
[
Sch98
, Définition 1.2]
, where it is given in the context of projective
k
-schemes without embedded points. The notion of
generalized rank
introduced in
[
Dre06
]
and
[
Sav19
]
is essentially equivalent. Finally, a common definition in projective algebraic geometry is the polarized rank, that we will discuss later on in the present section.

In order to introduce the degree of coherent sheaves, from now on we
assume that
X
is projective curve over a base field
k
. Recall that the Euler characteristic of a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
is

\chi(\mathcal{F}):=\sum(-)^{i}\dim_{k}H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}).

Definition 2.9
.

(Degree of a coherent sheaf)
Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
has rank
r
on
X
. Then, the
degree
of
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is defined as the fractional number:

\deg\mathcal{F}=\chi(X,\mathcal{F})-r\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}).

Remark 2.10
.

If the rank of
\mathcal{F}
is integer, the degree is integer as well. If
X
is integral, the degree of a sheaf is an integer and coincides with the classical notion of degree for sheaves on integral curves.

The following technical lemma is very useful.

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume that
X
is irreducible. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf of rank
r
on
X
and let
\mathcal{E}
be a locally free sheaf of rank
n
on
X
. Then

\chi(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{E})=r\deg(\mathcal{E})+n\chi(X,\mathcal{F}).

Proof.

The proof is inspired by
[
Sta19
,
Tag 0AYV
]
and uses devissage for coherent sheaves as stated in
[
Sta19
,
Tag 01YI
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be the property of coherent sheaves
\mathcal{F}
on
X
expressing that the formula of the Lemma holds. By additivity of rank and Euler characteristic in short exact sequences,
\mathcal{P}
satisfy the two-out-of-three property. The integral subschemes
Z
of
X
are the reduced subscheme
C
with support equal to
X
, and the closed points of
X
. For
Z=C
, the formula of the Lemma for
\mathcal{O}_{C}
is:

\chi(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C})=\frac{\deg(\mathcal{E})}{\operatorname{mult}_{X}C}+n\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{C}).

This is true by definition of degree of
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}=\mathcal{E}_{|C}
on
C
and the fact that
\deg(\mathcal{E})=\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C)\deg(\mathcal{E}_{|C})
. Then
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{O}_{C})
holds. If
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
is a closed point, the formula of the Lemma is true for
i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
since it is a torsion sheaf of rank 0 and
\chi(X,\mathcal{E}\otimes i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z})=n\chi(X,i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z})
. Then
\mathcal{P}(i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Z})
holds.
∎

Let
H
be any polarization of
X
; then, the notion of polarized rank and degree of sheaves can be defined. Denote with
H_{|C_{i}}
the restriction of
H
to the closed subscheme
C_{i}\subseteq X
. Since
H
is locally free of rank
1
, the degree of
H
on
X
is related to the degrees of each
H_{|C_{i}}
on
C_{i}
by the formula:

\deg H=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg H_{|C_{i}}.

Definition 2.12
.

(Polarized rank and degree of a torsion-free sheaf)
Let
H
be any polarization of
X
with degree
\deg H=\delta
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
. The
polarized rank and degree
of
\mathcal{F}
are the rational numbers
r_{H}(\mathcal{F})
and
d_{H}(\mathcal{F})
determined by the Hilbert polinomial of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
H
:

P(\mathcal{F},n,H):=\chi(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))=\delta r_{H}(\mathcal{F})n+d_{H}(\mathcal{F})+r_{H}(\mathcal{F})\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}).

The polarized rank and degree of a sheaf depend strictly on the degrees of the restrictions
H_{|C_{i}}
, as the following theorem shows.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
H
be a polarization of
X
and let
\mathcal{F}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
. The polarized rank of
\mathcal{F}
is related to the multirank of
\mathcal{F}
by the formula

r_{H}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{rk}_{X_{i}}(\mathcal{F})\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg H_{|C_{i}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg H_{|C_{i}}}.

Proof.

Since the Hilbert polynomial of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
H
has degree
1
, its leading term can be computed in terms of Euler characteristic as

\chi(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))-\chi(X,\mathcal{F}).

Since
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H
is the degree of
H
on
X
, by Definition
2.12
we have to prove:

\chi(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))-\chi(X,\mathcal{F})=n\sum_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{rk}_{X_{i}}(\mathcal{F})\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H.

First, we reduce to the case of
X
irreducible. Consider the exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{F}_{|X_{i}}\rightarrow T\rightarrow 0

where
T
is a torsion sheaf supported only at the intersections of the irreducible components. Tensoring by
nH
, we obtain another exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))_{|X_{i}}\rightarrow T\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH)\rightarrow 0.

By additivity of the Euler characteristic with respect to exact sequences we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}

\displaystyle\left(\chi(X_{i},(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))_{|X_{i}})-\chi(X_{i},\mathcal{F}_{|X_{i}})\right)=

\displaystyle=\chi(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))-\chi(X,\mathcal{F})+\chi(X,T\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))-\chi(X,T).

Since
T
has dimension 0, the characteristics
\chi(X,T\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))
and
\chi(X,T)
are the same, hence:

\chi(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))-\chi(X,\mathcal{F})=\sum_{i}\left(\chi(X_{i},(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))_{|X_{i}})-\chi(X_{i},\mathcal{F}_{|X_{i}})\right).

We are then reduced to prove the statement on each irreducible component
X_{i}
. In other words we have to prove that, if
X
is a irreducible (possibly non-reduced) curve with reduced structure
C
, then:

\chi(X,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH))-\chi(X,\mathcal{F})=n\deg_{X}(H)\operatorname{rk}_{X}(\mathcal{F}).

This is exactly the content of Lemma
2.11
, with
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(nH)
.
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be any torsion-free sheaf with well-defined rank. Then, its polarized rank
r_{H}(\mathcal{F})
and degree
d_{H}(\mathcal{F})
are equal respectively to
\operatorname{rk}_{X}(\mathcal{F})
and
\deg_{X}(\mathcal{F})
for any polarization
H
on
X
. f
X
is irreducible, the rank and degree of a sheaf coincide with the polarized counterparts, for any polarization.

Proof.

The statement about the rank follows from Theorem
2.13
, where
r_{i}=r
for each irreducible component
X_{i}
. To prove the statement about the degree, note that for any polarization
H
:

d_{H}(\mathcal{F})=\chi(X,\mathcal{F})-r_{H}(\mathcal{F})\chi(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}).

If
r_{H}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F})
, then
d_{H}(\mathcal{F})=\deg(\mathcal{F})
by Definition
2.9
. The last statement follows from the fact that the
\operatorname{rk}_{X}(\mathcal{F})
is well-defined for any torsion-free sheaf when
X
has only one irreducible component.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

For reducible curves, the definitions of polarized rank and degree are the most general. As pointed out in
[
Ló05
, §2]
, being of polarized rank
1
for a torsion-free sheaf does not ensure that the sheaf is supported on the whole curve. Moreover, there are cases of torsion-free sheaves of polarized rank
1
whose polarized rank on the restrictions to the irreducible components is different from
1
and not an integer, even for reduced curves.

2.2.
Moduli of torsion-free sheaves “of rank 1”

In this subsection, we introduce the moduli spaces for generalized line bundles, for torsion-free rank-1 sheaves and for torsion-free sheaves with polarized rank 1, over a (possibly reducible, non-reduced) projective curve
X
, defined over a base field
k
, with irreducible components
X_{1},\dots,X_{s}
.

Since we are dealing with moduli spaces, we recall first the definition of slope and stability for torsion-free sheaves.

Definition 2.16
.

Let
H
be a polarization on
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
with polarized rank
r_{H}(\mathcal{F})
and polarized degree
d_{H}(\mathcal{F})
.

The
slope
of
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
H
is defined as the rational number
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{F})=d_{H}(\mathcal{F})/r_{H}(\mathcal{F})
.

A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is
H
-
stable
(respectively
H
-
semi-stable
) if it is torsion-free and for any proper subsheaf
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{F}
the equality
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E})<\mu_{H}(\mathcal{F})
holds (respectively
\leq
).

We start from the last moduli space, which is the larger and contains all the others.

Definition/Lemma 2.17
.

Let
H
be a polarization on
X
and let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer number. The
Simpson Jacobian stack
of degree
d
on
X
is the algebraic stack
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d)
such that for any
k
-scheme
T
,
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,d)(T)
is the groupoid of
T
-flat coherent sheaves on
X\times_{k}T
whose fibers over
T
are torsion-free sheaves of polarized rank 1 and polarized degree
d
on
X\simeq X\times_{k}\{t\}
with respect to
H
.

The
Simpson Jacobian
of degree
d
is the projective scheme
\overline{J}_{tf}(X,H,d)
which is the good moduli space representing S-equivalence classes of
H
-semistable torsion-free sheaves of polarized rank 1 and polarized degree
d
on
X
.

The union of the Simpson Jacobians of all degrees is denoted
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H)
(resp.
\overline{J}_{tf}(X,H)
).

Proof.

The algebraicity of
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d)
follows from the algebraicity of the stack of coherent sheaves on
X
(see
[
Lie06
, 2.1]
and
[
CW18
, Theorem 7.20]
). The moduli space of semistable sheaves with fixed polarized rank and degree was constructed by Simpson in
[
Sim94
]
.
∎

In the following proposition, we show that the multirank of sheaves splits
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d)
in unions of connected components.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
H
be a fixed polarization on
X
. For any
{\underline{r}}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{s})\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{s}
, let
W_{\underline{r}}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d)
be the substack parametrizing torsion-free sheaves with multirank equal to
{\underline{r}}
. Then
\{W_{\underline{r}}|W_{\underline{r}}\neq\emptyset\}_{{\underline{r}}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{s}}
is a finite collection of pairwise disjoint substacks of
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d)
that covers the whole space. Moreover, each
W_{\underline{r}}
is open and closed, hence union of connected components of
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d)
.

Proof.

The multi-rank of a sheaf is well-defined, hence the substacks
W_{\underline{r}}
are pairwise disjoint.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf with polarized rank
r_{H}
equal to
1
. By Theorem
2.13
its multirank
{\underline{r}}
must satisfy:

1=r_{H}(\mathcal{F})=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}r_{i}(t)\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H}.

Since each
r_{i}
is a non-negative fraction with integer numerator and denominator equal to
\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})
, the possible values for
{\underline{r}}
form a finite subset of
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{s}
, once that
H
and
r_{H}
are fixed. Then, only a finite number of
W_{\underline{r}}
is non-empty, hence
\{W_{\underline{r}}|W_{\underline{r}}\neq\emptyset\}
is a finite collection.

We claim that each
W_{\underline{r}}
is closed; since the collection is finite and the
W_{\underline{r}}
’s are pairwise disjoint, this implies that each
W_{\underline{r}}
is open and closed, and hence union of connected components.

To prove that
W_{\underline{r}}
is closed, let
T
be any
k
-scheme and let
\mathcal{F}
be any family of torsion-free sheaves of polarized rank
1
on
X\times_{k}T\xrightarrow{\pi}T
. Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
has generically multirank
{\underline{r}}
on
X
, meaning that
\operatorname{rk}_{X_{i}}(\mathcal{F}_{|\pi^{-1}(\eta)})=r_{i}
for each irreducible component
X_{i}
of
X
and any generic point
\eta
of
T
.
Let
t
be any point in the closure of
\{\eta\}
. Since the length is upper-semicontinuous, the rank of
\mathcal{F}_{|\pi^{-1}(t)}
at each
X_{i}
is a rational number
r_{i}(t)
greater or equal than
r_{i}
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{F}_{|\pi^{-1}(t)}
have polarized rank
1
; hence, thanks to Theorem
2.13
, we can write:

\displaystyle 1=r_{H}(\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}r_{i}(t)\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H}\geq

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}r_{i}\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H}=1.

By difference we obtain:

\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s}(r_{i}(t)-r_{i})\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{mult}_{X}(C_{i})\deg_{C_{i}}H}=0.

Since all the terms of the expression are non-negative, the only possibility is that
r_{i}(t)=r_{i}
for each
i
. We conclude that the whole family
\mathcal{F}
belongs to
W_{\underline{r}}
.
∎

We come now to torsion-free sheaves with well-defined rank 1 on
X
.

Definition/Lemma 2.19
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer number. The
compactified Jacobian stack
of degree
d
on
X
is the algebraic stack
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X,d)
such that for any
k
-scheme
T
,
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X,d)(T)
is the groupoid of
T
-flat coherent sheaves on
X\times_{k}T
whose fibers over
T
are torsion-free sheaves of rank 1 and degree
d
on
X\simeq X\times_{k}\{t\}
.

Let
H
be a polarization on
X
. The
compactified Jacobian scheme
of degree
d
is the good moduli space
\overline{J}(X,H,d)
representing S-equivalence classes of
H
-semistable torsion-free sheaves of rank 1 and degree
d
on
X
.

The union of the compactified Jacobians of all degrees is denoted
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)
(resp.
\overline{J}(X,H)
).

Proof.

The property of having rank
1
on
X
implies that the polarized rank is equal to
1
for any polarization; moreover, the degree is well defined and equal to the polarized degree for any polarization. Fix any polarization
H
on
X
. Then,
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X,d)
can be seen as the open and closed substack
W_{(1,\dots,1)}
of
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d)
(Lemma
2.18
), and hence is algebraic. The same argument holds for
\overline{J}(X,H,d)
, with the difference that the notion of semi-stability strictly depends on the choice of
H
.
∎

The following proposition shows that
\overline{J}(X,H,d)
is actually a compactification of
J^{d}(X)
.

Proposition 2.20
.

For any polarization
H
on
X
,
\overline{J}(X,H,d)
is a projective scheme, union of connected components of
\overline{J}_{tf}(X,H,d)
, and contains
J^{d}(X)
as an open subscheme.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X,d)
is an open and closed substack of
\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d)
, then
\overline{J}(X,H,d)
is an open and closed subscheme of
\overline{J}_{tf}(X,H,d)
and union of connected components. It is non-empty, as it contains the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Moreover
\overline{J}_{tf}(X,H,d)
is projective, hence
\overline{J}(X,H,d)
is projective too. Finally, the condition of being locally free is open and implies both stability and rank 1; hence,
J^{d}(X)
is an open subscheme of
\overline{J}(X,H,d)
.
∎

We come now to the last moduli space, which parametrizes generalized line bundles.

Definition/Lemma 2.21
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer number. The
generalized Jacobian stack
of degree
d
on
X
is the algebraic stack
\operatorname{\textit{G}\mathbb{J}}(X,d)
such that, for any
k
-scheme
T
,
\operatorname{\textit{G}\mathbb{J}}(X,d)(T)
is the groupoid of
T
-flat coherent sheaves on
X\times_{k}T
whose fibers over
T
are generalized line bundles of degree
d
on
X\simeq X\times_{k}\{t\}
.

Let
H
be a polarization on
X
. The
generalized Jacobian scheme
of degree
d
is the good moduli space
\operatorname{\textit{GJ}}(X,H,d)
representing S-equivalence classes of
H
-semistable generalized line bundles of degree
d
on
X
.

The union of the generalized Jacobians of all degrees is denoted
\operatorname{\textit{G}\mathbb{J}}(X)
(resp.
\operatorname{\textit{GJ}}(X,H)
).

Proof.

Generalized line bundles are torsion-free sheaves of rank 1; moreover, the condition of being generically locally free is open, hence
\operatorname{\textit{G}\mathbb{J}}(X,d)
can be seen as an open substack of
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X,d)
. In particular,
\operatorname{\textit{G}\mathbb{J}}(X,d)
is an algebraic stack. Similarly,
\operatorname{\textit{GJ}}(X,H,d)
is an open subscheme of
\overline{J}(X,H,d)
.
∎

To sum up, we defined a number of moduli spaces satisfying the following chain of inclusions:

J^{d}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{\subseteq}}\operatorname{\textit{G}\mathbb{J}}(X,d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{\subseteq}}\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X,d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(3)}}{{\subseteq}}\overline{\mathbb{J}}_{tf}(X,H,d),

and:

J^{d}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1^{\prime})}}{{\subseteq}}\operatorname{\textit{GJ}}(X,H,d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2^{\prime})}}{{\subseteq}}\overline{J}(X,H,d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(3^{\prime})}}{{\subseteq}}\overline{J}_{tf}(X,H,d).

Remark 2.22
.

Inclusions
(1)
and
(2)
(resp.
(1^{\prime})
and
(2^{\prime})
) above are open embeddings; inclusion
(3)
(resp.
(3^{\prime})
) is an open and closed embedding. In general they are strict and not dense, in the sense that for any of them there exists a (possibly non-reduced or reducible) curve
X
such that the closure of the smaller space in the bigger one is contained strictly. Anyway, when
X
satisfies additional conditions, some of the inclusions above are actually equalities.

•

If
X
is irreducible, inclusion
(3)
(resp.
(3)^{\prime}
) is an equality.

•

If
X
is reduced, inclusion
(2)
(resp.
(2^{\prime})
) is an equality.

•

If
X
is integral and smooth, inclusions
(1)
,
(2)
and
(3)
(resp.
(1^{\prime})
,
(2^{\prime})
and
(3^{\prime})
) are equalities.

3.
Review of generalized divisors

In this section, we review R. Hartshorne’s theory of generalized divisors in the case of curves, in connection with the theory of torsion-free sheaves presented in Section
2
. We show that generalized divisors with given degree
d
, up to linear equivalence, correspond to generalized line bundles of degree
-d
; this fact is reflected in the definition of the Abel map given at the end of this section, comparing the moduli space of effective generalized divisors with the generalized Jacobian.

Remark 3.1
.

The theory of generalized divisors is developed by Hartshorne in his papers
[
Har86
]
,
[
Har94
]
and
[
Har07
]
, in order to generalize the notion of Cartier divisor on schemes satisfying condition
S_{2}
of Serre. Since we are dealing with schemes of dimension 1, the condition
S_{2}
of Serre coincides with the condition
S_{1}
, which in turn coincides with the fact of not having embedded components (i.e. embedded points). In particular, any scheme of pure dimension 1 is also
S_{1}
, and hence
S_{2}
. Similarly, a coherent sheaf on a curve satisfies condition
S_{2}
if and only if it is torsion-free.

3.1.
Generalized divisors

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a curve and let
\mathcal{K}_{X}
be the sheaf of total quotient rings on
X
. A
generalized divisor
on
X
is a nondegenerate fractional ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, i.e. a subsheaf
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{X}
that is a coherent sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules and such that
\mathcal{I}_{\eta}=\mathcal{K}_{X,\eta}
for any generic point
\eta\in X
. It is
effective
if
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
. It is
Cartier
if
\mathcal{I}
is an invertible
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module, or equivalently locally principal. It is
principal
if
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{O}_{X}\cdot f
(also denoted
(f)
) for some global section
f\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{K}^{*}_{X})
.

The set of generalized divisors on
X
is denoted with
\operatorname{GDiv}(X)
, the subset of Cartier divisors with
\operatorname{CDiv}(X)
and the set of principal divisors with
\operatorname{Prin}(X)
.

Using the usual notion of subscheme associated to a sheaf of ideals, the set
\operatorname{GDiv}^{+}(X)
of effective generalized divisors on
X
is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of closed subschemes
D\subset X
of pure codimension one (i.e. of dimension zero). With a slight abuse of notation, also for non-effective divisors, we denote with
D
the generalized divisor and we refer to
\mathcal{I}
(or
\mathcal{I}_{D}
) as the
fractional ideal
of
D
(also called
defining ideal
of
D
, or
ideal sheaf
of
D
if
D
is effective).

Let
D_{1},D_{2}
be generalized divisors on
X
, with fractional ideals
\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2}
. The
sum

D_{1}+D_{2}
is the generalized divisor associated to the fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{X}
. The sum is commutative, associative, with neutral element
0
defined by the trivial ideal
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The
inverse
of a generalized divisor
D
associated to
\mathcal{I}
is the generalized divisor
-D
associated to the fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}^{-1}
, i.e. the sheaf which on any open subset
U
of
X
is defined as
\{f\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{K}_{X})\hskip 3.00003pt|\hskip 3.00003pt\mathcal{I}(U)\cdot f\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\}
.

The inverse operation behaves well with the sum only for Cartier divisors. For any pair of generalized divisors
D,E
on
X
with
E
Cartier,
-(D+E)=(-D)+(-E)
, but
D+(-D)=0
if and only if
D
is a Cartier divisor. As a consequence, the set
\operatorname{GDiv}(X)
of all generalized divisors over
X
endowed with the sum operation is not a group, but the subset
\operatorname{CDiv}(X)
of Cartier divisors is. The set
\operatorname{Prin}(X)
of principal divisors is a subgroup of
\operatorname{CDiv}(X)
and both the groups act by addition on the set
\operatorname{GDiv}(X)
.

Tthe following Lemma, inspired by
[
Har94
, Proposition 2.11]
, shows that any generalized divisor is equal to the sum of an effective generalized divisor and the inverse of an effective Cartier divisor, as

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a curve and let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}(X)
be any generalized divisor on
X
. Then, there exist an effective generalized divisor
D^{\prime}
and an effective Cartier divisor
E
on
X
such that
D=D^{\prime}+(-E)
.

Proof.

Cover
X
by open affines
U_{i}=\operatorname{Spec}(A_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,r
. For each
i
, denote with
I_{i}
the fractional ideal of
D
restricted to
U_{i}
. This is a finitely generated
A_{i}
-module, so there exists a nonzero-divisor
f_{i}\in A_{i}
such that
f\cdot I_{i}\subseteq A
. Let
Y_{i}\subset U_{i}
be the closed subscheme defined by
f_{i}
, which is an effective Cartier divisor of
U_{i}
; after the composition with
U_{i}\hookrightarrow X
,
Y_{i}
becomes an effective Cartier divisor of
X
. Now, the sum of divisors
D+\sum_{i=1}^{r}Y_{i}
is effective as it is sum of effective divisors on each
U_{i}
. By putting
E=\sum_{i=1}^{r}Y_{i}
and
D^{\prime}=D+E
we get the result.
∎

Two generalized divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
over
X
are
linearly equivalent
(written
D_{1}\sim D_{2}
) if there is a divisor
(f)\in\operatorname{Prin}(X)
such that
D_{1}+(f)=D_{2}
. We define the
generalized Picard of X
as the set of divisors on
X
modulo linear equivalence:

\operatorname{GPic}(X)=\operatorname{GDiv}(X)/\operatorname{Prin}(X).

We have the following commutative diagram of sets, where the vertical maps are quotients by
\operatorname{Prin}(X)
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{CDiv}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subseteq}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{GDiv}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Pic}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subseteq}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{GPic}(X)}

Taking inverse and sums preserve linear equivalence, so the two operations are well defined also on
\operatorname{GPic}(X)
and the subset
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{GPic}(X)
is a group that acts on
\operatorname{GPic}(X)
by addition. For a curve
X
, the condition
\operatorname{GDiv}(X)=\operatorname{CDiv}(X)
is also equivalent to
\operatorname{GPic}(X)=\operatorname{Pic}(X)
, which is also equivalent to the curve
X
being smooth.

As in the case of Cartier divisors, also the set
\operatorname{GPic}(X)
has an alternative description in terms of abstract sheaves. First, we need a definition.

The set
\operatorname{GPic}(X)
has an alternative description in terms of generalized line bundles. Let
D
be a generalized divisor on
X
; then, its fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}
is a generalized line bundle. If
D^{\prime}
is another generalized line bundle, it is linearly equivalent to
D
if and only if its fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is a generalized line bundle isomorphic to
X
as an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. Viceversa, any generalized line bundle
\mathcal{F}
on
X
is isomorphic to the fractional ideal of some generalized divisor
D

[
Har07
, Proposition 2.4]
. Then,
\operatorname{GPic}(X)
can be also defined as the set of generalized line bundles of
X
, up to isomorphism
.
Under this description, classes of Cartier divisors correspond to isomorphism classes of line bundles, and the operations of sum by a Cartier divisor and inverse of a divisor are replaced with tensor product and taking dual of the corresponding sheaves.

3.2.
Degree of generalized divisors

We now
assume that
X
is a projective curve over a base field
k
. In this case, the notion of
degree of a divisor
can be introduced.

Definition/Lemma 3.4
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}^{+}(X)
be an effective generalized divisor on
X
with ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{D}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and let
x\in X
be any point of
X
in codimension 1. We define the
degree of
D
at
x
as the non-negative integer:

\deg_{x}(D)=\ell_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathcal{I}_{D,x}\big)[\kappa(x):k].

The degree of any generalized divisor
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}(X)
at
x
is defined as
\deg_{x}(D)=\deg_{x}(E)-\deg_{x}(F)
, where
D=E-F
with
E,F
effective generalized divisors and
F
Cartier by Lemma
3.3
.

The degree of
D
on
X
(also denoted as
\deg(D)
when there is no ambiguity) is equal to the sum of the degrees of
D
at all points of
X
in codimension 1:

\deg_{X}(D)=\sum_{x\textrm{ cod 1}}\deg_{x}(D).

Proof.

First, note that the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathcal{I}_{D,x}
in nonzero if and only if
x
is in the support of
D
. In such case the local ring has Krull dimension 0, so it is Artinian and hence has finite length.

The definition of degree at a point of any generalized divisor is well-given thanks to
[
Har94
, Lemma 2.17]
, and
\deg_{x}(-D)=-\deg_{x}(D)
.

Finally, the supporty of
D
contains only finitely many points, hence the degree of
D
on
X
is a well-defined integer.
∎

The degree of a generalized divisor is strictly related to the degree of its fractional ideal, as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
D
be a generalized divisor on
X
with fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}_{D}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{X}
. Then, the degree of
\mathcal{I}_{D}
as a torsion-free sheaf is well defined and equal to
-\deg_{X}(D)
. Moreover, linearly equivalent divisors have the same degree on
X
.

Proof.

The fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}_{D}
is a generalized line bundle, hence it has rank
1
on
X
and well-defined degree as a torsion-free sheaf. In order to compute
\deg(\mathcal{I}_{D})
consider the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}_{D}\rightarrow 0.

By definition of degree,
\deg(\mathcal{I}_{D})=\chi(\mathcal{I}_{D})-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. Then, by additivity of Euler characteristic with respect to exact sequences, we deduce that
\deg(\mathcal{I}_{D})=-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}_{D})
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}_{D}
has dimension 0, its Euler characteristic is equal to
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}_{D}):=\dim_{k}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}_{D})
. The support of
\mathcal{O}_{D}=\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}_{D}
is by definition the support of
D
and
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}_{D})=\oplus_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}D}\mathcal{O}_{D,x}
. Then we compute:

\displaystyle\deg(\mathcal{I}_{D})

\displaystyle=-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}_{D})=-\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}D}\dim_{k}\mathcal{O}_{D,x}=

\displaystyle=-\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}D}\dim_{\kappa(x)}\big(\mathcal{O}_{D,x}\big)[\kappa(x):k]=

\displaystyle=-\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}D}\ell_{\mathcal{O}_{X},x}\big(\mathcal{O}_{D,x}\big)[\kappa(x):k]=

\displaystyle=-\deg_{X}(D).

Consider now a generalized divisor
D
with fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}_{D}
and let
D=E-F
with
E,F
effective and
F
Cartier by Lemma
3.3
. Since
F
is effective, consider first the short exact sequence:

(3)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow T\rightarrow 0

where
T
is a sheaf of dimension 0. Since
F
is Cartier, we can tensor by
\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}
to obtain:

(4)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}\otimes T\rightarrow 0.

Using addivity of Euler characteristics in the exact sequences
3
and
4
and the fact that
\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}\otimes T)=\chi(T)
, we see that:

\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1})-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F}).

Also
E
is effective, hence there is a short exact sequence:

(5)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 0

where
Q
is a sheaf of dimension 0. Tensoring again by
\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}
we obtain:

(6)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}\otimes Q\rightarrow 0

where
\mathcal{I}_{D}\simeq\mathcal{I}_{E}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}
since
D=E-F
and
F
is Cartier. Using addivity of Euler characteristics in the exact sequences
5
and
6
and the fact that
\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}\otimes Q)=\chi(Q)
, we can compute the degree of
\mathcal{I}_{D}
:

\displaystyle\deg(\mathcal{I}_{D})

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathcal{I}_{D})-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1})-\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1}\otimes Q)-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1})-\chi(Q)-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F}^{-1})-2\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})+\chi(\mathcal{I}_{E})=

\displaystyle=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})-\chi(\mathcal{I}_{F})+\chi(\mathcal{I}_{E})-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}).

Since
E
and
F
are effective, we conclude that:

\deg(\mathcal{I}_{D})=-(\deg_{X}(E)-\deg_{X}(F))=-\deg_{X}(D).

To prove the last statement, let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be linearly equivalent divisors; then, their fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}_{D}
and
\mathcal{I}_{D^{\prime}}
are isomorphic. Hence,

\deg_{X}(D)=-\deg(\mathcal{I}_{D})=-\deg(\mathcal{I}_{D}^{\prime})=\deg_{X}(D^{\prime}).

∎

Remark 3.6

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}HS_{1}&\dots&HS_{r}\end{array}\right]=H\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}S_{1}&\dots&S_{r}\end{array}\right]

The 0-th Fitting ideal of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}\mathcal{J}
, restricted to
V
, is the ideal of
(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})_{|V}
generated by the
n\times n
minors of the matrix

. Any such minor is equal to a
n\times n
minor of the matrix
\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}S_{1}&\dots&S_{r}\end{array}\right]
multiplied by
\det H
. Then, by Definition
4.1
,

\operatorname{Fitt}_{0}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I}\mathcal{J})_{|V}=\operatorname{Fitt}_{0}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{I})_{|V}\cdot\operatorname{Fitt}_{0}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{J})_{|V}

∎

Definition/Lemma 5.3
.

(Direct image of a generalized divisor)
Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}(X)
be a generalized divisor on
X
, such that
D=D^{\prime}-E
with
D^{\prime}\in\operatorname{GDiv}^{+}(X)
and
E\in\operatorname{CDiv}^{+}(X)
by Lemma
3.3
. The
direct image of D with respect to
\pi
, denoted with
\pi_{*}(D)
, is the generalized divisor
\pi_{*}(D^{\prime})-\pi_{*}(E)
.

Proof.

To prove that it is well defined, let
D=D^{\prime}-E=\widetilde{D}^{\prime}-\widetilde{E}
, with
D^{\prime},\widetilde{D}^{\prime}
effective and
E,\widetilde{E}
effective Cartier.
Since
E,\widetilde{E}
are Cartier,
D^{\prime}+\widetilde{E}=\widetilde{D}^{\prime}+E
; then, by Lemma
5.2
we have:

\pi_{*}(D^{\prime})+\pi_{*}(\widetilde{E})=\pi_{*}(\widetilde{D}^{\prime})+\pi_{*}(E).

Since
\pi_{*}(E)
and
\pi_{*}(\widetilde{E})
are also Cartier by Lemma
5.2
, they can be subtracted from each side in order to obtain:

\pi_{*}(D^{\prime})-\pi_{*}(E)=\pi_{*}(\widetilde{D}^{\prime})-\pi_{*}(\widetilde{E}).

This shows that
\pi_{*}(D)
does not depend on the choice of
D^{\prime}
and
E
.
∎

We study now some properties of the direct image for generalized divisors.

Proposition 5.4
.

(Properties of direct image)

(1)

Let

be a Cartier divisor. Then,
\pi_{*}(D)
is a Cartier divisor and

\pi_{*}(D)

(2)

. Then,

Proof.

To prove (1), consider
D=E-F
with

effective and
F
Cartier by Lemma
3.3
. Since

is Cartier and

, then also

,

\pi_{*}(D)=\pi_{*}(E)-\pi_{*}(F).

By Lemma

, it is a difference of Cartier divisors and hence it is Cartier. To compute
\pi_{*}(-D)
, note that since

is Cartier then

, and it is a difference of effective Cartier divisors; then, apply Definition
5.3

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(-D)

\displaystyle=\pi_{*}(F)-\pi_{*}(E)=

\displaystyle=-\pi_{*}(D).

To compare

1.3
, let
\mathcal{I}
and

be the ideal sheaves of

and

and let

To prove (2), consider

To prove (3), if

To prove (4), let

We are now ready to define the direct image for generalized line bundles.

Definition/Lemma 5.5
.

(Direct image for generalized line bundles)
The direct image for generalized divisors induce a direct image map between the sets of generalized line bundles, defined as:

\displaystyle[\pi_{*}]:\hskip 20.00003pt\operatorname{GPic}(X)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{GPic}(Y)

\displaystyle[D]

Corollary 5.9
.

Let

be a line bundle on

Proof.

On the other side, by Proposition

5.2.

In this subsection, we define the inverse image for generalized divisors and generalized line bundles and we study the relation of the inverse image with the direct image. We start from the case of effective divisors.

Definition/Lemma 5.11

(Inverse image of an effective generalized divisor)
Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}^{+}(Y)
be an effective generalized divisor with ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. The
inverse image of
D
relative to
\pi
, denoted
\pi^{*}(D)
, is the effective generalized divisor with ideal sheaf
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{I})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Proof.

The inverse image ideal
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{I})
is an ideal sheaf of
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
-modules and can be extended to
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules via the canonical map of sheaves of rings
\pi^{\sharp}:\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}
. It is coherent since
\mathcal{I}
is coherent. If
\eta
is a generic point of
X
, then
\pi(\eta)
is a generic point of
Y
, hence

(\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{I})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\eta}=\mathcal{I}_{\pi(\eta)}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta}=\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta}

since
\mathcal{I}_{\pi(\eta)}=\mathcal{O}_{Y,\pi(\eta)}
.
∎

Remark 5.12
.

In the setting of Definition
5.11
, consider the short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{D}\rightarrow 0.

Since
\pi
is flat and surjective, the pullback functor
\_\otimes_{\pi^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is exact as well as the inverse image functor
\pi^{-1}
. Then, the previous exact sequence induces the following exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\pi^{*}\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}\rightarrow 0.

Since
\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, the pullback sheaf
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{I})
has a canonical injection in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, whose image is exactly the inverse image ideal
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{I})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}^{+}(Y)
be a generalized effective divisor and
E\in\operatorname{CDiv}^{+}(Y)
be an effective Cartier divisor on
Y
. Then, the inverse image divisor
\pi^{*}(E)
is Cartier and
\pi^{*}(D+E)=\pi^{*}(D)+\pi^{*}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be the ideal sheaves of
D
and
E
respectively. The ideal sheaf
\mathcal{J}
is locally principal, hence its inverse image
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{J})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}
is again locally principal; then,
\pi^{*}(E)
is Cartier. The generalized divisor
D+E
is defined by the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{J}
, whose inverse image is:

\displaystyle\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{J})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}

\displaystyle=(\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{I})\cdot\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{J}))\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}=

\displaystyle=(\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{I})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X})\cdot(\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{J})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}),

which is the defining ideal of
\pi^{*}(D)+\pi^{*}(E)
.
∎

Now, we can extend the definition of inverse image to any generalized divisor and study its properties.

Definition/Lemma 5.14
.

(Inverse image of a generalized divisor)
Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}(Y)
be any generalized divisor and let
D=E-F
with
E,F
effective generalized divisors and
F
Cartier by Lemma
3.3
. The
inverse image of
D
relative to
\pi
, denoted
\pi^{*}(D)
, is the generalized divisor
\pi^{*}(E)-\pi^{*}(F)
.

Proof.

To prove that it is well defined, let
D=D^{\prime}-E=\widetilde{D}^{\prime}-\widetilde{E}
, with
D^{\prime},\widetilde{D}^{\prime}
effective and
E,\widetilde{E}
effective Cartier.
Since
E,\widetilde{E}
are Cartier,
D^{\prime}+\widetilde{E}=\widetilde{D}^{\prime}+E
; then, by Lemma
5.13
we have:

\pi^{*}(D^{\prime})+\pi^{*}(\widetilde{E})=\pi^{*}(\widetilde{D}^{\prime})+\pi^{*}(E).

Again by Lemma
5.13

\pi^{*}(E)
and
\pi^{*}(\widetilde{E})
are Cartier, so they can be subtracted from each side in order to obtain:

\pi^{*}(D^{\prime})-\pi^{*}(E)=\pi^{*}(\widetilde{D}^{\prime})-\pi^{*}(\widetilde{E}),

so
\pi^{*}(D)
does not depend on the choice of
D^{\prime}
and
E
.
∎

Proposition 5.15
.

(Properties of inverse image)

(1)

Let
D\in\operatorname{CDiv}(Y)
be a Cartier divisor on
Y
. Then,
\pi^{*}(D)
is a Cartier divisor and
\pi^{*}(-D)=-\pi^{*}(D)
. Moreover,
\pi^{*}(D)
coincides with
\pi^{*}(D)
of Definition
1.3
.

(2)

Let
D,E\in\operatorname{GDiv}(Y)
be generalized divisors, such that
E
is Cartier. Then,
\pi^{*}(D+E)=\pi^{*}(D)+\pi^{*}(E)
.

(3)

Let
V\subset Y
be an open subset, and denote with
\pi_{U}
the restriction of
\pi
to
U=\pi^{-1}(V)\subset X
. Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}(Y)
be a generalized divisor on
Y
. Then,

(\pi_{U})^{*}(D_{|V})=\pi^{*}(D)_{|U}

(4)

Let
D,D^{\prime}\in\operatorname{GDiv}(Y)
be generalized divisors such that
D\sim D^{\prime}
. Then,
\pi^{*}(D)\sim\pi^{*}(D^{\prime})
.

Proof.

To prove (1), consider
D=E-F
with
E,F
effective and
F
Cartier by Lemma
3.3
on
Y
. Since
D
is Cartier and
E=D+F
, then also
E
is Cartier. By Definition
5.14
,

\pi^{*}(D)=\pi^{*}(E)-\pi^{*}(F).

By Lemma
5.13
, it is a difference of Cartier divisors and hence it is Cartier. To compute
\pi(-D)
, note that since
F
is Cartier then
-D=F-E
, and it is a difference of effective Cartier divisors; then, apply Definition
5.14
to obtain

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(-D)

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}(F)-\pi^{*}(E)=

\displaystyle=-\pi^{*}(D).

To compare
\pi^{*}(D)
with Definition
1.3
, let
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
be the ideal sheaves of
E
and
F
and let
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an open cover of
Y
such that
\mathcal{I}_{|V_{i}}and

\mathcal{J}_{|V_{i}}
are principal ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{Y|V_{i}}
-modules generated by regular sections
s_{i}
and
t_{i}
of
\Gamma(V_{i},\mathcal{O}_{Y})
, respectively on each
i\in I
. The fractional ideal of
D
is generated on each
V_{i}
by the meromorphic regular section
u_{i}=s_{i}/t_{i}
of
\Gamma(V_{i},\mathcal{K}_{Y})
. By Definition
5.11
, the ideal sheaves of
\pi^{*}(E)
and
\pi^{*}(F)
are generated on each
U_{i}=\pi^{-1}(V_{i})
by
\pi_{U_{i}}^{\sharp}(s_{i})
and
\pi_{U_{i}}^{\sharp}(t_{i})
respectively. Then, by Definition
5.14
, the fractional ideal of
\pi^{*}(D)
is generated on each
U_{i}
by the meromorphic regular section
\pi_{U_{i}}^{\sharp}(s_{i})/\pi_{U_{i}}^{\sharp}(t_{i})
of
\Gamma(U_{i},\mathcal{K}_{X})
. These are exactly the local generators for
\pi^{*}(D)
as defined in Definition
1.3
.

To prove (2), consider
D=D_{1}-D_{2}
and
E=E_{1}-E_{2}
, with
D_{1},D_{2},E_{1},E_{2}
effective and
D_{2},E_{2}
Cartier. Note that
D+E=(D_{1}+E_{1})-(D_{2}+E_{2})
, and it is a difference of effective divisors with
D_{2}+E_{2}
Cartier. Then, applying Definition
5.14
and Lemma
5.13
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(D+E)

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}((D_{1}+E_{1})-(D_{2}+E_{2}))=

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}(D_{1}+E_{1})-\pi^{*}(D_{2}+E_{2})=

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}(D_{1})+\pi^{*}(E_{1})-\pi^{*}(D_{2})-\pi^{*}(E_{2})=

\displaystyle=(\pi^{*}(D_{1})-\pi^{*}(D_{2}))-(\pi^{*}(E_{1})-\pi^{*}(E_{2}))=

\displaystyle=\pi^{*}(D)+\pi^{*}(E).

To prove (3), if
D
is effective, the result follows the fact that the inverse image functor
\pi^{-1}
commutes with restrictions. Then, observe that the operations of product and inverse of fractional ideals also commute with restrictions.

To prove (4), let
f\in\Gamma(Y,\mathcal{K}_{Y})
be a global section that generates a principal divisor
E=(f)\in\operatorname{Prin}(Y)
such that
D=D^{\prime}+E
. The inverse image
\pi^{*}(E)
is the principal divisor
(\pi^{\sharp}(f))\in\operatorname{Prin}(X)
. Then, by Lemma
5.13
,
\pi^{*}(D)=\pi^{*}(D^{\prime})+(\pi^{\sharp}(f))
; so
\pi^{*}(D)
and
\pi^{*}(D^{\prime})
are linearly equivalent.
∎

We are now ready to define the inverse image for generalized line bundles.

Definition/Lemma 5.16
.

(Inverse image for generalized line bundles)
The inverse image for generalized divisors induce a inverse image map between the sets of generalized line bundles, defined as:

\displaystyle[\pi^{*}]:\hskip 20.00003pt\operatorname{GPic}(Y)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{GPic}(X)

\displaystyle[D]

\displaystyle\mapsto[\pi^{*}(D)].

Proof.

Recall that for any curve
X
, the set
\operatorname{GPic}(X)
can be seen equivalently as the set of generalized line bundles or as the set of generalized divisors modulo linear equivalence. Here,
[\pi^{*}]
is defined in terms of generalized divisors modulo linear equivalence. By Proposition
5.15
, the direct images of linearly equivalent divisors are linearly equivalent, hence
[\pi^{*}]
is well defined.
∎

Remark 5.17
.

If
\mathcal{L}
is a generalized line bundle on
Y
and
D
is a generalized divisor with fractional ideal
\mathcal{I}
isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}
, then the inverse image divisor
\pi^{*}(D)
has fractional ideal isomorphic to the pullback sheaf
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{I})
by Remark
5.12
. Since
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{I})
is isomorphic to
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{L})
as abstract
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules, we conclude that the inverse image
[\pi^{*}](\mathcal{L})
of the generalized line bundle
\mathcal{L}
is equal to the pullback sheaf
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{L})
.

We prove now a result on the composition of the direct image with the inverse image of generalized divisors.

Proposition 5.18
.

(Composition of the direct image with the inverse image)

Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}(Y)
be a generalized divisor on
Y
. Then,

\pi_{*}(\pi^{*}(D))=n\cdot D.

Proof.

Since both of the terms are linear with respect to the sum of Cartier divisor, we can suppose that
D
is effective with ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. First note that, from the exact sequence:

0\rightarrow\pi^{*}\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\rightarrow\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{D}\rightarrow 0

together with Remark
5.12
, we obtain
\mathcal{O}_{X}/(\pi^{-1}\mathcal{I}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X})=\mathcal{O}_{X}/\pi^{*}\mathcal{I}=\pi^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}/\mathcal{I})
. To prove the thesis, we show that the equality

\operatorname{Fitt}_{0}\big(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\pi^{*}\mathcal{I})\big)=\mathcal{I}^{n}

holds locally around any point
y\in Y
. Let
V\subseteq Y
be an open neighborhood of
y
such that
(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})_{|V}\simeq(\mathcal{O}_{Y|V})^{\oplus n}
and
\mathcal{I}_{|V}
is generated by sections
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}
of
\Gamma(V,\mathcal{I})
. Then, consider the following presentation:

\mathcal{O}_{Y|V}^{\oplus r}\xrightarrow{(\cdot s_{1},\dots,\cdot s_{r})}\mathcal{O}_{Y|V}\rightarrow(\mathcal{O}_{Y}/\mathcal{I})_{|V}\rightarrow 0.

Pulling back with
\pi^{*}
, we obtain the following exact sequence on
U=\pi^{-1}(V)
:

\mathcal{O}_{X|U}^{\oplus r}\xrightarrow{(\cdot s_{1},\dots,\cdot s_{r})}\mathcal{O}_{X|U}\rightarrow(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\pi^{*}\mathcal{I})_{|U}\rightarrow 0.

In order to compute
\operatorname{Fitt}_{0}\big(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\pi^{*}\mathcal{I})\big)_{|V}
, we consider then the pushforward sequence:

\big(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus r}\big)_{|V}\xrightarrow{(\cdot s_{1},\dots,\cdot s_{r})}\big(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\big)_{|V}\rightarrow\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\pi^{*}\mathcal{I})_{|V}\rightarrow 0.

Since
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\pi^{*}\mathcal{I})_{|V}\simeq\big(\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus n}\big)_{|V}
, the map on the left is represented by the following
n\times nr
matrix with entries in
\Gamma(V,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
:

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\begin{matrix}s_{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&s_{1}\end{matrix}&\dots&\begin{matrix}s_{n}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&s_{n}\end{matrix}\end{array}\right].

Now,
\operatorname{Fitt}_{0}\big(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\pi^{*}\mathcal{I})\big)_{|V}
is generated by the
n\times n
minors of
M
, i.e. all the possible products of
n
generators of
\mathcal{I}
on
V
, with ripetitions. This shows that
\operatorname{Fitt}_{0}\big(\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}/\pi^{*}\mathcal{I})\big)_{|V}=\mathcal{I}_{|V}^{n}
.
∎

5.3.
Relation with the degree

In this subsection we show that
\pi_{*}
preserves the degree of divisors under the condition that
Y
is smooth over
k
. In general, however, the direct image of a generalized divisor
D
may not have the same degree of
D
, as the following example shows. Since we are dealing with degrees, we
assume that
X
and
Y
are projective curves over a base field

k
.

Example 5.19
.

Fix
k
an algebraically closed field. Let
A=k[x,y]/(y^{2}-x^{4})
be the affine coordinate ring of a curve
X=\operatorname{Spec}A
with a tacnode at the point
P
corresponding to the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}=(x,y)
.

The involution
\sigma
on
A
defined by
x\mapsto-x,y\mapsto y
induces a involution
\sigma_{X}
on the curve
X
. The geometric quotient
Y=X/\sigma_{X}
is an affine curve with coordinate ring equal to the ring of invariants
A^{\sigma}=k[x^{2},y]/(y^{2}-x^{4})
, that is isomorphic to
B=k[s,t]/(t^{2}-s^{2})
putting
s\mapsto x^{2}
and
t\mapsto y
. The quotient curve
Y
has a simple node at the point
Q
corresponding to the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{q}=(s,t)
.

The inclusion map
A^{\sigma}\subset A
gives to
A
the structure of free
B
-module, with basis
\{1,x\}
; so, the corresponding morphism of curve
\pi:X\rightarrow Y=X/\sigma_{X}
is a finite, locally free map of degree
2
sending
P
to
Q
.

Let
D
be the generalized divisor on
X
defined by the ideal
I=(x^{2},y)\subset A
; note that
D
is supported only on the tacnode
P
. Since we want to compare
\deg(D)=\deg_{P}(D)
with
\deg(\pi_{*}(D))=\deg_{Q}(\pi_{*}(D))
, we can restrict to work locally around
P
and
Q
. Let
B_{\mathfrak{q}}
be the local ring of
Y
at
Q
. The induced map
B_{\mathfrak{q}}\rightarrow A_{\mathfrak{p}}
makes
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
a free
B_{\mathfrak{q}}
-module of rank 2.

Let
E=A_{\mathfrak{p}}/I_{\mathfrak{p}}\simeq k[x]/(x^{2})
be the local ring of
D
at the
P
. We have:

\deg_{P}(D)=\ell(E)=2.

Observe that
E
has the following free presentation as
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module:

A_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{(\cdot x^{2},\cdot y)}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow E\rightarrow 0.

Since
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a free
B_{\mathfrak{q}}
-module of rank 2,
E
has also a presentation as
B_{\mathfrak{q}}
-module:

B_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\oplus 4}\xrightarrow{\varphi}B_{\mathfrak{q}}^{\oplus 2}\rightarrow E\rightarrow 0

where

\varphi=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc}s&0&t&0\\
0&s&0&t\end{array}\right].

Then, the 0-th Fitting ideal of
E
as
B_{\mathfrak{q}}
-module is
F_{0}(E)=(s^{2},t^{2},st)\subset B_{\mathfrak{q}}
. We have:

\deg_{Q}(\pi_{*}(D))=\ell(B_{\mathfrak{q}}/F_{0}(E))=3.

Note that there are divisors of degree 2 on
X
, supported at
P
, whose direct image has degree
2
on
Y
. For example, take
D^{\prime}=(x)
.

Remark 5.20
.

The previous example shows, in particular, that Proposition 2.33 in
[
Vas04
]
is false.

We now prove that the degree is preserved if the direct image is Cartier. We show first a proposition that computes the degree at any point where the direct image is locally principal.

Proposition 5.21
.

(Degree of the direct image of a generalized divisor)

Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}(X)
be a generalized divisor on
X
and let
y\in Y
be a point in codimension 1 of the support of
\pi_{*}(D)
. Suppose that
\pi_{*}(D)
is locally principal at
y
. Then,

\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(D))=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D).

Proof.

First, suppose that
D
is effective. Let
V=\operatorname{Spec}(B)
be an affine open neighborhood of
y
with affine pre-image
U=\pi^{-1}(V)=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, and let
I\subset A
denote the ideal of
D
restricted to
U
. The coordinate ring of
D
on
U
is the Artin ring
A/I
whose spectrum is equal to
\operatorname{Spec}(A)\cap\operatorname{Supp}(D)=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{s}\}
; hence we have:

A/I=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(A/I)_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\subset B
denote the maximal ideal corresponding to
y
in
\operatorname{Spec}(B)
and let
B_{\mathfrak{q}}
be the associated local ring of dimension 1. Then, the localization of
A/I
at
\mathfrak{q}
, denoted
E
, is the coordinate ring of
D
restricted to the fiber of
y
:

E=(A/I)_{\mathfrak{q}}=\prod_{\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\mathfrak{q}}(A/I)_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}.

Since
\pi_{*}(D)
is effective, the degree of
\pi_{*}(D)
at
y
is:

\displaystyle\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(D))

\displaystyle=\ell\big(\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}/F_{0}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{D})_{y}\big)[\kappa(y):k]=

\displaystyle=\ell\big(B_{\mathfrak{q}}/F_{0}(E)\big)[\kappa(y):k].

By hypothesis
F_{0}(E)
is an invertible module, so by
[
Vas04
, Proposition 2.32]
, we have
\ell\big(B_{\mathfrak{q}}/F_{0}(E)\big)=\ell(E)
.
On the other hand, thanks to
[
Sta19
,
Tag 02M0
]
,
note that:

\displaystyle\ell(E)

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\prod_{\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\mathfrak{q}}(A/I)_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\right)=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\mathfrak{q}}\ell(A_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}/I_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}})][k(\mathfrak{p}_{i}):k(\mathfrak{q})]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\ell\big(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathcal{I}_{x}\big)[\kappa(x):\kappa(y)]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D)[\kappa(x):\kappa(y)].

Putting everything together we have:

\displaystyle\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(D))

\displaystyle=\ell(E)[\kappa(y):k]=

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D)[\kappa(x):\kappa(y)]\right)[\kappa(y):k]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D)[\kappa(x):k]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D)

for
D
effective generalized divisor on
X
.

Let now
D=E-F
be a generalized divisor, written as a difference of effective generalized divisors with
F
Cartier by Lemma
3.3
. Since
\pi_{*}(F)
is Cartier, using the result for effective divisors estabilished before, we have:

\displaystyle\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(D))

\displaystyle=\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(E)-\pi_{*}(F))=

\displaystyle=\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(E))-\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(F))=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\left[\deg_{x}(E)-\deg_{x}(F)\right]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D).

∎

Corollary 5.22
.

Let
D\in\operatorname{GDiv}(X)
be a generalized divisor on
X
such that
\pi_{*}(D)
is Cartier. Then,

\deg_{Y}(\pi_{*}(D))=\deg_{X}(D).

Proof.

Applying Definition
3.4
and Proposition
5.21
, we get:

\displaystyle\deg_{Y}(\pi_{*}(D))

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\textrm{ cod 1}}\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(D))=

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\textrm{ cod 1}}\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D).

By the properties of the Fitting image,
\pi_{*}(D)
is supported on the set-theoretic image of the support of
D
; hence, the
x
appearing in the last sum are all the
x
for which
\deg_{x}(D)
is not zero. Then, the previous sum gives:

\displaystyle\deg_{Y}(\pi_{*}(D))

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\textrm{ cod 1}}\deg_{x}(D)=\deg_{X}(D).

∎

Corollary 5.23
.

Suppose that
Y
is smooth. Then, for any generalized divisor
D
on
X
,

\deg_{Y}(\pi_{*}(D))=\deg_{X}(D).

Proof.

Let
D
be a generalized divisor on
X
. The direct image
\pi_{*}(D)
is a generalized divisor on
Y
smooth, hence Cartier. Then, apply Corollary
5.22
.
∎

Proposition
5.21
yields another useful corollary about the surjectivity of the direct image morphism.

Corollary 5.24
.

(Surjectivity of the direct image for effective divisors)

Suppose that
Y
is smooth and
k
is algebraically closed. Then, the direct image for effective divisors:

\pi_{*}^{+}:\operatorname{GDiv}^{+}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{CDiv}^{+}(Y)

is surjective.

Proof.

Let
E\in\operatorname{CDiv}^{+}(Y)
be an effective Cartier divisor on
Y
and let
V=\operatorname{Spec}(R)\subseteq Y
be an affine open subset of
Y
such that
E
is supported on
V
. Let
\operatorname{Supp}(E)=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{r}\}
be the support of
E
and for each
i=1,\dots,r
let

d_{i}=\deg_{y_{i}}(E)/[\kappa(y_{i}):k]=\ell_{\mathcal{O}_{Y,y_{i}}}(\mathcal{O}_{E,y_{i}}).

Let
U=\operatorname{Spec}(S)=\pi^{-1}(V)
be the preimage of
V
. For each
i
, pick one element
x_{i}\in\pi^{-1}(y_{i})
in the finite fiber of
y_{i}
and let
\mathfrak{M}_{i}
be the maximal ideal of
S
corresponding to the point
x_{i}
. Then, the ideal
I=\mathfrak{M}_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdot\dots\cdot\mathfrak{M}_{r}^{d_{r}}
in
S
defines a divisor
D
on
U
(and hence of
X
) such that
\deg_{y_{i}}(E)=\deg_{x_{i}}(D)
. Looking at its direct image
\pi_{*}(D)
, it is an effective divisor on
Y
with the same support of
E
and the same degree at any point by Propostion
5.21
. Since
Y
is smooth, we conclude that
\pi_{*}(D)=E
.
∎

The direct and inverse image for families of generalized divisors

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
be a
finite, flat map of degree
n
between projective curves over a field
k
. In the present section, we discuss the definition of direct and inverse image for families of effective generalized divisors. Under suitable conditions, recalling Definition
3.7
, we aim to define a pair of geometric morphisms:

\displaystyle\pi_{*}

\displaystyle:\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{Hilb}_{Y}

\displaystyle\pi^{*}

\displaystyle:\operatorname{Hilb}_{Y}\rightarrow\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}

that, on
k
-valued points, coincide with the direct and inverse image between
\operatorname{GDiv}^{+}(X)
and
\operatorname{GDiv}^{+}(Y)
.

Remark 6.1
.

Giving a definition of the direct image for families of effective generalized divisors is not possible in general when the curve
Y
is not smooth over
k
. Consider, for example, the setting of Example
5.19
; since
X
and
Y
are reduced curves with planar singularities, their Hilbert schemes of generalized divisors of given degree are connected (see
[
AIK77
]
,
[
BGS81
]
). The effective divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
on
X
defined by
(x^{2},y)
and
(x)
on
X
have both degree
2
, but their direct images on the quotient node

have degree respectively equal to
3
and
2
. Then, their

-points
D_{1},D_{2}
in the connected component
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{2}
of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}
are sent to different connected components of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{Y}
. This shows that
the direct image of divisors in general is not defined as a geometric map
.

In the rest of the section, consider
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
a finite, flat map of degree
n
between projective curves over
k
, and
suppose that
Y
is smooth over
k
. Recall that, in such case,
\operatorname{Hilb}_{Y}=\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}
.

Definition/Lemma 6.2
.

(Direct image for families of effective generalized divisors)
Let
T
be any
k
-scheme. The
direct image map for the Hilbert scheme of effective generalized divisors
is defined on the
T
-valued points as:

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(T):\hskip 20.00003pt\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}(T)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}(T)

\displaystyle D\subseteq X\times_{k}T

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathcal{Z}\left(\operatorname{Fitt}_{0}(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{O}_{D}))\right)

where
\pi_{T}:X\times_{k}T\rightarrow Y\times_{k}T
is the morphism induced by base change of
\pi
.

Proof.

Let
D\subseteq X\times_{k}T
be a
T
-flat family of effective divisors of
X
, defined by an ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}
such that
\mathcal{O}_{D}=\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}/\mathcal{I}
is flat over
S
. From the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{D}\rightarrow 0

we deduce that also
\mathcal{I}
is flat over
T
. Since
\pi
is finite and flat,
\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{I})
is also flat over
T
, fiberwise locally free since
Y
is smooth and hence locally free on
Y\times_{k}T
by
[
HL10
, Lemma 2.1.7]
. Moreover, it fits the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{I})\xrightarrow{\varphi}\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T})\rightarrow\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{O}_{D})\rightarrow 0.

Then, by Definition
4.1
, the
0
-th Fitting ideal of
\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{O}_{D})
is the image of the canonical injection

\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{I})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)^{-1}\xhookrightarrow{\det(\varphi)}\mathcal{O}_{Y\times_{k}T}

and this is locally free over
Y\times_{k}T
, hence flat over
T
. Then, it defines a
T
-flat family of effective divisors of
Y
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

For any
T
-family of effective generalized divisors
D\subseteq X\times_{k}T
and for any point
t\in T
, the fiber
\pi_{*}(T)(D)_{t}
is equal to the direct image
\pi_{*}(D_{t})
defined for the effective divisor
D_{t}
on
X
. Moreover, since
Y
is smooth, by Corollary

we have:

\deg_{Y}(\pi_{*}(D_{t}))=\deg_{X}(D_{t}).

Then, for any

,
\pi_{*}
restricts to a map:

\pi_{*}^{d}:\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{d}\longrightarrow\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}^{d}.

Definition/Lemma 6.4
.

(Inverse image for families of effective generalized divisors)
Let
T
be any
k
-scheme. The
inverse image map for the Hilbert scheme of effective Cartier divisors
is defined on the
T
-valued points as:

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(T):\hskip 20.00003pt\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}(T)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}(T)

\displaystyle D\subseteq Y\times_{k}T

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathcal{Z}\left(\pi_{T}^{-1}(\mathcal{I}_{D})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)

where
\pi_{T}:X\times_{k}T\rightarrow Y\times_{k}T
is the morphism induced by base change of

and

is the ideal sheaf of

.

Since
\mathcal{I}
is locally principal,
\pi_{T}^{-1}(\mathcal{I})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}
is locally principal. The restriction of
\pi^{*}(T)(D)
to the fiber over any

has ideal sheaf
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{I}_{t})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}t}
, which is equal to the defining ideal of

by Definition

. Hence,
\pi^{*}(T)(D)
is a locally principal subscheme of

, such that all fibers over

are effective Cartier divisors by Lemma
5.13
. Then,
\pi^{*}(T)(D)
is
T
-flat by
[
Sta19
,
Tag 062Y
]
, hence it is a
T
-family of Cartier divisors.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

For any integer
d\geq 0
,
\pi^{*}
restricts to a map:

\pi^{*}_{d}:\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}^{d}\longrightarrow\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}^{nd}.

We study now some properties of the direct and inverse image for families of effective generalized divisors. With a slight abuse of notation, we will write
\pi_{*}
and
\pi^{*}
instead of

and

, when it is clear that we are working on

-points.

Proposition 6.6
.

(Properties of the direct and inverse image for families of effective generalized divisors)

Let

be any
k
-scheme. Then, the following fact holds.

(1)

Let

be
T
-families of effective divisors over
X
such that

is a family of Cartier divisors. Then,
\pi_{*}(D+E)=\pi_{*}(D)+\pi_{*}(E)
.

(2)

Let

be

-families of effective divisors over

. Then,

.

(3)

If

The proof of parts (1) and (2) follows the proofs of the second part of Lemma

∎

7.
The Norm and the inverse image for families of torsion-free rank-1 sheaves

In the present section, we provide the definition of the Norm map for families torsion-free sheaves of rank 1 and the related inverse image map. Since we are dealing with families of sheaves, we
assume that
X
and
Y
are projective curves over a field
k
.

In order to be compatible with the direct image for generalized line bundles, our definition of the Norm map on
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)
will be inspired by the sheaf-theoretic formula of Proposition
1.7
. By Proposition
5.8
and its corollaries, the generalization of such formula to generalized divisors and torsion-free sheaves involves taking the double
\omega
-dual of the exterior power of the pushforward of generalized line bundles. In general, this operation does not behave well in families if
Y
is not smooth.

Then, in accordance with the previous section, we
suppose that
Y
is smooth over
k
; in such case, the moduli space
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(Y)
is actually equal to the Jacobian
J(Y)
. Then, the aim of this section is to provide a pair of geometric morphism:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}:\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)

\displaystyle\rightarrow J(Y)

\displaystyle\pi^{-1}:J(Y)

\displaystyle\rightarrow J(X)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)

that, on
k
-valued points, coincide with the direct and inverse image maps between
\operatorname{GPic}(X)
and
\operatorname{GPic}(Y)=\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
.

Recall also (Definition
3.8
) that, for any line bundle
M
on
X
, the compactified Jacobian of
X
is related to the Hilbert scheme of effective generalized divisors via the twisted Abel map
\mathcal{A}_{M}
. We will show that the direct image map and the Norm map are compatible as well as the inverse image maps, meaning that for any
M\in J(X)
and
N\in J(Y)
there are commutative diagrams of
k
-schemes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\textit{G}\mathbb{J}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\subseteq}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(Y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(M)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(X).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\pi^{*}(N)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}}

We give first the definition for the Norm map on
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)
.

Definition/Lemma 7.1
.

(Norm map for torsion-free rank-1 sheaves)
Let
T
be any
k
-scheme. The
Norm map between compactified Jacobians
associated to
\pi
is defined on the
T
-valued points as:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(T):\hskip 20.00003pt\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)(T)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow J(Y)(T)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
T
-family of torsion-free sheaves of rank 1 on
X
, i.e. a
T
-flat coherent sheaf on
X\times_{k}T
, whose fibers over
T
are torsion-free sheaves of rank 1. The push-forward
\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L})
is a
T
-flat coherent sheaf on
Y\times_{k}T
such that, for any
t\in T
, the fiber
(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{L})_{t}
equals
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{t})
on
Y\simeq Y\times_{k}{t}
, . Since
Y
is smooth,
\pi_{*}(\mathcal{L}_{t})
is a locally free sheaf of rank
n
for any
t\in T
. Then, by
[
HL10
, Lemma 2.1.7]
,
\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L})
is a locally sheaf of rank
n
on
Y\times_{k}T
. Its determinant bundle is a line bundle on
Y\times_{k}T
, hence flat over
T
.
∎

The definition of the inverse image is standard but we give it for the sake of completeness.

Definition/Lemma 7.2
.

(Inverse image map for line bundles)
Let
T
be any
k
-scheme. The
inverse image map between Jacobians
associated to
\pi
is defined on the
T
-valued points as:

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(T):\hskip 20.00003ptJ(Y)(T)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow J(X)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)(T)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\pi_{T}^{*}(\mathcal{N}).

Proof.

By hypothesis,
\mathcal{N}
is a
T
-flat coherent sheaf on
Y\times_{k}T
that is a line bundle on any fiber over
T
; then, by
[
HL10
, Lemma 2.1.7]
, it is a line bundle on
Y\times_{k}T
. We conclude that
\pi^{*}\mathcal{N}
is a line bundle on
X\times_{k}T
and hence a
T
-flat family of line bundles over
T
.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

When there is no ambiguity, we will write
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}
and
\pi^{*}
instead of
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(T)
and
\pi^{*}(T)
. The Norm and the inverse image for generalized line bundles define morphisms of algebraic stacks

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}:\hskip 20.00003pt\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow J(Y)

\displaystyle\pi^{*}:\hskip 20.00003ptJ(Y)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow J(X).

For any integer
d
, they restricts to:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{d}:\hskip 20.00003pt\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X,d)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow J^{d}(Y)

\displaystyle\pi^{*}_{d}:\hskip 20.00003ptJ^{d}(Y)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow J^{nd}(X).

Moreover, the Norm for torsion-free rank-1 sheaves, restricted to the locus of line bundles

, coincides with the classical Norm map from
J(X)
to
J(Y)
of Definition
1.10
.

We study now some properties of the Norm map on
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)
and the inverse image map. First, we need a technical lemma.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
T
be a fixed
k
-scheme. For any
T
-flat family
\mathcal{L}
of torsion-free rank-1 sheaves on
X\times_{k}T
and any line bundle
\mathcal{M}
on

, there is an isomorphism:

\displaystyle\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)\simeq

\displaystyle\simeq\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{M})\right).

Proof.

The proof is similar to the second part of the proof of Proposition
5.8
.

Since
\pi_{T}:X\times_{k}T\rightarrow Y\times_{k}T
is finite and flat,
\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L})
is a
T
-flat coherent sheaf on
Y\times_{k}T
, that is locally free on any fiber over
T
since
Y
is smooth; then,
\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L})
is locally free of rank
n
by
[
HL10
, Lemma 2.1.7]
. The same holds for
\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})
. On the other hand, by
[
Gro61
, Proposition 6.1.12]
,
\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{M}
is a locally free
\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module of rank
1
. In particular, there is open cover
\{V_{i}\}_{I}
of
Y
such that
\mathcal{M}
is a trivial
\mathcal{O}_{X|U_{i}}
-module on each
U_{i}=\pi_{T}^{-1}(V_{i})
, i.e. there are isomorphisms
\lambda_{i}:\mathcal{M}_{|U_{i}}\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\mathcal{O}_{X|U_{i}}
for each
i\in I
. On the intersections
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
, the collection
\{\lambda_{i}\circ\lambda_{j}^{-1}\}
of automorphisms of
{\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}}_{|U_{i}\cap U_{j}}
is a cochain that measures the obstruction for the
\lambda_{i}
’s to glue to a global isomorphism. We define now an isomorphism

\alpha:\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)\longrightarrow\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{M})\right)

by glueing a collection of isomorphisms
\alpha_{i}
defined on each
V_{i}
. To do so, we define each
\alpha_{i}
as the following composition of arrows:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\det\big(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})\big)\otimes\det(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X})\right)_{|V_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{L}\big)\otimes\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{M}\big)\right)_{|V_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\det\big(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})_{|U_{i}}\big)\otimes\det(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X|U_{i}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{L}_{|U_{i}}\big)\otimes\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{M}_{|U_{i}}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{L}_{|U_{i}}\big)\otimes\det(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X|U_{i}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{L}_{|U_{i}}\big)\otimes\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X|U_{i}}\big)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\lambda_{i}\big)\otimes\id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id\otimes\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\lambda_{i}^{-1}\big)}

i.e.
\alpha_{i}:=\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\lambda_{i}\big)\otimes\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\lambda_{i}^{-1}\big)
. Since
\det\big(\pi_{T,*}\_\big)
is functorial, the obstruction
\alpha_{i}\circ\alpha_{j}^{-1}
is trivial on any

, whence the

’s glue together to a global isomorphism

.
∎

Proposition 7.5
.

(Properties of the Norm and the inverse image map)

Let
T
be any
k
-scheme. Then, the following facts hold.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{M}\in\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)(T)
such that
\mathcal{M}
is a
T
-flat family of line bundles. Then,
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})\simeq\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{L})\otimes\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{M})
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\in J(Y)(T)
. Then,
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{\prime})\simeq\pi^{*}(\mathcal{N})\otimes\pi^{*}(\mathcal{N}^{\prime})
.

(3)

Let
\mathcal{N}\in J(Y)(T)
. Then,
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{N})
is a
T
-flat family of line bundles over
X
and
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{N}))\simeq\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}
.

Proof.

To prove (1), note that
\mathcal{M}
is a
T
-flat coherent sheaf on
X\times_{k}T
that is a line bundle on any fiber over
T
; hence it is a line bundle on
X\times_{k}T
by
[
HL10
, Lemma 2.1.7]
. Then, applying Definition
7.1
and Lemma
7.4
, we have:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{M})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)^{-1}\simeq

\displaystyle\simeq\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{L})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\mathcal{M})\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)^{-2}\simeq

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{L})\otimes\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{M}).

Part (2) follows from the associative properties of the tensor product.

To prove (3), compute by Definitions
7.1
and
7.2
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{N}))=\det\left(\pi_{T,*}(\pi_{T}^{*}(\mathcal{N}))\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)^{-1}.

By the projection formula
[
Sta19
,
Tag 01E8
]
and the standard properties of determinants, we have:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{N}))

\displaystyle=\det\left(\mathcal{N}\otimes\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)^{-1}\simeq

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{N}^{n}\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)\otimes\det\left(\pi_{T,*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{k}T}\right)^{-1}\simeq

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{N}^{n}.

∎

We compare now the Norm and the inverse image maps between the compactified Jacobians with the direct and inverse image maps between the Hilbert schemes of divisors, respectively.

Let

Proposition 7.7
.

(Comparison of the inverse image maps via the Abel map)

For any line bundle

of degree

on

and any

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}^{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}^{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(Y,-d+e)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(X,-d+e)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}_{d}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{N}^{d}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}_{-d+e}}

Proof.

Let
T
be any
k
-scheme, let

be a

-flat family of divisors of degree

on

with ideal sheaf

, and denote with

the pullback of

to

. By Definition

\displaystyle\pi^{*}_{d}(\mathcal{A}_{N}^{d}(D))

\displaystyle=\pi_{T}^{*}(\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{N})\simeq\pi_{T}^{*}(\mathcal{I})\otimes\pi_{T}^{*}(\mathcal{N})\simeq

\displaystyle\simeq\mathcal{A}_{\pi^{*}(N)}^{d}(\pi^{*}_{-d+e}(\mathcal{N})).

∎

8.
The fibers of the Norm map and the Prym stack

In the present section we study the fibers of the Norm map defined in Section
7
. The purpose is to generalize the Prym scheme associated to a finite, flat morphism between projective curves to the context of torsion-free sheaves of rank 1. Fix an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic 0. Let
X,Y
be projective curves over
k
, such that
Y
is smooth, and let
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
be a finite, flat morphism of degree
n
. We start by recalling the following definition.

Definition 8.1
.

The
Prym scheme
of
X
associated to
\pi
is the locus
\Pr(X,Y)\subseteq J(X)
of line bundles whose Norm with respect to
\pi
is trivial. In other words:

\Pr(X,Y)=\{\mathcal{L}\in J(X):\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{L})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Y}\}.

We can now extend the definition of the Prym stack to the context of torsion-free rank 1 sheaves using the Norm map defined in the previous section.

Definition 8.2
.

The
Prym stack
of
X
associated to
\pi
is the locus
\overline{\Pr}(X,Y)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)
of torsion-free rank-1 sheaves whose Norm with respect to
\pi
is trivial. In other words:

\overline{\Pr}(X,Y):=\{(\mathcal{L},\lambda):\mathcal{L}\in\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)\ \mathrm{and}\ \lambda:\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{L})\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\}.

Remark 8.3
.

It follows from the definition that:

\Pr(X,Y)=\overline{\Pr}(X,Y)\cap J(X).

Since being locally-free is an open condition,
\Pr(X,Y)
is open in
\overline{\Pr}(X,Y)
.

The Prym stack is a fiber of the Norm map associated to
\pi
. In the present section, we study the fibers of the Norm map assuming that the curve
X
is reduced with locally planar singularities. The study of such fibers involves the study of the fibers of the direct image map for effective divisors defined in Section
7
, and the study of the fibers of the Hilbert-Chow morphism. We start from the last.

8.1.
The fibers of the Hilbert-Chow morphism

In this section, we refer to
[
Ber12
]
for notations and known results.

Definition 8.4
.

Let
X
be a projective curve over
k
and let
d
be a positive integer. The
d
-
symmetric power

X^{(d)}
of
X
is the quotient
X^{d}/\Sigma_{d}
of the Cartesian product
X^{n}
by the action of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{d}
in
d
-letters permuting the factors. Note that, for
d=1
,
X^{(1)}=X
. For
d=0
, we put
X^{(0)}=\{0\}
.
The
scheme of effective
0
-cycles
(or of
effective Weil divisors
) of
X
is the algebraic scheme:

\operatorname{WDiv}^{+}(X)=\bigsqcup_{d\geq 0}X^{(d)}=\bigsqcup_{d\geq 0}\operatorname{WDiv}^{d}(X).

The
Hilbert-Chow morphism
of degree
d
associated to
X
is the map of schemes

\displaystyle\rho_{X}^{d}:\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{d}

\displaystyle\rightarrow X^{(d)}

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{x\textrm{ cod 1}}\deg_{x}(D)\cdot[x]

Denote with
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}\rho}_{X}^{d}
the restriction of
\rho_{X}^{d}
to the subscheme
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}^{d}\subseteq\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}^{d}
parametrizing Cartier divisors. The collection of Hilbert-Chow morphisms in non-negative degrees gives rise to the Hilbert-Chow morphism in any degree:

\rho_{X}:\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{WDiv}^{+}(X).

Finally, denote with
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}\rho}_{X}
the restriction of
\rho_{X}
to
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}\subseteq\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}
.

Remark 8.5
.

If
X
is a smooth projective curve, then
\rho_{X}
is an isomorphism and coincides with
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}\rho}_{X}
.

We study now the fibers of the Hilbert-Chow morphism in the case when
X
is reduced with locally planar singularities.

Proposition 8.6
.

Suppose that
X
is reduced with locally planar singularities and that
Y
is smooth. Let
W\in\operatorname{WDiv}^{d}(X)
be an effective Weil divisor of degree
d
on
X
and let
\rho_{X}^{-1}(W)
be the corresponding fiber in
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}
. The locus
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}\rho}_{X}^{-1}(W)
of Cartier divisors in
\rho_{X}^{-1}(W)
is an open and dense subset of
\rho_{X}^{-1}(W)
.

Proof.

First, note that
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}\rho}_{X}^{-1}(W)=\rho_{X}^{-1}(W)\cap\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}
. Since
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}\subseteq\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}
is open (see for example
[
MRV17
, Fact 2.4]
),
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}\rho}_{X}^{-1}(W)
is open in
\rho_{X}^{-1}(W)
.

To prove that it is dense, let
W=\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\cdot[x_{i}]
with the
x_{i}
’s are
s
distinct points. For each
i
, the fiber of
\rho_{X}
over the cycle
n_{i}\cdot[x_{i}]
is equal by definition to the punctual Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X,x_{i}}^{n_{i}}
parametrizing
0
-dimensional subschemes of
X
supported at
x_{i}
having length
n_{i}
over
k
. Since the
x_{i}
are distinct, we have:

\displaystyle\rho_{X}^{-1}(W)

\displaystyle=\rho_{X}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\cdot[x_{i}]\right)=

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\rho_{X}^{-1}(n_{i}\cdot[x_{i}])=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{Hilb}_{X,x_{i}}^{n_{i}}.

For each
i
, let
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X,x_{i}}^{n_{i}}=\operatorname{Hilb}_{X,x_{i}}^{n_{i}}\cap\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}
. An effective divisor is Cartier if and only if it is Cartier at each point of its support, hence:

\operatorname{{}^{\ell}\rho}_{X}^{-1}(W)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(\operatorname{Hilb}_{X,x_{i}}^{n_{i}}\cap\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X,x_{i}}^{n_{i}}.

For each
i
,
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X,x_{i}}^{n_{i}}
is the smooth locus of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X,x_{i}}^{n_{i}}
by
[
BGS81
, Proposition 2.3]
, so it is dense; hence
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}\rho}_{X}^{-1}(W)
is dense in
\rho_{X}^{-1}(W)
.
∎

8.2.
The fibers of the direct image between Hilbert schemes

In the present subsection, we study the fibers of the direct image map
\pi_{*}
defined between the Hilbert schemes of generalized divisors of
X
and
Y
.

First, we introduce a similar notion for Weil divisors and we see how it relates to the direct image for generalized divisors.

Definition 8.7
.

The
direct image for Weil divisors
associated to
\pi
is the morphism of schemes:

\pi_{*}:\operatorname{WDiv}^{+}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{WDiv}^{+}(Y)

given on the level of points by

\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}\cdot[x_{i}]\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}\cdot[\pi(x_{i})].

Proposition 8.8
.

Assume that
Y
is smooth. There is a commutative square of schemes over
k
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{WDiv}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{WDiv}(Y).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}

Proof.

Let
D
be an effective generalized divisor on
X
. Following the bottom-left side of the square, we obtain:

\pi_{*}(\rho_{X}(D))=\sum_{x\textrm{ cod 1}}\deg_{x}(D)\cdot[\pi(x)],

while on the top-right side of the square we have

\rho_{Y}(\pi_{*}(D))=\sum_{y\textrm{ cod 1}}\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(D))\cdot[y].

Since
Y
is smooth,
\pi_{*}(D)
is Cartier locally at each point of
Y
. By Proposition
5.21
, for any point
y
in codimension 1 of
Y

\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(D))=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D).

Then we compute:

\displaystyle\rho_{Y}(\pi_{*}(D))

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\textrm{ cod 1}}\deg_{y}(\pi_{*}(D))\cdot[y]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\textrm{ cod 1}}\left(\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D)\right)\cdot[y]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\textrm{ cod 1}}\sum_{\pi(x)=y}\deg_{x}(D)\cdot[\pi_{*}(x)]=

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\textrm{ cod 1}}\deg_{x}(D)\cdot[y]=\pi_{*}(\rho_{X}(D)).

∎

We are now ready to study the fibers of the direct image for generalized divisors.

Proposition 8.9
.

Assume that
Y
is smooth and
X
is reduced with locally planar singularities and . Let
E\in\operatorname{Hilb}_{Y}
be an effective divisors of degree
d
on
Y
and let
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)
be the corresponding fiber in
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}
. Then,
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)\cap\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}
is an open and non-empty dense subset of
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
8.8
, there is a commutative diagram of schemes over
k
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{WDiv}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{WDiv}(Y).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}

First note that, since
Y
is smooth, the map
\pi_{*}
is surjective by Corollary
5.24
, hence
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)
is non-empty. Moreover, the Hilbert-Chow morphism
\rho_{Y}
is an isomorphism. Then,

\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)=\rho_{X}^{-1}\pi_{*}^{-1}(\rho_{Y}(E)).

Let
S=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{r}\}
be the support of
E
and let
d_{i}=\deg_{y_{i}}E
for each
i
; then

\rho_{Y}(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\cdot[y_{i}].

For each
i
, denote with
\{x_{i}^{1},\dots,x_{i}^{n_{i}}\}
the discrete fiber of
\pi
over
y_{i}
. Since points can occur with multeplicity in the geometric fibers,
n_{i}\leq n
holds for each
i
. The fiber of
\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\cdot[y_{i}]
is then the discrete subset of
\operatorname{WDiv}(X)
given by:

\pi_{*}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\cdot[y_{i}]\right)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}c_{ij}\cdot[x_{i}^{j}]\ \middle|\ c_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ \sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}c_{ij}=d_{i}\textrm{ for any }i\right\}.

Denote with
Z
the set of all tuples
(c_{ij})
of positive integers with the conditions that
\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}c_{ij}=d_{i}
for any
i
. Then, we can write the previous set as

\pi_{*}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\cdot[y_{i}]\right)=\bigsqcup_{(c_{ij})\in Z}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}c_{ij}\cdot[x_{i}^{j}]\right\}.

Then, applying
\rho_{*}^{-1}
, we otbain:

\displaystyle\rho_{X}^{-1}\pi_{*}^{-1}(\rho_{Y}(E))

\displaystyle=\rho_{X}^{-1}\pi_{*}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}\cdot[y_{i}]\right)=

\displaystyle=\rho_{X}^{-1}\left(\bigsqcup_{(c_{ij})\in Z}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}c_{ij}\cdot[x_{i}^{j}]\right\}\right)=

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{(c_{ij})\in Z}\rho_{X}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}c_{ij}\cdot[x_{i}^{j}]\right),

where the last disjoint union is a disjoint union of topological subspaces of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}
in the same connected component. Then,
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)
is a non-emtpy disjoint uniont of a finite number of fibers of the Hilbert-Chow morphism. By Proposition
8.6
, the intersection of any such fiber with the Cartier locus
\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}
is open and dense in the same fiber. Taking the disjoint union,
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)\cap\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}
is a non-empty open and dense subset of
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)
.
∎

8.3.
The fibers of the Norm map and
\overline{\Pr}(X,Y)

In this subsection, assuming that
Y
is smooth and
X
is reduced with locally planar singularities, we prove that the generalized Prym of
X
with respect to
Y
is non-empty, open and dense in the Prym stack of
X
with respect to
Y
.
The theorem is based upon the following two auxiliaries proposition.

Proposition 8.10
.

Assume that
Y
is smooth and
X
is reduced with locally planar singularities and let
\mathcal{N}\in J(Y)
be any line bundle on
Y
. Then, there is a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
such that
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{L})\simeq\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a Cartier divisor on
Y
such that
\mathcal{N}\in[D]
and let
D=E-F
where
E,F
are effective Cartier divisors by Lemma
3.3
. By Proposition
8.9
there are effective Cartier divisors
H,K
on
X
such that
\pi_{*}(H)=E
and
\pi_{*}(K)=F
. Then, by Lemma
5.2

\pi_{*}(H-K)=D
. Set
H-K=C
and let
\mathcal{L}
be the defining ideal of
C
, seen as a line bundle. By Lemma
5.9
we conclude that
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(\mathcal{L})\simeq\mathcal{N}
.
∎

Proposition 8.11
.

Assume that
Y
is smooth and
X
is reduced with locally planar singularities and let
\mathcal{N}\in J(Y)
be any line bundle on
Y
. Then, the fiber
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})
is non-empty and contains
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})\cap J(Y)
as an open and dense substack.

Proof.

First, note that
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)=\operatorname{\textit{G}\mathbb{J}}(X)
since
X
is reducible. The fiber
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})
is non-empty by Proposition
8.10
and the substack
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})\cap J(Y)
is open in
\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})
since being locally free is an open condition. To prove that it is dense, recall that for any fixed line bundle
M\in J(X)
there is a commutative diagram of stacks over
k
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Hilb}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(Y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(M)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}}

Moreover, by
[
MRV17
, Proposition 2.5]
there is a cover of
\overline{\mathbb{J}}(X)
by
k
-finite type open subsets
\{U_{\beta}\}
such that, for each
\beta
, there is a line bundle
M_{\beta}\in J(X)
with the property that
\mathcal{A}_{M_{\beta}|V_{\beta}}:\mathcal{A}_{M_{\beta}}^{-1}(U_{\beta})=V_{\beta}\rightarrow U_{\beta}
is smooth and surjective.

Since density can be checked locally, fix
M=M_{\beta}
,
U=U_{\beta}
and
V=V_{\beta}
. Then, we have:

\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})\cap U=\mathcal{A}_{M}\left(V\cap\pi_{*}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(M)}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\right)\right)

and

\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})\cap J(X)\cap U=\mathcal{A}_{M}\left(V\cap\pi_{*}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(M)}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\right)\cap\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}\right).

Put
\textbf{K}=\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}(M)}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. Then, the topological space underlying
\pi_{*}^{-1}(\textbf{K})
contains the topological union of fibers of the points in
K
, so that:

\pi_{*}^{-1}(\textbf{K})\supseteq\bigcup_{E\in\textbf{K}}\pi_{*}^{-1}(E).

By Proposition
8.9
,
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)\cap\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}
is a non-empty open and dense subscheme of
\pi_{*}^{-1}(E)
for any closed point
E\in\textbf{K}
. Hence,
\pi_{*}^{-1}(\textbf{K})\cap\operatorname{{}^{\ell}Hilb}_{X}
is non-empty, open and dense in
\pi_{*}^{-1}(\textbf{K})
. Intersecting with
V
and composing with
\mathcal{A}_{M}
, we get the thesis.
∎

We finally come to Prym stack of
X
with respect to

. Recall that by definition:

\displaystyle\Pr(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})\cap J(X),

\displaystyle\overline{\Pr}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Nm}_{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y}).

Corollary 8.12
.

\Pr(X,Y)
is non-empty, open and dense in
\overline{\Pr}(X,Y)
.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
and apply Proposition
8.11
.
∎

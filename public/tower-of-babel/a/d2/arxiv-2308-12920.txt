Abstract
We show that cancellation of free modules holds in the stable class Ω 3 ​ ( ℤ ) \Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) over dihedral groups of order 4 ​ n 4n . In light of a recent result on realizing k k –invariants for these groups, this completes the proof that all all dihedral groups satisfy the D(2) property.

Introduction

In 1965 Wall showed that for
n>2
, if a finite cell complex is cohomologically
n
dimensional (in the sense of having no non-trivial cohomology in dimensions above
n
with respect to any coefficient bundle), then it is in fact homotopy equivalent to an actual
n
dimensional cell complex
[
18
]
. Subsequently it was shown by Swan and Stallings that the only cohomologically 1 dimensional finite cell complexes are disjoint unions of wedges of circles
[
15
,
16
]
. However decades later the case
n=2
remains a major open problem, known as Wall’s D(2)–problem.

The problem may be phrased in terms of realizing algebraic complexes geometrically
[
7
,
10
,
12
]
, or in terms of group presentations
[
13
]
. Cell complexes which potentially offer a counterexample to Wall’s D(2)–problem have been postulated
[
6
,
1
]
, though proving that they are counterexamples would appear to require some new obstruction. Where progress has been made is extending Wall’s result to the case
n=2
for all finite cell complexes with a specified fundamental group.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a group
G
satisfies the
D(2) property
if all cohomologically 2 dimensional finite cell complexes with fundamental group
G
are in fact homotopy equivalent to a 2 dimensional finite cell complex.

At present, the D(2) property in known to hold only in comparatively few cases. These include cyclic groups, products of the form
C_{\infty}\times C_{n}

[
2
]
and, more relevantly to the present paper, the dihedral groups
D_{4n+2}

[
5
,
7
]
.

The first finite nonabelian non periodic group shown to have the D(2) property was the dihedral group of order 8
[
9
]
. The methodology adopted in
[
9
]
was an instance of the approach developed and laid out in
[
7
]
. This approach for verifying the D(2) property for a given group
G
involves two steps.

The first of these is to classify all possible modules over
\mathbb{Z}[G]
which may arise as the second homotopy group of a connected
cohomologically 2 dimensional finite cell complex
X
. The Hurewicz homomorphism identifies
\pi_{2}(X)
with
H_{2}(\tilde{X})
. As
X
is cohomologically 2 dimensional, consideration of the homotopy type of the algebraic cellular chain complex of
\tilde{X}
yields an exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}[G]
modules:

0\to\pi_{2}(X)\to F_{2}\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}\to 0,

where the
F_{i}
are finitely generated (henceforth denoted f.g.​) free
[
11
]
modules
[
7
, Appendix B]
. Thus by Schanuel’s lemma all such
\pi_{2}(X)
are stably isomorphic.

This stable class of modules, denoted
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
, is discussed extensively in
[
7
]
, in particular in §29. For our purposes it is sufficient to recall that
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
may be viewed as a directed tree, with modules represented by vertices and partitioned into levels, which are well-ordered. If
G
is finite then the level of a module is determined simply by its
\mathbb{Z}
–rank. An element of
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is called
minimal
if it occurs at the minimal level.

For modules over
\mathbb{Z}[D_{8}]
one may use a strong cancellation result of Swan
[
17
, Theorem 6.1]
to deduce that
\pi_{2}(X)\cong J\oplus\mathbb{Z}[D_{8}]^{r}
for some integer
r
, where
J
is the ‘standard’ minimal element of the stable class
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
(see (
2.1
)).

Having classified all possible modules
K
which may arise as
\pi_{2}(X)
in this way, the second step is to show that for each
K
, any cohomologically 2 dimensional finite cell complex
X
with
\pi_{2}(X)\cong K
, is homotopy equivalent to a 2 dimensional cell complex. This involves realizing the so called ‘
k
–invariants’
[
7
, Chapter 6]
.

In
[
9
]
the second step is completed for dihedral groups of order
2^{n}
, and

K\cong J\oplus\mathbb{Z}[D_{2^{n}}]^{r}
for any
r
. Recently these methods have been extended to the more general case of all dihedral groups of order
4n
:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
14
, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 2.6]
Suppose that
X
is a cohomologically 2 dimensional finite cell complex with fundamental group
D_{4n}
and
\pi_{2}(X)\cong J\oplus\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{r}
, for some integer
r
. Then
X
is homotopy equivalent to a 2 dimensional finite cell complex.

This key result is what our proof is predicated on. In order to prove that the remaining dihedral groups satisfy the D(2) property, it remains to show that over
\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]
any module in
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
must be of the form
J\oplus\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{r}
. This is our main result.

Theorem A

Over
\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]
any module in
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is of the form
J\oplus\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{r}
.

We note that this has been proved for modules in
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
which are geometrically realizable as the second homotopy group of an actual 2–complex
[
8
,
3
]
. However from the point of view of the D(2)–problem, showing the existence of such a 2–complex is a key difficulty, so it is not clear if these methods could be extended to address the D(2)–problem for
D_{4n}
.

Having proved Theorem A, we may conclude from
[
5
,
7
]
and Theorem 1.2:

Theorem B

All finite dihedral groups satisfy the D(2) property.

Outline of Proof of Theorem A
: In §
2
we note that the only modules in
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
which may not be of the required form must occur at the minimal level. We then observe that any such minimal element of
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is the kernel of some surjective map
j\colon\thinspace\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{3}\to W_{2}
, where
W_{2}\subset\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{2}
is the image of the ‘standard’
\partial_{2}
.

In §
3
we consider separately the components
j^{\prime},j^{\prime\prime}
of the map
j
into the first and second summands of
\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{2}
. By Schanuel’s Lemma we know that ker
(j^{\prime\prime})
is stably equivalent to ker
(\partial_{2}^{\prime\prime})
, which we denote
M
. However
M
contains a free summand, so by the Swan–Jacobinski Theorem we know that ker
(j^{\prime\prime})\cong M
. The kernel of
j
is just the kernel of the restriction
j^{\prime}|_{{\rm ker}(j^{\prime\prime})}
. The image of this restriction of
j^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the module
\mathbb{Z}[C_{2}]
, so we may conclude that any minimal element of
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
is in fact the kernel of a surjective homomorphism
M\to\mathbb{Z}[C_{2}]
.

In §
4
we construct isomorphisms between kernels of such maps, using elementary matrices and other means. In particular we define modules
K_{x,1}
for integers
x
and show that any kernel of a surjective homomorphism
M\to\mathbb{Z}[C_{2}]
is isomorphic to
K_{l,1}
, for
l
some positive factor of
2n
.

In §
5
we construct maps
{{\partial_{2}}^{(l)}}\colon\thinspace\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{3}\to\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{2}
for
l
a positive factor of
2n
. In each case ker
({{\partial_{2}}^{(l)}})=K_{l,1}
. Also
{{\partial_{2}}^{(1)}}={{\partial_{2}}}
which has kernel
J
. It remains to show that the kernels of the remaining
{{\partial_{2}}^{(l)}}
are not stably isomorphic to
J
.

We do this by noting that the kernel of
{{\partial_{2}}^{(l)}}
is stably isomorphic to
J
only if the cokernel of
{{\partial_{2}}^{(l)}}
is stably isomorphic to
I
, the augmentation ideal. As cancellation of free modules holds in the stable class of the augmentation ideal, this is the same as saying that the cokernel of
{{\partial_{2}}^{(l)}}
is isomorphic to the augmentation ideal.

For
l>1
we show that this is not the case, leaving
J
as the sole minimal element of
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Minimal elements of
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

Fix the presentation
D_{4n}=\langle a,b|\,a^{2n}=b^{2}=1,\,\,aba=b\rangle.

We work throughout over the integral group ring
\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]

\displaystyle 0\to J\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{Z}\to 0.

\displaystyle\partial_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Sigma&0&1+ba\\

\displaystyle 0\to K\oplus F\to\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{3}\oplus F^{\prime}\to W_{2}\to 0.

Lemma 2.1
.

The inclusion in

)

\iota\colon\thinspace F\to\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{3}\oplus F^{\prime}

Proof.

The cokernel of

Thus

Lemma 2.2
.

Any minimal element of
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
occurs as the kernel of some surjective map
\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\longrightarrow}}W_{2}

Thus in order to prove our main result (Theorem A), it suffices to show that any kernel of a surjective map

3
Restricting to submodules

In this section we take an arbitrary surjection
\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\longrightarrow}}W_{2}

\mathbb{Z}[D_{4n}]^{3}
and
W_{2}

. In the next section we classify all possible kernels of surjective maps between these submodules. Thus we will have a comprehensive list of all modules which may be a minimal element of

Recall from (

1.
Introduction

The metastable behavior of continuous-time Markov chains has attracted
some interest in recent years. We refer to the monographs
[
51
,
13
,
32
,
37
]
for the latest developments. In this article, we
propose to investigate the Markov chains metastable behaviour from an
analytical perpective, by showing that the Markov chains semigroup and
large deviations rate function encode the metastable properties of the
process. The main results explain how to extract from these
functionals the metastable time-scales, states and wells.

To tackle this problem we consider a sequence of continuous-time
Markov chains
(X^{(n)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
evolving on a finite state space

V
. Under a natural hypothesis on the jump rates of these chains,
stated in equation (
2.4
) below, we prove the existence of

(a)

time-scales
\theta^{(1)}_{n},\dots,\theta^{({\mathfrak{q}})}_{n}

such that, as
n\to\infty
,
\theta^{(1)}_{n}\to\infty
,

\theta^{(p+1)}_{n}/\theta^{(p)}_{n}\to\infty
for
1\leq p<{\mathfrak{q}}
;

(b)

and metastable states
\pi^{(p)}_{1},\dots,\pi^{(p)}_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}}
,

1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
.

The parameter
p
is called the level and indicates the depth of the
wells or, equivalently, the time-scale at which a metastable behaviour
is observed. The metastable states
\pi^{(p)}_{j}
are probability
measures on
V
. It will be shown that, for each fixed level
p
, the
support of the measures
\pi^{(p)}_{1},\dots,\pi^{(p)}_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}}
are
disjoint. They represent the wells among which the process
X^{(n)}_{t}

evolves in the time-scale
\theta^{(p)}_{n}
. The number of metastable
set decreases as the time-scales increase:
{\mathfrak{n}}_{p+1}<{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}
. A
metastable state at level
p+1
is a convex combination of metastable
states at level
p
: for each
1\leq p<{\mathfrak{q}}
and

1\leq m\leq{\mathfrak{n}}_{p+1}
,

\pi^{(p+1)}_{m}=\sum_{j}\omega^{(m)}_{j}\pi^{(p)}_{j}
for some
probability measure
\omega^{(m)}
on
\{1,\dots,{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}\}
.

The first main result of this article, Theorem
3.1
.(b), states
that for all
t>0
,
x\in V
, the distribution of

X^{(n)}_{t\theta^{(p)}_{n}}
starting from
x
converges to a convex
combination of the measures
\pi^{(p)}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}
. More
precisely, denote by
p^{(n)}_{t}(x,y)
the transition probabilities of
the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
. Then, for each
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
,
t>0
,

x\in V
, there exists a probability measure

\omega^{(p)}_{t,x}(\,\cdot\,)
on
\{1,\dots,{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}\}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}p^{(n)}_{t\theta^{(p)}_{n}}(x,\,\cdot\,)\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}}\omega^{(p)}_{t,x}(j)\;\pi^{(p)}_{j}(\,\cdot\,)\;.

(1.1)

The weights
\omega^{(p)}_{t,x}(j)
of this convex combination
naturally depend on
x
and
t
, and are obtained by a recursion
procedure.

Theorem
3.1
also characerises the asymptotic behavior of the
transition probabilities at all intermediate time-scales

\beta_{n}
. Fix
0\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
, set
\theta^{(0)}_{n}=1
,

\theta^{({\mathfrak{q}}+1)}_{n}=+\infty
, and consider a sequence
\beta_{n}

such that
\beta_{n}/\theta^{(p)}_{n}\to\infty
,

\beta_{n}/\theta^{(p+1)}_{n}\to 0
. Theorem
3.1
provides a formula
for the limit of
p^{(n)}_{\beta_{n}}(x,\,\cdot\,)
as
n\to\infty
.
It corresponds to the limit obtained in (
1.1
) by letting

t\to\infty
after
n\to\infty
.

Freidlin and Koralov
[
20
]
, after
[
3
]
and
[
43
]
,
examined sequences of Markov chains on finite state spaces under the
same hypothesis (
2.4
) assumed below and taken from
[
3
,
43
]
. Their main results describes the asymptotic behavior of the
transition probabilities at the intermediate time-scales
\beta_{n}

introduced above. These results demonstrate the interest of the theory
developed in
[

,
5
,
35
,
50
,
37
]
, which permits to
investigate the asymptotic behavior of the Markov chain exactly at
the metastable time-scale, and not just before or after it.

We turn to the large deviations. Denote by
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
the level two
large deviations rate functional of the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
, as

t\to\infty

[
55
]
. Under the hypothesis of reversibility, the
second main result of this article provides a
\Gamma
-expansion of
the functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
as

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\;=\;{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}\;+\;\sum_{p=1}^{{\mathfrak{q}}}\frac{1}{\theta^{(p)}_{n}}\,{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}\;.

(1.2)

This expansion has to be understood in the sense that
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
,

\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
,
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
,
\Gamma
-converge to

{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
,
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}
, respectively. The rate functionals

{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}
take finite values only at convex combinations of the
metastable states
\pi^{(p)}_{j}
:
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}(\mu)<\infty
if, and
only if,
\mu=\sum_{j\in S_{p}}\omega_{j}\,\pi^{(p)}_{j}
for some
probability measure
\omega
in
S_{p}
.

Therefore, both the semigroup and the level two large deviations rate
functionals encode all characteristics of the metastable behaviour of
a Markov chain. They provide the time-scales, the metastable states
and the wells. In particular, it becomes a natural problem to prove
such an expansion in other contexts.

We believe that the inductive approach presented here provides a
general method to derive these results, as well as the metastable
behavior in the classical sense
[
1
]
, of Markov chains with
wells of different depths, even if the state space is not fixed, as
assumed here. To be applied, one needs (a) to show that the process
quickly reaches one of the wells (the initial step of the induction
procedure) and (b) to compute the capacities (
2.7
) and the
asymptotic jump rates (
2.10
).

More precisely, inspecting the proof of Theorem

reveals that
it essentially relies on the convergences of the generator of the
trace process on the wells (more exactly on the convergence of the
average rates
r^{(p)}_{n}(i,j)
introduced in (
2.9
) below). Since
this convergence has been obtained in many different contexts, by
following the strategy proposed here it should be possible to derive
the metastable
\Gamma
-expansion of the large deviations level two
rate function for dynamics in which the state space is not fixed.

This includes random walks in potential fields
[
39
,
41
]
,
condensing zero-range models
[
2
,
31
,
53
]
, inclusion
processes
[
25
,
17
,
9
,
27
,
28
]
, or statistical
mechanical models in which the volume grows as the temperature
decreases. For example, the Curie-Weiss model in random environment

[
11
,
10
]
, the Blume-Capel model
[
34
]
, the Potts
model
[
40
,
30
,
44
]
, or the Kawasaki dynamics for the
Ising model
[
24
]
.

In particular, it should be possible to apply this approach to
non-reversible diffusions in potential fields,
[

,
12
,

,

,

,

2.
The model

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}G=(V,E)
be a finite directed graph, where
V

represents the finite set of vertices, and
E
the set of directed
edges. Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}(X^{(n)}_{t}:t\geq 0)
,
n\geq 1
, a
sequence of
V
-valued, irreducible continuous-time Markov chains,
whose jump rates are represented by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}R_{n}(x,y)
. We assume
that
R_{n}(x,y)>0
for all
(x,y)\in E
and
n\geq 1
. The generator
reads as

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}({\mathscr{L}}_{n}f)(x)}\;=\;\sum_{y\colon(x,y)\in E}R_{n}(x,y)\,\{\,f(y)\,-\,f(x)\,\}\;.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda_{n}(x)
,
x\in V
, the holding rates of
the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
and by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}p_{n}(x,y)
,
x
,

y\in V
, the jump probabilities, so that

R_{n}(x,y)=\lambda_{n}(x)\,p_{n}(x,y)
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\pi_{n}
stand for the unique stationary state. The
so-called Matrix tree Theorem
[
21
, Lemma 6.3.1]
provides a
representation of the measure
\pi_{n}
in terms of arborescences of the
graph
(V,E)
.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}D({\mathbb{R}}_{+},W)
,
W
a finite set, the space
of right-continuous functions
{\mathfrak{x}}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to W
with left-limits
endowed with the Skorohod topology and the associated Borel

\sigma
-algebra. Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbf{P}}_{\!x}={\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}
,

x\in V
, be the probability measure on the path space
D({\mathbb{R}}_{+},V)

induced by the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
starting from
x
. Expectation
with respect to
{\mathbf{P}}_{\!x}
is represented by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbf{E}}_{x}
.

Denote by
p^{(n)}_{t}(x,y)
the transition probability of
the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}p^{(n)}_{t}(x,y)}\;:=\;{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[\,X_{t}\,=\,y\,\big]\;,\quad x\,,\,y\in V\;,\;\;t\,>\,0\;.

Since the chain is irreducible and
\pi_{n}
its stationary state, by
the ergodic theorem for finite state-spaces Markov chains,

\lim_{t\to\infty}p^{(n)}_{t}(x,y)\;=\;\pi_{n}(y)\;\;\text{for all $x$, $y\in V$}\;.

Longer time-scales

Assume that
\lim_{n}R_{n}(x,y)
exists for all
(x,y)\in E
, and denote
by
{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)\in[0,\infty)
its limit:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)}\;:=\;\lim_{n}R_{n}(x,y)\;,\quad(x,y)\,\in\,E\;.

(2.1)

Let
{\mathbb{E}}_{0}
be the set of edges whose asymptotic rate is positive:

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\,:=\,\{\,(x,y)\in E:{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)>0\,\}
, and assume that
{\mathbb{E}}_{0}\not=\varnothing
. The jump
rates
{\mathbb{R}}_{0}(x,y)
induce a continuous-time Markov chain on
V
,
denoted by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}({\mathbb{X}}_{t}:t\geq 0)
, which, of course, may be
reducible. Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{L}}^{(0)}
its generator.

Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{V}}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{V}}_{{\mathfrak{n}}}
,

{\mathfrak{n}}\geq 1
, the closed irreducible classes of
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
, and let

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S}\;:=\;\{1,\dots,{\mathfrak{n}}\}\;,\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{V}}}\;:=\;\bigcup_{j\in S}{\mathscr{V}}_{j}\;,\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Delta}\;:=\;V\,\setminus\,{\mathscr{V}}\;.

(2.2)

The set
\Delta
may be empty and some of the sets
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
may be
singletons.

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathbb{Q}}_{x}
be the probability measure on

D({\mathbb{R}}_{+},V)
induced by the Markov chain
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
starting from

x
.

For two sequences of positive real numbers
(\alpha_{n}:n\geq 1)
,

(\beta_{n}:n\geq 1)
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\alpha_{n}\prec\beta_{n}
or

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\beta_{n}\succ\alpha_{n}
means that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}=0
. Similarly,

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\alpha_{n}\preceq\beta_{n}
or

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\beta_{n}\succeq\alpha_{n}
indicates that either

\alpha_{n}\prec\beta_{n}
or
\alpha_{n}/\beta_{n}
converges to a positive
real number
a\in(0,\infty)
.

Let

\gamma_{n}\;:=\;\max_{(x,y)\in E\setminus{\mathbb{E}}_{0}}R_{n}(x,y)

so that
\gamma_{n}\prec 1
. Choose a sequence
\beta_{n}
such that

1\prec\beta_{n}\prec\gamma_{n}^{-1}
. Couple

X^{(n)}_{t}
and
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
making them jump as much as possible
together. Denote by
\widehat{{\mathbf{P}}}_{\!x}
the coupling
measure. Since
\beta_{n}\prec\gamma_{n}^{-1}
, for all
x\in V

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{{\mathbf{P}}}_{\!x}\,\big[\,{\mathbf{X}}^{(n)}_{t}\,=\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\;,0\leq t\leq\beta_{n}\,\big]\;=\;1\;.

In particular, for all
x
,
y\in V

\lim_{n\to\infty}p^{(n)}_{\beta_{n}}(x,y)\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{{\mathbf{P}}}_{\!x}\,\big[\,{\mathbb{X}}_{\beta_{n}}\,=\,y\,\big]\;=\;\sum_{j\in S}{\mathfrak{a}}^{(0)}(x,j)\,\pi^{\sharp}_{j}(y)\;,

where
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\pi^{\sharp}_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq{\mathfrak{n}}
, represents the
stationary states of the Markov chain
{\mathbb{X}}
restricted to
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}

and
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(0)}(x,j)
the probability that the chain
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}

starting from
x
is absorbed by the closed recurrent class
{\mathscr{V}}_{j}
:

{\mathfrak{a}}^{(0)}(x,j)\;:=\;\lim_{t\to\infty}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\!x}\,\big[\,{\mathbb{X}}_{t}\,\in\,{\mathscr{V}}_{j}\,\big]\;.

(2.3)

In the first part of this article, we investigate the asymptotic
behaviour of
p^{(n)}_{\beta_{n}}(x,y)
in different time-scales

\beta_{n}
. The definition of the time-scales and the description of
the asymptotic behaviour is based on a construction of a tree

[
3
,
43
]
presented after the statement of the main hypothesis
of the article.

The main assumption

Two sequences of positive real numbers
(\alpha_{n}:n\geq 1)
,

(\beta_{n}:n\geq 1)
are said to be

if

\alpha_{n}\prec\beta_{n}
,
\beta_{n}\prec\alpha_{n}

A set of sequences

(\alpha^{{\mathfrak{u}}}_{n}:n\geq 1)
,
{\mathfrak{u}}\in{\mathfrak{R}}
, of positive real
numbers, indexed by some finite set

, is said to be comparable

,
{\mathfrak{v}}\in{\mathfrak{R}}
the sequence

(\alpha^{{\mathfrak{u}}}_{n}:n\geq 1)
,
(\alpha^{{\mathfrak{v}}}_{n}:n\geq 1)
are

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
, and let
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Sigma_{m}
,

, be the set of functions

such that

that for every

the set of sequences

\big(\,\prod_{(x,y)\in E}R_{n}(x,y)^{k(x,y)}:n\geq 1\,\big)\;,\quad k\in\Sigma_{m}\;,

is comparable.

A rooted tree

In this subsection, we present the construction, proposed in

[
3
,
43
]
, of a rooted tree which describes all different
metastable behaviours of the Markov chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
. This
construction plays a fundamental role in the statement of the main
theorems of this article. The reader will find at the end of this
section a simple example which may help to understand the
construction.

The tree satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

Each vertex of the tree represents a subset of
V
;

(b)

Each generation forms a partition of
V
;

(c)

The children of each vertex form a partition of the parent.

(d)

The generation
p+1
is strictly coarser than the
generation
p
.

The tree is constructed by induction starting from the leaves to the
root. It corresponds to a deterministic coalescence process. Denote
by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathfrak{q}}
the number of steps in the recursive
construction of the tree. At each level
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
, the
procedure generates a partition

\{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}},\Delta_{p}\}
, a
time-scale
\theta^{(p)}_{n}
and a
\{1,\dots,{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}\}
-valued
continuous-time Markov chains
{\mathbb{X}}^{(p)}_{t}
which describes the
evolution of the chain
X^{(n)}_{t\theta^{(p)}_{n}}
among the subsets

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}}
, called hereafter

wells
.

The leaves are the sets
{\mathscr{V}}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{V}}_{{\mathfrak{n}}},\Delta

introduced in (
2.2
). We proceed by induction. Let

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{1}=S
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathfrak{n}}_{1}={\mathfrak{n}}
,

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{V}}^{(1)}_{j}={\mathscr{V}}_{j}
,
j\in S_{1}
,

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\Delta_{1}=\Delta
, and assume that the recursion has
produced the sets

{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{1},\dots,{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}},\Delta_{p}
for some

p\geq 1
, which forms a partition of
V
.

Denote by
H_{{\mathscr{A}}}
,
H^{+}_{{\mathscr{A}}}
,
{{\mathscr{A}}}\subset V
, the hitting
and return time of
{{\mathscr{A}}}
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}H_{{\mathscr{A}}}}\;:=\;\inf\big\{t>0:X^{(n)}_{t}\in{{\mathscr{A}}}\big\}\;,\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}H^{+}_{{\mathscr{A}}}}\;:=\;\inf\big\{t>\tau_{1}:X^{(n)}_{t}\in{{\mathscr{A}}}\big\}\;,

(2.5)

where
\tau_{1}
represents the time of the first jump of the chain

X^{(n)}_{t}
:

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\tau_{1}=\inf\{t>0:X^{(n)}_{t}\not=X^{(n)}_{0}\}
.

For two non-empty, disjoint subsets
{\mathscr{A}}
,
{\mathscr{B}}
of
V
, denote by

{\rm cap}_{n}({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{B}})
the capacity between
{\mathscr{A}}
and
{\mathscr{B}}
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\rm cap}_{n}({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{B}})}\;:=\;\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{A}}}\pi_{n}(x)\,\lambda_{n}(x)\,{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[\,H_{{\mathscr{B}}}<H^{+}_{{\mathscr{A}}}\,\big]\;.

(2.6)

Set
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}S_{p}=\{1,\dots,{\mathfrak{n}}_{p}\}
, and let

\theta^{(p)}_{n}
be defined by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\frac{1}{\theta^{(p)}_{n}}}\;:=\;\sum_{i\in S_{p}}\frac{{\rm cap}_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i},\breve{{\mathscr{V}}}^{(p)}_{i})}{\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i})}\;,\quad\text{where}\;\;{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\breve{{\mathscr{V}}}^{(p)}_{i}}\,:=\,\bigcup_{j\in S_{p}\setminus\{i\}}{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}\;.

(2.7)

The ratio

\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i})/{\rm cap}_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i},\breve{{\mathscr{V}}}^{(p)}_{i})

represents the time it takes for the chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
, starting from
a point in
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}
to reach the set
\breve{{\mathscr{V}}}^{(p)}_{i}
. Therefore,
\theta^{(p)}_{n}
corresponds to the smallest
time needed to observe such a jump.

Recall from (
A.1
) the definition of the trace of a Markov
chain. Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\{Y^{n,p}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
the trace of

\{X^{(n)}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
on
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}
, and by

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}R^{(p)}_{n}:{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}\times{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

its jump rates. By equation (2.5) in
[
32
]
,

R^{(p)}_{n}(x,y)\;=\;\lambda_{n}(x)\;{\mathbf{P}}^{n}_{\!x}\big[H_{y}=H^{+}_{{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}}\big]\;,\quad x\,,\;y\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}\,,\;x\not=y\;.

(2.8)

Denote by
r^{(p)}_{n}(i,j)
the mean rate at which the trace process
jumps from
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}
to
{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}
:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r^{(p)}_{n}(i,j)}\;:=\;\frac{1}{\pi_{n}({\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i})}\sum_{x\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{i}}\pi_{n}(x)\sum_{y\in{\mathscr{V}}^{(p)}_{j}}R^{(p)}_{n}(x,y)\;.

(2.9)

In this section, we enunciate the main results of the article. The

starting from

,

and

\pi^{(p)}_{j}

are the metastable states of the process
X^{(n)}_{t}
observed on the
time-scale
\theta^{(p)}_{n}

Part (b) of this theorem states that, starting from

Large deviations rate function expansion.

We assume from now on that the dynamics is reversible:
\pi_{n}(x)\,R_{n}(x,y)=\pi_{n}(y)\,R_{n}(y,x)
for all
(x,y)\in E
.
For a probability measure
\nu
on a finite space
W
and two
functions
f
,
g:W\to{\mathbb{R}}
, let

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\langle\,f\,,\,g\,\rangle_{\nu}}\;=\;\sum_{x\in W}f(x)\,g(x)\,\nu(x)\;.

By
[
55
]
, for each fixed
n\geq 1
, the occupation time
distribution of the chain
X^{n}_{t}
, defined
by

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{X^{n}_{s}}\;ds\;,

satisfies a large deviations principle as
t\to\infty
, the so-called
level 2 LDP. In this formula,
\delta_{x}
,
x\in V
, represents the
Dirac measure concentrated at
x
, so that

t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{X^{n}_{s}}\;ds
is a random element of

\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{P}}(V)
, the space of probability measures on
V
.
Denote by
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}:{\mathscr{P}}(V)\to[0,\infty]
the level two large
deviations rate function:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)}\;=\;-\,\inf_{u}\sum_{x\in V}\frac{({\mathscr{L}}_{n}u)(x)}{u(x)}\;\mu(x)\;,

(3.9)

where the infimum is performed over all functions

u:V\to(0,\infty)
. Since we assumed reversibility and

\pi_{n}(x)>0
for all
x\in V
, for all measures
\mu\in{\mathscr{P}}(V)
, by

[
19
, Theorem 5]
,

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)\;=\;\langle\,\sqrt{f_{n}}\,,\,(-\,{\mathscr{L}}_{n})\sqrt{f_{n}}\,\rangle_{\pi_{n}}\;,

(3.10)

where
f_{n}(x)=\mu(x)/\pi_{n}(x)
.

The second main result of this article provides an expansion of the
rate function
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
. Recall that we denote by
{\mathbb{L}}^{(0)}
the
generator of the Markov chain
{\mathbb{X}}_{t}
introduced right after
(
2.1
). Let
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}:{\mathscr{P}}(V)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be given by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}(\mu)}\;=\;-\,\inf_{u>0}\,\sum_{x\in V}\mu(x)\frac{({\mathbb{L}}^{(0)}u)(x)}{u(x)}\;,

(3.11)

where the supremum is carried over all functions

u:V\to(0,\infty)
. Theorem
3.3
below states that the
sequence of rate functions
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to

{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
. In (
8.8
), we show that

{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}(\mu)\,=\,0
if and only if there exists a probability
measure
\omega
on
S_{1}
such that

\mu\;=\;\sum_{j\in S_{1}}\omega_{j}\,\pi^{(1)}_{j}\;.

(3.12)

For such measures
\mu
, it is natural to consider the limit
\beta_{n}{\mathscr{I}}_{n}(\mu)
for some sequence
\beta_{n}\to\infty
.

Fix
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
. Denote by
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{P}}(S_{p})
the set of
probability measures on
S_{p}
. Let

{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}\colon{\mathscr{P}}(V)\to[0,+\infty]
be the functional given
by

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}(\mu)}\,:=\,\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\,\inf_{{\mathbf{h}}}\,\sum_{j\in S_{p}}\omega_{j}\,\frac{{\mathbb{L}}^{(p)}{\mathbf{h}}}{{\mathbf{h}}}\quad\text{if}\;\;\mu=\sum_{j\in S_{p}}\omega_{j}\,\pi^{(p)}_{j}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\omega\in{\mathscr{P}}(S_{p})\;,\\
&+\infty\quad\text{otherwise}\;.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.13)

In this formula, the infimum is carried over all functions

{\mathbf{h}}:S_{p}\to(0,\infty)
. We prove in (
8.12
) that

{\mathscr{I}}^{(p+1)}(\mu)\;<\;\infty\quad\text{if and only if}\quad{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}(\mu)\;=\;0\;.

By (
3.12
), this assertion holds also for
p=0
.

Recall the definition of
\Gamma
-convergence. We refer to
[
16
]

for an overview on this subject. Fix a Polish space
{\mathcal{X}}
and a
sequence
(U_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}})
of functionals on
{\mathcal{X}}
,

U_{n}\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to[0,+\infty]
. The sequence
U_{n}

\Gamma
-converges
to the functional

U\colon{\mathcal{X}}\to[0,+\infty]
if and only if the two following
conditions are met:

(i)

\Gamma
-liminf.
The functional
U
is a

\Gamma
-liminf for the sequence
U_{n}
: For each
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
and each
sequence
x_{n}\to x
, we have that
\liminf_{n}U_{n}(x_{n})\geq U(x)
.

(ii)

\Gamma
-limsup.
The functional
U
is a

\Gamma
-limsup for the sequence
U_{n}
: For each
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
there
exists a sequence
x_{n}\to x
such that
\limsup_{n}U_{n}(x_{n})\leq U(x)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The functional
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to
{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}
. Moreover,
for each
1\leq p\leq{\mathfrak{q}}
, the functional
\theta^{(p)}_{n}\,{\mathscr{I}}_{n}

\Gamma
-converges to
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}
.

This theorem provides an expansion of the large deviations rate
function
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
which can be written as

{\mathscr{I}}_{n}\;=\;{\mathscr{I}}^{(0)}\;+\;\sum_{p=1}^{{\mathfrak{q}}}\frac{1}{\theta^{(p)}_{n}}\;{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}\;.

(3.14)

Therefore, the rate function
{\mathscr{I}}_{n}
encodes all the characteristics
of the metastable behavior of the chain
X^{(n)}_{t}
. The time-scales

\theta^{(p)}_{n}
appear as the weights of the expansion, and the
meta-stable states
\pi^{(p)}_{j}
,
j\in S_{p}
, generate the space where
the rate functional
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}(\mu)
is finite. Indeed, by
(
8.9
),
{\mathscr{I}}^{(p)}(\mu)
is finite if and only if
\mu
is a
convex combination of the measures
\pi^{(p)}_{j}
,
j\in S_{p}
.

Theorem
3.3
extends to the context of continuous-time Markov
chains evolving on finite state-spaces a result by Di Gesù and
Mariani
[
23
]
proved for reversible diffusions with a single
valley at each different depth.

Remark 3.4
.

Theorem
3.3
should hold for nonreversible
dynamics. Reversibility is assumed here only to compute the

\Gamma
-limsup through formula (
3.10
). It should also be possible
to obtain a metastable
\Gamma
-expansion for the level 2.5 large
deviations rate function derived in
[
8
]
.

Remark 3.5
.

The proof of Theorems
3.1
and
3.3
do not require the full
strength of assumption (
2.4
), but only the ability to
compute some capacities, the limit of the ratio of some measures and
of mean jump rates. Stating, however, the minimal conditions would

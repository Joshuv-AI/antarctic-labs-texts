Resolvent Estimates in L ∞ L^{\infty} for the Stokes Operator in Nonsmooth Domains
Jun Geng, Zhongwei Shen

Abstract
We establish resolvent estimates in spaces of bounded solenoidal functions for the Stokes operator in a bounded domain Ω \Omega in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} under the assumptions that Ω \Omega is C 1 C^{1} for d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and Lipschitz for d = 2 d=2 . As a corollary, it follows that the Stokes operator generates a uniformly bounded analytic semigroup in the spaces of bounded solenoidal functions in Ω \Omega . The smoothness conditions on Ω \Omega are sharp. The case of exterior domains with nonsmooth boundaries is also studied. The key step in our proof involves new estimates that connect the pressure to the gradient of the velocity in the L q L^{q} average, but only on scales above certain level. Keywords : Resolvent Estimate; Stokes Operator; Bounded Function; Nonsmooth Domains. MR (2020) Subject Classification : 35Q30.

Introduction

This paper studies the resolvent problem for the Stokes operator with the Dirichlet condition,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
is a (given) parameter, and

\Sigma_{\theta}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}:\ |\text{arg}(z)|<\pi-\theta\right\}

(1.2)

for
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
.
By the Lax-Milgram theorem it follows that for any
F\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
, there exist a unique
u
in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
and a
scalar function
p\in L^{2}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
such that (
1.1
) holds in the sense of distributions.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the energy estimate,

|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(1.3)

where
C
depends on
d
and
\theta
.
In this paper we shall be interested in the
L^{q}
resolvent estimates for the Stokes operator,

|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

(1.4)

for
q\neq 2
.
Such estimates lead to the analyticity in the solenoidal
L^{q}
spaces of the Stokes semigroup, the solution operator to the nonstationary
Stokes equations.
They play a crucial role in the study of the nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations in the domain
\Omega\times(0,T)
and
have been investigated extensively in the last forty years.

If
\Omega
is a bounded or exterior domain with
C^{1,1}
boundary, the
L^{q}
resolvent estimate (
1.4
),
together with estimates of
\nabla^{2}u
and
\nabla p
in
L^{q}(\Omega)
,
holds for
1<q<\infty

[
29
,
20
,
7
,
14
]
.
See
[
14
]
for a review as well as a comprehensive list of references in the case of smooth domains.
Also see
[
21
,
13
,
27
,
32
]
and their references for related work and applications to the nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations.
If
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, it was proved in
[
28
]
by the second author of the present paper that
the resolvent estimate,

|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

(1.5)

holds if
d\geq 3
and

\Big|\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\Big|<\frac{1}{2d}+\varepsilon,

(1.6)

where
\varepsilon>0
depends on
\Omega
.
In particular, this shows that the estimate (
1.5
) holds if
(3/2)-\varepsilon<q<3+\varepsilon
and
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Using an approach similar to that in
[
28
]
, F. Gabel and P. Tolksdorf
[
15
]
were able to establish (
1.5
)
for
d=2
and
(4/3)-\varepsilon<q<4+\varepsilon
.
We mention that in
[
9
]
P. Deuring constructed a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
for which the estimate (
1.5
)
fails for large
q
.
Very recently, the present authors proved in
[
18
]
that if
\Omega
is a bounded or exterior
C^{1}
domain,
then the resolvent estimate (
1.4
) holds
for any
1<q<\infty
.
In view of the work
[
9
]
, the
C^{1}
condition on the domain is more or less sharp for
d\geq 3
.
The approach used in
[
18
]
is based on a perturbation argument.
A key step is to establish the resolvent estimate (
1.4
) in the region above a Lipschitz graph,

\mathbb{H}_{\psi}=\big\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\text{ and }x_{d}>\psi(x^{\prime})\big\}

(1.7)

under the assumption that
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}<c_{0}
for some small
c_{0}>0
depending on
d
,
\theta
and
q
.

We now move on to the case
q=\infty
, which has been a long-standing open problem.
The closely related problem regarding the analyticity of the Stokes semigroup in spaces of bounded solenoidal functions
was finally solved in
[
1
,
2
]
by K. Abe and Y. Giga for smooth domains, using a blow-up argument.
Previously, the result was only known for a half-space by explicit calculations
[
8
]
.
In
[
3
]
, using a more direct approach,
the resolvent estimate (
1.4
) was established for
q=\infty
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
by K. Abe, Y. Giga, and M. Hieber.
One of the key steps in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
relies on a pressure estimate,

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\delta(x)|\nabla p(x)|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},

(1.8)

where
\delta(x)=\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.
This estimate was proved in
[
1
,
2
]
for domains with
C^{3}
boundaries.
It was noted in
[
3
]
that the estimate holds in
C^{1,\alpha}
domains by using a result obtained independently in
[
25
]

in the study of elliptic homogenization problems.
As a result, the resolvent estimates in
[
3
]
hold for domains with
C^{2}
boundaries
(the other parts of the argument in
[
3
]
still require
C^{2}
).
Also see
[
22
,
6
]
for related work on the Stokes semigroup in
L^{\infty}
in smooth exterior domains.

In this paper we study the
L^{\infty}
resolvent estimates for the Stokes operator in
C^{1}
and Lipschitz domains.
Let

L^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=\left\{F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}):\ \text{\rm div}(F)=0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }F\cdot n=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\},

(1.9)

where
n
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
The following is one of the main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
, or a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
, where
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
.
Then for any
F\in L_{\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the solution of the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) in

W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})

satisfies the estimate,

|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(1.10)

Moreover, if
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{1,\alpha}
domain for some
\alpha>0
, then

|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(1.11)

The constants
C
in (
1.10
)-(
1.11
) depend only on
d
,
\theta
and
\Omega
.

A few remarks are in order.

Remark 1.2
.

The smoothness conditions on
\Omega
in Theorem
1.1
are sharp.
In view of the counter-example given in
[
9
]
, the
L^{\infty}
resolvent estimate (
1.10
) fails in a general Lipschitz domain for
d\geq 3
.
Also, the gradient estimate (
1.11
) cannot be expected in a
C^{1}
domain, as there is no boundary Lipschitz estimate in
C^{1}
domains
even for the Laplacian.

Remark 1.3
.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
by interpolation and duality that if
d=2
, the resolvent estimate (
1.5
)
holds in a Lipschitz domain for any
1<q<\infty
.
This extends the work
[
15
]
, which proved the estimate for
(4/3)-\varepsilon<q<4+\varepsilon
by
a different method.

Remark 1.4
.

If
\Omega
is
C^{1}
for
d\geq 3
or Lipschitz for
d=2
,
our proof of Theorem
1.1
yields the estimate for the pressure,

|\lambda|^{1/2}\delta(x)|\nabla p(x)|\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

(1.12)

for any
x\in\Omega
with
\delta(x)\geq|\lambda|^{-1/2}
.
Moreover, if
\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
, then

|\lambda|^{1/2}\|p\|_{B\!M\!O(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

(1.13)

and (
1.12
) holds for any
x\in\Omega
.
In this case, since
p
is harmonic in
\Omega
, by
[
12
]
,
one may further improve (
1.12
) to a Carleson measure estimate,

|\lambda|\int_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}\delta(x)|\nabla p(x)|^{2}\,dx\leq C\|F\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}r^{d-1}

(1.14)

for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
, where
r_{0}
is given by (
1.27
).

Let
C_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
denote the closure of

C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)=\left\{F\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d}):\ \text{\rm div}(F)=0\text{ in }\Omega\right\}

in
L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
If
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain,
it is known that

C_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)=\left\{F\in C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C}^{d}):\ \text{\rm div}(F)=0\text{ in }\Omega\text{ and }F=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\}

[
1
, Lemma 6.3]
.
For
F\in C_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
, define
u=R(\lambda)F
to be the solution of (
1.1
).
By
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
,
if
\Omega
is
C^{1}
, then
R(\lambda)F\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
for any
p>d
.
Moreover, by the results in Section
9
,
if
d=2
and
\Omega
is Lipschitz, we have
R(\lambda)F\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2})
for some
p>2
.
In both cases, we obtain
R(\lambda)F\in C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C}^{d})
by Sobolev imbedding. As a result,
it follows from the estimate (
1.10
) that
\{R(\lambda):\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}\}
is a pseudo-resolvent in
C_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
and that
there exists a closed operator
A
such that
R(\lambda)=(\lambda-A)^{-1}
.
As in
[
3
]
, we call
A
the Stokes operator in
C_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
.
The Stokes operator in
L^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
is defined in a similar manner.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
, or a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then the Stokes operator
A
generates a uniformly bounded analytic semigroup on
C_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)

of angle
\pi/2
. It also generates a (non-
C_{0}
) uniformly bounded analytic semigroup on
L^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
of angle
\pi/2
.

We now describe our approach to Theorem
1.1
, which is based on the method used by K. Abe, Y. Giga, and M. Hieber in
[
3
]

for
L^{\infty}
resolvent estimates in smooth domains.
This method was inspired by the works of K. Masuda
[
26
]
and H.B. Stewart
[
30
]
for general elliptic operators
in the space of continuous functions.
The basic idea is to localize the
L^{q}
resolvent estimates for some
q>d
and use the estimate (
1.8
) to control the error terms involving the pressure.
The major issue with this method for a nonsmooth domain is the lack of
L^{\infty}
estimates for
\nabla u
,
as both
\nabla u
and
p
are not expected to be bounded up to the boundary in a
C^{1}
domain.
Moreover, the main set-up form for a priori estimate,

\displaystyle M_{q}(u,p)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=|\lambda||u(x)|+|\lambda|^{1/2}|\nabla u(x)|+|\lambda|^{d/2q}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B(x,|\lambda|^{-1/2}))}+|\lambda|^{d/2q}\|\nabla p\|_{L^{q}(\Omega\cap B(x,|\lambda|^{-1/2}))},

used in
[
3
]
, involves
\nabla^{2}u
and
\nabla p
, which cannot be controlled even for
C^{1,\alpha}
domains.

We manage to resolve these issues by replacing (
1.8
) with several new and stronger estimates that relate the pressure to the gradient of the velocity
in the
L^{q}
average, but only on scales above a given level.
A key observation is that such estimates are only needed on scales above
|\lambda|^{-1/2}
when it is used to bound the
L^{\infty}
norm of
u

by an interpolation inequality.
With the
L^{q}
resolvent estimates we obtained recently in
[
18
]
for
1<q<\infty
,
this allows us to treat the case of
C^{1}
domains without the Lipschitz bound for the velocity.
In the case
d=2
, the approach, together with a perturbation argument, which upgrades the
L^{2}
estimates for
\nabla u
to
L^{q}
estimates for some
q>2
,
yields the
L^{\infty}
resolvent estimate for Lipschitz domains.
We mention that our new estimates for the pressure, which are of independent interest, are proved for Lipschitz domains.
By taking limits in the scale, it follows that the estimate (
1.8
) used in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
in fact holds for Lipschitz domains.
However, as we pointed out above, it is not strong enough for treating nonsmooth domains.

In the following we give a sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.1
and provide a more detailed
description of the new estimates mentioned above.

Step 1. We localize the generalized
L^{q}
resolvent estimates obtained in
[
18
]
for
1<q<\infty
in a graph domain
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
, given by (
1.7
),
where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function with small Lipschitz norm
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}
.
This, together with local regularity estimates for the Stokes equations (with
\lambda=0
) in
C^{1}
domains, allows us to show that for any
N\geq 2

and
d<q<\infty
,

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{4d+8}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

(1.15)

\displaystyle+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t<r<r_{0}\\
B(x_{0},2r)\subset\Omega\end{subarray}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{C}\end{subarray}}\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\\
t<r<r_{0}\end{subarray}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{C}\end{subarray}}\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\},

where
t=|\lambda|^{-1/2}
,
\Omega
is
C^{1}
, and
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.
See Sections
2
and
3
.

Step 2.
To bound the terms in (
1.15
) involving the pressure
p
, we study the Neumann problem for Laplace’s equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\phi&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}&=(n_{i}\partial_{j}-n_{j}\partial_{i})g_{ij}&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.16)

where
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain and the repeated indices
i,j
are summed from
1
to
d
.
Let
g=(g_{ij})
and
I(x_{0},r)=B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega
.
For
0<r<r_{0}
, we introduce a new maximal function,

M_{q}(g)(r)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\\
r\leq s<r_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(\fint_{I(x_{0},s)}|g|^{q}\right)^{1/q},

(1.17)

which measures the
L^{q}
average of
g
above the scale
r
.
Let
2\leq q<\infty
and
0<r<r_{0}
.
We show that

\sup_{B(x,2r)\subset\Omega}\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}|\phi-\fint_{B(x,r)}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

(1.18)

and that for
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|\phi-\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

(1.19)

\displaystyle+\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\phi-\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.
See Sections
4
and
5
.
Since
\phi
is harmonic, it follows from (
1.18
) that

\delta(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq CM_{q}(g)(\delta(x))

(1.20)

for any
x\in\Omega
, where
\delta(x)=\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.
In particular, this implies that

\sup_{x\in\Omega}\delta(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},

(1.21)

which gives (
1.8
) if
g_{ij}=\partial_{j}u_{i}
.
It is also interesting to point out that the estimate (
1.19
) implies

\|\phi\|_{B\!M\!O(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},

(1.22)

which is sharp, as
\phi
is related to
g
by some singular integral operator on
\partial\Omega
.

Step 3.
As in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, note that if
(u,p)
is a smooth solution of (
1.1
) with
F\in C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
then
\Delta p=0
in
\Omega
and

\nabla p\cdot n=(n_{i}\partial_{j}-n_{j}\partial_{i})\partial_{j}u_{i}\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

As a result, we may use estimates (
1.18
)-(
1.19
) to bound the last two terms in the right-hand side of (
1.15
)
and obtain

|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq CN^{4d+8}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|^{1/2}M_{q}(|\nabla u|)(t),

(1.23)

where
t=|\lambda|^{-1/2}
.
By using local regularity estimates for the Stokes equations in
C^{1}
domains, we deduce that for
t=|\lambda|^{-1/2}
,

M_{q}(|\nabla u|)(t)\leq C\left\{|\lambda|^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+|\lambda|^{-1/2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\},

(1.24)

where
C
depends on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
. See Lemma
3.6
.
By combining (
1.23
) with (
1.24
), we see that

|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}N^{4d+8}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+C_{0}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(1.25)

To finish the proof of (
1.10
), we fix
q>d
and choose
N
so large that
C_{0}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}\leq(1/2)
.

Step 4. If
\Omega
is
C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
,
the local estimates for the Stokes equations give

|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\{|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}

(1.26)

for
|\lambda|>r_{0}^{-2}
.
See Remark
3.8
.
The gradient estimate (
1.11
) follows from (
1.10
) and (
1.26
).

Step 5. A careful inspection of the argument outlined above indicates that the
L^{\infty}
resolvent estimate (
1.10
) holds in
\Omega
,
as long as (1) one is able to establish the generalized
L^{q}
resolvent estimates for
u
and
\nabla u
for some
q>d
;
(2) the local boundary
L^{q}
estimate holds for the Stokes equations (with
\lambda=0
)
in
\Omega
for some
q>d
, which is needed for bounding

M_{q}(|\nabla u|)(|\lambda|^{-1/2})
.
These are indeed possible for Lipschitz domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by using a perturbation argument to upgrade the
L^{2}
estimates for
\nabla u
.
See Section
9
.

The approach we use for bounded domains works equally well for graph domains.
It also works well for exterior domains, provided that
|\lambda|
is sufficiently large.
We call
\Omega
an exterior Lipschitz (or
C^{1}
, resp.)
domain if
\Omega
is open, connected and
\overline{\Omega}^{c}
is a nonempty bounded set
with Lipschitz (or
C^{1}
, resp.) boundary.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega
be an exterior
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
or an exterior Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
, where
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
.
There exists
\lambda_{0}>1
, depending only on
d
,
\theta
and
\Omega
, with the property that
for any
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
with
|\lambda|>\lambda_{0}
and for any
F\in L^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
,
there exist a unique
u\in W^{1,2}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})

and
p\in L^{2}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
such that (
1.1
) holds in
the sense of distributions and that
p(x)=\beta+o(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate (
1.10
).
Furthermore, if
\Omega
is an exterior
C^{1,\alpha}
domain for some
\alpha>0
, then the estimate (
1.11
)
holds.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
\Omega
be an exterior
C^{1}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
or an exterior Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then the Stokes operator
A
generates an analytic semigroup on
C_{0,\sigma}(\Omega)
and
L^{\infty}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
.

Since the
L^{\infty}
resolvent estimates are only proved for
|\lambda|>\lambda_{0}
in Theorem

,
it is not known that the Stokes semigroup in Corollary

is uniformly bounded.
In the case of smooth exterior domains, the results on the boundedness can be found in
[
6
,
22
]
.

We end this section by recalling the definition of a bounded Lipschitz (or
C^{1}
, resp.) domain.
Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We say
\Omega
is Lipschitz (or
C^{1}
, resp.) if there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,

\Omega\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})=D\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})\quad\text{ and }\quad\partial\Omega\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})=\partial D\cap B(x_{0},r_{0}),

(1.27)

where
D=x_{0}+O\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
for some
d\times d
orthogonal matrix
O
and some Lipschitz (or
C^{1}
, resp.)
function
\psi
in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}

with
\psi(0)=0
.

2
Localization

Let
1<q<\infty
.
In this section we carry out a localization procedure, using
L^{q}
resolvent estimates in a region above a Lipschitz graph with small Lipschitz norm,
obtained in
[
18
]
.

Let

\mathbb{H}_{\psi}=\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\text{ and }x_{d}>\psi(x^{\prime})\right\},

(2.1)

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function and
\psi(0)=0
.
Consider the generalized resolvent problem for the Stokes equations,

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f)&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{H}_{\psi},\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=g&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{H}_{\psi},\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

where
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
.
For
1<q<\infty
and
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
, define

\displaystyle A_{\psi}^{q}=L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C})+\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C})\quad\text{ and }\quad B_{\psi}^{q}=L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C})\cap\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}),

(2.3)

where

\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C})=\left\{u\in L^{q}_{{\rm loc}}({\mathbb{H}_{\psi}};\mathbb{C}):\ \nabla u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d})\right\},

with the norm
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}
,
and
\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C})
denotes the dual of
\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C})
.
Observe that
A_{\psi}^{q}
and
B_{\psi}^{q}=\left(A_{\psi}^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}
are Banach spaces with the usual norms,

\|p\|_{A_{\psi}^{q}}=\inf\left\{\|p_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+\|\nabla p_{2}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}:\ \ p=p_{1}+p_{2}\text{ in }\mathbb{H}_{\psi}\right\}

and

\|g\|_{B_{\psi}^{q}}=\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}.

Let
\nabla^{\prime}\psi
denote the gradient of
\psi
in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
The following theorem was proved by the present authors in
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and
1<q<\infty
.
There exists
c_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending only on
d
,
q
and
\theta
, such that
if
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}
, then
for any
F\in L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
and
g\in B_{\psi}^{q}
,
there exists a unique
(u,p)
such that
u\in W_{0}^{1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d})
,

p\in A_{\psi}^{q}
, and (
2.2
) holds.
Moreover, the solution satisfies

\displaystyle|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+|\lambda|\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}\right\},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.

For
r>0
, let

D_{r}=B(0,r)\cap\mathbb{H}_{\psi}\quad\text{ and }\quad I_{r}=B(0,r)\cap\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi}.

(2.5)

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and
2<q<\infty
.
Suppose
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}
, where
c_{0}=c_{0}(d,q,\theta)
is given by Theorem
2.1
.
Let
(u,p)\in W^{1,q}(D_{Nr};\mathbb{C}^{d})\times L^{q}(D_{Nr};\mathbb{C})
be a weak solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F&\quad&\text{ in }D_{Nr},\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }D_{Nr},\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }I_{Nr},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

where
N\geq 2
and
F\in L^{\infty}(D_{Nr};\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
Then

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(D_{r})}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(D_{r})}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg\{\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}}+\left(\fint_{D_{Nr}}|u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\left((Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}-2}+(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\fint_{D_{Nr}}(|\nabla u|+|p|)^{q}\right)^{1/q}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}-1}+\left(\fint_{I_{Nr}}(|\nabla u|+|p|)^{q}\right)^{1/q}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}-1}\Bigg\},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.

Proof.

We begin by choosing a cut-off function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,Nr))
such that

\varphi=1
in
B(0,r)
,

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C(Nr)^{-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C(Nr)^{-2},

(2.8)

where
C
depends only on
d
.
Note that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta(u\varphi)+\nabla(p\varphi)+\lambda(u\varphi)&=h&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{H}_{\psi},\\
\text{\rm div}(u\varphi)&=g&\quad&\text{ in }\mathbb{H}_{\psi},\\
u\varphi&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.9)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} h&=F\varphi-2(\nabla u)(\nabla\varphi)-u\Delta\varphi+p(\nabla\varphi),\\
g&=u\cdot\nabla\varphi.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.10)

It follows by Theorem
2.1
that

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u

\displaystyle\|_{L^{q}(D_{r})}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(D_{r})}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|\|u\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(u\varphi)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|h\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+|\lambda|\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}\right\},

where we have used the fact
\varphi=1
in
B(0,r)
for the first inequality.

We now proceed to bound the right-hand side of (
2.11
) by the right-hand side of (
2.7
).
Using (
2.8
),
it is not hard to see that

\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-1}
,
and that

\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big\{\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-1}

\displaystyle+\|p\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-1}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-2}\Big\}.

In view of (
2.7
),
this gives the desired estimates for the first two terms in the right-hand side of (
2.11
).

It remains to bound the third term
|\lambda|\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}
.
To this end, let
\eta\in\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})
with
\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}\leq 1
.
Note that for any
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}g\eta

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}\text{\rm div}(u\varphi)(\eta-\beta)=\lambda\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}(u\cdot\nabla\varphi)(\eta-\beta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}\{(F+\Delta u-\nabla p)\cdot\nabla\varphi\}(\eta-\beta)

\displaystyle=J_{1}+J_{2},

where we have used (
2.6
).
Let
\beta=\fint_{D_{2Nr}}\eta
.
Clearly, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|J_{1}|

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}(F\cdot\nabla\varphi)(\eta-\beta)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{Nr})}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}\|\eta-\beta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(D_{Nr})}.

By applying a Poincaré inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|J_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{Nr})}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(D_{2Nr})}(Nr)

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}},

where we have used the fact
\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}\leq 1
.
Next, we use integration by parts to obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}((\Delta u-\nabla p)\cdot\nabla\varphi)(\eta-\beta)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}(\partial_{j}u^{i}-\delta_{ij}p)(\partial^{2}_{ij}\varphi)(\eta-\beta)-\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}(\partial_{j}u^{i}-\delta_{ij}p)(\partial_{i}\varphi)(\partial_{j}\eta)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial H_{\psi}}n_{j}(\partial_{j}u^{i}-\delta_{ij}p)(\partial_{i}\varphi)(\eta-\beta)

\displaystyle=J_{21}+J_{22}+J_{23},

where
n=(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})
denotes the outward unit normal to
\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
and
the repeated indices
i,j
are summed from
1
to
d
.
We point out that the use of integration by parts in
J_{2}
can be justified by an approximation argument with the observation (
A.8
).

Again, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|J_{21}+J_{22}|

\displaystyle\leq C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}\|\eta-\beta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(D_{2Nr})}(Nr)^{-2}

\displaystyle+C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(D_{2Nr})}(Nr)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-1},

where
\beta=\fint_{D_{2Nr}}\eta
and we have applied a Poincaré inequality.
Similarly,

\displaystyle|J_{23}|

\displaystyle\leq C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(I_{Nr})}\|\eta-\beta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(I_{2Nr})}(Nr)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(I_{Nr})}\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}(Nr)^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},

where we have used the Poincaré inequality,

\left(\int_{I_{R}}|\eta-\beta|^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\leq CR^{1-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{D_{2R}}|\nabla\eta|^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}},

(2.13)

with
\beta=\fint_{D_{2R}}\eta
.
See Remark
2.3
below for a proof of (
2.13
).
As a result, we have proved that

\displaystyle|J_{1}+J_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}}+C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-1}

\displaystyle+C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(I_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-1+\frac{1}{q}}.

By duality, we obtain

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}}+C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(D_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-1}

\displaystyle+C\||\nabla u|+|p|\|_{L^{q}(I_{Nr})}(Nr)^{-1+\frac{1}{q}}.

This, together with (
2.11
) as well as the estimates for
\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}
and
\|h\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}
,
completes the proof of (
2.7
).
∎

Remark 2.3
.

The Poincaré inequality (
2.13
), with
\beta=\fint_{D_{2R}}\eta
and
1<q^{\prime}<\infty
,
holds for any Lipschitz graph domain
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
. The smallness
condition
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}
is not needed.
To see this, by dilation, it suffices to consider the case
R=1
.
Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,2))
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and
\varphi=1
on
B(0,1)
.
Note that
n_{d}=-(1+|\nabla^{\prime}\psi|^{2})^{-1/2}
on
\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
.
Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle(1+\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}^{2})^{-1/2}\int_{I_{1}}|\eta-\beta|^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\partial D_{2}}n_{d}\varphi|\eta-\beta|^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{D_{2}}\partial_{d}\varphi|\eta-\beta|^{q^{\prime}}-\int_{D_{2}}\varphi\partial_{d}|\eta-\beta|^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{D_{2}}|\eta-\beta|^{q^{\prime}}+C\int_{D_{2}}|\eta-\beta|^{q^{\prime}-1}|\nabla\eta|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{D_{2}}|\eta-\beta|^{q^{\prime}}+C\int_{D_{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{D_{2}}|\nabla\eta|^{q^{\prime}},

where we have used Hölder’s inequality as well as the Poincaré inequality

\|\eta-\beta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(D_{2})}\leq C\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(D_{2})}
.

The next theorem provides the generalized resolvent estimates for the Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
See
[
18
, Theorem 2.1]
for a proof.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
1<q<\infty
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
.
For any
F\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
,
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
, and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})\cap\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
,
there exists a unique
u\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}^{d})
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F+\text{\rm div}(f),\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=g\end{aligned}\right.

(2.14)

hold in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
p\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
in the sense of distributions.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate,

\left\{\begin{aligned} |\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\},\\
|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\lambda|\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right\},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.15)

and
p\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})+\accentset{\circ}{W}^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C})
,
where
C
depends on
d
,
q
and
\theta
.

Let
B_{r}=B(0,r)
. The following theorem is the interior version of Theorem
2.2
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and
2<q<\infty
.
Let
(u,p)\in W^{1,q}(B_{Nr};\mathbb{C}^{d})\times L^{q}(B_{Nr};\mathbb{C})

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F&\quad&\text{ in }B_{Nr},\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }B_{Nr},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(2.16)

where
N\geq 2
and
F\in L^{\infty}(B_{Nr};\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
Then

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg\{\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Nr})}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}}+\left(\fint_{B_{Nr}}|u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\left((Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}-2}+(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\fint_{B_{Nr}}(|\nabla u|+|p|)^{q}\right)^{1/q}(Nr)^{\frac{d}{q}-1}\Bigg\},

,

.

.

).

) with

replaced by
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

) in

3
Estimates for the velocity

Recall that
B_{r}=B(0,r)
and
D_{r}=B(0,r)\cap\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
, where
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
is given by (
2.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
u\in W^{1,q}(B_{r})
for some
q>d
. Then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq Cr^{-\frac{d}{q}}\left\{\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}+r\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\right\},

(3.1)

where
C
depends only on
d
and
q
.

Proof.

The lemma is well known and follows from a Sobolev inequality in
B_{1}
by rescaling.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in W^{1,q}(D_{2r})
for some
q>d
.
Suppose
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq M
for some
M>0
.
Then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{r})}\leq Cr^{-\frac{d}{q}}\left\{\|u\|_{L^{q}(D_{2r})}+r\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(D_{2r})}\right\},

(3.2)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
M
.

Proof.

This lemma is also more or less well known.
See
[
3
]
for a proof in the case of uniform
C^{1}
domains by a compactness argument.
Here we sketch a direct argument for the case of Lipschitz domains.
By dilation we may assume
r=1
.
Moreover, in the case of the flat boundary where
\psi=0
, the estimate follows from the inequality,

\Big|u(x)-\fint_{B^{+}_{1}}u\Big|\leq C\int_{B^{+}_{1}}\frac{|\nabla u(y)|}{|x-y|^{d-1}}\,dy

(3.3)

for any
x\in B_{1}^{+}
, where
B_{1}^{+}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in B_{1}:x_{d}>0\}
.
In the general case, by flattening the boundary, one may show that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{r})}\leq Cr^{-\frac{d}{q}}\left\{\|u\|_{L^{q}(D_{C_{0}r})}+r\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(D_{C_{0}r})}\right\},

for some
C_{0}=C_{0}(M)>1
.
This, together with (
3.1
), yields (
3.2
) by a covering argument.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(u,p)
be the same as in Theorem
2.5
.
Suppose
q>d
and
r=R|\lambda|^{-1/2}
for some
R\geq 1
.
Then

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Nr})}RN^{\frac{d}{q}}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Nr})}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}\right\}

(3.4)

\displaystyle+C|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\fint_{B_{Nr}}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}+C|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\inf_{\beta\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\fint_{B_{Nr}}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
\theta
and
q
.

Proof.

It follows from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{-\frac{d}{q}}\left\{|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}R\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\right\}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq CRr^{-\frac{d}{q}}\left\{|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{r})}\right\},

where we have used the assumptions that
q>d
and
r=R|\lambda|^{-1/2}
for some
R\geq 1
.
This, together with (
2.17
), gives

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq CR\Bigg\{\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Nr})}N^{\frac{d}{q}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Nr})}\left(N^{\frac{d}{q}-2}r^{-2}+N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}r^{-1}|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle+\left(\fint_{B_{Nr}}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}r^{-1}+\left(\fint_{B_{Nr}}|p|^{q}\right)^{1/q}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}r^{-1}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg\{\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Nr})}RN^{\frac{d}{q}}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{Nr})}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}

\displaystyle+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\fint_{B_{Nr}}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\fint_{B_{Nr}}|p|^{q}\right)^{1/q}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}\Bigg\}.

The estimate (
3.4
) follows from (
3.6
),
as
(u,p-\beta)
is also a solution for any
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

The next lemma is the boundary version of Lemma

.

\displaystyle+C|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}\left\{\left(\fint_{D_{Nr}}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+\left(\fint_{I_{Nr}}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\}

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with
C^{1}
boundary.
Let
c_{0}=c_{0}(d,q,\theta)\in(0,1)
be given by Theorem
2.2
.
Then there exists
r_{0}>0
with the property that for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,
there exists a Cartesian coordinate system with origin
x_{0}
, obtained by translation and rotation,
such that

\displaystyle B(x_{0},16r_{0})\cap\Omega

\displaystyle=B(x_{0},16r_{0})\cap\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{d}>\psi(x^{\prime})\right\},

(3.8)

\displaystyle B(x_{0},16r_{0})\cap\partial\Omega

\displaystyle=B(x_{0},16r_{0})\cap\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x_{d}=\psi(x^{\prime})\right\},

where
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}
function and
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}
.

We need some regularity estimates for the Stokes equations (with
\lambda=0
) in
C^{1}
domains.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain and
2\leq q<\infty
.
Let
(u,p)\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\times L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a weak solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p&=F&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.9)

where
F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
.
Then, if
B(x,2r)\subset\Omega
,

\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C\left\{r^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+r\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\},

(3.10)

and if
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
,

\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C\left\{r^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+r\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\},

(3.11)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

The estimate (
3.10
) follows from the well known interior regularity estimates for the Stokes equations
[
16
]
.
To see (
3.11
), let
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
.
By the
W^{1,q}
estimate for the Stokes equations in
C^{1}
domains
[
11
,
17
,
10
]
and a localization procedure,
we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{r}\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},4r)\cap\Omega}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+Cr\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x_{0},4r)\cap\Omega)}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{r^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+r\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\},

where we have used the fact
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
To bound the second term in the left-hand side of (
3.11
), we introduce

\left\{\begin{aligned} w(x)&=\int_{\Omega\cap B(x_{0},4r)}\Phi(x-y)F(y)\,dy,\\
\pi(x)&=\int_{\Omega\cap B(x_{0},4r)}\Pi(x-y)F(y)\,dy,\end{aligned}\right.

where
(\Phi(x),\Pi(x))
denotes the fundamental solution for the Stokes operator in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with pole at the origin.
Let
v=u-w
.
Note that

-\Delta v+\nabla(p-\pi)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{\rm div}(v)=0

in
\Omega\cap B(x_{0},4r)
.
Since
\Omega
is
C^{1}
, it follows that

\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|\nabla v|^{q}\leq C\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\partial\Omega}|\nabla_{\tan}v|^{q}+C\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla v|^{q},

(3.13)

where
\nabla_{\tan}v
denotes the tangential gradient of
v
on
\partial\Omega
and
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.
The inequality (
3.13
) follows from Theorem
A.1

by a localization procedure.

Since
v=-w
on
B(x_{0},4r)\cap\partial\Omega
, it follows from (
3.13
) that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\partial\Omega}|\nabla w|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle+C\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+C\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|\nabla w|^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

Finally, we note that
|\nabla\Phi(x)|\leq C|x|^{1-d}
and as a result,

\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x_{0},4r))}\leq Cr\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

This, together with (
3.12
), gives the desired estimate for the second term in the left-hand side of
(
3.11
).
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain.
Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and
d<q<\infty
.
Let
(u,p)\in W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})\times L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
be a weak solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u+\nabla p+\lambda u&=F&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\text{\rm div}(u)&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.17)

where
F\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{d})
. Let
t=|\lambda|^{-1/2}
and
N\geq 2
.

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{4d+8}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

(3.18)

\displaystyle+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t<r<r_{0}\\
B(x,2r)\subset\Omega\end{subarray}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{C}\end{subarray}}\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\\
t<r<r_{0}\end{subarray}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{C}\end{subarray}}\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\},

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
,
\theta
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

Suppose
B(x,2r)\subset\Omega
for some
r>0
.
By the standard interior regularity theory for the Stokes equations (with
\lambda=0
) (see e.g.
[
16
, Ch. IV]
),

\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}|u|^{q_{2}}\right)^{1/q_{2}}\leq C\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x,2r)}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+r^{2}\left(\fint_{B(x,2r)}|F-\lambda u|^{q_{1}}\right)^{1/q_{1}}\right\},

(3.19)

where

for
d\geq 3
and
\frac{1}{q_{2}}>\frac{1}{q_{1}}-1
for

, and

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,r))}\leq C\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x,2r)}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+r^{2}\left(\fint_{B(x,2r)}|F-\lambda u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\}.

(3.20)

It follows from (
3.19
) by a bootstrapping argument that

\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}|u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq C(1+r^{2}|\lambda|)^{d}\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x,2r)}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+r^{2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}

(3.21)

if
B(x,2r)\subset\Omega
.
This, together with (
3.20
), leads to

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x,r))}\leq C(1+r^{2}|\lambda|)^{d+1}\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x,2r)}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+r^{2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}.

(3.22)

Under the assumption that
\Omega
is
C^{1}
, we have the boundary estimate
[
19
]
,

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega)}\leq C\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+r^{2}\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|F-\lambda u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\},

(3.23)

as well as a boundary version of (
3.19
),
where
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,
0<r<r_{0}
, and

C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.
As a result, by a similar bootstrapping argument as in the interior case,
we also obtain

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega)}\leq C(1+r^{2}|\lambda|)^{d+1}\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},2r)\cap\Omega}|u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}+r^{2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}.

(3.24)

It follows from (
3.22
) and (
3.24
) as well as the
L^{2}
resolvent estimate
(|\lambda|+1)\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C(1+|\lambda|)^{d+1}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

(3.25)

Consequently, it suffices to prove (
3.18
) for the case
|\lambda|>16r_{0}^{-2}N^{4}
.

Next, let

.
Since

, we have
2Nr<r_{0}
.
It follows from Lemma
3.4
by translation and rotation that for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega)}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}N^{\frac{d}{q}+1}+|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}\right\}

\displaystyle+|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{\frac{d}{q}-1}\left\{\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},Nr)\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},Nr)\cap\partial\Omega}|\nabla u|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\}

), gives

\displaystyle|\lambda|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{r})}

where

.

\displaystyle+CN^{\frac{d}{q}-1}|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\inf_{\beta\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\fint_{B(x,N|\lambda|^{-1/2})}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q},

where we also used (

).

4
A Neumann problem in a bounded Lipschitz domain

In order to bound the terms involving the pressure
p
in (
3.18
),
we consider the Neumann problem for Laplace’s equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\phi&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}&=(n_{i}\partial_{j}-n_{j}\partial_{i})g_{ij}&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

where the repeated indices
i,j
are summed from
1
to
d
and
g_{ij}\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega)
.
Throughout this section we assume that
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.

Let
g=(g_{ij})
and
I(x_{0},r)=B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega
.
For
0<r<r_{0}
, define

M_{q}(g)(r)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\\
r\leq s<r_{0}\end{subarray}}\left(\fint_{I(x_{0},s)}|g|^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

(4.2)

It is not hard to see that
M_{q}(g)(r)\approx M_{q}(g)(2r)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a weak solution of (
4.1
).
Let
2\leq q<\infty
.
Then, for
0<r<r_{0}
,

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\end{subarray}}\left(\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}|\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

(4.3)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
.
Let
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be a weak solution of the Neumann problem,

\Delta v=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=\eta\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(4.4)

where
\eta\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, supp
(\eta)\subset I(x_{0},r)
and
\int_{\partial\Omega}\eta=0
.
Since
1<q^{\prime}\leq 2
,
under the assumption that
\Omega
is Lipschitz, the estimate

\|(\nabla v)^{*}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}
holds and
\nabla v
has non-tangential limits a.e. on
\partial\Omega
, where

(\nabla v)^{*}
denotes the non-tangential maximal function of
\nabla v
.
Moreover, assuming
\int_{\partial\Omega}v=0
, we have

v(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(N(x,y)-N(x,x_{0})\right)\eta(y)\,dy

(4.5)

for any
x\in\Omega
, where
N(x,y)
denotes the Neumann function for
-\Delta
in
\Omega
.
Using the estimate,

|N(x,y)-N(x,x_{0})|\leq\frac{Cr^{\sigma}}{|x-y|^{d-2+\sigma}}

(4.6)

for
x\in\Omega\setminus B(x_{0},2r)
and
y\in I(x_{0},r)
, where
\sigma>0
depends on
\Omega
, we obtain

|v(x)|\leq\frac{Cr^{\sigma}}{|x-x_{0}|^{d-2+\sigma}}\|\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}r^{\frac{d-1}{q}}.

(4.7)

We refer the reader to
[
24
]
for proofs of these results.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{I(x_{0},r)}\Big(\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi\Big)\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi\cdot\eta=\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=\int_{\partial\Omega}v\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}v(n_{i}\partial_{j}-n_{j}\partial_{i})g_{ij}=-\int_{\partial\Omega}g_{ij}(n_{i}\partial_{j}-n_{j}\partial_{i})v.

Hence,

\displaystyle\big|\int_{I(x_{0},r)}\Big(\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi\Big)\eta\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega}|\nabla v||g|=C\sum_{j=0}^{J+1}A_{j},

(4.9)

where
2^{J}r\approx r_{0}
, and

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{I(x_{0},4r)}|\nabla v||g|,

(4.10)

\displaystyle A_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{I(x_{0},2^{j+2}r)\setminus I(x_{0},2^{j+1}r)}|\nabla v||g|\quad\text{ for }j=1,2,\dots,J,

\displaystyle A_{J+1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus I(x_{0},2^{J+2}r)}|\nabla v||g|.

We now proceed to bound each term in (
4.10
).
First, observe that by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\left(\fint_{I(x_{0},4r)}|g|^{q}\right)^{1/q}r^{\frac{d-1}{q}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}\left(\fint_{I(x_{0},4r)}|g|^{q}\right)^{1/q}r^{\frac{d-1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}M_{q}(g)(r)\,r^{\frac{d-1}{q}},

where we have used the notation (
4.2
).

Next, note that if
\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=0
on
I(y_{0},2R)
for some
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and

r<R<r_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{I(y_{0},R)}|\nabla v|^{q^{\prime}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\fint_{I(y_{0},R)}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\frac{C}{R}\fint_{B(y_{0},2R)\cap\Omega}|v|

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\sigma}R^{1-d}\|\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}r^{\frac{d-1}{q}},

where we have used the
L^{2}
estimates for the Neumann problem for the second inequality
[
24
]

and (
4.7
) for the last.
This, together with Hölder’s inequality, allows us to deduce that

A_{j}\leq C2^{-j\sigma}\|\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}M_{q}(g)(r)\ r^{\frac{d-1}{q}}

for
j=1,2,\dots,J
.
The same argument also yields
A_{J+1}\leq C\|\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}M_{q}(g)(r)r^{\frac{d-1}{q}}
.
By summation we obtain

\big|\int_{I(x_{0},r)}\Big(\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi\Big)\eta\big|\leq C\|\eta\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}M_{q}(g)(r)\,r^{\frac{d-1}{q}}.

By duality this implies that

\left(\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}|\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r)

for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3

Let

Then, for
0<r<r_{0}
,

\sup_{x_{0}\in\partial\Omega}\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r).

(4.14)

Moreover, for any
x\in\Omega
,

\delta(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq CM_{q}(g)(\delta(x)).

(4.15)

The constants
C
in (
4.14
)-(
4.15
) depend at most on
d
,
q
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
let
\beta=\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi
.
Write
\phi-\beta=\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}
, where
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are bounded harmonic functions in
\Omega
and

\phi_{1}=(\phi-\beta)\chi_{I(x_{0},4r)}
on
\partial\Omega
.
Note that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\phi_{1}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\frac{1-d}{q}}\|(\phi_{1})^{*}\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\leq Cr^{\frac{1-d}{q}}\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\frac{1-d}{q}}\|\phi-\beta\|_{L^{q}(I(x_{0},4r))}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

where
(\phi_{1})^{*}
denotes the non-tangential maximal function of
\phi_{1}
and we have used Lemma
4.1
for
the last step.
We point out that in a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
,
the estimate
\|(\phi_{1})^{*}\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|\phi_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}
holds for any
q\geq 2

[
24
]
.

Next, we will show that for any
x\in B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega
,

|\phi_{2}(x)|\leq CM_{q}(g)(r).

(4.16)

To this end, we represent
\phi_{2}
by a Poisson integral,

\phi_{2}(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus B(x_{0},4r)}P(x,y)(\phi(y)-\beta)dy.

Let
G(x,y)
denote the Green function for
-\Delta
in
\Omega
.
Note that

\displaystyle|\phi_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus B(x_{0},4r)}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)||\phi(y)-\beta|dy

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{J}\int_{I(x_{0},2^{j+2}r)\setminus I(x_{0},2^{j+1}r)}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)||\phi-\beta|\,dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega\setminus I(x_{0},2^{J+2}r)}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)||\phi(y)-\beta|\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}A_{j}+A_{J+1},

where
2^{J}r\approx r_{0}
.
Using the estimate

\int_{I(x_{0},2R))\setminus I(x_{0},R)}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{2}\,dy\leq\frac{C}{R^{3}}\int_{[B(x_{0},3R)\setminus B(x_{0},R/2)]\cap\Omega}|G(x,y)|^{2}\,dy

(4.17)

for
R\geq 4r
and
x\in B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega
,
and

|G(x,y)|\leq\frac{Cr^{\sigma}}{|x-y|^{d-2+\sigma}}

(4.18)

for
|x-y|\geq r
, where
\sigma>0
depends on
\Omega
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{I(x_{0},2R)\setminus I(x_{0},R)}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)||\phi(y)-\beta|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\sigma}\left(\fint_{I(x_{0},2R)}|\phi-\beta|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\sigma}\ln\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)M_{q}(g)(r).

For the second inequality in (
4.19
), we have used (
4.3
) as well as the observation that

\Big|\fint_{I(x_{0},R)}\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi\Big|\leq C\ln\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)\sup_{r\leq t\leq R}\fint_{I(x_{0},t)}\big|\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},t)}\phi\big|.

(4.20)

It follows from (
4.19
) that

A_{j}\leq Cj2^{-j\sigma}M_{q}(g)(r)

for
j=1,2,\dots,J
.
Similar, we may show that

A_{J+1}\leq Cr^{\sigma}|\ln r|M_{q}(g)(r).

As a result, we obtain (
4.16
) by summation and complete the proof of (
4.14
).

Finally, since
\phi
is harmonic in
\Omega
, we have

\delta(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq C\inf_{\beta\in\mathbb{C}}\fint_{B(x,\delta(x))}|\phi-\beta|.

This, together with (
4.14
), implies that if
\delta(x)\leq cr_{0}
, then

\delta(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq CM_{q}(g)(\delta(x)).

(4.21)

By using the maximum principle on
\nabla\phi
, one may show that (
4.21
) holds for any
x\in\Omega
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

It follows from (
4.15
) that

\delta(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\quad\text{ for any }x\in\Omega.

(4.22)

The estimate (
4.22
) was proved in
[
25
]
under the assumption that
\Omega
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
domain for some

\alpha>0
, using the estimate for the Neumann function,

|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}N(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-d}

for
x,y\in\Omega
.
Independently, the estimate (
4.22
) was proved for bounded and exterior domains with
C^{3}
boundaries in the study of the
L^{\infty}
Stokes semigroup by
K. Abe and Y. Giga in
[
1
,
2
]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
and
2\leq q<\infty
.
Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a solution of (
4.1
).
Then,

\sup_{B(x,2r)\subset\Omega}\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}|\phi-\fint_{B(x,r)}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

(4.23)

and for
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
,

\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|\phi-\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\phi-\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

(4.24)

where
C

depends only on
d
,
q
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

The estimate (
4.23
) follows from (

) by using a Poincaré inequality,
while (
4.24
) is contained in Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a weak solution of the Neumann problem,

\left\{\begin{aligned} \Delta\phi&=0&\quad&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}&=(n_{i}\partial_{j}-n_{j}\partial_{i})g_{ij}+h&\quad&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.25)

where
g_{ij}\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and
h\in C(\partial\Omega)
.
Then the estimates (
4.23
)-(
4.24
)
hold with
M_{q}(g)(r)
replaced by
M_{q}(g)(r)+\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}
.
Indeed,
in view of Theorem
4.5
, we only need to consider the case
g=0
.
To this end,
we assume
\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi=0
.
This allows us to represent
\phi
by the Neumann function,

\phi(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}N(x,y)h(y)\,dy.

Using the estimates
|N(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{2-d}
for
d\geq 3
and

|N(x,y)|\leq C(1+|\ln|x-y||)
for

, we deduce that

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},

(4.26)

which gives the desired estimates.

5
A Neumann problem in an exterior Lipschitz domain

Throughout this section we assume that
\Omega
is an exterior Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
We begin with some classical results on the asymptotic behavior of harmonic functions at infinity.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\phi
be harmonic in
\Omega
.
Suppose that as
|x|\to\infty
,
\phi(x)=O(1)
if
d\geq 3
, and

\phi(x)=o(\log|x|)
if
d=2
. Then

\phi(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\beta+O(|x|^{2-d})&\quad&\text{ if }\ d\geq 3,\\
&\beta+O(|x|^{-1})&\quad&\text{ if }\ d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.1)

for some
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Moreover,

|\nabla\phi(x)|=\left\{\begin{aligned} &O(|x|^{1-d})&\quad&\text{ if }\ d\geq 3,\\
&O(|x|^{-2})&\quad&\text{ if }\ d=2,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

as
|x|\to\infty
.

Proof.

See e.g.
[
4
]
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Let
\phi
be harmonic in
\Omega
.
Suppose
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\phi(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Let
D=B(0,R)\cap\Omega
, where
R
is large.
Let
\Gamma^{x}(y)=\Gamma(x-y)
denote the fundamental solution for
-\Delta
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By applying Green’s representation formula to
\phi
,

\phi(x)=\int_{\partial D}\Gamma^{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\phi-\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial\Gamma^{x}}{\partial n}\phi

for
x\in D
, and then letting
R\to\infty
,
we obtain

\phi(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Gamma^{x}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Gamma^{x}}{\partial n}\phi

(5.3)

for
x\in\Omega
. In the case
d=2
, using the observation that
\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=0
,
we have

\phi(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\Gamma^{x}-\Gamma^{x}(y_{0})\right)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Gamma^{x}}{\partial n}\phi

(5.4)

for any
x\in\Omega
,
where
y_{0}\in\Omega^{c}
.

Recall that
I(x_{0},r)=B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega
and
M_{q}(g)(r)
is defined by (
4.2
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a solution of (
4.1
) and

2\leq q<\infty
.
Assume that
\phi(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Then, for
0<r<r_{0}
,

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{0}\in\partial\Omega\end{subarray}}\left(\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}|\phi-\fint_{I(x_{0},r)}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+\big|\fint_{\partial\Omega}\phi\big|\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

(5.5)

where
C
depends only on
d,q
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is harmonic in
\Omega
, it follows from the assumption

\phi(x)=o(1)
as
|x|\to\infty
that

|\phi(x)|+|x||\nabla\phi(x)|=\left\{\begin{aligned} &O(|x|^{2-d})&\quad&\text{ for }d\geq 3,\\
&O(|x|^{{-1}})&\quad&\text{ for }d=2,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(5.6)

as
|x|\to\infty
.
Moreover, if
\int_{\partial\Omega}\eta=0
,
the Neumann problem (
4.4
) possesses a solution with the properties that
(\nabla v)^{*}\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
and

|v(x)|+|x||\nabla v(x)|=O(|x|^{1-d})\quad\text{ as }|x|\to\infty.

(5.7)

See
[
33
]
for a proof by the method of layer potentials in Lipschitz domains.
This, together with (
5.6
), allows us to justify the use of integration by parts in the third equality in (
4.8
).
As in the case of bounded domains, we deduce by duality that the first term in the left-hand side of (
5.5
)
is bounded by
CM_{q}(g)(r)
.

Next, let
v
be a solution of the Neumann problem (
4.4
) in
\Omega
with
\eta=1
and the property that

v(x)=O(|x|^{2-d})
for
d\geq 3
, and
v(x)=O(|\ln|x||)
for
d=2
as
|x|\to\infty
.
It follows that
|\nabla v(x)|=O(|x|^{1-d})
for
d\geq 2
as
|x|\to\infty
.
Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi=\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=\int_{\partial\Omega}v\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}-\int_{\partial B(0,R)}v\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}+\int_{\partial B(0,R)}\phi\frac{\partial v}{\partial n},

where
R>1
is so large that
\Omega^{c}\subset B(0,R/2)
.
This gives

\big|\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi\big|\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\int_{\partial B(0,R)}\left\{|v||\nabla\phi|+|\phi||\nabla v|\right\}.

(5.8)

Using the decay properties at
\infty
for
\phi
and
v
,
we can show the last integral in (
5.8
) converges to zero as
R\to\infty
.
As a result, we obtain

\big|\fint_{\partial\Omega}\phi\big|\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

which completes the proof of (
5.5
).
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let
\phi
be the same as in Lemma
5.3
.
Since
\phi(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
, it follows that

\|(\phi)^{*}\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}

(5.9)

for
2\leq q<\infty
. This, together with (
5.5
), yields

\|(\phi)^{*}\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

(5.10)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\Omega
be an exterior Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
2\leq q<\infty
.
Let
\phi
be the same as in Lemma
5.3
.
Then, for
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
,

\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|\phi-\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}\phi|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

(5.11)

where
C

depends only on
d
,
q
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.
Moreover, for any
x\in\Omega
,

\delta(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq C\left\{M_{q}(g)(\delta(x))+\|g\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}.

(5.12)

Proof.

Fix
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
x_{0}=0
and

B(0,16r_{0})\cap\Omega=B(0,16r_{0})\cap\mathbb{H}_{\psi}\quad\text{ and }\quad B(0,16r_{0})\cap\partial\Omega=B(0,16r_{0})\cap\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi},

where
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
is given by (
2.1
) and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function with
\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\leq M
.
Let
\beta=\fint_{B(0,r)\cap\partial\Omega}\phi
.
Let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be harmonic functions in
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}

such that

\phi_{1}=(\phi-\beta)\chi_{B(0,2r)\cap\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi}},\quad\phi_{2}=(\phi-\beta)\chi_{\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi}\cap(B(0,4r_{0})\setminus B(0,2r))}

on
\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
,
and that
(\phi_{1})^{*},(\phi_{2})^{*}\in L^{2}(\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi})
.
Using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
4.5
,
we may show that

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B(0,r)\cap\Omega}|\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

(5.13)

where we also have used Lemma
5.3
.

Next, let
\phi_{3}=\phi-\beta-(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})
.
Note that
\phi_{3}
is harmonic in
B(0,4r_{0})\cap\Omega
and

\phi_{3}=0
on
B(0,4r_{0})\cap\partial\Omega
.
Since
\Omega
is Lipschitz, it follows that
\phi_{3}
is Hölder continuous in

B(0,3r_{0})\cap\Omega
and that

\left(\fint_{B(0,r)\cap\Omega}|\phi_{3}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq Cr^{\sigma}\left(\fint_{B(0,3r_{0})\cap\Omega}|\phi_{3}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

for some
\sigma>0
.
This leads to

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B(0,r)\cap\Omega}|\phi_{3}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\sigma}\left\{\|(\phi)^{*}\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}+|\beta|+\|(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})^{*}\|_{L^{q}(\partial\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\sigma}\left\{M_{q}(g)(r)+|\beta|+\|\phi-\beta\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\sigma}(1+|\ln r|)M_{q}(g)(r)

\displaystyle\leq CM_{q}(g)(r),

where we have used (
5.10
) and (
4.20
).
As a result, we obtain (
5.11
).

As in the case of bounded domains, it follows from (
5.11
) that

\delta(x)|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq CM_{q}(g)(\delta(x))

if
\delta(x)\leq cr_{0}
.
Since
|\nabla\phi(x)|\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
, by using the maximum principle,
we deduce that
|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq C\|g\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}
if
\delta(x)\geq cr_{0}
.
Finally, choose
R_{0}
large so that
\Omega^{c}\subset B(0,R_{0}-3)
.
By applying the formulas in (
5.3
)-(
5.4
) to the harmonic functions

\nabla\phi
in
B(0,R_{0})^{c}
, we may show that for any
x\in B(0,R_{0}+2)^{c}
,

|\nabla\phi(x)|\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C|x|^{2-d}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,R_{0}+1)\setminus B(0,R_{0}-1))}&\quad&\text{ if }d\geq 3,\\
&C|x|^{-1}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,R_{0}+1)\setminus B(0,R_{0}-1))}&\quad&\text{ if }d=2.\end{aligned}\right.

Consequently, we obtain (
5.12
) for any
x\in\Omega
.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a solution of the Neumann problem (
4.25
),
where
g_{ij}\in C^{1}(\partial\Omega)
and
h\in C(\partial\Omega)
.
Assume that
\phi(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
.
In the case
d=2
, we also assume that
\int_{\partial\Omega}h=0
.
Then, as in the case of bounded domains,
estimates (
5.5
), (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) hold
if we add the term
C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}
in the right-hand sides.

To see this, it suffices to show that there exists a harmonic function
v
in
\Omega
such that

\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=h
on
\partial\Omega
,
v(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
, and

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}
.
To this end, we first note that there exists a harmonic function in
\Omega
such that
\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=h
on
\partial\Omega

and
\|(\nabla v)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
.
If
d\geq 3
, the solution satisfies
v(x)=O(x|^{2-d})
as
|x|\to\infty
.
In the case
d=2
, using the assumption that
\int_{\partial\Omega}h=0
, we have

v(x)=O(|x|^{-1})
as
|x|\to\infty
.
We refer the reader to
[
33
]
for a proof in Lipschitz domains.
Furthermore, we note that in
[
33
]
, the solution was constructed using
a single layer potential with a density
f
satisfying
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
.
In the case
d=2
, we also have
\int_{\partial\Omega}f=0
.
As a consequence, we obtain
\|(v)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
.

Finally, we use the local estimates for Neumann problem on Lipschitz domains
[
24
]
to obtain

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{r_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{2r_{0}})}+\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\{\|(v)^{*}\|_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)},

where
\Omega_{r}=\{x\in\Omega:\delta(x)<r\}
.
This, together with the maximum principle, yields the desired estimate
|v(x)|\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}
for any
x\in\Omega
.

6
Estimates for the pressure

In this section we establish the key estimates for the pressure.
We first deal with the case of bounded domains.
Recall that
\delta(x)=\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}
and
q>2
.
Let
(u,p)
be a weak solution of (
1.1
), where

F\in C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain.
Suppose
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C}^{d})
and
p\in C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C})
.
Let
B(x,2r)\subset\Omega
.
Then

\left(\fint_{B(x,r)}|p-\fint_{B(x,r)}p|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(|\nabla u|)(\delta(x)),

(6.1)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
(u,p)
be the same as in Theorem
6.1
.
Suppose
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
0<r<r_{0}
.
Then

\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\Omega}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}+\left(\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}|p-\beta|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq CM_{q}(|\nabla u|)(r),

(6.2)

where
\beta=\fint_{B(x_{0},r)\cap\partial\Omega}p
and
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

The proofs of these two theorems rely on the a priori estimates we established in Section
4
for the Neumann problem (
4.25
).
We begin by choosing a sequence of smooth domains
\{\Omega_{\ell}\}
with uniform Lipschitz characters such that

\Omega_{\ell}\nearrow\Omega
.
Let
n^{\ell}
denote the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega_{\ell}
.
Since
F\in C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we see that
\Delta p=0
in
\Omega
and that

\nabla p\cdot n^{\ell}=\Delta u\cdot n^{\ell}-\lambda u\cdot n^{\ell}

on
\partial\Omega_{\ell}
, if
\ell
is sufficiently large.
Moreover, since
\text{\rm div}(u)=0

Appendix B
A divergence problem for a Lipschitz graph domain

In this appendix we provide a proof for the following theorem, which is used in the proof of Lemma
9.1
.

Theorem B.1
.

Let
g\in B_{\psi}^{q}=L^{q}(H_{\psi};\mathbb{C})\cap\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C})
, where
1<q<\infty
and

\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function with
\|\nabla^{\prime}\psi\|_{\infty}\leq M
.
Then there exists
w\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi};\mathbb{C}^{d})
such that
\text{\rm div}(w)=g
in
\mathbb{H}_{\psi}
in the sense of distributions and

\|\nabla w\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+\|w\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}\leq C\left\{\|g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}+\|g\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})}\right\},

(B.1)

where
C
depends only on
d
,
q
and
M
.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1<q<\infty
.
Suppose that
\Omega
is star-like with respect to every point of
B(x_{0},r_{0})
for some
B(x_{0},r_{0})\subset\Omega
.
Then for any
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
with
\int_{\Omega}f=0
, there exists
u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\text{\rm div}(u)=f
in
\Omega
and

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

(B.2)

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\Omega)}.

(B.3)

Moreover, the constants
C
in (
B.2
)-(
B.3
) satisfy

C\leq C_{0}\left[\text{\rm diam}(\Omega)/r_{0}\right]^{d}\left(1+\text{\rm diam}(\Omega)/r_{0}\right),

(B.4)

where
C_{0}
depends on
d
and
q
.

Proof.

This is a classical result of M.E. Bogovskiĭ
[
5
]
.
We point out that although the estimate (
B.3
) is not usually stated for bounded domains,
it follows from the Bogovskiĭ formula.
For the reader’s convenience, we sketch a proof, following the presentation in
[
16
, pp.162-167]
.

By translation and dilation, we may assume
x_{0}=0
and
r_{0}=1
.
Choose
\omega\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,1))
such that
\int_{B(0,1)}\omega=1
.
Let

u(x)=\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)f(y)\,dy,

where
K(x,y)=(K_{1}(x,y),\dots,K_{d}(x,y))
and

\displaystyle K_{i}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(x_{i}-y_{i})\int_{1}^{\infty}\omega(y+t(x-y))t^{d-1}\,dt

(B.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{|x-y|^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega\Big(x+t\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big)\left(|x-y|+t\right)^{d-1}\,dt.

Note that

|K(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{1-d}\quad\text{ and }\quad|\nabla_{x}K(x,y)|+|\nabla_{y}K(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{-d}.

Moreover,

\partial_{j}u_{i}(x)=f(x)\int_{\Omega}\frac{(x_{j}-y_{j})(x_{i}-y_{i})}{|x-y|^{2}}\omega(y)\,dy+T_{ij}(f)(x),

where

T_{ij}(f)(x)=\text{p.v.}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}K_{i}(x,y)f(y)\,dy.

Furthermore, using (
B.5
),
a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}K_{i}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{|x-y|^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega\Big(x+t\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big)\big(|x-y|+t\big)^{d-1}\,dt

(B.6)

\displaystyle+\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{|x-y|^{d+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{j}\omega\Big(x+t\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big)(|x-y|+t)^{d}\,dt,

\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}K_{i}(x,y)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}K_{i}(x,y)+\frac{x_{i}-y_{i}}{|x-y|^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{j}\omega\Big(x+t\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}\Big)\big(|x-y|+t)^{d-1}\,dt,

(B.7)

and that
\text{\rm div}(u)=f
in
\Omega
.
It follows by expanding the term
(|x-y|+t)^{d}
in (
B.6
) that

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}K_{i}(x,y)=\frac{k_{ij}(x,x-y)}{|x-y|^{d}}+G_{ij}(x,y),

where
k_{ij}(x,z)
and
G_{ij}(x,y)
satisfy the conditions,
|k_{ij}(x,z)|\leq C
,
k_{ij}(x,tz)=k_{ij}(x,z)
for
t>0
,

\int_{|z|=1}k_{ij}(x,z)dz=0\quad\text{ and }\quad|G_{ij}(x,y)|\leq C|x-y|^{1-d}.

As a result, it follows from the Calderón-Zygmund theory of singular integrals that

\|T_{ij}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}
. This gives the estimate (
B.2
).

Finally, to prove (
B.3
), note that
for
h=(h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{d})\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}u\cdot h\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\Omega}f(y)\left\{\int_{\Omega}K_{i}(x,y)h_{i}(x)\,dx\right\}dy\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{y}\int_{\Omega}K_{i}(x,y)h_{i}(x)\,dx\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

Moreover,

\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\int_{\Omega}K_{i}(x,y)h_{i}(x)\,dx=h_{i}(y)a_{ij}(y)+\text{\rm p.v.}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}K_{i}(x,y)h_{i}(x)\,dx,

where
|a_{ij}(y)|\leq C
, and by (
B.7
),

\Big|\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}K_{i}(x,y)+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}K_{i}(x,y)\Big|\leq C|x-y|^{1-d}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{y}\int_{\Omega}K_{i}(x,y)h_{i}(x)\,dx\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}+C\sum_{i,j}\|T_{ij}^{*}(h_{i})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|h\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)},

where
T_{ij}^{*}
denotes the adjoint of
T_{ij}
.
By duality we deduce that
\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{\accentset{\circ}{W}^{-1,q}(\Omega)}
.
∎

\ell
is large,

\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}w_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\varphi=-\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}\Big(g-\fint_{E_{\ell}}g\Big)\varphi.

By letting
\ell\to\infty
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}w\cdot\nabla\varphi=-\int_{\mathbb{H}_{\psi}}g\varphi,

where we have used the fact
\fint_{E_{\ell}}g\to 0
for
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{H}_{\psi})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

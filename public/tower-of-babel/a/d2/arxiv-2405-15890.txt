Conformal trajectories in 3 3 -dimensional space forms
Rafael López, Marian Ioan Munteanu

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of conformal trajectories in three-dimensional Riemannian manifolds M 3 M^{3} . Given a conformal vector field V ∈ 𝔛 ⁡ ( M 3 ) V\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{3}) , a conformal trajectory of V V is a regular curve γ \gamma in M 3 M^{3} satisfying ∇ γ ′ γ ′ = q ​ V × γ ′ \nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=q\,V\times\gamma^{\prime} , for some fixed non-zero constant q ∈ ℝ q\in\mathbb{R} . In this paper, we study conformal trajectories in the space forms ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3} and ℍ 3 \mathbb{H}^{3} . For (non-Killing) conformal vector fields in 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3} (respectively in ℍ 3 \mathbb{H}^{3} ), we prove that conformal trajectories have constant curvature and its torsion is a linear combination of trigonometric (respectively hyperbolic) functions on the arc-length parameter. In the case of Euclidean space ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} , we obtain the same result for the radial vector field and characterizing all conformal trajectories.

1.
Introduction and motivation

In a
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle)
, a magnetic field is a closed
2
-form
F
. A curve
\gamma\colon I\to M^{n}
is called a magnetic trajectory of
F
if
\gamma
satisfies the Lorentz equation
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\Phi(\gamma^{\prime})
, where
\Phi
is the
(1,1)
-tensor field on
M
defined by
\langle\Phi X,Y\rangle=F(X,Y)
,
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{n})
. The notion of magnetic trajectory extends that of geodesic when
F=0
.

Suppose that the dimension of
M^{n}
is
n=3
. In such a case, the study of magnetic trajectories can be introduced with a different approach. This is due because
2
-forms and vector fields are identified via the Hodge star operator
*
and the volume form
\omega
of
M^{3}
. In particular, magnetic fields are vector fields with zero divergence and the Lorentz force can be expressed in terms of the cross product
\times
of
M^{3}
. Killing vector fields are examples of magnetic fields. Each Killing vector field defines a magnetic field
F_{V}
by means of
F_{V}=i_{V}\omega
. Then the Lorentz force
\Phi
of
F_{V}
is given by

\Phi(X)=V\times X.

Thus a magnetic trajectory
\gamma
of
V
is characterized by the identity

\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=V\times\gamma^{\prime}.

Looking this identity, the property that
V
is magnetic can be ignored and it only uses the cross product of
M^{3}
. This observation allows us to generalize the notion of magnetic curve by replacing a Killing vector field by a conformal vector field. A vector field
V\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{n})
is said to be
conformal
if

\mathcal{L}_{V}\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle=\lambda\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle,

where
\mathcal{L}
is the Lie derivative and
\lambda\in C^{\infty}(M^{n})
is a function on
M^{n}
. Equivalently, we have

(1)

\langle\nabla_{X}V,Y\rangle+\langle X,\nabla_{Y}V\rangle=\lambda\langle X,Y\rangle,\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{n}).

In the literature, it is also used the terminology of conformal Killing vector field. If
\lambda=0
, then
V
is just a Killing vector field. Those ones that are not Killing will be caller proper conformal vector fields.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
V\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{3})
be a conformal vector field. A
conformal trajectory
of
V
is a regular curve
\gamma\colon I\subset\mathbb{R}\to M^{3}
satisfying the equation

(2)

\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=q\,V\times\gamma^{\prime},

where
q\in\mathbb{R}
,
q\not=0
, is fixed.

The number
q
in (
2
) is due because conformal trajectories cannot be rescaled, such as it occurs in the case of geodesics. Indeed, if
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory, then

\frac{d}{ds}\langle\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle=2\langle\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}(s)}\gamma^{\prime}(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle=2\langle V\times\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle=0.

As a consequence the speed
|\gamma^{\prime}(s)|
of
\gamma
is constant.

The purpose of this paper is to study conformal trajectories in the space forms
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. For magnetic fields, the study of magnetic trajectories in space forms have been developed in
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
]
.
In each of these space forms, we will consider particular examples of proper conformal vector fields. To be precise,

(1)

In Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let
V
be the radial vector field
V(x,y,z)=x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}+z\partial_{z}
.

(2)

In the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, proper conformal vector fields are generated by
V(p)=\vec{a}-\langle\vec{a},p\rangle p
, where
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

(3)

In the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, consider the Lorentzian model where
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is viewed as a quadric of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. The proper conformal vector fields are generated by
V(p)=\vec{a}+\langle\vec{a},p\rangle p
, where
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}_{1}
.

In all cases, when we express Eq. (
2
) in coordinates, the equation turns on a second order differential equation whose explicit solutions are difficult to find in general. However, we can summarize a property of the conformal trajectories.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M^{3}
be a space form and let
V\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{3})
be the conformal vector field described above. Let
\gamma\colon I\subset\mathbb{R}\to M^{3}
be a regular (non-geodesic) curve parametrized by arc-length
\gamma=\gamma(s)
. If
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory of
V
, then its curvature
\kappa
is a non-zero constant and its torsion
\tau
is:

(1)

in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\tau(s)=a_{1}s+a_{2}
;

(2)

in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\tau(s)=a_{1}\sin s+a_{2}\cos s
;

(3)

in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
,
\tau(s)=a_{1}\sinh s+a_{2}\cosh s
,

where
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
.

The proof of this theorem will be done in Sects.
3
,
4
and
5
in each one of the space forms
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, respectively. In Sect.
2
we present all conformal vector fields in space forms and we calculate the curvature and the torsion of conformal trajectories for proper conformal vector fields.

2.
Conformal vector fields in space forms

In
3
-dimensional space forms, the space of conformal vector fields is
10
-dimensional,
from which
6
correspond to Killing vector fields. We describe in each space form these vector fields. General references are
[
2

]

(1)

Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Consider
(x,y,z)
canonical coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and let

\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle=dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}
be the Euclidean metric. The Killing vector fields are generated by the translations
\partial_{x}
,
\partial_{y}
and
\partial_{z}
and the rotations
-y\partial_{x}+x\partial_{y}
,
-z\partial_{x}+x\partial_{z}
and
-z\partial_{y}+y\partial_{z}
. The proper conformal vector fields are generated by the radial vector field

(3)

V=x\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}+z\partial_{z},

and the vector fields

(4)

W(p)=\frac{|p|^{2}}{2}\vec{a}-\langle\vec{a},p\rangle p,\quad p\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

where
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The vector fields
V
and
W
are conformal because

\mathcal{L}_{V}\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle=2\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle
and

\mathcal{L}_{W}\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle=-2\langle\vec{a},p\rangle\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle
.

(2)

Sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Let
\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle
be the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.
Let
(x,y,z,t)
be canonical coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and let identify a vector field with its coordinates
(x,y,z,t)
with respect to the canonical basis
\{\partial_{x},\partial_{y},\partial_{z},\partial_{t}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Then the Killing vector fields are generated by

\displaystyle K_{1}=(-y,x,-t,z),\quad

\displaystyle K_{2}=(-y,x,t,-z),

\displaystyle K_{3}=(-z,-t,x,y,)\quad

\displaystyle K_{4}=(-z,t,x,-y),

\displaystyle K_{5}=(-t,z,-y,x),\quad

\displaystyle K_{6}=(-t,-z,y,x).

Proper conformal vector fields are the projections on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
of the analogous vector fields
W
given in (
4
) but defined in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Thus, there is a description of these vector fields free of coordinates, namely,

(5)

V(p)=\vec{a}-\langle\vec{a},p\rangle p,\quad p\in\mathbb{S}^{3},

where
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Here
\mathcal{L}_{V}\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle=-2\langle\vec{a},p\rangle\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle
.

(3)

Hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Consider the Lorentzian model, that is, the Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{4}
with canonical coordinates
(x,y,z,t)
, endowed with the Lorentzian metric

\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle=dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}-dt^{2}
.
Then
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is the quadric
\mathbb{H}^{3}=\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:\langle p,p\rangle=-1,\penalty\ t>0\}
. As in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, we identify a vector field with its coordinates with respect to the canonical basis of tangent vectors. The Killing vector fields are generated by

\displaystyle K_{1}=(-y,x,t,z)\quad

\displaystyle K_{2}=(y,-x,t.z)

\displaystyle K_{3}=(-z,t,x,y)\quad

\displaystyle K_{4}=(z,t,-x,y)

\displaystyle K_{5}=(t,-z,y,x)\quad

\displaystyle K_{6}=(t,z,-y,x).

Similarly as in the case of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, the space of proper conformal vector fields are generated by

(6)

V(p)=\vec{a}+\langle\vec{a},p\rangle p,\quad p\in\mathbb{H}^{3},

where
\vec{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
. In this case
\mathcal{L}_{W}\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle=2\langle\vec{a},p\rangle\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle
.

Let
V\in\mathfrak{X}(M^{3})
be a conformal vector field with
\mathcal{L}_{V}\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle=\lambda\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle
. Let
\gamma\colon I\to M^{3}
be a regular curve parametrized by arc-length. If
\gamma
is a geodesic, then it is immediate the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

A geodesic
\gamma\colon I\to M^{3}
is a conformal trajectory of
V
if and only if
V(\gamma(s))
and
\gamma^{\prime}(s)
are collinear for all
s\in I
.

From now on, suppose that
\gamma
is not a geodesic. Then we can assign to
\gamma
its Frenet frame
\{T,N,B\}
, where
T(s)=\gamma^{\prime}(s)
and
T\times N=B
.
The Frenet equations are given by

\begin{split}\nabla_{T}T&=\kappa N,\\
\nabla_{T}N&=-\kappa T+\tau B,\\
\nabla_{T}B&=-\tau B,\end{split}

where
\kappa
and
\tau
are the curvature and the torsion of
\gamma
, respectively. Let denote
V(s)=V(\gamma(s))

In the following result, we study the tangent part of
V
along
\gamma
for those conformal vector fields
V
satisfying that
\nabla_{T}V
is proportional to
T
along
\gamma
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\gamma\colon I\to M^{3}
be a curve parametrized by arc-length. If
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory of
V
then

(7)

\langle V(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\int^{s}\lambda(\gamma(t))dt.

If, in addition,
\nabla_{T}V
is proportional to
T
for all
s\in I
, then the curvature
\kappa
is constant,
\kappa(s)=\kappa_{0}
and the torsion is

(8)

\tau(s)=q\langle V(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle.

Proof.

We know
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\nabla_{T}T=\kappa N
. We now compute the right hand-side of (
2
). For this, let us write

(9)

V(s)=a(s)T(s)+b(s)N(s)+c(s)B(s),

where
a,b,c\in C^{\infty}(I)
. Then
V\times\gamma^{\prime}=V\times T=cN-bB
. Thus Eq. (
2
) becomes

\kappa N=q(cN-bB).

This implies
b=0
and
\kappa=qc
. In particular, we have
c\not=0
.
Using this, we obtain

(10)

\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}V=a^{\prime}T+\kappa(a-\frac{\tau}{q})N+\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{q}B.

Multiplying by
T
and using (
1
) because
V
is a conformal vector field, we have

\lambda=\lambda\langle\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle=2\langle\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}V,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle=2a^{\prime}.

This proves (
7
). If now we assume that
\nabla_{T}V
is proportional to
T
,
then (
10
) yields
\kappa^{\prime}(s)=0
for all
s\in I
and
\tau(s)=qa(s)
.
Thus
\kappa
is a nonzero constant and the torsion is given by (
8
)
because of (
7
).
∎

We can use this proposition for the proper conformal vector field in space forms described previously.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\colon I\to M^{3}
be a curve parametrized by arc-length, where
M^{3}
is a space form
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{H}^{3}

\gamma
is not a geodesic.

(1)

Case
\mathbb{R}^{3}

V
be the radial vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined in (
3
). If
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory of

, then the curvature of
\gamma
is constant and its torsion is

\tau(s)=q\langle\gamma(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle.

(2)

Case
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Let
V
be a proper conformal vector field defined in (
5
). If
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory of
V
, then the curvature of
\gamma
is constant and its torsion is

\tau(s)=q\langle\vec{a},\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle.

(3)

Case
\mathbb{H}^{3}
. Let
V
be a proper conformal vector field defined in (

). If
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory of
V
, then the curvature of
\gamma

\tau(s)=q\langle\vec{a},\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle.

Proof.

In all cases, we have
\nabla_{T}V
is proportional to
T

2.2

∎

3.
The Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
: the radial vector field

In this section we study the conformal trajectories of the radial vector field
V
defined in (
3
). Notice that the conformal transformations of
V
are dilations from the origin. By Prop.
2.1
, a straight-line
\gamma(s)=p+s\vec{v}
is a conformal trajectory of
V
if and only if
p
and
\vec{v}
are collinear, that
\gamma
is the straight-line acrosses the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

From now, we will assume that
\gamma
is not a straight-line. Suppose that
\gamma
is parametrized by arc-length. The left hand-side of (
2
) is
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime\prime}
. Thus
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory if and only if

(11)

\gamma^{\prime\prime}=q\,\gamma\times\gamma^{\prime}.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory of the radial vector field (
3
),
then
\gamma
is a curve with constant curvature
\kappa(s)=\kappa_{0}
, and its
torsion is an affine function in the parameter of the curve. Moreover,

(12)

\gamma(s)=(s+a_{0})\gamma^{\prime}(s)+\frac{1}{q}\gamma^{\prime}(s)\times\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s).

Proof.

Notice that
\nabla_{T}V=\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}V=\gamma^{\prime}=T
. Thus we are under the assumptions of Prop.
2.2
, in particular, the curvature
\kappa
is constant and (
7
) gives

\langle\gamma(s),\gamma^{\prime}(s)\rangle=\int^{s}\ ds=s+a_{0},

where
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is an integration constant. Moreover, from (
8
), we obtain
\tau(s)=q(s+a_{0})
. Using the same notation as in the proof of Prop.
2.2
, equation (
10
) implies
c(s)=c_{0}\not=0
is constant and

\kappa(s)=qc_{0}
. Notice that
cB=\frac{\kappa}{q}T\times N=\frac{1}{q}\gamma^{\prime}\times\gamma^{\prime\prime}
. This yields (
12
).
∎

Equation (
12
) implies that the radial vector of
\gamma
lies in the rectifying plane at each point. This class of curves were studied by B-Y. Chen in
[
1
]
. However identity (
12
) does not characterize conformal trajectories of the radial vector field. This is because the curvature and torsion determine a spatial curve of
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\mathbb{R}^{3}

2

A consequence of (
12

|\gamma(s)|^{2}=(s+a_{0})^{2}+c_{0}^{2}.

This allows to give the converse of Thm.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\gamma\colon I\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a curve parametrized by arc-length. Suppose that the curvature of
\gamma
is constant,
\kappa(s)=\kappa_{0}

is a conformal trajectory of

Proof.

Differentiating twice (

\langle\gamma,T\rangle=s+a_{0}
and
\langle\gamma,N\rangle=0
. By expressing

Then
\gamma\times\gamma^{\prime}=a_{1}N

∎

4.
The unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}

In this section we study the conformal trajectories on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{3}
for all proper conformal vector fields. We know from Sect.
2
that the proper conformal vector fields on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
are generated by the vector fields
V
defined in (
5
). Recall that
\lambda(p)=-2\langle\vec{a},p\rangle
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
be a proper conformal vector field given by (
5
).
Let
\gamma\colon I\to\mathbb{S}^{3}
be a curve parametrized by arc-length. If
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory of
V
, then

(17)

\langle\gamma(s),\vec{a}\rangle=a_{1}\cos(s)+a_{2}\sin(s),

where
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\gamma
is a geodesic, then
\gamma
is a great circle of

\mathbb{S}^{3}
obtained by intersecting the sphere with a 2-plane containing
\vec{a}
.
Otherwise, the curvature of
\gamma
is constant,

\kappa(s)=|q|\sqrt{|\vec{a}|^{2}-(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})}
,
and the torsion is

\tau(s)=-q(a_{1}\sin(s)-a_{2}\cos(s)).

Proof.

We know the relation between the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and
that of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
\nabla^{e}
, namely,

\nabla_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}^{e}Y+\langle X,Y\rangle p.

As
\gamma
is parametrized by arc-length, we deduce that

\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime\prime}+\gamma
.

Let
\omega
be the volume element on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
defined by
\omega(u,v,w)=\mbox{det}(u,v,w,p)
, where
u,v,w\in T_{p}\mathbb{S}^{3}
. If
u,v\in T_{p}\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
the cross product
u\times v
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
is defined as the unique vector in
T_{p}\mathbb{S}^{3}
such that
\langle u\times v,w\rangle=\omega(u,v,w)=\mbox{det}(u,v,w,p)
for any
w\in T_{p}\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Therefore, the cross product
V\times\gamma^{\prime}
may be expressed as

(18)

V\times\gamma^{\prime}=\left|\begin{matrix}a&x&x^{\prime}&\partial_{x}\\
b&y&y^{\prime}&\partial_{y}\\
c&z&z^{\prime}&\partial_{z}\\
d&t&t^{\prime}&\partial_{t}\end{matrix}\right|

where
\vec{a}=(a,b,c,d)
.
Obviously,
V\times\gamma^{\prime}
is perpendicular to
\vec{a}
.
Thus, taking the scalar product with
\vec{a}
in (
2
) we obtain

\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\langle\gamma,\vec{a}\rangle+\langle\gamma,\vec{a}\rangle=0.

It follows that there exist two constants
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
such that

\langle\gamma(s),\vec{a}\rangle=a_{1}\cos(s)+a_{2}\sin(s).

By definition of curvature, we have

\kappa^{2}=\langle\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime},\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}\rangle
.

The special case when
\gamma
is a geodesic implies on one hand that

\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s)+\gamma(s)=0
and on the other hand
V(s)\times\gamma^{\prime}(s)=0
.
The first equation yields
\gamma(s)=\cos(s)\vec{v}+\sin(s)\vec{w}
,
where
\vec{v}
and
\vec{w}
are unitary and perpendicular. The second condition
implies that the first three columns in the determinant (
18
) are
linearly dependent. As a consequence,
\vec{a}
lies in the 2-plane spanned by

\vec{v}
and
\vec{w}
. This proves the first statement of theorem.

If
\gamma
is not a geodesic, the unit normal vector is defined by

\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\kappa N
.
To obtain the curvature, we use (
17
) to compute

\begin{split}\langle V\times\gamma^{\prime},V\times\gamma^{\prime}\rangle&=\langle V,V\rangle\langle\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle-\langle V,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle^{2}=\langle\vec{a},\vec{a}\rangle-\langle\gamma,\vec{a}\rangle^{2}-\langle\gamma^{\prime},\vec{a}\rangle^{2}\\
&=|\vec{a}|^{2}-(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}).\end{split}

Hence
\gamma
has constant curvature

\kappa=|q|\sqrt{|\vec{a}|^{2}-(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})}
. The expression of the torsion
\tau
is obtained by Thm.
2.3
and identity (
17
).

∎

Example 4.2
.

We prove that non-great circles of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
are conformal trajectories. To be precise, let
\vec{a}=(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
and the corresponding conformal vector field
V(p)=\vec{a}-\langle\vec{a},p\rangle p

\vec{a}^{\perp}
consider the orthonormal basis
\{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}\}
given by

\epsilon_{1}=\frac{(-d,c,-b,a)}{|\vec{a}|}\penalty\ ,\epsilon_{2}=\frac{(-c,-d,a,b)}{|\vec{a}|}\penalty\ ,\epsilon_{3}=\frac{(-b,a,d,-c)}{|\vec{a}|}\penalty\ .

Let
R\in(0,1)

\gamma

\mathbb{S}^{3}

\gamma(s)=R\Big(\cos\frac{s}{R}\penalty\ \epsilon_{1}+\sin\frac{s}{R}\penalty\ \epsilon_{2}\Big)+\sqrt{1-R^{2}}\penalty\ \epsilon_{3}.

Obviously
\gamma

V(\gamma(s))=\vec{a}

\begin{split}\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}&=\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s)+\gamma(s)=\frac{R^{2}-1}{R}\left(\cos\frac{s}{R}\penalty\ \epsilon_{1}+\sin\frac{s}{R}\penalty\ \epsilon_{2}\right)+\sqrt{1-R^{2}}\penalty\ \epsilon_{3},\\

By choosing
q=-\frac{\sqrt{1-R^{2}}}{|\vec{a}|R}
, then
\gamma
satisfies Eq. (
2

\langle\gamma(s),\vec{a}\rangle=0
and thus
a_{1}=a_{2}=0
in (
17
). This shows that the torsion of
\gamma
is
0

\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

We particularize for a conformal vector field. Let

in (

Here

Consider the Lorentzian model for

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
V\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{H}^{3})
be a proper conformal vector field given by (
6
).
Let
\gamma\colon I\to\mathbb{H}^{3}
be a curve parametrized by arc-length. If
\gamma
is a conformal trajectory of
V
, then

(19)

\langle\gamma(s),\vec{a}\rangle=a_{1}\cosh(s)+a_{2}\sinh(s),

where
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. If
\gamma
is a geodesic, then
\gamma
is obtained by intersecting
\mathbb{H}^{3}
with a
Minkowski
2
-plane containing
\vec{a}
. If
\gamma
is not a geodesic, then the curvature of
\gamma
is constant,

\kappa(s)=|q|\sqrt{||\vec{a}|^{2}+a_{1}^{2}-a_{2}^{2}|}
,
and the torsion is

\tau(s)=q(a_{1}\sinh(s)+a_{2}\cosh(s)).

Proof.

The proof is similar as in Thm.
4.1
. We know the relation between the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
and
that of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
\nabla^{e}
, namely,

\nabla_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}^{e}Y-\langle X,Y\rangle p.

As
\gamma
is parametrized by arc-length, we deduce that

\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime\prime}-\gamma
. Since
\langle V\times\gamma^{\prime},\vec{a}\rangle=0
, from (
2
) we have

\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\langle\gamma,\vec{a}\rangle-\langle\gamma,\vec{a}\rangle=0.

This gives (
19
).
If
\gamma
is a geodesic on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, then
\gamma^{\prime\prime}(s)-\gamma(s)=0
and this implies

\gamma(s)=\cosh(s)\vec{v}+\sinh(s)\vec{w}
, where
\langle\vec{v},\vec{v}\rangle=-1
,

\langle\vec{w},\vec{w}\rangle=1
and
\langle\vec{v},\vec{w}\rangle=0
.
Since
V(s)\times\gamma^{\prime}(s)=0
, then
\vec{a}
lies in the 2-plane spanned by

\vec{v}
and
\vec{w}
.

Suppose now that
\gamma
is not a geodesic. Using (
19
) and
\kappa N=V\times\gamma^{\prime}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\kappa^{2}&=\langle V\times\gamma^{\prime},V\times\gamma^{\prime}\rangle=\langle V,V\rangle\langle\gamma^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\rangle-\langle V,\gamma^{\prime}\rangle^{2}\\
&=\langle\vec{a},\vec{a}\rangle+\langle\gamma,\vec{a}\rangle^{2}-\langle\gamma^{\prime},\vec{a}\rangle^{2}=|\vec{a}|^{2}+a_{1}^{2}-a_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

Thus
\gamma
has constant curvature. For the last part of the theorem, we use Thm.
2.3
in combination with (
19
).

∎

Example 5.3
.

Let
\vec{a}=(1,0,0,0)

Proposition 5.4
.

If
\gamma
is conformal trajectory on
\mathbb{H}^{3}

then

must be a space-like vector.

Proof.

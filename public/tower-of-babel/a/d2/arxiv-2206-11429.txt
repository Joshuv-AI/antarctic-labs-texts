Homotopy cartesian squares in extriangulated categories ★ † † footnotetext: ★ Panyue Zhou was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant No. 11901190).
Jing He, Chenbei Xie, Panyue Zhou

Abstract
Let ( 𝒞 , 𝔼 , 𝔰 ) (\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s}) be an extriangulated category. Given a composition of two commutative squares in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , if two commutative squares are homotopy cartesian, then their composition is also a homotopy cartesian. This covers the result by Mac Lane (1998) for abelian categories and the result by Christensen and Frankland (2022) for triangulated categories. Keywords: extriangulated categories; homotopy cartesian squares; triangulated categories; abelian categories; push-out and pull-back 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10



1
Introduction

Most of homological algebra can be carried out in the setting of abelian
categories, a class of categories which includes all categories of modules.
We recall the properties of pull-back and push-out squares in an abelian category.
Given two morphisms
x^{\prime}\colon A^{\prime}\to B^{\prime}
and
b\colon B\to B^{\prime}
. Then a commutative diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x^{\prime}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}}

is
pull-back
if and only if

0\xrightarrow{~}A\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}A^{\prime}\oplus B\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x^{\prime}&-b\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}B^{\prime}

is left exact. Given two morphisms
x\colon A\to B
and
a\colon A\to A^{\prime}
. Then a commutative diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x^{\prime}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}}

is
push-out
if and only if

A\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}A^{\prime}\oplus B\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x^{\prime}&-b\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}B^{\prime}\xrightarrow{~}0

is right exact. By the above properties, we can obtain that the commutative diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x^{\prime}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}}

is
pull-back and push-out
if and only if

0\xrightarrow{~}A\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}A^{\prime}\oplus B\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x^{\prime}&-b\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}B^{\prime}\xrightarrow{~}0

is exact.

Triangulated categories were introduced in the mid 1960’s by Verdier
in his thesis. Having their origins in algebraic geometry and
algebraic topology, triangulated categories have by now become indispensable
in many different areas of mathematics.
Neeman
[
N
, Definition 1.4.1]
introduced the notion of homotopy cartesian squares in triangulated categories.
More precisely, let
\mathcal{C}
be a triangulated category with shift functor
\Sigma
. A commutative square

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x^{\prime}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}}

s called
homotopy cartesian
if there exists a triangle

A\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}A^{\prime}\oplus B\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x^{\prime}&-b\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}B^{\prime}\xrightarrow{~\partial~}\Sigma A

The morphism
\partial
is called a
differential
of the homotopy cartesian square.
We can think of a homotopy cartesian square as the triangulated analogue of
a pull-back and push-out square in an abelian category.

Recently, extriangulated categories were recently introduced by Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
by extracting those properties of
{\rm Ext}^{1}(-,-)
on exact categories and on triangulated categories that seem relevant from the point of view of cotorsion pairs. In particular, triangulated categories and exact categories are extriangulated categories. There are a lot of examples of extriangulated categories which are neither triangulated categories nor exact categories, see
[
NP
,
ZZ
,
HZZ
,
ZhZ
,
NP1
]
. Hence, many results on exact categories (abelian categories are exact categories) and triangulated categories can be unified in the same framework.
Motivated by this idea, we study
homotopy cartesian squares in an extriangulated category.
Let us recall the definition of homotopy cartesian squares in an extriangulated category.

Definition 1.1
.

[
H
, Definition 3.1]

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category.
Then a commutative square

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x^{\prime}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}}

is called
homotopy cartesian
if there exists an
\mathbb{E}
-triangle

A\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}A^{\prime}\oplus B\xrightarrow{~\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x^{\prime}&-b\end{smallmatrix}\right]~}B^{\prime}\overset{\theta}{\dashrightarrow}

The
\mathbb{E}
-extension
\theta\in\mathbb{E}(B^{\prime},A)
is called a
differential
of the homotopy cartesian square.

This gives a simultaneous generalization of push-out and pullback squares in an abelian category
and homotopy cartesian squares in triangulated categories.

The main result
of this article is the following, which is a simultaneous generalization of a result of
Mac Lane
[
M
]
for abelian categories and of a result of Christensen-Frankland
[
CF
]
for triangulated categories.

Theorem 1.2
.

(see Theorem
3.2
for more details)

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category.
Consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\scriptstyle{x}
\scriptstyle{\rm(I)}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b}
\scriptstyle{y}
\scriptstyle{\rm(II)}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{c}
\textstyle{A^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x^{\prime}}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{y^{\prime}}
\textstyle{C^{\prime}}

If the two squares
(I)
and

are homotopy cartesian in

,

Moreover, given differentials

\theta_{P}

\theta_{R}=x_{\ast}\theta_{P}~~\mbox{and}~~\theta_{L}=(y^{\prime})^{\ast}\theta_{P}.

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the definition
of an extriangulated category. In Section 3, we give a composition of two commutative squares, if both squares are homotopy cartesian, then their composition is also a homotopy cartesian.

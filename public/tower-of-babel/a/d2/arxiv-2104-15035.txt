The radial defocusing energy-supercritical NLS in dimension four
Chao Lu, Jiqiang Zheng

Abstract
. We consider the radial defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equations i ​ u t + Δ ​ u = | u | p ​ u iu_{t}+\Delta u=|u|^{p}u with supercritical exponent p > 4 p>4 in four space dimensions, and prove that any radial solution that remains bounded in the critical Sobolev space must be global and scatter.



Key Words:
Nonlinear Schrödinger equation; scattering; long-time Strichartz estimate; energy supercritical;
concentration-compactness.

1.
Introduction

This paper is
devoted to study the initial-value problem for defocusing
nonlinear Schrödinger equations of the form

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u=|u|^{p}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u(t,x):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
, and
s_{c}=2-\tfrac{2}{p}
with
p>4
. We prove that for
s_{c}>3/2
(i.e.
p>4
), any radial maximal-lifespan solution that remains uniformly bounded in
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
must be global and scatter. In
[
10
,
23
]
, the authors proved the analogous statement for (
1.1
) with
2<p\leq 4
and non-radial initial data. In this paper, we treat the remaining case
p>4
but for radial initial data.

The equation (
1.1
) has the scaling invariance symmetry:

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{\frac{2}{p}}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),

(1.2)

in the sense that both the equation and the
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
-norm are
invariant under the scaling transformation:

\|u_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=\|u(\lambda^{2}t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

This scaling defines a notion of
criticality
for (
1.1
). If we take
u_{0}\in\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
, then for
s=s_{c}
we call the problem (
1.1
)
critical
. For
s>s_{c}
we call the problem
subcritical
, while for
s<s_{c}
we call the problem
supercritical
.

If the solution
u
of (
1.1
) has sufficient decay at
infinity and smoothness, it conserves mass and energy:

\begin{split}M(u)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx=M(u_{0}),\\
E(u)&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{p+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u(t,x)|^{p+2}dx=E(u_{0}).\end{split}

(1.3)

As similarly explained in
[
2
]
, the above quantities are also
conserved for the energy solutions
u\in C^{0}_{t}(\mathbb{R},H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))
. We call
\dot{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
the energy space.

There is a lot of works on the global well-posedness and scattering
for Schrödinger equations

i\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=\pm|u|^{p}u,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\quad\text{(NLS)}

especially for mass-critical
(p=4/d)
or
energy-critical
(p=4/(d-2),~d\geq 3)
(NLS), most notably by Bourgain
[
1
]
,
Colliander, Keel, Staffilanni, Takaoka and Tao
[
4
]
, Kenig
and Merle
[
14
]
and Killip and Visan
[
18
,
19
]
and Visan

[
31
,
32
]
, and Dodson
[
9
]
for the energy-critical case and Tao,
Visan and Zhang
[
29
]
, Killip, Tao and Visan

[
16
]
, Killip, Visan and Zhang
[
21
]
and Dodson

[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
for the mass-critical case.

So far, there is no technology for treating large-data (NLS) without
some a priori control of a critical norm other than the
energy-critical (NLS) and mass-critical (NLS) at the present moment.
Kenig-Merle
[
15
]
first showed that if the radial solution
u
to NLS is
such that
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}(I;\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with

s_{c}=1/2
, then
u
is
global and scatters. They were able to handle this case by making
use of their concentration compactness technique (as in
[
14
]
),
together with the Lin-Strauss Morawetz inequality which scales like

\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Lately, such result has been extended to high dimensional and inter-critical cases by Murphy
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
.
In
[
17
]
, Killip–Visan
proved some cases of the energy-supercritical regime. In
particular, they deal with the case of a cubic nonlinearity for

d\geq 5
, along with some other cases for which
s_{c}>1
and

d\geq 5
. The restriction to high dimensions stems from
the so-called double Duhamel trick;
see
[
17
]
for more details. Recently, by making use of the
tool “long time Strichartz estimate” à la Dodson
[
5
]
, and a frequency-localized interaction Morawetz inequality, the authors

[
10
,
23
]
treat the case
2<p\leq 4
(i.e.
1<s_{c}\leq 3/2
) in four space dimensions. In this paper, we address
the remaining cases
p>4
but for radial initial data, where the techniques in
[
10
,
23
]
break down.

Now, let us make the notion of a solution more precise.
A function
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
on a non-empty
time interval
I\ni 0
is a
solution
to
(
1.1
) if
it belongs to
C_{t}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{4})\cap L_{t,x}^{3p}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{4})

for any compact interval
J\subset I
and
obeys the Duhamel formula

u(t)=e^{it\Delta}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F(u(s))\,ds

(1.4)

for each
t\in I
. We call
I

the
lifespan
of
u
. We say that
u
is a
maximal-lifespan solution

if it cannot be extended to any strictly larger interval.
We call
u

global
if
I=\mathbb{R}.

For a solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
), we define the
scattering size
of
u
on
I
by

S_{I}(u):=\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u(t,x)|^{3p}\,dx\,dt.

(1.5)

If there exists
t_{0}\in I
such that
S_{[t_{0},\sup I)}(u)=\infty
, we say that
u

blows up forward in time
. If there exists
t_{0}\in I
such that
S_{(\inf I,t_{0}]}(u)=\infty
, we say that
u

blows up backward in time
.

If
u
is a global and obeys
S_{\mathbb{R}}(u)<\infty
, then standard arguments show that
u

scatters
in the sense
tha there exist unique
u_{\pm}\in\dot{H}^{s_{c}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
such that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\|u(t)-e^{it\Delta}u_{\pm}\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=0.

Our main result is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s_{c}>3/2
, i.e.
p>4
. Suppose

u:~I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
is a radial maximal-lifespan solution to
(
1.1
) such that

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}).

(1.6)

Then
u
is global and scatters, with

S_{\mathbb{R}}(u)\leq C(\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))})

for some function
C:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty).

Let us turn now to an outline of the arguments we will use to establish Theorem
1.1
.

1.1.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
1.1

We will argue by contradiction.
For any
0\leq E_{0}<+\infty,
we define

L(E_{0}):=\sup\Big\{S_{I}(u):~u:~I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}\ \text{such\ that}\ \sup_{t\in I}\big\|u\big\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}\leq E_{0}\Big\},

where the supremum ranges all solutions
u:~I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) satisfying
\big\|u\big\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}\leq E_{0}.
Thus,
L:\ [0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is
a non-decreasing function. Moreover, from the small data theory (via Strichartz estimates and contraction mapping, cf.
[
2
,
20
]
), one has

L(E_{0})\lesssim E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad E_{0}\leq\eta_{0}^{2},

where
\eta_{0}=\eta(d)
is the threshold from the small data theory.

It follows from the stability theory that

L
is continuous. Thus, there must exist a unique critical

E_{c}\in(0,+\infty]
such that
L(E_{0})<+\infty
for
E_{0}<E_{c}
and

L(E_{0})=+\infty
for
E_{0}\geq E_{c}
. In particular, if

u:~I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
is a maximal-lifespan solution to
(
1.1
) such that
\sup\limits_{t\in I}\big\|u\big\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}<E_{c},
then
u
is global and moreover,

S_{\mathbb{R}}(u)\leq L\big(\big\|u\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\big).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is
equivalent to show
E_{c}=+\infty.
We
argue by contradiction. The failure of Theorem
1.1
would
imply the existence of very special class of solutions. On the other
hand, these solutions have so many good properties that they do not
exist. Thus we get a contradiction. While we will make some further
reductions later, the main property of the special counterexamples
is almost periodicity modulo symmetries:

Definition 1.2

(Almost periodic solutions)
.

Let
s_{c}>0.
A solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) is called
almost periodic
(
modulo symmetries
) if
u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})
and there exist functions

N:~I\to\mathbb{R}^{+},

x:I\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
and
C:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
for all
t\in I
and
\eta>0
,

\int_{|x-x(t)|\geq\frac{C(\eta)}{N(t)}}\big||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u(t,x)\big|^{2}\,dx+\int_{|\xi|\geq C(\eta)N(t)}|\xi|^{2s_{c}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\eta.

(1.7)

We call
N(t)
the
frequency scale function
,
x(t)
the
spatial center function
, and

C(\eta)
the
compactness modulus function
.

Remark 1.3
.

(i)
As a consequence of radiality, the solutions we consider can only concentrate near the spatial origin. In particular, we may take
x(t)\equiv 0
.

(ii)

The Arzelà–Ascoli theorem tells us that a family of functions
\mathcal{F}
is precompact in
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})

if and only if it is norm-bounded and there exists a compactness modulus function
C(\eta)
such that

\int_{|x|\geq C(\eta)}\big||\nabla|^{s_{c}}f(x)\big|^{2}\,dx+\int_{|\xi|\geq C(\eta)}|\xi|^{2s_{c}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\eta

uniformly for
f\in\mathcal{F}
. Thus we see that a solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
is radial almost periodic if and only if

\{u(t):t\in I\}\subset\{\lambda^{2/p}f(\lambda x):\lambda\in(0,\infty),\;\text{and}\;f\in K\}

for some compact
K\subset\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).
We deduce that there exists a function
c:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\int_{|x|\leq\frac{c(\eta)}{N(t)}}\big||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u(t,x)\big|^{2}\,dx+\int_{|\xi|\leq c(\eta)N(t)}|\xi|^{2s_{c}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq\eta

(1.8)

for all
t\in I
.

By the same argument as in
[
10
,
20
,
23
]
, we can show that the failure of Theorem
1.1
would
imply the existence of the almost periodic solutions as follows.

Theorem 1.4

(Reduction to almost periodic solutions)
.

If Theorem
1.1
fails, then there exists a radial maximal-lifespan solution
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) that is almost periodic and blows up in both time directions.

Furthermore, one can adopt the proof of
Lemma 5.18 in
[
20
]
to prove that the almost periodic
solutions satisfy the following local constancy property:

Lemma 1.5

(Local constancy)
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be a maximal-lifespan almost periodic solution to (
1.1
). Then there exists
\delta=\delta(u)>0
such that for all
t_{0}\in I
,

[t_{0}-\delta N(t_{0})^{-2},t_{0}+\delta N(t_{0})^{-2}]\subset I.

Moreover,
N(t)\sim_{u}N(t_{0})

for
|t-t_{0}|\leq\delta N(t_{0})^{-2}.

Definition 1.6

(Interval of local constancy)
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
almost periodic solution. We can divide
I
into consecutive
intervals
J_{k}
such that

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{t,x}^{3p}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}=1,~\text{and}~N(t)\equiv N_{k},~t\in J_{k}.

These intervals are called as the intervals of local constancy. If

J\subset I
is a union of consecutive intervals of local
constancy, then we have

\sum_{J_{k}}N(J_{k})^{1-2s_{c}}\sim\int_{J}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}dt.

(1.9)

Lemma
1.5
provides information about the behavior of the frequency scale at blowup (cf.
[
20
, Corollary 5.19]
):

Corollary 1.7

(
N(t)
at blowup)
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be a maximal-lifespan almost periodic solution to (
1.1
). If
T
is a finite endpoint of
I
then
N(t)\gtrsim_{u}|T-t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

We also need the following result, which relates the frequency scale
function of an almost periodic solution to its Strichartz norms.

Lemma 1.8

(Spacetime bounds)
.

Let
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
). Then, there holds

\int_{I}N(t)^{2}\,dt\lesssim_{u}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{q}\lesssim_{u}1+\int_{I}N(t)^{2}\,dt,

for
(q,r)
admissible
(
see Definition
2.2
below
)
with
q<\infty
.

Proof.

See Lemma 5.21 in
[
20
]
.
∎

We now refine the class of almost periodic solutions that we consider. By rescaling arguments as in
[
16
,
18
,
29
]
, we can guarantee that the almost periodic solutions we consider do not escape to arbitrarily low frequencies on at least half of their maximal lifespan, say
[0,T_{\rm max})
. Using Lemma
1.5
to divide
[0,T_{\rm max})
into characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
, we arrive at the following theorem.

Theorem 1.9

(Two scenarios for blowup)
.

If Theorem
1.1
fails, then there exists a raidal almost periodic solution
u:[0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
that blows up forward in time and satisfies

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}([0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}).

(1.10)

Furthermore, we may write
[0,T_{max})=\cup_{k}J_{k}
, where

N(t)\equiv N_{k}\geq 1\quad\text{for}\quad t\in J_{k},\quad\text{with}\quad|J_{k}|\sim_{u}N_{k}^{-2}.

(1.11)

We classify
u
according to the following two scenarios: either

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{\rm max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt<\infty\quad(\text{rapid frequency-cascade solution}),

(1.12)

or

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{\rm max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt=\infty\quad(\text{quasi-soliton solution}).

(1.13)

In view of this theorem, our goal is to preclude the possibilities
of all the scenarios in the sense of Theorem
1.9
.
The quantity appearing in (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) is related to the the
Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality
of
[
22
]
, which is given by

\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt\lesssim\||\nabla|^{1/2}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}.

(1.14)

Due to the weight
\tfrac{1}{|x|}
, the Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality is well suited for preventing concentration near the origin, and hence it is most effective in the radial setting. In fact, it is the use of Lin–Strauss Morawetz inequality that leads to the restriction to the radial setting in Theorem
1.1
. We cannot use this estimate directly, however, as the solutions we consider need only belong to
L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}
(and so the right-hand side of (
1.14
) need be infinite).

A further manifestation of the minimality of
u
as a blow-up
solution is the absence of the scattered wave at the endpoints of
the lifespan
I
; more formally, we have the following Duhamel
formula, which is important for
showing the additional
decay and negative regularity for the rapid frequency cascade. This
is a robust consequence of almost periodicity modulo symmetries;
see, for example,
[
4
]
.

Lemma 1.10

(Reduced Duhamel formula)
.

Let
u:[0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be a maximal-lifespan almost periodic solution to (
1.1
). Then for all
t\in[0,T_{\rm max})
we have

u(t)=\lim_{T\nearrow T_{\rm max}}i\int_{t}^{T}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F(u(s))\,ds

(1.15)

as a weak limit in
\dot{H}_{x}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
.

We will use this lemma to show that the almost periodic
solution enjoys the “long time Strichartz estimate” à la
Dodson
[
5
]
, see Theorem
3.1
below.
We will utilize this “long time Strichartz estimate” and the no-waste Duhamel formula to
show the rapid frequency cascade solutions admit the negative
regularity property. Then making use of a similar method used in

[
16
,
17
,
21
]
, we can also show that the mass of the
rapid frequency cascade solution is zero and so we get a
contradiction.

Finally, to preclude the quasi-soliton solutions, one can show that it admits additional decay in the sprit of no-waste
Duhamel formula and the compactness, see
[
23
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 1.11

(Additional decay,
[
23
]
)
.

Let
s_{c}>1
. Suppose
u:[0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
is an almost periodic solution to (
1.1
) such that
\inf_{t\in[0,T_{\rm max})}N(t)\geq 1
. Then

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{p+1}([0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}).

(1.16)

This together with long-time Strichartz estimate allows us to show
the localized Morawetz inequality enjoys the bounds as
follows.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
u
be a quasi-soliton solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of Theorem

1.9
. Then for any
\eta>0
, there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\eta)

such that for all
N\leq N_{0}

\begin{split}K_{I}\lesssim_{u}\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|u_{\geq N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\;dx\;dt\lesssim_{u}\eta\big(N^{1-2s_{c}}+K_{I}\big),\end{split}

for all
I\subset[0,+\infty),
where
K_{I}:=\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}dt.

This leads to a contradiction in the case when
\int_{0}^{\infty}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}=+\infty,
since
K_{I}
can be taken arbitrarily large.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give some
useful lemmas. In Section
3
, we show the long time Strichartz
estimate. In Section
4
, we exclude the existence of rapid frequency cascade solutions in the sense of
Theorem
1.9
. In Section

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Some notation

For nonnegative quantities
X
and
Y
, we will write
X\lesssim Y
to denote the estimate
X\leq CY
for some
C>0
. If
X\lesssim Y\lesssim X
, we will write
X\sim Y
. Dependence of implicit constants on the power
p
or the dimension will be suppressed; dependence on additional parameters will be indicated by subscripts. For example,
X\lesssim_{u}Y
indicates
X\leq CY
for some
C=C(u)
.

We will use the expression
\text{\O}(X)
to denote a finite linear combination of terms
that resemble
X
up to Littlewood–Paley projections, complex conjugation, and/or maximal functions.
For example, We will use the expression
X\pm
to denote
X\pm\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

For a spacetime slab
I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
, we write
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})
for the Banach space of functions
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}:=\bigg(\int_{I}\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\bigg)^{1/q},

with the usual adjustments when
q
or
r
is infinity. When
q=r
, we abbreviate
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{q}=L_{t,x}^{q}
.
We will also often abbreviate
\|f\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}
to
\|f\|_{L_{x}^{r}}.
For
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
we use
r^{\prime}
to denote the dual exponent to
r
, i.e. the solution to
\tfrac{1}{r}+\tfrac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1.

We define the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi):=\tfrac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx.

We can then define the fractional differentiation operator
|\nabla|^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
via

\widehat{|\nabla|^{s}f}(\xi):=|\xi|^{s}\hat{f}(\xi),

with the corresponding homogeneous Sobolev norm

\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}:=\||\nabla|^{s}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

2.2.
Basic harmonic analysis

We will make frequent use of the Littlewood–Paley projection operators.
Specifically, we let
\varphi
be a radial bump function supported
on the ball
|\xi|\leq 2
and equal to 1 on the ball
|\xi|\leq 1
.
For
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define the Littlewood–Paley projection operators by

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{\leq N}f}(\xi):=\mathcal{F}(f_{\leq N})(\xi):=\varphi(\xi/N)\widehat{f}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{>N}f}(\xi):=\mathcal{F}(f_{>N})(\xi):=(1-\varphi(\xi/N))\widehat{f}(\xi),

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{N}f}(\xi):=\mathcal{F}(f_{N})(\xi):=(\varphi(\xi/N)-\varphi({2\xi}/{N}))\widehat{f}(\xi).

We may also define

P_{M<\cdot\leq N}:=P_{\leq N}-P_{\leq M}=\sum_{M<N^{\prime}\leq N}P_{N}^{\prime}

for
M<N.
All such summations should be understood to be over
N^{\prime}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}.

The Littlewood–Paley operators commute with derivative operators,
the free propagator, and the conjugation operation. These operators are
self-adjoint and bounded on every
L^{p}_{x}
and
\dot{H}^{s}_{x}
space
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\geq 0
. They also obey the
following standard Bernstein estimates:
For
1\leq r\leq q\leq\infty
and
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{\pm s}P_{N}f\big\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle\thicksim N^{\pm s}\big\|P_{N}f\big\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s}P_{\leq N}f\big\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s}\big\|P_{\leq N}f\big\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{4}{r}-\frac{4}{q}}\big\|P_{\leq N}f\big\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

We will need the following fractional calculus estimates and paraproduct estimates.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

(
Fractional product rule,
[
3

)

Let
s\geq 0
, and
1<r,r_{j},q_{j}<\infty
satisfy

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{r_{i}}+\frac{1}{q_{i}}
for

\big\||\nabla|^{s}(fg)\big\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{L_{x}^{r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}}\big\||\nabla|^{s}g\big\|_{{L_{x}^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}}+\big\||\nabla|^{s}f\big\|_{{L_{x}^{r_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}}\|g\|_{{L_{x}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}}.

(2.1)

(ii)

(
Paraproduct estimate,
[
23
]
)

Let
0<s<1
. If
1<r<r_{1}<\infty
and
1<r_{2}<\infty
satisfy

\tfrac{1}{r_{1}}+\tfrac{1}{r_{2}}=\tfrac{1}{r}+\tfrac{s}{4}<1,

then

\||\nabla|^{-s}(fg)\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{-s}f\|_{L_{x}^{r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\||\nabla|^{s}g\|_{L_{x}^{r_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(2.2)

(iii)

(
Basic estimate
)
From the Hardy inequality and interpolation, we easily get for

\big\||\nabla|^{s}\big(\tfrac{x}{|x|}u\big)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\big\||\nabla|^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(2.3)

2.3.
Strichartz estimates

Let
e^{it\Delta}
be the free Schrödinger propagator, given by

[e^{it\Delta}f](x)=\tfrac{-1}{16\pi^{2}t^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{i|x-y|^{2}/4t}f(y)\,dy

(2.4)

for
t\neq 0
. From this explicit formula we can read off the dispersive estimate

\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim|t|^{-2}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

for
t\neq 0
. Interpolating with
\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\equiv\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}
then yields

\big\|e^{it\Delta}f\big\|_{L^{q}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C|t|^{-2(1-\frac{2}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

(2.5)

for
t\neq 0
and
2\leq r\leq\infty
, where
\tfrac{1}{q}+\tfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1.
This estimate implies the standard Strichartz estimates, which we will state below. First, we need to make the following definition:

Definition 2.2

(Admissible pairs)
.

A pair of exponents
(q,r)
is called
Schrödinger admissible
if
2\leq q,r\leq\infty

and
\tfrac{2}{q}+\tfrac{4}{r}=2.
For a spacetime slab
I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
, we define the Strichartz norm

\|u\|_{S^{0}(I)}:=\sup\big\{\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}:(q,r)\text{ Schr\"{o}dinger admissible}\big\}.

We denote
S^{0}(I)
to be the closure of all
test functions under this norm and write
N^{0}(I)
for the dual of

S^{0}(I)
.

We may now state the standard Strichartz estimates in the form that we will need them.

Proposition 2.3

(Strichartz
[
11
,
13
,
27
]
)
.

Let
s\geq 0
and suppose
u:I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
is a solution to
(i\partial_{t}+\Delta)u=F
. Then

\||\nabla|^{s}u\|_{S^{0}(I)}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{s}u(t_{0})\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}+\||\nabla|^{s}F\|_{N^{0}(I)}

for any
t_{0}\in I
.

3.
Long time Strichartz estimate

In this section we establish a long-time Strichartz estimate for almost periodic solutions to (
1.1
) as in Theorem
1.9
.

Theorem 3.1

(Long-time Strichartz estimate)
.

Let
s_{c}>3/2,
and let
u:[0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost
periodic solution to (
1.1
) with
N(t)\equiv N_{k}\geq 1
on
each characteristic
J_{k}\subset[0,T_{\rm max}).
Suppose

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}([0,T_{\rm max}),\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4})),\quad\text{for some}\quad s_{c}-\tfrac{1}{2}<s\leq s_{c}.

(3.1)

Then on
any compact time interval
I\subset[0,T_{max}),
which is a union of
characteristic subintervals
J_{k}
, and for any
N>0,
we
have

\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim_{u}1+N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2},

(3.2)

where
K_{I}:=\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt
and
\sigma(s)=2s_{c}-s-\frac{1}{2}
. In particular, for
s=s_{c}
, we have

\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim_{u}1+N^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}K_{I}^{1/2}.

(3.3)

Moreover, for any
\eta>0
, there
exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\eta)
such that for all
N\leq N_{0},

\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim_{u}\eta\big(1+N^{s_{c}-1/2}K_{I}^{1/2}\big).

(3.4)

Furthermore, the implicit constants in (
3.2
) and
(
3.4
) are independent of
I
.

We fix
I\subset[0,T_{\rm max})
to be a union of contiguous characteristic subintervals. Throughout the proof,
all spacetime norms will be taken over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
unless explicitly stated otherwise. For
N>0
, we define the quantities

B(N):=\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{k}(N):=\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

We will prove Theorem
3.1
by induction. For the base case, we have the following

Lemma 3.2
.

The estimate (
3.2
) holds for
N\geq N_{\rm max}:=\sup\limits_{J_{k}\subset I}N_{k}.

Proof.

We have by Lemma
1.8

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}1+\int_{I}N(t)^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}1+N_{\rm max}^{2s_{c}-1}\int_{I}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+N^{2\sigma(s)}K_{I},

which shows (
3.2
).
∎

To complete the induction, we establish a recurrence relation for
B(N)
. To this end, we first let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be small parameters to be determined later. We use the compact property to find
c=c(\varepsilon)
so that

\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq cN(t)}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}<\varepsilon.

(3.5)

The recurrence relations we will use take the following form.

Lemma 3.3

(Recurrence relations for
B(N)
)
.

\begin{split}B(N)\lesssim_{u}&\inf_{t\in I}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}(t)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0})N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}+\varepsilon^{p}B(N/\varepsilon_{0})\\
&+\sum_{M>N/\varepsilon_{0}}(\tfrac{N}{M})^{s_{c}}B(M)\end{split}

(3.6)

uniformly in
N
for some positive constant

C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0})
.

We also have the following refinement of (
3.6
):

\begin{split}B(N)\lesssim_{u}g(N)\big(1+N^{s_{c}-1/2}K_{I}^{1/2}\big)+\varepsilon^{p}B(N/\varepsilon_{0})+\sum_{M>N/\varepsilon_{0}}(\tfrac{N}{M})^{s_{c}}B(M),\end{split}

(3.7)

where

g(N):=\inf_{t\in I}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}(t)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0})\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(3.8)

Proof.

First, by Strichartz estimate, we get

B(N)\lesssim\inf_{t\in I}\big\|{|\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}(t)}\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq N}F(u)\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}}.

(3.9)

Hence we only need to estimate the nonlinear term. To this end, we decompose

F(u)=F(u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}})+\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}})\big).

Using Bernstein, Hölder, Sobolev embedding, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}})\big)\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}}\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{s_{c}}\big\|F(u)-F(u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}})\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{s_{c}}\|u\|^{p}_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2p}}\|u_{>N/\varepsilon_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{M>N/\varepsilon_{0}}\big(\tfrac{N}{M}\big)^{s_{c}}B(M).

(3.10)

To estimate the term
\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq N}F(u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}})\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}},
we restrict our attention to a single characteristic interval
J_{k}
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq N}F(u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}})\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2p}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{p}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq c(\varepsilon)N_{k}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{p}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}

(3.11)

\displaystyle+\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{>c(\varepsilon)N_{k}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{p}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(3.12)

By (
3.5
), we have

\eqref{equ:term21}\lesssim\varepsilon^{p}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

Note that it suffices to consider the case
c(\varepsilon)N_{k}\leq{N}/{\varepsilon_{0}}
in (
3.12
), i.e
\frac{N}{c(\varepsilon)N_{k}\varepsilon_{0}}\geq 1
. In this case, using Hölder, Bernstein, Sobolev embedding, interpolation, Lemma
1.8
, and (
1.6
), we derive that

\displaystyle\eqref{equ:term22}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{N}{c(\varepsilon)N_{k}\varepsilon_{0}}\big)^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\tfrac{N}{c(\varepsilon)N_{k}\varepsilon_{0}}\big)^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\tfrac{N}{\varepsilon_{0}}\big)^{s_{c}-s}\|u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq N}F(u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}})\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{J_{k}\subset I}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}P_{\leq N}F(u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}})\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{J_{k}\subset I}\Big[\varepsilon^{2p}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}+\big(\tfrac{N}{cN_{k}\varepsilon_{0}}\big)^{2(s_{c}-\frac{1}{2})}\big(\tfrac{N}{\varepsilon_{0}}\big)^{2(s_{c}-s)}\|u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2p}B(N/\varepsilon_{0})+c^{-2(s_{c}-\frac{1}{2})}\varepsilon_{0}^{-2\sigma(s)}\sup_{J_{k}\subset I}\|u_{\leq N/\varepsilon_{0}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}^{s}(J_{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}N^{2\sigma(s)}K_{I}.

This together with (
3.10
) implies Lemma
3.3
.

∎

Next, we turn to prove Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

From Lemma
3.2
, we see that (
3.2
) holds for

N\geq N_{\rm max}
. That is, we have

B(N)\leq C(u)\big(1+N^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\big),

(3.13)

for all
N\geq N_{\rm max}
. Clearly this
inequality remains true if we replace
C(u)
by any larger constant.

We now suppose that (
3.13
) holds for frequencies above
N
and use the recurrence formula (
3.6
) to show that (
3.13
) holds at frequency
N/2
. Choosing
\varepsilon_{0}<1/2
, we use (
3.6
) and (
3.13
) to obtain

\displaystyle B\big(N/2\big)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(u)\Big(1+C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0})\big(N/2\big)^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}+\varepsilon^{p}B(N/2\varepsilon_{0})+\sum_{M>N/2\varepsilon_{0}}(\tfrac{N}{2M})^{s_{c}}B(M)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(u)\Big(1+C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0})\big(N/2\big)^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}+\varepsilon^{p}\big(1+(N/2\varepsilon_{0})^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C(u)\sum_{M>N/2\varepsilon_{0}}(\tfrac{N}{2M})^{s_{c}}\big(1+M^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}(u)\Big(1+C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0})\big(N/2\big)^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}+\varepsilon^{p}\big(1+(N/2\varepsilon_{0})^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+C(u)\varepsilon_{0}^{s_{c}}+C(u)\varepsilon_{0}^{s_{c}-\sigma(s)}(N/2)^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}(u)\Big(1+\varepsilon^{p}+C(u)\varepsilon_{0}^{s_{c}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}(u)\big(C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0})+\varepsilon^{p}\varepsilon_{0}^{-\sigma(s)}+C(u)\varepsilon_{0}^{s_{c}-\sigma(s)}\big)(N/2)^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2},

where we use
s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}<s
in the third inequality to guarantee the

If we now choose
\varepsilon_{0}
possibly even smaller depending on
\tilde{C}(u)
;
\varepsilon
sufficiently small depending on
\tilde{C}(u)
and
\varepsilon_{0}
; and
C(u)
possibly larger such that

\displaystyle B\big(N/2\big)

\displaystyle\leq C(u)\Big(1+(N/2)^{\sigma(s)}K_{I}^{1/2}\Big).

Thus (
3.13
) holds at frequency

, and so we conclude (
3.2
) by induction.

Next, we will use the recurrence formula (
3.7
) to show (
3.4
). To do this, we note that for fixed

, we can use the compact property and the fact that

to get

(3.14)

where

∎

4.
The rapid frequency-cascade scenario

In this section, we rule out the existence of rapid frequency-cascade solutions, that is, almost periodic solutions as in Theorem
1.9
such that
\int_{0}^{T_{\rm max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt<\infty.
The proof will rely primarily on the long-time Strichartz estimate proved in the previous section.

Theorem 4.1

(No rapid frequency-cascades)
.

Let
s_{c}>3/2.
Then there are no radial almost periodic solutions
u:[0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
to (
1.1
) with
N(t)\equiv N_{k}\geq 1
on each characteristic subinterval
J_{k}\subset[0,T_{\rm max})
such that

\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{3p}([0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}=+\infty

(4.1)

and

K:=\int_{0}^{T_{\rm max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt<+\infty.

(4.2)

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that
u
were such a solution. Then, using (
4.2
) and Corollary
1.7
, we see

\lim_{t\to T_{\rm max}}N(t)=\infty,

(4.3)

whether
T_{\rm max}
is finite or infinite. Combining this with the compact property, we see that

\lim_{t\to T_{\rm max}}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}(t)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=0

(4.4)

for any
N>0
.

Proposition 4.2

(Lower regularity)
.

Let
s_{c}>3/2.
Let
u:[0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be an almost periodic solution with (
4.2
). Suppose

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}([0,T_{\rm max}),\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))\quad\text{for some}\quad s_{c}-\tfrac{1}{2}<s\leq s_{c}.

(4.5)

Then,

u\in L_{t}^{\infty}([0,T_{\rm max}),\dot{H}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{4})),\quad\forall~s-\sigma(s)<\alpha\leq s_{c},

(4.6)

where
\sigma(s)=2s_{c}-s-\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

Let
I_{n}\subset[0,T_{\rm max})
be a nested sequence of compact time intervals, each of which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals. We claim that for any
N>0
, we have

\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(I_{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim_{u}\inf_{t\in I_{n}}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}(t)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+N^{\sigma(s)}.

(4.7)

Indeed, defining

B_{n}(N):=\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}(I_{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})},

we have by (
3.6
) and (
4.2
)

\displaystyle B_{n}(N)\lesssim_{u}\inf_{t\in I_{n}}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}(t)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+C(\varepsilon,\varepsilon_{0})N^{\sigma(s)}+\varepsilon^{p}B(N/\varepsilon_{0})+\sum_{M>N/\varepsilon_{0}}(\tfrac{N}{M})^{s_{c}}B_{n}(M).

Arguing as we did to obtain (
3.2
), we derive (
4.7
).

Now, letting
n\to\infty
in (
4.7
) and using (
4.4
), we obtain

\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}([0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim_{u}N^{\sigma(s)}

(4.8)

for all
N>0
.

We now claim that (
4.8
) implies

\big\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}([0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim_{u}N^{2s_{c}-1/2}

(4.9)

for all
N>0.
To this end, we first use the reduced Duhamel formula and Strichartz to get

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}([0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\big\||\nabla|^{s}P_{\leq N}F(u)\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}([0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(4.10)

We now decompose
F(u)
by

\displaystyle F(u)=F(u_{\leq N})+\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})\big)

(4.11)

and estimate the contribution of each piece individually.

We begin by estimating the contribution of the first term in (
4.11
)
to (
4.10
). Using Hölder, the fractional product rule, the fractional chain rule, Sobolev embedding, interpolation, (
1.6
), (
4.5
) and (
4.8
), we estimate

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s}P_{\leq N}F(u_{\leq N})\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{4p}{s_{c}-s+2}}}^{p}\big\||\nabla|^{s}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{2/p+s-1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}^{s_{1}}}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u_{\leq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{u}

\displaystyle N^{\sigma(s)},

(4.12)

where
s_{1}=2-\frac{s_{c}-s+2}{p}\in(s,s_{c}].

Next, we estimate the contribution of the second term in
(
4.11
) to (
4.10
). We can use Hölder, Bernstein, (
2.2
), (
4.5
) and (
4.8
) to estimate

\displaystyle\quad\big\||\nabla|^{s}P_{\leq N}\big(F(u)-F(u_{\leq N})\big)\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s_{c}}\Big\||\nabla|^{-(s_{c}-s)}\int_{0}^{1}F^{\prime}(u_{\leq N}+\lambda u_{>N})u_{>N}\;d\lambda\Big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4/3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}N^{s_{c}}\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}-s}F^{\prime}(u_{\leq N}+\lambda u_{>N})\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{\frac{2}{s_{c}-s+1}}}\big\||\nabla|^{-(s_{c}-s)}u_{>N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{2/p+s-1}}}\;d\lambda

).

), and (
4.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{\alpha}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{\alpha}u_{\geq 1}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\sum_{N\leq 1}N^{\alpha-s}\big\||\nabla|^{s}u_{N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big\||\nabla|^{s_{c}}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\sum_{N\leq 1}N^{\alpha+\sigma(s)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1,

where we need the restriction

∎

5.
The frequency-localized Morawetz inequality

In this section, we establish spacetime bounds for the high-frequency portions of almost periodic solutions to (

Theorem 5.1

(Frequency-localized Morawetz inequality)
.

Let
s_{c}>3/2
, and
u:[0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}

) such that

on each characteristic subinterval

be a compact time interval, which is a contiguous union of characteristic subintervals

such that for all

\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt\lesssim_{u}\eta\big(N^{1-2s_{c}}+K_{I}\big),

(5.1)

where

. Furthermore,
N_{0}
and the implicit constants above are independent of the interval
I
.

We will use the following corollary to prove Theorem
5.1
.

Lemma 5.2

(Low and high frequencies control)

(5.4)

Proof.

Throughout the proof, all spacetime norms will be taken over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

By Bernstein’s inequality and (

), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\geq N}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{M\geq N}M^{-s_{c}}\big(1+M^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}K_{I}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-s_{c}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2}}K_{I}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{\frac{1}{2}},

which shows (
5.2
).

Using Remark
1.3

(ii)

\displaystyle N^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{\geq N}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{N\leq\cdot\leq c(\eta)}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+N^{s_{c}-\frac{1}{2}}\big\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{\geq c(\eta)}\big\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}

∎

The proof of Theorem
5.1
:
Throughout the proof, all spacetime norms will be taken over
I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Let
0<\eta\ll 1

\|u_{>N/\eta^{2}}\|_{L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{4}}\lesssim_{u}\eta N^{-s_{c}}(1+N^{2s_{c}-1}K_{I})^{1/2}.

(5.5)

Define the Morawetz action

Since

6.
The quasi-soliton scenario

In this section, we rule out the existence of quasi-soliton soutions, that is, solutions as in Theorem

\int_{0}^{T_{\rm max}}N(t)^{3-2s_{c}}\,dt=\infty.
The proof will rely primarily on the frequency-localized Morawetz estimate.

Theorem 6.1

(No quasi-solitons)
.

Let
s_{c}>3/2
. Then there are no radial almost periodic solutions
u:[0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
to (

) with

(6.1)

Lemma 6.2

(Lower bound)
.

Let
u:[0,T_{\rm max})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
be a radial almost periodic solution as in Theorem
1.9

s_{c}>3/2
. Let
I\subset[0,T_{\rm max}).
Then there exists
N_{0}>0
such that for any

Proof.

First, by the same argument as (7.3) in

we deduce that there exists

sufficiently large and

so that for

\displaystyle\iint_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|u_{>N}(t,x)|^{p+2}}{|x|}\,dx\,dt

Thus, we complete the proof of Lemma

.
∎

Finally, we turn to prove Theorem

.

. Therefore, we conclude Theorem

.

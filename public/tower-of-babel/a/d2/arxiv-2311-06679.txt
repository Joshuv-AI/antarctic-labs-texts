Theory of Compression Channels for Postselected Quantum Metrology
Jing Yang

Abstract
Postselected quantum metrological scheme is especially advantageous when the final measurements are either very noisy or expensive in practical experiments. In this work, we put forward a general theory on the compression channels in postselected quantum metrology. We define the basic notions characterizing the compression quality and illuminate the underlying structure of lossless compression channels. Previous experiments on Postselected optical phase estimation and weak-value amplification are shown to be particular cases of this general theory. Furthermore, for two categories of bipartite systems, we show that the compression loss can be made arbitrarily small even when the compression channel acts only on one subsystem. These findings can be employed to distribute quantum measurements so that the measurement noise and cost are dramatically reduced.

In standard quantum metrology, given an ensemble of metrological samples, quantum mechanics allows one to optimize the quantum measurements so that the information about the signal is maximally extracted. Yet, another metrological scheme, called Postselected quantum metrology arises in the context of weak-value amplification (WVA)

In this work, we answer this question by proposing a theory that unifies
weak-value metrology, Postselected metrology, as well as standard
metrology. The crucial observation is that standard metrology only
makes use of the measurement statistics and discards the post-measurement
states completely while Postselected metrology utilizes a specific
set of post-measurement states and discards the rest. As such, we
generalize the optimal measurement condition from standard metrology
to Postselected metrology. By keeping track of these conditions,
we identify the generic structure of the lossless post-selection
channel for pure states. Previous setup on Postselected metrology
Arvidsson-Shukur et al. 2020
;
Lupu-Gladstein et al. 2022
;
Jenne and Arvidsson-Shukur 2022
;
Salvati et al. 2023

and WVA

general theory. Finally, for bipartite entangled states, when the
compression channel is restricted to one subsystem, we construct two
categories of examples, which can be compressed substantially with
only a negligible amount of loss.

efficient

Conclusion.—
We propose a unified theory, which implies that quantum measurements can be viewed as either information-extracting apparatus as in the standard quantum metrology, or information filters as in the Postselected quantum metrology. It can be employed
to distribute the optimal measurements through post-selections so
that the cost of the final detections are dramatically reduced, in synergy with recent efforts on distributed quantum sensing
(see e.g.
(
Malitesta et al. 2023
;
Malia et al. 2022
;
Zhang and Zhuang 2021
;
Ge et al. 2018
)
). As a result, we anticipate our results will find applications
in quantum sensing technologies, such as
optical
imaging and interferometry
(
Demkowicz-Dobrzanski et al. 2015
;
Tsang et al. 2016
;
Zhou et al. 2019
)
,
magnetometry
(
Zhu et al. 2022
)
, frequency estimation
(
Dutta and Mukherjee 2020
)
,
etc.
Many problems are open for future exploration, including the
compression of mixed states, multi-parameter states
(
Liu et al. 2019
;
Szczykulska et al. 2016
)
,
multipartite-entangled states, etc.

VII
Expression for
\ket{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}

and
\ket{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}
in terms of

\rho_{x}

It is straightforward to compute

\displaystyle\ket{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}

\displaystyle=\left(\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}-\ket{\psi_{x}}\braket{\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\right)\left(\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}-\bra{\psi_{x}}\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\psi_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}-\braket{\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}-\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\psi_{x}}\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}

\displaystyle+\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\psi_{x}}\braket{\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}.

(S94)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{x}\rho_{x}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}+\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\right)\left(\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}+\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}+\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\psi_{x}}\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}+\braket{\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}

\displaystyle+\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}.

(S95)

Apparently, the above two expressions differ by the last term. Furthermore,
we note

\displaystyle\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\psi_{x}}\braket{\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}

\displaystyle=\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\left(\mathbb{I}-\ket{\psi_{x}^{\perp(1)}}\bra{\psi_{x}^{\perp(1)}}\right)\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}

\displaystyle=\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}-\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\psi_{x}^{\perp(1)}}\braket{\psi_{x}^{\perp(1)}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}

\displaystyle=\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}-g(\rho_{x}).

(S96)

Thus we conclude

\ket{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}=\left(\partial_{x}\rho_{x}\right)^{2}-g(\rho_{x})\rho_{x}.

(S97)

The projector along
\ket{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}
can then
be expressed as

\rho_{x}^{\perp}=\frac{\left(\partial_{x}\rho_{x}\right)^{2}}{g(\rho_{x})}-\rho_{x}.

(S98)

Following similar procedure we find,

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{x}\rho_{x}\right)\rho_{x}

\displaystyle=\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}+\ket{\psi_{x}}\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}},

(S99)

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\left(\partial_{x}\rho_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\ket{\psi_{x}}\braket{\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}+\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}.

(S100)

Taking the difference between the equations leads to

[\partial_{x}\rho_{x},\,\rho_{x}]=\ket{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}-\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}.

(S101)

Furthermore we find

\displaystyle\ket{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}+\ket{\psi_{x}}\bra{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\ket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}-\ket{\psi_{x}}\braket{\psi_{x}\big.|\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}\right)\bra{\psi_{x}}+\ket{\psi_{x}}\left(\bra{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}}-\braket{\partial_{x}\psi_{x}\big.|\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\rho_{x}.

(S102)

Thus, we conclude

\ket{\partial_{x}^{\perp}\psi_{x}}\bra{\psi_{x}}=\frac{1}{2}\left([\partial_{x}\rho_{x},\,\rho_{x}]+\partial_{x}\rho_{x}\right).

(S103)

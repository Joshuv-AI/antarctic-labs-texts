An efficient method for solving equations in generalized quaternion
and octonion algebras

Abstract




Abstract.

Quaternions often appear in wide areas of applied
science and engineering such as wireless communications systems, mechanics,
etc. It is known that are two types of non-isomorphic generalized quaternion
algebras, namely: the algebra of quaternions and the algebra of
coquaternions. In this paper, we present the formulae to pass from a basis
in the generalized quaternion algebras to a basis in the division
quaternions algebra or to a basis in the coquaternions algebra and vice
versa. The same result was obtained for the generalized octonion algebra.
Moreover, we emphasize the applications of these results to the algebraic
equations and De Moivre’s formula in generalized quaternion algebras and in
generalized octonion division algebras.

17A35;

generalized quaternion algebras; De Moivre’s formula.

1. An isomorphism between the algebras
\mathbb{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}),
with
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0,
and
\mathbb{H}(1,1)

Everywhere in this section, we will consider
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
. An
isomorphism between the algebras
\mathbb{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\mathbb{H}(1,1)
is given by the operator
A
and its inverse
A^{-1}
, where

A:\quad e_{1}\mapsto i\sqrt{\gamma_{1}},\quad e_{2}\mapsto j\sqrt{\gamma_{2}},\quad e_{3}\mapsto k\sqrt{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}.

It is easy to prove the following properties for the operator
A:

1)
A\left(\lambda x\right)=\lambda A\left(x\right),\forall\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\forall\;x\in\mathbb{H}\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right);

2)
A\left(x+y\right)=A\left(x\right)+A\left(y\right),\forall\;x,y\in\mathbb{H}\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right);

3)
A\left(xy\right)=A\left(x\right)A\left(y\right),\forall\;x,y\in\mathbb{H}\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right).

From
here, it results that the operators
A
and
A^{-1}
are additive and
multiplicative.

Proposition 1.1.

The operators
A
and
A^{-1}

are continuous and their norms are equal with

1
.

Proof.
We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})}
the Euclidian norm in
\mathbb{H}\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)
. Since the spaces
\mathbb{H}\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)
and
\mathbb{H}(1,1)
are normed spaces, then the
continuity of
A
is equivalent with the boundedness of
A,
i.e. there is a
real constant
c
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{H}\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right),
we have

\frac{\left\|A\left(x\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{H}\left(1,1\right)}}{\left\|x\right\|_{\mathbb{H}\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)}}\leq c.

It results that

\frac{\left\|x_{0}+x_{1}i\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}+x_{2}j\sqrt{\gamma_{2}}+x_{3}k\sqrt{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}\right\|}{\left\|x_{0}+x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+x_{3}e_{3}\right\|}=

=\frac{\sqrt{x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\gamma_{1}+x_{2}^{2}\gamma_{2}+x_{3}^{2}\gamma_{3}}}{\sqrt{x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}\gamma_{1}+x_{2}^{2}\gamma_{2}+x_{3}^{2}\gamma_{3}}}=1.

\,\Box\vskip 12.0pt plus 4.0pt minus 4.0pt

3. Application to the algebraic equations

Let

Theorem 3.1.

Let

be a root of the equation

\mathbb{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

\mathbb{H}(1,1)

\mathbb{H}(1,-1)

\gamma_{1}

\gamma_{2}

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0

A\big(f(x^{0})\big)=Af\big(A(x^{0})\big)=A(0)=0.\

To prove the converse statement we apply the operator
A^{-1}
to the

f(x^{0})=0
. The remaining cases can be proved
similarly.
\Box

In the following, we will generalize in a natural way De Moivre formula and
Euler’s formula for the division octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}\left(1,1,1\right).
For this, we will use some ideas and notations from
[
3
]
. We consider the sets

\mathcal{S}^{3}=\{a\in\mathbb{O}(1,1,1):\;N(a)=1\},

\mathcal{S}_{G}^{3}=\{a\in\mathbb{O}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):\;N(a)=1\},

\mathcal{S}^{2}=\{a\in\mathbb{O}(1,1,1):\;t(a)=0,N\left(a\right)=1\}.

\mathcal{S}_{G}^{2}=\{a\in\mathbb{O}(1,1,1):\;t(a)=0,N\left(a\right)=1\}.

We remark that for all elements
a\in\mathcal{S}^{2},
we have
a^{2}=-1.

Let
a\in\mathcal{S}^{3},a=a_{0}+a_{1}f_{1}+a_{2}f_{2}+a_{3}f_{3}+a_{4}f_{4}+a_{5}f_{5}+a_{6}f_{6}+a_{7}f_{7}.
This element can be write under the form

where
\cos\lambda=a_{0}~
and

w=\frac{a_{1}f_{1}+a_{2}f_{2}+a_{3}f_{3}+a_{4}f_{4}+a_{5}f_{5}+a_{6}f_{6}+a_{7}f_{7}}{\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2}+a_{4}^{2}+a_{5}^{2}+a_{6}^{2}+a_{7}^{2}}}=

Since
w^{2}=-1,
we obtain the following Euler’s formula:

\displaystyle e^{\lambda w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overset{\infty}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left(\lambda w\right)^{n}}{n!}=\overset{\infty}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}\lambda^{2n}}{\left(2n\right)!}+w\overset{\infty}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n-1}\lambda^{2n-1}}{\left(2n-1\right)!}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\lambda+w\sin\lambda.

The cosinus function is constant for all

\mathcal{S}^{2}.\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

By straightforward calculations

Proposition 5.3.
(De Moivre formula for octonions)
With
the above notations, we have that

a^{n}=e^{n\lambda w}=\left(\cos\lambda+w\sin\lambda\right)^{n}=\cos n\lambda+w\sin n\lambda,

where

and

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

For
n>0,
by induction. We obtain

\displaystyle a^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\cos\lambda+w\sin\lambda\right)^{n+1}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\cos\lambda+w\sin\lambda\right)^{n}\left(\cos\lambda+w\sin\lambda\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos n\lambda+w\sin n\lambda)\left(\cos\lambda+w\sin\lambda\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos(n+1)\lambda+w\sin(n+1)\lambda.

Since
a^{-1}=\cos\lambda-w\sin\lambda=\cos(-\lambda)+w\sin(-\lambda),
it results the asked formula for all

-1
and
t\left(w\right)=0,
therefore
w^{2}=1.
Denoting
\cosh\lambda=a_{0}
and
\sinh\lambda=\sqrt{a_{0}^{2}-1},\lambda\in\mathbb{R},
it results:

Proposition 6.10.

With the above notations, we have Euler’s
formula:

a=e^{\lambda w}=\cosh\lambda+w\sinh\lambda.

Proof.
Indeed,
e^{\lambda w}=\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=0}{\sum}}\frac{\left(\lambda w\right)^{n}}{n!}
=
\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=0}{\sum}}\frac{\lambda^{2n}}{\left(2n\right)!}+w\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=0}{\sum}}\frac{\lambda^{2n+1}}{\left(2n+1\right)!}
=
\cosh\lambda+w\sinh\lambda.\Box

If
a\in a\in\mathbb{O}(1,1,-1)
with
N\left(a\right)<0,
it results
a=\sqrt{\left|N\left(a\right)\right|}(\cosh\lambda+w\sinh\lambda).

Proposition 6.11.

1) If
a\in\mathbb{O}(1,1,-1),

then

a^{n}=(\sqrt{\left|N\left(a\right)\right|})^{n}(\cosh n\lambda+w\sinh n\lambda).

2)

The equation

x^{n}=a

has only one root:

\sqrt[2n]{\left|N\left(a\right)\right|}\left(\cosh\frac{\lambda}{n}+w\sinh\frac{\lambda}{n}\right).\Box\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

Remark 6. 12.
Using the above technique, De Moivre’s formula and
Euler’s formula can be easy proved for the octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma),
with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\mathbb{O}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is split.
Thus, the study of algebraic equations in an arbitrary algebra
\mathbb{O}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
with
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
was reduced to study of the corresponding algebraic
equation in one of the following two algebras: division octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}(1,1,1)
or algebra
\mathbb{O}(1,1,-1)
.

Conclusion.
In this paper, we used isomorphism between the real
quaternion algebras
\mathbb{H}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
and
\mathbb{H}(1,1)
or
\mathbb{H}(1,-1)
to reduced the study of some algebraic
equations in an arbitrary algebra
\mathbb{H}\left(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\right)
with
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
to
study of the corresponding algebraic equation in one of the following two
algebras: division quaternion algebra or coquaternion algebra. The same
result was obtained for the generalized octonion algebra
\mathbb{O}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
. De Moivre’s formula in generalized quaternion algebras
and generalized octonion division algebras was proved using this new method.

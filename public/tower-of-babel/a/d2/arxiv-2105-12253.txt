Euler-Schrödinger Transformation Preprint: APS/123-QED
Ahmad Zareei

Abstract
Here we present a transformation that maps the Schrödinger equation of quantum mechanics to the incompressible Euler equations of fluid mechanics. The transformation provides a wave solution and a potential function based on fluid properties that satisfy the Schrödinger equation given that the fluid velocity and pressure satisfy the Euler equations. Interestingly, in our transformation, the equivalent of quantum potential becomes the physical surface tension. This is contrary to the Madelung transformation that maps the Schrödinger equation to the compressible Euler equations where there is no physical counterpart for the quantum potential. Lastly, we show that using this transformation, the Bohm equation can be mapped to a particle’s equation of motion moving on the free surface of the fluid.



We present a transformation between the Schrödinger
equation of quantum mechanics and the incompressible Euler equations (
incompressible
, inviscid, and irrotational hydrodynamic equations for the surface gravity waves). It is to be noted that the transformation between the Schrödinger equation and
compressible
Euler equations is known as the Madelung transform. Contrary to the Madelung transformation where there is no classical analog for the quantum potential, we find a classical counterpart to the quantum potential which becomes the surface tension with the Planck constant acting similar to the surface tension over density. We present two versions of our transformation, where we add complexity to the transformation step by step: (i) in the first version, we map the Schrödinger equation to the surface gravity wave equations for an inviscid, irrotational, and incompressible fluid with surface tension in deep water; (ii) next, we assume a finite depth fluid and also a particle bouncing on the free surface (bouncind droplet setup
Couder et al. 2005
), and show how the transformation changes in mapping Schrödinger equation to the Euler equations. Assuming Bohmian mechanics, we find the analog of Bohm equation using our transformation, and show that under certain conditions, the transformation of Bohm equation matches with the Newton equation of motion for the bouncing droplet. This process may help us understand why the bouncing droplet problem
Couder et al. 2005
has shown quantum resemblance (such as single-slit patterns and double-slit diffraction
Couder and Fort 2006
, tunnelling
Eddi et al. 2009
, orbital quantization
Fort et al. 2010
, level splitting
Eddi et al. 2012
, and wavelike statistics in a confined region
Harris et al. 2013
).

I
(I) Simplified Euler-Schrödinger Transformation

Schrödinger equation
– The position-space Schrödinger equation for a single non-relativistic particle with mass
m
in a potential
V(\textbf{q},t)
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}(\textbf{q},t)=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta\psi(\textbf{q},t)+V(\textbf{q})\psi(\textbf{q},t)

(1)

where
\hbar
is the reduced Planck constant (
\hbar=h/2\pi
), and
\psi(\textbf{q},t):\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a wave function that assigns a complex number to each point
\textbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
in space at each time
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Euler equations
– Considering the irrotational motion of a homogeneous,
incompressible, and inviscid fluid with the surface tension

\sigma
and density
\rho
; the governing equations in terms of the velocity
potential
\phi
and surface elevation
\eta
read

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\phi=0,\qquad z\leq\eta,

(2a)

\displaystyle\eta_{t}+\nabla(\eta-z).\nabla\phi=0,\qquad z=\eta,

(2b)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+g\eta+\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\nabla.\hat{\textbf{n}}=0\qquad z=\eta,

(2c)

\displaystyle\phi_{z}=0,\qquad z\rightarrow-\infty,

(2d)

where
\hat{\textbf{n}}
is the unit normal vector at the surface
z=\eta
. In the
governing equations, (
2a
) is the continuity
equation in the fluids domain, (
2b
) and (
2d
)
are kinematic boundary conditions at the surface and bottom, and
(
2c
) is the dynamic boundary condition at the
surface.

The Euler-Schrödinger Transformation
– In the Schrödinger equation, we pick
m=1
,
\hbar=\sqrt{{2\sigma}/{\rho}}
and define the potential function as

,

assume a wave function in the form of

\displaystyle-H(\eta-z)\phi_{t}+\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\nabla^{2}H(\eta-z)-\frac{1}{2}H(\eta-z)|\nabla\phi|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\frac{P}{\rho}+gz\right)\cdot H(\eta-z)=0,

(3a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}H(\eta-z)+\nabla H(\eta-z)\cdot\nabla\phi\right)+\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}H(\eta-z)\nabla^{2}\phi=0.

(3b)

Here Eq. (

\displaystyle\qquad+\iiint_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}H(\eta-z)f(\mathbf{x})\nabla^{2}\phi\textrm{d}V=0,

(4)

which reduces to

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{S}}\left[f(\mathbf{x})\left(\eta_{t}+\nabla(\eta-z).\nabla\phi\right)\right]_{z=\eta}\textrm{d}A

\displaystyle\qquad+\iint_{\mathcal{S}}\textrm{d}A\int_{-\infty}^{\eta}\textrm{d}zf(\mathbf{x})\nabla^{2}\phi=0,

(5)

where
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the free surface of the

is an arbitrary function, we
obtain

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\phi=0,\qquad-\infty\leq z\leq\eta

(6a)

\displaystyle\eta_{t}+\nabla(\eta-z).\nabla\phi=0\qquad z=\eta

(6b)

which are respectively the incompressibility and the kinematic

3a

condition (
2c

be a function with

(g)=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[a,b]

,

, and
\delta>0
. We
assume the normal derivative of this function on the surface is zero,

\displaystyle\iiint_{\Omega}H(\eta-z)g(\mathbf{x})\left(\phi_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{P}{\rho}+g\right)\textrm{d}V

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\iiint_{\Omega}g(\mathbf{x})\nabla\ \cdot\left(-\textbf{n}\delta(n)\right)=0,

(7)

where we used the fact that
\nabla H(\eta-z)=-\hat{\textbf{n}}~\delta(n)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{S}}\textrm{d}A\int_{a^{\prime}}^{\eta}\textrm{d}zg(\mathbf{x})\left(\phi_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{P}{\rho}+g\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\left[\iiint_{\Omega}\left(g(\mathbf{x})\nabla\cdot\hat{\textbf{n}}\delta(n)+g(\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\delta(n)\right)\textrm{d}V\right]=0.

(8)

and as a result

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{S}}\textrm{d}A\int_{a^{\prime}}^{\eta}\textrm{d}zg(\mathbf{x})\left(\phi_{t}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{P}{\rho}+g\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\iiint_{\Omega}g(\mathbf{x})\nabla\cdot\hat{\textbf{n}}\delta(n)\,\textrm{d}V=-\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\iiint_{\Omega}g(\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\delta(n)\,\textrm{d}V.

(9)

The right-hand side of the above equation is zero: if we
use integral by parts on the right-hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\iiint_{\Omega}g(\mathbf{x})\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\delta(n)\textrm{d}V

\displaystyle\qquad=\iiint_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial n}\left(g(\mathbf{x})\delta(n)\right)\textrm{d}V-\iiint_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial g(\mathbf{x})}{\partial n}\right)\delta(n)\,\textrm{d}V,

(10)

where (i) the first and (ii) the second integrals are zero, since (i)
by changing the coordinates from Cartesian coordinate
(x,y,z)
to

(t,s,n)
, this integral vanishes with the compactness of

g(\mathbf{x})
and (ii) we assumed that
\partial g/\partial n|_{z=\eta}=0
. Looking back at Eq. (
I
), since
g(\mathbf{x})
is arbitrary, we obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{t}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{P}{\rho}+gz+\left(\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\nabla.\textbf{n}\right)\delta(n)=0,\quad z\leq\eta

(11)

which is the same as equation (
2c
) on the surface. Note that Eq. (
11
) is what we obtain starting from Navier-Stokes equations, and in the Euler equations we only consider the above equation on the free surface, i.e.,
z=\eta
. Equations (
6
) and (
11
) together are collectively the Euler equations (Eq. (
2
)). Note that, since the domain is unbounded in the

z

\phi
vanishing at infinity (i.e.,

2d
) and is a condition for the velocity potential to be bounded.

(II) Euler-Schrödinger transformation

Schrödinger equation
– As discussed in the previous section, the Schrödinger equation is

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}(\textbf{x},t)=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta\psi(\textbf{x},t)+V(\textbf{x})\psi(\textbf{x},t),

(12)

where
\hbar
is the reduced Plank constant, and

\psi(\textbf{x},t):\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the wave function of a
particle with mass
m

\textbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\frac{\textrm{d}\textbf{Q}}{\textrm{d}t}=\frac{\hbar}{m}\Im\left\{\frac{\nabla\psi}{\psi}(\textbf{x},t)\right\},

(13)

where

gives the imaginary part of the complex number

III
Remarks

In this section, we review some basic conclusions that can be made
based on the transformation.

•

The probability of finding a particle at a point is proportional
to
|\psi|^{2}
and the integral of this probability over the whole
space should add up to unity

\displaystyle\int|\psi|^{2}\textrm{d}V=1.

(26)

The equivalent of this condition, using the transformation,
translates into the fact that the volume of the fluid is constant,

\displaystyle\int H(\eta-z)\cdot H(\eta-z)\textrm{d}V=\int_{\Lambda}\textrm{d}V=V_{0},

(27)

where
\Lambda
is the fluids domain and
V_{0}
is the
total volume of fluid.

•

The flow of probability using the Schrödinger equation is obtained
as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial|\psi|^{2}}{\partial t}+\nabla.\left[\frac{\hbar}{2m\mathrm{i}}\left(\psi^{*}\nabla\psi-\psi\nabla\psi^{*}\right)\right]=0.

(28)

The equivalent form of this equation, using the Euler-Schrödinger equation
becomes

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{\partial H(\eta-z)\cdot H(\eta-z)}{\partial t}+

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad\frac{1}{2}\nabla.(H(\eta-z)\cdot H(\eta-z)\nabla\phi)=0,

(29)

\displaystyle\qquad\delta(\eta-z)\left[\eta_{t}+\nabla(\eta-z).\nabla\phi\right]+

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad\frac{1}{2}H(\eta-z)\nabla^{2}\phi=0,

(30)

which is the same as surface kinematic boundary condition together
with the Laplacian equation (conservation of mass).

•

The scale at which quantum effects are observed is such that

\hbar/ML^{2}T^{-1}\approx 1
, where
M,L
, and
T
are the mass, length
and time scales of the problem. Similar arguments for the bouncing
droplet’s problem, yield
\sigma/\rho gL^{2}\approx 1
. As a
result, the length scale at which we expect to see quantum-like
behaviors for the silicone oil becomes
L\approx 2.5mm
, which
is close to the length scale of experiments.

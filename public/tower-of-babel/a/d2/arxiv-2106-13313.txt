KPZ equation with a small noise, deep upper tail and limit shape
Pierre Yves Gaudreau Lamarre, Yier Lin, and Li-Cheng Tsai

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the KPZ equation under the weak noise scaling. That is, we introduce a small parameter ε \sqrt{\varepsilon} in front of the noise and let ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . We prove that the one-point large deviation rate function has a 3 2 \frac{3}{2} power law in the deep upper tail. Furthermore, by forcing the value of the KPZ equation at a point to be very large, we prove a limit shape of the solution of the KPZ equation as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 . This confirms the physics prediction in [ KK07 , KK09 , KMS16 , MKV16 , LDMRS16 , HMS19 ] .



1.
Introduction

The
KPZ
(
KPZ
) equation
[
KPZ86
]
is a non-linear stochastic PDE which describes the random growth of an interface that has a property of lateral growth and relaxation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}h=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}h+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h)^{2}+\xi.

Here
\xi
is the space-time white noise, which can be informally understood as a Gaussian field with Dirac-delta correlation function
\mathbb{E}[\xi(t,x)\xi(s,y)]=\delta(t-s)\delta(x-y)
. The KPZ equation has been studied intensively over the past 35 years. We refer to
[
FS10
,
Qua11
,
Cor12
,
QS15
,
CW17
,
CS20
]
for some surveys of the mathematical studies of the
KPZ
equation.

Care is needed to make sense of the solution to (
1.1
) due to the non-linearity and space-time white noise in the equation. One way of defining the solution is through the
Hopf-Cole transform
. That is, we define
h:=\log Z
, where
Z
solves the
SHE
(
SHE
)

\partial_{t}Z=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}Z+\xi Z.

We say that
Z
is the
mild solution
to the SHE if

(1.2)

Z(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t,x-y)Z(0,y)dy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-s,x-y)Z(s,y)\xi(s,y)dsdy,

where
p(t,x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}}
is the heat kernel. The solution theory of the
SHE
is standard;
see
[
Qua11
, Sections 2.1-2.6]
for more details. Moreover, for function-valued initial data
Z(0,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\geq 0
that is not identically zero,
[
Mue91
]
shows that
Z
is always positive, i.e. almost surely
Z(t,x)>0
for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. This guarantees the wellposedness of
h
. One often considered initial data is
Z(0,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=\delta({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
, where
\delta({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
is a Dirac-delta function. We refer to this as the Dirac-delta initial data for
Z
and the
narrow wedge initial data
for
h
.
[
MF14
]
shows that under the Dirac-delta initial data, almost surely
Z
is positive for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Other definitions and constructions of the solution to the
KPZ
equation are given by regularity structure
[
Hai14
]
, paracontrolled distribution
[
GIP15
]
or the notion of energy solution
[
GJ14
,
GP18
]
.

In recent years, the large deviations of the
KPZ
equation have received much attention in the mathematics and physics communities. The large deviations of the
KPZ
equation can be studied in two regimes: long time regime
(t\to\infty)
and short time regime
(t\to 0)
. For the long time regime,
the work
[
CG20b
]
rigorously proved a detailed bound for the lower tail of the KPZ equation under the narrow wedge initial data. This bound captures a cubic to
\frac{5}{2}
crossover; see also the physics work
[
KLD18
]
.
[
CG20a
]
obtained similar bounds for the
KPZ
equation under general initial data. Under the narrow wedge initial data, the exact one-point lower tail large deviation rate function was derived in the physics works
[
SMP17
,
CGK+18
,
KLDP18
,
LD20
]
and was proved rigorously by
[
Tsa18
,
CC21
]
.
[
KLD19
]
showed that the four methods in
[
SMP17
,
CGK+18
,
KLDP18
,
Tsa18
]
are closely related. For the upper tail, the physics work
[
LDMS16
]
predicted the
\frac{3}{2}
-power law for the entire rate function of the KPZ equation narrow wedge initial data.
[
DT21
]
gave a rigorous proof for the upper tail
LDP
(
LDP
). The result was extended to general initial data by
[
GL20
]
.

For the large deviations of the
KPZ
equation in the short time regime, the results are fruitful in the physics literature; see
[
Kra19
]
. In particular, the physics literature
[
KK07
,
KK09
,
KMS16
,
MKV16
]
predicted that for the narrow wedge and flat initial data, the one-point large deviation rate function exhibits a
\frac{3}{2}
-power law in the deep upper tail, a quadratic power law in the near-center tail and a
\frac{5}{2}
-power law in the deep lower tail.
The physics work
[
LDMRS16
]
derived the entire one-point rate function, from which the authors are able to confirm these power laws. Their prediction was backed by the numerical result
[
HLDM+18
]
. The one-point large deviations were rigorously proved in
[
LT21
]
. The authors also rigorously proved the quadratic to
\frac{5}{2}
-power law crossover in the lower tail rate function.

Studying the KPZ equation in the short time regime is the same as studying the KPZ equation in the
weak noise regime
. That is,
we introduce a small parameter
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
in front of the noise,

(1.3)

\partial_{t}h_{\varepsilon}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}h_{\varepsilon}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h_{\varepsilon})^{2}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\xi.

The solution to the above equation is defined to be
h_{\varepsilon}:=\log Z_{\varepsilon}
where
Z_{\varepsilon}
solves the
SHE

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z_{\varepsilon}=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}Z_{\varepsilon}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\xi Z_{\varepsilon}.

Throughout the paper, we set
Z_{\varepsilon}(0,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=\delta({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
. The short time regime of (
1.1
) is related to (
1.3
) through scaling, namely,
h(\varepsilon^{2}{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\varepsilon{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})+\log\varepsilon\overset{d}{=}h_{\varepsilon}({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
. We add
\log\varepsilon
to guarantee that
h_{\varepsilon}
starts from the narrow wedge initial data.

The
LDP
of
Z_{\varepsilon}=Z_{\varepsilon}({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
under the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
has been rigorously proven in
[
LT21
]
. The rate function is of Freidlin-Wentzell type. In particular, by the contraction principle, for
\lambda\geq 0
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}\big[h_{\varepsilon}(2,0)+\log\sqrt{4\pi}\leq-\lambda\big]

\displaystyle=-\Phi(-\lambda),

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}\big[h_{\varepsilon}(2,0)+\log\sqrt{4\pi}\geq\lambda\big]

\displaystyle=-\Phi(\lambda),

where
\Phi
is the infimum of the Freidlin-Wentzell rate function subject to the relevant constraint. Extracting the asymptotics of
\Phi
is non-trivial.
[
LT21
]
proved that
\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\lambda^{-2}\Phi(\lambda)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
and
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-\frac{5}{2}}\Phi(-\lambda)=\tfrac{4}{15\pi}
.

1.1.
Main results.

The first result of the current paper concerns the deep upper tail of the rate function
\Phi
. In other words, we look at the asymptotic of
\Phi(\lambda)
as
\lambda\to\infty
. It has been predicted in the physics literature
[
KK07
,
KK09
,
KMS16
,
MKV16
,
LDMRS16
]
that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-3/2}\Phi(\lambda)=\tfrac{4}{3}
. Our first result gives a rigorous proof of the
\frac{3}{2}
-power law in the deep upper tail.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-3/2}\Phi(\lambda)=\tfrac{4}{3}
.

The second result of the current paper proves the limit shape of (the solution of) the
KPZ
equation under the weak noise scaling and the deep upper tail conditioning. This limit shape was predicted in the physics works
[
KK07
,
KK09
,
KMS16
,
MKV16
,
HMS19
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Define
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}=\lambda^{-1}h_{\varepsilon}(t,\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}x)
. For arbitrary fixed
\delta>0
, we have

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\big[\|h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}-\mathsf{h}_{*}\|_{L^{\infty}([\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}])}<\delta\,\big|\,h_{\varepsilon}(2,0)+\log\sqrt{4\pi}\geq\lambda\big]=1.

Here, we use
\|{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}
to denote the
L^{\infty}
norm on the domain
D
. The limit shape
\mathsf{h}_{*}
is given by

(1.7)

\mathsf{h}_{*}(t,x):=\begin{cases}-|x|+\frac{t}{2},&\text{when }|x|\leq t,\\
-\frac{x^{2}}{2t},&\text{when }|x|\geq t.\end{cases}

See Figure
1
for illustration.

Figure 1.
The graph of
\mathsf{h}_{*}(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
, when
t=0.5,1,1.5
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.1
gives the
\lambda\to\infty
limit of
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}
under the upper-tail conditioning.
A natural related question is to obtain the limit under the lower-tail conditioning
h_{\varepsilon}(2,0)+\log\sqrt{4\pi}\leq-\lambda
.
The latter question has recently been solved in
[
LT22
]
.
We emphasize that the mechanisms for the large deviations are very different in the upper- and lower-tail conditioning.
In the upper-tail conditioning, the contribution of the noise
\xi
concentrates around
x=0
; in the lower-tail conditioning, the contribution of the noise spans a wide region in spacetime.
The required analysis in the current paper and in
[
LT22
]
hence differ.

Remark 1.4
.

The limit shape
\mathsf{h}_{*}
was predicted earlier in the physics work
[
KK07
,
KK09
,
KMS16
,
MKV16
]
via the weak noise theory, and in
[
HMS19
]
by simulations. Recently, the physics work
[
KLD21
]
solved the finite
\lambda
limit shape of
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}
. Using this, they confirmed the rate function
\Phi
discovered in
[
LDMRS16
]
. In the limit of large
\lambda
, they discussed how the form (
1.7
) emerges in the exact solution.

Remark 1.5
.

The reason that we set
h_{\varepsilon}(2,0)+\log\sqrt{4\pi}>\lambda
in the theorem instead of
h_{\varepsilon}(2,0)>\lambda
is purely for the convenience of the proof. It makes no difference since we let
\lambda\to\infty
.

Remark 1.6
.

Our method does not rely on exact formulas and may apply to other initial data.
In particular, our method should apply to the flat initial data
h_{\varepsilon}(0,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=0
.
The result in Theorem
1.1
remains the same for the flat initial data, while Theorem
1.2
holds with a different limit shape

\mathsf{h}^{\text{flat}}_{*}(t,x):=\begin{cases}-|x|+\frac{t}{2},&\text{when }|x|\leq\frac{t}{2},\\
0,&\text{when }|x|>\frac{t}{2}.\end{cases}

More broadly, one can consider applying our method to a function-valued, symmetrically decreasing initial data:
h_{\text{ic}}(x)=h_{\text{ic}}(|x|)
and
h_{\text{ic}}(|x|)
non-increasing in
|x|
.
We conjecture that the result in Theorem
1.1
remains the same; the limit shape (in-general) needs to be adjusted according to the initial data.

Going beyond symmetrically decreasing initial data, one may see different behaviors of the deviations.
In particular, a dynamical phase transition triggered by a symmetry breaking has been predicted in
[
JKM16
,
SKM18
]
(see also
[
KLD17
,
HMS21
,
KLD22
]
) for the Brownian initial data.
For such initial data, we do not expect our method to apply directly and new ideas are needed.

1.2.
A review of the Freidlin-Wentzell
LDP
for the
SHE

To motivate the proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we recall the Freidlin-Wentzell
LDP
for the
SHE

\{Z_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
. The result was established for the
SHE
under function-valued initial data and the narrow wedge initial data in
[
LT21
, Proposition 1.7]
. For our propose, we only state the result for the narrow wedge initial data.

Let us first recall the definition of an
LDP
. Let
\Omega
be a topological space. We say that a sequence of
\Omega
-valued random variables
\{Y_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

satisfies an
LDP
with speed
\varepsilon^{-1}
and rate function
I
if

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}(Y_{\varepsilon}\in F)

\displaystyle\leq-\inf_{x\in F}I(x)\qquad\text{ if }F\subseteq\Omega\text{ is closed},

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}(Y_{\varepsilon}\in G)

\displaystyle\geq-\inf_{x\in G}I(x)\qquad\text{ if }G\subseteq\Omega\text{ is open}.

We now state the
LDP
for the SHE
\{Z_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
. Fix
T>0
and
\delta\in(0,T)
. We take
\Omega=C([\delta,T]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}])
with the uniform topology and view
Z_{\varepsilon}
as an
\Omega
-valued random variable. The reason that we avoid
t=0
in our choice of
\Omega
is because
Z_{\varepsilon}
starts from the Dirac-delta initial data, which is singular. For
t>0
, the heat kernel in (
1.2
) smoothes out the singularity, so
Z_{\varepsilon}
is
\Omega
-valued for any fixed
\delta>0
; see
[
Qua11
]
.

Since
Z_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.4
) is driven by the space-time white noise
\sqrt{\varepsilon}\xi
, it would be helpful to first look at the
LDP
of
\{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\xi\}_{\varepsilon>0}
. We view
\rho\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
as a deviation
\sqrt{\varepsilon}\xi
. Since
\xi
has Dirac-delta correlation function, we have informally
\mathbb{P}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\xi\approx\rho)\approx\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})})
for small
\varepsilon
. We replace the noise
\sqrt{\varepsilon}\xi
with its deviation
\rho
and consider the PDE

(1.8)

\partial_{t}\mathsf{Z}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\mathsf{Z}+\rho\mathsf{Z},\qquad\mathsf{Z}(0,x)=\delta(x),

where
\mathsf{Z}=\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,x)
,
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Like the
SHE
, the solution to this PDE is understood in the mild form

(1.9)

\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,x)=p(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-s,x-y)\rho(s,y)\mathsf{Z}(\rho;s,y)dsdy.

By iteration of (
1.9
), the solution
\mathsf{Z}(\rho)
admits a series expansion and we have the Feynman-Kac formula

(1.10)

\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{0\to x}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\rho(s,B_{\text{b}}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]p(t,x),\qquad t\in(0,T]\times\mathbb{R},

where
B_{\text{b}}
is a Brownian bridge such that
B_{\text{b}}(0)=0
and
B_{\text{b}}(t)=x
.

It is standard to see that
\mathsf{Z}(\rho)
is a continuous function on
(0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, we refer to
[
LT21
, Section 2]
for more detail. Fix
\delta>0
. We view
\mathsf{Z}:\rho\mapsto\mathsf{Z}(\rho)
as a map from
L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
to
C([\delta,T]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}])
. We now state the
LDP
for
\{Z_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Fix
T<\infty
and
0<\delta<T
.
Let
Z_{\varepsilon}
be the solution to (
1.4
) with the Dirac-delta initial data.
\{Z_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
satisfies an
LDP
in
C([\delta,T]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}])
with speed
\varepsilon^{-1}
and the rate function

I(f):=\inf\big\{\tfrac{1}{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,:\,\rho\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}),\mathsf{Z}(\rho)=f\big\},\quad f\in C([\delta,T]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]),

where
\mathsf{Z}(\rho)
is the unique solution to

\partial_{t}\mathsf{Z}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\mathsf{Z}+\rho\mathsf{Z}
with the Dirac-delta initial data
\mathsf{Z}(0,x)=\delta(x)
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
[
LT21
, Proposition 1.7]
Part (b). ∎

Since
Z_{\varepsilon}=e^{h_{\varepsilon}}
, we have
\mathbb{P}[h_{\varepsilon}(2,0)+\log\sqrt{4\pi}\geq\lambda]=\mathbb{P}[Z_{\varepsilon}(2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}]
. By (
1.6
), we have
\Phi(\lambda)=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}[Z_{\varepsilon}(2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}]
.
Taking
T=2
in Proposition
1.7
and applying contraction principle yield that for
\lambda\geq 0
,

(1.11)

\Phi(\lambda)=\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}\,:\rho\in L^{2}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R}),\,\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}\Big\}.

1.3.
Proof ideas

We explain the ideas for proving Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. The first step towards proving Theorem
1.1
is to apply a scaling to the variational formula (
1.11
). By the Feynman-Kac formula (
1.10
), we know that for arbitrary
\rho\in L^{2}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R})
,

\mathsf{Z}(\lambda\rho(\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}});t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{Z}(\rho;\lambda t;\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}x).

Using this relation and applying the scaling
\rho\mapsto\lambda\rho(\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
, one can rewrite the rate function in (
1.11
) as

(1.12)

\Phi(\lambda)=\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}:\rho\in L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R}),\,\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\lambda}}e^{\lambda}\Big\}.

The value of the infimum should be of constant order, this explains the
\tfrac{3}{2}
-power in Theorem
1.1
.
To prove Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that

(1.13)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\inf\Big\{\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}:\rho\in L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R}),\,\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\lambda}}e^{\lambda}\Big\}=\frac{4}{3}.

We explain why the
\frac{4}{3}
appears on the right hand side. To motivate the discussion,
let us assume that the minimizers (there might be more than one) of (
1.13
) become asymptotically
time-independent
as
\lambda\to\infty
. Under this assumption, we only need to consider
\rho
such that
\rho(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=\varphi({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
for
t\in[0,2\lambda]
and some
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. So (
1.13
) simplifies to

(1.14)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\inf\Big\{\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}:\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\,\mathsf{Z}(\varphi;2\lambda,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\lambda}}e^{\lambda}\Big\}=\frac{4}{3}.

Recall that
\mathsf{Z}(\varphi)
solves the PDE
\partial_{t}\mathsf{Z}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\mathsf{Z}+\varphi\mathsf{Z}
. Since
\varphi
does not depend on time, we treat
\mathsf{Z}(t)
as function-valued and view the PDE as a function-valued ODE

\partial_{t}\mathsf{Z}(t)=A^{\varphi}\mathsf{Z}(t)
, where
A^{\varphi}:=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}+\varphi
is a Schrödinger-type operator.
Set the largest eigenvalue of
A^{\varphi}
to be
F(\varphi)
, whose expression is given in (
2.3
). It is natural to expect that
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,0)
at large time
t
grows as
\exp(tF(\varphi))
.
In particular, we have
\mathsf{Z}(\varphi;2\lambda,0)\sim\exp(2\lambda F(\varphi))
as
\lambda\to\infty
. This suggests that

(1.15)

\text{LHS of }\eqref{eq:heuristic2}=\inf\Big\{\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}:F(\varphi)\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big\}.

The problem of finding the minimizers of (
1.15
) can be transformed into understanding when the equality of the Gagliardo–Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality holds, see Lemma
2.4
. It turns out that the minimizers of the infimum are given by

\operatorname{sech}^{2}(x+v)
, where
v
is an arbitrary constant. Since
\|\operatorname{sech}^{2}(x+v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\frac{4}{3}
for all
v
, we know that the right hand side of (
1.15
) equals
\tfrac{4}{3}
. This explains why Theorem
1.1
should hold.

We proceed to explain the idea for proving Theorem
1.2
. Under the time-independence assumption, it is natural to believe that the minimizers of the infimum in (
1.12
) converges to the minimizers of the infimum in (
1.15
) as
\lambda\to\infty
. However, from the previous paragraph, the minimizer of the infimum in (
1.15
) is not unique and this causes
a
problem to our analysis. To resolve this, we show that any minimizer of the infimum in (
1.12
) has to be symmetric in the
x
coordinate (in fact the function should reach its peak at
0
and decrease on both sides of
0
). The proof of this is carried out in Section
3.1.2
. Consequently, we see that the minimizer of (
1.13
) should converge to
\operatorname{sech}^{2}x
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

We denote
\rho_{*}(t,x):=\rho_{*}(x):=\operatorname{sech}^{2}x
. By the Freidlin-Wentzell
LDP
stated in Proposition
1.7
and scaling, one can show that for fixed
\lambda
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the space-time path
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}
concentrates around
\lambda^{-1}\log\mathsf{Z}(\rho_{*};\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
. By the Feynman-Kac formula (
1.10
),

(1.16)

\lambda^{-1}\log\mathsf{Z}(\rho_{*};\lambda t,\lambda x)=\lambda^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}-\lambda^{-1}\log\sqrt{4\pi}.

We seek to compute the
\lambda\to\infty
limit of the first term on the right hand side of (
1.16
).

Assume
x\neq 0
. The Brownian bridge starts from
\lambda x
, which is far way from
0
. Since
\rho_{*}(x)=\operatorname{sech}^{2}(x)
decays exponentially as
|x|\to\infty
, the path of
B_{\text{b}}
contributes little to the integral until it arrives near
0
.
Fix
s\in[0,t]
, the probability of the event that the first time
B_{\text{b}}
hits
0
around time
\lambda s
is approximately
\exp(-\frac{\lambda x^{2}(t-s)}{2st})
. After arriving at
0
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{0\to 0}[\exp(\int_{\lambda s}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(r))dr)]\approx\exp(\frac{\lambda(t-s)}{2})
for large
\lambda
.
Optimizing over
s\in[0,t]
, we expect that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\Big(-\frac{x^{2}(t-s)}{2st}+\frac{t-s}{2}\Big)=\mathsf{h}_{*}(t,x)+\frac{x^{2}}{2t}.

Using this together with (
1.16
) shows that
\lambda^{-1}\log\mathsf{Z}(\rho_{*};\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
converges to
\mathsf{h}_{*}
defined in (
1.7
).

1.4.
Technical Difficulties

In this section, we emphasize some of the technical difficulties in our proof. Denote the set of minimizers of (
1.12
) to be
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
. As mentioned in the previous section, one important step in our proof is to show that
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
converges to
\rho_{*}
as
\lambda\to\infty
. We explain the proof ingredients of it in Sections
1.4.1
and
1.4.2
. In Section
1.4.3
, we describe a technical issue for proving the limit shape.

1.4.1.
Properties of
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}

To prove that the elements of
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
converge to
\rho_{*}
as
\lambda\to\infty
, we first need to show that
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
is not empty, i.e. the minimizer of the infimum in (
1.12
) exists. To prove this, we need 1). the compactness of the level sets
\{\rho:\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq r\}
; 2). the continuity of the map
\rho\mapsto\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)
. Unfortunately, the level sets
\{\rho:\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq r\}
are not compact in
L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})
. To overcome this, we consider an abstract Wiener space
(\mathcal{B},\mu)
such that
L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
is the Cameron-Martin space. This gives us the compactness of
\{\rho:\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq r\}
in
\mathcal{B}
and it preserves the continuity of the map
\rho\mapsto\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)
.

We also need to show that the elements of
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
are symmetric and decreasing in space (see Section
3.1.2
for the precise definition). We prove this by considering the symmetric and decreasing rearrangement of
\rho
. Using the rearrangement inequalities from
[
BLL74
,
LL01
]
, we show that the symmetric and decreasing rearrangement of
\rho
preserves the
L^{2}
norm while it increases the value of
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)
. This implies that the elements in
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
must be symmetric and decreasing in space.

1.4.2.
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
converges to
\rho_{*}

The key innovations for proving the convergence are 1). A near-optimal upper bound of
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)
in terms of an exponential integral of the ground state
F(\rho)
, see Proposition
2.9
; 2). A perturbative analysis of
F
, see Lemma
3.9
.

For 1), one can obtain an upper bound of
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)
via iterating (
1.9
) and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, see Lemma
2.3
. However, such bounds are not optimal. In Proposition
2.9
, we prove a near-optimal bound of
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)
. The proof of the proposition relies on the time-dependent semigroup
\partial_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}-\rho
, an energy estimate when
\rho
is smooth and compactly supported, and the method of approximation for
\rho\in L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})
, which is accomplished in Lemmas
2.3
,
2.6
and
2.7
.

We explain 2) in more detail. Using the
L^{4}
Gagaliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality, we obtain an optimal upper bound of
F(\varphi)
as well as identify its optimizers; see Lemma
2.4
. The optimizer in Lemma
2.4
is only unique up to shifting. Our perturbative analysis says that when
\varphi
is symmetric and decreasing and
F(\varphi)
is near its optimal upper bound,
\varphi
must be close to the symmetric optimizer. The proof of this is through a weak-convergence argument.

The proof of the convergence is carried out in Proposition
3.10
. Combining items 1) and 2), the condition
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
implies that for most
r\in[0,2\lambda]
,
\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
is close to
\|\rho_{*}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
and
F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))
is close to
F(\rho_{*})
, which is the optimal upper bound. This implies the convergence of
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
to
\rho_{*}
, as
\lambda\to\infty
.

1.4.3.
The equi-continuity

By the Freidlin-Wentzell LDP, we can show that for fixed
\lambda>0
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
h_{\varepsilon,\lambda}
concentrates around the set of functions
\lambda^{-1}\log\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{Z}(\mathcal{K}_{\lambda};\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
. In Section
1.4.2
, we explain how to prove that
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
converges to
\rho_{*}
, as
\lambda\to\infty
. To prove Theorem
1.2
, we need to show additionally that the distance between
\lambda^{-1}\log\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{Z}(\mathcal{K}_{\lambda};\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
and
\lambda^{-1}\log\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{Z}(\rho_{*};\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
is small as
\lambda\to\infty
. This is proved by establishing the equi-continuity of the maps
f_{\lambda}:\rho\mapsto\lambda^{-1}\log\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{Z}(\rho;\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},\lambda{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
, see Proposition
4.1
.

The key to the proof is to consider the exponential moment of the

\mathsf{Z}(\varphi;2\lambda,0)=\mathbb{E}_{0\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\varphi(B_{\text{b}}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]p(2\lambda,0).

To analyze (
2.1
),
let us recall some background knowledge from
[
Che10
, Section 4.1]
. Fixing a bounded continuous function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define
T^{\varphi}_{t}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as

(T^{\varphi}_{t}g)(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(B(s))ds\Big)g(B(t))\bigg].

By
[
Che10
, page 105]
,
(T^{\varphi}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
forms a strongly-continuous, self-adjoint and non-negative semi-group on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
A^{\varphi}
be the generator of the semi-group
(T^{\varphi}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Denote the domain of the generator
A^{\varphi}
by
\mathcal{D}(A^{\varphi})
. Let
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be the space of compactly supported smooth function. Let
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
to be the Sobolev space
\{g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):g^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}
. By
[
Che10
, Theorem 4.1.2 and Lemma 4.1.3]
,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(A^{\varphi})\subseteq H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Further, for
g\in\mathcal{D}(A^{\varphi})
,

(2.2)

\langle A^{\varphi}g,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)g(x)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime}(x)^{2}dx.

For
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, define

(2.3)

F(\varphi):=\sup\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)g(x)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime}(x)^{2}dx:g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1\Big\}.

Assume that
\varphi
is bounded and continuous. Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is dense in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(w.r.t. the
H^{1}
-norm), we have

(2.4)

F(\varphi)=\sup\{\langle A^{\varphi}g,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}:g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1\}.

Further, we record two useful inequalities from
[
Che10
, Eq 4.1.25 and 4.1.29]
. For
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,

(2.5)

\exp\big(t\langle A^{\varphi}g,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\big)\leq\langle T^{\varphi}_{t}g,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\exp\big(tF(\varphi)\big).

It is known that (see
[
Che10
, Thereom 4.1.6]
) for bounded continuous
\varphi
,

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{1}{\lambda}\log}\mathbb{E}_{0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi(B(s))ds\Big)\bigg]=F(\varphi).

We prove a similar result for the Brownian bridge, allowing the starting position to deviate from zero by an amount less than
\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix bounded continuous
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\alpha\in(0,1/2)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}_{x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]=F(\varphi).

Remark 2.2
.

Here we only need the
x=0
result for proving (

LimSup

4

Proof of Lemma

.

The idea of the proof is from
[
Che10
, Theroem 4.1.6]
. Recall that for a Brownian bridge
B_{\text{b}}(s)
with
B_{\text{b}}(0)=x
and
B_{\text{b}}(\lambda)=0
, the random variable
B_{\text{b}}(1)
and
B_{\text{b}}(\lambda-1)
has joint probability density function

f_{B_{\text{b}}(1),B_{\text{b}}(\lambda-1)}(y,z):=\frac{p(1,x-y)p(\lambda-2,y-z)p(1,z)}{p(\lambda,x)}.

Using the boundedness of
\varphi
and the preceding joint density in order, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\mathbb{E}_{B_{\text{b}}(0)=x,B_{\text{b}}(\lambda)=0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{1}^{\lambda-1}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Cp(\lambda,x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(1,x-y)\mathbb{E}_{y}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{1}^{\lambda-1}\varphi(B(s))ds\Big)p(1,B(\lambda-1))\bigg]dy.

Since
\varphi
is fixed, we omit the dependence on
\varphi
in the constant
C
. For any compactly supported
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, since
|x|\leq\lambda^{\alpha}
,
\frac{1}{p(\lambda,x)}\geq C\sqrt{\lambda}
and
p(1,x-y)\geq\frac{1}{C(g)}|g(y)|\exp(-C\lambda^{2\alpha})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p(\lambda,x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(1,x-y)\mathbb{E}_{y}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{1}^{\lambda-1}\varphi(B(s))ds\Big)p(1,B(\lambda-1))\bigg]dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\exp(-C\lambda^{2\alpha})}{C(g)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(y)\mathbb{E}_{y}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{1}^{\lambda-1}\varphi(B(s))ds\Big)g(B(\lambda-1))\bigg]dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\exp(-C\lambda^{2\alpha})}{C(g)}\langle T^{\varphi}_{\lambda-2}g,g\rangle\geq\frac{\exp(-C\lambda^{2\alpha})}{C(g)}\exp\Big((\lambda-2)\langle A^{\varphi}g,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big).

The last inequality follows from (
2.5
). Inserting the above bound into
the right hand side of (

) yields

\mathbb{E}_{x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]\geq\frac{\exp(-C\lambda^{2\alpha})}{C(g)}\exp\Big((\lambda-2)\langle A^{\varphi}g,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big).

Taking the logarithm of the both sides, dividing the result by
\lambda
and sending

, we obtain that uniformly for
|x|\leq\lambda^{\alpha}

Taking the supremum of the right hand side over

To prove the reverse inequality, we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\{|B_{b}(1)|\leq\lambda^{2},|B_{b}(\lambda-1)|\leq\lambda^{2}\}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\{|B_{b}(1)|\leq\lambda^{2},|B_{b}(\lambda-1)|\leq\lambda^{2}\}}\bigg]+C\exp(-C^{-1}\lambda^{2}).

Recall that
|x|\leq\lambda^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x\to 0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\{|B_{b}(1)|\leq\lambda^{2},|B_{b}(\lambda-1)|\leq\lambda^{2}\}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}_{B_{\text{b}}(0)=x,B_{\text{b}}(\lambda)=0}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{1}^{\lambda-1}\varphi(B_{b}(s))ds\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\{|B_{b}(1)|\leq\lambda^{2},|B_{b}(\lambda-1)|\leq\lambda^{2}\}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{p(\lambda,x)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(1,x-y)\mathbf{1}_{\{|y|\leq\lambda^{2}\}}\mathbb{E}_{y}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{1}^{\lambda-1}\varphi(B(s))ds\Big)p(1,B(\lambda-1))\mathbf{1}_{\{|B(\lambda-1)|\leq\lambda^{2}\}}\bigg]dy.

2.2.
Proof of (

LimInf

Fix
T>0
and
\rho\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
. Let us define the time-dependent semigroup
P(\rho;s\to t):L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
0\leq s<t\leq T
. Given a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, fix
s
and consider the PDE

(2.11)

\partial_{r}\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(r,x)=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{xx}\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(r,x)+\rho(r,x)\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(r,x),\qquad\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(s,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=f.

We define
P(\rho;s\to t)f:=\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
. Note that
\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}
is the unique solution to the integral equation

\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-s,x-y)f(y)dy+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-r,x-y)\rho(r,y)\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(r,y)drdy.

Via Picard iteration, we have
\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}}P((s,x)\to(t,y))f(y)dy
where the kernel

\displaystyle P(\rho;(s,x)\to(t,y))

(2.12)

\displaystyle:=p(t-s,x-y)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{s<t_{n}<\dots<t_{1}<t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}p(t_{i-1}-t_{i},x_{i-1}-x_{i})\rho(t_{i},x_{i})dt_{i}dx_{i}.

Here, we set
t_{0}=t
and
x_{0}=x
,
t_{n+1}=s
and
x_{n+1}=y
.

Next we establish four bounds for the time-dependent semigroup.

Lemma 2.3
.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that for all
s<t
and
a>0
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\|P(\rho;(s,0)\to(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(t-s)^{-1/4}\exp(a^{2}C(t-s)+\frac{1}{a}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}),

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|P(\rho;(s,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\to(t,0)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(t-s)^{-1/4}\exp(a^{2}C(t-s)+\frac{1}{a}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}),

(2.15)

\displaystyle\|P(\rho;s\to t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\exp(a^{2}C(t-s)+\frac{1}{a}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}),

(2.16)

\displaystyle|P(\rho;(s,y)\to(t,x))|\leq C\exp(a^{2}C(t-s)+\frac{1}{a}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})})p(t-s,x-y).

Proof.

In the proof, we assume without loss of generality that
s=0
.

Let us first prove (
2.13
).
View the right hand side of (
2.12
) as a series of function in
x
, and bound the
L^{2}
norm of each function. For the zeroth function in (
2.12
), we have
\|p(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
For the
n
-th functions in (
2.12
), applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\text{(n-th)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0<t_{n}<\dots<t_{1}<t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}p(t_{i-1}-t_{i},x_{i-1}-x_{i})^{2}dt_{i}dx_{i}dx\frac{\|\rho\|^{2n}_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{n}}{\Gamma(n/2)}t^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\|\rho\|^{2n}_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}}{n!}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\|P(\rho;(0,0)\to(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{C^{n}t^{n/4}}{\Gamma(n/2)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{\|\rho\|^{n}_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}}{(n!)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{n}C^{n}t^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{a^{-n}\|\rho\|^{2n}_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}}{n!}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This concludes (
2.13
).

The left hand sides of (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) are the same upon time reversal, so (
2.14
) follows.

The proof of (
2.15
) is similar in spirit to (
2.13
). Using
(P(\rho;0\to t)f)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}P((0,x)\to(t,y))f(y)dy
and (
2.12
), we express
P(\rho;0\to t)f
as a series of functions. We bound the
L^{2}
norm of each function in the series.
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

(2.17)

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t,x-y)f(y)dy\Big)^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t,x-y)f(y)^{2}dy.

Integrating both sides of (
2.17
) in
x
gives that for the zero-th function,
\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}-y)f(y)dy\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}
.
For the
n
-th function, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle\|\text{(n-th)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0<t_{n}<\dots<t_{1}<t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\int p(t_{n},x_{n}-y)f(y)dy\Big)^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(t_{i-1}-t_{i},x_{i-1}-x_{i})^{2}dt_{i}dx_{i}dx\frac{\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2n}}{n!}.

Using (
2.17
) and
p(t,x)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}t}p(t,x)
, we get

\|\text{(n-th)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq\frac{\|\rho\|^{2n}_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}}{n!}\int_{0<t_{n}<\dots<t_{1}<t}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t_{i-1}-t_{i})}}dt_{i}=\frac{C^{n}t^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}\frac{\|\rho\|^{2n}_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}}{n!}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Taking the square root of both sides and summing over
n
, the rest of the proof is similar to the proof of (
2.13
).

To prove (
2.16
), we view the right hand side of (
2.12
) as a series of real numbers. We bound the value of each term in the series. The zeroth term equals
p(t-s,x-y)
. For the
n
-th term, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle|(\text{n-th})|^{2}\leq\int_{0<t_{n}<\dots<s_{1}<t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}p(t_{i-1}-t_{i},x_{i-1}-x_{i})^{2}dt_{i}dx_{i}\frac{\|\rho\|_{L([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2n}}{n!}\leq\frac{C^{n}t^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}\frac{\|\rho\|^{2n}_{L^{2}([0,t]\times\mathbb{R})}}{n!}p(t,x-y)^{2}.

Taking the square root of both sides and summing over
n
, the rest of the proof is similar to the proof of (
2.13
).

∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For every
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

(2.18)

F(\varphi)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{4})^{2/3}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{4}{3}}.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\varphi({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=\alpha^{2}\operatorname{sech}^{2}(\alpha({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}-v))
for some
\alpha\geq 0
.

Proof.

Referring to (
2.3
), we have

(2.19)

\displaystyle F(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\Big\{\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big(\int g(x)^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\int g^{\prime}(x)^{2}dx\Big\}:g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1\Big\}

(2.20)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\Big\{3^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}:g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1\Big\}

(2.21)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{4})^{2/3}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{4}{3}}.

The inequality (
2.19
) is due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The inequality (
2.20
) follows from the one-dimensional
L^{4}
Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality, which states that, for
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
g^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

(2.22)

\|g\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 3^{-1/8}\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{1/4}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{3/4}.

The inequality (
2.21
) follows from the elementary inequality
3^{-\frac{1}{4}}yx^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{4})^{2/3}y^{\frac{4}{3}}
.

To prove the if and only if statement, we need to investigate when the inequalities (
2.19
)-(
2.21
) become equalities.
The inequality (
2.19
) becomes an equality if and only if
\varphi=\text{const}\cdot g
. By
[
DELL14
, Proposition 3.1]
, the inequality (
2.20
), i.e. (
2.22
) becomes an equality if and only if
g(x)=a\operatorname{sech}(b(x-v))
for some
a,v
and
b>0
; the inequality (
2.21
) becomes an equality if and only if
\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=(\frac{1}{2})^{2/3}3^{-1/6}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2/3}
. Combining these with
\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1
gives that

g({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=a\operatorname{sech}\big(2a^{2}({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}-v)\big),\qquad\varphi({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=4a^{4}\operatorname{sech}^{2}\big(2a^{2}({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}-v)\big).

Replacing
2a^{2}
with
\alpha
implies that
\varphi({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=\alpha^{2}\operatorname{sech}^{2}(\alpha({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}-v))
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

We only need (
2.18
) for this section, and the if and only if part is required in Section
3
.

The key for proving (

LimInf

) is an improved upper bound (compared with (
2.15
)) for
\|P(\rho;s\to t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
stated in Proposition
2.9
. To prove this upper bound, we need to first establish the following two lemmas about continuity.

Lemma 2.6
.

Fix
0\leq s<t
. The map
\|P({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}};s\to t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}:L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous.

Proof.

Fix
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Since
\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(t,x)=(P(\rho;s\to t)f)(x)
solves (
2.11
), by the Feynman-Kac formula,

\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(\rho;t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}\rho(t-r,B(r))dr\Big)f(B(t))\bigg].

Given
\rho,\widetilde{\rho}\in L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})
. We write
\rho=\rho-(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho}+(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho}
in the exponent above and apply the Hölder inequality to the result. We have for
\zeta\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(\rho;t,x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg[\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}(\rho-(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho})(t-r,B(r))dr\Big)\big(f(B(t))\big)^{\zeta}

\displaystyle\hskip 52.00005pt\cdot\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho}(t-r,B(r))dr\Big)\big(f(B(t))\big)^{1-\zeta}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}\big(\zeta^{-1}(\rho-(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho});t,x\big)^{\zeta}\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(\widetilde{\rho};t,x)^{1-\zeta}.

Squaring both sides, integrating in
x
and using the Hölder inequality again gives

\big\|\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(\rho;t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\big\|\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}\big(\zeta^{-1}(\rho-(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho});t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\zeta}\big\|\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(\widetilde{\rho};t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{1-\zeta}.

Taking the supremum over
\{f:\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1\}
yields that

(2.23)

\big\|P(\rho;s\to t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\big\|P\big(\zeta^{-1}(\rho-(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho});s\to t\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\zeta}\big\|P(\widetilde{\rho};s\to t)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{1-\zeta}.

Set
G(\rho):=\log\|P(\rho;s\to t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
. Our goal is to show that
G
is continuous in
\rho
. We do this by showing that
G
is both upper and lower semi-continuous. Taking the logarithm of both sides of (
2.23
) and applying (
2.15
) yields

\displaystyle G(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta G\big(\zeta^{-1}(\rho-(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho})\big)+(1-\zeta)G(\widetilde{\rho})

(2.24)

\displaystyle\leq C(s,t)\zeta+C\zeta\|\zeta^{-1}(\rho-(1-\zeta)\widetilde{\rho})\|_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}+(1-\zeta)G(\widetilde{\rho}).

Subtracting
(1-\zeta)G(\widetilde{\rho})+\zeta G(\rho)
from both sides and applying the inequality
\|f+g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2(\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2})
yield

\displaystyle(1-\zeta)(G(\rho)-G(\widetilde{\rho}))

\displaystyle\leq C(s,t)\zeta+C\zeta^{-1}(1-\zeta)^{2}\|\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}+C\zeta\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}-\zeta G(\rho)

\displaystyle\leq C\zeta^{-1}(1-\zeta)^{2}\|\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\zeta C(s,t,\rho).

Taking
\zeta=\|\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}
and letting
\widetilde{\rho}\to\rho
, we see that
\limsup_{\widetilde{\rho}\to\rho}G(\widetilde{\rho})\leq G(\rho)
. Thus,
G
is upper semi-continuous.

On the other hand, if we swap
\rho
and
\widetilde{\rho}
in (
2.24
), a similar argument gives

G(\widetilde{\rho})-G(\rho)\leq\zeta C(s,t,\rho)+C\zeta^{-1}\|\widetilde{\rho}-\rho\|_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Taking
\zeta=\|\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}
and letting
\widetilde{\rho}\to\rho
, we see that
G
is lower semi-continuous. This concludes the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix
0\leq s<t
and
M\geq 0
. There exists a constant
C(M)>0
. For
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
0\leq\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\leq M
almost everywhere, we have

|F(\varphi_{1})-F(\varphi_{2})|\leq C(M)\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

By the definition of
F(\varphi_{2})
in (
2.3
), for any
\zeta>0
, there exists
g_{\zeta}
that
\|g_{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=1
and

(2.25)

F(\varphi_{2})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{2}g_{\zeta}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(g^{\prime}_{\zeta})^{2}+\zeta.

Moreover, we have
F(\varphi_{1})\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{1}g_{\zeta}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(g^{\prime}_{\zeta})^{2}
. Using this and (
2.25
), we have

\displaystyle F(\varphi_{2})-F(\varphi_{1})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1})g_{\zeta}^{2}+\zeta.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the right hand side above and then applying (
2.22
) to the result, we have

(2.26)

F(\varphi_{2})-F(\varphi_{1})\leq 3^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\|g_{\zeta}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\zeta.

By (
2.25
),
\frac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime}_{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{2}g_{\zeta}^{2}-F(\varphi_{2})+\zeta.
Since
0\leq\varphi_{2}\leq M
and
\|g_{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1
, we can upper bound the first term on the right hand side by
M
and the second term by a universal constant
C
. Hence, we have

\frac{1}{2}\|g^{\prime}_{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq C(M).
Inserting this to the right hand side of (
2.26
) and letting
\zeta\to 0
yields

F(\varphi_{2})-F(\varphi_{1})\leq C(M)\|\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

Swapping
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
concludes the lemma.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Fix
0\leq s<t
and
M\geq 0
. There exists a constant
C(M,t,s)
. For
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})

∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Fix
0\leq s<t
. For all
\rho\in L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\rho\geq 0
almost everywhere, we have

(2.28)

\|P(\rho;s\to t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))dr\Big).

Proof.

Fix smooth and compactly supported
\rho
and
f
. Since
\rho
is smooth, the function
\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(t,x)=(P(s\to t)f)(x)
solves (
2.11
) point-wise.
Multiply both side by
\mathsf{Z}
, integrate the result over
x\in\mathbb{R}
and interchange the integration and differentiation. We have

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}\|\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))\|\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Integrating in time from
s
to
t
gives

\|\mathsf{Z}^{s,f}(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\exp\Big(2\int_{s}^{t}F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))dr\Big).

For fixed smooth and compact supported
\rho
, by approximation, the above inequality also holds for general
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus, we have proven
(
2.28
) for smooth and compactly supported
\rho
.

The general result follows by approximation and monotonicity.
Fix arbitrary
M\geq 0
. We first prove that (
2.28
) holds for all
\rho\in L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})
such that almost everywhere
0\leq\rho\leq M
. By a density argument, there exists smooth and compactly supported functions
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
0\leq\rho_{n}\leq M
and
\|\rho_{n}-\rho\|_{L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. (
2.28
) holds when
\rho
is replaced by
\rho_{n}
. Let
n\to\infty
, by Lemma
2.6
and Corollary
2.8
, (
2.28
) also holds for
\rho
.

Finally, we prove (
2.28
) for
\rho\in L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})
such that almost everywhere
\rho\geq 0
. As
n\to\infty
,
\rho\mathbf{1}_{\{\rho\leq n\}}\to\rho
in
L^{2}([s,t]\times\mathbb{R})
. In the preceding paragraph, we have proved that (
2.28
) holds when
\rho
is replaced by
\rho\mathbf{1}_{\{\rho\leq n\}}
, for all
n
. Using this and the monotonicity of

\|P(\rho\mathbf{1}_{\{\rho\leq n\}};s\to t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}F(\rho\mathbf{1}_{\{\rho\leq n\}}(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))dr\Big)\leq\exp\Big(\int_{s}^{t}F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))dr\Big).

Letting

, by Lemma
2.6
, the left hand side above converges to
\|P(\rho;s\to t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
. Hence, we conclude (
2.28
).
∎

Based on the preceding results, we now proceed to prove (

LimInf

). The proof amounts to showing that,
for any
\rho\in L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}
, we have
\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}\geq\frac{4}{3}-o_{\lambda}(1)
where
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}o_{\lambda}(1)=0
.

With loss of generality, we can assume that

(2.29)

\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda])\times\mathbb{R}}\leq 2.

Moreover, define
\rho_{+}:=\min(\rho,0)
. Because
\mathsf{Z}(\rho_{+};2\lambda,0)\geq\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)
and
\|\rho_{+}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}
, we can also assume that
\rho\geq 0
almost everywhere.
Note that
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)=P(\rho;(0,0)\to(2\lambda,0))

3.1.2.
Symmetric decreasing function in space

In this subsection, we show that every
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
is symmetric decreasing in space.

We say that
f
is
symmetric decreasing
if there exists a non-increasing function
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
f(x)=g(|x|)
holds for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We say that
f
is
strictly symmetric decreasing
if
g
is strictly decreasing. We say that a measurable function
\rho:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
is
symmetric decreasing in space
if for almost every
0\leq s\leq T
,
\rho(s,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
is symmetric decreasing.

Proposition 3.3
.

Fix
\lambda>0
, every element
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
is symmetric decreasing in space.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a measurable set with finite Lebesgue measure
|A|
. We define the
symmetric rearrangement
of
A
, denoted
A^{*}
, as the interval
[-|A|/2,|A|/2]
. Given a measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
, we can express
f
by the layer-cake representation
f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}:f(y)>\ell\}}(x)d\ell
. We define its
symmetric decreasing rearrangement
as

f^{*}(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}:f(y)>\ell\}^{*}}(x)d\ell.

It is straightforward to show that for arbitrary fixed
\ell\geq 0
, the sets
\{x:f(x)\geq\ell\}
and
\{x:f^{*}(x)\geq\ell\}
have the same Lebesgue measure. As a consequence,
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=\|f^{*}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}
for every
p\geq 1
.

Let
\rho:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
. We define the
Steiner symmetrization
of
\rho
along the time-axis, denoted
\rho^{\mathsf{s}}
, as follows: For every fixed
0\leq s\leq T
, we define the function
\rho^{\mathsf{s}}(s,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}):=\rho^{*}(s,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
.

To prove Proposition
3.3
, we rely on the following two rearrangement inequalities known respectively as the Hardy-Littlewood inequality and the Brascamp-Lieb-Luttinger inequality.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
LL01
, Theorem 3.4]
)
.

For any non-negative
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)g(x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{*}(x)g^{*}(x)dx.

Moreover, if
f=f^{*}
is strictly symmetric decreasing, then the inequality above becomes an equality if and only if
g=g^{*}
almost everywhere.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
BLL74
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
f_{j},1\leq j\leq k
, be non-negative measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}
, and let
a_{jm}
,
1\leq j\leq k
,
1\leq m\leq n
be real numbers, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{j}(\sum_{m=1}^{n}a_{jm}x_{m})dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{j}^{*}(\sum_{m=1}^{n}a_{jm}x_{m})dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}.

The following result contains the major step for proving Proposition
3.3
.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\rho\in L^{2}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R})
is non-negative, then
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2,0)\leq\mathsf{Z}(\rho^{\mathsf{s}};2,0)
. In addition, the equality holds if and only if
\rho
is symmetric decreasing in space.

Proof.

Note that

(3.5)

\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2,0)=p(2,0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0<t_{n}<\dots<t_{1}<t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}p(t_{i-1}-t_{i},x_{i-1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho(t_{i},x_{i})dt_{i}dx_{i},

where
t_{0}=2
and
x_{0}=t_{n+1}=x_{n+1}=0
. For the
n=1
term in the sum above, by Lemma
3.4
,

(3.6)

\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(2-s,x)p(s,x)\rho(s,x)dsdx\leq\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(2-s,x)p(s,x)\rho^{\mathsf{s}}(s,x)dsdx.

Since
p(2-s,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})p(s,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
is strictly symmetric decreasing for every fixed
s\in(0,2)
, the above becomes an equality if and only if
\rho
is symmetric decreasing in space.

For the
n\geq 2
term. View the integrand as product of functions in space for fixed
t_{n}<t_{n-1}<\dots<t_{1}
and note that
p(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})=p^{\mathsf{s}}(t,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})

\displaystyle\int_{0<t_{n}<\dots<t_{1}<t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}p(t_{i-1}-t_{i},x_{i-1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho(t_{i},x_{i})dt_{i}dx_{i}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0<t_{n}<\dots<t_{1}<t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n+1}p(t_{i-1}-t_{i},x_{i-1}-x_{i})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\rho^{\mathsf{s}}(t_{i},x_{i})dt_{i}dx_{i}.

Combining (
3.5

\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2,0)\leq\mathsf{Z}(\rho^{\mathsf{s}};2,0)
. Furthermore, the equality holds if and only if

∎

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\big[\mathrm{dist}_{\delta}(Z_{\varepsilon},\mathsf{Z}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}))<\delta\,\big|\,Z_{\varepsilon}(2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}\big]=1,

and then explain how (
3.11
) implies (
3.10
).

To prove (
3.11
), consider the set
\mathsf{G}_{\lambda,\delta}:=\{f\in C([\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]):f(2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}\text{ and }\mathrm{dist}_{\delta}(f,\mathsf{Z}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}))\geq\delta\}
. Since
\mathsf{G}_{\lambda,\delta}
is a closed set in
C([\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}])
, by Proposition
1.7
, we have

(3.12)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}\big[Z_{\varepsilon}\in\mathsf{G}_{\lambda,\delta}\big]\leq-\inf_{f\in\mathsf{G}_{\lambda,\delta}}I(f).

By (
1.11
), we also have

(3.13)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}\big[Z_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}\big]=-q_{\lambda},

recall that
q_{\lambda}
is equal to (
3.2
). Using

\mathbb{P}\big[\mathrm{dist}_{\delta}(Z_{\varepsilon},\mathsf{Z}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}))\geq\delta\,\big|\,Z_{\varepsilon}(2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}\big]=\frac{\mathbb{P}[Z_{\varepsilon}\in\mathsf{G}_{\lambda,\delta}]}{\mathbb{P}[Z_{\varepsilon}(2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}]}

and applying (
3.12
)-(
3.13
), we have

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\log\mathbb{P}\big[\mathrm{dist}_{\delta}(Z_{\varepsilon},\mathsf{Z}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}))\geq\delta\,\big|\,Z_{\varepsilon}(2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}\big]\leq q_{\lambda}-\inf_{f\in\mathsf{G}_{\lambda,\delta}}I(f).

By Lemma
3.7
, there exists
\zeta>0
such that
\inf_{f\in\mathsf{G}_{\lambda,\delta}}I(f)\geq q_{\lambda}+\zeta
. The above inequality implies

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\big[\mathrm{dist}_{\delta}(Z_{\varepsilon},\mathsf{Z}(\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}))\geq\delta\,\big|\,Z_{\varepsilon}(2,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}}e^{\lambda}\big]=0.

Therefore, We conclude (
3.11
).

Finally, we show how (
3.11
) implies (
3.10
).
Recall that
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,x)=P(\rho;(0,0)\to(t,x))
. Referring to (
2.12
), due to the non-negativity of
\rho
, we have the lower bound
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,x)\geq p(t,x)
. The upper bound of
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,x)
is given by (
2.16
). This implies the existence of a constant
M
such that for all
\rho\in\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\lambda}
,

(3.14)

M^{-1}\leq\|\mathsf{Z}(\rho)\|_{L^{\infty}([\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}])}\leq M.

(
3.10
) follows from (
3.11
), (
3.14
) and the uniform continuity of the
\log
function on the interval
[(2M)^{-1},2M]
.
∎

3.3.
The
\lambda\to\infty
asymptotic of
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}

For simplicity, we adopt the shorthand notation
\|{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}\|_{2}:=\|{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
. The functional
F
defined in (
2.3
)
enjoys a scaling property: Letting
\varphi_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha^{2}\varphi(\alpha x)
, we have
F(\varphi_{\alpha})=\alpha^{2}F(\varphi)
. Choosing
\alpha=\|\varphi\|_{2}^{-2/3}
to normalize the
L^{2}
norm of
\varphi
, we have

(3.15)

F(\varphi)=\|\varphi\|_{2}^{\frac{4}{3}}F(\varphi_{\|\varphi\|_{2}^{-2/3}}),\qquad\|\varphi_{\|\varphi\|_{2}^{-2/3}}\|_{2}=1.

We set

(3.16)

\mathsf{r}_{\star}(x):=(\frac{3}{4})^{2/3}\operatorname{sech}^{2}((\frac{3}{4})^{1/3}x).

Also, let
\mathsf{SD}
denote the space of symmetric decreasing functions (see Section
3.1.2
for the definition).

Lemma 3.9
.

For any
\zeta>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for all
\varphi\in\mathsf{SD}
,

F(\varphi)\geq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{4})^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\varphi\|_{2}^{\frac{4}{3}}(1-\delta)\ \text{ implies }\ \|\varphi_{\|\varphi\|_{2}^{-2/3}}-\mathsf{r}_{\star}\|_{2}<\zeta.

Proof.

By the scaling (
3.15
) we assume without loss of generality that
\|\varphi\|_{2}=1
. The desired statement is equivalent to the following: Any sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}\subseteq\mathsf{SD}
with
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{2}=1
such that
F(\varphi_{n})\to\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{4})^{\frac{2}{3}}
has a subsequence that converges in
L^{2}
to
\mathsf{r}_{\star}
. Fix any such
\varphi_{n}
. Let
f_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\|f_{n}\|_{2}=1
and
\int\varphi_{n}f_{n}^{\prime 2}-\frac{1}{2}f_{n}^{\prime 2}dx\to\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{4})^{2/3}
. Since
\{(\varphi_{n},f_{n},f_{n}^{\prime})\}
is bounded in
(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))^{3}
, by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, after passing to a subsequence,
(\varphi_{n},f_{n},f_{n}^{\prime})
converges weakly to
(\mathsf{r}_{*},f_{*},g_{*})
with
\|r_{*}\|_{2}\leq 1
,
\|f_{*}\|_{2}\leq 1
and
\|g_{*}\|\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}^{\prime}\|_{2}
. Since
(f_{n},f_{n}^{\prime})
converges weakly to
(f_{*},g_{*})
, we must have
f^{\prime}_{*}=g_{*}
. Moreover, since
f_{n}
and
f_{n}^{\prime}
are bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
f_{n}
is locally uniformly bounded and equi-continuous. By the Arzela-Ascoli theorem, we know that
f_{n}\to f_{*}
on compact sets. Next, because
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{2}=1
and because
\varphi_{n}\in\mathsf{SD}
,
\varphi_{n}(\pm M)\leq M^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Therefore,
\int_{|x|>M}\varphi_{n}f_{n}^{2}dx\leq M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=M^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. This gives

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{n}f_{n}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x|\leq M\}}-\frac{1}{2}f_{n}^{\prime 2}dx\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{3}{4}\Big)^{\frac{2}{3}}-M^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

The left hand side is bounded above by
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathsf{r}_{*}f_{*}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}{f_{*}^{\prime}}^{2}dx
. Sending
M\to\infty
gives
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathsf{r}_{*}f_{*}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}{f^{\prime}_{*}}^{2}dx\geq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{4})^{2/3}
. This together with
\|\mathsf{r}_{*}\|_{2}\leq 1
and
\|f_{*}\|_{2}\leq 1
implies that
F(\mathsf{r}_{*})\geq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{4})^{2/3}
. By the if and only if part in Lemma
2.4
and the fact that
\mathsf{r}_{*}\in\mathsf{SD}
, we know that
\mathsf{r}_{*}=\mathsf{r}_{\star}
. Since
\varphi_{n}
weakly converges to
\mathsf{r}_{\star}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varphi_{n}\|_{2}=1=\|\mathsf{r}_{\star}\|_{2}
, we conclude that
\varphi_{n}
converges to
\mathsf{r}_{\star}
in
L^{2}
.
∎

Recall that
\rho_{*}(t,x)=\rho_{*}(x)=(\operatorname{sech}x)^{2}
. The main result of this section says that the elements of
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
tend to
\rho_{*}
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Proposition 3.10
.

We have
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\sup\{\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\rho-\rho_{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}:\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}\}=0
.

Proof.

Set
\zeta>0
. According to (
1.13
), which is proved in Section
2
, for large enough
\lambda
and all
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
,

(3.17)

\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{4}{3}+\zeta.

By
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;2\lambda,0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\lambda}}e^{\lambda}
and (
2.30
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\lambda}}e^{\lambda}\leq C\exp\Big(C\lambda^{2/3}+\int_{0}^{2\lambda}F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))dr\Big).

Taking the logarithm of both sides and then applying Lemma
2.4
to the right hand side yields that for all
\lambda
large enough,

\lambda(1-\zeta)\leq\int_{0}^{2\lambda}F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))dr\leq\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{3}{4}\Big)^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{\frac{4}{3}}dr.

Young’s inequality gives (
(\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2})^{2/3}\leq\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3}(\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2})
). Furthermore, there exists a strictly increasing function
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\psi(0)=0
,
(\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2})^{2/3}\leq\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2}{3}(\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2})-\psi(|1-\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2}|)
. Applying this to the right hand side above yields

\displaystyle\lambda(1-\zeta)\leq\int_{0}^{2\lambda}F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))dr\leq\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{3}{4}\Big)^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{\frac{4}{3}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\psi(1-\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2})dr

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(1+\zeta)-\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\frac{1}{2}\psi(1-\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2})dr.

The last inequality is due to (
3.17
). Using the inequalities above, we have for all
\lambda
large enough,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\psi(|1-\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2}|)dr\leq 4\lambda\zeta,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{3}{4}\Big)^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{\frac{4}{3}}-F(\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}))dr\leq 4\lambda\zeta.

Since
\psi
is strictly increasing with
\psi(0)=0
. For any
\zeta^{\prime}>0
, we have
\psi(z)\geq\psi(\zeta^{\prime})=:C(\zeta^{\prime})
when
z\geq\zeta^{\prime}
. Applying this to (
3.18
) gives that for all
\lambda
large enough,

(3.20)

\text{Leb}\bigg[\Big\{r\in[0,2\lambda]:\Big|1-\frac{3}{4}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2}\Big|>\zeta^{\prime}\Big\}\bigg]\leq\frac{4\lambda\zeta}{C(\zeta^{\prime})}.

Here,
Leb
denotes the Lebesgue measure.
Since
\rho\in\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}
, by Proposition
3.3
,
\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\in\mathsf{SD}
for almost all
r\in[0,2\lambda]
. Using (
3.19
) and Lemma
3.9
we conclude that for any
\zeta^{\prime}>0
, there exists
C(\zeta^{\prime})>0
such that

(3.21)

\text{Leb}\bigg[\Big\{r\in[0,2\lambda]:\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})_{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{-2/3}}-r_{\star}\|_{2}>\zeta^{\prime}\Big\}\bigg]\leq\frac{\lambda\zeta}{C(\zeta^{\prime})},

where we denote
\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})_{\alpha}=\alpha^{2}\rho(r,\alpha{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
. We have

\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})_{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{-2/3}}-r_{\star}\|_{2}=\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{-1}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-(r_{\star})_{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2/3}}\|_{2}.

Applying this to (
3.21
) yields

(3.22)

\text{Leb}\bigg[\Big\{r\in[0,2\lambda]:\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-(r_{\star})_{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2/3}}\|_{2}>\zeta^{\prime}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}\Big\}\bigg]\leq\frac{\lambda\zeta}{C(\zeta^{\prime})}.

Taking
\zeta\leq\min(C(\zeta^{\prime}),1)\zeta^{\prime}
, the right hand side of (
3.22
) is upper bounded by
\lambda\zeta^{\prime}
. By (
3.20
), we know that for most
r\in[0,2\lambda]
,
\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}
is around
\sqrt{\frac{4}{3}}
. Applying this and
(r_{\star})_{(\sqrt{\frac{4}{3}})^{2/3}}=\rho_{*}
to (
3.22
) gives that for any
\zeta^{\prime}>0
,

(3.23)

\text{Leb}\bigg[\Big\{r\in[0,2\lambda]:\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-\rho_{*}\|_{2}>\zeta^{\prime}\Big\}\bigg]\leq C\lambda\zeta^{\prime}

holds for all
\lambda
large enough.
The inequality (
3.23
) together with the fact
\|\rho_{*}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{4}{3}

implies

\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-\rho_{*}\|_{2}\leq\zeta^{\prime}\}}dr\geq\frac{4}{3}-C\zeta^{\prime}.

This, together with (
3.17
) (we have taken
\zeta\leq\zeta^{\prime}
) imply that

(3.24)

\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-\rho_{*}\|_{2}>\zeta^{\prime}\}}dr\leq C\zeta^{\prime}.

In addition, by
\|\rho_{*}(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{4}{3}
and (
3.23
),

(3.25)

\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\|\rho_{*}(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|_{2}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-\rho_{*}\|_{2}>\zeta^{\prime}\}}dr\leq C\zeta^{\prime}.

Combining (
3.24
) and (
3.25
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|\rho-\rho_{*}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-\rho_{*}(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|^{2}_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-\rho_{*}\|_{2}>\zeta^{\prime}\}}dr

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\int_{0}^{2\lambda}\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-\rho_{*}(r,{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})\|^{2}_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\rho(r,{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})-\rho_{*}\|_{2}\leq\zeta^{\prime}\}}dr\leq C\zeta^{\prime}.

Letting
\lambda\to\infty
and then
\zeta^{\prime}\to 0
concludes the proposition.
∎

4.
The Limit shape

The goal of the section is to prove Theorem
1.2
.

4.1.
Equi-continuity of
\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}

Recall from Section
3.2
that
\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\rho;t,x)=\lambda^{-1}\log\big(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathsf{Z}(\rho;\lambda t,\lambda x)\big)
.
Let
L_{\geq 0}^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})
denote the set of non-negative functions which belong to
L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})
. Consider the normalized norm

\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}
. The following proposition settles the equi-continuity of
\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},t,x):L_{\geq 0}^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to this norm.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists a constant
C
such that for all
\lambda>0,\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in L_{\geq 0}^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})
and
(t,x)\in(0,2]\times\mathbb{R}
, if
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}<1
,

|\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\rho_{1};t,x)-\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\rho_{2};t,x)|\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}(1+\lambda^{-1}\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\lambda^{-1}\|\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}).

Proof.

Set
\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho;t,x)=\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,x)/p(t,x)
. Since

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)-\lambda^{-1}\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\log\mathsf{Z}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)-\lambda^{-1}\log\mathsf{Z}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)-\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x),

it suffices to prove that for
\lambda>0
and
(t,x)\in(0,2]\times\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\big|\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)-\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)\big|

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}(1+\lambda^{-1}\|\rho_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\lambda^{-1}\|\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}).

Note that
\mathsf{Z}(\rho;t,x)=P(\rho;(0,0)\to(t,x))
. We apply (
2.16
) with
a=1
to obtain

(4.2)

\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho;\lambda t,\lambda x)\leq C\exp(C\lambda t+\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,\lambda t]\times\mathbb{R})}).

The constant
C
here is universal. Further, by the Feynman-Kac formula,

\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho;\lambda t,\lambda x)=\mathbb{E}_{0\to\lambda x}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\rho(s,B_{\text{b}}(s))\Big)\Big].

Let us use (
4.2
) and the Feynman-Kac formula to prove (
4.1
).
Fix
\zeta\in(0,1)
. Similar to the proof of Lemma
2.6
, by the Feynman-Kac formula and the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\zeta^{-1}(\rho_{1}-(1-\zeta)\rho_{2});\lambda t,\lambda x)^{\zeta}\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)^{1-\zeta}.

Taking the logarithm of both sides yields

\displaystyle\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\zeta^{-1}(\rho_{1}-(1-\zeta)\rho_{2});\lambda t,\lambda x)+(1-\zeta)\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\zeta^{-1}(\rho_{1}-(1-\zeta)\rho_{2});\lambda t,\lambda x)+\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x).

The last inequality is due to
\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};t,x)\geq 1
, since
\rho_{2}
is non-negative.

Subtracting
\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)
from both sides of (
4.3
) and applying (
4.2
) to the resulting right hand side, we get

\displaystyle\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)-\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)

\displaystyle\leq\zeta\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\zeta^{-1}(\rho_{1}-(1-\zeta)\rho_{2});\lambda t,\lambda x)

\displaystyle\leq C\zeta\lambda t+C\zeta^{-1}\|\rho_{1}-(1-\zeta)\rho_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,\lambda t]\times\mathbb{R})}.

Applying
t\leq 2
and the inequality
\|f+g\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq 2(\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2})
to the right hand side leads to

(4.4)

\displaystyle\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)-\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)\leq C\zeta\lambda+C\zeta^{-1}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,\lambda t]\times\mathbb{R})}+C\zeta\|\rho_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,\lambda t]\times\mathbb{R})}.

The constant
C
is universal and does not depend on
\lambda,t,x
. Swapping
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
in (
4.4
) gives that

\big|\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)-\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)\big|\leq C\Big(\zeta^{-1}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,\lambda t]\times\mathbb{R})}+\zeta(\lambda+\|\rho_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,\lambda t]\times\mathbb{R})}+\|\rho_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,\lambda t]\times\mathbb{R})})\Big).

Dividing both sides by
\lambda
and using
t\leq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\big|\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{1};\lambda t,\lambda x)-\log\widetilde{\mathsf{Z}}(\rho_{2};\lambda t,\lambda x)\big|

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\zeta^{-1}\lambda^{-1}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}+\zeta\lambda^{-1}(\lambda+\|\rho_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}+\|\rho_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})})\Big).

Taking
\zeta=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,2\lambda]\times\mathbb{R})}\in(0,1)
,
we conclude (
4.1
).
∎

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We begin with a reduction.
Recall that Proposition
3.8
states, for arbitrary fixed
\lambda,\delta>0
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\big[\mathrm{dist}_{\delta}(h_{\varepsilon,\lambda},\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}))<\delta\,\big|\,h_{\varepsilon}(2,0)+\log\sqrt{4\pi}\geq\lambda\big]=1.

Combining Propositions
3.10
and
4.1
gives

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\delta}(\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}),\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\rho_{*}))=0
.
Given these results, it suffices to prove

(4.6)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathrm{dist}_{\delta}(\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\rho_{*}),\mathsf{h}_{*})=0,\qquad\text{for any }\delta>0.

The proof of (
4.6
) starts with the Feynman-Kac formula.
Set
\varphi=\rho_{*}
in (
2.1
) and take logarithm on both sides to get

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}_{\lambda}(\rho_{*};t,x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))ds\Big)\Big]-\frac{x^{2}}{2t}-\lambda^{-1}\log\sqrt{4\pi}.

For
U_{i}=U_{i}(t,x,\lambda)>0
, we write
U_{1}\sim U_{2}
if
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1}\log(U_{1}/U_{2})=0
uniformly over
(t,x)\in[\delta,2]\times[-\delta^{-1},\delta^{-1}]
.
Our goal is to estimate the expectation in (
4.7
).
Fix a mesoscopic scale
\lambda^{a}
. Any
a\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
will do, and we fix
a=\frac{1}{4}
for the sake of concreteness.
The Brownian bridge in (
4.7
) starts at
B_{\text{b}}(0)=\lambda x
and returns to
B_{\text{b}}(\lambda t)=0
.
Consider the first time the bridge enters the region
[-\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}},\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}]
, namely
\tau:=\inf\{s\geq 0:|B_{\text{b}}(s)|\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}\}
.
Decompose the integral in (
4.7
) into
\int_{0}^{\tau}+\int_{\tau}^{\lambda t}
.
In the first integral, we have
|B_{\text{b}}(s)|>\lambda^{\frac{1}{4}}
, which gives
|\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))|\leq\exp(-\lambda^{1/4}/C)
.
This shows that the contribution of
\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))
within
s\in[0,\tau]
is negligible, so

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))\mathrm{d}s\Big)\Big]\sim\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{\tau}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))\mathrm{d}s\Big)\Big].

To proceed, condition on
\tau
in (
4.8
), namely
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}[{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}]=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}[\mathbb{E}[{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}}|\tau]]
,
and recognize the inner expectation as
\mathbb{E}[\exp(\int_{\tau}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))\mathrm{d}s)|\tau]=U(\tau,t)
, where
U(s^{\prime},t):=\mathbb{E}_{\lambda^{1/4}\to 0}[\exp(\int_{0}^{\lambda t-s^{\prime}}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))\mathrm{d}s)]
.
Fix any
\zeta\in(0,\delta)
.
By Lemma
2.1
and
2.4
,
U(s^{\prime},t)\sim\frac{1}{2}\exp(\lambda t-s^{\prime})
, uniformly over
t\in[\delta,2]
and
s^{\prime}\in[0,\lambda t-\lambda\zeta]
.
This approximation extends to
s^{\prime}\in[0,\lambda t]
.
To see why, note that when
s^{\prime}\in(\lambda t-\lambda\zeta,\lambda t]
, we have
|\int_{\tau}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))\mathrm{d}s|\leq\lambda\zeta
,
and note that
\zeta
can be taken to be arbitrarily small.
Therefore,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}\Big[\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{\lambda t}\rho_{*}(B_{\text{b}}(s))\mathrm{d}s\Big)\Big]\sim e^{\frac{\lambda t}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}[e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tau}].

Next we bound the right side of (
4.9
).
By symmetry it suffices to consider
x\geq 0
, which we assume hereafter.
Let
\mathsf{T}(s,u)
be the first hitting time of
0
of the Brownian bridge that starts from
u
at time
0
and returns to
0
at time
s
.
For fixed
u>0
,
\mathsf{T}(s,u)
is stochastically increasing in
s
.
This fact can be proven by expressing the bridge as a drifted Brownian motion via Doob’s h transform.
Recall that
\tau
is the first time
B_{\text{b}}
enters
[-\lambda^{1/4},\lambda^{1/4}]
.
After the first entrance, consider the excess amount of time it takes for
B_{\text{b}}
to hit
0
, namely
\sigma:=\inf\{s\geq 0:B_{\text{b}}(s+\tau)=0\}
.
Indeed,
\tau+\sigma=\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x)
, so
\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}[e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tau}]=\mathbb{E}[e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x)}e^{\frac{1}{2}\sigma}]
.
Conditioned on
\tau
,
\sigma
is equal in law to
\mathsf{T}(\lambda t-\tau,\lambda^{1/4})
, which is stochastically bounded above by
\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda^{1/4})
.
Using Hölder’s inequality and the stochastic bound, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x)}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}[e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tau}]\leq\big(\mathbb{E}[e^{-\frac{n+1}{2n}\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x)}]\big)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\big(\mathbb{E}[e^{\frac{n+1}{2}\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda^{1/4})}]\big)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

Given (
4.10
), we seek to estimate
\mathbb{E}[\exp(-\beta\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x))]
.
The first step is to derive the probability density function of
\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x)
.
Express the Brownian bridge
B_{\text{b}}
by a Brownian motion as
B_{\text{b}}(s)=(1-\frac{s}{\lambda t})(\lambda x-\sqrt{\lambda t}B(\frac{s}{\lambda t-s}))
;
relate
\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x)
to a hitting time of
B
;
use the known density function of the hitting time of
B
.
The result reads

(\text{density function of }\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x))(s)=(\sqrt{\lambda^{3}tx^{2}}/\sqrt{2\pi s^{3}(\lambda t-s)})\exp(-\tfrac{\lambda t-s}{2\lambda ts}(\lambda x)^{2}).

Use this density function to express
\mathbb{E}[\exp(-\beta\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x))]
as an integral, and perform a change of variables
s\mapsto\lambda ts
. We have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-\beta\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x)}]=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda x^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi ts^{3}(1-s)}}\exp\big(\lambda\,V_{\beta}(s,t,x)\big)\mathrm{d}s,

where
V_{\beta}(s,t,x):=-\beta ts-\frac{1-s}{2st}x^{2}
.
This integral can be analyzed by Laplace’s method.
Differentiating
V
in
s
, one finds that
V({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}},x)
attains its unique maximum at
s=\min\{\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\beta}\,t},1\}
, and
\partial^{2}_{s}V_{\beta}=\frac{x^{2}}{ts^{3}}
.
Using these properties in (
4.11
), it is not hard to show that

\mathbb{E}[\exp(-\beta\mathsf{T}(\lambda t,\lambda x))]\sim\exp(-\lambda tV_{\beta}(\min\{\frac{x}{\sqrt{2\beta}\,t},1\},t,x))
,
for fixed
\beta>0
and uniformly over
[t,x]\in[\delta,2]\times[0,\delta^{-1}]
.
Insert this estimate into (
4.10
); take
\lambda^{-1}\log({\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{1.8}{$\cdot$}}})
of the result; send
\lambda\to\infty
first and
n\to\infty
later.
We obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1}\log\mathbb{E}_{\lambda x\to 0}[e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tau}]=V_{1/2}(\min\{\tfrac{x}{t},1\},t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{,}l}\frac{x^{2}}{2t}-x&\text{ when }x\in[0,t],\\
-\frac{t}{2}&\text{ when }x>t,\end{array}\right.

uniformly over
[t,x]\in[\delta,2]\times[0,\delta^{-1}]
.
Inserting this into (
4.9
) and then inserting the result into (
4.7
) completes the proof of (
4.6
) and hence the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Analyzing a Seneta’s conjecture by using the Williamson transform
Edward Omey, Meitner Cadena

Abstract
Considering slowly varying functions (SVF), Seneta in 2019 conjectured the following implication, for α ≥ 1 \alpha\geq 1 , ∫ 0 x y α − 1 ​ ( 1 − F ⁡ ( y ) ) ​ 𝑑 y ​ is SVF ⟹ ∫ [ 0 , x ] y α ​ 𝑑 F ​ ( y ) ​ is SVF, as x → ∞ , \int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha-1}(1-F(y))dy\textrm{\ is SVF}\ \Longrightarrow\ \int_{[0,x]}y^{\alpha}dF(y)\textrm{\ is SVF, as $x\to\infty$,} where F ⁡ ( x ) F(x) is a cumulative distribution function on [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) . By applying the Williamson transform, an extension of this conjecture is proved. Complementary results related to this transform and particular cases of this extended conjecture are discussed.



Keywords:
regular variation, de Haan class, truncated moments, the Williamson transform

MSC code: 26A12, 60E05

A function
f(x)
is slowly varying (SVF) if, for any
t>0
,
f(tx)\big/f(x)\to 1
as
x\to\infty
.
If
F(x)
is a distribution function and
\overline{F}(x)=1-F(x)
is its tail,
recently Seneta
[
Seneta(2019)
]
conjectured the following.
Given
\alpha\geq 1
,

\int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy\ \textrm{is SVF}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\int_{[0,x]}y^{\alpha}dF(y)\ \textrm{is SVF}\textrm{, as $x\to\infty$.}

(1)

Nowadays, Kevei in
[
Kevei(2021)
]

presented an extension of such a conjecture and its proof.
Coincidentally, we have been working on such a subject as a part of other researches related to results shown by
[
Seneta(2019)
]
,
see
[
Omey and Cadena(2020a)
]
and

[
Omey and Cadena(2020b)
]
.
Unlike Kevei’s proofs, ours are based on mainly the Williamson transform.
Also, the application of this transform to
F
allows the formulation of another conjecture more.

In the following section, we present our main results.
Previously, we start introducing notation and preliminary results.

1
Main results

1.1
Some notation and transforms

In what follows,
F(x)
denotes a distribution function (d.f.) defined on
[0,\infty)
with
F(0)=0
, and
\overline{F}(x)=1-F(x)
denotes its tail.
The
\alpha
–th moment of
F(x)
is denoted by
m(\alpha)
.
It is said that
f(x)\sim g(x)
if
f(x)\big/g(x)\to 1
as
x\to\infty
.
The case
f(x)\sim 0
will be understood as
f(x)\to 0
as
x\to\infty
.
The class of regularly varying functions with index
\alpha
, denoted by
RV_{\alpha}
, consists of functions
f(x)
satisfying, for any
t>0
,
f(tx)\sim t^{\alpha}f(x)
.
If the index
\alpha=0
,
f(x)
is said slowly varying.
If
L\in RV_{0}
, then the de Haan class, denoted by
\Pi_{\beta}(L)
, consists of functions
f(x)
satisfying, for any
t>1
,
(f(tx)-f(x))\big/L(x)\sim\beta\log t
.

Let
\alpha>0
.

We define the following
transformations:

H_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha}dF(y)\textrm{,}

and

W_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy\textrm{.}

Note that Lebesgue’s
theorem on dominated convergence shows that
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)=0
. Hence, we have

H_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy-x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\textrm{.}

(2)

Then, we
get the following result.

Proposition 1

We have, for
x\geq 0
,

\overline{F}(x)=\alpha\int_{x}^{\infty}z^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(z)dz-x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)\textrm{.}

(3)

Proof.
Let us consider the integral
\int_{x}^{a}z^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(z)dz
for
a\geq x\geq 0
. We have, by using (
2
),

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{a}z^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(z)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{a}z^{-\alpha-1}\left(H_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{x}^{z}y^{\alpha}dF(y)\right)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}(x^{-\alpha}-a^{-\alpha})H_{\alpha}(x)+\int_{x}^{a}\int_{y}^{a}z^{-\alpha-1}y^{\alpha}dzdF(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}(x^{-\alpha}-a^{-\alpha})H_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{x}^{a}(y^{-\alpha}-a^{-\alpha})y^{\alpha}dF(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}(x^{-\alpha}-a^{-\alpha})H_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{1}{\alpha}(F(a)-F(x))-\frac{1}{\alpha}a^{-\alpha}(H_{\alpha}(a)-H_{\alpha}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{1}{\alpha}(F(a)-F(x))-\frac{1}{\alpha}a^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(a)\textrm{.}

Taking limits as
a\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain that
a^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(a)\rightarrow 0
and hence also that

\int_{x}^{\infty}z^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(z)dz=\frac{1}{\alpha}x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{1}{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\textrm{.}

This proves the result.

Now, we recall
the Williamson transform which is
defined by, see
[
Williamson(1956)
]
,

G_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\left(1-\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)^{\alpha}\right)dF(t)\textrm{.}

Note that

G_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}P\left(Z>\frac{t}{x}\right)dF(t)=EF(Zx)=P\left(\frac{X}{Z}\leq x\right)\textrm{,}

where
Z
is independent of
X
and
P(Z\leq x)=x^{\alpha},0\leq x\leq 1
.
Among others, this shows that
G_{\alpha}(x)
is also a d.f. (with
G_{\alpha}(0)=0
).
Also, we have the following result.

Proposition 2

We have, for
x\geq 0
,

(i)

G_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}t^{\alpha-1}F(t)dt
.

(ii)

F(x)=G_{\alpha}(x)+\frac{x}{\alpha}G_{\alpha}^{\prime}(x)
.

Proof.
(i) We have, by using partial integration,

G_{\alpha}(x)=F(x)-x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}t^{\alpha}dF(t)=\alpha x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}t^{\alpha-1}F(t)dt\textrm{.}

(ii)
Writing the previous result as
x^{\alpha}G_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\int_{0}^{x}t^{\alpha-1}F(t)dt
,
deriving this relation, and dividing it by
x^{\alpha-1}\big/\alpha
give the result claimed.

The following result provides relations among
\overline{F}(x)
,
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)
,
H_{\alpha}(x)
and
W_{\alpha}(x)
.

Proposition 3

We have, for
x\geq 0
,

(i)

\overline{F}(x)=\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)-x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)
.

(ii)

\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x^{-\alpha}W_{\alpha}(x)
.

Proof.
(i) Using
H_{\alpha}(x)
and Proposition
2
(i), we have that
G_{\alpha}(x)=F(x)-x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)
and
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)=\overline{F}(x)+x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)
.

(ii) Using the tail of
G_{\alpha}(x)
,
W_{\alpha}(x)
, and Proposition
2
(i), we find

\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}t^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(t)dt=\alpha x^{-\alpha}W_{\alpha}(x)\textrm{.}

1.2
Main relations

It follows our first main result.

Theorem 4

Let
\alpha>0
and
0\leq\theta\leq\alpha
.
We have:

(i)

If
0<\theta<\alpha
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
;

(b)

\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
;

(c)

W_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
;

(d)

\overline{F}(x)\big/\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\sim\theta\big/\alpha
;

(e)

H_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
;

(f)

x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/H_{\alpha}(x)\sim\theta\big/(\alpha-\theta)
;

(g)

x^{\alpha}\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\big/H_{\alpha}(x)\sim\alpha\big/(\alpha-\theta)
; and,

(h)

x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/W_{\alpha}(x)\sim\theta
.

(ii)

If
\theta=0
, then the statements (b), (c), (d), (e), (f), (g) and (h) are equivalent.

(iii)

If
\theta=\alpha
, then the statements (a), (b) and (c) are equivalent, and (b)
\Longrightarrow
(d).

\alpha>0
and
\theta>\alpha
.
Assume

.

(b)

\overline{W}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\alpha-\theta}

Theorem 8

Let
\alpha>0
.
Let
L(x)\in RV_{0}
.
Let
\beta,\lambda\geq 0
so that
\beta=\alpha\lambda
.
The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

x^{\alpha}\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\beta}(L)
;

(b)

x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/L(x)\sim\lambda
; and,

(c)

H_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\beta}(L)

(c) was identified in Theorem 1.1 by

.

To prove the previous theorems, we present several results which
are organized in subsections by considering relations among
\overline{F}
,
H_{\alpha}
,
W_{\alpha}
and
\overline{G}_{\alpha}
.
They are based mainly on the following well-known result, see
[
Karamata(1930)
]

and e.g. Theorem 1.2.1 by
[
de Haan(1970)
]
:

Proposition 10

Suppose
U:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is Lebesgue-summable on finite intervals. Then

\displaystyle U(x)\in RV_{\alpha}\textrm{, }\alpha>-1

iff

\displaystyle xU(x)\sim(\alpha+1)\int_{0}^{x}U(t)dt\textrm{;}

(4)

\displaystyle U(x)\in RV_{\alpha}\textrm{, }\alpha<-1

iff

\displaystyle xU(x)\sim-(\alpha+1)\int_{x}^{\infty}U(t)dt\textrm{.}

(5)

The following proposition is also used to prove some of those results.

Proposition 11

Let
\alpha>0
.
Assume that there exist
A(x)
,
B(x)
, and
C(x)
satisfying, for any
z>1
,

\frac{W(zx)-W(x)}{A(x)}\to B(z)\textrm{\hskip 10.22217ptand \hskip 10.22217pt}\frac{W(zx)-W(x)}{A(zx)}\to C(z)\textrm{,}

such that, for some
\xi
,
B(z)\big/(z^{\alpha}-1)\to\xi\big/\alpha
and
C(z)\big/(1-z^{-\alpha})\to\xi\big/\alpha
.
Then, we have

\frac{\overline{F}(x)x^{\alpha}}{A(x)}\to\xi\textrm{.}

Proof.
For
z>1
, we have
W_{\alpha}(zx)-W_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{x}^{zx}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy
. Since
\overline{F}(x)
is nonincreasing, we find that

\frac{1}{\alpha}\overline{F}(xz)t^{\alpha}(z^{\alpha}-1)\leq W_{\alpha}(zx)-W_{\alpha}(x)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)x^{\alpha}(z^{\alpha}-1)\textrm{,}

and, then, also that

\displaystyle\frac{W_{\alpha}(zx)-W_{\alpha}(x)}{A(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)}{A(x)}\frac{1}{\alpha}(z^{\alpha}-1)\textrm{,}

\displaystyle\frac{(xz)^{\alpha}\overline{F}(xz)}{A(xz)}\frac{1}{\alpha}z^{-\alpha}(z^{\alpha}-1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{W_{\alpha}(zx)-W_{\alpha}(x)}{A(zx)}\textrm{.}

Taking limits, we obtain that

\displaystyle\alpha\frac{B(z)}{z^{\alpha}-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{\overline{F}(x)x^{\alpha}}{A(x)}\textrm{,}

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{(xz)^{\alpha}\overline{F}(xz)}{A(xz)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\frac{C(z)}{1-z^{-\alpha}}\textrm{,}

or

\alpha\frac{B(z)}{z^{\alpha}-1}\leq\lim\binom{\sup}{\inf}\frac{\overline{F}(x)x^{\alpha}}{W_{\alpha}(x)}\leq\alpha\frac{C(z)}{1-z^{-\alpha}}\textrm{.}

Now, let
z\downarrow 1
to find, by hypothesis, that
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/A(x)\sim\xi
.

.

11
, but taking
\overline{W}_{\alpha}(x)

,

Lemma 13

Let
\alpha>0
.
We have:

(i)

Suppose that
0<\theta\leq\alpha
. We have
\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}(x)
iff
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
iff
W_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
. Each of the statements implies that
\overline{F}(x)\big/\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\sim\theta\big/\alpha
.

(ii)

\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{-\alpha}
iff
W_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{0}
. Each of the statements implies that
\overline{F}(x)\big/\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\sim 0
.

(iii)

If
\overline{F}(x)\big/\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\sim\lambda
,
0\leq\lambda<1
, then
\overline{G}_{\alpha}\in RV_{\alpha(\lambda-1)}
.

(iv)

Let
L(x)\in RV_{0}
. Then
x^{\alpha}\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\beta}(L)
iff
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/L(x)\sim\lambda
with
\beta=\alpha\lambda
.

Proof.
Proof of (i). Consider
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy
because of Proposition
3
(ii).
This implies that
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
iff
W_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
.
Next, assume that
\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
,
0<\theta\leq\alpha
. Consider again
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy
and note that
\alpha-1+\theta-\alpha=\theta-1>-1
. We have, by applying (
4
),
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\sim\alpha\overline{F}(x)\big/\theta
. Hence,
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
.
Also, we have that
\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
implies that

\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\big/\overline{F}(x)\sim\alpha\big/\theta
.

Now, assume that
W_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
and
0<\theta\leq\alpha
.
By applying Proposition
11
with
A(x)=W_{\alpha}(x)
,
B(x)=x^{\theta}-1
,
C(x)=1-x^{-\theta}
, and
\xi=\theta
, we
find that
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/W_{\alpha}(x)\sim\theta
and, hence,
\overline{F}\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
.

Proof of (ii).
Consider again
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha x^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy=\alpha x^{-\alpha}W_{\alpha}(x)
.
Then, it follows that
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{-\alpha}
iff
W_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{0}
.
Assume that
W_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{0}
.
By applying Proposition
11
with
A(x)=W_{\alpha}(x)
,
B(x)=0
,
C(x)=0
, and
\xi=0
, we get

x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/W_{\alpha}(x)\sim 0
and, thus,
\overline{F}(x)\big/\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\sim 0
.

Proof of (iii). Assume
\overline{F}(x)\big/\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\sim\lambda
,
0\leq\lambda<1
. By Proposition
2
, we have that
\overline{F}(x)=\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)-xG_{\alpha}^{\prime}(x)\big/\alpha
. We get

\frac{xG_{\alpha}^{\prime}(x)}{\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)}=\alpha\frac{\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)-\overline{F}(x)}{\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)}\sim\alpha(1-\lambda)\textrm{.}

Hence, by applying L’Hopital rule, we have

\frac{\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)}{\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{-1}\overline{G}_{\alpha}(y)dy}\sim\alpha(1-\lambda)\textrm{.}

Then, noticing
-\alpha(1-\lambda)-1<-1
, by applying (
5
), we have
x^{-1}\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{-\alpha(1-\lambda)-1}
.
This result is equivalent to
\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\alpha(\lambda-1)}
.

Proof of (iv). First, assume that
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/L(x)\sim\lambda
. Using
W_{\alpha}(x)
, in the proof of Proposition
11

we showed that for
z>1
, we have

Then, we find that

\displaystyle\frac{W_{\alpha}(zx)-W_{\alpha}(x)}{L(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{zx}y^{-1}\frac{y^{\alpha}\overline{F}(y)}{L(x)}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{z}y^{-1}\frac{(xy)^{\alpha}\overline{F}(xy)}{L(xy)}\frac{L(xy)}{L(x)}dy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\lambda\log z\textrm{.}

It follows that
W_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\lambda}(L)
.
Because of

(zx)^{\alpha}\overline{G}_{\alpha}(zx)-x^{\alpha}\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\left(W_{\alpha}(zx)-W_{\alpha}(x)\right)\textrm{,}

we also have
x^{\alpha}\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\beta}(L)
with
\beta=\alpha\lambda
.

For the converse, noting that
L(zx)\big/L(x)\sim 1
for any

,
by applying Proposition
11
with
A(x)=L(x)
,
B(x)=C(x)=\lambda\log x
, and

, we

\overline{F}(x)x^{\alpha}\big/L(x)\sim\lambda
.

Remark 14

\alpha>0
.

,
we have

\frac{x^{-\alpha}m(\alpha)-\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)}{\overline{F}(x)}=\alpha\frac{x^{-\alpha}\overline{W}_{\alpha}(x)}{\overline{F}(x)}=\alpha\frac{\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy}{x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)}\textrm{.}

Then, noticing that
-\theta+\alpha-1<-1
, we have that, by applying (
5
),

\frac{x^{-\alpha}m(\alpha)-\overline{G}_{\alpha}(x)}{\overline{F}(x)}=\alpha\frac{x^{-\alpha}\overline{W}_{\alpha}(x)}{\overline{F}(x)}=\alpha\frac{\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy}{x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)}\sim\frac{\alpha}{\theta-\alpha}\textrm{,}

is equivalent to
x^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{-\theta+\alpha-1}
, i.e.
\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{-\theta}
.
The claim then follows.

1.2.2
Relations between
\overline{F}(x)
and
H_{\alpha}(x)

Lemma 16

Let
\alpha>0
.
We have:

(i)

If
0<\theta<\alpha
, then
H_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
iff
\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
.

(ii)

If
0\leq\theta<\alpha
, then
H_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}

iff
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/H_{\alpha}(x)\sim\theta\big/(\alpha-\theta)
.

(iii)

Let
L(x)\in RV_{0}
. Then
H_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\lambda}(L)
iff
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/L(x)\sim\delta
with
\lambda=\alpha\delta
.

Proof.
(i) First, suppose that
H_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
where
0\leq\theta<\alpha
. In this case we have
x^{-\alpha-1}H(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha-1}
and, since
\theta-\alpha-1<-1
, (
5
) gives that

\frac{x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)}{\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(y)dy}\sim\alpha-\theta\textrm{.}

On the other hand, we have (
3
):

\overline{F}(x)=\alpha\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(y)dy-x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)\textrm{.}

(6)

It follows that

\frac{\overline{F}(x)}{x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)}\sim\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-\theta}-1=\frac{\theta}{\alpha-\theta}\textrm{.}

It follows that
\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
when
\theta>0
.

Conversely, suppose that
\overline{F}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha}
and
0<\theta<\alpha
. Using (
2
), we have

H_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\int_{0}^{x}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy-x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\textrm{,}

and, then, we find that

\frac{H_{\alpha}(x)}{x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)}\sim\frac{\alpha}{\theta}-1=\frac{\alpha-\theta}{\theta}\textrm{.}

Hence,
H_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
.

(ii) If
\theta\geq 0
, in (i) we proved that
H_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
implies that
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/H_{\alpha}(x)\sim\theta\big/(\alpha-\theta)
. Now, we consider the converse.
From (
6
), we see that

\frac{\alpha\int_{x}^{\infty}z^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(z)dz}{x^{-\alpha}H_{\alpha}(x)}\sim\frac{\theta}{\alpha-\theta}+1=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-\theta}\textrm{.}

Noting that
\theta-\alpha-1<-1
, we have, by applying (
5
),
x^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta-\alpha-1}
.
We conclude that
H_{\alpha}(x)\in RV_{\theta}
.

(iii) First, suppose that
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/L(x)\sim\delta
. Using (
2
), we have

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(tx)-H_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{x}^{xt}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy-(xt)^{\alpha}\overline{F}(xt)+x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{1}^{t}(xy)^{\alpha}\overline{F}(xy)y^{-1}dy-(xt)^{\alpha}\overline{F}(xt)+x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\textrm{.}

Then, it follows that, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\frac{H_{\alpha}(tx)-H_{\alpha}(x)}{L(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{1}^{t}\frac{(xy)^{\alpha}\overline{F}(xy)}{L(xy)}\frac{L(xy)}{L(x)}y^{-1}dy-\frac{(xt)^{\alpha}\overline{F}(xt)}{L(xt)}\frac{L(xt)}{L(x)}+\frac{x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)}{L(x)}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha\delta\log t\textrm{.}

Hence, we get that
H_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\lambda}(L)
with
\lambda=\alpha\delta
.

Conversely, suppose that
H_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\lambda}(L)
. Using (
3
) we have

\displaystyle x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-\alpha-1}H_{\alpha}(xy)dy-H_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-\alpha-1}(H_{\alpha}(xy)-H_{\alpha}(x))dy\textrm{.}

Since
H_{\alpha}(x)\in\Pi_{\lambda}(L)
, we find that (cf.
[
Geluk and de Haan(1987)
]
, Cor. 1.15)

\displaystyle\frac{x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)}{L(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-\alpha-1}\frac{H_{\alpha}(xy)-H_{\alpha}(x)}{L(x)}dy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\lambda\alpha\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-\alpha-1}\log ydy=\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\textrm{.}

We conclude that
x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\big/L(x)\sim\lambda\big/\alpha
. This proves the result.

Remark 17

If
H_{\alpha}(\infty)<\infty
, then

m(\alpha)-H_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha\int_{x}^{\infty}y^{\alpha-1}\overline{F}(y)dy+x^{\alpha}\overline{F}(x)\textrm{.}

Using (
3
), we also have

\overline{F}(x)=x^{-\alpha}(m(\alpha)-H_{\alpha}(x))-\alpha\int_{x}^{\infty}z^{-\alpha-1}(m(\alpha)-H_{\alpha}(z))dz\textrm{.}

We briefly discuss statements about the derivatives

(8)

.

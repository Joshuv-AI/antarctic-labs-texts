Abstract
. The waves (including acoustic, electromagnetic and elastic ones) propagating in the presence of a cluster of inhomogeneities undergo multiple interactions between them. When these inhomogeneities have sub-wavelength sizes, the dominating field due to the these multiple interactions is the Foldy-Lax field. This field models the interaction between the equivalent point-like scatterers, located at the centers of the small inhomogeneities, with scattering coefficients related to geometrical/material properties of each inhomogeneities, as polarization coefficients. One of the related questions left open for a long time is whether we can reconstruct this Foldy-Lax field from the scattered field measured far away from the cluster of the small inhomogeneities. This is the Foldy-Lax approximation (or Foldy-Lax paradigm). In this work, we show that this approximation is indeed valid as soon as the inhomogeneities enjoy critical scales between their sizes and contrasts. These critical scales allow them to generate resonances which can be characterized and computed. The main result here is that exciting the cluster by incident frequencies which are close to the real parts of these resonances allow us to reconstruct the Fold-Lax field from the scattered waves collected far away from the cluster itself (as the farfields). In short, we show that the Foldy-Lax approximation is valid using nearly resonating incident frequencies. This results is demonstrated by using, as small inhomogeneities, dielectric nanoparticles for the 2 2 D TM model of electromagnetic waves and bubbles for the 3 3 D acoustic waves.

The scattered field by the collection of inhomogeneities
D_{j},j=1,...,M,
is given by a linear combination of poles or dipoles centered at the centers of the inhomogeneities. We call this dominating field the Foldy-Lax field as it is of the form of the scattered field generated by point-like scatterers (or Dirac-like potentials).
This field encodes the multiples scattering effect between the small inhomogeneities.
A natural, and actually an outstanding open question, was whether from the scattered field measured away from the collection of inhomogeneities we can reconstruct the field due to multiple interaction between them.
In mathematical terms, is the approximating term, i.e. the Foldy-Lax field or its iterations (see later), dominating the error of the approximation? We call this property, the Foldy-Lax paradigm (or approximation).
There are attempts to justify this paradigm but without real success.
To our knowledge, the only published paper addressing this issue is
[
13

where acoustic waves in the harmonic regime was considered with sound-soft inhomogeneities (obstacles with Dirichlet condition).
The Foldy-Lax approximation was justified when the

In this work, we show that for general shapes and even general transmission condition the Foldy-Lax approximation is valid as soon as nearly resonating incident frequencies are used. Our message is that the main reason why we can see the multiple scattering between the inhomogeneities is the fact that we excite them with incident frequencies close to their own resonances. \displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{2}\,\frac{1}{a_{0}}\,\tau\,\sum_{m=1}^{M}\;\Phi_{k}(x,z_{m})\;\int_{D_{m}}v_{m}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle k^{2}\,\frac{1}{a_{0}}\,\tau\,\sum_{m=1}^{M}\,\int_{D_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\underset{y}{\nabla}\Phi_{k}(x,z_{m}+t(y-z_{m}))\cdot(y-z_{m})\,dt\;v_{m}(y)\,dy.

We estimate the second term on the right hand side as:

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle k^{2}\,\frac{1}{a_{0}}\,\tau\,\sum_{m=1}^{M}\,\int_{D_{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\underset{y}{\nabla}\Phi_{k}(x,z_{m}+t(y-z_{m}))\cdot(y-z_{m})\,dt\;v_{m}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\left|J_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\tau\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\underset{y}{\nabla}\Phi_{k}(x,z_{m}+t(\cdot-z_{m}))\cdot(\cdot-z_{m})dt\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}\left\|v_{m}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}.

\tau\sim\delta^{-2}\left|\log(\delta)\right|^{-1}

x

D

\left|J_{1}\right|\lesssim\left|\log(\delta)\right|^{-1}\,\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left\|v_{m}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}\leq\left|\log(\delta)\right|^{-1}\,\left\|v\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D)}\overset{(\ref{prioriest})}{=}\mathcal{O}\left(\delta\,\left|\log(\delta)\right|^{h-1}\right).

Therefore,

(3.25)

\displaystyle u^{s}(x,\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{2}\,\frac{1}{a_{0}}\,\tau\,\sum_{m=1}^{M}\,\Phi_{k}(x,z_{m})\,\int_{D_{m}}v_{m}(y)\,dy+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta\,\left|\log(\delta)\right|^{h-1}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\,\Phi_{k}(x,z_{m})\,C_{m}^{\star}\,Q_{m}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta\,\left|\log(\delta)\right|^{h-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\,\Phi_{k}(x,z)\,\,C^{\star};\tilde{Q}\rangle+\langle\,\Phi_{k}(x,z)\,\,C^{\star};\,(Q-\tilde{Q})\rangle+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta\,\left|\log(\delta)\right|^{h-1}\right).

We need to estimate the second term on the right hand side of the previous equation. To do this, we set
J_{2}:=\langle\,\Phi_{k}(x,z)\,\,C^{\star}\,;\,(Q-\tilde{Q})\rangle
. Then,

|J_{2}|\leq\|\,\Phi_{k}(x,z)\,C^{\star}\,\|\,\|Q-\tilde{Q}\|\overset{(\ref{DiffQ-Q})}{\leq}\|\,\Phi_{k}(x,z)\,\,C^{\star}\,\|\;\delta^{1-t}\;|\log(\delta)|^{h-1}.

Again, we suppose that the point
x
is away from the domain
D
, which implies the smoothness of the functions

,

J_{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{1-t}\;|\log(\delta)|^{2(h-1)}\right).

Finally,
(\ref{SK})
becomes,

(3.27)

Estimation of the scattering coefficient
C_{m}
and

We recall that:

C_{m}:=\int_{D_{m}}w(x)\,dx,

where

\left[A^{0}-\left(\lambda_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{2+h}\right)\right)\right]w=1.

We can write
C_{m}

(4.2)

C_{m}:=\int_{D_{m}}w(x)\,dx=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\langle w;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}\;\int_{D_{m}}e_{n}(x)\;dx.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\int_{D_{m}}e_{n}(x)\,dx=\int_{D_{m}}1\;e_{n}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{EquaW})}{=}

\displaystyle\int_{D_{m}}\left[A^{0}-\left(\lambda_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{2+h}\right)\right)\right](w)(x)\;e_{n}(x)\;dx

\displaystyle\langle\left[A^{0}-\left(\lambda_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{2+h}\right)\right)\right]w;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle A^{0}w;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}-\left(\lambda_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{2+h}\right)\right)\,\langle w;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}.

Using the fact that
A^{0}
is a self adjoint operator with
\left(\lambda_{n};e_{n}\right)
as an eigen-system we derive, from the previous equation, the coming relation linking the Fourier coefficient of
w
with the Fourier coefficient of function unity
1

(4.3)

\langle w;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}=\frac{\langle 1;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}}{\left(\lambda_{n}-\left(\lambda_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{2+h}\right)\right)\right)}.

Then, by combining
(\ref{wserie})
with
(\ref{coeffw})
, we obtain

(4.4)

C_{m}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{\left(\langle 1;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}\right)^{2}}{\left(\lambda_{n}-\left(\lambda_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{2+h}\right)\right)\right)}.

To see the behaviour of
\langle 1;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}
, with respect to the radius of
D_{m}
, i.e.
\delta
, we start by scaling:

\int_{D_{m}}e_{n}(x)\;dx=\delta^{3}\;\int_{B}e_{n}(z_{m}+\delta\,\xi)\;d\xi=\delta^{3}\;\int_{B_{m}}\tilde{e}_{n}(\xi)\;d\xi.

We define the following orthonormal basis:

\overline{e}_{n}=\frac{\tilde{e}_{n}}{\|\tilde{e}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}},

where, in effortless manner, we can prove that

\|\tilde{e}_{n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}=\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}
.
Synthesizing what precedes we deduce:

(4.5)

\int_{D_{m}}e_{n}(x)\;dx=\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}\;\int_{B}\overline{e}_{n}(\xi)\;d\xi.

Using the derived scales, the equation
(\ref{RMSeries})
becomes

and, by scaling the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}=\delta^{2}\,\tilde{\lambda}_{n}
and
\lambda_{n_{0}}=\delta^{2}\,\tilde{\lambda}_{n_{0}}
, we obtain

(4.6)

C_{m}=\delta\,\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{n}-(\tilde{\lambda}_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{h}))}=\delta^{1-h}\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n_{0}}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}+\delta\sum_{n\neq n_{0}}\frac{\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}}{\tilde{\lambda}_{n}-\left(\tilde{\lambda}_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{h}\right)\right)}.

The preceding formula is consistent if

\sum_{n}\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}\;<\infty.

Now, we need to show the convergence of the previous series. For this, let
\beta
such that

\beta-\underset{n}{\max}\,\,\lambda_{n}>>1

and
w_{\beta}
solution of

\left(A^{0}-\beta\right)\,w_{\beta}=1.

\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}=\left(\beta-\lambda_{n}\right)\;\frac{\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}}{\left(\beta-\lambda_{n}\right)}\leq\beta\;\frac{\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}}{\left(\beta-\lambda_{n}\right)}.

Hence, after taking the series with respect to
n
on both sides, we get

\sum_{n}\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}\,\leq\,\beta\;\sum_{n}\frac{\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}}{\left(\beta-\lambda_{n}\right)}.

In addition, using
(\ref{wbeta})
, we can derive that

\sum_{n}\frac{\left(\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right)^{2}}{\left(\beta-\lambda_{n}\right)}=-\int_{D}w_{\beta}(x)\;dx<\infty.

Which proves that
(\ref{intw})
is well defined. Finally, we deduce that

(4.8)

C_{m}:=\int_{D_{m}}w(x)\,dx=\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{1-h}\right).

To estimate the
\mathbb{L}^{2}-
norm of
w
, we start by writing its series expansion given by

\left\|w\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\left|\langle w;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}\right|^{2}\overset{(\ref{coeffw})}{=}\sum_{n}\frac{\left|\langle 1;e_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}\right|^{2}}{\left|\left(\lambda_{n}-\left(\lambda_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{2+h}\right)\right)\right)\right|^{2}}.

We use the scale of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}

, and the scale of the eigenfunction integral, given by
(\ref{scaleeigfct})
, to obtain:

\left\|w\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}=\delta^{-1}\;\sum_{n}\frac{\left|\langle 1;\overline{e}_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(B)}\right|^{2}}{\left|\left(\tilde{\lambda}_{n}-\left(\tilde{\lambda}_{n_{0}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{h}\right)\right)\right)\right|^{2}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{-1-2h}\right).

Finally, we deduce that:

(4.9)

\left\|w\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(D_{m})}=\mathcal{O}\left(\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}-h}\right).

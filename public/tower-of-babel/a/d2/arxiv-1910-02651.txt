Generalized spaces of pointwise regularity: To a general framework for the WLM
L. Loosveldt, S. Nicolay

Abstract
In this work we generalize the spaces T u p T^{p}_{u} introduced by Calderón and Zygmund using a pointwise version of conditions defining the generalized Besov spaces and give conditions binding the functions belonging to these spaces and the wavelet coefficients of such functions. Next, we propose a multifractal formalism based on the new spaces wich generalize the so-called wavelet leaders method and show that it is satisfied on a prevalent set.



Keywords
: Pointwise Regularity Spaces, Multifractal Formalisms, Wavelets

Introduction

The Hölder-regularity can be seen as a notion that fills gaps between being
n
times continuously differentiable and
n+1
times continuously differentiable. More precisely, a function
f
from
L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
belongs to the space
T^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
(with
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
p\in[1,\infty]
and
u>0
) if there exists a polynomial
P_{x_{0}}
of degree strictly less than
u
such that

r^{-u}\|f-P_{x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq C,

(1)

for
r>0
, where
B(x_{0},r)
denotes the open ball centered at
x_{0}
with radius
r
(see
[
2
]
);
T^{\infty}_{u}(x_{0})
is called a Hölder space and is usually denoted by
\Lambda^{u}(x_{0})

[
14
]
. These spaces are embedded and the Hölder exponent of
f
at
x_{0}
is defined as

h_{\infty}(x_{0}):=\sup\{u>0:f\in T^{\infty}_{u}(x_{0})\}.

(2)

The discrete wavelet transform provides a useful tool for studying the Hölder space (for more details, see
[
10
]
), since the condition on
f
at
x_{0}
can be transposed to a condition on some wavelet coefficients near
x_{0}
, the so-called wavelet leaders (see Definition
3.1
with
p=\infty
). Indeed, if a function belongs to a space
\Lambda^{u}(x_{0})
, the wavelet leaders of
x_{0}
satisfy an inequality somehow similar to (
1
). Conversely, if this condition on the wavelet leaders is met, the corresponding function belongs to a space close to
T^{\infty}_{u}(x_{0})
; more precisely, one has

\theta_{u}^{-1}(r)\|f-P_{x_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq C,

(3)

with
\theta_{u}(r)=r^{u}|\ln(r)|
. In other words,
f
belongs to
T^{\infty}_{u}(x_{0})
up to a logarithmic correction. If such a result holds, we will say that we have a quasi-characterization of the space; let us remark that such a quasi-characterization provides an exact characterization of the Hölder-regularity, i.e. of the Hölder exponent
h_{\infty}(x_{0})
.

This notion of regularity can be generalized in several ways. First, one can replace the expression
r^{-u}
appearing in (
1
) with a function
\theta_{u}(r)
satisfying some requirements, as in inequality (
3
). The space of functions satisfying (
3
) has been studied in
[
16
]
, where a quasi-characterization is obtained. One can also replace the Hölder space appearing in (
2
) with a
T^{p}_{u}
space, in order to study non-locally bounded functions. This approach has been undertaken in
[
12
]
, where generalized wavelet leaders, called
p
-leaders, are introduced. However, this definition is not a direct generalization of the usual leaders and fails to quasi-characterize the
T^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
spaces, although they still can be used to study the corresponding generalized Hölder exponent. The first part of this paper consists in combining these two points of view, by considering the spaces of functions satisfying the condition

\theta_{u}^{-1}(r)\|f-P_{x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq C.

(4)

Indeed, we consider an even larger class of spaces called spaces of generalized pointwise smoothness (see Definition
2.2
) corresponding, in some way, to a pointwise version of the generalized Besov spaces (see
[
18
]
and references therein). We obtain a quasi-characterization of such spaces by introducing a variant definition of the
p
-leaders that naturally extends the classical case where
p=\infty
.

The second part of this paper aims at providing a multifractal formalism suited for the spaces introduced here. A multifractal formalism is an empirical method that allows to estimate the quantity

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h_{p}(x_{0})=h\},

where
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension with the convention
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\emptyset)=-\infty
(see
[
6
]
for example) and
h_{p}(x_{0})
is the generalized Hölder obtained by replacing
T^{\infty}_{u}(x_{0})
with
T^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
in (
2
). Usually, one requires such a method to be valid for a large class of functions. Such a multifractal formalism was first presented in
[
22
]
in the context of the analysis of fully developed turbulence velocity data. We show here that, from the prevalence point of view, almost every function belonging to a space of generalized smoothness satisfies a multifractal formalism derived from the formalism relying on the wavelet leaders; in other words, the generalized Besov spaces provide a natural framework for supporting this theory.

This paper can be seen as a generalization of the ideas and techniques employed in
[
11
,
13
,
7
,
16
]
.

The notations used here are rather standard. Throughout this paper, we will use Euler’s notation for the derivatives, i.e.
D_{j}f
designates the derivative of
f
following the
j
-th component.

2
Generalized spaces of pointwise smoothness

2.1
Admissible sequences

Let us recall the notion of admissible sequence (see e.g.
[
15
]
and references therein).

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{j})_{j}
of real positive numbers is called admissible if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

C^{-1}\sigma_{j}\leq\sigma_{j+1}\leq C\sigma_{j},

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

If
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is such a sequence, we set

\underline{\sigma}_{j}=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{\sigma_{j+k}}{\sigma_{k}}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\sigma}_{j}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{\sigma_{j+k}}{\sigma_{k}}

and define the lower and upper Boyd indices as follows,

\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\lim_{j}\frac{\log_{2}\underline{\sigma}_{j}}{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\lim_{j}\frac{\log_{2}\overline{\sigma}_{j}}{j}.

Since
(\log\underline{\sigma}_{j})_{j}
is a subadditive sequence, such limits always exist. The following relations about such sequences are well known (see e.g.
[
15
]
). If
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is an admissible sequence, let
\varepsilon>0
; there exists a positive constant
C
such that

C^{-1}2^{j(\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})-\varepsilon)}\leq\underline{\sigma}_{j}\leq\frac{\sigma_{j+k}}{\sigma_{k}}\leq\overline{\sigma}_{j}\leq C2^{j(\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+\varepsilon)},

for any
j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
In this paper,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
will always stand for an admissible sequence and, given
u>0
, we set
\boldsymbol{u}=(2^{ju})_{j}
. Of course, we have
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{u})=\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{u})=u
.

2.2
Definition of the generalized spaces of pointwise smoothness

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
f\in L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;
f
belongs to
T_{p,q}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(x_{0})
whenever

(\sigma_{j}2^{jd/p}\sup_{|h|\leq 2^{-j}}\|\Delta_{h}^{\lfloor\overline{s}(\sigma)\rfloor+1}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{h}(x_{0},2^{-j}))})_{j}\in\ell^{q},

where, given
r>0
,

B_{h}(x_{0},r)=\{x:[x,x+(\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rfloor+1)h]\subset B(x_{0},r)\}.

It is easy to check that
T_{\infty,\infty}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(x_{0})
is the generalized Hölder space
\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(x_{0})
introduced in
[
16
]
. These spaces can also be seen as a generalization of the spaces
T^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
introduced by Calderón and Zygmund in
[
2
]
, as Corollary
2.11
will show.

Let us give an alternative definition of
T_{p,q}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(x_{0})
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
f\in L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})>0
. We have
f\in T_{p,q}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}(x_{0})
if and only if there exists a sequence of polynomials
(P_{j,x_{0}})_{j}
of degree less or equal to
\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rfloor
such that

(\sigma_{j}2^{jd/p}\|f-P_{j,x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))})_{j}\in\ell^{q}.

(5)

Proof.

The necessity of the condition being a consequence of the Whitney Theorem, let us check the sufficiency. Let
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
; for any polynomial
P
of degree less or equal to
n:=\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rfloor
, we have, given
x,h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|\Delta_{h}^{n+1}f(x)|\leq|\Delta_{h}^{n+1}\big(f(x)-P(x)\big)|\leq C_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}|f(x+kh)-P(x+kh)|,

for a constant
C_{n}
. Therefore, for
|h|\leq 2^{-j}
and
x\in B_{h}(x_{0},2^{-j})
, we get

\|\Delta_{h}^{n+1}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{h}(x_{0},2^{-j}))}\leq C_{n}(n+2)\|f-P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))},

hence the conclusion.
∎

2.3
Independence of the polynomial from the scale

Under some additional assumptions on the admissible sequence
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
, the sequence of polynomials
(P_{j,x_{0}})_{j}
appearing in inequality (
5
) can be replaced by a unique polynomial
P_{x_{0}}
independent from the scale
j
:
P_{x_{0}}=P_{j,x_{0}}
.

We first need some preliminary results. Let us first state a somehow standard result about inequalities on polynomials; we sketch a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.4
.

Given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a radius
r>0
,
p\in(0,\infty]
and a maximum degree
n
, there exist two constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
only depending on
p
such that, for any polynomial
P
of degree lower or equal to
n
,

\|D^{\alpha}P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq Cr^{-|\alpha|}\|P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))},

for any multi-index
\alpha

and

\sup_{x\in B(x_{0},r)}|P(x)|\leq C^{\prime}r^{d/p}\|P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}.

Proof.

For the first inequality, let us recall that the Markov inequality affirms that, given a convex bounded set
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, there exists a constant
C_{E,p}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, we have

\|D_{k}P\|_{L^{p}(E)}\leq C_{E,p}(n+1)^{2}\|P\|_{L^{p}(E)},

for any polynomial
P
of degree less or equal to
n
. As a consequence, given
r>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
n
and
p
such that, for any multi-index
\alpha
, we have

\|D^{\alpha}P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq Cr^{-|\alpha|}\|P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}.

That being done, using Sobolev’s inequality, we can now write

\sup_{x\in B(x_{0},r)}|P(x)|\leq C^{\prime}r^{d/p}\|P\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))},

for a constant
C^{\prime}>0
which only depends on
n
and
p
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{-1})>m
and
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
with
q\in[1,\infty]
; there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
such that

\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{j}2^{jm}\sigma_{j}\leq\xi_{J}2^{Jm}\sigma_{J},

for all
J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\delta,\delta^{\prime}>0
be such that
-2\delta^{\prime}>m+\overline{s}(\sigma)+\delta
; given
J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have, using Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{j}2^{jm}\sigma_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{j}2^{(j-J)(m+\overline{s}(\sigma)+\delta)}2^{Jm}\sigma_{J}

\displaystyle\leq C(\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}(\varepsilon_{j}2^{-\delta^{\prime}(j-J)})^{q})^{1/q}(\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}2^{-p\delta^{\prime}(j-J)})^{1/p}2^{Jm}\sigma_{J},

where
p
is the conjugate exponent of
q
(with the usual modification if one of the indices is
\infty
). It remains to check that the sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}
defined by

\xi_{j}=C(\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}(\varepsilon_{j}2^{-\delta^{\prime}(j-J)})^{q})^{1/q}

belongs to
\ell^{q}
, which is easy.
∎

In the same way, we can get the following result.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{-1})<m
and
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
with
q\in[1,\infty]
; there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{J}\varepsilon_{j}2^{jm}\sigma_{j}\leq\xi_{J}2^{Jm}\sigma_{J},

for all
J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Lemma
2.5
generalizes the relation
\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}\leq C\sigma_{J}
, satisfied whenever
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{-1})>0
, while Lemma
2.6
should be compared with
\sum_{j=0}^{J}2^{jm}\sigma_{j}\leq C2^{Jm}\sigma_{J}
, holding for
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{-1})<m
(
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
) (see e.g.
[
15
]
for more details).

The main theorem of this section relies on the following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
f\in L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that
0\leq n:=\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rfloor<\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. If
f
belongs to
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}(x_{0})
, the sequence of polynomials
(P_{j,x_{0}})_{j}
satisfying (
5
) is such that, given a multi-index
\alpha
for which
|\alpha|\leq n
, there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
satisfying

2^{-|\alpha|j}\sigma_{j}|D^{\alpha}(P_{j,x_{0}}-P_{k,x_{0}})(x_{0})|\leq\xi_{j},

whenever
j<k
.

In particular, under the same hypothesis, the sequence
(D^{\alpha}P_{j,x_{0}}(x_{0}))_{j}
is Cauchy and its limit does not depend on the chosen sequence of polynomials satisfying (
5
).

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
be such that

\sigma_{j}2^{jd/p}\|f-P_{j,x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))}\leq\varepsilon_{j},

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Given a multi-index
\alpha
satisfying the hypothesis and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we know that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|D^{\alpha}(P_{j,x_{0}}-P_{j+1,x_{0}})\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-(j+1)}))}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{|\alpha|(j+1)}\|P_{j,x_{0}}-P_{j+1,x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-(j+1)}))}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{|\alpha|(j+1)}\|P_{j,x_{0}}-f\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-(j+1)}))}+\|f-P_{j+1,x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-(j+1)}))}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{|\alpha|(j+1)}(\varepsilon_{j}2^{jd/p}\sigma_{j}^{-1}+\varepsilon_{j+1}2^{(j+1)d/p}\sigma_{j+1}^{-1}),

which implies, from what we have obtained so far,

|D^{\alpha}(P_{j,x_{0}}-P_{j+1,x_{0}})(x_{0})|\leq C^{\prime}(\varepsilon_{j}+\varepsilon_{j+1})2^{|\alpha|j}\sigma_{j}^{-1}.

For
j<k
, Lemma
2.5
then implies

|D^{\alpha}(P_{j,x_{0}}-P_{k,x_{0}})(x_{0})|\leq\xi_{j}2^{|\alpha|j}\sigma_{j}^{-1},

for the right sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
.

It remains to show that the limit
\mathscrsfs{D}^{\alpha}f(x_{0})
of the sequence
(D^{\alpha}P_{j,x_{0}}(x_{0}))_{j}
is independent of the peculiar choice of the sequence
(D^{\alpha}P_{j,x_{0}}(x_{0}))_{j}
; let
(Q_{j,x_{0}})_{j}
be another sequence of polynomials satisfying (
5
). With the same reasoning as before, we get

|D^{\alpha}(P_{j,x_{0}}-Q_{j,x_{0}})(x_{0})|\leq C2^{|\alpha|j}\sigma_{j}^{-1},

for
j
large enough, which is sufficient to assert that

|D^{\alpha}Q_{j,x_{0}}(x_{0})-\mathscrsfs{D}^{\alpha}f(x_{0})|

tends to zero as
j
tends to infinity.
∎

We are now able to show the existence of the unique polynomial
P_{x_{0}}
introduced in the beginning of this section.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
f\in L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that
0\leq n:=\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rfloor<\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
. The following assertions are equivalent:

•

f
belongs to
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}(x_{0})
,

•

there exists a unique polynomial
P_{x_{0}}
of degree less or equal to
n
such that

(\sigma_{j}2^{jd/p}\|f-P_{x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))})_{j}\in\ell^{q}.

(6)

Proof.

We need to prove that the first assertion implies the second one. As
f
belongs to
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}(x_{0})
, there exists a sequence of polynomials
(P_{j,x_{0}})_{j}
of degree less or equal to
n
such that

(\sigma_{j}2^{jd/p}\|f-P_{j,x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))})_{j}\in\ell^{q}.

Given a multi-index
\alpha
satisfying
|\alpha|\leq n
, let us set

\mathscrsfs{D}^{\alpha}f(x_{0}):=\lim_{j}D^{\alpha}P_{j,x_{0}}(x_{0})

and define the polynomial

P_{x_{0}}:x\mapsto\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\mathscrsfs{D}^{\alpha}f(x_{0})\,\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}}{|\alpha|!}.

(7)

One directly gets

\|P_{j,x_{0}}-P_{x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}|D^{\alpha}P_{j,x_{0}}(x_{0})-\mathscrsfs{D}^{\alpha}f(x_{0})|\,2^{-j(|\alpha|+d/p)}.

That being said, we know from the previous lemma that, given
\alpha
, there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(\alpha)}\in\ell^{q}
such that

|D^{\alpha}P_{j,x_{0}}(x_{0})-\mathscrsfs{D}^{\alpha}f(x_{0})|\leq\xi^{(\alpha)}_{j}2^{|\alpha|j}\sigma_{j}^{-1}.

We thus have

(\sigma_{j}2^{jd/p}\|P_{j,x_{0}}-P_{x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))})_{j}\in\ell^{q},

which proves the first part of the theorem.

Concerning the uniqueness of the polynomial, the idea of the proof is the same as the one given in
[
2
]
for the spaces
T^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
. Let
P
and
Q
be two polynomials satisfying a relation of type (
6
); one directly gets
P(x_{0})=Q(x_{0})
. That being said, let us define

L:=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}(\cdot-x_{0})^{\alpha},

where
m
is the lowest degree of
P-Q
, with

c_{\alpha}:=\frac{D^{\alpha}(P-Q)(x_{0})}{|\alpha|!}.

If
m<\sup\{l\in\mathbb{Z}:l<\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\}
, one can write

\|L\|_{L^{1}(B(x_{0},1))}\leq C(2^{-mj}\sigma_{j}^{-1}+2^{-j}),

for a constant
C
, which means
L=0
. For
m=\sup\{l\in\mathbb{Z}:l<\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\}
, we simply get
\|L\|_{L^{1}(B(x_{0},1))}\leq C2^{-mj}\sigma_{j}^{-1}
, which implies
L=P-Q=0
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

In the previous result, if
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is the usual sequence
\boldsymbol{u}
with
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it is easy to check that the polynomial
P_{x_{0}}
is unique if one requires its degree to be strictly smaller than
n
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Given
p\in[1,\infty]
,
u>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
T^{\boldsymbol{u}}_{p,\infty}(x_{0})=T^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
, where
T^{p}_{u}(x_{0})
denotes the class of functions
f\in L^{p}
such that there exists a polynomial
P_{x_{0}}
of degree strictly less than
u
with the property that

r^{-d/p}\|f-P_{x_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},r))}\leq Cr^{u},

for a constant
C>0
.

3
Wavelet criteria

Various function spaces can be characterized by wavelets. We combine here the approaches adopted for the classical
T^{p}_{u}
spaces, the generalized Besov spaces and the generalized pointwise Hölder spaces to obtain a “nearly” characterization of the spaces
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}
.

3.1
Definitions

Let us briefly recall some definitions and notations about wavelets (for more precisions, see e.g.
[
5
,
20
,
19
]
). Under some general assumptions, there exist a function
\varphi
and
2^{d}-1

functions
(\psi^{(i)})_{1\leq i<2^{d}}
, called wavelets, such that

\{\varphi(x-k):k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}\cup\{\psi^{(i)}(2^{j}x-k):1\leq i<2^{n},k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

form an orthogonal basis of
L^{2}
. Any function
f\in L^{2}
can be decomposed as follows,

f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}C_{k}\varphi(x-k)+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{1\leq i<2^{d}}c^{(i)}_{j,k}\psi^{(i)}(2^{j}x-k),

where

c^{(i)}_{j,k}=2^{nj}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\psi^{(i)}(2^{j}x-k)\,dx

and

C_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\varphi(x-k)\,dx.

(8)

Let us remark that we do not choose the
L^{2}
normalization for the wavelets, but rather an
L^{\infty}
normalization, which is better fitted to the study of the Hölderian regularity.

Let
\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}
denote the dyadic cube

\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}:=\frac{i}{2^{j+1}}+\frac{k}{2^{j}}+[0,\frac{1}{2^{j+1}})^{d}.

In the sequel, we will often omit any reference to the indices
i
,
j
and
k
for such cubes by writing
\lambda=\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}
. We will also index the wavelet coefficients of a function
f
with the dyadic cubes
\lambda
so that
c_{\lambda}
will refer to the quantity
c^{(i)}_{j,k}
. The notation
\Lambda_{j}
will stand for the set of dyadic cubes
\lambda
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with side length
2^{-j}
and the unique dyadic cube from
\Lambda_{j}
containing the point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
will be denoted
\lambda_{j}(x_{0})
. The set of the dyadic cubes is
\Lambda:=\cup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\Lambda_{j}
. Two dyadic cubes
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
are adjacent if there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j}
and
\text{\rm dist}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=0
. The set of the
3^{d}
dyadic cubes adjacent to
\lambda
will be denoted by
3\lambda
.

As for the wavelet-based study of the pointwise Hölder spaces, we will work with wavelet leaders
[
11
]
. However, as we work here with
L^{p}
norms, we need to introduce a generalized version.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a dyadic cube
\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}
at scale
j
, the
p
-wavelet leader of
\lambda
(
p\in[1,\infty]
) is defined by

d^{p}_{\lambda}=\sup_{j^{\prime}\geq j}(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}},\lambda^{\prime}\subset\lambda}(2^{(j-j^{\prime})d/p}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{p})^{1/p}.

Given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set

d^{p}_{j}(x_{0})=\sup_{\lambda\in 3\lambda_{j}(x_{0})}d^{p}_{\lambda}.

Remark 3.2
.

The definition of the wavelet leaders given here is different from the one presented in
[
17
]
. The quantities introduced here are easier to work with and naturally generalize the usual wavelet leaders
d_{j}(x_{0})
introduced in
[
11
]
, since we have
d_{j}(x_{0})=d^{\infty}_{j}(x_{0})
.

We will need the following definition (see
[
21
]
), ensuring a minimum regularity condition for a function.

Definition 3.3
.

Given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a function
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
belongs to the Xu space
\dot{X}^{u}_{p,q}(x_{0})
(
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
) if there exists a constant
C_{*}>0
such that

(\sum_{|k-2^{j}x_{0}|<C_{*}2^{j}}(2^{(u-d/p)j}|c_{\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}}|)^{p})^{1/p}\in\ell^{q}.

3.2
Characterization using compactly supported wavelets

In this section, we consider a compactly supported wavelet basis of regularity
r>\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{-1})
; such wavelets are considered in
[
4
]
. In this context,
j_{0}
is a natural number such that the support of each wavelet is contained in
B(0,2^{j_{0}})
.

Let us first give a necessary condition for a function to belong to
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}(x_{0})
.

Theorem 3.4
.

If
f
belongs to the space
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}(x_{0})
, then

(\sigma_{j}d_{j}^{p}(x_{0}))_{j}\in\ell^{q}.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
and
(P_{j})_{j}
be a sequence of polynomial of degree less or equal to
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
such that

\sigma_{j}2^{jd/p}\|f-P_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))}\leq\varepsilon_{j},

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let us set choose
j_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
2\sqrt{d}+2^{j_{0}}\leq 2^{j_{1}}
and fix
n\geq j_{1}
. For
\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}\subset 3\lambda_{n}(x_{0})
, we have

|\frac{k}{2^{j}}-x_{0}|\leq 2\sqrt{d}2^{-n}.

By setting

\Lambda_{j,n}:=\{\lambda_{j,k}^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{j}:|k-2^{j}x_{0}|\leq 2\sqrt{d}2^{j-n}\},

for
\lambda\in 3\lambda_{n}(x_{0})
, we can write

\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j},\lambda^{\prime}\subset\lambda}2^{(n-j)d}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p}\leq\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}2^{(n-j)d}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p},

whenever
p\not=\infty
. Let us set

s_{n,j}:=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p}

and define

g_{n,j}:=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p-1}\text{\rm sign}(c_{\lambda^{\prime}})\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}.

One easily check the the support of
g_{n,j}
is contained in
B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n})
and

s_{n,j}=2^{jd}\langle f,g_{n,j}\rangle=2^{jd}\int_{B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n})}(f(x)-P_{n-j_{1}}(x))\overline{g_{n,j}(x)}\,dx,

so that, if we denote by
q
the conjugate exponent of
p
,

s_{n,j}\leq 2^{jd}\|f-P_{n-j_{1}}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n}))}\,\|g_{n,j}\|_{L^{q}}.

To estimate
\|g_{n,j}\|_{L^{q}}
, let us remark that there exists a constant
C_{*}>0
that does not depend on
\lambda
nor the scale
j
such that the cardinal of

\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j}:\supp(\psi_{\lambda})\cap\supp(\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}})\not=\emptyset\}

is bounded by
C_{*}
. Therefore, given
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we can choose a partition
E_{1},\ldots,E_{C_{*}}
of
\Lambda_{j}
such that
\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}\in E_{m}
(
1\leq m\leq C_{*}
) and

\supp(\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}})\cap\supp(\psi_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}})\not=\emptyset

implies
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime\prime}
. For
p\not=1
, we easily get

|g_{n,j}|^{q}\leq C_{*}^{q}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p}|\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{q}

and thus

\|g_{n,j}\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{*}2^{-jd/q}s_{n,j}^{1/q}\max_{1\leq i<2^{d}}\|\psi^{(i)}\|_{L^{q}}.

(9)

If
p=1
, one easily checks that one has

\|g_{n,j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{*}2^{-jd/q}\max_{1\leq i<2^{d}}\|\psi^{(i)}\|_{L^{\infty}},

so that (
9
) is still satisfied in this case.

That being done, since we have

s_{n,j}^{1/p}\leq C\varepsilon_{n-j_{1}}2^{(j-n)d/p}\sigma_{n}^{-1},

for a constant
C>0
, we get

\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j},\lambda^{\prime}\subset\lambda}2^{(n-j)d}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p}\leq 2^{(n-j)d}s_{n,j}\leq C\varepsilon_{n-j_{1}}^{p}\sigma_{n}^{-p},

which is sufficient to conclude in the case
p\not=\infty
.

Finally, let us consider the case
p=\infty
. Indeed, the conclusion is straightforward since, given
\lambda\subset 3\lambda_{n}(x_{0})
, one easily check that, using an analogous reasoning, we can write

|c_{\lambda}|\leq C\varepsilon_{n-j_{1}}\sigma_{n},

for a constant
C>0
.
∎

For the sufficient condition, we need the following definition.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f
be a function from
L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
; if
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is an admissible sequence such that
2^{-jd/p}\sigma_{j}^{-1}
tends to
0
as
j
tends to
\infty
, we says that
f
belongs to
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q,\log}(x_{0})
if there exists
J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for which

(\frac{2^{jd/p}\sigma_{j}}{\log_{2}(2^{-jd/p}\sigma_{j}^{-1})}\sup_{|h|\leq 2^{-j}}\|\Delta_{h}^{\lfloor\overline{s}(\sigma^{-1})\rfloor+1}f\|_{L^{p}(B_{h}(x_{0},2^{-j}))})_{j\geq J}\in\ell^{q}.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f
be a function from
L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
; let also
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that
2^{-jd/p}\sigma_{j}^{-1}
tends to
0
as
j
tends to
\infty
and
\underline{\sigma}_{1}>2^{-d/p}
. If
f
belongs to
\dot{X}^{\eta}_{p,q}(x_{0})
for some
\eta>0
, then

(\sigma_{j}d_{j}^{p}(x_{0}))_{j}\in\ell^{q}

implies
f\in T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q,\log}(x_{0})
.

Proof.

Let us first suppose that
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\geq 0
and set
n:=\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rfloor
. We need to define some quantities. First, choose
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
k/2^{j}\in B(x,r)
implies
\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}\subset B(x,2^{m}r)
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
r\geq 2^{-j}
. Let also
m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be such that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
B(x,2^{-j})
is included in some dyadic cube of side length
2^{m^{\prime}-j}
and define
J_{0}:=j_{0}+m+m^{\prime}
. Let
C_{*}>0
be such that

(\sum_{|k-2^{j}x_{0}|\leq C_{*}2^{j}}(2^{\eta-d/p)j}|c_{\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}}|)^{p})^{1/p}\in\ell^{q}

and choose a number
J_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for which we have
(1+2^{j_{0}})\leq C_{*}2^{J_{1}}
. We also need a sequence
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
satisfying
\sigma_{j}d_{j}^{p}(x_{0})\leq\varepsilon_{j}
, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Finally, given
J\geq\max\{J_{0},J_{1}\}
, define

P_{J}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}(\frac{(\cdot-x_{0})^{\alpha}}{|\alpha|!}\sum_{j=-1}^{J}D^{\alpha}f_{j}(x_{0})),

where

f_{-1}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}C_{k}\varphi_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{j}:=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}c_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda},

for
j\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle 2^{Jd/p}\|f-P_{J}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-1}^{J}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\frac{(\cdot-x_{0})^{\alpha}}{|\alpha|!}D^{\alpha}f_{j}(x_{0})\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

(10)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}.

(11)

Let us fix
y\in B(x_{0},2^{-J})
and
|\alpha|=n+1
. We will first consider the case
p\not=\infty
. We have
D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}}(y)\not=0
only if
k/2^{j}
belongs to
B(y,2^{j_{0}-j})
; for
J_{0}\leq j\leq J
, we have

\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}\subset B(y,2^{m-j-j_{0}})\subset\lambda_{j-J_{0}}(x_{0}),

so that we can write, using the same reasoning as in the previous proof,

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}f_{j}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq C2^{jp(n+1)}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}|c_{\lambda}|^{p}|D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{jp(n+1)}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},\lambda\subset\lambda_{j-J_{0}}(x_{0})}|c_{\lambda}|^{p}|D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}(y)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{jp(n+1)}\varepsilon_{j-J_{0}}^{p}\sigma_{j}^{-p},

since
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is an admissible sequence. Moreover, as the wavelet coefficients are finite and there exists a constant
C_{d}
, which only depends on
d
, such that

\#\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:k\in B(y,2^{j_{0}})\}\leq C_{d},\quad\#\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:k/2^{j}\in B(y,2^{j_{0}-j})\}\leq C_{d},

we also have

|D^{\alpha}f_{j}(y)|^{p}\leq C2^{jp(n+1)}\sigma_{j}^{-p},

for all
j\in\{-1,\ldots,J_{0}-1\}
. As a consequence, we can write, for any
j\in\{-1,\ldots,J\}
,

\|f_{j}-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\frac{(\cdot-x_{0})^{\alpha}}{|\alpha|!}D^{\alpha}f_{j}(x_{0})\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}\leq\theta_{j}2^{-J(n+1+d/p)}2^{j(n+1)}\sigma_{j}^{-1},

for some sequence
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\ell^{q}
. A similar reasoning gives the same inequality for
p=\infty
. Now, since
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})<n+1
, (
10
) is upperbounded by

C^{\prime}2^{-J(n+1)}\sum_{j=-1}^{J}\theta_{j}2^{j(n+1)}\sigma_{j}^{-1}\leq C^{\prime}\xi_{J}\sigma_{J}^{-1},

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
, where the sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is given by Lemma
2.5
.

For the second term, let us fix
j\geq J+1
and
p\not=\infty
to define

\Lambda_{j,J}:=\{\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}\in\Lambda_{j}:B(k/2^{j},2^{j_{0}}/2^{j})\cap B(x_{0},2^{-J})\not=\emptyset\}.

By proceeding as before for
x\in B(x_{0},2^{-J})
, we get

\displaystyle\|f_{j}(x)\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}^{p}\leq C\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,J}}2^{-dj}|c_{\lambda}|^{p},

(12)

for some constant
C
, which gives

2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}(x)\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}\leq C\varepsilon_{J-J_{0}}\sigma_{J}^{-1}.

Moreover, since the coefficient
c_{\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}}
does not vanish in the sum (
12
) only if the condition
|k-2^{j}x_{0}|\leq C_{*}2^{j}
is satisfied, we also have

\|f_{j}(x)\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}^{p}\leq\delta_{j}^{p}2^{-\eta pj},

for a sequence
\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\ell^{q}
, as
f
belongs to the space
\dot{X}^{\eta}_{p,q}(x_{0})
. Let us obtain upperbounds when
p=\infty
; for
x\in B(x_{0},2^{-J})
,
k/2^{j}\in B(x,2^{j_{0}-j})
implies
\lambda^{(i)}_{j,k}\subset\lambda_{J-j_{0}}(x_{0})
, so that we have
|c_{\lambda^{(k)}_{j,l}}|\leq C\varepsilon_{J-J_{0}}\sigma_{N}
. The same reasoning as before leads to

\|f_{j}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}\leq C\delta_{j}2^{-\eta j}.

Let us now set
j_{*}(J):=\lceil|\log_{2}(2^{-Jd/p}\sigma_{J}^{-1})|/\eta\rceil
and choose
\eta
small enough in order to insure that we have
\log_{2}(2^{d/p}\underline{\sigma}_{1})/\eta>1
. With such a definition, we have
j_{*}(J)=j_{*}(J^{\prime})
if and only if
J=J^{\prime}
and we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=J+1}^{j_{*}(J)}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}+\sum_{j=j_{*}(J)+1}^{\infty}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=J+1}^{j_{*}(J)}\varepsilon_{J-J_{0}}\sigma_{J}^{-1}+C2^{Jd/p}\sum_{j=j_{*}(J)+1}^{\infty}\delta_{j}2^{-\eta j}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon_{J-J_{0}}+\xi_{j_{1}(J)})\,|\log_{2}(2^{-Jd/p}\sigma_{J}^{-1})|\sigma_{J}^{-1},

for
J
large enough, where the sequence
(\xi_{j_{*}(J)})_{J}
belongs to
\ell^{q}
.

It only remains to consider the situation where
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})<0
. In this case, let us set
P_{J}=0
whenever
J\geq\max\{J_{0},J_{1}\}
; once again, there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
such that

|f_{j}(y)|\leq\xi_{j}\sigma_{J}^{-1},

for
y\in B(x_{0},2^{-J})
, any
J\geq\max\{J_{0},J_{1}\}
and any
j\in\{-1,\ldots,J\}
. As done previously, we gets

\displaystyle 2^{Jd/p}\|f-P_{J}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=-1}^{J}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}+\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{J}|\log_{2}(2^{-Jd/p}\sigma_{J}^{-1})|\sigma_{J}^{-1},

with
\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\ell^{q}
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

It is well known that Theorem
3.4
has no converse: the “logarithmic correction” appearing in Theorem
3.6
is necessary in the classical case (see e.g.
[
11
]
).

3.3
Characterization using wavelets in the Schwarz class

In practise, compactly supported wavelets are used most often; however, for theoretical applications, in can be handy to have similar results concerning wavelets in the Schwarz class
[
20
]
. We will thus consider such wavelets in this section.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that either
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})>-d/p
,
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})<0
or
0\leq n:=\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rfloor<\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
; if
f\in L^{p}
belongs to
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}(x_{0})
, then we have

(\sigma_{j}2^{j(d-u)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x_{0},2^{-j})}\frac{|f(x)-P(x)|}{|x_{0}-x|^{u}}\,dx)_{j}\in\ell^{q},

for any
u>\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+d
, where
P
is the polynomial given by Theorem
2.9
.

Proof.

Let us set
R:=f-P
; without loss of generality, we can assume
x_{0}=0
. Let us define, for
r>0
,

\varphi(r):=\int_{B(0,r)}|R(x)|\,dx;

we know that there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
such that

\varphi(2^{-j})\leq 2^{-jd}\varepsilon_{j}\sigma_{j}^{-1},

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Moreover, for
r\geq 1
, we have

\varphi(r)\leq Cr^{d(1-1/p)}\|f\|_{L^{p}}+cr^{n+d}\leq Cr^{d+\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}.

Using spherical coordinates, we can write

\varphi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\psi(\rho)\,d\rho,

with

\psi(\rho):=\rho^{d-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{\pi}|R(x(\rho,\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d-1}))|\,d\Omega_{d},

where
d\Omega_{d}
stands for

\sin^{d-2}(\theta_{1})\cdots\sin(\theta_{d-2})\,d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{d-1}.

Since, for all
r>0
, we have

\frac{\varphi(r)}{r^{u}}-\varphi(2^{-j})2^{ju}=\int_{B(0,2^{-j})}\frac{|R(x)|}{|x|^{u}}\,dx-\int_{2^{-j}}^{r}\frac{u}{\rho^{u+1}}\,\varphi(\rho)\,d\rho,

we get

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r)\setminus B(0,2^{-j})}\frac{|R(x)|}{|x|^{u}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varphi(r)}{r^{u}}+\int_{1}^{r}\frac{u}{\rho^{u+1}}\,\varphi(\rho)\,d\rho+\sum_{k=1}^{j}\int_{2^{-k}}^{2^{1-k}}\frac{u}{\rho^{u+1}}\,\varphi(\rho)\,d\rho.

Since

\varphi(r)/r^{u}\leq C\leq C2^{j(u-d)}2^{-\delta j}\sigma_{j}^{-1},

where
\delta>0
has been chosen such that
\delta<u-d-\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
, we can write

\int_{1}^{r}\frac{u}{\rho^{u+1}}\,\varphi(\rho)\,d\rho\leq C2^{j(u-d)}2^{-j\delta}\sigma_{j}^{-1}.

Finally, as
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is admissible and
u>\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+d
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{j}\int_{2^{-k}}^{2^{1-k}}\frac{u}{\rho^{u+1}}\,\varphi(\rho)\,d\rho\leq 2^{j(u-d)}\xi_{j}\sigma_{j}^{-1},

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
is given by Lemma
2.5
. Putting all these informations together, we can claim that there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\ell^{q}
such that the inequality

\int_{B(0,r)\setminus B(0,2^{-j})}\frac{|R(x)|}{|x|^{u}}\,dx\leq 2^{j(u-d)}\theta_{j}\sigma_{j}^{-1}

holds for
r\geq 1
.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that either
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})>-d/p
,
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})<0
or
0\leq\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\rfloor<\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
; if
f\in L^{p}
belongs to
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}(x_{0})
, then we have

(\sigma_{j}d_{j}^{p}(x_{0}))_{j}\in\ell^{q}.

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
be such that

\sigma_{j}2^{jd/p}\|f-P_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))}\leq\varepsilon_{j},

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, choose
j_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
2\sqrt{d}\leq 2^{j_{1}}
and fix
n\geq j_{1}+1
.

Let us first suppose that
p\in(1,\infty)
; define

\Lambda_{j,n}:=\{\lambda_{j,k}^{(l)}\in\Lambda_{j}:|k-2^{j}x_{0}|\leq\sqrt{d}2^{j+1-n}\},

so that
\lambda\in 3\lambda_{n}(x_{0})
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}
implies
\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,n}
,

s_{j,n}:=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p}

and

g_{j,n}:=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p-1}\text{\rm sign}(c_{\lambda^{\prime}})\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}.

We have

\displaystyle s_{j,n}

\displaystyle=2^{jd}\int_{B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}(f(x)-P(x))\overline{g_{j,n}(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}(f(x)-P(x))\overline{g_{j,n}(x)}\,dx.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we can write

\displaystyle 2^{jd}\int_{B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}(f(x)-P(x))\overline{g_{j,n}(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{n-j_{1}-1}2^{jd}2^{-nd/p}\|g_{j,n}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\sigma_{n}^{-1},

where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
, with

\|g_{j,n}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\leq C2^{-jd/p^{\prime}}s_{j,n}^{1/p^{\prime}},

for a constant
C>0
, thanks to the wavelet characterization of the
L^{p}
spaces (see e.g.
[
20
]
). Now, for all
u>d+\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
, it is easy to check, thanks to the fast decay of the wavelets, that there exists a constant
C_{d,u}>0
such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})
,

(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}|\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{p})^{1/p}\leq C_{d,u}/(2^{j}|x-x_{0}|)^{u}.

Using the previous lemma, we can claim that there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\ell^{q}
for which

2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}(f(x)-P(x))\overline{g_{j,n}(x)}\,dx\leq\theta_{n}s_{j,n}^{1/p^{\prime}}2^{(j-n)d/p}\sigma_{n}^{-1}.

As a consequence, there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
such that

s_{j,n}^{1/p}\leq\xi_{n}2^{(j-n)d/p}\sigma_{n}^{-1}.

If
p=1
, keeping the same notations, we have

\displaystyle s_{j,n}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{jd}\int_{B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}|f(x)-P(x)|\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}|\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}|\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}|f(x)-P(x)|\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j,n}}|\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}|\,dx.

To bound the first integral, remark that
\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j}}|\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}}|
is bounded and

\|f-P\|_{L^{1}(B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1}))}\leq C\varepsilon_{n-j_{1}-1}2^{-nd}\sigma_{n}^{-1}.

The second integral can be treated as in the case
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Finally, suppose that
p=\infty
, fix
j\geq n
and suppose that
\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}
satisfies
\lambda\in 3\lambda_{n}(x_{0})
. We have

\displaystyle|c_{\lambda}|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{jd}\int_{B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}|f(x)-P(x)|\,|\psi_{\lambda}|\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}|f(x)-P(x)|\,|\psi_{\lambda}|\,dx.

Once again, it is sufficient to bound the first integral, which is easy since we have

2^{jd}\int_{B(x_{0},2^{j_{1}-n+1})}|f(x)-P(x)|\,dx\leq C\varepsilon_{n-j_{1}-1}\sigma_{n}^{-1},

for some constant
C>0
.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
f\in L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence such that
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})>-d/p
and
\underline{\sigma}_{1}>2^{-d/p}
. If there exists
\eta>0
such that
f\in B^{\eta}_{p,q}
, then

(\sigma_{j}d_{j}^{p}(x_{0}))_{j}\in\ell^{q}

implies
f\in T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q,\log}(x_{0})
.

Proof.

Let us use the definitions of
n
,
m
,
J_{0}
,
J_{1}
,
J
,
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
,
P_{J}
and
f_{j}
introduced in the proof of Theorem
3.6
. Of course, we have

\displaystyle 2^{Jd/p}\|f-P_{J}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-1}^{J}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\frac{(\cdot-x_{0})^{\alpha}}{|\alpha|!}\,D^{\alpha}f_{j}(x_{0})\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

(13)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}.

(14)

Let us first consider the term (
13
) of the last bound. Let
\alpha
be a multi-index such that
|\alpha|=n+1
; from Taylor’s formula, we need to bound
|D^{\alpha}f_{j}(x)|
for
x\in B(x_{0},2^{-J})
. Assume now that
j
is such that
j-\lceil j/2\rceil\geq J_{0}
and define

\Lambda_{j,0}:=\{\lambda_{j,k}^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{j}:|2^{j}x_{0}-k|\leq 1\},

for
l
such that
1\leq l\leq\lceil j/2\rceil
,

\Lambda_{j,l}:=\{\lambda_{j,k}^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{j}:2^{l-1}<|2^{j}x_{0}-k|\leq 2^{l}\}

and

\Lambda_{j,*}:=\{\lambda_{j,k}^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{j}:|2^{j}x_{0}-k|\geq 2^{\lceil j/2\rceil}\}.

A sum over
\Lambda_{j}
can be decomposed into a sum over the sets
\Lambda_{j,l}
(with
l\in\{0,\ldots,\lceil j/2\rceil\})
and
\Lambda_{j,*}
. For
1\leq l\leq\lceil j/2\rceil
, we have, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,l}}|c_{\lambda}|\,|D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,l}}|c_{\lambda}|^{p})^{1/p}(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,l}}|D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}(y)|^{p^{\prime}})^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon_{j-l-J_{0}}2^{ld/p}\sigma_{j-l}^{-1})(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,l}}(\frac{1}{(1+|2^{j}x-k|)^{2^{d+1}+u+d/p})^{p^{\prime}}})^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{j-l-J_{0}}2^{-ul}\sigma_{j-l}^{-1},

where
u
is such that
u>\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})
and
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
p
; for
l=0
, we can write

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,0}}|c_{\lambda}|\,|D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}(x)|\leq\varepsilon_{j-J-J_{0}}\sigma_{j}^{-1}.

For the last set, we get

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,*}}|c_{\lambda}|\,|D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}(x)|\leq\delta_{j}\sigma_{j}^{-1},

for a sequence
\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\ell^{q}
, as
f\in B^{\eta}_{p,q}
. Using these results, we obtain

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}|c_{\lambda}|\,|D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}(x)|\leq\delta_{j}\sigma_{j}^{-1}+\sum_{l=0}^{\lceil j/2\rceil}\varepsilon_{j-l-J0}2^{-ul}\sigma_{j-l}^{-1}\leq(\delta_{j}+\xi_{j})\sigma_{j}^{-1},

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
is defined as in the proof of Lemma
2.5
. For the first term (
13
), we still have to consider the case
j-\lceil j/2\rceil<J_{0}
; since
f\in B^{\eta}_{p,q}
, we can write

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}|c_{\lambda}|\,|D^{\alpha}\psi_{\lambda}(x)|\leq\delta_{j}2^{-\eta j}2^{jd/p}\leq C\delta_{j}\sigma_{j}^{-1},

so that
|D^{\alpha}f_{j}(x)|
is bounded by
C^{\prime}(\delta_{j}+\xi_{j})2^{n+1}\sigma_{j}^{-1}
, for any
j\leq J
; we thus have

\displaystyle\|f_{j}-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\frac{(\cdot-x_{0})^{\alpha}}{|\alpha|!}\,D^{\alpha}f_{j}(x_{0})\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta_{j}+\xi_{j})2^{-(n+1+d/p)J}2^{(n+1)j}\sigma_{j}^{-1}.

Finally, as
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})<n+1
, (
13
) is bounded by

C2^{-(n+1)J}\sum_{j=-1}^{J}(\delta_{j}+\xi_{j})2^{(n+1)j}\sigma_{j}^{-1}\leq\theta_{J}\sigma_{J}^{-1},

where
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\ell^{q}
is given by Lemma
2.6
.

Let us now consider the second term (
14
); we actually need to bound the
L^{p}
-norm of
f_{j}
for
j\geq J
. Let us, in the same spirit as before, define

\Lambda^{\prime}_{j,0}:=\{\lambda_{j,k}^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{j}:|2^{j}x_{0}-k|\leq 2^{j+J_{0}-J}\},

for
l
such that
1\leq l\leq J
,

\Lambda^{\prime}_{j,l}:=\{\lambda_{j,k}^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{j}:2^{j+J_{0}-J+l-1}<|2^{j}x_{0}-k|\leq 2^{j+J_{0}-J+l}\}

and

\Lambda^{\prime}_{j,*}:=\{\lambda_{j,k}^{(i)}\in\Lambda_{j}:2^{j}<|2^{j}x_{0}-k|\}.

Using the wavelet characterization of the
L^{p}
spaces, we can write

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}_{j,0}}c_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}

\displaystyle\leq C(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},\lambda\subset\lambda_{J}(x_{0})}2^{-dj}|c_{\lambda}|^{p})^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{J}2^{-Jd/p}\sigma_{J}^{-1}.

For
l\in\{1,\ldots,J\}
, we get this time

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}_{j,l}}|c_{\lambda}|\,|\psi_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C\varepsilon_{J-l}2^{-(j-J+l)u}\sigma_{J-l}^{-1}\leq C2^{-ul}\varepsilon_{J-l}\overline{\sigma}_{l}\sigma_{J}^{-1},

for

and

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}_{j,*}}|c_{\lambda}|\,|\psi_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C2^{-\delta J}\sigma_{J}^{-1},

for some
\delta>0
. As previously, we get that there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\rho}\in\ell^{q}
such that

2^{Jd/p}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-J}))}\leq\rho_{J}\sigma_{J}^{-1},

so that we can conclude using the same arguments as in the compactly supported case.
∎

4
A multifractal formalism

We show here that the generalized Besov spaces provide a natural framework for the multifractal formalism based on the
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}
spaces.

4.1
Definitions

As the wavelet leaders method rests on the oscillation spaces
\mathcal{O}^{s,s^{\prime}}_{p}
(see
[
11
,
13
]
), we need to adapt these spaces to our general framework.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
; a function
f
belongs to
\mathcal{O}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,r,q}
if the sequence
(C_{k})_{k}
defined by (
8
) belongs to
\ell^{q}
and if

(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}(\sigma_{j}2^{-dj/r}d_{\lambda}^{p})^{r})^{q/r})^{1/q}\leq C,

for some constant
C>0
.

We will show that these spaces are closely related to the generalized Besov spaces introduced in Definition
4.2
; as expected, these are obtained by replacing the dyadic sequence appearing in the usual definition with an admissible sequence (see e.g.
[
18
]
).

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(\varphi_{j})_{j}
be a sequence of functions belonging to the Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}
such that

•

\supp(\varphi_{0})\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|\leq 2\}
,

•

\supp(\varphi_{j})\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:2^{j-1}\leq|x|\leq 2^{j+1}\}
(
j\geq 1
),

•

for any multi-index
\alpha
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}>0
such that we have
\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{\alpha}\varphi_{j}(x)|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{-j|\alpha|}
,

•

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\varphi_{j}=1
.

Given
p,q\in[1,\infty]
; the space
B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}
is defined as

B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}:=\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|\sigma_{j}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi_{j}\mathcal{F}f)\|_{L^{p}}^{q})^{1/q}<\infty\},

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform.

We will use the wavelet characterization of these spaces (see
[
1
]
).

Definition 4.3
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
; if, given
h>-d/p
,
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}
is an admissible sequence, the family of admissible sequences
h\mapsto\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}
is
(p,q)
-decreasing if it satisfies
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)})>-d/p
,
\underline{\gamma}_{1}^{(h)}>2^{-d/p}
for any
h>-d/p
and if
-d/p<h<h^{\prime}
implies

T^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}_{p,q}(x_{0})\subset T^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h^{\prime})}}_{p,q}(x_{0}).

In the sequel, we will only consider families of admissible sequences
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\cdot)}
that are implicitly
(p,q)
-decreasing. This notion was introduced in
[
16
]
, where criteria to obtain such families were presented.

A multifractal formalism is an empirical method that allows to estimate the quantity

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h(x_{0})=h\},

where
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension with the convention
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\emptyset)=-\infty
(see
[
6
]
for example) and
h(x_{0})
is some kind of generalized Hölder exponent. Usually, one requires such a method to be valid for a large class of functions. We aim at providing here a multifractal formalism for the exponents (
15
) defined from the
T^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}
spaces, thus generalizing the wavelet leaders method
[
11
,
13
]
.

Definition 4.4
.

Given
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and a family of admissible sequences
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\cdot)}
, the generalized
(p,q)
-Hölder exponent associated to
f\in L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\cdot)}
at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by

h_{p,q}(x_{0}):=\sup\{h>-d/p:f\in T^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}_{p,q}(x_{0})\}.

(15)

The most natural family of admissible sequences is
h\mapsto(2^{jh})_{j}
; in this case,
h_{\infty,\infty}(x_{0})
is the usual Hölder exponent
[
11
]
, while
h_{p,\infty}(x_{0})
is the
p
-exponent considered in
[
13
]
.

Given
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, a family of admissible sequences
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\cdot)}
and a function
f\in L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
, we set

D_{p,q}(h):=\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h_{p,q}(x_{0})=h\}.

4.2
Preliminary results

In this section, we will implicitly work with indices
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
, a function
f
that belongs to
L_{\text{\rm loc}}^{p}
, a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a family of admissible sequences
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\cdot)}
and an admissible sequence
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If

\gamma^{(h)}_{j}2^{\eta j}d_{j}^{p}(x_{0})\in\ell^{q},

for some
\eta>0
such that
\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)})+\eta\rfloor=\lfloor\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)})\rfloor
, then
h_{p,q}(x_{0})\geq h
.

Proof.

We know that there exists a sequence of polynomial
(P_{j})_{j}
of degree at most
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)})
and a sequence
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
such that

\gamma_{j}^{(h)}2^{jd/p}\|f-P_{j}\|_{L^{p}(B(x_{0},2^{-j}))}\leq C\varepsilon_{j}2^{-\eta j}|\log_{2}(2^{-\eta j-jd/p}/\gamma_{j}^{(h)})|,

for
j
large enough, which implies
f\in T^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}_{p,q}(x_{0})
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

If the function
f
belongs to both
B^{\eta}_{p,q}
for some
\eta>0
and
\mathcal{O}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,r,q}
, then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h_{p,q}(x_{0})<h\}\leq d+r\overline{s}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}).

Proof.

Let
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
be such that
\varepsilon_{j}\not=0
and

(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}(\sigma_{j}2^{-jd/r}d_{\lambda}^{p})^{r})^{1/r}\leq\varepsilon_{j},

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let us first consider the case
r=\infty
; if
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}/\boldsymbol{\sigma})<0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\gamma_{j}^{(h)}2^{\delta j}d_{j}^{p}(x_{0})\leq C\varepsilon_{j}
for any
j
and
h_{p,q}(x_{0})\geq h
for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As a consequence, we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h_{p,q}(x_{0})<h\}=-\infty=d+r\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}/\boldsymbol{\sigma}).

On the other hand, if
\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}/\boldsymbol{\sigma})\geq 0
,

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h_{p,q}(x_{0})<h\}\leq d\leq d+r\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}/\boldsymbol{\sigma}).

Now, suppose
r<\infty
, fix
h>-d/p
and define, given
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\delta>0
sufficiently small,

E_{j,\delta}^{h}:=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}:d_{\lambda}^{p}\geq\varepsilon_{j}2^{-\delta j}/\gamma_{j}^{(h)}\}

and set
n=\#E_{j,\delta}^{h}
. As
f\in\mathcal{O}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,r,q}
, we have

\sigma_{j}^{r}2^{-jd}n(2^{-\delta j}/\gamma_{j}^{(h)})^{r}\leq\varepsilon_{j}^{-r}\sigma_{j}^{r}2^{-jd}\sum_{\lambda\in E_{j,\delta}^{h}}(d_{\lambda}^{p})^{r}\leq 1,

so that

n\leq 2^{jd}(2^{-\delta j}/\gamma_{j}^{(h)})^{-r}/\sigma_{j}^{r}.

Now, define
\Lambda_{j,\delta}^{h}
as the set of the dyadic cubes
\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}
such that there exists a neighbor
\lambda^{\prime}\in 3\lambda
that belongs to
E_{j,\delta}^{h}
. Finally, define

F_{\delta}^{h}:=\limsup_{j}\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\lambda_{j}(x_{0})\in\Lambda_{j,\delta}^{h}\}.

If
x_{0}
does not belong to
F_{\delta}^{h}
, then there exists
J\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
j\geq J
implies
\lambda_{j}(x_{0})\not\in\Lambda_{j,\delta}^{h}
and, from what we have obtained for
n
, there exists a constant
C>0
for which
j\geq J
implies

2^{\delta j}\gamma_{j}^{(h)}d_{j}^{p}(x_{0})\leq C\varepsilon_{j},

and therefore

\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h_{p,q}(x_{0})<h\}\subset F_{\delta}^{h}.

(16)

Let
\alpha>0
, set
j_{1}:=\inf\{j:\sqrt{d}2^{-j}<\alpha\}
and

E_{\delta}:=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j,\delta}^{h}:j\geq j_{1}\}.

It is easy to check that
E_{\delta}
is an
\alpha
-covering of
F_{\delta}^{h}
; given
s,\eta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in E_{\delta}}\text{diam}(\lambda)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}\#F_{j}^{h}(\sqrt{d}2^{-j})^{s}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\geq j_{0}}2^{(d-s)j}(2^{-\delta j}/\gamma_{j}^{(h)})^{-r}/\sigma_{j}^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}2^{rj(\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}/\boldsymbol{\sigma})+\delta+\eta}2^{(d-s)j}.

As a consequence, we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(F_{\delta}^{h})\leq d+r(\overline{s}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})+\delta+\eta),

for any
\eta>0
and we can conclude thanks to (
16
).
∎

Of course, for the natural choices of the families of admissible sequences
[
16
]
,
h\mapsto\overline{s}(\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)})
is continuous; in such a case, the previous result can be improved.

Remark 4.7
.

If there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
converging to
0^{+}
such that

\overline{s}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h+\varepsilon_{j})}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})\to\overline{s}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}),

we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:h_{p,q}(x_{0})\leq h\}\leq d+r\overline{s}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}).

Proposition 4.8
.

If
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
is an admissible sequence such that
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})>0
and
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})-d/r>-d/p
, we have
\mathcal{O}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,r,q}=B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,q}
.

Proof.

We obviously have
\mathcal{O}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,r,q}\hookrightarrow B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,q}
. If
f
belongs to
B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,q}
, we have

\displaystyle(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}(\sigma_{j}2^{-jd/r}d_{\lambda}^{p})^{r})^{q/r}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}(\sigma_{j}2^{-jd/r})^{q}\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}\big(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}},\lambda^{\prime}\subset\lambda}(2^{(j-j^{\prime})d/p}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{p}\big)^{r/p}\Big)^{q/r},

(17)

for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Let us first suppose that
r\leq p
; in this case, (
17
) is bounded by

(\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}(\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{-1}2^{(j-j^{\prime})d/p}2^{(j^{\prime}-j)d/r})^{r}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}2^{-j^{\prime}d/r}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{r})^{q/r}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be such that
\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})-\varepsilon-d/r>-d/p
; there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{-1}<C_{\varepsilon}2^{(\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})-\varepsilon)(j-j^{\prime})}.

If
q\leq r
, (
17
) is bounded by

C(\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}(2^{(\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+\varepsilon-d/r-d/r)(j-j^{\prime})})^{q}(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}2^{-j^{\prime}d/r}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{r})^{q/r}).

As
f
belongs to
B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,q}
, we can write

(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}(\sigma_{j}2^{-jd/r}d_{\lambda}^{p})^{r})^{q/r})^{1/q}\leq C(\sum_{j^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}2^{-j^{\prime}d/r}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{r})^{q/r})^{1/q},

wich implies

. If
r<q
, by denoting

the conjugate exponent of

, we can use Hölder’s inequality to bound (

) by

\displaystyle C(\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}(2^{-\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+\varepsilon-d/p-d/r)(j^{\prime}-j)})^{rs/2})^{q/(rs)}

\displaystyle\quad\Big(\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}(2^{-\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+\varepsilon-d/p-d/r)(j^{\prime}-j)})^{q/(2r)}(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}2^{-j^{\prime}d/r}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{r})^{q/r}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}(2^{-\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+\varepsilon-d/p-d/r)(j^{\prime}-j)})^{q/(2r)}(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}2^{-j^{\prime}d/r}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{r})^{q/r}\Big),

so that

belongs to
\mathcal{O}^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,r,q}

We still have to consider the case

; by Jensen’s inequality, we can bound (

\displaystyle(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}(\sigma_{j}2^{-jd/r})^{r}\sum_{j\geq j^{\prime}}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}},\lambda^{\prime}\subset\lambda}2^{(j-j^{\prime})d}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|^{r})^{q/r}

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}(\sigma_{j}/\sigma_{j^{\prime}})^{r}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}2^{-j^{\prime}d/r}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{r})^{q/r},

so that we can conclude as in the other cases.
∎

4.3
The notion of prevalence

In this section, we very briefly introduce the notion of prevalence (see
[
3
,
9
,
8
]
for more details).

In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is well known that if one can associate a probability measure
\mu
to a Borel set
B
such that
\mu(B+x)
vanishes for very
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}(B)
of
B
also vanishes. For the notion of prevalence, this property is turned into a definition in the context of infinite-dimensional spaces.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
E
be a complete metric vector space; a borel set
B
of
E
is Haar-null if there exists a compactly-supported probability measure
\mu
such that
\mu(B+x)=0
, for every
x\in E
. A subset of
E
is Haar-null if it is contained in a Haar-null Borel set; the complement of a Haar-null set is a prevalent set.

Is
E
is finite-dimensional,
B
is Haar-null if and only if
\mathcal{L}(B)=0
; if
E
is infinite-dimensional, the compact sets of
E
are Haar-null. Moreover, it can be shown that a translated of a Haar-null set is Haar-null and that a prevalent set is dense in
E
. Finally, the intersection of a countable collection of prevalent sets is prevalent.

Let us make some remarks about how to show that a set is Haar-null. A common choice for the measure in Definition
4.9
is the Lebesgue measure on the unit ball of a finite-dimensional subset
E^{\prime}
of
E
. For such a choice, one has to show that
\mathcal{L}(B\cap(E^{\prime}+x))
vanishes for every
x
. If
E
is a function space, one can choose a random process
X
whose sample paths almost surely belong to
E
. In this case, one can show that a property only holds on a Haar-null set by showing that the sample path
X
is such that, for any
f\in E
,
X_{t}+f
almost surely does not satisfy the property.

If a property holds on a prevalent set, we will say that it holds almost everywhere from the prevalence point of view.

4.4
A multifractal formalism associated to the generalized Besov spaces

We propose here the following formula to estimate the spectrum
D_{p,q}
related to a function
f\in B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,s}
:

D_{p,q}(h)=d+r\overline{s}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})

and show that, under natural smooth conditions, this equality is satisfied almost everywhere from a prevalent point of view.

Definition 4.10
.

An admissible sequence
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
and a family of admissible sequences
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\cdot)}
are compatible for
p,q,r,s\in[1,\infty]
with
s\leq q
if

•

\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})>0
,

•

\underline{s}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})-d/r>-d/p

•

\zeta
, defined on
(-d/p,\infty)
by

\zeta(h):=\underline{s}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=\overline{s}(\frac{\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(h)}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}),

is a non decreasing continuous function such that

\{h>-d/p:\zeta(h)<-d/r\}\not=\emptyset.

We call
\zeta
the ratio function. We will also frequently use the quantity

h_{\min}(r):=\sup\{h>-d/p:\zeta(h)<-d/r\}.

The following remark stresses the importance of

.

Remark 4.11
.

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\cdot)}
are compatible as in the previous definition. If

belongs to
B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}
, there exists
\eta>0
such that
B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{p,q}\hookrightarrow B^{\eta}_{p,q}
. For

and
j^{\prime}\geq j
, we have

\displaystyle(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}},\lambda^{\prime}\subset\lambda}(2^{(j-j^{\prime})d/p}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{p})^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{jd/p}(\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{j^{\prime}}}(\sigma_{j^{\prime}}2^{-j^{\prime}d/p}|c_{\lambda^{\prime}}|)^{p})^{1/p}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{jd/p}\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{-1},

for a sequence
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\ell^{q}
. As a consequence, there exists
\eta>0
and a sequence
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\ell^{q}
given by Lemma
2.5
such that, for

d_{\lambda}^{p}\leq C\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}2^{jd/p}\varepsilon_{j^{\prime}}\sigma_{j^{\prime}}^{-1}\leq C\xi_{j}2^{-\eta j}/\gamma_{j}^{(h)}

for all

such that

That being done, if

Thus, if

f\in B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,q}
and
h_{p,q}(x_{0})\geq h_{\min}(r)
.

From what we have done so far, we get the following corollary.

Let

\boldsymbol{\sigma}
be an admissible sequence and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\cdot)}

Theorem 4.20

Let us fix
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and define

F_{n_{0}}:=\{f\in B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,s}:\forall j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\forall\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}\cap\mathcal{K}_{x_{0}}(2),|c_{\lambda}|\leq n_{0}\theta^{(n)}_{j}/j\}.

As shown before,
F_{n_{0}}
is a Borel set. For
f\in B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,s}
and
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
f+ag^{(n)}\in F_{n_{0}}
and
f+a^{\prime}g^{(n)}\in F_{n_{0}}
, we get

|a-a^{\prime}|\leq 2n_{0}/j,

so that the Lebesgue measure of
\{a\in\mathbb{R}:f+ag^{(n)}\in F_{n_{0}}\}
vanishes, implying that
F_{n_{0}}
is Haar-null. As we have

\{f\in B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,s}:f\in T^{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(n)}}_{p,q}(x_{0})\}\subset\bigcup_{l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}F_{l},

for almost every
f\in B^{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{r,s}
, we have
h_{p,q}(x_{0})\leq\zeta^{-1}(-d/r)+1/n
, which leads to the conclusion.

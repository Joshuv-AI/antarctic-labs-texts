Abstract
. Let G G be a subgroup of the three dimensional projective group PGL ​ ( 3 , q ) \mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q) defined over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} of order q q , viewed as a subgroup of PGL ​ ( 3 , K ) \mbox{\rm PGL}(3,K) where K K is an algebraic closure of 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . For the seven nonsporadic, maximal subgroups G G of PGL ​ ( 3 , q ) \mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q) , we investigate the (projective, irreducible) plane curves defined over K K that are left invariant by G G . For each, we compute the minimum degree d ⁡ ( G ) d(G) of G G -invariant curves, provide a classification of all G G -invariant curves of degree d ⁡ ( G ) d(G) , and determine the first gap ε ⁡ ( G ) \varepsilon(G) in the spectrum of the degrees of all G G -invariant curves. We show that the curves of degree d ⁡ ( G ) d(G) belong to a pencil depending on G G , unless they are uniquely determined by G G . We also point out that G G -invariant curves of degree d ⁡ ( G ) d(G) have particular geometric features such as Frobenius nonclassicality and an unusual variation of the number of 𝔽 q i \mathbb{F}_{q^{i}} -rational points. For most examples of plane curves left invariant by a large subgroup of PGL ​ ( 3 , q ) \mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q) , the whole automorphism group of the curve is linear, i.e., a subgroup of PGL ​ ( 3 , K ) \mbox{\rm PGL}(3,K) . Although this appears to be a general behavior, we show that the opposite case can also occur for some irreducible plane curves, that is, the curve has a large group of linear automorphisms, but its full automorphism group is nonlinear.



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be an algebraically closed field. A classical result on automorphisms of (projective, irreducible) plane curves defined over
K
is that, if the curve is nonsingular and of degree at least four, then its automorphism group is linear, that is, a subgroup of the three-dimensional projective general linear group
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,K)
. By a result of B. Segre, this theorem holds independently of the characteristic of the field
K
, see
[
22
, Theorem 11.29]
. The hypothesis of nonsingularity cannot be dropped, nevertheless, singular curves may have many linear automorphisms. If this occurs, the curve often happens to have useful applications to finite geometry and coding theory.

In this paper, we work over an algebraic closure
K
of a prime field of characteristic
p>0
. For a given finite subgroup
G
of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,K)
, let
d(G)
denote the smallest integer that is the degree of a
G
-invariant irreducible plane curve other than a line. Clearly,
d(G)

does not change if we replace
G
with another group from the conjugacy class of
G
in
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,K)
. We raise the following three questions for subgroups of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,K)
.

(i)

find
d(G)
for groups
G
of larger order compared to
d
;

(ii)

find a positive integer
\varepsilon(G)
depending on
q
such that no
G
-invariant irreducible plane curve of degree
d(G)+\varepsilon(G)
exists.

(iii)

determine all
G
-invariant irreducible plane curves of degree
d(G)
.

Note that the finiteness of
G
implies
G\leq\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})=\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
for some power
q
of
p
. We choose the smallest such
q
and address the above three questions for maximal, nonsporadic, subgroups of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
for a given
q
. We provide a complete solution for each. A summary of the results is provided in Section
3
, where some consequences regarding the geometry of low degree
G
-invariant irreducible plane curves are also stated, especially from the point of view of the Stöhr-Voloch theory.

The essential idea underlying our investigation has its origin in a basic result from classical invariant theory, which finds that if
\Gamma\leq{\rm GL}(3,K)
is a linear group and

F_{1},F_{2}\in K[X,Y,Z]
are both
\Gamma
-invariant forms (homogeneous polynomials) of the same degree
d
, then any non-trivial linear combination
F=\lambda_{1}F_{1}+\lambda_{2}F_{2}
is also a
\Gamma
-invariant form. The projective version concerns the projective linear group
G=\Gamma/Z(\Gamma)
, together with the pencil
\Lambda
generated by the curves
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
of homogeneous equations
F_{1}=0,F_{2}=0
, respectively. It states that
\Lambda
is
G
-fixed, that is, each curve
\mathcal{C}
of degree
d
of equation
F=\lambda_{1}F_{1}+\lambda_{2}F_{2}=0
is
G
-invariant. A useful refinement that still ensures that the pencil is
G
-fixed requires only that the rational function
F_{1}/F_{2}
is
\Gamma
-invariant. Once such a
G
-fixed pencil
\Lambda
has been found, if
\mathcal{D}
is any
G
-invariant plane curve of degree
d\geq d(G)
, then
|G|
is a lower bound on the number of common points of
\mathcal{D}
with a generically chosen curve
\mathcal{C}
from
\Lambda
. Comparison of this lower bound with the upper bound derived from the Bézout theorem yields
|G|\leq d(G)d
. In other words, if
d<d(G)/|G|
, then
\mathcal{D}
has to be a curve in the pencil
\Lambda
. Thus the problem arises whether a
G
-fixed pencil of curves of degree
d(G)
exist for each of the maximal, nonsporadic subgroups of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
, namely
G\cong\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q),\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q),\overline{\mbox{\rm AGL}}(2,q),\mbox{\rm PSU}(3,d),NS_{q},\Delta_{q}
, and
\mbox{\rm PGL}(2,q)
for
q
odd. The definitions of these groups are found in Section
2.2
.
We will show that the answer to this is affirmative. In particular, the exact value of
d(G)
is computed, which resolves Question (i) for these groups.
Furthermore,
\varepsilon(G)\geq d(G)(1-1/|G|)
. Actually, as we will show, equality attains and hence a complete solution for Question(ii) is obtained for each of the above seven groups. The study of Question (iii) requires a closer look at the special features of the
G
-fixed pencils. They differ depending on which of the above seven groups is taken for
G
. Nevertheless, a case-by-case analysis is enough to obtain a complete solution also for this question.

2.
Background and preliminary results

2.1.
Plane curves and their automorphisms

For definitions and basic properties of plane algebraic curves, see
[
22
, Chapters 1-5]
.
In the projective plane
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
equipped with a projective reference system in coordinates
(X:Y:Z)
, a plane (algebraic) curve
\mathcal{C}
of homogeneous equation
F(X,Y,Z)=0
, with a homogeneous polynomial (or form)
F(X,Y,Z)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[X,Y,Z]
, consists of all points
P=(a:b:c)
such that
F(a,b,c)=0
. As customary, we regard
\mathcal{C}
as a curve over a fixed algebraic closure
K
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Doing so, the points
P=(a:b:c)
of
{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
for which
F(a:b:c)=0
are exactly the points of
\mathcal{C}
. An affine equation of
\mathcal{C}
is
f(X,Y)=0
where
f(X,Y)=F(X,Y,1)
. We use standard notation. The
degree
of
\mathcal{C}
is
\mbox{\rm deg}(F(X,Y,Z))
. A
component
of
\mathcal{C}
is a curve
\mathcal{G}
of homogeneous equation
G(X,Y,Z)=0
such that
G(X,Y,Z)
divides
F(X,Y,Z)
. A curve
\mathcal{C}
is (absolutely)
irreducible
if
F(X,Y,Z)
is irreducible over
K
; otherwise,
\mathcal{C}
is
reducible
as it splits in irreducible curves, the
components
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Given two plane curves
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{F}
, for any point
P\in{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
, the
intersection multiplicity

I(P,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F})
between
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{F}
at
P
is a main tool in the study of plane curves, see
[
22
, Chapter 3]
. A point
P
is a common point of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{F}
if and only if
I(P,\mathcal{C}\cap\mathcal{F})\geq 1
, and
P
is a
singular point
of
\mathcal{C}
, with multiplicity
r
, if
I(P;\mathcal{C}\cap\ell)=r
for all but finitely many lines
\ell
through
P
. The exceptions are the
tangent lines
to
\mathcal{C}
at
P
. They satisfy
I(P,\mathcal{C}\cap\ell)>r
, and their number is positive but does not exceed
r
. By the fundamental Bézout theorem, for any two plane curves
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{F}
without a common component,
\mbox{\rm deg}(\mathcal{C})\mbox{\rm deg}(\mathcal{F})=\sum I(P,\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G})
where
P
ranges over the set of all common points of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{F}
. A
pencil
generated by the plane curves
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
of homogeneous equations
F(X,Y,Z)=0
and
G(X,Y,Z)=0
, respectively, consists of all plane curves
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
of homogeneous equations
\lambda F(X,Y,Z)+G(X,Y,Z)=0
with
\lambda
ranging over
K\cup\{\infty\}
where
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}=\mathcal{F}
. For every point
P\in{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
which is not a base point, i.e. it is not a common point of any two curves in the pencil, there exists a unique curve from the pencil passing through
P
. A
net

\Gamma
generated by such a pencil
\Lambda
together with further plane curves
\mathcal{H}
, off the pencil with homogeneous equations
H(X,Y,Z)=0
, consists of the curves in
\Lambda
together with the plane curves
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
of homogeneous equations
\lambda F(X,Y,Z)+\mu G(X,Y,Z)+H(X,Y,Z)=0
with
(\lambda,\mu)
ranging over
K\times K
. In other words, the net
\Gamma
consists of all plane curves of homogeneous equation
\lambda F(X,Y,Z)+\mu G(X,Y,Z)+\tau H(X,Y,Z)=0
with
(\lambda;\mu;\tau)
ranging over the non-trivial homogeneous triples over
K
, i.e., over the points of
{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
.
For every incident point-line pair
(P,t)
of
{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
, the net
\Gamma
contains some curve
\mathcal{C}
through
P
and tangent to
\mathcal{C}
at
P
.

A
linear
automorphism of
\mathcal{C}
is a projectivity
\alpha
represented by a
3\times 3
regular matrix
A=(a_{ij})
such that
\mathcal{C}
is
\alpha
-invariant, i.e.,
F(X,Y,Z)=cF(\bar{X},\bar{Y},\bar{Z})
for some
c\in K
where the column vector
[X,Y,Z]
is the product of
A
by the column vector
[\bar{X},\bar{Y},\bar{Z}]
. The linear automorphisms of
\mathcal{C}
form the
linear automorphism group
of
\mathcal{C}
which is a subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,K)
. If
\mathcal{C}
is an irreducible plane curve other than a line, then its linear automorphism group is finite. It should be noted that
\mathcal{C}
may have nonlinear automorphisms, i.e., there may exist a rational map
\alpha:(X,Y,Z)\mapsto(U,V,W)
with
U,V,W\in K[X,Y,Z]
homogeneous polynomials of the same degree
\geq 2
such that
F(X,Y,Z)=SF(U,V,W)
with some homogeneous polynomial
S\in K[X,Y,Z]
. The
automorphism group
of
\mathcal{C}
consists of its linear and nonlinear automorphisms, and it is finite unless
\mathcal{C}
is either rational or elliptic.

We point out that irreducible plane curves with many linear automorphisms often have a truly geometric property related to inseparable function field extensions that curves in zero characteristic cannot possess.
An irreducible plane curve
\mathcal{C}
is
nonclassical
if it has infinitely many inflection points, that is, if
I(P,\mathcal{C}\cap t)>2
for infinitely many nonsingular points
P\in\mathcal{C}
where
t
is the tangent line to
\mathcal{C}
at
P
. This impossible phenomena in zero characteristic was the starting point of the Stöhr-Voloch theory in 1986
[
33
]
; for recent results see
[
22
, Sections 8.5, 8.6]
and
[
2
,
5
]
.
Such nonclassicality occurs for
\mathcal{C}
if and only if there is a power
p^{h}\geq 3
of
p
, denoted by
\varepsilon_{2}(\mathcal{C})
, such that
I(P,\mathcal{C}\cap t)=p^{h}
for infinitely many nonsingular points
P\in\mathcal{C}
with tangent line
t
to
\mathcal{C}
at
P
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an irreducible plane curve with homogeneous equation
F(X,Y,Z)=0
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is nonclassical if and only if there exist four homogeneous polynomials

U_{i}(X,Y,Z),H(X,Y,Z)\in K[X,Y,Z]
with the same degree such that

(1)

U_{1}(X,Y,Z)^{p^{h}}X+U_{2}(X,Y,Z)^{p^{h}}Y+U_{3}(X,Y,Z)^{p^{h}}Z=H(X,Y,Z)F(X,Y,Z)

where
\varepsilon_{2}(\mathcal{C})=p^{h}
. If
\mathcal{C}
is nonsingular, then
H(X,Y,Z)
is constant. An irreducible plane curve
\mathcal{C}
defined over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
is
Frobenius nonclassical
if the tangent line
t
to
\mathcal{C}
at
P
passes through the Frobenius image of
P
for infinitely many nonsingular points
P\in\mathcal{C}
. Here, the
Frobenius image
of the point
P(a:b:c)
of
{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
is the point
\Phi(P)=(a^{p^{k}}:b^{p^{k}}:c^{p^{k}})
of
{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
. For odd
p
, any Frobenius nonclassical curve is nonclassical. The converse does not hold in general, as
\mathcal{C}
is Frobenius nonclassical if and only if (
1
) holds and there exists a homogeneous polynomial
L(X,Y,Z)\in K[X,Y,Z]
such that

(2)

U_{1}(X,Y,Z)X^{p^{k-h}}+U_{2}(X,Y,Z)Y^{p^{k-h}}+U_{3}(X,Y,Z)Z^{p^{k-h}}=L(X,Y,Z)F(X,Y,Z).

Frobenius nonclassical curves are relevant in the study of plane curves of
{\rm{PG}}(2,p^{k})
which have many points in
{\rm{PG}}(2,p^{k})
compared with the degree of the curve.
In fact for
p>2
, if the number
N_{p^{k}}
of points of an irreducible plane curve defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
of degree
d
exceeds
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}d(p^{k}+d-1)
, then the curve is Frobenius nonclassical.
On the other hand, for an irreducible plane curve
\mathcal{C}
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{k}}
containing some points in
{\rm{PG}}(2,p^{k})
, a lower bound on
N_{p^{k}}
is
|G|/|G_{P}|
where
G
is a linear automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}
and
G_{P}
is the stabilizer of
P
in
G
. This shows that if
|G|
is large (with
|G_{P}|
relatively small) then
\mathcal{C}
has to be Frobenius classical.

2.2.
Maximal Subgroups of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)

The projective general linear group
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
has order
(q^{3}-1)q^{3}(q^{2}-1)=(q^{2}+q+1)q^{3}(q+1)(q-1)^{2}
and its proper subgroups have been known for a long time, see H.H Mitchell
[
29
]
and R.W. Hartley
[
15
]
, for
q
odd and
q
even, respectively. For an exposition in standard notation, see
[
24
]
. For our purpose, we limit ourselves to recall the classification for those subgroups which are not isomorphic to a subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q^{\prime})
for
q^{\prime}<q
.

For
q
odd:

(i)

\mbox{\rm PSL}(3,q)
for
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, having order
\frac{1}{3}(q^{2}+q+1)q^{3}(q+1)(q-1)^{2}

(ii)

the stabilizer of a point of
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
, having order
q^{3}(q+1)(q-1)^{2}

(iii)

the stabilizer of a line of
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
, having order
q^{3}(q+1)(q-1)^{2}

(iv)

the stabilizer of an Hermitian curve of
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
for
q=n^{2}
, having order
n^{3}(n^{3}+1)(n-1)^{2}

(v)

the stabilizer of a triangle of
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
, having order
6(q-1)^{2}

(vi)

the stabilizer of an imaginary triangle (i.e., a triangle in
{\rm{PG}}(2,q^{3})\setminus{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
), having order
3(q^{2}+q+1)

(vii)

the stabilizer of an irreducible conic, having order
q(q+1)(q-1)

(viii)

sporadic subgroups: the Hessian groups of order
216
if
q-1
is divisible by
9,72
and
36
if
q-1
is divisible by
3
. Groups of order
168
(when
-7
is a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
),
360
(when
5
is a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
) and there is a non-trivial cube root of unity in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
720
(when
q
is an even
power of
5
), and
2520
(when
p=5
and
q
is an even power of
5
).

Notice that (ii) and (iii) are isomorphic but not conjugate in
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
. Up to conjugacy in
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
(equivalently, up to a change of projective frame in
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
), (ii) is the affine group
\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)
, (iii) is the dual affine group
\overline{\mbox{\rm AGL}}(2,q)
, (iv) is the projective unitary group
\mbox{\rm PGU}(3,n)
. Also, (v) is a semidirect product of
C_{q-1}\times C_{q-1}
by a complement isomorphic to
{\rm{Sym}}_{3}
,
(vi) is the normalizer of a Singer subgroup of
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
of order
q^{2}+q+1
, and it is a semidirect product of
C_{q^{2}+q+1}
by a complement
C_{3}
.
The groups of orders
168,360,720
, and
2520
are isomorphic to
\mbox{\rm PSL}(2,7)
),
\rm{Alt}_{6}
,
\rm{Alt}_{6}\cdot 2

and
\rm{Alt}_{7}
respectively. The groups of orders
216
and
72
are isomorphic to
\mbox{\rm PGU}(3,2)
and
\mbox{\rm PSU}(3,2)
, respectively, with the
group of order
36
a subgroup of the latter.

For
q
even, case (vii) does not occur, while there is a unique sporadic subgroup, namely a group of order
360
for
q=4
isomorphic to
{\rm{Alt}}_{6}
.

3.
Summary of the results

The most important family of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
-invariant irreducible plane curves consists of the double-Frobenius nonclassical planes curves
\mathcal{F}_{n,m}
, introduced and investigated in
[
5
]
, where
{\rm{gcd}}(m,n)=1
and
\mathcal{F}_{n,m}
stands for the irreducible plane curve of affine equation

(3)

\frac{(X^{q^{n}}-X)(Y^{q^{m}}-Y)-(Y^{q^{n}}-Y)(X^{q^{m}}-X)}{(X^{q^{2}}-X)(Y^{q}-Y)-(Y^{q^{2}}-Y)(X^{q}-X)}=0

of genus
g(\mathcal{F}_{n,m})=(q^{n-m}+q^{m})({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}q^{n}-(1+q+q^{2})+(q+1)(1+q+q^{2})
.
The special case
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}
, named DGZ curve in
[
14
]
, is widely known and of particular interest as being, for the case
q=p
, the only known example, up to birational equivalency, of an ordinary curve with more than
cg^{3/2}
automorphisms, for some constant
c
independent from
g
and
p
. This conforms with the conjecture made by Guralnick and Zieve in 1998 that no other such examples existed, see
[
14
]
.
The dual curve of
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}
is the curve
\mathcal{F}_{3,2}
, that is, the dual DGZ curve. From Theorem
4.13
,
d(\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q))=q^{3}-q
,
\varepsilon(\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q))=q^{2}-q
, and the DGZ curve is the only
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
-invariant irreducible plane curve of degree
q^{3}-q
. If
q\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, then
\mbox{\rm PSL}(3,q)
is a maximal subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
of index
3
but the same result holds, see Theorem
4.15
.

The subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
preserving a line of
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
is a maximal subgroup isomorphic to the affine group
\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)
of order
q^{2}(q-1)(q^{3}-q)
. From Theorems
4.3
,
4.4
,
d(\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q))=q^{3}-q^{2}
,
\varepsilon(\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q))=q^{2}-q
. Furthermore, from Theorem
4.5
, all
\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)
-invariant irreducible curves of degree

\frac{(X^{q^{3}}-X)(Y^{q}-Y)-(Y^{q^{3}}-Y)(X^{q}-X)}{(X^{q^{2}}-X)(Y^{q}-Y)-(Y^{q^{2}}-Y)(X^{q}-X)}-\lambda=0,\quad\lambda\in K.

The subgroup
\overline{\mbox{\rm AGL}}(2,q)
of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
fixing a point of
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
is also a maximal subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
, which is isomorphic (but not conjugate) to
\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)
. From Theorems
4.7
and
4.8
,
d(\overline{\mbox{\rm AGL}}(2,q))=q^{3}-q^{2}=d(\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q))
and

\varepsilon(\overline{\mbox{\rm AGL}}(2,q)=q^{2}-q=\varepsilon(\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q))
. Furthermore, from Theorem
4.9
, all
\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)
-invariant irreducible curves of degree

with

For

square, another large maximal subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
is the three-dimensional projective unitary group
\mbox{\rm PGU}(3,\mathbb{F}_{n})
with

. The best known example of plane curves of
{\rm{PG}}(2,q)
whose automorphism group is

(4)

Y^{n}+Y-X^{n+1}=0.

As well known,
\mathcal{H}_{n}
is the unique curve of genus
g
with more than
16g^{4}
automorphisms, see
[
32
]
. From Theorem
4.17
,
d(\mbox{\rm PGU}(3,n))=n+1
,
\varepsilon(\mbox{\rm PGU}(3,n))=qn-n
, and
\mathcal{H}_{q}
is the only
\mbox{\rm PGU}(3,n)
-invariant irreducible plane curve of degree
n+1
.

Further maximal subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
is a stabilizer
\Delta_{q}
of a triangle, which is the semidirect product of an abelian group of order
(q-1)^{2}
fixing the vertices of the triangle by a complement of order
6
which acts on the set of the vertices as
{\rm{Sym}}_{3}
on three letters. From Theorem
4.22
,
d(\Delta_{q})=q-1
,
\varepsilon(\Delta_{q})=q-1
, and the only
\Delta_{q}
-invariant irreducible plane curve of degree
q-1
is the Fermat curve. Moreover, those that are
\Delta_{q}
-invariant, and of degree
2q-2
, have homogeneous equation
\lambda(X^{q-1}+Y^{q-1}+Z^{q-1})^{2}+(XY)^{q-1}+(YZ)^{q-1}+(ZX)^{q-1})=0
with
\lambda\in K.

Another maximal subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
preserves a triangle, but this time in
{\rm{PG}}(2,q^{3})
. It is the normalizer
NS_{q}
of the Singer subgroup that is the semi-direct product of a cyclic group of order
q^{2}+q+1
(the Singer subgroup) by a complement
C_{3}
of order
3
. All irreducible plane curves of degree
d
that are invariant by the Singer subgroup have been determined for
d\leq 2q+2
, see Lemma
4.20
and Theorem
4.21
, which
states that
d(NS_{q})=q+2
,
\varepsilon(NS_{q})=q-1
, and that the Pellikaan curve of homogeneous equation

(5)

X^{q+1}Y+Y^{q+1}Z+Z^{q+1}X=0

is the only
NS_{q}
-invariant curve of degree
q+2
, up to projectivity, see
[
31
,
10
,
11
]
.

For
q
odd, the list of nonsporadic maximal subgroups of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
also includes the two dimensional projective general linear group
\mbox{\rm PGL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. Several examples of irreducible plane curves whose automorphism group is
\mbox{\rm PGL}(2,{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
are presented in the literature. Apart from the unique
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
-invariant conic, those of minimum degree
q+1
belong, up to projectivity, to the pencil consisting of the plane curves of homogeneous equation

(6)

Y^{q+1}-(X^{q}Z+XZ^{q})-\lambda(Y^{2}-2XZ)^{(q+1)/2}=0,\quad\lambda\in K\cup\{\infty\}.

The above quoted theorems provide a complete solution for Questions (i), (ii), and (iii) when
G
is one of the maximal, nonsporadic subgroups of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
, namely,
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q),\mbox{\rm PSL}(3,q),q\equiv 1\pmod{3},\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q),\overline{\mbox{\rm AGL}}(2,q),

\mbox{\rm PGU}(3,q),\mbox{\rm PGL}(2,q),NS_{q}
, and
\Delta_{q}
. In particular, the solution of Question (iii) proves the existence of previously unknown infinite families of
G
-invariant curves of degree
d(G)
. Moreover, as a byproduct of our investigation, Theorem
7.1
gives the number of points of the DGZ curve over some extensions
\mathbb{F}_{q^{i}}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and analogous results can be obtained for other curves
\mathcal{F}_{n,m}
.

In most examples of plane curves with large linear automorphism groups which we show in this paper, the whole automorphism group of the curve is linear. Although this appears to be a general behavior, we point out
in Section
5
that the opposite case can also occur for some irreducible plane curve
\mathcal{C}
, that is, the full automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}
is large and nonlinear, but its linear automorphisms are still numerous. More precisely, we will show that this happens to the irreducible plane curve
\mathcal{K}
of genus
g=q^{2}-q-1
and affine equation

(7)

(X+Y)^{q+1}=2(XY+(XY)^{q}),

whose full automorphism group is isomorphic to
\mbox{\rm PGL}(2,q)
. In fact, the linear automorphisms of
\mathcal{K}
form a subgroup of order
q(q-1)>g
. This curve was encountered in the study of hemisystems of the Hermitian surface arising from the Fuhrmann-Torres
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-maximal curves, see
[
26
]
. Actually,
\mathcal{K}
belongs to a larger family of curves with the same behavior. The family is found and investigated with an approach similar to that used in the previous sections. It turns out that one may obtain results analogous to those therein when investigating curves that are invariant under some finite subgroup of the group of birational automorphisms of the projective plane
{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
, the Cremona group.

4.1.

Now take the pencil
\Lambda

together with the line

consists of all curves

\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)

is an
\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)
-fixed pencil.

Lemma 4.1
.

Every curve in
\Lambda
is reducible, but none of the irreducible components of a curve in
\Lambda
is
\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)
-invariant.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
with

be a curve in
\Lambda
. Define

to be
\mu^{q-1}=\lambda

\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
has affine equation

\left(\mu\begin{vmatrix}X&X^{q}&X^{q^{2}}\\
Y&Y^{q}&Y^{q^{2}}\\
1&1&1\end{vmatrix}\right)^{q-1}-1=0

which can also be written as

\prod_{\kappa\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}}\left(\mu\begin{vmatrix}X&X^{q}&X^{q^{2}}\\
Y&Y^{q}&Y^{q^{2}}\\
1&1&1\end{vmatrix}-\kappa\right)=0.

This shows that
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda}
splits into
q-1
plane curves
\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}
with

-invariant. From this, our claim follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

No irreducible plane curve of degree smaller than

is
\mbox{\rm AGL}(2,q)
-invariant.

Let

be an

\Lambda

through

\mbox{\rm PGL}(2,q)

In
[
26

\mathcal{K}
with affine equation (
7
) came up as a useful tool in the construction of a new family of hemisystems of the Hermitian surface. In Section
8

\mathcal{K}
is an unramified degree-2 cover of the Hermitian curve
\mathcal{H}_{q}

\beta_{1}:(X,Y)\mapsto(X+\alpha,Y+\alpha),\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

\beta_{2}:(X,Y)\mapsto(\mu X,\mu Y),\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}

\beta_{3}:(X,Y)\mapsto(X^{-1},Y^{-1})
;

\beta_{4}:(X,Y)\mapsto(Y,X)

More precisely,
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}
generate a group

and
\beta_{4}
centralizes

Clearly,
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{4}

\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
but this does not hold true for
\beta_{3}
as the homogeneous equation of
\beta_{3}
is
(X,Y,Z)\mapsto(YZ,XZ,XY)
. Therefore,
\mathcal{K}
is an irreducible plane curve which is left invariant under a nonlinear
\mbox{\rm PGL}(2,q)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The subgroup
G\cap\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
has order
2q(q-1)
.

Proof.

Let
L=\{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}|\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\mu\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}\}
. Then
L
is a subgroup of
H
of order
q(q-1)
. In particular,
L
is a maximal subgroup of
H
. Therefore, the elements of
H
other than those in
L
are not in
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
. Since
\beta_{4}\in\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
and each element of
G
is a product of
\beta_{4}
and element from
H
, the claim follows.

6.
nonclassicality of
G
-invariant irreducible plane curves

Within the scope of the Stöhr-Voloch theory, a fundamental question concerns the nonclassicality or Frobenius nonclassicality of a given curve.
Curves possessing such features are somewhat rare, but their characterization is relevant due to applications in many areas, such as coding theory and finite geometry.
The Frobenius nonclassical property in some of our G-invariant curves have already been established in the literature. For instance, it is well know that the Hermitian curve over

\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is Frobenius nonclassical and that the DGZ curve is an example of a double-Frobenius nonclassical curve.

In this section we present new Frobenius nonclassical curves by characterizing all such curves among those
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
of degree
d<q^{3}(q^{2}-1)

that are
\mbox{\rm PGU}(3,q)
-invariant. More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq{\rm{PG}}(2,K)
be the
\mathrm{PGU}(3,q)
-invariant curve

Y^{q^{3}}Z+YZ^{q^{3}}-X^{q^{3}+1}-\lambda\left(Y^{q}Z+YZ^{q}-X^{q+1}\right)^{q^{2}-q+1}=0,\quad\lambda\in K\backslash\{1\}.

Then
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\prime}}
-Frobenius nonclassical if and only if either
q^{\prime}=q^{6}
and
\lambda=0
, or

q^{\prime}=q^{4}
and
\lambda^{q+1}=1
.
In the latter case, the curve
\mathcal{C}
has exactly
\left(q^{3}+1\right)\left(q^{4}-q^{3}+1\right)
rational points over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
.

Proof.

Let us set
F(X,Y,Z)=Y^{q}Z+YZ^{q}-X^{q+1}
,
G(X,Y,Z)=Y^{q^{3}}Z+YZ^{q^{3}}-X^{q^{3}+1}
, and
V(X,Y,Z)=G(X,Y,Z)-\lambda F(X,Y,Z)^{q^{2}-q+1}
.
Considering the partial derivatives of
V
, the polynomials
U_{1},U_{2}
, and
U_{3}
, defined as follows

(12)

\begin{cases}V_{X}=-X^{q^{3}}+\lambda F^{q^{2}-q}X^{q}=U_{1}^{q}\\
V_{Y}=Z^{q^{3}}-\lambda F^{q^{2}-q}Z^{q}=U_{2}^{q}\\
V_{Z}=Y^{q^{3}}-\lambda F^{q^{2}-q}Y^{q}=U_{3}^{q},\end{cases}

are such that
U_{1}^{q}X+U_{2}^{q}Y+U_{3}^{q}Z=V
. Thus, from (
1
) and (
2
) in Section 2, the curve
\mathcal{C}
is nonclassical, and it is

\mathbb{F}_{q^{\prime}}
-Frobenius nonclassical if and only if

(13)

U_{1}X^{q^{\prime}/q}+U_{2}Y^{q^{\prime}/q}+U_{3}Z^{q^{\prime}/q}\equiv 0\pmod{V}.

Note that if
\lambda=0
, then
\mathcal{C}
is the curve
Y^{q^{3}}Z+YZ^{q^{3}}-X^{q^{3}+1}=0
, which is clearly
\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
-Frobenius nonclassical.
Let us assume that
\lambda\neq 0
and
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{\prime}}
-Frobenius nonclassical. Then
[
22
, Theorem 8.77]
gives
\sqrt{q^{\prime}}+1\leq q^{3}+1\leq\frac{q^{\prime}-1}{q-1}
, that is,
q^{4}-q^{3}+q\leq q^{\prime}\leq q^{6}
. It is clear that
q^{\prime}>q^{3}
, and we claim that
q^{\prime}<q^{6}
. Indeed, since
\mathcal{C}
is nonsingular of degree
d=q^{3}+1
, if

q^{\prime}=q^{6}
, then
d=\sqrt{q^{\prime}}+1
and
[
6
, Corollary 3.2]
imply that
\mathcal{C}
is projectively equivalent to the curve
X^{q^{3}+1}+Y^{q^{3}+1}+Z^{q^{3}+1}=0
. This is clearly impossible, as the partial derivatives of
V(X,Y,Z)
in (
12
) are not
q^{3}
powers of linear forms. Hence

q^{3}<q^{\prime}<q^{6}
. In addition, note that (
12
) also yields

(14)

U_{1}X^{q^{\prime}/q}+U_{2}Y^{q^{\prime}/q}+U_{3}Z^{q^{\prime}/q}\equiv(YZ^{q^{\prime}/q^{3}}+ZY^{q^{\prime}/q^{3}}-X^{q^{\prime}/q^{3}+1})^{q^{2}}\pmod{F}.

Thus, since the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
-rational points of
F=Y^{q}Z+YZ^{q}-X^{q+1}=0
are points of the curve
\mathcal{C}
, it follows from (
13
) and (
14
) that all such
q^{3}+1
points lie on the curve

YZ^{q^{\prime}/q^{3}}+ZY^{q^{\prime}/q^{3}}-X^{q^{\prime}/q^{3}+1}=0,

and then
q^{3}<q^{\prime}<q^{6}
implies
q^{\prime}=q^{4}
. Now using (
12
) and (
13
), a direct computation gives
F^{q^{3}}(1-\lambda^{q+1})\equiv 0\pmod{V}
, and then

\lambda^{q+1}=1
. The converse is straightforward. The number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
-rational points of
\mathcal{C}
follows directly from the Hefez-Voloch formula for nonsingular Frobenius nonclassical curves, see
[
16
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

7.
On the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{i}}
-rational points of the DGZ curve

In this Section, we show how knowledge of the (linear) action of a subgroup of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
preserving a plane curve can be used to count its points in
{\rm{PG}}(2,q^{i})
for
i\geq 1
. For this purpose,
we refer to the DGZ curve
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}
whose number
N_{i}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{i}}
-rational points has already been computed in
[
5
,
14
]
for
i=1,2,3
. More precisely,
N_{1}=0,N_{2}=q^{4}-q
, and
N_{3}=q^{6}-q^{5}-q^{4}+q^{3}
.
Our result is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 7.1
.

N_{i}=\begin{cases}q^{4}-q,&i=4,\\
0,&i=5,\\
q^{6}-q^{5}+q^{3}-q,&i=6.\end{cases}

In particular, the DGZ curve has no
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
-rational point other than its
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-rational points, and no
\mathbb{F}_{q^{6}}
-rational point other than its
\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-rational points.

Proof.

Since
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{F}_{3,1})|=|\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)|>84(\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{F}_{3,1})-1)
, by
[
22
, Theorem 11.56]
,
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
has at most three short orbits on
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}
. In
[
14
]
, it is actually shown that there are exactly two
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
-short orbits on
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}
, one tame, which coincides with
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{3}})
and one non-tame, which coincides with
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}})
.

We begin with the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
-rational points. Let
P\in\mathcal{F}_{3,1}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}}
-rational point of
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}
which is not
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
-rational. Then the orbit
\Omega
of
P
under
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
is long, that is to say,

(15)

|\mathcal{F}_{3,1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}})|\geq q^{4}-q+q^{3}(q^{2}+q+1)(q-1)^{2}(q+1).

From the Hasse-Weil Bound,

(16)

|\mathcal{F}_{3,1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{4}})|\leq q^{4}+1+2({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}q(q-1)(q^{3}-2q-2)+1)q^{2}.

By comparing Inequalities (
15
) and (
16
), a computation shows

q^{8}-q^{6}-q^{5}+q^{4}+q^{3}-q<q^{7}-q^{6}-2q^{5}+q^{4}+2q^{3}+2q^{2}+1,

which holds true only for
-1<q<1.38552
, a contradiction.

Arguing in a similar way as in the case
i=4
, we see that
\mathcal{F}_{3,1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{5}})
is a union of
\mbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
-long orbits, hence

(17)

|\mathcal{F}_{3,1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{5}})|\geq q^{3}(q^{2}+q+1)(q-1)^{2}(q+1).

This time, the Hasse-Weil Bound reads

(18)

|\mathcal{F}_{3,1}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{5}})|\leq q^{5}+1+2({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}q(q-1)(q^{3}-2q-2)+1)q^{5/2}.

Since the lower bound in (
17
) is smaller than the upper bound (
18
) (assuming
q
positive) only for
q<1,53095
, we get a contradiction.

The proof for the case
i=6
is analogous to the case
i=4
.
∎

Lemma 8.6

-subgroups of

-subgroups of

-subgroup

properly containing

still fixes both points

, and that its normalizer

in

and
\Phi

For this purpose, two cases are treated separately according as the center

of

with

is contained in

and

If

is an admissible choice for

is replaced by

fixes
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. Now, define

to be a

-subgroup of

whose order is maximal with respect to the following two properties:

and

Since

. Furthermore,

is uniquely determined. In fact, since all elements in

whose order is a power of

are contained in a unique

-subgroup, the

-subgroups of

with

. Furthermore, since

is uniquely determined, and

fixes both

and

. Since

interchanges

To finish the proof, we use an idea from the proof of

. Let

be the quotient curve of

by

its function field. Since

,

has a subgroup

of order

isomorphic to

fixes the point of

lying under

. Moreover,

is not rational, as

is abelian but non-cyclic. Therefore,

. Then

the Riemann-Hurwitz genus formula applied to

\begin{array}[]{lll}(4\mathfrak{g}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})+2)q\geq|\bar{U}||Q|=2p^{r}q(q-1)=2p^{r}(q^{2}-q-2)+4p^{r}=2p^{r}(\mathfrak{g}-1)+4p^{r}\geq\\

whence

8.5
together with Lemma
8.9
and the Riemann-Hurwitz formula applied to

give that
\mathcal{X}/T
has genus

.
Let
F_{0}=F(u,v)
be the subfield of

which is generated by
u=x^{q}-x
and
v=x-y
. Then
[F:F_{0}]=q
and

is the fixed field of

. Furthermore,

\begin{array}[]{lll}(x+y)^{q+1}-2((xy)^{q}+xy)-\lambda(x-y)^{q+1}=(2x-v)^{q+1}-2(x^{q}(x-v)^{q}+x(x-v)-\lambda v^{q+1}=\\

whence, with

Remark 8.14
.

As
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
has genus
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}=q-2
, its equation (
21
) is
W^{2}=V^{2\bar{\mathfrak{g}}+2}+\tau V^{\bar{\mathfrak{g}}+1}+1
with
\tau=2\lambda
. It should be noticed that the already quoted paper of Miller
[
28
]
also concerned the Hasse-Witt matrix of hyperelliptic function fields of equation
w^{2}=v^{2\bar{\mathfrak{g}}+2}+\tau v^{\bar{\mathfrak{g}}+1}+1
. Unfortunately, his claim stating invertibility apart from finitely many values of
\tau
, does not apply to
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}
since that claim,
[
28
, Proposition 2]
, was proven under the extra-condition that
p\mid\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
whereas
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}=q-2
in our case. Thus, it remains open the problem of finding values of
\lambda
for which the curves
\mathcal{X}_{\lambda}
is ordinary.

We just need to consider the case
q=3
; in this case, we prove the following.

Theorem 8.15
.

Theorem
8.12
holds true for
q=3
, with a unique exception,
\lambda=0
, in which case
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})\cong SmallGroup(192,181)
.

Proof.

We use some ideas and results from
[
17
]
and
[
3
]
. From Proposition
8.5
,
\mathcal{X}
is a genus
5
curve. Assume that
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
is larger than
H\times\langle\Phi\rangle
. As we have pointed out at the beginning of the proof of Theorem

8.12
,
H\times\langle\Phi\rangle
is not a normal subgroup of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
. Therefore,
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|=d|H\times\langle\Phi\rangle|=48d
with
d\geq 4
. In particular,
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|>48=12(\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X})-1)
and hence
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
has at most two tame short orbits.
Let
u>3
be a prime dividing
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|
, and take a subgroup
U
of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
of order
u
. Since
2\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X})-2=8
and
\rm{g.c.d.}(u,8)=1
, the Riemann Hurwitz formula applied to
U
shows that
U
has a fixed point on
\mathcal{X}
. From Lemma
8.10
, any fixed point
P
of
U
is in a tame short orbit
\mathcal{O}
of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
. By Lemma
8.11
, some point in a tame short orbit
\mathcal{Q}
is fixed by an involution
J\in H\times\langle\Phi\rangle
. By the Riemann-Hurwitz formula,
J
has either
4
, or
8
, or
12
fixed points. The latter case can be discarded since happens as it would imply that
\mathcal{X}
is hyperelliptic, which is not the case. If
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{Q}
then the stabilizer of
P
has a cyclic subgroup of order
2u
containing
U
. Therefore,
J
centralizes
U
. Otherwise,
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are the two tame short orbits of
\mathcal{X}
, and
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|\leq 84(\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X})-1)=366
by the proof of case (III) of
[
22
, Theorem 11.56]
. In the latter case,
d\leq 7
.

From
[
17
, Theorem 1, Theorem 2(c)]
, either
u=5
, or
u=11
. We investigate these possibilities separately.

If
u=11
, then
[
3
, Theorem 10]
yields that
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|=11\,c
, for
c\in\{1,2,3\}
but this contradicts that
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|\geq 48
.

If
u=5
, then
[
3
, Proposition 11]
shows that
U
fixes two points on
\mathcal{X}
and that
\mathcal{X}/U
is elliptic.
Assume that
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
has a unique tame short orbit.
Since at least one fixed point of
U
is also fixed by
J
, it turns out that
J
has as many as
2+5m
fixed points on
\mathcal{X}
where
m\geq 0
. But then
J
has either
2
, or
7
, or
12
fixed points, a contradiction.
Therefore,
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
has two short orbits, say
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{Q}
. Then
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|\leq 84(\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{X})-1)=366
implies
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|=240
, and the Riemann-Hurwitz formula applied to
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
reads
8=-480+280+(240-|\mathcal{O}|)+(240-|\mathcal{Q}|),

whence
|\mathcal{O}|+|\mathcal{Q}|=272
. But this contradicts
|\mathcal{O}|+|\mathcal{Q}|\leq 120+48
.

We may assume
|\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})|=192
. Take a point
P\in\mathcal{X}
fixed by the subgroup
T
of
H
order
3
. From Lemma
8.10
, the normalizer of
T
has order
12
and its center has order
2
. Therefore,

(i)

the normalizer of a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
is a dihedral group of order
12
.

Moreover, the subgroup
H\times\langle\Phi\rangle
of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
is not normal. In fact, otherwise, all Sylow
3
-subgroups of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
would be contained in
H
, and hence their number would be four. On the other hand, from (i), that number is equal to
192/12=16
.

(ii)

\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
contains a non-normal subgroup isomorphic to
\mbox{\rm PGL}(2,3)\times C_{2}
.

Let
S_{2}
be a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
. Then
S_{2}
has order
64
. From
[
1
, Remark 3.5]
,
S_{2}
has three orbits on
\mathcal{X}
and they have lengths
32,16
and
8
respectively. Therefore, if
P
is a point in the
S_{2}
-orbit of length
8
, then the stabilizer of
P
in

is a cyclic group of order
8
. Therefore,

(iii)

The exponent of
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
is at least
8
.

By an exhaustive search supported by the MAGMA algebra system, there exist, up to isomorphisms, five groups of order

satisfying the above three properties, each but one has center of order
2
. The exception is named
SmallGroup(192,956)
, and it has trivial center. We show that the exceptional case cannot actually occur in our case. Assume on the contrary
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})\cong SmallGroup(192,956)
. The MAGMA database shows that
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{X})
has a unique normal subgroup

of order

, namely

-subgroups of

∎

Remark 8.16
.

We point out a connection between
\mathcal{C}_{0}
, i.e. the irreducible plane sextic of affine equation
(X+Y)^{4}-2((XY)^{3}+XY)=0,
and the well known genus
5
complex curve
W_{192}
of Wiman of
affine equation
Y^{4}-X^{2}(X^{4}-1)=0
, see
[
34
,
19
,
20
]
. The notoriety of
W_{192}
stems from its property of attaining the maximum number of automorphisms that a genus
5
curve can possess.
Actually, in characteristic
3
, the above equation still defines a nonsingular curve of genus
5
whose automorphism group is isomorphic to that of
W_{192}
. A MAGMA computation shows that this curve is isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{0}
over
\mathbb{F}_{81}
. It should be stressed however that the classical approach used in
[
34
]
, as well as its variants and other approaches also depending on tools from modern algebraic geometry
[
8
,
9
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
23
]
, do not seem plausible to extend straightforwardly to our case mostly due to the fact that
\mbox{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{C}_{0})
is non-tame in characteristic
3
, and hence the different in the Hurwitz Riemann formula changes passing from characteristic zero to characteristic
3
. It would be interesting to study the existence of some more genus
5
curves in characteristic
3
with
192
automorphisms.

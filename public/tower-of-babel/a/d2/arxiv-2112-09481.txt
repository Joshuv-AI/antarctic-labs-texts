Abstract
. Let p ⁡ ( n ) p(n) be the ordinary partition function. In the 1960s Atkin found a number of examples of congruences of the form p ⁡ ( Q 3 ​ ℓ ​ n + β ) ≡ 0 ( mod ℓ ) p(Q^{3}\ell n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell} where ℓ \ell and Q Q are prime and 5 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31 5\leq\ell\leq 31 ; these lie in two natural families distinguished by the square class of 1 − 24 ​ β ( mod ℓ ) 1-24\beta\pmod{\ell} . In recent decades much work has been done to understand congruences of the form p ⁡ ( Q m ​ ℓ ​ n + β ) ≡ 0 ( mod ℓ ) p(Q^{m}\ell n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell} . It is now known that there are many such congruences when m ≥ 4 m\geq 4 , that such congruences are scarce (if they exist at all) when m = 1 , 2 m=1,2 , and that for m = 0 m=0 such congruences exist only when ℓ = 5 , 7 , 11 \ell=5,7,11 . For congruences like Atkin’s (when m = 3 m=3 ), more examples have been found for 5 ≤ ℓ ≤ 31 5\leq\ell\leq 31 but little else seems to be known. Here we use the theory of modular Galois representations to prove that for every prime ℓ ≥ 5 \ell\geq 5 , there are infinitely many congruences like Atkin’s in the first natural family which he discovered and that for at least 17 / 24 17/24 of the primes ℓ \ell there are infinitely many congruences in the second family.

1.
Introduction

The partition function
p(n)
counts the number of ways to write the positive integer
n
as the sum of a non-increasing sequence of positive integers
(by convention we agree that
p(0)=1
and that
p(n)=0
if
n\not\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}
).
The study of the arithmetic properties of
p(n)
has a long and rich history; interest in this topic stems not only from the fact that
p(n)
is a fundamental function in additive number theory and combinatorics, but also from the fact that its generating function is a modular form of weight
-\frac{1}{2}
on the full modular group.

The most famous examples of arithmetic phenomena for the partition function are the Ramanujan congruences

(1.1)

\displaystyle p(\ell n+\beta_{\ell})\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{for }\ell=5,7,11\text{,}

where
\beta_{\ell}:=\frac{1}{24}\pmod{\ell}
.
Extensions of these results for arbitrary powers of
5,7,11
were conjectured and proved by Ramanujan, Watson and Atkin

[
Ram19
,
Ram20
,
Ram21
,
Wat38
,
Atk67
]
.
On the other hand, after the work of the first author and Boylan
[
AB03
]
it is known that there are no congruences of the form (
1.1
)
with
\ell\geq 13
.

Further examples of congruences for primes
\ell\leq 31
were found by Newman, Atkin, and O’Brien
[
New57
,
AO67
,
Atk68
]
.
These examples take the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle p(Q^{m}\ell n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\text{,}

where
Q
is a prime distinct from
\ell
, and
m=3
or
4
.

Many years later, Ono
[
Ono00
]
showed that for every
\ell\geq 5
, there are infinitely many primes
Q
for which we have a congruence (
1.2
) with
m=4
. After the work of the first author and Ono
[
AO01a
]
, we have the following (see
[
AO01b
, Thm. 1]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and that
\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)\in\{0,-1\}
.
Then a positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv-1\pmod{\ell}
have the property that

(1.3)

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{3}n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\qquad\text{if $Q\nmid n$ \ and \ $n\equiv 1-24\beta\pmod{\ell}$.}

For any such
\beta
, selecting
n
in one of
Q-1
residue classes modulo
Q
gives a congruence of the form

(1.4)

\displaystyle p(Q^{4}\ell n+\beta^{\prime})\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}

with
\left(\frac{1-24\beta^{\prime}}{\ell}\right)=\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)
.
Radu
[
Rad13
]
confirmed a conjecture of the first author and Ono by proving that if there is a congruence

\displaystyle p(mn+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{with }\ell\geq 5\ \ \text{prime}\text{,}

then
\ell\mid m
and
\left(\frac{1-24\beta}{\ell}\right)\in\{0,-1\}
.

After this discussion we know that there are many congruences of the form (
1.2
) with
m\geq 4
and no congruences other than
(
1.1
) with
m=0
. It therefore becomes natural to ask about the existence of such congruences when

m=1,2,3
.

Recent work of the first author, Beckwith and Raum
[
ABR
]
has shown that for
m=1
and
m=2
, and for any prime
\ell\geq 5
, congruences of this form
(if they exist at all) are extremely scarce in a precise sense. Since the main theorems of that paper require some notation to state, we mention here only
Corollary 1.2:
If
17\leq\ell<10000
is prime, and
S
is the set of primes
Q
for which there is a congruence

\displaystyle p(Q\ell n+\beta)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell},

then
S
has density zero.

This leaves open only the case
m=3
, which is the focus of this paper.
In this case, Atkin
[
Atk68
]
discovered many congruences of the form

(1.5)

\displaystyle p\left(Q^{3}\ell n+\beta\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}

for small primes
\ell
. These arise from two families which we describe in detail.

Let
13\leq\ell\leq 31
be prime.
Atkin
[
Atk68
, eq. (52)]
gave examples of primes
Q
such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{2}\ell n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{if}\ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon_{Q}

for some
\varepsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Fixing
n
in one of the allowable residue classes modulo
Q
produces a congruence of the form (
1.5
).
For these small values of
\ell
, the relevant generating functions are eigenforms of the Hecke operators,
and Atkin’s method relies on finding what he calls “accidental” eigenvalues (Atkin works with modular functions rather than modular forms, but the effect is the same).
We will say that congruences (
1.6
) are of type “Atkin I.”

Later, Weaver
[
Wea01
]
found more accidental eigenvalues for these primes (as well as more examples of congruences (
1.3
) with
\ell\mid n
).
As an application of his performant algorithm to compute large values of the partition function, Johansson
[
Joh12
]

extended this list substantially; there are now more than
22
billion examples for primes
\ell\leq 31
.

For each of
\ell=5,7
and
13
, Atkin
[
Atk68
, Thm. 1, 2]
showed that if
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
then

(1.7)

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{2}n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{if}\ \ \left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=-1\ \ \text{and}\ \ \left(\frac{-n}{Q}\right)=-1.

We will say that congruences (
1.7
) are of type “Atkin II.” To the authors’ knowledge, no examples of such congruences are known for
\ell\geq 13
. After this discussion there are two natural questions:

(1)

Are there congruences of type Atkin I for primes
\ell\geq 31
?

(2)

Are there congruences of type Atkin II for primes
\ell\geq 13
?

We will refer to these congruences simply as “Type I” and “Type II” in what follows.

Remark
.

Once
\ell\geq 37
the spaces of modular forms which are relevant for congruences of Type I are no longer one-dimensional. However, one may still perform a search for accidents in the sense of Atkin.
For example, when
\ell=37
, the relevant space is two-dimensional.
A computation of the Hecke eigenvalues of the two newforms in this space for
Q<10000
yields three “accidents”:
there are three primes
Q
for which the
Q
th eigenvalue of each newform lies in the required residue class modulo a prime above
\ell
in the field generated by its coefficients.
In particular we have a congruence (
1.6
) when
Q=6599
,
7541
, and
9547
. For example,

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{6599^{2}\cdot 37n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{37}\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ \ \left(\frac{n}{6599}\right)=-1,

which leads to
3299
congruences modulo
37
of the form (
1.5
) with
m=3
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{7541^{2}\cdot 37n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{37}\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ \ \left(\frac{n}{7541}\right)=1.

Our goal in this paper is to prove that there are many congruences of the types which Atkin discovered. In particular we will prove the following theorems.
(Note that for
\ell=5,7,11
, the statements about congruences of Type I are trivially true in view of (
1.1
).)

The first result shows that congruences of Type I hold for every prime
\ell

(an explicit description of what is meant by “positive proportion” is given at the end of the Introduction).

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime.
Then a positive proportion of the primes
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
have the property that

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{2}\ell n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\qquad\text{if}\qquad\left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{\frac{\ell-3}{2}}.

The second result shows that for many primes
\ell
we have congruences of Types I and II involving primes
Q
in a different residue class modulo
\ell
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, and that

(1.8)

there exists an integer
a
with
2^{a}\equiv-1\pmod{\ell}
.

Then

(1)

A positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
have the property that for some
\varepsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
, we have

(1.9)

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{2}\ell n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{if}\ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon_{Q}.

(2)

A positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
have the property that

(1.10)

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{2}n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{if}\ \ \left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=-1\ \ \text{and}\ \ \left(\frac{-n}{Q}\right)=-1.

Remark
.

By a result of Hasse
[
Has66
]
, the proportion of primes
\ell
for which (
1.8
) is satisfied is
17/24\approx.708
.

Finally, we prove an analogous result under a similar assumption at the prime
3
, although the situation here is slightly more complicated.
We use the notation
S_{k}^{\rm new}\left(6,\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\right)
to denote the new subspace of modular forms of integral weight
k
on

\Gamma_{0}(6)
with eigenvalues
\varepsilon_{2}
and
\varepsilon_{3}
under the Atkin-Lehner involutions
W_{2}
and
W_{3}
(see the next section for details).

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime, and that

(1.11)

there exists an integer
a
with
3^{a}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
.

Suppose further that
there is no congruence modulo any prime above
\ell
between distinct newforms in
S_{\ell-3}^{\rm new}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{-\ell}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{-\ell}\right)\right)
.
Then
a positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
have the property that for some
\varepsilon_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}
, we have

(1.12)

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{2}\ell n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{if}\ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\varepsilon_{Q}.

If (
1.11
) holds and there is no congruence modulo any prime above
\ell
between distinct newforms in
S_{\ell^{2}-3}^{\rm new}\left(6,-1,-1\right)

then a positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
have the property that

(1.13)

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{2}n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{if}\ \ \left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=-1\ \ \text{and}\ \ \left(\frac{-n}{Q}\right)=-1.

Remark
.

Approximately
82.8\%
of the primes
\ell<100000
satisfy either (
1.8
) or (
1.11
).
For those which satisfy
(
1.11
)
but not (
1.8
) the additional hypothesis that there is no congruence between newforms is required
due to a technical issue which is described in the last section.
One expects that this condition should almost always be satisfied, and a computation shows that there
is no congruence modulo any prime above
\ell
between distinct newforms in
S_{\ell-3}^{\rm new}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{-\ell}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{-\ell}\right)\right)
for any

\ell<150
.

However it seems very difficult to remove this condition. For example when
\ell=71
, there is such a congruence between two newforms in the space

S_{\ell-3}^{\rm new}\left(6,\left(\frac{8}{-\ell}\right),\left(\frac{12}{-\ell}\right)\right)
.
In particular, this space contains a Galois orbit consisting of three newforms defined over a field which is ramified at
71
,
and this orbit gives rise to only two distinct reductions modulo the prime above
71
.

To prove the existence of congruences (
1.4
), one must find primes
Q
for which the Hecke operator of index
Q

annihilates a suitable space of modular forms modulo some power of
\ell
; the existence of such primes is guaranteed by a result of Serre

[
Ser76
, ex. 6.4]
. This approach extends to congruences modulo powers of
\ell

[
Ahl00
,
AO01a
]
, and by work of
Treneer
[
Tre06
,
Tre08
]
to a wide class of weakly holomorphic modular forms.
The current situation is more delicate; we need to find primes
Q
for which the
Hecke operator acts diagonally with a specified eigenvalue, which entails a much more careful study of the Galois representations which arise.
In recent work, Raum
[
Rau21
, Thm. E]
has considered the converse question; given the existence of such a congruence as (
1.4
) or (
1.5
) he deduces information about the generalized Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{Q}
on a natural subspace of modular forms.

The Galois theoretic results which we prove may be of independent interest.
The following theorem is the main theoretical input in the proof of Theorem
1.2

(here
S_{k}^{\rm new}(6)
denotes the new subspace of cusp forms on
\Gamma_{0}(6)
whose coefficients are integral at all primes above
\ell
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
m
is a positive integer and that
\ell\geq 5
is prime.
Then a positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{m}}
have the following property: for every

g\in S_{\ell-3}^{\rm new}\left(6\right)
we have

\displaystyle g\big|T(Q)\equiv g\pmod{\ell^{m}}.

Above and in what follows, the positive proportion of primes appearing in our theorems are
\{2,3,\ell\}
-frobenian in the sense of
[
Ser12
, §3.3]
(we do not prove that the set of all primes satisfying the conclusions of our theorems is frobenian, just that it contains a frobenian subset).
This means that there is a finite Galois extension
E/\mathbb{Q}
unramified outside of
\{2,3,\ell\}
and a subset
C\subseteq\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb{Q})
that is a union of conjugacy classes such that the conclusion of the theorem holds for any prime
Q
with
\operatorname{Frob}_{Q}\in C
.
We have not attempted to give a lower bound on the size of
\lvert C\rvert/\lvert G\rvert
, hence on the proportion of such primes
Q
, although in principle this is possible.
We note the following subtlety.
We first prove Theorem
1.5
under the further hypothesis that
g
is a newform, where the frobenian set is more transparent, but modulo some possibly higher power
\ell^{m^{\prime}}
depending on how the lattice spanned by newforms sits inside of
S_{\ell-3}^{\rm new}\left(6\right)
(see §
2.3
).
The density of our frobenian set depends on this
m^{\prime}
, hence on the relationship between these two lattices.

In the next section we give some background on modular forms and Galois representations. Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.5
. The proof is technical, and most of the difficulty arises in establishing the result in the case
m=1
.
In Section 4 we use a different argument to prove two analogues of Theorem
1.5
in arbitrary weight; these results
are the main input for the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
. The last section contains the proofs of the three main theorems.

2.
Background

2.1.
Modular forms

If
f
is a function on the upper half-plane,
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, and
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})
,
we define

\displaystyle\left(f\big|_{k}\gamma\right)(\tau):=(\det\gamma)^{\frac{k}{2}}(c\tau+d)^{-k}f(\gamma\tau).

Given
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, a positive integer
N
, a multiplier system
\nu
in weight
k
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and a subring
A\subseteq\mathbb{C}
,
we denote by
M_{k}(N,\nu,A)
,
S_{k}(N,\nu,A)
, and
M_{k}^{!}(N,\nu,A)
the spaces of modular forms, cusp forms, and weakly holomorphic modular forms
of weight
k
and multiplier
\nu
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
whose Fourier coefficients lie in
A
. For basic properties of mutiplier systems and modular forms one may consult for example
[
Kno70
]
and
[
DS05
]
. These forms satisfy the transformation law

\displaystyle f\big|_{k}\gamma=\nu(\gamma)f\ \ \ \text{for}\ \ \ \gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N)

as well as the appropriate cusp conditions (weakly holomorphic forms are allowed poles at the cusps).
If
\nu=1
we omit it from the notation.
If in addition
k
is even, we let
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\mathbb{C})\subseteq S_{k}(N,\mathbb{C})
denote the new subspace, and define

S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,A):=S_{k}(N,A)\cap S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\mathbb{C})
.

When
N
is square-free, there is an Atkin-Lehner involution
W_{p}
on
S_{k}(N,\mathbb{C})
for every prime divisor
p
of
N

[
AL70
]
. Given a
tuple
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{p})_{p\mid N}
where each
\varepsilon_{p}\in\{\pm 1\}
, let
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\mathbb{C},\varepsilon)
be the subspace consisting of those forms
f
for which
f\big|_{k}W_{p}=\varepsilon_{p}f
for each prime
p\mid N
.

Throughout,
\ell\geq 5
will denote a fixed prime number.
When
A
is the subring of algebraic numbers that are integral at all primes above
\ell
, we omit it from the notation and simply write
M_{k}(N,\nu)
,
S_{k}(N,\nu)
,
M_{k}^{!}(N,\nu)
, and
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
.
If in addition
\ell\nmid N
, then we write
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\varepsilon)
for the subspace of
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
attached to the tuple
\varepsilon
(this makes sense since for such
\ell
, each
involution
W_{p}
acts on
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
.)

The Dedekind eta function is defined by

\displaystyle\eta(\tau):=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n}),\qquad q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}.

Then the eta-multiplier
\nu_{\eta}
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\eta(\gamma\tau)=\nu_{\eta}(\gamma)(c\tau+d)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\eta(\tau),\qquad\gamma=\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{matrix}\right)\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\text{.}

We collect some facts about modular forms which transform with a power of the eta-multiplier. Proofs for some of the non-obvious facts can be found
in Section 2 of
[
ABR
]
.
If
f\in M_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)
,
then
\eta^{-r}f\in M^{!}_{k-\frac{r}{2}}(1)
.
It follows that
f
has a Fourier expansion of the form

(2.2)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{n\equiv r\,(24)}a(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\text{.}

By (2.6) of
[
ABR
]
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle M_{k}(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r})=\{0\}\ \ \text{unless}\ \ r\equiv 2k\pmod{4}.

Let
\nu_{\theta}
be the multiplier on
\Gamma_{0}(4)
attached to the theta function
\theta(\tau)=\sum q^{n^{2}}

and define
f(\tau)\big|V_{d}:=f(d\tau)
.
By (2.7) of
[
ABR
]
and (
2.3
) we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle f\in M_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)\implies f\big|V_{24}\in M_{k}\left(576,\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right)\nu_{\theta}^{r}\right)=M_{k}\left(576,\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right)\nu_{\theta}^{2k}\right)\text{.}

In particular
f\big|V_{24}
is a modular form of half-integral weight in the sense of Shimura
[
Shi73
]
.

For each prime
Q\geq 5
we have the Hecke operator

\displaystyle T(Q^{2})\,:\,S_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)\rightarrow S_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)\text{.}

If
f\in S_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)
with
(r,24)=1
has Fourier expansion (
2.2
) then we have (see e.g.
[
Yan14
, Proposition 11]
)

(2.5)

\displaystyle f\big|T(Q^{2})=\sum\left(a(Q^{2}n)+Q^{k-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{12n}{Q}\right)a(n)+Q^{2k-2}a\left(\frac{n}{Q^{2}}\right)\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}.

For each squarefree
t
with
(t,6)=1
there is a Shimura lift
\operatorname{Sh}_{t}
on
S_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)
, defined via (
2.4
) and the Shimura lift
[
Shi73
]
on
S_{k}\left(576,\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right)\nu_{\theta}^{2k}\right)
.
The action on Fourier expansions is given by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sh}_{t}\left(\sum a(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\right)=\sum A_{t}(n)q^{n},

where

(2.7)

\displaystyle A_{t}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\left(\frac{-1}{d}\right)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{12t}{d}\right)d^{k-\frac{3}{2}}\,a\left(\frac{tn^{2}}{d^{2}}\right)\text{.}

Then we have (see
[
ABR
, (2.13)]
)

(2.8)

\displaystyle f\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\iff\operatorname{Sh}_{t}(f)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\qquad\text{for all squarefree $t$.}

From work of Yang
[
Yan14
]

it follows that we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sh}_{t}\,:\,S_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r}\right)\longrightarrow S_{2k-1}^{\operatorname{new}}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{r}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{r}\right)\right)\otimes\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right).

Moreover, for all primes
Q\geq 5
we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sh}_{t}\left(f\big|T(Q^{2})\right)=\left(\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f\right)\big|T(Q)\text{,}

where
T(Q)
is the Hecke operator of index
Q
on the integral weight space.

The connection with partitions is given by the fundamental relationship

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta(\tau)}=\sum p\left(\frac{n+1}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}.

For our applications there are
two important modular forms for each
\ell
(see
[
ABR
, §2]
for the construction).
In particular, there is a modular form
f_{\ell}\in S_{\frac{\ell-2}{2}}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{-\ell},\mathbb{Z}\right)
with

(2.11)

\displaystyle f_{\ell}\equiv\sum p\left(\frac{\ell n+1}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

There is also a form
g_{\ell}\in S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-2}{2}}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{-1},\mathbb{Z}\right)
with

(2.12)

\displaystyle g_{\ell}\equiv\sum_{\left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=-1}p\left(\frac{n+1}{24}\right)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\pmod{\ell}.

From the discussion in the Introduction, we have
g_{\ell}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
, and

\displaystyle f_{\ell}\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\iff\ \ell=5,7,11.

From the discussion above, each Shimura lift of
f_{\ell}
is in the space

\displaystyle S_{\ell-3}^{\rm new}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{-\ell}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{-\ell}\right)\right)\otimes\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right),

and each Shimura lift of
g_{\ell}
is in the space

(2.13)

\displaystyle S_{\ell^{2}-3}^{\rm new}\left(6,-1,-1\right)\otimes\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right).

2.2.
Modular Galois representations

Let
k
and
N
be positive integers with
k
even and
N
coprime to
\ell
.
We recall properties of the Galois representations attached to eigenforms in
S_{k}(N)
that will be used in the next section.
We recall that
\ell\geq 5
.

We let
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
in
\mathbb{C}
.
For each prime
p
, we fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and an embedding
\iota_{p}\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
Via
\iota_{p}
, we view
G_{p}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
as a subgroup of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
.
We let
I_{p}\subseteq G_{p}
denote the inertia subgroup and let
\operatorname{Frob}_{p}\in G_{p}/I_{p}
denote the arithmetic Frobenius.
We view the coefficients of any
f\in S_{k}(N)
as elements of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
via
\iota_{\ell}
.

We denote by
\chi\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
(resp.
\omega\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
) the
\ell
-adic (resp. the mod
\ell
) cyclotomic character, and similarly with
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
replaced by
G_{K}
for
K/\mathbb{Q}
finite or by
G_{p}
with
p
a prime, etc.
We let
\omega_{2},\omega^{\prime}_{2}\colon I_{\ell}\to\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}^{\times}
denote Serre’s fundamental characters of level
2

[
Ser87
, §2.1]
.
These are characters of order
\ell^{2}-1
with
\omega_{2}^{\ell}=\omega_{2}^{\prime}
,
\omega_{2}^{\prime\ell}=\omega_{2}
, and
\omega_{2}^{\ell+1}=\omega_{2}^{\prime\ell+1}=\omega
.

The next theorem summarizes some important properties of modular Galois representations, and is due to many people, including Deligne, Fontaine, Langlands, Ribet, and Shimura.
See
[
Hid00
, §3.2.2]
and
[
Edi92
, §2]
for more details and references.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f=q+\sum_{n\geq 2}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(N)
be a normalized Hecke eigenform.
There is a continuous irreducible representation
\rho_{f}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
with semisimple mod
\ell
reduction

\overline{\rho}_{f}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})

satisfying the following properties.

(1)

If
p\nmid\ell N
, then
\rho_{f}
is unramified at
p
and the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is
X^{2}-\iota_{\ell}(a_{p})X+p^{k-1}
. This uniquely characterizes
\rho_{f}
. In particular,
\det\rho_{f}=\chi^{k-1}
.

(2)

If
q\mid N
and
q^{2}\nmid N
, then
\rho_{f}|_{I_{q}}
is unipotent. In particular, the prime-to-
\ell
Artin conductor
N(\overline{\rho}_{f})
of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is not divisible by
q^{2}
.

If further
f
is
q
-new, then
\rho_{f}|_{G_{q}}
is an extension of
\psi
by
\chi\psi
where
\psi\colon G_{q}\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
is the unramified character with
\psi(\operatorname{Frob}_{q})=\iota_{\ell}(a_{q})
.

(3)

Assume that
2\leq k\leq\ell+1
. Then

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is an
\ell
-adic unit,
then
\rho_{f}|_{G_{\ell}}
is reducible and
\rho_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}
is an extension of the trivial character by
\chi^{k-1}
.

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is not an
\ell
-adic unit, then
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{G_{\ell}}
is irreducible and
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}\cong\omega_{2}^{k-1}\oplus\omega_{2}^{\prime(k-1)}
.

Although we have suppressed it from the notation, we note that the representations
\rho_{f}
and
\overline{\rho}_{f}
do depend on the choice of embedding
\iota_{\ell}\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
, at least up to the prime that it determines in the coefficient field
\mathbb{Q}(\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1})\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
f
.

2.3.
Congruences for non-eigenforms

We will ultimately be interested in congruences for cusp forms which are not necessarily eigenforms.
To do so, we record two lemmas which allow us to bootstrap from the case of newforms to the general case.

Let
k
and
N
be positive integers with
k
even and
N
coprime to
\ell
.
Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
and write

(2.14)

\displaystyle f_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i,j}g_{i},

with newforms
g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
and
c_{i,j}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
E
be a number field which contains
the coefficients of each
g_{i}
as well as all of the coefficients
c_{i,j}
.
Fix a prime
\lambda
of
E
over
\ell
and let
e
be its ramification index.
Define

(2.15)

\displaystyle m(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}):=\max(0,-\min(\operatorname{ord}_{\lambda}(c_{i,j}))).

With this notation we have the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let the notation be as above and let
m\geq 1
be an integer.
Assume that there is a prime
Q\nmid N\ell
, an integer
a
, and an integer
r\geq 1
such that

g_{i}\big|T(Q)\equiv ag_{i}\bmod\lambda^{r}
for each
1\leq i\leq d
.
If
r\geq m+m(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
, then for
1\leq j\leq n
we have

\displaystyle f_{j}\big|T(Q)\equiv af_{j}\mod\lambda^{m}.

If further
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\mathbb{Z})
and
r\geq em+m(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
then for
1\leq j\leq n
we have

\displaystyle f_{j}\big|T(Q)\equiv af_{j}\pmod{\ell^{m}}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the definitions of
m(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
and ramification index.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer.
Let
Q\nmid N\ell
be prime, let
a
be an integer, and let
r\geq 1
be an integer such that
g_{i}\big|T(Q)\equiv ag_{i}\bmod{\lambda^{r}}
for every newform
g\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
.
There is an integer
c\geq 0
, depending on
\ell
,
k
, and
N
but not on
Q
,
a
, or
m
, such that if
r\geq em+c
, then

\displaystyle f\big|T(Q)\equiv af\pmod{\ell^{m}}

for all
f\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
.

Proof.

Let
A\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
be the subring of elements that are integral at all primes above
\ell
(so
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)=S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,A)
).
Let
h_{1},\ldots,h_{d}
generate
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
over
A
and let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
be a basis for
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\mathbb{Z})
(in fact we can take
n=d
).
Since
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\mathbb{Z})
generates
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, we can write

(2.16)

\displaystyle h_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i,j}f_{i},

with
d_{i,j}\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Choose
c_{0}\geq 0
such that
\ell^{c_{0}}d_{i,j}\in A
for each
i,j
.
To show that
f\big|T(Q)\equiv af\pmod{\ell^{m}}
for all
f\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
, it suffices to show that
f_{i}\big|T(Q)\equiv af_{i}\pmod{\ell^{m+c_{0}}}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
.
Setting
c=ec_{0}+m(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
with
m(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
as in (
2.15
), the lemma now follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Remark
.

When
N
is squarefree, the same result as Lemma
2.3
holds, replacing
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N)
with
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}(N,\varepsilon)
.

3.
Congruences in low weight

In this section, we use Galois representations together with some group theoretic arguments to prove Theorem
1.5
, which implies Theorem
1.2
.
The proof is by induction with the bulk of the work devoted to proving the base case, which is essentially the combination of Propositions
3.3
and
3.8
.
A key technical lemma is Lemma
3.7
, which roughly states that the mod
\ell
Galois representations associated to sufficiently distinct normalized eigenforms cut out sufficiently disjoint field extensions.
This lemma and its proof are inspired by and similar to that of
[
ACC+
, Lemma 7.1.5(3)]
.

We recall the standing assumption that
\ell\geq 5
is prime.
We again let
k
and
N
be positive integers with
k
even and
N
coprime to
\ell
, and further assume that
N
is squarefree.
Recall that we have a fixed embedding
\iota_{\ell}\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
that allows us to view Fourier coefficients of modular forms in
S_{k}(N)
as elements of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.

The following two lemmas are consequences of Theorem
2.1
and give us information on the image of the mod
\ell
Galois representation associated to a normalized eigenform in
S_{k}(N)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f,g\in S_{k}(N)
be normalized eigenforms such that
\overline{\rho}_{g}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\eta
for some nontrivial continuous character
\eta\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
.
Then

(1)

\eta=\omega^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}
.

(2)

Assume further that
k\leq\ell-1
.
Letting
a_{\ell}
denote the
\ell
-th Fourier coefficient of
f
, we have

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is an
\ell
-adic unit, then
k=\frac{\ell+1}{2}
.

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is not an
\ell
-adic unit, then
k=\frac{\ell+3}{2}
.

Proof.

To prove that
\eta=\omega^{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq\ell-2
, it suffices to show that
\eta
is unramified outside of
\ell
.
Take any prime
p\neq\ell
.
Since we are assuming that
N
is squarefree, parts
1
and
2
of Theorem
2.1
imply that

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{I_{p}}\cong\begin{pmatrix}1&\ast\\
&1\end{pmatrix},

and similarly for
\overline{\rho}_{g}|_{I_{p}}
.
Since
\overline{\rho}_{g}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\eta
, we must have
\eta|_{I_{p}}=1
.
To see that
i=\frac{\ell-1}{2}
, we use that

\displaystyle\det\overline{\rho}_{f}=\omega^{k-1}=\det\overline{\rho}_{g}=\eta^{2}\det\overline{\rho}_{f},

so
\eta=\omega^{i}
is quadratic.

For part
2
, we use part
3
of Theorem
2.1
.
If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is an
\ell
-adic unit, it implies that
\omega^{k-1}=\omega^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}
, so
k=\frac{\ell+1}{2}
.
If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is not an
\ell
-adic unit, it implies that

\displaystyle\omega_{2}^{k-1}=(\omega_{2}^{\prime})^{k-1}\omega^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}=\omega_{2}^{\ell(k-1)+\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{2}}.

Since
\omega_{2}
has order
\ell^{2}-1
, we obtain
k=\frac{\ell+3}{2}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f=q+\sum_{n\geq 2}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(N)
be a normalized eigenform.
Assume that
2\leq k\leq\ell-1
and that there is a prime
q\mid N
such that
f
is
q
-new and
q^{k-1}\not\equiv q^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
.
Then the following are true.

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible.

(2)

Assume there is a quadratic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{G_{K}}
is reducible.
Then
\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\omega^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}
and

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is an
\ell
-adic unit, then
k=\frac{\ell+1}{2}
.

•

If
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is not an
\ell
-adic unit, then
k=\frac{\ell+3}{2}
.

Proof.

To establish part
1
,
assume that
\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{2}
for characters
\psi_{i}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
.
Since
N
is squarefree,
N(\overline{\rho}_{f})\mid N
is also squarefree and for every prime
p\neq\ell
, at most one of
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
is ramified at
p
(see
[
Car89
, §1.1]
, for example).
On the other hand,
\det\overline{\rho}_{f}=\omega^{k-1}
, so
\psi_{1}\psi_{2}
is unramified at all primes
p\neq\ell
.
It follows that
\psi_{1}=\omega^{a}
and
\psi_{2}=\omega^{b}
for some
0\leq a,b\leq\ell-2
.
Reordering if necessary, part
3
of Theorem
2.1
implies that
\psi_{1}=\omega^{k-1}
and
\psi_{2}=1
.
This together with our assumption on
q
contradicts part
2
of Theorem
2.1
.

We turn to part
2
.
Since
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible we must have
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{G_{K}}\cong\psi_{1}\oplus\psi_{2}
for nontrivial characters
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\colon G_{K}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
which are permuted by
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
.
It follows that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is the induction of a character
\psi\colon G_{K}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
and letting
\eta
be the quadratic character of
K/\mathbb{Q}
, that
\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\eta
.
We then apply part
2
of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

We apply the preceding two lemmas to our particular space of interest
S_{\ell-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Recall that
\ell\geq 5
. For any newform
f\in S_{\ell-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
, the image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains a conjugate of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

Proof.

We first note that
S_{2}(6)=\{0\}
, so we may assume that
\ell\geq 7
.

By
[
DDT97
, Theorem 2.47(b)]
, there are four possibilities for the image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
:

(1)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is reducible.

(2)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is dihedral, i.e.
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible but
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{G_{K}}
is reducible for some quadratic
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

(3)

\overline{\rho}_{f}
is exceptional, i.e. the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is conjugate to one of
A_{4}
,
S_{4}
, or
A_{5}
.

(4)

The image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains a conjugate of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

We rule out each of the first three possibilities in turn.

First,
2^{\ell-4}\not\equiv 2^{\pm 1}\pmod{\ell}
for any
\ell\geq 7
, so part
1
of Lemma
3.2
shows that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is irreducible.
If further
\ell\geq 11
, then
\ell-3\neq\frac{\ell+1}{2},\frac{\ell+3}{2}
, so part
2
of Lemma
3.2
shows that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is not dihedral.
For
\ell=7
, the space
S_{4}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
is one-dimensional and spanned by the newform

(3.1)

\displaystyle f=q-2q^{2}-3q^{3}+4q^{4}+6q^{5}+6q^{6}-16q^{7}+\cdots

by
[
LMF20
]
.
If
\overline{\rho}_{f}
were dihedral, then part
2
of Lemma
3.2
would imply that
\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\overline{\rho}_{f}\otimes\omega^{3}
.
Since
\omega^{3}(\operatorname{Frob}_{5})=-1
, this would imply that
\operatorname{tr}\overline{\rho}_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{5})=0
.
But
a_{5}=6\not\equiv 0\pmod{7}
, a contradiction.

It remains to rule out the exceptional case, and for this it suffices to show that the projective image contains an element of order
\geq 6
.
Since the characters
\omega
and
(\omega_{2}/\omega_{2}^{\prime})
have orders
\ell-1
and
\ell+1
, respectively,
the description of
\overline{\rho}_{f}|_{I_{\ell}}
in part
3
of Theorem
2.1
implies that the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
contains an element of order
\frac{\ell-1}{\gcd\{\ell-1,\ell-4\}}
if
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is a unit, and an element of order
\frac{\ell+1}{\gcd\{\ell+1,\ell-4\}}
if
\iota_{\ell}(a_{\ell})
is not a unit.
These are both
\geq 6
if
\ell=11
,
17
, or
\ell\geq 23
.
When
\ell=19
, there are three newforms in
S_{16}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
with LMFDB labels 6.16.a.a, 6.16.a.b, and 6.16.a.c.
The values of
a_{\ell}
for these three newforms are
2163188180
,
4934015444
, and
-5895116260
, respectively.
In each case
a_{\ell}\not\equiv 0\pmod{19}
and
\frac{18}{\gcd\{18,15\}}=6
, so part
3
of Theorem
2.1
again shows that
\overline{\rho}_{f}
cannot be exceptional when
\ell=19
.

For
\ell=7,13
, we can rule out the possibility of exceptional image as follows.
Say we have a newform
f=q+\sum_{n\geq 2}a_{n}q^{n}
in
S_{\ell-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
and a prime
p\nmid 6\ell
such that
a_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Setting
u(p):=a_{p}^{2}/p^{9}
,
if the projective image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}
is
A_{4},S_{4}
, or
A_{5}
, then we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle u(p)\equiv 4,0,1,2\pmod{\ell}\quad\text{or}\quad u(p)^{2}-3u(p)+1\equiv 0\pmod{\ell},

depending on whether the image of
\overline{\rho}_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
in
\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
has order 1,2,3,4, or 5 (see for example
[
Rib85
, p. 189]
).
When
\ell=7
, one can check directly that (
3.2
) is not satisfied when
p=5
for the unique newform
f\in S_{4}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
with Fourier expansion (
3.1
) above.
When
\ell=13
, there is again a unique newform
f\in S_{10}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
and it has Fourier expansion

\displaystyle f=q-16q^{2}+81q^{3}+256q^{4}+2694q^{5}+\cdots

by
[
LMF20
]
.
We can again check directly that (
3.2
) is not satisfied when
p=5
.
∎

Remark
.

The conclusion of Proposition
3.3
does not hold in general for the spaces
S_{\ell^{2}-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
;
this is the main reason that we are able to say more about Type I congruences than those of Type II.

Next we will prove a few group theoretic lemmas (Lemmas
3.5
,
3.6
and
3.7
) leading to Proposition
3.8
.
Before continuing we need to introduce some notation.

Notation
.

Let
G
be a group and let
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.

•

For a homomorphism
\overline{\rho}\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
, we write
{}^{\tau}\overline{\rho}\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
for the composite of
\overline{\rho}
with the automorphism
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
induced by
\tau\colon\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
.

•

Similarly, for a homomorphism
r\colon G\to\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
, we write
{}^{\tau}r\colon G\to\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
for the composite of
r
with the automorphism
\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})\to\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
induced by
\tau\colon\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
.

•

For two homomorphisms
r_{1},r_{2}\colon G\to\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
, we write
r_{1}\cong r_{2}
if they are conjugate by an element of
\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
.

•

For a normalized eigenform
f\in S_{k}(N)
, we let
r_{f}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
be the composite of
\overline{\rho}_{f}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
with the projection
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})\to\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f,g\in S_{k}(N)
be normalized eigenforms.
If
r_{f}\cong r_{g}
and
\overline{\rho}_{f}\not\cong\overline{\rho}_{g}
, then
\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\overline{\rho}_{g}\otimes\omega^{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}
.

Proof.

Conjugating if necessary, we can assume that
r_{f}=r_{g}
.
Then we can define a character
\eta\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}
by
\eta(\sigma)=\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)\overline{\rho}_{g}(\sigma)^{-1}
, and we have
\overline{\rho}_{f}\cong\overline{\rho}_{g}\otimes\eta
.
The lemma now follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

For
i=1,2
, let
\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
be the field of cardinality
q_{i}
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
, with
q_{i}
some power of
\ell
, and let
r_{i}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\PGL_{2}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell})
be a continuous homomorphism with image containing
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
and contained in
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
.
Let
L_{i}
be the subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
fixed by
\ker(r_{i})
and let
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
be the subextension of
L_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}/K_{i})\cong\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

L_{1}\cap L_{2}\not\subseteq K_{1}K_{2}
.

(2)

L_{1}=L_{2}
.

(3)

There is
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
such that
r_{1}\cong{}^{\tau}r_{2}
.

Proof.

First note that for each
i=1,2
,
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
is simple since
\lvert\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}}\rvert\geq 4
(recall that
\ell\geq 5
).

Clearly, (
2
) implies (
1
).
On the other hand, since
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
is simple and is the unique nontrivial proper normal subgroup of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
, if
L_{1}\cap L_{2}\not\subseteq K_{1}K_{2}
, then
L_{1}\subseteq L_{2}
or
L_{2}\subseteq L_{1}
, and
K_{1}=K_{2}
.
In either case, by comparing Jordan–Holder factors, we must have
L_{1}=L_{2}
.

It is immediate that (
3
) implies (
2
).
Conversely, if
L_{1}=L_{2}
, then there is an isomorphism of groups
\varphi\colon r_{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\cong r_{2}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
In particular, this implies that
q_{1}=q_{2}
.
So letting
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}=\mathbb{F}_{q_{2}}
, we have either that
r_{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=r_{2}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\varphi
is an automorphism of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
or that
r_{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=r_{2}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\varphi
is an automorphism of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
The automorphism group of both
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
(see
[
Ste16
, Theorem 30]
), which implies that
r_{1}
is conjugate to
{}^{\tau}r_{2}
for some
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G_{1},\ldots,G_{s}
and
H
be simple nonabelian groups.
Any surjective homomorphism
f\colon\prod_{i=1}^{s}G_{i}\to H
factors through some projection
p_{j}\colon\prod_{i=1}^{s}G_{i}\to G_{j}
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq n\leq s
, let
\iota_{n}\colon G_{n}\to\prod_{i=1}^{s}G_{i}
be the canonical injection.
Assume that
f
does not factor through any
p_{j}
.
Then there are indices
1\leq m\neq n\leq s
such that
f\circ\iota_{m}\colon G_{m}\to H
and
f\circ\iota_{n}\colon G_{n}\to H
are nontrivial.
Since
G_{n}
and
G_{m}
are simple,
f\circ\iota_{m}
and
f\circ\iota_{n}
are injective.
Since
\iota_{m}(G_{m})
and
\iota_{n}(G_{n})
are normal subgroups of
\prod_{i=1}^{s}G_{i}
and
f
is surjective,
f\circ\iota_{m}(G_{m})
and
f\circ\iota_{n}(G_{n})
are normal subgroups of
H
.
We see that
f\circ\iota_{m}
and
f\circ\iota_{n}
are isomorphisms.
Since
H
is nonabelian, we can then choose
x\in G_{m}
and
y\in G_{n}
such that
f\circ\iota_{m}(x)
and
f\circ\iota_{n}(y)
do not commute.
But
\iota_{m}(x)
and
\iota_{n}(y)
commute in
\prod_{i=1}^{s}G_{i}
, which gives a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\in S_{k}(N)
be normalized eigenforms.
For each
1\leq i\leq s
, assume that
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains a conjugate of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
,
and let
M_{i}
be the subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
fixed by
\ker(\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}})
. Then we have the following.

(1)

For each
1\leq i\leq s
, there is an extension
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
of degree at most
2
contained in
M_{i}
and a finite extension
\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
such that
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})})
is conjugate to
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
.

(2)

Assume moreover that for each
1\leq i\neq j\leq s
, there is no
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
such that
r_{f_{i}}\cong{}^{\tau}r_{f_{j}}
.
Let
K_{i}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}}
be as in part
1
, and set
M=M_{1}\cdots M_{s}
and
K=K_{1}\cdots K_{s}
.
Then
\operatorname{Gal}(M(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\prod_{i=1}^{s}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq i\leq s
, since
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains a conjugate of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
,
[
DDT97
, Theorem 2.47(b)]
implies that the image of
r_{f_{i}}
is conjugate to either
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
or
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
for some finite extension
\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
.
Replacing
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}
by a conjugate if necessary, we assume that
r_{f_{i}}
has image either
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
or
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
.
We then let
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
be the extension of degree at most
2
such that
r_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}})=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
, which is a simple group since
\ell\geq 5
.
Then
r_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}}(\zeta_{\ell}))=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
as well, so
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})})
is a subgroup of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}^{\times}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
which contains
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
and has trivial determinant.
It follows that
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})})=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
.

To prove part
2
, we first establish some preliminaries.
For each
1\leq i\leq s
, let
L_{i}
be the subfield of
M_{i}
fixed by
\ker(r_{f_{i}})
.
We claim the following.

(a)

\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
.

(b)

\operatorname{Gal}(M_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
.

(c)

For
1\leq i\neq j\leq s
, the fields
L_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})
and
L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})
are disjoint over
K(\zeta_{\ell})
.

Since
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}/K_{i})\cong\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
is nonabelian and simple and
K(\zeta_{\ell})/K_{i}
is abelian, these extensions are disjoint and
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
, which gives claim
(a)
.
Further, since the unique nontrivial proper quotient of
\operatorname{Gal}(M_{i}K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})/K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
is
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})/K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
, we have

\displaystyle M_{i}K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})\cap K(\zeta_{\ell})\neq K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})\Leftrightarrow L_{i}K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})\cap K(\zeta_{\ell})\neq K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell}).

So
M_{i}K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})
and
K(\zeta_{\ell})
are also disjoint over
K_{i}(\zeta_{\ell})
, which proves claim
(b)
.
We now prove claim
(c)
.
If this were not the case, we would have
L_{i}\subseteq L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})
or
L_{j}\subseteq L_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
L_{i}\subseteq L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})
.
But
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})/\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(L_{j}/\mathbb{Q})\times\operatorname{Gal}(K(\zeta_{\ell})/\mathbb{Q})
has a unique nonabelian Jordan–Holder factor, namely the one isomorphic to
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{j}/K_{j})
.
So
L_{i}\subseteq L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})
implies that
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}L_{j}/\mathbb{Q})
also has a unique nonabelian Jordan–Holder factor, which implies that
L_{i}\cap L_{j}\not\subseteq K_{i}K_{j}
.
By Lemma
3.5
, this contradicts our hypotheses on
r_{f_{i}}
and
r_{f_{j}}
.

To conclude, we prove by induction on
1\leq j\leq s
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}(M_{1}\cdots M_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\prod_{i=1}^{j}\operatorname{Gal}(M_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\prod_{i=1}^{j}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}}).

The
j=1
case follows from claim
(b)
of the previous paragraph.
Now take
1\leq j-1\leq s
, and assume that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Gal}(M_{1}\cdots M_{j-1}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\operatorname{Gal}(M_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}}).

We want to show that
M_{1}\cdots M_{j-1}K(\zeta_{\ell})
and
M_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})
are disjoint over
K(\zeta_{\ell})
.
Assume otherwise.
Since
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{j}})
is the unique nontrivial proper quotient of
\operatorname{Gal}(M_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\cong\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{j}})
, we must then have
L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})\subseteq M_{1}\cdots M_{j-1}K(\zeta_{\ell})
.
Since
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))
is nonabelian simple, the centre of
\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})
maps trivially under the surjective morphism

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})\cong\operatorname{Gal}(M_{1}\cdots M_{j-1}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))\to\operatorname{Gal}(L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell})),

and this map factors through

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{i}})\cong\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell})).

By Lemma
3.6
, this map further factors through some
\operatorname{Gal}(L_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})/K(\zeta_{\ell}))
with
1\leq i\leq j-1
.
But this implies that
L_{j}K(\zeta_{\ell})\subseteq L_{i}K(\zeta_{\ell})
, contradicting claim
(c)
from the previous paragraph.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\in S_{k}(N)
be normalized eigenforms such that for each
1\leq i\leq s
,
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains a conjugate of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.
Then for any
\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, there is an element
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}))
such that
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(\sigma)
is conjugate to
\gamma
for each
1\leq i\leq s
.

Proof.

Say
r_{f_{i}}\cong{}^{\tau}r_{f_{j}}
for some
i\neq j
and
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
.
Then by Lemma
3.4
,
\overline{\rho}_{f_{i}}(\sigma)
is conjugate to
{}^{\tau}\overline{\rho}_{f_{j}}(\sigma)
for any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}))
.
If further
\overline{\rho}_{f_{j}}(\sigma)
is conjugate to
\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
, then
{}^{\tau}\overline{\rho}_{f_{j}}(\sigma)
is conjugate to
\gamma
as well.
We can thus assume that for each
1\leq i\neq j\leq s
, there is no
\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
such that

.
The result now follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

We can now prove Theorem
1.5
, using Propositions
3.3
and

as a base case for induction.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Choose a number field
E
containing all Fourier coefficients of all the newforms in
S_{\ell-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
.
Let
\lambda
be the prime of
E
induced by our fixed embedding
\iota_{\ell}\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
.
Let
E_{\lambda}
be the completion of
E
at
\lambda
, let
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
be its ring of integers, and let
\mathbb{F}=\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda
be the residue field.
Then for any newform
f\in S_{\ell-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
, the Galois representations
\rho_{f}
and
\overline{\rho}_{f}
of Theorem
2.1
can be defined over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{F}
, respectively.
By Lemma
2.3
, there is an integer
r\geq m
such that it suffices to show that there is a positive density set of primes
Q
with
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{m}}
and
f\big|T(Q)\equiv f\pmod{\lambda^{r}}
for all newforms
f\in S_{\ell-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
.

By Propositions
3.3
and
3.8
, we can find an element
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell}))
such that
\overline{\rho}_{f}(\sigma)
is conjugate to
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
for any newform
f\in S_{\ell-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
.
In particular,
\rho_{f}(\sigma)
has characteristic polynomial congruent to
x^{2}-x+1\pmod{\lambda}
. Enlarging
E
if necessary, we can assume that
x^{2}-x+1
factors in
\mathbb{F}
and its roots are the two primitive 6-th roots of unity, which we denote by
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
. Since
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
are distinct,
we can factor the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\sigma)
over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
by Hensel’s lemma, and
\rho_{f}(\sigma)
is conjugate to a diagonal matrix with entries
\alpha,\beta
such that

\alpha\equiv\xi\pmod{\lambda}
and
\beta\equiv\xi^{\prime}\pmod{\lambda}
. Letting
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
again denote the primitive 6-th roots of unity in
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
, we write

\alpha=\xi\gamma
and
\beta=\xi^{\prime}\delta
with
\gamma,\delta\equiv 1\pmod{\lambda}
. Now
\rho_{f}(\sigma^{\ell^{r-1}})
is conjugate to a diagonal matrix with entries

\alpha^{\ell^{r-1}}=\xi^{\ell^{r-1}}\gamma^{\ell^{r-1}}
and
\beta^{\ell^{r-1}}=\xi^{\prime\ell^{r-1}}\delta^{\ell^{r-1}}

and

are congruent to 1 modulo

(recall that

), and that

. Thus the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{f}(\sigma^{\ell^{r-1}})
is congruent to

modulo
\lambda^{r}
. Also,
\sigma^{\ell^{r-1}}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{\ell^{m}}))
since

has order

and

. Chebotarev’s density theorem implies that for a positive density set of primes
Q
,
\operatorname{Frob}_{Q}
is conjugate to
\sigma^{\ell^{r-1}}
.
For such
Q
, we have
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{m}}
and for any newform
f\in S_{\ell-3}^{\mathrm{new}}(6)
, we have

\displaystyle f\big|T(Q)=(\operatorname{tr}\rho_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{Q}))f\equiv f\pmod{\lambda^{r}}.

The theorem is now proven.
∎

4.
Congruences in arbitrary weight

In this section, we use a different argument to prove two variants of Theorem
1.5
in arbitrary integral weight
k\geq 2
with additional hypotheses on the prime
\ell
.
It will be convenient for us to fix a number field
E
containing all Fourier coefficients of all newforms in
S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(6,\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})
.
Let
\lambda
be the prime of
E
induced by our fixed embedding
\iota_{\ell}\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
and let
e
be the ramification index.

We begin with an elementary lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
a
is an integer with
2^{a}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
, then

2^{\ell^{m-1}(a-1)+1}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell^{m}}
for any
m\geq 1
.

Proof.

We induct on
m
. Suppose that
2^{b}\equiv-1\pmod{\ell^{m}}
, and write

\displaystyle 2^{\ell b}+1=(2^{b}+1)\left((2^{b})^{\ell-1}-(2^{b})^{\ell-2}+\cdots-2^{b}+1\right).

Each summand in the second factor is 1 modulo
\ell
, and there are
\ell
summands, so the second term is divisible by
\ell
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and that there exists an integer
a
for which
2^{a}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
. Let
m
be a natural number and let
\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\in\{\pm 1\}
.
Then a positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell^{m}}
have the following property: for every
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(6,\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})
, we have
f\big|T(Q)\equiv-(-\varepsilon_{2})^{a}Q^{\frac{k-2}{2}}f\pmod{\ell^{m}}
.

Proof.

Let
E_{\lambda}
be the completion of
E
at
\lambda
, let
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
be its ring of integers, and let
\mathbb{F}=\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}/\lambda
be the residue field. Then for any newform
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(6,\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})
, the Galois representations
\rho_{f}
and
\overline{\rho}_{f}
of Theorem
2.1
can be defined over
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
and
\mathbb{F}
, respectively.
By Lemma
2.3
and the remark that follows it, there is an integer
r\geq m
such that it suffices to show there is a positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell^{m}}
with the property that for every newform
f\in S_{k}^{\mathrm{new}}(6,\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3})
, we have
f\big|T(Q)\equiv-(-\varepsilon_{2})^{a}Q^{\frac{k-2}{2}}f\pmod{\lambda^{r}}
.
By Lemma
4.1
,
2^{\ell^{r-1}(a-1)+1}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell^{r}}
.
Since
a
and
\ell^{r-1}(a-1)+1
have the same parity, we can replace
a
with
\ell^{r-1}(a-1)+1
and assume that
2^{a}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell^{r}}
.

We have
(\rho_{f}|_{G_{2}})^{\mathrm{ss}}\cong\chi\psi\oplus\psi
for the unramified character
\psi\colon G_{2}\to\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}^{\times}
with
\psi(\operatorname{Frob}_{2})=\iota_{\ell}(a_{2})
. By
[
AL70
, Theorem 3]
,
a_{2}=-\varepsilon_{2}2^{\frac{k-2}{2}}
. Let
K
be the fixed field of the kernel of
\rho_{f}\mod\lambda^{r}
. By Chebotarev’s density theorem, a positive proportion of primes

have
\operatorname{Frob}_{Q}
conjugate to
\operatorname{Frob}_{2}^{a}
in

\displaystyle Q\equiv\chi(\operatorname{Frob}_{Q})\equiv\chi(\operatorname{Frob}_{2}^{a})\equiv 2^{a}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell^{r}}.

We also have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} a_{Q}=\operatorname{tr}\rho_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{Q})\equiv\operatorname{tr}\rho_{f}(\operatorname{Frob}_{2}^{a})&\equiv(-\varepsilon_{2}2^{\frac{k-2}{2}})^{a}2^{a}+(-\varepsilon_{2}2^{\frac{k-2}{2}})^{a}\\
&\equiv(-\varepsilon_{2})^{a}2^{a\frac{k-2}{2}}(2^{a}+1)\equiv-(-\varepsilon_{2})^{a}Q^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\mod\lambda^{r}.\end{aligned}

The theorem is now proven.
∎

5.
The application to partitions

We use the results of the last two sections to prove Theorems
1.2
–
1.4
from the Introduction.
We begin with two lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be prime, and suppose that
f\in S_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r},\mathbb{Z}\right)
where
(r,24)=1
and
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
.
Suppose that

is a prime and that
\lambda_{Q}
is an integer with

\displaystyle g\big|T(Q)\equiv\lambda_{Q}g\pmod{\ell}\ \ \text{ for all\ \ }g\in S_{2k-1}^{\operatorname{new}}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{r}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{r}\right),\mathbb{Z}\right).

Then

\displaystyle f\big|T(Q^{2})\equiv\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\lambda_{Q}f\pmod{\ell}.

Proof.

For each squarefree
t
let

\displaystyle F_{t}\in S_{2k-1}^{\operatorname{new}}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{r}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{r}\right),\mathbb{Z}\right)

be the form with

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f=F_{t}\otimes\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right).

For each
t
we have

\displaystyle\left(F_{t}\otimes\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right)\right)\big|T(Q)=\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\left(F_{t}\big|T(Q)\right)\otimes\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right)\equiv\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\lambda_{Q}F_{t}\otimes\left(\frac{12}{\bullet}\right)\pmod{\ell}.

In other words, for each squarefree
t
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Sh}_{t}(f\big|T(Q^{2}))=\left(\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f\right)\big|T(Q)\equiv\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\lambda_{Q}\operatorname{Sh}_{t}f\pmod{\ell}.

The lemma now follows from (
2.8
).
∎

The next lemma describes the consequence of finding a “good” eigenvalue.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be prime, and suppose that
f\in S_{k}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{r},\mathbb{Z}\right)
where
(r,24)=1
and
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
.
Suppose that
Q\geq 5
is prime and that there exists
\alpha_{Q}\in\{\pm 1\}

with

\displaystyle f\big|T(Q^{2})\equiv\alpha_{Q}Q^{k-\frac{3}{2}}f\pmod{\ell}.

Then we have

\displaystyle a(Q^{2}n)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\alpha_{Q}\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

This follows from (
2.5
). For such
n
, the middle term in the definition of the Hecke operator
cancels against the same term in
\alpha_{Q}Q^{k-\frac{3}{2}}f
, and the third term does not contribute.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For
\ell\geq 13
, let
f_{\ell}=\sum a(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\in S_{\frac{\ell-2}{2}}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{-\ell},\mathbb{Z}\right)
be the modular form in (
2.11
).
By Theorem
1.5
, a positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
have the property that for all

g\in S_{\ell-3}^{\operatorname{new}}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{-\ell}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{-\ell}\right)\right)
we have

\displaystyle g\big|T(Q)\equiv g\pmod{\ell}.

For such primes it follows from Lemma
5.1
that

\displaystyle f_{\ell}\big|T(Q^{2})\equiv\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)f_{\ell}\pmod{\ell}.

Since
a(n)\equiv p\left(\frac{\ell n+1}{24}\right)\pmod{\ell}
, it follows from Lemma
5.2

that

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{Q^{2}\ell n+1}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\qquad\text{if}\qquad\left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{\frac{\ell-3}{2}}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 13
is a prime such that
2^{a}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
for some
a
.
Theorem
4.2
guarantees that there exists
\beta\in\{\pm 1\}
and a positive proportion of primes

Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle g\big|T(Q)\equiv\beta Q^{\frac{\ell-5}{2}}g\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ \ g\in S_{\ell-3}^{\operatorname{new}}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{-\ell}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{-\ell}\right),\mathbb{Z}\right).

It follows from Lemma
5.1
that

(5.2)

\displaystyle f_{\ell}\big|T(Q^{2})\equiv\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)\beta Q^{\frac{\ell-5}{2}}f_{\ell}\pmod{\ell}.

From Lemma
5.2
we conclude that there are Type I congruences for such primes
Q
.

To prove the existence of Type II congruences for
\ell\geq 5
we argue in a similar way, starting with the
modular form

g_{\ell}=\sum b(n)q^{\frac{n}{24}}\in S_{\frac{\ell^{2}-2}{2}}\left(1,\nu_{\eta}^{-1},\mathbb{Z}\right)
defined in (
2.12
).
In this case we have

\displaystyle b(n)\equiv p\left(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{ n + 1}{24}$}}\right)\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \ \text{when\ \ \ \ $\left(\frac{-n}{\ell}\right)=-1$}.

By (
2.13
) we have
\varepsilon_{2}=-1
in Theorem
4.2
;
we conclude using that result and Lemma
5.1
that for a positive proportion of primes
Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
we have

\displaystyle g_{\ell}\big|T(Q^{2})\equiv-\left(\frac{12}{Q}\right)Q^{\frac{\ell^{2}-5}{2}}g_{\ell}\pmod{\ell}.

By Lemma
5.2
we conclude that

\displaystyle b(Q^{2}n)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ \ \left(\frac{n}{Q}\right)=-\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right)^{\frac{\ell^{2}-3}{2}}=-\left(\frac{-1}{Q}\right),

which gives a congruence of the form (
1.7
).
∎

Finally, we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Here the situation is complicated by the lack of an analogue of Lemma
4.1
for the prime
3
;
this necessitates the added assumption that there are no congruences between newforms in the relevant spaces.
We first need a straighforward lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that the space
S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}\left(6,\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3}\right)
(where
k
is even) is spanned by newforms
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}
.
Let
E
be a number field containing the coefficients of
g_{1},\dots g_{d}
, let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers and let
\lambda
be a prime of
E
over the rational prime
\ell
.
Suppose that there is no
congruence
g_{i}\equiv g_{j}\pmod{\lambda}
with
i\neq j
.
Then if a non-zero modular form
F\in S_{k}^{\operatorname{new}}\left(6,\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{3},\mathcal{O}\right)
is expressed as a linear combination

(5.3)

\displaystyle F=\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{i}g_{i}\qquad\text{with $c_{i}\in E$,}

we have
\operatorname{ord}_{\lambda}(c_{i})\geq 0
for all
i
.

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

If the conclusion were false, then
clearing denominators in (
5.3
) would show that the set
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\}
is linearly dependent over

\mathcal{O}/\lambda
.
Let
j<d
be the maximal index for which
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{j}\}
is linearly independent over
\mathcal{O}/\lambda
.
Then there is a relation

(5.4)

\displaystyle g_{j+1}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{j}\alpha_{i}g_{i}\pmod{\lambda}.

Write
g_{i}=\sum b_{i}(n)q^{n}
, and assume without loss of generality that
\alpha_{1}\not\equiv 0\pmod{\lambda}
.
By assumption we can find a prime
p\geq 5
for which

(5.5)

\displaystyle b_{j+1}(p)\not\equiv b_{1}(p)\pmod{\lambda}.

Applying the Hecke operator
T(p)
to (
5.4
) gives

\displaystyle b_{j+1}(p)\sum_{i=1}^{j}\alpha_{i}g_{i}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{j}b_{i}(p)\alpha_{i}g_{i}.

Since
\alpha_{1}(b_{j+1}(p)-b_{1}(p))\not\equiv 0\pmod{\lambda}
, this gives a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Suppose that
\ell\geq 13
is a prime such that
3^{a}\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
for some
a
.
Applying Theorem
4.3
with
m=1
shows that
there exists
\beta\in\{\pm 1\}
and a positive proportion of primes

Q\equiv-2\pmod{\ell}
such that for every newform

g\in S_{\ell-3}^{\operatorname{new}}\left(6,-\left(\frac{8}{-\ell}\right),-\left(\frac{12}{-\ell}\right)\right)
we have

\displaystyle g\big|T(Q)\equiv\beta Q^{\frac{\ell-5}{2}}g\pmod{\lambda}.

By Lemma
5.3
it follows that (
5.1
) holds, and we argue as before to obtain the first conclusion of Theorem
1.4
.
The second conclusion follows in similar fashion.
∎

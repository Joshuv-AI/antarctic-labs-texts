Theory of Energy Dispersion of Chiral Phonons
Hirokazu Tsunetsugu 1 and Hiroaki Kusunose 1,2

Abstract
We have developed a microscopic theory on phonon energy dispersion in chiral crystals within a harmonic approximation. One of the main issues is about the splitting of sound velocity of acoustic phonons with opposite “crystal” angular momenta. We have shown that the splitting must be zero even in chiral crystals and the difference starts from the order of at least k 2 k^{2} or higher in their energy dispersion. Splitting is evident for chiral optical phonons, and we have derived a formula for their k k -linear splitting. Another important finding is about possible interactions of atomic displacements in microscopic models. We have found that antisymmetric interactions of 𝑫 i ​ j ⋅ ( 𝒅 i × 𝒅 j ) \bm{D}_{ij}\cdot(\bm{d}_{i}\times\bm{d}_{j}) type are not allowed in microscopic Hamiltonians for chiral phonons because of the stability of equilibrium structure. We have identified that the splitting in both acoustic and optical modes arises from the harmonic potentials with the electric toroidal quadrupole of G u G_{u} -type symmetry. These constraint are important for modeling real materials. Most of our microscopic calculations have been performed for (quasi-)one-dimensional systems with a trigonal crystal symmetry including Te, but these results generally hold also for other chiral phonon systems.

Now we summarize the procedure of calculating phonon energy dispersion.
A starting point is a lattice deformation energy functional

\mathscr{V}
represented in terms of atomic displacements

\{\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l})\}
,
and it is customary for it to employ a quadratic form of

\{\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l})\}
.
After Fourier transformation,
the coefficient in the quadratic form for each wavevector
\bm{k}

is reduced to a matrix with the dimension of

3\mspace{-1mu}\times\mspace{-1mu}\mbox{(no.~of sublattices)}
.
This is called the dynamical matrix,
and its each eigenvalue
\lambda_{\alpha\bm{k}}

determines the corresponding phonon energy dispersion as

\hbar\omega_{\alpha\bm{k}}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\hbar\sqrt{\lambda_{\alpha\bm{k}}/M}

with the atomic mass
M
.
Since our concern is their
\bm{k}
-dependence,
we use the units of
M\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}1
and
\hbar\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}1
.
In this paper, we treat for simplicity the cases
that constituent atoms are all identical
and they have an isotropic mass tensor,
but it is straightforward to generalize these points.

Let us then construct a simple model for the deformation energy

\mathscr{V}
describing two-atom interactions as explained before.
To this end, two requirements are crucial.
First, its energy must be
non-negative for any displacements
to guarantee the stability of the equilibrium structure.
Secondly, its functional form must match the lattice symmetry.
Namely, it should be invariant upon any symmetry operation of the lattice.
Considering
\mathscr{V}
is scalar,
a product of two displacement vectors

\bm{d}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l})
and

\bm{d}^{\prime}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{m})

appears in it with a coefficient that is either a scalar (
v
),
symmetric second-rank tensor (
v_{i\mspace{-1mu}j}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}v_{j\mspace{1mu}i}
),
or antisymmetric tensor (
v_{ij}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\epsilon_{i\mspace{-1mu}j\mspace{1mu}k}D_{k}
).
The last case corresponds to an interaction of
Dzyloshinskii-Moriya (DM) type
\bm{D}\cdot(\bm{d}\times\bm{d}^{\prime})
,
but this type is not allowed in our case.
Clearly, it does not fulfill the first requirement,
and one cannot resolve this problem by generalizing its form.
The remaining two cases are all together summarized to a quadratic form

(\bm{d}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\bm{d}^{\prime})\cdot\mathsf{v}\,(\bm{d}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\bm{d}^{\prime})

with a real symmetric
3\mspace{-1mu}\times\mspace{-1mu}3
matrix

\mathsf{v}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{v}(\bm{r}_{l}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\bm{r}_{m})
,
which will be referred to as
stiffness matrix
henceforth.
This complies with the first requirement,
if
\mathsf{v}
has no negative eigenvalue.
Furthermore, energy cost is zero when
\bm{d}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\bm{d}^{\prime}
,
which meets our expectation for
\mathscr{V}

since the related atomic bond is intact.
The second requirement imposes that
\mathsf{v}
’s principal axes

\{\tilde{\bm{e}}_{i}\}
(
i\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}1,2,3
)
point to the local symmetric directions of the bond
connecting the atomic sites
\bm{r}_{l}
and
\bm{r}_{m}
.

We are ready to write down an explicit form of
\mathscr{V}

for the case of Te-like lattice with “left” handedness.
It consists of a part for inside each helix (
\mathscr{V}_{L,1}
)
and a part between neighboring helices (
\mathscr{V}_{L,2}
).
The latter part will be discussed later.
For
\mathscr{V}_{L,1}
,
we consider contributions of only nearest-neighbor pairs

\mathscr{V}_{L,1}=\!\sum_{\bm{r}_{l}}\bigl[\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l})\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\bm{t}_{l,l+1})\bigr]\cdot\mathsf{v}_{l,l+1}\,\bigl[\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l})\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\bm{t}_{l,l+1})\bigr],

(2)

where
\mathsf{v}_{l,l+1}
is the stiffness matrix
for the bond
\bm{t}_{l,l+1}

and is subject to the symmetry constraints

\mathsf{v}_{2,0}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}\mathsf{v}_{0,1}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\mathsf{v}_{1,2}\mathsf{C}_{3}
.
Here,

is the
3\mspace{-1mu}\times\mspace{-1mu}3

z

\bm{t}_{2,0}

-rotation about the

As for phonon energy, this leads to the asymptotics

\omega_{1}^{(\pm 1)}(k_{z})\mspace{-2mu}\sim\mspace{-2mu}|k_{z}|\mspace{2mu}\bigl[u\pm(w_{5}/2u)\mspace{2mu}k_{z}^{3}\bigr]

with the sound velocity
u
,
and thus an energy splitting appears
in the order
O(k_{z}^{4})
.
Note that in more general models,
corrections may start from a lower-order such as
O(k_{z}^{3})
,
and then it leads to an energy splitting of
O(k_{z}^{2})
.

Energy splitting due to chiral structure is more evident
for the optical modes (
\alpha\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}2,3
).
It appears in the order
O(k_{z})
as

\lambda_{\alpha}^{(m)}(k_{z})\sim\lambda_{\alpha}(0)\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\varGamma_{\alpha}\mspace{2mu}k_{z}\mspace{2mu}m.

(6)

Its coefficient
\varGamma_{\alpha}

is an important indicator that quantifies
the effects of chiral structure on phonon dispersion,
and we will call it
splitting coefficient
.
Since
k_{z}
is a polar vector element and
m
is axial,
the splitting coefficient
\varGamma_{a}
needs to be a pseudo-scalar.
It should change sign under
z
-plane mirror operation
but remains invariant under time reversal.
In order to see which parts of the chiral structure dominate

\varGamma_{\alpha}
’s,
it is desirable to realize a smooth control of chiral lattice
structure and how it affects the values of
\varGamma_{\alpha}
’s.

Such a control of lattice chirality and its handedness
is actually accomplished
by glueing a chiral crystal together with its mirror image,
i.e.,
enantiomorph
.
In the present case, one realization is the lattice
with the unit cell shown in Fig.
1
(c),
which we will call
double-handed triple helix
(DHTH).
This contains three sublattice sites positioned
at
\bm{r}_{l}
in Eq. (
1
)
but now with
\bm{s}_{l}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\delta\bm{a}_{l}
,
and then two types of bonds are assigned for opposite windings

\bm{t}_{l,l\pm 1}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\bm{s}_{l\pm 1}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\bm{s}_{l}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\bm{a}_{3}
.
While the deformation energy of
the
left-hand
wound (
L
) bonds is
given by Eq. (
2
) with this redefinition,
the right-hand wound (
R
) bonds contribute its counterpart

\mathscr{V}_{R,1}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\!\sum_{\bm{r}_{l}}\bigl[\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l})\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\bm{t}_{l,l-1})\bigr]\cdot\mathsf{v}_{l,l-1}\bigl[\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l})\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\bm{d}(\bm{r}_{l}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\bm{t}_{l,l-1})\bigr]
.
To realize a completely nonchiral limit, one sets as

\mathsf{v}_{l+1,l}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{m}_{z}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{l,l+1}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{m}_{z}

with the mirror operation

\mathsf{m}_{z}\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}\mbox{diag }(1,1,-1)
,
since
\bm{t}_{l+1,l}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}-\mathsf{m}_{z}\mspace{2mu}\bm{t}_{l,l+1}
.
In particular,
\hat{\mathsf{v}}\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}\mathsf{v}_{0,2}
is identical to

\mathsf{v}_{2,0}
in Eq. (
3
)
except for the plus sign for
\varDelta K
.
However, in more general cases
in which handedness cancellation is incomplete,
the parameters
K_{jj}
and
\varDelta K
differ
between
L
- and
R
-bonds,
and henceforth, we will add the label
L
or
R
to distinguish them.
As before, the symmetry constraint determines
the stiffness matrix for the other bonds

\mathsf{v}_{0,2}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{1,0}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{2,1}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}
.

In a general DHTH lattice, the total deformation energy is

\mathscr{V}_{L,1}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\mathscr{V}_{R,1}
,
and this leads to the dynamical matrix

\underline{V}_{L,1}(\bm{k})\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\underline{V}_{R,1}(\bm{k})\mspace{2mu}\equiv\underline{V}(\bm{k})
.
As before, we will concentrate on the case of
\bm{k}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}(0,0,k_{z})
.
Then,
\underline{V}_{R,1}(k_{z})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\underline{V}_{R,1}(0,0,k_{z})

is given by modifying the form in Eq. (
4
):
first replace
\bar{\mathsf{v}}
by
\hat{\mathsf{v}}
,
and then interchange
\mathsf{C}_{3}
and
\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}
,
and lastly operate complex conjugation.
Since this also commutes with
\underline{C}_{3}
defined before,

\underline{V}_{R,1}(k_{z})
is reduced to a
3\mspace{-1mu}\times\mspace{-1mu}3
effective matrix

\mathsf{v}_{\mathrm{eff},R}^{(m)}(k_{z})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{F}(\phi_{m}^{-})\mspace{2mu}\hat{\mathsf{v}}\mspace{4mu}\mathsf{F}(\phi_{m}^{-})
,
while its counterpart is

\mathsf{v}_{\mathrm{eff},L}^{(m)}(k_{z})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{F}(\phi_{m}^{+})\mspace{2mu}\bar{\mathsf{v}}\mspace{4mu}\mathsf{F}(\phi_{m}^{+})
.
Here,

\phi_{m}^{\pm}\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}k_{z}c\pm{\textstyle\frac{2m\pi}{3}}

and it has a different factor of
k_{z}c

from the one in Eq. (
2
) due to the redefinition of
the bond vectors
\bm{t}_{l,l\pm 1}
.
The total effective dynamical matrix is

\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(m)}(k_{z})\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}\mathsf{v}_{\mathrm{eff},L}^{(m)}(k_{z})\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\mathsf{v}_{\mathrm{eff},R}^{(m)}(k_{z})
,
and it is instructive to rewrite it into the following form

\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(m)}\mspace{1mu}(k_{z})=\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{0}-\zeta^{\mspace{2mu}-m}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{s}(-p)\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}-\zeta^{\mspace{2mu}m}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{s}(p)\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3},

(7)

where

p\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}k_{z}c
.
The constant term is

\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{0}\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\mspace{2mu}\mbox{diag }\bigl(K_{11,+}\mspace{-4mu}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\mspace{-2mu}3K_{22,+},3K_{11,+}\mspace{-4mu}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\mspace{-2mu}K_{22,+},4K_{33,+}\mspace{-2mu}\bigr)\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\varDelta K_{-}\mathsf{X}

defined with

(\mathsf{X})_{\mu,\nu}\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}\delta_{\mu,y}\delta_{\nu,z}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\delta_{\mu,z}\delta_{\nu,y}

and the (anti-)symmetrized parameters

K_{jj,\pm}\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}K_{jj,L}\pm K_{jj,R}
and

\varDelta K_{\pm}\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}\varDelta K_{L}\pm\varDelta K_{R}
.
The
k_{z}
-dependence comes from

\mathsf{v}_{s}(p)\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\cos p\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{+}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}i\sin p\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{-}

defined with

\mathsf{v}_{\pm}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mbox{diag }\bigl(K_{11,\pm},K_{22,\pm},K_{33,\pm}\bigr)\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}\varDelta K_{\mp}\mathsf{X}
.
Note that
\mathsf{v}_{s}(p)
is not hermitian,
but has the relation

\mathsf{v}_{s}(-p)\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{v}_{s}(p)^{*}
.
As in the previous case of single helix,
the time reversal symmetry leads to the relation

\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(-m)}(-k_{z})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(m)}(k_{z})^{*}
,
and this guarantees the energy degeneracy of chiral phonons

\omega_{\alpha}^{(-m)}(-k_{z})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\omega_{\alpha}^{(m)}(k_{z})
,
in general DHTH lattices as shown in Fig.
3
.
The degeneracy at the BZ boundary

k_{z}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\pm k_{0}

differs from the behavior in Fig.
2
(a).
As the DHTH lattice is now symmorphic,
there holds the relation

\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(m)}(k_{0})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(m)}(-k_{0})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(-m)}(\pm k_{0})^{*}
,
and this leads to the degeneracy

\omega_{\alpha}^{(+1)}(k_{0})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\omega_{\alpha}^{(+1)}(-k_{0})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\omega_{\alpha}^{(-1)}(\pm k_{0})
.
Note that when
\mathscr{V}_{R,1}
is set to zero,
the system is reduced to decoupled three copies of a Te-like lattice
but their lattice constant
c
is tripled.

K_{i,R}/K_{i,L}

’s.

m\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\pm 1
CAM modes are degenerate in (a).

It is important to examine the nonchiral limit
where the right-handed part is a precise mirror image of the left-handed part.
In this case, the antisymmetrized parameters vanish
(
K_{jj,-}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\varDelta K_{-}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}0
),
and this leads to the special symmetries that

\mathsf{v}_{s}(-p)\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{m}_{z}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{s}(p)\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{m}_{z}

and
\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{0}
is a diagonal matrix.
Combining them with the property

[\mathsf{C}_{3}^{\pm 1},\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{m}_{z}]\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}0
,
it is easy to show

\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(m)}(-k_{z})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\mathsf{m}_{z}\mspace{2mu}\bar{\mathsf{v}}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(m)}(k_{z})\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{m}_{z}
.
Together with the chiral symmetry,
this leads to the degeneracy of phonon energy dispersion
between the opposite CAM modes,

\omega_{\alpha}^{(m)}(k_{z})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\omega_{\alpha}^{(-m)}(k_{z})
.
Note that this additional symmetry is specific to the nonchiral limit.

Finally, let us evaluate the energy splitting
for the opposite CAM modes,
which determines the splitting coefficient

\varGamma_{\alpha}
introduced before.
We can calculate this by following the standard procedure
of the first-order perturbation in
k_{z}
.
Let
\vec{d}_{\alpha}^{\mspace{4mu}(m)}
denote the 9-dimensional eigenvectors
of
\underline{V}(k_{z}\!=\!0)

in the CAM subspaces
m\!=\!\pm 1
.
They satisfy the relation

\vec{d}_{\alpha}^{\mspace{4mu}(-1)}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}[\vec{d}_{\alpha}^{\mspace{4mu}(+1)}]^{\,*}
.
We can show immediately

m\mspace{2mu}\varGamma_{\alpha}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\langle\vec{d}_{\alpha}^{\mspace{4mu}(m)}\mspace{2mu}|\mspace{2mu}\underline{D}_{z}\mspace{2mu}|\mspace{2mu}\vec{d}_{\alpha}^{\mspace{4mu}(m)}\rangle

with

\underline{D}_{z}\equiv\bigl[d\underline{V}(k_{z})/dk_{z}\bigr]_{k_{z}=0}
,
and this new matrix is represented as

\underline{D}_{z}=-i\left[\begin{array}[]{@{\hs{2pt}}c@{\hs{4pt}}c@{\hs{4pt}}c@{\hs{2pt}}}\hskip 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil\mathsf{0}\hfil\hskip 4.0&z_{01}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{-}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}\hfil\hskip 4.0&z_{02}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{-}\hfil\hskip 2.0\\[2.0pt]
\hskip 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil z_{10}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{-}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}\hfil\hskip 4.0&\mathsf{0}\hfil\hskip 4.0&z_{12}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{-}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\hfil\hskip 2.0\\[2.0pt]
\hskip 2.0pt\lx@intercol\hfil z_{20}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{-}\hfil\hskip 4.0&z_{21}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{v}_{-}\mspace{2mu}\mathsf{C}_{3}^{T}\hfil\hskip 4.0&\mathsf{0}\hfil\hskip 2.0\end{array}\right].

(8)

Here,
z_{ll^{\prime}}
is the
z
-component of
the bond vector
\bm{t}_{ll^{\prime}}
, and

z_{l^{\prime}l}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}-z_{ll^{\prime}}
.
Thus, this hermitian matrix
\underline{D}_{z}
is pure imaginary
and similar
to a current in the sense that it changes sign under
each of time reversal and space inversion operations.
It is possible to separate the pseudo-scalar part
\varGamma_{a}
alone
by combining the CAM operator:

\displaystyle\varGamma_{\alpha}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\langle\vec{d}_{\alpha}^{\mspace{4mu}(m)}\mspace{2mu}|\mspace{2mu}\underline{\varGamma}\mspace{2mu}|\mspace{2mu}\vec{d}_{\alpha}^{\mspace{4mu}(m)}\rangle,\ \ \mbox{with }\underline{\varGamma}\equiv\underline{L}_{z}\,\underline{D}_{z}.

(9)

Note that
\underline{\varGamma}
is real symmetric,
since
\underline{L}_{z}
and
\underline{D}_{z}
commute.
Thus, this
\underline{\varGamma}
is the operator
that defines the chirality (handedness) of the system.
We skip the detail of calculating the matrix element
\varGamma_{\alpha}

and show the final result:

\varGamma_{\alpha}={\textstyle\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}\,\Bigl[\bigl(1\mp\cos\beta\bigr)K_{33,-}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigl(1\pm\cos\beta\bigr)\bigl(K_{11,-}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}K_{22,-}\bigr)\Bigr],

(10)

where the upper and lower signs are for

\alpha\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}2
and 3, respectively, and

\beta\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}\tan^{-1}\bigl[\sqrt{8}\mspace{2mu}\varDelta K_{-}(K_{11,+}\mspace{-2mu}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}K_{22,+}\mspace{-2mu}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}2K_{33,+})^{-1}\bigr]
.
It is interesting that they satisfy the simple sum rule

\varGamma_{2}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}\varGamma_{3}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\sqrt{3}\mspace{2mu}\bigl[K_{33,-}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigl(K_{11,-}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}K_{22,-}\bigr)\bigr]\mspace{-2mu}\equiv\mspace{-2mu}\varGamma_{s}
,
which is also represented in terms of the tensor’s principle values as

\varGamma_{s}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}(K_{2,-}\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}K_{3,-}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}2K_{1,-})\mspace{-2mu}+\mspace{-2mu}{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}(K_{3,-}\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}K_{2,-})\cos 2\theta
,
where
\theta
is the effective bond angle introduced before
for defining
\mathsf{v}_{2,0}
.
This
\varGamma_{s}
is completely determined
by the antisymmetrized parameters
(i.e., imbalance between the left- and right-handed parts),
and independent of the symmetrized parameters
(i.e., common factors in the two parts).
Therefore, we may consider
\varGamma_{s}

as the most fundamental indicator characterizing
the effects of chiral structure on phonon dispersion.
The parameter
\beta
determines how the sum

\varGamma_{s}
is distributed between the two optical modes.
Note that one can apply these results
to the systems with one chiral part alone
such as Te lattice,
and in those cases

K_{jj,+}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}|K_{jj,-}|
and

|\varDelta K_{+}|\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}|\varDelta K_{-}|
.
It is clear that
\varGamma_{s}
changes its sign
upon a reversal of lattice handedness.

It is important to notice that one can represent
this indicator as an averaged uniaxial anisotropy

Q_{u}(\mathsf{v})\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\!{\textstyle\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}}\,\mbox{Tr}\mspace{2mu}[(1\mspace{-2mu}-\mspace{-2mu}3\mathsf{m}_{z})\,\mathsf{v}]

of the local stiffness matrices
weighted by the bond sign of handedness

h\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}+1
for left and
-1
for right bonds:

\varGamma_{s}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}N^{-1}\sum_{\bm{R}}h(\bm{R})\,Q_{u}(\mathsf{v}(\bm{R}))
with

N
being the number of sites.

\bm{R}
denotes a bond center position measured from the nearest
chiral axis,
and if multiple
\mathsf{v}
’s exist at the same
\bm{R}
,
they should be all counted.

Since the splitting coefficients
\varGamma_{\alpha}
are
a kind of the “order parameters”
of the chiral system, they belong to a nontrivial representation
of the supergroup
D_{3h}
.
Considering
\underline{L}_{z}
and
\underline{D}_{z}

in Eq. (
9
) belong to
A_{2}^{\prime}
and
A_{2}^{\prime\mspace{-2mu}\prime}
, respectively,

\varGamma_{\alpha}
’s have the same symmetry
as
m\cdot k_{z}
, i.e.,

A_{2}^{\prime}\otimes A_{2}^{\prime\mspace{-2mu}\prime}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}A_{1}^{\prime\mspace{-2mu}\prime}
.
In the language of cluster multipoles,
they correspond to the pseudo-scalar (electric toroidal)
multipoles of
G_{0}
and
G_{u}
-type
[
5
,
63
]
, the latter of which gives the mono-axial anisotropy.
A caution is necessary for the term “pseudo-scalar”
in the systems without inversion or any mirror symmetries.
It is instructive to elevate the system’s symmetry
by supplementing generator(s) of mirror or inversion type.
In our case,
with the mirror
\mathsf{m}_{z}
supplemented,
the chiral point group
D_{3}
is
elevated to its nonchiral supergroup
D_{3h}
,
which is the symmetry of the DHTH with the symmetric couplings

K_{j,R}\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}K_{j,L}
.
Pseudo-scalars are defined as bases of its
A_{1}^{\prime\mspace{-2mu}\prime}
representation,
which changes sign under improper rotations and mirror operations.
When the system is chiral (
K_{j,R}\neq K_{j,L}
),

\underline{\varGamma}
falls into the identity representation
A_{1}

of the
D_{3}
point group,
and gives a nonvanishing contribution to the CAM mode splitting.
Similarly, in the cubic chiral systems,
the lowest-order pseudo-scalar multipoles
(
A_{1u}
,
A_{2}
,
A_{u}
in the supergroup
O_{h}
,
T_{d}
,
T_{h}
)
of
G_{0}
and
G_{4}
-type
[
5
,
63
]

induce a CAM splitting.

To summarize, we have developed a microscopic theory
on the energy dispersion of chiral phonons
within the harmonic approximation.
Those chiral phonons are characterized by the crystal angular momentum

m\mspace{-3mu}=\mspace{-3mu}\pm 1
, and the splitting of
their energy dispersions depending on
m
.
This
k_{z}
-linear energy splitting in the optical modes
is indicated by nonvanishing splitting coefficients
\varGamma_{\alpha}
.
They are thus order parameters of the chiral system,
belonging to the nontrivial
A_{1}^{\prime\mspace{-2mu}\prime}
representation
of the supergroup, and related to
the
G_{0}
- and
G_{u}
-type electric toroidal multipoles
(or
G_{4}
type when the chiral system is cubic).
Analyticity of the dynamical matrix in
\bm{k}
enforces
an identical value of sound velocity for their acoustic modes,
and a splitting appears in the order of at least
k^{2}
or higher.
It is also important that stability of the equilibrium structure
forbids the presence of antisymmetric interactions,
because they otherwise violate
the positivity of stiffness matrices.
These constraints are crucial for modeling real materials
in the first-principles phonon calculations.
The splitting is much more visible in optical modes,
and it starts from the order
O(k^{1})
.
Its size is determined by the uniaxially anisotropic component
of the stiffness matrices about the chiral axis.
These fundamental findings of the present paper will provide
further insights for elucidating the phonon and
related phenomena in chiral systems.

Weak convergence of Monge-Ampère measures on compact Hermitian manifolds
Sławomir Kołodziej and Ngoc Cuong Nguyen

Abstract
. We give a sufficient condition on a sequence of uniformly bounded ω \omega -plurisubharmonic functions, ω \omega being a Hermitian metric, for which the sequence of associated Monge-Ampère measures converges weakly. This criterion can be used to obtained a bounded ω \omega -plurisubharmonic solution to the Monge-Ampère equation.



1.
Introduction

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
. The complex Monge-Ampère equation on those manifolds has been studied extensively in recent years. The classical solution in the smooth case was provided by Tosatti-Weinkowe
[
TW10
]
(for
n=2
the equation was solved earlier by Cherrier
[
Ch87
]
). The weak solutions to the equation were studied in
[
DK12
]
,
[
KN15
,
KN18
,
KN19a
,
KN21a
]
,
[
LPT21
]
and recent advances for the semi-positive Hermitian form are contained in
[
GL21b
]
. The equation has found numerous geometric applications
[
To15
]
,
[
Ng16
]
,
[
Di16
]
,
[
Ni17
]
,
[
Td18
]

[
KT21
]
and
[
GL21a
]
.

To obtain the weak solutions one often uses the stability of potentials of the approximating equations.
It is well-known that the
L^{p}
-convergence of potentials does not imply the weak convergence of corresponding Monge-Ampère measures. Furthermore, in the Hermitian setting there are fewer such criterions available as compared to the Kähler manifolds. In this note, we prove such a criterion under suitable assumptions: of uniform boundedness and domination by Monge-Ampère measures of another ”nice” sequence. Recall the Bedford-Taylor capacity: for a Borel subset
E\subset X
,

cap_{\omega}(E):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n}:w\in PSH(X,\omega),0\leq w\leq 1\right\}.

Here
PSH(X,\omega)
denotes the set of all
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (
\omega
-psh) functions on
X
.
A sequence
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset PSH(X,\omega)
is said converge in capacity to
\varphi\in PSH(X,\omega)
if for a given
\varepsilon>0
,

\lim_{j\to+\infty}cap_{\omega}(|\varphi_{j}-\varphi|>\varepsilon)=0.

The main result of this expository note is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}
be a uniformly bounded sequence of
\omega
-psh functions. Assume
(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}\leq C(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{n}
for some uniformly bounded sequence
\{\varphi_{j}\}
such that
\varphi_{j}\to\varphi\in PSH(X,\omega)
in capacity. If
u_{j}\to u\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
in
L^{1}(X)
, then a subsequence of
(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}
converges weakly to
(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}
.

This is a generalization of
[
CK06
, Lemma 2.1]
from the local setting to compact Hermitian manifolds that is pointed out in the proof of
[
KN23b
, Lemma 2.11]
(see also
[
KN23a
]
). On compact Käher manifolds there are stronger results
[
H08
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
DH12
]
. We will see that if the dimension
n=2
, then we have a similar result (Proposition
2.5
). However, we do not know if it still holds for dimensions
n\geq 3
.
It is also possible to extend the theorem to the case of Hermitian semi-positive
(1,1)
-forms
\alpha
and we will consider it in a future paper.

An application of the theorem is that it provides a shorter proof of the existence of bounded
\omega
-psh solutions to Monge-Ampère equations with the right hand side in
L^{p}(X)
,
p>1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
0\leq f\in L^{p}(X)
and
p>1
. Then, there exists a bounded function
u\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
and a constant
c>0
solving
\omega_{u}^{n}=cf\omega^{n}.

Proof.

Approximating
f_{j}\to f
by smooth positive function
f_{j}
. By the Tosatti-Weinkove theorem
[
TW10
]
there are
u_{j}\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap C^{\infty}(X)
and
c_{j}>0
solving

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=c_{j}f_{j}\omega^{n},\quad\sup_{X}u_{j}=0.

Using
[
KN15
, Eq. (5.12)]
we have
c_{j}\leq C_{0}
with a uniform
C_{0}>0
. Therefore, by
[
DK12
]
we have
-C_{1}\leq u_{j}\leq 0
. By passing to a subsequence we may assume that
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}(X)
and
c_{j}\to c\geq 0
. Then,

\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=\int_{X}|u-u_{j}|c_{j}f_{j}\omega^{n}\leq C\|u_{j}-u\|_{L^{1}(X)}^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
1/p+1/q=1
.
Hence, by Lemma
2.4
(below) there is a subsequence of
\{u_{j}\}
, for simplicity still denoted by
\{u_{j}\}
, such that
\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}\to\omega_{u}^{n}=cf\omega^{n}
weakly. Note that since
u
is bounded, it follows from
[
KN15
, Remark 5.7]
that
c>0
.
∎

Another application is proving the existence of bounded solutions for a more general class of measures.
A positive Radon measure
\mu
on
X
is well dominated by capacity or belong to
\mathcal{F}(X,h)
(see
[
Ko05
]
) if

(1.1)

\mu(E)\leq F_{h}(cap_{\omega}(E)),

for any Borel set
E\subset X
, where
F_{h}(x)=x/h(x^{-\frac{1}{n}})
for some increasing
function
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
satisfying

(1.2)

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x[h(x)]^{\frac{1}{n}}}\,dx<+\infty.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure with
\mu(X)>0
. Assume that
\mu
is well dominated by capacity. Then, there exists a bounded solution
u\in PSH(X,\omega)
and
c>0
solving

\omega_{u}^{n}=c\mu.

Proof.

It follows easily from the proof of
[
KN23a
, Theorem 3.1]
. ∎

Remark 1.4
.

The solutions obtained in Corollary 2.7 and Corollary 2.8 are Hölder continuous and continuous, respectively. However, to get these statements, we need a stronger stability of solutions as in
[
KN15
,
KN21a
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and convergence in capacity

This section is devoted to prove the main theorem.
Since all functions under consideration are uniformly bounded, by normalizing

(2.1)

\sup_{X}u_{j}=\sup_{X}\varphi_{j}=0,

they belong to the subset
\mathcal{P}_{0}
of
PSH(X,\omega)
given by

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\left\{v\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X):\sup_{X}v=0\right\}.

Let us denote
dV=\omega^{n}
the volume form of
X
.
We have the following result essentially due to Cegrell
[
Ce98
]
, a detailed proof is given in
[
KN23a
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\lambda
be a finite positive Radon measure on
X
vanishing on pluripolar sets. Suppose moreover that
\{u_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{0}
converges
dV
-a.e. to
u\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Then there exists a subsequence
\{u_{j_{s}}\}\subset\{u_{j}\}
such that

\lim_{j_{s}\to+\infty}\int_{X}u_{j_{s}}d\lambda=\int_{X}ud\lambda.

An immediate consequence is

Corollary 2.2
.

There exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{u_{j}\}
, such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|d\lambda=0.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.1
twice to the sequences
\{u_{j}\}
and
\max\{u_{j},u\}
, we have (still denoting by
\{u_{j}\}
the resulting subsequence)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}u_{j}d\lambda=\int_{X}ud\lambda,\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}\max\{u_{j},u\}d\lambda=\int_{X}ud\lambda.

Since
\max\{u,u_{j}\}=(u_{j}+u+|u_{j}-u|)/2
, integrating both sides and using the previous equations we get that

u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}(d\lambda).

∎

The next result is a global analogue of
[
KN23b
, Lemma 2.3]
which says that under the assumption of Theorem
1.1
, the sequence converges uniformly in
L^{1}
-norm with respect to a family of measures. On compact complex manifolds the Cegrell inequality will not be needed. For the reader convenience, we give details of the proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a uniformly bounded sequence that converges in capacity to
w\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Assume
\sup_{j}\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}\leq C_{1}
for some
C_{1}>0.
Then,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}|u-u_{j}|(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=0\quad\text{for }k=0,1,...,n.

Proof.

The case
k=0
is the assumption on
\{u_{j}\}
, and we only give here the proof of the last inductive step, the other steps are the very similar. In the proof that follows the constant
C>0
is a uniform constant depending only on
X,\omega
and the uniform bounds for
\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|w_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, it may change from line to line.

Note that
|u-u_{j}|=(\max\{u,u_{j}\}-u_{j})+(\max\{u,u_{j}\}-u)
. By quasi-continuity of
\omega
-psh functions and Hartogs’ lemma, we have
\phi_{j}:=\max\{u,u_{j}\}\to u
in capacity. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then, when
j
is large,

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\max\{u,u_{j}\}-u)(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|\phi_{j}-u|>\varepsilon\}}(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}+\varepsilon\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq Ccap_{\omega}(|\phi_{j}-u|>\varepsilon)+C_{1}\varepsilon.

Therefore,
\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u)(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}=0
. Next, we have for
j>k
,

\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}-\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})(\omega+dd^{c}w_{k})^{n}=\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})dd^{c}(w_{j}-w_{k})\wedge T,

where
T=T(j,k)=\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\omega_{w_{j}}^{s}\wedge\omega_{w_{k}}^{n-1-s}
. By integration by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})dd^{c}(w_{j}-w_{k})\wedge T

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(w_{j}-w_{k})dd^{c}\left[(\phi_{j}-u_{j})\wedge T\right].

Note that for
h=\phi_{j}-u_{j}
,

\displaystyle dd^{c}(hT)

\displaystyle=dd^{c}h\wedge T+dh\wedge d^{c}T-d^{c}h\wedge dT+hdd^{c}T

\displaystyle=dd^{c}h\wedge T+dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge T_{1}-d^{c}h\wedge d\omega\wedge T_{2}

\displaystyle=:S_{0}+S_{1}+S_{2}.

Here notice that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are positive currents, and similar type as of
T
.

We now estimate each term
S_{0},S_{1}
and
S_{2}
separately. Firstly, for the term
S_{0}
,

\int_{X}(w_{j}-w_{k})dd^{c}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})\wedge T\leq\int_{X}|w_{j}-w_{k}|(\omega_{\phi_{j}}+\omega_{u_{j}})\wedge T.

Since
\|w_{j}\|_{\infty},\|u_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq A
in
X
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{X}|w_{j}-w_{k}|(\omega_{\phi_{j}}+\omega_{u_{j}})\wedge T

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{\{|w_{j}-w_{k}|>\varepsilon\}}(\omega_{\phi_{j}}+\omega_{u_{j}})\wedge T

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{\{|w_{j}-w_{k}|\leq\varepsilon\}}(\omega_{\phi_{j}}+\omega_{u_{j}})\wedge T

\displaystyle\leq A^{n+1}cap_{\omega}(|w_{j}-w_{k}|>\varepsilon)+C\varepsilon,

where the uniform bound for the second integral on the right hand side follows from uniform boundedness of the potentials (see. e.g.,
[
DK12
, Proposition 2.3]
).
Since
w_{j}\to w
in capacity, it follows that there exists
k_{0}
such that for every
j>k\geq k_{0}
the left hand side is less than
2C\varepsilon
.

Secondly, for the term
S_{1}
, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
[
Ng16
, Proposition 1.4]
in the Hermitian setting,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}(w_{j}-w_{k})dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge T_{1}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{X}|w_{j}-w_{k}|dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega\wedge T_{1}\int_{X}|w_{j}-w_{k}|\omega^{2}\wedge T_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{X}|w_{j}-w_{k}|\omega^{2}\wedge T_{1}.

Therefore, by a similar argument as in the first case for the integral on the right hand side is bounded by
C\varepsilon
for every
j>k\geq k_{0}
(we may increase
k_{0}
if necessary).

Lastly, the term
S_{2}
is estimated similarly as
S_{1}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})(\omega+dd^{c}w_{k})^{n}

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})\omega_{w_{j}}^{n}-\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})\omega_{w_{k}}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})(\omega+dd^{c}w_{k})^{n}+2C\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}|u-u_{j}|(\omega+dd^{c}w_{k})^{n}+2C\varepsilon.

Fixing
k=k_{0}
and applying Corollary
2.2
for
d\lambda=(\omega+dd^{c}w_{k_{0}})^{n}
, we get that

\int_{X}(\phi_{j}-u_{j})(dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}\leq(2C+1)\varepsilon\quad\text{for }j\geq k_{1}\geq k_{0}.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, the proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.3
and the following
weak convergence.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that

(2.2)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}=0.

Then, there exists a subsequence
\{u_{j_{s}}\}
of
\{u_{j}\}
such that
\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}
converges to
\omega_{u}^{n}
weakly.

Proof.

Let
A>0
be such that
-A\leq u_{j},u\leq 0
.
By passing to a subsequence we may assume further that
u_{j}\to u
a.e. in
X
with respect to
dV
.
Note that

u=(\limsup_{j\to\infty}u_{j})^{*}=\lim_{j\to\infty}(\sup_{\ell\geq j}u_{\ell})^{*}.

Set

(2.3)

w_{j}=\max\{u_{j},u-1/j\}.

By the Hartogs lemma
w_{j}
converges to
u
in capacity. Therefore, by the convergence theorem in
[
DK12
]
(see also
[
BT82
]
),

\lim_{j\to\infty}\omega_{w_{j}}^{n}=\omega_{u}^{n}.

Thanks to Lemma
2.3
and the assumption
w_{j}\to u
in capacity, we have

(2.4)

\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}\to 0\quad\text{as }j\to+\infty.

Now we are ready to conclude the proof of the lemma. By (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) we can choose a subsequence
\{u_{j_{s}}\}\subset\{u_{j}\}
so that

\int_{X}|u-u_{j_{s}}|(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j_{s}})^{n}+\int_{X}|u-u_{j_{s}}|(\omega+dd^{c}w_{s})^{n}<1/s^{2}.

Recall from (
2.3
) that
w_{s}=\max\{u_{j_{s}},u-1/s\}
and this implies

{\bf 1}_{\{u_{j_{s}}>u-1/s\}}(\omega+dd^{c}w_{s})^{n}={\bf 1}_{\{u_{j_{s}}>u-1/s\}}(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j_{s}})^{n}.

Therefore, for
\eta\in C^{\infty}(X)
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}\eta\omega_{u}^{n}-\int_{X}\eta\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{X}\eta\omega_{u}^{n}-\int_{X}\eta\omega_{w_{s}}^{n}\right|+\left|\int_{X}\eta\omega_{w_{s}}^{n}-\int_{X}\eta\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{X}\eta\omega_{u}^{n}-\int_{X}\eta\omega_{w_{s}}^{n}\right|+\left|\int_{\{u_{j_{s}}\leq u-1/s\}}\eta\omega_{w_{s}}^{n}-\eta\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}\right|.

The first term on the right hand side goes to zero as
\omega_{w_{s}}^{n}\to\omega_{u}^{n}
. It remains to estimate the second term. Firstly, by the choice of
\{u_{j_{s}}\}
at the beginning of this proof,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\{u_{j_{s}}\leq u-1/s\}}\eta\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\{u_{j_{s}}\leq u-1/s\}}\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq s\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{X}|u-u_{j_{s}}|\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}\leq\frac{1}{s}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\to 0\quad\text{as }s\to+\infty.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\{u_{j_{s}}\leq u-1/s\}}\eta\omega_{w_{s}}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\int_{\{u_{j_{s}}\leq u-1/s\}}\eta\omega_{w_{s}}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq s\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{X}|u-u_{j_{s}}|\omega_{w_{s}}^{n}\to 0\quad\text{as }s\to+\infty.

The last two estimates complete the proof of the lemma. This also completed the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

We shall have a similar criterion for convergence in capacity
[
KN23a
, Proposition 2.8]
. Let us recall
[
DK12
, Theorem 3.5]
and
[
KN15
, Lemmas 2.1, 2.2]
.
Let
B>0
be a uniform constant satisfying

\displaystyle-B\omega^{2}\leq 2ndd^{c}\omega\leq B\omega^{2},

\displaystyle-B\omega^{3}\leq 4n^{2}d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\leq B\omega^{3}.

Then, for
\varphi,\psi\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
satisfying
\sup_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}(\psi-\varphi)\leq 1
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\omega_{\psi}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\omega_{\varphi}^{n}+C_{n}\max\{1,B\}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\{\psi<\varphi\}}\omega_{\varphi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k},

where
C_{n}
is a dimensional constant. If the dimension
n=2
, then we have a better estimate, namely,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\omega_{\psi}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{\varphi<\psi\}}\omega_{\varphi}^{2}+C_{n}\max\{1,B\}\int_{\{\psi<\varphi\}}\omega^{2},

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}
be the sequence in Theorem
1.1
. Then,
u_{j}
converges to
u
in capacity if and only if

(2.7)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|\omega_{u_{j}}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=0,\quad k=0,...,n.

In particular, if
n=2
, then the sequence converges in capacity.

Proof.

Suppose that
u_{j}\to u
in capacity. It follows from Lemma
2.3
that (
2.7
) holds for every
k=0,1,...,n
. Conversely, suppose that (
2.7
) holds true. Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed and without loss of generality we may assume that

-1\leq u_{j},u\leq 0.

We have

cap_{\omega}(|u_{j}-u|>\varepsilon)\leq cap_{\omega}(u_{j}-u>\varepsilon)+cap_{\omega}(u-u_{j}>\varepsilon).

By Hartogs’ lemma,
\max\{u_{j},u\}\to u
in capacity. Hence,

\lim_{j\to+\infty}cap_{\omega}(u_{j}-u>\varepsilon)\leq\lim_{j\to+\infty}cap_{\omega}(\max\{u_{j},u\}-u>0)=0.

It remains to prove that
cap_{\omega}(u-u_{j}>\varepsilon)\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
. Let
-1\leq\rho\leq 0
be a function in
PSH(X,\omega)
. Then,

\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon\}\subset\left\{u_{j}<(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4})u+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\rho-\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}\right\}\subset\left\{u_{j}<u-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right\}

Applying (
2.5
) for
\varphi=u_{j}
and
\psi=(1-\varepsilon/4)u+\varepsilon\rho/4-3\varepsilon/4
and then using the previous inclusions for domains of integration on the left hand side and on the right hand side, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon\}}\left[\omega+(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4})dd^{c}u+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}dd^{c}\rho\right]^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon/2\}}\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}+C_{n}\max\{1,B\}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon/2\}}\omega_{u_{j}}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k},

Since the integrand on the left hand side is larger than
(\varepsilon/4)^{n}\omega_{\rho}^{n}
, it follows that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)^{n}cap_{\omega}(u_{j}<u-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon/2\}}\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}

\displaystyle+C_{n}\max\{1,B\}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon/2\}}\omega_{u_{j}}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

Notice that for
k=0,...,n
we have

\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon/2\}}\omega_{u_{j}}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|\omega_{u_{j}}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}.

Combining this inequality with (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) we obtain

\lim_{j\to+\infty}cap_{\omega}(u_{j}<u-\varepsilon)=0
. The proof is completed.

Finally let us finish the proof in the case
n=2
. Indeed, by (
2.6
) the inequality corresponding to (
2.8
) reads

\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\right)^{2}cap_{\omega}(\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon\})\leq\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon/2\}}\omega_{u_{j}}^{2}+C_{n}\max\{1,B\}\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon/2\}}\omega^{2}.

The right hand side is bounded by

\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|\omega_{u_{j}}^{2}+\frac{2C_{n}}{\varepsilon}\max\{1,B\}\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|\omega^{2},

which tends to zero as
j
goes to infinity.
Since
\varepsilon>0
is fixed we conclude the convergence in capacity in dimension 2.
∎

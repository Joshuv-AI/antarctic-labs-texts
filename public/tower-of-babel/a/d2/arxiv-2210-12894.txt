Introduction

Feller 1939

In the present paper we are interested in the Feller diffusion as the continuum limit of a Bienaymé-Galton-Watson (BGW) process, and applications to population genetics

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-x_{0}\mu(t;\alpha)\frac{1-z}{1-p(s,t;\alpha)z}\right\},

which is of the form of Eq. (

∎

It will be convenient below to consider the pgf of

since the population at time

Since the

Hence

\displaystyle G_{A_{\infty}(s;t)\mid X(t)>0}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{A_{\infty}(s;t)\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)>0}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{A_{\infty}(s;t)}(z)-G_{A_{\infty}(s;t)}(0)}{1-G_{A_{\infty}(s;t)}(0)}

\displaystyle\frac{\exp\left\{-x_{0}\mu(t;\alpha)\frac{1-z}{1-p(s,t;\alpha)z}\right\}-\exp\left\{-x_{0}\mu(t;\alpha)\right\}}{1-\exp\left\{-x_{0}\mu(t;\alpha)\right\}}.

3.2
Sample Coalescence

To calculate the coalescent distribution for a sample of size
n
in terms of the coalescent distribution of the population we begin with the following
lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Conditional on
A_{\infty}(s;t)=k>0
, the probability that a sample of size
n>0
taken at time
t
has
j\leq k
ancestors at time
t-s
is

\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;t)=j\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)=k)={k\choose j}\frac{n!}{k_{(n)}}{n-1\choose j-1},\qquad 1\leq j\leq k,

where
k_{(n)}=k(k+1)\cdots(k+n-1)
is the rising factorial.
The distribution of
A_{n}(s;t)
conditional on
A_{\infty}(s;t)
is independent of the total population size at
t
.

Proof.

Since at a given time each family size within the population descended from an individual is i.i.d. exponentially distributed,
the distribution of relative family sizes of the population at time
t
descended from the
k
ancestors at time
t-s

is Dirichlet
(\mathbf{1}_{k})
, where the vector
\mathbf{1}_{k}=(1,\ldots,1)
has
k
entries, independent of the total population size. Hence the distribution of family
sizes
(N_{1},\ldots,N_{k})
in a sample of size
n
is Dirichlet-multinomial
(n,\mathbf{1}_{k})

[
20
, p80]
,

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{N}=\mathbf{n}\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)=k)=\frac{n!}{k_{(n)}}.

This is a uniform distribution over the simplicial lattice
\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 0:\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}=n\}
.
Any point
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})
in this lattice corresponding to exactly
j
ancestors has
j
non-zero components and
n-j
zero components,
with the non-zero components summing to
n
. Thus the number of points in this lattice corresponding to

j
ancestors is the number of ways of choosing the
j
ancestors from
k
, times the number of points in the simplicial lattice

\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}>0:\sum_{i=1}^{j}n_{i}=n\}
, that is,

{k\choose j}\times{n-1\choose j-1},

and the result follows.
∎

Theorem 2
.

In a sample of size
n>0
taken at time
t
, the distribution of the number of ancestors at time
t-s
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;t)=j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n!{n-1\choose j-1}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}{k\choose j}\frac{1}{k_{(n)}}\mathbb{P}\left(A_{\infty}(s;t)=k\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)>0\right),\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n.

Proof.

Because the sample size
n
is assumed to be strictly positive, so the number of ancestors of
the entire population at time
t-s
must also be strictly positive. Equivalently, conditioning on
A_{\infty}(s;t)>0

has no effect on the probability of the event
A_{n}(s;t)=j
. Then
from Lemma
1
, for
1\leq j\leq k
and
0<n<\infty
we have the joint distribution

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;t)=j,A_{\infty}(s;t)=k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;t)=j,A_{\infty}(s;t)=k\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)>0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;t)=j\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)=k)\mathbb{P}(A_{\infty}(s;t)=k\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)>0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n!{n-1\choose j-1}{k\choose j}\frac{1}{k_{(n)}}\mathbb{P}(A_{\infty}(s;t)=k\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)>0).

Note that the conditioning
A_{\infty}(s;t)>0
in this proof is essential to ensure that the joint distribution is correctly
normalised: The range of
A_{\infty}(s;t)
includes zero, corresponding to extinction of the population, whereas the range of
A_{n}(s;t)
for
n>0
,
does not include zero. Since
A_{\infty}(s;t)\geq A_{n}(s;t)>0
, the event
A_{\infty}(s;t)=0
must be excluded.
Summing over
k\geq j
then gives the required marginal distribution of
A_{n}(s;t)
.
∎

Coalescence at fixed

The asymptotic limit as

Noting that for

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mu(t;\alpha)=0,\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}p(s,t;\alpha)=e^{-|\alpha|s},

This is the pgf of a shifted geometric distribution,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{\infty}(s;t)=k\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)>0)=(1-e^{-|\alpha|s})e^{-|\alpha|(k-1)s},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(T_{k}>s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{\infty}(s;t)\geq k\mid A_{\infty}(s;t)>0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(1-e^{-|\alpha|s})e^{-|\alpha|(j-1)s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-|\alpha|(k-1)s}.

Thus

has an exponential distribution with rate
|\alpha|(k-1)
and mean

.
The mean coalescence times

are the same as in the Kingman coalescent apart from scale. If a mutation occurs while
k
ancestors then the mutant relative frequency

U
in the population is Beta
(1,k-1)
with density
(k-1)(1-u)^{k-2},\>u\in(0,1)

4.1
Quasi-stationary subcritical sample coalescent

Substituting Eq. (

\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;\infty)=j)=n!{n-1\choose j-1}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}{k\choose j}\frac{1}{k_{(n)}}(1-e^{-|\alpha|s})e^{-|\alpha|(k-1)s}.

Writing

\frac{1}{k_{(n)}}=\frac{(k-1)!}{(k+n-1)!}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\cdot B(k,n)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}u^{k-1}(1-u)^{n-1}du,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;\infty)=j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n{n-1\choose j-1}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}{k\choose j}\int_{0}^{1}u^{k-1}(1-u)^{n-1}du\cdot(1-e^{-|\alpha|s})e^{-(k-1)|\alpha|s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n{n-1\choose j-1}(1-e^{-|\alpha|s})\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-(j-1)|\alpha|s}}{(1-ue^{-|\alpha|s})^{j+1}}u^{j-1}(1-u)^{n-1}du.

We are interested in the expected sample inter-coalescent waiting times. Begin with the integral

\mathcal{I}_{j}(u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-|\alpha|s})\frac{e^{-(j-1)|\alpha|s}}{(1-ue^{-|\alpha|s})^{j+1}}ds=\frac{1}{|\alpha|j(j-1)(1-u)^{j-1}},

which follows from induction by checking that
\mathcal{I}_{2}(u)=\{2|\alpha|(1-u)\}^{-1}

\mathcal{I}_{j+1}(u)=(j+1)^{-1}\mathcal{I}_{j}^{\prime}(u)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[W_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[T_{j}-T_{j+1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\{\mathbb{P}(T_{j}>s)-\mathbb{P}(T_{j+1}>s)\}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\{\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;\infty)\geq j)-\mathbb{P}(A_{n}(s;\infty)\geq j+1)\}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(A_{n}(s;\infty)=j\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n{n-1\choose j-1}\frac{1}{|\alpha|j(j-1)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{j-1}(1-u)^{n-j}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n{n-1\choose j-1}\frac{1}{|\alpha|j(j-1)}B(j,n-j+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\alpha|j(j-1)}.

Once again this leads to the same expected waiting times as the Kingman coalescent, up to a scale.
If neutral mutations between multiple alleles are included, in the limit of small mutation rates these waiting times lead to
an identical distribution as that for a neutral stationary Wright-Fisher population
[

.
This equivalence has also been discovered by

using Laplace transform methods.

In addition to its expectation, one can also deduce the distribution of

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(T_{k}>t_{k}\mid T_{k+1}=t_{k+1})=\mathbb{P}(A_{k}(W_{k},\infty)=k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(1-e^{-|\alpha|w_{k}})\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-|\alpha|(k-1)w_{k}}}{(1-ue^{-|\alpha|w_{k}})^{k+1}}u^{k-1}(1-u)^{k-1}du,

by substituting

in Eq, (

\mathbb{P}(W_{k}>w)=k(1-e^{-|\alpha|w})\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-|\alpha|(k-1)w}}{(1-ue^{-|\alpha|w})^{k+1}}u^{k-1}(1-u)^{k-1}du,

which differs from the exponential distribution waiting time for a Kingman coalescent. The formula applies both to the coalescent tree of
the entire population and to the coalescent tree of a finite sample.

\alpha\tau=\log\left(1+(e^{\alpha s}-1)e^{\rho_{s}}\right),\qquad 0\leq\rho_{s}<\infty.

Coalescent times in the RRP of a super-critical Feller diffusion

The following theorem gives the distribution of coalescent times as defined in Fig.
3
and of the number of ancestors at time
\tau
before present.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\cdots<T_{j}<\cdots<T_{3}<T_{2}<T_{1}
be coalescence times in the coalescent tree of the reverse reconstructed process
of a super-critical Feller diffusion beginning with population size
x
. The coalescent tree is assumed to be extended back in time so that there is a single
ancestor. Then the coalescence times are distributed as a non-homogeneous Poisson process back in time with rate
\frac{d}{d\tau}\{-x/\beta(\tau)\}
,
where
\beta(\tau)
is defined by Eq. (
8
), and

\mathbb{P}\left(N(\tau)=j\right)=\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{j}\exp\left\{-\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right\},\>\tau>0,\ j=1,2,\ldots,

(25)

where
N(\tau)
is be the number of ancestors at time
\tau
back.

Proof.

Suppose that the current time is
t
from the initiation of the population with one founder.
We argue conditional on
M(t-s)=m
where
\{M(u)\}_{0\leq u\leq t-s}
is the RP in Fig.
5
(a),
then let
m\to\infty,s\to 0,\frac{1}{2}sm=x
, conditioning on the diffusion population size at
t
being
x
in the limit.
Coalescent times before taking the limit form a reverse Markov chain with

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(T_{j}>\tau_{j}\mid T_{j+1}=\tau_{j+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-j\int_{\tau_{j+1}}^{\tau_{j}}\mu_{\text{eff}}(\xi)d\xi\right\}

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-j\log\left(\frac{e^{\alpha\tau_{j}}-1}{e^{\alpha\tau_{j+1}}-1}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e^{\alpha\tau_{j+1}}-1}{e^{\alpha\tau_{j}}-1}\right)^{j},\ j=m-1,\ldots,1

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(T_{m}>\tau_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e^{\alpha s}-1}{e^{\alpha\tau_{m}}-1}\right)^{m},\ \tau_{m}>s.

(27)

The distribution of
T_{j}
given
T_{j+1}=\tau_{j+1}
is seen to be that of the minimum of
j
independent random variables with distribution function
F
such that

1-F_{\tau_{j+1}}(\tau)=\frac{e^{\alpha\tau_{j+1}}-1}{e^{\alpha\tau}-1},\quad\tau>\tau_{j+1}.

That is,
T_{m},T_{m-1},\ldots T_{1}
are distributed as order statistics from small to large in an independent sample of
m
from a distribution with

1-F(\tau)=\frac{e^{\alpha s}-1}{e^{\alpha\tau}-1},\quad\tau>s,

and corresponding density

f(\tau)=\frac{\alpha e^{\alpha\tau}(e^{\alpha s}-1)}{(e^{\alpha\tau}-1)^{2}},\quad\tau>s,

because

\mathbb{P}\left(T_{j}>\tau_{j}\mid T_{j+1}=\tau_{j+1}\right)=\left(\frac{1-F(\tau_{j})}{1-F(\tau_{j+1})}\right)^{j},

agreeing with (
26
).
This is the probability that there are
j
sample points larger than
\tau_{j}
in the distribution truncated below at
\tau_{j+1}
.
The dependence in
s
appears in the distribution of
T_{m}
in (
27
).
Consider the pre-limit density of the
r
maximal points
T_{1}>T_{2}>\cdots>T_{r}
for fixed
r
,

\frac{m!}{(m-r)!}f(\tau_{1})\cdots f(\tau_{t})F(\tau_{r})^{m-r},\ \tau_{1}>\tau_{2}>\cdots>\tau_{r}.

Now

mf(\tau)\to\frac{2x\alpha^{2}e^{\alpha\tau}}{(e^{\alpha\tau}-1)^{2}}=\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\frac{-x}{\beta(\tau)}\right),

and

F(\tau)^{m-r}\to\exp\left\{-\frac{2\alpha x}{e^{\alpha\tau}-1}\right\}=\exp\left\{\frac{-x}{\beta(\tau)}\right\}.

The limit density of
T_{1},\ldots,T_{r}
is therefore

\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{d}{d\tau_{j}}\left\{\frac{-x}{\beta(\tau_{j})}\right\}\right)\exp\left\{\frac{-x}{\beta(\tau_{r})}\right\},\quad\tau_{1}>\tau_{2}>\cdots>\tau_{r}>0.

These are finite-dimensional densities of points in the non-homogeneous Poisson process in the statement of the theorem, so the proof is completed.
The Poisson distribution (
25
) follows, however it can be argued directly. In the pre-limit the probability that in a sample of
m
there are

m-j
points less than
\tau
and
j
points greater than
\tau
is

{m\choose j}\left(1-F(\tau)\right)^{j}F(\tau)^{m-j}

whose limit is Eq. (
25
).

∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
N_{n}(\tau)
be the number of ancestors of a sample of
n
taken from a super-critical Feller
diffusion with current population size
x
. The coalescent tree of the sample is assumed
to be extended back in time so that there is a single ancestor. Then for
j=n,n-1,\ldots,1

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(N_{n}(\tau)=j\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n_{[j]}}{n_{(j)}j!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{j}\exp\left\{-\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right\}M\left(j,j+n,\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(j-1)!}{n\choose j}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{j}\int_{0}^{1}\exp\left\{-(1-v)\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right\}v^{j-1}(1-v)^{n-1}dv,

where
M
is Kummer’s confluent hypergeometric function defined by
[
1
, Eq. (13.1.2)]

M(a,b,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{(k)}}{b_{(k)}}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}.

Let
\left\{W^{(n)}_{j}\right\}_{j=2}^{n}
be the inter-coalescent times in a sample of
n
.
The mean inter-coalescent times are

\mathbb{E}\left[W^{(n)}_{j}\right]=\frac{2(2\alpha)^{j-1}x^{j}}{(j-1)!}{n\choose j}\int_{0}^{1}v^{j-1}(1-v)^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{j-1}}{1+u}e^{-2\alpha x(1-v)u}dudv.

(29)

Proof.

From Lemma
1

\mathbb{P}(N_{n}(\tau)=j\mid N(\tau)=l)={l\choose j}\frac{n!}{l_{(n)}}{n-1\choose j-1},\qquad 1\leq j\leq l,

(30)

because
N_{n}(\tau)
is independent of the total current population size
X
conditional on
N_{\infty}(\tau)
. The distribution (
30
)
also only depends on the ancestry at time
\tau
back from present time, not on the fact that the process is initiated from one founder.

Then from Theorem
4
the unconditional probability

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(N_{n}(\tau)=j\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=j}^{\infty}{l\choose j}\frac{n!}{l_{(n)}}{n-1\choose j-1}\frac{1}{l!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{l}\exp\left\{-\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{j!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{j}\exp\left\{-\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right\}\frac{(n-1)!n!}{(n-j)!(n-1+j)!}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{k}\frac{j_{(k)}}{(j+n)_{(k)}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{n_{[j]}}{n_{(j)}j!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{j}\exp\left\{-\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right\}M\left(j,j+n,\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right),

where the summation shift
l=k+j
has been used in the second line. The alternate integral form in Eq. (
1
) follows from

\displaystyle e^{-x/\beta(\tau)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{k}\frac{j_{(k)}}{(j+n)_{(k)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-x/\beta(\tau)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{k}\frac{B(j+k,n)}{B(j,n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{B(j,n)}e^{-x/\beta(\tau)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{x}{\beta(\tau)}\right)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}v^{j+k-1}(1-v)^{n-1}dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(j+n-1)!}{(j-1)!(n-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-(1-v)x/\beta(\tau)}v^{j+k-1}(1-v)^{n-1}dv.

The mean inter-coalescent times are

\mathbb{E}\left[W^{(n)}_{j}\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(N_{n}(\tau)=j\right)d\tau

which evaluates to Eq. (
29
) after change of integration variable

u=\frac{1}{2\alpha\beta(\tau)}=\frac{1}{e^{\alpha\tau}-1},\qquad\tau=\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{u}\right),

d\tau=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{du}{u(u+1)}

and some further simplification.

8
Summary and Conclusions

The results of this paper hinge on the two key theorems of Section
3
which give formulae for the distribution of the number of
ancestors at time
t-s
of the population (Theorem
1
) and of a uniform random sample of size
n
(Theorem
2
) from a
Feller diffusion at a time
t
since initiation of the process in terms of the parameter
\alpha
of the diffusion and an initial condition
X(0)=x_{0}
.
Two applications of these theorems are given.
The first application is an efficient derivation of sample coalescent waiting times for the quasi-stationary subcritical Feller diffusion (Section
4
).

In the second application the coalescent tree of a supercritical Feller diffusion is identified as the limit of the RP of a BD process with appropriately
scaled birth and death rates (Theorem
3
). The corresponding coalescent tree assuming a single ancestor is then used
to calculate the distribution of coalescent times before the present time
(Theorem
4
) and to obtain a formula for expected coalescent times in the ancestry of a finite sample
(Corollary
1
).

With the intention of being of interest to the population genetics community, who are familiar with the
[
23
]
limit
in which the population size is taken to infinity while generation times and mutation rates shrink to zero, we have taken the approach of treating the Feller
diffusion primarily as the continuum limit of a discrete BGW process. The results of our theorems can be translated to all physically meaningful quantities in the
underlying BGW process being approximated,
including the mean
\lambda
and variance
\sigma^{2}
of the number of offspring per parent per generation, initial population counts at initiation of a BG
process
m_{0}
and
y_{0}
, and observed population counts at a later time
y
, via Eqs. (
5
), (
6
), and (
24
).

Stability through non-shadows
Jun Gao, Hong Liu, Zixiang Xu

Abstract
We study families ℱ ⊆ 2 [ n ] \mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]} with restricted intersections and prove a conjecture of Snevily in a stronger form for large n n . We also obtain stability results for Kleitman’s isodiametric inequality and families with bounded set-wise differences. Our proofs introduce a new twist to the classical linear algebra method, harnessing the non-shadows of ℱ \mathcal{F} , which may be of independent interest.

Introduction

1.1
Restricted intersections

The celebrated theorem of Erdős, Ko and Rado
[
8
]
states that when
n\geq 2k
, every
k
-uniform intersecting family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
has size at most
\binom{n-1}{k-1}
. We can view the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem as a result on families with restricted intersections where the empty intersection is forbidden.
A family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is
Sperner
if for any distinct
A,B\in{\mathcal{F}}
,
A\not\subseteq B
. Another cornerstone result in extremal set theory is Sperner’s theorem
[
30
]
from 1928, stating that any Sperner family in
2^{[n]}
has size at most
\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
. In Sperner’s theorem,
|A\setminus B|=0
is forbidden.

What can we say if we instead prescribe all possible pairwise intersection sizes? Formally, for a subset
L
of non-negative integers, a family
\mathcal{F}
is
L
-intersecting if for any distinct
F,F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
,
|F\cap F^{\prime}|\in L
. Results of this type date back to the work of Fisher
[
9
]
, who considered the
L
-intersecting problem when
L
consists of a single element. More precisely, Fisher
[
9
]
proved that a uniform family with this property cannot contain more sets than the size of its underlying set. Later, this problem was extensively studied via linear algebra methods by e.g., Frankl-Wilson
[
14
]
, Snevily
[
29
]
, Ray-Chaudhuri-Wilson
[
26
]
and Alon-Babai-Suzuki
[
1
]
. We also refer the interested readers to the recent survey
[
15
]
.

The following fundamental result was proved by Ray-Chaudhuri and Wilson
[
26
]
in 1975.

Theorem 1.1

(Ray-Chaudhuri-Wilson
[
26
]
)
.

Let
L=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
be a set of
s
non-negative integers and
k
be a positive integer. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
k
-uniform
L
-intersecting family, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{s}
.

Snevily
[
28
]
proposed the following conjecture in 1995.

Conjecture 1.2

(Snevily
[
28
]
)
.

Let
L=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
be a set of
s
non-negative integers and
K=\{k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r}\}
be a set of
r
positive integers with
\max{\ell_{i}}<\min{k_{j}}
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\binom{[n]}{k_{i}}
be an
L
-intersecting family, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{s}
.

This conjecture, if true, would be a generalization of the Ray-Chaudhuri-Wilson theorem. Indeed, Theorem
1.1
shows that Conjecture
1.2
is true when
|K|=1
. Using the multilinear polynomial methods, Alon, Babai and Suzuki
[
1
]
proved that families as above satisfy
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{s}+\cdots+\binom{n}{s-r+1}
and Snevily
[
27
]
showed that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n-1}{s}+\cdots+\binom{n-1}{0}
. Both of these bounds verify Conjecture
1.2
asymptotically, that is,
|\mathcal{F}|\leq(1+o(1))\binom{n}{s}
when
n\rightarrow\infty
.

In support of Conjecture
1.2
, Snevily
[
28
]
proved the special case when
L=\{0,1,\ldots,s-1\}
, and for the general case that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=s-2r+1}^{s}\binom{n-1}{i}
, implying that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{s}+\binom{n}{s-1}
for large
n
. For more results related to Conjecture
1.2
, we refer the readers to
[
6
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
31
]
and the reference therein.

Observe that, if a set system
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
satisfies the conditions in Conjecture
1.2
, then
\mathcal{F}
is Sperner. By LYM inequality (Theorem
2.2
), It is clear that Conjecture
1.2
is true when
n<2s-1
. We propose a stronger conjecture for Sperner systems as follows.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
n,s
be two integers with
n\geq 2s-1
,
L=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
be a set of
s
non-negative integers and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be an
L
-intersecting Sperner system, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{s}
.

Clearly, Conjecture
1.3
implies Conjecture
1.2
. Our first result verifies Conjecture
1.3
when
0\in L
for reasonably large
n
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
L=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
be a set of
s
non-negative integers and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be an
L
-intersecting Sperner family, we have
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s}\binom{n-1}{i}
. In particular, if
0\in L
and
n\geq 3s^{2}
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{s}
, with equality holds if and only if
L=\{0,1,2,\ldots,s-1\}
and
\mathcal{F}=\binom{[n]}{s}
.

Note that when
L=\{0\}
,
\mathcal{F}=\{\{1\},\{2\},\ldots,\{n\}\}
is a
\{0\}
-intersecting Sperner family, showing that the above upper bound
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s}\binom{n-1}{i}
cannot be improved in general.

What if
0\notin L
? We say a family
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{m}\}\subseteq 2^{n}
has
rank

t
if
\max_{i\in[m]}{|F_{i}|}=t
, and say
\mathcal{F}
is
trivially

\ell
-intersecting if there is a set
A
with size
\ell
such that
A\subseteq\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}F_{i}
. We obtain better bounds when
0\notin L
and
\mathcal{F}
has relatively small rank.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
L=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
be a set of
s
positive integers with
\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{s}
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be an
L
-intersecting Sperner family with rank
M
. Then either
\mathcal{F}
is trivially
\ell_{1}
-intersecting and then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s}{\binom{n-2}{i}}={\binom{n}{s}}-\Omega_{s}(n^{s-1})
, or
|\mathcal{F}|=O_{M,s}(n^{s-1})
.

Combining Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
, we see that Conjecture
1.2
is true when
n
is sufficiently large.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
L=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
be a set of
s
non-negative integers and let
K=\{k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r}\}
be a set of
r
positive integers with
\max{\ell_{i}}<\min{k_{j}}
. There exists
n_{0}
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}
and
L
-intersecting family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\binom{[n]}{k_{i}}
,
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{s}
.

1.2
Restricted symmetric/set-wise differences

For set systems with bounded symmetric differences, Kleitman
[
22
]
proved the following classical result: for integers
n>d
and a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
with
|A\triangle B|\leq d
for any
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
,

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}&d=2k;\\
2\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-1}{i}&d=2k+1.\end{cases}

The bounds in both cases above are optimal. For the even case, the upper bound can be attained by the radius-
k
Hamming ball
\mathcal{K}(n,k):=\{F:F\subseteq[n],|F|\leq k\}
, and for the odd case, consider the family
\mathcal{K}_{y}(n,k):=\{F:F\subseteq[n],|F\setminus\{y\}|\leq k\}
. The original proof of Kleitman is combinatorial. Recently, Huang, Klurman and Pohoata
[
17
]
provided an algebraic proof via the Cvetković bound
[
7
]
and extended the results when the allowed symmetric differences lie in a set of consecutive integers.

For a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
and a subset
S\subseteq[n]
, we define the
translate
of
\mathcal{F}
by
S
as
\mathcal{F}\triangle S:=\{F\triangle S:F\in\mathcal{F}\}
. Recently, Frankl
[
12
]
proved the following stability result for Kleitman’s theorem.

Theorem 1.7

(Frankl
[
12
]
)
.

Let
n\geq d+2
,
d\geqslant 0
and let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
with
|A\triangle B|\leq d
for any
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
.

1.

If
d=2k
and
\mathcal{F}
is not contained in any translate of
\mathcal{K}(n,k)
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n-k-1}{k}+1
.

2.

If
d=2k+1
and
\mathcal{F}
is not contained in any translate of
\mathcal{K}_{y}(n,k)
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-1}{i}-\binom{n-k-2}{k}+1
.

Frankl’s proof is combinatorial and relatively involved, making use of his earlier stability result for Katona theorem
[
13
]
. We prove a finer stability result for Kleitman’s theorem for the odd case.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family of subsets of
[n]
with
|A\triangle B|\leq 2k+1
for any
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
, then
either
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-2\binom{n-5k-1}{k}
, or
\mathcal{F}
is contained in some translate of
\mathcal{K}(n,k+1)
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{F}
is contained in some translate of
\mathcal{K}(n,k+1)
, then either
\mathcal{F}
is contained in some translate of
\mathcal{K}_{y}(n,k)
, or
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-1}{i}-\binom{n-k-2}{k}+1
.

Note that if
\mathcal{F}
is not contained in any translate of
\mathcal{K}(n,k+1)
, our upper bound
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-2\binom{n-5k-1}{k}
is better than the bound
|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-1}{i}-\binom{n-k-2}{k}+1
in Theorem
1.7
when
n\geq 10k^{2}
. We remark that our method does also apply to the even case. However, it yields a slighly weaker bound
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n+1}{i}-\binom{n-5k}{k}
when
\mathcal{F}
is not a translate of
\mathcal{K}(n,k)
.

Another type of well-studied restricted intersection problem is to bound the set-wise differences. In 1983, Katona
[
21
]
asked if we require
|A\setminus B|\leq k
for any distinct
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
, then what is the largest such
\mathcal{F}
? Frankl
[
10
]
proved that
for a set
L
of non-negative integers, given a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
so that for any distinct
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
,
|A\setminus B|\in L
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{|L|}\binom{n}{i}
.

Our method also yields stability result for families with bounded set-wise differences as follows.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
n,t,k
be positive integers with
k<t\leq\frac{k+n}{2}
and
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{m}\}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family with rank
t
and
|F_{i}\setminus F_{j}|\leq k
for any
F_{i},F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
, then either
\mathcal{F}
is trivially
(t-k)
-intersecting and
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-(t-k)}{i}
, or
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n-t-2k}{k}
. In the latter case, if
n
is sufficiently large in terms of
t
and
k
, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq(1-c)\binom{n-(t-k)}{k},
for some constant
c>0
.

We remark that the upper bound on the rank
t\leq\frac{k+n}{2}
is not restrictive. Indeed, for any sets
A,B
,
|A\setminus B|=|B^{c}\setminus A^{c}|
. Thus, when
t>\frac{k+n}{2}
, we can consider the family consisting of
complement

F^{c}
of all sets
F\in\mathcal{F}
instead.

For families with set-wise differences lying in
L
, where
|L|=k
, Frankl
[
10
]
conjectured that if the family is Sperner, then the upper bound
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}
can be improved to
\binom{n}{k}
. As a corollary, we show that this conjecture holds for
L=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
and large
n
. The following corollary can also be deduced from the main results in
[
11
]
.

Corollary 1.10
.

Given integer
k
and Sperner family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
with
|A\setminus B|\leq k
for any
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
, if
n
is sufficiently large, then
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{k}
, with equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}=\binom{[n]}{k}
.

1.3
Our method

Linear algebra method has proven to be a powerful tool in extremal set theory, see e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
19
,
25
,
26
,
29
]
. Given a family
{\mathcal{F}}
, in this approach we associate members of
{\mathcal{F}}
with multilinear polynomials that are linearly independent. We can then bound the cardinality of the family by the dimension of the underlying space. Improvements can often be made by cleverly adding an extra set of polynomials, which together with the original ones are still linearly independent.

We introduce a new twist to this classical method, utilizing the non-shadows of the family to find a large collection of polynomials that can be added. There are several advantages of our variation. First of all, the linear independence can usually be verified easily. A more important feature is that, as the new polynomials are associated with non-shadows of
{\mathcal{F}}
, we can gather additional structural information of
{\mathcal{F}}
, which not only offers optimal bounds in many scenarios but also provides a stability result as shown in Theorems
1.8
and
1.9
.

Organization.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we will collect some useful tools and give a short proof of a special case of the Katona intersection theorem (Theorem
2.4
) to illustrate our method. We will prove the stability results, Theorems
1.8
and
1.9
, in Section
3
. The proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
will be presented in Section
4
.

Notations.
In this paper, we usually regard
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
as the ground set. For a set
A\subseteq[n]
, we write
A^{c}
for its complement, that is,
A^{c}=[n]\setminus A
. For a subset
A\subseteq[n]
, we will use
\binom{A}{k}
to denote the family of all subsets of
A
with size
k
and
\binom{A}{\leq k}
to denote the family of all subsets of
A
with size at most
k
. For a pair of vectors
\bm{x}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
\bm{y}=\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}\}
, we define their inner product as
\bm{x}\cdot\bm{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
. We use
\bm{1}_{n}
to denote the length-
n
all-ones vector
(1,1,\ldots,1)
and omit the subscript when the dimension is clear. Given a polynomial
f
in
n
variables
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}
, define its
multilinear reduction

\tilde{f}
to be the polynomial obtained from
f
by replacing each
x_{i}^{t}
term by
x_{i}
for any positive integer
t
and
1\leq i\leq n
. For a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
, we denote the
k
-shadow
of
\mathcal{F}
as
\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}}:=\{T\in\binom{[n]}{k}:T\subseteq F\ \text{for\ some\ }F\in\mathcal{F}\}
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Some useful tools

Hilton and Milner
[
16
]
showed the following result, which states that the size of non-trivial intersecting families is noticeably smaller than that of the trivial ones.

Theorem 2.1

(Hilton-Milner
[
16
]
)
.

Let
n,k
be positive integers with
n>2k
. If
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
is an intersecting family and
\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F=\emptyset
, then we have

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n-1}{k-1}-\binom{n-k-1}{k-1}+1.

For Sperner families, the Lubell–Yamamoto–Meshalkin inequality, discovered by Bollobás
[
5
]
, Lubell
[
23
]
, Mešalkin
[
24
]
and Yamamoto
[
32
]
independently, is useful.

Theorem 2.2

(LYM inequality)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a Sperner family of subsets of
[n]
, then

\sum\limits_{A\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{|A|}}\leq 1.

We also need the triangular criterion when we want to prove a sequence of polynomials are linearly independent.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m}
be functions in a linear space. If
v^{(1)},v^{(2)},\ldots,v^{(m)}
are vectors such that
f_{i}(v^{(i)})\neq 0
for
1\leq i\leq m
and
f_{i}(v^{(j)})=0
for
i>j
, then
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m}
are linearly independent.

2.2
Warm up: Katona intersection theorem

As a warm-up and illustration of our method, we will give a short proof of a special case of the Katona intersection theorem.

Theorem 2.4

(Katona
[
20
]
)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k+1}
is an intersecting family. Then
|\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}}|\geq|\mathcal{F}|
.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{m}\}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k+1}
is an intersecting family. Let
\bm{a}_{i},\bm{b}_{i}
be the characteristic vectors of
F_{i},F_{i}^{c}
, respectively. For
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
i\in[m]
, we define
g_{i}(\bm{x})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{b}_{i}-j)
and let
f_{i}=\tilde{g}_{i}
be the multilinear reduction of
g_{i}
. Note that
\ker(f_{i})
corresponds to characteristic vectors of subsets
F\subseteq[n]
with
|F\setminus F_{i}|\in[k]
,

Let
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{r}\}=\binom{[n]}{\leq k-1}
with
|S_{i}|\leq|S_{j}|
for
i<j
, so
r=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{n}{i}
.
For each
S_{i}
, let
f_{S_{i}}
be the multilinear reduction of
g_{S_{i}}(\bm{x}):=(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{1}-k-1)\cdot\prod_{\ell\in S_{i}}x_{\ell}
, and
\bm{a}_{S_{i}}
be the characteristic vector of
S_{i}
. Let
\{T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{h}\}=\binom{[n]}{k}\setminus\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}}
be the collection of all
k
-sets that are not
k
-shadow of
\mathcal{F}
. For each
T_{i}
, we define
f_{T_{i}}(\bm{x}):=\prod_{j\in T_{i}}x_{j}
, and
\bm{a}_{T_{i}}
to be the characteristic vector of
T_{i}
. Here we can see that
\ker(f_{T_{i}})
corresponds to characteristic vectors of subsets of
[n]
which do not contain
T_{i}
, and
\ker(g_{S_{i}})
corresponds to characteristic vectors of subsets of
[n]
which contain
k+1
elements or do not contain
S_{i}
.

Claim 2.5
.

The polynomials
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},f_{S_{1}},\ldots,f_{S_{r}},f_{T_{1}},\ldots,f_{T_{h}}\}
are linearly independent.

Proof of claim.

We shall show that these polynomials satisfy the triangular criterion in Proposition
2.3
.
First note that
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})=(-1)^{k}k!\neq 0
as
\bm{a}_{i}\cdot\bm{b}_{i}=0
for each
i
. For
i\neq j
, we have
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{j})=0
as
\bm{a}_{j}\cdot\bm{b}_{i}=|F_{j}\cap F_{i}^{c}|=|F_{j}\setminus F_{i}|\in[k]
.
Next,
f_{S_{i}}(\bm{a}_{S_{i}})=(|S_{i}|-k-1)\neq 0
. Since
|F_{i}|=k+1
, we know that
f_{S_{i}}(\bm{a}_{j})=0
. By definition, if
i<j
, we have
|S_{i}|\leq|S_{j}|
and
S_{j}\setminus S_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Thus for any
i<j
, we have
f_{S_{j}}(\bm{a}_{S_{i}})=0
. For
f_{T_{i}}
’s, we have
f_{T_{i}}(\bm{a}_{T_{i}})=1\neq 0
, and for any
T\in\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{i-1},T_{i+1}\ldots,T_{h},S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{r}\}
, we can see
|T|\leq|T_{i}|,T\neq T_{i}
and
T_{i}\setminus T\neq\emptyset
, so we have
f_{T_{i}}(\bm{a}_{T})=0
. Lastly, since
T_{i}\not\subseteq F_{j}
for any
i,j
, we have
f_{T_{i}}(\bm{a}_{j})=0
. Hence, Proposition
2.3
implies that
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m},f_{S_{1}},\ldots,f_{S_{r}},f_{T_{1}},\ldots,f_{T_{h}}\}
are linearly independent.
∎

Since all of these polynomials have degree at most
k
, we have
|\mathcal{F}|+r+h\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}
. As
r=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\binom{n}{i}
and
h=\binom{n}{k}-|\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}}|
, we have
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}}|
as desired.

∎

3
Stability

3.1
Families with bounded symmetric differences

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.8
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{o}
consist of all sets of odd size in
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}_{e}
consist of all sets of even size in
\mathcal{F}
. Observe that for any subsets
X,Y,Z\subseteq[n]
,
(X\triangle Y)\triangle(X\triangle Z)=Y\triangle Z
. Hence, by translating all sets in
{\mathcal{F}}
by some appropriate
U\in{\mathcal{F}}
, we may assume
\emptyset\in\mathcal{F}
and
|F|\leq 2k+1
for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Then without loss of generality, we can further assume that
|\mathcal{F}_{o}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{e}|
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{e}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\}
and
\bm{a}_{i},\bm{b}_{i}
be the characteristic vectors of
F_{i},F_{i}^{c}
, respectively. For
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
i\in[m]
, we define
g_{i}(\bm{x})=\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{b}_{i}+(\bm{1}-\bm{x})\cdot\bm{a}_{i}-2\ell)
and
f_{i}=\tilde{g}_{i}
. It is easy to see that
\ker(g_{i})
corresponds to characteristic vectors of subsets
F\subseteq[n]
with
|F_{i}\triangle F|\in\{2,4,\ldots,2k\}
.

By definition,
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})=(-2)^{k}k!\neq 0
as
\bm{a}_{i}\cdot\bm{b}_{i}=0
for each
i
. As each set in
\mathcal{F}_{e}
has even size,
\bm{a}_{i}\cdot\bm{b}_{j}+\bm{b}_{i}\cdot\bm{a}_{j}=|F_{i}\triangle F_{j}|\in\{2,4,\ldots,2k\}
for any
i\neq j
, so we have
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{j})=0
. By Proposition
2.3
,
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are linearly independent, which implies
m\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}
since the polynomials
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
lie in the space of
n
-variate polynomials of degree at most
k
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\binom{[n]}{k}\setminus\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}}_{e}:=\{T\in\binom{[n]}{k}:T\not\subseteq F_{i},\text{ for all }i\in[m]\}
. For each subset
T\in\mathcal{S}
, we define a polynomial
h_{T}(\bm{x})=\prod_{i\in T}x_{i}
and let
\bm{a}_{T}
be the characteristic vector of
T
. Similarly,
\ker(h_{T})
corresponds to characteristic vectors of subsets of
[n]
which do not contain
T
as a subset. The following claim will establish an upper bound
|\mathcal{F}_{e}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-|\mathcal{S}|
.

Claim 3.1
.

The polynomials
\{h_{T}\}_{T\in\mathcal{S}}
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are linearly independent.

Proof of claim.

For any
T\in\mathcal{S}
and
i\in[m]
, since
T\not\subseteq F_{i}
, we have
h_{T}(\bm{a}_{i})=0
. For any
T,S\in\mathcal{S}
, it is easy to check that
h_{T}(a_{S})\neq 0
if and only if
T=S
. By Proposition
2.3
,
\{h_{T}\}_{T\in\mathcal{S}}
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are linearly independent.
∎

If
|\mathcal{S}|\geq\binom{n-5k-1}{k}
, then we have

|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2|\mathcal{F}_{e}|\leq 2\big(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-|\mathcal{S}|\big)\leq 2\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-2\binom{n-5k-1}{k},

yielding the first alternative.

We may then assume
|\mathcal{S}|<\binom{n-5k-1}{k}
.
Relabelling
\mathcal{F}_{e}
if necessary, we may assume
2k+1\geq|F_{1}|\geq|F_{2}|\geq\cdots\geq|F_{m}|
. As
|F_{1}|
is maximum, we have
|F_{i}\setminus F_{1}|\leq|F_{1}\setminus F_{i}|
for any
1\leq i\leq m
. Since
|F_{1}\triangle F_{i}|\leq 2k+1
and both of
|F_{1}|
and
|F_{i}|
are even, we have
|F_{i}\setminus F_{1}|\leq k
and
|F_{1}|\leq 2k
. We can then draw the following claim.

Claim 3.2
.

If
|F_{i}\setminus F_{1}|=k
, then we have
|F_{1}\setminus F_{i}|=k
, and
|F_{1}|=|F_{i}|
.

Since
|\mathcal{S}|<\binom{n-5k-1}{k}
and
n-|F_{1}|>n-5k-1
, there exists some
k
-set
R\subseteq F_{1}^{c}
such that
R\in\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}_{e}}
. Consequently, there exists some
E\in\mathcal{F}_{e}
such that
R\subseteq E
and
|E\setminus F_{1}|\geq|R|=k
. In fact,
|E\setminus F_{1}|=k
as
|E\setminus F_{1}|\leq k
. Claim
3.2
then implies that
|F_{1}|=|E|
. Fix such
E
and let
A:=F_{1}\cap E
.

Claim 3.3
.

For any
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
|F\triangle A|\leq k+1
.

Proof of claim.

Suppose that there exists
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that
|F\triangle A|\geq k+2
. Since
|F_{1}|\leq 2k
,
|E\setminus F_{1}|=k
and
|F|\leq 2k+1
, we have
|F_{1}\cup E\cup F|\leq 5k+1
.
Since
|\mathcal{S}|<\binom{n-5k-1}{k}
, there exists some
k
-set
Q\subseteq[n]\setminus(F_{1}\cup E\cup F)
such that
Q\in\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}}_{e}
. Thus there is some
T\in\mathcal{F}_{e}
such that
Q\subseteq T
and
|T\cap([n]\setminus(F_{1}\cup E\cup F))|\geq k
. As
|T\setminus F_{1}|\leq k
, we have
T\cap([n]\setminus(F_{1}\cup E\cup F))=T\setminus F_{1}
.
Therefore
T\setminus F_{1}
,
E\setminus A
and
F_{1}\setminus A
are pairwise disjoint sets of size
k
. By Claim
3.2
,
|T\triangle F_{1}|=2k
,
|T\triangle E|=2k
and
T=A\cup(T\setminus F_{1})=A\cup(T\cap([n]\setminus(F_{1}\cup E\cup F)))
. Then we have

|T\triangle F|=|T\cap([n]\setminus(F_{1}\cup E\cup F))|+|A\triangle F|\geq k+k+2=2k+2,

a contradiction.
∎

By Claim
3.3
, if
|\mathcal{S}|<\binom{n-5k-1}{k}
, then
\mathcal{F}
should be contained in a translate of
\mathcal{K}(n,k+1)
. Without loss of generality, assume
\mathcal{F}
is contained in
\mathcal{K}(n,k+1)
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{H\in\mathcal{F},|H|=k+1\}
. Note that
\mathcal{H}
is an intersecting family, because the symmetric difference of any pair of subsets in
\mathcal{F}
is bounded by
2k+1
. If
\mathcal{H}
is a non-trivial intersecting family, by Hilton-Milner Theorem, we have

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}+|\mathcal{H}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}+\binom{n-1}{k}-\binom{n-k-2}{k}+1=2\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-1}{i}-\binom{n-k-2}{k}+1.

Otherwise, there exists some
y\in[n]
such that
y\in H
for any
H\in\mathcal{H}
, which implies
\mathcal{F}
is contained in
\mathcal{K}_{y}(n,k)
. This completes the proof.

3.2
Families with bounded set-wise differences

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.9
and then apply it to show Corollary
1.10
.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{m}\}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
be a family with rank
t
and
|F_{i}\setminus F_{j}|\leq k
for any
F_{i},F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
|F_{j}|\geq|F_{i}|
for any
i>j
, then we have
|F_{1}|=t
and
|F_{j}\setminus F_{i}|\geq 1
for any
i>j
.

Let
\bm{a}_{i},\bm{b}_{i}
be the characteristic vectors of
F_{i},F_{i}^{c}
, respectively. For
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
i\in[m]
, we define
g_{i}(\bm{x})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{b}_{i}-j)
and
f_{i}=\tilde{g}_{i}
. Here
\ker(f_{i})
corresponds to characteristic vectors of subsets
F\subseteq[n]
with
|F\setminus F_{i}|\in[k]
.

By definition,
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{i})=(-1)^{k}k!\neq 0
as
\bm{a}_{i}\cdot\bm{b}_{i}=0
for each
i
. For
i>j
, we have
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{j})=0
as
\bm{a}_{j}\cdot\bm{b}_{i}=|F_{j}\setminus F_{i}|\in[k]
. By Proposition
2.3
,
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are linearly independent, which implies
m\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{n}{j}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\binom{[n]}{k}\setminus\partial_{k}{\mathcal{F}}
. For each subset
T\in\mathcal{S}
, we define a polynomial
h_{T}(\bm{x})=\prod_{i\in T}x_{i}
. The following claim can be derived the same way as Claim
3.1
.

Claim 3.4
.

The polynomials
\{h_{T}\}_{T\in\mathcal{S}}
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are linearly independent.

Claim
3.4
implies that
m\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-|\mathcal{S}|
. Thus, we shall assume
|\mathcal{S}|<\binom{n-t-2k}{k}
, otherwise the second alternative holds.

Claim 3.5
.

For any pair of distinct
E,F\in\mathcal{F}
with
|F|=t,|E\setminus F|=k
, we must have
|E|=t
and
|F\setminus E|=k
.

Proof of claim.

Note that
\max_{i\in[m]}\{|F_{i}|\}=t
, thus we have
k\geq|F\setminus E|\geq|E\setminus F|\geq k
and so
|F\setminus E|=k
. Also,
|E|=|E\cup F|-|F\setminus E|=|E\cup F|-|E\setminus F|=|F|=t
.
∎

By our assumption, we have
\binom{|F_{1}^{c}|}{k}=\binom{n-t}{k}>|\mathcal{S}|
, hence there exists some
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that
|F\cap F_{1}^{c}|=|F\setminus F_{1}|=k
. By Claim
3.5
, we have
|F|=t
and
|F\cap F_{1}|=t-|F\setminus F_{1}|=t-k
. Let
F\cap F_{1}=A
. We can further extract more structural properties of
\mathcal{F}
as follows.

Claim 3.6
.

For any
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
, if
|F^{\prime}\cap F_{1}^{c}|=k
, then we have
F^{\prime}\cap F_{1}=A
.

Proof of claim.

Since
|F_{1}^{c}\setminus(F^{\prime}\cup F)|\geq n-t-2k
and
|\mathcal{S}|<\binom{n-t-2k}{k}
, there exist
T\in\binom{F_{1}^{c}\setminus(F^{\prime}\cup F)}{k}
and
E\in\mathcal{F}
such that
T\subseteq E
. Since
|T|=k
, we have
E\setminus F^{\prime}=E\setminus F=T\subseteq F_{1}^{c},
by Claim
3.5
we have
|E|=t
. Also note that
F\cap E\subseteq F_{1}
, so
|F\cap E|=|F\cap E\cap F_{1}|=t-k
, which implies
E\cap F_{1}=F\cap F_{1}=A
. Applying the same analysis on the (ordered) triple
(F^{\prime},E,F_{1})
instead of
(E,F,F_{1})
, we have
F^{\prime}\cap F_{1}=E\cap F_{1}=A
as desired.
∎

Next, we will show that for any
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
A\subseteq F^{\prime}\cap F_{1}
, and so
\mathcal{F}
is trivially
(t-k)
-intersecting with
A\subseteq\bigcap_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}F^{\prime}
. Suppose there exists some
F^{\prime}
, such that
A\setminus(F^{\prime}\cap F_{1})\neq\emptyset
, then since
|F_{1}^{c}\setminus F^{\prime}|\geq n-t-k
, and
|\mathcal{S}|<\binom{n-t-2k}{k}
, there exist subsets
T\in\binom{F_{1}^{c}\setminus F^{\prime}}{k}
and
E\in\mathcal{F}
such that
T\subseteq E
. By Claim
3.6
we know that
E\cap F_{1}=A
, so
|E\setminus F^{\prime}|
=
|T\cup(A\setminus F^{\prime})|\geq k+1
, which is a contradiction.

Then we have that
A\subseteq F_{i}
for all
i\in[m]
. Since
|F_{i}|\leq t
, we have
|F_{i}\setminus A|\leq k
, which implies that
m\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-(t-k)}{i}
.
The proof of Theorem
1.9
is finished.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.10
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{m}\}\subseteq 2^{[n]}
is a rank
t
Sperner family such that
|F_{i}\setminus F_{j}|\leq k
.
Suppose first that
t>\frac{k+n}{2}
, as
|F_{i}\setminus F_{j}|\leq k
for any
F_{i},F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
, we know
\min_{i\in[m]}\{|F_{i}|\}\geq t-k
. Define
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}:=\{F_{1}^{c},F_{2}^{c},\ldots,F_{m}^{c}\}
.
Then
\max_{i\in[m]}\{|F_{i}^{c}|\}=n-\min_{i\in[m]}\{|F_{i}|\}\leq n-(t-k)\leq\frac{k+n}{2}
and
|F_{i}^{c}\setminus F_{j}^{c}|=|F_{j}\setminus F_{i}|\leq k
for any
F_{i}^{c},F_{j}^{c}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, so we can assume
t\leq\frac{k+n}{2}
.

If
t>k
, by Theorem
1.9
, we have either

|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n}{i}-\binom{\frac{n-5k}{2}}{k}<\binom{n}{k},

when
n\gg k
, or there exists
A
with
|A|=t-k
and
A\subseteq F_{i}
for any
i\in[m]
, which implies
\{F_{i}\setminus A\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a Sperner family with size at most
k
.
By Theorem
2.2
, we have

1\geq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{\binom{n-(t-k)}{|F\setminus A|}}\geq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{\binom{n-(t-k)}{k}},

which implies
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n-(t-k)}{k}<\binom{n}{k}
.

If
t\leq k
. By Theorem
2.2
, we have

1\geq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{|F|}}\geq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}},

which implies
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{k}
, the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}=\binom{[n]}{k}
.
∎

4
Sperner families with restricted intersections

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
, respectively.

4.1
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Let
L=\{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
be a set of
s
non-negative integers with
\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\cdots<\ell_{s}
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{m}\}
be an
L
-intersecting Sperner family. By relabelling, we can assume there exists some integer
r
such that
1\in F_{i}
if and only if
1\leq i\leq r
. For each
1\leq i\leq m
, let
\bm{a}_{i}
be the characteristic vector of
F_{i}
.
For
\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
i\in[m]
, we define
g_{i}(\bm{x})=\prod_{\ell\in L,\ell\neq|F_{i}|}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{a}_{i}-\ell)
and
f_{i}(\bm{x})
be the multilinear reduction of
g_{i}(\bm{x})
with
x_{1}=1
, that is,
f_{i}(\bm{x})
is obtained from
g_{i}(\bm{x})
by replacing each
x_{i}^{t}
term by
x_{i}
for any
2\leq i\leq n
and
t\geq 2
, and
x_{1}^{t}
term by
1
for any
t\geq 1
.

Note that
\bm{a}_{i}\cdot\bm{a}_{j}=|F_{i}\cap F_{j}|
, and we replace
x_{1}^{t}
with
1
in our multilinear reduction to get
f_{i}
, this acts as if
1\in F_{j}
when we evaluate
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{j})
and hence

f_{i}(\bm{a}_{j})=\prod_{\ell\in L,\ell\neq|F_{i}|}(|F_{i}\cap(F_{j}\cup\{1\})|-\ell).

Moreover, observe that if
i>j
and
r\geq i
, then
|F_{i}\cap(F_{j}\cup\{1\})|=|F_{i}\cap F_{j}|
as
1\in F_{j}
, and if
i>j
and
r<i
, then
|F_{i}\cap(F_{j}\cup\{1\})|=|F_{i}\cap F_{j}|
also holds as
1\notin F_{i}
. Therefore, if
i>j
, we have
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{j})=\prod_{\ell\in L,\ell\neq|F_{i}|}(|F_{i}\cap F_{j}|-\ell)
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is a Sperner family, we know
|F_{i}\cap F_{j}|\in L\setminus\{|F_{i}|\}
, which implies that
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{j})=0
for
i>j
. If
i=j
, then
f_{i}(\bm{a}_{j})=\prod_{\ell\in L,\ell\neq|F_{i}|}(|F_{i}|-\ell)\neq 0
. By Proposition
2.3
, the polynomials
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are linearly independent, which implies
m\leq\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom{n-1}{j}
since for
i\in[m]
,
f_{i}(\bm{x})
has
n-1
variables
x_{2},x_{3},\ldots,x_{n}
and degree at most
s
. The first result then follows.

For the second part, let
\ell_{1}=0\in L
. We will prove
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{n}{s}
by induction on
s
, where
s=|L|
and
n\geq 3s^{2}
.
When
s=1
, by the first result, we have
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom{n-1}{j}=\binom{n}{1}
, and it is easy to see that equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}={[n]\choose 1}
.

Now, suppose
s\geq 2
and the result holds for any positive integer less than
s
. If
L=\{0,1,2,\ldots,s-1\}
, then for any
S\in\binom{[n]}{s}
, there exists at most one set in
\mathcal{F}
containing
S
. Construct a new family
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}:=\{F^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,F^{\prime}_{m}\}
, where
F^{\prime}_{i}
is an arbitrary subset of
F_{i}
with size
s
if
|F_{i}|>s
, and
F^{\prime}_{i}=F_{i}
otherwise. Since
\mathcal{F}
is a Sperner family,
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}
is also a Sperner family with
|F^{\prime}_{i}|\leq s
. Then by Theorem
2.2
, we have
m\leq\binom{n}{s}
. It is easy to see that the equality holds if and only if
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\binom{[n]}{s}
. Next, we assume that
L\neq\{0,1,2,\ldots,s-1\}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=\{F:F\in\mathcal{F},|F|\leq s\}
and
L^{\prime}=L\cap\{0,1,2,\ldots,s-1\}
. Then
\mathcal{H}
is an
L^{\prime}
-intersecting Sperner family, with
0\in L^{\prime}
and
|L^{\prime}|\leq s-1
. By the induction hypothesis, we have
|\mathcal{H}|\leq\binom{n}{s-1}
.

For each
x\in[n]
, let
\mathcal{F}_{x}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:x\in F\}
, and
L\setminus\{0\}=\{\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
. Then
\mathcal{F}_{x}
is an
L\setminus\{0\}
-intersecting Sperner family with
|L\setminus\{0\}|\leq s-1
, and by the first part
|\mathcal{F}_{x}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\binom{n-1}{i}
. Then via double counting, we have

(m-|\mathcal{H}|)(s+1)\leq\sum_{x\in[n]}|\mathcal{F}_{x}|\leq n\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\binom{n-1}{i}.

Since
n\geq 3s^{2},s\geq 2
, we have
\sum_{i=0}^{s-2}\binom{n-1}{i}\leq\binom{n-1}{s-2}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{s-2}2^{-i}\leq 2\binom{n-1}{s-2}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{H}|+\frac{n}{s+1}\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\binom{n-1}{i}\leq\binom{n}{s-1}+\frac{n}{s+1}\binom{n-1}{s-1}+\frac{n}{s+1}\sum_{i=0}^{s-2}\binom{n-1}{i}

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{s-1}+\frac{s}{s+1}\binom{n}{s}+\frac{2n}{s+1}\binom{n-1}{s-2}=\binom{n}{s-1}+\frac{s}{s+1}\binom{n}{s}+\frac{2(s-1)}{s+1}\binom{n}{s-1}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{3s-1}{s+1}\cdot\frac{s}{n-s+1}+\frac{s}{s+1}\bigg)\binom{n}{s}\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{s+1}\cdot\frac{3s^{2}-s}{3s^{2}-s+1}+\frac{s}{s+1}\bigg)\binom{n}{s}<\binom{n}{s}.

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.5

Let
T\in\binom{[n]}{\ell_{1}}
be the set satisfying that
|\{F\in\mathcal{F}:T\subseteq F\}|
is the largest among all sets in
\binom{[n]}{\ell_{1}}
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:T\subseteq F\}
. If
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{F}
, then
{\mathcal{F}}
is trivially
\ell_{1}
-intersecting and by Theorem
1.4
, as
|T|=\ell_{1}\geq 1
, we have
\{F\setminus T:F\in\mathcal{F}\}\subseteq 2^{[n]\setminus T}
and so

|\mathcal{F}|=\big|\{F\setminus T:F\in\mathcal{F}\}\big|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s}\binom{n-2}{i}=\binom{n}{s}-\binom{n-1}{s-1}+\sum_{i=0}^{s-2}\binom{n-2}{i}=\binom{n}{s}-\Omega_{s}(n^{s-1}).

We may then assume
{\mathcal{H}}\neq{\mathcal{F}}
and so there exists some
E\in\mathcal{F}
with
T\not\subseteq E
. For any
F\in\mathcal{H}
we have
|F\cap E|\geq\ell_{1}
, so
(E\setminus T)\cap F\neq\emptyset
, for otherwise
T\subseteq E
. Consequently, for each
x\in E\setminus T
, letting
\mathcal{F}_{x}=\{F:F\in\mathcal{H},x\in F\}
, we have
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in E\setminus T}\mathcal{F}_{x}
. Since for any sets
A,B\in\mathcal{F}_{x}
,
\{x\}\cup T\subseteq A\cap B
,
F_{x}
is an
\{\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ldots,\ell_{s}\}
-intersecting Sperner family. Then, by Theorem
1.4
, we have
|\mathcal{F}_{x}|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\binom{n-1}{i}
and
|\mathcal{H}|\leq M\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\binom{n-1}{i}
.

Take an arbitrary set
X\in\mathcal{F}
. For any set
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
|X\cap F|\geq\ell_{1}
, thus we can partition the whole family
\mathcal{F}
into subfamilies
\mathcal{H}_{Y}
according to which
\ell_{1}
-subset
Y\in\binom{X}{\ell_{1}}
its members contain. Note that for each such
{\mathcal{H}}_{Y}
,
|\mathcal{H}_{Y}|\leq|\mathcal{H}|
. As
|X|\leq M
, we have
|\mathcal{F}|\leq\binom{M}{\ell_{1}}|\mathcal{H}|=O_{M,s}(n^{s-1})
.

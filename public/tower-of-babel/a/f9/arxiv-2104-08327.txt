Polynomial Hermite–Padé m m -system for meromorphic functions on a compact Riemann surface
A. V. Komlov

Abstract
For an arbitrary tuple of m + 1 m+1 germs of analytic functions at a fixed point, we introduce the so-called polynomial Hermite–Padé m m -system (of order n n , n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} ), which consists of m m tuples of polynomials; these tuples, which are indexed by a natural number k ∈ [ 1 , … , m ] k\in[1,\dots,m] , are called the k k th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé m m -system . We study the weak asymptotics of the polynomials of the Hermite–Padé m m -system constructed at the point ∞ \infty from the tuple of germs [ 1 , f 1 , ∞ , … [1,f_{1,\infty},\dotsc , f m , ∞ ] f_{m,\infty}] of the functions 1 , f 1 , … , f m 1,f_{1},\dots,f_{m} that are meromorphic on some ( m + 1 ) (m+1) -sheeted branched covering π : ℜ → ℂ ^ \pi\colon\mathfrak{R}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}} of the Riemann sphere ℂ ^ \widehat{\mathbb{C}} of a compact Riemann surface ℜ \mathfrak{R} . In particular, under some additional condition on π \pi , we find the limit distribution of the zeros and the asymptotics of the ratios of the k k th polynomials for all k ∈ [ 1 , … , m ] k\in[1,\dots,m] . It turns out that in the case, where f j = f j f_{j}=f^{j} for some meromorphic function f f on ℜ \mathfrak{R} , the ratios of some k k th polynomials of such Hermite–Padé m m -system converge to the sum of the values of the function f f on the first k k sheets of the Nuttall partition of the Riemann surface ℜ \mathfrak{R} into sheets.

Introduction

Assume first that
f_{0,\infty}(z)\equiv 1,f_{1,\infty}(z),\dots,f_{m,\infty}(z)
are
m+1

arbitrary analytic germs at infinity (on the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
).
We fix a natural number
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
the tuple of
{m+1}\choose{k}
‘
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system
’ of order
n
,
which are constructed from the tuple of germs
[1,f_{1,\infty},\dots,f_{m,\infty}]
at the point
\infty\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
as follows. These are the polynomials

P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
,
0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{k}\leq m
, such that

\deg P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\leq(m+1-k)n
, at least one

P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\not\equiv 0
, and, for each index set
0<j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
,

P_{n;j_{1}\dots,j_{k}}(z)+\sum_{s=1}^{k}(-1)^{s}P_{n;0,j_{1},\dots,j_{s-1},j_{s+1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)f_{j_{s},\infty}(z)=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{kn+1}}\right)

(1)

as
z\to\infty
.
It is easily seen that condition (
1
) is a system of
(n(m+1)+1){m\choose k}
linear homogeneous equations
for
(n(m+1-k)+1){m+1\choose k}=n(m+1){m\choose k}+{m+1\choose k}
unknown coefficients of the polynomials
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
.
The coefficients of this system are some linear expressions of the first
(m+1)n
Taylor coefficients of the germs
f_{s,\infty}
. Hence the
polynomials
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
always exist, but, in general, are not unique.
(Note that the Hermite–Padé
m
-system can be constructed also from the germs
f_{j,\infty}
which
are meromorphic at
\infty
. In this case, one should increase the maximum possible degree
of the polynomials
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
. For example, it suffices to require that
\deg P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\leq(m+1-k)n+M
, where
M

is the maximal order of the poles of the germs
f_{j,\infty}
at
\infty
.)

It is clear that conditions (
1
) are linearly independent; however, the index 0 in them ‘plays a special role’.
One can give a different, the so-called homogeneous definition of the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system,
in which the conditions are no longer linearly independent:
\deg P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\leq(m+1-k)n
, at least one

P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}\not\equiv 0
, and, for each index set
0\leq i_{0}<i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq m
,

\sum_{s=0}^{k}(-1)^{s}P_{n;i_{0},\dots,i_{s-1},i_{s+1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)f_{i_{s},\infty}(z)=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{kn+1}}\right)\quad\text{as }z\to\infty.

(2)

It is clear that all conditions (
1
) are contained in (
2
) (for
[i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{k}]:=[0,j_{1},\dots,j_{k}]
).
In order to verify that conditions (
1
) are sufficient for (
2
) to hold with
i_{0}\neq 0
,
it suffices to substitute in (
2
) the expressions for all
P_{n;i_{0},\dots,i_{s-1},i_{s+1},\dots,i_{k}}
,
which are obtained from (
1
) for
[j_{1},\dots,j_{k}]:=[i_{0},\dots,i_{s-1},i_{s+1},\dots,i_{k}]
, and
check that the right-hand side of (
2
) is
0
up to
O(z^{-(kn+1)})
.
So, definitions (
1
) and (
2
) are equivalent. In what follows, we will mostly use the first one.

Let us now recall the definitions of the classical Hermite–Padé polynomials of type I and II.
The Hermite–Padé polynomials of type I of order
n
constructed from the tuple of germs
[1,f_{1,\infty},\dots,f_{m,\infty}]
at the point
\infty\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
are the polynomials
Q_{n,i}
,
0\leq i\leq m
,
such that
\deg Q_{n,i}\leq n
, at least one
Q_{n,i}\not\equiv 0
, and in the neighbourhood of infinity

\sum_{j=0}^{m}Q_{n,j}(z)f_{j,\infty}(z)=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{m(n+1)}}\right)\quad\text{as }z\to\infty.

(3)

The Hermite–Padé polynomials of type II of order
n
constructed from the tuple of germs
[1,f_{1,\infty},\dots,f_{m,\infty}]
at the point
\infty\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
are the polynomials
q_{n,i}
,
0\leq i\leq m
,
such that
\deg q_{n,i}\leq mn
, at least one
q_{n,i}\not\equiv 0
, and, for all
1\leq j\leq m
,

q_{n,0}(z)f_{j,\infty}(z)-q_{n,j}(z)=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{n+1}}\right)\quad\text{as }z\to\infty

(4)

in the neighbourhood of infinity.

Note that conditions (
1
), which define the
1
st polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system (that is, for
k=1
),
coincide (up to a sign) with conditions (
4
), which define the Hermite–Padé polynomials of type II.
So, the
1

m
-system are precisely the Hermite–Padé polynomials of
type II, that is,
P_{n;i}\equiv q_{n,i}
. At the same time, condition (
1
),
which defines the
m
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system (that is for
k=m
)
is practically equal to condition (
3
), which defines the Hermite–Padé polynomials of type I.
More precisely, if one defines
P_{n;0,1,\dots,j-1,j+1,\dots,m}:=(-1)^{j}Q_{n;j}
, then the left-hand side of (
1
) for

and (
3
)
will be equal and the order of contact of 0 in their right-hand sides will be

and

So, the polynomials

automatically satisfy
condition (
1
), that is, the Hermite–Padé polynomials of type I
(with altered sign of the odd polynomials) are the particular case of the
m
th polynomials of the
Hermite–Padé
m
-system.
Moreover, in what follows, we will be mostly interested in the so-called weak asymptotics of the polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system
(in the spirit of the classical Stahl’s theorem for usual Padé polynomials, see
[
3
,
4
]
),
hence the above discrepancy in the order of contact by a fixed number

Like the Hermite–Padé polynomials of type I and II, a polynomial Hermite–Padé

\displaystyle u(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=-m\log{|z|}+O(1),\quad\mathbf{z}\to\boldsymbol{\infty}^{(0)},

(5)

\displaystyle u(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=\log{|z|}+O(1),\quad\mathbf{z}\to\pi^{-1}(\infty)\setminus\boldsymbol{\infty}^{(0)}.

The function

Remark 2
.

We emphasize that the definition of the function

is also correct in the case

Let

(6)

If

\displaystyle F_{j}

\displaystyle:=\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}:u_{j-1}(z)=u_{j}(z)\},\quad j=1,\dots,m,

Moreover, rows with numbers from 0 to

F\setminus F_{k}

the functions
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)/M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
are glued as meromorphic functions.

Let us introduce some notations that will be adhered to in what follows.
By
\xrightarrow{*}
we will denote the weak* convergence (indicating if required the space on which it is
considered). By
\xrightarrow{\operatorname{cap}}
we will denote the convergence in the (logarithmic) capacity (indicating if required the set
on which it is considered). Let
d\sigma:=\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{dz\wedge d\overline{z}}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}

be the normalized area form of the spherical metric on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
In order to speak about the asymptotic behaviour of the
k
th polynomials of a Hermite–Padé
m
-system,
we need to fix their normalization. Therefore, along with the polynomials
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
(see (
1
)), we will
use the polynomials
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}^{*}:=c_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
(
c_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}>0
are constants) for which the functions

\log|P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}^{*}|
are spherically normalized:

\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\log|P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}^{*}|d\sigma=0.

(10)

Note that relation (
1
) is not satisfied in general for
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}^{*}
.
We will also denote by
\operatorname{dd^{c}}
the standard analogue of the Laplace operator on Riemann surfaces;
in general case, this operator transforms currents of degree 0 to currents of degree 2, and on
smooth functions
\varphi
it acts in the local coordinate
\zeta=x+iy
as
\operatorname{dd^{c}}\varphi=(\varphi_{xx}+\varphi_{yy})dxdy=\Delta\varphi dxdy
.
(For the required properties of the operator
\operatorname{dd^{c}}
and results of the theory of potential on compact Riemann surfaces,
see
[
5
]
, Attachment 2, and
[
8
]
.
We will refer to these facts retaining, if possible, the corresponding notation.)

So, recall that
P_{n;j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}
are the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system
constructed from the tuple of germs
[1,f_{1,\infty},\dots,f_{m,\infty}]
at the point
\infty
of the functions
f_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,m
, which are
meromorphic on the Riemann surface
\mathfrak{R}
. The following results hold.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
, which is constructed from
\pi
, is connected. Then

1)
There exists a number
L\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any neighbourhood
V
of the compact set
F_{k}
, for all sufficiently large
n
,
n>N=N(V)
,
there are at most
L
zeros of the polynomials
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
outside the neighbourhood
V
.

2)
For an arbitrary
p\in[1,\infty)
, as
n\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{n}\log|P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}^{*}(z)|\to-\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}u_{s}(z)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{p}(\widehat{\mathbb{C}},d\sigma),

(11)

where the function
\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}u_{s}(z)
is spherically normalized:
\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}u_{s}(z)d\sigma=0
.

3)
As
n\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{dd^{c}}\log|P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)|\xrightarrow{*}-\operatorname{dd^{c}}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}u_{s}(z)\right)\quad\text{in}\quad C(\widehat{\mathbb{C}})^{*}.

(12)

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
, which is constructed from
\pi
, is connected.
Then, for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus F_{k}
, as
n\to\infty
,

\frac{P_{n;j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)}{P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{cap}}\frac{M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)}{M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)},\quad z\in K.

(13)

Moreover, for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
,

\operatorname{cap}\left\{z\in K:\left|\frac{P_{n;j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)}{P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)}-\frac{M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)}{M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)}\right|^{1/n}\cdot e^{u_{k}(z)-u_{k-1}(z)}\geq 1+\varepsilon\right\}\to 0.

(14)

3
Reconstruction of the values of a function meromorphic on
\mathfrak{R}
from its germ via the polynomial
Hermite–Padé
m
-system

In this section, we will consider the following problem. Let
f\in\mathscr{M}(\mathfrak{R})
. Assume that we are given some germ
of a multivalued analytic function
f(\pi^{-1}(z))
at a point
z_{0}\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
defined in terms of its Taylor series.
(Without loss of generality we assume that
z_{0}=\infty
.)
The question is, how
to constructively reconstruct the values of
f
in ‘as large a region as possible’ on
\mathfrak{R}
?
The most obvious way is to use the Weierstrass continuation by reexpanding the Taylor series at points ‘close’ to the boundary of the disk of convergence.
However, this method is not constructive (see
[
13
]
).
Another method is to use Padé approximations. By Stahl’s theorem (see
[
3
]
), Padé approximations
reconstruct the values of
f
in a domain
\mathbf{D}
on
\mathfrak{R}
, which projects one-to-one
onto
\pi(\mathbf{D})=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S
, where
S
is the Stahl compact set, which consists of a finite number
of analytic arcs (featuring certain regularity at endpoints). So, one can say that the Padé approximations
reconstruct the values of
f
on one sheet
\mathbf{D}
of our
(m+1)
-sheeted Riemann surface
\mathfrak{R}
.
We will show that in the case when all the surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
,
k=1,\dots,m
, are connected,
the values of
f
on all Nuttall sheets, except the ‘last’
\mathfrak{R}^{(m)}
and except of the set

\pi^{-1}(F)
(see (
8
)), are constructively reconstructed with the help of the polynomial Hermite–Padé
m
-system.
It is worth pointing out that the sheet
\mathbf{D}
is not related in any way to the Nuttall sheets of
\mathfrak{R}^{(j)}
.

So, let
f\in\mathscr{M}(\mathfrak{R})
and let the functions
1,f,f^{2},\dots,f^{m}
be independent over the field of rational functions
\mathbb{C}(z)
.
In what follows, in this section we will consider the polynomial Hermite–Padé
m
-system for
f_{j}:=f^{j}
, that is,
this system is constructed from the tuple of germs
[1,f(\pi_{0}^{-1}(z)),f^{2}(\pi_{0}^{-1}(z)),\dots,f^{m}(\pi_{0}^{-1}(z))]
, where
\pi_{0}^{-1}

is the inverse mapping of
\pi
in the neighbourhood of
\boldsymbol{\infty}^{(0)}
. So, by definition

M_{0,1,\dots,k-1}(z):=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&f(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})&\ldots&f^{k-1}(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})\\
1&f(\mathbf{z}^{(1)})&\ldots&f^{k-1}(\mathbf{z}^{(1)})\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
1&f(\mathbf{z}^{(k-1)})&\ldots&f^{k-1}(\mathbf{z}^{(k-1)})\end{pmatrix},

M_{0,1,\dots,k-2,k}(z):=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&f(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})&\ldots&f^{k-2}(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})&f^{k}(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})\\
1&f(\mathbf{z}^{(1)})&\ldots&f^{k-2}(\mathbf{z}^{(1)})&f^{k}(\mathbf{z}^{(1)})\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
1&f(\mathbf{z}^{(k-1)})&\ldots&f^{k-2}(\mathbf{z}^{(k-1)})&f^{k}(\mathbf{z}^{(k-1)})\end{pmatrix}.

Thus,
M_{0,1,\dots,k-1}(z)
is the Vandermonde determinant, that is,

M_{0,1,\dots,k-1}(z)=\prod_{0\leq i<j<k}(f(\mathbf{z}^{(i)})-f(\mathbf{z}^{(j)})),

and
M_{0,1,\dots,k-2,k}(z)
are the matrices
obtained from the Vandermond matrix by increasing the powers of all elements in the last column by
1
, that is,

M_{0,1,\dots,k-2,k}(z)=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}f(\mathbf{z}^{(s)})\cdot\prod_{0\leq i<j<k}(f(\mathbf{z}^{(i)})-f(\mathbf{z}^{(j)})).

Therefore,
M_{0,\dots,k-2,k}(z)/M_{0,\dots,k-1}(z)=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}f(\mathbf{z}^{(s)})
. The next result now follows from Theorem
2
.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose that the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
, which is constructed from
\pi
, is connected.
Let
f\in\mathscr{M}(\mathfrak{R})
and let the functions
1,f,f^{2},\dots,f^{m}
be independent over
\mathbb{C}(z)
.
We set
f_{j}:=f^{j}
. Then, for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus F_{k}
, as
n\to\infty

\frac{P_{n;0,1,\dots,k-2,k}(z)}{P_{n;0,1,\dots,k-1}(z)}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{cap}}\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}f(\mathbf{z}^{(s)}),\quad z\in K.

(15)

Moreover, for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
,

\operatorname{cap}\left\{z\in K:\left|\frac{P_{n;0,1,\dots,k-2,k}(z)}{P_{n;0,1,\dots,k-1}(z)}-\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}f(\mathbf{z}^{(s)})\right|^{1/n}\cdot e^{u_{k}(z)-u_{k-1}(z)}\geq 1+\varepsilon\right\}\to 0.

(16)

Let us assume that the projection
\pi
is such that the surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
are connected for all
k=1,\dots,m
. (In Statement
4

it will be shown that this property is satisfied by all
\pi
with the condition that all critical points of
\pi
are of the first order and
over each point
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
there is at most one critical point of
\pi
, that is, the class of such
\pi
is quite broad.)
So, evaluating the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system for all
k=1,\dots,m
and considering
the ratios
P_{n;0,1,\dots,k-2,k}/P_{n;0,1,\dots,k-1}(z)
,
we will in succession asymptotically reconstruct
\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}f(\mathbf{z}^{(s)})
outside
\pi^{-1}(F_{k})
. Hence (since
F:=\cup_{j=1}^{m}F_{j}
),
we will also reconstruct the values of
f
on all Nuttall sheets of
\mathfrak{R}
, except
\mathfrak{R}^{(m)}
, outside
the set
\pi^{-1}(F)
. Since for the evaluation of the
k
th polynomials
of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system
of order
n
it suffices to know the first
(m+1)n
Taylor coefficients of the germs that define the polynomials
(see definition (
1
)), our approximations of
P_{n;0,1,\dots,k-2,k}/P_{n;0,1,\dots,k-1}(z)
are constructive.
Note that the idea that using suitable polynomials one should reconstruct the sum of the values on the first
k
sheets,
rather than the values of the function
f
on the Nuttall sheets, was expressed for the first time in
[
6
]
.

4
The Riemann surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
and the definition of the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system in terms of this surface

First of all, we fix
k\in[1,\dots,m]
. We introduce the compact Riemann surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
, which is associated
with
\mathfrak{R}
, and which is, in general, disconnected; along with
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
we introduce the branched covering
\widetilde{\pi}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
which is constructed from the projection
\pi
.
Since
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}

(\mathbf{z}\mapsto z)
is an
(m+1)
-sheeted holomorphic branched covering of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and since
\Sigma
is the set of
critical values of the projection
\pi
, we can consider
\mathfrak{R}
as the standard compactification of the Riemann surface
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}

of some
(m+1)
-valued global analytic function (GAF)
w(z)
defined in the domain
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
.
In fact,
w(z)=\pi^{-1}(z)
. Informally speaking, the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
is the standard compactification of the Riemann surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
of all
possible
unordered
collections of
k
distinct germs of the function
w(\cdot)

that are considered at the same points
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
.
More precisely, the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
consists of the pairs
(z,\{w_{1}^{z},\dots,w^{z}_{k}\})
, where
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
,
and
\{w^{z}_{1},\dots,w^{z}_{k}\}
is an
unordered
collection of
k
distinct germs of the function
w(\cdot)
at the point
z
.
The structure of the Riemann surface on this set is introduced as in the process of construction
of the Riemann surface of a GAF. So, by a neighbourhood
of a point
(z_{0},\{w^{z_{0}}_{1},\dots,w^{z_{0}}_{k}\})
we mean the set of points
(z,\{w^{z}_{1},\dots,w^{z}_{k}\})
such that
1)
z\in B_{z_{0}}(\delta)
, where
B_{z_{0}}(\delta)
is a disc with centre
z_{0}
and a radius
\delta

such that the germs
w^{z_{0}}_{1},\dots,w^{z_{0}}_{k}
are holomorphic in
B_{z_{0}}(\delta)
, and 2) there exists
a bijection between elements of the collections
\{w^{z_{0}}_{1},\dots,w^{z_{0}}_{k}\}
and
\{w^{z}_{1},\dots,w^{z}_{k}\}
such that the
corresponding germs are direct analytic continuations of each other. (Since
w(\cdot)
is an
(m+1)
-sheeted function,
there exists at most one such a bijection.)
It is clear that the Riemann surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
is a
{m+1\choose k}
-sheeted (disconnected, in general)
holomorphic covering of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
(with the natural projection
z:(z,\{w^{z}_{1},\dots,w^{z}_{k}\})\mapsto z
). Next,
using the standard process of compactification of a finite-sheeted covering of the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}

with finite number of punctures, we get from
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
a compact (disconnected, in general) Riemann surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
and
a holomorphic covering
\widetilde{\pi}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
(branched at points of
\Sigma
), which extends the original
covering
z:\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
.
The points of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
will be written in boldface and marked with a tilde; their projections, as before,
will be denoted in lightface (for example,
\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
, and
\widetilde{\pi}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})=z
).
We emphasize that, unlike Theorems
1
and
2
,
in this section the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
can be disconnected.

For
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
all the inequalities in (
6
) are strict, and hence, for
such
z
, we have
\pi^{-1}(z)=\{\mathbf{z}^{(0)},\mathbf{z}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{z}^{(m)}\}
, where
\mathbf{z}^{(j)}\in\mathfrak{R}^{(j)}
. So,
over
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
the Riemann surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
splits into
{m+1\choose k}
disjoint sheets

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{k})}
,
0\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
. The
sheet
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{k})}
consists of
unordered collections
\{w^{\mathbf{z}^{(j_{1})}}(\cdot),w^{\mathbf{z}^{(j_{2})}}(\cdot),\dots,w^{\mathbf{z}^{(j_{k})}}(\cdot)\}
, where
w^{\mathbf{z}^{(j_{l})}}(\cdot)

is the germ of
w(\cdot)
at the point
\mathbf{z}^{(j_{l})}
, which are considered for all
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
.
It is clear that on all sheets the mapping
\widetilde{\pi}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{k})}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
is biholomorphic.
The point of the sheet
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{k})}
lying over
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F

will be denoted by
\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(j_{1}j_{2}\dots j_{k})}
. Note that we have
u_{k-1}(z)<u_{k}(z)
for
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{k}
in (
6
).
Therefore, the unordered tuple
\{w^{\mathbf{z}^{(0)}}(\cdot),w^{\mathbf{z}^{(1)}}(\cdot),\dots,w^{\mathbf{z}^{(k-1)}}(\cdot)\}

is defined over
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{k}
. Moreover, extending it along all possible paths in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{k}
,
we will always get the same collection. So, the sheet
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(01\dots k-1)}
is defined over
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{k}
(not only
over
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
) and the projection
\widetilde{\pi}:\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(01\dots k-1)}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{k}
is biholomorphic.

Our next aim is to give a different definition of the
k
th polynomials of a Hermite–Padé
m
-system in terms of new
functions which are meromorphic on the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
. Let us construct such functions from the original
functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
.
For each tuples of indices
0\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
and
0\leq l_{1}<l_{2}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m
, by

M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
we denote the minor of the matrix
A
(see (
9
))
corresponding to the columns with numbers
j_{1},\dots,j_{k}
and the rows with numbers
l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{k}
.
(In particular,
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{0,\dots,k-1}\equiv M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}
.) By
A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)

we denote the algebraic complement of the minor
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
(that is,

A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
is the complementary minor to
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
taken
with the sign
(-1)^{j_{1}\dots+j_{k}+l_{1}\dots+l_{k}}
).
Like the matrix
A
, the minors
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
and their algebraic complements

A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
are defined for
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
.
By
\|M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)\|
and
\|A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)\|
, we will denote the matrices
composed of all elements of
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
and
A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
, respectively.
It is well known (see, for example, Theorem 2.4.1 in
[
7
]
) that since
\det A\not\equiv 0
, then

\|M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)\|\cdot\left\|\frac{A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}{\det A(z)}\right\|^{T}=\operatorname{Id},

(17)

where
\operatorname{Id}
is the identity matrix of size
{m+1\choose k}\times{m+1\choose k}
.
For
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
, we introduce the matrix

M_{w}(z):=\|w^{j}(\mathbf{z}^{(l)})\|_{l,j=0}^{m}=\begin{pmatrix}1&w(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})&\ldots&w^{m}(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})\\
1&w(\mathbf{z}^{(1)})&\ldots&w^{m}(\mathbf{z}^{(1)})\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
1&w(\mathbf{z}^{(m)})&\ldots&w^{m}(\mathbf{z}^{(m)})\end{pmatrix}.

(18)

For each index set
0\leq l_{1}<l_{2}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m
, we denote by
M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)

the minor of the matrix
M_{w}(z)
corresponding to the columns with numbers
0,1,\dots,k-1
and the
rows with numbers
l_{1},l_{2},\dots,l_{k}
. Note that
M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)\not\equiv 0
. Indeed, it is the Vandermonde determinant,
hence
M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(w(\mathbf{z}^{(l_{j})})-w(\mathbf{z}^{(l_{i})}))
, and
w(\mathbf{z}^{(s)})\neq w(\mathbf{z}^{(t)})
for all
s\neq t
,
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus F
, by the definition of
w(z)
as the algebraic function
defining the surface
\mathfrak{R}
.
Therefore, for each index set
0\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
, the following functions are well defined for
\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}\setminus\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
:

M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}):=\frac{M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}{M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)},

(19)

A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}):=\frac{A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}{\det A}.

(20)

Proposition 1
.

For each index set
0\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
, the functions

M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
and
A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
extend to meromorphic functions on the whole Riemann surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
.

Proof.

Let us first prove the conclusion of the proposition for
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
. By definition (
19
)
it is clear that the function
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})

is meromorphic on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}\setminus\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
. Since, as noted before,
F
is a one-dimensional piecewise analytic set
without isolated points (that is, in fact,
F
is the closure of a finite number of analytic arcs), the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)

has the same property. Hence it suffices to check that
the functions
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})
and
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(i_{1}\dots i_{k})})
are glued together in a single function
when we cross the arcs from the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
across that we go from the sheet

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}
to the sheet
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(i_{1}\dots i_{k})}
. Indeed, since across the chosen arc we go from the sheet

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}
to the sheet
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(i_{1}\dots i_{k})}
, the minor
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)

is transformed, up to a sign, to the minor
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
and
the minor
M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
goes, up to a sign, to
M_{w}^{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)

as the projection of this arc is crossed. In addition, the minors
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
and
M_{w}^{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
change
or do not change the sign simultaneously, since the appearance of the sign change depends only
on the structure of the gluing of the sheets of the original surface
\mathfrak{R}
.

Let us prove the proposition for
A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
.
By (
17
), we have

\|M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|\cdot\|A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{T}=\operatorname{Id}.

(21)

So, for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus F
, the values of the function
A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})

on the sheets of the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
are contained in the row numbered by the tuple
j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}
, of the
inverse matrix of the matrix whose columns contain the values of the functions
M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
on the sheets
of the same surface.
The functions
A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
defined from the functions
M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
in this way
can always be extended to meromorphic functions on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
.
(It is worth pointing out that for this purpose it is sufficient that the original functions
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
,
which are meromorphic on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
, are only linearly independent over
\mathbb{C}(z)
(this is
equivalent to the condition that
\det\|M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|\not\equiv 0
), that is,
it is not necessary that they are minors of the matrix
A
).
To check this, we need to use the well-known formula, which represents elements of the inverse matrix
as cofactors of the elements of the original matrix divided by its determinant.
Using this formula, one can easily verify that the functions
A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})

are glued into a single one as the boundaries of the sheets are crossed.
Note that the analogous property of a collection of meromorphic functions for
a Nuttall partition was pointed out in
[
1
]
, see also Proposition 2 in
[
5
]
.
∎

Now we can give a new definition of the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system.

Theorem 3
.

There exists a
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

(
not depending on
n)
such that the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}

satisfy the following relations
:

\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq m}P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{nk+1-p}}\right)\quad\text{as }z\to\infty

(22)

for all
0\leq l_{1}<l_{2}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m
, where
l_{1}\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
be the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system, that is, the solution of (
1
).
Define the function

\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}):=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq m}P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}).

(23)

Since
A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})\in\mathscr{M}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]})
(see Proposition
1
), we have
\widetilde{R}_{n}\in\mathscr{M}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]})
. We need to check that for
\widetilde{R}_{n}
the right-hand side of (
22
)
holds true.
We look at the identity

\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq m}P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})

(24)

for all
0\leq l_{1}<l_{2}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m
as a system of linear homogeneous equations for
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
.
The matrices
\|M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|
and
\|A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{T}

are mutually inverse (see (
21
)), and hence, solving this system, we have

P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)=\sum_{0\leq l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}).

(25)

Substituting these expressions for
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
in (
1
), we see that, for any fixed index set

0<j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
,

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}\Biggl[M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})+

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{k}(-1)^{s}M_{0,j_{1},\dots,j_{s-1},j_{s+1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})f_{j_{s},\infty}(z)\Biggr]\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{kn+1}}\right)

as
z\to\infty
. Substituting in (
26
) the explicit expression for
M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})
(
19
) and taking into account that in the neighbourhood of
\infty
the germ
f_{j_{s},\infty}(z)=f_{j_{s}}(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}\Biggl[M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)+

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{k}(-1)^{s}M_{0,j_{1},\dots,j_{s-1},j_{s+1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)f_{j_{s}}(\mathbf{z}^{(0)})\Biggr]\frac{\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})}{M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{kn+1}}\right)

as
z\to\infty
. Note that in the square brackets in (
27
) we have
the expansion of the minor
M_{0,j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{0,l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)
of the matrix
A
along the zero row.
(In particular, for
l_{1}=0
we have
0
in the square brackets.) Hence (
27
) is equivalent to the following

\sum_{0<l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}\frac{M_{0,j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{0,l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}{M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{kn+1}}\right)\quad\text{as }z\to\infty.

(28)

Let
O_{\infty}\>=\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}:|z|>\delta\}
be a neighborhood of infinity such that
O_{\infty}\cap F_{1}=\varnothing
.
(Recall that
F_{1}
is the boundary of the sheet
\mathfrak{R}^{(0)}
of the original surface
\mathfrak{R}
.)
Then over
O_{\infty}
sheets
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(0s_{2}\dots s_{k})}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}
for
l_{1}>0
have no
common boundary points. Hence

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}\cap\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(O_{\infty})=\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{0}\sqcup\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}
, where
\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{0}:=\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(O_{\infty})\cap\overline{\bigcup_{0<l_{2}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(0l_{2}\dots l_{k})}}
and
\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}:=\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(O_{\infty})\cap\overline{\bigcup_{0<l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}}
.
For
0<j_{1}<\dots j_{k}\leq m
, we define on
\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}\setminus\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
the functions
B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}
by

B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}):=\frac{M_{0,j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{0,l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}{M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}.

(29)

Since 1) for fixed
0<l_{1}<\dots l_{k}\leq m
, the functions
B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})
are identically equal
to the expression in the square brackets in (
26
), 2) by Proposition
1
,
all functions
M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
are meromorphic on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
(and, in particular, on
\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}
),
and 3) the germs
f_{i,\infty}
are holomorphic on
O_{\infty}
, the functions
B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})

extend to meromorphic functions on
\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}
.

Let us now look at expression (
28
) for all
0<j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
as a system of linear homogeneous equations for

\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})
for
0<l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m
, assuming that
\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}\in\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}\setminus\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
.
First of all, we evaluate the determinant of the matrix of this system

\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|
. By the generalized Sylvester identity (see, for example,
§ 2.7 in
[
7
]
), which expresses the determinant of the matrix composed of all minors
of a given order and containing a fixed corner minor (in our case, an element) in terms of the determinant of the original matrix, we have

\det\|M_{0,j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{0,l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)\|=(\det A)^{{m-1\choose k-1}}
. Therefore,

\det\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|=\det\left\|\frac{M_{0,j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{0,l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}{M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}\right\|=\frac{(\det A)^{{m-1\choose k-1}}}{\prod\limits_{0<l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}.

(30)

Since, as pointed out above,
\det A\not\equiv 0
(because the original functions
1,f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{C}(z)
) and
M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)\not\equiv 0

for all
0\leq l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m
(see the definition of the
functions
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})
(
19
)),
we also have
\det\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|\not\equiv 0
. Hence the
matrix
\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|
is invertible. Moreover, since
the columns of the matrix
\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|
contain the
values on the sheets of the surface
\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}
of functions that are meromorphic on this surface, we obtain that
the rows of the inverse matrix
\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{-1}
contain
values on the sheets of the surface
\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}
of functions that are also meromorphic on this surface.
(This property, which can be verified directly, is, in fact, established in the proof of Proposition
1

for the functions
A_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
: here one only needs to replace the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
by
\mathbf{O}_{\infty}^{1}
.)
So, the singularities of the matrix
\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{-1}
over
\infty
are ‘finite’.
More precisely, let
O\subset O_{\infty}
be a neighborhood of
\infty
such that on the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(O)
the poles
of the meromorphic functions, contained in the rows of the matrix
\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{-1}
,
may lie only at points of the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)
. Then there exists a
p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}

such that the matrix
z^{p}\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{-1}
(that is, the matrix obtained from

\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{-1}
by multiplication of each of its element by
z^{p}
) is bounded in
O
.
Solving system (
28
) for
\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})
, we arrive at the conclusion of the theorem.
∎

Remark 3
.

In the case
\infty\notin\Sigma
and if all the functions
A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})

have no poles at points of the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)
, there always exist polynomials
\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)

of degree at most
(m+1-k)n
satisfying (
22
) for
p=0
. Indeed, it is easily seen that in this case
conditions (
22
), as well as conditions (
1
), form a system of
(n(m+1)+1){m\choose k}
linear homogeneous equations for
(n(m+1-k)+1){m+1\choose k}=n(m+1){m\choose k}+{m+1\choose k}
unknown coefficients of the polynomials
\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
.
Moreover, in the case
\infty\notin\Sigma
all the germs
A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})
for

0\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq m
and
0<l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m
can be replaced by a tuple of arbitrary holomorphic germs, and still
polynomials
\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
of degree
\leq(m+1-k)n
satisfying (
22
) for
p=0

will exist. (However, they will have nothing to do with our original problem).

However, in the case
\infty\in\Sigma
the situation is completely different.
In this case, we cannot a priori (without recourse to Theorem
3
) assert that the
polynomials
\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
exist even if the right-hand side of (
22
)
is replaced by
O(z^{-1})
and the functions
A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
are holomorphic at points of the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)
.
This is so, because the functions
A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
, as considered as functions of
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
may have branch points at points of the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)
.

The following corollary shows that, for some ‘general position condition’, system (
22
) for
p=0
gives
a new definition of the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system, which is
equivalent to the original one. (All the notation used in this definition is inherited from the proof of Theorem
3
.)

Corollary 2
.

Let
\infty\notin\Sigma
and let the functions
B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
and the functions from the rows of the matrix
\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{-1}

have no poles on the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)
. Then the
k
th polynomials
of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system
(
as defined in (
1
)
)
and the polynomials
of degree at most
(m+1-k)n
satisfying (
22
) for
p=0
coincide.

Proof.

Let
P_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
be the
k
th polynomials of the Hermite–Padé
m
-system, that is, the solution of (
1
).
Since the functions contained in the rows of the matrix
\|B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|^{-1}

have no poles on the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)
, we can put
p=0
in the proof of Theorem
3
.

Conversely, let
\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
be polynomials of degree at most
(m+1-k)n
satisfying (
22
) for
p=0
.
Consider on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
the meromorphic function

\widetilde{R}^{\prime}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}):=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq m}\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)A_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}).

(31)

Working in the same way as in the proof of Theorem
3
, we express
\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
from (
31
):

\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)=\sum_{0\leq l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}M_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\widetilde{R}^{\prime}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}).

(32)

Substituting these expressions for
\widetilde{P}_{n;i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}(z)
in the left-hand side of (
1
),
carrying out the same transformations as in the proof
of Theorem
3
in (
26
)–(
28
), and using (
29
), we find that

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{n;j_{1}\dots,j_{k}}(z)+\sum_{s=1}^{k}(-1)^{s}\widetilde{P}_{n;0,j_{1},\dots,j_{s-1},j_{s+1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)f_{j_{s},\infty}(z)

(33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\widetilde{R}^{\prime}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}).

Note that by the assumption the functions
B_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
have no poles at the points of the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)
.
Besides, condition (
22
) for
p=0
is equivalent to the relation

\widetilde{R}^{\prime}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{m})})=O(z^{-(nk+1)})
as
z\to\infty
for all
0<l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m
. Hence from (
33
) we have

\widetilde{P}_{n;j_{1}\dots,j_{k}}(z)+\sum_{s=1}^{k}(-1)^{s}\widetilde{P}_{n;0,j_{1},\dots,j_{s-1},j_{s+1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)f_{j_{s},\infty}(z)=O\left(\frac{1}{z^{nk+1}}\right)

(34)

as

, which is condition (
1
).
∎

Proposition 2
.

If the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
is connected, then, for any index set
0\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
, the meromorphic function

M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
on this surface is not identically
0
and the function
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)

does not identically vanish in any domain in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
.

Proof.

Assume that, for some index set
0\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{k}\leq m
, we have
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})\equiv 0

in some domain on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
. Since
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
is connected, this means that
M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})\equiv 0
on the whole
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
. Therefore,
\det\|M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|\equiv 0
for
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
.
On the other hand, since
\|M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)\|
is the matrix composed of all minors of
A
of size
k\times k
, we have

\det\|M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})})\|=\det\left\|\frac{M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}{M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}\right\|=\frac{(\det A(z))^{m\choose k-1}}{\prod\limits_{0\leq l_{1}<\dots<l_{k}\leq m}M_{w}^{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}(z)}.

(35)

Therefore,
\det A\equiv 0
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
. This contradicts the linear independence over
\mathbb{C}(z)

of the original functions
1,f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
.

The second assertion of the proposition is clear from the fact that, for
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F
,

M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(z)=M_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(01\dots k-1)}){M_{w}^{0,1,\dots,k-1}(z)}.

(36)

∎

5
Proofs of Theorems
1
and
2

As pointed out above, our proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
are close to the proofs of the analogous theorems
for the Hermite–Padé polynomials of type I from
[
5
]
.
Roughly speaking, we will replace the function
u(\mathbf{z})
, which defines the Nuttall partition of the surface
\mathfrak{R}
,
by the function
-\widetilde{u}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
, which we will now construct from
u
,
and the remainder function for Hermite–Padé polynomials of type I by
\widetilde{R}_{n}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
, which is defined by (
23
).

5.1
Auxiliary results

So, for
\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}\setminus\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
, we define the function

\widetilde{u}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}):=u_{l_{1}}(z)+\dots+u_{l_{k}}(z),

(37)

where
u_{j}(z)
are introduced in (
6
).

Proposition 3
.

The function
\widetilde{u}(\widetilde{\mathbf{z}})
extends to a harmonic function on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}\setminus\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)

with the following logarithmic singularities at the points
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\infty)
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(\mathbf{z}^{(0l_{2}\dots l_{k})})

\displaystyle=-(m+1-k)\log{|z|}+O(1),\quad z\to\infty,

(38)

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}(\mathbf{z}^{l_{1}l_{2}\dots l_{k}})

\displaystyle=k\log{|z|}+O(1),\quad z\to\infty,\quad l_{1}\neq 0.

Proof.

As pointed out above,
F
is a one-dimensional piecewise analytic set without isolated points (that is, in fact,
F

is the closure of a finite number of analytic arcs), and hence the set
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
has the same properties.
So, it suffices to show that the function
\widetilde{u}
is glued into a single harmonic function on the arcs from
\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
.
Assume that when crossing such an (open) arc
\gamma\in\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(F)
we go from the sheet
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(l_{1}\dots l_{k})}
to the sheet

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{(i_{1}\dots i_{k})}
. Consider a small neighborhood
O
of the arc
\widetilde{\pi}(\gamma)
such that
O\cap F=\widetilde{\pi}(\gamma)
. Over
O
the Riemann
surface
\mathfrak{R}
splits into the union of
m+1
disconnected neighbourhoods
\mathbf{O}_{s}
,
s=0,\dots,m
. We set
u_{(s)}(z):=u|_{\mathbf{O}_{s}}(\mathbf{z})
.
Then, from each side of
\widetilde{\pi}(\gamma)
, the function
u_{j}
coincides with some of the functions
u_{(s)}
. Besides, if the
set
\mathbf{O}_{s}\cap\pi^{-1}(\widetilde{\pi}(\gamma))
locally separates some sheets
\mathfrak{R}^{(l)}
and
\mathfrak{R}^{(i)}
(the case
l=i

is possible), then the functions
u_{l}
and
u_{i}
are glued on
\widetilde{\pi}(\gamma)
into a single harmonic function
u_{(s)}
.
Hence, by construction of the surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
, when we go through the arc
\widetilde{\pi}(\gamma)
, the functions
u_{l_{1}}(z)+\dots+u_{l_{k}}(z)
and
u_{i_{1}}(z)+\dots+u_{i_{k}}(z)
are glued into a single harmonic function. This means that on
\gamma
the function
\widetilde{u}

is glued into a harmonic function.

The behaviour of

Moreover, if

is disconnected, then the condition

Besides that, as
\widetilde{R}_{n}
, the functions

may identically vanish on some connected components of

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}

is disconnected,
then
in the representation for

the principal asymptotic term may not correspond to the sheet

even if we assume that the problem of normalization of

it may happen that in (

Moreover, if

h_{n;j_{1},\dots,j_{k}}
(

Now let us discuss the condition of connectedness of the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
.
Recall that we fixed the compact Riemann surface
\mathfrak{R}
and
the
(m+1)
-sheeted branched covering
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
So, we can consider
\mathfrak{R}
as a standard compactification of the Riemann surface
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
of the

(m+1)
-sheeted global analytic function (GAF)

in the domain
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
(where
\Sigma

is the set of all critical values of
\pi
).
The points of
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
are pairs

, where

is a germ of the GAF

at the point

The surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
is defined as the standard compactification of the Riemann surface

of all unordered collections of

distinct germs of the GAF

considered at the same points

(for more details, see §

So, the connectedness of

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}

In our arguments, we will prove the connectedness or disconnectedness exactly of the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
, not specifying it in formulations. As before, the points of the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
will be denote by
(z,\{w_{1}^{z},\dots,w^{z}_{k}\})
,
where
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
and

k
distinct germs of the function

at the point

.

First, we note that for

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[1]}^{\prime}
precisely coincides with the surface
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{R}\setminus\pi^{-1}(\Sigma)
by construction.
It is easily seen that, for

, the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[m]}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the surface
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
.
Indeed, let

We define

as the only of the germs of the GAF

The required isomorphism sends

So, the surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[1]}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[m]}
are always connected.

Now let us show that for the following class of projections
\pi

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}

Statement 4
.

Assume that a projection
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is such that all its critical points are of the first order and
that for each point

there is at most one critical point of
\pi

\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
,

Proof.

Since, as mention above, the surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[1]}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[m]}
are always connected, we will assume that
m\geq 3
and some
k=2,\dots,m-1
is fixed. Let us show that the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
is connected.

Let the set
\Sigma
of branch points of the GAF
w:=\pi^{-1}
consist of the points
a_{1},\dots,a_{J}
.
The condition of the theorem means that over each point
a_{j}
there is precisely one branch point of the GAF
w
and its order is 2.
Since the set
\Sigma
is finite, there exists a point
a\in\mathbb{C}
such that all closed intervals
[a,a_{j}]
,
j=1,\dots,J
,
intersect only in the point
a
(if
\infty\in\Sigma

[a,\infty]

we mean any ray from
a
to
\infty

). We set

. Then,
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S

It is clear that over
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S

\mathfrak{R}
splits into
m+1
disjoint connected sheets and
\pi
is
biholomorphic on each of them. In terms of
w

\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S
the GAF
w

m+1

distinct holomorphic functions (the branches of

w_{1}(\cdot),\dots,w_{m+1}(\cdot)
.
In what follows, we assume that for

the germ
w_{i}^{z}
is the germ of exactly the function
w_{i}(\cdot)

at the point
z
,
i=1,\dots,m+1
.
Fix

. We will
show that, for any tuple of distinct
k+1
indices
i_{1},\dots,i_{k+1}
,
1\leq i_{s}\leq m+1
,
there exists a path
\gamma\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
beginning and ending at
z^{*}

such that continuing each of the germs
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{s}}
,

, along this path, we again
get the germ
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{s}}
, and continuing
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}
we get
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
.
So, the lifting of this path to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
connects the point

(z^{*},\{w^{z^{*}}_{i_{1}},\dots,w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k-1}},w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}\})
to the point
(z^{*},\{w^{z^{*}}_{i_{1}},\dots,w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k-1}},w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}\})
.
It is clear that taking a composition of several paths analogous to
\gamma

we can construct a path connecting
the point
(z^{*},\{w^{z^{*}}_{i_{1}},\dots,w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}\})
to an arbitrary given point
(z^{*},\{w^{z^{*}}_{j_{1}},\dots,w^{z^{*}}_{j_{k}}\})\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
.
Since
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S
is connected, the last means that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
is also connected.

So, let us construct the path
\gamma
.
By the assumption, the surface
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
of the GAF
w
is connected,
and hence there exists a path
\gamma^{\prime}\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
such that the continuation of
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}

along this path gives
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
.
For each
j=1,\dots J-1
, we fix an (oriented) loop
\alpha_{j}
around the point

with beginning and end at
z^{*}
such that
\alpha_{j}
intersects
S
in a unique point that lies on the interval
(a,a_{j})
.
Then the path
\gamma^{\prime}
, as a path in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
,
is homotopic to the path
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
that consists of compositions of some paths
\alpha_{j}
and
\alpha_{j}^{-1}
, where

\alpha_{j}^{-1}
is the loop
\alpha_{j}
gone in opposite direction.
Consequently, the results of continuation of any germ of the GAF
w
along the paths
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}

are the same; in particular,
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}
is continued along
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
to
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
.
Let us see what happens with the germs of the GAF
w
after their continuation along the loops
\alpha_{j}
.
Since all the branch points of

are of second order, the results of continuaton of germs of
w
at the point
z^{*}
along the loops
\alpha_{j}
and
\alpha_{j}^{-1}
coincide. Therefore we further assume that the path
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
consists only of the loops
\alpha_{j}
.
Since over each point
a_{j}
there is precisely one branch point of second order, we see that when we go along the loop
\alpha_{j}
, only two germs from the whole collection

Suppose that, when we go along he loop
\alpha_{j}
, the germ
w^{z^{*}}_{s}
is transformed to the germ
w^{z^{*}}_{t}
(possibly coinciding with
w^{z^{*}}_{s}
). Then we write
w^{z^{*}}_{s}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{j}}w^{z^{*}}_{t}
.
So, assume that the path
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
consists of
B

\alpha_{j}
:

\gamma^{\prime\prime}=\alpha_{j_{B}}\circ\dots\circ\alpha_{j_{1}},

(92)

where

.
Set
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{1}}:=w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}
,
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{B+1}}:=w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
.
Let
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l}}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{j_{l}}}w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l+1}}
,

, that is,

w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}=:w^{z^{*}}_{r_{1}}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{j_{1}}}w^{z^{*}}_{r_{2}}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{j_{2}}}\dots\xrightarrow{\alpha_{j_{B-1}}}w^{z^{*}}_{r_{B}}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{j_{B}}}w^{z^{*}}_{r_{B+1}}:=w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}.

(93)

Let
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l}}=w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l^{\prime}}}
for some
1\leq l<l^{\prime}\leq B+1
. Then, deleting the
piece
\alpha_{j_{l^{\prime}}}\circ\dots\circ\alpha_{j_{l}}
from the representation for
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
(
92
),
we again obtain a path that transforms
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}
to
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
.
Hence in what follows we assume that
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
is a path for that all
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l}}
in (
93
) are distinct.
Let us show that in this case the path

\gamma:=\alpha_{j_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\alpha_{j_{B-1}}\circ\alpha_{j_{B}}\circ\alpha_{j_{B-1}}\circ\dots\circ\alpha_{j_{1}}

(94)

is the required one.

First we show that the continuation of
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}
along
\gamma
gives
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
.
Since, by construction, all
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l}}
in (
93
) are distinct,
when we go along the loop
\alpha_{j_{l}}
,
l=1,\dots,B
,
the germs
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l}}
and
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l+1}}
are interchanged and all other germs of
w
at
z^{*}
remain unchanged. So, when we go along the loop
\alpha_{j_{l}}
,
l=1,\dots,B-1
,
the germ
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
is unchanged.
The continuation of
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}
along
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
(
92
) gives
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
,
and hence, taking into account the form of
\gamma
(
94
),
we conclude that the continuation of
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k}}
along
\gamma
also gives
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{k+1}}
.
Now let us show that, for all
s=1,\dots,k-1
, the continuation of
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{s}}
along
\gamma
gives
the same germ
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{s}}
. We fix such an

. Let the continuation
of
w^{z^{*}}_{i_{s}}
along the path
\alpha_{j_{B-1}}\circ\dots\circ\alpha_{j_{1}}
be some germ
w^{z^{*}}_{b}
.
Let us show that when we go along the loop
\alpha_{j_{B}}

w^{z^{*}}_{b}
is unchanged. Assume the contrary.
Then, since all
w^{z^{*}}_{r_{l}}
in (

w^{z^{*}}_{b}=w^{z^{*}}_{r_{B+1}}
.
Hence the continuation of the germ

On the other hand, when we go along the loop

Though Statement
4
shows that the class of projections
\pi
for that the surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
are connected
is quite broad, the following Proposition
4
gives a natural class of projections
\pi
for that the
surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
for all

are disconnected.

Since
\mathfrak{R}
is a compact Riemann surface and since
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is an
(m+1)
-sheeted branched covering of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, the
covering
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is isomorphic to the covering of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
by the algebraic surface
defined in the affine part of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}_{z}\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}_{w}
by some algebraic equation
P(z,w)=0
, where
P

is an irreducible polynomial of degree
(m+1)
with respect to
w
, with the natural projection
(z,w)\mapsto z
.
Respectively,
the surface
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the Riemann surface of the GAF
w
in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be the Riemann surface defined by the algebraic equation
w^{m+1}=R(z)
, where
m\geq 3

and
R(z)
is an arbitrary rational function, and let
\pi\colon\mathbf{z}=(z,w)\mapsto z
. Then the surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}
for all
k=2,\dots,m-1
are disconnected.

Proof.

The surfaces
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[k]}^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[m+1-k]}^{\prime}
are isomorphic (the required isomorphism
sends
(z,\{w_{1}^{z},\dots,w^{z}_{k}\})
to the point
(z,\{w_{k+1}^{z},\dots,w^{z}_{m+1}\})
, where
w_{k+1}^{z},\dots
,
w^{z}_{m+1}

are the germs of
w
at the point
z
that are not contained among the germs
w_{1}^{z},\dots,w^{z}_{k}
). So we can assume that

k\leq(m+1)/2
.

The branch points of the GAF
w(z)=\sqrt[m+1]{R(z)}
are contained among the zeros and poles
of the function
R(z)
. Let
a
be a zero or a pole of
R(z)
of order
l
,
and let
d
be the greatest common divisor of
(m+1)
and
l
. Then
over
a
the GAF
w
has
d
branch points of order
\frac{m+1}{d}
.
Therefore, when we go around the point
a
, the ratio of any two germs of the function
w
is preserved.
Hence if
w^{z^{*}}_{r},w^{z^{*}}_{t}
are two germs of the GAF
w(z)
at an arbitrary point
z^{*}\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
, then
the ratio
w^{z^{*}}_{r}/w^{z^{*}}_{t}
is preserved under the continuation along any path
\gamma
in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
with beginning and end at
z^{*}
.

We fix a point
z^{*}\in\mathbb{C}
that is neither a zero nor a pole of the function
R(z)
. Let
w^{z^{*}}_{1}
be some germ of
w
at
z^{*}
.
For each
j=2,\dots,m+1
, we denote by
w^{z^{*}}_{j}
the germ of
w
at
z^{*}
such that

w^{z^{*}}_{j}(z^{*})=e^{2\pi i\frac{(j-1)}{m+1}}w^{z^{*}}_{1}(z^{*}).

Recall that we assume that
k\leq(m+1)/2
. Let us show that there is no path
\gamma\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma
with beginning and end at
z^{*}

such that the continuation along this path transforms the unordered collection of germs
\{w^{z^{*}}_{1},w^{z^{*}}_{2},\dots,w^{z^{*}}_{k}\}
to the collection
\{w^{z^{*}}_{1},w^{z^{*}}_{2},\dots,w^{z^{*}}_{k-1},w^{z^{*}}_{s}\}
, where
s=(m+1)/2+1
for odd

and
s=m/2+1
for even

.
Indeed, we have
\arg(w^{z^{*}}_{s}(z^{*})/w^{z^{*}}_{1}(z^{*}))=2\pi(s-1)/(m+1)
,
and the arguments of all possible ratios
w^{z^{*}}_{r}(z^{*})/w^{z^{*}}_{t}(z^{*})
for
r,t\leq k\leq(m+1)/2

are not greater than
2\pi(s-2)/(m+1)
(modulo

). Therefore, among the germs
w^{z^{*}}_{1},w^{z^{*}}_{2},\dots,w^{z^{*}}_{k}

there are no such ones whose ratio at the point

is
w^{z^{*}}_{s}(z^{*})/w^{z^{*}}_{1}(z^{*})
.
Since, according to the above, the ratio of two arbitrary germs of
w
is preserved under the continuation along any closed path,
the last claim proves that the required
\gamma
does not exist.
∎

In the end of this section we will consider in more detail the simplest case where
m=3
and, respectively,
k=2
.
Since in this case the covering
\pi\colon\mathfrak{R}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is 4-sheeted,
it follows that the covering
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is 6-sheeted, and it is easily seen that
any connected component of
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is at least 2-sheeted. So, we have three possibilities:
1)
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
consists of three 2-sheeted connected components,
2)
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
consists of one 2-sheeted and one 4-sheeted connected components,
3)
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a connected 6-sheeted covering.
We will show that all these three possibilities are realized giving the explicit examples.
All the corresponding surfaces
\mathfrak{R}
will be topological spheres (i.e. of zero genus).

Example 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a compactification of the Riemann surface of the GAF
w(z)=\sqrt{z}+\sqrt{z-1}
and
let
\pi\colon(z,w)\mapsto z
. It is easily seen that the covering
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is isomorphic
(under a base preserving isomorphism) to the disjoint union of the coverings of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with the help of the GAFs
w_{(1)}(z)=\sqrt{z}
,
w_{(2)}(z)=\sqrt{z-1}
,
w_{(3)}(z)=\sqrt{z/(z-1)}
.

Example 2
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a compactification of the Riemann surface of the GAF
w(z)=\sqrt[4]{z}
and let
\pi\colon(z,w)\mapsto z
. It is easily seen that
the covering
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is isomorphic (under a base preserving isomorphism)
to the disjoint union of the coverings of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with the help of the GAFs
w_{(1)}(z)=\sqrt{z}
,
w_{(2)}(z)=\sqrt[4]{z}
.

Examples
1
and
2
show that, for coverings
\pi
defined explicitly via the GAF
w(z)=\pi^{-1}(z)
that can be
expressed in terms of radicals of
z
,
it is hard to expect the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}
to be connected. In addition, it is easily shown that if
w
satisfies the
polynomial equation
P(z,w)=0
that is biquadratic in
w
,
then the corresponding surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}
is always disconnected.

Statement
4
provides a broad class of examples where the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}
is connected.
However, statement
4
itself does not explicitly define such surfaces, for example, as the solution of a corresponding equation.
Let a GAF
w
be defined by an algebraic equation
z=R(w):=P_{4}(w)/Q_{2}(w)
, where
P_{4}(w)
and
Q_{2}(w)=(w-a)(w-b)

are polynomials of degrees 4 and 2, respectively, that have no common roots and
a\neq b
. We also require that the derivative
R^{\prime}(w)
has no
multiple zeros (that is, all the zeros
w_{1},\dots,w_{5}
of the polynomial
P_{4}^{\prime}Q_{2}-P_{4}Q_{2}^{\prime}
are distinct).
Let
\mathfrak{R}
be a compactification of the Riemann surface of this GAF
w
and let
\pi\colon(z,w)\mapsto z
. Then
\mathfrak{R}

is a topological sphere (since
w
is a global coordinate) and the GAF
w=\pi^{-1}

has precisely 5 branch points of second order in the affine part
R(w_{1}),\dots,R(w_{5})
and
one branch point of second order at
\infty
. Therefore, in order for such
\pi
to satisfy the
condition of Statement
4
, and respectively,
the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}
to be connected, it is necessary to require that
R(w_{j})\neq R(w_{l})
for
l\neq j
.
Let us give an explicit example of such a surface.

Example 3
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}
be the Riemann surface of the algebraic function
w
defined as the solution of the equation

z=\frac{w^{4}-(1+i)w^{3}+3iw^{2}}{w^{2}+\frac{1+i}{3}w+\frac{i}{3}}

(96)

and let
\pi\colon(z,w)\mapsto z
. Then the surface
\widetilde{\mathfrak{R}}_{[2]}
is connected.

Proof.

We denote by
R(w)
the right-hand side of (
96
), by
P_{4}(w)
its numerator, and by
Q_{2}(w)
its denominator. Then

P_{4}^{\prime}(w)Q_{2}(w)-P_{4}(w)Q_{2}^{\prime}(w)=2w(w^{4}-1).

Consequently,
0,\pm 1,\pm i
are the zeros of
R(w)
.
In view of the above we should check that the function
R

R(0)=0
,

R(i)=(-3+6i)/5
,
R(-i)=-3+6i
.

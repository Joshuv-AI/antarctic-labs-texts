Computation of the nearest structured matrix triplet with common null space
Nicola Guglielmi, Volker Mehrmann

Abstract
We study computational methods for computing the distance to singularity, the distance to the nearest high index problem, and the distance to instability for linear differential-algebraic systems (DAEs) with dissipative Hamiltonian structure. While for general unstructured DAEs the characterization of these distances is very difficult, and partially open, it has been shown in [ 32 ] that for dissipative Hamiltonian systems and related matrix pencils there exist explicit characterizations. We will use these characterizations for the development of computational methods to compute these distances via methods that follow the flow of a differential equation converging to the smallest perturbation that destroys the property of regularity, index one or stability.

dissipative Hamiltonian systems, structured distance to singularity, structured distance to high index problem, structured distance to instability, low-rank perturbation, differential-algebraic system

Introduction

We derive computational methods for determining the distance to singularity, the
distance to the nearest high index problem, and the distance to instability for
linear, time-invariant differential-algebraic systems (DAEs) with dissipative Hamiltonian structure (dHDAEs)
. Such systems arise as linearization of general dHDAEs along a stationary solution and have the form

E\dot{x}=(J-R)x+f,

(1)

with constant coefficient matrices
E,J,R\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n,n}
,
J=-J^{\top}
, and
E=E^{\top},R=R^{\top}
symmetric positive semidefinite, a differentiable state function

and a right hand side

, see

[
5
,
18
,
24
,
31
,

,
33
,
37
,
40
,
38
,
39

for slightly varying definitions and a detailed analysis of such systems also in the context of the more general
port-Hamiltonian systems
. The matrix

is associated with the Hessian of the associated
Hamiltonian
energy function, which in the quadratic case has the form
\mathcal{H}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Ex
. It is well-known
[
5
,
33
,
40
]
that pHDAEs satisfy a
dissipation inequality

{\mathcal{H}}\big(x(t_{1})\big)-{\mathcal{H}}\big(x(t_{0})\big)\leq 0
for
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
.

Such pHDAE systems arise in all areas of science and engineering

[
4
,

,
14
,
33
,
40

as linearizations, space discretization, or approximation of physical systems and are usually model descriptions with uncertainties. It is therefore important to know whether the model is close to an ill-posed or badly formulated model, and this has been an important research topic recently, see
[

,

. Since the system properties of (

dissipative Hamiltonian (dH) matrix pencil

L(\lambda):=\lambda E-(J-R),

(2)

the discussed nearness problems can be characterized by determining the distance to the nearest singular pencil, i.e., a pencil with a
\det(\lambda E-J+R)
identically zero, the distance to the nearest high-index problem, i.e., a problem with Jordan blocks associated to the eigenvalue
\infty
of size
bigger than one, or the nearest problem on the boundary of the unstable region, i.e. a problem with purely imaginary eigenvalues. To compute these distances is very difficult for general linear systems
[

,

These methods are usually based on non-convex optimization approaches. In contrast to such approaches, we derive computational methods to compute these structured distances by following the flow of a differential equation. This approach has been shown to be extremely effective for computing the distance to singularity for general matrix pencils
[
22
]
and we will show that this holds even more so in the structured case.

Neither the methods based on non-convex optimization nor the methods based on following a flow for general pencils or structured pencils are really feasible for large scale problems.
To treat the large sparse case they have to be combined with projections on the sparsity structure and model reduction methods, see
[
2
,
3
]
, which intertwine the optimization step with model reduction via interpolation. Here we discuss only the small scale case, but the combination with interpolation methods can be carried out in an analogous way as in
[

\varepsilon

To characterize the properties of dHDAEs of the form (

S(\lambda E-A)T=\mathop{\operator@font diag}\nolimits({\cal L}_{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,{\cal L}_{\epsilon_{p}},{\cal L}^{\top}_{\eta_{1}},\ldots,{\cal L}^{\top}_{\eta_{q}},{\cal J}_{\rho_{1}}^{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,{\cal J}_{\rho_{r}}^{\lambda_{r}},{\cal N}_{\sigma_{1}},\ldots,{\cal N}_{\sigma_{s}}),

(3)

For real matrices and real transformation matrices

and

of

,

\lambda E-A
, and
\lambda_{0}=\infty

\lambda E-A
if zero is an eigenvalue of
the
\lambda A-E

The size of the largest block

The definition of stability for differential-algebraic systems varies in the literature. We call a pencil

Lyapunov stable (asymptotically stable)
if it is regular, all finite eigenvalues are in the closed (open) left half plane, and the ones lying on the imaginary axis (including
\infty
) are semisimple

13

.
Note that pencils with eigenvalues on the imaginary axis or at
\infty

3
ODE-based gradient flow approaches

In the previous section we have seen that for dH pencils the distance to singularity is characterized
by the distance to the nearest common nullspace of three structured matrices and the distance to high index and instability by the distance to the nearest common nullspace of two symmetric positive definite matrices, with perturbations that keep the structure.

The perturbation matrices that give the structured distance to singularity can be alternatively expressed as

\displaystyle(\Delta E_{*},\Delta R_{*},\Delta J_{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min\limits_{\Delta E,\Delta R,\Delta J}\|(\Delta E,\Delta R,\Delta J)\|

(9)

subj. to

\displaystyle E+\Delta E,R+\Delta R\in\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n}_{\geq 0},\Delta J\in\mathrm{Skew}^{n,n},

and

\displaystyle(E+\Delta E)x=0,\quad(R+\Delta R)x=0,\quad(J+\Delta J)x=0

\displaystyle\mbox{for some}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},x\neq 0.

Then
d_{\sing}^{\mathcal{L}}\big(\lambda E-J+R\big)=\|(\Delta E_{*},\Delta R_{*},\Delta J_{*})\|
and our algorithmic approach to minimize this functional is based on this reformulation.

3.1
A two-level minimization

To determine the minimum in (
9
) we use a two-level minimization. As an inner iteration, for a perturbation size
\varepsilon
, we consider perturbed matrices
E+\varepsilon\Delta
,
R+\varepsilon\Theta
and
J+\varepsilon\Gamma
with
\|(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)\|_{F}\leq 1
satisfying the constraints in (
9
).
Let us denote

(i)

by
(\lambda,x)
an eigenvalue/eigenvector pair of
E+\varepsilon\Delta
associated with the smallest eigenvalue and
\|x\|=1
;

(ii)

by
(\nu,u)
an eigenvalue/eigenvector pair of
R+\varepsilon\Theta
associated with the smallest eigenvalue, and
\|u\|=1
;

(iii-a)

if
n
is even, by
({\bf i}\mu,w)
an eigenvalue/eigenvector pair of
J+\varepsilon\Gamma
, with
\mu>0
such that
{\bf i}\mu
is the eigenvalue with smallest positive imaginary part and
\|w\|=1
,

(iii-b)

if
n
is odd, by
(0,w)
an eigenvalue/eigenvector pair of
J+\varepsilon\Gamma
(this exists for all
\Gamma
).

In the inner iteration, for any fixed
\varepsilon
we compute a (local) minimizer of (
9
) that is, however, different for even or odd
n
.

The case that
n
is odd

In this case the skew-symmetric matrix always has a zero eigenvalue (with an associated real eigenvector
w
) so that the only contribution to the optimization is through the alignment of
w
with
x
and
u
.
Hence, the functional to be minimized in (
9
) can be expressed in the simplified form

F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{od}}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2}+1-(x^{\top}u)^{2}+1-(x^{\top}w)^{2}\Big)

(10)

with
\|\left(\Delta,\Gamma,\Theta\right)\|_{F}\leq 1
.
It is, however, possible to include a further term
1-|u^{\top}w|^{2}
in the functional, which does not change
the solution but may have an impact on the conditioning of the problem and hence the numerical performance.

The case that
n
is even

In this case, when two eigenvalues
\pm{\bf i}\mu
(
\mu>0
) coalesce at
0
, they form
a semi-simple double eigenvalue and the associated eigenvectors
w=w_{1}+{\bf i}w_{2}
and
\overline{w}=w_{1}-{\bf i}w_{2}
form a two-dimensional
nullspace spanned by the two real vectors
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
. These can be assumed to be orthogonal to each other, i.e.
w_{1}^{\top}w_{2}=0
and have the same norm
1/\sqrt{2}
so that still
\|w\|=1
. Using
w_{1},w_{2}
, we define the real orthogonal matrix

W=\sqrt{2}\left[w_{1},w_{2}\right],

and to satisfy the constraint in (
9
), we require that

Wz=x\qquad\mbox{for some}\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

This leads to the minimization of

\|Wz-x\|\qquad\mbox{for some}\ z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Since
W
is orthogonal, the solution is
z=W^{\top}x
, and the functional to be minimized takes the form

1-x^{\top}WW^{\top}x=1-2\,(x^{\top}w_{1})^{2}-2\,(x^{\top}w_{2})^{2},

which is positive if
x
does not lie in the range of
W
and zero otherwise.

In summary, the functional in the even case is given by

F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{ev}}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2}+\mu^{2}+1-(x^{\top}u)^{2}+1-2\,(x^{\top}{\mbox{\rm Re}}(w))^{2}-2\,(x^{\top}{\mbox{\rm Im}}(w))^{2}\Big)

(11)

with
\|\left(\Delta,\Gamma,\Theta\right)\|_{F}\leq 1
.

Remark 4
.

In both the odd and the even case we have that

\min_{\|\left(\Delta,\Gamma,\Theta\right)\|_{F}\leq 1}F_{\varepsilon}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)=\min_{\|\left(\Delta,\Gamma,\Theta\right)\|_{F}=1}F_{\varepsilon}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma).

To see this, consider
(\Delta_{*},\Theta_{*},\Gamma_{*})
of Frobenius norm less than or equal to
1
giving a minimizer of the left-hand side and suppose that
(x_{*},\lambda_{*})
is the minimizing eigenvalue/eigenvector pair of

. Then choosing a matrix

such that
\widetilde{\Delta}x_{*}=0
and
\langle\Delta_{*},\widetilde{\Delta}\rangle=0

is of unit Frobenius norm and has

F_{\varepsilon}(\Delta_{1},\Theta_{*},\Gamma_{*})=F_{\varepsilon}(\Delta_{*},\Theta_{*},\Gamma_{*})

Using the functionals (
10
), respectively (
11

\min_{\|\left(\Delta,\Gamma,\Theta\right)\|_{F}=1}F_{\varepsilon}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)
is determined as an equilibrium point of the associated gradient system. Note, however, that in general this may not be a global minimizer.

For the outer iteration we consider a continuous branch, as a function of
\varepsilon

\left(\Delta(\varepsilon),\Gamma(\varepsilon),\Theta(\varepsilon)\right)
and vary
\varepsilon

in order find the smallest solution of the scalar equation

3.2
Derivatives of eigenvalues and eigenvectors

The considered minimization is an eigenvalue optimization problem. We will solve this problem by integrating a
differential(-algebraic) equation with trajectories that follow the gradient descent and satisfy further constraints. To develop such a method, we first recall a classical result, see e.g.
[
25

and an associated eigenvector of a matrix

t

Here we use the notation

Lemma 6
.

[
25

Consider a continuously differentiable matrix valued function

C(t):\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{n,n}

C(t)C(t)^{\top}=C(t)^{\top}C(t)
for all
t

Let
\lambda(t)
be a simple eigenvalue of
C(t)
for all
t

x(t)
with
\|x(t)\|=1

be the associated (right and left) eigenvector. Then
\lambda(t)
is differentiable with

\dot{\lambda}(t)=x(t)^{H}\dot{C}(t)x(t).

(12)

For
A\in\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n}
consider a perturbation matrix
\varepsilon\Delta(t)\in\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n}
that depends on a real parameter
t
. By Lemma
6
, for a simple eigenvalue
\lambda(t)\in\mathbb{R}
of
A+\varepsilon\Delta(t)

with associated eigenvector
x(t)
,
\|x(t)\|=1
, we have (omitting the dependence on

)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lambda^{2}=\varepsilon\,\lambda\,x^{\top}\dot{\Delta}x=\varepsilon\,\lambda\,\langle xx^{\top},\dot{\Delta}\rangle.

(13)

Similarly, which is needed in the case that

is even, for all
t
, if
{\bf i}\mu(t)\in{\bf i}\mathbb{R}

\mu(t)\geq 0
) is a simple eigenvalue of a matrix-valued function

, with associated eigenvector

, then we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|\mu|^{2}=\varepsilon\,\mu\,\langle{\bf i}ww^{H},\dot{\Theta}\rangle=-\varepsilon\,\mu\,\langle{\mbox{\rm Im}}\left(ww^{H}\right),\dot{\Theta}\rangle.

(14)

To derive the gradient system associated with our optimization problem, we make use of the following definition.

Definition 7
.

Let
M\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n,n}
be a singular matrix with a simple zero eigenvalue.
The
group inverse
(reduced resolvent) of

MG=GM,\qquad GMG=G,\quad\mbox{and}\quad MGM=M.

It is well-known, see

Lemma 8
.

[

Consider a sufficiently often differentiable matrix function

C:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}^{n,n}.

Let
\lambda(t)
be a simple eigenvalue of

for all

and let
x(t)
, with
\|x(t)\|=1

be the associated right eigenvector function.
Moreover, let

be the group inverse of
M(t)

satisfies the system of differential equations

\dot{x}=x\,x^{H}G(t)\dot{M}(t)x-G(t)\dot{M}(t)x.

(15)

Moreover, if

is pointwise normal, then

\dot{x}(t)=-G(t)\dot{M}(t)x(t)

4.1
Computation of the gradient

The functional
F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{od}}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)
in (
10
) has several parts.
Applying Lemma
6
for perturbations
\varepsilon\Delta(t)
of
E
and
\varepsilon\Theta(t)
of
R
, the computation of the gradient of the part
\frac{1}{2}\Big(\lambda^{2}+\nu^{2}\Big)
is obtained from the expressions

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\lambda^{2}=\varepsilon\,\lambda\,\langle xx^{\top},\dot{\Delta}\rangle,\quad\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\nu^{2}=\varepsilon\,\nu\,\langle uu^{\top},\dot{\Theta}\rangle.

Considering orthogonal projections with respect to the Frobenius inner product onto the matrix manifold

\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n}
,
we identify the constrained gradient directions of these terms as

\dot{\Delta}\propto\lambda\,xx^{\top},\qquad\dot{\Theta}\propto\nu\,uu^{\top},

respectively. (Here
\propto
denotes proportionality.) In order to treat the other terms, we observe that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(|x^{\top}u|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(x^{\top}uu^{\top}x\right)=x^{\top}uu^{\top}\dot{x}+u^{\top}xx^{\top}\dot{u},

and thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(1-|x^{\top}u|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left((x^{\top}u)u^{\top}G\dot{\Delta}x+(u^{\top}x)x^{\top}N\dot{\Theta}u\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\Big\langle\theta\,G^{\top}ux^{\top},\dot{\Delta}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\theta\,N^{\top}xu^{\top},\dot{\Theta}\Big\rangle\right),

where
\theta=x^{\top}u
,
G
is the pseudoinverse of
E+\varepsilon\Delta-\lambda I
, and
N
is the pseudoinverse of

R+\varepsilon\Theta-\nu I
.

Since
n
is odd, which means that (generically)
0
is a simple eigenvalue of
J+\varepsilon\Gamma
,
for the last term of (
10
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(1-(x^{\top}w)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\Big\langle\eta\,G^{\top}wx^{\top},\dot{\Delta}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\eta\,P^{\top}xw^{\top},\dot{\Gamma}\Big\rangle\right),

where
\eta=x^{\top}w
and
P
is the pseudoinverse of
J+\varepsilon\Gamma
.

4.2
The gradient system of ODEs for the flow in the odd case

In order to compute the steepest descent direction, we minimize the gradient of
F_{\varepsilon}
and collect the
summands involving
\dot{\Delta}
,
\dot{\Theta}
and those involving
\dot{\Gamma}
.
Letting

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta G^{\top}u\ +\eta G^{\top}w,

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta N^{\top}x,

(17)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta P^{\top}x,

we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F_{\varepsilon}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\,\langle\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top},\dot{\Delta}\rangle+\varepsilon\,\langle\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top},\dot{\Theta}\rangle+\varepsilon\,\langle r\,w^{\top},\dot{\Gamma}\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\left(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\langle\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top}\right),\dot{\Delta}\rangle+\langle\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top}\right),\dot{\Theta}\rangle+\varepsilon\,\langle\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right),\dot{\Gamma}\rangle\right),

where we have used the structural properties of
\dot{\Delta},\dot{\Theta}
(symmetric) and
\dot{\Gamma}

(skew-symmetric) and the property that for real matrices
A
and
B
,
\langle\mathrm{Sym}(A),\mathrm{Skew}(B)\rangle=0
.
Equation (
18
) identifies the gradient of the functional,

{\mathbf{G}}=\left(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top}\right),\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top}\right),\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right)\right):=\left({\mathbf{G}}_{E},{\mathbf{G}}_{R},{\mathbf{G}}_{J}\right).

(19)

Since we want to impose a norm constraint on the perturbation
(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)
we need the
following result.

Lemma 9

(Direction of steepest admissible ascent)
.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}=\left({\mathbf{G}}_{E},{\mathbf{G}}_{R},{\mathbf{G}}_{J}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n,3n}
,
{\mathbf{Z}}=\left(Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3}\right),{\mathbf{M}}=\left(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n,3n}

with
{\|(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)\|_{F}=1}
.
A solution of the optimization problem

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Z}}_{\star}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\|{\mathbf{Z}}\|_{F}=1,\,\,\langle{\mathbf{M}},{\mathbf{Z}}\rangle=0}\ \langle{\mathbf{G}},{\mathbf{Z}}\rangle

(20)

subj. to

\displaystyle\Delta,\Theta\in\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n},\Gamma\in\mathrm{Skew}^{n,n},

is given by

\displaystyle\mu{\mathbf{Z}}_{\star}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\mathbf{G}}+\varrho\,{\mathbf{M}},

(22)

\displaystyle\varrho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\langle\Delta,\mathrm{Sym}\left((\lambda x+p)\,x^{\top}\right)\rangle+\langle\Theta,\mathrm{Sym}\left((\nu u+q)\,u^{\top}\right)\rangle+\langle\Gamma,\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right)\rangle\right)

where
\mu
is the Frobenius norm of the matrix on the right-hand side. The solution is unique if
{\mathbf{G}}
is not a multiple of
{\mathbf{M}}
.

Proof.

The result follows on noting that the function to minimize is a real inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n,3n}
, and the real inner product with a given vector
(which here is a matrix) is minimized over a subspace by orthogonally projecting the vector onto that subspace. The expression in (
22
) is
the orthogonal projection of
G
to the tangent space at
{\mathbf{M}}
of the manifold of matrices of unit Frobenius norm.
∎

Taking into consideration projection with respect to the Frobenius inner product of the vector field onto the manifolds of symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices, this leads to the system of differential equations for the perturbation matrices

\displaystyle\dot{\Delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top}\right)+\varrho\Delta,

\displaystyle\dot{\Theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top}\right)+\varrho\Theta,

(23)

\displaystyle\dot{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right)+\varrho\Gamma,

where, for
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n,n}
,
\mathrm{Sym}(X)=\frac{X+X^{\top}}{2}
,
\mathrm{Skew}(X)=\frac{X-X^{\top}}{2}
, and

\varrho=\left(\langle\Delta,\mathrm{Sym}\left((\lambda x+p)\,x^{\top}\right)\rangle+\langle\Theta,\mathrm{Sym}\left((\nu u+q)\,u^{\top}\right)\rangle+\langle\Gamma,\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right)\rangle\right)

is used to ensure the norm conservation, i.e.
\langle(\dot{\Delta},\dot{\Theta},\dot{\Gamma}),\left(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma\right)\rangle=0
.

Theorem 10
.

Let
(\Delta(t),\Theta(t),\Gamma(t))
of unit Frobenius norm satisfy the differential equation (
23
).
If
\lambda(t)
is a simple eigenvalue of
A+\varepsilon\Delta(t)
, then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F_{\varepsilon}\left(\Delta(t),\Theta(t),\Gamma(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

(24)

Proof.

The result follows directly by the fact that (
23
) is a constrained gradient system.

In this way we have preserved the symmetry of
E,R
and the skew-symmetry of
J
.
It may happen however, that along the solution trajectory of (
23
), due to the projection on the matrix manifolds the smallest eigenvalue

\nu
of
R+\varepsilon\Theta
and/or the smallest eigenvalue
\lambda
of
E+\varepsilon\Delta
become negative. In this case the perturbed system is not a dissipative Hamiltonian system any longer. This, however, is in general not an issue for the optimization algorithm, since the dynamical gradient system leads to eigenvalues

\nu
,
\lambda
, with
|\nu|
as small as possible, for a given
\varepsilon
, and thus drives them to zero when
\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{*}
, so that in the limiting situation also the positive semidefiniteness of
E+\varepsilon\Delta
and
R+\varepsilon\Theta
holds.

4.3
Stationary points of (
23
) and low rank property

In this subsection we discuss the existence of stationary points of the solution trajectory of (
23
).

Lemma 11
.

Let
\varepsilon
be fixed and
F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{od}}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)>0
. Let
\lambda
be a simple eigenvalue of
E+\varepsilon\Delta
with associate normalized eigenvector
x
, let
\nu
be a simple eigenvalue of
R+\varepsilon\Theta
with associate normalized eigenvector
u
, and let
0
be a simple eigenvalue of
J+\varepsilon\Gamma
with associate normalized eigenvector
w
. Then, in the generic situation, i.e., if

\lambda,\nu\neq 0
,
\theta=x^{\top}u\neq 0
, and
\eta=x^{\top}w\in(0,1)
, we have

\lambda x+p\neq 0,\qquad\nu u+q\neq 0,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad r\neq 0.

(25)

Proof.

The proofs for the three cases are similar.

(i)

Exploiting the property that
Gx=0
, see
[
35
]
, we obtain that

x^{\top}p=0
.
If we had

x^{\top}\left(\lambda x+p\right)=0
, then this would imply
\lambda x^{\top}x=0
and thus, since
\lambda\neq 0
, we get a contradiction, since
\|x\|=1
.

(ii)

Exploiting the property
Nu=0
, we obtain that

u^{\top}q=0
.
If we had
u^{\top}\left(\nu u+q\right)=0
, then we get
\nu u^{\top}u=0
, and again we have a contradiction.

(iii)

Having assumed
\eta=x^{\top}w\neq 1
we have that
x
and
w
are not aligned. As a consequence

\eta P^{\top}x\neq 0
.

Using Lemma
11
, we have the following characterization of stationary points..

Theorem 12
.

Let
(\Delta(t),\Theta(t),\Gamma(t))
of unit Frobenius norm satisfy the differential equation (

).
Moreover, suppose that for all

;

(

)

,
\dot{\Theta}=0
,

(

The proof follows directly by equating to zero the right hand side of (

∎

We have also the following extremality property.

Theorem 13
.

Consider the functional (
10
) and suppose that

\displaystyle-\Pi_{J}\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right)+\varrho\Gamma,

Rank two dynamics

Theorem
12
motivates to search for a differential equation on the manifold of rank two symmetric/skew-symmetric matrices,
which still leads to a gradient system for

\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma
lying
in the respective tangent spaces.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{n,n}:=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:{\rm rank}(X)=2\}
. Then we restrict the perturbations to the matrix manifolds

\Delta,\Theta\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n}},\qquad\Gamma\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\mathrm{Skew}},

(33)

where
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n}}={\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{n,n}\cap\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\mathrm{Skew}^{n,n}}={\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{n,n}\cap\mathrm{Skew}^{n,n}

Following
[
26

, every real symmetric rank two matrix

of dimension

can be written in
the form

X=USU^{\rm T},

(34)

where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times 2}
has orthonormal
columns, i.e.,
U^{\rm T}U=I_{2}

and
S\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2\times 2}

is diagonal.
Note that the representation (

\widetilde{U}=UU_{1}

\widetilde{S}=U_{1}^{\rm T}SU_{1}

Let

\displaystyle T_{U}{\mathcal{V}}_{n,2}

Following
[

, every tangent matrix

The orthogonal projection onto the tangent space

5.2
Monotonicity of the functional

We have the following monotonicity result, which establishes that (
37
) is a gradient system for

F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{od}}(\Delta(t),\Theta(t),\Gamma(t))
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\Delta(t),\Theta(t)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\mathrm{Sym}^{n,n}},\Gamma(t)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{\mathrm{Skew}^{n,n}}
satisfy the differential equation (
37
), and suppose that

F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{od}}(\Delta(t),\Theta(t),\Gamma(t))>0.

If
\lambda(t)
is a simple eigenvalue of
E+\varepsilon\Delta(t)
,
\mu(t)
is a simple eigenvalue of
R+\varepsilon\Theta(t)
, and
0

a simple eigenvalue of
J+\varepsilon\Gamma
, then

\frac{d}{dt}F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{od}}(\Delta(t),\Theta(t),\Gamma(t))<0.

(41)

Proof.

We note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\frac{d}{dt}F_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{od}}(\Delta,\Theta,\Gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\,\langle xx^{\top},\dot{\Delta}\rangle+\nu\,\langle uu^{\top},\dot{\Theta}\rangle+\left(\Big\langle px^{\top},\dot{\Delta}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle qu^{\top},\dot{\Theta}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle rw^{\top},\dot{\Gamma}\Big\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}-\Big\langle\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top},-P^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\Delta}\Bigl(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\rangle+\varrho\langle\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top},\Delta\rangle

\displaystyle{}-\Big\langle\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top},-P^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\Theta}\Bigl(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\rangle+\varrho\langle\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top},\Theta\rangle

\displaystyle{}-\Big\langle r\,w^{\top},-P^{\mathrm{Skew}}_{\Gamma}\Bigl(\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\rangle+\varrho\langle r\,w^{\top},\Theta\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho^{2}-\Big\|P^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\Delta}\Bigl(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\|^{2}-\Big\|P^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\Theta}\Bigl(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Big\|P^{\mathrm{Skew}}_{\Gamma}\Bigl(\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\|^{2},

with

\displaystyle\varrho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\Delta,P^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\Delta}\Bigl(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\lambda x+p\right)\,x^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\Theta,P^{\mathrm{Sym}}_{\Theta}\Bigl(\mathrm{Sym}\left(\left(\nu u+q\right)\,u^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big\langle\Gamma,P^{\mathrm{Skew}}_{\Gamma}\Bigl(\mathrm{Skew}\left(r\,w^{\top}\right)\Bigr)\Big\rangle.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality proves the assertion.

Conclusions and further work

We have investigated a structured distance problem related to the study of port-Hamiltonian systems, that is determining
the closest triplet of matrices to a given one, sharing a common null-space.

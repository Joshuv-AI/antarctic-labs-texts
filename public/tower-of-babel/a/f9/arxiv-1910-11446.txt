Finite-Dimensional Irreducible Modules of the Racah Algebra at Characteristic Zero
Hau-Wen HUANG † and Sarah BOCKTING-CONRAD ‡

Abstract
Assume that 𝔽 \mathbb{F} is an algebraically closed field with characteristic zero. The Racah algebra ℜ \Re is the unital associative 𝔽 \mathbb{F} -algebra defined by generators and relations in the following way. The generators are A A , B B , C C , D D and the relations assert that [ A , B ] = [ B , C ] = [ C , A ] = 2 ​ D [A,B]=[B,C]=[C,A]=2D and that each of [ A , D ] + A ​ C − B ​ A [A,D]+AC-BA , [ B , D ] + B ​ A − C ​ B [B,D]+BA-CB , [ C , D ] + C ​ B − A ​ C [C,D]+CB-AC is central in ℜ \Re . In this paper we discuss the finite-dimensional irreducible ℜ \Re -modules in detail and classify them up to isomorphism. To do this, we apply an infinite-dimensional ℜ \Re -module and its universal property. We additionally give the necessary and sufficient conditions for A A , B B , C C to be diagonalizable on finite-dimensional irreducible ℜ \Re -modules.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we adopt the following conventions. Let
\mathbb{F}
denote an algebraically closed field and let
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{F}
denote the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\mathbb{Z}
denote the set of integers and let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of nonnegative integers. The bracket
[\,,\,]
stands for the commutator.

In this paper we consider the Racah algebra
\Re
over
\mathbb{F}
defined by generators and relations as follows. The generators are
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
and the relations assert that

\displaystyle[A,B]=[B,C]=[C,A]=2D

and that each of

\displaystyle[A,D]+AC-BA,\qquad[B,D]+BA-CB,\qquad[C,D]+CB-AC

is central in
\Re
. The Racah algebra
\Re
is a universal analog of the original Racah algebras which first appeared in
[
17
]
. In that paper, the original Racah algebras were used to establish a link between representation theory and the quantum mechanical coupling of three angular momenta. Since that time, the connections between the Racah algebras and many other areas have been explored. We mention a few of them here. Their connections with the additive double-affine Hecke algebra of type
\big(C_{1}^{\vee},C_{1}\big)
, the Bannai–Ito algebra, and the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{su}(2)
,
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
were investigated in
[
6
,
9
,
10
,
14
]
. Their realizations via the Racah polynomials, the intermediate Casimir operators, and the superintegrable models in two dimensions were presented in
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
]
. For information concerning the higher rank Racah algebras, see
[
3
,
4
]
.

We now mention an error in the literature on Racah algebras.
In
[
2
]
, the authors considered the finite-dimensional irreducible modules of the original Racah algebras when
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{F}=0
. In
[
2
, Lemma 5.6]
, it was claimed that the defining generators can be diagonalized on any finite-dimensional irreducible module of the Racah algebras. This result was then used in their classification of finite-dimensional irreducible modules of the Racah algebras in
[
2
, Section 6]
. It turns out that
[
2
, Lemma 5.6]
is conditional. We give the following example to help illustrate the issue arising in
[
2
]
.

Example 1.1
.

It is routine to verify that there exists a five-dimensional
\Re
-module
V
that has an
\mathbb{F}
-basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i=0}^{4}
with respect to which the matrices representing
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
are
\frac{1}{4}
times

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}15&0&0&0&0\\
4&3&0&0&0\\
0&4&-1&0&0\\
0&0&4&3&0\\
0&0&0&4&15\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}15&-36&0&0&0\\
0&3&-6&0&0\\
0&0&-1&-6&0\\
0&0&0&3&-36\\
0&0&0&0&15\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-9&36&0&0&0\\
-4&15&6&0&0\\
0&-4&23&6&0\\
0&0&-4&15&36\\
0&0&0&-4&-9\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}18&-54&0&0&0\\
6&-15&-3&0&0\\
0&2&0&3&0\\
0&0&-2&15&54\\
0&0&0&-6&-18\end{pmatrix},

respectively. It follows that

\displaystyle[A,D]+AC-BA=[B,D]+BA-CB=[C,D]+CB-AC=0

on the
\Re
-module
V
. For each of
A
,
B
,
C
, it is straightforward to verify that its minimal polynomial on
V
is

\displaystyle\left(x+\frac{1}{4}\right)\left(x-\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2}\left(x-\frac{15}{4}\right)^{2}.

Therefore none of
A
,
B
,
C
is diagonalizable on
V
. We now show that
V
is in fact irreducible. Let
W
denote a nonzero
\Re
-submodule of
V
. We will show that
W=V
. Observe that the element
B
has exactly three eigenvalues on
V
, namely
\frac{15}{4}
,
\frac{3}{4}
,
-\frac{1}{4}
. A direct calculation yields that the corresponding eigenspaces are each of dimension
1
and are spanned by

\displaystyle v_{0},\qquad 3v_{0}+v_{1},\qquad 27v_{0}+12v_{1}+8v_{2},

(1.1)

respectively. Since
W
is nonzero, at least one of
\frac{15}{4}
,
\frac{3}{4}
,
-\frac{1}{4}
is an eigenvalue of
B
on
W
. Therefore
W
contains at least one of the elements listed in (
1.1
). Observe that the
\Re
-module
V
is generated by
v_{0}
. Thus, if
v_{0}\in W
then
W=V
. If
3v_{0}+v_{1}\in W
then

\displaystyle v_{0}=\frac{2}{9}\left(A-2D-\frac{9}{4}\right)(3v_{0}+v_{1})\in W

and hence
W=V
. If
27v_{0}+12v_{1}+8v_{2}\in W
then

\displaystyle 3v_{0}+v_{1}=-\frac{1}{18}\left(A+2D-\frac{11}{4}\right)(27v_{0}+12v_{1}+8v_{2})\in W

and hence
W=V
. Therefore
W=V
. Since the
\Re
-module
V
is irreducible, we now have a counterexample to
[
2
, Lemma 5.6]
.

In light of the above example, we see that the finite-dimensional irreducible
\Re
-modules are not yet completely classified. The goal of this paper is to provide such a classification. The idea of our classification comes from
[

]
. We mention that a similar issue arises in the case of the Bannai–Ito algebra

[

which is addressed by the first author in
[

. The result
[
15
, Theorem 5.4]
reveals that the Racah algebra

is isomorphic to an

-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{BI}

[
16
]
and this result, the lattices of
\Re
-submodules of finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{BI}
-modules are classified in
[
14
]
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we state our classification of finite-dimensional irreducible
\Re
-modules in Theorem
2.5

3
we display an infinite-dimensional
\Re
-module and describe its universal property. In Section

we give necessary and sufficient conditions for the irreducibility of finite-dimensional
\Re
-modules. In Section

we study the isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional irreducible
\Re
-modules. In Section

we give our proof of Theorem

.

2
Statement of results

In this section we more formally introduce the Racah algebra
\Re
and state the main result of the paper which gives a classification of the finite-dimensional irreducible modules of the Racah algebra
\Re

Definition 2.1

(
[
1

)
.

The
Racah algebra

\mathbb{F}
-algebra defined by generators and relations in the following way. The generators are
A
,
B
,
C
,
D

\displaystyle[A,B]=[B,C]=[C,A]=2D

and that each of

\displaystyle[A,D]+AC-BA,\qquad[B,D]+BA-CB,\qquad[C,D]+CB-AC

\Re

It follows from the above definition that the element
A+B+C
is also central in
\Re
. For notational convenience, we let

\displaystyle\alpha=[A,D]+AC-BA,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\beta=[B,D]+BA-CB,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\gamma=[C,D]+CB-AC,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\delta=A+B+C.

(2.5)

Lemma 2.2
.

(i)

The Racah algebra
\Re
is generated by the elements
A
,
B
,
C
.

(ii)

The Racah algebra
\Re
is generated by the elements
A
,
B
,
\delta
.

Proof.

(i) By (
2.1
) the element
D
can be expressed in terms of
A
,
B
. Hence (i) follows from Definition
2.1
.
(ii) By (
2.5
) the element
C
can be expressed in terms of
A
,
B
,
\delta
. Hence (ii) follows from (i).
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

The
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
\Re
has a presentation given by generators
A
,
B
,
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\delta
and relations

\displaystyle A^{2}B-2ABA+BA^{2}-2AB-2BA=2A^{2}-2A\delta+2\alpha,

(2.6)

\displaystyle AB^{2}-2BAB+B^{2}A-2AB-2BA=2B^{2}-2B\delta-2\beta,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\alpha A=A\alpha,\qquad\beta A=A\beta,\qquad\delta A=A\delta,

\displaystyle\alpha B=B\alpha,\qquad\beta B=B\beta,\qquad\delta B=B\delta,\qquad\alpha\delta=\delta\alpha,\qquad\beta\delta=\delta\beta.

Proof.

We know from Lemma
2.2
(ii) that
A
,
B
,
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\delta
generate
\Re
. Observe that

by (

) and

by (

). The result can now be obtained by either using these two facts to eliminate

,
D
from the presentation of
\Re
given in Definition

or by using

to eliminate
D
from the presentation of
\Re
given in
[
1
, Proposition 3.4]
.
∎

In the following proposition, we assert the existence of certain finite-dimensional
\Re

Proposition 2.4
.

For any

and any

there exists a

-dimensional

satisfying each of the following conditions:

(i)

There exists an
\mathbb{F}
-basis
\{v_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
for
R_{d}(a,b,c)
with respect to which the matrices representing
A
and
B
are

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{0}&&&&{\bf 0}\\
1&\theta_{1}\\
&1&\theta_{2}\\
&&\ddots&\ddots\\
{\bf 0}&&&1&\theta_{d}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{0}^{*}&\varphi_{1}&&&{\bf 0}\\
&\theta_{1}^{*}&\varphi_{2}\\
&&\theta_{2}^{*}&\ddots\\
&&&\ddots&\varphi_{d}\\
{\bf 0}&&&&\theta_{d}^{*}\end{pmatrix},

respectively, where

\displaystyle\theta_{i}=\big(a+\textstyle\frac{d}{2}-i\big)\big(a+\textstyle\frac{d}{2}-i+1\big),\qquad 0\leq i\leq d,

\displaystyle\theta^{*}_{i}=\big(b+\textstyle\frac{d}{2}-i\big)\big(b+\textstyle\frac{d}{2}-i+1\big),\qquad 0\leq i\leq d,

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}=i(i-d-1)\big(a+b+c+\textstyle\frac{d}{2}-i+2\big)\big(a+b-c+\textstyle\frac{d}{2}-i+1\big),\qquad 1\leq i\leq d.

(ii)

The elements
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\delta
act on

as scalar multiplication by

\displaystyle(c-b)(c+b+1)\big(a-\textstyle\frac{d}{2}\big)\big(a+\textstyle\frac{d}{2}+1\big),

\displaystyle(a-c)(a+c+1)\big(b-\textstyle\frac{d}{2}\big)\big(b+\textstyle\frac{d}{2}+1\big),

\displaystyle\textstyle\frac{d}{2}\big(\textstyle\frac{d}{2}+1\big)+a(a+1)+b(b+1)+c(c+1),

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.3
, this result can be verified through routine, though tedious, computations.
∎

In order to state our main result more succinctly, we will use the following conventions and definitions.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{P}_{d}
denote the set of all

\displaystyle a+b+c+1,-a+b+c,a-b+c,a+b-c\not\in\big\{\tfrac{d}{2}-i\,\big|\,i=1,2,\ldots,d\big\}.

We define an action of the abelian group

\displaystyle(a,b,c)^{(-1,1,1)}=(-a-1,b,c),\qquad(a,b,c)^{(1,-1,1)}=(a,-b-1,c),

\displaystyle(a,b,c)^{(1,1,-1)}=(a,b,-c-1)

\mathbf{P}/\{\pm 1\}^{3}
denote the set of the
\{\pm 1\}^{3}
-orbits of
\mathbf{P}
. For

, let

denote the
\{\pm 1\}^{3}
-orbit of
\mathbf{P}
that contains
(a,b,c)
. We are now ready to state the classification of finite-dimensional irreducible
\Re

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that
\mathbb{F}
is algebraically closed with
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{F}=0
. Let
d
denote a nonnegative integer. Let
\mathbf{M}
denote the set of all isomorphism classes of irreducible

given by

\displaystyle[a,b,c]\mapsto\hbox{the isomorphism class of $R_{d}(a,b,c)$}

for all

.

We will give a proof of Theorem
2.5

Proposition 4.3

\Re

(\Leftarrow)

Theorem 6.3

Given any scalar

\displaystyle\vartheta_{i}(\kappa)=(\kappa-i)(\kappa-i+1)\qquad\hbox{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

Since
\operatorname{char}\mathbb{F}=0
, for any distinct integers

are equal if and only if

Since
\mathbb{F}
is algebraically closed, we may choose a scalar

such that

is an eigenvalue of

on

is of dimension

such that
\vartheta_{j}(\kappa)
is an eigenvalue of

is not an eigenvalue of

\displaystyle a=\kappa-j-\tfrac{d}{2}.

Similarly, there exists a scalar

and an integer

such that

is an eigenvalue of
B
but
\vartheta_{k-1}(\lambda)
is not an eigenvalue of
B
in
V
. We set

\displaystyle b=\lambda-k-\tfrac{d}{2}.

Observe that under these settings, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{i}=\vartheta_{i+j}(\kappa)\qquad\hbox{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$},

(6.1)

\displaystyle\theta_{i}^{*}=\vartheta_{i+k}(\lambda)\qquad\hbox{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

(6.2)

, the element
\delta
acts on

is algebraically closed, there exists a scalar

\displaystyle\eta=\textstyle\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{d}{2}+1\big)+a(a+1)+b(b+1)+c(c+1).

\Re

Given any

Lipschitz estimates in the Besov settings for Young and rough differential equations
Peter Friz, Hannes Kern, Pavel Zorin-Kranich

Abstract
We develop a set of techniques that enable us to effectively recover Besov rough analysis from p p -variation rough analysis. Central to our approach are new metric groups, in which some objects in rough path theory that have been previously viewed as two-parameter can be considered as path increments. Furthermore, we develop highly precise Lipschitz estimates for Young and rough differential equations, both in the variation and Besov scale.

Introduction

Rough path theory gives meaning to differential equations of the form

dY_{t}=\phi(Y_{t})dX_{t}

(1.1)

with a (multidimensional) driver
X
that has (locally) finite
r
-variation.
When
r<2
, this equation can be understood as a Young integral equation.
For
r\geq 2
, it was understood by T. Lyons
[
Lyo98
]
that
X
needs to be enhanced with additional information to restore well-posedness of the problem. In particular, for
r\in[2,3)
, an appropriate driver is a level-
2
rough path of the form
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
, where
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}(s,t)
is typically interpreted as
\int_{s}^{t}(X_{u}-X_{s})\otimes dX_{u}
.

While finite
r
-variation is the minimum regularity hypothesis on
X
needed to make sense of (
1.1
), higher regularity hypotheses may lead to stronger well-posedness results for the solution
Y
.
A classical hypothesis is Hölder continuity, and several authors
[
PT16
,
FP18
,
LPT19
,
FSZ22
,
LPT22
]
have contributed to extending rough path estimates to Sobolev and Besov scale.
We note that the Besov sewing lemma of
[
FSZ22
]
has found recent applications
[
BW24
]
in non-linear SPDE theory.

In this article, we present a way of upgrading
r
-variation estimates for differential equations of the form (
1.1
) to Besov norm estimates.
At the highest level, our approach consists of two steps.
Firstly, we show equivalence between Besov norms and metrics on the space of drivers
\mathbf{X}
and nested norms involving local
r
-variation and an outer
2
-parameter Besov norm.
Secondly, we show local
r
-variation estimates for differential equations that are directly applicable to these nested norms.
We found the previously existing
r
-variation estimates not to be sufficiently precise for this purpose.

It would be interesting to know whether an analogous treatment of
partial
differential equations subjected to rough noise, also in space-time, is possible, starting with suitably localized estimates and recovering well-posedness results in a range of function spaces. Initial progress in this direction was made in the article
[
Zor22
]
, which contains a localized reconstruction theorem that recovers the Besov space case first shown in
[
HL17
]
.

Let

The

2
Besov and Variation norms of rough paths

2.1
Besov norms

Let us recall the definition of Besov spaces together with some basic embeddings. Similar embeddings for the real-valued case can be found in
[
Sim90
]
,
[
Tri92
]
.
Our setting is slightly more general, in that we consider Besov norms of two-parameter processes that are not necessarily the difference/distance of a one-parameter process.
We consider a fixed time horizon
T>0
and denote the simplex by

\Delta_{T}:=\{(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}\penalty\ |\penalty\ s\leq t\}\,.

Then, given a
\chi:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)
, we define

\Omega_{p}^{\chi}(t):=\sup_{0<h\leq t}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\chi(s,s+h)^{p}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,

for any
0<p\leq\infty
, with the usual modification for
p=\infty
. The Besov norm of
\chi
is then defined as follows:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha>0,0<p,q\leq\infty
. We then set

\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\Omega_{p}^{\chi}(t)}{t^{\alpha}}\right)^{q}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,

with the usual modification for
q=\infty
.
We write
\chi\in B^{\alpha}_{p,q}
if the above norm is finite.
We further set

\left\|\chi\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\alpha q}\left(\int_{0}^{T-t}\chi(s,s+t)^{p}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

This setting allows us to simultaneously consider one-parameter processes in metric spaces and two-parameter processes in normed spaces. If
f:[0,T]\to X
for some metric space
(X,d)
, we set

\left\|f\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}:=\left\|d(f(\cdot),f(\cdot))\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}},\qquad\left\|f\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}:=\left\|d(f(\cdot),f(\cdot))\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

and if
A:\Delta_{T}\to E
maps into a normed space
(E,\left|\cdot\right|)
, we set

\left\|A\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}:=\left\|\left|A_{\cdot,\cdot}\right|\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}},\qquad\left\|A\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}:=\left\|\left|A_{\cdot,\cdot}\right|\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,.

The following assumption is motivated by the (quasi-)metric case but extends to the variation norm of
f
, see Remark
2.17
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
\chi
is
\rho
-subadditive
for some
\rho>0
if, for all
s,u,t\in[0,T]
with
s\leq u\leq t
, we have

\chi(s,t)^{\rho}\leq\chi(s,u)^{\rho}+\chi(u,t)^{\rho}\,.

(2.1)

In the case
\rho=1
, that is,

\chi(s,t)\leq\chi(s,u)+\chi(u,t),

(2.2)

we say that
\chi
is
subadditive
.

Under this condition, it turns out that
\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}
and
\left\|\chi\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}
are equivalent, as the following Lemma shows.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\chi:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be
\rho
-subadditive in the sense of (
2.1
) for some
\rho>0
.
Further assume
\alpha\in(0,1],p,q\in(0,\infty]
. Then

\left\|\chi\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\leq\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\leq(2^{\alpha\tilde{\rho}}-1)^{-1/\tilde{\rho}}\left\|\chi\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}},

where
\tilde{\rho}=\min(1,\rho,p,q)
.

Proof.

It is clear from the definition that

\left\|\chi\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\leq\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,,

(2.3)

so we only need to show the second inequality.
It suffices to consider that case
\left\|\chi\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}<\infty
.

Since
\rho
-subadditivety implies
\hat{\rho}
-subadditivity for any
\hat{\rho}\in(0,\rho)
, we may assume that
\rho=\tilde{\rho}=\min(1,\rho,p,q)
.
Let

a(h):=\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\chi(s,s+h)^{p}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad 0<h\leq T.

Using
\rho
-subadditivity of
\chi
, we see that, for every
h_{1},h_{2}>0
with
h_{1}+h_{2}\leq T
, we have

\displaystyle a(h_{1}+h_{2})^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{T-h_{1}-h_{2}}\chi(s,s+h_{1}+h_{2})^{\rho(p/\rho)}\text{d}s\right)^{\rho^{2}\frac{1}{p/\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T-h_{1}-h_{2}}(\chi(s,s+h_{1})^{\rho}+\chi(s+h_{1},s+h_{1}+h_{2})^{\rho})^{p/\rho}\text{d}s\right)^{\rho^{2}\frac{1}{p/\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\left(\int_{0}^{T-h_{1}-h_{2}}(\chi(s,s+h_{1})^{\rho})^{p/\rho}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p/\rho}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int_{0}^{T-h_{1}-h_{2}}(\chi(s+h_{1},s+h_{1}+h_{2})^{\rho})^{p/\rho}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p/\rho}}\bigg)^{\rho^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(a(h_{1})^{\frac{1}{\rho}}+a(h_{2})^{\frac{1}{\rho}})^{\rho^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq a(h_{1})^{\rho}+a(h_{2})^{\rho}\,,

so that
a
is
\rho
-subadditive, in the sense that

a(h_{1}+h_{2})^{\rho}\leq a(h_{1})^{\rho}+a(h_{2})^{\rho}

whenever both sides are defined.

This in turn implies that

\displaystyle a(t)^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{h\in(t/3,2t/3)}a(t-h)^{\rho}+a(h)^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\frac{3}{t}\int_{t/3}^{2t/3}(a(t-h)^{\rho}+a(h)^{\rho})^{q/\rho}\text{d}h\bigr)^{\rho/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\alpha\rho}\bigl(\int_{t/3}^{2t/3}(h^{-\alpha}a(h))^{q}\frac{\text{d}h}{h}\bigr)^{\rho/q}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\alpha\rho}(\left\|\chi\right\|^{*}_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}})^{\rho}.

In particular,
\lim_{t\to 0}a(t)=0
.
Since every number in
(0,1]
can be written as a sum of distinct negative integer powers of
2
, this allows us to use
\rho
-subadditivity of
a
to conclude

\sup_{h\in(0,t]}a(h)^{\rho}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a(2^{-j}t)^{\rho}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(t^{-\alpha\rho}\sup_{0<h\leq t}a(h)^{\rho}\right)^{q/\rho}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\right)^{\rho/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(t^{-\alpha\rho}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a(2^{-j}t)^{\rho}\right)^{q/\rho}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\right)^{\rho/q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(t^{-\alpha\rho}a(2^{-j}t)^{\rho}\right)^{q/\rho}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\right)^{\rho/q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j\alpha\rho}\left(\int_{0}^{2^{-j}T}\left(t^{-\alpha\rho}a(t)^{\rho}\right)^{q/\rho}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\right)^{\rho/q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{-j\alpha\rho}(\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}^{*})^{\rho}.\qed

[

f

Lemma 2.4

(Dependence on
\alpha,q
)
.

Let
\chi:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
0<\alpha,\tilde{\alpha}
and
0<p,q,\tilde{q}\leq\infty
. Assume that
\alpha<\tilde{\alpha}
and
q\leq\tilde{q}
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
only depending on
\alpha,\tilde{\alpha},q,\tilde{q}
and
T
such that

\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\leq c\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\tilde{\alpha}}_{p,\tilde{q}}}\,.

Proof.

The proof is simple for
q=\tilde{q}
, so we assume
q<\tilde{q}
. We also only show the proof for
q,\tilde{q}<\infty
, the other extensions to the infinite cases are straightforward.

Our main tool is the Hölder inequality. Set
\theta=\tilde{q}/q
and
\tilde{\theta}
such that
1=\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\tilde{\theta}}
. That is,
\tilde{\theta}=\frac{\tilde{q}}{\tilde{q}-q}
. It then follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\alpha q}\Omega_{p}^{\chi}(t)^{q}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\leq\underbrace{\left(\int_{0}^{T}t^{[(\tilde{\alpha}-\alpha+\frac{1}{\tilde{q}})q-1]\tilde{\theta}}\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\theta}}}}_{=:c(\alpha,\tilde{\alpha},q,\tilde{q},T)}\left(\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\tilde{\alpha}\tilde{q}-1}\Omega_{p}^{\chi}(t)^{\tilde{q}}\text{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\,.

The claim then follows from

c(\alpha,\tilde{\alpha},q,\tilde{q},T)^{\tilde{\theta}}=\int_{0}^{T}t^{-1+(\tilde{\alpha}-\alpha)\frac{q\tilde{q}}{\tilde{q}-q}}\text{d}t<\infty\,,

since
(\tilde{\alpha}-\alpha)\frac{q\tilde{q}}{\tilde{q}-q}>0
.
∎

Lemma 2.5

(Dependence on p)
.

Let
\chi:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
0<\alpha
and
0<p,\tilde{p},q\leq\infty
with
p\leq\tilde{p}
. Then there exists

such that

\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\leq c(p,\tilde{p},T)\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{\tilde{p},q}}\,.

Proof.

We again only show the case
p,\tilde{p}<\infty
. Set
\theta=\tilde{p}/p\geq 1
and
\tilde{\theta}\geq 1
such that
1=\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\tilde{\theta}}
. Then the Hölder inequality gives us for all

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\chi(s,s+h)^{p}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}1\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\theta}p}}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\chi(s,s+h)^{\tilde{p}}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\tilde{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq T^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\theta}p}}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\chi(s,s+h)^{\tilde{p}}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\tilde{p}}}\,.

Putting this into the definition of
\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}
shows the claim.
∎

Our next objective is analyzing the dependence on

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\chi:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}

If
\chi
is subbadditive, it follows that there are constants

c(\alpha,q,p,T)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2^{n\alpha}\Omega_{p}^{\chi}(2^{-n}T))^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\leq C(\alpha,q,p,T)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2^{n\alpha}\Omega_{p}^{\chi}(2^{-n}T))^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

(2.5)

Proof.

If
\chi
is subadditive, we immediately have

\Omega_{p}^{\chi}(T)\leq 2\Omega_{p}^{\chi}(T/2)

(2.6)

if

We use

Lemma 2.7

(Dependence on
q
)
.

Let
\chi:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\left\|A\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\leq c(\alpha,p,q,\tilde{q},T)\left\|A\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,\tilde{q}}}

Proof.

The case

Before we show the embedding itself, we need some preliminary lemmas. The first one shows that it suffices to consider small

Proposition 2.11

(Dependence on

2.2
Variation norm and composition with Besov norms

For any

and an interval

V^{r}\chi_{I}:=\sup_{\pi}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\chi_{\pi_{i},\pi_{i+1}}^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\,,

where the supremum goes over all finite increasing sequences
\pi=\{\pi_{1}<\dots<\pi_{N}\}\subset I

Similar to the Besov-case, we set the variation norm of a one-parameter process

for a metric space
(X,d)

to be

V^{r}f_{I}:=V^{r}(d(f(\cdot),f(\cdot)))_{I}\,,\qquad V^{r}A_{I}:=V^{r}(\left|A_{\cdot,\cdot}\right|)_{I}\,.

If

, we write
V^{r}\chi:=V^{r}\chi_{[0,T]}
and if
V^{r}\chi<\infty
, we say
\chi\in V^{r}
. Let us recall the following embedding between Besov and variation spaces.

Proposition 2.15

(
[

]

For every
r\geq 1
and

, and this continuous function satisfies

V^{r}f\leq c(r,T)\left\|f\right\|_{B^{1/r}_{r,1}}\,.

\displaystyle(\text{substitute }\tau\mapsto 2^{l}\tau)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l\geq 0}2^{l(1/r-\alpha)}L^{q}_{\text{d}\tau/\tau,[0,2^{-l}T]}\tau^{-\alpha}\sup_{0\leq h\leq\tau}L_{\text{d}x,[0,T-h]}^{p}\chi(x,x+h)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\chi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,,

where
\lesssim
hides constants depending on
\alpha,r,p,q
.
∎

Remark 2.21
.

Theorem
1.1
follows from Theorem
2.20
applied with
\chi(s,t)=d(f(s),f(t))
.

3
Rough paths and nested norms

In this section, we introduce rough paths and controlled rough paths along with some basic properties. We then develop metric groups relevant to rough paths, which enables us to reduce several two-parameter processes to paths within these groups. This reduction directly leads to Theorem
1.2
as a consequence of Theorem
2.20
. Additionally, we present several Besov embeddings for rough paths.

3.1
Rough paths

Rough paths were introduced in
[
Lyo98
]
and controlled paths in
[
Gub04
]
. For a long time, the theory concentrated on Hölder continuous paths; a good exposition of this case is in the book
[
FH20
]
. The treatment of
V^{r}
paths is adapted from
[
FZ18
]
, for more details see
[
FV10
]
. For an introduction to Besov rough paths, see
[
FSZ22
]
.

Let
(E,\left|\cdot\right|)
be a normed space, and assume that the tensor product
E\otimes E
is equipped with a compatible norm, i.e. a norm which fulfills

\left|x\otimes y\right|=\left|x\right|\left|y\right|

for all
x,y\in E
. For example,
\mathbb{R}^{d},(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\otimes 2}\cong\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
equipped with the usual
p
-norm for any
p\geq 1
fulfill this. A level
2
rough path is then defined as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
r\in[2,3)
. An
r
-rough path consists of a map
(s,t)\mapsto\mathbf{X}_{s,t}:=(X_{s,t},\mathbb{X}_{s,t})\in E\oplus E^{\otimes 2}
, such that:

•

X
is of finite
r
-variation and
\mathbb{X}
is of finite
r/2
-variation.

•

We have for all
s\leq u\leq t
that

(1+X_{s,u}+\mathbb{X}_{s,u})\otimes(1+X_{u,t}+\mathbb{X}_{u,t})=1+X_{s,t}+\mathbb{X}_{s,t}\,,

(3.1)

where
\otimes
is understood as the truncated tensor product in
E\oplus E^{\otimes 2}
.

Writing out the second property in components gives the following:

\displaystyle X_{s,t}

\displaystyle=X_{s,u}+X_{u,t}

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{X}_{s,u}+\mathbb{X}_{u,t}+X_{s,u}\otimes X_{u,t}\,.

where the second identity is called Chen’s identity. Let us recall the definition of a controlled rough path:

Definition 3.2

(Controlled path)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
be an
r
-rough path in
E
and let
F
be some normed space.
An
\mathbf{X}
-controlled
r
-rough path

\mathbf{Y}=(Y,Y^{\prime})
consists of
Y:[0,T]\to F
,
Y^{\prime}:[0,T]\to L(E,F)
such that
Y^{\prime}\in V^{r}
and

R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}:=Y_{s,t}-Y^{\prime}_{s}X_{s,t}\in V^{r/2}.

Note that we do not require
Y\in V^{r}
, as this implicitly follows from the bounds on
Y^{\prime},R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}
. We further introduce the notion of a controlled
(r,\alpha)
-rough path, which consists of
Y:[0,T]\to F,Y^{\prime}:[0,T]\to L(E,F)
, such that

Y\in V^{r},\qquad Y^{\prime}\in V^{r/\alpha},\qquad R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\in V^{r/(\alpha+1)}\,.

This is motivated by the controlled rough path
\phi(\mathbf{Y}):=(\phi(Y),D\phi(Y)Y^{\prime})
for a
1+\alpha
-Hölder function
\phi
, see Lemma
6.1
for details. The case
\alpha=1
recovers classical controlled
r
-rough paths. We denote the supremum norm by

\left\|Y\right\|_{\sup}:=\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\left|Y(s)\right|\,.

If we have two rough paths
\mathbf{X},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
, we denote by

\Delta X:=X-\tilde{X}\,,

with similar notation extending to
\Delta\mathbb{X}
, as well as
\Delta Y,\Delta Y^{\prime},\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}
if we have an
\mathbf{X}
-controlled rough path
\mathbf{Y}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
-controlled rough path
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
.

Lemma 3.3

(Implicit bound)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be an
r
-rough path and
\mathbf{Y}
an
\mathbf{X}
-controlled path.
Then

V^{r}Y_{I}\leq\left\|Y^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}X_{I}+V^{r/2}(R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})_{I}.

(3.2)

for all intervals
I\subset[0,T]
. Furthermore, if we have another rough path
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
and controlled rough path
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
, we have the stability estimate

V^{r}(\Delta Y)_{I}\leq\left\|\Delta Y^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}X_{I}+\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}(\Delta X)_{I}+V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})_{I}.

(3.3)

Proof.

(
3.2
) follows directly from the definition of
R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}
. To see (
3.3
), one writes

Y_{s,t}-\tilde{Y}_{s,t}=(Y^{\prime}_{s}X_{s,t}+R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t})-(\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{s}\tilde{X}_{s,t}+R^{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}_{s,t})\\
=(Y^{\prime}_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s})X_{s,t}+\tilde{Y}_{s}(X_{s,t}-\tilde{X}_{s,t})+(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}).

∎

Example 3.4
.

For every rough path
(X,\mathbb{X})
, the path
Y_{t}:=\mathbb{X}_{0,t}
is an
\mathbf{X}
-controlled rough path with
Y^{\prime}_{t}=X_{t}
, as the following shows:

R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}=\mathbb{X}_{0,t}-\mathbb{X}_{0,s}-X_{0,s}\otimes X_{s,t}=\mathbb{X}_{s,t}\in V^{r/2}\,.

Note that we use the embedding
E
embedds into
L(E,E\otimes E)
via
x\mapsto(x\otimes\cdot)
.

3.2
Groups relevant to rough paths

One can equip the space
E\oplus E^{\otimes 2}
with a group product
(x,y)\mapsto x*y
, such that for every rough path
\mathbf{X}
we have

\mathbf{X}_{s,t}=\mathbf{X}_{0,s}^{-1}*\mathbf{X}_{0,t}\,,

turning
\mathbf{X}
into a one-parameter process. See e.g.
[
FH20
, Chapter 2]
. We want to construct similar groups to estimate the two-parameter processes
\Delta\mathbb{X}
and
\Delta R^{\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}}
for two rough paths
\mathbf{X},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
and controlled rough paths
\mathbf{Y},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
.

To this end, consider two linear spaces
X_{1},X_{2}
equipped with a linear map
B:X_{1}\times X_{1}\to X_{2}
. We claim that
X_{1}\times X_{2}
can be seen as a metric group:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2}
be vector spaces and
B:X_{1}\times X_{1}\to X_{2}
a bilinear map.
Then the set
X_{1}\times X_{2}
equipped with the product

(a,r)*_{B}(\tilde{a},\tilde{r})=(a+\tilde{a},r+\tilde{r}+B(a,\tilde{a}))

is a group. Suppose that
X_{1},X_{2}
are equipped with norms and that the bilinear map
B
is bounded with respect to these norms with
\left\|B\right\|\leq 2
, where
\left\|B\right\|
is the operator norm.
Then,

\left\|(a,r)\right\|=\max\left(\left\|a\right\|_{X_{1}},\left\|r\right\|_{X_{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(3.4)

is subadditive in the sense

\forall w,\tilde{w}\in X_{1}\times X_{2}\quad\left\|w*_{B}\tilde{w}\right\|\leq\left\|w\right\|+\left\|\tilde{w}\right\|.

Proof.

It is clear that
X_{1}\times X_{2}
is a group, so let us show the subadditivity of
\left\|\cdot\right\|
. Let
w=(a,r),\tilde{w}=(\tilde{a},\tilde{r})\in X_{1}\times X_{2}
. Observe that

\displaystyle\left\|r+\tilde{r}+B(a,\tilde{a})\right\|_{X_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|r\right\|_{X_{2}}+\left\|\tilde{r}\right\|_{X_{2}}+\left\|B(a,\tilde{a})\right\|_{X_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|w\right\|^{2}+\left\|\tilde{w}\right\|^{2}+2\left\|w\right\|\left\|\tilde{w}\right\|=(\left\|w\right\|+\left\|\tilde{w}\right\|)^{2}\,.

The claim easily follows.
∎

As stated above, this space can be equipped with a metric as follows.

Corollary 3.6
.

In the situation of Lemma
3.5
, the functional

\left\|w\right\|_{\mathrm{sym}}=\max(\left\|w\right\|,\left\|w^{-1}\right\|)

is subadditive and

d(w,\tilde{w}):=\left\|w*\tilde{w}^{-1}\right\|_{\mathrm{sym}}

is a left-invariant metric on
(X_{1}\times X_{2},*_{B})
.

Note that the construction of our norm is similar to
[
TZ20
, Section 2.2]
. See also
[
HS90
, Theorem 2]
for an alternative construction of homogeneous metrics. Since
(a,r)^{-1}=(-a,-r+B(a,a))
and

\left\|-r+B(a,a)\right\|_{X_{2}}\leq\left\|r\right\|_{X_{2}}+2\left\|a\right\|_{X_{2}}^{2}\leq 3\left\|(a,r)\right\|^{2},

we have

\left\|(a,r)\right\|\leq\left\|(a,r)\right\|_{\mathrm{sym}}\leq\sqrt{3}\left\|(a,r)\right\|.

Example 3.7
.

If we set
X_{1}=E
,
X_{2}=E^{\otimes 2}
with the bilinear map
B(x,y)=x\otimes y
, we get the typical group for rough paths, see also
[
FH20
, Section 2.3]
. This shows that
\mathbf{X}\in E\oplus E^{\otimes 2}
is indeed a one-parameter process in
G_{1}=E\oplus E^{\otimes 2}
. By setting
\left\|x\right\|_{X_{1}}=\left|x\right|
for all
x\in E
and
\left\|y\right\|_{X_{2}}=2\left|y\right|
for all
y\in E\otimes E
, we recover the subadditive norm

\left\|(X,\mathbb{X})\right\|=\max\left(\left|X\right|,\left(2\left|\mathbb{X}\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

constructed in
[
TZ20
, Section 2.2]
.

Example 3.8
.

Now consider two rough paths
\mathbf{X},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
over
E
. We claim that

F_{s,t}=(X_{s,t},\tilde{X}_{s,t},\Delta X_{s,t},\Delta\mathbb{X}_{s,t})
is a one-parameter process in the group

G_{2}=(E\oplus E\oplus E)\oplus E^{\otimes 2}\,.

To this end, recall that the
\delta
-operator of a two-parameter process
A:\Delta_{T}\to E
is defined by

\delta A_{s,u,t}=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}\,.

(3.5)

Direct calculation leads to

\delta(\Delta\mathbb{X})_{s,u,t}=\Delta X_{s,u}\otimes X_{u,t}+\tilde{X}_{s,u}\otimes\Delta X_{u,t}\,,

showing that
F_{s,u}*F_{u,t}=F_{s,t}
for the product

(x,\tilde{x},\bar{x},X)*(y,\tilde{y},\bar{y},Y)=(x+y,\tilde{x}+\tilde{y},\bar{x}+\bar{y},X+Y+\bar{x}\otimes y+\tilde{x}\otimes\bar{y})\,.

This fits our setting with

X_{1}=E\oplus E\oplus E,\qquad\hskip 20.0ptX_{2}=E^{\otimes 2},\qquad B((x,\tilde{x},\bar{x}),(y,\tilde{y},\bar{y}))=\bar{x}\otimes y+\tilde{x}\otimes\bar{y}\,.

It will be useful for the proof of Lemma
3.10
and Proposition
3.14
in Section
3.3
to incorporate two constants
N,N_{\Delta}>0
into our norms, so let us note here that for any
N,N_{\Delta}>0
, we can chose

\left\|(x,\tilde{x},\bar{x})\right\|_{X_{1}}=\max\left(\frac{\left|x\right|}{N},\frac{\left|\tilde{x}\right|}{N},\frac{\left|\bar{x}\right|}{N_{\Delta}}\right)\qquad\left\|X\right\|_{X_{2}}=\frac{\left|X\right|}{NN_{\Delta}}\,.

Example 3.9
.

Let us build a group for
\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}
. To this end, let
\mathbf{X},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
be rough paths and let
\mathbf{Y}
be a
\mathbf{X}
-controlled rough path and
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
a
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
-controlled rough path in
F
. We look at

Z_{s,t}=((\tilde{Y}_{s,t}^{\prime},\Delta Y_{s,t}^{\prime},X_{s,t},\Delta X_{s,t}),\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t})\,.

This lies in

G_{3}=(L(E,F)\oplus L(E,F)\oplus E\oplus E)\oplus F\,.

This can be turned into a group by using

X_{1}=(L(E,F)\oplus L(E,F)\oplus E\oplus E),\qquad X_{2}=F,\qquad B((a,b,c,d),(\tilde{a},\tilde{b},\tilde{c},\tilde{d}))=b\tilde{c}+a\tilde{d}\,.

One then uses the
\delta
-operator

\delta\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,u,t}=\Delta Y^{\prime}_{s,u}X_{u,t}+\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{s,u}\Delta X_{u,t}\,,

together with direct calculation to show
Z_{s,u}*Z_{u,t}=Z_{s,t}
for all
s\leq u\leq t
. Thus,
Z
is a one-parameter process in
G_{3}
. As in Example
3.8
, it will be useful for the proof of Proposition
3.12
to incorporate some constants
N_{a},N_{b},N_{c},N_{d}>0
into the norms. We set
N_{r}=N_{a}N_{d}+N_{b}N_{c}
and set

\left\|(a,b,c,d)\right\|_{X_{1}}=\max\left(\frac{\left|a\right|}{N_{a}},\frac{\left|b\right|}{N_{b}},\frac{\left|c\right|}{N_{c}},\frac{\left|d\right|}{N_{d}}\right),\qquad\left\|r\right\|_{X_{2}}=\frac{\left|r\right|}{N_{r}}\,.

Direct calculation gives
\left\|B\right\|\leq 2
.

3.3
Besov and variation estimates for rough paths

Let us now apply some of the estimates from Section
2
to the above examples. We start by applying Corollary
2.16
to Example
3.7
to show that Besov rough paths à la
[
FSZ22
]
, that is maps
\mathbf{X}:(s,t)\mapsto(X_{s,t},\mathbb{X}_{s,t})
fulfilling (
3.1
) as well as

\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}<\infty\,,\qquad\left\|\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}<\infty\,

for certain parameters
\alpha>0,p,q\in(0,\infty]
, are rough paths in the variation setting.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
r\in[2,3)
and
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
be an
r
-rough path. Assume that
\frac{1}{\alpha}<r\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
. Then there exists a
c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)>0
such that

\displaystyle V^{r}X

\displaystyle\leq c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}

\displaystyle\leq c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)(\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}^{2}+\left\|\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}})\,.

If
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
is another
r
-rough path, we have (possibly for another constant
c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)>0
)

\displaystyle V^{r}(\Delta X)

\displaystyle\leq c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle V^{r/2}(\Delta\mathbb{X})

\displaystyle\leq c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)\bigg((\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\tilde{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}})\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}\bigg)\,.

Proof.

V^{r}X\lesssim\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}
and
V^{r}(\Delta X)\lesssim\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}
are direct applications of Corollary
2.16
to
X,\Delta X
. By Example
3.7
,
\mathbf{X}
is a path in the metric space
G_{1}
with metric
d(\mathbf{X}_{s},\mathbf{X}_{t})=\left\|\mathbf{X}_{s,t}\right\|
and
\left\|(X_{s,t},\mathbb{X}_{s,t})\right\|\sim\left|X_{s,t}\right|+\left|\mathbb{X}_{s,t}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}
for all
s\leq t
. Applying Corollary
2.16
to
\mathbf{X}
gives us

\displaystyle(V^{r/2}\mathbb{X})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=V^{r}(\left|\mathbb{X}_{\cdot,\cdot}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}})\lesssim V^{r}(\mathbf{X})\lesssim\left\|\mathbf{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

For our last inequality, we consider the path
F_{s,t}=(X_{s,t},\tilde{X}_{s,t},\Delta X_{s,t},\Delta\mathbb{X}_{s,t})
in
G_{2}
from Example
3.8
.
Applying Corollary
2.16
to
F
gives for any
N,N_{\Delta}>0
:

\displaystyle V^{r/2}\left(\frac{\Delta\mathbb{X}}{NN_{\Delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq V^{r}F

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|F\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N}+\frac{\left\|\tilde{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N}+\frac{\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N_{\Delta}}+\left(\frac{\left\|\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}}{NN_{\Delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Choosing the constants

N:=\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\tilde{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}},\quad N_{\Delta}:=\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

gives

V^{r/2}\left(\frac{\Delta\mathbb{X}}{NN_{\Delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1+\left\|\frac{\Delta\mathbb{X}}{NN_{\Delta}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}\,.

Rearranging this gives

V^{r/2}(\Delta\mathbb{X})\lesssim NN_{\Delta}+\left\|\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}\,,

showing the claim by our choice of
NN_{\Delta}
.
∎

The same logic can be applied to controlled rough paths:

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathbf{Y}=(Y,Y^{\prime})
be an
\mathbf{X}
-controlled rough path,
r\in[2,3)
and assume that
\frac{1}{\alpha}<r\leq p\leq\infty,0<q\leq\infty
. Then there exists a
c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)>0
such that

\displaystyle V^{r}Y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)\left\|Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle V^{r/2}R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}

\displaystyle\leq c(r,\alpha,p,q,T)(\left\|Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}^{2}+\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}^{2}+\left\|R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}})\,.

We call
\mathbf{Y}
a Besov-controlled rough path with parameters
\alpha,p,q
, if

\left\|Y\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}<\infty\,,\qquad\left\|Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}<\infty\,,\qquad\left\|R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}<\infty\,.

Proof.

V^{r}Y^{\prime}\lesssim\left\|Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}
is again a simple application of Corollary
2.16
to
t\mapsto Y^{\prime}_{t}
. For the second inequality, we use the group
G_{3}
from Example
3.9
. By setting
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=0
, we simplify it to
(L(E,F)\oplus E)\oplus F
,

Z_{s,t}=(Y^{\prime}_{s,t},X_{s,t},R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t})\,.

We use the norm

\left\|(y,x,r)\right\|=\max\left(\left|y\right|,\left|x\right|,\left(\frac{1}{2}\left|r\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

on this space, which is the norm of
G_{3}
with constants
N_{a}=N_{b}=N_{c}=N_{d}=1
. Applying Corollary
2.16
to
Z
gives

(V^{r/2}R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim V^{r}(Z)\lesssim\left\|Z\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\|Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

∎

We now move on to the main result of this section, namely the application of Theorem
2.20
to rough paths. By applying this theorem to Example
3.9
, we get the following.

Proposition 3.12
.

Consider two
r
-rough paths
\mathbf{X},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
with controlled rough paths
\mathbf{Y},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
for
r\in[2,3)
. Assume
0<\frac{1}{\alpha}<r\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
. Then:

\left\|V^{r/2}\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}\lesssim\left\|\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}+\left\|\Delta Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,.

Proof.

By Exampel
3.9
,
Z_{s,t}=((\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{s,t},\Delta Y^{\prime}_{s,t},X_{s,t},\Delta X_{s,t}),\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t})\in G_{3}
is a path in a metric space. Thus, Theorem
2.20
gives us

\left\|V^{r}Z\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\|Z\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,.

(3.6)

We chose the following constants for the norm of
G_{3}
:

\displaystyle N_{a}

\displaystyle=\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle N_{b}

\displaystyle=\left\|\Delta Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle N_{a}

\displaystyle=\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle N_{a}

\displaystyle=\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,.

Recall that
N_{r}=N_{a}N_{d}+N_{b}N_{c}
. By the definition of
\left\|\cdot\right\|
in
G_{3}
and (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|V^{r}(\left|\frac{R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}}{N_{r}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}})\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N_{a}}+\frac{\left\|\Delta Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N_{b}}+\frac{\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N_{c}}+\frac{\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N_{d}}+\left\|\left|\frac{R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}}{N_{r}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\left\|\left|\frac{R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}}{N_{r}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,.

Thus, we conclude

\left\|V^{r/2}R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}\lesssim N_{r}+\left\|R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}\,.

The claim then follows from
N_{r}=\left\|\Delta Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}
.
∎

From this result, one immediately gets estimates for the nested norms of
\mathbb{X},R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}
as well as
\Delta\mathbb{X}
.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
\mathbf{X},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
be
r
-rough paths and
\mathbf{Y},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
be controlled
r
-rough paths. Let
r\in[2,3)
and assume
0<\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
.
Then:

\displaystyle\left\|V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}+\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}^{2}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\|V^{r/2}R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}+\left\|Y^{\prime}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}+\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}(\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\tilde{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}})

(3.9)

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.12
using that
\mathbb{X}
is an
\mathbf{X}
-controlled rough path with
\mathbb{X}_{s,t}=R^{\mathbb{X},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}
(see Example
3.4
), and setting
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}=0
whenever necessary.
∎

We conclude this section by showing a technical inequality necessary for Theorem
7.5
. In the proof of said proposition, we are in the situation that we need to estimate
V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}
in the
B^{\alpha}_{p,q}
-norm instead of the
B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}
-norm, as the scaling of
\Delta\mathbb{X}
would imply. Thanks to Example
3.8
, this can easily be done with classical Besov embeddings.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\mathbf{X},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
be
r
-rough paths for
r\in[2,3)
and assume that
0<\frac{1}{\alpha}<r\leq p\leq\infty
and
1\leq p\leq\infty,\frac{1}{2}\leq q\leq\infty
. Then:

\left\|V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim(\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\tilde{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}})\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}

(3.10)

Proof.

The proof works similarly to the one of Lemma
3.10
. We consider the process
F_{s,t}=(X_{s,t},\tilde{X}_{s,t},\Delta X_{s,t},\Delta\mathbb{X}_{s,t})
in
G_{2}
from Example
3.8
. Note that
V^{r}F
is monotone in the sense of (
2.7
) and subadditive in the sense of (
2.2
). Thus, we can use the Besov embedding given by Proposition
2.11
to see

\left\|V^{r}F\right\|_{B^{\alpha/2}_{2p,2q}}\lesssim\left\|V^{r}F\right\|_{B^{\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+1/p)}_{p,2q}}\,.

Using
\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+1/p)\leq\alpha
as well as Proposition
2.7
and
2.4
, we see that

\left\|V^{r}F\right\|_{B^{\alpha/2}_{2p,2q}}\lesssim\left\|V^{r}F\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,.

We then have for any
N,N_{\Delta}>0

\displaystyle\left\|V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}/(NN_{\Delta})\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V^{r}F\right\|_{B^{\alpha/2}_{2p,2q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|V^{r}F\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left\|V^{r}X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N}+\frac{\left\|V^{r}\tilde{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N}+\frac{\left\|V^{r}\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N_{\Delta}}+\left(\frac{\left\|V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}}{NN_{\Delta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

We chose the constants

N:=\left\|X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}+\left\|\tilde{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}},\quad N_{\Delta}:=\left\|\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\,.

By this choice and Corollary
3.13
, we have

\frac{\left\|V^{r}X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N}+\frac{\left\|V^{r}\tilde{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N}+\frac{\left\|V^{r}\Delta X\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}}{N_{\Delta}}\lesssim 1\,,

implying

\left\|V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}\lesssim NN_{\Delta}+\left\|V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}\,.

(3.11)

Applying Corollary
3.13
one more time to estimate
\left\|V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}\right\|_{B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}}
shows the claim.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

Note that the right-hand side of (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) are the same. By (
3.11
), one can replace the
B^{2\alpha}_{p/2,q/2}
-norm of
V^{r/2}\Delta\mathbb{X}
with
B^{\alpha}_{p,q}
at the cost of adding

, which gets absorbed in the constant in

in (

).

4
Sewing Lemma

The sewing lemma was originally introduced by Feyel and de La Pradelle
[
FL06
,
FDM08
]
as well as Gubinelli
[
Gub04
]
.
It gives a criterion for convergence of Riemann sums and generalizes earlier work by Young
[
You36
]
.
It is widely used in rough paths theory
[
Lyo98
]
to construct and estimate rough integrals.

In this section, we extend this lemma to the case of
p
-metrics.
This allows one to recover the Besov sewing lemma formulated in
[
FSZ22
]
as a simple corollary of the variation sewing lemma.
We show this fact in Theorem
4.7
.
We also recover a version of the sewing lemma from
[
FZ18
]
, which we present in Theorem
4.5
.

4.1
Sewing in the
r
-variation setting

Let
A:\Delta_{T}\to E
be a two-parameter process into some Banach space
E
. For any
r>0
, we define the
r
-variation of its
\delta
-operator defined in (
3.5
) by

V^{r}\delta A:=\sup_{\pi}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-2}\left|\delta A_{\pi_{j},\pi_{j+1},\pi_{j+2}}\right|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\,,

where the supremum runs over all partitions
\pi=\{0=\pi_{1}<\dots<\pi_{k}=T\}
of
[0,T]
. Note that we explicitly allow
r<1
. Given a partition
\pi
, we consider the Riemann sum

\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}A_{\pi_{j},\pi_{j+1}}\,.

We present a sewing lemma with a short proof. As noted already by Young in
[
You36
]
, any
2
-parameter process whose
\delta
-operator has finite
r
-variation for
r<1
satisfies a bound on its Riemann sums. Before we show this, let us recall the definition of a
p
-metric.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(E,+)
be a commutative group and
p\in(0,1]
. Then we call a map
\left|\cdot\right|:E\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
a
p
-norm, if for all
x,y\in E

\displaystyle\left|x\right|

\displaystyle=0\qquad\Leftrightarrow\quad x=0

\displaystyle\left|x+y\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left|x\right|^{p}+\left|y\right|^{p}\,.

By the Aoki–Rolewicz theorem, see e.g.
[
KPR84
, Theorem 1.2]
, any quasinorm is equivalent to a
p
-norm for some
p\in(0,1]
. We set for any
\theta>0

\zeta(\theta):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\theta}\,.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.2

(Sewing, cf.
[
You36
, §5]
)
.

Let
0<r<p\leq 1
.
Let
E
be a commutative group equipped with a
p
-norm
\left|\cdot\right|
.
Let
A:\Delta_{T}\to E
with

V^{r}\delta A<\infty.

Then, for any partition
\pi
of
[0,T]
, we have

\left|\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}-A_{0,T}\right|\leq\zeta(p/r)^{1/p}V^{r}\delta A\,.

Proof.

By induction on the partition size
k\geq 2
, we will show that, for every partition
0=\pi_{1}<\dotsb<\pi_{k}=T
, we have

\left|A_{0,T}-\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}\right|^{p}\leq\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{-p/r}\bigr)(V^{r}\delta A)^{p},

For
k=2
, we have
\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}=A_{0,T}
, which serves as the induction base.

Suppose that the claim is known for all partitions of size
k
and let
0=\pi_{0}<\dotsb<\pi_{k}=T
be a partition of size
k+1
.
Since

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left|\delta A_{\pi_{j},\pi_{j+1},\pi_{j+2}}\right|^{r}\leq(V^{r}\delta A)^{r},

there exists
j
such that

\left|\delta A_{\pi_{j},\pi_{j+1},\pi_{j+2}}\right|\leq k^{-1/r}V^{r}\delta A.

Let
\pi^{\prime}:=\pi\setminus\left\{\pi_{j+1}\right\}
, so that

\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}-\mathcal{I}^{\pi^{\prime}}A_{0,T}=\delta A_{\pi_{j},\pi_{j+1},\pi_{j+2}}.

Then, by the inductive hypothesis,

\displaystyle\left|A_{0,T}-\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left|A_{0,T}-\mathcal{I}^{\pi^{\prime}}A_{0,T}\right|^{p}+\left|\mathcal{I}^{\pi^{\prime}}A_{0,T}-\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}j^{-p/r}\bigr)(V^{r}\delta A)^{p}+k^{-p/r}(V^{r}\delta A)^{p}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{-p/r}\bigr)(V^{r}\delta A)^{p}.

This finishes the inductive step.
∎

Recall that partitions
\pi
of
[0,T]
are a directed set with the preorder given by refinements. Thus,
\pi\mapsto\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}
is a net. We denote the net limit over partitions (also called RRS limit in
[
FZ18
]
) with
\lim_{\pi}
. One can show the following sewing result for net limits:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<r<p\leq 1
and let
E
be a complete
p
-metric group.
Let
A:\Delta_{T}\to E
be such that
V^{r}\delta A<\infty
and

\lim_{\pi}\sup_{j,\pi_{j}\leq s<t<u\leq\pi_{j+1}}\left|\delta A_{s,t,u}\right|=0.

(4.2)

Then, the following net limit exists,

\mathcal{I}A_{0,T}:=\lim_{\pi}\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T},

and one has the estimate

\left|\mathcal{I}A_{0,T}-A_{0,T}\right|\leq\zeta(p/r)^{1/p}V^{r}\delta A.

Remark 4.4
.

For the Sections
5
and
6
, it suffices to show Lemma
4.3
for
p=1
, i.e. for metric groups. The extension to
p
-metric groups allows us to recover
[
FSZ22
, Theorem 3.1]
, which we show in Theorem
4.7
.

Proof.

Let
r<r^{\prime}<p
.
For partitions
\pi^{\prime}\subseteq\pi
, by Lemma
4.2
with
r^{\prime}
in place of
r
, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}-\mathcal{I}^{\pi^{\prime}}A_{0,T}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\left|\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{\pi^{\prime}_{j},\pi^{\prime}_{j+1}}-A_{\pi^{\prime}_{j},\pi^{\prime}_{j+1}}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\zeta(p/r^{\prime})\sum_{j}(V^{r^{\prime}}_{\pi^{\prime}_{j},\pi^{\prime}_{j+1}}\delta A)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\zeta(p/r^{\prime})\Bigl(\sup_{j,\pi^{\prime}_{j}\leq s<t<u\leq\pi^{\prime}_{j+1}}\left|\delta A_{s,t,u}\right|^{p(1-r/r^{\prime})}\Bigr)\sum_{j}(V^{r}_{\pi^{\prime}_{j},\pi^{\prime}_{j+1}}\delta A)^{pr/r^{\prime}}.

The supremum converges to
0
by the hypothesis (
4.2
), and

\sum_{j}(V^{r}_{\pi^{\prime}_{j},\pi^{\prime}_{j+1}}\delta A)^{pr/r^{\prime}}\leq\bigl(\sum_{j}(V^{r}_{\pi^{\prime}_{j},\pi^{\prime}_{j+1}}\delta A)^{r}\bigr)^{p/r^{\prime}}\leq\bigl((V^{r}\delta A)^{r}\bigr)^{p/r^{\prime}}

is bounded.
This shows that
\pi\to\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T}
is a Cauchy net, so it convergence by the completeness hypothesis.
Finally, the estimate follows from a separate application of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

We finally introduce the sewing lemma we use throughout Sections
5
and
6
.
Recall that a
control
is a function
\omega:\Delta_{T}\to[0,\infty)

that is
super-additive
in the sense
\omega(s,t)+\omega(t,u)\leq\omega(s,u)
for all
s\leq t\leq u
(setting
s=t=u
, this implies in particular that
\omega(s,s)=0
).

Theorem 4.5

(Sewing, cf.
[
FZ18
, Theorem 2.5]
)
.

Let
N\geq 0
,
\theta>1
, and
\alpha_{1,n},\alpha_{2,n}\geq 0
with
\alpha_{1,n}+\alpha_{2,n}\geq\theta
for all
n\in\left\{1,\dotsc,N\right\}
.
Let
\omega_{1,n},\omega_{2,n}
be controls and
A:\Delta_{T}\to E
with a Banach space
E
.
Assume

\left|\delta A_{s,u,t}\right|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\omega_{1,n}^{\alpha_{1,n}}(s,u)\omega_{2,n}^{\alpha_{2,n}}(u,t).

(4.3)

Then the following net limit exists,

\mathcal{I}A_{0,T}:=\lim_{\pi}\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A_{0,T},

and one has the estimate

\left|\mathcal{I}A_{0,T}-A_{0,T}\right|\leq\zeta(\theta)\Bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\omega_{1,n}^{\alpha_{1,n}/\theta}(0,T-)\omega_{2,n}^{\alpha_{2,n}/\theta}(0+,T)\Bigr)^{\theta},

(4.4)

where
\zeta(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\theta}
.

Our proof saves a factor
2^{\theta}
on the right-hand side of (
4.4
) compared to
[
FZ18
]
.

Proof.

For any partition
\pi
, by hypothesis (
4.3
), Hölder’s inequality, monotonicity of
\ell^{q}
norms, and superadditivity of controls, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\left|\delta A_{\pi_{k},\pi_{k+1},\pi_{k+2}}\right|^{1/\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\Bigl(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\omega_{1,n}^{\alpha_{1,n}}(\pi_{k},\pi_{k+1})\omega_{2,n}^{\alpha_{2,n}}(\pi_{k+1},\pi_{k+2})\Bigr)^{1/\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bigl(\omega_{1,n}^{\alpha_{1,n}}(\pi_{k},\pi_{k+1})\omega_{2,n}^{\alpha_{2,n}}(\pi_{k+1},\pi_{k+2})\bigr)^{1/\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\omega_{1,n}^{\alpha_{1,n}+\alpha_{2,n}}(\pi_{k},\pi_{k+1})\Bigr)^{\frac{\alpha_{1,n}}{\alpha_{1,n}+\alpha_{2,n}}/\theta}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\omega_{2,n}^{\alpha_{1,n}+\alpha_{2,n}}(\pi_{k+1},\pi_{k+2})\Bigr)^{\frac{\alpha_{2,n}}{\alpha_{1,n}+\alpha_{2,n}}/\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\omega_{1,n}(\pi_{k},\pi_{k+1})\Bigr)^{\alpha_{1,n}/\theta}\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{J-1}\omega_{2,n}(\pi_{k+1},\pi_{k+2})\Bigr)^{\alpha_{2,n}/\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\omega_{1,n}(\pi_{0},\pi_{J})^{\alpha_{1,n}/\theta}\omega_{2,n}(\pi_{1},\pi_{J+1})^{\alpha_{2,n}/\theta}.

Moreover, (
4.3
) implies (
4.2
) by
[
FZ18
, Lemma 2.1]
.
The claim follows from Lemma
4.3
with
r=1/\theta
.
∎

4.2
Connection to Besov sewing

In this section, we want to show that
r
-variation norms can be used to quickly recover the Besov sewing lemma from
[
FSZ22
]
. To do so, we recall the Besov norm of a three-parameter process:

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\delta A:\{(s,u,t)\in[0,T]\penalty\ |\penalty\ s\leq u\leq t\}\to E
for some normed space
E
,
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
\alpha>0
. We then set

\displaystyle\bar{\Omega}_{p}^{\delta A}(t):=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq u\leq v\leq t}\left(\int_{0}^{T-v}\left|\delta A_{s,s+u,s+v}\right|^{p}\text{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\left\|\delta A\right\|_{\bar{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q}}:=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\bar{\Omega}_{p}^{\delta A}(t)}{t^{\alpha}}\right)^{q}\frac{\text{d}t}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,

with the usual extension to
p=\infty
and
q=\infty
.

We can then show the main statement of
[
FSZ22
]
, Theorem 3.1:

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
A:\Delta_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and let
0<p,q\leq\infty,\gamma>\max(1,1/p,1/q)
.
Assume that

\left\|A\right\|_{B^{\gamma}_{p,q}},\left\|\delta A\right\|_{\bar{B}^{\gamma}_{p,q}}<\infty\,.

Then the sewing
\mathcal{I}A=\lim_{\pi}\mathcal{I}^{\pi}A
exists and fulfills for some
c(\gamma,p,q,T)>0

\left\|A-\cal IA\right\|_{B^{\gamma}_{p,q}}\leq c(\gamma,p,q,T)\left\|\delta A\right\|_{\bar{B}^{\gamma}_{p,q}}\,.

Proof.

The key technique of the proof is to define for any
0\leq a\leq b\leq T
the
2
-parameter object

\Xi_{s,t}(a,b):=A_{a+s(b-a),a+t(b-a)}\,,

such that
\Xi(a,b):\Delta_{1}\to B^{\gamma}_{p,q}=:E
. Note that the Besov-norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}
is a
\min(1,p,q)
-norm, so we can apply Lemma
4.3
to
\Xi
. By construction, it holds for any partition
\pi=\{0<\pi_{1}<\dots<\pi_{k}=1\}
of
[0,1]
, that
\Xi_{0,1}(a,b)=A_{a,b}
and

\mathcal{I}^{\pi}\Xi(a,b)=\mathcal{I}^{\pi^{\prime}(a,b)}A\,,

where
\pi^{\prime}(a,b)=\{a+\pi_{j}(b-a)\penalty\ |\penalty\ \pi_{j}\in\pi\}
is the corresponding partition of
(a,b)
. Thus,
\mathcal{I}A
exists if and only if
\mathcal{I}\Xi
exists and we have

\mathcal{I}\Xi_{s,t}(a,b)=\mathcal{I}A_{a+s(b-a),a+t(b-s)}\,.

It holds that

\displaystyle\delta\Xi_{s,t,u}(a,b)

\displaystyle=A_{a+s(b-a),a+u(b-a)}-A_{a+s(b-a),a+t(b-a)}-A_{a+t(b-a),a+u(b-a)}

\displaystyle=\delta A_{a+s(b-a),a+t(b-a),a+u(b-a)}\,.

Hence, we have the following for all
s\leq t\leq u
.

\displaystyle\Omega_{p}^{\delta\Xi_{s,t,u}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq h\leq\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left|\delta\Xi_{s,t,u}(r,r+h)\right|^{p}\text{d}r\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq h\leq\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{T-h}\left|\delta A_{r+sh,r+th,r+uh}\right|^{p}\text{d}r\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\bar{\Omega}_{p}^{\delta A}(\tau(u-s)).

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|\delta\Xi_{s,t,u}\right\|_{E}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\tau^{-\gamma}\Omega_{p}^{\delta\Xi_{s,t,u}}(\tau)\right)^{q}\frac{\text{d}\tau}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\tau^{-\gamma}\bar{\Omega}_{p}^{\delta A}(\tau(u-s))\right)^{q}\frac{\text{d}\tau}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq(u-s)^{\gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\tau^{-\gamma}\bar{\Omega}_{p}^{\delta A}(\tau)\right)^{q}\frac{\text{d}\tau}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=(u-s)^{\gamma}\left\|\delta A\right\|_{\bar{B}^{\gamma}_{p,q}}\,.

This especially shows (
4.2
). It further follows that

\displaystyle V^{r}\delta\Xi

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\pi}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-2}\left|\pi_{j+2}-\pi_{j}\right|^{\gamma r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left\|\delta A\right\|_{\bar{B}^{\gamma}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\delta A\right\|_{\bar{B}^{\gamma}_{p,q}}<\infty

provided that
r\gamma\geq 1
. By choosing
r=\frac{1}{\gamma}<\max(p,1)
, we can thus apply Lemma
4.3
to get the existence of
\mathcal{I}\Xi
(and thus
\mathcal{I}A
) as well as

\left\|A-\mathcal{I}A\right\|_{B^{\gamma}_{p,q}}=\left\|\mathcal{I}\Xi_{0,1}-\Xi_{0,1}\right\|_{B^{\gamma}_{p,q}}\lesssim\left\|\delta A\right\|_{\bar{B}^{\gamma}_{p,q}}\,.

∎

5
Lipschitz estimates for Young differential equations

Consider the Young equation

Y_{t}=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(Y_{s})\text{d}X_{s}

(5.1)

where
Y_{0}=y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the initial condition,
\phi
is a given, smooth enough function and we assume that the driving noise
X\in V^{r}
for some
r\in[1,2)
.
\int_{0}^{t}\phi(Y_{s})dX_{s}
is given by the Young integral originally introduced in
[
You36
]
. The goal of this section is to establish the Lispchitz dependence of the solution
Y
on the data
X,y_{0}
and
\phi
. To this end, we consider a second solution

\tilde{Y}_{t}=\tilde{y}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y}_{s})\text{d}\tilde{X}_{s}

for some data
\tilde{X},\tilde{y}_{0},\tilde{\phi}
and show an estimate of the form

V^{r}\Delta Y_{s,t}\leq c_{1}(s,t)(\left|\Delta y_{0}\right|+V^{r}\Delta X_{0,T}+\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}+\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{\sup})+c_{2}(s,t)V^{r}\Delta X_{s,t}\,.

(5.2)

Here,
\Delta X=X-\tilde{X}
with similar notation extending to
\Delta Y,\Delta y_{0}
and
\Delta\phi
. Estimates of the form (
5.2
) are by no means new, see for example
[
Gal23
,
Lej10
]
. We improve on these by giving precise forms of
c_{1}(s,t),c_{2}(s,t)
for
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
. This is necessary to estimate the Besov-norm of
(s,t)\mapsto V^{r}\Delta Y_{s,t}
, as the Besov-norm of the right-hand side of (
5.2
) might explode otherwise.

We derive our estimate by constructing
Y
in a solution space that dynamically depends on
X
. That is, we consider the solution space

\mathcal{Y}(X)=\{Y\penalty\ |\penalty\ V^{r}Y_{I}\leq 2\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{I}\text{ for all intervals }I\subset[0,T]\}\,.

(5.3)

for a constant
\Phi_{0}>0
such that
\left\|\phi\right\|_{\sup}\leq\Phi_{0}
. This allows us to easily get bounds on
V^{r}Y_{I}
for any
I\subset[0,T]
.

In this section, we show an a priori estimate in Proposition
5.4
, local existence and uniqueness (Proposition
5.7
) of the solution of (
5.1
), and finally the desired Lipschitz estimate in Theorem
5.9
.

Let us start by fixing some notation. We fix an
r\in[1,2)
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
with
r<1+\alpha
and set
\theta=(1+\alpha)/r
. Recall that
C^{\alpha}
denotes the space of Hölder functions with seminorm

\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}=\sup_{s<t}\frac{\left|\phi(t)-\phi(s)\right|}{\left|t-s\right|^{\alpha}}\,,

(5.4)

where we make the usual extension that for all
k\in\mathbb{N},\alpha\in(0,1]
,
C^{k,\alpha}_{b}
denotes the space of
k
-times bounded, differentiable functions with bounded derivatives and
D^{k}\phi\in C^{\alpha}
. This space is equipped with the usual norm

\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{k,\alpha}_{b}}:=\sum_{n\leq k}\left\|D^{n}\phi\right\|_{\sup}+\left\|D^{k}\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}\,.

Throughout this section, we assume
X:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n},X\in V^{r}
and
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}_{b}
. We further assume
Y:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that is
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to L(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We allow jumps, as in
[
FZ18
]
.
All estimates are of Davie form
[
Dav08
]
(see also
[
FV10
, Chapter 10]
).

5.1
A
4
-point Taylor formula with fractional reminder

Before we solve (
5.1
), we give the following 4-point Taylor formula as a preliminary result. Similar formulas can be found in
[
BHR23
, Lemma 15]
and
[
FV10
, Lemma 10.22]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,1]
, every
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}_{b}
, every
\tilde{\phi}\in C^{0,\alpha}_{b}
, and any
Y_{s},Y_{t},\tilde{Y}_{s},\tilde{Y}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\left|\Delta\Bigl(\phi(Y_{t})-\phi(Y_{s})\Bigr)\right|\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}\left|\Delta Y_{s,t}\right|+\frac{\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}}{1+\alpha}(\left|Y_{s,t}\right|^{\alpha}+\left|\tilde{Y}_{s,t}\right|^{\alpha})\left|\Delta Y_{s}\right|+\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}\left|\tilde{Y}_{s,t}\right|^{\alpha}.

(5.5)

Proof.

We split

\displaystyle\left|\Delta\Bigl(\phi(Y_{t})-\phi(Y_{s})\Bigr)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|(\phi(Y_{t})-\phi(Y_{s}))-(\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t})-\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}))\right|

(5.6)

\displaystyle+\left|(\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t})-\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}))-(\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y}_{t})-\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y}_{s}))\right|.

(5.7)

Note that (
5.7
) is easily bound by
\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}\left|\tilde{Y}_{s,t}\right|^{\alpha}
. To estimate (
5.6
), we use the fundamental theorem of calculus to get

\displaystyle\left|(\phi(Y_{t})-\phi(Y_{s}))-(\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t})-\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}))\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\Delta Y_{t}\int_{0}^{1}D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t}+u\Delta Y_{t})\text{d}u+\Delta Y_{s}\int_{0}^{1}D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}+u\Delta Y_{s})\text{d}u\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\Delta Y_{s,t}\right|\int_{0}^{1}\left|D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t}+u\Delta Y_{t})\right|du

(5.8)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\Delta Y_{s}\right|\int_{0}^{1}\left|D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t}+u\Delta Y_{t})-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}+u\Delta Y_{s})\right|du

(5.9)

(
5.8
) is bounded by
\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}\left|\Delta Y_{s,t}\right|
.
The difference inside the integral of (
5.9
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}\left|(\tilde{Y}_{t}+u\Delta Y_{t})-(\tilde{Y}_{s}+u\Delta Y_{s})\right|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}(\left|uY_{s,t}\right|+(1-u)\left|\tilde{Y}_{s,t}\right|)^{\alpha}.

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}(\left|u\right|^{\alpha}\left|Y_{s,t}\right|^{\alpha}+(1-u)^{\alpha}\left|\tilde{Y}_{s,t}\right|^{\alpha}).

Integrating this estimate in
u
, we obtain the claimed estimate for (
5.6
).
∎

5.2
A priori estimate

We show an a priori estimate for the solution of (
5.1
). This is based on the following estimate for Young integrals.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\geq 1
with
\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{1}{r_{2}}=:\theta>1
. Let
Y\in V^{r_{1}},X\in V^{r_{2}}
.
Then, the Young integral
Z_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}Y\text{d}X,t\in[0,T]
exists and satisfies

\left|Z_{s,t}-Y_{s}X_{s,t}\right|\leq\zeta(\theta)V^{r_{1}}Y_{[s,t)}V^{r_{2}}X_{(s,t]}\,,

(5.10)

where
\zeta(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\theta}
.

Proof.

Recall that the Young integral
Z_{t}
is given as the sewing of the germ

(s,t)\mapsto A_{s,t}=Y_{s}X_{s,t}\,.

With

\left|\delta A_{s,u,t}\right|=\left|Y_{s,u}\right|\left|X_{s,u}\right|\leq V^{r_{1}}Y_{[s,u]}V^{r_{2}}X_{[u,t]}\,.

Since
\theta>1
, the sewing Lemma (Theorem
4.5
) immediately gives us that
Z
exists and (
5.10
).
∎

We are especially interested in the case in which
Y
is composed with a Hölder continuous function
\phi
:

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
r\in[1,2)
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
with
r<1+\alpha
. Let
\theta=(1+\alpha)/r
.
Let
X,Y\in V^{r}
and
\phi\in C^{\alpha}
.
Then, the Young integral
Z_{T}:=\int_{0}^{T}\phi(Y)\text{d}X
exists and satisfies

\left|Z_{s,t}-\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t}\right|\leq\zeta(\theta)\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}V^{r}Y_{[s,t)}^{\alpha}V^{r}X_{(s,t]}\,,

(5.11)

where
\zeta(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\theta}
.

Proof.

For any path
Y
and interval
I\subset[0,T]
, it is easy to see that

V^{r/\alpha}(\phi(Y))_{I}\leq\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}(V^{r}Y_{I})^{\alpha}.

The claim is then a direct consequence of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Proposition 5.4

(A priori estimate)
.

Let
r,\alpha
be as above.
Let
\phi\in C^{0,\alpha}_{b}
with two constants
\Phi_{0},\Phi_{\alpha}>0
, such that

\left\|\phi\right\|_{\sup}\leq\Phi_{0},\quad\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq\Phi_{\alpha}.

(5.12)

For any
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, let
X,Y\in V^{r}_{[s,t]}
with

Y_{u}=Y_{s}+\int_{s}^{u}\phi(Y_{z})\text{d}X_{z},\quad\text{for all }u\in[s,t].

Then, assuming that there is an
\epsilon>0
with

V^{r}X_{(s,t]}\leq\epsilon,\quad 2^{\alpha}\zeta(\theta)\Phi_{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\leq\Phi_{0}^{1-\alpha},

(5.13)

we have

V^{r}Y_{[s,t]}\leq 2\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{[s,t]}.

(5.14)

Proof.

We write
\phi(Y)\delta X
for the
2
-parameter proccess
(s,t)\mapsto\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t}
. By Corollary
5.3
, we have

V^{r/(1+\alpha)}(Y-\phi(Y)\delta X)_{[s,t]}\leq\zeta(\theta)\Phi_{\alpha}V^{r}Y_{[s,t)}^{\alpha}V^{r}X_{(s,t]}.

(5.15)

By (
5.15
), the hypothesis (
5.13
), and the AMGM inequality, we find that

\displaystyle V^{r/(1+\alpha)}(Y-\phi(Y)\delta X)_{[s,t]}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\alpha}V^{r}Y_{[s,t)}^{\alpha}(\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{(s,t]})^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha 2^{-1}V^{r}Y_{[s,t)}+(1-\alpha)\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{(s,t]}.

Hence,

\displaystyle V^{r}Y_{[s,t]}

\displaystyle\leq V^{r/(1+\alpha)}(Y-\phi(Y)\delta X)_{[s,t]}+V^{r}(\phi(Y)\delta X)_{[s,t]}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha 2^{-1}V^{r}Y_{[s,t)}+(1-\alpha)\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{(s,t]}+\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{[s,t]},

so that

(1-\alpha/2)V^{r}Y_{[s,t]}\leq(2-\alpha)\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{[s,t]}.

This shows (
5.14
).
∎

Lemma 5.5

(Solution space)
.

Let
\phi\in C^{0,\alpha}_{b},\Phi_{0},\Phi_{\alpha}>0
be such that (
5.12
) holds and
X\in V^{r}
such that (
5.13
) holds for some
\epsilon>0
.
Recall our solution space

\mathcal{Y}(X):=\left\{Y\penalty\ |\penalty\ \forall I\quad V^{r}Y_{I}\leq 2\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{I}\right\}.

(5.16)

Then, every solution to the Young ODE (
5.1
) lies in
\mathcal{Y}(X)
, and
\mathcal{Y}(X)
is invariant under

Y\mapsto\Bigl(y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(Y)\text{d}X\Bigr)_{t}.

(5.17)

Proof.

Proposition
5.4
shows that every solution lies in
\mathcal{Y}(X)
.
By Corollary
5.3
and the hypothesis (
5.13
), for every
Y\in\mathcal{Y}(X)
and any
s\leq t
, we have

\left|\int_{s}^{t}\phi(Y)\text{d}X-\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t}\right|\leq 2^{\alpha}\zeta(\theta)\Phi_{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}V^{r}X_{[s,t)}^{\alpha}V^{r}X_{(s,t]}\leq\Phi_{0}V^{r}X_{[s,t]}.

This implies that (
5.17
) maps
\mathcal{Y}(X)
to
\mathcal{Y}(X)
.
∎

5.3
Convergence of Picard iteration

Before we show that the map (
5.17
) is a contraction, we need the following estimate:

Lemma 5.6

(Stability of composition)
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Suppose
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}_{b}
and
\tilde{\phi}\in C^{0,\alpha}_{b}
.
Let
Y,\tilde{Y}\in V^{r}
be paths.
Then we have for any interval
I\subset[0,T]
:

V^{r/\alpha}(\phi(Y)-\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y}))_{I}\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}V^{r/\alpha}\Delta Y_{I}+\frac{\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}}{1+\alpha}(V^{r}Y^{\alpha}_{I}+V^{r}\tilde{Y}^{\alpha}_{I})\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}+\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}V^{r}\tilde{Y}^{\alpha}_{I}.

(5.18)

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of applying
V^{r/\alpha}
to both sides of the 4 point Taylor formula (
5.5
).
∎

Proposition 5.7

(Contractivity of Picard iteration)
.

Let
\Phi_{\alpha},\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},\Phi_{1,\alpha}>0
.
Let
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}_{b}
with (
5.12
) and

\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}\leq\Phi_{1},\quad\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq\Phi_{1,\alpha}.

(5.19)

Let
\epsilon>0
with (
5.13
) and

\zeta(\theta)\Phi_{1}\epsilon\leq 1/8,\quad\zeta(\theta)2^{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\epsilon^{1+\alpha}\leq 1/8.

(5.20)

Then, for any
X\in V^{r}
with
V^{r}X_{[0,T]}<\epsilon
and any
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the map (
5.17
) is a strict contraction on the set

\left\{Y\in\mathcal{Y}(X)\penalty\ |\penalty\ Y_{0}=y_{0}\right\}

with the metric

d(Y,\tilde{Y})=\inf\left\{\delta\geq 0\penalty\ |\penalty\ \forall I\subseteq[0,T]\quad V^{r}\Delta Y_{I}\leq\delta V^{r}X_{I}\right\}\,.

Proposition
5.7
and Lemma
5.5
imply that the Young differential equation locally has a unique solution for
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}_{b}
. Under some natural conditions on our constants, one can reduce some of our assumptions. Classical estimates give

\displaystyle\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}^{1+\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\phi\right\|_{\sup}^{\alpha}\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\left\|\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}^{\alpha}(2\left\|\phi\right\|_{\sup})^{1-\alpha}\,,

allowing us to choose
\Phi_{0},\Phi_{\alpha},\Phi_{1},\Phi_{1,\alpha}
in such a way that

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}^{1+\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\Phi_{1,\alpha}

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}^{\alpha}(2\Phi_{0})^{1-\alpha}\,.

Under this condition, the second bound in (
5.20
) implies the first up to a multiplicative constant, and the first bound in (
5.20
) implies (
5.13
) up to a multiplicative constant.

Proof.

Let
Y,\tilde{Y}\in\mathcal{Y}(X)
with
Y_{0}=\tilde{Y}_{0}=y_{0}
and
d(Y,\tilde{Y})=\delta
.
Then,

\left|\Delta Y_{s}\right|=\left|\Delta Y_{0,s}\right|\leq\delta\epsilon.

By Lemma
5.6
, we have

\displaystyle V^{r/\alpha}(\phi(Y)-\phi(\tilde{Y}))_{I}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}V^{r/\alpha}\Delta Y_{I}+\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}(V^{r}Y_{I}^{\alpha}+V^{r}\tilde{Y}_{I}^{\alpha})\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup,I}

(by (
5.16
))

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}V^{r}\Delta Y_{I}+2^{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}V^{r}X_{I}^{\alpha}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup,I}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}\delta V^{r}X_{I}+2^{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}V^{r}X_{I}^{\alpha}\delta\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\delta\epsilon\left(\Phi_{1}+2^{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\right).

Here,

\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup,I}:=\sup_{x\in I}\left|\Delta Y_{x}\right|\,.

Let
Z_{T}:=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\phi(Y)\text{d}X
,
\tilde{Z}_{T}:=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{T}\phi(\tilde{Y})\text{d}X
.
By the sewing lemma (Theorem
4.5
, cf. Lemma
5.8
with
\tilde{X}=X
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|Z_{s,u}-\tilde{Z}_{s,u}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\zeta(\theta)V^{r/\alpha}(\phi(Y)-\phi(\tilde{Y}))_{[s,u)}V^{r}X_{(s,u]}+\Phi_{1}\left|Y_{s}-\tilde{Y}_{s}\right|\left|X_{s,u}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\zeta(\theta)\delta\epsilon(\Phi_{1}+2^{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha})V^{r}X_{(s,u]}+\delta\epsilon\Phi_{1}\left|X_{s,u}\right|.

This implies

d(Z,\tilde{Z})\leq\zeta(\theta)\delta\epsilon(\Phi_{1}+2^{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha})+\delta\Phi_{1}\epsilon\leq\delta/2,

where we used the hypothesis (
5.20
) in the last step.
This shows the strict contractivity of the map (
5.17
).
∎

5.4
Lipschitz dependence on data

Before we show the desired Lipschitz estimate in this section, we show a preliminary result.

Lemma 5.8

(Stability of Young integration)
.

Let
X,\tilde{X},\in V^{r},Y,\tilde{Y}\in V^{r/\alpha}
for
r\in[1,2),\alpha\in(0,1]
such that
r<1+\alpha
.
Then,

\left|\int_{s}^{t}Y\text{d}X-\int_{s}^{t}\tilde{Y}\text{d}\tilde{X}-(Y_{s}X_{s,t}-\tilde{Y}_{s}\tilde{X}_{s,t})\right|\leq\\
2^{\theta-1}\zeta(\theta)V^{r/\alpha}\Delta Y_{[s,t)}V^{r}X_{(s,t]}+2^{\theta-1}\zeta(\theta)V^{r/\alpha}\tilde{Y}_{[s,t)}V^{r}\Delta X_{(s,t]}\,,

(5.21)

where
\theta=(1+\alpha)/r
and
\zeta(\theta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-\theta}
.

Proof.

Let
A_{s,t}:=Y_{s}X_{s,t}-\tilde{Y}_{s}\tilde{X}_{s,t}
.
Then,

\left|\int_{s}^{t}Y\text{d}X-\int_{s}^{t}\tilde{Y}\text{d}\tilde{X}-(Y_{s}X_{s,t}-\tilde{Y}_{s}\tilde{X}_{s,t})\right|=\left|\mathcal{I}A_{s,t}-A_{s,t}\right|.

We have

\delta A_{\tau,u,\nu}=Y_{\tau,u}X_{u,\nu}-\tilde{Y}_{\tau,u}\tilde{X}_{u,\nu}=\Delta Y_{\tau,u}X_{u,\nu}+\tilde{Y}_{\tau,\nu}\Delta X_{u,\nu}.

By the sewing lemma (Theorem
4.5
), this gives the claimed bound.
∎

For our main result, we need to strengthen the smallness conditions on
\epsilon
by the constant
2^{\theta-1}
. To this end, we strengthen (
5.13
) to

2^{\alpha+\theta-1}\zeta(\theta)\Phi_{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\leq\Phi_{0}^{1-\alpha},

(5.22)

and (
5.20
) to

2^{\theta-1}\zeta(\theta)\Phi_{1}\epsilon\leq 1/8,\quad 2^{\theta-1}\zeta(\theta)2^{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\epsilon^{1+\alpha}\leq 1/8.

(5.23)

With this in hand, we can show this section’s main result:

Theorem 5.9

(Lipschitz stability)
.

Let
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}_{b}
satisfy (
5.12
) and (
5.19
).
Let
\tilde{\phi}\in C^{0,\alpha}_{b}
satisfy (
5.12
).
Suppose that
\epsilon
satisfies the smallness conditions (
5.22
) and (
5.23
).

Let
X,\tilde{X}
be paths with
V^{r}X_{[0,T]}<\epsilon
and
V^{r}\tilde{X}_{[0,T]}<\epsilon
.
Let
y_{0},\tilde{y}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be initial data.
Let
Y
,
\tilde{Y}
be solutions of Young ODEs with respective drivers, coefficients, and initial data.
Then, for every interval
I\subseteq[0,T]
, we have

V^{r}\Delta Y_{I}\leq\gamma V^{r}X_{I}+2\Phi_{0}V^{r}\Delta X_{I}

(5.24)

with

\displaystyle\gamma\lesssim

\displaystyle((C_{\mathrm{sew}}+1)\Phi_{1}+C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha+1}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha})\Phi_{0}V^{r}\Delta X_{[0,T]}+(C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha+1}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}+\Phi_{1})\left|y_{0}-\tilde{y}_{0}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}+\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{\sup}\,,

where
C_{\mathrm{sew}}=2^{\theta-1}\zeta(\theta)
.

Setting
X=0
,
\phi=0
, this qualitatively recovers the a priori estimate in Proposition
5.4
(up to a larger sewing constant).

Proof.

By Proposition
5.4
, we have
Y\in\mathcal{Y}(X)
and
\tilde{Y}\in\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{X})
.
It follows that the conclusion (
5.24
) holds with
\gamma=4\Phi_{0}
.

Let
\gamma
be the smallest number such that (
5.24
) holds.
Let
\gamma^{\prime}=2\Phi_{0}
.
Let

\beta:=V^{r}\Delta X_{[0,T]},\quad\beta^{\prime}:=\left|y_{0}-\tilde{y}_{0}\right|,\quad\beta^{\prime\prime}:=\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}},\quad\beta^{\prime\prime\prime}:=\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{\sup}.

By our choice of
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
, we have

\delta^{\prime\prime}:=\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}\leq\beta^{\prime}+\gamma\epsilon+\gamma^{\prime}\beta.

Note

\displaystyle\left|\Delta\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t}-\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s})X_{s,t}\right|+\left|\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s})X_{s,t}-\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y}_{s})X_{s,t}\right|+\left|\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y}_{s})X_{s,t}-\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y}_{s})\tilde{X}_{s,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}\left|\Delta Y_{s}\right|\left|X_{s,t}\right|+\beta^{\prime\prime\prime}\left|X_{s,t}\right|+\Phi_{0}\left|\Delta X_{s,t}\right|,

and thus

\displaystyle V^{r}(\Delta\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t})_{I}

\displaystyle\leq(\Phi_{1}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}+\beta^{\prime\prime\prime})V^{r}X_{I}+\Phi_{0}V^{r}\Delta X_{I}

\displaystyle\leq(\Phi_{1}\gamma\epsilon+\Phi_{1}\gamma^{\prime}\beta+\Phi_{1}\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime\prime})V^{r}X_{I}+\Phi_{0}V^{r}\Delta X_{I}.

By Lemma
5.6
, we have

\displaystyle V^{r/\alpha}(\phi(Y)-\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y}))_{I}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}V^{r/\alpha}\Delta Y_{I}+\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}(V^{r}Y_{I}^{\alpha}+V^{r}\tilde{Y}_{I}^{\alpha})\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup,I}+\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}V^{r}\tilde{Y}_{I}^{\alpha}

(by (
5.16
))

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}V^{r}\Delta Y_{I}+2^{\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}(\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}V^{r}X_{I}^{\alpha}+\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}V^{r}\tilde{X}_{I}^{\alpha})\delta^{\prime\prime}+2^{\alpha}\beta^{\prime\prime}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}V^{r}\tilde{X}_{I}^{\alpha}

(by (
5.24
))

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}(\gamma\epsilon+\gamma^{\prime}\beta)+2^{1+\alpha}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\delta^{\prime\prime}+2^{\alpha}\beta^{\prime\prime}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}.

By (
5.16
) and (
5.22
), we have

V^{r/\alpha}\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y})_{I}\leq\Phi_{\alpha}V^{r}\tilde{Y}_{I}^{\alpha}\leq 2^{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}V^{r}\tilde{X}_{I}^{\alpha}\leq 2^{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\leq C_{\mathrm{sew}}^{-1}\Phi_{0}\,.

By Lemma
5.8
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Delta Y_{s,t}-\Delta\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{sew}}V^{r/\alpha}\Delta\phi(Y)_{[s,t)}V^{r}X_{(s,t]}+C_{\mathrm{sew}}V^{r/\alpha}\tilde{\phi}(\tilde{Y})_{[s,t)}V^{r}\Delta X_{(s,t]}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{sew}}(\Phi_{1}(\gamma\epsilon+\gamma^{\prime}\beta)+2^{\alpha+1}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\delta^{\prime\prime}+2^{\alpha}\beta^{\prime\prime}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha})V^{r}X_{(s,t]}+\Phi_{0}V^{r}\Delta X_{(s,t]}.

Hence, using (
5.22
) and (
5.23
) together with
C_{\mathrm{sew}}\geq 1
,

\displaystyle V^{r}\Delta Y_{I}

\displaystyle\leq V^{r}(\Delta(Y_{s,t}-\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t}))_{I}+V^{r}(\Delta\phi(Y_{s})X_{s,t})_{I}

(5.25)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{sew}}(\Phi_{1}(\gamma\epsilon+\gamma^{\prime}\beta)+2^{\alpha+1}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}(\beta^{\prime}+\gamma\epsilon+\gamma^{\prime}\beta)+2^{\alpha}\beta^{\prime\prime}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha})V^{r}X_{I}

\displaystyle+\Phi_{0}V^{r}(\Delta X)_{I}

\displaystyle+(\Phi_{1}\gamma\epsilon+\Phi_{1}\gamma^{\prime}\beta+\Phi_{1}\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime\prime})V^{r}X_{I}+\Phi_{0}V^{r}\Delta X_{I}

\displaystyle\leq(\gamma/2+C_{\mathrm{sew}}\Phi_{1}\gamma^{\prime}\beta+C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha+1}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}(\beta^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}\beta)+C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\beta^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle+\Phi_{1}\gamma^{\prime}\beta+\Phi_{1}\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime\prime})V^{r}X_{I}+2\Phi_{0}V^{r}\Delta X_{I}.

So, we obtain

\gamma\leq\gamma/2+C_{\mathrm{sew}}\Phi_{1}\gamma^{\prime}\beta+C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha+1}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}(\beta^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}\beta)+C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\beta^{\prime\prime}+\Phi_{1}\gamma^{\prime}\beta+\Phi_{1}\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime\prime}.

This implies

\gamma\lesssim C_{\mathrm{sew}}\Phi_{0}\Phi_{1}\beta+C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha+1}\frac{\Phi_{1,\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}(\beta^{\prime}+\Phi_{0}\beta)+C_{\mathrm{sew}}2^{\alpha}\Phi_{0}^{\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\beta^{\prime\prime}+\Phi_{1}\Phi_{0}\beta+\Phi_{1}\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime\prime}.

∎

6
Lipschitz estimates for RDEs

The goal of this section is to derive a similar estimate to (
5.24
) for solutions of rough differential equations

Y_{t}=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(\mathbf{Y}_{s})\text{d}\mathbf{X}_{s}\,.

(6.1)

Although similar estimates can be found in textbooks
[
FH20
,
FV10
]
, the existing versions do not seem sufficiently strong to recover Besov norm estimates from variation norm estimates.
For instance, unpacking
[
FV10
, Corollary 10.27]
leads to quotients of the form
V^{r/2}(\Delta\mathbb{X})/(V^{r}X+(V^{r/2}\mathbb{X})^{\frac{1}{2}}+V^{r}\tilde{X}+(V^{r/2}\tilde{\mathbb{X}})^{\frac{1}{2}})
, which we have been unable to bound in Besov norm.

Throughout this section, we fix an
r\in[2,3)
and an
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Let
\mathbf{X}=(X,\mathbb{X})
be an
r
-rough path over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we assume that
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to L(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Eventually, we will assume that
\phi\in C^{2,1}
, but we will clarify for each Lemma the minimal regularity for
\phi
.

To keep this section more concise, we omit the dependence on
\Delta\phi
by assuming
\phi=\tilde{\phi}
and only track the dependences on
\Delta X,\Delta\mathbb{X}
and
\Delta y_{0}
. We further use a solution space depending on
V^{r}X_{I},V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}_{I}
for each
I\subset[0,T]
, similar to (
5.3
). The precise definition can be found in (
6.33
).

As in Section
5
, we show an a priori estimate (Proposition
6.5
), local existence and uniqueness (Theorem
6.10
) and the desired Lipschitz estimate (Theorem
6.11
).

6.1
Operations on controlled rough paths

Let us recall some stability estimates on controlled rough paths. Similar estimates can be found in Gubinelli’s original paper
[
Gub04
]
, as well as
[
FH20
]
in the Hölder setting.

Throughout this subsection, we fix two
r
-rough paths
\mathbf{X},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
with controlled rough paths
\mathbf{Y},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
. As in the Young case, we use the notation

\Delta Y=Y-\tilde{Y},\quad\Delta Y^{\prime}=Y^{\prime}-\tilde{Y}^{\prime},\quad\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}=R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}-R^{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}},\quad\Delta X=X-\tilde{X},\quad\Delta\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}-\tilde{\mathbb{X}},\\
\quad\Delta\phi(Y)=\phi(Y)-\phi(\tilde{Y}),\quad\Delta R^{\phi(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{X}}=R^{\phi(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{X}}-R^{\phi(\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}),\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}.

The next lemma defines
\phi(\mathbf{Y})
and shows some stability estimates for the expression:

Lemma 6.1

(Composition with a smooth function)
.

Let
r\in[2,3)
,
\alpha\in(0,1]
and let
\phi\in C^{1,\alpha}_{b}
.
Then
\phi(\mathbf{Y}):=(\phi(Y),D\phi(Y)Y^{\prime})
is an
\mathbf{X}
-controlled
(r,\alpha)
-rough path, and we have for each interval
I\subset[0,T]
:

V^{r/\alpha}(\phi(\mathbf{Y})^{\prime})_{I}=V^{r/\alpha}(D\phi(Y)Y^{\prime})_{I}\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}V^{r/\alpha}(Y^{\prime})_{I}+\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}V^{r}Y_{I}^{\alpha}\left\|Y^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup},

(6.2)

V^{r/(1+\alpha)}(R^{\phi(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{X}})_{I}\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}V^{r/(1+\alpha)}(R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})_{I}+\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}V^{r}Y_{I}^{1+\alpha}.

(6.3)

Furthermore, if
\phi\in C^{2,1}
, we have the following stabillity estimates for any interval
I\subset[0,T]
:

V^{r}(\Delta\phi(Y))_{I}\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}(\Delta Y)_{I}+\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}\tilde{Y}_{I},

(6.4)

V^{r}(\Delta\phi(\mathbf{Y})^{\prime})_{I}\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}(\Delta Y^{\prime})_{I}+\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}V^{r}Y_{I}\left\|\Delta Y^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}+\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}(\tilde{Y}^{\prime})_{I}\\
+\left\|D^{2}\phi\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}(\Delta Y)_{I}\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}+\left\|D^{2}\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}(\tilde{Y})_{I}\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup},

(6.5)

V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\phi(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{X}})_{I}\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})_{I}+\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r/2}(R^{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}})_{I}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\left\|D^{2}\phi\right\|_{\sup}(V^{r}Y_{I}+V^{r}\tilde{Y}_{I})V^{r}(\Delta Y)_{I}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|D^{2}\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}(\tilde{Y})_{I}^{2}.

(6.6)

Interestingly, these estimates do not depend on
\mathbf{X}
.

Proof.

Adding and subtracting
D\phi(Y_{t})Y^{\prime}_{s}
, we obtain

D\phi(Y_{t})Y^{\prime}_{t}-D\phi(Y_{s})Y^{\prime}_{s}=D\phi(Y_{t})(Y^{\prime}_{t}-Y^{\prime}_{s})+(D\phi(Y_{t})-D\phi(Y_{s}))Y^{\prime}_{s}.

(6.7)

This implies (
6.2
). By Taylor’s formula,

\displaystyle\left|R^{\phi(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\phi(Y_{t})-\phi(Y_{s})-D\phi(Y_{s})Y_{s}^{\prime}X_{s,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\phi(Y_{t})-\phi(Y_{s})-D\phi(Y_{s})Y_{s,t}\right|+\left|D\phi(Y_{s})R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\left\|D\phi\right\|_{C^{\alpha}}\left|Y_{s,t}\right|^{1+\alpha}+\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}\left|R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}\right|,

which implies (
6.3
).

Let us now tackle the stability estimates (
6.4
) - (
6.6
), starting with (
6.4
).

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\phi(Y_{t})-\phi(Y_{s}))&-(\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t})-\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}))\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}D\phi(Y_{s}+rY_{s,t})Y_{s,t}\text{d}r-\int_{0}^{1}D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}+r\tilde{Y}_{s,t})\tilde{Y}_{s,t}\text{d}r\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl(D\phi(Y_{s}+rY_{s,t})(Y_{s,t}-\tilde{Y}_{s,t})\\
&\quad+(D\phi(Y_{s}+rY_{s,t})-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}+r\tilde{Y}_{s,t}))\tilde{Y}_{s,t}\Bigr)\text{d}r\,.\end{split}

(6.8)

Then

\displaystyle\left|D\phi(Y_{s}+rY_{s,t})-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}+r\tilde{Y}_{s,t})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left|(Y_{s}+rY_{s,t})-(\tilde{Y}_{s}+r\tilde{Y}_{s,t})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}(r\left|\Delta Y_{t}\right|+(1-r)\left|\Delta Y_{s}\right|)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}\,,

shows (
6.4
). In order to show (
6.5
), we write

(D\phi(Y_{t})Y^{\prime}_{t}-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t})\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{t})-(D\phi(Y_{s})Y^{\prime}_{s}-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s})\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{s})\\
=D\phi(Y_{t})(\Delta Y^{\prime}_{t}-\Delta Y^{\prime}_{s})+(D\phi(Y_{t})-D\phi(Y_{s}))\Delta Y^{\prime}_{s}\\
+(D\phi(Y_{t})-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t}))\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{s,t}+((D\phi(Y_{t})-D\phi(Y_{s}))-(D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{t})-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s})))\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{s}.

The first 3 terms contribute the first 3 terms to (
6.5
).
The 4-fold difference in the last term can be written as in (
6.8
) with
D\phi
in place of
\phi
, which gives the last 2 terms in (
6.5
).

).
By adding and subtracting
D\phi(Y_{s})Y_{s,t}
and
D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s})\tilde{Y}_{s,t},
one has

\begin{split}R^{\phi(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}-R^{\phi(\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}),\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}_{s,t}&=\phi(Y)_{s,t}-D\phi(Y_{s})Y_{s}^{\prime}X_{s,t}-(\phi(\tilde{Y})_{s,t}-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s})\tilde{Y}_{s}^{\prime}\tilde{X}_{s,t})\\

&+D\phi(Y_{s})R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s})R^{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}_{s,t}\end{split}

(6.9)

By Taylor’s formula, the first line in (

) equals

\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\bigl(D^{2}\phi(Y_{s}+rY_{s,t})Y_{s,t}^{\otimes 2}-D^{2}\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}+r\tilde{Y}_{s,t})\tilde{Y}_{s,t}^{\otimes 2}\bigr)\text{d}r.

The integrand can be written in the form

D^{2}\phi(Y_{s}+rY_{s,t})(Y_{s,t}^{\otimes 2}-\tilde{Y}_{s,t}^{\otimes 2})+(D^{2}\phi(Y_{s}+rY_{s,t})-D^{2}\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}+r\tilde{Y}_{s,t}))\tilde{Y}_{s,t}^{\otimes 2},

and this contributes the last line to (

The second line in (

\displaystyle D\phi(Y_{s})R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s})R^{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}_{s,t}

\displaystyle=D\phi(Y_{s})(R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}_{s,t}-R^{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}_{s,t})+(D\phi(Y_{s})-D\phi(\tilde{Y}_{s}))R^{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}_{s,t}

Lemma 6.2

(Rough path integration)
.

and

\mathbf{X}

Let us now clarify the space of solutions

Lemma 6.7

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{Y}(\mathbf{X}):=\bigg\{\mathbf{Y}\penalty\ |\penalty\ &V^{r}Y_{I}\leq c\omega_{I},V^{r/2}(R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})_{I}\leq(c-1)\omega_{I},V^{r/\alpha}(Y^{\prime})_{I}\leq c^{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}\omega_{I}^{\alpha},\\

6.4
Contraction of fixed point map

It is now straight-forward to show that
Step
from (
6.34
) is a contraction on
\mathcal{Y}(\mathbf{X})
. Recall that for
\alpha=1
, we use the notation
\Phi_{0,1}:=\Phi_{\alpha}
and set

A:=\max(1,\Phi_{0,1})c\omega_{[0,T]}\,.

(6.37)

This especially implies that
V^{r}Y\leq A,V^{r/2}R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}\leq A
and
V^{r}Y^{\prime}\leq A
for all
Y\in\cal Y(\mathbf{X})
. Then, one can show the following:

Lemma 6.9

(Contractivity of the iteration)
.

For every
\phi\in C^{2,1}_{b}
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that, for every rough path
\mathbf{X}
with
V^{r}X<\epsilon
and
V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}<\epsilon
,
the map (
6.34
) is strictly contractive on the set

\left\{Y\in\mathcal{Y}(\mathbf{X})\penalty\ |\penalty\ Y_{0}=y_{0},Y^{\prime}_{0}=\phi(y_{0})\right\}

(6.38)

with respect to the metric

d(\mathbf{Y},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}})=\max(V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}}),V^{r}(\Delta Y^{\prime}),CV^{r}(\Delta Y))\,,

(6.39)

where
C=2(\Phi_{1}+A\Phi_{1,1})
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbf{Y},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathcal{Y}(\mathbf{X})
with
d(\mathbf{Y},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}})=\alpha
and
Y_{0}=\tilde{Y}_{0}=y_{0}
as well as
Y_{0}^{\prime}=\tilde{Y}_{0}^{\prime}=\phi(y_{0})
.
Then,

\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}\leq V^{r}(\Delta Y)\leq\frac{\alpha}{C}.

By Lemma
6.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle V^{r}(\Delta\phi(Y))

\displaystyle\leq\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}(\Delta Y)+\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}\tilde{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha}{C}(\Phi_{1}+A\Phi_{1,1})\leq\alpha/2.

\displaystyle V^{r}(\Delta\phi(\mathbf{Y})^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}V^{r}(\Delta Y^{\prime})+\Phi_{1,1}V^{r}(Y)\left\|\Delta Y^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}+\Phi_{1,1}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}\tilde{Y}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Phi_{2}V^{r}(\Delta Y)\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}+\Phi_{2,1}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}\tilde{Y}\left\|\tilde{Y}^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha C_{1}\,,

for
C_{1}=\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{1,1}A+\frac{\Phi_{1,1}A}{C}+\frac{\Phi_{2}\Phi_{0}}{C}+\frac{\Phi_{2,1}\Phi_{0}A}{C}
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\phi(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{X}})

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{1}V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})+\Phi_{1,1}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r/2}(R^{\tilde{\mathbf{Y}},\tilde{\mathbf{X}}})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{2}(V^{r}Y+V^{r}\tilde{Y})V^{r}(\Delta Y)+\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{2,1}\left\|\Delta Y\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}\tilde{Y}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha C_{2}\,,

where
C_{2}=\Phi_{1}+\frac{\Phi_{1,1}A}{C}+\frac{\Phi_{2}A}{C}+\frac{\Phi_{2,1}A^{2}}{2C}
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=\operatorname{Step}(\mathbf{Y})
,
\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}=\operatorname{Step}(\tilde{\mathbf{Y}})
.
Then

V^{r}(\Delta Z^{\prime})=V^{r}(\Delta\phi(Y))\leq\alpha/2.

By Lemma
6.2
, where we use
\left\|\Delta\phi(\mathbf{Y})^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}\leq V^{r}(\Delta\phi(\mathbf{Y})^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{X}})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Delta\phi(\mathbf{Y})^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}

\displaystyle\quad+\zeta(3/r)4^{3/r-1}\left(V^{r}(\Delta\phi(\mathbf{Y})^{\prime})V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}+V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\phi(\mathbf{Y}),\mathbf{X}})V^{r}X\right)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\epsilon C_{3}\,.

For
C_{3}=\left(C_{1}+\zeta(3/r)4^{3/r-1}(C_{1}+C_{2})\right)
. By Lemma
3.3
, this implies

V^{r}(\Delta Z)\leq\left\|\Delta Z^{\prime}\right\|_{\sup}V^{r}X+V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{X}})\leq V^{r}(\Delta Z^{\prime})\epsilon+C_{3}\epsilon\alpha\leq\epsilon\alpha\left(\frac{1}{2}+C_{3}\right).

Choosing
\epsilon
small enough, we obtain the claim.
∎

This directly leads to the following local existence and uniqueness result:

Theorem 6.10

(Existence of solutions)
.

For every
\phi\in C^{2,1}_{b}
, there exist
\epsilon>0
such that, for every rough path
\mathbf{X}
with
V^{r}X<\epsilon
and
V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}<\epsilon
and every

, there exists a unique
\mathbf{X}
-controlled path

such that, for every

, we have

\mathbf{Y}_{t}=y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\phi(\mathbf{Y})\text{d}\mathbf{X},\quad Y^{\prime}_{t}=\phi(Y_{t}).

(6.40)

Proof.

Let
A,\epsilon
be as in Lemma
6.9
.
Define a sequence of controlled paths by

(\mathbf{Y}_{0})_{t}=y_{0},\quad(\mathbf{Y}_{0})^{\prime}_{t}=0,\quad(\mathbf{Y}_{j+1})=\operatorname{Step}(\mathbf{Y}_{j}).

For

(\mathbf{Y}_{j})
are elements of (

It follows from Lemma

that this sequence is Cauchy with respect to the metric (
6.39

The uniqueness of the solution follows from the strict contractivity of (

∎

Since our a priori estimates already imply that any solution lies in

-controlled rough paths over small enough intervals. By subdividing a larger interval into smaller intervals, one can also extend this result to global solutions.

Large jumps of
\mathbf{X}
have to be handled separately.
We omit the tedious details.

(6.41)

\displaystyle V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})_{I}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\bigg((V^{r}X_{I}^{2}+V^{r}X_{I}\cdot V^{r}\tilde{X}_{I}+V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}_{I}+V^{r/2}\tilde{\mathbb{X}}_{I})\gamma

(6.42)

\displaystyle\qquad+(V^{r}X_{I}+V^{r}\tilde{X}_{I}+V^{r/2}\mathbb{X}_{I}+V^{r/2}\tilde{\mathbb{X}}_{I})V^{r}(\Delta X)_{I}+V^{r/2}(\Delta\mathbb{X})_{I}\bigg)

Proof.

\displaystyle V^{r}(\Delta Y)_{I}

\displaystyle V^{r/2}(\Delta R^{\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X}})_{I}

7
Lipschitz estimates in the Besov setting

As an application of our results, we show in this section how one can derive Besov estimates from variation estimates. To this end, we recall some results from

7.1
Young integration

Let
f\in B^{\alpha_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}},g\in B^{\alpha_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}

and

Theorem 7.1

(
[
FSZ22
]

.

Let
\alpha>1,\alpha_{0}>\frac{1}{p_{0}},\alpha_{1}>\frac{1}{p_{1}}

Then the Young integral

\mathcal{I}\Xi_{s,t}:=\int_{s}^{t}f(z)\text{d}g(z)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}\Xi-\Xi\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}(\alpha,p,q)\left\|f\right\|_{B^{\alpha_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}}\left\|g\right\|_{B^{\alpha_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}

(7.1)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}\Xi\right\|_{B^{\alpha_{1}}_{p,q_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1})(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{0}}}+\left\|f\right\|_{B^{\alpha_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}})\left\|g\right\|_{B^{\alpha_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}\,,

for some constants
c_{1}(\alpha,p,q),c_{2}(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1})>0

Note that the assumption
\alpha_{i}>\frac{1}{p_{i}},i=0,1
especially imply
p_{0},p_{1}>1
.

Remark 7.2
.

Let us mention that directly constructing this integral in Besov spaces allows one to replace
\alpha_{0}>\frac{1}{p_{0}},\alpha_{1}>\frac{1}{p_{1}}
with the less restrictive condition
\alpha>\frac{1}{p}
. Our approach requires the stricter condition as constructing the Young integral in the variation setting requires

to have some finite

-variations, which puts individual requirements on
\alpha_{0},p_{0}
and
\alpha_{1},p_{1}

Let us make some observations before we tackle the proof. Note that

we can choose

in such a way that for

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle<r_{i}\leq p_{i}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{1}}

\displaystyle=\theta>1\,.

Thus, by Corollary
2.16
, it follows that

have unique continuous representations fulfilling

showing especially that these variations are finite. We can thus prove our theorem by Young integration for variation norms:

Proof of Theorem
7.1

Since

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|V^{r_{0}}fV^{r_{1}}g\right\|_{B^{\alpha}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|V^{r_{0}}f\right\|_{B^{\alpha_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}}\left\|V^{r_{1}}g\right\|_{B^{\alpha_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}

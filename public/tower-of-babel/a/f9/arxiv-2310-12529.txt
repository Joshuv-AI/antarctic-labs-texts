Introduction

Steady (gradient) Ricci soliton, as a singular model of type II of Ricci flow, has been extensively studied. In dimensions 2, Hamilton
[
22
,
14
]
proved that the cigar solution is the only 2d steady Ricci soliton up to scaling. In dimensions 3, Perelman conjectured that the Bryant Ricci soliton is the only 3d
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady Ricci soliton up to scaling
[
28
]
. The conjecture has been proved by Brendle
[
4
]
. These two Ricci solitons are both rotationally symmetric. In dimension

, besides the Bryant soliton
[
10
]
, Lai recently constructed a family of
{\rm SO}(n-1)
-symmetric solutions with positive curvature operator
[
24
]
. Thus it is interesting to classify steady Ricci solitons under suitable conditions of symmetry and curvature.

To characterize the Bryant Ricci soliton, Brendle introduced the following notion
[
5
]
.

Definition 0.1
.

A complete steady gradient Ricci soliton
(M^{n},g,f)
of dimension
n
is called asymptotically cylindrical if the following holds:

(i) The scalar curvature satisfies
\frac{C_{1}}{\rho(x)}\leq R(x)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\rho(x)}
as
\rho(x)>>1
, where
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
are two positive constants.

(ii) Let
p_{i}
be an arbitrary sequence of marked points going to infinity. Consider the rescaled metrics

(0.1)

\displaystyle g_{p_{i}}(t)=r_{i}^{-1}\phi^{*}_{r_{i}t}(g),

where
r_{i}R(p_{i})=1
and
\phi_{t}
is a family of transformations generated by
-\nabla f
, the flow
(M,g_{p_{i}}(t);p_{i})
converges in the Cheeger-Gromov sense to a family of shrinking cylinders
(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R},~\bar{g}(t))
,
t\in(0,1)
. The metric
\bar{g}(t)
is given by

(0.2)

\displaystyle\bar{g}(t)=(1-t)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(1)}+ds^{2},

where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(1)
is the unit sphere of dimension
(n-1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In
[
5
]
, Brendle proved that any steady gradient Ricci soliton with nonnegative sectional curvature must be isometric to the Bryant Ricci soliton up to scaling if it is asymptotically cylindrical. Later, Deng-Zhu found that the Brendle’s result still holds if one of the two conditions in Definition

-noncollapsed steady Ricci solitons with nonnegative curvature operator

Thus it is a natural question to ask: is the Brendle’s result true if the condition (ii) is satisfied by (at least) one sequence? In this paper, we give a positive answer for 4d

-noncollapsed steady Ricci solitons with nonnegative sectional curvature.

A splitting theorem

A complete Riemannian metric
g
on
M
is called a gradient Ricci soliton if there exists a smooth function
f
(which is called a defining function) on
M
such that

(1.1)

R_{ij}(g)+\rho g_{ij}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f,

where
\rho\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. The gradient Ricci soliton is called expanding, steady and shrinking according to
\rho>,=,<0
, respectively. These three types of Ricci solitons correspond to three different blow-up solutions of Ricci flow
[
22
]
.

In case of steady Ricci solitons, we can rewrite (
1.1
) as

(1.2)

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Ric}(g)=L_{X}g,

where
L_{X}
is the Lie operator along the gradient vector field (VF)
X=\nabla f
generalized by
f
.
Let
\{\phi^{*}_{t}\}_{t\in(-\infty,\infty)}
be a 1-ps of transformations generated by
-X
. Then

g(t)=\phi^{*}_{t}(g)
(
t\in(-\infty,\infty))
is a solution of Ricci flow. Namely,
g(t)
satisfies

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=-2{\rm Ric}(g),~g(0)=g.

For simplicity, we call
g(t)
the soliton Ricci flow of
(M,g)
.

By (
1.2
), we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla R,\nabla f\rangle=-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f),

where
R
is the scalar curvature of
g
.
It follows

R+|\nabla f|^{2}={\rm Const.}

Since
R
is alway positive (
[
30
,
11
]
), the above equation can be normalized by

(1.5)

\displaystyle R+|\nabla f|^{2}=1.

We recall that an ancient
\kappa
-solution is a
\kappa
-noncollapsed solution of Ricci flow (
1.3
) with
{\rm R_{m}}(\cdot,t)\geq 0
defined for any

.
The following result is a version of Perelman’s compactness theorem for higher dimensional ancient

-solutions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\left(M^{n},g_{i}(t);p_{i}\right)

be any sequence of

-dimensional ancient

-solutions on a noncompact manifold

(M,g_{i}(t);~

p_{i})
subsequently converge to a splitting flow

in the Cheeger-Gromov sense. Here

(1.6)

\displaystyle\bar{g}(t)=h(t)+ds^{2},

and
(N,h(t))
is an
(n-1)
-dimensional ancient
\kappa
-solution.

The convergence of

comes from
[

[

with the help of Perelman’s asymptotic volume ratio estimate for

-solutions
[

In fact, for a sequence of rescaling Ricci flows arising from a steady Ricci soliton, we can improve Theorem

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)

(n\geq 3)
be a noncompact
\kappa
-noncollapsed steady gradient Ricci soliton with

. Let

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}R(x,t)\geq 0,~{\rm on}~M\setminus K.

Then according to the proof of Theorem

(see Lemma 3.5-3.7 for details there), for the convergence part in the proposition, we only need to show that the following asymptotic scalar curvature estimate,

(1.8)

\displaystyle{\rm limsup}_{x\rightarrow\infty}R(x)d^{2}(o,x)=\infty,

where

is a fixed point.
As a consequence,

(M,g_{p_{i}}(t);p_{i})
has uniformly local curvature estimate, and therefore

(M,g_{p_{i}}(t);p_{i})
subsequently converges to a limit ancient
\kappa
-solution

We note that (

On the contrary, we suppose that (

\displaystyle R(x)\leq\frac{C}{d^{2}(o,x)}=o(\frac{1}{d(o,x)}).

In particular, the scalar curvature decays to zero uniformly. Due to a result in

\displaystyle c_{1}\rho(x)\leq f(x)\leq c_{2}\rho(x).

Thus by

. But this contradicts to

In the following, our goal is to show that

First we prove the volume ratio estimate,

\displaystyle{\rm AVR}(g)=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm Vol}(B(p,r))}{r^{n}}=0.

\displaystyle R(q_{i})s_{i}^{2}\rightarrow\infty,

Consider a sequence of the rescaled flows

\displaystyle r(p_{i})=\sup\{\rho|~{\rm Vol}(B(p_{i},\rho))\geq\frac{\omega}{2}\rho^{n}\}.

Proof of Corollary

.

By the assumption, the

(N,h(t))
of limit of

is a quotient of
round sphere. We need to show that

On the contrary,
{\rm Ric}(g)
is not strictly positive. We note that

without

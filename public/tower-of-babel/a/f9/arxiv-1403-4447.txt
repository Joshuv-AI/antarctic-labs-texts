A note on q q -analogue of Boole polynomials Thanks:
Dae San Kim, TAEKYUN KIM, JONG JIN SEO

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the q q -extensions of Boole polynomials. From those polynomials, we derive some new and interesting properties and identities related to special polynomials.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed odd prime number. Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\mathbb{C}_{p}
will denote the ring of
p
-adic integers, the field of
p
-adic numbers and the completion of algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The
p
-adic norm
{|\cdot|}_{p}
is normalized as
|p|_{p}={1/p}
. The space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is denoted by
C\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
. Let
q
be an indeterminate in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|1-q|_{p}<p^{-1/p-1}
. The
q
-number of
x
is defined by
[x]_{q}={{1-q^{x}}\over{1-q}}
. Note that
\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}{[x]_{q}=x}
. For
f\in C\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)
, the fermionic
p
-adic
q
-integral on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is defined by Kim to be

\begin{split}&I_{-q}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}f(x)d\mu_{-q}(x)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}{1\over{[p^{N}]_{-q}}}\sum_{x=0}^{p^{N}-1}f(x)(-1)^{x},\\
&\textnormal{where}\ [x]_{-q}={{1-(-q)^{x}}\over{1+q}}\ (\textnormal{see}\ [1-9]).\end{split}

(1.1)

From (1.1), we note that

\begin{split}&q^{n}I_{-q}(f_{n})+(-1)^{n-1}I_{-q}(f)=[2]_{q}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1-l}q^{l}f(l),\\
&\textnormal{where}\ f_{n}(x)=f(x+n),(n\geq 1)\ (\textnormal{see}\ [4]).\end{split}

(1.2)

In particular, for
n
=1,

qI_{-q}(f_{1})+I_{-q}(f)=[2]_{q}f(0).

(1.3)

As is well known, the Boole polynomials are defined by the generating function to be

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n}(x|\lambda){t^{n}\over{n!}}={1\over{1+(1+t)^{\lambda}}}(1+t)^{x},\ (\textnormal{see}\ [2,11]).

(1.4)

When
\lambda=1,2Bl_{n}(x|1)=Ch_{n}(x)
are Changhee polynomials which are defined by

{2\over{t+2}}(1+t)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Ch_{n}(x){t^{n}\over{n!}}\ (\textnormal{see}\ [2]).

(1.5)

The Euler polynomials of order
\alpha
are defined by the generating function to be

\left({2\over{e^{t}+1}}\right)^{\alpha}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x){t^{n}\over{n!}},\ (\textnormal{see}\ [2,11]).

(1.6)

When
x=0,E_{n}^{(\alpha)}=E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(0)
are called the Euler numbers of order
\alpha
.

In particular, for
\alpha=1,E_{n}(x)=E_{n}^{(1)}(x)
are called the ordinary Euler polynomials.

The Stirling number of the first kind is given by the generating function to be

\log{(1+t)^{m}}=m!\sum_{l=m}^{\infty}S_{1}(l,m){t^{l}\over{l!}},(m\geq 0),

(1.7)

and the Stirling number of the second kind is defined by the generating function to be

(e^{t}-1)^{m}=m!\sum_{l=m}^{\infty}S_{2}(l,m){t^{l}\over{l!}},\ (\textnormal{see}\ [11]).

(1.8)

In this paper, we consider the
q
-extensions of Boole polynomials. From those polynomials, we derive new and interesting properties and identities related to special polynomials.

2.
q
-analogue of Boole polynomials

In this section, we assume that
t\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
{|t|}_{p}<p^{-1\over{p-1}}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
\lambda\neq 0
. From (1.3), we note that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(1+t)^{x+\lambda y}d\mu_{-q}(y)&={1+q\over{1+q(1+t)^{\lambda}}}(1+t)^{x}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[2]_{q}Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda){t^{n}\over{n!}},\end{split}

(2.1)

where
Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda)
are the
q
-Boole polynomials which are defined by

{1\over{1+q(1+t)^{\lambda}}}(1+t)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda){t^{n}\over{n!}}.

(2.2)

From (2.1), we can derive the following equation :

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\binom{x+\lambda y}{n}d\mu_{-q}(y)={[2]_{q}\over{n!}}Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda).

(2.3)

When
x=0,Bl_{n,q}(\lambda)=Bl_{n,q}(0|\lambda)
are called the
q
-Boole numbers.

Now, we observe that

\begin{split}(1+t)^{x+\lambda y}&=e^{(x+\lambda y)\log{(1+t)}}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{(x+\lambda y)^{m}\over{m!}}\bigl(\log(1+t)\bigr)^{m}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}{(x+\lambda y)^{m}\over{m!}}m!\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,m){t^{n}\over{n!}}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{m=0}^{n}(x+\lambda y)^{m}S_{1}(n,m)\right\}{t^{n}\over{n!}}.\end{split}

(2.4)

The
q
-Euler polynomials are defined by the generating function to be

{[2]_{q}\over{qe^{t}+1}}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n,q}(x){t^{n}\over{n!}}.

(2.5)

Note that
\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}{E_{n,q}(x)}=E_{n}(x)
.

When
x=0,E_{n,q}=E_{n,q}(0)
are called the
q
-Euler numbers.
By (1.3), we easily get

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{(x+y)t}d\mu_{-q}(y)&={[2]_{q}\over{qe^{t}+1}}e^{xt}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n,q}(x){t^{n}\over{n!}}.\end{split}

(2.6)

Thus, by (2.6), we get

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(x+y)^{n}d\mu_{-q}(y)=E_{n,q}(x),(n\geq 0).

(2.7)

From (2.1), (2.4) and (2.7), we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(1+t)^{x+\lambda y}d\mu_{-q}(y)&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{m=0}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(x+\lambda y)^{m}d\mu_{-q}(y)S_{1}(n,m)\right\}{t^{n}\over{n!}}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{m=0}^{n}\lambda^{m}E_{m,q}\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right)S_{1}(n,m)\right\}{t^{n}\over{n!}}.\end{split}

(2.8)

Therefore, by (2.1), (2.3) and (2.8), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda)={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\lambda^{m}E_{m,q}\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right)S_{1}(n,m),

and

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\binom{x+\lambda y}{n}d\mu_{-q}(y)={[2]_{q}\over{n!}}Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda).

From (2.3), we note that

Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda)={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(x+\lambda y)_{n}d\mu_{-q}(y).

When
\lambda=1
, we have

Bl_{n,q}(x|1)={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(x+y)_{n}d\mu_{-q}(y).

(2.9)

As is known,
q
-Changhee polynomials are defined by the generating function to be

{[2]_{q}\over{[2]_{q}+qt}}(1+t)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Ch_{n,q}(x){t^{n}\over{n!}}.

(2.10)

Thus, by (2.10), we get

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(1+t)^{x+y}d\mu_{-q}(y)={[2]_{q}\over{[2]_{q}+qt}}(1+t)^{x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Ch_{n,q}(x){t^{n}\over{n!}}.

(2.11)

From (2.11), we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(x+y)_{n}d\mu_{-q}(y)=Ch_{n,q}(x),\\
&\textnormal{where}(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1).\end{split}

(2.12)

By (2.9) and (2.12), we get

Bl_{n,q}(x|1)={1\over{[2]_{q}}}Ch_{n,q}(x).

(2.13)

By replacing
t
by
e^{t}-1
in (2.2), we see that

\begin{split}{[2]_{q}\over{qe^{\lambda t}+1}}e^{xt}&=[2]_{q}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda){1\over{n!}}(e^{t}-1)^{n}\\
&=[2]_{q}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda)\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}S_{2}(m,n){t^{m}\over{m!}}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{m}[2]_{q}Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda)S_{2}(m,n){t^{m}\over{m!}},\end{split}

(2.14)

and

\begin{split}{[2]_{q}\over{qe^{\lambda t}+1}}e^{xt}&={[2]_{q}\over{qe^{\lambda t}+1}}e^{\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right)\lambda t}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E_{m,q}\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right)\lambda^{m}{t^{m}\over{m!}}.\end{split}

(2.15)

Therefore, by (2.14) and (2.15), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
m\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{m}Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda)S_{2}(m,n)={1\over{[2]_{q}}}E_{m,q}\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right)\lambda^{m}.

Let us define the
q
-Boole numbers of the first kind with order
k(\in\mathbb{N})
as follows :

[2]_{q}^{k}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\lambda(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}))_{n}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k}),(n\geq 0).

(2.16)

Thus, by (2.16), we see that

\begin{split}[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda){t^{n}\over{n!}}&=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{\lambda(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k})}{n}t^{n}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(1+t)^{\lambda(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k})}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\left({1+q\over{1+q(1+t)^{\lambda}}}\right)^{k}\\
&=[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\cdots,l_{k}}Bl_{l_{1,q}}\cdots Bl_{l_{k,q}}\right){t^{n}\over{n!}}.\end{split}

(2.17)

Therefore, by (2.17), we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}=\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\cdots,l_{k}}Bl_{l_{1,q}}\cdots Bl_{l_{k,q}}.

The
q
-Euler polynomials of order
k
are defined by the generating function to be

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}e^{(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}+x)t}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\left({[2]_{q}\over{qe^{t}+1}}\right)^{k}e^{xt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n,q}^{(k)}(x){t^{n}\over{n!}}.\end{split}

(2.18)

Thus, by (2.18), we get

\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}+x)^{n}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})=E_{n,q}^{(k)}(x).

When
x=0,E_{n,q}^{(k)}=E_{n,q}^{(k)}(0)
are called the
q
-Euler numbers of order
k
.

From (2.16), we note that

\begin{split}[2]_{q}^{k}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)&=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\lambda(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k}))_{n}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\lambda^{l}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k})^{l}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\lambda^{l}E_{l,q}^{(k)}.\end{split}

(2.19)

Therefore, by (2.19), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)={1\over{[2]_{q}^{k}}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\lambda^{l}E_{l,q}^{(k)}.

By replacing
t
by
e^{t}-1
in (2.17), we get

\begin{split}[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda){1\over{n!}}(e^{t}-1)^{n}&=\left({[2]_{q}\over{qe^{\lambda t}+1}}\right)^{k}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E_{m,q}^{(k)}\lambda^{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!},\end{split}

(2.20)

and

\begin{split}[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda){1\over{n!}}(e^{t}-1)^{n}&=[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}S_{2}(m,n){t^{m}\over{m!}}\\
&=[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{n=0}^{m}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)S_{2}(m,n)\right\}{t^{m}\over{m!}}.\end{split}

(2.21)

Therefore, by (2.20) and (2.21), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
m\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{m}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)S_{2}(m,n)={1\over{[2]_{q}^{k}}}E_{m,q}^{(k)}\lambda^{m}.

Let us define the higher-order
q
-Boole polynomials of the first kind as

follows :

\begin{split}&[2]_{q}^{k}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\lambda x_{1}+\cdots+\lambda x_{k}+x)_{n}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k}),\\
&\textnormal{where}\ n\geq 0\ \textnormal{and}\ k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

(2.22)

From (2.22), we can derive the generating function of the higher-order
q
-Boole polynomials of the first kind as follows :

\begin{split}[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda){t^{n}\over{n!}}&=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(1+t)^{\lambda x_{1}+\cdots+\lambda x_{k}+x}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\left({[2]_{q}\over{1+q(1+t)^{\lambda}}}\right)^{k}(1+t)^{x}\end{split}

(2.23)

By (2.17), we easily get

\begin{split}\left({[2]_{q}\over{1+q(1+t)^{\lambda}}}\right)^{k}(1+t)^{x}&=[2]_{q}^{k}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}Bl_{l,q}^{(k)}(\lambda){t^{l}\over{l!}}\right)\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}m!\binom{x}{m}{t^{m}\over{m!}}\right)\\
&=[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}m!\binom{x}{m}{n!\over{m!(n-m)!}}Bl_{n-m,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)\right){t^{n}\over{n!}}\\
&=[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}m!\binom{x}{m}\binom{n}{m}Bl_{n-m,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)\right){t^{n}\over{n!}}.\end{split}

(2.24)

Therefore, by (2.23) and (2.24), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}Bl_{n-m,q}^{(k)}(\lambda)(x)_{m}.

Replacing
t
by
e^{t}-1
in (2.23), we have

\begin{split}[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda){(e^{t}-1)^{n}\over{n!}}&=\left({[2]_{q}\over{1+qe^{\lambda t}}}\right)^{k}e^{xt}\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E_{m,q}^{(k)}\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right)\lambda^{m}{t^{m}\over{m!}},\end{split}

(2.25)

and

[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda){(e^{t}-1)^{n}\over{n!}}=[2]_{q}^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)S_{2}(m,n)\right){t^{m}\over{m!}}.

(2.26)

Thus, from (2.25) and (2.26), we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
m\geq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{m}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)S_{2}(m,n)={1\over{[2]_{q}^{k}}}\lambda^{m}E_{m,q}^{(k)}\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right).

From (2.22), we have

\begin{split}[2]_{q}^{k}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)&=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\lambda x_{1}+\cdots+\lambda x_{k}+x)_{n}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\lambda x_{1}+\cdots+\lambda x_{k}+x)^{l}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\lambda^{l}E_{l,q}^{(k)}\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right).\end{split}

(2.27)

Therefore, by (2.27), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

For
n\geq 0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)={1\over{[2]_{q}^{k}}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\lambda^{l}E_{l,q}^{(k)}\left({x\over{\lambda}}\right).

Now, we consider the
q
-analogue of Boole polynomials of the second kind as follows :

\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda)={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(-\lambda y+x)_{n}d\mu_{-q}(y),(n\geq 0).

(2.28)

Thus, by (2.28), we get

\begin{split}\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda)&={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)(-1)^{l}\lambda^{l}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\left(-{x\over{\lambda}}+y\right)^{l}d\mu_{-q}(y)\\
&={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)(-1)^{l}\lambda^{l}E_{l,q}\left(-{x\over{\lambda}}\right).\end{split}

(2.29)

When
x=0,\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(\lambda)=\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(0|\lambda)
are called the
q
-Boole numbers of the second kind.
From (2.28), we can derive the generating function of
\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda)
as follows:

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda){t^{n}\over{n!}}&={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(1+t)^{-\lambda y+x}d\mu_{-q}(y)\\
&={(1+t)^{\lambda}\over{q+(1+t)^{\lambda}}}(1+t)^{x}.\end{split}

(2.30)

By replacing
t
by
e^{t}-1
in (2.30), we get

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda){(e^{t}-1)^{n}\over{n!}}&={e^{\lambda t}\over{q+e^{\lambda t}}}e^{xt}\\
&={1\over{qe^{-\lambda t}+1}}e^{xt}\\
&={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}{\lambda}^{m}E_{m,q}(-\frac{x}{\lambda}){t^{m}\over{m!}},\end{split}

(2.31)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda){(e^{t}-1)^{n}\over{n!}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m}\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda)S_{2}(m,n)\right){t^{m}\over{m!}}.

(2.32)

Therefore, by (2.31) and (2.32), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

For
m\geq 0
, we have

{(-1)^{m}\lambda^{m}\over{[2]_{q}}}E_{m,q}(-\frac{x}{\lambda})=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda)S_{2}(m,n),

and

\widehat{Bl}_{m,q}(x|\lambda)={1\over{[2]_{q}}}\sum_{l=0}^{m}S_{1}(m,l)(-1)^{l}\lambda^{l}E_{l,q}\left(-{x\over{\lambda}}\right).

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let us define the
q
-Boole polynomials of the second kind with order
k
as follows :

\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)={1\over{[2]_{q}^{k}}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(-(\lambda x_{1}+\cdots+\lambda x_{k})+x)_{n}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k}).

(2.33)

Then we have

[2]_{q}^{k}\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,l)\lambda^{l}(-1)^{l}E_{l,q}\left(-{x\over{\lambda}}\right).

From (2.33), we can derive the generating function of
\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)
as follows :

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda){t^{n}\over{n!}}&={1\over{[2]_{q}^{k}}}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(1+t)^{-(\lambda x_{1}+\cdots+\lambda x_{k})+x}d\mu_{-q}(x_{1})\cdots d\mu_{-q}(x_{k})\\
&=\left({(1+t)^{\lambda}\over{q+(1+t)^{\lambda}}}\right)^{k}(1+t)^{x}\\
&=\left({1\over{q(1+t)^{-\lambda}+1}}\right)^{k}(1+t)^{x}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Bl_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|-\lambda){t^{n}\over{n!}}.\end{split}

(2.34)

Thus, by (2.34), we get

\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|\lambda)={Bl}_{n,q}^{(k)}(x|-\lambda),(n\geq 0).

(2.35)

Indeed,

\begin{split}(-1)^{n}[2]_{q}{Bl_{n,q}(x|\lambda)\over{n!}}&=(-1)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\binom{x+\lambda y}{n}d\mu_{-q}(y)\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\binom{-y\lambda-x+n-1}{n}d\mu_{-q}(y)\\
&=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n-1}{n-m}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\binom{-y\lambda-x}{m}d\mu_{-q}(y)\\
&=\sum_{m=1}^{n}{\binom{n-1}{m-1}\over{m!}}m!\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\binom{-y\lambda-x}{m}d\mu_{-q}(y)\\
&=[2]_{q}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{m-1}{\widehat{Bl}_{m,q}(-x|\lambda)\over{m!}},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}(-1)^{n}[2]_{q}{\widehat{Bl}_{n,q}(x|\lambda)\over{n!}}&=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n-1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\binom{-x+y\lambda}{m}d\mu_{-q}(y)\\
&=[2]_{q}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{m-1}{\widehat{Bl}_{m,q}(-x|\lambda)\over{m!}}.\end{split}

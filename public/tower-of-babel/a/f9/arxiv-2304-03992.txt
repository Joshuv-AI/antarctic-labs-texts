Introduction

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field. A polynomial
t(x)\in\mathbb{K}[x]
is stable over
\mathbb{K}
if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
-th iterate
t^{(n)}(x)=t(t(\dots t(t(x))))
of
t
is irreducible over
\mathbb{K}
.
Problems concerning stability of polynomials over fields date back to the
1980
s, when Odoni
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
, motivated by a number-theoretic question involving prime divisors of a non-linear recurrence relation, discovered a stable polynomial
t_{1}(x)=x^{2}-x+1
over
\mathbb{Q}
. On the other hand, for any prime
p
and integer
n\geq 3
, Odoni proved in
[
9
, Corollary 1.6]
that the
n
-th iterate of
t_{2}(x)=x^{p}-x-1
is reducible over the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Since then, stability of polynomials, especially those of low degrees, over various fields have been extensively studied. For example, Ahmadi et al. showed in
[
1
, Theorem 4, Corollary 11]
that
almost all
monic quadratic polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
are stable over
\mathbb{Q}
and that no quadratic polynomial is stable over a finite field of characteristic
2
. In
2010
, Goméz-Pérez and Nicolás first estimated the number of stable quadratic polynomials over a finite field of odd characteristic
[
4
, Theorem 1]
. Together with with Ostafe and Sardonil, they also estimated the number of stable polynomials of an arbitrary degree
d\in\mathbb{N}
over a finite field of odd characteristic in 2014
[
5
, Theorem 5.5]
. In
2012
, Jones and Boston gave necessary and sufficient conditions (in terms of the so-called adjusted critical orbits) for a quadratic polynomial to be stable over a finite field of odd characteristic
[
6
, Proposition 2.3]
(and see
[
7
]
for errata). Based on their results, Ostafe and Shparlinski estimated the complexity of testing the stability of quadratic polynomials over a finite field of odd characteristic
[
10
, Corollary 2]
.

For polynomials of degree greater than
2
, determining whether such a polynomial is stable over a field remains challenging, and not many examples of stable polynomials of degree
\geq 3
are known to date. For example, according to
[
5
, Theorem 4.3]
, no polynomials of the form
ax^{3}+bx+c
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{{3}^{k}}
. When it comes to cubic polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{k}}
, Ahmadi and Monsef-Shokri suggests that
f(x)=x^{3}+x^{2}+1
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{2}

[
2
, Conjecture 14]
. Motivated by this conjecture, we present several new classes of stable polynomials of degrees less than or equal to
4
and thus confirm the conjecture of Ahmadi and Monsef-Shokri.

The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows. In
Section

, we introduce a new class of polynomials, to which we apply the stability test proposed in
[
2
]
. Based on the test, stable polynomials of the form

f(x)=b^{m-1}(x+a)^{m}+c(x+a)+d

where

, are constructed in Sections

,
4
and
5
, respectively. In addition, in

. In

2
A sequence-based stability test

This section centers around a stability test based on the following Capelli’s lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
2
, Lemma 13]
,
[
3
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
q
be any prime power, and let
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be an irreducible polynomial of degree
d\in\mathbb{N}
. If
f^{(n)}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
f^{(n+1)}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff
f(x)-\alpha
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
for some root
\alpha
of
f^{(n)}(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

Capelli’s Lemma provides a trade-off between polynomials of higher degrees (i.e.,
\left(f^{(n)}(x)\right)_{n\geq 1}
) over a field of small size and polynomials of lower degrees (i.e.,
\left(f(x)-\alpha_{n}\right)_{n\geq 1}
, where
\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
and
f^{(n)}(\alpha_{n})=0
) over larger fields.

Next we define a new class of polynomials (which we call
shift-resistant
polynomials) and study their stability by repeated use of Capelli’s lemma.

Definition 2.3
.

A polynomial
f(x)
is called an
SR(q,d)
-polynomial (where
SR
stands for shift-resistant) if
\deg(f)=d
and for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{r}}
,
f(x)-a
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{dr}}
.

Example 2.4
.

Every quadratic polynomial is an
SR(q,2)
-polynomial. Indeed, if
f(x)
is quadratic, then
f(x)-a
either has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{r}}
or is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{r}}
. In the latter case,
f(x)-a
has a root in its splitting field
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2r}}
. Similarly, every cubic polynomial is an
SR(q,3)
-polynomial.

Example 2.5
.

Every quartic polynomial is an
SR(q,4)
-polynomial. Indeed, assume that
f(x)
is quartic. If
f(x)-a
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{r}}
, then the claim is clearly true. Otherwise, there are two possibilities. If
f(x)-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{r}}
, then it has a root in its splitting field
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4r}}
. If
f(x)-a
is factored as a product of two irreducible quadratic factors over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{r}}
, then it has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2r}}\subset\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4r}}
.

Example 2.6
.

The quintic polynomial
f(x)=\left(x^{2}+x+1\right)\left(x^{3}+x+1\right)
is not an
SR(2,5)
polynomial because it has
2
roots in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
3
roots in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{3}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and therefore none of its roots is in
\mathbb{F}_{2^{5}}
.

Let
f(x)
be any
SR(q,d)
-polynomial. Let
\alpha_{0}=0
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\alpha_{n}
be a root of
f(x)-\alpha_{n-1}
in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
. Then it is easy to see that

f^{(n)}(\alpha_{n})=f^{(n-1)}(f(\alpha_{n}))=f^{(n-1)}(\alpha_{n-1})=\dots=f(\alpha_{1})=\alpha_{0}=0

In other words,
\alpha_{n}
is a root of
f^{(n)}(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
. Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let

f_{n}(x)=f(x)-\alpha_{n}

In the notation above, the following result is a restatement of Capelli’s lemma.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
f(x)
be any
SR(q,d)
-polynomial and let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Assume that
f^{(n)}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
f^{(n+1)}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff
f_{n}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
.

We note that
\alpha_{0}=0
is a root of
f^{(0)}(x)=x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and that
f_{0}(x)=f(x)
. So
2.7
holds trivially when
n=0
. Using the above result, a stability test for
SR(q,d)
-polynomials is obtained.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
f(x)
be any
SR(q,d)
-polynomial. Then
f(x)
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
f_{n}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
.

Proof
If
f(x)
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
f^{(n)}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. By
2.7
,
f_{n}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Conversely, if
f_{n}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{n}}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
f_{0}(x)=f(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
f_{1}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d}}
. By
2.7
,
f^{(2)}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then, as
f_{2}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{d^{2}}}
,
f^{(3)}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
due to
2.7
. In an iterative manner, we see that
f^{(n)}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore,
f(x)
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

\square

SR(q,d)
-polynomials for

Let
q

Theorem 3.1
.

If
a,b,c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
b\neq 0
and

\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}_{q}

q\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

Proof
Following the idea introduced in
Section

2

(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, where
\alpha_{0}=0
and
f(\alpha_{n})=\alpha_{n-1}\ (n\in\mathbb{N})

f_{n}(x)=f(x)-\alpha_{n}=b(x+a)^{2}+c(x+a)-(\alpha_{n}+a)+\frac{c^{2}-2c}{4b}

By
2.8
,
f(x)
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff
f_{n}(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n}}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The change of variables
y=b(x+a)
implies that latter claim is true iff

g_{n}(y)=y^{2}+cy-\beta_{n}

is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n}}}
for each

\beta_{n}=b(\alpha_{n}+a)-\frac{c^{2}-2c}{4}

Equivalently,
f(x)
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\Delta_{n}=c^{2}+4\beta_{n}

is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n}}}
for each

. It suffices to verify that the claim is true for all

because by assumption,
\Delta_{0}=4ab+2c
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since
f(\alpha_{n})=\alpha_{n-1}
, we have that
g_{n-1}\left(\beta_{n}+\frac{c^{2}-2c}{4}\right)=0
, i.e., that

\left(\beta_{n}+\frac{c^{2}-2c}{4}\right)^{2}+c\left(\beta_{n}+\frac{c^{2}-2c}{4}\right)=\beta_{n-1}

meaning that

\left(\frac{\Delta_{n}-c^{2}}{4}+\frac{c^{2}-2c}{4}\right)^{2}+c\left(\frac{\Delta_{n}-c^{2}}{4}+\frac{c^{2}-2c}{4}\right)+\frac{c^{2}}{4}=\frac{\Delta_{n-1}-c^{2}}{4}+\frac{c^{2}}{4}

from which it follows that

\Delta_{n}^{2}=4\Delta_{n-1}

(1)

Case 1:
q\equiv 3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

In this case,
q+1\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
. If
\gamma
is primitive in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
, then
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\left<\gamma^{q+1}\right>
. Hence, every element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
4
-th power in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
. So
\Delta_{1}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{Eq1})}}{{=}}4\Delta_{0}=s^{4}
for some
s\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
, meaning that
\Delta_{1}=(st)^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
for some
4
-th root of unity

. Hence,
g_{1}(x)
is reducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2}}
, and
f(x)
is not stable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Case 2:
q\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)

In this case, we show that
\Delta_{n}
a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n}}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume towards a contradiction that the claim is false. Then there exists a smallest
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Delta_{n_{0}}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n_{0}}}}
. If
n_{0}-1=0
, then
\Delta_{n_{0}-1}=\Delta_{0}
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
n_{0}-1\geq 1
, then by minimality of
n_{0}
,
\Delta_{n_{0}-1}
is again a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n_{0}-1}}}
. In either case, we have that

\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n_{0}}}}\cong\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n_{0}-1}}}[x]/\penalty\left<x^{2}-\Delta_{n_{0}-1}\right>\cong\left\{u+vx_{0}:\ u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n_{0}-1}}}\right\}

where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n_{0}}}}
and
x_{0}^{2}=\Delta_{n_{0}-1}
. Since
\Delta_{n_{0}}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{2^{n_{0}}}}

\Delta_{n_{0}}=(u+vx_{0})^{2}

4x_{0}^{2}=4\Delta_{n_{0}-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{Eq1})}}{{=}}\Delta_{n_{0}}^{2}

2x_{0}=\epsilon\Delta_{n_{0}}=\epsilon(u+vx_{0})^{2}=\epsilon\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\Delta_{n_{0}-1}\right)+2\epsilon uvx_{0}

The second equation implies that

Proposition 4.1

(

)

If

,

If

Having done all the preparatory work, we now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}

is even as the other case can be proven in the same way. Let

. Following the idea introduced in
Section

2

(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 0}

\alpha_{0}=0
and that
f(\alpha_{n})=\alpha_{n-1}\ (n\in\mathbb{N})

In this section, we give examples of stable quartic polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
where

is an odd prime power. To begin with, we recall the following result.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 8]
)
.

Let
q
be an odd prime power and let
f(x)
be a polynomial of degree
d
which is a product of
r
pairwise distinct irreducible polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
r\equiv d\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)
iff the discriminant of
f
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

The following is an immediate consequence of
5.1
.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
q
is a prime power and
q\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
, then
f(x)=x^{4}-a
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff
a
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof
Since
4\mid q-1
,
-1
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
f(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then by
5.1
, the discriminant of
f(x)
, i.e.,
D(f)=256(-a)^{3}=(-1)(16a)^{2}a
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and that requires
a
to be a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Conversely, if
a
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, so is
D(f)
. By
5.1
,
f(x)
is either irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
or factored as a product of two linear factors and an irreducible quadratic factor over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The latter case cannot happen. Indeed, assume that

\displaystyle x^{4}-a

\displaystyle=\left(x^{2}+rx+s\right)\left(x^{2}+tx+u\right)

\displaystyle=x^{4}+(r+t)x^{3}+(rt+s+u)x^{2}+(ru+st)x+su

where
r,s,t,u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Matching coefficients of corresponding terms, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}r+t&=0\\
rt+s+u&=0\\
ru+st&=0\\
su&=-a\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\begin{cases}t&=-r\\
s+u&=r^{2}\\
r(-s+u)&=0\\
su&=-a\end{cases}

If
r=0
, then
u=-s
. So
a=-su=s^{2}
. If
r\neq 0
, then
u=s
. So
a=(-1)s^{2}
. In both cases,
a
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is impossible. Therefore
f(x)
is irreducible.

\square

Based on

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

be a prime power such that

Assume that

If

\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Therefore, we know that
bf(x)=(b(x+a))^{4}-ab

\mathbb{F}_{q}

Conversely, assume that

\mathbb{F}_{q}

2

By
2.8

is stable over

is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n}}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We see from the change of variables

g_{n}(y)=y^{4}-\beta_{n}

is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n}}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where

\beta_{n}=b(\alpha_{n}+a)

By
5.2
,
f(x)
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff
\beta_{n}
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n}}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\beta_{0}=ab

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Assume towards a contradiction that the claim is false. Then there exists a smallest

\beta_{n_{0}}

If

\mathbb{F}_{q}

\beta_{n_{0}-1}
is again a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n_{0}-1}}}

is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n_{0}-1}}}
. If
x_{0}

in
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n_{0}}}}

\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n_{0}}}}\cong\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n_{0}-1}}}[x]/\penalty\left<g_{n_{0}-1}(x)\right>\cong\left\{r+sx_{0}+tx_{0}^{2}+ux_{0}^{3}:\ r,s,t,u\in\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n_{0}-1}}}\right\}

\beta_{n}^{4}=\beta_{n-1}

In particular, we know that

\begin{cases}r^{p}&=r\beta_{n_{0}-1}^{k}\\

(14)

\beta_{n_{0}-1}=vr^{8}

. As before, we construct a sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 0}

and that

. For each

f_{n}(x)=f(x)-\alpha_{n}=b^{3}(x+a)^{4}+c(x+a)-(\alpha_{n}+a)

By

,

iff

is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{{q}^{4^{n}}}
for each

To proceed, we now prove a theorem describing the factorization patterns of the polynomials

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
q=3^{m}
and

. If
g(x)=x^{3}-dx-c^{2}
and

(1)

h(x)

\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff

such that

are squares in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
;

(2)

h(x)
is factored as a product of two irreducible quadratic factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff
g\left(u_{j}\right)=0\ (1\leq j\leq 3)
for some pairwise distinct

such that
u_{1},u_{2}
are non-squares in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and that
u_{3}=r^{2}
for some
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
-r^{2}\pm cr^{-1}
are non-squares in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
;

(3)

h(x)
has a unique root
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff
g(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
;

(4)

h(x)
has exactly two roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
iff
g(x)
has a unique root
r^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}

r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
r^{4}-c^{2}r^{-2}
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}

h(x)

has a unique root which is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof
First, we observe that if
h(x)
is factored into a product of two (possibly reducible) quadratic factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\displaystyle x^{4}+cx+d

\displaystyle=\left(x^{2}+rx+s\right)\left(x^{2}+tx+u\right)

\displaystyle=x^{4}+(r+t)x^{3}+(rt+s+u)x^{2}+(ru+st)x+su

Then we have that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}r+t&=0\\
rt+s+u&=0\\
ru+st&=c\\
su&=d\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\begin{cases}t&=-r\\
s+u&=r^{2}\\
r(-s+u)&=c\\
su&=d\end{cases}

By the third equation,

. Solving the second and third equation for

. These, together with the fourth equation, imply that

. Thus,

iff there is an

and the latter claim is true iff

has a root which is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We also note that the discriminant of the first quadratic factor in

First, we prove item (

is separable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Assume that

has four pairwise distinct roots. Then it is easy to check that they give rise to exactly three pairwise distinct factorizations in the form of

Next, we prove Item (

h(x)

\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By

either has three roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
or is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The latter case cannot happen since we have shown that
g(x)
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence,
g(x)
has two other roots in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
which must be non-squares in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(because if one of them is a square, then

into irreducible factors over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

Then we prove Item (

[

or is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

, giving rise to a factorization of

into two quadratic factors via

g
must be irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The other direction is straightforward.

Now we prove Item (
4
). If
h(x)
is factored as a product of two linear factors and an irreducible quadratic factor, then there is an
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying
Eq.

15
such that
r^{2}
is a root of
g(x)
and that exactly one of
-r^{2}\pm cr^{-1}
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Meanwhile,
5.1
implies that the discriminant of
h(x)
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus,
d
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and by
[
12
, Theorem 2]
,
g(x)
has no other root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The other direction is straightforward.

Finally, we prove Item (
5
). If
h(x)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then like in the previous proof,
5.1
and
[
12
, Theorem 2]
imply that
g(x)
has a unique root
u
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since no
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfies
Eq.

15
,
u
is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

\square

Let
a,b,c
and
(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be as previously defined. Then
5.4
leads to the following result.

Corollary 5.5
.

The polynomial
f(x)=b^{3}(x+a)^{4}+c(x+a)-a
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{{3}^{m}}
iff for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
x^{3}-\beta_{n}x-c^{2}
has a unique root which is a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{{3}^{4^{n}m}}
.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, several classes of stable shift-resistant polynomials of degrees
2,3
and
4
are constructed by repeated use of Capelli’s lemma. As a result, we confirm
the conjecture of Ahmadi and Monsef-Shokri
[
2
]
that the polynomial
f(x)=x^{3}+x^{2}+1
is stable over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
When
q
is a prime power and
q\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
, an improvement to the lower bound of the number of stable quadratic polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is obtained. No lower bound of the number of stable polynomials of an arbitrary degree
d
over finite fields seems to have been established (although upper bounds have been studied in
[
4
, Theorem 1]
and
[
5
, Theorem 5.5]
using character sums). For the future study, we are hopeful that based on the stability test in
Section

2
, stable quadratic polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be constructed when
q\equiv 3\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 4)
and examples of stable polynomials of degree
\geq 5
can be found. Also, we are working towards establishing a better estimate on the number of stable polynomials over finite fields.

k k -Contraction in a Generalized Lurie System
Ron Ofir, Jean-Jacques Slotine, Michael Margaliot

Abstract
We derive a sufficient condition for k k -contraction in a generalized Lurie system (GLS), that is, the feedback connection of a nonlinear dynamical system and a memoryless nonlinear function. For k = 1 k=1 , this reduces to a sufficient condition for standard contraction. For k = 2 k=2 , this condition implies that every bounded solution of the GLS converges to an equilibrium, which is not necessarily unique. We demonstrate the theoretical results by analyzing k k -contraction in a biochemical control circuit with nonlinear dissipation terms.

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\left(\tilde{J}_{cl}(x)+\tilde{J}_{cl}^{\mathsf{T}}(x)\right)\leq-\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}\text{ for all }x.

Proof:

To simplify the notation, let

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(x,u)

\displaystyle:=\Theta(x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x,u)\Theta^{-1}(x),

\displaystyle\;\tilde{B}(x,u)

\displaystyle:=\Theta(x)\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}(x,u),

\displaystyle\tilde{C}(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(x)\Theta^{-1}(x),

\displaystyle J_{\Phi}(y)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y}(y).

By (

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{cl}(x)

\displaystyle=\Theta(x)J_{cl}(x)\Theta^{-1}(x)+\dot{\Theta}_{cl}(x)\Theta^{-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{A}(x,-\Phi(g(x)))+\dot{\Theta}_{cl}(x)\Theta^{-1}(x)

\displaystyle-\tilde{B}(x,-\Phi(g(x)))J_{\Phi}(g(x))\tilde{C}(x).

Omitting the arguments of the Jacobians and of
P

that
(\tilde{J}_{cl}+\tilde{J}_{cl}^{\mathsf{T}})^{[k]}
is equal to

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{A}+\tilde{A}^{\mathsf{T}}+\dot{\Theta}_{cl}\Theta^{-1}+\Theta^{-\mathsf{T}}\dot{\Theta}_{cl}^{\mathsf{T}}-\tilde{B}J_{\Phi}\tilde{C}-\tilde{C}^{\mathsf{T}}J_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)^{[k]}

\displaystyle\preceq-\eta_{1}I_{r}-\left(\tilde{B}\tilde{B}^{\mathsf{T}}+\tilde{C}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{C}+\tilde{B}J_{\Phi}\tilde{C}+\tilde{C}^{\mathsf{T}}J_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{B}^{\mathsf{T}}\right)^{[k]}

(20)

\displaystyle=-\eta_{1}I_{r}-\left(\tilde{B}\tilde{B}^{\mathsf{T}}+\tilde{C}^{\mathsf{T}}\tilde{C}+(\tilde{B}+\tilde{C}^{\mathsf{T}}J_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{T}})(\tilde{B}+\tilde{C}^{\mathsf{T}}J_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{T}})^{\mathsf{T}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.-\tilde{B}\tilde{B}^{\mathsf{T}}-\tilde{C}^{\mathsf{T}}J_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{T}}J_{\Phi}\tilde{C}\right)^{[k]}

\displaystyle\preceq-\eta_{1}I_{r}+\left(\tilde{C}^{\mathsf{T}}(J_{\Phi}^{\mathsf{T}}J_{\Phi}-I_{p})\tilde{C}\right)^{[k]}.

Now if (

(21)

V-A

k
-contraction w.r.t. a state-dependent metric for tridiagonal systems

One possible approach for determining a suitable metric
\Theta(x)
, based on considering (
16
) and (
13
), is to find a
\Theta(x)
such that
\Theta\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\Theta^{-1}
is skew-symmetric. We now demonstrate an application of this idea to a specific class of dynamical systems. Let
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{n}
denote the canonical basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Consider the system (
11
) with

f(x,u)=\begin{bmatrix}h_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\\
h_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\\
\vdots\\
h_{n-1}(x_{n-2},x_{n-1},x_{n})\\
h_{n}(x_{n-1},x_{n})\end{bmatrix}+\xi^{m}u,

and
y=(\xi^{\ell})^{\top}x
, for some
m,\ell\in[1,n]
.
The vector field here corresponds for example to a
set of agents ordered on a 1D chain such that every agent directly communicates only with its two nearest neighbors.
This is accompanied by a nonlinear static feedback
\Phi(y)
from
x_{\ell}
to
\dot{x}_{m}
.

Assume that every
h_{i}
is
C^{2}
, and let
h_{i,j}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}h_{i}
. Assume also
that there exist
s_{1},s_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle s_{1}<-h_{i,i+1}<s_{2}\text{ and }s_{1}<h_{i+1,i}<s_{2}\text{ for all }i,x.

(27)

In particular,
\dot{x}_{i}
increases [decreases] when
x_{i-1}
[
x_{i+1}
] increases.
The Jacobian
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
is the tridiagonal matrix

\begin{bmatrix}h_{1,1}&h_{1,2}&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
h_{2,1}&h_{2,2}&h_{2,3}&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
&&&&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&h_{n-1,n-2}&h_{n-1,n-1}&h_{n-1,n}\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&h_{n,n-1}&h_{n,n}\end{bmatrix}.

Define

\Theta(x):=\operatorname{diag}(\delta_{1}(x),\dots,\delta_{n}(x))

(28)

with
\delta_{1}(x)=1
,
\delta_{2}(x)=\sqrt{-h_{1,2}(x)/h_{2,1}(x)}
,
\delta_{3}(x)=\delta_{2}(x)\sqrt{-h_{2,3}(x)/h_{3,2}(x)}
, and so on. Then

M(x):=\Theta(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x)\Theta^{-1}(x)
is the sum of a diagonal and a skew-symmetric matrix,
so
M+M^{T}=2\operatorname{diag}(h_{1,1},\dots,h_{n,n})
.
Since
\Theta(x)
is diagonal, so is
\dot{\Theta}_{ol}(x)\Theta^{-1}(x)
.
Eq. (
13
) implies that
\tilde{J}_{ol}+\tilde{J}_{ol}^{\top}
is diagonal, with

(\tilde{J}_{ol}+\tilde{J}_{ol}^{\top})_{i,i}=2h_{i,i}+\left(\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\delta_{i}\right)^{\top}f+f^{\top}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\delta_{i}\right)\right)\delta^{-1}_{i}.

Also,
\Theta\frac{\partial f}{\partial u}\frac{\partial f^{\mathsf{T}}}{\partial u}\Theta^{\mathsf{T}}
is diagonal with all entries zero except for
\delta_{m}^{2}
at entry
(m,m)
, and
\Theta^{-\mathsf{T}}\frac{\partial g^{\mathsf{T}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}\Theta^{-1}

is diagonal with all entries zero except for
\delta_{\ell}^{-2}
at entry
(\ell,\ell)
. Thus, in this case
H
in (
15
) is
diagonal
, and determining the value
\eta_{1}
such that (
16
) holds is greatly simplified.

Also, conditions
17

and (
18
) simplify to

\delta_{m}^{2}((\Phi^{\prime})^{2}-1)\leq-\eta_{2}

and

\delta_{\ell}^{-2}((\Phi^{\prime})^{2}-1)\leq-\eta_{2}
,
respectively.

Summarizing, when the nonlinear sub-system in the GLS is a tridiagonal system satisfying (
27
) then there exists a natural state-dependent metric for analyzing
k
-contraction.

V-B

Networked systems

We describe an application of Thm.
1
to the general nonlinear networked system:

\dot{x}(t)=-d(x(t))+W_{1}f\left(W_{2}x(t)\right)+v,

(29)

where

x\in\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
d(x)=\begin{bmatrix}d_{1}(x_{1})&\dots&d_{n}(x_{n})\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}
,

W_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m},W_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times n}
are matrices of interconnection weights,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a constant “offset” vector, and
f:\mathbb{R}^{q}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We assume that the state space
\Omega
is convex, and that
f
and
d
are
C^{1}
.
Let

J_{f}(z):=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z_{1}}(z)&\dots&\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z_{q}}(z)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\\
\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial z_{1}}(z)&\dots&\frac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial z_{q}}(z)\end{bmatrix}

denote the Jacobian of the vector field
f
.

Note that (
29
) cannot be expressed naturally as a standard Lurie system, as it does not include a linear sub-system.

Equations of the form (
29
) are common in recurrent neural network models.
In this context, the function
f
in (
29
) is typically diagonal, that is,
q=m
and

f(z)=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(z_{1})&\dots&f_{q}(z_{q})\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}},

where the
f_{i}
s are the neuron activation functions. Thus,
J_{f}(z)
is diagonal.
More generally, the
f_{i}
s may represent functions that are bounded or saturated,
and are thus nonlinear.

Typically,
d(x)
represents nonlinear dissipation and this will be used to establish
k
-contraction.
Intuitively, increasing the dissipation should make
the system “more stable”. This is formalized in
the next result.

Theorem 2
.

Consider system (
29
). Fix
k\in[1,n]
. Define

\alpha_{k}:=k^{-1}\inf\big\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}d^{\prime}_{i_{j}}(x_{i_{j}})\,|\,x\in\Omega,1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n\big\}.

(30)

If
\alpha_{k}>0
and

{\sup_{x\in\Omega}}\|J_{f}(W_{2}x)\|_{2}^{2}\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2}(W_{1})\sigma_{i}^{2}(W_{2})\ <\ \alpha_{k}^{2}k

(31)

then (
29
) is
k
-contracting w.r.t. the
L_{2}
norm.
Furthermore, if these conditions hold for
k=2
then every bounded trajectory of (
29
) converges to an equilibrium point (which is not necessarily unique).

Remark 4
.

The condition
\alpha_{k}>0
amounts to requiring that for any
x\in\Omega

the sum of every
k
eigenvalues of the matrix

D(x):=\operatorname{diag}(d^{\prime}_{1}(x_{1}),\dots,d^{\prime}_{n}(x_{n}))

(32)

is positive. In particular, for
k=1
, this amounts to requiring that
D(x)
is a positive diagonal matrix for any
x\in\Omega
, but for
k>1
some of the
d_{i}^{\prime}
s may be negative, as long as the sum of every
k
of the
d_{i}^{\prime}
s is positive.

It is natural to expect that a sufficient condition for
k
-contraction implies
j
-contraction for any
j>k
. Note that if
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
with
a_{1}\geq\dots\geq a_{n}
, and
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
then

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}-\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}a_{i}\geq\frac{a_{k}-a_{k+1}}{k+1}\geq 0.

Using this
it is straightforward to verify that if (
31
) holds for some
k
then it also holds for
k+1
, and thus for any
j>k
.

Proof:

By (
31
), there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\gamma<\alpha_{k}\text{ and }{\sup_{x\in\Omega}}\|J_{f}(W_{2}x)\|_{2}^{2}\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2}(W_{1})\sigma_{i}^{2}(W_{2})<\gamma^{2}k.

(33)

Represent (
29
) as
a GLS, namely, the interconnection of the nonlinear system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=-d(x)+v+\gamma u,\quad y=x,

(34)

and the time-invariant nonlinearity

\Phi(y):=-\gamma^{-1}W_{1}f(W_{2}y),

(35)

where the input to the dynamical system is
u=\Phi(y)
.
We now apply Thm.
1

to this GLS. Let
\Theta=qI
, with
q>0
. Then (
16
) becomes

-2\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(D(x))+kq^{2}\gamma^{2}+kq^{-2}\leq-\eta_{1},

(36)

with
D
as in (
32
).
By (
30
),
k\alpha_{k}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(D(x))
,
so (
36
) will hold if

\left(-2\alpha_{k}+\gamma^{2}p+p^{-1}\right)k\leq-\eta_{1},

with
p:=q^{2}
.
Taking
p:=\alpha_{k}\gamma^{-2}
gives that (
36
) indeed holds for
\eta_{1}=(\alpha_{k}^{2}-\gamma^{2})/\alpha_{k}
, and (
33
) implies that
\eta_{1}>0
.

To verify that condition (
18
)
in Thm.
1

also holds, note that in (
34
) we have
y=g(x)=x
, so we can use the approach in Remark
3
.
Using Lemma
2

and (
33
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2}(J_{\Phi})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2}(-\gamma^{-1}W_{1}J_{f}W_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2}(W_{1}J_{f})\sigma_{i}^{2}(W_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{-2}\sigma_{1}^{2}(J_{f})\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_{i}^{2}(W_{1})\sigma_{i}^{2}(W_{2})

\displaystyle<k.

Thus,
there exists
\eta_{2}>0

such that (
26
) holds,
and Thm.

implies that (

) is
k
-contracting. Since

, the closed-loop system
is
k
-contracting w.r.t. the (unweighted)

norm.

If (
31

then (

[

∎

A biochemical control circuit

Consider the feedback system

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}

\displaystyle=-d_{1}(x_{1})+r(x_{n}),

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{2}

\displaystyle=-d_{2}(x_{2})+x_{1},

(37)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{n}

\displaystyle=-d_{n}(x_{n})+x_{n-1},

with
d_{i},r:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
continuously differentiable.

Remark 7
.

Ref.
[
54
, Chap. 4]
used this model, in the particular
case of linear dissipation, that is,

d_{i}(x_{i})=c_{i}x_{i}
,
c_{i}>0
,
and the positive feedback function

r(s)=(1+s)/(\zeta+s)
,
with
\zeta>1
,
as a model of the
control of protein synthesis in the cell. Every
x_{i}
represents the concentration of mRNA molecules or enzymes or products, so the state space is
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.
This system was also analyzed using contraction theory in
[
38
]
.

We analyze the more general case of not necessarily linear dissipation and a general feedback function
r
using Thm.
2
. Write (
V-C
) as the networked system (
29
)
with
d(x)=\begin{bmatrix}d_{1}(x_{1})&\dots&d_{n}(x_{n})\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}
,
W_{1}=W_{2}=I_{n}
,
v=0
, and
f(y)=\begin{bmatrix}r(y_{n})&y_{1}&\dots&y_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}
. Thus,

\|J_{f}(W_{2}x)\|_{2}^{2}=\max\{(r^{\prime}(x_{n}))^{2},1\}.

The next result follows from Thm.
2
.

Corollary 1
.

Assume that
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is an invariant set of (
V-C
). Fix
k\in[1,n]
. Suppose that
\alpha_{k}>1
,
and that

\max_{x_{n}\geq 0}(r^{\prime}(x_{n}))^{2}<\alpha_{k}^{2}.

(38)

Then (
V-C
) is
k
-contracting on
\Omega
.

Corollary
1
provides a simple and easy to check sufficient condition for
k
-contraction for any
k\in[1,n]
.
Note that this condition depends on a balance between the “
k
-total dissipation”
\alpha_{k}
on the one hand,
and the maximal derivative of the feedback function
r
on the other hand. Also, the analysis in
[
54
, Chapter 4]
is based on the theory of irreducible cooperative systems, and this requires that
r^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
for all
s\geq 0
. Corollary
1
does not constrain the sign of
r^{\prime}
. In particular,
r^{\prime}(s)
may have a different sign for different values of
s
.

As a specific example, consider (
V-C
) with
n=3
,
d_{1}(x_{1})=\sin(x_{1})+(1/2)
,
d_{2}(x_{2})=3x_{2}
,
d_{3}(x_{3})=3x_{3}
, and
r(s)=(1+s)/(2+s)
, that is,

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\sin(x_{1})-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1+x_{3}}{2+x_{3}},

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{2}

\displaystyle=-3x_{2}+x_{1},

(39)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{3}

\displaystyle=-3x_{3}+x_{2}.

Since
r(s)\geq 1/2
for all
s\geq 0
, we have that
\dot{x}_{1}\geq-\sin(x_{1})
, implying that
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
is an invariant set of
(
V-C
)
.

A point

is an equilibrium of (
V-C
) if
e_{2}=3e_{3}
,
e_{1}=3e_{2}=9e_{3}
,
and

\sin(9e_{3})+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1+e_{3}}{2+e_{3}}.

It is straightforward to verify that there are infinitely many equilibrium points in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
,
so the system is not contracting (i.e., not
1
-contracting) w.r.t. any norm.
Consider the conditions in Corollary
1
for
k=2
. Here,

\alpha_{2}=3/2>1,

and

r^{\prime}(x_{3})=(2+x_{3})^{-2},
so
r^{\prime}(x_{3})\leq 1/4
for all
x_{3}\geq 0
, and (

VI
Conclusion

A GLS is
the feedback connection of nonlinear dynamical system and a nonlinear feedback function. GLSs typically have more than a single equilibrium and are
thus not
1
-contracting w.r.t. any norm.
If they are
2
-contracting then
every bounded solution converges to an equilibrium (which is not necessarily unique)
and establishing such a global property is important in many applications. For example, in a neural network model of an associative memory every stored memory pattern corresponds to an equilibrium, and
2
-contraction implies that for any initial condition
the solution will indeed converge to some stored pattern.

Topics for further research include: (1) a given nonlinear system can often be represented as a GLS in several ways. How can one find the “best” possible representation?
(2) our results only consider
k
-contraction w.r.t. (scaled)
L_{2}
norms and it may be of interest to analyze
k
-contraction w.r.t. other norms; and
(3) Thm.
2

provides a sufficient condition for
k
-contraction that uses the singular values of the matrices
W_{1}
,
W_{2}
in the networked system (
29
).
In other words, it is based on the spectral properties of the connection graphs (see also Remark
6
). An interesting research direction is to further develop this into a graph-theoretic approach to
k
-contraction.

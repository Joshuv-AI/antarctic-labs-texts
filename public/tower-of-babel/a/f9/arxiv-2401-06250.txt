Abstract

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

In this paper we follow up on our work in
[
5
]
by analyzing the possible values for the proportions of so-called anomalous primes (as introduced in
[
5
]
). Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be 2-isogenous elliptic curves defined over
\mathbf{Q}
. We call a good prime
p>2
for
E
and
E^{\prime}

anomalous
if
E(\mathbf{F}_{p})\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p})
but
E(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}})\not\simeq E^{\prime}(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}})
.

The proportion that we study in this paper is defined concretely as

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})=\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\text{anomalous}~p~\text{for $E$ and $E^{\prime}$}\leq X\}}{\pi(X)}\mathchar 59\relax

where

is the prime-counting function. That this limit exists is ultimately due to the Chebatorev Density Theorem, applied to the images

and
G^{\prime}
of the 2-adic representations of

and
E^{\prime}
, respectively.

Because
E
and
E^{\prime}
are 2-isogenous over
\mathbf{Q}
, each has a rational 2-torsion point and so

and
G^{\prime}
each have index at least

in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
. When
[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2}):G]=[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2}):G^{\prime}]=3

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})=1/30

[
5
, Thm. 1.3.4]
. The purpose of this paper is to extend that result to all pairs of rationally 2-isogenous elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
and to explore some of the arithmetic consequences.

We break the large task of working out
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
for every pair
(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
of 2-isogenous elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
into two main steps; we set

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\mathrm{head}}+\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\mathrm{tail}}

and evaluate each term separately. We delay a formal definition of
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\mathrm{head}}
and
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\mathrm{tail}}

1.2.
Background and First Definitions

In order to efficiently state our main results, we start by recalling some of the terminology of
[
5
]

are 2-isogenous, the only way we can have

Definition 1.3
.

We say that an anomalous prime

has
defect

(a\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}b)
if

,

has full

-torsion but not full

-torsion, and
E(\mathbf{F}_{p^{2}})
has full
2^{b}
-torsion, but not full

-torsion.

We proved in
[

that the defect of a prime can only be of the form
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
or
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
, where

. We organize primes by defect via the 2-adic representation. Let

and
F^{\prime}
be matrices that represent the Frobenius endomorphism on

and
E^{\prime}
, respectively, as elements of

Proposition 1.4
.

A good prime

for

and
E^{\prime}
has defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
if and only if

\displaystyle F\equiv-I\pmod{2^{m}}\text{ and }F\not\equiv-I\pmod{2^{m+1}}\mathchar 59\relax\text{ and}

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\equiv-I\pmod{2^{m-1}}\text{ and }F^{\prime}\not\equiv-I\pmod{2^{m}}\mathchar 59\relax

with similar congruences for primes of defect
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)

Remark 1.5
.

Proposition

was proved in
[
5
, §5.1]
with the stated hypothesis that

and
G^{\prime}
have index 3 in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
. In fact, this hypothesis is not used in the proof.

Definition 1.6
.

Let

. We define

to be the proportion of primes such that
F^{\prime}\not\equiv-I\pmod{2^{k}}
given that
F\equiv-I\pmod{2^{k}}
. Similarly, we define
c_{2^{k}}^{\prime}
to be the proportion of primes such that
F\not\equiv-I\pmod{2^{k}}
, given that
F^{\prime}\equiv-I\pmod{2^{k}}
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
denote the respective images of the 2-adic representations of
E
and
E^{\prime}
, and
G(2^{k})
and

representations. When

have index 3 in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
, we determined that

by proving that

for all

and then expressing

as the sum of Chebotarev densities

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})=\sum_{k\geq 2}\left(\frac{c_{2^{k}}}{|G(2^{k})|}+\frac{c^{\prime}_{2^{k}}}{|G^{\prime}(2^{k})|}\right).

If

and

have index 3 in

, from which the value of 1/30 follows immediately by summing a geometric series. By Proposition

1.3.
Main Results

With our terminology in place, we can now turn to the main business of the paper. Our first result is on the constants

and
c_{2^{k}}^{\prime}
.

Proposition 1.8
.

For all

, the constants

and

of (

c_{2^{k}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}c_{2^{k}}^{\prime}\in\{0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1/2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1\}

Roughly, the proof amounts to re-interpreting the conditional probabilities

in terms of certain field extensions. The fact that their values are restricted to

,

Turning to the calculation of (
1.7

is an elliptic curve and
-I\in G(32)
, then
-I\in G

[

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\mathrm{head}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{4}\left(\frac{c_{2^{k}}}{|G(2^{k})|}+\frac{c_{2^{k}}^{\prime}}{|G^{\prime}(2^{k})|}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\mathrm{tail}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 5}\left(\frac{c_{2^{k}}}{|G(2^{k})|}+\frac{c_{2^{k}}^{\prime}}{|G^{\prime}(2^{k})|}\right)\mathchar 59\relax

and prove the following.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
E
and
E^{\prime}
be 2-isogenous elliptic curves defined over
\mathbf{Q}
with 2-adic Galois images
G
and
G^{\prime}
, respectively. Suppose

and

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\mathrm{tail}}=0

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\mathrm{tail}}=\frac{8}{15}\left(\frac{1}{|G(32)|}+\frac{1}{|G^{\prime}(32)|}\right).

The proof of Theorem

comes down to showing that either

•

for every

we have
c_{2^{k}}=c^{\prime}_{2^{k}}=0
(which occurs if and only if

for every

This observation relies on the fact that for elliptic curves over

Remark 1.10
.

Theorem

gives an easy procedure for computing

If an elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
has CM, then so does any
\mathbf{Q}
-isogenous elliptic curve. Thus, when considering the “CM case” we can safely assume that both

E^{\prime}
have CM. If

and

have CM, then the formulas for

Theorem 1.11

Let

E^{\prime}
be 2-isogenous elliptic curves over

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})=0

Remark 1.12

In
[

Remark 1.13

In all cases, if

Background

2.1.
Prior Work

We start with a brief contextualization of this project. Let

are
\ell

[

•

This explains our interest in elliptic curves where

and

Given the situation when

and

. In
[
5

we took the point of view of “fix

and

\mathbf{Q}
for which the isogeny is also defined over
\mathbf{Q}
(we call such a pair

and

have a well-defined proportion. Under the condition that

are maximal in

, we showed that this proportion exists and is computable. It will follow from our work in this paper that

always exists and is computable, due to the fact that the

As already noted, when

\displaystyle\frac{1}{30}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{32}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{32\cdot 16}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{32\cdot 16^{m}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{32}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{32\cdot 16}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{32\cdot 16^{m}}+\cdots\right).

The parenthetical expressions are identical, owing to the fact that

are isomorphic when they have index 3 in

. Within each expression the factors of 1/2 are the

is due to the maximality of

it must also be the case that
F\equiv-I\pmod{2^{m}}
as well. Therefore, there cannot exist primes of defect
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
and so
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=0
. Similarly, if
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
then we must have
c_{2^{m}}=0
.
∎

Four cases remain:
[KK^{\prime}:K]=[LL^{\prime}:L]=1
,
[KK^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]=[LL^{\prime}:L^{\prime}]=1
,
[KK^{\prime}:K]=[LL^{\prime}:L]=2
, and
[KK^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]=[LL^{\prime}:L^{\prime}]=2
. Each of these cases can occur and yields coefficients
c_{2^{m}}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}\in\{0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1/2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1\}
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Suppose
G(2^{m})
and
G^{\prime}(2^{m})
each contain
-I\pmod{2^{m}}
. With all field notation as above, we have the following cases:

(1)

Suppose
[KK^{\prime}:K]=[LL^{\prime}:L]=1
.

(a)

If
K^{\prime}=K
then
c_{2^{m}}=c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=0
.

(b)

If
K^{\prime}\neq K
then
c_{2^{m}}=0
and
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=1/2
.

(2)

Suppose
[KK^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]=[LL^{\prime}:L^{\prime}]=1
.

(a)

If
K^{\prime}=K
then
c_{2^{m}}=c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=0
.

(b)

If
K^{\prime}\neq K
then
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=0
and
c_{2^{m}}=1/2
.

(3)

Suppose
[KK^{\prime}:K]=[LL^{\prime}:L]=2
.

(a)

If
K\subseteq K^{\prime}
then
c_{2^{m}}=1/2
and
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=0
.

(b)

If
K\not\subseteq K^{\prime}
then
c_{2^{m}}=c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=1/2
.

(4)

Suppose
[KK^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]=[LL^{\prime}:L^{\prime}]=2
.

(a)

If
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
then
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=1/2
and
c_{2^{m}}=0
.

(b)

If
K^{\prime}\not\subseteq K
then
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=c_{2^{m}}=1/2
.

Proof.

We prove cases (1) and (3), since (2) and (4) follow by identical arguments. Recall our standing assumption that
[L\colon K]=[L^{\prime}\colon K^{\prime}]=2
; it will be used throughout this proof.

Suppose
[KK^{\prime}:K]=[LL^{\prime}:L]=1
. Then we have
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
, hence
c_{2^{m}}=0
by Lemma
3.14
. If, in addition,
K\subseteq K^{\prime}
so that
K=K^{\prime}
, then
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=0
. This proves 1(a). Now consider the case of 1(b) and suppose
K\neq K^{\prime}
. Then
[KK^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]=2
, hence

X^{\prime}=\frac{2}{2}-\frac{1}{[LL^{\prime}:L^{\prime}]}.

If we can show
[LL^{\prime}:L^{\prime}]=2
, we are done. Since

[LL^{\prime}\colon K]=[LL^{\prime}\colon KK^{\prime}][KK^{\prime}\colon K]=[LL^{\prime}\colon L][L\colon K]=2\mathchar 59\relax

we have
2=[LL^{\prime}\colon KK^{\prime}]
. But this gives

[LL^{\prime}\colon K^{\prime}]=[LL^{\prime}\colon L^{\prime}][L^{\prime}\colon K^{\prime}]=[LL^{\prime}\colon KK^{\prime}][KK^{\prime}\colon K^{\prime}]=4.

So
[L^{\prime}\colon K^{\prime}]=2
implies
[LL^{\prime}\colon L^{\prime}]=2
, proving 1(b).

Now we turn to case 3. Since
[KK^{\prime}:K]=[LL^{\prime}:L]=2
, we immediately have
c_{2^{m}}=1/2
by Proposition
3.6
. Thus

[LL^{\prime}:K]=[LL^{\prime}:L][L:K]=4\mathchar 59\relax

from which it follows that
[LL^{\prime}:KK^{\prime}]=2
. Now we consider possibilities 3(a) and 3(b) separately: either
K\subseteq K^{\prime}
or
K\not\subseteq K^{\prime}
.

If
K\subseteq K^{\prime}
, then
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=0
by Lemma
3.14
, proving 3(a). If
K\not\subseteq K^{\prime}
then
KK^{\prime}\neq K^{\prime}
, whence
[KK^{\prime}:K^{\prime}]=2
. Together with
[LL^{\prime}:KK^{\prime}]=2
, we see that
[LL^{\prime}:L^{\prime}]=2
and so
X^{\prime}=2/2-1/2=1/2=c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}
, proving 3(b).
∎

Our next proposition shows that if the proportion of primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
or
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
is zero, then there are exactly zero primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
or
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
, respectively. We use this result in the proof of Theorem
1.11
, where we show that if there exists a single prime of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
, then there must be a positive proportion.

Proposition 3.16
.

Suppose there exists a prime of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
or
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
. Then the proportion of primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
or
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
, respectively, is positive.

Proof.

If there exists a prime of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
, then in the group
G(2^{m})\times G^{\prime}(2^{m})
the Frobenius at such a prime is represented by the pair
(-I\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}F^{\prime})
, where
F^{\prime}
is conjugate in
G(2^{m})
to
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&2^{m-1}\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the conjugacy class of
G(2^{m})\times G^{\prime}(2^{m})
to which
(-I\mathchar 59\relax F^{\prime})
belongs. Observe that conjugation on each component preserves the fact that the the first component acts as
-I
and the second does not.

The Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(LL^{\prime}/\mathbf{Q})
is a subgroup of
G(2^{m})\times G^{\prime}(2^{m})
and, because there exists a prime of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
, intersects
\mathcal{C}
nontrivially. Thus the class in
\operatorname{Gal}(LL^{\prime}/\mathbf{Q})
containing
(-I\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}F^{\prime})
is nonempty and by the Chebatorev Density Theorem the primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
will have a positive density.
∎

Remark 3.17
.

\operatorname{Gal}(LL^{\prime}/\mathbf{Q})

and

(which shows that

we have
[KK^{\prime}:K]=[LL^{\prime}:L]=2
,
K\not\subseteq K^{\prime}
, and

.

Example 3.18
.

Let
E
be the elliptic curve
277440.dv5
and
E^{\prime}
the curve

. Set

. Then

and

.

Let

be the elliptic curve
2304.i1
and

the curve

. These curves have CM and both

.

Now set

and consider the fields

,

,

, and

. We check that

while

of the polynomial

4.
Elliptic Curves without CM

We assume throughout this section that
E
and
E^{\prime}
do not have CM so that
G
and
G^{\prime}
are finite index subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
of level at most 32. Recall that we write

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm head}+\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm tail}.

One of the key distinctions in
[
15
]
on the classification of 2-adic images of elliptic curves over
\mathbf{Q}
is between those groups that contain
-I
and those that do not. The next lemma shows that this is an important distinction for us as well.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
G
and
G^{\prime}
do not contain
-I
. Then
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm tail}=0
.

Proof.

If
-I\not\in G
(resp.
G^{\prime}
), then
-I\not\in G(32)
(resp.
G^{\prime}(32)
) and therefore
-I\not\in G(2^{m})
(resp.
G^{\prime}(2^{m})
) for
m\geq 5
. By our review in Section
2.2
, we conclude there can be no anomalous primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
or
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
for
m\geq 5
, and so
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm tail}=0
.
∎

On the other hand, if
-I
is in both
G
and
G^{\prime}
then we are guaranteed the existence of primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
and
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
for
m\geq 5
, as the next proposition shows.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
G
and
G^{\prime}
contain
-I
. Then there exist primes of defect
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
and
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
for all
m\geq 5
.

Proof.

Since
G
and
G^{\prime}
contain
-I
, we see that
-I\in G(2^{m})
for all
m
. The image of the 2-adic representation of an elliptic curve over
\mathbf{Q}
without CM has level at most
32
, so for each
m\geq 5
,
G(2^{m+1})
contains an element
F
of the form

F=-I+2^{m}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

By
[
5
, Thm. 5.2.4]
, the existence of such matrices implies the existence of primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
. An identical argument applied to
G^{\prime}
shows there exist primes of defect
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
as well.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose
G
and
G^{\prime}
contain
-I
. Then

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm tail}=\frac{8}{15}\left(\frac{1}{|G(32)|}+\frac{1}{|G^{\prime}(32)|}\right).

Proof.

Fix
m\geq 5
. Since both
G
and
G^{\prime}
contain
-I
, Proposition
4.2
implies that there exist primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
and
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
. Therefore, both
c_{2^{m}}
and
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}
are nonzero. By Proposition
3.15
, this means
c_{2^{m}}=c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=1/2
.

Consider the primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
. The proportion of primes for which
F\equiv-I\pmod{2^{m}}
is
1/|G(2^{m})|
by the Chebatorev Density Theorem. Since
G
has level at most
32
, we have
|G(2^{m})|=|G(32)|\cdot 16^{m-5}
. Thus, the proportion of primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
is

\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{|G(2^{m})|}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{|G(32)|\cdot 16^{m-5}}.

Similarly, the proportion of primes of defect
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
is
\left(2\cdot|G^{\prime}(32)|\cdot 16^{m-5}\right)^{-1}
. Summing over
m\geq 5
gives

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm tail}=\sum_{m\geq 5}\left(\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{|G(32)|}\cdot\frac{1}{16^{m-5}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{|G^{\prime}(32)|}\cdot\frac{1}{16^{m-5}}\right)=\frac{8}{15}\left(\frac{1}{|G(32)|}+\frac{1}{|G^{\prime}(32)|}\right).

∎

It remains to determine
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm head}
, which is just a matter of putting together results already established.

Theorem 4.4
.

Fix
m
satisfying
2\leq m\leq 4
. The proportion of primes of defect
(m+1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m)
is
c_{2^{m}}/|G(2^{m})|
with
c_{2^{m}}\in\{0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1/2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1\}
. Similarly, the proportion of primes of defect
(m\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}m+1)
is
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}/|G^{\prime}(2^{m})|
with
c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}\in\{0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1/2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1\}
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Proposition
3.15
.
∎

Together, Theorems
4.4
and
4.3
prove Theorem
1.9
.

5.
Extremal Values

The results of the previous sections give an algorithm for computing
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
for any rationally 2-isogenous
E
and
E^{\prime}
. Here we explore the range of values it can take.

Proposition 5.1
.

With all notation as above, we have
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})\leq 1/4
.

Proof.

Suppose
p
is anomalous for the pair
(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
. For some
m\geq 2
, we have that either
F\equiv-I\pmod{2^{m}}
or
F^{\prime}\equiv-I\pmod{2^{m}}
, or both. Therefore, either
F\equiv-I\pmod{4}
or
F^{\prime}\equiv-I\pmod{4}
, which implies that either
-I\in G(4)
or
-I\in G^{\prime}(4)
, or both. We consider two cases.

First suppose that
F\equiv-I\pmod{4}
for every anomalous prime
p
, or
F^{\prime}\equiv-I\pmod{4}
for every anomalous prime
p
. Switching
E
and
E^{\prime}
as necessary, assume
F\equiv-I\pmod{4}
for every anomalous prime
p
. In this case, the proportion of anomalous primes is at most

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\#\{p\leq X:F\equiv-I\pmod{4}\}}{\pi(X)}

which is precisely
{|G(4)|^{-1}}
by the Chebotarev Density Theorem.

We show that
|G(4)|^{-1}\leq 1/4
by showing that
|G(4)|\geq 4
. The Chebotarev Density Theorem implies that
G(4)
contains the same number of elements with determinant
1\pmod{4}
and with determinant
3\pmod{4}
. Since
\pm I\in G(4)
, two elements with determinant
1
, there must be at least two elements of
G(4)
with determinant
3
, and so
|G(4)|\geq 4
.

The second case is that for some anomalous prime
p_{1}\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}

F\not\equiv-I\pmod{4}
; and for some (necessarily distinct) anomalous prime
p_{2}
,
F^{\prime}\not\equiv-I\pmod{4}
. Since
p_{1}
(respectively
p_{2}
) is anomalous, and
F\not\equiv-I\pmod{4}
(resp.
F^{\prime}\not\equiv-I\pmod{4}
), it must be the case that
F^{\prime}\equiv-I\pmod{4}
(resp.
F\equiv-I\pmod{4}
). Thus, both
G(4)
and
G^{\prime}(4)
contain
-I
, and the pair
(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
has anomalous primes of defect
(3\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}2)
and of defect
(2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}3)
. By Proposition
3.16
, these sets have positive Chebotarev densities.

Since both densities are nonzero, we must have
c_{4}=c_{4}^{\prime}=1/2
, by Proposition
3.15
. Since each of
G(4)
and
G^{\prime}(4)
contain at least three distinct elements of determinant 1 (
I
,
-I
,
F
, and
I
,
-I
,
F^{\prime}
, respectively), and since the determinants must be equally distributed modulo 4, it follows that each of
|G(4)|
and
|G^{\prime}(4)|
must be at least
6
. Note that
|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z})|=96
. Since
E
and
E^{\prime}
each have a rational point of order 2, both
G(4)
and
G^{\prime}(4)
have index divisible by
3
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z})
. This implies that
G(4
) and
G^{\prime}(4)
are 2-groups, and so
|G(4)|\mathchar 59\relax|G^{\prime}(4)|\geq 8
. Therefore, the proportion of primes of defect
(3\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}2)
or
(2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}3)
is bounded by

\frac{1}{2|G(4)|}+\frac{1}{2|G^{\prime}(4)|}=\frac{1}{16}+\frac{1}{16}=\frac{1}{8}.

We now must show that the remaining anomalous primes do not contribute more than another 1/8 to this proportion.

If the only anomalous primes for the pair
(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
have defect
(3\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}2)
and
(2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}3)
, then we are done by our work above. If not, then we consider the sizes of the groups
|G(2^{m})|
and
|G^{\prime}(2^{m})|
. By
[
15
, Cor. 1.3]
, the index of
G
and
G^{\prime}
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
must divide 96.

It follows that for each
m\geq 4
, we have both
|G(2^{m})|
and
|G^{\prime}(2^{m})|\geq 16\cdot 16^{m-3}
, so in particular both
|G(32)|
and
|G^{\prime}(32)|\geq 16^{3}
. Therefore, by Theorem
1.9

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{{\rm tail}}\leq\frac{8}{15}\left(\frac{1}{|G(32)|}+\frac{1}{|G^{\prime}(32)|}\right)\leq\frac{16}{15\cdot 16^{3}}=\frac{1}{15\cdot 16^{2}}.

It remains to give an upper bound on the
m=3
and 4 terms.

Using the fact (again) that
G
and
G^{\prime}
have index dividing 96 in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
, we have that
|G(8)|\mathchar 59\relax|G^{\prime}(8)|\geq 16
and
|G(16)|\mathchar 59\relax|G^{\prime}(16)|\geq 16^{2}
. By Proposition
3.15
, we have

(c_{2^{m}}\mathchar 59\relax c^{\prime}_{2^{m}})\in\{(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}0)\mathchar 59\relax(1/2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}0)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1/2)\mathchar 59\relax(1/2\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1/2)\mathchar 59\relax(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1)\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}(1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}0)\}.

A maximum theoretical value of
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
is then achieved when all
|G(2^{m})|
and
|G^{\prime}(2^{m})|
are minimal and weighted by any of the pairs
(c_{2^{m}}\mathchar 59\relax c^{\prime}_{2^{m}})\in\{(1/2\mathchar 59\relax 1/2)\mathchar 59\relax(1\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}0)\mathchar 59\relax(0\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}1)\}
. Putting all this together we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{16}+\frac{1}{16^{2}}+\frac{1}{15\cdot 16^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{23}{120}<\frac{1}{4}.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

In
[
5
, Example 5.2.1]
we gave an explicit example where
\mathcal{P}(G\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}G^{\prime})=1/4
, showing that this bound can indeed be achieved. Note that in that example, all anomalous primes have defect
(3\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}2)
.

Turning to small values of
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
the idea is to engineer examples where
c_{2^{m}}=c_{2^{m}}^{\prime}=0
for small values of
m
, but such that they are non-zero when
G(2^{m})
and
G^{\prime}(2^{m})
are large. We present two examples here, due to Jeremy Rouse, of small values of
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})
both of which are less than
1/30
. We thank him for allowing us to include them here.

Example 5.3
.

Let
E
be the elliptic curve
400.g1
and
E^{\prime}
the elliptic curve
400.g2
. Then
G
and
G^{\prime}
contain
-I
and each has level 16 with
|G(16)|=|G^{\prime}(16)|=256
, so that

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm tail}=\frac{8}{15}\left(\frac{1}{|G(32)|}+\frac{1}{|G^{\prime}(32)|}\right)=\frac{8}{15}\left(\frac{1}{4096}+\frac{1}{4096}\right)=\frac{1}{3840}.

Explicit computations with the division polynomials in
Magma
show that the 4-torsion and 8-torsion fields of
E
and
E^{\prime}
are isomorphic, whence

c_{4}=c_{4}^{\prime}=c_{8}=c_{8}^{\prime}=0
.
However, for the 16-torsion we are in Case 3(b) and 4(b) of Proposition
3.15
so that
c_{16}=c_{16}^{\prime}=1/2
and so
\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm head}=(1/2)/256+(1/2)/256=1/256
. Therefore,

\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm head}+\mathcal{P}(E\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}E^{\prime})^{\rm tail}=\frac{1}{256}+\frac{1}{3840}=\frac{1}{240}.

Using
Magma
we checked these numbers experimentally. We sampled the first 100,000 good primes for
E
and
E^{\prime}
and found

192

primes of defect (4,5)

185

When

have CM there are only two possibilities for

Let

be a curve with CM by an order

in a quadratic imaginary field and let

,

. Thus (writing

will (outside of finitely many exceptions) be horizontal (if

appears to reduce modulo

The key starting point for our result on CM elliptic curves is Proposition

below. For completeness, we remind the reader of several exceptional cases which do not affect our study of anomalous primes:

(1)

(2)

The endomorphism rings will not be preserved under reduction modulo

\mathcal{O}
or
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}

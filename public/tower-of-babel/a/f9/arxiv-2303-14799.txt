Subtractive spaces of semirings
Amartya Goswami

Abstract
. Using the closure operator that defines a subtractive ideal of a semiring S S , in this note we introduce a topology on the set of all ideals of S S induced by that operator. We show that the corresponding subtractive space is T 0 T_{0} and every nonempty irreducible closed set has a unique generic point, whereas the restricted subspace of subtractive ideals is T 1 T_{1} . Using a semiring homomorphism, we obtain a continuous map between the corresponding subtractive spaces.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Since the introduction of semirings in
[
5
]
, it is natural to compare and extend results from rings to semirings. One may think that semirings can always be extended to rings, but
[
6
]
gives examples of semirings that can not be embedded in rings. Furthermore, the lack of ‘subtraction’ in a semiring makes the behaviour of ideals substantially different from that of rings. To minimize this gap, the notion of a ‘
k
-ideal’ (also called a subtractive ideal in
[
1
]
) has been introduced in
[
2
]
. There is a natural closure operator that defines a subtractive ideal. The aim of this note is to introduce a topology on the set of all ideals of a semiring induced by this closure operator. We study a few topological properties of these spaces.

A (commutative)
semiring
is a system
(S,+,0,\cdot,1)
such that
(S,+,0)
is a commutative monoid,
(S,\cdot,1)
is a commutative monoid,
0\cdot x=0=x\cdot 0
for all
x\in S,
and
\cdot
distributes over
+
. We shall write
xy
for
x\cdot y.

A
semiring homomorphism

\phi\colon S\to S^{\prime}
is a map such that
\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y),

\phi(xy)=\phi(x)\phi(y),
and
\phi(1)=1
for all
x,

y\in S.

An
ideal

I
of a semiring
S
is an additive submonoid of
S
such that

rx\in I

for all
x\in I
and
r\in S.
An ideal
I
is called
proper
if
I\neq S.
We also use the symbol
0
to denote the zero ideal of
S
.

Recall from
[
1
]
that a
subtractive ideal

I
of
S
is an ideal of
R
such that
x,

x+y\in I
imply
y\in I
. Equivalently, an
ideal
I
of
S
is called a
k
-ideal if
x+y\in I
implies either
x,

y\in I
or
x,

y\notin I.
Surely, the zero ideal is subtractive and is contained in every
k
-ideal of
S
. We denote the set of all ideals and all subtractive ideals of
S
by
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
and
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S)
respectively.
The notion of a subtractive ideal can also be characterized in terms of a closure operator endowed on
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
. Suppose
I\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
. A
subtractive closure
(also known as
k
-closure, see
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
) operator
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}
is defined by

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)=\{r\in S\mid r+x\in I\;\text{for some}\;x\in I\}.

(1.1)

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
I
,
\{I_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, and
J
be ideals of a semiring
S
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}
has the following properties.

(1)

I\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I).

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(0)=0.

(3)

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S)=S.

(4)

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I))=\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I).

(5)

I\subseteq J
implies
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(J).

(6)

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I\cup J)\supseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\cup\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(J).

(7)

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}\left(\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}I_{\lambda}\right)=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{\lambda}).

(8)

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)
is the smallest subtractive ideal containing
I
.

(9)

I
is a subtractive if and only if
I=\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I).

Proof.

The proofs of (
1
)–(
7
) are straightforward. For (
8
), see
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
, whereas for (
9
), we refer to
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
.
∎

It is obvious from from Lemma
1.1
(
5
) that
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(\sqrt{I})
for all
I\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S).
Note that we may use Lemma
1.1
(
9
) as an alternative definition of a subtractive ideal of a semiring. From (
8

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}
is indeed a map

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}\colon\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)\to\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S)

defined by (
1.1
). Considering the inclusion map
\iota\colon\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S)\to\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
, it is easy to see the following.

Proposition 1.2
.

The pair
(\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}},\iota)
forms a Galois connection.

Lemma 1.3
.

If
I
and
J
are two subtractive ideals of a semiring
S
, then their product
IJ
is also a subtractive ideal of
R
, and
IJ\subseteq I\cap J.

Proof.

Suppose
x,

x+y\in IJ
. Then
x=ij
and
x+y=i^{\prime}j^{\prime}
for some
i,

i^{\prime}\in I
and
j,

j^{\prime}\in J.
Since
I
is an ideal,
ij,

i^{\prime}j^{\prime}\in I
, that is,
x,

x+y\in I
. Since
I
is also a subtractive ideal, this implies
y\in I.
Similarly, we can show that
y\in J.
Hence,
y\in IJ,
and this proves that
IJ
is a subtractive ideal.

Let
r\in IJ
. Then there exists an
x\in IJ
such that
r+x\in IJ.
Since
I
and
J
are ideals of
S
, we definitely have
IJ\subseteq I\cap J
, which implies that
r+x,

x\in I\cap J
. Since
I
and
J
are also subtractive ideals, we must have
r\in I\cap J
, as required.
∎

If
\{I_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a family of subtractive ideals, then their intersection
\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}I_{\lambda}
is also a subtractive ideal. Note that the sum of two subtractive ideals of a semiring need not be a subtractive ideal. Recall from
[
1
, Example 6.19]
that
2\mathds{N}
and
3\mathds{N}
are subtractive ideals of the semiring
\mathds{N}
, however
2\mathds{N}+3\mathds{N}=\mathds{N}\setminus\{1\}
is not a subtractive ideal of
\mathds{N}
, however it is so in a lattice ordered semiring (
cf
.

[
1
, Corollary 21.22]
).
The lattice of all ideals of a ring is modular, whereas the same is not true for a semiring. Nevertheless, we have the following result that announced in
[
2
]
. For a proof, see
[
1
, Proposition 6.38]
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
S
be a semiring. Then
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S)
is a modular lattice.

2.
Subtractive spaces

From Lemma
1.1
(
1
)–(
5
), we observe that a closure operator
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}
satisfies the axioms of an algebraic closure operators. However, it is not true in general that
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}
is closed under finite unions, and hence, it is not a Kuratowski closure operator. Considering the subsets
\{\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\}_{I\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)}
of
S
as subbasic closed sets, nevertheless, induce a topology on
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
, which we call a
subtractive topology
and denote by
\tau_{s}
. For a semiring
S
, the set
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
endowed with a subtractive topology is called a
subtractive space
, and instead of
(\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S),\tau_{s})
, we denote the space also by
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The subbasic closed sets of a subtractive space
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
are the subtractive ideals of
S
.

Proof.

The proof follows from (
8
) and (
9
)) of Lemma
1.1
.
∎

If
I,

I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
and
I\neq I^{\prime}
, then it is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\neq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I^{\prime})
, and hence we have

Lemma 2.2
.

Every subtractive space is
T_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}.

Recall that a nonempty closed subset
D
of a topological space is
irreducible
if
D\neq D_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}\cup D_{\scriptscriptstyle 2}
for any two proper closed subsets
D_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}
and
D_{\scriptscriptstyle 2}
of
D
. A point
x
in a closed subset
D
is called a
generic point
of
D
if
D=\overline{\{x\}}.

Proposition 2.3
.

Every nonempty subbasic closed set of a subtractive space is irreducible.

Proof.

We show that
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)=\overline{\{I\}}
for all
I\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S).
Since
\overline{\{I\}}
is the smallest closed set containg
I
, it follows from Lemma
1.1
(
1
) that
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\supseteq\overline{\{I\}}
. To have the other inclusion, first consider the trivial case of
\overline{\{I\}}=\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S).
For this we have

\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)=\overline{\{I\}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\subseteq\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S),

and hence
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\subseteq\overline{\{I\}}.
Now suppose

\overline{\{I\}}=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{i\lambda})\right).

This means that
I\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{i\lambda})
for some
i
and each
\lambda\in\Lambda
. But that implies

\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}\left(\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{\lambda i})\right)=\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{\lambda i})\subseteq\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{i\lambda})\right),

and hence we have the desired inclusion.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S)
is the largest
T_{{\scriptscriptstyle 1}}
-subspace of a subtractive space
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
.

Proof.

If
I\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S),
then by Proposition
2.3
, we have
I=\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)=\overline{\{I\}}.

∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Every nonempty irreducible closed subset of a subtractive space has a unique generic point.

Proof.

Suppose
D
is a nonempty irreducible closed subset of a subtractive space
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
. Then
D=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
, where each
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
is a finite union of subbasic closed sets of
\tau_{s}.
Since
D
is irreducible, for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
, there exists an
I_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
such that

D\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{\lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},

and this implies

D=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{\lambda})=\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}\left(\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}I_{\lambda}\right)=\overline{\left\{\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}I_{\lambda}\right\}},

where, the last equality follows from Proposition
2.3
. This proves the existence of the generic point, whereas the uniqueness of it follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Using a semiring homomorphism, our aim in this section is to construct a continuous map between the corresponding subtractive spaces and study some of the properties of these maps. The main difference compared to Zariski topology is that we have to use subbasic-closed-set formulation to study these maps.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\phi\colon S\to S^{\prime}
is a semiring homomorphism and
J\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S^{\prime})
. Then the following hold.

(1)

\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(J)
is a subtractive ideal of
S
. In particular,
\mathrm{ker}\phi
is a subtractive ideal of
S
.

(2)

\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(J)=\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(J)).

Proof.

For the first part of (
1
), it is well-known that
\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(J)\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S).
Suppose
x,

x+y\in\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(J).
Then
\phi(x),

\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)\in J
. Since
J\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S^{\prime}),
we must have
\phi(y)\in J,
and hence
y\in\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(J).
For the second part of (
1
), let
x,

x+y\in\mathrm{ker}\phi.
This implies
\phi(y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)=\phi(x+y)=0,
and hence
y\in\mathrm{ker}\phi.
The proof of (
2
) follows immediately from (
1
).
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose
\phi\colon S\to S^{\prime}
is a semiring homomorphism.

(1)

The map
\phi
induces a continuous map
\phi_{!}\colon\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S^{\prime})\to\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
defined by
\phi_{!}(J)=\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(J)
, where
J\in\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S^{\prime}).

(2)

If
\phi
is surjective, then the subtractive spaces
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S)
are homeomorphic.

Proof.

To show (
1
), let
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)
be a subbasic closed set of the subtractive space
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}(S)
, and for us it is sufficient to show that
\phi(\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I))\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(\langle\phi(I)\rangle).
Let
s^{\prime}\in\phi(\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)).
This implies
\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I),
and hence

\phi\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(s^{\prime})+\phi(i)\in\phi(I)\subseteq\langle\phi(I)\rangle,

for some
i\in I.
From this we conclude that
s^{\prime}\in\phi\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(s^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(\langle\phi(I)\rangle).

For (
2
), it is easy to see that the map
\phi_{!}
is injective. Since by hypothesis
\phi_{!}
is surjective and by (
1
),
\phi_{!}
is continuous, what remains is to show that
\phi_{!}
is closed. Notice that if
\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I)
is a subbasic closed subset of
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S^{\prime})
, then by Lemma
2.6
(
2
),
\phi_{!}(\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I))
is also a subbasic closed set of
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S^{\prime}).
Now if
K
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S^{\prime})
, then there exists a collection
\{I_{j\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda,1\leqslant j\leqslant m_{\lambda}\}
of subtractive ideals of
S^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\phi_{!}(K)

\displaystyle=\phi_{!}\left(\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left(\bigcup_{j\,=1}^{m_{\lambda}}\mathcal{C}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(I_{j\lambda})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\phi_{!}\left(\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left(\bigcup_{j\,=1}^{m_{\lambda}}I_{j\lambda}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_{\lambda}}\phi^{\raisebox{0.60275pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle-1$}}(I_{j\lambda}),

a closed subset of
\mathcal{I}\!\textit{dl}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{sub}}}(S).

∎

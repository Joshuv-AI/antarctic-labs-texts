Polynomial stability of the homology of Hurwitz spaces
Andrea Bianchi, Jeremy Miller

Abstract
. For a finite group G G and a conjugation-invariant subset Q ⊆ G Q\subseteq G , we consider the Hurwitz space Hur n ​ ( Q ) \mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q) parametrising branched covers of the plane with n n branch points, monodromies in G G and local monodromies in Q Q . For i ≥ 0 i\geq 0 we prove that ⨁ n H i ​ ( Hur n ​ ( Q ) ) \bigoplus_{n}H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)) is a finitely generated module over the ring ⨁ n H 0 ​ ( Hur n ​ ( Q ) ) \bigoplus_{n}H_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)) . As a consequence, we obtain polynomial stability of homology of Hurwitz spaces: taking homology coefficients in a field, the dimension of H i ​ ( Hur n ​ ( Q ) ) H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)) agrees for n n large enough with a quasi-polynomial in n n , whose degree is easily bounded in terms of G G and Q Q . Under suitable hypotheses on G G and Q Q , we prove classical homological stability for certain sequences of components of Hurwitz spaces. Our results generalise previous work of Ellenberg-Venkatesh-Westerland, and rely on techniques introduced by them and by Hatcher-Wahl.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and let
Q\subseteq G
be a conjugation-invariant subset.
For
n\geq 0
, the Hurwitz space
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
considered in this article is
a certain homotopy quotient of the set
Q^{n}
of
n
-tuples of elements in
Q
by an action of the braid group
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
: see Definition
2.1
. The homotopy type of
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
coincides with that of the moduli space of certain “decorated” branched covers of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
; see Subsection
1.2
for more details, and for the link between the Hurwitz spaces of this article and the Hurwitz spaces usually considered in algebraic geometry.

We are broadly interested in stability properties of homology of components of Hurwitz spaces. Suitable stability results for
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q))
can find applications in enumerative number theory: the main instance of this is are the work of Ellenberg-Venkatesh-Westerland on the Cohen-Lenstra heuristics
[
EVW16
]
and the work of Ellenberg-Tran-Westerland on the Malle conjecture
[
ETW23
]
.
The results of this article are an attempt to generalise the topological part of
[
EVW16
]
, as we do not require
Q\subseteq G
to be a single conjugacy class with the “non-splitting property”, and we consider homology in any Noetherian ring
R
; yet we have not been able to find a counterpart to the nice linear stability ranges from
[
EVW16
]
, making our results of a more qualitative than quantitative nature; in Subsection
1.2
we briefly explain why this prevents applications of our results in enumerative number theory.

1.1.
Statement of results

The disjoint union
\mathrm{Hur}(Q)=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
has a natural structure of topological monoid,

As a consequence,

A=H_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q))

Theorem A
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian commutative ring. For
i\geq 0
, the module
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)
is finitely generated over
A\otimes R=H_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)

A quantitative consequence of the previous theorem is the following
corollary: see Definition
2.5
for the constant
k(G,Q)\geq 1

Corollary A’
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and let
i\geq 0
. Let
\ell\geq 1
be such that
a^{\ell}=\mathbbl{1}\in G
for all
a\in Q
. Then there is a quasi-polynomial
p{F}_{i}(t)

of degree at most
k(G,Q)-1
and period dividing
\ell
such that, for
n
large enough,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q);\mathbb{F})=(p{F}_{i})_{[n]_{\ell}}(n)
.

We note that the condition
k(G,Q)=1
is the “non-splitting property” for the couple
(G,Q)
, introduced in
[
EVW16
]
: if
k(G,Q)=1
then
Q
is a single conjugacy class in
G
and for every subgroup
H\subseteq G
, the intersection
Q\cap H
is either empty or a single conjugacy class in
H
. In this case,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q);\mathbb{F})
is eventually equal to a quasi-polynomial in
n
of degree
0
, i.e. it eventually coincides with a periodic function of
n
: this is an abstract version of homology stability, in the sense that for
n,n^{\prime}
large enough with respect to
i

is divisible by
\ell
then the vector spaces
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q);\mathbb{F})
and
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n^{\prime}}(Q);\mathbb{F})
are abstractly isomorphic, but no explicit isomorphism is provided. We remark that in the case
k(G,Q)=1
, Theorem
A
is proved in
[
EVW16
]
, under the additional hypotheses that homology is taken with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
(or at least
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{|G|}]
): more precisely, for a suitable constant
D\geq 1
an explicit map
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q);\mathbb{Q})\to H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n+D}(Q);\mathbb{Q})
is constructed, and for suitable constants

this map is shown to be an isomorphism provided that

, i.e. a linear stability range is proved.
The given stable isomorphism
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q);\mathbb{Q})\to H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n+D}(Q);\mathbb{Q})
is defined as a
sum
of maps induced in homology by topological stabilisation maps
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)\to\mathrm{Hur}_{n+D}
.

The philosophy that stability results should be formulated in terms of finiteness results as modules over algebras was popularized by the field of representation stability (see e.g.
[
CEF15
,
CEFN14
]
). As is common in representation stability, we leverage the fact that the algebra governing stability

is Noetherian. This helps us analyze a certain spectral sequence constructed using an arc complex introduced by Hatcher-Wahl
[
HW10
]
. These spectral sequences also appeared in work of Ellenberg-Venkatesh-Westerland
[
EVW16
]

[

KMT22
]
.

For
\omega\in G

\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega}\subset\mathrm{Hur}(Q)

(see Definition

The ring

contains a subring
A{1}=H_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q){1})
, and
for
\omega\in G

H_{*}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
makes the submodule

into a left
A{1}
-module, for all

. Our second main result is the following.

Theorem B

Let
R
be a Noetherian commutative ring. Let
i\geq 0
and let
\omega\in G
; then
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is finitely generated over
A{1}\otimes R=H_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q){1};R)
.

A quantitative consequence of the previous theorem is the following
(see Definition
2.6

Corollary B’
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and let
i\geq 0
. Let

. Then there is a quasi-polynomial

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)_{\omega};\mathbb{F})=(p{F}_{i,\omega})_{[n]_{\ell}}(n)

We compare Theorems

and

However, if

1.2.
Connections to algebraic geometry and enumerative number theory

The space
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
considered in this article is homotopy equivalent to the certain moduli space of regular branched covers of the complex plane
p\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{C}
endowed with the following data:

(1)

an identification of the deck transformation group
\Aut(\mathcal{F},p)
with
G
;

(2)

a trivialisation over a suitable lower half-plane
\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{C}
of the restricted
G
-principal bundle
p^{-1}(\mathbb{H})\cong G\times\mathbb{H}
, up to replacing
\mathbb{H}
by smaller and smaller half-planes,

such that there are precisely
n
branch points, and such that local monodromies around the branch points have values in
Q
. A branched cover is “regular” if its group of deck transformations acts transitively on each fibre; we use the term “
G
-cover” for a regular branched cover endowed with a decoration as (1) above.

Romagny-Wewers
[
RW06
]
describe how to construct, for
n\geq 0
and a finite group
G
, a scheme
\mathcal{H}_{n,G}
of finite type over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
whose associated analytic space
\mathcal{H}_{n,G}(\mathbb{C})
can be identified with the moduli space of branched
G
-covers as above, but with
\mathbb{P}^{1}{C}
as target,
without the decoration (2), and without the restriction that local monodromies be in
Q
. The construction already appears in Wewers’ PhD thesis
[
Wew98
]
, and it is preceeded by work of Fulton
[
Ful69
]
and of Fried-Völklein
[
FV91
]
: Fulton constructs for
d\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
a scheme
\mathcal{H}_{d,n}
of finite type over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
whose complex points isomorphism classes of degree-
d
simple branched covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}{C}
with
n
branch points, and Fried-Völklein construct the basechange
\mathcal{H}_{n,G}\times_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Q})
, which is of finite type over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Q})
.

Ellenberg-Venkatesh-Westerland
[
EVW16
]
show that at least when
Q\subset G
is a
rational
conjugation-invariant subset (that is, for all
a\in Q
and all
n\geq 1
coprime with the order of
a
in
G
, one has
a^{n}\in Q
), then Wewers’ Hurwitz scheme
\mathcal{H}_{n,G}
contains a subscheme
\mathcal{H}_{n,G}^{Q}
whose complex points are isomorphism classes of branched
G
-covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}{C}
with local monodromies lying in
Q
. Similarly, assuming again that
Q
is rational, they show the existence of a subscheme
\mathrm{Hn}_{G.n}^{Q}
of
\mathcal{H}_{n+1,G}

whose complex points are isomorphism classes of branched
G
-covers of
\mathbb{C}
with local monodromies in
Q
.
The only missing “decoration” is (2) in the list above, namely a trivialisation of the
G
-cover over some lower half-plane; it follows that
\mathrm{Hn}^{Q}_{G,n}(\mathbb{C})
should be thought of as corresponding to the quotient
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)/G
, where
G
acts by global conjugation on the set
Q^{n}
, the action is compatible with that of
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
, and hence we obtain an induced action of
G
on
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
. It would be interesting to know, without any restriction on
Q\subseteq G
, whether there exists a scheme
\tilde{\mathrm{Hn}}^{Q}_{G,n}
, possibly of finite type over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
(and ideally, an affine scheme) whose associated analytic space
\tilde{\mathrm{Hn}}^{Q}_{G,n}(\mathbb{C})
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
.

In
[
EVW16
]
, rational homological stability for
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
is proved under the non-splitting hypothesis, and an
explicit linear stability range
is provided. This leads to an exponential upper bound on the size of the rational cohomology of
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
that does not depend on
n
: there is a constant
C>0
such that in each degree
i\geq 0
one has
\dim{Q}H^{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q);\mathbb{Q})<C^{i}
. This in turn leads to a similar upper bound on the size of
H^{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)/G;\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{i}(\mathrm{Hn}_{G,n}^{Q}(\mathbb{C});\mathbb{Q})
, when
Q
is a rational conjugacy class of
G
. For a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb}{F}_{q}
, the latter upper bound can be used to control the size of the étale cohomology of the basechange
\mathrm{Hn}_{G,n}^{Q}\times_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{\mathbb}{F}_{q})
. One can then use the Grothendieck-Lefschetz trace formula to express the
|\mathrm{Hn}^{Q}_{G,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
as the alternating sum of the traces of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius acting on the étale cohomology of
\mathrm{Hn}_{G,n}^{Q}\otimes_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathrm{Spec}(\bar{\mathbb}{F}_{q})
. The above bounds on the dimension of the étale cohomology groups, together with the Deligne bounds on the size of the eigenvalues of the Frobenius, give an estimate of
|\mathrm{Hn}^{Q}_{G,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
, and describe, at least for
q
large, how
|\mathrm{Hn}^{Q}_{G,n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})|
grows for
n\to\infty
.

The lack of an explicit linear stability range in the results of this article seems to exclude the possibility to employ our results in a similar framework as that of
[
EVW16
]
.
However, if an explicit linear stable range could be established, these kinds of polynomial stability results plausibly would have implications for point count problems.

Preliminaries

Throughout the article,
G
denotes a finite group and
Q\subseteq G
a conjugation-invariant subset.
We denote by
q_{1},\dots,q_{m}
the elements of
Q
, where
m=|Q|
, and we let
\ell\geq 1
be a positive integer such that
a^{\ell}=\mathbbl{1}\in G
for each
a\in Q
: for instance, we could take
\ell
to be the least common multiple of the multiplicative orders in the group
G
of the elements of
Q
.

2.1.
Hurwitz spaces as homotopy quotients

For
n\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
the Artin braid group on
n
strands,
with generators
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
satisfying the usual braid and commuting relations.
The group
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
acts on the set
Q^{n}
of
n
-tuples of elements of
Q
as follows: for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, the standard generator
\sigma_{i}\in\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
sends

\sigma_{i}\colon(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\mapsto(a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},a_{i+1},a_{i}^{a_{i+1}},a_{i+2},\dots,a_{n}),

where, for
a,b\in Q
, we denote
a^{b}=b^{-1}ab\in Q
. The fact that the action is well-defined is a consequence of the fact that
Q
, with the operation of conjugation restricted from
G
, is a quandle.
The following is the definition of Hurwitz spaces that we are going to use throughout the article.

Definition 2.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we define the Hurwitz space
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
as the homotopy quotient

\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)=Q^{n}\sslash\mathfrak{Br}_{n}.

A priori, a homotopy quotient is only defined as a homotopy type; in this article we realise homotopy quotients by the standard bar construction; for example
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
, as a concrete topological space, is the geometric realisation of the simplicial set
B_{\bullet}(*,\mathfrak{Br}_{n},Q^{n})
.

2.2.
Components of Hurwitz spaces

By definition,
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
is the homotopy quotient of the set
Q^{n}
by the action of the discrete group
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
: it follows that
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q))
is in natural bijection with the set of orbits of the action of
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
on the set
Q^{n}
.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in Q^{n}
we denote by
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q,\underline{a})\subset\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)
the component corresponding to the orbit of
\underline{a}
under the action of
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
.

The space
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q,\underline{a})
is aspherical.
Let us denote by
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\cdot\underline{a}\subset Q^{n}
the orbit of
\underline{a}
under the braid group action: then
\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q,\underline{a})
is canonically homeomorphic to
(\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\cdot\underline{a})\sslash\mathfrak{Br}_{n}=|B_{\bullet}(*,\mathfrak{Br}_{n},\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\cdot\underline{a})|
, and the fundamental group of
(\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\cdot\underline{a})\sslash\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
based at the 0-simplex
\underline{a}
is canonically isomorphic to the subgroup of
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
stabilising
\underline{a}
, which we denote
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}(\underline{a})\subseteq\mathfrak{Br}_{n}
.

2.3.
Topological monoid structure

For
n,m\geq 0
, consider the standard concatenating map of sets

Q^{n}\times Q^{m}\overset{\cong}{\to}Q^{n+m}

and the standard concatenating map of braid groups
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\times\mathfrak{Br}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{Br}_{n+m}
.
If we let
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\times\mathfrak{Br}_{m}
act on
Q^{n+m}
through its inclusion into
\mathfrak{Br}_{n+m}
, we have that the map
Q^{n}\times Q^{m}\overset{\cong}{\to}Q^{n+m}
is
(\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\times\mathfrak{Br}_{m})
-equivariant; we also say that the map of sets
Q^{n}\times Q^{m}\overset{\cong}{\to}Q^{n+m}
is equivariant with respect to the map of groups
\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\times\mathfrak{Br}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{Br}_{n+m}
. This equivariance gives rise to a map between the homotopy quotients

\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)\times\mathrm{Hur}_{m}(Q)\cong(Q^{n}\times Q^{m})\sslash(\mathfrak{Br}_{n}\times\mathfrak{Br}_{m})\to Q^{n+m}\sslash\mathfrak{Br}_{n+m}=\mathrm{Hur}_{n+m}(Q),

and these maps assemble into a topological monoid structure on the disjoint union

\mathrm{Hur}(Q)=\coprod_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q),

with unit given by the unique point of
\mathrm{Hur}_{0}(Q)
.

Taking connected components, we obtain a discrete monoid
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
; if we denote by
\hat{q}_{i}
the connected component of
\mathrm{Hur}(Q)
given by the (contractible) space
\mathrm{Hur}_{1}(Q,q_{i})
, for all

, we have the following presentation by generators and relations of
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
as an associative, unital monoid:

\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))\cong\left<\hat{q}_{1},\dots,\hat{q}_{m}\ |\ \hat{q}_{i}\cdot\hat{q}_{j}=\hat{q}_{j}\cdot\widehat{q_{i}^{q_{j}}}\right>.

Definition 2.3
.

We denote by
\pi_{0}^{\ell}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))\subseteq\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
the unital submonoid generated by the elements
\hat{q}_{j}^{\ell}
for
1\leq i\leq m
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The monoid
\pi_{0}^{\ell}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
is commutative, and for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
the following relation holds in
\pi_{0}^{\ell}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
:

\hat{q}_{i}^{\ell}\cdot\hat{q}_{j}^{\ell}=\widehat{q_{i}^{q_{j}}}^{\ell}\cdot\hat{q}_{j}^{\ell}

Proof.

We note that the elements
\hat{q}_{i}^{\ell}
are central elements of the monoid
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
: indeed for all

we have
\hat{q}_{j}\cdot\hat{q}_{i}^{\ell}=\hat{q}_{i}^{\ell}\cdot\widehat{q_{j}^{q_{i}^{\ell}}}=\hat{q}_{i}^{\ell}\cdot\hat{q}_{j}
. This implies in particular that
\pi_{0}^{\ell}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
is a commutative monoid.

Moreover for all
i,j
we have in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
the equality
\hat{q}_{i}^{\ell}\cdot\hat{q}_{j}=\hat{q}_{j}\cdot\widehat{q_{i}^{q_{j}}}^{\ell}
, which by the previous argument is also equal to
\widehat{q_{i}^{q_{j}}}^{\ell}\cdot\hat{q}_{j}
; multiplying both sides on right by
\hat{q}_{j}^{\ell-1}
we obtain the described relations among the generators of
\pi_{0}^{\ell}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
.
∎

2.4.
Constants attached to finite groups

The following constants, attached to a group

Definition 2.5

in which the conjugation-invariant subset

decomposes.

Definition 2.6

We denote by

containing

Definition 2.7

Let

and assume that

is

Note that if

and let

.

Invariants of components

The action of

.

2.6.
Quasi-polynomials

Let

, and for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
denote by

the class of

modulo

. We will use the following notion of quasi-polynomial.

Definition 2.9
.

A quasi-polynomial of period dividing
\ell
, denoted

, is the datum of
\ell
polynomials

.

The degree of a non-zero quasi-polynomial is the maximum among the degrees of the polynomials

; the degree of the zero quasi-polynomial is set to be

.

A quasi-polynomial induces a function

As one can see, the argument

must be input twice, once as index of the polynomial, once as value of the variable

, in order to evaluate a quasi-polynomial at

.

.

We observe that

Lemma 3.3
.

The associative ring
A
is finitely generated as a
B
-module. As a consequence we have the following:

(1)

is Noetherian: a sub-module of a finitely generated left or right

-module is finitely generated.

(2)

A{1}
is also finitely generated as a
B
-module, and is also a Noetherian ring.

(3)

A
is finitely generated as a
A{1}
-module.

Proof.

The commutative ring
B
is Noetherian, as it is a quotient of a polynomial ring over
\mathbb{Z}
with
m

is a quotient of a free abelian monoid on
m

is a finitely generated
B
-module, we immediately have that

is Noetherian: for if

is a finitely generated (left or right)
A
-module and

is a submodule, then

is also finitely generated over
B
, and by Noetherianity of

we have that

is finitely generated over

, and a fortiori over

. This proves that the main statement implies (1); it also implies (2), as

is a sub-

-module of

, and

is Noetherian; Noetherianity of

then follows from the same argument used to prove (1). Finally, (3) follows from the inclusion

together with the fact that
A
is finitely generated as a
B
-module.

We now prove the main statement. Recall that
\pi_{0}^{\ell}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
is a central submonoid in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
: this implies that
B
is a central subring of
A
.
Our next goal is to show that
A
is a finitely generated

-module: more precisely, the products

and there are

6.1.

Proposition 6.1
.

There is a polynomial

Proof.

In the proof we set

.
Fix a field
\mathbb{F}
and let

; then

-module. The

We now want to argue that the degree of

•

Lower bound.
Let

is the union of precisely
k

be the subgroup of

generated by

at the beginning. Let

be the list of all elements in

and for every splitting

these elements are all distinct, for different choices of

•

Upper bound.

It follows that the subgroup

It follows that if
b,c\in Q
can be obtained from another by a sequence of conjugations by
\omega^{\pm 1}
or
a^{\pm 1}
, then
[b]^{\ell}x=[c]^{\ell}x
. Since
\omega
is large, the group
\left<\omega,a\right>
is the entire
G
, and by assumption
Q
is a unique conjugacy class in
G
. We conclude for any homology class
x
of the form
[a]^{\ell}y
and for any
b,c\in Q
we have
[b]^{\ell}x=[c]^{\ell}x
.

The claim now follows from the definition of
\bar{n}
: for
n\geq\bar{n}
, every class in
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is a linear combination of classes of the form
[a]^{\ell}y
, for possibly different values of
a\in Q
and
y\in H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n-\ell}(Q)_{\omega};R)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
C
for
R
Noetherian.

By Theorem
B

H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is a finitely generated
A\otimes R
-module, and by Lemma
3.3
we have that
A\otimes R
is finitely generated over
B\otimes R
; it follows that
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is finitely generated over
B\otimes R
; since
B\otimes R
is Noetherian, and since
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\geq\bar{n}}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is a sub-
B\otimes R
-module of
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega};R)
, we obtain that also
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\geq\bar{n}}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is finitely generated over
B\otimes R
. It is then a consequence of Lemma
7.2
that
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\geq\bar{n}}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is in fact a finitely generated
C\otimes R
-module.

Let now
a\in Q
; the ring
C\otimes R
is isomorphic to the polynomial algebra over
R
generated by
[a]^{\ell}
, and in particular also
C\otimes R
is Noetherian. We conclude that
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\geq\bar{n}}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is not only finitely generated, but also finitely presented. Choosing
\tilde{n}\geq\bar{n}
such that
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\geq\bar{n}}(Q)_{\omega};R)
admits a presentation in weights
\leq\tilde{n}
, we obtain that for
n>\tilde{n}
the multiplication
[a]^{\ell}\cdot-
induces a bijection
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)_{\omega};R)\cong H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n+\ell}(Q)_{\omega};R)
; this multiplication coincides with
\lst(a)^{\ell}_{*}
.

We conclude by observing that, by Lemma
7.2
, for
n\geq\bar{n}
and for varying
a\in Q
, the stabilisation maps
\lst(a)_{*}^{\ell}\colon H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)_{\omega};R)\to H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n+\ell}(Q)_{\omega};R)
are equal to each other.
∎

We conclude the subsection by proving Theorem
C
for a general ring
R
; so in the following proof we drop the hypothesis that
R
is Noetherian.

Proof of Theorem
C
in the general case.

Let
i\geq 0
, let
G,Q,\omega
as in the statement of Theorem
C
, and let
a\in Q
. Then the proof of Theorem
C
for integral homology has the following direct consequence: there is
\bar{n}\geq 0
such that for
n\leq\bar{n}
the map
\lst(a)^{\ell}\colon\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)_{\omega}\to\mathrm{Hur}_{n+\ell}(Q)_{\omega}
is an integral homology isomorphism in all homological degrees
\leq i
; by the universal coefficient theorem for homology, it follows that for
n\geq\bar{n}
the same map induces an isomorphism in
R
-homology in the same range of degrees, for any ring
R
.

In particular, the direct sum
\bigoplus_{n\leq\bar{n}}H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)_{\omega};R)
generates
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega};R)
over
B\otimes R
; we can now repeat the argument of Lemma
7.2
, and show that the sub-
B\otimes R
-module

is in fact a

-module; this implies that for
n\geq\bar{n}+\ell
the stabilisation map
\lst(a)^{\ell}_{*}\colon H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)_{\omega};R)\to H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n+\ell}(Q)_{\omega};R)

.
∎

7.2.
Homology of the group completion

We recall the group-completion theorem by McDuff and Segal
[
MS76
]

(see also
[
FM94
, Theorem Q.4]
).

Theorem 7.3

(Group-completion theorem)
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
M
be a topological monoid; suppose that the localisation
H_{*}(M;R)[\pi_{0}(M)^{-1}]

can be constructed by right fractions. Then the canonical
map

H_{*}(M;R)[\pi_{0}(M)^{-1}]\to H_{*}(\Omega BM;R)

is an isomorphism of rings.

Lemmas
5.3
and
6.3
imply that the multiplicative set
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
of the ring
H_{*}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)
satisfies the Ore condition; hence the localisation

H_{*}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)[\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))^{-1}]

can be constructed by right fractions, and Theorem
7.3
is thus applicable to compute
H_{*}(\Omega B\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
.

Proof of Corollary
C’
.

For a fixed homological degree
i\geq 0
, the localisation of the
A\otimes R
-module
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)
at the multiplicative subset
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))\subset A\otimes R
coincides with the homology
H_{i}(\Omega B\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)
. This localisation can be constructed by right fractions; moreover the multiplicative set
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))\subset A\otimes R
is generated multiplicatively by the finite set of elements
[a]=[a]\otimes 1\in A\otimes R
.

We define
\mathfrak{w}=\hat{q}_{1}^{\ell}\dots\hat{q}_{m}^{\ell}\in\pi_{0}^{\ell}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
, and denote by
[\mathfrak{w}^{-1}]\in B\otimes R\subset A\otimes R
the corresponding generator; this allows us to identify the module localisation
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)[\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))^{-1}]
with
H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)[\mathfrak{w}^{-}1]
. Taking the zero component
\Omega_{0}B\mathrm{Hur}(Q)
, we can identify
H_{i}(\Omega_{0}B\mathrm{Hur}(Q);R)
with the colimit of the following sequential diagram, where

\alpha
is the chosen element in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega})
(but for the following diagram any other
\alpha^{\prime}\in\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q))
would work):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\alpha}(Q);R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\mathfrak{w}\alpha}(Q);R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\mathfrak{w}^{2}\alpha}(Q);R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\mathfrak{w}]\cdot-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\mathfrak{w}]\cdot-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\mathfrak{w}]\cdot-}

The map
[\mathfrak{w}]\cdot-\colon H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega})\to H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}(Q)_{\omega})
can be written as a composition
\lst(q_{m})^{\ell}_{*}\circ\dots\circ\lst(q_{1})^{m}_{*}
, and by Theorem
C
, for
n
large enough and for fixed
a\in Q
, each of the maps
\lst(q_{i})^{\ell}_{*}\colon H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n}(Q)_{\omega};R)\to H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{n+\ell}(Q)_{\omega};R)
is an isomorphism and coincides with
\lst(a)^{\ell}_{*}
, where
a\in Q
is our fixed element. It follows that the sequential diagram above can be regarded as an “index-
m
” subdiagram of the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\alpha}(Q);R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\hat{a}^{\ell}\alpha}(Q);R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{i}(\mathrm{Hur}_{\hat{a}^{2\ell}\alpha}(Q);R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[a]^{\ell}\cdot-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[a]^{\ell}\cdot-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[a]^{\ell}\cdot-}

The last diagram stabilises by Theorem

, and its colimit is

∎

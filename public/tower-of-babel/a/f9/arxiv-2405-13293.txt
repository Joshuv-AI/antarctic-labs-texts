Digraphs in which every t t vertices share exactly λ \lambda out-neighbors and exactly λ \lambda in-neighbors
Hojin Chu, Suh-Ryung Kim

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the notion of two-way ( t , λ ) (t,\lambda) -liking digraphs as a way to extend the results for generalized friendship graphs. A two-way ( t , λ ) (t,\lambda) -liking digraph is a digraph in which every t t vertices have exactly λ \lambda common out-neighbors and λ \lambda common in-neighbors. We first show that if λ ≥ 2 \lambda\geq 2 , then a two-way ( 2 , λ ) (2,\lambda) -liking digraph of order n n is k k -diregular for a positive integer k k satisfying the equation ( n − 1 ) ​ λ = k ⁡ ( k − 1 ) (n-1)\lambda=k(k-1) . This result is comparable to the result by Bose and Shrikhande in 1969 and actually extends it. Another main result is that if t ≥ 3 t\geq 3 , then the complete digraph on t + λ t+\lambda vertices is the only two-way ( t , λ ) (t,\lambda) -liking digraph. This result can stand up to the result by Carstens and Kruse in 1977 and essentially extends it. In addition, we find that two-way ( t , λ ) (t,\lambda) -liking digraphs are closely linked to symmetric block designs and extend some existing results of ( t , λ ) (t,\lambda) -liking digraphs.



Keywords.
Two-way Liking digraph; Liking digraph; Generalized Friendship graph; Diregular digraph; Symmetric Block Design.

In 1966, Erdös

The Friendship Theorem can be stated, in graph-theoretical terms, as follows: if any pair of vertices in a graph has exactly one common neighbor, then there exists a vertex adjacent to the others.
The graph described in the Friendship Theorem is referred to as a “friendship graph”.
A

A

A

•

.

Let

An extension of results on
(t,\lambda)
-liking digraphs

Proposition 4.1
.

A
(\lambda+1,\lambda)
-liking digraph is a two-way
(\lambda+1,\lambda)
-liking digraph for a positive integer
\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
D
be a
(\lambda+1,\lambda)
-liking digraph for a positive integer
\lambda
.
To prove that
D
is a two-way
(\lambda+1,\lambda)
-liking digraph, we first show that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V(D)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}\left|\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{-}(v)\right|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V(D)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}\lambda.

Noting that

\left|\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{-}(v)\right|=\sum_{w\in\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{-}(v)}1,

for a vertex set
S
of
D
,
we may change the order of the double summation as follows:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V(D)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}\left|\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{-}(v)\right|=\sum_{w\in V(D)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N^{+}(w)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}1.

By Proposition
2.4
,
d^{+}(w)=d^{-}(w)
for each vertex
w
in
D
and so

\sum_{w\in V(D)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N^{+}(w)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{w\in V(D)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N^{-}(w)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}1.

By changing the order of the double summation, we obtain

\sum_{w\in V(D)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq N^{-}(w)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V(D)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}\left|\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{+}(v)\right|.

Since
D
is a
(\lambda+1,\lambda)
-liking digraph,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V(D)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}\left|\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{+}(v)\right|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V(D)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}\lambda.

Accordingly, we have shown that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V(D)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}\left|\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{-}(v)\right|=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq V(D)\\
|S|=\lambda+1\end{subarray}}\lambda.

By the way, since
D
is a
(\lambda+1,\lambda)
-liking digraph,
for any (
\lambda+1
)-subset
S
of
V(D)
,

\left|\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{-}(v)\right|\leq\lambda.

Therefore, for any (
\lambda+1
)-subset
S
of
V(D)
,

\left|\bigcap_{v\in S}N^{-}(v)\right|=\lambda.

That is, any
\lambda+1
vertices in
D
have exactly
\lambda
common in-neighbors.
Hence,
D
is a two-way
(\lambda+1,\lambda)
-liking digraph.
∎

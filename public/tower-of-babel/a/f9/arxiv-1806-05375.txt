The 𝒒 \bm{q} -Borel Sum of Divergent Basic Hypergeometric Series 𝝋 𝒔 𝒓 ​ ( 𝒂 , 𝒃 , 𝒒 , 𝒙 ) \bm{{}_{r}\varphi_{s}(\bm{a};\bm{b};q,x)}
Shunya ADACHI

Abstract
We study the divergent basic hypergeometric series which is a q q -analog of divergent hypergeometric series. This series formally satisfies the linear q q -difference equation. In this paper, for that equation, we give an actual solution which admits basic hypergeometric series as a q q -Gevrey asymptotic expansion. Such an actual solution is obtained by using q q -Borel summability, which is a q q -analog of Borel summability. Our result shows a q q -analog of the Stokes phenomenon. Additionally, we show that letting q → 1 q\to 1 in our result gives the Borel sum of classical hypergeometric series. The same problem was already considered by Dreyfus, but we note that our result is remarkably different from his one.

2.1
Basic notions

Let us fix our notations. We denote by
\bm{0}_{n}
the
n
-vector
(0,0,\ldots,0)
.

For
n=0,1,2,\ldots,
we set the Pochhammer symbol as

\displaystyle(a)_{n}:=\begin{cases}1,&n=0,\\
a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1),&n\geq 1,\end{cases}

and the
q
-shift factorial is defined by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\big(1-aq^{j}\big),\qquad(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\big(1-aq^{j}\big).

For any
a\in\mathbb{C}
, the infinite product
(a;q)_{\infty}
is convergent. We set

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{n}:=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}(a_{j};q)_{n}\qquad\text{for $n=0,1,2,\ldots$ or $n=\infty$}.

The theta function with the base
q
is defined by

\displaystyle\theta_{q}(x):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}x^{n},

which is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}^{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. The following properties hold:

\displaystyle\theta_{q}(x)=(q,-x,-q/x;q)_{\infty},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\theta_{q}(q^{n}x)=x^{-n}q^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\theta_{q}(x),\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\theta_{q}(qx)=\frac{\theta_{q}(x)}{x}=\theta_{q}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(2.3)

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we set the
q
-spiral
[\lambda;q]:=\lambda q^{\mathbb{Z}}=\{\lambda q^{n};\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. From the equality (
2.1
), we see

\displaystyle\theta_{q}\left(-\frac{\lambda}{x}\right)=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad x\in[\lambda;q].

We remark that the
q
-spiral
[\lambda;q]
is identified with an element of
\mathbb{C}^{*}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The
q
-gamma function
\Gamma_{q}(x)
is defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{q}(x):=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(q^{x};q)_{\infty}}(1-q)^{1-x}.

When
q
is a real number and satisfies
0<q<1
, the limit of
\Gamma_{q}(x)
as
q\to 1
gives the gamma function (cf. Gasper–Rahman
[
7
, Section 1.10]
)

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 1}\Gamma_{q}(x)=\Gamma(x).

(2.4)

2.2
Review of
\bm{q}
-Borel summability

We review the theory of
q
-Borel summability (cf. Zhang
[
13
,
14
]
and Dreyfus–Eloy

Let
\mathbb{C}[[x]]
be the ring of formal power series of

\hat{f}(x)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}a_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{C}[[x]]
. For

, we define its

\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}\colon\mathbb{C}[[x]]\to\mathbb{C}[[\xi]]
by

\displaystyle(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}\hat{f})(\xi):=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\xi^{n}.

We denote
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}^{*},0)
the field of functions that are meromorphic on some punctured neighborhood of 0 in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. More explicitly
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}^{*},0)
if and only if there exists
V
, an open neighborhood of 0, such that

is analytic on

.

Let

and

. It is said that

if there exist a positive constant
\varepsilon
and a domain

such that:

•

\displaystyle\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{*};\,\left|x-\lambda q^{m}\right|<\varepsilon\left|q^{m}\lambda\right|\right\}\subset\Omega
.

•

The function
f
can be continued to an analytic function on
\Omega
with
q
-exponential growth at infinity, which means that there exist positive constants
L
and
M
such that for any

, the following holds:

\displaystyle|f(x)|<L\theta_{|q|}(M|x|).

We note that this estimate does not depend on the choice of
\lambda
.

Let
[\lambda;q]\in\mathbb{C}^{*}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
. For
f\in\mathbb{H}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}
, we define its

-Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}\colon\mathbb{H}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}\to\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}^{*},0)
by

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{L}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}f\big)(x):=\frac{1}{1-q}\int_{0}^{\lambda\infty}\frac{f(\xi)}{\theta_{q}\big(\frac{\xi}{x}\big)}\frac{d_{q}\xi}{\xi}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{f(\lambda q^{n})}{\theta_{q}\big(\frac{\lambda q^{n}}{x}\big)}.

Here, this transformation is given in Jackson integral (cf. Gaspar–Rahman
[
7
, Section 1.10]
). For sufficient small

, the function

has poles of order at most one that are contained in the
q
-spiral
[-\lambda;q]
. We remark that the well-definedness of

-Laplace transform can be shown in the same way as Théorème 1.3.2 of Zhang
[
13
]
. If the function
\hat{f}(x)\in\mathbb{C}[[x]]
satisfies

, we call that

is

-summable and
\big(\mathcal{L}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}\circ\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}\hat{f}\big)(x)

-sum of

.

We give some fundamental properties of

-Borel–Laplace transform without proofs.

Proposition 2.1

(Dreyfus–Eloy
[
6
, Section 1]
)
.

Let
[\lambda;q]\in\mathbb{C}^{*}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
and

be an analytic function. Then, the followings hold:

•

\big(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}f\big)\in\mathbb{H}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}
,

•

\big(\mathcal{L}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}\circ\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}f\big)=f
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
[\lambda;q]\in\mathbb{C}/q^{\mathbb{Z}}
. For
\hat{f}(x)\in\mathbb{C}[[x]]
and

, the followings hold.

•

\big(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}\big(x^{m}\sigma_{q}^{n}\hat{f}\big)\big)=q^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}\xi^{m}\sigma_{q}^{n+m}\big(\hat{\mathcal{B}}_{q;1}\hat{f}\big)

•

\big(\mathcal{L}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}(\xi^{m}\sigma_{q}^{n}g)\big)=q^{-\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}x^{m}\sigma_{q}^{n-m}\big(\mathcal{L}_{q;1}^{[\lambda;q]}g\big)
.

The first equality is shown by direct calculation. The second equality can be proved by using the property (2.2) of theta function. From this proposition, we have the following important corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\hat{f}(x)\in\mathbb{C}[[x]]
be a formal solution of a linear

-difference equation. If
\hat{f}(x)
is
[\lambda;q]
-summable, its

-sum satisfies the same

-difference equation with

.

At the end of this section, we give asymptotic properties of the

-Borel–Laplace transform.

Let

,

and

\displaystyle f\sim_{1}^{[\lambda;q]}\hat{f},

if for any positive numbers

we have

\displaystyle\left|f(x)-\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}a_{n}x^{n}\right|\leq LM^{N}|q|^{-\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}|x|^{N}.

(Dreyfus–Eloy
[

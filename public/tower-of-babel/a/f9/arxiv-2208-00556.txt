The integral Chow rings of the stacks of hyperelliptic Weierstrass points
Dan Edidin, Zhengning Hu

Abstract
. We compute the integral Chow rings of the stacks ℋ g , n w {\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}^{w} parametrizing hyperelliptic curves with n n marked hyperelliptic Weierstrass points. We prove that the integral Chow ring of each of these stacks is generated as an algebra by any of the ψ \psi -classes and that all relations live in degree one.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to compute the integral Chow rings
of the stacks
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}^{w}
parametrizing smooth hyperelliptic curves
with
n
marked Weierstrass points. Aside from their intrinsic interest,
products of these stacks naturally arise as boundary strata of proper
moduli stacks of curves such as
\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{g}
, parametrizing stable hyperelliptic curves. Thus, any program to compute the integral Chow ring of
\overline{{\mathcal{H}}}_{g}
via the stratification method of
[
8
]
will require our results as input.

We now describe the moduli stacks considered in this paper.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
denote the moduli stack of smooth hyperelliptic curves, and let

\mathcal{C}_{g}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
be the universal hyperelliptic curve. A result of Kleiman and
Lønsted
[
7
]
implies that there is a smooth Cartier
divisor
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}\subset\mathcal{C}_{g}
parametrizing the hyperelliptic Weierstrass points in the fibers of
\mathcal{C}_{g}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
. Moreover, if
\Char k\neq 2
, then
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
is finite and étale of degree
2g+2
over
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
. The stack
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
which we call the
stack of hyperelliptic Weierstrass points
parametrizes families of smooth hyperelliptic curves with a Weierstrass section.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{g,w}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
be the family of hyperelliptic curves obtained by base change along the finite étale morphism
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
. The pullback
of the Weierstrass divisor
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
decomposes as the disjoint union of
the universal Weierstrass section and a divisor

{\mathcal{H}}_{g,2}^{w}
which is finite and étale over
{\mathcal{H}}^{w}_{g,1}:={\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
of degree
2g+1
. Iterating this construction, we obtain a tower of finite étale covers

{\mathcal{H}}_{g,2g+2}^{w}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g,2g+1}^{w}\to\ldots\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g}

where
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}^{w}
parametrizes hyperelliptic curves with
n
-Weierstrass sections and the morphism
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n+1}^{w}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}^{w}
is finite and étale of degree
2g+2-n
. Each of the stacks
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}^{w}
is a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe over the stack
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2-n}]
which parametrizes families of rational curves with
n
-marked points and a disjoint divisor of degree
2g+2-n
which is étale over the base.

Putting this together we obtain the following cartesian diagram (
1.1
) where the horizontal arrows are
\mu_{2}
-gerbes and the vertical arrows are finite, étale and representable.

1.1.
Statement of results

Conventions and notation.
Throughout this paper, we fix a
natural number
g\geq 2
and work over an algebraically closed field

k
whose characteristic does not
divide
2g+2
. We will use the notation
\mathbb{A}(N)
to denote the
affine space of binary forms of degree
N
. As such
\mathbb{A}(N)\simeq\mathbb{A}^{N+1}
. There is a natural action of
\GL_{2}
on
\mathbb{A}(N)
given by

A\cdot f(x)=f(A^{-1}x)
. The kernel of this action is the
diagonal subgroup
\mu_{N}
.

As noted above, the integral Chow ring of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
was computed by Edidin–Fulghesu, Fulghesu–Viviani and Di Lorenzo. In order to contrast it with
our work we restate their results.

Theorem 1.1
.

The integral Chow ring of the stack of hyperelliptic curves is as follows.

•

(Edidin–Fulghesu
[
3
]
) If
g
is even, then

A^{*}({\mathcal{H}}_{g})=\mathbb{Z}[c_{1},c_{2}]/(2(2g+1)c_{1},g(g-1)c_{1}^{2}-4g(g+1)c_{2}).

•

(Fulghesu–Viviani
[
5
]
, Di Lorenzo
[
2
]
) If
g
is odd, then

A^{*}({\mathcal{H}}_{g})=\mathbb{Z}[\tau,c_{2},c_{3}]/(4(2g+1)\tau,8\tau^{2}-2g(g+1)c_{2},2c_{3}).

Theorem 1.2
.

The integral Chow ring of
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2}]
is

A^{*}([{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2}])=\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}[\tau,c_{2},c_{3}]}{(2\tau^{2}-2g(g+1)c_{2},2(2g+1)\tau,2c_{3},p(\tau,c_{2},c_{3}))}

where

•

if
g
is odd, then

p(\tau,c_{2},c_{3})=(g+1)^{2}\tau^{g}(\tau^{2}+c_{2})^{\frac{g+1}{2}}c_{2}+\tau^{\frac{g+3}{2}}(\tau^{3}+\tau c_{2}-c_{3})^{\frac{g+1}{2}};

•

if
g
is even, then

p(\tau,c_{2},c_{3})=g(g+2)\tau^{g+1}(\tau^{2}+c_{2})^{\frac{g}{2}}c_{2}+\tau^{\frac{g}{2}}(\tau^{3}+\tau c_{2}-c_{3})^{\frac{g+2}{2}}.

Once we mark at least one Weierstrass point, the presentation of the Chow rings no longer depends on the parity of the genus
g
.

Theorem 1.3
.

The integral Chow rings of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
and
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+1}]
are

A^{*}({\mathcal{H}}_{g,w})=\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}[\psi]}{(4g(2g+1)\psi)},\quad A^{*}([{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+1}])=\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}[l]}{(2g(2g+1)l)},

respectively. Here
\psi
is the restriction of the
\psi
-class on
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,1}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

The integral Chow rings of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,2}^{w}
and
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g}]
are

A^{*}({\mathcal{H}}_{g,2}^{w})=\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}[\psi]}{(4g\psi)},\quad A^{*}([{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g}])=\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}[l]}{(2gl)},

respectively. Here
\psi
is the restriction of either of the two
\psi
-classes,

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,2}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,2}^{w}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For
3\leq n\leq 2g+2
, the integral Chow rings of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}^{w}
and
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2-n}]
are

A^{*}({\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}^{w})=\mathbb{Z}[\psi]/(2\psi),\quad A^{*}([{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2-n}])=\mathbb{Z}.

Here
\psi
is the restriction of any of the
\psi
-classes on
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,n}^{w}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

2.1.
Description of
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2}]
as a quotient
by
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\PGL_{2}

The quotient stack
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2}]
can be
identified with the stack
\mathcal{D}_{2g+2}
parametrizing pairs
(P\to S,D_{2g+2}\subset P)
where
P\to S
is a twisted
\mathbb{P}^{1}
over
S
and
D_{2g+2}
is an
effective Cartier divisor which is finite and étale over
S
of degree
2g+2
. By
[
6
, Proposition 3.4]
, the algebraic stack
\mathcal{D}_{2g+2}
can be
identified with the quotient stack

[\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)/(\GL_{2}/\mu_{2g+2})]
, where
\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)
is
the affine space of binary forms of degree
2g+2
with distinct roots, and the
action on
\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)
is given by
A\cdot f(x)=f(A^{-1}x)
.

Remark 2.1
.

By
[
1
, Theorem 4.1]
, the stack
{\mathcal{H}}_{g}
of smooth hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
is isomorphic to the quotient stack

[\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)/(\GL_{2}/\mu_{g+1})]
with action defined by
A\cdot f(x)=f(A^{-1}x)
. Consider the
short exact sequence

1\to\mu_{2}\to\mu_{2g+2}\to\mu_{g+1}\to 1

where the second arrow is
given by

\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{2}
. There is an induced map of quotient groups

\GL_{2}/\mu_{g+1}\to\GL_{2}/\mu_{2g+2}
which is a
\mu_{2}
-torsor. This in turn induces a map of quotient stacks

{\mathcal{H}}_{g}=[\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)/(\GL_{2}/\mu_{g+1})]\to\mathcal{D}_{2g+2}=[\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)/(\GL_{2}/\mu_{2g+2})]

which is a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe.

Since
2g+2
is always even,
[
1
, Proposition 4.4]

implies that the homomorphism

\GL_{2}/\mu_{2g+2}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\PGL_{2}
, given by
[A]\mapsto(\det(A)^{g+1},[A])
is an isomorphism. Under this identification of groups, the action of

\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\PGL_{2}
on the affine space of binary forms
\mathbb{A}(2g+2)
is given by

(\lambda,[A])\cdot f(x)=\lambda^{-1}\det(A)^{g+1}f(A^{-1}x)
.

2.2.
Computing the
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\PGL_{2}
-equivariant Chow ring of

\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)
using
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}
-counterparts

In order to compute the Chow ring of the quotient stack
[\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)/(\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\PGL_{2})]
, we follow the method used by Di Lorenzo in

[
2
, Section 2.3]
by finding a
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}
-counterpart of the

\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\PGL_{2}
-scheme
\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)
to resolve the issue
that the group
\PGL_{2}
is non-special. Let us briefly recall the
concepts from
[
2
]
here.

Given a
\PGL_{2}
-scheme
X
of finite type over
\Spec k
, a

\GL_{3}
-counterpart of
X
is another scheme
Y
equipped with a

\GL_{3}
-action such that
[Y/\GL_{3}]\cong[X/\PGL_{2}]
. In

[
2
, Theorem 1.4]
, Di Lorenzo proves that a
\GL_{3}
-counterpart always
exists for a given
\PGL_{2}
-scheme. Indeed, with the adjoint
representation of
\PGL_{2}
, we have a morphism of algebraic groups
\PGL_{2}\to\GL_{3}
. It follows
that
the quotient
\GL_{3}/\PGL_{2}
is a
\GL_{3}
-counterpart of
\Spec k
. Pulling back
[\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)/\PGL_{2}]
along the
\GL_{3}
-torsor

\GL_{3}/\PGL_{2}\to[\Spec k/\PGL_{2}]\cong{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
will in turn give a

\GL_{3}
-counterpart of the
\PGL_{2}
action on
\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)
. The
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}
-counterpart can be defined similarly.

Let
\Delta^{\prime}
denote the complement of
\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)
in
\mathbb{A}(2g+2)
,
i.e.
the discriminant locus of
\mathbb{A}(2g+2)
. Because the weight of the action
of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
in the quotient stack
[\mathbb{A}_{sm}(2g+2)/(\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\PGL_{2})]
presenting
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2}]
is different from the weight
of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action that Di Lorenzo used in
his computation of
A^{*}({\mathcal{H}}_{g})
for
g
odd
[
2
]
, our next proposition
describes the
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}
-counterpart for a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
action
with an arbitrary weight. Precisely, if

\lambda\cdot f=\chi(\lambda)f
for some character
\chi
of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
,
we consider the
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}
-counterparts for
\mathbb{A}(2g+2)
and
\Delta^{\prime}
, where
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\PGL_{2}

acts on
\mathbb{A}(2g+2)
by

(\lambda,A)\cdot f=\chi(\lambda)(\det A)^{g+1}f(A^{-1}x).

Proposition 2.2
.

[
2
, Proposition 2.3 & 2.6]

Let
V_{g+1}
be the scheme parametrizing pairs
(q,f)
where
q
is a global section of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}_{S}}(2)
with zero locus
Q
, and
f
is a global section of
\mathcal{O}_{Q}(g+1)
. Let
D^{\prime}\subset V_{g+1}
be the singular locus inside
V_{g+1}
. Then

•

V_{g+1}
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}
-counterpart of
\mathbb{A}(2g+2)

with the action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}
given by

(\lambda,A)\cdot(q,f):=(\det(A)q(A^{-1}x),\chi(\lambda)f(A^{-1}x)).

•

D^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}
-counterpart of
\Delta^{\prime}
.

Applying Proposition
2.2
we see that to compute

A^{*}([{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2}])
we must compute

A^{*}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}}(V_{g+1}\setminus D^{\prime})
where
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}

acts by

(\lambda,A)\cdot(q,f)=(\det(A)q(A^{-1}x),\lambda^{-1}f(A^{-1}x)).

This computation is almost identical to the computation made by
Di Lorenzo in
[
2
, Section 4]
. The only difference is when
passing to the projectivization of

in order to trivialize
the global
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action. Let
\sigma_{0}
denote the

\GL_{3}
-counterpart of

, which turns out to be the

V_{g+1}\setminus\sigma_{0}\to\mathbb{P}(V_{g+1})
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor over
\mathbb{P}(V_{g+1})
with the associated
line bundle
\mathcal{V}^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-1)
. Here

\mathcal{V}
is the standard character of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
. By contrast in
[

]

the associated line bundle is

.

Following
[
2
]
, we denote by

D
the projectivization of

.
The
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor
V_{g+1}\setminus D^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}(V_{g+1})\setminus D

induces a surjective

A^{*}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}}(\mathbb{P}(V_{g+1})\setminus D)\to A^{*}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\GL_{3}}(V_{g+1}\setminus D^{\prime})

The same method used in the calculation of

Proposition 2.3
.

The integral Chow ring of

Finally, checking that the monic polynomial

by using

3.
Description of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
and
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+1}]
as quotients
by Borel subgroups of rank 2

In this section we give a description of the stacks
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
and
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+1}]
as quotients by Borel subgroups of
\GL_{2}/\mu_{g+1}

and
\GL_{2}/\mu_{2g+2}
respectively.

Recall that Pernice
[
9
, Proposition 1.3]
proved that the stack
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,1}

is equivalent to the stack
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{g,1}
parametrizing the following data

(P\to S,{\mathcal{L}},i\colon{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{P},\sigma_{P},j)

where
P\to S
is a twisted
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
{\mathcal{L}}
is a line bundle on
P
which restricts to a line bundle of degree
-(g+1)
on the fibers of
P\to S
,

\sigma_{P}\colon S\to P
is a section and
j\colon\sigma_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
satisfies
j^{\otimes 2}=\sigma_{P}^{*}(i)
.

Given this data Pernice follows
[
1
]
to obtain a family of pointed
hyperelliptic curves
by taking the double cover

C=\Spec_{{\mathcal{O}}_{P}}({\mathcal{O}}_{P}\oplus{\mathcal{L}})\to P
where the
{\mathcal{O}}_{P}
-algebra structure on
{\mathcal{O}}_{P}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}
is induced by
i
.
If
f:C\to S
is the corresponding family
of hyperelliptic curves, then Pernice shows that the pair
(\sigma_{P},j)
determines a section

\sigma\colon S\to C
such that
\sigma_{P}=f\circ\sigma
.

Now the section
\sigma
is a Weierstrass section if and only if
\sigma
is in the ramification divisor of the double cover

C\to P
. This is equivalent to the condition that

j=0
as a map
\sigma_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
.
Putting this together we obtain
the following description of
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The stack
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
is equivalent to the stack
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{g,w}

parametrizing the data

(P\to S,{\mathcal{L}},i\colon{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{P},\sigma_{P},0).

Let
{\mathbb{H}}(2g+2,1)=\{(f,s)|f(0,1)=s^{2}\}\subset\mathbb{A}(2g+2)\times\mathbb{A}^{1}

and let
{\mathbb{H}}_{sm}(2g+2,1)
be the open set where the form
f
has distinct roots. Likewise let
{\mathbb{H}}^{w}(2g+2,1)
be the divisor defined by the equation
s=0
,
and let
{\mathbb{H}}^{w}_{sm}(2g+2,1)
be its intersection with
{\mathbb{H}}_{sm}(2g+2,1)
.
By
[
9
, Proposition 1.5]
, the stack
{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{g,1}

is equivalent to the quotient stack
[{\mathbb{H}}_{sm}(2g+2,1)/(B_{2}/\mu_{g+1})]

where
B_{2}
is the Borel subgroup of lower triangular matrices in
\GL_{2}
,
and the action is given as follows.
If
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
b&c\end{bmatrix}\in B_{2}/\mu_{g+1}
then

A\cdot(f(x),s)=\left(f(A^{-1}x),c^{-(g+1)}s\right).

Note that our notation
{\mathbb{H}}_{sm}(2g+2,1)
differs from that of
[
9
]
.

The stack
{\mathcal{H}}_{g,w}
is the quotient by
B_{2}/\mu_{g+1}
of the divisor

{\mathbb{H}}^{w}_{sm}(2g+2,1)
defined by the equation

s=0
in
{\mathbb{H}}_{sm}(2g+2,1)
.

By
[
1
, Proposition 4.4]
, the group
G=B_{2}/\mu_{g+1}
is isomorphic to
B_{2}
if
g
is even, and if
g
is odd to

\mathbb{G}_{m}\times(B_{2}/\mathbb{G}_{m})
where
B_{2}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the group of lower triangular matrices in
\PGL_{2}
.
Under these identifications, the action of
B_{2}/\mu_{g+1}
on
{\mathbb{H}}_{sm}(2g+2,1)

is given by

•

If
g
is even,
G=B_{2}
acts by

A\cdot(f(x),s):=\left((\det A)^{g}f(A^{-1}x),a^{{g\over{2}}}c^{-{{g+2}\over{2}}}s\right),\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
b&c\end{pmatrix}.

•

If
g
is odd,
G=\mathbb{G}_{m}\times(B_{2}/\mathbb{G}_{m})
acts by

(\alpha,A)\cdot\left(f(x),s\right):=\left(\alpha^{-2}\det(A)^{g+1}f(A^{-1}x),\alpha^{-1}a^{{g+1\over{2}}}c^{-{g+1\over{2}}}s\right)

where
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
b&c\end{bmatrix}
is a lower triangular matrix in
\PGL_{2}
.
Note that the expression
\det(A)^{g+1}f(A^{-1}x)
is invariant under homotheties so this gives a well-defined action of
\PGL_{2}
.

Now consider the quotient stack
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2-n}]
.

For any
n\geq 1
, the quotient stack
[{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2g+2}/S_{2g+2-n}]

parametrizes the data

We begin with a general

5.1.
The equivariant Chow ring of
\mathbb{A}(N)\setminus\Delta^{\prime}

Suppose that we are given an arbitrary action of
T=(\mathbb{G}_{m})^{2}
on
\mathbb{A}(N)
, the affine space of binary forms of degree
N
in
x_{0},x_{1}
, by

(5.1)

\displaystyle(t_{0},t_{1})\cdot f(x_{0},x_{1})=t_{0}^{a}t_{1}^{b}f(t_{0}^{-\alpha_{0}}t_{1}^{-\alpha_{1}}x_{0},t_{0}^{-\beta_{0}}t_{1}^{-\beta_{1}}x_{1}).

The goal of this section is to give a presentation for the equivariant
Chow ring
A^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{A}(N)\setminus\Delta^{\prime})
where
\Delta^{\prime}
is the
discriminant locus of
\mathbb{A}(N)
as defined before, in terms of the
weights of the action.

Let
\lambda_{1}:T\to\mathbb{G}_{m},(t_{0},t_{1})\mapsto t_{0}
and
\lambda_{2}:T\to\mathbb{G}_{m},(t_{0},t_{1})\mapsto t_{1}
be the standard generators for the character group of the torus
T
. Set
c_{1}(\lambda_{1})=l_{1}
and
c_{1}(\lambda_{2})=l_{2}
in
A^{*}(BT)
. Define the characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
by
\chi_{1}(t)=t_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}},\chi_{2}(t)=t_{0}^{\beta_{0}}t_{1}^{\beta_{1}}
, and set

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle:=c_{1}(\chi_{1})=c_{1}(\lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{0}}\lambda_{2}^{\alpha_{1}})=\alpha_{0}l_{1}+\alpha_{1}l_{2},

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle:=c_{1}(\chi_{2})=c_{1}(\lambda_{1}^{\beta_{0}}\lambda_{2}^{\beta_{1}})=\beta_{0}l_{1}+\beta_{1}l_{2}.

Let
{\mathbb{B}}(N)=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{A}(N))
be the projectivization of the affine space
\mathbb{A}(N)
of binary forms of degree
N
.
We denote by
\Delta
the image of the discriminant locus
\Delta^{\prime}
under the projectivization map.

Proposition 5.1
.

The equivariant Chow ring
A^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{A}(N)\setminus\Delta^{\prime})
is a quotient ring

\mathbb{Z}[l_{1},l_{2},\xi]/(\alpha_{1,0}(\xi),\alpha_{1,1}(\xi),al_{1}+bl_{2}-\xi,p(\xi))

where
\xi=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{B}}(N)}(1))
is the hyperplane class and

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,0}

\displaystyle=2(N-1)\xi-N(N-1)(T_{1}+T_{2})

\displaystyle=2(N-1)\xi-N(N-1)[(\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0})l_{1}+(\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1})l_{2}],

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\xi^{2}-(T_{1}+T_{2})\xi-N(N-2)T_{1}T_{2}

\displaystyle=\xi^{2}-[(\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0})l_{1}+(\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1})l_{2}]\xi-N(N-2)(\alpha_{0}l_{1}+\alpha_{1}l_{2})(\beta_{0}l_{1}+\beta_{1}l_{2}),

\displaystyle p(\xi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{N}[\xi-(N-i)T_{1}-iT_{2}]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=0}^{N}[\xi-(N-i)(\alpha_{0}l_{1}+\alpha_{1}l_{2})-i(\beta_{0}l_{1}+\beta_{1}l_{2})].

Proof.

Choose coordinates
(X_{0}:X_{1}:\ldots:X_{N})
on
{\mathbb{B}}(N)
so that the coordinate function
X_{i}
is the coefficient of
x_{0}^{N-i}x_{1}^{i}
in a binary form of degree
N
. Then there is an induced action of
T
on
{\mathbb{B}}(N)
given by

t\cdot(X_{0}:\ldots:X_{N})=(\chi_{1}^{-N}(t)X_{0}:\ldots:\chi_{1}^{-(N-i)}(t)\chi_{2}^{-i}(t)X_{i}:\ldots:\chi_{2}^{-N}(t)X_{N}).

Following
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
which is proved for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-actions in
[
4
, Section 3.3]
, we obtain that
A^{*}_{T}({\mathbb{B}}(N))=\mathbb{Z}[l_{1},l_{2},\xi]/p(\xi)
where
\xi=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{B}}(N)}(1))
is the hyperplane class, and
p(\xi)=\prod_{i=0}^{N}(\xi-(N-i)T_{1}-iT_{2})
is a degree
N+1
monic polynomial since
\{-(N-i)T_{1}-iT_{2}\}_{i=0}^{N}
is the set of equivariant Chern roots of the representation
\mathbb{A}(N)
.

By
[
3
, Proposition 4.2]
the ideal generated by the image of
A^{*}_{T}(\Delta)\to A^{*}_{T}({\mathbb{B}}(N))
is computed, and we get

A^{*}_{T}({\mathbb{B}}(N)\setminus\Delta)=A^{*}_{T}({\mathbb{B}}(N))/(\alpha_{1,0}(\xi),\alpha_{1,1}(\xi)).

Now we have a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-torsor
\mathbb{A}(N)\setminus\Delta^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{B}}(N)\setminus\Delta
associated to the line bundle
\lambda_{1}^{\otimes a}\otimes\lambda_{2}^{\otimes b}\otimes\mathcal{O}(-1)
. An argument similar to the one used in the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
shows that the pullback
A^{*}_{T}({\mathbb{B}}(N)\setminus\Delta)\to A^{*}_{T}(\mathbb{A}(N)\setminus\Delta^{\prime})
is surjective with kernel generated by
al_{1}+bl_{2}-\xi
. Substituting
\xi=al_{1}+bl_{2}
yields the presentation of the proposition.
∎

Proof.

We recall that

is

(0,1,\infty,p_{4},\ldots,p_{2g+2})

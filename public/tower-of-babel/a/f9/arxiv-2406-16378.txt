On a variant of Schu’s lemma
R. I. Boţ and C. Zălinescu

Abstract
In this note, we demonstrate that an incorrect statement has been propagated in multiple papers, stemming from the substitution of “lim” with “limsup” for a sequence in Lemma 1.3 of the paper [J. Schu: Weak and strong convergence to fixed points of asymptotically nonexpansive mappings, Bull. Austral. Math. Soc. 43 (1991), 153–159]. This occurred over a span of more than 20 years, with the earliest paper we identified using this incorrect statement dating back to 2002.



In Schu’s paper
[
1
]
, the following result is stated without
proof, with “[8]” referring to Zeidler’s book, which we cite as

[
2
]
:

“LEMMA 1.3. (compare [8, p. 484])
Let
(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
be a uniformly convex Banach space,
0<b<c<1
,
a\geq 0
,

(t_{n})\subset[b,c]^{\mathbb{N}}
and
(x_{n}),(y_{n})\in E^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\limsup\left\|x_{n}\right\|\leq a
,

\limsup\left\|y_{n}\right\|\leq a
and
\lim\left\|t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}\right\|=a
. Then
\lim\left\|x_{n}-y_{n}\right\|=0
.”

Interestingly, Dotson had presented a similar result 21 years
earlier, but most of the literature we know of using this result
attributes it to Schu. On page 68 of
[
3
]
, Dotson states the
following:

“The following lemma is an easy consequence of uniform convexity.

LEMMA 3.
Suppose
E
is a uniformly convex Banach space.
Suppose
0<a<b<1
, and
\{t_{n}\}
is a sequence in
[a,b]
. Suppose

\{w_{n}\}
,
\{y_{n}\}
are sequences in
E
such that
\left\|w_{n}\right\|\leq 1
,
\left\|y_{n}\right\|\leq 1
for all
n
.
Define
\{z_{n}\}
in
E
by
z_{n}=(1-t_{n})w_{n}+t_{n}y_{n}
. If

\lim\left\|z_{n}\right\|=1
, then
\lim\left\|w_{n}-y_{n}\right\|=0
.
”

Note that
[
3
, Lemma 3]
is recalled in
[
4
, page
376]
.

Taking
t_{n}:=t\in(0,1)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}

(:=\{1,2,...,n,...\})
,
[
1
, Lemma 1.3]
reduces to Problem
10.1 (c) from
[
2
, p. 484]
. Additionally, note that the
version of
[
1
, Lemma 1.3]
where the interval
[b,c]
is
replaced by
[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]
with

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, is attributed also to
“(Zeidler (1986))” in
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
.

In
[
6
]
, a variant of Schu’s lemma, formulated with a

weaker hypothesis
, is introduced as follows:

“In 1991, Sahu [21] established an important
property of UCBS, which can be stated as follows.

Lemma 2.2. [21]
Assume that
\Omega
is a UCBS and

\{z_{\ell}\}
be a sequence in
(0,1)
for all
\ell\geq 1
. If
\{a_{\ell}\}

and
\{b_{\ell}\}
are in
\Omega
such that
\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\left\|a_{\ell}\right\|\leq m
,
\limsup_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\left\|b_{\ell}\right\|\leq m
and
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\left\|(1-z_{\ell})a_{\ell}+z_{\ell}b_{\ell}\right\|=m
for some

m\geq 0
. Then
\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\left\|a_{\ell}-b_{\ell}\right\|=0
.
”

In fact, reference [21] from the preceding quoted text is
none other than Schu’s paper
[
1
]
. Additionally, note
that Lemma 2.5 from
[
7
]
, also attributed to

[
1
]
, is equivalent to
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Note that this variant of Schu’s lemma
[
1
, Lemma 1.3]
is

false
, as illustrated by the following example.

Example 1

Let
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
be a non-trivial normed
vector space,
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
,
x_{n}:=x
and
y_{n}:=-x
for

n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast};
moreover, consider
\{t_{n}\}\subset(0,1)
with

t_{n}\rightarrow 0
. Then
\left\|x_{n}\right\|=\left\|y_{n}\right\|=\left\|x\right\|
, and so
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|=\left\|x\right\|>0
; moreover,
t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}=(2t_{n}-1)x
, and so
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}\right\|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left|2t_{n}-1\right|\cdot\left\|x\right\|=\left\|x\right\|.

It is obvious that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-y_{n}\right\|=2\left\|x\right\|\neq 0
.

In
[
8
]
, Laowang and Panyanak introduced an extension of
Schu’s lemma to the setting of uniformly convex hyperbolic spaces,
first announced in
[
9
, Lemma 2.7]
for CAT
(0)
spaces,
as follows:

“The following result is a characterization of
uniformly convex hyperbolic spaces which is an analog of Lemma 1.3 of Schu
[25]. It can be applied to a CAT
(0)
space as well.

Lemma 2.9.
Let
(X,d,W)
be a uniformly convex hyperbolic space with
modulus of convexity
\eta
, and let
x\in X
. Suppose that
\eta
increases
with
r
(for a fixed
\varepsilon
) and suppose that
\{t_{n}\}
is a
sequence in
[b,c]
for some
b,c\in(0,1)
, and
\{x_{n}\}
,
\{y_{n}\}
are
sequences in
X
such that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},x)\leq r
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(y_{n},x)\leq r
, and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d((1-t_{n})x_{n})\oplus t_{n}y_{n},x)=r
for some
r\geq 0
. Then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},y_{n})=0
. (2.17)
”

The proof of
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
is an adaptation to this context of
that for Problem 10.1 (c) in Zeidler’s book
[
2
]
.

The formulation and the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
are replicated in

[
10
, Lemma 4.5]
, while, for
t_{n}:=1/2
for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, it can be found as Lemma 2.2 in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.9 from
[
8
]
is (practically) replicated also in
[
12
, Lemma
2.5]
, where an
alternative proof
is provided. However, this
proof contains the following
false assertion
: “Since

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(x_{n},x)\leq r
and
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}d(y_{n},x)\leq r
we have: (i)
d(x_{n},x)\leq r+\frac{1}{n}
;
(ii)
d(y_{n},x)\leq r+\frac{1}{n}
for each
n\geq 1
”.

Lemma 2.9 from
[
8
]
is also presented as Lemma 2.5 in

[
13
]
, with a reference to
[
13
, Lemma 2.3]
, and as Lemma
3.2 in
[
14
]
.

Note that
[
8
, Lemma 2.9]
is stated and proved for
X
as a
CAT
(0)
space in
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
under the supplementary
condition
0<b(1-c)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
. This condition appears in many papers
from our bibliography. It is easy to observe that this condition is
superfluous. Indeed,
b,c\in(0,1)
implies that
0<b(1-c)
, while the fact
that (there exists)
t_{n}\in[b,c]
implies that
b\leq c
; hence

0<b\leq c<1
. Because
b^{2}+(b-1)^{2}=2b^{2}-2b+1>0
and
b>0
, it follows
that
\frac{1}{2b}>1-b\geq 1-c
. Consequently,
b(1-c)<\frac{1}{2}
.

Furthermore, an extension of Schu’s lemma to the setting of modular
function spaces was introduced in
[
16
]
(see also

[
17
, Lemma 4.2]
):

“Lemma 3.2.
Let
\rho\in\Re
be (UUC1) and
let
\{t_{k}\}\subset(0,1)
be bounded away from
0
and
1
. If there exists

R>0
such that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho(f_{n})\leq R
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho(g_{n})\leq R
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho(t_{n}f_{n}+(1-t_{n})g_{n})=R
, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho(f_{n}-g_{n})=0.
”

In the following we present an
alternative proof for

[
1
, Lemma 1.3]
:

Since
\limsup\left\|x_{n}\right\|\leq a
and
\limsup\left\|y_{n}\right\|\leq a
, there exists
R>0
such that
\left\|x_{n}\right\|,\left\|y_{n}\right\|\leq R
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
(E,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
being uniformly convex, by

[
18
, Theorem 4.1 (ii)]
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2}
is a
uniformly convex function on
B:=\{x\in E\mid\left\|x\right\|\leq R\}
. This means that there exists a strictly increasing function

\rho:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
\rho(0)=0
, such that

\left\|\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\right\|^{2}\leq\lambda\left\|x\right\|^{2}+(1-\lambda)\left\|y\right\|^{2}-\lambda(1-\lambda)\rho\left(\left\|x-y\right\|\right)\quad\forall x,y\in B,\ \forall\lambda\in[0,1].

Setting
\gamma:=\min\{b,1-c\}

(>0)
, one has
\lambda(1-\lambda)\geq\gamma^{2}
, and so

\gamma^{2}\rho\left(\left\|x_{n}-y_{n}\right\|\right)\leq t_{n}\left\|x_{n}\right\|^{2}+(1-t_{n})\left\|y_{n}\right\|^{2}-\left\|t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}\right\|^{2}\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1)

Assume that the conclusion does not hold. Then there exist a
strictly increasing sequence
(n_{k})\subset\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
and

\varepsilon>0
such that
t_{n_{k}}\rightarrow t\in[b,c]
as

k\to\infty
, and
\left\|x_{n_{k}}-y_{n_{k}}\right\|\geq\varepsilon
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
. Replacing, if
necessary,
n
by
n_{k}
in (
1
), using that
\rho\left(\left\|x_{n_{k}}-y_{n_{k}}\right\|\right)\geq\rho(\varepsilon)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, and passing to

\limsup

in (
1
) as
k\to\infty
, one gets the contradiction
\gamma^{2}\rho(\varepsilon)\leq ta^{2}+(1-t)a^{2}-a^{2}=0
.
\square

[

, Kim, Kiuchi, and Takahashi introduced a variant
of Schu’s lemma in uniformly convex Banach spaces, assuming a

, with “[9]” referring to
[

“Lemma 2.4 ([9]).
Let
E
be a uniformly convex
Banach space, let
0<b\leq t_{n}\leq c<1
for all

\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
be sequences of E such that
\overline{\lim}\left\|x_{n}\right\|\leq a
,
\overline{\lim}\left\|y_{n}\right\|\leq a
, and
\overline{\lim}\left\|t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}\right\|=a
for some

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-y_{n}\right\|=0
.
”

No proof is given for
[

. However, Agarwal,
O’Regan, and Sahu formulated the following result in their book

[

]
, for which a proof is also given:

“Theorem 2.3.13
Let

be a uniformly convex Banach
space and let
\{t_{n}\}
be a sequence of real numbers in
(0,1)

and

\{x_{n}\}
and

such that

{\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|\leq a,\ \ {\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\left\|y_{n}\right\|\leq a}\text{\ \ {and}}{\ ~\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}\right\|=a}

for some
a\geq 0
. Then
\lim\left\|x_{n}-y_{n}\right\|=0

Replacing

by

in
[

(\alpha,\beta)

one gets the

In fact
[
19
, Lemma 2.4]
(as well as
[
20
, Theorem 2.3.13]
) is
false
, as demonstrated by the following
example.

Example 2

Let
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|)
be a non-trivial normed
vector space,
x\in X
with
\left\|x\right\|=1

(=:a)
,
x_{n}:=x,

y_{2n}:=0,

y_{2n-1}:=x,

t_{n}:=\lambda\in(0,1)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
. Then
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|=1,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|y_{n}\right\|=1,

t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}=\lambda x
for even
n
,
t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}=x

for odd
n
, and so
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|t_{n}x_{n}+(1-t_{n})y_{n}\right\|=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}1=1.
It is
obvious that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|x_{n}-y_{n}\right\|

does not exist, and so it is different from
0
.

Complete characterization of non-Abelian topological phase transitions and detection of anyon splitting with projected entangled pair states
Wen-Tao Xu, Jose Garre-Rubio, Norbert Schuch

Abstract
It is well-known that many topological phase transitions of intrinsic Abelian topological phases are accompanied by condensation and confinement of anyons. However, for non-Abelian topological phases, more intricate phenomena can occur at their phase transitions, because the multiple degenerate degrees of freedom of a non-Abelian anyon can change in different ways after phase transitions. In this paper, we study these new phenomena, including partial condensation, partial deconfinement and especially anyon splitting (a non-Abelian anyon splits into different kinds of the new anyon species) using projected entangled pair states (PEPS). First, we show that anyon splitting can be observed from the topologically degenerate ground states. Next, we construct a set of PEPS describing all possible degrees of freedom of the same non-Abelian anyon. From the overlaps of this set of PEPS of a given non-Abelian anyon with the ground state, we can extract the information of partial condensation. Then, we construct a central object, a matrix defined by the norms and overlaps among the PEPS in that set. The information of partial deconfinement can be extracted from this matrix. In particular, we use it to construct an order parameter which can directly detect anyon splitting. We demonstrate the power of our approach by applying it to a range of non-Abelian topological phase transitions: From D ⁡ ( S 3 ) D(S_{3}) quantum double to toric code, from D ⁡ ( S 3 ) D(S_{3}) quantum double to D ⁡ ( Z 3 ) D(Z_{3}) quantum double, from Rep ( S 3 ) (S_{3}) string-net to toric code, and finally from double Ising string-net to toric code.



I
Introduction

Since the discovery of the fractional quantum Hall effect
Tsui et al. 1982
;
Laughlin 1983
, topological phases of matter have become an essential concept in modern condensed matter physics. A remarkable feature of topological phases is that they can support exotic excitations with fractional statistics: the so-called anyons. Importantly, non-Abelian anyons carry internal degrees of freedom (DOFs), which are essential to perform fault-tolerant quantum computation
Kitaev 2003
. Recently, topological phases have been experimentally implemented on quantum processors
Satzinger et al. 2021
and quantum simulators
Semeghini et al. 2021
. Theoretically, topological phases can be realized by exactly solvable lattice models, such as the Kitaev’s quantum double models
Kitaev 2003
and the Levin-Wen string-net models
Levin and Wen 2005
.

A powerful tool to study topologically ordered phases are projected entangled pair states (PEPS). On the one hand, PEPS can not only represent the ground states of the quantum double models and the string-net models exactly
Gu et al. 2009
;
Buerschaper et al. 2009
, but also characterize their topological order at the entanglement level of PEPS
Schuch et al. 2010
;
Bultinck et al. 2017
. On the other hand, PEPS can be used to study topological phase transitions by either deforming the PEPS representing the ground states of these exactly solvable models
Mariën et al. 2017
;
Haegeman et al. 2015a
;
Haegeman et al. 2015b
;
Xu and Zhang 2018
;
Zhu and Zhang 2019
;
Xu et al. 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Xu and Schuch 2021
, or serving as an ansatz for numerical variational optimizations
Vanderstraeten et al. 2017
;
Schotte et al. 2019
;
Iqbal and Schuch 2021
.

Through a topological phase transition, anyons can disappear from the excitation spectrum in two ways: First, anyons can condense into ground states, and second, anyons become confined into pairs. Due to the lack of local order parameters to characterize topological phases, it is challenging to study topological phase transitions. Among the various approaches to study topological phase transitions, the PEPS approach stands out as a very powerful one, because PEPS are not only capable of describing anyon condensation, confinement and identification through order parameters on the entanglement level of PEPS, but can also be used to study the nature of a topological phase transition via critical exponents extracted from these order parameters
Duivenvoorden et al. 2017
;
Iqbal et al. 2018
. Recently, some progress has been made to generalize these order parameters to non-Abelian topological phase transitions of string-net models
Xu and Schuch 2021
. For non-Abelian theories, the ways in which the behavior of anyons changes after topological phase transitions are more intricate. First, a non-Abelian anyon can simultaneously condense into the vacuum and become identified with other deconfined anyons, which we dub partial condensation. Second, a non-Abelian anyon of the initial topological phase can turn into another anyon of the new topological phase with smaller quantum dimension, often into an Abelian anyon, through a mechanism called partial deconfinement. Third, a non-Abelian anyon might split into two or more distinct anyons in the new topological phase; such a phenomenon is dubbed anyon splitting. Unlike the condensation and deconfinement order parameters for Abelian anyons, there is no obvious way to construct order parameters directly detecting anyon splitting.

In this paper, we show that anyon splitting can be observed from the transfer operator spectrum of a topologically degenerate ground state. To define the non-Abelian anyonic order parameters, we construct a complete set of PEPS carrying all possible DOFs of a given non-Abelian anyon. This set of PEPS allows us to generalize the condensate and deconfinement order parameters for Abelian anyons, which are numbers, to two matrices
N
and
M
for non-Abelian anyons, which are the key objects in our analysis. The entries of
N
are overlaps between the ground state and the aforementioned set of PEPS, while the entries of
M
are norms or overlaps among PEPS in that set. From
N
and
M
, we can extract the information of partial condensation and partial deconfinement respectively. We further demonstrate that if the anyon does not split, the matrix
M
has a tensor product structure. By taking the structure of
M
into consideration, we then construct order parameters which allow to directly probe anyon splitting.

We apply the generalized condensation and deconfinement order parameters as well as the splitting order parameters to various topological phase transitions, including the phase transition between the
D(S_{3})
quantum double and the toric code, the phase transition between the Rep
(S_{3})
string-net and the toric code, the phase transition between the
D(S_{3})
quantum double and the
D(Z_{3})
quantum double, and finally the phase transition between the double Ising (DIsing) string-net and the toric code. We also propose a three-parameter phase diagram for the deformed
D(S_{3})
quantum double PEPS and a two-parameter phase diagram for the deformed DIsing string-net PEPS. In addition, we also show that the PEPS describing these topological phase transitions can be exactly mapped to many well-known statistical mechanics models, including the Ising model, the Potts models, and the Ashkin-Teller model.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the PEPS description of topological states and anyon excitations, focusing the construction of non-Abelian anyons in PEPS. In Sec.
III
, we generalize condensate and deconfinement order parameters to the non-Abelian setting, and propose new order parameters for anyon splitting. In Sec.
IV
, we apply our approach to the
D(S_{3})
quantum double model and the Rep
(S_{3})
string-net model. In Sec.
V
, we apply our approach to the DIsing string-net model. Conclusions and discussions and are presented in Sec.
VI
. We provide technical details and proofs in Appendices.

II
PEPS characterization of topological phases

II.1
PEPS and matrix product operators

Projected entangled pair states (PEPS) are states defined by local tensors placed on the vertices of a two-dimensional lattice. An example of such a PEPS is illustrated in Fig.
1
(a) for the square lattice. The PEPS is given by the contraction of the indices corresponding to the edges of the lattice, the so-called virtual indices, of every local tensor. Throughout the paper we will use the standard graphical representation of tensors, where tensors are depicted as shapes and their indices are represented as legs. For the square lattice, the local tensor is a sphere with five legs: one leg corresponding to a physical index and the other four legs for the virtual indices.

Despite of its simplicity, the framework of PEPS is able to capture nontrivial phases of matter. In particular, the local structure characterizing PEPS with
nonchiral
topological order has been developed
Schuch et al. 2010
;
Buerschaper 2014
;
Bultinck et al. 2017
;
Şahinoğlu et al. 2021
. It turns out that topological order in PEPS is characterized by symmetries acting purely on the virtual level. Concretely, the input data of topological order are a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, with simple objects
a,b,c,....
satisfying
a\otimes b=\bigoplus_{c}N_{ab}^{c}c
where
N_{ab}^{c}
is the fusion multiplicity. The virtual symmetry is realized by matrix product operators (MPOs)
\{O_{a}|a\in\mathcal{C}\}
, which form a representation of the fusion category
\mathcal{C}
:
O_{a}O_{b}=\sum_{c}N_{ab}^{c}O_{c}
. The MPO symmetry can be exactly derived from the PEPS with an analytic form, or extracted numerically from generic variational PEPS
Francuz et al. 2020
;
Francuz and Dziarmaga 2020
. The local tensors of the PEPS satisfy the so-called pulling through condition when acting with the MPOs on the virtual level, see Fig.
1
(b). In this case the PEPS with these symmetries are called MPO-injective PEPS
Şahinoğlu et al. 2021
.

The pulling through condition implies that any of these MPOs inserted at the virtual level of the PEPS can be moved freely, so that local operators, such as the local Hamiltonian terms, cannot detect them. Therefore, the PEPS with nontrivial MPOs at the virtual level naturally represent the different ground states of the Hamiltonian hosting a topological phase associated with
\mathcal{C}
, see Fig.
1
(c). Fig.
1
(d) shows the most general ground state that can be constructed by inserting the MPOs along the two directions. In the crossing point of the MPOs, two new tensor different from the previous ones are placed. We denote the set of these enlarged MPOs, including the two new tensors plus the MPOs in one direction, as
\left\{O_{ac}^{bd}\right\}
, which form a
C^{*}
-algebra
Lan and Wen 2014
;
Bultinck et al. 2017
.
That is, these MPOs form a closed algebra under multiplication and Hermitian conjugation. Moreover,
O_{ac}^{bd}
reduces to the original MPO
O_{d}
when
a=c=1
since this implies that
b=d
, so that
O_{d}=O_{11}^{dd}
.

(a) Graphical representation of a PEPS on a square lattice, local tensor depicted on the bottom-right. (b) The local tensor of a PEPS describing a topologically ordered state satisfies the pulling through condition, where the blue lines denotes the MPO
O_{a}
. (c) Inserting an MPO
O_{a}
at the virtual level of the PEPS results in a new ground state. (d) General form of a ground state on the cylinder for topologically ordered PEPS with MPOs in the two directions cross forming the enlarged MPO

, the green dots are tensors at the crossing.

II.2
Ground states and excited states in terms of PEPS

In the following, we will review the PEPS construction of the ground space and the anyonic excitations of MPO-injective PEPS
Bultinck et al. 2017
;
Bultinck et al. 2017
;
Şahinoğlu et al. 2021
. The construction will be given in terms of the idempotents of the algebra formed by the enlarged MPOs. We will also explicitly show how the internal states of non-Abelian anyons are realized in the PEPS framework.

It is known that any
C^{*}
-algebra can be decomposed into its central idempotents, which we denote by
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},\dots
; they are of the form

P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\sum_{abd}C^{bd}_{aa}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})O_{aa}^{bd},

(1)

where the coefficients
C^{bd}_{aa}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
are determined by the algebra, as shown in Appendix
A
. The central idempotents are Hermitian and orthogonal to each other:

(P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})^{\dagger}=P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\quad P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}P^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=\delta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha\gamma}}P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}.

(2)

Importantly, these central idempotents are in correspondence with the anyons of the model. Concretely,
P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
defines the subspace at the virtual level that corresponds to the topological sector in which the anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is supported.

There are algebras of enlarged MPOs (related to non-Abelian topological order) where the central idempotents defined in
Eq. (
1
) can be decomposed further into its simple components:

P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\sum_{a}P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{aa},\quad P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{aa}=\sum_{bd}C_{aa}^{bd}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})O_{aa}^{bd},

(3)

where
P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{aa}
is a simple idempotent satisfying
(P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{aa})^{2}=P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{aa}
. The nilpotents of the algebra can also be defined in these terms
Bultinck et al. 2017
:

P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ac}=\sum_{bd}C_{ac}^{bd}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})O_{ac}^{bd},\quad a\neq c

(4)

and they satisfy
(P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ac})^{2}=0
.
The general relations between simple idempotents and the nilpotents are

P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{pq}P^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{mn}=\delta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha\gamma}}\delta_{qm}P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{pn},\quad(P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{pq})^{\dagger}=P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{qp}.

(5)

Fig.
2
(a) shows a PEPS on a cylinder with the simple idempotent or nilpotent
P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{ac}
inserted at the virtual level. On the torus, we must have
a=c
, so we obtain the ground state
|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{aa}\rangle
. Using Eq. (
5
), it can be proven that
|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{aa}\rangle
with different
a
labels are equal; we thus drop the label
a
and denote
|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle\equiv|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{aa}\rangle
from now on. These states correspond to the so-called minimally entangled states (MES)
Zhang et al. 2012
.

In what follows we show how the anyons are constructed using the formalism of PEPS. Given a non-Abelian anyon
{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
of the model, the state
|\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{x},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{y}\rangle_{s}
on a torus with the internal DOFs
x,y
of a pair of anyons
(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
can be constructed by inserting at the virtual level of the PEPS the open MPO string
O_{s}
and dressing the ends with the tensors
(E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{sy})^{\dagger}
and
E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{sx}
, see Fig.
2
(c). In order to construct a well-defined
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
anyon, the end point tensor
E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{sx}
should satisfy the following property under the action of the simple idempotents and nilpotents of the algebra:

P^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{s^{\prime}t}E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{sx}=\delta_{\boldsymbol{\alpha\gamma}}\delta_{st}E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{s^{\prime}x}\ .

(6)

Fig.
2
(b) shows the case when
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}
and
s=t
: the string attached to the anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is changed from
s
to
s^{\prime}
by the nilpotent
P^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{s^{\prime}s}
when
s^{\prime}\neq s
. Using this property, it can be derived that the string

between a pair of anyons is arbitrary, that is,
|\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{x},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{y}\rangle_{s}
and
|\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{x},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{y}\rangle_{s^{\prime}}
are the same state, so we simply denote the state by
|\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{x},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{y}\rangle
.

Notice that an isolated anyon can be considered in an infinite large system (with open boundary conditions). Specifically, the state
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{s}
can be constructed by inserting into the infinite PEPS the tensor
E^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{sx}
with a semi-infinite MPO string
O_{s}
attached to it, as shown in Fig.

|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{s}
and
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{t}

The set of tensors

satisfying property (

, as shown in Appendix

and we further impose the normalization condition for open boundary conditions:

by multiplying the end tensor

III
PEPS characterization of topological phase transitions

In this section, we describe the different ways in which anyons are affected by topological phase transitions, and show how they can be characterized through the transfer operator of the PEPS, as well as through anyonic order parameters. Importantly, we extend the framework developed in Ref.
Duivenvoorden et al. 2017
;
Iqbal et al. 2018
;
Iqbal and Schuch 2021
for Abelian theories to non-Abelian theories by generalizing the condensate and deconfinement order parameters and proposing a novel order parameter for anyon splitting.

III.1
Anyons after topological phase transitions

After a topological phase transition, the anyons of the initial topological phase can suffer from condensation, identification, confinement and splitting. These changes in the nature of the anyons can be predicted by some general rules
Bais et al. 2002
;
Bais and Slingerland 2009
using the properties of the anyons. Let us briefly review them here.

Only an anyon
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
with trivial self-statistics can condense.
When
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
is non-Abelian, its self-statistics depends on the fusion channels, and it can condense when its self-statistics is trivial in at least one fusion channel. Noncondensed anyons which differ between themselves just by a condensed anyon
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
become identical, i.e. they are identified as the same anyon. Any anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
whose mutual statistics with a condensed anyon
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
is nontrivial becomes confined. The mutual statistics between non-Abelian anyons
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
depends on their fusion channels. It can thus happen that in some channels the non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
has trivial statistics with the condensed anyon
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
, while in other channels is has nontrivial statistics; then, in the former channels
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is deconfined, while in the latter channels
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is confined. We dub this scenario partial deconfinement. In such a situation, the non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
becomes anther anyon with a smaller quantum dimension in the new topological phase.

Let us assume that after a phase transition the condensate is given by the set
S_{\text{cond}}=\{\boldsymbol{1},\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\delta},\cdots\}
, which consists of anyons of the initial topological order. If two identical non-Abelian anyons
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
can fuse into two or more different anyons in the condensate, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\otimes\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta}\oplus\cdots
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\delta}\in S_{\text{cond}}
, then
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits into two or more distinguishable anyons
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{x}\oplus\boldsymbol{y}\oplus\cdots
, where
\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}
etc. are the anyons of the new topological order. It could happen that a partially deconfined anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits. In addition, when the non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits into the trivial particle
\boldsymbol{1}
, we say that the non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
partially condenses.

III.2
Transfer operator characterization of topological phase transitions

The different changes of the nature of the anyons after a topological phase transition can be read off from the leading eigenvalue of the PEPS transfer operator
Schuch et al. 2013
. We first review this characterization of condensation and confinement, and then demonstrate that also anyon splitting can be observed through the leading eigenvalue of the PEPS transfer operator.

We denote by
\mathbb{T}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
the transfer operator of the MES overlap
\langle\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\rangle
, as shown in Fig.
2
(e). For Abelian theories, the MES can be obtained by using the central idempotents (they are also simple) defined in the previous section.
Let us denote the dominant eigenvalue of
\mathbb{T}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
as
t^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
, and define
\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=t^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}/t^{\boldsymbol{1}}_{\boldsymbol{1}}
, where
\boldsymbol{1}
denotes to the trivial anyonic sector.
The following characterization for an Abelian anyon arises
Schuch et al. 2013
: if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
condenses,
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{1}}=1
; if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
are identified,
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=1
; if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is confined,
\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}<1
.

This characterization can be generalized to non-Abelian theories. First of all, the definition of
\mathbb{T}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}
, and thus of
\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, is more subtle in the non-Abelian case. This is because every central idempotent can be decomposed into its simple idempotents, see Eq. (
3
). It can be shown that the transfer operators defined by all different simple idempotents of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
share the same spectrum, so that
\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
from the central idempotent is naturally degenerate. However, restricting to one of the simple sectors lifts these irrelevant degeneracies. So we adopt the simple idempotents for non-Abelian anyons instead of central ones. Second, if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
partially condenses and partially identifies with
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
, we can still observe that
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{1}}=1
and
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=1
. In summary:

(i) If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
fully or partially condenses,
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{1}}=1
;

(ii)If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
are fully or partially identified,
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=1
;

(iii)If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully confined,
\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}<1
.

We remark that MES change in the same way as the changes of anyons. For example, if anyons
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
are identified,
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=1
implies that MES
|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle
and
|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\rangle
are the same state; if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is confined,
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}<1
implies the MES
|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle
becomes a null state. We also remark that if the non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully deconfined before a phase transition and partially deconfined after a phase transition,
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
is always one and its degeneracy does not change, so we cannot differentiate between partial and full deconfinement. The reason behind this deficiency is that there is only one MES for a given of non-Abelian anyon, which cannot capture the change of its internal property (quantum dimension). In the next subsection, we show that by considering the internal structure of a non-Abelian anyon excitation, we can differ full and partial deconfinement.

In the following Let us consider the characterization of anyon splitting. Assuming that the non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits into various Abelian anyons
\boldsymbol{x}
:
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{x}}c_{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{x}}\boldsymbol{x}
, where
c_{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{x}}
is the multiplicity, we claim that:

(iv) If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits,
\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=1
and deg
(\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\geqslant\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}}c_{\boldsymbol{\alpha x}}^{2}
;

where deg
(\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
stands for the degeneracy of
\lambda^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
. In Ref.
Fernández-González et al. 2016
and all of the examples studied in Section
IV
, the degeneracies can be observed. Let us explain how anyon splitting contributes to this degeneracy. Because there is a one-to-one correspondence between the MES and the types of anyons, after splitting
|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle
becomes a superposition of the MES
|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle
of the new anyons
\boldsymbol{x}
:

|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}}c_{\boldsymbol{\alpha x}}|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle,

(9)

where
\langle\Psi^{\boldsymbol{x}}|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{x}}\rangle=\langle\Psi^{\boldsymbol{y}}|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{y}}\rangle,\forall\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}
and
\langle\Psi^{\boldsymbol{x}}|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{y}}\rangle=0,\forall\boldsymbol{x}\neq\boldsymbol{y}
. We see that an MES splits in the same way as the splitting of corresponding anyon. Combine with the changes of the MES in the cases of identification and confinement, we can understand how ground space varies through phase transitions.

From Eq. (
9
), it can be found that the norm
\langle\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|\Psi^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\rangle
equals to a sum of the norms of the
\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}}c_{\boldsymbol{\alpha x}}^{2}
new MES, from which it can be derived that the degeneracy of
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
is at least
\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}}c_{\boldsymbol{\alpha x}}^{2}
.
In Appendix
B
, we prove Eq. (
9
) and derive this degeneracy for the quantum double models.
The degeneracy indicates that there should exist a correlation function with long range order associated with anyon splitting, and this long range order is just the order parameter of anyon splitting.

III.3
Condensate, identification and deconfinement order parameters of non-Abelian anyons

The changes in the topological nature of the anyons can also be detected through anyonic order parameters defined at the virtual level of PEPS
Duivenvoorden et al. 2017
;
Iqbal et al. 2018
;
Iqbal and Schuch 2021
. Compared to the characterization through the transfer operator, anyonic order parameters are physically more tangible and easier to calculate. Importantly, we will see that the anyonic order parameters can detect partial confinement, which cannot be observed from the leading eigenvalue
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
of the transfer operator. Moreover, when the topological phase transitions are continuous, there are critical exponents
\beta
associated with the anyonic order parameters
O\propto|k-k_{c}|^{\beta}
, where
k
is the tuning parameter and
k_{c}
is the critical point, which can be used to identify the universality class of the phase transition.

In essence, the anyonic order parameters detect the changes of the symmetry breaking patterns of the fixed point space of the transfer operator
\mathbb{T}
. For instance, suppose that the virtual symmetry of the PEPS is described by a group
G
and the MPOs are
O_{g},g\in G
, the transfer operator
\mathbb{T}
has the symmetry
G\otimes G
. The fixed point space of
\mathbb{T}
can be degenerate, implying the spontaneous symmetry breaking of
\mathbb{T}
. Let
H\subset G
be a subgroup of
G
and
Q\subset H
be a normal subgroup of
H
. If a fixed point
\rho
of
\mathbb{T}
satisfies
O_{h}\rho O_{h}^{\dagger}=\rho,h\in H
and
O_{q}\rho=\rho O^{\dagger}_{q}=\rho,q\in Q
, then other fixed points of
\mathbb{T}
have the symmetries isomorphic to that of
\rho
, i.e., the fixed point
\rho^{\prime}=\rho O_{k},k\notin Q
satisfies
O_{h}\rho^{\prime}O_{k^{-1}hk}^{\dagger}=\rho^{\prime}
and
O_{q}\rho^{\prime}=\rho^{\prime}O^{\dagger}_{k^{-1}qk}=\rho^{\prime}
. So we denote the symmetry of the fixed point space of
\mathbbm{T}
using the symbol
H\boxtimes Q
, which comes from Ref.
Duivenvoorden et al. 2017
;
Iqbal et al. 2018
, and
H
is called diagonal symmetry while
Q
is called off-diagonal symmetry. Particularly, the symmetry of the fixed point space of
\mathbb{T}
in the original phase is always
G\boxtimes Z_{1}
. For the PEPS whose virtual MPO symmetry is described a unitary fusion category, the diagonal symmetry of the fixed point
\rho
of
\mathbb{T}
is
\{O_{a}|O_{a}\rho O_{a}^{\dagger}=\rho+\cdots\}
and the off-diagonal symmetry is
\{O_{b}|O_{b}\rho=\rho O_{b}^{\dagger}=\rho+\cdots\}
, where ”
\cdots
” denotes other possible fixed points of
\mathbb{T}
. In addition, we emphasize that for these non-Abelian PEPS, different transfer operator fixed points can have different symmetries which are also isomorphic to each other under the unitary fusion category.

In the following, we provide the definitions of the order parameters for condensation, identification and deconfinement in non-Abelian theories, which naturally extend the ones defined for Abelian anyons
Iqbal et al. 2018
. Let us denote the PEPS without any anyon as
|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle
on an infinite plane (open boundary conditions) and normalize it as
\langle\boldsymbol{1}|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle=1
. In this situation we can define a state
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{s}
with an isolated anyon, as shown in Fig.
2
(d). The order parameters are based on the overlaps
{}_{s}\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{y}\rangle_{t}
, shown in Fig.
2
(f).

To detect the condensation of a non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, we can compute a matrix
N^{\boldsymbol{1}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
with entries

N^{\boldsymbol{1}}_{sx}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\langle\boldsymbol{1}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{s}.

(10)

If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
does not condense,
N^{\boldsymbol{1}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=0
, and a nonzero
N(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
implies that
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
condenses. We say that

(i)
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
fully condenses if the rank of
N^{\boldsymbol{1}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=0
is full;

(ii)
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
partially condenses if
N^{\boldsymbol{1}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\neq 0
and its rank is not full.

Since
N(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is non-Hermitian in general, we use its singular values to characterize its rank. If we want to detect the identification of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
with an Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
, we can proceed similarly and compute a matrix
N^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
with entries

N^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}_{sx}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\langle\boldsymbol{\gamma}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{s}

(11)

and its singular values. In addition, from the nonzero singular values of
N^{\boldsymbol{1}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
and
N^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
, the critical exponents associated with the partial condensation and identification can be defined; we will find later that they are universal for all models we study.

In order to detect the confinement of the non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, we can consider the matrix
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
with entries

M_{sx,ty}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})={}_{s}\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{y}\rangle_{t}.

(12)

The matrix
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is Hermitian and semi-positive definite. The space
\mathcal{V}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
where
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is supported is a tensor product of a space
\mathcal{V}_{\text{str}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
associated with the string labels and a space
\mathcal{V}_{\text{int}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
associated with the internal DOF labels:
\mathcal{V}_{\text{str}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\otimes\mathcal{V}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{int}}
. If the anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully confined, the matrix
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=0
. However, when the anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully or partially deconfined,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
can be nonzero. To distinguish the two cases, we define the matrix

M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
by tracing the subspace
\mathcal{V}_{\text{int}}
:

M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):=\text{tr}_{\text{int}}[M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})].

(13)

Notice that for the string-net models, every label
x
is associated with a quantum dimension
d_{x}
; when doing the partial trace we account for the quantum dimensions, i.e.,
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{st}=\sum_{x}d_{x}M_{sx,tx}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
, and it is implicit in the partial trace. We say that

(iii)
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully deconfined if the rank of
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is full;

(iv)
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is partially deconfined if
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\neq 0
and its rank is not full.

Since
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is Hermitian, we use its eigenvalues to characterize the rank.

Let us explain why our proposal works. Before phase transitions, there is a transfer operator fixed point
\rho
with symmetry
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes Z_{1}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is the MPO symmetry of the PEPS (
Z_{1}
is the trivial group with one element). Suppose that the MPO strings
\{O_{s},O_{t},\cdots\}=\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\subset\mathcal{C}
can be attached to
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, we must have
M_{sx,ty}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\propto\delta_{st}
, because the fixed point
\rho
has no off-diagonal symmetry, see proof in Appendix.
C
. Therefore
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is block diagonal in
\mathcal{V}_{\text{str}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, and for convenience, we define for every diagonal block the matrix
B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
supported in the subspace
\mathcal{V}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{int}}
with entries
B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})_{xy}=M_{sx,sy}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
. Moreover, using Eq. (
6
), we prove that
\forall s,B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
are equal in Appendix.
C
, so we can drop the superscript
s
. Then,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has the following tensor product structure:

M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\mathbbm{1}_{\text{str}}\otimes B_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}),

(14)

where
\mathbbm{1}_{\text{str}}
is an identity matrix in the space
\mathcal{V}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{\text{str}}
. So
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
defined in Eq. (
13
) has
|\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|
(cardinal number of
\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
) eigenvalues all equal to
\text{tr}[B_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})]
before a phase transition.

In the following we consider what happens with
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
after topological phase transitions. For simplicity, we assume that the symmetry of the transfer operator fixed point
\rho
breaks from
\mathcal{C}\boxtimes Z_{1}
down to
\mathcal{D}\boxtimes Z_{1}
, where
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C}
. If
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\equiv\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\cap\mathcal{D}=\varnothing
, where
\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
is the set of strings that can be attached to
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, then
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=0
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully confined.

If
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has the form

M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\bigoplus_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}).

(15)

Notice that the blocks
B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
are not necessarily equal.
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has
|\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|
nonzero eigenvalues
\text{tr}[B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})],\forall s\in\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, so
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully deconfined.

If
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\neq\varnothing
and
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\neq\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, we must have
B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=0,\forall s\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\perp}\equiv(\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}-\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})
, and
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has the form:

M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\bigoplus_{s\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}).

(16)

Then
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has
|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}|
nonzero eigenvalues
\text{tr}[B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})],s\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, and
|\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\perp}|
zero eigenvalues. So
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is partially deconfined and the quantum dimension of the anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
reduces from
d_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}d_{s}
to
d_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\sum_{s\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}d_{s}
.

There are some comments about partial deconfinement. First, when the transfer operator fixed points have an off-diagonal symmetry after phase transitions,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is not block diagonal. However we can always find an unitary transformation
U_{\text{str}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\text{int}}
to block diagonalize
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
, as shown in Appendix
C
, so our approach can still distinguish partial and full deconfinement from the eigenvalues of
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
. Second, one may wonder why we do not use the eigenvalues of
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
themselves to characterize the partial deconfinement of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
. The reason is that the eigenvalues of
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
can be zero even if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully deconfined, which could happen at the renormalization fixed point of the
\mathcal{D}\boxtimes Z_{1}
phase. So the eigenvalues of
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
are better suited to characterize the partial deconfinement of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
.

III.4
Order parameters of anyon splitting

After a phase transition, if the anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully or partially deconfined, it may become a non-simple anyon, i.e., it may split into various new anyons of the new topological order. We will discuss the construction of the anyon splitting order parameters in this subsection. First, Let us consider the case where the anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
does not split. When
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is fully deconfined, it can be seen that also after the phase transition
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has the form shown in Eq. (
14
), see Appendix
D
.
We also show that in Appendix
D
when
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
is partially deconfined,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=P_{\text{str}}\otimes B_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
, where
P_{\text{str}}
is a projector. Therefore, if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
does not split,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has a tensor product structure, which means the string DOFs and internal DOFs are not entangled. Equivalently, if
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
does not have such a tensor product structure, then
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
must split.

We now define the anyon splitting order parameters using the matrix
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
. At the renormalization fixed point of the topological phase, because of the orthonormality shown in Eq. (
8
),
B_{\text{int}}=\mathbbm{1}
. However, if we perturb away from the renormalization fixed point, the orthonormality may be violated even before the phase transition. To define a well-behaved splitting order parameter, it is necessary to work in an orthonormal basis. Without loss of generality, we assume that
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is already block diagonalized by
U_{\text{str}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\text{int}}
. If there is partial deconfinement, then some diagonal blocks
B^{[s]}_{\text{int}}=0,\forall s\in\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\perp}
and we can always discard them.
There are two steps for constructing the splitting order parameter.

Step 1: finding an orthonormal basis.
Such a basis can be found by diagonalizing
M_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):=\text{tr}_{\text{str}}M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
using a unitary matrix
U_{\text{int}}
,
M_{\text{int}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=U_{\text{int}}DU_{\text{int}}^{\dagger}
, where
D
is a diagonal matrix. Notice that the quantum dimensions of the strings are implicit in the partial trace. Then, we define a new matrix

M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=d_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[\mathbbm{1}_{\text{str}}\otimes(D^{-\frac{1}{2}}U_{\text{int}}^{\dagger})]M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})[\mathbbm{1}_{\text{str}}\otimes(U_{\text{int}}D^{-\frac{1}{2}})],

(17)

whose entries are
M^{\prime}_{sx,ty}={}_{s}\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}_{x}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}_{y}\rangle_{t}
and

|\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}_{x}\rangle_{s}=\sqrt{\frac{d_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}{D_{xx}}}\sum_{y}(U_{\text{int}})_{yx}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{y}\rangle_{s}.

(18)

If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
does not split,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has a tensor product structure and it can be diagonalized by
\mathbbm{1}_{\text{str}}\otimes U_{\text{int}}
, therefore the bases of
M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
are orthonormal

{}_{s}\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}_{x}|\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}_{y}\rangle_{t}=\delta_{st}\delta_{xy}.

(19)

However, if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
does not have a tensor product structure and
\mathbbm{1}_{\text{str}}\otimes U_{\text{int}}
can not diagonalize
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
.
Therefore, if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
does not split,
M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is an identity matrix. Equivalently, if
M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is not an identity matrix,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits.

Step 2: constructing splitting order parameters from the eigenvalues of
M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
.
If
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits,
M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
can be diagonalized by another unitary matrix, and we denote the diagonalized matrix as
M^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
. Then we apply a discrete Fourier transformation
F_{\text{int}}
to
M^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
to measure the differences between the eigenvalues of
M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
:

\tilde{M}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=(\mathbbm{1}_{\text{str}}\otimes F_{\text{int}})M^{\prime\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})(\mathbbm{1}_{\text{str}}\otimes F^{\dagger}_{\text{int}}).

(20)

Then
\tilde{M}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is still an identity matrix if
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
does not split, and it is a nondiagonal matrix after splitting. Therefore the off-diagonal entries of
\tilde{M}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
can serve as the splitting order parameters:

(v)
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
splits if
\forall s,\exists x\neq y
such that
\tilde{M}_{sx,sy}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\neq 0
.

There are some remarks.
First, along some fine-tuned paths of the phase transition,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
and they are diagonal.
Second, when the topological phase transition is continuous, the splitting order parameters exhibit a universal critical exponent. Next, as we mentioned before, the degenerate leading eigenvalue
\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
of the transfer operator implies that there exists a correlation function with long range order, and it is just
{}_{s}\langle\bar{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}_{x},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{x}|\bar{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}_{y},\tilde{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{y}\rangle_{s}
,
which reduces to the splitting order parameters
|\tilde{M}_{sx,sy}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|^{2}
when the pair of anyons separate far apart.
In addition, for all anyon splitting examples considered in the next sections,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
is
4\times 4
, and it can be checked that all nonzero off-diagonal entries of
\tilde{M}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
satisfy
\tilde{M}_{11,12}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\tilde{M}_{21,22}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=-\tilde{M}_{12,11}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=-\tilde{M}_{22,21}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
, so there is only one splitting order parameter and we denote it as
\tilde{M}_{\text{off}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=|\tilde{M}_{11,12}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|
.

quantum double model
Kitaev 2003

(S_{3})
string-net model
Levin and Wen 2005

The input data of quantum double
D(G)

(G)

D(G)
quantum double models are labeled by the conjugacy classes
K
of
G

C_{k}=\{g\in G|gk=kg\}
, where

is a representative of
K

Each

Buerschaper and Aguado 2009

Lootens et al. 2022

(G)
string-net model is a fusion category
\mathcal{C}=\text{Rep}(G)
, where the simple objects are irreps of
G

To obtain topological phase transitions, we act on the physical level of the PEPS the deformation operator
\bigotimes_{e}\exp(kO_{e})

to a fixed point Hamiltonian of a string-net model or quantum double model. For the sake of simplicity, we adopt the PEPS approach to obtain phase transitions in this paper.

IV.1
Phase diagram of
D(S_{3})
quantum double model

The simplest non-Abelian quantum double model is the
D(S_{3})
quantum double model, constructed using the simplest non-Abelian group
S_{3}=\{e,r,\bar{r},s,sr,s\bar{r}\}
, where the generators
s
and
r
satisfy
r^{3}=s^{2}=e,r^{2}=\bar{r},sr=\bar{r}s
. The physical DOFs are labeled by group elements of
S_{3}
. The conjugacy classes and irreps of the centralizers are shown in Table
1
, from which we can obtain the anyons in
D(S_{3})
quantum double, labeled by
\{\boldsymbol{1},\boldsymbol{A},\cdots,\boldsymbol{G}\}
, see Table
2
.

All possible topological phase transitions from the
D(S_{3})
quantum double to Abelian topological phases can be obtained by deforming the ground state
|\Psi_{0}\rangle
of the fixed point
D\left(S_{3}\right)
quantum double model using a three-parameter deformation operator
Q(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
:

|\Psi(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\rangle=Q^{\otimes N}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})|\Psi_{0}\rangle,

(21)

where the on-site deformation operator is a
6\times 6
matrix:

\displaystyle Q(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q(k_{1},k_{2})&0_{3\times 3}\\
0_{3\times 3}&e^{-k_{3}}q(k_{1},k_{2})\\
\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle q(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1+2f(k_{1})&f(k_{2})&f(k_{2})\\
f(k_{2})&1-f(k_{1})&f(k_{2})\\
f(k_{2})&f(k_{2})&1-f(k_{1})\\
\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle f(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-e^{-k}}{1+2e^{-k}},

and the ordering of the bases supporting
Q(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
is
{|e\rangle,|r\rangle,|\bar{r}\rangle,|s\rangle,|sr\rangle,|s\bar{r}\rangle}
. The phase diagram of the deformed PEPS is shown in Fig.
3
. Every phase is labeled by the symmetry of the transfer operator fixed points. There are two different toric code phases, which are dual to each other. We focus on phase transitions along three axes. As shown in Appendix
E.1
, along the
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
axes, the deformed PEPS can be mapped to the 3-state Potts model, from which we can infer that the critical points are at
k_{c}=\log(1+\sqrt{3})/2\approx 0.5025
and are described by the 3-state Potts universality class. In addition, for the deformation along the
k_{2}
axis, the deformed PEPS can be mapped to the Rep
(S_{3})
string-net model deformed by a string tension; we discuss this case in subsection
IV.3
. Finally, along the
k_{3}
axis, the deformed PEPS can be mapped to the Ising model, from which we infer that the critical point is at
k_{c}=\log(1+\sqrt{2})/2\approx 0.4407
and belongs to the Ising universality class.

Next, we calculate the anyonic order parameters along the three axes. We compute every entry of
N^{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
and
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
defined in Eqs. (
10
), (
11
) and (
12
). These entries are contractions of norms or overlaps of PEPS, which can be performed by the variational uniform matrix product state (VUMPS) algorithm

VI
Conclusions and discussions

In this paper, we have extended the PEPS characterization of condensation and deconfinement of Abelian anyons to include partial condensation, partial deconfinement, and splitting of non-Abelian anyons in the PEPS characterization. We showed that anyon splitting can be observed from the topologically degenerate ground states in terms of MES. We constructed a complete set of PEPS
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{s}
carrying a non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
with different strings
s
and internal DOFs
x
. We generalized the usual condensate order parameter to a matrix
N^{\boldsymbol{1}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
given by the overlaps between the set of PEPS
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{s}
and the ground state
|\boldsymbol{1}\rangle
. We also generalized the usual deconfinement order parameter to a matrix
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
defined by the norms and overlaps among the PEPS
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{x}\rangle_{s}
. From the singular values of
N^{\boldsymbol{1}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
, we can distinguish full and partial condensation. By partially tracing
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
,
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\text{tr}_{\text{int}}[M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})]
, we can identify partial and full deconfinement from the eigenvalues of
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
. Moreover, we show that if the anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
does not split,
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
has a tensor product structure. By expressing
M(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
in a suitable orthonormal basis, we obtain a matrix
M^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
which allows to construct the order parameters
\tilde{M}_{\text{off}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
detecting the splitting of the non-Abelian anyon
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
from its eigenvalues.

We also propose a three-parameter phase diagram of the deformed
D(S_{3})
quantum double PEPS, where there are a
Z_{2}\boxtimes Z_{1}
toric code phase and a dual
S_{3}\boxtimes Z_{3}
toric code phase, as well as a
D(Z_{3})
quantum double phase. The deformed
D(S_{3})
PEPS along the
k_{2}
axis is also dual to the string tension deformed Rep
(S_{3})
string-net PEPS. Through the phase transitions from the
D(S_{3})
or the Rep
(S_{3})
phase to the toric code phase, partial condensation and partial deconfinement occurs, which we observe through the singular values of
N^{\boldsymbol{1}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
and eigenvalues of
M_{\text{str}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
. We demonstrate that through all phase transitions, the splitting order parameters
\tilde{M}_{\text{off}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
are fully capable of detecting anyon splitting. In addition, we also propose a two-parameter phase diagram of the deformed DIsing PEPS, and we detect the splitting of the anyon
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
using the splitting order parameter
\tilde{M}_{\text{off}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\bar{\boldsymbol{\sigma}})
through the phase transition to the toric code phase.

There are also some remaining open questions. First, we prove our statements about anyon splitting using the language of group theory; it could be better to rigorously describe anyon splitting using the language of category theory
Kong 2014
or of Hopf algebras
Molnar et al. 2022
, such that all nonchiral topological states are included. Second, when partial deconfinement happens, we cannot observe it from the leading eigenvalue of the transfer operator, and the critical exponent of the deconfinement order parameter cannot be derived from the universality class of the critical point (similar to the critical exponent for deconfinement found in Ref.
Iqbal and Schuch 2021
), so a better understanding of partial deconfinement is necessary. Third, one can easily design Hamiltonians with tuning parameters realizing all transitions in our examples, and variantionally optimizing PEPS to approach the ground states of the Hamiltonians
Iqbal and Schuch 2021
. However, it is very challenging to construct the generalized condensate and deconfinement order parameters as well as the splitting order parameters for the variationally optimized PEPS. These problems deserve further explorations.

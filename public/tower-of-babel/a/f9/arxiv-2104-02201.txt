Abstract
We present new Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array Band 7 observational results of a Lyman break galaxy at z = 7.15 z=7.15 , B14-65666 (“Big Three Dragons”), which is an object detected in [ O iii ] 88 μ ​ m \mathrm{\mu m} , [ C ii ] 158 μ ​ m \mathrm{\mu m} , and dust-continuum emission during the epoch of reionization. Our targets are the [ N ii ] 122 μ ​ m \mathrm{\mu m} fine-structure emission line and underlying 120 μ ​ m \mathrm{\mu m} dust continuum. The dust continuum is detected with a ∼ \sim 19 σ \sigma significance. From far-infrared spectral energy distribution sampled at 90, 120, and 160 μ ​ m \mathrm{\mu m} , we obtain a best-fit dust temperature of 40 40 K ( 79 79 K) and an infrared luminosity of log 10 ⁡ ( L IR / L ⊙ ) = 11.6 \log_{10}(L_{\rm IR}/{\rm L}_{\odot})=11.6 ( 12.1 12.1 ) at the emissivity index β = 2.0 \beta=2.0 (1.0). The [ N ii ] 122 μ ​ m \mathrm{\mu m} line is not detected. The 3 σ \sigma upper limit of the [ N ii ] luminosity is 8.1 × 10 7 8.1\times 10^{7} L ☉ \mathrm{L_{\sun}} . From the [ N ii ], [ O iii ], and [ C ii ] line luminosities, we use the Cloudy photoionization code to estimate nebular parameters as functions of metallicity. If the metallicity of the galaxy is high ( Z > 0.4 Z>0.4 Z ☉ \mathrm{Z_{\sun}} ), the ionization parameter and hydrogen density are log 10 ⁡ U ≃ − 2.7 ± 0.1 \log_{10}U\simeq-2.7\pm 0.1 and n H ≃ 50 ​ – ​ 250 n_{\text{H}}\simeq 50\mbox{--}250 cm − 3 \mathrm{cm^{-3}} , respectively, which are comparable to those measured in low-redshift galaxies. The nitrogen-to-oxygen abundance ratio, N / O \text{N}/\text{O} , is constrained to be sub-solar. At Z < 0.4 Z<0.4 Z ☉ \mathrm{Z_{\sun}} , the allowed U U drastically increases as the assumed metallicity decreases. For high ionization parameters, the N / O \text{N}/\text{O} constraint becomes weak. Finally, our Cloudy models predict the location of B14-65666 on the BPT diagram, thereby allowing a comparison with low-redshift galaxies.

INTRODUCTION

The Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) has contributed to several pioneering works on far-infrared (FIR) fine-structure lines in star-forming galaxies at redshift
z>7
, providing new insights into galaxy formation and evolution at the earliest epochs.
The [
C
ii
] 158
\mathrm{\mu m}

(
Capak et al. 2015

;

;
Bakx et al. 2020

, even at

;
Hashimoto et al. 2019
;
Carniani et al. 2020
)
.
While the [

;

;
Fujimoto et al. 2021

, the low ionization potential of C

C
ii

H

(
Russell et al. 1980
;
Tielens & Hollenbach 1985
;
Appleton et al. 2013
, e.g.,)
.
The [
O
iii
] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}

(

)
and has also been detected at
z\gtrsim 7

(

;
Carniani et al. 2017
;
Laporte et al. 2017
;
Marrone et al. 2018
;
Hashimoto et al. 2018
;
Hashimoto et al. 2019
;

.
In contrast to the [
C

O
iii
] line arises only from
H

regions due to high ionization potential of O
2+

The combination of these two FIR lines provides information on the physical conditions of the interstellar medium (ISM) in individual high-redshift galaxies, which are difficult to probe with weak nebular emission lines in the rest-frame ultraviolet (UV) band.

Inoue et al. 2016
found a deficit in [
C
ii
]-to-[

] luminosity ratios compared with local galaxies, possibly suggesting a highly ionized state in high-redshift galaxies.
This is supported by recent observational studies (
Hashimoto et al. 2019
;
Harikane et al. 2020
; but see
Carniani et al. 2020
)

In addition to fine-structure lines, high sensitivity observations with ALMA enable the detection of FIR continuum emission at
z>7

(
Watson et al. 2015
;
Laporte et al. 2017
;
Hashimoto et al. 2019
;
Tamura et al. 2019
)
.
Infrared luminosity is dominated by thermal dust emission, reflecting UV energy absorbed by dust.
Therefore, FIR spectral energy distribution (SED) is a clue for constraining dust properties, including dust temperature and dust mass.

Bakx et al. 2020
reported the non-detection of dust continuum at
160

\mathrm{\mu m}
in a Lyman break galaxy (LBG) at

\mathrm{\mu m}

These results suggest an unusually high dust temperature

\beta>2
.
While FIR SED is usually fitted by a modified blackbody function with an assumed dust temperature,
Inoue et al. 2020
proposed a new algorithm for determining dust temperature based on radiative equilibrium on dust grains.

This paper presents new observations of an LBG, B14-65666, which is the first example of a galaxy detected in [

It is important to study galaxy ISM with emission-line diagnostics thorough a wide range of redshifts at
z=0\mbox{--}7

At
z=0\mbox{--}2
, physical properties in star-forming galaxies are probed in detail using rest-frame optical emission lines.
The optical wavelength range includes emission lines from different elements (e.g., H, O, N, S), excited states (e.g., [
N
ii
]
\lambda\lambda
6549,6585), and ionization states (e.g., O

The nitrogen-to-oxygen (

Among dominant ionizing sources of strong line emitters, star-formation and active galactic nuclei (AGNs) can be distinguished on the [

The distribution on the BPT diagram is theoretically interpreted as a combination of the nebular physical parameters and hardness of ionizing radiation
(
Kewley et al. 2013
)
.

Galaxy properties at
z=2
have been explored using near-infrared (NIR) instruments to detect redshifted optical lines.
On the BPT diagram, the sequence of star-forming galaxies at
z\sim 2

(

;
Erb et al. 2006
, e.g.,)
, which is statistically confirmed using the KBSS-MOSFIRE sample
(

;

;

)

(

;

;

)
, and Subaru/FMOS observations
(
Yabe et al. 2014
;
Hayashi et al. 2015
;
Kashino et al. 2017
)
.
This offset on the BPT diagram originates from the redshift evolution of ionization states in star-forming galaxies.

Strom et al. 2018
derived the
\text{N}/\text{O}
abundance ratios of KBSS-MOSFIRE galaxies to find that the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio at
z\sim 2
is comparable with local abundance ratios
(
Pilyugin et al. 2012
)
at fixed metallicities even though an increase in the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio is suggested from local to
z\sim 2
in some studies
(
Masters et al. 2014
;
Sanders et al. 2016
;
Kojima et al. 2017
, e.g.,)
.

The optical and NIR observations are powerful tools for galaxy properties, but a redshift range is limited from
z\sim 0
to
2
.
We have to wait for MIR observations with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) to extend the studies for higher redshifts.
On the other hand, few galaxies are detected in FIR fine-structure lines including [
O
iii
] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}
at
0<z<3

(
Ferkinhoff et al. 2010
;
Zhang et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
, because FIR observations of fine-structure lines are mainly available for galaxies at
z\gtrsim 2\mbox{--}3
using ALMA or for local galaxies using telescopes such as Herschel, ISO, AKARI, and SOFIA.
This redshift gap in FIR observations makes it difficult to discuss a continuous galaxy evolution scenario.
We propose comparisons of the physical ISM properties at various redshifts estimated from either FIR or optical emission lines to overcome this difficulty, with the aid of a photoionization model.

IV.1
Modified Blackbody Fitting

We perform FIR SED fitting to estimate the IR luminosity and dust mass by combining the new 120
\mathrm{\mu m}
dust continuum emission with previous measurements of 90 and 160
\mathrm{\mu m}
dust continua underlying [
O
iii
] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}
and [
C
ii
] 158
\mathrm{\mu m}
.
In this paper, we only discuss the total FIR SED, assuming a constant dust temperature in the entire system of B14-65666, while the galaxy is composed of two components and suggested to be a merging system
(
Hashimoto et al. 2019
)
.
First, we fit a standard modified blackbody function to the observed FIR flux densities:

F_{\nu}^{\rm obs}=\frac{1+z}{d_{\rm L}^{2}}M_{\rm d}\kappa_{\nu}\left\{B_{\nu}(T_{\rm d})-B_{\nu}(T_{\rm CMB})\right\}\,,

(1)

where
z
is the source redshift, and
d_{\rm L}
is the luminosity distance.

M_{\rm d}
is the dust mass, which is the normalization of the equation.

\kappa_{\nu}
is the dust emissivity at the frequency
\nu
.

B_{\nu}(T)
is the blackbody function, and
T_{\rm d}
is the dust temperature of the source.

T_{\rm CMB}
is the cosmic microwave background (CMB) temperature at the source redshift, and
B_{\nu}(T_{\rm CMB})
denotes CMB intensity.
The negative
B_{\nu}(T_{\rm CMB})
term accounts for a correction of CMB effect in interferometric observations
(
da Cunha et al. 2013
)
.

Although assumptions on the emissivity value systematically affect estimates of dust mass
(
Fanciullo et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
, there are large variations among empirical estimates, theoretical models, and laboratory measurements of emissivity, as briefly reviewed in
Inoue et al. 2020
.
Following
Inoue et al. 2020
, we assume a typical value of
\kappa_{\nu}=30~{\rm cm^{2}~g^{-1}}(100~{\micron}/\lambda)^{\beta}
with the wavelength
\lambda=c/\nu
and the light speed
c
and the emissivity index
\beta=1.0
,
1.5
, or
2.0
.
The pivot value of
30\ \mathrm{cm^{2}~g^{-1}}
at 100
\mathrm{\mu m}
is very similar to those of Astronomical silicate
(
Draine & Lee 1984
;
Weingartner & Draine 2001
)
and the THMIS model
(
Jones et al. 2017
)
.
Figure
3
shows the results obtained from the least-
\chi^{2}
fitting with two free parameters of
M_{\rm d}
and
T_{\rm d}
.
There is a degeneracy between
M_{\rm d}
and
T_{\rm d}
because we do not constrain the peak of FIR SED yet.
A data point at a shorter wavelength than 90 µm is important to break this degeneracy.
The best-fit
T_{\rm d}
spans 80 K to 40 K and is lower for a larger
\beta
.
The best-fit
\log_{10}(M_{\rm d}/{\rm M}_{\odot})
increases from 6.6 to 7.5 with increasing
\beta
.
The corresponding total IR luminosity changes from
\log_{10}(L_{\rm IR}/{\rm L}_{\odot})=12.0
to
11.6
.
The obtained values and their uncertainties are listed in appendix Table
A1
.
Although we find a smallest
\chi^{2}
value for
\beta=1.0
,
\chi^{2}
differences compared to
\beta=1.5
or
2.0
are not statistically significant.

IV.2
Radiative Equilibrium Fitting

Inoue et al. 2020
presented a new algorithm to derive dust temperature and mass using radiative equilibrium on dust grains.
Radiative equilibrium connects
T_{\rm d}
with
M_{\rm d}
and breaks degeneracy between them.
Therefore, we may obtain tighter constraints on them even without observing the FIR SED peak.
The algorithm requires the observed UV luminosity,
L_{\rm UV}
, and the physical radius of the system,
R
.
We adopt values taken from
Hashimoto et al. 2019
.
The total UV luminosity is
L_{\rm UV}=(7.6\pm 1.3)\times 10^{44}
erg s
-1
.
The observed full-width half-maximum along the major and minor axes of the entire dust emission in Band 6 are
a=3.8\pm 1.1
kpc and
b=0.8\pm 0.5
kpc in the proper coordinate, respectively.
The Band 6 observation in Hashimoto et al.​ has higher spatial resolution than our Band 7 observation.
Assuming a spherical symmetric structure for the analytic treatment of
Inoue et al. 2020
, we adopt the radius of
R=\sqrt{ab}/2=0.87\pm 0.30
kpc.

Following
Inoue et al. 2020
, we perform least-
\chi^{2}
fitting for FIR SED with the radiative equilibrium algorithm in three geometries: spherical shell, homogeneous sphere, and clumpy sphere.
The spherical shell and homogeneous sphere geometries require only a single free parameter,
M_{\rm d}
(or
T_{\rm d}
), and the other quantity of
T_{\rm d}
(or
M_{\rm d}
) is derived from
M_{\rm d}
(or
T_{\rm d})
thanks to the radiative equilibrium.
The clumpy geometry requires an additional free parameter to control the clumpiness,
\xi_{\rm cl}
, which is a non-dimensional parameter defined by the ratio between a single clump size relative to the entire system size and the volume filling factor of the clumps.
Figure
4
shows the fitting results for the case of
\beta=2.0
.
The other two
\beta
cases are shown in appendix Figure
11
.
Radiative equilibrium requires the relations between
T_{\rm d}
and
M_{\rm d}
shown by the short-dashed, long-dashed, and dotted lines for the shell, homogeneous, and clumpy geometries, respectively.
We consider the uncertainties of
L_{\rm UV}
and
R
in addition to FIR flux densities in the fitting using a Monte Carlo method
3
3

3

In each trial of the fitting, we fluctuate the observables assuming a Gaussian function with the standard deviation equal to the observed uncertainty. We then repeat the trials, and calculate the 68-percentile of the distribution of the best-fit values.

(
Inoue et al. 2020
)
.
Therefore, the resultant uncertainties in
M_{\rm d}
and
T_{\rm d}
are still large.
The clumpy geometry case gives the same best-fit solutions as those of the modified blackbody fitting because the clumpiness parameter functions as an adjuster
(
Inoue et al. 2020
)
.
Figure
5
shows the best-fit FIR SEDs for
\beta=2.0
and Figure
6
shows the distribution of the solutions in the
M_{\rm d}
and
\xi_{\rm cl}
plane for
\beta=2.0
.
Other
\beta
cases are found in Figures
12
and
13
, respectively.
The numerical values are summarized in appendix Table
A1
.
For the shell and homogeneous geometries, the cases of
\beta=1.5
and 2.0 result in larger
\chi^{2}
values and are not favored statistically compared to the case of
\beta=1.0
.
For the clumpy geometry, all
\beta
cases cannot be regarded to be different statistically.

The best-fit
T_{\rm d}
values are
\simeq 100
K in the shell geometry,
80
–100 K in the homogeneous geometry, and
40
–80 K in the clumpy geometry.
The corresponding
\log_{10}(M_{\rm d}/{\rm M}_{\odot})
values are
6.4\mbox{--}6.6
,
6.5\mbox{--}6.7
, and
6.6\mbox{--}7.5
, respectively.
The IR luminosities are
\log_{10}(L_{\rm IR}/{\rm L}_{\odot})=12.5\mbox{--}13.0
,
12.4\mbox{--}12.6
, and
11.6\mbox{--}12.1
, respectively.
Comparing our results for B14-65666 to those for another
z\simeq 7
DSFG, A1689zD1
(
Inoue et al. 2020
)
, we find some similarities in the dust properties of those high-redshift objects.
In the shell and homogeneous geometries, both objects exhibit high IR luminosities and corresponding high SFRs, possibly indicating the invalidity of these simple geometries.
Hereafter, we adopt the case of the clumpy geometry at
\beta=2.0
for the IR luminosity as the fiducial case.

In the clumpy geometry, the best-fit clumpiness parameter is
\xi_{\rm cl}=0.1\mbox{--}0.4
for B14-65666, which is similar to that for A1689zD1.
As discussed in
Inoue et al. 2020

\sim 10
pc in the local Universe
(

;
Fukui et al. 2008
)
, these values correspond to a clump volume filling factor of 3%–10%.
Although observing such tiny clouds are difficult without significant gravitational lensing, a comparison between the clumpiness expected from the radiative equilibrium algorithm and galaxy formation simulations will be interesting.

Notably, our model cannot reproduce the high

\mathrm{Z_{\sun}}

The crosses in Figure

In this case,

Specifically,

Z>0.4

\mathrm{Z_{\sun}}
and
n_{\text{H}}\lesssim 10

\mathrm{cm^{-3}}
at
Z<0.4

\mathrm{Z_{\sun}}
.
However, qualitative tendencies of

to

It is informative to compare our photoionization models with previous works focusing on [
O
iii
] and [
N
ii
] lines.

Rigopoulou et al. 2018
used the ionization models in
Pereira-Santaella et al. 2017
to estimate gaseous metallicity from a line ratio of [

] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}
to [

] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
([

/
[

\text{N}/\text{O}
–
Z
relation.
The [
O

]
/
[

(
Pereira-Santaella et al. 2017
)
.
Rigopoulou et al. firstly inferred the ionization parameter from 88-to-122
\mathrm{\mu m}
dust continuum ratios, and then derived the metallicity from the ionization parameter and [
O
iii
]
/
[
N
ii

The dust continuum ratio of B14-65666 is
\simeq\!1.4\mbox{--}2.9
(Table

), corresponding to
\log_{10}U\sim-2
to
-1
at
n_{\text{H}}=100

\mathrm{cm^{-3}}

Our measurement of [
O
iii
]
/
[
N
ii
]
\gtrsim 40
for B14-65666 gives a weak metallicity constraint of
Z\lesssim 1

\mathrm{Z_{\sun}}
for
\log_{10}U\sim-2
(the highest ionization parameter value in Pereira-Santaella et al.​ model) and a marginal constraint of
Z\lesssim 0.6

\mathrm{Z_{\sun}}
for
\log_{10}U\simeq-2.7
, which is consistent with the metallicity range estimated from the SED fitting
(
Hashimoto et al. 2019
)
.
The reason of the high ionization parameter inferred from the 88-to-122
\mathrm{\mu m}
dust continuum ratio is unclear, but it may be related with the fact that high-redshift galaxies tend to exhibit higher dust temperatures
(
Bakx et al. 2020
)
than local galaxies that were used for their model calibration.

Although we assume that [
C
ii
] is emitted from
H

regions and PDRs, [

It can be a cause for concern that shocks contribute to the [

ii
] line flux since B14-65666 exhibits a merger morphology
(
Hashimoto et al. 2019
)
.

Appleton et al. 2013
measured the [
C
ii
] flux originating from shocks by observing the intergalactic medium in Stephan’s Quintet.
They report that the [
C
ii
]-to-IR luminosity ratios are
>10^{-1.5}
, which is higher than those measured in star-forming galaxies, including B14-65666
(
Hashimoto et al. 2019
,
\sim 10^{-2.5}
,)
.
The [

\sim 10^{7}

\mathrm{L_{\sun}}
) is also lower than for B14-65666 (
\sim 10^{9}

\mathrm{L_{\sun}}
).
We, therefore, conclude that shock excitation is less dominant in [

V.3
Nitrogen-to-Oxygen Abundance Ratio

As seen in dwarf galaxies
(
Lequeux et al. 1979
;
Vila-Costas & Edmunds 1993
, e.g.,)
and SDSS galaxies
(
Andrews & Martini 2013
)
, the
\text{N}/\text{O}
abundance ratio is almost constant as
\log_{10}(\text{N}/\text{O})\simeq-1.5
at low metallicity, and it drastically increases when metallicity exceeds a certain value.
A simple explanation of this
\text{N}/\text{O}
trend is a combination of the primary and secondary nucleosynthetic nitrogen; the primary production of nitrogen is independent of the initial metallicities in stars, whereas the secondary production that occurred in the CNO cycle is proportional to the initial carbon or oxygen abundance
(
Pagel 2009
)
.

The
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio affects the intensity ratios between nitrogen and oxygen atoms/ions emission lines.
In some photoionization models, the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio is assumed as a function of metallicity
(
Nagao et al. 2011
;
Pereira-Santaella et al. 2017
;
Rigopoulou et al. 2018
)
, but it is possible that this relation changes at high redshift.
Given the nebular parameters and input spectrum (i.e., a certain fixed ionization structure), photoionization models can predict abundance ratios from observed emission-line intensity ratios.
In FIR bands, [
N
iii
] 57
\mathrm{\mu m}
/
[
O
iii
] 52
\mathrm{\mu m}
line ratio is used for
\text{N}/\text{O}
measurements
(
Lester et al. 1983
;
Rubin et al. 1988
;
Peng et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
, as both lines have similar ionization potentials and critical densities.
For B14-65666, [
N
iii
] 57
\mathrm{\mu m}
and [
O
iii
] 52
\mathrm{\mu m}
lines are accessible in ALMA Band 9; however, they still requires expensive observations.
Instead, by assuming the nebular parameters derived from the [
O
iii
] and [
C
ii
] emission lines (Section
V.2
), we constrain the
\text{N}/\text{O}
abundance ratio at
z\sim 7
from our upper limits of the luminosity ratio between [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
and [
O
iii
] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}
(
L_{\text{[NII]122}}/L_{\text{[OIII]88}}
).

We convert
L_{\text{[NII]122}}/L_{\text{[OIII]88}}
to
\text{N}/\text{O}
with Cloudy models and the nebular parameters obtained in Section
V.2
, which are functions of metallicity (see Figure
8
).
The observed line luminosity ratio is
L_{\text{[NII]122}}/L_{\text{[OIII]88}}<-1.63
.
For each nebular parameter set, we prepare models with
\log_{10}(\text{N}/\text{O})
ranging from
-2
to
0
in steps of
0.5
.
We note that
n_{\text{H}}
is included in our calculations even though
L_{\text{[NII]122}}/L_{\text{[OIII]88}}
is almost independent of
n_{\text{H}}
due to similar critical densities between [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
and [
O
iii
] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}
.
We compare the observed
L_{\text{[NII]122}}/L_{\text{[OIII]88}}
ratios and the Cloudy models to obtain the upper limits of
\text{N}/\text{O}
as a function of
U
and
Z
.
The left panel of Figure
9
shows a Cloudy model grid in the case of
\log n_{\text{H}}=2.0
and
\log L_{\text{[NII]122}}/L_{\text{[OIII]88}}=-1.63
.
At a fixed
Z
, higher
U
results in a higher
\text{N}/\text{O}
upper limit (i.e., a weaker
\text{N}/\text{O}
constraint).
Since the low
\mathrm{SFR}
yields high-
U
solutions among the three
\mathrm{SFR}
cases considered in Section
V.2
, the estimated
\text{N}/\text{O}
upper limits become the highest (weakest) at the low
\mathrm{SFR}
.
To simply express
\text{N}/\text{O}
as a function of
Z
, we take the highest
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratios as a upper limit at each
Z
in the following.

The right panel of Figure
9
illustrates the upper limits of the
\text{N}/\text{O}
abundance ratio for B14-65666 as a function of metallicity.
Given that the solar value of
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})
is
8.69

(
Asplund et al. 2009
)
, the metallicity
Z
is converted to the
\text{O}/\text{H}
abundance ratio.
The
\text{N}/\text{O}
value is well constrained at a high metallicity regime.
This originates from a model prediction of relatively high
L_{\text{[NII]122}}/L_{\text{[OIII]88}}
for the range of ionization parameters at high metallicities.
At
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})\gtrsim 8.4
,
\log_{10}(\text{N}/\text{O})
is less than the solar value of
-0.84

(
Asplund et al. 2009
)
.
At a higher metallicity of
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})>8.5
, the
\log_{10}(\text{N}/\text{O})
upper limits become less than the average relation of extragalactic
H
ii
regions
(
Pilyugin et al. 2012
)
.
This is consistent with an implication by the
Rigopoulou et al. 2018
model that the metallicity of B14-65666 is
\lesssim 0.6

\mathrm{Z_{\sun}}
if
\log_{10}U\simeq-2.7
and the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio follows a local
\text{N}/\text{O}
–
Z
relation (Section
V.2
).
On the other hand, the
\text{N}/\text{O}
constraint is very weak at
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})<8.3
due to high ionization parameters of
\log U\gtrsim-2.5
.
If the metallicity of B14-65666 is
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})<8.3
, our observations cannot produce a meaningful constraint on the
\text{N}/\text{O}
abundance, implying that we need more sensitive observations by 1–2 order of magnitude to detect the [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
emission line from the
z\sim 7
galaxy.
The small orange circles in Figure
9
depict the case of
C_{\text{PDR}}=0.1
.
We find that the low PDR covering fraction only weakly affects the upper limits of the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio by 0.3-dex at most.
As shown in Figure
8
, the low
C_{\text{PDR}}
slightly changes
U
in the low
\mathrm{SFR}
case, which influences the
\text{N}/\text{O}
upper limit.
Although
n_{\text{H}}
decreases in this case,
L_{\text{[NII]122}}/L_{\text{[OIII]88}}
is almost independent of
n_{\text{H}}
and the N/O constraints are not affected.
Therefore, the lower
C_{\text{PDR}}
does not change the
\text{N}/\text{O}
abundance very much.

The weak
\text{N}/\text{O}
upper limits at
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})<8.3
originates from the high ionization parameters of
\log_{10}U\gtrsim-2.5
.
If the ionization parameter of B14-65666 is similar to those of local and
z\sim 2
galaxies, the upper limits would become more stringent.
We test whether this case is possible for our galaxy, by fixing
\log_{10}U=-2.7
and by changing
C_{\text{PDR}}
from
0.05
to
1.0
on the
L_{\text{[OIII]88}}/L_{\mathrm{H\alpha}}
–
L_{\text{[CII]158}}/L_{\mathrm{H\alpha}}
diagram.
In this analysis we choose nebular parameter sets that can model the observed value within the
\mathrm{SFR}
uncertainty of
162<\mathrm{SFR}<282

\mathrm{M_{\sun}\ yr^{-1}}
and take the highest
\text{N}/\text{O}
in the parameter sets as the upper limits at fixed metallicities.
The results are depicted with the orange upside-down triangles in Figure
8
.
The assumption of
\log_{10}U=-2.7
gives similar upper limits to the
C_{\text{PDR}}=1.0
case (red circles) at
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})>8.3
, while the upper limit is constant at
\log_{10}(\text{N}/\text{O})=-0.87
at lower metallicities of
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})<8.3
, as predicted from the left panel of Figure
9
.
This
\text{N}/\text{O}
upper limit is 3 times lower than those shown by the red and orange circles for which
\log U
increases as the metallicity decreases.
If
\log_{10}U=-2.7
, the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio of this galaxy is restricted to be sub-solar, irrespective of its intrinsic metallicity.
Our model requires a low PDR covering fraction (
C_{\text{PDR}}<1
) and high
\mathrm{SFR}
(
282

\mathrm{M_{\sun}\ yr^{-1}}
) to keep
\log_{10}U=-2.7
at lower metallicities.
We cannot plot the data point at
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})=8.0
in the figure because there are no models with
\log_{10}U=-2.7
at this metallicity within the
\mathrm{SFR}
uncertainty interval.

Our
\text{N}/\text{O}
upper limit is roughly consistent with
z\sim 2
studies.

Steidel et al. 2016
stacked the KBSS-MOSFIRE spectra to compute the typical
\text{N}/\text{O}
of them and
Strom et al. 2018
estimated
\text{N}/\text{O}
for individual KBSS-MOSFIRE galaxies with photoionization models.
These
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratios are comparable to those of the local extragalactic
H
ii
regions presented by
Pilyugin et al. 2012
.
The relatively low metallicity galaxies at
z\sim 2
studied by
Kojima et al. 2017
exhibit higher
\log_{10}(\text{N}/\text{O})
than local galaxies at fixed metallicities, which are measured using a direct temperature method.
Most of these
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratios at
z\sim 2
are lower than the upper limit of B14-65666.
Our upper limits are also consistent with the nearby analogs of LBGs in
Loaiza-Agudelo et al. 2020
.

V.4
Predicted BPT Diagram

As noted in the previous section, the photoionization model can convert an emission-line flux into another line flux for the same ion.
This enables us to predict the position of B14-65666 on the BPT diagram from our FIR line fluxes.
We estimate optical-line ratios of
[\mbox{O\,{\sc iii}}]\lambda 5007/\mbox{H$\beta$}
and
[\mbox{N\,{\sc ii}}]\lambda 6583/\mbox{H$\beta$}
from the FIR [
O
iii
] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}
and [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
line fluxes, respectively, using the Cloudy models with the nebular parameters obtained in Section
V.2
.
In this model,
[\mbox{N\,{\sc ii}}]\lambda 6583/\mbox{H$\beta$}
is given as an upper limit because the [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
flux is constrained as the
3\sigma
upper limit in our observations.
The
\mbox{H$\alpha$}/\mbox{H$\beta$}
line ratio is also computed from the model.
In this way, we obtain the modeled
[\mbox{O\,{\sc iii}}]\lambda 5007/\mbox{H$\beta$}
and
[\mbox{N\,{\sc ii}}]\lambda 6583/\mbox{H$\alpha$}
line ratios for B14-65666.

Figure
10
plots a BPT diagram.
In general, dust attenuation is negligible on the BPT diagram because the diagram takes the ratios of the emission lines with close wavelengths.
The FIR [
O
iii
] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}
and [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
fluxes are not affected by dust attenuation due to their long wavelengths.
For these reasons, our estimations can be compared with values in literature without concerns about dust attenuation.

VI
Summary

We have performed ALMA Band 7 observations of a LBG at
z=7.15
, B14-65666, to target the [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
FIR fine-structure line and underlying dust continuum emission.
B14-65666 is the first object detected in [
O
iii
] 88 µm, [
C
ii
] 158 µm, and dust continuum emission at such high-redshift
(
Hashimoto et al. 2019
, “Big Three Dragons”,)
.

The dust continuum at 120
\mathrm{\mu m}
is detected with
\text{S}/\text{N}=18.9
.
We combine the dust-continuum flux at 120
\mathrm{\mu m}
with the previous measurements at 90
\mathrm{\mu m}
and 160
\mathrm{\mu m}
to perform two types of FIR SED fitting.
The modified blackbody fitting results in a dust temperature
T_{\text{d}}=80
to
40
K and a dust mass
M_{\text{d}}\sim 10^{6.6}
to
10^{7.5}

\mathrm{M_{\sun}}
with an emissivity index
\beta=1
to
2
.
The corresponding IR luminosity spans
\log_{10}(L_{\rm IR}/{\rm L}_{\odot})=12.0
to
11.6
.
The results of the radiative equilibrium fitting, proposed by
Inoue et al. 2020
, are found to be similar to the results for another
z\simeq 7
dusty star-forming galaxy, A1689zD1.
Simple assumptions of the shell and homogeneous geometries appear to be invalid because the geometries prefer too high IR luminosity.
The clumpy geometry leads to the same best-fit results as the modified blackbody, with a best-fit clumpiness parameter of
\xi_{\rm cl}=0.1\mbox{--}0.4
.

The [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
emission line is not detected.
The 3
\sigma
upper limit of [
N
ii
] luminosity is
8.1\times 10^{7}

\mathrm{L_{\sun}}
.
We constrain the nebular parameters of B14-65666 as functions of metallicity with a photoionization code Cloudy, by modeling the [
N
ii
] 122
\mathrm{\mu m}
upper limits, along with the [
O
iii
] 88
\mathrm{\mu m}
and [
C
ii
] 158
\mathrm{\mu m}
line fluxes and the SED
\mathrm{SFR}
.
If the metallicity of B14-65666 is high (
Z>0.4

\mathrm{Z_{\sun}}
), the ionization parameters and hydrogen densities are
\log_{10}U\simeq-2.7\pm 0.1
and
n_{\text{H}}\simeq 50\mbox{--}250

\mathrm{cm^{-3}}
, respectively.
The two nebular parameter values are consistent with those measured in low-redshift galaxies.
If
Z<0.4

\mathrm{Z_{\sun}}
, the
U
and
n
drastically increases and decreases, respectively, with a decrease in metallicity.
This is due to the high
L_{\text{[OIII]88}}/L_{\mathrm{H\alpha}}
ratio, that is, the observed high
L_{\text{[OIII]88}}/\mathrm{SFR}
ratio of this galaxy.
In the case of a low PDR covering fraction (
C_{\text{PDR}}=0.1
), lower
U
and
n_{\text{H}}
are expected, while the results are qualitatively the same.

The constraints on the nitrogen-to-oxygen abundance ratio,
\text{N}/\text{O}
, also largely depend on the assumed metallicity.
The obtained upper limit of the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio monotonically decreases as the assumed metallicity increases.
At
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})\gtrsim 8.4
, the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratios sould be sub-solar and upper limits are comparable to the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio of local and
z\sim 2
galaxies.
In contrast, our observations cannot provide meaningful constraint at
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})<8.3
.
The
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratio is insignificantly affected by the differences of the PDR covering fractions between
C_{\text{PDR}}=0.1
and
1.0
.
If we fix the ionization parameter at
\log U=-2.7
in our model, the
\text{N}/\text{O}
ratios are restricted to be sub-solar even at
12+\log_{10}(\text{O}/\text{H})<8.3
with a small PDR covering fraction and a high
\mathrm{SFR}
.

The Cloudy models also predict the location of the galaxy at
z\sim 7
on the BPT diagram, using the nebular parameters estimated from the FIR lines.
The upper limits of B14-65666 are predicted to be located in the distribution of star-forming galaxies at
z\sim 2
.
The location of B14-65666 may be below the
z\sim 2
average relation given the weak
\text{N}/\text{O}
upper limits.
In the case of
C_{\text{PDR}}=0.1
, the upper limits are located around the distribution of local galaxies.
Further ALMA statistical observations and rest-frame optical-line observations with JWST will provide opportunities for addressing the high-redshift BPT diagram.

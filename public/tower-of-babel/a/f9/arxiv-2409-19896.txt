1.
Introduction

A classical topic in the field of nonlinear analysis is the study of
problems with critical growth. The literature on this topic is vast, but, in a sense, the common denominator of this line of research is that the critical power is typically the one for which, on the one hand, the standard methods of functional analysis become invalid, but, on the other hand, being in a borderline situation, several new phenomena may take place.

In this spirit, another source of criticality may arise when the problem is set in the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, since mass could escape to infinity and standard compactness arguments may not be used in this situation.

Overcoming these difficulties required the introduction of powerful tools, which have now become foundational and ubiquitously used: in particular, the search of new solutions often relied on topological methods, such as the Mountain Pass Theorem, see e.g.
[
2
,
8
]
, and the loss of compactness due to an unbounded domain was on many occasion compensated by the concentration-compactness principle,
see e.g.
[
9
]
.

To construct new solutions, perturbation methods came in handy. In particular,
in
[
1
]
, global solutions of the problem

-\Delta u=\varepsilon h(x)u^{q}+u^{2^{*}-1},

where, as usual,
2^{*}:=\frac{2N}{N-2}
,
were obtained by an apt combination of the above methods:
specifically,
\varepsilon
was considered as a small, positive parameter,
modulating a suitable sublinear perturbation, giving rise to two new solutions,
one branching off from the trivial one, and one created by the mountain pass structure of the perturbed functional.

The analogue of this problem in the fractional setting was first studied in
[
6
]

and also contributed to the investigation of the concentration-compactness principle in the fractional setting. In particular, in
[
6
]
the existence theory was carried over for equations driven by the fractional Laplacian

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx.

Here above and in the rest of this paper, normalizing constants are omitted for the sake of simplicity and the integral is taken in the sense of its principal value, to compensate for singularities.

The existence theory in
[
6
]
is valid for all
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
.
However, it was incorrectly claimed in
[
6
]
that the methods utilized there would establish the same result for all the fractional parameter range
s\in(0,1)
.

Motivated by an intriguing question by
Norihisa Ikoma
, to whom we are deeply indebted, we show here
that, on the one hand, the methods of
[
6
]
are not sufficient by themselves to cover the full fractional parameter range
s\in(0,1)
, but, on the other hand, the result
in
[
6
]
does hold true for all
s\in(0,1)
, though with a different proof that we present here (actually, this proof is valid for all
s\in(0,1)
, without needing to distinguish any threshold induced by the somewhat special exponent
s=\frac{1}{2}
).

The mathematical details go as follows.
We look for positive solutions

Let

Then, there exists

Also, if

, then these solutions are strictly positive in

.

In particular, if

,
then these solutions are strictly positive.

We stress that, compared to the local case

, the fractional case
presents further difficulties. To avoid such complications,
in
[

the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension method is used
to reduce the nonlocal problem (

However, this strategy does not work for

as we show in Appendix

nonlocal problem, adapting the variational approach for the local

[

]
.

With respect to
[
1
]
, however, several technical modifications are needed to address the nonlocal scenario.
The first main difference is that the concentration-compactness
principle by Lions cannot be directly used to deal with the fractional case.
To overcome this complication, we adapt a concentration-compactness
result in
[
3
]
. In particular, due to the nonlocality
of the probelm, using cut-off functions to prove convergence becomes more
challenging as we have to deal with integrals presenting also contributions on
unbounded domains. To resolve this issue we give some convergence
results carefully tailored to address our framework.
We prove this by using a dyadic argument (see Lemmata

and
3.4

Moreover, in order to prove the existence of a second solution,

[
1

the same localization procedure to find the first one is applied to a translated functional, defined by means of
the positive part of a function (see
[

Here, the approach is not the same as in
[
1

and requires carefulness,
noting that the fractional gradient of the positive part of a
function is harder to deal than the case of the classical
gradient (see Proposition
5.4

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section

The proof of Theorem

More specifically, Section

We remark that the proofs carried out here will make no use of extension methods, and rather rely on suitable modifications of the original arguments in
[
1

, combined with the concentration-compactness theory put forth in
[

Finally, in Appendix

2.
Notation

As customary, for all
s\in(0,1)
, we consider the Gagliardo-Dirichlet form, defined by

[u]_{s}^{2}:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

and the functional space

\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\Big\{u\in L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\,[u]_{s}<+\infty\Big\}.

We remark that

[u]_{s}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}u(x)|^{2}\,dx,

where

|D^{s}u(x)|^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x+h)-u(x)|^{2}}{|h|^{N+2s}}\,dh.

We also consider the fractional Sobolev constant

(2.1)

S:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\frac{[u]_{s}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}}.

We will seek nonnegative solutions of (
1.1
) as
critical points of the functional
f_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

(2.2)

f_{\varepsilon}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy-\frac{\varepsilon}{q+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)u_{+}^{q+1}(x)\,dx-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx.

Notice indeed that, for all
v\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(2.3)

\begin{split}\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v\rangle&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)u_{+}^{q}(x)v(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)v(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Moreover, critical points of the functional (
2.2
)
are nonnegative, according to Proposition 2.2.3 in
[
6
]
.

3.
Preliminaries from the literature

We gather here some useful results from the existing literature, stated in a form which is convenient for our purposes. For this, we let
\phi\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},[0,1])
be such that

\mbox{supp}(\phi)\subset B_{1}
and
\phi=1
in
B_{1/2}
.

Given
r>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
we define

(3.1)

\phi_{r,x_{0}}(x):=\phi\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{r}\right).

In this setting, we recall Corollary 2.3 in
[
3
]
, used here with
p=2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\phi_{r,x_{0}}
be defined as in (
3.1
).
Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

|D^{s}\phi_{r,x_{0}}(x)|^{2}\leqslant C\min\left\{r^{-2s},r^{N}|x-x_{0}|^{-(N+2s)}\right\},

where
C>0
depends on
N
,
s
and
\|\phi_{r,x_{0}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

Thee next result corresponds to Lemma 2.4 in
[
3
]

with
p=q=2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
w\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and assume that there exist
\alpha>0
and
C>0
such that

(3.2)

0\leqslant w(x)\leqslant C|x|^{-\alpha}.

Then, if
\alpha>2s
, the space
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
compactly embedded in
L^{2}(w\,dx;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, that is, for any sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k}\subset\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
k\to+\infty
,
we have that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{k}(x)-u(x)|^{2}w(x)\,dx=0.

The proofs of the next two results can be found in
[
3
]
, but since they are not presented as stand alone
statements, we provide here the proof for the facility of the reader.
For the sake of completeness, we also mention that the argument after formula (2.9)
in
[
3
]
should be amended by using a dyadic argument, as detailed

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\phi_{\delta,x_{0}}
be defined
as in (
3.1
). Then,

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2}|D^{s}\phi_{\delta,x_{0}}(x)|^{2}\,dx=0.

Proof.

In light of Lemma
3.1
, we see that

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2}|D^{s}\phi_{\delta,x_{0}}(x)|^{2}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leqslant C\left(\delta^{-2s}\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx+\delta^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\,dx\right).\end{split}

Also, using the Hölder inequality with exponents
\frac{2^{*}_{s}}{2}

and
\frac{N}{2s}
,

\displaystyle\delta^{-2s}\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\delta^{-2s}\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}}|B_{\delta}|^{\frac{2s}{N}}=C\left(\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}},

for some
C>0
depending on
N
and
s
but not on
\delta
.

Therefore, since
u\in L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it follows that

(3.4)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{-2s}\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx=0.

Furthermore, we point out that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\delta^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\delta^{N}\int_{B_{2^{k+1}\delta}(x_{0})\setminus B_{2^{k}\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k(N+2s)}}\frac{1}{\delta^{2s}}\int_{B_{2^{k+1}\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k(N+2s)}}\frac{1}{\delta^{2s}}\left(\int_{B_{2^{k+1}\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}}|B_{2^{k+1}\delta}|^{\frac{2s}{N}}

\displaystyle=c\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{Nk}}\left(\int_{B_{2^{k+1}\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}},

where
c
only depends on
N
and
s
.

Now, let
\varepsilon>0
and take
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, depending on
\varepsilon
, such that

c\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{Nk}}<\varepsilon.

In this way, we deduce from (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\delta^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+c\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\frac{1}{2^{Nk}}\left(\int_{B_{2^{k_{0}+1}\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+C\left(\int_{B_{2^{k_{0}+1}\delta}(x_{0})}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}},

for some
C>0
depending on
N
,
s
and
k_{0}
(and therefore on
\varepsilon
).

As a result, we obtain that

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\,dx\leqslant\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}.

Taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
we conclude that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\delta^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\delta}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+2s}}\,dx=0.

From this, (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we obtain the desired result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\phi_{R,0}
be defined as in (
3.1
).
Let also
\phi_{R}:=1-\phi_{R,0}
.

Then,

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2}|D^{s}\phi_{R}(x)|^{2}\,dx=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have that

(3.6)

|D^{s}\phi_{R}(x)|^{2}=|D^{s}\phi_{R,0}(x)|^{2}\leqslant C\min\left\{R^{-2s},R^{N}|x|^{-(N+2s)}\right\}.

As a consequence,

(3.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2}|D^{s}\phi_{R}(x)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant C\left(R^{-2s}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx+R^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{N+2s}}\,dx\right).

Now, we observe that

\begin{split}R^{-2s}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx&=R^{-2s}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\int_{B_{{R}/{2^{k}}}\setminus B_{{R}/{2^{k+1}}}}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx\\
&\leqslant R^{-2s}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\int_{B_{{R}/{2^{k}}}\setminus B_{{R}/{2^{k+1}}}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}}|B_{{R}/{2^{k}}}|^{\frac{2s}{N}}\\
&\leqslant c\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{2sk}}\left(\int_{B_{{R}/{2^{k}}}\setminus B_{{R}/{2^{k+1}}}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}}.\end{split}

We point out that for all
\varepsilon>0
we can find
k_{0}
such that

c\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{2^{2sk}}<\varepsilon,

and therefore

\displaystyle R^{-2s}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+c\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\frac{1}{2^{2sk}}\left(\int_{B_{{R}/{2^{k}}}\setminus B_{{R}/{2^{k+1}}}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+c\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}}\frac{1}{2^{2sk}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{{R}/{2^{k_{0}+1}}}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}}.

As a consequence,

\limsup_{R\to+\infty}R^{-2s}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\varepsilon\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain that

(3.8)

\lim_{R\to+\infty}R^{-2s}\int_{B_{R}}|u(x)|^{2}\,dx=0.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle R^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{N+2s}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle R^{N}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\frac{N(N+2s)}{2s}}}\right)^{\frac{2s}{N}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}|u(x)|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}_{s}}},

for some
C>0
depending on
N
and
s
.

Since
u\in L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, this gives that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}R^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{N+2s}}\,dx=0.

From this, (
3.7
)
and (
3.8
), we obtain the desired claim.
∎

4.
Existence of a local minimum

In this section we prove the existence of a first solution to
problem (
1.1
) as a local minimim of the functional
f_{\varepsilon}
given in (
2.2
). To this end,
we start with a result about the convergence of sequences of
functions in bounded domains.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K
be any bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
be a
sequence in
L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
converging, as
n\to+\infty
, to some
u
in
L^{r}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
r\in[1,2^{*}_{s})
.

Then,

(4.1)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{K}|u_{n}^{q}(x)-u^{q}(x)|^{\frac{r}{q}}\,dx=0

and

(4.2)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{K}|u_{n}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)-u^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)|^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\,dx=0.

Proof.

For any
t\geqslant-1
, let

f(t):=\frac{|(1+t)^{q}-1|}{|t|^{q}}.

Recalling that
q\in(0,1)
, one can show that

f(-1)=1,\qquad\lim_{t\to 0}f(t)=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to+\infty}f(t)=1.

Thus, defining

L:=\sup_{t\geqslant-1}f(t),

we have that
L\in[1,+\infty)
.

Now we claim that

(4.3)

|a^{q}-b^{q}|\leqslant L|a-b|^{q}

for any
a
,
b\geqslant 0
. Without loss of generality, we can suppose
that
b\neq 0
, and write
t:=\frac{a}{b}-1
.

Then, we have that

|a^{q}-b^{q}|=b^{q}|(1+t)^{q}-1|\leqslant Lb^{q}|t|^{q}=L|a-b|^{q},

which proves (
4.3
).

From (
4.3
) and the convergence of
u_{n}
, we find that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{K}|u_{n}^{q}(x)-u^{q}(x)|^{\frac{r}{q}}\,dx\leqslant L^{\frac{r}{q}}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{K}|u_{n}(x)-u(x)|^{r}\,dx=0,

which establishes (
4.1
).

Now we prove (
4.2
). To do this, we first notice that,
for any
a\geqslant b\geqslant 0
,

a^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-b^{2^{*}_{s}-1}=(2^{*}_{s}-1)\int_{b}^{a}t^{2^{*}_{s}-2}\,dt\leqslant(2^{*}_{s}-1)a^{2^{*}_{s}-2}(a-b)\leqslant(2^{*}_{s}-1)(a+b)^{2^{*}_{s}-2}(a-b).

Actually, by possibly exchanging the roles of
a
and
b
, we conclude that,
for any
a
,
b\geqslant 0
,

|a^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-b^{2^{*}_{s}-1}|\leqslant(2^{*}_{s}-1)(a+b)^{2^{*}_{s}-2}|a-b|.

As a consequence, for any
a
,
b\geqslant 0
,

|a^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-b^{2^{*}_{s}-1}|^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\leqslant(2^{*}_{s}-1)^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}(a+b)^{\frac{2^{*}_{s}-2}{2^{*}_{s}-1}r}|a-b|^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}.

From this and the Hölder inequality with exponents
2^{*}_{s}-1
and
\frac{2^{*}_{s}-1}{2^{*}_{s}-2}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{K}

\displaystyle|u_{n}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)-u^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)|^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant(2^{*}_{s}-1)^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\int_{K}(u_{n}(x)+u(x))^{\frac{2^{*}_{s}-2}{2^{*}_{s}-1}r}(u_{n}(x)-u(x))^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant(2^{*}_{s}-1)^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\left(\int_{K}(u_{n}(x)+u(x))^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2^{*}_{s}-2}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\left(\int_{K}(u_{n}(x)-u(x))^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(2^{*}_{s}-1)^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\|u_{n}+u\|_{L^{r}(K)}^{\frac{r(2^{*}_{s}-2)}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{r}(K)}^{r(2^{*}_{s}-1)}.

From the convergence of
u_{n}
, we have that
\|u_{n}+u\|_{L^{r}(K)}\leqslant\|u_{n}\|_{L^{r}(K)}+\|u\|_{L^{r}(K)}

is bounded uniformly in
n
, while

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{r}(K)}=0,

therefore (
4.2
) holds.
∎

With this convergence result, we can establish the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
weakly
converging, as
n\to+\infty
, to some

in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Suppose that

(4.4)

{\lim_{n\to+\infty}}\sup_{{v\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\atop{[v]_{s}\leqslant 1}}\big|\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}),v\rangle\big|=0.

Then,
u
is a nonnegative weak solution of (
1.1
).

Proof.

We point out that

(4.5)

u_{n}\rightarrow u\mbox{ in }L^{r}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\;{\mbox{ for every~$r\in[1,2^{*}_{s})$}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad u_{n}\to u\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Now we let
v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
we use (
4.4
) to say that

(4.6)

\begin{split}0&=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}),v\rangle\\
&=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Bigg(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)(u_{n})_{+}^{q}(x)v(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)v(x)\,dx\Bigg).\end{split}

Let also
K
be a ball containing supp
(v)
.
Thanks to the strong convergence in (
4.5
),
we can use the Hölder inequality and
Lemma
4.1
to see that,
for every
r\in(2^{*}_{s}-1,2^{*}_{s})
,

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to+\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)\Big((u_{n})_{+}^{q}(x)-u_{+}^{q}(x)\Big)v(x)\,dx\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\lim_{n\to+\infty}C\left(\int_{K}|(u_{n})_{+}^{q}(x)-u_{+}^{q}(x)|^{\frac{r}{q}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}=0\end{split}

and

(4.8)

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to+\infty}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big((u_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)-u_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)\Big)v(x)\,dx\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\lim_{n\to+\infty}C\left(\int_{K}|(u_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)-u_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)|^{\frac{r}{2^{*}_{s}-1}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2^{*}_{s}-1}{r}}=0,\end{split}

for some
C>0
.

Moreover, setting

\xi_{n}(x,y):=\frac{u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}},

we see that
the sequence
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
, and so
there exists
\xi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
such that

(4.9)

\xi_{n}\rightharpoonup\xi
weakly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
as
n\to+\infty
.

Notice also that the function
\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}

belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})
.
As a consequence,

(4.10)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\xi_{n}(x,y)\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}\,dx\,dy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\xi(x,y)\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}\,dx\,dy.

Furthermore, since
u_{n}\to u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have that,
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
,

(4.11)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\xi_{n}(x,y)=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}.

Now we claim that

(4.12)

\xi(x,y)=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}\quad{\mbox{a.e. in~$\mathbb{R}^{2N}$}}.

To prove this, let
w\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2N})

with

(4.13)

supp
(w)\cap\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}\mbox{ s.t. }x=y\}=\varnothing
.

For short, we let
S:=
supp
(w)
.
By the strong convergence in (
4.5
), we have that
u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)

converges in
L^{1}(S)
, and therefore (see e.g. Theorem 4.9 in
[
4
]
) we have that
|u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y)|\leqslant g(x,y)
, with
g\in L^{1}(S)
.
This, (
4.11
),
(
4.13
) and the Dominated Convergence Theorem
entail that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\xi_{n}(x,y)\,w(x,y)\,dx\,dy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}\,w(x,y)\,dx\,dy.

From this and the weak convergence in (
4.9
),
we obtain that

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\xi(x,y)\,w(x,y)\,dx\,dy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}\,w(x,y)\,dx\,dy,

which gives the desired result in (
4.12
).

Thanks to (
4.10
) and (
4.12
)
we conclude that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Combining this information with (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we gather that
\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v\rangle=0
.

Now, to complete the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we perform a density argument.
Indeed, if
v\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists a
sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

converges to
v
strongly in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
We have that

(4.14)

\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v\rangle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v\rangle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v-v_{n}\rangle+\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v_{n}\rangle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v-v_{n}\rangle.

Also, we observe that, by the fractional Sobolev embedding and the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v_{n}-v\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant[u]_{s}[v_{n}-v]_{s}-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)u_{+}^{q}(x)(v_{n}(x)-v(x))\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)(v_{n}(x)-v(x))\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant[u]_{s}[v_{n}-v]_{s}+c_{1}\|v_{n}-v\|_{2^{*}_{s}}+c_{2}\|v_{n}-v\|_{2^{*}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leqslant[u]_{s}[v_{n}-v]_{s}+c_{3}[v_{n}-v]_{s},

for some
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
,
c_{3}>0
.

This entails that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v_{n}-v\rangle=0,

which, together with (
4.14
),
gives that
\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u),v\rangle=0
.

The sign of
u
has been commented on at the end of Section
2
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
, as desired.
∎

Our objective is now to prove that the functional
f_{\varepsilon}

defined in (

) satisfies the Palais-Smale
condition below a suitable level. This goal will be accomplished in the forthcoming Proposition

.
To this end, we need to identify the appropriate energy level,
and this operation will rely on the following observation:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

r:=\frac{2^{*}_{s}}{2^{*}_{s}-q-1}.

Then, there exists
C_{\star}>0
, depending only on
N
,
s
,
q

and
\|h\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, such that, for any

,

\frac{s}{N}a^{2^{*}_{s}}-\varepsilon\left(\frac{1}{q+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\|h\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\,a^{q+1}\geqslant-C_{\star}\,\varepsilon^{r}.

Proof.

Since
2^{*}_{s}>q+1
, given
\alpha
,
\beta>0
, we have that

c(\alpha,\beta,s,q):=\sup_{x>0}\big(\beta x^{q+1}-\alpha x^{2^{*}_{s}}\big)<+\infty.

In particular, choosing

,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{r}c(\alpha,\beta,s,q)\geqslant\beta\varepsilon^{r}x^{q+1}-\alpha\varepsilon^{r}x^{2^{*}_{s}}=\beta\varepsilon^{r-\frac{r(q+1)}{2^{*}_{s}}}a^{q+1}-\alpha a^{2^{*}_{s}}=\beta\varepsilon a^{q+1}-\alpha a^{2^{*}_{s}},

hence the desired result follows by choosing

C_{\star}\geqslant c\left(\frac{s}{N},\left(\frac{1}{q+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\|h\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},s,q\right).\qed

The notation in Lemma
4.3
will be implicitly assumed from now on.

(h_{0})

{\lim_{n\to+\infty}}\sup_{{v\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\atop{[v]_{s}\leqslant 1}}\big|\langle f^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u_{n}),v\rangle\big|=0.

strongly converges in

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(v(x)-v(y))(v_{-}(x)-v_{-}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v(x))v_{-}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(v(x)-v(y))(v_{-}(x)-v_{-}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(v_{-}(x)-v_{-}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy,

yielding that
v_{-}
vanishes identically, hence
v\geqslant 0
.

The claims about the equations satisfied by
v
and
u
are now a direct consequence of
the nonnegativity of
v
and
u_{\varepsilon}
and (
5.4
).
∎

In light of Lemma
5.1
, from now on we will focus our attention
on the translated functional
I_{\varepsilon}
, showing that it satisfies the
hypotheses of the Mountain Pass Theorem under a suitable threshold, provided
that
v=0
is the only critical point of
I_{\varepsilon}
. This will allow us
to perform a contradiction argument and show the existence of a second
nontrivial critical point (strictly speaking, the second critical point is therefore
not necessarily of mountain pass type, but it is obtained through the application of the Mountain Pass Theorem).

We start our analysis with the following result highlighting the connection between the functional
f_{\varepsilon}
defined in (
2.2
) and
I_{\varepsilon}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the nonnegative
local minimum of
f_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

given by Proposition
4.5
.

Then,
v=0
is a local minimum of
I_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Since
u_{\varepsilon}
is a local minimum of
f_{\varepsilon}

in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there exists
\eta>0
such that,
for all
[v]_{s}<\eta
,
we have that

(5.5)

f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}+v)\geqslant f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

Also, recalling (
2.2
) and (
5.2
)
(and using also the fact that
u_{\varepsilon}
is nonnegative),

(5.6)

\begin{split}&I_{\varepsilon}(v)-f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}+v_{+})+f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v(x)|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}(u_{\varepsilon}+v_{+})(x)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx\\
&\qquad+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)v_{+}(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}v_{+}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

We notice that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}(u_{\varepsilon}+v_{+})(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v_{+}(x)|^{2},dx

\displaystyle+2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))(v_{+}(x)-v_{+}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy.

Moreover, since
u_{\varepsilon}
is a nonnegative critical point of
f_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle f_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon}),v_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y))(v_{+}(x)-v_{+}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)v_{+}(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)v_{+}(x)\,dx.

Gathering these pieces of information, we deduce from (
5.6
) that

(5.7)

I_{\varepsilon}(v)-f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}+v_{+})+f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v(x)|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v_{+}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

Now, since
|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\geqslant|v_{+}(x)-v_{+}(y)|^{2}
for any
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have that

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v(x)|^{2}\,dx\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v_{+}(x)|^{2}\,dx.

This and (
5.7
) entail that

I_{\varepsilon}(v)-f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}+v_{+})+f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geqslant 0.

Accordingly, from this and (
5.5
)
we conclude that, if
[v_{+}]_{s}\leqslant[v]_{s}<\eta
,

I_{\varepsilon}(v)\geqslant f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}+v_{+})-f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geqslant 0=I_{\varepsilon}(0).

Hence
0
is a local minimum of
I_{\varepsilon}
, as desired.
∎

Now we prove that if
v=0
is the only critical point of
I_{\varepsilon}
, then
I_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the Palais-Smale
condition below a suitable threshold.
To this end, we present the following result.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
(h_{0})
and
(h_{1})
hold true and let
\{v_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that

(5.8)

\displaystyle{\lim_{n\to+\infty}}I_{\varepsilon}(v_{n})=c<\frac{s}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}

and

(5.9)

\displaystyle{\lim_{n\to+\infty}}\sup_{{w\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\atop{[w]_{s}\leqslant 1}}\big|\langle I^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(v_{n}),w\rangle\big|=0.

Then, the sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

We first show that, for all
\delta>0
, there exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that

(5.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(G(x,v_{n}(x))-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\right)\,dx\leqslant C(\varepsilon+\delta)\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}+C\varepsilon+C_{\delta}

for some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and
\delta
.

To prove this, recalling the definition of
I_{\varepsilon}
in (
5.2
), we write
g=g_{1}+g_{2}
and
G=G_{1}+G_{2}
, where

\displaystyle g_{1}(x,t):=\varepsilon h(x)\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}-u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)\Big),

\displaystyle g_{2}(x,t):=(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x),

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,t):=\frac{\varepsilon h(x)}{q+1}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q+1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{q+1}(x)\Big)-\varepsilon h(x)u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)t_{+}

\displaystyle{\mbox{and }}

\displaystyle G_{2}(x,t):=\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{2^{*}_{s}}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\Big)-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)t_{+}.

Since
q\in(0,1)
, we have that, for any
\tau\geqslant 0
,

(5.11)

(1+\tau)^{q}-1=q\int_{0}^{\tau}(1+\theta)^{q-1}\,d\theta\leqslant q\int_{0}^{\tau}\theta^{q-1}\,d\theta=\tau^{q}

and

(5.12)

(1+\tau)^{q+1}-1=(q+1)\int_{0}^{\tau}(1+\theta)^{q}\,d\theta\leqslant(q+1)(1+\tau)^{q}\tau.

We observe that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(5.13)

\begin{split}&(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}-u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)\leqslant t_{+}^{q}\\
{\mbox{and }}\qquad&(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q+1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{q+1}(x)\leqslant(q+1)(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}t_{+}.\end{split}

Indeed, if
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=0
, then the inequalities in (
5.13
)
are trivially satisfied. If instead
u_{\varepsilon}(x)>0
,
we take
\tau:=t_{+}/u_{\varepsilon}(x)

in (
5.11
)
and (
5.12
)
and
we find that

\displaystyle(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}-u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)\big((1+\tau)^{q}-1\big)\leqslant u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)\tau^{q}=t_{+}^{q}

and

\displaystyle(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q+1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{q+1}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}^{q+1}(x)\big((1+\tau)^{q+1}-1\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant(q+1)u_{\varepsilon}^{q+1}(x)(1+\tau)^{q}\tau=(q+1)(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}t_{+},

which give the desired inequalities in (
5.13
).

As a consequence of (
5.13
), we have that

\displaystyle|g_{1}(x,t)|\leqslant\varepsilon|h(x)|t_{+}^{q}

and

\displaystyle|G_{1}(x,t)|\leqslant\varepsilon|h(x)|(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}t_{+}+\varepsilon|h(x)|u_{\varepsilon}^{q}(x)t_{+}\leqslant 2\varepsilon|h(x)|(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}t_{+},

which yield that

(5.14)

|G_{1}(x,t)|+|g_{1}(x,t)t|\leqslant 2\varepsilon|h(x)|(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}t_{+}+\varepsilon|h(x)|t_{+}^{q+1}\leqslant 3\varepsilon|h(x)|(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{q}t_{+}.

We also recall that
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and
u_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
thanks to Proposition
4.5
, which entails that
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon

(see e.g. formula (5.1.6)
and the last formula in the proof of
Proposition 5.1.1 in
[
6
]
).
For this reason, it follows from (
5.14
) that

(5.15)

|G_{1}(x,t)|+|g_{1}(x,t)t|\leqslant C\varepsilon|h(x)|(1+t_{+}^{q+1})

for some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Additionally, since
q+1<2^{*}_{s}
, we have that
t_{+}^{q+1}\leqslant 1+t_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}}
,
and therefore (
5.15
) entails that

(5.16)

|G_{1}(x,t)|+|g_{1}(x,t)t|\leqslant C\varepsilon|h(x)|(1+t_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}})

up to changing
C
.

Moreover, by Lemma 6.2.1 in
[
6
]
, for any
\delta>0
there
exists
M_{\delta}>0
such that, for any
\alpha
,
\beta\geqslant 0

and
m>0
,

(\alpha+\beta)^{m+1}-\alpha^{m+1}-(m+1)\alpha^{m}\beta-\beta((\alpha+\beta)^{m}-\alpha^{m})\leqslant\delta\beta^{m+1}+M_{\delta}\alpha^{m+1}.

Now, given
\delta\in(0,1)
, we use this inequality
with
\alpha:=u_{\varepsilon}
,
\beta:=t_{+}
and
m:=2^{*}_{s}-1
to see that

\displaystyle G_{2}(x,t)-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}g_{2}(x,t)t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{2^{*}_{s}}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)-2^{*}_{s}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)t_{+}-(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{2^{*}_{s}-1}t+u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)t\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{2^{*}_{s}}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)-2^{*}_{s}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)t_{+}-(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+t_{+})^{2^{*}_{s}-1}t_{+}+u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)t_{+}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\big(\delta t_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}}+M_{\delta}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\big).

Together with (
5.16
), this gives that

\displaystyle G(x,t)-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}g(x,t)t

\displaystyle=G_{1}(x,t)-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}g_{1}(x,t)t+G_{2}(x,t)-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}g_{2}(x,t)t

\displaystyle\leqslant C\varepsilon|h(x)|(1+t_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}})+\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\big(\delta t_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}}+M_{\delta}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant C(\varepsilon+\delta)t_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}}+C\varepsilon|h(x)|+M_{\delta}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x),

up to changing the constants
C
and
M_{\delta}
.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(G(x,v_{n}(x))-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C(\varepsilon+\delta)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx+C\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h(x)|\,dx+M_{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C(\varepsilon+\delta)\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}+C\varepsilon+M_{\delta}

up to renaming
C
and
M_{\delta}
once more, and therefore the claim in (
5.10
) is established.

Now we show that

(5.17)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx\geqslant\frac{\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}}{8}-\widehat{C}

for some
\widehat{C}>0
.

To prove this claim, for any
\tau\geqslant 0
, we set

\ell(\tau):=\frac{\tau^{2^{*}_{s}-1}}{2}-(1+\tau)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}+1.

We observe that
\ell(0)=0
and

\lim_{\tau\to+\infty}\ell(\tau)=-\infty.

Therefore,

L:=\sup_{\tau\geqslant 0}\ell(\tau)\in[0,+\infty),

and thus

(5.18)

(1+\tau)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-1=\frac{\tau^{2^{*}_{s}-1}}{2}-\ell(\tau)\geqslant\frac{\tau^{2^{*}_{s}-1}}{2}-L.

We point out that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(5.19)

g_{2}(x,v_{n}(x))\geqslant\frac{(v_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)}{2}-Lu_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x).

Indeed, if
u_{\varepsilon}(x)=0
, then
g_{2}(x,v_{n}(x))=(v_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)
, which entails (
5.19
). If insted
u_{\varepsilon}(x)\neq 0
,
then we can take
\tau:=(v_{n})_{+}(x)/u_{\varepsilon}(x)

in (
5.18
) and obtain that

\displaystyle g_{2}(x,v_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+(v_{n})_{+}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)=u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)\big((1+\tau)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-1\big)

\displaystyle\geqslant u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)\left(\frac{\tau^{2^{*}_{s}-1}}{2}-L\right)=\frac{(v_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)}{2}-Lu_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x),

thus completing the proof of (
5.19
).

Hence, integrating (
5.19
) and using the Young inequality, we obtain that

(5.20)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{2}(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{2}(x,v_{n}(x))(v_{n})_{+}(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\geqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{(v_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)}{2}-Lu_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)(v_{n})_{+}(x)\right)\,dx\geqslant\frac{\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}}{4}-\widetilde{C}\end{split}

for some
\widetilde{C}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Additionally, by (
5.16
) we have that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{1}(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx\right|\leqslant C\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|h(x)|\big(1+(v_{n})_{+}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\big)\,dx\leqslant C\varepsilon\Big(1+\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}\Big),

up to renaming
C
.

Combining this with (
5.20
), we obtain that, if
\varepsilon

is sufficiently small,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{1}(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{2}(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}}{4}-\widetilde{C}-C\varepsilon\Big(1+\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{2^{*}_{s}}^{2^{*}_{s}}}{8}-\widehat{C},

for some
\widehat{C}>0
.
This establishes the claim in (
5.17
).

Now, we show that the sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For this, let
\lambda>1
.
From (
5.8
) and (
5.9
)
we have that

(5.21)

I_{\varepsilon}(v_{n})-\frac{1}{\lambda}\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle\leqslant M+\frac{M}{\lambda}[v_{n}]_{s},

for some
M>0
.

Exploiting this inequality with
\lambda:=2
, we get that

\displaystyle M+\frac{M}{2}[v_{n}]_{s}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}(v_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(x,v_{n}(x))\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(x,v_{n}(x))\,dx+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(x,v_{n}(x))\,dx+\frac{s}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx.

Accordingly, given
\delta>0
we use (
5.10
) and (
5.17
)
and we thereby arrive at

M+\frac{M}{2}[v_{n}]_{s}\geqslant-C(\varepsilon+\delta)\|(v_{n})_{+}\|^{2^{*}_{s}}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}-C\varepsilon-C_{\delta}+\frac{s}{N}\left(\frac{\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}}{8}-\widehat{C}\right).

We now take
\delta:=\frac{s}{16CN}
(which is now fixed once and for all)
and we get that

M+\frac{M}{2}[v_{n}]_{s}\geqslant-C\varepsilon\|(v_{n})_{+}\|^{2^{*}_{s}}_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}-C{\varepsilon}-C+\frac{s}{N}\left(\frac{\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}}{16}-\widehat{C}\right).

We also take
\varepsilon
sufficiently smally, say
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\frac{s}{32CN}\right)
, and we obtain that

M+\frac{M}{2}[v_{n}]_{s}\geqslant\frac{s}{N}\frac{\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}}{32}-C,

up to renaming the constant
C>0
.

We can write this inequality as

(5.22)

\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}\leqslant\widetilde{M}\big([v_{n}]_{s}+1\big)

for some
\widetilde{M}>0
.

Furthermore, employing (
5.10
) with
\delta:=1

and (
5.21
) with
\lambda:=2^{*}_{s}
, we gather that

\displaystyle M+\frac{M}{2^{*}_{s}}[v_{n}]_{s}

\displaystyle\geqslant I_{\varepsilon}(v_{n})-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\langle I_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(v_{n}),(v_{n})\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\right)[v_{n}]_{s}^{2}+\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(x,v_{n}(x))v_{n}(x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(x,v_{n}(x))\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{s}{N}[v_{n}]_{s}^{2}-C\Big(\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{2^{*}_{s}}^{2^{*}_{s}}+1\Big)

provided that
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small.

As a consequence,

[v_{n}]_{s}^{2}\leqslant\widehat{M}\Big([v_{n}]_{s}+\|(v_{n})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}+1\Big)

for some
\widehat{M}>0
.

From this and (
5.22
), we infer that

[v_{n}]_{s}^{2}\leqslant M\big([v_{n}]_{s}+1\big),

for some
M>0
.
Thus
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, as desired.
∎

We now prove that the functional
I_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the
Palais-Smale condition below a suitable level
c
.
The strategy of the proof is similar
to that of Proposition
4.4
, nevertheless we provide
here below a self-contained proof,
as in this case there are some additional difficulties to take care of.

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that
(h_{0})
and
(h_{1})
hold true.
Assume that
v=0
is the only critical point of
I_{\varepsilon}
in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that

(5.23)

\displaystyle{\lim_{n\to+\infty}}I_{\varepsilon}(v_{n})=c<\frac{s}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}

and

(5.24)

{\lim_{n\to+\infty}}\sup_{{w\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\atop{[w]_{s}\leqslant 1}}\big|\langle I^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(v_{n}),w\rangle\big|=0.

Then, up to a subsequence,
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
strongly converges to
v=0
in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
n\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
5.3
, we know that
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and thus so is
\{(v_{n})_{+}\}_{n}
.
Therefore, up to a subsequence,

\begin{split}&v_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{\infty}\mbox{ weakly in }\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
&v_{n}\rightarrow v_{\infty}\mbox{ in }L^{r}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\;{\mbox{ for every~$r\in[1,2^{*}_{s})$,}}\\
&v_{n}\to v_{\infty}\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&(v_{n})_{+}\rightharpoonup(v_{\infty})_{+}\mbox{ weakly in }\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
&(v_{n})_{+}\rightarrow(v_{\infty})_{+}\mbox{ in }L^{r}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})\;{\mbox{ for every~$r\in[1,2^{*}_{s})$}}\\
\mbox{and}\qquad&(v_{n})_{+}\to(v_{\infty})_{+}\mbox{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{split}

From the strong convergence of
(v_{n})_{+}
, we can assume up to a
subsequence that
u_{\varepsilon}+(v_{n})_{+}
converges to
u_{\varepsilon}+(v_{\infty})_{+}
in
L^{r}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
r\in[1,2^{*}_{s})
.

We point out that

(5.25)

v_{\infty}
is a critical point of
I_{\varepsilon}
.

To check this, one can argue exactly as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
,
with suitable modifications for the convergence of the following terms:
given
w\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with supp
(\phi)\subset K
,
we can use the Hölder inequality and
Lemma
4.1
(applied with
u_{n}:=(u_{\varepsilon}+(v_{n})_{+})
) to see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}

5.47
), we obtain that

\begin{split}\frac{s}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}&>\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\right)\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+(v_{n})_{+}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx\Bigg].\end{split}

From this, (
5.27
) and (
5.30
) we conclude that

\frac{s}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}>\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\right)\left(\sum_{i\in I}\nu_{i}+\nu_{\infty}\right)=\frac{s}{N}\left(\sum_{{i\in I}\atop{\nu_{i}\neq 0}}\nu_{i}+\nu_{\infty}\right).

This, (
5.28
), (
5.31
),
(
5.32
) and (
5.40
) entail that
\nu_{i}=0

for all
i\in I
and that
\nu_{\infty}=0
. The proof of (
5.41
) is thereby complete.

From (
5.27
), (
5.31
)
and (
5.41
), we gather that

(5.50)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+(v_{n})_{+}(x)\big)^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx.

Now, in light of (
5.24
),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\langle I^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big[\varepsilon h(x)\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+(v_{n})_{+}(x))^{q}-u_{\varepsilon}^{q}\Big)+\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+(v_{n})_{+}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\Big)\Big](v_{n})_{+}(x)\,dx\Bigg].

Accordingly, exploiting (
5.44
),
(
5.46
) and (
5.49
),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+(v_{n})_{+}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(v_{n})_{+}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+(v_{n})_{+}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u_{\varepsilon}(x)+(v_{n})_{+}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}-1}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx\Bigg].

We can now use (
5.48
)
and (
5.50
) to conclude that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{s}v_{n}(x)|^{2}\,dx,

which completes the proof of Proposition
5.4
.
∎

Now, in order to prove the existence of a second solution, we consider
solutions
u\in\mathcal{D}^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to the equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u=u^{2^{*}_{s}-1}.

Notice that this equation has a manifold
Z
of positive solutions

Z:=\left\{z_{\mu,\xi}(x)=\mu^{-\frac{N-2s}{2}}z\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\mu}\right){\mbox{ with }}\,\mu>0,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\right\},

where

(5.51)

z(x):=\frac{C_{N,s}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}}

for a suitable
C_{N,s}>0
.

Furthermore, we set

(5.52)

\begin{split}\widetilde{G}(x,v)&:=\int_{0}^{v}\widetilde{g}(x,t)dt\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad\widetilde{g}(x,t)&:=\begin{cases}(u_{\varepsilon}+t)^{q}-u_{\varepsilon}^{q}&\mbox{if }t\geqslant 0,\\
0&\mbox{if }t<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

We observe that

(5.53)

\widetilde{G}(x,0)=0

and also, since
\widetilde{g}(x,t)\geqslant 0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, that

(5.54)

\widetilde{G}(x,v)\geqslant 0\mbox{ for any }v\geqslant 0.

Moreover, we write the ball
B
given by
(h_{1})

as
B_{\mu_{0}}(\xi)
for some
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\mu_{0}>0
.
In this way,

(5.55)

h>0
in
B_{\mu_{0}}(\xi)
.

We point out that
\xi
and
\mu_{0}
are now fixed once and for all.

We pick a cut-off function
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\mu_{0}}(\xi),[0,1])
with

(5.56)

\phi(x)=1\mbox{ for any }x\in B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi).

Also, for any
\mu>0
, recalling (
5.51
), we set

(5.57)

z_{\mu,\xi}(x):=\mu^{-\frac{N-2s}{2}}z\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\mu}\right).

By scaling,

(5.58)

[z_{\mu,\xi}]_{s}^{2}=\|z_{\mu,\xi}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2^{*}_{s}}=S^{\frac{N}{2s}}.

In addition, by Proposition 21 in
[
10
]
,

(5.59)

[\phi z_{\mu,\xi}]_{s}^{2}\leqslant S^{\frac{N}{2s}}+C\mu^{N-2s}

and,
by Lemma 6.5.1 in
[
6
]
,

(5.60)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(1-\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\big)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx\leqslant C\mu^{N},

for some
C>0
.

The next result states that we can concentrate the mass
near the set where
h
is positive to obtain a positive integral.

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\mu>0
and
t\geqslant 0
, we have that

(5.61)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)\,\widetilde{G}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx\geqslant 0.

Proof.

Since
\phi(x)=0
if
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\mu_{0}}(\xi)
, using (
5.53
) we have that
\widetilde{G}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))=0

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\mu_{0}}(\xi)
.

As a result,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)\,\widetilde{G}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx=\int_{B_{\mu_{0}}(\xi)}h(x)\,\widetilde{G}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx.

From
(h_{1})
and (
5.54
) we obtain the desired result.
∎

Now we show that the functional
I_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the
geometry of the Mountain Pass Theorem. To this end, we first point out that Proposition
5.2
gives that
0
is a local minimum for
I_{\varepsilon}
.

Now, we show that the path induced by the function
\phi z_{\mu,\xi}
attains negative values. In order to do this,
we introduce the auxiliary functional

(5.62)

\begin{split}&I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(v):=\frac{1}{2}[v]_{s}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{\star}(x,v)\,dx,\\
{\mbox{where }}\quad&G^{\star}(x,v):=\int_{0}^{v}g^{\star}(x,t)\,dt\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad&g^{\star}(x,v):=\begin{cases}(u_{\varepsilon}+t)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}&\mbox{if }t\geqslant 0,\\
0&\mbox{if }t<0.\end{cases}\end{split}

We have the following result:

Lemma 5.6
.

There exists
\mu_{1}\in(0,\mu_{0})
such that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sup_{\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})}I^{\star}_{\varepsilon}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})=-\infty.

In particular, there exists
T_{1}>0
such that

(5.63)

\sup_{\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})}I^{\star}_{\varepsilon}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\leqslant 0

for any
t\geqslant T_{1}
.

Proof.

We observe that, if
v\geqslant 0
,

(5.64)

G^{\star}(x,v)=\int_{0}^{v}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}+t)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\Big)\,dt=\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}+v)^{2^{*}_{s}}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}\Big)-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}v.

Moreover, by the Young inequality,

u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)\leqslant u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)+z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x).

From this and (
5.64
) we obtain that

\displaystyle G^{\star}(x,t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}+t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})^{2^{*}_{s}}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}\Big)-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}t\phi z_{\mu,\xi}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\Big((t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})^{2^{*}_{s}}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}\Big)-tu_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}}-tz_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}.

Thus, integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and recalling
(h_{0})
and (
5.58
), we gather that

(5.65)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{\star}(x,t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\,dx\geqslant\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx-C-Ct

for some
C>0
.

Furthermore, from (
5.58
) and (
5.60
) we deduce that there exists
\mu_{1}\in(0,1)

such that if
\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})
then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx\geqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx-C\mu^{N}=S^{\frac{N}{2s}}-C\mu^{N}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}.

Inserting this in (
5.65
) we obtain that, if
\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{\star}(x,t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\,dx\geqslant\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{s}}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}-C-Ct.

This and (
5.62
) give that

I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\leqslant\frac{t^{2}}{2}[\phi z_{\mu,\xi}]_{s}+C(1+t)-\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{s}}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}.

Hence, recalling (
5.59
), we find that

I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\leqslant C(1+t+t^{2})-\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2\cdot 2^{*}_{s}}S^{\frac{N}{2s}},

up to renaming constants, for any
\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})
.
Since
2^{*}_{s}>2
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
5.6
we have the following:

Corollary 5.7
.

There exists
\mu_{1}\in(0,\mu_{0})
such that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\sup_{\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})}I_{\varepsilon}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})=-\infty.

In particular, there exists
T_{1}>0
such that

(5.66)

\sup_{\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})}I_{\varepsilon}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\leqslant 0

for any
t\geqslant T_{1}
.

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
G
and
\widetilde{G}
in (
5.1
)
and (
5.52
) respectively, we can write
G=G^{\star}+\varepsilon h\widetilde{G}
. Thus, from Lemma
5.5

we see that

(5.67)

I_{\varepsilon}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})=I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(x)\widetilde{G}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx\leqslant I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi}).

From this and Lemma
5.6
we obtain the desired result.
∎

In addition, we have the following result for
the functional
I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

There exists
\mu_{\star}\in(0,\mu_{0})
such that if
\mu\in(0,\mu_{\star})
then

(5.68)

\sup_{t\geqslant 0}I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})<\frac{s}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{1}>0
and
T_{1}>0
as in Lemma
5.6
.
We take
\mu_{\star}\in(0,\mu_{1}]
. Without loss of generality, we can suppose
that
\mu_{\star}\leqslant\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{16}
whenever needed.

In this way, in light of (
5.63
) we see that

(5.69)

\sup_{t\geqslant T_{1}}\sup_{\mu\in(0,\mu_{\star})}I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\leqslant\sup_{t\geqslant T_{1}}\sup_{\mu\in(0,\mu_{1})}I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\leqslant 0<\frac{s}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}.

Hence, the claim in (
5.68
) will be completed if we prove that,
for any
\mu\in(0,\mu_{\star})
,

(5.70)

\sup_{t\in[0,T_{1}]}I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})<\frac{s}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}.

In order to do so, we set

(5.71)

m:=\begin{cases}2&\mbox{if }N>4s,\\
2^{*}_{s}-1&\mbox{if }N\in(2s,4s],\end{cases}

and

(5.72)

\Omega:=\begin{cases}B_{2\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)\setminus B_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)&\mbox{if }N>4s,\\
\mathbb{R}^{N}&\mbox{if }N\in(2s,4s].\end{cases}

We notice that, if
N\in(2s,4s]
, then
m-2=\frac{6s-N}{N-2s}>0
, and so

(5.73)

m-2\geqslant 0\mbox{ for every }N>2s.

We claim that, for any
t\in[0,T_{1}]
, any
\mu\in(0,\mu_{\star})

and any
x\in\Omega
,

(5.74)

G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\geqslant\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)}{2^{*}_{s}}+\frac{cu_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-m}(x)t^{m}\phi^{m}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{m}(x)}{m},

for some
c>0
.

To prove this claim,
we treat separately the cases
N>4s
and
N\in(2s,4s]
. If
N>4s
,
we take
a:=u_{\varepsilon}(x)
and

b\in\left[0,t\inf_{x\in\Omega}\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)\right].

We point out that

a\geqslant\inf_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}\geqslant\inf_{B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}=:j_{\varepsilon}

and we stress that
j_{\varepsilon}>0
, thanks to (
5.55
) and
Proposition
4.5
.

Moreover, recalling the definition of
z
in (
5.51
),

b\leqslant t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)\leqslant tz_{\mu,\xi}(x)=t\mu^{-\frac{N-2s}{2}}z\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\mu}\right)=\frac{C_{N,s}t\mu^{-\frac{N-2s}{2}}}{\left(1+\left|\frac{x-\xi}{\mu}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}}=\frac{C_{N,s}t\mu^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}}{(\mu^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2})^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}}.

Since
x\in B_{2\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)\setminus B_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)
,
we have that
|x-\xi|\geqslant\sqrt{\mu}
, and so

b\leqslant\frac{C_{N,s}t\mu^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}}{(\mu^{2}+\mu)^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}}\leqslant\frac{C_{N,s}t\mu^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}}{\mu^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}}=C_{N,s}t\leqslant C_{N,s}T_{1}.

From this, we obtain that
\frac{b}{a}\leqslant k

with
k:=\frac{C_{N,s}T_{1}}{j_{\varepsilon}}
, hence we can apply Lemma
3.7
to
obtain that

\displaystyle G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+b)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)\Big)\,db

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}\Big((a+b)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-a^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\Big)\,db

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}\Big(b^{2^{*}_{s}-1}+c_{2^{*}_{s}-1,k}a^{2^{*}_{s}-2}b\Big)\,db

\displaystyle=\frac{(t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}+c_{2^{*}_{s}-1,k}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-2}(x)\frac{(t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))^{2}}{2}.

This and (
5.71
) yield (
5.74
) when
N>4s
.

To prove (
5.74
) when
N\in(2s,4s]
, we first observe
that

2^{*}_{s}-1=\frac{N+2s}{N-2s}\geqslant 2.

Hence, we choose

a\in\left[0,\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)\right]

and
b:=u_{\varepsilon}(x)
and we use Lemma
3.6
to see that

\displaystyle G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+a)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)\Big)\,da

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}\Big((a+b)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-b^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\Big)\,da

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}\Big(a^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-c_{2^{*}_{s}-1}a^{2^{*}_{s}-2}b\Big)\,da

\displaystyle=\frac{(t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}+c_{2^{*}_{s}-1}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\frac{(t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}-1}}{2^{*}_{s}-1}.

This and (
5.71
) give (
5.74
) when
N\in(2s,4s]
, thus completing the proof of (
5.74
).

Now we claim that, for any
t\in[0,T_{1}]
,

\mu\in(0,\mu_{\star})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

(5.75)

G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\geqslant\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)}{2^{*}_{s}}.

We remark that (
5.74
) is a stronger inequality than (
5.75
),
but it only holds in
\Omega
,
while (
5.75
) holds in the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and this is
an advantage when
N>4s
(recall the definition of
\Omega
in (
5.72
)).

In order to prove (
5.75
), we use Lemma
3.5

with
a:=u_{\varepsilon}(x)
and
b\in[0,+\infty)
to see that

\displaystyle G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}\Big((u_{\varepsilon}(x)+b)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)\Big)\,db

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}\Big((a+b)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}-a^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\Big)\,db

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{0}^{t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x)}b^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\,db

\displaystyle=\frac{(t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}},

which establishes (
5.75
).

Combining (
5.74
) and (
5.75
), we obtain that

(5.76)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx+\int_{\Omega}G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)}{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)}{2^{*}_{s}}+\frac{cu_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-m}(x)t^{m}\phi^{m}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{m}(x)}{m}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx+\frac{ct^{m}}{m}\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-m}(x)\phi^{m}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{m}(x)\,dx.

Now, we show that

(5.77)

\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-m}(x)\phi^{m}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{m}(x)\,dx\geqslant c^{\prime}\mu^{\beta},

for some
c^{\prime}>0
, where

(5.78)

\beta:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{N}{2}&\mbox{if }N>4s,\\
\\
\dfrac{N-2s}{2}&\mbox{if }N\in(2s,4s].\end{cases}

To prove this, when
N>4s
we observe that, for small
\mu
, we have
that
B_{2\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)\subset B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi)
, and in this set
\phi(x)=1
thanks to (
5.56
).

Hence, recalling (
5.71
) and (
5.72
) we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-m}(x)\phi^{m}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{m}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)\setminus B_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-2}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\inf_{B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-2}\int_{B_{2\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)\setminus B_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)}z_{\mu,\xi}^{2}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\mu^{-(N-2s)}\inf_{B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-2}\int_{B_{2\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)\setminus B_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)}z^{2}\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\mu}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\mu^{2s}\inf_{B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-2}\int_{B_{{2}/{\sqrt{\mu}}}\setminus B_{{1}/{\sqrt{\mu}}}}z^{2}(y)\,dy.

Thus, recalling (
5.51
) and taking
\mu
small enough,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-m}(x)\phi^{m}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{m}(x)\,dx\geqslant c_{1}\mu^{2s}\int_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2/\sqrt{\mu}}\frac{\rho^{N-1}}{(1+\rho^{2})^{N-2s}}\,d\rho

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{1}\mu^{2s}\int_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2/\sqrt{\mu}}\frac{\rho^{N-1}}{(2\rho^{2})^{N-2s}}\,d\rho=c_{2}\mu^{\frac{N}{2}},

for some
c_{1}
,
c_{2}>0
, and this proves (
5.77
) when
N>4s
.

Now we prove (
5.77
) when
N\in(2s,4s]
. For this, we recall (
5.71
) and (
5.72
) and observe that, for
\mu

sufficiently small,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{s}-m}(x)\phi^{m}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{m}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\phi^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\mu^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}\int_{B_{2\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}(x)z^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\mu}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant\mu^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}\inf_{B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{2\sqrt{\mu}}(\xi)}z^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\left(\frac{x-\xi}{\mu}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=\mu^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}\inf_{B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{{2}/{\sqrt{\mu}}}}z^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\geqslant\mu^{\frac{N-2s}{2}}\inf_{B_{\mu_{0}/2}(\xi)}u_{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{1}}z^{2^{*}_{s}-1}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\geqslant c^{\prime}\mu^{\frac{N-2s}{2}},

for some
c^{\prime}>0
, which concludes the proof in the case

.
Thus, the claim in (
5.77
) is established.

Now, using (
5.77
) in (
5.76
), we obtain that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx\geqslant\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}(x)\,dx+\frac{c\,t^{m}\mu^{\beta}}{m},

up to renaming

.

From this and (
5.60
), we infer that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{\star}(x,t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\,dx\geqslant\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}z_{\mu,\xi}^{2^{*}_{s}}\,dx+\frac{c\,t^{m}\mu^{\beta}}{m}-\frac{Ct^{2^{*}_{s}}\mu^{N}}{2^{*}_{s}}.

This and (
5.58
) give that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G^{\star}(x,t\phi(x)z_{\mu,\xi}(x))\,dx\geqslant\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}+\frac{c\,t^{m}\mu^{\beta}}{m}-\frac{Ct^{2^{*}_{s}}\mu^{N}}{2^{*}_{s}}.

As a consequence, recalling (
5.59
), up to renaming constants,

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{t^{2}}{2}[\phi z_{\mu,\xi}]_{s}-\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}-\frac{ct^{m}\mu^{\beta}}{m}+\frac{Ct^{2^{*}_{s}}\mu^{N}}{2^{*}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{t^{2}}{2}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}-\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}S^{\frac{N}{2s}}-\frac{c\,t^{m}\mu^{\beta}}{m}+\frac{Ct^{2^{*}_{s}}\mu^{N}}{2^{*}_{s}}+\frac{Ct^{2}\mu^{N-2s}}{2},

and so

(5.79)

I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\leqslant S^{\frac{N}{2s}}\Psi(t),

with

\Psi(t):=\frac{t^{2}}{2}-\frac{t^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}-\frac{c\,t^{m}\mu^{\beta}}{m}+\frac{Ct^{2^{*}_{s}}\mu^{N}}{2^{*}_{s}}+\frac{Ct^{2}\mu^{N-2s}}{2}

for some
c
,
C>0
.

Now we claim that, if
\mu
is sufficiently small,

(5.80)

\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\Psi(t)<\frac{s}{N}.

To show this, we observe that
\Psi(0)=0
and, if
\mu
is sufficiently small,

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\Psi(t)=-\infty,

since
2^{*}_{s}>\max\{2,m\}
, thanks to (
5.71
).

On this account, we conclude that
\Psi
attains its maximum at some point
T\in[0,+\infty)
. Clearly, it
T=0
, then
\Psi(T)=0
and (
5.80
) is obvious, so we can assume that
T\in(0,+\infty)
.
Consequently, we have
\Psi^{\prime}(T)=0
, and therefore

0=\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(T)}{T}=1-T^{2^{*}_{s}-2}-c\mu^{\beta}T^{m-2}+C\mu^{N}T^{2^{*}_{s}-2}+C\mu^{N-2s}.

We set

\Phi_{\mu}(t):=1-t^{2^{*}_{s}-2}-c\mu^{\beta}t^{m-2}+C\mu^{N}t^{2^{*}_{s}-2}+C\mu^{N-2s}

and we have that
T=T(\mu)
is a solution of
\Phi_{\mu}(t)=0
.

We remark that

\Phi_{\mu}^{\prime}(t)=-(2^{*}_{s}-2)(1-C\mu^{N})t^{2^{*}_{s}-3}-c\mu^{\beta}(m-2)t^{m-3}<0,

since
m-2\geqslant 0
(recall (
5.73

and
(2^{*}_{s}-2)(1-C\mu^{N})\geqslant 0
if
\mu
is
small enough.
This says that
\Phi_{\mu}
is strictly decreasing, hence
T=T(\mu)
is the unique solution of
\Phi_{\mu}(t)=0
.

It is convenient to write
\tau(\mu):=T(\mu^{\frac{1}{\beta}})

and
\eta:=\mu^{\beta}
, so that our equation becomes

\displaystyle 0=\Phi_{\mu}(T(\mu))=\Phi_{\mu}(\tau(\mu^{\beta}))=\Phi_{\mu}(\tau(\eta))=1-\big(1-C\eta^{\frac{N}{\beta}}\big)(\tau(\eta))^{2^{*}_{s}-2}-c\eta(\tau(\eta))^{m-2}+C\eta^{\frac{N-2s}{\beta}}.

Hence, if we differentiate in
\eta

(5.81)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\left(1-(1-C\eta^{\frac{N}{\beta}})(\tau(\eta))^{2^{*}_{s}-2}-c\eta(\tau(\eta))^{m-2}+C\eta^{\frac{N-2s}{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=-(2^{*}_{s}-2)\big(1-C\eta^{\frac{N}{\beta}}\big)(\tau(\eta))^{2^{*}_{s}-3}\tau^{\prime}(\eta)+C\frac{N}{\beta}\eta^{\frac{N}{\beta}-1}(\tau(\eta))^{2^{*}_{s}-2}

\displaystyle-c(\tau(\eta))^{m-2}-c(m-2)\eta(\tau(\eta))^{m-3}\tau^{\prime}(\eta)+\frac{C(N-2s)}{\beta}\eta^{\frac{N-2s}{\beta}-1}.

Now, we observe that, by (

\displaystyle\frac{N-2s}{\beta}-1

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{2(N-2s)}{N}-1&\mbox{if }N>4s,\\

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\dfrac{N-4s}{N}&\mbox{if }N>4s,\\
\\

\frac{N-2s}{\beta}-1>0.

We also notice that when
\mu=0

T=1

.
Hence, we evaluate (

and use (

.
In this way,

\tau^{\prime}(0)=-\frac{c}{2^{*}_{s}-2},

which gives that

and accordingly

T(\mu)=\tau(\mu^{\beta})=1-\frac{c\mu^{\beta}}{2^{*}_{s}-2}+o(\mu^{\beta})=1-c_{0}\mu^{\beta}+o(\mu^{\beta}),

for some

.

Consequently, we have that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\Psi(t)

\displaystyle=\Psi(T(\mu))

\displaystyle=(1+C\mu^{N-2s})\frac{(T(\mu))^{2}}{2}-(1-C\mu^{N})\frac{(T(\mu))^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}-\frac{c\mu^{\beta}(T(\mu))^{m}}{m}

\displaystyle=(1+C\mu^{N-2s})\frac{(1-c_{0}\mu^{\beta}+o(\mu^{\beta}))^{2}}{2}-(1-C\mu^{N})\frac{(1-c_{0}\mu^{\beta}+o(\mu^{\beta}))^{2^{*}_{s}}}{2^{*}_{s}}

\displaystyle-\frac{c\mu^{\beta}(1-c_{0}\mu^{\beta}+o(\mu^{\beta}))^{m}}{m}

\displaystyle=(1+C\mu^{N-2s})\frac{1-2c_{0}\mu^{\beta}}{2}-(1-C\mu^{N})\frac{1-2^{*}_{s}c_{0}\mu^{\beta}}{2^{*}_{s}}-\frac{c\mu^{\beta}}{m}+o(\mu^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-2c_{0}\mu^{\beta}}{2}-\frac{1-2^{*}_{s}c_{0}\mu^{\beta}}{2^{*}_{s}}-\frac{c\mu^{\beta}}{m}+o(\mu^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}-\frac{c\mu^{\beta}}{m}+o(\mu^{\beta})

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{N},

which proves (

Using (
5.79
) and (

\sup_{t\in[0,T_{1}]}I_{\varepsilon}^{\star}(t\phi z_{\mu,\xi})\leqslant S^{\frac{N}{2s}}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\Psi(t)<\frac{s}{N}S^{\frac{N}{2s}},

which gives (

Appendix A
On Proposition 3.1.1 in
[
6
]

As mentioned in the introduction, the results presented here were proved in
[
6
]

in the parameter range
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
. It was incorrectly stated in
[
6
]
that the methods presented there were able to capture the full range
s\in(0,1)
and the objective of this appendix is to clarify “what goes wrong” in
[
6
]
when
s>\frac{1}{2}
.

As a matter of fact, all the methods in
[
6
]
can cover the full fractional range
s\in(0,1)
, with the exception of Proposition 3.1.1 there, which is only valid
when
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
.

The technical issue in the proof of Proposition 3.1.1 in
[
6
]
is that it relied on Muckenhoupt weights, which is totally fine when
s\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
,
but when
s>\frac{1}{2}
the exponent
q
used in that proof becomes less than
1
, thus going away from the Muckenhoupt class. At a first glance, this may seem a minor technical glitch, to be fixed through one of the many weighted Sobolev inequalities that are available in the literature – and one may suspect that this is the case also because usually the parameter range below
\frac{1}{2}
(not above!) is the more “problematic” one, and one may imagine that for inequalities valid for
s=1
and below
s=\frac{1}{2}
the use of a smart interpolation trick could fix all technical issues.

But no, the situation is surprisingly more complicated, as shown by the next observation.

Lemma A.1
.

Proposition 3.1.1 in
[
6
]
is not valid
when
s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
.

More specifically, for all
N\geqslant 2
and
s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
, setting
\gamma:=1+\frac{2}{N-2s}
, there exists
a sequence of functions
U_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty),\,[0,1])
such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)}y^{1-2s}|U_{n}(x,y)|^{2\gamma}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}}{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)}y^{1-2s}|\nabla U_{n}(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}=+\infty.

Proof.

We use the short notation
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)

and denote points by
X=(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,+\infty)
.

Let
R>1
and
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1},[0,1])
with
\Phi=1
in
B_{1/2}
.
Let also
U(X)=U(x,y):=\Phi(x,y-R)
.

Then, if
s\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}y^{1-2s}|U(X)|^{2\gamma}\,dX\geqslant\int_{B_{1/2}(Re_{n+1})}y^{1-2s}|\Phi(x,y-R)|^{2\gamma}\,dX

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{B_{1/2}(Re_{n+1})}y^{1-2s}\,dX\geqslant|B_{1/2}|\,\left(R+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{1-2s}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}y^{1-2s}|\nabla U(X)|^{2}\,dX=\int_{B_{1}(Re_{n+1})}y^{1-2s}|\nabla\Phi(x,y-R)|^{2}\,dX

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\left(R-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{1-2s}\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla\Phi(X)|^{2}\,dX.

As a result,

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}y^{1-2s}|U(X)|^{2\gamma}\,dX\right)^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}}{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}y^{1-2s}|\nabla U(X)|^{2}\,dX\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\geqslant c\,\frac{\displaystyle\left(R+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1-2s}{2\gamma}}}{\displaystyle\left(R-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1-2s}{2}}}\geqslant cR^{\frac{1-2s}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-1\right)}

for some
c>0
possibly varying from step to step.

Hence, since
\gamma>1
, and thus
\frac{1-2s}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-1\right)>0
,
we conclude that

\lim_{R\to+\infty}\frac{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}y^{1-2s}|U(X)|^{2\gamma}\,dX\right)^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}}{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}y^{1-2s}|\nabla U(X)|^{2}\,dX\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}=+\infty,

as claimed.
∎

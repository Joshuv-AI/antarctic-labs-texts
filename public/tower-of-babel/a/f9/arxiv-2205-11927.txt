Image Trinarization Using a Partial Differential Equations: A Novel Approach to Automatic Sperm Image Analysis Journal: Applied Mathematics and Computation
B. A. Jacobs

Abstract
Partial differential equations have recently garnered substantial attention as an image processing framework due to their extensibility, the ability to rigorously engineer and analyse the governing dynamics as well as the ease of implementation using numerical methods. This paper explores a novel approach to image trinarization with a concrete real-world application of classifying regions of sperm images used in the automatic analysis of sperm morphology. The proposed methodology engineers a diffusion equation with non-linear source term, exhibiting three steady-states. The model is implemented as an image processor using a standard finite difference method to illustrate the efficacy of the proposed approach. The performance of the proposed approach is benchmarked against standard image clustering/segmentation methods and shown to be highly effective.

2.1
Model

The model, given below, is engineered to exhibit three steady states, corresponding to background, sperm head and acrosomal region. Explicitly, the model is defined by

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=c_{D}\nabla u+c_{S}u(u-1)(u-a)(u-b),

(1)

\displaystyle u(x,y,0)

\displaystyle=\text{Image}(x,y).

The given model is constructed in two spatial dimensions, requiring that the input image be grayscale. Although, the extension to RGB images is straightforward under the PDE framework. The model is also subjected to zero-flux boundary conditions along all boundaries of the domain. This choice of boundary condition ensures smoothness at the boundaries and is motivated by previous works
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
. Explicitly, the boundary conditions are given by,

\displaystyle u_{x}(0,y,t)=0

\displaystyle u_{x}(1,y,t)=0

\displaystyle u_{y}(x,0,t)=0

\displaystyle u_{y}(x,1,t)=0

In engineering the dynamics of the present model, we require the model to exhibit three stable steady states. This is achieved by the careful construction of the source term. Figure
2
illustrate the phase space dynamics of the source term. This structure is an extension of the Fitzhugh-Nagumo model used in previous works
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
. The source term exhibits five steady states;
u=0,u=a,u=b,u=c
and
u=1
. Assuming that
0\leq a<b<c\leq 1
, the stable states exist at
u=0,u=1
and
u=b
as illustrated in Figure
2
.

Moreover, due to the nature of the input images, specifically high magnification with uniform illumination, there is likely minimal noise captured in the image which is associated with the camera sensor. The coefficients
c_{D}
and
c_{S}
are tunable to balance the bias of smoothing (denoising) versus trinarization.

2.2
Numerical Method

The standard finite-difference implementation offers a conditionally stable, explicit and parallelizable
[
6
]
method. Although the method is rudimentary its ease of implementation offers an attractive option for robust experimentation. The model is discretised as follows:

\displaystyle u(x_{i},y_{j},t_{n})

\displaystyle=u(i\Delta x,j\Delta y,n\Delta n)=u_{i,j}^{n},

(2)

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{i,j}^{n+1}-u_{i,j}^{n}}{\Delta t}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta t),

(3)

\displaystyle u_{x}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{i+1,j}^{n}-u_{i-1,j}^{n}}{2\Delta x}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^{2}),

(4)

\displaystyle u_{y}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{i,j+1}^{n}-u_{i,j-1}^{n}}{2\Delta y}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta y^{2}),

(5)

\displaystyle u_{xx}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{i+1,j}^{n}-2u_{i,j}^{n}+u_{i-1,j}^{n}}{\Delta x^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^{2}),

(6)

\displaystyle u_{yy}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{u_{i,j+1}^{n}-2u_{i,j}^{n}+u_{i,j-1}^{n}}{\Delta y^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\Delta y^{2}).

(7)

The computational domain is prescribed by the input image, but is artificially normalised to
(x,y)\in[0,1]\times[0,1]
. The image dimensions,
N
and
M
, thus dictate
\Delta x=1/(N-1)
and
\Delta y=1/(M-1)
. Discretising the model in (
1
) yields the explicit update scheme,

\displaystyle u_{i,j}^{n+1}=u_{i,j}^{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta x^{2}}\left(u_{i+1,j}^{n}-2u_{i,j}^{n}+u_{i-1,j}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta y^{2}}\left(u_{i,j+1}^{n}-2u_{i,j}^{n}+u_{i,j-1}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle-\Delta tc_{S}u_{i,j}^{n}(u_{i,j}^{n}-1)(u_{i,j}^{n}-a)(u_{i,j}^{n}-b)(u_{i,j}^{n}-c).

(8)

The resulting scheme is consistent with the model equation (
1
) as
\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta t\rightarrow 0
.

The boundary conditions are discretised using the central difference formulae (
4
) and (
5
) to ensure second order accuracy at the boundaries. Explicitly we have

\displaystyle u_{x}(0,y,t)

\displaystyle=0\Rightarrow u_{-1,j}^{n}=u_{1,j}^{n},

(9)

\displaystyle u_{x}(1,y,t)

\displaystyle=0\Rightarrow u_{N+1,j}^{n}=u_{N-1,j}^{n},

(10)

\displaystyle u_{y}(x,0,t)

\displaystyle=0\Rightarrow u_{j,-1}^{n}=u_{j,1}^{n},

(11)

\displaystyle u_{y}(x,1,t)

\displaystyle=0\Rightarrow u_{M+1,j}^{n}=u_{M-1,j}^{n}.

(12)

For simplicity, the scheme can be written in matrix form

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{n+1}

\displaystyle={\bf u}^{n+1}+\frac{cD\Delta t}{\Delta x^{2}}{\bf A}.{\bf u}^{n}+\frac{c_{D}\Delta t}{\Delta y^{2}}{\bf u}^{n}.{\bf A}^{T}

\displaystyle-c_{S}\Delta t{\bf u}^{n}\circ({\bf u}^{n}-1)\circ({\bf u}^{n}-a)\circ({\bf u}^{n}-b)\circ({\bf u}^{n}-c),

(13)

where
\circ
denotes the Hadamard product and

{\bf u}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}u_{0,0}^{n}&u_{0,1}^{n}&\ldots&u_{0,M-1}^{n}&u_{0,M}^{n}\\
u_{1,0}^{n}&u_{1,1}^{n}&&u_{1,M-1}^{n}&u_{1,M}^{n}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
u_{N-1,0}^{n}&u_{N-1,1}^{n}&&u_{N-1,M-1}^{n}&u_{N-1,M}^{n}\\
u_{N,0}^{n}&u_{N,1}^{n}&\ldots&u_{N,M-1}^{n}&u_{N,M}^{n}\\
\end{array}\right),

(14)

{\bf A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}-2&2&0&\ldots&0&0&0\\
1&-2&1&&0&0&0\\
0&1&-2&&0&0&0\\
\vdots&&&\ddots&&&\vdots\\
0&0&0&&-2&1&0\\
0&0&0&&1&-2&1\\
0&0&0&\ldots&0&2&-2\\
\end{array}\right).

(15)

2.3
Stability Analysis

To accommodate the nonlinearity in the stability analysis, the source term is replaced by the worst case contribution. This leads to stability conditions which are stricter than necessary in most cases. Nevertheless, the stability requirement does not overly impose on the functioning the method.

Consider the source term in (
1
),

f(u)=-u(u-1)(u-a)(u-b)(u-c).

(16)

This function is maximal (in absolute value) under constraints
0\leq u\leq 1
,
0\leq a\leq 1
and
0\leq b\leq 1
with

\max_{u,a,b,c}|f(u)|=\frac{256}{3125}.

(17)

The von Neumann stability analysis is conducted by introducing the ansatz

u_{i,j}^{n}=\zeta^{n}e^{Ii\Delta xj\Delta y\omega},

(18)

where
\omega
indicates an arbitrary Fourier wave number and
I=\sqrt{-1}
. Deriving the conditions on
\Delta x
,
\Delta y
and
\Delta t
that ensure the amplification factor,
\zeta
, is smaller than one in absolute value,
|\zeta|<1
, is sufficient to guarantee the numerical method is stable. Substituting the ansatz into equation (
8
) we have

\displaystyle\zeta=1

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta x^{2}}+\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta x^{2}}\frac{e^{I\Delta xj\Delta y\omega}+e^{-I\Delta xj\Delta y\omega}}{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta y^{2}}+\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta y^{2}}\frac{e^{Ii\Delta x\Delta y\omega}+e^{-Ii\Delta x\Delta y\omega}}{2}+\Delta tc_{S}f(u_{i,j}^{n}),

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle+\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta x^{2}}\left(\cos\left(2\frac{j\Delta x\Delta y\omega}{2}\right)-1\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta y^{2}}\left(\cos\left(2\frac{i\Delta x\Delta y\omega}{2}\right)-1\right)+\Delta tc_{S}f(u_{i,j}^{n}),

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta x^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(j\Delta x\Delta y\omega\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta y^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(i\Delta x\Delta y\omega\right)+\Delta tc_{S}f(u_{i,j}^{n}).

(19)

The stability condition
|\zeta|<1
leads to

\displaystyle\Delta tc_{S}|f(u_{i,j}^{n})|

\displaystyle\leq\Delta tc_{S}\frac{256}{3125},

\displaystyle<\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta x^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(j\Delta x\Delta y\omega\right)+\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta y^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(i\Delta x\Delta y\omega\right),

\displaystyle<2\Delta tc_{D}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta x^{2}}+\frac{1}{\Delta y^{2}}\right),

and

\displaystyle-2

\displaystyle<-\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta x^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(j\Delta x\Delta y\omega\right)-\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta y^{2}}\sin^{2}\left(i\Delta x\Delta y\omega\right)+\Delta tc_{S}f(u_{i,j}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta x^{2}}-\frac{2\Delta tc_{D}}{\Delta y^{2}}+\Delta tc_{S}f(u_{i,j}^{n})

\displaystyle 2\Delta tc_{D}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta x^{2}}+\frac{1}{\Delta y^{2}}-\frac{1}{\Delta tc_{D}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta tc_{S}|f(u_{i,j}^{n})|\leq\Delta tc_{S}\frac{256}{3125}.

(21)

The above criteria can be summarised as

|\Delta tc_{S}\frac{128}{3125}|\leq c_{D}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{2}}+\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y^{2}}\right).

(22)

Since
\Delta x
and
\Delta y
are prescribed by the input image’s dimensions,
\Delta t
should be chosen so that
\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{2}}
and
\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y^{2}}
are
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Then
c_{S}
and
c_{D}
can be chosen to balance the effect of trinarization and smoothing respectively, while still obeying equation (
22
), thus ensuring the resulting implementation is stable.

Performance Metrics

The quantitative performance of the assessed clustering methodologies requires a robust performance measure. The performance of each algorithm is broken down into two binary classification tasks; measuring the number of pixels classified as head and measuring the number of pixels classified as acrosome. The classification results are shown in Figures

A natural approach to measuring the accuracy of a binary classification task is to count the number of pixels classified as black which are also classified as black in the ground truth image, and scale that count by the total number of pixels in the image; in essence giving a percentage of “correctly classified" pixels. These results are presented in Table
2
for each image and each method. However, this is biased in the case where the number of target pixels is much less than the total number of pixels in the entire image. For example, if the binary ground truth image contained only one black pixel, then a pure white image would be considered almost perfectly accurate, but in reality the importance of capturing that one black pixel is the core task. The overestimation of a method’s performance is evident in the high accuracies obtained by all methods in Table

As such, many researchers adopt the F-measure, or
F_{1}

[

F_{1}=2\frac{\text{precision}\times\text{recall}}{\text{precision}+\text{recall}}=\frac{\text{tp}}{\text{tp}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\text{fp}+\text{fn}\right)}.

(23)

Conclusion

The present work extends the PDE based framework for image processing previously established for document image binarization
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
. The application of CASA raises the issue of image trinarization, whereby an image data is classified into three distinct classes. This classification task is performed dynamically by the carefully constructed PDE model, which is shown to exhibit three steady states (corresponding to the three distinct classes). This proposed model is benchmarked against several other general purpose clustering algorithms and shown to perform exceptionally well for a variety of input images and is robust, even with fixed parameter values.

The extensibility of the present image processing framework opens opportunities for novel processing algorithms, for new application domains. Specifically, the inclusion of local adaptivity

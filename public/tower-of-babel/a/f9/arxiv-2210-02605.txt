A question on the Cauchy problem in the Gevrey classes for weakly hyperbolic equations
Tatsuo Nishitani

Abstract
For a homogeneous polynomial p p in ξ ∈ ℝ n \xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n} with Gevrey coefficients, it is known that the Cauchy problem for any realization of p p is well-posed in the Gevrey class of order s < 2 s<2 if the characteristic roots are real. In this note, we give examples showing the situation of the converse direction, in particular the optimality of the Gevrey order s = 2 s=2 .

Introduction

Consider a polynomial in
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
of degree
m
with Gevrey coeffcietnts

P(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{m}=p(x,\xi)+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}P_{j}(x,\xi)

where
a_{\alpha}(x)
are in some Gevrey classes
\gamma^{(s)}(\Omega)
or
\gamma^{\langle{s}\rangle}(\Omega)
defined in a neighborhood of the origin of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
p(x,\xi)
,
P_{j}(x,\xi)
denotes the homogeneous part of degree
m
and
j<m
in
\xi
respectively. Gevrey class
\gamma^{(s)}(\Omega)
is the set of functions
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that for any compact set
K\Subset\Omega
there exist constants
C>0,A>0
for which the following inequalities hold:

(1.1)

|D^{\alpha}f(x)|\leq CA^{|\alpha|}(|\alpha|!)^{s},\;\;x\in K,\;\;\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}.

For a given
P(x,\xi)
we consider its several realizations (quantizations) as differential operators. We define
{\rm op}^{t}({P})
,
0\leq t\leq 1
by

({\rm op}^{t}({P})u)(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int e^{i(x-y)\xi}P((1-t)x+ty,\xi)u(y)dyd\xi.

Note that, assuming that
a_{\alpha}(x)
are constant outside some compact neighborhood of the origin for simplicity, we see

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}u(x)={\rm op}^{0}({P})u(x),\quad\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}D^{\alpha}\big(a_{\alpha}(x)u(x)\big)={\rm op}^{1}({P})u(x).

When
t=1/2
the quantization
{\rm op}^{1/2}({P})
is called Weyl quantization and also denoted by
{\rm op}^{w}({P})
. Note that

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-m}e^{-\lambda x\xi}{\rm op}^{t}({P})e^{\lambda x\xi}=p(x,\xi)

so that the
principal symbol
of
{\rm op}^{t}({P})
is independent of realization and given by the highest homogeneous part of
P(x,\xi)
.

Denote
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=(x_{1},x^{\prime})
and
consider the Cauchy problem

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm op}^{t}({P})u(x)=0,\quad(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\omega\cap\{x_{1}>\tau\},\\[8.0pt]
D_{1}^{j}u(0,x^{\prime})=u_{j}(x^{\prime}),\;\;j=0,\ldots,m-1,\;\;x^{\prime}\in\omega\cap\{x_{1}=\tau\}\end{array}\right.

where
\omega\subset\Omega
is some open neighborhood of the origin of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. The Cauchy problem (
1.2
) is (uniformly) well-posed in
\gamma^{(s)}
near the origin if there exist
\omega
and
\epsilon>0
such that for any
u_{j}\in\gamma^{(s)}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})
and for any
|\tau|<\epsilon
the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) has a unique solution
u\in C^{m}(\omega)
. We say that the Cauchy problem is locally solvable in
\gamma^{(s)}
at the origin if for any
u_{j}\in\gamma^{(s)}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1})
one can find a neighborhood
\omega
of the origin, which may depend on
\{u_{j}\}
, such that (
1.2
) with
\tau=0
has a solution
u\in C^{m}(\omega)
. We assume that the hyperplanes
x_{1}=\text{const.,}
are non-characteristic, that is

(1.3)

p(x,\theta)\neq 0,\;\;\theta=(1,0,\ldots,0),\;\;x\in\Omega,

which is almost necessary for

[

). Then without restrictions one may assume

. If the Cauchy problem (

\gamma^{(s)}
,
s>1

p(0,\xi_{1},\xi^{\prime})=0
has only real roots for any
\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
(
[
9

) so we assume without restrictions

(1.4)

p(x,\xi-i\theta)=0,\;\;\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},\;\;x\in\Omega.

The next results are implicit in

]

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
P(x,\xi)=p(x,\xi)+\sum_{j=0}^{r}P_{j}(x,\xi)

. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1

is well-posed in
\gamma^{(s)}
near the origin for
1<s<\min{\{m/r,2\}}

Corollary 1.1
.

Assume that the coefficients

belong to

. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
the Cauchy problem for
{\rm op}^{t}({p})
is well-posed in
\gamma^{(s)}
near the origin for

.

To confirm the results, note that under the assumption there is some
M>0
such that

(1.5)

P(x,\xi+i\tau\theta)\neq 0,\quad|\tau|\geq M(1+|\xi|)^{r/m},\quad x,\;\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

that is

is

). Then Theorem

for

, symbols of classical pseudodifferential operators. In particular, we have

On the other hand, from

The first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for the quantum queer superalgebra
Zhihua Chang, Yongjie Wang

Abstract
The classical invariant theory for the queer Lie superalgebra is an investigation of the U ⁡ ( 𝔮 n ) \mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{q}_{n}) -invariant sub-superalgebra of the symmetric superalgebra Sym ⁡ ( V ⊕ r ⊕ V ∗ ⊕ s ) \mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s}) for V = ℂ n | n V=\mathbb{C}^{n|n} . We establish the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for the quantum queer superalgebra U q ​ ( 𝔮 n ) \mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n}) . The key ingredient is a quantum analog 𝒪 r , s \mathcal{O}_{r,s} of the symmetric superalgebra Sym ⁡ ( V ⊕ r ⊕ V ∗ ⊕ s ) \mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s}) that is created as a braided tensor product of a quantization 𝖠 r , n \mathsf{A}_{r,n} of Sym ⁡ ( V ⊕ r ) \mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r}) and a quantization 𝖠 ¯ s , n \bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n} of Sym ⁡ ( V ∗ ⊕ s ) \mathrm{Sym}(V^{*\oplus s}) . Since the quantum queer superalgebra U q ​ ( 𝔮 n ) \mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n}) is not quasi-triangular, our braided tensor product is created via an explicit intertwining operator instead of the universal ℛ \mathcal{R} -matrix. MSC(2020): 17B37, 16T20, 20G42. Keywords: Quantum queer superalgebra; Howe duality; Invariant theory.

Introduction

Let
G
be a classical group and
M
a commutative algebra on which
G
acts as automorphisms. Then the
G
-invariant subspace

M^{G}=\big\{v\in M|gv=v,\ \ \forall g\in G\big\}

is a subalgebra of
M
. The first and second fundamental theorems of classical invariant theory describe generators and relations for the algebra
M^{G}
, we refer reader to
[
13
]
for details. The fundamental theorems of the invariant theory of a group is an indispensable part of its representation theory. They also play significant roles in other branches of mathematics and physics, including the construction of the Jones polynomial of knots in the topological quantum field theory.

The fundamental theorems of invariant theory are usually formulated in three equivalent ways: the linear
formulation on tensor modules
[
13
]
, the commutative algebraic formulation on symmetric algebras of multi-copies of the natural modules in
[
6
,
14
]
and the
Schur-Weyl duality formulation
[
24
]
. These formulations of the
fundamental theorems can be described in a unified way by using the category
of Brauer diagrams
[
8
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
25
]
.

We are particularly interested in the commutative algebraic formulation which deals with the invariants in the situation where
M=\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s})
for the natural
G
-module
V
and its dual
V^{*}
. A systematic method to demonstrate the fundamental theorems in this situation had been developed in
[
13
]
. As a super analog of classical invariant theory, Sergeev
[
23
]
explicitly described the invariants in the
\mathrm{U}(\mathfrak{g})
-supermodule
\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s})
for a finite-dimensional simple Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{gl}(V)
.

Mathematicians have also made remarkable progress on the fundamental theorems of invariant theory for quantum groups in recent decades. The first fundamental theorem for the quantum linear group
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
was established in
[
14
]
, in which a quantization of
\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s})
was given as a braided tensor product of a quantum coordinate algebra and a dual quantum coordinate algebra. The universal
\mathcal{R}
-matrix of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
played a crucial role in defining the braided tensor product. For the quantum general linear superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m|n})
, this problem was solved in
[
28
]
. The authors in
[
15
]
investigated the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for quantum ortho-symplectic supergroups from a new apporach by using the technique of ribbon tensor categories and Etingof-Kazhdan quantization
[
9
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to establish the first fundamental theorem (FFT) of invariant theory for the quantum queer superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
.
The quantum queer superalgebra was constructed in Olshanski’s letter
[
21
]
with the Faddeev-Reshetikhin-Takhtajan (FRT) formalism. based on which a celebrated Schur-Weyl duality, often referred to as Sergeev-Olshanski duality, had been established.
It has been further generalized to mixed tensor powers in
[
2
]
.The highest weight representation theory and their crystal basis theory for quantum queer superalgebra was investigated in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. The authors established the Howe duality for quantum queer superalgebra by introducing quantum coordinate superalgebra
[
4
]
, which is isomorphic to a braided symmetric superalgebra
[
3
]
. Also in
[
3
]
, they define a monoidal supercategory of quantum type
Q
webs and show it admits a full, essentially surjective
functor onto the monoidal supercategory of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules generated by the quantum symmetric powers of the natural module and their duals. Recently, Savage in
[
22
]
provided categorical tools
for studying the representation theory of the quantum queer (called isomeric) superalgebras by introducing the quantum queer supercategory and the quantum affine queer supercategory.

The queer Lie superalgebras form a family of “strange” Lie superalgebras since they do not possess non-degenerate invariant even bilinear forms. The strange phenomena is also observed in quantum setting. The quantum queer superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
is not a quasi-triangular Hopf superalgebra because of the absence of a universal
\mathcal{R}
-matrix. Thus the existence of an intertwining operator between two finite-dimensional
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules are not guaranteed except the polynomials modules. Recently, Savege has affirmed the existence of an intertwining operator between a finite-dimensional
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule and the natural module
V
. Another remarkable difference between the quantum qeer superalgebra and the quantum general linear superalgebra is that the tensor product of two nature modules is not irreducible as an
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule. Similar “strange” phenomena is also observed
[
1
]
in periplectic Lie superalgebras and their quantum enveloping superalgebras.

Our approach to obtain the first fundamental theorem for quantum queer superalgebras takes advantage of the Howe duality between the quantum queer superalgebras
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
and
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
. According to the Howe duality, there is an associative superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
(a quantization of
\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r})
) that admits a multiplicity free decomposition as a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules. A quantization of
\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus*s})
is also obtained in the same manner. These lead to a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
.

In order to define a braided multiplication on
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
such that it is compatible with the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-action, we need an intertwining operator

\Upsilon:\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}.

For those quantum superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
that is a quasi-triangular Hopf superalgebra (see
[
26
,
27
]
), such a intertwining operator is the composition of the permutation operator
P
and the canonical image of the universal
\mathcal{R}
-matrix. The universal
\mathcal{R}
-matrix plays important roles in the study of the fundamental theorems of invariant theory for a quasi-triangular quantum group (supergroups) as in
[
14
,
28
]
, as well as the proof of the Harish-Chandra theorem for these quantum superalgebras
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
of type
A
-
G
in
[
20
]
.

The main obstacle for the quantum queer superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
is the absence of a universial
\mathcal{R}
-matrix. Instead, we will explicit create an intertwining operator
\Upsilon
without using the universal
\mathcal{R}
-matrix. The definition of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
involves an intertwining operator
\check{S}=P\circ S:V\otimes V\rightarrow V\otimes V
on the tensor product of the natural
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-module, we will show that the intertwining operator
\check{S}
indeed determines an intertwining operator
\Upsilon:\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
. We point out that calculation with generating matrices instead of generators effectively simplifies the tedious verification on the well-definedness of
\Upsilon
and subsequent discussion. Most calculations in this paper will be conducted using generating matrices. It seems to be a neat tool for dealing with the quantum algebras with Faddeev-Reshetikhin-Takhtajan presentation.

Once the intertwining operator
\Upsilon:\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
is given, we obtain a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra

\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
that is a quantization of
\mathrm{Sym}\left(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s}\right)
. It is also equipped with the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-action commuting with the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-action. If
n\geqslant\max(r,s)
, we show that the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-invariant sub-superalgebra
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule that has the same multiplicity free decomposition as
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
. This motivates us to define a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}:\mathsf{A}_{r,s}\rightarrow\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
. Then the first fundamental theorem follows from a careful analysis on the behaviors of
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
when
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
and
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
act on
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
, as well as we multiply elements in
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
. If
n<\max(r,s)
, the problem is reduced to the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-invariant sub-superalgebra
\left(\mathcal{O}_{m,m}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
with
m=\min(r,s,n)
since
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
is generated by
\left(\mathcal{O}_{m,m}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
as a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule. The second fundamental theorem requires a meticulous discussion on the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule structure of
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
. We will treat it in a upcoming paper
[
5
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. We review some basic facts related to the Howe duality of quantum queer superalgebras in Section
2
. Then we explicitly construct the intertwining operator
\Upsilon
in Section
3
, that leads to a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-module superalgebraic structure on
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
. Section
4
serves for finding
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-invariant elements in
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
and calculating relations satisfied by these invariant elements. Then we establish the first fundamental theorem of the invariant theory for quantum queer superalgebra in Section
5
.

We always assume that the base field
\mathbb{C}(q)

\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[4]}R^{23}T_{+}^{2[5]}T_{+}^{3[5]}\right)=\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{3[5]}T_{+}^{2[5]}S^{23}\right).

(3.9)

The generators
\bar{t}_{\alpha b}
’s of
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
satisfy

\bar{T}^{1[4]}\bar{T}^{2[4]}S^{12}=S^{12}\bar{T}^{2[4]}\bar{T}^{1[4]}.

We similarly verify that

\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[4]}\bar{T}^{2[4]}S^{12}T_{+}^{3[5]}\right)=\Upsilon\left(S^{12}\bar{T}^{2[4]}\bar{T}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{3[5]}\right).

(3.10)

Moreover,
\bar{t}_{-\alpha,-b}=(-1)^{|\alpha|+|b|}\bar{t}_{\alpha,b}
for
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
and
b\in I_{n|n}
. It can be written in the matrix form
\bar{T}=(J_{n}\otimes 1)\bar{T}(J_{s}\otimes 1)
, where

J_{n}=\sum_{a\in I_{n|n}}(-1)^{|a|}E_{-a,a}.

(3.11)

Since
(J_{n}\otimes 1)S=S(J_{n}\otimes 1)
, we also verify that

\displaystyle(J_{n}\otimes 1\otimes 1\otimes 1)T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[4]}=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}(J_{n}\otimes 1\otimes 1\otimes 1)\bar{T}^{1[4]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[4]}(J_{s}\otimes 1\otimes 1\otimes 1).

which yields that

\Upsilon\left(\left(J_{n}\otimes 1\otimes 1\otimes 1\right)\bar{T}^{1[3]}\left(J_{s}\otimes 1\otimes 1\otimes 1\right)T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)=\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right).

(3.12)

Now, the well-definedness of
\Upsilon
follows from (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and (
3.12
).
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

The
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\Upsilon:\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
is a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules.

Proof.

For
u\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
and
f\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
, if
\Delta(u)=\sum\limits_{(u)}u_{(1)}\otimes u_{(2)}
, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(u)}\Upsilon\circ\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)(1\otimes f)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(u)}\Upsilon\left(\varepsilon(u_{(1)})\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}(f)\right)=\Upsilon\left(1\otimes\Phi_{u}(f)\right)=\Phi_{u}(f)\otimes 1,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(u)}\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)\circ\Upsilon(1\otimes f)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(u)}\Phi_{u_{(1)}}(f)\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}(1)=\sum\limits_{(u)}\Phi_{u_{(1)}}(f)\otimes\varepsilon(u_{(2)})=\Phi_{u}(f)\otimes 1,

which yields

\sum\limits_{(u)}\Upsilon\circ\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)(1\otimes f)=\sum\limits_{(u)}\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)\circ\Upsilon(1\otimes f)

for
f\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
. A similar calculation also shows

\sum\limits_{(u)}\Upsilon\circ\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)(\bar{g}\otimes 1)=\sum\limits_{(u)}\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)\circ\Upsilon(\bar{g}\otimes 1)

for
\bar{g}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
.

In order to show that

\sum_{(u)}\Upsilon\circ\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)(\bar{t}_{\alpha b}\otimes t_{ka})=\sum_{(u)}\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)\circ\Upsilon(\bar{t}_{\alpha b}\otimes t_{ka}),

for
a,b\in I_{n|n}
,
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
and
k=1,\ldots,r
, it suffices to consider the case where
u=L_{ij}
with
i\leqslant j
. Then the verification can be done using matrix computations as follows:

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(L\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)=

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(\left(L^{[3]5}\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}\bar{T}^{1[3]}\right)\left(L^{[4]5}\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(\left(S^{-1}\right)^{15}\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}S^{25}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(S^{-1}\right)^{15}T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[4]}S^{25}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{15}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}S^{25}\bar{T}^{1[4]}.

\displaystyle L\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)=

\displaystyle L\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}\left(T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[4]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(L^{[3]5}\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}T_{+}^{2[3]}\right)\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\left(L^{[4]5}\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}\bar{T}^{1[4]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{2[3]}S^{25}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{15}\bar{T}^{1[4]}.

Using the quantum Yang-Baxter equation for
S
again, we obtain

\Upsilon\left(L\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)=L\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right).

Now,
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
is generated by
1,t_{ka}
,
k=1,\ldots,r
and
a\in I_{n|n}
, while
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
is generated by
1,\bar{t}_{\alpha,b}
,
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
and
b\in I_{n|n}
. Using the commutative diagrams (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we inductively deduce that

\sum_{(u)}\Upsilon\circ\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)(\bar{g}\otimes f)=\sum_{(u)}\left(\Phi_{u_{(1)}}\otimes\Phi_{u_{(2)}}\right)\circ\Upsilon(f\otimes\bar{g}),

for all
f\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
\bar{g}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
. This shows
\Upsilon
is a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

The
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\Upsilon:\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
is a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodules under the action
\bar{\Psi}
.

Proof.

It suffices to verify

\Upsilon\circ\left(\bar{\Psi}_{u}\otimes 1\right)=\left(1\otimes\bar{\Psi}_{u}\right)\circ\Upsilon,\qquad u\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s}).

We first check it on generators.

\displaystyle\Upsilon\circ(\bar{\Psi}_{u}\otimes 1)(\bar{g}\otimes 1)=

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(\bar{\Psi}_{u}(\bar{g})\otimes 1\right)=1\otimes\bar{\Psi}_{u}(\bar{g})=(1\otimes\bar{\Psi}_{u})\circ\Upsilon(\bar{g}\otimes 1),

\displaystyle\Upsilon\circ(\bar{\Psi}_{u}\otimes 1)(1\otimes f)=

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(\varepsilon(u)\otimes f\right)=f\otimes\varepsilon(u)=(1\otimes\bar{\Psi}_{u})\circ\Upsilon(1\otimes f),

for
f\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
\bar{g}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
. We also have

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(L^{[3]5}\underset{\Psi}{\cdot}(\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]})\right)=

\displaystyle\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[3]}\tilde{S}^{13}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)=\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)\tilde{S}^{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[4]}\tilde{S}^{13},

\displaystyle L^{[4]5}\underset{\Psi}{\cdot}\Upsilon\left(\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)=

\displaystyle L^{[4]5}\underset{\Psi}{\cdot}\left(T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[4]}\right)=T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[4]}\tilde{S}^{13},

which implies

\Upsilon\circ(\bar{\Psi}_{u}\otimes 1)(\bar{t}_{\alpha b}\otimes t_{ka})=(1\otimes\bar{\Psi}_{u})\circ\Upsilon(\bar{t}_{\alpha b}\otimes t_{ka}),

for
k=1,\ldots,r
,
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
and
a,b\in I_{n|n}
. Using the commutative diagrams (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we deduce the following equation by induction

\Upsilon\circ(\bar{\Psi}_{u}\otimes 1)(\bar{g}\otimes f)=(1\otimes\bar{\Psi}_{u})\circ\Upsilon(\bar{g}\otimes f),

for
f\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
\bar{g}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We have shown in Propositions
3.4
and
3.5
that

is a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodules as well as
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})

is not a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
-supermodules under the action

) that

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\bar{t}_{\alpha b}\otimes t_{ia})=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a|(|\alpha|+|b|)}q^{-\varphi(b,a)}t_{ia}\otimes\bar{t}_{\alpha b}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|a||\alpha|}\xi\sum\limits_{p<a}t_{ip}\otimes\left(\delta_{ab}\bar{t}_{\alpha p}+(-1)^{|p|}\delta_{a,-b}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-p}\right),

for

,

and

, the action of

on

is given by

\Psi_{L_{ij}}(t_{ka})=(-1)^{|a|}\delta_{jk}t_{-i,-a},\quad k=1,\ldots,r,\quad a\in I_{n|n}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a|(|\alpha|+|b|)}\xi^{2}\delta_{jk}\sum\limits_{p\in I_{n|n}}t_{-i,-p}\otimes\left(\delta_{a,-b}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-p}+\delta_{ab}(-1)^{|p|}\bar{t}_{\alpha p}\right),

for

, which shows that

is not necessarily equal to
(\Psi_{L_{ij}}\otimes 1)\circ\Upsilon
.

Combining with Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.3
, we obtain a braided multiplication on

\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}

\displaystyle\bar{t}_{\alpha b}t_{ia}=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a|(|\alpha|+|b|)}q^{-\varphi(b,a)}t_{ia}\bar{t}_{\alpha b}

(3.13)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|a||\alpha|}\xi\sum\limits_{p<a}t_{ip}\left(\delta_{ab}\bar{t}_{\alpha p}+\delta_{a,-b}(-1)^{|p|}\bar{t}_{\alpha,-p}\right),

for
i=1,\ldots,r
,

and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
. It is also written in the following matrix form:

\bar{T}^{1[3^{\prime}]}T_{+}^{2[3]}=T_{+}^{2[3]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[3^{\prime}]}.

We obtain an associative superalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
, which could be viewed as the quantization of the symmetric superalgebra
\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s})

By Propositions
3.4

4
Invariants

In the previous section, we created the intertwining operator

\Upsilon:\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}

and thus obtained a braided tensor product superalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
, which is a quantization of the symmetric superalgebra
\mathrm{Sym}(V^{\oplus r}\oplus V^{*\oplus s})
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule superalgebra, the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-invariants

\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}=\left\{z\in\mathcal{O}_{r,s}|\ \Phi_{u}(z)=\varepsilon(u)z,\ \forall u\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})\right\}

is a sub-superalgebra of
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
. We aim to provide an explicit presentation of the invariant sub-superalgebra
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q})}
in terms of generators and relations. Such a description of generators and relations are usually called the first and second fundamental theorem (FFT and SFT) in the invariant theory of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
, respectively. We will prove the FFT in Section
5
and discuss the SFT in a separate paper. We find a few invariant elements and discuss the properties of the invariant sub-superalgebra
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
in this section.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
, the element

\displaystyle x_{i\alpha}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p\in I_{n|n}}t_{ip}\otimes\bar{t}_{\alpha p},

are contained in
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
.

Proof.

We set

X=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{\alpha\in I_{s|s}}E_{i\alpha}\otimes x_{i\alpha}.

(4.1)

Then
X^{1[2]}=T_{+}^{1[2]}\bar{T}^{1[2^{\prime}]}
. Since
\Delta(L^{[2]3})=L^{2[3]}\otimes L^{[2^{\prime}]3}
, the action of generating matrix
L
on
X
under
\Phi
is

\displaystyle L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}X^{1[2]}=

\displaystyle\left(L^{[2]3}\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}T_{+}^{1[2]}\right)\left(L^{[2^{\prime}]3}\underset{\Phi}{\cdot}\bar{T}^{1[2^{\prime}]}\right)=T_{+}^{1[2]}S^{13}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{13}\bar{T}^{1[2^{\prime}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{1[2]}\bar{T}^{1[2^{\prime}]}=X^{1[2]}.

Since the counit
\varepsilon
of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
satisfies
\varepsilon(L)=I
, we obtain
x_{i\alpha}\in\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

In the superalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
, the elements
x_{i\alpha}
,
t_{ja}
and
\bar{t}_{\alpha b}
’s satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle R^{12}X^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[3]}=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{2[3]}X^{1[3]},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bar{T}^{1[3^{\prime}]}X^{2[3]}S^{12}=

\displaystyle X^{2[3]}\bar{T}^{1[3^{\prime}]},

(4.3)

where
R
and
S
are given in (
3.4
) and (
2.1

Note that

3.2
and Theorem

R^{12}T_{+}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[3]}=T_{+}^{2[3]}T_{+}^{1[3]}S^{12},\text{ and }\bar{T}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{1[2]}=T_{+}^{1[2]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{12}\bar{T}^{1[3]}

∎

In the superalgebra

Theorem 4.4
.

The invariant sub-superalgebra
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule under the action
\Psi\otimes\bar{\Psi}
. Moreover, it admits a multiplicity free decomposition

\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathrm{SP}\atop\ell(\lambda)\leqslant\min(r,s,n)}\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda)\right).

(4.5)

Proof.

Since the superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
(resp.
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
) is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule (resp. a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule) and has the multiplicity free decomposition (
2.9
) (resp. (
2.13
)), the superalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
is decomposed into the direct sum

\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\bigoplus_{\lambda,\mu\in\mathrm{SP}\atop\ell(\lambda)\leqslant\min(r,n),\ell(\mu)\leqslant\min(s,n)}\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}(\lambda)\right)\otimes\left(\mathsf{L}_{s}(\mu)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}^{*}(\mu)\right),

as a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule.

We denote

\delta(\lambda)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\ell(\lambda)\text{ is even},\\
1,&\text{if }\ell(\lambda)\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Then Schur’s Lemma for queer superalgebra states that

\dim\left(\mathsf{L}_{n}(\lambda)\otimes\mathsf{L}_{n}^{*}(\mu)\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}2^{\delta(\lambda)},

which implies that

\left(\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\otimes\mathsf{L}_{n}(\lambda)\right)\otimes\left(\mathsf{L}_{s}(\mu)\otimes\mathsf{L}_{n}^{*}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}\cong\delta_{\lambda,\mu}2^{\delta(\lambda)}\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\otimes\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda).

Recall from
[
12
]
that
\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\otimes\mathsf{L}_{n}(\lambda)
(resp.
\mathsf{L}_{s}(\mu)\otimes\mathsf{L}_{n}^{*}(\mu)
) is the direct sum of
2^{\delta(\lambda)}
(resp.
2^{\delta(\mu)}
) copies of
\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}(\lambda)
(resp.
\mathsf{L}_{s}(\mu)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}^{*}(\mu)
).
Hence,

2^{\delta(\lambda)+\delta(\mu)}\left(\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}(\lambda)\right)\otimes\left(\mathsf{L}_{s}(\mu)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}^{*}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}\cong\delta_{\lambda,\mu}2^{\delta(\lambda)+\delta(\lambda)}\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda),

which yields

\left(\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}(\lambda)\right)\otimes\left(\mathsf{L}_{s}(\mu)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}^{*}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}\cong\delta_{\lambda,\mu}\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda)

Hence,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathrm{SP}\atop\ell(\lambda)\leqslant\min(r,n),\ell(\mu)\leqslant\min(s,n)}\left(\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}(\lambda)\right)\otimes\left(\mathsf{L}_{s}(\mu)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{n}^{*}(\mu)\right)\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathrm{SP}\atop\ell(\lambda)\leqslant\min(r,s,n)}\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda)\right).

This proves the decomposition (
4.5
).
∎

5
The First Fundamental Theorem

This section is devoted to demonstrating the first fundamental theorem in the invariant theory of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
that describes the generators of the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-invariant subalgebra
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
.

We first consider the case where
n\geqslant\max(r,s)
. In this situation,
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
(resp.
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
) can be regarded as a sub-superalgebra of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
generated by
L_{ij}
with
i,j\in I_{r|r}
(resp.
i,j\in I_{s|s}
). As shown in
[
4
]
, the sub-superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
of
\mathsf{A}_{n}
generated by
1,t_{i\alpha}
with
i\in I_{r|r}
and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
and admits a multiplicity free decomposition same as the decomposition in (
4.5
). This suggest us to connect
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
with
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
.

Following the strategy of
[
14
]
, the superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{n}
is also a bi-superalgebra with comultiplication

\Delta^{\circ}(t_{i\alpha})=\sum_{b\in I_{n|n}}(-1)^{(|i|+|b|)(|b|+|\alpha|)}t_{ib}\otimes t_{b\alpha},\qquad i,\alpha\in I_{n|n},

and
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(\mathsf{A}_{n})=\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{n}
. Hence, we have a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map

\tilde{\Delta}=(1\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\circ})\circ\Delta^{\circ}:\quad\mathsf{A}_{n}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{n}.

Provided that
n\geqslant\max(r,s)
, the
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\tilde{\Delta}
maps the sub-superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
into the subspace
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}=\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
.
We denote the restriction of
\tilde{\Delta}
on
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
by

\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}:\quad\mathsf{A}_{r,s}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{r,s}.

(5.1)

The same arguments as
[
14
, Lemma 6.11]
shows that

Lemma 5.1
.

If
n\geqslant\max(r,s)
, then the
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is injective,

\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(t_{i\alpha})=x_{i\alpha},

(5.2)

for
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
, and
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(\mathsf{A}_{r,s})\subseteq\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
n\geqslant\max(r,s)
. If
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
and
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
are regarded as
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
-supermodules under the action
\Psi
and
\Psi\otimes 1
, respectively, then the
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
-supermodules.

Proof.

For
f\in\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
and
u\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
,

\Psi_{u}(f)=\sum\limits_{(f)}(-1)^{|f_{(1)}||u|}\langle f_{(1)},\mathcal{S}(u)\rangle f_{(2)}.

Then we deduce that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\circ\Psi_{u}(f)=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(f)}(-1)^{|f_{(1)}||u|}\langle f_{(1)},\mathcal{S}(u)\rangle\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(f_{(2)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(f)}(-1)^{|f_{(1)}||u|}\langle f_{(1)},\mathcal{S}(u)\rangle f_{(2)}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(3)}),

\displaystyle(\Psi_{u}\otimes 1)\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(f)=

\displaystyle\sum_{(f)}\Psi_{u}(f_{(1)})\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(2)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(f)}(-1)^{|f_{(1)}||u|}\langle f_{(1)},\mathcal{S}(u)\rangle f_{(2)}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(3)}).

It shows
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\circ\Psi_{u}(f)=(\Psi_{u}\otimes 1)\circ\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(f)
, and hence
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})
-supermodules.
∎

Note that the
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is not a homomorphism of superalgebras. However, we will show that the superalgebra and
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule structures on
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
and
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
are closely related to each other via the
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
.

For the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule structure, we consider the automorphism
\sigma=\mathcal{S}^{2}
of the Hopf superalgebra
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the antipode of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
. The action of
\sigma
on the generators
L_{ij}
are given by

\sigma(L)=(1\otimes D^{-1})L(1\otimes D),

where is the diagonal matrix
D=\sum\limits_{\alpha\in I_{s|s}}q^{2(-1)^{|\alpha|}\alpha}E_{\alpha\alpha}
.

Given an arbitrary
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule
M
, there is another
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-super- module
M^{\sigma}
obtained by twisting the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-action with
\sigma
. Namely,
M^{\sigma}
has the same underlying vector space with
M
, and the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-action

u\underset{\sigma}{\cdot}x=\sigma(u)\cdot x,\qquad u\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s}),\ x\in M.

In particular,
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
is equipped with a new structure of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule
A_{r,s}^{\sigma}
. We denote the new
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-action by
\tilde{\Phi}
, i.e.,
\tilde{\Phi}_{u}=\Phi_{\sigma(u)}
for
u\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that
n\geqslant\max(r,s)
. If
\mathrm{A}_{r,s}
and
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
are regarded as
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodules under the action
\tilde{\Phi}
and
\bar{\Psi}
respectively, then the
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodules.

Proof.

We need to check
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\circ\tilde{\Phi}_{u}=(1\otimes\bar{\Psi}_{u})\circ\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
.

Using the pairing
\langle\mathsf{A}_{n},\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})\rangle
, the action
\tilde{\Phi}
of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
on
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
and the action
\bar{\Psi}
of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
on
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
can be written as

\tilde{\Phi}_{u}(f)=\sum\limits_{(f)}(-1)^{|u||f|}f_{(1)}\langle f_{(2)},\sigma(u)\rangle,\quad\bar{\Psi}_{u}(\bar{g})=\sum\limits_{(\bar{g})}(-1)^{|\bar{g}_{(1)}||u|}\langle\bar{g}_{(1)},\mathcal{S}(u)\rangle\bar{g}_{(2)},

for
u\in\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
,
f\in\mathsf{A}_{r,n}
and
\bar{g}\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
. Since

\Delta^{\circ}\left(\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f)\right)=\sum_{(f)}(-1)^{|f_{(1)}||f_{(2)}|}\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(2)})\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(1)}),

we have

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}_{u}(\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f))=

\displaystyle\sum_{(f)}(-1)^{|f_{(1)}||f_{(2)}|+|f_{(2)}||u|}\langle\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(2)}),\mathcal{S}(u)\rangle\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(f)}(-1)^{|f_{(1)}||f_{(2)}|+|f_{(2)}||u|}\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(1)})\langle f_{(2)},\mathcal{S}^{2}(u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(f)}(-1)^{|f||u|}\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(1)})\langle f_{(2)},\sigma(u)\rangle.

Then we compute that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\circ\tilde{\Phi}_{u}(f)=

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\left(\sum\limits_{(f)}(-1)^{|u||f|}f_{(1)}\langle f_{(2)},\sigma(u)\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(f)}(-1)^{|u||f|}f_{(1)}\otimes\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(2)})\langle f_{(3)},\sigma(u)\rangle,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(f)}(-1)^{|u||f_{(1)}|}f_{(1)}\otimes\Psi_{u}\left(\mathcal{S}^{\circ}(f_{(2)})\right)=(1\otimes\bar{\Psi}_{u})\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(f),

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

The same results also holds in the cases of the general linear group
[
14

and the general linear supergroup
[
28

.

Next, we consider the behavior of
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
when taking product in
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}

\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(fg)
is not necessarily equal to

R

Proposition 5.5

There exists a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map

\Omega:\mathsf{A}_{r,s}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,s}\rightarrow\mathsf{A}_{r,s}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,s}

(5.3)

such that

\Omega(f\otimes 1)=f\otimes 1,\quad\Omega(1\otimes f)=1\otimes f,\quad\Omega\left(T_{+}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}\right)=T_{+}^{1[3]}T_{+}^{2[4]}S^{12},

(5.4)

for

\Omega\circ(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)=(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\Omega^{[1][3]}\Omega^{[2][3]},\quad\Omega\circ(1\otimes\mathrm{mul})=(1\otimes\mathrm{mul})\Omega^{[1][3]}\Omega^{[1][2]}.

(5.5)

Proof.

Recall from Lemma
3.2
that
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
is generated by
1
and
t_{i,\alpha}
with
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}

5.4

\Omega(f\otimes g)
when

are those generators of
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}

\Omega

\mathsf{A}_{r,s}\otimes\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
according to (
5.5
). It suffices to show
\Omega

Thus, we need to check the following two equations

\displaystyle\Omega\left(R^{12}T_{+}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{3[5]}\right)=\Omega\left(T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{1[4]}S^{12}T_{+}^{3[5]}\right),

(5.6)

and

\displaystyle\Omega\left(T_{+}^{1[4]}R^{23}T_{+}^{2[5]}T_{+}^{3[5]}\right)=\Omega\left(T_{+}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{3[5]}T_{+}^{2[5]}S^{23}\right).

(5.7)

By (

\displaystyle\Omega\left(R^{12}T_{+}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{3[5]}\right)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{12}(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\Omega^{[4][6]}\Omega^{[5][6]}\left(T_{+}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{2[5]}T_{+}^{3[6]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{12}(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\Omega^{[4][6]}\left(T_{+}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{2[5]}T_{+}^{3[6]}S^{23}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{12}(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\left(T_{+}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{2[5]}T_{+}^{3[6]}S^{13}S^{23}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{12}T_{+}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{3[5]}S^{13}S^{23}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{1[4]}S^{12}T_{+}^{3[5]}S^{13}S^{23}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{1[4]}T_{+}^{3[5]}S^{12}S^{13}S^{23}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Omega\left(T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{1[4]}S^{12}T_{+}^{3[5]}\right)=

\displaystyle\Omega\circ(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\left(T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{1[5]}T_{+}^{3[6]}\right)S^{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\Omega^{[4][6]}\Omega^{[5][6]}\left(T_{+}^{2[4]}T_{+}^{1[5]}T_{+}^{3[6]}\right)S^{12}

The equality (

S
satisfies quantum Yang-Baxter equation. Similarly, one can prove (

∎

Lemma 5.7
.

For
1\leqslant p<k
, we have

\displaystyle\Omega\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p,[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[b]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p,[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[b]}S^{(p,k)}S^{(p,k-1)}\cdots S^{(p,p+1)},

where
S^{(p,j)}=S^{1j}S^{2j}\cdots S^{pj}
for
j=p+1,\ldots,k
.

Proof.

The case where
p=1
and
k=2
is given by (
5.4
), we prove the statement by induction.

Firstly, we consider the case where
k=p+1
. By (
5.5
), we have

\displaystyle\Omega\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p-1,[a]}T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega\circ(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p-1,[a]}T_{+}^{p[b]}T_{+}^{p+1,[c]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\Omega^{[a][c]}\Omega^{[b][c]}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p-1,[a]}T_{+}^{p[b]}T_{+}^{p+1,[c]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\Omega^{[a][c]}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p-1,[a]}T_{+}^{p[b]}T_{+}^{p+1,[c]}\right)S^{p,p+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{mul}\otimes 1)\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p-1,[a]}T_{+}^{p[b]}T_{+}^{p+1,[c]}\right)S^{(p-1,p+1)}S^{p,p+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p-1,[a]}T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}S^{(p,p+1)}.

For
k\geqslant p+2
, we deduce by the second equality in (
5.5
) that

\displaystyle\Omega\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k-1,[b]}T_{+}^{k[b]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega(1\otimes\mathrm{mul})\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k-1,[b]}T_{+}^{k[c]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1\otimes\mathrm{mul})\Omega^{[a][c]}\Omega^{[a][b]}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k-1,[b]}T_{+}^{k[c]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1\otimes\mathrm{mul})\Omega^{[a][c]}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k-1,[b]}T_{+}^{k[c]}\right)S^{(p,k-1)}\cdots S^{(p,p+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1\otimes\mathrm{mul})\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k-1,[b]}T_{+}^{k[c]}\right)S^{(p,k)}S^{(p,k-1)}\cdots S^{(p,p+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k-1,[b]}T_{+}^{k[b]}S^{(p,k)}S^{(p,k-1)}\cdots S^{(p,p+1)}.

It completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

The
\mathbb{C}(q)
-linear map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}:\ \mathsf{A}_{r,s}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
satisfies

\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(fg)=\mathrm{mul}\circ\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\right)\circ\Omega(f\otimes g).

(5.8)

Proof.

We first observe that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}(f)=\mathrm{mul}\circ\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\right)\circ\Omega(f\otimes 1)=\mathrm{mul}\circ\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\right)\circ\Omega(1\otimes f),

which shows (
5.8
) holds when
f=1
or
g=1
.

The definition of
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
implies that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[a]}\right)=

\displaystyle T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[a]}\bar{T}^{k[b]}\cdots\bar{T}^{2[b]}\bar{T}^{1[b]}.

On the other hand, Lemma
5.7
yields that

\displaystyle\mathrm{mul}\circ\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\right)\circ\Omega\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[a^{\prime}]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[a^{\prime}]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{mul}\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}\right)\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\left(T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[b]}\right)\right)S^{(p,k)}S^{(p,k-1)}\cdots S^{(p,p+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{mul}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}\bar{T}^{p[a^{\prime}]}\cdots\bar{T}^{1[a^{\prime}]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[b]}\bar{T}^{k[b^{\prime}]}\cdots\bar{T}^{p+1[b^{\prime}]}\right)

\displaystyle\cdot S^{(p,k)}S^{(p,k-1)}\cdots S^{(p,p+1)}.

Since the multiplication in
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
satisfies

\mathrm{mul}(\bar{T}^{i[a^{\prime}]}T_{+}^{j[b]})=T_{+}^{j[a]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{ij}\bar{T}^{i[a^{\prime}]},\quad i<j,

and

\left(S^{-1}\right)^{ij}\bar{T}^{i[a^{\prime}]}\bar{T}^{j[b^{\prime}]}=\bar{T}^{j[b^{\prime}]}\bar{T}^{j[b^{\prime}]}\left(S^{-1}\right)^{ij},\quad i<j,

we deduce that

\mathrm{mul}\circ\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\right)\circ\Omega\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{p[a]}T_{+}^{p+1,[b]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[b]}\right)=\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[a]}\right).

This implies that (
5.8
) holds when
f
and
g
are monomials in
t_{i\alpha}
’s. Now,
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
is spanned by all monomials in
t_{i\alpha}
’s, thus (
5.8
) holds for all
f,g\in\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Using (
5.8
), we can explicitly formulate the images of all monomials
t_{i_{1},\alpha_{1}}\cdots t_{i_{k},\alpha_{k}}\in\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
under the map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
.

\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}\left(T_{+}^{1[a]}T_{+}^{2[a]}\cdots T_{+}^{k[a]}\right)=X^{1[a]}X^{2[a]}\cdots X^{k[a]}S^{(1,2)}S^{(1,3)}\cdots S^{(1,k)}.

(5.9)

Now, we state our first main result, which is the quantum analogue of the first fundamental theorem of invariant theory for the quantum queer superalgebra.

Theorem 5.10

(FFT for
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
)
.

The invariant sub-superalgebra
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
is generated by elements
x_{i\alpha}
with
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem splits into the following two cases:
n\geqslant\text{max}(r,s)
or otherwise.

Case 1:
n\geqslant\max(r,s)
.

In this situation, we have shown in Lemma
5.1
that
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}:\mathsf{A}_{r,s}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
is an injective linear map. Moreover, if
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
(resp.
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
) is regarded as the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule via the action
\Psi\otimes\tilde{\Phi}
(resp.
\Psi\otimes\bar{\Psi}
), the linear map
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is also a homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodules.

As a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule under the action
\Psi\otimes\Phi
, the superalgebra
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
admits the multiplicity free decomposition

\mathsf{A}_{r,s}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathrm{SP}\atop\ell(\lambda)\leqslant\min(r,s)}\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda)\right).

(5.10)

The action
\tilde{\Phi}
of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
on
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
is obtained by twisting
\Phi
via the automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
. If we twist both-sides of the decomposition by the automorphism
\sigma
, we obtain the same decomposition where
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
is the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule under the action
\Psi\otimes\tilde{\Phi}
.

On the other hand, Theorem
4.4
implies that the invariant sub-superalgebra
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
also has the decomposition

\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathrm{SP}\atop\ell(\lambda)\leqslant\min(r,s)}\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda)\right)

(5.11)

provided that
n\geqslant\max(r,s)
.

Now,
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is an injective homomorphism of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodules, where
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
and
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
are under the
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-action
\tilde{\Phi}
and
\bar{\Psi}
, respectively. The decomposition (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) shows that every irreducible highest weight
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule with highest weight
\lambda
has the same multiplicities in both
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
and
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
. Hence,
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is surjective.

Although
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
is not a homomorphism of superalgebras, Proposition
5.8
shows that
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}
sends a product in
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
to a linear combinations of products in
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
. Since
\mathsf{A}_{r,s}
is generated by
1
and
t_{i\alpha}
with
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
and
\tilde{\Delta}_{r,s}(t_{i\alpha})=x_{i\alpha}
, we conclude that
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
is generated by
x_{i\alpha}
with
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
.

Case 2:
n<\max(r,s)
.
Let
m=\min(n,r,s)
. We follow the strategy of
[
14
, Theorem 6.10]
that reduces
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}
to a small superalgebra where the result of Case 1 is applied.

For
m\leqslant m^{\prime}
, the sub-superalgebra of
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{m^{\prime}})
generated by
L_{ij}
with
i\leqslant j
and
i,j\in I_{m|m}\subseteq I_{m^{\prime}|m^{\prime}}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{m})
. Following the standard technique in
[
14
]
and
[
4
]
, for each
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{m^{\prime}})
-supermodule
\mathsf{M}
, the subspace

\mathsf{M}^{[m]}:=\left\{v\in\mathsf{M}|L_{ii}.v=v,m<i\leqslant m^{\prime}\right\}

is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{m})
-supermodule.

Note that the superalgebra
\mathcal{O}_{r,s}=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}
is a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-supermodule, we consider the appropriate
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{m})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{m})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})
-sub- supermodule

\mathcal{O}_{r,s}(m)=\mathsf{A}_{r,n}^{[m]}\otimes\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}^{[m]},

where

\mathsf{A}_{r,n}^{[m]}:=\left\{f\in\mathsf{A}_{r,s}|\Psi_{L_{ii}}(f)=f,m<i\leqslant r\right\}

and

\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}^{[m]}:=\left\{f\in\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{r,s}|\Psi_{L_{ii}}(f)=f,m<i\leqslant s\right\}.

It is shown in
[
4
]
that
\mathsf{A}_{r,n}^{[m]}
(resp.
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{s,n}^{[m]}
) is isomorphic
\mathsf{A}_{m,m}
(resp.
\bar{\mathsf{A}}_{m,m}
). Hence,

\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}(m)\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}=\left(\mathcal{O}_{m,m}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}

that is generated by
x_{i\alpha}
with
i=1,\ldots,m
and
\alpha\in I_{m|m}
as shown in Case 1.

By Theorem
4.4
, the invariant sub-superalgebra has the decomposition

\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}=\bigoplus_{\ell(\lambda)\leqslant m}\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda)\right)

(5.12)

as a
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})
-supermodule under the action
\Psi\otimes\bar{\Psi}
. It follows from
[
4
, Lemma 4.3]
that the irreducible highest weight
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{m^{\prime}})
-supermodule
\mathsf{L}_{m^{\prime}}(\lambda)
gives the irreducible highest weight
\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{m})
-supermodule
\mathsf{L}_{m^{\prime}}^{[m]}(\lambda)\cong\mathsf{L}_{m}(\lambda)
if
\ell(\lambda)\leqslant m
, and a highest weight vector in
\mathsf{L}_{m^{\prime}}(\lambda)
is also a highest weight vector in
\mathsf{L}^{[m]}_{m^{\prime}}(\lambda)
. Hence,

\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r}).\mathsf{L}_{m}^{*}(\lambda)=\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\ \text{ and }\ \mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s}).\mathsf{L}_{m}(\lambda)=\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda).

It follows that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathrm{SP}\atop\ell(\lambda)\leqslant m}\left(\mathsf{L}_{r}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{s}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})\right).\left(\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathrm{SP}\atop\ell(\lambda)\leqslant m}\mathsf{L}_{m}^{*}(\lambda)\circledast\mathsf{L}_{m}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})\right).\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}(m)\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})},

which shows
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
is generated by
\left(\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\otimes\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})\right).x_{i\alpha}
with
i=1,\ldots,m
and
\alpha\in I_{m|m}
.
Therefore,
\left(\mathcal{O}_{r,s}\right)^{\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{n})}
is generated by
x_{i\alpha}
with
i=1,\ldots,r
and
\alpha\in I_{s|s}
since

\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{r})\underset{\Psi}{\cdot}x_{i\alpha}=\mathrm{span}\left\{x_{j\alpha},\ x_{j,-\alpha}|\ j=1,\ldots,r\right\},

and

\mathrm{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{q}_{s})\underset{\Psi}{\cdot}x_{i\alpha}=\mathrm{span}\left\{x_{i\beta}|\ \beta\in I_{s|s}\right\}.

This completes the proof in the case
n<\max(r,s)
.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

Sergeev investigated the invariant theory for classical Lie superalgebra. Explicitly, there are two families inavriants for queer Lie superalgebra which corresponding to
x_{ia}
for
i=1,\ldots,r,a=1,\ldots s
and
x_{ia}
for
i=1,\ldots,r,a=-1,\ldots-s
, see
[
23
, Theorem 1.5.1]
.

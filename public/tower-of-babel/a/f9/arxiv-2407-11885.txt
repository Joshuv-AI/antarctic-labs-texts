On collectively almost (limitedly, order) L-weakly compact sets of operators
Safak Alpay, Eduard Emelyanov, Svetlana Gorokhova

Abstract
We prove collective versions of semi-duality theorems for sets of almost (limitedly, and order) L-weakly compact operators.



Keywords:
L-weakly compact set, Banach lattice, domination problem

MSC 2020:
46B50, 47B01, 47B07, 47B65

Introduction and Preliminaries

The theory of L-weakly compact operators goes back to P. Meyer-Nieberg.
The most powerful result of the Meyer-Nieberg theory is the duality
theorem
[
13
, Satz 3]
. This theory was contributed recently in several papers
(see, for example
[
2
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
15
]
).
The aim of the present paper is to prove collective versions of the semi-duality
theorems
[
7
, Thm. 2.5]
,
[
2
, Thm. 4]
, and
[
12
, Thm. 2.3]

for almost (limitedly, order) L-weakly compact operators.
Note that the considerably easier duality theorem for collectively L-weakly compact sets
of operators was proved in
[
9
, Thm. 2]
. We also investigate
the domination problem for collectively almost (limitedly, and order)
L-weakly compact sets of operators.

Throughout the paper, vector spaces are real; operators are linear and bounded;
letters

and

stand for Banach spaces;
E
and

for Banach lattices;

denotes the closed unit ball of
X

the identity operator on

;

the order continuous part of

;

\text{\rm L}(X,Y)
(resp.,
\text{\rm K}(X,Y)
,
\text{\rm W}(X,Y)
)
the space of bounded (resp., compact, weakly compact) operators from
X
to
Y
.
A subset
A
of
F
is L-weakly compact
[
13
, pp. 146–147]

(briefly,

sequence in the solid hull
\text{\rm sol}(A)=\bigcup\limits_{a\in A}[-|a|,|a|]

of

T\in\text{\rm L}(X,F)
is

)
if
T(B_{X})
is an
Lwc

We shall use the following lemma (see
[

, Lm. 1]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
A
be a nonempty bounded subset of
F^{\prime}
. The following are equivalent.

i)

is an
Lwc

F^{\prime}
.

ii)

Each disjoint sequence in

for every
w
∗

]

2
Collectively qualified L-weakly compact sets of operators

We begin with the following definition of collectively qualified L-weakly compact
sets of operators.

Definition 1
.

A bounded subset
{\cal T}
of
\text{\rm L}(X,F)
is:

a)

collectively L-weakly compact (briefly,
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf Lwc}(X,F)
)
if the set
{\cal T}A=\bigcup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}T(A)

is
Lwc
for each bounded
A\subseteq X
.

b)

collectively almost L-weakly compact (briefly,
a-Lwc
(X,F))
if
{\cal T}A
is
Lwc
for each relatively weakly compact
A\subseteq X
.

c)

collectively limitedly L-weakly compact (briefly,
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(X,F)
)
if
{\cal T}A
is
Lwc
for each limited
A\subseteq X
.

A bounded subset
{\cal T}
of
\text{\rm L}(E,F)
is:

d)

collectively order L-weakly compact (briefly,
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)
)
if the set
{\cal T}A
is
Lwc
for each order bounded
A\subseteq E
.

The next two elementary propositions concern the topological
properties of the sets of operators introduced in Definition
1
.
The strong closure
\overline{{\cal T}}^{s}
of
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf Lwc}(X,F)

also satisfies
\overline{{\cal T}}^{s}\in\text{\bf Lwc}(X,F)

[
9
, Prop. 1]
.

Proposition 1
.

The following holds.

i)

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(X,F)\Longrightarrow\overline{{\cal T}}^{s}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(X,F)
.

ii)

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(X,F)\Longrightarrow\overline{{\cal T}}^{s}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(X,F)
.

iii)

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)\Longrightarrow\overline{{\cal T}}^{s}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)
.

Proof.

We give a proof of
i)
only. Other two cases are similar.
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(X,F)
and
A
be a weakly compact subset of
X
.
Since
{\cal T}A
is an
Lwc
subset of
F
then
the norm closure
\overline{{\cal T}A}
of
{\cal T}A
is likewise
Lwc

(see, for example,
[
9
, Lm. 3]
). The rest of the proof follows from

\overline{{\cal T}}^{s}A\subseteq\overline{{\cal T}A}
.
∎

Similarly to
[
9
, Thm. 1]
, we have the following result.

Proposition 2
.

Every totally bounded set of almost (limitedly, order) L-weakly compact operators
is collectively almost (limitedly, order) L-weakly compact.

Proof.

As all three cases are analogues, we include only the proof for
a-Lwc
operators.
Let
{\cal T}
be totally bounded subset of
\text{\rm a-Lwc}(X,F)
and let
A
be a
weakly compact subset of
X
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
pick a finite subset

{\cal T}_{k}
of
{\cal T}
such that, for every
T\in{\cal T}
there exists
S\in{\cal T}_{k}

with
\|T-S\|\leq\frac{1}{2k}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
the set
{\cal T}_{k}A
is
Lwc

by
[
14
, Cor. 3.6.4]
. So,
{\cal T}_{k}A\subseteq[-x_{k},x_{k}]+\frac{1}{2k}B_{F}

for some
x_{k}\in F^{a}_{+}
by
[
14
, Prop. 3.6.2]
, and hence

{\cal T}A\subseteq[-x_{k},x_{k}]+\frac{1}{k}B_{F}
. One more application
of
[
14
, Prop. 3.6.2]
gives that
{\cal T}A
is an
Lwc
set.
It follows
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(X,F)
.
∎

2.1

The next result strengthens
[
2
, Thm. 2]
. Indeed,

[
2
, Thm. 2]
follows from Theorem
1
by taking
{\cal T}=\{T\}
and
applying the standard fact that each
Lwc
subset of
F
lies in
F^{a}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\cal T}
be a bounded subset of
\text{\rm L}(X,F)
. The following are equivalent.

i)

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(X,F)
.

ii)

The set
{\cal T}x=\{Tx:T\in{\cal T}\}

is
Lwc
for each
x\in X
.

iii)

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx)|\to 0
for every
x\in X
and
every disjoint
(f_{n})
in
B_{F^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

i)\ \Longrightarrow\ ii)
It is trivial.

ii)\ \Longrightarrow\ iii)
Let
x\in X
and let
(f_{n})
be a disjoint sequence in
B_{F^{\prime}}
.
By the assumption, every disjoint sequence in the bounded set

\text{\rm sol}({\cal T}x)
is norm-null, and hence is uniformly
null on
B_{F^{\prime}}
. By the Burkinshaw–Dodds theorem (see, for example,
[
1
, Thm. 5.63]
),

f_{n}\rightrightarrows 0({\cal T}x)
. Therefore,

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx)|\to 0
.

iii)\ \Longrightarrow\ i)
Suppose in contrary
{\cal T}A
is not
Lwc
for some limited
A\subseteq X
.
By using
[
1
, Thm. 5.63]
, take a disjoint sequence
(f_{n})
in
B_{F^{\prime}}

such that
f_{n}\not\rightrightarrows 0({\cal T}x)
. Thus, for some
\varepsilon>0

there exist sequences
(x_{n})
in
A
and
(T_{n})
in
{\cal T}

satisfying
|(T^{\prime}_{n}f_{n})x_{n}|=|f_{n}(T_{n}x_{n})|\geq\varepsilon
for all
n
.
Since
A
is limited,
(T^{\prime}_{n}f_{n})
is not
w
∗
-null, and hence

(T^{\prime}_{n}f_{n})x\not\to 0
for some
x\in X
. A contradiction to the assumption
iii)

completes the proof.
∎

The first inclusions in (1) and (2) below are trivial and
the second ones follow from Theorem
1
.

\text{\bf Lwc}(X,F)\subseteq\text{\bf a-Lwc}(X,F)\subseteq\text{\bf l-Lwc}(X,F).

[formula]

\text{\bf Lwc}(X,F)\subseteq\text{\bf o-Lwc}(X,F)\subseteq\text{\bf l-Lwc}(X,F).

[formula]

All inclusions in (1) and (2) are proper. It can be seen by taking
{\cal T}=\{T\}
and considering
standard examples from
[

,

,

,

]
.

By the following example (cf.
[
9
, Ex. 2]
), it is quite often that even a bounded set of
almost (limitedly, order)

operators need not to be
collectively almost (limitedly, order)
Lwc
.

Example 1
.

Let
\dim(F^{a})=\infty
. Take a positive disjoint normalized sequence
(u_{n})
in
F^{a}

and define rank one operators
T_{n}:\ell^{\infty}\to F
by
T_{n}x=x_{n}u_{n}
.
Then each
T_{n}
is
Lwc
since
T_{n}(\ell^{\infty})\subseteq F^{a}
.
Moreover,
\|T_{n}\|=1
for all
n
.
However,
{\cal T}=\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\notin\text{\bf l-Lwc}(\ell^{\infty},F)
,
and hence
{\cal T}\notin\text{\bf a-Lwc}(\ell^{\infty},F)
and
{\cal T}\notin\text{\bf o-Lwc}(\ell^{\infty},F)
.
Indeed, the set
A=\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\ell^{\infty}
is
limited by Phillip’s lemma (cf.
[
1
, Thm. 4.67]
), yet
the set
{\cal T}A=\{0\}\cup\{T_{n}e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=\{0\}\cup\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is not
Lwc

Theorem 2
.

Let
{\cal T}
be a bounded subset of
\text{\rm L}(X,F)
. The following are equivalent.

i)

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(X,F)
.

ii)

The set
\{Tx_{k}:T\in{\cal T},k\in\mathbb{N}\}

is
Lwc
for every
w
-convergent
(x_{n})
in
X
.

iii)

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx_{n})|\to 0

for every disjoint
(f_{n})
in
B_{F^{\prime}}
and every
w
-convergent
(x_{n})
.

Proof.

i)\ \Longrightarrow\ ii)
It is trivial.

ii)\ \Longrightarrow\ iii)
Let
x_{n}\convw x\in X
and
(f_{n})
be disjoint in
B_{F^{\prime}}
.
By the assumption, each disjoint sequence in

\text{\rm sol}({\cal T}(\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}))

B_{F^{\prime}}
. By
[

, Thm. 5.63]
,

f_{n}\rightrightarrows 0({\cal T}(\{x_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}))
.
In particular,
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx_{n})|\to 0
.

iii)\ \Longrightarrow\ i)
Suppose in contrary
{\cal T}A
is not
Lwc

for some relatively weakly compact
A\subseteq X
.
By
[
1
, Thm. 5.63]
, there exists a disjoint sequence
(f_{n})
in
B_{F^{\prime}}

such that
f_{n}\not\rightrightarrows 0({\cal T}A)
. Thus, for some
\varepsilon>0

there exist sequences
(x_{n})
in

and
(T_{n})
in
{\cal T}

satisfying
|f_{n}(T_{n}x_{n})|\geq\varepsilon
for all

.
By using the relative weak compactness of

and passing to a subsequence,
we may assume

. A contradiction to the assumption
iii)

3
Main results

Recall that an operator
T:E\to Y
is M-weakly compact if

(Tx_{n})
is norm null for each disjoint sequence
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.
By the Meyer-Nieberg result
[
13
, Satz 3]
,

Lwc
and
Mwc
operators are in the duality
(cf.
[
14
, Prop. 3.6.11]
,
[
1
, Thm. 5.64]
).
Almost (limitedly, order) L-weakly compact operators do not enjoy such a duality,
however, for them still there are useful semi-duality results proved in

[
7
, Thm. 2.5]
,
[
2
, Thm. 4]
, and
[
12
, Thm. 2.3]

respectively. For establishing the main result of the present paper,
we need the following notion of collectively qualified M-weakly compact
sets of operators.

Definition 2
.

A bounded subset
{\cal T}
of
\text{\rm L}(E,Y)
is:

a)

collectively M-weakly compact (
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf Mwc}(E,Y)
) if

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}\|Tx_{n}\|\to 0
for every disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.

b)

collectively almost M-weakly compact (
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Mwc}(E,Y)
) if

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx_{n})|\to 0
for every weakly convergent
(f_{n})
in
Y^{\prime}
and
every disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.

c)

collectively limitedly M-weakly compact (
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(E,Y)
) if

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f(Tx_{n})|\to 0
for every
f\in Y^{\prime}
and
every disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.

A bounded subset
{\cal T}
of
\text{\rm L}(E,F)
is:

d)

collectively order M-weakly compact (
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(E,F)
) if

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx_{n})|\to 0
for every order bounded
(f_{n})
in
F^{\prime}
and
every disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.

3.1
The semi-duality theorem.

Now, we are in the position to present our main result,
Theorem
4
. It is worth noting that
[
7
, Thm. 2.5]
,

[
2
, Thm. 4]
, and
[
12
, Thm. 2.3]

follow from Theorem
4
by taking
{\cal T}=\{T\}
.
It can be also seen by considering examples from
[
2
,
7
,
12
,
15
]

for the case
{\cal T}=\{T\}
that implications in
(ii_{1})
,
(ii_{2})
, and
(ii_{3})
are not invertable.

Theorem 4
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf Mwc}(E,Y)\Longleftrightarrow{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf Lwc}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

(ii)

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf Lwc}(X,F)\Longleftrightarrow{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf Mwc}(F^{\prime},X^{\prime})
.

(i_{1})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Mwc}(E,Y)\Longleftrightarrow{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

(ii_{1})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(X,F)\Longleftarrow{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf a-Mwc}(F^{\prime},X^{\prime})
.

(i_{2})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(E,Y)\Longleftrightarrow{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

(ii_{2})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(X,F)\Longleftarrow{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(F^{\prime},X^{\prime})
.

(i_{3})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(E,F)\Longleftrightarrow{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(F^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

(
ii_{3}
)

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)\Longleftarrow{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(F^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Items
(i)
and
(ii)
are proved in
[
9
, Thm. 2]

(i_{1})

(\Longrightarrow)
: Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Mwc}(E,Y)
and
f_{k}\convw f
in
Y^{\prime}
. Then

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{k_{n}}(Tx_{n})|\to 0
for every
subsequence
(f_{k_{n}})
and every disjoint sequence
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.
Therefore, for each disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
,

x_{n}\rightrightarrows 0\big(\{T^{\prime}f_{k}\}_{T\in{\cal T},k\in\mathbb{N}}\big)
.
By Lemma
1
,
\{T^{\prime}f_{k}\}_{T\in{\cal T},k\in\mathbb{N}}
is
an
Lwc
subset of
E^{\prime}
. Since
f_{k}\convw f
is arbitrary
then
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
by Theorem
2
.

(\Longleftarrow)
: Let
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
and
f_{k}\convw f
in
Y^{\prime}
.
Then
\{T^{\prime}f_{k}\}_{T\in{\cal T},k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an
Lwc
subset of
E^{\prime}

by Theorem
2
. Thus,
x_{n}\rightrightarrows 0(\{T^{\prime}f_{k}\}_{T\in{\cal T},k\in\mathbb{N}})

by Lemma
1
for each disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.
In particular,
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx_{n})|\to 0

for each disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.
Since
f_{k}\convw f
is arbitrary then
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Mwc}(E,Y)
.

(ii_{1})
Let
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf a-Mwc}(F^{\prime},X^{\prime})
and
x_{n}\convw x
in
X
.
Then
{\hat{x}_{n}}\convw{\hat{x}}
in
X^{\prime\prime}
, and hence

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx_{n})|=\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|(T^{\prime}f_{n})x_{n}|=\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|{\hat{x}_{n}}(T^{\prime}f_{n})|\to 0

for every disjoint
(f_{n})
in
B_{E^{\prime}}
. Since
x_{n}\convw x
is arbitrary,

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(X,F)
by Theorem
2
.

(i_{2})

(\Longrightarrow)
: Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(E,Y)
and
f\in Y^{\prime}
. Then
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f(Tx_{n})|\to 0
,
and hence
x_{n}\rightrightarrows 0(\{T^{\prime}f:T\in{\cal T}\})
for each disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.
By Lemma
1
,
\{T^{\prime}f:T\in{\cal T}\}
is an
Lwc
subset of
E^{\prime}
.
Since

is arbitrary, Theorem
1
implies
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

(\Longleftarrow)
: Let
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
and
f\in Y^{\prime}
. Then
\{T^{\prime}f:T\in{\cal T}\}
is an
Lwc

subset of
E^{\prime}
. By Lemma
1
,
x_{n}\rightrightarrows 0(\{T^{\prime}f:T\in{\cal T}\})
,
or equivalently,
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f(Tx_{n})|\to 0
for each disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
.
Since
f\in Y^{\prime}
is arbitrary,
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(E,Y)
.

(ii_{2})
Let
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(F^{\prime},X^{\prime})
and
(f_{n})
be disjoint in
B_{F^{\prime}}
.
Then
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|g(T^{\prime}f_{n})|\to 0
for each

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|(T^{\prime}f_{n})x|\to 0
for each

.
By Theorem
1
,
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(X,F)
.

(i_{3})

(\Longrightarrow)
: Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(E,F)
and
(f_{k})
be order bounded in
F^{\prime}
.
Then
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{k_{n}}(Tx_{n})|\to 0
for every subsequence

(f_{k_{n}})
of
(f_{k})
and every disjoint
(x_{n})
in

. Therefore,

x_{n}\rightrightarrows 0(\{T^{\prime}f_{k}:T\in{\cal T},k\in\mathbb{N}\})
for each
disjoint
(x_{n})
in
B_{E}
, and hence
\{T^{\prime}f_{k}:T\in{\cal T},k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is
an
Lwc
subset of

by Lemma
1
. Since the order bounded
sequence

is arbitrary,
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(F^{\prime},E^{\prime})
by Theorem
3
.

(\Longleftarrow)
: Let
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(F^{\prime},E^{\prime})
and
(f_{k})
be order bounded in
F^{\prime}
.
Then, the set
\{T^{\prime}f_{k}\}_{T\in{\cal T},k\in\mathbb{N}}
is
Lwc
in

by Theorem

. Therefore, for each disjoint
(x_{n})
in

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f_{n}(Tx_{n})|\to 0

(x_{n})
in

.
As the order bounded

.

(ii_{3})
Let
{\cal T}^{\prime}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(F^{\prime},E^{\prime})
and
(x_{k})

.

({\hat{x}_{k}})

for every disjoint
(f_{n})
in

. Since the order bounded sequence

3.3
The collective domination problem.

We need the following notion of domination
[
9
, Def. 2]
.

Definition 3
.

Let
{\cal S}
and
{\cal T}
be two subsets of
\text{\rm L}_{+}(E,F)
.
We say that
{\cal S}
is dominated by
{\cal T}
if, for each
S\in{\cal S}

there exists
T\in{\cal T}
such that
S\leq T
.

It is straightforward that
{\cal S}\subset\text{\rm L}_{+}(E,F)

is bounded whenever
{\cal S}
is dominated by a bounded
{\cal T}\subset\text{\rm L}_{+}(E,F)
.
Our last result gives a version of
[
9
, Thm. 6]
for
collectively almost (limitedly, order) L-weakly compact sets of operators.

Theorem 5
.

Let
{\cal S}\subset\text{\rm L}_{+}(E,F)
be dominated by
{\cal T}\subset\text{\rm L}_{+}(E,F)
. The following hold.

(i_{1})

If
E
is a KB-space, then

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(E,F)\Longrightarrow{\cal S}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(E,F)
.

(i_{2})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(E,F)\Longrightarrow{\cal S}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(E,F)
.

(ii_{2})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(E,F)\Longrightarrow{\cal S}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(E,F)
.

(i_{3})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)\Longrightarrow{\cal S}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)
.

(ii_{3})

{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(E,F)\Longrightarrow{\cal S}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(E,F)
.

Proof.

(i_{1})
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(E,F)
and
A\subseteq E
be weakly compact.
Since
{\cal S}
is dominated by
{\cal T}
, then
{\cal S}
is bounded and

{\cal S}A\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}T(\text{\rm sol}(A))
.
Indeed, let
x\in A
and
{\cal S}\ni S\leq T_{S}\in{\cal T}
.
Since
Sx\leq S|x|\leq T_{S}|x|
then
Sx\in[-T_{S}|x|,T_{S}|x|]\subseteq T_{S}(\text{\rm sol}(A))\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}T(\text{\rm sol}(A))
.
As
E
is a KB-space,
\text{\rm sol}(A)
is relatively weakly compact by the
Abramovich–Wickstead theorem (cf.,
[
1
, Thm. 4.39]
).
Since
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(E,F)
then

\bigcup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}T(\text{\rm sol}(A))
is an
Lwc
set,
and hence
{\cal S}A
is an
Lwc
set. Therefore,
{\cal S}\in\text{\bf a-Lwc}(E,F)
.

(i_{2})
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(E,F)
and
x\in E
.
Then
\{T|x|:T\in{\cal T}\}
, and hence
\{[-T|x|,T|x|]:T\in{\cal T}\}
is an
Lwc
set.
Since
{\cal S}
is dominated by
{\cal T}
,

\{Sx:S\in{\cal S}\}\subseteq\{[-T_{S}|x|,T_{S}|x|]:S\in{\cal S}\}\subseteq\{[-T|x|,T|x|]:T\in{\cal T}\}.

[formula]

Since
x\in E
is arbitrary, (3) implies
{\cal S}\in\text{\bf l-Lwc}(E,F)
by Theorem
1
.

(ii_{2})
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(E,F)
,
f\in Y^{\prime}
and
(x_{n})
be disjoint in
B_{E}
.
It follows
\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}|f|(T|x_{n}|)\to 0
, and hence

\sup\limits_{S\in{\cal T}}|f(Sx_{n})|\to 0
because
{\cal S}
is dominated by
{\cal T}
.
Therefore,
{\cal S}\in\text{\bf l-Mwc}(E,F)
.

(i_{3})
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)
and
x\in E_{+}
.
Since
{\cal S}
is dominated by
{\cal T}
then

{\cal S}[-x,x]\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}T[-x,x]
.
Since
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)
then

\bigcup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}T[-x,x]
is an
Lwc
set,
and hence
{\cal S}[-x,x]
is likewise
Lwc
.
Therefore,
{\cal S}\in\text{\bf o-Lwc}(E,F)
.

(ii_{3})
Let
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(E,F)
,

(f_{n})
be order bounded in
F^{\prime}
, and

(x_{n})
be disjoint in
B_{E}
.
Then
(|f_{n}|)
is order bounded in
F^{\prime}
and

(|x_{n}|)
is disjoint in
B_{E}
, and hence

\sup\limits_{T\in{\cal T}}||f_{n}|(T|x_{n}|)|\to 0

because
{\cal T}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(E,F)
.
Since
{\cal S}
is dominated by
{\cal T}
,
for every
S\in{\cal S}
there exists
T_{S}\in{\cal T}

with
|f_{n}(Sx_{n})|\leq|f_{n}|(S|x_{n}|)\leq|f_{n}|(T_{S}|x_{n}|)
.
So,
\sup\limits_{S\in{\cal S}}|f_{n}(Sx_{n})|\to 0
, and hence

{\cal S}\in\text{\bf o-Mwc}(E,F)
.
∎

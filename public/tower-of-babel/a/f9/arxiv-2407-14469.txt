Persistent Intrinsic Volumes
David Cohen-Steiner, Antoine Commaret

Abstract
We develop a new method to estimate the area, and more generally the intrinsic volumes, of a compact subset X X of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} from a set Y Y that is close in the Hausdorff distance. This estimator enjoys a linear rate of convergence as a function of the Hausdorff distance under mild regularity conditions on X X . Our approach combines tools from both geometric measure theory and persistent homology, extending the noise filtering properties of persistent homology from the realm of topology to geometry. Along the way, we obtain a stability result for intrinsic volumes.



Keywords:

Geometric Measure Theory, Intrinsic Volumes, Lipschitz-Killing Curvatures,
Quermassintegrals, Persistent Homology, Geometric Inference.

53C65, 28A75, 55N31, 49Q15.

Introduction

Geometric inference deals with the retrieval of geometric characteristics of an unknown shape
X
from a finite sample of
X
. Among such geometric characteristics, the
intrinsic volumes

V_{0}(X),\dots,V_{d}(X)
of a subset
X
of a Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are of particular importance.
Indeed, these quantities enjoy general properties such as additivity, isometry invariance and positive homogeneity, making them relevant in various fields such as integral geometry, where intrinsic volumes are known as
quermassintegrals
, or in differential geometry
where they are called
Lipschitz-Killing curvatures
.
Notably, the codimension 1 intrinsic volume
V_{d-1}(X)
is a multiple of the boundary area of
X
, whose estimation is an important question in geometric inference. It is of practical relevance in a large swath of scientific and industrial domains. For example, biologists seek to measure the area of tissues such as lungs
[
28
]
, bones
[
29
]
, blood vessels
[
20
]
or brains
[
39
]
. Applications in other fields such as chemistry, geology, engineering are so numerous that they are difficult to summarize.
From a mathematical perspective, we want to understand under which conditions and at which rate we are able to retrieve the intrinsic volumes of
X
from an approximating set
Y
. In order to achieve this, we introduce a novel method to estimate the intrinsic volumes of
X
using techniques from persistent homology and geometric measure theory. This allows to weaken the regularity conditions required in the current literature. Furthermore, we obtain rates of convergence with respect to the Hausdorff distance
d_{H}(X,Y)
, allowing
X
to be approximated by point clouds or voxels.

Intrinsic volumes were first defined for convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
40
,
6
]
and sets whose boundary is a smooth submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
42
]
. In either case, while

V_{d}(X)

coincides with
\mathcal{H}^{d}(X)
, the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
X
(i.e. the volume of
X
), the remaining intrinsic volumes
V_{i}(X)_{0\leq i\leq d-1}
depend on the curvature of its boundary. If
X
is an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
bounded by a hypersurface, denote by
(\kappa_{i}(x))_{1\leq i\leq d-1}
its principal curvatures at
x\in\partial X
. Then, for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
, we have (see for instance
[
36

):

V_{i}(X)=\frac{1}{(d-i)\omega_{d-i}}\int_{\partial X}\Sigma_{d-i-1}(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{d-1})d\omega(x)

where
\Sigma_{i}(X_{1},\dots,X_{d-1})=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\dots<j_{i}\leq d-1}X_{j_{1}}\dots X_{j_{i}}
is the elementary symmetric polynomial in
d-1
variables of degree
i
,
\omega_{i}
the
i
-th volume of the unit ball in dimension
i
and

the canonical volume form of
\partial X

is the
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure

i.e. the area of
\partial X
,

is the integral of the mean curvature and

is the integral of the Gauss curvature which by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem is the Euler characteristic
\chi(X)
of
X
. Similar formulas exist when
X
is a hypersurface of dimension
k
, in which case
V_{k}(X)
is a multiple of its area.
When
X
is a convex polytope, the
i

i
-dimensional facets multiplied by their appropriately normalized exterior cone angle.

The problem of extending the definition of intrinsic volumes to broader classes of sets was first posed by Milnor
[
33
]
. Since then, consistent definitions were given for Whitney-stratified sets

Previous work

Previous results focused mostly on the estimation of the area

V_{d-1}(X)
. One approach is to estimate the area by the area of a piecewise linear reconstruction of the data. For example, the tangent complex triangulation
[
5
]
guarantees that this estimator converges to
V_{d-1}(X)
at a linear rate in the Hausdorff distance

when
X
is a smooth submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
Y
a noise-free sample.
Using Crofton’s formula,
[
1
]
builds an estimator for the surface area
V_{d-1}(X)
from a point cloud sample

and obtains a square-root rate of convergence

O(d_{H}(X,Y)^{1/2})
in the general case. Other works have focused on the retrieval of
curvature measures
, which are local, more informative versions of the intrinsic volumes. A convergence rate of
d_{H}(X,Y)^{1/2}
for the curvature measures was obtained in
[
12
]
under the condition
d_{H}(X,Y)\leq\reach(X)
.
Chazal et al.
[
11
]
obtain the convergence of the curvature measures of
Y^{r}
to that of
X^{r}
at a square root rate, for any fixed
r>0
, where
Z^{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\tq d_{Z}(x)\leq r\}
is the
r
-offset of any subset
Z
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Linear convergence rates were obtained for the estimation of the first intrinsic volume using persistent homology of height functions and Crofton’s formula.
Authors in
[
18
]
showed a linear rate of convergence of
V_{1}(Y)
to
V_{1}(X)
with respect to the Fréchet distance between
X
and
Y
when they are both compact surfaces of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
or both curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
assuming their total absolute curvature is bounded.
Building on these ideas, Edelsbrunner et al.
[
21
]
obtained more recently a linear rate of convergence for the first intrinsic volume of voxelizations of smooth sets, in addition to showing the convergence of all intrinsic volumes of spheres voxelized with ever increasing precision.

Another line of research has focused on non-deterministic geometric inference from uniform samples of convex sets. Notably, the authors of
[
7
,
37
]
worked with convex sets with a
C^{k}
boundary, where
k\geq 2
. The expected intrinsic volumes of the convex hull of the sample were shown to converge each to the ideal intrinsic volume at a rate of
C_{X}n^{-2/(d+1)}
where

a constant depending on

Contributions

In this paper, we define quantities
V^{\varepsilon}_{i}(Y)
depending on a parameter
\varepsilon
, that approach
V_{i}(X)
at a linear rate in
d_{H}(X,Y)
assuming only mild regularity conditions on
X
. This rate is easily seen to be optimal. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first estimators that come with theoretical guarantees beyond sets with positive reach. Even for the basic problem of estimating the boundary area of 3-dimensional object with reach zero, we are not aware of any other provably correct method.

Theorem

(Main Results)
.

Let
X,Y
be two compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\mu\in(0,1],\varepsilon>0
be such that
d_{H}(X,Y)\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{4}\reach_{\mu}(X)
.
Then we have:

\left\lvert V^{\varepsilon}_{i}(Y)-V_{i}(X^{2\varepsilon})\right\rvert\leq C_{d}K(X^{2\varepsilon})\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu},

where
C_{d}
is a constant depending on
d
,
K(X^{2\varepsilon})\coloneqq\mathcal{H}^{d}(X^{2\varepsilon})+\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon}))
and
\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon})
is the unit normal bundle of
X^{2\varepsilon}
.

Further assuming that
\reach(X)>0
, we prove a linear rate of convergence for the intrinsic volumes of offsets:

\left\lvert V_{i}(X)-V_{i}(X^{2\varepsilon})\right\rvert\leq C_{d}\left(K(X)+K(X^{2\varepsilon})\right)\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}.

It is worth noting that the second claim holds even when
\reach(X)
is arbitrarily smaller than
\varepsilon
, a case for which, to the best of our knowledge, no quantitative convergence result between the curvatures of
X^{2\varepsilon}
and
X
was known. We also conjecture that this claim holds when
X
is subanalytic. Taken together, the two claims above provide a way to estimate the intrinsic volumes of an unknown shape
X
with positive reach from a Hausdorff approximation
Y.

From this point of view, the condition that
\reach(X)
is positive is not restrictive since sets with positive reach form a dense family of compact subsets for the Hausdorff distance.

A byproduct of our methods is an answer to the second open question asked by Milnor in
[
33
]
: In which sense do

and

-controlled homotopy equivalence (see
[
9
]
for a related notion) is sufficient, assuming a bound on the volume of the unit normal bundle of both sets.
More precisely, say that
two subsets

and
Y
of a Euclidean space
are
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-homotopy equivalent
if there exist two continuous maps
f:X\to Y
,
g:Y\to X
such that
{\left\lvert\left\lvert f-\Id\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon}
,
\left\lvert\left\lvert g-\Id\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
and such that there exist homotopies
H_{1}
(resp.
H_{2}
) between
f\circ g
and
\Id_{Y}
(resp.
g\circ f
and
\Id_{X}
) satisfying
\left\lvert\left\lvert H_{1}(t,\cdot)-\Id_{Y}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\infty}\leq 2\delta
(resp.
\left\lvert\left\lvert H_{2}(t,\cdot)-\Id_{X}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\infty}\leq 2\delta

.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with positive reach. If
X
and
Y
are
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-homotopy equivalent for
\varepsilon

\left\lvert V_{i}(X)-V_{i}(Y)\right\rvert\leq C_{d}\max(\varepsilon,\delta)\left(K(X)+K(Y)\right).

For our inference problem, a naive approach would be to estimate the intrinsic volumes of
X
by the intrinsic volumes of small offsets of
Y.
However, this leads to a trade-off between the bias induced by too large offset parameters and the spurious geometric details that come with small offset parameters. Optimizing this trade-off yields a sublinear rate of convergence. We use the noise-filtering properties of persistent homology to improve over this sublinear behavior by studying the inclusion
Y^{\varepsilon}\subset Y^{3\varepsilon}
instead, similar to the usual method for estimating Betti numbers
[
14

17

. Our approach uses the principal kinematic formula from integral geometry to express the intrinsic volumes as integrals of certain Euler characteristics. We then define our persistent intrinsic volumes by replacing these Euler characteristics with persistent Euler characteristics associated with the pair
Y^{\varepsilon}\subset Y^{3\varepsilon}
. A stability theorem for image persistence then allows us to prove a linear rate of convergence for our estimators.

Intrinsic Volumes and normal bundles

Background on Intrinsic Volumes

Intrinsic volumes of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are geometric quantities of importance in geometric measure theory. They were first defined by Steiner
[
40
]
for compact convex sets and by Weyl
[
42
]
for smooth submanifolds. Federer
[
23
]
extended their definition to the class of sets with
positive reach
. Specifically, he proved the
tube formula
which states that the volume of small offsets of a set with positive reach is a polynomial whose coefficients provide a definition for the intrinsic volumes, generalizing the definitions of Steiner and Weyl:

\forall\;0\leq r\leq\reach(X),\;\mathcal{H}^{d}(X^{r})\eqqcolon\sum_{i=0}^{d}\omega_{i}V_{d-i}(X)r^{i},

where for any
s\geq 0
,
\mathcal{H}^{s}
denotes the
s
-Hausdorff measure of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The polynomial in the right-hand side is called
the Steiner polynomial of X
and we denote it by
Q_{X}
.

Proposition 3.1

(Properties of intrinsic volumes)
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be two sets of positive reach. The intrinsic volumes
V_{i}
satisfy the following properties for all
0\leq i\leq d
:

(Isometry Invariance)

For any isometry
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
X
and
g(X)
have the same intrinsic volumes.

(Hausdorff Continuity)

The restriction of
V_{i}
to the class of convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is continuous in the Hausdorff metric;

(Additivity)

If both
X\cup Y
and
X\cap Y
have positive reach, we have:

V_{i}(X\cup Y)+V_{i}(X\cap Y)=V_{i}(X)+V_{i}(Y).

The celebrated Hadwiger theorem
[
27
,
30
]
states that intrinsic volumes form a basis of the functionals satisfying these three properties over the class of compact convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The additivity property allows in particular to extend the definition of intrinsic volumes to e.g., finite unions of convex sets, using the inclusion-exclusion principle.

The principal kinematic formula (see for instance
[
23
, Part 6]
for the paper of Federer or
[
36
, Chapter 6]
for a more modern presentation) connects the intrinsic volumes of sets of positive reach
A,B
with the integral of
V_{i}(A\cap g(B))
with respect to the Haar measure over the group of affine isometries
g
. In particular, since from the Gauss-Bonnet theorem
[
36
, 4.8]

V_{0}
is the Euler characteristic, taking
i=0
and
B
a ball of radius
r>0
this formula reads as an extension of the tube formula when the offset parameter goes beyond the reach.

Theorem 3.2

(Particular case of the Principal Kinematic Formula)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set with positive reach. Then for any
r>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(X\cap B(x,r))\mathrm{d}x=Q_{X}(r).

Intrinsic volumes have been extended to other classes of sets while preserving the properties and results listed above, notably the class of subanalytic sets
[
26
]
, the
U_{PR}
class i.e., generic unions of sets with positive reach
[
44
]
and the WDC class
[
25
]
. We will use another extension of intrinsic volumes, namely to the class of sets admitting a normal bundle, which we define in the following section.

Sets admitting a normal bundle

We introduce a class of sets defined so as to contain small tubular neighborhoods of sets with positive
\mu
-reach for any
\mu\in(0,1]
.
We recall
[
24
]
that the
tangent cone

\Tan(X,x)
of a set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
at a point
x\in X
is defined as the cone spanned by the set of limits of sequences of the form
(x_{n}-x)/\left\lvert\left\lvert x_{n}-x\right\rvert\right\rvert
where
x_{n}
is a sequence in
X
converging to
x
and not equal to
x
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. When
X
has positive reach, the
normal cone
of
X
at
x
is

\Nor(X,x)\coloneqq\Tan(X,x)^{\text{o}}

where for any set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, its dual cone is

S^{\text{o}}\coloneqq\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\tq\langle u,v\rangle\leq 0,\,\forall\,v\in S\}.

Definition 3.3

(Sets admitting a normal bundle)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
When
{}^{\neg}X\coloneqq\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus X}
has positive reach and is a Lipschitz domain
1
1

1

A Lipschitz domain is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that is locally the epigraph of a Lipschitz function.
, define

\Nor(X,x)\coloneqq-\Nor(^{\neg}X,x).

This definition is consistent in case both

have positive reach. If either
\reach(X)>0

\Nor(X)\coloneqq\bigcup_{x\in\partial X}\{x\}\times(\Nor(X,x)\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}).

The pair

\Tan(\Nor(X),(x,n))
is a
(d-1)

The structure of the tangent spaces of

Proposition 3.4

(Tangent spaces of normal bundles

Let

Principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}=\kappa_{i}(x,n)
are unique up to permutations.
When
\reach(X)>0

is larger than
-\reach(X)^{-1}
. Moreover, assume that

At

We are now able to define the

Definition 3.5

(

Let

M_{R}(X)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\Nor(X)}\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\left\lvert 1+t\kappa_{i}(x,n)\right\rvert}{\sqrt{1+\kappa_{i}(x,n)^{2}}}\diff\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(x,n)\diff t.

Proposition 3.6

(R-curvature mass of a set with smooth boundary)
.

When
X

C^{1,1}

(d-1)
-hypersurface as boundary, this quantity can be expressed as an integral over
\partial X

\mathbb{R}^{d}

M_{R}(X)=\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\partial X}\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\left\lvert 1+t\kappa_{i}\right\rvert d\omega_{\partial X}\diff t.

Proof.

If

.
∎

Proposition 3.7

(Bounds on

)
.

Let

If

, we have:

For any

The equality comes from the fact that for any

and thus the coefficient in front of

integrates over

for any

.
∎

When

Let

Definition 4.1

(Persistence modules)

Given an interval

whose coordinates are the bounds of the intervals decomposing

\dgm(M)\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\{(\inf I,\sup I)\}.

Elements of a persistence diagram are usually referred to as either intervals or bars.

Definition 4.4

(Bottleneck distance)
.

A
\delta

For any pair of pointwise finite-dimensional persistence modules

Image Persistence

Definition 5.1

(Image Persistence)
.

Let
M,N
be two persistence modules and
{f:M\to N}
be a morphism between them. The

\im f
is the persistence module with vector space

{(\im f)_{t}=f(M_{t})}
at value

and whose connecting maps
(\im f)_{a}\to(\im f)_{b}
are the restrictions of
N_{a}\to N_{b}
to
(\im f)_{a}
for every pair

.

We will only deal with image persistence modules arising in the following situation. Let

and

. The homology with coefficients in

or

and

or

. Furthermore, the inclusion

yields for every degree

\iota^{\bullet}_{j}

\iota^{\bullet}_{j}:H_{j}(A_{\bullet})\to H_{j}(B_{\bullet}).

Assuming that each

at filtration value

Now we use results from Bauer & Lesnick

C^{\prime}_{i}
to be the respective largest subsets of
\mathcal{I}_{i}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{i}
matched bijectively by
\gamma
. We have:

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\left\lvert\chi(D(t))-\chi(D^{\prime}(t))\right\rvert\diff t\leq\sum_{i=0}^{d}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{I_{i}\in C_{i}}\int_{a}^{b}\left\lvert\one_{I_{i}}-\one_{\gamma(I_{i})}\right\rvert\diff t

\displaystyle+\hskip-7.0pt\sum_{I_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{i}\setminus C_{i}}\int_{a}^{b}\one_{I_{i}}(t)\diff t+\sum_{I^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}_{i}\setminus C^{\prime}_{i}}\int_{a}^{b}\one_{I^{\prime}_{i}}(t)\diff t\Bigr).

Since
\gamma
is an
\varepsilon
-matching, each of these integrals is bounded by
2\varepsilon
,
either because there are two parts of length at most
\varepsilon
where matched intervals do not overlap or because the length of non-matched intervals is at most
2\varepsilon
.
Now for the sum over
C_{i}
, we have
\mathrm{supp}\left\lvert\one_{I_{i}}-\one_{\gamma(I_{i})}\right\rvert\subset I_{i}\cup\gamma(I_{i})
, meaning that the integral over
[a,b]
vanishes if none of these intervals intersect with
(a,b)
.
Similarly, for the second and third sums, only intervals intersecting with
(a,b)
contribute to the sum.
Overall, there is at most one non-vanishing contribution per interval in
D\cup D^{\prime}
whose intersection with
(a,b)
is non-empty, and no contribution otherwise. The total number of non-vanishing integrals is bounded by
N_{a}^{b}(D)+N_{a}^{b}(D^{\prime})
.
∎

We use the previous lemmas to establish a linear rate of convergence of
the persistent Steiner function
Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}
to the Steiner polynomial
Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}
over
[0,R]
for any positive real

.

Theorem 6.6

(Approximating the Steiner polynomial in the
L^{1}
norm)
.

Let
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact sets and
\varepsilon,\mu>0
be such that

. Then we have:

\left\lvert\left\lvert Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{L^{1}([0,R])}\leq\frac{8\varepsilon}{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}N_{0}^{R}(D^{x}_{X^{2\varepsilon}})\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Indeed, we have the following:

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{Y}^{\varepsilon}-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}\right\|_{L^{1}([0,R])}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\left|Q_{Y}^{\varepsilon}(r)-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}(r)\right|\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi\!\left(D_{Y}^{\varepsilon,x}(r)\right)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi\!\left(D_{X^{2\varepsilon}}^{x}(r)\right)\,dx\right|\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\chi\!\left(D_{Y}^{\varepsilon,x}(r)\right)-\chi\!\left(D_{X^{2\varepsilon}}^{x}(r)\right)\right|\,dx\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{8\varepsilon}{\mu}\,N_{0}^{R}\!\left(D_{X^{2\varepsilon}}^{x}\right)\,dx,

where the last line follows from
Lemma

6.5
and the bottleneck bound of
Proposition

6.4
.

-curvature mass of

Lemma 6.7

(Number of critical points of
d_{x}
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set and
\varepsilon,\mu>0
be such that
0<2\varepsilon<\reach_{\mu}(X)
(which implies that
X^{2\varepsilon}
admits a normal bundle). For any

, define
\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}:\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon})\times[0,R]\to\mathbb{R}^{d},(y,n,t)\mapsto y+tn
.

Then
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-almost everywhere in
x
,

•

\mathrm{card}\;\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)
is finite;

•

N_{0}^{R}(D^{x}_{X^{2\varepsilon}})\leq\one_{X^{2\varepsilon}}(x)+\mathrm{card}\;\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)
.

Moreover, we have:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}N_{0}^{R}(D^{x}_{X^{2\varepsilon}})\diff x\leq\Vol(X^{2\varepsilon})+M_{R}(X^{2\varepsilon}),

where
M_{R}(X^{2\varepsilon})
is the
R
-curvature mass defined in
Definition

3.5
.

Proof.

The map
\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}
is a Lipschitz map between two rectifiable sets of dimension
d
. By the coarea formula of Federer
[
24
, Theorem 3.2.22]
, denoting by
J_{d}\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}
the
d
-dimensional Jacobian of
\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}
, we have:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{card}\;\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)\diff x=\int_{\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon})\times[0,R]}J_{d}\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}(y,n,t)\diff\mathcal{H}^{d}(y,n,t).

(1)

Since
\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}
is Lipschitz on the compact set
\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon})\times[0,R]
, this quantity has to be finite, yielding the finiteness of
\mathrm{card}\;\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)
almost everywhere.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
X^{2\varepsilon}_{t}\coloneqq X^{2\varepsilon}\cap d_{x}^{-1}(-\infty,t]
be the
t^{2}
-sublevel sets of
{f_{x}\coloneqq(d_{x})^{2}|_{X^{2\varepsilon}}}
. When
t<0
, the set
X^{2\varepsilon}_{t}
is empty. When
t=0
, this set is either a point when
x\in X^{2\varepsilon}
, or empty.
The function
f_{x}:X^{2\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
cannot be Morse in the classical sense of Milnor
[
34
]
as
X^{2\varepsilon}
is not a submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Nevertheless by the
\mu
-reach assumption,
X^{2\varepsilon}
is an offset of
X
at a regular value of its distance function, a setting to which the generalized Morse theory of
[
19
]
applies.
Specifically, if
f_{x}

is a Morse function in the sense of
[
19
]
, the changes in homology of the filtration
(X^{2\varepsilon}_{t})_{t>0}
happen at the square roots of the critical values of
f_{x}
and there is exactly one homology change per critical point of this map. The number of bars of a persistence diagram of a Morse function is bounded by its number of critical points and we have:

N_{0}^{R}(D^{x}_{X^{2\varepsilon}})\leq\one_{X^{2\varepsilon}}(x)+\mathrm{card}\Bigl\{\text{Critical points of }f_{x}\text{ with distance to }x\text{ in }(0,R]\Bigr\}.

We prove that the set of points
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
f_{x}
is a Morse function in the sense of this generalized Morse theory has full Lebesgue measure, and we relate the number of critical points of
f_{x}
to the map
\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}
. Following
[
19
, Definition 2.5]
, a point
z\neq x
is a critical point for
f_{x}
with value in
(0,R^{2}]
if and only if the pair
\left(z,\frac{z-x}{\left\lvert\left\lvert z-x\right\rvert\right\rvert}\right)\eqqcolon(z,n)
belongs to the normal bundle
\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon})
and
\left\lvert\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq R
, that is, when
(z,n,\left\lvert\left\lvert x-z\right\rvert\right\rvert)\in\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)
. In case
f_{x}
is Morse, the previous inequality becomes:

N_{0}^{R}(D^{x}_{X^{2\varepsilon}})\leq\one_{X^{2\varepsilon}}(x)+\mathrm{card}\;\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x).

We shall prove that
\mathcal{H}^{d}
-almost everywhere in
x
, the critical points of
f_{x}
are non-degenerate.
Let
M
be the set of points
x
such that
\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)
is infinite and let
\mathrm{Bad}(X^{2\varepsilon})
be the set of non-regular pairs of
\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon})
. The set
{\mathrm{reg}(X^{2\varepsilon})\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus(M\cup\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}(\mathrm{Bad}_{X^{2\varepsilon}}\times[0,R])\cup\partial X^{2\varepsilon})}
has full Lebesgue measure.

Take
x\in\mathrm{reg}(X^{2\varepsilon})
and let
(b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
be an orthonormal basis of principal directions of
\Tan\left(\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon}),\left(z,\frac{z-x}{\left\lvert\left\lvert z-x\right\rvert\right\rvert}\right)\right)
associated with the principal curvatures
(\kappa_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
.
Let
u=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}b_{i}
,
v=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nu_{i}b_{i}
be any pair in
\Tan\left(\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon}),\left(z,\frac{z-x}{\left\lvert\left\lvert z-x\right\rvert\right\rvert}\right)\right)
and
r=\left\lvert\left\lvert z-x\right\rvert\right\rvert
. Following notations from
[
19
, Definition 2.5]
, the Hessian of
f_{x}
at
z
is:

(H_{|X^{2\varepsilon}}f_{x})(z):(u,v)\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m}(2+2r\kappa_{i})\lambda_{i}\nu_{i}.

This bilinear form is degenerate if and only if
\displaystyle r=-\frac{1}{\kappa}
where
\kappa
is a principal curvature of a point in
\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)
. There are at most
d
distinct principal curvatures, from which we know that the set

\displaystyle\mathrm{Degen}(X^{2\varepsilon})\coloneqq\left\{\Bigl(z,n,-\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)

\displaystyle\bigg|\;(z,n)\in\Nor(X^{2\varepsilon})\text{ is regular,}

\displaystyle\kappa\text{ is a principal curvature at }(z,n)\Bigr\}.

has Hausdorff dimension at most
(d-1)
. Thus
\mathrm{reg}(X^{2\varepsilon})\setminus\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}(\mathrm{Degen}(X^{2\varepsilon}))
is a set of full Lebesgue measure whose elements

N_{0}^{R}(D^{x}_{X^{2\varepsilon}})\leq\one_{X^{2\varepsilon}}(x)+\mathrm{card}\;\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)
.

Now by the structure of tangent spaces (

3.4
) we have:

J_{d}\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}(x,n,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\left\lvert 1+t\kappa_{i}(x,n)\right\rvert}{\sqrt{1+\kappa_{i}(x,n)^{2}}}.

and thus by the co-area formula

,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}N_{0}^{R}(D^{x}_{X^{2\varepsilon}})\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\one_{X^{2\varepsilon}}(x)+\mathrm{card}\;\pi_{X^{2\varepsilon},R}^{-1}(x)\right)\diff x=\Vol(X^{2\varepsilon})+M_{R}(X^{2\varepsilon}).

∎

We are now in position to prove our main theorems.

Theorem 6.8

(Linear convergence of the persistent intrinsic volumes)
.

Let
X,Y
be compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\varepsilon,\mu>0
be such that
d_{H}(X,Y)\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{4}\reach_{\mu}(X)
. There exist constants
P(i,d)
such that for any
R>0
, we have:

\left\lvert V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)-V_{i}(X^{2\varepsilon})\right\rvert\leq\frac{\varepsilon P(i,d)}{\mu R^{d-i+1}}\left(\Vol(X^{2\varepsilon})+M_{R}(X^{2\varepsilon})\right).

Proof.

Applying
Theorem

6.6
and
Lemma

6.7
we obtain for any positive
R
:

\left\lvert\left\lvert Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{L^{1}([0,R])}\leq\frac{8\varepsilon}{\mu}(\Vol(X^{2\varepsilon})+M_{R}(X^{2\varepsilon})).

Let
i\leq j
and let
(L_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the Legendre polynomials on
[0,1]
- recall that they form an orthonormal basis of the space of polynomials equipped with the inner product of
L^{2}([0,1])
. We obtain after renormalization and reparametrization an orthonormal basis of the polynomials of degree at most
d
on
[0,R]
formed by the following polynomials:

P^{R}_{j}(X)\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{2j+1}{R}}L_{j}\left(\frac{X}{R}\right).

The fact that
\left\lvert\left\lvert L_{i}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\infty,[0,1]}\leq 1
yields
\left\lvert\left\lvert P^{R}_{j}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\infty,[0,R]}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2j+1}{R}}
for any positive
R
.

Denoting by
c_{i}(P^{R}_{j})
the
i
-th coefficient of
P^{R}_{j}
, we have for any
i\leq j
:

\left\lvert c_{i}(P^{R}_{j})\right\rvert=\frac{1}{R^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\sqrt{2j+1}\binom{j}{i}\binom{i+j}{i}

and
c_{i}(P^{R}_{j})=0
if
i>j
. Now the persistent Steiner polynomial is the
orthogonal projection of
Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}
on the space of polynomials of degree at most
d
. Decomposing it in the basis
P^{R}_{j}
and taking the coefficient of
X^{d-i}
yields

V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)=\frac{1}{\omega_{d-i}}\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(D^{\varepsilon,x}_{Y}(r))\sum_{j=d-i}^{d}P^{R}_{j}(r)c_{d-i}(P^{R}_{j})\diff x\diff r.

Since we have

\left\lvert\left\lvert P^{R}_{j}c_{d-i}(P^{R}_{j})\right\rvert\right\rvert_{\infty,[0,R]}\leq\frac{(2j+1)}{R^{d-i+1}}\binom{j}{d-i}\binom{d-i+j}{j},

we see that:

\left\lvert V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)-V_{i}(X^{2\varepsilon})\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{R^{d-i+1}}\left\lvert\left\lvert Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{L^{1}([0,R])}\frac{1}{\omega_{d-i}}\sum_{j=d-i}^{d}(2j+1)\binom{j}{d-i}\binom{d-i+j}{j}.

We now obtain the desired inequality by applying
Lemma

6.7
and
Theorem

6.6
and putting

P(i,d)\coloneqq\frac{8}{\omega_{d-i}}\sum_{j=d-i}^{d}(2j+1)\binom{j}{d-i}\binom{d-i+j}{j}.

∎

Remark 6.9
.

Our approach consists in bounding the
L^{1}
norm of
Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}
(
Theorem

6.6
and
Lemma

6.7
):

\left\lvert\left\lvert Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{L^{1}([0,R])}\leq\frac{8\varepsilon}{\mu}\left(\Vol(X^{2\varepsilon})+M_{R}(X^{2\varepsilon})\right),

and retrieving quantities that are close to the rescaled coefficients
(V_{i}(X^{2\varepsilon}))_{0\leq i\leq d}
of the polynomial
Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}
from the persistent Steiner function
Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}
, which is in

but is not a priori a polynomial.
We chose in

6.1
the coefficients
V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)
of its orthogonal projection on the space
\mathbb{R}_{d}[X]
of polynomials of degree at most
d
. From this definition and from the explicit formulas for Legendre polynomials (see the proof of
Theorem

6.8
) we obtain:

\left\lvert V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)-V_{i}(X^{2\varepsilon})\right\rvert\leq\frac{P(i,d)}{8R^{d-i+1}}\left\lvert\left\lvert Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{L^{1}([0,R])}.

(2)

Taking the
i
-th coefficients of orthogonal projections is one way to build linear forms
\phi^{R}_{i,d}:L^{2}([0,R])\to\mathbb{R}
such that, restricted to the space
\mathbb{R}_{d}[X]
of polynomials with degree at most
d
,
\phi^{R}_{i,d}
is the map
\sum_{0\leq j\leq d}a_{j}X^{j}\mapsto a_{i}
. In fine we defined
V_{i}^{\varepsilon,R}(Y)
as
\phi^{R}_{d-i,d}(Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y})/\omega_{d-i}
. By
Equation

2
the linear forms
\phi^{R}_{i,d}
are
\omega_{d-i}P(i,d)/8R^{i+1}
-Lipschitz for the
L^{1}
norm over
[0,R]
. The bound we infer on
V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)-V_{i}(X^{2\varepsilon})
from
\left\lvert\left\lvert Q^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-Q_{X^{2\varepsilon}}\right\rvert\right\rvert_{L^{1}([0,R])}
comes from a bound on the Lipschitz constant of
\phi^{R}_{d-i,d}
obtained using Legendre polynomials. Yet, by the Hahn-Banach extension theorem, the best Lipschitz constant possible for a linear form
\phi^{R}_{i,d}
whose restriction to
\mathbb{R}_{d}[X]
is the
i
-th coefficient map is exactly the Lipschitz constant of
(\phi^{R}_{i,d})|_{\mathbb{R}_{d}[X]}
which we denote by

. When

by classical work in optimization (e.g.,
[
38
, 4.9, p. 117]
), we have:

l^{R}_{d,d}=\frac{4^{d}}{R^{d+1}}\leq\frac{(2d+1)}{R^{d+1}}\binom{2d}{d},

where the right-hand side is the bound on the Lipschitz constant we explicitly obtained by the Legendre polynomials method. This shows that there exist alternate ways of defining the persistent intrinsic volumes from the persistent Steiner function that lead to a strictly better bound than the one in
Theorem

6.8
.

Theorem 6.10

\left\lvert V_{i}(X^{2\varepsilon})-V_{i}(X^{\delta})\right\rvert\leq\frac{\varepsilon P(i,d)}{2\mu R^{d-i+1}}\left(\Vol(X^{2\varepsilon})+M_{R}(X^{2\varepsilon})+\Vol(X^{\delta})+M_{R}(X^{\delta})\right).

(3)

\displaystyle\;M_{R}(X).

(Linear rate of approximation for the intrinsic volumes)

{\reach(X)>0}

\left\lvert V_{i}(X)-V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)\right\rvert\leq\frac{\varepsilon P(i,d)}{2\mu R^{d-i+1}}\left(\Vol(X)+2\Vol(X^{2\varepsilon})+M_{R}(X)+2M_{R}(X^{2\varepsilon})\right).

We prove

7
Computing persistent intrinsic volumes

We conclude this paper by discussing how one may compute our estimators in practice, assuming
Y
is given as a finite set of points.
From the proof of
Theorem

6.8
the
V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)
are given by:

V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)=\int_{0}^{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(D^{\varepsilon,x}_{Y}(r))S_{i,d}(r)\diff x\diff r,

where
S_{i,d}
is a polynomial.

Computing this integral exactly would involve computing a
d
-dimensional family of persistence diagrams, which is computationally daunting. We may however easily approximate
V^{\varepsilon,R}_{i}(Y)
with arbitrary accuracy using a Monte-Carlo method by sampling points
x
uniformly in the
R
-offset of
Y
and computing the image persistence diagrams of their distance function
d_{x}
using e.g.,
[
3
]
. The number of trials required to reach accuracy
\delta
is of the order of
\delta^{-2}
times the variance of the random variable
\displaystyle V=\int_{0}^{R}\chi(D^{\varepsilon,x}_{Y}(r))S_{i,d}(r)\diff r
.

The variance of
V
is bounded by
\sup\left\lvert V\right\rvert^{2}
and we have:

\sup\left\lvert V\right\rvert\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}N_{0}^{R}(D^{\varepsilon,x}_{Y})\sup_{r\in[0,R]}RS_{i,d}(r),

that is, a polynomial in
R
times the maximum size of the image persistence diagram of
d_{x}
. Now by
Proposition

6.4
, the sizes of these image persistence diagrams are at most the sizes of the persistence diagrams of
(d_{x})|_{X^{2\varepsilon}}
.

One situation where we can uniformly bound the size of these diagrams is when
X
is a semi-algebraic set, since by Thom-Milnor
[
32
]
the number of critical points of
(d_{x})|_{X^{2\varepsilon}}
is bounded by a function of the degrees of the polynomial equalities and inequalities defining
X
. Another bound is provided by
Lemma

6.2
. This bound is pessimistic in general as the smallest simplicial complex sandwiched between
Y^{\varepsilon}
and
Y^{3\varepsilon}
may have a large number of simplices, yet one expects the number of critical points of
(d_{x})|_{C}
to be much smaller on average. We leave more in-depth investigations of the computational aspects of our method for future work.

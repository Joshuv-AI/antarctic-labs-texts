Abstract




Abstract.
This paper provides the upper and lower bounds of blowup time and blowup rate as well as the exponential growth estimate of blowup solutions for a pseudo-parabolic equation with singular potential. These results complement the ones obtained in the previous literature.

Keywords:
Pseudo-parabolic equation; Blowup time; Blowup rate; Growth estimate.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
: 35K20; 35B44.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we focus on the study of the following IBVP of pseudo-parabolic equation with singular potential:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{u_{t}}{|x|^{s}}-\Delta u-\Delta u_{t}=|u|^{p-2}u,&x\in\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\displaystyle u(x,t)=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\ t>0,\\
\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n>2)
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
,
s\in[0,2]
,
p\in(2,{2n}/(n-2))
and
u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

In recent years, the semilinear pseudo-parabolic equation

u_{t}-\Delta u-k\Delta u_{t}=f(u),

(1.4)

being used to describe the deformation of a thermoelastic body, has attracted a lot of attention from scholars (
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
). For instance, Cao et al.
[
1
]
investigated the existence, uniqueness and large time behavior of solutions for the Cauchy problem of (
1.4
) when
k>0
and
f(u)=u^{q}
with
q>0
, and they determined the critical global existence exponent and critical Fujita exponent; Yang et al.
[
10
]
further obtained the second critical exponent for the Cauchy problem of (
1.4
) when
k>0
and
f(u)=u^{q}
with
q>1
; Xu and Su
[
9
]
provided the existence of global and blowup solutions for the IBVP of (
1.4
) when
k=1
and
f(u)=u^{q}
with
1<q<\infty
if
n=1,2
as well as
1<q\leq(n+2)/(n-2)
if
n>2
; Luo
[
6
]
investigated the finite time blowup and bounds of blowup time for the IBVP of (
1.4
) when
k=1
and
f(u)=u^{q}
with
q>1
; Later on, Xu and Zhou
[
8
]
proved a new blowup condition and a new upper bound of blowup time for the IBVP of (
1.4
) when
k=1
and
f(u)=|u|^{q-1}u
with
q>1
; When
k=1
and
f
satisfies some general assumptions, Han
[
4
]
established the existence of blowup solutions of the IBVP of (
1.4
) and estimated the upper bound of blowup time.

In fluid mechanics, it is noticed that
|x|^{-s}
may appear in non-Newton filtration equation describing the motion of a non-Newton fluid in a rigid porous medium under certain assumptions, representing a special medium void (see
[
7
,
11
]
). Considering the porous media in the thermoelastic model (
1.4
) with
k=1
and
f(u)=|u|^{p-2}u
, Lian et al.
[
5
]
recently proved the existence and asymptotic behavior of global solutions for (
1
) at low and critical initial energies. Moreover, they showed the existence of blowup solutions with three initial energy levels and derived the upper bound of blowup time.

It is worth noting that blowup time and blowup rate are indispensable aspects in blowup theory and have always been of interest to researchers (see
[
3
,
2
]
). In view of the results obtained in
[
5
]
, we find that the blowup rate, the growth estimate and the lower bound of blowup time of blowup solutions to (
1
) have not been studied. Therefore, the purpose of the present paper is to complement the blowup results in
[
5
]
. Firstly, we provide a different method from the concavity method used in
[
5
]
to prove the existence of blowup solutions at low initial energy. Based on it, we determine the upper bound of blowup rate and derive the exponential growth estimate of blowup solutions. Also, we present a new upper bound for blowup time, which is better than the one in
[
5
]
in some specific circumstances. Finally, we estimate the lower bounds of blowup time and blowup rate when a blowup occurred.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 contains some symbols and lemmas. Section 3 contains the statement of main results and their proofs.

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we denote the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-inner product by
(\cdot,\cdot)
and the
L^{q}(\Omega)
-norm (
1\leq q\leq\infty
) by
\|\cdot\|_{q}
.
For
\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, we define the functionals

J(\varphi):=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\|\varphi\|_{p}^{p},\quad I(\varphi):=\|\nabla\varphi\|_{2}^{2}-\|\varphi\|_{p}^{p},

(2.1)

and the mountain pass level

d:=\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{N}}J(\varphi),

where
\mathcal{N}=\{\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\mid I(\varphi)=0\}
denotes the Nehari manifold. From
[
5
, Lemma 2.2]
, we have

d=\frac{p-2}{2p}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}},

(2.2)

where
C_{*}>0
is the best constant satisfying

\|\varphi\|_{p}\leq C_{*}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{2},\quad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.3)

By
[
5
, Remark 1]
, we know that there exists a constant
C_{**}>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx\leq C_{**}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{2}^{2},\quad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.4)

The definition of weak solutions is as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

(Weak solution)
A function
u\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\right)
with
u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
is called a weak solution of (
1
) over
\Omega\times[0,T)
, if
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and the equality

(|x|^{-s}u_{t},\phi)+(\nabla u,\nabla\phi)+(\nabla u_{t},\nabla\phi)=(|u|^{p-2}u,\phi)

holds for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and any
\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Let
u=u(t)
,
t\in[0,T)
be the weak solution of (
1
) where
T
denotes the maximum existence time in this and next sections. Lemma
2.1
below is derived directly by equations
(2.2)
,
(4.12)
and
(4.16)
of
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
t\in[0,T)
, we have that

\begin{split}&J(u)+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{\tau}\|_{2}^{2}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{\tau}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dxd\tau=J(u_{0}),\\
&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right)=-I(u).\end{split}

The next lemma is crucial in studying the upper bound of blowup rate.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
I(u_{0})<0
and
0\leq J(u_{0})<d
, then there exists a constant
\theta_{2}>\theta_{1}:=C_{*}^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}
such that

\|u\|_{p}\geq\theta_{2},\quad\|\nabla u\|_{2}\geq\frac{\theta_{2}}{C_{*}},\quad t\in[0,T),

(2.5)

where
C_{*}>0
is defined in (
2.3
). Additionally, we have that

\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}}\geq\theta_{0}:=\left(\frac{p}{2}-pC_{*}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}J(u_{0})\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}>1.

(2.6)

Proof.

Noting that
I(u_{0})<0
and (
2.3
), we obtain
\|u_{0}\|_{p}^{p}>\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\geq C_{*}^{-2}\|u_{0}\|_{p}^{2}
. Thus,
\|u_{0}\|_{p}>\theta_{1}:=C_{*}^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}
.
From (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), one has

J(u)\geq\frac{1}{2C_{*}^{2}}\|u\|_{p}^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

(2.7)

Define
h(\theta)=\frac{1}{2C_{*}^{2}}\theta^{2}-\frac{1}{p}\theta^{p}
for all
\theta\geq 0
. Then
h(\theta)
is decreasing on
[\theta_{1},\infty)
, increasing on
[0,\theta_{1}]
, and
h(\theta_{1})=\frac{p-2}{2p}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}=d
.

Owing to
J(u_{0})<d
and (
2.7
), there exists a constant
\theta_{2}>\theta_{1}
such that
h(\theta_{2})=J(u_{0})\geq h(\|u_{0}\|_{p})
, which, together with
\|u_{0}\|_{p}>\theta_{1}
, yields
\|u_{0}\|_{p}\geq\theta_{2}
. Next we prove
\|u\|_{p}\geq\theta_{2}
for
t\in(0,T)
. Indeed, if not, then by
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
, one can choose a
t_{0}\in(0,T)
such that
\theta_{1}<\|u(t_{0})\|_{p}<\theta_{2}
. According to (
2.7
), we obtain
J(u_{0})=h(\theta_{2})<h(\|u(t_{0})\|_{p})\leq J(u(t_{0}))
, which contradicts that
J(u_{0})\geq J(u(t_{0}))
(see Lemma
2.1
). In addition, we deduce from (
2.3
) that
C_{*}\|\nabla u\|_{2}\geq\|u\|_{p}\geq\theta_{2}
. Therefore, (
2.5
) holds.

Next, we show that (
2.6
) is true. Let
\theta_{3}=\theta_{2}/\theta_{1}>1
. Then we infer from
h(\theta_{2})=J(u_{0})
and
\theta_{1}=C_{*}^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}
that

J(u_{0})=h(\theta_{1}\theta_{3})=\theta_{1}^{2}\theta_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2C_{*}^{2}}-\frac{\theta_{1}^{p-2}\theta_{3}^{p-2}}{p}\right)=\theta_{3}^{2}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta_{3}^{p-2}}{p}\right).

Thanks to
J(u_{0})\geq 0
and
\theta_{3}>1
, we derive

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\theta_{3}^{p-2}}{p}=\frac{C_{*}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}J(u_{0})}{\theta_{3}^{2}}\leq C_{*}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}J(u_{0}).

Since
J(u_{0})<d=\frac{p-2}{2p}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}
, one has

\frac{\theta_{2}}{\theta_{1}}=\theta_{3}\geq\left(\frac{p}{2}-pC_{*}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}J(u_{0})\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}>\left(\frac{p}{2}-\frac{p-2}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}=1,

which gives (
2.6
).
∎

3
Main results

In this section, the main results of this paper and their proofs will be stated.
The following theorem is mainly about the upper bounds on blowup rate and time as well as the exponential growth estimate of blowup solutions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. If
J(u_{0})<d
and
I(u_{0})<0
, then
u
blows up in the finite time
T
in the sense of

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right)=\infty.

(3.1)

Furthermore,

(i)
the upper bounds of blowup time and rate are given by

T\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}(C_{1}-2)\mathcal{G}(0)}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right),

(3.2)

and

\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{C_{1}(C_{1}-2)\mathcal{G}(0)}{\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{C_{1}}{2}}}\right]^{\frac{2}{2-C_{1}}}(T-t)^{-\frac{2}{C_{1}-2}},

(3.3)

where

\mathcal{G}(0):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-J(u_{0}),&\hbox{~if~}J(u_{0})<0;\\
\\
\displaystyle d-J(u_{0}),&\hbox{~if~}0\leq J(u_{0})<d\end{array}\right.

and

C_{1}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle p,&\hbox{~if~}J(u_{0})<0;\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{\left(\theta_{0}^{p}-1\right)(p-2)}{\theta_{0}^{p}}+2,&\hbox{~if~}0\leq J(u_{0})<d.\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Here,
C_{*}
and
\theta_{0}
are given in (
2.3
) and (
2.6
), respectively.

(ii)
the exponential growth estimate of blowup solutions is given by

\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\geq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)e^{C_{2}t},

where

C_{2}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{p-2}{C_{**}+1},&\hbox{~~~if~}J(u_{0})<0;\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)\left(\theta_{0}^{2}-1\right)}{\theta_{0}^{2}(C_{**}+1)},&\hbox{~~~if~}0\leq J(u_{0})<d.\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

Here,
C_{**}
and
\theta_{0}
are given in (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), respectively.

Proof.

The proof is divided into two parts.

Part 1: Blowup and the upper bounds of blowup time and rate.

For
t\in[0,T)
, we set

\mathcal{H}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right)

(3.6)

and

\mathcal{G}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-J(u),&\hbox{~if~}J(u_{0})<0;\\
\\
\displaystyle d-J(u),&\hbox{~if~}0\leq J(u_{0})<d,\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

If
J(u_{0})<0
, then we obtain from Lemma
2.1
and (
2.1
) that

0<\mathcal{G}(0)\leq\mathcal{G}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

(3.8)

In accordance with (
3.6
), Lemma
2.1
, (
2.1
), (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), one has

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)=-I(u)=\frac{p-2}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-2J(u)\geq p\mathcal{G}(t).

(3.9)

If
0\leq J(u_{0})<d
, then we deduce from (
3.7
), Lemma
2.1
, (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.2
that

\begin{split}0<\mathcal{G}(0)\leq\mathcal{G}(t)&=d-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{p-2}{2p}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}-\frac{1}{2C_{*}^{2}}\|u\|_{p}^{2}+\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\\
&\leq\frac{p-2}{2p}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}-\frac{\theta_{1}^{2}}{2C_{*}^{2}}+\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{p-2}{2p}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}-\frac{1}{2}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}+\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.\end{split}

(3.10)

Noting that (
3.6
), Lemma
2.1
, (
2.1
), (
3.7
), (
2.2
), Lemma
2.2
and (
3.10
), we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)=-I(u)&=\frac{p-2}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}-2J(u)=\frac{p-2}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+2\mathcal{G}(t)-2d\\
&=\frac{p-2}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+2\mathcal{G}(t)-\frac{p-2}{p}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}=\frac{p-2}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+2\mathcal{G}(t)-\frac{p-2}{p}\left(\frac{\theta_{1}}{\theta_{2}}\right)^{p}\theta_{2}^{p}\\
&\geq\frac{p-2}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}+2\mathcal{G}(t)-\frac{p-2}{p\theta_{0}^{p}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\geq\left[\frac{\left(\theta_{0}^{p}-1\right)(p-2)}{\theta_{0}^{p}}+2\right]\mathcal{G}(t).\end{split}

(3.11)

Define
C_{1}
as in (
3.4
). From (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we arrive at

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)\geq C_{1}\mathcal{G}(t)>0.

(3.12)

By Lemma
2.1
, one has

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{t}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{2}^{2},

(3.13)

In accordance with (
3.13
), (
3.6
), Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality and (
3.12
), we reach

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(t)\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(t)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{t}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{uu_{t}}{|x|^{s}}dx\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla u_{t}dx\right)^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{uu_{t}}{|x|^{s}}dx\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla u_{t}dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t))^{2}\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)\mathcal{G}(t),\end{split}

which implies
\frac{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathcal{G}(t)}\geq\frac{C_{1}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)}{2\mathcal{H}(t)}
. Integrating from
0
to
t
on both sides and making use of (
3.12
), we have

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)}{(\mathcal{H}(t))^{\frac{C_{1}}{2}}}\geq\frac{C_{1}\mathcal{G}(0)}{(\mathcal{H}(0))^{\frac{C_{1}}{2}}}.

(3.14)

Integrating (
3.14
) from
0
to
t
yields

(\mathcal{H}(t))^{\frac{2-C_{1}}{2}}\leq(\mathcal{H}(0))^{\frac{2-C_{1}}{2}}-\frac{C_{1}\mathcal{G}(0)(C_{1}-2)}{2(\mathcal{H}(0))^{\frac{C_{1}}{2}}}t.

(3.15)

Owing to
C_{1}>2
, (
3.15
) cannot hold for all
t\geq 0
. Thus,
u
blows up in the finite time
T
, namely,

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\mathcal{H}(t)=\infty
, where

T\leq\frac{2\mathcal{H}(0)}{C_{1}\mathcal{G}(0)(C_{1}-2)},

which gives (
3.2
). Moreover, integrating (
3.14
) from
t
to
T
and using
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\mathcal{H}(t)=\infty
, we reach

\mathcal{H}(t)\leq\left[\frac{C_{1}\mathcal{G}(0)(C_{1}-2)}{2(\mathcal{H}(0))^{\frac{C_{1}}{2}}}\right]^{\frac{2}{2-C_{1}}}(T-t)^{-\frac{2}{C_{1}-2}},

which gives (
3.3
).

Part 2: Exponential growth estimate.

If
J(u_{0})<0
, then by (
3.6
), Lemma
2.1
, (
2.1
) and (
2.4
), it is clear that

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)=-I(u)\geq\frac{p-2}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-pJ(u_{0})>\frac{p-2}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{p-2}{C_{**}+1}\mathcal{H}(t).

If
0\leq J(u_{0})<d
, then we obtain from (
3.6
), Lemma
2.1
, (
2.1
), (
2.2
), Lemma
2.2
and (
2.4
) that

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)&=-I(u)\geq\frac{p-2}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-pJ(u_{0})>\frac{p-2}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{p-2}{2}C_{*}^{-\frac{2p}{p-2}}\\
&=\frac{p-2}{2}\left(\frac{\theta_{1}^{2}}{\theta_{2}^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\theta_{2}^{2}-\theta_{1}^{2}}{\theta_{2}^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\right)-\frac{p-2}{2C_{*}^{2}}\theta_{1}^{2}\geq\frac{(p-2)\left(\theta_{0}^{2}-1\right)}{2\theta_{0}^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{(p-2)\left(\theta_{0}^{2}-1\right)}{\theta_{0}^{2}(C_{**}+1)}\mathcal{H}(t).\end{split}

Then
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)>C_{2}\mathcal{H}(t)
, where
C_{2}
is defined as in (
3.5
). By (
3.6
), one has

\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\geq\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)e^{C_{2}t}.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

(i)
In the case of
J(u_{0})<0
, the upper bound of blowup time in our theorem is equal to the one in
[
5
, Theorem 4.11]
.

(ii)
In the case of
0<J(u_{0})<d
, we conclude that the upper bound of blowup time in our theorem is less than that in
[
5
, Theorem 4.13]
if
\left[\frac{(1-\varepsilon)p+2\varepsilon}{2}\right]^{-\frac{p}{p-2}}<\frac{p-1}{p-2}-\left[\frac{1}{(p-2)^{2}}+\frac{p}{4(p-1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}
, where
\varepsilon\in\left[\frac{J(u_{0})}{d},1\right)
.

The next theorem is about the lower bounds of blowup time and rate.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. If the weak solution
u
blows up in the finite time
T
in the sense of (
3.1
), then

T\geq\frac{1}{C_{*}^{p}(p-2)}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}},

and

\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{C_{*}^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(p-2)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}}(T-t)^{-\frac{2}{p-2}},

(3.16)

where
C_{*}>0
is given in (
2.3
).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be defined as in (
3.6
). From the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\mathcal{H}(t)=\infty
. According to Lemma
2.1
, (
2.1
) and (
2.3
), we discover

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)=-\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq C_{*}^{p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{p}\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{*}^{p}(\mathcal{H}(t))^{\frac{p}{2}},

which gives

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)}{(\mathcal{H}(t))^{\frac{p}{2}}}\leq 2^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{*}^{p}.

(3.17)

Integrating it from
0
to
t
gives

\frac{2}{2-p}(\mathcal{H}(t))^{\frac{2-p}{2}}\leq\frac{2}{2-p}(\mathcal{H}(0))^{\frac{2-p}{2}}+2^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{*}^{p}t.

Letting
t\rightarrow T^{-}
and using
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow T^{-}}\mathcal{H}(t)=\infty
yield the lower bound of blowup time

T\geq\frac{2^{\frac{2-p}{2}}}{C_{*}^{p}(p-2)}(\mathcal{H}(0))^{\frac{2-p}{2}}=\frac{1}{C_{*}^{p}(p-2)}\left(\int_{\Omega}\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{|x|^{s}}dx+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}.

Integrating (
3.17
) from
t
to
T

Homogenization of a multivariate diffusion with semipermeable interfaces
Olga Aryasova, Ilya Pavlyukevich, Andrey Pilipenko

Abstract
We study the homogenization problem for a system of stochastic differential equation with local time terms that models a multivariate diffusion in presence of semipermeable hyperplane interfaces with oblique penetration. We show that this system has a unique weak solution and determine its weak limit as the distances between the interfaces converge to zero. In the limit, the singular local times terms vanish and give rise to an additional regular interface-induced drift.

Setting and the main results

The mathematical homogenization problem usually deals with the study of effective parameters of a system with rapidly varying spatial characteristics.
Its original motivation comes from the analysis of composite materials with periodic structure.
If the period of the structure is very small in comparison to the object’s macroscopic size one can consider the material as a new homogeneous substance.
A typical example here is the analysis of material’s thermal conductivity. In mathematical terms, one solves
a boundary value problem
-\nabla(A(x/\varepsilon)\nabla u^{\varepsilon}(x))=f
in some domain
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

with certain boundary conditions on
\partial G
. The matrix (thermal conductivity tensor)
A(\cdot)
is assumed to be periodic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the
parameter
\varepsilon>0
is small. The solution
u^{\varepsilon}
gives the temperature distribution in the domain
G
.
The goal of homogenization consists in determining the
effective

thermal conductivity
A
such that
u^{\varepsilon}\to u
as
\varepsilon\to 0
where
u
is the solution of the homogenized equation
A\Delta u=f
.
There is vast literature devoted to this subject see, e.g., Bensoussan et al.
[
1
]
, Jikov et al.
[
16
]
, and Berdichevsky et al.
[
2
]
, Chechkin et al.
[
5
]
that comprises both analytic and stochastic methods.

In the present paper, we consider a different type of a stochastic homogenization problem:
homogenization of a diffusion in the presence of narrowly
located semipermeable hyperplane interfaces.
In simple words, our model may remind of a dynamics of heat conduction in a
foiled
composite material consisting of a media interlaced with
very thin plates of different permeability. In material science such models are referred to as reinforced materials like
a glass wool reinforced by aluminium foil, as it was considered
in Yüksel et al.
[
43
]
.

Such systems also appear in chemistry, biology and physics, see, e.g., Tanner
[
40
]
,
Dudko et al.
[

]
,

[

]
,

[

]
,

[

]
.

To formulate the model rigorously, we introduce a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
and
assume that the state space
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, is sliced into layers by countably many
hyperplane interfaces (membranes)

\displaystyle\{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

(1.1)

such that the family
\{a^{\varepsilon}_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
has no accumulation points.

We look for a
n+1
dimensional continuous strong Markov process

(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})=(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon,1},\dots,Y^{\varepsilon,n})
,
X^{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}
,
Y^{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that behaves as follows.

Between the membranes
\{\{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

the process
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
is a usual diffusion with the drift vector
(b^{i})_{0\leq i\leq n}
and
the diffusion matrix
(\sigma_{l}^{i})_{0\leq i\leq n,1\leq l\leq m}
,
m\geq 1

Upon hitting a membrane
\{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
at a point

(a_{k}^{\varepsilon},y)
at time
\tau
,
the process
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
starts anew and ‘leaves’ the membrane in the direction
\pm(1,\theta(a_{k}^{\varepsilon},y))

with
the so-called penetration probabilities

\frac{1}{2}(1\pm\varepsilon\beta(a_{k}^{\varepsilon},y))
.

We look for such a process
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
as a solution of the
stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\displaystyle X^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=x+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{l}^{0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b^{0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\beta(a_{k}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}L^{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}}_{s}(X^{\varepsilon}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}

\displaystyle=y^{i}+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{i}_{l}(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b^{i}(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\beta(a_{k}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\theta^{i}(a_{k}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}L^{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}}_{s}(X^{\varepsilon}),

\displaystyle i=1,\dots,n,

where
W=(W^{1},\dots,W^{m})
is a standard
m
-dimensional Brownian motion and

L^{a}(X^{\varepsilon})
is the symmetric local time of
X^{\varepsilon}
at
a\in\mathbb{R}
(see the end of this Section for precise definitions).

The case
n=0
corresponds to a one-dimensional diffusion
X^{\varepsilon}
and point interfaces
\{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}
located on the real line.

The goal of this paper is to establish the existence and uniqueness of a weak solution of the system (
1.2
) and to study
its
homogenization
limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We make the following assumptions about the coefficients in the system (
1.2
) and the location of the membranes.
By
C^{k}_{b}

k

Assumptions A:

A
{}_{\text{coeff}}
:

\displaystyle\beta,b^{i},\theta^{i},\sigma^{i}_{l}\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}),\quad i=0,\dots,n,\ l=1,\dots,m.

(1.3)

A
a
:
The points
\{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}
satisfy

\displaystyle a^{\varepsilon}_{k}=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon k}d(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

A

The main result of this paper is formulated in the following theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let Assumptions
A
hold true. Then there is
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1]
small enough such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]

the system (

Remark 1.2

The condition that the parameter
\varepsilon>0
is small is essential. First, since the values

have the meaning of penetration probabilities through the membranes, we have to demand that

does not exist even in the

[

Another condition on the smallness of
\varepsilon
will be needed for the proof of
uniqueness of the solution in a neighbourhood of each membrane
a^{\varepsilon}_{k}

2

Theorem 1.3
.

Let Assumptions
A
hold true and let
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
be unique weak solutions of (
1.2
). In the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
the weak convergence

\displaystyle(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})\Rightarrow(X,Y)

(1.6)

in
C(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
holds true, where
(X,Y)
is a (unique) weak solution of the SDE

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=x+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{l}^{0}(X_{s},Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(b^{0}(X_{s},Y_{s})+\beta(X_{s},Y_{s})\frac{\Sigma^{00}(X_{s},Y_{s})}{d(X_{s})}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(1.7)

\displaystyle Y^{i}_{t}

\displaystyle=y^{i}+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{i}_{l}(X_{s},Y_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(b^{i}(X_{s},Y_{s})+\beta(X_{s},Y_{s})\theta^{i}(X_{s},Y_{s})\frac{\Sigma^{00}(X_{s},Y_{s})}{d(X_{s})}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

\displaystyle\quad i=1,\dots,n.

As we see, the limiting process is a diffusion with the same diffusion matrix
\{\sigma^{i}_{l}\}
as the processes
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
.
The semipermeable membranes, i.e., the local time terms of (

additional drift which can alter diffusion’s behaviour significantly as it is demonstrated in the following example.

Example 1.4
.

Let

. We consider a two-dimensional stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle X^{0}_{t}

\displaystyle=x+W^{1}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}^{0}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(1.8)

\displaystyle Y^{0}_{t}

\displaystyle=y+W^{2}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}^{0}\,\mathrm{d}s,

that describes, e.g., the velocity of a charged particle in a constant magnetic field subject to external stochastic electric field

[

. The random trajectory of a particle tends to make windings around 0 in the positive direction, see Fig.

Let us add to the model spatial membranes (lines) located equidistantly at

,

, so that

Assume that in some large ball around the origin the permeability characteristics

given by

\displaystyle\beta(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{2y^{3}}{\gamma+y^{2}},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\theta(x,y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x^{3}y}{(\gamma^{2}+x^{2})(\gamma+y^{2})},\quad\gamma=10^{-2}.

Outside this ball we define them in such a way that they satisfy the assumptions

\displaystyle=x+W_{t}^{1}-\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{2Y^{3}_{s}}{\gamma+Y^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(1.10)

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=y+W^{2}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}X_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{2X_{s}^{3}Y_{s}^{4}}{(\gamma^{2}+X^{2}_{s})(\gamma+Y^{2}_{s})^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

The novelty of this paper, besides the stochastic homogenization Theorem
1.3
, consists in the proof of the existence and uniqueness
Theorem
1.1
in a
multivariate
setting.

In dimension one, the first result on a existence and uniqueness for an SDE with local time was obtained by
[
14
]
. They proved that a skew Brownian motion is a unique strong solution to an SDE
X_{t}=x+W_{t}+(2\beta-1)L^{0}_{t}(X)
for
|\beta|\leq 1
. In the proof, by using the speed measure the initial equation was transformed regularly into an equivalent one with the locally constant coefficients and without local times. Later, Le Gall

[
20
]
generalized this approach and applied it to the general one-dimensional SDEs with local times of the unknown process. The resulting SDE has possibly discontinuous coefficients but does not involve local times. The general theory was outlined in
Engelbert and Schmidt
[
7
]
, see also Lejay
[
21
]
.

In higher dimensions, diffusion processes with reflection (i.e., with
\varepsilon\beta(x,y)=1
) are best studied. The existence problem for them is usually formulated in terms of a martingale problem (see, e.g., Stroock and Varadhan
[
38
]
) or the Skorokhod problem (see, e.g.,
Tanaka

[
39
]
, and
Lions and Sznitman

[
22
]
). Zaitseva
[
44
]
considered an SDE for a multidimensional Brownian motion with oblique skewness at a hyperplane
provided that the skewing coefficient and the reflection direction are constant. Under this condition, the tangential coordinates do not depend
on the normal one. This allows to obtain the strong existence and uniqueness of a solution by standard arguments.
Unfortunately, this method is inapplicable in the case of oblique reflection since the coordinates are necessarily dependent.

The general case of an oblique skew diffusion with non-constant coefficients is much more complicated and the question of existence and uniqueness is still open.
Portenko
[
33
,
34
]
formulated the problem of existence and uniqueness of a diffusion process with semipermeable membranes in term of a parabolic conjugation boundary problem.
With the help of potential theory methods it was proved that the solution of such a problem
defines the semigroup of operators that corresponds to a process which diffusion characteristics exist in the sense of generalized functions.
Portenko and Kopytko

[
32
]
obtained the same result for a
diffusion with a semipermeable membrane on a hyperplane with oblique reflection.
However it does not follow immediately from these works that the constructed process is a (unique) solution of an SDE with local time terms.

We also mention the works by Ouknine et al.
[
28
]
and Ramirez
[
35
]
who
investigated diffusions with infinitely many interfaces in dimensions one and two.

Concerning the homogenization problem, besides the works cited in the beginning of the paper,
the following authors studied homogenization of (regular) diffusions by analytic and stochastic methods:
Pardoux
[
29
]
,
Pavliotis and Stuart
[
30
]
,
Hairer and Pardoux

[
13
]
,
Makhno
[
23
]
.

Results on homogenization for diffusions with interfaces are rather sparse. Weinryb
[
41
]

studied the periodic homogenization of planar diffusions with permeable membranes on lines or circles under the assumption on existence and uniqueness of solution to the corresponding martingale problem.
Hairer and Manson
[
11
,
12
,
10
]
studied periodic diffusion homogenization with one interface.
Limit theorems for one-dimensional diffusions with interfaces were obtained by Makhno
[
24
,
25
]

and Krykun
[
18
]
.

Sketch of the proof.

The main idea of the proof is based on the decomposition of the solution
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
into segments that live on random time intervals between the
subsequent hittings of the neighbouring membranes.
The existence and uniqueness result (Theorem
1.1
) for the system (
1.2
) will be obtained with the help of a
multivariate coordinate transformation
(U^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon})=(F^{\varepsilon}(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}),G(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}))
that transforms the system (
1.2
)
into an equivalent system of SDEs with discontinuous coefficients
without local times
in a neighbourhood of each membrane.
The uniqueness will follow from the result by Gao
[
8
]

and the global solution is obtained by glueing these solutions together (see Section
2
).

To prove the convergence Theorem
1.3
we note that the essential dynamics of the diffusion with local times
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
is catched by the embedded
Markov chain that comprises the values
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
at the hitting times of the membranes.

To obtain the formula for the infinitesimal operator
\mathcal{L}
of the limit diffusion we study the pseudo-characteristic operator of the embedded Markov chain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}f(x,y):=\frac{\mathbf{E}_{x,y}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}},Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})-f(x,y)}{\mathbf{E}_{x,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}},\quad x\in\{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.11)

where
\tau^{\varepsilon}
is the first hitting time of a neighbouring membrane.

Let for brevity
n=1
. Writing the Taylor formula for a function

we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x,y}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}},Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})-f(x,y)

\displaystyle=f_{x}(x,y)\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-x)+f_{y}(x,y)\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)

(1.12)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(x,y)\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-x)^{2}+f_{xy}(x,y)\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-x)(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{yy}(x,y)\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)^{2}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\mathbf{E}_{x,y}|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|^{3}+\mathbf{E}_{x,y}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|^{3}\Big)

We will be able to calculate the fine asymptotics of the average exit time from the current strip
\mathbf{E}\tau^{\varepsilon}
, which will be of the order
\varepsilon^{2}

as well as the
moments
\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(X_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}-x)
,
\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(Y_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}-y)
, etc, with accuracy
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-\delta})
,
\delta\in(0,1)

(see Lemma
3.5
and
Section
3.4
).
These estimates will be derived by approximating the transformed diffusion without local times by a diffusion with
coefficients
“frozen” at the initial point
(x,y)
.

The limit second order generator
\mathcal{L}
that determines the limit diffusion
(X,Y)
,
see equation (
1.7
), is a pointwise limit of the sequence
\{\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}\}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The proof of the convergence Theorem
1.3
consists of
a) the standard step of establishing the weak relative compactness of the family
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
(Section

)
and b) of verification that any limit process

satisfies the (well-posed) martingale problem

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[f(X_{t},Y_{t})-f(X_{s},Y_{s})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{L}f(X_{u},Y_{u})\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|\mathscr{F}_{s}\Big]=0,\quad f\in C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathbb{R}).

(1.13)

Existence and uniqueness. Proof of Theorem
1.1

First, we consider the system (
1.2
) in a neighbourhood of one membrane. Without loss of generality we fix
k=0
so that

a_{0}^{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
. Since outside the membrane
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
is a diffusion with regular coefficients, we assume that the initial values are
x=0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
With some abuse of notation we denote
\beta(y):=\beta(0,y)
,
\theta(y):=\theta(0,y)

and look for the solution
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
of the following SDE with one membrane:

\displaystyle X^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{l}^{0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b^{0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\beta(Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}L^{0}_{s}(X^{\varepsilon}),

(2.1)

\displaystyle Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}

\displaystyle=y^{i}+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma^{i}_{l}(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}b^{i}(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\beta(Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\theta^{i}(Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}L^{0}_{s}(X^{\varepsilon}),\quad i=1,\dots,n.

We construct the transformation of
(X^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon})
into a diffusion without the local time terms. Let
\varepsilon>0

be small enough such that
\varepsilon\|\beta\|_{\infty}\leq 1/2
,
and let

\displaystyle B^{\varepsilon}(y):=\frac{1-\varepsilon\beta(y)}{1+\varepsilon\beta(y)},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.2)

We have

\displaystyle|B^{\varepsilon}(y)-1|

\displaystyle\leq 4\varepsilon\|\beta\|_{\infty},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla B^{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 8\varepsilon\|\nabla\beta\|_{\infty}.

(2.4)

Define the functions

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}(x,y)

\displaystyle=x\mathbb{I}_{(-\infty,0)}(x)+xB^{\varepsilon}(y)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(x)=x+x^{+}(B^{\varepsilon}(y)-1);

(2.5)

\displaystyle G(x,y)

\displaystyle:=y-x\theta(y).

It is clear that the transformation
(x,y)\mapsto(F^{\varepsilon}(x,y),G(x,y))
does not have to be a one-to-one bijection of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
However, this map is injection in a “thin” strip

, for any

The proof will consist in the application of the Hadamard’s global inverse function theorem, see Theorem 6.2.4 in Krantz and Parks
[
17
]

Theorem 2.1

(Hadamard)
.

Let

,
H\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

and that the Jacobian determinant of

is nonzero at each point. Finally, assume

\displaystyle\|(DH)^{-1}(\cdot)\|_{\infty}<\infty.

Then

.

For any

\varepsilon>0

Moreover, for

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\int_{0}^{\widecheck{\tau}^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\sigma_{l}^{0}(X_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})+\phi_{l}^{\varepsilon,0}(X_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\widecheck{A}\varepsilon\delta_{2}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\widecheck{\tau}^{\varepsilon}+3\sum_{l=1}^{m}\|\sigma_{l}^{0}\|^{2}_{\infty}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\widecheck{\tau}^{\varepsilon}

and the lower bound for
\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}
follows.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For each
k\geq 1
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|V^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|^{2k}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2k},

(3.34)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|^{2k}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2k}.

In particular, for any
\delta\in(0,1)
and any
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Big(\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|V^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|\geq\varepsilon^{1-\delta}\Big)\lesssim\varepsilon^{\infty},

(3.35)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Big(\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|\geq\varepsilon^{1-\delta}\Big)\lesssim\varepsilon^{\infty}.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle V^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}=y^{i}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(b^{i}-\theta^{i}b^{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{i}_{y^{j}}\Sigma^{0j}+\psi^{i}\Big)(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.36)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sigma_{l}^{i}-\theta^{i}\sigma_{l}^{0}+\psi_{l}^{i}\Big)(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s}.

With the help of the Doob inequality and (
2.20
), we estimate for each
i=1,\dots,n
and
k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq\widehat{\tau}^{\varepsilon}}(V^{\varepsilon,i}_{t}-y^{i})^{2k}

(3.37)

\displaystyle\leq(m+1)^{2k-1}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\widehat{\tau}^{\varepsilon}}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Big(b^{i}-\theta^{i}b^{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{i}_{y^{j}}\Sigma^{0j}+\psi^{i}\Big)(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|^{2k}

\displaystyle+(m+1)^{2k-1}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\widehat{\tau}^{\varepsilon}}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sigma_{l}^{i}-\theta^{i}\sigma_{l}^{0}+\psi_{l}^{i}\Big)(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s}\Big|^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\mathbf{E}\Big[(\widehat{\tau}^{\varepsilon})^{2k-1}\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}^{\varepsilon}}\Big((b^{i}-\theta^{i}b^{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta^{i}_{y^{j}}\Sigma^{0j}+\psi^{i})(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\Big)^{2k}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]

\displaystyle+C_{2}\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbf{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{\widehat{\tau}^{\varepsilon}}\Big((\sigma_{l}^{i}-\theta^{i}\sigma_{l}^{0}+\psi_{l}^{i})(X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big]^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[(\widehat{\tau}^{\varepsilon})^{k}+(\widehat{\tau}^{\varepsilon})^{2k}\Big]\leq C_{4}\varepsilon^{2k}.

By Markov’s inequality, for any
k\geq 1
and
\delta\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{0,y}\Big(\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|V^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|\geq\varepsilon^{1-\delta}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{0,y}\Big(\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|V^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|^{2k}\geq\varepsilon^{2k-2k\delta}\Big)

(3.38)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-2k+2k\delta}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|V^{\varepsilon}(t)-y|^{2k}\leq C_{5}\varepsilon^{2k\delta}.

Choosing
k
large enough, the order of the r.h.s. can be made arbitrarily small.

The estimates for
Y^{\varepsilon}
follow from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

3.3
Accurate estimates for
X^{\varepsilon}

Recall (
3.19
) and decompose the process
U^{\varepsilon}
into the sum

\displaystyle U^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=R_{t}+A_{t}^{\varepsilon}+M_{t}^{\varepsilon},

(3.39)

with

\displaystyle R_{t}

\displaystyle=b^{0}(y)t+\sum_{l=1}^{m}\sigma_{l}^{0}(y)W^{l}_{t},

(3.40)

\displaystyle A_{t}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\partial_{x}b^{0}(s)X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{y^{i}}b^{0}(s)\cdot(Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{i})+\phi^{\varepsilon,0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(3.41)

\displaystyle M^{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\partial_{x}\sigma_{l}^{0}(s)X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{y^{i}}\sigma_{l}^{0}(s)(Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{i})+\phi_{l}^{\varepsilon,0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W^{l}_{s},

(3.42)

where the terms
\partial_{x}b^{0}(s)
,
\partial_{y^{i}}b^{0}(s)
, etc. are bounded by Assumption
A
.
In the representation (
3.39
), the process
R
is a Brownian motion with drift, and
A^{\varepsilon}
and
M^{\varepsilon}
are “small” on
t\in[0,\tau^{\varepsilon}]
as shown in the
next Lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\varepsilon>0
small enough we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|A^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|\lesssim\varepsilon^{3},

(3.43)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|M^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}.

Proof.

To obtain these estimates, we use Remark
2.4
, Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
as well as the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|A^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(|\partial_{x}b^{0}(s)X^{\varepsilon}_{s}|+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\partial_{y^{i}}b^{0}(s)\cdot(Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{s}-y^{i})|+|\phi^{\varepsilon,0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})|\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.44)

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}\Big(\varepsilon+\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}+\Big(\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|\tau^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}.

To estimate the martingale
M^{\varepsilon}
we apply the Doob inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|M^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\partial_{x}\sigma_{l}^{0}(s)X^{\varepsilon}_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{y^{i}}\sigma_{l}^{0}(s)\cdot(Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{s}-y^{i})+\phi_{l}^{\varepsilon,0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.45)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}+\Big(\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|\tau^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{t\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}-y|^{4}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim\varepsilon^{4}.

∎

We will prove that the exit probabilities of
X^{\varepsilon}
from
(-a_{-}^{\varepsilon},a_{+}^{\varepsilon})
coincide
with the exit probabilities of the Brownian motion with drift

R
from the modified interval
(-a_{-}^{\varepsilon},a_{+}^{\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y)))
up to the terms of the order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2-\delta})

and calculate the moments of
X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}
with accuracy of the order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-\delta})
. Note that the local time terms
that disappeared upon transition to the process
U^{\varepsilon}

contribute now to the asymmetry of the exit interval.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\delta\in(0,1)
and for
\varepsilon>0
small enough we have

\displaystyle p_{\pm}^{\varepsilon}:=p_{\pm}^{\varepsilon}(y)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{0,y}\Big(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}=\pm a^{\varepsilon}_{\pm}\Big)=\frac{1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{b^{0}(y)d}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}+\beta(y)-\frac{d^{\prime}}{2d}\Big)\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2-\delta}),

(3.46)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\beta(y)d+\frac{b^{0}(y)d^{2}}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}\Big)\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-\delta}),

(3.47)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\left(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-\delta}),

(3.48)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}).

(3.49)

Proof.

1. On the one hand we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=-a_{-}^{\varepsilon}p_{-}^{\varepsilon}+a_{+}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}[B^{\varepsilon}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})\mathbb{I}(X_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon})].

(3.50)

The Taylor formula for the mapping
(\varepsilon,Y)\mapsto B^{\varepsilon}(Y)
yields

\displaystyle B^{\varepsilon}(Y)=1-2\varepsilon\beta(y)+r(\varepsilon,Y-y),

(3.51)

where the remainder is estimated by

\displaystyle|r(\varepsilon,Y-y)|\leq C(\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon|Y-y|+\varepsilon|Y-y|^{2})

(3.52)

for some
C>0
.
Hence we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[B^{\varepsilon}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})\mathbb{I}(X_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon})\Big]

\displaystyle=p_{+}^{\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y))+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[r(\varepsilon,Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)\mathbb{I}(X_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon})\Big]

(3.53)

and for
\delta\in(0,1)
with the help of Lemma
3.3
we estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[r(\varepsilon,Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)\mathbb{I}(X_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon})\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|r(\varepsilon,Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)|=\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[|r(\varepsilon,Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)|\mathbb{I}(|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|<\varepsilon^{1-\delta})\Big]

(3.54)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[|r(\varepsilon,Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)|\mathbb{I}(|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|\geq\varepsilon^{1-\delta})\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim C\varepsilon(\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{2-\delta}+\varepsilon^{3-2\delta})

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|\mathbb{I}(|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|\geq\varepsilon^{1-\delta})\Big]+\varepsilon\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|^{2}\mathbb{I}(|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|\geq\varepsilon^{1-\delta})\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{3-\delta}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\infty})\lesssim\varepsilon^{3-\delta}.

Hence using (
3.1
) we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=-a_{-}^{\varepsilon}p_{-}^{\varepsilon}+a_{+}^{\varepsilon}p_{+}^{\varepsilon}\Big(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y)\Big)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-\delta})

(3.55)

\displaystyle=-(1-p_{+}^{\varepsilon})\Big(d\varepsilon-\frac{d^{\prime}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\Big)+p_{+}^{\varepsilon}\Big(d\varepsilon+\frac{d^{\prime}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\Big)\Big(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y)\Big)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-\delta})

\displaystyle=2p_{+}d\varepsilon-d\varepsilon+\frac{d^{\prime}\varepsilon^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-\delta}).

2. On the other hand, taking into account (
3.39
) and (
3.41
) we estimate

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}U_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}A^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}

(3.56)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+\Big(\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}),

where

\displaystyle\rho^{\varepsilon}:=\inf\Big\{t\geq 0\colon R_{t}\notin(-a_{-}^{\varepsilon},a_{+}^{\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y)))\Big\}.

(3.57)

To calculate
\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}
,
use the explicit formula 3.0.4 (a) on p. 309 from Borodin and Salminen
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{0,y}(R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=a_{-}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{b^{0}(y)a_{-}^{\varepsilon}}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}}\frac{\sinh\Big(a_{+}^{\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y))\frac{|b^{0}(y)|}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}\Big)}{\sinh\Big((a_{+}^{\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y))+a_{-}^{\varepsilon})\frac{|b^{0}(y)|}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}\Big)}

(3.58)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(-\frac{b^{0}(y)d}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}-\beta(y)+\frac{d^{\prime}}{2d}\Big)\varepsilon+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})

that gives us the asymptotics

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=-a_{-}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{P}_{0,y}(R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=a_{-}^{\varepsilon})+a_{+}^{\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y))\mathbf{P}_{0,y}(R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y)))

(3.59)

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{0}(y)d^{2}}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}).

The mean value
\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\rho^{\varepsilon}
is obtained with the help of the formula 3.0.1 on p. 309 from Borodin and Salminen
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\rho^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{2}}{\Sigma^{00}(y)}\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}).

(3.60)

Note that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|\leq\|b\|_{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|\tau^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}|.

(3.61)

Hence by Lemma
3.6
(below) we get the estimate

\displaystyle|\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-\mathbf{E}_{0,y}R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}|\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}.

(3.62)

Combining (
3.56
) and (
3.59
) we get (
3.46
) and hence immediately (
3.47
) and (
3.48
).
The asymptotics (
3.49
) follows from (
3.60
) and Lemma
3.6
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For
\varepsilon>0
small enough we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|\tau^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}|\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}.

(3.63)

Proof.

1.
Let
\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon}(1-2\varepsilon\beta(y))
and

\displaystyle h(u)=(\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-u)(u+a_{-}^{\varepsilon}),

(3.64)

so that
h^{\prime}(u)=-2u+\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-a_{-}^{\varepsilon}
,
h^{\prime\prime}(u)=-2
.
The Itô formula on the event
\{\rho^{\varepsilon}\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}\}

yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[h(U_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]

\displaystyle=h(U^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[\int_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}(-2U_{s}^{\varepsilon}+a_{+}^{\varepsilon}-a_{-}^{\varepsilon})(b^{0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\phi^{\varepsilon,0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}))\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]

(3.65)

\displaystyle-\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[\int_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\sigma_{l}^{0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})+\phi_{l}^{\varepsilon,0}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq h(U^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})+C_{1}\varepsilon\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[\tau^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]-\delta_{1}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big[\tau^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]

where we used that
\sum_{l=1}^{m}\sigma_{l}^{0}(x,y)^{2}\geq\delta_{1}>0
,

|b^{0}(x,y)+\phi^{\varepsilon,0}(x,y)|\leq\delta_{2}
,
and
|U^{\varepsilon}_{t}|\leq C\varepsilon
, for some
C>0
fixed and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently
small. We get

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}\mathbf{E}\Big[\tau^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big]\leq h(U^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})-\mathbf{E}\Big[h(U_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\Big].

(3.66)

We also have

\displaystyle h(U^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})

\displaystyle=\Big(\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-(A^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+M^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})\Big)\Big(R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+a_{-}^{\varepsilon}+(A^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+M^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})\Big)

(3.67)

\displaystyle\leq(\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})(R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+a_{-}^{\varepsilon})+(A^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+M^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})(\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-2R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-a_{-}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=(A^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}+M^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})(\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-2R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}}-a_{-}^{\varepsilon})

The second factor in the latter formula is of the order

\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
, and by Lemma
3.4

we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|h(U^{\varepsilon}_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}.

(3.68)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|h_{\varepsilon}(U_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})|

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big|(\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})(U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}+a_{-}^{\varepsilon})\Big|

(3.69)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big|(\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})(U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}+a_{-}^{\varepsilon})\Big|\mathbb{I}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big|\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon}-U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big|\mathbb{I}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\Big|1-2\varepsilon\beta(y)-B^{\varepsilon}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})\Big|\mathbb{I}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|r(\varepsilon,Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y)|\mathbb{I}(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}=a_{+}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{4-\delta}.

2.
On the event
\{\tau^{\varepsilon}\leq\rho^{\varepsilon}\}
we have analogously:

\displaystyle R_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}=U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-M_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}-A_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon},

(3.70)

and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\Big[h(R_{\rho^{\varepsilon}})\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big]=h(R_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})+b(y)\mathbf{E}\Big[\int^{\rho^{\varepsilon}}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}(-2R_{s}^{\varepsilon}+a_{+}^{\varepsilon}-a_{-}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big]-\Sigma^{00}(y)\mathbf{E}\Big[\int^{\rho^{\varepsilon}}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big]

(3.71)

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\Sigma^{00}(y)}{2}\mathbf{E}\Big[\rho^{\varepsilon}-\tau^{\varepsilon}\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq h(R_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})+(A_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}+M_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}))(U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}+a_{-}^{\varepsilon}-(A_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}+M_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}))

(3.72)

\displaystyle\leq h(U_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})+(A_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}+M_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})(2U^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}+a_{-}^{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{a}_{+}^{\varepsilon})

and the estimates on
h(U_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})
and
A_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}
, and

M_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}
apply as in the previous step.

Combining the estimates in steps 1 and 2 yields
(
3.63
).
∎

3.4
Accurate estimates containing
Y^{\varepsilon}

In this section we sketch the derivation of the expectations

\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(Y^{i,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{i})(Y^{j,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{j})
,

\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(Y^{i,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{i})X_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}
, and

\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(Y^{i,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{i})
.

1. For
1\leq i,j\leq n
we have:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(Y^{i,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{i})(Y^{j,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{j})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(V^{i,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{i}+\theta^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau})X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})(V^{j,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{j}+\theta^{j}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})

(3.73)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(V^{i,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{i})(V^{j,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{j})

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(V^{i,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{i})\theta^{j}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\theta^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}(V^{j,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y_{j})

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\theta^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})\theta^{j}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}.

Let us apply the Itô formula, Lemma
3.2
, and Lemma
3.3
. We write only the essential terms; the
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})
estimate follows from the bounds of Remark
2.4
):

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(V^{i,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{i})(V^{j,\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\sigma^{i}_{l}(X_{s},Y_{s})-\theta^{i}(Y_{s})\sigma_{l}^{0}(X_{s},Y_{s})\Big)\Big(\sigma_{l}^{j}(X_{s},Y_{s})-\theta^{j}(Y_{s})\sigma_{l}^{0}(X_{s},Y_{s})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})

(3.74)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{m}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\sigma_{l}^{i}(0,y)-\theta^{i}(y)\sigma_{l}^{0}(0,y)\Big)\Big(\sigma_{l}^{j}(0,y)-\theta^{j}(y)\sigma_{l}^{0}(0,y)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle=\Big(\Sigma^{ij}(y)-\theta^{i}(y)\Sigma^{0j}(y)-\theta^{j}(y)\Sigma^{0i}(y)+\theta^{i}(y)\theta^{j}(y)\Sigma^{00}(y)\Big)\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3}).

Applying the Taylor formula to
\theta^{i}
,
\theta^{j}
and using (
3.48
) and (
3.49
) we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\theta^{i}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})\theta^{j}(Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\theta^{i}(y)\theta^{j}(y)\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|^{2}|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|^{2})

(3.75)

\displaystyle=\theta^{i}(y)\theta^{j}(y)d^{2}\varepsilon^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3-\delta})+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{4})

\eta>0
and
f\in C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\mathbb{R})
. Then for
\varepsilon>0
small enough

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\mathcal{L}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{u},Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})-\eta\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\mathbf{E}_{x,y}f(X_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon},Y_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})-f(x,y)\leq\mathbf{E}_{x,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\mathcal{L}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{u},Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})+\eta\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u

(4.34)

uniformly over
x\in\{a_{k}^{\varepsilon}\}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let for definiteness
x=0
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The Taylor formula yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}},Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})-f(0,y)

\displaystyle=f_{x}(0,y)\mathbf{E}_{0,y}X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{y^{i}}(0,y)\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{i})

(4.35)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}f_{xx}(0,y)\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{xy^{i}}(0,y)\mathbf{E}_{0,y}X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}(Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}f_{y^{i}y^{j}}(0,y)\mathbf{E}_{0,y}(Y^{\varepsilon,i}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{i})(Y^{\varepsilon,j}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y^{j})+Q^{\varepsilon}

where

\displaystyle|Q^{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|^{3}+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|^{3}+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|+\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}||Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}}-y|^{2}

(4.36)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}\leq\frac{\eta}{3}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}.

Taking into account the asymptotics from Sections
3.3
and
3.4
and estimates from Lemma
3.3
we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0,y}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}},Y^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}})-f(0,y)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathcal{L}f(0,y)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{1-\delta})\Big)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}

(4.37)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\mathcal{L}f(0,y)+\frac{\eta}{3}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\mathcal{L}f(X_{u}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{u})+\mathcal{L}f(0,y)-\mathcal{L}f(X_{u}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{u})+\frac{\eta}{3}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u.

Since
(x,y)\mapsto\mathcal{L}f(x,y)
is Lipschitz continuous, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{u\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|\mathcal{L}f(0,y)-\mathcal{L}f(X_{u}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{u})|\leq C\Big(\varepsilon+\sup_{u\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{u}-y|\Big)

(4.38)

and thus by Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\varepsilon}}\Big(\mathcal{L}f(0,y)-\mathcal{L}f(X_{u}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{u})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}+\Big(\mathbf{E}_{0,y}|\tau^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\sup_{u\leq\tau^{\varepsilon}}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{u}-y|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(4.39)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}\leq\frac{\eta}{3}\mathbf{E}_{0,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}.

The estimate from below follows analogously.
∎

Now we prove (
4.33
).
For
s\geq 0
, let
\tau^{\varepsilon}(s)
be the first hitting time of a membrane after
s
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tau^{\varepsilon}(s)=\tau_{\nu_{s}^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon}=\min\{\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}\colon\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}\geq s\}.

(4.40)

We estimate the conditional expectation:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[f(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle-f(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{L}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{u},Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\varepsilon}\Big]

(4.41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbf{E}\Big[\mathbb{I}_{\{s<\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}\leq t\}}\Big(f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}},Y_{\tau_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})-f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}},Y_{\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})-\int_{\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}}^{\tau_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{L}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{u},Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}u\Big)\Big|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\varepsilon}\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbf{E}\Big[f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}(s)},Y_{\tau^{\varepsilon}(s)}^{\varepsilon})-f(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\int_{s}^{{\tau^{\varepsilon}(s)}}\mathcal{L}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{u},Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\varepsilon}\Big]

\displaystyle-\mathbf{E}\Big[f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}(t)},Y_{\tau^{\varepsilon}(t)}^{\varepsilon})-f(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})-\int_{t}^{{\tau^{\varepsilon}(t)}}\mathcal{L}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{u},Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\varepsilon}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbf{E}\Big[\mathbb{I}_{\{s<\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}\leq t\}}\mathbf{E}\Big(f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}},Y_{\tau_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})-f(X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}},Y_{\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}}^{\varepsilon})-\int_{\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}}^{\tau_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{L}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{u},Y_{u}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}u\Big|\mathscr{F}_{\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\Big|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\varepsilon}\Big]

\displaystyle+C\sup_{x,y}\mathbf{E}_{x,y}\Big[|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}(0)}-x|+|Y_{\tau^{\varepsilon}(0)}^{\varepsilon}-y|+\tau^{\varepsilon}(0)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\delta\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbf{E}\Big[\mathbb{I}_{\{s<\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon}\leq t\}}(\tau_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}-\tau_{k}^{\varepsilon})\Big|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\varepsilon}\Big]+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\delta\mathbf{E}\Big[\tau^{\varepsilon}(t)-\tau^{\varepsilon}(s)\Big|\mathscr{F}_{s}\Big]+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\delta(t-s)+\delta\sup_{x,y}\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(\tau^{\varepsilon}(t)-t)+\delta\sup_{x,y}\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(\tau^{\varepsilon}(s)-s)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)=\delta(t-s)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).

Here we used (
4.9
) implies the estimates

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y}\mathbf{E}_{x,y}(\tau^{\varepsilon}(t)-t)=\sup_{x,y}\mathbf{E}_{x,y}\tau^{\varepsilon}(0)\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}

(4.42)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{x,y}\mathbf{E}_{x,y}\Big[|X^{\varepsilon}_{\tau^{\varepsilon}(0)}-x|+|Y_{\tau^{\varepsilon}(0)}^{\varepsilon}-y|\Big]\lesssim\varepsilon.

(4.43)

A similar estimate from below holds true, too, hence multiplying this conditional expectation by the functions
h_{1},\dots,h_{l}
and taking expectation
yields the limit (
4.33
).

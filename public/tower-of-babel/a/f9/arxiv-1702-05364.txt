Abstract
The contribution of O 2 − O^{2-} ions to antiferromagnetism in L ​ a 2 − x ​ A ​ e x ​ C ​ u ​ O 4 La_{2-x}Ae_{x}CuO_{4} ( OPEN A ​ e = S ​ r , B ​ a ) Ae=Sr,Ba) is highly sensitive to doped holes. The difference causes the precarious and, respectively, robust antiferromagnetic phase of these cuprates. The same sensitivities affect the doping dependence of the incommensurability of density waves, δ ⁡ ( x ) \delta(x) . In the hole-doped compounds this gives rise to a doping offset for magnetic and charge density waves, δ m , c p ​ ( x ) ∝ x − x 0 ​ p N \delta_{m,c}^{p}(x)\propto\sqrt{x-x_{0p}^{N}} . Here x 0 ​ p N x_{0p}^{N} is the doping concentration where the Néel temperature vanishes, T N ​ ( x 0 ​ p N ) = 0 T_{N}(x_{0p}^{N})=0 . No such doping offset occurs for density waves in the electron-doped compound. Instead, excess oxygen (necessary for stability in crystal growth) of concentration y y causes a different doping offset in the latter case, δ m , c n ​ ( x ) ∝ x − 2 ​ y \delta_{m,c}^{n}(x)\propto\sqrt{x-2y} . The square-root formulas result from the assumption of superlattice formation through partitioning of the C ​ u ​ O 2 CuO_{2} plane by pairs of itinerant charge carriers. Agreement of observed incommensurability δ ⁡ ( x ) \delta(x) with the formulas is very good for the hole-doped compounds and reasonable for the electron-doped compound. III
SUMMARY AND REMARK

Leaving aside the deviation in Fig. 2b for clarification by future experiment, the overall finding for 214 copper oxides is a universal
square-root
dependence of density-wave incommensurability
\delta(x)
on the doping of charge carriers—be they holes, be they electrons—quantitatively expressed by Eqs. (1) and (2). Their commonality is the underlying assumption that the
CuO_{2}
plane is partitioned by pairs of itinerant charge carriers.
The major difference between Eqs. (1) and (2) is the doping offset by
\Delta x_{p}=x_{0p}^{N}
with
Ae
(hole) doping but
no
corresponding offset with
Ce
(electron) doping. The difference is related on the one hand to the precarious sensitivity of the
O^{2-}
ions’ contribution to 3D-AFM when doped by holes, together with the necessity to keep 3D-AFM suppressed by a hole concentration
x_{0p}^{N}
. On the other hand it is related to the much lesser sensitivity of the
Cu^{2+}
ions’ contribution to 3D-AFM when doped by electrons. A minor difference between Eqs. (1) and (2) concerns the influence of excess oxygen—necessary for stability in crystal growth of
{Ln_{2-x}Ce_{x}CuO_{4+y}}
(
Ln=Pr,Nd,Sm
)—by
\Delta x_{n}=-2y
.

As a refinement, the doping offset
\Delta x_{p}=x_{0p}^{N}
in the radical of Eq. (1) may be treated as doping dependent,
\Delta x_{p}(x)
. When for
x\approx 1/8=0.125
a value of
\Delta x_{p}(x)=0.01
(instead of
\Delta x_{p}(x)=x_{0p}^{N}=0.02
) is used, agreement with experiment improves from very good (3% deviation) to excellent (1% deviation or less). In the framework of the above interpretation this could be understood as a lesser need to exempt holes from the CDW to keep 3D-AFM suppressed when overall more doped holes are present.

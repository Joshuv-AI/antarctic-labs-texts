Existence of BV solution for the Euler-Poisson system in one dimension with large initial data
Shyam Sundar Ghoshal, Boris Haspot and Animesh Jana

Abstract
This paper deals with the existence of BV solution for the Euler-Poisson system endowed with a γ \displaystyle\gamma pressure law. More precisely, we prove the existence of weak solution in the BV framework with arbitrary large initial data when γ = 1 + 2 ​ ϵ \displaystyle\gamma=1+2\epsilon satisfies a smallness condition. We use the Glimm scheme combined with a splitting method as introduced in [Poupaud, Rascle and Vila, J. Differential Equations, 1995]. Existence of BV solution of 1-D isentropic Euler equation for large data and γ = 1 + 2 ​ ϵ \displaystyle\gamma=1+2\epsilon is proved in [Nishida and Smoller, Comm. Pure Appl. Math, 1973]. Due to the presence of electric field, the difficulty arises while controlling the Glimm functional for the Euler-Poisson system. It requires a subtle study of wave interaction. In the later part of this article, we discuss the initial-boundary value problem for the Euler-Poisson system. We prove the existence of B ​ V \displaystyle BV solution for the initial-boundary value problem with large initial and boundary data. By an explicit example, we also show ill-posedness of initial-boundary value problem for the isentropic Euler equation.

F_{b}(O^{n+1}_{1})\leq F_{b}(O^{n})(1+A_{1}\Delta t)+B_{1}\Delta t,

where the constants
\displaystyle A_{1},B_{1}
are as in (
77
)–(
78
). Our BV estimation will be completed if we are able to make a bound of
\displaystyle F_{b}(O^{n+1})
with respect to
\displaystyle F_{b}(O^{n+1}_{1})
or in other words, we want to compare strength of
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n+1},U_{n+1,2}]
with the waves intersecting
\displaystyle J_{0}^{n}
. We can not do this directly. Instead, we rely on the following three steps: (i) compare
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[(\varrho_{*},u_{*}),U_{n+1,2}]
with the waves intersecting
\displaystyle J_{0}^{n}
where
\displaystyle(\varrho_{*},u_{*})=\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n},U_{n,1}](\Delta t,0)
. This will be same as an interaction away from boundary. (ii) Compare the strength of 2-wave arising in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n},U_{n+1,2}]
with
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[(\varrho_{*},u_{*}),U_{n+1,2}]
by using Lemma
5.3
and then finally (iii) we compare strength of 2-wave arising in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n+1},U_{n+1,2}]
with the strength of 2-wave arising in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n},U_{n+1,2}]
by using Lemma
5.4
. Next we justify these steps in more details.

Step-(i):
We first consider a Riemann problem with
\displaystyle U_{L}=U_{*}
where
\displaystyle U_{*}=(\varrho_{*},u_{*})=\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n},U_{n,1}](\Delta t,0)
and
\displaystyle U_{n+1,2}=(\varrho_{n+1,2},u_{n+1,2})
. Suppose the Riemann problem
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[U_{*},U_{n+1,2}]
consists of 1-wave (say
\displaystyle\alpha_{1}
) of strength
\displaystyle\sigma_{1}^{+}
and 2-wave (say
\displaystyle\alpha_{2}
) of strength
\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{+}
. Let
\displaystyle{F}_{*}^{n+1}
be defined as

\displaystyle\displaystyle F^{n+1}_{*}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=(\sigma_{1}^{+})_{-}+(\sigma_{2}^{+})_{-}+K(\sigma_{1}^{+})_{-}^{2}+V(\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\limits\left\{\left|\beta\right|(\sigma_{1}^{+})_{-},\,\beta\mbox{ is a shock approaching }\alpha_{1}\mbox{ and $\displaystyle\beta$ is on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\limits\left\{\left|\beta\right|(\sigma_{2}^{+})_{-},\,\beta\mbox{ is a shock approaching }\alpha_{2}\mbox{ and $\displaystyle\beta$ is on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\left\{\left|\beta\right|\left|\gamma\right|:\beta,\gamma\;\mbox{ cross $\displaystyle J$ and approach and $\displaystyle\beta,\gamma$ are on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\left\{\left|\beta\right|^{2}:\,\beta\mbox{ is 1-shock and lies on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}.

Again by similar argument as in section
4.2
(see also
[
37
]
), we get

F^{n+1}_{*}\leq F_{b}(\mathcal{O}^{n+1}_{1})\leq F_{b}(O^{n})(1+A_{1}\Delta t)+B_{1}\Delta t.

Step-(ii):
Now we consider the boundary Riemann problem
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n},U_{n+1,2}]
with boundary condition
\displaystyle m_{b}^{n}
. Let
\displaystyle(\varrho^{\#},u^{\#})
be defined as
\displaystyle(\varrho^{\#},u^{\#})=\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n},U_{n+1,2}](\Delta t,0)
which implies
\displaystyle\varrho^{\#}u^{\#}=m_{b}^{n}
. Let
\displaystyle\sigma^{\#}
be the strength of 2-wave (say
\displaystyle\alpha^{\#}
) in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n},U_{n+1,2}]
. We define

\displaystyle\displaystyle F^{n+1}_{\#}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=(\sigma^{\#})_{-}+V(\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\limits\left\{\left|\beta\right|(\sigma^{\#})_{-},\,\beta\mbox{ is a shock approaching }\alpha^{\#}\mbox{ and $\displaystyle\beta$ is on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\left\{\left|\beta\right|\left|\gamma\right|:\beta,\gamma\;\mbox{ cross $\displaystyle J$ and approach and $\displaystyle\beta,\gamma$ are on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\left\{\left|\beta\right|^{2}:\,\beta\mbox{ is 1-shock and lies on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}.

By applying Lemma
5.3
, we see that

F^{n+1}_{\#}\leq F^{n+1}_{*}\leq F_{b}(O^{n})(1+A_{1}\Delta t)+B_{1}\Delta t.

Step-(iii):
Finally, we consider the boundary Riemann problem
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n+1},U_{n+1,2}]
and compare it with
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{b}[m_{b}^{n},U_{n+1,2}]
. Let
\displaystyle\sigma^{\ddagger}
be the strength of 2-wave (say
\displaystyle\alpha^{\ddagger}
) arising in
\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[m_{b}^{n+1},U_{n+1,2}]
, then by Lemma
5.4
we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle F_{b}(\mathcal{O}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(\sigma^{\ddagger})_{-}+V(\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\limits\left\{\left|\beta\right|(\sigma^{\ddagger})_{-},\,\beta\mbox{ is a shock approaching }\alpha^{\ddagger}\mbox{ and $\displaystyle\beta$ is on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\left\{\left|\beta\right|\left|\gamma\right|:\beta,\gamma\;\mbox{ cross $\displaystyle J$ and approach and $\displaystyle\beta,\gamma$ are on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+K\sum\left\{\left|\beta\right|^{2}:\,\beta\mbox{ is 1-shock and lies on $\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{n+1}\setminus J_{0}^{n+1}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq F^{n+1}_{\#}\leq F_{b}(O^{n})(1+A_{1}\Delta t)+B_{1}\Delta t.

Rest of the proof follows in exactly same way as in Theorem
1.1
.

Quantum geometry of bosonic Bogoliubov quasiparticles
Isaac Tesfaye, André Eckardt

Abstract
Bosonic Bogoliubov de Gennes (BBdG) Hamiltonians describe the excitations of weakly interacting Bose condensates as well as photonic systems under parametric driving. Their topological features have been studied mainly by utilizing a generalized symplectic version of the Berry curvature and related Chern numbers. However, a full characterization of geometrical features in BBdG systems is still lacking. Here, we propose a symplectic quantum geometric tensor (SQGT), whose imaginary part leads to the previously studied symplectic Berry curvature, while the real part gives rise to a symplectic quantum metric, providing a natural distance measure in the space of bosonic Bogoliubov modes. The SQGT is directly related to observable properties of BBdG systems. We show how to measure all components of the SQGT by extracting excitation rates in response to periodic modulations of the systems’ parameters. Moreover, we connect the symplectic Berry curvature to a generalized symplectic anomalous velocity term for Bogoliubov-Bloch wave packets. We test our results for a bosonic Bogoliubov-Haldane model.

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}_{\text{int}}=2\pi A^{2}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\left\lvert(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}(\partial_{1}\mathcal{M})\bm{w}^{n}\right\rvert^{2}}{s_{m}(\tilde{\Omega}_{m}-\tilde{\Omega}_{n})^{2}}=2\pi A^{2}g^{n}_{11}.

from quasiparticle scattering rates.

We can generalize the above results to initial states of multiple quasiparticles.
A generic perturbation,

V(t)=\sum_{ij}\{A_{ij}(t)\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}[B_{ij}(t)\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.}]\}+\sum_{i}[C_{i}(t)\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}+\text{h.c.}]

Symplectic anomalous velocity.
—
We show that, like in the number-conserving case
[
65
,
66
,
67
,
103
]
,
a Bogoliubov lattice system acquires an anomalous transverse velocity proportional to the symplectic Berry curvature and transverse to an applied homogeneous force
\bm{\mathbf{F}}
.
Consider a quasiparticle prepared in the Bogoliubov-Bloch mode
\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}_{0})
.
In response to the force, the quasimomentum becomes
\bm{\mathbf{q}}(t)=\bm{\mathbf{q}}_{0}+\bm{\mathbf{F}}t
, inducing a modulation of the dynamical matrix
D[\bm{\mathbf{q}}(t)]
. In first-order adiabatic perturbation theory with respect to
\bm{\mathbf{F}}
, the time evolved state is given by
[
70
]

\displaystyle\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})+i\sum_{m\neq n}\bm{w}^{m}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\frac{(\bm{w}^{m}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}))^{\dagger}(\nabla_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\mathcal{M})\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\bm{\mathbf{F}}}{s_{m}[\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})-\tilde{\Omega}_{m}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})]^{2}}.

(18)

The expectation value of the Bogoliubov velocity operator
\bm{\mathbf{v}}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\nabla_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
then reads

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\mathbf{v}}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\rangle=\nabla_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})+\bm{\mathbf{B}}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\times\bm{\mathbf{F}},

(19)

with
\bm{\mathbf{B}}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\equiv\epsilon_{\mu\nu\kappa}B^{n}_{\mu\nu}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{e}_{\kappa}
.
The first term corresponds to the slope of the occupied Bogoliubov energy band,
and the second term provides the symplectic anomalous transverse velocity.
This result is complementary to that of Ref.
[
103
]
, where the dynamics of Bogoliubov Bloch wave packets has also been studied using a time-dependent variational principle, but without the connection to the symplectic Berry curvature.
The effect of the symplectic anomalous velocity can be verified in semiclassical dynamics of Bogoliubov-Bloch wave packets or by measuring the modified thermal depletion of a BEC due to the presence of the symplectic Berry curvature
[
103
]
.

Conclusion.
—
We have introduced the manifestly gauge-invariant symplectic quantum geometric tensor (SQGT) for BBdG systems, whose imaginary part is proportional to the symplectic Berry curvature.
The real part of the SQGT defines a symplectic quantum metric, which is shown to provide a natural distance measure in the space of Bogoliubov modes.
All components of the symplectic quantum geometric tensor can be measured via tracking Bogoliubov excitation rates
and the symplectic Berry curvature describes an anomalous velocity in Bogoliubov Bloch systems.
State-of-the-art experiments with bosonic ultracold atoms
[
104
,
105
,
106
,
107
]

are well-suited to test our proposals.
For future work, it will be interesting to connect recent topological notions for mixed quantum states
[
108
,
109
,
110
]

to thermal BBdG systems and
to see whether Euler invariants
[
111
,
112
,
113
]

may be generalized to the BBdG context.

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Phi}^{\dagger}\Omega\hat{\Phi}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{K}\right\}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\omega_{n}\left(\hat{b}_{n}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{K}\right\}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\omega_{n}\hat{b}_{n}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{n}+C,

(S22)

with
C\equiv\sum_{n}\omega_{n}/2-\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{K}\right\}/2
.
The ground state
{\lvert}\psi_{0}{\rangle}
of this Hamiltonian (
S22
)
is generally given by a multi-mode squeezed state with a non-zero number of original

\hat{a}^{(\dagger)}_{i}
bosons, which is also called the Bogoliubov vacuum.
This Bogoliubov vacuum is defined by the condition that it contains
no Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitations,
i.e.,
\hat{b}_{n}{\lvert}\psi_{0}{\rangle}=0
for all
n

[
121
,
122
]
.

S2
Symmetries and Krein space perspective

Here, we briefly discuss the symmetries and the inherent (real) Krein space structure of
the BBdG system (
S1
), mainly following Refs.
[
30
,
73
]
.
First, we note that the BdG Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
(
S1
) acts on
the entire bosonic Fock space
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathcal{H}
is the finite-dimensional complex single-particle Hilbert space
[
30
]
.
The Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
can be cast into a block diagonal (Nambu spinor) representation, giving rise to the linear coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}
(
S1
), which acts
on a so-called particle-hole Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}}=\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
The matrix
\mathcal{M}
, consists matrices
K
and
G
(
S1
), which themselves act on
\mathcal{H}

[
30
]
.

To analyze the symmetries of BBdG systems, we first introduce three additional operators which act on
\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}}
,

\displaystyle\tau_{x}=\sigma_{x}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbbm{1}_{N}\\
\mathbbm{1}_{N}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\tilde{\tau}_{y}=-i\tau_{y}=-i\sigma_{y}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathbbm{1}_{N}\\
\mathbbm{1}_{N}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{N}&-i\mathbbm{1}_{N}\\
\mathbbm{1}_{N}&i\mathbbm{1}_{N}\end{pmatrix}.

(S23)

All operators of (
S23
) together with
\tau_{z}
are unitary.
The operators
\tau_{x}
and
\tau_{z}
square to the identity,
whereas
\tilde{\tau}_{y}
squares to minus the identity, i.e.,

\tau_{x}^{2}=\tau_{z}^{2}=\mathbbm{1}_{2N}=-\tilde{\tau}_{y}^{2}
.
The operators
\tau_{i}
are just like generalized Pauli matrices, with the same properties, i.e.,

\tau_{i}\tau_{j}=\delta_{ij}\mathbbm{1}_{2N}+i\epsilon_{ijk}\tau_{k}
.
The last operator
c
is called a Cayley transform
[
30
]
and
is used to translate between different representations of the BdG Hamiltonian (
S1
), which will become clearer
once the position-momentum quadrature representation of the BdG Hamiltonian is introduced
(cf.
Section

S2.1
).

We know that the bosonic BdG spectrum of (
S1
) does not follow
from the spectrum of the hermitian coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{\dagger}

but rather from the non-hermitian dynamical matrix
D\equiv\tau_{z}\mathcal{M}
,
which also determines the dynamics of the system
[
30
,
31
]
.
Hence, we will analyze the symmetries of the dynamical matrix
D
.

The coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}
(
S1
) possesses the
partice-hole symmetry
(PHS)

\mathcal{M}=\tau_{x}\mathcal{M}^{*}\tau_{x}
, which translates to the PHS of the dynamical matrix

\displaystyle\tau_{x}D^{*}\tau_{x}=-D.

(S24)

We note that the PHS should, more correctly, actually be called a particle-hole constraint, as it does not describe an actual symmetry of the system
[
31
]
.
This is further discussed in
S11.2
.
However, in the following we will still use the term PHS to connect to the previous literature
[
6
,
30
]
.
Moreover, the dynamical matrix is also
\tau_{z}
-
pseudo-hermitian
,

\displaystyle D^{\dagger}=\tau_{z}D\tau_{z},

(S25)

which acts on the so-called
Krein space

(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z})
.
Before we continue, we briefly collect some definitions and properties of Krein spaces

[
73
,
30
]
.

Definition 1
(Krein space)

A Krein space
(\mathcal{K},J)
is a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
equipped with a
so-called fundamental symmetry, a self-adjoint unitary
J
, which
fulfills
J^{2}=\mathbbm{1}
and
J=J^{\dagger}
.

Note that the fundamental symmetry
J
further induces an indefinite scalar product
of the form
v,w\in\mathcal{K}\mapsto v^{\dagger}Jw
.
Given an additional real structure
C
(complex conjugation) on
\mathcal{K}
,
one can also define a
real
Krein space as follows
[
73
]

(a linear operator
A
is called real if
CAC\equiv A^{*}=A
holds).

Definition 2
(Real Krein space)

A Real Krein space
(\mathcal{K},J,S)
of kind
(\eta,\zeta)=(\pm 1,\pm 1)

is a complex Krein space
(\mathcal{K},J)
with a real fundamental symmetry
J=J^{*}

and an additional real unitary
S
.
The real unitary
S
squares to
\eta\mathbbm{1}

and it either commutes (
\zeta=+1
) or anti-commutes (
\zeta=-1
) with
J
, i.e.,
JS=\zeta SJ
.

Here, we have we deal with a real Krein space
(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})

of kind
(1,-1)

[
73
]
, as
\tau_{z}
is a real hermitian unitary and

\tau_{x}
is a real unitary which anti-commutes with
\tau_{z}
, i.e.,
\tau_{z}\tau_{x}=-\tau_{x}\tau_{z}
.
Hence, in our finite-dimensional case,
\text{dim}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}})=2N
, the dynamical matrix
D
is an element of the
Lie algebra

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})

\displaystyle=\{D\in\mathbb{C}^{2N\times 2N}\,|\,D^{\dagger}=\tau_{z}D\tau_{z},\,\tau_{x}D^{*}\tau_{x}=-D\},

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}K&G\\
-G^{*}&-K^{*}\end{pmatrix}\,|\,K=K^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N},\,G=G^{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}\right\},

(S26)

where in the second equality, we have explicitly expressed the conditions
using the form of the coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}=\tau_{z}D
(
S1
).

The
\tau_{z}
-
paraunitary
(Bogoliubov) transformation matrices
W

(
S11
) are elements of the associated Lie group which are obtained by exponentiation
W=e^{iD}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})

\displaystyle=\{W\in\text{GL}(2N,\mathbb{C})\,|\,W^{\dagger}\tau_{z}W=\tau_{z},\,\tau_{x}W^{*}\tau_{x}=W\},

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}U&V^{*}\\
V&U^{*}\end{pmatrix}\,|\,U,V\in\text{GL}(N,\mathbb{C}),\ UU^{\dagger}-V^{*}V^{T}=\mathbbm{1}_{N},\,V^{*}U^{T}-UV^{\dagger}=0_{N}\right\},

(S27)

where in the second equality above we have explicitly expressed the conditions (cf. (
S9
)) in terms of elements of the general linear group
\text{GL}(2N,\mathbb{C})=\{A\in\mathbb{C}^{2N\times 2N}\,|\,\det(A)\neq 0\}
.

Note that both properties of the Lie group
\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})
directly follow from the properties of the Lie algebra, i.e.,

W^{\dagger}\tau_{z}W=e^{-iD^{\dagger}}\tau_{z}e^{iD}=\tau_{z}e^{-iD}\tau_{z}\tau_{z}e^{iD}=\tau_{z}

and
\tau_{x}W^{*}\tau_{x}=\tau_{x}e^{-iD^{*}}\tau_{x}=e^{iD}=W
.

Both the particle-hole-symmetry and the pseudo-hermiticity of the dynamical matrix
D
also
determine the spectrum of
D
.
From the particle-hole-symmetry (
S24
) we can deduce that for every eigenvalue
\lambda
there exists a corresponding negative complex conjugated eigenvalue
-\lambda^{*}
.
The pseudo-hermiticity (
S25
)
implies that the eigenvalues of
D
always come in complex conjugated pairs.
Thus, when
D
has complex eigenvalues they always come in quadrupoles (
-\lambda^{*},\lambda^{*},\lambda,-\lambda
).
However, for a spectrum that is purely real, as for a positive definite matrix
\mathcal{M}=\tau_{z}D
, they always come in pairs (
\lambda,-\lambda
)
[
73
]
.

S2.1
On the origin of the term
symplectic

We briefly explain the origin of the terminology
symplectic
which has been established by previous literature e.g., for the symplectic polarization, symplectic Berry curvature and Chern number, and symplectic orthonormalization (cf. Refs.
[
23
,
6
,
7
,
30
,
11
]
).

To this end, we first note that the BdG Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
(
S1
) given in the
\hat{a}_{j}
bosonic mode representation via the operators can also be equivalently expressed in the position-momentum quadrature representation
(\hat{q}_{j},\hat{p}_{j})
via

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{q}_{j}+i\hat{p}_{j}),\quad\hat{a}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{q}_{j}-i\hat{p}_{j}),\quad\hat{q}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a}_{j}+\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}),\quad\hat{p}_{j}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}-\hat{a}_{j}).

(S28)

The position and momentum quadratures fulfill the canonical commutation relation
[\hat{q}_{k},\hat{p}_{l}]=i\delta_{kl}
(whereas the mode operators
\hat{a}_{k}^{(\dagger)}
fulfill bosonic commutation relations
[\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{ij}
(
S3
)).
By writing the vector of mode operators (bosonic Nambu spinor) via
\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}\equiv(\hat{\bm{a}}^{\dagger},\hat{\bm{a}})
(
S2
) and the
\hat{S}^{T}=(\hat{q}_{1},\ldots,\hat{q}_{N},\hat{p}_{1},\ldots,\hat{p}_{N})\equiv(\hat{\bm{q}},\hat{\bm{p}})
, we can see that the Cayley transform
c
(
S23
) allows us to switch between these two representations,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}=({\bm{a}}^{\dagger},\hat{\bm{a}})=(\hat{\bm{q}},\hat{\bm{p}})\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{N}&\mathbbm{1}_{N}\\
i\mathbbm{1}_{N}&-i\mathbbm{1}_{N}\end{pmatrix}=\hat{S}^{T}c^{\dagger}

(S29)

or equivalently
\hat{\Psi}=c\hat{S}
.
Similar to the commutation relations for the vector of mode operators reading
[\hat{\Psi}_{i},\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}_{j}]=(\tau_{z})_{ij}
(
S3
), we also have commutations relations for the vector of
position-momentum quadratures

\displaystyle[\hat{S}^{T}_{k},\hat{S}_{l}]=-i(\tilde{\tau}_{y})_{kl}=-i\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathbbm{1}_{N}\\
\mathbbm{1}_{N}&0\end{pmatrix}_{kl},

(S30)

with being
\tilde{\tau}_{y}
(
S23
) the standard symplectic form
[
71
]
.
Using (
S29
), the BdG Hamiltonian (
S1
) in the position-momentum quadrature representation reads
[
140
]

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}\hat{\Psi}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{S}^{T}c^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}c\hat{S}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{S}^{T}A\hat{S},\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}B&E\\
E^{T}&F\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\text{with}\ B=B^{T}=\mathrm{Re}[K+G],\ E=\mathrm{Im}[K-G],\ F=F^{T}=\mathrm{Re}[K-G].

(S31)

Here,
A=A^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N}
denotes the symmetric coefficient matrix
in the
(\hat{\bm{q}},\hat{\bm{p}})
-quadrature representation where
B
and
F
are real symmetric matrices and
E
is a general real matrix.

The paraunitary matrices
W
diagonalizing the BdG Hamiltonian in the mode representation (
S1
) preserve the commutations relations of the mode operators (
S3
).
Here, to diagonalize
\hat{H}
in this representation (
S31
) we transform into a new set of quadrature operators
\hat{R}^{T}=(\hat{\bm{Q}},\hat{\bm{P}})
via

(\hat{\bm{q}},\hat{\bm{p}})=\hat{S}^{T}=\hat{R}^{T}\tilde{W}^{T}
.
Importantly, the new quadrature operators must also fulfill
[\hat{R}^{T}_{k},\hat{R}_{l}]=-i(\tilde{\tau}_{y})_{kl}
(
S30
).
Similarly, here, the transformation matrices
\tilde{W}
diagonalizing the BdG Hamiltonian in the position-momentum quadrature representation (
S31
)
must preserve the canonical (position-momentum) commutation relations (
S30
).

This directly leads to

\displaystyle-i(\tilde{\tau}_{y})_{kl}=[\hat{S}^{T}_{k},\hat{S}_{l}]=\sum_{m,n}\tilde{W}^{T}_{km}[\hat{R}^{T}_{m},\hat{R}_{n}]\tilde{W}_{nl}=-i\sum_{m,n}\tilde{W}^{T}_{km}(\tilde{\tau}_{y})_{mn}\tilde{W}_{nl},

(S32)

which is equivalent to
\tilde{\tau}_{y}=\tilde{W}^{T}\tilde{\tau}_{y}\tilde{W}
, namely,
the defining properties of the real symplectic group
\tilde{W}\in\text{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})
(
S33
).

Now, the Cayley transform
c
allows us to directly switch between paraunitary Lie group

\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})
(
S27
)
and the Lie group
\text{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})
of the symplectic transformations.
To see this, we apply the Cayley transform
c
(
S23
)
directly onto
\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{W}}=c^{\dagger}\mathbb{W}c=\left\{\tilde{W}\in\text{GL}(2N,\mathbb{R})\,|\,\tilde{W}^{\dagger}\tilde{\tau}_{y}\tilde{W}=\tilde{\tau}_{y},\,\tilde{W}^{*}=\tilde{W}\right\}=\text{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R}),

(S33)

which coincides with the real symplectic group
\text{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})

[
30
]
.
The elements of the real symplectic group,
\tilde{W}\in\text{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})
,
directly follow from applying the Cayley transform onto elements of

\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})
,
i.e.,
\tilde{W}=c^{\dagger}Wc
with
W\in\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})
.
Moreover, the defining properties of the symplectic group
\text{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})
are
directly inherited from the properties paraunitary group

\mathbb{W}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z}\tau_{x})
, i.e.,

\tilde{W}^{\dagger}\tilde{\tau}_{y}\tilde{W}=(c^{\dagger}W^{\dagger}c)\tilde{\tau}_{y}(c^{\dagger}Wc)=-ic^{\dagger}W^{\dagger}\tau_{z}Wc=-ic^{\dagger}\tau_{z}c=\tilde{\tau}_{y}
and

\tilde{W}^{*}=c^{T}W^{*}c^{*}=c^{T}\tau_{x}W\tau_{x}c^{*}=c^{\dagger}Wc=\tilde{W}
.
Here, we have used that the Cayley transform
c
(
S23
)
is unitary and
allows us to cycle between different
\tau_{i}
operators

[
134
]
,

\displaystyle c\tau_{x}c^{\dagger}=\tau_{y}=c^{\dagger}\tau_{z}c,\quad c\tau_{y}c^{\dagger}=\tau_{z}=c^{\dagger}\tau_{x}c,\quad c\tau_{z}c^{\dagger}=\tau_{x}=c^{\dagger}\tau_{y}c.

(S34)

This explains the common use of the term
symplectic
in the literature of BBdG systems, even though we and many in the literature
[
23
,
6
,
46
]
work in the bosonic mode
representation (
S1
), where only paraunitary conditions
(
S37
) are present.

We briefly discuss how, in the absence of pairing terms
G=0
in (
S1
), the paraunitary (
S27
) and symplectic structures (
S33
) reduce to simple unitary structures.
For
G=0
, the coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}
(
S1
) becomes block-diagonal,
\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}K&0\\
0&K^{*}\end{pmatrix}
, with
K=K^{\dagger}
, such that doubling of the degrees of freedom to the particle-hole space
\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}}
is purely artificial.
In this case, the problem simply reduces to a simple unitary diagonalization of the hermitian matrix
K=K^{\dagger}
via the unitary
U\in U(N,\mathbb{C})
.
This can be seen by noting that the paraunitary transformation matrix
W
(
S27
) reduces to a block-diagonal unitary transformation

W=\begin{pmatrix}U&0\\
0&U^{*}\end{pmatrix}
with
V=0
blocks vanishing (
S5
) such that
W
becomes a simple enlarged unitary matrix
W^{\dagger}W=\mathbbm{1}_{2N}
(
S9
).

Now, for the
(\hat{\bm{q}},\hat{\bm{p}})
-quadrature representation of the bosonic BBdG system (
S31
) with
G=0
, the coefficient matrix
A
becomes

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Re}[K]&\mathrm{Im}[K]\\
-\mathrm{Im}[K]&\mathrm{Re}[K]\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}X&Y\\
-Y&X\end{pmatrix},

(S35)

which is just the embedding of the hermitian matrix
K=X+iY=K^{\dagger}
into the real-valued symmetric matrix
A=A^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N\times 2N}
, since
X=X^{T}
and
Y=-Y^{T}
.
Moreover, the symplectic transformation
\tilde{W}
in the quadrature representation
can be computed via the Cayley transform
\tilde{W}=c^{\dagger}Wc
and reads

\displaystyle\tilde{W}=c^{\dagger}Wc=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{N}&\mathbbm{1}_{N}\\
i\mathbbm{1}_{N}&-i\mathbbm{1}_{N}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}U&0\\
0&U^{*}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{N}&-i\mathbbm{1}_{N}\\
\mathbbm{1}_{N}&i\mathbbm{1}_{N}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Re}[U]&\mathrm{Im}[U]\\
-\mathrm{Im}[U]&\mathrm{Re}[U]\end{pmatrix}.

(S36)

This is again a real embedding, here for the complex unitary
U=\alpha+i\beta\in U(N,\mathbb{C})
.
We note that since
U
is unitary,
U^{\dagger}U=\mathbbm{1}_{N}
, the real and imaginary parts
\alpha=\mathrm{Re}[U]
and
\beta=\mathrm{Im}[U]
fulfill
\alpha^{T}\alpha+\beta^{T}\beta=\mathbbm{1}_{N}
and
\alpha^{T}\beta=\beta^{T}\alpha
.
From this we can see that the real-valued symplectic transformation

\tilde{W}
(
S36
) fulfills
\tilde{W}^{T}\tilde{W}=\mathbbm{1}_{2N}
, i.e.,
\tilde{W}\in\mathrm{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})\cap\mathrm{O}(2N)
, which is the orthogonal symplectic group, a compact subgroup of the real symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})
.
Moreover, it follows directly from the map
f:U(N,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})\cap\mathrm{O}(2N)
defined via
f(U)=\tilde{W}=c^{\dagger}W(U)c
(
S36
) that this real orthogonal symplectic group is isomorphic to the complex unitary group
\mathrm{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})\cap\mathrm{O}(2N)\simeq U(N,\mathbb{C})

[
141
]
.
Hence, in the absence of pairing terms
G=0
, both the paraunitary transformation
W
in the bosonic mode representation and the symplectic transformation
\tilde{W}
in the position-momentum quadrature representation reduce to simple unitary transformations, as expected.

S3
Indefinite Bogoliubov inner product space

We collect some useful relations for the indefinite inner product space spanned by
the Bogoliubov modes
\bm{w}^{n}
.
For a more rigorous introduction into these concepts pertaining indefinite inner product spaces, we refer to Ref.
[
124
]
.
Given a Bogoliubov transformation matrix
W\in\mathbb{C}^{2N\times 2N}
which satisfies
(
S11
)

\displaystyle W\tau_{z}W^{\dagger}=W^{\dagger}\tau_{z}W=\tau_{z}

(S37)

and whose
n
-th column corresponds to the Bogoliubov mode
\bm{w}^{n}
,
we can define a projector
P^{n}
onto the
n
-th non-degenerate Bogoliubov eigenspace
(and its complement
Q^{n}
) [
Eq.

6
in the main text]

\displaystyle P^{n}=W\Gamma^{n}W^{-1}=W\Gamma^{n}\tau_{z}W^{\dagger}\tau_{z}=s_{n}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}\tau_{z},\quad Q^{n}=\mathbbm{1}_{2N}-P^{n},

(S38)

with
(\Gamma^{n})_{ml}=\delta_{nm}\delta_{ml}
being the
2N\times 2N
matrix whose only non-zero element
is the
n
-th diagonal element being equal to
+1

and where
s_{n}=1\,(-1)\,\text{for}\ n\leq N\,(n>N)
for the so-called particle (hole) states.
We note that these projectors
X^{n}
are necessarily invariant
under (local)
U(1)
gauge transformations for the Bogoliubov modes of the form

\bm{w}^{n}\rightarrow e^{i\phi}\bm{w}^{n}
, i.e.,
X^{n}\rightarrow X^{n}

for
X^{n}\in\{P^{n},Q^{n}\}
(
S38
).

Both
P^{n}
and
Q^{n}
are Krein-hermitian (pseudo-hermitian)

(X^{n})^{\dagger}=\tau_{z}X^{n}\tau_{z}
for
X^{n}\in\{P^{n},Q^{n}\}

[
73
,
31
]
,
which directly follows from (
S37
).
Moreover, the projectors are idempotent
P^{n}P^{k}=\delta_{nk}P^{n}
and
span the entire inner product space
\sum_{n}P^{n}=\mathbbm{1}_{2N}
.
This allows for a resolution of the identity in the indefinite Bogoliubov inner product space,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{2N}=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}P^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{2N}s_{n}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}(P^{l}_{+}+P^{l}_{-})=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\left(\bm{w}^{l}_{+}(\bm{w}^{l}_{+})^{\dagger}-\bm{w}^{l}_{-}(\bm{w}^{l}_{-})^{\dagger}\right)\tau_{z},

(S39)

where in the third equality we have separated the projectors onto particle parts
P^{l}_{+}=\bm{w}^{l}_{+}(\bm{w}^{l}_{+})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}
and hole parts
P^{l}_{-}=-\bm{w}^{l}_{-}(\bm{w}^{l}_{-})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}
,
which correspond to the first
N
and last
N
elements of the projector
P^{n}
respectively.
Furthermore, we note that the particle and hole parts are not independent but
are directly related via the particle-hole symmetry (PHS) (
S27
)

\bm{w}^{l}_{-}=\tau_{x}(\bm{w}^{l}_{+})^{*}
, with
\tau_{x}
defined in (
S23
).
Thus, the PHS also relates the particle and hole parts of the projectors via

\displaystyle P_{+}^{l}=\tau_{x}(P_{-}^{l})^{*}\tau_{x}.

(S40)

Eq.

S40
also follows directly from (
S38
), when using
\tau_{x}W^{*}\tau_{x}=W
(
S27
),
\tau_{x}\tau_{z}=-\tau_{z}\tau_{x}
, and
\tau_{x}\Gamma^{n}\tau_{x}=\Gamma^{n+N}
.

The Bogoliubov modes
\bm{w}^{n}
adopt a generalized (pseudo) orthonormalization
condition
[
47
,
11
,
16
,
2
]
following from (
S37
),

\displaystyle(\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w}^{m}=s_{n}\delta_{nm},\,\Leftrightarrow(\bm{w}^{l}_{\pm})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}{\bm{w}^{k}_{\pm}}=\pm\delta_{lk},\ (\bm{w}^{l}_{\pm})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}{\bm{w}^{k}_{\mp}}=0.

(S41)

Next, note that the trace of a matrix
A\in\mathbb{C}^{2N\times 2N}
can be evaluated
in any basis which spans the entire
\mathbb{C}^{2N}
.
The Bogoliubov modes are such a basis which can be seen from
the resolution of the identity (
S39
).
Thus, the trace of a matrix
A
can be expressed in terms of the Bogoliubov modes as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{A}\right\}

In the last equality of (

S4
Symplectic quantum geometric tensor

In addition to the manifestly gauge-invariant form of the symplectic quantum geometric tensor

\eta^{n}_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{\partial_{\mu}P^{n}Q^{n}\partial_{\nu}P^{n}}\right\}

7

We again assume implicitly that the BdG Hamiltonian (

\bm{\lambda}

should be understood as derivates

\partial_{\mu}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{\mu}}

Using the definition

we see that the SQGT

\displaystyle\eta^{n}_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{\partial_{\mu}P^{n}Q^{n}\partial_{\nu}P^{n}}\right\}

\displaystyle\eta^{n}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}s_{m}[(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\mu}P^{n}Q^{n}\partial_{\nu}P^{n})\bm{w}^{m}]=\sum_{m}s_{m}[(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w}^{n}\ (\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}Q^{n}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})(\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w}^{m}]

which directly follows from (
S38

In the final equality, the orthonormality conditions (
S41
)

Eq.

To obtain the spectral representation of the SQGT, we first derive a
symplectic version of the Hellmann-Feynman theorem.
To this end, we first observe from eigenvalue equation (

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mu}D)\bm{w}^{n}+D\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n}=(\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\Omega}_{n})\bm{w}^{n}+\tilde{\Omega}_{n}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n},

(S47)

\displaystyle({\bm{w}^{m}})^{\dagger}(\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{M}){\bm{w}^{n}}=(\tilde{\Omega}_{n}-\tilde{\Omega}_{m})({\bm{w}^{m}})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n}).

Next, by inserting two resolutions of the identity (

P^{n}P^{m}=\delta_{nm}P^{n}

\displaystyle\eta^{n}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}s_{n}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}P^{m}(\mathbbm{1}_{2N}-P^{n})P^{m^{\prime}}}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}s_{n}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}(\delta_{mm^{\prime}}P^{m}-\delta_{nm^{\prime}}\delta_{nm}P^{m})}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})

\displaystyle=s_{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\neq n\\

(S49)

where in the last step,
the symplectic Hellmann-Feynman theorem (
S48

has been used.

S4.2
Particle-hole symmetry of the SQGT

We use the particle-hole symmetry of the BBdG system (
S24
) to relate
the particle parts of the SQGT,
\eta^{l,+}_{\mu\nu}
, to the hole parts,
\eta^{l,-}_{\mu\nu}
.
Here,
l
runs from
1
to
N
, where
\eta^{l,+}
corresponds to SQGT
\eta^{n}
of the particle bands
n<N
and
\eta^{l,-}
to the SQGT
\eta^{n}
of the hole bands
n>N
.
Using the PHS of the projectors
P^{l}_{+}=\tau_{x}(P^{l}_{-})^{*}\tau_{x}

(which follows from (
S27
)), we can see that

\displaystyle\eta^{l,+}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{\partial_{\mu}P^{l}_{+}Q_{+}^{l}\partial_{\nu}P^{l}_{+}}\right\}=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{\partial_{\mu}(\tau_{x}(P^{l}_{-})^{*}\tau_{x})\tau_{x}(Q_{-}^{l})^{*}\tau_{x}\partial_{\nu}(\tau_{x}(P^{l}_{-})^{*}\tau_{x})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left\{{\partial_{\mu}(P^{l}_{-})^{*}(Q_{-}^{l})^{*}\partial_{\nu}(P^{l}_{-})^{*}}\right\}=(\eta^{l,-}_{\mu\nu})^{*}

(S50)

Thus, the particle part of the SQGT is equal to the complex conjugate of the hole part of the SQGT.
This implies that the particle and hole parts of symplectic quantum metric

g^{n}_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{Re}[\eta^{n}_{\mu\nu}]
are equal, whereas the particle and hole parts of the symplectic Berry curvature
B^{n}_{\mu,\nu}=-2\mathrm{Im}[\eta^{n}_{\mu\nu}]
are opposite in sign

\displaystyle g^{l,+}_{\mu\nu}=g^{l,-}_{\mu\nu},\quad B^{l,+}_{\mu\nu}=-B^{l,-}_{\mu\nu}.

(S51)

S4.3
Symplectic quantum metric

We show that the symplectic quantum metric naturally arises when considering the distance
ds^{2}

between two infinitesimally close Bogoliubov modes.
In analogy to Refs.
[
61
,
60
]
, we define the distance
ds^{2}
between nearby states

\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\lambda})
and
\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\lambda}+d\bm{\lambda})
(omitting the index
n
in the following) via

\displaystyle ds^{2}=1-f^{2}=1-\left\lvert(\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda}))^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}{\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda}+d\bm{\lambda})}\right\rvert^{2},

(S52)

with the fidelity
f=\left\lvert(\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda}))^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}{\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda}+d\bm{\lambda})}\right\rvert
.
We first expand
\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda}+d\bm{\lambda})
to second order in
d\bm{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda}+d\bm{\lambda})=\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda})+\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda})d\lambda^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}(\bm{\lambda})d\lambda^{\mu}d\lambda^{\nu},

(S53)

where we use the Einstein summation convention over Greek indices in the following.
We then insert this expression (
S53
)
into (
S52
),
using orthonormalization conditions (
S41
).
Collecting only terms up to second order in
d\bm{\lambda}
then yields

\displaystyle ds^{2}=

\displaystyle 1-\Big\{(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})^{2}+(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})[((\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})+(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}))]d\lambda^{\mu}+[((\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}))]d\lambda^{\mu}d\lambda^{\nu}

\displaystyle\ \ +\frac{1}{2}(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})[((\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})+(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}))]d\lambda^{\mu}d\lambda^{\nu}\Big\}+\mathcal{O}(\left\lvert d\bm{\lambda}\right\rvert^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})[(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w})]-(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w}\ \bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w})\}d\lambda^{\mu}d\lambda^{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(\left\lvert d\bm{\lambda}\right\rvert^{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}Q}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w})\right]d\lambda^{\mu}d\lambda^{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(\left\lvert d\bm{\lambda}\right\rvert^{3})

(S54)

where the explicit parameter dependence of the states has been omitted for clarity.
In the second equality above, we have on the one hand used
(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})^{2}=1
(
S41
)
and on the other hand that the linear terms (
\sim d\lambda^{\mu}
) vanish, which follows from
the relation
(\bm{w})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})=-(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\bm{w})

(
S41
).
From this relation, it moreover follows that

((\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{w})+(\bm{w}^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}))=-[(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w})+(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w})]

which has also been used in the second equality above (
S54
).
Finally, comparing this expression to the definition of the symplectic quantum geometric tensor in
Eq.

S46
,
we see that the symplectic quantum geometric tensor (SQGT) naturally emerges as a distance measure
in the space of the Bogoliubov modes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\eta_{\mu\nu}d\lambda^{\mu}d\lambda^{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(\left\lvert d\bm{\lambda}\right\rvert^{3})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}+\eta_{\nu\mu}\right)d\lambda^{\mu}d\lambda^{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(\left\lvert d\bm{\lambda}\right\rvert^{3})=g_{\mu\nu}d\lambda^{\mu}d\lambda^{\nu}+\mathcal{O}(\left\lvert d\bm{\lambda}\right\rvert^{3}).

(S55)

More precisely, since the distance measure
ds^{2}
(
S54
) is a symmetric bilinear form,
the antisymmetric part of the SQGT does not contribute to
ds^{2}
, i.e.,
only the symmetric part, i.e., the symplectic quantum metric

g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}+\eta_{\nu\mu}\right)=\mathrm{Re}[\eta_{\mu\nu}]
,
determines the distance measure between two infinitesimally close Bogoliubov states,
hence, the second equality in
Eq.

S55
.

S4.4
Local conservation law for symplectic Berry curvature

We show that the symplectic Berry curvature, admits
a local conservation law over all subspaces (Bogoliubov subspaces),
including both the particle and hole subspaces
[
11
]
.
The first simple proof directly makes use of the particle-hole symmetry
of the symplectic Berry curvature (
S51
), i.e.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{2N}B^{n}_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{l=1}^{N}(B^{l,+}_{\mu\nu}+B^{l,-}_{\mu\nu})=\sum_{l=1}^{N}(B^{l,+}_{\mu\nu}-B^{l,+}_{\mu\nu})=0.

(S56)

For completeness, we also present a second proof without explicitly utilizing the PHS.
For this, we note that using
Eq.

S46
,
the symplectic Berry curvature of the subspace
n
,

B_{\mu\nu}^{n}=-2\mathrm{Im}\,[\eta^{n}_{\mu\nu}]
can be written as

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}^{n}=is_{n}((\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})-(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})),

(S57)

where we omit the explicit parameter dependence for clarity.
Next, inserting the resolution of the identity
(
S39
) into (
S57
),
we see that we may also write
B_{\mu\nu}^{n}
as

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}^{n}

\displaystyle=is_{n}\left\{\sum_{m}s_{m}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}{\bm{w}^{m}}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})-(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})\right\}

\displaystyle=is_{n}\left\{\sum_{m}\left[s_{m}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}{\bm{w}^{n}}(\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m})\right]-(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})\right\},

(S58)

where in the second equality we used

(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})=-(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\bm{w}^{n})

twice, which itself simply follows from
Eq.

S41
.
Now, by summing
Eq.

S58
over all subspaces
n
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n}B_{\mu\nu}^{n}

\displaystyle=i\left\{\sum_{m}\left[s_{m}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}\left(\sum_{n}s_{n}{\bm{w}^{n}}(\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}\right)(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m})\right]-\sum_{n}s_{n}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})\right\},

\displaystyle=i\left\{\sum_{m}\left[s_{m}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m})\right]-\sum_{n}\left[s_{n}(\partial_{\nu}\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}{\tau_{z}}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n})\right]\right\}=0,

(S59)

we immediately see that the symplectic Berry curvature
admits a local conservation law for all parameter values
\bm{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n}B^{n}_{\mu\nu}(\bm{\lambda})=0,\ \forall\bm{\lambda}.

(S60)

One immediate consequence of this conservation law is that the total (first) Chern number over
both the particle
and
hole subspaces is zero.
We stress that this is distinct from the conventional, particle-number-conserving case,
where both the sum of the Berry curvature and hence the sum of the Chern numbers over all particle bands is zero.
In particular, there are situations where the sum of the Berry curvature over all particle bands becomes non-zero.
A consequence of this is the weaker statement that the total Chern number over the particle subspace can become non-zero, which has already been noticed in Ref.
[
46
]
.
The situation occurs in BBdG systems which are dynamically stable, but non-positive definite, such as
photonic driven systems in a rotating frame governed by an external pump frequency.
Dynamical stability requires that the eigenvalues of the dynamical matrix
D
are real,
although the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
(
S1
) itself must not be positive definite

[
46
]
.
We note that thermodynamic stability is a stronger condition than dynamical stability,
as the former implies the latter, but not vice versa
[
30
]
.

the following time-dependence for the Nambu spinor of the new bosonic operators

\hat{\Phi}(t)=W^{-1}\hat{\Psi}(t)
(
S6
)

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}(t)=e^{-i\tilde{\Omega}t}\hat{\Phi}(0)=e^{-i\tilde{\Omega}t}W^{-1}\hat{\Psi}(0),

(S65)

where we have utilized that

W^{-1}DW=\tau_{z}W^{\dagger}\mathcal{M}W=\tau_{z}\Omega\equiv\tilde{\Omega}
which follows from

Eq.

S20
.
Thus, each new bosonic operator acquires a phase factor associated to eigenenergy

\hat{b}_{n}(t)=e^{-i\omega_{n}t}\hat{b}_{n}(0)

[
16
,
69
]
.

Next, we note that we may also absorb the time-dependence of the original operators
\hat{\Psi}(t)

into the (inverse) Bogoliubov matrix,

\hat{\Phi}(t)=W^{-1}\hat{\Psi}(t)\equiv W^{-1}(t)\hat{\Psi}(0)
, i.e.,
in (
S65

W^{-1}(t)=e^{-i\tilde{\Omega}t}W^{-1}(0)=W^{-1}(0)e^{-iDt}
.
This leads to the immediate question, what kind of equation of motion

fulfills.
In compact matrix form, the answer is given by the following set of
equations for the Bogoliubov modes,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}W^{-1}(t)=-iW^{-1}(t)D\ \Leftrightarrow\partial_{t}W(t)=iDW(t).

(S66)

Since we know that
W^{-1}D=\tilde{\Omega}W^{-1}

we see that (

W^{-1}(t)=e^{-i\tilde{\Omega}t}W^{-1}(0)

which is in agreement with (
S65
).
This may also be expressed in as an effective Schrödinger equation for the

\bm{w}^{n}(t)

[

,
103

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}\bm{w}^{n}(t)=D\bm{w}^{n}(t),

(S67)

which is the sought after effective time-dependent Schrödinger equation.
From
Eq.

S67
, for a time-independent dynamical matrix

D
, we can define the paraunitary
time evolution operator
P(t)
,

\displaystyle P(t)=\exp\left(iDt\right),

(S68)

such that
\bm{w}^{n}(t)=P(t)\bm{w}^{n}(0)

For a time-dependent dynamical matrix
D(t)
in (
S67

the paraunitary time evolution operator
reads

\displaystyle P(t)=\mathcal{T}\exp\left(i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}D(t^{\prime})\right),

(S69)

where
\mathcal{T}
denotes the time-ordering operator.
Note that the paraunitarity of

\tau_{z}P^{\dagger}(t)\tau_{z}=P^{-1}(t)

D^{\dagger}(t)=\tau_{z}D(t)\tau_{z}

Thus, the time evolution of the Bogoliubov modes
\bm{w}^{n}
(
S67

directly specifies the time-dependence of the Heisenberg-picture quasiparticle operators

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{n}(t)=\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(t)\tau_{z}\bm{w}^{n}=\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(0)e^{i(D^{\dagger})t}\tau_{z}\bm{w}^{n}=\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(0)\tau_{z}e^{iDt}\bm{w}^{n}=\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}(0)\tau_{z}\bm{w}^{n}(t),

(S70)

where we have used (
S64
)
and the pseudo-hermiticity of the dynamical matrix

S6
Time-dependent perturbation theory for the effective Schrödinger equation

Consider the effective time-dependent single-particle Schrödinger equation (
S67
)
for some state
\bm{\psi}(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{2N}
in the indefinite inner product space spanned
by the Bogoliubov modes
\bm{w}^{n}(t)

with the addition of a small time-dependent perturbation

added to the dynamical matrix, i.e.,

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}\bm{\psi}(t)=D(t)\bm{\psi}(t)=\left[D+V(t)\right]\bm{\psi}(t)

(S71)

where
V(t)
shall only be present for

Adapting standard time-dependent perturbation theory
[
126
]
to the space of Bogoliubov modes,
we now derive the effect of the perturbation

on the population of Bogoliubov modes.

At

\bm{\psi}(0)
to be
given by the Bogoliubov eigenstate
\bm{w}^{n}

(
S21
).
At this point,
\bm{w}^{n}
adheres to the effective time-dependent Schrödinger equation without the perturbation (
S67
),
which is easily solved by

\bm{w}^{n}(t)=e^{iDt}\bm{w}^{n}(0)=e^{i\tilde{\Omega}_{n}t}\bm{w}^{n}(0)
(
S67
).
To first order in the perturbation
V(t)
, we now compute the resulting excitation probability
to other distinct Bogoliubov modes
\bm{w}^{m}(t)

at time
t
.

First, we introduce the interaction picture

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{I}(t)=\tilde{P}(t)\bm{\psi}(t)=e^{-iDt}\bm{\psi}(t).

(S72)

The transformation is explicitly given by

\tilde{P}(t)=e^{-iDt}
where
D
is the static part in

Eq.

S71
.
Due to the pseudo-hermiticity of the dynamical matrix,
D^{\dagger}=\tau_{z}D\tau_{z}
,
in the equation of motion (
S67
),
the transformation
\tilde{P}(t)
must be paraunitary,
i.e.,
\tilde{P}^{\dagger}(t)=\tau_{z}\tilde{P}^{-1}(t)\tau_{z}
.
This directly follows from

\tau_{z}\tilde{P}^{\dagger}(t)\tau_{z}=\tau_{z}e^{iD^{\dagger}t}\tau_{z}=e^{iDt}=\tilde{P}^{-1}(t)
.
The effective time-dependent Schrödinger equation in the interaction picture then reads

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}\bm{\psi}_{I}(t)=D_{I}(t)\bm{\psi}_{I}(t),

(S73)

where we used
Eq.

S67
and introduced
D_{I}(t)
,
the dynamical matrix in the interaction picture given by

\displaystyle D_{I}(t)=\tilde{P}(t)D(t)\tilde{P}^{-1}(t)-i(\partial_{t}\tilde{P}(t))\tilde{P}^{-1}(t).

(S74)

With
\tilde{P}(t)=e^{-iDt}
,
Eq.

S73
then becomes

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}\bm{\psi}_{I}(t)=V_{I}(t)\bm{\psi}_{I}(t),

(S75)

where
V_{I}(t)\equiv\tilde{P}(t)V(t)\tilde{P}^{-1}(t)=e^{-iDt}V(t)e^{iDt}

is the perturbation in the interaction picture.
The solution to (
S75
) is then given by a Dyson-type series,

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}_{I}(t)=\bm{\psi}_{I}(0)+i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}V_{I}(t^{\prime})\bm{\psi}_{I}(0)+i^{2}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime\prime}V_{I}(t^{\prime})V_{I}(t^{\prime\prime})\bm{\psi}_{I}(0)+\dots,

(S76)

where we are only interested in the first order contribution.
Now, given an initial eigenstate
\bm{\psi}_{I}(0)=\bm{w}^{n}(0)
, we compute
the transition amplitude
c^{\psi}_{n}(t)
to some other arbitrary mode
\bm{w}^{m}(t)
to first order at time
t
by
first transforming back to the original picture (
S72
)

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}(t)=e^{iDt}\bm{\psi}_{I}(t)=e^{i\tilde{\Omega}_{n}t}\bm{w}^{n}(0)+e^{iDt}i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}V_{I}(t^{\prime})\bm{w}^{n}

(S77)

and then projecting onto

s_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}
[cf.
Eq.

S43
],

\displaystyle c^{\psi}_{m}(t)

\displaystyle=s_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\bm{\psi}(t)=s_{m}s_{n}\delta_{nm}e^{i(\tilde{\Omega}_{n}-\tilde{\Omega}_{m})t}+is_{m}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}V(t^{\prime})\bm{w}^{n}e^{i(\tilde{\Omega}_{n}-\tilde{\Omega}_{m})t^{\prime}},

(S78)

where we have utilized that the dynamical matrix is pseudo-hermitian,
D^{\dagger}=\tau_{z}D\tau_{z}
,
which implies that
\exp(iDt)^{\dagger}=\tau_{z}\exp(-iDt)\tau_{z}
.
Eventually, the probability for the system to transition into some distinct orthogonal state
m\neq n
is given by

\displaystyle p_{mn}\equiv\left\lvert c^{\psi}_{m}(t)\right\rvert^{2}=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}((\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}V(t^{\prime})\bm{w}^{n})e^{i(\tilde{\Omega}_{n}-\tilde{\Omega}_{m})t^{\prime}}\right\rvert^{2}.

(S79)

Next, we consider a periodic perturbation of the form
V(t)=F\cos(\omega t-\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}F(e^{i(\omega t-\varphi)}+e^{-i(\omega t-\varphi)})
,
where
F
is some constant real-valued amplitude.
Thus, using standard assumptions of
t\gg 1/\omega
and
\omega\simeq(\tilde{\Omega}_{m}-\tilde{\Omega}_{n})

[
126
]
,

Eq.

S79
becomes

\displaystyle p_{mn}(\omega,t)=\frac{2\pi}{4}t\left\lvert((\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}Fe^{i\varphi}\bm{w}^{n})\right\rvert^{2}\delta_{t}(\tilde{\Omega}_{m}-\tilde{\Omega}_{n}-\omega),

(S80)

where
\delta_{t}(\alpha)=\frac{\sin^{2}(\alpha t/2)}{\pi\alpha^{2}t}
approaches a Dirac-Delta distribution in the limit of large observation times
t
,
which then yields

Eq.

14
in the main text.

S8
Time-dependent perturbation theory for many-body Bogoliubov Fock states

In the main text we have shown that the symplectic quantum geometric tensor can be extracted from
integrated excitation rates to other Bogoliubov modes in response to weak periodic modulations of
bosonic BdG systems
Eq.

13
.
For this, we have used the results of first-order perturbation theory for the effective Schrödinger equation
(
S80
).
In this appendix, we now show that this derivation is equivalent to applying
standard first-order time-dependent perturbation theory (Fermi’s Golden rule) to
treat transitions between many-body Bogoliubov Fock states with quasiparticle excitations present.

Let us first consider the general case, where, in addition to the already solved (unperturbed) problem of (
S1
),
we apply a generic time-dependent particle-number-conserving perturbation of the form

V(t)=\sum_{i,j}\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}A_{ij}(t)\hat{a}_{j}
to the system (
S1
).
To understand how this perturbation affects the population of Bogoliubov quasiparticle
excitations, we express
V(t)
via the diagonal (quasiparticle) basis of the unperturbed problem using
(
S5
)

\displaystyle V(t)=\sum_{i,j}

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}A_{ij}(t)\hat{a}_{j}=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}(t)\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m}(U^{\dagger})_{mi}+\hat{b}_{m}(V^{T})_{mi}\right)A_{ij}(t)\left(U_{jm^{\prime}}\hat{b}_{m^{\prime}}+(V^{*})_{jm^{\prime}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle[\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m}\hat{b}_{m^{\prime}}\sum_{ij}(U^{\dagger})_{mi}A_{ij}(t)U_{jm^{\prime}}+\hat{b}_{m}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}\sum_{ij}(V^{T})_{mi}A_{ij}(t)(V^{*})_{jm^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}\sum_{ij}(U^{\dagger})_{mi}A_{ij}(t)(V^{*})_{jm^{\prime}}+\hat{b}_{m}\hat{b}_{m^{\prime}}\sum_{ij}(V^{T})_{mi}A_{ij}(t)U_{jm^{\prime}}].

(S93)

Note that the ground state of the original BdG Hamiltonian (
S22
)
contains zero Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitations.
In the main text, we have argued that such a generic perturbation (
S93
),
can be used to controllably create
pairs of quasiparticle excitations in desired Bogoliubov energy bands via terms
\propto\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}
or

\propto\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m}
[cf.
Fig.

2
(a) in the main text].
The presence of such Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitations is the starting point
for the protocol to extract the symplectic quantum geometric tensor from excitation rates between Bogoliubov modes,
as will show in the following
[cf.
Eq.

14
in the main text].

S8.1
Transitions between Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitations lead to SQGT

Now, as a first simple case, we consider the transition of single Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitation from some occupied
mode
\beta
to some unoccupied mode
\alpha
via the perturbation
V(t)
(
S93

As an initial state
{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}

with a single quasiparticle excitation in some mode
\beta
and, i.e.,
{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}={\lvert}\ldots,(0)_{\alpha},(1)_{\beta},\ldots{\rangle}

and as a final state the state with an excitation in some mode
\alpha
, i.e.,
{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}={\lvert}\ldots,(1)_{\alpha},(0)_{\beta},\ldots{\rangle}

Standard first-order time-dependent perturbation theory
[
126
]
, tells us that we have to look at matrix elements

{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}V(t){|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}
to analyze the transitions between the initial and final states.
So the only matrix elements of the perturbation
{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}V(t){|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}
that contribute to the transition
are only the ones which are number-conserving in the Bogoliubov basis, such that using (
S93
)
we obtain

\displaystyle{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}V(t){|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}={\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\rvert}\sum_{m,m^{\prime}}\left[\delta_{\alpha,m}\delta_{\beta,m^{\prime}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m}\hat{b}_{m^{\prime}}\sum_{ij}(U^{\dagger})_{mi}A_{ij}(t)U_{jm^{\prime}}+\delta_{\alpha,m^{\prime}}\delta_{\beta,m}\hat{b}_{m}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}\sum_{ij}(V^{T})_{mi}A_{ij}(t)(V^{*})_{jm^{\prime}}\right]{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}(U^{\dagger})_{\alpha i}A_{ij}(t)U_{j\beta}{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{b}_{\beta}{|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}+(V^{T})_{\beta i}A_{ij}(t)(V^{*})_{j\alpha}{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}\hat{b}_{\beta}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}{|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}(U^{\dagger})_{\alpha i}A_{ij}(t)U_{j\beta}+(V^{\dagger})_{\alpha j}(A^{*})_{ji}(t)V_{i\beta}=\sum_{ij}\begin{pmatrix}U^{\dagger}&V^{\dagger}\\
V^{T}&U^{T}\end{pmatrix}_{\alpha i}\ \begin{pmatrix}A(t)&0\\
0&A^{*}(t)\end{pmatrix}_{ij}\ \begin{pmatrix}U&V^{*}\\
V&U^{*}\end{pmatrix}_{j\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}(W^{\dagger})_{\alpha i}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ij}(t)W_{j\beta}=\bm{w}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(t)\bm{w}_{\beta},\ \text{with}\ \tilde{\mathcal{M}}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}A(t)&0\\
0&A^{*}(t)\end{pmatrix},

(S94)

where in the second to last step we have introduced the
2N\times 2N
matrix
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(t)
and
reformulated the Bogoliubov transformation in terms of the paraunitary matrix

W
(
S6
), where both
the
\alpha
and
\beta
indices are restricted to the particle sector, i.e.,

1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N
for the equality to hold.
In the last step of (
S94
), we have introduced the column vector
\bm{w}_{\beta}
given by the

\beta
-th column of
W

and the row vector
\bm{w}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}
given by the
\alpha
-th row of
W^{\dagger}
, where
again
1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq N
(restriction to the particle modes).

We can generalize this result to the case of an initial eigenstate of (
S22
)
with
n_{\beta}
quasiparticle excitations in mode
\beta
and
n_{\alpha}
excitations in mode
\alpha
,
i.e.,
{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}={\lvert}\ldots,(n_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(n_{\beta})_{\beta},\ldots{\rangle}
, and
a final state with
n_{\beta}-1
quasiparticle excitations in mode
\beta
, and
n_{\alpha}+1

excitation in mode
\alpha
, i.e.,
{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}={\lvert}\ldots,(n_{\alpha}+1)_{\alpha},(n_{\beta}-1)_{\beta},\ldots{\rangle}
.
This will yield a similar result as in (
S94
) but with an
additional bosonic enhancement factor of due to

\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{b}_{\beta}{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}=\sqrt{n_{\beta}(n_{\alpha}+1)}{\lvert}\phi_{\alpha}{\rangle}
,

\displaystyle{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}\mathcal{A}(t){|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}=\sqrt{n_{\beta}(n_{\alpha}+1)}\bm{w}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(t)\bm{w}_{\beta}.

(S95)

Now we explicitly specify the explicit form of the perturbation.
Inspired by Ref.
[
64
]
, we assume an original parameter-dependent coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}(\bm{\lambda}(t))
, where

\bm{\lambda}(t)=(\lambda^{1}(t),\ldots,\lambda^{r}(t))
is a set of time-dependent parameters.
We first restrict ourselves only to have parameter dependence in the diagonal (particle-number-conserving) blocks of
the coefficient matrix

\mathcal{M}(\bm{\lambda}(t))
, i.e.,
K(\bm{\lambda}(t))
and
G=\text{const.}
(cf.
Eq.

S1
).
The system shall initially be prepared at some parameter values
\bm{\lambda}=\bm{\lambda}_{0}
.
Let us then, for simplicity, consider a single parameter modulation
\lambda^{1}(t)
of the form

\lambda^{1}(t)=\lambda^{1}_{0}+2(A/\omega)\cos(\omega t)
with
A/\omega\ll 1
.
The rest of the parameters are assumed to be constant, i.e.,
\lambda^{\nu}(t)=\lambda^{\nu}_{0}
for
\nu\neq 1
.
We then Taylor-expand the relevant particle-number-conserving perturbation
K(\bm{\lambda}(t))
to first order

\displaystyle K(\bm{\lambda}(t))=K(\bm{\lambda}_{0})+\partial_{1}K(\bm{\lambda}_{0})\times(2A/\omega)\cos(\omega t)=K(\bm{\lambda}_{0})+\tilde{K}(t),

(S96)

\displaystyle\text{with}\ \tilde{K}(t)=f(t)\tilde{K},\ f(t)\equiv(2A/\omega)\cos(\omega t),\ \tilde{K}\equiv\partial_{1}K(\bm{\lambda}_{0}).

Here, the matrix
(\tilde{K})_{ij}(t)
plays the role of the matrix
A_{ij}(t)
in the general perturbation considered in

Eq.

S93
.
We note that this explicit form of the parameter modulations leading to (
S96
)
is not arbitrary
but readily appears in the co-moving frame of periodically shaken systems
(
S81
)
[
63
,
64
]
.

On the level of the Hamiltonian (
S1
)
the perturbation provides an additional term of the form

\displaystyle V(t)=\sum_{ij}\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\tilde{K}_{ij}(t)\hat{a}_{j}=\left(\sum_{ij}\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\tilde{K}_{ij}\hat{a}_{j}\right)f(t)\equiv\tilde{V}f(t),

(S97)

Now, initially, we assume the system to be in some eigenstate
{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}
of (
S22
).
As before, we let us first discuss the case of
a single excitation and generalize to multiple excitations later.
Thus, we start with a state with a single Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitation
in some mode
\beta
, i.e.,
{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}={\lvert}\ldots,(0)_{\alpha},(1)_{\beta},\ldots{\rangle}
with the corresponding
energy eigenvalue equation
\hat{H}{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}=\omega_{\beta}{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}
(
S22
).
Our goal is to compute the transition probability to some final eigenstate
{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}
, which for now shall consist
of a single Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitation in some other mode
\alpha
, i.e.,
{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}={\lvert}\ldots,(1)_{\alpha},(0)_{\beta},\ldots{\rangle}

with corresponding energy eigenvalue equation
\hat{H}{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}=\omega_{\alpha}{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}
.

Then, given the periodic perturbation of type (
S96
)-(
S97
),
we can use Fermi’s Golden rule
(first-order time-dependent perturbation theory)
[
126
]
to compute this transition probability
at time
t
with via

\displaystyle n_{\alpha\beta}(\omega,t)

\displaystyle=2\pi t\left\lvert{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}\tilde{V}{|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}\right\rvert^{2}\delta(\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta}-\omega)=2\pi t\left\lvert{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}\sum_{ij}\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger}\tilde{K}_{ij}\hat{a}_{j}{|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}\right\rvert^{2}\delta(\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta}-\omega),

(S98)

where
\hbar\equiv 1
in the following.
Into
Eq.

S98
we can insert the explicit expansion of the perturbation
\tilde{V}

(
S97
) in terms of the
Bogoliubov quasiparticle operators (
S93
)-(
S94
)
leading to

\displaystyle n_{\alpha\beta}(\omega,t)

\displaystyle=2\pi t\left\lvert\sum_{ij}[(U^{\dagger})_{\alpha i}\tilde{K}_{ij}U_{j\beta}{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}\hat{b}_{\beta}{|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}+(V^{T})_{\beta i}\tilde{K}_{ij}(V^{*})_{j\alpha}{\langle}\psi_{\alpha}{\,|}\hat{b}_{\beta}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}{|\,}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}]\right\rvert^{2}\delta(\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta}-\omega)

\displaystyle=2\pi t\frac{A^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\left\lvert\bm{w}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\partial_{1}{\mathcal{M}})\bm{w}_{\beta}\right\rvert^{2}\delta(\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta}-\omega).

(S99)

Next, we compute the total excitation rate by summing
Eq.

S99

over all possible final states

{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}
with a single excitation in some mode
\alpha
,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}(\omega)=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}n_{\alpha\beta}(\omega,t)=\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}2\pi\frac{A^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\left\lvert\bm{w}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\partial_{1}{\mathcal{M}})\bm{w}_{\beta}\right\rvert^{2}\delta(\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta}-\omega)

(S100)

Then, inspired by Ref.
[
63
]
, we introduce the integrated excitation rate
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\text{int}}=\int d\omega\,\Gamma^{\alpha}(\omega)=2\pi A^{2}\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta,\ \alpha=1}^{N}\frac{\left\lvert\bm{w}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\partial_{1}\mathcal{M})\bm{w}_{\beta}\right\rvert^{2}}{(\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta})^{2}}.

(S101)

The last expression already resembles the spectral representation of the symplectic quantum metric
(
S49
),
although not exactly, since the sum over
\alpha
in (
S101
) is only
restricted to the particle Bogoliubov modes
(\bm{w}_{\alpha}^{\dagger})_{i}=(W^{\dagger})_{\alpha i}
with

1\leq\alpha\leq N
.
However, since the spectral representation of the symplectic quantum metric is defined via the sum
over all Bogoliubov modes, including the hole modes with
N+1\leq\alpha\leq 2N
,
we need to extend the sum in (
S101
).
This is allowed by the fact that the operator
\partial_{1}\mathcal{M}

(with only particle-number-conserving parts)
does not couple the particle and hole Bogoliubov modes.
By this, we arrive at

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{int}}=\int d\omega\sum_{\alpha}\Gamma^{\alpha}(\omega)=2\pi A^{2}\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta,\ \alpha=1}^{2N}s_{\alpha}\frac{\left\lvert\bm{w}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(\partial_{1}\mathcal{M})\bm{w}_{\beta}\right\rvert^{2}}{(\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta})^{2}}=2\pi A^{2}g_{11}^{\beta}.

(S102)

We observe that the integrated excitation rates between states
{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}
and
{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}
,
with single Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitations, give rise to the symplectic quantum metric (
S49
).
This agrees with the result derived from first-order perturbation theory
for the effective Schrödinger equation
(
S80
)
[
Eq.

15
in the main text].

As before this result can be generalized, to the case of an initial Bogoliubov Fock state
with
n_{\beta}
quasiparticle excitations in mode
\beta
and
n_{\alpha}
excitations in mode
\alpha
,
i.e.,
{\lvert}\phi_{\beta}{\rangle}={\lvert}\ldots,(n_{\alpha})_{\alpha},(n_{\beta})_{\beta},\ldots{\rangle}
, and
a final state with
n_{\beta}-1
quasiparticle excitations in mode
\beta
, and
n_{\beta}

excitation in mode
\alpha
, i.e.,
{\lvert}\psi_{\alpha}{\rangle}={\lvert}\ldots,(n_{\alpha}+1)_{\alpha},(n_{\beta}-1)_{\beta},\ldots{\rangle}
.
This will lead to an additional bosonic enhancement factor of
\sqrt{n_{\beta}}

in the transition matrix elements as in (
S95
).
The energy difference between the initial and final state is still given by

[(n_{\alpha}+1)\omega_{\alpha}+\omega_{\beta}(n_{\beta}-1)]-[\omega_{\beta}n_{\beta}-\omega_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}]=\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta}

such that we can directly generalize the Fermi’s Golden rule result of

Eq.

S102
to

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{int}}=\int d\omega\sum_{\alpha}\Gamma^{\alpha}(\omega)=n_{\beta}(n_{\alpha}+1)\times 2\pi A^{2}g_{11}^{\beta}.

(S103)

S9
Adiabatic perturbation theory for the effective Schrödinger equation

In this appendix, we derive the first order correction to the adiabatic theorem for the
time evolution of Bogoliubov modes (
S67
).
This is a prerequisite for deriving the symplectic anomalous velocity term in the following

Section

S10
.
To this end, we adapt the standard theory of adiabatic perturbation theory for conventional
systems
[
128
,
129
]
to the symplectic Bogoliubov structure here.

We consider a time-dependent dynamical matrix
D(t)\equiv D(\bm{\lambda}(t))
, where the time dependence
is encoded via some dimensionless parameters
\bm{\lambda}(t)=(\lambda^{1}(t),\dots,\lambda^{r}(t))
.
At each point in time
t
, we assume that the Bogoliubov system is thermodynamically stable
(the coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}(\bm{\lambda}(t))
is positive definite
\forall t

[
69
]
),
such that an instantaneous eigenvalue equation of the following form holds
(
S21
)

\displaystyle D(t)\bm{w}^{n}(t)=\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(t)\bm{w}^{n}(t),

(S104)

where the time-dependence of the Bogoliubov modes

\bm{w}^{n}(t)
is again specified through the parameters
\bm{\lambda}(t)
.
In what follows, we assume a non-degenerate spectrum

\tilde{\Omega}_{n}\neq\tilde{\Omega}_{m}
for
n\neq m
for all times
t
.
This assumption can be relaxed
both regarding states that are not coupled to
\bm{w}^{n}
as a result of symmetry
and when considering the adiabatic theorem
with respect to a degenerate eigenspace, which is however beyond the scope of this work.
We are interested in the time-evolution an arbitrary state
\bm{\psi}(t)
in the space spanned by the Bogoliubov modes
(
S43
),

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}(t)=\sum_{n}c_{n}(t)\bm{w}^{n}(t),

(S105)

which is governed by the effective time-dependent Schrödinger equation (
S67
),

\displaystyle-i\partial_{t}\bm{\psi}(t)=D(t)\bm{\psi}(t).

(S106)

Now, under the assumption of slow parameter variations of
\bm{\lambda}(t)
(adiabatic limit),
we would like to solve
Eq.

S106
perturbatively.
To this end, we first insert the instantaneous expansion of
\bm{\psi}(t)

(
S105
) into
the effective Schrödinger equation (
S106
),
then project onto a specific mode via multiplying with

s_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}
from the left,
by which we arrive

\displaystyle\dot{c}_{m}+\sum_{n}c_{n}s_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{t}\bm{w}^{n}=i\tilde{\Omega}_{m}c_{m}.

(S107)

The explicit time-dependence will be omitted for brevity in the following.
Next, we perform a gauge transformation

c_{m}(t)\rightarrow c_{m}(t)e^{i\xi_{m}(t)}
,
where
\xi_{m}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\tilde{\Omega}_{m}(t^{\prime})
.
This removes the dynamical phase factor from the equations, which will, however,
obviously be reintroduced at the end.
This leads to the following differential equation for the time-dependent coefficients
c_{m}(t)

\displaystyle\dot{c}_{m}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n}c_{n}s_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{t}\bm{w}^{n}e^{i\xi_{nm}(t)}

(S108)

with
\xi_{nm}(t)\equiv\xi_{n}(t)-\xi_{m}(t)
.
We formally integrate (
S108
) and obtain

\displaystyle c_{m}(t)=c_{m}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{n}c_{n}(t^{\prime})s_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}e^{i\xi_{nm}(t^{\prime})}

(S109)

where we have used that

\partial_{t}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\lambda}(t))=\sum_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}

with
\partial_{\mu}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda^{\mu}}
.

Let us now consider the situation, where the system is initially
occupying a single Bogoliubov mode, i.e.,
\bm{\psi}(0)=\bm{w}^{m}

in (
S105
),
such that
c_{m}(0)=1
and
c_{n}(0)=0
for
n\neq m
.
In the limit of
\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}\to 0
all transition amplitudes to other modes
are highly suppressed, and the only leading order contribution in (
S109
)
stems from the diagonal
n=m
term in the sum
[
129
]
.
Thus, to zeroth order, the solution for
c_{m}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle c_{m}(t)=c_{m}(0)e^{i\gamma^{m}(t)}.

(S110)

Here,
\gamma^{m}
denotes the symplectic version of the Berry phase
[
130
]

\displaystyle\gamma^{m}(t)=i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}s_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}=\int_{\bm{\lambda}(0)}^{\bm{\lambda}(t)}A_{\mu}^{m}(\bm{\lambda})d\lambda^{\mu}=\int A^{m},

(S111)

which may also written as an integral over the symplectic Berry connection
[
103
,
11
,
47
,
6
]

\displaystyle A^{m}=A^{m}_{\mu}d\lambda^{\mu}=[is_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m}]d\lambda^{\mu}.

(S112)

The result (
S110
) represents
the conventional adiabatic theorem (adiabatic limit) adapted to the
time evolution Bogoliubov modes, i.e., under slow parameter variations the system will remain
in the initially occupied mode while acquiring an additional symplectic Berry phase
\gamma^{m}(t)
.
We stress that this represents the
0
-th order contribution, since Berry phase is independent
of the rate of change in the parameters

\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}
[cf.
Eq.

S111
].

Next, we aim to solve (
S109
) perturbatively, to self-consistently
obtain corrections to first order in
\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}
.
To this end, we insert the
0
-th order solution
c_{m}(t)
(
S110
)
into the right-hand side of the
Eq.

S109
, by which we obtain

\displaystyle c_{m}(t)

\displaystyle\approx c_{m}(0)-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\sum_{n}c_{n}(0)e^{i\gamma^{n}(t^{\prime})}s_{m}(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{n}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}e^{i\xi_{nm}(t^{\prime})}

(S113)

Now for all other coefficients
c_{k}(t)
with
k\neq m
,
we only need to consider the
n=m
term within the sum in (
S113
),
where
m
is again the index of the initially occupied mode.
To first order, this leads to the following equation for
c_{k\neq m}(t)

\displaystyle c_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}c_{m}(0)e^{i\gamma^{m}(t^{\prime})}s_{k}(\bm{w}^{k})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}e^{i\xi_{km}(t^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}e^{i\gamma^{m}(t^{\prime})}s_{k}\frac{(\bm{w}^{k})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m}}{\tilde{\Omega}_{km}}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}\frac{d}{dt^{\prime}}e^{i\xi_{km}(t^{\prime})},

(S114)

with
\tilde{\Omega}_{km}\equiv\tilde{\Omega}_{k}-\tilde{\Omega}_{m}
and where
we have used that
\frac{d}{dt^{\prime}}e^{i\xi_{km}(t^{\prime})}=\tilde{\Omega}_{km}e^{i\xi_{km}(t^{\prime})}
.
Due to the fast oscillating exponential factor in
Eq.

S114
,
we can integrate this expression by parts
[
129
]
and obtain the following
solution for the off-diagonal coefficients

\displaystyle c_{k}(t)=is_{k}e^{i\gamma^{m}(t)}\frac{(\bm{w}^{k})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m}}{\tilde{\Omega}_{km}}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}e^{i\xi_{km}(t)}.

(S115)

Thus, to first order in
\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}
,
the state
\bm{\psi}(t)
(
S105
)
is finally given by

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}(t)=

\displaystyle e^{-i(\xi_{m}(t)-\gamma^{m}(t))}\left[\bm{w}^{m}+i\sum_{k\neq m}\bm{w}^{k}s_{k}\frac{(\bm{w}^{k})^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{\mu}\bm{w}^{m}}{(\tilde{\Omega}_{km})}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(\xi_{m}(t)-\gamma^{m}(t))}\left[\bm{w}^{m}+i\sum_{k\neq m}\bm{w}^{k}s_{k}\frac{(\bm{w}^{k})^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{M}\bm{w}^{m}}{(\tilde{\Omega}_{km})^{2}}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}\right],

(S116)

where we have reintroduced the dynamical phase
c_{n}(t)\to c_{n}(t)e^{-i\xi_{n}(t)}

and utilized the symplectic version of the Hellmann-Feynman theorem
(
S48
)
in the second equality.

Eq.

S116
constitutes the first
order correction (in
\dot{\lambda}^{\mu}
)
to the adiabatic theorem for the time evolution of Bogoliubov modes.

If required, one may also normalize the state
\bm{\psi}(t)
(
S116
)
up to second order
[
131
]
,
i.e., up to
(\dot{\lambda}^{\mu})^{2}\equiv(v^{\mu})^{2}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}(t)=e^{-i(\xi_{m}(t)-\gamma^{m}(t))}\left[(1+\beta_{\mu}^{m}(v^{\mu})^{2})\bm{w}^{m}+iv^{\mu}\sum_{k\neq m}\alpha^{km}_{\mu}\bm{w}^{k}\right],

(S117)

where have defined

\alpha^{km}_{\mu}\equiv s_{k}\frac{(\bm{w}^{k})^{\dagger}\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{M}\bm{w}^{m}}{(\tilde{\Omega}_{km})^{2}}

with
\beta_{\mu}^{m}\equiv-\frac{1}{2}s_{m}\sum_{k\neq m}\left\lvert\alpha^{km}_{\mu}\right\rvert^{2}
.

S10
Symplectic anomalous velocity

In this appendix, we provide a more detailed derivation of the symplectic anomalous velocity term,
which is proportional to the symplectic Berry curvature.
Consider a translationally invariant BdG system such that the Bogoliubov eigenmodes of the
dynamical matrix
D
admit Bloch function type solution, i.e.,
D\bm{\psi}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=De^{i\bm{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{r}}}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})e^{i\bm{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{r}}}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
.
Here,
\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
is the cell-periodic part of the Bogoliubov Bloch function for the
n
-th band,
which fulfill the Bogoliubov eigenvalue equation for the quasimomentum transformed dynamical matrix

D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
.
To make connections to the conventional anomalous velocity, we consider a two-dimensional
time-dependent quasimomentum parameter space
\bm{\mathbf{q}}(t)=(q_{x}(t),q_{y}(t))
.
We imagine a uniform force
\bm{\mathbf{F}}
to be applied to the Bogoliubov
system of interest, such that quasimomenta satisfy

\dot{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\bm{\mathbf{F}}
, leading to
\bm{\mathbf{q}}(t)=\bm{\mathbf{q}}(0)+\bm{\mathbf{F}}t
.

Next, we consider an initially populated Bogoliubov particle mode
\bm{\psi}(0)=\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
and
let the applied field point in the
x
-direction, i.e.,
\bm{\mathbf{F}}=F_{x}\bm{e}_{x}
.
Using the results of
Eq.

S116
,
we see that the solution to the time-dependent effective Schrödinger equation for
the Bogoliubov modes is, to first order in
F_{x}
, given by

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})+iF_{x}\sum_{m\neq n}\bm{w}^{m}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})s_{n}\frac{(\bm{w}^{m}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}))^{\dagger}\partial_{q_{x}}\mathcal{M}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})}{(\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})-\tilde{\Omega}_{m}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}))^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(F_{x}^{2}),

(S118)

where the irrelevant phase factors have been omitted for clarity.
To obtain the velocity in the
y
-direction to first order in
F_{x}
, we take the expectation value of
the quasiparticle velocity operator
v_{y}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\partial_{q_{y}}D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})

with respect to the state of
Eq.

S118
,
where
D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
is again the dynamical matrix in the quasimomentum space representation.
Note that
v_{y}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\partial_{q_{y}}D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})

just follows from the Heisenberg equations of motion for the position operator
\bm{\mathbf{r}}

transformed into the quasimomentum space
[
65
]
and then extended to Nambu space.
The velocity then equates to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle v_{y}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\rangle&=(\bm{\psi}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}))^{\dagger}\tau_{z}\partial_{q_{y}}D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{\psi}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\\
&=(\bm{w}^{n})^{\dagger}\partial_{q_{y}}\mathcal{M}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{w}^{n}+\left(iF_{x}\sum_{m\neq n}s_{n}\frac{\bm{w}^{n}\partial_{q_{y}}\mathcal{M}\bm{w}^{m}\times(\bm{w}^{m})^{\dagger}\partial_{q_{x}}\mathcal{M}\bm{w}^{n}}{(\tilde{\Omega}_{n}-\tilde{\Omega}_{m})^{2}}+\text{c.c.}\right)+\mathcal{O}(F_{x}^{2}).\end{split}

(S119)

By making use of the eigenvalue equation for the Bogoliubov modes,

D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})

(
S21
),
as well as the spectral representation of the symplectic
Berry curvature
B^{n}_{\mu\nu}=-2\,\mathrm{Im}[\eta^{n}_{\mu\nu}]
(
S49
),
we see that the velocity
v_{y}
of the Bogoliubov state in
n
-th band is given by

\displaystyle\langle v_{y}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\rangle=\frac{\partial\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})}{\partial q_{y}}+F_{x}B^{n}_{xy}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}).

(S120)

By symmetry, we can immediately generalize this result (
S120
) to
general directions of the applied force
\bm{\mathbf{F}}

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\mathbf{v}}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\rangle=\nabla_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})+\bm{\mathbf{B}}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\times\bm{\mathbf{F}},

(S121)

with
\bm{\mathbf{B}}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\equiv\epsilon_{\mu\nu\kappa}B^{n}_{\mu\nu}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{e}_{\kappa}
.
Here, the first term corresponds to the slope of the occupied Bogoliubov energy band
\tilde{\Omega}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
and the second term
is proportional to the symplectic Berry curvature
\bm{\mathbf{B}}_{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
.
The second term describes a transverse velocity component
and may be viewed as a symplectic generalization of the conventional anomalous velocity term
[
65
,
66
,
67
]
.

The results above may easily be generalized to the case of an initial Bogoliubov wave packet consisting
of a superposition (particle) Bogoliubov modes
\bm{\psi}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=e^{i\bm{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{r}}}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
of a
given band
n
,

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{n}=\sum_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}c(\bm{\mathbf{q}})e^{i\bm{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{r}}}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}),

(S122)

which is normalized with respect to the Bogoliubov norm, i.e.,

{\langle}\phi^{n}{\,|}\tau_{z}{|\,}\phi^{n}{\rangle}=\sum_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\left\lvert c(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\right\rvert^{2}=1

[
103
]
.
The velocity of such a Bogoliubov wave packet may then be related to a bulk observable,
such as the total current density
j_{y}
in real space

\displaystyle j_{y}=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\rho(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\langle v_{y}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\rangle=\int_{1.\,\text{BZ}}d^{2}\bm{\mathbf{q}}\ D(\bm{\mathbf{r}},\bm{\mathbf{q}})\rho(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\langle v_{y}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\rangle,

(S123)

where
L^{2}
is the area of the system and

\rho(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\left\lvert c(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\right\rvert^{2}
the distribution function of Bogoliubov quasiparticles in the
respective band.
We note that special care is required when switching to the continuum of quasimomentum states in
Eq.

S123
, as a so-called modified density of states
D(\bm{\mathbf{r}},\bm{\mathbf{q}})
must be included for the integration when a finite Berry curvature is present (see also Refs.
[
103
,
133
,
142
,
65
]
).
Although
j_{y}
(
S123
) is not quantized by the (symplectic) Chern number of the respective Bogoliubov band (for the typical case of a non-uniformly populated Bogoliubov band),
the current density still gives rise to a finite transverse velocity contribution,
which is directly related to the anomalous term governed by the underlying symplectic Berry curvature of the Bogoliubov band.
Our results (
S121
), which are based on first order adiabatic perturbation theory, are complementary to those of Ref.
[
103
]
.
The authors of Ref.
[
103
]
have also studied the dynamics of the Bogoliubov wave packets using a time-dependent variational principle, however, no explicit connection to the symplectic Berry curvature has been made therein.
Based on Ref.
[
103
]
, we see that the anomalous velocity term can also be verified by measuring the modified thermal depletion of a condensate for a weakly interacting BEC.

In this appendix, we provide a brief derivation of the Bogoliubov-Haldane model

Adding to this, we also review the numerical algorithm described in Refs.

The starting point is a Bose-Hubbard model on a two-dimensional lattice with two sublattice sites,

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\sum_{\ell\ell^{\prime},ss^{\prime}}h_{\ell s,\ell^{\prime}s^{\prime}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\ell s}\hat{a}_{\ell^{\prime}s^{\prime}}+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{\ell,s}\hat{n}_{\ell s}(\hat{n}_{\ell s}-1)

The non-interacting (quadratic) part is governed by the matrix elements

in position space and the interacting part by the on-site interaction strength

The first summand is the non-interacting part, which may diagonalized by first moving into quasimomentum space via

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{ss^{\prime}}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}h_{\ell s,\ell^{\prime}s^{\prime}}e^{-i(\bm{\mathbf{r}}_{\ell s}-\bm{\mathbf{r}}_{\ell^{\prime}s^{\prime}})\cdot\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\left(h_{0}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\mathbbm{1}+\bm{\mathbf{h}}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}\right)_{ss^{\prime}},

At each quasimomentum point,
\bm{\mathbf{q}}

describes an operator acting on a two-dimensional
Hilbert space, whose elements can be represented on the Bloch sphere.
The north and south pole are denoted by
{\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}A{\rangle}
and
{\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}B{\rangle}

Due to the non-interacting nature of the system, the model can be further diagonalized by rotating into the eigenbasis

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}-{\rangle}&=\sin(\theta_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}/2){\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}A{\rangle}-\cos(\theta_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}/2)e^{i\varphi_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}}{\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}B{\rangle},\quad{\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}+{\rangle}=\cos(\theta_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}/2){\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}A{\rangle}+\sin(\theta_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}/2)e^{i\varphi_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}}{\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}B{\rangle}.\end{split}

(S126)

which reside at
\pm\hat{\bm{\mathbf{h}}}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})

in (

\bm{\mathbf{q}}_{0}

in the lower band
\epsilon_{-}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})

{\lvert}\psi_{B}{\rangle}=(\sqrt{N!})^{-1}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}_{0}-})^{N}{\lvert}\text{vac}{\rangle}

Here,

shall denote the total number of bosons in the system and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},\pm}
the creation operator
for a boson at quasimomentum

In this work, we consider the Haldane model

Having discussed the non-interacting part, we turn to the Bogoliubov theory, which accounts for additional
quantum (and thermal) fluctuations on top of a mean-field solution for a weakly interacting bosonic system
[
121
,
122
,
135
]
.
For this, we first obtain a mean-field solution via a minimization of an associated Gross-Pitaevskii (GP) energy functional.
The stationary solutions gained by minimizing the GP energy functional will then yield the mean-field ground state, which
is assumed to be macroscopically occupied.
Then, we will describe quantum fluctuations on top of the condensate, by considering
non-condensed deviations from this mean-field solution.
This procedure can be formally understood by replacing the operators within the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
(
S124
)
via
\hat{a}_{\ell s}\to\sqrt{n_{0}}\zeta_{\ell s}+\hat{a}_{\ell s}
,
where the first term denotes the complex-valued mean-field solution,
\zeta_{\ell s}\in\mathbb{C}
, and the second term
the additional quantum fluctuations where
n_{0}
denotes condensate
density of the system.
Expanding the Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian (
S124
) in orders of the condensate
density
n_{0}
then yields
[
23
]

\displaystyle\hat{H}=n_{0}E_{\text{GP}}+n_{0}^{1/2}\hat{H}^{(L)}+\hat{H}^{(B)}+\mathcal{O}\left(n_{0}^{-1/2}\right).

(S131)

The first term denotes the GP energy functional
E_{\text{GP}}
which is a function of the mean-field solutions

\zeta_{\ell s}
.
The next two terms
\hat{H}^{(L)}
and
\hat{H}^{(B)}
are linear and quadratic in the bosonic operators

\hat{a}^{(\dagger)}_{\ell s}
, where the latter is known as the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian, which is sought after in the following.

First, the energy functional
E_{\text{GP}}
is found by replacing the operators
\hat{a}^{(\dagger)}_{\ell s}
in

\hat{H}
by complex-valued functions
\sqrt{n}\zeta_{\ell s}^{(*)}
,
where it is assumed that almost all bosons are condensed, i.e.,

n_{0}\approx n=N/V
, with
V=2N_{\text{uc}}
and
N
denoting the total number of bosons in the system
and
N_{\text{uc}}
the number of unit cells in the lattice
[
23
]
.
For the parameters of the bosonic Haldane model considered here, the condensate is formed at the single zero quasimomentum mode

\bm{\mathbf{q}}=\bm{0}
in the lower band such that a suitable ansatz for the ground state is given
by a spatially homogeneous function
\zeta_{\ell s}=\chi_{s},\ s\in\{A,B\}
.
This leads to the following energy functional in quasimomentum space

\displaystyle E_{GP}=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{A}^{*}\ \chi^{*}_{B}\end{pmatrix}(\mathcal{H}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}=\bm{0})-\mu_{\text{eff}}\mathbbm{1}_{2})\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{A}\\
\chi_{B}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{U}{2}n\left(\left\lvert\chi_{A}\right\rvert^{4}+\left\lvert\chi_{B}\right\rvert^{4}\right),

(S132)

where we have introduced
\mu_{\text{eff}}
as a Lagrange multiplier for satisfying the
conservation of the total number of bosons in the system, i.e.,
\sum_{\ell s}\left\lvert\zeta_{\ell s}\right\rvert^{2}=N
.
We minimize
E_{GP}
by taking the variational derivative of
E_{GP}
with respect to
\chi_{s}^{*}
which leads to
the so-called GP equations,

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{H}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}=\bm{0})-\mu_{\text{eff}}\mathbbm{1}_{2}\big)\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{A}\\
\chi_{B}\end{pmatrix}+nU\begin{pmatrix}\left\lvert\chi_{A}\right\rvert^{2}\chi_{A}\\
\left\lvert\chi_{B}\right\rvert^{2}\chi_{B}\end{pmatrix}=0.

(S133)

Since the non-interacting condensate is formed at the
{\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}=\bm{0},-{\rangle}
mode we parametrize
\chi_{s}
via the Bloch
sphere representation
{\lvert}\bm{\mathbf{q}}-{\rangle}
(
S126
)
[
24
]

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{A}\\
\chi_{B}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\sin(\theta/2)\\
-\cos(\theta/2)e^{i\varphi}\end{pmatrix}.

(S134)

Multiplying (
S133
) by
\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{A}^{*}\,\chi_{B}^{*}\end{pmatrix}
and
\begin{pmatrix}-\chi_{B}^{*}\,\chi_{A}^{*}\end{pmatrix}
from the left
yields three equations
[
24
,
136

\displaystyle(h_{0}(\bm{0})-\mu_{\text{eff}})-h(\bm{0})[\cos(\theta_{0})\cos(\theta)+\sin(\theta_{0})\sin(\theta)\cos(\varphi-\varphi_{0})]+2Un(\left\lvert\chi_{A}\right\rvert^{4}+\left\lvert\chi_{B}\right\rvert^{4})=0,

(S135)

\displaystyle h(\bm{0})[\cos(\theta_{0})\sin(\theta)\cos(\varphi)-\cos(\varphi_{0})\sin(\theta_{0})\cos(\theta)]-Un\sin(\theta)\cos(\theta)\cos(\varphi)=0,

(S136)

\displaystyle(h_{0}(\bm{0})-\mu_{\text{eff}})\sin(\theta)\sin(\varphi)+h(\bm{0})\sin(\theta_{0})\sin(\varphi_{0})+Un\sin(\theta)\sin(\varphi)=0,

(S137)

where
\bm{\mathbf{h}}(\bm{0})=h(\bm{0})\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\varphi_{0})\sin(\theta_{0}),\sin(\varphi_{0})\sin(\theta_{0}),\cos(\theta_{0})\end{pmatrix}^{T}

and
h_{0}(\bm{0})
stem from (
S125

For a given interaction energy

Since the BEC occurs in the lowest band at

(S139)

\beta_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\hat{b}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},+}\,\hat{b}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},-}\,\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}},+}\,\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}},-}\end{pmatrix}^{T}

and
\beta_{+}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\hat{b}_{\bm{0},+}\,\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{0},+}\end{pmatrix}^{T}

consisting of the new bosonic quasi-particle annihilation (creation) operators

\hat{b}^{(\dagger)}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},\pm}
at quasimomentum
\bm{\mathbf{q}}
in the upper (
+
) or lower Bogoliubov energy band (
-
).
The
4\times 4
(
2\times 2
) Bogoliubov transformation matrix
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
(
W_{+}
) can be parametrized
(
S6
) as

\displaystyle W_{+}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}u_{\bm{0}}&v_{\bm{0}}^{*}\\
v_{\bm{0}}&u_{\bm{0}}^{*}\end{pmatrix},\quad W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\begin{pmatrix}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&V_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{*}\\
V_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&U_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{*}\end{pmatrix}

(S147)

\displaystyle\text{with}\ U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}u_{A,+}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&u_{A,-}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\\
u_{B,+}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&u_{B,-}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix},\quad V_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{A,+}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&v_{A,-}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\\
v_{B,+}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&v_{B,-}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}.

(S148)

Since these quasi-particle operators still have to obey the bosonic commutation relations,
the Bogoliubov transformation matrix is paraunitary [cf.
Eq.

S37
], i.e.,

W^{\dagger}\tau W=W\tau W^{\dagger}=\tau,\ \text{with}\ \tau=\sigma_{z}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}

(
\tau=\sigma_{z}
) for
W=W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
(
W=W_{+}
).
These paraunitary matrices are specifically constructed
[
2
,
11
]
such that

\displaystyle W^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\text{diag}\begin{pmatrix}E_{+}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}),E_{-}(\bm{\mathbf{q}}),E_{+}(-\bm{\mathbf{q}}),E_{-}(-\bm{\mathbf{q}})\end{pmatrix},\quad W^{\dagger}_{+}\mathcal{M}_{+}W_{+}=\text{diag}\begin{pmatrix}E_{+}(\bm{0}),E_{+}(\bm{0})\end{pmatrix},

(S149)

holds,
by which the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian is eventually diagonalized
[
24
]
to

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(B)}=\sum_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}E_{+}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},+}\hat{b}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},+}+\sum_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}\neq 0}E_{-}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},-}\hat{b}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},-}+\text{const.}\,.

(S150)

Here,
E_{\pm}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
is the Bogoliubov energy spectrum of the upper (
+
) and lower (
-
) band.

The Bogoliubov energy spectrum can be obtained by (numerically) solving the energy eigenvalue problem
(
S21
) of the (non-hermitian) dynamical matrix

D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\tau_{z}\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
, i.e.,
D(\bm{\mathbf{q}})\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})=\tilde{\Omega}_{n}\bm{w}^{n}(\bm{\mathbf{q}})
.
However, the eigenmodes obtained from this diagonalization procedure
do
not
lead to the correct paraunitary conditions for the Bogoliubov modes
(
S37
).

As described in Refs.
[
2
,
11
]
,
the correct paraunitary Bogoliubov matrices can be obtained via an algorithm, which
includes a Cholesky decomposition of the coefficient matrices (
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}},\ \mathcal{M}_{+}
).
For completeness, we briefly review this algorithm in the next section.

S11.1
Algorithm for constructing paraunitary Bogoliubov transformation matrix

Here, mainly following Refs.
[
2
,
11
]
,
we briefly discuss how to (numerically) obtain a paraunitary Bogoliubov transformation matrix
such as
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
and
W_{+}
(
S147
)
when given a bosonic BdG Hamiltonian.

We discuss the algorithm for a translationally invariant bosonic BdG Hamiltonian
such that we have quasimomentum dependent
2N\times 2N
(hermitian) coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
(
S1
)
of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\begin{pmatrix}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&G_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\\
G^{*}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&K^{*}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix},

(S151)

with
K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
and
G_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
being quasimomentum dependent
N\times N
matrices with
N
denoting
the number internal degrees of freedom (e.g., the number sublattice sites within a unit cell).
For each quasimomentum
\bm{\mathbf{q}}
the bosonic system
can then be diagonalized by a paraunitary matrix
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
(
S18
)
such that

\displaystyle W^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix},\ \text{with}\ \bm{\omega}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\text{diag}\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{1,\bm{\mathbf{q}}},\ldots,\omega_{N,\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix},

(S152)

where
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
again fulfill the paraunitary conditions (
S37
),

\displaystyle W^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\tau_{z}W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\tau_{z}W^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\tau_{z}\ \text{with}\ \tau_{z}=\sigma_{z}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N}.

(S153)

Now, given that
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
is a positive-definite matrix
(all eigenvalues of
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
are strictly positive),
the paraunitary matrix
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
can then be constructed
via the following steps
[
2
]
.

First, we apply a Cholesky decomposition to the coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}

(which is possible since
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
is positive-definite)
(
S151
), which decomposes

\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
into a product between an upper triangular matrix
K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}

and its hermitian conjugate
K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
.
We note that the positive-definiteness of
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
also ensures the existence of
the inverse
K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{-1}
.

Second, we introduce a hermitian matrix
T_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\equiv K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\tau_{z}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}

from which we obtain the unitary matrix
U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
via the eigendecomposition of
T_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
,

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}T_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&-\bm{\omega}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}.

(S154)

These two diagonal matrices

\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
and
\bm{\omega}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
already provide
the Bogoliubov energy spectrum of
Eq.

S152

[
11
]
.
One can further show that both
\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
and
\bm{\omega}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
are real and strictly positive
[
2
]
.

Third, the paraunitary matrix
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
is then constructed via

\displaystyle W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{-1}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix},

(S155)

which will diagonalize the coefficient matrix
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
(
S151
)
and importantly fulfill the paraunitary conditions (
S153
).
This concludes the construction of the paraunitary matrix
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
for each quasimomentum
\bm{\mathbf{q}}
.

One can then show the diagonalization of
Eq.

S152
by the
direct application of (
S155
)

\displaystyle W^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=W^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}(K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger})^{-1}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{-1}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}.

(S156)

Moreover, we can see that the construction of
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
(
S155
)
also fulfills the paraunitary conditions (
S153
),

\displaystyle W^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\tau_{z}W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}(K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger})^{-1}\tau_{z}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{-1}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}T_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{-1}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}

(S157)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{-1}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&-\bm{\omega}^{-1}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&\bm{\omega}^{1/2}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{1}_{N}&0\\
0&-\mathbbm{1}_{N}\end{pmatrix}=\tau_{z},

(S158)

where in the first step we have just inserted the definition of
W_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}

(
S155
).
In the second equality, we used that
T_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{-1}=(K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger})^{-1}\tau_{z}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{-1}
,
which itself directly follows from
T_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\tau_{z}K_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}
.
In the second equality, following from (
S154
)
we used that
U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{\dagger}T_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}^{-1}U_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{\omega}^{-1}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}&0\\
0&-\bm{\omega}^{-1}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\end{pmatrix}
.

S11.2
Topological classification of the Bogoliubov-Haldane model

We briefly discuss the topological classification of the Bogoliubov-Haldane model (
S144
).
In BBdG systems, time-reversal symmetry (TRS) is given by
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=(\mathcal{M}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}})^{*}

[
41
]
,
where the particle-hole symmetry (PHS) is given by

\tau_{x}(\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}})^{*}\tau_{x}=\mathcal{M}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
(
S24
).
Here, the Bogoliubov-Haldane Hamiltonian (
S144
)
explicitly breaks TRS,
\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\neq(\mathcal{M}_{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}})^{*}
, due the Peierls phases
\theta
and the chiral symmetry (CS) is also broken due to the presence of the mass term
\Delta
(
S127
).

Following Ref.
[
31
]
, we note that only the positive energy (particle) spectrum of
D_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}=\tau_{z}\mathcal{M}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}
(
S144
) is a genuine Hilbert space with a positive definite
\tau_{z}
- or Krein inner product.
It is exactly this positive energy subspace of the Krein space
(\mathcal{H}_{\text{ph}},\tau_{z},\tau_{x})
(Definition
2
) which is the relevant Bogoliubov state space for the topological classification
[
31
]
.
Now, according to Wigner’s theorem
[
132
]
physical symmetries have to be unitarily or anti-unitarily implemented with respect to this Krein inner product structure in this space.
This implies that the PHS in BBdG systems is actually not a physical symmetry but just a

constraint
, since the PHS operation of
Eq.

S24
is not a valid Krein-anti-unitary on this space, as it intertwines the positive and negative energy subspaces of the Krein space

[
31
]
.
This has direct implications on the ten-fold AZ classification scheme of topological insulators
[
25
,
29
]
.
Falsely viewing the PHS (
S24
) of the BBdG Hamiltonian (
S1
) as an actual symmetry would, in the absence of TRS and CS, place the Bogoliubov-Haldane model (
S144
) into class D in the AZ classification scheme (as claimed in Ref.
[
6
]
for example).
However, when correctly viewing the PHS as a constraint
[
31
]
, the Bogoliubov-Haldane model
is of class A, with an integer valued Chern invariant
\mathbb{Z}

[
31
,
32
]
.

S11.4
Signatures for the distribution of the original particles

Our measurement protocol for extracting the SQGT
requires measuring the excitation fraction in specific Bogoliubov (particle) modes.
Focusing on the Bogoliubov-Haldane model (
S142
), we
discuss the signatures for the quasimomentum distribution of the original particles
a_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},s}

given the presence of the single or multiple quasiparticles excitations
\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},\pm}

on top of the Bogoliubov vacuum, which can then be measured e.g., via time-of-flight techniques
in optical lattice platforms
[
101
,
96
,
102
,
107
]
.

We start by noting that the ground state
{\lvert}\psi_{0}{\rangle}
of the Bogoliubov-Hamiltonian
(
S150
) is given by the vacuum state of the Bogoliubov quasiparticles
fulfilling the conditions,

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},+}{\lvert}\psi_{0}{\rangle}=0,\ \forall\bm{\mathbf{q}},\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{b}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},-}{\lvert}\psi_{0}{\rangle}=0,\ \forall\bm{\mathbf{q}}\neq 0,

(S160)

where in the second equality the condensation mode has to be explicitly excluded,
as the Bogoliubov theory is only concerned with excitations outside the condensate mode
at
\bm{\mathbf{q}}=\bm{0}
in the lower band.
Even though the Bogoliubov ground state contains no quasiparticles,
there is still a finite amount of original particles outside the condensation mode
which are (virtually) excited by interparticle interactions.
Since these excitations are only driven by quantum fluctuations,
this result is called quantum depletion.

We now consider the more general situation where a finite number of
l
quasiparticles are excited on top
of the Bogoliubov vacuum at quasimomentum
\bm{\mathbf{q}}
in band
n\in\{\pm\}
,
i.e,
(b_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},n}^{\dagger})^{l}/\sqrt{l!}\,{\lvert}\psi_{0}{\rangle}\equiv{\lvert}\phi_{0}{\rangle}
.
For
l=0
this state is again the Bogoliubov ground state (
S160
).
Now, for the state
{\lvert}\phi_{0}{\rangle}
with
l
quasiparticle excitations,
we compute the original particle contributions outside the condensation mode

{\langle}\phi_{0}{\,|}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},s}\hat{a}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},s^{\prime}}{|\,}\phi_{0}{\rangle}
where
s,s^{\prime}\in\{A,B\}

S142

To this end, we explicitly use the Bogoliubov transformations for

S146

S148

\displaystyle\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},s}\hat{a}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},s}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},A}\hat{a}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},B}\rangle

\displaystyle=l\,\left[(u_{A,m}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}})^{*}u_{B,m}^{\bm{\mathbf{q}}}\right]\delta_{m,n}+(v_{A,+}^{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}})(v_{B,+}^{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}})^{*}+(v_{A,-}^{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}})(v_{B,-}^{-\bm{\mathbf{q}}})^{*}=\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},B}\hat{a}_{\bm{\mathbf{q}},A}\rangle^{*},

(S161)

For

initial quasiparticle excitation present in band

in

S161

As an example, let us consider

We believe that the following experimental protocol
can be used to measure the Bogoliubov excitation fraction
after periodic modulation of the systems’ parameters to extract the SQGT
as described in the main text.
First, one should measure the TOF momentum distribution
without performing the periodic modulation and without exciting any quasiparticles
to obtain a reference measurement.
Then, as a second step, we imagine exciting Bogoliubov quasiparticles at
a desired quasimomentum
\bm{\mathbf{q}}
in band
n
on top of the Bogoliubov vacuum
with the protocol described in the main text or the one of Refs.
[
96
,
101
]
,
whose momentum distribution should then be measured via TOF.
The difference of these first two measurements then provides
a measure of the excited Bogoliubov quasiparticle distribution of step two.
As a third step, after exciting initial Bogoliubov quasiparticles,
one should perform the periodic parameter modulation protocol
to induce scattering of Bogoliubov quasiparticles into the other band.
Then, the momentum distribution of the system should be measured again via TOF.
Eventually, the difference of this final momentum distribution
and the one after step two gives us
the induced Bogoliubov quasiparticle excitation fraction
which can be used to extract the SQGT as described in the main text.

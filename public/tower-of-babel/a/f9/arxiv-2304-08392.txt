4
Interactions via Holomorphic Gauge Theory

So far we have dealt exclusively with a single free relativistic particle. The requirement that wave functions be holomorphic makes it difficult to introduce interactions through potentials as done in the nonrelativistic theory. We shall instead introduce them
covariantly
through a method we call
Holomorphic Gauge Theory
[
4
]
.
The probability density
\rho{(z)}=|\psi{(z)}|^{2}
and the microlocal current

(
125

\psi{(z)}{\,\mapsto\,}\psi{(z)}\chi
, where

is holomorphic in
{\mathcal{T}}_{+}
to preserve the holomorphy of

\chi

fiber metric
9
9

9

g{(z)}>0
into the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{K}}

(

\displaystyle\|\psi\|^{2}_{\Gamma}=\int_{\Gamma}{\rm d}\gamma{(z)}\,\rho{(z)}

(150)

\displaystyle\rho{(z)}=\psi{(z)}g{(z)}\psi{(z)}^{*}

(151)

covariant probability density.

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}{(z)}=\rho{(z)}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ g^{\prime}{(z)}=\chi{(z)}^{-1}g{(z)}(\chi{(z)}^{*})^{-1}.

(152)

To find the gauge potential and its curvature field, use the exterior derivative

\displaystyle{\rm d}={\rm d}x^{\mu}{\frac{\!\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}}+{\rm d}y^{\mu}{\frac{\!\partial}{\partial y^{\mu}}}={\rm d}z^{\mu}{\frac{\!\partial}{\partial z^{\mu}}}+{\rm d}{\bar{z}}^{\mu}{\frac{\!\partial}{\partial{\bar{z}}^{\mu}}}=\partial+{\bar{\partial}}

\displaystyle\partial\rho=\partial(\psi g\psi^{*})

(157)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}{(z)}

\displaystyle={\rm d}{\mathcal{A}}{(z)}={\bar{\partial}}{\mathcal{A}}={\bar{\partial}}\partial\ln g{(z)}.

\displaystyle-\Box_{z}(\psi{(z)}g{(z)})=\psi{(z)}M{(z)},\ \ \text{where}\ \ M{(z)}=M{(z)}^{*}

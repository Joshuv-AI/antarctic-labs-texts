Perturbation analysis of a class of composite optimization problems
Peipei Tang, Chengjing Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we study the perturbation analysis of a class of composite optimization problems, which is a very convenient and unified framework for developing both theoretical and algorithmic issues of constrained optimization problems. The underlying theme of this paper is very important in both theoretical and computational study of optimization problems. Under some mild assumptions on the objective function, we provide a definition of a strong second order sufficient condition (SSOSC) for the composite optimization problem and also prove that the following conditions are equivalent to each other: the SSOSC and the nondegeneracy condition, the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the nonsmooth system at a Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) point, and the strong regularity of the KKT point. These results provide an important way to characterize the stability of the KKT point. As for the convex composite optimization problem, which is a special case of the general problem, we establish the equivalence between the primal/dual second order sufficient condition and the dual/primal strict Robinson constraint qualification, the equivalence between the primal/dual SSOSC and the dual/primal nondegeneracy condition. Moreover, we prove that the dual nondegeneracy condition and the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the subproblem corresponding to the augmented Lagrangian method are also equivalent to each other. These theoretical results lay solid foundation for designing an efficient algorithm.



Keywords:

composite optimization problem, strong regularity, strong second order sufficient condition, constraint nondegeneracy

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following general class of composite optimization problems

\displaystyle(P)\quad\quad\quad\min_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}g(F(x)),

where
g:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is a lower semicontinuous proper convex function and
F:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m}
is a smooth mapping. Problem
(P)
is equivalent to the following problem

\displaystyle\min_{(x,c)\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{R}}\quad\Big\{c\,\Big|\,(F(x),c)\in\operatorname{epi}g\Big\}.

(1)

The Lagrangian function of problem (
1
) takes the following form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}((x,c);(\mu,\gamma))=\langle\mu,F(x)\rangle+c(1+\gamma),\quad(\mu,\gamma)\in\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{epi}g}(x,c).

Since the minimum of the Lagrangian function with respect to
c
has the value
-\infty
if
\gamma\neq-1
, we obtain the dual of problem
(P)
as follows

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{-g^{*}(\mu)+\min_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}\langle\mu,F(x)\rangle\Big\}.

The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) condition corresponding to problem
(P)
takes the following form

\displaystyle[F^{\prime}(x)]^{*}\mu=0,\quad\mu\in\partial g(F(x)),

(2)

which can be equivalently written as

\displaystyle R(x,\mu):=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}[F^{\prime}(x)]^{*}\mu\\
\mu-\operatorname{Prox}_{g^{*}}(F(x)+\mu)\end{array}\right]=0.

During the last decades, numerious research work, e.g.,
[
21
,
28
]
, have been emerged to study the stability analysis of the composite optimization problem whose objective function takes the form of
\varphi(x):=\varphi_{0}(x)+g(\Phi(x))
with
\Phi(x):=(\varphi_{1}(x),\varphi_{2}(x),\ldots,\varphi_{m}(x))
and
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}
,
i=0,1,\ldots,m
, a smooth mapping. The objective function
\varphi(x)
can be equivalently written as the composite of
g(c,x):=c+g(x)
and
F(x):=(\varphi_{0}(x),\Phi(x))
. The classical constrained optimization problem

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\min\limits_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}\varphi_{0}(x)\\
\mbox{s.t.}\quad\Phi(x)\in\Theta,\end{array}\right.

where
\Theta
is a convex subset of
\mathcal{R}^{m}
, can also be considered as a particular case of the composite optimization problem
(P)
with
F(x):=(\varphi_{0}(x),\Phi(x))
and
g(c,x):=c+\delta_{\Theta}(x)
. Therefore, the presented model
(P)
covers many classes of optimization problems including the linear programming, the nonlinear programming, the nonlinear second-order cone programming and the nonlinear semidefinite programming, and so on.

As we know, great efforts have been devoted to study the sensitivity and stability analysis of solution maps to parameter-dependent optimization problems. Tremendous monographs for the derivation of conditions ensuring various stability properties of solution maps are emerging. Strong regularity, which dates back to the landmark work of Robinson
[
34
]
, is one of the most important concepts to describe the stability of the solution mapping of the following generalized equation

\displaystyle 0\in\phi(x)+\mathcal{M}(x),

(7)

where
\phi:\mathcal{R}^{t}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{t}
and
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{R}^{t}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{t}
is a set-valued mapping. How to characterize the strong regularity of the canonically perturbed KKT system at the solution point of a given optimization problem became an important topic during last decades. For nonlinear programming problems, it has been proven by Robinson
[
34
]
that the strong regularity of the canonically perturbed KKT system is equivalent to the strong second-order sufficient condition (SSOSC) and the linear independence constraint qualification condition (LICQ) at the solution point. Mordukhovich and Sarabi
[
28
]
established the equivalence between the full stability of local minimizers and the strong regularity of the associated KKT system for a kind of composite optimization problems involving convex piecewise linear functions.
As for the general constrained optimization problem (
1
), they have established the equivalence of the strong regularity, the Lipschitzian strong stability, the Lipschitzian full stability and the second-order subdifferential condition at a local minimizer of the given problem under the reducibility condition and the nondegeneracy condition in Theorem 5.6 of
[
27
]
. As mentioned in
[
5
]
, the property of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the KKT system is also an important tool to characterize the stability for the KKT system at a given solution point. The characterizations of the strong regularity in terms of the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the KKT system for second-order cone programming problems and nonlinear semidefinite programming problems are given in
[
3
]
and
[
41
]
, respectively. As far as we know, for the counterpart of the general composite optimization problem
(P)
, this kind of characterization is still unknown.

For problem
(P)
, if
F
is an affine function, then it becomes a convex composite optimization problem. As for this special case, the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) which dates back to
[
12
,
32
]
and is further studied in
[
37
,
38
,
19
]
is an efficient approach, especially for large-scale problems. An appealing feature lies in the asymptotic superlinear convergence rate of the dual sequence generated by the ALM under the metric subregularity at the dual solution
[
7
]
. Meanwhile, the metric subregularity is equivalent to the calmness property (see 3H of
[
9
]
). It is also known from
[
1
]
that the metric subregularity of the dual solution mapping at a dual optimal solution for the origin is equivalent to the quadratic growth condition at the corresponding dual optimal solution. We need the quadratic growth condition to guarantee the superlinear convergence rate. It is usually difficult to check the quadratic growth condition directly and one may deal with it alternatively by looking for other sufficient conditions. One of such conditions is the second order sufficient condition (SOSC). As shown in Theorem 3.109 of
[
4
]
, under the conditions that
g
is a lower semicontinuous proper convex function and the set
\operatorname{epi}g
outer second order regular, the SOSC implies the quadratic growth condition. Other generic properties including the strict Robinson constraint qualification (SRCQ), the constraint nondegeneracy condition are also important for the corresponding problem. A natural question is whether the other conditions have any relationships with the SOSC. Furthermore, during the iterations of the ALM, we usually need to find an approximate solution of the corresponding inner subproblem efficiently with a given accuracy. If the Hessian of the inner subproblem is invertible we may apply some existing second order method such as the semismooth Newton (SSN) method to find the desired solution efficiently with a high accuracy. How to guarantee and characterize the nonsingularity of the Hessian is another important problem.

As we know, the second order conditions including the SOSC and the SSOSC are essentially important to establish the perturbation analysis of optimization problems. In this paper, under the assumption that the function
g
is
C^{2}
-cone reducible and
\operatorname{Prox}_{g}
is directionally differentiable, we provide a definition of a SSOSC (defined in Section
4
) for the composite optimization problem
(P)
thanks to some basic properties related to the function
g
and
\operatorname{Prox}_{g}
. It needs to mention that the
C^{2}
-cone reducible function is rich and includes all the indicator functions of
C^{2}
-cone reducible sets. Based on the given definition of the SSOSC, we prove that the following conditions are equivalent to each other: the SSOSC plus the nondegeneracy condition, the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the mapping
R
at a solution
(x,\mu)
of the KKT system (
2
), and the strong regularity of the KKT point
(x,\mu)
. As for the convex composite optimization problem, we establish the equivalence between the primal/dual SOSC and the dual/primal SRCQ, the equivalence between the primal/dual SSOSC and the dual/primal nondegeneracy condition. Moreover, we prove the equivalence between the dual nondegeneracy condition and the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the subproblem of the ALM. All the results are definitely important in both the computational and theoretical study of general composite optimization problems.

The approach used in this paper is related to the techniques from
[
4
,
9
,
25
,
26
,
40
]
. It is based on the notions of several derivatives of set-valued mappings, including the graphical derivative, regular coderivative and limiting coderivative. The graphical derivative dates back to
[
2
]
(where it is called the contingent derivative) and the limiting coderivative was first introduced in
[
23
]
. These derivatives have been extensively used in many aspects in variational analysis, optimization, the control theory, etc. since then. It is the utilization of these tools enables us to obtain the definition of the SSOSC for the composite optimization problem
(P)
.

The remaining parts of this paper are organized as follows. In Section
2
, we summarize some preliminaries from variational analysis and generalized differentiation including the second order subderivative and derivatives of set-valued mappings that are broadly used in the paper. In Section
3
, we provide some elementary properties related to the function
g
and its corresponding proximal mapping for further use. In Section
4
, we introduce the SRCQ, SOSC, SSOSC and nondegeneracy condition, particularly give the definition of the SSOSC which is a cornerstone to investigate the key equivalence conditions in perturbation analysis. In Section
5
, the equivalent conditions for the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the nonsmooth system at a KKT point are presented. In Section
6
, we establish the equivalence conditions for the convex composite optimization problem. Meanwhile, we introduce the SSN based ALM. In Section
7
, we give our conclusions .

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some basic knowledge on variational analysis and generalized differentiation that are broadly used in the subsequent sections. More details can be found in the monographs
[
4
,
40
,
25
,
26
]
, from which we borrow the standard notations used below.

Let
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be a real valued function with its epigraph
\operatorname{epi}r
defined as the set

\displaystyle\operatorname{epi}r:=\Big\{(x,c)\,\Big|\,x\in\operatorname{dom}r,\ c\in\mathcal{R},\ r(x)\leq c\Big\}

and its conjugate at
x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
defined by

\displaystyle r^{*}(x):=\sup_{u\in\operatorname{dom}r}\Big\{\langle x,u\rangle-r(u)\Big\},

where
\operatorname{dom}r:=\{x\,|\,r(x)<+\infty\}
.
The extended function
r
is said to be proper if its epigraph is nonempty and contains no vertical lines, i.e., there exists at least one
x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
such that
r(x)<+\infty
and
r(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
, or in other words,
r
is proper if and only if
\operatorname{dom}r
is nonempty and
r
is finite in its domain; otherwise,
r
is improper. For a proper lower semicontinuous function
r
, recall the Moreau envelope function
e_{\sigma r}
and the proximal mapping
\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}
corresponding to
r
with a parameter
\sigma>0
as follows

\displaystyle e_{\sigma r}(x):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{r(u)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|u-x\|^{2}\Big\},\quad\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}(x):=\mathop{\operatorname{argmin}}_{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{r(u)+\frac{1}{2\sigma}\|u-x\|^{2}\Big\}.

Furthermore, if
r
is a lower semicontinuous proper convex function, it follows from Theorem 2.26 of
[
40
]
that the Moreau envelope function
e_{\sigma r}
is also convex and continuously differentiable with

\displaystyle\nabla e_{\sigma r}(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma}\big(x-\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}(x)\big).

The proximal mapping
\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}
is single-valued, Lipschitz continuous, and satisfies the following Moreau identity (see e.g., Theorem 31.5 of
[
35
]
)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma r}(x)+\sigma\operatorname{Prox}_{\sigma^{-1}r^{*}}(\sigma^{-1}x)=x,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}.

For the extended real-valued function
r
and
x\in\operatorname{dom}r
, the Fréchet (regular) subdifferential of
r
at
x
is the set

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial}r(x):=\Big\{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\Big|\,r(x^{\prime})\geq r(x)+\langle v,x^{\prime}-x\rangle+o(\|x^{\prime}-x\|)\Big\}.

The limiting (Mordukhovich) subdifferential of
r
at
x
, denoted by
\partial r(x)
, is given by

\displaystyle\partial r(x):=\Big\{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\Big|\,\exists\,x^{k}\rightarrow x\ \mbox{with}\ r(x^{k})\rightarrow r(x),\,v^{k}\in\widehat{\partial}r(x^{k})\ \mbox{with}\ v^{k}\rightarrow v\Big\}.

The singular subdifferential of
r
at
x
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial^{\infty}r(x):=\Big\{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\Big|\,\exists\,t_{k}\downarrow 0,\ x^{k}\rightarrow x\ \mbox{with}\ r(x^{k})\rightarrow r(x),\,v^{k}\in\widehat{\partial}r(x^{k})\ \mbox{and}\ t_{k}v^{k}\rightarrow v\Big\}.

Let
x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
be a point such that
r(x)
is finite. The lower and upper directional epiderivatives of
r
are defined as follows

\displaystyle r^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d):=\liminf_{{t\downarrow 0}\atop{d^{\prime}\rightarrow d}}\frac{r(x+td^{\prime})-r(x)}{t},\quad r^{\downarrow}_{+}(x,d):=\sup_{\{t_{n}\}\in\operatorname{\Sigma}}\left(\liminf_{{n\rightarrow\infty}\atop{d^{\prime}\rightarrow d}}\frac{r(x+t_{n}d^{\prime})-r(x)}{t_{n}}\right),

where
\operatorname{\Sigma}
denotes the set of positive real sequences
\{t_{n}\}
converging to zero. We say that
r
is directionally epidifferentiable at
x
in a direction
d
if
r^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d)=r^{\downarrow}_{+}(x,d)
. If
r
is convex, then
r^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d)=r^{\downarrow}_{+}(x,d)
. Assuming that
r(x)
and the respective directional epiderivatives
r^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d)
and
r^{\downarrow}_{+}(x,d)
are finite, we can define the lower and upper second order epiderivatives as

\displaystyle r^{\downdownarrows}_{-}(x;d,w)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\liminf_{{t\downarrow 0}\atop{w^{\prime}\rightarrow w}}\frac{r(x+td+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}w^{\prime})-r(x)-tr^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d)}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}},

\displaystyle r^{\downdownarrows}_{+}(x;d,w)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{\{t_{n}\}\in\operatorname{\Sigma}}\left(\liminf_{{n\rightarrow\infty}\atop{w^{\prime}\rightarrow w}}\frac{r(x+t_{n}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{n}^{2}w^{\prime})-r(x)-t_{n}r^{\downarrow}_{+}(x,d)}{\frac{1}{2}t_{n}^{2}}\right).

The function
r
is parabolically epidifferentiable at
x
in a direction
d
if
r^{\downdownarrows}_{-}(x;d,\cdot)=r^{\downdownarrows}_{+}(x;d,\cdot)
. Denote

\displaystyle\Delta_{\tau}^{2}r(x,u)(d):=\frac{r(x+\tau d)-r(x)-\tau\langle u,d\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}\tau^{2}},\quad\tau>0.

Recall the second order subderivative of
r
at a feasible point
x\in\operatorname{dom}r
for
u\in\partial r(x)
defined by

\displaystyle d^{2}r(x,u)(d):=\liminf_{{t\downarrow 0}\atop{d^{\prime}\rightarrow d}}\Delta_{t}^{2}r(x,u)(d^{\prime}).

The function
r
is called twice epidifferentiable at
x
for
u
if
\Delta_{t}^{2}r(x,u)
epi-converges to
d^{2}r(x,u)
as
t\downarrow 0
.
Note that
d^{2}r(x,u)
is lower semicontinuous due to the lower epi-limit of the corresponding ratio function. By Proposition 13.5 of
[
40
]
, the function
d^{2}r(x,u)
is lower semicontinuous and positively homogeneous of degree 2 with
\operatorname{dom}d^{2}r(x,u)\subseteq\{d\,|\,r^{\downarrow}_{-}(x,d)=\langle u,d\rangle\}
. Furthermore, if the function
r
is a proper lower semicontinuous convex function, the function
\frac{1}{2}d^{2}r(x,u)
is convex and proper and its conjugate function is
\frac{1}{2}d^{2}r^{*}(u,x)
if
x\in\operatorname{dom}r
and
u\in\partial r(x)
. One may also see Theorem 2.2 and Theorem 2.4 of
[
39
]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
r:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be an extended real-valued function,
x\in\operatorname{dom}r
and
u\in\partial r(x)
. It is said that the function
r
is parabolically regular at the point
x
in the direction
d
for
u
, if

\displaystyle\inf_{w\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{r^{\downdownarrows}_{-}(x;d,w)-\langle u,w\rangle\Big\}=d^{2}r(x,u)(d).

Given a set
C\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{m}
and a point
x\in C
, recall that the (Painlevé-Kuratowski) outer and inner limit of the set-valued mapping
S:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{n}
as
x^{\prime}\rightarrow x
are defined by

\displaystyle\limsup_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}S(x):=\Big\{y\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\,\Big|\,\exists\,x^{k}\rightarrow x,\ y^{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ y^{k}\in S(x^{k})\Big\}

and

\displaystyle\liminf_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}S(x):=\left\{y\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\,\left|\,\forall\,x^{k}\rightarrow x,\ \exists\,N\in\mathbb{N}_{\infty},\ y^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N}}{{\longrightarrow}}y\ \mbox{with}\ y^{k}\in S(x^{k})\right.\right\},

respectively.
The (Bouligand) tangent/contingent cone to
C
at
x
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{C}(x):=\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{C-x}{t}=\Big\{y\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\Big|\,\exists\ t_{k}\downarrow 0,\ y^{k}\rightarrow y\ \mbox{with}\ x+t_{k}y^{k}\in C\Big\}.

The corresponding regular tangent cone to
C
at
x
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{C}(x):=\liminf_{{t\downarrow 0,x^{\prime}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\rightarrow}}}x}}\frac{C-x^{\prime}}{t}.

The (Fréchet) regular normal cone to
C
at
x
can be defined equivalently by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{C}(x):=\left\{y\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,\left|\,\limsup_{x^{\prime}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\rightarrow}}}x}\frac{\langle y,x^{\prime}-x\rangle}{\|x^{\prime}-x\|}\leq 0\right.\right\}=\mathcal{T}_{C}^{\circ}(x).

The basic/limiting normal cone to
C
at
x
admits the following form

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{C}(x):=\limsup_{x^{\prime}{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle C}}{{\rightarrow}}}x}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{C}(x^{\prime}),

which is equivalent to the original definition by Mordukhovich
[
22
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{C}(x):=\limsup_{x^{\prime}\rightarrow x}\left\{\operatorname{pos}(x^{\prime}-\operatorname{\Pi}_{C}(x^{\prime}))\right\},\quad\mathcal{N}_{C}(x)^{\circ}=\widehat{\mathcal{T}}_{C}(x),

if
C
is locally closed at
x
.

When the set
C
is convex, both tangent and regular tangent cones reduce to the classical tangent cone while both regular and limiting normal cones reduce to the classical normal cone of convex analysis with
\mathcal{T}_{C}(x)
and
\mathcal{N}_{C}(x)
as the commonly used notations, respectively.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a finite dimensional Euclidean space. A set

Recall that a function

For a set valued mapping
S:\mathcal{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{n}
, the range of

is taken to be the set
\operatorname{rge}S:=\{u\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\,|\,\exists\,x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\ \mbox{with}\ u\in S(x)\}
. The inverse mapping
S^{-1}:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{m}
is defined by
S^{-1}(u):=\{x\in\mathcal{R}^{m}\,|\,u\in S(x)\}
. We define the corresponding graphical derivative and coderivative constructions generated
by the tangent cone, the regular normal cone and the limiting normal cone, respectively. Given a point

, the graphical derivative of

at

is the mapping

defined by

\displaystyle z\in DS(x,u)(w)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(w,z)\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{gph}S}(x,u),

where
\operatorname{gph}S
is the graph of
S
a subset of
\mathcal{R}^{m}\times\mathcal{R}^{n}
, namely
\operatorname{gph}S:=\{(x,y)\,|\,y\in S(x)\}
. The limiting coderivative is the mapping

defined by

\displaystyle v\in D^{*}S(x,u)(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(v,-y)\in\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{gph}S}(x,u).

The regular coderivative
\widehat{D}^{*}S(x,u):\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{R}^{m}
is defined by

\displaystyle v\in\widehat{D}^{*}S(x,u)(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(v,-y)\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{\operatorname{gph}S}(x,u).

Here the notations

,

By the definition of

\displaystyle DS(x,u)(w)=\limsup_{{t\downarrow 0}\atop{w^{\prime}\rightarrow w}}\frac{S(x+tw^{\prime})-u}{t}.

The graphical derivative mapping

and the limiting coderivative mapping

\displaystyle\quad w\in D(S^{-1})(u,x)(z),

\displaystyle v\in D^{*}S(x,u)(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad-y\in D^{*}(S^{-1})(u,x)(-v).

The limiting coderivative enables us to characterize an important behavior of multifunctions, called the Aubin property.

\operatorname{Prox}_{g^{*}}^{\prime}(\bar{x}+\bar{u},h)=0
.

3.

g^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{x},h)=\langle\bar{u},h\rangle
and
d^{2}g(\bar{x},\bar{u})(h)=0
.

Proof.

At the beginning,
\operatorname{Prox}_{g}
is directionally differentiable at
\bar{x}+\bar{u}
by Assumption
2.1
. Based on Proposition
3.1
, it yields
\operatorname{dom}D(\partial g)(\bar{x},\bar{u})=\operatorname{dom}d^{2}g(\bar{x},\bar{u})=\left\{d\,\left|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{x},d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\right.\right\}
.
Since
\bar{u}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
and
\bar{x}=\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})=(I+\partial g)^{-1}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Prox}_{g}^{\prime}(\bar{x}+\bar{u},h+d)=h\quad

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad h\in D(I+\partial g)^{-1}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(h+d)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad h+d\in D(I+\partial g)(\bar{x},\bar{x}+\bar{u})(h)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad d\in D(\partial g)(\bar{x},\bar{u})(h)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad d\in\partial\left(\frac{1}{2}d^{2}g(\bar{x},\bar{u})\right)(h),

where the first and the third equivalence relation is based on the definition (
2
), and the fourth equivalence relation is based on Theorem 13.40 of
[
40
]
. Therefore, if
\operatorname{Prox}_{g}^{\prime}(\bar{x}+\bar{u},h+d)=h
holds, by Theorem 23.5 of
[
35
]
we have

\displaystyle\langle h,d\rangle=\frac{1}{2}d^{2}g(\bar{x},\bar{u})(h)+\frac{1}{2}d^{2}g^{*}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(d).

Based on Corollary 15.3.2 of
[

, we have the inequality

\displaystyle\langle h,d\rangle\leq\sqrt{d^{2}g(\bar{x},\bar{u})(h)}\sqrt{d^{2}g^{*}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(d)}.

It follows that
\langle h,d\rangle=d^{2}g(\bar{x},\bar{u})(h)
.

Due to Moreau’s identity, we have
\operatorname{Prox}^{\prime}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u},h)=h-\operatorname{Prox}^{\prime}_{g^{*}}(\bar{x}+\bar{u},h)
. The second part of the results follows easily by Proposition 13.20, Theorem 13.40 of
[
40
]
and the fact that

\displaystyle d^{2}g(\bar{x},\bar{u})(h)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\in D(\partial g)(\bar{x},\bar{u})(h).

SRCQ, SOSC, SSOSC and nondegeneracy conditions

In this section, we introduce the SRCQ, SOSC, SSOSC and nondegeneracy condition for the general composite optimization problem
(P)
. Let
(\bar{x},\bar{c})
be an optimal solution of problem (
1
) with
(\bar{\mu},-1)
being the corresponding dual solution and
\mathcal{K}:=\operatorname{epi}g

\displaystyle(F^{\prime}(\bar{x}),1)(\mathcal{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{R})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(\bar{x}),\bar{c})=\mathcal{R}^{m}\times\mathcal{R},

(12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(\bar{x}),\bar{c})=\left\{(d,d_{c})\,\left|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d)\leq d_{c}\right.\right\}.

Due to Theorem 3.9 and Proposition 3.17 of
[
4

12
) holds at
(\bar{x},\bar{c})
. Then the RCQ for problem
(P)
takes the following form

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\bar{x})\mathcal{R}^{n}-\Big\{d\,\Big|\,\exists\,d_{c}\in\mathcal{R},\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d)\leq d_{c}\Big\}=\mathcal{R}^{m}.

Due to Proposition 8.12 and Exercise 8.23 of
[
40
]

\displaystyle\left\{d\,\left|\,\exists\,d_{c}\in\mathcal{R},\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d)\leq d_{c}\right.\right\}=\operatorname{dom}g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),\cdot)=\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{dom}g}(F(\bar{x})),

the RCQ for problem
(P)
can be written equivalently as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{dom}g}(F(\bar{x}))\cap\operatorname{NULL}(F^{\prime}(\bar{x})^{*})=\{0\}\quad\textrm{or}\quad\partial^{\infty}g(F(\bar{x}))\cap\operatorname{NULL}(F^{\prime}(\bar{x})^{*})=\{0\}.

Therefore, it follows from
[
15
]
that

\displaystyle\partial(g\circ F)(\bar{x})=F^{\prime}(\bar{x})^{*}\partial g(F(\bar{x})).

(13)

The SRCQ is said to hold at
(\bar{x},\bar{c})
with
(\bar{\mu},-1)
if

\displaystyle(F^{\prime}(\bar{x}),1)(\mathcal{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{R})-\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(\bar{x}),\bar{c})\cap(\bar{\mu},-1)^{\perp}=\mathcal{R}^{m}\times\mathcal{R}.

(14)

Note that the Slater condition always holds for the function

[

\displaystyle(\bar{\mu},-1)\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(\bar{x}),g(F(\bar{x})))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\bar{\mu}\in\partial g(F(\bar{x}))

\displaystyle g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d)\geq\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle,\quad\forall\,d\in\mathcal{R}^{m},

we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(\bar{x}),\bar{c})\cap(\bar{\mu},-1)^{\perp}=\Big\{(d,d_{c})\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d)=\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle=d_{c}\Big\}.

Therefore, the SRCQ for problem
(P)
can be written as

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\bar{x})\mathcal{R}^{n}-\Big\{d\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d)=\langle\bar{\mu},d\rangle\Big\}=\mathcal{R}^{m}.

(15)

It follows from Proposition 4.50 of
[
4
]
that the solution set of the dual problem is a singleton if the SRCQ (

(\bar{x},\bar{c})
.

The nondegeneracy condition for problem (

) holds at

if

\displaystyle(F^{\prime}(\bar{x}),1)(\mathcal{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{R})-\operatorname{lin}\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}(F(\bar{x}),\bar{c})=\mathcal{R}^{m}\times\mathcal{R}

and due to the structure of

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\bar{x})\mathcal{R}^{n}-\Big\{d\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d)\leq-g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),-d)\Big\}=\mathcal{R}^{m},

which can be written equivalently as

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\bar{x})\mathcal{R}^{n}-\Big\{d\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d)=-g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),-d)\Big\}=\mathcal{R}^{m}

(16)

due to Corollary 4.7.2 of
[
35
]
.

The critical cone of problem (
1
) at
(\bar{x},\bar{c})

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\bar{x},\bar{c})=\Big\{(d,d_{c})\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d)=0,d_{c}=0\Big\},

which is also equivalent to

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\bar{x},\bar{c})=\Big\{(d,d_{c})\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d)=\langle F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d,\mu\rangle=0,d_{c}=0\Big\}.

Therefore, it is easy to write out the critical cone of problem
(P)
at
\bar{x}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\Big\{d\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d)=0\Big\}=\Big\{d\,\Big|\,F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d\in\mathcal{N}_{\partial g(F(\bar{x}))}(\mu)\Big\},

By Assumption
2.1
that
g
is
C^{2}
-cone reducible at
\bar{x}
, the SOSC at
(\bar{x},\bar{c})
for problem (
1

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\Lambda(\bar{x})}\Big\{\langle\tilde{d},\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}((\bar{x},\bar{c});(\mu,-1))\tilde{d}\rangle-\sup_{(w,\gamma)\in\mathcal{T}^{2}}\big\{\langle\mu,w\rangle-\gamma\big\}\Big\}>0,\,\forall\,\tilde{d}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\bar{x},\bar{c})\setminus\{0\},

where

\bar{x}
for problem
(P)
,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{2}:=\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\operatorname{epi}g}((F(\bar{x}),\bar{c}),(d,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(F(\bar{x}),d))).

For simplicity, denote

\displaystyle\varphi_{g}(x,\mu)(d):=\inf\limits_{w\in\mathcal{R}^{m}}\Big\{g^{\downdownarrows}_{-}(x;d,w)-\langle w,\mu\rangle\Big\}.

By Proposition 3.30 of
[

\displaystyle-\sup_{(w,\gamma)\in\mathcal{T}^{2}}\Big\{\langle\mu,w\rangle-\gamma\Big\}=\varphi_{g}(x,\mu)(d).

Then the SOSC at

for problem (

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu\in\Lambda(\bar{x})}\Big\{\langle\mu,F^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})(d,d)\rangle+\varphi_{g}(F(\bar{x}),\mu)(F^{\prime}(\bar{x})d)\Big\}>0,\,\forall\,d\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})\setminus\{0\},

Given

\displaystyle\Gamma_{g}(\bar{x},\bar{u})(v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{{d\in\mathcal{R}^{m},v=Ud}\atop{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}}\langle v,d-v\rangle,&\mbox{if}\ v\in\bigcup\limits_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U,\\
+\infty,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
\bar{u}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
, for any
U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
and
v,d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
which satisfy
v=U(v+d)
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma_{g}(\bar{x},\bar{u})(v)\leq\langle v,d\rangle.

(17)

Furthermore, one also has

\displaystyle\bigcup_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(U)=\Big\{v\,\Big|\,\Gamma_{g^{*}}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(v)=0\Big\}.

(18)

Proof.

Combining the definition of
\Gamma_{g}(\bar{x},\bar{u})
, the result (
17
) can be derived easily. In the following, we prove the second result (
18
).

On one hand, for any
v^{\prime}\in\bigcup\limits_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(U)
, there exists
U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
such that
Uv^{\prime}=0
, which is also equivalent to
v^{\prime}=(I-U)v^{\prime}
with
I-U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g^{*}}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
. Applying the result (
17
) to
\Gamma_{g^{*}}(\bar{u},\bar{x})
with
v=v^{\prime}
and
d=0
, it follows that
\Gamma_{g^{*}}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(v^{\prime})\leq 0
. Combing Proposition
3.3
, we know
\Gamma_{g^{*}}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(v)\geq 0
for all
v\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
. Therefore, it follows that
\Gamma_{g^{*}}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(v^{\prime})=0
.
On the other hand, if
\Gamma_{g^{*}}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(v)=0
, there exist
V\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g^{*}}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
and
d\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
with
v=Vd
such that
\Gamma_{g^{*}}(\bar{u},\bar{x})(v)=\langle v,d-v\rangle=0
. Therefore, we have
\langle Vd,(I-V)d\rangle=\langle d,(V-V^{2})d\rangle=0
. Since
V-V^{2}
is positive semidefinite due to Proposition
3.3
, we derive that
(V-V^{2})d=0
which also implies that
(I-V)v=0
. Then we find
U=(I-V)\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
with
Uv=0
. The desired result follows.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}g
and
\bar{u}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
. We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigcup_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U\right)\subseteq\operatorname{aff}\left(\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))\right)\right).

Proof.

At the beginning, we prove that

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))\right)^{\circ}=\left\{d\,\Big|\,0\in\widehat{D}^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(-d)\right\}.

(19)

Pick
y\in\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))\right)^{\circ}
and consider an arbitrary pair
(w,z)\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{gph}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
. Particularly we have
z\in\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))
, which yields that

\displaystyle\langle(0,y),(w,z)\rangle=\langle y,z\rangle\leq 0.

Therefore,
(0,y)\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{\operatorname{gph}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
and
0\in\widehat{D}^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(-y)
. Conversely, let
\tilde{y}\in\left\{d\,\Big|\,0\in\widehat{D}^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(-d)\right\}
. For any
z\in\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))
, we can select
w\in\mathcal{R}^{m}
such that
z=D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(w)
and
(w,z)\in\mathcal{T}_{\operatorname{gph}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
. It follows that

\displaystyle\langle\tilde{y},z\rangle=\langle(0,\tilde{y}),(w,z)\rangle\leq 0,

which leads to
\tilde{y}\in\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))\right)^{\circ}
.

On one hand, since

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))\right)\right)^{\circ}\subseteq\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))\right)^{\circ},

by (
19
), it yields that

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})))\right)\right)^{\circ}\subseteq\operatorname{lin}\left\{d\,\Big|\,0\in\widehat{D}^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(-d)\right\}.

On the other hand, it follows from the definition of the cone
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{\operatorname{gph}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}}(\bar{x}+\bar{u},\bar{x})
that

\displaystyle v\in\widehat{D}^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(y)\Longleftrightarrow\langle v,x-\bar{x}-\bar{u}\rangle\leq\langle y,u-\bar{x}\rangle+o(\|(x,u)-(\bar{x}+\bar{u},\bar{x})\|),\,\forall\,u=\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(x).

We can conclude that for any
d\in\operatorname{lin}\left\{d\,\Big|\,0\in\widehat{D}^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(-d)\right\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle d,\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(x)-\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})\right\rangle=o(\|(x,u)-(\bar{x}+\bar{u},\bar{x})\|)=o(\|x-\bar{x}-\bar{u}\|).

(20)

Let
\mathcal{D}_{F}
be the set consisting of the points where
\operatorname{Prog}_{g}
is differentiable. Choose any sequence
\{x^{k}\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{F}
with
x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}+\bar{u}
. Then
\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(x^{k})-\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})=\operatorname{Prox}^{\prime}_{g}(x^{k})(x^{k}-\bar{x}-\bar{u})+o(\|x^{k}-\bar{x}-\bar{u}\|)
when
k
is sufficiently large. In combination with (
20
), it yields that

\displaystyle\left\langle d,\operatorname{Prox}^{\prime}_{g}(x^{k})(x^{k}-\bar{x}-\bar{u})\right\rangle=o(\|x^{k}-\bar{x}-\bar{u}\|).

Letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
, it follows that for any
U\in\partial_{B}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
, we have
Ud=0
. Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})))\right)\right)^{\circ}\subseteq\bigcap_{U\in\partial_{B}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(U),

which can also be written equivalently as

\displaystyle\operatorname{aff}(\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})))\right)

\displaystyle\supseteq\sum_{U\in\partial_{B}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{U\in\partial_{B}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U

\displaystyle=\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigcup_{U\in\partial_{B}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigcup_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U\right),

where the second equality of the above formula is based on Theorem 3.8 of
[
35
]
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}g
and
\bar{u}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Big\{d\,\Big|\,0\in D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(d)\Big\}=\bigcup_{W\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(W)

\displaystyle\qquad=\operatorname{aff}\left\{d\,\Big|\,g^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d)=\langle\bar{x},d\rangle\right\}\cap\Big(\bigcup_{W\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(W)\Big).

Proof.

Firstly, for any

\displaystyle d\in\bigcup\limits_{W\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(W),

there exists
W\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
such that
Wd=0
, which is also equivalent to
d=(I-W)d
with

. Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{W\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(W)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g^{*}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}}\operatorname{rge}U.

It is known from Proposition 13.5 and Theorem 13.40 of
[
40
]
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{rge}\left(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g^{*}}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})\right)=\operatorname{dom}D(\partial g^{*})(\bar{u},\bar{x})\subseteq\Big\{d\,\Big|\,g^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d)=\langle\bar{x},d\rangle\Big\}.

Applying Lemma
4.2
to
g^{*}
at
\bar{u}
with
\bar{x}
and the fact that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{W\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(W)

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{aff}\left\{d\,\Big|\,g^{*\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{u},d)=\langle\bar{x},d\rangle\right\}.

Secondly, for any

there exists
W\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})
such that
Wd=0
. By Corollary 13.53 of
[
40
]
, it yields that

\displaystyle 0\in\operatorname{conv}D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(d)

and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\langle-d,\operatorname{conv}D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(d)\rangle\Big\}

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\langle-d,W_{i}d\rangle,W_{i}\in\partial_{B}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}),\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}=1,\lambda_{i}\geq 0\Big\}

\displaystyle=

We obtain that there exists

and

\displaystyle\bigcup_{W\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{NULL}(W)\subseteq\Big\{d\,\Big|\,0\in D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})(d)\Big\}.

The inverse inclusive relation is obvious and this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Given
\bar{x}\in\operatorname{dom}g
and
\bar{u}\in\partial g(\bar{x})
, suppose that Assumption
2.1
is valid and Assumption
2.2
holds at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
. One has

\displaystyle\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigcup_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U=\operatorname{aff}\left\{d\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{x},d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\right\}.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
3.1
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))=\operatorname{dom}D(\partial g)(\bar{x},\bar{u})=\left\{d\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{x},d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\right\},

which implies that

\displaystyle\operatorname{aff}\left(\operatorname{conv}\left(\operatorname{rge}(D\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))\right)\right)=\operatorname{aff}\left\{d\,\Big|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{x},d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\right\},

since the set
\left\{d\,\left|\,g^{\downarrow}_{-}(\bar{x},d)=\langle\bar{u},d\rangle\right.\right\}
is convex.

By Assumption
2.2
, Corollary 13.53 of
[
40
]
and noting that

\displaystyle\operatorname{rge}(D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u}))

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{rge}(\operatorname{conv}(D^{*}\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})))=\bigcup_{U\in\partial\operatorname{Prox}_{g}(\bar{x}+\bar{u})}\operatorname{rge}U,

6
The convex composite optimization problem

In this section, we consider the following convex composite optimization problem

\displaystyle(P^{\prime})\qquad\min_{x\in\mathcal{R}^{n}}f(\mathcal{A}x-b)+h(\mathcal{B}x-c)

where
f:\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]
is a proper lower semicontinuous convex function which may be twice continuously differentiable,
h:\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty]

\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}

\mathcal{B}:\mathcal{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}

\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
\mathcal{B}^{*}

and

are given vectors and

. We see that problem
(P^{\prime})
can be regarded as a special case of problem
(P)
with
F(x):=(\mathcal{A}x-b,\mathcal{B}x-c)
and
g(a,b):=f(a)+h(b)
. Let
(\bar{x},\bar{\mu})
be a solution of the KKT system (
2
) with
\bar{\mu}:=(\bar{u},\bar{v})
,
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}
and
\bar{v}\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}
. In the following statements, we assume that both
g
and
g^{*}
satisfy Assumption
2.1
,
g
satisfies Assumptions
2.2
and
2.3
at
F(\bar{x})
for
\bar{\mu}
, and
g^{*}
satisfies Assumptions
2.2
and
2.3
at
\bar{\mu}
for
F(\bar{x})
. Then
f
,
h
,
f^{*}
and
h^{*}
are

-cone reducible and outer second order regular at
\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b
,
\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c
,
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}

The corresponding dual problem related to problem
(P^{\prime})
is given by

\displaystyle(D^{\prime})\quad\quad\quad\min_{{u\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}}\atop{v\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}}}\Big\{f^{*}(u)+h^{*}(v)+\langle b,u\rangle+\langle c,v\rangle\,\Big|\,\mathcal{A}^{*}u+\mathcal{B}^{*}v=0\Big\}.

For any

\displaystyle L(x,u,v)

\displaystyle=\inf_{{s\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{1}}}\atop{t\in\mathcal{R}^{m_{2}}}}\Big\{f(\mathcal{A}x-b-s)+h(\mathcal{B}x-c-t)+\langle u,s\rangle+\langle v,t\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{*}u+\mathcal{B}^{*}v=0,\quad\mathcal{A}x-b\in\partial f^{*}(u),\quad\mathcal{B}x-c\in\partial h^{*}(v).

u-\operatorname{Prox}_{f^{*}}(\mathcal{A}(x)-b+u)\\
v-\operatorname{Prox}_{h^{*}}(\mathcal{B}(x)-c+v)\end{array}\right]=0.

6.2
The equivalence of the nondegeneracy condition and SSOSC

In this section, we prove the equivalence of the nondegeneracy condition of the primal/dual problem and the SSOSC of the dual/primal problem for the problems
(P^{\prime})

\bar{x}

\bar{x}

The nondegeneracy conditions for the primal problem
(P^{\prime})
at
\bar{x}
and the dual problem
(D^{\prime})

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{A}\\
\mathcal{B}\end{array}\right)\mathcal{R}^{n}-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Big\{d\,\Big|\,f^{\downarrow}_{-}(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,\mathcal{A}d)=-f^{\downarrow}_{-}(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,-\mathcal{A}d)\Big\}\\

respectively. We can write out the SSOSC for the primal problem

\displaystyle\sup_{(u,v)\in\Lambda(\bar{x})}\Big\{\Gamma_{f}(\mathcal{A}\bar{x}-b,u)(\mathcal{A}d)+\Gamma_{h}(\mathcal{B}\bar{x}-c,v)(\mathcal{B}d)\Big\}>0,\,\forall\,d\neq 0.

.

\bar{x}
be an optimal solution of the primal problem
(P^{\prime})
,
(\bar{u},\bar{v})

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have provided a comprehensive perturbation analysis for the composite optimization problem. The definition of the SSOSC for general optimization problems is given. We have proven that these conditions including the strong regularity, the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the nonsmooth system, the SSOSC with the nondegeneracy condition are equivalent to each other under some mild conditions. As for the convex composite optimization problem, we have established the equivalence between the primal/dual SOSC and the dual/primal SRCQ, the equivalence between the primal/dual SSOSC and the dual/primal nondegeneracy condition. Moreover, we have proven the equivalence of the dual nondegeneracy condition and the nonsingularity of Clarke’s generalized Jacobian of the subproblem of the ALM. All of these results are bound to be an important basis for further research on optimization problems in both computational and theoretical perspectives.

\displaystyle-Z\in D^{*}\mathcal{N}_{S^{m}_{+}}(X,U)(-V)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V\in D^{*}\operatorname{\Pi}_{S^{m}_{+}}(X+U)(Z+V),

we can conclude that

\displaystyle P\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\widetilde{V}_{\alpha\alpha}&\widetilde{V}_{\alpha\beta}&\widehat{\operatorname{\Sigma}}_{\alpha\gamma}\circ\widetilde{V}_{\alpha\gamma}\\
\widetilde{V}_{\beta\alpha}&\widetilde{V}_{\beta\beta}&0\\
\widehat{\operatorname{\Sigma}}_{\gamma\alpha}\circ\widetilde{V}_{\gamma\alpha}&0&0\end{array}\right)P^{*}\in\mathop{\operatorname{argmin}}_{{D\in S^{m},V=WD}\atop{W\in\partial\operatorname{\Pi}_{S^{m}_{+}}(X+U)}}\langle V,D-V\rangle\cap\Big\{D\,\Big|\,V\in D^{*}\operatorname{\Pi}_{S^{m}_{+}}(X+U)(D)\Big\}.

The desired result follows.

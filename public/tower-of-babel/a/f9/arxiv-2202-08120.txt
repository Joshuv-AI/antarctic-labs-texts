Descent and étale-Brauer obstructions for 0-cycles Thanks: MSC 2020. Primary: 11G35, 14C25, 14G05, 14G12
Francesca Balestrieri, Jennifer Berg

Abstract
. For 0-cycles on a variety over a number field, we define an analogue of the classical descent set for rational points. This leads to, among other things, a definition of the étale-Brauer obstruction set for 0-cycles, which we show is contained in the Brauer-Manin set and is compatible with Suslin’s singular homology of degree 0. We then transfer some tools and techniques used to study the arithmetic of rational points into the setting of 0-cycles. For example, we extend the strategy developed by Y. Liang, relating the arithmetic of rational points over finite extensions of the base field to that of 0-cycles, to torsors. We give applications of our results to study the arithmetic behaviour of 0-cycles for Enriques surfaces, torsors given by (twisted) Kummer varieties, universal torsors, and torsors under tori.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Obstruction sets for rational points.

Let
k
be a number field and
X
be a smooth, proper, geometrically integral variety over
k
. In order to investigate questions concerning the qualitative arithmetic behaviour of the set
X(k)
of rational points on
X
, a well-known strategy is that of defining so-called
obstruction sets
– namely, certain subsets of the set
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
of adelic points on
X
that still contain
X(k)
– and trying to exploit the more tractable local nature of these sets to study the specific question at hand. Such sets can be used, for example, to determine whether
X(k)=\emptyset
by refining local-to-global principles, or to classify varieties according to their arithmetic behaviour. The theory of obstruction sets in the context of rational points has been developed quite extensively over the last several decades. In
[
29
]
, Manin first constructed an obstruction set by using the Brauer group
\Br(X):=H^{2}_{\et}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
and class field theory to define what is now known as the
Brauer-Manin set
, namely

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}:=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Br X}\left\{(x_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\in X({\mathbb{A}}_{k}):\textstyle\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\inv_{v}\alpha(x_{v})=0\right\}.

Later, Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc
[
7
]
defined a new type of obstruction sets known as
descent sets
, based not on the Brauer group, but rather on the notion of torsors under algebraic groups. That is, if
G
is a linear algebraic group over
k
and if
[g:Y\to X]\in H^{1}_{\et}(X,G)
is (the
k
-class of) a
G
-torsor over
X
, then we can define the
descent set associated to
g
as

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{g}:=\bigcup_{[\tau]\in H^{1}(k,G)}g^{\tau}(Y^{\tau}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})),

where the
G^{\tau}
-torsor
g^{\tau}:Y^{\tau}\to X
is the twist of the torsor
g:Y\to X
by
\tau
(see
[
38
]
*Ch. 2).
We can also combine these two main types of obstructions together to yield potentially finer obstruction sets. For example, the
étale-Brauer
set,

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\et,\Br}:=\bigcap_{F\textrm{ finite lin alg $k$-group}}\enskip\bigcap_{[f:Y\to X]\in H^{1}_{\et}(X,F)}\enskip\bigcup_{\tau\in H^{1}_{\et}(k,F)}f^{\tau}(Y^{\tau}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br})),

can be obtained by considering the Brauer-Manin sets of all finite étale covers of
X
. Although the obstruction sets defined using the Brauer group and those defined by using torsors have quite different natures, there is sometimes a close
interrelation between them – see, for example
[
38
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
41
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
39
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
.
Unfortunately, despite the richness of the different obstructions available, the arithmetic behaviour of rational points on varieties is still not completely understood in general: for example, in
[
31
]
Poonen constructed a variety
X
over a number field
k
such that
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\et\Br}\neq\emptyset
but
X(k)=\emptyset
, showing that even the finest obstruction set currently at our disposal, i.e., the étale-Brauer set, is not quite refined enough to capture the lack of rational points.

1.2.
Obstruction sets for 0-cycles.

Let us now consider the theory of obstruction sets in a different context, namely, that of 0-cycles on
X
, which can be viewed as generalisations of rational points. Given a fixed integer
d
, the main object of interest is the set
Z_{0}^{d}(X)
of 0-cycles of degree
d
on
X
, that is, the set of all formal
{\mathbb{Z}}
-sums
z:=\sum_{x}n_{x}x
of closed points
x
on
X
such that
\deg(z):=\sum_{x\in X}n_{x}[k(x):k]=d
, where
k(x)
is the residue field of
x
. Much in the same way as we did for rational points, we can ask qualitative arithmetic questions about
Z_{0}^{d}(X)
– for example, whether
Z_{0}^{d}(X)=\emptyset
. The basic strategy to tackle such questions remains the same, that is, we consider subsets of the set
Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})
of adelic degree
d
0-cycles on
X
and try to exploit the local nature of these subsets to draw conclusions about
Z_{0}^{d}(X)
.

There are two striking differences between the theory of obstructions for rational points and that for 0-cycles, however. First, while there is a wide range of obstructions sets and tools currently available for rational points, the same cannot be said for 0-cycles. One key complication is that, in the definition of 0-cycles, several different field extensions need to be considered at once, thereby creating challenges in generalising obstruction sets from rational points to this new context. Currently, the only obstruction set that has been generalised is the Brauer-Manin obstruction
[
10
]
, defined in a similar way as in the context of rational points, but with the extra use of corestriction maps to deal with the different residue fields of the support of the 0-cycles, namely

Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\Br}:=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Br X}\left\{\left(\sum_{x_{v}\in X_{k_{v}}}n_{x_{v}}x_{v}\right)_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}}):\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\sum_{x_{v}\in X_{k_{v}}}n_{x_{v}}\inv_{v}\left(\cores_{k_{v}(x_{v})/k_{v}}\alpha(x_{v})\right)=0\right\}.

The second difference concerns the arithmetic behaviour of
0
-cycles. While, as previously mentioned, even the finest known obstruction cannot explain all failures of the Hasse principle for rational points, the qualitative arithmetic of 0-cycles is, conjecturally, completely captured by the Brauer-Manin obstruction – and thus much more well behaved.
Indeed, Colliot-Thélène
[
10
]
conjectured that the Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only one to weak approximation for
0
-cycles of degree
1
on any smooth, proper, geometrically integral variety
X
over
k
(see also
[
25
]
). Colliot-Thélène’s conjecture is encompassed by the following main guiding conjecture in the context of
0
-cycles, which is known for a few cases (see
[
34
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
42
]
,
[
27
]
, and
[
9
]
*p. 383-4 for a detailed list of references) but remains open in general.

Conjecture 1.1

(Conjecture (E))
.

For any smooth, projective variety
X
over a number field
k
, the following complex is exact,

\widehat{\CH_{0}(X)}\longrightarrow\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\widehat{\CH_{0}^{\prime}(X_{k_{v}})}\longrightarrow\Hom(\Br X,{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}),

where
\widehat{\ \ \ \ }
denotes the profinite completion, and where
\CH_{0}^{\prime}(X_{k_{v}})
coincides with
\CH_{0}(X_{k_{v}})
at finite places and is a modification of the Chow group at infinite places (see
[
42
]
*§1.1 or
[
10
]
*§1 for further details).

1.3.
Main results

The main aim of this paper is to
fill in some significant gaps in the theory of obstructions for 0-cycles by defining an analogue of the descent set associated to a torsor for
0
-cycles.
Given a fixed
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and a fixed torsor
g:Y\to X
under a linear algebraic group
G
over
k
, we define the
degree
d
descent set associated to
g
(see Definition
3.3
) to be

Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{g}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\textrm{ finite non-empty}\\
\textrm{ set of finite field}\\
\textrm{extensions of $k$}\end{subarray}}\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}\left(\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}:\\
\textup{$T_{L}\neq\emptyset$ finite for all $L\in S$}\end{subarray}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left((\Delta_{\tau})_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}:\\
\Delta_{\tau}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{ for all $\tau$},\\
\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\Delta_{\tau}[L:k]=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y_{{\mathbb{A}}_{L}}^{\tau})\right),

where

\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}:\left(\left(\left(\left(\sum_{y\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y}\;y\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\Omega_{L}:\\
w|v\end{subarray}}\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}\right)_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\longmapsto\left(\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}\sum_{y\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y}(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}(y)\right)_{v\in\Omega_{k}}

and where
g_{L_{w}}^{\tau}\colon Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}\to X_{L_{w}}
and
s_{L_{w}}\colon X_{L_{w}}\to X_{k_{v}}
are the natural maps. This set
Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{g}
contains
Z_{0}^{d}(X)
and, moreover, generalises the descent set
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{g}
when
d=1
. This set arises in a very natural way that mimics the way in which the descent set for rational points is defined, once we take into account the more complex structure of 0-cycles (see Sections
2
and
3
for more details).

The definition of the descent set leads to, among other results, a definition of the étale-Brauer obstruction for
0
-cycles (Definition
3.6
), namely

\small Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\etBr}:=\!\!\!\!\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\neq\emptyset\textrm{ finite set}\\
\textrm{ of finite field}\\
\textrm{ extns of $k$}\end{subarray}}\ \bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\text{ finite lin.}\\
\text{alg.\ $k$-group}\end{subarray}}\,\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}[f:Y\to X]\\
\in H^{1}(X,G)\end{subarray}}\textbf{f}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}\left(\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}\\
\textup{$T_{L}\neq\emptyset$ finite}\\
\text{for all $L\in S$}\end{subarray}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left((\Delta_{\tau})_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}:\\
\Delta_{\tau}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{ for all $\tau$},\\
\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\Delta_{\tau}[L:k]=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y_{{\mathbb{A}}_{L}}^{\tau})^{\Br}\right).

In Section
3
, we show how the descent set and its variations involving the Brauer group are compatible with an important equivalence relation on 0-cycles, namely the finite correspondences equivalence relation, which gives rise to Suslin’s singular homology groups of degree 0; for proper varieties, Suslin’s singular homology groups of degree 0 correspond to Chow groups.

More generally, these definitions afford the possibility of a more systematic theory of obstructions for
0
-cycles, analogous to that of rational points. It also provides new potential avenues for investigating Conjecture
1.1
: if Conjecture
1.1
holds, it implies that the Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only one for weak approximation for 0-cycles of degree 1 (see
[
27
]
*Theorem A); this, on the other hand, should imply that the étale-Brauer set and the Brauer-Manin set for 0-cycles of degree 1 are “equal” in the Chow group, in the sense that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for any finite set
S^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{k}
of places of
k
, if
(z_{v})_{v}\in Z_{0}^{1}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\Br}
, then there is some
(\tilde{z}_{v})_{v}\in Z_{0}^{1}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\etBr}
such that
z_{v}
and
\tilde{z}_{v}
have the same image in
\CH_{0}(X_{k_{v}})/n
for any
v\in S^{\prime}
.

Equipped with our new definitions, in §
5
we begin to transfer some of the tools and techniques used to study descent obstructions to rational points into the
0
-cycles setting. We do so in the spirit of some of the ideas developed by Liang in
[
27
]
. His strategy consists of trying to show that, under certain conditions, if some arithmetic property (such as, for example, the Brauer-Manin obstruction being the only one for weak approximation) holds for rational points for enough field extensions of finite degree of the base field, then an analogous arithmetic property holds for 0-cycles as well.
In §
5.1
, we extend Liang’s strategy to torsors.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth, proper, geometrically integral variety over a number field
k
. Let
f:Y\to X
be an
F
-torsor for some linear algebraic group
F
over
k
and with
Y
geometrically integral. Let
d
be any integer. Assume that

(i)

for any finite extension
K/k
and any

, the quotient

is finite, and there exists a finite extension

of

so that for all finite extensions

of

linearly disjoint from

over

\res_{L/K}:\Br_{nr}(Y^{\tau}_{K})/\Br_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{K})\to\Br_{nr}(Y^{\tau}_{L})/\Br_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{L})

(ii)

for any finite extension

1.4.
Notation and terminology

Let
k
be a number field and let
{\overline{k}}
denote a fixed algebraic closure of
k
; we will take any finite extension of
k
to be inside
{\overline{k}}
. Let
\Omega_{k}
denote the set of non-trivial places of
k
and
{\mathbb{A}}_{k}
the ring of adeles of

. For a

, write

for the base change of

to the extension
K/k
, and
{\overline{X}}:=X_{{\overline{k}}}
. A variety over
k
is defined as a separated scheme of finite type over
k
. For a closed point
x
of a
k
-scheme

, let
k(x)
denote the residue field (over
k
). The Brauer group
\Br(X):=H^{2}_{\et}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
of a
k
-variety

is equipped with a natural filtration,
\Br_{0}(X)\subset\Br_{1}(X)\subset\Br(X)
, where
\Br_{0}(X):=\im(\Br(k)\to\Br(X))
and
\Br_{1}(X):=\ker(\Br X\to\Br{\overline{X}})
.
For an abelian group
A
and an integer

, we let
A[d]
denote the
d
-torsion subgroup of

. When

is prime, let

denote the
d
-primary part
\varprojlim_{n}A[d^{n}]
of

.

2.
A partition for global 0-cycles using torsors

Let
X
be a variety over a number field
k
. Throughout this section, we fix
g\colon Y\to X
to be a
G
-torsor for some linear algebraic group
G
over
k
and a
Y
smooth
k
-variety. In this section, we generalise the standard partition of
X(k)
using the torsor
g:Y\to X
to 0-cycles. This partition will allow us, in Section
3
, to define the descent set
Z_{0}^{d}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{k}))^{g}
and the étale-Brauer set
Z_{0}^{d}(X({\mathbb{A}}_{k}))^{\et,\Br}
for 0-cycles, analogous to the corresponding sets
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{g}
and
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\et,\Br}
for rational points.

Before we are able to give the definition of the descent set for 0-cycles (see Definition
3.3
), let us try to motivate a bit how the construction arises by looking first at what happens for rational points. For rational points, a standard result from the theory of torsors (see e.g.
[
38
, p.22]
) tells us that, using the torsor
g:Y\to X
, the set of
k
-rational points of
X
can be partitioned as

(2.1)

X(k)=\coprod_{\tau\in H_{\et}^{1}(k,G)}g^{\tau}(Y^{\tau}(k)),

where
g^{\tau}:Y^{\tau}\to X
is the
G^{\tau}
-torsor over
X
obtained by twisting
g:Y\to X
by
\tau
. Since
Y^{\tau}(k)\subset Y^{\tau}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
, we then get

X(k)=\coprod_{\tau\in H_{\et}^{1}(k,G)}g^{\tau}(Y^{\tau}(k))\subset\bigcup_{\tau\in H^{1}_{\et}(k,G)}g^{\tau}(Y^{\tau}({\mathbb{A}}_{k}))=:X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{g},

where
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{g}
is the
g
-descent set. The main goal of this section is to generalise the partition (
2.1
) in the context of 0-cycles.
In Definition
2.4
, we construct a set
Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}
that is the analogue for 0-cycles of the set
\coprod_{\tau\in H_{\et}^{1}(k,G)}g^{\tau}(Y^{\tau}(k))
in (
2.1
): in Proposition
2.5
, we indeed prove that
Z_{0}^{d}(X)=Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}
, thus yielding the analogue for global 0-cycles of the partition (
2.1
). In Section
3
, we then exploit this partition for 0-cycles in order to define the
g
-descent
Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{g}
for 0-cycles (see Definition
3.3
).

The natural motivation behind the construction of the set
Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}
– which, at first sight, might appear a bit abstract – lies completely in the proof of Proposition
2.5
: given a 0-cycle
z\in Z_{0}^{d}(X)
, the points in
\supp(z)
are rational points over their respective residue fields; hence, by using the partition (
2.1
) and grouping together the points in
\supp(z)
with a same residue field
L
and a same pullback class
\tau\in H^{1}_{\et}(L,G)
of
[g:Y\to X]\in H^{1}(X,G)
, we can decompose
z
as a collection of “scattered” 0-cycles, whose degrees are compatible in a certain explicit way, living on (potentially) different
Y^{\tau}_{L}
’s, for some extensions
L/k
and
\tau\in H_{\et}^{1}(L,G)
depending on
\supp(z)
only. We can then use the “recombining” map
\textbf{g}_{\ast}
(defined in Definition
2.2
) to recombine these “scattered” 0-cycles into
z
. This shows that any 0-cycle in
Z_{0}^{d}(X)
is in
Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}
, and yields the very natural justification for all the objects and conditions appearing in the definition of
Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}
.

Let us now delve into the construction.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k
be a number field and let
{\overline{k}}
be a fixed separable closure of
k
.
We let

\mathfrak{F}_{k}:=\{K\subset{\overline{k}}:\textrm{ $K$ is a finite field extension of $k$}\}.

Recall that if
z:=\sum n_{y}y\in Z_{0}(Y)
, the
pushforward
of
z
is

g_{\ast}(z)=\sum_{y\in Y}n_{y}g_{\ast}(y)=\sum_{y\in Y}n_{y}[k(y):k(g(y))]g(y)\in Z_{0}(X).

Definition 2.2
.

We define the “recombining” map

\textbf{g}_{\ast}\colon\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}\\
\textup{$S\neq\emptyset$ finite}\end{subarray}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}\\
\textup{$T_{L}\neq\emptyset$ finite for all $L\in S$}\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{L})\to Z_{0}(X)

as follows. Let

z:=\left(\left(\sum_{y\in Y_{L}^{\tau}}n_{y}y\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}\in\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}\\
\textup{$S\neq\emptyset$ finite}\end{subarray}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}\\
\textup{$T_{L}\neq\emptyset$ finite for all $L\in S$}\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{L}).

Then
\textbf{g}_{\ast}(z)
is the
0
-cycle on
X
given by

\displaystyle\textbf{g}_{\ast}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{y\in Y_{L}^{\tau}}n_{y}(s_{L}\circ g_{L}^{\tau})_{\ast}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{y\in Y_{L}^{\tau}}n_{y}[L(y):k(s_{L}\circ g_{L}^{\tau}(y))](s_{L}\circ g_{L}^{\tau})(y),

where
g_{L}^{\tau}\colon Y^{\tau}_{L}\to X_{L}
and
s_{L}\colon X_{L}\to X
are the natural morphisms.

The map
\textbf{g}_{\ast}
is compatible with degrees of 0-cycles in the following way.

Lemma 2.3
.

Fix an integer
d
. Then we have a map

\mathbf{g}_{\ast}\colon\coprod_{S\subset{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}}\coprod_{(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left((\Delta_{\tau})_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}:\\
\Delta_{\tau}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{ for all $\tau$},\\
\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\Delta_{\tau}[L:k]=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y^{\tau}_{L})\to Z_{0}^{d}(X).

Proof.

Let

z:=\left(\left(\sum_{y\in Y_{L}^{\tau}}n_{y}y\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}\in\coprod_{S\subset{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}}\coprod_{(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left((\Delta_{\tau})_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}:\\
\Delta_{\tau}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{ for all $\tau$},\\
\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\Delta_{\tau}[L:k]=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y^{\tau}_{L}).

Then, by definition of
\textbf{g}_{\ast}
, we have that

\textbf{g}_{\ast}(z)=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{y\in Y^{\tau}_{L}}n_{y}[L(y):k(s_{L}\circ g_{L}^{\tau}(y))](s_{L}(g_{L}^{\tau}(y))).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\deg(\textbf{g}_{\ast}(z))

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{y\in Y^{\tau}_{L}}n_{y}[L(y):k(s_{L}(g_{L}^{\tau}(y)))][k(s_{L}(g_{L}^{\tau}(y))):k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{y\in Y^{\tau}_{L}}n_{y}[L(y):k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{y\in Y^{\tau}_{L}}n_{y}[L(y):L][L:k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}[L:k]\sum_{y\in Y^{\tau}_{L}}n_{y}[L(y):L]

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}[L:k]\Delta_{\tau}

\displaystyle=d,

where the fifth equality follows from the fact that the inner summation represents the degree of the 0-cycle
\sum_{y\in Y^{\tau}_{L}}n_{y}y
, which is by definition
\Delta_{\tau}
, and the final equality is by definition of the integers
\Delta_{\tau}
.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

Let

. The

of

Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}\\
\textup{$S\neq\emptyset$ finite}\end{subarray}}\textbf{g}_{\ast}\left(\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}\\

The following is the 0-cycles analogue of the partition (
2.1
).

Proposition 2.5
.

There is an equality of sets,
Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}=Z_{0}^{d}(X)
.

Proof.

The forward containment is the content of Lemma
2.3
.
For the reverse inclusion, let
z:=\sum\limits_{x\in X}n_{x}x\in Z_{0}^{d}(X)
, and suppose that

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}:=\supp(z).

By the classical properties of torsors for rational points (see
[
38
]
*§5.3), for each
x_{i}
we take any closed point
x_{i}^{\prime}
of
X_{k(x_{i})}
projecting to
x_{i}
, and we have the partition

x_{i}^{\prime}\in X(k(x_{i}))=\coprod_{\sigma\in H^{1}(k(x_{i}),G)}g^{\sigma}_{k(x_{i})}\left(Y^{\sigma}_{k(x_{i})}(k(x_{i}))\right).

Hence there exists some (unique)
\sigma_{i}\in H^{1}(k(x_{i}),G)
such that
x_{i}
lifts to some
y_{i}\in Y^{\sigma_{i}}_{k(x_{i})}(k(x_{i}))
, which we fix for each
x_{i}
and which we can consider as closed points on
Y^{\sigma_{i}}_{k(x_{i})}
. Note that
k(x_{i})(y_{i})=k(x_{i})
, since
k(x_{i})(y_{i})\subset k(x_{i})
as
y_{i}
is a
k(x_{i})
-point.
So, if we consider the morphism

s_{k(x_{i})}\circ g_{k(x_{i})}^{\sigma_{i}}\colon Y_{k(x_{i})}^{\sigma_{i}}\to X_{k(x_{i})}\to X,

then the pushforward of
y_{i}\in Z_{0}(Y^{\sigma_{i}}_{k(x_{i})})
is

\displaystyle(s_{k(x_{i})}\circ g_{k(x_{i})}^{\sigma_{i}})_{\ast}(y_{i})

\displaystyle=[k(x_{i})(y_{i}):k(s_{k(x_{i})}\circ g_{k(x_{i})}^{\sigma_{i}}(y_{i}))]x_{i}

\displaystyle=[k(x_{i})(y_{i}):k(x_{i})]x_{i}

\displaystyle=x_{i}\in Z_{0}(X).

Consider the set

M:=\{(k(x_{i}),\sigma_{i}):i=1,...,r\}=\{(L_{1},\tau_{1}),\dots,(L_{s},\tau_{s})\},

where
L_{i}\in\{k(x_{1}),\dots,k(x_{r})\}
and
\tau_{i}\in\{\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{r}\}
.
We partition the set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}
as follows. The points
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
with
i\neq j
belong to the same partition set
P_{(L,\tau)}
if and only if
L=k(x_{i})=k(x_{j})
and
\tau=\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}
. We write

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r}\}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{s}P_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}.

For each
(L_{i},\tau_{i})\in M
, define

\Delta_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}:=\sum_{x\in P_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}}n_{x}[L_{i}(y):L_{i}]=\sum_{x\in P_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}}n_{x},

where we recall that
y
denotes the fixed lift of
x
and the
n_{x}
are the coefficients of the support of the
0
-cycle
z\in Z_{0}^{d}(X)
. Then, for each
(L_{i},\tau_{i})
, we have

\sum_{x\in P_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}}n_{x}y\in Z_{0}^{\Delta_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}}(Y_{L_{i}}^{\tau_{i}}).

Let
S:=\{L_{1},...,L_{s}\}
. For each
L\in S
, we let
T_{L}:=\{\tau:(L,\tau)\in M\}
.
Consider the tuple of
0
-cycles

w:=\left(\sum\limits_{x\in P_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}}n_{x}y\right)_{i\in\{1,\dots,s\}}=\left(\left(\sum\limits_{x\in P_{(L,\tau)}}n_{x}y\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}.

We claim that
\textbf{g}_{\ast}(w)=z
. Indeed, we have

\textbf{g}_{\ast}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{x\in T_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}}n_{x}(s_{L_{i}}\circ g_{L_{i}}^{\tau_{i}})_{\ast}(y)\,=\sum_{x\in\supp(z)}n_{x}x=z.

Moreover, we claim that

w
is in

\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}\\
\textup{$S\neq\emptyset$ finite}\end{subarray}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}\\
\textup{$T_{L}\neq\emptyset$ finite for all $L\in S$}\end{subarray}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left((\Delta_{\tau})_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}\\
\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\Delta_{\tau}[L:k]=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y^{\tau}_{L})

To show this, we check that the condition on the degrees
\Delta_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}
holds. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\Delta_{(L,\tau)}[L:k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\Delta_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}[L_{i}:k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{x\in P_{(L_{i},\tau_{i})}}n_{x}[L_{i}:k]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}n_{x_{j}}[k(x_{j}):k]

\displaystyle=\deg z

\displaystyle=d

Hence, the inclusion
Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}\supset Z_{0}^{d}(X)
holds and thus
Z_{0}^{d}(X)^{g}=Z_{0}^{d}(X)
, as required.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The subset of

in Definition

is equal to

.
Indeed, any effective 0-cycle in

. Thus for

, we have

. (Strictly speaking, when

But the only way in which

for some

.

One can check that

3.
The descent and étale-Brauer sets for 0-cycles

In this section, we extend the definitions from the previous section to the local setting and we define the descent and the étale-Brauer sets for 0-cycles.

Recall that, for any variety
V
over a number field
k
and any integer
d
, the
set of adelic 0-cycles of degree
d
of
V
is the subset
Z_{0}^{d}(V_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})
of
\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}Z_{0}^{d}(V_{k_{v}})
of 0-cycles
(z_{v})_{v}
such that, for all but finitely many
v\in\Omega_{k}
, we have that
z_{v}
extends to a 0-cycle over some model
\mathcal{V}\to\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{k_{v}})
of
V_{k_{v}}\to\Spec(k_{v})
. If
V
is proper, then
Z_{0}^{d}(V_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}Z_{0}^{d}(V_{k_{v}})
.

Let
X
be a variety over a number field
k
. For any integer
d
and any place
v\in\Omega_{k}
, we briefly recall how the natural map
\res_{v}:Z_{0}^{d}(X)\to Z_{0}^{d}(X_{k_{v}})
is defined.
For
z:=\sum_{x\in X}n_{x}x\in Z_{0}^{d}(X)
,
we let

\res_{v}(z):=\sum\limits_{x\in X}\sum\limits_{w\in\Omega_{k(x)}:w\mid v}n_{x}(x)_{w},

where
(x)_{w}\in X(k(x)_{w})
is the image of
x
under the natural inclusion
X(k(x))\to X(k(x)_{w})
.
For any
x\in X
,
we have by e.g.
[
30
]
*Ch. II, Cor. 8.4 that

\sum_{w\in\Omega_{k(x)}:w\mid v}[k(x)_{w}:k_{v}]=[k(x):k]

and thus

\deg(z_{v})=\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{k(x)}:w\mid v}n_{x}[k(x)_{w}:k_{v}]=\sum_{x\in X}n_{x}[k(x):k]=\deg(z).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a variety over a number field
k
. Let
g:Y\to X
be a
G
-torsor over
X
, where
G
is a linear algebraic group over
k
.
We define the map

\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}\colon\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subset{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}\\
\textup{$S\neq\emptyset$ finite}\end{subarray}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}\\
\textup{$T_{L}\neq\emptyset$ finite for all $L\in S$}\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{{\mathbb{A}}_{L}})\to Z_{0}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})

as follows.
We first observe that

Z_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{{\mathbb{A}}_{L}})\subset\prod_{w\in\Omega_{L}}Z_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}})=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\prod_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}Z_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}}).

Hence, an element
\tilde{y}
in the domain of
\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}
can be written as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{y}&:=\left(\left(\left(\left(\sum_{y\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y}\;y\right)_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w|v}\right)_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}\\
&=\left(\left(\left(\left(\sum_{y\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y}\;y\right)_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w|v}\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}\right)_{v\in\Omega_{k}},\end{array}

and we define

\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}(\tilde{y}):=\left(\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}\sum_{y\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y}(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}(y)\right)_{v\in\Omega_{k}}

where
g_{L_{w}}^{\tau}\colon Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}\to X_{L_{w}}
and

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a variety over a number field
k
. Let
g:Y\to X
be a torsor under some linear algebraic group
G
over
k
.
Fix

\mathbf{g}_{\ast,\textnormal{ad}}\colon\coprod_{S\subset{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}}\coprod_{(T_{L}\subset H^{1}(L,G))_{L\in S}}\coprod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left((\Delta_{\tau})_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}:\\
\Delta_{\tau}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\textrm{ for all $\tau$},\\
\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\Delta_{\tau}[L:k]=d\end{subarray}}\prod_{L\in S}\prod_{\tau\in T_{L}}Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y_{{\mathbb{A}}_{L}}^{\tau})\to Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}}).

Proof.

The proof follows in a similar manner to that of Lemma
2.3
. Let

\tilde{y}:=\left(\left(\left(\left(\sum_{y\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y}\;y\right)_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w|v}\right)_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}

\displaystyle\deg(\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}(\tilde{y})_{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}\sum_{y\in Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}}}n_{y}[L_{w}(y):k_{v}(s_{L_{w}}(g^{\tau}_{L_{w}}(y)))][k_{v}(s_{L_{w}}(g^{\tau}_{L_{w}}(y))):k_{v}]

\Delta_{\tau}

∎

Remark 3.9
.

For rational points, the
g
-descent set
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{g}
can be also written as

(3.5)

\bigcup_{\sigma\in H_{\et}^{1}(k,G)}g^{\sigma}(Y^{\sigma}({\mathbb{A}}_{k}))=\left\{(x_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\in X({\mathbb{A}}_{k}):\exists\ \sigma\in H^{1}_{\et}(k,G)\textrm{ s.t.}\res_{v}(\sigma)=[g_{k_{v}}^{-1}(x_{v})]\ \forall\ v\in\Omega_{k}\right\}.

In our construction of

) to 0-cycles. Nonetheless, when

is commutative, we have that

is a

and not just a pointed set. Hence, in this case, using the same ideas as in the construction of

(i.e. using natural commutative diagrams, corestriction maps, and the fact that

is a group for each

).
It is natural to ask, in this context, what the relationship between

and

(3.7)

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\displaystyle\sum_{x_{v}\in X_{k_{v}}}n_{x_{v}}\cores_{k_{v}(x_{v})/k_{v}}\left(\alpha_{k_{v}}(x_{v})\right)\\

3.1.
Descent obstruction and compatibility with Chow groups and Suslin homology.

When considering the Brauer-Manin set for 0-cycles, if

\langle-,\rangle_{BM}\colon Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})\times\Br(X)\to{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
induces a pairing

\langle-,\rangle_{BM}\colon\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\CH_{0}^{d}(X_{k_{v}})\times\Br(X)\to{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}},

where
\CH_{0}^{d}(X_{k_{v}})
denotes the set of 0-cycles of degree

on

modulo rational equivalence. It turns out that when

is proper, the descent set also induces an obstruction set modulo rational equivalence. In order to show this, we first need the definition of Suslin’s singular homology of degree 0.

For any varieties

and
W
over a field
K
with
V

V
to
W
over
K

whose elements are formal

of

that are finite and surjective over

. For example, if
X
is a variety over a field
K
, then

is just the group of 0-cycles on
X
.

Consider the points 0 and 1 on the affine line

. For a variety
X
over
K
, we define a map

\displaystyle\textrm{Corr}({\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{1},X)

\displaystyle\to\textrm{Corr}(\Spec K,X)

\displaystyle\sum_{Z}n_{Z}Z

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{Z}n_{Z}(\iota_{0}^{\ast}(Z)-\iota_{1}^{\ast}(Z)),

where, if
\lambda\in{\mathbb{A}}_{K}^{1}
is a
K

\iota_{\lambda}^{\ast}(Z)
denotes the pullback of

along the inclusion
\iota_{\lambda}:X\to X\times_{K}{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{K}
given by

is finite and surjective, we can view
\iota_{0}^{\ast}(Z)-\iota_{1}^{\ast}(Z)
as a 0-cycle on
X
.
We define the
Suslin’s singular homology group of degree 0 on
X
to be the group
h_{0}(X)
of 0-cycles on
X
modulo the equivalence relation generated by those 0-cycles on
X
coming from finite correspondences from

to

under the map above. Suslin’s singular homology of degree 0 behaves well in the following sense (see
[
16
]
):

(i)

if
X
is a proper variety over a field

, then
h_{0}(X)=\CH_{0}(X)
;

(ii)

Let
X
and

be varieties over a field
K
. Then, for any
k
-morphism
f\colon Y\to X
, the pushforward map
f_{\ast}\colon Z_{0}(Y)\to Z_{0}(X)
induces a morphism
f_{\ast}\colon h_{0}(Y)\to h_{0}(X)
;

(iii)

the degree map
\deg\colon Z_{0}(X)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
for 0-cycles factors over the map
s_{\ast}:h_{0}(X)\to h_{0}(\Spec K)={\mathbb{Z}}
induced by the structure morphism

.

Even though we will not use it in this paper, we mention here a useful moving lemma for 0-cycles modulo the finite correspondences equivalence, analogous to

Proposition 3.11

(

Definition 3.12

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
X
be a variety over a number field
k
. Let
g:Y\to X
be a torsor under a linear algebraic group
G
over
k
. Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
(z_{v})_{v}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{g}
. Let
[(z_{v})_{v}]:=\left([z_{v}]\right)_{v}\in\prod_{v}h_{0}^{d}(X_{k_{v}})
be the class of
(z_{v})_{v}
under the finite correspondences equivalence. Then
[(z_{v})_{v}]\in h_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{g}
.

Proof.

Since
(z_{v})_{v}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{g}
, by definition there exist a non-empty finite set
S
of finite extensions of
k
, a non-empty finite set
T_{L}\subset H^{1}_{\et}(L,G)
for each
L\in S
, a tuple of integers
((\Delta_{\tau})_{\tau\in T_{L}})_{L\in S}
satisfying
\sum_{L\in S}[L:k]\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\Delta_{\tau}=d
, and adelic 0-cycles
\left(\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}y_{w}^{\tau}\right)_{w\in\Omega_{L}}\in Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y^{\tau}_{{\mathbb{A}}_{L}})
with

\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}(y_{w}^{\tau})=z_{v},

for any
v\in\Omega_{k}
.

For each
L\in S
, each
\tau\in T_{L}
, and each
w\in\Omega_{L}
, consider the class

\left[\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}y_{w}^{\tau}\right]=\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}[y_{w}^{\tau}]\in h_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}})

of
\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}y_{w}^{\tau}\in Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}})
under the finite correspondences equivalence. It follows that

\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}\left(\left(\left(\left(\left[\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y^{\tau}_{w}}y_{w}^{\tau}\right]\right)_{w\in\Omega_{L}}\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}\right)=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}([y_{w}^{\tau}])

Since the push-forward map
(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}\colon Z_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}})\to Z_{0}(X_{k_{v}})
induces a map

(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}\colon h_{0}(Y^{\tau}_{L_{w}})\to h_{0}(X_{k_{v}}),

we have that
(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}([y^{\tau}_{w}])=[(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}(y^{\tau}_{w})].
Hence, for each
v\in\Omega_{k}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}[z_{v}]&=\left[\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}(y_{w}^{\tau})\right]\\
&=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}\left[(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}(y_{w}^{\tau})\right]\\
&=\sum_{L\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{L}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L}:w\mid v}\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y_{w}^{\tau}}(s_{L_{w}}\circ g_{L_{w}}^{\tau})_{\ast}\left([y_{w}^{\tau}]\right)\\
&=\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}\left(\left(\left(\left(\left[\sum_{y_{w}^{\tau}\in Y_{L_{w}}^{\tau}}n_{y^{\tau}_{w}}y_{w}^{\tau}\right]\right)_{w\in\Omega_{L}}\right)_{\tau\in T_{L}}\right)_{L\in S}\right).\end{array}

It follows that
[(z_{v})_{v}]\in h_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{g}
, as required.

Suslin’s singular homology of degree 0 is also compatible with the unramified Brauer-Manin pairing, as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
X
be an integral variety over a number field
k
. Let
B\subset\Br_{nr}(X)
be a non-empty subset of the unramified Brauer group of
X
. Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then the Brauer-Manin pairing for 0-cycles

\langle-,-\rangle_{BM}\colon\prod_{v}Z_{0}^{d}(X_{k_{v}})\times B\to{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}

is compatible with the finite correspondences equivalence and thus induces a paring

\langle-,-\rangle_{BM}\colon\prod_{v}h_{0}^{d}(X_{k_{v}})\times B\to{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

In order to show that the Brauer-Manin pairing is compatible with the finite correspondences equivalence, it suffices to show that, for any
\alpha\in\Br X
, for any
v\in\Omega_{k}
, for any elementary finite correspondence
Z\subset X_{k_{v}}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}}
, we have that the evaluation of
\alpha
at the 0-cycle
\iota_{0}^{\ast}(Z)-\iota_{1}^{\ast}(Z)\in Z_{0}(X_{k_{v}})
is 0, where we recall that
\iota_{\lambda}:X_{k_{v}}\to X_{k_{v}}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}}
is the inclusion
x\mapsto(x,\lambda)
.

Fix
\alpha\in\Br(X)
, a place
v\in\Omega_{k}
, and an elementary finite correspondence
Z\subset X_{k_{v}}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}}
. By definition, the projection of
X_{k_{v}}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}}
onto its second factor induces a finite surjective morphism
f\colon Z\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}}
, while projection onto its first factor induces a morphism
p\colon Z\to X_{k_{v}}
. We follow, with some modifications, the proof of
[
9
]
*Proposition 6.4.2.

Since
Z
is integral and
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}}
is a normal, locally Noetherian scheme of dimension 1 over
k_{v}
, and since
f
is surjective (and thus non-constant), it follows that
f
is flat (see e.g.
[
28
, Chap.4, Cor.3.10]
). Moreover, since
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}}
is locally Noetherian and
f
is finite and flat, it follows from
[
2
]
*Tag 02KB that
f
is finite and locally free of constant rank (since the rank is constant on irreducible components).

Let
z_{0}\in Z_{0}(Z)
and
z_{1}\in Z_{0}(Z)
be the 0-cycles associated to the finite schemes
\Spec(A_{0})=f^{-1}(0)
and
\Spec(A_{1})=f^{-1}(1)
, respectively. Then
\iota_{\lambda}^{\ast}(Z)=p_{\ast}(z_{\lambda})\in Z_{0}(X_{k_{v}})
, for
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
. Let
\alpha_{v}:=\res_{k_{v}/k}(\alpha)\in\Br(X_{k_{v}})
and let
\beta:=p^{\ast}(\alpha_{v})\in\Br(Z)
. Then, by
[
9
]
*Lemma 6.4.1, we have

\langle\beta,z_{\lambda}\rangle_{BM}=\cores_{A_{\lambda}/k_{v}}(\beta_{A_{\lambda}})\in\Br(k_{v}),

for
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
. Since
f
is finite and locally free of constant rank, by
[
9
]
*Proposition 3.8.1
we have that

\cores_{A_{\lambda}/k_{v}}(\beta_{A_{\lambda}})=\langle\cores_{Z/{\mathbb{A}}_{k_{v}}^{1}}(\beta),\lambda\rangle_{BM},

for
\lambda\in\{0,1\}
. Since
k_{v}
is a perfect field, the natural map
\Br(k_{v})\to\Br({\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}})
is an isomorphism by
[
9
]
*Theorem 5.6.1(viii). Hence,
\cores_{Z/{\mathbb{A}}_{k_{v}}^{1}}(\beta)\in\Br({\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{k_{v}})=\Br(k_{v})
is a constant class, and thus
\langle\beta,z_{0}\rangle_{BM}=\langle\beta,z_{1}\rangle_{BM},

that is,

\langle\beta,z_{0}-z_{1}\rangle_{BM}=0.

Finally, since
\beta:=p^{\ast}(\alpha_{v})
, from
[
9
]
*(6.2) it follows that

0=\langle p^{\ast}(\alpha_{v}),z_{0}-z_{1}\rangle_{BM}=\langle\alpha_{v},p_{\ast}(z_{0}-z_{1})\rangle_{BM}=\langle\alpha,\iota_{0}^{\ast}(Z)-\iota_{1}^{\ast}(Z)\rangle_{BM},

as required.
∎

4.
Weak approximation for 0-cycles

Let
Y
be a smooth, geometrically integral variety over a field
k
. Recall that, when
Y
is proper, the
Chow group

\CH_{0}(Y)
of
Y
is the quotient of
Z_{0}(Y)
by the subgroup generated by all
0
-cycles of the form
\phi_{\ast}(\textrm{div}_{C}(g))
, for all
\phi:C\to Y
proper morphisms over
k
from normal integral
k
-curves
C
and for all
g\in k(C)^{\times}
. In other words,
\CH_{0}(Y)
is the quotient of
Z_{0}(Y)
by the subgroup of
0
-cycles rationally equivalent to zero.

Now let
X
be a smooth, geometrically integral variety over a number field
k
.
While weak approximation for 0-cycles is usually defined, when
X
is proper, by using the Chow groups
\CH_{0}(X_{k_{v}})
for each
v\in\Omega_{k}
, in this paper we will need a slightly more general definition which works for non-proper varieties as well. Instead of the Chow groups
\CH_{0}(X_{k_{v}})
, we thus define weak approximation by using the Suslin’s singular homology groups
h_{0}(X_{k_{v}})
. We note that, when
X
is proper, our definition of weak approximation coincides with the classical one.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth, geometrically integral variety over a number field
k
. For any
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we say that
X

satisfies weak approximation for 0-cycles of degree
d
if the following condition holds: for any positive integer
n
and for any finite subset
S\subset\Omega_{k}
of places of
k
, if
(z_{v})_{v}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})
, then there exists a global 0-cycle
z_{n,S}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X)
such that
z_{n,S}
and
z_{v}
have the same image in
h_{0}(X_{k_{v}})/n
for any
v\in S
. We can also refine the notion of weak approximation by replacing the set
Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})
by some potentially smaller set
Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\omega}
still containing
Z_{0}^{d}(X)
, where
\omega
is some “obstruction” compatible with Suslin’s singular homology.

We will need the notion of two 0-cycles
being sufficiently close
.

Definition 4.2
.

(
[
26
, Définition 1.1(3)]
)
Let
k
be a number field and let
v\in\Omega_{k}
be a place of
v
. Let
Y
be a variety over
k_{v}
. Given a closed point
y\in Y
, we fix a
k_{v}
-embedding
k_{v}(y)\to\overline{k_{v}}
, so that we can view
y
as a
k_{v}(y)
-point of
Y
. Let
y^{\prime}\in Y
be a closed point and let
U_{y}\subset Y(k_{v}(y))
be an open neighbourhood of
y
with respect to the
v
-analytic topology. We say that
y^{\prime}
is
sufficiently close to
y
(with respect to
U_{y}
)
if
y^{\prime}
has residue field
k_{v}(y^{\prime})=k_{v}(y)
and if we can
choose a
k_{v}
-embedding
k_{v}(y^{\prime})\to\overline{k_{v}}
such that
y^{\prime}
, when viewed as a
k_{v}(y)
-point of
Y
, is contained in
U_{y}
. The general definition for two 0-cycles
z
and
z^{\prime}
on
Y
of the same degree to be
sufficiently close
(with respect to, say, a system of open neighbourhoods of the points in the support of
z
) is obtained by extending
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearly the above definition for closed points.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety over a number field
k
. Let
\alpha\in X
and let
(z_{v})_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})
. By the the fact that the evaluation of
\alpha
at
z_{v}
is locally constant for all
v\in\Omega_{k}
, we deduce that, for each
v\in\Omega_{k}
, there is a system of open neighbourhoods of the support of
z_{v}
such that, if
z^{\prime}_{v}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X_{k_{v}})
is sufficiently close to
z_{v}
with respect to this system of neighbourhoods (or smaller neighbourhoods), then the evaluation of
\alpha
at
z^{\prime}_{v}
is the same as the evaluation of
\alpha
at
z_{v}
(in fact, it is the same “point-wise”, for each point in the support of

).

We recall that, in characteristic 0, effective 0-cycles

of degree

on

are in one-to-one correspondence with
k_{v}
-points
[z_{v}]\in\Sym^{d}(X_{k_{v}})(k_{v})

z_{v}
be a 0-cycle of degree

z_{v}=z_{v}^{+}-z_{v}^{-}
, where

and

Hence,

corresponds to a pair of

([z_{v}^{+}],[z_{v}^{-}])\in\Sym^{d_{+}}(X_{k_{v}})(k_{v})\times\Sym^{d_{-}}(X_{k_{v}})(k_{v})
.
It turns out that, under the above identifications, if two 0-cycles are sufficiently close, then they also have the same image in
h_{0}(X_{k_{v}})/n
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let

be a smooth variety over a number field

and

have the same image in

Let

\pi:X^{\prime}\to\hat{X}

Remark 5.2

6.1.
Enriques surfaces

In this section, we study the arithmetic behaviour of 0-cycles on Enriques surfaces using our newly defined obstruction sets and some recent results on K3 surfaces.
There is indeed a well-known relationship between Enriques surfaces and K3 surfaces: any Enriques surface

Conjecturally, the qualitative arithmetic behaviour of rational points on K3 surfaces is completely determined by the Brauer-Manin obstruction.

Conjecture 6.1

(Skorobogatov,
[
40
]
)
.

The Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to the Hasse principle and weak approximation of rational points on K3 surfaces over number fields.

Some recent evidence towards Conjecture

]

[

]
,
[

]

Theorem 6.2

(
[

]
*Theorem 1.2)

6.2.
(Twisted) Kummer varieties as torsors

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a number field
k
of dimension
\geq 2
. Let
\sigma:=[T\to\Spec k]\in H^{1}_{\et}(k,A[2])
. Under the natural morphism
H^{1}_{\et}(k,A[2])\to H^{1}_{\et}(k,A)
, we have that
\sigma
gives rise to a 2-covering
\rho:V\to A
, where
V
has the structure of a
k
-torsor under
A
of period dividing 2. The involution
[-1]:A\to A
, fixing
A[2]
, induces an involution
\iota:V\to V
fixing
T=\rho^{-1}(0_{A})
. Let
\tilde{V}\to V
be the blow-up of
V
at
T
. Then the involution
\iota
induces an involution
\tilde{\iota}:\tilde{V}\to\tilde{V}
fixing the exceptional divisors of the blow-up. The quotient
\tilde{V}/\tilde{\iota}
is called the
(twisted) Kummer variety associated to
A
and
\sigma
. In what follows, we sometimes omit the references to
A
and
\sigma
and just talk about
(twisted) Kummer varieties
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
Y
be a (twisted) Kummer variety over a number field
k
. Then
Y
has a 0-cycle of degree a power of 2.

Proof.

Since
Y
is a (twisted) Kummer variety, it admits a double cover by a smooth, proper variety
Z
which is birational to a torsor
V
under an abelian variety of dimension
g
of period
P(V)
dividing
2
. If
I(V)
denotes the index of
V
, that is, the gcd of the degrees of all closed points on
V
, then the following divisibility relation between the period and index is well-known:

P(V)\ |\ I(V)\ |\ P(V)^{2g}.

In particular,
I(V)
must be a power of 2. Since, for
k
a number field, the index is a birational invariant of smooth varieties, it follows that
I(V)=I(Z)
. Hence, by definition of the index,
Z
has a 0-cycle of degree
I(Z)
, a power of 2. By pushing forward this 0-cycle from
Z
to
Y
, we obtain a 0-cycle on
Y
of degree a power of 2, as required.
∎

Given that there is a close relationship between (twisted) Kummer varieties and
k
-torsors under abelian varieties, and since, conditionally on the finiteness of the relevant Tate-Shafarevich group, the (algebraic) Brauer-Manin obstruction is the only one for the existence of rational points on

-torsors under abelian varieties (see e.g.,
[

,
14
]
), it is natural to ask the following question (and to possibly expect a positive answer).

6.3.

Recall that if
X
is a variety over

over

\chi_{\ast}({\overline{Y}})\to{\overline{X}}

(see
[

]
*Lemma 2.3.1),

is also a universal torsor for

(under

) for any finite extension

. Universal torsors satisfy many nice properties, in the context of rational points. For example, we have the following.

(
[

]

.

be a variety over

with

and

-module. Assume that

. Then

.

(2)

if, moreover,

Proof.

(1)
Let
(z_{v})_{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{g}
. Then, by definition,
there exist a non-empty finite set
S
of field extensions of
k
, non-empty finite sets
T_{K}\subset H^{1}_{\et}(K,G)
for each
K\in S
, and a tuple
((\Delta_{\tau})_{\tau\in T_{K}})_{K\in S}
of integers satisfying

\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}[K:k]\Delta_{\tau}=d
, and 0-cycles
(z^{\tau}_{v^{\prime}})_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}}\in Z_{0}^{\Delta_{\tau}}(W^{\tau}_{{\mathbb{A}}_{K}})
for all
\tau\in T_{K}
and all
K\in S
such that

\textbf{g}_{\ast,\textrm{ad}}\left(\left(\left((z^{\tau}_{v^{\prime}})_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}}\right)_{\tau\in T_{K}}\right)_{K\in S}\right)=(z_{v})_{v}.

For each
\tau\in T_{K}
(for each
K\in S
), we can fix a closed point
y_{\tau}\in W^{\tau}_{K}
, with
F_{\tau}:=K(y_{\tau})
and degree say
\delta_{y_{\tau}}:=[F_{\tau}:K]
. Then, arguing as in
[
27
]
*Theorem 3.2.1, we can construct an extension
L_{\tau}/K
of degree

[L_{\tau}:K]\equiv\Delta_{\tau}\ (\bmod\ n\delta_{y_{\tau}}),

say
[L_{\tau}:K]=\Delta_{\tau}+\lambda_{\tau}n\delta_{y_{\tau}}
for some integer
\lambda_{\tau}
, and an adelic point
(m_{w})_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}}\in W^{\tau}({\mathbb{A}}_{L_{\tau}})
with
\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}r_{L_{\tau,w}/K_{v^{\prime}}}(m_{w})
sufficiently close to
z_{v^{\prime}}^{\tau}
for all the places
v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}
above the places in
S^{\prime}
, where
r_{L_{\tau,w}/K_{v^{\prime}}}:W^{\tau}_{L_{\tau},w}\to W^{\tau}_{K_{v^{\prime}}}
is the natural map. By Proposition
4.4
,
\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}r_{L_{\tau,w}/K_{v^{\prime}}}(m_{w})
and
z_{v^{\prime}}^{\tau}
also have the same image in
h_{0}(W^{\tau}_{K_{v^{\prime}}})/n
.

Let
({x}^{(\tau)}_{w})_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}}:=(g_{L_{\tau}}^{\tau}(m_{w}))_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}}
. Then
({x}^{(\tau)}_{w})_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}}\in X({\mathbb{A}}_{L_{\tau}})^{g_{L_{\tau}}}
. But
g_{L_{\tau}}:W_{L_{\tau}}\to X_{L_{\tau}}
is again a universal torsor (under
G_{L_{\tau}}
). Hence, Theorem
6.9
yields that
({x}^{(\tau)}_{w})_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}}\in X({\mathbb{A}}_{L_{\tau}})^{\Br_{1}(X_{L_{\tau}})}
. Similarly,
(\tilde{{x}}^{(\tau)}_{w^{\prime}})_{w^{\prime}\in\Omega_{F_{\tau}}}:=(g^{\tau}_{F_{\tau}}((y_{\tau})_{w^{\prime}}))_{w^{\prime}\in\Omega_{F_{\tau}}}\in X({\mathbb{A}}_{F_{\tau}})^{\Br_{1}(X_{F_{\tau}})}
.

Consider the adelic 0-cycle on
X
whose
v
-adic component is given by

u_{v}:=\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\left(\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}s_{L_{\tau,w}/k_{v}}({x}^{(\tau)}_{w})\ -\lambda_{\tau}n\ \sum_{w^{\prime}\in\Omega_{F_{\tau}}:w^{\prime}|v^{\prime}}s_{F_{\tau,w^{\prime}}/k_{v}}(\tilde{{x}}^{(\tau)}_{w^{\prime}})\right),

where
s_{L_{\tau,w}/k_{v}}:X_{L_{\tau,w}}\to X_{k_{v}}
and
s_{F_{\tau,w^{\prime}}/k_{v}}:X_{F_{\tau,w^{\prime}}}\to X_{k_{v}}
are the natural maps,
with degree

\begin{array}[]{rl}\deg(u_{v})=&\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\left(\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}[(L_{\tau})_{w}:k_{v}]\ -\lambda_{\tau}n\ \sum_{w^{\prime}\in\Omega_{F_{\tau}}:w^{\prime}|v^{\prime}}[(F_{\tau})_{w^{\prime}}:k_{v}]\right)\\
=&\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\left([L_{\tau}:K]-\lambda_{\tau}n[F_{\tau}:K]\right)\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}[(K)_{v^{\prime}}:k_{v}]\\
=&\sum_{K\in S}[K:k]\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\left([L_{\tau}:K]-\lambda_{\tau}n[F_{\tau}:K]\right)\\
=&\sum_{K\in S}[K:k]\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\Delta_{\tau}\\
=&d.\end{array}

We claim that
(u_{v})_{v}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\Br_{1}}
. Indeed, for any
\alpha\in\Br_{1}(X)
,

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\left(\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}\inv_{v}\left(\cores_{(L_{\tau})_{w}/k_{v}}\left(\alpha\left({x}^{(\tau)}_{w}\right)\right)\right)\right.\\
&\left.-\lambda_{\tau}n\ \sum_{w^{\prime}\in\Omega_{F_{\tau}}:w^{\prime}|v^{\prime}}\inv_{v}\left(\cores_{(F_{\tau})_{w^{\prime}}/k_{v}}\left(\alpha\left(\tilde{{x}}^{(\tau)}_{w^{\prime}}\right)\right)\right)\right)\\
=&\left(\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}\inv_{v}\left(\cores_{(L_{\tau})_{w}/k_{v}}\left(\alpha\left({x}^{(\tau)}_{w}\right)\right)\right)\right)\\
&-\lambda_{\tau}n\left(\sum_{v}\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\ \sum_{w^{\prime}\in\Omega_{F_{\tau}}:w^{\prime}|v^{\prime}}\inv_{v}\left(\cores_{(F_{\tau})_{w^{\prime}}/k_{v}}\left(\alpha\left(\tilde{{x}}^{(\tau)}_{w^{\prime}}\right)\right)\right)\right)\\
\end{array}

Now,

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}\inv_{v}\left(\cores_{(L_{\tau})_{w}/k_{v}}\left(\alpha\left({x}^{(\tau)}_{w}\right)\right)\right)\\
=&\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}\inv_{w}\left(\alpha\left({x}^{(\tau)}_{w}\right)\right)\\
=&\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}}\inv_{w}\left(\alpha\left({x}^{(\tau)}_{w}\right)\right)\\
=&\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}0\\
=&0,\end{array}

where in the first equality we have used the commutative diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Br(\Spec((L_{\tau})_{w}))&\xrightarrow{\inv_{w}}&{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\\
\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\xleftarrow{\rule{9.04166pt}{0.0pt}}$}}{\cores_{(L_{\tau})_{w}/k_{v}}}&&\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\xleftarrow{\rule{9.04166pt}{0.0pt}}$}}{=}\\
\Br(\Spec(k_{v}))&\xrightarrow{\inv_{v}}&{\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\\
\end{array}

and in third equality we have used the fact that
({x}^{\tau}_{w})_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}}\in X({\mathbb{A}}_{L_{\tau}})^{\Br_{1}(X_{L_{\tau}})}
.
Similarly, one can show that

\sum_{v\in\Omega_{k}}\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\ \sum_{w^{\prime}\in\Omega_{F_{\tau}}:w^{\prime}|v^{\prime}}\inv_{v}\left(\cores_{(F_{\tau})_{w^{\prime}}/k_{v}}\left(\alpha\left(\tilde{{x}}^{(\tau)}_{w^{\prime}}\right)\right)\right)=0.

Hence,
(u_{v})_{v}\in Z_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\Br_{1}}
, as required.

Finally, we remark that, since
\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}r_{L_{\tau,w}/K_{v^{\prime}}}(m_{w})
and
z_{v^{\prime}}^{\tau}
have the same image in

, all
K\in S
, and all places
v^{\prime}
above the places

, and by the compatibility of Suslin’s homology with pushforward morphisms, it follows that
\sum_{K\in S}\sum_{\tau\in T_{K}}\sum_{v^{\prime}\in\Omega_{K}:v^{\prime}|v}\sum_{w\in\Omega_{L_{\tau}}:w|v^{\prime}}s_{L_{\tau,w}/k_{v}}({x}^{(\tau)}_{w})

have the same image in

, and thus that

for all

. Since

is proper, we note that

(2) The assumption that

is finite can only be true when

is torsion-free: since
H^{3}_{\et}(k,{\overline{k}}^{\times})=0
for any number field

, by the long exact sequence coming from the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence

has non-trivial torsion.
Let
K/k
be a finite Galois extension such that

*Prop 3.1.1, we have that, for any finite extension

Let
(z_{v})_{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\Br_{1}}

Finally, we consider torsors under tori. In the rational points setting, Harpaz and Wittenberg have proved the following nice result.

Theorem 6.11

(
[
21
]
*Théorème 2.1)
.

Let
X
be a smooth, geometrically integral variety over
k
. Let
f:Y\to X
be a torsor under a
k
-torus
T
. Let
A\subset\Br X
be the inverse image of
\Br_{nr}(Y)\subset\Br Y
under
f^{\ast}:\Br X\to\Br Y
. Then

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{A}\subset\bigcup_{\sigma\in H^{1}(k,T)}f^{\sigma}(Y^{\sigma}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br_{nr}}).

A very similar proof to that of Theorem
6.10
(2), together with Theorem
6.11
, yields the following immediate statement for 0-cycles in the spirit of Theorem
6.11
, albeit with the usual restrictions on the Brauer groups.

Theorem 6.12
.

Let
X
be a smooth, proper, geometrically integral variety over
k
. Let
f:Y\to X
be a torsor under a
k
-torus
T
. Assume that
\Br X/\Br_{0}X
is finite and that there is some finite extension
F/k
such that
\res_{l/k}:\Br X/\Br_{0}X\to\Br_{nr}(X_{l})/\Br_{0}(X_{l})
is surjective for all finite extensions
l/k
linearly disjoint from
F
over
k
. Then, for any integer
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, for any positive integer
n
, and for any finite subset
S^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{k}
of places of
k
, we have that
(z_{v})_{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{\Br_{nr}}
implies that there exists some
(u_{v})_{v}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{0}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{A}}_{k}})^{f,\Br_{nr}}
such that
z_{v}
and
u_{v}
have the same image in
\CH_{0}(X_{k_{v}})/n
for all

.

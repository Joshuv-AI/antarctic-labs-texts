A general error analysis for randomized low-rank approximation methods Thanks:
Youssef Diouane, Selime Gürol, Alexandre Scotto di Perrotolo, Xavier Vasseur

Abstract
We propose a general error analysis related to the low-rank approximation of a given real matrix in both the spectral and Frobenius norms. First, we derive deterministic error bounds that hold with some minimal assumptions. Second, we derive error bounds in expectation in the non-standard Gaussian case, assuming a non-trivial mean and a general covariance matrix for the random matrix variable. The proposed analysis generalizes and improves the error bounds for spectral and Frobenius norms proposed by Halko, Martinsson and Tropp. Third, we consider the Randomized Singular Value Decomposition and specialize our error bounds in expectation in this setting. Numerical experiments on an instructional synthetic test case demonstrate the tightness of the new error bounds.



keywords

Low-rank approximation, randomized algorithms, Singular Value Decomposition.

Introduction

Low-rank approximation of large-scale matrices is a key ingredient in numerous applications in data analysis and scientific computing. These applications include principal component analysis
[
20
]
, data compression
[
15
]
and approximation algorithms for partial differential and integral equations
[
10
]
, to name a few. Namely, let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
be a rectangular matrix satisfying
n\geq m
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
any full column rank random matrix (with
p\leq\rank(A)\leq m
). If
\pi(Z)
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the range of
Z
and
I_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
the identity matrix of order
n
, we aim at analyzing the general quantity of interest

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2,F},

(1.1)

where
\|.\|_{2,F}
represents a shortcut for either the spectral norm (
\|.\|_{2}
) or the Frobenius norm (
\|.\|_{F}
), respectively.

In our analysis, we will consider the standard Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of
A
, i.e.,
A=U\Sigma V^{\top}
, where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
V\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
are orthogonal matrices containing the left and right singular vectors of
A
respectively and
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
is a diagonal matrix containing the singular values of
A
denoted as
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}
(sorted in decreasing order, i.e.
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\dots\geq\sigma_{m}\geq 0
). For a target rank
k\in\mathinner{\left\{1,\dots,\rank{(A)}\right\}}
, which is assumed to be much smaller than
n
, a central decomposition appearing in the error analysis is then given by

A=\begin{bmatrix}U_{k}&\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\Sigma_{k}&\\
&\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}V_{k}^{\top}\\
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{V}_{k}^{\top}\end{bmatrix},

(1.2)

with
U_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
,
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times(n-k)}
,
V_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
,
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{V}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times(m-k)}
,
\Sigma_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times(m-k)}
. We also set
A_{k}=U_{k}\Sigma_{k}V_{k}^{\top}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{V}_{k}^{\top}
so that
A=A_{k}+\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}.

The Eckart-Young theorem
[
5
]
states that the optimal rank-
k
approximation of
A
is given by
A_{k}=\pi(U_{k})A
. In practice, for large-scale applications, computing
U_{k}
can be computationally challenging or too expensive. In this context, randomized algorithms for approximating
U_{k}
have become increasingly popular
[
11
,
16
]
in the past few years. They have been proved to be easy to implement, computationally efficient and numerically robust. The general idea of randomized subspace iteration methods is to use random sampling to identify a subspace that approximates the range of a given matrix. In this manuscript, we propose a general error analysis related to the low-rank approximation to a given real matrix in the spectral and Frobenius norms.

Related research

The error analysis of randomized algorithms for low-rank approximation to a given matrix has been extensively considered in the literature; see, e.g., the survey papers
[
11
,
15
,
16
,
25
]
for a general overview. This theoretical analysis takes into account the distribution of the random matrix to derive either error bounds in expectation or tail bounds of the error distribution. The case of standard Gaussian matrices is usually considered, even though alternative theoretical results exist, based on either random column selection matrices
[
3
]
or Subsampled Random Fourier Transform matrices
[
11
]
. Halko, Martinsson and Tropp have developed a reference error analysis in expectation
[
11
, Theorems 10.5 and 10.6]
in the Frobenius and spectral norms for
Z=A\Omega
,
\Omega
being a standard Gaussian matrix. Later, Gu has refined these error bounds
[
8
, Theorem 5.7]
.
More recently, Saibaba
[
21
]
has proposed a complementary average case error analysis in terms of principal angles between appropriate subspaces that is available in any unitarily invariant norm.

Motivations

In certain situations, a priori knowledge of
Z
(or of the corresponding projection subspace) may be available. This naturally arises when considering, e.g., the solution of large-scale nonlinear systems of equations, requiring the solution of a sequence of linear systems of equations. Approximate spectral information based on Ritz or Harmonic Ritz vectors can be easily retrieved to form such a subspace. Exploiting this a priori knowledge is thus of primary interest to design fast, robust and efficient low-rank approximation algorithms. Hence we aim at developing a general theoretical error analysis of randomized algorithms for the low-rank approximation, assuming the existence of a non-trivial mean and of a general covariance matrix for
Z
.

Contributions

In this manuscript, for a given target rank, we propose to analyze theoretically the low-rank approximation to a given matrix in both the spectral and Frobenius norms, when
Z
is drawn from a non-standard Gaussian distribution. Namely, we will first derive in Theorems
4
and
8
deterministic error bounds that hold with some minimal assumptions. Second, we will derive error bounds in expectation in the non-standard Gaussian case (with a non-trivial mean value and a general covariance matrix) in Theorems
17
,
19
and
20
, respectively. Our analysis simultaneously generalizes and improves the error bounds for spectral and Frobenius norms proposed in
[
11
]
. We specialize our error bounds to the Randomized Singular Value Decomposition (RSVD) in Corollaries
24
,
26
and
27
, respectively and provide numerical experiments on a synthetic test case that illustrate the tightness of the obtained error bounds.

2
Preliminaries

We first introduce notation used throughout the manuscript and remind specific results.

Gaussian matrix

We use the notation
x\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
to define a scalar random variable
x
that follows a Gaussian distribution with mean
\mu
and standard deviation
\sigma
. A Gaussian matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}
is a matrix whose columns are independent
m
-dimensional Gaussian vectors, that is
M=[m_{1},~\dots,~m_{p}]
with
m_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
for
1\leq i\leq p
. If
m_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{m}_{i},C)
where
\widehat{m}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
C\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
, then we write

M\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{M},C)\quad\text{with}\quad\widehat{M}=[\widehat{m}_{1},~\dots,~\widehat{m}_{p}]\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}.

(2.1)

Hence the covariance matrix of the random matrix variable
M
, later explicitly denoted by
\Covmat(M)
, is equal to
C
. An equivalent formulation
[
9
, Chapter 5]
also reads

M=\widehat{M}+\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}G\quad\text{with}\quad G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{I}_{m}),

(2.2)

where
\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}
refers either to the positive definite square root of
\Covmat(M)
if
\Covmat(M)
is positive definite or to the unique positive semidefinite square root of
\Covmat(M)
if
\Covmat(M)
is positive semidefinite
[
14
, Theorem 7.2.6]
, respectively.

Partial ordering on the set of symmetric matrices

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m},N\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
be two symmetric matrices. The notation
M\preccurlyeq N
means that
N-M
is positive semidefinite. It defines a partial ordering on the set of symmetric matrices
[
14
, Section 7.7]
. An important property is that the partial ordering is preserved under the conjugation rule, i.e.,

M\preccurlyeq N\implies Q^{\top}MQ\preccurlyeq Q^{\top}NQ,\quad\forall Q\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}.

(2.3)

We note that, in particular, for all
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
N\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
such that
M^{\top}M\preccurlyeq N^{\top}N
, we have
\|M\|_{2,F}\leq\|N\|_{2,F}
.

Projection matrices

Suppose that
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
has full column rank with column range space denoted by
\range(M)
. We denote by
M^{\dagger}
the left multiplicative inverse of
M
, i.e., the Moore-Penrose inverse of
M
, see, e.g.,
[
14
]
. The orthogonal projection on
\range(M)
is then given by
\pi(M)=MM^{\dagger}
, in particular, one has
\range(\pi(M))=\range(M)
.

Sherman-Morrison formula

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
N\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
such that
{I}_{n}+NM
is non-singular. Then,
{I}_{m}+MN
is also non-singular and
({I}_{m}+MN)^{\mathsf{-1}}={I}_{m}-M({I}_{n}+NM)^{\mathsf{-1}}N

[
7
, Section 2.1.4]
.

Strong submultiplicativity of Frobenius and spectral norms

The strong submultiplicativity of the spectral and Frobenius norms
[
13
, Relation(B.7)]
reads

\forall M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p},\forall N\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q},\forall Q\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times m},\|MNQ\|_{2,F}\leq\|M\|_{2}~\|N\|_{2,F}~\|Q\|_{2}.

(2.4)

3
Error bounds for the low-rank approximation of a matrix

Our main objective is to derive error bounds related to an approximation of rank
k
to
A
using the orthogonal projection
\pi(Z)
where
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
,
p\leq\rank(A)
. First, in Section
3.1
, we consider the general case with the minimal assumption that
Z
is full column rank. In this case, we are able to derive deterministic error bounds using a systematic approach. Second, in Section
3.2
, we focus on error bounds that are tractable now from a stochastic point of view, where we assume that the matrix
Z
corresponds to a general Gaussian matrix. Namely,
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
will be drawn as a Gaussian full column rank matrix of mean
\widehat{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and covariance matrix
\Covmat(Z)
, that is,
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{Z},\Covmat(Z))
. In this case, we develop an error analysis in expectation with respect to the random variable
Z
.

3.1
Deterministic analysis

Without loss of generality, we aim at deriving error bounds for the following quantity

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|^{2}_{2,F}-\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|^{2}_{2,F},

(3.1)

where
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is a full column rank matrix (with
p\leq\rank(A)
). Since
\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\leq\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}
, we note that (
3.1
) is an upper bound of

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|^{2}_{2,F}-\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|^{2}_{2,F},

(3.2)

a quantity which is frequently considered in the analysis of low-rank approximation methods, see, e.g.,
[
11
]
. In this sense, our error bounds will naturally cover existing bounds from the literature. The next lemma will be helpful to derive the deterministic error bound for (
3.1
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
such that
n\geq m
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
a full column rank matrix with
p\leq\rank(A)
. For a given
k\in\{1,\dots,p\}
, set
\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}Z\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times p}
such that
\Omega_{k}
is full row rank (i.e.,
\rank(\Omega_{k})=k
).
Then, one has

U_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\quad\preccurlyeq\quad S_{k}^{\top}S_{k}\quad\preccurlyeq\quad T_{k}^{\top}T_{k},

(3.3)

with
T_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times k}
and
S_{k}=({I}_{n-k}+T_{k}T_{k}^{\top})^{-\frac{1}{2}}T_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times k}
.

Proof.

By assumption,
\Omega_{k}
has full row rank and therefore has a right multiplicative inverse
\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}
. Hence
\bar{Z}_{k}=Z\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}
satisfies the two relations

U_{k}^{\top}\bar{Z}_{k}={I}_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\bar{Z}_{k}=T_{k}.

Moreover, we have
\range(\bar{Z}_{k})\subset\range(Z)
. Hence, by applying
[
11
, Proposition 8.5]
, one gets
{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)\preccurlyeq{I}_{n}-\pi(\bar{Z}_{k})
. By using the conjugation rule (
2.3
) and the identity
U_{k}U_{k}^{\top}+\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}={I}_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle U_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\preccurlyeq U_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(\bar{Z}_{k})]U_{k}

\displaystyle=U_{k}^{\top}\left({I}_{n}-\bar{Z}_{k}(\bar{Z}_{k}^{\top}\bar{Z}_{k})^{\mathsf{-1}}\bar{Z}_{k}^{\top}\right)U_{k},

\displaystyle={I}_{k}-U_{k}^{\top}\bar{Z}_{k}(\bar{Z}_{k}^{\top}\bar{Z}_{k})^{\mathsf{-1}}\bar{Z}_{k}^{\top}U_{k},

\displaystyle={I}_{k}-(\bar{Z}_{k}^{\top}\bar{Z}_{k})^{\mathsf{-1}},

\displaystyle={I}_{k}-\left(\bar{Z}_{k}^{\top}(U_{k}U_{k}^{\top}+\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top})\bar{Z}_{k}\right)^{\mathsf{-1}},

\displaystyle={I}_{k}-\left({I}_{k}+T_{k}^{\top}T_{k}\right)^{\mathsf{-1}},

\displaystyle=T_{k}^{\top}\left({I}_{n-k}+T_{k}T_{k}^{\top}\right)^{\mathsf{-1}}T_{k},

where the last equality is obtained using the Sherman-Morrison formula. Then we observe that
T_{k}^{\top}\left({I}_{n-k}+T_{k}T_{k}^{\top}\right)^{\mathsf{-1}}T_{k}=S_{k}^{\top}S_{k}
and
\left({I}_{n-k}+T_{k}T_{k}^{\top}\right)^{\mathsf{-1}}\preccurlyeq{I}_{n-k}
. We
conclude the proof by applying the conjugation rule to deduce
S_{k}^{\top}S_{k}\preccurlyeq T_{k}^{\top}T_{k}
.

Remark 2
.

In the particular case of
Z=A\Omega
(
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
), we have
\Omega_{k}=\Sigma_{k}(V_{k}^{\top}\Omega)
,
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{V}_{k}^{\top}\Omega)
and
T_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{V}_{k}^{\top}\Omega)(V_{k}^{\top}\Omega)^{{}^{\dagger}}\Sigma_{k}^{{}^{\mathsf{-1}}}
. With the notation of
[
11
]
, this gives
\Omega_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Sigma_{1}\Omega_{1}
,
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Sigma_{2}\Omega_{2}
and
T_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\Sigma_{2}\Omega_{2}\Omega_{1}^{{}^{\dagger}}\Sigma_{1}^{{}^{\mathsf{-1}}}
, respectively. We therefore point out that our notation is related but not directly equivalent to the one employed in
[
11
]
.

Remark 3
.

We note that the positive singular values of
T_{k}
represent the tangent of the canonical angles between
\range(Z\Omega_{k}^{\dagger})
and
\range(U_{k})

[
26
, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.1]
, while the positive singular values of
S_{k}
represent the sine of the canonical angles between the same subspaces. Hence we stress this information in the notation and refer the reader to
[
21
]
for a theoretical analysis of low-rank approximation methods in terms of subspace angles.

The next theorem introduces unified deterministic error bounds for the quantity of interest (
3.1
).

Theorem 4
.

(Deterministic error bounds in Frobenius and spectral norms)
Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
such that
n\geq m
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
a full column rank matrix with
p\leq\rank(A)
. For a given
k\in\{1,\dots,p\}
, set
\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}Z\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times p}
such that
\Omega_{k}
is full row rank (i.e.,
\rank(\Omega_{k})=k
). Then, one has

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2,F}^{2}-\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\leq\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\leq\min\mathinner{\left\{\|S_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\|\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}^{2},~\|T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\right\}},

(3.4)

where
T_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times k}
and
S_{k}=({I}_{n-k}+T_{k}T_{k}^{\top})^{-\frac{1}{2}}T_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times k}
.

Proof.

Using the identity
AA^{\top}=A_{k}A_{k}^{\top}+\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}^{\top}
, we obtain for the spectral norm case

\displaystyle\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]AA^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\|_{2},

\displaystyle=\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)][A_{k}A_{k}^{\top}+\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}^{\top}][{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\|_{2}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus, by using the unitarily invariance of the spectral norm, we get

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}^{2}-\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

(3.5)

Moreover, using Lemma
1
and the conjugation rule, we get

\Sigma_{k}^{\top}U_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\preccurlyeq\Sigma_{k}^{\top}S_{k}^{\top}S_{k}\Sigma_{k}\preccurlyeq\Sigma_{k}T_{k}^{\top}T_{k}\Sigma_{k}.

(3.6)

Hence, since
[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]^{2}={I}_{n}-\pi(Z)
, we obtain

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|^{2}_{2}=\|\Sigma_{k}^{\top}U_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}\leq\|\Sigma_{k}^{\top}S_{k}^{\top}S_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}\leq\|\Sigma_{k}^{\top}T_{k}^{\top}T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2},

or equivalently,

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\|S_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

Using the strong submultiplicativity of the spectral norm, the latter inequality implies

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\min\mathinner{\left\{\|S_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\|\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}^{2},~\|T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right\}}.

(3.7)

Combining (
3.5
) and (
3.7
) completes the proof for the spectral norm case.

For the Frobenius norm, similar arguments are used. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tr\left([{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]AA^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\right),

\displaystyle=\tr\left([{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)][A_{k}A_{k}^{\top}+\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}^{\top}][{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\right),

\displaystyle=\tr\left([{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A_{k}A_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\right)+\tr\left([{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\right),

\displaystyle=\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{F}^{2}+\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}.

Thus,

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{F}^{2}-\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}=\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{F}^{2}.

(3.8)

Using (
3.6
), we obtain

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|^{2}_{F}=\tr\left(\Sigma_{k}^{\top}U_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\right)\leq\tr\left(\Sigma_{k}^{\top}S_{k}^{\top}S_{k}\Sigma_{k}\right)\leq\tr\left(\Sigma_{k}^{\top}T_{k}^{\top}T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\right).

Hence we obtain

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|^{2}_{F}\leq\min\mathinner{\left\{\|S_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\|\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}^{2},~\|T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\right\}}.

(3.9)

Combining relations (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) completes the proof for the Frobenius norm case.

Remark 5
.

We have shown in Theorem
4
that

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2,F}^{2}-\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\leq\|S_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\leq\|T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}.

(3.10)

Hence,
\|S_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}
definitively represents a sharper upper bound in the deterministic case. Nevertheless we have decided to use the submultiplicativity to bound
\|S_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}
, since only this formulation authorizes a possible treatment in the stochastic setting as detailed in Section
3.2
.

Remark 6
.

In the particular case of
Z=A\Omega
(
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
), with the notation of
[
11
]
, we have
\|T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2,F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\|\Sigma_{2}\Omega_{2}\Omega_{1}^{{}^{\dagger}}\|_{2,F}
, a quantity which precisely appears in the reference upper bound
[
11
, Theorem 9.1]
. We note that our error bound (
3.4
) is tighter whenever
\|S_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\|\Sigma_{k}\|_{2}^{2}<\|T_{k}\Sigma_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}
. Hence Theorem
4
either recovers or improves the reference error bound
[
11
, Theorem 9.1]
in this setting.

Remark 7
.

Using
\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}\leq\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2,F}^{2}
and
\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\leq a+b
for any real positive scalars
a
and
b
, we note that Theorem
4
implies
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
when stated in the spectral and Frobenius norms. As
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
, Theorem
4
can be extended to the case of any Schatten-
p
class of norms.

If we target to bound
\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}^{2}-\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|^{2}_{2}
instead of
\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}^{2}-\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}
, we are able to improve the result given in Theorem
4
. This is detailed next.

Theorem 8
.

(Improved deterministic error bound in spectral norm)
Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
such that
n\geq m
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
a full column rank matrix with
p\leq\rank(A)
. For a given
k\in\{1,\dots,p\}
, set
\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}Z\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}Z\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times p}
such that
\Omega_{k}
is full row rank (i.e.,
\rank(\Omega_{k})=k
). Then, one has

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}^{2}-\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|^{2}_{2}\leq\min\mathinner{\left\{\|S_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\|_{2}^{2},~\|T_{k}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right\}},

(3.11)

where
\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}=\left(\Sigma_{k}^{2}-\sigma_{k+1}^{2}{I}_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
,
T_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times k}
and
S_{k}=({I}_{n-k}+T_{k}T_{k}^{\top})^{-\frac{1}{2}}T_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times k}
.

Proof.

First, we note that
\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|^{2}_{2}=\sigma_{k+1}^{2}
. Then, by definition of
\sigma_{k+1}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}
, one has
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}^{\top}\preccurlyeq\sigma_{k+1}^{2}{I}_{n-k}
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle AA^{\top}

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq U_{k}\Sigma_{k}^{2}U_{k}^{\top}+\sigma_{k+1}^{2}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top},

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq U_{k}\Sigma_{k}^{2}U_{k}^{\top}+\sigma_{k+1}^{2}({I}_{n}-U_{k}U_{k}^{\top}),

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq U_{k}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}^{2}U_{k}^{\top}+\sigma_{k+1}^{2}{I}_{n}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]AA^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\|_{2},

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{k+1}^{2}\|{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)\|_{2}+\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}^{2}U_{k}^{\top}[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\|_{2},

\displaystyle\leq\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|^{2}_{2}+\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]U_{k}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

Then, using (
3.7
) with
\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}
instead of
\Sigma_{k}
, the rest of the proof
follows straightforwardly.

By definition of
\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}
, one has
\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\preccurlyeq\Sigma_{k}
. Thus we deduce that if we target to bound
\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}^{2}-\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|^{2}_{2}
, then the error bound (
3.11
) is tighter than (
3.4
). To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
8
is new and provides an improved error bound in the spectral norm for a fairly general choice of
Z
.

Finally, we emphasize that both Theorems
4
and
8
will play a key role for deriving new improved error bounds in expectation for the low-rank approximation to a given matrix. This is detailed next.

3.2
Stochastic analysis

We now provide error bounds in expectation and consider the case where
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is drawn as a Gaussian matrix of mean
\widehat{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and covariance matrix
\Covmat(Z)
, that is,
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{Z},\Covmat(Z))
, with
2<p\leq\min\mathinner{\left\{\rank(A),\,\rank(\Covmat(Z))\right\}}
. For
k\in\{1,\ldots,p-2\}
, we define
\Omega_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times p}
as
\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})
, respectively. The condition
p\leq\rank{(\Covmat(Z))}
ensures that
Z-\widehat{Z}
has full column rank with probability one
[
6
]
. We assume that
\Omega_{k}
has full row rank in this section.

This general approach offers several advantages but rises additional technical difficulties. In particular,
\Omega_{k}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}
are now stochastically dependent on the distribution law of
Z
. Thus, before stating our main results in Section
3.2.2
, we provide in Section
3.2.1
preparatory lemmas that extend existing probabilistic results to the non-standard Gaussian case. In this section, we consider the following block partitioning of the projected covariance matrix
U^{\top}\Covmat(Z)\,U

U^{\top}\Covmat(Z)\,U=\begin{bmatrix}~U_{k}^{\top}~\\
~\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}~\end{bmatrix}\Covmat(Z)\begin{bmatrix}~U_{k}&\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}~\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\Covmat_{k}(Z)&\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)^{\top}\\
\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)&\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)\end{bmatrix},

with

\displaystyle\Covmat_{k}(Z)

\displaystyle=U_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)U_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)

\displaystyle=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)U_{k}\in\real^{(n-k)\times k},

\displaystyle\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)

\displaystyle=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}\in\real^{(n-k)\times(n-k)}.

3.2.1
Preparatory lemmas

Given that
\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})
, then by using elementary properties of Gaussian vectors, one gets that
\Omega_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Covmat_{k}(Z))
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z))
. We note that, although
\Omega_{k}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}
are centered Gaussian matrices, the conditional law of
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}
with respect to
\Omega_{k}
follows a Gaussian distribution that is not necessarily centered
[
17
, Theorem 1.2.11]
. We therefore adapt this result to our setting in the next lemma.

Lemma 9
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
such that
n\geq m
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
a Gaussian full column rank matrix of mean
\widehat{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and covariance matrix
\Covmat(Z)
, that is,
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{Z},\Covmat(Z))
satisfying
2<p\leq\min\mathinner{\left\{\rank(A),\,\rank(\Covmat(Z))\right\}}
. For a given
k\in\mathinner{\left\{1,\dots,p-2\right\}}
, set
\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
,
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times p}
such that
\Omega_{k}
is full row rank (i.e.
\rank(\Omega_{k})=k
). If the projected covariance matrix
\Covmat_{k}(Z)
is non-singular, then the random matrix
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}
conditioned by
\Omega_{k}
follows a Gaussian distribution of mean

\mathbb{E}\left[\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right]=\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}\Omega_{k},

(3.13)

and of covariance matrix given by

\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)=\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)-\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)\left[\Covmat_{k}(Z)\right]^{\mathsf{-1}}\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)^{\top},

(3.14)

where
\Covmat_{k}(Z)=U_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)U_{k}
,
\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)U_{k}
and
\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}
.

Remark 10
.

When
\Covmat{(Z)}=AA^{\top}
, we note that
\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)~=~0
, which yields
\mathbb{E}\left[\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right]=0
and
\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)=\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)
.

The next two lemmas aim at proposing key results about Gaussian matrices in the non-standard case. In particular, we extend
[
11
, Propositions 10.1 and 10.2]
to the case of a non-standard Gaussian matrix with general covariance matrix and potentially nonzero mean term.

Lemma 11
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
be a given matrix and
M\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
be a Gaussian matrix such that
M\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{M},\Covmat(M))
with mean
\widehat{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
and covariance matrix
\Covmat(M)\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\|MN\|_{2}\right]\leq\|\widehat{M}N\|_{2}+\|\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2}\|N\|_{F}+\|\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\|N\|_{2},

(3.15)

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\|MN\|_{F}^{2}\right]=\|\widehat{M}N\|_{F}^{2}+\|\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}^{2}\|N\|_{F}^{2}.

(3.16)

Proof.

Using the definition of a non-standard Gaussian matrix (
2.2
), we have
M=\widehat{M}+\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}G
with
G\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
a standard Gaussian matrix (i.e.
G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{k})
). Thus, by applying the triangle inequality in the spectral norm, we get

\|MN\|_{2}=\|(\widehat{M}+\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}G)N\|_{2}\leq\|\widehat{M}N\|_{2}+\|\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}GN\|_{2}.

Then, by applying
[
11
, Proposition 10.1]
to obtain an upper bound of
\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}GN\|_{2}\right]
, we deduce (
3.15
).
In the Frobenius norm, we have

\|MN\|_{F}^{2}=\|\widehat{M}N+\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}GN\|_{F}^{2}=\|\widehat{M}N\|_{F}^{2}+\|\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}GN\|_{F}^{2}+2\tr(N^{\top}\widehat{M}^{\top}\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}GN).

Taking the expectation and using
[
11
, Proposition 10.1]
, we get

\mathbb{E}\left[\|MN\|_{F}^{2}\right]=\|\widehat{M}N\|_{F}^{2}+\|\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}^{2}\|N\|_{F}^{2}+2~\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(N^{\top}\widehat{M}^{\top}\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}GN)\right].

By using the linearity of the expectation and the fact that
\mathbb{E}\left[G\right]=0
, we remark that

\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(N^{\top}\widehat{M}^{\top}\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}GN)\right]=\tr\left(N^{\top}\widehat{M}^{\top}\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[G\right]N\right)=0.

This concludes the proof.

In the next lemma, we show how to bound (in expectation) the quantity
\|M^{\dagger}N\|_{2}
or
\|M^{\dagger}N\|^{2}_{F}
, where
N\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
is a given fixed matrix and
M\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
follows a centered Gaussian distribution.

Lemma 12
.

For a fixed integer
k>1
, let
N\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
be a given matrix and
M\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
(with
p>k+1
) be a centered Gaussian matrix
M\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Covmat(M))
. If the covariance matrix
\Covmat(M)
is non-singular, then

\mathbb{E}\left[\|M^{\dagger}N\|_{F}^{2}\right]=\frac{\|(N^{\top}[\Covmat(M)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}}{p-k-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\|M^{\dagger}N\|_{2}\right]\leq\frac{e\sqrt{p}}{p-k}\sqrt{\|N^{\top}[\Covmat(M)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N\|_{2}},

where
e
denotes exponential of
1
, i.e.,
e=\exp(1)
.

Proof.

Since
M
is a random matrix with
p
independent columns, where each column is a multivariate Gaussian distribution with zero mean and covariance matrix
\Covmat(M)
, the matrix
MM^{\top}
follows a Wishart distribution of the form
\mathcal{W}_{k}(p,\Covmat(M))
[
17
, Definition 3.1.3]
. One has also
\|M^{\dagger}N\|_{F}^{2}=\tr(N^{\top}[(M^{\dagger})^{\top}M^{\dagger}]N)=\tr(N^{\top}[MM^{\top}]^{\mathsf{-1}}N)
, where the second equality holds with probability one since
p>k+1
. In fact, if
\Covmat(M)
is non-singular, the matrix
MM^{\top}
is non-singular almost surely, see
[
17
, Theorem 3.1.4]
. In this case, according to
[
17
, Theorem 3.2.12]
for
p>k+1
, one has, for any matrix
N\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[N^{\top}[MM^{\top}]^{\mathsf{-1}}N\right]=N^{\top}\mathbb{E}\left[[MM^{\top}]^{\mathsf{-1}}\right]N=\displaystyle\frac{N^{\top}\Covmat(M)^{\mathsf{-1}}N}{p-k-1}.

Hence,

\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(N^{\top}[MM^{\top}]^{\mathsf{-1}}N)\right]=\displaystyle\frac{\tr(N^{\top}\Covmat(M)^{\mathsf{-1}}N)}{p-k-1}=\displaystyle\frac{\|(N^{\top}\Covmat(M)^{\mathsf{-1}}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}}{p-k-1},

which concludes the proof for the Frobenius norm.
In the spectral norm, using (
2.2
), we have
M=\Covmat(M)^{\frac{1}{2}}G
with
G\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
following a standard Gaussian distribution. If
\Covmat(M)
is non-singular, then one has
M^{\dagger}=G^{\dagger}\Covmat(M)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and thus, for any matrix
N\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
, we have

\|M^{\dagger}N\|_{2}=\|G^{\dagger}\Covmat(M)^{-\frac{1}{2}}N\|_{2}\leq\|G^{\dagger}\|_{2}\|\Covmat(M)^{-\frac{1}{2}}N\|_{2}=\|G^{\dagger}\|_{2}\sqrt{\|N^{\top}\Covmat(M)^{\mathsf{-1}}N\|_{2}}.

Then, we take the expectation and apply
[
11
, Proposition 10.2]
to bound the quantity
\mathbb{E}\left[\|G^{\dagger}\|_{2}\right]
, which concludes the proof.

We now introduce a final lemma based on Lemmas
9
,
11
and

, which is central in the derivation of our error bounds in expectation.

Lemma 13
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}

, satisfying

, set
\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}

\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times p}

\Omega_{k}
is full row rank (i.e.
\rank(\Omega_{k})=k

.

If the covariance matrix
\Covmat_{k}(Z)
is non-singular, then, for any matrix
N\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
, one has

\mathbb{E}\left[\|T_{k}N\|_{2}\right]\leq\mathsf{c}^{\hskip 1.42271pt\text{tot}}_{2}(\Covmat(Z),N)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathsf{c}^{\hskip 1.42271pt\text{dep}}_{2}(\Covmat(Z),N)+\mathsf{c}_{2}(\Covmat(Z),N),

(3.17)

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\|T_{k}N\|_{F}^{2}\right]=\mathsf{c}^{\hskip 1.42271pt\text{tot}}_{F}(\Covmat(Z),N)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathsf{c}^{\hskip 1.42271pt\text{dep}}_{F}(\Covmat(Z),N)^{2}+\mathsf{c}_{F}(\Covmat(Z),N)^{2}.

(3.18)

The (positive) constants are defined as

\mathsf{c}^{\hskip 1.42271pt\text{dep}}_{2,F}(\Covmat(Z),N)=\|\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N\|_{2,F},

(3.19)

\displaystyle\mathsf{c}_{2}(\Covmat(Z),N)

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2}\|(N^{\top}[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}}{\sqrt{p-k-1}}

(3.20)

\displaystyle+\frac{e\sqrt{p}}{p-k}\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\|(N^{\top}[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2},

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{c}_{F}(\Covmat(Z),N)=\frac{\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\|(N^{\top}[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}}{\sqrt{p-k-1}},

(3.21)

where
\Covmat_{k}(Z)=U_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)U_{k}
,
\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)U_{k}
,
\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}
and
\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)=\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)-\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)\left[\Covmat_{k}(Z)\right]^{\mathsf{-1}}\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)^{\top}
.

Proof.

In the spectral norm, Lemma
11
gives, for any matrix
N\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{2}\mid\Omega_{k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbb{E}\left[\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right]\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{2}+\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2}\|\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{F}

(3.22)

\displaystyle+\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\|\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{2}.

From Lemma
9
, one has for any matrix
N\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right]\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N=\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}\Omega_{k}\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N=\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N,

which is a deterministic constant. Hence, taking the total expectation of (
3.22

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|T_{k}N\|_{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Omega_{\perp}\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{2}\mid\Omega_{k}\right]\right],

\displaystyle\leq\|\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N\|_{2}+\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{F}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{2}\right].

Finally, by using Lemma
12
, one has

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{F}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{F}^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\|(N^{\top}[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{F}}{\sqrt{p-k-1}}

and

\mathbb{E}\left[\|\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}N\|_{2}\right]\leq~\frac{e\sqrt{p}}{p-k}\|(N^{\top}[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}N)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2}.

Remark 14
.

We note that both
\mathsf{c}^{\hskip 1.42271pt\text{dep}}_{2}(\Covmat(Z),N)
and
\mathsf{c}^{\hskip 1.42271pt\text{dep}}_{F}(\Covmat(Z),N)
do depend on
\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)
, which is the term related to the statistical dependence between
\Omega_{k}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}
. Those two terms cancel out whenever
\Omega_{k}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}
are independent. By contrast,
\mathsf{c}_{2}(\Covmat(Z),N)
and

will not cancel out if
\Omega_{k}
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}
are independent, but do approach zero as the number of samples

increases.

3.2.2

We are now able to provide a first key result. Given
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{Z},\Covmat(Z))
, we aim at bounding (in expectation) the quantity
\|S_{k}\|_{2,F}
, where

T_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\Omega_{k}^{\dagger}

represent the sine of the canonical angles between
\range{(Z\Omega_{k}^{\dagger})}
and
\range{(U_{k})}

[

where the analysis is based on the tangent of the canonical angles (via the matrix

Proposition 15
.

(resp.

(resp.

Proposition 16
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
such that
n\geq m
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
a Gaussian matrix of mean
\widehat{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
and covariance matrix
\Covmat(Z)
, that is,
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(\widehat{Z},\Covmat(Z))
, satisfying
2<p\leq\min\mathinner{\left\{\rank(A),\,\rank(\Covmat(Z))\right\}}
. Let
\pi(Z)
and
\pi(Z-\widehat{Z})
denote the orthogonal projections onto the vector spaces spanned by the columns of
Z
and
Z-\widehat{Z}
, respectively.

Then, for any
k\in\mathinner{\left\{1,\dots,p-2\right\}}
, one has

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2,F}-\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2,F}\right]\leq\frac{e\sqrt{r}}{r-p}\frac{\|\widehat{Z}\|_{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{r}}}\|A_{k}\|_{2,F}+\mathbb{E}\left[\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z-\widehat{Z})]A_{k}\|_{2,F}\right],

where
r
denotes the rank of
\Covmat(Z)
and
\lambda_{r}
the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of
\Covmat(Z)
.

Proof.

First, we begin with the trivial observation that

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2,F}-\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2,F}\leq\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{2,F}.

Let us define
W=Z-\widehat{Z}
, which is a centered Gaussian matrix with covariance matrix
\Covmat(W)=\Covmat(Z)
. It yields

\displaystyle\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{2,F}

\displaystyle=\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(W)+\pi(W)-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{2,F},

\displaystyle=\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(W)]A_{k}+[\pi(W)-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{2,F},

\displaystyle\leq\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(W)]A_{k}\|_{2,F}+\|[\pi(W)-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{2,F},

\displaystyle\leq\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(W)]A_{k}\|_{2,F}+\|[\pi(W)-\pi(Z)]\|_{2}\|A_{k}\|_{2,F},

\displaystyle=\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(W)]A_{k}\|_{2,F}+\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\pi(W)\|_{2}\|A_{k}\|_{2,F},

where the last inequality follows from
[
4
, Lemma 1.5]
. Then, adapting the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
, we observe that

\displaystyle[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\pi(W)

\displaystyle=[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]WW^{\dagger},

\displaystyle=[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)](W-Z+Z)W^{\dagger},

\displaystyle=[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)](W-Z)W^{\dagger}+[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]ZW^{\dagger},

\displaystyle=[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)](W-Z)W^{\dagger}.

Therefore, taking the spectral norm and using the submultiplicativity yields

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]\pi(W)\|_{2}\leq\|W-Z\|_{2}\|W^{\dagger}\|_{2}=\|\widehat{Z}\|_{2}\|W^{\dagger}\|_{2}.

Overall, we have

\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A_{k}\|_{2,F}\leq\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(W)]A_{k}\|_{2,F}+\|\widehat{Z}\|_{2}\|W^{\dagger}\|_{2}\|A_{k}\|_{2,F}.

Since
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is full column rank, one has
W^{\dagger}=(W^{\top}W)^{\mathsf{-1}}W^{\top}
and due to the unitarily invariance of the spectral norm,

\|W^{\dagger}\|_{2}=\|(W^{\top}W)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2}=\sqrt{\|(W^{\top}W)^{\mathsf{-1}}\|_{2}}.

Given
r=\rank(\Covmat{Z})
, the covariance matrix
\Covmat(Z)
has the following thin eigendecomposition,
\Covmat(Z)=Q_{r}\Lambda_{r}Q_{r}^{\top}

where
Q_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}
is an orthogonal matrix containing the first
\lambda_{r}
eigenvectors of
\Covmat(Z)
and
\Lambda_{r}=\diag(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
contains the corresponding nonzero eigenvalues sorted in decreasing order, i.e.
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{r}>0
. Then, using (
2.2
), one has
W=\Covmat(Z)^{\frac{1}{2}}G
with
G\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
such that
G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{I}_{n})
. Thus,

W^{\top}W=G^{\top}\Covmat(Z)G=G^{\top}Q_{r}\Lambda_{r}Q_{r}^{\top}G=Y_{r}^{\top}\Lambda_{r}Y_{r}\succcurlyeq\lambda_{r}Y_{r}^{\top}Y_{r},

where
Y_{r}=Q_{r}^{\top}G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{I}_{r})
.
Then,
(W^{\top}W)^{\mathsf{-1}}\preccurlyeq\lambda_{r}^{\mathsf{-1}}(Y_{r}^{\top}Y_{r})^{\mathsf{-1}}
leading to

\|W^{\dagger}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{r}}}\sqrt{\|(Y_{r}^{\top}Y_{r})^{\mathsf{-1}}\|_{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{r}}}\|Y_{r}^{\dagger}\|_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{r}}}\|(Y_{r}^{\top})^{\dagger}\|_{2},

with
Y_{r}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}
(with
p\leq r
by assumption) and
Y_{r}^{\top}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{I}_{p})
. We then take the expectation and apply Lemma
12
(with
N={I}_{r}
) to deduce an upper bound of
\mathbb{E}\left[\|(Y_{r}^{\top})^{\dagger}\|_{2}\right]
, which completes the proof.

\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times p}
,
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}(Z-\widehat{Z})\in\mathbb{R}^{(n-k)\times p}
such that
\Omega_{k}
is full row rank (i.e.
\rank(\Omega_{k})=k
). Let
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x\mapsto x/\sqrt{1+x^{2}}
for
x\geq 0
.

If the covariance matrix
\Covmat_{k}(Z)
is non-singular, then, one has

\mathbb{E}\left[\|[{I}_{n}-\pi(Z)]A\|_{2}\right]-\|\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{A}_{k}\|_{2}\leq\frac{e\sqrt{r}}{r-p}\frac{\|\widehat{Z}\|_{2}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{r}}}\|A_{k}\|_{2}+\min\mathinner{\left\{\widehat{c}_{k},~\varphi(\widehat{d}_{k})\|\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\|_{2}\right\}},

where
\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}=\left(\Sigma_{k}^{2}-\sigma_{k+1}^{2}{I}_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
,

denotes the rank of
\Covmat(Z)
,
\lambda_{r}
the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of
\Covmat(Z)
and

\displaystyle\widehat{c}_{k}

\displaystyle=\|\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}\|_{2}+\frac{\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2}\|(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}^{\top}[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}}{\sqrt{p-k-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{e\sqrt{p}}{{p-k}}\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\|(\widehat{\Sigma}_{k}^{\top}[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}\widehat{\Sigma}_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\widehat{d}_{k}

\displaystyle=\|\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{\mathsf{-1}}\|_{2}+\frac{\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2}\|[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}}{\sqrt{p-k-1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{e\sqrt{p}}{{p-k}}\|\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{F}\|[\Covmat_{k}(Z)]^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{2},

with
\Covmat_{k}(Z)=U_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)U_{k}
,
\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)U_{k}
and
\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}\Covmat(Z)\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}

.

Remark 21
.

Since

and due to the partial ordering property, we deduce that

4
Application to the Randomized Singular Value Decomposition

As an illustration, we consider the Randomized Singular Value Decomposition (RSVD), a popular algorithm for obtaining a low-rank approximation to a given matrix
[
11
,
16
]
. In this method,

is constructed to approximate the

dominant left singular vectors of

. Given

and

, the Randomized Singular Value Decomposition considers
Z=A_{q}G
. In the single-pass setting (

), error bounds have been notably provided in
[
11
,
12
]
for the spectral norm. For

, we provide error bounds which, to the best of our knowledge, are new. As detailed next, the relation

leads to significant simplifications in the expressions of the error bounds given in Theorems
17
,
19
and
20
, respectively. Deriving these error bounds implies to first express the projected covariance matrices defined in (

). This is the main purpose of the next lemma.

Lemma 22
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
such that
n\geq m
and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
with
2<p\leq\rank(A)
such that

with
A_{q}=(AA^{\top})^{q}A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
(
q\in\mathbb{N}
) and
G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}
a standard Gaussian matrix (
G\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{I}_{m})
). For a given integer

, set
\Omega_{k}=U_{k}^{\top}Z
and
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}=\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{U}_{k}^{\top}Z
. The projected covariance matrices are then given by

\displaystyle\Covmat_{k}(Z)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{k}^{4q+2},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)

\displaystyle=(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}^{\top})^{2q+1}.

Then the random matrix
\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}
conditioned by

follows a Gaussian distribution of mean

\mathbb{E}\left[\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right]=0,

(4.2)

and of covariance matrix

\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)=(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}^{\top})^{2q+1}.

(4.3)

Proof.

A straightforward calculation gives

\Omega_{k}=\Sigma_{k}^{2q+1}V_{k}^{\top}G
. Hence
\Omega_{k}
has full row rank with probability one

6
]
. Since

\Covmat(Z)=A_{q}A_{q}^{\top}
can be expressed as

\Covmat(Z)=U(\Sigma\Sigma^{\top})^{2q+1}U^{T}.

Thus
\Covmat(Z)
and

have the same rank and we deduce

\displaystyle\Covmat_{k}(Z)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{k}^{4q+2},

\displaystyle\Covmat_{\perp,k}(Z)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\underaccent{~\rule[-2.25pt]{10.5486pt}{0.5pt}}{\Covmat}_{k}(Z)

\displaystyle=(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}^{\top})^{2q+1}.

We apply relations (
3.13
) and (

) of Lemma

to deduce

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\Covmat\left(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Omega}_{k}\mid\Omega_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=(\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}\underaccent{\rule[-2.25pt]{3.76735pt}{0.5pt}}{\Sigma}_{k}^{\top})^{2q+1},

which concludes the proof.

Remark 23

From Lemma

is non-singular, allowing us to apply Theorems

6
Conclusions

We have analyzed theoretically the low-rank approximation to a given matrix in both the spectral and Frobenius norms. First, we have derived in Theorems
4
and
8
deterministic error bounds that hold with some minimal assumptions. Second, we have derived error bounds in expectation in the non-standard Gaussian case, assuming a non-trivial mean and a general covariance matrix for the random matrix variable (Theorems
17
,
19
and
20
). This analysis generalizes and improves the error bounds proposed in
[
11
]
. Then, we have applied our analysis to the Randomized Singular Value Decomposition and have deduced the related error bounds in expectation (Corollaries
24
,
26
and
27
). Numerical experiments on a synthetic test case have shown the tightness of the new error bounds.

In a near future, we plan to derive the probabilistic error bounds related to the error analysis proposed in Section
3
and specialize them to the case of algorithms based on randomized subspace iterations. More specifically, we aim at extending our analysis to the Generalized Singular Value Decomposition
[
19
,
24
]
and to the generalized Nyström method
[
18
]
, due to possible applications in weighted inverse problems, uncertainty quantification and model reduction to name a few. In all these applications, exploiting prior information on the singular vectors of
A
to define the covariance matrix
\Covmat(Z)
is expected to be beneficial for the accuracy of the low-rank approximation.

Higher Order Fractal Differential Equations
Alireza Khalili Golmankhaneh, Claude Depollier, Diana Pham

Abstract
This paper provides a summary of the fractal calculus framework. It presents higher-order homogeneous and nonhomogeneous linear fractal differential equations with α \alpha -order. Solutions for these equations with constant coefficients are obtained through the method of variation of parameters and the method of undetermined coefficients. The solution space for higher α \alpha -order linear fractal differential equations is defined, showcasing its non-integer dimensionality. The solutions to α \alpha -order linear fractal differential equations are graphically depicted to illustrate their non-differentiability. Additionally, equations of motion governing the behavior of two masses in fractal time are proposed and solved.

7.1

Consider an
n^{th}

\alpha

L[f]=a_{0}f^{n\alpha}(x)+a_{1}f^{(n-1)\alpha}(x)+...+a_{n-1}f^{\alpha}(x)+a_{n}f(x)=g(x),

(34)

If
g(x)
can be expressed as a sum of polynomials, exponentials, sines, cosines, or products of such functions, the method of undetermined coefficients is employed. To find a particular solution
F(x)
, a suitable combination of these functions is chosen.

7.2
The Method of Variation of Parameters

The variation of parameters method for determining a particular solution of a nonhomogeneous
n^{th}

\alpha
-order fractal linear differential equation is as follows. Consider the equation:

L[f]=f^{\alpha n}(x)+p_{1}(x)f^{\alpha(n-1)}(x)+...+p_{n-1}(x)f^{\alpha}(x)+p_{n}(x)f(x)=g(x),

(40)

Suppose that
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}
are fundamental solutions of the homogeneous part of Eq.(
40
). The general solution is then expressed as:

f_{c}(x)=c_{1}f_{1}(x)+c_{2}f_{2}(x)+...+c_{n}f_{n}(x).

(41)

To find a particular solution
F(x)
of Eq.(
40
), assume:

F(x)=u_{1}(x)y_{1}(x)+u_{2}(x)y_{2}(x)+...+u_{n}(x)y_{n}(x)

(42)

Now, find the functions
u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{n}
. By taking the fractal derivative of Eq.(
42
), we get:

\displaystyle F^{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=(u_{1}(x)f_{1}^{\alpha}(x)+u_{2}(x)f_{2}^{\alpha}(x)+...+u_{n}(x)f_{n}^{\alpha}(x))

\displaystyle+(u_{1}^{\alpha}(x)f_{1}(x)+u_{2}^{\alpha}(x)f_{2}(x)+...+u_{n}^{\alpha}(x)f_{n}(x))

(43)

The first condition to find
u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{n}
is:

u_{1}^{\alpha}(x)f_{1}(x)+u_{2}^{\alpha}(x)f_{2}(x)+...+u_{n}^{\alpha}(x)f_{n}(x)=0

(44)

Continue this process by calculating successive fractal derivatives
F^{2\alpha},...,F^{\alpha(n-1)}
. After each
F^{\alpha}
-differentiation, set the sum of terms involving the fractal derivative of
u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{n}
equal to zero. This leads to
n-2

u_{1}^{\alpha}(x)f_{1}^{m\alpha}(x)+u_{2}^{\alpha}(x)f_{2}^{m\alpha}(x)+...+u_{n}^{\alpha}(x)f_{n}^{m\alpha}(x)=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ m=1,2,...,n-2.

(45)

As a result, the expression for
F^{2\alpha},...,F^{(n-1)\alpha}
reduces to:

F^{m\alpha}(x)=u_{1}(x)f_{1}^{m\alpha}(x)+u_{2}(x)f_{2}^{m\alpha}(x)+...+u_{n}(x)f_{n}^{m\alpha}(x),

(46)

Finally, impose the condition that
F
must be a solution of Eq.(
40
). The
n^{th}

\alpha
-order differential of
F
is:

\displaystyle F^{n\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle=(u_{1}(x)f_{1}^{n\alpha}(x)+u_{2}(x)f_{2}^{n\alpha}(x)+...+u_{n}(x)f_{n}^{n\alpha}(x))

\displaystyle+(u_{1}^{\alpha}(x)f_{1}^{(n-1)\alpha}(x)+u_{2}^{\alpha}(x)f_{2}^{(n-1)\alpha}(x)+...+u_{n}^{\alpha}(x)f_{n}^{(n-1)\alpha}(x))

(47)

Now, substitute
F(x)
and its fractal derivatives into Eq.(
40
), and group the terms involving each of the functions
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}
and their fractal derivatives. Most terms drop out because each
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}
satisfies
L[f_{i}]=0
for
i=1,2,...,n
. The remaining terms yield the relation:

u_{1}^{\alpha}(x)f_{1}^{(n-1)\alpha}(x)+u_{2}^{\alpha}(x)f_{2}^{(n-1)\alpha}(x)+...+u_{n}^{\alpha}(x)f_{n}^{(n-1)\alpha}(x)=g(x)

(48)

Eqs. (
48
), (
44
), and the
n-2
Eqs. (
45
) provide
n

u_{1}^{\alpha},u_{2}^{\alpha},...,u_{n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle f_{1}u_{1}^{\alpha}+f_{2}u_{2}^{\alpha}+...+f_{n}u_{n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle f_{1}^{\alpha}u_{1}^{\alpha}+f_{2}^{\alpha}u_{2}^{\alpha}+...+f_{n}^{\alpha}u_{n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle f_{1}^{2\alpha}u_{1}^{\alpha}+f_{2}^{2\alpha}u_{2}^{\alpha}+...+f_{n}^{2\alpha}u_{n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle f_{1}^{(n-1)\alpha}u_{1}^{\alpha}+f_{2}^{(n-1)\alpha}u_{2}^{\alpha}+...+f_{n}^{(n-1)\alpha}u_{n}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=g

(49)

Using Cramer’s rule, solve the system of equations (
49

u_{m}^{\alpha}=\frac{g(x)W_{m}(x)}{W(x)},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ m=1,2,...,n,

(50)

Here,

m^{th}

(0,0,...,0,1)

(51)

where

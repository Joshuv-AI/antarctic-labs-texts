On the energy and helicity conservation of the incompressible Euler equations
Yanqing Wang, Wei Wei, Gang Wu and Yulin Ye

Abstract
In this paper, we are concerned with the minimal regularity of weak solutions implying the law of balance for both energy and helicity in the incompressible Euler equations. In the spirit of recent works due to Berselli [ 5 ] and Berselli-Georgiadis [ 6 ] , it is shown that the energy of weak solutions is invariant if v ∈ L p ​ ( 0 , T , B 2 ​ p p − 1 , c ⁡ ( ℕ ) 1 p ) v\in L^{p}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{\frac{2p}{p-1},c(\mathbb{N})}) with 1 < p ≤ 3 1<p\leq 3 and the helicity is conserved if v ∈ L p ​ ( 0 , T , B 2 ​ p p − 1 , c ⁡ ( ℕ ) 2 p ) v\in L^{p}(0,T;B^{\frac{2}{p}}_{\frac{2p}{p-1},c(\mathbb{N})}) with 2 < p ≤ 3 2<p\leq 3 for both the periodic domain and the whole space, which generalizes the classical work of Cheskidov-Constantin-Friedlander-Shvydkoy in [ 10 ] . This indicates the role of the time integrability, spatial integrability and differential regularity of the velocity in the conserved quantities of weak solutions of the ideal fluid.

Introduction

The evolution of homogeneous inviscid incompressible flows on
\Omega
is described by the following Euler equations

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla\Pi=0,\\
&\text{div}\,v=0,\\
&v|_{t=0}=v_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is either the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
d=2,3

The unknown vector

is velocity field and
\Pi
represents the pressure.
The
initial velocity
v_{0}
satisfies
\text{div}\,v_{0}=0

\omega=\text{curl\,}v
of the velocity field is determined by

\omega_{t}+v\cdot\nabla\omega-\omega\cdot\nabla v=0,~~\text{div\,}\omega=0.

(1.2)

As we know, there exist only two conserved quantities of second order in the Euler equations: kinetic energy conservation

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|v(x,t)|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|v(x,0)|^{2}dx

(1.3)

and helicity preservation

\int_{\Omega}\omega(x,t)\cdot v(x,t)dx=\int_{\Omega}\omega(x,0)\cdot v(x,0)dx.

Sobolev spaces:
First, we introduce some notations used in this paper.
For

Besov spaces:
We denote
\mathcal{S}
the Schwartz class of rapidly decreasing functions,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
the
space of tempered distributions and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}
the quotient space of tempered distributions which modulo polynomials.
We use
\mathcal{F}f
or

to denote the Fourier transform of a tempered distribution
f
.
To define Besov spaces, we need the following dyadic unity partition(see e.g.
[
1
]
). Choose two nonnegative radial
functions
\varrho
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

supported respectively in the ball
\mathcal{B}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
and the shell
\mathcal{C}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
such that

\varrho(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d};\qquad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\neq 0.

Write
h=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi
and
\tilde{h}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varrho
, then nonhomogeneous dyadic blocks
\Delta_{j}
are defined by

\Delta_{j}u:=0~~\text{if}~~j\leq-2,~~\Delta_{-1}u:=\varrho(D)u=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(y)u(x-y)dy,

\text{and}~~\Delta_{j}u:=\varphi\left(2^{-j}D\right)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy~~\text{if}~~j\geq 0.

The nonhomogeneous low-frequency cut-off operator
S_{j}
is defined by

S_{j}u:=\sum_{k\leq j-1}\Delta_{k}u=\varrho(2^{-j}D)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy,~j\in\mathbb{N}\cup{0}.

Analogously, the homogeneous dyadic blocks
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
and homogeneous low-frequency cut-off operators
\dot{S}_{j}
are defined for
\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}
by

\dot{\Delta}_{j}u:=\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy,~j\in\mathbb{Z}

\text{ and }~~\dot{S}_{j}u:=\varrho(2^{-j}D)u=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy,~j\in\mathbb{Z}

Now we introduce the definition of Besov spaces. Let
(p,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2},s\in\mathbb{R}
, the nonhomogeneous Besov space

B_{p,r}^{s}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right);\|f\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}:=\left\|2^{js}\right\|\Delta_{j}f\left\|{}_{L^{p}}\right\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\right\}

and the homogeneous space

\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)/\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right);\|f\|_{\dot{B}_{p,r}^{s}}:=\left\|2^{js}\right\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\left\|{}_{L^{p}}\right\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty\right\}.

and

, we may write the

\left\|f\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}=\left\|f\right\|_{{L^{p}}}+\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}-2\Big[S_{N}(\omega\times v)-S_{N}\omega\times S_{N}v\Big]\cdot S_{N}\omega dx.

(4.2)

To control the right hand side of (
4.2
), we recall the identity

\nabla(A\cdot B)=A\cdot\nabla B+B\cdot\nabla A+A\times\text{curl}B+B\times\text{curl}A

which together with

\mathrm{div}\,\omega=0
yields that

\text{div}(v\otimes v)=\frac{1}{2}\nabla|v|^{2}+\omega\times v,

which gives

\omega\times v=\text{div}(v\otimes v)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|v|^{2}.

As a consequence, we deduce that

S_{N}(\omega\times v)=S_{N}(v\cdot\nabla v)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla S_{N}(|v|^{2})=\text{div}S_{N}(v\otimes v)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla S_{N}(|v|^{2})

and

S_{N}\omega\times S_{N}v=S_{N}v\cdot\nabla S_{N}v-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|S_{N}v|^{2}=\text{div}(S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|S_{N}v|^{2}.

Plugging the latter two equations into (
4.2
), we use integration by parts to obtain that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}S_{N}v\cdot S_{N}\omega dx

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}\text{div}\big(S_{N}(v\otimes v)-(S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v)\big)\cdot S_{N}\omega+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\left(|S_{N}v|^{2}-S_{N}(|v|^{2})\right)\cdot S_{N}\omega dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}\big(S_{N}(v\otimes v)-S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v\big):\nabla S_{N}\omega dx.

Making use of the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}\big(S_{N}(v\otimes v)-S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v\big):\nabla S_{N}\omega dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|S_{N}(v\otimes v)-S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|\nabla S_{N}\omega\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Thanks to the Constantin-E-Titi identity in
[
13
]
, we observe that

\displaystyle S_{N}(v\otimes v)-S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)[v_{i}(x-y)-v_{i}(x)][v_{j}(x-y)-v_{j}(x)]dy-(v_{i}-S_{N}v_{i})(v_{j}-S_{N}v_{j}),

where we used
2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)dy=\mathcal{F}(\tilde{h}(\cdot))|_{\xi=0}=1.

For
0\leq\theta\leq 2
, we deduce from the Minkowski inequality and
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
that,

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(v_{i}v_{j})-S_{N}v_{i}S_{N}v_{j}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|\|v(x-y)-v(x)\|^{\theta}_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v(x-y)-v\|^{2-\theta}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dy

\displaystyle+\|v_{i}-S_{N}v_{i}\|^{\theta}_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{j}-S_{N}v_{j}\|^{2-\theta}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|\|v(x-y)-v(x)\|^{\theta}_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dy+\|v_{i}-S_{N}v_{i}\|^{\theta}_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II.

On the one hand, applying the Bernstein inequality in Lemma
2.1
, we observe that

\displaystyle\|v(x-y)-v(x)\|_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\Big[\sum_{j<N}2^{j}|y|\|{\Delta}_{j}v\|_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\sum_{j\geq N}\|{\Delta}_{j}v\|_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big].

(4.4)

Before going further, we write

\displaystyle\Gamma(j)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &2^{j\alpha},~~~~~~~~\text{if}~j\leq 0,\\
&2^{-(1-\alpha)j},~~\text{if}~j>0,\end{aligned}\right.

and
d_{j}=2^{j\alpha}\|{\Delta}_{j}v\|_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
.

Then we can rewrite (
4.4
) as

\displaystyle\|v(x-y)-v(x)\|_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(2^{N(1-\alpha)}|y|\sum_{j<N}2^{-(N-j)(1-\alpha)}2^{j\alpha}\|{\Delta}_{j}v\|_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+2^{-\alpha N}\sum_{j\geq N}2^{(N-j)\alpha}2^{j\alpha}\|{\Delta}_{j}v\|_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(2^{N}|y|+1)2^{-\alpha N}\left(\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right)(N),

which turns out that

\displaystyle\|v(x-y)-v(x)\|^{\theta}_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq

\displaystyle C(2^{N}|y|+1)^{\theta}2^{-\alpha\theta N}\left(\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right)^{\theta}(N).

Some straightforward calculations yield that

\sup_{N}2^{3N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\tilde{h}(2^{N}y)|(2^{N}|y|+1)^{\theta}dy<\infty,

which means that

I\leq C2^{-\alpha\theta N}\left(\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right)^{\theta}(N).

On the other hand, notice that

|II|\leq\|v-{S}_{N}v\|^{\theta}_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\Big(\sum_{j\geq N}\|{\Delta}_{j}v\|_{L^{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big)^{\theta}\leq C2^{-\alpha\theta N}\left(\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right)^{\theta}(N),

where we used
N>0.

Therefore, we end up with,

\displaystyle\|S_{N}(v_{i}v_{j})-S_{N}v_{i}S_{N}v_{j}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C2^{-\theta\alpha N}\left(\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right)^{\theta}(N),

(4.5)

Owing to the Bernstein inequality, we conclude that

\|\nabla S_{N}\omega\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\sum_{j\leq N}2^{2j}\|{\Delta}_{j}v\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq 2^{N(2-\alpha)}\left(\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right)(N),

(4.6)

Combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we infer that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(S_{N}(v\otimes v)^{\varepsilon}-S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v\big):\nabla S_{N}\omega dx\Big|

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{(2-\alpha-\theta\alpha)N}\left(\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right)^{\theta}(N)\left(\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right)(N).

To proceed further, we pick
\alpha
such that
0<\alpha<1
and
2-\alpha-\theta\alpha=0
. Hence, we have
\Gamma\in l^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Letting
\alpha=\frac{2}{p}
and
q=\frac{2p}{p-1}
, it follows from (
4.7
)
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(S_{N}(v\otimes v)-S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v\big):\nabla S_{N}\omega dx\Big|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\left[\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right]^{\theta}(N)\left[\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right](N)\Big|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|^{\theta}_{L^{\frac{p\theta}{p-1}}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|^{\theta+1}_{L^{p}(0,T;B^{\alpha}_{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}<\infty,

where we used the facts that

\frac{p\theta}{p-1}=p
and
\frac{2q\theta}{q\theta-2}=q.

Invoking the
the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(S_{N}(v\otimes v)-S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v\big):\nabla S_{N}\omega dx\Big|dt

(4.8)

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\left[\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right]^{\theta}(N)\left[\Gamma\ast{d}_{j}\right](N)\Big|dt\rightarrow 0,~\text{as}\ N\rightarrow+\infty.

By integrating (
4.3
) in time over
(0,T)
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{N}v(x,T)\cdot S_{N}\omega(x,T)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{N}v(x,0)\cdot S_{N}\omega(x,0)dx

(4.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(S_{N}v\otimes S_{N}v-S_{N}(v\otimes v)\big):\nabla S_{N}\omega dxdt.

With (
4.8
) in hand, taking
N\rightarrow+\infty

5
Conclusion

A celebrated result in the study of Onsager conjecture is that

v\in L^{3}(0,T;B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
guarantees the energy conservation of the Euler equations on the whole space, which is shown by Cheskidov-Constantin-Friedlander-Shvydkoy in
[
10
]
. Inspired by recent works
[
6
,
5
]
of
Berselli and Berselli-Georgiadis, we extend it to a more general case
v\in L^{p}(0,T;B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{\frac{2p}{p-1},c(\mathbb{N})})
for
1\leq p\leq 3.
We provide two different methods to show it. The first is interpolation technique and the second one is Littlewood-Paley theory as
[
10
]
for the case
1<p\leq 3.
It seems that the first one is better than the second one. As a byproduct, a observation is that
the general case immediately implies energy conservation sufficient condition in terms of gradient in space
L^{p}(0,T;L^{\frac{6p}{5p-5}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
derived in
[
7
,
28
,
35
,
3
,
4
]
for the Navier-Stokes equations.
In addition, parallel to energy, the helicity conservation of weak solutions in inviscid flow is also considered. It seems that our argument can be applied to other fluid equations.

It is shown that the space
B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}
for the energy conservation are sharp in
[
10
]
. It is an interesting problem to construct an example to illustrate the sharpness of space
B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{\frac{2p}{p-1},c(\mathbb{N})}
for
1\leq p\leq 3.

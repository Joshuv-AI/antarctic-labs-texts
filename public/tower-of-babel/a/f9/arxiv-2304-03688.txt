Abstract
We establish a parametric framework for obtaining obstruction characterizations of graph parameters with respect to a quasi-ordering ⩽ \leqslant on graphs. At the center of this framework lies the concept of a ⩽ \leqslant -parametric graph : a non ⩽ \leqslant -decreasing sequence 𝒢 = ⟨ 𝒢 t ⟩ t ∈ ℕ \mathscr{G}=\langle\mathscr{G}_{t}\rangle_{t\in\mathbb{N}} of graphs indexed by non-negative integers. Parametric graphs allow us to define combinatorial objects that capture the approximate behaviour of graph parameters. A finite set 𝔊 \mathfrak{G} of ⩽ \leqslant -parametric graphs is a ⩽ \leqslant -universal obstruction for a parameter 𝗉 \mathsf{p} if there exists a function f : ℕ → ℕ f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N} such that, for every k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} and every graph G G , 1) if 𝗉 ⁡ ( G ) ≤ k {\sf p}(G)\leq k , then for every 𝒢 ∈ 𝔊 , \mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}, 𝒢 f ⁡ ( k ) ⩽̸ G \mathscr{G}_{f(k)}\not\leqslant G , and 2) if for every 𝒢 ∈ 𝔊 , \mathscr{G}\in\mathfrak{G}, 𝒢 k ⩽̸ G \mathscr{G}_{k}\not\leqslant G , then 𝗉 ⁡ ( G ) ≤ f ⁡ ( k ) . {\sf p}(G)\leq f(k). To solidify our point of view, we identify sufficient order-theoretic conditions that guarantee the existence of universal obstructions and in this case we examine algorithmic implications on the existence of fixed-parameter tractable algorithms. Our parametric framework has further implications related to finite obstruction characterizations of properties of graph classes. A ⩽ \leqslant -class property is defined as any set of ⩽ \leqslant -closed graph classes that is closed under set inclusion. By combining our parametric framework with established results from order theory, we derive a precise order-theoretic characterization that ensures ⩽ \leqslant -class properties can be described in terms of the exclusion of a finite set of ⩽ \leqslant -parametric graphs.

Well-quasi-orderings.

A quasi-ordering
(X,\leqslant)
is a
well-quasi-ordering (wqo)
if it is well-founded and
X
contains no infinite
\leqslant
-antichains.
A well-quasi-ordering is a
well-partial-ordering (wpo)
if it is also antisymmetric.

Dilworth’s theorem.

Now, let
(X,\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
We call the size of the largest
\leqslant
-antichain of
X
the
width
of
(X,\leqslant).

Moreover, we call any non-decreasing sequence
x_{1}\leqslant x_{2}\leqslant x_{3}\ldots
of elements of
X
a
\leqslant
-
chain
of
X.

A classic result in order theory by Dilworth relates infinite partial-orderings of fixed finite width with a partition of its universe into as many chains.

Theorem 2.1

(Dilworth’s Theorem)
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a partial-ordering.
Then
(X,\leqslant)
has finite width
w
if and only if it can be partitioned into
w
many
\leqslant
-chains.

Closed sets.

Next, let
(X,\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
Given a subset
A
of
X
we define the
upward
\leqslant
-closure
(resp.
downward
\leqslant
-closure
) of
A
in
X
as the set

{\uparrow}_{\leqslant}{A}\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid\exists y\in A:y\leqslant x\}
(resp.
{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{A}\coloneqq\{x\in X\mid\exists y\in A:x\leqslant y\}
).

When
A={\uparrow}_{\leqslant}{A}
(resp.
A={\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{A}
) we say that
A
is
upward

\leqslant
-
closed
(resp.
downward

\leqslant
-
closed
) in
X
.
We denote by
\mathsf{Up}_{\leqslant}(X)
(resp.
\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(X)
) the set of all upward
\leqslant
-closed (resp. downward
\leqslant
-closed) subsets of
X
.
The following proposition gives an equivalent characterization of a well-quasi-ordering by lifting the required condition to the set of all downward-closed sets being well-founded by the standard inclusion relation on sets.

Proposition 2.2

(​​
[
79
]
)
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
Then
(X,\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering if and only if
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(X),\subseteq)
is well-founded.

Bases of upward closed sets.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
Consider an upward
\leqslant
-closed subset
U\in\mathsf{Up}_{\leqslant}(X)
of
X.

A
\leqslant
-antichain
U_{0}
such that
U={\uparrow}_{\leqslant}{U_{0}}
is called a
\leqslant
-
basis
of
U
in
X
.
In the case that
(X,\leqslant)
is a partial-ordering, for every upward
\leqslant
-closed subset
U
of
X
that admits a
\leqslant
-basis
U_{0}
we have that
U_{0}={\sf min}_{\leqslant}(U).

If moreover
(X,\leqslant)
is well-founded, then every upward
\leqslant
-closed subset
U
of
X
admits a
\leqslant
-basis
U_{0}
and if moreover
(X,\leqslant)
is well-quasi-ordered,
U_{0}
is finite.

The finite representation of upward
\leqslant
-closed subsets via their
\leqslant
-bases is called the
finite basis property
of a well-quasi-ordering

(X,\leqslant).

This property can also serve as a finite representation of downward
\leqslant
-closed subsets in terms of excluding a finite
\leqslant
-basis of its complement: every downward
\leqslant
-closed subset
D\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(X)
of
X
is equal to
X\setminus{\uparrow}_{\leqslant}{U_{0}},
where
U_{0}
is a
\leqslant
-basis of the upward
\leqslant
-closed subset
X\setminus D.

Ideals.

There is another way to finitely represent downward
\leqslant
-closed sets in a well-quasi-ordering
(X,\leqslant).

A non-empty subset
A
of
X
is
\leqslant
-directed
if for every two elements
a
and
b
in
A
there exists an element
z
in
A
such that
x\leqslant z
and
y\leqslant z.

A downward
\leqslant
-closed subset of
X
that is
\leqslant
-directed is called a
\leqslant
-
ideal
of
X.

We write
{\sf Idl}_{\leqslant}(X)
for the set of all
\leqslant
-ideals of
X.

In well-quasi-ordering theory
\leqslant
-ideals are what we call
irreducible subsets
: for every
\leqslant
-ideal
I,
if
I\subseteq D_{1}\cup D_{2},
where both
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are downward
\leqslant
-closed subsets of
X,
then either
I\subseteq D_{1}
or
I\subseteq D_{2}.

Exploiting the irreducibility property of
\leqslant
-ideals one can show that in a well-quasi-ordering every downward
\leqslant
-closed set uniquely corresponds to a finite set of
\leqslant
-ideals as follows.

Proposition 2.3

(​​
[
79
]
)
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering.
Then every downward
\leqslant
-closed subset of
X
is equal to the union of a unique finite inclusion-antichain of
\leqslant
-ideals of
X.

In fact more is known.

Proposition 2.4

(Lemma 5.3,
[
44
]
)
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
Then, every downward
\leqslant
-closed subset of
X
is equal to the union of a finite set of
\leqslant
-ideals of
X
if and only if
(X,\leqslant)
contains no infinite
\leqslant
-antichains.

As a result the following two propositions hold.

Proposition 2.5

(​​
[
79
]
)
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a countable well-quasi-ordering.
Then
\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(X),

\mathsf{Up}_{\leqslant}(X)
and
{\sf Idl}_{\leqslant}(X)
are countable.

Proposition 2.6

(​​
[
79
]
)
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering.
Then
({\sf Idl}_{\leqslant}(X),\subseteq)
is a well-quasi-ordering if and only if
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(X),\subseteq)
is a well-quasi-ordering.

“Lifting” relations to powersets.

Finally we present a general mechanism which allows to “lift” a binary relation on
X
to its powerset, which we denote by
2^{X}.

Definition 2.7

(Smyth extension)
.

Let
\leqslant
be a binary relation on a set
X.

The
Smyth extension
of
\leqslant
on
2^{X}
, denoted by
\leqslant^{*}
, is defined as follows.
For every two subsets
A
and
B
of
X
:

\text{$A\leqslant^{*}B$ if and only if for every $b\in B$ there exists $a\in A$ such that $a\leqslant b$}.

One may easily observe that the Smyth extension of a quasi-ordering maintains being a quasi-ordering: given a quasi-ordering
(X,\leqslant),

(2^{X},\leqslant^{*})
is a quasi-ordering.
However, in the case that
(X,\leqslant)
is a partial-ordering,
(2^{X},\leqslant^{*})
may fail to be a partial-ordering.

The following proposition in order theory (see e.g.
[
59
]
) shows how the Smyth extension of a quasi-ordering relation translates to ordering downward
\leqslant
-closed subsets by inclusion.

Proposition 2.8

(​​
[
59
]
)
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
Then for every two subsets
A
and
B
of
X
:

A\leqslant^{*}B
if and only if
X\setminus{\uparrow}_{\leqslant}{A}\subseteq X\setminus{\uparrow}_{\leqslant}{B}.

\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-orderings.

Finally, one may wonder whether
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(X),\subseteq)
is a well-quasi-ordering, given a well-quasi-ordering
(X,\leqslant).

The answer to this question is negative as
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(X),\subseteq)
may indeed fail to be a well-quasi-ordering.
The classic counterexample was given by Rado.

Figure 1
:
An illustration of the Rado structure.

The
Rado structure
(see
Figure 1
) is defined as the pair

(\{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\mid i<j\},\leqslant_{\mathsf{r}}),

where

\text{$(i,j)\leqslant_{\mathsf{r}}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})$ if and only if ($i=i^{\prime}$ and $j\leq j^{\prime}$) or ($j<i^{\prime}$)}.

This leads to the definition of a “second-order lifting” of a well-quasi-ordering (see
[
43
,
58
]
).

Definition 2.9

(​​
[
43
]
)
.

A quasi-ordering
(X,\leqslant)
is an
\omega^{2}
-
well-quasi-ordering
if it is a well-quasi-ordering and does not contain an isomorphic copy of the Rado structure.

The following Theorem due to Jančar in
[
43
]
(see also the work of Marcone in
[
59
]
) gives a characterization of
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-orderings
(X,\leqslant)
in terms of the well-quasi-ordering status of subsets of
X
ordered by the Smyth extension of
\leqslant
.

Theorem 2.10

(​​
[
43
]
)
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
Then
(2^{X},\leqslant^{*})
is a well-quasi-ordering if and only if
(X,\leqslant)
is an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering.

Notice that
Subsection 2.1
allows for comparing downward
\leqslant
-closed subsets in terms of the Smyth extension on subsets.
This combined with
Theorem 2.10
gives the following corollary:

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
Then
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(X),\subseteq)
is a well-quasi-ordering if and only if
(X,\leqslant)
is an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering.

Moreover
Subsection 2.1
implies the following variant of
Subsection 2.1
:

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
(X,\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering.
Then
({\sf Idl}_{\leqslant}(X),\subseteq)
is a well-quasi-ordering if and only if
(X,\leqslant)
is an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering.

Graph theory

Sets and integers.

We denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of non-negative integers, by
\mathbb{N}_{\geq n},

n>1,
to be the set
\mathbb{N}\setminus\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid m<n\},

\mathbb{N}^{\mathsf{even}}
the set of even numbers in
\mathbb{N}.

Given two integers
p,q,

[p,q]
the set

For an integer

we set

For a set

we denote by

the set of all subsets of
S
and by
\binom{S}{2}
the set of all subsets of
S
of size
2.

If
\mathcal{S}
is a collection of objects where the operation

is defined, then we denote
{\boldsymbol{\bigcup}}S=\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{S}}X.

Also, given a function

by slightly abusing the notation, we always consider its extension
f\colon 2^{A}\to B
such that for every
X\subseteq A,

f(X)=\{f(a)\mid a\in X\}.

Graphs.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, throughout this paper we consider only finite, undirected, and simple graphs.
Formally, a
graph

G
is a pair
(V,E\subseteq{V\choose 2})
where
V
is the finite set of its
vertices
and
E
is the set of its
edges
.
We use
V(G)
(resp.
E(G)
) to denote the
vertex set
(resp. the
edge set
) of a graph
G
.
A graph
G
is not allowed to have
loops
, i.e, for every vertex
u
of
G
, the edge
\{u,u\}\notin E(G)
.
The
order
of
G
, denoted by
|G|
is defined as
|V(G)|
.

If
H
is a
subgraph
of
G,
that is
V(H)\subseteq V(G)
and
E(H)\subseteq E(G)
, we denote this by
H\subseteq G.

Given a vertex
v\in V(G),
we denote by
N_{G}(v)
the set of vertices of
G
that are adjacent to
v
in
G.

Given an edge
e=\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, the
contraction
of
e
results in a graph obtained from
G\setminus\{u,v\}
by adding a new vertex
w
adjacent to all vertices incident to
u
or
v
in
G
that is not
u
or
v
.
A graph
H
is a
minor
of a graph
G
if
H
can be obtained from a subgraph of
G
after contracting edges.
We denote this relation by
H\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}G
.

Throughout this paper, whenever we use the symbol
\leqslant,
we shall refer to a well-founded quasi-ordering relation on the set of all graphs, denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}
.
For the sake of simplicity we work with the canonical partial-ordering corresponding to
\leqslant
, i.e. for each equivalence class of
\equiv_{\leqslant}
, we arbitrarily choose a representative graph and (re)define
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}
to be the set of all chosen representatives.
Then,
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-founded partial-ordering.

Additionally, as we shall see, our results on universal obstructions apply to any arbitrary well-founded quasi-ordering relation on
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

However, we shall we use the minor relation throughout the presentation of our results in an attempt to enhance the readability of this work.

Graph classes.

Respecting the order theoretic terminology we introduced in the previous subsection, we define a
\leqslant
-
closed
class to be any set
\mathcal{G}
of graphs that belongs to
\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}).

Additionally, for every
\leqslant
-closed class
\mathcal{G}
we define the
\leqslant
-
obstruction set
of
\mathcal{G},
denoted by
{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G})
to be the set
{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\setminus\mathcal{G}).

Note that
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\setminus\mathcal{G}
belongs to
\mathsf{Up}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})
and that
{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G})
is the (unique)
\leqslant
-basis of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\setminus\mathcal{G}.

Moreover,
{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G})
is a
\leqslant
-antichain.

For a set of graphs
\mathcal{Z}
, we define the class of
\mathcal{Z}
-
\leqslant
-
free graphs
as
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H}):=\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\setminus{\uparrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathcal{H}}
.
Note that
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{Z})=\{G\in\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\mid\forall Z\in\mathcal{Z}:Z\nleqslant G\}
and that
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})
is
\leqslant
-closed.
Also note that
\mathcal{G}={\sf excl}_{\leqslant}({\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}))
.

The following proposition is a restatement of
Subsection 2.1
in the terminology introduced in this paragraph.
It gives us a way to test whether a
\leqslant
-closed class class is a subclass of another in terms of the Smyth extension of
\leqslant
on their
\leqslant
-obstruction sets.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be a quasi-ordering.
Then for every two sets of graphs
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{H}\leqslant^{*}\mathcal{F}
if and only if
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{H})\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{F}).

As already stated in the introduction, according to the Robertson-Seymour theorem
[
75
]
,
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}
is well-quasi-ordered by the minor relation.
One of the main consequences of this theorem is that it directly implies that for every
\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}),

{\sf obs}_{\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}}(\mathcal{G})
is a finite set.

3
The parametric framework

As we discussed in the introduction, our main motivation is the formalization of a theory of finite obstructions for “second order” properties of graph classes as well as the introduction of a universal framework for dealing with constructive proofs of obtaining such finite characterizations of individual properties of interest.

3.1
Class properties

With this target in mind we commence our story with the introduction of class properties.

For the purposes of the definitions that follow, we furthermore assume that
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering.
As implied by
Subsection 2.1
, the former statement is equivalent to
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}),\subseteq)
being well-founded.
We first define
\leqslant
-
class properties
.

Definition 3.1

(Class property)
.

A
\leqslant
-
class property
is any
subset of
\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})
that is inclusion-closed.

To illustrate the aforementioned definitions, let us introduce a relatively simple family of
\leqslant
-class properties as a running example.
For every
\leqslant
-closed class
\mathcal{G},
we define the
\leqslant
-class property
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G})
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G})\ \coloneqq\ \{\mathcal{H}\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\mid\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\}.

Class obstruction sets.

Next, we define the
\leqslant
-
class obstruction set
of a
\leqslant
-class property.

Definition 3.2

(Class obstruction set)
.

For every
\leqslant
-class property
\mathbb{CP},
the
\leqslant
-

of
\mathbb{CP}
, denoted by
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP}),

{\sf min}_{\subseteq}(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\setminus\mathbb{CP}).

Note that, for every
\leqslant
-class property
\mathbb{CP},
the set
\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\setminus\mathbb{CP}
is an upward inclusion-closed subset of
\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})
and that
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP})
is the (unique) inclusion-basis of
\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\setminus\mathbb{CP},
which by the former assumption exists for every
\leqslant
-class property
\mathbb{CP}.

Additionally, by definition, a
\leq
-closed class
\mathcal{G}
belongs to
\mathbb{CP}

\leqslant

\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP})

for every
\leqslant

\mathbb{CP}
.
Also, by minimality,

One may quickly observe that for every

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}))\ \coloneqq\ \{{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\{Z\}}\mid Z\in{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G})\}.

Moreover, by

, we have the following observation on the cardinality of the

.

Let

.

Let

Definition 3.5

(Directed

A
\leqslant

\mathbb{CP}

if for every two
\leqslant

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{H}

\leqslant

\mathcal{F}

Directed

\leqslant

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering.
Then, a
\leqslant
-class property
\mathbb{CP}
is directed if and only if
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP})
is an inclusion-antichain of
\leqslant
-ideals of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

Proof.

We prove the forward direction by proving the contrapositive.
Assume that there exists
\mathcal{Z}\in\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP})
that is not a
\leqslant
-ideal of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

Then, by
Subsection 2.1
, there exists a unique finite set
\{\mathcal{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{G}_{z}\},

z\in\mathbb{N},
of
\leqslant
-closed classes such that
\mathcal{Z}=\bigcup_{i\in[z]}\mathcal{G}_{i}.

Moreover, since
\mathcal{Z}
is not a
\leqslant
-ideal of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},
every
\mathcal{G}_{i}
is a proper subclass of
\mathcal{Z}.

Also, since
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP})
is an inclusion-antichain, we have that
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}\not\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{i},
for every
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}\in\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP})
and every

Now, since
\mathbb{CP}
is directed there must exist a
\leqslant
-closed class
\mathcal{H}\in\mathbb{CP}
such that
\bigcup_{i\in[z]}\mathcal{G}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{H},
which leads to a contradiction.

For the reverse direction, let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
be two
\leqslant
-closed classes in
\mathbb{CP}.

This implies that
\mathcal{Z}\not\subseteq\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{Z}\not\subseteq\mathcal{G},
for every
\leqslant
-ideal
\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}
in
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP}).

We claim that
\mathcal{Z}\not\subseteq\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{G},
for every
\mathcal{Z}\in\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathbb{CP}).

Indeed, since every such
\mathcal{Z}
is irreducible, if
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{H}\cup\mathcal{G},
then either
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
or
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq\mathcal{G},
which contradicts our assumptions.

,
Subsection 2.1
, and

.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)

is an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering;

2.

the

Proof.

The equivalency between
1.
and

3.2
Graph parameters

As we shall see in the coming sections, under certain order theoretic assumptions, graph parameters play a vital role in the quest for a unifying framework for characterizations of class properties.
The goal of this subsection is to introduce graph parameters and set the groundwork for the study of the approximate behaviour.

For the purposes of formalizing the theory of universal obstructions for graph parameters, we consider a slight extension of the definition of a graph parameter we gave in the introduction, where we allow for the parameter to be
infinite
for certain inputs. In this setting:

Definition 3.8

(Graph parameter)
.

A
graph parameter
is a function mapping graphs to non-negative integers and infinity, i.e.
{\sf p}\colon\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\infty}.

For a graph parameter
{\sf p},
we define its
domain
, denoted by
\textsf{dom}({\sf p})
, to be the set
\{G\in\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\mid{\sf p}(G)\in\mathbb{N}\}.

We say that a graph parameter
{\sf p}
is
\leqslant
-
monotone
if for every two graphs
H
and
G,

H\leqslant G
implies that
{\sf p}(H)\leq{\sf p}(G).

For every
k\in\mathbb{N},
we define the
class of graphs of
{\sf p}
at most
k
, denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k}
, as the class
\{G\in\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\mid{\sf p}(G)\leq k\}.

Note that whenever
{\sf p}
is
\leqslant
-monotone,
\textsf{dom}({\sf p})
is a
\leqslant
-closed class and so is
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k},
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

In order to compare graph parameters and discuss their (approximate) relative behavior, we introduce a quasi-ordering relation
\preceq
on the set of all graph parameters.
Let
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
be two graph parameters.
We write
{\sf p}\preceq{\sf p}^{\prime}
if
\textsf{dom}({\sf p}^{\prime})\subseteq\textsf{dom}({\sf p})
and there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, called the
gap function
of the relation, such that for every graph
G\in\textsf{dom}({\sf p}^{\prime}),
it holds that
{\sf p}(G)\leq f({\sf p}^{\prime}(G)).

One can easily verify that
\preceq
is a quasi-ordering on the set of graph parameters.
Moreover, we use
\sim
to denote the equivalence relation
\equiv_{\preceq}
induced by
\preceq
: we say that
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
are
equivalent
with gap function
f
, denoted by
{\sf p}\sim{\sf p}^{\prime},
if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
{\sf p}\preceq{\sf p}^{\prime}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}\preceq{\sf p}
both with gap function
f
.
One may notice that even if we allow the gap function
f
above to differ for the two relations, the definition of equivalence remains the same.

The following lemma gives an equivalent way to approximately compare parameters in terms of their classes of graphs of parameter at most some non-negative integer.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
be
\leqslant
-monotone parameters.
Then,
{\sf p}\preceq{\sf p}^{\prime}
if and only if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},f(k)},
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Assume that
{\sf p}\preceq{\sf p}^{\prime}.

Then
\textsf{dom}({\sf p}^{\prime})\subseteq\textsf{dom}({\sf p})
and there exists
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that for every
G\in\textsf{dom}({\sf p}^{\prime}),

{\sf p}(G)\leq f({\sf p}^{\prime}(G)).

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
G\in\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k}.

Equivalently
{\sf p}^{\prime}(G)\leq k
and thus
{\sf p}(G)\leq f(k),
which implies
G\in\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},f(k)}.

For the reverse direction, assume that there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},f(k)},
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Clearly
\textsf{dom}({\sf p}^{\prime})\subseteq\textsf{dom}({\sf p}).

Let
G\in\textsf{dom}({\sf p}^{\prime})
such that
{\sf p}^{\prime}(G)=k.

Then
{\sf p}(G)\leq f(k).

Equivalently,
{\sf p}(G)\leq f({\sf p}^{\prime}(G)).

3.3
Parametric graphs

Having introduces graph parameters in the previous sections, we move on to the second key notion of our parametric viewpoint.
In this subsection we delve into the realm of parametric graphs.

A
graph sequence

\mathscr{H}
is a countable sequence of graphs indexed by non-negative integers, denoted by
\mathscr{H}:=\langle\mathscr{H}_{i}\rangle_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
In some cases, instead of a sequence, we treat
\mathscr{H}
as a set of graphs.
When this is the case we say that

of
\mathscr{H}

Note that in a sequence
\mathscr{H}
the same graph is allowed to repeat for distinct indices, but the set of
\mathscr{H}
is not a
multiset
, i.e. only a single copy per distinct graph is contained in the set of
\mathscr{H}
.
Moreover, while
\mathscr{H}
is infinite, the set of
\mathscr{H}
may be finite.
We define the
\leqslant
-
width
of a graph sequence, denoted by
{\sf w}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}),
as the width of the partial-ordering
(\mathscr{H},\leqslant)
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N},
we write
\mathscr{H}_{\geq k}
for the sequence
\langle\mathscr{H}_{i}\rangle_{i\geq k}
, i.e., for the sequence obtained from
\mathscr{H}
after removing all graphs with index smaller that
k.

We call a graph sequence
\leqslant
-
rational
if
{\sf w}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H})\in\mathbb{N},

\leqslant
-
prime
if
{\sf w}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H})=1
, and
\leqslant
-
increasing
if for
i\leq j,

\mathscr{H}_{i}\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{j}.

With this we are now ready to define parametric graphs.

Definition 3.10

(Parametric graph)
.

Any
\leqslant
-increasing graph sequence is a
\leqslant
-
parametric graph
.

The monotonicity of the definition of
\leqslant
-parametric graphs allows for a natural way to compare them.
We define a quasi-ordering relation
\lesssim
on the set of all
\leqslant
-parametric graphs.
For two
\leqslant
-parametric graphs
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{F},
we write
\mathscr{H}\lesssim\mathscr{F}
if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{H}_{k}\leqslant\mathscr{F}_{f(k)}
.
If we wish to specify the function
f
in the definition above we shall denote this by
\mathscr{H}\lesssim_{f}\mathscr{F}.

It is not hard to see that
\lesssim
is a quasi-ordering on the set of
\leqslant
-parametric graphs.

Moreover, we say that
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{F}
are
equivalent
, denoted by
\mathscr{H}\approx\mathscr{F}
, if
\mathscr{H}\lesssim\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{F}\lesssim\mathscr{H}
, i.e., if they are equivalent under the equivalence relation induced by
\lesssim
.
Once more, if we wish to specify the function
f
that certifies both directions of the above equivalence, we shall denote this by
\mathscr{H}\approx_{f}\mathscr{F}.

Note that forcing a single function that bounds both directions in the definition of
\approx_{f}
gives an equivalent definition to
\approx
.

\leqslant

where

Subsection 2.2
, this would imply that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{G}},b}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}},a}
and hence
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}\preceq{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{G}}
which concludes the proof.
Recall that
\mathfrak{G}_{\geq b}=\{\mathscr{G}^{(m)}_{g}\mid m\in[r_{1}]\}\cup\{\mathscr{G}^{(m)}_{b}\mid m\in[r_{1},r]\}
.
Consider a graph
Z\in{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq a})
.
In the case that
Z=\mathscr{G}^{(m)}_{g}
, for some
m\in[r_{1}]
, we immediately conclude.
In the case that
Z=\mathscr{G}^{m}_{n}
, for some

\leqslant

In the case that
Z=\mathscr{I}^{m}_{n}
, for some

Notice that, by definition of

∎

By the previous claim,
\mathfrak{G}
is the desired set of sequences
\mathfrak{H}
. ∎

Notice that

, applied to a
\leqslant

3.4
From graph parameters to class properties and their class obstructions

In this subsection we associate graph parameters to a particular subclass of class properties in a “natural” way that respects their approximate behaviour.
Via this correspondance we establish the notion of a class obstruction for a graph parameter and discuss how this set captures the approximate behaviour of the parameter.

Classes where a parameter is bounded.

Let
{\sf p}
be a graph parameter.
We say that
{\sf p}
is
bounded
in a graph class
\mathcal{G}
if there exists
c\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
G\in\mathcal{G},

{\sf p}(G)\leq c.

Via the previous definition, we may associate every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter to
\leqslant
-class property as follows.

Definition 3.17

(Class property associated to monotone parameter)
.

For every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter, we define the set
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
of
\leq
-closed classes where
{\sf p}
is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\ \coloneqq\ \{\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\mid\textrm{${\sf p}$ is bounded in $\mathcal{G}$}\}.

Note that, if
{\sf p}
is bounded in a
\leqslant
-closed class
\mathcal{G},
then
{\sf p}
is also bounded in any subclass of
\mathcal{G}.

Therefore
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
is a
\leqslant
-class property.

Dually, for every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter, we may also define the set

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\

\displaystyle\coloneqq\ \{\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\mid\text{${\sf p}$ is \emph{unbounded} in $\mathcal{G}$}\}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\setminus\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

Note that
\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
is upward
\subseteq
-closed in
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}),\subseteq).

The set
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
and the approximate behaviour of
{\sf p}
.

Having introduced the
\leqslant
-class property
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
for every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p},
we may observe the following.

Observation 3.18
.

Let
{\sf p}
be a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter.
Then,
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

The previous observation immediately leads to the following second observation:

Observation 3.19
.

Let
{\sf p}
be a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter.
Then,
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
if and only if there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k}
.

The following lemma show how comparing parameters approximately via the relation
\preceq
reduces to checking for inclusion of their corresponding
\leqslant
-class properties.

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
be two
\leqslant
-monotone parameters.
Then,
{\sf p}\preceq{\sf p}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

Proof.

For the proof of the forward direction, assume that
{\sf p}\preceq{\sf p}^{\prime}.

By
Subsection 3.2
, there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},f(k)},
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, by an application of
Subsection 3.4
for
{\sf p}
and
f(k)
we have that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

By a second application of
Subsection 3.4
for
{\sf p}^{\prime},
we conclude that
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

For the reverse direction, assume that
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

By an application of
Subsection 3.4
to
{\sf p}^{\prime},
we have that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

An application of
Subsection 3.4
to
{\sf p}
and
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
for every
k\in\mathbb{N},
implies the existence of a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},f(k)},
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, by
Subsection 3.2
, we conclude that
{\sf p}\preceq{\sf p}^{\prime}.

∎

A consequence of the lemma above and the fact that
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}),\subseteq)
is a partial-ordering is the following corollary.

Corollary 3.21
.

Let
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
be two
\leqslant
-monotone parameters. Then,
{\sf p}\sim{\sf p}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathbb{B}({\sf p})=\mathbb{B}({\sf p}^{\prime})
.

We should remark that the previous corollary implies that,
\leqslant
-monotone parameters in the same equivalence class of
\sim
are precisely characterized by the same
\leqslant
-class property, in the sense that the
\leqslant
-graph classes where any of the parameters in the equivalence class is bounded are precisely the same.

The previous discussion is a further indication that the
\leqslant
-class properties we associate to
\leqslant
-monotone parameters precisely capture their approximate behaviour.

Class obstruction of a parameter.

Motivated by the observations above, we define our first obstruction notion for a
\leqslant
-parameter to be the
\leqslant
-class obstruction set of its corresponding
\leqslant
-class property
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

Formally:

Definition 3.22

(Class obstruction)
.

For every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p},
we define the
\leqslant
-
class obstruction
of
{\sf p}
as the
\leqslant
-class obstruction set of
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
denoted by
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
when it exists.

Note that, if
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering, then the set
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathsf{p})
exists, for every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
\mathsf{p}.

We now know that every (equivalence class of a)
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p}
precisely corresponds to the
\leqslant
-class property
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

A natural question to ask is whether every
\leqslant
-class property corresponds to some (equivalence class of a)
\leqslant
-monotone parameter.
The answer to this question is negative.
We can observe that, for every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p}
,
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
belongs to a specific subclass of
\leqslant
-class properties.

Observation 3.23
.

For every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
\mathsf{p}
,
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
is a directed
\leqslant
-class property.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
is closed under union.
Indeed, let
\mathcal{G}
(resp.
\mathcal{H}
) be a
\leqslant
-closed class where
{\sf p}
is bounded by
c\in\mathbb{N}
(resp.
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
), for every graph in
\mathcal{G}
(resp.
\mathcal{H}
).
Then,
{\sf p}
is also bounded in
\mathcal{G}\cup\mathcal{H}
by
\max\{c,c^{\prime}\}.

∎

Combining the previous observation with
Theorem 3.6
shows, assuming well-quasi-ordering, the
\leqslant
-class obstruction of every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter is an inclusion-antichain of
\leqslant
-ideals of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

Corollary 3.24
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering.
Then, for every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
\mathsf{p},
the
\leqslant
-class obstruction of
\mathsf{p}
is an inclusion-antichain of
\leqslant
-ideals of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

Later we will see that, under certain order theoretic assumptions, every directed
\leqslant
-class property also corresponds to some suitably defined
\leqslant
-monotone parameter.
Moreover, under the same condition, we will also see how every
\leqslant
-class property corresponds to some finite set of
\leqslant
-monotone parameters.

3.5
Parametric families

In this subsection we introduce parametric families and slowly work towards our definition of “canonical representatives” for graph parameters.

We begin with the introduction of
\leqslant
-parametric families, a notion instrumental for our definition of
\leqslant
-universal obstructions in the coming subsections.

Definition 3.25
.

Any countable
\lesssim
-antichain of
\leqslant
-parametric graphs is a
\leqslant
-
parametric family
.

Moreover, to every
\leqslant
-parametric family
\mathfrak{H}
we associate the
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}
as in
Subsection 3.3
.

As we have done for all previously introduced notions, we shall introduce a quasi-ordering relation
\lesssim^{*}
on the set of all
\leqslant
-parametric families.
Let
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{F}
be two
\leqslant
-parametric families.
We write
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}\mathfrak{F}
if for every
\leqslant
-parametric graph
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
there exists a
\leqslant
-parametric graph
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}
such that
\mathscr{H}\lesssim\mathfrak{H},
i.e.,
\lesssim^{*}
corresponds to the Smyth extension of
\lesssim
on the set of all
\leqslant
-parametric families.
We also introduce a weaker form of
\lesssim^{*}
.
We write
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}
if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that for every
\leqslant
-parametric graph
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
there exists a
\leqslant
-parametric graph
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}
such that
\mathscr{H}\lesssim_{f}\mathfrak{H}.

We also define two equivalence relations on the set of all
\leqslant
-parametric families.
The first one corresponds to the equivalence relation induced by
\lesssim^{*}
and the second to the equivalence relation induced by
\lesssim^{*}_{f}.

We write
\mathfrak{H}\equiv^{*}\mathfrak{F}
if
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}\lesssim^{*}\mathfrak{H}
.
We write
\mathfrak{H}\equiv^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}
if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}
and
\mathfrak{F}\lesssim^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{H}
.

Comparing parameteric families.

One may quickly observe that in the case that we compare two
\leqslant
-parametric families
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{F}
where
\mathfrak{F}
is
finite
then
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}\mathfrak{F}
is equivalent to
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}
for some function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}.

Lemma 3.26
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{F}
be two
\leqslant
-parametric families such that
\mathfrak{F}
is finite.
Then,
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}\mathfrak{F}
if and only if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}.

Proof.

We only show the forward direction since the reverse trivially holds by definition.
Assume that
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}\mathfrak{F}
.
By definition, for every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
there exists
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}
such that
\mathscr{H}\lesssim_{f_{\mathscr{F}}}\mathscr{F},
for some function
f_{\mathscr{F}}\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}.

This implies that, for every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
there exists
\mathscr{H}\in\mathscr{H}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N},

\mathscr{H}_{k}\leqslant\mathscr{F}_{f_{\mathscr{F}}(k)}.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be the function such that
f(k)\coloneqq\max\{f_{\mathscr{F}}(k)\mid\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}\},
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\mathfrak{F}
is finite,
f
is well-defined.
Moreover, since
\mathscr{F}
is
\leqslant
-increasing, for every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{H},
it is implied that, for every
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
there exists
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N},

\mathscr{H}_{k}\leqslant\mathscr{F}_{f(k)}.

Therefore,
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}
and we conclude.
∎

We should remark that it is unclear whether
\lesssim^{*}
and
\lesssim^{*}_{f}
are equivalent when both
\leqslant
-parametric graphs are infinite.
The reason is that it is unclear whether there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N},
defined as
f(k)\coloneqq\sup\{f_{\mathscr{F}}(k)\mid\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}\},
for every
k\in\mathbb{N},
precisely because the supremum
\sup\{f_{\mathscr{F}}(k)\mid\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}\}
may not exist.

An immediate corollary of the previous lemma is the following.

Corollary 3.27
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{F}
be two finite
\leqslant
-parametric families.
Then,
\mathfrak{H}\equiv^{*}\mathfrak{F}
if and only if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{H}\equiv^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}.

\equiv^{*}_{f}
arises when attempting to compare the parameters corresponding to any two
\leqslant
-parametric families via

by comparing the
\leqslant
-parametric families via

Lemma 3.28
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
and

We have that

\{\mathscr{F}_{k}\mid\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}\}\leqslant^{*}\{\mathscr{H}_{f(k)}\mid\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}\}

for every

for every

there exists
\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}
such that

.

Parametric families and the class obstructions of their parameters.

We first present a lemma that precisely determines the
\leqslant
-closed classes where
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}
is bounded, for any graph sequence
\mathscr{H}
.

Lemma 3.34
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a graph sequence. Then

1.

\mathbb{B}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})={\downarrow}_{\subseteq}{\{{\sf excl}({\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq k}))\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}}
and

2.

\mathbb{B}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})=\{\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\mid\exists k\in\mathbb{N}:\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\sf excl}({\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq k}))\}.

Proof.

We may quickly observe that the fact that
{\downarrow}_{\subseteq}{\{{\sf excl}({\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq k}))\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}}=\{\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})\mid\exists k\in\mathbb{N}:\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\sf excl}({\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq k}))\}
follows directly from the definition of downward
\subseteq
-closure for the set
\{{\sf excl}({\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq k}))\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}
in
\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})
.
Now, by
Subsection 3.4
,
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})
if and only if
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}},k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, our claim follows, since by
Subsection 3.3
,
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}},k}={\sf excl}({\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq k})).

∎

We continue with a second lemma on how the comparison of two
\leqslant
-parameteric graphs via the quasi-ordering relation
\lesssim
we have defined on the set of
\leqslant
-parametric graphs, translates to the comparison of their corresponding parameters via
\preceq,
and to the comparison of their respective
\leqslant
-closures in terms of inclusion.

Lemma 3.35
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{F}
be two
\leqslant
-parametric graphs.
Then, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}\succeq{\sf p}_{\mathscr{F}};

2.

{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}\subseteq{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{F}};

3.

\mathscr{H}\lesssim\mathscr{F}.

Proof.

We first show how
1.
implies
2.
.
Suppose that
G
is a graph of
{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}
.
Then, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
G\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{k}
.
It follows that
\{G\}\leqslant^{*}\{\mathscr{H}_{k}\}
.
In turn, by
Subsection 2.2
, this implies that
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{G\})\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{H}_{k}\})
.
Since
\mathscr{H}
is
\leqslant
-increasing, we have that
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{H}_{k}\})={\sf excl}_{\leqslant}({\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq k}))
.
By
Subsection 3.5
, we obtain that
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{G\})\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})
.
Since
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}\succeq{\sf p}_{\mathscr{F}}
, by
Subsection 3.4
, we have
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{F}})
and thereby
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{G\})\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{F}})
.
By
Subsection 3.5
, there exists
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{G\})\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}({\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{F}_{\geq k^{\prime}}))
.
Again, since
\mathscr{F}
is
\leqslant
-increasing, we have
{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{F}_{\geq k^{\prime}})=\{\mathscr{F}_{k^{\prime}}\}
.
Thereby, by
Subsection 2.2
,
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{G\})\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{F}_{k^{\prime}}\})
implies
\{G\}\leqslant^{*}\{\mathscr{F}_{k^{\prime}}\}
.
It follows that there exists
k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
G\leqslant\mathscr{F}_{k^{\prime}}
, in other words,
G\in{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{F}}
.

Now, we show how
2.
implies
3.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N},

\mathscr{H}_{i}\in{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}.

Since
\mathscr{H}_{i}\in{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{F}},
by definition there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{H}_{i}\leqslant\mathscr{F}_{j}.

To conclude, we show how
3.
implies
1.

Let
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}).

By
Subsection 3.5
, there exists
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{H}_{i}\}).

By assumption, there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{H}_{i}\leqslant\mathscr{F}_{j},
equivalently
\{\mathscr{H}_{i}\}\leqslant^{*}\{\mathscr{F}_{j}\}.

By
Subsection 2.2
, we have
{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{H}_{i}\})\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{F}_{j}\}),
which implies that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{F}_{j}\})
.
This implies that
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{F}}),
therefore
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}\succeq{\sf p}_{\mathscr{F}}
follows from
Subsection 3.4
.
∎

The following two lemmas precisely characterize the class obstruction of parameters associated to parametric graphs and, in turn, to finite parametric families as the set of
\leqslant
-closed classes that correspond to the
\leqslant
-closures of the involved
\leqslant
-parametric graphs.

Lemma 3.36
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a
\leqslant
-parametric graph.
Then,

\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})=\{{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}\}.

Proof.

By definition of
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}
and the fact that
\mathscr{H}
is a
\leqslant
-parametric graph, we have that
{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})
.
Now, consider any
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})
.
Then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
such that
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}(G)>k.

This implies that
\mathscr{H}_{k}\leqslant G.

Since
\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})
,
\mathscr{H}_{k}\in\mathcal{G}
and thereby
{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
.
Hence
\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})={\uparrow}_{\subseteq}{({\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}})}.

∎

Lemma 3.37
.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
be a finite
\leqslant
-parametric family.
Then,

\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}})=\bigcup_{\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}}\{\{{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}\}\}.

Proof.

One may observe that
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}})=\bigcap_{\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}}\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})
.
Let
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}).

By definition of
{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}},
there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{H}_{k}\mid\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}\})
.
This in turn implies that,
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}({\{\mathscr{H}_{k}\}}),
for every
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}.

Then, by definition of
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}},

\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}),
for every
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}
and therefore
\mathcal{G}\in\bigcap_{\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}}\mathbb{B}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}).

For the reverse, let
\mathcal{G}\in\bigcap_{\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}}\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}).

Then, for every
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H},
by definition of
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}},
there exists
k_{\mathscr{H}}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{H}_{k_{\mathscr{H}}}\}).

Since
\mathfrak{H}
is finite, we may define
k\coloneqq\max\{k_{\mathscr{H}}\mid\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}\}
.
Then, since every
\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}
is
\leqslant
-increasing, we have that
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\sf excl}_{\leqslant}(\{\mathscr{H}_{k}\mid\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}\}).

This in turn shows that, by definition of
{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}},

\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}).

Then, we have that
\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}})=\bigcup_{\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}}\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}).

Since
\mathfrak{H}
is a
\lesssim
-antichain, by
Subsection 3.5
, we have that
\bigcup_{\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}}\{\{{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}\}\}
is an
\subseteq
-antichain.
Hence by
Subsection 3.5
, it is the inclusion-basis of
\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}).

∎

Two examples of minor-parametric families.

, such that

.

implies that the
\leqslant
-class obstruction

is exactly the set containing the class of forests, i.e.

.

An

to denote the class of all apex forests.
An

We use

to denote the class of outerplanar graphs.

.

For every apex forest

(resp.

).

As in the case of forests, combining this fact with

-class obstruction of

3.6
Universal obstructions

We are now in the position to describe the main obstruction notion for graph parameters of our parametric viewpoint that will allow us to express graph parameters in a “canonical obstructing” form, via the exclusion of a set of parametric graphs.
We furthermore examine sufficient order theoretic conditions that ensure the existence of universal obstructions.
We also introduce the notion of
parametric obstruction
for a graph parameter, which is a variant of its class obstruction.

We first introduce the definition of a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction for a graph parameter.

Definition 3.40

(Universal obstruction)
.

A
\leqslant
-parametric family
\mathfrak{H}
is a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction for a graph parameter
{\sf p}
if
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}
are equivalent.

Building on the results of the previous subsection, we show that, for any
\leqslant
-monotone parameter that admits a finite
\leqslant
-universal obstruction, its
\leqslant
-class obstruction exists and is closely related to the
\leqslant
-parametric graphs contained in its
\leqslant
-universal obstruction.

Theorem 3.41
.

Let
{\sf p}
be a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter.
If
{\sf p}
has a finite
\leqslant
-universal obstruction
\mathfrak{H}
then

\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})=\bigcup_{\mathscr{H}\in\mathfrak{H}}\{\{{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}\}\}.

Proof.

By definition of
\leqslant
-universal obstructions,
{\sf p}\sim{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}.

By
Subsection 3.4
,
\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})=\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}).

Then, by
Subsection 3.5
, our claim holds.
∎

The next result shows that the class of
\leqslant
-rational parameters corresponds precisely to the
\leqslant
-monotone parameters that have finite
\leqslant
-universal obstructions.

Theorem 3.42
.

A graph parameter
{\sf p}
is
\leqslant
-rational if and only if it has a finite
\leqslant
-universal obstruction.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a
\leqslant
-rational sequence such that
{\sf p}\sim{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}.

By
Theorem 3.15
, there exists a finite
\leqslant
-parametric family
\mathfrak{H}
such that
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}\sim{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}.

Then by definition
\mathfrak{H}
is a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction for
{\sf p}.

For the reverse direction, let
\mathfrak{H}=\{\mathscr{H}^{(i)}\mid i\in[r]\}
be a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction for
{\sf p}
.
We define a graph sequence
\mathscr{H}=\langle\mathscr{H}_{k}\rangle_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathscr{H}_{k}=\mathscr{H}^{(i)}_{j}
, where
k=i\cdot r+j
, for some choice of
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
j\in[r]
.
Clearly
\mathscr{H}
is a
\leqslant
-rational sequence.
Define the
\leqslant
-parametric family
\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
to be the set obtained starting from
\mathfrak{H}
after removing from each
\leqslant
-parametric graph in
\mathfrak{H}
all duplicates of elements that appear only a finite number of times in the
\leqslant
-parametric graph, as well as all elements that appear in any other
\leqslant
-parametric graph of
\mathfrak{H}
.
Since
\mathfrak{H}
is a finite
\lesssim
-antichain, it is not hard to see that
\mathfrak{H}\equiv^{*}\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
.
Then, by
Subsection 3.5
,
{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}\sim{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}}
.
Observe that
\mathfrak{H^{\prime}}
satisfies all necessary properties in order to apply
Subsection 3.3
of
Theorem 3.15
for
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathfrak{G}
being
\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
, which shows that
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}\sim{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}
.
We conclude that
{\sf p}
is
\leqslant
-rational.
∎

Two examples of minor-universal obstructions.

Bienstock, Robertson, Seymour, and Thomas proved in
[

that for every forest
F
, every graph with no minor isomorphic to
F
has pathwidth at most

.
Thereofore, every graph excluding
\mathscr{T}_{k}
as a minor has pathwidth

.
Moroever, it is easy to verify that

).
This implies that the singleton

is a minor-universal obstruction for

(recall

).
Additionally,

implies that the minor-class obstruction of
{\sf pw}
is the set that contains the class of forests, i.e.
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}}({\sf pw})=\{\mathcal{G}_{\text{\rm{forest}}}\}
.

Similar implications can be argued for biconnected pathwidth.
It was proven in

that there exists a function

.

3.7
Parametric obstructions

In the previous subsections we introduced the notions of
\leqslant
-class obstructions and
\leqslant
-universal obstructions as a means of approximately characterizing graph parameters by an equivalent representative parameter in a “canonical” form.
In this manner a
\leqslant
-monotone graph parameter that admits a finite
\leqslant
-universal obstruction
\mathfrak{H}
, also admits a finite representation in the sense that the both its
\leqslant
-class obstruction and the set
\mathfrak{H}
are finite and very closely related.
However, we should stress that these two notions do not yet offer a truly finite characterization as each
\leqslant
-parametric graph in
\mathfrak{H}
is an infinite object and the same holds for each
\leqslant
-closed class in its
\leqslant
-class obstruction.

In this subsection, to circumvent the issue above, we introduce a variant of the
\leqslant
-class obstruction which we shall call the
\leqslant
-parametric obstruction, that under certain order theoretic assumptions will serve as a fully finite obstruction characterization for graph parameters.

We proceed with the definition of the
\leqslant
-parametric obstruction of a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter.

Definition 3.43

(Parametric obstruction)
.

The
\leqslant
-
parametric obstruction

of a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p}
is the set of
\leqslant
-obstruction sets
\{{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G})\mid\mathcal{G}\in\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\}
, which we denote by
\mbox{\rm{pobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
when
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
exists.

Note that, if
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering, then the set
\mbox{\rm{pobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathsf{p})
exists, for every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
\mathsf{p},
and moreover every
\leqslant
-obstruction set in
\mbox{\rm{pobs}}_{\leqslant}(\mathsf{p})
is finite.

Having introduced all of our obstruction notions for graph parameters, we are now in the position to state a theorem that shows how graph parameters may be equivalently compared through all the different notions we have seen so far.

Theorem 3.44
.

Let
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
be two
\leqslant
-monotone parameters. If
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
both admit
\leqslant
-universal obstructions and their
\leqslant
-class obstructions exist, then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

{\sf p}\succeq{\sf p}^{\prime}
;

2.

\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\subseteq^{*}\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime})
;

3.

\mbox{\rm{pobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\leqslant^{**}\mbox{\rm{pobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime})
;
11
11

11

\leqslant^{**}
denotes the Smyth extension of
\leqslant^{*}
.

4.

for every
\leqslant
-universal obstruction
\mathfrak{H}
of
{\sf p}
and every
\leqslant
-universal obstruction
\mathfrak{F}
of
{\sf p}^{\prime}
, there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}
;

5.

for every
\leqslant
-closed class
\mathcal{G},
if
{\sf p}
is bounded in
\mathcal{G}
then
{\sf p}^{\prime}
is bounded in
\mathcal{G}.

Proof.

By
Subsection 3.4
,
{\sf p}\succeq{\sf p}^{\prime}
if and only if
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime}).

By
Subsection 3.4
and
Subsection 2.1
,
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime})
if and only if
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}({\sf p})\subseteq^{*}\mbox{\rm{cobs}}({\sf p}^{\prime}).

Then, by
Subsection 3.7
and
Subsection 2.2
,
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}({\sf p})\subseteq^{*}\mbox{\rm{cobs}}({\sf p}^{\prime})
if and only if
\mbox{\rm{pobs}}({\sf p})\leqslant^{**}\mbox{\rm{pobs}}({\sf p}^{\prime}).

Also, by
Subsection 3.6
and
Subsection 3.5
,
{\sf p}\succeq{\sf p}^{\prime}
if and only if there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{H}\lesssim^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F},
for any
\leqslant
-universal obstruction
\mathfrak{H}
of
{\sf p}
(resp.
\mathfrak{F}
of
{\sf p}^{\prime}
).
∎

We remark that
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is assumed to be a partial-ordering.
Moreover,
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}),\subseteq)
is also a partial-ordering.
These two facts, combined with the fact that the
\leqslant
-class obstructions and the
\leqslant
-parametric obstructions are inclusion-antichains and
\leqslant^{*}
-antichains respectively, and
Theorem 3.44
, imply the following corollary.

Corollary 3.45
.

Let
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
be two
\leqslant
-monotone parameters. If
{\sf p}
and
{\sf p}^{\prime}
both admit
\leqslant
-universal obstructions and their
\leqslant
-class obstructions exist, then the following statements are equivalent:

1.

{\sf p}\sim{\sf p}^{\prime}
;

2.

\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})=\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime})
;

3.

\mbox{\rm{pobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})=\mbox{\rm{pobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}^{\prime})
;

4.

for every
\leqslant
-universal obstruction
\mathfrak{H}
of
{\sf p}
and every
\leqslant
-universal obstruction
\mathfrak{F}
of
{\sf p}^{\prime}
, there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{H}\equiv^{*}_{f}\mathfrak{F}
;

5.

for every
\leqslant
-closed class
\mathcal{G},

{\sf p}
is bounded in
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
{\sf p}^{\prime}
is bounded in
\mathcal{G}.

\leqslant
-class obstructions and

3.8
Omnivores

In this subsection we introduce the notion of a
\leqslant
-omnivore of a
\leqslant
-closed class in an attempt to understand which
\leqslant
-closed classes admit a regular description in terms of the
\leqslant
-closure of some
\leqslant
-parametric graph.
We provide an exact order theoretic characterization for when
\leqslant
-closed classes admit
\leqslant
-omnivores.
This results will have a tremendous impact in our quest for characterizing class properties in terms of graph parameters in a “canonical” way.

We proceed with the definition of a
\leqslant
-omnivore of a
\leqslant
-closed class.

Definition 3.46

(Omnivore)
.

A
\leqslant
-parametric graph
\mathscr{H}
is a
\leqslant
-
omnivore
of a
\leqslant
-closed graph class
\mathcal{G}
if
{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{H}}=\mathcal{G}.

It turns out that the
\leqslant
-closed classes that admit
\leqslant
-omnivores are precisely the
\leqslant
-ideals of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

Lemma 3.47
.

A
\leqslant
-closed class
\mathcal{G}
has a
\leqslant
-omnivore if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is a
\leqslant
-ideal of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a
\leqslant
-omnivore of
\mathcal{G}.

We show that
\mathcal{G}
is
\leqslant
-directed.
For every
G\in\mathcal{G}
we have that there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
G\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{k}.

For
G,G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G},
let
k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
G\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{k}
and
G^{\prime}\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{k^{\prime}}.

Let
m:=\max\{k,k^{\prime}\}.

By definition of
\mathscr{H},

G\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{m}
and
G^{\prime}\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{m}.

As
\mathscr{H}_{m}\in\mathcal{G},
it follows that
\mathcal{G}
is
\leqslant
-directed, therefore it is a
\leqslant
-ideal of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

For the reverse direction, we assume that
\mathcal{G}
is
\leqslant
-directed and we show how to define a
\leqslant
-omnivore
\mathscr{H}
of
\mathcal{G}.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{r}\coloneqq\{G\in\mathcal{G}\mid|V(G)|\leq r\}
, for every
r\in\mathbb{N}.

We define
\mathscr{H}_{1}
as a graph in
\mathcal{G}
such that for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{1}
,
G\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{1}
.
For
k>1,
we define
\mathscr{H}_{k}
as a graph in
\mathcal{G}
such that for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
,
G\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{k}
and
\mathscr{H}_{k-1}\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{k}
.

\mathscr{H}_{k}
is well-defined, since
\mathcal{G}_{k}\cup\{\mathscr{H}_{k-1}\}
is finite and
\mathcal{G}
is
\leqslant
-directed.
By definition
\mathscr{H}_{k-1}\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{k}.

Also, for any graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
we have that
G\leqslant\mathscr{H}_{|V(G)|}.

Therefore
\mathscr{H}=\langle\mathscr{H}_{k}\rangle_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
\leqslant
-omnivore of
\mathcal{G}.

∎

Two examples of minor-omnivores.

Recall that the graph class of forests is exactly the set of minors of complete ternary trees (
Subsection 3.5
).
In the terminology introduced in this subsection this translates to the fact that the parametric graph
\mathscr{T}
is a minor-omnivore of the class of forests
\mathcal{G}_{\text{\rm{forest}}}
.
Similarly,
Subsection 3.5
implies that the parametric graph
\mathscr{T}^{a}
(resp.
\mathscr{T}^{a*}
) is a minor-omnivore of the class of apex forests
\mathcal{G}_{\text{\rm{apex forest}}}
(resp. the class of outerplanar graphs
\mathcal{G}_{\text{\rm{outerplanar}}}
).

Beforehand, with
Subsection 3.4
, we saw that, every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter corresponds to a directed
\leqslant
-class property.
With
\leqslant
-omnivores we now have a powerful tool at hand that will allow us, under suitable order theoretic assumptions, to prove the reverse
Subsection 3.4
.

Starting from the
\leqslant
-class obstruction set of a directed
\leqslant
-class property we shall construct a
\leqslant
-parametric family, whose corresponding

Given a

Let

.

\leqslant
is

Then every

-rational parameter

Proof.

Let

5
Towards finite universal obstructions

In
Section 3
we saw the central role that finite
\leqslant
-parametric families play, in obtaining finite
\leqslant
-universal obstructions for
\leqslant
-rational parameters as well as for representing directed
\leqslant
-class properties whose
\leqslant
-class obstruction sets are finite.
In this section, our goal is to identify appropriate order theoretic assumptions on
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
implying that finite
\leqslant
-parametric families suffice to characterize
every

\leqslant
-monotone parameter in terms of a finite
\leqslant
-universal obstruction.
Moreover, we identify a necessary and sufficient order theoretic condition that implies a representation of
every

\leqslant
-class property in terms of a finite collection of
\leqslant
-parametric families.

5.1
More implications of well-quasi-ordering

In this first subsection we consider the implications on the existence of
\leqslant
-universal obstructions when
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering.

Obstruction chains.

Consider a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p}
and let
I\in\{[n]\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{\mathbb{N}\}
be a countable index set.
Let
\mathscr{C}=\langle\mathscr{C}_{i}\rangle_{i\in I}
be a
\leqslant
-chain such that for every
i\in I,

\mathscr{C}_{i}\in{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i}).

We call
\mathscr{C}
a
\leqslant
-obstruction chain
of
{\sf p}.

In the case that
I=[n]
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
we say that
\mathscr{C}
is finite and of length
|I|,
while in the case that
I=\mathbb{N}
we say it is infinite.
Given a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p}
, we define

\mathfrak{C}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\coloneqq\{\mathscr{C}\mid\textrm{$\mathscr{C}$ is a $\leqslant$-obstruction chain of ${\sf p}$}\}

and from this point forward let
\mathfrak{F}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\cup\mathfrak{I}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
be a partition of
\mathfrak{C}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
into the set of finite (
\mathfrak{F}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
) and the set of infinite (
\mathfrak{I}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
)
\leqslant
-obstruction chains of
{\sf p}.

Recall that, by
Theorem 3.48
, so far we have only been able to fully characterize the
\leqslant
-class obstructions and obtain
\leqslant
-universal obstructions, only for
\leqslant
-monotone parameters whose corresponding directed
\leqslant
-class property has a finite class
\leqslant
-obstruction set.
However, one could attempt to repeat the arguments in the proof of
Theorem 3.48
and try to show that, by taking a
\leqslant
-omnivore for each
\leqslant
-closed class in an infinite inclusion-antichain of
\leqslant
-ideals
\mathbb{I},
one could define a
\leqslant
-parametric family, where the
\leqslant
-class obstruction of its corresponding parameter is precisely
\mathbb{I}.

In this way, we could argue that we find a certificate of a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter that has an infinite
\leqslant
-universal obstruction and that captures a directed
\leqslant
-class property with an infinite
\leqslant
-class obstruction set.
However, this approach seems to fail, and the intuitive reason why it does seems to lie in our inability to certify that, the two equivalence relations
\equiv^{*}
and
\equiv^{*}_{f}
on the set of
\leqslant
-parametric families, are equivalent in the presence of infinite
\leqslant
-parametric families.

Therefore, should we wish to prove the existence of
\leqslant
-universal obstructions for arbitrary
\leqslant
-monotone parameters we need to be more careful in how we define our
\leqslant
-parametric graphs.
In the theorem that follows, we demonstrate that, for every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter, its
\leqslant
-class obstruction consists of the inclusion-minimal classes of graphs that corresponds to the
\leqslant
-closures of the infinite
\leqslant
-obstructions chains of
{\sf p}
.
Moreover, this set of minimal infinite
\leqslant
-obstruction chains of
{\sf p}
corresponds to a
\leqslant
-parametric family that is a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction for
{\sf p}.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering.
Then, for every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p}
, any
\lesssim
-minimization
\mathfrak{Z}
of the set
\mathfrak{I}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
is a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction for
{\sf p}.

Moreover,

\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})=\bigcup_{\mathscr{Z}\in\mathfrak{Z}}\{\{{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{Z}}\}\}.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering, by
Subsection 2.1
,
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}),\subseteq)
is well-founded.
Then, by
Subsection 3.5
,
\lesssim
-minimizations are well-defined.
Let
\mathfrak{Z}
be any
\lesssim
-minimization of the set
\mathfrak{I}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

Again, by
Subsection 3.5
, we have that the set
\mathbb{Z}\coloneqq\{{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{Z}}\mid\mathscr{Z}\in\mathfrak{Z}\}
is an inclusion-antichain.
We first demonstrate that
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})=\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).
We show that there exists
\mathscr{I}\in\mathfrak{I}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
such that
{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{I}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N},
since
{\sf p}
is unbounded in
\mathcal{G},
there exists
G_{k}\in{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k})
such that
G_{k}\in\mathcal{G}.
For
i\leqslant j
we have that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},j}.
By
Subsection 2.2
, we have that
{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i})\leqslant^{*}{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},j}).
Then, by definition of
\leqslant^{*},
there exists a finite
\leqslant
-chain
\mathscr{F}^{(k)}\in\mathfrak{F}({\sf p})
of length
k
whose
\leqslant
-maximum element is
G_{k}.
We define an infinite
\leqslant
-obstruction chain
\mathscr{I}
of
{\sf p}
as follows:

Let
\mathfrak{F}^{0}=\{\mathscr{F}^{(k)}\mid k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\mathcal{B}^{1}=\{H\in{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},1})\mid\exists\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}^{0}:H=\mathscr{F}_{1}\}.

Choose
\mathscr{I}_{1}
from
\mathcal{B}^{1}
such that
\{\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}^{0}\mid\mathscr{F}_{1}=\mathscr{I}_{1}\}
is infinite.
This choice is valid, since
\mathcal{B}^{1}
is finite (by the assumption that
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering) and
\mathfrak{F}^{0}
is infinite.

Now, assume that we have defined
\mathfrak{F}^{0},\dots,\mathfrak{F}^{i-1},

\mathcal{B}^{1},\dots,\mathcal{B}^{i}
and
\mathscr{I}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{I}_{i},
for some
i>0,
such that
\{\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}^{i-1}\mid\mathscr{F}_{i}=\mathscr{I}_{i}\}
is infinite.
Define
\mathfrak{F}^{i}=\{\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}^{i-1}\mid\mathscr{F}_{i}=\mathscr{I}_{i}\}
and
\mathcal{B}^{i+1}=\{H\in{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i+1})\mid\exists\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}^{i}:H=\mathscr{F}_{i+1}\}.

Choose
\mathscr{I}_{i+1}
from
\mathcal{B}^{i+1}
such that
\{\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}^{i}\mid\mathscr{F}_{i+1}=\mathscr{I}_{i+1}\}
is infinite.
As before, the choice is valid, since
\mathcal{B}^{i+1}
is finite (by the assumption that
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering) and
\mathfrak{F}^{i}
is infinite (by the assumption that
\{\mathscr{F}\in\mathfrak{F}^{i-1}\mid\mathscr{F}_{i}=\mathscr{I}_{i}\}
is infinite).
By the axiom of choice,
\mathscr{I}
is well-defined and by its definition,
\mathscr{I}\in\mathfrak{I}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})
and
{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{I}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}.

Let
\mathbb{I}({\sf p})=\{{\downarrow}_{\leqslant}{\mathscr{I}}\mid\mathscr{I}\in\mathfrak{I}({\sf p})\}.
Recall that by definition, every
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{I}({\sf p})
contains a graph in
{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}.
Hence,
{\sf p}
is unbounded for any
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{I}({\sf p}),
i.e.
\mathbb{I}({\sf p})\subseteq\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).
Since for every
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{U}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}),
we have that there exists
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{I}({\sf p})
such that
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{G},
it must be that
\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})=\mathbb{Z},
since
\mathbb{Z}={\sf min}_{\subseteq}(\mathbb{I}({\sf p}))
.

We now argue that
\mathfrak{Z}
is indeed a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction for
\mathsf{p}.

By
Subsection 3.6
and
Subsection 3.4
, it suffices to show that
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})=\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{Z}}).

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a
\leqslant
-closed class in
\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{Z}}).

This implies that there exists
c\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G},

{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{Z}}(G)\leq c.

By definition of
{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{Z}},
this implies that for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G},

\{\mathscr{Z}_{c}\mid\mathscr{Z}\in\mathfrak{Z}\}\nleqslant^{*}\{G\}.

By definition of
\leqslant^{*},
this implies that for every graph
G\in\mathscr{G},

\mathscr{Z}_{c}\not\leqslant G,
and since
\mathcal{G}
is
\leqslant
-closed, this implies that
\mathscr{Z}_{c}\not\in\mathcal{G},
for every
\mathscr{Z}\in\mathfrak{Z}.

Therefore,
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

For the reverse direction, assume that
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}).

This implies that there exists
c\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G},

{\sf p}(G)\leq c.

In other words, for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G},

G\in\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},c}.

This implies that for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G},

Z\not\leqslant G,
for every
Z\in{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},c}).

Now, it suffices to observe that by definition of
\leqslant
-obstruction chains,
\{\mathscr{Z}_{c}\mid\mathscr{Z}\in\mathfrak{Z}\}\subseteq{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},c}).

Combining the two previous remarks we have that, for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G},

\mathscr{Z}_{c}\not\leqslant G,
for every
\mathscr{Z}\in\mathfrak{Z}.

With this we conclude that
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{B}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{Z}}).

∎

From the algorithmic point of view, under the well-quasi-ordering assumption, we get the following stronger version of
Theorem 3.42
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering and
\leqslant
be
FPT
-decidable.
Then, a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter
{\sf p}
is
\leqslant
-rational if and only if it has an effective
\leqslant
-universal obstruction.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering, for every
\mathcal{G}\in\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}),

{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G})
is finite.
Then, the forward direction is implied by
Subsection 4.4
, since
\textsf{core}_{\leqslant}({\sf p})\neq\emptyset.

The reverse direction is trivially implied by
Theorem 3.42
.
∎

Furthermore, under the well-quasi-ordering assumption we can additionally show that, every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter is
\leqslant
-real, i.e., corresponds to a graph sequence, in the sense that it is equivalent to the parameter corresponding to said sequence.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering.
Then, every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter is
\leqslant
-real.

Proof.

Let
{\sf p}
be a
\leqslant
-monotone parameter.
We define the graph sequence
\mathscr{H}:=\langle{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k})\rangle_{k\in\mathbb{N}}.

We first prove the following claim.

Claim 5.4
.

There exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq f(k)})={\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k}).

Proof of claim.

We define the desired function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
as follows.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}.

We define
f(k)
so that
\mathscr{H}_{\geq f(k)}\cap\bigcup_{j<k}{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},j})=\emptyset.

Let
i>k.

Since
{\sf p}
is
\leqslant
-monotone, we have that
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i}.

Then, by
Subsection 2.2
, we have that
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k})\leqslant^{*}{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i}).

Now, observe that
\mathscr{H}_{\geq f(k)}=\bigcup_{j\geq k}{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},j}).

Thus we have that for every
H\in\mathscr{H}_{\geq f(k)}
there exists
H^{\prime}\in{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k})
such that
H^{\prime}\leqslant H
and moreover, since
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k})
is an
\leqslant
-antichain,
{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq f(k)})={\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k}).

∎

We show that
{\sf p}\sim{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}.
Let
G
be a graph such that
{\sf p}(G)\leq k.

Then for every
H\in{\sf obs}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k}),

H\nleqslant G.

By
Subsection 5.1
,
{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq f(k)})={\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},k}).

Then, by definition of
{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}},
it is implied that
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}(G)\leq f(k).

Now, let
G
be a graph such that
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}(G)\leq k.

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}
be maximum such that
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i})\cap\{\mathscr{H}_{j}\mid j\in[k]\}\neq\emptyset.

By
Subsection 5.1
,
{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq f(i+1)})={\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i+1}).

By definition of
\mathscr{H},

f(i+1)\geq k.
Then, clearly
{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq k})\leqslant^{*}{\sf min}_{\leqslant}(\mathscr{H}_{\geq f(i+1)})
and thus
{\sf obs}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p},i+1})\nleqslant^{*}\{G\},
which implies
{\sf p}(G)\leq i+1.

∎

Moreover, we show that, under the well-quasi-ordering assumption, every graph sequence (not necessarily rational as in
Theorem 3.15
) also corresponds to a
\leqslant
-parametric family with respect to equivalence of their corresponding parameters.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be a well-quasi-ordering.
Then, for every graph sequence
\mathscr{H}
there exists a
\leqslant
-parametric family
\mathfrak{H}
such that
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}\sim{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{H}
be a
\leqslant
-parametric family defined as any
\lesssim
-minimization of the set of infinite obstruction
\leqslant
-chains in
\mathfrak{I}({\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}})
.
Note that, since
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering, by
Subsection 2.1
,
(\mathsf{Down}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}),\subseteq)
is well-founded.
Then, by
Subsection 3.5
,
\lesssim
-minimizations are well-defined.
Then, our claim follows by
Theorem 5.1
, since it implies that
\mathfrak{H}
is a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction of
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}
and therefore
{\sf p}_{\mathscr{H}}\sim{\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}}.

∎

5.2
Finite representation of class properties

In the previous subsection we saw that under the well-quasi-ordering assumption, every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter admits a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction.
In this subsection, we establish sufficient order theoretic conditions that moreover certify the existence of finite
\leqslant
-universal obstructions for all
\leqslant
-monotone parameters.
Moreover, we study the potential of these order-theoretic assumptions, paired with our viewpoint of
\leqslant
-parametric families, in obtaining a representation of every
\leqslant
-class property via a finite set of finite
\leqslant
-parametric families.

As we saw in
Theorem 5.1
, under the assumption that
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is a well-quasi-ordering, we can show that every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter admits a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction.
However, we would like to identify a sufficient order-theoretic condition which furthermore implies the existence of finite
\leqslant
-universal obstructions.
Such a condition of course has to be “stronger” that the well-quasi-ordering assumption.

Therefore it is natural to consider whether the “second order” well-quasi-ordering notion, i.e., an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering suffices for our purposes.
Towards this direction,
Subsection 3.1
tells us that assuming that
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
is an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering, suffices to show that the
\leqslant
-class obstruction set of every
\leqslant
-class property is finite.

The observation above directly allows for a generalization of
Theorem 3.53
.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering and
\mathbb{CP}
be any
\leqslant
-class property.
Then,
\mathbb{CP}
is representable by a finite
\leqslant
-parametric family if and only
\mathbb{CP}
is directed.

Now, let
\mathbf{F}_{\leqslant}
denote the set of all equivalence classes of
\equiv^{*}_{f}
, namely,

\mathbf{F}_{\leqslant}\coloneqq\{[\mathfrak{H}]_{f}\mid\text{$\mathfrak{H}$ is a $\leqslant$-parametric family}\}

and let
\mathbf{I}_{\leqslant}
denote the set of all inclusion-antichains of
\leqslant
-ideals of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},
namely,

\mathbf{I}_{\leqslant}\coloneqq\{\mathbb{I}\in 2^{{\sf Idl}_{\leqslant}(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}})}\mid\text{$\mathbb{I}$ is an inclusion-antichain}\}.

Subsection 3.1
has as a consequence that
\mathbf{I}_{\leqslant}
is equal to its restricted counterpart
\mathbf{I}^{\mathsf{fin}}_{\leqslant},
which we introduced in
Subsection 3.8
, and via
Subsection 3.5
, that
\mathbf{F}_{\leqslant}
is also equal to its restricted counterpart
\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{fin}}_{\leqslant}.

This implies that the bijection
\rho_{\ref{bijection_coll_ideals}}\colon\mathbf{F}^{\mathsf{fin}}_{\leqslant}\to\mathbf{I}^{\mathsf{fin}}_{\leqslant}
extends to a bijection between
\mathbf{F}_{\leqslant}
and
\mathbf{I}_{\leqslant}.

Observation 5.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering.
Then,
\rho_{\ref{bijection_coll_ideals}}\colon\mathbf{F}_{\leqslant}\to\mathbf{I}_{\leqslant}
is a unique bijection such that, for every
[\mathfrak{G}]_{f}\in\mathbf{F}_{\leqslant},
for every
\mathfrak{H}\in[\mathfrak{G}]_{f},

\mbox{\rm{cobs}}_{\leqslant}({\sf p}_{\mathfrak{H}})=\rho_{\ref{bijection_coll_ideals}}([\mathfrak{G}]_{f}).

Also, let
\mathbf{P}_{\leqslant}
denote the set of all equivalence classes (with respect to
\sim
) of
\leqslant
-monotone parameters, namely,

\mathbf{P}_{\leqslant}\coloneqq\{[{\sf p}]_{\sim}\mid\textrm{${\sf p}$ is a $\leqslant$-monotone parameter}\}.

Now, there are a number of ways to see that, the
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering assumption, also implies that
\mathbf{P}_{\leqslant}
is equal to its restricted counterpart
\mathbf{P}_{\leqslant}^{\mathsf{rational}},
i.e., that every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter is
\leqslant
-rational.
One way is to observe that, by
Subsection 3.4
, the
\leqslant
-class obstruction of every
\leqslant
-monotone parameter is an inclusion-antichain of
\leqslant
-ideals of
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}.

Since, by
Subsection 3.1
, every such inclusion-antichain is finite, it is implied that the bijection
\rho_{\ref{bijection_rational_ideals}}\colon\mathbf{P}_{\leqslant}^{\mathsf{rational}}\to\mathbf{I}_{\leqslant}^{\mathsf{fin}}
extends to a bijection between
\mathbf{P}_{\leqslant}
and
\mathbf{I}_{\leqslant}.

Observation 5.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}},\leqslant)
be an

6
Some examples of obstructions for graph parameters

In this section we present obstructions for a small indicative sample of graph parameters that are monotone under certain partial-ordering relations on graphs.
All results of this section are consequences of known results for the graph parameters that we present.

6.1
One more example of a minor-monotone parameter

Throughout the previous sections we presented the minor-monotone parameters
pw
and
bi
-
pw
.
In the next paragraph we present one more (perhaps the most famous one) example of such a graph parameter.

Treewidth.

The graph parameter treewidth was defined by Robertson and Seymour in
[
73
]
and used as a cornerstone parameter of their Graph Minors series (see also
[
9
]
).
Among the (many) equivalent definitions of treewidth we pick the following that is similar to the definition of pathwidth that we gave in
Subsection 2.2
.
The
treewidth
of a graph
G,
denoted by
{\sf tw}(G),
is defined as the minimum
k
for which there is a vertex ordering
\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle
of the vertices of
G
such that, for each
i\in[n],
there are at most
k
vertices in
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1}\}
that are adjacent with vertices in the connected component of
G[\{v_{i},\ldots,v_{n}\}]
that contains
v_{i}
(see
[
23
]
).

According to the
grid theorem
, proved by Robertson and Seymour in
[
67
]
(see also
[
69
,
25
,
17
,
19
]
), there exists a function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that every graph of treewidth at least
f(k)
contains as a minor the
(k\times k)
-grid
Γ_{k}.
13
13

13

The
(k\times k)
-grid is defined as the Cartesian product of two paths on
k
vertices.

As
{\sf tw}(Γ_{k})\geq k
(see e.g.,
[
14

), it follows that the singleton
\{Γ\},
where
Γ=\langle Γ_{k}\rangle_{k\in\mathbb{N}},
is a
\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}
-universal obstruction of
{\sf tw}.

Using our terminology, it follows that
{\sf obs}_{\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf tw},k})
contains a planar graph, for every

This means that by considering one planar graph from each
{\sf obs}_{\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf tw},k}),k\in\mathbb{N},
we define the obstruction set of the classes
\mathcal{G}_{{\sf p}^{\prime},k},k\in\mathbb{N},
of a graph parameter
{\sf p}^{\prime}
that is equivalent to
{\sf tw}.

In other words, among the (exponentially many, according to
[
65
]
) obstructions in
{\sf obs}_{\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}}(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf tw},k}),

just one of them
is sufficient to determine the approximate behaviour of treewidth and this holds for
every
other parameter that is equivalent to treewidth (see
[
40

).
The same phenomenon has been observed for many other parameters for which similar approximate characterizations are known.
It is also easy to observe that the class
\mathcal{G}_{\text{\rm{planar}}}
of all planar graphs is exactly the class of all minors of grids (see e.g.,
[
69

We conclude with the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

The set
\{\Gamma\}

Moreover,

Theorem 3.44

6.2
Two examples of immersion-monotone parameters

In this subsection we consider graphs that may contain multiple edges but no loops.
For this, given a graph
G,
we see its edge set as a multiset.
We use
\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}^{\mathsf{e}}
to denote the class of all graphs with multiple edges.

Let
G
be a graph and let
e_{1}=\{x,y\}
and
e_{2}=\{y,z\}
be two edges of
G
where
y
is a common endpoint of both.
The result of the operation of
lifting
the edges
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
in
G
is the graph obtained from
G
if we first remove
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
from
G
and then add the edge
\{x,z\}.

As we deal with multigraphs, we agree that if any of
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
has multiplicity bigger than two, then its removal reduces its multiplicity.
Also if the edge
\{x,z\}
already exists, then we just increase its multiplicity by one.
Moreover, if
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are parallel edges then the lifting operation simply removes both of them.
That is because we consider multigraphs with only multiple edges and therefore we suppress all loops that may arise from the lifting operation.

We define the

We say that a graph

denoted by

Observe that if

G

Recall that Robertson and Seymour proved in
[

is a wqo in

This directly implies that for every

7
Conclusion

In this paper we introduced a parametric framework in an attempt to bridge the gap between finite obstruction characterizations for
\leqslant
-class properties and the study of graph parameters.
We remark that all concepts and results of
Section 2
,
Section 3
, and
Section 5

can be directly transferred to any kind of (finite) structures where some notion of “size” can be defined. This may certainly include directed graphs, hypergraphs, matroids, and other combinatorial structures.

A first remark is that the definition of a
\leqslant
-universal obstruction for a parameter
{\sf p}
does not require that
{\sf p}
is monotone under some quasi-ordering
\leqslant
on graphs.
The choice of such a “
\leqslant
” for a graph parameter so that
\leqslant
is a well-quasi-ordering on graphs is not always easy (or even possible).
An indicative example is the graph parameter
tree-partition treewidth
, denoted by
\mathsf{tpw}
where, given a graph
G,

\mathsf{tpw}(G)
is the minimum
k
such that there is a partition
\{X_{t}:t\in V(T)\}
of
V(G),
indexed by the vertices of a tree
T
such that (a)
|X_{t}|\leq k,
for every
t\in V(T)
and (b) for every edge
e=\{x,y\}
of
G,
if
x\in X_{t}
and
y\in X_{t^{\prime}},
then either
\{t,t^{\prime}\}\in E(T)
or
t=t^{\prime}.

In
[
26
]
, Ding and Oporowski gave a finite
\leqslant_{\mathsf{tp}}
-universal obstruction for
\mathsf{tpw}
where
\leqslant_{\mathsf{tp}}
is the topological minor relation.
Interestingly, neither
\mathsf{tpw}(G)
is topological minor-monotone (see
[
26
]
) nor
\leqslant_{\mathsf{tp}}
is a wqo on all graphs.
This indicates that, for particular parameters, (finite) universal obstructions may exist even without the general requirements of
Theorem 5.12
about
{\sf p}
and
\leqslant
.

As we have seen in
Subsection 5.2
, to have a general theory that guarantees the existence of
finite

\leqslant
-universal obstructions we need that
\leqslant
is an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering on graphs.
For the minor (
\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}
) and the immersion (
\leqslant_{\mathsf{i}}
) relation, this is an important and wide open question in order theory
[
64
,
70
]
.
Especially for the minor relation, the greatest known advance in this direction is the celebrated result of Thomas in
[
82
]
, stating that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N},

\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}
is
\omega^{2}
-wqo when we restrict to graphs of treewidth at most
k,
i.e.,
(\mathcal{G}_{{\sf tw},k},\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}})
is an
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering.
In fact, Thomas proved that
\leqslant_{\mathsf{m}}
is a
better-quasi-ordering
in the more general case of infinite graphs that exclude as a minor some finite planar graph.
We avoid here the technical definition of better-quasi-ordering; we only mention that it implies
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering.
A consequence of this is that when we restrict our universe of graphs to bounded treewidth graphs, it is indeed the case that all minor-universal-obstructions are finite.
We hope that the present work may motivate towards resolving the
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering conjecture for the minor relation (and perhaps also for other relations that are known to be well-quasi-orderings on all graphs).

We conclude with the remark that independently of the status of the
\omega^{2}
-well-quasi-ordering conjecture for the minor or immersion relation, it is still important to develop theories that can automatically produce finite obstructions sets for wide families of parameters.
An example of such a theory is the Erdős–Pósa property for minors: given a (finite) set of graphs
\mathcal{Z}
we define the parameter
\mathsf{apex}_{\mathcal{Z}}\colon\mathcal{G}_{{\text{\rm{all}}}}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathsf{apex}_{\mathcal{Z}}(G)
is the minimum size of a set
S
of vertices such that none of the graphs in
{\mathcal{Z}}
is a minor of
G-S
(that is the graph obtained from
G
if we remove the vertices of
S
).
According to the results of
[
74
]
(see also
[
84
]
for an optimized version), if
\mathcal{Z}
contains a planar graph then the set
\{\langle k\cdot Z\rangle_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mid Z\in\mathcal{Z}\}
is a (finite) minor-universal obstruction for
\mathsf{apex}_{\mathcal{Z}}.

Employing the parametric viewpoint introduced in this paper,
[
62
,
63
]
extend these results to arbitrary
\mathcal{Z}
as a result of providing a finite minor-universal obstruction for
\mathcal{H}
-treewidth
14
14

14

\mathcal{H}
-treewidth is a natural extension of treewidth that measures the tree decomposability of a graph into small pieces and pieces belonging to a fixed class
\mathcal{H}
.

[
30
]
, for every minor-closed class
\mathcal{H}.

Is it possible to prove similar results about the existence of finite universal obstructions for other families of parameters and obtain finite class obstruction sets for families of class properties?
Such kind of general conditions have been investigated for obstructions of minor-closed graph classes (see e.g.,
[
78
,
56
,
55
,
3
]
).
It is an open challenge whether analogous combinatorial or algorithmic results may be developed for parametric obstructions, even without the a priori knowledge that they are finite.

BV operators of the Gerstenhaber algebras of holomorphic polyvector fields on toric varieties
Yang Deng, Wei Hong

Abstract
. The vector space of holomorphic polyvector fields on any complex manifold has a natural Gerstenhaber algebra structure. In this paper, we study BV operators of the Gerstenhaber algebras of holomorphic polyvector fields on smooth compact toric varieties. We give a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of BV operators of the Gerstenhaber algebra of holomorphic polyvector fields on any smooth compact toric variety.



1.
introduction

Polyvector fields appeared in the recent work of many mathematicians.
Barannikov and Kontsevich
[
B-K98
]
show that polyvector fields play important roles in deformation theory and mirror symmetry.
Polyvector fields also appeared in the work of Bandiera, Stiénon and Xu
[
B-S-Xu19
]
.
In differential geomtry, holomorphic polyvector fields appeared in the work of Hitchin
[
Hitchin11
]
.
It is well known that the vector space of polyvector fields on a smooth manifold
M
has
a Gerstenhaber algebra structure by the wedge product and the Schouten bracket of polyvector fields. In the same way, the vector space of holomorphic polyvector fields on a complex manifold
X
becomes a Gerstenhaber algebra.
And the Gerstenhaber algebra structure of polyvector fields on the algebraic torus is studied in
[
M-R19
]
.

This paper is a subsequent work of
[
Hong20
]
. For any smooth compact toric variety
X
, the vector space of holomorphic polyvector fields on
X
has been given an explicit description in
[
Hong20
]
.
In this paper, we study Batalin-Vilkovisky operators for the Gerstenhaber algebras of holomorphic polyvector fields on smooth compact toric varieties.
We give a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of BV operators.
And we also give the explicit forms of the BV operators.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Gerstenhaber algebra and Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra

Let us recall some classical knowledge of Gerstenhaber algebra and Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra (BV algebra). One may consult
[
Roger09
]
and
[
Xu99
]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{k}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space over a field

\mathbf{k}
. For any element
a\in\mathcal{A}^{k}
, we denote by
|a|=k
the degree of
a
.
Let
\mathcal{A}[1]=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}[1]^{k}
, where
\mathcal{A}[1]^{k}=\mathcal{A}^{k+1}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A graded vector spcae
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{k}
is a Gerstenhaber algebra if one has:

(1)

An associative, graded commutative multiplication

\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A},

ı.e., for every
a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

•

a\cdot(b\cdot c)=(a\cdot b)\cdot c
,

•

a\cdot b=(-1)^{|a||b|}b\cdot a
.

(2)

A graded Lie algebra bracket
[,]:\mathcal{A}[1]\times\mathcal{A}[1]\rightarrow\mathcal{A}[1]
, ı.e.,
we have
[\mathcal{A}^{k},\mathcal{A}^{l}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{k+l-1}
, and for every
a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
,

•

[a,b]=-(-1)^{(|a|-1)(|b|-1)}[b,a]
(graded anti-symmetry),

•

(-1)^{(|a|-1)(|c|-1)}[[a,b],c]+(-1)^{(|b|-1)(|a|-1)}[[b,c],a]+(-1)^{(|c|-1)(|b|-1)}[[c,a],b]=0
(graded Jacobi identity).

(3)

The multiplication and bracket satisfying the Leibniz relation, ı.e.,
for every
a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
,

[a,b\cdot c]=[a,b]\cdot c+(-1)^{(|a|-1)|b|}b\cdot[a,c].

We have the following examples of Gerstenhaber algebras.

Example 2.2
.

Let
M
be a smooth real manifold. Let

\mathcal{A}^{k}=\begin{cases}\Gamma(M,\wedge^{k}TM),\quad k>0;\\
C^{\infty}(M),\quad k=0;\\
0,\quad k<0.\end{cases}

Then
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{k}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space over
\mathbb{R}
.
Let the multiplication on
\mathcal{A}
be the wedge product on
\Gamma(M,\wedge^{k}TM)
.
The bracket
[,]
is given by the Schouten bracket of polyvector fields.
Then
(\mathcal{A},\wedge,[,])
is a Gerstenhaber algebra.

Example 2.3
.

Let
X
be a complex manifold.
Let
\mathcal{A}^{k}=H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X})
be the vector space of holomorphic
k
-vector fields on
X
for
k>0
. Let
\mathcal{A}^{0}=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be the vector space of holomorphic functions on
X
. And let
\mathcal{A}^{k}=0
for
k<0
.
Then
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{k}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let the multiplication on
\mathcal{A}
be the wedge product of polyvector fields.
The bracket
[,]
is given by the Schouten bracket of polyvector fields.
Then
(\mathcal{A},\wedge,[,])
is a Gerstenhaber algebra.

Next we give two equivalent definitions of Batalin-Vilkovisky algebras.

Definition 2.4
.

A graded vector space
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{k}
over
\mathbf{k}
is a
Batalin-Vilkovisky algebra (BV algebra) if

(1)

\mathcal{A}
is an associative, graded commutative algebra, i.e., there is an associative, graded commutative multiplication on
\mathcal{A}
.

(2)

There is a differential
\mathbf{k}
-linear operator
D:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
of order
2
and degree
-1
.
Here
D
is a differential operator of order
2
in the sense that, for all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{A}
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle D(abc)=D(ab)c+(-1)^{|a|}aD(bc)+(-1)^{|b|(|a|+1)}bD(ac)-D(a)bc-(-1)^{|a|}aD(b)c-(-1)^{|a|+|b|}abD(c)

(3)

D^{2}=0
.

A BV algebra
(\mathcal{A},\cdot,D)
is always a Gerstenhaber algebra with the same multiplication, and the bracket is given by

[a,b]=(-1)^{|a|}(D(ab)-D(a)b-(-1)^{|a|}aD(b))

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.

The following is an equivalent definition of BV algebra.

Definition 2.5
.

Given a Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A},\cdot,[,])
, if there is exist a degree
-1

\mathbf{k}
-linear operator
D:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
such that

(1)

(2.2)

[a,b]=(-1)^{|a|}(D(ab)-D(a)b-(-1)^{|a|}aD(b))

for any
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
,

(2)

D^{2}=0
,

then the Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A},\cdot,[,])
is said to be exact,
and
D
is called a generating operator.

That a Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A},\cdot,[,])
is exact is equivalent to that
(\mathcal{A},\cdot,D)
is a BV algebra. And the generating operator
D
is also called a BV operator.

2.2.
Holomorphic polyvector fields on toric varieties

Recall that a toric variety
[
1
]
is an irreducible variety
X
such that

(1)

T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
is a Zariski open set of
X
,

(2)

the action of
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
on itself extends to an action of
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
on
X
.

One may consult
[
3
]
and
[
2
]
for more details of toric varieties.

Let
N=\Hom(\mathbb{C}^{*},T)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
M=\Hom(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})
.
Then
M\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,\mathbb{Z})
and
N\cong\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.
Each element
m
in
M
gives rise to a character
\chi^{m}\in Hom(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})
,
which can be also considered as a rational function on
X
.
Let
N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}

be the dual space of
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
X=X_{\Delta}
be a smooth compact toric variety associated with a fan
\Delta
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The
T
-action on
X
induces a
T
-action on
H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X})
,
the vector space of holomorphic
k
-vector fields on
X
.
Denote by
V_{I}^{k}
the weight space corresponding to the character
I\in M
.
We have

(2.3)

H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X})=\bigoplus_{I\in M}V_{I}^{k}.

Since
H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X})
is a finite dimensional vector space,
there are only finite elements
I\in M
such that
V_{I}^{k}\neq 0
.

Some technical notations are necessary for us in this paper.

(1)

Let
N_{\mathbb{C}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
.
Since
T\cong N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we have
\text{Lie}(T)\cong N_{\mathbb{C}}
,
where
\text{Lie}(T)
denotes the Lie algebra of
T
.
We define a map
\rho:N_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(X)
by

(2.4)

\rho:N_{\mathbb{C}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\cong\text{Lie}(T)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(X),

where
\text{Lie}(T)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(X)
is defined by the infinitesimal action of the Lie algebra
\text{Lie}(T)
on
X
.
And the map
\rho:N_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(X)
is determined by

\rho(x)(\chi^{m})=\langle x,m\rangle\chi^{m}

for any
x\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
and
m\in M
.
Since the action map
T\times X\rightarrow X
is holomorphic,
the images of
\rho
are holomorphic vector fields on
X
.
By the abuse of notation, the induced maps

(2.5)

\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X}):\quad x_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge x_{k}\rightarrow\rho(x_{1})\wedge\ldots\wedge\rho(x_{k}),\quad\forall x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in N_{\mathbb{C}},

are also denoted by
\rho
for
2\leq k\leq n
.
Let
W=\rho(N_{\mathbb{C}})
,
W^{k}=\wedge^{k}W
and
W^{0}=\mathbb{C}
.
Then
W^{k}
is a subspace of
H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X})
.
And the map
\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{\rho}W^{k}
is an isomorphism.

(2)

Let
\Delta(k)

(0\leq k\leq n)
be the set of all
k
-dimensional cones in

\Delta
.
Suppose that
\alpha_{t}~(1\leq t\leq r)
are all one dimensional cones in
\Delta(1)
.
Let
e(\alpha_{t})\in N

(1\leq t\leq r)
be the corresponding primitive elements, i.e., the unique generator of
\alpha_{t}\cap N
.
Let
P_{\Delta}
be a polytope in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

P_{\Delta}=\bigcap_{\alpha_{t}\in\Delta(1)}\{I\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle I,e(\alpha_{t})\rangle\geq-1\}.

Since
X_{\Delta}
is compact,
P_{\Delta}
is a compact polytope in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let

S_{\Delta}=\{I\mid I\in M\cap P_{\Delta}\}.

Then
S_{\Delta}
is a non-empty finite set and
0\in S_{\Delta}
.
We denote by
P_{\Delta}(i)
the set of all
i
-dimensional faces of the polytope
P_{\Delta}
. Let

S(\Delta,i)=\bigcup_{F_{j}\in P_{\Delta}(n-i)}\{I\in int(F_{j})\cap M\}

for
0\leq i\leq n
, where
int(F_{j})
denotes the relative interior of the face
F_{j}
.
Let

S_{k}(\Delta)=\bigcup_{0\leq i\leq k}S(\Delta,i)

for all
0\leq k\leq n
.

(3)

For any
I\in S_{\Delta}
, there exists a unique face
F_{I}(\Delta)
of
P_{\Delta}

such that
I\in int(F_{I}(\Delta))\cap M
.
Let

E_{I}(\Delta)=\{e(\alpha_{t})\mid\alpha_{t}\in\Delta(1)~\text{and}~\langle I,e(\alpha_{t})\rangle=-1\}.

Suppose that
E_{I}(\Delta)=\{e(\alpha_{s_{1}}),\ldots e(\alpha_{s_{l}})\}
,
where
1\leq s_{1}<\ldots<s_{l}\leq r
.
Let
F^{\perp}_{I}(\Delta)\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
be the normal space of
F_{I}(\Delta)
,
defined by

F^{\perp}_{I}(\Delta)=\sum_{1\leq t\leq l}\mathbb{R}\cdot e(\alpha_{s_{t}}).

And we set

F^{\perp}_{I}(\Delta)=0\quad\text{if}\quad E_{I}(\Delta)=\varnothing.

(4)

Suppose that
I\in S(\Delta,i)
.
Then we have
\dim F_{I}(\Delta)=n-i
and
\dim F^{\perp}_{I}(\Delta)=i
.
Considering
F_{I}^{\perp}(\Delta)\otimes\mathbb{C}
as a subspace of
N_{\mathbb{C}}
,
we have
\wedge^{i}(F^{\perp}_{I}(\Delta)\otimes\mathbb{C})\cong\mathbb{C}
.

Let
\hat{F}_{I}(\Delta)
be the affine space in
M_{\mathbb{C}}
with the lowest dimension such that
F_{I}(\Delta)\subseteq\hat{F}_{I}(\Delta)
, where
F_{I}(\Delta)\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
is considered as a subset of
M_{\mathbb{C}}
in the natural way. Then we have

(2.6)

\hat{F}_{I}(\Delta)=\{I+\alpha\mid\alpha\in(F^{\perp}_{I}(\Delta)\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\perp}\},

where
(F^{\perp}_{I}(\Delta)\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\perp}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}
is the annihilator of

F_{I}^{\perp}(\Delta)\otimes\mathbb{C}\subseteq N_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Especially, if
I\in M
is a vertex of
P_{\Delta}
, then we have
\hat{F}_{I}(\Delta)=\{I\}
.

(5)

Let

N_{I}^{k}(\Delta)=\begin{cases}\wedge^{i}(F_{I}^{\perp}(\Delta)\otimes\mathbb{C})\wedge(\wedge^{k-i}N_{\mathbb{C}})\quad&\text{for}~0<i\leq k,\\
\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}}&\text{for}~i=0,\\
0\quad&\text{for}~i>k.\end{cases}

Then
N_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
is a subspace of
\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}}
.
We have to notice that

N_{I}^{k}(\Delta)\begin{cases}\neq 0\quad\text{for}~~I\in S_{k}(\Delta)\\
=0\quad\text{for}~~I\notin S_{k}(\Delta)\\
\end{cases}

Moreover, we have

(2.7)

N_{I}^{n}(\Delta)=\wedge^{n}N_{\mathbb{C}}

for all
I\in S_{\Delta}
.

(6)

Let

W_{I}^{k}(\Delta)=\rho(N_{I}^{k}(\Delta))

be a subspace of
H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X})
.
We define

V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)=\chi^{I}\cdot W_{I}^{k}(\Delta)=\{\chi^{I}\cdot w\mid w\in W_{I}^{k}(\Delta)\}.

The elements in
V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
are considered as meromorphic
k
-vector fields on
X=X_{\Delta}
. In
[
Hong20
]
, it is proved that for any
I\in S_{k}(\Delta)
, the

k
-vector fields in
V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
have moveable singularities. And we use them to represent the corresponding holomorphic
k
-vector fields.

The next theorem gives a description of the holomorphic polyvector fields on toric varieties.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
Hong20
]

Let
X=X_{\Delta}
be a smooth compact toric variety associated with a fan
\Delta
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then we have the following decomposition

(2.8)

H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X})=\bigoplus_{I\in S_{k}(\Delta)}V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)

for all
0\leq k\leq n
, where
V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)=V_{-I}^{k}
for all
I\in S_{k}(\Delta)
.

2.3.
The Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A}_{T},\wedge,[,])

In the case of
X=T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
, the Gerstenhaber algebra structure of the polyvector fields (in algebraic version) has been studied in
[
M-R19
]
.
Let us recall the results.

Any
k
-polyvector field (in algebraic version) on
X=T
can be written as the finite sum

\sum_{I\in M}c_{I}\cdot\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A_{I}),

where
c_{I}\in\mathbb{C}
,
A_{I}\in\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let

\mathbb{C}[M]=\{\sum_{I\in M}c_{I}\cdot\chi^{I}\mid c_{I}\in\mathbb{C},~~~\text{and there are only finite $c_{I}\neq 0$ in the sum}\},

which can be considered as the group algebra of the abelian group
M
.

Let

\mathcal{A}_{T}^{k}=\mathbb{C}[M]\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}},

where
0\leq k\leq n
.
Then the vector space of
k
-polyvector field (in algebraic version) on
X=T
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}_{T}^{k}
by the map

\sum_{I\in M}c_{I}\cdot\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A_{I})\longleftrightarrow\sum_{I\in M}c_{I}\cdot\chi^{I}\otimes A_{I}

Let

\mathcal{A}_{T}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{k}_{T},

where
\mathcal{A}_{T}^{k}=0
for
k<0
or
k>n
.
The Gerstenhaber algebra structure of the polyvector fields on
X=T
can be described in the following way.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
M-R19
]

(1)

The vector space
(\mathcal{A}_{T},\cdot,[,])
is a Gerstenhaber algebra, with the multiplication and the bracket given by the following way.

•

The multiplication on
\mathcal{A}_{T}
is given by

(\chi^{I}\otimes A_{I})\cdot(\chi^{J}\otimes A_{J})=\chi^{I+J}\otimes(A_{I}\wedge A_{J}),

where
I,J\in M
,
A_{I}\in\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}},A_{J}\in\wedge^{l}N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

•

The bracket
[,]
on
\mathcal{A}_{T}
is given by

[\chi^{I}\otimes A_{I},\chi^{J}\otimes A_{J}]=(-1)^{k+1}\chi^{I+J}\otimes((\imath_{J}A_{I})\wedge A_{J}+(-1)^{k}A_{I}\wedge(\imath_{I}A_{J})),

where
I,J\in M
,
A_{I}\in\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}},A_{J}\in\wedge^{l}N_{\mathbb{C}}
, and

\imath_{J}(A_{I})
is the contraction of
J\in M\subset M_{\mathbb{C}}

with
A_{I}\in\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

Let
D:\mathcal{A}_{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{T}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-linear operator given by

D(\chi^{I}\otimes A_{I})=-\chi^{I}\otimes(\imath_{I}A_{I}),

where
I\in M
and
A_{I}\in\wedge^{k}N_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
D
is a BV operator for the Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A}_{T},\cdot,[,])
.

Remark 2.8
.

In the second part of Proposition
2.7
, the original statement in
[
M-R19
]

is that
-D
is a BV operator for the Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A}_{T},\cdot,-[,])
,
where the bracket on
\mathcal{A}_{T}
is choosing to be

-[,]
(negative the bracket
[,]
), the operator
D
, the bracket
[,]
and the multiplication
\cdot
is shown in the first part of Proposition
2.7
.

3.
BV operators of the Gerstenhaber algebras of holomorphic polyvector fields on toric varieties

3.1.
The Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta},\wedge,[,])

Let
X=X_{\Delta}
be a smooth compact toric variety.
Let
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\Delta}=H^{0}(X,\wedge^{k}\mathcal{T}_{X})
be the vector space of holomorphic
k
-vector fields on
X
for
k>0
.
Let
\mathcal{A}^{0}_{\Delta}=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=\mathbb{C}
and
let
\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\Delta}=0
for
k<0
and
k>n
.
Let

\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\Delta}.

As shown in Example
2.3
,
(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta},\wedge,[,])
is a Gerstenhaber algebra.

By Theorem
2.6
, we have

(3.1)

\mathcal{A}^{k}_{\Delta}=\bigoplus_{I\in S_{k}(\Delta)}V_{I}^{k}(\Delta).

The elements in
V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
can be written as

\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A_{I})
, where
I\in S_{k}(\Delta)
and
A_{I}\in N_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(1)

The Gerstenhaber algebra structure on
(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta},\wedge,[,])
can be described by the following way. For any
\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A_{I})\in V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
and
\chi^{J}\cdot\rho(A_{J})\in V_{J}^{l}(\Delta)
, we have

\displaystyle(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A_{I}))\wedge(\chi^{J}\cdot\rho(A_{J}))=\chi^{I+J}\cdot\rho(A_{I}\wedge A_{J}),

\displaystyle[\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A_{I}),\chi^{J}\cdot\rho(A_{J})]=(-1)^{k+1}\chi^{I+J}\cdot\rho((\imath_{J}A_{I})\wedge A_{J}+(-1)^{k}A_{I}\wedge(\imath_{I}A_{J})),

where
I\in S_{k}(\Delta),A_{I}\in N_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
, and
J\in S_{l}(\Delta),A_{J}\in N_{J}^{l}(\Delta)
.
As a consequence, we have
V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)\wedge V_{J}^{l}(\Delta)\subseteq V_{I+J}^{k+l}(\Delta)
and

[V_{I}^{k}(\Delta),V_{J}^{l}(\Delta)]\subseteq V_{I+J}^{k+l-1}(\Delta)
.

(2)

Let
\gamma:\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{T}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map defined by

\gamma(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A_{I}))=\chi^{I}\otimes A_{I},

where
\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A_{I})\in V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
,
I\in S_{k}(\Delta)

and
A_{I}\in N_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
.
Then
\gamma:\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{T}
is an injective map.
And
\gamma:(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta},\wedge,[,])\rightarrow(\mathcal{A}_{T},\wedge,[,])
is a morphism of Gerstenhaber algebras. Thus
(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta},\wedge,[,])
can be considered as a
sub-Gerstenhaber algebra of
(\mathcal{A}_{T},\wedge,[,])
.

Proposition
3.1
can be proved in a similar way to Proposition
2.7
, which can be find in
[
M-R19
]
. Here we skip the details.

3.2.
BV operators of the Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta},\wedge,[,])

Let
X=X_{\Delta}
be a smooth compact toric variety.
A natural question is when the Gerstenhaber algebra

(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta},\wedge,[,])
will be exact?
In other words, is there any degree
-1

\mathbb{C}
-linear operator

D:\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}
such that

(1)

(3.2)

[a,b]=(-1)^{|a|}(D(a\wedge b)-D(a)\wedge b-(-1)^{|a|}a\wedge D(b))

for any
a,b\in\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}
,

(2)

(3.3)

D^{2}=0.

Since
\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}^{k}=0
for
k<0
and
k>n
, we only need to consider the linear maps

D:\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}^{k}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\Delta}^{k-1}
in the case of
1\leq k\leq n
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D
be a BV operator on the Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A}_{\Delta},\wedge,[,])
.
Then there exists an element
\delta\in M_{\mathbb{C}}
such that,
for all
\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)\in V_{I}^{k}(\Delta)

(3.4)

D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A))=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A),

where

and
A\in N_{I}^{k}(\Delta)
.

Proof.

•

Let us consider the linear map

.
Let

be defined by

(3.5)

\langle\delta,x\rangle=D(\rho(x)),

for all

•

First, we prove the lemma in the case of
k=n
.
In Equation (
3.2
), let
b=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)
, where

and
A\in N_{I}^{n}(\Delta)=\wedge^{n}N_{\mathbb{C}}
;
and let
a=\rho(x)
, where

.
By proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle[a,b]=[\rho(x),\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)]=\langle I,x\rangle\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A),

where
\langle I,x\rangle
is the pairing of

I\in S_{\Delta}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}
and
x\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
.
On the right hand of Equation (
3.2
),
we have
a\wedge b=0
since that the complex dimension of

is
n
,
and we have
D(a)=D(\rho(x))=\langle\delta,x\rangle
by Equation (
3.5
).
By Equation (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\langle I,x\rangle\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)=\langle\delta,x\rangle)\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)-\rho(x)\wedge D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)),

which implies

(3.6)

\langle\delta-I,x\rangle\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)=\rho(x)\wedge D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)).

On the other hand, we have
x\wedge A=0
, which implies

\displaystyle\imath_{\delta-I}(x\wedge A)=\langle\delta-I,x\rangle A-x\wedge(\imath_{\delta-I}A)=0.

Therefore we have

\langle\delta-I,x\rangle A=x\wedge(\imath_{\delta-I}A).

As a consequence, we get

(3.7)

\displaystyle\langle\delta-I,x\rangle\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\langle\delta-I,x\rangle A)=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(x\wedge(\imath_{\delta-I}A))=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(x)\wedge\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A).

By Equation (
3.6
) and Equation (
3.7
), we get

(3.8)

\rho(x)\wedge(D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A))-\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A))=0.

By Proposition
3.1
and Equation (
3.8
), we have

(3.9)

x\wedge\gamma(D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A))-\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A))=0

for all
x\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence

\gamma(D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A))-\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A))=0.

As a consequence, we get

D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A))=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A).

Therefore, we proved the lemma for

•

Next we prove the lemma by recursion. Suppose the lemma holds in the case of

(l\geq 2)

In Equation (
3.2
), let
b=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)
, where

;

, where

.
By Proposition

, we have

\displaystyle[a,b]=[\rho(x),\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)]=\langle I,x\rangle\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A).

On the right hand of Equation (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle D(a\wedge b)=D(\rho(x)\wedge(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)))=D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(x\wedge A))=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}(x\wedge A)),

\displaystyle D(a)\wedge b=D(\rho(x))\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)=\langle\delta,x\rangle\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A),

\displaystyle a\wedge D(b)=\rho(x)\wedge D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)).

By Equation (
3.2
), we have

\langle I,x\rangle\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)=-\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}(x\wedge A))+\langle\delta,x\rangle\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)-\rho(x)\wedge D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A)).

As a consequence, we have

\displaystyle\rho(x)\wedge D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A))=\chi^{I}\cdot(\langle\delta-I,x\rangle\rho(A)-\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}(x\wedge A)))

\displaystyle=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(x\wedge\imath_{\delta-I}A)=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(x)\wedge\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A)=\rho(x)\wedge(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A)).

Therefore, we have

\rho(x)\wedge(D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A))-\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A))=0

for all
x\in N_{\mathbb{C}}
.
By similar reason as we have shown in the case of
k=n
, we get

D(\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(A))=\chi^{I}\cdot\rho(\imath_{\delta-I}A).

Thus if the lemma holds in the case of

.

Lemma 3.3

(A basic fact of linear algebra)
.

Let
V
be a
n
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbf{k}
,
and

be a

-dimensional subspace of

be a nonzero element in

, we have that

if and only if

, where

.

.

(2)

If

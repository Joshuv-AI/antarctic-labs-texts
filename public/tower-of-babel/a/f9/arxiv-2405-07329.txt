Abstract
. We extend the affine inequalities on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} for Sobolev functions in W s , p W^{s,p} with 1 ≤ p < n s 1\leq p<\frac{n}{s} obtained recently by Haddad–Ludwig [ 16 , 17 ] to the remaining range p ≥ n s p\geq\frac{n}{s} . For each value of s s , our results are stronger than affine Moser–Trudinger and Morrey inequalities. As a byproduct, we establish the analog of the classical L p L^{p} Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu inequalities related to the Moser–Trudinger case p = n p=n . Our main tool is the affine invariant provided by Besov polar projection bodies.

2.1.
Symmetrization

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set of finite measure. The
Schwarz symmetral

E^{\star}
of
E
is the closed centered Euclidean ball with
|E^{\star}|=|E|
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable function with corresponding level sets
\{f\geq t\}
of finite measure for every
t>0
. Note that
f
can be recovered from
\{f\geq t\}
via the so-called

:

f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{f\geq t\}}(x)\,dt,

where
\mathbf{1}_{E}
denotes the indicator function of the measurable set
E
. This representation can be applied to introduce the concept of Schwarz symmetral: Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function such that
\{|f|\geq t\}
has finite measure for every

. Then
f^{\star}
, the
Schwarz symmetral
of

, can be introduced as

f^{\star}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{|f|\geq t\}^{\star}}(x)\,dt,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Note that
f^{\star}
is a radially decreasing functions with
|\{|f|\geq t\}|=|\{f^{\star}\geq t\}|
. The function
f^{\star}
is also commonly known as spherically symmetric rearrangement.

2.2.
Star bodies

Let

be a star-shaped (with respect to the origin) set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with corresponding gauge function defined by

\|x\|_{K}=\inf\{\lambda>0:x\in\lambda K\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It is plain to see that

, can be expressed as

|K|=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{K}(\xi)^{n}\,d\xi,

where
15
15

15

We assume that
\rho_{K}
is a measurable function.

\rho_{K}
is defined by

\rho_{K}(x)=\|x\|_{K}^{-1}=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0:\lambda x\in K\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

The star-shaped set

is said to be a

if

is a strictly positive and continuous function on

.

Let

and

(3.22)

\int_{\Omega_{f}}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{p}_{Z_{p}^{*}K}\,dx\lesssim\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}^{p}}{t^{p}}\leq\sup_{t>0}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}^{p}}{t^{p}}.

Putting together (
3.16
) and (
3.22
), we get

\sup_{t>0}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}}{t}\approx\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{f}}\|\nabla f(x)\|^{p}_{Z_{p}^{*}K}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It remains to show that

(3.23)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}}{t}=\sup_{t>0}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}}{t}.

Let

L=\liminf_{t\to 0}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}}{t}

and choose a sequence of positive numbers
\{t_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
t_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
and

(3.24)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t_{j})_{p}}{t_{j}}=L.

For any
t>0
, we let
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k_{j}t_{j}\leq t<(k_{j}+1)t_{j}
. Note that
\lim_{j\to\infty}k_{j}t_{j}=t
. Hence, taking into account the continuity of
\omega_{K}(f,t)
as a function of
t
and using (
3.12
) and (
3.24
), we have

\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}}{t}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,k_{j}t_{j})_{p}}{k_{j}t_{j}}\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t_{j})_{p}}{t_{j}}=L.

This implies
\sup_{t>0}\frac{\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}}{t}\leq L
and the proof of (
3.23
) is complete.
∎

An important result in classical theory of function spaces claims that moduli of smoothness does not increase under symmetrizations. Namely, for every
t>0
,

(3.25)

\omega(f^{\star},t)_{p}\leq\omega(f,t)_{p},

where
\omega(f,t)_{p}
is given by (
3.4
). See
[
21
]
and the extensive list of references given there. Next we extend this result from
K=B^{n}
to the general anisotropic setting.

Proposition 3.26
.

Assume that
f\in L^{p},1\leq p<\infty
. Then, for each
t>0
,

(3.27)

\omega_{K^{\star}}(f^{\star},t)_{p}\leq\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}.

Proof.

We first observe that it is enough to show the validity of (
3.27
) for non-negative functions. Indeed, the case of general functions
f
can be reduced to the latter since
\omega_{K}(|f|,t)_{p}\leq\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}
. Then we restrict our attention to non-negative functions
f
.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\|h\|_{K}<t}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\,dh

\displaystyle=\int_{\|h\|_{K}<t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x+h)-f(x)|^{p}\,dx\,dh

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\|x-y\|_{K}<t}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}\,dy\,dx

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(y)-f(x)|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{Z_{t}}(x-y)\,dy\,dx,

where
Z_{t}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|z\|_{K}<t\}
. Furthermore, we find that

(3.29)

z\in Z_{t}\iff z\in tK.

Indeed, it is clear that
z\in tK
yields
\|z\|_{K}\leq t
. Conversely, if
\|z\|_{K}<t
then there exists
\lambda<t
such that
z\in\lambda K
, i.e.,
z=\lambda k
for some
k\in K
. In particular,
z=t\frac{z}{t}=t\frac{\lambda k}{t}
and
\frac{\lambda k}{t}\in K
, since
\lambda/t\in(0,1)
and
k\in K
(recall that
K
is a star body).

In view of (
3.29
), one can rewrite (
3.28
) as

(3.30)

\int_{\|h\|_{K}<t}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\,dh=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx.

Taking into account that
f
is non-negative, one can write
17
17

17

Recall that
f_{+}=\max\{f,0\}
and
f_{-}=-\min\{f,0\}
.

(f(x)-f(y))_{+}^{p}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{f<r\}}(y)\,dr.

The case
p=1
in the previous formula should be adequately interpreted as
(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}=\mathbf{1}_{\{f\geq r\}}(x)
. Then, by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-f(y))_{+}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{f<r\}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx\,dr

(3.31)

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}(1-\mathbf{1}_{\{f\geq r\}}(y))\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx\,dr.

For every
r,t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}(1-\mathbf{1}_{\{f\geq r\}}(y))\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip-199.16928pt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{f\geq r\}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx

(3.32)

\displaystyle\hskip-199.16928pt=t^{n}|K|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{f\geq r\}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx,

where the last step is justified by the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\,dx<\infty
. The latter follows since
f\in L^{p}
and therefore
\{f>r\}
has finite measure. Moreover, applying symmetrization properties, we can write

|K|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\,dx=|K^{\star}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f^{\star}(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\,dx.

On the other hand, the second term in (
3.32
) can be estimated using Riesz’s rearrangement inequality. Indeed

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{f\geq r\}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f^{\star}(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq r\}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{tK^{\star}}(x-y)\,dy\,dx.

Accordingly

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}(1-\mathbf{1}_{\{f\geq r\}}(y))\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip-199.16928pt\geq t^{n}|K^{\star}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f^{\star}(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f^{\star}(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq r\}}(y)\mathbf{1}_{tK^{\star}}(x-y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip-199.16928pt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f^{\star}(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}(1-\mathbf{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq r\}}(y))\mathbf{1}_{tK^{\star}}(x-y)\,dy\,dx,

where the last step follows from (
3.32
) (after replacing
f
and
K
by
f^{\star}
and
K^{\star}
, respectively). Therefore (
3.31
) can be estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-f(y))_{+}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle\geq p\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f^{\star}(x)-r)_{+}^{p-1}(1-\mathbf{1}_{\{f^{\star}\geq r\}}(y))\mathbf{1}_{tK^{\star}}(x-y)\,dy\,dx\,dr

(3.33)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y))_{+}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{tK^{\star}}(x-y)\,dy\,dx,

where the last step follows from (
3.31
) (after replacing
f
and
K
by
f^{\star}
and
K^{\star}
, respectively).

On the other hand, since

(f(x)-f(y))_{-}^{p}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}(r-f(y))_{-}^{p-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{f<r\}}(x)\,dr

one can apply the above reasoning line by line in order to get (cf. (
3.33
))

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(x)-f(y))_{-}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y))_{-}^{p}\mathbf{1}_{tK^{\star}}(x-y)\,dy\,dx.

Taking into account that
|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}=(f(x)-f(y))^{p}_{+}+(f(x)-f(y))^{p}_{-}
(and analogously for

), we can invoke (
3.33
)-(
3.34
) to achieve

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{tK}(x-y)\,dy\,dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f^{\star}(x)-f^{\star}(y)|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{tK^{\star}}(x-y)\,dy\,dx,

or in other words (cf. (

) and its counterpart in terms of

and

)

\int_{\|h\|_{K}<t}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\,dh\geq\int_{\|h\|_{K^{\star}}<t}\|\Delta_{h}f^{\star}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\,dh.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Anisotropic Besov norms

As can be seen in
[
2
,
9
,
33
,
35
]
, Besov spaces are cornerstones in the theory of function spaces. There are several available approaches to introduce these spaces. Among them, the method relying on moduli of smoothness is of special relevance (cf. Remark
3.36
below). We start this subsection by presenting anisotropic versions of Besov spaces, which are naturally introduced using anisotropic moduli of smoothness.

Definition 3.35
.

Let
K
be a star body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
s\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. The
anisotropic Besov space

B^{s}_{p,q;K}
is formed by all
f\in L^{p}
such that

\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;K}}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty

(where the usual modification is made if
q=\infty
).

Remark 3.36
.

If
K=B^{n}
then
B^{s}_{p,q;K}=B^{s}_{p,q}
, the classical Besov space given by

(3.37)

\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\omega(f,t)_{p}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
\omega(f,t)_{p}
is defined by (
3.4
).

Now we show that anisotropic Besov spaces extend anisotropic fractional Sobolev spaces of Ludwig
[
23
,
24
]
: the space
W^{s,p}_{K},\,s\in(0,1),\,p\in[1,\infty)
, is equipped with

\|f\|_{W^{s,p}_{K}}=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{\|x-y\|_{K}^{sp+n}}\,dx\,dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The case
K=B^{n}
corresponds to the classical Gagliardo (semi-)norm (
1.3
).

Proposition 3.38
.

We have

B^{s}_{p,p;K}=W^{s,p}_{K}

with

\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,p;K}}=(|K|(sp+n))^{-\frac{1}{p}}\,\|f\|_{W^{s,p}_{K}}.

Proof.

Changing the order of integration, we derive

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,p;K}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sp}\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}^{p}\,\frac{dt}{t}=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sp-n}\int_{\|h\|_{K}<t}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\,dh\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\int_{\|h\|_{K}}^{\infty}t^{-sp-n}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,dh=\frac{1}{|K|(sp+n)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}}{\|h\|_{K}^{sp+n}}\,dh

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|K|(sp+n)}\,\|f\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}_{K}}.

∎

Theorem 3.39
.

Let
s\in(0,1),p\in[1,\infty),
and
q\in[1,\infty]
. If
f\in B^{s}_{p,q;K}
then

(3.40)

\|f^{\star}\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;K^{\star}}}\leq\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;K}}.

In particular, letting
p=q
(cf.
[
16
, Theorem 11]
)

\|f^{\star}\|_{W^{s,p}_{K^{\star}}}\leq\|f\|_{W^{s,p}_{K}}.

Proof.

It is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.26
.
∎

Anisotropic Besov spaces can be characterized in terms of differences as follows.

Proposition 3.41
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty)
. We have

\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;K}}\approx\bigg(\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\|\Delta_{h}f\|_{L^{p}}^{q}}{\|h\|_{K}^{sq+n}}\,dh\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where the hidden equivalence constants depend only on
n
.

Proof.

By virtue of Proposition
3.5
,

\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}\approx\bigg(\frac{1}{t^{n}|K|}\int_{\|h\|_{K}<t}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\,dh\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where equivalence constants depend only on
n
. Then, a change of the order of integration yields

\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;K}}^{q}\approx\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq-n}\int_{\|h\|_{K}<t}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\,dh\,\frac{dt}{t}\approx\frac{1}{|K|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|h\|_{K}^{-sq-n}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\,dh.

∎

3.3.
Besov polar projection bodies: definition and basic properties

We extend the notion of the fractional polar projection body
\Pi^{*,s}_{p}
due to Haddad and Ludwig
[
16
,
17
]
to the Besov polar projection body
\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}
. Such an extension is made in a natural way, in the same fashion as fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}
are part of the more general class formed by Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}
; recall that
W^{s,p}=B^{s}_{p,p}
(cf. Proposition
3.38
).

To avoid repetition, throughout this subsection, we always assume that
s\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty)
.

Definition 3.42
.

For a measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
,
Besov polar projection bodies

\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f
are defined as the star-shaped sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with gauge function given by

\|\xi\|^{sq}_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\|\Delta_{t\xi}f\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 3.43
.

It is obvious that
\Pi^{*,s}_{p,p}f=\Pi^{*,s}_{p}f
, cf. (
2.2
).

Remark 3.44
.

The set
\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f
is invariant under translations of
f
and volume preserving affine transformations:

(3.45)

\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}(f\circ\phi)=\phi^{-1}\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f

for every
\phi\in\text{SL}(n)
. Indeed, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\|\xi\|^{sq}_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}(f\circ\phi)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\|\Delta_{t\xi}(f\circ\phi)\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\|\Delta_{t\phi\xi}f\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}=\|\phi\xi\|_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}}^{sq}.

The relation between the measure of Besov polar projection bodies and classical Besov norms is given in the following result.

Proposition 3.46
.

The following inequality holds

n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n+sq}{n}}|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f|^{-\frac{sq}{n}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|h|^{-sq}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\,\frac{dh}{|h|^{n}}.

In particular

(3.47)

|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f|^{-\frac{sq}{n}}\lesssim\|f\|^{q}_{B^{s}_{p,q}}.

Proof.

Applying Hölder’s inequality with exponent
\rho=1+\frac{sq}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|^{sq}_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}\,d\xi\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|^{-\frac{sq\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}}_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}\,d\xi\bigg)^{\frac{\rho}{\rho^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\|\Delta_{t\xi}f\|_{L^{p}}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,d\xi\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|^{-n}_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}\,d\xi\bigg)^{\frac{sq}{n}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|h|^{-sq}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\,\frac{dh}{|h|^{n}}\bigg)(n|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f|)^{\frac{sq}{n}}.

Note that
|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|=n\omega_{n}
. Then we have shown that

n\omega_{n}^{\frac{n+sq}{n}}\leq\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|h|^{-sq}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\,\frac{dh}{|h|^{n}}\bigg)|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f|^{\frac{sq}{n}}.

∎

Proposition 3.48
.

We have

(3.49)

\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}}}=\omega_{n}^{-\frac{s}{n}}|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}|^{\frac{s}{n}}\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}},

and

(3.50)

\frac{1}{4(2^{n}+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\leq\bigg(\frac{n}{sq+n}\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}}\leq 4(2^{n}+1)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Proof.

We first note that
\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}
is a ball since
f^{\star}
is radially symmetric (use that
\|\Delta_{tA\xi}f^{\star}\|_{L^{p}}=\|\Delta_{t\xi}f^{\star}\|_{L^{p}}
if
A\in O(n)
). In particular
\|h\|_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}}=\omega_{n}^{1/n}|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}|^{-1/n}|h|
, that yields to the following relation between anisotropic and classical moduli of smoothness (cf. (
3.4
))

\displaystyle\omega_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}}(f,t)_{p}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}|t^{n}}\int_{|h|<t|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}|^{1/n}\omega_{n}^{-1/n}}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dh\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\omega(f,t|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}|^{1/n}\omega_{n}^{-1/n})_{p}.

Inserting this into the definition of anisotropic Besov space given in Definition
3.35
and making a change of variables, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\omega_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}}(f,t)_{p}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\omega(f,t|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}|^{\frac{1}{n}}\omega_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n}})_{p}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}^{-\frac{s}{n}}|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f^{\star}|^{\frac{s}{n}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\omega(f,t)_{p}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

This proves (
3.49
).

Let us now show (
3.50
). We concentrate on the upper estimate, since the lower estimate can be obtained using similar ideas. It follows from (
3.6
) and Fubini’s theorem that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;K}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\omega_{K}(f,t)_{p}^{q}\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 4(2^{n}+1)^{1/q}|K|^{-\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq-n}\int_{\|h\|_{K}<t}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,dh\,\frac{dt}{t}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=4(2^{n}+1)^{1/q}|K|^{-\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\int_{\|h\|_{K}}^{\infty}t^{-sq-n}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,dh\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=4(2^{n}+1)^{1/q}|K|^{-\frac{1}{q}}(sq+n)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|h\|_{K}^{-sq-n}\|\Delta_{h}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\,dh\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Then, using polar coordinates,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;K}}

\displaystyle\leq 4(2^{n}+1)^{1/q}|K|^{-\frac{1}{q}}(sq+n)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|_{K}^{-sq-n}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-sq}\|\Delta_{t\xi}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}}\,\frac{dt}{t}\,d\xi\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=4(2^{n}+1)^{1/q}|K|^{-\frac{1}{q}}(sq+n)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|_{K}^{-sq-n}\|\xi\|_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}^{sq}\,d\xi\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

In particular, letting
K=\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f
and using (
2.1
), the previous estimate reads as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}}

\displaystyle\leq 4(2^{n}+1)^{1/q}|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f|^{-\frac{1}{q}}(sq+n)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}^{-n}\,d\xi\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=4(2^{n}+1)^{1/q}|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f|^{-\frac{1}{q}}(sq+n)^{-\frac{1}{q}}(n|\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f|)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=4\bigg(\frac{n(2^{n}+1)}{sq+n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

This proves the upper estimate in (
3.50
).
∎

Remark 3.51
.

Constants in (
3.50
) are not sharp. For instance, one can carry out the above argument but now relying on (
3.7
) (rather than (
3.6
)) and then

\bigg(\frac{n}{sq+n}\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}}\leq 1\qquad\text{if}\qquad p\leq q

and

\bigg(\frac{n}{sq+n}\bigg)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q;\Pi^{*,s}_{p,q}f}}\geq 1\qquad\text{if}\qquad p\geq q.

In the special case
p=q
, from Proposition
3.38
and Remark
3.43
, we derive the following formula

n^{-\frac{1}{p}}|\Pi^{*,s}_{p}f|^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{W^{s,p}_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p}f}}=1.

3.4.
The Pólya–Szegő inequality for Besov polar projection bodies

The classical Pólya–Szegő inequality for Besov norms asserts that (cf. (
3.25
) and (
3.37
))

(3.52)

\|f^{\star}\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}\leq\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}.

Next we establish the affine version of this inequality.

Theorem 3.53
.

We have

(3.54)

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{1,\frac{c_{p}}{3}\text{w}(\Omega,\xi)^{-1}t\bigg\}\|f\|_{L^{p}}

This proves the desired estimate (
1.22
).

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. From (
3.15
), we derive

\displaystyle\|\xi\|^{ps}_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p}f}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-ps-1}\|\Delta_{t\xi}f\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-ps-1}\min\{2\|f\|_{L^{p}},t\|\nabla f\cdot\xi\|_{L^{p}}\}^{p}\,dt

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\frac{2\|f\|_{L^{p}}}{\|\nabla f\cdot\xi\|_{L^{p}}}}t^{p(1-s)-1}\,dt\bigg)\|\nabla f\cdot\xi\|^{p}_{L^{p}}+2^{p}\bigg(\int_{\frac{2\|f\|_{L^{p}}}{\|\nabla f\cdot\xi\|_{L^{p}}}}^{\infty}t^{-ps-1}\,dt\bigg)\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{p(1-s)}}{ps(1-s)}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{p(1-s)}\|\nabla f\cdot\xi\|_{L^{p}}^{ps}=\frac{2^{p(1-s)}}{ps(1-s)}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{p(1-s)}\|\xi\|_{\Pi^{*}_{p}f}^{ps}.

Therefore

\displaystyle|\Pi^{*,s}_{p}f|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|_{\Pi^{*,s}_{p}f}^{-n}\,d\xi\gtrsim(s(1-s))^{\frac{n}{sp}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{-\frac{(1-s)n}{s}}\,\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\|\xi\|_{\Pi^{*}_{p}f}^{-n}\,d\xi=(s(1-s))^{\frac{n}{sp}}\,\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{-\frac{(1-s)n}{s}}|\Pi^{*}_{p}f|.

This yields (recall that
f\in W^{s,p}\subset L^{\infty}
)

\displaystyle|\Pi^{*,s}_{p}f|^{-\frac{s}{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(s(1-s))^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}}^{1-s}|\Pi^{*}_{p}f|^{-\frac{s}{n}}\leq(s(1-s))^{-\frac{1}{p}}|\text{supp }f|^{\frac{1-s}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-s}|\Pi^{*}_{p}f|^{-\frac{s}{n}}.

Inserting (
CLYZM
) in the last estimate, we find

|\Pi^{*,s}_{p}f|^{-\frac{s}{n}}\lesssim(s(1-s))^{-\frac{1}{p}}|\text{supp }f|^{\frac{1-s}{n}}|\Pi^{*}_{p}f|^{-\frac{1}{n}},

i.e., (
1.23
) holds.

The relation (
1.24
) is an immediate application of (
1.5
). ∎

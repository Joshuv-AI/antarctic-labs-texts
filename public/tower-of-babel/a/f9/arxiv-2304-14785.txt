Improved estimates for the sharp interface limit of the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation with space-time white noise
Ľubomír Baňas, Jean Daniel Mukam

Abstract
. We study the sharp interface limit of the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation with cubic double-well potential and additive space-time white noise ϵ σ ​ W ˙ \epsilon^{\sigma}\dot{W} , where ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 is an interfacial width parameter. We prove that, for sufficiently large scaling constant σ > 0 \sigma>0 , the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation converges to the deterministic Mullins-Sekerka/Hele-Shaw problem for ϵ → 0 \epsilon\rightarrow 0 . The convergence is shown in suitable fractional Sobolev norms as well as in the L p L^{p} -norm for p ∈ ( 2 , 4 ] p\in(2,4] in spatial dimension d = 2 , 3 d=2,3 . This generalizes the existing result for the space-time white noise to dimension d = 3 d=3 and improves the existing results for smooth noise, which were so far limited to p ∈ ( 2 , 2 ​ d + 8 d + 2 ] p\in\left(2,\frac{2d+8}{d+2}\right] in spatial dimension d = 2 , 3 d=2,3 . As a byproduct of the analysis of the stochastic problem with space-time white noise, we identify minimal regularity requirements on the noise which allow convergence to the sharp interface limit in the ℍ 1 \mathbb{H}^{1} -norm and also provide improved convergence estimates for the sharp interface limit of the deterministic problem.



1.
Introduction

We consider the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation with additive space-time white noise

(1)

\displaystyle du^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\Delta\left(-\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}f(u^{\epsilon})\right)dt+\epsilon^{\sigma}dW(t)\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }\mathcal{D}_{T}:=(0,T)\times\mathcal{D},

\displaystyle\partial_{\vec{n}}u^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\vec{n}}\Delta u^{\epsilon}=0

\displaystyle\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\mathcal{D},

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}(0)

\displaystyle=u^{\epsilon}_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in }\mathcal{D},

where
\sigma>0
,
T>0
are fixed constants and
\epsilon>0
is a (small) interfacial width parameter.
For simplicity we take
\mathcal{D}=(0,1)^{d}
to be the unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
,
with
\vec{n}
the outer unit normal to
\partial\mathcal{D}
and
W
is the space-time white noise.
The nonlinearity
f
in (
1
) ensures that asymptotically the solutions
\{u^{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon>0}
of (
1
) remain within the physically meaningful range
-1\leq u^{\epsilon}\leq 1
.
One of the most widely used choices is
f(u)=F^{\prime}(u)=u^{3}-u

where
F(u)=\frac{1}{4}(u^{2}-1)^{2}
is a double-well potential with minima at
\pm 1
.

Formally, equation (
1
) can be equivalently written in the mixed form

(2)

\begin{array}[]{rclll}du^{\epsilon}&=&\Delta w^{\epsilon}dt+\epsilon^{\sigma}dW(t)&\text{in}\;\mathcal{D}_{T},\\
w^{\epsilon}&=&-\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}dt+\frac{1}{\epsilon}f(u^{\epsilon})&\text{in}\;\mathcal{D}_{T},\end{array}

where
w^{\epsilon}
is the so-called chemical potential. For sufficiently smooth noise and data the
chemical potential
w^{\epsilon}
has sufficient regularity so that the formulation (
2
) can be made rigorous, cf.
[
2
]
.

The Cahn-Hilliard equation is a prototype model for mass conservative phase separation and
coarsening phenomena in binary alloys
[
7
,
8
]
.
The solution
u^{\epsilon}
of (
1
) is an order parameter
which approaches
\pm 1
in the regions occupied by the pure phases. The pure phases are separated by a
thin layer where
|u^{\epsilon}|<1
, so-called diffuse interface,
with thickness proportional to the (small) interfacial width parameter
\epsilon
.
It has been observed in
[
12
]
that
the simulation results obtained with the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation with the space-time white noise
are in better agreement with physical experiments than those obtained by deterministic simulations.

In the deterministic setting (i.e., when
W\equiv 0
in (
1
)) the Cahn-Hilliard equation reads as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcllll}\partial_{t}u^{\epsilon}_{D}&=&\Delta w^{\epsilon}_{D}&\text{ in }\mathcal{D}_{T},\\
w^{\epsilon}_{D}&=&-\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}_{D}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}f(u^{\epsilon}_{D})&\text{ in }\mathcal{D}_{T}.\end{array}

The sharp interface limit of the deterministic Cahn-Hilliard equation has been analyzed in
[
1
]
, where
it was shown that, for
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, the function
w^{\epsilon}_{D}
tends to a function
v
, which together with the free boundary
\Gamma_{t}:=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\Gamma_{t}^{\epsilon}

(where
\Gamma_{t}^{\epsilon}:=\{x\in\mathcal{D}:\;{u^{\epsilon}_{D}(t,x)=0}\}
,
t\in(0,T)
) satisfies the Mullins-Sekerka/Hele-Shaw problem:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllll}\Delta v&=0&\text{in}\;\mathcal{D}\setminus\Gamma_{t},\\
\partial_{\vec{n}}v&=0&\text{on}\;\partial\mathcal{D},\\
v&=\lambda H&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{t},\\
\mathcal{V}&=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\vec{n}_{\Gamma}}v^{+}-\partial_{\vec{n}_{\Gamma}}v^{-})&\text{on}\;\Gamma_{t},\\
\Gamma_{0}&=\Gamma_{00},\end{array}

where
H
is the mean curvature of
\Gamma_{t}
,
\mathcal{V}
is the normal velocity of the interface,

\vec{n}_{\Gamma}
is the unit normal vector to
\Gamma_{t}
and
v^{+}
,
v^{-}
are respectively the restriction
of
v
on
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{t}
,
\mathcal{D}^{-}_{t}
(the exterior and interior of
\Gamma_{t}
in
\mathcal{D}
).

In the stochastic setting with trace class noise, it is shown in
[
4
]
that, for suitable scaling of the noise, the sharp interface limit of the Cahn-Hilliard equation is the deterministic Hele-Shaw model (
1
) (cf.
[
3
]
for the case of multiplicative noise). The work
[
2
]
studies convergence of the numerical approximation of the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation
with smooth noise to the sharp interface limit and also obtains the first result on uniform pointwise convergence to the deterministic Hele-Shaw problem (
1
).
The sharp interface limit (
1
) of the Cahn-Hilliard equation with singular space-time white noise has been studied
in
[
6
]
. We note that all aforementioned results (also including the present work)
rely on the spectral estimate for the deterministic problem, cf.
[
1
]
,
the use of which requires appropriate scaling of the noise with respect to the interfacial width parameter
\epsilon
.
We also mention the recent work
[
5
]

which employs a (discrete) stochastic counterpart of the principal eigenvalue problem to
derive a posteriori error estimates for the numerical approximation of the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation
and
[
13
]
where a stochastic Hele-Shaw problem is obtained as the sharp-interface limit of the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation with (unscaled) smooth-in-time noise.

Throughout this paper we assume that for a given smooth closed hypersurface
\Gamma_{00}\subset\mathcal{D}

the Hele-Shaw problem (
1
) admits a smooth solution
(v,\{\Gamma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]})
.
Under this assumption, it is possible to construct an approximation
(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}},w^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}})
of (
1

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rclll}\partial_{t}u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}&=&\Delta w^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}&\text{in}\;\mathcal{D}_{T},\\
w^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}&=&-\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}f(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}})+r^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}&\text{in}\;\mathcal{D}_{T},\end{array}

with the same boundary conditions as in (
1
), cf.,
[
1

.
Furthermore, for any
K>0
and

\displaystyle k>(d+2)\frac{d^{2}+6d+10}{4d+16},

the following estimates hold, cf.
[
1
, Theorems 2.1 & 4.12]
and
[
1

:

(12a)

\displaystyle\|r^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|_{C(\mathcal{D}_{T})}\leq C\epsilon^{K-2},\quad\|w^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}-v\|_{C(\mathcal{D}_{T})}\leq C\epsilon,

(12b)

\displaystyle\quad\|u^{\epsilon}_{D}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{p})}\leq C\epsilon^{k}\quad\text{ for }p\in\left(2,\frac{2d+8}{d+2}\right],

For

.
This convergence result is suboptimal in the case of the double-well potential where
u^{\epsilon},u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\in L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})
.

In the stochastic setting with trace-class noise one has the following error estimates, cf.,
[
4
, Theorem 3.10]
:

(13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{p})}\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma}\right\}\right)\geq 1-C_{l}\epsilon^{l},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{-1})}\leq C\epsilon^{g(\sigma,\gamma)}\right\}\right)\geq 1-C_{l}\epsilon^{l},

(15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})}\leq C\epsilon^{h(\sigma,\gamma)}\right\}\right)\geq 1-C_{l}\epsilon^{l},

for suitable

,

, where
u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}
is the (deterministic) solution of (
1
) and

is as in (

).
Hence, as in the deterministic setting, the best spaces in which the convergence for the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation takes place in
d=2
and
d=3

are
L^{3}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{3})
and
L^{\frac{14}{5}}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{\frac{14}{5}})
, respectively.

The sharp interface limit of the two dimensional stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation driven by singular space-time white noise was recently analyzed
in
[
6
]
where error estimate (
13
) (with
p=3
) and (
14
) were obtained.
Due to regularity restrictions, an analogue of the error estimate (
15
)
is not available in the case of space-time white noise.

In the previous works on the sharp interface limits of the deterministic and the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation
[
1
,
4
]
, the following inequality (see
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
)
was employed to estimate the nonlinearity:

(16)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{D}}\epsilon^{-1}\mathcal{N}(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}},v)v\leq C\epsilon^{-1}\|v\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{p}},\quad p\in(2,3],

where
\mathcal{N}(u,v):=f(u+v)-f(u)-f^{\prime}(u)v
.
The above estimate is combined with dimension dependent interpolation inequalities
which yield suboptimal dimension dependent estimates for the double-well nonlinearity.
In the stochastic case with the space-time white noise,
an analogous approach restricts the analysis to spatial dimension
d=2
, cf.
[
6
]
.

The (probabilistically) strong variational solution of the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation enjoys the following regularity : (i)
u\in L^{4}\left(\Omega;C([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{4})\right)\cap L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})\right)
for
sufficiently regular trace class noise (see
[
9
, Proposition 2.2]
) and (ii)
u\in L^{4}\left(\Omega;C([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{4})\right)\cap L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2-\frac{d}{2}-\vartheta})\right)

for arbitrary
\vartheta>0
for space-time white noise (see Theorem
3.1
below).
In the present work,
instead of employing the general formula (
16
), we estimate the double-well nonlinearity by an explicit calculation
and use a new interpolation inequality (Lemma
4.5
below). This approach yields
error estimates which are optimal with respect to the aforementioned regularity of the solution of the (stochastic) Cahn-Hilliard equation and also allows us to generalize the analysis to the case
of the space-time white noise in dimension
d=3
.

The main contributions of the present paper are the following.

(i)

We prove (

) for

for any

(ii)

(iii)

We derive an analogue of the error estimate (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2-\frac{d}{2}-\vartheta})}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma}{3}-4}\right\}\right)\geq 1-C_{\delta,\eta}\epsilon^{\delta+\eta}-C_{\vartheta,\kappa}\epsilon^{\vartheta+\kappa},

for any

and for

Note that it is not clear whether an error estimate in

(iv)

We identify minimal regularity properties of the noise required for the

We adopt the approach of

]

which is based on introducing the stochastic convolution (

In order to obtain estimates that are robust with respect to the interfacial width parameter

By

\mathbb{L}^{2}

(.,.)

.
For

The Neumann Laplace operator

is self-adjoint, positive and has compact resolvent.
We consider an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{L}^{2}

consisting of eigenvectors
\{e_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
of the Neumann Laplacian, with corresponding eigenvalues
(\lambda_{j})
such that

Note that for

\lambda_{k}
satisfies
\lambda_{k}\simeq|k|^{2}
, where
|k|^{2}=\lambda_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}^{2}
and
it holds that

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\lambda_{j}^{\alpha}<+\infty\quad\text{iff}\quad\alpha<-\frac{d}{2}.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and

we define the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
as

(18)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\lambda^{s}_{j}u_{j}e_{j}\quad\text{for}\;u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}u_{j}e_{j},

where the domain of
(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
is given by

\displaystyle D((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}):=\left\{u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}u_{j}e_{j}:\;\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\lambda_{j}^{s}u_{j}^{2}<\infty\right\}.

We introduce the seminorm and semiscalar product

\displaystyle|v|_{s}=\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|\quad\text{and}\quad(u,v)_{s}=\left((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u,(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\right)\quad u,v\in D((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}})

\displaystyle\|v\|_{s}=\left(|v|^{2}_{s}+m^{2}(v)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad v\in D((-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}).

For
s\in[0,2]
the norm
\|.\|_{s}
is equivalent to the usual norm on
\mathbb{H}^{s}
and

is a closed subspace of
\mathbb{H}^{s}
,
see, e.g.,
[
9

.

The term

\displaystyle W(x,t)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\beta_{j}(t)e_{j}(x),

3.
Existence and regularity of the solution

In this section we summarize existence and regularity properties of the solution of the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1
)
with space-time white noise.

We introduce the stochastic convolution

(20)

Z^{\epsilon}(t):=\epsilon^{\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\epsilon\Delta^{2}}dW(s)=\epsilon^{\sigma}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{i}^{2}(t-s)\epsilon}e_{i}d\beta_{i}(s)\quad t\in[0,T].

The following two lemmas will be useful in the rest of this paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
p\in[1,\infty)
, there exists a constant
C=C(p)\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Z^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\right]\leq C(p)\epsilon^{(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})p}.

Proof.

Taking the expectation in both sides of (
20
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Z^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\right]=\epsilon^{\sigma p}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{i}^{2}(t-s)\epsilon}d\beta_{i}(s)e_{i}(x)\right|^{p}\right]dx.

Using the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality
[
10
, Theorem 4.36]
and the uniform boundedness of
(e_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Z^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\epsilon^{\sigma p}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}{\left(e^{-\lambda_{i}^{2}(T-s)\epsilon}\right)^{2}}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\epsilon^{\sigma p}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2\lambda_{i}^{2}(T-s)\epsilon}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\epsilon^{(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})p}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\leq C(p)\epsilon^{(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})p},

where in the last step we used the fact that
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\lambda_{i}^{-2}<\infty
, since
d=2,3
, see (
17
).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
\vartheta>0
,
p\geq 2
, there is a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}Z^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{p}\right]\leq C\epsilon^{\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)p}.

Proof.

From (
20
) and (
18
), it follows that

(21)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}Z^{\epsilon}(t)=\epsilon^{\sigma}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{1-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}e^{-\lambda_{i}^{2}(t-s)\epsilon}e_{i}d\beta_{i}(s),\quad t\in[0,T].

Taking the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-norm in (
21
), raising to power
p
, using the embedding
\mathbb{L}^{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{2}
(
p\geq 2
), taking the supremum, the expectation and using the BDG inequality
[

, Proposition 4.36]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}Z^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{p}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}Z^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\sigma p}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda_{i}^{1-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}e^{-\lambda_{i}^{2}(t-s)\epsilon}d\beta_{i}(s)e_{i}(x)\right|^{p}\right]dx

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\sigma p}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}\lambda_{i}^{2-\frac{d}{2}-\vartheta}e^{-2\lambda_{i}^{2}(T-s)\epsilon}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})p}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{d}{2}-\vartheta}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\leq C\epsilon^{(\sigma-\frac{1}{2})p},

where in the last step we used the fact that
\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{d}{2}-\vartheta}<\infty
, see (
17
).
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u^{\epsilon}_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{-1}
, then there exists a unique strong variational solution

of (

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{-1})\right)\cap L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2-\frac{d}{2}-\vartheta})\right)\cap L^{4}\left(\Omega;L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})\right),

Furthermore, for any

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{p}(u^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{-1})}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\|u^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})}+\epsilon\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leq C\epsilon^{-1}.

From
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
, we have
\|u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}-u^{\epsilon}_{D}\|_{C^{1}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}\leq C\epsilon
.
Using triangle inequality, (
57
) and Theorem
6.1
below, it follows that

(58)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}):=\|u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{-1})}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\|u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})}+\epsilon^{4}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leq C\epsilon^{-1}.

Next, we consider the following subpace
\widetilde{\Omega}_{1}\subset\Omega

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Omega}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\;\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{-1})}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 28.45274pt+\epsilon^{4}\|(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}(u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma-1}\right\}.

By Theorem
4.1
, it holds
\mathbb{P}[\widetilde{\Omega}_{1}^{c}]\leq C_{\delta,\eta}\epsilon^{\delta+\eta}+C_{\vartheta,\kappa}\epsilon^{\vartheta+\kappa}
. We set

\displaystyle{\tt Err}_{\mathrm{A}}:=

\displaystyle\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{-1})}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})}

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{4}\|(-\Delta)^{1-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\vartheta}{2}}(u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, triangle inequality,
\mathcal{E}_{4}(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}})\leq C\mathcal{E}(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}})^{2}

(by the embedding
\mathbb{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2-\frac{d}{2}-\vartheta}
), (
3.1
) (with
p=4
) and (
58
), it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\tt Err}_{\mathrm{A}}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}1\!\!1_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{1}}{\tt Err}_{A}d\mathbb{P}(\omega)+\int_{\Omega}1\!\!1_{\widetilde{\Omega}^{c}_{1}}{\tt Err}_{\mathrm{A}}d\mathbb{P}(\omega)

(59)

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma-1}+C\left(\mathbb{P}[\widetilde{\Omega}^{c}_{1}]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{4}(u^{\epsilon})+\mathcal{E}(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{A}})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma-1}+C_{\delta,\eta}\epsilon^{\frac{\delta+\eta-1}{2}}+C_{\vartheta,\kappa}\epsilon^{\frac{\vartheta+\kappa-1}{2}}.

The statement of the theorem then follows from (
4
), (
56
) and Theorem
6.1

by the triangle inequality.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
4.1
we obtain
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. convergence of the solution
u^{\epsilon}
of the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation to the solution of the deterministic Hele-Shaw problem
(
1
) in the sense that
\{(t,x)\in\mathcal{D}_{T}:\,\,t\in(0,T),\,\,\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}u^{\epsilon}(t,x)\rightarrow\pm 1\}
respectively converge to the exterior and interior of the interface
\{\Gamma_{t}\}_{t\in(0,T)}
.
The proof of the result follows along the lines of
[
6
, Corollary 4.5]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

There exists a subsequence
\{\epsilon_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u^{\epsilon_{k}}=1-2\chi_{\mathcal{D}^{-}}\quad\text{in}\;L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{p})\text{ for }p\in(2,4],

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. on

, where

5.
Limiting case with
\mathbb{H}^{1}
spatial regularity

In this section we consider the stochastic Cahn-Hilliard equation with a slightly more regular noise

(60)

\displaystyle du^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\Delta\left(-\epsilon\Delta u^{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}f(u^{\epsilon})\right)dt+\epsilon^{\sigma}d\widetilde{W}(t)\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}_{T},

with the noise of the form

(61)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(t,x)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}q_{i}e_{i}(x)\beta_{i}(t)\quad x\in\mathcal{D},\;t\in[0,T],

where
\{q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
are such that
q_{i}\approx\lambda_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\upsilon}{2}}
and
\upsilon\in(0,1]
can be arbitrarily small.
The noise
\widetilde{W}
is more regular than the space-time white noise
W
(
19
), nevertheless
since
1-\frac{d}{2}-\upsilon\geq-\frac{d}{2}
the series (
61
) do not converge in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
.

We define the operator
Q:\mathbb{L}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{L}^{2}
as

\displaystyle Qu=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}q_{i}(u,e_{i})e_{i}\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{L}^{2}.

Noting (
18
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}Q\right)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}Qe_{i},e_{i}\right)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}q_{i}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}e_{i},e_{i}\right)\approx\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{d}{4}-\frac{\upsilon}{2}}.

For
d=3
the above identity implies that
\mathrm{Tr}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}Q\right)=\infty
, see (
17
). Hence, the condition
[
4
, Assumption 3.1]
is not satisfied for (
61
) in the case
d=3
.

Similarly to Section
3
we introduce the stochastic convolution

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}^{\epsilon}(t):=\epsilon^{\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\epsilon\Delta^{2}}d\widetilde{W}(s)=\epsilon^{\sigma}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}q_{i}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{i}^{2}(t-s)\epsilon}e_{i}d\beta_{i}(s)\quad t\in[0,T].

Analogously to Lemma
3.1
and
3.2
, for
1\leq p<\infty
one can show the estimate

(62)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\widetilde{Z}^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\nabla\widetilde{Z}^{\epsilon}(t)\|^{p}\right]\leq C(p)\epsilon^{\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)}.

Owing to the better regularity properties of the convolution (
62
) it is straightforward to modify the proof of Theorem
3.1

to show that the solution of (
60
) has the following regularity properties

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathbb{H}^{-1})\right)\cap L^{2}\left(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})\right)\cap L^{4}\left(\Omega;L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})\right),

Moreover, for any
p\geq 2
the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}(u^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[\|u^{\epsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{-1})}+\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}\|\nabla u^{\epsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{2})}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{\frac{p}{2}}}\|u^{\epsilon}\|^{2p}_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\epsilon^{-\frac{p}{2}}+\epsilon^{\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)p}+\epsilon^{\left(2\sigma-\frac{3}{2}\right)p}\right).

The remaining results of Section
4
hold true with the fractional Sobolev norm replaced by the
\mathbb{H}^{1}
norm.
In particular (
51
) improves to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{\gamma}{3}-4}\right\}\right)\geq 1-C_{\delta,\eta}\epsilon^{\delta+\eta}-C_{\upsilon,\kappa}\epsilon^{\upsilon+\kappa},

for any
\upsilon>0
.
The above estimate generalizes
[
4
, Theorem 3.10]
in the case
d=3
to the case of less regular noise.

Finally, we also obtain an analogue of the estimate in Corollary
4.1
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{-1})}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})}+\epsilon^{4}\|u^{\epsilon}-u^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma-1}+C_{\delta,\eta}\epsilon^{\frac{\delta+\eta-1}{2}}+C_{\upsilon,\kappa}\epsilon^{\frac{\upsilon+\kappa-1}{2}}.

6.
The deterministic problem

In this section we derive improved estimates for the sharp interface limit of the deterministic Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,1]
be sufficiently small,
2<r\leq\frac{8}{3}
and
u^{\epsilon}_{D}
be the solution to the deterministic Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1
). Then for any
\alpha>0
(arbitrarily small),
\gamma>\frac{(7-2\alpha)r+6\alpha-8}{r-2}
and
K
in (
12
) large enough such that
\frac{3}{2}(K-1)\geq\gamma
, there exists a constant
C=C(r)>0
independent of
\epsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{\epsilon}_{D}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}}+\epsilon^{4}\|u^{\epsilon}_{D}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1})}+\frac{13}{8\epsilon}\|u^{\epsilon}_{D}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}\|_{L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})}\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma-1}.

Remark 6.1
.

Theorem
6.1
improves
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
in the case
f(u)=u^{3}-u
and
d=3
, in the sense that here we obtain error estimates in
L^{p}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{p})
,
p\in(2,4]
, i.e., with
p
exceeding the barrier
p\leq\frac{2d+8}{d+2}
prescribed in
[
1
]
. Note that even for
d=2
the error estimates in
[
1
]
are only in
L^{3}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{3})
, while we obtain an error estimates in
L^{4}(0,T;\mathbb{L}^{4})
.

Proof.

The proof goes along the same lines as the one of Theorem
4.1
, we only sketch some details. Setting
R^{\epsilon}_{D}:=u^{\epsilon}_{D}-u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}
, with
u^{\epsilon}_{D}
being the solution to the CHE (
1
). Then
R^{\epsilon}_{D}
satisfies the following PDE

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}R^{\epsilon}_{D}=-\epsilon\Delta^{2}R^{\epsilon}_{D}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Delta(f(R^{\epsilon}_{D}+u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}})-f(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}))+\Delta r^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}},\quad R^{\epsilon}_{D}(0)=0.

Testing the above equation with
(-\Delta)^{-1}R^{\epsilon}_{D}
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}}+\epsilon\|\nabla R^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\left(f(R^{\epsilon}_{D}+u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}})-f(u^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}),R^{\epsilon}_{D}\right)+\left(r^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}},R^{\epsilon}_{D}\right)=0.

As in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}}+\epsilon^{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{2}ds+\frac{13}{8\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{4}_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}ds

(63)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{3}_{\mathbb{L}^{3}}ds+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|r^{\epsilon}_{\mathsf{A}}(s)\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{C(\mathcal{D})}ds.

We define

\displaystyle T_{\epsilon}=T\wedge\inf\left\{t\in[0,T]:\;\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{3}_{\mathbb{L}^{3}}ds>\epsilon^{\gamma}\right\},

for some
\gamma>0
, which will be specified later.

From (
6
), using (
12a
) and noting the definition of
T_{\epsilon}
, it follows for all
t\leq T_{\epsilon}
that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}}+\epsilon^{4}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{2}ds+\frac{13}{8\epsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{4}_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}ds

(64)

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma-1}+C\epsilon^{\frac{3K-5}{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma-1},

where we choose
K
large enough such that
\gamma-1\leq\frac{3K-5}{2}
.

Next, we show that
T_{\epsilon}=T
for suitable
\gamma
. We proceed by contradiction and assume that
T_{\epsilon}<T
. Then for all
t\leq T_{\epsilon}
, using Lemma
4.5
with
\alpha>0
and (
6
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{3}_{\mathbb{L}^{3}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{4}_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}ds+C\epsilon^{r\alpha-3\alpha}\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{\frac{4-r}{2}}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{\frac{3r-4}{2}}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}ds

(65)

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma+\alpha}+C\epsilon^{r\alpha-3\alpha}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}}^{\frac{4-r}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{\frac{3r-4}{2}}_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma+\alpha}+C\epsilon^{r\alpha-3\alpha}\epsilon^{\left(\frac{4-r}{4}\right)(\gamma-1)}\epsilon^{4-3r}\epsilon^{\left(\frac{3r-4}{4}\right)(\gamma-1)}

\displaystyle=C\epsilon^{\gamma+\alpha}+C\epsilon^{\frac{r}{2}\gamma+\frac{(2\alpha-7)r}{2}+4-3\alpha}.

The right hand side of (
6
) is bounded above by
\epsilon^{\gamma}
for sufficiently small
\epsilon
, if we have
\frac{r}{2}\gamma+\frac{(2\alpha-7)r}{2}+4-3\alpha>\gamma
, i.e. if
\gamma>\frac{(7-2\alpha)r+6\alpha-8}{r-2}
. Hence for such value of
K
and
\gamma
we have
\int_{0}^{t}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(s)\|^{3}_{\mathbb{L}^{3}}ds\leq\epsilon^{\gamma}
, which contradicts the definition of
T_{\epsilon}
. Hence
T_{\epsilon}=T
. It then follows from (
6
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{-1}}+\epsilon^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla R^{\epsilon}_{D}(t)\|^{2}dt+\frac{13}{18\epsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\|R^{\epsilon}_{D}(t)\|^{4}_{\mathbb{L}^{4}}dt\leq C\epsilon^{\gamma-1},

which completes the proof.
∎

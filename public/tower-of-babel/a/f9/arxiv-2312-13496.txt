Decay properties of Vlasov fields on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds
Anibal Velozo Ruiz, Renato Velozo Ruiz

Abstract
. In this paper, we study pointwise decay estimates in time for Vlasov fields on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds. We prove optimal decay estimates in time for the spatial density induced by Vlasov fields on these geometric backgrounds in dimension two. First, we show exponential decay for Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space supported away from the zero velocity set. In contrast, we obtain inverse polynomial decay for general Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space. In the second part of the article, we prove exponential decay for Vlasov fields on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds supported away from the zero velocity set. The proofs are obtained through a commuting vector field approach. We exploit the hyperbolicity of the geodesic flow in these geometric backgrounds, by making use of a commuting vector field in the unstable invariant distribution of phase space.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the evolution in time of collisionless many-particle systems on a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
. We consider collisionless many-particle systems described statistically by a distribution function satisfying a transport equation on phase space. More precisely, we investigate the linear dynamics of the solutions
f:[0,\infty)\times T\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
to the
Vlasov equation on a Riemannian manifold

(\mathcal{M},g)
, given by

\partial_{t}f+Xf=0,

in terms of the generator of the geodesic flow
X\in TT\mathcal{M}
of the Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
. The Vlasov equation, also known as the Liouville equation, is motivated by classical statistical mechanics. See
[
Tol79
,
LP81
]
for further details.

The Vlasov equation on a Riemannian manifold describes the evolution in time of a collisionless system whose particles follow the trajectories set by the geodesic flow in the geometric background. The trajectories determined by the geodesic flow describe the motion of free falling particles on a Riemannian manifold. The Vlasov equation on a Riemannian manifold is motivated by its fundamental role in non-linear kinetic PDE systems on Riemannian manifolds. See
[
Gla96
,
CK02
]
for more information on PDE models arising in kinetic theory. Consider, for instance, the Vlasov–Poisson system on a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
. This kinetic model describes a collisionless system on
(\mathcal{M},g)
, for which the trajectories described by its particles are determined by the generator of the geodesic flow in
(\mathcal{M},g)
, and the mean field generated by the many-particle system. The Vlasov–Poisson system on Euclidean space
({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{x},\delta_{E})
has been extensively studied in the scientific literature due to its physical relevance
[
LP81
,
BT11
]
. See
[
DILS16
]
for more information about the Vlasov–Poisson system on hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
, and the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
.

The Vlasov equation on a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is a linear transport equation along the geodesic flow in
(\mathcal{M},g)
. Naturally, the dynamics described by Vlasov fields on a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
depend strongly on the particular form of the geodesic flow in the phase space of the background. We consider a suitable class of
non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic geometric backgrounds
for which the corresponding geodesics escape to infinity. In this article, we are specifically interested on geometric collisionless systems that are
dispersive
, in the sense that decay estimates in time hold for the
spatial density
induced by Vlasov fields
f(t,x,v)
on a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
. We define the
spatial density
on a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
, by

\rho(f)(t,x):=\int f(t,x,v)\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v),

in terms of the corresponding volume form on
T\mathcal{M}
with respect to the corresponding Riemannian metric.

Classical dispersive collisionless systems are given by Vlasov fields on Euclidean space
({\mathbb{R}}^{n},\delta_{E})
. In the PDE literature, the first decay estimates for Vlasov fields on Euclidean space were obtained by Bardos and Degond
[
BD85
]
, who proved that the spatial density decays inverse polynomially in time for compactly supported initial data. The decay estimates for the spatial density in
[
BD85
]
use the method of characteristics which exploits the explicit representation of the geodesic flow in Euclidean space. Later, Strichartz estimates were obtained for Vlasov fields on Euclidean space by Castella and Perthame
[
CP96
]
. More recently, new decay estimates for Vlasov fields on Euclidean space were obtained by Smulevici
[
Smu16
]
, who proved that the spatial density decays inverse polynomially in space and time without assuming compact support on the initial distribution. The decay estimates for the spatial density in
[
Smu16
]
use a robust vector field method that exploits commuting vector fields for the Vlasov equation on Euclidean space. The methods developed in
[
BD85
]
and
[
Smu16
]
to obtain decay estimates for the spatial density on Euclidean space, were used in these works to prove the
non-linear stability of the vacuum solution
for the Vlasov–Poisson system on Euclidean space. The
vacuum solution
for the Vlasov–Poisson system on Euclidean space is defined as the distribution
f\equiv 0
that vanishes everywhere.

Vector field methods have been used to obtain decay estimates for collisionless systems in several settings. In
[
FJS17
]
, Fajman, Joudioux, and Smulevici, developed a vector field method to prove decay estimates in space and time for velocity averages induced by
relativistic Vlasov fields
on Minkowski spacetime. The methods developed in
[
FJS17
]
were used later to prove the non-linear stability of Minkowski spacetime, as a solution of the Einstein–massive Vlasov system by Fajman et al.
[
FJS21
]
, and as a solution of the Einstein–massless Vlasov system by Bigorgne et al.
[
BFJ+21
]
. Around the same time, and independently, vector fields methods were used to prove the non-linear stability of Minkowski spacetime, as a solution of the Einstein–massless Vlasov system by Taylor
[
Tay17
]
, and as a solution of the Einstein–massive Vlasov system by Lindblad and Taylor
[
LT20
]
. We observe that the article by Taylor
[
Tay17
]
underscores the relevance of Jacobi fields in the tangent bundle of spacetime for the use of vector field methods in geometric backgrounds.

In this paper, we study the Vlasov equation on non-compact Riemannian manifolds, for which the associated geodesic flow is
hyperbolic
. The geodesic flow in a Riemannian manifold is
hyperbolic
, if there exists an invariant decomposition of the tangent of the unit tangent bundle, into three subspaces: the subspace
E_{0}
spanned by the generator of the geodesic flow, a
stable subspace

E_{s}
where the differential of the geodesic flow contracts uniformly, and an
unstable subspace

E_{u}
where the differential of the geodesic flow expands uniformly. Anosov
[
Ano67
]
proved that the class of Riemannian manifolds with bounded and strictly negative sectional curvature have hyperbolic geodesic flows. See
[
KH95
,
Pat99
]
for further details on hyperbolic geodesic flows. In particular,
hyperbolic space

{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
– the unique simply connected Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature equal to minus one – has a hyperbolic geodesic flow. The Vlasov equation on compact negatively curved Riemannian manifolds is a classical subject of study in hyperbolic dynamical systems, motivated by the rich chaotic dynamics determined by the corresponding geodesic flows. We, on the other hand, are interested in studying the Vlasov equation on Riemannian manifolds where the corresponding geodesic flow disperse.

In this article, we prove pointwise decay in time for the spatial density induced by a Vlasov field on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds. Decay estimates for the spatial density on these geometric backgrounds hold due to the lack of recurrence of the corresponding geodesic flows. We establish pointwise decay estimates for the spatial density by proving time decay for the volume of the velocity support of the distribution function. The proofs of our main results exploit the hyperbolicity of the geodesic flow to estimate the derivatives of the spatial density. We make use of commuting vector fields contained in suitable distributions of phase space

The geodesics on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds escape to infinity, thus, the main part of the analysis of Vlasov fields on these backgrounds is carried out in the far-away region. We consider asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, so the geometry in the far-away region is close to the one in hyperbolic space. With this motivation, we first prove pointwise decay in time for the spatial density induced by Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space. The hyperbolicity of the geodesic flow in

Later, we consider Vlasov fields on a non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifold

\rho(f)
and its derivatives, for Vlasov fields on
(\mathcal{M},g)

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}:=\{g_{x}(v,v)\geq\alpha^{2}\}

In other words, we obtain decay rates determined by the rates of expansion and contraction of the differential of the geodesic flow on the stable and unstable subspaces.

We also show decay estimates for the spatial density induced by Vlasov fields without the support constraint near the zero velocity set
\{g_{x}(v,v)=0\}

({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})

T{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}
becomes zero. Thus, we do not expect to obtain exponential decay for general Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space. In this setup, we obtain inverse polynomial decay of the spatial density
\rho(f)
and its derivatives, for Vlasov fields on
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
. Similar pointwise decay estimates in time hold in higher dimensions.

We investigate the Vlasov equation on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds to offer new insights on the study of stability results for geometric collisionless systems, where the corresponding Hamiltonian flows are
hyperbolic
. The decay estimates derived in this article are particularly suitable to address the non-linear stability problem of the vacuum solution for the Vlasov–Poisson system on hyperbolic space. See
[
VRVR23
,
BVRVR23
]
for related works on small data solutions for the Vlasov–Poisson system with the unstable trapping potential
\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}
, where the corresponding Hamiltonian flows are hyperbolic. A broad class of geometric collisionless systems arise in general relativity, where the corresponding distribution functions satisfy the
relativistic Vlasov equation
(see
[
And11
]
for more information). Consider, for instance, the timelike geodesic flow in de Sitter spacetime, which defines a hyperbolic flow. We hope the methods used in this paper will be helpful to understand more complicated collisionless systems.

1.1.
Vlasov fields on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds

In this section, we put in precise mathematical terms the objects we study through the paper: Vlasov fields on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds.

1.1.1.
The geometric backgrounds

First, we introduce the Riemannian manifolds
(\mathcal{M},g)
where the Vlasov fields studied in this paper are set.

Hyperbolic space

({\mathbb{H}}^{n},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})
. We consider hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})
written in the model of the upper branch of the hyperboloid

{\mathbb{H}}^{n}=\Big\{(\cosh r,(\sinh r)\gamma)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:~\gamma\in{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1},~r\geq 0\Big\}

contained in Minkowski spacetime
({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},~\eta:=-\mathrm{d}t\otimes\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}x^{1}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{1}+\dots+\mathrm{d}x^{n}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{n})
, with the Riemannian metric
g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}
induced by the Lorentzian metric
\eta
of Minkowski spacetime, given by

g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}=\mathrm{d}r\otimes\mathrm{d}r+\sinh^{2}r\mathrm{d}\gamma_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}},

in terms of the standard Riemannian metric on the unit sphere
\mathrm{d}\gamma_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
.

Non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds
(\mathcal{M},g)
.
Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be an oriented complete Riemannian manifold with bounded and strictly negative Gaussian curvature
K_{g}
. In the following, we denote the closed ball of radius
R_{0}
centered at the origin of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
by
\overline{B(R_{0})}
. We consider the following definitions.

Definition 1.1.1
.

A Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is
asymptotically hyperbolic
if there exists a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{M}
,
R_{0}>0
,
\beta>2
, and a diffeomorphism
\Psi:{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\setminus\overline{B(R_{0})}\to\mathcal{M}\setminus K
such that

|D_{g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}}^{j}(\Psi^{*}g-g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})|_{g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}}=O(e^{-\beta r}),

for every
j\in\{0,1,2\}
.

Definition 1.1.2
.

A Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is
non-trapping
if the orbit under the geodesic flow of any pair
(x,v)\in T^{1}\mathcal{M}
is unbounded.

In the rest of the paper, when considering Riemannian manifolds with variable curvature, we will focus on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic Riemannian manifolds according to the previous definitions.

1.1.2.
The Vlasov equation on a Riemannian manifold

In this article, we consider a distribution function
f:[0,\infty)\times T\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying the Vlasov equation on a non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
. The
Vlasov equation on a Riemannian manifold

(\mathcal{M},g)
, with respect to the coordinate system
(x,v)\in T\mathcal{M}
, takes the form

(1)

\partial_{t}f+v^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}f-v^{i}v^{j}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\partial_{v^{k}}f=0,

where
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
are the Christoffel symbols of
(\mathcal{M},g)
. We consider initial data for the Vlasov equation
f_{0}:T\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
. The Vlasov equation on a complete
C^{k}
Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is
well-posed
with initial data
f_{0}\in C_{x,v}^{j}(T\mathcal{M})
for every
j\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
. The well-posedness of the Vlasov equation on a complete
C^{k}
Riemannian manifold follows directly by the representation formula of Vlasov fields in terms of the geodesic flow, by using the regularity of the flow map.

In the specific case of hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
, the Vlasov equation takes the form

\partial_{t}f+v^{r}\partial_{r}f+v^{\theta}\partial_{\theta}f+(v^{\theta})^{2}\sinh r\cosh r\partial_{v^{r}}f-2v^{r}v^{\theta}\coth r\partial_{v^{\theta}}f=0,

in terms of the Christoffel symbols of
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
in the coordinate system previously introduced. See Subsection
2.1.1
for the precise Christoffel symbols of
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
in the hyperboloidal coordinate system. The Vlasov equation on
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
is well-posed with initial data
f_{0}\in C_{x,v}^{j}(T\mathcal{M})
for every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

1.2.
The main results

In this subsection, we present the main decay estimates for the Vlasov fields studied in this paper. First, we consider Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space, and later Vlasov fields on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds.

From now on, we use the notation
A\lesssim B
to specify that there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
, where
C
depends only on the dimension
n
, the corresponding order of regularity, or other fixed constants.

1.2.1.
Decay for Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space

In this section, we state pointwise decay estimates in time for the
spatial density
induced by a solution
f(t,x,v)
to the Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
, given by

\rho(f)(t,x)=\int f(t,x,v)\sinh r\mathrm{d}v^{r}\mathrm{d}v^{\theta},

in terms of the volume form on
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
written in local coordinates. In order to avoid the region of phase space
\{g_{x}(v,v)=0\}
where dispersion degenerates, we define an invariant subset
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
of the tangent bundle, given by

{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}:=\Big\{(x,v)\in T{\mathbb{H}}^{2}:~g_{x}(v,v)\geq\alpha^{2}\Big\},

where we will assume the initial distribution function is supported. In the following, we take derivatives of the spatial density using the normalized frame
\{\partial_{r},\sinh^{-1}r\partial_{\theta}\}
on
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
.

Theorem 1.1

(Exponential decay for Vlasov fields supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
. Let
f_{0}\in C_{x,v}^{1}(T{\mathbb{H}}^{2})
be an initial data for the Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space that is compactly supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
. Then, the spatial density induced by the corresponding Vlasov field
f
satisfies

(2)

\displaystyle\qquad|\rho(f)(t,x)|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{\exp(\alpha t)}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}},

(3)

\displaystyle\Big|\dfrac{1}{\sinh r}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)(t,x)

\displaystyle\Big|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{\exp(2\alpha t)}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}},\qquad|\partial_{r}\rho(f)(t,x)|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{\exp(\alpha t)}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}},

for every
t\geq 0
and every
x\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
.

Remark 1.2.1
.

(a)

We obtain
optimal decay rates
for the spatial density and its first order derivatives. The decay rates are expressed in terms of the
minimal Lyapunov exponent

\alpha
associated to the geodesics on the support of the distribution function. We also emphasize the difference between the decay rates obtained for the radial and angular derivatives of the spatial density. This discrepancy comes from weights that arise when estimating the derivatives of the spatial density in terms of the commuting vector fields for the Vlasov equation. See Subsection
3.2
for further details.

(b)

The decay rates in Theorem
1.1
degenerate when
\alpha
becomes zero. This holds because the hyperbolic expansion/contraction associated to the geodesics in hyperbolic space degenerates when
g_{x}(v,v)
becomes zero. For this reason, Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space do not decay exponentially in time for general Vlasov fields. This behavior is compatible with inverse polynomial decay in time.

Similar decay estimates in time hold for the spatial density on hyperbolic space in higher dimensions. We describe the modifications required to treat this case in Subsection
3.4
.

Theorem 1.2

(Polynomial decay for Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space)
.

Let
f_{0}\in C_{x,v}^{1}(T{\mathbb{H}}^{2})
be a compactly supported initial data for the Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space. Then, the spatial density induced by the corresponding Vlasov field
f
satisfies

(4)

\displaystyle\qquad|\rho(f)(t,x)|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{t^{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}},

(5)

\displaystyle\Big|\dfrac{1}{\sinh r}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)(t,x)

\displaystyle\Big|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{t}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}},\qquad|\partial_{r}\rho(f)(t,x)|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{t}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}},

for every
t\geq 1
and every
x\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
.

Remark 1.2.2
.

We obtain
optimal decay rates
for the spatial density and its first order derivatives. The decay rate of
\rho(f)

coincides
with the corresponding rate for Vlasov fields on Euclidean space. In contrast, the decay rate of derivatives of
\rho(f)
is
slower
than the corresponding rate for Vlasov fields on Euclidean space. The precise decay rates come from particle energy weights that appear when using the commuting vector fields for the Vlasov equation. See Subsection
3.3
for more details.

1.2.2.
Decay for Vlasov fields on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds

Let
\kappa_{1}>\kappa_{2}>0
. Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be asymptotically hyperbolic, non-trapping, and with Gaussian curvature
-\kappa_{1}\leq K_{g}\leq-\kappa_{2}
.

In this section, we state pointwise decay estimates in time for the
spatial density
induced by a solution
f(t,x,v)
to the Vlasov equation on
(\mathcal{M},g)
, given by

\rho(f)(t,x)=\int f(t,x,v)\sqrt{\det g}\mathrm{d}v^{r}\mathrm{d}v^{\theta},

in terms of the explicit volume form on
(\mathcal{M},g)
. In order to avoid the region of phase space
\{g_{x}(v,v)=0\}
where dispersion degenerates, we define an invariant subset
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
of the tangent bundle, given by

{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}:=\Big\{(x,v)\in T\mathcal{M}:~g_{x}(v,v)\geq\alpha^{2}\Big\},

where we will assume the initial distribution function is supported. In the following, we take derivatives of the spatial density using the normalized frame
\{\partial_{r},\sinh^{-1}r\partial_{\theta}\}
on
(\mathcal{M},g)
.

Theorem 1.3

(Exponential decay for Vlasov fields supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
. Let
f_{0}\in C_{x,v}^{1}(T\mathcal{M})
be an initial data for the Vlasov equation on
(\mathcal{M}^{2},g)
that is compactly supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
. Then, the spatial density induced by the corresponding Vlasov field
f
satisfies

(6)

\displaystyle\qquad|\rho(f)(t,x)|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{\exp(\alpha t)}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}},

(7)

\displaystyle\Big|\dfrac{1}{\sinh r}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)(t,x)

\displaystyle\Big|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{\exp(2\alpha t)}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}},\qquad|\partial_{r}\rho(f)(t,x)|\lesssim\dfrac{1}{\exp(\alpha t)}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}},

for every
t\geq 0
and every
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 1.2.3
.

(1)

We obtain optimal decay rates for the spatial density and its first order derivatives. The decay rates for the spatial density induced by Vlasov fields on
(\mathcal{M},g)
coincides with the decay rates for Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space. We make key use of the rate of convergence of the metric
g
to the one in hyperbolic space
g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}
at infinity. We do not require the metric to be a perturbation of hyperbolic space. The proof of Theorem
1.3
exploits a commuting vector field in the unstable invariant distribution of phase space.

(2)

The decay rates in Theorem
1.3
degenerate when
\alpha
becomes zero. This holds because the hyperbolic expansion/contraction associated to the geodesics in
(\mathcal{M},g)
degenerates when
g_{x}(v,v)
becomes zero. For this reason, Vlasov fields on a non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
do not decay exponentially in time for general Vlasov fields.

We finish the paper with a general (non-optimal) decay estimate for the spatial density induced by a Vlasov field on an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold. See Appendix

To the knowledge of the authors, the only stability result for Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space is given in the work of Diacu, Ibrahim, Lind, and Shen
[
DILS16
]
. In that paper, the authors extend the classical Vlasov–Poisson system on Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
to hyperbolic space

, and the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
.
[
DILS16

studies the local well-posedness properties of the Vlasov–Poisson system on these geometric backgrounds. This paper also derives the Vlasov–Poisson system in the corresponding two dimensional cases by using works on the

1.3.
Ingredients of the proof

In this paper, we prove decay estimates in time for the spatial density on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds by proving decay in time for the size of the velocity support of the distribution function. The decay of the spatial density on holds due to the lack of recurrence of the geodesic flow in these geometric backgrounds. We suitably integrate the geodesic flow to estimate the velocity coordinates in time. The proof of the decay of
\rho(f)
follows from these arguments. We only require for the initial distribution function to have enough regularity for the induced spatial density to be well-defined, in order to show the corresponding decay estimates.

Moreover, we prove decay estimates in time for derivatives of the spatial density on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds by using commuting vector fields for the Vlasov equation. As a result, we reduce the estimates for derivatives of
\rho(f)
to the decay in time of the velocity support of the distribution. First, we write a derivative of the spatial density, as the spatial density of a derivative of the distribution function by

(8)

\partial_{x^{i}}\rho(f)(t,x)=\int(\partial_{x^{i}}-v^{j}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{v^{k}})f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)=:\rho\Big(\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{x^{i}})f\Big)(t,x),

in terms of the
horizontal lift

\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{x^{i}})
of the corresponding vector field
\partial_{x^{i}}
. See Section
2
for more information about horizontal lifts. Later, we use a suitable class
\lambda
of commuting vector fields for the Vlasov equation, that arises by studying the Jacobi equation on the tangent bundle of the corresponding Riemannian manifold, with respect to the Sasaki metric. In particular, we exploit the commuting vector fields for the Vlasov equation that are contained in the unstable invariant distribution of phase space. We write the horizontal lifts in terms of commuting vector fields plus errors than can be controlled after integration by parts in the fibers of the tangent bundle. The proof of the decay estimates (
3
) are finally obtained using the decay of the size of the velocity support of the distribution function.

2.
Preliminaries: Jacobi fields and commuting vector fields

In this section, we introduce the setup and tools required in the proofs of our main results. Firstly, we introduce the Sasaki metric on the tangent bundle of a Riemannian manifold. Secondly, we review the hyperbolicity of the geodesic flow in hyperbolic space, and negatively curved surfaces. We finish this section setting commuting vector fields for the study of the Vlasov equation.

2.1.
The Sasaki metric on the tangent bundle

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a Riemannian manifold. The
Sasaki metric

\overline{g}
is a Riemannian metric induced on
T\mathcal{M}
. To define this Riemannian structure, we recall the decomposition of the tangent space of the tangent bundle at a point
(x,v)\in T\mathcal{M}
given by

T_{(x,v)}T\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{H}_{(x,v)}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{(x,v)}

into the
horizontal subspace

\mathcal{H}_{(x,v)}
and the
vertical subspace

\mathcal{V}_{(x,v)}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{(x,v)}

\displaystyle:=\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(T_{x}\mathcal{M})=\{\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(Y):~Y\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{(x,v)}

\displaystyle:=\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(T_{x}\mathcal{M})=\{\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(Y):~Y\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}\},

where the
horizontal lift

\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}:T_{x}\mathcal{M}\to T_{(x,v)}T\mathcal{M}
and the
vertical lift

\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}:T_{x}\mathcal{M}\to T_{(x,v)}T\mathcal{M}
are defined in local coordinates as

\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(Y^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}):=Y^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}-Y^{i}v^{j}\Gamma_{ij}^{k}\partial_{v^{k}},\qquad\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(Y^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}):=Y^{i}\partial_{v^{i}},

for an arbitrary vector field
Y^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}\in T\mathcal{M}
.

The vertical subspace
\mathcal{V}_{(x,v)}
can be defined as the kernel of the differential
d\pi:TT\mathcal{M}\to T\mathcal{M}
of the canonical projection
\pi:T\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, the horizontal subspace
\mathcal{H}_{(x,v)}
can be defined as the kernel of the
connection map

K:TT\mathcal{M}\to T\mathcal{M}
defined in terms of the Levi-Civita connection. See
[
Pat99
, Chapter 1]
for further details about the connection map.

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a Riemannian manifold. We define the
Sasaki metric

\bar{g}
on the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{M}
as the unique metric for which

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{(x,v)}(\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(Y),\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(Z))

\displaystyle=g_{x}(Y,Z),

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{(x,v)}(\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(Y),\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(Z))

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{(x,v)}(\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(Y),\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(Z))

\displaystyle=g_{x}(Y,Z),

for every
(x,v)\in T\mathcal{M}
, and every
Y,Z\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 2.1.1
.

The
generator of the geodesic flow
on a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is given by the horizontal vector field

\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v)=v^{i}\partial_{x^{i}}-v^{i}v^{j}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}\partial_{v^{k}}.

The Vlasov equation on a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is written in terms of this horizontal vector field.

2.1.1.
The Sasaki metric on the tangent bundle of hyperbolic space

In hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
, written in the model of the upper branch of a hyperboloid, the non-trivial Christoffel symbols are given by

\Gamma_{\theta\theta}^{r}=-\cosh r\sinh r,\qquad\Gamma_{r\theta}^{\theta}=\coth r.

Lifting the vector fields
\partial_{r}
and
\partial_{\theta}
into the tangent of the tangent bundle of hyperbolic space, we obtain

(9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{r})&=\partial_{r}-\coth rv^{\theta}\partial_{v^{\theta}},\\
\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{\theta})&=\partial_{\theta}+\cosh r\sinh rv^{\theta}\partial_{v^{r}}-\coth rv^{r}\partial_{v^{\theta}},\\
\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{r})&=\partial_{v^{r}},\\
\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{\theta})&=\partial_{v^{\theta}},\end{aligned}

by using the Christoffel symbols written above. Then, the Sasaki metric
\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}
on the tangent bundle of hyperbolic space
T{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
can be set.

In the following, we use the orthonormal frame on the tangent bundle of hyperbolic space given by

\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{r}),\quad\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}\Big(\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}\Big),\quad\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{r}),\quad\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}\Big(\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}\Big).

The Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
can be written using this frame as

\partial_{t}f+v^{r}\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{r})f+v^{\theta}\sinh r\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}\Big(\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}\Big)f=0,

in terms of the previous orthonormal frame on the tangent bundle of hyperbolic space.

2.2.
Jacobi fields along the geodesic flow

Given a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
, we recall the
geodesic flow map

\phi_{t}:T\mathcal{M}\to T\mathcal{M}
defined as the mapping
t\mapsto\phi_{t}(x,v)
that determines the unique geodesic with initial data
(x,v)
. In this subsection, we first recall the definition of Jacobi fields and relate them to the differential of the geodesic flow map. Later, we will describe the behavior of Jacobi fields on hyperbolic space, and negatively curved surfaces.

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Let
\gamma_{\tau}:I\to\mathcal{M}
be a one-parameter family of geodesics in
\mathcal{M}
, where
\tau\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
and
\gamma:=\gamma_{0}
. A vector field
J(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}\mathcal{M}
of the form

J(t)=\dfrac{\partial\gamma_{\tau}}{\partial\tau}(t)\Big|_{\tau=0}

is called a
Jacobi field in
(\mathcal{M},g)
. A Jacobi field
J
satisfies the
Jacobi equation

\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}J=R(\dot{\gamma},J)\dot{\gamma}.

The Jacobi fields in
(\mathcal{M},g)
are
generated
by the differential of the geodesic flow. Let us make this statement precise. We recall the canonical projection
\pi:T\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
of the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{M}
into the manifold
\mathcal{M}
. We define an
adapted curve
c_{V}
to a vector

V\in T_{(x,v)}T\mathcal{M}
as an arbitrary curve
c_{V}:(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\to T\mathcal{M}
such that

c_{V}(0)=(x,v),\qquad\dot{c}_{V}(0)=V.

The Jacobi fields in
(\mathcal{M},g)
are
generated
by the differential of the geodesic flow, in the sense that the map
(t,\tau)\mapsto\pi(\phi_{t}(c_{V}(\tau)))
defines a variation of geodesics that induces the Jacobi field

J_{V}(t)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\pi(\phi_{t}(c_{V}(\tau)))\Big|_{\tau=0},

whose initial conditions are
J_{V}(0)=\mathrm{d}\pi(x,v)(V)
and
\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}J_{V}(0)=K(x,v)(V)
.

We also consider
Jacobi fields in the tangent bundle

(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
. For our purposes, we relate the Jacobi fields in the tangent bundle with the differential of the geodesic flow map.

Definition 2.2.2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Let
\bar{\gamma}_{\tau}:I\to T\mathcal{M}
be a one-parameter family of geodesics in
T\mathcal{M}
, where
\tau\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
and
\bar{\gamma}:=\bar{\gamma}_{0}
. A vector field
\bar{J}(t)\in T_{\bar{\gamma}(t)}T\mathcal{M}
of the form

\bar{J}(t):=\dfrac{\partial\bar{\gamma}_{\tau}}{\partial\tau}(t)\Big|_{\tau=0},

is called a
Jacobi field in
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
. A Jacobi field
\bar{J}
satisfies the
Jacobi equation

\overline{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\nabla}_{X}\bar{J}=\bar{R}(X,\bar{J})X,

where
\overline{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
(T\mathcal{M},\overline{g})
,
\bar{R}
is the Riemann curvature tensor on
(T\mathcal{M},\overline{g})
, and
X
is the generator of the geodesic flow.

Similarly to the case of Jacobi fields in
(\mathcal{M},g)
, the Jacobi fields in
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
are
generated
by the differential of the geodesic flow. Consider an adapted curve
c_{V}
to a vector
V\in T_{(x,v)}T\mathcal{M}
. The map
(t,\tau)\mapsto\phi_{t}(c_{V}(\tau))
defines a variation of geodesics in
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
that induces a Jacobi field

\bar{J}_{V}(t)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\phi_{t}(c_{V}(\tau))\Big|_{\tau=0}=\mathrm{d}\phi{{}_{t}}(x,v)(V).

The following lemma establishes the precise relation between the Jacobi fields in a Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
and the Jacobi fields in the tangent bundle
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

The differential of the geodesic flow map
\phi_{t}:T\mathcal{M}\to T\mathcal{M}
satisfies

\mathrm{d}\phi{{}_{t}}(x,v)(V)=\mathrm{Hor}_{\phi_{t}(x,v)}(J_{V}(t))+\mathrm{Ver}_{\phi_{t}(x,v)}(\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}J_{V}(t)),

for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, every
(x,v)\in T\mathcal{M}
, and every vector
V\in T_{(x,v)}T\mathcal{M}
.

See
[
Pat99
, Lemma 1.40]
for a proof of Lemma
2.2.1
.

2.2.1.
Jacobi fields in hyperbolic space

In hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
, written in the model of the upper branch of a hyperboloid, we consider an orthogonal parallel frame along an arbitrary geodesic
\gamma
given by

(10)

\dot{\gamma}:=v^{r}\partial_{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r},\qquad N:=-\sinh rv^{\theta}\partial_{r}+v^{r}\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}.

We write the Jacobi equation on hyperbolic space as a linear system of odes for the components of
J
in terms of the moving frame (
10
). We use that the Riemann curvature tensor along a geodesic
\gamma
satisfies

R(\dot{\gamma},N)\dot{\gamma}=|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}N,\qquad R(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})\dot{\gamma}=0.

Proposition 2.2.2
.

Let
J
be a Jacobi field in hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
. Then, the Jacobi equation satisfied by the components of the Jacobi field
J=J^{0}\dot{\gamma}+J^{N}N
is reduced to

(11)

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}J^{0}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=0,\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}J^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}J^{N}.

In the tangent bundle of hyperbolic space
(T{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
, we consider a moving frame along an arbitrary geodesic
\bar{\gamma}
, given by

\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v),\qquad\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N),\qquad\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(v),\qquad\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N).

By combining the Jacobi equation on hyperbolic space (
11
) with Lemma
2.2.1
, we obtain the Jacobi fields in
(T{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
. In the following, we set

J_{u}^{N}:=\frac{1}{2}\Big(J^{N}+\frac{1}{|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}}\frac{\mathrm{d}J^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big),\qquad J_{s}^{N}:=\frac{1}{2}\Big(J^{N}-\frac{1}{|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}}\frac{\mathrm{d}J^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big).

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Let
\bar{J}
be a Jacobi field in the tangent bundle of hyperbolic space
(T{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
. Then, the Jacobi equation satisfied by the components of the Jacobi field

\displaystyle\bar{J}(t)

\displaystyle=J^{0}\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v)+\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J^{0}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad+J_{u}^{N}\Big(\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)+|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)\Big)+J_{s}^{N}\Big(\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)-|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)\Big)

is reduced to

(12)

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}J^{0}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=0,\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J_{u}^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}J_{u}^{N},\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J_{s}^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=-|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}J_{s}^{N}

Proposition
2.2.3
shows that the geodesic flow in
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
is
hyperbolic
, in the sense that the restriction of the geodesic flow to the unit tangent bundle
\phi_{t}:(T^{1}{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\overline{g}|_{T^{1}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})\to(T^{1}{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\overline{g}|_{T^{1}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
defines a hyperbolic flow according to
[
KH95
, Chapter 17, Section 4]
. In other words, there exists an invariant decomposition of the tangent of the unit tangent bundle

T_{(x,v)}T^{1}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=E_{0}(x,v)\oplus E_{u}(x,v)\oplus E_{s}(x,v),

into the subspace
E_{0}(x,v):=\spann\{\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v)\}
spanned by the generator of the geodesic flow, the
stable subspace

E_{s}(x,v):=\spann\{\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)-\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)\}
, and the
unstable subspace

E_{u}(x,v):=\spann\{\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)+\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)\}
. The differential of the geodesic flow contracts exponentially the distribution
E_{s}(x,v)
, and expands exponentially the distribution
E_{u}(x,v)
.

Remark 2.2.1
.

The Jacobi equation on the tangent bundle of hyperbolic space
(T{\mathbb{H}}^{n},\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})
in higher dimensions can be written similarly to (
12
). Consider an orthonormal parallel frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N_{1},N_{2},\dots,N_{n-1}\}
along a geodesic
\gamma
. Set

J_{u}^{i}:=\frac{1}{2}\Big(J^{i}+\frac{1}{|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}}\frac{\mathrm{d}J^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big),\qquad J_{s}^{i}:=\frac{1}{2}\Big(J^{i}-\frac{1}{|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}}\frac{\mathrm{d}J^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big),

for every
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
. Then, the Jacobi equation satisfied by the components of the Jacobi field

\displaystyle\bar{J}(t)

\displaystyle=J^{0}\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v)+\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J^{0}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}J_{u}^{i}\Big(\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N_{i})+|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N_{i})\Big)+J_{s}^{i}\Big(\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N_{i})-|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N_{i})\Big)

is reduced to

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}J^{0}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=0,\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J_{u}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}J_{u}^{i},\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J_{s}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}J_{s}^{i}

for every
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
. In particular, the geodesic flow in hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})
is also hyperbolic.

2.2.2.
Jacobi fields in a negatively curved manifold

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be an oriented pinched negatively curved Riemannian manifold. Let
\gamma
be a geodesic in
(\mathcal{M},g)
. We consider the unique positively oriented frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
along
\gamma
such that

|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}=|N|_{g},\qquad g(\dot{\gamma},N)=0.

The frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
contains two parallel vector fields along
\gamma
. We write the Jacobi equation on
(\mathcal{M},g)
as a linear system of odes for the components of
J
in terms of the moving frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
. We use that the Riemann curvature tensor along a geodesic
\gamma
satisfies

R(\dot{\gamma},N)\dot{\gamma}=-K_{g}|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}N,\qquad R(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})\dot{\gamma}=0,

in terms of the Gaussian curvature
K_{g}
of
(\mathcal{M},g)
.

Proposition 2.2.4
.

Let
J
be a Jacobi field in
(\mathcal{M},g)
. Then, the Jacobi equation satisfied by the components of the Jacobi field
J=J^{0}\dot{\gamma}+J^{N}N
is reduced to

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}J^{0}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=0,\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}J^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=-|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}K_{g}J^{N}.

We consider the quotient
q(t):=(J^{N})^{-1}\dot{J}^{N}
that satisfies the Riccati equation

(13)

\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)+q^{2}(t)+|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}K_{g}(x(t))=0,

as long as
J^{N}(t)\neq 0
. Let
T\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Set
J^{N}_{T}(t,x,v)
to be the solution of the Jacobi equation along
\gamma
with

J^{N}_{T}(0,x,v)=1,\qquad J^{N}_{T}(T,x,v)=0.

By the pinching assumption the metric
g
does not have conjugate points so the solution
J^{N}_{T}(t,x,v)
does not vanish for
t\neq T
. Consider the induced solution
q_{T}(t,x,v):=(J_{T}^{N})^{-1}\dot{J}_{T}^{N}
of the Riccati equation (
13
). By the assumption
J^{N}_{T}(T,x,v)=0
the function
q_{T}(t,x,v)
is defined for
t<T
and
\lim_{t\to T}q_{T}(t,x,v)=-\infty
. By the work of Hopf
[
Hop48
]
, the limits

q_{s}(t,x,v):=\lim_{T\to\infty}q_{T}(t,x,v),\qquad q_{u}(t,x,v):=\lim_{T\to\infty}q_{-T}(t,x,v),

exist for every
(t,x,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times T\mathcal{M}
. By construction, the solutions
q_{u}
and
q_{s}
of the Riccati equation are invariant, in other words,
q_{u}(t,x,v)=q_{u}(x,v)
and
q_{s}(t,x,v)=q_{s}(x,v)
. By construction, the functions
q_{s}
and
q_{u}
satisfy the Riccati equation

(14)

\displaystyle Xq+q^{2}+|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}^{2}K_{g}=0.

Moreover, the estimate
q_{u}>0>q_{s}
holds uniformly. For the functions
q_{s}
and
q_{u}
, there are corresponding functions
J^{N}_{s}
and
J^{N}_{u}
, which satisfy
q_{s}=(J_{s}^{N})^{-1}\dot{J}_{s}^{N}
and
q_{u}=(J_{u}^{N})^{-1}\dot{J}_{u}^{N}
, respectively. For general hyperbolic flows (not necessarily geodesic flows), the functions
q_{u}
and
q_{s}
are only Hölder continuous after Hirsch, Pugh, and Shub
[
HPS77
]
. In the specific case of negatively curved surfaces, the functions
q_{u}
and
q_{s}
belong to
C^{2-}(T\mathcal{M}):=\cap_{\delta>0}C^{2-\delta}(T\mathcal{M})
by the work of Hurder and Katok
[
HK90
]
.

In the tangent bundle of
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
, we consider a moving frame along a geodesic
\bar{\gamma}
given by

\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v),\qquad\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N),\qquad\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(v),\qquad\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N).

We obtain the Jacobi fields in
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
by integrating the equations
q_{s}=(J_{s}^{N})^{-1}\dot{J}_{s}^{N}
and
q_{u}=(J_{u}^{N})^{-1}\dot{J}_{u}^{N}
, for the functions
J_{s}^{N}
and
J_{u}^{N}
, respectively.

Proposition 2.2.5
.

Let
\bar{J}
be a Jacobi field in
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
. Then, the Jacobi equation satisfied by the components of the Jacobi field

\displaystyle\bar{J}(t)

\displaystyle=J^{0}\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v)+\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J^{0}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad+J^{N}_{u}\Big(\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)+q_{u}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)\Big)+J^{N}_{s}\Big(\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)+q_{s}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)\Big)

is reduced to

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}J^{0}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}=0,\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J_{u}^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t}=q_{u}J^{N}_{u},\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}J_{s}^{N}}{\mathrm{d}t}=q_{s}J^{N}_{s}.

Proposition
2.2.5
shows that the geodesic flow in
(\mathcal{M},g)
is
hyperbolic
, in the sense that the restriction of the geodesic flow to the unit tangent bundle
\phi_{t}:(T^{1}\mathcal{M},\overline{g}|_{T^{1}\mathcal{M}})\to(T^{1}\mathcal{M},\overline{g}|_{T^{1}\mathcal{M}})
defines a hyperbolic flow according to
[
KH95
, Chapter 17, Section 4]
. In other words, there exists an invariant decomposition of the tangent of the unit tangent bundle

T_{(x,v)}T^{1}\mathcal{M}=E_{0}(x,v)\oplus E_{u}(x,v)\oplus E_{s}(x,v),

into the subspace
E_{0}(x,v):=\spann\{\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v)\}
spanned by the generator of the geodesic flow, the
stable subspace

E_{s}(x,v):=\spann\{\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)+q_{s}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)\}
, and the
unstable subspace

E_{u}(x,v):=\spann\{\mathrm{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)+q_{u}\mathrm{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)\}
. The differential of the geodesic flow contracts exponentially the distribution
E_{s}(x,v)
, and expands exponentially the distribution
E_{u}(x,v)
.

.

2.3.
Commuting vector fields for the Vlasov equation

In this subsection, we introduce two classes of vector fields, one in
(T{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
, and another one in
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
. We will later use these classes of vector fields to obtain decay estimates for Vlasov fields. The vector fields introduced in this section arise from the dynamics of the Jacobi fields in
(T{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
, and the Jacobi fields in
(T\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
.

2.3.1.
On hyperbolic space

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic in hyperbolic space. We consider the orthogonal parallel frame along
\gamma
given by (
10
). Lifting the vector fields in this frame into
TT{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
, we obtain the moving frame

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:=\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v)=v^{r}\partial_{r}+v^{\theta}\partial_{\theta}+(v^{\theta})^{2}\sinh r\cosh r\partial_{v^{r}}-2v^{r}v^{\theta}\coth r\partial_{v^{\theta}},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle:=\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(v)=v^{r}\partial_{v^{r}}+\sinh rv^{\theta}\dfrac{\partial_{v^{\theta}}}{\sinh r},

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle:=\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N)=-\sinh rv^{\theta}\partial_{r}+v^{r}\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}+\cosh rv^{r}v^{\theta}\partial_{v^{r}}+\Big((v^{\theta})^{2}-\dfrac{(v^{r})^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\Big)\cosh r\partial_{v^{\theta}},

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle:=\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N)=-\sinh rv^{\theta}\partial_{v^{r}}+v^{r}\dfrac{\partial_{v^{\theta}}}{\sinh r}.

The commutators between the generator of the geodesic flow
X
, and the vector fields
H
,
Y
,
V
are given by

(15)

[X,Y]=-X,\qquad[X,H]=-E^{2}V,\qquad[X,V]=-H,

in terms of the particle energy
E:=|\dot{\gamma}|_{g}
. The identities (
15
) are part of the standard structure equations of the Lie algebra of vector fields on
T{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
.

The commutators (
15
) show the following commuting vector fields with the Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space,

(a)

generator of the flow
X
,

(b)

uniform motion
tX+Y
,

(c)

unstable vector field
U:=e^{Et}(H+EV)
,

(d)

stable vector field
S:=e^{-Et}(H-EV)
.

We define the class of vector fields

\lambda:=\Big\{X,tX+Y,U,S\Big\}.

The collection of vector fields
\lambda
will be used later to obtain decay estimates for the Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space.

Lemma 2.3.1
.

Let
f
be a regular Vlasov field on hyperbolic space. Then,
Zf
is also a solution of this equation for every
Z\in\lambda
.

Proof.

We use the commuting relations (
15
) to show that
[\partial_{t}+X,Z]=0
, for every
Z\in\lambda
. Since
f
is a Vlasov field on hyperbolic space, we thus have

(\partial_{t}+X)(Zf)=Z(\partial_{t}+X)f+[\partial_{t}+X,Z]f=0,

and therefore
Zf
is a solution as well.
∎

Remark 2.3.1
.

(a)

The class of commuting vector fields
\{t\partial_{x^{i}}+\partial_{v^{i}},\partial_{x^{i}}\}
for the Vlasov equation on Euclidean space
({\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{n},\delta_{E})
, is composed by Jacobi fields in the tangent bundle of Euclidean space
({\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{n},\bar{\delta}_{E})
with respect to the Sasaki metric along an arbitrary geodesic in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{x}
. The class of commuting vector fields
\{t\partial_{x^{i}}+\partial_{v^{i}},\partial_{x^{i}}\}
for the Vlasov equation on Euclidean space has played an important role in previous stability results of the vacuum solution for the Vlasov–Poisson system on Euclidean space
[
Smu16
,
Dua22
]
.

(b)

We observe that the stable derivative of Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
grows exponentially in time. Using the commuting vector field
S
of the Vlasov equation contained in the stable distribution of phase space, we obtain

(H-EV)f(t,x,v)=e^{Et}(H-EV)f_{0}(t,x_{0},v_{0}),

in terms of the corresponding point
(x_{0},v_{0})
in the support of the initial distribution function
f_{0}
. This property of Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space contrasts with Vlasov fields on Euclidean space, for which all derivatives decay in time.

2.3.2.
On a negatively curved manifold

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic in
(\mathcal{M},g)
. We consider the orthogonal parallel frame along the geodesic
\gamma
given by
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
. Lifting the vector fields in this frame into
TT\mathcal{M}
, we obtain the moving frame

\displaystyle X:=\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v),\quad Y:=\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(v),\quad H:=\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N),\quad V:=\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N).

The commutators between the generator of the geodesic flow
X
, and the vector fields
H
,

are given by

(16)

in terms of the Gaussian curvature

Recall the functions

3.
Decay for Vlasov fields on hyperbolic space

In this section, we prove decay estimates for the spatial density induced by a Vlasov field on hyperbolic space. The geodesic flow in hyperbolic space is determined by the geodesic equations

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=v^{\theta},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}v^{\theta}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-2\coth rv^{r}v^{\theta},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=v^{r},\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}v^{r}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\cosh r\sinh r(v^{\theta})^{2}.

It is well-known that the geodesics in hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
are characterised as the intersection between the hyperboloid
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
with the two dimensional linear subspaces of Minkowski spacetime in dimension
2+1
.

The geodesic flow in hyperbolic space can also be viewed as a
Hamiltonian flow
in the cotangent bundle
(T^{*}{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
, where the Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
is given by

H(x,v):=\dfrac{1}{2}\Big(v_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}r}v_{\theta}^{2}\Big).

We define the
particle energy

E:T{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
of a geodesic
\gamma
by

E(x,v):=g_{x}(v,v)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{(v^{r})^{2}+\sinh^{2}r(v^{\theta})^{2}},

which is a conserved along the geodesic flow. The Hamiltonian
H
can be written in terms of the particle energy as
H=\frac{1}{2}E^{2}
. We also define the
angular velocity

l:T{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\to[0,\infty)
of a geodesic
\gamma
by

l(x,v):=g(\partial_{\theta},\dot{\gamma})=v^{\theta}\sinh^{2}r,

which is conserved along the geodesic flow since
\partial_{\theta}
is a Killing field. The particle energy
E
and the angular velocity
l
are related by

(18)

E^{2}=(v^{r})^{2}+\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r},

for every geodesic on hyperbolic space. We observe that the geodesic flow in hyperbolic space
(T^{*}{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\bar{g}_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}},H)
is a
completely integrable Hamiltonian flow
in the sense of Liouville, due to the existence of the two independent conserved quantities in involution
H
and
l
.

Remark 3.0.1
.

Hyperbolic space has a large group of isometries given by the Lie group
SO(1,n-1)
of linear transformations in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that leave invariant the Lorentzian metric of Minkowski spacetime in dimension
n+1
. The Lie algebra of Killing fields on hyperbolic space induces several conserved quantities along the geodesic flow that can be used to show the
complete integrability
of the geodesic flow in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
.

3.1.
Estimates for the geodesic flow

The decay estimate for the spatial density is proven by obtaining time decay of the velocity support for the corresponding Vlasov fields. We first show estimates in time for the velocity coordinates along the geodesics in
\supp(f)
.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

The radial coordinate
r(t)
along a geodesic
\gamma
such that
\gamma(0)\in\supp(f_{0})
satisfies that for every
t\geq 0
,

(19)

ce^{Et}\leq\sinh r(t)\leq Ce^{Et},

where
c,C
are constants depending only on
\supp(f_{0})
.

Proof.

The geodesic equation for the radial coordinate can be written as

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v^{r}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\coth r\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r},

by using the angular velocity
l
. As a result, the first coordinate
\cosh r
of a geodesic on the hyperboloid
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
satisfies the linear ode

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\cosh r=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\sinh rv^{r})=\cosh r(v^{r})^{2}+\cosh r\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}=E^{2}\cosh r,

which can be integrated explicitly. The radial terms given by

c_{\pm}(t):=\cosh r(t)\pm\dfrac{1}{E}\sinh r(t)v^{r}(t)

satisfy the linear odes

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}c_{\pm}(t)=\sinh rv^{r}\pm E\cosh r=\pm E\Big(\cosh r\pm\dfrac{1}{E}\sinh rv^{r}\Big)=\pm Ec_{\pm}(t),

and consequently

\cosh r(t)\pm\dfrac{1}{E}\sinh r(t)v^{r}(t)=\Big(\cosh r(0)\pm\dfrac{1}{E}\sinh r(0)v^{r}(0)\Big)e^{\pm Et}.

In particular, the first coordinate
\cosh r
of a geodesic on the hyperboloid
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
is given by

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \cosh r(t)=~&c_{+}(0)e^{Et}+c_{-}(0)e^{-Et}\\
=~&\dfrac{e^{Et}}{2}\Big(\cosh r(0)+\dfrac{1}{E}\sinh r(0)v^{r}(0)\Big)+\dfrac{e^{-Et}}{2}\Big(\cosh r(0)-\dfrac{1}{E}\sinh r(0)v^{r}(0)\Big),\end{aligned}

in terms of the radial coordinates
r(0)
and
v^{r}(0)
.

We observe that the identity (
18
) relating the particle energy
E
and the angular velocity
l
can be written as

c_{+}(t)c_{-}(t)=\cosh^{2}r(t)-\dfrac{1}{E^{2}}\sinh^{2}r(t)v^{r}(t)^{2}=1+\dfrac{l^{2}}{E^{2}},

which shows that the terms
c_{\pm}(0)
are strictly positive on the initial data, see (
20
). In particular, there are positive constants
\epsilon,M
such that
M\geq c_{\pm}(0)\geq\epsilon,
for all initial data in the support of
f_{0}
. Hence, for any initial data in the support of the initial distribution function we bound the radial coordinate along the geodesic flow

\log(\epsilon e^{Et})\leq r(t)=\log\Big(c_{+}(0)e^{Et}+c_{-}(0)e^{-Et}+\Big((c_{+}(0)e^{Et}+c_{-}(0)e^{-Et})^{2}-1\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\leq\log(4Me^{Et}),

by applying the inverse of hyperbolic cosine on the identity (
20
) combined with the bounds on
c_{\pm}(0)
. In particular, the radial coordinate along any geodesics emanating from the support of the initial distribution function satisfies

(21)

ce^{Et}\leq\sinh r(t)\leq Ce^{Et},

where
c,C
are positive constants depending only on the support of the initial distribution function.
∎

We proceed to estimate the velocity coordinates of an arbitrary geodesic on hyperbolic space with initial data in the support of the initial distribution function, by exploiting the estimate (
19
) on the radial coordinate
r(t)
.

Proposition 3.1.2
.

The velocity variables along a geodesic
\gamma
such that
\gamma(0)\in\supp(f_{0})
satisfy that for every
t\geq 0
,

(22)

|v^{\theta}\sinh r(t)|\leq\dfrac{L}{c\exp{(Et)}},\qquad E^{2}-(v^{r})^{2}\leq\dfrac{L^{2}}{c^{2}\exp{(2Et)}},

where
c>0
is a constant depending only on
\supp(f_{0})
.

Proof.

By the compact support assumption, the absolute value of the angular velocity
|l|
is uniformly bounded by a constant
L
, among all geodesics determined by the support of the initial distribution. We obtain that the angular velocity coordinate decays exponentially

|v^{\theta}\sinh r(t)|=\dfrac{|l|}{\sinh r(t)}\leq\dfrac{L}{c\exp{(Et)}},

by using the definition of
l
. Furthermore, the radial velocity coordinate converges exponentially to the particle energy

E^{2}-(v^{r})^{2}=\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}\leq\dfrac{L^{2}}{c^{2}\exp{(2Et)}},

by using the identity (
18
) relating the particle energy and the angular velocity.
∎

In particular, for every Vlasov field initially supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
, we obtain a uniform exponential decay estimate among all geodesics emanating from the support of the initial distribution.

Corollary 3.1.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
. Let
f_{0}
be a regular initial data for the Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space that is compactly supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
. The velocity variables along a geodesic
\gamma
such that
\gamma(0)\in\supp(f_{0})
satisfy that for every
t\geq 0
,

(23)

|v^{\theta}\sinh r(t)|\leq\dfrac{L}{c\exp(\alpha t)},\qquad\bigg(E^{2}-\dfrac{L^{2}}{c^{2}\exp{(2\alpha t)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|v^{r}(t)|\leq E,

where
c>0
is a constant depending only on
\supp(f_{0})
.

Proof.

It follows directly by using that
E
is bounded below by
\alpha
among all the geodesics determined by the support of the initial distribution.
∎

3.2.
Decay for Vlasov fields supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}

In this subsection, we prove exponential decay of the spatial density induced by a Vlasov field on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
compactly supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
.

Proof of the estimate (
2
).

Let us first introduce some notation. Given
x\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
we define

\Omega(t,x):=\Big\{v\in T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}:f(t,x,v)\neq 0\Big\}.

Observe that the set
\Omega(t,x)
can be considered as the domain of integration of the spatial density. Since
f(t,x,v)=f_{0}(\phi_{-t}(x,v))
, we have that

(24)

\Omega(t,x)=\Big\{v\in T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}:\text{there exists $(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\supp(f_{0})$ such that $\phi_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})=(x,v)$}\Big\},

and that
\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}=\|f(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}
.

We proceed to estimate the spatial density
\rho(f)(t,x)
. Fix
x=(r,\theta)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
and
t\geq 0
. It follows from (
23
) and (
24
) that if
(v^{\theta},v^{r})\in\Omega(t,x)
, then

(25)

|v^{\theta}\sinh r|\leq\dfrac{L}{c\exp(\alpha t)},\qquad\bigg(E^{2}-\dfrac{L^{2}}{c^{2}\exp{(2\alpha t)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|v^{r}|\leq E,

and therefore

(26)

\vol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(\Omega(t,x))=\int_{\Omega(t,x)}\sinh r\mathrm{d}v^{r}\mathrm{d}v^{\theta}\leq\frac{D_{0}}{\exp(\alpha t)},

where
D_{0}
is a positive constant depending on the support of
f_{0}
. Finally,

|\rho(f)(t,x)|=\Big|\int f(t,x,v)\sinh r\mathrm{d}v^{r}\mathrm{d}v^{\theta}\Big|\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\ \int_{\Omega(t,x)}\sinh r\mathrm{d}v^{r}\mathrm{d}v^{\theta}\lesssim\dfrac{\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}}{\exp(\alpha t)}.

∎

Next, we prove decay estimates for derivatives of the spatial density induced by Vlasov fields supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
. We consider the explicit parallel frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
set in (
10
). We observe that the frame
\{\partial_{r},(\sinh r)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\}
can be written in terms of the parallel frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
as

\partial_{r}=\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}\dot{\gamma}-\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}N,\qquad\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}=\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}\dot{\gamma}+\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}N.

In the following, we combine the class of commuting vector fields
\lambda
for the Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space, with the decay in time of the velocity support of the distribution function. We start with a remark.

Remark 3.2.1
.

Let
Z\in\lambda
. Note that
\supp(Zf_{0})\subset\supp(f_{0})
, and that since
Zf
is a solution to the Vlasov equation (see Lemma
2.3.1
), we have that
Zf(t,x,v)=Zf_{0}(\phi_{-t}(x,v))
. Then

(27)

\|Zf(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}=\|Zf(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\quad\text{ and
}\quad\{v\in T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}:Zf(t,x,v)\neq 0\}\subset\Omega(t,x).

Proof of the estimate (
3
).

Since every spatial derivative of the spatial density is equal to the spatial density of the corresponding horizontal derivative of the distribution function, we have that

(28)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{r}\rho(f)(t,x)&=\rho(\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{r})f)(t,x)=:\rho(H_{r}f)(t,x),\\
\dfrac{1}{\sinh r}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)(t,x)&=\rho\Big(\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}\Big(\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}\Big)f\Big)(t,x)=:\rho(H_{\theta}f)(t,x),\end{aligned}

where the horizontal vector fields
H_{r}
and
H_{\theta}
can be written as

(29)

H_{r}=\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}X-\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}H,\qquad H_{\theta}=\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}X+\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}H,

in terms of the vector fields
H
and
X
. According to the proof of the estimate (
2
), see (
23
), the weight
v^{r}
converges exponentially to the particle energy
E
, whereas the weight
\sinh rv^{\theta}
decays exponentially in time.

Estimate for the derivative
(\sinh r)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)
.
By the previous decomposition of
H_{\theta}
, the angular derivative
(\sinh r)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)
can be decomposed into

\dfrac{1}{\sinh r}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)(t,x)=\rho\Big(\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}Xf\Big)(t,x)+\rho\Big(\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Hf\Big)(t,x)=:A_{1}+B_{1}.

On the one hand, the first term
A_{1}
can be written using the commuting vector field
tX+Y
by

A_{1}=\dfrac{1}{t}\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}(tX+Y)f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)-\dfrac{1}{t}\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}Yf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v),

where the second term in the RHS can be integrated by parts

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}Yf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}v^{r}}{E^{2}}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)+\int\dfrac{\sinh r(v^{\theta})^{2}}{E^{2}}\partial_{v^{\theta}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{4}}(\sinh^{2}r(v^{\theta})^{2}-(v^{r})^{2})f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int\dfrac{2\sinh rv^{\theta}(v^{r})^{2}}{E^{4}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v).

We obtain that

A_{1}=\dfrac{1}{t}\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}(tX+Y)f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)+\dfrac{1}{t}\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v).

Moreover, we can make use of the decay of the velocity support of the distribution function, see (
26
), together with (
25
) and (
27
) to obtain that

\displaystyle|A_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\dfrac{1}{t}\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}(tX+Y)f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)\Big|+\Big|\dfrac{1}{t}\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{t}\|Yf_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\int_{\Omega(t,x)}\dfrac{|\sinh rv^{\theta}|}{E^{2}}\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)+\dfrac{1}{t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\int_{\Omega(t,x)}\dfrac{|\sinh rv^{\theta}|}{E^{2}}\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{te^{2\alpha t}}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}},

where in the second inequality we used (
27
) and in the third estimate we used (
25
).

On the other hand, the second term
B_{1}
can be written using the unstable vector field
U
by

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\rho\Big(\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Hf\Big)(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}(H+EV)f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)-\int\dfrac{v^{r}}{E}Vf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v),

\displaystyle=\int\dfrac{1}{e^{Et}}\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Uf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)-\int\dfrac{v^{r}}{E}Vf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v),

where the second term in the RHS can be integrated by parts

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{v^{r}}{E}Vf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\dfrac{(v^{r})^{2}}{E}\dfrac{\partial_{v^{\theta}}f}{\sinh r}\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)-\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}v^{r}}{E}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}(v^{r})^{2}}{E^{3}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)+\int\dfrac{\sinh^{3}r(v^{\theta})^{3}}{E^{3}}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v).

We obtain that

B_{1}=\int\dfrac{1}{e^{Et}}\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Uf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)-\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v).

Similarly to the estimate of
A_{1}
, we estimate
B_{1}
by combining Remark
3.2.1
, the decay of
\sinh rv^{\theta}
and the decay of the velocity support of the distribution function

\displaystyle|B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int\dfrac{1}{e^{Et}}\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Uf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)\Big|+\Big|\int\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E}f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{e^{\alpha t}}\|Uf_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\int_{\Omega(t,x)}\dfrac{|v^{r}|}{E^{2}}\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)+\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\int_{\Omega(t,x)}\dfrac{|\sinh rv^{\theta}|}{E}\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{e^{2\alpha t}}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}}.

Therefore, we obtain that the angular derivative of the spatial density
(\sinh r)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)
is bounded above by

|(\sinh r)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)|\leq|A_{1}|+|B_{1}|\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}}e^{-{2\alpha t}}.

Estimate for the derivative
\partial_{r}\rho(f)
.
By the previous decomposition of
H_{r}
, the radial derivative
\partial_{r}\rho(f)
can be decomposed into

\partial_{r}\rho(f)(t,x)=\rho\Big(\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Xf\Big)(t,x)-\rho\Big(\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}Hf\Big)(t,x)=:A_{2}-B_{2}.

First, we write
A_{2}
using the commuting vector field
tX+Y
by

A_{2}=\rho\Big(\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Xf\Big)(t,x)=\dfrac{1}{t}\int\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}(tX+Y)f\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v)-\dfrac{1}{t}\int\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Yf\dvol_{T_{x}{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}(v),

where the second term in the RHS can be integrated by parts

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{v^{r}}{E^{2}}Yf

Similarly, the second term

\displaystyle=\rho\Big(\dfrac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{E^{2}}Hf\Big)(t,x)

3.3.
Decay for compactly supported Vlasov fields

In this subsection, we prove exponential decay of the spatial density induced by a compactly supported Vlasov field on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}

Given

3.4.
Decay for Vlasov fields in higher dimensions

Similar decay estimates for the spatial density induced by a Vlasov field on hyperbolic space
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})
, can be proven by using a commuting vector field approach akin to the one considered in dimension two. The spatial density induced by a Vlasov field
f
on
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})
is given by

\rho(f)(t,x)=\int f(t,x,v)\sinh^{n-1}r\mathrm{d}v^{r}\dvol_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}(v),

in terms of the volume form on
({\mathbb{H}}^{n},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})
in local coordinates. The decay of the velocity support of the distribution function can be proven using the same strategy performed in the two dimensional case. First, one studies the ode satisfied by the first coordinate
\cosh r
of a geodesic on the hyperboloid
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
given by

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\cosh r=E^{2}\cosh r,

in terms of the particle energy
E^{2}:=g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}}(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})
. Later, one can use an orthonormal parallel frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N_{1},\dots,N_{n-1}\}
to build commuting vector fields as in Section
2.3
. Using the commuting vector fields

\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v),\quad t\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(v)+\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(v),\quad e^{\pm Et}(\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(N_{i})\pm E\mbox{Ver}_{(x,v)}(N_{i})),

we can estimate the derivatives of the spatial density

\partial_{x^{i}}\rho(f)(t,x)=\rho(\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{x^{i}})f)(t,x),

by decomposing every horizontal vector field
\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{x^{i}})
in terms of the commuting vector fields plus errors that can be controlled after integration by parts in the velocity variables. Decay estimates can finally be derived using bounds for the geodesic flow in the support of the distribution function.

4.
Decay for Vlasov fields on asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds

In this section, we prove decay estimates for the spatial density induced by a Vlasov field on a non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
. The geodesic flow in
(\mathcal{M},g)
is determined by the geodesic equations

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=v^{i},\qquad\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v^{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\Gamma^{i}_{jk}v^{j}v^{k},

in terms of the Christoffel symbols
\Gamma^{i}_{jk}
of
(\mathcal{M},g)
. The geodesic flow in
(\mathcal{M},g)
can also be viewed as a
Hamiltonian flow
in the cotangent bundle
(T^{*}\mathcal{M},\bar{g})
, where the corresponding Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
is given by

H(x,v):=\dfrac{1}{2}g^{ij}v_{i}v_{j}.

We define the
particle energy

E:T\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
of a geodesic
\gamma
by

E(x,v):=g_{x}(v,v)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{g_{ij}v^{i}v^{j}}.

The particle energy
E
is conserved along the geodesic flow. Note that the Hamiltonian
H
can be written in terms of the particle energy as
H=\frac{1}{2}E^{2}
. We also define the
angular velocity

l:T\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
of a geodesic
\gamma
by

l(x,v):=g(\partial_{\theta},\dot{\gamma}).

The angular velocity
l
is almost conserved along the geodesic flow since the vector field
\partial_{\theta}
is Killing on hyperbolic space.

Lemma 4.0.1
.

Let
E_{\mathrm{max}}>0
. Let
(x,v)\in T\mathcal{M}
such that
E(x,v)\leq E_{\mathrm{max}}
. Then, the velocity coordinates of a geodesic
\gamma
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)=v
satisfy

(33)

|v^{r}|\leq E_{\mathrm{max}}+1,\qquad|v^{\theta}\sinh r|\leq E_{\mathrm{max}}+1,

for
r
sufficiently large. Furthermore, the particle energy and the angular velocity satisfy

(34)

E^{2}=(v^{r})^{2}+\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}+O(e^{-\beta r}),\qquad l=v^{\theta}\sinh^{2}r+O(e^{-(\beta-1)r}).

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{M},g)
is asymptotically hyperbolic, we have

(35)

|E^{2}(x,v)-(v^{r})^{2}-\sinh^{2}r(v^{\theta})^{2}|=O(e^{-\beta r}).

Using this bound combined with the conservation along the geodesic flow of
E
, we obtain the estimates (
33
). By definition of the angular velocity

l=v^{r}g\Big(\frac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r},\partial_{r}\Big)\sinh r+v^{\theta}\sinh^{2}rg\Big(\frac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r},\frac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}\Big)=v^{\theta}\sinh^{2}r+O(e^{-(\beta-1)r}),

where we have used the estimates (
33
). In particular, we obtain
l^{2}=(v^{\theta})^{2}\sinh^{4}r+O(e^{-(\beta-2)r}),
which combined with (
35
) results in

E^{2}=(v^{r})^{2}+\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}+O(e^{-\beta r}).

∎

We also show that the geodesic flow converges at infinity to the geodesic flow in hyperbolic space. First, we take a look at the geodesic equations on
(\mathcal{M},g)
.

Lemma 4.0.2
.

The geodesic flow
\phi_{t}(x,v)
on
(\mathcal{M},g)
satisfies that for every
t\geq 0
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}v^{\theta}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-2\coth rv^{r}v^{\theta}+O(e^{-\beta r}),\qquad\frac{\mathrm{d}v^{r}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\cosh r\sinh r(v^{\theta})^{2}+O(e^{-\beta r}).

Proof.

By definition of the Christoffel symbols, for every
i
,
j
,
k\in\{r,\theta\}
, we have
|\Gamma_{jk}^{i}(g)-\Gamma_{jk}^{i}(g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})|=O(e^{-\beta r})
on an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold. Furthermore, we have boundedness of the components of
\gamma
by Lemma
4.0.1
. The lemma is a straightforward application of these bounds on the geodesic equations in
(\mathcal{M},g)
.
∎

4.1.
Estimates for the geodesic flow

For our purposes, we first show estimates in time for the velocity coordinates along the geodesics in
\supp(f)
.

Lemma 4.1.1
.

Let
R_{1}>0
be sufficiently large. The radial coordinate
r(t)
along a geodesic
\gamma
in
\mathcal{M}\setminus\overline{B(R_{1})}
such that
\gamma(0)\in\supp(f_{0})
satisfies that for every
t\geq 0
,

ce^{Et}\leq\sinh r(t)\leq Ce^{Et},

where
c,C
are positive constants depending only on
\supp(f_{0})
.

Proof.

From the first equation in (
34
), we have
v^{r}>\frac{\alpha}{2}>0
in the far-away region of
(\mathcal{M},g)
, for a positive constant
c^{\prime}>0
. Thus, the radial coordinate along the flow satisfies
r(t)\geq r(0)+\frac{\alpha}{2}t
. By Cauchy stability of the geodesic flow, we have
r(t)\geq 1+\frac{\alpha}{2}t
for all geodesics in
\supp(f)
. We proceed to improve this elementary estimate by using the radial geodesic equation.

The geodesic equation for the radial coordinate can be written in terms of the angular velocity by

(36)

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}v^{r}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\coth r\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}+O(e^{-\beta r}),

where we have used Lemma
4.0.1
and Lemma
4.0.2
. As a result, the quantity
\cosh r
satisfies the linear ode

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\cosh r=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\sinh rv^{r})=\cosh r(v^{r})^{2}+\cosh r\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}+O(e^{-(\beta-1)r})=E^{2}\cosh r+O(e^{-(\beta-1)r}).

The radial terms given by

c_{\pm}(t):=\cosh r(t)\pm\dfrac{1}{E}\sinh r(t)v^{r}(t)

satisfy the linear odes

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}c_{\pm}(t)

\displaystyle=\sinh rv^{r}\pm E\cosh r+O(e^{-(\beta-1)r})=\pm E\Big(\cosh r\pm\dfrac{1}{E}\sinh rv^{r}\Big)+O(e^{-(\beta-1)r})

\displaystyle=\pm Ec_{\pm}(t)+O(e^{-(\beta-1)r}).

Rearranging this ode, we obtain

\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big[c_{\pm}(t)e^{\mp Et}\Big]=e^{\mp Et}O(e^{-(\beta-1)r}),

so integrating, we have

c_{\pm}(t)=e^{\pm Et}c_{\pm}(0)+e^{\pm Et}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mp E\tau}O(e^{-(\beta-1)r(\tau)})\mathrm{d}\tau.

In particular, we have
c_{+}(t)=e^{Et}(c_{+}(0)+O(1))
and
c_{-}(t)=e^{-Et}c_{-}(0)+O(1)
by using the lower bound
r(t)\geq 1+\frac{\alpha}{2}t
for all geodesics in
\supp(f)
. Furthermore, we have

\cosh r(t)=\frac{1}{2}(c_{+}(t)+c_{-}(t))=\frac{1}{2}(e^{Et}(c_{+}(0)+O(1))+e^{-Et}c_{-}(0)+O(1)).

We observe that the identity in (
34
) relating the particle energy
E
and the angular velocity
l
implies

c_{+}(t)c_{-}(t)=\cosh^{2}r(t)-\dfrac{1}{E^{2}}\sinh^{2}r(t)v^{r}(t)^{2}=1+\dfrac{l^{2}}{E^{2}}+O(e^{-(\beta-2)r}),

which shows that the terms
c_{\pm}(0)
are strictly positive on the initial data. In particular, there are positive constants
\epsilon,M
such that
M\geq c_{\pm}(0)\geq\epsilon,
for all initial data in the support of
f_{0}
.

Hence, for any initial data in the support of the initial distribution function we bound the radial coordinate along the geodesic flow

\log(\epsilon e^{Et})\leq r(t)\leq\log(4Me^{Et}),

by applying the bounds on
c_{\pm}
. In particular, the radial coordinate along any geodesics emanating from the support of the initial distribution function satisfies

ce^{Et}\leq\sinh r(t)\leq Ce^{Et},

where
c,C
are positive constants depending only on the support of the initial distribution function.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain uniform boundedness in time of the angular velocity
l
along an arbitrary geodesic with initial data in the support of the initial distribution function.

Corollary 4.1.2
.

Let
R_{1}>0
be sufficiently large. The angular velocity
l(t)
along a geodesic
\gamma
in
\mathcal{M}\setminus\overline{B(R_{1})}
such that
\gamma(0)\in\supp(f_{0})
satisfies that for every
t\geq 0
,

|l(t)|\leq L,

where
L>0
depends only on
\supp(f_{0})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.0.2
, we have
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(v^{\theta}\sinh^{2}r)=O(e^{-(\beta-2)r}).
The corollary follows by using Lemma
4.1.1
to integrate this ode in time.
∎

We proceed to estimate the velocity coordinates of an arbitrary geodesic on
\mathcal{M}
with initial data in the support of the initial distribution function.

Proposition 4.1.3
.

Let
R_{1}>0
be sufficiently large. The velocity variables along a geodesic
\gamma
in
\mathcal{M}\setminus\overline{B(R_{1})}
such that
\gamma(0)\in\supp(f_{0})
satisfy that for every
t\geq 0
,

|v^{\theta}\sinh r(t)|\leq\dfrac{C}{\exp{(Et)}},\qquad E^{2}-(v^{r})^{2}\leq\dfrac{C}{\exp{(2Et)}},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\supp(f_{0})
.

Proof.

By the compact support assumption of the initial distribution and Corollary
4.1.2
, the angular velocity satisfies
|l(t)|\leq L
among all geodesics determined by the support of the initial distribution. We obtain that the angular velocity coordinate decays exponentially

|\sinh rv^{\theta}(t)|=\dfrac{|l|}{\sinh r(t)}+O(e^{-\beta r})\leq\dfrac{C}{\exp{(Et)}},

by using Lemma
4.0.1
. Furthermore, we obtain that the radial velocity coordinate converges exponentially to the particle energy

E^{2}-(v^{r})^{2}=\dfrac{l^{2}}{\sinh^{2}r}+O(e^{-\beta r})\leq\frac{C}{\exp{(2Et)}},

by using the identity relating the particle energy and the angular velocity in Lemma
4.0.1
.
∎

In particular, for every Vlasov field initially supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
, we obtain a uniform exponential decay estimate among all geodesics emanating from the support of the initial distribution function.

Corollary 4.1.4
.

Let
\alpha>0
. Let
R_{1}>0
be sufficiently large. Let
f_{0}
be a regular initial data for the Vlasov equation on hyperbolic space that is compactly supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
. The velocity variables along a geodesic
\gamma
in
\mathcal{M}\setminus\overline{B(R_{1})}
such that
\gamma(0)\in\supp(f_{0})
satisfy that for every
t\geq 0
,

(37)

|v^{\theta}\sinh r(t)|\leq\dfrac{C}{\exp(\alpha t)},\qquad\bigg(E^{2}-\dfrac{C}{\exp{(2\alpha t)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|v^{r}(t)|\leq E,

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
\supp(f_{0})
.

4.2.
Decay for Vlasov fields supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}

In this subsection, we prove a general dispersion estimate for the spatial density induced by a Vlasov field on a Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
. We start by introducing some notation. Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be asymptotically hyperbolic and non-trapping. By definition, there exists a compact set
K\subset\mathcal{M}
,
R_{0}>0
and a diffeomorphism
\Psi:{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\setminus\overline{B(R_{0})}\to\mathcal{M}\setminus K
, satisfying the conditions in Definition
1.1.1
. For
r>R_{0}
we define
K_{r}:=K\cup\Psi(\overline{B(r)}\setminus\overline{B(R_{0})})
, and
\widehat{K}_{r}:=\pi^{-1}(K_{r})
, where we recall that
\pi:T\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is the canonical projection. Since
(\mathcal{M},g)
is asymptotically hyperbolic, as
r\to\infty
, the spaces
(\mathcal{M}\setminus K_{r},g)
and
({\mathbb{H}}^{n}\setminus\overline{B(r)},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{n}})
are
C^{2}
-close under the map
\Psi
.

As in Subsection
3.2
, we introduce the domain of integration of the spatial density. For
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
t\geq 0
we define

\Omega(t,x)=\Big\{v\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}:f(t,x,v)\neq 0\Big\}.

Since
f(t,x,v)=f_{0}(\phi_{-t}(x,v))
, we have that

\Omega(t,x)=\Big\{v\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}:\text{there exists $(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\supp(f_{0})$ such that $\phi_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})=(x,v)$}\Big\}.

Observe that

\rho(f)(t,x)=\int_{\Omega(x,t)}f(t,x,v)\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v).

In order to prove the exponential decay in time of the spatial density, we proceed as in Subsection
3.2
to establish the decay of the volume of the velocity support of the distribution function
\Omega(t,x)
. The following lemma states that if
(\mathcal{M},g)
is asymptotically hyperbolic and non-trapping, then geodesics escape to infinity uniformly.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be asymptotically hyperbolic and non-trapping, and
Q\subset\mathcal{M}
a compact set. Assume that
f_{0}
is compactly supported and its support lies on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
, for some
\alpha>0
. Then there exists
T>0
such that
\rho(f)(t,x)=0
, for every
x\in Q
and
t\geq T
.

Proof.

From the identity (
18
), we have
v^{r}>c>0
in the far-away region of hyperbolic space, for
c>0
. Similarly, from the first equation in (
34
), we have
v^{r}>c>0
in the far-away region of
(\mathcal{M},g)
, for
c>0
. In other words, the same property holds for
\Phi^{*}g
-geodesics in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\setminus B(r)
for sufficiently large
r
, and therefore, if a
\Psi^{*}g
-geodesic enters the region
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\setminus B(r)
, it will not escape from it. Equivalently, if a
g
-geodesic enters
\mathcal{M}\setminus K_{r}
, it will stay in
\mathcal{M}\setminus K_{r}
. We choose
r
large enough so that
Q
and
\pi(\supp(f_{0}))
are subsets of
K_{r}
.

Since
(\mathcal{M},g)
is non-trapping, any non-zero vector in
\widehat{K_{r}}
eventually enters
T\mathcal{M}\setminus\widehat{K_{r}}
. Define
T
as the minimum time that a vector in
\supp(f_{0})\subset\widehat{K_{r}}
needs to flow to enter
T\mathcal{M}\setminus\widehat{K_{r}}
; this is well defined since
\supp(f_{0})
is compact and a subset of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
. Then, if
t\geq T
and
(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\supp(f_{0})
we have that
\phi_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in T\mathcal{M}\setminus\widehat{K_{r}}
. We conclude that if
t\geq T
and
x\in Q

\Omega(t,x)=\Big\{v\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}:\text{there exists $(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\supp(f_{0})$ such that $\phi_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})=(x,v)$}\Big\}=\emptyset,

and therefore
\rho(f)(t,x)=0
.
∎

Remark 4.2.1
.

Using the previous lemma, we can obtain a
non-optimal
decay estimate for the spatial density appealing to a geometric argument based on Rauch’s comparison theorem and the hyperbolic law of cosines. See Appendix
A
for a short proof of a non-optimal decay estimate for Vlasov fields on non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic Riemannian manifolds.

Finally, we proceed to prove Theorem
1.3
. First we obtain the estimate (
6
) and then the estimate (
7
).

Proof of the estimate (
6
).

Let us first introduce some notation. Given
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
t\geq 0
we define

\Omega(t,x):=\Big\{v\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}:f(t,x,v)\neq 0\Big\}.

Observe that the set
\Omega(t,x)
can be considered as the domain of integration of the spatial density. Since
f(t,x,v)=f_{0}(\phi_{-t}(x,v))
, we have that

(38)

\Omega(t,x)=\Big\{v\in T_{x}\mathcal{M}:\text{there exists $(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in\supp(f_{0})$ such that $\phi_{t}(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})=(x,v)$}\Big\},

and that
\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}=\|f(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}
.

We proceed to estimate the spatial density
\rho(f)(t,x)
. Fix
x=(r,\theta)\in\mathcal{M}
and
t\geq 0
. It follows from (
37
) and (
38
) that if
(v^{\theta},v^{r})\in\Omega(t,x)
, then

(39)

|\sinh rv^{\theta}|\leq\dfrac{C}{\exp(\alpha t)},\qquad\bigg(E^{2}-\dfrac{C}{\exp{(2\alpha t)}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq|v^{r}|\leq E,

and therefore

\vol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\Omega(t,x))=\int_{\Omega(t,x)}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)\leq\frac{D_{0}}{\exp(\alpha t)},

where

is a positive constant depending on the support of

. Finally, we have

|\rho(f)(t,x)|=\Big|\int f(t,x,v)\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)\Big|\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\ \int_{\Omega(t,x)}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)\leq\dfrac{D_{0}}{\exp(\alpha t)}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

∎

Next, we prove decay estimates for derivatives of the spatial density induced by Vlasov fields supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
. First, we consider the orthogonal parallel frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
along an arbitrary geodesic
\gamma
. We write this frame in local coordinates by

(40)

\dot{\gamma}:=v^{r}\partial_{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r},\qquad N:=-N^{r}\partial_{r}+N^{\theta}\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r},

where

defined as the unique vector field such that

g(N,N)=g(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma}),\qquad g(N,\dot{\gamma})=0,

and
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
is a positively oriented basis of
T_{x}\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, we have the estimates

(41)

\displaystyle N^{r}

\displaystyle=\sinh rv^{\theta}+O(e^{-\beta r}),\qquad

\displaystyle\partial_{v^{r}}N^{r}=O(e^{-\beta r}),\qquad

\displaystyle\partial_{v^{\theta}}N^{r}=\sinh r+O(e^{-\beta r}),

(42)

\displaystyle N^{\theta}

\displaystyle=v^{r}+O(e^{-\beta r}),\qquad

\displaystyle\partial_{v^{r}}N^{\theta}=1+O(e^{-\beta r}),\qquad

\displaystyle\partial_{v^{\theta}}N^{\theta}=O(e^{-\beta r}),

since
(\mathcal{M},g)
is asymptotically hyperbolic. We also observe that the frame
\{\partial_{r},(\sinh r)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\}
can be written in terms of the parallel frame
\{\dot{\gamma},N\}
as

\partial_{r}=\frac{N^{\theta}\dot{\gamma}-\sinh rv^{\theta}N}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}},\qquad\dfrac{\partial_{\theta}}{\sinh r}=\frac{N^{r}\dot{\gamma}+v^{r}N}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}.

Before proving the estimates in (
7
), we obtain a technical lemma concerning the decay of the solutions
q_{u}
and
q_{s}
to the Riccati equation (
14
). The estimates in the following lemma will be required to bound the error terms coming out when integrating by parts the vertical part of the unstable vector field
U
.

Lemma 4.2.2
.

The solution
q_{u}:C^{2-}(T\mathcal{M})\to(0,\infty)
to the Riccati equation (
14
) satisfies

(43)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{q_{u}}{E}-1\Big|=O(e^{-\beta r}),\qquad\Big|\partial_{v^{r}}\Big(\frac{q_{u}}{E}\Big)\Big|=O(e^{-\beta r}),\qquad\Big|\frac{1}{\sinh r}\partial_{v^{\theta}}\Big(\frac{q_{u}}{E}\Big)\Big|=O(e^{-\beta r}).

Proof.

By Hurder–Katok
[
HK90
]
the function
q_{u}
belongs to
C^{2-}(T\mathcal{M})
. We recall that the function
q_{u}
parametrizes the unstable invariant distribution
E_{u}
of the geodesic flow in
(\mathcal{M},g)
. See Section
2.2.2
for more details. The distribution
E_{u}
is also the tangent of the unstable manifolds of the geodesic flow. By the standard stable manifold theorem
[
KH95
, Chapter 17]
applied to the geodesic flow in
(\mathcal{M},g)
, the function
q_{u}
appears when proving the contraction property of the action of the flow map
\phi_{t}
on unstable graphs along the flow. Since
(\mathcal{M},g)
is asymptotically hyperbolic, the generator of the geodesic flow
X
converges like
e^{-\beta r}
to the generator
X_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}
of the geodesic flow in hyperbolic space. Thus, the unstable manifolds associated to the geodesic flow in
(\mathcal{M},g)
converge like
e^{-\beta r}
to the unstable manifolds associated to the geodesic flow in
({\mathbb{H}}^{2},g_{{\mathbb{H}}^{2}})
. In particular, we obtain the estimates stated above.
∎

The proof of Lemma
4.2.2
follows the same strategy applied by Hintz
[
Hin21
]
to show a stable manifold theorem for a
C^{k}
normally hyperbolic flow converging to a
C^{k}
stationary flow. Specifically,
[
Hin21
]
shows that the stable and unstable manifolds of a perturbation of the normally hyperbolic flow converge in
C^{k}
to their stationary counterparts.

Proof of the estimate (

Since every spatial derivative of the spatial density is equal to the spatial density of the corresponding horizontal derivative of the distribution function, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\rho(f)(t,x)

\displaystyle=\rho(\mbox{Hor}_{(x,v)}(\partial_{r})f)(t,x)=:\rho(H_{r}f)(t,x),

where the horizontal vector fields

and

can be written as

in terms of the vector fields

and

.

Estimate for the derivative

.
By the previous decomposition of

can be decomposed into

On the one hand, the first term

can be written using the commuting vector field

\displaystyle\int\frac{N^{r}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}Yf\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{N^{r}v^{r}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{N^{r}v^{r}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{\partial_{v^{r}}N^{r}v^{r}+N^{r}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int\frac{N^{r}v^{r}(\partial_{v^{r}}N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}\partial_{v^{r}}N^{\theta}+N^{\theta})}{(N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta})^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v).

\displaystyle|C_{1}|

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle|D_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\Big|\frac{N^{r}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}q_{u}\Big|f+\Big|\frac{v^{r}N^{r}E}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\partial_{v^{r}}\Big(\frac{q_{u}}{E}\Big)\Big|f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int\Big|\frac{N^{r}v^{r}N^{\theta}q_{u}}{(N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta})^{2}}\Big|f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)+e^{-2\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-2\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

We obtain that

B_{1}=\int\dfrac{1}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}q_{u}d\tau}}\frac{v^{r}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}Uf\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)-D_{1}+D_{2}.

Using the same arguments for the first term of
B_{1}
and applying the estimates derived for
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int\dfrac{1}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}q_{u}d\tau}}\frac{v^{r}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}Uf\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)\Big|+|D_{1}|+|D_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{e^{2\alpha t}}\|Uf_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\frac{1}{e^{2\alpha t}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{e^{2\alpha t}}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}}.

Therefore, we obtain that the angular derivative of the spatial density
(\sinh r)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)
is bounded above by

|(\sinh r)^{-1}\partial_{\theta}\rho(f)|\leq|A_{1}|+|B_{1}|\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}}e^{-{2\alpha t}}.

Estimate for the derivative
\partial_{r}\rho(f)
.
By the previous decomposition of
H_{r}
, the radial derivative
\partial_{r}\rho(f)
can be decomposed into

\partial_{r}\rho(f)(t,x)=\rho\Big(\frac{N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}Xf\Big)-\rho\Big(\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}Hf\Big)=:A_{2}-B_{2}.

First, we write
A_{2}
using the commuting vector field
tX+Y
by

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=\rho\Big(\frac{N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}Xf\Big)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{t}\int\frac{N^{\theta}(tX+Y)f}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)-\dfrac{1}{t}\int\frac{N^{\theta}Yf}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v),

where the second term in the RHS can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\int\frac{N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}Yf\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{N^{\theta}v^{r}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{N^{\theta}v^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\partial_{v^{\theta}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=E_{1}+E_{2}.

Integrating by parts the first term, we have

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{N^{\theta}v^{r}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{\partial_{v^{r}}N^{\theta}v^{r}+N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int\frac{N^{\theta}v^{r}(\partial_{v^{r}}N^{\theta}v^{r}+N^{\theta}+\sinh rv^{\theta}\partial_{v^{r}}N^{r})}{(N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r})^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v).

We make use of the decay of the velocity support of the distribution together with (
39
), (
41
), and (
42
), to obtain

\displaystyle|E_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\Big|\frac{N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\Big|f+\frac{(N^{\theta})^{2}|v^{r}|}{(N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r})^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)+e^{-\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

Integrating by parts the second term, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{N^{\theta}v^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\partial_{v^{\theta}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{\partial_{v^{\theta}}N^{\theta}v^{\theta}+N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int\frac{N^{\theta}v^{\theta}(\partial_{v^{\theta}}N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}\partial_{v^{\theta}}N^{r}+\sinh rN^{r})}{(N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r})^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v).

We make use of the decay of the velocity support of the distribution together with (
39
), (
41
), and (
42
), to obtain

\displaystyle|E_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\Big|\frac{N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\Big|f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int\frac{|N^{\theta}N^{r}v^{\theta}\sinh r|}{(N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r})^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)+e^{-2\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

Using the same arguments for the first term of
A_{2}
and applying the estimates derived for
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|A_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\dfrac{1}{t}\int\frac{N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}(tX+Y)f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)\Big|+\dfrac{1}{t}|E_{1}|+\dfrac{1}{t}|E_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{te^{\alpha t}}\|Yf_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\dfrac{1}{te^{\alpha t}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{te^{\alpha t}}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}}.

Similarly, the second term
B_{2}
can be written using the unstable vector field
U
by

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\rho\Big(\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}Hf\Big)(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}(H+q_{u}V)f}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)-\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}q_{u}Vf}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\dfrac{1}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}q_{u}d\tau}}\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}Uf}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)-\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}q_{u}Vf}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v),

where the second term in the RHS can be integrated by parts

\displaystyle\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}q_{u}Vf}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}q_{u}\dfrac{\partial_{v^{\theta}}f}{\sinh r}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}q_{u}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=:F_{1}-F_{2}.

Integrating by parts the first term
F_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{\theta}E}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\frac{q_{u}}{E}\dfrac{\partial_{v^{\theta}}f}{\sinh r}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}\partial_{v^{\theta}}N^{\theta}+\sinh rN^{\theta}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\dfrac{q_{u}f}{\sinh r}+\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{\theta}\partial_{v^{\theta}}E}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}\frac{q_{u}f}{E\sinh r}\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{\theta}E}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\dfrac{\partial_{v^{\theta}}}{\sinh r}\Big(\frac{q_{u}}{E}\Big)f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{\theta}(\partial_{v^{\theta}}N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+N^{r}\sinh r+v^{r}\partial_{v^{\theta}}N^{\theta})}{(N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta})^{2}}\dfrac{q_{u}}{\sinh r}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v).

We make use of the decay of the velocity support of the distribution together with (
39
), (
41
), and (
42
), to obtain

\displaystyle|F_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\Big|\frac{N^{\theta}q_{u}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\Big|f+\Big|\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{\theta}E}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\frac{\partial_{v^{\theta}}}{\sinh r}\Big(\frac{q_{u}}{E}\Big)\Big|f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int\frac{|\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{\theta}N^{r}|}{(N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta})^{2}}q_{u}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)+e^{-\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

Integrating by parts the second term
F_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}E}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\frac{q_{u}}{E}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}\partial_{v^{r}}N^{r}}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}q_{u}f+\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}E}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\partial_{v^{r}}\Big(\frac{q_{u}}{E}\Big)f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int\frac{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}(\partial_{v^{r}}N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+N^{\theta}+v^{r}\partial_{v^{r}}N^{\theta})}{(N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta})^{2}}q_{u}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}\partial_{v^{r}}E}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\frac{q_{u}}{E}\partial_{v^{r}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v).

We make use of the decay of the velocity support of the distribution together with (
39
), (
41
), and (
42
), to obtain

\displaystyle|F_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int\Big|\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}E}{N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta}}\partial_{v^{r}}\Big(\frac{q_{u}}{E}\Big)\Big|f+\frac{|N^{\theta}N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}q_{u}|}{(N^{r}\sinh rv^{\theta}+v^{r}N^{\theta})^{2}}f\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)+\frac{\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}}{e^{\alpha t}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\alpha t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

Using the same arguments for the first term of
B_{2}
and applying the estimates derived for
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|B_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int\dfrac{1}{e^{\int_{0}^{t}q_{u}d\tau}}\frac{\sinh rv^{\theta}}{N^{\theta}v^{r}+\sinh rv^{\theta}N^{r}}Uf\dvol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(v)\Big|+|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{e^{3\alpha t}}\|Uf_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\dfrac{1}{e^{\alpha t}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{e^{\alpha t}}\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}}.

Finally, we obtain that the radial derivative of the spatial density
\partial_{r}\rho(f)
is bounded above by

|\partial_{r}\rho(f)|\leq|A_{2}|+|B_{2}|\lesssim\|f_{0}\|_{W^{1,\infty}_{x,v}}e^{-{\alpha t}}.

∎

Appendix A
Proof of (non-optimal) decay for Vlasov fields supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}

Finally, we obtain a non-optimal decay estimate for Vlasov fields on a non-trapping asymptotically hyperbolic manifold supported on
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}
. The following proposition follows by using Rauch comparison theorem and the hyperbolic law of cosines.

Proposition A.0.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
. Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be asymptotically hyperbolic and non-trapping. Let
f_{0}
be an initial data for the Vlasov equation on
(\mathcal{M},g)
that is compactly supported on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}
. Then, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\epsilon}\geq 0
, such that the spatial density induced by the corresponding Vlasov field
f
satisfies

|\rho(f)(t,x)|\leq\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{\exp{(\alpha(n-1)(1-\epsilon)t)}}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}},

for every
t\geq 0
and every
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta>0
and choose
r
sufficiently large such that the sectional curvatures of
\Phi^{*}g
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\setminus\overline{B(r)}
lies in
[-1-\delta,-1+\delta]
, or equivalently, that the same holds for the sectional curvatures of
g
in
\mathcal{M}\setminus K_{r}
. We assume that
\supp(f_{0})\subset\widehat{K_{r}}
and we set
D=\mbox{\rm diam}(\widehat{K_{r}})
. It suffices to prove the estimate when
x
is at distance at least
3D
from
K_{r}
(see Lemma
4.2.1
). In order to bound
\rho(f)(t,x)
we will estimate
\vol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}\Omega(x,t)
. Firstly, note that if
v\in\Omega(t,x)
, then
\phi_{t}(x,-v)\in\widehat{K}_{r},
and
|v|_{g}\geq\alpha
. In particular, if
v_{1},v_{2}\in\Omega(t,x),
then
d(\phi_{t}(x,-v_{1}),\phi_{t}(x,-v_{2}))\leq D.
By the triangle inequality we obtain

(44)

d(\phi_{t-{2D}/{|v_{1}|_{g}}}(x,-v_{1}),\phi_{t-{2D}/{|v_{2}|_{g}}}(x,-v_{2}))\leq 5D.

Note that the geodesic triangle with vertices
x
,
\phi_{t-{2D}/{|v_{1}|_{g}}}(x,-v_{1})
, and
\phi_{t-{2D}/{|v_{2}|_{g}}}(x,-v_{2})
is contained in
\mathcal{M}\setminus K_{r}
, where the sectional curvature is bounded between
-1-\delta
and
-1+\delta
. It follows from inequality (
44
), the Rauch comparison theorem and the hyperbolic law of cosines that the angle between
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
is smaller than
Ce^{-\alpha\sqrt{1-\delta}t}
, for some constant
C
that depends on the support of
f_{0}
and
\delta
, but it is independent of
t
and
x
. We conclude that
\vol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}\Omega(x,t)\leq C^{\prime}e^{-\alpha(n-1)\sqrt{1-\delta}t}
, and therefore

|\rho(f)(t,x)|\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\vol_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}\Omega(x,t)\leq C^{\prime}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}e^{-\alpha(n-1)\sqrt{1-\delta}t},

for some constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\delta,f_{0})
independent of
t
and
x
.
∎

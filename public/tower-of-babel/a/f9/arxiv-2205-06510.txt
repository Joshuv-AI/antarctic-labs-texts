Representations of the Kottwitz gerbes
Sergei Iakovenko

Abstract
. Let F F be a local or global field and let G G be a linear algebraic group over F F . We study Tannakian categories of representations of the Kottwitz gerbes Rep ⁡ ( Kt F ) \operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F}) and the functor G ↦ B ⁡ ( F , G ) G\mapsto B(F,G) defined by Kottwitz in [ 28 ] . In particular, we show that if F F is a function field of a curve over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} , then Rep ⁡ ( Kt F ) \operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F}) is equivalent to the category of Drinfeld isoshtukas. In the case of number fields, we establish the existence of various fiber functors on Rep ⁡ ( Kt ℚ ) \operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}) and its subcategories and show that Scholze’s conjecture [ 41 , Conjecture 9.5] follows from the full Tate conjecture over finite fields [ 47 ] .



Introduction

0.1.
Context

Let
F
be a local or global field and let
G
be a linear algebraic group over
F
. In
[
28
]
, Kottwitz introduced a functor
B(F,G)
from linear algebraic groups over
F
to pointed sets. We geometrize a purely cohomological construction of Kottwitz and study categories
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
that are Tannakian categories over
F
attached to Kottwitz gerbes
\operatorname{Kt}_{F}
, which are the main ingredients of the construction of
B(F,G)
.

In order to sketch the definition of
\operatorname{Kt}_{F}
and
B(F,G)
, we first recall Tate-Nakayama isomorphisms for local and global fields. Let
K/F
be a finite Galois extension. If
F
is a local field, we set
X_{K/F}=\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{D}_{K/F}=\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

If
F
is a global field, then a
\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)
-module
X_{K/F}
is defined as the kernel of the following homomorphism of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)
-modules

\bigoplus_{v\in V_{K/F}}{\mathbb{Z}}\cdot v\xrightarrow{\deg}{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\sum_{v}n_{v}v\mapsto\sum_{v}n_{v}

where
V_{K/F}
is the set of all places of
K
over
F
. Then we define
\mathbb{D}_{K/F}
as a pro-torus over
F
, whose group of characters is
X_{K/F}
.

There are canonical classes
\alpha_{K/F}\in H^{2}(\operatorname{Gal}(K/F),\mathbb{D}_{K/F}(K))
, such that
the cup product

(0.1)

H^{0}(\operatorname{Gal}(K/F),X_{K/F})\xrightarrow{\cup\,\alpha_{K/F}}H^{2}(\operatorname{Gal}(K/F),\mathbb{G}_{m}(K))=\operatorname{Br}(K/F)

is an isomorphism of Tate cohomology groups. Note that the existence of
\alpha_{K/F}
in the global cases was shown by Tate in
[
46
]
, where he also established Tate-Nakayama isomorphisms for cohomology of algebraic tori over global fields.

Kottwitz used the canonical classes
\alpha_{K/F}
, or rather the extensions

(0.2)

1\to\mathbb{D}_{K/F}(K)\to\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}\to\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\to 1

corresponding to these classes, to construct the functor
G\mapsto B(F,G)
. More precisely, for any finite Galois extension
K/F
, Kottwitz
[
28
, Section 2]
defined the set of equivalence classes of algebraic
1
-cocycles of
\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}
with values in
G(K)
that is denoted
H^{1}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F},G(K))
. If
L\supset K\supset F
is a tower of finite Galois extensions, then there is a naturally defined map of pointed sets
H^{1}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F},G(K))\to H^{1}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\operatorname{Kt}_{L/F},G(L))
arising from a compatibility between the canonical classes
\alpha_{L/F}
and
\alpha_{K/F}
. Subsequently, in
[
28
, Section 10]
the pointed set
B(F,G)
is defined as follows

B(F,G)=\varinjlim_{K}H^{1}_{\operatorname{alg}}(\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F},G(K)),

where the colimit is taken over the set of finite Galois extensions
K/F
contained in a fixed separable closure
\bar{F}
of
F
. Moreover, the compatibility mentioned above gives rise to the canonical class
\alpha_{F}
given by the limit of
\alpha_{K/F}
(see Section
4
).

Remarkably, Kottwitz’ construction of
B(F,G)
works uniformly over local and global fields of any characteristic and has other surprising properties. For instance,
B(F,G)
behaves nicely under localization, meaning that if
F
is a global field, there are naturally defined maps
B(F,G)\to B(F_{v},G)
, where
F_{v}
is the completion of
F
with respect to a chosen valuation. In particular, one gets such a localization in the case where
F
is
\mathbb{Q}
and
v
is archimedean, that is
F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
. In
[
16
, Section 5]
Fargues introduced a twistor projective line
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}
and proved that
B(\mathbb{R},G)=H^{1}_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{\mathbb{R}},G)
. The relation between
B(\mathbb{R},G)
and Shimura data was investigated by Jaburi in his Master’s thesis
[
21
]
.

On the other hand, the local
B(F_{v},G)
’s for non-archimedean places have long been known for their relation to the theory of isocrystals, which are objects of semi-linear algebra playing an important role in studying
p
-divisible groups, special fibers of Shimura varieties, and the geometry of the Fargues-Fontaine curve.

The category of isocrystals is defined as follows. For simplicity, let
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with the residue field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, also denote by
\mathbb{F}
the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
\breve{F}
denote the field of fractions of the ring of Witt vectors of
\mathbb{F}
. Note that
\breve{F}
is the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Additionally,
\breve{F}
is endowed with the action of the topological generator of the Galois group of
\mathbb{F}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which we denote by
\sigma
.

An

-isocrystal over
\mathbb{F}
is a finite-dimensional vector space

over
\breve{F}
with a
\sigma
-linear isomorphism
\alpha:V\xrightarrow{{\smash{\sim}}}V
.
F
-isocrystals over an algebraically closed field were classified by Dieudonné and Manin (see
[
14
]
and
[
31
]
).

In
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
, Kottwitz introduced

-isocrystals with

-structure while studying
\sigma
-conjugacy classes of linear algebraic groups over non-archimedean local fields. In particular, he proved the following:

Theorem 0.1

(Kottwitz
[
27
]
)
.

Let

be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic

. Then

is isomorphic to the set of
\otimes
-isomorphism classes of exact

-functors from the category of representations of

to the category of
F

In particular,

is identified with the set of isomorphism classes of

-isocrystals of dimension

.

Note that in
[
28
]
, Kottwitz does not provide a similar interpretation of
B(F,G)
for global fields even in the case

. Using the foundational work of Giraud on non-abelian cohomology
[
18
]
and the paper of Breen

and the extensions

can be interpreted in terms of gerbes. Recall that gerbes can be viewed as stacks that are locally equivalent to stacks of torsors for some sheaf of groups.

Scholze suggested to apply general Tannakian duality to the Kottwitz gerbes
\operatorname{Kt}_{F}
. The duality was introduced by Saavedra Rivano
[
40
]
and completed by Deligne
[
9
]
, it produces Tannakian categories

The significance of

was emphasized in the ICM talk of Scholze (see Section 9 in
[
41
]
), where he noticed the analogy between

and the category of isoshtukas over curves over finite fields. The latter was introduced by Drinfeld in his work on the Langlands correspondence in the function field case (see
[
15
]
). Scholze also stated the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1

(Scholze
[
41
]
)
.

There is a Weil cohomology theory

for varieties

over

\mathbb{F}

\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Under the functor
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})\to\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})
, it maps to crystalline

\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})\to\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}_{l}})

, it maps to étale
cohomology. Under the restriction
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})\to\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{R}})

0.2.
Summary of contents and main results

This work is organized as follows. In the first sections, we recall main definitions related to gerbes and present several approaches to gerbes and their categories of representations. We also prove a general result on gerbes with a smooth band that extends several well-known comparison isomorphisms into the setting of non-abelian
H^{2}
, namely we show that
H^{2}_{\operatorname{sm}}(F,G)=H^{2}_{\operatorname{fppf}}(F,G)
, where
G
is a smooth algebraic group over
F
(see Theorem
3.1
.)

In Section
6
, we study categories of representations of Kottwitz gerbes with
G
-structure, namely the categories of
\otimes
-functors
\operatorname{Rep}(G)\to\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
. It is shown in Proposition
6.1
that the set of
\otimes
-isomorphism classes of such functors is identified with
B(F,G)
.

In Section
7
, we describe
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
in the non-archimedean local cases, re-establishing the equivalence between
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
and the category of isocrystals in a very explicit fashion in contrast to a somewhat more abstract comparison that existed in the literature (see
[
26
]
). Namely, using the explicit definition of the category of representations of
\operatorname{Kt}_{F}
given in Section
5.3
and main results of local class field theory, we prove the following:

Theorem 0.2

(Theorem
7.1
)
.

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field. Then the category of representations of the Kottwitz gerbe
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
is equivalent to the category of pairs
(V,\rho)
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\breve{F}
, and
\rho:V\xrightarrow{{\smash{\sim}}}V
is a
\sigma
-linear automorphism.

The case of archimedean local fields is treated in Section
8
, where we prove the following:

Theorem 0.3

(Theorem
8.1
)
.

The category of representations of the real Kottwitz gerbe
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{R}})
is equivalent to the category of finite dimensional
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded complex vector spaces with semi-linear automorphism
\alpha
, such that
\alpha^{2}
acts on
V^{m}
by
(-1)^{m}
.

In Section
9
we get the case of global fields off the ground by showing that
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
has a fibre functor in any extension
\widetilde{F}/F
of cohomological dimension
\leq 1
. In particular, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 0.4

(Theorem
9.1
)
.

•

\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})
has a fiber functor in finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cycl}}
, where
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cycl}}
is the extension of
\mathbb{Q}
obtained by adjoining all roots of unity.

•

If
F
is the function field of an algebraic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
has a fiber functor in finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\breve{F}=F\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}
.

Roughly speaking, the first statement says that
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})
can be described as the category of representations of the protorus
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}
over
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cycl}}
with extra data corresponding to descent from
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cycl}}
to
\mathbb{Q}
. Unfortunately, this descent data cannot be made sufficiently explicit at the moment, due to its highly abstract nature.

The second part of Theorem
9.1
leads us to the work of Drinfeld on shtukas and Langlands correspondence for curves over a finite field. Namely, in
[
15
]
Drinfeld introduced the category of isoshtukas that is defined as the category of pairs
(V,\varphi)
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional vector space over
F\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}
, and
\varphi
is a
\operatorname{Frob}
-linear automorphism of

, where
\operatorname{Frob}
is a toplogical generator of the Galois group of
\mathbb{F}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

In Section
10
, we obtain one of the main technical ingredients needed for the comparison of
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
and the category of isoshtukas. Namely, in Theorem
10.1
we establish a local-global principle for Tannakian categories over

banded by

, where
\mathbb{D}_{F}
is the pro-torus with the module of characters
X_{F}=\varinjlim X_{K/F}
.

The classification of isoshtukas obtained by Drinfeld combined with local Tate’s duality and the local-global principle give us the following result:

Theorem 0.5

(Theorem
12.1
)
.

Let
F
be a function field of a smooth algebraic curve over a finite field. Then the category of representations of the Kottwitz gerbe
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
is equivalent to the category of Drinfeld isoshutkas.

As a consequence, we obtain the following description of
B(F,G)
for global function fields:

Theorem 0.6

(Theorem

12.2
)
.

Let
F
be a global function field and let
G
be a linear algebraic group over

is isomorphic to the set of isomorphism classes of isoshtukas with
G
-structure.

Note that
G

were recently studied by Hamacher and Kim
[
20
]
from a somewhat different perspective. Namely, they defined isoshtukas as
G
-torsors

over
F\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathbb{F}
together with an isomorphism
\operatorname{Frob}^{\ast}V\simeq V
and classified them very explicitly

by Steinberg theorem (see

in

are isomorphic and the set of isomorphism classes in
\operatorname{Hom}^{\otimes}(\operatorname{Rep}(G),\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F}))
can be computed after fixing fibre functors. A standard Tannakian argument (see
[

) shows that the set of isomorphism classes of

.

As it is often the case for seemingly analogous results for function fields and number fields, the study of the Kottwitz gerbes and their representations over number fields is admittedly more complicated. One can study
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})
via its subcategories given by the categories of representations of algebraic quotients of the Kottwitz gerbe
\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Recall that a gerbe is called algebraic if it is locally equivalent to a stack of torsors for an algebraic group.

In Section
13
, we prove the following refinement of the results on the existence of fibre functors.
Let
\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}
be an algebraic quotient
\operatorname{Kt}_{K,S}
of
\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where

is a finite Galois extension and

).

Theorem 0.7

(Theorem

)
.

For any algebraic quotient
\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime}
of
\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}
as above, there exists infinitely many rational primes

\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime})
has a fiber functor in finite-dimensional vector spaces over the
q
th cyclotomic field

The main advantage here is that we are reduced to finite cyclic Galois extensions that are ramified at a unique finite prime, namely at

\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\prime})
more concrete and easier to handle, as compared to the situation of Theorem

.

Finally, in Section

Theorem 0.8

(Corollary
14.1
)
.

Scholze’s Conjecture
1
follows from the Tate’s conjecture over finite fields.

The key ingredient in proving this result is the explicit description of the category of numerical motives

obtained by Langlands and Rapoport in

under the assumption of the Tate conjecture (see Conjecture

and

.

1.
Gerbes: from abstract definitions to group extensions

A gerbe
\mathcal{E}
over a site
S
is defined as a stack fibred in groupoids that satisfies the following extra conditions:

•

There exists a cover
U\in\operatorname{Ob}(S)
such that the fibre groupoid
\mathcal{E}_{U}
is not empty.

•

For all
U\in\operatorname{Ob}(S)
, any two objects
x,y\in\mathcal{E}_{U}
are locally isomorphic, i.e. there exists a cover
U^{\prime}\xrightarrow{p}U
such that
p^{\ast}x\simeq p^{\ast}y
.

In this work we consider fppf, fpqc and smooth sites over a field
F
. Gerbes form a
2
-subcategory of the
2
-category of stacks.

We refer the reader to
[
18
]
for generalities on non-abelian cohomology and gerbes.

1.1.
Local description of gerbes

We recall several results on local description of gerbes (see Section 4 in
[
5
]
). Firstly, we sketch the main steps in describing a gerbe via local data consisting of a concrete bitorsor satisfying several compatibility conditions, we proceed further by describing
1
- and
2
-morphisms of gerbes via local data.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a gerbe over
S
. Choosing a cartesian section
x
of
\mathcal{E}
over some
U\in\operatorname{Ob}(S)
, we obtain an equivalence

(1.1)

\Phi:\mathcal{E}\times U\to\operatorname{TORS}(U,G)

that sends an object
y
of the fiber groupoid
\mathcal{E}_{V}
over
V\xrightarrow{p}U
to the torsor
\operatorname{Isom}(p^{\ast}x,y)
. In this situation, we say that
G=\operatorname{Aut}(x)
is the band of
\mathcal{E}
, or that
\mathcal{E}
is banded by
G
. When
G
is a smooth algebraic group scheme,
\mathcal{E}
is called a smooth algebraic gerbe.

Throughout the rest of the section, we assume that all gerbes are banded by smooth bands, since it is always the case in our applications.

After pulling back the equivalence (
1.1
) to
U\times U
, we get an equivalence of stacks over
U^{2}

(1.2)

\varphi:p_{1}^{\ast}\Phi\circ(p_{2}^{\ast}\Phi)^{-1}:\operatorname{TORS}(U^{2},p_{1}^{\ast}G)\to\operatorname{TORS}(U^{2},p_{2}^{\ast}G),

which restricts to the identity on the diagonal.

Using the definition of
\Phi
and the fact that the category of equivalences between
\operatorname{TORS}(U\times U,p_{1}^{\ast}G)
and
\operatorname{TORS}(U\times U,p_{2}^{\ast}G)
is equivalent to the category of
(p_{2}^{\ast}G,p_{1}^{\ast}G)
-bitorsors (see Chap. IV, Prop. 5.2.5 in
[
18
]
), we may pass to the
(p_{2}^{\ast}G,p_{1}^{\ast}G)
-bitorsor

(1.3)

E=\operatorname{Isom}(p_{1}^{\ast}x,p_{2}^{\ast}x)

over
U^{2}
, whose set of sections over
V\xrightarrow{(f,g)}U^{2}
is given by the set
\operatorname{Isom}(f^{\ast}x,g^{\ast}x)
of arrows in the groupoid
\mathcal{E}_{V}
, moreover,
E
restricts to
G=\operatorname{Aut}(x)
on the diagonal
\Delta:U\to U^{2}
.

Note that the assumption that
G
is smooth implies that
E
is representable by a smooth scheme over
U^{2}
. Additionally, since
E
is a gerbe, the projection map
E\to U^{2}
is an epimorphism. Combining the latter with the monomorphism
\Delta^{\ast}(E)\to E
we obtain the following sequence of morphisms

(1.4)

G=\operatorname{Aut}(x)\to E=\operatorname{Isom}(p_{1}^{\ast}(x),p_{2}^{\ast}(x))\to U^{2}.

In order to define the descent data on
\operatorname{TORS}(U,G)
, the equivalence (
1.2
) and therefore the bitorsor
E
must satisfy several compatibility conditions after pulling back to
U^{3}
and
U^{4}
; namely, the condition over
U^{3}
translates into an isomorphism of
(p^{\ast}_{1}G,p^{\ast}_{3}G)
-bitorsors

(1.5)

\psi:p^{\ast}_{12}E\wedge p^{\ast}_{23}E\xrightarrow{{\smash{\sim}}}p^{\ast}_{13}E

given by associating an element of
\operatorname{Isom}(p^{\ast}_{3}(x),p^{\ast}_{1}(x))
to the composition of
f\in\operatorname{Isom}(p^{\ast}_{2}x,p^{\ast}_{1}x)
and
g\in\operatorname{Isom}(p^{\ast}_{3}x,p^{\ast}_{2}x)
. The condition over
U^{4}
boils down to the equality of the two possible induced maps

(1.6)

p^{\ast}_{12}E\wedge p^{\ast}_{23}\wedge p^{\ast}_{34}E\to p^{\ast}_{14}E.

1.2.
Local description of morphisms between gerbes

Given a gerbe
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
with a section
y\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{U}
such that
\operatorname{Aut}(y)=H
, where
H
is a smooth algebraic group over
U
, we let
\varphi^{\prime}
and
\psi^{\prime}
denote the equivalence analogous to (
1.2
) and the cocycle isomorphism (
1.5
) attached to
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
y
, respectively. One describes a morphism
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
locally as follows. Note that giving a morphism
f:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is equivalent to giving a morphism
f_{U}:\operatorname{TORS}(U,G)\to\operatorname{TORS}(U,H)
and an isomorphism

(1.7)

\alpha:p^{\ast}_{2}f_{U}\circ\varphi\xrightarrow{{\smash{\sim}}}\varphi^{\prime}\circ p^{\ast}_{1}f_{U}

that is compatible with the cocycle isomorphisms
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
over
U^{3}
.

Let
E^{\prime}=\operatorname{Isom}(p^{\ast}_{1}(y),p^{\ast}_{2}(y))
be the
(p^{\ast}_{1}H,p^{\ast}_{2}H)
-bitorsor attached to
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and a trivializing object
y\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{U}
. Assuming for simplicity that
f_{U}(x)\simeq y
in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{U}
, we obtain a morphism of bitorsors
\alpha\circ p_{2}^{\ast}f_{U}:E\to E^{\prime}
over
U^{2}
compatible with the cocycle isomorphisms and whose restriction to the diagonal defines a morphism
\nu:G\to H
of smooth algebraic groups over
U
.

1.3.
Specializing to the case of Galois covers

Let
U=\operatorname{Spec}(K)
, where
K/F
is a Galois extension. In this case, we have the isomorphism
\operatorname{Spec}(K)\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(F)}\operatorname{Spec}(K)=\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\times\operatorname{Spec}(K)
dual to the isomorphism
K\otimes_{F}K\to\prod_{\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)}K
given by sending
a\otimes b
to
(a\sigma(b))_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}{K/F}}
. Using this isomorphism and taking
U
-points of schemes in (
1.4
), we obtain the short exact sequence of groups

(1.8)

1\to G(K)\to E(K)\to\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\to 1.

For brevity, extensions of the form (
1.8
) together with a sheaf of groups
G
over
\operatorname{Spec}(U)
are called
K/F
-Galois gerbes.

The group structure on
E(K)
is given by the isomorphism
\psi
in (
1.5
). More explicitly, we identify
U^{3}
with
U\times\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\times\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)
, so that
p^{\ast}_{1}(x)=x
,
p^{\ast}_{2}(x)=\sigma^{\ast}x
, and

3.1.

Definition 2

Given a stack

over

3.2.
Inverse and direct images of gerbes

If

is a gerbe over

(see III. Corollaire 2.1.5.6 in

From now on, we assume that

is a gerbe over

banded by a sheaf of groups

We would like to deduce a sufficient criterion for

over

There is a canonical isomorphism of sheaves between

). This implies that

is a gerbe over

Moreover, there is an exact sequence of pointed sets (see
[

, V. Prop. 3.1.3]
)

(3.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where the first arrow is induced by an equivalence of stacks

Let

We remark that the proof follows the lines of

Remark 1
.

If

that can be composed with with the quotient by

which gives us a section

since the morphism above factors through

(locally isomorphic to

is equivalent to the category of pairs

where

Note that

is defined by adjunction

. In this case we have an isomorphism of

by

We obtain a cartesian functor from the

The previous argument shows that any

4.
Definition of Kottwitz gerbes

4.1.
Local fields

Let
F
be a local field and let
K/F
be a finite Galois extension with the Galois group
\Gamma_{K/F}
. Then there is an isomorphism between Tate cohomology groups

(4.1)

H^{0}(\Gamma_{K/F},{\mathbb{Z}})\xrightarrow{{\smash{\sim}}}H^{2}(\Gamma_{K/F},K^{\times})

given by the cup product with the canonical class of local class field theory

\alpha_{K/F}\in H^{2}(\Gamma_{K/F},\operatorname{Hom}({\mathbb{Z}},K^{\times})).

Using the fact that the latter cohomology group also classifies the extensions of
\Gamma_{K/F}
by
K^{\times}
, we define
\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}
as an extension

(4.2)

1\to K^{\times}\to\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}\to\Gamma_{K/F}\to 1

corresponding to the class
\alpha_{K/F}
. Hilbert’s Theorem 90 ensures that
\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}
is essentially well-defined, namely, it is defined up to the conjugation by an element of
K^{\times}
.

In order to define
\operatorname{Kt}_{F}
, Kottwitz considered the limit of
\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}
over finite Galois extensions
K/F
. More accurately, consider
F\subset K\subset L
such that
L
is finite Galois over
F
, then the canonical class
\alpha_{K/F}
can be considered as an element of
H^{2}(\Gamma_{L/F},L^{\times})
via inflation, to which one associates a gerbe
\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}^{\operatorname{inf}}
(see Section 2.10 in
[
28
]
). Moreover, there is a relation

(4.3)

\operatorname{inf}(\alpha_{K/F})=[L:K]\alpha_{L/F},

which implies the existence of a homomorphism
\operatorname{Kt}_{L/F}\to\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}^{\operatorname{inf}}
that is given by the map
p_{L/K}:x\mapsto x^{[L:K]}
when restricted to
L^{\times}
.

Therefore,
\operatorname{Kt}_{F}
corresponds to the element
\alpha_{F}\in\varprojlim_{K/F\text{ fin. Galois}}H^{2}(\Gamma_{K/F},K^{\times})
defined by the limit of canonical classes
\alpha_{K/F}
.

Applying Hilbert’s Theorem 90, we see that inflation
H^{2}(\Gamma_{K/F},K^{\times})\to H^{2}(F_{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}},\mathbb{G}_{m})
is injective, this way
\alpha_{F}
defines a unique class in
\varprojlim H^{2}(F,\mathbb{G}_{m})
, where the transition maps on cohomology are induced by the maps
p_{L/K}
. Finally, applying Hilbert’s Theorem 90 we obtain that
\alpha_{F}
can be considered as an element of
H^{2}(F_{\operatorname{fpqc}},\widetilde{\mathbb{G}_{m}})
, where
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
is the pro-torus over
F
whose module of characters is given by
\mathbb{Q}
.

Consequently, the construction of Kottwitz defines an equivalence class of gerbes over
F_{\operatorname{fpqc}}
banded by
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
.

Remark 3
.

Note that
H^{1}(F_{\operatorname{fpqc}},\widetilde{\mathbb{G}_{m}})\neq 0
, despite the fact that
H^{1}(F_{\operatorname{fpqc}},\mathbb{G}_{m})=0
, namely this cohomology group is given by
\varprojlim^{1}F^{\times}
with the transition maps given by raising to
n
-th powers for any natural
n
. In general, such transition maps are not surjective and the corresponding inverse system fails Mittag-Leffler condition.

4.2.
Global fields

Let
F
be a global field and let
K/F
be a finite Galois extension. Here we define the global Kottwitz classes and explain that the construction of Kottwitz gives us a well-defined class in
H^{2}(F_{\operatorname{fpqc}},\mathbb{D}_{F})
, that is an equivalence class of gerbes banded by a pro-torus
\mathbb{D}_{F}
introduced below (see Section

). Although the situation is quite similar to the local case, there are several differences, for instance, one is forced to prove several vanishing theorems for the first cohomology of various (pro-)tori, that are in general no longer split over

.

Let us first recall Tate’s construction from
[
46
]
. Let
K/F
be a finite Galois extension. Let

be a finite subset of the set of places of

, we define

as the set of places in
K
lying over
S
,
and

denotes a set of lifts for the places in

(that is, over each

satisfies the following list of conditions

(T1)

S
contains all archimedean places and all places that ramify in

(T2)

Every ideal class of

(K1)

For every

such that

(K2)

For every

\dot{v}\in\dot{S}_{K}
such that

The conditions (T1) and (T2) were introduced by Tate in
[

and are sufficient for the construction of Tate-Nakayama classes, the conditions (K1) and (K2) appeared in a paper by Kaletha (see Conditions 3.3.1 in
[
23

) and are sufficient for proving that

and an injectivity statement, consequences of which we will recall in Lemma

Here we compare the Kottwitz set

Recall that every morphism of gerbes is banded by a unique morphism of bands, and that every morphism of bands is

Let

Firstly, we describe

9.

In this section we show that
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
have fibre functors over any extension of

of cohomological dimension
\leq 1
(see
[
42

More precisely, an extension

is of cohomological dimension
\leq 1

\operatorname{Br}(L)=0
for every algebraic extension

of

. It is, in particular, equivalent to the assertion that, for every finite algebraic Galois extension

, the
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\widetilde{F})
-module

is cohomologically trivial (for Tate cohomology).

Recall that If

is a function field of a curve over

, then by Tsen’s theorem

is of dimension
\leq 1

If

, then any totally imaginary field such that for every ultrametric place the corresponding local degree is
\infty
(as a supernatural number) is of dimension
\leq 1
(see Proposition 9 and Corollary, Chap. II, §3 in
[
42
]
). In particular,
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cycl}}
satisfies this condition.
The main result of this section is stated as follows.

Theorem 9.1
.

•

\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})
has a fiber functor in finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cycl}}
, where
\mathbb{Q}^{\operatorname{cycl}}
is the extension of
\mathbb{Q}
obtained by adjoining all roots of unity.

•

If
F
is the function field of an algebraic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{F})
has a fiber functor in finite-dimensional vector spaces over
\breve{F}=F\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

9.1.
Rephrasing the existence of a fiber functor in cohomological terms

Gerbes over
F_{\operatorname{fpqc}}
banded by
B
(which we assume to be an abelian affine group scheme) are classified by
H^{2}(F_{\operatorname{fpqc}},B)
. Thus the existence of a fiber functor of
\operatorname{Rep}(\mathcal{G})
, where
\mathcal{G}
is such a gerbe, over
\widetilde{F}\supseteq F
is equivalent to the vanishing of the class of
\mathcal{G}
in
H^{2}(\widetilde{F}_{\operatorname{fpqc}},B\times\operatorname{Spec}(\widetilde{F}))
. In particular, the vanishing of the latter cohomology group implies the existence of a fiber functor.

The problem we face working with the Kottwitz gerbes for global fields is that even the bands of
\operatorname{Kt}_{K/F}
are pro-algebraic groups. Consequently, we must be careful with the cohomology groups mentioned above, since projective limits do not commute with
\operatorname{fpqc}
cohomology.

In the following sections, we establish exact sequences that relate
\operatorname{fpqc}
cohomology groups of a projective limit with the projective limit of
\operatorname{fpqc}
-cohomology groups of its algebraic quotients; we also compare cohomology of
\mathbb{D}_{F}
with that of a simpler pro-torus
\mathbb{T}_{F}
, and deduce the vanishing of
H^{2}(\widetilde{F}_{\operatorname{fpqc}},\mathbb{D}_{F}\times\operatorname{Spec}{\widetilde{F}})
from these two results.

9.2.
Cohomological generalities

In Section 3 of
[

]
Bhatt and Scholze studied replete topoi, and established several properties of limits in such topoi. We only need some of those results in the special case of the topos of

In what follows we will apply the results of this section in the case

, where

In this section
F

11.1.
Drinfeld’s classification of
\varphi
-spaces

Let
F
be a function field with the field of constants
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We are going to introduce the category of
\varphi
-spaces (or isoshtukas) defined by Drinfeld in
[
15
]
.

Definition 6
.

Let
B
be an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A
\varphi
-space over
B
is a finite dimensional vector space
V
over
F\otimes B
with an
\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{Frob}_{B}
-linear map
\varphi:V\xrightarrow{{\smash{\sim}}}V
. A morphism between two
\varphi
-spaces
(V_{1},\varphi_{1})
and
(V_{2},\varphi)
is an
F\otimes B
-linear map
\alpha:V_{1}\to V_{2}
such that
\alpha\circ\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}\circ\alpha
.

The
\varphi
-spaces over
B
form an
F
-linear Tannakian
\otimes
-category
\varphi\text{-Vect}(B)
. In order to prove that this category is semi-simple, when
B=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}
, and to describe the set of simple objects, Drinfeld introduces the notion of a
\varphi
-pair.

Definition 7
.

A
\varphi
-pair is a pair
(L,a)
, where
L
is a commutative finite-dimensional
F
-algebra, and where
a\in L^{\times}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies the following property: for any proper
F
-subalgebra
L^{\prime}
of
L
,
a\notin(L^{\prime})^{\times}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Notice that the latter condition in the definition of a
\varphi
-pair ensures that the group of automorphisms of a given pair is trivial, it also implies that
L
is separable, and the set of isomorphisms of
\varphi
-pairs is identified with the set of orbits of
(F^{\text{sep}})^{\times}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
under the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\text{sep}}/F)
. On the other hand, it follows from global class field theory that

(11.1)

(F^{\text{sep}})^{\times}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\varinjlim_{E/F}\operatorname{Div}^{0}(E)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}.

To every
\varphi
-space
(V,\varphi)
, Drinfeld associates a
\varphi
-pair
(L_{(V,\varphi)},a_{(V,\varphi)})
, and notices that if
(V,\varphi)
is a simple object in the category of
\varphi
-spaces, then

\mu_{w}^{\prime}:X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{K,S})\xrightarrow{b^{\prime}}X^{\ast}(\mathbb{T}_{K,S})\xrightarrow{\mu_{w}}{\mathbb{Z}}=X^{\ast}(\mathbb{G}_{m}),

where the first map is an inclusion and the second is given by

, where

is a chosen place in

lying above

Recall that

. Consequently, the canonical class

corresponds to the homomorphism

Thus the homomorphism

is given by the composition

. If

then

(12.20)

\alpha(K_{w}/F_{u})\circ\mu_{w}^{\prime}(a)=\frac{n_{w}}{[K_{w}:F_{u}]}\pmod{{\mathbb{Z}}}.

13.
More on fiber functors

In this section, we prove the following result.

Theorem 13.1
.

For any finite Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
and any finite set of places of
\mathbb{Q}
satisfying the conditions of Section
4.2
, the class
\alpha_{K,S}
of the Kottwitz gerbe is split by a cyclotomic extension of the form
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
, where
q
is a prime number.

This is done in two steps: in Section

, we reduce the question about the vanishing of restrictions of the class

to a question about the vanishing of the corresponding class in adelic cohomology, which in its turn provides us with a criterion of splitting formulated purely in terms of the local behaviour of the Galois extension to which

has the desired local behaviour.

13.1.
Reduction to an existence theorem

Let
K/F
be a finite Galois extension of number fields and let

be a finite set of places in

For any finite Galois extension

H^{2}(F,\mathbb{D}_{K,S})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}_{L}}H^{2}(L,\mathbb{D}_{K,S}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(F)}\operatorname{Spec}(L))

\operatorname{res}_{L}(\alpha_{K,S})=0

, but know the images of this class under localization maps, we may consider the following commutative diagram

(13.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(F,\mathbb{D}_{K,S})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{2}(L,\mathbb{D}_{K,S}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(F)}\operatorname{Spec}(L))}

Consequently, after replacing

By construction of

(2)

13.3.
Existence theorem

The proof of the following theorem will occupy the rest of this section.

Theorem 13.3
.

Let
S=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\}
be a fixed finite set of primes in
\mathbb{Q}
. Then there exists a constant
c\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(that depends only on
S
) such that for any integer
r
satisfying
c\mid r
, there exists infinitely many prime numbers
q\notin S
with the following properties:

(
\star
)

q\equiv 1\pmod{r}
,
\gcd(\frac{q-1}{r},r)=c
.

(
\star\star
)

let
L
denote
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})
, then for every
p_{i}\in S
, and for any prime ideal
P_{i}
in
\mathcal{O}_{L}
lying above
p_{i}
the degree of the local extension
L_{P_{i}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p_{i}}
is divisible by
\frac{r}{c}
.

To simplify notation, we denote
r_{\ast}=\frac{r}{c}
, where
c
is an integral divisor of
r
. We will specify this constant below.

We rephrase the condition (
\star\star
) as follows.
Note that since
q\notin S
, every extension
L_{P_{i}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p_{i}}
as above is unramified of degree
f_{i}
, which is computed as the order of
p_{i}
in
(\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{q\mathbb{Z}}})^{\times}
, more concretely,
f_{i}
is the smallest natural number such that
p_{i}^{f_{i}}\equiv 1\pmod{q}
(see Theorem 2.13 in
[
49
]
). Thus, the degree of
L_{P_{i}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p_{i}}
is divisible by
r_{\ast}
if and only if
f_{i}
is divisible by
r_{\ast}
.

Focusing on the prime
p=p_{i}
, we see that its order in
(\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{q\mathbb{Z}}})^{\times}
can be computed by analysing the extension
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r},p^{1/r})/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
, in which
q
is unramified. Since
r\mid q-1
, the degree of the local extension over
q
is equal to the degree of the extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(p^{1/r})/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Using Kummer theory (see Chapter IV, Theorem 3.3 in
[
37
]
), we obtain that
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q}(p^{1/r})/\mathbb{F}_{q})
has order equal to the order of
p^{\frac{(q-1)}{r}}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. More precisely, in this case
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{{r}}\simeq\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{(r)}}
is isomorphic to the group of characters
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q}),\mu_{r})
via the map

p\mapsto\chi_{p}(\operatorname{Frob}_{q})=\frac{\operatorname{Frob}_{q}(p^{1/r})}{p^{1/r}}=p^{\frac{q-1}{r}}\in\mu_{r}.

Thus, the order of
p
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{r}
is equal to the order of
\chi_{p}(\operatorname{Frob}_{q})
, and the latter is equal to the order of
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q}(p^{1/r})/\mathbb{F}_{q})
. In particular, the order of
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q}(p^{1/r})/\mathbb{F}_{q})
divides the order of
p
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

In order to combine the local conditions for different
p_{i}
’s, we consider the following tower of extensions

\mathbb{Q}
\mathbb{Q}_{r}:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r})
E
\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2}}:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r^{2}})
\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r}:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r^{2}},\sqrt[r]{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[r]{p_{s}})
\mathbb{Q}_{r,r}:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{r},\sqrt[r]{p_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[r]{p_{s}})

where
E
denotes the intersection of
\mathbb{Q}_{r,r}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2}}
.

We claim that there are infinitely many rational primes
q
, such that
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r}/\mathbb{Q})
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\operatorname{Frob}_{q}=1\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{r}/\mathbb{Q})
.

(2)

\operatorname{Frob}_{q}
generates the subgroup
\nicefrac{{c\mathbb{Z}}}{{r\mathbb{Z}}}\subseteq\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2}}/\mathbb{Q}_{r})
.

(3)

for any
1\leq i\leq s
and for every projection
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{r,r}/\mathbb{Q}_{r})\to\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{r}(\sqrt[r]{p_{i}})/\mathbb{Q}_{r})
the image of
\operatorname{Frob}_{q}
in the latter Galois group is at least of order
\frac{r}{c}
.

Note that it follows from the discussion above that the third condition implies that the orders of
p_{i}
’s in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
are divisible by
\frac{r}{c}
.

First, we show that
H=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{r,r}/E)
contains a subgroup
(\nicefrac{{c\mathbb{Z}}}{{r\mathbb{Z}}})^{s}
. Notice that by definition of
E
, the group
H
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r}/\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2}})
. Letting

in Theorem
13.2
, we obtain

(13.6)

\frac{r^{2s}}{\deg(\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r^{2}}/\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2}})}=\frac{r^{s}}{\deg(\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r}/\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2}})}\cdot\frac{r^{s}}{\deg(\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r^{2}}/\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r})}\mid c_{S}

\frac{r^{s}}{\deg(\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r}/\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2}})}
is an integer dividing

. On the other hand,

is a subgroup of
(\nicefrac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{(r)}})^{s}
, thus any generator of the preimage of any factor

is at least of order

(\nicefrac{{c_{S}\mathbb{Z}}}{{r\mathbb{Z}}})^{s}\subseteq H

Note that since
\mathbb{Q}_{r^{2},r}

\mathbb{Q}_{r,r}

and of

. Next, we consider the diagram

14.1.

Let
q=p^{n}
. Recall that an algebraic number

-number of weight

•

for every embedding

•

for some

The set of all Weil

is an abelian group denoted by

. If

and

, we have a group homomorphism
W(q)\to W(q^{\prime})
:
\pi\mapsto\pi^{n^{\prime}/n}
. Define the group of germs of Weil numbers

(14.1)

W=\varinjlim W(q).

There is an action of the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}
on
W
, and
P
is defined as a protorus over
\mathbb{Q}
such that

(14.2)

X^{\ast}(P)=W.

In order to represent
P
as a projective limit of algebraic tori, we rewrite
W
as a colimit of finitely generated Galois modules. Namely, for any CM-field
K/\mathbb{Q}
, we define
W^{K}
as a subset of
\pi\in W
having a representative in
K
and such that for any place
v\mid p
in
K
:

(14.3)

\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(\pi)}{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(p^{n})}\cdot[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\in\mathbb{Z}.

Define
P^{K}
as a group of multiplicative type defined by
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\text{al}}/\mathbb{Q})
-module
W^{K}
.
Then
W=\varinjlim_{K}W^{K}
and
P=\varprojlim_{K}P^{K}
, where the limit is being taken over the directed set of finite CM-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
K^{+}
denote the maximal totally real subfield of
K
, and let
V_{p,K}
and
V_{p,K^{+}}
denote the set of
p
-adic primes in
K
and
K^{+}
respectively. Then there is an exact sequence of Galois modules

(14.4)

0\to W^{K}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\bigoplus_{v\in V_{p,K}}\mathbb{Z}\cdot v\bigoplus\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\beta}\bigoplus_{w\in V_{p,K^{+}}}\mathbb{Z}\cdot w\to 0,

where
\alpha
is the map

(14.5)

\pi\mapsto(\sum_{v\in V_{p,K}}\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(\pi)}{\operatorname{ord}_{v}{p^{n}}}\cdot[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}],wt(\pi)),

and
\beta
is given by the formula

(14.6)

(\sum_{v\in V_{p,K}}n_{v}v,m)\mapsto\sum_{v\in V_{p,K}}n_{v}\mid_{V_{p,K^{+}}}-[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]m\sum{w\in V_{p,K^{+}}}

Let
K^{\prime}
be a CM-field containing
K
. Then
W^{K}\subset W^{K^{\prime}}
, and the transition map can be described naturally using the exact sequence (
14.4
). Namely, the transition map is induced by the map

(14.7)

v\mapsto\sum_{v^{\prime}\mid v}[K^{\prime}_{v^{\prime}}:K_{v}]\cdot v^{\prime},

when
v\mid p
, and the identity map on
\mathbb{Z}
.

The short exact sequence of Galois modules (
14.4
) induces the short exact sequence of the corresponding tori and provides a very useful tool for analysis of the cohomology of
P^{K}
. The following results are due to Milne (see Section 3 in
[
34
]
).

Proposition 14.1
.

Let
K
be a CM-field, and let
P^{K}
be a torus attached to the Galois module
W^{K}
, then

•

\varprojlim^{i}_{K}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},P^{K})=0
, for
i=0,1
.

•

H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P)=\varprojlim_{K}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P^{K})
.

•

H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P^{K})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{A},P^{K})
is injective.

•

H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P)\to H^{2}(\mathbb{A},P)
is injective.

14.2.
W^{K}
and the characters of the Kottwitz protorus

One can also realize
W^{K}
as a submodule of the character module of the Kottwitz pro-torus
\mathbb{D}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proposition 14.2
.

Let
K
be a finite CM-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then there is an injective homomorphism of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
modules

(14.8)

\omega_{K}:W^{K}\hookrightarrow X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{K/F}).

Proof.

Let
\pi\in W^{K}
be a representative of a Weil
p^{n}
-Number of weight
m
. We may apply the product formula to
\pi
, namely

(14.9)

\prod_{v\in V_{K}}\left\lVert\pi\right\rVert_{v}=1.

Moreover, for any non-archimedean
v\notin V_{p,K}
, we have
\left\lVert\pi\right\rVert_{v}=1
. Notice that for any
v\mid p
, we have
\left\lVert\pi\right\rVert_{v}=p^{-f_{v}\operatorname{ord}_{v}(\pi)}
, where
f_{v}
is the degree of the extension of the residue field of
K_{v}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. The latter can be expressed as
[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]/e_{v}
, where
e_{v}
is the ramification index of
v
in
K
that can be computed as
\operatorname{ord}_{v}(p)
. Since
K
is a CM-field, every archimedean place
\infty^{\prime}\mid\infty
is in fact complex, and by definition of Weil
p^{n}
-number of weight
m
, we have
\left\lVert\pi\right\rVert_{\infty^{\prime}}=(p^{n})^{m}
. Combining these observations, we deduce

(14.10)

\sum_{v\in V_{p,K}}\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{v}{\pi}}{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(p)}\cdot[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]-\sum_{\infty^{\prime}\in V_{\infty,K}}nm=0.

Notice that by definition of
W^{K}
and from the fact that
\pi\in W^{K}
, the equality (
14.10
) is integrally divisible by
n
(see (
14.3
)). Define

(14.11)

i_{K}:W^{K}\to\mathbb{Z}[V_{K}],\quad\pi\mapsto\sum_{v\in V_{p,K}}\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{v}{\pi}}{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(p^{n})}\cdot[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\cdot v-\sum_{\infty^{\prime}\in V_{\infty,K}}m\cdot\infty^{\prime}=0.

Since
W^{K}
is torsion-free, this map is injective. Moreover, it follows from (
14.10
) that
i_{K}
induces an injective map
W^{K}\to X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{K/\mathbb{Q}})
.
∎

In order to construct the morphism
\varinjlim_{K}W^{K}\to X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we need to check that the morphisms
\omega_{K}
are compatible with the transition maps
W^{K}\to W^{K^{\prime}}
and
X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{K/\mathbb{Q}})\to X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}})
, where
K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
is a CM-extension containing
K
. Recall, that he transition maps for the character modules of Kottwitz protori are induced by the maps (
9.11
), that is by the same formulae as (
14.7
). We denote by
\omega
the injective homomorphism of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\text{al}}/\mathbb{Q})
-modules
W\to X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})
induced by
\omega_{K}
for varying CM-fields
K
.

14.3.
Image of the Kottwitz class under
\omega

Let
\omega:W\hookrightarrow X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})
be the homomorphism defined above. By abuse of notation, we also denote by
\omega
the epimorphism of protori
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}\to P
. Because
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\varprojlim H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{D}_{K,S})
and
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P)=\varprojlim H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P^{K})
, there is a well-defined morphism

(14.12)

\omega^{\ast}:H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P).

We are interested in computing the class
\omega^{\ast}(\alpha_{\mathbb{Q}})
, where
\alpha_{\mathbb{Q}}
denotes the class of the Kottwitz gerbe in
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. By Proposition
14.1
,
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P)\hookrightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{A},\ P)
, therefore it suffices to describe the image of the given class locally.

Proposition 14.3
.

The image of
\omega^{\ast}(\alpha_{\mathbb{Q}})
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{A},P)
is given by the class
(0_{\mathbb{A}^{\{p,\infty\}}},\alpha_{p},\alpha_{\mathbb{R}})
. Where
\alpha_{p}
and
\alpha_{\mathbb{R}}
are the canonical local classes.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{l}
denote the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
. Recall that since
\lim^{1}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{l},P)=0
, we may apply the arguments of Section
12.5
, to describe local cohomology groups of the Weil protorus, namely

(14.13)

H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{l},P)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(W^{\Gamma_{l}},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),

where
l
any non-archimedean prime.

Recall also that the map
\omega:W\hookrightarrow X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})
induces the map of
\Gamma_{l}
-modules

(14.14)

\operatorname{loc}_{l}:W\xrightarrow{\omega}X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})\hookrightarrow X^{\ast}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\mu_{l}}\mathbb{Q},

where
\mu_{l}
is defined in Section
12.6
. Namely,
\mu_{l}
sends a sum of valuations with rational coefficients to the coefficient corresponding to the fixed
l
-adic prime of
\mathbb{Q}^{\text{al}}
.

Therefore, for any
l\neq p
, we have that the image of
W
in
\mathbb{Q}
of the composition
\operatorname{loc}_{l}
in (
14.14
) is
0
. Hence, the corresponding class in
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{l},P)
is trivial.

If
l=p
, we see that the composition
W^{\Gamma_{p}}\to W\xrightarrow{\operatorname{loc}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]^{-1}}\mathbb{Q}
is surjective. It follows from the fact that for any CM-field
K
and
\pi\in W^{K}
we have
\operatorname{loc}_{v}(\pi)=\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(\pi)}{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(p^{n})}\cdot[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
v
is the
p
-adic place of
K
induced by the fixed
p
-adic place of
\mathbb{Q}^{\text{al}}
. If
\pi
is moreover
\Gamma_{v}
-invariant, where
\Gamma_{v}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is the decomposition group of
v
, then we deduce from the product formula and equality (
14.10
) that
\operatorname{ord}_{v}(\pi)/\operatorname{ord}_{v}(p^{n})
takes value
\frac{m}{2}
, where
m
is the weight of
\pi
. We obtain that the colimit of the cokernels of the maps

(14.15)

(W^{K})^{\Gamma_{v}}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{loc}_{v}}\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]^{-1}}\frac{1}{2\cdot[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\mathbb{Z},\quad\pi\mapsto\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(\pi)}{\operatorname{ord}_{v}(p^{n})}

vanishes, since their orders are bounded by
[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
, and the transition maps for
K\subset K^{\prime}
, where
K^{\prime}
is a finite CM-field over
\mathbb{Q}
, are given by multiplication by the local degrees
[K^{\prime}_{v^{\prime}}:K_{v}]
, hence kill the cokernels eventually.

Consequently, the map
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{Hom}(W^{\Gamma_{p}},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is injective and maps the class of the natural projection
\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, that is the class
\alpha_{p}
, to the image of
\omega^{\ast}(\alpha_{\mathbb{Q}})
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},P)
.

To complete the proof, we notice that when
l=\infty
is the arhimedean place, then
\operatorname{loc}_{\infty}(\pi)=-m
. Hence the image of
\omega^{\ast}(\alpha_{\mathbb{Q}})
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R},P)
coincides with the image of the projection
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
under the map
\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb{R}))\to\operatorname{Hom}(W^{\Gamma_{\infty}},\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb{R}))
, where
\Gamma_{\infty}\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})
.
∎

14.4.
Conjectural relation to
\operatorname{Mot}(\mathbb{F})

Let
\zeta(V,s)
denote the zeta function of a variety
V
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
A^{r}(V)
denote the quotient of the space of algebraic cycles of codimension
r
on
V
by the subspace of cycles numerically equivalent to
0
. In this section, we assume the full Tate conjecture.

Conjecture 2

(Tate conjecture)
.

For all smooth projective varieties over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
r\geq 0
, the dimension of
A^{r}(V)
is equal to the order of the pole of
\zeta(V,s)
at
s=r
.

Under this assumption the category of motives over
\mathbb{F}
can be described fairly explicitly. Namely, it follows from theorem of Jannsen
[
22
]
, that
\operatorname{Mot}(\mathbb{F})
is a semi-simple Tannakian category over
\mathbb{Q}
. Moreover, the classes of simple objects correspond to the orbits of the action
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\text{al}}/\mathbb{Q})
on
W
, the abelian group of germs of Weil numbers defined in Section
14.1
. Applying Tannakian duality, we see that
\operatorname{Mot}(\mathbb{F})
can be recovered up to equivalence from the class it defines in
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P)
(
\operatorname{fpqc}
-cohomology). Using the fact that
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P)\hookrightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{A},P)
, we deduce that it suffices to describe the images of the class of
\operatorname{Mot}(\mathbb{F})
in cohomology groups of completions of
\mathbb{Q}
at all places. One can apply Proposition
12.1
to compute the local classes via isocrystals. Namely, it turns out that the localization functor (see Theorem 1.18 in
[
33
]
for the definition)

\operatorname{Mot}(\mathbb{F})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\operatorname{Isoc}(\mathbb{F})

corresponds to the morphism of gerbes induced by the map of characters
W\to\mathbb{Q}
given by the formula

(14.16)

\pi\mapsto\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\pi)}{n}

where
\operatorname{ord}_{p}
is the extension of the
p
-adic valuation on
\mathbb{Q}
corresponding to a chosen embedding
\mathbb{Q}^{\text{al}}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\text{al}}
. The latter can be deduced from the section “The isocrystal of a motive” in
[
33
]
. Finally, one obtains the following description of
\operatorname{Mot}(\mathbb{F})
.

Theorem 14.1

(Langlands-Rapoport, Milne (Th.3.29 in
[
33
]
))
.

Assume the Tate conjecture. Then the category of motives over
\mathbb{F}
is a semi-simple Tannakian category over
\mathbb{Q}
. Its band is given by the protorus
P
corresponding to the
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{\text{al}}/\mathbb{Q})
-module
W
. The equivalence class of
\operatorname{Mot}(\mathbb{F})
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P)
is identified with the class
(0_{\mathbb{A}^{\{p,\infty\}}},\alpha_{p},\alpha_{\mathbb{R}})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{A},P)
via the inclusion
H^{2}(\mathbb{Q},P)\hookrightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{A},P)
.

The naturality of such a description is treated in Theorem 6.3 in
[
34
]
, which combined with Proposition
14.3
leads us to the following statement.

Corollary 14.1
.

Let
\omega^{\ast}(\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}))
be the subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, defined by the image of the global Kottwitz class
\alpha_{\mathbb{Q}}
along the map
\omega:W\hookrightarrow X^{\ast}(\mathbb{D}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. There exists an equivalence
\omega^{\ast}(\operatorname{Rep}(\operatorname{Kt}_{\mathbb{Q}}))\to\operatorname{Mot}(\mathbb{F})
compatible with the natural realization functors.

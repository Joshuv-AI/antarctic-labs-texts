A systematic approach to general higher-order majorization-minimization algorithms for (non)convex optimization
Ion Necoara, Daniela Lupu

Abstract
Majorization-minimization algorithms consist of successively minimizing a sequence of upper bounds of the objective function so that along the iterations the objective decreases. Such a simple principle allows to solve a large class of optimization problems, even nonconvex and nonsmooth. We propose a general higher-order majorization-minimization algorithmic framework for minimizing an objective function that admits an approximation (also called surrogate) such that the corresponding error function has a higher-order Lipschitz continuous derivative. We present convergence guarantees for our new method for general optimization problems with (non)convex and/or (non)smooth objective function. For convex (possibly nonsmooth) problems we provide global sublinear convergence rates, while for problems with uniformly convex objective function we obtain faster local superlinear convergence rates. We also prove global first-order optimality conditions guarantees and sublinear convergence rates for general nonconvex (possibly nonsmooth) problems and under Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz property of the objective we derive local convergence rates ranging from sublinear to superlinear for our majorization-minimization algorithm. Finally, for unconstrained nonconvex problems we derive convergence rates in terms of first- and second-order optimality conditions.



keywords

(Non)convex optimization, majorization-minimization, higher-order methods, convergence rates.

Introduction

The principle of successively minimizing upper bounds of the objective function is often called
majorization-minimization

[
15
,
16
,
28
]
. Most techniques, e.g., gradient descent, utilize convex quadratic majorizers based on first-order oracles in order to guarantee that the majorizer is easy to minimize. Despite the empirical success of first-order majorization-minimization algorithms to solve difficult optimization problems, the convergence speed of such methods is known to slow down close to saddle points or in ill-conditioned landscapes
[
18
,
27
]
. Higher-order methods are known to be less affected by these problems
[
4
,
20
,
7
,
8
]
.
In this work, we focus our attention on higher-order majorization-minimization methods to find (local) minima of (potentially nonsmooth and nonconvex) objective functions. At each iteration, these algorithms construct and optimize a (usually Taylor-based) local model of the objective using higher-order derivatives with an additional penalty term that enforces the model to approximate well the real objective locally.

Contributions
. This paper provides a unified algorithmic framework based on the notion of higher-order upper bound approximations of the (non)convex and/or (non)smooth objective function, leading to a
general higher-order majorization - minimization
algorithm, which we call GHOM. Then, we present convergence guarantees for the GHOM algorithm for general optimization problems when the upper bounds approximate the objective function up to an error that is
p\geq 1
times differentiable and has a Lipschitz continuous
p
derivative; we call such upper bounds
higher-order surrogate
functions. More precisely, on general convex (possibly nonsmooth) problems GHOM algorithm achieves global sublinear convergence rate for the function values. When we apply our method to optimization problems with uniformly convex objective function, we obtain faster local superlinear convergence rates in multiple criteria: function values, distance of the iterates to the optimal point and norm of subgradients. The last criterion also implies convergence rates for the residual of the first-order optimality conditions. Then, for general nonconvex (possibly nonsmooth) problems we prove for GHOM global asymptotic convergence to a point from which there is no descent direction as well as convergence rates for the residual of the first-order optimality conditions. We also characterize the convergence rates of GHOM algorithm locally in terms of function values under the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz (KL) property. Our results show that the convergence behavior of GHOM ranges from sublinear to superlinear depending on the parameter of the underlying KL geometry. Finally, on smooth unconstrained nonconvex problems we derive convergence rates in terms of first- and second-order optimality conditions.

The folllowing remarks clarify better our contributions. First, it is known that for general nonsmooth, nonconvex problems, answering to the question of whether a descent direction exists from a point is NP-hard

Majorization-minimization algorithms
approximate at each iteration the objective function by a majorizing function that is easy to minimize
[
3
,
15
]
. Most techniques, e.g., gradient descent, utilize convex quadratic majorizers in order to guarantee that the model at each iteration is easy to minimize. The framework of first-order majorization-minimization methods, i.e. methods that are using only gradient information to build the upper model, has been analyzed widely in the literature of the recent two decades, see e.g.,
[
3
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
28
]
.
However, to the best of our knowledge there are no results on the convergence behavior of general higher-order majorization-minimization algorithms, i.e. methods that are using higher-order derivatives to build the upper model.
In this paper we provide a general framework for the design of higher-order majorization-minimization algorithms and derive a complete convergence analysis for them (global and local convergence rates) covering a large class of optimization problems, that is convex or nonconvex, smooth or nonsmooth.

1.2
Notations and preliminaries

We denote a finite-dimensional real vector space with
\mathbb{E}
and by
\mathbb{E}^{*}
its dual space composed by linear functions on
\mathbb{E}
. For such a function
s\in\mathbb{E}^{*}
, we denoted by
\langle s,x\rangle

x\in\mathbb{E}.
Utilizing a self-adjoint positive-definite operator
B:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{*}
(notation
B=B^{*}\succ 0
), we can endow these spaces with
conjugate Euclidean norms

\|x\|=\langle Bx,x\rangle^{1/2}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{E},\quad\|y\|_{*}=\left\langle y,B^{-1}y\right\rangle^{1/2}\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{E}^{*}.

For a convex set
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{E}
, the normal cone of
\mathcal{X}
at
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{X}
is defined as
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(\bar{x})=\{g:\langle g,\bar{x}-x\rangle\geq 0\;\;\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\}
. For an extended real-valued function
\psi:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}\,(:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
), we always assume that it is proper (i.e.,
\psi\not=\infty
), lower semicontinuous (lsc) and its domain is
\text{dom}\psi=\{x\in\mathbb{E}:\;\psi(x)<\infty\}
. The regular subdifferential of
\psi
at
\bar{x}\in\text{dom}\psi
is defined as
[
29
]
:

\hat{\partial}\psi(\bar{x})=\{\psi^{\bar{x}}:\liminf\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\bar{x},x\neq\bar{x}\end{subarray}}\frac{\psi(x)-\psi(\bar{x})-\langle\psi^{\bar{x}},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq 0.

Further, the limiting subdifferential of
\psi
at
\bar{x}
is defined as the sequential outer limit

\partial\psi(\bar{x})=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\xrightarrow{\psi}\bar{x}\end{subarray}}\hat{\partial}\psi(x),

where the notation
\begin{subarray}{c}x\xrightarrow{\psi}\bar{x}\end{subarray}
signifies that
x\to\bar{x}
with
\psi(x)\to\psi(\bar{x})
. Note that
\partial\psi(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
if
\psi
is locally Lipschitz around
\bar{x}
. Moreover, for
\psi
convex function,
\partial\psi(x)
denotes its subdifferential at
x
, which is a closed set. Let us denote:

S(x)=\text{dist}(0,\partial\psi(x))\;\left(:=\inf_{\psi^{x}\in\partial\psi(x)}\|\psi^{x}\|_{*}\right)\quad\forall x\in\text{dom}\,\psi.

If
\partial\psi(x)=\emptyset
, we set
S(x)=\infty
. For a smooth function
\psi:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
, which is
p\geq 1
times continuously differentiable on the convex and open domain
\text{dom}\psi=\{x\in\mathbb{E}:\psi(x)<\infty\}

\nabla\psi(x)
its gradient and
\nabla^{2}\psi(x)
its Hessian at the point
x\in\text{dom}\psi
. Note that
\nabla\psi(x)\in\mathbb{E}^{*}
and
\nabla^{2}\psi(x)d\in\mathbb{E}^{*}

D^{p}\psi(x)\left[d_{1},\ldots,d_{p}\right]

D\psi(x)[d]=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\left(\psi(x+td)-\psi(x)\right)/t

Note that

\|D^{p}\psi(x)\|:=\max_{\left\|d_{1}\right\|,\cdots,\left\|d_{p}\right\|\leq 1}\left|D^{p}\psi(x)\left[d_{1},\ldots,d_{p}\right]\right|=\max_{\left\|d\right\|\leq 1}\left|D^{p}\psi(x)\left[d\right]^{p}\right|\text{.}

Since for any fixed

Definition 1

Let

\|D^{p}\psi(x)-D^{p}\psi(y)\|\leq L_{p}^{\psi}\|x-y\|\quad\forall x,y\in\text{dom}\psi.

(3)

We denote the Taylor approximation of

|\psi(y)-T_{p}^{\psi}(y;x)|\leq\frac{L_{p}^{\psi}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\quad\forall x,y\in\text{dom}\psi.

Applying the same reasoning for the functions

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi(y)-\nabla T_{p}^{\psi}(y;x)\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{\psi}}{p!}\|y-x\|^{p},

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\psi(y)-\nabla^{2}T_{p}^{\psi}(y;x)\|\leq\frac{L_{p}^{\psi}}{(p-1)!}\|y-x\|^{p-1}.

For the Hessian we consider the spectral norm of self-adjoint linear operators (maximal module of all eigenvalues computed with respect to operator

Therefore, when minimizing convex functions with higher-order algorithms one can use a large number of powerful methods from convex optimization for solving the subproblem

Definition 6
.

A function
\psi:\mathbb{E}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is uniformly convex of degree
q\geq 2

\text{dom}\;\psi
if there exists a positive constant
\sigma_{q}>0
such that:

\psi(y)\geq\psi(x)+\left\langle\psi^{x},y-x\right\rangle+\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x-y\|^{q}\quad\forall x,y\in\text{dom}\,\psi,

where
\psi^{x}
is an arbitrary vector from the subdifferential

at

Next, we establish a relation between uniform convexity of a function and its minimum over a convex set.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{E}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be a uniform convex function satisfying (
7
) and
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}\,\psi
a closed convex set. Then, the function
\psi+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}
is uniform convex on
\mathcal{X}
and the following relation holds:

\displaystyle\min_{y\in\mathcal{X}}\psi(y)\geq\psi(x)-\frac{q-1}{q}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\left\|\psi_{X}^{x}\right\|_{*}^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

(8)

where
\psi_{\mathcal{X}}^{x}
is an arbitrary vector from the subdifferential of the convex function
\psi+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}
at
x
, where
\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}
denotes the indicator function of the set
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Since the function
\psi:\mathbb{E}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is uniform convex on
\text{dom}\,\psi
(i.e., it satisfies (
7
)) and the indicator function
\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}
is convex, adding them and using basic calculus rules for subdifferential of convex functions (i.e.,
\partial\psi+\partial\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq\partial(\psi+\textbf{1}_{X})
), we also get that the sum
\psi+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}
is uniform convex on
\text{dom}(\psi+\textbf{1}_{X})=\mathcal{X}
, i.e.:

(\psi+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(y)\geq(\psi+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x)+\left\langle\psi_{\mathcal{X}}^{x},y-x\right\rangle+\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x-y\|^{q}\quad\forall x,y\in\text{dom}(\psi+\textbf{1}_{X})=\mathcal{X}.\vskip-8.5359pt

Minimizing both sides of previous inequality w.r.t.

, we get our statement.

Note that for
q=2
in (

) we recover the usual definition of a strongly convex function. Moreover, (

Example 8

is of the form
\kappa(t)=\sigma_{r}^{\frac{1}{r}}\frac{r}{r-1}t^{\frac{r-1}{r}}

[
2
]
. In this case the uniformized KL property establishes the following local geometry of the nonconvex function
\psi
around a compact set
\Omega
:

\psi(x)-\psi_{*}\leq\sigma_{r}S(x)^{r}\quad\forall x\!:\;\text{dist}(x,\Omega)\leq\delta,\;\psi_{*}<\psi(x)<\psi_{*}+\epsilon.

(9)

If
\psi
is uniformly convex of degree
q\geq 2
, then from (
8
) we get that
\kappa
in Definition
9
is of the form
\kappa(t)=(q/\sigma_{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}(q-1)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}t^{\frac{1}{q}}
. Note that the relevant aspect of the KL property is when
\Omega
is a subset of
critical points
for
\psi
, i.e.,
\Omega\subseteq\{x:0\in\partial\psi(x)\}
, since it is easy to establish the KL property when
\Omega
is not related to critical points.

Finally, for a function
\psi
, we denote its sublevel set at a given

by:

\mathcal{L}_{\psi}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{E}:\;\psi(x)\leq\psi(x_{0})\}.

2
General higher-order majorization-minimization algorithms

In what follows, we study the following general nonsmooth optimization problem:

\min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}f(x),

(10)

where
f:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
is a proper (possibly nonconvex) lower semicontinuous (lsc) function defined over its domain
\text{dom}f
and
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}f
is a closed convex set having nonempty relative interior (this condition ensures validity of the calculus rules for subdifferentiability of the function
f+\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}
). Since
f
is extended valued, it allows the inclusion of other constraints in addition to
x\in\mathcal{X}
. We assume that a solution
x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
exists for problem (
10
), hence the optimal value is finite and
f
is bounded from below by some
f^{*}>-\infty
. In the convex case we consider
f^{*}=f(x^{*})
(the optimal value). Throughout the paper we assume that
f
admits directional derivatives and the limiting subdifferential is nonempty at any point in
\mathcal{X}
, respectively. Then, at a regular point of (local) minimum
x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
, the first-order necessary optimality conditions for (
10
) can be written as follows
[
29
]
[Theorem 8.15]:

\displaystyle Df(x^{*})[x-x^{*}]\geq 0\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}\quad\iff\quad 0\in\partial f(x^{*})+\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(x^{*}).

(11)

Moreover, when
\text{dom}\,f=\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{E}
these conditions are equivalent to
0\in\partial f(x^{*})
. When
f
is convex function, the conditions (
11
) are also sufficient for
x^{*}
to be a global minimum of function
f
over the convex set
\mathcal{X}
. The first-order optimality conditions (
11
) are convenient for defining an approximate solution to problem (
10
). A point
\hat{x}\in\mathcal{X}
satisfies the first-order optimality conditions of (local) minimum of function
f
over the convex set
\mathcal{X}
with accuracy
\epsilon
if:

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{Df(\hat{x})[x-\hat{x}]}{\|x-\hat{x}\|}\geq-\epsilon.

In our convergence analysis below we assume that the sublevel set
\mathcal{L}_{f}(x_{0})
is bounded. Then, there exists
R>0
such that:

\|x-x^{*}\|\leq R\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{L}_{f}(x_{0}).

These basic assumptions are standard in the literature, see e.g.,
[
6
,
8
,
20
,
4
,
24
]
. The main approach we adopt in solving the optimization problem (
10
) is to use a class of functions that approximates well the objective function
f
but are easier to minimize. We call this class
higher-order surrogate
functions. The main properties of our higher-order surrogate function are described next:

Assumption 11
.

For a given proper lower semicontinuous (lsc) function
f:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
and a closed convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}f
, there exist an integer
p\geq 1
and a proper lsc function
g(\cdot;x):\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
at any
x\in\mathcal{X}
, called
p
higher-order surrogate of
f
at
x
, having
\text{dom}\,g(\cdot;x)=\text{dom}f
and satisfying the following properties:

(i)

the surrogate function is bounded from below by the original function

g(y;x)\geq f(y)\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

there exists an error function
h(\cdot;x):\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
with
\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)
an open set,
p
times differentiable and having the
p
derivative smooth (i.e., Lipschitz continuous) with Lipschitz constant
L_{p}^{h}
on the set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)
, whose restriction to the set
\mathcal{X}
agrees with
g-f
(i.e.,
h(y;x)=g(y;x)-f(y)
for all
y\in\mathcal{X}
).

(iii)

the
i
th directional derivatives of the error function
h
at any
x\in\mathcal{X}
satisfy

D^{i}h(x;x)[\cdot]=0\quad\forall i=0:p,

where
i=0
means that
h(x;x)=0
, or equivalently
g(x;x)=f(x)
.

Note that
[
16
]
provided similar conditions as in Assumption
11
, but only for a first-order surrogate function and used it in the context of stochastic optimization. Next, we give several nontrivial examples of higher-order surrogate functions.

Example 12
.

(proximal functions). For a general (possibly nonsmooth and nonconvex) function
f:\mathbb{E}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
and a closed convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}f
, one can consider for any
M_{p}>0
and integer
p\geq 1
, the following
p
higher-order surrogate function:

g(y;x)=f(y)+\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

This surrogate satisfies all the conditions of Assumption
11
. Indeed,
\text{dom}\,g(\cdot;x)=\text{dom}f
and since
M_{p}\geq 0
, the first property of the surrogate is immediate. Moreover, the error function
h(y;x)=\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}
has
\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)=\mathbb{E}
and satisfies
h=g-f
on any set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}\,f\subseteq\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)
. Next, according to Example
2
, the error function,
h(y;x)=\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}
, has the
p
derivative Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{p}^{h}=M_{p}
. For the last property of a surrogate, we notice that the
i
derivative
\nabla^{i}(\|y-x\|^{p+1})_{|_{\begin{subarray}{c}y=x\end{subarray}}}=0
for all
i=0:p
. Thus,
D^{i}h(x;x)[\cdot]=0\;\;\forall i=0:p
. Note that the function
h
is always convex w.r.t.
y
, while the surrogate function
g
is convex only when the objective function
f
is convex.

Example 13
.

(hessian aware proximal functions).
For a nonconvex function
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
that is twice differentiable and with the second derivative Lipschitz continous with constant
L_{2}^{f}
on the full domain
\mathbb{E}
and a closed convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{E}
, one can consider for any
M_{2}\geq L_{2}^{f}
the following second-order surrogate function:

\displaystyle g(y;x)=f(y)-\frac{1}{2}(y-x)^{T}\nabla^{2}_{-}f(x)(y-x)+\frac{M_{2}}{3}\|y-x\|^{3}\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},

(12)

where
\nabla^{2}_{-}f(x)
denotes the negative part of the Hessian of
f
at
x
1
1

1

For a symmetric matrix
A
, with both positive and negative eigenvalues, the
LDU
factorization leads to
U=L^{T}
and
D
diagonal which can be separated into the positive portion
D_{+}
and the negative portion
D_{-}
(they have all positive or all negative eigenvalues and zeros). Then,
A=L(D_{+}+D_{-})L^{T}
and the negative part is
A_{-}=LD_{-}L^{T}
.
. First, one should notice that this surrogate is always a convex function in
y
, even if
f
non-convex. Indeed, since
f
has the Hessian Lipschitz continuous, from (
6
) we have:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{y}g(y;x)

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}f(y)-\nabla^{2}_{-}f(x)+M_{2}\left(\|y-x\|B+\frac{B(y-x)(y-x)^{T}B}{\|y-x\|}\right)

\displaystyle\succeq\nabla^{2}f(y)-\nabla^{2}f(x)+M_{2}\left(\|y-x\|B+\frac{B(y-x)(y-x)^{T}B}{\|y-x\|}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:TayAppG2}}{\succeq}-L_{2}^{f}\|y-x\|B+M\left(\|y-x\|B+\frac{B(y-x)(y-x)^{T}B}{\|y-x\|}\right)

\displaystyle=(M_{2}-L_{2}^{f})\|y-x\|B+M_{2}\frac{B(y-x)(y-x)^{T}B}{\|y-x\|}\succeq 0\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{E}.

Hence, compared to the surrogate of Example
12
, the second-order surrogate (
12
) is always convex for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{E}
(although
f
is nonconvex). In fact, one can easily notice that this surrogate is uniformly convex (if
M_{2}>L_{2}^{f}
) and has the Hessian Lipschitz continuous. Hence, it can be minimized by a gradient type method that has a linear rate of convergence depending only on absolute constants, see e.g.,
[

,
21

.

Moreover, this surrogate (
12
) satisfies all the conditions of Assumption
11
. Indeed, we have
\text{dom}\,g(\cdot;x)=\text{dom}f=\mathbb{E}
and since for any given
x\in\mathbb{E}
we have
-\nabla^{2}_{-}f(x)\succeq 0
and
M_{2}\geq 0
, it follows that
g(y;x)\geq f(y)
for any
y\in\mathbb{E}
, i.e., the first property of the surrogate holds. Moreover, the error function
h(y;x)=-\frac{1}{2}(y-x)^{T}\nabla^{2}_{-}f(x)(y-x)+\frac{M_{2}}{3}\|y-x\|^{3}
has
\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)=\mathbb{E}
, satisfies
h=g-f
on any set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{E}
(including
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{E}
) and is always convex w.r.t.
y
. Additonally, this surrogate has the gradient Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
L_{1}^{h}=\max_{x,y\in\mathcal{L}_{f}(x_{0})\cap\mathcal{X}}\|-\nabla^{2}_{-}f(x)+M_{2}(\|y-x\|B+B(y-x)(y-x)^{T}B\|y-x\|^{-1})\|
(recall that the level set
\mathcal{L}_{f}(x_{0})
is assumed bounded). Finally, we have
g(x;x)=f(x)
and
\nabla g(x;x)=\nabla f(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{E}
. Hence, according to our GHOM algorithm defined below, this surrogate provides one of the first implementable higher-order proximal point type algorithms for minimizing twice differentiable smooth nonconvex functions that enjoys convergence guarantees.

For a convex function
f
we can change the previous surrogate as follows:

g(y;x)\!=\!f(y)-\frac{1}{2}(y-x)^{T}\nabla^{2}f(x)(y-x)+\frac{M_{0}}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}+\frac{M_{2}}{3}\|y-x\|^{3}\;\;\forall x,y\!\in\!\mathcal{X},

where
M_{2}\geq L_{2}^{f}
and
M_{0}\geq 0
. Following the same analysis as before, one can easily show that this surrogate is convex (even for
M_{0}=0
) and satisfies all the conditions of Assumption
11
(however, in order to ensure
g(y;x)\geq f(y)
we need
M_{0}\geq\|\nabla^{2}f(x)\|
). Of course, cubic Newton is an efficient scheme for minimizing functions with second derivative smooth. However, GHOM algorithm based on the previous surrogates leads to proximal point type schemes which usually yields larger steps (i.e., larger decrease in the objective) than cubic Newton scheme. For example, if
T_{\text{CN}}
is the cubic Newton step and
T_{\text{PP}}
is the proximal point step from
x
, we have:

\displaystyle f(T_{\text{CN}})\leq f(x)-\frac{\bar{M}_{2}}{12}\|T_{\text{CN}}-x\|^{3}\quad(\text{see}\;[22])

\displaystyle f(T_{\text{PP}})\leq f(x)-\frac{M_{2}}{3}\|T_{\text{PP}}-x\|^{3}-\frac{1}{2}(T_{\text{PP}}-x)^{T}\left(M_{0}B-\nabla^{2}f(x)\right)(T_{\text{PP}}-x),

respectively, where
\bar{M}_{2},M_{2}\geq L_{2}^{f}
. The last inequality follows from
g(T_{\text{PP}};x)\leq g(x;x)=f(x)
and note that
M_{0}B-\nabla^{2}f(x)\succeq 0
if
M_{0}\geq\|\nabla^{2}f(x)\|
. A similar decrease is also valid for the surrogate (
12
).

Example 14
.

(smooth derivative functions). For a function
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
that is
p\geq 1
times differentiable and with the
p
derivative Lipschitz on the full domain
\mathbb{E}
of constant
L_{p}^{f}
and a closed convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{E}
, one can consider for any
M_{p}\geq L_{p}^{f}
the following
p
higher-order surrogate function over
\mathcal{X}
:

g(y;x)=T_{p}^{f}(y;x)+\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

This higher-order surrogate also satisfies all the conditions of Assumption
11
. Indeed, since
M_{p}\geq L_{p}^{f}
, from (
4
) we have
g(y;x)\geq f(y)
for any
y
, i.e., the first property of the surrogate holds. Moreover, the error function
h(y;x)=g(y;x)-f(y)
has
\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)=\text{dom}\,g(\cdot;x)=\text{dom}f=\mathbb{E}
and
\nabla^{p}h(y;x)=\nabla^{p}f(x)-\nabla^{p}f(y)+\nabla^{p}(M_{p}/(p+1)!\,\|y-x\|^{p+1})
. Hence, for
x
fixed,
h
is obviously
p
smooth with Lipschitz constant
L_{p}^{h}=L_{p}^{f}+M_{p}
and satisfies also the third property of a surrogate. Note that if we choose
M_{p}\geq pL_{p}^{f}
, then from (
6
) it follows that the error function
h
is always convex, while the higher-order surrogate
g
is convex only if
f
is convex (see Lemma
5
). Note that in this example we can consider any convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{E}
.

Remark 15
.

If the function
f
additionally satisfies
f(y)\geq T_{p}^{f}(y;x)
for all
y
, then we get for
h
a better bound for the constant in the right hand side of (
4
) than
L_{p}^{h}=M_{p}+L_{p}^{f}
. Indeed, using the third property of a
p
higher-order surrogate function, we have
T_{p}^{h}(y;x)=0
for all
y
and thus:

\displaystyle|h(y;x)-T_{p}^{h}(y;x)|

\displaystyle\!=|g(y;x)-f(y)|=\left|f(y)-T_{p}^{f}(y;x)-\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\right|.

We observe that the last term in the equation above is positive and due to the additional condition
f(y)\geq T_{p}^{f}(y;x)
, we also have that
f(y)-T_{p}^{f}(y)\geq 0
. Hence, using
|a-b|\leq\max\{a,\,b\}
for any two positive scalars
a
and
b
, we further get:

\displaystyle|h(y;x)-T_{p}^{h}(y;x)|\leq\max\left(f(y)-T_{p}^{f}(y;x),\,\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:TayAppBound}}{\leq}\max\left(\frac{L_{p}^{f}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1},\,\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\right)=\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}.

Therefore, in this case we can use in (
4
) for function
h
constant
M_{p}
instead of
L_{p}^{h}=M_{p}+L_{p}^{f}
. Functions that satisfy the additional condition from Remark
15
, are e.g., the convex functions for
p=1
(since in this case from convexity of
f
we automatically have
f(y)\geq T_{1}^{f}(y;x)
).

Example 16
.

(composite functions). Let
f_{1}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
have the
p\geq 1
derivative smooth on
\mathbb{E}
with constant
L_{p}^{f_{1}}
,
f_{2}:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper closed convex function with domain
\text{dom}f_{2}
and
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}f_{2}
a closed convex set. Then, for the composite function
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
one can take the following
p
higher-order surrogate:

g(y;x)=T_{p}^{f_{1}}(y;x)+\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}+f_{2}(y)\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},

where
M_{p}\geq L_{p}^{f_{1}}
. This surrogate satisfies all the conditions of Assumption
11
. Indeed, we observe that
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}f=\text{dom}\,g(\cdot;x)=\text{dom}f_{2}
and the error function
h(y;x)=T_{p}^{f_{1}}(y;x)+\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}-f_{1}(y)
has
\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)=\mathbb{E}
(thus
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)
). Following the same arguments as in Example
14
we obtain that
h
is
p
smooth with Lipschitz constant
L_{p}^{h}=L_{p}^{f_{1}}+M_{p}
. Additionally, if we choose
M_{p}\geq pL_{p}^{f_{1}}
, then the error function
h
is always convex, while the higher-order surrogate function
g
is convex only if
f
is convex.

Example 17
.

(difference of convex functions). Let the function
f_{1}:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{R}}
be proper closed and convex with domain
\text{dom}f_{1}
, the function
f_{2}:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{R}
have the
p_{2}\geq 1
derivative smooth on
\mathbb{E}
with constants
L_{p_{2}}^{f_{2}}
and a convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}f_{1}
. Then, for the difference of convex functions
f=f_{1}-f_{2}
one can take the following higher-order surrogate having two diferent reguralization powers:

g(y;x)=f_{1}(y)+\frac{M_{p_{1}}}{(p_{1}+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p_{1}+1}\!-T_{p_{2}}^{f_{2}}(y;x)+\frac{M_{p_{2}}}{(p_{2}+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p_{2}+1}\;\;\forall x,y\!\in\!\mathcal{X},

where
M_{p_{1}}\geq 0,M_{p_{2}}\geq L_{p_{2}}^{f_{2}}
and
p_{1}
satisfies
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}
and it is odd. Using the same arguments as for the previous examples, one can easily see that this surrogate also satisfies all the conditions of Assumption
11
for
p=p_{2}
. In particular, one can notice that the error function
h(y;x)=\frac{M_{p_{1}}}{(p_{1}+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p_{1}+1}\!-T_{p_{2}}^{f_{2}}(y;x)+\frac{M_{p_{2}}}{(p_{2}+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p_{2}+1}+f_{2}(y)
is
p_{2}
smooth as
\nabla^{p_{2}}(M_{p_{1}}/(p_{1}+1)!\,\|y-x\|^{p_{1}+1})
is either constant or linear, provided that
p_{1}
is odd and
p_{1}\leq p_{2}
. Note that the surrogate corresponding to
p_{1}=p_{2}=1
is frequently employed in the difference of convex functions literature. Moreover, if
f_{1}
is e.g., a simple function or a convex quadratic function and
p_{2}=2
one can easily minimize this surrogate using the same techniques developed for cubic Newton method
[
24
]
. Hence, GHOM algorithm based on such surrogate is one of the first (implementable) schemes for minimizing difference of convex functions that uses higher information than of gradient type. Other choices for the higher-order surrogate are possible depending on the smoothness of the functions
f_{1}
and/or
f_{2}
.

Example 18
.

(bounded derivative functions).
We consider a function
f:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is
p+1
times continuously differentiable and a closed convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{E}
. Assume that the
p+1
derivative of
f
is upper bounded by a positive constant
M_{p+1}
on
\mathcal{X}
, i.e.,
\|D^{p+1}f(x)\|\leq M_{p+1}\;\forall x\in\mathcal{X}
, or, if

p+1
symmetric multilinear forms
\mathcal{Q}_{p+1},\mathcal{H}_{p+1}
on

, i.e.,
\mathcal{Q}_{p+1}[h]^{p+1}\leq D^{p+1}f(x)[h]^{p+1}\leq\mathcal{H}_{p+1}[h]^{p+1}\;\forall x\in\mathcal{X},h\in\mathbb{E}
(e.g., for
p=1
this means that the Hessian is bounded on
\mathcal{X}
by two symmetric matrices, not necessarily positive semidefinite, i.e.,
f
is a weakly convex function with smooth gradient). Then, the
p
derivative of

is Lipschitz continuous on
\mathcal{X}
with
L_{p}^{f}=M_{p+1}
or
L_{p}^{f}=\max(\|\mathcal{H}_{p+1}\|,\|\mathcal{Q}_{p+1}\|)
, and then one has the following

g(y;x)=T_{p}^{f}(y;x)+\frac{\bar{M}_{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\;\text{or}\;=T_{p}^{f}(y;x)+\frac{\mathcal{H}_{p+1}[y-x]^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},

where
\bar{M}_{p+1}\geq M_{p+1}
. One can easily see that these surrogates satisfy Assumption

. The first property of the surrogate follows from Taylor’s theorem with Lagrange form of the remainder. Moreover, the error function
h=g-f
has
\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x)=\text{dom}\,f=\mathbb{E}
and is

\mathcal{X}
with Lipschitz constant
L_{p}^{h}=M_{p+1}+\bar{M}_{p+1}
or
L_{p}^{h}=L_{p}^{f}+\|\mathcal{H}_{p+1}\|
.
Note that the functions considered in Example

may be different from those covered here, as one can find functions with the

Moreover, our algorithm GHOM defined below based on the previous surrogates with

is a (projected) gradient or Netwon scheme with constant Hessian.

The reader can find other examples of higher-order surrogate functions depending on the structure of the objective function
f
in (
10
) and we believe that this paper opens a window of opportunity for higher-order algorithmic research.

Now, we are ready to define our General Higher-Order Majorization-Minimization (GHOM) algorithmic framework.

Algorithm 1

Algorithm GHOM

Given
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and
p\geq 1
, for
k\geq 0
do:

1. Compute the
p
surrogate function
g(y;x_{k})
of
f
near
x_{k}

2. Compute a stationary point
x_{k+1}
of the subproblem:

\min_{y\in\mathcal{X}}g(y;x_{k}),

(13)

satisfying the following descent property

g(x_{k+1};x_{k})\leq g(x_{k};x_{k})\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(14)

From Examples
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
and
18
, one can notice that the algorithm GHOM is simple and depends on at most one parameter,
M_{p}
, whose value can be determined through a line search procedure when e.g., the Lipschitz constant is unknown. Moreover, GHOM generates
x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}
for all
k\geq 0
. If
f
is convex function, by choosing the regularization parameter of the surrogate appropriately we can also have
g(\cdot;x_{k})
convex function (see Lemma
5
) and thus we can use efficient methods from convex optimization to find the global solution
x_{k+1}
of the convex subproblem (
13
) at each iteration, see e.g.,
[
6
,
5
,
13
,
21
]
. When
f
is nonconvex function, our convergence analysis below requires only the computation of a stationary point
x_{k+1}
for the subproblem (
13
) (i.e., finding
x_{k+1}\in\mathcal{X}
such that
0\in\partial g(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{X}}(x_{k+1})
or equivalently
Dg(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathcal{X}
) satisfying, additionally, the descent (
14
). Moreover, when
\text{dom}\,g=\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{E}
the previous stationarity conditions are reduced to
0\in\partial g(x_{k+1};x_{k})
. Note that the vast majority of nonconvex optimization algorithms are able to identify stationary points of nonconvex problems.

3
Convergence analysis of GHOM for convex optimization

In this section we analyze the convergence of algorithm GHOM under various assumptions on the convexity of the objective function
f
over the convex set
\mathcal{X}
.
Note that throughout this section, where the function
f
is considered convex, we assume that
x_{k+1}
is a global minimum of the convex subproblem (
13
). We state this as an assumption:

Assumption 19
.

When the objective function
f
is convex we assume that the higher-order surrogate
g(\cdot;x)
is also a convex function for any
x\in\mathcal{X}
and
x_{k+1}
is a global minimum of the convex subproblem (
13
).

3.1
Global sublinear convergence of GHOM

In this section we derive the global rate of convergence of order
\mathcal{O}(1/k^{p})
for GHOM in terms of function values when (
10
) is a general convex optimization problem. Let
x^{*}\in X
be a minimum point of this convex problem (
10
).

Theorem 20
.

Let the objective function
f
in the optimization problem (
10
) be proper, lower semicontinuous and convex on the closed convex set
\mathcal{X}
.
Additionally, let Assumptions
11
and
19
hold.
Then, the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by algorithm GHOM has the following global sublinear convergence rate:

\displaystyle f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{p!\left(1+\frac{k}{p+1}\right)^{p}}.

(15)

Proof.

Since
h(y,x)
, defined as the error between the
p
higher-order surrogate
g(y;x)
and the function
f
, has the
p
derivative smooth with Lipschitz constant
L_{p}^{h}
, then from (
4
) we get:

h(y;x)\leq T_{p}^{h}(y;x)+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

However, based on the condition (iii) from Assumption
11
the Taylor approximation of
h
of order
p
at
x
satisfies:

T_{p}^{h}(y;x)=h(x;x)+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}h(x;x)[y-x]^{i}=0.

Therefore, we obtain:

\displaystyle h(y;x)=g(y;x)-f(y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1},

which implies that

\displaystyle g(y;x)

\displaystyle\leq f(y)+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(16)

Recall that when
f
is convex, we consider
x_{k+1}
to be a global minimum of
g(\cdot;x_{k})
over convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}\,g(\cdot;x_{k})=\text{dom}f
and since
g(\cdot;x_{k})
is bounded from below by
f
(see Assumption
11
(i)), we further get:

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq g(x_{k+1};x_{k})\!=\min_{y\in\mathcal{X}}g(y;x_{k})\overset{\eqref{eq:5}}{\leq}\min_{y\in\mathcal{X}}(f(y)+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x_{k}\|^{p+1}).

(17)

Since
f
is assumed to be convex, we can choose
y=x_{k}+\alpha(x^{*}-x_{k})
, with
\alpha\in[0,\,1]
, and obtain further:

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\alpha\in[0,\,1]}f(x_{k}+\alpha(x^{*}-x_{k}))+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|x_{k}+\alpha(x^{*}-x_{k})-x_{k}\|^{p+1}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\alpha\in[0,\,1]}f(x_{k})-\alpha(f(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\alpha^{p+1}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{p+1}.

(18)

Let us show that GHOM is a decent algorithm. Indeed, from (
14
) we have:

f(x_{k})=g(x_{k};x_{k})\geq g(x_{k+1};x_{k})\geq f(x_{k+1})\quad\forall k\geq 0.

Hence, all the iterates
x_{k}
are in the level set
\mathcal{L}_{f}(x_{0})
and thus satisfy
\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|\leq R
for all
k\geq 0
. Subtracting the optimal value on both side of (
18
) and recalling the fact that the sublevel set of
f
at
x_{0}
is assumed bounded, we obtain:

f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})\leq\min_{\alpha\in[0,\,1]}(1-\alpha)(f(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))+\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\alpha^{p+1}.

(19)

For simplicity, we denote
\Delta_{k}=f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})
. We consider two cases in (
19
) (see also
[
20
,
21
]
):

First case: if
\Delta_{k}>\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{p!}
, then optimal point is
\alpha^{*}=1
and
\Delta_{k+1}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}.

Second case: if
\Delta_{k}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{p!}
, then optimal point is
\alpha^{*}=\sqrt[p]{\frac{\Delta_{k}p!}{R^{p+1}L_{p}^{h}}}
and obtain:

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\Delta_{k}\left(1-c\Delta_{k}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right),\quad\Delta_{k+1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}\geq\Delta_{k}^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left(1-c\Delta_{k}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\Delta_{k}^{-\frac{1}{p}}\left(1+\frac{c}{p}\Delta_{k}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)=\Delta_{k}^{-\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{c}{p},

where
c=\frac{p}{p+1}\sqrt[p]{\frac{p!}{R^{p+1}L_{p}^{h}}}
and the last inequality is given by
(1-x)^{-p}\geq 1+px
for
x\in[0,\,1]
, see e.g.,
[
27
]
. We now apply recursively the previous inequalities, starting with
k=1
. If
\Delta_{0}\geq\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{p!}
, we are in the first case and then
\Delta_{1}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}
. Then, we will subsequently be in the second case for all
k\geq 2
and

\Delta_{k}\leq\Delta_{1}\left(1+\frac{(k-1)c}{p}\Delta_{1}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{-p}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{(p+1)!}\left(1+\frac{k-1}{p+1}(p+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{-p}.

Otherwise, if
\Delta_{0}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{p!}
, then we are in the second case and obtain:

\Delta_{k}\leq\Delta_{0}\left(1+\frac{kc}{p}\Delta_{0}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)^{-p}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}R^{p+1}}{p!}\left(1+\frac{k}{p+1}\right)^{-p}.

These prove the statement of the theorem.

Note that in the previous proof we have not used that the surrogate
g(\cdot;x_{k})
is a convex function, we only used that
x_{k+1}
is a global minimum of the subproblem (
13
). Moreover, for
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{E}
our convergence rate (
15
) recovers the usual convergence rates
\mathcal{O}(1/k^{p})
of higher-order Taylor-based methods in the unconstrained convex case
[
20
]
(Example
14
) and composite convex case
[
21
]
(Example
16
), respectively. However, from our best knowledge, other examples of surrogate functions (such as Examples
13
,
18
) have not been investigated in the literature yet. Moreover, the convergence results from
[
20
,
21
]
assume Lipschitz continuity of the
p
derivative of the objective function
f
, while Theorem
20
assumes Lipschitz continuity of the
p
derivative of the error function
h=g-f
. Hence, our proof is different from
[
20
,
21
]
. Therefore, Theorem
20
provides a unified convergence analysis for higher-order majorization-minimization algorithms, that covers in particular unconstrained and composite convex problems, under possibly more general assumptions than in
[
20
,
21
]
. In fact there is a major difference between the Taylor expansion approach from
[
20
,
21
]
and the model approximation based on our general majorization-minimization approach. Taylor expansion yields unique approximation model around a given point, while in the majorization-minimization approach one may consider many upper bound models and every model leads to a different optimization method.

3.2
Superlinear convergence of GHOM

Next, by assuming uniform convexity of
f
on
\text{dom}f
, we prove that GHOM can achieve faster rates. More precisely we get local
superlinear
convergence rates for GHOM in several optimality criteria: function values, distance of the iterates to the optimal point and norm of subgradients. For these derivations we first need some auxiliary results.

Lemma 21
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
20
hold. Then, for the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by algorithm GHOM we have
-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1})
and the following relation holds:

\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p}\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(20)

Proof.

Since
x_{k+1}
is the global minimum of the convex subproblem (
13
) (see Assumption
19
), we have that there exists
g^{x_{k+1}}\in\partial g(x_{k+1};x_{k})
such that
\langle g^{x_{k+1}},y-x_{k+1}\rangle\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathcal{X}
. Since the error function
h(y;x_{k})=g(y;x_{k})-f(y)
is differentiable, we obtain from basic calculus rules for convex functions that there exists a subgradient
f^{x_{k+1}}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})
such that
[
29
]
:

f^{x_{k+1}}+\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})=g^{x_{k+1}}.

Since
f
is convex function, we have:

\displaystyle f(y)

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{k+1})+\langle f^{x_{k+1}},y-x_{k+1}\rangle=f(x_{k+1})+\langle g^{x_{k+1}}-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k}),y-x_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{k+1})+\langle-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k}),y-x_{k+1}\rangle\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{X}.

Since
f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}
is also convex and
x_{k+1}\in\mathcal{X}
, from the previous relation we get that
-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1})
, which proves the first part of the lemma. Further, from the definition of the
p
higher-order surrogate function, we know that the error function
h
has the
p
derivative Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{p}^{h}
. Thus, we have the inequality (
5
) for
y=x_{k+1}
, that is:

\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})-\nabla T_{p}^{h}(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p}.

Since the Taylor approximation of
h
at
x_{k}
,
T_{p}^{h}(y;x_{k})
, is zero for any
y\in\mathcal{X}
, we get:

\displaystyle\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p},

(21)

which proves the second part of the lemma.

Lemma 22
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
20
hold and additionally assume that the error function
h=g-f
is convex on
\mathcal{X}
. Then, for the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by the algorithm GHOM, the following relation holds:

\langle\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k+1}\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(22)

Proof.

The error function
h
is
p\geq 2
differentiable satisfying
h(y;x_{k})\geq 0
and
h(x_{k};x_{k})=0
. Combining these relations with the convexity of
h(\cdot;x_{k})
, we have:

\displaystyle 0=h(x_{k};x_{k})

\displaystyle\overset{h\;\text{convex}}{\geq}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\langle\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{h\geq 0}{\geq}\langle\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k+1}\rangle,

which yields our statement.

Now we are ready to prove the local superlinear convergence of the GHOM algorithm in function values for general uniformly convex objective functions.

Theorem 23
.

Let the objective function
f
in the optimization problem (
10
) be proper, lower semicontinuous and uniformly convex of degree
q\geq 2
with constant
\sigma_{q}
on the closed convex set
\mathcal{X}
. Additionally,
let Assumptions
11
and
19
hold
and assume that the error function
h=g-f
is convex on the convex set
\mathcal{X}
. Then, the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by algorithm GHOM has the following convergence rate in function values:

\displaystyle f\left(x_{k+1}\right)-f(x^{*})\leq(q-1)q^{\frac{p-q+1}{q-1}}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{q}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{q-1}}\left(\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\left(f\left(x_{k}\right)-f(x^{*})\right)^{\frac{p}{q-1}}.

(23)

Additionally,
\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|
converges to zero, i.e.:

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|\leq\left(\frac{q}{\sigma_{q}}(f(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(24)

Proof.

From Lemma
21
we have
-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1})
. Recall that
x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
denotes a minimum point of the convex problem (
10
) and
x_{k},x_{k+1}\in\mathcal{X}
. Then, using Lemma
7
, for any
k\geq 0
we have:

\displaystyle f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\geq f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{Lemma}\,\ref{lema:uc}}{\geq}\langle-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k}),x_{k}-x_{k+1}\rangle+\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x_{k}-x_{k+1}\|^{q}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:27}}{\geq}\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x_{k}-x_{k+1}\|^{q}\overset{\eqref{eq:26}}{\geq}\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\left(\frac{p!}{L_{p}^{h}}\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:probConv}}{\geq}\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\left(\frac{p!}{L_{p}^{h}}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\left(\frac{q\sigma_{q}^{\frac{1}{q-1}}}{q-1}\left(f\left(x_{k+1}\right)-f(x^{*})\right)\right)^{\frac{q-1}{p}},

which proves both statements of the theorem.

Note that if
p>q-1
, then the sequence generated by GHOM has local
superlinear
convergence in function values. Indeed, from Theorem
20
we have
f\left(x_{k}\right)-f(x^{*})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
and consequently the right hand side term
\beta_{k}=(q-1)q^{\frac{p-q+1}{q-1}}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{q}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{q-1}}\times\\
\left(\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\right)^{\frac{q}{q-1}}\left(f\left(x_{k}\right)-f(x^{*})\right)^{\frac{p}{q-1}-1}
also converges to zero, provided that
\frac{p}{q-1}>1
. E.g., if
q=2
(
f
strongly convex) and
p=2
, then the rate is quadratic. If
q=2
and
p=3
, then the rate is cubic. Next, we show local superlinear convergence for a sequence of subgradients of the convex objective function in the problem (
10
).

Theorem 24
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
23
the sequence of the norm of subgradients
-\nabla h(x_{k};x_{k-1})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k})
has the following convergence rate:

\displaystyle\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\left(\frac{q}{\sigma_{q}}\right)^{\frac{p}{q-1}}\|\nabla h(x_{k};x_{k-1})\|_{*}^{\frac{p}{q-1}}\quad\forall k\geq 1.

Additionally, the residual of the first-order optimality conditions for (
10
) satisfies:

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{Df(x_{k+1})[x-x_{k+1}]}{\|x-x_{k+1}\|}\geq-\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|\quad\forall k\geq 0.

Proof.

From Lemma
21
we have
-\nabla h(x_{k};x_{k-1})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k})
for all
k\geq 1
. Since GHOM is a descent method and
f
is uniformly convex, we further have:

f(x_{k})\overset{\eqref{eq:main}}{\geq}f(x_{k+1})\overset{\eqref{eq:unifConv}}{\geq}f(x_{k})+\left\langle-\nabla h(x_{k};x_{k-1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{q}\;\;\forall k\geq 1.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we further get:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle-\nabla h(x_{k};x_{k-1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{q}

\displaystyle\geq-\|\nabla h(x_{k};x_{k-1})\|_{*}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|+\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{q},

or equivalently

\|\nabla h(x_{k};x_{k-1})\|_{*}\geq\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{q-1}\quad\forall k\geq 0.

(25)

Using again Lemma
21
, we get:

\displaystyle\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\overset{\eqref{eq:26}}{\leq}\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p}\overset{\eqref{eq:gradrel1}}{\leq}\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\left(\frac{q}{\sigma_{q}}\right)^{\frac{p}{q-1}}\|\nabla h(x_{k};x_{k-1})\|_{*}^{\frac{p}{q-1}}.

Since according to Theorem
23
we have
\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|
converges to zero, the previous relation shows that the sequence of subgradients
-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1})
converges also to zero. This proves the first statement of the theorem.

Further, since we assumed that
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}\,f
has nonempty relative interior, then the following holds
[
29
]
:
\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1})=\partial f(x_{k+1})+\partial\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(x_{k+1})
. Since we have already proved that
-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1})
, it follows that
-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\in\partial f(x_{k+1})+\partial\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}}(x_{k+1})
and thus there exists
f^{x_{k+1}}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})
such that:

\displaystyle\langle f^{x_{k+1}},x-x_{k+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|\|x-x_{k+1}\|\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},k\geq 0,

which yields the second statement of the theorem as
f
is convex and
Df(x_{k+1})[x-x_{k+1}]=\max_{\bar{f}^{x_{k+1}}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})}\langle\bar{f}^{x_{k+1}},x-x_{k+1}\rangle
, see
[
29
]
.

From Theorem
24
we conclude that for
\frac{p}{q-1}>1
the sequence of subgradients
-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1})
converges to zero at a local superlinear rate and it is of the same order as that for the residual of the function values.

Our superlinear convergence results from this Section
3.2
have been derived under the assumption that
h
is convex. Note that from Lemma
5
we can see that by choosing appropriately
M_{p}
in Examples
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
and
M_{p+1}
in Example
18
, we obtain that the error functions,
h
, in these examples are indeed convex. However, if we remove the convexity assumption on
h
we can still prove superlinear convergence for GHOM in function values, but the rate is slightly worse. This result is stated next.

Theorem 25
.

Let the objective function
f
in the optimization problem (
10
) be proper, lower semicontinuous and uniformly convex of degree
q\geq 2
with constant
\sigma_{q}
on the closed convex set
\mathcal{X}
. Additionally,
let Assumptions
11
and
19
hold
. Then, the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by algorithm GHOM has the following convergence rate in function values:

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\left(\frac{q}{\sigma_{q}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{q}}\!\!(f(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))^{\frac{p+1}{q}}.

(26)

Proof.

If
f
is uniformly convex on the convex set
\mathcal{X}
, then it has a unique optimal point
x^{*}\in\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, from (
17
), we have:

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq\min_{y\in\mathcal{X}}(f(y)+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x_{k}\|^{p+1})

\displaystyle\overset{y=x^{*}}{\leq}f(x^{*})+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{p+1}.

(27)

Further, since
f
is uniformly convex of degree
q
, then using
Lemma
7
for
f
and the fact that
\langle f^{x^{*}},x_{k}-x^{*}\rangle\geq 0
, that
x^{*},x_{k}\in\mathcal{X}
and that
f^{x^{*}}\in\partial f(x^{*})
, we get:

\displaystyle f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})\geq\frac{\sigma_{q}}{q}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{q}.

(28)

Combining the inequalities (
27
) and (
28
), we further obtain:

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{p+1}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\left(\frac{q}{\sigma_{q}}(f(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))\right)^{\frac{p+1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\left(\frac{q}{\sigma_{q}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{q}}\!\!(f(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))^{\frac{p+1}{q}-1}\right)(f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})),

thus proving the statement of the theorem.

Remark 26
.

If
q<p+1
in (
26
), we have that
\beta_{k}=\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\left(\frac{q}{\sigma_{q}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{q}}(f(x_{k})-f(x^{*}))^{\frac{p+1}{q}-1}
converges to zero, since from Theorem
20
we have
f\left(x_{k}\right)-f(x^{*})\to 0
, thus obtaining again superlinear convergence in function values. Finally, using the inequality (
28
) in the convergence rates (
23
) and (
26
), respectively, we immediately obtain superlinear convergence also for
\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|
. Since the derivations are straightforward, we omit them.

4
Convergence analysis of GHOM for nonconvex optimization

In this section we analyze the convergence behavior of algorithm GHOM for solving the nonconvex nonsmooth optimization problem (
10
). Hence, in this section we assume a general (possibly nonconvex) proper lsc objective function
f:\mathbb{E}\to\bar{\mathbb{R}}
and a closed convex set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}f
. In these general settings, we only assume that
x_{k+1}
is a stationary point of subproblem (
13
) satisfying, additionally, descent (
14
) (i.e.,
x_{k+1}
satisfies
Dg(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]\geq 0\;\;\forall y\in\mathcal{X}
and (
14
)). Let us define the notion of asymptotic stationary points generated by GHOM algorithm for problem (
10
).

Definition 27
.

Assume that the directional derivatives of the function
f
at any
x\in\mathcal{X}
along
y-x
exist for all
y\in\mathcal{X}
, denoted
Df(x)[y-x]
. Then, a sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
satisfies the asymptotic first-order optimality conditions for the nonconvex problem (
10
) if the following holds:

\liminf_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{Df(x_{k})[x-x_{k}]}{\left\|x-x_{k}\right\|}\geq 0.

Note that if
f
is differentiable on
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{E}
, then the directional derivative
Df(x)[y-x]=\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\rangle
and the asymptotic first-order optimality conditions imply that the sequence
(\nabla f(x_{k}))_{k\geq 0}
converges to
0
.

4.1
Global convergence of GHOM for nonconvex optimization

In this section we analyze the global convergence behavior of the iterates of GHOM in the general nonconvex settings. We first derive some auxiliary result:

Lemma 28
.

Let
\tilde{h}
be a function
p\geq 2
differentiable and with the
p
derivative smooth with constant
L_{p}^{\tilde{h}}
. For any
x,y\in\text{dom}\,\tilde{h}
and scalar
M_{p}^{\tilde{h}}\geq L_{p}^{\tilde{h}}
let us define:

H=\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}(x)+\sum_{i=3}^{p}\frac{1}{(i-1)!}D^{i}\tilde{h}(x)[y-x]^{i-2}+\frac{M_{p}^{\tilde{h}}}{p!}\|y-x\|^{p-1}B.

Then, we have the following bounds on the matrix
H
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(x+\tau(y-x))d\tau\preceq H

\displaystyle\preceq\int_{0}^{1}\left(\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}(x+\tau(y-x))+\frac{M_{p}^{\tilde{h}}+L_{p}^{\tilde{h}}}{(p-1)!}\tau^{p-1}\|y-x\|^{p-1}B\right)\!d\tau.

Proof.

We note that:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}(x)+\sum_{i=3}^{p}\frac{\tau^{i-2}}{(i-2)!}D^{i}\tilde{h}(x)[y-x]^{i-2}+\frac{M_{p}^{\tilde{h}}\tau^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}\|y-x\|^{p-1}B\right)\!d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\nabla^{2}T_{p}^{\tilde{h}}(x+\tau(y-x);x)+\frac{M_{p}^{\tilde{h}}\tau^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}\|y-x\|^{p-1}B\right)\!d\tau

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:TayAppG2}}{\preceq}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}(x+\tau(y-x))+\frac{M_{p}^{\tilde{h}}+L_{p}^{\tilde{h}}}{(p-1)!}\tau^{p-1}\|y-x\|^{p-1}B\right)\!d\tau.

Similarly, we have:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\!\!\overset{\eqref{eq:TayAppG2}}{\succeq}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\left(\!\!\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}(x\!+\!\tau(y\!-\!x))\!+\!\frac{M_{p}^{\tilde{h}}\!-\!L_{p}^{\tilde{h}}}{(p-1)!}\tau^{p-1}\|y-x\|^{p-1}B\right)\!d\tau\!\succeq\!\int_{0}^{1}\!\!\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}(x\!+\!\tau(y\!-\!x))d\tau,

since
M_{p}^{\tilde{h}}\geq L_{p}^{\tilde{h}}
and
B\succeq 0
. These conclude our statement.

Next theorem shows that the sequence generated by GHOM satisfies the asymptotic first-order optimality conditions.

Theorem 29
.

Let the (possibly nonconvex) objective function
f
in the optimization problem (
10
) be proper, lower semicontinuous, have directional derivates at any point
x\in\mathcal{X}
and satisfy Assumption
11
for a given
p\geq 1
. Then, the sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
generated by GHOM satisfies the asymptotic first-order optimality conditions and
\left(f(x_{k})\right)_{k\geq 0}
monotonically decreases.

Proof.

Using the properties of the surrogate (see Assumption
11
) and the descent property (
14
), we obtain:

f(x_{k})=g(x_{k};x_{k})\geq g(x_{k+1};x_{k})\geq f(x_{k+1})\quad\forall k\geq 0.

This relation guarantees that
(f(x_{k}))_{k>0}
is nonincreasing sequence and thus convergent, since
f
is assumed to be bounded from below by
f^{*}
. Moreover, since we also assume the level set
{\cal L}_{f}(x_{0})
bounded (see Section
2
), then it follows that the sequence generated by GHOM
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subset{\cal L}_{f}(x_{0})
is also bounded. Using further the definition of the error function
h
:

\displaystyle 0\leq h(x_{k+1};x_{k})=g(x_{k+1};x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\leq f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1}).

(29)

Telescoping the previous relation for
k=0:\infty
, we get:

0\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\leq f(x_{0})-f^{*}<\infty.

Therefore, the positive term of the series,
(h(x_{k+1};x_{k}))_{k\geq 0}
, necessarily converges to 0. Since
g=h+f
, it also follows that the sequence
(g(x_{k+1};x_{k}))_{k\geq 0}
converges to the same limit as the sequence
(f(x_{k}))_{k>0}
.

For proving the asymptotic first-order optimality conditions we consider two cases:
p\geq 2
and
p=1
. First, for
p\geq 2
, from the optimality of
x_{k+1}
we have that:

Dg(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]\geq 0\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{X}.

Further, from the definition of the error function
h=g-f
, its differentiability and the fact that
f
is assumed to have directional derivatives at any point
x\in\mathcal{X}
, we have from calculus rules that
[
29
]
[Theorem 10.1]:

\displaystyle Df(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle[y-x_{k+1}]=Dg(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]-Dh(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]

\displaystyle\geq-Dh(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]=-\langle\nabla h(x_{k+1};{x}_{k}),y-x_{k+1}\rangle\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{X}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the previous relation, we get:

Df(x_{k+1})[y-x_{k+1}]\geq-\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})\|_{*}\,\|y-x_{k+1}\|\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{X}.

(30)

According to Assumption
11
, the error function
h(\cdot;x_{k})
has the
p
derivative smooth with Lipschitz constant
L_{p}^{h}
on
\mathcal{X}
. Further, let us consider the following auxiliary point
y_{k+1}
(note that in practice we do not need to compute
y_{k+1}
):

y_{k+1}=\argmin_{y\in\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x_{k})}T_{p}^{{h}}(y;x_{k+1})+\frac{M_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1},

where
M_{p}^{h}>L_{p}^{h}
(recall our notation:
T_{p}^{{h}}(y;x_{k+1})
means the Taylor approximation of the function
h(\cdot;x_{k})
around
x_{k+1}
and evaluated at
y
, i.e.,
T_{p}^{{h}}(y;x_{k+1})=h(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]^{i}
). Using the (global) optimality of
y^{k+1}
(recall that in Assumption
11
we consider
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x_{k})
), we have:

\displaystyle T_{p}^{{h}}(y_{k+1};x_{k+1})+\frac{M_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}

\displaystyle\leq T_{p}^{{h}}(x_{k+1};x_{k+1})+\frac{M_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}

\displaystyle=h(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})[x_{k+1}-x_{k+1}]^{i}=h(x_{k+1};x_{k}).

Moreover, writting explicitly the left term of the previous inequality, we get:

\displaystyle h(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})[y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}]^{i}+\frac{M_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}

\displaystyle\leq h(x_{k+1};x_{k}).

Thus, we have:

\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})[y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}]^{i}+\frac{M_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}\leq 0.

(31)

From relation (
4
) we also obtain:

h(y;x_{k})\leq T_{p}^{{h}}(y;x_{k+1})+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}\quad\forall y\in\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x_{k}).

We rewrite this relation for our chosen point
y_{k+1}
:

\displaystyle h(y_{k+1};x_{k})\leq T_{p}^{{h}}(y_{k+1};x_{k+1})+\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}

\displaystyle=h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\!+\!\sum_{i=1}^{p}\!\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})[y_{k+1}\!-\!x_{k+1}]^{i}\!+\!\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:24_domf}}{\leq}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})-\frac{M_{p}^{h}-L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}.

Recalling that
h
is nonnegative, then it follows that:

\displaystyle 0\leq h(x_{k+1};x_{k})-\frac{M_{p}^{h}-L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}.

(32)

This leads to:

h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\geq\frac{M_{p}^{h}-L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}\geq 0.

Since
(h(x_{k+1};x_{k}))_{k\geq 0}
converges to 0, then necessarily
(y_{k+1}-x_{k+1})_{k\geq 0}
converges to
0
and is bounded, since
h
is continuous and
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is bounded (recall that we assume that the sublevel set
\mathcal{L}_{f}(x_{0})
is bounded). Consequently, the sequence
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is also bounded. Moreover, from the first-order necessary optimality conditions for
y_{k+1}
(recall that according to Assumption
11
,
\text{dom}\,h(\cdot;x_{k})
is an open set), we have:

\displaystyle\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})+H_{k+1}[y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}]=0,

(33)

where we denote the matrix

\displaystyle H_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\sum_{i=3}^{p}\frac{1}{(i-1)!}D^{i}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})[y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}]^{i-2}

(34)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{M_{p}^{h}}{p!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p-1}B.

From Lemma
28
, we have that:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{2}h(x_{k+1}+\tau(y_{k+1}-x_{k+1});x_{k})d\tau\preceq H_{k+1}

(35)

\displaystyle\preceq\int_{0}^{1}\!\left(\nabla^{2}h(x_{k+1}\!+\!\tau(y_{k+1}\!-\!x_{k+1});x_{k})\!+\!\frac{M_{p}^{h}\!+\!L_{p}^{h}}{(p-1)!}\tau^{p-1}\|y_{k+1}\!-\!x_{k+1}\|^{p-1}\!B\!\right)\!d\tau.

Since the sequences
(x_{k})_{k>0}
and
(y_{k})_{k>0}
are bounded and
h
is
p\geq 2
times continuously differentiable, then
\nabla^{2}h(x_{k+1}+\tau(y_{k+1}-x_{k+1});x_{k})
is bounded for
\tau\in[0,1]
. Moreover,
y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\rightarrow 0
as
k\to\infty
and it is bounded. Therefore,
H_{k+1}
is bounded and consequently from (
33
) it follows that

\displaystyle\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\to 0\;\text{as}\;k\to\infty.

(36)

For the case
p=1
we can just take
y_{k+1}=x_{k+1}-1/L_{1}^{h}\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})
. Then, using that
h(\cdot;x_{k})
has gradient Lipschitz with constant
L_{1}^{h}
we obtain
[
18
]
:

0\leq h(y_{k+1};x_{k})\leq h(x_{k+1};x_{k})-\frac{1}{2L_{1}^{h}}\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|^{2}_{*},

which further yields

\frac{1}{2L_{1}^{h}}\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|^{2}_{*}\leq h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\to 0\;\text{as}\;k\to\infty,

since we have already proved that the sequence
h(x_{k+1};x_{k})
converges to zero. Therefore, also in the case
p=1
we have (
36
) valid. Finally, using (
36
) in (
30
), minimizing over
y\in\mathcal{X}
and taking the infimum limit, we get that the sequence
\left(x_{k}\right)_{k\geq 0}
satisfies the asymptotic first-order optimality conditions for the nonconvex problem (
10
). This concludes our statements.

Note that the main difficulty in the previous proof is to handle
h
having
p\geq 2
derivative smooth. We overcome this difficulty by introducing a new sequence
(y_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, proving that it has similar properties as the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by GHOM, and then using the optimality conditions for
y_{k}
instead of
x_{k}
. Note that in practice we do not need to compute the auxiliary sequence
y_{k}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{E}
, the previous theorem states that the minimum norm sequence of points from the limiting subdifferential
(\partial f(x_{k}))_{k\geq 0}
, denoted
S(x_{k})
, converges to
0
as
k\to\infty
.

Obviously, the previous theorem does not yield convergence rate for the residual of the first-order optimality conditions. Hence, the requirements from Assumption
11
on the surrogate function
g
do not seem to be reach enough to enable convergence rate in the nonconex settings. More exactly, the bound
h(y;x)\geq 0
for any
y\in\mathcal{X}
is not sufficiently tight when analysing convergence of the residual of the first-order optimality conditions. However, at a closer look one can notice that the surrogate
g
of Example
14
induces the following inequality on the error function
h
:

\displaystyle h(y;x)\!=\!T_{p}^{f}(y;x)+\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}-f(y)\!\geq\!\frac{M_{p}-L_{p}^{f}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\;\;\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},

where
M_{p}>L_{p}^{f}
. In fact, using the same reasoning, it is easy to see that such a relation holds for all the surrogate functions from Examples
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
and
18
. Hence, let us additionally assume that our surrogate function satisfies the following inequality in terms of the error function
h
for some positive constant
C_{p}>0
:

\displaystyle C_{p}\|y-x\|^{p+1}\leq h(y;x)\;\left(:=g(y;x)-f(y)\right)\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(37)

Hence, we prove below that if we require additionally the condition (
37
), we can get convergence rate to a
point from which there is no descent direction, i.e., we can strengthen the results from Theorem
29
.

Theorem 30
.

Let the assumptions from Theorem
29
hold. Additionally, assume that our surrogate function satisfies the relation (
37
). Then, there exists an iteration index
i_{*}\in\{0,\cdots,k-1\}
such that the following convergence rate in terms of first-order optimality conditions holds:

\inf_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\frac{Df(x_{i^{*}})[x-x_{i^{*}}]}{\left\|x-x_{i^{*}}\right\|}\geq-\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\left(\frac{(f(x_{0})-f^{*})}{C_{p}k}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}.

Proof.

Since the error function
h
has the
p
derivative Lipschitz, we have:

\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})-\nabla T_{p}^{h}(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p}.

Using that
\nabla T_{p}^{h}(x_{k+1};x_{k})=0
(according to Assumption
11
(iii)), we get:

\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p}.

Combining this relation with our assumptions, we further obtain:

\displaystyle f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\overset{\eqref{eq: hf_dom}}{\geq}h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\overset{\eqref{eq:unifh_domf}}{\geq}C_{p}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p+1}

\displaystyle=C_{p}\left(\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\geq C_{p}\left(\frac{p!}{L_{p}^{h}}\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}.

Telescoping from
i=0:k-1
the previous inequality, we further get:

\displaystyle f(x_{0})-f^{*}

\displaystyle\!\geq C_{p}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{p!}{L_{p}^{h}}\|\nabla h(x_{i+1};x_{i})\|_{*}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\!\!\!=\!C_{p}\left(\frac{p!}{L_{p}^{h}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\|\nabla h(x_{i+1};x_{i})\|_{*}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq k\cdot C_{p}\left(\frac{p!}{L_{p}^{h}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\min_{i=0:k-1}\|\nabla h(x_{i+1};x_{i})\|_{*}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}

or equivalently

\displaystyle\min_{i=0:k-1}\|\nabla h(x_{i+1};x_{i})\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{h}}{p!}\left(\frac{f(x_{0})-f^{*}}{C_{p}k}\right)^{\frac{p}{p+1}}.

Let
i_{*}\in\{0,\cdots,k-1\}
be the iteration index where the minimum in the previous relation is achieved. Then, combining the previous relation with (
30
) and minimizing over
y\in\mathcal{X}
, we obtain our statement.

It is known that for general nonconvex problems deciding whether a descent direction exists from a point is NP-hard

. However, for an adaptive regularization algorithm (called AR

,
8
]
or for a first-order algorithm on problems with composite structure

]
similar convergence rates as in Theorem

have been derived. The results for GHOM algorithm on global convergence to a point from which there is no descent direction (including convergence rates) from Theorems

and

are more general than the ones from literature as they cover more complicated objective functions (e.g., general composite models or nonsmooth objective functions) and many new surrogate functions (see Examples

and

4.2
Local convergence of GHOM under the KL property

The KL property was first analyzed in details in
[
2
]
and then widely applied to analyze the convergence behavior of various first-order
[
1
,
2
]
and second-order
[
11
,
30
]
algorithms for nonconvex optimization.
However, to the best of our knowledge there are no studies analyzing the convergence rate of higher-order majorization-minimization algorithms under the KL property
. The main difficulty comes from the fact that
f
satisfies the KL property while the error function
h
is assumed smooth and it is hard to establish connections between them. In the next theorem we connect the geometric property of nonconvex function
f
with the smooth property of
h
through the point
y_{k+1}
and establish local convergence of GHOM in the full parameter regime of the KL property. Let us denote the set of limit points of the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by algorithm GHOM with
\Omega(x_{0})
.

Lemma 31
.

Let the assumptions from Theorem
29
hold and, additionally, assume that
f
is continuous. Then,
\Omega(x_{0})
is compact set and
f
is constant on
\Omega(x_{0})
.

Proof.

From Theorem
29
we have that the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is bounded, hence the set of limit points
\Omega(x_{0})
is also bounded. Closedness of
\Omega(x_{0})
also follows by observing that
\Omega(x_{0})
can be viewed as an intersection of closed sets, i.e.
\Omega(x_{0})=\cap_{j\geq 0}\overline{\cup_{k\geq j}\{x_{k}\}}
. Hence,
\Omega(x_{0})
is a compact set and
\text{dist}(x_{k},\Omega(x_{0}))\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Further, let us also show that
f(\Omega(x_{0}))
is constant. From Theorem
29
we have that
\left(f(x_{k})\right)_{k\geq 0}
is monotonically decreases and since
f
is assumed bounded from below by
f^{*}>-\infty
, it converges, let us say to
f_{*}>-\infty
, i.e. ,
f(x_{k})\to f_{*}
as
k\to\infty
. On the other hand let
x_{*}
be a limit point of the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
, i.e.,
x_{*}\in\Omega(x_{0})
. This means that there is a subsequence
(x_{k_{j}})_{j\geq 0}
such that
x_{k_{j}}\to x_{*}
as
j\to\infty
. If
f
is continuous, then
\lim_{j\to\infty}f(x_{k_{j}})=f(x_{*})
and
f(x_{*})=\lim_{j\to\infty}f(x_{k_{j}})=f_{*}
.
Hence, we get
f(\Omega(x_{0}))=f_{*}
.

Note that in order to derive rates we need to consider explicit form for the function
\kappa
in Definition
9
. Below we consider the case of semi-algebraic functions, i.e., we assume that
f
satisfies the KL property (
9
) (from previous lemma we note that all the conditions of the KL property (
9
) are satisfied).

Theorem 32
.

Let the (possibly nonconvex) objective function
f
in the optimization problem (
10
) be proper, continuous, have directional derivates at any point
x\in\mathcal{X}
and satisfy Assumption
11
for a given
p\geq 1
. Additionally, assume that
f
satisfies the KL property (
9
) for some
r>1
. Then, the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by GHOM satisfies:

1.

If

, then

converges locally to
f_{*}
at a superlinear rate.

2.

If
r=p+1
, then
f(x_{k})
converges locally to
f_{*}
at a linear rate.

3.

If
r<p+1
, then
f(x_{k})
converges locally to
f_{*}
at a sublinear rate.

Proof.

Note that the set of limit points
\Omega(x_{0})
of the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by GHOM is compact,
\text{dist}(x_{k},\Omega(x_{0}))\to 0
as
k\to\infty
and
f(\Omega(x_{0}))
is constant taking value
f_{*}
(see Lemma

). Moreover, we have that
\left(f(x_{k})\right)_{k\geq 0}

. Then, for any

k_{0}
such that:

x_{k}\in\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\text{dist}(x,\Omega(x_{0}))\leq\delta,f_{*}<f(x)<f_{*}+\epsilon\}\quad\forall k\geq k_{0}\;\text{ and }\;\eqref{eq:kl}\;\text{ holds}.

Hence, all the conditions of the KL property from Definition
9
are satisfied and we can exploit the KL inequality (
9
). Combining (
29
) with (
32
) we get:

\displaystyle\frac{M_{p}^{h}-L_{p}^{h}}{(p+1)!}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{p+1}\leq f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(38)

for any fixed
M_{p}^{h}>L_{p}^{h}
. From
x_{k+1}
being a stationary point of subproblem (
13
), we have that
Dg(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathcal{X}
. From the relation

, using that
h
is differentiable we further get that
Dh(x_{k+1};{x}_{k})[y-x_{k+1}]+Df(x_{k+1})[y-x_{k+1}]\geq 0
for all
y\in\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, using that
h
is differentiable,
Df(x_{k+1})[y-x_{k+1}]=\sup_{f^{x_{k+1}}\in\partial f(x_{k+1})}\langle f^{x_{k+1}},y-x_{k+1}\rangle
and
\mathcal{X}
is convex set, from calculs rules we get that
-\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\in\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1})
. Then, using (
33
) and the definition of
H_{k+1}
from (
34
),
we obtain:

\displaystyle S(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\text{dist}(0,\partial(f+\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{X}})(x_{k+1}))\leq\|\nabla h(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}

\displaystyle=\|H_{k+1}(y_{k+1}-x_{k+1})\|_{*}\leq c_{\max}\cdot\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(39)

where
c_{\max}=\max_{k\geq 0}\|H_{k+1}\|<\infty
(see (
35
)). From the KL property (
9
), we have:

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{r}S(x_{k+1})^{r}\overset{\eqref{eq:nfyx}}{\leq}\sigma_{r}c_{\max}^{r}\|y_{k+1}-x_{k+1}\|^{r}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:hfy}}{\leq}\sigma_{r}c^{r}_{\max}\left(\frac{(p+1)!}{M_{p}^{h}-L_{p}^{h}}\right)^{\frac{r}{p+1}}(f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1}))^{\frac{r}{p+1}}\quad\forall k\geq k_{0}.

Let us denote
\Delta_{k}=f(x_{k})-f_{*}
and
C_{\max}=\sigma_{r}c^{r}_{\max}\left(\frac{(p+1)!}{M_{p}^{h}-L_{p}^{h}}\right)^{\frac{r}{p+1}}
. Then, we obtain:

\displaystyle\Delta_{k+1}\leq C_{\max}(\Delta_{k}-\Delta_{k+1})^{\frac{r}{p+1}}\quad\forall k\geq k_{0}.

(40)

If we denote
\tilde{\Delta}_{k}=C^{\frac{p+1}{r-p-1}}_{\max}\Delta_{k}
, we further get the recurrence:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{k+1}^{\frac{p+1}{r}}\leq\tilde{\Delta}_{k}-\tilde{\Delta}_{k+1}\quad\forall k\geq k_{0}.

(41)

We distinguish the following cases:

Case (i):

r>p+1
. Then, from (
41
) we have:

\tilde{\Delta}_{k+1}\leq\left(1+\tilde{\Delta}_{k+1}^{\frac{p+1}{r}-1}\right)^{-1}\tilde{\Delta}_{k},

and since
f(x_{k})\to f_{*}
it follows that
\tilde{\Delta}_{k+1}\to 0
and hence
\tilde{\Delta}_{k+1}^{\frac{p+1}{r}-1}\to\infty
as
\frac{p+1}{r}-1<0
. Therefore, in this case
f(x_{k})
locally converges to
f_{*}
at a superlinear rate.

Case (ii):

r=p+1
. Then, from (
40
) we have:

(1+C_{\max})\Delta_{k+1}\leq C_{\max}\Delta_{k},

hence in this case
f(x_{k})
locally converges to
f_{*}
at a linear rate.

Case (iii):

r<p+1
. Then, from (
41
) we have:

\tilde{\Delta}_{k+1}^{1+\frac{p+1-r}{r}}\leq\tilde{\Delta}_{k}-\tilde{\Delta}_{k+1},\;\text{with}\;\;\frac{p+1-r}{r}>0,

and from Lemma 11 in
[
21
]
we have for some constant
\alpha>0
:

\tilde{\Delta}_{k}\leq\frac{\tilde{\Delta}_{k_{0}}}{(1+\alpha(k-k_{0}))^{\frac{r}{p+1-r}}}\quad\forall k\geq k_{0},

Therefore, in this case
f(x_{k})
locally converges to
f_{*}
at a sublinear rate.

Note that the superlinear rate from Theorem
23
was derived for uniformly convex functions (i.e., the function
\kappa
in Definition
9
is of the form
\kappa(t)=c_{\text{unif}}\cdot t^{1/q}
, with
q\geq 2
), while the superlinear rate from Theorem
32
was obtained for the class of (possibly nonconvex) semi-algebraic functions (i.e., function
\kappa
in Definition
9
is of the form
\kappa(t)=c_{\text{sa}}\cdot t^{1-1/r}
, with
r>1
). Consequently, also the proofs in these two theorems are different.

Further, for nonconvex unconstrained problems we can further derive convergence rates for the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by GHOM in terms of first- and second-order optimality criteria. We use the notation
a^{+}=\max(a,0)
.

Theorem 33
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
p>1
times differentiable function with smooth
p
derivative having Lipschitz constant
L_{p}^{f}
and
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this case, according to Example
14
, we can consider the surrogate function:

g(y;x)=T_{p}^{f}(y;x)+\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|y-x\|^{p+1},

with
M_{p}>L_{p}^{f}
. Additionally assume that
x_{k+1}
is a local minimum of subproblem (
13
). Then, the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
generated by GHOM satisfies the following convergence rate in terms of first and second-order optimality conditions:

\displaystyle\min_{i=1:k}

\displaystyle\max\!\left(\!\left(-\lambda_{\min}^{+}(\nabla^{2}f(x_{i}))\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\!,p^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\lambda_{\max}^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}(B)\!\left(\!\frac{M_{p}+L_{p}^{f}}{(p-1)!}\!\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p(p-1)}}\!\!\|\nabla f(x_{i})\|_{*}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\!\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p(p+1)}{((p-1)!)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\cdot\frac{(M_{p}+L_{p}^{f})^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}}{M_{p}-L_{p}^{f}}\cdot(f(x_{0})-f^{*})\cdot\frac{\lambda_{\max}^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}(B)}{k}.

Proof.

From the descent property (
14
), we have:
g(x_{k};x_{k})\geq g(x_{k+1};x_{k})
. Further, taking into account the property
(iii)
of the surrogate function, we get:

\displaystyle f(x_{k})\geq f(x_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}f(x_{k})[x_{k+1}-x_{k}]^{i}+\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p+1}.

If we define
r_{k}=\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|
, then we further get:

-\frac{M_{p}}{(p+1)!}r_{k+1}^{p+1}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}f(x_{k})[x_{k+1}-x_{k}]^{i}.

(42)

In view of relation (
4
), we have:

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})\leq T_{p}^{f}(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\frac{L_{p}^{f}}{(p+1)!}r_{k+1}^{p+1}

\displaystyle\leq f(x_{k})+\!\sum_{i=1}^{p}\!\frac{1}{i!}D^{i}f(x_{k})[x_{k+1}\!-x_{k}]^{i}\!+\frac{L_{p}^{f}}{(p+1)!}r_{k+1}^{p+1}\!\overset{\eqref{eq:11}}{\leq}\!f(x_{k})\!-\frac{M_{p}-L_{p}^{f}}{(p+1)!}r_{k+1}^{p+1}.

Hence, we obtain the following descent relation:

f(x_{k})-f(x_{k+1})\geq\frac{M_{p}-L_{p}^{f}}{(p+1)!}r_{k+1}^{p+1}.

(43)

Further, from the optimal conditions for
x_{k+1}
we obtain:

\nabla g(x_{k+1};x_{k})=\nabla T_{p}^{f}(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\frac{M_{p}}{p!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p-1}B(x_{k+1}-x_{k})=0.

(44)

Using inequality (
5
) for
f
, we get:

\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})-\nabla T_{p}^{f}(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|_{*}\leq\frac{L_{p}^{f}}{p!}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p}.

This yields the following relation:

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla T_{p}^{f}(x_{k+1},x_{k})\|_{*}+\frac{L_{p}^{f}}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:9}}{\leq}\|-\frac{M_{p}}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}B(x_{k+1}-x_{k})\|_{*}+\frac{L_{p}^{f}}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p}\leq\frac{M_{p}+L_{p}^{f}}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p}

or equivalently

\left(\frac{p!}{M_{p}+L_{p}^{f}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|_{*}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\leq r_{k+1}^{p+1}.

(45)

Moreover, since
x_{k+1}
is a local minimum of
g(\cdot;x_{k})
, we have:

\nabla^{2}g(x_{k+1};x_{k})\succcurlyeq 0.

Computing explicitly the above expression, we obtain:

\nabla^{2}T_{p}^{f}(x_{k+1};x_{k})+\frac{M_{p}(p-1)}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-3}B(x_{k+1}-x_{k})(x_{k+1}-x_{k})^{T}B+\frac{M_{p}}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}B\succcurlyeq 0.

(46)

However, from (
6
) applied to
f
, we have:

\nabla^{2}f(x_{k+1})+\frac{L_{p}^{f}}{(p-1)!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}B\succcurlyeq\nabla^{2}T_{p}^{f}(x_{k+1};x_{k}).

Returning with the above relation in (
46
) we get:

\nabla^{2}f(x_{k+1})+\frac{M_{p}+pL_{p}^{f}}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}B+\frac{M_{p}(p-1)}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-3}B(x_{k+1}-x_{k})(x_{k+1}-x_{k})^{T}B\succcurlyeq 0.

Rearranging the terms, we obtain:

\displaystyle-\nabla^{2}f(x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq\frac{M_{p}+pL_{p}^{f}}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}B+\frac{M_{p}(p-1)}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-3}B(x_{k+1}-x_{k})(x_{k+1}-x_{k})^{T}B

\displaystyle\preccurlyeq\frac{M_{p}+pL_{p}^{f}}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}B+\frac{M_{p}(p-1)}{p!}r_{k+1}^{p-3}r_{k+1}^{2}B\preccurlyeq\frac{M_{p}+L_{p}^{f}}{(p-1)!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}B.

Tacking the maximum eigenvalue, we obtain:

\displaystyle\!\lambda_{\max}(\!-\!\nabla^{2}\!f(x_{k+\!1})\!)\!\leq\!\!\frac{M_{p}\!+\!L_{p}^{f}}{(p\!-\!1)!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}\lambda_{\max}(B)\,\text{or}-\!\!\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^{2}\!f(x_{k+\!1})\!)\!\!\leq\!\!\frac{M_{p}\!+\!L_{p}^{f}}{(p\!-\!1)!}r_{k+1}^{p-1}\lambda_{\max}(B).

Finally, using the notation
a^{+}=\max(a,0)
and the fact that if
a\leq b
for some
b\geq 0
, then also
a^{+}\leq b
, the above inequality yields:

\left(\frac{(p-1)!}{(M_{p}+L_{p}^{f})\lambda_{\max}(B)}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}(-\lambda_{\min}^{+}(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k+1})))^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}\leq r_{k+1}^{p+1}.

(47)

By combining (
45
) and (
47
) we get the following compact form:

\displaystyle\zeta_{k+1}:=\max\Bigg(\left(\frac{(p-1)!}{(M_{p}+L_{p}^{f})\lambda_{\max}(B)}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}(-\lambda_{\min}^{+}(\nabla^{2}f(x_{k+1})))^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(\frac{p!}{M_{p}+L_{p}^{f}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\|\nabla f(x_{k+1})\|_{*}^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\Bigg)\leq r_{k+1}^{p+1}.

Telescoping (
43
), we get:

f(x_{0})-f^{*}\geq\frac{M_{p}-L_{p}^{f}}{(p+1)!}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}r_{i+1}^{p+1}\geq\frac{M_{p}-L_{p}^{f}}{(p+1)!}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\zeta_{i+1}\geq\frac{k(M_{p}-L_{p}^{f})}{(p+1)!}\min_{i=1:k}\zeta_{i}.

Rearranging the terms we get the statement of the theorem.

Note that
[
4
,
6
,
8
]
obtains similar convergence results for their Taylor-based algorithsm as in Theorem
33
. However,
x_{k+1}
in these papers must satisfy some second-order optimality conditions. E.g., in
[
6
]
the authors require:
\max\left(0,\!-\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^{2}g(x_{k+1};x_{k})\right)\!\leq\!\theta\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p-1}
for some
\theta\!>0
and
\|\nabla g(x_{k+1};x_{k})\|\!\leq\!\theta\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{p}
.

5
Conclusions

This paper has derived a unified analyzis of the convergence behaviour of higher-order majorization-minimization algorithms for minimizing convex/nonconvex functions that admit a surrogate model such that the corresponding error function has a
p\geq 1
higher-order Lipschitz continuous derivative. Under these settings we have derived global convergence results for our algorithm in terms of function values or first-order optimality conditions. Faster rates of convergence were established under uniform convexity or KL property of the objective function. One can also notice that although the conditions for the surrogate from Assumption
11
were sufficienttly powerful to derive convergence results for general (non)convex problems, for deriving convergence rates in some first-order optimality criterion an additional condition (
37
) on the surrogate was required in the nonconex setting. Finally, for unconstrained nonconvex problems we have derived convergence rates in terms of first- and second-order optimality conditions. Hence, our algorithmic framework led to an elegant and novel convergence analysis, yielding new results or covering results that are otherwise scattered across a dozen works.

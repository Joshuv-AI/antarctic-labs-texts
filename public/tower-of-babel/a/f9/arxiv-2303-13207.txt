Exact Large Charge in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM


Abstract
In four-dimensional 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 super Yang-Mills theory with gauge group S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) , we present a closed-form solution for a family of integrated four-point functions involving stress tensor multiplet composites of arbitrary R-charge. These integrated correlators are shown to be equivalent to a one-dimensional semi-infinite lattice of harmonic oscillators with nearest-neighbor interactions, evolving over the fundamental domain of S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℤ ) SL(2,\mathbb{Z}) . The solution, exceptionally simple in an S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℤ ) SL(2,\mathbb{Z}) -invariant eigenbasis, is exact in the R-charge p p , rank N N , and complexified gauge coupling τ \tau . This permits a systematic and non-perturbative large charge expansion for any N N and τ \tau . Especially novel is a double-scaled “gravity regime” in which p ∼ N 2 ≫ 1 p\sim N^{2}\gg 1 , holographically dual to a large charge regime of semiclassical type IIB string theory in AdS 5 × {}_{5}\,\times S 5 . Our results in this limit provide a holographic computation of integrated semiclassical string amplitudes at arbitrary string coupling, including an emergent string scale with a large charge dressing factor. We compare to extremal correlators in superconformal QCD, for which we predict new genus expansions at large charge scaling with N N .

Appendix A
Properties of Eisenstein series

{app:Eisensteins}

The real-analytic (non-holomorphic) Eisenstein series is the unique modular-invariant solution to the following eigenvalue equation

\Delta_{\tau}E_{s}(\tau)=s(1-s)E_{s}(\tau)\,,

(A.1)

where
\Delta_{\tau}
is the scalar hyperbolic Laplacian

\Delta_{\tau}=-y^{2}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}\right),

(A.2)

where
\tau=x+iy
. The completed Eisenstein series is given by

E_{s}^{*}(\tau):=\Lambda(s)E_{s}(\tau)\,,

(A.3)

where
\Lambda(s)
is the completed Riemann zeta function

\Lambda(s):=\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\zeta(2s)=\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{2}-s\right).

(A.4)

The completed Eisenstein series satisfies the functional equation

E_{s}^{*}(\tau)=E_{1-s}^{*}(\tau)\,,

(A.5)

which is manifest from its Fourier expansion

E_{s}^{*}(\tau)=\Lambda(s)y^{s}+\Lambda(1-s)y^{1-s}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}4\cos(2\pi kx)\frac{\sigma_{2s-1}(k)}{k^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}\sqrt{y}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi ky)\penalty\ .

(A.6)

There is a simple pole at
s=1
with constant residue,

\Res_{s=1}E_{s}^{*}(\tau)={1\over 2}

(A.7)

The Eisenstein series also admits a representation in terms of the following lattice sum

E_{s}^{*}(\tau)=\frac{\Gamma(s)}{2}\sum_{(m,n)\neq(0,0)}Y_{mn}(\tau)^{-s}\penalty\ ,\qquad Y_{mn}(\tau)=\pi\frac{|m+n\tau|^{2}}{y}\penalty\ .

(A.8)

We refer to Section 2 of
[
25
]
for further details.

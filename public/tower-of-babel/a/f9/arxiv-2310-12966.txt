Bialgebra structures on flat Lie algebras
Amine Bahayou

Abstract
In this paper, we give a complete characterization of Lie bialgebra structures on nondegenerate flat Lie algebras. Furthermore, we illustrate this classification with examples from various classes.



1
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to provide a complete characterization of bialgebra structures on
2
-step solvable Lie algebras of particular type called
flat Lie algebras
. This is motivated by our prior work
[
1
]
.

The main theorem specifies the form that cocycles must take depending on whether the flat Lie algebra is degenerate or nondegenerate. A characterization of the invariant bivectors for a flat Lie algebra is presented. This helps classify cocycles on such algebras. The main theorem provides a decomposition of a cocycle of a flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
into a coboundary part and a cocycle part that maps elements of its abelian subalgebra
\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{z}
to the set of its invariant bivectors
\left(\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

The structure of the paper is as follows: Firstly, we present a brief introduction to the key concepts of Lie bialgebras and flat Lie algebras. Next, we state and prove the main theorem, which classifies Lie bialgebra structures on nondegenerate flat Lie algebras. Finally, we discuss a few examples.

We expect that these findings will significantly enhance the understanding of Poisson-Lie group structures endowed with a flat invariant metric.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Lie bialgebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a real Lie algebra and
\xi:\mathfrak{g}\to\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
a
1
-cocycle with respect to the adjoint representation, i.e.

\xi([x,y])=\operatorname{ad}_{x}\xi(y)-\operatorname{ad}_{y}\xi(x)\ \text{for all}\ x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Here, the adjoint representation of
\mathfrak{g}
is extended to the vector space
\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
as follows:

\operatorname{ad}_{x}(y\wedge z):=\operatorname{ad}_{x}(y)\wedge z+y\wedge\operatorname{ad}_{x}(z)\ \text{for all}\ x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

The pair
(\mathfrak{g},\xi)
is called a
Lie bialgebra
if the transpose map
\xi^{t}:\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
induces a Lie bracket on the dual vector space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The Lie bialgebra structure described above constitutes the underlying infinitesimal structure of the Poisson-Lie group structure. (see
[
4
]
).

2.2
Flat Lie algebras

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(real of finite dimension) is said to be
flat
if it is endowed with a positive definite scalar product
\langle\,,\rangle
for which the associated infinitesimal Levi-Civita connection, defined for all
x,y,z
in
\mathfrak{g}
by

2\langle\nabla_{x}y,z\rangle=\langle[x,y],z\rangle+\langle[z,x],y\rangle+\langle[z,y],x\rangle

(1)

has zero curvature

R(x,y,z)=\nabla_{[x,y]}z-\left(\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}z-\nabla_{y}\nabla_{x}z\right)\equiv 0.

(2)

In other words, the associated Lie group
G
of
\mathfrak{g}
, endowed with the unique left invariant Riemannian metric extending
\langle\,,\rangle
has its Levi-Civita connection flat.

Milnor in
[
6
]
characterized these flat Lie algebras. Some refinements have been provided later in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\rangle)
be a flat Lie algebra. Then

\mathfrak{g}
decomposes orthogonally as

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],

where
\mathfrak{z}
is the center of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{s}
is an abelian Lie subalgebra,
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is the commutator ideal satisfying the following conditions:

1.

[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is abelian and even dimensional,

2.

\operatorname{ad}_{x}=\nabla_{x}
, for any
x
in
\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{s}
.

Moreover, from
[
2
]
, we have the following
normal form
of a flat Lie algebra:

\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k_{0}},z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{2m}\}

(3)

where
\spann\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{2m}\}
is the commutator ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
which is abelian,
\spann\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}\}
its center (possibly trivial) and
\spann\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}\}
its abelian subalgebra such that
k_{0}\leq m
and

[s_{i},d_{2j-1}]=\lambda_{ij}\,d_{2j},\ [s_{i},d_{2j}]=-\lambda_{ij}\,d_{2j-1}\ \text{for all}\ i=1,\ldots,k_{0},\,\text{and}\ j=1,\ldots,m.

(4)

Examples 2.2
.

1.

Any commutative Lie algebra is flat.

2.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
with the brackets

[s,d_{1}]=d_{2},\ [s,d_{2}]=-d_{1},\ [d_{1},d_{2}]=0,

is the smallest non abelian flat Lie algebra.

In the sequel, it is assumed that the flat Lie algebras we are considering are noncommutative.

Remarks 2.3
.

1.

A flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is unimodular,
2
-step solvable whose nilradical is precisely
\mathfrak{z}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.

2.

A
complete
Lie algebra has a trivial center and all its derivations are inner. Conversely, a flat Lie algebra is not complete since there exists a derivation that maps to zero on the subalgebra
\mathfrak{s}
and acts as the identity on the derived subalgebra
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and it is not an inner derivation.

2.2.1
Characteristic matrix

The
characteristic matrix
of a flat Lie algebra is defined in terms of its structural constants (
4
) as follows:

\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{11}&\lambda_{21}&\lambda_{31}&\dots&\lambda_{k_{0}1}\\
\lambda_{12}&\lambda_{22}&\lambda_{32}&\dots&\lambda_{k_{0}2}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\lambda_{1m}&\lambda_{2m}&\lambda_{3m}&\dots&\lambda_{k_{0}m}\end{pmatrix}.

(5)

The key property of this matrix is its injectivity and none of the matrix rows are zero, which is a direct result of
\mathfrak{z}\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\{0\}
.

The change of orthonormal basis of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
that preserves the property of
\operatorname{ad}_{s_{k}}
being a block diagonal matrix consists precisely of the following transformations:

1.

Permutations of the planes
P_{i}=\mathrm{Span}\{d_{2i-1},d_{2i}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.

2.

Rotations of each of these planes using an orthogonal matrix
O_{i}\in\mathrm{O}(2)
.

The resulting operation on the characteristic matrix
\Lambda
involves changing its rows
L_{i}
to
L_{\sigma(i)}
according to permutation
\sigma\in S_{m}
, and changing
L_{i}
to
-L_{i}
for
O_{i}\in\mathrm{O}(2)
with
\det O_{i}=-1
.

We rearrange the planes
P_{i}
of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
so that the first row vectors
L_{1},\ldots,L_{k_{0}}
of
\lambda
are linearly independent. Then we fix this new ordered orthonormal basis of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and only allow rotations that take
L_{i}
to
-L_{i}
, if needed.

2.3
Degenerate and nondegenerate flat Lie algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
be a flat Lie algebra with a fixed basis of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
for which (
4
) is satisfied.
\mathfrak{g}
is
nondegenerate
if there exists a basis of
\mathfrak{s}
such that for all
i\neq j
in
\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

\text{there exists}\ k\ \text{in}\ \{1,\ldots,k_{0}\},\ \text{such that}\ \lambda_{kj}^{2}\neq\lambda_{ki}^{2}.

(6)

Otherwise, the Lie algebra is
degenerate
.

Remarks 2.4
.

1.

The flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\spann\{s\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},d_{4}\}
with the brackets

[s,d_{1}]=d_{2},\ [s,d_{2}]=-d_{1},\ [s,d_{3}]=\alpha d_{4},\ [s,d_{4}]=-\alpha d_{3},\ \alpha\neq 0

is nondegenerate for
\alpha\neq-1,1
and degenerate for
\alpha=\pm 1
.

2.

The above definitions are coordinate-free. Indeed, a degenerate flat Lie algebra is characterized by the existence of
i\neq j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
L_{j}=\varepsilon L_{i},\ \varepsilon=\pm 1
(where
L_{i},L_{j}
are the rows of the characteristic matrix (
5
)) and this is invariant under change of basis of
\mathfrak{s}
(see
[
1
]
).

\mathfrak{g}=\spann\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}\}\oplus\spann\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}\}\oplus\spann\{d_{1},\ldots,d_{2m}\}.

. The coefficients of
\xi(s_{k})
,
\xi(z_{k})

\displaystyle\xi(s_{k})=

3.2
The set of invariants

Let
\mathfrak{g}
a flat Lie algebra. The invariants of
\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
are defined as follows

\left(\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}=\left\{r\in\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\ \operatorname{ad}_{x}r=0\ \text{for all}\ x\in\mathfrak{g}\right\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a flat Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
we have

\left(\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\right)^{\mathfrak{g}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\left(\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{z}\right)\oplus\left(\bigwedge^{2}P_{i}\right)&\text{if}\ \mathfrak{g}\ \text{is nondegenerate},\\
\left(\bigwedge^{2}\mathfrak{z}\right)\oplus\left(\bigwedge^{2}P_{i}\right)\oplus\left(\underset{i<j}{\oplus}A_{ij}\oplus B_{ij}\right)&\text{if}\ \mathfrak{g}\ \text{is degenerate},\end{array}\right.

where
P_{i}=\spann\{d_{2i-1},d_{2i}\}
and the last sum stands for the couples
i<j
such that
L_{j}=\varepsilon L_{i}
with
A_{ij}=\mathbb{R}(d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j-1}+\varepsilon d_{2i}\wedge d_{2j})
and
B_{ij}=\mathbb{R}(d_{2i-1}\wedge d_{2j}+\varepsilon d_{2j-1}\wedge d_{2i})
.

Proof.

Following our notations, for
r\in\mathfrak{g}
we have
\operatorname{ad}_{s_{\ell}}r=0
for all
\ell=1,\ldots,k_{0}
if and only if

c_{ij}=e_{ij}=g_{ij}=h_{ij}=0

and

\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda_{\ell j}&\phantom{-}\lambda_{\ell i}\\
\phantom{-}\lambda_{\ell i}&-\lambda_{\ell j}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}n_{ij}\\
n_{ji}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\phantom{-}\lambda_{\ell j}&-\lambda_{\ell i}\\
-\lambda_{\ell i}&\phantom{-}\lambda_{\ell j}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}m_{ij}\\
p_{ij}\end{pmatrix}.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is nondegenerate, there is
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k_{0}\}
such that
\lambda_{\ell i}^{2}-\lambda_{\ell j}^{2}\neq 0
, then

m_{ij}=p_{ij}=n_{ij}=n_{ji}=0.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is degenerate and for all
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k_{0}\}

\lambda_{\ell i}^{2}-\lambda_{\ell j}^{2}=0
then
\lambda_{\ell j}=\varepsilon_{ij}\lambda_{\ell i}
, (
\varepsilon_{ij}=\pm 1
) and

p_{ij}=\varepsilon_{ij}m_{ij},\ n_{ji}=\varepsilon_{ij}n_{ij}.

Let
\{s_{1}^{*},\ldots,s_{k_{0}}^{*},z_{1}^{*},\ldots,z_{\ell_{0}}^{*},d_{1}^{*},\ldots,d_{2m}^{*}\}
be the dual basis of the dual vector space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. We have

s_{i}^{*}\wedge d_{2\ell}^{*}\left(\operatorname{ad}_{d_{2\ell-1}}r\right)=0=z_{i}^{*}\wedge d_{2\ell}^{*}\left(\operatorname{ad}_{d_{2\ell-1}}r\right),

and we get
s_{i}^{*}\left(\Phi_{\ell}\right)=0
and
z_{i}^{*}\left(\Phi_{\ell}\right)=0
. But we have

\bigg(s_{i}^{*}\left(\Phi_{\ell}\right)\bigg)_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\ell\leq m\\
1\leq i\leq k_{0}\end{subarray}}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{11}&\lambda_{21}&\dots&\lambda_{k_{0}1}\\
\lambda_{12}&\lambda_{22}&\dots&\lambda_{k_{0}2}\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\lambda_{1m}&\lambda_{2m}&\dots&\lambda_{k_{0}m}\end{pmatrix}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&a_{12}&\cdots&a_{1k_{0}}\\
-a_{12}&0&\cdots&a_{2k_{0}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-a_{1k_{0}}&-a_{2k_{0}}&\cdots&0\end{array}\right).

By the injectivity of the characteristic matrix
\Lambda
we deduce that
a_{ij}\equiv 0
and also
b_{ij}\equiv 0
.
∎

5
Conclusion

Despite the simple appearance of flat Lie algebras, we have found that classifying all bialgebra structures on these algebras is a very technical and computational task. The degenerate case remains to be studied, but we hope that this classification will be very important, and that the remaining case can be deduced from the nondegenerate case, since degenerate flat Lie algebras are contractions of nondegenerate ones.

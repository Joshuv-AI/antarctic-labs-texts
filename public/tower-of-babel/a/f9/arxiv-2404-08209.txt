On the dependence of the affine Springer fibers on their root valuation datum
Zongbin Chen

Abstract
For the group GL d \mathrm{GL}_{d} , we confirm a conjecture of Goresky, Kottwitz and MacPherson, which states that the cohomology of the affine Springer fibers depend only on the root valuation datum of their defining elements. The proof relies on a microlocal analysis of the intermediate extensions appearing in Ngô’s support theorem, and a Whitney regularity property of the union of the strict δ \delta -strata over the equisingular strata for the miniversal deformation of plane curve singularities.

2.1
Laumon’s work

In an attempt to deform the affine Springer fibers, Laumon
[
L
]
discovered a remarkable relationship between the affine Springer fibers and the compactified Jacobian of an algebraic curve. By definition, the affine Springer fiber
\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}
parametrizes the lattices
L
in
F^{n}
such that
\gamma L\subset L
. Such lattices can be naturally identified with sub-
\mathcal{O}[\gamma]
-modules of finite type in
E:=F[\gamma]\cong F^{d}
. Moreover, such
\mathcal{O}[\gamma]
-modules can be globalized.
Let
C_{\gamma}
be an irreducible projective algebraic curve over
\mathbf{C}
, with two points

\infty

(i)

has unique singularity at
c

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{C_{\gamma},\,c}\cong\mathcal{O}[\gamma]
,

(ii)

is isomorphic to
\mathbf{P}^{1}
by an isomorphism sending

to
\infty\in C_{\gamma}
.

The curve

will be called the

of

.
Let

be a sub-
\mathcal{O}[\gamma]
-modules of finite type in

Similar to the obvious fact that

-module

of generic rank

Recall that the compactified Jacobian

parametrizes the isomorphism class of the torsion-free coherent
\mathcal{O}_{C_{\gamma}}
-modules of generic rank
1
and degree
0
.
Consider the functor
\overline{P}{}_{C_{\gamma}}^{\natural}
which associates to an affine
\mathbf{C}
-scheme
S
the groupoïd of couples
(M,\iota)
, where
M
is a torsion free coherent
\mathcal{O}_{C_{\gamma}\times S}
-module of generic rank

and degree
0
, and

is a trivialization of

. One verifies that

is representable by a
\mathbf{C}
-scheme. With the glueing construction, we get a natural morphism

\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}\to\overline{P}_{C_{\gamma}}^{\natural},

which sends a lattice

\gamma L\subset L
to the torsion-free
\mathcal{O}_{C_{\gamma}}
-module obtained by gluing

, together with the obvious trivialization. Forgetting the trivialization

, we get a morphism
\overline{P}_{C_{\gamma}}^{\natural}\to\overline{P}_{C_{\gamma}}

\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}\to\Lambda^{0}\backslash\mathcal{X}_{\gamma}^{0}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Laumon
[
L
]
)
.

The morphism

is finite radical and surjective, and so is the induced morphism

Unlike the affine Springer fiber or its quotients, the compactified Jacobian can be deformed naturally.
Let
\pi:(\mathcal{C},C_{\gamma})\to({\mathcal{B}},0)

.
Then

fits in the projective flat family of relative compactified Jacobians

The compactified Jacobians of curves with planar singularities have very nice properties, which we can exploit to understand the affine Springer fibers.

On the other hand, it is more difficult to deform

-stratum

Let

There exists a

2.2
Ngô’s support theorem

Consider the family
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}
and its relative compactified Jacobians

f:\overline{\mathcal{P}}\to{\mathcal{B}}.

According to Fantechi-Göttsche-van Straten
[
FGS
]
, the total space
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
is smooth over
\mathbf{C}
.
As the morphism
f:\overline{\mathcal{P}}\to{\mathcal{B}}

is projective, the complex
Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
is pure by Deligne’s Weil-II
[
D
]
. The decomposition theorem of Beilinson, Bernstein, Deligne and Gabber
[
BBDG
]
then implies that

Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{2\delta_{\gamma}}{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{n}(Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-n],

where
\delta_{\gamma}
is the
\delta
-invariant of
C_{\gamma}
, which is also the relative dimension of the flat morphism
f
.

Theorem 2.4

(Ngô’s support theorem
[
N
]
)
.

We have decomposition

Rf_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{2\delta_{\gamma}}j_{!*}(R^{n}f^{\mathrm{sm}}_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})[-n],

where
j:{\mathcal{B}}^{\rm sm}\to{\mathcal{B}}
is the natural inclusion. In particular,

H^{n}\big(\overline{P}_{C_{\gamma}},\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\big)=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\mathcal{H}^{n-i}\big(j_{!*}{\textstyle{\bigwedge^{i}}}R^{1}f^{\mathrm{sm}}_{*}\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}\big)_{0},\hskip 10.00002ptn=0,\cdots,2\delta_{\gamma}.

Remark 2.5
.

We refer the reader to our work
[
C
]
for a sketch of the proof. Actually, the theorem extends to the following situation: Let
B\to{\mathcal{B}}
be an embedding of a smooth algebraic variety over
\mathbf{C}
, suppose that
B
is transversal to all the
\delta
-strata
{\mathcal{B}}_{\delta}
, i.e. for any point
x\in B\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{\delta}
the tangent space
T_{x}B
is transversal to the tangent cone of
{\mathcal{B}}_{\delta}
at
x
. Then for the sub-family

, Ngô’s support theorem continues to hold. Indeed, by the local regularity criteria of Fantechi-Göttsche-van Straten
[
FGS
]
(to be recalled below as theorem
3.1
), the total space
\overline{\mathcal{P}}\times_{{\mathcal{B}}}B
is smooth over
\mathbf{C}
. Moreover, Severi’s inequality
\mathrm{Codim}(B_{\delta})\geqslant\delta
continues to hold by the transversality condition. We get then all the main ingredients in the proof of Ngô’s support theorem.

3
Microlocal analysis of the intermediate extensions in Ngô’s support theorem

We will exploit Ngô’s support theorem to understand the cohomology of
\overline{P}_{C_{\gamma}}

For simplicity we denote

The main ingredient for the analysis is the local regularity criteria of Fantechi-Göttsche-van Straten
[
FGS
]

Let

be an irreducible projective algebraic curve over

with

at its unique close point

Let

be the closed sub formal scheme of

which parametrizes the deformation of

with constant geometric genus. According to Diaz-Harris
[
DH

, the tangent cone of

can be naturally identified with the sub vector space

V(R_{c}):={\mathfrak{a}}_{c}/(f_{c},\partial_{x}f_{c},\partial_{y}f_{c})\subset T_{0}(\widehat{B}_{c})=\mathbf{C}[\![x,y]\!]/(f_{c},\partial_{x}f_{c},\partial_{y}f_{c}),

where

is the

of

.
Note that

is then of codimension
\delta(R_{c})
in

.
Let
\phi:(C,C_{0})\to(B,0)
be a deformation of

such that the restriction of

to the formal neighbourhood

of

Let

be the union of the linear subspaces of

which is the preimage of

under the natural surjection

for all

Theorem 3.1

(Fantechi-Göttsche-van Straten
[
FGS
]
)

Let
C_{T}\to T
be a relative curve with base

by the base change with a morphism

\overline{\mathrm{Pic}}{}^{0}_{C_{T}/T}
is smooth over
\mathbf{C}
if and only if the image of the tangent space of

Calculation of some vanishing cycles

By the general theory of stratification (cf.

Under the above settings, we have

\delta

Let
x^{\prime}

at

T_{x^{\prime}}({\mathcal{B}})\twoheadrightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I_{x^{\prime}}}\mathrm{Def}_{R_{i}}^{\rm top}(k[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}).

(3.1)

Let
\tau_{i}
be the Tjurina number of the local singularity at

, and
\delta_{i}
the
\delta
-invariant. It is known that the tangent space

and that

Let

be the fiber of
p
passing through

is contained in the
\delta
-stratum with

(3.2)

where the second inequality is due to the semicontinuity of the
\delta
-invariant (
[
T2
]

\dim(H_{x^{\prime}})=\tau_{\gamma}-(d_{\beta}+1)\geqslant\sum_{i\in I_{x^{\prime}}}\delta_{i}-1.

(3.3)

(1)
In case that
{\mathcal{B}}_{\beta}
is not one of the strict
\delta
-strata, the first inequality in the equation (

in

for all

With the decomposition of proposition

\delta

with a unique ordinary double point as singularity, let

.
We use the iterated fibrations
p_{1},\cdots,p_{\delta_{\gamma}+1}
to calculate the cohomology

\mathcal{H}^{*}(j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})_{0}=H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big).

(3.9)

Note that the first fibration
p_{1}
is just the projection
p
in the previous section. By proposition
3.6
, we have

H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)=H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap p_{1}^{-1}(t_{1}),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big).

With the fibration
p_{2}:{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap p_{1}^{-1}(t_{1})\to\mathbf{C}^{1}
, the above cohomological group can be built up from

H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap p_{2}^{-1}(t_{2}),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)

and the vanishing cycles of the complex
j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}
with respect to
p_{2}
. By construction, the vanishing cycles are supported at the finite subscheme
\pi_{1}^{-1}(t_{1})\cap P_{0}({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon),H_{2})
. This process can be iterated with the fibrations
p_{3}
,
p_{4}
and so on until
p_{\delta_{\gamma}+1}
.

We can simplify the calculations with stratified Morse theory. Indeed, the ramification of
p_{k+1}:{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{k}^{-1}(t_{k})\to\mathbf{C}^{1}
can be very complicated. We replace the projection
p_{k+1}
by a stratified Morse function

\varphi_{k+1}:{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{k}^{-1}(t_{k})\to\mathbf{R}^{1}

with respect to the stratification of
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{k}^{-1}(t_{k})
induced from the canonical Whitney stratification
\{{\mathcal{B}}_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathfrak{B}}}
, which approximates sufficiently well to the function
{\rm Re}(p_{k+1})
. Let
v_{k}
be the smallest critical value of
\varphi_{k+1}
, then

H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{k}^{-1}(t_{k})\cap\varphi_{k+1}^{-1}(-\infty,v_{k}),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)=H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap p_{k+1}^{-1}(t_{k+1}),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big),

and the cohomology group
H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{k}^{-1}(t_{k}),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)
can be built up from it and the Morse groups at the critical points of
\varphi_{k+1}
. For simplicity, we denote
F^{k}={\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{k}^{-1}(t_{k})
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Under the above setting, let
x\in F^{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{\beta}
be a critical point of
\varphi_{k+1}
with critical value
v
, let
\lambda
be the Morse index of the restriction of
\varphi_{k+1}
to
F^{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{\beta}
.
Let
a
be a sufficiently small positive real number.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{B}}_{\beta}
is not one of the strict
\delta
-strata, then

H^{*}\big(F^{k}_{>v+a},F^{k}_{<v-a};j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }i.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{B}}_{\beta}
is one of the strict
\delta
-strata
{\mathcal{B}}_{\delta}^{\circ}
, then for all
i
we have

H^{q}\big(F^{k}_{>v+a},F^{k}_{<v-a};j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{x}^{{i-\delta}},&\text{ if }q=\lambda+\delta,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

According to Goresky and MacPherson
[
GM
]
, part II, 6.4 and the appendix 6.A, to calculate the Morse group, we take a transversal slice
N
to
F^{k}\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{\beta}
in
F_{k}
and a generic projection
p:N\to\mathbf{C}^{1}
sending
x
to
0
, then the Morse group in question is equal to the vanishing cycle

\big(R^{d_{N}-1}\Phi_{\bar{\eta}_{0}}j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)_{x},

(3.10)

where
R\Phi_{\bar{\eta}_{0}}
is the vanishing cycle for the projection
p
at the point
0
and
d_{N}
is the dimension of
N
.
Note that the projection
p_{k}
and the point
t_{k}
have been chosen generic, hence the fiber
F^{k}
intersects transversally with the discriminant
\Delta
.
Consequently, for any point
x^{\prime}\in F^{k}\cap\Delta
, the restriction of
\pi:\mathcal{C}\to{\mathcal{B}}
to the formal neighbourhood of
x^{\prime}
in
F^{k}
defines a versal deformation of the singularities of
\mathcal{C}_{x^{\prime}}
by the openness of versality.
As explained in remark
3.4
, this implies that the restriction of the complex
j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}
to the transversal slice
N
depends only on the singularities of
\mathcal{C}_{x}
, and it is essentially the same as the restriction of
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}
to the transversal slice
V
in theorem
3.3
, as both can be reduced to transversal slices in the miniversal deformations of the singularities of
\mathcal{C}_{x}
. In particular, the vanishing cycle (
3.10
) equals that of theorem

and the assertion follows from the theorem.

∎

Remark 3.9
.

In conclusion, the cohomology groups

H^{*}(F^{k},j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})=H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{k}^{-1}(t_{k}),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big),\hskip 10.00002ptk=1,\cdots,\delta_{\gamma}+1,

can be calculated inductively, starting from
H^{*}(F^{\delta_{\gamma}+1},j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})
and arriving at

H^{*}(F^{1},j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})=H^{*}\big({\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}(t_{1}),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)=\mathcal{H}^{*}(j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})_{0}.

With stratified Morse theory, the cohomology
H^{*}(F^{k},j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})
can be built up from

H^{*}\big(F^{k}\cap\varphi_{k+1}^{-1}(-\infty,v_{k}),j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)=H^{*}(F^{k+1},j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})

and the Morse groups
H^{*}\big(F^{k}_{>v+a},F^{k}_{<v-a};j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)
at the critical values
v
via the long exact sequence

\cdots\to H^{*}\big(F^{k}_{<v-a},j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)\to H^{*}\big(F^{k}_{>v+a},j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)\to H^{*}\big(F^{k}_{>v+a},F^{k}_{<v-a};j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)\to\cdots.

To actually carry out this process, we still need to know how to attach

H^{q}\big(F^{k}_{>v+a},F^{k}_{<v-a};j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big)\to H^{q+1}\big(F^{k}_{<v-a},j^{\prime}_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}\big),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }q,

which seems to be out of reach now.
Nonetheless, the above proposition tells us that the Morse groups are non-trivial only over the strict
\delta
-strata, which is enough for our purpose.

4
Whitney regularity along the equisingular strata

Based on a study of the geometry of the discriminant
\Delta
, we will show that the union of the strict
\delta
-strata is Whitney regular over the equisingular strata. In conjunction with the microlocal analysis of the complexes
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i}
, this implies that the sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{*}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})
are locally constant over the equisingular strata.

4.1
The geometry of the discriminant

Teissier
[
T1
]
has discovered a very remarkable property of the discriminant of the miniversal deformation of isolated hypersurface singularities, which is further extended to the isolated complete intersection singularities by Looijenga
[
Loo
]
. We make a recount of their result.

Let
(X_{0},0)\subset(\mathbf{C}^{n},0)
be a germ of isolated complete intersection singularity.
Let
\mathrm{Def}_{(X_{0},0)\hookrightarrow(\mathbf{C}^{n},0)}
be the deformation functor for the embedding
(X_{0},0)\hookrightarrow(\mathbf{C}^{n},0)
, and let
\mathrm{Def}_{(X_{0},0)}
be the deformation functor for the germ
(X_{0},0)
.
Let
T^{1}_{(X_{0},0)\hookrightarrow(\mathbf{C}^{n},0)}
and
T_{(X_{0},0)}^{1}
be their tangent spaces, i.e.

T^{1}_{(X_{0},0)\hookrightarrow(\mathbf{C}^{n},0)}=\mathrm{Def}_{(X_{0},0)\hookrightarrow(\mathbf{C}^{n},0)}(\mathbf{C}[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{2}),\hskip 10.00002ptT_{(X_{0},0)}^{1}=\mathrm{Def}_{(X_{0},0)}(\mathbf{C}[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^{2}).

Let
I_{0}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n},0}
be the defining ideal of
X_{0}
, then its conormal sheaf
I_{0}/I_{0}^{2}
is locally free, and we get an exact sequence

0\to I_{0}/I_{0}^{2}\to\Omega_{\mathbf{C}^{n}}^{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n}}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}\to\Omega_{X_{0}}^{1}\to 0.

(4.1)

Let
\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}/\mathbf{C}^{n}}:=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}}(I_{0}/I_{0}^{2},\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}})
, we call it the normal sheaf of
X_{0}
.
According to the general deformation theory (cf.
[
GLS
]
), we can identify the tangent space

T^{1}_{(X_{0},0)\hookrightarrow(\mathbf{C}^{n},0)}=\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}/\mathbf{C}^{n},0},

and the tangent space
T^{1}_{(X_{0},0)}
fits in the exact sequence

0\to\Theta_{X_{0},0}\to\Theta_{\mathbf{C}^{n},0}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n},0}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{0},0}\to\mathcal{N}_{X_{0}/\mathbf{C}^{n},0}\to T^{1}_{(X_{0},0)}\to 0,

(4.2)

which is obtained from the exact sequence (
4.1
) by taking dual. Note that this leads to the isomorphism

T^{1}_{(X_{0},0)}\cong\mathcal{E}xt_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{0},0}}^{1}(\Omega_{X_{0},0}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{0},0}).

Let
f=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{k})
be a minimal set of generators for the defining ideal
I_{0}
of
X_{0}
, let
Df
be the Jacobian matrix

Df=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{bmatrix}.

With the exact sequence (
4.2
), we calculate

T_{(X_{0},0)}^{1}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n},0}^{k}/(Df\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n},0}^{n}+I_{0}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n},0}^{k}).

The
\mathcal{O}_{X_{0},0}
-module at the right hand side is traditionally called
Tjurina module
.
Let
g_{1},\cdots,g_{\tau}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n},0}^{k}
,
g_{i}=(g_{i,1},\cdots,g_{i,k})^{t}
, be a set of representatives for a basis of the above quotient as a vector space over
\mathbf{C}
.
Let

F_{i}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{t})=f_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})+\sum_{j=1}^{\tau}t_{j}g_{i,j}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),\hskip 10.00002pti=1,\cdots,k.

Let
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{\tau}}
be the ideal generated by
F_{1},\cdots,F_{k}
, and let
X
be the closed subscheme of
\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{\tau}
defined by
I
. With the second projection
\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{\tau}\to\mathbf{C}^{\tau}
, we get a family

\psi:(X,0)\to(S=\mathbf{C}^{\tau},0),

which is a miniversal deformation of
X_{0}
. One verifies that
X
is nonsingular.
Let
\Sigma\subset X
be the
critical locus
of the morphism
\psi
, i.e. it is the closed subscheme defined by the Fitting ideal
{\rm F}_{n-k}(\Omega^{1}_{X/S})
. Then all the geometric fibers of
\psi
have isolated complete intersection singularities at its intersection with
\Sigma
.
In concrete terms,
\Sigma
is the closed subscheme of
X
defined by the condition that the Jacobian matrix

\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial F_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{bmatrix}

is of rank
<k
, i.e. all the minors of the Jacobian matrix of size
k\times k
vanish.

At each geometric point
x\in\Sigma
, let
s=\psi(x)
, we get the Tjurina module
T^{1}_{(X_{s},x)}
. They can be put in family as
x
varies. We start from the exact sequence

0\to\psi^{*}\Omega_{S}^{1}\to\Omega_{X}^{1}\to\Omega_{X/S}^{1}\to 0.

Take its dual, we get

\cdots\to\Theta_{X}\to\psi^{*}\Theta_{S}\to\mathcal{E}xt_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{1}(\Omega_{X/S}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{X})\to 0.

(4.3)

Let
\mathcal{T}_{X/S}^{1}=\mathcal{E}xt_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{1}(\Omega_{X/S}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{X})
, then

\mathcal{T}_{X/S}^{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{s},x}=\mathcal{E}xt_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{s},x}}^{1}(\Omega_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{s},x}}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{s},x}})=T^{1}_{(X_{s},x)}.

(4.4)

Let
F=(F_{1},\cdots,F_{k})^{t}\in\mathcal{O}^{k}_{\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{\tau},0}
, and let
DF
be its Jacobian matrix with respect to the variables
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
. The exact sequence (
4.2
) can be put in families as well, and from it we calculates

\mathcal{T}_{X/S}^{1}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{\tau},0}^{k}/(DF\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{\tau},0}^{n}+I\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{C}^{n}\times\mathbf{C}^{\tau},0}^{k}).

The
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module at the right hand side is called the
relative Tjurina module
of the family
\psi:X\to S
.

Actually the above construction of
\mathcal{T}_{X/S}^{1}
works for any deformation
\psi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}
of
X_{0}
. From the analogue of the exact sequence (
4.3
), for any geometric point
x\in X^{\prime}
with image
s\in S^{\prime}
, we deduce a morphism

\Theta_{S^{\prime},s}\xrightarrow{\psi^{-1}}(\psi^{*}\Theta_{S^{\prime}})_{x}\to\mathcal{T}^{1}_{X^{\prime}/S^{\prime},x}.

Modulo the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}_{S^{\prime},s}
of
\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime},s}
, we get the
Kodaira-Spencer
map

\kappa_{x}:T_{s}S^{\prime}\to T_{(X^{\prime}_{s},x)}^{1}

It is known that
\kappa_{x}
is surjective if
\psi^{\prime}
is versal and isomorphic if
\psi^{\prime}
is miniversal (cf.
[
GLS
]
).

Let
\Delta\subset S
be the image of
\Sigma
, called the
discriminant
of the morphism
\psi
, it is the closed subscheme defined by the Fitting ideal
{\rm F}_{0}(\psi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma})
.

Theorem 4.1

(Purity of the discriminant, Teissier
[
T1
]
)
.

Let
\psi:(X,0)\to(S,0)
be a miniversal deformation of an isolated complete intersection singularity as above. Then
\Delta\subset(S,0)
is a hypersurface which is locally analytically irreducible at
0
, and the induced morphism
\Sigma\to\Delta
is the normalization map.

Proof.

We give a sketch of the proof.
From the above concrete description of
\psi
, one deduce that
X
is regular and it is of dimension
n-k+\tau
, and that the restriction
\psi:\Sigma\to\Delta
is a finite morphism. In particular,
\dim(\Sigma)=\dim(\Delta)
. As the generic fiber of
\psi
is smooth, we obtain that
\dim(\Delta)\leqslant\tau-1
.
On the other hand, for any geometric point
x\in X
, take a local coordinate of
X
at
x
, the tangent map induces a morphism
{\rm d}\psi_{x}:(X,x)\to{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{C}^{n-k+\tau},\mathbf{C}^{\tau})
.
For
m,d\in\mathbf{N}
and
r=0,\cdots,\min(m,d)
, let

\mathcal{O}_{r}(m,d)=\big\{A\in{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{C}^{m},\mathbf{C}^{d})\mid\mathrm{rk}(A)=r\big\}.

Consider the action of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}\times\mathrm{GL}_{d}
on
{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{C}^{m},\mathbf{C}^{d})
, one verifies that
\mathcal{O}_{r}(m,d)
is a smooth irreducible affine algebraic variety of codimension
(m-r)(d-r)
in
{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{C}^{m},\mathbf{C}^{d})
, and that the Zariski closure satisfies
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{r}(m,d)=\bigsqcup_{r^{\prime}=0}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{r^{\prime}}(m,d)
.
Then the germ
(\Sigma,x)
is the inverse image of
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\tau-1}(n-k+\tau,\tau)
under the morphism
{\rm d}\psi_{x}
.
Hence the codimension of
(\Sigma,x)
in
(X,x)
is at most
n-k+1
, i.e.
\dim(\Sigma)\geqslant\tau-1
. Combining the two inequalities, we obtain that both
\Sigma
and
\Delta
are of pure dimension
\tau-1
. In particular,
\Delta
is a hypersurface in
(\mathbf{C}^{\tau},0)
.

On verifies then that
\Sigma
is Cohen-MaCaulay.
With the concrete description of
\psi
, one verifies that
{\rm F}_{n-k}(\Omega_{X/S}^{1})={\rm F}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{X/S}^{1})
.
As
X
is regular and
\Sigma
is of pure codimension
n-k+1
, we have

{\rm depth}\big({\rm F}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{X/S,x}^{1}),\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\big)=n-k+1.

(4.5)

By definition,
\mathcal{T}_{X/S}^{1}
has presentation

\mathcal{O}_{X}^{n}\xrightarrow{DF}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{k}\to\mathcal{T}_{X/S}^{1}\to 0.

According to Buchsbaum-Rim
[
BR
]
, Corollary 2.7, the equality (
4.5
) implies that the homological dimension
{\rm hd}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma,x})
is
n-k+1
. As
X
is regular, we have the equality of Auslander-Buchsbaum

{\rm hd}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma,x})+{\rm depth}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma,x})=\dim(X),

whence
{\rm depth}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,x}}(\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma,x})=\tau-1=\dim(\Sigma)
and so
\Sigma
is Cohen-MaCaulay.

By Serre’s criteria of normality, we need to verify that the singular locus of
\Sigma
is of codimension at least
2
. For this we introduce the
Thom strata
: Let
\Theta_{X}
and
\Theta_{S}
be the sheaves of holomorphic vector fields on
X
and
S
respectively, the pull-back and the tangent map induces the morphisms of sheaves

\psi^{-1}:\Theta_{S}\to\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptt\psi:\Theta_{X}\to\psi^{*}\Theta_{S}.

Lemma 4.2
.

The miniversal deformation
\psi
is
infinitesimally stable
, i.e. for any point
x\in X
with image
s
, we have

\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S,s})=\psi^{-1}(\Theta_{S,s})+t\psi(\Theta_{X,x}).

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{m}}_{x}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
be the maximal ideal at
x
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}\subset\mathcal{O}_{S,s}
the maximal ideal at
s
. We identify
{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}
with its image in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
via the morphism
\mathcal{O}_{S,s}\to\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
.
We claim that the assertion is equivalent to

\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S,s})=\psi^{-1}(\Theta_{S,s})+t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})+{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S,s}).

(4.6)

Indeed, the above equation is equivalent to

\frac{\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S,s})}{t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}=\frac{\psi^{-1}(\Theta_{S,s})+t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}{t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}+\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S,s})+t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}{t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}.

Let
M=\frac{\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S,s})}{t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}
, the equation can be rewritten as

M=\frac{\psi^{-1}(\Theta_{S,s})+t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}{t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}+{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}M.

(4.7)

One verifies that
M
is an
\mathcal{O}_{S,s}
-module of finite type. By Nakayama’s lemma, the above equation is equivalent to

M=\frac{\psi^{-1}(\Theta_{S,s})+t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}{t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})},

which is exactly the equation in the lemma.

For the equation (
4.6
) or equivalently (
4.7
), it is enough to verify the surjectivity of

T_{s}S\xrightarrow{\psi^{-1}}M/{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}M.

(4.8)

Note that

M/{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}M=\frac{\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S,s})}{{\mathfrak{m}}_{y}\psi^{*}(\Theta_{S,s})+t\psi(\Theta_{X,x})}=T_{(X_{s},x)}^{1},

and that the morphism (
4.8
) is just the Kodaira-Spencer map, here we have used the exact sequence (
4.3
) and the isomorphism (
4.4
). Now the surjectivity of (
4.8
) follows from the versality of
\psi
.

∎

According to Mather
[
M1
]
, the morphism
\psi
is
stable
, i.e. it is analytically equivalent to any sufficiently small perturbation of it. This implies that the tangent map
{\rm d}\psi_{x}:(X,x)\to{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{C}^{n-k+\tau},\mathbf{C}^{\tau})
is transversal to the stratification
{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{C}^{n-k+\tau},\mathbf{C}^{\tau})=\bigsqcup_{r=0}^{\tau}\mathcal{O}_{r}(n-k+\tau,\tau)
.
Consequently, for
r=0,\cdots,\tau-1
, the inverse image

\Sigma_{r,x}=({\rm d}\psi_{x})^{-1}\big(\mathcal{O}_{r}(n-k+\tau,\tau)\big)

is smooth.
\Sigma_{r,x}
is clearly independent of the local coordinate of
X
at
x
, hence when
x
runs through points on
X
they glue together to a smooth locally closed subscheme
\Sigma_{r}
of
X
, called
Thom strata
.
It is clear that
\Sigma
equals the Zariski closure of
\Sigma_{\tau-1}
. As
\Sigma_{\tau-1}
is itself smooth, the singularity of
\Sigma
is contained in the closure of
\Sigma_{\tau-2}
, which is of codimension
2(n-k+2)-(n-k+1)=n-k+3\geqslant 3
in
\Sigma
. This finishes the proof that
\Sigma
is normal.

As the fibers of
\psi
have at worst isolated complete intersection singularities and the singularities can be deformed independently, the fibers of
\psi
over the generic point of each local irreducible component of
(\Delta,0)
can have only a unique singularity.
Moreover, for any point
x
of
\Sigma_{\tau-1}
, one verifies by the openness of versality that the singularity
(X_{\psi(x)},x)
must be ordinary quadratic.
With the deformation theory of ordinary quadratic singularity, the restriction of
\psi
to
\Sigma_{\tau-1}
must be analytically locally an isomorphism.
Hence
\psi
is generically an isomorphism over each local irreducible component of
(\Delta,0)
.
The normality of
\Sigma
implies that it is locally analytically irreducible, this implies that
\Delta
is locally analytically irreducible at
0
as well.
As
\psi
is finite generically an isomorphism and
\Sigma
is normal,
\psi
must be the normalization.

∎

The Thom stratification inspires the following construction:
For
m,d\in\mathbf{N}
with
m>d
, let

\mathcal{N}_{m,d}=\big\{A\in{\rm Hom}(\mathbf{C}^{m},\mathbf{C}^{d})\mid\mathrm{rk}(A)<d\big\},

which is also the Zariski closure of
{\mathcal{O}_{d-1}(m,d)}
. It admits a resolution called
Springer resolution

4.2
Whitney regularity

Whitney stratification is the most widely accepted notion of “equisingularity”. In this section, we make a brief recall of Thom-Mather’s theory of Whitney stratification and Teissier’s numerical criteria for the Whitney regularity condition.
They are used to show the Whitney regularity of the union of the strict
\delta
-strata over the equisingular strata. We then use this result to show that the sheaves
\mathcal{H}^{*}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}^{i})
are locally constant over the equisingular strata.

(1) Whitney stratification

Let
X
be a reduced complex analytic variety, a
stratification
of
X
is a decomposition into disjoint union
X=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in A}X_{\alpha}
of locally closed smooth complex analytic subvarieties, called
strata
, satisfying the
frontier condition
:

X_{\alpha}\cap\overline{X_{\beta}}\neq\emptyset\iff X_{\alpha}\subset\overline{X_{\beta}},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,\alpha,\beta\in A.

The stratification is said to be
locally finite
if the number of strata is locally finite.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
X
be a reduced locally closed subvariety of a smooth complex analytic variety
M
, let
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
be a stratification of
X
. A pair of strata
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
with
X_{\alpha}\subset\overline{X_{\beta}}
is said to satisfy Whitney’s condition at
y\in X_{\alpha}
if

(a)

For any sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbf{N}}\subset X_{\beta}
tending to
y
, we have

T_{y}X_{\alpha}\subset\lim_{i\to\infty}T_{x_{i}}X_{\beta}.

(b)

For any sequences
\{y_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbf{N}}\subset X_{\alpha},\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbf{N}}\subset X_{\beta}
tending to
y
, we have

\lim_{i\to\infty}\overline{x_{i}y_{i}}\subset\lim_{i\to\infty}T_{x_{i}}X_{\beta},

where
\overline{x_{i}y_{i}}
is the secant line passing through
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
for some local coordinate system of
M
.

In case that the conditions are satisfied for all points of
X_{\alpha}
, we say that
X_{\beta}
is
Whitney regular
over
X_{\alpha}
.
If the Whitney conditions are satisfied for any pair of strata
(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta})
with
X_{\alpha}\subset\overline{X_{\beta}}
, we call the stratification
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
a

.

The above conditions will be called Whitney’s condition
(a)
and
(b)
. It is known that the condition
(b)
implies condition

.

Lemma 4.18

We will show that the root valuation datum of
\gamma
determines such datum.

Up to conjugation, we can write
\gamma=\mathrm{diag}(\gamma_{i})_{i=1}^{r}
with
\gamma_{i}\in\mathfrak{gl}_{d_{i}}(\mathcal{O})
such that the characteristic polynomial of
\gamma_{i}
is irreducible over

For each

for some

.

In case that

\begin{cases}x=t^{d_{i}},&\\
y=\sum_{n=n_{i}}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}.\end{cases}

(4.15)

\beta_{0}=1

(\beta_{0})=(1)
. If

(5.2)

We deduce from it a long exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathcal{H}^{m}(i_{x}^{*}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])\to R^{m}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\xrightarrow{\rm can}R^{m}\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\to\mathcal{H}^{m+1}(i_{x}^{*}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])\to\cdots.

(5.3)

The vanishing result (
5.1
), together with the support condition
\mathcal{H}^{m}(i_{x}^{*}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])=0
for
m\geqslant 0
, implies that

\mathcal{H}^{m}((j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x})=\begin{cases}{\rm Ker}\big\{R^{-1}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\xrightarrow{\rm can}R^{-1}\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\big\},&\text{ for }m=-1,\\
R^{m}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x},&\text{ for }-d\leqslant m\leqslant-2,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Take the Poincaré-Verdier dual of the distinguished triangle (
5.2
), we get another distinguished triangle

{i}_{X_{0}}^{!}[1]\to R\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{var}}R\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}\xrightarrow{+1}.

(5.4)

For
K=j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d]
, similar vanishing results as above imply that

0\to R^{-1}\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\xrightarrow{\rm var}i_{x}^{!}R^{-1}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])\to\mathcal{H}^{1}(i_{x}^{!}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])\to 0.

(5.5)

Note that we have factorization
T-{\rm Id}=\mathrm{var}\circ\mathrm{can}
. Moreover, as the vanishing cycle is supported only at
x
, the factorization can be refined to

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{x,*}i_{x}^{*}R\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{x,{*}}i_{x}^{!}R\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(K).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T-{\rm Id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{can}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{var}}

(5.6)

With the injectivity at the left of the equation (
5.5
), we get

{\rm Ker}\big\{R^{-1}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\xrightarrow{\rm can}R^{-1}\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\big\}={\rm Ker}\big\{i_{x}^{*}R^{-1}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])\xrightarrow{T-{\rm Id}}i_{x}^{!}R^{-1}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])\big\}.

This finishes the proof of the first assertion. The second assertion follows by Poincaré duality.

For the third assertion, the vanishing result (
5.1
) states that

R^{m}\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }m\neq-1.

The long exact sequence (
5.3
) and the property of
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d]
imply the surjection

R^{-1}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\xrightarrow{\rm can}R^{-1}\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}\to 0.

The assertion about
R^{-1}\Phi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F}[d])_{x}
then follows from it and the factorization (
5.6
), together with the injectivity at the left of the equation (
5.5
).

∎

(2) Iterated fibrations of the nearby fiber

We proceed to analyze the nearby cycle in theorem
5.1
. Topologically, there exist sufficiently small positive real numbers
\epsilon_{0}
and
\delta_{0}
, such that for any
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
and
0<\delta<\delta_{0}
we have

\displaystyle i_{x}^{*}R^{m}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F})=H^{m}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap p^{-1}(t),j_{!*}\mathcal{F}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,0\neq t\in D_{0}(\delta),

(5.7)

\displaystyle i_{x}^{!}R^{n}\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}(j_{!*}\mathcal{F})=H_{c}^{n}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap p^{-1}(t),j_{!*}\mathcal{F}),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,0\neq t\in D_{0}(\delta),

(5.8)

where
B_{0}(\epsilon)
is the open ball of radius
\epsilon
with center
0
in
\mathbf{C}^{N}
and
D_{0}(\delta)
is the open disk of radius
\delta
with center
0
in
\mathbf{C}
.
The cohomologies at the right hand side of both (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) can be calculated by iterated fiberations.
Note that the restriction of
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
to the nearby fiber
X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap p^{-1}(t)
remains an intersection complex, as
p^{-1}(t)
intersects transversally with the Whitney stratification
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathfrak{A}}}
inside
X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)
.

As our analysis concerns the restriction of
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
to
X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)
, there is no loss of generality to assume that the cardinal of the strata
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathfrak{A}}}
is finite.
Applying proposition
4.11
and
4.14
to the Zariski closure of each stratum
X_{\alpha}
, as they are of finite cardinal, we obtain the existence of a dense open subscheme
\Omega_{\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathfrak{A}}}}
of the partial flag variety parametrizing the sequences of linear subspaces

\{0\}\subset H_{d}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq H_{2}\subsetneq H_{1}\subsetneq\mathbf{C}^{N},\hskip 10.00002pt\dim(H_{i})=N-i\text{ for all }1\leqslant i\leqslant d,

for which the conclusions of both propositions hold for all
\overline{X_{\alpha}},\alpha\in{\mathfrak{A}}
.
Let
\pi_{i}:\mathbf{C}^{N}\to\mathbf{C}^{i}
be the projection with kernel
H_{i}
.
Then the critical locus of the restriction of
\pi_{i}
to the germ
(X,x)
is the local polar variety
P_{0}(X,H_{i})
, which is either empty or reduced of pure dimension
i-1
, and the restriction of
\pi_{i}
to it is finite.
Let
t_{i}\in\mathbf{C}^{i}
be a point in general position and sufficiently close to
0
, then
\pi_{i+1}:\mathbf{C}^{N}\to\mathbf{C}^{i+1}
induces a projection

p_{i+1}:X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})\to\mathbf{C}^{1},

and the critical locus of
p_{i+1}
is the finite subscheme
\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})\cap P_{0}(X,H_{i+1})
.
As
P_{0}(X,H_{i+1})
is a proper closed subscheme of
X
, we obtain that the critical points of
p_{i+1}
doesn’t lie on the boundary
X\cap\partial B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})
.
Similar results hold for
\overline{X_{\alpha}}
in place of
X
.

Let
p_{1}:X\to\mathbf{C}^{1}
be the restriction of
\pi_{1}
to
X
, it is clear that theorem
5.1
holds for it and we get the equations (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) for
p=p_{1}
and
t=t_{1}
.
The projection
p_{2}:X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}(t_{1})\to\mathbf{C}^{1}
makes the first fiberation of the nearby fiber.
It has critical points at the intersection
\pi_{1}^{-1}(t_{1})\cap P_{0}(X,H_{2})
, away from the boundary, hence both
Rp_{2,*}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
and
Rp_{2,!}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
are locally constant away from the images of the critical points. The fiber of
Rp_{2,*}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
over a generic point of
{\rm Im}(p_{2})
can be identified with

H^{*}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(t_{2}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F})

for some point
t_{2}
in general position and sufficiently closed to
0
,
and
H^{*}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}(t_{1}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F})
can be built up from it and the vanishing cycles of the sheaf
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
for the morphism
p_{2}
at the critical points
\pi_{1}^{-1}(t_{1})\cap P_{0}(X,H_{2})
.
Similar results hold for
H_{c}^{*}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{1}^{-1}(t_{1}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F})
.

To calculate
H^{*}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(t_{2}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F})
and
H_{c}^{*}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(t_{2}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F})
, we make the second fiberation
p_{3}:X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(t_{2})\to\mathbf{C}^{1}
.
Again, it has critical points at the intersection
\pi_{2}^{-1}(t_{2})\cap P_{0}(X,H_{3})
, away from the boundary, hence both
Rp_{3,*}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
and
Rp_{3,!}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
are locally constant away from the images of the critical points. The fiber of
Rp_{3,*}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
over a generic point of
{\rm Im}(p_{3})
can be identified with
H^{*}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{3}^{-1}(t_{3}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F})
for some point
t_{3}
in general position and sufficiently closed to
0
,
and
H^{*}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(t_{2}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F})
can be built up from it and the vanishing cycles of the sheaf
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
for the morphism
p_{3}
at the critical points
\pi_{2}^{-1}(t_{2})\cap P_{0}(X,H_{3})
. Similar results hold for
H_{c}^{*}(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(t_{2}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F})
.

This process can be iterated. At the end, we need to calculate the vanishing cycle of the sheaf
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
for the morphisms
p_{i+1}:X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})\to\mathbf{C}^{1}
at their critical points
\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})\cap P_{0}(X,H_{i+1})
, and these datum can be assembled to get both
i_{x}^{*}R\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
and
i_{x}^{!}R\Psi_{\bar{\eta}}j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
.
This can be facilitated with the stratified Morse theory of Goresky and MacPherson
[
GM
]
. Let
\varphi_{i+1}:X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})\to\mathbf{R}
be a stratified Morse function which approximates sufficiently well the function
{\rm Re}(p_{i+1})
.
Let
v_{i}\in\mathbf{R}
be its smallest critical value, then

H^{*}\big(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})\cap\varphi_{i+1}^{-1}(-\infty,v_{i}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F}\big)=H^{*}\big(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i+1}^{-1}(t_{i+1}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F}\big),

and
H^{*}\big(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F}\big)
is built up from it and the Morse groups of
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
for the function
\varphi_{i+1}
.
Similar results hold for
H_{c}^{*}\big(X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i}),j_{!*}\mathcal{F}\big)
.
Note that as both
\pi_{i}
and
t_{i}
are chosen generically,
\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})
intersects transversally with the Whitney stratification
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathfrak{A}}}
inside
X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)
, hence the restriction of
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
to
X\cap B_{0}(\epsilon)\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(t_{i})
remains an intersection complex.
This makes the calculation of the Morse groups of
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
for the function
\varphi_{i+1}
much more easier than that of the vanishing cycles of
j_{!*}\mathcal{F}
for the projection
p_{i+1}
(cf.
[
GM
]
, Part II, Chap. 6).

Note that the local polar varieties
P_{0}(X,H_{i})
plays a fundametal role in the above analysis, this may help understanding the condition
(1)
in theorem
4.13
.

Fast and Robust Phase Retrieval via Deep Expectation-Consistent Approximation
Saurav K. Shastri, Philip Schniter

Abstract
Accurately recovering images from phaseless measurements is a challenging and long-standing problem. In this work, we present “deepECpr,” which combines expectation-consistent (EC) approximation with deep denoising networks to surpass state-of-the-art phase-retrieval methods in both speed and accuracy. In addition to applying EC in a non-traditional manner, deepECpr includes a novel stochastic damping scheme that is inspired by recent diffusion methods. Like existing phase-retrieval methods based on plug-and-play priors, regularization by denoising, or diffusion, deepECpr iterates a denoising stage with a measurement-exploitation stage. But unlike existing methods, deepECpr requires far fewer denoiser calls. We compare deepECpr to the state-of-the-art prDeep (Metzler et al., 2018), Deep-ITA (Wang et al., 2020), DOLPH (Shoushtari et al., 2023) , and Diffusion Posterior Sampling (Chung et al., 2023) methods for noisy phase-retrieval of color, natural, and unnatural grayscale images on oversampled-Fourier and coded-diffraction-pattern measurements and find improvements in both PSNR and SSIM with significantly fewer denoiser calls.

II-B

Expectation-Consistent Approximation

We now consider statistical estimation of
\bm{x}\sim p_{\mathsf{x}}
from
\bm{y}
under the GLM (
2
).
When both
p_{\mathsf{x}}
and
p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}
are Gaussian, the maximum a posteriori (MAP) and minimum mean-squared error (MMSE) estimates of
\bm{x}
coincide and are analytically computable.
But Gaussian
p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}
is not useful for phase retrieval.
When both
p_{\mathsf{x}}
and
p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}
are log-concave, the posterior
p_{\mathsf{x}|\mathsf{y}}
is also log-concave and thus the MAP estimate of
\bm{x}
can be computed using standard convex-optimization algorithms
[
60
,
61
]
. Although the
p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}
in (
4
) is indeed log-concave, high-fidelity image priors
p_{\mathsf{x}}
are not.
Furthermore, the widespread use of PSNR as a recovery-performance metric suggests the use of MMSE estimation over MAP.

The expectation-consistent (EC) approximation framework
[
49
,
50
]
is a well established method to approximate the MMSE estimate of
\bm{x}
from
\bm{y}
, i.e., the conditional mean
\E\{\bm{x}|\bm{y}\}
.
The application of EC to the SLM (
3

2
) is sometimes referred to as vector AMP (VAMP)
[
62
]
and generalized VAMP
[
25
]
, respectively.
Rigorous analyses of VAMP have established its MSE optimality under high-dimensional rotationally invariant
\bm{A}
and various assumptions on
p_{\mathsf{x}}
and
p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}

[
51
,
63
,
64
,
65
]
.

In the above works, EC is employed to MMSE-estimate
\bm{x}
from
\bm{y}
using the prior
p_{\mathsf{x}}
and the likelihood
p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{x}}
from (
2
).
This approach, however, fails for many deterministic choices of
\bm{A}
,

such as the Fourier-based operators that arise in phase retrieval (see the discussion in
[
35
,
20
]
) and magnetic resonance imaging (see the discussion in
[
66
,
67
,
68
,
69
]
).
In this paper, we

instead employ EC to MMSE-estimate
\bm{z}
from
\bm{y}
, using the prior

and likelihood

.
Separately, we estimate
\bm{x}
from
\bm{z}=\bm{Ax}
.
Thus, when reviewing EC below, we do so in the context of estimating
\bm{z}
using

and

.
The more “traditional” application of EC (estimating
\bm{x}
using

To begin our review of EC, notice that one can write the true posterior distribution

\displaystyle p_{\mathsf{z}|\mathsf{y}}(\cdot|\bm{y})=\argmin_{q}D\big(q\|p_{\mathsf{z}|\mathsf{y}}(\cdot|\bm{y})\big)

(5)

\displaystyle=\argmin_{q}D\big(q\|p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}(\bm{y}|\cdot)\big)+D(q\|p_{\mathsf{z}})+H(q)

(6)

\displaystyle=\argmin_{q_{1}=q_{2}=q_{3}}\underbrace{D\big(q_{1}\|p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}(\bm{y}|\cdot)\big)+D(q_{2}\|p_{\mathsf{z}})+H(q_{3})}_{\displaystyle\triangleq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}J(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})}},

III-A

Stochastic Damping

The EC Alg.
1
is not guaranteed to converge for general
p_{\mathsf{z}}
and
p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}
.
One technique that helps promote convergence is “damping”
[
72
,
67
]
, which slows the updates in a way that preserves the EC fixed points.

Damping is also referred to as “under-relaxation” in the context of iterative numerical methods and computational fluid dynamics
[
73
]
.
It typically takes the form
\bm{\varphi}_{\mathsf{new}}\leftarrow\mu\bm{\varphi}_{\mathsf{raw}}+(1-\mu)\bm{\varphi}_{\mathsf{old}}
when iteratively updating
\bm{\varphi}
using the most recent computation
\bm{\varphi}_{\mathsf{raw}}
.
Here,
\mu\in(0,1]
is a user-selectable parameter, where lower values increase stability but slow convergence.

The damping technique from
[
67
]
considers the
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}
and
\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}
computed in lines
9
-
10
and
14
-
15
of Alg.
1
as “raw” quantities and damps them using the additional steps
(for
i\in\{1,2\}
)

\displaystyle\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\big(\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}\sqrt{\smash{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}_{\mathsf{raw}}}\vphantom{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}}}+(1-\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)})\sqrt{\smash{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}_{\mathsf{old}}}\vphantom{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}}}\big)^{2}

(15a)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}\bm{\bar{z}}_{\mathsf{raw}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}+(1-\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)})\bm{\bar{z}}_{\mathsf{old}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)},

(15b)

where
\overline{v}_{\mathsf{old}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}
and
\bm{\bar{z}}_{\mathsf{old}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}
denote the values of
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}
and
\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}
from the previous iteration and

\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(i)}\in(0,1]
is a fixed damping constant.
When
\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=1=\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
, the original EC is recovered.

We now propose a “stochastic damping” procedure inspired by diffusion methods like
[
74
]
.
It damps
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
as in (
15a
) but constructs
\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
by adding AWGN of a prescribed variance:

\displaystyle\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\big(\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\sqrt{\smash{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{\mathsf{raw}}}\vphantom{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}}+(1-\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})\sqrt{\smash{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{\mathsf{old}}}\vphantom{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}}}\big)^{2}

(16a)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\bm{\bar{z}}_{\mathsf{raw}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}+\bm{\epsilon},\quad\bm{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\max\{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}-\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{\mathsf{raw}},0\}}\bm{I}).

(16b)

To better understand (

\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\bm{z}_{\mathsf{true}}+\bm{e}

\bm{e}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\bm{I})

\bm{\bar{z}}_{\mathsf{raw}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\bm{z}_{\mathsf{true}}+\bm{\varepsilon}

\bm{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\overline{v}_{\mathsf{raw}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\bm{I})

This implies that a valid
\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
can be constructed from
\bm{\bar{z}}_{\mathsf{raw}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
by adding additional AWGN of variance
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}-\overline{v}_{\mathsf{raw}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
, as done in (

).

Here, it is expected that
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}>\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}_{\mathsf{raw}}
because the variances decrease over the iterations and damping aims to slow down that decrease.
In Sec.
IV
, we show that (
16
) has advantages over (
15
), perhaps because (
16
) attempts to enforce the EC model (
14
).

III-B

deepEC for general
\bm{A}

As formulated in Alg.
1
, EC estimates
\bm{z}\sim p_{\mathsf{z}}
from observations
\bm{y}\sim p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}(\cdot|\bm{z})
.
To solve inverse problems using the GLM model (
2
), we instead want to estimate
\bm{x}\sim p_{\mathsf{x}}
from
\bm{y}\sim p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}(\cdot|\bm{z})
where
\bm{z}=\bm{Ax}
.
In principle, this can be done using a minor modification of Alg.
1
that rewrites
\hat{\bm{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
in line
8
as a function of an MMSE estimate of
\bm{x}
:

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\E\{\bm{z}|q_{2}(\cdot;\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})\}

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\E\{\bm{z}|\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\bm{z}+\bm{e}\},~\bm{z}\sim p_{\mathsf{z}},~\bm{e}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\bm{I})

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{A}\underbrace{\E\{\bm{x}|\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\bm{Ax}+\bm{e}\},~\bm{x}\sim p_{\mathsf{x}},~\bm{e}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\bm{I})}_{\displaystyle\triangleq\hat{\bm{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}},\qquad

(19)

and then returns
\hat{\bm{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
as the EC output.
The practical challenge, however, is that computing
\hat{\bm{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
via (
19
) involves MMSE estimation of
\bm{x}
under the SLM (
3
), which is itself non-trivial.

Fortunately, it is now commonplace to train a deep networks to solve SLMs
[
75
]
.
Writing
\hat{\bm{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\approx\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)};\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})
for deep network
\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}
, we could train the network parameters
\bm{\theta}
via

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bm{\theta}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\E\big\{\|\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{Ax}_{t}+\bm{e};\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})\|^{2}\big\}

(20)

with training data
\{\bm{x}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
, random noise
\bm{e}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\bm{I})
, and random noise variance
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,v_{\max}]
for some
v_{\max}
.
Likewise, we could train another deep network
h_{\bm{\phi}}
to approximate the posterior variance
\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
.
Writing
\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\approx h_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)};\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})
, its network parameters
\bm{\phi}
might be trained via

\arg\min_{\bm{\phi}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\E\big\{\big|\tfrac{1}{m}\|\bm{z}_{t}-\bm{A}\hat{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\|^{2}-h_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{Ax}_{t}+\bm{e};\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})\big|\big\},

(21)

where
\hat{\bm{x}}_{t}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\triangleq\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{Ax}_{t}+\bm{e};\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})
and where
\bm{x}_{t}
,
\bm{e}
, and
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
are constructed as they were for (
20
).

We will refer to EC with these deep-network approximations as “deepEC.”
To summarize, deepEC is EC Algorithm
1
but with
\hat{\bm{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
computed via
\bm{Af}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)};\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})
and
\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
computed via
h_{\bm{\phi}}(\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)};\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})
.
As such, deepEC solves GLM problems by plugging deep SLM networks into EC Alg.
1

III-C

deepEC when
\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{A}=\bm{I}

One practical drawback to deepEC is that the deep networks
\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}
and
h_{\bm{\phi}}
are dependent on the forward operator
\bm{A}
, and so
the networks must be retrained for each new choice of
\bm{A}
.
There is, however, an important exception to this rule, which is when
\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{A}=\bm{I}
.
For such column-orthogonal
\bm{A}
, a sufficient statistic
[
76
]
for the estimation of
\hat{\bm{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
in (
19
) is

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\triangleq\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\bm{x}+\bm{\epsilon},~~\bm{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\bm{I}),

(22)

in which case
\hat{\bm{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
solves the MMSE denoising problem

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\E\{\bm{x}|\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\bm{x}+\bm{\epsilon}\},~\bm{x}\sim p_{\mathsf{x}},~\bm{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\bm{I}),

(23)

and we can approximate
\hat{\bm{x}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
using a deep
denoising
network
\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})
that is invariant to
\bm{A}
.
Likewise, we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{m}\tr(\Cov\{\bm{z}|\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\})

(24)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{m}\tr(\bm{A}\Cov\{\bm{x}|\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\}\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}})

(25)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{m}\tr(\bm{A}\Cov\{\bm{x}|\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\}\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}})

(26)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{m}\tr(\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{A}\Cov\{\bm{x}|\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\})

(27)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{m}\tr(\Cov\{\bm{x}|\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\}),

(28)

and so the network
h_{\bm{\phi}}
that deepEC uses to approximate
\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
can also be invariant to
\bm{A}
.
One way to construct
h_{\bm{\phi}}
would be to use the Monte-Carlo scheme from
[
77
]
, which employs random
\{\bm{p}_{c}\}_{c=1}^{C}
such that
\E\{\bm{p}_{c}\bm{p}_{c}^{\top}\}=\bm{I}
and small
\delta>0
in

\displaystyle\tr(\Cov\{\bm{x}|\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\frac{\bm{p}_{c}^{\top}[\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\!+\!\delta\bm{p}_{c},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})-\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})]}{\delta}

(29)

at the expense of
C\geq 1
additional calls of the denoiser
\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}
.

In summary, for any forward operator
\bm{A}
that obeys
\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{A}=\bm{I}
, the proposed deepEC scheme computes approximate-MMSE solutions to GLM problems by iteratively calling a deep denoiser, similar to the PnP
[
28
]
or RED
[
29
,
30
]
schemes.
But it requires far fewer iterations, as we demonstrate in Sec.
IV
.

For phase retrieval,
\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{A}=\bm{I}
holds for the two classes of
\bm{A}
that dominate the literature: i) oversampled-Fourier (OSF) and ii) coded diffraction pattern (CDP)
[
78
]
.
For OSF,

\displaystyle\bm{A}=\bm{F}_{m}\bm{O}\text{~~for~~}\bm{O}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\bm{I}_{d}\\
\bm{0}_{m-d\times d}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times d},

(30)

where
\bm{F}_{m}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{m\times m}
is a unitary 2D Fourier transform and
\bm{O}
pads the vectorized image with zeros.
For CDP,

\displaystyle\bm{A}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{F}_{d}\Diag(\bm{c}_{1})\\[-2.84526pt]
\vdots\\
\bm{F}_{d}\Diag(\bm{c}_{K})\end{bmatrix},

(31)

where
\bm{F}_{d}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d\times d}
is a unitary 2D Fourier transform and
\bm{c}_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
are random code vectors with entries drawn independently and uniformly from the unit circle in
{\mathbb{C}}
.
By inspection,
\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{A}=\bm{I}
under (

m\geq d
and (
31
) with
K\geq 1
.

III-D

deepEC for Phase Retrieval

For PR, we propose to apply the deepEC algorithm from Sec.
III-C
with the stochastic damping scheme from Sec.
III-A
.
In addition, we use the minor modifications described below.
The overall scheme, “deepECpr,” is detailed in Alg.
2
.

Initialization:

Unlike the non-informative
\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
initialization used in Alg.
1
, deepECpr uses

an informative initialization.
For OSF
\bm{A}
, we first run the HIO algorithm
[
13
]
to produce
\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathsf{init}}
, as in prDeep
[
20
]
and Deep-ITA
[
32
]
.
For CDP
\bm{A}
, we find that it suffices to run only the first iteration of HIO, and so
\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathsf{init}}=\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}(\bm{y}e^{j\angle{\bm{A}\bm{1}}})
, where
e^{j\angle{\bm{A}\bm{1}}}
is computed and applied element-wise.
Then, to initialize
\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
, deepECpr transforms
\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathsf{init}}

to the
\bm{z}
-domain and adds circular-complex AWGN:

\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\leftarrow\bm{A}\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathsf{init}}+\bm{n}\text{~with~}\bm{n}\sim\mathcal{CN}(\bm{0},\overline{v}_{\mathsf{init}}\bm{I}).

(32)

By using a large noise variance (nominally
\overline{v}_{\mathsf{init}}\approx 100^{2}
for pixel amplitudes in
[0,255]
) the structured error artifacts in
\bm{A}\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathsf{init}}
are suppressed.
Because the error variance of
\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
is larger than
\overline{v}_{\mathsf{init}}
due to the error in
\bm{A}\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathsf{init}}
, the initial
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
is set to
\zeta\overline{v}_{\mathsf{init}}
for some
\zeta>1
(nominally
\zeta=1.2
).

Real-valued
\bm{x}
and complex-valued
\bm{A}
:

The sufficient statistic
\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
in (
22
) would be appropriate if both
\bm{x}
and
\bm{A}
were real-valued, or if both were complex-valued.
However, for the PR problems that we consider,
\bm{x}
is real-valued and
\bm{A}
is complex-valued, and so we form the sufficient statistic as

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\triangleq\Re\{\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}\bm{\bar{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\}=\bm{x}+\bm{\epsilon},\quad\bm{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},0.5\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\bm{I}),

(33)

and call the deepECpr denoiser as
\hat{\bm{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\bm{A}\bm{f}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{\bar{r}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},0.5\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)})
.

Simplified variance-prediction:

As described in Sec.
III-B
, the deep variance predictor
h_{\bm{\phi}}
aims to estimate the error variance
\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
of the transformed denoiser output
\hat{\bm{z}}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
.
Although the Monte-Carlo scheme (
29
) is an option, it involves additional denoiser calls, which would increase runtime.
We thus propose to use the simple approximation

\displaystyle\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\approx\beta\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},

(34)

where
\beta\in(0,1)
is a denoiser-specific constant.
In words, (
34
) models the denoiser’s output error variance
\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
as a fixed fraction of its input error variance
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
.

Laplace approximation:

For PR, we adopt the likelihood

\displaystyle p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}(y_{i}|z_{i})=\mathcal{N}(y_{i};|z_{i}|,v),~~i=1,\dots,m,

(35)

due to its excellent performance in practice
[
59
]
.
However, the resulting EC posterior

\displaystyle q_{2}(z_{i};\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)})

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}(y_{i}|z_{i})\mathcal{CN}(z_{i};\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)})}{\int p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}(y_{i}|z_{i}^{\prime})\mathcal{CN}(z_{i}^{\prime};\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z_{i}^{\prime}}

(36)

\displaystyle\triangleq p_{\mathsf{z}|\mathsf{y}}(z_{i}|y_{i};\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)})

(37)

does not have analytically tractable mean or variance.
Consequently, we employ the Laplace approximation
[
79
]
, which assigns
\widehat{z}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
to the posterior mode (i.e., the MAP estimate) and
\widehat{v}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
to the trace of the inverse Hessian of
-\ln p_{\mathsf{z}|\mathsf{y}}(z_{i}|y_{i};\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i},\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)})
at
z_{i}=\widehat{z}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
, both of which have closed-form expressions.
In particular, the MAP estimate of
z_{i}
is given by
[
32
]

\displaystyle\widehat{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i}=\frac{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}y_{i}+2v|\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i}|}{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}+2v}e^{j\angle\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i}},

(38)

and the trace-inverse-Hessian at
z_{i}=\widehat{z}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
is

\displaystyle\widehat{v}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\frac{\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}(\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}y_{i}+4v|\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i}|)}{2|\overline{z}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}_{i}|(\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}+2v)}

(39)

as derived in App.
B
.
The overall posterior variance
\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
is then set to the average value over all
i
, i.e.,
\widehat{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=\tfrac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\widehat{v}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}
.

We test on three image datasets.

[

When recovering the FFHQ test images, we use a denoiser that was constructed by rescaling (see

[

[

The resulting diffusion denoiser “

The prDeep and Deep-ITA methods, however, do not have a way to estimate

IV-B

Like the prDeep and Deep-ITA papers, we employ OSF and CDP forward operators
\bm{A}
(recall (

\times

(Gaussian approximated)
Poisson shot-noise mechanism from
[
20

\displaystyle y_{i}^{2}=|z_{i}|^{2}+w_{i}\text{~with~}w_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\alpha^{2}|z_{i}|^{2}),

(40)

where

is approximately
\mathrm{Poisson}((|z_{i}|/\alpha)^{2})
for sufficiently small noise levels
\alpha

Note that, because we scale
\bm{A}
to have unit-norm columns, our
\alpha
may have a different meaning than the
\alpha

For example, with OSF measurements, our

to match the prDeep paper
[

[

With CDP measurements, our

IV-C

Performance Evaluation

We quantify performance using PSNR and SSIM
[
85
]
after resolving certain ambiguities fundamental to PR.

Phaseless Fourier measurements are unaffected by spatial translations, conjugate flips, and global phase
[
86
]
.
The phase ambiguity is circumvented when the image pixels are non-negative, as with all of our test images.
Furthermore, the translation ambiguity is avoided when oversampling is used with images that have a sufficient number of non-zero edge pixels, as with our color FFHQ test images, but not our grayscale test images.
Thus, when recovering the grayscale test images from OSF measurements, we correct for both translations and flips, but when recovering the color FFHQ test images, we correct for flips only.
With color images, however, one further complication arises with HIO.
Because HIO separately recovers each color channel, the flip ambiguity can manifest differently in each channel.
To address this latter problem, we fix the first channel of the HIO recovery and flip of each remaining channel to maximize its correlation with the first channel.

Phaseless CDP measurements experience only a global-phase ambiguity
[
87
]
, which is inconsequential with our
non-negative
test images, and so we perform no ambiguity resolution.

Algorithm Setup

For HIO, we ported the MATLAB implementation from
[
88
]
to Python and set the tunable step-size parameter to
0.9
.
For prDeep, we use the Python implementation from
[
89
]
.
For Deep-ITA
and DOLPH
, we implemented the algorithm in Python/PyTorch since no public information is available.
For DPS, we use the Python implementation from
[
81
]
.
As in the prDeep and Deep-ITA papers, we use
p_{\mathsf{y}|\mathsf{z}}
from (
4
) for all algorithms (except HIO, which does not use a likelihood).
For deepECpr, we use damping factors
\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(1)}=0.3
and
\mu^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=0.075
and initialization factor
\zeta=1.2
.
EM-based auto-tuning
[
90
]
is used to re-evaluate
\overline{v}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
for the first 3 and 10 iterations in the FFHQ and grayscale experiments, respectively.
The initial noise level
\overline{v}_{\mathsf{init}}
is set to
120^{2}
for the FFHQ experiments and
70^{2}
for the grayscale experiments.
Experiment-specific settings are described later.

Initialization/Reporting for OSF:

For the OSF experiments, we apply the initialization/reporting protocol used in both the prDeep and Deep-ITA papers to those algorithms, as well as to HIO and deepECpr.
First, HIO is run 50 times, for 50 iterations each, from a random initialization.
The candidate
\hat{\bm{x}}
with the lowest measurement residual
\|\bm{y}-|\bm{Ax}|\|
is then selected as the HIO initialization, after which HIO is run for 1000 iterations.
In the case of color images, the second and third channels are flipped to best match the first.
Finally, the resulting HIO output is used to initialize prDeep, Deep-ITA, and deepECpr.
The entire procedure is repeated three times, and the reconstruction with the lowest measurement residual is reported as the final output for each algorithm.
For DPS
and DOLPH
, we follow the
DPS
authors’ recommendation to run the algorithm four times and report the reconstruction with the lowest measurement residual.

Initialization/Reporting for CDP:

For the CDP experiments, we use the initialization

\hat{\bm{x}}_{\mathsf{init}}=\bm{A}^{\textsf{H}}(\bm{y}e^{j\angle{\bm{A}\bm{1}}})

for HIO, prDeep, Deep-ITA, and deepECpr, where
e^{j\angle{\bm{A}\bm{1}}}
is computed and applied element-wise.
Each algorithm was run only once.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed “deepECpr,” which combines expectation-consistent (EC) approximation with deep denoising networks to advance the state-of-the-art in phase retrieval. In addition to a non-traditional application of EC, deepECpr employs a novel stochastic damping scheme.
Experimental results with oversampled-Fourier and coded-diffraction-pattern operators, and with color, natural, and unnatural grayscale images, demonstrate deepECpr’s advantages in image recovery from noise-corrupted phaseless measurements.
In almost all cases, deepECpr yielded improved PSNR and SSIM over the state-of-the-art prDeep, Deep-ITA,
DOLPH,
and DPS methods with
significantly
fewer denoiser calls.
Although not explicitly tested, the proposed deepEC approach should be directly applicable to other generalized-linear-model problems like dequantization, logistic regression, and image recovery in non-Gaussian noise.

Here we derive
\nabla_{z}^{2}(-\ln p(z|y;\overline{z},\overline{v}))

(50)

(51)

(56)

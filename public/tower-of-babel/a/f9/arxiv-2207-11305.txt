Abstract
. We establish “vertical versus horizontal inequalities” for functions from nonabelian simply connected nilpotent Lie groups and not virtually abelian finitely generated groups of polynomial growth into uniformly convex Banach spaces using the vector-valued Littlewood–Paley–Stein theory approach of Lafforgue and Naor (2012). This is a quantitative nonembeddability statement that shows that any Lipschitz mapping from the aforementioned groups into a uniformly convex space must quantitatively collapse along certain subgroups. As a consequence, a ball of radius r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 in the aforementioned groups must incur bilipschitz distortion at least a constant multiple of ( log ⁡ r ) 1 / q (\log r)^{1/q} into a q ( ≥ 2 ) q(\geq 2) -uniformly convex Banach space. This bound is sharp for the L p L^{p} ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ) spaces. In the special case of mappings of Carnot groups into the L p L^{p} ( 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty ) spaces, we prove that the quantitative collapse occurs on a larger subgroup that is the commutator subgroup; this is in line with the qualitative Pansu–Semmes nonembeddability argument given by Cheeger and Kleiner (2006) and Lee and Naor (2006). We prove this by establishing a version of the classical Dorronsoro theorem on Carnot groups. Previously, in the setting of Heisenberg groups, Fässler and Orponen (2019) established a one-sided Dorronsoro theorem with a restriction 0 < α < 2 0<\alpha<2 on the range of exponents α \alpha of the Laplacian; this restriction does not appear in the commutative setting and is caused by their use of horizontal polynomials as approximants. We identify the correct class of approximant polynomials and prove the two-sided Dorronsoro theorem with the full range 0 < α < ∞ 0<\alpha<\infty of exponents in the general setting of Carnot groups, thus strengthening and extending the work of Fässler and Orponen.

Introduction

It is well known that nonabelian simply connected nilpotent Lie groups and not virtually abelian finitely generated groups of polynomial growth fail to embed bilipschitzly (or quasi-isometrically) into uniformly convex Banach spaces (or, more generally, Banach spaces with the Radon–Nikodým property). This is because nonabelian Carnot groups do not bilipschitzly embed into Banach spaces with the Radon–Nikodým property
[
CK06
,
LN06
]
, and nonabelian simply connected nilpotent Lie groups have nonabelian Carnot groups as asymptotic cone and hence do not quasi-isometrically embed into Banach spaces with the Radon-Nikodým property (see
[
HS20
]
for example), while not virtually abelian finitely generated groups of polynomial growth are quasi-isometric to nonabelian simply connected nilpotent Lie groups
[
Gro81
,
Mal49
]
and hence fail to embed quasi-isometrically into Banach spaces with the Radon–Nikodým property. The aim of this paper is to provide quantitative counterparts to these qualitative nonembeddability statements.

Let

\nabla f\coloneqq(X_{1}f,\cdots,X_{k}f),

Theorem 5
.

Let
G
be a nonabelian simply connected nilpotent Lie group. Let
v\in Z(G)
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{G}\left(\frac{\|f(hv^{t})-f(h)\|_{X}}{t^{1/\rho}}\right)^{p}d\mu(h)\right)^{\max\{p,q\}/p}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/\max\{p,q\}}\\
&\lesssim\max\left\{(p-1)^{1/q-1},K_{q}(X)\right\}\left(\int_{G}\left\|\nabla f(h)\right\|_{\ell_{2}^{k}(X)}^{p}d\mu(h)\right)^{1/p}.\end{aligned}

(1)

In particular, when
p=q
,

\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{G}\left(\frac{\|f(hv^{t})-f(h)\|_{X}}{t^{1/\rho}}\right)^{q}d\mu(h)\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim K_{q}(X)\left(\int_{G}\left\|\nabla f(h)\right\|_{\ell_{2}^{k}(X)}^{q}d\mu(h)\right)^{1/q}.

(2)

Note that the
v
described in this theorem exists, for example, if we choose

where

The definition of Carnot groups is as follows.

Definition 12

That a nonabelian Carnot group

of

would also be bilipschitz, resulting in a Carnot group

. This is impossible because a group homomorphism

must send the nontrivial commutator subgroup

to

. Later this argument was generalized, independently by Cheeger and Kleiner
[

]
and Lee and Naor
[

]
, to Banach space targets with the Radon–Nikodým property, which includes uniformly convex spaces.

1.1.
Dorronsoro’s theorem on Carnot groups

As mentioned earlier, a byproduct of this paper is a formulation and proof of a Dorronsoro theorem for Carnot groups, which is used to prove Theorem

Let
G
be a Carnot group in this subsection.
Following Folland
[

]
and denoting by

the sub-Laplacian and

the corresponding heat kernel, we define the operator
(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha}
for

for all

such that the limit exists in the
L^{p}
norm. Then, for
1<p<\infty
and
\alpha\geq 0
, the Sobolev space

is the Banach space
\operatorname{Dom}((-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2})
with norm

\|\cdot\|_{p,\alpha}\coloneqq\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(G)}+\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}(\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

Fix a basis
X_{r,1},\cdots,X_{r,k_{r}}
, where
k_{r}=\dim V_{r}
, of each stratum
V_{r}
. As

is nilpotent and simply connected, the exponential map
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\to G
is a diffeomorphism. Thus, each point

; let this be the single coordinate chart on

. For polynomials of the coordinates
x_{r,i}
, we assign weight

,

.

Recall that

is the Hausdorff dimension of
G
. We have

, as we must have
s\geq 2
,
k_{1}\geq 2
and

is nonabelian.

For
r>0
, recall the notation for open balls

B_{r}=\{h\in G:d_{G}(h,e_{G})<r\},~B_{r}(g)=\{h\in G:d_{G}(h,g)<r\}=gB_{r},\quad g\in G,~r>0.

Recall that
\delta_{\lambda}
is a scaling in the Carnot–Carathéodory metric:

d_{G}(\delta_{\lambda}(p),\delta_{\lambda}(p^{\prime}))=\lambda d_{G}(p,p^{\prime}),\quad p,p^{\prime}\in G.

Thus
B_{r}=\delta_{r}(B_{1})
,

d_{G}(y,e_{G})\asymp_{G}\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}|y_{r,i}|^{1/r}.

Let

and let

denote the family of polynomials

of weighted degree

gh=\sum_{m=1}^{s}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{m}\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}r_{1}+s_{1}>0\\
\cdots\\
r_{m}+s_{m}>0\end{smallmatrix}}\frac{[g^{r_{1}}h^{s_{1}}g^{r_{2}}h^{s_{2}}\dotsm g^{r_{m}}h^{s_{m}}]}{(\sum_{j=1}^{m}(r_{j}+s_{j}))\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}!s_{i}!},

where the sum is finite since

[g^{r_{1}}h^{s_{1}}\dotsm g^{r_{m}}h^{s_{m}}]=[\underbrace{g,[g,\dotsm[g}_{r_{1}},[\underbrace{h,[h,\dotsm[h}_{s_{1}},\dotsm[\underbrace{g,[g,\dotsm[g}_{r_{m}},[\underbrace{h,[h,\dotsm h}_{s_{m}}]]\dotsm]].

\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}x^{0}_{r,i}X_{r,i}\right)\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}x^{1}_{r,i}X_{r,i}\right)=\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}x^{2}_{r,i}X_{r,i}\right)

x^{2}_{r,i}=x^{0}_{r,i}+x^{1}_{r,i}+(\mathrm{homogeneous~polynomial~of~}\{x^{0}_{r^{\prime},i^{\prime}}\}_{r^{\prime}<r},\{x^{1}_{r^{\prime},i^{\prime}}\}_{r^{\prime}<r}\mathrm{~of~weighted~degree~}r).

Therefore a polynomial of weighted degree

precomposed with a left translation is still of weighted degree

(8)

in the sense that

Lemma 37
.

Suppose that

and

. Then for every Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
and every smooth and compactly supported

(26)

Proof.

Recalling the semigroup property
\dot{h}_{2t}=h_{t}*\dot{h}_{t}
, we have

\dot{h}_{t}*f(g)-\dot{h}_{2t}*f(g)=\dot{h}_{t}*f(g)-h_{t}*\dot{h}_{t}*f(g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{t}(u)\left(\dot{h}_{t}*f(g)-\dot{h}_{t}*f(gv^{-u})\right)du.

(27)

For

, let

be a measurable family of geodesics parametrized by arclength joining

to

. (Such a measurable family exists by the Aumann measurable selection theorem

). For every

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{t}*f(g)-\dot{h}_{t}*f(gv^{-u})

\displaystyle=\dot{h}_{t}*f(gv^{-u}\gamma_{u}(d_{G}(v^{u},e_{G})))-\dot{h}_{t}*f(gv^{-u})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{d_{G}(v^{u},e_{G})}\frac{d}{d\theta}\dot{h}_{t}*f(gv^{-u}\gamma_{u}(\theta))d\theta.

Because

is horizontal and parametrized by arclength, and because convolution with

We thus obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{G}\left\|\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{t}(u)\left(\dot{h}_{t}*f(g)-\dot{h}_{t}*f(gv^{-u})\right)du\right\|_{X}^{p}dg\right)^{1/p}

Similarly, for

Lemma 38

Fix

, so that
w\in xv_{\Gamma}^{k}B^{\Gamma}_{r}
. Therefore

\|F(hv_{\Gamma}^{t})-f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\left(\sum_{z\in B^{\Gamma}_{r}}\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k}z)-f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

The case
k=t=0
gives

\|F(h)-f(x)\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\left(\sum_{z\in B^{\Gamma}_{r}}\|f(xz)-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Therefore by the triangle inequality,

\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})-f(x)\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\|F(hv_{\Gamma}^{t})-F(h)\|_{X}+\left(\sum_{z\in B^{\Gamma}_{r}}\Big(\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k}z)-f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})\|_{X}^{p}+\|f(xz)-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\Big)\right)^{1/p}.

Integration over
h\in xU
gives

\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})-f(x)\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{xU}\|F(hv_{\Gamma}^{t})-F(h)\|_{X}^{p}dh\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{z\in B^{\Gamma}_{r}}\Big(\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k}z)-f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})\|_{X}^{p}+\|f(xz)-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\Big)\right)^{1/p}.

Summing over
x\in\Gamma
and using that the sets
\{xU\}_{x\in\Gamma}
are pairwise disjoint,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{G}\|F(hv_{\Gamma}^{t})-F(h)\|_{X}^{p}dh\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{z\in B^{\Gamma}_{r}}\Big(\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k}z)-f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})\|_{X}^{p}+\|f(xz)-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\Big)\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Lemma~}\ref{lem:global poincare-simple}}}{{\lesssim_{\Gamma}}}\left(\int_{G}\|F(hv_{\Gamma}^{t})-F(h)\|_{X}^{p}dh\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{a\in S}\|f(xa)-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Integrating over
t\in[k,k+1]
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{1+\max\{p,q\}/s}}\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{\max\{p,q\}/p}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\max\{p,q\}}\int_{k}^{k+1}\left(\int_{G}\|F(hv_{\Gamma}^{t})-F(h)\|_{X}^{p}dh\right)^{\max\{p,q\}/p}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\max\{p,q\}/s}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C^{\max\{p,q\}}}{k^{1+\max\{p,q\}/s}}\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{a\in S}\|f(xa)-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{\max\{p,q\}/p},

where
C=C_{G}\in(0,\infty)
is a constant depending on
G
. Summing over
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{1+\max\{p,q\}/s}}\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{\max\{p,q\}/p}\right)^{1/\max\{p,q\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\Gamma}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{G}\|F(hv_{\Gamma}^{t})-F(h)\|_{X}^{p}dh\right)^{\max\{p,q\}/p}\frac{dt}{t^{1+\max\{p,q\}/s}}\right)^{1/\max\{p,q\}}+\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{a\in S}\|f(xa)-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

(39)

The desired inequality follows from (
38
) and (
4
) along with Theorem
5
for
F
(although it may be the case that
d_{G}(v_{\Gamma}^{t},e_{G})\nleq t^{1/s}
, a simple rescaling argument gives the same inequality up to constant factors depending on
v_{\Gamma}
).
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

The argument follows the proof of Theorem 3.1 of
[
LN14
]
, which in turn follows the proof of Claim 7.2 in
[
ANT13
]
. Recall
c
is such that

d_{W}(e_{\Gamma},v_{\Gamma}^{k})\leq ck^{1/s}.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and an

. By translation, we may assume
\sum_{x\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}}f(x)=0
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left(\sum_{x\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}}\|f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}&=\left(\sum_{x\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}}\left\|\frac{1}{|B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}|}\sum_{y\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}}(f(x)-f(y))\right\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{|B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}|}\sum_{x,y\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}}\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Lemma~}\ref{lem:metric bruce}}}{{\lesssim_{\Gamma}}}n\left(\sum_{x\in B^{\Gamma}_{3(c+3)n}}\sum_{a\in S}\|f(xa)-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.\end{aligned}

(40)

Define the cutoff function
\xi:\Gamma\to[0,1]

\xi(x)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&x\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+1)n},\\
c+2-\frac{d_{W}(x,e)}{n}&x\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+2)n}\setminus B^{\Gamma}_{(c+1)n},\\
0&x\in\Gamma\setminus B^{\Gamma}_{(c+2)n}.\end{array}\right.

Then

is supported on

and we may apply Theorem
41
. As
\xi

\frac{1}{n}
-Lipschitz and takes values in

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\phi(x)-\phi(xa)\|_{X}&\leq|\xi(x)-\xi(xa)|\cdot\|f(x)\|_{X}+|\xi(xa)|\cdot\|f(x)-f(xa)\|_{X}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{n}\|f(x)\|_{X}+\|f(x)-f(xa)\|_{X}.\end{aligned}

(41)

We have

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n^{s}}\frac{1}{k^{1+q/s}}\left(\sum_{x\in B^{\Gamma}_{n}}\|f(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})-f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{q/p}\right)^{1/q}\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{1+q/s}}\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\|\phi(xv_{\Gamma}^{k})-\phi(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{q/p}\right)^{1/q}.

(42)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{x\in\Gamma}\sum_{a\in S}\|\phi(xa)-\phi(x)\|^{p}_{X}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}}\sum_{a\in S}\|\phi(xa)-\phi(x)\|^{p}_{X}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:tildef}}}{{\leq}}\frac{|S|^{1/p}}{n}\left(\sum_{x\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}}\|f(x)\|_{X}^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\sum_{x\in B^{\Gamma}_{(c+3)n}}\sum_{a\in S}\|f(xa)-f(x)\|^{p}_{X}\right)^{1/p}

where the first equality holds since

Proof of Theorem

: sublinear growth of cocycles

Our goal in this section is to prove Theorem

. The proof follows closely that of
[

]
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a
q
-uniformly convex space. We will see by
[
NP11
]
that is enough to prove Theorem
9
for
1
-Lipschitz
1
-cocycles. Here,
f\in Z^{1}(\pi)
be a
1
-cocycle with respect to an action
\pi:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aut}(X)
of
\Gamma
on
X
by linear isometric automorphisms if the function
f:\Gamma\to X
satisfies
f(xy)=\pi(x)f(y)+f(x)
for all
x,y\in\Gamma
. The
1
-Lipschitz requirement for

is equivalent to

for all

.

We first recall the following property of
q
-uniformly convex spaces.

Lemma 42

(
[
ANT13
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be
q
-uniformly convex. For a fixed
z\in X
and linear operator
T:X\to X
with
\|T\|\leq 1
, define

s_{n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}T^{j}z,\quad n\geq 0.

Then we have

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{l}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{l}-1}\left\|s_{(i+1)l}-T^{j2^{il}}s_{il}\right\|_{X}^{q}\leq(2K_{q}(X))^{q}\|z\|_{X}^{q},\quad l\in\mathbb{N}.

(43)

On the other hand, as
X
is reflexive,
X
is ergodic
[
DS88
, p.662]
, i.e., for every linear isometry
T:X\to X
and
x\in X
the sequence
\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^{j}x\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges in norm. Thus, the operator
P:X\to X
defined by

Px\coloneqq\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\pi(v)^{n}x,\quad x\in X,

is well-defined, has norm
\leq 1
, is a contraction onto the subspace
X_{0}
of
\pi(v)
-invariant vectors, and is idempotent. As
v
is a central element of
\Gamma
,
P
commutes with
\pi(g)
for all
g\in\Gamma
, and so the maps
Pf,(I-P)f:\Gamma\to X
are both Lipschitz and are
1
-cocycles.

Now define linear operators
P_{n}:X\to X
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by

P_{n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\pi(v)^{j}.

Of course,
\|P_{n}\|\leq 1
and
P_{n}
commutes with
\pi(g)
for all
g\in\Gamma
.

Lemma 43

(Analogue of
[
ANT13
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a
q
-uniformly convex space. Then for every
l,k,m\in\mathbb{N}
there exist integers
i\in[k+1,k+m]
and
j\in[0,2^{l}-1]
such that

\left\|\pi\left(v^{-j2^{il}}\right)P_{(i+1)l}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)-P_{il}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{G}\frac{K_{q}(X)n}{m^{1/p}}.

(44)

Proof.

By Lemma
42
and the fact that
\|f(a)\|_{X}\leq 1
, we have for each
a\in S

\sum_{i=k+1}^{k+m}\frac{1}{2^{l}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{l}-1}\left\|\pi\left(v^{-j2^{il}}\right)P_{(i+1)l}f(a)-P_{il}f(a)\right\|_{X}^{q}=\sum_{i=k+1}^{k+m}\frac{1}{2^{l}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{l}-1}\left\|P_{(i+1)l}f(a)-\pi\left(v^{j2^{il}}\right)P_{il}f(a)\right\|_{X}^{q}\leq(2K_{q}(X))^{q}.

Thus

\displaystyle|S|(2K_{q}(X))^{q}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=k+1}^{k+m}\frac{1}{2^{l}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{l}-1}\sum_{a\in S}\left\|\pi\left(v^{-j2^{il}}\right)P_{(i+1)l}f(a)-P_{il}f(a)\right\|_{X}^{q}

\displaystyle\geq m\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+1\leq i\leq k+m\\
1\leq j\leq 2^{l}-1\end{subarray}}\sum_{a\in S}\left\|\pi\left(v^{-j2^{il}}\right)P_{(i+1)l}f(a)-P_{il}f(a)\right\|_{X}^{q},

and so there exist
i\in[k+1,k+m]
,
j\in[0,2^{l}-1]
such that

\max_{a\in S}\left\|\pi\left(v^{-j2^{il}}\right)P_{(i+1)l}f(a)-P_{il}f(a)\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\frac{K_{q}(X)}{m^{1/q}}.

As
v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}=a_{1}\cdots a_{b}
for some
a_{i}\in S
, where
b=O_{\Gamma}(n)
, we have by the cocycle identity

f(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor})=\sum_{i=1}^{b}\pi(a_{1}\cdots a_{i-1})f(a_{i}).

Since
P_{r}
,
r\geq 0
, and
\pi(g)
,
g\in\Gamma
, commute, and
v
and
g\in\Gamma
commute, and
\pi(g)
is an isometry for
g\in\Gamma
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\pi\left(v^{-j2^{il}}\right)P_{(i+1)l}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)-P_{il}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{b}\left\|\pi\left(v^{-j2^{il}}\right)P_{(i+1)l}f\left(a_{i}\right)-P_{il}f\left(a_{i}\right)\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\frac{K_{q}(X)n}{m^{1/q}},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 44

(Analogue of
[
ANT13
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

For every
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\|P_{m}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\frac{n^{(\rho^{2}+\rho-1)/(2\rho-1)}}{2^{m(\rho-1)/(2\rho-1)}}.

Proof.

If
\frac{n^{\rho}}{2^{m}}\geq\rho-1
then we have the obvious bound

\left\|P_{m}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\leq\left\|f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\leq d_{W}\left(e_{\Gamma},v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\lesssim_{\Gamma}n\left(\frac{n^{\rho}}{2^{m}}\right)^{(\rho-1)/(2\rho-1)}.

Thus we assume
\frac{n^{\rho}}{2^{m}}<\rho-1
.

Because

P_{m}-\pi(v^{k})P_{m}=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\pi(v)^{j}-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{j=2^{m}}^{2^{m}+k-1}\pi(v)^{j},

we have

\left\|P_{m}-\pi(v^{k})P_{m}\right\|\leq\frac{2k}{2^{m}}.

By the cocycle identity,

f\left(v^{\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor-1}\pi\left(v^{j\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)

Because
f
is
1
-Lipschitz,
d_{W}\left(e_{\Gamma},v^{\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\lesssim_{\Gamma}kn
, and
\|P_{m}\|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle kn

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{\Gamma}\left\|P_{m}f\left(v^{\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}=\left\|\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor P_{m}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)-\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor-1}\left(P_{m}-\pi\left(v^{j\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)P_{m}\right)f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\geq\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor\left\|P_{m}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}-\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor-1}\left\|P_{m}-\pi\left(v^{j\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)P_{m}\right\|\cdot\left\|f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\geq\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor\left\|P_{m}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}-\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k^{\rho}\rfloor-1}\frac{2j\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}{2^{m}}d_{W}\left(e_{\Gamma},v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)

and rearranging terms,

\left\|P_{m}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\frac{n}{k^{\rho-1}}+\frac{n^{\rho+1}k^{\rho}}{2^{m}}.

Since this is true for all
k
, choosing
k=\left\lceil\left(\frac{(\rho-1)2^{m}}{\rho n^{\rho}}\right)^{1/(2\rho-1)}\right\rceil
gives the stated bound. Indeed, by
\frac{n^{\rho}}{2^{m}}<\rho-1
we have
k\asymp\left(\frac{(\rho-1)2^{m}}{\rho n^{\rho}}\right)^{1/(2\rho-1)}
and plugging in gives the desired bound.
∎

Lemma 45

(Analogue of
[
ANT13
, Lemma 4.3]
)
.

For every
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\|f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)-P_{m}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}2^{m/(\rho+1)}n^{1/(\rho+1)}.

Proof.

If we define
\tilde{f}:\Gamma\to X
by

\tilde{f}(h)\coloneqq f(h)-P_{m}f(h)=(I-P_{m})f(h),

then
\tilde{f}\in Z^{1}(\pi)
. Let
k\geq 1
be an integer to be determined later. If we set

w\coloneqq-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tilde{f}(v^{j}),

then

\|w\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}j^{1/\rho}\lesssim k^{1/\rho}.

For every
h\in\Gamma
we have the following identity:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\pi(h)w+\tilde{f}(h)&=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\pi(h)\tilde{f}(v^{j})+\tilde{f}(h)\right)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tilde{f}(hv^{j})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\tilde{f}(v^{j}h)\\
&=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\pi(v^{j})\tilde{f}(h)+\tilde{f}(v^{j})\right)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\pi(v^{j})\tilde{f}(h)-w.\end{aligned}

(45)

But

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\pi(v^{j})\tilde{f}(h)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\pi(v^{j})f(h)-\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{m}-1}\pi(v^{j+i})f(h)\right)=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{m}-1}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\pi(v^{j})-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=i}^{i+k-1}\pi(v^{j})\right)f(h),

so that

\left\|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\pi(v^{j})\tilde{f}(h)\right\|_{X}\leq\frac{d_{W}(h,e_{\Gamma})}{2^{m}}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{m}-1}\frac{2i}{k}\leq\frac{2^{m}}{k}d_{W}(h,e_{\Gamma}).

Because of (
45
),
\tilde{f}
is close to a coboundary in the following sense:

\left\|\tilde{f}(h)-(\pi(h)w-w)\right\|\leq\frac{2^{m}}{k}d_{W}(h,e_{\Gamma}).

Writing
v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}=a_{1}\cdots a_{b}
for some
a_{i}\in S
, where
b=O_{\Gamma}(n)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{f}\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{b}\pi(a_{1}\cdots a_{i-1})\tilde{f}(a_{i})\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{b}\pi(a_{1}\cdots a_{i-1})\left(\pi(a_{i})w-w\right)\right\|_{X}+\frac{n2^{m}}{k}

\displaystyle=\left\|\pi\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)w-w\right\|_{X}+\frac{n2^{m}}{k}\lesssim_{\Gamma}k^{1/\rho}+\frac{n2^{m}}{k}.

With
k=\lceil n^{\frac{\rho}{\rho+1}}2^{\frac{m\rho}{\rho+1}}\rceil
, we have the stated bound.
∎

Proof of Theorem
9
.

By

is a

for all

. Thus, we may assume without loss of generality that

for some action
\pi
of
\Gamma

Let

\left\|\pi\left(v^{-j2^{il}}\right)P_{(i+1)l}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)-P_{il}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\frac{K_{q}(X)n}{m^{1/p}}.

(50)

n\coloneqq\left\lceil\frac{1}{C_{1}}m^{\frac{\rho+1}{\rho p}}2^{\frac{i\ell}{\rho}}\right\rceil,

(51)

Expanding, we have

f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)=\pi\left(v^{-j2^{i\ell}}\right)P_{(i+1)\ell}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)+\left(P_{i\ell}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)-\pi\left(v^{-j2^{i\ell}}\right)P_{(i+1)\ell}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right)+\left(f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)-P_{i\ell}f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right).

Thus by Lemmas

\omega_{f}\left(d_{W}\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor},e_{\Gamma}\right)\right)\leq\left\|f\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor}\right)\right\|_{X}\lesssim_{\Gamma}\frac{n^{(\rho^{2}+\rho-1)/(2\rho-1)}}{2^{(i+1)l(\rho-1)/(2\rho-1)}}+\frac{K_{q}(X)n}{m^{1/p}}+2^{il/(\rho+1)}n^{1/(\rho+1)}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def l}\wedge\eqref{eq:def n}}}{{\lesssim_{\Gamma}}}\frac{K_{q}(X)n}{m^{1/p}}.

(52)

We compute

d_{W}\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor},e_{\Gamma}\right)\geq C_{1}n\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def n}}}{{\geq}}m^{\frac{\rho+1}{\rho p}}2^{\frac{i\ell}{\rho}}\geq m^{\frac{\rho+1}{\rho p}}2^{\frac{(k+1)\ell}{\rho}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def l}}}{{\geq}}m^{\frac{\rho+1}{\rho p}+\frac{(\rho^{2}+2\rho-2)(k+1)}{p\rho(\rho-1)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def k}}}{{\geq}}t

and

\displaystyle d_{W}\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor},e_{\Gamma}\right)\leq C_{2}n\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def n}}}{{\leq}}\frac{2C_{2}}{C_{1}}m^{\frac{\rho+1}{\rho p}}2^{\frac{i\ell}{\rho}}\leq\frac{2C_{2}}{C_{1}}m^{\frac{\rho+1}{\rho p}}2^{\frac{(k+m)\ell}{\rho}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def l}}}{{\leq}}\frac{2C_{1}}{C_{2}}2^{\frac{k+m}{\rho}}m^{\frac{\rho+1}{\rho p}+\frac{k(\rho^{2}+2\rho-2)}{p\rho(\rho-1)}}m^{\frac{m(\rho^{2}+2\rho-2)}{p\rho(\rho-1)}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def k}}}{{<}}\frac{2C_{1}}{C_{2}}2^{\frac{k+m}{\rho}}tm^{\frac{m(\rho^{2}+2\rho-2)}{p\rho(\rho-1)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def k}}}{{\leq}}\frac{2C_{1}}{C_{2}}2^{\frac{p(\rho-1)}{\rho^{2}+2\rho-2}\log_{m}t}2^{\frac{m}{\rho}}tm^{\frac{m(\rho^{2}+2\rho-2)}{p\rho(\rho-1)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def m}}}{{\leq}}t^{3/2}2^{\frac{p(\rho-1)}{\rho^{2}+2\rho-2}\log_{m}t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:prop m}}}{{\leq}}t^{2}.

Therefore
t\leq d_{W}\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor},e_{\Gamma}\right)\leq t^{2}
. The definition of
m
implies
m\gtrsim_{\Gamma}\min\left\{\log n,\frac{p\log n}{\log\log n}\right\}
, and
thus by (
52
), we have

\frac{\omega_{f}\left(d_{W}\left(v^{\lfloor n^{\rho}\rfloor},e_{\Gamma}\right)\right)}{n}\lesssim K_{q}(X)\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^{1/p}.

The proof of Theorem
9
is complete.
∎

6.
Derivation of Theorem
24
from Dorronsoro’s Theorem
22

In this section, we prove Theorem
24
, which proves Theorem
17
since it is a special case. This section follows Section 7 of
[
FO20a
]
closely.

Using an argument similar to the Vitali covering lemma, we may find a collection
\mathcal{B}_{r}
of balls
B
of radius
r
whose union covers
G
, and such that the concentric balls
\hat{B}
of radius
(n+1)r
have bounded overlap. Then

\displaystyle\int_{G}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}f(x(\delta_{r}(v))^{j})\right|\right)^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}}\int_{B}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}f(x(\delta_{r}(v))^{j})\right|\right)^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}^{p}\sum_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}}\int_{B}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\left|(f(x(\delta_{r}(v))^{j})-A^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}_{\hat{B}}f(x(\delta_{r}(v))^{j})\right|\right)^{p}dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{B}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}A^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}_{\hat{B}}f(x(\delta_{r}(v))^{j})\right|\right)^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,p}\sum_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}}\int_{\hat{B}}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\left|f(x)-A^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}_{\hat{B}}f(x)\right|\right)^{p}dx+0

\displaystyle=\sum_{B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{r}}\left[\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\beta_{f,\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,p}(\hat{B})\right]^{p}|\hat{B}|\lesssim_{G,\alpha}\int_{G}\left[\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\cdot\beta_{f,\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,p}(B_{2(n+1)r}(x))\right]^{p}dx.

We have used in the penultimate inequality the fact that since
A^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}_{\hat{B}}f
is a polynomial of weighted degree at most
\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
, and since
\delta_{r}(v)\in V_{\lfloor\alpha/n\rfloor+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
,
A^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}_{\hat{B}}f(x(\delta_{r}(v))^{j})
is a polynomial in
j
of degree at most
n-1
, and hence

\Delta_{\delta_{r}(v)}^{n}f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j}A^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}_{\hat{B}}f(x(\delta_{r}(v))^{j})=0.

In the last inequality we used Corollary
47
. Now, the inequality (
9
) follows from Minkowski’s integral inequality and Theorem
22
with
q=p
:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{G}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}f(x(\delta_{r}(v))^{j})\right|\right)^{p}dx\right]^{2/p}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{G,\alpha,n,p}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\int_{G}\left[\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\beta_{f,\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,p}(B_{2(n+1)r}(x))\right]^{p}dx\right]^{2/p}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{G}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\beta_{f,\lfloor\alpha\rfloor,p}(B_{2(n+1)r}(x))\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right]^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}\lesssim_{G,\alpha,n,p}\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

The proof of Theorem
24
is complete.

We will first prove Theorem

7.2

that the

1\leq q<\frac{\min\{p,2\}n_{h}}{n_{h}-\min\{p,2\}}

For the

7.2.1

7.2.2

0<\alpha<1

[
CRTN01
, Theorem 5]
(subsubsection
7.2.4
), and for
\alpha>1
nonintegral we may use an induction argument on
\alpha
(subsubsection
7.2.5
). We finish off the case of
\alpha\geq 1
integral by an interpolation argument (subsubsection
7.2.6
), with the extra terms arising in this process having been taken care of by a homogenization argument (subsubsection
7.2.3
).

For the
\gtrsim
direction, we assume

and the finiteness of a certain singular integral and need to derive

54
. For
0<\alpha\leq 1
we again use characterizations of the fractional Laplacian given by
[
CRTN01

for

[

7.3.2
).

For simplicity, we define the
L^{1}
-beta numbers

\beta_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))\coloneqq\beta_{f,d,1}(B_{r}(x)).

If we define the function

\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\beta_{f,\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{\alpha}}\right)^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2},\quad x\in G,

then Theorem

states that

\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\asymp_{G,\alpha,p}\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}(G)},\quad f\in S^{p}_{\alpha}.

(53)

Before we begin the proof, we briefly remark on coordinate and multi-index notation on
G
. Recall the coordinate system
x=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}x_{r,i}X_{r,i}\right)
. A multi-index
\gamma=((\gamma_{r,i})_{i=1}^{k_{r}})_{r=1}^{s}
is a multi-index on
\sum_{r=1}^{s}k_{r}
entries, and we denote

|\gamma|\coloneqq\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}r|\gamma_{r,i}|\in\mathbb{N},\quad\gamma!\coloneqq\prod_{r=1}^{s}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{r}}\gamma_{r,i}!,\quad x^{\gamma}\coloneqq\prod_{r=1}^{s}\prod_{i=1}^{k_{r}}x_{r,i}^{\gamma_{r,i}}\in\mathbb{R}.

The latter should not be confused with the previous notation
x^{t}=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}tx_{r,i}X_{r,i}\right)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

We now define the weighted degree of linear combinations of ‘polynomial differential operators’, that is, linear combinations of differential operators of the form

x^{\gamma_{1}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{\gamma_{2}},

by assigning weight
|\gamma_{2}|-|\gamma_{1}|
to the above term. Then, a polynomial differential operator of homogeneous weighted degree
d_{1}
will act on a polynomial of homogeneous weighted degree
d_{2}
to produce a polynomial of homogeneous weighted degree
d_{2}-d_{1}
. We observe that the left-invariant differential operators
X_{r,i}
is a polynomial differential operator of homogeneous weighted degree
r
for
r=1,\cdots,s
and
i=1,\cdots,k_{r}
.

Recall from subsection
1.1
that the polynomials of weighted degree
\leq r
are left-invariant.

7.1.
L^{1}
-optimality of the
A^{d}_{x,r}f
’s

We begin the proof by discussing some basic properties of
A^{d}_{x,r}f
and
\beta_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))
.

It is well-known that
A^{d}_{x,r}f
is the optimal
L^{2}(B_{r}(x))
-approximation of
f
in
{\mathcal{A}}_{d}
. It turns out that it is also an optimal
L^{1}(B_{r}(x))
-approximation up to constants. To see this, we first observe that for all
d\geq 0
, there exists an integral formula for the coefficients of
A^{d}_{x,r}f
, given by the Gram–Schmidt process. For example in
d=1
,

A^{1}_{x,r}f(xy)=\langle f\rangle_{B_{r}(x)}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\fint_{B_{r}(x)}f(z)(z_{1,j}-x_{1,j})dz}{\fint_{B_{r}(x)}(z_{1,j}-x_{1,j})^{2}dz}y_{j},\quad y\in G.

(54)

More generally, for each
d\geq 0
, there exists a family
\{p^{d}_{\gamma}\}_{|\gamma|\leq d}
of polynomials such that

A^{d}_{0,1}f(y)=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq d}\left(\int_{B_{1}}fp^{d}_{\gamma}\right)y^{\gamma},\quad y\in G,

and by rescaling we have for
x\in G
and
r>0
that

A^{d}_{x,r}f(xy)=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq d}r^{-n_{h}-|\gamma|}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}f(z)p^{d}_{\gamma}(\delta_{1/r}(x^{-1}z))dz\right)y^{\gamma}.

Therefore

\|A^{d}_{x,r}f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}\lesssim_{G,d}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)|dy.

(55)

In other words,
A^{d}_{x,r}
is a linear projection of
L^{1}(B_{r}(x))
onto
\left.\mathcal{A}_{d}\right|_{B_{r}(x)}
which is bounded in the
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
norm.

We now prove the
L^{1}
-optimality of the
A^{d}_{x,r}f
’s.

Lemma 46
.

For
x\in G
,
r>0
,
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{r}(x))
, and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-A^{d}_{x,r}f(y)|dy\lesssim_{G,d}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-A(y)|dy,\quad A\in\mathcal{A}_{d}.

Proof.

From (
55
),

\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-A^{d}_{x,r}f|\leq\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-A|+\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|A^{d}_{x,r}(f-A)|\lesssim_{G,d}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-A|.

∎

It follows that
\beta
possesses a weak monotonicity property.

Corollary 47
.

Let
x,y\in G
,
0<r<s<\infty
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be such that
B_{r}(x)\subset B_{s}(y)
. Then

\beta_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))\lesssim_{G,d}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{n_{h}}\beta_{f,d}(B_{s}(y)).

By Corollary
47
, we have that

\beta_{f,d}(x,r)\lesssim_{G,d}\beta_{f,d}(x,s)\lesssim_{G,d}\beta_{f,d}(x,2r),\quad r\leq s\leq 2r.

Thus
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)
is comparable to the series

\left(\sum_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[2^{-i\alpha}\beta_{f,d}(x,2^{i})\right]^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

One can also see that, if
X
is a left-invariant differential operator of weighted order
n
, then because
A^{d}_{x,r}f
is a polynomial of weighted degree
d
,

\|XA^{d}_{x,r}f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}\lesssim_{G,n,d,X}r^{-n}\|A^{d}_{x,r}f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}\lesssim_{G,d}r^{-n}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f|.

(56)

7.2.
Proof of the
\lesssim
direction,
q=1

7.2.1.
Approximation by smooth compactly supported functions

It is enough to prove the
\lesssim
statement of Theorem
22
for
f
in the space
\mathcal{D}
of smooth compactly supported functions on
G
. Indeed, by
[
Fol75
, Theorem 4.5]
,
\mathcal{D}
is dense in
S^{p}_{\alpha}
for all
1<p<\infty
and
\alpha\geq 0
. Given
f\in S^{p}_{\alpha}
, choose a sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{D}
that converges to
f
in
S^{p}_{\alpha}
. Then
f_{j}\to f
in
L^{p}(G)
, so by the contraction property (
55
) we have

\beta_{f_{j},\lfloor{\alpha}\rfloor}(B_{r}(x))\to\beta_{f,\lfloor{\alpha}\rfloor}(B_{r}(x))\mbox{ as }j\to\infty,\quad x\in G,~r>0.

Now, by two applications of Fatou’s lemma, it follows that

\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim_{G,\alpha,p}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(G)}=\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}.

This completes the proof of our claim.

7.2.2.
Taylor polynomials

Recall the coordinate system on
G
where each
y\in G
is uniquely expressed as
y=\exp\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}y_{r,i}X_{r,i}\right)
,
y_{r,i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{D}
and
x\in G
. The well-known Taylor formula tells us that for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

f(xy)=f(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\left.\frac{d^{j}}{dt^{j}}\right|_{t=0}f(xy^{t})+\frac{1}{n!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{n}\frac{d^{n+1}}{dt^{n+1}}f(xy^{t})dt.

As
y^{t}
is the one-parameter subgroup generated by
\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}y_{r,i}X_{r,i}
, we have

f(xy)=f(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}y_{r,i}X_{r,i}\right)^{j}f(x)+\frac{1}{n!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{n}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}y_{r,i}X_{r,i}\right)^{n+1}f(xy^{t})dt.

For each multi-index
\gamma
, if we denote by
\operatorname{Sym}(X^{\gamma})
the ‘coefficient operator’ of
y^{\gamma}
in
\frac{\gamma!}{j!}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{r}}y_{r,j}X_{r,j}\right)^{|\gamma|}
where
j=\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}\gamma_{r,i}
, then the above expression can be rewritten as

f(xy)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\mathrm{~multi-index}\\
\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}\gamma_{r,i}\leq n\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\gamma!}\left[\operatorname{Sym}(X^{\gamma})f(x)\right]y^{\gamma}+(n+1)\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\mathrm{~multi-index}\\
\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}\gamma_{r,i}=n+1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\gamma!}\left[\operatorname{Sym}(X^{\gamma})f(xy^{t})\right]y^{\gamma}dt.

By isolating the terms with multi-index
\gamma
such that
|\gamma|\leq n
, we see that there exist for each multi-index
\gamma
with
|\gamma|\geq n+1
and
\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}\gamma_{r,i}\leq n+1
a function
q_{x,\gamma}f\in\mathcal{D}
such that

f(xy)=\underbrace{\sum_{|\gamma|\leq n}\frac{1}{\gamma!}\left[\operatorname{Sym}(X^{\gamma})f(x)\right]y^{\gamma}}_{\eqqcolon T^{n}_{x}f(xy)}+\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma|\geq n+1\\
\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}\gamma_{r,i}\leq n+1\end{subarray}}q_{x,\gamma}f(y)y^{\gamma}}_{=o(d_{G}(y,e_{G})^{n})\mathrm{~as~}d_{G}(y,e_{G})\to 0},\quad y\in G,

where we denote the first sum to be the
Taylor polynomial of
f
of degree
n
at
x\in G
, while we observe the second term to be
o(d_{G}(y,e_{G})^{n})
as
d_{G}(y,e_{G})\to 0
because
d_{G}(y,e_{G})\asymp_{G}\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}|y_{r,i}|^{1/r}
.

We may observe that if
Y
is a left-invariant homogeneous differential operator of degree
d\leq n
, then

Yf(xy)=\underbrace{\sum_{|\gamma|\leq n}\frac{1}{\gamma!}\left[\operatorname{Sym}(X^{\gamma})f(x)\right]Yy^{\gamma}}_{=T^{n-d}_{x}Yf(xy)}+\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\gamma|\geq n+1\\
\sum_{r=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{r}}\gamma_{r,i}\leq n+1\end{subarray}}Y(q_{x,\gamma}f(y)y^{\gamma})}_{=o(d_{G}(y,e_{G})^{n-d})\mathrm{~as~}d_{G}(y,e_{G})\to 0},\quad y\in G,

with the second sum being
o(d_{G}(y,e_{G})^{n-d})
and the first sum being a polynomial of weighted degree at most
n-d
and hence is the Taylor polynomial of
Yf
of degree
n-d
at
x\in G
. This proves that

YT^{n}_{x}f=T^{n-d}_{x}Yf.

In particular, we have for the left-invariant horizontal vector fields that with
d\geq 1
,

X_{i}T^{d}_{x}f=T^{d-1}_{x}X_{i}f,\quad i=1,\cdots,k.

We may define the
\beta
-numbers and
\mathfrak{G}
-function using the Taylor polynomial instead.

\tilde{\beta}_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))\coloneqq\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-T^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}_{x,r}f|,

and

\tilde{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}f(x)\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\tilde{\beta}_{f,\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}(B_{r}(x))\right)^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2},\quad x\in G.

Proposition 48
.

If
f\in\mathcal{D}
and
\alpha
is nonintegral, then
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)\asymp_{G,\alpha}\tilde{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}f(x)
for all
x\in G
.

Proof.

That
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)\lesssim_{G,\alpha}\tilde{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}f(x)
follows from Lemma
46
.
Denote
d=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
and

A^{d}_{x,r}f(xy)=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq d}f_{\gamma}(x,r)y^{\gamma},

then

f_{\gamma}(x,r)=\frac{1}{\gamma!}\operatorname{Sym}(X^{\gamma})A^{d}_{x,r}f(x).

Also denote

T^{d}_{x}f(xy)=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq d}f_{\gamma}(x)y^{\gamma},

then, in the case
f\in\mathcal{D}
,
f_{\gamma}(x,r)\to f_{\gamma}(x)
as
r\to 0
by Taylor expansion. Indeed, recalling that
f(xy)=T^{d}_{x}f(xy)+O_{f}(d_{G}(y,e_{G})^{d+1})
,

\|A^{d}_{x,r}f-T^{d}_{x}f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}=\|A^{d}_{x,r}[f-T^{d}_{x}f]\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{claim}}}{{\lesssim}}_{G,d}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-T^{d}_{x}f|=O_{G,d,f}(r^{d+1})\quad\mathrm{as~}r\to 0.

By the above expansions for
A^{d}_{x,r}f
and
T^{d}_{x}f
, we have

\|\sum_{|\gamma|\leq d}(f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x))r^{|\gamma|}y^{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}=\|\sum_{|\gamma|\leq d}(f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x))y^{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}=O_{G,d,f}(r^{d+1}).

But because the space
\mathcal{A}^{d}
of polynomials of weighted degree
\leq d
normed by
L^{\infty}(B_{1})
is finite dimensional, all norms are equivalent, and hence

\max_{|\gamma|\leq d}r^{|\gamma|}|f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x)|=O_{G,d,f}(r^{d+1}).

This completes the proof of the convergence
f_{\gamma}(x,r)\to f_{\gamma}(x)
as
r\to 0
.

With this, we provide a different bound on
|f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x)|
using the
\beta
numbers. For each
x\in G
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x)|&\leq\sum_{i=-\infty}^{0}|f_{\gamma}(x,2^{i}r)-f_{\gamma}(x,2^{i-1}r)|\\
&=\frac{1}{\gamma!}\sum_{i=-\infty}^{0}\left|\operatorname{Sym}(X^{\gamma})(A^{d}_{x,2^{i}r}f-A^{d}_{x,2^{i-1}r}f)(x)\right|\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{polydiff}}}{{\lesssim_{G,|\gamma|}}}\sum_{i=-\infty}^{0}2^{-i|\gamma|}r^{-|\gamma|}\fint_{B_{2^{i-1}r}(x)}|f-A^{d}_{x,2^{i}r}f|\\
&=\ \sum_{i=-\infty}^{0}2^{-i|\gamma|}r^{-|\gamma|}\beta_{f,d}(B_{2^{i}r}(x))\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Corollary}~\ref{monotoneBeta}}}{{\lesssim_{G,d}}}\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{u}(x))u^{-|\gamma|}\frac{du}{u}.\end{aligned}

(57)

(With the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality, we may prove that
|f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x)|\lesssim_{G,\alpha}r^{\alpha-|\gamma|}\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)
for
|\gamma|<\alpha
; see Proposition
54
for the computation. This is weaker than the
O_{G,d,f}(r^{d+1-|\gamma|})
bound we get using Taylor approximation. This is because the Taylor approximation argument leverages on the fact that
f
has
(d+1)
and higher derivatives, whereas the
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f
(in principle) only measures the “
\alpha
-th derivative” of
f
at
x
; however, the latter approach is more natural since we have to work with
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f
.)

Now we bound

\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))

\displaystyle=\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-T^{d}_{x}f|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|f-A^{d}_{x,r}f|+\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|T^{d}_{x}f-A^{d}_{x,r}f|

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))+C\sum_{|\gamma|\leq d}|f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x)|r^{|\gamma|}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))+C\sum_{i=0}^{d}r^{i}\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{u}(x))u^{-i}\frac{du}{u},

where
C
is a constant depending on
G
and
d
.
By plugging into the definition of
\tilde{\mathfrak{G}}
and using the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}f(x)\leq\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)+C\sum_{i=0}^{d}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[r^{i-\alpha}\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{u}(x))u^{-i}\frac{du}{u}\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}.

But by Hardy’s inequality (
23
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathfrak{G}}_{\alpha}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)+C\sum_{i=0}^{d}\frac{1}{\alpha-i}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{\beta_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))}{r^{\alpha}}\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{G,\alpha}\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x).

This is where we use the fact that
\alpha
is nonintegral.
∎

7.2.3.
Inhomogeneous estimates to homogeneous estimates

We provide one more reduction in the proof of Theorem
22
. It is enough to prove the weaker estimate

\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim_{G,p,\alpha}\|f\|_{p,\alpha}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}+\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}(G)},\quad f\in\mathcal{D}.

(In fact, the inequality Dorronsoro proves in
[
Dor85
, Theorem 2]
is

\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}+\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\asymp_{\alpha,p}\|f\|_{p,\alpha}

when
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.) This is because a dimensional analysis reveals that the above inequality is inhomogeneous, so by exploiting scaling properties we may remove the inhomogeneous term
\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}
.

Lemma 49
.

For
1<p<\infty
and
\alpha>0
, if we have the estimate,

\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{G,p,\alpha}\|f\|_{p,\alpha},\quad f\in\mathcal{D},

(58)

then with the same constant
C_{G,p,\alpha}
,

\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C_{G,p,\alpha}\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}(G)},\quad f\in\mathcal{D}.

(59)

Proof.

Given
f\in\mathcal{D}
, set
f_{s}\coloneqq f\circ\delta_{s}\in\mathcal{D}
, for
s>0
, where
\delta_{s}
is the Carnot group dilation. It is easy to see that

\begin{cases}\|f_{s}\|_{L^{p}({G})}=s^{-n_{h}/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}({G})},\\
\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f_{s}\|_{L^{p}(G)}=s^{\alpha-n_{h}/p}\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)},\end{cases}\quad s>0.

Also, it is morally clear that we should have

\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}f_{s}\|_{L^{p}(G)}=s^{\alpha-n_{h}/p}\|(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}f\|_{L^{p}(G)},\quad s>0,

and one may indeed verify this using the definition of
(-\Delta_{p})^{\alpha/2}
; see the proof of
[
FO20a
, Lemma 2.6]
for details.

We now obtain (
59
) for
f
from (
58
) for
f_{s}
and taking
s\to\infty
.
∎

The case

We now begin proving Theorem

Fix

and

(Analogue of

.

Let

Let

7.2.6.
Interpolation and the case
\alpha
integral

The case
\alpha=d\in\mathbb{Z}
is proven by complex interpolation. This subsubsection follows Section 5 of
[
FO20a
]
closely.

For
1<p<\infty
and
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\infty
, the pair
(S^{p}_{\alpha_{0}},S^{p}_{\alpha_{1}})
of Banach spaces is an interpolation pair in the sense of Calderón, as they embed continuously in the space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
of tempered distributions on
G
. Thus, we may define the complex interpolation space
[S^{p}_{\alpha_{0}},S^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}]_{\theta}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
.

The following lemma asserts that we have a continuous embedding

S^{p}_{(1-\theta)\alpha_{0}+\theta\alpha_{1}}\hookrightarrow[S^{p}_{\alpha_{0}},S^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}]_{\theta}.

Lemma 53

(
[
FO20a
, Lemma 5.1]
)
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\infty
, and
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then

\|f\|_{[S^{p}_{\alpha_{0}},S^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}]_{\theta}}\lesssim_{G,\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},p}\|f\|_{p,(1-\theta)\alpha_{0}+\theta\alpha_{1}}.

The proof is exactly the same as in
[
FO20a
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Next, we define, for
\alpha>0
, the Banach space
H_{\alpha}
of functions
F:B_{1}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
with norm

\|F\|_{H_{\alpha}}\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\fint_{B_{1}}|F(y,r)|dy\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

Then, for
1<p<\infty
, we may define the function space
L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha})
. Now, for
0<\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{1}<\infty
,

(L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{0}}),L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{1}}))

is a compatible couple, as both embed continuously into
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(G\times B_{1}\times(0,\infty))
. As in
[
Dor85
,
FO20a
]
, we apply the results of
[
BL12
, p. 107, 121]
so that for
\theta\in(0,1)
,

[L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{0}}),L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{1}})]_{\theta}=L^{p}(G,[H_{\alpha_{0}},H_{\alpha_{1}}]_{\theta})=L^{p}(G,H_{(1-\theta)\alpha_{0}+\theta\alpha_{1}}).

Now, for
d-1<\alpha_{0}<d<\alpha_{1}<d+1
(recall
d=\alpha
) and
\theta\in(0,1)
, consider the linear map
T:S^{p}_{\alpha_{0}}+S^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}\to L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{0}})+L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{1}})
given by

f\mapsto Tf(x,r,y)=f(x\delta_{r}(y))-A^{d}_{x,r}(x\delta_{r}(y)).

By Lemma
46
and Theorem
22
for
\alpha\in(d-1,d)\cup(d,d+1)
, we have

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha})}\lesssim_{G,d}\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim_{G,\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},p}\|f\|_{p,\alpha},\quad f\in S^{p}_{\alpha},~\alpha=\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},

i.e.,
T:S^{p}_{\alpha_{0}}\to L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{0}})
and
T:S^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}\to L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{1}})
are bounded. By complex interpolation,

T:[S^{p}_{\alpha_{0}},S^{p}_{\alpha_{1}}]_{\theta}\to[L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{0}}),L^{p}(G,H_{\alpha_{1}})]_{\theta}=L^{p}(G,H_{(1-\theta)\alpha_{0}+\theta\alpha_{1}})

is bounded.

Now set
\alpha_{0}=d-1/2
,
\alpha_{1}=d+1/2
, and
\theta=1/2
. Then

\|\mathfrak{G}_{d}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}=\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(G,H_{d})}\lesssim_{G,d}\|f\|_{[S^{p}_{d-1/2},S^{p}_{d+1/2}]_{1/2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Lemma~}\ref{lem:interpolation}}}{{\lesssim_{G,d,p}}}\|f\|_{p,d},\quad f\in\mathcal{D}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
22
for
\alpha=d\in\mathbb{Z}
.

7.3.
Proof of the
\gtrsim

Now, given a
f\in L^{p}(G)

Proposition 54
.

Let

and
\alpha>0
with
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(G)
. Denote

A^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}_{x,r}f(xy)=\sum_{|\gamma|\leq\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}f_{\gamma}(x,r)y^{\gamma},

Then

(1)

For each multi-index
\gamma
with
|\gamma|<\alpha

|f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x,s)|\lesssim_{G,\alpha}r^{\alpha-|\gamma|}\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)

and

, where

denotes the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator. In particular,

with
\|f_{\gamma}(\cdot,r)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim_{G,\alpha}\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}
.

(2)

For each multi-index
\gamma
with
|\gamma|<\alpha

f_{\gamma}\in L^{p}(G)
such that
f_{\gamma}(\cdot,r)\to f_{\gamma}(\cdot)
as

, satisfying

|f_{\gamma}(x,r)-f_{\gamma}(x)|\lesssim_{G,|\gamma|}\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{u}(x))u^{-|\gamma|}\frac{du}{u}\lesssim_{G,\alpha}r^{\alpha-|\gamma|}\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)

for a.e.

and

.

(3)

If
\alpha

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\fint_{B_{r}}|f(xy)-\sum_{|\gamma|\leq\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}f_{\gamma}(x)y^{\gamma}|dy\right)^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}\asymp_{G,\alpha}\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x),\quad\mathrm{a.e.~}x\in G,

(4)

If
\alpha
is integral and
|\gamma|=\alpha
, then
|f_{\gamma}(x,r)|\lesssim_{G,\alpha}\log(2/r)\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f(x)+Mf(x)
,
0<r<1
. Thus,
f_{\gamma}(\cdot,r)\in L^{p}(G)
with
\|f_{\gamma}(\cdot,r)\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim_{G,\alpha}\log(2/r)\|\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}
.

(5)

If
0<\alpha<1
, then
f_{0}(x)=f(x)
for a.e.
x\in G
.

Proof.

(1)

By repeating the computation (
57
) made in the proof of Proposition

, it follows that

In particular, by Cauchy–Schwartz,

Also, for
0<r<1
,

, where the latter inequality used the fact that

is given as the integral of

(2)

.

By plugging into the definition of
\tilde{\mathfrak{G}}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\fint_{B_{r}}|f(xy)-\sum_{|\gamma|\leq\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}f_{\gamma}(x)y^{\gamma}|dy\right)^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}

(4)

be a sequence of balls. We have

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{2^{-k}r}(z_{k})}|f-A_{z_{0},r}^{d}f|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{B_{2^{-k}r}(z_{k})}|f-A_{z_{k},2^{-k}r}^{d}f|+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\fint_{B_{2^{-k}r}(z_{k})}|A_{z_{i},2^{-i}r}^{d}f-A_{z_{i+1},2^{-i-1}r}^{d}f|

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{B_{2^{-k}r}(z_{k})}|f-A_{z_{k},2^{-k}r}^{d}f|+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left\|A_{z_{i+1},2^{-i-1}r}^{d}(f-A_{z_{i},2^{-i}r}^{d}f)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2^{-i-1}r}(z_{i+1}))}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{claim}}}{{\lesssim_{G,d}}}\fint_{B_{2^{-k}r}(z_{k})}|f-A_{z_{k},2^{-k}r}^{d}f|+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\fint_{B_{2^{-i-1}r}(z_{i+1})}|f-A_{z_{i},2^{-i}r}^{d}f|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{G}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\fint_{B_{2^{-i}r}(z_{i})}|f-A^{d}_{z_{i},2^{-i}r}f|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Corollary~}\ref{monotoneBeta}}}{{\lesssim_{G,d}}}\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{s}(y))\frac{ds}{s}

so by Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem for doubling metric measure spaces, for a.e.
y\in B_{r}(x)
,

|f(y)-A_{x,r}^{d}f(y)|=\lim_{B_{2^{-k}r}(z_{k})\to y}\fint_{B_{2^{-k}r}(z_{k})}|f-A_{z_{0},r}^{d}f|\lesssim_{G,d}\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{s}(y))\frac{ds}{s}.

As the operator
\Delta_{h}^{2,\mathrm{sym}}
annihilates polynomials of weighted degree
1
, we obtain for a.e.
x\in G

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r}}|\Delta_{h}^{2,\mathrm{sym}}f(x)|dh=\fint_{B_{r}}|\Delta_{h}^{2,\mathrm{sym}}(f-A_{x,2r}^{1}f)(x)|dh

\displaystyle=\fint_{B_{r}}|(f-A_{x,2r}^{1}f)(xh)+(f-A_{x,2r}^{1}f)(xh^{-1})-2(f-A_{x,2r}^{1}f)(x)|dh

\displaystyle\lesssim_{G,d}\fint_{B_{r}}\left(\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{s}(xh))\frac{ds}{s}+\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{s}(xh^{-1}))\frac{ds}{s}+2\int_{0}^{2r}\beta_{f,d}(B_{s}(x))\frac{ds}{s}\right)dh

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{2r}M\beta_{f,1}(B_{s}(\cdot))(x)\frac{ds}{s}.

By Hardy’s inequality (
23
) with
\nu=\frac{3}{2}
and
p=2
, we obtain

S_{1}f(x)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[r^{-1}\fint_{B_{r}}|\Delta_{y}^{2,\mathrm{sym}}f(x)|dy\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim_{G,d}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[M\beta_{f,1}(B_{r}(\cdot))(x)\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}.

Now we argue that

\left(\int_{G}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[M\beta_{f,1}(B_{r}(\cdot))(x)\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}\lesssim_{G}\max\{p,\frac{1}{p-1}\}\left(\int_{G}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\beta_{f,1}(B_{r}(\cdot))(x)\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

This is an instance of the Fefferman–Stein vector-valued maximal function inequality

\left(\int_{G}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}M(g_{j})^{u}(x)\right]^{v/u}dx\right)^{1/v}\lesssim_{G}\max\{1,\frac{1}{u-1}\}\max\{v,\frac{1}{v-1}\}\left(\int_{G}\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{j}(x)^{u}\right]^{v/u}dx\right)^{1/v}

(61)

on
n_{h}
-regular metric measure spaces for measurable functions
(g_{j}:G\to\mathbb{R})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
u,v\in(1,\infty)
, due to
[
GLY09
, Theorem 1.2]
. Indeed, we apply (
61
) to the functions

g_{j}(x)=\beta_{f,1}(B_{2^{-j}}(x))

and exponents
u=2>1
and
v=p>1
, noting by Corollary
47
that

M(\beta_{f,1}(B_{r}(\cdot)))\lesssim_{G}M(g_{j}),\quad r\in[2^{-j-1},2^{-j}],

and

g_{j}\lesssim_{G}\beta_{f,1}(B_{r}(\cdot)),\quad r\in[2^{-j},2^{-j+1}],

Thus, we have
\|S_{1}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}\lesssim_{G,p}\|\mathfrak{G}_{1}f\|_{L^{p}(G)}
,
1<p<\infty
, as desired.

Lemma 57

(Analogue of
[
Dor85
, Lemma 2]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(G)
with
\mathfrak{G}_{\alpha}f\in L^{p}(G)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(xe_{1,i}^{r})-f(x)}{r}-f_{(1,i)}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|f(xe_{1,i}^{r})-f_{0}(xe_{1,i}^{r},2r)|}{r}+\frac{|f(x)-f_{0}(x,2r)|}{r}+\left|\frac{f_{0}(xe_{1,i}^{r},2r)-f_{0}(x,2r)}{r}-f_{(1,i)}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{4r}s^{-\eta}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}M_{\eta}(\beta_{f,d}(B_{2s}(\cdot))\chi_{B_{5r}(x)}(\cdot))(y)^{q}dy\right)^{1/q}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p,q}\int_{0}^{4r}s^{-\eta}\left(\int_{B_{5r}(x)}\beta_{f,d}(B_{2s}(z))^{w}dz\right)^{1/w}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{G}\int_{0}^{4r}s^{-\eta}r^{n_{h}/w}\left[M(\beta_{f,d}(B_{2s}(\cdot))^{w})(x)\right]^{1/w}\frac{ds}{s},

where in the second inequality we used Minkowski’s inequality and in the fourth inequality we used the boundedness of
M_{\eta}:L^{w}(G)\to L^{q}(G)
. By (
64
), we have

\beta_{f,d,q}(B_{r}(x))\lesssim_{G,\alpha,p,q}r^{\eta}\int_{0}^{4r}s^{-\eta-1}\left[M(\beta_{f,d}(B_{2s}(\cdot))^{w})(x)\right]^{1/w}ds.

Next, noting that
\alpha>\eta
and using Hardy’s inequality (
23
) with
\nu=\alpha-\eta+1/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\beta_{f,d,q}(B_{r}(x))\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{G,\alpha,p,q}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2\eta-2\alpha-1}\left[\int_{0}^{4r}s^{-\eta-1}\left[M(\beta_{f,d}(B_{2s}(\cdot))^{w})(x)\right]^{1/w}ds\right]^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,q}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[M(r^{-w\alpha}\beta_{f,d}(B_{2r}(\cdot))^{w})(x)\right]^{2/w}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{1/2}.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{G}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\beta_{f,d,q}(B_{r}(x))\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{G,\alpha,p,q}\left(\int_{G}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[M(r^{-w\alpha}\beta_{f,d}(B_{2r}(\cdot))^{w})(x)\right]^{2/w}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{G,\alpha,p,q}\left(\int_{G}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}\beta_{f,d}(B_{r}(x))\right]^{2}\frac{dr}{r}\right)^{p/2}dx\right)^{1/p},

where in the second inequality we applied the Fefferman–Stein vector-valued maximal function inequality (
61
) with functions

g_{j}(x)=2^{\alpha wj}\beta_{f,d}(B_{2^{-j+1}}(x))

and exponents
u=2/w>1
and
v=p/w>1
, along with Corollary
47
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
22
.

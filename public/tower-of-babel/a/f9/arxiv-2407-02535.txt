Eccentricity and Algebraic Connectivity of Graphs
B. Afshari , M. Afshari

Abstract
Let G G be a graph on n n nodes with algebraic connectivity λ 2 \lambda_{2} . The eccentricity of a node is defined as the length of a longest shortest path starting at that node. If s ℓ s_{\ell} denotes the number of nodes of eccentricity at most ℓ \ell , then for ℓ ≥ 2 \ell\geq 2 , λ 2 ≥ 4 ​ s ℓ ( ℓ − 2 + 4 n ) ​ n 2 . \lambda_{2}\geq\frac{4\,s_{\ell}}{(\ell-2+\frac{4}{n})\,n^{2}}. As a corollary, if d d denotes the diameter of G G , then λ 2 ≥ 4 ( d − 2 + 4 n ) ​ n . \lambda_{2}\geq\frac{4}{(d-2+\frac{4}{n})\,n}. It is also shown that λ 2 ≥ s ℓ 1 + ℓ ⁡ ( e ⁡ ( G ℓ ) − m ) , \lambda_{2}\geq\frac{s_{\ell}}{1+\ell\left(e(G^{\ell})-m\right)}, where m m and e ⁡ ( G ℓ ) e(G^{\ell}) denote the number of edges in G G and in the ℓ \ell -th power of G G , respectively.

Keywords

Let
G
be a simple graph on
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
with
m
edges
E
.
The
distance
between nodes
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
, denoted by
\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},v_{j})
, is the number of edges in a shortest path joining them.
If there is no such path, then we define this value to be
\infty
.
The
diameter
of
G
, denoted by
d
is the maximum distance between any pair of nodes of
G
.
The
\ell
-th
power
of
G
, denoted by
G^{\ell}
, is the graph with the same node set as
G
such that two nodes are adjacent in
G^{\ell}
if and only if their distance is at most
\ell
in
G
.
The
eccentricity
of a node in
G
is defined as the length of a longest shortest path starting at that node.
We denote the number of nodes of eccentricity at most
\ell
by
s_{\ell}
.

The Laplacian matrix of
G
is defined as
L(G)=D-A
, where
A
is the adjacency matrix of
G
and
D
is a diagonal degree matrix with
D(i,i)=\sum_{j}A(i,j)
.
The smallest eigenvalue of
L(G)
is 0, where the corresponding eigenvector is the all one vector.
The second smallest eigenvalue of
L(G)
, denoted by
\lambda_{2}
, is called the
algebraic connectivity
of
G
.
It is easy to see that the number of nodes with degree
n-1
is a lower bound for this parameter, that is,

\lambda_{2}\geq s_{1}.

Also it is known that
[
1
]

\lambda_{2}\geq\frac{s_{2}}{n}.

Here it is shown that for
\ell\geq 3
,

\lambda_{2}\geq\frac{4\,s_{\ell}}{(\ell-2+\frac{4}{n})n^{2}}.

(1)

This result can be viewed as a generalization, with a slight improvement, of the well-known bound
[
3
]
:

\lambda_{2}\geq\frac{4}{dn},

as setting
\ell=d
in (
1
) leads to
s_{d}=n
, and yields

\lambda_{2}\geq\frac{4}{(d-2+\frac{4}{n})n}.

It is also shown that

\lambda_{2}\geq\frac{s_{\ell}}{1+\ell\left(e(G^{\ell})-m\right)},

(2)

where
e(G^{\ell})
denotes the number of edges in
G^{\ell}
.
This also generalizes the known result
[
2
]
:

\lambda_{2}\geq\frac{n}{1+d\,e({\overline{G}})}.

Proofs

Proof of (
1
).

If
G
is not connected, then
s_{\ell}=0
, and the statement holds trivially.
Therefore, we assume that
G
in connected.
Let
\gamma=(\ell-2+\frac{4}{n})\frac{n^{2}}{4}
.
It is sufficient to show that
M=\gamma L(G)-L(G^{\ell})
is positive semi-definite.
Because if so, assuming that
z
is the unit eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{2}
, we can deduce (
1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=z^{T}L(G)z\geq\frac{1}{\gamma}z^{T}L(G^{\ell})z\geq\frac{1}{\gamma}\lambda_{2}(G^{\ell})\geq\frac{1}{\gamma}s_{1}(G^{\ell})=\frac{1}{\gamma}s_{\ell}.

Assume, to the contrary, that the minimum eigenvalue of
M
, denoted by
\mu
, is negative, and let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
be an eigenvector corresponding to
\mu
.
Since the all one vector is an eigenvector corresponding to eigenvalue 0 of
M
, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=0
.
We may assume that

x_{n}\leq x_{n-1}\leq\ldots\leq x_{1}.

For
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
, we define
E_{k}
as the set of edges
v_{i}v_{j}
in
G
where
i\leq k<j
.
Let
k
be chosen such that

\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E_{k}}|x_{i}-x_{j}|

is maximized, with the assumption that this sum equals 1 without loss of generality.
So, in particular, every edge
v_{i}v_{j}
in
G
satisfies
|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq 1
. We proceed to show that

\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\ell}):\,i\leq k<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq\gamma.

(3)

Let
S=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{k}\}
.
For
r\geq 1
, we define
A_{r}
as the set of nodes in
S
whose shortest paths to
V\setminus S
have length
r
.
Similarly,
B_{r}
is the set of nodes in
V\setminus S
whose shortest paths to
S
have length
r
.
If
\mathrm{dist}(v_{i},v_{j})=q
for some
i\leq k<j
, then
v_{i}\in A_{r}
and
v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}
for some
r,r^{\prime}\geq 1
where
r+r^{\prime}=q+1
.
Therefore,

\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\ell}):\,i\leq k<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq\sum_{r,r^{\prime}\geq 1:\,r+r^{\prime}\leq\ell+1}\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r},v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}}(x_{i}-x_{j}).

Let
r,r^{\prime}\geq 2
.
Let
s
be the smallest number such that
v_{s}\in A_{r-1}
and
t
be the largest number such that
v_{t}\in B_{r^{\prime}-1}
.
We have

\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r},v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}}(x_{i}-x_{j})\leq\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r},v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}}(x_{s}-x_{t})+\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r}}\sum_{v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}:\,j>t}(x_{t}-x_{j})+\sum_{v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}}\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r}:\,i<s}(x_{i}-x_{s}).

There is a path
P
of length
r-1
from
v_{s}
to a node in
V\setminus S
, and a path
P^{\prime}
of length
r^{\prime}-1
from
v_{t}
to a node in
S
.
Both paths
P
and
P^{\prime}
have an edge in
E_{k}
.
Therefore, we conclude:

\displaystyle x_{s}-x_{t}\leq\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(P)\cup E(P^{\prime})}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq r+r^{\prime}-3.

Moreover, for each
v_{i}\in A_{r}
with
i<s
, there is some edge
v_{i}v_{j}
with
j\geq s
in
G
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r}:\,i<s}(x_{i}-x_{s})\leq\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E_{s-1}}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq 1.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}:\,j>t}(x_{t}-x_{j})\leq\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E_{t}}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq 1.

In consequence,

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r},v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}}(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle\leq|A_{r}||B_{r^{\prime}}|(r+r^{\prime}-3)+|A_{r}|+|B_{r^{\prime}}|.

For each
v_{j}\in B_{1}
, let
v_{j^{\prime}}
be the node adjacent to
v_{j}
in a shortest path from
v_{s}
to
v_{j}
.
Also, denote by
P_{sj^{\prime}}
a shortest path from
v_{s}
to
v_{j^{\prime}}
.
We then have:

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r},v_{j}\in B_{1}}(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v_{j}\in B_{1}}\big(\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r}}(x_{s}-x_{j})+\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{r}:\,i<s}(x_{i}-x_{s})\big)

\displaystyle\leq|A_{r}|\sum_{v_{j}\in B_{1}}\big((x_{s}-x_{j^{\prime}})+(x_{j^{\prime}}-x_{j})\big)+|B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq|A_{r}|\sum_{v_{j}\in B_{1}}\sum_{v_{a}v_{b}\in P_{sj^{\prime}}}|x_{a}-x_{b}|+|A_{r}|\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E_{k}}|x_{i}-x_{j}|+|B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq|A_{r}||B_{1}|(r-2)+|A_{r}|+|B_{1}|.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{1},v_{j}\in B_{r^{\prime}}}(x_{i}-x_{j})

\displaystyle\leq|A_{1}||B_{r^{\prime}}|(r^{\prime}-2)+|A_{1}|+|B_{r^{\prime}}|.

Let
A_{1}=\{v_{t_{1}},v_{t_{2}},\ldots,v_{t_{p}}\}
and
B_{1}=\{v_{t_{p+1}},v_{t_{p+2}},\ldots,v_{t_{q}}\}
where
t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{q}
.
Note that for
i=1,2,\ldots,q-1
, we have

|E_{k}\cap E_{t_{i}}|\geq\min\{i,q-i\},

as there are edges in
E_{k}
with end nodes
v_{t_{1}},v_{t_{2}},\ldots,v_{t_{i}}
if
i\leq p
, and
v_{t_{i+1}},v_{t_{i+2}},\ldots,v_{t_{q}}
if
i\geq p+1
.
Suppose the vector
y
is obtained from
x
by translation such that
y_{t_{\lceil q/2\rceil}}
becomes zero.
This leads to,

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{1},v_{j}\in B_{1}}(x_{i}-x_{j})=\sum_{v_{i}\in A_{1},v_{j}\in B_{1}}(y_{i}-y_{j})\leq\sum_{i<j}|y_{t_{i}}-y_{t_{j}}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\sum_{j=1}^{q}|y_{t_{i}}-y_{t_{j}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\big(|y_{t_{i}}|+|y_{t_{j}}|\big)

\displaystyle=q\sum_{i=1}^{q}|y_{t_{i}}|

\displaystyle=q\big(\sum_{i\leq q/2}y_{t_{i}}-\sum_{i>q/2}y_{t_{i}}\big)

\displaystyle=q\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}\min\{i,q-i\}(y_{t_{i}}-y_{t_{i+1}})

\displaystyle\leq q\sum_{i=1}^{q-1}|E_{k}\cap E_{t_{i}}|(y_{t_{i}}-y_{t_{i+1}})

\displaystyle=q\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E_{k}}|y_{i}-y_{j}|\leq q.

Now, we can deduce that,

\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\ell}):\,i\leq k<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq|A_{1}|+|B_{1}|+\sum_{r,r^{\prime}\geq 1:\,3\leq r+r^{\prime}\leq\ell+1}{(r+r^{\prime}-3)|A_{r}||B_{r^{\prime}}|+|A_{r}|+|B_{r^{\prime}}|}.

The last term is bounded by the maximum possible value of

a_{1}+b_{1}+\sum_{r,r^{\prime}\geq 1:\,3\leq r+r^{\prime}\leq\ell+1}{(r+r^{\prime}-3)a_{r}b_{r^{\prime}}+a_{r}+b_{r^{\prime}}},

where
a_{r},b_{r}\geq 0
for each
r
, subject to the constraints
\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}a_{r}=k
and
\sum_{r^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}b_{r^{\prime}}=n-k
.
By fixing the
b_{r^{\prime}}
values, this term reaches its maximum when
a_{t}=k
for some
t
and
a_{r}=0
for
r\neq t
.
Subsequently, after fixing the values for
a_{r}
’s, the term is maximized when
b_{\ell-t+1}=n-k
and
b_{r^{\prime}}=0
for
r^{\prime}\neq\ell-t+1
.
Therefore, the inequality (
3
) is derived as:

\displaystyle\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\ell}):\,i\leq k<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq(\ell-2)k(n-k)+n\leq(\ell-2)\frac{n^{2}}{4}+n=\gamma.

Now, if
e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the
i
-th standard basis vector, then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E_{k}}|x_{i}-x_{j}|-\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\ell}):\,i\leq k<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\leq k}\big(\gamma\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E:\,j>k}{(x_{i}-x_{j})}-\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\ell}):\,j>k}{(x_{i}-x_{j})}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\leq k}\big(\gamma\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E}{(x_{i}-x_{j})}-\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\ell})}{(x_{i}-x_{j})}\big)

\displaystyle=(e_{1}+e_{2}+\ldots+e_{k})^{T}Mx

\displaystyle=\mu\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}

\displaystyle<0,

which is a contradiction.
Therefore, the proof is now complete.
∎

Proof of (
2
).

We need to show that
\left(1+\ell\left(e(G^{\ell})-m\right)\right)L(G)-L(G^{\ell})
is positive semi-definite.
Consider a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
v_{r}v_{s}\in E(G^{\ell})
be such that
|x_{r}-x_{s}|
is maximum.
Denote by
P
a shortest path between
v_{r}
and
v_{s}
.
We then have:

\displaystyle x^{T}L(G^{\ell})x

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G^{\ell})}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq x^{T}L(G)x+\left(e(G^{\ell})-m\right)(x_{r}-x_{s})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq x^{T}L(G)x+\ell\left(e(G^{\ell})-m\right)\sum_{v_{i}v_{j}\in P}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\ell\left(e(G^{\ell})-m\right)\right)x^{T}L(G)x.

This completes the proof. ∎

A first-principles thermodynamic model for the Ba−Zr−S system in equilibrium with sulfur vapour
Prakriti Kayastha, Giulia Longo, Lucy D. Whalley

Abstract
The chalcogenide perovskite BaZrS 3 \text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} has strong visible light absorption and high chemical stability, is nontoxic, and is made from earth-abundant elements. As such, it is a promising candidate material for application in optoelectronic technologies. However the synthesis of BaZrS 3 \text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} thin-films for characterisation and device integration remains a challenge. Here we use density functional theory and lattice dynamics to calculate the vibrational properties of elemental, binary and ternary materials in the Ba-Zr-S system. This is used to build a thermodynamic model for the stability of BaZrS 3 \text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} , BaS x \text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}} , and ZrS x \text{ZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}} in equilibrium with sulfur gas, across a range of temperatures and sulfur partial pressures. We highlight that reaction thermodynamics are highly sensitive to sulfur allotrope and the extent of allotrope mixing. We use our model to predict the synthesis conditions in which BaZrS 3 \text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} and the intermediate compound BaS 3 \text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} , which is associated with fast reaction kinetics, can form. At a moderate temperature of 500 °C 500\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius} we find that BaS 3 \text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} is stable at pressures above 3 × 10 5 Pa 3\text{\times}{10}^{5}\text{\,}\mathrm{Pa} . We also find BaZrS 3 \text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} is stable against decomposition into sulfur-rich binaries up to at least 1 × 10 7 Pa 1\text{\times}{10}^{7}\text{\,}\mathrm{Pa} . Our work provides insights into the chemistry of this promising material and suggests the experimental conditions required for the successful synthesis of BaZrS 3 \text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} .

2
Methodology

2.1
Classical thermodynamics

We use classical thermodynamics to evaluate the stability of materials through the balance of free energies.
The feasibility of a spontaneous reaction at temperature
T
and total pressure
P
is determined by the change in Gibbs free energy
\Delta G
:

\Delta G_{\mathrm{f}}(T,P)=\sum_{\mathrm{products}}n_{i}\mu_{i}(T,p_{i})-\sum_{\mathrm{reactants}}n_{i}\mu_{i}(T,p_{i}),

(1)

where
n_{i}
is the absolute change in stoichiometry,
\mu_{i}
is the chemical potential and
p_{i}
is the partial pressure of each product or reactant.
For reactions with
\Delta G<0
, the reaction is energy releasing and thermodynamically favourable.
We note that meeting this condition is not necessarily sufficient for forming a given product over a reasonable timescale; kinetic factors may inhibit the reaction.

We assume that solids are incompressible and consider vibrational contributions to entropy only, giving the following expression for Gibbs free energy at finite temperature and pressure:
Jackson and Walsh 2013

\mu_{i}(T,P)=E^{\text{DFT}}+E^{\text{ZP}}+\int_{0}^{T}C_{p}(T)\text{d}T+PV\\
-TS_{\mathrm{vib.}}(T)

(2)

Density functional theory (DFT) is used to calculate the total electronic energy
E^{\text{DFT}}
and equilibrium volume
V
at
0\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
and
0\text{\,}\mathrm{Pa}
. We use first-principles lattice dynamics to calculate the zero point energy
E^{\text{ZP}}
, vibrational entropy
S_{\mathrm{vib.}}(T)
and heat capacity
C_{p}(T)
. Note that
C_{p}=C_{v}
for incompressible solids.

We assume that
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}
follow the ideal gas law, giving:
Jackson and Walsh 2013

\mu_{i}(T,p_{i})=E^{\text{DFT}}+E^{\text{ZP}}+[H^{\theta}-H^{0}]-\int_{T^{\theta}}^{T}C_{p}(T,p_{i})\text{d}T\\
+RT\text{ln}[p_{i}/p_{i}^{\theta}]-TS(T,p_{i}^{\theta}).

(3)

The NIST-JANAF thermochemical data tables provide experimental data for the standard enthalpy
[H^{\theta}-H^{0}]
,
C_{p}(T,p_{i})
and
S(T,p_{i}^{\theta})
.
Chase 1998

\theta
denotes the reference state used in the tables (
T=
298\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
,
P=
1\text{\times}{10}^{5}\text{\,}\mathrm{Pa}
).
To compare calculated
\mu
values across sulfur allotropes, we report
\mu
on a per-atom basis, i.e.
\mu_{S_{8}}/8
for
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}
and
\mu_{S_{2}}/2
for
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

To account for allotrope mixing, we applied a published first-principles model for the chemical potential of sulfur gas.
Jackson et al. 2016
This model considers 13 low-energy allotropes of sulfur (
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
-
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}
) and is parameterised using first-principles lattice dynamics with the hybrid PBE0 functional.
Adamo and Barone 1999

We evaluated and visualised thermodynamic potentials and sulfur model using our in-house code
ThermoPot
.
Kayastha et al. 2022
This analysis is available in an online repository.
Kayasatha and Whalley 2023

ThermoPot
is a general-purpose and material-agnostic code which post-processes first-principles data.
We identified low energy materials in the Ba-Zr-S system using total energies reported on the Materials Project.
Jain et al. 2013

More details on the database search are provided in the Supplementary Information.

2.2
Quantum chemical calculations

First-principles calculations were carried out with the all-electron numerical atom-centered orbital code FHI-aims
Blum et al. 2009
. Equilibrium crystal structures were obtained using a parametrically constrained geometry relaxation implemented in the AFLOW Library interface to FHI-aims.
Lenz et al. 2019
The geometry relaxations were performed using the generalized gradient approximated (GGA) PBEsol
Perdew et al. 2008
functional with a default tight basis set. The structures were relaxed until the maximum force component was below
5\text{\times}{10}^{-3}\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}\text{\,}{\mathrm{\text{Å}}}^{-1}
.

The initial geometries for the sulfur gas species (
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}
) were obtained from the CCCBDB database.
Johnson 1999

The geometry relaxations were performed at the MP2 level with a def2/TZVP basis set. The structures were relaxed until the maximum force component was below
1\text{\times}{10}^{-5}\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}\text{\,}{\mathrm{\text{Å}}}^{-1}
.

Harmonic phonon dispersions were evaluated using the finite-difference method with a
0.01\text{\,}\mathrm{\text{Å}}
step-size as implemented in Phonopy.
Togo and Tanaka 2015
The forces are evaluated with the PBEsol functional with a convergence criteria of
1\text{\times}{10}^{-6}\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}\text{\,}{\mathrm{\text{Å}}}^{-1}
. A Monkhorst-Pack grid was used with a minimum
k
-spacing of
0.5\text{\,}{\mathrm{\text{Å}}}^{-1}
.
All materials which we predict to be thermodynamically stable at some point within
10\text{\times}{10}^{0}\text{\,}\mathrm{Pa}10\text{\times}{10}^{7}\text{\,}\mathrm{Pa}
and
400\text{\,}\mathrm{K}1500\text{\,}\mathrm{K}
are kinetically stable (ie, no phonon modes with imaginary frequency). The Supplementary Information contains phonon bandstructures for the binary materials included in this study (Figures S42 to S50).

Predictions for phase stability are highly sensitive to the calculated total energies from DFT.
To allow comparison between layered and three-dimensional Ba-Zr-S phases with diverse bonding we evaluated the total electronic energies using the meta-GGA SCAN functional.
Sun et al. 2015
A comparison against results generated using the hybrid HSE06
Krukau et al. 2006
and GGA PBEsol
Perdew et al. 2008
functionals are provided in the Supplementary Information.
For all total energy calculations the densities and forces were converged to
{10}^{-7}\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}\text{\,}{\mathrm{\text{Å}}}^{-1}
and
{10}^{-6}\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}\text{\,}{\mathrm{\text{Å}}}^{-1}

All other inputs were set to the default value within FHI-aims. The Supplementary Information contains electronic bandstructures (Figures S32 to S41, at HSE06 and PBEsol levels of theory) for
\text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

3.2
Reaction thermodynamics in sulfur gas

It may be thermodynamically favourable to
\text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
from
BaS
and
\text{ZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\sim$600\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}$

Increasing synthesis temperature overcomes the kinetic barrier, with crystalline material typically grown at
800\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
and above.
Hahn and Mutschke 1957
;
Comparotto et al. 2020
Sulfur partial pressure is an additional parameter which has been tuned to accelerate the reaction dynamics of
\text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
.
In particular, over-stoichiometric amounts of sulfur are shown to promote formation of perovskite at growth temperatures suitable for thin-film synthesis (
<
600\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
).
Wang et al. 2001
;
Yu et al. 2021
;
Turnley et al. 2022
;
Freund et al. 2022
;
Vincent et al. 2023
;
Yang et al. 2023

A common synthesis approach is to start with sulfur in the solid state (
\alpha
-sulfur), followed by heat treatment in a sealed ampule.
During this annealing step the sulfur sublimates to form sulfur gas.
Assuming the gas is in equilibrium, the ideal gas law can be applied to determine the sulfur partial pressure from annealing temperature, ampule volume and sulfur amount.
Another common assumption made in this calculation is that the sulfur gas is composed of a single allotrope (no allotrope mixing).
However in actuality gaseous sulfur forms a range of open and closed S
n
species with equilibrium allotrope ratios that are highly sensitive to temperature and pressure.
Dobbie and Fox 1919
;
Jackson et al. 2016

Furthermore, annealing may occur in non-equilibrium conditions resulting from the experimental setup, such as gas flow, gas escape or temperature gradients.
Ren et al. 2017

Experimental insight into reaction conditions required for
\text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
synthesis is limited by the challenges associated with in-situ monitoring of sulfur gas partial pressure during material synthesis.

For a system in equilibrium, quantum chemical modelling is deemed to give the most accurate understanding of sulfur gas constitution.
Steudel et al. 2003

Jackson et al. use a global structure search and first-principles lattice dynamics to identify the equilibrium ratios of 13 low-energy sulfur gas allotropes.
Jackson et al. 2016

They conclude that the dominant components are
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}
.
The trend is for high-
n
(
n
=8) species to dominate at low temperatures and high pressures, and low-
n
(
n
=2) species to dominate at high temperatures and low pressures. This is in agreement with experimental measurements.
Dobbie and Fox 1919
;
Steudel et al. 2003
;
Jackson et al. 2016

The transition temperature at which the dominant phase changes (
\mu_{S_{2}}=\mu_{S_{8}}
) is highly
pressure- dependent, with higher pressures corresponding to higher transition temperatures.
In addition higher pressures correspond to a wider temperature range in which contributions from the cyclic allotropes
\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}

\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}

Jackson et al. 2016

\text{S}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}

4
Conclusions

In this work we have considered the reported phases in the Ba-Zr-S system within
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
of the convex hull.
Our results demonstrate the thermodynamic feasibility of forming perovskite at moderate temperatures; we predict that across the temperature range
230\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}1000\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}

\text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is stable against decomposition into binary or elemental competing phases. Importantly, this is true when annealed in an mixed-allotrope (equilibrium) sulfur vapour up to partial pressures of
1\text{\times}{10}^{7}\text{\,}\mathrm{Pa}
.

Our model predicts that several sulfr-rich
\text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}
and
\text{ZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}
materials can be formed when the binary systems are annealed in sulfur vapour.
A general trend is that to form a more sulfur-rich species the temperature must be reduced, or the sulfur partial pressure increased.
At
500\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolCelsius}
a sulfur partial pressure above
1\text{\times}{10}^{4}\text{\,}\mathrm{Pa}
is required to form
\text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
.
At any temperature and pressure where
\text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is formed,
\text{ZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
is also predicted to form.
For this reason, our results support the idea of using
\text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{ZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
precursors for fast perovskite synthesis, before kinetically-limiting
\text{ZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
can be formed.

We have focused our analysis on the most commonly reported competing phases or reaction intermediates,
and emphasize that thermodynamic free energies for all possible product and reactant combinations reported in this study can be predicted using our computational notebook
Kayasatha and Whalley 2023
and material-agnostic ThermoPot software.
Kayastha et al. 2022

To widen this material space or incorporate the effects of disorder recent advances in computational modelling could be applied. For example, thermodynamic integration techniques to incorporate liquid
\text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{x\/}}}
phases, or statistical methods to quantify the configurational entropy term associated with Ruddlesden-Popper phase inter-mixing. Oxide materials might also play a role in
\text{BaZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
formation, with
\text{ZrO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
formation potentially acting as a Zr ‘sink’.

Our model predicts formation of the sulfur rich binaries
\text{BaS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
and
\text{ZrS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
at higher partial pressures than that measured experimentally.
This motivates further studies to close the gap between theory and experiment.
On the theory side, the ab-initio model for lattice dynamics could be extended to include the effects of lattice anharmonicity and thermal expansion.
For experiment, improved experimental measurement and control of temperature and sulfur partial pressure, with analysis that accounts for allotrope mixing, would provide more accurate measurements for comparison.

A key result is that the constitution of sulfur vapour has a significant impact on reaction thermodynamics.
It is difficult to maintain a constant temperature and sulfur partial pressure during synthesis and—as has been seen for
CZTS
Zhang et al. 2019
—the proportion of sulfur allotropes may fluctuate depending on sample position, dwell time, and the particulars of the experimental setup.
Ren et al. 2017

As a result there could be challenges for reproducibility, with fluctuations in the rate and feasibility of perovskite formation for comparable experimental setups.
It also suggests that a mechanism for forming non-equilibrium sulfur vapour reactants might provide an additional mechanism through which to control the synthesis of sulfur-based materials.

ℓ \ell -adic properties and congruences of ℓ \ell -regular partition functions
Ahmad El-Guindy 1∗, Mostafa M. Ghazy 2

Abstract
We study ℓ \ell -regular partitions by defining a sequence of modular forms of level ℓ \ell and quadratic character which encode their ℓ \ell -adic behavior. We show that this sequence is congruent modulo increasing powers of ℓ \ell to level 1 1 modular forms of increasing weights. We then prove that certain ℤ / ℓ m ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbb{Z} -modules generated by our sequence are isomorphic to certain subspaces of level 1 1 cusp forms of weight independent of the power of ℓ \ell , leading to a uniform bound on the ranks of those modules and consequently to ℓ \ell -adic relations between ℓ \ell -regular partition values.

Introduction

The study of the partition function and its variants has been a source of inspiration for many number theorists. A partition of a nonnegative integer
n
is a non-ascending sequence of positive integers that add up to
n
. Letting
p(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
, we have the generating function

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}.

(1)

The Ramanujan congruences and their prime power extensions, which were established by Atkin
Atkin (1967)
, Ramanujan
Ramanujan (1921)
and Watson
Watson (1938)
, are some of the most fundamental and elegant arithmetic properties of
p(n)
. Suppose
\ell\geq 5
is prime, and for
m\geq 1
define
\delta_{\ell}(m)
by
24\delta_{\ell}(m)\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{m}}
and
1\leq\delta_{\ell}(m)\leq\ell^{m}-1
. Then, for all
n\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&p(5^{m}n+\delta_{5}(m))\equiv 0\pmod{5^{m}},\\
&p(7^{m}n+\delta_{7}(m))\equiv 0\pmod{7^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1}},\\
&p(11^{m}n+\delta_{11}(m))\equiv 0\pmod{11^{m}}.\end{split}

(2)

It is natural to seek extensions of those remarkable congruences, as well as attempt to place them in a wider context. Fundamental work by Ahlgren and Ono
Ahlgren (2000)
;
Ahlgren and
Ono (2001)
;
Ono (2000)
proved the existence of infinitely many non-nested arithmetic progressions
An+B
where
p(An+B)\equiv 0\pmod{M}
for fixed
M
coprime to 6. More recently, Folsom, Kent, and Ono incorporated the theory of
\ell-
adic modular forms in the sense of Serre as in
Serre (1973)
to establish a general framework for partition congruences
Folsom
et al. (2012)
, unveiling additional congruences such as

p(13^{4}n+27,371)\equiv 25p(13^{2}n+162)\pmod{13^{2}}.

(3)

To be more specific, they defined a sequence of functions
L_{\ell}(b,z)
encoding
\ell
-adic properties of
p(n)
, and they relate them to certain finite dimensional spaces of modular forms of level 1. The cases where those spaces are of dimension
0
correspond to (
2
), while other cases lead to extended and systematic families of congruences such as (
3
). The work of
Folsom
et al. (2012)
was elaborated on by Boylan and Webb in
Boylan and Webb (2013)
, and it was also extended to the study of
r
-colored partitions and Andrews’ spt function in
Belmont
et al. (2014)
.

In the present work, we wish to extend those results and techniques to study
\ell
-regular partition functions, which we shall recall their definition below. This also requires the extension of results from
Folsom
et al. (2012)
;
Boylan and Webb (2013)
to the setting of modular forms with quadratic Nebentypus.

Modular properties of
R_{\ell}(b;z)
and related functions

2.1
Basic definitions

For a positive integer
N
, a function of the form
\displaystyle f(z)=\prod_{\delta\mid N}\eta(\delta z)^{r_{\delta}}
, where
r_{\delta}\in\mathbb{Z}
, is called an
eta-quotient
. Such eta-quotients will figure repeatedly in our work, and the following well-known result (see for example Theorems 1.64 and 1.65 of
Ono (2004)
) describes their modularity properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and
r_{\delta}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that

\sum_{\delta\mid N}\delta r_{\delta}\equiv\sum_{\delta\mid N}\frac{N}{\delta}r_{\delta}\equiv 0\pmod{24}.

If
f(z)=\displaystyle\prod_{\delta\mid N}\eta(\delta z)^{r_{\delta}}
,
then for every
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(N),
we have
f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=\chi(d)(cz+d)^{k}f(z)
, where
k=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\delta|N}r_{\delta}
and

\chi(d)=\left(\frac{(-1)^{k}\prod_{\delta\mid N}\delta^{r_{\delta}}}{d}\right).

Furthermore, If
c,d
are coprime positive integers with
d\mid N
then the order of vanishing of
f
at the cusp
\frac{c}{d}
is given by

\textrm{ord}_{\frac{c}{d}}(f)=\frac{N}{24}\sum_{\delta\mid N}\frac{\text{gcd}(d,\delta)^{2}r_{\delta}}{gcd(d,\frac{N}{d})d\delta}.

(10)

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that the character corresponding to
R_{\ell}(0;z)
is

\chi_{\ell}(d)=\left(\frac{(-1)^{\frac{c}{2}}\ell}{d}\right).

By quadratic reciprocity, we see that
\chi_{\ell}
in fact coincides with the Legendre symbol
\left(\frac{\bullet}{\ell}\right)
. When the context is clear, we shall omit the subscript on
\chi_{\ell}
.

Modifying the construction in
Folsom
et al. (2012)
to fit the present study, we define the operator
D_{c}(\ell)
by

f\mid D_{c}(\ell):=(f\cdot\Phi_{\ell}^{c}(z))\mid U(\ell).

(11)

To study congruence properties of
\ell
-regular partitions we will consider the alternating action by the operators
U(\ell),D_{c}(\ell)
. It will sometimes be useful to think of this sequence as simply repeated use of the operators
U(\ell)\circ D_{c}(\ell)
or
D_{c}(\ell)\circ U(\ell)
. To this end, we define

f\mid X_{c}(\ell):=(f\mid U(\ell))\mid D_{c}(\ell)~,\text{and}

(12)

f\mid Y_{c}(\ell):=(f\mid D_{c}(\ell))\mid U(\ell).\

(13)

We now prove a result about the modularity of the function
R_{\ell}(b;z)
. Using standard notation, we denote by
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
(resp.
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
) the space of holomorphic modular forms (resp. cusp forms) of weight
k
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and character
\chi
, and we denote by
M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
the space of weakly holomorphic modular forms of weight
k
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and character
\chi
, i.e. those forms are whose poles (if any) are supported at the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For all
b\geq 0
,
R_{\ell}(b;z)\in M_{\frac{c}{2}}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi_{\ell})\cap\mathbb{Z}\llbracket q\rrbracket
.

Proof.

For
b=0
, we have that
R_{\ell}(0;z)=\eta(\ell z)\eta(z)^{c-1}\in M_{\frac{c}{2}}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi_{\ell})\cap\mathbb{Z}\llbracket q\rrbracket
. It also follows that
\Phi_{\ell}(z)\in M_{0}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell^{2}))\cap\mathbb{Z}\llbracket q\rrbracket
. From its definition we easily see that
U(\ell)
preserves
\mathbb{Z}\llbracket q\rrbracket
, and it is also well-known that

U(\ell):M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell^{2}),\chi)\to M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi),

(14)

and

U(\ell):M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi)\to M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi).

(15)

Combining the facts above for
k=\frac{c}{2}
, an inductive argument show that
R_{\ell}(b;z)
is in
M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi_{\ell})\cap\mathbb{Z}\llbracket q\rrbracket
for all
b\geq 1
.
∎

Recall that for
f(z)\in M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
and
\ell\nmid N
the Hecke operator
T(\ell,k)
is defined by

f(z)\mid T(\ell,k)=f(z)\mid U(\ell)+\ell^{k-1}f(z)\mid V(\ell).

(16)

It is well-known that
T(\ell,k)
preserves
M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
(as well as
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
and
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
). Hence we obtain the following stability properties of
U(\ell)
modulo
\ell^{k-1}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be prime, and let
f(z)\in M_{k}^{!}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}((q))
.

1.

We have
f(z)\mid T(\ell,k)\equiv f(z)\mid U(\ell)\pmod{\ell^{k-1}}
.

2.

The operator
U(\ell)
stabilizes
M_{k}^{!}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}((q))
modulo
\ell^{k-1}
.

2.2
Filtration

The theory of filtration provides many remarkable and deep results in the study of modular forms. It enables a systematic study of the phenomena of a modular form with
\ell
-integral coefficients, usually of higher level and possibly with Nebentypus, being congruent
\pmod{\ell}
to modular forms of level
1
. As we shall see, this applies to our sequence of interest
R_{\ell}(b;z)
. In order to proceed, we review some of the most pertinent facts, following
Serre (1973)
and
Swinnerton-Dyer (1973)
.
For
0\neq f\in\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\llbracket q\rrbracket
, define the filtration of
f
modulo
\ell
by

w_{\ell}(f):=~{\text{inf}}_{k\geq 0}\{k:f\equiv g\pmod{\ell}~{\text{for some}}~g\in M_{k}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\llbracket q\rrbracket\}.

(17)

If
f\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}
then define
w_{\ell}(f)=-\infty
. Note that if
f\equiv g\pmod{\ell}
and
g\in M_{k}
, then we have
w_{\ell}(f)\equiv k\pmod{\ell-1}
.

The following is part of Lemme 2 on p. 213 of
Serre (1973)
; it gives a bound of the filtration of the action of
U(\ell)
on
q
-series.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
f\in\mathbb{Z_{(\ell)}}\llbracket q\rrbracket
is congruent modulo
\ell
to a nonzero modular form in some
M_{k}
of level
1
(i.e.
w_{\ell}(f)\geq 0
), then we have

w_{\ell}(f\mid U(\ell))\leq\ell+\frac{(w_{\ell}(f)-1)}{\ell}.

In the case of
R_{\ell}(0;z)
, we shall see that applying
U(\ell)
and
D_{c}(\ell)
decreases the filtration. To describe this precisely we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\llbracket q\rrbracket
be congruent to a nonzero modular form modulo
\ell\geq 5
. Set
k_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}(c\ell+\ell-1)
, and suppose that

w_{\ell}(f)\equiv 12~\left\lceil\frac{\ell-1}{24}\right\rceil\pmod{\ell-1}.

(18)

(a)

If
w_{\ell}(f)\leq k_{\ell}
, then we have
w_{\ell}(f\mid U(\ell))\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell-1)+\frac{c}{2}
and
w_{\ell}(f\mid X_{c}(\ell))\leq k_{\ell}
.

(b)

If
w_{\ell}(f)=\frac{1}{2}(\ell-1)+\frac{c}{2}
, then we have
w_{\ell}(f\mid D_{c}(\ell))\leq k_{\ell}
.

(c)

If
w_{\ell}(f)>k_{\ell}
, then we have
w_{\ell}(f\mid X_{c}(\ell))<w_{\ell}(f)
.

Proof.

To prove the first part of (a), we utilize Lemma
2.4
and the fact that
w_{\ell}
is always either
-\infty
or an integer (actually an even nonnegative integer, but we won’t need that detail) to get

\displaystyle w_{\ell}\left(f\mid U(\ell)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\ell+\frac{\frac{1}{2}(c\ell+\ell-1)-1}{\ell}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\ell+\frac{c}{2}+\frac{\ell-3}{2\ell}\right\rfloor=\ell+\frac{c}{2}.

Note that we also have
w_{\ell}(f|U(\ell))\equiv w_{\ell}(f)\equiv 12~\lceil\frac{\ell-1}{24}\rceil\pmod{\ell-1}
. The maximal positive integer
\leq\ell+\frac{c}{2}
satisfying that congruence is easily seen to be
\frac{1}{2}(\ell-1)+\frac{c}{2}
, and the result follows. The second inequality of (a) follows from the first one and from part (b), which we now turn our attention to. Utilizing the fact that
\Phi^{c}_{\ell}(z)\equiv\Delta^{\frac{c(\ell+c-1)}{24}}\pmod{\ell}
, and the latter is in
S_{c\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{2}\right)}
, by a similar reasoning to the above we get

\displaystyle w_{\ell}\left(f\mid X_{c}(\ell)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\ell+\frac{\frac{1}{2}(\ell-1+c+c(\ell^{2}-1))-1}{\ell}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\ell+\frac{c}{2}\ell+\frac{\ell-3}{2\ell}\right\rfloor=\ell\left(1+\frac{c}{2}\right).

Since
w_{\ell}(\Phi_{\ell}^{c})=\frac{c}{2}(\ell^{2}-1)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell-1}
, we see that
w_{\ell}(f|X_{c}(\ell))\equiv w_{\ell}(f)\pmod{\ell-1}
. The result follows since the maximal positive integer
\leq\ell\left(1+\frac{c}{2}\right)
satisfying the congruence (
18
) is indeed
k_{\ell}
. Part (c) follows in a similar manner, and we omit the details for brevity.

∎

We conclude this section with a simple lemma that enables the lifting of congruences modulo
\ell
to congruences modulo
\ell^{j}
by taking appropriate powers.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
f(z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\llbracket q\rrbracket
has
f(z)\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
. Then for all
j\geq 1
, we have
f(z)^{\ell^{j-1}}\equiv 1\pmod{\ell^{j}}
.

3
Eisenstein series of level
\ell
and Legendre character

In this section, we recall a pair of Eisenstein series that we shall denote
E_{k,\chi}
and
F_{k,\chi}
of level
\ell
and quadratic character. Boylan studied the former in
Boylan (2001)
(where it was denoted by
E_{\ell,k,\chi}
) using methods from
Schoeneberg (1974)
with ideas going back to Hecke
Hecke (1927)
. Further details about those Eisenstein series and the methods used to study them can be found in
Diamond and
Shurman (2005)
. Whenever
k\geq 2
has the same parity as
\chi
, the Eisenstein subspace in
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi)
has dimension
2
(the number of cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(\ell)
), and
E_{k,\chi},F_{k,\chi}
form a basis for it. Let the generalized Bernoulli numbers
B_{k,\chi}
be defined by the generating series

\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}\chi(a)\frac{te^{at}}{e^{\ell t}-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k,\chi}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}.

Then we have

\displaystyle E_{k,\chi}(z)

\displaystyle:=1-\frac{2k}{B_{k,\chi}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{d\mid n,d>0}\chi(d)d^{k-1}\right)q^{n},

(19)

\displaystyle F_{k,\chi}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{k-1}\left(\sum_{d\mid n,d>0}\chi(d)d^{1-k}\right)q^{n}\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi_{\ell}).

(20)

For the quadratic character
\chi=\chi_{\ell}
we also recall the following evaluation connecting the value of the Dirichlet
L
-function at a posititve integer
k
to the generalized Bernoulli numbers
B_{k,\chi}

L(k,\chi)=\frac{-\mathfrak{g}_{\chi}(-2\pi i)^{k}B_{k,\chi}}{2\ell^{k}k!},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\chi}
denotes the Gauss sum sum corresponding to
\chi=\chi_{\ell}
, which is defined by

\mathfrak{g}_{\chi}=\sum_{n=0}^{\ell-1}\chi(n)e^{\frac{2\pi in}{\ell}}.

It is well-known (see Chapter 6 of
Ireland and
Rosen (1990)
for instance) that

\mathfrak{g}_{\chi}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\ell}\ \ \ \ \textrm{ if $\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}$},\\
i\sqrt{\ell}\ \ \ \ \textrm{ if $\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}$}.\end{cases}

Example 3.1
.

When
\ell=19
,
k=9
we have

E_{9,\chi_{19}}=\frac{19}{3708443635}\left(\frac{3708443635}{19}+q-255q^{2}-6560q^{3}+65281q^{4}+\dots\right)\equiv 1\pmod{19},

and

F_{9,\chi_{19}}=q+255q^{2}+6560q^{3}+65281q^{4}+390626q^{5}+\dots.

We now consider the action of the Atkin-Lehner involution on
E_{k,\chi},F_{k,\chi}
. Recall that if
f
is any function on the upper half-plane and
\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is a real matrix with positive determinant, then the weight
k
slash action of
\gamma
on
f
is defined by

(f|_{k}\gamma)(z):=(ad-bc)^{\frac{k}{2}}(cz+d)^{-k}f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right).

(21)

The Atkin-Lehner operator corresponds to the action of
W_{\ell}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
\ell&0\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Note that for
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
\ell c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_{0}(\ell)
, we have

W_{\ell}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
\ell c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}d&-c\\
-\ell b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)W_{\ell}.

It follows that if
\psi
is any character on
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)^{*}
and
f\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\psi)
, then
f|_{k}W_{\ell}\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\overline{\psi})
. In particular, when
\psi
is either the trivial character or the quadratic character
\chi_{\ell}
, the space
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\psi)
is preserved by
W_{\ell}
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{\ell+1}
be distinct coset representatives of SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
over
\Gamma_{0}(\ell)
. Then the trace operator
\text{Tr}:M_{k}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell))\rightarrow M_{k}^{!}
is defined by

\text{Tr}(f):=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell+1}f|_{k}\gamma_{j}.

(22)

Using explicit coset representatives as in
Serre (1973)
, we get

\text{Tr}(f)=f+\ell^{1-\frac{k}{2}}(f\mid_{k}W_{\ell})\mid U(\ell).

(23)

Note that Tr maps
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell))
to
M_{k}
.

It could be shown (for instance, using methods similar to Chapter 4 of
Diamond and
Shurman (2005)
) that

F_{k,\chi}|_{k}W_{\ell}=\frac{\mathfrak{g}_{\chi}B_{k,\chi}}{-2k}\ell^{-\frac{k}{2}}\chi(-1)E_{k,\chi},

and

\displaystyle E_{k,\chi}|_{k}W_{\ell}=\frac{-2k\ell^{\frac{k}{2}}}{\mathfrak{g}_{\chi}B_{k,\chi}}F_{k,\chi}

(24)

As we shall see, in the study of modular forms of level
\ell
and character
\chi_{\ell}
, the Eisenestein series
E_{\chi}:=E_{\frac{\ell-1}{2},\chi}
plays a crucial role, similar to that of
E_{\ell-1}
, in the context of
\ell
-adic congruences. This stems from the fact that the denominator of
B_{\frac{\ell-1}{2},\chi}
is
exactly
divisible by
\ell
(see
Carlitz (1959)
for instance), hence we have

E_{\chi}\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}.

(25)

Setting
F_{\chi}:=F_{\frac{\ell-1}{2},\chi}
, we get from (
24
)

E_{\chi}|_{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}W_{\ell}=C_{\ell}F_{\chi},

(26)

where

C_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\frac{-(\ell-1)\ell^{\frac{\ell-3}{4}}}{B_{\frac{\ell-1}{2},\chi}}\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\ell})\textrm{ if $\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}$},\\
\frac{(\ell-1)\ell^{\frac{\ell-3}{4}}\sqrt{-1}}{B_{\frac{\ell-1}{2},\chi}}\in\mathbb{Q}(i)\textrm{ if $\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}$}.\end{cases}

(27)

Thus, unlike the cases with trivial Nebentypus addressed in
Folsom
et al. (2012)
and
Belmont
et al. (2014)
, we need to work in the ring of integers of
K_{\ell}:=\mathbb{Q}(C_{\ell})
in order to study “congruences” modulo
\ell
. More precisely, let
\mathfrak{l}
denote a prime ideal in the ring of integers of
K_{\ell}
above
\ell
, and
v_{\mathfrak{l}}
denote the valuation with respect to
\mathfrak{l}
, normalized so that
v_{\mathfrak{l}}(\mathfrak{l})=1
. We thus have

v_{\mathfrak{l}}(\ell)=\begin{cases}2\textrm{ if $\ell\equiv 1\pmod{4}$},\\
1\textrm{ if $\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}$}.\end{cases}

(28)

In what follows we shall drop the subscript
\mathfrak{l}
on
v
whenever the context it clear. We also extend the definition of the valuation
v
to Fourier series with rational coefficients in the standard way by setting

v\left(\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}\right):=\inf\{v(a_{n}):n\geq n_{0}\}.

(29)

Applying
v
to (
27
) we get

v\left(E_{\chi}|_{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}W_{\ell}\right)=v(C_{\ell})=\frac{\ell+1}{4}v(\ell).

(30)

Lemma 3.2
.

Set

h_{\chi}:=E_{\chi}-C_{\ell}E_{\chi}|_{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}W_{\ell}=E_{\chi}-C_{\ell}^{2}F_{\chi}\in\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\llbracket q\rrbracket.

(31)

Then we have:

1.

h_{\chi}\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
.

2.

h_{\chi}|_{\frac{(\ell-1)}{2}}W_{\ell}=C_{\ell}F_{\chi}-\chi(-1)C_{\ell}E_{\chi}=C_{\ell}(F_{\chi}-\chi(-1)E_{\chi}).

3.

v\left(h_{\chi}|_{\frac{(\ell-1)}{2}}W_{\ell}\right)=\frac{(\ell+1)}{4}v(\ell)
.

Proof.

The proof follows easily from (
25
), (
26
) and (
30
).
∎

Thus, in this setting, we have the following generalization of Lemme 9 of
Serre (1973)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
f\in M_{\kappa}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\chi_{\ell})
set
f_{m}:={\text{T}r}(fh_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}})
, then we have

v(f_{m}-f)\geq\min(v(f)+(m+1)v(\ell),v(f|_{\kappa}W_{\ell})+\frac{v(\ell)}{2}(\ell^{m}-\kappa+2))

(32)

Proof.

Let
k_{m}
denote the weight of
f_{m}
, namely
k_{m}=\kappa+\frac{(\ell-1)\ell^{m}}{2}
. We have

\displaystyle f_{m}-f

\displaystyle=fh_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}+\ell^{1-\frac{k_{m}}{2}}fh_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}|_{k_{m}}W_{\ell}|U(\ell)-f

\displaystyle=f(h_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}-1)+\ell^{1-\frac{k_{m}}{2}}fh_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}|_{k_{m}}W_{\ell}|U(\ell).

So,

v(f_{m}-f)\geq\min(v(f(h_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}-1)),v(\ell^{1-\frac{k_{m}}{2}}fh_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}|_{k_{m}}W_{\ell}|U(\ell))).

By Lemma
3.2
(1) and Lemma
2.6
, the first part gives

v(f(h_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}-1))=v(f)+v(h_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}-1)\geq v(f)+(m+1)v(\ell),

while the second part leads to

\displaystyle v(\ell^{1-\frac{k_{m}}{2}}fh_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}|_{k_{m}}W_{\ell}|U(\ell))

\displaystyle\geq v(\ell^{1-\frac{k_{m}}{2}}fh_{\chi}^{\ell^{m}}|_{k_{m}}W_{\ell}))=\left(1-\frac{k_{m}}{2}\right)v(\ell)+v(f|_{\kappa}W_{\ell})+\ell^{m}\frac{(\ell+1)}{4}v(\ell)

\displaystyle=v(f|_{\kappa}W_{\ell})+v(\ell)\left(1+\frac{\ell^{m}(\ell+1)}{4}-\frac{\kappa}{2}-\frac{\ell^{m}(\ell-1)}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=v(f|_{\kappa}W_{\ell})+\frac{v(\ell)}{2}(\ell^{m}-\kappa+2),

(where the first inequality follows since
v(F|U(\ell))\geq v(F)
for any
F\in\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\llbracket q\rrbracket
, as the coefficients of
F|U(\ell)
are a subset of those of
F
) and the result follows.
∎

4
\ell
-adic properties of
U(\ell)
and
D_{c}(\ell)
and higher congruences for
R_{\ell}(b;z)

In this section, we show that the functions
R_{\ell}(b;z)
, which are weakly holomorphic modular forms with Nebentypus

is the identity map on
\Omega_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(m)
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.6
, we have the following nesting of
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbb{Z}
-modules:

\displaystyle S^{\text{odd}}(\ell,m)\supseteq\Lambda_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(1,m)\supseteq\Lambda_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(3,m)\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\Lambda_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(2b+1,m)\supseteq\cdots.

Since
S_{k_{\ell}(m)}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\llbracket q\rrbracket
is finitely generated, we see that
S^{\text{odd}}(\ell,m)
is a finite module, hence we must have
\Lambda_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(b,m)=\Lambda_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(b+2,m)
within a finite number of steps, afterwards the sequence must stabilize to
\Omega_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(m)
since the process of going from
b
to
b+2
is identical to the one used to go from
b+2
to
b+4
. Essentially by definition, we have
\Lambda_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(b+2,m)=X_{c}(\Lambda_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(b,m))
. It follows that
X_{c}(\ell)
is a surjective endomorphism of the finite
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Omega_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(m)
, and hence a bijection. Since the automorphism group of a finite ring is itself a finite group; it follows that each element of it has a finite order.
∎

Consider the following submodules of
S^{\text{odd}}(\ell,m)

S_{0}=\left\{f(z)E_{\ell-1}^{\frac{1}{2}(\ell^{m-1}-c-1)}(z):f(z)\in S_{\frac{c\ell+\ell-1}{2}}\cap\mathbb{Z}_{(\ell)}\llbracket q\rrbracket\right\},

S_{1}=\left\{g(z):g(z)=\sum_{j=m_{0}}^{\infty}a_{j}q^{j}\in S_{\frac{1}{2}\ell^{m-1}(\ell-1)+\frac{c}{2}}\text{ with }m_{0}>\text{dim}(S_{\frac{c\ell+\ell-1}{2}})\right\}.

It is well known that any level 1 modular form can be expressed as a polynomial in the forms
E_{4},E_{6}
which have integral coefficients. Hence we can construct a basis
\{f_{i}=q^{i}+O(q^{i+1}):1\leq i\leq n=\dim(S_{\frac{1}{2}\ell^{m-1}(\ell-1)+\frac{c}{2}})\}
for

for

and
f_{k}(z)\in S_{1}

. In particular, we have a natural projection map
\pi_{0}:S^{\text{odd}}(\ell,m)\rightarrow S_{0}

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\ell\geq 5
be prime, and let
m\geq 1
. Then there exist an injective
\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbb{Z}-
module homomorphism

\Pi_{o}:\Omega_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(m)\hookrightarrow S_{\frac{1}{2}(c\ell+\ell-1)}

such that for all
f\in\Omega_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(m)
we have

(a)

\text{ord}_{\infty}(f)=\text{ord}_{\infty}(\Pi_{o}(f))
, and

(b)

\Pi_{o}(f)\equiv f\pmod{\ell^{v(f)+1}}.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
, we see that
\Pi_{o}(f):={\pi_{0}(f)}{E_{\ell-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}(\ell^{m-1}-c-1)}}
indeed gives an injection from

\Omega_{\ell}^{\text{odd}}(m)
into
S_{\frac{1}{2}(c\ell+\ell-1)}
. Part (a) follows since
\pi_{0}
preserves the order of vanishing at
\infty
and
\text{ord}_{\infty}(E_{\ell-1})=0
. Furthermore, again by Lemma
5.2
, we have
f\equiv\pi_{0}(f)\pmod{\ell^{i+1}}
for
f\in\Omega_{\ell}^{\text{odd}}(m)
with
v(f)=i
. Utilizing
E_{\ell-1}\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
we get

\ell^{-i}f\equiv\ell^{-i}\pi_{0}(f)E_{\ell-1}^{\frac{1}{2}(\ell^{m-1}-c-1)}\pmod{\ell}.

Multiplying by
\ell^{i}
we obtain
f\equiv\Pi_{o}(f)\pmod{\ell^{i+1}}
, completing the proof.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The stabilization of the nested sequence (
7
) into
\Omega_{\ell,\text{reg}}^{\text{odd}}(m)
follows by the proof of Lemma
5.1
. Since
\text{ord}_{\infty}(\Phi_{\ell}^{c})=\frac{c(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}

q

\text{ord}_{\infty}(F\mid D_{c}(\ell))\geq\frac{c(\ell^{2}-1)}{24\ell}.

\text{ord}_{\infty}(\Pi_{o}(f))=\text{ord}_{\infty}(f)\geq\frac{c(\ell^{2}-1)}{24\ell}.

We thus conclude the following bound on the rank:

(43)

For
\ell\equiv 1\pmod{24}
, the bound (
8
) follows directly from (
43
). For
\ell\not\equiv 1\pmod{24}
, write
\ell=24t+\delta
with
5\leq\delta\leq 23
; we thus have
c=25-\delta
. Elementary calculations show that

\frac{c(\ell^{2}-1)}{24}=\left(ct+\delta-\frac{\delta^{2}-1}{24}\right)\ell+\left((\delta-1)t+\frac{\delta^{2}-1}{24}-1\right),

hence we have

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\frac{c(\ell^{2}-1)}{24\ell}\right\rfloor=ct+\delta-\frac{\delta^{2}-1}{24}.

(44)

We note that for all prime
\ell\geq 5
we have
\frac{c\ell+\ell-1}{2}\not\equiv 2\pmod{12}

1
and the elementary formula

(c+1)(24t+\delta)-1=\left((c+1)t+\delta-\frac{\delta^{2}-1}{24}\right)24+2(\delta-1),

we conclude

\displaystyle\dim(S_{\frac{c\ell+\ell-1}{2}})=\left\lfloor\frac{(c+1)\ell-1}{24}\right\rfloor=(c+1)t+\delta-\frac{\delta^{2}-1}{24}+\left\lfloor\frac{\delta-1}{12}\right\rfloor.

(45)

Combining (

44

In order to prove the isomorphism

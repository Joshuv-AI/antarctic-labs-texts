Global dynamics of a two-stage structured diffusive population model in time-periodic and spatially heterogeneous environments
H. M. Gueguezo, T. J. Doumatè, R. B. Salako

Abstract
This work examines the global dynamics of classical solutions of a two-stage (juvenile-adult) reaction-diffusion population model in time-periodic and spatially heterogeneous environments. It is shown that the sign of the principal eigenvalue λ ∗ \lambda_{*} of the time-periodic linearized system at the trivial solution completely determines the persistence of the species. Moreover, when λ ∗ > 0 \lambda_{*}>0 , there is at least one time-periodic positive entire solution. A fairly general sufficient condition ensuring the uniqueness and global stability of the positive time-periodic solution is obtained. In particular, classical solutions eventually stabilize at the unique time-periodic positive solutions if either each subgroup’s intra-stage growth and inter-stage competition rates are proportional, or the environment is temporally homogeneous and both subgroups diffuse slowly. In the later scenario, the asymptotic profile of steady states with respect to small diffusion rates is established.



Key words.
Diffusion-reaction model, stage-structured model, periodic-solution, stability

\begin{cases}0=(r(x)-c(x)w_{1}^{0}(x))w_{2}^{0}(x)-(a(x)+s(x)+b(x)w_{1}^{0}(x))w_{1}^{0}(x),\cr 0=(s(x)-g(x)w_{2}^{0}(x))w_{1}^{0}(x)-(e(x)+f(x)w_{2}^{0}(x))w_{2}^{0}(x),\end{cases}

(5.2)

whereas
w_{1}^{0}(x)=w_{2}^{0}(x)=0
when
h^{0}(x)\leq 0
.

Proof.

The continuity of
w_{2}^{0}
and the fact that
\|w_{2}^{\varepsilon}-w_{2}^{0}\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
follows from the implicit function theorem. This in turn implies that
w_{1}^{0}\in C(\bar{\Omega})
and a composition of two such functions, and
\|w_{1}^{\varepsilon}-w_{1}^{0}\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Clearly, we have that
w_{2}^{0}\geq 0
, which thanks to (
4.2
) implies that
w_{1}^{0}\geq 0
. Thus
\|{\bf w}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf w}^{0}\|\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. This in turn with the fact that
\|\tilde{\bf w}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf w}^{\varepsilon}\|\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
(see (
4.14
)) implies that
\|\tilde{\bf w}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf w}^{0}\|\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Clearly,
w_{2}^{0}(x)=w_{1}^{0}(x)=0
whenever
h^{0}(x)=0
.

Next, fix
x\in\bar{\Omega}
such that
h^{0}(x)>0
. Then,
F^{0}(x,0)=h^{0}(x)>0
. Hence, since
F^{0}(x,\tau_{2})<0
(where
\tau_{2}
is as in Lemma
4.2
), it follows from the intermediate value theorem, the fact that
F^{0}(x,\tau)
is strictly decreasing in
\tau\geq 0
, and the fact that
F^{0}(x,w_{2}^{0}(x))=0
that
w_{2}^{0}(x)>0
. This along with (
4.2
) implies that
w_{1}^{0}(x)>0
. The first equation of (
5.2
) follows from (
4.3
) with
\varepsilon=0
and
\tau=w_{2}^{0}(x)
. Furthermore, since
h^{0}(x)>0
, then it follows from the proof of (
4.11
) that equality holds in this case with
\varepsilon=0
, which yields the validity of the second equation of (
5.2
).
∎

Next, for each
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{*}
, consider the linear eigenvalue problem of the cooperative system

\begin{cases}\lambda^{\varepsilon}v_{1}=d_{1}\Delta v_{1}+(r-c\tilde{w}_{1}^{\varepsilon})v_{2}-(a+s)v_{1}&x\in\Omega,\cr\lambda^{\varepsilon}v_{2}=d_{2}\Delta v_{2}+(s-g\tilde{w}_{2}^{\varepsilon})v_{1}-ev_{2}&x\in\Omega,\cr 0=\partial_{\vec{n}}v_{1}=\partial_{\vec{n}}v_{2}&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(5.3)

Denote by
\lambda^{\varepsilon}_{*}
the principal eigenvalue of (
5.3
). Note from (
5.2
) that for every
x\in\bar{\Omega}
satisfying
h^{0}(x)>0
, that is
r(x)s(x)>e(x)(a(x)+s(x))
, we have that

\displaystyle(r(x)-c(x)w_{1}^{0}(x))(s(x)-g(x)w_{2}^{0}(x))-(a(x)+s(x))e(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a(x)+s(x))w_{2}^{0}(x)+e(x)b(x)w_{1}^{0}(x)+f(x)b(x)w_{1}^{0}(x)w_{2}^{0}(x)>0.

Hence
((r-cw_{1}^{0})(s-gw_{2}^{0})-(a+s)e)_{\max}>0
. Therefore, since
\|\tilde{\bf w}^{\varepsilon}-{\bf w}^{0}\|\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
(see Lemma
5.1
), then there is
0<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{*}<\varepsilon_{*}
such that
((r-c\tilde{w}_{1}^{\varepsilon})(s-g\tilde{w}_{2}^{\varepsilon})-(a+s)e)_{\max}>0
for
0\leq\varepsilon<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{*}
. Therefore, by
[
10
, Proposition 1]
, for every
0<\varepsilon<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{*}
, there is
\tilde{d}^{\varepsilon}_{*}>0
such that
\tilde{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}_{*}>0
for every
0<d_{1},d_{2}<\tilde{d}^{\varepsilon}_{*}
.

The following lemma is needed.

Lemma 5.2
.

Fix
0<\varepsilon<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{*}
and
0<d_{1},d_{2}<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{*}
. Then there is a unique positive steady state
{\bf v}(\cdot;{\bf d})
to the cooperative system

\begin{cases}0=d_{1}\Delta v_{1}+(r-c\tilde{w}_{1}^{\varepsilon})v_{2}-(a+s+bv_{1})v_{1}&x\in\Omega,\cr 0=d_{2}\Delta v_{2}+(s-g\tilde{w}_{2}^{\varepsilon})v_{1}-(e+fv_{2})v_{2}&x\in\Omega,\cr 0=\partial_{\vec{n}}v_{1}=\partial_{\vec{n}}v_{2}&x\in\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(5.4)

Furthermore,
{\bf v}(\cdot;{\bf d})\to{\bf v}^{\varepsilon}
as
\max\{d_{1},d_{2}\}\to 0^{+}
locally uniformly in
\Omega
, where for each

,
{\bf v}^{\varepsilon}(x)

\begin{cases}0=(r-c\tilde{w}_{1}^{\varepsilon})v_{2}^{\varepsilon}-(a+s+bv_{1}^{\varepsilon})v_{1}^{\varepsilon}&x\in\Omega,\cr 0=(s-g\tilde{w}_{2}^{\varepsilon})v_{1}^{\varepsilon}-(e+fv_{2}^{\varepsilon})v_{2}^{\varepsilon}&x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

(5.5)

Proof.

Since

for every

and system (

{\bf v}(\cdot;{\bf d})

as
\max\{d_{1},d_{2}\}\to 0

.
∎

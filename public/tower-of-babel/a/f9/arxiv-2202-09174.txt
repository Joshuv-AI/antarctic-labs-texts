Foliations and stable maps
Bertrand Toën and Gabriele Vezzosi

Abstract
. This paper is part of an ongoing series of works on the study of foliations on algebraic varieties via derived algebraic geometry. We focus here on the specific case of globally defined vector fields and the global behaviour of their algebraic integral curves. For a smooth and proper variety X X with a global vector field ν \nu , we consider the induced vector field ν g , n \nu_{g,n} on the derived stack of stable maps, of genus g g with n n marked points, to X X . When ( g , n ) (g,n) is either ( 0 , 2 ) (0,2) or ( 1 , 0 ) (1,0) , the derived stack of zeros of ν g , n \nu_{g,n} defines a proper moduli of algebraic trajectories of ν \nu . When ( g , n ) = ( 0 , 2 ) (g,n)=(0,2) algebraic trajectories behave very much like rational algebraic paths from one zero of ν \nu to another, and in particular they can be composed. This composition is represented by the usual gluing maps in Gromov-Witten theory, and we use it give three categorical constructions, of different categorical levels, related, in a certain sense, by decategorification. In order to do this, in particular, we have to deal with virtual fundamental classes of non-quasi-smooth derived stacks. When ( g , n ) = ( 1 , 0 ) (g,n)=(1,0) , zeros of ν 1 , 0 \nu_{1,0} might be thought as algebraic analogues of periodic orbits of vector fields on smooth real manifolds. In particular, we propose a Zeta function counting the zeros of ν 1 , 0 \nu_{1,0} , that we like to think of as an algebraic version of Ruelle’s dynamical Zeta function. We conclude the paper with a brief indication on how to extend these results to the case of general one dimensional foliation F F , by considering the derived stack of F F -equivariant stable maps.



Introduction

This paper is part of an ongoing series of works on the study of foliations on algebraic varieties
via derived techniques (
[
TVc
,
TVb
,
TVa
]
). We focus here on the specific case of globally defined vector fields (and more generally one dimensional foliations, see our section §4) and the global behaviour of their algebraic integral curves.
The results presented in this paper are preliminary, and we will limit ourselves to survey
the main constructions and results, more details as well as more general results will appear elsewhere.

For a smooth and proper variety
X
and a global vector field
\nu\in H^{0}(X,\mathbb{T}_{X})
,
we consider curves in
X
which are globally invariant by
\nu
, with a particular emphasis on their
moduli and on the various numerical/homological invariants they give rise to. For this, we will show how
\nu

defines an induced vector field
\nu_{g,n}
on
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)
, the derived moduli stack of
stable maps of genus
g
with
n
marked points in
X
(see Section
1.1
).
An important first observation is that when
(g,n)
is either
(0,2)
or
(1,0)
, the zeros of the vector field
\nu_{g,n}

on
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)
are precisely given by stable maps that are globally invariant by
\nu
,
or equivalently by
algebraic trajectories
of
\nu
in
X
. The derived stack
of zeros of
\nu_{g,n}
therefore defines a proper moduli of algebraic trajectories of
\nu
,
which is the central object of this work.

When
(g,n)=(0,2)
, algebraic trajectories appear as broken rational curves in
X
along
\nu

whose marked points are sent to zeros of the original vector field
\nu
on
X
. Therefore, they behave
very much like algebraic paths from one zero of
\nu
to another, and in particular they can be composed.
This composition is represented by gluing maps on the level of derived moduli stacks, induced by the usual gluing
construction of stable maps in Gromov-Witten theory. Out of this, we extract three constructions, of
different categorical levels, that can be thought as the starting point of an
enumerative geometry of algebraic
trajectories
. We start by considering derived categories attached to the derived moduli
Z
of zeros
of
\nu_{0,2}
, namely the derived category of coherent complexes on
Z
which are relatively perfect along
the evaluation map
Z\to Z_{0}\times Z_{0}
where
Z_{0}
is the derived zero locus of
\nu
(see Definition
3.1.1
). As base change always holds in derived algebraic geometry (see
[
Toë14
, 3.1]
),
the gluing maps induce a composition on these derived categories, and we get this way a linear
2
-category

\widetilde{\mathsf{Traj}}(X,\nu)
whose set of objects is the set of zeros of
\nu
in
X
, and whose categories of maps are derived
categories of the derived stack of zeros of
\nu_{0,2}
(see Definition
3.1.1
). This structure can be thought as a
generalization of the categorical Gromov-Witten invariants of
[
MR18
]
suitably localized around the zeros of the vector field
\nu
. It is also
a vector field analogue of the usual localization techniques in Gromov-Witten theory for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-actions (see for instance
[
GP99
]
).

The
2
-category
\widetilde{\mathsf{Traj}}(X,\nu)
can then be decategorified twice. As a first decategorification,
we construct a
1
-category, whose set of objects is again the zeros of
\nu
, and the
space of morphisms are given by Borel-Moore homology of the derived zero loci of
\nu_{0,2}
(again relative
to the evaluation map). A second decategorification is obtained by
considering virtual fundamental classes of the derived zero loci of
\nu_{0,2}
. This is now
a bivector in the homology of the zero locus of
\nu
, and is the vector field
version of the usual Gromov-Witten invariants in cohomology. On a technical level, the derived moduli stacks involved in this
construction are not quasi-smooth in general, and thus their virtual fundamental class does not
exist as is. We show however that virtual fundamental classes always exist as
rational functions
in an indeterminate
t
,
and that the bivector mentioned above has only
\mathbb{Q}(t)
coefficients. More precisely, if
Z_{0}
is the zero locus of
\nu
in
X
, our construction
provides an element in
H_{*}(Z_{0})^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathbb{Q}(t)
.
When further assumptions on
X
are made,
for instance when
X
is convex, the derived moduli are indeed quasi-smooth, and the above rational functions are
polynomials which can then be evaluated at
t=-1
. Under these conditions, we obtain a linear
endomorphism of the vector space span by zeros of
\nu
(assuming these zeros are simple),
which we like to think of an algebraic version of the Morse differential. Finally, we prove that
this endomorphism, even though does not square to zero, satisfies a remarkable formula close to
the splitting/associativity formula in usual Gromov-Witten theory (see e.g.
[
BM96
, Def. 7.1-(3)]
).

We finish the paper with some thoughts about the case
(g,n)=(1,0)
, for which zeros of

\nu_{1,0}
can be thought as algebraic analogues of periodic orbits of vector fields on smooth real manifolds. In particular, we
use our numerical invariants with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}(t)
in order to
propose a formula for a Zeta function counting zeros of
\nu_{1,0}
, which we like to think of as
an algebraic version of the dynamical Zeta function of a flow (see e.g.

[
Rue92
, §1.4-(B)]
). We hope to come back to this aspect in future work.
We conclude the paper with a short section explaining how to extend our results to the case of general one dimensional foliations
instead of just global vector fields, which will certainly provide a wider range of applicability of our results.

1.
Vector fields and stable maps

In this first section we show how a global vector field on an algebraic
variety

induces
a vector field on
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)
, the derived moduli stack of
stable maps to

(g,n)=(0,2)

1.1.
Recollections on the derived moduli of stable maps

The reader can find details concerning the derived moduli stack of stable
maps in
[
STV15
]
. We remind below some notations and constructions.

We let
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
be the Artin stack of prestable curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points.
We denote by
\pi:\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\to\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
be the universal prestable curve. The morphism

\pi
induces an adjunction on the
\infty
-categories of relative derived stacks

\pi^{*}:\mathbf{dSt}/\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}\leftrightarrows\mathbf{dSt}/\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}:\pi_{*},

where
\pi^{*}
is the usual base change
\infty
-functor. The right adjoint
\pi_{*}

is also known as the Weil restriction along
\pi
.

Because
\pi
is a proper and flat morphism, it is known that
\pi_{*}
preserves derived Artin stacks locally
of finite presentation.
In particular, if
X
is any smooth and proper scheme over
k
, we can consider

\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X):=\pi_{*}(X\times\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}),

which is a derived Artin stack endowed with a natural projection to
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
.

Definition 1.1.1
.

The
derived stack of pre-stable maps on
X
(of genus
g
with
n
marked points)
is the derived Artin stack
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)
defined above.

The projection
p:\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)\to\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
is a morphism
of derived Artin stacks, whose fibers are the derived mapping schemes of pre-stable curves
to
X
. In more precise terms, for any derived scheme
S
and morphism
S\to\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
,
the pull-back
S\times_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)
is canonically identified with

\mathbf{Map}_{S}(\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\times_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}S,X\times S)
, the derived mapping stack over
S

from the curve
\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\times_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}S
to
X\times S
. We see in particular that
the morphism
p:\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)\to\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
is representable and its fibers are
derived schemes locally of finite type.

As usual, the derived stack of stable maps is defined as the open substack

\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)\subset\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)
whose geometric points consists of
stable maps to
X
. The derived stack
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)
is Deligne-Mumford, and
each component
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
, of stable maps
f:C\to X

with
f_{*}([C])=\beta
, is proper over
\mathbf{Spec}\,k
(see
[
BM
, Thm. 3.14]
).
Here
\beta
is an element in
H_{2}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-1))
,
is a class in
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-adic homology of
\overline{X}=X\otimes_{k}\overline{k}
?

We then define the open (and closed) substacks
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\subset\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)
by considering geometric points
f:\mathbf{Spec}\,K\to\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)

corresponding to stable maps
C\to X\otimes_{k}\overline{k}
,
with
C
a prestable curve over

K
, such that
f_{*}([C])=\beta\in H_{2}(X\otimes_{k}K,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-1))
, where

[C]\in H_{2}(C,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-1))
is the fundamental class of
C
.

Definition 1.1.2
.

The
derived stack of stable maps on
X
(of genus
g
with
n
marked points)
is the derived Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)
defined above. The open and
closed
substack of stable maps of class

\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(1))
is
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
defined above.

An important fact that we will use in a crucial way is the
explicit computation of the tangent complex of
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)
. For this, we
consider the evaluation morphism

ev:\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)\times_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\longrightarrow X,

as well as the projection to the first factor

q:\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)\times_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X).

The tangent complex of
p:\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)\to\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
is then given by (see
[
Toë14
, §3.4]
)

\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)/\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\simeq q_{*}(ev^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X})),

where
T_{X}
is the tangent vector bundle of
X
relative to
k
. As
q
is a flat and proper curve,
we see immediately that
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)/\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}
is a perfect complex whose amplitude is contained
in
[0,1]
. To obtain the full tangent complex of
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)
, relative to
k
, we use the exact triangle

\textstyle{\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)/\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{p^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}).}

As
\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
is a smooth Artin stack over
k
,
p^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}})
is itself perfect
and of amplitude in
[-1,0]

\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)}
is
perfect and of amplitude in
[-1,1]

is thus quasi-smooth over

\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)
is itself a quasi-smooth and proper derived Deligne-Mumford stack over
k

(its tangent complex is a complex and of Tor-amplitude
[0,1]
).

1.2.
Induced vector fields

We keep working with
X

Propositon 1.2.1
.

Any vector field
\nu\in H^{0}(X,\mathbb{T}_{X})
determines a natural vector field

\nu_{g,n}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X),\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)}).

Proof.
Start with
\nu:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathbb{T}_{X}
on
X
. By pull-back along the evaluation map

ev:\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)\times_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\bar{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\longrightarrow X

a new section
ev^{*}(\nu):\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)}\to ev^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X})
. Its push-foward by

q:\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)\to\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}
produces a morphism

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\longrightarrow q_{*}q^{*}(\mathcal{O})\longrightarrow q_{*}ev^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X}).

We now use that
q_{*}ev^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X})\simeq\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)/\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}
, as well as
the natural morphism
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)/\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)}

in order to get a canonical element
\nu_{g,n}\in H^{0}(\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X),\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)})
.
\Box

Remark 1.2.2
.

One can understand the above proposition in various ways. For instance, it can
be seen as the fact that an infinitesimal automorphism of

of the construction

field defines a derived foliation of dimension

in the sense of

]

the above proposition is a very particular case of a more general result stating the existence
of direct images of derived foliations (see

It will be important to remind (see the proof of Proposition

is in fact the image

by the canonical map
\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)/\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}}\to\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}^{pr}(X)}

Our main object of interest is

We consider

.

and
\infty
. We can describe the vector field
\nu_{g,n}

at the point

as follows. The tangent complex of
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)
at

(see
[

)

\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X),f}\simeq\mathbb{H}(C,\mathbb{T}_{C}(-0-\infty)\to f^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X})).

The vector field

provides a morphism
\mathbb{H}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C})\to\mathbb{H}(C,f^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X}))\to\mathbb{H}(C,\mathbb{T}_{C}(-0-\infty)\to f^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X}))
. Since

, and the corresponding morphism of complexes of

is the vector field
\nu_{0,2}
evaluated at the point

. We also note that

is
\nu^{\prime}_{2,0}
evaluated at
f
, where
\nu^{\prime}_{0,2}
is the
relative induced vector field of Definition

.

We now consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathbb{H}(C,f^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{H}(C,\mathbb{T}_{C}(-0-\infty)\to f^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X}))}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces k\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\nu^{\prime}_{0,2}}
\scriptstyle{\nu_{0,2}}

A homotopy to zero of
\nu_{0,2}
then consists of a factorization of
\nu^{\prime}_{0,2}
via the
canonical morphism induced by the tangent map of

.

Tf:\mathbb{H}(C,\mathbb{T}_{C}(-0-\infty))\longrightarrow\mathbb{H}(C,f^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X})).

Again as

, we have
\mathbb{H}(C,\mathbb{T}_{C}(-0-\infty))\simeq k
, and a canonical generator
is given by the vector field

on

which vanishes at

and

.
We thus arrive at the conclusion that the geometric point

lies in the substack

if and only if there exists

such that the image of

by

is
\nu
restricted to

. In other words,

if and only if

as an algebraic trajectory of
\nu
in
X
.

We have seen that a stable map

, which is a zero
of the induced vector field

must be an algebraic trajectory of
\nu
on
X
.
In particular, the images of

and

must be a zero of

(and moreover these

and

,

if and only if each component

of

trajectory of
\nu
in

. Therefore,

becomes a trajectories of broken
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in

for
the vector field
\nu
, each irreducible components of

sitting between two zeros of
\nu
.

Case
(g,n)=(1,0)
.
Let us now assume that
(g,n)=(1,0)
. The same reasoning shows that the geometric points of

correspond to stable maps

, with

a curve of genus

, such that there is
a global vector field

whose image by

is the restriction
of

to

. These are again the trajectories of

in

.

As a conclusion, we have defined for any vector field

on

an induced vector

on the derived stack of stable maps

. When

is either

, or to

, the zeros of

on

. Therefore, the derived stack of zeros

is a Deligne-Mumford derived stack over

, which can be written
as a disjoint union of proper Deligne-Mumford derived stack

,
and is starting point to study enumerative properties of algebraic trajectories.
However,
the derived stack

is not quasi-smooth in general, and
extracting numerical or cohomological invariants out of it requires more work. We will see
in the next section that

2.
Localized Euler class in the derived setting

In this section we study the zero locus of a section
s
of
a perfect complex
E
, of amplitude contained in
[0,1]
, over
a quasi-smooth derived Deligne-Mumford stack
X
. We will see how we can
associate several cohomological, numerical and categorical invariants
to such data. In the next section, it will be applied to the special
case where
E=\mathbb{T}_{\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X)}
and
s=\nu_{g,n}
is the induced vector field of Definition
1.2.3
.

We fix once for all a quasi-smooth derived Deligne-Mumford stack
X
over
k
,

E\in\mathsf{Perf}(X)
of amplitude contained in
[0,1]
and

s:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to E
a morphism in
\mathsf{Perf}(X)
. We denote by
Z(s)\subset X
the derived
substack of zeros of
s
, which is defined to be

\mathbf{Spec}\,(\mathcal{O}_{X}\otimes_{Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E^{\vee})}\mathcal{O}_{X})
, where the first map

Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E^{\vee})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
is induced by the section
s
, and the second
map
Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E^{\vee})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
is the natural augmentation (i.e. the previous map, with
s=0
).

When the perfect complex
E
is of amplitude
0
(i.e. is a vector bundle),

Z(s)
is quasi-smooth and
thus gives rise to a virtual fundamental class
[Z(s)]^{vir}\in G_{0}(Z(s))
, in the Grothendieck
group of coherent sheaves (or in some Borel-Moore homology theory, see Appendix). However, when
E

is no more a vector bundle, then
Z(s)
is not quasi-smooth in general, as
it is the derived intersection of quasi-smooth derived stacks inside an ambient quasi-smooth
derived stack. The virtual class
[Z(s)]^{vir}
thus no more makes sense in this generality. However,
we will see that the Euler class do exists as a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant class (with support on
Z(s)
)
on
\mathbb{V}(E[1])=\mathbf{Spec}\,(Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E[1]))
, the linear stack associated to the shifted complex
E[1]
.
The purpose of this section is to explain the construction of this class, as well as of the
numerical and cohomological invariants that can be extracted from it. We will also provide a categorical interpretation
in terms of certain categories of singularities associated to functions of degree
-1
(Section
2.3
).

2.1.
The refined Euler class

We consider the linear derived
stack
\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})=\mathbf{Spec}\,Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E^{\vee})
, associated to the dual complex
E^{\vee}
(see
[
Mon21
,
Toë14
]
for more on linear stacks).
It comes equipped with a natural projection
\pi:Z\to X
making it into
an affine derived scheme relative to
X
.
The derived stack
\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})
is thus itself
a derived Deligne-Mumford stack which is affine over
X
. Moreover,
as
E^{\vee}
has its amplitude contained in
[-1,0]
,
Z
is also quasi-smooth and so is the natural
projection
\pi:\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})\to X
.

The projection
\pi
possesses two sections
e:X\to\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})
and
s:X\to\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})
. The section
e
is the
zero section and corresponds to the natural augmentation
Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E^{\vee})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The section

s
is induced by
s:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to E
and by the induced morphism of commutative
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras

Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E^{\vee})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
. We have an
\infty
-functor

\underline{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(s^{*}e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}),-):\mathsf{QCoh}(X)\longrightarrow\mathsf{QCoh}(X).

We let
\mathcal{A}:=\underline{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}),e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))
, considered
as a quasi-coherent associative
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra. By construction, the above
\infty
-functor
factors as

\textstyle{\mathsf{QCoh}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{A}-\mathsf{Mod}^{qcoh}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{QCoh}(X),}

where
\mathcal{A}-\mathsf{Mod}^{qcoh}
is the
\infty
-category of
quasi-coherent
\mathcal{A}
-modules, and the second
\infty
-functor is the
forgetful
\infty
-functor. The first
\infty
-functor will be denoted by

Kos_{s}:\mathsf{QCoh}(X)\to\mathcal{A}-\mathsf{Mod}^{qcoh}
.

By construction, the underlying quasi-coherent complex of
\mathcal{A}

is naturally equivalent to
\oplus_{n\geq 0}(Sym^{n}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E^{\vee}[1]))^{\vee}
. Therefore, there exists
a canonical inlcusion morphism
E[-1]\to\mathcal{A}
corresponding to the the
n=1
factor. This in turns induces
a morphism on cohomology sheaves

H^{1}(E)[-2]\to H^{*}(\mathcal{A})
. The graded algebra
H^{*}(\mathcal{A})
is moreover graded commutative, as

\mathcal{A}
underlies an
E_{\infty}
-structure, and therefore the above morphism induces a natural morphism of (underived) graded

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
-algebras

\mathcal{B}:=Sym_{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})}(H^{1}(E))\longrightarrow H^{*}(\mathcal{A}),

where
H^{1}(E)
sits in weight
2
in the graded algebra
\mathcal{B}
.
We warn the reader here that the
Sym_{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})}
must be understood in the non-derived setting.
In particular, any quasi-coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module
M\in\mathcal{A}-\mathsf{Mod}^{qcoh}
gives rises
to a sheaf of graded
\mathcal{B}
-modules by considering
H^{*}(M)
with its graded
H^{*}(\mathcal{A})
-module structure
and induced graded
\mathcal{B}
-module structure from the above morphism.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

For any perfect complex
M\in\mathsf{Perf}(X)
,
the sheaf of graded
\mathcal{B}
-modules
H^{*}(Kos_{s}(M))
is
locally of finite type.

Proof.
The statement is local for the étale topology on
X
and we can thus
assume that
X=\mathbf{Spec}\,A
is affine. Moreover, as
\mathcal{O}_{X}

generates perfect complexes (because
X
is assumed to be affine), it is enough to prove the statement for

M=\mathcal{O}_{X}
. We can present
E
as the fiber of a morphism
d
of vector bundles

\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d}
\textstyle{E_{1}}
. This defines a closed embedding
of total spaces
j:\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}(E_{0}^{\vee})
as well as an
epimorphism of graded algebras
H^{0}(Sym_{A}(E_{1}))\simeq Sym_{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})}(H^{0}(E_{1}))\to\mathcal{B}
.

The derived scheme
\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})
sits inside
\mathbb{V}(E_{0}^{\vee})
as the fiber at
0

of the morphism
\mathbb{V}(E_{0}^{\vee})\to\mathbb{V}(E_{1}^{\vee})
induced by

. This implies that
for two bounded coherent complexes
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
on

\mathbb{V}(\mathbb{E}^{\vee})
, the quasi-coherent complex
\underline{Hom}(j_{*}(\mathcal{F}),j_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))

comes equipped with a canonical structure of a module over
Sym_{A}(E_{1}^{\vee}[1])
.
This structure is moreover such that we have

\underline{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\simeq\underline{Hom}_{Sym_{A}(E_{1}^{\vee}[1])}(A,\underline{Hom}(j_{*}(\mathcal{F}),j_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))).

In our situation, we apply this to
\mathcal{F}=e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=s_{*}(M)
, and we
therefore see that because
M
is perfect,
j_{*}(\mathcal{F})
and
j_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
are again
perfect over
\mathbb{V}(E_{0}^{\vee})
.
Therefore,
\underline{Hom}(j_{*}(\mathcal{F}),j_{*}(\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
is perfect
A
-module, and thus it is bounded
with coherent cohomologies. As an
Sym_{A}(E^{\vee}[1])
-module, it thus
lies in the thick triangulated subcategory of generated by
H^{0}(A)
-module of finite type (considered
as
Sym_{A}(E^{\vee}[1])
-modules via the augmentation
Sym_{A}(E^{\vee}[1])\to A\to H^{0}(A)
. By the formula above,
this implies that
\underline{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is, as a module over

\underline{End}_{Sym_{A}(E_{1}^{\vee}[1])}(A)
, in the thick triangulated subcategory generated
by
\underline{End}_{Sym_{A}(E_{1}^{\vee}[1])}(A)\otimes_{A}P
with
P
an
H^{0}(A)
-module of finite type.
In particular,

H^{*}(\underline{Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}))
is a finitely generated graded module over

H^{*}(\underline{End}_{Sym_{A}(E_{1}^{\vee}[1])}(A))\simeq Sym_{H^{0}(A)}(H^{0}(E_{1}))\otimes_{H^{0}(A)}H^{*}(A)
, and thus
over
Sym_{H^{0}(A)}(H^{0}(E_{1}))
because
H^{*}(A)
is a finite
H^{0}(A)
-module.
This graded module is the image of
H^{*}(Kos_{s}(M))
by the canonical epimorphism

Sym_{H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})}(H^{0}(E_{1}))\to\mathcal{B}
, and thus we conclude that
H^{*}(Kos_{s}(M))
is also finitely generated
as a graded
\mathcal{B}
-module.
\Box

We now consider the determinant line bundle
\omega_{E}=det(E)
on
X
and consider

Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1})\in\mathcal{A}-\mathsf{Mod}^{qcoh}
. By the above lemma, we have a graded

\mathcal{B}
-module
H^{*}(Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))
. By construction,

\mathcal{B}
can be canonically identified with
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])})
, the
H^{0}
-sheaf of rings
of functions on
\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee}[1])\to X
, where the graduation is now induced by the
canonical
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\mathbb{V}(E[1])
given by weight
2
dilation along the fibers.
In order to make this weight
2
-action specific, and to distinguish it
from the natural weight
1
-action we will consider later, we will use the notation

\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])
.

The graded

\mathcal{B}
-module
H^{*}(Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))
thus defines a class in the Grothendieck
group of graded modules of finite type, which by definition is the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant
Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves on the truncation
\tau_{0}\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])
.
As the pushforward along the inclusion
\tau_{0}\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])\hookrightarrow\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])
induces an
isomorphism on Grothendieck group of coherent sheaves (by dévissage, see Appendix),
we get this way a well defined class

[H^{*}(Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))]\in G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])).

Finally, we note that
H^{i}(Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))=Ext^{i}(s^{*}e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}),\omega_{E}^{-1})
is zero
outside of
Z=Z(s)\subset X
the zero locus of
s
, and thus the class

[H^{*}(Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))]
above in fact lies in the
G
-theory with support on
Z
,
or, again by dévissage, in
G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z})
, where

\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z}=\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])\times_{X}Z

is the restriction of
\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])
to
Z
.

Definition 2.1.2
.

The class
[H^{*}(Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))]\in G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z})
defined above
is called the
refined Euler class of the section
s
. It is denoted by

\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s):=[H^{*}(Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))]\in G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z}).

We conclude this section with a couple of comments.

The bundle case.
When
E
is a vector bundle, then
the graded ring
\mathcal{B}
is simply
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and
H^{*}(Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))
is
a graded
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
-module of finite type. In fact,
Kos_{s}(\omega_{E}^{-1})
is
a perfect complex on
X
, which by Grothendieck duality is

s^{*}e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X})[-r]
, where
r
is the rank of
E
(note that
\omega_{E}[r]
is the
dualizing complex of the closed embedding
s:X\to\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})
). Therefore, the
class
[\widetilde{Eu}(s)]\in G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z})\simeq G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(Z)\simeq G_{0}(Z)[t,t^{-1}]
is nothing else than

\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s)=(-1)^{r}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}[Tor^{\mathcal{O}_{X})}_{i}(e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}s_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}))]t^{i}.

The value at
t=-1
of this Laurent polynomial is, up to a sign, thus the usual
Euler class of
s

\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s)(t=-1)=(-1)^{r}[s^{*}e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X})]\in G_{0}(Z)

where
Z=Z(s)
is the zero locus of
s
. Using base change for the cartesian diagram of derived stacks

\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{e}
\textstyle{\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee}),}

we see that
\widetilde{Eu}(s)(t=-1)
is the usual (virtual) fundamental class
[Z]^{vir}:=[\mathcal{O}_{Z}]\in G_{0}(Z)

of the derived scheme
Z
.

Interpretation in terms of singular supports.

The class
\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s)
of Definition
2.1.2

is inspired, and very closely related to, the singular support of coherent sheaves of
[
AG15
]
.
Indeed, we can apply
[
AG15
, Thm. 4.18]
to the bounded coherent complexes
e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
and

s_{*}(\omega_{E}^{-1})
on
the quasi-smooth derived stack
\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E^{\vee})
, and get
a graded coherent sheaf on the singular schemes of

\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E^{\vee})
. In our setting, we consider
the relative version over
X
, as both
e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
and
s_{*}(\omega_{E}^{-1})
are relatively
perfect over
X
,
and thus this support lies on the relative singular scheme of
\mathbb{V}(E^{\vee})\to X
. This relative
singular scheme is nothing else than
\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])
, and our class
\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s)
is thus an
incarnation of
the relative singular support of
Hom(e_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}),s_{*}(\omega_{E}^{-1}))
, or rather the relative
characteristic cycle (i.e. the singular support but counted with its natural multiplicities).

2.2.
The virtual Euler series and Euler numbers

In the previous paragraph we have constructed the refined Euler class
\widetilde{Eu}(s)
. We now explain
how this
class can be somehow pushed down to
Z\subset X
itself, under the form of a rational function
with coefficients in Borel-Moore homology of
Z
. When
Z
is proper (e.g. when
X
is so),
this can be used in order to define Euler numbers which morally count the number of zeros of the
section
s
.

We start by consider the projection
p:\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z}\to Z
, pull-back from the projection

\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])\to X
along the closed embedding
Z=Z(s)\hookrightarrow X
. The projection

p
is an affine morphism equipped with a natural
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action of dilations along the
fiber (with weight

2
in our setting). Algebraically,
this corresponds to the graded simplicial commutative
\mathcal{O}_{Z}
-algebra
Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{Z}}(E[1])
,
where
E

is given weight
2
. We thus get a direct image
on quasi-coherent complexes

p_{*}:\mathsf{QCoh}([\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z}/\mathbb{G}_{m}])\to\mathsf{QCoh}(Z\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}).

As usual,
we will identify the
\infty
-category
\mathsf{QCoh}(Z\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})
with the
\infty
-category of
graded quasi-coherent complexes on
Z
.
Using that
E
is perfect, it is easy to see that this push-forward sends
coherent complexes to graded quasi-coherent complexes
M
on
Z
satisfying the following
two conditions.

(1)

For each
n
, the
graded piece
M(n)
is bounded coherent on
Z
.

(2)

For
n<<0
we have
M(n)=0
.

We let
\widehat{\mathsf{Coh}}(Z\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})\subset\mathsf{QCoh}(Z\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})
be this full sub-
\infty
-category. Note that it contains
\mathsf{Coh}(Z\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})
,
the
graded coherent complexes on
Z
, but is strictly bigger as it also contains
objects with infinitely many non-zero positive weight pieces. Symbolically,
objects in
\mathsf{Coh}(Z\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})
will be denoted by

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}M(i).t^{i}
, and thus will be considered
as Laurent formal power series with coefficients in
\mathsf{Coh}(Z)
. Another natural notation
for
\widehat{\mathsf{Coh}}(Z\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})
is thus
\mathsf{Coh}(Z)[[t]][t^{-1}]
.

The pushfoward
\infty
-functor

p_{*}:\mathsf{Coh}([\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z}/\mathbb{G}_{m}])\to\mathsf{Coh}(Z)[[t]][t^{-1}]

then induces
a morphism on the corresponding Grothendieck groups

p_{*}:G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z})\longrightarrow G_{0}(Z)[[t]][t^{-1}].

Finally, we can compose with the Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch transformation (see Appendix)

\tau_{Z}:G_{0}(Z)[[t]][t^{-1}]\longrightarrow H_{2*}^{BM}(Z,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-*))[[t]][t^{-1}],

where the right hand side is formal power series with coefficients in Borel-Moore homology.
The composed
morphism will be denoted by

\psi_{Z}:G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z})\longrightarrow H_{2*}^{BM}(Z,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-*))[[t]][t^{-1}].

The following result is a version of the existence of Hilbert polynomials and is well known.

Propositon 2.2.1
.

The morphism

\psi_{Z}:G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z})\longrightarrow H_{2*}^{BM}(Z,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-*))[[t]][t^{-1}]

factors through
the subgroup consisting of formal Laurent series

there exist integers

Proof.
We start by assuming that

sheaf of
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
-modules
\mathcal{F}
is a quotient of a vector bundle

on

. Under this
assumption, the perfect complex

can be presented as the fiber of a morphism of
vector bundles
d:E_{0}\to E_{1}
. In this case, we have a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant closed embedding

setting over
Z
. The morphism
p_{*}
can then be written as

q_{*}j_{*}
where

is the natural projection, and as

preserves
bounded coherent complexes, the proposition reduces to the case where

is a shift of
a vector bundle.
By homotopy invariance, the pull-back now induces an isomorphism

p^{*}:G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(Z)\simeq G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E_{1})_{Z}).

Moreover, by the projection formula (see Appendix) we have
p_{*}p^{*}(x)=p_{*}(1).x
, where
we consider
G_{0}(Z)[[t]][t^{-1}]
as a module over the ring

K_{0}(Z)[[t]][t^{-}1]
and
1

in

Therefore, any element

.
As the GRR transformation

on
K
-groups, we see that it is enough to prove there is an integer

The element

S_{t^{2}}([E_{1}^{\vee}])=\sum_{i\geq 0}[Sym^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{Z}}(E_{1}^{\vee})].t^{2}

Finally, for the general case, we can use the Chow envelope techniques of

[

]

to produce a proper morphism

Remark 2.2.2

A slightly weaker conclusion of Proposition

For this, we remind the notion of critical value of a rational function

with coefficients
in some field

By definition, the critical value of

not additive in general.

Remark 2.2.4
.

(1)

2.3.
Categories of shifted singularities

In this section we introduce a categorification of what we have seen so far. For this
we introduce certain categories of singularities for shifted potentials, that is
for functions of cohomological degree
-1
. In the same manner that
categories of singularities for a usual potential
f
reflects the singularities of
f
,
these categories of shifted singularities reflects singularities of shifted functions
in the sense that they see the locus where the shifted function is not quasi-smooth.

Let
Y
be a quasi-smooth derived Deligne-Mumford stack and
f:Y\to\mathbb{A}^{1}[-1]:=\mathbf{Spec}\,k[\epsilon_{-1}]
be a function of degree
-1
(here
\epsilon_{-1}
sits is cohomological
degree
-1
). The function
f
can also be considered as a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-algebras

\mathcal{O}_{Y}[\epsilon_{-1}]\to\mathcal{O}_{Y},

that sends
\epsilon_{-1}
to
f
. Therefore, any perfect complex
M
on
Y

is also a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{Y}[\epsilon_{-1}]
-module. We will say that
M\in\mathsf{Perf}(Y)

is
f
-perfect if it is also a perfect
\mathcal{O}_{Y}[\epsilon_{-1}]
-module, and denote
by
\mathsf{Perf}(Y,f)\subset\mathsf{Perf}(Y)
the full sub-
\infty
-category of
f
-perfect complexes.

We then set

\mathsf{Sing}(Y,f):=\mathsf{Perf}(Y)/\mathsf{Perf}(Y,f),

where the quotient is taken in
k
-linear dg-categories up to Morita equivalences.
In the same manner, when
Y
comes equipped with a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action, and
f:Y\to\mathbb{A}^{1}[-1]

is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant, for the standard
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{1}[-1]
of weight
1
,
we have an equivariant version

\mathsf{Sing}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(Y,f):=\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(Y)/\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(Y,f).

We note that
\mathsf{Sing}(Y,f)
is naturally enriched over
\mathsf{Perf}(Y)
, and thus can be
considered as a quasi-coherent stack of dg-categories over
Y
(see

[
Toë12
]
for the general notion of quasi-coherent
stacks of dg-categories). As such,
it is important to notice the following fact.

Propositon 2.3.1
.

As a quasi-coherent stack of dg-categories on
Y
,
\mathsf{Sing}(Y,f)
(resp.
\mathsf{Sing}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(Y,f)

in the equivariant case) is supported
on the (closed) locus where
f:Y\to\mathbb{A}^{1}[-1]
is not quasi-smooth.

Proof.
We have to show that if
f:Y\to\mathbb{A}^{1}[-1]
is quasi-smooth
then
\mathsf{Sing}(Y,f)\simeq 0
, or equivalently that all perfect complexes on
Y

are
f
-perfect. For this, consider the graph

g:Y\to Y[\epsilon_{-1}]:=Y\times\mathbb{A}^{1}[-1]
of the morphism
f
.
As
f
is quasi-smooth, so is
g
. Moreover,
g
is a closed immersion and thus
proper. As a result,
g_{*}:\mathsf{QCoh}(Y)\to\mathsf{QCoh}(Y[\epsilon_{-1}])
preserves
perfect complexes. This is precisely the statement that
\mathsf{Perf}(Y,f)=\mathsf{Perf}(Y)
.

\Box

In our specific situation we apply this to
Y=\mathbb{V}(E[1])
and

f_{s}:\mathbb{V}(E[1])\to\mathbb{A}^{1}[-1]

is the function induced from the section
s
. Indeed,
s:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to E
induces
a morphism
s[1]:\mathcal{O}_{X}[1]\to E[1]
, and thus a morphism on the corresponding
linear stacks

\mathbb{V}(E[1])\to\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{O}_{X}[1])\simeq X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}[-1],

whose second projection is, by definition, our function
f_{s}
. This function is moreover compatible with
the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action by weight
1
. We thus have a dg-category

\mathsf{Sing}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]),f_{s})=\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]))/\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]),f_{s}).

We want to see the dg-category
\mathsf{Sing}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]),f_{s})
, together with its natural object

\omega_{E}^{-1}\in\mathsf{Sing}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]),f_{s})
,
as some sort of categorification of the pair

(G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z}),\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s))
where
\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s)

is our refined Euler class.
Indeed, we will exhibit below a natural
\infty
-functor

|-|^{t}:\mathsf{Sing}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]),f_{s})\longrightarrow\mathsf{Coh}_{\heartsuit}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1]))_{Z},

where
\mathsf{Coh}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{\heartsuit}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1]))_{Z}
is the abelian category of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant
sheaves on
\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])
set theoretically supported on
Z
, such that
[|\omega_{E}^{-1}|^{t}]=\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s)

in
G_{0}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}^{(2)}(E[1])_{Z})
.

Construction of the functor
|-|^{t}
.
In order to define the functor
|-|^{t}
, first we notice that we have to distinguished two objects in

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}[\epsilon_{-1}]
-modules. We have

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}
, endowed with its
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}[\epsilon_{-1}]
-module structure
coming from the augmentation
\epsilon_{-1}\to 0
. We also have

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}
with its natural
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebra morphism

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}[\epsilon_{-1}]\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}
sending
\epsilon_{-1}

to
f_{s}
. These are two different
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}[\epsilon_{-1}]
-module structures
on the same object
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}
itself.
More generally,
for any
M\in\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]))
we have two different

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}(E[1])}[\epsilon_{-1}]
-module structures on
M
, denoted by

M\{0\}
and
M\{f_{s}\}
.
For
M\{0\}
,
\epsilon_{-1}
acts by
0
, whereas
for
M\{f_{s}\}
the element
\epsilon_{-1}
acts by mutiplication by
f_{s}
.
For the sake of simplicity,
M\{0\}
will simply be denoted by
M
, but we will
keep the notation

for the second one.

We start by constructing a dg-functor

|-|:\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]))\longrightarrow\mathsf{Perf}^{gr}(Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E[1])[u]),

where
u
is a variable in cohomological degree
2
and weight
-1
(note here
that

has weight
1
), and

are sheaves
of perfect graded
Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E[1])[u]
-dg-modules.
This dg-functor is essentially given by negative cyclic homology, and
the weight

The action of

The full sub-dg-category
\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]),f_{s})

|-|:\mathsf{Sing}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]),f_{s})\longrightarrow\mathsf{Perf}^{gr}(Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E[1])[u,u^{-1}]).

with the same weight

implies
that

\mathsf{Perf}^{gr}(Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E[-1])[t,t^{-1}])

sits in cohomological degree

and

-1
. This last dg-category is naturally equivalent to
\mathsf{Perf}(Sym_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E[-1]))
,

We consider the determinant line bundle
\omega_{E}^{-1}=det(E)^{-1}

\mathbb{V}(E[1])
which sits in
\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{V}(E[1]))

Propositon 2.3.2
.

With the above notations we have

[|\omega_{E}^{-1}|^{t}]=\widetilde{Eu}_{E}(s)

The dg-functor

.

]

Finally, our

3.
Applications

In this final section, we will apply the results and constructions of the previous section to the specific
case of the derived stack of stable maps endowed with an induced vector field as in Definition
1.2.3
.

3.1.
Categories associated to a vector field

Let
X
be a smooth and proper algebraic variety, and
\nu\in H^{0}(X,\mathbb{T}_{X})
a global vector field on
X
. We will
construct three invariants of
\nu
, based on the induced vector field
\nu_{0,2}
on
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2}(X)
. These
are of three different levels, 2-categorical, categorical and numerical/homological, and are
somehow related by some form of decategorification. They are all incarnations of a possible algebraic
version of Morse homology, or more precisely the specific point of view based
on
moduli broken lines
exposed in
[
LT18
]
.

We first set
\mathcal{M}:=\coprod_{\beta}\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2}(X,\beta)
, where
\beta
runs over all
curve classes in
H_{2}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-1))
. The additive monoid of curve classes will be denoted by
N\subset H_{2}(\overline{X},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-1))
.
As a side comment,
\mathcal{M}
is not quasi-compact, and when considering non-quasi-compact derived stacks
we will only consider coherent complexes supported on quasi-compact substacks. In particular,
our notation for
\mathsf{Coh}(\mathcal{M})
will mean
\oplus_{\beta}\mathsf{Coh}(\mathcal{M})
rather than the
infinite product
\prod_{\beta}\mathsf{Coh}(\mathcal{M})
. The same convention will hold each time we
consider
\mathsf{Coh}
of some derived stack which is an infinite disjoint union of
quasi-compact derived stacks.

The two marked points of prestable curves will be denoted by
0
and
\infty
, and we think of a stable
map
f:C\to X
as a path from
f(0)
to
f(\infty)
. The gluing construction provides a composition morphism

\mu:\mathcal{M}\times_{X}\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M},

where the two maps
\mathcal{M}\to X
defining the fibered product on the left hand side
are the two evaluation morphisms. This composition makes
\mathcal{M}
into an associative monoid (without units)
in the
\infty
-category
\mathbf{dSt}/(X\times X)
of derived stacks over
X\times X
endowed with
the monoidal structure given by convolution. Another possible terminology here could be that

\mathcal{M}
is a categorical object in derived stacks whose object of objects is
X
. As such,

\mathcal{M}
is very similar to the path groupoid of a topological space.

The vector field
\nu
induces a vector field
\nu_{0,2}
on
\mathcal{M}
as explained in Proposition
1.2.1
. Moreover,
\nu
and

\nu_{0,2}
are compatible with the evaluation
\mathcal{M}\to X\times X
as well as with the composition
morphism
\mu
in an obvious sense. As a result, the derived scheme of zeros
\nu_{0,2}
, denoted
by
Z\subset\mathcal{M}
, comes equipped with an induced composition map

\mu:Z\times_{Z_{0}}Z\longrightarrow Z

making it again into an associative monoid (without units) in
\mathbf{dSt}/(Z_{0}\times Z_{0})
,
where
Z_{0}\subset X
is the derived subscheme of zeros of
\nu
.

The 2-category of algebraic trajectories.

Let
ev:Z\to Z_{0}\times Z_{0}
be the evaluation morphism. We consider

\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)\subset\mathsf{Coh}(Z)
, the full sub-dg-category
of coherent complexes which are of finite Tor-dimension over
Z_{0}\times Z_{0}

(i.e. that are relatively perfect with respect to
ev
). This is a
\mathsf{Perf}(Z_{0}\times Z_{0})

linear dg-category, and will be considered as a quasi-coherent sheaf of dg-categories
over
Z_{0}\times Z_{0}
(see
[
Toë12
]
). We note that the monoid structure on

induces a monoid structure on
\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)
,
as an object in quasi-coherent sheaves of dg-categories over
Z_{0}\times Z_{0}
endowed with
the convolution monoidal structure. In concrete terms, this monoid structure is given by

\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)\otimes_{k}\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)\longrightarrow\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)

sending an object
(E,E^{\prime})
to
\mu_{*}(d^{*}(E\boxtimes E^{\prime}))
, where

is the natural embedding. Note that

does preserve coherent complexes which are relatively perfect over

even though
it does not preserve coherent complexes in general.
The dg-functor
\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)\otimes_{k}\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)\longrightarrow\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)
is linear over the
multiplication (tensor product)
\mathsf{Perf}(Z_{0})\otimes\mathsf{Perf}(Z_{0})\to\mathsf{Perf}(Z_{0})
and thus
descends to the mentioned monoid structure

\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)\otimes_{\mathsf{Perf}(Z_{0})}\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)\to\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev).

We therefore have defined a "linear
2
-category" (without units), whose "set" of object is
Z_{0}
,
whose dg-category of morphisms is given by
\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)
.

Definition 3.1.1
.

The
2
-category of algebraic trajectories of
\nu
on
X
is

\mathsf{Coh}(Z/ev)
, considered as a monoid in
Dg^{c}(Z_{0}\times Z_{0})

A very important special case is when

is of dimension zero, and thus is of the form

\coprod_{i\in I}\mathbf{Spec}\,A_{i}
for some local artinian

, where

is the set of

\nu
in

The derived stack

then splits as

By our convention,

The category of algebraic trajectories.

Definition 3.1.2

The

Numerical and homological invariants.
We now go one step further in the decategorification and

[

An easy example.
We finish this section by the most simple example, namely

and
\beta=1

\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2}(X,\beta)\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

the evaluation map. Given
(a,b)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
outside of the diagonal,
the corresponding stable map

is given by any linear endomorphism of

sending

to

and
\infty
to

, the corresonding stable map is such that

has two components, one with the two marked points entirely contracted sent to

one identified with
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with the identity map. Let

be the standard

with two simple zeros at

and

. The induced vector field

is the direct sum

as a vector field on

3.2.
An algebraic dynamical Zeta function

We now turn to the case of genus
1
. Let
\nu
be again a vector field on
a smooth and proper scheme
X
. For each curve class
\beta\in N
we
have the total Euler series of the vector field
\nu_{1,0}

on
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{1,0}(X,\beta)
of stable maps of genus
1
with no marked points.
The zeros of
\nu_{1,0}
corresponds to "periodic orbits" of
\nu
in
X
,
that is elliptic curves
f:E\to X
whose image is globally invariant by

\nu
. Let us fix a polarization
\omega
on
X
, so that
we have a degree map
\beta\mapsto|\beta|

|-|:=deg(\omega.-):N\to\mathbb{N}.

We suggest to form the following generating series

Z(X,\nu;(t,z)):=exp\left(-\sum_{\beta\in N}\frac{N_{\beta}}{|\beta|}z^{\beta}\right),

where
N_{\beta}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(t)
is the total Euler series of
\nu_{1,0}

on
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{1,0}(X,\beta)
. This generating series is an element of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(t)[[N]]
, the
completed group ring of
N
over the field of rational function
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(t)
.

We like to think of
Z(X,\nu;(t,z))
as the algebraic analogue of Ruelle’s dynamical
zeta function (see e.g.

[
Rue92
, §1.4-(B)]
) that counts periodic orbit of the flow associated to a global vector field.
It is tempting to ask if
Z(X,\nu;(t,z))
is rational, but we currently have not enough intuition
whether this is a reasonable statement to expect or not. We also note that
similar generating series, without any vector field considerations, already appear
in
[
Man98
]
.

3.3.
Higher genus considerations

So far we have started with a global vector field
\nu
on
X
, so that
(irreducible) invariant curves can only be of genus
0
or
1
. In particular,
the zero locus of the induced vector field
\nu_{g,n}
on
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is not a very pertinent
object to consider when

, as this corresponds
to stable maps that factor through the zero locus of
\nu
in
X
.
In order to get something interesting
for all genuses, we have to allow ourselves to replace vector fields by more general
foliations
.

It is a general fact from derived foliation theory, see
[
TVc
,
TVb
]
, that
any foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
induces a derived foliation
\mathcal{F}_{g,n}
on

\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
, but again, when
g>1
the singularities of this
induced foliation is a rather poor geometric object (it consists essentially of pointwise
invariant curves, i.e. stable maps to the singular locus of the original
foliation on
X
). It is however possible to replace the zero locus of
\nu_{g,n}
on

\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
with a
more relevant derived stack
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{\mathcal{F}}
of

\mathcal{F}
-equivariant stable maps
defined as follows. For simplicity of exposition, we
suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a one dimensional foliation on
X
, defined by
a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
together with a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
s:\mathcal{L}\to T_{X}
.
This defines a one dimensional foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, possibly with singularities at the
points in
X
where
s
vanishes.
By definition, an
S=\mathbf{Spec}\,A
-point in the
derived stack
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{\mathcal{F}}
consists of a pair

(f,u)
, where
f:C\to X
is stable map (where
C\to S
is a relative
curve of genus
g
with n marked points), and
u
is a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules

u:f^{*}(\mathcal{L})\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{C}(-\Sigma),

making commutative the diagram

\textstyle{f^{*}(\mathcal{L})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s}
\scriptstyle{u}
\textstyle{f^{*}(\mathbb{T}_{X})}
\textstyle{\mathbb{T}_{C/S}(-\Sigma)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T_{C/S},\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Tf}

where
\Sigma=\coprod_{n}S\hookrightarrow C
is the relative divisor of marked points in
C
.
This will define
a derived Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{\mathcal{F}}
, endowed with a forgetful map
to
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
, from which we can indeed
extract interesting numerical invariants. As in the case studied in this paper,
these derived stacks are not quasi-smooth, and thus only Euler series as in Section
2.2
will make sense in
general,
unless specific conditions on
\mathcal{F}
ensures that we have quasi-smoothness. When
\mathcal{L}
is the trivial
line bundle, so
s
is global vector field on
X
, the derived stack
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{\mathcal{F}}

coincides with the derived locus of
\nu_{g,n}
. Essentially all of our results and constructions in the previous sections extend from
vector fields to more general one dimensional foliations, simply by using
\mathbb{R}\bar{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{\mathcal{F}}
.
The general study of the derived stack of
\mathcal{F}
-equivariant stable maps
will appear elsewhere.

4.
Appendix: K-theory and G-theory of quasi-smooth derived stacks

We collect in this Appendix a few facts about the
K
-theory and
G
-theory of derived stacks
that we have used in the main text. The interested reader will find more details e.g. in

[
Kha22
]
.

Recall that for a
k
-cdga
A
(non-positively graded and Noetherian, for simplicity), an
A
-dg-module
M
is said to be
coherent
if it is cohomologically bounded, and each
H^{i}(M)
is finitely generated over
H^{0}(A)
.

If
X
is a Noetherian derived Artin stack, we denote by
\mathsf{QCoh}(X)
the derived
\infty
-category of quasi-coherent complexes on
X

(see
[
Toë14
]
, 3.1).
\mathsf{QCoh}(X)
is a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category (via the derived tensor product over
\mathcal{O}_{X})
, and we denote by
\mathsf{Perf}(X)
its full subcategory consisting of objects which are dualizable with respect to this tensor product. We write
\mathsf{Coh}(X)
(respectively
\mathsf{Coh}(X)_{\geq 0}
, respectively
\mathsf{Coh}(X)_{\leq 0}
) for the full subcategory of
\mathsf{QCoh}(X)
consisting of complexes
E
such that, for any smooth map
u:S=\mathrm{Spec}\,A\to X
,
u^{*}E
is a coherent (respectively, and connective, respectively, and co-connective)
A
-dg module. The pair
(\mathsf{Coh}(X)_{\geq 0},\mathsf{Coh}(X)_{\leq 0})
defines a t-structure on
\mathsf{Coh}(X)
(i.e. a usual t-structure on the homotopy category
\mathrm{h}\mathsf{Coh}(X)
,
[
Lur17
, 1.2]
), whose heart will be denoted by
\mathsf{Coh}_{\heartsuit}(X)
. Note that
\mathsf{Coh}_{\heartsuit}(X)
is equivalent to the abelian category of coherent complexes on the truncation
\tau_{0}(X)
of
X
.

Both
\mathsf{Perf}(X)
and
\mathsf{Coh}(X)
are stable
\infty
-categories (
[
Lur17
, 1.1]
), and therefore (
[
Bar16
,
BGT13
]
) we can consider their (connective)
K
-theory spectra,
\mathbf{K}(X):=\mathbf{K}(\mathsf{Perf}(X))
,
\mathbf{G}(X):=\mathbf{K}(\mathsf{Coh}(X))
.

Recall that a morphism
f:X\to Y
of derived Artin stacks is (derived)
lci
if it is locally of finite and the relative cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{f}
is of Tor-amplitude
\leq 1
. Then,
X
is
quasi-smooth
(or, synonymously,
lci
) if
X\to\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathbb{C}
is lci. Note that an arbitrary (a priori non-noetherian) derived Artin stack that is lci over an arbitrary regular noetherian base derived Artin stack is automatically noetherian and its structure sheaf is coherent.

It is easy to see that a lci morphism
X\to Y
is of finite Tor-amplitude (e.g.
[
Kha22
, Lemma 1.15]
). The pullback
f^{*}:\mathbf{K}(Y)\to\mathbf{K}(X)
exists for any morphism
f:X\to Y
of derived Artin stacks (because the inverse image preserves perfect complexes), and the pushforward
f_{*}:\mathbf{K}(X)\to\mathbf{K}(Y)
exists if
f
is proper and lci.
On
G
-theory, a morphism
f:X\to Y
induces a pullback
f^{*}:\mathbf{G}(Y)\to\mathbf{G}(X)
if
f
has finite Tor-dimension (e.g. it is lci) because the inverse image preserves coherence, and
f
induces a pushforward
f_{*}:\mathbf{G}(X)\to\mathbf{G}(Y)
if
f
is proper and of finite cohomological dimension.

The pushforward along the inclusion
\tau_{0}(X)\to X
induces an equivalence
\mathbf{G}(\tau_{0}(X))\simeq\mathbf{G}(X)
(
[
Bar16
, Thm. 6.1]
).

Note that, if
\mathcal{E}\in\mathsf{Perf}(X)
and
\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{Coh}(X)
, then
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{Coh}(X)
(derived tensor product), so that we get a well defined map of spectra

\cap:\mathbf{K}(X)\otimes\mathbf{G}(X)\to\mathbf{G}(X)

inducing a
\mathbf{K}(X)
-module structure on
\mathbf{G}(X)
.

We have the following
projection formula
(
[
Kha22
, Proposition 3.7]
). If
f:X\to Y
is proper and of finite cohomological dimension, we have the following equality in
\mathbf{G}(Y)

y\cap f_{*}(x)=f_{*}(f^{*}(y)\cap x)

for any
x\in\mathbf{G}(X)
, and
y\in\mathbf{K}(Y)
.

Given a (homotopy) pullback diagram of derived Artin stacks

\textstyle{X^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{f^{\prime}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Y^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{Y}

with
g
proper and of finite cohomological dimension, and
g
is of finite Tor-amplitude, we have a canonical homotopy
f^{*}g_{*}\simeq g^{\prime}_{*}f^{\prime*}
of morphisms
\mathbf{G}(Y^{\prime})\to\mathbf{G}(X)
(see
[
Kha22
, Proposition 3.8]
).

For a quasi-smooth derived Artin stack
X
of finite type (over
k
), there is a Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch transformation

\tau_{X}:\mathrm{G}_{0}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{2*}^{BM}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-*)):=\oplus_{n}\mathrm{H}_{2n}^{BM}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-n))

where
H_{2n}^{BM}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(-n))
denotes the
2n
-th Borel-Moore homology of the truncation
\tau_{0}X
(
[
Kha22
, 6.3]
,
[
Kha
, Def. 2.1]
).

Note that an arbitrary (a priori non-noetherian) derived Artin stack that is lci over an arbitrary regular noetherian base derived Artin stack is automatically noetherian and its structure sheaf is coherent. In particular, a quasi-smooth derived Artin stack
X
over
k
has a fundamental class
[X]=[\mathcal{O}_{X}]\in\mathrm{G}_{0}(X)
, which under the isomorphism
\mathrm{G}_{0}(X)\simeq\mathrm{G}_{0}(\tau_{0}X)
corresponds to a class
[X]_{vir}\in\mathrm{G}_{0}(\tau_{0}X)
, called the virtual fundamental class of
X
. We have

[X]^{vir}=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[H^{i}(\mathcal{O}_{X})]

(the sum being finite since
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is bounded). Analogously (
[
Kha
, Construction 3.6]
), there is a fundamental class
[X]_{\mathrm{BM}}\in\mathrm{H}_{2d}^{BM}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(d)
in Borel-Moore homology, where
d
is the virtual dimension of
X
(i.e. the Euler characteristic of its cotangent complex). The classes
\tau_{X}([X])
and
[X]_{\mathrm{BM}}
differ by multiplication by the inverse of the Todd class of the cotangent complex of
X
(Kontsevich Formula, see
[
Kha22
, Thm. 6.12]
).

There is a Chern character ring morphism

Ch:K_{0}(X)\to H^{2*}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(*)):=\oplus_{n}\mathrm{H}^{2n}(X,\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(n))

to
\ell
-adic cohomology of
X
, that enters in a Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch (GRR) formula for lci morphism; we do not need such a GRR formula in the main text.

Toeplitz operators on CR manifolds and group actions
Andrea Galasso, Chin-Yu Hsiao

Abstract
Let ( X , T 1 , 0 ​ X ) (X,\,T^{1,0}X) be a connected orientable compact CR manifold of dimension 2 ​ n + 1 , n ≥ 1 2n+1,\,n\geq 1 with non-degenerate Levi curvature. In this paper, we study the algebra of Toeplitz operators on X X and we establish star product for some class of symbols on X X . In the second part of this paper, we consider a compact locally free Lie group G G acting on X X and we investigate the associated algebra of G G -invariant Toeplitz operators.



Keywords:
CR manifolds, Toeplitz operators, group actions

Introduction

Let
(X,\,T^{1,0}X)

Given a pseudo-differential operator

T_{P}:=S^{(q)}\circ P\circ S^{(q)}

[

]

acting on

G
-invariant Toeplitz operators, secondly we study the associated Fourier component in the presence of a transversal locally free circle action.

. For each
l=1,\dots\lvert G_{y}\rvert
, the oscillatory integral

\int_{G}(1-\chi_{l}(g))T_{P,-}(x,\,g\circ y)\,\mathrm{dV}_{G}(g)\equiv 0\qquad\text{on }U\,.

We take
\chi_{1}
to be a conjugate
G
-invariant bump function, that is,

\chi_{1}(ghg^{-1})=\chi_{1}(h)
, for every
g,h\in G
. The reason why the bump function can be taken to be conjugate
G
-invariant is as follows. If
\chi
is a bump function in a neighborhood of the origin in the Lie algebra, then we can take a new bump function

\eta(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}{v})=\int_{G}\chi(g\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}{(v)}g^{-1})\mathrm{dV}_{G}(g)\,.

It is invariant under the action of conjugation. In order to prove that
\eta
is a bump function one can see that
\eta(e)=1
and
\eta
is constant on a small ball center in the origin. In fact, the scalar product on the Lie algebra is
\mathrm{Ad}_{g}
-invariant, this means that
\lVert v\rVert=\lVert\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(v)\rVert
. Furthermore
\chi
can be chosen to be constant on spherical shell, in particular is zero outside a ball of radius say
r
. This means that
\chi(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathrm{Ad}_{g}(v)))
is zero whenever
v
is chosen so that
\lVert v\rVert>r
. This implies that also
\eta
is zero outside a ball of radius
r
.

Hence, we can write

T^{G}_{P,-}(x,\,y)=\sum_{l=1}^{\lvert G_{y}\rvert}\int_{V_{l}}\chi_{l}(g)\,T_{P,-}(x,\,g\circ y)\,\mathrm{dV}_{G}(g)\,.

(152)

Furthermore, notice that
V_{l}
can be chosen so that
V_{l}=g_{l}\,V_{1}
,
l=2,\ldots,\lvert G_{y}\rvert
, and

\chi_{l}(g)=\chi_{1}(g_{l}^{-1}\,g)\,,

we can change variables
g^{-1}_{l}\,g\mapsto g
in (
152
) and obtain

T^{G}_{P,-}(x,\,y)\equiv\sum_{l=1}^{\lvert G_{y}\rvert}\int_{V_{1}}\chi_{1}(g)\,T_{P,-}(x,\,g_{l}g\circ y)\,\mathrm{dV}_{G}(g)\,.

Thus
T^{G}_{P,-}(x,\,y)
is a sum of
\lvert G_{y}\rvert
oscillatory integrals, let us denote each summand
T^{G}_{P,-}(x,\,y)_{l}
and focus on one of them.

Given
g=\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}{v}
, we have
g_{l}g\circ y=g_{l}gg_{l}^{-1}\circ y=\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{l}}(v)})\circ y
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle T^{G}_{P,-}(x,\,y)_{l}=\int_{V_{1}}\chi_{1}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}{v})\,T_{P,-}(x,\,\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}({\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{l}}(v)})\circ y)\,\mathrm{dV}_{\mathfrak{g}}(v)

Notice that
\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{l}}\in GL(\mathfrak{g})
and we can change variables
\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{l}}(v)\mapsto v
in
\mathrm{d}v
. The absolute value of Jacobian determinant of the latter transformation is
|\det\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{l}}\rvert=1
, since the group is uni-modular. Thus, we get

\displaystyle T^{G}_{P,-}(x,\,y)_{l}=\int_{V_{1}}\chi_{1}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}{v})\,T_{P,-}(x,\,\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}({v})\circ y)\,\mathrm{dV}_{\mathfrak{g}}(v)

where we use that
\chi_{1}
is invariant under conjugation:

\chi_{1}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathrm{Ad}_{g_{l}}(v))=\chi_{1}(g_{l}\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}{(v)}g_{l}^{-1})=\chi_{1}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}{v})\,.

Thus each summand
T^{G}_{P,-}(x,\,y)_{l}
does not depend on
l
, (
132
) can be deduced arguing as in the proof of Theorem
1.5
in

Proof of Theorem

Let us consider the integral

T^{G}_{f,m}(x,\,y)=\frac{1}{2\,\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{G}T_{f}(x,\,e^{\imath\,\theta}\cdot g\circ y)\,e^{\imath m\,\theta}\mathrm{dV}_{G}(g)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,.

It is easy to proof that the oscillatory integral has a rapidly decreasing asymptotic as
m\rightarrow+\infty
far away from a local neighborhood of those elements
(e^{\imath\,\theta},\,g)\in S^{1}\times G
such that

e^{\imath\,\theta}\cdot g\circ y=y\,.

(154)

We recall some notations. Since the action is free
\mu^{-1}(0)/S^{1}
is an orbifold, let us denote with
\pi\,:\,\mu^{-1}(0)\rightarrow\mu^{-1}(0)/S^{1}
the projection. Furthermore the action of
S^{1}
commutes with the one of
G
, then we have a smooth locally free action of
G
on
\mu^{-1}(0)/S^{1}
. Given
y\in X
and
g\in G_{\pi(y)}
, there exist
\lvert S_{x}^{1}\rvert
elements
e^{\imath\,\theta_{g,j}}\in S^{1}
(
j=1,\dots,\lvert S_{x}^{1}\rvert
) such that

g\circ y=e^{-\imath\,\theta_{g,j}}\circ y\,.

Thus, all the elements
(e^{\imath\,\theta},\,g)\in S^{1}\times G
satisfying (
154
) are of the form

e^{\imath\theta_{g,j}}\cdot g
for each
g\in G_{\pi(y)}
. Thus,

\displaystyle T^{G}_{f,\,m}(x,\,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\,\pi}\sum_{h\in G_{\pi(y)}}\sum_{j=1}^{\lvert S_{x}^{1}\rvert}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{V_{0}}\mathrm{d}v\left[T_{f}(x,\,e^{\imath(\theta_{h,j}+\theta)}\cdot h\,e^{\imath\,v}\circ y)\,e^{\imath m(\theta_{h,j}+\theta)}m(v)\right]

where
\delta>0
,
V_{0}
is a small neighborhood of the identity (we omit the bump functions for ease of exposition). We use the same trick as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
:

e^{\imath\theta_{h}}\cdot h\,e^{\imath\,v}h^{-1}\,h\circ y=h\,e^{\imath\,v}h^{-1}\circ y=\,e^{\imath\,\mathrm{Ad}_{h}(v)}\circ y\,;

changing variables
v\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}_{h}(v)
we obtain

\displaystyle T^{G}_{f,\,m}(x,\,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\,\pi}\sum_{h\in G_{\pi(y)}}\sum_{j=1}^{\lvert S_{x}^{1}\rvert}e^{\imath m\,\theta_{h,j}}\,\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\mathrm{d}\theta\,\int_{V_{0}}\mathrm{d}v\left[T_{f}(x,\,e^{\imath\,\theta}\cdot e^{\imath\,v}\circ y)\,e^{\imath m\theta}m(v)\right]\,.

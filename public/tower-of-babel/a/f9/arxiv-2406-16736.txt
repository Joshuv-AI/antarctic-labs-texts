Structure of geodesics for Finsler metrics arising from Riemannian g.o. metrics


Abstract
Homogeneous geodesics of homogeneous Finsler metrics derived from two or more Riemannian geodesic orbit metrics are investigated. For a broad newly defined family of positively related Riemannian geodesic orbit metrics, geodesic lemma is proved and it is shown that the derived Finsler metrics have also geodesic orbit property. These Finsler metrics belong to the newly defined class of the α i \alpha_{i} -type metrics which includes in particular the ( α 1 , α 2 ) (\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}) metrics. Geodesic graph for the sphere S 7 = Sp ⁡ ( 2 ) ​ U ​ ( 1 ) / Sp ⁡ ( 1 ) ​ diagU ​ ( 1 ) {\mathrm{S}}^{7}={\mathrm{Sp(2)}}{\mathrm{U}}(1)/{\mathrm{Sp(1)}{\mathrm{diag}}{\mathrm{U}}(1)} with geodesic orbit Finsler metrics of the new type ( α 1 , α 2 , α 3 ) (\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}) , arising from two or more Riemannian geodesic orbit metrics, is analyzed in detail. This type of metrics on S 7 S^{7} is one of the missing cases in a previously published classification of geodesic orbit metrics on spheres.

Introduction

On a smooth manifold, Finsler metrics of the type
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
were introduced
by S. Deng and M. Xu in
[
9
]
as metrics of the form

\displaystyle F(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\alpha_{1}(y_{1}),\alpha_{2}(y_{2})),

(1)

where
\alpha_{i}
are nondegenerate symmetric bilinear forms on complementary subbundles of the tangent bundle
and
y_{i}
are projections of vectors
y\in TM
onto these subbundles.
These metrics were discussed also in some further papers,
but we did not find analysis of the function
f

(conditions which
f
must satisfy to obtain Finsler metric
F
),
neither nontrivial examples of such metrics.
On the other hand, in the paper
[
16
]
, M.A. Javaloyes and M. Sánchez considered metrics of the type

\displaystyle F(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{L(F_{1}(y),\dots,F_{k}(y),\beta_{1}(y),\dots,\beta_{l}(y))},

(2)

where
F_{i}
are Finsler metrics and
\beta_{j}
are one-forms on
M
.
They derived conditions for the function
L
(to obtain the Finsler metric
F
)
and these conditions allow to construct many explicit examples of such metrics.
We are going to consider a special case of metrics (
2
)
on homogeneous manifolds. They naturally provide examples of metrics of type (
1
)
and lead also to their generalization.

A homogeneous Finsler manifold
(M,F)

admits a connected Lie group
G
of isometries which acts transitively on
(M,F)
.
If we choose an origin
p\in M
and denote by
H
the isotropy group at
p
,
the manifold
(M,F)
can be naturally identified with the homogeneous space
(G/H,F)
.
We denote by
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}
be the Lie algebras of
G
and
H
, respectively.
One can find a reductive decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{m}}+{\mathfrak{h}}
,
where
{\mathfrak{m}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
is an
{\rm Ad}(H)
-invariant vector subspace
for the adjoint representation
{\mathrm{Ad}}\colon H\times{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
of
H
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
If we fix a reductive decomposition,
the vector space
{\mathfrak{m}}
is naturally identified with the tangent space
T_{p}M
.
Using this identification, given the Finsler metric
F
on
M
, we obtain the
{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-invariant
Minkowski norm and the
{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-invariant fundamental tensor on
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Standard concepts in Finsler geometry can be found in monographs, for instance

[
5
]
by D. Bao, S.-S. Chern and Z. Shen,

[
21
]
by Y.-B. Shen and Z. Shen,
or
[
8
]
by S. Deng.
For the notion of derivative along a curve and geodesics, we use the Chern connection.

A geodesic
\gamma(t)
through
p
is
homogeneous
if it is an orbit
of a one-parameter subgroup of the group of isometries
G=I_{0}(M)
.
If
(M,F)
is identified with the homogeneous space
(G/H,F)
,
there exists a nonzero
geodesic vector

X\in{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\gamma(t)={\rm exp}(tX)(p)
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The homogeneous space
(G/H,F)
is called a
geodesic orbit space
or just
g.o. space
,
if every geodesic of
(G/H,F)
is homogeneous.
Homogeneous geodesics and g.o. spaces were studied broadly in Riemannian geometry by many authors,
see for example
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
,

[
15
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
and the references therein,
or the monograph
[
6
]
by V. Berestovskii and Yu. Nikonorov.
One of the techniques for the study of g.o. spaces is based on the concept of geodesic graphs,
proposed by J. Szenthe in
[
22
]
, in the affine setting.
This concept was further developed by O. Kowalski and S. Nikčević in
[
17
]
for the Riemannian setting.
A
geodesic graph
is an
{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-equivariant map
\xi\colon{\mathfrak{m}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}

such that
X+\xi(X)
is a geodesic vector for each
o\neq X\in{\mathfrak{m}}
.
We are interested in the algebraic structure of the geodesic graph.
The existence of a linear geodesic graph is equivalent with the natural reductivity of the space
G/H
.
In general, the components of the Riemannian geodesic graph are rational functions
\xi_{i}=P_{i}/P
,
where
P_{i}
and
P
are homogeneous polynomials and
{\mathrm{deg}}(P_{i})={\mathrm{deg}}(P)+1
.
See for example the recent survey paper
[
10
]
by the second author for
details and references to geodesic graphs on various classes of Riemannian g.o. manifolds.

Recently, g.o. spaces attained attention in Finsler geometry.
In
[
25
]
, Z. Yan and S. Deng studied relations of some Finsler g.o. spaces with Riemannian g.o. spaces,
for example for Randers g.o. metrics on spheres
S^{2n+1}={\mathrm{U}}(n+1)/{\mathrm{U}}(n)

and for weakly symmetric metrics on some nilpotent groups.
In
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
, the second author investigated
Randers geodesic orbit metrics and special families of weakly symmetric Finsler metrics
on modified H-type groups using geodesic graphs.
Relations of Finslerian geodesic graphs with the Riemannian geodesic graphs were observed.
In
[
13
]
, second author studied general homogeneous Finsler
(\alpha,\beta)
metrics,
which cover Randers metrics as a special case.
It was proved that all
(\alpha,\beta)
metrics arising from a Riemannian g.o. metric
\alpha

and an invariant one-form
\beta
are Finsler g.o. metrics, possibly with respect to an extended
isometry group. See also the paper
[
24
]
by Z. Yan for a study of
(\alpha,\beta)
metrics.
In the paper
[
2
]
, present authors illustrated constructions of geodesic graphs
for geodesic orbit
(\alpha,\beta)
metrics on spheres.
Classification list of these metrics on spheres and on projective spaces was given.
Geodesic orbit Finsler metrics on spheres were studied also by M. Xu in
[
23
]
.
A classification list of types of these metrics for each possible isometry group was given,
with some missing cases.
In the present paper, we analyze the g.o. property of Finsler metrics of the type (
2
),
without one-forms and with the assumption that
F_{i}
are norms determined by Riemannian g.o. metrics
g_{i}
,
hence
F_{i}=\sqrt{g_{i}}
.
We focus on the natural family of positively related initial Riemannian g.o. metrics, see Definition
2
.
We derive geodesic lemma for the characterization of geodesic vectors
and we prove that the Finsler metric
F
given by the formula

\displaystyle F(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{L(\sqrt{g_{1}(y)},\dots,\sqrt{g_{k}(y)})},

(3)

is also a geodesic orbit metric.
It will be observed that these metrics provide natural examples of metrics of the type (
1
).
This type can be further generalized to the new
\alpha_{i}
-type metrics, see Definition
1
below.
With an example of
S^{7}
, we illustrate the construction of geodesic graph in detail for metrics given by formula
(
3
), which appear to be also of the type
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
.
Metrics of this type are missing in the classification list in
[
23
]
.

Preliminaries

Let us consider a homogeneous manifold
M
expressed as a homogeneous space
G/H

with a fixed reductive decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}+{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Let
p\in M
and
F_{p}
be an
{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-invariant Minkowski norm on
{\mathfrak{m}}\simeq T_{p}M
.
For any
q\in M
, we obtain a Minkowski norm
F_{q}
at
T_{q}M
by the formula

\displaystyle F_{q}(\sigma_{*}X)=F_{p}(X),\qquad X\in T_{p}M,

where
\sigma\in G
and
\sigma(p)=q
. It leads to the Finsler metric
F
on
M
.
We shall work mostly just with the vector space
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
We denote the
{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-invariant
Minkowski norm on
{\mathfrak{m}}
also by
F
and its fundamental tensor on
{\mathfrak{m}}
by
g
.

Definition 1

Let
(G/H,F)
be a homogeneous Finsler space with a reductive decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{m}}+{\mathfrak{h}}
.
Consider the adjoint action of
H
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
, which leads to the irreducible decomposition

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oplus_{i=1}^{s}{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}.

Choose symmetric positively definite
{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-invariant bilinear forms
\alpha_{i}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i},i=1\dots s

and let
y_{i}
be the corresponding projections of a vector
y\in{\mathfrak{m}}
onto
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
.
The Minkowski norm
F
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
and the corresponding homogeneous Finsler metric on
G/H
is of the
\alpha_{i}
-type
if there exist a smooth function
f\colon[0,\infty)^{s}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle F(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\alpha_{1}(y_{1}),\dots,\alpha_{s}(y_{s})),\qquad y\in{\mathfrak{m}}.

(4)

This definition obviously covers the homogeneous
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
metrics from
[
9
]
.
However, as far as we know, necessary and sufficient conditions for the function
f
,
which imply that the function
F
is a Minkowski norm, were never investigated.
We are also not aware of any explicit and nontrivial examples of these metrics.

Another approach how to construct new Finsler metrics from given Finsler metrics was proposed in
[
16
]
,
see formula (
2
) above.
For the simplicity and technical reasons later, we shall avoid the one-forms and
consider given metrics
F_{i}
to be Riemannian. Hence we write the new Minkowski norm
F
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
in the form
(
3
), where
g_{i}
are scalar products on
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
According to Theorem 4.1 in
[
16
]
, to obtain a Minkowski norm
F
, the continuous function
L

must satisfy:

(i) be smooth and positive away from
0
,

(ii) be positively homogeneous of degree 2,

(iii)
L_{,i}\geq 0
, for
i=1,\dots,k
,

(iv)
Hess(L)
be positive semi-definite,

(v)
L_{,1}+\dots+L_{,k}>0
.

Here the comma in
L_{,i}
means the derivative with respect to the corresponding coordinate.
Obviously, there are many functions which satisfy these conditions,
for example, one can choose
L=\sqrt{g_{1}}+\dots+\sqrt{g_{k}}
.

Definition 2

Let
G/H
be a homogeneous space with a reductive decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{m}}+{\mathfrak{h}}
.
Consider the
{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-invariant irreducible decomposition
{\mathfrak{m}}=\oplus_{i=1}^{s}{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}

and let
\alpha_{i}
be
{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-invariant scalar products on the respective spaces
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}

We consider the family of scalar products

\displaystyle g(c_{1},\dots,c_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}\cdot\alpha_{i},

for any numbers

This family of scalar products on
{\mathfrak{m}}
and corresponding family of Riemannian metrics on
G/H

will be called scalar products positively related and metrics positively related.

If the scalar products
g_{j},j=1,\dots,k
, are positively related,
then obviously the Finsler metric

given by the formula (
3

for some function

\alpha_{i}
-type.
We are going to analyze geodesic orbit property for this type of Minkowski norms on
{\mathfrak{m}}

and corresponding Finsler metrics on
M

Throughout the rest of the paper, we always assume that the function

satisfies the conditions
(i)-(v) above.

Let us recall the geodesic lemma, which was proved for general Finsler metrics by D. Latifi in

Lemma 3

Let

be a homogeneous Finsler space with a reductive decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{m}}+{\mathfrak{h}}

A nonzero vector

is geodesic vector if and only if it holds

g_{y_{\mathfrak{m}}}(y_{\mathfrak{m}},[y,u]_{\mathfrak{m}})=0\qquad\forall u\in{\mathfrak{m}},

(5)

Lemma 4

Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
be homogeneous Riemannian metrics on
G/H

and let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{m}}+{\mathfrak{h}}
be a reductive decomposition.
We use the same notation for the bilinear forms on
{\mathfrak{m}}

Let
F=\sqrt{L(\sqrt{g_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{g_{k}})}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
, which gives a homogeneous Finsler metric on
G/H

For arbitrary vectors

, the fundamental tensor
g
of

satisfies the formula

\displaystyle g_{y}(y,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j}(y)\cdot g_{j}(y,v),

(6)

where the functions
B_{j}(y)
are given by

\displaystyle B_{j}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{L_{,j}}{2\,\sqrt{g_{j}(y,y)}}=\frac{L_{,j}}{2\,F_{j}(y)},\quad j=1\dots k.

(7)

Proof.

Let
y,v\in{\mathfrak{m}}
be arbitrary fixed vectors.
For arbitrary
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for the vector
w=y+tv\in{\mathfrak{m}}
, it holds

\displaystyle F^{2}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L(\sqrt{g_{1}(w,w)},\dots,\sqrt{g_{k}(w,w)}).

We now differentiate with respect to
t
and apply the chain rule.
We obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F^{2}(w)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}L_{,j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{j}(w,w)}}g_{j}(w,v)

and

\displaystyle 2\,g_{y}(y,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F^{2}(w)}{\partial t}\Big|_{t=0}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}L_{,j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{g_{j}(y,y)}}g_{j}(y,v).

Using the notation (
7
), we obtain the statement.

\hfill\square

Conjecture 5

Consider a family of positively related Riemannian metrics.
If one metric of this family is a g.o. metric, then all metrics from this family are also g.o. metrics.

\displaystyle C_{i}(y)

Theorem 7

Let
G/H
be a homogeneous space with a reductive decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}+{\mathfrak{m}}
and with the

{\mathrm{Ad}}(H)
-irreducible decomposition
{\mathfrak{m}}=\oplus_{i=1}^{s}{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
.
Let
g_{j},j=1\dots k,
be from a family of positively related Riemannian metrics on
G/H
,
all of which are g.o. metrics.
Then any homogeneous Finsler metric of the type

F=\sqrt{L(\sqrt{g_{1}},\sqrt{g_{2}},\dots,\sqrt{g_{k}})}
on

is also a g.o. metric.

Proof.

We consider condition (
12
) and we fix the arbitrary vector

.
The values

\displaystyle C_{1}(y)\cdot\alpha_{1}+\dots+C_{s}(y)\cdot\alpha_{s}

\hfill\square

4
Example

S^{7}={\mathrm{Sp}}(2)\cdot{\mathrm{U}}(1)/{\mathrm{Sp}}(1)\cdot{\mathrm{diag}}({\mathrm{U}}(1))

On the Lie algebra level, we choose a basis
\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{sp}}(1)
,
such that, with respect to this basis, an element of
{\mathfrak{h}}
is given by a matrix

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}ih_{1}&-h_{2}-ih_{3}&0&0\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}h_{2}-ih_{3}&-ih_{1}&0&0\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}0&0&0&0\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}0&0&0&0\end{array}\right).

The reductive complement
{\mathfrak{m}}
in the decomposition
{\mathfrak{sp}}(2)={\mathfrak{sp}}(1)+{\mathfrak{m}}

and its basis
B=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{4},Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3}\}
will be chosen such that,
with respect to this basis
B
, each element of
{\mathfrak{m}}
is given by the matrix

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&0&x_{1}+ix_{2}&-x_{3}-ix_{4}\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}0&0&x_{3}-ix_{4}&x_{1}-ix_{2}\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}-x_{1}+ix_{2}&-x_{3}-ix_{4}&iz_{1}&-z_{2}-ix_{3}\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}x_{3}-ix_{4}&-x_{1}-ix_{2}&z_{2}-iz_{3}&-iz_{1}\end{array}\right).

It is straightforward to determine the Lie brackets on
{\mathfrak{g}}\simeq{\mathfrak{sp}}(2)
.
One can find explicit formulas for example in
[
2
]
,
where geodesic graphs for
(\alpha,\beta)
metrics on this space were calculated,
in a modified reductive decomposition.
The adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{h}}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}
is given by the operators

\displaystyle{\rm ad}(H_{1})|_{\mathfrak{m}}=A_{12}+A_{34},\quad{\rm ad}(H_{2})|_{\mathfrak{m}}=A_{13}-A_{24},\quad{\rm ad}(H_{3})|_{\mathfrak{m}}=A_{14}+A_{23}.

We further introduce the operator

\displaystyle W={\rm ad}(Z_{1})|_{\mathfrak{m}}=2B_{23}-A_{12}+A_{34}.

Here
A_{ij}
and
B_{ij}
are the elementary operators acting nontrivially by

A_{ij}(X_{i})=X_{j}
and
A_{ij}(X_{j})=-X_{i}
, resp.

B_{ij}(Z_{i})=Z_{j}
and
B_{ij}(Z_{j})=-Z_{i}
.
We put
\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathrm{span}}\{{\mathfrak{h}},W\}\simeq{\mathrm{sp(1)}}\oplus{\mathrm{u(1)}}
.

We will now consider the family of invariant scalar products with respect to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}

and corresponding invariant Riemannian metrics.
Obviously, the
{\mathrm{Ad}}(\widetilde{H})
-invariant subspaces of
{\mathfrak{m}}
are

{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}={\mathrm{span}}(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})
,
{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}={\mathrm{span}}(Z_{1})
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{3}={\mathrm{span}}(Z_{2},Z_{3})
.
If we choose any
{\mathrm{Ad}}(\widetilde{H})
-invariant scalar products
\alpha_{i}
on
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
, then any scalar product

g=\sum_{i=1}^{3}c_{i}\alpha_{i}
for

is

-invariant.
It is well known that all these scalar products determine invariant Riemannian g.o. metrics on
\widetilde{G}/\widetilde{H}

and we can observe this fact also from the further considerations.
Clearly, metrics from this 3-parameter family are positively related.

Let us now use the basis
B
above and the natural scalar product

in which this basis is orthonormal.
We put
\alpha_{i}=g_{0}|_{{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}
.
Let the scalar products

, be

for some fixed

.
Consider a Finsler metric
F=\sqrt{L(\sqrt{g_{1}},\sqrt{g_{2}},\dots,\sqrt{g_{k}})}
.
Note that
F
can be alternatively written in the form
F=f(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})

for some function
f
determined by
L
.
Hence the Finsler metric
F
belongs to the family of the
\alpha_{i}
-type metrics.
In this case, it is of the
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
type.

For the fundamental tensor of
F
, we have

\displaystyle g_{y}(y,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}B_{j}(y)\cdot g_{j}(y,v)

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}(y)\cdot\alpha_{1}(y,v)+C_{2}(y)\cdot\alpha_{2}(y,v)+C_{3}(y)\cdot\alpha_{3}(y,v),

according to formula (
10
). We use formula (
12
) in the form

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}C_{i}(y)\cdot\alpha_{i}\Bigl(y,[\xi(y),u]_{\mathfrak{m}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{3}C_{i}(y)\cdot\alpha_{i}\Bigl(y,[y,u]_{\mathfrak{m}}\Bigr),\quad\forall u\in{\mathfrak{m}}.

We write each vector
y\in{\mathfrak{m}}
and each vector
\xi(y)\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}
in the forms

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}X_{1}+\dots+x_{4}X_{4}+z_{1}Z_{1}+z_{2}Z_{2}+z_{3}Z_{3},

\displaystyle\xi(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(y)H_{1}+\xi_{2}(y)H_{2}+\xi_{3}(y)H_{3}+\xi_{4}(y)W.

(24)

We obtain the system of linear equations for components
\xi_{i}=\xi_{i}(y)
, whose extended matrix

({\mathbb{A}}|{\mathbb{B}})
is

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc|c}{x_{2}}&{x_{3}}&{x_{4}}&{-x_{2}}&(1-2\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}})z_{1}x_{2}+(1-2\frac{C_{3}}{C_{1}})(z_{2}x_{3}+z_{3}x_{4})\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}{-x_{1}}&{-x_{4}}&{x_{3}}&{x_{1}}&-(1-2\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}})z_{1}x_{1}+(1-2\frac{C_{3}}{C_{1}})(z_{2}x_{4}-z_{3}x_{3})\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}{x_{4}}&{-x_{1}}&{-x_{2}}&{x_{4}}&-(1-2\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}})z_{1}x_{4}+(1-2\frac{C_{3}}{C_{1}})(-z_{2}x_{1}+z_{3}x_{2})\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}{-x_{3}}&{x_{2}}&{-x_{1}}&{-x_{3}}&(1-2\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}})z_{1}x_{3}-(1-2\frac{C_{3}}{C_{1}})(z_{2}x_{2}+z_{3}x_{1})\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}0&0&0&2z_{3}&2z_{1}z_{3}(\frac{C_{2}}{C_{3}}-1)\\
{\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt}0&0&0&-2z_{2}&2z_{1}z_{2}(1-\frac{C_{2}}{C_{3}})\end{array}\right),

using the short notation
C_{i}=C_{i}(y)
. We denote further

\displaystyle K_{1}=K_{1}(y)=\frac{C_{2}}{C_{3}}-2\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}},\quad K_{2}=K_{2}(y)=1-2\frac{C_{3}}{C_{1}},\quad K_{3}=K_{3}(y)=\frac{C_{2}}{C_{3}}-1.

The unique solution of the above system of equations is

\displaystyle\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{K_{1}z_{1}(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2}-x_{4}^{2})+2K_{2}\bigl[z_{2}(x_{2}x_{3}-x_{1}x_{4})+z_{3}(x_{1}x_{3}+x_{2}x_{4})\bigr]}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}},

\displaystyle\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2K_{1}z_{1}(x_{2}x_{3}+x_{1}x_{4})+K_{2}\bigl[z_{2}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}-x_{4}^{2})+2z_{3}(x_{3}x_{4}-x_{1}x_{2})\bigr]}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}},

(32)

\displaystyle\xi_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2K_{1}z_{1}(x_{2}x_{4}-x_{1}x_{3})+K_{2}\bigl[2z_{2}(x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}x_{4})+z_{3}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2})\bigr]}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}},

(33)

\displaystyle\xi_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{3}z_{1}.

(34)

From Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system to incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier-Poisson system: convergence for classical solutions Thanks: June 1, 2019
Mengmeng Guo, Ning Jiang, Yi-Long Luo

Abstract
. For the one-species Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann (VPB) system in the scaling under which the moments of the fluctuations formally converge to the incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier-Poisson (NSFP) system, we prove the uniform estimates with respect to the Knudsen number ϵ \epsilon for the fluctuations. As a consequence, the existence of the global-in-time classical solutions of VPB with all ϵ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \epsilon\in(0,1] is established in whole space under small size of initial data, and the convergence to incompressible NSFP as ϵ \epsilon go to 0 is rigorously justified. Keywords. One-species Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system; incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier-Poisson equations; classical solutions; uniform energy estimates; convergence AMS subject classifications. 76P05; 82C40; 82D05



1.
Introduction

1.1.
One-species Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann system

In this paper, we study the following scaled Vlasov-Poisson-Boltzmann (briefly, VPB) system (see Arsénio-Saint-Raymond’s book
[
8
]
)

(1.1)

\displaystyle\epsilon\partial_{t}f_{\epsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{\epsilon}+\epsilon\gamma\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{\epsilon}=\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}B(f_{\epsilon},f_{\epsilon})\,,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}=\tfrac{\gamma}{\epsilon}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\epsilon}dv-1\Big)\,,

where
\epsilon>0
denotes the Knudsen number, which is the ratio of the mean free path to the macroscopic length scale, and
\gamma>0
is the ratio of the electric repulsion according to Gauss’s law and the Knudsen number. Here the unknown
f_{\epsilon}=f_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)\geq 0
is a nonnegative function standing for the number density of gas particles which have velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
at position

x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
at time
t>0
. The self-consistent electric potential
\phi_{\epsilon}=\phi_{\epsilon}(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}
is coupled with the distribution function
f_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)
through the Poisson equation. The bilinear function
B
with hard-sphere interaction is defined by

(1.3)

B(f,f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}(f^{\prime}f_{1}^{\prime}-ff_{1})|(v-v_{1})\cdot\omega|\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}v_{1}\,,

where

f=f(t,x,v)\,,\quad f^{\prime}=f(t,x,v^{\prime})\,,\quad f_{1}=f(t,x,v_{1}),\quad f^{\prime}_{1}=f(t,x,v_{1}^{\prime})\,,

v^{\prime}=v-[(v-v_{1})\cdot\omega]\omega\,,\quad v_{1}^{\prime}=v_{1}+[(v-v_{1})\cdot\omega]\omega\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\,.

This hypothesis is satisfied for all physical model and is more convenient to work with but do not impede the generality of our results. The system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) describes the evolution of a gas of one species of charged particles (ions or electrons) subject to an auto-induced electrostatic force. We then impose on the initial data of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
)

(1.4)

\displaystyle f_{\epsilon}(0,x,v)=f_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)\geq 0\,.

A function
\phi_{\epsilon,0}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
can thereby be introduced by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}\phi_{\epsilon,0}=\tfrac{\gamma}{\epsilon}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\epsilon,0}\mathrm{d}v-1\Big)\,,

which is consequently the initial value of
\phi_{\epsilon}
.

A physically relevant requirement for solutions to the VPB system are their mass, momentum and energy are preserved with time. This is also an
a priori
property of the VPB system on the torus, which reads

(1.6)

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x=0\,,\ \tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}vf_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x=0\,,

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}f_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)=0\,.

It is well-known that the global equilibrium for the VPB system is the normalized global
Maxwellian

M(v)
defined as

M(v)=\tfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\,e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}\,.

Our goal in current paper is to justify the incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier-Poisson (briefly, NSFP) limit of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). More precisely, we consider the fluctuation around the global Maxwellian with size
\epsilon
(this is called Navier-Stokes scaling, see
[
9
]
),

f_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)=M(1+\epsilon g_{\epsilon}(t,x,v))\,.

We are interested in justifying the limit of the fluctuation
g_{\epsilon}
as the Knudsen number
\epsilon
tends to zero. This leads to the perturbed VPB system

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\epsilon\partial_{t}g_{\epsilon}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}g_{\epsilon}+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}Lg_{\epsilon}-\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}-\gamma\epsilon v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}g_{\epsilon}+\gamma\epsilon\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{v}g_{\epsilon}=Q(g_{\epsilon},g_{\epsilon})\,,\\
\Delta_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}=\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g_{\epsilon}M\mathrm{d}v\,,\end{array}\right.

where the bilinear symmetric operator
Q
is

(1.8)

Q(g,h)=\tfrac{1}{2M}\big[B(Mg,Mh)+B(Mh,Mg)\big]\,,

and the linearized Boltzmann collisional operator
L
takes the form

(1.9)

Lg=-2Q(g,1)=-\tfrac{1}{M}\big\{B(M,Mg)+B(Mg,M)\big\}\,.

Moreover, the collisional frequency for the hard sphere interaction can be defined as (see
[
9
]
for instance)

(1.10)

\displaystyle\nu(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v-v_{1}|M(v_{1})\mathrm{d}v_{1}\,.

Furthermore, the linearized Boltzmann collisional operator
L
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle Lg=\nu g+Kg\,,

where

\displaystyle Kg(v)=-\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{1})\cdot\omega|(g^{\prime}+g_{1}^{\prime}-g_{1})M_{1}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}(v,v_{1})g(v_{1})\mathrm{d}v_{1}

is a self-adjoint compact operator on
L^{2}(Mdv)
with a real symmetric integral kernel
\mathcal{K}(v,v_{1})
(see
[
50
]
for instance). The null space of the operator
L
is the five-dimensional space spanned by the collision invariants
[
9
]

(1.11)

\mathcal{N}=\textrm{Ker}L=\textrm{Span}\{1,v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},|v|^{2}\}\,.

Notice that the projection operator
L
is nonnegative. We also denote
P
by the projection from
L^{2}(Mdv)
to
\mathcal{N}
. In what follows, we write
Pg_{\epsilon}
in the form of coordinates

(1.12)

Pg_{\epsilon}=\{a_{\epsilon}(t,x)+b_{\epsilon}(t,x)\cdot v+c_{\epsilon}(t,x)|v|^{2}\}\,,

where
a_{\epsilon},\ b_{\epsilon}=(b_{\epsilon,1},b_{\epsilon,2},b_{\epsilon,3})
and
c_{\epsilon}
are the coefficients of the macroscopic components
Pg_{\epsilon}
.

Furthermore, without loss of generality, the initial data
f_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)
of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) can be given the form of

(1.13)

\displaystyle f_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)=M(v)\big[1+\epsilon g_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)\big]\geq 0\,,

where
g_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}
is the initial data of the perturbed system (
1.7
), namely,

(1.14)

\displaystyle g_{\epsilon}(0,x,v)=g_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)\,,

Moreover, the function
\phi_{\epsilon,0}
given in (
1.5
) is equivalently defined by

(1.15)

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}\phi_{\epsilon,0}(x)=\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)M(v)\mathrm{d}v\,.

\nu

If
w=1

L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{v}=L^{p}_{x}L^{q}_{v}(1)

The multi-index
\alpha=[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha]
and

in

If each component of

\tilde{\alpha}

(1.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(g,\phi)=\|g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}}+\|(I-P)g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x,v}}

(1.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)=\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\|(I-P)g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x,v}(\nu)}+\|\nabla_{x}Pg\|^{2}_{H^{N-1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\langle g,1\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\|^{2}_{H^{N-1}_{x}}\,.

Let integer
N\geq 3

(g_{\epsilon}(t,x,v),\phi_{\epsilon}(t,x))
satisfying
g_{\epsilon}\in H^{N}_{x,v}
,
\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}\in H^{N}_{x}
, and the global estimate

(1.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon}(t),\phi_{\epsilon}(t))+c_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g_{\epsilon}(t))\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})\,,

where
c_{0}>0

\epsilon

\phi_{\epsilon}(t,x)
and

f(t,x,v)=M(v)(1+\epsilon g_{\epsilon}(t,x,v))\geq 0

1.1
)-(

The next theorem is about the limit to the incompressible NSFP system

(1.19)

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla_{x}u+\nabla_{x}p=\mu\Delta_{x}u+\rho\nabla_{x}\theta\,,\\
&\nabla_{x}\cdot u=0\,,\\
&\partial_{t}(\tfrac{3}{5}\theta-\tfrac{2}{5}\rho)+u\cdot\nabla_{x}(\tfrac{3}{5}\theta-\tfrac{2}{5}\rho)=\kappa\Delta_{x}\theta\,,\\
&\Delta_{x}(\rho+\theta)=\gamma^{2}\rho\,,\end{cases}

where the viscosity
\mu
and heat conductivity
\kappa
are given by

(1.20)

\mu=\tfrac{1}{15}\langle A_{ij},\widehat{A}_{ij}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\,,\quad\kappa=\tfrac{2}{15}\langle B_{i},\widehat{B}_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\,.

Here

A(v)=v\otimes v-\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{3}I,\quad\quad B(v)=(\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{2}-\tfrac{5}{2})v.

More precisely, it can be shown that
[
9
]
, in general, the linearized Boltzmann operator
L
is self-adjoint and its range is exactly the orthogonal complement of its kernel. It follows that
A\in L^{2}(Mdv)
and
B\in L^{2}(Mdv)
belong to the range of
L
. Thus, there are inverses
\widehat{A}\in L^{2}(Mdv)
and
\widehat{B}\in L^{2}(Mdv)
such that

A=L\widehat{A}\,\quad\text{and}\quad B=L\widehat{B}\,,

which can be uniquely determined by the fact that they are orthogonal to the kernel of
L
.

.

\displaystyle g_{\epsilon,0}(x,v)\rightarrow g_{0}(x,v)=\rho_{0}(x)+u_{0}(x)\cdot v+\theta_{0}(x)(\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{2}-\tfrac{3}{2})

strongly in

1.3.
Difficulties and ideas

The key point of current paper is to derive a global-in-time energy bound to the perturbed VPB system (
1.7
) uniformly
\epsilon\in(0,1]
under small size of the initial data. Similar to the incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier limit from the perturbed Boltzmann equation (see
[
36
]
and
[
47
]
, for instance), there are different singularities between the microscopic part and fluid part. So, we naturally divide the kinetic function
g_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)
into two different parts to derive a uniform energy estimate. More precisely, for fixed
(t,x)
, the function
g_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle g_{\epsilon}=Pg_{\epsilon}+(I-P)g_{\epsilon}\,,

where
Pg_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N}
is called the fluid (macroscopic) part of
g_{\epsilon}
with coefficients
(a_{\epsilon},b_{\epsilon},c_{\epsilon})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
and
(I-P)g_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
is the kinetic (microscopic) part of
g_{\epsilon}
. Here
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
is the orthogonal space of
\mathcal{N}
in
L^{2}_{v}
. Plugging the above decomposition into the scaled equation (
1.7
), one gets the macroscopic evolution

(1.25)

\begin{split}\epsilon\partial_{t}(a_{\epsilon}+b_{\epsilon}\cdot v+c_{\epsilon}|v|^{2})+v\cdot\nabla_{x}(a_{\epsilon}+b_{\epsilon}\cdot v+c_{\epsilon}|v|^{2})-\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}=l+h+m\,,\end{split}

where

(1.26)

\displaystyle l=

\displaystyle-\epsilon\partial_{t}(I-P)g_{\epsilon}-v\cdot\nabla_{x}(I-P)g_{\epsilon}-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}L(I-P)g_{\epsilon}\,,

\displaystyle h=

\displaystyle Q(g_{\epsilon},g_{\epsilon})\,,

\displaystyle m=

\displaystyle\gamma\epsilon(v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}g_{\epsilon}-\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{v}g_{\epsilon})\,.

On the other hand,
a_{\epsilon}
,
b_{\epsilon}
and
c_{\epsilon}
obey the local macroscopic balance laws of mass, moment and energy. In fact, multiplying the first
f_{\epsilon}
-equation (
1.1
) of the VPB system by the collision invariant in (
1.11
) and integrating by parts over
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}v+\nabla_{x}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vf_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}v=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vf_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}v+\nabla_{x}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\otimes vf_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}v-\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}v=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}f_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}v+\nabla_{x}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}vf_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}v-2\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vf_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}v=0\,.

By plugging the perturbed form
f_{\epsilon}=M(1+\epsilon g_{\epsilon})
and using the decomposition (
1.12
), we compute all moments appearing in the above system to obtain the following macroscopic balance laws

(1.27)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(a_{\epsilon}+3c_{\epsilon})+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\nabla_{x}\cdot b_{\epsilon}=0\,,

(1.28)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b_{\epsilon}+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\nabla_{x}(a_{\epsilon}+5c_{\epsilon})+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}(I-P)g_{\epsilon},v\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\tfrac{\gamma}{\epsilon}\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}-\gamma(a_{\epsilon}+3c_{\epsilon})\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}=0\,,

(1.29)

\displaystyle 3\partial_{t}a_{\epsilon}+15\partial_{t}c_{\epsilon}+\tfrac{5}{\epsilon}\nabla_{x}\cdot b_{\epsilon}+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}(I-P)g_{\epsilon},|v|^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-2\gamma b_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}=0\,,

(1.30)

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}=\gamma(a_{\epsilon}+3c_{\epsilon})\,,

where the evolution (
1.29
) for
c_{\epsilon}
can be written as

(1.31)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}c_{\epsilon}+\tfrac{1}{3\epsilon}\nabla_{x}\cdot b_{\epsilon}+\tfrac{1}{6\epsilon}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}(I-P)g_{\epsilon},|v|^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}=\tfrac{\gamma}{3}b_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi_{\epsilon}\,.

Our main goal of this paper is to derive the uniform global energy estimates to the perturbed VPB system (
1.7
). We will divide into three steps to achieve our goal:

1) We derive the pure spatial derivative energy estimates, which will give us the kinetic dissipation
\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\|(I-P)g_{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(\nu)}
due to the coercivity of linearized Boltzmann collision operator
L
as shown in Lemma
2.3
. Moreover, the singular term
\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}g_{\epsilon}
will disappear since
\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}g_{\epsilon},\partial^{\alpha}_{x}g_{\epsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
. Thanks to
\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g_{\epsilon},g_{\epsilon})\in\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
, the equality

1.4.
Historical remarks

There has been lot of works on the well-posedness of VPB. In the context of weak solutions, after DiPerna-Lions’ breakthrough on renormalized solutions of the Boltzmann equation
[
22
]
, Lions generalized this theory to Vlasov type equations, including VPB
[
51
]
. In the context of classical solutions, in early 2000, in a series works
[
35
,
33
]
, Guo developed the so-called nonlinear energy method to build up global in time well-posedness of Boltzmann equations, VPB and other kinetic equations. Later on, there are several works on more general collision kernels, among many papers, we only list
[
23
,
24
,
65
,
66
]
.

One of the most important features of the Boltzmann type equations is its connection to the fluid equations. The so-called fluid regimes of the Boltzmann type equation are those of asymptotic dynamics of the scaled Boltzmann equations when the Knudsen number
\epsilon
is very small. Justifying these limiting processes rigorously has been an active research field from late 70’s. Among many results obtained, the main contributions are the incompressible Navier-Stokes and Euler limits. There are two types of results in this field:

(1)

First obtaining the solutions of the scaled Boltzmann equation
uniform
in the Knudsen number
\epsilon
, then extracting a convergent (at least weakly) subsequence converging to the solutions of the fluid equations as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0\,;
.

(2)

First obtaining the solutions for the limiting fluid equations, then constructing a sequence of special solutions (around the Maxwellian) of the scaled Boltzmann equations for small Knudsen number
\epsilon
.

The key difference between the results of type (1) and (2) are: in type (1), the solutions of the fluid equations are
not
known a priori, and are completely obtained from taking limits from the Boltzmann equation. In short, it is “from kinetic to fluid”; In type (2), the solutions of the fluid equations are
known
first. In short, it is “from fluid to kinetic”.

The most successful program in type (1) is the so-called BGL program. As mentioned above, the DiPerna-Lions’s renormalized solutions for cutoff kernel
[
22
]
(also the non-cutoff kernels in
[
6
]
) are the only solutions known to exist globally without any restriction on the size of the initial data so far. From late 80’s, Bardos-Golse-Levermore initialized the program (BGL program in brief) to justify Leray’s solutions to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations from DiPerna-Lions’ renormalized solutions
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
. They proved the first convergence result with 5 additional technical assumptions. After 10 years effects by Bardos, Golse, Levermore, Lions and Saint-Raymond, see for example
[
11
,
52
,
53
,
26
]
, the first complete convergence result without any additional compactness assumption was proved by Golse and Saint-Raymond in
[
28
]
for cutoff Maxwell collision kernel, and in
[
30
]
for hard cutoff potentials. Later on, it was extended by Levermore-Masmoudi
[
49
]
to include soft potentials. Recently Arsenio got the similar results for non-cutoff case
[
7
]
. Furthermore, by Jiang, Levermore, Masmoudi and Saint-Raymond, these results were extended to bounded domain where the Boltzmann equation was endowed with the Maxwell reflection boundary condition
[
55
,
43
,
45
]
, based on the solutions obtained by Mischler
[
56
]
.

The BGL program says that, given any
L^{2}\mbox{-}
bounded functions
(\rho_{0},\mathrm{u}_{0},\theta_{0})
, and for any physically bounded initial data (as required in DiPerna-Lions solutions)
F_{\epsilon,0}=M+\epsilon\sqrt{M}g_{\epsilon,0}
, such that suitable moments of the fluctuation
g_{\epsilon,0}
, say,
(\mathcal{P}(g_{\epsilon,0},v\sqrt{M})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})},(g_{\epsilon,0},(\tfrac{|v|^{2}}{5}-1)\sqrt{M})_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})})
converges in the sense of distributions to
(\mathrm{u}_{0},\theta_{0})
, the corresponding DiPerna-Lions solutions are
F_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)
. Then the fluctuations
g_{\epsilon}
(defined by
F_{\epsilon}=M+\epsilon\sqrt{M}g_{\epsilon}
) has weak compactness, such that the corresponding moments of
g_{\epsilon}
converge weakly in
L^{1}
to
(\mathrm{u},\theta)
which is a Leray solution of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation whose viscosity and heat conductivity coefficients are determined by microscopic information, with initial data
(\mathrm{u}_{0},\theta_{0})
. Under some situations, for example the well-prepared initial data or in bounded domain with suitable boundary condition, the convergence could be strong
L^{1}
.

We emphasize that the BGL program indeed gave a new proof of Leray’s solutions to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation, in particular the energy inequality which can be derived from the entropy inequality of the Boltzmann equation. Any a priori information of the Navier-Stokes equation is
not
needed, and completely derived from the microscopic Boltzmann equation. In this sense, BGL program is spiritually a part of Hilbert’s 6th problem: derive and justify the macroscopic fluid equations from the microscopic kinetic equations (see
[
60
]
).

Another direction in type (1) is in the context of classical solutions. The first work in this type is Bardos-Ukai
[
12
]
. They started from the scaled Boltzmann equation for cut-off hard potentials, and proved the global existence of classical solutions
g_{\epsilon}
uniformly in
0<\epsilon<1
. The key feature of Bardos-Ukai’s work is that they only need the smallness of the initial data, and did not assume the smallness of the Knudsen number
\epsilon
. After having the uniform in
\epsilon
solutions
g_{\epsilon}
, taking limits can provide a classical solution of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with small initial data. Bardos-Ukai’s approach heavily depends on the sharp estimate especially the spectral analysis on the linearized Boltzmann operator
\mathcal{L}
, and the semigroup method (the semigroup generated by the scaled linear operator
\epsilon^{-2}\mathcal{L}+\epsilon^{-1}v\cdot\nabla_{\!x}
). It seems that it is hardly extended to soft potential cutoff, and even harder for the non-cutoff cases, since it is well-known that the operator
\mathcal{L}
has continuous spectrum in those cases. On the torus, semigroup approach has been employed by Briant
[
14
]
and Briant, Merino-Aceituno and Mouhot
[
17
]
to prove incompressible Navier-Stokes limit by employing the functional analysis breakthrough of Gualdani-Mischler-Mouhot
[
32
]
. Again, their results are for cut-off kernels with hard potentials. Recently, there is type (1) convergence result on the incompressible Navier-Stokes limit of the Boltzmann equation. In
[
47
]
, the uniform in
\epsilon
global existence of the Boltzmann equation with or without cutoff assumption was obtained and the global energy estimates were established. Then taking limit as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, it was proved the incompressible Navier-Stokes limit.

Most of the type (2) results are based on the Hilbert expansion and obtained in the context of classical solutions. It was started from Nishida and Caflisch’s work on the compressible Euler limit
[
58
,
18
,
48
]
. Their approach was revisitied by Guo, Jang and Jiang, combining with nonlinear energy method to apply to the acoustic limit
[
38
,
39
,
42
]
. After then this process was used for the incompressible limits, for examples,
[
21
]
and
[
36
]
. In
[
21
]
, De Masi-Esposito-Lebowitz considered Navier-Stokes limit in dimension 2. More recently, using the nonlinear energy method, in
[
36
]
Guo justified the Navier-Stokes limit (and beyond, i.e. higher order terms in Hilbert expansion). This result was extended in
[
46
]
to more general initial data which allow the fast acoustic waves. These results basically say that, given the initial data which is needed in the classical solutions of the Navier-Stokes equation, it can be constructed the solutions of the Boltzmann equation of the form
F_{\epsilon}=M+\epsilon\sqrt{M}(g_{1}+\epsilon g_{2}+\cdots+\epsilon^{n}g_{\epsilon})
, where

can be determined by the Hilbert expansion, and

is the error term. In particular, the first order fluctuation

, where
(\rho_{1},\mathrm{u}_{1},\theta_{1})
is the solutions to the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations.

The main purpose of the current paper is to prove a type (1) convergence result in the context of classical solutions for one-species VPB. This can be viewed as an analogue of incompressible Navier-Stokes limit of the Boltzmann equation recently done by the second-named author with Xu and Zhao

&+\tfrac{b_{k}}{\epsilon^{2}}\|(I-P)g^{n+1}\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(\nu)}+\tfrac{c_{k}}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N\\
1\leq|\beta|\leq k\end{subarray}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{v}^{\beta}(I-P)g^{n+1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\nu)}\\
&\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}\phi^{n}\|_{H^{N}_{x}}\big(\|g^{n+1}\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x,v}}+\|(I-P)g^{n+1}\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x,v}(\nu)}\big)\\
&+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\big(\|g^{n}\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x,v}}+\|g^{n}\|_{H^{N}_{x,v}}\|(I-P)g^{n}\|_{H^{N}_{x,v}(\nu)}\big)\|(I-P)g^{n+1}\|_{H^{N}_{x,v}(\nu)}\end{split}

for all
0\leq|\beta|=k\leq N
.

We define the so-called instant iterating energy
\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g,\phi)

(2.34)

\begin{split}\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g,\phi)=&\|g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}}+a_{N}\|(I-P)g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{N}_{x,v}}\\
+&\tfrac{2}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\big(b_{N}\|(I-P)g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(\nu)}+c_{N}\|(I-P)g\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{N}_{x,v}(\nu)}\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\end{split}

and the initial energy
\mathscr{E}_{N}(g_{0},\phi_{0})
is given as

(2.35)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{N}(g_{0},\phi_{0})=\|g_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}}+a_{N}\|(I-P)g_{0}\|^{2}_{\widetilde{H}^{N}_{x,v}}\,.

It is obvious that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g,\phi)\thicksim\mathcal{E}_{N}(g,\phi)+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|(I-P)g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x,v}(\nu)}\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

Now we claim that there are small
\ell,T\in(0,1]
, independent of
\epsilon
, such that if
0<T\leq 1
,
\mathscr{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})\leq C_{\#}\mathcal{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})\leq C_{\#}\delta\leq\ell
for some constant
C_{\#}>0
and

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g^{n}(t),\phi^{n}(t))\mathrm{d}t\leq 2\ell\,,

then

(2.36)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g^{n+1}(t),\phi^{n+1}(t))\leq 2\ell\,.

Indeed, from taking
k=N
in the inequality (
2.33
) and integrating over
[0,t]
, we derive that for all
t\in[0,T]

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g^{n+1}(t),\phi^{n+1}(t))\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})+(1+t)\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g^{n}(t),\phi^{n}(t))\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g^{n+1}(t),\phi^{n+1}(t))

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(1+\sqrt{t})\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g^{n}(t),\phi^{n}(t))\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g^{n+1}(t),\phi^{n+1}(t))\,,

which immediately implies that for some positive constant
C

(2.37)

\displaystyle\big[\tfrac{3}{4}-C(1+T)\sqrt{2\ell}\big]\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g^{n+1}(t),\phi^{n+1}(t))\leq\ell+4C(1+T)\ell^{2}\,.

We first take
T\in(0,1)
and
\ell\in(0,1)
such that
\tfrac{3}{4}-2C\sqrt{2\ell}\geq\tfrac{5}{8}
, hence
0<\ell<\min\{1,\tfrac{1}{512C^{2}}\}
. Thus, we have

(2.38)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g^{n+1}(t),\phi^{n+1}(t))\leq\tfrac{8}{5}\ell+\tfrac{64C}{5}\ell^{2}

We further restrict
0<\ell<\min\{1,\tfrac{1}{512C^{2}},\tfrac{1}{32C}\}
such that
64C\ell\leq 2
. Then we have

(2.39)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathscr{E}_{N,\epsilon}(g^{n+1}(t),\phi^{n+1}(t))\leq\tfrac{8}{5}\ell+\tfrac{2}{5}\ell=2\ell\,,

and the claim (
2.36
) holds.

Consequently, by employing the standard compactness arguments and taking
n\rightarrow\infty
, we can obtain a solution
(g_{\epsilon},\phi_{\epsilon})
to (
1.7
)-(
1.14
) for any fixed
0<\epsilon\leq 1
. Moreover, the uniform bound (
2.36
) implies the energy bound (
2.7
). Thus, the proof of Lemma
2.5
is completed.
∎

3.
Uniform estimates and global solutions

In this section, our goal is to globally extend the local solutions to (
1.7
)-(
1.14
) constructed in Lemma
2.5
by deriving an energy estimates uniformly in
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. For notational simplicity, we drop the lower index
\epsilon
of
g_{\epsilon}
and
\phi_{\epsilon}
in the perturbed VPB system (
1.7
), i.e.,

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}g+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}g+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}Lg-\tfrac{\gamma}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi-\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi g+\gamma\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}g=\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}Q(g,g)\,,\\
\Delta_{x}\phi=\gamma\langle g,1\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\,.\end{array}\right.

3.1.
Pure spatial derivative estimates: kinetic dissipations

In this subsection, we will consider the energy estimates on the pure spatial derivative of
g
. The following lemma will be established.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
g(t,x,v)
is the solution to the perturbed VMB system (
1.7
)-(
1.14
) constructed in Lemma
2.5
. Then there are constants
\delta
and
C
such that

(3.2)

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\|g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}}\Big)+\tfrac{\delta}{\epsilon^{2}}\|(I-P)g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(\nu)}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\end{split}

for all
0<\epsilon\leq 1
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
with
|\alpha|\leq N
, applying the derivative operator
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}
to the first
g
-equation (
3.1
) yields

(3.3)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g)+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}L\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g-\tfrac{\gamma}{\epsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\phi\\
&=\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q(g,g)+\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g-\gamma\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\\
&+\sum_{0\neq\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\alpha_{1}}(\gamma v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\phi\partial_{x}^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}g-\gamma\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}\partial_{x}^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}g)\,.\end{split}

Then, we take
L^{2}_{x,v}
-inner product in the above equation by dot with
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g
and integrate by parts over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We thereby have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\|+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\langle L\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}-\tfrac{\gamma}{\epsilon}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\phi,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q(g,g),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\gamma\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g-\nabla_{x}\phi\nabla_{v}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\gamma\sum_{0\neq\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\alpha_{1}}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\phi\partial_{x}^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}g-\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{\alpha_{1}}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}\partial_{x}^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}g,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{3}}\,.

First, Lemma
2.3
tells us that there is a constant
\delta>0
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\langle L\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\geq\delta\|(I-P)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\nu)}^{2}\,.

Noticing that the local conservation law of mass is

(3.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle g,1\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\nabla_{x}\cdot\langle g,v\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}=0\,,

we derive from the Poisson equation of
\phi
in (
3.1
), i.e.,
\Delta_{x}\phi=\gamma\langle g,1\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}
, that

(3.7)

\displaystyle-\tfrac{\gamma}{\epsilon}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\phi,\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}=\tfrac{\gamma}{\epsilon}\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\phi,\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla_{x}\cdot\langle g,v\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\phi,\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\langle g,1\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}}=-\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\phi,\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\Delta_{x}\phi\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\,.

Consequently, we arrive at

(3.8)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|\nabla_{x}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}})+\tfrac{\delta}{\epsilon^{2}}\|(I-P)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\nu)}\leq I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
with
|\alpha|\leq N
.

Next, let us estimate the terms
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
on the right-hand side of (
3.4
). We split
g=Pg+(I-P)g
, so that
I_{1}
is decomposed into

(3.9)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q(Pg,Pg),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(I-P)g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{11}}+\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q(Pg,(I-P)g),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(I-P)g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{12}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q((I-P)g,Pg),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(I-P)g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{13}}+\underbrace{\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\langle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q((I-P)g,(I-P)g),\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(I-P)g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{14}}\,,

where we also utilize the fact
Q(g,h)\in\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
. On the other hand, we plug
Pg=a+b\cdot v+c|v|^{2}
into the first term

(3.10)

\displaystyle|I_{11}|\lesssim\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}Q(Pg,Pg)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(I-P)g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha_{1}\leq\alpha}\|\,|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x}a|\,|\partial^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}_{x}a|+|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x}b|\,|\partial^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}_{x}b|+|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x}c|\,|\partial^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}_{x}c|\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(I-P)g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\nu)}\,.

If

\displaystyle\big(\|\nabla_{x}a\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\nabla_{x}b\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\nabla_{x}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\big)\big(\|a\|_{H^{1}_{x}}+\|b\|_{H^{1}_{x}}+\|c\|_{H^{1}_{x}}\big)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}Pg\|_{H^{N-1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|g\|_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

In summary, we get

\displaystyle|I_{11}|\lesssim\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\|\nabla_{x}Pg\|_{H^{N-1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|g\|_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}(I-P)g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\nu)}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,,

where the functionals

1.17

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\|g\|_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|(I-P)g\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}(\nu)}\lesssim\epsilon\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,.

Consequently, we estimate that

(3.12)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq|I_{11}|+|I_{12}|+|I_{13}|+|I_{14}|\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)

for all

.
One notices that integration by parts over

(3.13)

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi,|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}g|^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi,|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}Pg|^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{21}}+\underbrace{\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi,|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(I-P)g|^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{22}}

From Lemma
2.2
and the Sobolev embedding

, we derive that

(3.14)

\displaystyle I_{22}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(I-P)g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\nu)}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,.

If

, the term

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{L^{3}_{x}}\|vPg\|_{L^{6}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|(I-P)g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{H^{1}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}(a,b,c)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|(I-P)g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\nu)}

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|v\partial^{\alpha}_{x}Pg\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}(I-P)g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

2.2

(3.15)

\displaystyle I_{23}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,.

It remains to estimate the term
I_{21}
. If
\alpha\neq 0

I_{21}

\displaystyle I_{21}\lesssim\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{H^{N}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}Pg\|_{H^{N-1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,.

\displaystyle\|Pg\|_{H^{N}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle g,1\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\|_{H^{N-1}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}Pg\|_{H^{N-1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,.

Here we make use of Lemma

. Plugging the estimates (

\displaystyle I_{21}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,.

Consequently, the bounds (

\displaystyle I_{2}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,.

Notice that

\displaystyle I_{3}=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\gamma\sum_{0\neq\alpha_{1}<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\alpha_{1}}\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x}\phi\partial^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}_{x}g-\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}\partial^{\alpha-\alpha_{1}}_{x}g,\partial^{\alpha}_{x}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{31}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\underbrace{\gamma\langle v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\phi g-\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}g,\partial^{\alpha}_{x}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}}_{I_{32}}\,.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g)\,,

and

\displaystyle I_{32}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lesssim\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}_{v}(I-P)g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\nu)}+\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g,\phi)D_{N,\epsilon}(g)

for all

One notices that the quantity

3.4.
Global classical solutions: proof of Theorem
1.1

From the differential inequality (

) in Remark
3.2
and the energy bound (

) in Lemma

, we deduce that for any

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g_{\epsilon},\phi_{\epsilon})\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{N,\epsilon}(g_{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\tfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\|(I-P)g_{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{N}_{x,v}(\nu)}\mathrm{d}t+|t_{2}-t_{1}|

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0\quad\textrm{as}\quad t_{2}\rightarrow t_{1}\,.

Thus the local solution
g_{\epsilon}(t,x,v)
to (
1.7
)-(
1.14
) constructed in Lemma
2.5
is such that the energy functional

is continuous in

.

We now define

\displaystyle T^{*}=\sup\Big\{\tau\geq 0;\ C\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\mathbb{E}_{N,\eta_{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g_{\epsilon},\phi_{\epsilon})(t)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\Big\}\geq 0\,.

From
\mathbb{E}_{N,\eta_{1}}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})\thicksim\mathcal{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})
and the initial condition in Theorem
1.1
, we derive that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{N,\eta_{1}}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})\leq C_{0}\mathcal{E}_{N}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})\leq C_{0}\ell_{0}

for some constant

, where

, where

is mentioned in Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}_{N,\eta_{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g_{\epsilon},\phi_{\epsilon})(0)=C\mathbb{E}_{N,\eta_{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})\leq C\sqrt{C_{0}\ell_{0}}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}<\tfrac{1}{2}\,.

Then the continuity of

. Consequently, we derive from the definition of

and the inequality (

) in Remark

that for all

and

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathbb{E}_{N,\eta_{1}}(g_{\epsilon},\phi_{\epsilon})+\mathbb{D}_{N,\epsilon,\eta_{1}}(g_{\epsilon})\leq 0\,.

From integrating the above inequality on
[0,t]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{N,\eta_{1}}(g_{\epsilon},\phi_{\epsilon})(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{D}_{N,\epsilon,\eta_{1}}(g_{\epsilon})(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\mathbb{E}_{N,\eta_{1}}(g_{\epsilon,0},\phi_{\epsilon,0})\leq C_{0}\ell_{0}

Thus, the continuity of

\square

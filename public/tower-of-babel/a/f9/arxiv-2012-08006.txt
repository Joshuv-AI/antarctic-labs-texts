Bounds for zeros of Collatz polynomials, with necessary and sufficient strictness conditions
Matt Hohertz

Abstract
In a previous paper, we introduced the Collatz polynomials P N ​ ( z ) P_{N}\left(z\right) , whose coefficients are the terms of the Collatz sequence of the positive integer N N . Our work in this paper expands on our previous results, using the Eneström-Kakeya Theorem to tighten our old bounds of the roots of P N ​ ( z ) P_{N}\left(z\right) and giving precise conditions under which these new bounds are sharp. In particular, we confirm an experimental result that zeros on the circle { z ∈ ℂ : | z | = 2 } \{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|=2\} are rare: the set of N N such that P N ​ ( z ) P_{N}\left(z\right) has a root of modulus 2 is sparse in the natural numbers. We close with some questions for further study.

General bounds

Lemma 1
(Eneström-Kakeya, Theorem A of
[
Anderson et al. 1981
]
)

Let
f(z)=a_{k}z^{k}+\cdots+a_{1}z+a_{0}
have all strictly positive coefficients and set

\displaystyle\alpha[f]

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\min_{j=0,\cdots,k-1}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j+1}}

(7)

\displaystyle\beta[f]

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\max_{j=0,\cdots,k-1}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j+1}}.

(8)

Then

\alpha[f]\leq\left|w\right|\leq\beta[f]

(9)

for all roots
w
of
f(z)
.

□

With Lemma
1
, we prove the following general bound for
\left|z_{N}\right|
:

Theorem I

\frac{2\cdot M\left(N\right)}{3\cdot M\left(N\right)+1}\leq\left|z_{N}\right|\leq 2,

(10)

where
M\left(N\right):=\displaystyle\max\Big(\{-1/2\}\cup\{\ell>1\>:\>\ell\mbox{ an odd Collatz iterate of }N\}\Big)
.

Proof

If
a_{j}
is odd and not 1, then

\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j+1}}=\frac{2a_{j}}{3a_{j}+1},

(11)

and if
a_{j}
is even, then

\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j+1}}=\frac{2a_{j}}{a_{j}}=2.

(12)

If
N
has an odd iterate other than 1, then the conclusion follows by Lemma
1
. Otherwise,
P_{N}(z)
is the partial sum

N\cdot\left(1+\frac{z}{2}+\cdots+\frac{z^{n}}{2^{n}}\right),

(13)

whose roots all lie on
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|z\right|=2\}
.

Strictness of the lower bound

Lemma 2
(Theorem B of
[
Anderson et al. 1981
]
)

For a polynomial
f(z)
of degree
k
with strictly positive coefficients, let
S[f]
be the set of all
j\in[k+1]
such that

\beta\left[f\right]>\frac{a_{k-j}}{a_{k+1-j}}.

(14)

Then the upper bound of Equation (
9
) is an equality if and only if
1<d:=\gcd\left(j\in S[f]\right)
, in which case

•

all the zeros on
\left|z\right|=\beta[f]
are simple and given by
\Big\{\beta[f]\cdot\exp\left(\frac{2\pi ij}{d}\right),\;j=1,\cdots,d-1\Big\}
and

•

f\left(\beta[f]\cdot z\right)=\left(1+z+\cdots+z^{d-1}\right)\cdot q_{m}\left(z^{d}\right)
for a degree
m

with strictly positive coefficients.

Moreover, if

belong to
\mathbb{D}
and
\beta[q_{m}]\leq 1
.

□

Define the

\tilde{f}

f(z)=a_{k}z^{k}+\cdots+a_{j}z^{j}+\cdots+a_{0}

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(z)

\displaystyle:=z^{k}\cdot f\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)

(15)

\displaystyle=a_{0}z^{k}+\cdots+a_{k-j}z^{j}+\cdots+a_{k}.

(16)

Note that

\displaystyle\beta[\tilde{f}]

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\max_{j=0,\cdots,k-1}\frac{a_{k-j}}{a_{k-j-1}}

(17)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\displaystyle\min_{j=0,\cdots,k-1}\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j+1}}}

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha[f]},

(19)

and that, more generally, an upper bound for the zeros of
\tilde{f}
is the reciprocal of a lower bound for the zeros of

Theorem II

Equality holds for the leftmost inequality of Equation (
10

N
is a power of 2.

Proof

We have observed the “if” direction in the proof of Theorem
I

again signify the minimum odd iterate of

\beta\left[\tilde{P_{N}}\right]=\frac{1}{\alpha\left[P_{N}\right]}=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{2\cdot M\left(N\right)}.

Now, for

\frac{a_{n-\left(n-j\right)}}{a_{n-\left(n+1-j\right)}}=\frac{a_{j}}{a_{j-1}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&a_{j-1}\mbox{ even and }j\neq n+1\\
\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{2a_{j-1}},&a_{j-1}\mbox{ odd}\\

is a non-sharp upper bound for the zeros of

Strictness of the upper bound

For a given
N
, define the set
T_{N}:=\{n+1\}\cup\{j\in[n]:c^{n-j}(N)\mbox{ is odd}\}
.
Then Lemma
2
implies the following sharpness result:

Theorem III

Let
d_{N}:=\gcd(j\in T_{N})
. Then the upper bound of Equation (
10
) is an equality if and only if
d_{N}>1
, in which case

1.

the zeros of
P_{N}
on
\{\left|z\right|=2\}
are simple and given precisely by
\{2\omega:\omega^{d_{N}}=1\land\omega\neq 1\}
and

2.

P_{N}
factors as

P_{N}(2z)=\left(1+z+\cdots+z^{d_{N}-1}\right)\cdot Q_{N}(z^{d_{N}}),

(22)

where
Q_{N}
is a polynomial with positive coefficients. Moreover, if
Q_{N}
is non-constant then all its zeros lie in
\mathbb{D}
and
\beta[Q_{N}]\leq 1
.

■

Proof

By the proof of Theorem
I
(and the fact that
a_{-1}=0
because
P_{N}
is a polynomial), the set
T_{N}
is precisely the set of
j\in[n+1]
such that
\frac{a_{n-j}}{a_{n+1-j}}<\beta[P_{N}]=2
. Thus the result follows from applying Lemma
2
.

■

The exacting conditions of Theorem
III
suggest that zeros on
\left|z\right|=2
are rare, and indeed we prove that this is so. First, we need two lemmas.

Lemma 3

The number of length
k
binary strings with no consecutive 1s is
F_{k+1}
, the
\left(k+1\right)^{th}
Fibonacci number. In particular, let
p_{k}
be the probability that a length
k
binary string selected uniformly at random contains no consecutive 1s. Then
\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}p_{k}=0
.

□

Proof

The first statement holds for
k=0
and
1
. Consider a binary string
x
of length
k\geq 2
and let
substring(x,j)
be the substring of
x
comprising its first
j
digits. If the last digit of
x
is 0, then
x
has two consecutive 1s if and only if
substring(x,k-1)
does. Otherwise, its last two digits are either
11
, in which case it has consecutive 1s, or
01
, in which case it has consecutive 1s if and only if
substring(x,k-2)
does. Therefore, the number of length
k
binary strings with no consecutive 1s satisfies the same recurrence relation as the Fibonacci numbers.

Since
F_{k}\sim\frac{\varphi^{k}}{\sqrt{5}}
,
p_{k}\sim\frac{\varphi}{\sqrt{5}}\left(\frac{\varphi}{2}\right)^{k}
, and this quantity has limit 0 as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma 4

Let
X
be the set of natural numbers
N
with the property that

c^{j}\left(N\right)\mbox{ odd}\Rightarrow c^{j+1}\left(N\right)\mbox{ even}

for all integers
j\geq 0
. The set
X
has density zero in the natural numbers.

□

Proof

Let
x_{j}\left(N\right):=c^{j}\left(N\right)\;\left(\mbox{mod }2\right).
By
[
Lagarias 1985
, Theorem B]
, the function
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}/2^{k+1}\mathbb{Z}}
defined by

N\rightarrow\left(x_{0}\left(N\right),\dots,x_{k}\left(N\right)\right)

(23)

is periodic with period
2^{k+1}
. For a fixed
k
, the set
X
is a subset of the preimage of the set of length
k+1
strings with no consecutive 1s under this function. By Lemma
3
, as
k\rightarrow\infty
, this preimage becomes sparse as a subset of the natural numbers.

5
Final remarks

We conclude with a few suggestions for further research.

1.

Strengthen Theorem
II
by finding an explicit, closed-form lower bound for
\left|z_{N}\right|
in terms of
N
.

2.

Strengthen Theorems
III
and
IV
by finding an explicit, closed-form upper bound for
\left|z_{N}\right|
in terms of
N
.

3.

Figure
1
suggests that certain subsets of
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\>:\>\left|z\right|\leq 2\}
contain large clusters of Collatz zeros while others are zero-free. Give an algorithm for proving that a subset of
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\>:\>\left|z\right|\leq 2\}
is free of Collatz zeros.

4.

While Descartes’ Rule of Signs implies that no Collatz zero is positive real, there appears to be a sequence of zeros that approaches
z=1
arbitrarily closely, bounded by a parabola with vertex at
z=1
. Prove or disprove the existence of such a convergent sequence and parabola.

5.

Under some modest assumptions, the zeros of a polynomial with random coefficients cluster near the unit circle with uniform angular distribution
[
Hughes and Nikeghbali 2008
]
. Prove or disprove that these assumptions hold for the Collatz polynomials.

6.

Find the Galois groups of
P_{N}
for general classes of
P_{N}
.

7.

Expanding on Theorem
III
, find conditions under which
P_{N}
has zeros at real multiples (other than 2) of roots of unity.

8.

Except for Theorem
II
, all the theorems in this paper are functions of the Collatz sequence of
N
rather than of
N
itself. Prove theorems on
z_{N}
which can be applied without calculating the Collatz sequence of
N
(
e.g.
, if
N\equiv 3\mbox{ mod }4
, then the zeros of
P_{N}
lie in …).

9.

Our results rest on the assumption, equivalent to the Collatz conjecture itself, that
P_{N}
is a polynomial for all
N
. Using other methods of proof, find a property of Collatz zeros that contradicts a theorem in this paper, thereby disproving the Collatz conjecture.

On the equivalence between Weak BMO and the space of derivatives of the Zygmund class
Eddy Kwessi

Abstract
In this paper, we will discuss the space of functions of weak bounded mean oscillation. In particular, we will show that this space is the dual space of the special atom space, whose dual space was already known to be the space of derivative of functions (in the sense of distribution) belonging to the Zygmund class of functions. We show in particular that this proves that the Hardy space H 1 H^{1} strictly contains the space special atom space.



Keywords:
BMO, Derivative, Distributions, Zygmund Class

AMS Subject Classification: 42B05, 42B30, 30B50, 30E20

Introduction

The space of functions of bounded means oscillation has taken central stage in the mathematical literature after the work of Charles Fefferman
[
4
]
where he showed that it is the dual space of the real Hardy space
\mathbb{H}^{1}

[
2

showed this result using a different method. Essentially, he showed that
\mathbb{H}^{1}
has an atomic decomposition. De Souza
[
3
]
showed there is a subset
B^{1}
of
\mathbb{H}^{1}

\mathbb{H}^{1}
. This space
B^{1}
has the particularity that it contains some functions whose Fourier series diverge. The question of whether
B^{1}
is equivalent to
\mathbb{H}^{1}
was never truly answered explicitly but it was always suspected that the inclusion
B^{1}\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}
was strict, that is, there must be at least one function in
\mathbb{H}^{1}
that is not in
B^{1}
. However, such a function had never being constructed nor given. Since the dual space
(\mathbb{H}^{1})^{*}
of
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is
BMO
and
B^{1}\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}
, it follows that the dual space
(B^{1})^{*}
of

must be a superset of
BMO
. A natural superset candidate of
BMO
is therefore the space
BMO^{w}
since
BMO\subset BMO^{w}
. So essence, that
BMO^{w}
is the dual of
B^{1}
would also prove that
B^{1}\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}
with a strict inclusion. Moreover, it was already proved that
(B^{1})^{*}\cong\Lambda_{*}^{\prime}
, where
\Lambda_{*}^{\prime}
is the space of derivative (in the sense of distributions) of functions in the Zygmund class
\Lambda_{*}
, see for example
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
. Therefore, if

Now we define the Zygmund class of functions.

Definition 8
.

Let

\displaystyle\sup_{Q\ni x}\;\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[(f_{n}(\xi)-f_{m}(\xi))-(f_{n}-f_{m})_{Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|+\ \sup_{Q\ni x}\;\left|(f_{n}-f_{m})_{Q}-(f_{n,Q}-f_{m,Q})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f_{n}-f_{m}\right\|_{BMO^{w}}+\sup_{Q\ni x}\;\left|(f_{n,Q}-f_{m,Q})\right|<2\epsilon\;.

It follows that
\left\{Mf_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy-sequence in
L^{\infty}_{loc}
. Let
\displaystyle h=\lim_{n\to\infty}Mf_{n}
.

Since
L^{\infty}_{loc}\subset L^{1}_{loc}
, we have
h\in L^{1}_{loc}
.

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}Mf_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\;\sup_{Q\ni x}\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f_{n}(\xi)-f_{n,Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{Q\ni x}\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[\;\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(\xi)-f_{Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|\;.

is finite on any cube

and
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{BMO^{w}}\to 0

Theorem 2.4

(Hölder’s type inequality)
.

Let
g\in BMO^{w}
and a hyper-cube
J\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the following operator
T_{g}:B^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\displaystyle T_{g}(f)=\int_{J}f(\xi)g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)
. Then,
T_{g}\in(B^{1})^{*}
with
\left\|T_{g}\right\|_{(B^{1})^{*}}\leq\left\|g\right\|_{BMO^{w}}
.

Moreover, the operator
H:BMO^{w}\to(B^{1})^{*}
defined as
H(g)=T_{g}
is onto.

Proof.

By linearity of the integral,
T_{g}
is a linear. To start, we study the action of this operator on special atoms. Indeed, let
\xi\in I\subseteq J
and suppose
f(\xi)=b(\xi)=\frac{1}{\phi(I)}[\chi_{{}_{R}}(\xi)-\chi_{{}_{L}}(\xi)]
, where
R,L
are sub-cubes of
I
such that
I=R\cup L
and
R\cap L=\emptyset
. Therefore we have:

\displaystyle T_{g}(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{J}b(\xi)g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)=\int_{I}b(\xi)g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I}b(\xi)[g(\xi)-g_{I}]d\lambda(\xi),\quad\mbox{since $\displaystyle\int_{I}b(\xi)g_{I}d\lambda(\xi)=0$}\;.

Taking the supremum over all
I\ni x
and using the fact that
b(\xi)\leq\frac{1}{\phi(I)}
, we have

\left|T_{g}(b)\right|\leq\sup_{\underset{I\subseteq J}{I\ni x}}\inf_{\alpha>0}\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{I}[g(\xi)-\alpha]d\lambda(\xi)\right|\leq\left\|g\right\|_{BMO^{w}}\;.

(2.4)

Now suppose
\displaystyle f(\xi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}b_{n}(\xi)
with
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|c_{n}\right|<\infty
is an element of
B^{1}
, where the
b_{n}
’s are special atoms defined on sub-cubes
I_{n}
of
J
with
I_{n}=R_{n}\cup L_{n}
and
R_{n}\cap L_{n}=\emptyset
. Let
\displaystyle I=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{n}
. We have for
\alpha>0

\displaystyle T_{g}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{J}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}b_{n}(\xi)\right)g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}b_{n}(\xi)\right)\left[g(\xi)-\alpha\right]d\lambda(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\int_{I}b_{n}(\xi)\left[g(\xi)-\alpha\right]d\lambda(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\int_{I_{n}}b_{n}(\xi)\left[g(\xi)-\alpha\right]d\lambda(\xi)\;.

It follows from (
2.4
) that

\left|T_{g}(f)\right|\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|c_{n}\right|\right)\cdot\left\|g\right\|_{BMO^{w}}\;.

Talking the infimum over all representations of
f
yields:

\left|T_{g}(f)\right|\leq\left\|f\right\|_{B^{1}}\cdot\left\|g\right\|_{BMO^{w}}\;.

Therefore
T_{g}
is a bounded linear operator on
B^{1}
with

\left\|T_{g}\right\|_{(B^{1})^{*}}=\sup_{\left\|f\right\|_{B^{1}}=1}\left|T_{g}(f)\right|\leq\left\|g\right\|_{BMO^{w}}\;.

(2.5)

Now suppose that
T
is a bounded linear functional on
B^{1}
, that is,
T\in(B^{1})^{*}
. We want to show that there exists a function
g\in BMO^{w}
such that
\displaystyle T(f)=T_{g}(f)=\int_{J}f(\xi)g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)
. That
T\in(B^{1})^{*}
implies the existence of an absolute constant
C
such that

\left|T(f)\right|\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{B^{1}},\quad\forall f\in B^{1}.

(2.6)

Recall that a function
G:J\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be absolutely continuous on
J
if for every positive number
\epsilon
, there exists a positive number
\delta
, such that for a finite sequence of pairwise disjoint sub-cubes
I_{n}=(x_{n},y_{n})
of
J
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(y_{n}-x_{n})<\delta\implies\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|G(y_{n})-G(x_{n})\right|<\epsilon\;.

(2.7)

Suppose such a sequence of sub-cubes exists. Now consider
G(x)=T\left(\chi_{{}_{[x-h,x+h)}}\right)
for some real number
h>0
. Then, given an cube
I_{n}
and a real number
h_{n}
, we have by linearity of
T
that

G(y_{n})-G(x_{n})=T\left(\left[\chi_{{}_{[y_{n}-h_{n},y_{n}+h_{n})}}-\chi_{{}_{[x_{n}-h_{n},x_{n}+h_{n})}}\right]\right)\;.

Now if we define
L_{n}=[x_{n}-h_{n},x_{n}+h_{n})
and
R_{n}=[y_{n}-h_{n},y_{n}+h_{n})
, we see that
R_{n}\cap L_{n}=\emptyset
their union forms a single cube
J_{n}
if we choose
h_{n}=(y_{n}-x_{n})/2
. Moreover, in that case, the length of the cube
J_{n}
is
y_{n}+h_{n}-x_{n}+h_{n}=2(y_{n}-x_{n})
. Therefore by linearity

G(y_{n})-G(x_{n})=2(y_{n}-x_{n})T\left(\frac{1}{2(y_{n}-x_{n})}\left[\chi_{{}_{[y_{n}-h_{n},y_{n}+h_{n})}}-\chi_{{}_{[x_{n}-h_{n},x_{n}+h_{n})}}\right]\right)\;.

We observe that
\displaystyle b_{n}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2(y_{n}-x_{n})}\left[\chi_{{}_{[y_{n}-h_{n},y_{n}+h_{n})}}(\xi)-\chi_{{}_{[x_{n}-h_{n},x_{n}+h_{n})}}(\xi)\right]
is a special atom with norm in
B^{1}
equal to 1. It follows that using (
2.6
)

\left|G(y_{n})-G(x_{n})\right|\leq 2(y_{n}-x_{n})\left|T(b_{n})\right|\leq 2C(y_{n}-x_{n})\left\|b_{n}\right\|_{B^{1}}=2C(y_{n}-x_{n})\;.

Hence,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|G(y_{n})-G(x_{n})\right|\leq 2C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(y_{n}-x_{n})\;.

We conclude by noting that given
\epsilon>0
, (
2.7
) is satisfied if we choose
\displaystyle\delta=\frac{\epsilon}{2C}
. We conclude that the function
G
is absolutely continuous on
J
. Therefore,
G
is differentiable almost everywhere, that is, there exists
g\in L^{1}
such that
\displaystyle G(x)=\int_{a}^{x}g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)
for all cubes
I=[a,b]\subseteq J
. Let
I\ni x
be a sub-cube of
J
. Therefore,
\displaystyle\sup_{I\ni x}\frac{1}{\lambda(I)}\left|\int_{I}g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)\right|<\infty
because an absolutely continuous function is a function with bounded variation. Since
g\in L^{1}
, we have that
\displaystyle g_{I}=\frac{1}{\lambda(I)}\left|\int_{I}g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)\right|<\infty.
It follows that

Mg(x)=\sup_{I\ni x}\frac{1}{\lambda(I)}\left|\int_{I}(g(\xi)-g_{I})d\lambda(\xi)\right|\leq\sup_{I\ni x}\frac{1}{\lambda(I)}\left|\int_{I}g(\xi)d\lambda(\xi)\right|+\sup_{I\ni x}\;g_{I}<\infty\;.

This proves that
g\in BMO^{w}
.
That is,
H:BMO^{w}\to(B^{1})^{*}
is onto with
H(g)=T=T_{g}
. Identifying
T
with the
g
, it follows from (
2.5
) that

\left\|g\right\|_{(B^{1})^{*}}=\left\|T\right\|_{(B^{1})^{*}}=\left\|T_{g}\right\|_{(B^{1})^{*}}\leq\left\|g\right\|_{BMO^{w}}\;.

(2.8)

∎

It follows that ,

\displaystyle\left\|F_{n}-F\right\|_{B^{1}_{A}}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left|F^{\prime}_{n}(z)-F^{\prime}(z)\right|dz\leq C\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}.

\displaystyle\sup_{Q\ni x}\;\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f(\xi)-f_{Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|<\infty

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f(\xi)-f_{Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f(\xi)-f_{n}(\xi)+f_{n}(\xi)-f_{n,Q}+f_{n,Q}-f_{Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f(\xi)-f_{n}(\xi)]d\lambda(\xi)\right|+\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f_{n}(\xi)-f_{n,Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|+\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f_{n,Q}-f_{Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f(\xi)-f_{n}(\xi)]d\lambda(\xi)\right|+\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f_{n}(\xi)-f_{n,Q}]d\lambda(\xi)\right|+\left|f_{n,Q}-f_{Q}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{Q}[f(\xi)-f_{n}(\xi)]d\lambda(\xi)\right|+\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{BMO^{w}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{1}_{loc}}+\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{BMO^{w}}\;.

Since
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of function is
BMO^{w}
, we have that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\;\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{BMO^{w}}=0
. Since
BMO^{w}\subset L^{1}_{loc}
, we have that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{1}_{loc}}=0
.
Hence
VMO^{w}
is a closed subspace of
BMO^{w}
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

We note that it was proved in
[
6
]
that
VMO
is a closed subspace of
BMO
. A stronger result even showed that if we restrict ourselves to
\mathbb{T}
, then the space of complex-valued and continuous functions
C(\mathbb{T})\subseteq VMO(\mathbb{T})
and the
BMO(\mathbb{T})
-closure of
C(\mathbb{T})
is precisely
VMO(\mathbb{T})
. It turns out this result is also true for
VMO^{w}
.

Theorem 2.10
.

The
BMO^{w}
-closure of
C(\mathbb{T})
is precisely
VMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})
, that is

\overline{C(\mathbb{T})}^{BMO^{w}}=VMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})\;.

Proof.

We observe that
C(\mathbb{T})\subseteq VMO(\mathbb{T})\subseteq VMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})\subseteq BMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})
. Therefore since
VMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})
is closed in
BMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})
, we have that

\overline{C(\mathbb{T})}^{BMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})}\subseteq\overline{VMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})}^{BMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})}=VMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})\;.

For the other direction , let
f\in VMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})
, and consider the sequence
\left\{R_{\epsilon}(f)\right\}_{\epsilon>0}
of rotations of
f
by angle
\epsilon
, defined on
\mathbb{T}
as
R_{\epsilon}(f)(e^{i\theta})=f(e^{i(\theta-\epsilon)});\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then from Theorem 2.1 in
[
6
]
, we have that for all
\epsilon>0,R_{\epsilon}(f)\in C(\mathbb{T})
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left\|R_{\epsilon}(f)-f\right\|_{BMO(\mathbb{T})}=0
. Since
BMO(\mathbb{T})\subseteq BMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})
, we also have that
\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left\|R_{\epsilon}(f)-f\right\|_{BMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})}=0
. That is,
f\in\overline{C(\mathbb{T})}^{BMO^{w}(\mathbb{T})}.

∎

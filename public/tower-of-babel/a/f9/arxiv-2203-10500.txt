Some examples of equivalent rearrangement-invariant quasi-norms defined via f ∗ f^{*} or f ∗ ⁣ ∗ f^{**}
Leo R. Ya. Doktorski, Pedro Fernández-Martínez, Teresa Signes

Abstract
. We consider Lorentz-Karamata spaces, small and grand Lorentz-Karamata spaces, and the so-called ℒ \mathcal{L} , ℛ \mathcal{R} , ℒ ​ ℒ \mathcal{LL} , ℛ ​ ℒ \mathcal{RL} , ℛ ​ ℒ \mathcal{RL} , and ℛ ​ ℛ \mathcal{RR} spaces. The quasi-norms for a function f f in each of these spaces can be defined via the non-increasing rearrangement f ∗ f^{*} or via the maximal function f ∗ ⁣ ∗ f^{**} . We investigate when these quasi-norms are equivalent. Most of the proofs are based on Hardy-type inequalities. As application we demonstrate how our general results can be used to establish interpolation formulae for the grand and small Lorentz-Karamata spaces.



1.
Introduction

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a totally
\sigma
-finite measure space with a non-atomic measure
\mu
and let
\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\mu)
be the set of all
\mu
-measurable functions on
\Omega
. Associated to each function
f\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\mu)
we consider
f^{*}
, the non-increasing rearrangement of
f
, and
f^{**}(t):=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(u)du
,
t>0
, the maximal function of
f^{*}

(see e.g.
[
3
]
). One of the possibilities to define rearrangement-invariant quasi-Banach
function spaces on
\Omega
is to use expressions for the quasi-norms in terms of
f^{*}
or
f^{**}
. For
example, if
0<p,q\leq\infty
and
b
is a slowly varying function on
(0,\infty)
(see Definition
2.1
below),
the Lorentz-Karamata spaces
L_{p,q;b}
and
L_{(p,q;b)}
are defined as the sets of all
f\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\mu)
such
that

(1.1)

\|f\|_{p,q;b}:=\big\|t^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}b(t)f^{*}(t)\big\|_{q,(0,\infty)}<\infty

or

(1.2)

\|f\|_{(p,q;b)}:=\big\|t^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}b(t)f^{**}(t)\big\|_{q,(0,\infty)}<\infty,

respectively. A natural question to ask is how this spaces are related. In
[
27
,
26
,
22
]
and
[
28
, Theorem 3.15]
it was proved, using weighted Hardy inequalities, that

(1.3)

L_{p,q;b}=L_{(p,q;b)}\quad\mbox{if }\quad 1<p\leq\infty\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ 0<q\leq\infty.

Previous equality means that the spaces are equal as linear spaces and their quasi-norms are equivalent.

An important feature of identity (
1.3
) is that it can be seen from the point of view of interpolation theory.
Recall that J. Peetre’s well-known formula for the
K
-functional of a function
f
in
L_{1}+L_{\infty}
asserts that

(1.4)

K(t,f;L_{1},L_{\infty})=\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(\tau)\,d\tau=tf^{**}(t)

(see
[
3
, Theorem V.1.6]
) and the interpolation spaces
(L_{1},L_{\infty})_{\theta,q;b}
are defined as the set of all
f\in L_{1}+L_{\infty}
such that

\|f\|_{\theta,q;b}:=\big\|t^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}b(t)K(t,f;L_{1},L_{\infty})\big\|_{q,(0,\infty)}<\infty,

where
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
b
is a slowly varying function.
Then

(L_{1},L_{\infty})_{1-\frac{1}{p},q;b}=L_{(p,q;b)}.

Due to (
1.3
), we have that

(L_{1},L_{\infty})_{1-\frac{1}{p},q;b}=L_{(p,q;b)}=L_{p,q;b},\qquad\mbox{if}\ 1<p<\infty,\ 0<q\leq\infty.

The aim of this paper is to prove similar identities for the limiting and extremal constructions
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{L}
,

\mathcal{LL}
,
\mathcal{RL}
,
\mathcal{RL}
and
\mathcal{RR}
(see precise definitions in §
3
). Specifically, these limiting and extremal methods applied to the couple
(L_{1},L_{\infty})
give spaces with quasi-norms expressed in terms of
f^{**}
. These expressions written with
f^{*}
instead of
f^{**}
produce quasi-norms that define, a priori, different quasi-Banach function spaces. Our goal is to prove that the spaces defined via
f^{*}
or
f^{**}
are equal and the quasi-norms are equivalent. See §
6
for the precise statements. In particular our results can be applied to grand and small Lebesgue spaces
[
19
]
, as well as grand and small Lorentz-Karamata spaces, see Theorem
6.3
and
6.4
.

We illustrate the utility of our results by including an application to the interpolation of the couple
\big(L^{p_{0}),q_{0},r_{0}}_{b_{0}},L_{\infty}\big)
(see Corollary
7.2
). Other examples can be seen in
[
8
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

The organization of the paper is the following: Section 2 contains the basic tools: the properties of slowly varying functions and new Hardy-type inequalities. In section 3, we describe the interpolation methods we shall work with, namely
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q;b}
, the
{\mathcal{R}}
and
{\mathcal{L}}
limiting interpolation spaces and the
\mathcal{LL}
,
\mathcal{RL}
,
\mathcal{RL}
and
\mathcal{RR}
extremal interpolation spaces. We also define the function spaces under consideration through quasi-norms expressed in terms of
f^{*}
or
f^{**}
.
In Section 4 we prove the main lemmas. Section 5 is devoted to the interpolation formulae for the couples
(L_{m},L_{\infty})
and
(L_{m,\infty},L_{\infty})
and in Section 6 we deduce the particular case
m=1
.
Finally, Section 7 contains the applications.

X\subset Y
for two (quasi-) normed spaces

. We write

X\subset Y

. In this case,

For

and

, where the constant

is independent of all

2.1.
Slowly varying functions

In this subsection we summarize some of the properties of slowly varying functions which will be required later. For more details, we refer to e.g.
[
13
,
15
,
22
,
28
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A positive Lebesgue measurable function
b
,
0\not\equiv b\not\equiv\infty
,
is said to be slowly varying on
(0,\infty)
(notation
b\in SV
) if, for each
\varepsilon>0
, the function
t\leadsto t^{\varepsilon}b(t)
is equivalent to an increasing function while
t\leadsto t^{-\varepsilon}b(t)
is equivalent to a decreasing function.

Examples of
SV
-functions include powers of logarithms,

\ell^{\alpha}(t)=(1+|\log t|)^{\alpha}(t),\quad t>0,\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

“broken” logarithmic functions of
[
12
]
,
reiterated logarithms
(\ell\circ\ldots\circ\ell(t))^{\alpha},\;t>0
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and also the family of functions
\exp(|\log t|^{\alpha}),\;t>0
for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
b,b_{1},b_{2}\in SV
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
t\in(0,\infty)
.

(i)

Then
b^{\lambda}\in SV
,
b(1/t)\in SV
,
b(t^{\alpha}b_{1}(t))\in SV
, and
b_{1}b_{2}\in SV
.

(ii)

If
f\approx g
, then
b\circ f\approx b\circ g
.

(iii)

\big\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(0,t)}\approx t^{\alpha}b(t)
and

\big\|u^{-\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(t,\infty)}\approx t^{-\alpha}b(t).

(iv)

The functions
\big\|u^{-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(0,t)}
and

\big\|u^{-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(t,\infty)}
(if exist) belong to
SV
. Moreover,

b(t)\lesssim\big\|u^{-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(0,t)}\quad\text{and}\quad b(t)\lesssim\big\|u^{-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(t,\infty)}.

(v)

\big\|u^{\lambda-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(t/2,t)}\approx\big\|u^{\lambda-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(t,2t)}\approx t^{\lambda}b(t).

Along the paper, we will often use these properties without explicit reference every time. The following simple lemma extends the above inequalities.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
b\in SV
. Then for all non-negative non-increasing Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
(0,\infty)
and for all
t>0
,

t^{\lambda}b(t)f(t)\lesssim\big\|u^{\lambda-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\big\|_{q,(0,t)},

t^{\lambda}b(t)f(t)\lesssim\big\|u^{\lambda-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\big\|_{q,(\frac{t}{2},\infty)}

and

t^{\lambda}b(t)f(2t)\lesssim\big\|u^{\lambda-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\big\|_{q,(t,2t)}.

Proof.

Since
f
is non-increasing, by Lemma
2.2
(v), we have

\displaystyle t^{\lambda}b(t)f(t)

\displaystyle\approx\|u^{\lambda-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\|_{q,(\frac{t}{2},t)}f(t)\leq\|u^{\lambda-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\|_{q,(\frac{t}{2},t)}\leq\|u^{\lambda-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\|_{q,(0,t)}.

The other two estimates can be proved similarly.
∎

2.2.
Hardy-type inequalities

The following Hardy-type inequalities and their corollaries will be essential parts of our later arguments.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
22
, Lemma 2.7]

Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
b\in SV
. The inequality

\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(0,\infty)}\lesssim\|u^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\|_{q,(0,\infty)}

holds for all non-negative Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
(0,\infty)
if and only if
\alpha<0
.

Next lemma recovers Corollary 2.9 from
[
22
]
, and additionally completes it with the cases
0<T<S<\infty
and
\alpha\leq-1
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
b\in SV
and
\alpha<0
. Then,

i)

If
0<q<1
, the inequality

(2.1)

\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(T,S)}\lesssim T^{\alpha}b(T)\int_{0}^{T}\!\!f(s)\,ds+\big\|u^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\big\|_{q,(T,S)}

holds for all
0\leq T<S\leq\infty
and all non-negative non-increasing Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
(0,\infty)
.

ii)

If
1\leq q\leq\infty
, (
2.1
) holds for all
0\leq T<S\leq\infty
and all non-negative Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

We can assume that
f\neq 0
.

Case
0<q<1
. Denote
G(u)=\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)d\tau
. It is easy to see that

(2.2)

G^{q}(u)=q\int_{0}^{u}G^{q-1}(\tau)f(\tau)d\tau,\qquad u>0.

and hence

\displaystyle\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|^{q}_{q,(T,S)}

\displaystyle=\int_{T}^{S}u^{\alpha q}b^{q}(u)G^{q}(u)\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=q\int_{T}^{S}u^{\alpha q}b^{q}(u)\int_{0}^{u}G^{q-1}(\tau)f(\tau)d\tau\frac{du}{u}.

Thus, by Fubini’s theorem, (
2.2
) and Lemma
2.2
, we deduce

\displaystyle\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|^{q}_{q,(T,S)}

\displaystyle\approx G^{q}(T)\int_{T}^{S}u^{\alpha q}b^{q}(u)\frac{du}{u}+\int_{T}^{S}\Big(\int_{\tau}^{S}u^{\alpha q}b^{q}(u)\frac{du}{u}\Big)G^{q-1}(\tau)f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq G^{q}(T)\int_{T}^{\infty}u^{\alpha q}b^{q}(u)\frac{du}{u}+\int_{T}^{S}\Big(\int_{\tau}^{\infty}u^{\alpha q}b^{q}(u)\frac{du}{u}\Big)G^{q-1}(\tau)f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\approx T^{\alpha q}b^{q}(T)G^{q}(T)+\int_{T}^{S}\tau^{\alpha q}b^{q}(\tau)G^{q-1}(\tau)f(\tau)d\tau.

Furthermore, the hypothesis
f\!\downarrow
implies
G(u)\geq f(u)\int_{0}^{u}d\tau=uf(u)
and, since
q-1<0
, we have
G^{q-1}(u)\leq(uf(u))^{q-1}
,
u>0
. Multiplying by
f(u)
, we obtain that the inequality

G^{q-1}(u)f(u)\leq u^{q-1}f^{q}(u)

holds for all
u>0
. Consequently,

\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|^{q}_{q,(T,S)}\lesssim T^{\alpha q}b^{q}(T)G^{q}(T)+\int_{T}^{S}\tau^{q(\alpha+1)-1}b^{q}(\tau)f^{q}(\tau)d\tau.

Case
1\leq q\leq\infty
. This time we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(T,S)}

\displaystyle\approx\big\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\big\|_{q,(T,S)}\int_{0}^{T}f(\tau)\,d\tau+\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\int_{T}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(T,S)}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(T)\int_{0}^{T}f(\tau)\,d\tau+\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\int_{T}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(T,S)}.

Moreover, if we apply Hardy-type inequality of Lemma
2.4
we obtain that the second term of the last expression
is bounded by
\|u^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\|^{q}_{q,(T,S)}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\int_{T}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(T,S)}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\chi_{(T,S)}(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl\|u^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\chi_{(T,S)}(u)\Bigr\|_{q,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|u^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)f(u)\Bigr\|_{q,(T,S)}.

This completes de proof.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\alpha<0
,
0<q,r\leq\infty
, and
a,b\in SV
. Then, for all non-negative non-increasing Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on
(0,\infty)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\biggl\|t^{-\frac{1}{r}}b(t)\Bigl\|u^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}a(u)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(t,\infty)}\biggr\|_{r,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\approx\Bigl\|t^{-\frac{1}{r}}b(t)\bigl\|u^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{q}}a(u)f(u)\bigr\|_{q,(t,\infty)}\Bigr\|_{r,(0,\infty)}.

Proof.

By hypothesis
f\!\downarrow
, then
\int_{0}^{u}f(\tau)d\tau\geq uf(u)
and the estimate “
\geq
” holds. Next we prove the reverse estimate. Lemma
2.5
yields

LHS(\ref{ec24})\lesssim\Bigl\|t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{r}}b(t)a(t)\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{r,(0,\infty)}+RHS(\ref{ec24}).

Moreover, by Hardy-type inequality of Lemma
2.4
, Lemma
2.3
and a change of variables we obtain that the first term of the previous sum is bounded by the second one. That is,

\displaystyle\Big\|t^{\alpha-\frac{1}{r}}b(t)a(t)\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Big\|_{r,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|t^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{r}}b(t)a(t)f(t)\big\|_{r,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigl\|t^{-\frac{1}{r}}b(t)\bigl\|u^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{q}}a(u)f(u)\bigr\|_{q,(\frac{t}{2},\infty)}\Bigr\|_{r,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\approx RHS(\ref{ec24}).

This completes the proof.
∎

.

,

, and

\displaystyle(u)\Bigl\|v^{\alpha-\frac{1}{q}}a(v)\int_{0}^{v}f(\tau)\,d\tau\Bigr\|_{q,(u,\infty)}\biggr\|_{r,(t,\infty)}\Biggr\|_{s,(0,\infty)}

Since

3.1.
Interpolation Methods

Next, we collect the definitions and basic properties of the real interpolation methods defined with slowly varying functions. This should give a sufficient background to follow the paper.

In what follows
\overline{A}=(A_{0},A_{1})
will be a compatible (quasi-) Banach couple such that
A_{0}\cap A_{1}\neq\{0\}
. For
t>0
, the
Peetre
K
-functional
is given by

\displaystyle K(t,f;A_{0},A_{1})\equiv K(t,f)=\inf\Big\{\|f_{0}\|_{A_{0}}+t\|f_{1}\|_{A_{1}}:\ f=f_{0}+f_{1},\ f_{i}\in A_{i},\;i=0,1\Big\}.

In recent years, the following scale of interpolation spaces with slowly varying functions has been intensively studied.

Definition 3.1
.

[
22
]

Let
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
a\in SV
. The real interpolation space
\overline{A}_{\theta,q;a}\equiv(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q;a}
consists of all
f
in
A_{0}+A_{1}
that satisfy

\|f\|_{\theta,q;a}:=\big\|t^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}a(t)K(t,f)\big\|_{q,(0,\infty)}<\infty.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
22
, Proposition 2.5]

Let
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
a\in SV
.
The space
\overline{A}_{\theta,q;a}
is a (quasi-) Banach space. Moreover,
A_{0}\cap A_{1}\hookrightarrow\overline{A}_{\theta,q;a}\hookrightarrow A_{0}+A_{1}
if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

0<\theta<1
,

(ii)

\theta=0
and
\big\|t^{-\frac{1}{q}}a(t)\big\|_{q,(1,\infty)}\!<\infty
or

(iii)

\theta=1
,
\big\|t^{-\frac{1}{q}}a(t)\big\|_{q,(0,1)}\!<\infty
.

If none of these conditions holds, then
\overline{A}_{\theta,q;a}=\{0\}
.

Definition 3.3
.

[
22
]

Let
0\leq\theta\leq 1
,
0<r,q\leq\infty
and
a,b\in SV
. The space
\overline{A}_{\theta,r,b,q,a}^{\mathcal{L}}\equiv(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,r,b,q,a}^{\mathcal{L}}
consists of all
f\in A_{0}+A_{1}
for which

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L};\theta,r,b,q,a}:=\Big\|t^{-\frac{1}{r}}b(t)\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}a(u)K(u,f)\|_{q,(0,t)}\Big\|_{r,(0,\infty)}<\infty.

Silimilarly, the space

\overline{A}_{\theta,r,b,q,a}^{\mathcal{R}}\equiv(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,r,b,q,a}^{\mathcal{R}}
consists of all
f\in A_{0}+A_{1}
for which

\|f\|_{\mathcal{R};\theta,r,b,q,a}:=\Big\|t^{-\frac{1}{r}}b(t)\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}a(u)K(u,f)\|_{q,(t,\infty)}\Big\|_{r,(0,\infty)}<\infty.

These spaces naturally arise in reiteration formulae for the limiting cases
\theta=0
or
\theta=1
. In literature (see e.g.
[
13
,
8
,

\Big\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)K(u,f;L_{m},L_{\infty})\Big\|_{q,(T,S)}\lesssim T^{1-\theta}b(T)f^{**}_{(m)}(T)+\Big\|v^{\frac{1-\theta}{m}-\frac{1}{q}}\dddot{b}(v)f^{*}(v)\Big\|_{q,(T^{m},S^{m})},

and, for all

and all
0\leq T<S\leq\infty
,

\displaystyle\Big\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)K(u,f;L_{m,\infty},L_{\infty})\Big\|_{q,(T,S)}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{1-\theta}b(T)f^{**}_{(m)}(T)

(4.4)

\displaystyle+\Big\|v^{\frac{1-\theta}{m}-\frac{1}{q}}\dddot{b}(v)f^{*}(v)\Big\|_{q,(T^{m},S^{m})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L_{m}+L_{\infty}
. By (
4.1
) it holds

\Big\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)K(u,f;L_{m},L_{\infty})\Big\|_{q,(T,S)}\approx\bigg\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\Big(\int_{0}^{u^{m}}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}d\tau\Big)^{1/m}\bigg\|_{q,(T,S)}.

For the last term, the suitable change of variables, Lemma
2.5
and (
4.2
) yield that

\displaystyle\bigg\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle b(u)\Big(\int_{0}^{u^{m}}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}d\tau\Big)^{1/m}\bigg\|_{q,(T,S)}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|u^{-\theta m-\frac{m}{q}}b(u)^{m}\int_{0}^{u^{m}}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}d\tau\bigg\|_{q/m,(T,S)}^{1/m}

\displaystyle\approx\bigg\|v^{-\theta-\frac{m}{q}}\dddot{b}(v)^{m}\int_{0}^{v}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}d\tau\bigg\|_{q/m,(T^{m},S^{m})}^{1/m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(T^{-\theta m}b(T)^{m}\int_{0}^{T^{m}}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}\,d\tau+\Big\|v^{1-\theta-\frac{m}{q}}\dddot{b}(v)^{m}f^{*}(v)^{m}\Big\|_{q/m,(T^{m},S^{m})}\bigg)^{1/m}

\displaystyle\approx T^{-\theta}b(T)\bigg(\int_{0}^{T^{m}}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}\,d\tau\bigg)^{1/m}+\Big\|v^{1-\theta-\frac{m}{q}}\dddot{b}(v)^{m}f^{*}(v)^{m}\Big\|_{q/m,(T^{m},S^{m})}^{1/m}

(4.5)

\displaystyle=T^{1-\theta}b(T)f^{**}_{(m)}(T)+\Big\|v^{\frac{1-\theta}{m}-\frac{1}{q}}\dddot{b}(v)f^{*}(v)\Big\|_{q,(T^{m},S^{m})}.

This concludes the first part of the proof.

Let
f\in L_{m,\infty}+L_{\infty}
and
u>0
. Since

\displaystyle K(u,f;L_{m,\infty},L_{\infty})

\displaystyle\approx\sup_{0<z<u^{m}}z^{\frac{1}{m}}f^{*}(z)\approx\sup_{0<z<u^{m}}f^{*}(z)\Big(\int_{0}^{z}d\tau\Big)^{1/m}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<z<u^{m}}\Big(\int_{0}^{z}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}d\tau\Big)^{1/m}=\Big(\int_{0}^{u^{m}}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}d\tau\Big)^{1/m}

we have that

\Big\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)K(u,f;L_{m,\infty},L_{\infty})\Big\|_{q,(T,S)}\lesssim\bigg\|u^{-\theta-\frac{1}{q}}b(u)\Big(\int_{0}^{u^{m}}f^{*}(\tau)^{m}d\tau\Big)^{1/m}\bigg\|_{q,(T,S)}.

Now, using (
4.5
) we obtain (
4.4
).

∎

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg\|t^{-\frac{1}{s}}c(t)\Big\|y^{-\frac{1}{r}}\dddot{b}(y)\|z^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\dddot{a}(z)f^{*}(z)\|_{q,(y,2y)}\Big\|_{r,(0,t^{m}/2)}\bigg\|_{s,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|t^{-\frac{1}{s}}c(t)\Big\|y^{-\frac{1}{r}}\dddot{b}(y)\|z^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\dddot{a}(z)f^{*}(z)\|_{q,(y,t^{m})}\Big\|_{r,(0,t^{m}/2)}\bigg\|_{s,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|t^{-\frac{1}{s}}c(t)\Big\|y^{-\frac{1}{r}}\dddot{b}(y)\|z^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\dddot{a}(z)f^{*}(z)\|_{q,(y,t^{m})}\Big\|_{r,(0,t^{m})}\bigg\|_{s,(0,\infty)}

\displaystyle\approx\bigg\|x^{-\frac{1}{s}}\dddot{c}(x)\Big\|y^{-\frac{1}{r}}\dddot{b}(y)\|z^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\dddot{a}(z)f^{*}(z)\|_{q,(y,x)}\Big\|_{r,(0,x)}\bigg\|_{s,(0,\infty)}=\|f\|_{L^{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{L}}_{p,s,\dddot{c},r,\dddot{b},q,\dddot{a}}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

7.
Applications

Here, we demostrate how our general assertions can be used to establish interpolation results for the grand and small Lorentz-Karamata spaces. For the sake of shortness, we present only one example.

Corollary 7.1
.

(Cf.
[
16
, Theorem 4.6]
.)
Let
0<p_{0}<\infty
,
0<r,r_{0},q_{0}\leq\infty
and
a,a_{0},b_{0}\in SV
, with
b_{0}
satisfying
\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u)\|_{r_{0},(0,1)}<\infty
. Let
\chi(t)=a_{0}(t)\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u)\|_{r_{0},(0,t)}
and
\rho(t)=t^{1/p_{0}}\chi(t)
,
t>0
.

(i)

If
\|u^{-1/r}a(u)\|_{r,(1,\infty)}<\infty
, then

\big(L_{p_{0},r_{0},b_{0},q_{0},a_{0}}^{\mathcal{R}},L_{\infty}\big)_{0,r;a}=L_{p_{0},(r,a\circ\rho,r_{0},b_{0},q_{0},a_{0})}^{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{L}}.

(ii)

If
0<\theta<1
, then

\big(L_{p_{0},r_{0},b_{0},q_{0},a_{0}}^{\mathcal{R}},L_{\infty}\big)_{\theta,r;a}=L_{p,r;a^{\#}},

where
\tfrac{1}{p}=\tfrac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}
and
a^{\#}=\chi^{1-\theta}a\circ\rho
.

(iii)

If
\|u^{-1/r}a(u)\|_{r,(0,1)}<\infty
, then

\big(L_{p_{0},r_{0},b_{0},q_{0},a_{0}}^{\mathcal{R}},L_{\infty}\big)_{1,r;a}=L_{\infty,r;a\circ\rho}.

Proof.

Choose some
m
such that
0<m<\min(p_{0},q_{0})
. Put
\theta_{0}=1-\frac{m}{p_{0}}
and
X=\big(L_{p_{0},r_{0},b_{0},q_{0},a_{0}}^{\mathcal{R}},L_{\infty}\big)_{\theta,r;a}
. By Theorem
5.4
we know that

L^{\mathcal{R}}_{p_{0},r_{0},b_{0},q_{0},a_{0}}=(L_{m},L_{\infty})^{\mathcal{R}}_{\theta_{0},r_{0},b_{0}(t^{m}),q_{0},a_{0}(t^{m})}.

Thus,

X=\big((L_{m},L_{\infty})^{\mathcal{R}}_{\theta_{0},r_{0},b_{0}(t^{m}),q_{0},a_{0}(t^{m})},L_{\infty}\Big)_{\theta,r;a}.

Case
0<\theta\leq 1
. By
[
8
, Theorem 23]
and Theorem
5.1
, we have

X=(L_{m},L_{\infty})_{\eta,r;y}=L_{p,r;x},

where
\eta=(1-\theta)\theta_{0}+\theta
,
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\eta}{m}
,

y(t)=\Big(a_{0}(t^{m})\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u^{m})\|_{r_{0},(0,t)}\Big)^{1-\theta}a\Big(t^{1-\theta_{0}}a_{0}(t^{m})\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u^{m})\|_{r_{0},(0,t)}\Big)

and

x(t)=y(t^{\frac{1}{m}})=\Big(a_{0}(t)\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u^{m})\|_{r_{0},(0,t^{\frac{1}{m}})}\Big)^{1-\theta}a\Big(t^{\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{m}}a_{0}(t)\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u^{m})\|_{r_{0},(0,t^{\frac{1}{m}})}\Big).

Note that
\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{m}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}
,
\frac{1-\eta}{m}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}
, and
a_{0}(t)\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u^{m})\|_{r_{0},(0,t^{\frac{1}{m}})}\approx\chi(t)
. Thus,
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}
and
x(t)\approx\chi(t)^{1-\theta}a\big(t^{1/p_{0}}\chi(t)\big)=a^{\#}(t)
.

Case
\theta=0
. By
[
9
, Theorem 28]
and Theorem
5.7
, we arrive at

X=(L_{m},L_{\infty})^{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{L}}_{\theta_{0},r,x,r_{0},b_{0}(t^{m}),q_{0},a_{0}(t^{m})}=L^{\mathcal{R},\mathcal{L}}_{p_{0},(r,\overline{x},r_{0},b_{0},q_{0},a_{0})}

where

x(t)=a\Big(t^{1-\theta_{0}}a_{0}(t^{m})\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u^{m})\|_{r_{0},(0,t)}\Big)

and hence,

\dddot{x}(t)=a\Big(t^{\frac{1-\theta_{0}}{m}}a_{0}(t)\|u^{-1/r_{0}}b_{0}(u^{m})\|_{r_{0},(0,t^{1/m})}\Big)\approx a\big(t^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\chi(t)\big)=(a\circ\rho)(t).

This completes the proof.
∎

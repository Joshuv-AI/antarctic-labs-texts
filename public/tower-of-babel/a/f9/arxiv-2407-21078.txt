Convergence rates for the Adam optimizer
Steffen Dereich, Arnulf Jentzen

Abstract
Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) optimization methods are nowadays the method of choice for the training of deep neural networks (DNNs) in artificial intelligence systems. In practically relevant training problems, usually not the plain vanilla standard SGD method is the employed optimization scheme but instead suitably accelerated and adaptive SGD optimization methods are applied. As of today, maybe the most popular variant of such accelerated and adaptive SGD optimization methods is the famous Adam optimizer proposed by Kingma & Ba in 2014. Despite the popularity of the Adam optimizer in implementations, it remained an open problem of research to provide a convergence analysis for the Adam optimizer even in the situation of simple quadratic stochastic optimization problems where the objective function (the function one intends to minimize) is strongly convex. In this work we solve this problem by establishing optimal convergence rates for the Adam optimizer for a large class of stochastic optimization problems, in particular, covering simple quadratic stochastic optimization problems. The key ingredient of our convergence analysis is a new vector field function which we propose to refer to as the Adam vector field . This Adam vector field accurately describes the macroscopic behaviour of the Adam optimization process but differs from the negative gradient of the objective function (the function we intend to minimize) of the considered stochastic optimization problem. In particular, our convergence analysis reveals that the Adam optimizer does typically not converge to critical points of the objective function (zeros of the gradient of the objective function) of the considered optimization problem but converges with rates to zeros of this Adam vector field. Even though our convergence results are only formulated for the Adam optimizer, the arguments in our convergence analysis can also be applied to other related SGD optimization methods and open the door for a systematic mathematical treatment of a large class of adaptive and/or accelerated SGD optimization methods.

2
Adam sequence space, Adam vector field and main convergence result

In this section, we introduce the central objects and the main theorems of this article.
For this we denote by
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},{\mathbb{P}})
a probability space and by
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
the dimension of the underlying problem.
Let us introduce the Adam algorithm with full details.

Definition 2.1

(Innovation)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a measurable space.
A pair
(X,U)
consisting of

(i)

a
\mathcal{U}
-valued random variable
U
and

(ii)

a product measurable mapping
X\colon\mathcal{U}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

is called
innovation
.

Definition 2.2

(Adam optimizer)
.

Let

(i)

\alpha,\beta\in[0,1)
,
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
with
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}
(the
damping parameters
),

(ii)

n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\theta_{n_{0}},m_{n_{0}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
v_{n_{0}}\in[0,\infty)^{d}
(the
initialisation
),

(iii)

a decreasing
(0,\infty)
-valued sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
(the
sequence of step-sizes
) and

(iv)

(X,U)
an innovation.

An
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued stochastic process
(\theta_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\cap[n_{0},\infty)}
is called
Adam algorithm with damping parameters
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
and step-sizes
(\gamma_{n})
started at time
n_{0}
in
(\theta_{n_{0}},m_{n_{0}},v_{n_{0}})
, if it satisfies for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cap(n_{0},\infty)
and
i=1,\dots,d

\displaystyle\begin{split}\theta^{(i)}_{n}=\theta^{(i)}_{n-1}+\gamma_{n}\,\sigma^{(i)}_{n}\,m^{(i)}_{n},\end{split}

(4)

where
(U_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cap(n_{0},\infty)}
is a family of independent copies of
U
and for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cap(n_{0},\infty)
and
i=1,\dots,d
,

•

m_{n}=\alpha\,m_{n-1}+(1-\alpha)\,X(U_{n},\theta_{n-1})
,

•

v^{(i)}_{n}=\beta\,v^{(i)}_{n-1}+(1-\beta)\,(X^{(i)}(U_{n},\theta_{n-1}))^{2}
and

•

\displaystyle\sigma^{(i)}_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{v_{n}^{(i)}/(1-\beta^{n})}+\epsilon}
.

We call the sequence
(t_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}t_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\end{split}

(5)

the
training times
of the Adam algorithm.

Technically, the Adam algorithm defines a Markov chain when considering the tuple
(\theta_{n},m_{n},v_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
as the process of states. When doing so one faces the difficulty that the
\sqrt{v_{n}^{(i)}}
-term in the definition of
\sigma_{n}^{(i)}
is not Lipschitz which makes it hard to analyse the system. We bypass that problem by interpreting the Adam algorithm as a delay equation where the evolution is governed by the whole history of innovations that are considered as an element of an infinite dimensional sequence space which we refer to as Adam sequence space.

Definition 2.3

(Adam sequence space)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in[0,1)
,
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)

with
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}
be damping factors.
We let

\displaystyle\varrho_{k}=(1-\alpha)\epsilon^{-1}\Bigl(\alpha^{-k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}}\beta^{-k/2}\Bigr),\qquad\text{ for all $k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}$},

(6)

and denote by
\ell^{d}_{\varrho}
the space of all sequences
\mathbf{x}=(x_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\in({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}:=\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\rho_{k}\,|x_{k}|<\infty.\end{split}

(7)

We equip the space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}
.
Moreover, we let for a tuple
(m,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times[0,\infty)^{d}

\displaystyle\begin{split}(m,v)_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}=\inf\Bigl\{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}:\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{\varrho}^{d},\ &m=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}x_{k}\text{ \ and }\\
&v^{(i)}=(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}(x_{k}^{(i)})^{2}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,d\Bigr\},\end{split}

(8)

where the infimum of the empty set is
\infty
.

Our main result is an ODE approximation for the Adam algorithm. The respective vector field driving the ODE is defined in the following definition.

Definition 2.4

(Adam vector field)
.

For a tuple
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
of damping parameters and an innovation
(X,U)
we call the mapping

f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{(i)}(\theta)=(1-\alpha)\,{\mathbb{E}}^{\theta}\Bigl[1/\Bigl(\sqrt{(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}X^{(i)}(U_{k},\theta)^{2}}+\epsilon\Bigr)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}X^{(i)}(U_{k},\theta)\Bigr]\end{split}

(9)

with
(U_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
being a family of independent copies of
U
, the
Adam vector field of the innovation
(X,U)
for the damping parameters
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
.

Theorem 2.5

(Convergence rates for the Adam optimizer)
.

Let

(I)

\alpha,\beta\in[0,1)
with
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
(the
damping parameters
),

(II)

a decreasing
(0,\infty)
-valued zero-sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
(
sequence of step-sizes
),

(III)

{\mathcal{K}},c_{1},c_{2}\in[0,\infty)
and
p\in(2,\infty)
,

(IV)

\|\cdot\|
a norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
being induced by a scalar product
\llangle\cdot,\cdot\rrangle

and suppose that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}^{2}}\leq 2c_{2}<2c_{1}.\end{split}

(10)

Then for every
c\in(0,c_{1}-c_{2})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
there exist
\mathfrak{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
\eta\in(0,\infty)
so that the following statement is true.

(i)

Innovation.
Let
(X,U)
be an innovation and
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a measurable set such that for every
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in V

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[|X(U,\theta)|^{p}]^{1/p}\leq{\mathcal{K}}\text{ \ and \ }{\mathbb{E}}[|X(U,\theta)-X(U,\theta^{\prime})|^{p}]^{1/p}\leq{\mathcal{K}}\,|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|.\end{split}

(11)

(ii)

Adam algorithm.
Let
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\cap[\mathfrak{n},\infty)
and
(\theta_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
be an Adam algorithm with innovation
(X,U)
started at time
n_{0}
in a state
(\theta_{n_{0}},m_{n_{0}},v_{n_{0}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times[0,\infty)^{d}
so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}}\,(m_{n_{0}},v_{n_{0}})_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}\leq{\mathcal{K}}.\end{split}

(12)

(iii)

ODE.
Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the Adam vector field of the innovation
(X,U)
for the damping parameters
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
and
\Psi:[t_{n_{0}},\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a solution to the ODE

\dot{\Psi}_{t}=f(\Psi_{t})

staying in
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\theta_{n_{0}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{0}}}\|\leq{\mathcal{K}}.\end{split}

(13)

(iv)

Local monotonicity of ODE.

Let
(\mathfrak{R}_{t})_{t\geq t_{n_{0}}}
be a
(0,\infty]
-valued function so that one has, for all
t\in[t_{n_{0}},\infty)
and
x\in V\cap\overline{B(\Psi_{t},\mathfrak{R}_{t})}
, that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\llangle f(x)-f(\Psi_{t}),x-\Psi_{t}\rrangle\leq-c_{1}\|x-\Psi_{t}\|^{2}.}\end{split}

(14)

(v)

Let
(R_{s})_{s\geq t_{n_{0}}}
be a decreasing
(0,\infty)
-valued function such that for every
s\geq t_{n_{0}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{s}/R_{s+1}\leq{\mathcal{K}}.\end{split}

(15)

Let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{N}=\inf\{n\geq n_{0}:\theta_{n}\not\in V\text{ \ or \ }{\|\theta_{n}-\Psi_{t_{n}}\|>\mathfrak{R}_{t_{n}}}\}.\end{split}

(16)

One has, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\cap[n_{0},\infty)
, that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq n\}}{\|\theta_{n}-\Psi_{t_{n}}\|^{p}}]^{1/p}\leq\Bigl(\eta+\Bigl((1+\varepsilon)\frac{{\|\theta_{n_{0}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{0}}}\|}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}}}+\eta\sqrt{(m_{n_{0}},v_{n_{0}})_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}}\Bigr)e^{-c(t_{n}-t_{n_{0}})}\Bigr)\sqrt{\gamma_{n+1}}\end{split}

(17)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[&\Bigl(\sup_{n:n_{0}\leq n\leq\mathfrak{N}}\frac{\|\theta_{n}-\Psi_{t_{n}}\|}{R_{t_{n}}}\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]\\
&\leq\eta\int_{t_{n_{0}}}^{\infty}R_{s}^{-p}\Bigl(1+\Bigl(\frac{\|\theta_{n_{0}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{0}}}\|}{\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}}}+\sqrt{(m_{n_{0}},v_{n_{0}})_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}}\Bigr)e^{-c(s-t_{n_{0}})}\Bigr)^{p}\,\Gamma_{s}^{(p-1)/2}\,\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

(18)

where

(\Gamma_{t})_{t>0}
is the real-valued function given by

\Gamma_{t}=\gamma_{n}
, for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
t\in(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
.

3
The innovation viewed as a non-expansive mapping on the Adam sequence space

Let
\alpha,\beta,\epsilon\in[0,\infty)
with
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}<1

and let
(\varrho_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
as in
6
.
In this section our main goal is to analyse the mapping

\displaystyle\begin{split}g\colon\ell_{\varrho}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\qquad\mathbf{x}=(x_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\mapsto\Biggl(\frac{(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}x^{(i)}_{k}}{\epsilon+\sqrt{(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}(x^{(i)}_{k})^{2}}}\Biggr)_{i\in\{1,\dots,d\}}\end{split}

(19)

(see
Lemma

3.1
below).
First, we note that
g
is well defined since for every
\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{\varrho}^{d}

and
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
we have that the series in the nominator in
19
is summable.
The denominator in
19

may attain the value
\infty
in which case
g
is defined to be zero.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\epsilon\in[0,\infty)
with
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}<1

and let
(\varrho_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}

and
g
as defined in
6
and
19
, respectively. Then
g
is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
1
and
g
is uniformly bounded by
\sqrt{d}\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\beta}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}}
.

Proof.

We first assume that
d=1
and briefly write
\ell_{\varrho}=\ell_{\varrho}^{1}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{\varrho}}=\|\cdot\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{1}}
.
For
\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{\varrho}
, let

\displaystyle\begin{split}m(\mathbf{x})=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}x_{k}\text{ \ and \ }v(\mathbf{x})=(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}x_{k}^{2}\end{split}

(20)

so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(\mathbf{x})=\frac{m(\mathbf{x})}{\sqrt{v(\mathbf{x})}+\epsilon}.\end{split}

(21)

Using Cauchy-Schwarz and the fact that
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}
we get that for all
\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{\varrho}

\displaystyle\begin{split}|m(\mathbf{x})|&=\Bigl|(1-\alpha)\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}(\alpha/\sqrt{\beta})^{-k}\sqrt{\beta}^{-k}x_{k}\Bigr|\leq(1-\alpha)\Bigl(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\alpha/\sqrt{\beta})^{2k}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}\beta^{-k}x_{k}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\beta}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}}\sqrt{v(\mathbf{x})}\end{split}

(22)

which entails the uniform bound for
g
that is stated in the lemma for
d=1
. The multivariate estimates follows immediately by applying this estimate onto the individual components.

To prove the Lipschitz continuity we again focus at first on the case
d=1
. Fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{-n},0,\dots)\in\ell_{\varrho}
. One has for all
k=0,\dots,-n
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{x_{k}}g(\mathbf{x})=(1-\alpha)\alpha^{-k}\frac{1}{\sqrt{v(\mathbf{x})}+\epsilon}-(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}\frac{m(\mathbf{x})}{(\sqrt{v(\mathbf{x})}+\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{v(\mathbf{x})}}x_{k}\end{split}

(23)

so that by using (
22
) and
\sqrt{v(\mathbf{x})}\geq\sqrt{1-\beta}\beta^{-k/2}|x_{k}|
we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\partial_{x_{k}}g(\mathbf{x})|\leq(1-\alpha)\epsilon^{-1}\alpha^{-k}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}}\epsilon^{-1}\beta^{-k/2}=\varrho_{k}.\end{split}

(24)

For another
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=(\tilde{x}_{0},\dots,\tilde{x}_{-n},0,\dots)\in\ell_{\varrho}
we let, for
\ell=0,\dots,n+1
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{x}^{\ell}=(\tilde{x}_{0},\dots,\tilde{x}_{-\ell+1},x_{-\ell},\dots,x_{-n},0,\dots).\end{split}

(25)

Note that
\mathbf{x}^{0}=\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}^{n+1}=\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
and we get with the triangle inequality and the mean value theorem that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|g(\mathbf{x})-g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})|\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}|g(\mathbf{x}^{\ell})-g(\mathbf{x}^{\ell+1})|\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\varrho_{-\ell}|x_{-\ell}-\tilde{x}_{-\ell}|=\|\mathbf{x}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}}.\end{split}

(26)

This inequality remains true for general
\mathbf{x},\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\in\ell_{\varrho}
since

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(\mathbf{x})=\lim_{n\to\infty}g(x_{0},\dots,x_{-n},0,\dots).\end{split}

(27)

Indeed, this entails that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|g(\mathbf{x})-g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})|\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}|g(x_{0},\dots,x_{-n},0,\dots)-g(\tilde{x}_{0},\dots,\tilde{x}_{-n},0,\dots)|\leq\|\mathbf{x}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}}.\end{split}

(28)

To obtain the result in the multivariate setting we proceed as above. Fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{-n},0,\dots)\in\ell_{\varrho}^{d}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=(\tilde{x}_{0},\dots,\tilde{x}_{-n},0,\dots)\in\ell_{\varrho}^{d}
and define for every
\ell=0,\dots,n+1
,

\mathbf{x}^{\ell}\in\ell_{\varrho}^{d}
accordingly. Then one has for every
\ell=0,\dots,n
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|g(\mathbf{x}^{\ell})-g(\mathbf{x}^{\ell+1})|=\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|g(\mathbf{x}^{\ell,(i)})-g(\mathbf{x}^{\ell+1,(i)})|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\leq\varrho_{-\ell}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{\ell}^{(i)}-\tilde{x}_{\ell}^{(i)}|^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},\end{split}

(29)

where we applied (
24
) for each of the
d
components.
This entails the multivariate variant of estimate (
26
) that is

\displaystyle\begin{split}|g(\mathbf{x})-g(\tilde{\mathbf{x}})|\leq\|\mathbf{x}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\|_{\ell^{d}_{\varrho}}.\end{split}

(30)

The rest follows in complete analogy to the one dimensional setting.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
one has that
\varrho_{k-1}\leq\sqrt{\beta}\varrho_{k}
and
the translation operator

\displaystyle\begin{split}T\colon\ell_{\varrho}^{d}\to\ell_{\varrho}^{d},\qquad\mathbf{x}=(x_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\mapsto(1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{k\not=0\}}x_{k+1})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\end{split}

(31)

is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
\sqrt{\beta}<1
. In particular, it maps
\ell_{\varrho}
to
\ell_{\varrho}
.

Proof.

By definition of
(\varrho_{k})
it immediately follows that, for every
k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varrho_{k-1}\leq\sqrt{\beta}\varrho_{k}.\end{split}

(32)

Consequently, one has that, for every
\mathbf{x}\in\ell_{\varrho}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T(\mathbf{x})\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}=\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\varrho_{k-1}|x_{k}|\leq\sqrt{\beta}\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\varrho_{k}|x_{k}|=\sqrt{\beta}\,\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}.\end{split}

(33)

The statement follows by linearity of
T
.
∎

4
Analysis of the Adam evolution with fixed
\theta
-parameter

Let us introduce the setting of this section. Let
(X,U)
be an innovation and
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
be damping parameters with
0\leq\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}<1
. The Adam algorithm for the innovation
(X,U)
is a stochastic process where in its iterative definition in
4

the momentum and damping terms are updated by using the current
\theta
-value of the process. In this section, we analyse a similar process where we keep the
\theta
-value that is used when updating the momentum and damping term fixed.

In the following, fix
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
(U_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be an i.i.d. sequence of copies of
U
. Moreover, for every
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, let
X_{n}=X(U_{n},\theta)
and

\mathbf{X}(n)=(X_{n+k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. We fix
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and consider the process

(\bar{\Theta}_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\Theta}_{n}=\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\,g(\mathbf{X}(k)).\end{split}

(34)

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
. One has for every

n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
n\geq n_{0}
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|\bar{\Theta}_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{\Theta}_{n}]|^{p}\bigr]^{2/p}\leq\kappa^{2}\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|X(U,\theta)-{\mathbb{E}}[X(U,\theta)]|^{p}\bigr]^{2/p},\end{split}

(35)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa=2C_{p}\bigl((1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-2}\varrho_{0}\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}+\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}^{2}\bigr),\end{split}

(36)

\varrho
is as in (
6
) and
C_{p}
is the constant appearing in the the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality for the
p
-th moment.

Proof.

For ease of notation we prove the statement for
d=1
. The generalisation to general
d
is straight-forward. Let
\tilde{X}_{0}
be identically distributed as
X_{0}
and independent of the sequence
(X_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and set

\tilde{X}_{n}=X_{n}
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\{0\}
.
We let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(n)=(\tilde{X}_{n+k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}.\end{split}

(37)

Thus
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(n)
agrees with
\mathbf{X}(n)
in all but at most one component.

We let
(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the filtration generated by
(X_{n})
and consider for fixed
N\geq n_{0}
the martingale
(M_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{n}={\mathbb{E}}[\bar{\Theta}_{N}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{\Theta}_{N}]\end{split}

(38)

We denote by
([M]_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
its quadratic variation process being defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}[M]_{n}=\sum_{r=-\infty}^{n}(M_{r}-M_{r-1})^{2}\end{split}

(39)

and analyse the
q/2
-th moment of its increments
\Delta[M]_{n}:=(M_{n}-M_{n-1})^{2}
.
Note that for every
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cap(-\infty,N]
, one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta M_{n}={\mathbb{E}}[\bar{\Theta}_{N}|\mathcal{F}_{n}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{\Theta}_{N}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\mathbf{X}(r))|\mathcal{F}_{n}\Bigr]-{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\mathbf{X}(r))|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\Bigr].\end{split}

(40)

Now using that
(X_{n})
is an i.i.d. sequence we conclude that, for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\mathbf{X}(r))\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\Bigr](\omega)={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\mathbf{X}(r-n))\Big|\forall k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:X_{k}=X_{k+n}(\omega)\Bigr]\end{split}

(41)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\mathbf{X}(r))\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\Bigr](\omega)&={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\mathbf{X}(r-n))\Big|\forall k\in-{\mathbb{N}}:X_{k}=X_{k+n}(\omega)\Bigr]\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(r-n))\Big|\forall k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:X_{k}=X_{k+n}(\omega)\Bigr].\end{split}

(42)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[&\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\mathbf{X}(r))\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n}\Bigr](\omega)-{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}g(\mathbf{X}(r))\Big|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\Bigr](\omega)\\
&={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\underbrace{\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{r}(g(\mathbf{X}(r-n))-g(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(r-n)))}_{=:\Upsilon_{n}[N]}\Big|\forall k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:X_{k}=X_{k+n}(\omega)\Bigr].\end{split}

(43)

By stationarity, the distribution of the conditional expectation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\Upsilon_{n}[N]\big|\forall k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:X_{k}=X_{k+n}(\omega)\bigr]\end{split}

(44)

is the same as the distribution of
{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\Upsilon_{n}[N]\big|X_{0},X_{-1},\dots\bigr]
. Using this and Jensen’s inequality we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\Delta[M]_{n}^{p/2}\bigr]={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|M_{n}-M_{n-1}|^{p}\bigr]={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\bigl|{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\Upsilon_{n}[N]\big|X_{0},X_{-1},\dots\bigr]\bigr|^{p}\bigr]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|\Upsilon_{n}[N]|^{p}\bigr].\end{split}

(45)

It follows with the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[[M]_{N}^{p/2}\bigr]^{2/p}={\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N}\Delta[M]_{n}\Bigr)^{p/2}\Bigr]^{2/p}\leq\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[(\Delta[M]_{n})^{p/2}\bigr]^{2/p}\leq\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|\Upsilon_{n}[N]|^{p}\bigr]^{2/p}.\end{split}

(46)

Now for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
n\leq N
we get with Lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\Upsilon_{n}[N]|\leq\sum_{r=n\vee(n_{0}+1)}^{N}\gamma_{r}|g(\mathbf{X}(r-n))-g(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(r-n))|\leq\sum_{r=n\vee(n_{0}+1)}^{N}\gamma_{r}\varrho_{n-r}|X_{0}-\tilde{X}_{0}|\end{split}

(47)

and again by the triangle inequality we have in terms of
C:={\mathbb{E}}[|X_{0}-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{0}]|^{p}]^{1/p}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[|\Upsilon_{n}[N]|^{p}]^{1/p}&\leq\sum_{r=n\vee(n_{0}+1)}^{N}\gamma_{r}\varrho_{n-r}\,{\mathbb{E}}[|X_{0}-\tilde{X}_{0}|^{p}]^{1/p}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}2C\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}\gamma_{n},&\text{ if }n\geq n_{0}+1,\\
2C(1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-1}\varrho_{n-(n_{0}+1)}\gamma_{n_{0}+1},&\text{ if }n\leq n_{0}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(48)

Together with (
46
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[[M]_{N}^{p/2}\bigr]^{2/p}&\leq\sum_{n=-\infty}^{N}{\mathbb{E}}[|\Upsilon_{n}[N]|^{p}]^{2/p}\\
&\leq 4C^{2}(1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-2}\gamma_{n_{0}+1}^{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{n_{0}}\varrho_{n-(n_{0}+1)}^{2}+4C^{2}\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}^{2}\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{n}^{2}\\
&\leq 4C^{2}\Bigl((1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-2}\varrho_{0}\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}\gamma_{n_{0}+1}^{2}+\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}^{2}\sum_{n=n_{0}+1}^{N}\gamma_{n}^{2}\Bigr).\end{split}

(49)

The statement follows with the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality since
\bar{\Theta}_{N}={\mathbb{E}}[\bar{\Theta}_{N}]+M_{N}
.
∎

Next, we derive an extension of the previous lemma that considers whole trajectories instead of single time instances.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
. One has for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
n\geq n_{0}

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\max_{k=n_{0},\dots,n}|\bar{\Theta}_{k}-{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{\Theta}_{k}]|^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\leq(1-2^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})})^{-1}\kappa\gamma_{n_{0}+1}\sqrt{n-n_{0}}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|X_{0}-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{0}]|^{p}\bigr]^{1/p},\end{split}

(50)

where
\kappa
is as in (
36
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
n_{0}=0
since the general statement then follows by an index shift.

Suppose that
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
2^{m-1}\leq N\leq 2^{m}-1
.
We call for every
k=1,\dots,m
, all intervals of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}[j,j+2^{m-k})\qquad(j\in(2^{m-k+1}{\mathbb{N}}_{0})\cap\{0,\dots,N\})\end{split}

(51)

and additionally the empty set an interval of the
k
-th layer. Note that by construction for every natural number
n\in\{0,\dots,N\}
the interval
[0,n)
can be uniquely written as pairwise disjoint union

\displaystyle\begin{split}[0,n)=[0,n_{1})\cup[n_{1},n_{2})\cup\ldots\cup[n_{m-1},n_{m})\end{split}

(52)

with each
[n_{k-1},n_{k})
being an interval of the
k
-th layer. Indeed, the representation is obtained for
n_{k}=2^{m-k}\lfloor 2^{-(m-k)}n\rfloor
.
We let
\bar{\Theta}_{k,\ell}:=\bar{\Theta}_{\ell}-\bar{\Theta}_{k}-{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{\Theta}_{\ell}-\bar{\Theta}_{k}]
for every
k,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
0\leq k\leq\ell
. Then for
n
as above we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\Theta}_{0,n}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\bar{\Theta}_{n_{k-1},n_{k}}.\end{split}

(53)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\max_{n\in\{0,\dots,N\}}|\bar{\Theta}_{0,n}|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}\max_{[n_{k-1},n_{k}):\text{ interval of $k$th layer}}|\bar{\Theta}_{n_{k-1},n_{k}}|\end{split}

(54)

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\max_{n\in\{0,\dots,N\}}|\bar{\Theta}_{0,n}|^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\max_{[n_{k-1},n_{k}):\text{ interval of $k$th layer}}|\bar{\Theta}_{n_{k-1},n_{k}}|^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}.

(55)

Now note that by Lemma
4.1
one has in terms of
C:={\mathbb{E}}[|X_{0}-{\mathbb{E}}[X_{0}]|^{p}]^{1/p}
that for an arbitrary non-empty interval
[n_{k-1},n_{k})
of the
k
-th layer

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[|\bar{\Theta}_{n_{k-1}:n_{k}}|^{p}]^{2/p}\leq\kappa^{2}\gamma_{1}^{2}2^{m-k}C^{2}\end{split}

(56)

so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\max_{[n_{k-1},n_{k}):\text{ interval of $k$th layer}}|\bar{\Theta}_{n_{k-1},n_{k}}|^{p}\Bigr]&\leq\sum_{[n_{k-1},n_{k}):\text{ interval of $k$th layer}}{\mathbb{E}}[|\bar{\Theta}_{n_{k-1},n_{k}}|^{p}]\\
&\leq\kappa^{p}\gamma_{1}^{p}C^{p}2^{k-1}(2^{m-k})^{p/2}.\end{split}

(57)

Together with (
55
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\max_{n\in\{0,\dots,N\}}|\bar{\Theta}_{0,n}|^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\leq\kappa\gamma_{1}C\sqrt{2^{m-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}2^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})(k-1)}\leq(1-2^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})})^{-1}\kappa\gamma_{1}C\sqrt{N}.\end{split}

(58)

∎

5
Introducing approximations of the Adam optimization processes

In the proof of Theorem
2.5
, we will work with approximations to the Adam algorithm that are sufficiently close but easier to analyse.
For this we fix damping parameters
\alpha,\beta,\epsilon\in[0,\infty)

with
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}<1
and an innovation
(X,U)
and denote by

(\theta_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}

an Adam algorithm started at time
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}

in a state
(\theta_{n_{0}},m_{n_{0}},v_{n_{0}})
.
We assume that there exists a deterministic

\mathbf{x}=(x_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\in\ell_{\varrho}^{d}

such that for all
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}m_{n_{0}}=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}x_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad v^{(i)}_{n_{0}}=(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}(x^{(i)}_{k})^{2}.\end{split}

(59)

We consider the
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued process
(X_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{k}=\begin{cases}x_{k-n_{0}},&\text{ if }k\leq n_{0},\\
X(U_{k},\theta_{k-1}),&\text{ if }k>n_{0}\end{cases}\end{split}

(60)

and we set for every
n\geq n_{0}
,
\mathbf{X}(n)=(X_{n+k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
.

The terms
m_{n}
and
v_{n}
(defined as in Def.
2.2
) may be directly represented as weighted series over the past innovations and we get that for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
i=1,\dots,d
one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma^{(i)}_{n}m_{n}^{(i)}=\frac{(1-\alpha)\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}\alpha^{-k}X^{(i)}_{n+k}}{\epsilon+\sqrt{(1-\beta)/(1-\beta^{n})\,\sum_{k=-\infty}^{0}\beta^{-k}(X^{(i)}_{n+k})^{2}}}.\end{split}

(61)

Note that up to an additional factor
(1-\beta^{n})
the latter expression agrees with

g(\mathbf{X}^{(i)}(n))
(cf.
19
). This means that for large
n
the increments of the process
(\theta_{n})
are similar to the ones of the process
(\Theta_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Theta_{n}:=\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\,g(\mathbf{X}(k)).\end{split}

(62)

For technical reasons we will consider dynamical systems with simpler dynamics. For this denote by
(n_{\ell})_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
an increasing
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
-valued sequence tending to infinity. Here deliberately the initial value
n_{0}
of the series coincides with the initial time of the Adam scheme.
We will use the times in the sequence
(n_{\ell})
as update-times for the
\theta
-parameter similar to an Euler approximation. More explicitly, we define
for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

n\in\{n_{\ell-1}+1,\dots,n_{\ell}\}
,
the process
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(n)=(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(n)_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(n)_{k}=\begin{cases}X(U_{n+k},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}),&\text{ \ if }n+k>n_{\ell-1},\\
X_{n+k},&\text{ \ else.}\end{cases}\end{split}

(63)

Comparing
\mathbf{X}(n)
and
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(n)
we see that the terms outside the
(n_{\ell-1},n_{\ell}]
-window agree and inside the window the
\theta
-parameter is fixed as
\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}
.
In analogy to before we consider the process
(\tilde{\Theta}_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}

given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\Theta}_{n}:=\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\,g(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(k)).\end{split}

(64)

Next, let
(U_{\ell,n})_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be a family of independent copies of
U
that is also independent of
(U_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. For every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
n\in\{n_{\ell-1}+1,\dots,n_{\ell}\}
, we consider
\tilde{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}(n)=(\tilde{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}(n)_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}(n)_{k}=\begin{cases}X(U_{n+k},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})=\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(n)_{k},&\text{ \ if }n+k>n_{\ell-1},\\
X(U_{\ell,n+k},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}),&\text{ \ else.}\end{cases}\end{split}

(65)

In analogy to before we define a process
(\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
via

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{n}=\sum_{k=n_{0}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\,g(\tilde{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}(k)).\end{split}

(66)

We see that in
\tilde{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}(n)
and
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(n)
the terms inside the
(n_{\ell-1},n_{\ell}]
-window agree. Moreover, the terms are chosen in such a way that conditionally on
\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}
,
\tilde{\tilde{\mathbf{X}}}(n)
is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables. Roughly speaking, this entail that the related approximation is in “stationary equilibrium”.

As last approximation we denote by
(\bar{\Theta}_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
the unique process satisfying for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
n=n_{\ell-1}+1,\dots,n_{\ell}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta\bar{\Theta}_{n}:=\bar{\Theta}_{n}-\bar{\Theta}_{n-1}=\gamma_{n}f(\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})\end{split}

(67)

and
\bar{\Theta}_{n_{0}}=0
.

In the analysis, we will impose the following regularity assumption on the innovation.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
C,\tilde{C},\tilde{L}\in[0,\infty)
,
p\in[2,\infty)
and
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a measurable set. An innovation
(X,U)
is called
p
-regular with parameter
(C,\tilde{C},\tilde{L})
on

, if for every
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in V
, one has

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[|X(U,\theta)|^{2}]^{1/2}\leq C,\ &{\mathbb{E}}[|X(U,\theta)-{\mathbb{E}}[X(U,\theta)]|^{p}]^{1/p}\leq\tilde{C}\text{ \ and}\\
&\qquad{\mathbb{E}}[|X(U,\theta)-X(U,\theta^{\prime})|^{p}]^{1/p}\leq\tilde{L}\,|\theta-\theta^{\prime}|.\end{split}

(68)

We give a quantitative statement that allows us to control the error between the Adam algorithm and its approximations.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let

(i)

\alpha,\beta\in[0,1)
with
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
(the
damping parameters
),

(ii)

n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\theta_{n_{0}},m_{n_{0}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
v_{n_{0}}\in[0,\infty)^{d}
(the initialisation),

(iii)

a decreasing
(0,\infty)
-valued sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
(
sequence of step-sizes
),

(iv)

(X,U)
an innovation,

(v)

C,\tilde{C},\tilde{L}\in[0,\infty)
,
p\in[2,\infty)
and
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a measurable set,

(vi)

(n_{\ell})_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a strictly increasing
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
-valued sequence.

Let
(X,U)
be a
p
-regular innovation with parameter
(C,\tilde{C},\tilde{L})
on
V

and
(\theta_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\cap[n_{0},\infty)}
the Adam algorithm with damping parameter
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
and step-sizes
(\gamma_{n})
started at time
n_{0}
in
(\theta_{n_{0}},m_{n_{0}},v_{n_{0}})
.
Moreover, let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathfrak{N}=\inf\{n\geq n_{0}:\theta(n)\not\in V\}\end{split}

(69)

and let
(t_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
as in (
5
).
One has the following for the approximations
(\Theta_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
,
(\tilde{\Theta}_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
and
(\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
:

(I)

for every
\mathfrak{n}\in\{n_{0},n_{0}+1,\dots\}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{\infty}1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq k\}}|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\Theta_{k}|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}&\leq\bigl(\kappa_{1}\beta^{\frac{1}{2}(\mathfrak{n}+1)-n_{0}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell^{d}_{\varrho}}+\kappa_{2}C\bigr)\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}\beta^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\end{split}

(70)

(II)

for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathfrak{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
n_{\ell-1}\leq\mathfrak{n}\leq n\leq n_{\ell}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{n}1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq k\}}|\Delta\Theta_{k}-\Delta\tilde{\Theta}_{k}|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}&\leq\kappa_{3}\tilde{L}(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}})(t_{n}-t_{\mathfrak{n}})\end{split}

(71)

(III)

for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[

\displaystyle 1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>n_{\ell-1}\}}\Bigl(\sum_{k=n_{\ell-1}+1}^{n_{\ell}}\bigl|\Delta{\tilde{\Theta}}_{k}-\Delta\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{k}\bigr|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\leq\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}\bigl(\kappa_{4}C+\kappa_{5}\beta^{(n_{\ell-1}-n_{0})/2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}),

(72)

(IV.a)

for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>n_{\ell-1}\}}|\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{n_{\ell}}-\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{n_{\ell-1}}-(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}})f(\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})|^{p}|\mathcal{F}_{n_{\ell-1}}]^{1/p}\leq\kappa_{6}\tilde{C}\Bigl(\sum_{k=n_{\ell-1}+1}^{n_{\ell}}\gamma_{k}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},\end{split}

(73)

(IV.b)

if
p>2
, then for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>n_{\ell-1}\}}\max_{n=n_{\ell-1},\dots,n_{\ell}}|\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{n}&-\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{n_{\ell-1}}-(t_{n}-t_{n_{\ell-1}})f(\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})|^{p}|\mathcal{F}_{n_{\ell-1}}]^{1/p}\\
&\leq(1-2^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})})^{-1}\kappa_{6}\tilde{C}\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}\sqrt{n_{\ell}-n_{\ell-1}},\end{split}

(74)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\kappa_{1}=\frac{2}{1-\beta^{3/2}},\ \kappa_{2}=\frac{2}{1-\beta^{3/2}}\bigl(\frac{\varrho_{0}}{1-\beta}+\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}\bigr),\kappa_{3}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\beta}\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}}d\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}},\\
&\kappa_{4}=2(1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-1}\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}},\ \kappa_{5}=(1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-1},\ \kappa_{6}=2C_{p}\bigl((1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-1}\sqrt{\varrho_{0}\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}}+\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}\bigr),\end{split}

(75)

(\varrho_{k})
is as in (
6
) and
C_{p}
is the constant in the Burholder-Davis-Gundy inequality when applied for the
p
th moment.

Proof.

1) We prove the first inequality.
Note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|\sigma^{(i)}_{k}m_{k}^{(i)}-g(\mathbf{X}^{(i)}(k))\bigr|&\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1/(1-\beta^{k})}}\Bigr)|g(\mathbf{X}^{(i)}(k))|\leq 2\beta^{k}\|\mathbf{X}^{(i)}(k)\|_{\ell_{\varrho}}\\
&\leq 2\beta^{k}\Bigl({\sqrt{\beta}}^{k-n_{0}}\|\mathbf{x}^{(i)}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}}+\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{k}\varrho_{r-k}|X^{(i)}_{r}|\Bigr).\end{split}

(76)

This entails that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq k\}}|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\Theta_{k}|^{p}]^{1/p}\leq 2\beta^{k}\Bigl({\sqrt{\beta}}^{k-n_{0}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell^{d}_{\varrho}}+C\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{k}\varrho_{r-k}\Bigr).\end{split}

(77)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{\infty}1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq k\}}\gamma_{k}&|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\Theta_{k}|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\leq 2\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\beta^{k}\Bigl({\sqrt{\beta}}^{k-n_{0}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell^{d}_{\varrho}}+C\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{k}\varrho_{r-k}\Bigr)\\
&\leq 2(1-\beta^{3/2})^{-1}\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}\beta^{\frac{3}{2}(\mathfrak{n}+1)-n_{0}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell^{d}_{\varrho}}+2C\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=r\vee(\mathfrak{n}+1)}^{n}\beta^{k}\varrho_{r-k}\end{split}

(78)

In the case where
r\leq\mathfrak{n}
, one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{k=r\vee(\mathfrak{n}+1)}^{n}\beta^{k}\varrho_{r-k}\leq(1-\beta^{3/2})^{-1}\varrho_{r-(\mathfrak{n}+1)}\beta^{\mathfrak{n}+1}\end{split}

(79)

and in the case where
r>\mathfrak{n}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{k=r\vee(\mathfrak{n}+1)}^{n}\beta^{k}\varrho_{r-k}\leq(1-\beta^{3/2})^{-1}\varrho_{0}\beta^{r}.\end{split}

(80)

Hence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[&\Bigl(\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{\infty}1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq k\}}\gamma_{k}|\Delta\theta_{k}-\Delta\Theta_{k}|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\\
&\leq 2(1-\beta^{3/2})^{-1}\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}\Bigl(\beta^{\frac{3}{2}(\mathfrak{n}+1)-n_{0}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell^{d}_{\varrho}}+\beta^{\mathfrak{n}+1}C\sum_{r=n_{0}+1}^{\mathfrak{n}}\varrho_{r-(\mathfrak{n}+1)}+C\sum_{r=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{n}\varrho_{0}\beta^{r}\Bigr)\\
&\leq 2(1-\beta^{3/2})^{-1}\bigl(\beta^{\frac{1}{2}(\mathfrak{n}+1)-n_{0}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell^{d}_{\varrho}}+(\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}+(1-\beta)^{-1}\varrho_{0})C\bigr)\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}\beta^{\mathfrak{n}+1}.\end{split}

(81)

2.) Fix
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Since for every
k\geq n_{0}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma^{(i)}_{k}m^{(i)}_{k}\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\beta}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}},\end{split}

(82)

one has for every
n=n_{\ell-1}+1,\dots,n_{\ell}

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\theta_{n}^{(i)}-\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}^{(i)}|=\Bigl|\sum_{k=n_{\ell-1}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\,\sigma^{(i)}_{k}m^{(i)}_{k}\Bigr|\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\beta}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}}(t_{n}-t_{n_{\ell-1}}).\end{split}

(83)

This implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq n\}}|X(U_{n},\theta_{n-1})-X(U_{n},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})|^{p}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}]^{1/p}\leq\underbrace{\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\beta}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}}d}_{=\kappa_{3}/\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}}\tilde{L}(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}}).\end{split}

(84)

Now note that for
k=n_{\ell-1}+1,\ldots,n_{\ell}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|g(\mathbf{X}(k))-g(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(k))\bigr|\leq\sum_{r=n_{\ell-1}+1}^{k}\rho_{r-k}\,|X(U_{r},\theta_{r-1})-X(U_{r},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})|.\end{split}

(85)

This implies that for
\mathfrak{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
with
n_{\ell-1}\leq\mathfrak{n}\leq n\leq n_{\ell}

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl(&\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{n}1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>k\}}\gamma_{k}\bigl|g(\mathbf{X}(k))-g(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(k))\bigr|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\\
&\leq\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl(1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>k\}}\bigl|g(\mathbf{X}(k))-g(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}(k))\bigr|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\\
&\leq\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl(1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>k\}}\sum_{r=n_{\ell-1}+1}^{k}\rho_{r-k}\,|X(U_{r},\theta_{r-1})-X(U_{r},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\\
&\leq\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}\sum_{r=n_{\ell-1}+1}^{k}\varrho_{r-k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl(1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>r\}}|X(U_{r},\theta_{r-1})-X(U_{r},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})|\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\\
&\leq\kappa_{3}\tilde{L}(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}})\sum_{k=\mathfrak{n}+1}^{n}\gamma_{k}.\end{split}

(86)

3.) Note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\Delta\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{\mathfrak{n},n}-\Delta\tilde{\Theta}_{\mathfrak{n},n}|=|\gamma_{n}\,(g(\mathbf{\tilde{\tilde{X}}}(n))-g(\mathbf{\tilde{X}}(n)))|\leq\gamma_{n}\,\|\mathbf{\tilde{\tilde{X}}}(n)-\mathbf{\tilde{X}}(n)\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}\end{split}

(87)

and for
n=n_{\ell-1}+1,\dots,n_{\ell}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathbf{\tilde{\tilde{X}}}(n)-\mathbf{\tilde{X}}(n)\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}&=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n_{\ell-1}}\varrho_{k-n}|X_{k}-X(U_{\ell,k},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})|\\
&\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n_{\ell-1}}\varrho_{k-n}|X_{k}|+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n_{\ell-1}}\varrho_{k-n}|X(U_{\ell,k},\theta_{n_{\ell-1}})|.\end{split}

(88)

Hence,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>n_{\ell-1}\}}\|\mathbf{\tilde{\tilde{X}}}(n)-\mathbf{\tilde{X}}(n)\|^{p}_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}\bigr]^{1/p}]&\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}{\sqrt{\beta}}^{n-n_{0}}+2C\sum_{k=-\infty}^{n_{\ell-1}}\varrho_{k-n}\\
&\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}{\sqrt{\beta}}^{n-n_{0}}+2(1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-1}C\varrho_{n_{\ell-1}-n}\end{split}

(89)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}>n_{\ell-1}\}}&\sum_{n=n_{\ell-1}+1}^{n_{\ell}}|\Delta\tilde{\tilde{\Theta}}_{n}-\Delta\tilde{\Theta}_{n}|^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\leq\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}\sum_{n=n_{\ell-1}+1}^{n_{\ell}}\bigl(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}{\sqrt{\beta}}^{n-n_{0}}+2(1-\sqrt{\beta})^{-1}C\varrho_{n_{\ell-1}-n}\bigr)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{\beta}}\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}\bigl({\sqrt{\beta}}^{n_{\ell-1}-n_{0}}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}+2\|\varrho\|_{\ell_{1}}C\bigr).\end{split}

(90)

4.) The inequalities (IV.a) and (IV.b) are direct consequences of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
when conditioning on
\mathcal{F}_{n_{\ell-1}}
.

\mathfrak{n}=n_{\ell-1}
and

Definition 5.3
.

Let
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and

(t_{n})
is as in (

Remark 5.4
.

In the definition we assume that
\rho\geq\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}}

\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}

(n_{\ell})

\lim_{n\to\infty}n_{\ell}=\infty

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a
(0,\infty)
-valued decreasing non-summable sequence and let

(n_{\ell})_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a
\rho
-partition for
(\gamma_{n})
.

(i)

For
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}<\rho^{2}
, one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}}\leq\frac{1}{\rho-\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}}}(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}}).\end{split}

(92)

(ii)

Assume that for all
n=n_{0}+1,n_{0}+2,\dots

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}^{2}}\leq\zeta\end{split}

(93)

and that
\rho\zeta\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}}<1
. Set
K=(1-\zeta\rho\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}})^{-1}
. One has for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}}{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}\leq K\text{ \ and \ }\frac{\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}}{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}\leq 1+\zeta K(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}}).\end{split}

(94)

Proof.

(i): This is an immediate consequence of

\displaystyle\begin{split}t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}}\geq\rho\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}}-\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}=(\rho-\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}})\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell-1}+1}}.\end{split}

(95)

(ii): Denote by
f:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
the function satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{split}f(t_{n})=\gamma_{n+1}\end{split}

(96)

for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and that is piecewise linear in between the points of
\{t_{0},t_{1},\ldots\}

First note that on each interval

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\|\cdot\|
be a norm induced by a scalar product
\llangle\cdot,\cdot\rrangle
on

. One has for every

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|x+y\|^{p}\leq\|x\|^{p}+p\|x\|^{p-2}\llangle x,y\rrangle+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}p(p-1)(\|x\|\vee\|x+y\|)^{p-2}\|y\|^{2}.\end{split}

(102)

Proof.

Let
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Note that the function
h(z)=\|z\|^{p}

(z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is twice continuously differentiable with differentials

\displaystyle\begin{split}Dh(z)(a)=p\,\|z\|^{p-2}\llangle z,a\rrangle\text{ \ and \ }D^{2}h(z)(a,b)=p(p-2)\|z\|^{p-4}\llangle z,a\rrangle\llangle z,b\rrangle+p|z|^{p-2}\llangle a,b\rrangle.\end{split}

(103)

By Taylor’s theorem, we thus have

\displaystyle\begin{split}|x+y|^{p}=|x|^{p}+p\,|x|^{p-2}\langle x,y\rangle+\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}h(x+ry)(y,y)(1-r)\,\mathrm{d}r.\end{split}

Note that for every

|x+ry|\leq|x|\vee|x+y|
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|D^{2}h(x+ry)(y,y)\|&\leq p(p-2)\|x+ry\|^{p-4}\|\llangle x+ry,y\rrangle\llangle x+ry,y\rrangle|+p\|x+ry\|^{p-2}\llangle y,y\rrangle\\
&\leq p(p-1)(\|x\|\vee\|x+y\|)^{p-2}\|y\|^{2}.\end{split}

(105)

The statement follows since
\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{2}
.

6
Ordinary differential equation (ODE) based error analyses for the Adam optimizer

In this section we prove the central estimates for the analysis of the Adam algorithm. The analysis is done in such a way that the impact of certain perturbation effects is made completely transparent in the propositions. Although this precise information is not used when deriving the main theorems, the results allow to immediately investigate the impact of the tuning parameters of Adam algorithms such as the damping parameters and the size of the mini-batch in the case where the innovation is obtained by the simulation of mini-batches.
So it is straight-forward to deduce variants of our main theorems, where the precise role of certain tuning parameters is revealed.

&\quad+\sqrt{2{\mathcal{K}}\eta_{4}}\sqrt{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon)\bar{e}_{\ell}+\eta_{5}\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}},\end{split}

(169)

where
\eta_{5}
is an appropriate constant.
By Proposition
6.2
, there is a constant
\eta_{6}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sup_{n:n_{\ell-1}\leq n\leq n_{\ell}\wedge\mathfrak{N}}\|\theta_{n}-\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}-(\Psi_{t_{n}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{\ell-1}}})\|^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}&\leq\eta_{6}\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}(\bar{e}_{\ell-1}+1)\\
&\leq\eta_{6}\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}((1+\varepsilon)\bar{e}_{\ell}+\eta_{5}\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}+1)\end{split}

(170)

Assuming that
\mathfrak{n}
is sufficiently large to guarantee that
\eta_{6}\sqrt{\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}}\leq\varepsilon
and
\eta_{5}\sqrt{\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}}\leq 1
we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sup_{n:n_{\ell-1}\leq n\leq n_{\ell}\wedge\mathfrak{N}}\|\theta_{n}-\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}-(\Psi_{t_{n}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{\ell-1}}})\|^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}&\leq\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)\bar{e}_{\ell}+2\eta_{6}\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}.\end{split}

(171)

Combining this estimate
with the triangle inequality and (
169
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sup_{n_{\ell-1}\leq n\leq n_{\ell}\wedge\mathfrak{N}}&\|\theta_{n}-\Psi_{t_{n}}\|^{p}\Bigr]^{1/p}\leq{\mathbb{E}}[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq n_{\ell}\}}\|\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{\ell-1}}}\|^{p}]^{1/p}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\sup_{n:n_{\ell-1}\leq n\leq n_{\ell}\wedge\mathfrak{N}}\|\theta_{n}-\theta_{n_{\ell-1}}-(\Psi_{t_{n}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{\ell-1}}})\|^{p}\bigr]^{1/p}\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\bar{e}_{\ell}+(\eta_{5}+2\eta_{6})\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}\\
&\leq\Bigl(\eta_{7}+e^{-c(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{0}})}\Bigl((1+\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{|\theta_{n_{0}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{0}}}|}{\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}}}+\eta_{7}\sqrt{(m_{0},v_{0})_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}},\end{split}

(172)

where
\eta_{7}
is an appropriate constant. This proves the first statement by noticing that, in particular, for
n\geq n_{0}
one can choose
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\in\{n_{\ell-1},\dots,n_{\ell}\}
and get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{\mathfrak{N}\geq n\}}&\|\theta_{n}-\Psi_{t_{n}}\|^{p}\bigr]^{1/p}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\bar{e}_{\ell}+(\eta_{5}+2\eta_{6})\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}}\\
&\leq\Bigl(\eta_{7}+e^{-c(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{0}})}\Bigl((1+\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{\|\theta_{n_{0}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{0}}}\|}{\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}}}+\eta_{7}\sqrt{(m_{0},v_{0})_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}}\Bigr)\Bigr)\sqrt{\gamma_{n+1}}.\end{split}

(173)

6. Proof of the second statement.
For ease of notation, we write in this part

\displaystyle\begin{split}a:=\eta_{7}\text{ \ and \ }b:=(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{\|\theta_{n_{0}}-\Psi_{t_{n_{0}}}\|}{\sqrt{\gamma_{n_{0}+1}}}+\eta_{7}(m_{n_{0}},v_{n_{0}})_{\ell_{\varrho}^{d}}.\end{split}

(174)

By assumption,
(R_{t})_{t\geq t_{n_{0}}}
is decreasing which implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[&\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}R_{t_{n_{\ell-1}}}^{-p}\sup_{n_{\ell-1}\leq n\leq n_{\ell}\wedge\mathfrak{N}}\|\theta_{n}-\Psi_{t_{n}}\|^{p}\Bigr]\\
&\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}R_{t_{n_{\ell-1}}}^{-p}\bigl(a+be^{-c(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{0}})})^{p}\,\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}^{p/2}\\
&\leq(\rho-\sqrt{\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}})^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}R_{t_{n_{\ell-1}}}^{-p}\bigl(a+be^{-c(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{0}})})^{p}\gamma_{n_{\ell}+1}^{(p-1)/2}(t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}})\\
&\leq(\rho-\sqrt{\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}})^{-1}\int_{t_{n_{0}}}^{\infty}R_{s}^{-p}\bigl(a+be^{-c(s-t_{n_{0}})})^{p}\,\Gamma_{s}^{(p-1)/2}\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(175)

By (a), we have that
t_{n_{\ell}}-t_{n_{\ell-1}}\leq 1
so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sup_{n=n_{0},\dots,\mathfrak{N}}R_{t_{n}}^{-p}\|\theta_{n}-\Psi_{t_{n}}\|^{p}\Bigr]&\leq{\mathcal{K}}^{p}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}R_{t_{n_{\ell-1}}}^{-p}\sup_{n_{\ell-1}\leq n\leq n_{\ell}\wedge\mathfrak{N}}\|\theta_{n}-\Psi_{t_{n}}\|^{p}\Bigr]\\
&\leq{\mathcal{K}}^{p}\,(\rho-\sqrt{\gamma_{\mathfrak{n}+1}})^{-1}\int_{t_{n_{0}}}^{\infty}R_{s}^{-p}\bigl(a+be^{-c(s-t_{n_{0}})})^{p}\,\Gamma_{s}^{(p-1)/2}\,\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

(176)

which implies the second statement of the theorem.
∎

8
Regularity properties and perturbation analysis for the Adam vector field

The Adam vector field as introduced in
Definition

2.4
depends in an intricate way on the innovation
(X,U)
and the damping parameters
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
. In this section, we want to investigate the dominant terms for particular choices of parameters.

In the following, let
(X,U)
be an innovation and let
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
be damping parameters with
0\leq\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}<1
. The related Adam vector field
f\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{(i)}(\theta)&=(1-\alpha)\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[1/\Bigl(\sqrt{(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}X^{(i)}(U_{k},\theta)^{2}}+\epsilon\Bigr)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}X^{(i)}(U_{k},\theta)\Bigr].\end{split}

(177)

Motivated by a Taylor approximation we will introduce a vector field

\tilde{f}=(\tilde{f}^{(1)},\dots,\tilde{f}^{(d)})\colon\allowbreak{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

which we call the
first order approximation
for the Adam field
f
. For this we need some more notation.

We denote by
(U_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
an i.i.d. sequence of copies of
U
and briefly write, for all
k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
X_{k}^{\theta}=X(U_{k},\theta)
and
\mathbf{X}^{\theta}=(X_{k}^{\theta})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. Again we write for
\mathbf{x}=(x_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\in\ell_{\varrho}

\displaystyle\begin{split}V(\mathbf{x})=(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}x_{k}^{2}\end{split}

(178)

and let

\displaystyle\begin{split}h:[0,\infty)\to(0,\epsilon^{-1}],\ x\mapsto\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}+\epsilon}.\end{split}

(179)

In this notation, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{(i)}(\theta)&=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h(V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))\,X^{\theta,(i)}_{k}\bigr].\end{split}

(180)

For
k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{r})_{r\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\in\ell_{\varrho}
, we let

\displaystyle\begin{split}V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{x})=(1-\beta)\sum_{r\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\backslash\{k\}}\beta^{-r}x_{r}^{2},\text{ \ for all $i=1,\dots,d$,}\end{split}

(181)

and we set, for all
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
i=1,\dots,d
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})}+\epsilon}\Bigr]\,{\mathbb{E}}[X^{\theta,(i)}_{0}]\\
&-(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}(\alpha\beta)^{-k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{(\sqrt{V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})}+\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})}}\Bigr]{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[(X^{\theta,(i)}_{0})^{3}\bigr]\end{split}

(182)

provided that the latter two expectations are well-defined and finite. Note that for every
k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, the random variable
V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})
stochastically dominates
V^{\not=0}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})
. Moreover, the distribution of
V^{\not=0}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})
agrees with the one of
\beta V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})
.
Consequently,
\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)
is well-defined and finite, if and only if

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{0}^{\theta,(i)}\in L^{3}\text{ \ and \ }V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})^{-1/2}\in L^{1}.\end{split}

(183)

We call
\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)
as in (
182
)
first order approximation
for the Adam vector field with innovation
(X,U)
and damping parameters
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
provided that it exists.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
(X,U)
be an innovation and
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
damping parameters with
0\leq\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}<1
. Let
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
i=1,\dots,d
and suppose that

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{0}^{\theta,(i)}\in L^{5}\text{ \ and \ }V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})^{-1/2}\in L^{3}.\end{split}

(184)

Then the first order approximation
\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)
for the Adam field with innovation
(X,U)
and damping factors
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
is well-defined in
\theta
and
i
and one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|f^{(i)}(\theta)-\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)|&\leq\frac{3}{8}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)^{2}}{\beta^{5/2}(1-\alpha\beta^{2})}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{(\sqrt{V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})}+\epsilon)^{2}\,V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})^{3/2}}\Bigr]\,{\mathbb{E}}^{\theta}[|X^{\theta,(i)}_{0}|^{5}\bigr].\end{split}

(185)

Remark 8.2
.

Supposing that
184
is satisfied for given
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
we can indeed use the previous theorem to show that
\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)
approximates
f^{(i)}(\theta)
well, when fixing
\alpha
and
\epsilon
and letting
\beta\to 1
. In this setting,
|f^{(i)}(\theta)-\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)|
is of order
(1-\beta)^{2}
and the first term on the right-hand side of (
182
) converges and the second term is of order
1-\beta
. Thus
\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)
consists of the two leading order terms. In particular, the second order term gives insights about the deviation of critical points of the Adam field from critical points of the vector field
\theta\mapsto{\mathbb{E}}[X(U,\theta)]
which is the field related to classical gradient descent.

Proof.

Note that one has that, for all
x\in[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}h^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{(\sqrt{x}+\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}\text{ \ and \ }h^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{x}+\epsilon)^{3}}\frac{1}{2x}+\frac{1}{(\sqrt{x}+\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{1}{4x^{3/2}}.\end{split}

(186)

Observe that one has

\displaystyle\begin{split}(1-\alpha)&\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\bigl(h(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))+h^{\prime}(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}(X_{k}^{\theta,(i)})^{2}\bigr)\,X_{k}^{\theta,(i)}\bigr]\\
&=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})}+\epsilon}\Bigr]\,{\mathbb{E}}[X^{\theta,(i)}_{0}]\\
&\quad-(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}(\alpha\beta)^{-k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{(\sqrt{V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})}+\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})}}\Bigr]{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[(X^{\theta,(i)}_{0})^{3}\bigr]\\
&=\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta),\end{split}

(187)

where we used independence of
V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})
and
X_{k}^{\theta,(i)}
in the previous step.
Using that for all
k\in\-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})=V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})+(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}(X_{k}^{(i)})^{2}
, we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}h&(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))+h^{\prime}(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}(X^{\theta,(i)}_{k})^{2}\leq h(V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))\\
&\leq h(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))+h^{\prime}(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}(X^{\theta,(i)}_{k})^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime\prime}(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))(1-\beta)^{2}\beta^{-2k}(X^{\theta,(i)}_{k})^{4}.\end{split}

(188)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|f^{(i)}(\theta)-\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)|&\leq(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\,\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta)^{2}\beta^{-2k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h^{\prime\prime}(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))|X^{\theta,(i)}_{k}|^{5}\bigr]\\
&=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\,\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta)^{2}\beta^{-2k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h^{\prime\prime}(V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))\bigr]\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|X^{\theta,(i)}_{0}|^{5}\bigr]\end{split}

(189)

Using that
V^{\not=k}(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})
stochastically dominates
\beta\,V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})
, the monotonicity of
h^{\prime\prime}
and the fact that

h^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq\frac{3}{4}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{x}+\epsilon)^{2}x^{3/2}}

we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|f^{(i)}(\theta)-\tilde{f}^{(i)}(\theta)|&\leq(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\,\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta)^{2}\beta^{-2k}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h^{\prime\prime}(\beta V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)}))\bigr]\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|X^{\theta,(i)}_{0}|^{5}\bigr]\\
&\leq\frac{3}{8}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)^{2}}{\beta^{5/2}(1-\alpha\beta^{2})}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{(\sqrt{V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})}+\epsilon)^{2}\,V(\mathbf{X}^{\theta,(i)})^{3/2}}\Bigr]\,{\mathbb{E}}^{\theta}[|X^{\theta,(i)}_{0}|^{5}\bigr].\end{split}

(190)

∎

In the second part of this section we investigate the Adam field when working with mini-batches. The crucial result will be a first order approximation when letting the size of the mini-badges tend to infinity.
For a fixed innovation
(X,U)
be an innovation, we let
{\mathbb{U}}=(U_{r})_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of independent copies of
U
and for every
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we call the innovation
(\mathbb{X}_{M},{\mathbb{U}})
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{X}_{M}(u)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^{M}X(u_{r},\theta)\end{split}

(191)

the
mini-batch innovation of
(X,U)
of size
M
.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
(X,U)
be an innovation and
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)
be damping parameters with
\alpha<\sqrt{\beta}<1
. Let
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
(\mathbb{X}_{M},{\mathbb{U}})
be the mini-batch innovation of
(X,U)
of size
M
. Moreover, let
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[X^{(i)}(U,\theta)]=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathbb{E}}[|h^{\prime}(\mathbb{V}_{M})|]<\infty.\end{split}

(192)

Set for
q\in(0,\infty)
,
\varphi^{(i)}_{q}={\mathbb{E}}[|X^{(i)}(U,\theta)|^{q}]
.
Then for the corresponding Adam vector field
f_{M}
, one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|f_{M}^{(i)}(\theta)|\leq\beta^{-3/2}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}{1-\alpha\beta}\varphi_{3}\,{\mathbb{E}}[|h^{\prime}(\mathbb{V}_{M}^{(i)})|]\,M^{-2}\\
&+\beta^{-5/2}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)^{2}}{1-\alpha\beta^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{V}_{M}^{(i)})](2C_{3}\varphi_{2}^{(i)}\varphi^{(i)}_{3}M^{-5/2}+5\varphi_{2}^{(i)}\varphi_{3}^{(i)}M^{-3}+\varphi_{5}^{(i)}M^{-4}),\end{split}

(193)

where
C_{3}
is the constant in the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality for the third moment.

In our proof of
Theorem

8.3
we will use the following lemma.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
A,B
and
C
be independent real random variables with
A>0
, almost surely, and let
h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\epsilon^{-1})
be as in (
179
).
Provided that
{\mathbb{E}}[|h^{\prime}(A)|]<\infty
one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bigl|{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h(A+(B+C)^{2})C\bigr]-\bigl({\mathbb{E}}[h(A+B^{2})]\,{\mathbb{E}}[C]+{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime}(A)]\bigl(2{\mathbb{E}}[B]\,{\mathbb{E}}[C^{2}]+{\mathbb{E}}[C^{3}]\bigr)\bigr)\bigr|\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(A)]\bigl(2{\mathbb{E}}[|B|^{3}]\,{\mathbb{E}}[C^{2}]+5{\mathbb{E}}[B^{2}]\,{\mathbb{E}}[|C|^{3}]+{\mathbb{E}}[|C|^{5}]\bigr).\end{split}

(194)

In particular, in the case where
{\mathbb{E}}[B]={\mathbb{E}}[C]=0
, one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h&(A+(B+C)^{2})C\bigr]-{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime}(A)]\,{\mathbb{E}}[C^{3}]\bigr|\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(A)]\bigl(2{\mathbb{E}}[|B|^{3}]\,{\mathbb{E}}[C^{2}]+5{\mathbb{E}}[B^{2}]\,{\mathbb{E}}[|C|^{3}]+{\mathbb{E}}[|C|^{5}]\bigr).\end{split}

(195)

Proof.

First note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}h(A+(B+C)^{2})C&=h(A+B^{2}+2BC+C^{2})C\\
&=\bigl(h(A+B^{2})+h^{\prime}(A+B^{2})(2BC+C^{2})+\psi_{1}\bigr)C,\end{split}

(196)

where as consequence of the Taylor formula and the monotonicity of
h^{\prime\prime}
,
|\psi_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime\prime}(A)(2BC+C^{2})^{2}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\begin{split}h^{\prime}(A+B^{2})=h^{\prime}(A)+\psi_{2},\end{split}

(197)

where
|\psi_{2}|\leq h^{\prime\prime}(A)B^{2}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|h&(A+(B+C)^{2})C-\bigl(h(A+B^{2})+h^{\prime}(A)(2BC+C^{2})\bigr)C\bigr|\\
&=|\psi_{1}C+\psi_{2}(2BC+C^{2})C|\leq h^{\prime\prime}(A)\bigl(2|B|^{3}C^{2}+5B^{2}|C|^{3}+|C|^{5}\bigr)\end{split}

(198)

and by independence of
A
,
B
and
C

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h&(A+(B+C)^{2})C\bigr]-{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\bigl(h(A+B^{2})+h^{\prime}(A)(2BC+C^{2})\bigr)C\bigr]\bigr|\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(A)]\bigl(2{\mathbb{E}}[|B|^{3}]\,{\mathbb{E}}[C^{2}]+5{\mathbb{E}}[B^{2}]\,{\mathbb{E}}[|C|^{3}]+{\mathbb{E}}[|C|^{5}]\bigr).\end{split}

(199)

The statement follows immediately by using the independence of
A
,
B
and
C
.
∎

Using
Lemma

8.4
we now present
the proof of
Theorem

8.3
.

Proof.

For the proof we can assume without loss of generality that
d=1
.
We denote by
({\mathbb{U}}(k))_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
a sequence of independent random variables each one being identically distributed as
{\mathbb{U}}
and we
set for
k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{k,r}=X({\mathbb{U}}_{r}(k),\theta)\text{ \ and \ }\mathbb{V}_{M}=(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}\Bigl(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^{M}X_{k,r}\Bigr)^{2}.\end{split}

(200)

Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{M}(\theta)&=(1-\alpha)\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[h(\mathbb{V}_{M})\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^{M}X_{k,r}\Bigr]\\
&=(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\sum_{r=1}^{M}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h(\mathbb{V}_{M})\mbox{$\frac{1}{M}$}X_{k,r}\bigr].\end{split}

(201)

We let

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{k,r}=(1-\beta)\sum_{\ell\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\backslash\{k\}}\beta^{-\ell}\Bigl(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{s=1}^{M}X_{\ell,s}\Bigr)^{2},\ B_{k,r}=\sqrt{(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{s\in\{1,\dots,M\}\backslash\{r\}}^{M}X_{k,s}\end{split}

(202)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{k,r}=\sqrt{(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}}\frac{1}{M}X_{k,r}\end{split}

(203)

and observe that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sqrt{(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}}\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h(\mathbb{V}_{M})\mbox{$\frac{1}{M}$}X_{k,r}\bigr]={\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h(A_{k,r}+(B_{k,r}+C_{k,r})^{2})C_{k,r}\bigr].\end{split}

(204)

For every choice of
k
and
r
, the random variables
A_{k,r}
,
B_{k,r}
and
C_{k,r}
are independent and
B_{k,r}
and
C_{k,r}
have mean zero. Therefore,
we can apply Lemma
8.4
and get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[&h(A_{k,r}+(B_{k,r}+C_{k,r})^{2})C_{k,r}\bigr]-{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h^{\prime}(A_{k,r})\,C_{k,r}^{3}\bigr]\bigr|\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(A_{k,r})]\bigl(2{\mathbb{E}}[|B_{k,r}|^{3}]\,{\mathbb{E}}[C_{k,r}^{2}]+5{\mathbb{E}}[B_{k,r}^{2}]\,{\mathbb{E}}[|C_{k,r}|^{3}]+{\mathbb{E}}[|C_{k,r}|^{5}]\bigr)\end{split}

(205)

Note that
A_{k,r}
stochastically dominates
\beta\mathbb{V}_{M}
so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[|h^{\prime}(A_{k,r})|]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[|h^{\prime}(\beta\mathbb{V}_{M})|]\text{ \ and \ }{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(A_{k,r})]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{V}_{M})].\end{split}

(206)

Moreover, for every
q\in\{2,3\}
one has

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[|B_{k,r}|^{q}]&=((1-\beta)\beta^{-k})^{q/2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{s\in\{1,\dots,M\}\backslash\{r\}}X_{0,s}\Bigr|^{q}\Bigr]\\
&\leq((1-\beta)\beta^{-k})^{q/2}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{s=1}^{M}X_{0,s}\Bigr|^{q}\Bigr]\\
&\leq((1-\beta)\beta^{-k})^{q/2}\,C_{q}\,{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\Bigl|\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{s=1}^{M}X_{0,s}^{2}\Bigr|^{q/2}\Bigr]\\
&\leq((1-\beta)\beta^{-k})^{q/2}\,C_{q}\,\frac{1}{M^{q/2}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|X_{0,1}|^{q}\bigr],\end{split}

(207)

where
C_{2}=1
and
C_{3}
is the constant appearing in the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality when applied for the moment

.
Next, observe that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[|C_{k,r}|^{q}]&=((1-\beta)\beta^{-k})^{q/2}\,\frac{1}{M^{q}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[|X_{0,1}|^{q}\bigr].\end{split}

(208)

Combining the previous three estimates with (
205
) we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[&h(A_{k,r}+(B_{k,r}+C_{k,r})^{2})C_{k,r}\bigr]\big|\\
&\leq{\mathbb{E}}[|h^{\prime}(\beta\mathbb{V}_{M})|]\,((1-\beta)\beta^{-k})^{3/2}\,\varphi_{3}M^{-3}\\
&\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(\beta\mathbb{V}_{M})]((1-\beta)\beta^{-k})^{5/2}(2\kappa_{3}\varphi_{2}\varphi_{3}M^{-7/2}+5\varphi_{2}\varphi_{3}M^{-4}+\varphi_{5}M^{-5}).\end{split}

(209)

With (
201
) and (
204
) we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|f_{M}(\theta)|&\leq(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\sqrt{(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}}^{-1}M\,{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[h(\mathbb{V}_{M})\mbox{$\frac{1}{M}$}X_{k,1}\bigr]\\
&\leq(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}(1-\beta)\beta^{-k}\varphi_{3}\,{\mathbb{E}}[|h^{\prime}(\beta\mathbb{V}_{M})|]\,M^{-2}\\
&\qquad+(1-\alpha)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}((1-\beta)\beta^{-k})^{2}\,{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(\beta\mathbb{V}_{M})]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(2C_{3}\varphi_{2}\varphi_{3}M^{-5/2}+5\varphi_{2}\varphi_{3}M^{-3}+\varphi_{5}M^{-4})\\
&\leq\beta^{-3/2}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)}{1-\alpha\beta}\varphi_{3}\,{\mathbb{E}}[|h^{\prime}(\mathbb{V}_{M})|]\,M^{-2}\\
&\qquad+\beta^{-5/2}\frac{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)^{2}}{1-\alpha\beta^{2}}{\mathbb{E}}[h^{\prime\prime}(\mathbb{V}_{M})](2C_{3}\varphi_{2}\varphi_{3}M^{-5/2}+5\varphi_{2}\varphi_{3}M^{-3}+\varphi_{5}M^{-4}),\end{split}

(210)

where we have used in the las step that for

and

.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let
\beta,\delta,p,q\in(0,\infty)
with
q<\beta^{p}
and
\beta<1
and let
\mathcal{Z}=(Z_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be a sequence of independent real-valued random variables satisfying for every
k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{k}^{2}<\delta)\leq q.\end{split}

(211)

One has

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{v(\mathcal{Z})^{p}}\Bigr]\leq\Bigl(\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}\Bigr)^{p}\frac{1-q}{\beta^{p}-q}\,\delta^{-p},\end{split}

(212)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}v:{\mathbb{R}}^{-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\backslash\{0\}\to[0,\infty),\ \mathbf{x}\mapsto(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}x_{k}^{2}.\end{split}

(213)

Proof.

As consequence of the assumptions on
\mathcal{Z}
one can couple
\mathcal{Z}
with a sequence of independent Bernoulli distributed random variables
(I_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
with success probability
1-q
such that for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}1\hskip-2.78857pt\mathrm{l}_{\{Z_{-k}^{2}\geq\delta\}}\geq I_{k}.\end{split}

(214)

We consider the geometrically distributed stopping time

\displaystyle\begin{split}T=\inf\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:I_{k}=1\}.\end{split}

(215)

One has that

v(\mathcal{Z})\geq(1-\beta)\beta^{T}\delta

so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathbb{E}}[v(\mathcal{Z})^{-p}]\leq((1-\beta)\delta)^{-p}\,{\mathbb{E}}[\beta^{-pT}]=((1-\beta)\delta)^{-p}\,\frac{1-q}{1-\beta^{-p}q}.\end{split}

(216)

∎

Proof of the theorem in the introduction (
Theorem

1.1
in

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We fix
d=1
and denote by
U
a uniformly bounded random variable. For every
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
(\mathbb{X}_{M},{\mathbb{U}})
be the innovation with
\mathbb{X}_{M}\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{\infty}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

\mathbb{X}_{M}(u,\theta)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}u^{(i)}-\theta

(217)

and
{\mathbb{U}}=(U^{(i)})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
being a sequence of independent
U
-distributed random variables.
Let
(\theta^{[M]}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
be an Adam algorithm with innovation
(\mathbb{X}_{M},{\mathbb{U}})
and denote by
({\mathbb{U}}_{k})_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
a sequence of independent copies of
{\mathbb{U}}
driving the Adam algorithm. We can represent the
related Adam vector field
f_{M}\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{M}(\theta)=(1-\alpha){\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\epsilon+\sqrt{(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}\mathbb{X}_{M}({\mathbb{U}}_{k},\theta)^{2}}}\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\mathbb{X}_{M}({\mathbb{U}}_{k},\theta)\Bigr].\end{split}

(218)

For
\rho\in[0,1)
and
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{M}_{M}^{\rho}(\theta)=(1-\rho)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\rho^{-k}\mathbb{X}_{M}({\mathbb{U}}_{k},\theta)\text{ \ and \ }\mathbb{V}_{M}(\theta)=(1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}\mathbb{X}_{M}({\mathbb{U}}_{k},\theta)^{2}.\end{split}

(219)

Provided that we can change differentiation and integration we get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{\prime}_{M}(\theta)=-{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\frac{1}{\epsilon+\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}({\theta})}}\Bigr]+{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl[\mathbb{M}^{\alpha}_{M}({\theta})\mathbb{M}^{\beta}_{M}({\theta})\frac{1}{(\epsilon+\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}(\theta)})^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}({\theta})}}\Bigr].\end{split}

(220)

As we will verify below (see (
225
)) the term in both expectations is uniformly bounded and continuous which justifies the latter identity.

We first verify that on an arbitrary compact set the vector field
f_{M}
is decreasing as long as
M
is sufficiently large.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact set. There exists
M_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup\{f^{\prime}_{M}(\theta):M\geq M_{0},\theta\in V\}<0.\end{split}

(221)

We thus showed that for
M\geq M_{0}
and the chosen
V
one has that for every
x,y\in V
with
x\geq y
, one has

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle f_{M}(x)-f_{M}(y),x-y\rangle=\int_{y}^{x}f_{M}^{\prime}(r)(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}r\leq-c_{1}(x-y)^{2}\end{split}

(222)

which implies monotonicity of the Adam vector field.

Proof.

We show that the integrands in the expectations of (
220
) are uniformly bounded.
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one has that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\mathbb{M}_{M}^{\alpha}(\theta)|&=(1-\alpha)\Bigl|\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-k}\beta^{k/2}\beta^{-k/2}\mathbb{X}_{M}({\mathbb{U}}_{k},\theta)\Bigr|\\
&\leq(1-\alpha)\Bigl(\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\alpha^{-2k}\beta^{k}\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}\mathbb{X}_{M}({\mathbb{U}}_{k},\theta)^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}\\
&=\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}\sqrt{1-\beta}}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}(\theta)}\end{split}

(223)

and by Jensen’s inequality that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl|\mathbb{M}_{M}^{\beta}(\theta)\bigr|\leq\Bigl((1-\beta)\sum_{k\in-{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\beta^{-k}\mathbb{X}_{M}({\mathbb{U}}_{k},\theta)^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}=\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}(\theta)}.\end{split}

(224)

This implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Bigl|\mathbb{M}^{\alpha}_{M}({\theta})\mathbb{M}^{\beta}_{M}({\theta})\frac{1}{(\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}({\theta})}+\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}({\theta})}}\Bigr|\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}/\beta}\sqrt{1-\beta}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}(\theta)}+\epsilon}.\end{split}

(225)

Next, take a convergent series
(\vartheta_{M})_{M\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and denote by
\vartheta
its limit. Then for
\rho\in\{\alpha,\beta\}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathbb{M}^{\rho}_{M}(\vartheta_{M})={\mathbb{E}}[U]-\vartheta,\text{ \ in probability,}\end{split}

(226)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathbb{V}_{M}(\vartheta_{M})=({\mathbb{E}}[U]-\vartheta)^{2},\text{ \ in probability.}\end{split}

(227)

This entails that, in probability,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}(\vartheta_{M})}+\epsilon}=\frac{1}{|{\mathbb{E}}[U]-\vartheta|+\epsilon}\end{split}

(228)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{M\to\infty}\mathbb{M}^{\alpha}_{M}({\vartheta_{M}})\mathbb{M}^{\beta}_{M}({\vartheta_{M}})\frac{1}{(\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}({\vartheta_{M}})}+\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathbb{V}_{M}({\vartheta_{M}})}}=\frac{|{\mathbb{E}}[U]-\vartheta|}{(|{\mathbb{E}}[U]-\vartheta|+\epsilon)^{2}}.\end{split}

(229)

With dominated convergence, we thus get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{M\to\infty}f^{\prime}_{M}(\vartheta_{M})=-\frac{1}{|{\mathbb{E}}[U]-\vartheta|+\epsilon}\Bigl(1-\frac{|{\mathbb{E}}[U]-\vartheta|}{|{\mathbb{E}}[U]-\vartheta|+\epsilon}\Bigr)<0.\end{split}

(230)

We prove the statement by contradiction. If the statement would not be true for every choice of
M_{0}

there would exist a sequence
(M_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of integers diverging to infinity and a
V
-valued sequence
(\vartheta_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\limsup_{k\to\infty}f^{\prime}_{M_{k}}(\vartheta_{k})\geq 0.\end{split}

(231)

By compactness of
V
, we can assume that
(\vartheta_{k})
is convergent with a limit
\theta\in V
(if the sequence is not convergent, we can instead work with a convergent subsequence). This contradicts our computations above, see (
230
).
∎

2.6 ​ M ⊙ 2.6M_{\odot} Compact Object and Neutron Stars within Eddington-Inspired Born-Infeld Theory of Gravity
I. Prasetyo, H. Maulana, H. S. Ramadhan, A. Sulaksono

Abstract
In the context of whether a massive compact object recently observed in the GW190814 event is a neutron star (NS) or not, we have studied the role of the parameters κ \kappa and Λ c \Lambda_{c} of the Eddington-inspired Born-Infeld (EiBI) gravity theory on the NS mass-radius relation, moment of inertia, and tidal deformability. The results are compared to recent observation constraints extracted from the analysis of NS observation data. The NS core equation of state (EoS) is calculated using the relativistic mean-field model with the G3 parameter set. In the hyperon sector, the SU(3) and hyperon potential depths are used to determine the hyperon coupling constants. For the inner and outer crusts, we use the crust EoS from Miyatsu et al. (2013). We also maintain the sound speed to not exceed c c / 3 \sqrt{3} at high densities. We have found that, in general, the NS mass significantly depends on the value of κ \kappa , and the radius R R is sensitive to the value of Λ c \Lambda_{c} . Moreover, as Λ c \Lambda_{c} is equal to zero or less than the accepted bound of the cosmological constant, the NS within the EiBI theory is compatible with observation constraints, including 2.0 ​ M ⊙ 2.0M_{\odot} mass, canonical radius R 1.4 ​ M ⊙ R_{1.4M_{\odot}} , moment of inertia, and tidal deformation. Our investigation also reveals that the 2.6 ​ M ⊙ 2.6M_{\odot} mass compact object and current observational constraint of canonical radius R 1.4 ​ M ⊙ R_{1.4M_{\odot}} can simultaneously be satisfied only when the Λ c \Lambda_{c} value is unphysically too large and negative. Therefore, within the spesific EoS employed in this work, we conclude that the secondary object with 2.6 ​ M ⊙ 2.6M_{\odot} observed in the GW190814 event Abbott:2020khf is not likely a static (or a slow-rotating) NS within the EiBI gravity theory.

S={1\over 8\pi G\kappa}\int_{(\mathcal{M},\bm{g})}d^{4}x~\left[\sqrt{-\det(g_{\mu\nu}+\kappa R_{\mu\nu}(\Gamma))}-\lambda\sqrt{-\det(g_{\mu\nu})}\right],

where the Palatini formalism has been used, i.e., the Ricci tensor is dependent not on the physical metric but on the connection

\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}={1\over 2}q^{\alpha\sigma}\left(\partial_{\gamma}q_{\beta\sigma}+\partial_{\beta}q_{\gamma\sigma}-\partial_{\sigma}q_{\beta\gamma}\right).

(10)

To make them similar to the Einstein field equation (EFE), we can manipulate them into

\displaystyle R^{\mu}_{\nu}(\bm{q})-\frac{1}{2}R^{\sigma}_{\sigma}\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}(\bm{q})=8\pi GT^{\mu}_{\mathrm{eff}~\nu}(\bm{g}),

(11)

\displaystyle T^{\mu}_{\mathrm{eff}~\nu}(\bm{g})=\tau T^{\mu}_{\nu}(\bm{g})-\left[\frac{\tau T^{\sigma}_{\sigma}(\bm{g})}{2}+\frac{1-\tau\lambda}{8\pi G\kappa}\right]\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}.

(12)

For brevity, we shall not write the arguments of the Ricci tensor
R_{\mu\nu}
and stress tensor
T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle C_{1,l}

\displaystyle=

The constants also have the following pattern:

\lambda_{l}
and get rid of
\mathcal{E}_{m}

and

into

\lambda_{l}=-\frac{(A_{1,l}+\varepsilon A_{3,l})Q^{*}(R)+(A_{2,l}+\varepsilon A_{4,l})P^{*}(R)+\varepsilon\left(A_{1,l}T^{*}(R)+A_{2,l}S^{*}(R)\right)}{(B_{1,l}+\varepsilon B_{3,l})Q^{*}(R)+(B_{2,l}+\varepsilon B_{4,l})P^{*}(R)+\varepsilon\left(B_{1,l}T^{*}(R)+B_{2,l}S^{*}(R)\right)},

(98)

where

\displaystyle Q^{*}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yQ^{2}_{l}(c)+c[d{Q^{2}_{l}}(c)/dc],

(99)

\displaystyle P^{*}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yP^{2}_{l}(c)+c[d{P^{2}_{l}}(c)/dc],

(100)

\displaystyle T^{*}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yT^{2}_{l}(c)+c[d{T^{2}_{l}}(c)/dc],

(101)

\displaystyle S^{*}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yS^{2}_{l}(c)+c[d{S^{2}_{l}}(c)/dc].

(102)

Then, we can obtain the Love number through

k_{l}={(2l-1)!!\over 2R^{2l+1}}\lambda_{l}.

(103)

Notice that because
\lambda_{l}\propto a_{i,l}/b_{i,l}\propto(GM)^{2l+1}

\lambda_{l}R^{-2l-1}\propto c^{2l+1}
. Thus, we can redefine

III.3.2
Non-vacuum case

From Eq. (
104
), we need
y(R),M=m(R),
and
r=R
as inputs to obtain the Love number. Thus, we need to run the calculation of
F(r)
in the interior.

From Eq.
6
, we obtain the following metric relations:

\displaystyle d=r^{2}/(ab),~e^{\nu}=e^{\beta}a/b^{3},~e^{\bar{\lambda}}=e^{\alpha}/(ab),~

(117)

\displaystyle H_{0}=H,~H_{2}=-Ha^{2}/b^{2},~F_{0}=-F_{2}=F.

(118)

We also need to define the perturbed stress tensor as

T^{\mu}_{\nu}=T^{\mu}_{0~~\nu}+\delta T^{\mu}_{\nu},

(119)

with
T^{\mu}_{0~~\nu}
as the usual ideal fluid and

\delta T^{\mu}_{\nu}=\text{diag}\left(-{d\epsilon\over dp},1,1,1\right)\delta p~Y_{lm}(\theta,\phi).

(120)

After evaluating Eq. (
7
) order by order and using identities of the spherical harmonics, one can arrive at the following relations:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{16\pi G\kappa\delta p\over b^{2}-a^{2}},

(121)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bar{G}\over ab}+{4\pi G\kappa\over ab}\left({1\over b^{2}}-{1\over a^{2}}{d\epsilon\over dp}\right)\delta p,

(122)

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi G\kappa\left({4\over b^{2}-a^{2}}-{3\over b^{2}}-{1\over a^{2}}{d\epsilon\over dp}\right)\delta p

(123)

Then, by eliminating
\delta p
, we have the following equations:

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{4\over a^{2}-b^{2}}\left({4\over a^{2}-b^{2}}+{3\over b^{2}}+{1\over a^{2}}{d\epsilon\over dp}\right)^{-1}F,

(124)

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ce^{(\alpha-\beta)/2},

(125)

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{1}\left({a\over b^{3}}-{l(l+1)\kappa\over 2r^{2}}\right).

(126)

Lastly, we have

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle e^{\alpha}\left({2\over r}+{r\over 2\kappa}\left(-4+{a\over b^{3}}+{3\over ab}\right)\right)F^{\prime}-\left({2\over r^{2}}-{e^{\alpha}\over\kappa}\left(2-{3a\over b^{3}}+{1\over ab}\right)\right)F

(127)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle e^{\alpha}\left({(l-1)(l+2)\over r^{2}}-{2\over ab\kappa}\right)G+K\left({2e^{\alpha}\over\kappa}\right)=0.

After some algebraic manipulations, the equation becomes

F^{\prime\prime}+f(r)F^{\prime}+g(r)F=0,

(128)

with

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{re^{\alpha(r)}}{\kappa}\left(\frac{1}{ab}-1\right)+\frac{e^{\alpha(r)}}{r}+\frac{1}{r},

(129)

\displaystyle g(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{\alpha}}{\kappa}{a\over b^{3}}\left(2-\frac{4}{\left(a^{2}-b^{2}\right)}\left(\frac{4}{a^{2}-b^{2}}+\frac{1}{a^{2}}{d\epsilon\over dp}+\frac{3}{b^{2}}\right)^{-1}\right)

(130)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{l(l+1)e^{\alpha}}{r^{2}}+\frac{2e^{\alpha}}{\kappa}+\beta^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right).

Defining

y^{\prime}(r)=-f(r)y(r)-rg(r)-\frac{y(r)^{2}}{r}+\frac{y(r)}{r}.

(131)

The boundary condition is
y(0)=l
.
Usually,
k_{2}
(using Eq. (
104

\Lambda

\Lambda={2k_{2}\over 3c^{5}},

(132)

because

.

The numerical procedure for the tidal calculation is as follows: First, we calculate all

, and

(Eqs. (
44
), (
42
) and (

code. The initial data at the center

are

,

and

where the pressure becomes zero

. At this point, we obtain

, and

. The three numbers are then used to calculate

using Eq. (
98
).

In this section, we show the numerical results. The EoS used here is the G3 parameter set. We include the hyperon contribution in the EoS with the speed of sound at high densities constrained by

i.e.,
M\gtrsim 2.0M_{\odot}

because it stiffens the matter and thus increases the maximum mass. This effect can slightly reduce the impact of hyperon contributions. The hyperon contribution softens the EoS quite significantly, thus decreasing the maximum mass. Furthermore, the known
constraints from some analysis
results are shown as a comparison to restrict the range of
\kappa
and
\Lambda_{c}

V
CONCLUSIONS

In conclusion, motivated by the assumption that the secondary compact object with
2.6M_{\odot}
observed in the GW190814 event could be an NS, we have systematically investigated the role of parameters
\kappa
and
\Lambda_{c}
of the EiBI gravity theory on the NS mass-radius relation, moment of inertia, and tidal deformability in the slow-rotating limit. The EoS of the core of an NS is calculated using the RMF model with the G3 parameter set
Kumara:2017bti
, where the SU(3) prescription and hyperon potential depths
Tolos:2017
are used to determine the hyperon coupling constants. For the inner and outer crusts, we use the crust EoS obtained from Miyatsu
et al.

MYN2013
. We also ensured that the speed of sound in the matter does not exceed
c

Introduction

In this paper we consider a boundary value problem for the
p
-Laplacian in a domain obtained from
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by perforating it
(i.e., removing small cavities) along the unit sphere. The problem is formulated as follows.
Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
S=S(\varepsilon)
be a finite set of points on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
the Euclidean distance between any two points in
S
is at least
\varepsilon.
Points in
S
will be referred to as
anchors
.

Figure 1:

Two examples of sets
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
that are unions of small cavities anchored to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}
:
congruent cones (left) and spheres, cubes and cylinders of the same
p
-capacity (right).

For each anchor
s
, let
K_{s}
be a compact subset of the closed unit ball
\bar{B}(0,1)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
0<\alpha=\alpha(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{1}{80}
satisfy
\alpha(\varepsilon)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Define

\displaystyle\Gamma=\Gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\underset{s\in S}{\bigcup}(s+\alpha\varepsilon K_{s})\,.

(1.1)

Two examples of such sets are depicted in Figure
1
.

Assume that
1<p<d
and let
u=u^{\varepsilon}
be the Perron solution of the boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{p}u={\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0&\mbox{in}&\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Gamma,\\
u=1&\mbox{on}&\Gamma,\\
u(x)\to 0&\mbox{as}&|{x}|\to\infty.\end{array}\right.

(We recall the definition of Perron solutions in Section 2.) We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of
u^{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
under the following two

key hypotheses:

(
{\bf H_{1}}
)

The anchors are asymptotically equidistributed, that is

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{d-1}\sum_{s\in S(\varepsilon)}\delta_{s}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\sigma\mu\>~\mbox{as}~\varepsilon\to 0,

(1.6)

where
\mu
is the uniform probability measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

and
\sigma>0
.

(
{\bf H_{2}}
)

All the sets
K_{s}
have the same
{\mathbf{p}}
-capacity
, i.e., there is some compact set
K\subseteq\bar{B}(0,1)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

such that
{\rm cap}_{p}(K_{s})={\rm cap}_{p}(K)>0
for all
\varepsilon>0
and
s\in S(\varepsilon)
, where

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(K):=\inf\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla\psi|^{p}:~\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),~\psi\geq 1~\mbox{on}~K\right\},

(1.7)

see
[
8
, Chapter 2]
. A special case to keep in mind is when all the
K_{s}
are rotated copies of
K
, as in the left part of Figure
1
.

The Perron solution
u
of (
1
) is related to the
p
-capacity of
\Gamma.

Specifically, the following identity holds
[
8
, Theorems 9.33 and 9.35]
:

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{p}.

(1.8)

In this context,
u
is called the
p
-equilibrium potential of
\Gamma.

In previous works in similar setups
[
9
,
10
,
6
]
(summarized at the end of this section), asymptotics of
u^{\varepsilon}
in the Sobolev space
H^{1,p}
were studied. The methods of the present paper enable us to obtain precise
L^{\infty}
asymptotics.

To motivate our results, note that
since the
p
-capacity is sub-additive
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
, we have:

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma)\leq\underset{s\in S}{\sum}{\rm cap}_{p}\bigl(s+\alpha\varepsilon K_{s}\bigr)=|S|(\alpha\varepsilon)^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)=\frac{(\alpha\varepsilon)^{d-p}}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}(\varepsilon^{d-1}|S|){\rm cap}_{p}(K)\,,

(1.9)

where we used the easily checked scaling relation

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(aK)=a^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K),\qquad\forall a>0.

(1.10)

Taking into account that
\varepsilon^{d-1}|S|\to\sigma
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma)\leq C\left(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha_{c}}\right)^{d-p}\,,

(1.11)

where
C
is some constant independent of
\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\alpha_{c}:=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\,,

(1.12)

and

\displaystyle\ \gamma:=\frac{d-p}{p-1}\,.

(1.13)

On the other hand, since
p
-capacity is monotone and
\Gamma\subset\bar{B}(0,1+\varepsilon)
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma)\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1)).

(1.14)

Thus, when the limiting value (as
\varepsilon\to 0
) of
\frac{\alpha}{\alpha_{c}}
increases from
0
to
\infty
, the natural upper bounds (
1.11
) and (
1.14
)
for
{\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma)
cross each other. It turns out that in the same regime, the
solution
u
of (
1
) (outside of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
)
gradually transitions from
0
to the
p
-equilibrium potential
U
of the unit ball, defined as the solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{p}U=0&\mbox{in}&\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\bar{B}(0,1),\\
U=1&\mbox{on}&\bar{B}(0,1),\\
U(x)\to 0&\mbox{as}&|x|\to\infty\,,\end{array}\right.

and given by

\displaystyle U(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&0\leq|x|\leq 1\,,\\
|x|^{-\gamma}&|x|>1\,.\end{array}\right.

The discussion above motivates our third hypothesis:

(
{\bf H_{3}}
)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\alpha(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{-1/\gamma}=\tau\in[0,\infty]
.

Most of the paper will be devoted to the analysis of the critical window, where
0<\tau<\infty
.
Our first result describes the asymptotics of the capacity
{\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})
and of the potential
u=u^{\varepsilon}
away from the unit sphere.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that hypotheses
{\rm(H_{1}),(H_{2}),(H_{3})}
hold.
Then, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(x)\to\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}0&\mbox{if}&\tau=0\,,\\
A_{*}U(x)&\mbox{if}&\ \tau\in(0,\infty)\,,\\
U(x)&\mbox{if}&\tau=\infty\end{array}\right.

uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

where for
\tau\in[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle A_{*}=A_{*}(\tau)=\frac{\left(\sigma\tau^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\left(\sigma\tau^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\left({\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\,.

(1.26)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon})\to\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}0&\mbox{if}&\tau=0\,,\\
A_{*}^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))+(1-A_{*})^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\sigma\tau^{d-p}&\mbox{if}&\ \tau\in(0,\infty)\,,\\
{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))&\mbox{if}&\tau=\infty\,.\end{array}\right.

It will be convenient to define
A_{*}=1
if
\tau=\infty
. If we assume that
\alpha=C\varepsilon^{\zeta}
for
\zeta>0
, then this theorem reveals a phase transition when the exponent
\zeta
crosses
1/\gamma
.

Example
. To illustrate the Theorem, consider the simple special case where

(so

and
\alpha_{c}=\varepsilon
) and

for all

.
Suppose that
\alpha=\tau\alpha_{c}
.
In this case,

and

can be interpreted as the probability that a Brownian motion started from

The expression for

simplifies to

For
\tau\in(0,\infty)
, the expression (
1.1
) suggests that

should approach
A_{*}^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))
as

.
This, however, contradicts the actual limiting value of the capacity given by (

). Indeed, the limiting equilibrium potential only captures the first summand in (

),
which accounts for the contribution from the bulk. The second term in (

), which accounts for the contribution of

The preceding observation raises the question: How does the solution of (
1
) behave near the unit sphere?
To answer this question, we will introduce the
p
-potential of
K_{s}
in a ball

, namely, the Perron solution

of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{p}V_{R}^{s}=0&\mbox{in}&B(0,R)\setminus K_{s},\\
V_{R}^{s}=1&\mbox{on}&K_{s},\\

We also define

by replacing the last requirement in (

as

. With

) and

, we define, for each

, the ansatz function

\displaystyle u_{A}(x):=AU_{1+\varepsilon}(x)+(1-A)\sum_{s\in S}V_{\frac{1}{10\alpha}}^{s}\left(\frac{x-s}{\alpha\varepsilon}\right)\,.

(1.35)

For

, the convergence

approximates

and

.

.

Suppose that hypotheses
{\rm(H_{1}),(H_{2}),(H_{3})}

\displaystyle\|\nabla u-\nabla u_{A_{*}}\|_{{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\to 0,

Remark 1.1
.

Several technical challenges arise because there are no regularity assumptions on the compact sets
K_{s}
. First, the solution
u
of (

) depends monotonically on

only if

are star-shaped. Moreover, in general

There is an extensive literature devoted to the

-Laplacian and nonlinear potential theory, see, e.g., the books

Most works on this topic focus

]

and references therein. In the papers
[

, which are most closely related to our study, the authors considered homogenization

[

]
the authors assume that

and the cavities are obtained by intersecting a periodic structure with a hyperplane; the critical scaling

, the hyperplane is replaced by a convex surface and

. In

-neighborhood of a smooth surface. The latter paper also contains a detailed review of earlier literature.

p
-capacity of the obstacle, and the limiting behavior of the solution

Roadmap.
While it is not so hard to show that the solution
u^{\varepsilon}
is generally well-behaved away from the cavities
s+\alpha\varepsilon K_{s}
, with rare regions of high local energy, the main obstacle to obtaining our results (especially the
L^{\infty}
estimates in Theorem
1.2
), is ruling out such exceptional regions.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents some necessary background and preliminaries on
p
-potentials. In Section
3
, we bound the oscillation of
u^{\varepsilon}
in cones and, using the Besicovich covering lemma, obtain a lower bound for
its energy in cones. In Section
4
, we combine this lower bound with the global energy minimization property of
u^{\varepsilon}
,
to infer a tight upper bound for its energy in cones; we then use this to deduce that
u^{\varepsilon}
is approximately constant on
\partial B(0,1+\delta)
(where
\varepsilon\ll\delta\ll 1
), and prove Theorem
1.1
. Section
5
shows that if
u^{\varepsilon}
is approximately constant on the boundary of a ball
\partial B(0,1+\delta)
, then it is also approximately constant inside this ball, with the exception of small balls
B(s,\varepsilon/10)
around the anchors.
The comparison argument in that section is purely analytic, but is motivated by the connection with noisy tug of war games described in
[
15
]
. In the final section, we combine the results from the preceding sections and derive Theorem
1.2
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

Given an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
H^{1,p}(\Omega)
denote the Sobolev space of
L^{p}(\Omega)
functions with weak gradient in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, and let

H^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
denote the closure in
H^{1,p}(\Omega)
of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Write
H_{loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
for the space of functions on
\Omega
which, when restricted to every subdomain
\Omega_{1}
compactly contained in
\Omega
, are in
H^{1,p}(\Omega_{1})
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
8
, Chapters 6,7]

Given an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a continuous function
w\in H_{loc}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is called
p
-
harmonic
if
\Delta_{p}w=0
weakly, i.e., if

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{p-2}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\eta=0\,,\quad\forall\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\,.

A function
\psi:\Omega\to(-\infty,\infty]
is
p
-
superharmonic
if it is lower semi-continuous, not identically
\infty
on any connected component of
\Omega
,
and satisfies the following comparison inequality on compactly contained subdomains
D\subset\Omega
: If
\psi\geq w
on
\partial D
and
w\in C(\bar{D})
is
p
-harmonic in
D
, then
\psi\geq w
in
D
.

The following principle, a special case of
[
8
, Proposition 7.6]
, will be used several times.

Lemma 2.1

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
v,w
be bounded
p
-harmonic
functions in an open set
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
If

\limsup_{x\to y}v(x)\leq\liminf_{x\to y}w(x)

for all
y\in\partial\Omega

(including
y=\infty
if
\Omega
is unbounded)
then
v\leq w
in
\Omega
.

Next we give the definition of
Perron solutions
from
[
8
, Chapter 9]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and boundary values
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
, where
\partial\Omega\subset M\subset\Omega^{c}
,
the upper class
U_{f}^{\Omega}
of
f
consists of
p
-superharmonic functions
\psi:\Omega\to(-\infty,\infty]
that are bounded below and satisfy

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to y}\psi(x)\geq f(y),\quad\forall y\in\partial\Omega.

(2.1)

(Recall that if
\Omega
is unbounded, then we include
\infty
in
\partial\Omega
.)
The upper Perron solution

h=\bar{H}_{f}^{\Omega}:\Omega\cup M\to[-\infty,\infty]

of the boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{p}h=0&\mbox{in}&\Omega\,,\\
h(y)=f(y)&\mbox{for}&y\in M\\
\end{array}\right.

is defined in
\Omega
by

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{f}^{\Omega}(x):=\inf\{\psi(x)\,:\,\psi\in U_{f}^{\Omega}\}

(2.5)

and extended to agree with
f
in
M
. The lower Perron solution of (
2.2
) is defined by
\underline{H}_{f}^{\Omega}:=-\bar{H}_{-f}^{\Omega}
.

We say that
f
is resolutive in
\Omega
if the upper and lower Perron solutions of (
2.2
) coincide; in this case we refer to both of these simply as the Perron solution.

Theorem 9.25 in
[
8
]
ensures that if
f
is continuous on
\partial\Omega
and

(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega)
contains a compact set of positive
p
-capacity, then
f
is resolutive.

Following
[
8
]
, we define the Dirichlet space

\displaystyle L^{1,p}(\Omega):=\{v\in H_{loc}^{1,p}(\Omega):~\nabla v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\},

(2.6)

and let
L_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
L^{1,p}(\Omega)
with respect to the semi-norm

\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, see
[
8
, Chapter 1.9, p. 13]
.

Definition 2.3
.

For a function
v\in L^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we define its energy in a Borel set
B\subset\Omega
by

\displaystyle E(v,B)=\int_{B}|\nabla v|^{p}\,.

(2.7)

We write
E(v,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
simply as
E(v)
.

We now define the
p
-capacity (with respect to a domain
\Omega
) of a compact set
K\subset\Omega
by

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(K,\Omega):=\inf\{E(\psi,\Omega),~\psi\geq 1~\mbox{on}~K,~\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\}.

(2.8)

This definition readily implies that

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(K,B(0,R))\downarrow{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\;{\rm as}\;R\uparrow\infty\,,

(2.9)

where

{\rm cap}_{p}(K)={\rm cap}_{p}(K,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
In
[
8
, p. 27-28]
it is shown that

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(K,\Omega)=\inf\{E(\psi,\Omega),~\psi\geq 1~\mbox{on}~K,~\psi\in H_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)\}.

(2.10)

Fix
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\phi=1
on
K
, and define the admissible class

\displaystyle{\mathscr{A}}(K,\Omega,\phi):=\{\psi\in L_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)~\mbox{\rm such that}~\psi-\phi\in L_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega\setminus K)\}\,.

(2.11)

Then we claim that

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(K,\Omega)\leq E(\psi,\Omega)\;\;\mbox{\rm for}\;\psi\in{\mathscr{A}}(K,\Omega,\phi)\,.

(2.12)

Indeed, suppose that
\psi\in L_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
h_{n}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\setminus K)
satisfy
\nabla(\psi-\phi-h_{n})\to 0
in
L^{p}(\Omega\setminus K)
. Then
\nabla(\phi+h_{n})=\nabla\phi=0
a.e. in
K
. Therefore,
(
2.10
) implies that

{\rm cap}_{p}(K,\Omega)\leq E(\phi+h_{n},\Omega)=E(\phi+h_{n},\Omega\setminus K)\to E(\psi,\Omega\setminus K)\,

as
n\to\infty
.

Next, let
\psi^{K}
be the Perron solution of

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{p}\psi^{K}=0&\mbox{in}&\Omega\setminus K\,,\\
\psi^{K}=1&\mbox{on}&K\,,\\
\psi^{K}=0&\mbox{on}&\partial\Omega\,,\end{array}\right.

where by convention,
\infty\in\partial\Omega
if
\Omega
is unbounded.
By
[
8
, Theorem 9.33]
,
\psi^{K}\in{\mathscr{A}}(K,\Omega,\phi)
, and it coincides with the
p
-potential of
K
defined in
[
8
, Chapter 6]
. Moreover,
\psi^{K}
is continuous and
p
-harmonic on
\Omega\setminus K
, and it satisfies
\psi^{K}\equiv 1
on
K
. It follows from Corollary 1.21 in
[

]
that
\nabla\psi^{K}(x)=0
for almost every

.
By
[
8

,

.
This yields an alternative definition of
p
-capacity: If
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies
\phi=1
on
K
, then

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(K,\Omega):=\min\{E(\psi,\Omega):~\psi\in{\mathscr{A}}(K,\Omega,\phi)\}.

(2.17)

By the strict convexity of the
L^{p}
norm,
\psi^{K}
is the unique (up to translation by a constant) minimizer of the extremal problem (
2.17
).

Thus the Perron solutions
u,V_{R}^{s}
and
U
, defined in (
1
), (
1
) and (
1
), have the following properties:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{p}={\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma),\quad\int_{B(0,R)}|\nabla V_{R}^{s}|^{p}={\rm cap}_{p}(K_{s},B(0,R)),\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla U|^{p}={\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))=\gamma^{p-1}\omega_{d-1}\,,

(2.18)

where
\omega_{d-1}
is the surface area of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the last equality is from
[
8
, Section 2.11]
.

Remark 2.1
.

Fix
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\phi=1
on
\bar{B}(0,1)
and
\phi=0
outside
B(0,2)
, and suppose that
R>2
.
Since
V_{R}^{s}
coincides with the
p
-potential of
K_{s}
in
B(0,R)
,
[
8
, Lemma 8.5]
yields that
V_{R}^{s}-\phi\in H_{0}^{1,p}(B(0,R)\setminus K_{s})
,
which readily implies that for any domain
\Omega
that contains
B(0,R)
, we have

V_{R}^{s}\in H_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega\setminus K_{s}
) and
E(V_{R}^{s},\Omega)=E(V_{R}^{s},B(0,R))
.
While the classical gradient of
V_{R}^{s}
on
\partial B(0,R)
need not exist, the distributional gradient may be taken to be zero on this boundary,
and in any case, does not affect the energy.

We will need an estimate on the rate of convergence in (
2.9
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
K\subset\bar{B}(0,1)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is compact. Then

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\leq{\rm cap}_{p}(K,B(0,R))\leq\bigl(1-R^{-\gamma}\bigr)^{-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\,.

(2.19)

Proof.

The first inequality in (
2.19
) was already noted in (
2.9
), so we focus on the second.
Let
K_{n}
be the union of the closed dyadic cubes of side length
2^{-n}
that intersect
K
.
Denote by
\psi_{n}
the
p
-potential of
K_{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
\delta>0
.
Since
K=\cap_{n=1}^{\infty}K_{n}
, Theorem 2.2(iv) in
[
8
]
implies that there exists
n
such that
K_{n}\subset B(0,1+\delta)
and

{\rm cap}_{p}(K_{n})\leq{\rm cap}_{p}(K)+\delta\,.

Note that
\psi_{n}
is continuous in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
since
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus K_{n}
is regular by the corkscrew condition (See
[
8
, Theorems 6.27 and 6.31]
.)
Recall that
U
given by (
1
) is the
p
-potential of the unit ball in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Then
\psi_{n}(x)\leq U\Bigl(\frac{x}{1+\delta}\Bigr)
for all
x
by the definition of upper Perron solutions,
so the function

\psi(x)=\max\Bigl\{0,\frac{\psi_{n}(x)-(1+\delta)^{\gamma}R^{-\gamma}}{1-(1+\delta)^{\gamma}R^{-\gamma}}\Bigr\}

is in
H_{0}^{1,p}(B(0,R))
by
[
8
, Lemmas 1.23 and 1.26]
.
Thus, using
\psi
in (
2.10
) yields

{\rm cap}_{p}(K,B(0,R))\leq E(\psi,B(0,R))\leq\bigl(1-(1+\delta)^{\gamma}R^{-\gamma}\bigr)^{-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K_{n})\,,

which implies the second inequality of (
2.19
) since
\delta>0
is arbitrary.
∎

2.2
Energy estimate on truncated cones

Definition 2.4
.

Let
y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
and let
Q_{\delta}(y):=B(y,\delta)\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
be the open spherical cap of Euclidean radius
\delta
centered at
y
.
We define the spherical cone

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\delta}(y):=\{rq:q\in Q_{\delta}(y)\;\,\text{\rm and}\;\,r>0\}.

(2.20)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
Q
be an open set on the unit sphere, and for
R>1
set

\displaystyle\Lambda(Q,R):=\{rq:q\in Q\;\,\text{\rm and}\;\,r>R\}\,.

(2.21)

Suppose that a nonnegative function
v\in L^{1,p}(\Lambda(Q,R))\cap C(\overline{\Lambda(Q,R)})
satisfies

\displaystyle v(x)\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad|x|\to\infty\,,

(2.22)

and

\displaystyle v(Ry)\geq\tilde{A}>0,\quad\forall y\in Q\,.

(2.23)

Then,

\displaystyle E(v,\Lambda(Q,R))\geq E(v_{\tilde{A}},\Lambda(Q,R))\,,

(2.24)

where
v_{\tilde{A}}(x)=\tilde{A}R^{\gamma}U(x)=\tilde{A}U(x/R)
for all
x\in\Lambda(Q,R)
.

Proof.

Assume (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) hold. Since
v\in L^{1,p}(\Lambda(Q,R))
, for
\mu
almost every
y\in Q
,
the gradient
\nabla v(ry)
exists and is finite for a.e.
r\in(R,\infty)
. Furthermore,
[
5
, Theorem 4.9.2]
implies that for
\mu
almost every
y\in Q
, the function

r\mapsto v(ry)\,
is absolutely continuous in
\,(R,\infty)
.
Thus, for
\mu
almost every
y\in Q
, Hölder’s inequality yields that

\displaystyle\tilde{A}\leq\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|\nabla v(ry)\right|dr=\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|\nabla v(ry)\right|r^{\frac{d-1}{p}}r^{\frac{1-d}{p}}\,dr\leq\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|\nabla v(ry)\right|^{p}r^{d-1}\,dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}r^{\frac{1-d}{p-1}}dr\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\gamma R^{\gamma}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|\nabla v(ry)\right|^{p}r^{d-1}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2.25)

Taking
p
-th power and rearranging terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{p}R^{d-p}\gamma^{p-1}\leq\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|\nabla v(ry)\right|^{p}r^{d-1}dr.

(2.26)

Let
\omega_{d-1}
denote the area of the unit sphere.
Integrating (
2.26
) over
Q
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{A}^{p}R^{d-p}\gamma^{p-1}\omega_{d-1}\mu(Q)\leq\omega_{d-1}\int_{Q}\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|\nabla v(ry)\right|^{p}r^{d-1}\,dr\,d\mu(y)=\int_{\Lambda(Q,R)}\left|\nabla v(x)\right|^{p}\,dx=E(v,\Lambda(Q,R))\,.

(2.27)

By the condition for equality in Hölder’s inequality, the second inequality in (
2.2
) is an equality if for some constant
C
,

\displaystyle\Bigl[|\nabla v(ry)|r^{\frac{d-1}{p}}\Bigr]^{p-1}=Cr^{\frac{1-d}{p}}\quad\mbox{\rm for a.e.}\;r\geq R\,.

(2.28)

The function
v_{\tilde{A}}(x)=\tilde{A}R^{\gamma}U(x)
satisfies (
2.28
) and the boundary condition
v_{\tilde{A}}(Ry)=\tilde{A}
for all
y\in Q
. Since

|\nabla v_{\tilde{A}}(ry)|=-\frac{d}{dr}v_{\tilde{A}}(ry)

for
y\in\partial B(0,1)
, the first inequality in (
2.2
) is also an equality if
v=v_{\tilde{A}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle E(v_{\tilde{A}},\Lambda(Q,R))=\tilde{A}^{p}R^{d-p}\gamma^{p-1}\omega_{d-1}\mu(Q)\,.

(2.29)

Combining (
2.27
) and (
2.29
), we obtain (
2.24
).
∎

2.3
Energy of an ansatz function

Recall from (
1.35
) the definition

u_{A}(x)=AU_{1+\varepsilon}(x)+(1-A)\sum_{s\in S}V_{\frac{1}{10\alpha}}^{s}\left(\frac{x-s}{\alpha\varepsilon}\right)\,.

Since the gradients of the summands on the right-hand side have disjoint supports, we have

\displaystyle E(u_{A})=A^{p}E(U_{1+\varepsilon})+(1-A)^{p}\sum_{s\in S}E\Bigl(x\mapsto V_{\frac{1}{10\alpha}}^{s}\Bigl(\frac{x-s}{\alpha\varepsilon}\Bigr)\Bigr)\,.

(2.30)

(By Remark
2.1
, we can compute the energy over all of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in the summands on the right-hand side.)
For any function
\psi\in L^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, vector
s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and scalar
r>0
, scaling arguments yield that

\displaystyle E\Bigl(x\mapsto\psi\Bigl(\frac{x-s}{r}\Bigr)\Bigr)=r^{d-p}E(\psi)\,.

(2.31)

Therefore,

\displaystyle E(u_{A})=A^{p}(1+\varepsilon)^{d-p}E(U)+(1-A)^{p}\sum_{s\in S}(\alpha\varepsilon)^{d-p}E\bigl(V_{\frac{1}{10\alpha}}^{s}\bigr).

(2.32)

Using the fact that
E(U)={\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(u_{A})=(1+\varepsilon)^{d-p}A^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))+(1-A)^{p}\sum_{s\in S}\kappa_{s}\,,

(2.33)

where

\displaystyle\kappa_{s}:={\rm cap}_{p}\left(\alpha\varepsilon K_{s},B\left(s,\frac{\varepsilon}{10}\right)\right)\,.

(2.34)

By Lemma
2.2
applied to
K_{s}
and hypothesis
{\rm(H_{2})}
on equality of capacities, we infer that

\displaystyle\Bigl|{\rm cap}_{p}\Bigl(K_{s},B\Bigl(0,\frac{1}{10\alpha}\Bigr)\Bigr)-{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\Bigr|\leq J(\alpha){\rm cap}_{p}(K)\,,

(2.35)

where

\displaystyle J(\alpha):=\bigl(1-(10\alpha)^{\gamma}\bigr)^{-p}-1\to 0\;\;\mbox{\rm as}\;\;\varepsilon\to 0\,.

(2.36)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Bigl|E(u_{A})-(1+\varepsilon)^{d-p}A^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))-(1-A)^{p}(\alpha\varepsilon)^{d-p}|S|{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\Bigr|\leq(1-A)^{p}(\alpha\varepsilon)^{d-p}|S|J(\alpha){\rm cap}_{p}(K)\,.

(2.37)

Next, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have by (
1.12
) and the definition of
\tau
in
{\rm(H_{3})}
that

\displaystyle(\alpha\varepsilon)^{d-p}|S|=\bigl(\alpha\varepsilon^{-1/\gamma}\bigr)^{d-p}\varepsilon^{d-1}|S|\to\tau^{d-p}\sigma\,.

(2.38)

In conjunction with (
2.37
), this gives

\displaystyle E(u_{A})\to\varphi_{\tau}(A)\;\mbox{\rm as}\;\varepsilon\to 0\,,

(2.39)

where

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau}(A)=A^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))+(1-A)^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\sigma\tau^{d-p}.

(2.40)

If
\tau\in[0,\infty)
, then the convergence in (
2.39
) is uniform in
A\in[0,1]
.
Note that
\varphi_{\infty}(1)={\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))
and
\varphi_{\infty}(A)=\infty
for all
A\in[0,1)
,
and in the latter case, the convergence in (
2.39
) means that the left-hand side tends to
\infty
.
For
\tau\in(0,\infty)
, the function
\varphi_{\tau}
is a continuous strictly convex function on
[0,1]
. Differentiation shows that it attains
its minimum at
A_{*}=A_{*}(\tau)
given by (
1.26
), with

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau}(A_{*})=\left(\frac{\left(\sigma\tau^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K){\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\left(\sigma\tau^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\left({\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\right)^{p-1}.

(2.41)

Clearly,
\varphi_{\tau}(A_{*})\leq\varphi_{\tau}(1)={\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1))
for all
\tau\in[0,\infty]
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\eta:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
cutoff function such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
B(0,1)
and
\eta\equiv 0
outside
B(0,2)
. Then for
R>2
, we have that
V_{R}^{s}-\eta\in L_{0}^{1,p}(B(0,R)\setminus K_{s})
for each
s\in S
.
Thus, for every anchor
s
,

V_{\frac{1}{10\alpha}}^{s}\left(\frac{x-s}{\alpha\varepsilon}\right)-\eta\ \left(\frac{x-s}{\alpha\varepsilon}\right)\in L_{0}^{1,p}\Bigl(B(s,\varepsilon/10)\setminus(s+\alpha\varepsilon K_{s})\Bigr)\,.

Consequently, for all
A\in[0,1]
, the ansatz function
u_{A}
given by (
1.35
) belongs to the admissible class
{\cal A}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d},\phi)
with

\phi(x):=A\eta\Bigl(\frac{x}{1+\varepsilon}\Bigr)+(1-A)\sum_{s\in S}\eta\Bigl(\frac{x-s}{\alpha\varepsilon}\Bigr)\,.

By (
2.12
), this implies that
E(u)\leq E(u_{A}).

Corollary 2.1
.

For every
y
on the unit sphere,
\delta>0
and
\tau\in[0,\infty]
, we have

\displaystyle E(u_{A},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\to\mu(Q_{\delta})\varphi_{\tau}(A)\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0\,,

(2.42)

and the convergence is uniform in
A\in[0,1]
and in
y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
, provided that
\tau<\infty
.
Moreover, for every
\delta\in(0,1/2)
and sufficiently small
\varepsilon
(that may depend on
\delta
),

\displaystyle\forall A\in[0,1],\qquad E(u_{A},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\geq(1-\delta)^{p}E(u_{A^{*}},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\;\;\mbox{\rm for}\;\;y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\,.

(2.43)

Proof.

By the definition of
u_{A}
and (
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle(1-A)^{p}\sum_{s\in S\cap Q_{\delta-\varepsilon}(y)}\kappa_{s}\leq E(u_{A},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))-A^{p}(1+\varepsilon)^{d-p}E(U,\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\leq(1-A)^{p}\sum_{s\in S\cap Q_{\delta+\varepsilon}(y)}\kappa_{s}\,,

(2.44)

where
\kappa_{s}
was defined in (
2.34
).
Since
U
is radial,

E(U,\Lambda_{\delta}(y))=\mu(Q_{\delta}){\rm cap}_{p}\bigl(\bar{B}(0,1)\bigr)\,.

The rest of the proof of (
2.42
) proceeds exactly like the proof of (
2.39
),
since the left-hand and right-hand sides of (
2.44
) have the same asymptotics:

\varepsilon^{d-1}|S\cap Q_{\delta\pm\varepsilon}(y)|\to\sigma\mu(Q_{\delta})\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0\,.

It remains to verify (
2.43
). If
0<\tau<\infty
, then this follows from the uniform convergence (in the parameter
A
) in (
2.42
).
If
\tau=0
, then
A_{*}=0
. In this case, (
2.43
)
is obvious if
A\leq\delta
. On the other hand, if
\tau=0
and
A>\delta
, then the left-hand side of (
2.43
) is at least
\delta^{p}E(U,\Lambda_{\delta}(y))>0
which does not depend on
\varepsilon
,
while the right-hand side of (
2.43
) tends to
0
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
; Thus, (
2.43
) holds in this case as well provided
\varepsilon
is small enough.

Finally, if
\tau=\infty
, then
A_{*}=1
and

u_{A^{*}}=U_{1+\varepsilon}
; in this case, (
2.43
)
is obvious if
A>1-\delta
, while the lower bound

E(u_{A},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\geq(1-A)^{p}\sum_{s\in S\cap Q_{\delta-\varepsilon}(y)}\kappa_{s}\,,

implies that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\min_{A\in[0,1-\delta]}E(u_{A},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))=\infty\,,

so (
2.43
) holds if
\tau=\infty
and
A\leq 1-\delta
as well.
∎

3
Bounding oscillation and energy of
u
in cones

In this section, the positive constants
C,C_{1},C_{2},\ldots
depend only on
d,p
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that for some
r>0
and
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle B(z,r)\subset B(z,5r/4)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Gamma

(3.1)

and for some

\lambda>0,\beta\geq 0
, the solution
u
of (
1
) satisfies

\displaystyle E(u,B(z,5r/4))\leq\lambda^{p}r^{d-1-\beta}\,.

(3.2)

Then,

\displaystyle\underset{B(z,r)}{\rm osc}~u\leq C_{1}\lambda r^{\frac{p-1-\beta}{p}}\,,

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle\underset{D}{\rm osc}~u:=\sup_{D}u-\inf_{D}u

(3.4)

stands for the oscillation over the set
D
.

Proof.

By Poincaré’s inequality, there exists a real
t
such that

\displaystyle J_{p}=\int_{B(z,5r/4)}\left|u-t\right|^{p}\leq C_{2}r^{p}\int_{B(z,5r/4)}|\nabla u|^{p}=C_{2}r^{p}E(u,B(z,5r/4))\,.

(3.5)

The hypothesis (
3.2
) and the inequality (
3.5
) give

\displaystyle J_{p}\leq C_{2}\lambda^{p}r^{p+d-1-\beta}\,.

(3.6)

Let

\displaystyle u_{1}(x)=\max\{u(x)-t,0\},\qquad u_{2}(x)=\max\{t-u(x),0\}.

(3.7)

By
[

, Lemma 3.6]
we have

\displaystyle\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B(z,r))}\leq C_{3}\left(\frac{J_{p}}{r^{d}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C_{4}\lambda r^{\frac{p-1-\beta}{p}},\qquad i=1,2,

(3.8)

which implies (
3.3
) with

.
∎

Lemma 3.3

Proof.

S_{\beta}\cap Q_{\delta}(y)

, where
\zeta_{j}=\zeta(s_{j})
for each

so that every point on the unit sphere belongs to at most

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\bf 1}_{\Lambda_{\zeta_{j}}(s_{j})}\leq C_{8}{\bf 1}_{\Lambda_{2\delta}(y)}\,.

(3.15)

For each
s\in S_{\beta}\cap Q_{\delta}(y)
, there is some
j\leq N
such that
s\in Q_{\zeta_{j}}(s_{j})
, whence the cap
Q_{\varepsilon/2}(s)
is contained in
Q_{2\zeta_{j}}(s_{j})
. Since the
\varepsilon/2
neighborhoods
Q_{\varepsilon/2}(s)
of anchors are disjoint,
for some

we have

\displaystyle C_{9}|S_{\beta}\cap Q_{\delta}(y)|\varepsilon^{d-1}\leq\mu\Bigl[\bigcup\{Q_{\varepsilon/2}(s)\,:\,s\in S_{\beta}\cap Q_{\delta}(y)\}\Bigr]\leq\mu\Bigl[\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}Q_{2\zeta_{j}}(s_{j})\Bigr]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mu[Q_{2\zeta_{j}}(s_{j})]\,.

(3.16)

Now by (
3.14
), for each

\displaystyle\mu[Q_{2\zeta_{j}}(s_{j})]\leq C_{10}\zeta_{j}^{d-1}\leq C_{10}\delta^{\beta}E(u,\Lambda_{\zeta_{j}}(s_{j}))\,.

(3.17)

Thus by (
3.16
) and (
3.17
),

\displaystyle|S_{\beta}\cap Q_{\delta}(y)|\varepsilon^{d-1}\leq\frac{C_{10}}{C_{9}}\delta^{\beta}\sum_{j=1}^{N}E(u,\Lambda_{\zeta_{j}}(s_{j}))=C_{11}\delta^{\beta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Bigl(|\nabla u|^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\bf 1}_{\Lambda_{\zeta_{j}}(s_{j})}\Bigr)\leq C_{11}\delta^{\beta}C_{8}E(u,\Lambda_{2\delta}(y))\,,

(3.18)

where we have used (
3.15
) in the last step.
Combining (
3.13
) and (

) concludes the proof.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\beta:=\frac{p-1}{2p}
. Then there exist
\delta_{0}>0
(which may depend on

) and

(which depend on

) such that for

with the following property. For each

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle E(u_{A},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\leq A^{p}(1+\varepsilon)^{d-p}E(U,\Lambda_{\delta}(y))+(1-A)^{p}\sum_{s\in S\cap Q_{\delta+\varepsilon}(y)}\kappa_{s}\,,

(3.22)

where

(3.23)

First observe that under the assumption (

), Lemma

gives that

\displaystyle\underset{\Lambda_{\delta}(y)\cap\partial B(0,1+\delta)}{\rm osc}~u\leq C_{1}\delta^{\frac{p-1-\beta}{p}}\leq C_{1}\delta^{\beta}\,.

Similarly,

Hence,

where

Lemma
3.2

5
Separation theorem

The oscillation and energy estimates we used in the previous section to prove Theorem

In particular, we do not assume the asymptotic equidistribution hypothesis
(H_{1})
in (
1.6
), and instead of the asymptotic relation
(H_{3})

\alpha(\varepsilon)\varepsilon^{-1/\gamma}\to\tau
we just assume that for some
\tau_{1}\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\alpha<\min\{1/80,\tau_{1}\varepsilon^{1/\gamma}\}\,.

Let
S\subset\partial B(0,1)
be a set of anchors, with Euclidean distance at least
\varepsilon
between any two anchors. The next theorem ensures that for
\alpha
in the critical window and below it, the bumps in the equilibrium potential are separated.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
\zeta>0
, write
\Omega_{\zeta}=B(0,1+\delta)\setminus\bigl[\cup_{s\in S}\bar{B}(s,\zeta\varepsilon)\bigr]
.
Let
w:\bar{B}(0,1+\delta)\to[0,1]
be the Perron solution of the boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{p}w=0&\mbox{in}&\Omega_{\alpha},\\
w=0&\mbox{on}&\partial B(0,1+\delta),\\
w=1&\mbox{on}&\cup_{s\in S}\partial B(s,\alpha\varepsilon)\,.\end{array}\right.

Then for some
C=C(d,p)
, we have

\sup_{z\in\Omega_{1/10}}|w(z)|\leq C\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\delta\,.

(5.5)

Figure 5:
The inner balls are centered at anchors on the unit sphere and have radius
\alpha\varepsilon
; on their boundary,
w=1
. Each of them is surrounded by two concentric spheres of radii
\varepsilon/20
and
\varepsilon/10
, respectively. All these spheres are contained in larger spheres of radii
1+\varepsilon/5
and
1+\delta
centered at the origin.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
is based on the lemma below which requires the following notation:

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle:=\sup\Bigl\{w(x)\mid x\in\cup_{s\in S}\;\partial B\left(s,\frac{\varepsilon}{20}\right)\Bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:=\sup\Bigl\{w(x)\mid x\in\cup_{s\in S}\;\partial B\left(s,\frac{\varepsilon}{10}\right)\Bigr\}\,,

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle:=\sup\Bigl\{w(x)\mid x\in\partial B\left(0,1+\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\right)\Bigl\}\,.

We note here that by the maximum principle,

\sup_{z\in\Omega_{1/10}}w(z)\leq F\,.

(5.6)

Lemma 5.1
.

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and
c_{3}\in(0,1)
that only depend on
d,p
,
such that

\displaystyle(a)\qquad D

\displaystyle\leq F+c_{1}\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\varepsilon(1-F)\,,

(5.7)

\displaystyle(b)\qquad G

\displaystyle\leq(1-c_{2}\varepsilon/\delta)F\,,

(5.8)

\displaystyle(c)\qquad F

\displaystyle\leq(1-c_{3})D+c_{3}G\,.

(5.9)

Proof.

Let
0<r<R
and set

h_{r,R}(x)=\frac{|x|^{-\gamma}-R^{-\gamma}}{r^{-\gamma}-R^{-\gamma}}\;\mbox{ \rm for }\;r<|x|<R\,.

(5.10)

Observe that
h_{r,R}
is
p
-harmonic in
B(0,R)\setminus\bar{B}(0,r)
and takes values
0
and
1
on
\partial B(0,R)
and
\partial B(0,r)
respectively.

(a) Given the definition of
F,
the comparison principle (Lemma
2.1
) implies that for each
s\in S
, we have

\displaystyle w(x)\leq F+(1-F)\nu_{1}(x-s),\qquad\forall x\in B\left(s,\frac{\varepsilon}{10}\right)\setminus B(s,\alpha\varepsilon),

(5.11)

where
\nu_{1}(x)=h_{r,R}(x)
with
r=\alpha\varepsilon
,
R={\varepsilon}/{10}.

Since

\displaystyle|x|=\varepsilon/20\;\Longrightarrow\;\nu_{1}(x)=\frac{(\varepsilon/20)^{-\gamma}-(\varepsilon/10)^{-\gamma}}{(\alpha\varepsilon)^{-\gamma}-(\varepsilon/10)^{-\gamma}}\leq\frac{(\varepsilon/20)^{-\gamma}}{(\alpha\varepsilon)^{-\gamma}}\leq c_{1}\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\varepsilon\,,

(5.12)

we infer from (
5.11
) that

\displaystyle w(x)\leq F+(1-F)c_{1}\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\varepsilon\qquad\forall x\in\partial B\left(s,\frac{\varepsilon}{20}\right)\,.

(5.13)

This gives (
5.7
).

(b) Observe that by (
5.6
) we have
w\leq F
on
\partial B(0,1+\varepsilon/10).
Hence, by the comparison principle, for all
s\in S
we have

\displaystyle w(x)\leq F\nu_{2}(x),\qquad\forall x\in B\left(0,1+\delta\right)\setminus B\left(0,1+\frac{\varepsilon}{10}\right)\,,

(5.14)

where
\nu_{2}(x)=h_{r,R}(x)
with
r=1+\varepsilon/10
and
R=1+\delta.

Since the derivative of
t\mapsto t^{-\gamma}
is bounded above and below by positive constants for
t\in[1,2]
, the mean value theorem gives

1-\nu_{2}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\right)=\frac{(1+\varepsilon/10)^{-\gamma}-(1+\varepsilon/5)^{-\gamma}}{(1+\varepsilon/10)^{-\gamma}-(1+\delta)^{-\gamma}}\geq c_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\,.

(5.15)

Combining (
5.14
) and (
5.15
), we have that

\displaystyle w(x)\leq\left(1-c_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\right)F,\qquad\forall x\in\partial B\left(0,1+\frac{\varepsilon}{5}\right).

(5.16)

This gives (
5.8
).

(c) Denote by
\xi
the standard basis vector
(1,0,\ldots,0)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d},
and consider the open cored half-ball

H:=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in B(0,8)\setminus\bar{B}(4\xi,1)\;:x_{1}>0\}.

(5.17)

The cored half-ball

H
obtained by removing a unit ball from the middle of a half-ball of radius 8.

Let
\psi
be the Perron solution of
\Delta_{p}\psi=0
in
H
, with boundary values
\psi=0
on the flat part
\{x\in\partial H\,:\,x_{1}=0\}
of
\partial H
, and
\psi=1
on the curved part
\{x\in\partial B(0,8)\,:x_{1}>0\}\cup\partial B(4\xi,1)
. (By Proposition 9.31 in
[
8
]
, the upper and lower Perron solutions coincide since the boundary conditions are lower semi-continuous and
H
is regular.) By the strong maximum principle (see
[
11
]
, Corollary 2.22), we have

\psi(x)<1
for all
x\in H.
By the continuity of
\psi
in
H
, for some
c_{3}>0
, we have

\sup\{\psi(x)\,:\,x\in\partial B(4\xi,2)\}=1-c_{3}\,.

(5.18)

Fix
s\in S
and let
s_{*}=(1+\varepsilon/5)s
. The cored half-ball

H_{s}:=\{x\in B(s_{*},2\varepsilon/5)\setminus\bar{B}(s,\varepsilon/20)\;:\langle x,s\rangle<1+\varepsilon/5\}\,

(5.19)

is a rotated and translated copy of
H
, scaled by
\varepsilon/20
. The function
w
is continuous on
\bar{H_{s}}
, and
p
-harmonic in
H_{s}
, with boundary values
w\leq G
on the flat part of
\partial H_{s}
and
w\leq D
on the curved part. Therefore, by the comparison principle and (
5.18
), we have

\sup\{w(x)\,:\,x\in\partial B(s,\varepsilon/10)\}\leq G+(1-c_{3})(D-G)=(1-c_{3})D+c_{3}G\,.

(5.20)

This proves (
5.9
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Combining (
5.7
)-(
5.9
), we have

\displaystyle F\leq(1-c_{3})\bigl[F+c_{1}\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\varepsilon(1-F)\bigr]+c_{3}\bigl[1-c_{2}\varepsilon/\delta\bigr]F.

(5.21)

This gives

\displaystyle 0\leq(1-c_{3})c_{1}\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\varepsilon(1-F)-c_{3}c_{2}(\varepsilon/\delta)F\,,

(5.22)

which in turn implies that

\displaystyle c_{3}c_{2}(\varepsilon/\delta)F\leq c_{1}\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\varepsilon\,,

(5.23)

whence

\displaystyle F\leq\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{3}c_{2}}\right)\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\delta\,.

(5.24)

Thus there exists a constant
C_{32}>0
such that

F\leq C_{32}\tau_{1}^{\gamma}\delta
. By (
5.6
), this completes the proof.
∎

6
Asymptotics for
u^{\varepsilon}
near the unit sphere: Proof of Theorem
1.2

We will need Clarkson’s inequalities, in the slightly more general form given in
[
2
]
for functions
f,g
taking values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

\displaystyle p\geq 2\Rightarrow\quad\left\|\frac{f+g}{2}\right\|_{p}^{p}+\left\|\frac{f-g}{2}\right\|_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\right)\,,

(6.1)

and

\displaystyle 1<p\leq 2\Rightarrow\quad\left\|\frac{f+g}{2}\right\|_{p}^{q}+\left\|\frac{f-g}{2}\right\|_{p}^{q}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{2}\|g\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,,

(6.2)

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that
\beta=(p-1)/(2p)
. As before, we let
C_{i}
denote constants that depend only on
d,p
. Pick a small
\delta>0
.
We invoke Lemma
4.1
with
m
chosen to satisfy
m\beta/3\geq d
, and infer from (
2.42
) that

\forall y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1},\quad E(u,\Lambda_{2\delta}(y))\leq E(u_{A_{*}},\Lambda_{2\delta}(y))+2^{d}C_{21}\delta^{d}\leq C_{33}\delta^{d-1}\,,

provided
\varepsilon
is small enough.
Thus the hypothesis of Lemma
3.4
holds for every
y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
, so that lemma yields

\displaystyle E(u,\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\geq(1-2\delta^{\beta/3})E(u_{A},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\,.

(6.3)

for some
A
that may depend on all parameters of the problem, including
\delta
and
\varepsilon
.
In conjunction with (
2.43
), this yields that for small enough
\delta
,

\displaystyle E(u,\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\geq(1-3\delta^{\beta/3})E(u_{A_{*}},\Lambda_{\delta}(y))\,.

(6.4)

By averaging this inequality over
y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
, Fubini’s Theorem yields (as in the proof of (
4.3
)) that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle E(u)\mu(Q_{\delta})\geq(1-3\delta^{\beta/3})E(u_{A_{*}})\mu(Q_{\delta})\,.

(6.5)

We deduce that for
\varepsilon
small enough,

\displaystyle 0\leq E(u_{A_{*}})-E(u)\leq C\delta^{\beta/3}\,.

(6.6)

Let
\phi
be the cutoff function defined in Remark
2.2
. Then
u,u_{A_{*}}
are both in the convex admissible class

{\mathscr{A}}(\Gamma,{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\phi)

defined in (
2.11
), and
u
minimizes the energy in this class, so the inequality

E\Bigl(\frac{u+u_{A_{*}}}{2}\Bigr)\geq E(u)

holds. In conjunction with (
6.6
) and Clarkson’s inequalities, this implies that

\|\nabla u-\nabla u_{A_{*}}\|_{p}=O(\delta^{c})

for some constant
c>0
and all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Since
\delta
can be arbitrarily small, this proves the first statement of the theorem.

It only remains to prove the
L^{\infty}
convergence.

Recall that we have already established in (
4.22
) that for small
\delta
and (given
\delta
) sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(0,1+\delta),\qquad(A_{*}-C_{\#}\delta^{\theta})U_{1+\delta}(x)\leq u(x)\leq(A_{*}+C_{\#}\delta^{\theta})U_{1+\delta}(x)\,.

(6.7)

for some
0<\theta<1
.

If
\tau=\infty
, then
A^{*}=1
and the first inequality in (
6.7
), together with the minimum principle,
imply that
u\geq 1-C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}
in
B(0,1+\delta)
, completing the proof in this case. Thus, we may assume that
\tau<\infty
.

Next, recall the continuous
p
-harmonic function

w
from Theorem
5.1
defined in the regular domain

\Omega_{\alpha}=B(0,1+\delta)\setminus\bigl[\cup_{s\in S}\bar{B}\bigl(s,\alpha\varepsilon\bigr)\bigr]

considered there. Theorem
5.1
implies that
w\leq C_{34}(2\tau)^{\gamma}\delta
in the closure of the domain

\Omega_{1/10}=B(0,1+\delta)\setminus\bigl[\cup_{s\in S}\bar{B}\bigl(s,\varepsilon/10\bigr)\bigr]\,.

The inequality
u(x)-A_{*}-C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}\leq w
holds on

\partial\Omega_{\alpha}
, so it must holds in
\Omega_{\alpha}
as well, by
the comparison principle (Lemma
2.1
). In particular, for
\delta<\delta_{0}
small enough (that may depend on
d,p,\sigma,\tau
), and
\epsilon
small
enough given
\delta
, we have

\forall x\in\partial B(s,\varepsilon/10),\quad u\leq A_{*}+C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}+C_{34}(2\tau)^{\gamma}\delta\leq A_{*}+2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}\,.

On the other hand,
u\geq A_{*}-C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}
in
B(0,1+\delta)
by (
6.7
) and the minimum principle (or by Lemma
2.1
.)
Thus

\displaystyle|u-u_{A_{*}}|\leq 2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{1/10}\,.

(6.8)

Finally, fix an anchor
s\in S
and let
D_{s}:=B(s,\varepsilon/10)\setminus(s+\alpha\varepsilon K_{s})
. Observe that
u_{A^{*}}
, restricted to
D_{s}
,
is the Perron solution of the
p
-Laplace equation in
D_{s}
, with the boundary condition
f_{s}
which is identically 1 on
s+\alpha\varepsilon K_{s}
and equals
A_{*}
on

\partial B(s,\varepsilon/10)
.
The inequality

\displaystyle u_{A_{*}}(x)-2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}\leq u(x)\leq u_{A_{*}}(x)+2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}

(6.9)

holds for
x\in\partial D_{s}
.

Let
\psi
be a superharmonic function in the upper class
U_{f}^{\Omega}
(see Definition
2.2
) corresponding to the boundary conditions

f\equiv 1\;\text{on}\;\Gamma,\quad f(\infty)=0

in the definition (
1
) of
u
as a Perron solution in
\Omega={\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Gamma
. Then
\psi+2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}
, restricted to
D_{s}
, is in the
upper class
U_{f_{s}}^{D_{s}}
by (
6.9
), so
\psi+2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}\geq u_{A_{*}}
in
D_{s}
. Taking an infimum over all such
\psi\in U_{f}^{\Omega}
,
we infer that

\displaystyle u+2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}\geq u_{A_{*}}\quad\text{in}\;D_{s}\,.

(6.10)

Similarly, let
\psi_{1}
be a superharmonic function in the upper class
U_{-f}^{\Omega}
. Then
\psi_{1}+2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}
, restricted to
D_{s}
, is in the
upper class
U_{-f_{s}}^{D_{s}}
by (
6.9
), so
\psi_{1}+2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}\geq-u_{A_{*}}
in
D_{s}
. Taking an infimum over all such
\psi_{1}\in U_{-f}^{\Omega}
,
we infer that

\displaystyle-u+2C_{\#}\delta^{\theta}\geq-u_{A_{*}}\quad\text{in}\;D_{s}\,.

(6.11)

Combining this with (
6.10
) concludes the proof, since
\delta
can be arbitrarily small.
∎

7
Concluding remarks.

1.
One method to obtain an asymptotically equidistributed set of anchors is to choose
S(\varepsilon)
as any

\epsilon
-separated set on the sphere of maximal cardinality. The asymptotic equidistribution

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|S(\varepsilon)|}\sum_{s\in S(\varepsilon)}\delta_{s}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\mu\>~\mbox{as}~\varepsilon\downarrow 0,

(7.1)

then follows from a classical argument of Maak, as presented, e.g., in

[
4
, Chapter 12]
, while the existence of the limit
\sigma=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{d-1}|S(\varepsilon)|
is established in
[
7
]
.

2.
The main results of this paper directly extend to problem (
1
) considered
in a ball
B(0,R)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
R>1.
Indeed, let
1<p<d,

R>1
and let
w=w^{\varepsilon}
be the Perron solution of the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta_{p}w=0&\mbox{in}&B(0,R)\setminus\Gamma,\\
w=1&\mbox{on}&\Gamma,\\
w=0&\mbox{on}&\partial B(0,R).\end{array}\right.

Define an ansatz

\displaystyle w^{\varepsilon}_{A}(x):=AW_{\varepsilon,R}(x)+(1-A)\sum_{s\in S}V_{\frac{1}{10\alpha}}^{s}\left(\frac{x-s}{\alpha\varepsilon}\right)\,,

(7.6)

where
W_{\varepsilon,R}
is the
p
-equilibrium potential of the ball
\bar{B}(0,1+\varepsilon)
relative to the ball
B(0,R)
,
given by

\displaystyle W_{\varepsilon,R}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&0\leq|x|\leq 1+\varepsilon\,,\\
\frac{|x|^{-\gamma}-R^{-\gamma}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{-\gamma}-R^{-\gamma}}&1+\varepsilon<|x|\leq R\,,\end{array}\right.

and
V_{\rho}^{s}
are as in (
1
).

The following corollary holds:

Corollary 7.1
.

Suppose that hypotheses
{\rm(H_{1}),(H_{2}),(H_{3})}
hold.
Then, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\displaystyle w^{\varepsilon}(x)\to\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}0&\mbox{if}&\tau=0\,,\\
A_{R}W_{0,R}(x)&\mbox{if}&\ \tau\in(0,\infty)\,,\\
W_{0,R}(x)&\mbox{if}&\tau=\infty\end{array}\right.

uniformly on compact subsets of
\bar{B}(0,R)\setminus\mathbb{S}^{d-1},

where for
\tau\in[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle A_{R}=A_{R}(\tau)=\frac{\left(\sigma\tau^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\left(\sigma\tau^{d-p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+\left({\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1),B(0,R))\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}\,.

(7.14)

Furthermore, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\displaystyle{\rm cap}_{p}(\Gamma_{\varepsilon},B(0,R))\to\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}0&\mbox{if}&\tau=0\,,\\
A_{R}^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1),B(0,R))+(1-A_{R})^{p}{\rm cap}_{p}(K)\sigma\tau^{d-p}&\mbox{if}&\ \tau\in(0,\infty)\,,\\
{\rm cap}_{p}(\bar{B}(0,1),B(0,R))&\mbox{if}&\tau=\infty\,.\end{array}\right.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|\nabla w-\nabla w^{\varepsilon}_{A_{R}}\|_{{L^{p}(\bar{B}(0,R))}}\to 0,

(7.19)

and

\displaystyle\|w-w_{A_{R}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\bar{B}(0,R))}\to 0\,.

(7.20)

The proof of this corollary is a line by line adaptation of the arguments in the preceding sections.

Global solution for superlinear stochastic heat equation on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} under Osgood-type conditions
Le Chen, Mohammud Foondun, Jingyu Huang, Mickey Salins

Abstract
We study the stochastic heat equation (SHE) on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} subject to a centered Gaussian noise that is white in time and colored in space.The drift term is assumed to satisfy an Osgood-type condition and the diffusion coefficient may have certain related growth. We show that there exists random field solution which do not explode in finite time. This complements and improves upon recent results on blow-up of solutions to stochastic partial differential equations. Keywords. Global solution; Stochastic heat equation; Reaction-diffusion; Dalang’s condition; superlinear growth; Osgood-type conditions.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the following superlinear
stochastic heat
equation
(SHE) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial u(t,x)}{\partial t}=\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+b\left(u(t,x)\right)+\sigma\left(u(t,x)\right)\dot{W}(t,x)\,,&t>0,\>x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where both
b
and
\sigma
are locally Lipschitz continuous, vanish at zero,
and may have superlinear growth at infinity. The noise
\dot{W}
is a centered
Gaussian noise which is white in time and colored in space with the following
covariance structure

\mathbb{E}\left[\dot{W}(s,y)\dot{W}(t,x)\right]=\delta(t-s)f(x-y)\,,

(1.2)

where
\delta
is the Dirac delta measure and
f
is a nonnegative and
nonnegative-definite function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The case when
f=\delta_{0}
refers to
the space-time white noise. We note that
f
induces an inner product

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{H}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\varphi(y)\psi(z)f(y-z)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}z

(1.3)

for
\varphi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e., smooth functions with compact
supports in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and define the Hilbert space
H
to be the completion of

C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under this inner product.

The solution to (
1.1
) is understood in the mild formulation:

\displaystyle u(t,x)=(p_{t}*u_{0})(x)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)b(u(s,y))\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

(1.4)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(u(s,y))W(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)\,,

where the stochastic integral is the
Walsh
integral

[
Wal86
,
Dal+09
]
,

p_{t}(x)=\left(2\pi t\right)^{-d/2}\exp\left(-|x|^{2}/2t\right)
is the heat
kernel, and “
*
” denotes the convolution in the spatial variable.

For the one-dimensional deterministic ordinary differential equation

\displaystyle\dfrac{dv}{dt}=b(v(t))\quad\text{with $b\geq 0$ and $v(0)=c>0$,}

(1.5)

the
Osgood condition

[
Osg98
]
characterizes finite time
explosion. Solutions explode in finite time if and only if the following

finite Osgood condition
holds:

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{\infty}\frac{1}{b(u)}\mathrm{d}u<+\infty.

(1.6)

This can be seen by rewriting (
1.5
) as follows

t=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{b(u(s))}\mathrm{d}u(s).
After a change of variable, we obtain

t=\int_{v(0)}^{v(t)}\frac{1}{b(s)}\mathrm{d}s.

This shows that (
1.6
) is both necessary and sufficient for
blow-up of solutions to (
1.5
). The Osgood condition does not fully
characterize finite time explosion for deterministic partial differential
equations as demonstrated by the famous example provided by
Fujita
[
Fuj66
]
.

Finite time explosion for
stochastic partial differential equations

(SPDEs) with superlinear
b
and
\sigma
have only recently gained some
attention. The case of a bounded spatial domain has received more attention.
Bonder and Groisman
[
FG09
]
demonstrated
that if
b
satisfies (
1.6
), then a one-dimensional stochastic
heat equation with additive space-time white noise will always explode. Dalang,
Khoshnevisan, and Zhang
[
DKZ19
]
established that
for the same equation but with multiplicative space-time white noise, global
solutions exist if
b
grows no faster than
u\log u
and
\sigma
grow slower
than
u(\log u)^{1/4}
. Foondun and Nualart
[
FN21
]

showed that, analogous to deterministic ordinary differential equations, the
Osgood condition (
1.6
) on
b
characterizes finite time explosion
for the stochastic heat equation with additive noise on bounded domains in any
spatial dimension. In other words, the additive-noise stochastic heat equation
with bounded initial data will explode in finite time if and only if
b

satisfies (
1.6
). In contrast, Salins
[
Sal22a
]

demonstrated that if
b
does not satisfy condition (
1.6
), i.e.,

b
satisfies the following
infinite Osgood condition
holds:

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{\infty}\frac{1}{b(u)}\mathrm{d}u=+\infty\quad\text{for all $c>0$,}

(1.7)

then to guarantee the existence of global solutions (i.e., solutions of all
time), one can allow
\sigma
to grow superlinearly as long as it satisfies an
appropriate Osgood-type assumption. Shang and
Zhang
[
SZ22
]
studied a superlinear stochastic heat
equation on a bounded domain driven by a Brownian motion (namely,
space-independent white noise).

Finite time explosion for the superlinear stochastic wave equations has been
investigated in
[
FN22
]

and
[
MS21
]
, which proved sufficient conditions for
finite time explosion and those for global solutions, respectively. In both
works, the compact support property, which is inherited from the fundamental
solution of the wave equation, plays a crucial role.

The question of finite time explosion for the stochastic heat equation on
unbounded spatial domains is more complicated because solutions to the
stochastic heat equation can be unbounded in space in the sense that
\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x}|u(t,x)|=+\infty\right)=1
for any
t>0
. Shang and
Zhang
[
SZ21
]
showed that if
b
grows like
u\log(u)
and
if
\sigma
is bounded and Lipschitz, then there exist global solutions to the
stochastic heat equation on
\mathbb{R}
. Salins
[
Sal22
]
proved an
Osgood-type assumption on
b
, allowing for faster growth than
u\log u
,
implies the existence of global solutions when
\sigma~\equiv 1
. Chen and
Huang
[
CH23
]
investigated the existence of global
solutions to the stochastic heat equation defined on an unbounded spatial domain
under assumptions that guarantee that the solutions are bounded in space. The
current paper is a major improvement over the results
of
[
CH23
]
, examining scenarios where solutions remain
spatially bounded, while allowing for more general assumptions that accommodate
faster growth of the superlinear
b
and
\sigma
terms.

For the stochastic noise, one commonly assumes the following strengthened

Dalang’s condition
:

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\alpha}\coloneqq(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1-\alpha}}<\infty\,,\quad\text{for some $0<\alpha<1$,}

(1.8)

where
\widehat{f}(\xi)
is the Fourier transform of
f
, namely,

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}x
. When
\alpha=0
, it reduces to a weaker
Dalang’s
condition

[
Dal99
]
:

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\beta)\coloneqq(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\widehat{f}(\mathrm{d}\xi)}{\beta+|\xi|^{2}}<+\infty\quad\text{for some and hence for all $\beta>0$.}

(1.9)

In order to get slightly stronger results, instead of
condition (
1.8
), we make the following slightly weaker assumption on
the noise:

Assumption 1.1
.

There exists
\alpha\in(0,1]
such that

\limsup_{s\downarrow 0}s^{1-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-s|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi<+\infty.

(1.10)

One can show that the strengthened Dalang’s condition (
1.8
) implies
Assumption
1.1
. In the one-dimensional space-time white noise setting,
Assumption
1.1
is satisfied with
\alpha=1/2
but the strengthened
Dalang’s condition (
1.8
) is satisfied only when
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
.

Regarding the drift term
b
and the diffusion coefficient
\sigma
, we make the
following Osgood-type assumptions following
[
Sal22b
]
.

Assumption 1.2
.

Assume that

1.

Both
b
and
\sigma
are locally Lipschitz continuous;

2.

b(0)=0
and
\sigma(0)=0
;

3.

There exists a positive, increasing function

h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that:

(a)

(Superlinear growth)
\mathbb{R}_{+}\ni u\to u^{-1}h(u)
is non-decreasing
with
\displaystyle\lim_{u\to 0}u^{-1}h(u)\geq\exp\left(1/\alpha\right)
;

(b)

(Osgood-type condition of the infinite type)
\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h(u)}\mathrm{d}u=+\infty
;

(c)

For all
u\in\mathbb{R}
,
|b(u)|\leq h(|u|)
;

(d)

For all
u\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

|\sigma(u)|\leq|u|^{1-\alpha/2}(h(|u|))^{\alpha/2}\left(\log\left(\frac{h(|u|)}{|u|}\right)\right)^{-1/2};

(1.11)

where the constant
\alpha
in parts (a) and (d) is given in
Assumption
1.1
.

Remark 1.3
.

In
[
Sal22a
]
, Salins assumed that
|\sigma(u)|\leq|u|^{1-\gamma}(h(|u|))^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma<\alpha/2
(where the paper
uses the notation
1-\eta=\alpha
). Our proof is based on exponential tail
estimates; see Lemma
2.4
below. The condition on

\sigma
, given by (
1.11
), allows for a faster growth rate for

\sigma
than that in
[
Sal22a
]
. The arguments based on the
exponential tail estimates can also be applied in the finite domain setting.

Let us introduce some notation. For
p\geq 1
, set

\displaystyle V_{p}\coloneqq L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\cdot\right\|_{V_{p}}\coloneqq\max\left(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\>\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

To guarantee that solutions to (
1.1
) remain bounded in space, we make
the following assumption on the initial data.

Assumption 1.4
.

The initial data
u_{0}\in V_{p}
for some
p\geq 2
.

Remark 1.5
.

The space of bounded
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-functions, namely,
V_{p}
, is monotone
increasing with respect to
p

\phi\in V_{p}
implies that
\phi\in V_{q}

for all
q\geq p\geq 1
; see Lemma
2.1
below. In
Assumption
1.4
, we require
p\geq 2
because we need to apply the

Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality
in Banach space; see
Appendix.

The aim of this present paper is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that the noise satisfies Assumption
1.1
with some
\alpha\in(0,1]
, the initial condition satisfies Assumption
1.4
for some

p\geq(2+d)/\alpha
, and that
b(\cdot)
and
\sigma(\cdot)
satisfy
Assumption
1.2
. Then, we have the following:

1.

There exists a unique mild solution
u(t,x)
to (
1.1
) for all

(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.

Moreover, if
f
satisfies the strengthened Dalang’s
condition (
1.8
) with some
\alpha\in(0,1]
, then the solution

u(t,x)
is Hölder continuous:
u\in C^{\alpha/2-,\,\alpha-}\left((0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
a.s., where
C^{\alpha_{1}-,\,\alpha_{2}-}\left(D\right)
denotes the Hölder continuous function on the
space-time domain
D
with exponents
\alpha_{1}-\epsilon
and

\alpha_{2}-\epsilon
in time and space, respectively, for any small

\epsilon>0
.

The conditions that
b(0)=0
,
\sigma(0)=0
and

guarantee that the
solution to (
1.1
) remains in
V^{p}
almost surely. This allows us to
perform a localization procedure on the solution. We can prove that solution
cannot explode in finite time by proving that a sequence of hitting times

\tau_{n}\uparrow+\infty
.

Our main result—Theorem
1.6
—provides the optimal condition on the
drift term
b
, which can be seen from the following theorem:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a locally Lipschitz continuous,
nondecreasing and convex function that vanishes at zero (
b(0)=0
). Suppose
that
\sigma(\cdot)
vanishes at zero and is bounded, namely,
\sigma(0)=0

and
\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}|\sigma(u)|\leq K

1.1
, if

satisfies the finite Osgood

1.6
), then for any

, there exists some

u_{0}(\cdot)\in V_{p}
to (

) such that

Remark 1.8
.

Theorem
1.7
might hold true in certain cases without the

Remark 1.9

Some preliminaries

In the following,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p}}
refers to

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}
with
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\left\|X\right\|_{p}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left(|X|^{p}\right)^{1/p}
.

We will often use the following simple but useful property of
V_{p}
, which shows
that the space
V_{p}
is monotone in
p
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
1\leq p\leq r<\infty
, then
V_{p}\subseteq V_{r}\subseteq L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and for any
v\in V_{p}
,

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{r}}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{V_{r}}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}}.

(2.1)

Proof.

The proof is straightforward. Let
r\in[p,+\infty)
. Then notice that

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{r}}^{r}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(x)|^{r}\mathrm{d}x
, which is less than

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\right)\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(x)|^{r-p}=\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{r-p}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}}^{r}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
b
and
\sigma
in (
1.1
) satisfy
Assumption
1.2
. For any
p\geq 1
, if
v\in V_{p}
, then the
compositions
f(v)\in V_{p}
and
\sigma(v)\in V_{p}
. Moreover,

\left\|b(v)\right\|_{V_{p}}\leq h\left(\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\sigma(v)\right\|_{V_{p}}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}}^{1-\alpha/2}h\left(\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(\log\left(\frac{h(\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}})}{\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}}}\right)\right)^{-1/2}.

(2.2)

Proof.

We first prove the
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm. We claim that

\displaystyle\left\|b(v)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq h\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\quad\text{and}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\sigma(v)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\alpha/2}h\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(\log\left(\frac{h(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}})}{\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}\right)\right)^{-1/2}.

Since
\left|b\left(v(x)\right)\right|\leq|h(v(x))|
for all
x\geq 0
, the
first inequality in (
2.3
) is proved by taking supremum on both
sides of this inequality proves. As for the second inequality
in (
2.3
), since
x\mapsto\frac{h(x)}{x}
is nondecreasing,
without loss of generality we may assume that
g(x)\coloneqq\frac{h(x)}{x}

is differentiable. By Assumption
1.2
, we see that the function

F(x)\coloneqq\frac{g(x)^{\alpha/2}}{\sqrt{\log\left(g(x)\right)}}
is
nondecreasing for
x\geq 0
since

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)=\frac{g(x)^{-1+\alpha/2}g^{\prime}(x)}{2\alpha\left[\log\left(g(x)\right)\right]^{3/2}}\times\left(\log\left(g(x)\right)-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\geq 0.

This proves the second inequality in (
2.3
).

The interesting part of the proof is showing that the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
norm is
bounded. To this end, observe that

\left\|b(v)\right\|^{p}_{L^{p}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|b\left(v(x)\right)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(x)|^{p}\left(\frac{h\left(|v(x)|\right)}{|v(x)|}\right)^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{h\left(|v(x)|\right)}{|v(x)|}\right)^{p},

where the first inequality is obtained by using the bound on
b
given in Assumption
1.2
.
From the assumption that
v\mapsto\frac{h(v)}{v}
is increasing, the above
display is bounded by

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\left(\frac{h\left(\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)}{\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}\right)^{p}\leq\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}}^{p}\left(\frac{h(\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}})}{\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}}}\right)^{p}.

(2.4)

Therefore, we can conclude that
\left\|b(v)\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq h(\left\|v\right\|_{V_{p}})
.
Combining this with the first relation in (
2.3
) proves the
first inequality in (
2.2
). The argument for the case of
\sigma
is
similar and one needs to use Assumption

Lemma 2.4

(Exponential estimates)
.

Assume that
\Phi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be an adapted and jointly
measurable random field and
p\geq 2
. If for any
T\geq 0
, there exists a
constant
M=M(T,p)\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\Phi(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\leq M,\quad\text{a.s.},

then, there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
M
and
T
such that for
any
\delta>0

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)\Phi(s,y)W(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)\right\|_{V_{p}}>\delta\right)\leq C{\left(1+T^{-d/2}\right)}e^{-C\delta^{2}M^{-2}T^{-\alpha}}

(2.17)

where
\alpha
is from Assumption
1.1
.

Proof.

Denote
Z(t,x)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G(t-s,x-y)\Phi(s,y)W(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)
. Fix an arbitrary
\lambda>0
. From Taylor series, we see that

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|Z(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\right)\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|Z(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}^{k}\right].

We claim that

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|Z(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\right)\right]\leq\left(1+T^{d/2}\right)T^{-d/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{k}C^{k}M^{k}T^{k\alpha/2}k^{k/2}}{k!}.

(2.18)

Indeed, the above inequality (
2.18
) follows from the moment
estimates in part (2) of Lemma
2.3
for
k>\max\left(p,(d+2)/\alpha\right)
. If
k\leq\max\left(p,(d+2)/\alpha\right)
, one can use
the Jensen inequality and then pick the leading constant big enough. This
proves the claim in (
2.18
).

In order to transform the summation in (
2.18
) into an exponential
form, we apply Stirling’s approximation
k!\sim\left(k/e\right)^{k}\sqrt{2\pi k}
to see that

\frac{k^{k/2}}{k!}\times\Gamma\left(k/2+1\right)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\sqrt{e/2}\right)^{k},

which implies that for some universal constant
\Theta>0

\displaystyle\frac{k^{k/2}}{k!}\leq\frac{\Theta^{k+1}}{\Gamma\left(k/2+1\right)},\quad\text{for all $k=0,1,2,\cdots$}.

Therefore, from (
2.18
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|Z(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+T^{-d/2}\right)\Theta\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Theta^{k}C^{k}\lambda^{k}M^{k}T^{k\alpha/2}}{\Gamma\left(k/2+1\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(1+T^{-d/2}\right)\Theta\exp\left(\Theta^{2}C^{2}\lambda^{2}M^{2}T^{\alpha}\right)\left[1+\text{Erf}\left(\Theta C\lambda MT^{\alpha}\right)\right],

where

is the error function and the equality can be found,
e.g., in Formula 7.2.6 in
[
Olv+10
]
. Notice that

\text{Erf}\left(x\right)\leq 1
. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|Z(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\right)\right]\leq 2\Theta\left(1+T^{-d/2}\right)\exp\left(\Theta^{2}C^{2}\lambda^{2}M^{2}T^{\alpha}\right),

(2.19)

Finally, we can derive the exponential tail estimates by the Chebyshev
inequality: for any
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|Z(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}>\delta\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\exp\left(\lambda\left\|Z(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\right)>\exp(\lambda\delta)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\lambda\delta\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\lambda\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|Z(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\Theta\left(1+T^{-d/2}\right)\exp\left(\Theta^{2}C^{2}\lambda^{2}M^{2}T^{\alpha}-\lambda\delta\right)

\displaystyle=2\Theta\left(1+T^{-d/2}\right)\exp\left(\Theta^{2}C^{2}M^{2}T^{\alpha}\left(\lambda-\frac{\delta}{2\Theta^{2}C^{2}M^{2}T^{\alpha}}\right)^{2}-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4\Theta^{2}C^{2}M^{2}T^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\Theta\left(1+T^{-d/2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{4\Theta^{2}C^{2}M^{2}T^{\alpha}}\right),

which proves Lemma
2.4

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{0}^{1/\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\beta s}e^{-s|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{1/\beta}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\beta s}e^{-s|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle~\eqqcolon I_{1}+I_{2}.

According to Assumption
1.1
,

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq C\int_{0}^{1/\beta}s^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}s=\frac{C}{\beta^{\alpha}}\,,

and similarly,

\displaystyle I_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{e^{-(2\beta+|\xi|^{2})\frac{1}{\beta}}}{2\beta+|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\frac{1}{2\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}\times\frac{1}{\beta}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\frac{C}{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^{\alpha-1}=\frac{C}{\beta^{\alpha}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\max\left(\inf\left\{\beta>0:a\Upsilon(2\beta)<\frac{1}{4}\right\}\,,\inf\left\{\beta>0:\frac{b}{2\beta^{2}}<\frac{1}{4}\right\}\right)\leq C\max\left(a^{1/\alpha},b^{1/2}\right)<\infty\,.

Finally, replacing
a
and
b
by
8pL_{\sigma}^{2}
and
L_{b}^{2}
, respectively,
proves part (1).
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Now we are ready to prove the main result – Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

The proof follows the same strategy as that in
[
Sal22b
]
.
First we define the cutoff functions for
b
and
\sigma
:

b_{n}(u)\coloneqq\begin{cases}b(-3^{n})&\text{ if }u<-3^{n}\\
b(u)&\text{ if }|u|\leq 3^{n}\\
b(3^{n})&\text{ if }u>3^{n}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{n}(u)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\sigma(-3^{n})&\text{ if }u<-3^{n}\\
\sigma(u)&\text{ if }|u|\leq 3^{n}\\
\sigma(3^{n})&\text{ if }u>3^{n}\end{cases},\quad\text{respectively.}

Since both
b_{n}(\cdot)
and
\sigma_{n}(\cdot)
are globally Lipschitz
continuous, by part (2) of Theorem
2.5
, for
p\geq(2+d)/\alpha
,
there is a unique
V_{p}
–valued solution solving

\displaystyle u_{n}(t,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t}(x-y)u_{0}(y)\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)b_{n}(u_{n}(s,y))\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma_{n}(u_{n}(s,y))W(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y).

Denote the following sequence of stopping times

\displaystyle\tau_{n}\coloneqq\inf\left\{t>0:\>\left\|u_{n}\left(t,\cdot\right)\right\|_{V_{p}}>3^{n}\right\}.

(3.1)

It is easy to check that the solutions are
consistent
in the sense
that
u_{n}(t,x)=u_{m}(t,x)
for all
t<\tau_{n}
whenever
n<m
. We can define a

local mild solution
to (
1.1
) by setting

u(t,x)\coloneqq u_{n}(t,x)\quad\text{when $t<\tau_{n}$}.

This local mild solution will exist until the explosion time

\tau_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{n}\tau_{n}
. A local solution is called a

global solution
if
\tau_{\infty}=\infty
with probability one.

We build the deterministic sequence

a_{n}\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{\Theta 3^{n+1}}{h\left(3^{n+1}\right)},\frac{1}{n}\right\},

(3.2)

with the constant
\Theta\in{(0,1/3)}
to be determined later. Just like
in
[
Sal22b
]
, the Osgood condition
\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h(u)}du=+\infty
guarantees that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}=+\infty.

Our goal is to show that the tripling times are bounded below by this
deterministic sequence
\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}\geq a_{n}
for all large
n
, which
implies that there is a global solution. To this end, we derive the following
moment estimates.

Claim:
There exist constants
C>0
and
q>1
, both
independent of
n
, such that

\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}<a_{n}\right)\leq Cn^{-q},\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

(3.3)

Indeed, as mentioned previously, each
\tau_{n}
is well-defined and the
solution
u(\tau_{n},\cdot)\in V_{p}
. Therefore, we can restart the process at
time
\tau_{n}
. For all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define

\displaystyle U_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t}(x-y)u(\tau_{n},y)\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle I_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)b(u(\tau_{n}+s,y))1_{\{s\in[0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]\}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle Z_{n}(t,x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(u(\tau_{n}+s,y))1_{\{s\in[0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]\}}W\left((\tau_{n}+\mathrm{d}s),\mathrm{d}y\right).

Then for all
t\in[0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]
,

\displaystyle u(\tau_{n}+t,x)=U_{n}(t,x)+I_{n}(t,x)+Z_{n}(t,x)

(3.4)

Furthermore, the presence of the indicator function
1_{\{s\in[0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]\}}
in the definitions of
I_{n}(t,x)
and
Z_{n}(t,x)

guarantees that the integrands are bounded in
V_{p}
–norm.

Because
p_{t}(\cdot)
is a probability density, it follows from Young’s
inequality for convolutions that for any
t>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|U_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\leq\left\|u(\tau_{n},\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}=3^{n}.

(3.5)

Because of the definition of the stopping time
\tau_{n}

in (
3.1
) and Lemma
2.2
, we see that

\left\|b(u(\tau_{n}+s,y))1_{\{s\in[0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]\}}\right\|_{V_{p}}\leq h(3^{n+1}).

(3.6)

Therefore, Lemma
2.3
with
M=h(3^{n+1})
guarantees that for
t\in[0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]
,

\left\|I_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\leq th(3^{n+1}).

(3.7)

In particular, if
t\in[0,a_{n}\wedge(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n})]
, then by the
definition of
a_{n}
in (
3.2
), we have that

|I_{n}(t,\cdot)|\leq a_{n}h(3^{n+1})\leq 3^{n}.

(3.8)

The event
\{\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}<{a_{n}}\}
can only occur if

\left\|u(\tau_{n}+t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}>3^{n+1}
for some
t\in(0,a_{n})
. But
because
\left\|U_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}
and
\left\|I_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}
are each
less than
3^{n}
if
t\in[0,{a_{n}}\wedge(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n})]
, the

V_{p}
–norm can only triple in this short amount of time if the stochastic term
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,a_{n+1}\wedge(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n})]}\left\|Z_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}>3^{n}.

Because of the definition of the stopping time
\tau_{n}
,
(
3.1
) and Lemma
2.2
, the
V
–norm of the
integrand of the stochastic integral is bounded with probability one by

\left\|\sigma(u(\tau_{n}+s,y))1_{\{s\in[0,\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}]\}}\right\|_{V_{p}}\leq 3^{(n+1)(1-\alpha/2)}\left(h(3^{n+1})\right)^{\alpha/2}\left(\log\left(\frac{h(3^{n+1})}{3^{n+1}}\right)\right)^{-1/2}

(3.9)

Using the exponential estimate (
2.17
) with

\displaystyle T=a_{n},\quad\delta=3^{n},\quad\text{and}\quad M=3^{(n+1)(1-\alpha/2)}\left[h\left(3^{n+1}\right)\right]^{\alpha/2}\left(\log\left(\frac{h(3^{n+1})}{3^{n+1}}\right)\right)^{-1/2},

we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}<a_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,a_{n}]}\left\|Z_{n}(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}\geq 3^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+a_{n}^{-d/2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{C3^{2(n+1)}\log\left(\frac{h(3^{n+1})}{3^{n+1}}\right)}{3^{2(n+1)}\left(\frac{h(3^{n+1})}{3^{n+1}}\right)^{\alpha}a_{n}^{\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+a_{n}^{-d/2}\right)\exp\left(-C\Theta^{-\alpha}\left\lvert\log\left(a_{n}/\Theta\right)\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ca_{n}^{C\Theta^{-\alpha}-d/2}\,.

The second-to-last inequality in the above display is a consequence of the
definition of
a_{n}
and (
3.2
), which guarantees that
a_{n}\leq\frac{\Theta 3^{n+1}}{h(3^{n+1})}
. Now set
q:=C\Theta^{-\alpha}-d/2
and
choose
\Theta\in(0,1/3)
small enough so that

. Then we obtain that

\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}<a_{n}\right)\leq Ca_{n}^{q}.

(3.10)

From the definition of
a_{n}
in (
3.2
), we also know that
a_{n}\leq 1/n
. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}<a_{n}\right)\leq Cn^{-q}.

(3.11)

This proves the claim in (
3.3
).

Finally, we can prove the main result. From the claim in (
3.3
),

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}<a_{n}\right)\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-q}<+\infty.

(3.12)

By the Borel-Cantelli Lemma, with probability one there exists a random

N(\omega)
such that for all
n\geq N(\omega)
,
\tau_{n+1}-\tau_{n}\geq a_{n}
. Because
\sum a_{n}=+\infty
this implies that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n}\tau_{n}=+\infty\right)=1

(3.13)

proving that the solutions cannot explode in finite time. This completes the
proof of Theorem
1.6
.
∎

An explosion example—the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We will prove this theorem via contradiction. Fix an arbitrary

. We assume that
the conclusion is false, namely, for all
u_{0}\in V_{p}
,

\displaystyle\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}<\infty,\quad\text{a.s. for all $t>0$,}

(4.1)

the initial condition of the following form, where
p_{1}(x)
is the heat kernel from (

),

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\Theta\>p_{1}(x),\quad\text{for some $\Theta>0$.}

(4.2)

It is clear that
u_{0}\in V_{p}
. The proof consists of the following two steps.

Step 1.
In this step, we claim that
under (
4.1
), there exists
\Theta_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{1/2}\geq 2L\right)>0,\quad\text{for all $L>0$ and $\Theta\geq\Theta_{0}$}.

(4.3)

Indeed, let
t\in(0,1)
. Multiply
p_{1-t}(x)
on both sides
of (
1.4
) and integrate over

to obtain

\displaystyle Y_{t}=Y_{0}+D_{t}+M_{t}\quad\text{a.s.~for all $t\in(0,1]$,}

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}Y_{t}&\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(t,x)p_{1-t}(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{with}\quad Y_{0}=(p_{1}*u_{0})(0)=\Theta\left(4\pi\right)^{-d/2},\\

The boundedness assumption on
\sigma
ensures that
M_{t}
is a martingale.
Assumption in (
4.1
) guarantees that
Y_{t}
is well
defined since

\displaystyle 0\leq Y_{t}\leq\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{V_{p}}<\infty,\quad\text{a.s. for all $t>0$.}

(4.5)

Note that the nonnegativity of
Y_{t}
comes from the comparison principle
(see
[
GM94
,
CH19
]
and references therein), which requires
conditions such as
\sigma(0)=0
and
b(0)=0
. However, we will show that

X_{t}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})
will blow up in at
t=1/2
provided that
\Theta
is
large enough, which then implies the claim in (
4.3
).

It remains to show the blow-up of
X_{t}
. By treating
p_{1-s}(y)\mathrm{d}yd\mathbb{P}
as
a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega
, we can apply Jensen’s
inequality to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(D_{t}\right)\geq\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}s\>b\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{d}y\>p_{1-s}(y)u(s,y)\right]\right)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}s\>b\left(\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{s}\right]\right),

from which we obtain the following integral inequality

\displaystyle X_{t}\geq\Theta(4\pi)^{-d/2}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, by the finite Osgood condition (
1.6
), for some
Y_{0}>0
,

blows up in finite time. By increasing the value of
\Theta
whenever
necessary, one can ensure that

. This completes the proof of
the claim in (
4.3
).

Step 2.
Notice that
Y_{t}
in (

) can be
equivalently written as

\displaystyle Y_{t}=Y_{1/2}+D_{t}^{*}+M_{t}^{*}\quad\text{a.s.~for all $t\in(1/2,1]$,}

(4.6)

where the initial condition

is finite a.s. thanks
to (

\displaystyle D_{t}^{*}\coloneqq\int_{1/2}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{1-s}(y)b\left(u(s,y)\right)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{and}\quad M_{t}^{*}\coloneqq\int_{1/2}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{1-s}(y)\sigma\left(u(s,y)\right)W(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y).

Step 2-1.
As in Step 1, the boundedness assumption on

\sigma
guarantees that

is a martingale. We
claim that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{t\in[1/2,1]}M_{t}^{*}\leq-L\Bigg|\mathcal{F}_{1/2}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{L^{2}}{2C_{f}\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}\right),\quad\text{a.s.~for all $L>0$,}

(4.7)

where

\displaystyle C_{f}\coloneqq\int_{1/2}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}\>p_{1-s}(y_{1})p_{1-s}(y_{2})f(y_{1}-y_{2})<\infty.

(4.8)

1.1
guarantees the finiteness of the

. Let
\lambda>0
be some constant to be chosen later. Since

\left\{\exp\left(-\lambda M_{t}^{*}\right):\>t\geq 1/2\right\}
is a
submartingale, by Doob’s submartingale inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{t\in[1/2,1]}M_{t}^{*}\leq-L\Bigg|\mathcal{F}_{1/2}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{t\in[1/2,1]}e^{-\lambda M_{t}^{*}}>e^{\lambda L}\Bigg|\mathcal{F}_{1/2}\right)\leq e^{-\lambda L}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\lambda M_{1}^{*}}\Bigg|\mathcal{F}_{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg(\exp\bigg\{-\lambda L+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}\int_{1/2}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{2}\>

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times p_{1-s}(y_{1})p_{1-s}(y_{2})\sigma(u(s,y_{1}))\sigma\left(u(s,y_{2})\right)f(y_{1}-y_{2})\bigg\}\bigg|\mathcal{F}_{1/2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\lambda L+\frac{1}{2}C_{f}\lambda^{2}\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right),\quad\text{a.s.,}

(4.9)

where in the last inequality we have used the fact that

is nonnegative.
Optimizing the constant
\lambda
in (
4
) proves the claim
in (
4.7
).

Step 2-2.
Consider the following deterministic equation,

\displaystyle\widehat{Y}_{t}=L+\int_{1/2}^{t}b\left(\widehat{Y}_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s,\quad\text{for $t\geq 1/2$.}

Since

satisfies the finite Osgood condition (
1.6
),
when
L>0
is large enough, the solution to the above equation will explode before
time

. In the following, we fix this constant

.

Next for all
t\in[1/2,1]
, another application of Jensen’s inequality with
respect to the measure
p_{1-s}(y)\mathrm{d}y
to in the term

in (

\displaystyle Y_{t}\geq\left(Y_{1/2}+M_{t}^{*}\right)+\int_{1/2}^{t}b\left(Y_{s}\right)\mathrm{d}s,\quad\text{a.s.~for all $t\in(1/2,1]$.}

\Theta>\Theta_{0}
. We claim that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{L})>0\quad\text{with}\quad\Omega_{L}\coloneqq\left\{Y_{1/2}+M_{t}^{*}\geq L:\>\text{for all $t\in[1/2,1]$}\right\}.

Indeed, by the claims in (

) and (
4.7
), we see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{L})

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{Y_{1/2}\geq 2L\right\}\cap\left\{\inf_{t\in[1/2,1]}M_{t}^{*}>-L\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{1/2}\geq 2L\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{t\in[1/2,1]}M_{t}^{*}>-L\bigg|Y_{1/2}\geq 2L\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{1/2}\geq 2L\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{L^{2}}{2C_{f}\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}\right)\right)>0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle Y_{t}\geq L+\int_{1/2}^{t}b(Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s,\quad\text{a.s. on $\Omega_{L}$ for all $t\in[1/2,1]$.}

Since

is nondecreasing, we see that
\widehat{Y}_{t}
provides a

Y_{t}
in the sense that

a.s. on

\Omega_{L}

with

,
which contradicts with (

1.

for each
(s,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\psi(s,\cdot,x)\in H
;

2.

for each
s>0
,
\left\|\psi(s,\cdot,\circ)\right\|_{H}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Then, for all
k>2
and
t>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(s,y,\circ)W(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}y)\right\|_{L^{p}}\right\|_{k}\leq C\sqrt{k}\left\|\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\left\|\psi(s,\cdot,\circ)\right\|_{H}\right\|^{2}_{L^{p}}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{k}\,,

(A.3)

where the constant
C
does not depend on
k
. Note that
inequality (
A.3
) is nontrivial only when the right-hand side of
the inequality is finite.

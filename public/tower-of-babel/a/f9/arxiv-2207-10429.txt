Nonrelativistic Transport Theory from Vorticity Dependent Quantum Kinetic Equation
Ömer F. Dayi, S. Efe Gürleyen

Abstract
We study the three-dimensional transport theory of massive spin-1/2 fermions resulting from the vorticity dependent quantum kinetic equation. This quantum kinetic equation has been introduced to take account of noninertial properties of rotating coordinate frames. We show that it is the appropriate relativistic kinetic equation which provides the vorticity dependent semiclassical transport equations of the three-dimensional Wigner function components. We establish the semiclassical kinetic equations of a linearly independent set of components. By means of them, kinetic equations of the chiral scalar distribution functions are derived. They furnish the 3D kinetic theory which permits us to study the vector and axial vector current densities by focusing on the mass corrections to the chiral vortical and separation effects.



I
Introduction

Transport of Dirac fermions in the presence of external electromagnetic fields can be studied by means of the covariant Wigner function which obeys the quantum kinetic equation (QKE)
Elze et al. 1986
;
Vasak et al. 1987
. The
Wigner function can be decomposed into some covariant fields whose equations of motion follow from the QKE.
Founded on these field equations, one derives relativistic transport theories of Dirac particles. A brief overview of the covariant Wigner function approach was given in
Gao et al. 2021
and recently it has been reviewed in details in
Hidaka et al. 2022
. Relativistic formalism has the advantage of being manifestly Lorenz invariant. Nevertheless, nonrelativistic transport equations are necessary for being able to start with initial distribution functions and construct solutions of transport equations
Bialynicki-Birula et al. 1991
;
Zhuang and Heinz 1998
;
Ochs and Heinz 1998
. There exist some different methods of formulating nonrelativistic kinetic theories of Dirac particles. One of these methods is to construct the four-dimensional (4D) transport equations of a set of covariant fields and then integrate them over the zeroth-component of four-momentum, so that the three-dimensional (3D) transport equations which are correlative to the 4D ones are extracted. Another method is to integrate all of the quantum kinetic equations of the covariant fields over the zeroth-component of four-momentum at the beginning and then derive the nonrelativistic transport theory from these 3D quantum kinetic equations
Zhuang and Heinz 1996
;
Ochs and Heinz 1998
. This is also called the equal-time formalism.
There also exists a strictly 3D approach of acquiring a transport equation of Dirac particles which does not refer to the Wigner function
Dayi et al. 2017
.

Quarks of the quark-gluon plasma formed in heavy-ion collisions are treated as massless
Gyulassy and McLerran 2005
;
Shuryak 2005
. Thus, chiral kinetic theory (CKT) is useful to inspect their dynamical features
Kharzeev et al. 2008
;
Fukushima et al. 2008
;
Kharzeev and Zhitnitsky 2007
;
Metlitski and Zhitnitsky 2005
;
Jensen et al. 2013
;
Son and Surowka 2009
;
Liang and Wang 2005
;
Becattini et al. 2008
;
Gao et al. 2012
.
The QKE of the relativistic Wigner function generates the anomalous magnetic effects as well as the vorticity effects correctly
Gao et al. 2012
. It is worth noting that the vorticity of fluid matches the angular velocity of the fluid in the comoving frame. The QKE possesses an explicit dependence on the electromagnetic fields but not on the vorticity of fluid.
When the Wigner function is expressed in the Clifford algebra basis, the QKE gives a set of equations for the chiral vector fields. In solving some of these equations one introduces the frame four-vector
Hidaka et al. 2017
;
Hidaka et al. 2018
;
Huang et al. 2018
. It can be identified with the comoving frame velocity which also appears in equilibrium distribution functions. Derivatives of the comoving frame four-velocity generate terms depending on the vorticity. These are the sources of vorticity dependence in the relativistic QKE formulations of the massless fermions. One cannot generate noninertial forces like the Coriolis force within this formalism. However, in
Stephanov and Yin 2012
, vortical effects were derived by using the similarity between the Lorentz and the Coriolis forces. This formulation has been shown to result in a rotating coordinate frame from the first principles
Huang and Sadofyev 2019
. To build in this similarity, a modification of QKE by means of enthalpy current was introduced in
Dayi and Kilinçarslan 2018
. The discrepancy in treating magnetic and vortical effects reflects itself drastically, especially in 3D CKT when both electromagnetic fields and vorticity are taken into account. The vorticity dependent quantum kinetic equation (VQKE) was shown to yield a 3D CKT which does not depend on the spatial coordinates
Dayi and Kilinçarslan 2018
. It is consistent with the chiral anomaly and generates the chiral magnetic and vortical effects and the Coriolis force. The underlying Lagrangian formalism which yields VQKE was presented in
Ö. F. Dayi, and E.
Kilinçarslan, 2021
.

Constituent quarks of the quark-gluon plasma created in heavy-ion collisions are approximately massless. Thus, to get a better understanding of their dynamical properties, one needs to uncover the mass corrections to chiral theories. Covariant kinetic theories of massive spin-1/2 particles have been studied in terms of QKE within two different approaches in
Weickgenannt et al. 2019
;
Hattori et al. 2019
. In principle, nonrelativistic transport equations can be provided by integrating the 4D kinetic equations. But, for massive fermions, extracting the 3D kinetic equations which are correlative to the 4D kinetic equations can only be done under some simplifying approximations as they have been shown within the VQKE approach in
Dayi and Kilinçarslan 2020
;
Ö. F. Dayi, and E.
Kilinçarslan, 2021
.
We have already mentioned that there also exists another nonrelativistic approach which is the so-called equal-time formalism. By integrating the equations of the relativistic Wigner function components over the zeroth-component of momentum, one sets up the equations of the components of the 3D Wigner function. Then, one employs these 3D equations to derive nonrelativistic kinetic equations of Dirac particles in the presence of the external electromagnetic fields. This has been studied in
Wang et al. 2019
by developing the original formulation of
Zhuang and Heinz 1996
. In contrast to the 4D Wigner function approaches, this 3D formalism does not generate vortical effects. Because, without solving some of the equations of the 4D Wigner function components one cannot generate vorticity dependent terms. Therefore, to obtain a similar 3D approach by taking account of vorticity of the fluid, the QKE of the Wigner function should possess an explicit dependence on the fluid vorticity. The VQKE is the unique covariant formalism which has this property

II
Vorticity Dependent Quantum Kinetic Equation

The quantum kinetic equation for a fluid in the comoving frame with the four-velocity
u_{\mu};

u_{\mu}u^{\mu}=1,
whose linear acceleration vanishes,
u_{\nu}\partial^{\nu}u_{\mu}=0,
is

\left[\gamma_{\mu}\left(\pi^{\mu}+\frac{i\hbar}{2}{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}\right)-m\right]W(x,p)=0.

(1)

Here,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}-j_{0}(\Delta)w^{\mu\nu}\partial_{p\nu},

(2)

\displaystyle\pi^{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle p^{\mu}-\frac{\hbar}{2}j_{1}(\Delta)w^{\mu\nu}\partial_{p\nu},

(3)

where
\partial^{\mu}\equiv\partial/\partial x_{\mu},

\partial_{p}^{\mu}\equiv\partial/\partial p_{\mu},
and

are spherical Bessel functions in

The 4D space-time derivative,
\partial_{\mu},
contained in

acts on

but not on the Wigner function,

On the contrary,

acts on the Wigner function, but not on

The action which generates (
1

Kilinçarslan, 2021

\displaystyle w_{\mu\nu}=(\partial_{\mu}h)u_{\nu}-(\partial_{\nu}h)u_{\mu}+\kappa h\Omega_{\mu\nu},

The fluid four-vorticity is defined as

The Wigner function can be written through the 16 generators of the Clifford algebra as

W=\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathcal{F}+i\gamma^{5}\mathcal{P}+\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{V}_{\mu}+\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}\right),

(5)

where

respectively, are the scalar, pseudoscalar, vector, axial-vector, and antisymmetric tensor components of the 4D Wigner function.
These covariant fields can be expanded in powers of the Planck constant:

\mathcal{C}_{a}=\sum_{n}\hbar^{n}\mathcal{C}^{(n)}_{a}.

(6)

We deal with the semiclassical approximation where only the zeroth- and first-order fields in
\hbar
are considered. Thus, to derive the equations which they satisfy, instead of (
2

3
), we only need to deal with

\displaystyle D^{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\mu}-w^{\mu\nu}\partial_{p\nu},

(7)

and

By plugging the decomposed Wigner function, (
5
), into the VQKE, (
1
), one derives the equations satisfied by the fields
\mathcal{C}_{a},
whose real parts are

\displaystyle p\cdot\mathcal{V}-m\mathcal{F}=0,

(8)

\displaystyle{p_{\mu}\mathcal{F}-\frac{\hbar}{2}D^{\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}-m\mathcal{V}_{\mu}=0},

(9)

\displaystyle{-\frac{\hbar}{2}D_{\mu}\mathcal{P}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p^{\nu}S^{\alpha\beta}+m\mathcal{A}_{\mu}=0},

(10)

\displaystyle{\frac{\hbar}{2}D_{[\mu}\mathcal{V}_{\nu]}-\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{\beta}-m\mathcal{S}_{\mu\nu}=0},

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2}D\cdot\mathcal{A}+m\mathcal{P}=0,

(12)

(13)

\displaystyle{p\cdot\mathcal{A}=0},

(14)

\displaystyle{\frac{\hbar}{2}D_{\mu}\mathcal{F}+p^{\nu}\mathcal{S}_{\nu\mu}=0},

(15)

\displaystyle{p_{\mu}\mathcal{P}+\frac{\hbar}{4}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}D^{\nu}\mathcal{S}^{\alpha\beta}=0},

(16)

\displaystyle{p_{[\mu}\mathcal{V}_{\nu]}+\frac{\hbar}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}D^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{\beta}=0}.

(17)

It can be observed that not all of the fields

are relevant to formulate a 4D kinetic theory. Depending on the choice of the independent set of fields, one establishes different relativistic kinetic theories

III
3D semiclassical transport and constraint equations

The equal-time transport theory of Dirac particles in the presence of external electromagnetic fields which has been proposed in
Bialynicki-Birula et al. 1991

Zhuang and Heinz 1996

Elze et al. 1986

p_{0}

V
Kinetic Theories of the right- and left-handed fermions

In heavy-ion collisions, because of considering the constituent quarks of the quark-gluon plasma as massless, one expects that the collective dynamics yield the chiral vortical and separation effects due to vorticity. We would like to study how the quark mass affects this picture. To study the mass corrections, we need the kinetic equations satisfied by the right- and left-handed distribution functions
f_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}},f_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}},
defined by

f_{\chi}=\frac{1}{2}(f_{0}+\chi f_{1}),

(58)

where
\chi=\{+,-\}
, and
f_{+}\equiv f_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}
and
f_{-}\equiv f_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}}
. However, the 3D kinetic equations (
53
), (
54
) and (
56
), (
57
) are given in terms of
f_{0}
and
\mathbf{g}_{0}.
Thus, we have to specify the spin current
\mathbf{g}_{0}
, by respecting the relations (
27
) and (
47
).
First, let the direction of the spin current be parallel to
\bm{p}.
Then, (
27
) implies that

\mathbf{g}_{0}^{(0)\pm}=\pm\frac{E_{p}}{\bm{p}^{2}}\bm{p}f_{1}^{(0)\pm}.

(59)

By plugging (
59
) into the classical kinetic equation of the spin current given by (
54
), we find

\Bigg(D_{t}\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\cdot\bm{\partial}_{\pm}^{(0)}\Bigg)f_{1}^{(0)\pm}=0.

(60)

Recall that it has the same form with the classical kinetic equation satisfied by
f_{0}^{(0)\pm}
, (
53
).
Additionally, (
59
) allows us to write the right-hand side of (
56
) in terms of
f_{1}^{(0)\pm}:

\displaystyle\Bigg(D_{t}\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\cdot\bm{\partial}_{\pm}^{(0)}\Bigg)f_{0}^{(1)\pm}=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{1}{2E_{p}\bm{p}^{2}}\Bigg({(1+\kappa)}(\bm{p}\times(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega}))-{\kappa}(\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{p})\bm{p}\Bigg)\cdot\bm{\nabla}f_{1}^{(0)\pm}

(61)

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\bm{p}^{2}}(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega})\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{p}f_{1}^{(0)\pm}.

Now, we desire to find the kinetic equation satisfied by
f_{1}^{(1)\pm}
. For this purpose, first observe that Eq. (
47
) can be solved as

\mathbf{g}_{0}^{(1)\pm}=\pm\frac{E_{p}\bm{p}}{\bm{p}^{2}}f_{1}^{(1)\pm}\mp\frac{\kappa\bm{\omega}}{2E_{p}}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}\pm E_{p}\bm{p}\times{\bm{F}}^{\pm},

\displaystyle-(\bm{p}\times(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega}))\cdot{\bm{F}}^{\pm}\mp\frac{\kappa\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}}{2E_{p}^{3}}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\nabla}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}

(63)

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa\bm{\omega}}{2E_{p}^{2}}\cdot\bm{p}\Bigg(D_{t}\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\cdot\bm{\partial}_{\pm}^{(0)}\Bigg)f_{0}^{(0)\pm}.

To have an equation compatible with (
61
), we choose
{\bm{F}}^{\pm}
to be

{\bm{F}}^{\pm}=\mp\frac{(\kappa+1)}{2E_{p}\bm{p}^{2}}\bm{\nabla}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2\bm{p}^{2}}\bm{\omega}\times\bm{\nabla}_{p}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}.

(64)

By inserting it into (
63
) one gets the kinetic equation

\displaystyle\Bigg(D_{t}\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\cdot\bm{\partial}_{\pm}^{(0)}\Bigg)f_{1}^{(1)\pm}=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{(\kappa+1)}{2E_{p}\bm{p}^{2}}(\bm{p}\times(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega}))\cdot\bm{\nabla}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}+\frac{(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})\kappa^{2}}{2\bm{p}^{2}}(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega})\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{p}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}

\displaystyle\mp\frac{\kappa\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{p}}{2E_{p}^{3}}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\nabla}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}+\frac{\kappa\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{p}}{2E_{p}^{2}}\Bigg(D_{t}\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\cdot\bm{\partial}_{\pm}^{(0)}\Bigg)f_{0}^{(0)\pm}.

53
). However, instead of doing that,
we add a similar vanishing term to the right-hand side of (
61
):

\displaystyle\Bigg(D_{t}\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\cdot\bm{\partial}_{\pm}^{(0)}\Bigg)f_{0}^{(1)\pm}=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{(\kappa+1)}{2E_{p}\bm{p}^{2}}(\bm{p}\times(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega}))\cdot\bm{\nabla}f_{1}^{(0)\pm}+\frac{(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})\kappa^{2}}{2\bm{p}^{2}}(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega})\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{p}f_{1}^{(0)\pm}

(66)

\displaystyle\mp\frac{\kappa}{2E_{p}}\frac{\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{p}}{\bm{p}^{2}}(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\nabla})f_{1}^{(0)\pm}+\frac{\kappa\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{p}}{2E_{p}^{2}}\Bigg(D_{t}\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\cdot\bm{\partial}_{\pm}^{(0)}\Bigg)f_{1}^{(0)\pm}.

Notice that adding the last term
is equivalent to a shift of

with the term
\frac{\kappa\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{p}}{2E_{p}^{2}}f_{1}^{(0)\pm}.
By combining (
65
) and (
66

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\Bigg(1-\hbar\frac{\chi\kappa(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})}{2E_{p}^{2}}\Bigg)\partial_{t}+\Bigg[\Bigg(1-\hbar\frac{\chi\kappa(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})}{2E_{p}^{2}}\Bigg)(\kappa+1)-\hbar\frac{\chi(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})\kappa^{2}}{2\bm{p}^{2}}\Bigg](\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega})\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{p}

(67)

\displaystyle+\Bigg[\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\mp\hbar\frac{\chi(\kappa+1)}{2E_{p}\bm{p}^{2}}\bm{p}\times(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega})\pm\hbar\frac{\chi\kappa m^{2}(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})}{4E_{p}^{3}\bm{p}^{2}}\bm{p}\Bigg]\cdot\bm{\nabla}\Bigg\}f_{\chi}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\pm\hbar\frac{\chi\kappa m^{2}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}}{4E_{p}^{3}\bm{p}^{2}}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\nabla}f_{-\chi}^{\pm}.

Therefore, we establish the kinetic theory

\Bigg[\sqrt{\eta}_{\chi}^{\pm}\partial_{t}+(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\mathbf{x}})^{\pm}_{\chi}\cdot\bm{\nabla}+(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\bm{p}})_{\chi}^{\pm}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{p}\Bigg]f_{\chi}^{\pm}=\pm\hbar\frac{\chi m^{2}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}}{4E_{p}^{3}\bm{p}^{2}}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\nabla}f_{-\chi}^{\pm},

(68)

with

\displaystyle\sqrt{\eta}_{\chi}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=1-\hbar\frac{\chi(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})}{2E_{p}^{2}},

(69)

\displaystyle(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\mathbf{x}})^{\pm}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{E_{p}}\pm\hbar\frac{\chi\bm{\omega}}{E_{p}}\mp\hbar\frac{\chi(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})\bm{p}}{4E_{p}}\Bigg(\frac{3}{\bm{p}^{2}}+\frac{1}{E_{p}^{2}}\Bigg),

(70)

\displaystyle(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\bm{p}})_{\chi}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\Bigg[2\Bigg(1-\hbar\frac{\chi(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})}{2E_{p}^{2}}\Bigg)-\hbar\frac{\chi(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})}{2\bm{p}^{2}}\Bigg](\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega}).

(71)

We set
\kappa=1
for acquiring the Coriolis force correctly. The term appearing on the right-hand side of (
68
) shows that for the massive fermions right- and left-handed distributions cannot be decoupled.

Getting inspiration from the left- and right-handed decompositions of the distribution functions,
f_{0}=f_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}+f_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}},\ f_{1}=f_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}-f_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}},
we write the shell shifts in (
39
) and (
40
) as

\displaystyle\Delta E_{f_{0}}^{\pm}=\Delta E_{f_{0}{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}}^{\pm}+\Delta E_{f_{0}{\scriptscriptstyle{L}}}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mp\frac{E_{p}\,\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}}{2\bm{p}^{2}}(f_{R}^{(0)\pm}-f_{L}^{(0)\pm}),

(72)

\displaystyle\Delta E_{f_{1}}^{\pm}=\Delta E_{f_{1}{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}}^{\pm}-\Delta E_{f_{1}{\scriptscriptstyle{L}}}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mp\frac{\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}}{2E_{p}}(f_{R}^{(0)\pm}+f_{L}^{(0)\pm}).

(73)

Hence, for the left- and right-handed fermions we define

\Delta E_{\chi}^{\pm}=\mp\frac{\chi}{4E_{p}}\Bigg(1+\frac{E_{p}^{2}}{\bm{p}^{2}}\Bigg)\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}.

(74)

Therefore, the dispersion relations are

\epsilon_{p,\chi}^{\pm}=\pm E_{p}\mp\hbar\frac{\chi}{4E_{p}}\Bigg(1+\frac{E_{p}^{2}}{\bm{p}^{2}}\Bigg)\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}.

(75)

The particle number current density can be written in terms of the equilibrium distribution function as

\bm{j}_{\chi}^{\pm}=\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\mathbf{x}})^{\pm}_{\chi}f_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(\epsilon_{p,\chi}^{\pm}).

(76)

Let the equilibrium distribution function be taken as the Fermi-Dirac distribution:

f_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(\epsilon_{p,\chi}^{\pm})=\frac{1}{e^{\pm(\epsilon_{p,\chi}^{\pm}-\mu_{\chi})/T}+1},

(77)

where
\mu_{\chi}
is the chiral chemical potential,
T
is the temperature, and we employed the dispersion relations (
75
).
We can expand (
77
) in Taylor series as

f_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(\epsilon_{p,\chi}^{\pm})\approx f_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(E_{p})\mp\hbar\frac{\chi}{4E_{p}}\Bigg(1+\frac{E_{p}^{2}}{\bm{p}^{2}}\Bigg)\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}\frac{df_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(E_{p})}{dE_{p}},

(78)

where

f_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(E_{p})=\frac{1}{e^{(E_{p}\mp\mu_{\chi})/T}+1}.

(79)

Notice that the equilibrium distribution function depends only on the magnitude of the momentum. Therefore, we can evaluate the angular part of the integral in (
76
), yielding

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{\chi}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\hbar\chi\bm{\omega}\int\frac{d|\bm{p}|}{24\pi^{2}}\bm{p}^{2}\Bigg[\Bigg(\pm\frac{8}{E_{p}}\pm\frac{m^{2}}{E_{p}^{3}}\Bigg)f_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(E_{p})-\Bigg(\frac{1}{E_{p}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bm{p}^{2}}\Bigg)\bm{p}^{2}\frac{df_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(E_{p})}{dE_{p}}\Bigg].

(80)

Since the classical terms vanish, the current densities are at the order of
\hbar.
Then, the vector and axial vector current densities,
\bm{j}_{\scriptscriptstyle{V}}=\bm{j}_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}+\bm{j}_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}},\ \ \bm{j}_{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}=\bm{j}_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}-\bm{j}_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}},
are accomplished as

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{{\scriptscriptstyle{V}},{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pm}\hbar\bm{\omega}\int\frac{d|\bm{p}|}{24\pi^{2}}\bm{p}^{2}\Bigg[\Bigg(\pm\frac{8}{E_{p}}\pm\frac{m^{2}}{E_{p}^{3}}\Bigg)f_{{\scriptscriptstyle{V}},{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}}^{eq\pm}(E_{p})-\Bigg(\frac{1}{E_{p}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\bm{p}^{2}}\Bigg)\bm{p}^{2}\frac{df_{{\scriptscriptstyle{V}},{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}}^{eq\pm}(E_{p})}{dE_{p}}\Bigg]

(81)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sigma_{{\scriptscriptstyle{V}},{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}}\bm{\omega}.

We introduced

f_{{\scriptscriptstyle{V}},{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{e^{(E_{p}\mp\mu_{R})/T}+1}\mp\frac{1}{e^{(E_{p}\mp\mu_{L})/T}+1}.

(82)

Observe that at zero temperature, the distribution functions transform into the Heaviside step function for positive energy particles and vanish for negative energy particles.
For simplicity, let us set
\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}}\equiv\mu
. Then, the vector current vanishes and the axial vector current gives

\lim_{T\rightarrow 0}\sigma_{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}=\frac{\hbar}{2\pi^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{3\mu^{2}-m^{2}}{3\mu}\sqrt{\mu^{2}-m^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\ln(\frac{\mu+\sqrt{\mu^{2}-m^{2}}}{m})\Bigg]\theta(\mu-m).

(83)

In the small mass limit we get

\lim_{T\rightarrow 0}\sigma^{+}_{A}=\frac{\hbar}{2\pi^{2}}\left[\mu\sqrt{\mu^{2}-m^{2}}-\frac{m^{2}}{3}\right]\theta(\mu-m).

(84)

This result is in harmony with the field theoretic calculations performed by means of the Kubo formula in
Buzzegoli et al. 2017
;
Lin and Yang 2018
when one ignores the last term. However, if one excludes the chemical potential terms only the
m^{2}
term survives. In fact, the latter is similar to the small mass correction obtained in curved space in
Flachi and Fukushima 2018
.

Although we do not consider the electromagnetic fields, by inspecting the kinetic equations obtained in
Wang et al. 2019
one can observe that the time evolution of spatial coordinates linear in the magnetic field can be acquired from (
70
) by substituting
\bm{\omega}
with
\bm{B}/E_{p}.
Hence, the related axial current will produce the finite mass corrections to the kinetic coefficient of the chiral separation effect.

Let us inspect the chiral (massless) limit: First of all, (
68
)-(
71
) generate the chiral kinetic theory

\Bigg[\sqrt{\eta}_{\chi}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}\partial_{t}+(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\mathbf{x}})^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}_{\chi}\cdot\bm{\nabla}+(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\bm{p}})_{\chi}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{p}\Bigg]f_{\chi}^{\pm}=0,

(85)

with

\displaystyle\sqrt{\eta}_{\chi}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}

\displaystyle=1-\hbar\frac{\chi\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{p}}{2\bm{p}^{2}},

(86)

\displaystyle(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\mathbf{x}})^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{\bm{p}}{|{\bm{p}}|}\mp\hbar\frac{\chi}{|{\bm{p}}|^{3}}\bm{p}(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})\pm\hbar\frac{\chi}{|{\bm{p}}|}\bm{\omega},

(87)

\displaystyle(\sqrt{\eta}\dot{\bm{p}})^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=2\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega}-\hbar\chi\frac{3(\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega})}{2\bm{p}^{2}}(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega}).

(88)

Then, (
75
) gives the dispersion relation for chiral particles as

\epsilon_{p,\chi}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}=\pm|{\bm{p}}|\mp\hbar\frac{\chi}{2}\hat{\bm{p}}\cdot\bm{\omega}.

(89)

It is consistent with the dispersion relation obtained in
Chen et al. 2014
;
Dayi et al. 2017
;
Liu et al. 2019
. Moreover, the dynamical evolution of the spatial coordinate vector, (
87
), coincides with the one established in
Dayi and Kilinçarslan 2018
.
Let the equilibrium distribution be given by the Fermi-Dirac distribution:

f_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(\epsilon_{p,\chi}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm})=\frac{1}{e^{\pm(\epsilon_{p,\chi}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}-\mu_{\chi})/T}+1},

(90)

Thus, the chiral particle number current densities are acquired as

\bm{j}_{\chi}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{C}}\pm}=\hbar\chi\bm{\omega}\int\frac{d|\bm{p}|}{3\pi^{2}}\Bigg(\pm{|\bm{p}|}f_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(|\bm{p}|)-\frac{1}{4}\bm{p}^{2}\frac{df_{\chi}^{eq\pm}(|\bm{p}|)}{d|\bm{p}|}\Bigg).

(91)

We can perform the integrals and obtain the vector and axial vector currents as

\bm{j}_{\scriptscriptstyle{V}}=\hbar\frac{\mu\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}}{2\pi^{2}}\bm{\omega},\ \ \bm{j}_{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}=\hbar\left(\frac{T^{2}}{12}+\frac{\mu^{2}+\mu^{2}_{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}}{4\pi^{2}}\right)\bm{\omega},

where
\mu=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}+\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}},

\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{A}}=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{R}}-\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle{L}}.

These coincide with the results reported in
Gao et al. 2012
. Therefore, we conclude that in the massless limit the chiral vector and separation effects are generated correctly.

VI
Discussions

The VQKE of the Wigner function leads to the transport equations of the components of the covariant Wigner function.
By integrating them over
p_{0},
we write the equations which the components of 3D Wigner function obey. They can be separated into the transport and constraint equations. The vector component of the covariant
D_{\mu}=(D_{t},\bm{D})
operator depends explicitly on
p_{0}
as in (
22
). Hence, also the transport equations depend explicitly on
p_{0},
in contrast to the transport equations which have been defined in
Zhuang and Heinz 1996
;
Wang et al. 2019
.
p_{0}
integrals are performed by employing the on-shell conditions of the covariant fields. Then,
\bm{D}
effectively becomes as in (
35
), which is very different from the
\bm{D}^{({\scriptscriptstyle{E}}{\scriptscriptstyle{M}})}
appearing in
Zhuang and Heinz 1996
;
Wang et al. 2019
. Therefore, it is not possible to generalize the method of
Wang et al. 2019
directly to our case. Nevertheless, to study the 3D transport and constraint equations, we follow the method proposed in
Wang et al. 2019
and let each component of the Wigner function satisfy a different on-shell condition at
\hbar
order.
We presented these shell shifts and by plugging them into the constraint equations we expressed the components of the 3D Wigner function at first order in terms of
f_{0},\mathbf{g}_{0}.
We consider
f_{0}
and
\mathbf{g}_{0}
as independent components. The main objective is to establish the semiclassical kinetic equations of the fields which are chosen as the independent set of components. After some cumbersome calculations we acquired them as in (
53
), (
54
) and (
56
), (
57
).

To accomplish the mass corrections to the chiral (massless) kinetic equations, we have fixed the spin current
\mathbf{g}_{0}
in terms of
f_{0}
and
f_{1}.
Then, we derived the kinetic equations of right- and left-handed distribution functions in (
67
), which provide us the kinetic theories of the right- and left-handed fermions. We acquired their dispersion relations and calculated particle number current densities by choosing the equilibrium distribution functions appropriately. We have shown that the massless case generates the chiral vortical and separation effects correctly. Therefore, we succeeded in accomplishing the mass corrections to the chiral effects.

In principle we can consider a system with the nonvanishing linear acceleration
a_{\mu}=u_{\nu}\partial^{\nu}u_{\mu},
by adding the term
(a_{\mu}u_{\nu}-a_{\nu}u_{\mu})
to
w_{\mu\nu}
given in (
4
).
The procedure which we employed here in obtaining mass shell shifts, relies on the solutions of the covariant equations reported in
Dayi and Kilinçarslan 2020
.
Hence, to deal with nonvanishing
a_{\mu},
one should first study solutions of the kinetic equations obeyed by the covariant Wigner function components with this altered
w_{\mu\nu},
which would be complicated.

A challenging future research direction is the study of 3D transport theory of VQKE in the presence of electromagnetic fields. As far as the contributions linear in electromagnetic fields and vorticity are concerned, this can simply be achieved by gathering the results obtained here and the ones reported in
Wang et al. 2019
, as we discussed after (
84
). However, establishing kinetic equations of
f_{0}
and
\mathbf{g}_{0}
up to the first order in
\hbar
in the presence of only vorticity or electromagnetic fields is already very difficult. Thus, when they are considered together, deriving the semiclassical kinetic equations of
f_{0}
and
\mathbf{g}_{0}
will be a demanding task. Covariant kinetic equations established in
Dayi and Kilinçarslan 2020
may give some hints to solve this problem.

Kinetic equations are useful mainly when collisions are taken into account. Thus, incorporating scatterings in the 3D formulation is desired. Unfortunately, we do not know how to do it for the VQKE. In principle, this can be achieved by considering the collisions in the covariant approach first and then deal with the 3D VQKE by integrating them over
p_{0}.
This will generate collision terms on the right-hand side of (
23a
)-(
24h
). In this respect, the methods employed in
Yang et al. 2020
;
Sheng et al. 2021
can be useful. The other method would be to introduce collisions to the kinetic equations of the independent set of fields (
53
), (
54
), (
56
), (
57
). This is another challenging open problem.

APPENDIX A
CALCULATION OF SHELL SHIFTS FROM THE COVARIANT FORMULATION

Semiclassical solutions of the kinetic equations obeyed by the components of 4D Wigner function, (
8
)-(
17
), have been presented in
Dayi and Kilinçarslan 2020
. By inspecting
\hbar
-order components of those solutions, one observes that some of them are expressed in the form

{\cal C}_{i}^{(1)}=\beta_{i}^{(1)}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})-\Delta E_{i}(p)\delta^{\prime}(p^{2}-m^{2}),

(1)

where
\beta_{i}^{(1)}
are first-order fields. One can notice that the 3D mass shell shifts for these fields can be obtained as

\Delta E_{i}(\bm{p})=\int\Delta E_{i}(p)\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})dp_{0}.

(2)

In this fashion, we calculated the on-shell energy shifts for the following components of 4D Wigner function.

•

The scalar field
{\cal F}:

{\cal F}^{(1)}=m\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})f_{V}^{1}-\frac{m}{2}\delta^{\prime}(p^{2}-m^{2})f_{A}^{0}\Sigma^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}w^{\mu\nu}.

(3)

f_{A}^{0},f_{V}^{1}
are scalars and

\Sigma^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}=-(1/m)\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p^{\alpha}s^{\beta},
where
s_{\mu}
is the spin quantization direction four-vector.

\Delta E^{\pm}_{f_{3}}(\bm{p})=\pm\frac{m}{2}\int dp_{0}\Sigma^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}w^{\mu\nu}f_{A}^{0}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})=\mp\frac{1}{2E_{p}}(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega})\cdot\mathbf{g}_{2}^{(0)\pm}-\kappa\mathbf{g}_{3}^{(0)\pm}\cdot\bm{\omega}.

(4)

•

The axial-vector field
{\cal A}_{\mu}:

{\cal A}^{(1)}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p^{\nu}\Sigma^{(1)\rho\sigma}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}w^{\rho\sigma}p^{\nu}f_{V}^{0}\delta^{\prime}(p^{2}-m^{2}).

(5)

\Sigma^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}
is an antisymmetric tensor field and
f_{V}^{0}
is a scalar.

For
{\cal A}_{0},

\displaystyle\Delta E_{f_{1}}^{\pm}(\bm{p})=\pm\frac{1}{2}\int dp_{0}\epsilon_{ijk}w^{jk}p^{i}f_{V}^{0}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})=\mp\frac{\kappa}{2E_{p}}\bm{p}\cdot\bm{\omega}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}.

(6)

For
\bm{{\cal A}},

\displaystyle\Delta{E}^{i\pm}_{\mathbf{g}_{0}}(\bm{p})=\mp\frac{1}{2}\int dp_{0}\epsilon^{i\nu\alpha\beta}w_{\alpha\beta}p_{\nu}f_{V}^{0}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})=-\Bigg(\frac{\kappa}{2}\bm{\omega}+\frac{\bm{\omega}\bm{p}^{2}-\bm{p}(\bm{\omega}\cdot\bm{p})}{2E_{p}^{2}}\Bigg)^{i}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}.

(7)

•

The antisymmetric tensor field
S_{\mu\nu}:

S^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}=m\Sigma^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})-mw_{\mu\nu}f_{V}^{0}\delta^{\prime}(p^{2}-m^{2}).

(8)

For
S_{0i},

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\mathbf{g}_{2}}^{i\pm}(\bm{p})=\pm m\int dp_{0}w^{i0}f_{V}^{0}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})=\frac{m(\bm{p}\times\bm{\omega})^{i}}{2E_{p}^{2}}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}.

(9)

For
S_{ij},

\displaystyle\Delta E_{\mathbf{g}_{3}}^{i\pm}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{m}{2}\int dp_{0}\epsilon^{ijk}w_{jk}f_{V}^{0}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})=\mp\frac{m\kappa}{2E_{p}}{\omega}^{i}f_{0}^{(0)\pm}.

(10)

These are the mass shell shifts which we determine from the covariant approach.

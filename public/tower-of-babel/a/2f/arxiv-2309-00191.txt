Periodic solutions for Boussinesq systems in weak-Morrey spaces
Pham Truong Xuan, Nguyen Thi Van, Tran Van Thuy

Abstract
. We prove the existence and polynomial stability of periodic mild solutions for Boussinesq systems in critical weak-Morrey spaces for dimension n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 . Those systems are derived via the Boussinesq approximation and describe the movement of an incompressible viscous fluid under natural convection filling the whole space ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Using certain dispersive and smoothing properties of heat semigroups on Morrey-Lorentz spaces as well as Yamazaki-type estimate on block spaces, we prove the existence of bounded mild solutions for the linear systems corresponding to the Boussinesq systems. Then, we establish a Massera-type theorem to obtain the existence and uniqueness of periodic solutions to corresponding linear systems on the half line time-axis by using a mean-ergodic method. Next, using fixed point arguments, we can pass from linear systems to prove the existence uniqueness and polynomial stability of such solutions for Boussinesq systems. Finally, we apply the results to Navier-Stokes equations.

2.1.
Lorentz and Lorentz-Morrey spaces

Consider
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the rearrangement function

f^{\ast}(t)=\inf\left\{s>0:m(\left\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>s\right\})\leqslant t\right\},
for
t>0,
where
m
denotes the Lebesgue measure in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A measurable function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

belongs to the Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
if the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p,q}}=\begin{cases}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{\frac{1}{p}}[f^{\ast\ast}(t)]\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}},&1<p<\infty,1\leqslant q<\infty,\\
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}[f^{\ast\ast}(t)],&1<p\leqslant\infty,\,q=\infty,\end{cases}

(2.1)

is finite, where
f^{\ast\ast}(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f^{\ast}(s)ds
is the
double-rearrangement of
f
. The pair
(L^{p,q}(\Omega),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p,q})
is a Banach space. Particularly, we have

L^{p}(\Omega)=L^{p,p}(\Omega)
, and
L^{p,\infty}
is the weak-
L^{p}
space.

A natural generalization of Morrey spaces
\mathcal{M}_{p,\lambda}
is the
so-called Lorentz-Morrey spaces, namely Morrey spaces based on Lorentz spaces.
For that, denote the open ball
D(a,\rho)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\left|x-a\right|<\rho\right\}
with
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\rho>0
. The Morrey-Lorentz space
\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}:=\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the class of all
f\in L_{loc}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying

\left\|f\right\|_{p,q,\lambda}:=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}}=\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\rho>0}\rho^{-\frac{\lambda}{p}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p,q}(D(x_{0},\rho))}<\infty,

(2.2)

where the quantity
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p,q,\lambda}
defines a norm
in
\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}
. The space
\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}
endowed
with
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p,q,\lambda}
is a Banach space. In the
case
p=q
and
q=\infty
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{p,p,\lambda}=\mathcal{M}_{p,\lambda}
and the weak-Morrey space
\mathcal{M}_{p,\infty,\lambda}
.
Moreover, we have the scaling
\left\|f(cx)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}}=c^{-\tau_{p,\lambda}}\left\|f(x)\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}}
, for all
c>0
, where
\tau_{p,\lambda}=\dfrac{n-\lambda}{p}.

We have the inclusion (see
[
16
]

\mathcal{M}_{p_{2},q_{2},\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{p_{1},q_{1},\mu},

for
0\leqslant\lambda,\mu<n,

1\leqslant p_{1}\leqslant p_{2}\leqslant\infty,\,1\leqslant q_{2}\leqslant q_{1}\leqslant\infty
and
\frac{n-\mu}{p_{1}}=\frac{n-\lambda}{p_{2}}
.
Comparing with more standard critical spaces, we have the continuous inclusions (see
[
3
,
7
,
28
]

L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{p,n-p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{p,\infty,n-p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In what follows, we recall Hölder inequality and a heat estimate in the

\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}
-setting (see

]

.

(i) Let

-block and

\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{p,q,\chi}
is a Banach

\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{p,q,\chi}}=\inf\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{k}|<\infty;\,h=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}b_{k}\hbox{ where $b_{k}$'s are $(p,q,\chi)$-blocks}\right\}.

given in the following lemma (see
[
16

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

,

, and

\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\chi}\right)^{\ast}=\mathcal{M}_{p,q,\lambda}

\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\chi}
and the space
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\chi}
is separable.

Proof.

The proof is extended from the one for the predual Morrey spaces (see for example Theorem 345 in
[
39
]
). Indeed, since
h\in\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\chi}
, there exists a sequence
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
satisfying
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|\alpha_{k}|<\infty
and a sequence of
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\chi)
-blocks
\{b_{k}(x)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
h(x)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{+\infty}\alpha_{k}b_{k}(x),\hbox{ for a.e., }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define the function
h_{N}(x):=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\alpha_{k}b_{k}(x),\hbox{ for a.e., }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We have that
h_{N}\in L_{c}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and

\left\|h-h_{N}\right\|_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\chi}}\leqslant\sum\limits_{k=N+1}^{+\infty}|\alpha_{k}|\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. Finally, since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is dense in
L^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it is also dense in
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\chi}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4

Hölder-type inequalities work well in the context of block spaces (see the proof in

\theta(t)\end{bmatrix}=e^{-tL}\begin{bmatrix}u_{0}\\

\theta\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}u\\
\theta\end{bmatrix}\right)(t)+T_{g}(\theta)(t)+\mathcal{C}\begin{bmatrix}F\\

Plugging (
4.11
) into (
4.8
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}}(t)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\kappa CMC_{2}|||g|||_{\beta,b}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\eta(t)\right\|_{p,\infty,\lambda}+CC_{1}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}F\\
f\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{\frac{p}{2},\infty}}

(4.12)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle K\rho^{2}+\kappa MC_{2}\rho|||g|||_{\beta,b}+C_{1}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}F\\
f\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{\frac{p}{2},\infty}}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle K\rho^{2}+\rho\kappa MC_{2}(C+1)|||g|||_{\beta,b}+C_{1}(C+1)\left\|\begin{bmatrix}F\\
f\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{\frac{p}{2},\infty}}<\rho

(4.14)

if
\rho
,
\left\|\begin{bmatrix}F\\
f\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{\frac{p}{2},\infty}}
and
|||g|||_{\beta,b}
are small enough. Therefore, we obtain that
\Phi(B_{\rho}^{T})\subset B_{\rho}^{T}
.

Moreover, for all
\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}
and
\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\in B_{\rho}^{T}
, by the same way as above we can define

\displaystyle\Phi\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-tL}\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}\\
\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}+B\left(\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\right)(t)+T_{g}(\xi)(t)+\mathcal{C}\begin{bmatrix}F\\
f\end{bmatrix}(t),

(4.15)

where
\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}\\
\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}
is the initial data which guarantees that
\Phi\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}
is a unique
T
-periodic solution of the system (
4.1
) with the right hand side of the first equation is

\mathcal{G}\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}(t)+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}\mathrm{div}[-\omega\otimes\omega+F]\\
\mathrm{div}(-\xi\omega+f)\end{bmatrix}(t).

Hence, it implies that

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}(t)-\Phi\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}(t)\right\|_{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-tL}\left[\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}\\
\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}v_{0}\\
\eta_{0}\end{bmatrix}\right]\right\|_{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}+\left\|B\left(\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\right)(t)-B\left(\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}\right)(t)\right\|_{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}

(4.16)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\|T_{g}(\xi)(t)-T_{g}(\eta)(t)\right\|_{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}\\
\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}v_{0}\\
\eta_{0}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}+K\left(\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}+\left\|\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}\right)\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega-v\\
\xi-\eta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}

(4.19)

\displaystyle+\kappa MC_{2}|||g|||_{\beta,b}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega-v\\
\xi-\eta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}\\
\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}v_{0}\\
\eta_{0}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}+\left(2\rho K+\kappa MC_{2}|||g|||_{\beta,b}\right)\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega-v\\
\xi-\eta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}.

(4.20)

By the same way to prove (
3.19
), we can show that

\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{0}\\
\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}v_{0}\\
\eta_{0}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}\leqslant\kappa MC_{2}|||g|||_{\beta,b}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\xi(t)-\eta(t)\right\|_{p,\infty,\lambda}

Plugging this into (
4.16
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}(t)-\Phi\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}(t)\right\|_{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\kappa CMC_{2}|||g|||_{\beta,b}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\xi(t)-\eta(t)\right\|_{p,\infty,\lambda}

\displaystyle+\left(2\rho K+\kappa MC_{2}|||g|||_{\beta,b}\right)\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega-v\\
\xi-\eta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\Big(2\rho K+\kappa MC_{2}(C+1)|||g|||_{\beta,b}\Big)\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega-v\\
\xi-\eta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}

due to
\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\omega\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}<\rho
and
\left\|\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}<\rho
.
This shows that the transformation
\Phi
is a contraction operator if

provided by

and

small enough. Based on the fixed point argument there exists a unique fixed point
\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}\\
\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}
of

in

. Combining this with the limit (
4.6
), we obtain that
\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}\\
\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}
is the unique solution of the system (
2.6
) (with the initial data
\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}_{0}\\
\hat{\theta}_{0}\end{bmatrix}
) in

with
\rho
small enough.
∎

4.2.
Asymptotic stability

In order to establish the asymptotic stability of the mild solution obtained in Theorem
4.1
,
we denote the Cartesian product space
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}_{q,\infty,\mu}\times\mathcal{M}_{r,\infty,\nu}
by
{\bf X}_{q,\mu;r,\nu}
and we define the norm on
{\bf X}_{q,\mu;r,\nu}
by

\left\|\begin{bmatrix}u\\
\theta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{\bf X}_{q,\mu;r,\nu}}:=\left\|u\right\|_{q,\infty,\mu}+\left\|\theta\right\|_{r,\infty,\nu}.

We will establish the asymptotic stability of the mild solution obtained in Theorem
4.1
in the space
{\bf X}_{q,\mu;r,\nu}
. For this purpose, we define the following time-dependent functional space

H_{q,r,\infty}:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}u\cr\theta\end{bmatrix}\in H_{p,\infty}:\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\alpha/2}u\cr t^{\gamma/2}\theta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}<\infty\right\}

endowed with the norm

\left\|\begin{bmatrix}u\\
\theta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{q,r,\infty}}:=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}u\cr\theta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{p,\infty}}+\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\alpha/2}u\cr t^{\gamma/2}\theta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}},

where
\alpha=1-\dfrac{p}{q}
and
\gamma=1-\dfrac{p}{r}
with
1<p<q\leqslant r<\infty
.

Now, we extend the bilinear estimate obtained in Theorem
3.3
to
H_{q,r,\infty}
as follows

Theorem 4.2
.

(Bilinear estimate in
H_{q,r,\infty}
)
Let
n\geqslant 3
and
1<p<q\leqslant r<\infty
. Let
B(\cdot,\cdot)
be the bilinear form (
2.8
). There exists a constant
K>0
such that

\left\|B\left(\begin{bmatrix}u\\
\theta\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\right)\right\|_{H_{q,r,\infty}}\leqslant K\left\|\begin{bmatrix}u\\
\theta\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{q,r,\infty}}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{H_{q,r,\infty}}

(4.21)

for all
\begin{bmatrix}u\\
\theta\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\in H_{q,r,\infty}
.

Proof.

We have that

\displaystyle B\left(\begin{bmatrix}u\\
\theta\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\right)(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}(u\otimes v)\\
u\xi\end{bmatrix}(s)ds

(4.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{x}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb{P}(u\otimes v)\\
\nabla_{x}e^{(t-s)\Delta}(u\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)ds

(4.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}B_{1}(u,v)(t)\\
B_{2}(u,\xi)(t)\end{bmatrix},

(4.24)

where

B_{1}(u,v)(t):=-\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\mathbb{P}(u\otimes v)(s)ds,\,\,B_{2}(u,\xi)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}e^{(t-s)\Delta}(u\xi)(s)ds.

(4.25)

Let
\dfrac{1}{d}=\dfrac{1}{q}+\dfrac{1}{r}
. By using Assertion (ii) in Lemma
2.1
and the continuous of Leray projector
\mathbb{P}
, we estimate that

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}B_{1}(u,v)(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}B_{2}(u,\xi)(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}(u\otimes v)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}(u\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{\frac{q}{2},\lambda;d,\lambda}}}ds

(4.26)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{0}^{t}\left(t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}\left\|(u\otimes v)(s)\right\|_{\frac{q}{2},\infty,{\lambda}}+t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}\left\|(u\xi)(s)\right\|_{d,\infty,{\lambda}}\right)ds

(4.27)

\displaystyle:=C(J_{1}+J_{2}),

(4.28)

where

\displaystyle J_{1}=\int_{0}^{t}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}\left\|(u\otimes v)(s)\right\|_{\frac{q}{2},\infty,{\lambda}}ds

\displaystyle J_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}\left\|(u\xi)(s)\right\|_{d,\infty,{\lambda}}ds.

From Hölder inequality in Lemma
2.3
we can estimate
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
as follows

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}\left\|u(s)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}\left\|v(s)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}ds

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}s^{-\alpha}ds\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}

(4.30)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle t^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}s^{-\alpha}ds\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}

(4.31)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}s^{-\alpha}ds\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}

(4.32)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1/2}(1-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}s^{-\alpha}ds+\int_{1/2}^{1}(1-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}s^{-\alpha}ds\right)

(4.34)

\displaystyle\times\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\xi(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(2^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p}{2q}}\int_{0}^{1/2}s^{-\alpha}ds+2^{1-\frac{p}{q}}\int_{1/2}^{1}(1-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}ds\right)

(4.36)

\displaystyle\times\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\xi(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}}{p}+\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}\right)\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\xi(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

(4.37)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{1}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\xi(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}},

(4.38)

where

C_{1}=\frac{q2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}}{p}+\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}.

By similar arguments, we can estimate that

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}s^{-\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{2}}ds\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{q,\infty,{\lambda}}\sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\left\|\xi(t)\right\|_{r,\infty,{\lambda}}

(4.39)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{2}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v(t)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\xi(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}},

(4.40)

where

C_{2}=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}}{\frac{p}{2q}+\frac{p}{2r}}+\frac{2^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2r}}}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{p}{2q}}.

Combining (
4.26
), (
4.29
), (
4.39
) and the bilinear estimate (
3.6
) in
H_{p,\infty}
, we obtain the bilinear estimate (
4.21
) in
H_{q,r,\infty}
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

(Polynomial stability)
Let
n\geqslant 3
,
2<p<q\leqslant r<\infty
,
r>q^{\prime}=\dfrac{q}{q-1}
,
p\leqslant n
and
b>\dfrac{p}{2}
such that
\dfrac{1}{p}<\dfrac{1}{b}+\dfrac{1}{r}<\min\left\{\dfrac{2}{p}+\dfrac{1}{q},\,1\right\}
. The mild solution
\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}\cr\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}
obtained in Theorem
4.1
(where
\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}\cr\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}}
is small enough) is polynomial stable in the sense that: if
\begin{bmatrix}v\cr\xi\end{bmatrix}
is another mild solution of the system (
2.6
) with the initial data
\begin{bmatrix}v_{0}\cr\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}
and the fields
g
and
\omega
such that
|||g|||_{\beta,b}=\sup_{t>0}{t^{\beta}}\left\|g(\cdot,t)\right\|_{b,\infty,\lambda}
and

|||g-\omega|||_{\beta,b}=\sup\limits_{t>0}t^{\beta}\left\|g(\cdot,t)-\omega(\cdot,t)\right\|_{b,\infty,{\lambda}}
(where
\beta=1-\frac{p}{2b}
) and

\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0}\cr\hat{\theta}_{0}-\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}}
are small enough. Then, we have

\sup_{t>0}\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\hat{u}(\cdot,t)-v(\cdot,t))\cr t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\hat{\theta}(\cdot,t)-\xi(\cdot,t))\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}<C,

(4.41)

where
\alpha=1-\dfrac{p}{q}
,
\gamma=1-\dfrac{p}{r}
and
C
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Since
\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}\cr\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}
and
\begin{bmatrix}v\cr\xi\end{bmatrix}
are solutions of the equation (
2.7
), we have
\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}-v\cr\hat{\theta}-\xi\end{bmatrix}
satisfies the following integral equation

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}-v\\
\hat{\theta}-\xi\end{bmatrix}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-tL}\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0}\\
\hat{\theta}_{0}-\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}+B\left(\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}\\
\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}\\
\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}\right)(t)-B\left(\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\xi\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}v\\
\xi\end{bmatrix}\right)(t)+T_{g}(\hat{\theta})(t)-T_{\omega}(\xi)(t)

(4.42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-tL}\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0}\\
\hat{\theta}_{0}-\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}(\hat{u}\otimes\hat{u})\\
\hat{u}\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}(s)-\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}(v\otimes v)\\
v\xi\end{bmatrix}(s)\right)ds

(4.44)

\displaystyle+T_{g}(\hat{\theta})(t)-T_{\omega}(\xi)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-tL}\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0}\\
\hat{\theta}_{0}-\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}((\hat{u}-v)\otimes\hat{u})\\
(\hat{u}-v)\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}(s)+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}(v\otimes(\hat{u}-v))\\
v(\hat{\theta}-\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)\right)ds

(4.46)

\displaystyle+T_{g}(\hat{\theta}-\xi)(t)+T_{g-\omega}(\xi)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-tL}\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0}\\
\hat{\theta}_{0}-\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}((\hat{u}-v)\otimes\hat{u})\\
(\hat{u}-v)\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}(s)+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}((v-\hat{u})\otimes(\hat{u}-v))\\
(v-\hat{u})(\hat{\theta}-\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)\right)ds

(4.49)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{P}(\hat{u}\otimes(\hat{u}-v))\\
\hat{u}(\hat{\theta}-\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)ds

\displaystyle+T_{g}(\hat{\theta}-\xi)(t)+T_{g-\omega}(\xi-\hat{\theta})(t)+T_{g-\omega}(\hat{\theta})(t).

To prove the polynomial stability (
4.41
), we show that integral equation (
4.42
) has a mild solution in a small ball of
H_{q,r,\infty}
. First, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\hat{u}-v)\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\hat{\theta}-\xi)\end{bmatrix}(t)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-tL}\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0})\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\hat{\theta}_{0}-\xi_{0})\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

(4.56)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbb{P}((\hat{u}-v)\otimes\hat{u})\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\hat{u}-v)\theta\end{bmatrix}(s)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbb{P}((v-\hat{u})\otimes(\hat{u}-v))\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(v-\hat{u})(\theta-\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbb{P}(\hat{u}\otimes(\hat{u}-v))\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\hat{u}(\hat{\theta}-\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}ds

\displaystyle+\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}T_{g}(\hat{\theta}-\xi)(t)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

\displaystyle+\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}T_{g-\omega}(\xi-\hat{\theta})(t)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

\displaystyle+\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}T_{g-\omega}(\hat{\theta})(t)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}+I_{5}+I_{6},

(4.57)

where

\displaystyle I_{0}=\left\|e^{-tL}\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0})\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\hat{\theta}_{0}-\xi_{0})\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

(4.58)

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbb{P}((\hat{u}-v)\otimes\hat{u})\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\hat{u}-v)\hat{\theta}\end{bmatrix}(s)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}ds

(4.59)

\displaystyle I_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbb{P}((v-\hat{u})\otimes(\hat{u}-v))\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}(v-\hat{u})(\hat{\theta}-\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}ds

(4.60)

\displaystyle I_{3}=\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla_{x}e^{-(t-s)L}\begin{bmatrix}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathbb{P}(\hat{u}\otimes(\hat{u}-v))\\
t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\hat{u}(\hat{\theta}-\xi)\end{bmatrix}(s)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}ds

(4.61)

\displaystyle I_{4}=\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}T_{g}(\hat{\theta}-\xi)(t)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

(4.62)

\displaystyle I_{5}=\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}T_{g-\omega}(\xi-\hat{\theta})(t)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}

(4.63)

\displaystyle I_{6}=\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}T_{g-\omega}(\hat{\theta})(t)\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{q,\lambda;r,\lambda}}}.

(4.64)

By using estimate (
2.4
),
I_{0}
can be estimated as follows

I_{0}\leqslant C\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0}\\
\hat{\theta}_{0}-\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}\right\|_{{{\bf X}_{p,\lambda}}}.

(4.65)

By using the bilinear estimate in
H_{q,r,\infty}
we have that

Remark 4.4
.

(i)

(External force) The condition of

which can not small enough as we desire in the proofs of Theorems
3.5
,
4.1
and
4.3
. However, these theorems are still valid if we assume that the coefficent of volume expansion

is
small enough. This condition guarantees the proofs of above theorems still hold since it is appeared the term

in the proofs of above theorems (see the same in Bénard problem in
[
15
, Corollary 2]
).

(ii)

Since the weak-Morrey spaces are larger than weak-Lorentz and Morrey spaces, our stability results extend the ones obtained in previous works

,

4.3.
Periodic solutions of Navier-Stokes equations revisited

If we consider the zero-temperature case, i.e.,
\theta=0
, then Boussinesq system
(
1.1
) becomes Navier-Stokes equations.

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{rll}u_{t}-\Delta u+\mathbb{P}\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)&=\mathbb{P}\mathrm{div}F&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t>0,\hfill\\
\operatorname{div}u&=\;0&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\geqslant 0,\\
u(x,0)&=\;u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\end{array}\right.

(4.91)

The existence and stability of periodic mild solutions for Navier-Stokes equations in weak-Lorentz spaces
L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
were established in some previous works (see for example
[
25
,
36
]
and references therein). Applying Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
, we can extend these results on weak-Morrey spaces
\mathcal{M}_{p,\infty,n-p}
which are larger than weak-Lorentz spaces
L^{n,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Our theorem is stated as follows:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
n\geqslant 3
and
2<p\leqslant n
. Setting
\lambda=n-p
and assume that the external force
F\in BC(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}_{\frac{p}{2},\infty,\lambda})

is a
T
-periodic function with respect to the time.

(i)

The well-posedness of periodic mild solutions of Navier-Stokes equations is stated as: if the norm
\left\|F\right\|_{BC(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}_{\frac{p}{2},\infty,\lambda})}
is small enough, then there exists a unique initial data
\hat{u}_{0}\in\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}_{p,\infty,\lambda}
which guarantees that for this initial data the Navier-Stokes equation (
4.91
) has a unique periodic mild solution
\hat{u}
with the periodicity
T
in a small ball of weak-Morrey space
\mathcal{M}^{\sigma}_{p,\infty,\lambda}
.

(ii)

Consider
q
such that
2<p<q<\infty
. The periodic mild solution
\hat{u}
obtained in Assertion (i) is polynomial stable in the sense that: if
v
is another bounded mild solution of the Cauchy problem of Navier-Stokes equations(
4.91
)
with the initial
v_{0}
such that
\left\|\hat{u}_{0}-v_{0}\right\|_{p,\infty,\lambda}
is small enough. Then, we have

\sup_{t>0}\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(\hat{u}(\cdot,t)-v(\cdot,t))\right\|_{q,\infty,\lambda}<C,

where
\alpha=1-\dfrac{p}{q}
and
C
is a positive constant.

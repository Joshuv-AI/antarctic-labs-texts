Abstract
. This paper gives particular emphasis to the nabla tempered fractional calculus, which involves the multiplication of the rising function kernel with tempered functions, and provides a more flexible alternative with considerable promise for practical applications. Some remarkable inequalities for such nabla fractional calculus are developed and analyzed, which greatly enrich the mathematical theory of nabla tempered fractional calculus. Numerical results confirm the validity of the developed properties once again, which also reveals that the introduction of tempered function provides high value and huge potential.

Main Results

In this section, a series of inequalities concerning nabla tempered fractional calculus will be developed.

Theorem 3.1
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
w:\mathbb{N}_{a}\to\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
with
\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0

,

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\leq{}_{a-1}^{\hskip 9.0pt\rm{R}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\leq{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k),x(a)\geq 0,}

(3.2)

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\geq{}_{a-1}^{\hskip 9.0pt\rm{R}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\geq{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k),x(a)\leq 0.}

Proof.

Let

. By using (
2.3
) and (
2.4

(3.3)

\begin{array}[]{rl}{}_{a-1}^{\hskip 9.0pt\rm{R}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}z(k)=&\hskip-6.0pt\nabla_{a-1}^{\hskip 9.0pt\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha-1}z(k)\\
=&\hskip-6.0pt\nabla\sum\nolimits_{j=a}^{k}{\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}z(j)}\\
=&\hskip-6.0pt{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}z(k)+\nabla\frac{{({k-a+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}z(a)\\
=&\hskip-6.0pt{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}z(k)+\frac{{({k-a+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma({-\alpha})}}z(a).\end{array}

Combining (

{\textstyle{}_{a-1}^{\hskip 9.0pt\rm{R}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)={}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)+\frac{{({k-a+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma({-\alpha})}}\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}x(a).}

From the definition, one has

(3.5)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{l}{}_{a}^{\rm{R}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\\
={\nabla^{1,w(k)}}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha-1,w(k)}x(k)\\
={w^{-1}}(k)\nabla\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{(k-j+1)\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}}w(j)x(j)\\

Under given conditions, it is not difficult to obtain that

Theorem 3.3
.

If
{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\geq{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}y(k)

, then for any

,

\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0

Following from

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)={}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}y(k)+c(k).}

Taking

{\textstyle x(k)-\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}x(a)=y(k)-\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}y(a)+{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}c(k).}

. According to the nonnegativeness of

and the invariance of the sign of

, one has

. As a result,

is implied in (

).
∎

{[{{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha-1,w(k)}x(k)}]_{k=a}}={[{{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha-1,w(k)}y(k)}]_{k=a}}
, the conclusion still holds. By using
{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)={}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)+\frac{{({k-a})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}x(a)
, one obtains that if
{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\geq{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}y(k)
is replaced by
{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\geq{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}y(k)
, the conclusion still can be guaranteed. It is generally difficult to obtain the relationship of two sequences’ fractional difference and therefore the corresponding inequalities are considered.

Theorem 3.4
.

If
{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\geq\mu x(k)+\gamma
,
{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}y(k)\leq\mu y(k)+\gamma
, where
x(a)=y(a)
, then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
w:\mathbb{N}_{a}\to\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
with
\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0
,
\mu<0
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{a+1}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, one has
x(k)\geq y(k)
.

Proof.

For
{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}u(k)\geq\mu x(k)+\gamma
, there must exist a compensation sequence
c_{1}(k)\geq 0
such that

(3.10)

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)=\mu x(k)+\gamma+c_{1}(k).}

Letting
\hat{x}(k)=w(k)x(k)
,
\hat{c}_{1}(k)=w(k)c_{1}(k)
, then one has
{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}\hat{x}(k)=\mu\hat{x}(k)+\gamma w(k)+\hat{c}_{1}(k)
. Therefore, by using the nabla linear system theory, it follows

(3.11)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{rl}\hat{x}(k)=&\hskip-6.0pt\hat{x}(a){{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,1}}({\mu,k,a})+{\rm\gamma}w(k)\ast{{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,\alpha}}({\mu,k,a})\\
&\hskip-6.0pt+\hat{c}_{1}(k)\ast{{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,\alpha}}({\mu,k,a}).\end{array}}

In a similar way, letting
\hat{y}(k)=w(k)y(k)
,
\hat{c}_{2}(k)=w(k)c_{2}(k)
where
c_{2}(k)
is a nonnegative compensation sequence, then one has
\hat{y}(a)=\hat{x}(a)
and
{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}\hat{y}(k)=\mu\hat{y}(k)+\gamma w(k)-\hat{c}_{2}(k)
, which implies

(3.12)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{rl}\hat{y}(k)=&\hskip-6.0pt\hat{y}(a){{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,1}}({\mu,k,a})+{\rm\gamma}w(k)\ast{{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,\alpha}}({\mu,k,a})\\
&\hskip-6.0pt-\hat{c}_{2}(k)\ast{{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,\alpha}}({\mu,k,a}).\end{array}}

Since
{{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,1}}({\mu,k,a})>0
,
{{\mathcal{F}}_{\alpha,\alpha}}({\mu,k,a})>0
hold for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\mu<0
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{a+1}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, when
w(k)>0
, one has
\hat{c}_{1}(k)\geq 0
,
\hat{c}_{2}(k)\geq 0
and
\hat{x}(k)\geq\hat{y}(k)
, which implies
x(k)\geq y(k)
. When
w(k)<0
, one has
\hat{c}_{1}(k)\leq 0
,
\hat{c}_{2}(k)\leq 0
and
\hat{x}(k)\leq\hat{y}(k)
. In a similar way, the desired result
x(k)\geq y(k)
follows. The case of
w(k)<0
can also be proved by applying Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

If
{}_{a}^{\rm{C}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\geq-\gamma({x(k)})+h(k)
,
{}_{a}^{\rm{C}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}y(k)\leq-\gamma({y(k)})

+h(k)

\mathcal{K}

\gamma
, bounded

Proof.

When

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm{C}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}[w(k)x(k)]\geq-w(k)\gamma(x(k))+w(k)h(k),}

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm{C}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}[w(k)y(k)]\leq-w(k)\gamma(y(k))+w(k)h(k).}

By combining (

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm{C}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}[w(k)y(k)-w(k)x(k)]\leq-w(k)[\gamma(y(k))-\gamma(x(k))].}

By substituting

The monotonicity of

,

Now, let us assume that there exist a constant

\begin{array}[]{l}\{{}_{a}^{\rm{C}}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}[w(k)y(k)-w(k)x(k)]\}_{k={k_{1}}}\\

\hskip 12.0pt+\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{{k_{1}}}{\frac{{({{k_{1}}-j+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma({-\alpha})}}w(j)[{y(j)-x(j)}]}\\

where
\frac{{0\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}=0
,

\frac{{({{k_{1}}-j+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma({-\alpha})}}=\frac{\Gamma({k_{1}-j-\alpha})}{\Gamma({k_{1}-j+1})\Gamma({-\alpha})}<0

i\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}^{k_{1}-1}

By using (

When

Theorem 3.6
.

If
W:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable convex,
W({0})=0
,
V(x(k)):=w^{-1}(k)W(z(k))
,

and
\frac{{\rm d}W({z(k)})}{{{\rm d}z(k)}}=\frac{{\rm d}V({x(k)})}{{{\rm d}x(k)}}
holds almost everywhere, then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
w:\mathbb{N}_{a}\to\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
with
\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}_{a+1}}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

(3.20)

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}V({x(k)})\leq\frac{{{\rm d}V({x(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{x^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k),}

(3.21)

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}V({x(k)})\leq\frac{{{\rm d}V({x(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{x^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k),}

(3.22)

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}V({x(k)})\leq\frac{{{\rm d}V({x(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{x^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k).}

Proof.

Similarly, only the case of
w(k)>0
is considered here. Owing to the differentiable convex condition, one has
W({z(j)})-W({z(k)})-\frac{{{\rm d}W({z(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{z^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}[{z(j)-z(k)}]\geq 0
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{a}^{k-1}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}
.

On the basis of these conclusions, the subsequent proof of this theorem will be divided into three parts.

\blacktriangleright
Part 1: the Caputo case

By using (
2.3
), (
2.12
) and the formula of summation by parts, it follows

(3.23)

\begin{array}[]{l}w(k)[{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}V({x(k)})-\frac{{{\rm d}V({x(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{x^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)]\\

=\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{f({j-1})\nabla g(j)}\\
={f({j})g(j)}|_{j=a}^{j=k}-\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\nabla f(j)g(j)},\end{array}

where
f(j):=\frac{{({k-j})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}
,

.

By using the property of rising function and taking first order difference with respect to the variable
j
, one has

\nabla f(j)=-\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma({-\alpha})}}
. According to the sign of Gamma function, one has
\nabla f(j)\geq 0
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}^{k-1}

f(a)>0
and
f(k)=0
. Due to the differentiable convex property of

with regard to
z(k)
(see
[
40
]
), one obtains
g(j)\geq 0
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{a}^{k-1}
and
g(k)=0
. Then the equation in (
3.26
) can be rewritten as

(3.24)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{l}w(k)[{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}V({x(k)})-\frac{{{\rm d}V({x(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{x^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)]\\
=-f(a)g(a)-\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k-1}{\nabla f(j)g(j)}\\
\leq 0.\end{array}}

Because of the positivity of

Part 2: the Riemann–Liouville case

By using the derived result in (

\begin{array}[]{l}w(k)[{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}V({x(k)})-\frac{{{\rm d}V({x(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{x^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)]\\

\hskip 12.0pt+\frac{{({k-a})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}W({z(a)})-\frac{{{\rm d}W({z(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{z^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}\frac{{({k-a})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}z(a)\\

Letting

\blacktriangleright
Part 3: the Grünwald–Letnikov case

Based on (

(3.27)

\textstyle{\begin{array}[]{l}w(k)[{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}V({x(k)})-\frac{{{\rm d}V({x(k)})}}{{{\rm d}{x^{\rm{T}}}(k)}}{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)]\\

which implies (

Theorem 3.9
.

If the function
\phi:{\mathbb{N}_{a+1}}\to\mathbb{R}
is monotonically decreasing and

is non-negative, then for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
w:\mathbb{N}_{a}\to\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
with
\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}_{a+1}}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

(3.41)

\textstyle{{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}[\phi^{\rm T}(k)x(k)]\leq\phi^{\rm T}(k){}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k),}

(3.42)

\textstyle{{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}[\phi^{\rm T}(k)x(k)]\leq\phi^{\rm T}(k){}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k),}

(3.43)

\textstyle{{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}[\phi^{\rm T}(k)x(k)]\leq\phi^{\rm T}(k){}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k).}

Proof.

Setting
w(k)>0
and defining
f(j):=\frac{{({k-j})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}
as previous, it follows
\nabla f(j)\geq 0
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}^{k-1}
,
[\nabla f(j)]_{j=k}=-1
,
f(a)>0
and
f(k)=0
. Letting
z(k):=w(k)x(k)
,
g(j):=[{\phi(j)-\phi(k)}]^{\rm T}z(j)
, one has
g(j)\geq 0
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{a}^{k}
and
g(k)=0
. Then the proof can be proceed as follows

(3.44)

\begin{array}[]{l}w(k)\{{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}[\phi^{\rm T}(k)x(k)]-\phi^{\rm T}(k){}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\}\\
={}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}[\phi^{\rm T}(k)z(k)]-\phi^{\rm T}(k){}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha}z(k)\\
=\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{{{({k-j+1})}^{\overline{-\alpha}}}}}{{\Gamma({-\alpha+1})}}\nabla[\phi^{\rm T}(j)z(j)]}\\
\hskip 12.0pt-\phi^{\rm T}(k)\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{{{({k-j+1})}^{\overline{-\alpha}}}}}{{\Gamma({-\alpha+1})}}\nabla z(j)}\\
=\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{f(j-1)\nabla g(j)}\\

=-f(a)g(a)-\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k-1}{\nabla f(j)g(j)}\\
\leq 0,\end{array}

which implies the correctness of (
3.41
). Along this way, the remainder proof can be completed smoothly.
∎

Theorem 3.11

If

,

\begin{array}[]{l}{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}[{u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)}]+[{u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)}]{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}1\\

\begin{array}[]{l}{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}[{u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)}]+[{u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)}]{}_{a}^{\rm R}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}1\\

\begin{array}[]{l}{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}[{u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)}]+[{u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)}]{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}1\\
\leq u^{\rm T}(k){}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}{v(k)}+v^{\rm T}(k){}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}{u(k)}.\end{array}

Proof.

Define
f(j):=\frac{{({k-j})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}
,

(3.48)

\begin{array}[]{l}w(k)\{{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}[{u(k)v(k)}]+[{u(k)v(k)}]{}_{a}^{\rm C}\nabla_{k}^{\alpha,w(k)}1\}\\

\hskip 12.0pt+[{u(k)v(k)}]\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}\nabla{w(j)}}\\
\hskip 12.0pt-u(k)\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}{{\nabla[w(j)v(j)]}}}\\
\hskip 12.0pt-v(k)\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{-\alpha}}}}{{\Gamma({1-\alpha})}}{{\nabla[w(j)u(j)]}}}\\

=-f(a)g(a)-\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k-1}{\nabla f(j)g(j)}\\

which leads to the desired result in (

2.2
, if Theorem
3.12
holds for

3.12
holds for
w(k)>0

for any

\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma(\alpha)}}w(j)

j
over the interval

\begin{array}[]{l}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)]+u^{\rm T}(i)v(i){}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1\\

Theorem 3.14
.

If
u_{i}:\mathbb{N}_{a+1}\to\mathbb{R}

The proof will be proven by induction.

\blacktriangleright

) reduces to

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{u_{1}}(k)\geq{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{u_{1}}(k),}

\blacktriangleright
Part 2: assume that when

, (
3.60

(3.62)

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}\prod\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}{{u_{i}}(k)}[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1]^{m-1}\geq\prod\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}{{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{u_{i}}(k)}.}

(3.63)

={}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[{u_{m+1}}(k){v_{m}}(k)]{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,\lambda}1[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1]^{m-1}\\
\geq{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{u_{m+1}(k)}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{v_{m}(k)}[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1]^{m-1}\\
\geq{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{u_{m+1}(k)}\prod\nolimits_{i=1}^{m}{{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{u_{i}}(k)}\\
=\prod\nolimits_{i=1}^{m+1}{{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{u_{i}}(k)},\end{array}

where

3.63

3.60

Theorem 3.15
.

If
x:\mathbb{N}_{a+1}^{b}\to[m,M]
,
m,M\in\mathbb{R}
, then for any
\alpha>0
,
k,b\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
w:\mathbb{N}_{a}\to\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
with
\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0
, one has

(3.64)

{\textstyle m{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1\leq{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\leq M{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1.}

Proof.

By using the definition and the condition,
w(k)>0
,
\frac{{(k-j+1)\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}}>0
,
x(k)\in[m,M]
, one has

(3.65)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{rl}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{x}(k)=&\hskip-6.0pt{w^{-1}}(k)\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{(k-j+1)\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}}w(j){x}(j)}\\
\geq&\hskip-6.0ptm{w^{-1}}(k)\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{(k-j+1)\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}}w(j)}\\
=&\hskip-6.0ptm{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1,\end{array}}

(3.66)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{rl}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}{x}(k)=&\hskip-6.0pt{w^{-1}}(k)\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{(k-j+1)\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}}w(j){x}(j)}\\
\leq&\hskip-6.0ptM{w^{-1}}(k)\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\frac{{(k-j+1)\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}}w(j)}\\
=&\hskip-6.0ptM{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1.\end{array}}

The proof is thus completed.

Theorem 3.16
.

For any
\alpha>0
,
u,v:\mathbb{N}_{a+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
w:\mathbb{N}_{a}\to\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
with
\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}_{a+1}}
, one has

(3.67)

{\textstyle\{{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)]\}^{2}\leq{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[u^{\rm T}(k)u(k)]{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[v^{\rm T}(k)v(k)].}

Proof.

Similarly, the
w(k)>0
case is equivalent of the
w(k)<0
case. Consequently, only the case of
w(k)>0
is considered. Let
\hat{u}(j):=\sqrt{\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma(\alpha)}}w(j)}u(j)
,
\hat{v}(j):=\sqrt{\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma(\alpha)}}w(j)}v(j)
. The following quadratic function is constructed

(3.68)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{rl}f(x)=&\hskip-6.0pt\big[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\hat{u}^{\rm T}(j)\hat{u}(j)}\big]x^{2}+2\big[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\hat{u}^{\rm T}(j)\hat{v}(j)}\big]x\\
&\hskip-6.0pt+\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\hat{v}^{\rm T}(j)\hat{v}(j)},\end{array}}

where
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

By using basic mathematical derivation, one has

(3.69)

{\textstyle f(x)=\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{[\hat{u}(j)x+\hat{v}(j)]^{\rm T}[\hat{u}(j)x+\hat{v}(j)]},}

which means that its discriminant is nonnegative, i.e.,

(3.70)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{rl}\Delta=&\hskip-6.0pt4\big[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\hat{u}^{\rm T}(j)\hat{v}(j)}\big]^{2}-4\big[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\hat{u}^{\rm T}(j)\hat{u}(j)}\big]\big[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\hat{v}^{\rm T}(j)\hat{v}(j)}\big]\\
\leq&\hskip-6.0pt0.\end{array}}

Multiplying this inequality by the positive factor
\frac{1}{4w^{2}(k)}
on both left and right hand sides and substituting
\hat{u}(j)
,
\hat{v}(j)
into (
3.70
), one has

(3.71)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{l}\{{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[u^{\rm T}(k)v(k)]\}^{2}-{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[u^{\rm T}(k)u(k)]{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[v^{\rm T}(k)v(k)]\\
=\frac{1}{4w^{2}(k)}\Delta\leq 0,\end{array}}

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.17
.

If
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex, then for any
\alpha>0
,
w:\mathbb{N}_{a}\to\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
with
\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}_{a+1}}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, one has

(3.72)

{\textstyle f\big([{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1]^{-1}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\big)\leq[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1]^{-1}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}f(x(k)),}

where
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Letting
\lambda_{j}:=[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1]^{-1}\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma(\alpha)}}\frac{w(j)}{w(k)}
, it is not difficult to obtain
\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\lambda_{j}}=1
and
\lambda_{j}>0
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}
. By using (
2.9
) and the newly defined
\lambda_{j}
, one has

(3.73)

{\textstyle f\big([{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1]^{-1}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}x(k)\big)=f\big(\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\lambda_{j}x(j)}\big),}

(3.74)

{\textstyle[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}1]^{-1}{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}f(x(k))=\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\lambda_{j}f(x(j))}.}

Now, the problem becomes

(3.75)

{\textstyle f\big(\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\lambda_{j}x(j)}\big)\leq\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{\lambda_{j}f(x(j))}.}

The mathematical induction will be adopted once again.

\blacktriangleright
Part 1: when
k=a+1
,
\lambda_{a+1}=1
, (
3.75
) holds naturally.

\blacktriangleright
Part 2: when
k=a+2
,
\lambda_{a+1}=\frac{\alpha w(a+1)}{\alpha w(a+1)+w(a+2)}
,
\lambda_{a+2}=\frac{w(a+2)}{\alpha w(a+1)+w(a+2)}
, (
3.75
) becomes

(3.76)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{l}f(\lambda_{a+1}x(a+1)+\lambda_{a+2}x(a+2))\\
\leq\lambda_{a+1}f(x(a+1))+\lambda_{a+2}f(x(a+2)).\end{array}}

By using the convexity of
f
, (
3.72
) can be obtained.

\blacktriangleright
Part 3: assume that when
k=m\in\mathbb{N}_{a+2}
, (
3.75
) holds, i.e.,

f\big(\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\lambda_{j}x(j)}\big)

\leq\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\lambda_{j}f(x(j))}
.
Since
\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m+1}{\lambda_{j}}=1
, it follows

(3.77)

{\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\lambda_{j}}=1-\lambda_{m+1}.}

Defining
\eta_{j}:=\frac{\lambda_{j}}{1-\lambda_{m+1}}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}^{m}
, then one has

(3.78)

{\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\eta_{j}}=\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\frac{\lambda_{j}}{1-\lambda_{m+1}}}=1,}

and
\eta_{j}>0
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}^{m}
.

On this basis, one has

(3.79)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{l}f\big(\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m+1}{\lambda_{j}x(j)}\big)\\
=f\big(\lambda_{m+1}x(m+1)+\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\lambda_{j}x(j)}\big)\\
=f\big(\lambda_{m+1}x(m+1)+(1-\lambda_{m+1})\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\eta_{j}x(j)}\big)\\
\leq\lambda_{m+1}f(x(m+1))+(1-\lambda_{m+1})f\big(\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\eta_{j}x(j)}\big)\\
\leq\lambda_{m+1}f(x(m+1))+(1-\lambda_{m+1})\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\eta_{j}f(x(j))}\\
=\lambda_{m+1}f(x(m+1))+\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m}{\lambda_{j}f(x(j))}\\
\leq\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{m+1}{\lambda_{j}f(x(j))},\end{array}}

which means that (
3.75
) holds for
k=m+1
.

To sum up, the proof is done.
∎

Theorem 3.18
.

For any
\alpha>0
,
u,v:\mathbb{N}_{a+1}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
w:\mathbb{N}_{a}\to\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
with
\frac{w(a)}{w(k)}>0
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}_{a+1}}
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, if
p>1
, one has

(3.80)

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[u(k)v(k)]\leq[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}u^{p}(k)]^{\frac{1}{p}}[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}v^{q}(k)]^{\frac{1}{q}}},

and if
p\in(0,1)
, one has

(3.81)

{\textstyle{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[u(k)v(k)]\geq[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}u^{p}(k)]^{\frac{1}{p}}[{}_{a}^{\rm{G}}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}v^{q}(k)]^{\frac{1}{q}}}.

Proof.

Similarly, consider
w(k)>0
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}_{a+1}}
. Let
a_{j}:=\sqrt[p]{\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma(\alpha)}}w(j)}u(j)
,
b_{j}:=\sqrt[q]{\frac{{({k-j+1})\overline{{}^{\alpha-1}}}}{{\Gamma(\alpha)}}w(j)}v(j)
. It follows
a_{j},b_{j}\geq 0
,
\forall j\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}^{k}
. Bearing this in mind, (
3.80
) can be expressed as

(3.82)

{\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{a_{j}b_{j}}\leq[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{a_{j}^{p}}]^{\frac{1}{p}}[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{b_{j}^{q}}}]^{\frac{1}{q}},

which is actually the discrete time Hölder inequality. (
3.81
) can be expressed as

(3.83)

{\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{a_{j}b_{j}}\geq[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{a_{j}^{p}}]^{\frac{1}{p}}[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{b_{j}^{q}}}]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

When
p\in(0,1)
, one has
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Let
p^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{p}
,
q^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{1-p}
, it is not difficult to obtain
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}>1
and
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. By applying the discrete time Hölder inequality once again, it yields

(3.84)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{rl}\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{a_{j}^{p}}=&\hskip-6.0pt\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{({a_{j}b_{j}})^{p}b_{j}^{-p}}\\
\leq&\hskip-6.0pt[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{({a_{j}b_{j}})^{pp^{\prime}}}]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{b_{j}^{-pq^{\prime}}}]^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\\
\leq&\hskip-6.0pt(\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{{a_{j}b_{j}}})^{p}[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{b_{j}^{q}}]^{1-p}.\end{array}}

Calculating the
\frac{1}{p}
power on both sides of (
3.84
) gives

(3.85)

{\textstyle\begin{array}[]{rl}[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{a_{j}^{p}}]^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq&\hskip-6.0pt\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{{a_{j}b_{j}}}[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{b_{j}^{q}}]^{\frac{1-p}{p}}\\
=&\hskip-6.0pt\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{{a_{j}b_{j}}}[\sum\nolimits_{j=a+1}^{k}{b_{j}^{q}}]^{-\frac{1}{q}},\end{array}}

which implies (
3.83
).
∎

{\textstyle\{{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}[u(k)+v(k)]^{p}\}^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq[{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha,w(k)}u^{p}(k)]^{\frac{1}{p}}+[{}_{a}^{\rm G}\nabla_{k}^{-\alpha}v^{p}(k)]^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

The proof completes immediately.

5.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, the nabla tempered fractional calculus has been investigated systemically. A series of interesting and promising inequalities are developed rigorously including the monotonicity, the comparison principle, the difference inequality, the sum inequality. These properties have huge potentials in stability analysis and controller design. The proposed properties could greatly enrich the comprehension of nabla tempered fractional calculus and facilitate its applications. In addition, it is hoped that the techniques and consequences of this work could inspire the researchers to explore more interesting sequel in this area.

Riesz multiplier convergent spaces of operator valued series and a version of Orlicz- Pettis theorem
Mahmut Karakuş and Ramazan Kama

Abstract
. It is not usual to characterize an operator valued series via completeness of multiplier spaces. In this study, by using a series of bounded linear operators, we introduce the space M R ∞ ​ ( ∑ k T k ) M^{\infty}_{R}\big(\sum_{k}T_{k}\big) of Riesz summability which is a generalization of the Cesàro summability. Therefore, we give the completeness criteria of these spaces with c 0 ​ ( X ) c_{0}(X) -multiplier convergent operator series. It is a natural consequence that one can characterize the completeness of a normed space through M R ∞ ​ ( ∑ k T k ) M^{\infty}_{R}\big(\sum_{k}T_{k}\big) which will be assumed that is complete for every c 0 ​ ( X ) c_{0}(X) -multiplier Cauchy operator series. Then, we characterize the continuity and the (weakly) compactness of the summing operator 𝒮 \mathcal{S} from the multiplier space M R ∞ ​ ( ∑ k T k ) M^{\infty}_{R}\big(\sum_{k}T_{k}\big) to an arbitrary normed space Y Y through c 0 ​ ( X ) c_{0}(X) -multiplier Cauchy and ℓ ∞ ​ ( X ) \ell_{\infty}(X) -multiplier convergent series, respectively. We also prove that if ∑ k T k \sum_{k}T_{k} is ℓ ∞ ​ ( X ) \ell_{\infty}(X) -multiplier Cauchy, then the multiplier space of weakly Riesz-convergence associated to the operator valued series M w ​ R ∞ ​ ( ∑ k T k ) M^{\infty}_{wR}\big(\sum_{k}T_{k}\big) is subspace of M R ∞ ​ ( ∑ k T k ) M^{\infty}_{R}\big(\sum_{k}T_{k}\big) . Among other results, finally, we obtain a new version of the well-known Orlicz-Pettis theorem by using Riesz-summability.

Through the rest of the paper, we deal with the Riesz transformation which is a generalization of Cesàro mean

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{clccl}C_{1}&:&\omega&\longrightarrow&\omega\\
&&x=(x_{k})&\longmapsto&C_{1}x=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.\end{array}

A series
\sum_{k}x_{k}
in a normed space

is said to be

or

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k+1)x_{k}\right]=x_{0}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(n-k+1)x^{*}(x_{k})\right]=x^{*}(x_{0})

. Riesz transformation is also given by

\displaystyle Rx=\left(\frac{1}{R_{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}x_{k}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

for any sequence

, where

is a sequence of nonnegative reals with

(1.5)

\displaystyle R_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}~\textrm{ with }~\lim_{n\to\infty}R_{n}=\infty.

\displaystyle r_{nk}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\frac{r_{k}}{R_{n}}&,&1\leq k\leq n,\\
0&,&k>n\end{array}\right.

for all

3.
A version of Orlicz-Pettis Theorem for Riesz summability

The Orlicz-Pettis Theorem is one of the important results in the Theory of Functional Analysis. Many generalizations and applications of this theorem can be found in
[
5
,
17
,
18
,
22
,
24
,
25
,
27
]
.
Before stating and proving the Orlicz-Pettis Theorem by means of Riesz convergence, we will give the following definition (see
[
22
]
):

Definition 3.1
.

The space
\lambda
has the infinite gliding hump property (
\infty
-GHP) if whenever
x\in\lambda
and
\{\sigma_{m}\}
is an increasing sequence of intervals, there exist a subsequence
\{p_{m}\}
and
t_{p_{m}}>0
,
t_{p_{m}}\to\infty
such that every subsequence of
\{p_{m}\}
has a further subsequence
\{q_{m}\}
such that the coordinatewise sum of the series
\sum_{m}t_{q_{m}}\chi_{\sigma_{q_{m}}}x\in\lambda
.

In the following, we give a new version of Orlicz-Pettis theorem for vector valued multiplier Riesz convergent space of OVS by using Antosik-Mikusinski matrix theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\lambda
have
\infty
-GHP and
T_{k}\in B(X:Y)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. If the series
\sum_{k}T_{k}
is
\lambda-
multiplier Riesz convergent with respect to weak topology of
Y
, then the series
\sum_{k}T_{k}
is
\lambda-
multiplier Riesz convergent with respect to strong topology of
Y
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
. If the conclusion is false, there exists
x\in\lambda
,
(y_{n}^{*})\subset Y^{*}
bounded sequence and an increasing sequence of intervals
\{\sigma_{n}\}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left|R-\sum_{k\in\sigma_{n}}y_{n}^{*}(T_{k}x_{k})\right|>\epsilon

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\lambda
has
\infty
-GHP, there exist a subsequence
\{p_{n}\}
and
t_{p_{n}}>0
,
t_{p_{n}}\to\infty
such that every subsequence of
\{p_{n}\}
has a further subsequence
\{q_{n}\}
such that
\sum_{n}t_{q_{n}}\chi_{\sigma_{q_{n}}}x\in\lambda
.
Now, we consider the matrix
H=[h_{ij}]
defined by

\displaystyle h_{ij}=\sum_{m\in\sigma_{p_{j}}}\frac{y^{*}_{p_{i}}}{t_{p_{i}}}(T_{m}(t_{p_{j}}x_{m}))

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
(y_{i}^{*})
is bounded and
t_{p_{i}}\to\infty
, the columns of
H
converge to 0. On the other hand, since
\lambda
has
\infty
-GHP, we have
\sum_{n}t_{q_{j}}\chi_{\sigma_{q_{j}}}x\in\lambda
for every subsequence
\{q_{j}\}
and hence the subseries

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\sum_{m\in\sigma_{q_{j}}}T_{m}(t_{q_{j}}x_{m})

is weakly Riesz convergent. Then, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\sum_{j}a_{iq_{j}}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\sum_{j}\sum_{m\in\sigma_{q_{j}}}\frac{y^{*}_{p_{i}}}{t_{p_{i}}}(T_{m}(t_{q_{j}}x_{m})).

From Antosik-Mikusinski theorem (
[
22
]
Appendix D), the matrix
H
is Riesz convergent to zero as diagonal, which is contradiction with (
3.1
).
∎

Pérez-Fernández et al.

gave a new characterization of

and

series via the completeness of some new subspaces of

obtained from almost convergence and also presented a new version of Orlicz-Pettis theorem. In
[
3
]
, they also generalized the results given in
[
1
]
to any regular matrix. In recent times,
Karakuş and Başar
[

introduced a slight generalization of almost convergence and gave some new multiplier spaces associated to the series
\sum_{k}x_{k}
in a normed space by means of this new summability method. Swartz constructed some new versions of the Orlicz-Pettis theorem for multiplier convergent series under continuity assumptions on some linear operators, and gave applications to spaces of continuous linear operators,
[
24
,
25
]
. In
[
27
]
, Yuanghong and Ronglu proved that multiplier convergence of OVS is closely related to the
AK
property of the sequence spaces, and obtained corresponding versions of Orlicz-Pettis theorems. Kama and Altay
[
10
]
obtained some new multiplier space by using Fibonacci sequence spaces, and Kama et al.
[
11
]
gave similar results for multiplier spaces which were obtained from backward difference matrix. Most recently, León-Saavedra et al.
[
17
,

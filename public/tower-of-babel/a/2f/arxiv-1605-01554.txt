Abstract
. In this paper we aim at proving well-posedness of solutions obtained as vanishing viscosity limits for the Cauchy problem on a traffic junction where m m incoming and n n outgoing roads meet. The traffic on each road is governed by a scalar conservation law ρ h , t + f h ​ ( ρ h ) x = 0 \rho_{h,t}+f_{h}(\rho_{h})_{x}=0 , for h ∈ { 1 , … , m + n } h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\} . Our proof relies upon the complete description of the set of road-wise constant solutions and its properties, which is of some interest on its own. Then we introduce a family of Kruzhkov-type adapted entropies at the junction and state a definition of admissible solution in the same spirit as in [ 17 , 15 , 2 , 1 , 4 ] .

1.1.
Preliminaries

We assume that the reader is acquainted with the notion of entropy solution to scalar conservation laws introduced by Kruzhkov
[
25
]
. This notion
is suitable for describing admissibility of solutions to (
1
) away from the junction.
But we recall, first, the formulation of the Bardos-LeRoux-Nédélec boundary condition for conservation laws in terms of
the Godunov numerical flux, which will be instrumental for the definition of admissible solutions at the junction and for the existence proof. Second, we recall that entropy solutions of non linearly degenerate scalar conservation laws admit boundary traces in the strong
\mathbf{L}^{1}
sense.

1.1.1.
Godunov’s flux

Let
u
be the entropy solution to the scalar conservation law with Lipschitz continuous flux

(3)

u_{t}+f(u)_{x}=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R}

corresponding to the Riemann initial condition

(4)

u_{0}(x)=\begin{cases}a,&\quad\text{if }x<0,\\
b,&\quad\text{if }x>0.\end{cases}

One calls Godunov flux the function which associates to the couple
(a,b)
the value
f(u(t,0^{-}))=f(u(t,0^{+}))
(the two values are equal due to the Rankine-Hugoniot condition). The analytical expression, see for example
[
22
]
, is given by

(5)

G(a,b)=\begin{cases}\min_{s\in[a,b]}f(s)\quad\text{if }a\leq b,\\
\max_{s\in[b,a]}f(s)\quad\text{if }a\geq b.\end{cases}

In the sequel, we denote by
\partial_{a}G
, resp.
\partial_{b}G
, the partial derivative of the Godunov flux
G
with respect to the first, resp. to the second argument.

The Godunov flux can be used for convergent numerical approximation of (
3
) by an explicit finite difference / finite volume scheme. This follows from the fact that
G
satisfies the following two basic properties, shared with several other numerical fluxes as for example Rusanov and Lax-Friedrichs (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
):

•

Consistency: for all
a\in[0,R]
,
G(a,a)=f(a)
;

•

Monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity: There exists
L>0
such that for all
(a,b)\in[0,R]^{2}
we have

(6)

0\leq\partial_{a}G(a,b)\leq L,\qquad\qquad-L\leq\partial_{b}G(a,b)\leq 0.

1.1.2.
A formulation of the Bardos-LeRoux-Nédélec boundary condition

In our setting, the main interest in using Godunov flux is related to the following observation (see
[
18
]
, see also
[
6
]
for a review on this topic). Consider the initial and boundary value problem (IBVP)

(7)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+f(u)_{x}=0,\qquad\text{for }(t,x)\>\text{in }\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R}_{-}\\
u(t,0)=u_{b}(t),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

and assume that
u
is a Kruzhkov entropy solution in the interior of the half plane
\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R}_{-}
. Then
u
satisfies the boundary condition in the sense of Bardos-LeRoux-Nédélec (see
[
9
]
) if and only if its trace
\gamma u(t)=u(t,0^{-})
satisfies
f(\gamma u(t))=G(\gamma u(t),u_{b}(t))
.

1.1.3.
Strong boundary traces of local entropy solutions

Consider (
3

\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times(a,b)

Assume that

Assume that the Lipschitz flux

Then (see

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{k}\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{b-k}^{b}\xi(t)|u(t,x)-\gamma u(t)|\,dx\,dt=0.

Notice that this property permits to extend the above interpretation of the Bardos-LeRoux-Nédélec boundary condition for problem (

to the case of general

1.2.
The notion of admissible solution and the outline of the paper

Our goal is to re-visit and complement the work
[
13
]
, which studies vanishing viscosity limits for problem (
1
).
The property of being a vanishing viscosity limit can be seen as a specific admissibility condition for a weak solution of (
1
), which boils down to

•

the standard Kruzhkov entropy conditions on each of the roads
\Omega_{h}
,
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
;

•

a specific “coupling” condition at the junction, whose description is the main object of the present paper.

An intermediate significant result of our work is the intrinsic characterization of the vanishing viscosity limits for (
1
): this is done either in terms of the Riemann solver at the junction, or in terms of
m+n
Dirichlet problems on
\Omega_{h}
,
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
coupled by a simple transmission condition, or in terms od “adapted” entropy inequalities.

The notion of solution we aim at using is roughly speaking the following. We consider
\vec{\rho}=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m+n})
in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})
as an admissible solution if, first, for any
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
,
\rho_{h}
is a weak entropy solution in the sense of Kruzhkov in the interior of
\Omega_{h}
. Second, recalling that
\rho_{i}
(resp.,
\rho_{j}
) admits a strong trace
\rho_{i}(\cdot,0^{-})=\gamma_{i}\rho_{i}(\cdot)
(resp.,
\rho_{j}(\cdot,0^{+})=\gamma_{j}\rho_{j}(\cdot)
) at
x=0
, i.e.

(9)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to 0^{-}}\frac{1}{k}\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{k}^{0}\xi(t)|\rho_{i}(t,x)-\gamma_{i}\rho_{i}(t)|\,dx\,dt=0,

\displaystyle\text{for }i=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{k}\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{k}\xi(t)|\rho_{j}(t,x)-\gamma_{j}\rho_{j}(t)|\,dx\,dt=0,

\displaystyle\text{for }j=m+1,\ldots,m+n,

we require that the
(m+n)
-uple of traces satisfies condition (
2
) for a.e.
t\in\mathbbm{R}_{+}
and, moreover,
for a.e.
t\in\mathbbm{R}_{+}
the values of the traces “coincide up to boundary layers”.
This choice is made in accordance with the fact that the vanishing viscosity approximation of (
1
) prescribes, for every viscosity parameter

\varepsilon>0
, the coincidence of all
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{h}(t,\cdot)
,
h=1,\ldots,m+n
, at
x=0
; and that taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
relaxes this condition analogously to the way in which the boundary condition in (
7
) is relaxed.

In order to give a more precise statement, which is the aim of this section, we need to introduce some notation.
In Section
2
, we will reformulate the problem in two different forms, suitable for proving the uniqueness and the existence, respectively.

1.2.1.
The junction as a collection of IBVPs

Given an initial condition
\vec{u}_{0}\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})

\vec{u}_{0}=(u^{0}_{1},\ldots,u^{0}_{m+n})
, we look for a function
\vec{\rho}=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m+n})

\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})
such that for any

\begin{cases}\rho_{h,t}+f_{h}(\rho_{h})_{x}=0,&\qquad\text{on }]0,T[\times\Omega_{h},\\
\rho_{h}(t,0)=v_{h}(t),&\qquad\text{on }]0,T[,\\
\rho_{h}(0,x)=u^{0}_{h}(x),&\qquad\text{on }\Omega_{h},\end{cases}

where the set of boundary conditions

is to be fixed in the sequel so to guarantee that, in particular, the conservativity condition (
2

Definition 1.1
.

We say that the function

•

For any test function

•

For a.e.

2.1.
Definition of the vanishing viscosity germ

In this section we describe the stationary admissible solutions of (
1
) that are constant on each road of
\Gamma
.
Because of the analogy with the discontinuous-flux setting of
[
1

4
]
we will use similar notation and terminology (cf. Remark

Definition 2.1
.

We call vanishing viscosity germ the subset of
[0,R]^{m+n}
defined by

(16)

\mathcal{G}_{VV}=\left\{\begin{aligned} \vec{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+n}):&\>\exists p\in[0,R]\>\text{such that}\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(u_{i},p)=\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p,u_{j})\\
&\>G_{i}(u_{i},p)=f_{i}(u_{i}),\quad G_{j}(p,u_{j})=f_{j}(u_{j}),\>\forall i,\,j\end{aligned}\right\}.

It is immediate to see that
\vec{u}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}
if and only if, seen as a vector function in
\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\Gamma\to[0,R]^{m+n}
,
\vec{u}
provides a solution of (
1
) in the sense of Definition
1.2

such that each component

of
\vec{u}
is constant both in
t
and

.

Following
[
4
]
, see also
[
17
]
, we can characterize
\mathcal{G}_{VV}

by a set of inequalities reminiscent of the celebrated Oleinik condition for scalar conservation laws.
Here and in the following we use the notation
I[a,b]
to indicate the closed interval
[\min\{a,b\},\max\{a,b\}]
in
\mathbbm{R}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The vanishing viscosity germ
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
coincides with the subset
of
[0,R]^{m+n}
defined by

(17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \vec{u}=&(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+n}):\>\exists p\in[0,R]\>\text{such that}\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(u_{i},p)=\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p,u_{j}),\\
&\forall s\in I[u_{i},p]\quad(p-u_{i})(f_{i}(s)-f_{i}(u_{i}))\geq 0,\>\text{for }i=1,\ldots,m,\\
&\forall s\in I[p,u_{j}]\quad(u_{j}-p)(f_{j}(s)-f_{j}(u_{j}))\geq 0,\>\text{for }j=m+1,\ldots,m+n\end{aligned}\right\}.

Proof.

Actually, the value
p
in (
17
) coincides with the value
p
in (
16
). One only needs to show that

\forall s\in I[u_{i},p]\colon\quad(u_{i}-p)(f_{i}(s)-f_{i}(u_{i}))\geq 0\Leftrightarrow G_{i}(u_{i},p)=f_{i}(u_{i}),

for any
i
. This readily comes from the definition of the Godunov flux, as the relation
G_{i}(u_{i},p)=f_{i}(u_{i})
rewrites as

(18)

\displaystyle f_{i}(u_{i})=\min_{s\in[u_{i},p]}f_{i}(s),\quad\text{if }(p-u_{i})\geq 0,

\displaystyle f_{i}(u_{i})=\max_{s\in[p,u_{i}]}f_{i}(s),\quad\text{if }(p-u_{i})\leq 0.

The proof for the
j
-index case is analogous.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Given
\vec{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+n})
in
[0,R]^{m+n}
, consider the problem

(19)

\text{find $p_{\vec{u}}\in[0,R]$ such that\;\;}\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(u_{i},p_{\vec{u}})=\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p_{\vec{u}},u_{j}).

(i)

The set
\mathcal{P}_{\vec{u}}
of solutions of (
19
) is non-empty.

(ii)

The values
(G_{i}(u_{i},p_{\vec{u}}))_{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}
,
(G_{j}(p_{\vec{u}},u_{j}))_{j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}}

do not depend on the choice of the value
p_{\vec{u}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\vec{u}}
.

Example 1
.

To give an example, consider a junction consisting of two incoming and one outgoing roads, on which the traffic is described through the following flux functions:

(20)

f_{h}(\rho)=-h\rho^{2}+h,\qquad\text{for }h=1,2,3.

Remark that, for the reader’s convenience, we consider now
\rho\in[-1,1]
,
f_{h}(-1)=f_{h}(1)=0
and
\bar{\rho}_{h}=0
. This does not change our results but allows for cleaner computations.
As above we call
G_{h}
the Godunov flux corresponding to
f_{h}
and
u_{h}
is the constant initial condition on the
h
-th road. If
u_{h}\neq 0
, let
\hat{u}_{h}\neq u_{h}
be the only solution to
f_{h}(u_{h})=f_{h}(\hat{u}_{h})
.
By using the standard Riemann Solver, see
[
22
]
, it is easy to check that the values of
G_{i}(u_{i},\cdot)
,
i=1,\,2
, as functions of
p
, are the following

•

If
u_{i}\leq 0
, then
G_{i}(u_{i},p)=f_{i}(u_{i})
for all
p\leq\hat{u}_{i}
and
G_{i}(u_{i},p)=f_{i}(p)
for all
p\geq\hat{u}_{i}
;

•

If
u_{i}\geq 0
, then
G_{i}(u_{i},p)=f_{i}(0)
for all
p\leq 0
and
G_{i}(u_{i},p)=f_{i}(p)
for all
p\geq 0
.

Similarly, the values of
G_{3}(\cdot,u_{3})
, as a function of
p
are

•

If
u_{3}\leq 0
, then
G_{3}(p,u_{3})=f_{3}(p)
for all
p\leq 0
and
G_{3}(p,u_{3})=f_{3}(0)
for all
p\geq 0
;

•

If
u_{3}\geq 0
, then
G_{3}(p,u_{3})=f_{3}(p)
for all
p\leq\hat{u}_{3}
and
G_{3}(p,u_{3})=f_{3}(u_{3})
for all
p\geq\hat{u}_{3}
.

One can check that if we take, as an example,
u_{1}=-\sqrt{1/2}
,
u_{2}=1/4
and
u_{3}=\sqrt{1/6}
, then all the values of
p
between
[-\sqrt{1/6},0]
satisfy the relation

(21)

G_{1}(u_{1},p)+G_{2}(u_{2},p)=G_{3}(p,u_{3}),

and that for all these values of
p
, the collection
\vec{G}^{*}(\vec{u})
of fluxes at the junction will be given by

\vec{G}^{*}(\vec{u})=\Bigl(G_{1}(u_{1},p),G_{2}(u_{2},p),G_{3}(p,u_{3})\Bigr)=(1/2,2,5/2).

Remark that, as explained in the paper
[
26
]
, the functions
G_{i}(u_{i},\cdot)
and
G_{j}(\cdot,u_{j})
are closely related to the equilibrium supply/demand functions introduced in the work by Lebacque and his collaborators. In particular we have that the equilibrium demand function
\Delta_{i}
of the
i
-th incoming road and the equilibrium supply function
\Sigma_{j}
of the
j
-th outgoing road can be defined as

(22)

u_{i}\mapsto\Delta_{i}(u_{i})=\max_{p}\{G_{i}(u_{i},p)\},\qquad u_{j}\mapsto\Sigma_{j}(u_{j})=\max_{p}\{G_{j}(p,u_{j})\}.

Now, we prove Lemma
2.3
.

Proof.

(i) Given
\vec{u}
we define the functions
\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}
and
\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}
from
[0,R]
to
\mathbbm{R}
by

(23)

\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}:p\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(u_{i},p),\qquad\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}:p\mapsto\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p,u_{j}).

A quick direct calculation gives us

(24)

\displaystyle G_{i}(u_{i},0)=\max_{s\in[0,u_{i}]}f_{i}(s)\geq 0,

\displaystyle G_{i}(u_{i},R)=\min_{s\in[u_{i},R]}f_{i}(s)=0,

\displaystyle G_{j}(0,u_{j})=\min_{s\in[0,u_{j}]}f_{j}(s)=0,

\displaystyle G_{j}(R,u_{j})=\max_{s\in[u_{j},R]}f_{i}(s)\geq 0,

so that

(25)

\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}(0)\geq 0=\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}(0)\quad\text{and }\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}(R)=0\leq\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}(R).

The existence of at least one solution
p_{\vec{u}}
of (
19
) is ensured by the continuity of
\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}-\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}
on
[0,R]
and the intermediate value theorem.

(ii) It may happen that there exist several values of
p
such that
\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}(p)
and
\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}(p)
coincide.
From the Lipschitz continuity and monotonicity property of Godunov flux (see §
1.1.1
) we have

(26)

\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\;\;\partial_{b}G_{i}(u_{i},p)\leq 0,\;\;\forall j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}\;\;\partial_{a}G_{j}(p,u_{j})\geq 0,

which means in particular that
\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}-\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}
is non-strictly decreasing. Therefore, the set
\mathcal{P}_{\vec{u}}
of solutions of (
19
) is a closed sub-interval of
[0,R]
. Since, moreover, each term of the sums defining
\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}-\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}
has the same monotonicity,
we find that all these terms are constant on
\mathcal{P}_{\vec{u}}
.
∎

Next, consider the map
\vec{G}^{*}:[0,R]^{m+n}\mapsto\mathbbm{R}^{m+n}
which is well defined, thanks to (ii):

(27)

\vec{G}^{*}(\vec{u})=\Bigl(G_{1}^{*}(\vec{u}),\ldots,G_{m+n}^{*}(\vec{u})\Bigr),\;\;\begin{array}[]{l}G^{*}_{i}(\vec{u}):=G_{i}(u_{i},p_{\vec{u}}),\;i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\\[2.0pt]
G^{*}_{j}(\vec{u}):=G_{j}(p_{\vec{u}},u_{j}),\;j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}\end{array}

and the map
F^{*}:[0,R]^{m+n}\mapsto\mathbbm{R}
defined by

(28)

F^{*}(\vec{u}):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}G^{*}_{i}(\vec{u})\equiv\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G^{*}_{j}(\vec{u}).

Lemma 2.4
.

With the above definitions, the following properties hold.

(i)

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, the map
\vec{u}\mapsto G^{*}_{i}(\vec{u})
fulfills

\partial_{u_{i}}G^{*}_{i}\leq L_{i}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\forall h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\},\,h\neq i\colon\;\;\partial_{u_{h}}G^{*}_{i}\leq 0.

For each
j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
, the map
\vec{u}\mapsto G^{*}_{j}(\vec{u})
fulfills

\partial_{u_{j}}G^{*}_{j}\geq-L_{j}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\forall h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\},\,h\neq j\colon\;\;\partial_{u_{h}}G^{*}_{j}\geq 0.

(ii)

The map
\vec{u}\mapsto F^{*}(\vec{u})
fulfills

\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\colon\;\;\partial_{u_{i}}F^{*}\geq 0\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\forall j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}\colon\;\;\partial_{u_{j}}F^{*}\leq 0.

The above differential inequalities should be understood in the sense of distributions, e.g., “
\partial_{u_{i}}F^{*}\geq 0
” means that
F^{*}
is non-decreasing in the variable
u_{i}
.

Proof.

(i) Without loss of generality, we can fix the normalization
p_{\vec{u}}:=\min\mathcal{P}_{\vec{u}}
. Observe that

(29)

\text{the map $\vec{u}\mapsto p_{\vec{u}}$ is monotone non-decreasing in each component $u_{h}$, $h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}$}.

Indeed, let
\vec{u}\leq\vec{v}
in the sense of the per component order: for all
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}

u_{h}\leq v_{h}
. Bearing in mind formula (
23
), the monotonicity properties of the Godunov fluxes
G_{i}(\cdot,p)
and
G_{j}(p,\cdot)
and the definition of
p_{\vec{u}}
yield

0=\Phi^{in}_{\vec{u}}(p_{\vec{u}})-\Phi^{out}_{\vec{u}}(p_{\vec{u}})\leq\Phi^{in}_{\vec{v}}(p_{\vec{u}})-\Phi^{out}_{\vec{v}}(p_{\vec{u}}).

Hence the monotonicity of
\Phi^{in}_{\vec{v}}-\Phi^{out}_{\vec{v}}
exhibited in the proof of Lemma
2.3
(ii) and the normalization of
p_{\vec{u}}
ensure that
p_{\vec{u}}\leq p_{\vec{v}}
, which proves (
29
).

Now the monotonicity claims of (i) are immediate from the monotonicity properties of
G_{h}
and from (
29
). To prove the one-sided Lipschitz continuity properties of
G^{*}_{h}
claimed in (i), let us focus on
h=1
. The other cases are proved in the same way. Given
\vec{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m+n})
and
\vec{v}=(v_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m+n})
with
v_{1}>u_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle G^{*}_{1}(\vec{v})-G^{*}_{1}(\vec{u})

\displaystyle=G_{1}(v_{1},p_{\vec{v}})-G_{1}(u_{1},p_{\vec{u}})

\displaystyle=G_{1}(v_{1},p_{\vec{v}})-G_{1}(u_{1},p_{\vec{v}})+G_{1}(u_{1},p_{\vec{v}})-G_{1}(u_{1},p_{\vec{u}})

\displaystyle\leq G_{1}(v_{1},p_{\vec{v}})-G_{1}(u_{1},p_{\vec{v}})\leq L_{1}(v_{1}-u_{1}),

which proves the claim.

(ii) The monotonicity properties of
F^{*}
readily stem from (
29
). If we look at the dependence of
F^{*}
in
u_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, it is enough to represent
F^{*}
with the last expression in (
28
) and combine (
29
) with the monotonicity of
G_{j}(\cdot,b)
,
j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
. If we look at the dependence of
F^{*}
in
u_{j}
,
j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
, then we represent
F^{*}
with the first expression in (
28
) and use the monotonicity of
G_{i}(a,\cdot)
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Actually, in the context of Lemma
2.4
(i) we can also prove that
G^{*}_{i}
(resp.,
G^{*}_{j}
) is monotone non-decreasing (resp., non-increasing) in the argument
u_{i}
(resp.,
u_{j}
), and therefore it is
L_{i}
(resp.,
L_{j}
) Lipschitz continuous. Indeed, it is enough to represent, e.g.,

G^{*}_{1}
by the following expression derived from (
19
):

G^{*}_{1}(p_{\vec{u}})=-\sum_{i=2}^{m}G_{i}(u_{i},p_{\vec{u}})+\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p_{\vec{u}},u_{j}),

and exploit (
29
).
However, we do not stress these finer properties of dependence of
G^{*}_{i}
on
u_{i}
because they are not essential for the subsequent analysis.

Now, we are ready to explore the crucial “dissipativity” properties of
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
.
For any
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
let
q_{h}:[0,R]^{2}\to\mathbbm{R}
denote the Kruzhkov entropy flux

(30)

q_{h}(u,v)=\sign(u-v)(f_{h}(u)-f_{h}(v)).

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
\vec{k}_{1}
,
\vec{k}_{2}
in
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
with
\vec{k}_{\ell}=(k_{1}^{\ell},\ldots,k_{m+n}^{\ell})
,
\ell=1,\,2
, there holds

(31)

\Delta(\vec{k}_{1},\vec{k}_{2}):=\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(k_{i}^{1},k_{i}^{2})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}q_{j}(k_{j}^{1},k_{j}^{2})\geq 0.

Inequality (
31
) will be exploited in this work to prove that the solutions we consider satisfy a generalized Kato’s inequality. The
\mathbf{L}^{1}
-contraction property and uniqueness will follow.

Proof.

To increase readability we use the shorter notation
s_{h}=\sign(k_{h}^{1}-k_{h}^{2})
, for
h=1,\ldots,m+n
, and we adopt the convention
\sign(0)=0
. Up to reordering the incoming and outgoing roads we can assume that
s_{i}\geq 0
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}
,
s_{i}=-1
for
i=\alpha+1
,
s_{i}\leq 0
for
i\in\{\alpha+1,\ldots,m\}
,
s_{j}\geq 0
for
j\in\{m+1,\ldots,\beta\}
,
s_{j}=-1
for
i=\beta+1
and
s_{i}\leq 0
for
i\in\{\beta+1,\ldots,m+n\}
.

Intuitively we deal with three cases. Actually, the third case is more general and includes the first two.

Case 1:

Assume that
s_{i}s_{j}\geq 0
for all
(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
.

Case 2:

Assume that
s_{i}s_{j}\leq 0
for all
(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
.

Case 3:

Let
\alpha\neq 1,\,m
and
\beta\neq m+1,\,m+n
.

Case 1
This is the easiest case. The definition of
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
implies that

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(k_{i}^{1},k_{i}^{2})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}q_{j}(k_{j}^{1},k_{j}^{2})=\pm\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(f_{i}(k_{i}^{1})-f_{i}(k_{i}^{2})\right)\mp\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}\left(f_{j}(k_{j}^{1})-f_{j}(k_{j}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\pm\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(k_{i}^{1},p_{1})\mp\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p_{1},k_{j}^{1})\mp\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{2})\pm\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p_{2},k_{j}^{2})=0,

where we call
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
the values of
p
associated respectively to
\vec{k}_{1}
and
\vec{k}_{2}
.

Case 2

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(k_{i}^{1},k_{i}^{2})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}q_{j}(k_{j}^{1},k_{j}^{2})=\pm\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(f_{i}(k_{i}^{1})-f_{i}(k_{i}^{2})\right)\pm\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}\left(f_{j}(k_{j}^{1})-f_{j}(k_{j}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\pm 2\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(k_{i}^{1},p_{1})\mp 2\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{2}).

Assume, to fix the ideas, that
s_{i}>0
, and
s_{j}<0
. Then the expression above writes as

(34)

2F^{*}(\vec{k}^{1})-2F^{*}(\vec{k}^{2})

with the notation (
28
), and this expression is for sure non negative thanks to the monotonicity properties of the function
F^{*}
established in Lemma
2.4
(ii).

Case 3
In this case we define the vector
\vec{w}
, whose components satisfy
w_{h}=\min\{k_{h}^{1},\,k_{h}^{2}\}
. It is important to notice that the vector
\vec{w}
does not belong (in general) to
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
.
From the proof of Lemma
2.4
(see (
29
)) we deduce that
p_{\vec{w}}\leq\min\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
where we denoted
p_{s}:=p_{\vec{k}^{s}}
,
s=1,2
. We have, using the notation (
23
) and the fact that
w_{i}=k_{i}^{1}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
w_{j}=k_{j}^{2}
for
j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(k_{i}^{1},k_{i}^{2})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{1},p_{1})-G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}s_{i}\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{1},p_{1})-G_{i}(w_{i},p_{2})\right)+\sum_{i=\alpha+1}^{m}s_{i}\left(G_{i}(w_{i},p_{1})-G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{2})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}s_{i}\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{1},p_{1})-G_{i}(w_{i},p_{\vec{w}})\right)+\sum_{i=\alpha+1}^{m}s_{i}\left(G_{i}(w_{i},p_{\vec{w}})-G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sign(k_{i}^{1}-w_{i})\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{1},p_{1})-G_{i}(w_{i},p_{\vec{w}})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sign(k_{i}^{2}-w_{i})\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{2})-G_{i}(w_{i},p_{\vec{w}})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi^{in}_{\vec{k}^{1}}(p_{1})-\Phi^{in}_{\vec{w}}(p_{\vec{w}})-\sum_{i=\alpha+1}^{m}\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{1},p_{1})-G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{\vec{w}})\right)

\displaystyle+\Phi^{in}_{\vec{k}^{2}}(p_{2})-\Phi^{in}_{\vec{w}}(p_{\vec{w}})-\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{2})-G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{\vec{w}})\right).

Analogous computations on the sum involving the outgoing roads give a similar result. Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(k_{i}^{1},k_{i}^{2})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}q_{j}(k_{j}^{1},k_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{i=\alpha+1}^{m}\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{1},p_{1})-G_{i}(k_{i}^{i},p_{w})\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\left(G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{2})-G_{i}(k_{i}^{2},p_{w})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=\beta+1}^{m+n}\left(G_{j}(k_{j}^{1},p_{1})-G_{i}(k_{j}^{1},p_{w})\right)+\sum_{j=m+1}^{\beta}\left(G_{j}(k_{j}^{2},p_{2})-G_{j}(k_{j}^{2},p_{w})\right).

Each of the four summands in the right hand side is positive, therefore the desired inequality holds.
∎

2.2.
Riemann problem at the junction

In this section we discuss the Riemann solver at the junction associated to the vanishing viscosity limit, i.e. to the Definition
1.2
of admissible solution. A very general definition of Riemann solver at junctions is provided in
[
10
, Def. 2.3]
. In the framework of the present paper, such definition specializes as follows

Definition 2.6
.

The Riemann solver associated to the vanishing viscosity limit is a function

(35)

\mathcal{RS}:[0,R]^{m+n}\to[0,R]^{m+n},\qquad\mathcal{RS}(\vec{u}_{0})=\vec{u},

with the following properties:

(1)

There exists
p\in[0,R]
such that

(36)

\displaystyle f_{i}(u_{i})=G_{i}(u^{0}_{i},p)\quad\text{if }i\in\{1,\ldots,m\};

(37)

\displaystyle f_{j}(u_{j})=G_{j}(p,u^{0}_{j})\quad\text{if }j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\},

where
G_{i}
and
G_{j}
are the Godunov fluxes associated to
f_{i}
and
f_{j}
respectively, and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(u_{i})=\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}f_{j}(u_{j})
.

(2)

The consistency condition
\mathcal{RS}\left(\mathcal{RS}(\vec{u}_{0})\right)=\mathcal{RS}(\vec{u}_{0})
holds for all
\vec{u}_{0}
in
[0,R]^{m+n}
.

It is clear from the definition above that
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
coincides with the set of
equilibria
, see
[
10
, Def. 2.5]
, for the Riemann Solver obtained as vanishing viscosity limit. The next lemma claim that a Riemann solver in the sense of Definition
2.6
exists.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any given initial condition
\vec{u}_{0}
in
[0,R]^{m+n}
there exists a self-similar function
\vec{\rho}=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m+n})
in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}\left(\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n}\right)
which is an admissible entropy solution of the Riemann problem at the junction in the sense of Definition
1.2
. In particular, this means that the vector
\vec{\gamma\rho}
of traces of this solution at the junction belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
.

Proof.

Given
\vec{u}_{0}
in
[0,R]^{m+n}
we apply Lemma
2.3
and find
p:=p_{\vec{u}_{0}}\in[0,R]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(u_{i}^{0},p)=\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p,u_{j}^{0})
. Then we consider the
h
initial boundary value problems with constant data

(38)

\begin{cases}\rho_{h,t}+f_{h}(\rho_{h})_{x}=0,&\qquad\text{on }]0,T[\times\Omega_{h},\\
\rho_{h}(t,0)=p,&\qquad\text{on }]0,T[,\\
\rho_{h}(0,x)=u^{0}_{h},&\qquad\text{on }\Omega_{h}.\end{cases}

Call
\rho_{h}
the Kruzhkov entropy weak solution to (
38
) and
\gamma_{h}\rho_{h}
its (strong) trace at
\partial\Omega_{h}
, satisfying the boundary condition in the sense of Bardos-Le Roux-Nédélec.
Because the solution is unique and the problem is invariant under the scaling
(t,x)\mapsto(ct,cx)
for all
c>0
, the solution is self-similar, i.e., each of the components
\rho_{h}
depends only on the ratio
\frac{x}{t}
.
To conclude the proof, it is enough to observe that

(39)

\displaystyle G_{h}(\gamma_{h}\rho_{h},p)

\displaystyle=f_{h}(\gamma_{h}\rho_{h})=G_{h}(u_{h}^{0},p),\quad\text{if }h\leq m,

\displaystyle G_{h}(p,\gamma_{h}\rho_{h})

\displaystyle=f_{h}(\gamma_{h}\rho_{h})=G_{h}(p,u_{h}^{0}),\quad\text{otherwise},

because in this case
\vec{\gamma\rho}
fulfills the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
with
p=p_{\vec{u}_{0}}
and consequently, one sees that
\vec{\rho}
is an admissible solution of the Riemann problem at the junction.
Equalities (
39
) follow from the observations of
[
18
]
. For the sake of completeness,
let’s point out that, e.g., for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

(40)

\gamma_{i}\rho_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{argmin}_{[u^{0}_{i},p]}f_{i},\quad\text{if }u^{0}_{i}\leq p,\\
&\text{argmax}_{[p,u^{0}_{i}]}f_{i},\quad\text{if }u^{0}_{i}\geq p.\end{aligned}\right.

With this value
\gamma_{i}\rho_{i}
, the classical Riemann problem
with endstates
u^{0}_{i}
and
\gamma_{i}\rho_{i}
is solved only with the waves of negative speed while the
classical Riemann problem
with endstates
\gamma_{i}\rho_{i}
and
p
is solved only with the waves of positive speed, so that the classical Riemann problem
with endstates
u^{0}_{i}
and
p
is solved by juxtaposition of the two. The definition of the Godunov flux in §
1.1.1
ensures that the values
f_{i}(\gamma_{i}\rho_{i})
,
G_{i}(u^{0}_{i},\gamma_{i}\rho_{i})
,
G_{i}(\gamma_{i}\rho_{i},p)
and

Remark 3
.

Given
\vec{u}\in[0,R]^{m+n}

be defined by (

According to the above proof, the self-similar admissible solution

(the flux is outgoing from

for

and incoming into

for

Lemma

(i) states that each flux

is one-sided Lipschitz with respect to

and monotone with respect to

for all

Observe that the last equality in (

These properties will permit us to formulate a monotone, in the sense of

]
, conservative finite volume scheme for approximation of admissible solutions of (

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\vec{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+n})
in
[0,R]^{m+n}
be such that the following family of inequalities holds

(41)

\forall\vec{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m+n})\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}\colon\;\;\;\Delta(\vec{u},\vec{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(u_{i},k_{i})-\!\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}q_{j}(u_{j},k_{j})\geq 0.

Then
\vec{u}
is in
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
.
Moreover, being understood that the fluxes
f_{h}
,
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
, fulfill the condition
(F)
, the conclusion “
\vec{u}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}
” still holds if in (
41
), the condition “
\vec{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}
” is replaced by the condition
“
\vec{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
”, where
\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
is the subset of
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
described by

(42)

\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}=\left\{\begin{aligned} \vec{k}=&(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m+n})\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}:\>\exists p\in[0,R]\>\text{such that}\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(k_{i},p)=\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p,k_{j}),\\
&\forall s\in I[k_{i},p]\setminus\{k_{i}\}\quad(p-k_{i})(f_{i}(s)-f_{i}(k_{i}))>0,\>\text{for }i=1,\ldots,m,\\
&\forall s\in I[p,k_{j}]\setminus\{k_{j}\}\quad(k_{j}-p)(f_{j}(s)-f_{j}(k_{j}))>0,\>\text{for }j=m+1,\ldots,m+n\end{aligned}\right\}.

Proof.

First, let us prove the result under the assumption (
41
). Take
\vec{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m+n})
as initial condition for a Riemann problem at the junction. Then consider the associated solution
\vec{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m+n})
and the traces
\vec{\gamma v}
, as in the proof on Lemma
2.7
. We know that
\vec{\gamma v}
is in
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
, and therefore by the assumption (
41
),

(43)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(u_{i},\gamma_{i}v_{i})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}q_{j}(u_{j},\gamma_{j}v_{j})\geq 0.

By construction we have (see the proof of Lemma
2.7
)
f_{h}(\gamma_{h}v_{h})=G_{h}(u_{h},p)=G_{h}(\gamma_{h}v_{h},p)
if
h\leq m
,
f_{h}(\gamma_{h}v_{h})=G_{h}(p,u_{h})=G_{h}(p,\gamma_{h}v_{h})
if
h>m
. Moreover, by maximum principle,
\gamma_{h}v_{h}
in between
u_{h}
and
p
. This means that

(44)

f_{i}(u_{i})-f_{i}(\gamma_{i}v_{i})=f_{i}(u_{i})-G_{i}(u_{i},p)=\begin{cases}f_{i}(u_{i})-\min_{s\in[u_{i},p]}f_{i}(s)\geq 0&\quad\text{if }u_{i}\leq p,\\
f_{i}(u_{i})-\max_{s\in[p,u_{i}]}f_{i}(s)\leq 0&\quad\text{if }u_{i}\geq p,\end{cases}

then
f_{i}(u_{i})-f_{i}(\gamma_{i}v_{i})
is non negative when
u_{i}\leq\gamma_{i}v_{i}
and non positive when
u_{i}\geq\gamma_{i}v_{i}
. This means that

(45)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(u_{i},\gamma_{i}v_{i})=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}|f_{i}(u_{i})-f_{i}(\gamma_{i}v_{i})|.

In the same way we show that

(46)

\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}q_{j}(u_{j},\gamma_{j}v_{j})=\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}|f_{j}(u_{j})-f_{j}(\gamma_{j}v_{j})|.

The sum of non positive terms can be non negative only if all the terms vanish. Therefore for any
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
we have
f_{h}(u_{h})=f_{h}(\gamma_{h}v_{h})=G_{h}(u_{h},p)
.
In view of (
16
), this shows that
\vec{v}
belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
.

Now, let us prove the last claim of the lemma. It is easily seen from the comparison of (
17
) and (
42
) that due to assumption
(F)
on the shape of the fluxes, the difference between the subsets
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
and
\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
of
[0,R]^{m+n}
consists in
m+n
-uplets
\vec{k}
for which at least one of the following
(m+n)
events occurs:

(A_{i})

f_{i}(k_{i})=f_{i}(p)
and
k_{i}<p
;

(B_{j})

f_{j}(k_{j})=f_{j}(p)
and
k_{j}>p
.

Indeed, if, for instance, there holds

(47)

\forall s\in I[k_{1},p]\colon\;(p-u_{1})(f_{1}(s)-f_{1}(k_{1}))\geq 0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\exists s_{0}\in I[k_{1},p]\setminus\{k_{1}\}\;\,\text{s.t.}\;\,f_{1}(s_{0})=f_{1}(k_{1}),

then the shape assumption
(F)
tells us that
s_{0}=p
and, moreover,
k_{1}<p
.

For the sake of being definite, assume that among
(A_{i})_{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}
,
(B_{j})_{j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}}
the only event that occurs is
(A_{1})
, namely,
f_{1}(k_{1})=f_{1}(p)
,
k_{1}<p
but neither
(A_{i})
,
i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
nor
(B_{j})
,
j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
occur.

Observe that in this case, the
(m+n)
-uplet
{\vec{k}}^{\prime}:=(p,k_{2},\ldots,k_{m+n})
belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
. Indeed, it belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
since it corresponds to the same value
p
, while the event (
47
) does not occur any more since for
k_{1}^{\prime}=p
,
I[k_{1}^{\prime},p]\setminus\{k_{1}^{\prime}\}
is empty.
Moreover, whatever be

, we have

q_{1}(u_{1},k_{1})=\sign(u_{1}-k_{1})(f_{1}(u_{1})-f_{1}(k_{1}))\geq\sign(u_{1}-p)(f_{1}(u_{1})-f_{1}(p))=q_{1}(u_{1},p).

Consequently,

\Delta(\vec{u},\vec{k})\geq\Delta(\vec{u},\vec{k}^{\prime})\geq 0,

where we have used the assumption of the last claim of the lemma and the fact that
{\vec{k}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
.

The general case is fully analogous,
so that we find
\Delta(\vec{u},\vec{k})\geq 0
not only for
\vec{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
, but for all
\vec{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}
.
We are reduced to the first claim of the lemma. This ends the proof.
∎

Adapted entropies and another reformulation of admissibility

2.5.
Equivalence of the three formulations of admissibility

As we already announced, the three definitions of solution admissibility actually describe one and the same notion.

2.9

Proof.

We only need to establish equivalence between

\bullet
We prove that Definition

3.1.
Uniqueness of admissible solutions of (
1
)

The goal of this section is to prove the following stability result

Proposition 3.1
.

For any given initial condition
\vec{u}_{0}=(u^{0}_{1},\ldots,u^{0}_{m+n})
in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Gamma;\mathbbm{R}^{m+n})
there exists at most one
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
-entropy solution
\rho
in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})
.

Moreover, if
\vec{\rho}
and
\vec{\hat{\rho}}
are the
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
-entropy solutions corresponding respectively to the initial conditions
\vec{u}_{0}
and
\vec{v}_{0}
, (
57
) holds and in particular, the
\mathbf{L}^{1}
-contraction estimate holds whenever the right-hand side is finite:

(58)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\left\|\rho_{i}(t)-\hat{\rho}_{i}(t)\right\|}_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbbm{R}_{-};\mathbbm{R})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}{\left\|\rho_{j}(t)-\hat{\rho}_{j}(t)\right\|}_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbbm{R}_{+};\mathbbm{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\left\|u^{0}_{i}-v^{0}_{i}\right\|}_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbbm{R}_{-};\mathbbm{R})}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}{\left\|u^{0}_{j}-v^{0}_{j}\right\|}_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(\mathbbm{R}_{+};\mathbbm{R})}.

Proof.

The first part of the proof is devoted to establish a Kato’s type inequality for
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
-entropy solutions.

Let
\xi
be a test function in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R};\mathbbm{R}_{+})
and define, for
k>0
,

(59)

\xi_{k}(t,x)=\xi(t,x)\min\left\{1,\frac{(|x|-k)^{+}}{k}\right\}.

Then, as
\rho_{h}
and
\hat{\rho}_{h}
are Kruzhkov entropy weak solutions in the interior of
\Omega_{h}
, by a standard doubling of variable argument we get

(60)

\sum_{h=1}^{m+n}\left(-\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\!\!\int_{\Omega_{h}}\!\!\left\{|\rho_{h}-\hat{\rho}_{h}|\xi_{k,t}+q_{h}(\rho_{h},\hat{\rho}_{h})\xi_{k,x}\right\}\,dx\,dt-\int_{\Omega_{h}}\!\!|u^{0}_{h}(x)-v^{0}_{h}(x)|\xi_{k}(0,x)\,dx\right)\leq 0.

An explicit computation shows that

(61)

\xi_{k,x}(t,x)=\xi_{x}(t,x)\min\left\{1,\frac{(|x|-k)^{+}}{k}\right\}+\frac{1}{k}\xi(t,x)(\mathds{1}_{]k,2k[}(x)-\mathds{1}_{]-2k,-k[}(x)),

then (
60
) rewrites as

(62)

\displaystyle\sum_{h=1}^{m+n}\left(-\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{h}}\left\{|\rho_{h}-\hat{\rho}_{h}|\xi_{k,t}+q_{h}(\rho_{h},\hat{\rho}_{h})\xi_{x}\min\left\{1,\frac{(|x|-k)^{+}}{k}\right\}\right\}\,dx\,dt\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=1}^{m+n}\frac{1}{k}\left(\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{h}\cap]-2k,-k[}\!\!q_{h}(\rho_{h},\hat{\rho}_{h})\xi\,dx\,dt-\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{h}\cap]k,2k[}\!\!q_{h}(\rho_{h},\hat{\rho}_{h})\xi\,dx\,dt\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=1}^{m+n}\left(-\int_{\Omega_{h}}|u^{0}_{h}(x)-v^{0}_{h}(x)|\xi_{k}(0,x)\,dx\right)\leq 0.

We have that

(63)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to 0^{+}}\sum_{h=1}^{m+n}\frac{1}{k}\left(\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{h}\cap]-2k,-k[}\!\!q_{h}(\rho_{h},\hat{\rho}_{h})\xi\,dx\,dt-\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{h}\cap]k,2k[}\!\!q_{h}(\rho_{h},\hat{\rho}_{h})\xi\,dx\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}(\gamma^{i}\rho_{i},\gamma^{i}\hat{\rho}_{i})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}q_{j}(\gamma^{j}\rho_{j},\gamma^{j}\hat{\rho}_{j})\right)\xi(t,0)\,dt,

and this term is positive due to the positivity assumption on
\xi
and the fact that
\vec{\gamma\rho}
and
\vec{\gamma\hat{\rho}}
are in
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
.
Therefore by taking the limit as
k
tends to
0
of the whole left hand side in (
62
) one can conclude that

(64)

\sum_{h=1}^{m+n}\left(-\int_{\mathbbm{R}_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{h}}\left\{|\rho_{h}-\hat{\rho}_{h}|\xi_{t}+q_{h}(\rho_{h},\hat{\rho}_{h})\xi_{x}\right\}\,dx\,dt-\int_{\Omega_{h}}|u^{0}_{h}(x)-v^{0}_{h}(x)|\xi(0,x)\,dx\right)\leq 0,

which is Kato’s inequality adapted to our setting.

The conclusion of the proof is classical, following
[
25
]
. One takes
\xi(t,x)
approximating the characteristic function of the trapezoid

\{(t,x)\in\mathbbm{R}_{+}\times\mathbbm{R};\;|x|\leq M+Lt\}
where
L=\max_{h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}}\max_{u\in[0,R]}|f_{h}^{\prime}(u)|
is the appropriate Lipschitz constant and
M
is a nonnegative parameter. For a.e. fixed
t\in\mathbbm{R}_{+}
, this yields for
M>Lt
the inequality (
57
).

As
M\to+\infty
, we find (
58
) as soon as its right-hand side is finite.
∎

3.2.
A finite volume numerical scheme and existence of an admissible solution

In this section we describe a finite volume numerical scheme for the junction based on Godunov fluxes. Discretizing a fixed initial datum, we prove convergence of the discrete solutions to the unique admissible solution. We stress that there exist other numerical schemes which can be used in practice to approximate admissible solutions of (
1
): we refer to
[
1
]
for the case
m=n=1
. The choice of Godunov’s fluxes is motivated by the fact that this scheme is well-balanced, i.e., all admissible stationary solutions are scheme’s exact solutions. The proofs of our theoretical results are easier in this setting. For other schemes based on monotone numerical fluxes (e.g., Rusanov flux), convergence to an admissible solution can also be proved, but the stationary solutions should replaced by numerical profiles (cf. Section
4
where analogous viscous profiles are constructed).

We fix a space step
\Delta x
. For
\ell\in\mathbbm{Z}
and
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
, set
x^{h}_{\ell}:=\ell\Delta x
. We consider the uniform spatial mesh on each
\Omega_{h}

(65)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\ell\leq-1}(x^{i}_{\ell},x^{i}_{\ell+1})\qquad\text{on }\Omega_{i},\quad\text{for }i\leq m,

(66)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{\ell\geq 0}(x^{j}_{\ell},x^{j}_{\ell+1})\qquad\text{on }\Omega_{j},\quad\text{for }j\geq m+1,

so that the position of the junction
x=0
on the road
\Omega_{h}
corresponds to
x^{h}_{0}
.
Then we fix a time step
\Delta t
satisfying the CFL condition

(67)

\Delta t\leq\frac{\max_{h}\{L_{h}\}}{2}\Delta x,

where
L_{h}
is the Lipschitz constant of
f_{h}
.

At all cell interfaces
x^{h}_{\ell}
for
\ell\neq 0
we consider the standard Godunov flux
G_{h}
corresponding to the flux
f_{h}
. At the junction
x_{0}^{h}
we take on each road
\Omega_{h}
the Godunov flux
G_{h}^{*}
corresponding to the admissible solution of the Riemann problem at the junction, see Remark
3
. More precisely, let
\vec{u_{0}}=(u^{0}_{1},\ldots,u^{0}_{m+n})
be an initial condition in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})
. We initialize our scheme by discretizing the initial conditions

(68)

u_{k+\frac{1}{2}}^{h,0}=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{x^{h}_{\ell}}^{x^{h}_{\ell+1}}u^{0}_{h}(x)\,dx,

for all
h=1,\ldots,m+n
and for
\ell\leq-1
if
h\leq m
,
\ell\geq 0
if
h\geq m+1
.

Then
\left(u_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s+1}\right)_{h,\ell}
is obtained from
\left(u_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s}\right)_{h,\ell}
through the following scheme

(I)

We solve

(69)

\text{find $p^{s}$ in $[0,R]$ s.t.}\;\;\sum_{i=1}^{m}G_{i}(u_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{i,s},p^{s})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G_{j}(p^{s},u_{\frac{1}{2}}^{j,s})=0,

i.e., setting

(70)

\vec{u}^{\,*,s}:=\Bigl(u_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{1,s},\ldots,u_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{m,s},u_{\frac{1}{2}}^{m+1,s},u_{\frac{1}{2}}^{m+n,s}\Bigr)

we take
p^{s}:=p_{\vec{u}^{\,*,s}}
according to the notation of Lemma
2.3
.

(II)

We compute

(71)

u_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s+1}=u_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s}-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\left(\mathcal{F}^{h,s}_{\ell+1}-\mathcal{F}^{h,s}_{\ell}\right),

where

(72)

\mathcal{F}^{h,s}_{\ell}=\begin{cases}G_{h}(u_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s},u_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s})\quad&\quad\text{if }h\leq m\>\text{ and }\ell\leq-1\>\text{ or }h\geq m+1\>\text{ and }\ell\geq 1,\\
G_{h}(u_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s},p^{s})\equiv G^{*}_{h}(\vec{u}^{\,*,s})&\quad\text{if }h\leq m\>\text{ and }\ell=0,\\
G_{h}(p^{s},u_{\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s})\equiv G^{*}_{h}(\vec{u}^{\,*,s})&\quad\text{if }h\geq m+1\>\text{ and }\ell=0.\end{cases}

The stage (II) is a standard marching scheme, up to the specific definition of the fluxes for
\ell=0
. The stage (I) is implicit: once per time step, we have to find a zero of a scalar nonlinear function. Moreover, the nonlinear term in (I) is monotone and continuous
(see Lemma
2.3
and its proof) but it is not everywhere differentiable, since
G_{h}
are not everywhere differentiable. In practice,
the value
p^{s}
can be efficiently computed using the
regula falsi
method, cf.
[
1
]
.

We introduce the notation

\Gamma_{\rm{discr}}=\Bigl(\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{\ell\in\mathbbm{Z},\;\ell\leq-1\}\Bigr)\bigcup\Bigl(\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}\times\{\ell\in\mathbbm{Z},\;\ell\geq 0\}\Bigr),

for the set of all degrees of freedom at a fixed time step; the notation

U^{s}=\Bigl(u^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\Bigr)_{(h,\ell)\in\Gamma_{\rm{discr}}}

for the set of all the unknowns of the scheme at time step
s
; and the notation
\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x}\vec{u_{0}}
to indicate the piecewise constant function corresponding to the discrete solution
\left(u_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}^{h,s}\right)_{h,\ell,s}=(U^{s})_{s}
:
\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x}\vec{u_{0}}=(u^{1,\Delta x},\ldots,u^{m,\Delta x},u^{m+1,\Delta x},\ldots,u^{m+n,\Delta x})
where

(73)

\displaystyle u^{i,\Delta x}=\sum_{s\in\mathbbm{N},\>\ell\leq-1}u_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}^{i,s}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{i}\cap]\ell,\ell+1[}(x)\mathds{1}_{]s,s+1[}(t),

\displaystyle\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},

\displaystyle u^{j,\Delta x}=\sum_{s\in\mathbbm{N},\>\ell\geq 0}u_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}^{j,s}\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{j}\cap]\ell,\ell+1[}(x)\mathds{1}_{]s,s+1[}(t),

\displaystyle\quad j\in\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

The above finite volume numerical scheme is well-balanced, i.e., every element of
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
corresponds to a stationary, constant per road solution of the scheme.

Proof.

Let
\vec{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}
and denote by
\vec{k}^{\Delta x}
the associated discrete function with entries
u^{h,\Delta x}\equiv k_{h}
,
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
. Then
\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x}\vec{k}^{\Delta x}=\vec{k}^{\Delta x}
, i.e.,
\vec{k}^{\Delta x}
is a stationary solution of the scheme.
Indeed, consider for instance an incoming road
i
.
Obviously, for all
\ell<-1
the first iteration of (
71
) initialized with constant initial values
u^{i,0}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}=k_{i}

yields

u^{i,1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}=k_{i}-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}(G_{i}(k_{i},k_{i})-G_{i}(k_{i},k_{i}))=k_{i}.

Moreover, due to (
69
), by definition of
G^{*}_{i}
and of
\mathcal{G}_{VV}
we have

G_{i}(k_{i},k_{i})=f(k_{i})=G(k_{i},p_{\vec{k}})=G^{*}_{i}(\vec{k}),

so that for
\ell=-1
we still find

u^{i}_{1,-\frac{1}{2}}=k_{i}-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}(G_{i}(k_{i},k_{i})-G^{*}_{i}(\vec{k}))=k_{i}.

Similarly, we find that (
69
), (
71
) preserves the constant value
k_{j}
on the
j
th outgoing road.
∎

Remark 6
.

Due to the classical properties of Godunov fluxes recalled in §
1.1.1
, to the CFL condition
67
and to Lemma
2.4
(i) (see also Remark
3
), the scheme is monotone in the following sense:

(74)

\forall s\in\mathbbm{N}\;\forall(h,\ell)\in\Gamma_{\rm{discr}}\colon\;\;\;\;u^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}=H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(U^{s})

for some functions
H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}
that are monotone non-decreasing with respect to each of the arguments (actually each of these functions depends on at most
(m+n+1)
entries of
U^{s}
).
This implies in particular the order-preservation property

(75)

\forall(h,\ell)\in\Gamma_{\rm{discr}}\colon\;\;\;\;u^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\geq\hat{u}^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\;\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\;\forall(h,\ell)\in\Gamma_{\rm{discr}}\colon\;\;\;\;u^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\geq\hat{u}^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}.

Since, moreover, the scheme is locally conservative by definition (due, in particular, to the condition (
69
)),
and because of the Lipschitz continuity of
G_{h}
for all
h
,
the scheme is conservative. It follows by the Crandall-Tartar Lemma (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
) that the scheme is
\mathbf{L}^{1}
-contractive in the sense that the discrete analogue
of (
58
) (with
\vec{\rho}
replaced by
\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x}\vec{u_{0}}
) and the initial condition
\vec{u}_{0}
replaced by the discretized initial condition) holds true. We need a bit more specific property, which is the numerical counterpart of the Kato inequalities (
64
), in order to justify convergence to an admissible solution.

Slightly extending the usual formalism (see
[
19
]
), let
\top
, resp.,
\bot
denote (component per component, in the case of vector-valued arguments) the maximum, resp. the minimum operation on real scalars, vectors or sequences: e.g.,

(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\bot(\hat{k}_{1},\hat{k}_{2},\hat{k}_{3})=(\min\{k_{1},\hat{k}_{1}\},\min\{k_{2},\hat{k}_{2}\},\min\{k_{3},\hat{k}_{3}\}).

Proposition 3.2
.

For any initial conditions
\vec{u_{0}},\hat{\vec{u}}_{0}
in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})
the corresponding discrete solutions
of the scheme (
68
)–(
72
) satisfy
discrete Kato inequalities
. Namely,
for

(h,\ell)\in\Gamma_{\rm{discr}}
, let
Q^{h}_{\ell}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]
be defined by

\displaystyle Q^{h}_{\ell}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]=G_{h}\Bigl(u^{h,s}_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\top\hat{u}^{h,s}_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}},u^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\top\hat{u}^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\Bigr)-G_{h}\Bigl(u^{h,s}_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\bot\hat{u}^{h,s}_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}},u^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\bot\hat{u}^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\Bigr),\;\;\ell\neq 0,

\displaystyle Q^{h}_{0}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]=G^{*}_{h}(\vec{u}^{\,*,s}\top\hat{\vec{u}}^{\,*,s})-G^{*}_{h}(\vec{u}^{\,*,s}\bot\hat{\vec{u}}^{\,*,s}).

Then for all
\xi\in\mathcal{D}(]0,+\infty[\times\mathbbm{R})
such that
\xi\geq 0
and
\partial_{x}\xi=0
on
[-\Delta x/2,\Delta x/2]
,
setting
\xi^{s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}:=\xi(s\Delta t,(\ell+\frac{1}{2})\Delta x)
we have

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1}^{+\infty}\Delta t\sum_{\ell\leq-1}\Delta x|u^{i,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{u}^{i,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}|\frac{\xi^{s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\xi^{s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1}^{+\infty}\Delta t\sum_{\ell\leq-1}\Delta xQ^{i}_{\ell}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]\frac{\xi^{s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\xi^{s+1}_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}\sum_{s=1}^{+\infty}\Delta t\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\Delta x|u^{j,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{u}^{j,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}|\frac{\xi^{s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\xi^{s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta t}

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}\sum_{s=1}^{+\infty}\Delta t\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\Delta xQ^{j}_{\ell}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]\frac{\xi^{s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\xi^{s+1}_{\ell-\frac{1}{2}}}{\Delta x}\leq 0.

Observe that in Proposition
3.2
, we limit our attention to test functions constant in a neighbourhood of the junction. Thanks to this precaution, borrowed from
[
1
]
, and to the conservativity of the Riemann solver at the junction the junction
\ell=0

does not contribute to the “
Q[U,\hat{U}]\partial_{x}\xi
” term of the discrete Kato inequality.

Proof.

The argument is essentially classical in the context of monotone finite volume schemes.

First, we state the “per cell contraction principle”: for all
(h,\ell)\in\Gamma_{\rm{discr}}

(76)

\frac{|u^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{u}^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}|-|u^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{u}^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}|}{\Delta t}+\frac{Q^{h}_{\ell+1}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]-Q^{h}_{\ell}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]}{\Delta x}\leq 0,

which readily follows from the observation that for all
a,b\in\mathbbm{R}
,
|a-b|=a\top b-a\bot b
and from the monotonicity of
H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}
in (
74
):

\displaystyle|u^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{u}^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}|=u^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\top\hat{u}^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-u^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\bot\hat{u}^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle u^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\top\hat{u}^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}=H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(U^{s})\top H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(\hat{U}^{s})\leq H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(U^{s}\top\hat{U}^{s}),

\displaystyle u^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}\bot\hat{u}^{h,s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}=H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(U^{s})\bot H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(\hat{U}^{s})\geq H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(U^{s}\bot\hat{U}^{s}),

\displaystyle H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(U^{s}\top\hat{U}^{s})-H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}(U^{s}\bot\hat{U}^{s})=|u^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{u}^{h,s}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}|-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\Bigl(Q^{h}_{\ell+1}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]-Q^{h}_{\ell}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]\Bigr),

where the formula (
71
) and the definition of
Q^{h}_{\ell}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]
are used in the last line to express the function
H^{h}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}
.

Second, observe that for all
\vec{k},\hat{\vec{k}}
we have by the conservativity property underlying the definition of
\vec{G}^{*}
(see Remark
3
):

\sum_{i=1}^{m}G^{*}_{h}(\vec{k}\top\hat{\vec{k}})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G^{*}_{h}(\vec{k}\top\hat{\vec{k}})=0=\sum_{i=1}^{m}G^{*}_{h}(\vec{k}\bot\hat{\vec{k}})-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}G^{*}_{h}(\vec{k}\bot\hat{\vec{k}}).

It remains to multiply the
(h,\ell)
’s inequality in (
76
) by the nonnegative quantity
\Delta t\Delta x\xi^{s+1}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}
and sum up; the sum is finite since the support of
\xi
is compact.
Paying attention to the fact that

\xi^{s}_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\xi(s\Delta t,0)=\xi^{s}_{\frac{1}{2}}
, from the definition of
Q^{h}_{0}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]
we see that for all
s
,

(77)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Delta xQ^{i}_{0}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]\xi^{s+1}_{-\frac{1}{2}}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{m+n}\Delta xQ^{j}_{0}[U^{s},\hat{U}^{s}]\xi^{s+1}_{\frac{1}{2}}=0.

Using the Abel transformation (discrete summation by parts) on each road with respect to the time superscripts
s
and to the space subscripts
\ell
, bearing in mind
(
77
) and the fact that for all
\ell\in\mathbbm{Z}
,
\xi^{0}_{\ell+\frac{1}{2}}=0
by the choice of
\xi
, we derive the required discrete Kato inequality.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Given an initial datum
\vec{u_{0}}\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})
, the numerical scheme (
68
)–(
72
) converges to the unique admissible (in the sense of the equivalent Definitions
1.2
,
2.9
,
2.10
) solution
\vec{\rho}
of (
1
), namely,
\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x}\vec{u_{0}}\to\vec{\rho}
as
\Delta x=0
, subject to the CFL restriction (
67
) on
\Delta t
.
This ensures, in particular, existence of an admissible solution of (
1
) for every
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})
initial datum.

Proof.

The proof follows the lines of the proof given in
[
1
]
(see also
[
4
]
). Let us only provide a sketch of the key arguments.

\bullet

We start with compactly supported
\mathbf{BV}
initial data. The compactness, in the sense of the a.e. convergence, of the family
\Bigl(\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x}\vec{u_{0}}\Bigr)_{\Delta x\in(0,1)}
of discrete solutions is obtained with the
\mathbf{BV}_{loc}
technique introduced in
[
11
]
. It relies upon the monotonicity and the Crandall-Tartar lemma (see Remark
6
).

The compactness permits to define
\vec{\rho}
as a limit of some sequence of discrete solutions
\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x_{r}}\vec{u_{0}}
,
\Delta_{r}\to 0
. At the end of the proof, having proved convergence to the unique admissible solution with datum
\vec{u_{0}}
, by the classical argument we can bypass the extraction of a sequence and get convergence of
\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x}\vec{u_{0}}
,
\Delta x\to 0
.

\bullet
It is classical (see
[
19
]
) to derive that for every
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
,
\rho_{h}
fulfills the first property required in Definitions
1.2
,
2.9
,
2.10
, namely, the Kruzhkov entropy inequalities (
11
) hold. In order to justify that
\vec{\rho}
is an admissible solution, we just need to assess the second property in Definition
2.10
, i.e., the adapted entropy inequalities (
48
) that involve the junction.

\bullet
The main ingredient of the proof is the discrete Kato inequality proved in Proposition
3.2
, where we choose
\hat{\vec{u}}_{0}=\vec{k}
with
\vec{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}
. Observe that by Lemma
3.2
, we have
\mathcal{S}^{\Delta x_{r}}\hat{\vec{u}}_{0}\equiv\vec{k}
; passing to the limit as
\Delta_{r}\to 0
, we will indeed derive the adapted entropy inequalities (
48
) for
\rho
and complete the proof for compactly supported data of bounded variation.

Following
[
1
]
, observe that test functions whose
x
-derivative vanishes near
x=0
are dense (e.g., in the
\mathbf{C}^{1}
topology) in
\mathcal{D}(]0,+\infty[\times\mathbbm{R})
. Starting with the inequalities of Proposition
3.2
,
with the same arguments as in the previous step (see
[
19
,
1
]
) we pass to the limit and find (
48
) first for such specific test functions
\xi
, and then (by density) for general test functions.

\bullet
Finally, as in
[
4
,
1
]
, in two steps we extend the convergence result to general
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
data
\vec{u_{0}}
. Extension
to
\mathbf{L}^{1}\cap\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
data follows by the density of compactly supported
\mathbf{BV}
data in
\mathbf{L}^{1}
topology, with the help of
\mathbf{L}^{1}
contractivity of both the admissible solution semigroup and the discrete solutions semigroups. Extension to
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}
data is due to the property of finite domain of dependence
(
57
) and to its discrete counterpart that follows from (
71
) and from the CFL condition.
∎

4.
Vanishing viscosity limits are admissible solutions of (
1
)

This section is devoted to the proof that the solutions obtained as limit of vanishing viscosity approximations are
admissible solutions in the sense of Definitions
1.2
,
2.9
,
2.10
, and are therefore unique.
The result follows from the combination of two ingredients: the construction of suitably many vanishing viscosity profiles, and the
\mathbf{L}^{1}
contraction
property known for the vanishing viscosity approximation. This ensures that vanishing viscosity solutions satisfy a family of adapted entropy inequalities
which is sufficiently large to fit the last claim of Theorem
2.11
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\vec{k}
in
\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
,
there exists
p\in[0,R]
and
\vec{\rho^{\varepsilon}}=(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{1},\ldots,\rho^{\varepsilon}_{m+n})
in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Gamma;[0,R]^{m+n})
such that
for all
h\in\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
,
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{h}
solves the ODE problem

(78)

\begin{cases}f_{h}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{h})_{x}=\varepsilon(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{h})_{xx},\quad\text{ in }\>\Omega_{h}\\
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{h}(0)=p,\\
\lim_{x\in\Omega_{h},\,|x|\to+\infty}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{h}(x)=k_{h}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We take the value
p\in[0,R]
that ensures that
\vec{k}\in\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
, according to the definition (
42
) of
\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
.
We consider here the case
h\leq m
, the other case being analogous. Let us integrate both sides of (
78
) on
]-\infty,x]

(79)

\varepsilon(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i})_{x}(x)=F_{i}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i}(x)),\;\;\text{where}\;F_{i}:\rho\in[0,R]\mapsto f_{i}(\rho)-f_{i}(k_{i}),

being understood that one should have
(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i})_{x}\to 0
as
x\to-\infty
.
Observe that by the definition of
\mathcal{G}_{VV}^{o}
,
k_{i}
is the only zero of
F_{i}
on
I[k_{i},p]
. Assume, for the sake of being definite, that
k_{i}<p
(the case
k_{i}>p
is analogous, while in the case
k_{i}=p
we have the obvious solution
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i}=k
).
Then, the map

P:]k_{i},p]\to]-\infty,0],\;\;r\mapsto-\int_{r}^{p}\frac{\varepsilon}{f_{i}(s)-f_{i}(k_{i})}\,ds

is well defined and strictly increasing, i.e., it admits the inverse
P^{-1}
satisfying
P^{-1}(0)=p
,
\lim_{x\to-\infty}P^{-1}(x)=k_{i}
,

\lim_{x\to-\infty}(P^{-1})^{\prime}(x)=0
. We find that
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i}
solves (
79

(80)

-x=\int_{x}^{0}\frac{\varepsilon\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i,x}}{f_{i}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i})-f_{i}(k_{i})}\,dx=-P(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i}),

i.e.
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{i}=P^{-1}(x)
is the required solution.
∎

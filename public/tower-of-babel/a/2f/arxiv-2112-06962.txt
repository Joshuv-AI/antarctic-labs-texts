Chung-Kwong Chan, Hui-Chun Zhang, Xi-Ping Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a vector-valued one-phase Bernoulli-type free boundary problem on a metric measure space ( X , d , μ ) (X,d,\mu) with Riemannian curvature-dimension condition R ​ C ​ D ​ ( K , N ) RCD(K,N) . We first prove the existence and the local Lipschitz regularity of the solutions, provided that the space X X is non-collapsed, i.e. μ \mu is the N N -dimensional Hausdorff measure of X X . And then we show that the free boundary of the solutions is an ( N − 1 ) (N-1) -dimensional topological manifold away from a relatively closed subset of Hausdorff dimension ⩽ N − 3 \leqslant N-3 .



1.
Introduction

Since the pioneer work of Alt-Caffarelli
[
AC81
]
, Dirichlet problems with free boundary on
Euclidean spaces have been extensively studied.
Consider the critical points of the one-phase Bernoulli energy functional:

(1.1)

J(u):=\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}+\chi_{\left\{u>0\right\}})\mathrm{d}x\text{,}\\

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open set. The domain
\Omega_{u}:=\{x\in\Omega\mid u(x)>0\}
is a priori unspecified and
\partial\{u>0\}\cap\Omega
is the
free boundary
. From
[
AC81
]
, the fundamental results about existence and regularity of minimizers of
J
and regularity
of free boundaries were established (see also
[
Caf87
,
Caf89
,
Caf88
]
for a view of viscosity solutions).
Recently, the studies on free boundary problems have been extended to the
fully nonlinear uniformly elliptic operators
[
DSFS15
]
and
uniformly elliptic operators with variable coefficients
[
Tre20a
,
Tre20b
]
.
In the meantime, the vector-valued Bernoulli-type free boundary problem have been systemically studied by Caffarelli-Shahgholian-Yeressian
[
CSY18
]
, Mazzoleni-Terracini-Velichkov
[
MTV17
,
MTV20
]
and Kriventsov-Lin
[
KL18
]
.
We refer the readers to surveys
[
FS15
,
F18
,
DSFS19
,
CS20
]
and their references for recent developments of the free boundary problems in the Euclidean settings.

In this paper, we will extend the study on one-phase Bernoulli-type free boundary problems from the Euclidean setting to the setting of
non-smooth
spaces satisfying a synthetic notion of lower bounds of Ricci curvature. More precisely, letting
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space (a complete metric space
(X,d)
equipped with a Radon measure
\mu
with
{\rm supp}(\mu)=X
), we assume that it satisfies the Riemannian curvature-dimension condition
RCD(K,N)
for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
. The main examples in the class of
RCD(K,N)
spaces include the Ricci limit spaces in the Cheeger-Colding theory
[
CC96
,
CC97
,
CC00
]
and finite dimensional Alexandrov spaces with curvature bounded from below (see
[
Pet11
]
and
[
ZZ10
, Appendix A]
).
The parameters
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty]
play the role of “Ricci curvature
\geqslant K
and dimension
\leqslant N
” in Riemannian geometry.
The theory of
RCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces and their geometric analysis have fast and remarkable developments, see
[
Amb18
]
for a recent survey on this topic.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD(K,N)
metric measure space for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
, and let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain. The Bernoulli-type energy functional is given by

(1.2)

J_{Q}({\bf u})=\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})\mathrm{d}\mu,\ \ \

where
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\ (=L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu))
with

(1.3)

0<Q_{\min}\leqslant Q(x)\leqslant Q_{\max}<+\infty\quad\mu-{\rm a.e.}\ x\in\Omega

for two positive real numbers
Q_{\min}
and
Q_{\max}
. According to
[
Che99
]
, it is now known that the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
is well-defined.
Given a boundary data
\mathbf{g}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\left[0,+\infty)^{m}\right)
, we consider the minimization problem:

(1.4)

\min_{{\bf u}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}}J_{Q}({\bf u}),\quad\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}:=\left\{{\bf u}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\left[0,+\infty\right)^{m})\mid{\bf u}-\mathbf{g}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\right\}\text{.}

It is a cooperative vector-valued one-phase Bernoulli-type free boundary problem.

Definition 1.1
.

A map
{\bf u}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
is called a
local minimizer
of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) if there exists some
\varepsilon_{\bf u}>0
such that
J_{Q}({\bf u})\leqslant J_{Q}({\bf v})
for every
{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
with
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
, where

(1.5)

\dist({\bf u},{\bf v}):=\left\|{\bf u}-{\bf v}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}+\left\|\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}-\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}\right|>0\right\}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\text{.}

If
\varepsilon_{\bf u}=+\infty
, we call that it is a
minimizer
of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
).

The fundamental problems include the existence and regularity of minimizers (or local minimizers) of
J_{Q}
and the regularity of the free boundary
\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega
.

1.1.
The Bernoulli-type free boundary problems on Euclidean spaces

We first recall some classical results on this problem in the Euclidean setting, i.e.,
(X,d,\mu)=(\mathbb{R}^{n},d_{\rm Eucl},\mathcal{L}^{n}).

In the seminal work of Alt-Caffarell
[
AC81
]
,
for the scalar case where
m=1
, they established the following:

\bullet
the existence of the minimizer of (
1.4
),

\bullet
Lipschitz continuity of any local minimizer
u
, and

\bullet
when
Q\in C^{\alpha}
, the free boundary
\partial\left\{u>0\right\}\cap\Omega
is a
C^{1,\alpha}
-manifold away from a relatively closed subset
\mathcal{S}_{u}
with
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\mathcal{S}_{u})=0
.

Nowadays, it is well-known that the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{u}
has
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{S}_{u})\leqslant n-k^{\ast}
for some
k^{\ast}\in\{5,6,7\}
(by Weiss
[
Wei99
]
, Caffarelli-Jerison-Kenig
[
CJK04
]
, De Silva-Jerison
[
DSJ09
]
and Jerison-Savin
[
JS15
]
).
Edelen-Engelstein
[
EE19
]
explored the rectifiable structure of the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{u}
.

Recently, the vector-valued case where
m\geqslant 2
, of Bernoulli-type free boundary problem for local minimizers of
J({\bf u})
have been systemically studied by Caffarelli-Shahgholian-Yeressian
[
CSY18
]
, Mazzoleni-Terracini-Velichkov
[
MTV17
,
MTV20
]
and Kriventsov-Lin
[
KL18
]
. See also
[
Tre20a
,
Tre20b
]
for
the uniformly elliptic operators with variable coefficients.

Theorem 1.2

(Caffarelli-Shahgholian-Yeressian
[
CSY18

.

Let

For each

(1)

(Lipschitz regularity)

{\bf u}

\Omega
.

(2)

(Local finiteness of perimeter and Euler-Lagrange equation)
If

, then the free boundary has locally finite perimeter, and hence the reduced boundary
\partial_{\rm red}\{|{\bf u}|>0\}

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. point

,
i=1,2,\cdots,m
, and
\eta>0
, the non-tangential limit

(1.6)

w_{i}(x):=\lim_{y\in\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\{-(y-x)\cdot\nu_{\{|{\bf u}|>0\}}(x)\geqslant\eta\},\ y\to x}\frac{u_{i}(y)}{|{\bf u}(y)|}

(1.7)

\Delta u_{i}=w_{i}\sqrt{Q}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\llcorner(\Omega\cap\partial_{\rm red}\{|{\bf u}|>0\})\quad{\rm for}\ \ i=1,2,\cdots,m.

(Regularity of free boundary)

and

is a minimizer of

is a closed set in the relative topology of
\Omega
with
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{S}_{\bf u})\leqslant n-k^{\ast}
for some
k^{\ast}\in\{5,6,7\}
, and the regular part of the free boundary

\partial_{\rm red}\left\{|{\bf u}|>0\right\}\cap\Omega

C^{1,\beta}
smooth for some
\beta\in(0,\alpha]

(
C^{k+1,\beta}
smooth or analytic if

is
C^{k,\alpha}
smooth or analytic, respectively).

There are many other important developments, for example,
two-phase free boundary problems
[
ACF84
,
DPSV21a
,
DPSV21b
]
, free boundary problems for almost minimizer
[
DT15
,
DET19
]
, for the
fully nonlinear uniformly elliptic operators
[
DSFS15
]
and for the fractional
\alpha
-Laplace operator
[
CRS10
]
.

The theory of free boundary problems was used by Caffarelli-Lin
[
CL08
]
to the study of the nodal sets of harmonic maps into a singular space with non-positive curvature in the sense of Alexandrov.

In general, the theory of free boundary problems can be divided into two main steps.
The first step is to establish the existence and the Lipschitz regularity of the solutions.
The second step is to explore the structure of free boundary of these solutions,
including the smoothness of its regular part and the size and structure of its singular part.
A blowup argument and an improved flatness property are applied to analyze the structure of the free boundary of solutions.
One may notice that some basic ideas in the theory of free boundary problem are similar to the ones in the theory of minimal surfaces
[
F69
,
Giu84
]
and harmonic maps
[
Sim96
,
SU82
]
.

1.2.
Free boundary problems in
RCD
-spaces and the main results

In this subsection, we state the main results of this paper.
Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD(K,N)
-space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,+\infty),
and let
Q
be a
\mu
-measurable function on
\Omega
with (
1.3
).
Given a map
\mathbf{g}=(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{m})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\left[0,+\infty\right)^{m})
, we consider the minimization problem (
1.4
).

The first result is the existence of a minimizer as following.

Proposition 1.3

(Existence of a minimizer)
.

If
{\rm diam}(\Omega)\leqslant{\rm diam}(X)/3
, then for each
{\bf g}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,+\infty)^{m})
, there exists a
{\bf u}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
such that

(1.8)

J_{Q}({\bf u})=\inf_{{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}}J_{Q}({\bf v})\text{.}

This proposition is somewhat known for experts. For the completeness, we include a proof in Section
3
.

We then consider the Lipschitz regularity of a local minimizer
{\bf u}
in (
1.4
). Up to our knowledge, the existing proofs of the Lipschitz regularity of
{\bf u}
in the Euclidean setting do not work directly in the setting of
RCD(K,N)
-spaces. In fact, some proofs
[
AC81
,
CSY18
,
Caf87
,
Caf88
,
Caf89
]
make heavy use of the Poisson formula, which is not clear on
RCD(K,N)
-spaces.
Other proofs
[
DT15
,
DSS20
]
rely on the fact that gradients of a harmonic function are again harmonic,
which fails even on smooth Riemannian manifolds.
In this paper, we will overcome this difficulty by using the Cheng-Yau gradient estimates for harmonic functions and a mean value property (see Lemma
5.3
), to obtain the following the Lipschitz continuity, provided that the space is non-collapsed.

Theorem 1.4

(Lipschitz regularity)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD(K,N)
-space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,\infty)
. Assume that
\mu=\mathscr{H}^{N}
, the
N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
X
. (I.e.,
X
is non-collapsed.) Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain. Suppose that
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
is a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) and that
Q
satisfies (
1.3
), then
{\bf u}
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega
. Precisely, for any ball
B_{R}(x)\subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
L
depending only on
N,K,\Omega,R,Q_{\max},\varepsilon_{\bf{u}}
and
\int_{B_{R}(x)}|{\bf u}|{\rm d}\mu
, such that

(1.9)

|u_{i}(y)-u_{i}(z)|\leqslant L\cdot d(y,z),\qquad\forall\ y,z\in B_{R/4}(x),\quad i=1,2,\cdots,m.

Remark 1.5
.

The results for the Lipschitz regularity of energy minimizing harmonic maps from/into/between singular spaces were established in
[
GS92
,
KS93
,
ZZ18
,
GJZ19
]
.

Our next result is about the finiteness of the perimeter of the free boundary of a local minimizer. We will also derive the associated Euler-Lagrange equation.

Theorem 1.6

(Local finiteness of perimeter and Euler-Lagrange equation)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
,
\Omega
and
{\bf u}
be as in the above Theorem
1.4
. Suppose
Q\in C(\Omega)
. Then
\Omega_{\bf u}:=\Omega\cap\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
is a set of locally finite perimeter. Moreover, it holds:

(1)

For all
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
,
\mathscr{H}^{N-1}\big(\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega^{\prime}\big)<+\infty
;

(2)

There exist nonnegative Borel functions
q_{i},\ i=1,2,\cdots,m,
such that

{\bf\Delta}u_{i}=q_{i}\cdot\mathscr{H}^{N-1}\llcorner\big(\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega\big)

in the sense of distributions (i.e.,
-\int_{\Omega}\left\langle{\nabla u_{i}},{\nabla\phi}\right\rangle{\rm d}\mu=\int_{\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega}\phi q_{i}{\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{N-1}
for any Lipschitz continuous
\phi
with compact support in
\Omega
), and

(1.10)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}q_{i}^{2}(x)=Q(x),\quad{\rm for}\ \mathscr{H}^{N-1}{\rm-a.e.}\ x\in\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega.

Remark 1.7
.

Recalling in the Euclidean setting, by using the non-tangential limits
w_{i},i=1,\cdots,m,
in (
1.6
), the densities in the Euler-Lagrange equation (
1.7
),
q_{i}=w_{i}\sqrt{Q}
, fulfil (
1.10
).
The proof of the existence of non-tangential limits
w_{i}
relies heavily on a
domain variation formula via a
C^{1}
vector field (see
[
CSY18
, Lemma 11]
). In the
RCD
setting, the notion of “non-tangential limit” is not well-defined at present. In this paper, we will prove (
1.10
) by applying a blow-up argument and the theory of sets of finite perimeter in the setting of
[
Amb01
,
Mir03
,
ABS19
]
(see Corollary
7.2
for the details).

To consider the regularity of free boundary of the local minimizers of
J_{Q}
, let us
recall that on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the dimension of singular part
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{S}_{\bf u})\leqslant n-k^{\ast}
for some
k^{\ast}\in\{5,6,7\}
(see Theorem
1.2
(3)). However, in the non-smooth setting, the singularities of the free boundary may arise from the singularities of the space itself, see the following example.

Example 1.8
.

Let
Y
be the doubling of an equilateral triangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(gluing two same equilateral triangles along their boundaries). This is a two-dimensional Alexandrov space with nonnegative curvature, and thus,
(Y,d_{Y},\mathscr{H}^{2})
is an
ncRCD(0,2)
metric measure space. Let
X:=\mathbb{R}\times Y
. It is clear that
X
is an
ncRCD(0,3)
-space. Assume that
\Omega=(-1,1)\times Y
and

f(t,y):=\begin{cases}t&{\rm if}\quad t\geqslant 0\\
0&{\rm if}\quad t<0.\end{cases}

It is easy to check that
f
is a minimizer of
J_{Q=1}
on
\Omega
.
The free boundary
\partial\{f>0\}=\{0\}\times Y
. It is clear that, assuming that
y\in Y
is one of vertexes of the equilateral triangle, the point
x:=(0,y)
is a singular point of the free boundary.

This example shows that the best expectation of the bound of the singular part of the free boundary in the
RCD(K,N)
-space without boundary is co-dimension
\geqslant 3
. We shall prove this bound for non-collapsed
RCD(K,N)
-spaces without boundary.

Two different notions of boundary of
RCD
-spaces have been introduced in
[
DPG18
]
and
[
KM19
]
respectively. Here we will use the one introduced in
[
DPG18
]
.
Let
(X,d,\mu:=\mathscr{H}^{N})
be a non-collapsed
RCD(K,N)
-space. Recall that
[
AnBS19
,
DPG18
]
the singular part of
X
has a stratification:

(1.11)

\mathcal{S}^{0}\subset\mathcal{S}^{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{S}^{N-1}=\mathcal{S}:=X\backslash\mathcal{R},

where
\mathcal{R}
is the regular part of
X
given by

\mathcal{R}:=\Big\{x\in X\big|\ {\rm each\ tangent\ cone\ at}\ x\ {\rm is}\ (\mathbb{R}^{N},d_{\rm Eucl})\Big\},

and, for any
0\leqslant k\leqslant N-1
,

\mathcal{S}^{k}:=\Big\{x\in X\big|\ {\rm no\ tangent\ cone\ at}\ x\ {\rm splits\ off}\ \mathbb{R}^{k+1}\Big\}.

It holds

(1.12)

\dim_{\mathscr{H}}(\mathcal{S}^{k})\leqslant k,\quad\forall\ k=1,2,\cdots,N-1.

(This was first given in
[
CC97
]
for non-collapsed Ricci limit spaces.) According to
[
DPG18
]
, the boundary of
X
is defined by

\partial X:=\overline{\mathcal{S}^{N-1}\backslash\mathcal{S}^{N-2}}.

Our last result is to show that the free boundary has a manifold structure away from a subset having co-dimension 3, which is similar to the one in
[
MS21
]
for the boundary of a set minimizing the perimeter in
RCD
-spaces.

Theorem 1.9

(Regularity of free boundary)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
and
\Omega
be as in the above Theorem
1.4
. Suppose that
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
is a minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) and that
Q
satisfies (
1.3
). Assume that
\Omega\cap\partial X=\emptyset
and
Q\in C(\Omega)
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a relatively open set
O_{\varepsilon}\subset\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

(
\varepsilon
-Reifenberg flatness)

For any
x\in O_{\varepsilon}
there exists a radius
r_{x}>0
such that for any ball
B_{r}(y)
with
y\in B_{r_{x}}(x)\cap\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
and
r\in(0,r_{x})
, it holds that
B_{r}(y)
is
\varepsilon r
-closed to
B_{r}(0^{N})
in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology and that
B_{r}(y)\cap\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
is
\varepsilon r
-closed to
B_{r}(0^{N-1})
in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology, where
B_{r}(0^{N})
is the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with centered at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and radius
r
;

(2)

(The smallnss of the remainder of
O_{\varepsilon}
)

(1.13)

\dim_{\mathscr{H}}\big((\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega)\backslash O_{\varepsilon}\big)\leqslant N-3.

Moreover, if
N=3
, then
(\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega)\backslash O_{\varepsilon}
is a discrete set of points.

In particular, the relatively open set
O_{\varepsilon}

is
C^{\alpha}
-biHölder homeomorphic to an
(N-1)
-dimensional manifold, where
\alpha=\alpha(\varepsilon)\in(0,1)
with
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\alpha(\varepsilon)=1
.

Remark 1.10
.

According to the above Example
1.8
, the bound (
1.13
) is sharp.

As a direct consequence, we have the following result.

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
(X,d,\mu),\Omega
and
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be as in the above Theorem
1.9
.
Assume that
\Omega\cap\partial X=\emptyset
and
Q\in C(\Omega)
. Let

\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega:=\mathcal{R}^{\Omega_{\bf u}}\cup\mathcal{S}^{\Omega_{\bf u}},

where
\mathcal{R}^{\Omega_{\bf u}}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\Omega_{\bf u}}
are the regular and singular parts of
the free boundary
\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega
. (That is,

x\in\mathcal{R}^{\Omega_{\bf u}}
means that each tangent cone at
x
is
(\mathbb{R}^{N},d_{\rm Eucl})
and each blow-up limit of
\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega
at
x
is an
(N-1)
-dimensional affine hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The singular part

\mathcal{S}^{\Omega_{\bf u}}:=(\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega)\setminus\mathcal{R}^{\Omega_{\bf u}}.
)

Then we have

\dim_{\mathscr{H}}\big(\mathcal{S}^{\Omega_{\bf u}}\big)\leqslant N-3.

Remark 1.12
.

In general, the regular part
\mathcal{R}^{\Omega_{\bf u}}
might not form a manifold.
In fact, it might not be relatively open in the free boundary
\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega
. (In the Euclidean case and if
Q\in C^{\alpha}
, the regular part is relatively open in the free boundary, see Theorem
1.2
(3)). This will be seen by the following simple example.
Recall that Y. Ostu and T. Shioya in
[
OS94
]
constructed a two-dimensional Alexandrov space without boundary, denoted by
Y_{OS}
, such that the singular set
\mathcal{S}
of
Y_{OS}
is dense in
Y_{OS}
. Recalling the above Example
1.8
, we replace the space
Y
in Example
1.8
by
Y_{OS}
. By using the same construction of
f
, we know that for any singular point
y\in Y_{OS}
, the point
x:=(0,y)
is a singular point of the free boundary. Thus, the singular part
\mathcal{S}^{\Omega_{f}}
is dense in the
free boundary
\partial\{f>0\}=\{0\}\times Y_{OS}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and
\mu
be a Radon measure on
X
with
{\rm supp}(\mu)=X.
The triple
(X,d,\mu)
is called a metric measure space. Given any
p\in X
and
R>0
, we
denote by
B_{R}(p)
the open ball centered at
p
with radius
R
.

2.1.
RCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces and their calculus

Let
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty]
. The curvature-dimension condition
CD(K,N)
for a metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
was introduced by Sturm
[
Stu06a
,
Stu06b
]

as follows:

\bullet

(X,d)
is a locally compact length space. In particular, for any

\bullet

If

\frac{\mu\big(B_{r_{2}}(p)\big)}{\mu\big(B_{r_{1}}(p)\big)}\leqslant C_{N,K,R}\Big(\frac{r_{2}}{r_{1}}\Big)^{N}

for some constant

and

\bullet
If

is a bounded set, then there exists a constant

such that

\frac{d^{+}}{dr}\mu(B_{r}(x)):=\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu\big(B_{r+\delta}(x)\backslash B_{r}(x)\big)}{\delta}\leqslant C_{N,K,\Omega}

for all

and

. In fact, the generalized Bishop-Gromov inequality implies

\frac{\mu\big(B_{r+\delta}(x)\backslash B_{r}(x)\big)}{\mu\big(B_{r}(x)\big)}\leqslant\frac{\bar{\mu}\big(B_{r+\delta}\backslash B_{r}\big)}{\bar{\mu}\big(B_{r}\big)},

, the simply connected space form with constant sectional curvature

, and

is a geodesic ball of radius

in

.
It follows

\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\bar{\mu}\left(B_{r+\delta}\backslash B_{r}\right)}{\delta\cdot\bar{\mu}(B_{r})}\leqslant\frac{C_{N,K}}{r},\quad\forall r\leqslant 1.

This gives

\mu(B_{r}(x))\leqslant C_{N,K,\Omega}\cdot r,\qquad\forall x\in\Omega,\ \ \forall r\leqslant 1,

Several different notions of Sobolev spaces for metric measure spaces have been given in
[
Che99
,
Sha00
,
AGS13
,
AGS14a
,
HK00
]
. They are equivalent to each other in the setting of
RCD
-metric measure spaces (see, for example,
[
AGS14a
,
AGS13
]
).
Given a continuous function
f
on
X
, the
pointwise Lipschitz constant
(
[
Che99
]
) of
f
at
x
is defined by

(2.4)

\begin{split}{\rm Lip}f(x):&=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{d(x,y)}\\
&=\limsup_{r\to 0}\sup_{d(x,y)\leqslant r}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{r},\end{split}

and
{\rm Lip}f(x)=0
if
x
is isolated. It is clear that
{\rm Lip}f
is
\mu
-measurable. Let
\Omega\subset X
be an open domain and let
1\leqslant p\leqslant+\infty
. The
W^{1,p}
-norm of a locally Lipschitz function

f\in Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
on
\Omega
is defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\left\|\Lip f\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\text{.}

The Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is defined by the completion of the set of locally Lipschitz functions
f
with
\left\|f\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}<+\infty
.
The space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
is defined by the closure of
Lip_{0}(\Omega)
under the
W^{1,p}
-norm, where
Lip_{0}(\Omega)
is
the set of Lipschitz continuous functions on
\Omega
with compact support in
\Omega
.
We denote
f\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
if
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime})
for every open subset
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, where “
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
”
means
\Omega^{\prime}
is compactly contained in
\Omega
. We itemize some basic properties of Sobolev functions as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
.

(1)

For each
f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, there is a function in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, denoted by
|\nabla f|
,
(
so-called weak upper gradient for
f
, see
[
Che99
, Sect.2]
,)
such that
\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
. Moreover, if
f\in Lip_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
then
|\nabla f|=\Lip f
holds
\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega

(
[
Che99
, Theorem 5.1]
).

(2)

(Lower semicontinuity of energy.)
If
f_{j}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
f_{j}\to f
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, then
\liminf_{j\to\infty}\||\nabla f_{j}|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\geqslant\||\nabla f|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.

(3)

If
f,g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
f|_{A}=g|_{A}
for some Borel set
A\subset\Omega
, then
|\nabla f|(x)=|\nabla g|(x)
at
\mu
-a.e.
x\in A
.

(4)

The
W^{1,2}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space, and the inner product
\left\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla g}\right\rangle\in L^{1}(\Omega)
for
f,g\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
can be given by the polarization
(
see
[
Gig15
]
)
:

(2.5)

\left\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla g}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{4}(|\nabla(f+g)|^{2}-|\nabla(f-g)|^{2}).

(5)

(Poincaré inequality, see
[
BB11
, Eq. (2.6)]
or
[
Raj12
]
.)
If
\Omega
is bounded, then there exists a constant
C_{P}>0
depending only on
p,K,N
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
, such that for every ball
B_{R}(x)\subset\Omega
with
R\leqslant{\rm diam}(X)/3
, it holds

\int_{B_{R}(x)}f^{p}\leqslant C_{P}\cdot R^{p}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|\nabla f|^{p},\qquad\forall\ f\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R}(x)).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
D,\Omega
be two open sets with
\overline{D}\subset\Omega
and
\mu(\partial D)=0
. Let
1<p<\infty
and
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. We denote
v\in W^{1,p}_{u}(\Omega)
whenever
v-u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Assume
g\in W_{u}^{1,p}(D)
and
h\in W^{1,p}_{u}(\Omega\backslash\overline{D})
. Then the function

f(x):=\begin{cases}g(x),&x\in D\\
h(x),&x\in\Omega\backslash\overline{D},\end{cases}

has a representative in
W^{1,p}_{u}(\Omega)
.

In particular,
if
g\in W^{1,p}_{0}(D)
, then its zero extension
\bar{g}
on
\Omega
(namely,
\bar{g}=g
on
D
and
\bar{g}=0
on
\Omega\backslash D
) is in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Since
g-u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(D)
, there are
\hat{g}_{j}\in Lip_{0}(D)
such that
\hat{g}_{j}\to g-u
in
W^{1,p}(D)
as
j\to\infty
. Similarly, there are
\hat{h}_{j}\in Lip_{0}(\Omega\backslash\overline{D})
such that
\hat{h}_{j}\to h-u
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega\backslash\overline{D})
. Consider the functions

\hat{f}_{j}(x):=\begin{cases}\hat{g}_{j}(x),&x\in D\\
0,&x\in\partial D\\
\hat{h}_{j}(x),&x\in\Omega\backslash\overline{D},\end{cases}

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\hat{f}_{j}\in Lip_{0}(\Omega)
for each
j=1,2,\cdots.
Since
\mu(\partial D)=0
, we have
{\rm Lip}\hat{f}_{j}={\rm Lip}\hat{g}_{j}
or
{\rm Lip}\hat{h}_{j}

\mu
-a.e. in
\Omega
, and then

\|\hat{f}_{j}-\hat{f}_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\|\hat{g}_{j}-\hat{g}_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}(D)}+\|\hat{h}_{j}-\hat{h}_{k}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega\backslash\overline{D})}

for all
j,k=1,2,\cdots.
It follows that

\{\hat{f}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence under
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-norm. Let
\hat{f}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
be the
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
-limit of
\{\hat{f}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
. Meanwhile, since
\mu(\partial D)=0
, it is clear that
\hat{f}_{j}\to(f-u)
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, as
j\to\infty
. Therefore,
\hat{f}=f-u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
. It follows that
\hat{f}
is a
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
-representative of
f-u
.

The second assertion follows from the first one, by taking
u=0
and
h=0
.

Definition 2.3

(Distributional Laplacian
[
Gig15
]
)
.

For each function
f\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, its
Laplacian

\boldsymbol{\Delta}f
on
\Omega
is a linear functional acting on
Lip_{0}(\Omega)
given by

(2.6)

\boldsymbol{\Delta}f(\phi)=-\int_{\Omega}\left\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla\phi}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}\mu

for all
\phi\in Lip_{0}(\Omega)
.
If there is a signed Radon measure
\nu
such that
\boldsymbol{\Delta}f(\phi)=\int_{\Omega}\phi\mathrm{d}\nu
for all
\phi\in Lip_{0}(\Omega)
, then we say that
\boldsymbol{\Delta}f=\nu
in the sense of distribution.

In general, the measure-valued Laplacian
{\bf\Delta}f
for a function
f\in W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
may not be absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
. We consider its Radon-Nikodym decomposition

{\bf\Delta}f=({\bf\Delta}f)^{\rm ac}\cdot\mu+({\bf\Delta}f)^{\rm sing}.

This Laplacian on
\Omega
is linear, and it satisfies the chain rule and the Leibniz rule
[
Gig15
]
.

Remark 2.4
.

(1) When
f\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, the test function in (
2.6
) can be taken any
\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(2) When
\Omega=X
, the inner product (
2.5
) provides a Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}(f,g):=\int_{X}\left\langle{\nabla f},{\nabla g}\right\rangle
on
X
. This Dirichlet form
(\mathcal{E},W^{1,2}(X))
has an infinitesimal generator
\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}
with domain
D(\Delta_{\mathcal{E}})\subset W^{1,2}(X)
, i.e., for any
f\in D(\Delta_{\mathcal{E}})
and any
g\in W^{1,2}(X),
it holds
(\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}f,g)_{L^{2}(X)}=-\mathcal{E}(f,g)
. In case
f\in D(\Delta_{\mathcal{E}})
, the measure valued Laplacian
{\bf\Delta}f
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
and
{\bf\Delta}f=\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}f\cdot\mu.

We recall the following Laplacian comparison theorem for distance functions.

Theorem 2.5

(Laplacian comparison theorem,
[
Gig15
, Corollary 5.15]
)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an

and

, and let

\boldsymbol{\Delta}\rho\leqslant(N-1)\cot_{\kappa}(\sqrt{-\kappa}\rho)\cdot\mu

in the sense of distribution on

,
where

\kappa=K/(N-1)\quad{\rm and}\quad\cot_{\kappa}(s)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\kappa}\cot(\sqrt{\kappa}s)&{\rm if}\quad\kappa>0,\\
1/s&{\rm if}\quad\kappa=0,\\
\sqrt{-\kappa}\coth(\sqrt{-\kappa}s)&{\rm if}\quad\kappa<0.\end{cases}

In particular, if
K<0,\ N>1
, then we have

(2.7)

{\bf\Delta}\phi_{N,K}(\rho)\leqslant 0

in the sense of distributions, where

\phi_{N,K}(s)=-\int^{1}_{s}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{-\kappa}t)}{\sqrt{-\kappa}}\right)^{1-N}\mathrm{d}t,\qquad\kappa=K/(N-1);

and if
\rho\leqslant 1

\boldsymbol{\Delta}(\rho^{2})=2|\nabla\rho|^{2}\cdot\mu+2\rho{\bf\Delta}\rho\leqslant 2(N+C\rho^{2})\cdot\mu

in the sense of distributions on

, where

depends only on

and

.

A function

if

According to

be a sequence of
RCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces and

(X_{j},d_{j},\mu_{j},p_{j})\overset{pmGH}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\mu_{\infty},p_{\infty}).

Then, given any Lipschitz function
f_{\infty}\in Lip(\overline{B_{R}(p_{\infty})})
with a Lipschitz constant
L>0
, there exist a sequence of Lipschitz functions
f_{j}\in Lip(\overline{B_{R}(p_{j})})
such that

f_{j}\rightarrow f_{\infty}
uniformly over
B_{R}(p_{\infty})
,

\||\nabla f_{j}|\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(p_{j}))}\leqslant L+1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(p_{j})}|\nabla f_{j}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{j}=\int_{B_{R}(p_{\infty})}|\nabla f_{\infty}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\infty}.

We also need a variant of it as follows.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
R>0
and let
(X_{j},d_{j},\mu_{j})
be as above in Lemma
2.10
. Let
F_{j}\in Lip(\overline{B_{R}(p_{j})})
be a sequence of Lipschitz functions with a uniform Lipschitz constant and satisfy that
F_{j}\to F_{\infty}
uniformly over
B_{R}(p_{j})
. Then, given any function
f_{\infty}\in Lip_{\rm loc}(B_{R}(p_{\infty}))
with
f_{\infty}-F_{\infty}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R}(p_{\infty}))
, for each
\delta>0
, there exist a sequence of functions
f_{j}
on
B_{R}(p_{j})
such that
f_{j}-F_{j}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R}(p_{j}))
,

f_{j}\rightarrow f_{\infty}
uniformly over
B_{R-\delta}(p_{j})
, and that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(p_{j})}|\nabla f_{j}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{j}=\int_{B_{R}(p_{\infty})}|\nabla f_{\infty}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\infty}.

Proof.

Fix any
\delta>0
. Since
f_{\infty}\in Lip(\overline{B_{R-\delta}(p_{\infty})})
, we can use the above Cheeger’s lemma, Lemma
2.10
, to obtain a sequence of Lipschitz functions
g_{j}\in Lip(\overline{B_{R-\delta}(p_{j})})
such that

g_{j}\to g_{\infty}:=f_{\infty}|_{\overline{B_{R-\delta}(p_{\infty})}}
uniformly over
B_{R-\delta}(p_{j})
,
\|\nabla g_{j}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R-\delta}(p_{j}))}\to\|\nabla f_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R-\delta}(p_{\infty}))}
as
j\to\infty
, and
|\nabla g_{j}|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R-\delta}(p_{j}))}\leqslant L_{\delta}
, where
L_{\delta}>0
is independent of
j
(may depend on
\delta
).

Denoted by
A_{\delta}(p_{j}):=B_{R}(p_{j})\backslash\overline{B_{R-\delta}(p_{j})}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}.
Let
G_{j}\in Lip(\partial{A_{\delta}(p_{j})})
be defined by
G_{j}=F_{j}
on
\partial B_{R}(p_{j})
and
G_{j}=g_{j}
on
\partial B_{R-\delta}(p_{j})
. Then, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we can extend
G_{j}
to a Lipschitz function
\bar{G}_{j}\in Lip(\overline{A_{\delta}(p_{j})})
. Since
g_{j}
and
F_{j}
have a uniform Lipschitz constant, we can assume that
\bar{G}_{j}
have a uniform Lipschitz constant
L^{\prime}_{\delta}.
Since
G_{j}\to G_{\infty}
uniformly on
\partial A_{\delta}(p_{j})
, we can also assume that
\bar{G}_{j}\to\bar{G}_{\infty}
uniformly (up to a subsequence, by Arzela-Ascoli theorem). Now we have
f_{\infty}-\bar{G}_{\infty}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(A_{\delta}(p_{\infty}))
. By
[
ZZ19
, Proposition 3.2(ii)]
, there exists a sequence
h_{j}
on
A_{\delta}(p_{j})
such that
h_{j}-\bar{G}_{j}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(A_{\delta}(p_{j}))
and
|\nabla h_{j}|_{L^{2}(A_{\delta}(p_{j}))}\to|\nabla f_{\infty}|_{L^{2}(A_{\delta}(p_{\infty}))}
as
j\to\infty
.

At last, we define the function
f_{j}
on
B_{R}(p_{j})
by
f_{j}:=g_{j}
on
\overline{B_{R-\delta}(p_{j})}
and
f_{j}:=h_{j}
on
A_{\delta}(p_{j})
. From Proposition
2.2
, we conclude that
f_{j}\in W^{1,2}(B_{R}(p_{j}))
and
f_{j}-F_{j}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R}(p_{j}))
. The functions
f_{j}
satisfy the desired assertions. The proof is finished.
∎

2.2.
Non-collapsed
RCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces

We will recall the notion of
non-collapsed

RCD
-space as introduced in
[
DPG18
]
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD(K,N)
-space with

and
N\in[1,+\infty)
. It is called a non-collapsed

-space, denoted by

\mu=\mathscr{H}^{N}

N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on

The main examples of
ncRCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces are non-collapsed Ricci limit spaces
[
CC97
,
CC00
,
CJN21
]
and
N
-dimensional Alexandrov space with curvature
\geqslant K/(N-1)
.
It was shown
[
DPG18
]
that if
\left\{(X_{\text{i}},d_{\text{i}},\mu_{\text{i}})\right\}
is a sequence of
ncRCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces and
(X_{j},d_{j},\mu_{j},p_{j})\overset{pmGH}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\mu_{\infty},p_{\infty})
, then
(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\mu_{\infty})
is of
ncRCD(K,N)
too.

If
(X,d,\mu)
is an
ncRCD(K,N)
-space, then

must be an integer, and there holds (from Corollary 2.14 in
[
DPG18
]
)

(2.13)

\mu\big(B_{r}(x)\big)\leqslant\bar{\mu}(B_{r})\leqslant C_{N,K}\cdot r^{N},\quad\forall\ x\in X\ {\rm and}\ r\leqslant 1,

for a constant
C_{N,K}>0
, where
\bar{\mu}
is the
N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{M}^{N}_{K/(N-1)}
, the simply connected space form with constant sectional curvature
K/(N-1)
, and
B_{r}
is a geodesic ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{M}^{N}_{K/(N-1)}
. Furthermore, if
N>1
,

(2.14)

\frac{d^{+}}{dr}\mu(B_{r}(x)):=\limsup_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{\mu\big(B_{r+\delta}(x)\backslash B_{r}(x)\big)}{\delta}\leqslant C_{N,K}\cdot r^{N-1}

for all
\ r\leqslant 1
and
x\in X
. In fact by (
2.3
) and

\mu(B_{r}(x))\leqslant\bar{\mu}(B_{r})
, we get
\mu\left(B_{r+\delta}(x)\backslash B_{r}(x)\right)\leqslant\bar{\mu}\left(B_{r+\delta}\backslash B_{r}\right).
It follows (
2.14
). Remark that it holds only (
2.2
) in general
RCD(K,N)
-spaces (without the assumption of non-collapsing).

Let
(X,d,\mu:=\mathscr{H}^{N})
be an
ncRCD(K,N)
-space. For each point
x\in X
, any tangent cone is a metric measure cone (a Euclidean cone with a natural measure). Indeed, the existence of the limit
\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}
implies that any tangent cone at

is a volume cone, and hence, by
[
DPG16
]
, it is a metric cone. It was shown that
[
DPG18
, Corollary 1.7]
a point

is regular (i.e. any one tangent cone is isometric to

) if and only if

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B_{r}(x))}{\omega_{N}r^{N}}=1.

2.3.
Sets of finite perimeter and the reduced boundary

The theory of Euclidean sets of finite perimeter of De Giorgi has been extended to
RCD(K,N)
-spaces
[
Amb01
,
Mir03
,
ABS19
]
, and recently
[
BPS19
,
BPS21
]
.

Definition 2.13
.

A function
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
is called a function of bounded variation, denoted by
f\in BV(X)
for short, if there exists a sequence
f_{j}\in Lip_{\rm loc}(X)
converging to
f
in
L^{1}(X)
such that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}|\nabla f_{j}|{\rm d}\mu<+\infty.

Its total variation is a finite Borel measure and denoted by
|Df|
. Moreover, for any open subset
A\subset X
,

|Df|(A):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f_{j}|{\rm d}\mu\ \big|\ f_{j}\in Lip_{\rm loc}(A),\ \ f_{j}\overset{L^{1}(A)}{\longrightarrow}f\right\}.

A function
f\in BV_{\rm loc}(X)
if
\phi f\in BV(X)
for any
\phi\in Lip_{0}(X)
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
E\subset X
be a Borel subset and let
A
be an open set. The perimeter
\mathcal{P}(E,A)
is given by

\mathcal{P}(E,A):=\inf\left\{\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{A}|\nabla f_{j}|{\rm d}\mu\ \big|\ f_{j}\in Lip_{\rm loc}(A),\ \ f_{j}\overset{L^{1}_{\rm loc}(A)}{\longrightarrow}\chi_{E}\right\}.

A Borel set
E
is called of finite perimeter in
X
if
\mathcal{P}(E,X)<\infty.
In that case, it is proved
[
Amb01
,
Mir03
]
that the set function
A\mapsto\mathcal{P}(E,A)
is the restriction to open sets of a finite Borel measure
\mathcal{P}(E,\cdot)
defined by

\mathcal{P}(E,B):=\inf\{\mathcal{P}(E,A)|\ B\subset A,\ A\subset X\ \ {\rm open}\}.

A subset
E\subset X
with
\mu(E)<\infty
is a set of finite perimeter if and only if the characteristic function
\chi_{E}\in BV(X)
, and its perimeter measure is
\mathcal{P}(E,\cdot):=|D\chi_{E}|(\cdot).
A subset
E\subset X
is called a set of locally finite perimeter if
\chi_{E}\in BV_{\rm loc}(X)
.

We recall the following basic properties, collected in
[
Amb01
,
Mir03
,
ABS19
]
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD(K,N)
metric measure space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
. Then the followings hold:

(1)

(Lower semicontinuity)

E\mapsto\mathcal{P}(E,X)
is lower semicontinuous with respect to the
L^{1}_{\rm loc}(X)
topology;

(2)

(Coarea formula)
Let
v\in BV(X)
. Then
\{v>r\}:=\{x|\ v(x)>r\}
has finite perimeter for
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, if
v\in BV(X)
is continuous and nonnegative, then, for any Borel function
f:X\to[0,+\infty]
, it holds

(2.15)

\int_{s\leqslant v<t}f{\rm d}|Dv|=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{X}f{\rm d}\Big(\mathcal{P}(\{v>r\},\cdot)\Big){\rm d}r

for any
0\leqslant s<t<+\infty
(see
[
Mir03
, Remark 4.3]
or
[
ABS19
, Corollary 1.9]
).

By applying to distance functions, we get the following weak convergence of the measures on spheres.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
(X_{j},d_{j},\mu_{j})
be a sequence of
RCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in[1,+\infty)
. Suppose that

(X_{j},d_{j},\mu_{j},p_{j})\overset{pmGH}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\mu_{\infty},p_{\infty})

\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.

, the functions

\chi_{B_{r}(p_{j})}
is in
BV(X_{j})
, and it holds

(2.16)

|D\chi_{B_{r}(p_{j})}|\rightharpoonup|D\chi_{B_{r}(p_{\infty})}|,\quad{\rm as}\ \ j\to\infty

in duality with
C_{0}(Z)
, where
Z
is given in Definition
2.7
(3).

Proof.

By the coarea formula, we know that for
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the functions
\chi_{B_{r}(p_{j})}\in BV(X_{j})
. For such
r>0
, from the observation that
\chi_{B_{r}(p_{j})}\to\chi_{B_{r}(p_{\infty})}
in

-strong and

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{X_{j}}g{\rm d}|D\chi_{B_{r}(p_{j})}|(X_{j})\geqslant\int_{X_{\infty}}g{\rm d}|D\chi_{B_{r}(p_{\infty})}|(X_{\infty}),\quad\forall 0\leqslant g\in Lip_{0}(Z),

2.7

On the other hand, for any

Therefore, we have
\lim_{j\to\infty}|D\chi_{B_{r}(p_{j})}|(X_{j})=|D\chi_{B_{r}(p_{\infty})}|(X_{\infty})
for

-a.e.

[

∎

Definition 2.17

3.
Existence of a minimizer

In this section, we will derive the existence of a minimizer of (
1.4
), where we always assume that
\Omega
is a bounded domain in an
RCD(K,N)
-space
(X,d,\mu)
with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
{\bf g}=(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots,g_{m})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,+\infty)^{m})
and let
\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
be given in (
1.4
).

Now we are ready to prove the existence of a minimizer, which is asserted in Proposition
1.3
.

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

Since
\mathbf{g}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
guarantees that
\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}\neq\emptyset
, there exists a minimizing sequence
\left\{{\bf u}^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
such that

(3.1)

\lim_{k\to\infty}J_{Q}({\bf u}^{k})=\inf_{{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}}J_{Q}({\bf v})\ \ \ (\leqslant J_{Q}({\bf g}))\text{.}

From the Poincaré inequality (see Proposition
2.1
(5)), we get

\int_{\Omega}\left|{\bf u}^{k}-{\bf g}\right|^{2}\leqslant C_{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla({\bf u}^{k}-{\bf g})\right|^{2},

where the constant
C_{1}>0
depends only on
N,K,\Omega
. Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|{\bf u}^{k}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle 2\left\|{\bf u}^{k}-{\bf g}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}+2\left\|\mathbf{g}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2(1+C_{1})\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla({\bf u}^{k}-\mathbf{g})\right|^{2}+2\left\|\mathbf{g}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{2}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla{\bf u}^{k}\right|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\mathbf{g}\right|^{2}\Big)+2\left\|\mathbf{g}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{2}J_{Q}({\bf u}^{k})+(C_{2}+2)\left\|\mathbf{g}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{2}(J_{Q}(\mathbf{g})+1)+(C_{2}+2)\left\|\mathbf{g}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}

for all sufficiently large
k
, where
C_{2}=4(1+C_{1})
.
Then, the fact that
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
is a Hilbert space implies that there exists a subsequence
\left\{{\bf u}^{k_{\ell}}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
of
\left\{{\bf u}^{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
\left\{{\bf u}^{k_{\ell}}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
weakly converges to some
{\bf u}
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and converges to
{\bf u}
almost everywhere on
\Omega
. Noted that
\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
is a closed convex subset of
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we conclude that
{\bf u}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
.

By noticing that
\left\{\chi_{\left\{x\in\Omega\mid\left|{\bf u}^{k_{\ell}}(x)\right|>0\right\}}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
converges to
1
almost everywhere on
\left\{x\in\Omega\mid\left|{\bf u}(x)\right|>0\right\}
and that
Q\geqslant 0
, we have

Q\chi_{\{|{\bf u}|>0\}}\leqslant\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}Q\chi_{\{|{\bf u}^{k_{\ell}}|>0\}}\quad\mu-{\rm a.e.\ in}\ \ \Omega.

It follows from the Fatou lemma that

\int_{\Omega}Q\chi_{\{|{\bf u}|>0\}}\leqslant\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}Q\chi_{\{|{\bf u}^{k_{\ell}}|>0\}}.

By combining this and the lower semicontinuity of energy in Proposition
2.1
(2), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{Q}({\bf u})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla{\bf u}^{k_{\ell}}\right|^{2}+\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}^{k_{\ell}}\right|>0\right\}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}J_{Q}({\bf u}^{k_{\ell}})

\displaystyle=\inf_{{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}}J_{Q}({\bf v})\text{,}

where we have used (
3.1
). Therefore,
{\bf u}
is a minimizer of
J_{Q}
. The proof is finished.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Here, we only need to assume that
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
Q\geqslant 0
almost everywhere on
\Omega.

Hölder continuity of local minimizers

In this section, we will derive the locally Hölder regularity for the local minimizers of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
). Recall the notations.
Let
(X,d,\mu)
be
RCD(K,N)
metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain and let

{\bf u}:=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,+\infty)^{m})

is a local minimizer of

in (

{\bf g}=(g_{1},g_{2},\cdots g_{m})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,+\infty)^{m})
and a number

To begin, we argue that the components of local minimizers are subharmonic, so that powerful analytic tools can be applied later on.

Lemma 4.1

(Subharmonicity)
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
on
\Omega
with
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then
\boldsymbol{\Delta}u_{i}\geqslant 0
on
\Omega
in the sense of distributions, for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Proof.

Fixed any ball
B_{R}(x)\subset\Omega
such that
\mu(B_{R}(x))\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{\bf u}}{2},

it suffices to show that
u_{i}
is subharmonic on
B_{R}(x)
, where
i=1,\cdots,m.

For each
0\leqslant\phi\in\Lip_{0}(B_{R}(x))
,
\phi\not=0
, let

(4.1)

{\bf v}_{i,\delta}:=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},(u_{i}-\delta\phi)^{+},u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{m})\text{,}

where
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}
and
\delta>0
, then
{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
and, by (
1.5
),

\begin{split}\dist({\bf v}_{i,\delta},{\bf u})&\leqslant\|{\bf u}-{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}+\|\chi_{\{|{\bf u}-{\bf v}_{i,\delta}|>0\}}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\\
&\leqslant\delta\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{R}(x))}+\mu(B_{R}(x)).\end{split}

This implies
\dist({\bf v}_{i,\delta},{\bf u})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
provided the
\delta<\delta_{0}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{\bf u}}{2\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{R}(x))}}
. Noted that
\left\{\left|{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\right|>0\right\}\subseteq\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}
, the local minimality of
{\bf u}
gives

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
. Hence, we get
\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\right|^{2}
.
This implies

(4.3)

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2}\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(u_{i}-\delta\phi)\right|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2}-2\delta\left\langle{\nabla u_{i}},{\nabla\phi}\right\rangle+\delta^{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2})

for all sufficiently small
\delta
. Therefore, by letting
\delta\to 0
, we obtain

(4.4)

\int_{\Omega}\left\langle{\nabla u_{i}},{\nabla\phi}\right\rangle\leqslant 0\text{.}

The arbitrariness of
\phi
implies
\boldsymbol{\Delta}u_{i}\geqslant 0
on
B_{R}(x)
in the sense of distributions. The proof is finished.
∎

An immediate consequence is the local boundedness of
{\bf u}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
on
\Omega
with
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then for all
R>0
, there exists a constant
C=C_{N,K,R}>0
, depending only on
N,K,R
, such that

\sup_{B_{R/2}(x)}|{\bf u}|\leqslant Cm\cdot\fint_{B_{R}(x)}|{\bf u}|

provided the ball
B_{R}(x)\subset\Omega
. Indeed, from Lemma
4.1
and
[
KS01
, Theorem 4.2]
, we conclude
\sup_{B_{R/2}(x)}|{u_{i}}|\leqslant C\cdot\fint_{B_{R}(x)}|{u_{i}}|
for each
i=1,2,\cdots,m.

We will prove the Hölder continuity of
{\bf u}
by using Campanato theory, so we need to obtain a decay estimate on
\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}{\rm d}\mu
(see, for example,
[
Gór09
]
).

Lemma 4.3

(Hölder continuity)
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then
{\bf u}\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
(means that it has a
C_{\rm loc}^{\alpha}
representative).

Proof.

Fixed any ball
B_{\bar{R}}(\bar{x})\subset\subset\Omega
such that
\bar{R}<{\rm diam}(\Omega)/3
, it suffices to show
{\bf u}\in C^{\alpha}(B_{\bar{R}/2}(\bar{x}))
. Since
{\bf u}\in L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. We denote
M_{1}:=\sup_{B_{\bar{R}}(\bar{x})}|{\bf u}|.

For each
x_{0}\in B_{\bar{R}/2}(\bar{x})
and
R\in(0,\bar{R}/4)
,
there exists some
{\bf v}\in W^{1,2}(B_{R}(x_{0}),\mathbb{R}^{m})
that solves the following (relaxed) Dirichlet problem
[
Che99
, Theorem 7.12]
:

(4.5)

\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\Delta}{\bf v}=0\qquad\text{ on }B_{R}(x_{0})\text{,}\\
{\bf u}-{\bf v}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(B_{R}(x_{0}),\mathbb{R}^{m})\text{.}\end{cases}

After extending
{\bf v}
by
{\bf u}
on
\Omega\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})
, (that is,
{\bf v}:={\bf u}
in
\Omega\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})
, see Proposition
2.2
), we have

{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
because all components of
{\bf v}
are nonnegative
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
by the maximum principle (see
[
Che99
, Theorem 7.17]
).

(i) We first check that
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
whenever
R<R_{0}
for some
R_{0}>0
depending only on
K,N,\bar{R}
and
{\bf u}
.

By the Poincaré inequality

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|{\bf u}-{\bf v}|^{2}\leqslant C_{1}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|^{2}

for some constant
C_{1}
depending only on
K,N,\bar{R}
(see, for example, Proposition
2.1
(5)), we get

\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leqslant(\sqrt{C_{1}}+1)(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|^{2})^{1/2},

and hence we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{2}(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|^{2})^{1/2}+\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}-\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}\right|>0\right\}}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{2}(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|^{2})^{1/2}+\mu(B_{R}(x_{0})),

where
C_{2}:=\sqrt{C_{1}}+1.

Note that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+2\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2},

by the Dirichlet energy minimizing property of
{\bf v}
. By the combination of (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and the facts that

|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we conclude that there is
R_{0}\in(0,1)
(depending only on
K,N,\bar{R}
and
{\bf u}
) such that
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
for all
R\in(0,R_{0})
.

(ii) Now by the local minimality of
{\bf u}
, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}\right|>0\right\}}-\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})Q

\displaystyle\leqslant\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\mu(B_{R}(x_{0}))

for all
R\in(0,R_{0})
. On the other hand, from
{\bf u}-{\bf v}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and Remark
2.4
(1), it can be taken as test functions for
{\bf\Delta}{\bf u}
and
{\bf\Delta}{\bf v}
. Hence, we have (recalling
{\bf\Delta}{\bf v}=0
) that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}({\bf u}-{\bf v}){\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}({\bf u}-{\bf v})

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}({\bf u}-{\bf v}){\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}({\bf u}+{\bf v})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2}).

Recall that
M_{1}:=\sup_{B_{\bar{R}}(\bar{x})}|{\bf u}|.
By the maximum principle and the Cheng-Yau’s estimate (Theorem
2.6
), we have

(4.10)

\begin{split}\sup_{B_{R/2}(x_{0})}|\nabla{\bf v}|&\leqslant C_{3}\sup_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|{\bf v}|\leqslant C_{3}\sup_{B_{3\bar{R}/4}(\bar{x})}|{\bf u}|\leqslant C_{3}M_{1},\end{split}

where we have used
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset B_{3\bar{R}/4}(\bar{x})
. By combining the equations (
4.8
)-(
4.10
), we conclude that for all
R\in(0,R_{0})
and
r<R/2
,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\right|^{2}+2\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\right|^{2}+2C_{3}M_{1}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}\cdot\mu(B_{R}(x_{0}))+2C_{3}M_{1}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x_{0})).

Let
R_{1}:=\min\{R_{0},2^{-N/2}\}
. Then for any
R<R_{1}
, by
(
4.11
) and taking
r=R^{1+2/N}\ (\leqslant\frac{1}{2}R)
, we have for all
R\in(0,R_{1})
that

(4.12)

\displaystyle r^{2}\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}r^{2}\frac{\mu(B_{R}(x_{0}))}{\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))}+2C_{3}M_{1}r^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}r^{2}C_{4}(\frac{R}{r})^{N}+2C_{3}M_{1}r^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(2\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}}C_{4}+2C_{3}M_{1})r^{\frac{4}{N+2}},

where we used
\frac{\mu(B_{R}(x_{0}))}{\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leqslant C_{4}(R/r)^{N}
for some
C_{4}
depending only on
K,N
, (by
r<R<1
, see (
2.1
)).
It follows from the local Poincaré inequality
[
Raj12
]
that

(4.13)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|{\bf u}-\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}{\bf u}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{N,K,\bar{R}}\Big(r^{2}\fint_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{K,N,\bar{R},\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}},M_{1}}\cdot r^{\frac{2}{N+2}}

for all
r
such that
r^{\frac{N}{N+2}}\in(0,R_{1})
, which guarantees that
{\bf u}\in C^{1/(N+2)}(B_{\bar{R}/2}(\bar{x}))
, due to the Campanato theorem on metric measure spaces
[
Gór09
, Theorem 3.2]
.
∎

Here the Hölder index
1/(N+2)
is not optimal. We will show, in the next section, that
{\bf u}
is locally Lipschitz continuous provided that the metric measure space
(X,d,\mu)
is non-collapsed and
Q
satisfies (
1.3
).

In particular, Lemma
4.3
implies that
\left\{x\in\Omega\mid\left|{\bf u}(x)\right|>0\right\}
is an open set. By combining with the argument in Lemma
4.1
, we get the following consequence.

Lemma 4.4

(Harmonicity)
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then each component
u_{i}
is harmonic on the open set
\Omega_{\bf u}:=\left\{x\in\Omega\mid\left|{\bf u}(x)\right|>0\right\}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Proof.

We know from Lemma
4.3
that the set
\Omega_{\bf u}
is open. It suffices to show that
{\bf\Delta}u_{i}=0
in the sense of distributions on each small ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega_{\bf u}
, for each
i=1,2,\cdots m.

For each
0\leqslant\phi\in Lip_{0}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
let

(4.14)

{\bf v}_{i,\delta}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},u_{i}+\delta\phi,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{m})\text{,}

where
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}
and
\delta>0
, then

{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
.
Noted that
\dist({\bf v}_{i,\delta},{\bf u})\leqslant\delta\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}}+Q_{\max}\mu({\rm supp}\phi)
, when both
\delta
and
\mu(B_{R}(x_{0}))
are sufficiently small, the local minimality of
{\bf u}
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla{\bf v}_{i,\delta}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})\text{,}

where we have observed
\{|{\bf v}_{i,\delta}|>0\}=\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
, as
\delta
small enough. Thus, we have

(4.15)

\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2}\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(u_{i}+\delta\phi)\right|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}(\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2}+2\delta\left\langle{\nabla u_{i}},{\nabla\phi}\right\rangle+\delta^{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2})

for all sufficiently small
\delta
. Therefore, the arbitrariness of
\delta
gives

(4.16)

-\int_{\Omega}\left\langle{\nabla u_{i}},{\nabla\phi}\right\rangle\leqslant 0\text{.}

This yields
\boldsymbol{\Delta}u_{i}\leqslant 0
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
in the sense of distributions.
Meanwhile, Lemma
4.1
asserts that
\boldsymbol{\Delta}u_{i}\geqslant 0
on

\Omega
in the sense of distributions.
Thus, we conclude that
u_{i}
is harmonic on
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega_{\bf u}
. The proof is finished.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Recently, N. Gigli and I. V. Violo
[
GV21
]
obtained the locally Hölder continuity of a solution to an obstructed problem on
RCD(K,N)
-spaces.

5.
Lipschitz continuity of local minimizers

In this section, we derive the Lipschitz regularity for local minimizers of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) on a non-collapsed
RCD
metric measure space. For this, we will begin with a mean value inequality on general
RCD(K,N)
-spaces.

5.1.
Mean value inequality

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD(K,N)
metric measure space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,+\infty).

Lemma 5.1

(Stokes formula on balls)
.

Let
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset X
and
\rho(\cdot)=d(\cdot,x_{0})
,
\phi\in C^{2}([0,R])
and let
\psi=\phi\circ\rho.
Suppose that

u\in C(\overline{B_{R}(x_{0})})\cap W^{1,2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
. If
\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi
is a signed Radon measure, then

(5.1)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi=-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle+\phi^{\prime}(r)\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r}\Big(\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u\Big)

holds for almost all
r\in(0,R)
.

Proof.

Since
\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi
and
\mu
are signed Radon measures, we have for almost all
r\in(0,R)
that

(5.2)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left|\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi\right|(\overline{B_{r+1/j}(x_{0})}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0}))=0

and

(5.3)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mu(\overline{B_{r+1/j}(x_{0})}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0}))=0.

Meanwhile, noted that
s\mapsto\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u
is locally Lipschitz continuous on
(0,R)
, it is differentiable almost everywhere on
(0,R)
too. We fix an
r
such that both of them hold. For
j
sufficiently large, let
u_{j}=\eta_{j}(\rho)u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
, where

(5.4)

\eta_{j}(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }t\in\left[0,r\right]\text{,}\\
1-j(t-r)&\text{if }t\in(r,r+\frac{1}{j})\text{,}\\
0&\text{if }t\in\left[r+\frac{1}{j},R\right]\text{.}\end{cases}

On the one hand,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{j}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{j}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi+\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{j}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\eta_{j}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi+\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi\text{,}

where

(5.6)

\left|\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\eta_{j}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi\right|\leqslant\left|\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi\right|(\overline{B_{r+1/j}(x_{0})}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0}))\left\|u\right\|_{C^{0}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}.

By combining this, (
5.2
) and (
5.6
), we have

(5.7)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{j}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi=\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi\text{.}

On the other hand, from Remark
2.4
(1) and
u_{j}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
, we have

(5.8)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{j}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u_{j}},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle\eta_{j}-\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla\eta_{j}},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle\eta_{j}-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla\eta_{j}},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle u\text{.}

We want to estimate the first and the third terms of right hand side in (
5.8
). From (
5.3
) and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle\eta_{j}\right|\leqslant\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|\left|\nabla\psi\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{\left[0,R\right]}\left|\phi^{\prime}\right|\Big(\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0}))^{\frac{1}{2}},

we conclude that the first term of right hand side in (
5.8
) converges to
0
as
j\to\infty.
Noticed that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla\eta_{j}},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle u=\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{-j\nabla\rho},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-j\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\phi^{\prime}(\rho)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-j\phi^{\prime}(r)\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}u-j\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\phi^{\prime}(\rho)-\phi^{\prime}(r)\big)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\phi^{\prime}(r)\frac{\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})}u-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u}{\frac{1}{j}}-j\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\phi^{\prime}(\rho)-\phi^{\prime}(r)\big)u\text{,}

and that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left|j\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\phi^{\prime}(\rho)-\phi^{\prime}(r)\big)u\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle j\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{1}{j}\sup_{\left[0,R\right]}\left|\phi^{\prime\prime}\right|\left|u\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{\left[0,R\right]}\left|\phi^{\prime\prime}\right|(\int_{B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|u\right|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(B_{r+\frac{1}{j}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r}(x_{0}))^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{,}

we conclude, by (
5.3
) and the fact that
r\mapsto\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u
is differentiable at
r
, that the third term of right hand side in (
5.8
) converges to
\phi^{\prime}(r)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u),
as
j\to\infty
. Therefore, letting
j\to\infty
in (
5.8
), we obtain for almost all
r\in(0,R)
that

(5.11)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi=-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle+\phi^{\prime}(r)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u).

The proof is finished.
∎

A similar argument with a different cut-off function yields the following slight variant.

Remark 5.2

(Stokes formula on annuli)
.

Let
\overline{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})}\backslash B_{R_{1}}(x_{0})\subset X
,
\rho(\cdot)=d(\cdot,x_{0})
,
\phi\in C^{2}(\left[R_{1},R_{2}\right])
, and let
\psi=\phi\circ\rho
.
Suppose that

(5.12)

u\in C(\overline{B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})}\backslash B_{R_{1}}(x_{0}))\cap W^{1,2}(B_{R_{2}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{R_{1}}(x_{0}))\text{,}

and if
\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi
is a signed Radon measure, then

(5.13)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\psi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\phi^{\prime}(r_{2})\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{2}}(\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u)-\phi^{\prime}(r_{1})\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}(\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u)

holds for almost all
R_{1}<r_{1}<r_{2}<R_{2}
.

Lemma 5.3

(Mean value inequality)
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD(K,N)
-space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,+\infty)
, and let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain. Suppose that
B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
and that

(5.14)

u\in C(\overline{B_{R_{0}}(x_{0})})\cap W^{1,2}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{0}),\left[0,+\infty\right))\text{,}

and let
\rho=d(\cdot,x_{0})
. If

(5.15)

\liminf_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{r^{N}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u(x){\rm d}\mu=0,

then

(5.16)

\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u\leqslant C_{1}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{e^{-C_{2}s^{2}}}{s^{N+1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\rho^{2}\rangle\mathrm{d}s

for all
R\in(0,R_{0})
, where the constants
C_{1},C_{2}
only depend on
N,K
and
\Omega
.

Remark 5.4
.

On the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u-u(x_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{1}{s}\fint_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\langle\nabla u,\nabla\rho^{2}\rangle\mathrm{d}s\text{.}

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Noted that
\boldsymbol{\Delta}\rho^{2}
is a signed Radon measure
[
Gig15
]
and
u\geqslant 0
, Lemma
5.1
asserts that

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}(\rho^{2})=-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\rho^{2}}\right\rangle+2r\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\big(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u\big)

holds for almost all
r\in(0,R)
.
By combining this and the Laplacian comparison theorem (see (
2.8
) in Theorem
2.5
), we get

(5.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}(\rho^{2})+\frac{1}{2r}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\rho^{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2r}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}2(N+C_{3}\rho^{2})u+\frac{1}{2r}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\rho^{2}}\right\rangle\text{.}

Here and in the following of this proof,
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\cdots,
will denote positive constants depending only on
N,K
and
\Omega
.
This gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}(\frac{1}{r^{N}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u)\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{3}r\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{r^{N}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u\Big)+\frac{1}{2r^{N+1}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\rho^{2}}\right\rangle

for almost all
r\in(0,R)
. Multiplying both sides by
\exp(-C_{3}r^{2}/2)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}r^{2}}\frac{1}{r^{N}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}r^{2}}}{2r^{N+1}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\rho^{2}}\right\rangle\text{.}

Since
r\mapsto\frac{1}{r^{N}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}r^{2}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u
is locally Lipschitz on
\left(0,R\right]
, by integrating the above inequality over
(r,R)
for any
r<R
, we get

(5.18)

\begin{split}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}R^{2}}}{R^{N}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u-&\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}r^{2}}}{r^{N}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u\\
&\leqslant\int_{r}^{R}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}s^{2}}}{2s^{N+1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\rho^{2}}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s\text{.}\end{split}

By substituting the assumption (
5.15
) into (
5.18
) and let
r\to 0^{+}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}R^{2}}}{R^{N}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\int_{0}^{R}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}s^{2}}}{2s^{N+1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\rho^{2}}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s\text{.}

Therefore, by
\mu(B_{R}(x_{0}))/R^{N}\geqslant C_{5}:=\mu(B_{{\rm diam}(\Omega)}(x_{0}))/[{\rm diam}(\Omega)]^{N}
(this follows from (
2.1
) and
R<{\rm diam}(\Omega)
), we conclude

\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u\leqslant e^{\frac{1}{2}C_{3}R^{2}}\frac{1}{C_{5}}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{3}s^{2}}}{2s^{N+1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla u},{\nabla\rho^{2}}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}s\text{.}

This implies (
5.16
) with
C_{1}:=\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}C_{3}R^{2}}}{2C_{5}}
and
C_{2}:=\frac{C_{3}}{2}
. The proof is finished.
∎

5.2.
Lipschitz continuity of local minimizers of
J_{Q}

From now on, we shall suppose that
(X,d,\mu)
is an
ncRCD(K,N)
metric measure space with
K\leqslant 0
and
N\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain and let
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy (
1.3
) for two positive numbers
Q_{\min}
and
Q_{\max}
. Recall that a map

{\bf u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,+\infty)^{m})

is a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) if there exist a data
{\bf g}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,+\infty)^{m})
and
\varepsilon_{\bf u}>0
such that
J_{Q}({\bf u})\leqslant J_{Q}({\bf v})
for all
{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\bf g}
with
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
, where the
\mathscr{A}_{\bf g}
and
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})
are given in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), respectively. From Lemma
4.3
, we can assume that
{\bf u}
is continuous on
\Omega
. The set
\Omega_{\bf u}:=\{x\in\Omega|\ |{\bf u}|(x)>0\}
is open.

The following lemma is inspired by the classical Caccioppoli inequality.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with
Q
satisfying (
1.3
),
and let
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
. Then there exists a constant
R_{0}\ (\leqslant 1)
depending only on
N,K,\Omega,d(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega),Q_{\max}
and
\varepsilon_{\bf u}
, such that for all balls
B_{r}(x)
with
r<R_{0}
and
d(x,\Omega^{\prime})<R_{0}
, it holds

(5.19)

-\int_{B_{r}(x)}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\phi\rangle\leqslant\big(Q_{\max}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x))\big)^{1/2}\cdot\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x))}

for all
i=1,\cdots,m
and
\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{r}(x))
and
\phi\geqslant 0
.

Proof.

From (
2.13
), there is a number
R_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}d(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega))
with
R_{0}<1
, (depending only on
N,K,d(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega),\Omega,Q_{\max}
and
\varepsilon_{\bf u}
,)
such that

(5.20)

\big(Q_{\max}\mu(B_{R_{0}}(x))\big)^{1/2}+Q_{\max}\mu(B_{R_{0}}(x))<\varepsilon_{\bf u},\qquad\forall\ x\in\Omega.

Fix any ball
B_{r}(x)
with
r<R_{0}
and
d(x,\Omega^{\prime})<R_{0}
, where
R_{0}
is given in the above (
5.20
). The inequality (
5.19
) obviously holds if
\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x))}=0
,
so we are assuming that
\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x))}>0
in the following.
Put

(5.21)

\delta:=\big(Q_{\max}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x))\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big/\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x))}\text{.}

Let
{\bf v}=(u_{1},\cdots,u_{i-1},u_{i}+\delta\phi,u_{i+1},\cdots,u_{m})\in\mathscr{A}_{\bf g}
. Note that

\displaystyle\dist({\bf v},{\bf u})

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta\cdot\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x)))}+Q_{\max}\mu({\rm supp}\phi)

\displaystyle\leqslant(Q_{\max}\mu(B_{r}(x)))^{\frac{1}{2}}+Q_{\max}\mu(B_{r}(x))<\varepsilon_{\bf u}

provided
r<R_{0}
, by (
5.20
). The local minimality of
{\bf u}
implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle J_{Q}({\bf u})-\int_{\Omega}Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}\leqslant J_{Q}({\bf v})-\int_{\Omega}Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}(Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}\right|>0\right\}}-Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2}+Q_{\max}\mu(B_{r}(x))

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+2\delta\int_{B_{r}(x)}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\phi\rangle+\delta^{2}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+Q_{\max}\mu(B_{r}(x))

for all
r<R_{0}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle-2\int_{B_{r}(x)}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\phi\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+\frac{Q_{\max}}{\delta}\mu(B_{r}(x))

\displaystyle\leqslant\delta\cdot\|\phi\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x))}^{2}+\frac{Q_{\max}}{\delta}\mu(B_{r}(x))\text{,}

which is equivalent to (
5.19
) by (
5.21
), and the proof is finished.
∎

Combining Lemma
5.5
and Lemma
5.3
, we are able to control the growth of local minimizers near the free boundary
\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega.

Lemma 5.6

(Optimal linear growth)
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with
Q
satisfying (
1.3
), and let
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
. Let
R_{0}
be the constant given in Lemma
5.5
. Suppose that
B_{r}(x_{0})
is a ball with radius
r<R_{0}
and
d(x_{0},\Omega^{\prime})<R_{0}
. Then, if
u_{i}(x_{0})=0
for some
i=1,\cdots,m
, it holds

(5.22)

\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u_{i}(x)\leqslant C\sqrt{Q_{\max}}\cdot r,

where
C=C_{N,K,\Omega}>0
depends only on
K,N
and
\Omega
.

Proof.

Since
(X,d,\mu)
is non-collapsed, from
u_{i}(x_{0})=0
and (
2.13
), we know that the condition (
5.15
) in Lemma
5.3
holds.
For each
s<R_{0}
, by using Lemma
5.5
to
\phi=s^{2}-\rho^{2}(x)
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\rho^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla(s^{2}-\rho^{2})\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(Q_{\max}\cdot\mu(B_{s}(x_{0}))\big)^{1/2}\cdot\|s^{2}-\rho^{2}\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{s}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(Q_{\max}\cdot\mu(B_{s}(x_{0}))\big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}(4\rho^{2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}+(s^{2}-\rho^{2})^{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 3\sqrt{Q_{\max}}\cdot\mu(B_{s}(x_{0}))\cdot s\quad\qquad({\rm by}\ \ \rho<s,\ \ s<R_{0}\leqslant 1).

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{N,K}\sqrt{Q_{\max}}\cdot s^{N+1}\quad\qquad({\rm by}\ \eqref{equ2.12},\ s\leqslant 1).

Since
{\bf u}
is continuous on
\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}
by Lemma
4.3
,
it follows from Lemma
5.3
and
u_{i}(x_{0})=0
that

(5.23)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{i}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{e^{-C_{2}s^{2}}}{s^{N+1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\rho^{2}\rangle\mathrm{d}s\leqslant C_{3}\sqrt{Q_{\max}}\cdot r\text{,}

for all
r<R_{0}
. Thus, by using the fact that
u_{i}
is subharmonic (Lemma
4.1
) and
u_{i}\geqslant 0
, we get (see, for example,
[
KS01
, Theorem 4.2]
)

\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u_{i}\leqslant C_{4}\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{i}\leqslant C_{4}C_{3}\sqrt{Q_{\max}}\cdot r,\qquad\forall\ r<R_{0}.

The proof is finished.
∎

As a corollary of the combination of the linear growth and the Cheng-Yau’s gradient estimate for harmonic functions, one can get the following gradient estimate near the free boundary
\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with
Q
satisfying (
1.3
), and let
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
.
There exists a positive constant
C=C_{N,K,\Omega}>0
(depending only on
K,N
and
\Omega
), such that: if
x_{1}\in\Omega^{\prime}
and if
d(x_{1},\{|{\bf u}|=0\}\cap\Omega)<R_{0}/8
, then it holds

(5.24)

\Lip u_{i}(x_{1})\leqslant C\sqrt{Q_{\max}},\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,m,

where
R_{0}
is given in Lemma
5.5
, and
\Lip u_{i}
is the pointwise Lipschitz constant defined in (
2.4
).

Proof.

We will finish this proof by considering two cases as follows.

(i) In the case where
d(x_{1},\{|{\bf u}|=0\}\cap\Omega)=0
. The continuity of
{\bf u}
implies the
\{|{\bf u}|=0\}
is relative closed in
\Omega
. This implies
x_{1}\in\{|{\bf u}|=0\}
in this case. By Lemma
5.6
, we have

\sup_{y\in B_{r/2}(x_{1})}\frac{|u_{i}(y)-u_{i}(x_{1})|}{r}\leqslant C\sqrt{Q_{\max}},\quad\ i=1,2,\cdots,m,

for all
r<R_{0}
. By (
2.4
) and letting
r\to 0
, this yields
\Lip u_{i}(x_{1})\leqslant C\sqrt{Q_{\max}}.

(ii) In the case where
d(x_{1},\{|{\bf u}|=0\}\cap\Omega)>0
. We put

r_{1}:=d(x_{1},\{|{\bf u}|=0\}\cap\Omega)\in(0,R_{0}/8).

Since
B_{r_{1}/2}(x_{1})\subset\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
,
from Lemma
4.4
, we have known that all
u_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,m,
are harmonic on
B_{r_{1}/2}(x_{1})
. By using Cheng-Yau estimate, Theorem
2.6
, we obtain

(5.25)

\Lip u_{i}(x_{1})\leqslant\sup_{y\in B_{r_{1}/4}(x_{1})}\Lip u_{i}(y)\leqslant\frac{C}{r_{1}}\sup_{y\in B_{r_{1}/4}(x_{1})}u_{i}(y),

where the constant
C
depends only on
N,K
and
\Omega
. Take
x_{2}\in B_{2r_{1}}(x_{1})\cap\{|{\bf u}|=0\}
.
By applying Lemma
5.6
to
B_{8r_{1}}(x_{2})
, (remark that
d(x_{2},\Omega^{\prime})<2r
and the assumption
8r_{1}<R_{0}
,) we have

(5.26)

\sup_{y\in B_{r_{1}/4}(x_{1})}u_{i}(y)\leqslant\sup_{y\in B_{4r_{1}}(x_{2})}u_{i}(y)\leqslant C\sqrt{Q_{\max}}\cdot r_{1}.

The combination of (
5.25
) and (
5.26
) implies the desired estimate (
5.24
). Now the proof is completed.
∎

Now we are in the position to show the local Lipschitz continuity of
{\bf u}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
B_{R}(x)\subset\Omega
. Let
R_{0}
be the constant given in Lemma
5.5
with respect to
\Omega^{\prime}:=B_{R/2}(x)
.

Take any
x_{1}\in B_{R/2}(x)
. If
d(x_{1},\Omega\cap\{|{\bf u}|=0\})<R_{0}/8
, then Lemma
5.7
asserts

\Lip u_{i}(x_{1})\leqslant C\sqrt{Q_{\max}},\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,m,

If
d(x_{1},\Omega\cap\{|{\bf u}|=0\})\geqslant R_{0}/8
, that is,
B_{R_{0}/10}(x_{1})\subset\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
, then Cheng-Yau’s estimate, Theorem
2.6
, asserts

\Lip u_{i}(x_{1})\leqslant\frac{C}{R_{0}}\sup_{\Omega}|{\bf u}|,\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,m.

By summing up the both cases and recalling Remark
4.2
, we conclude that
there exists a constant
L
depending only on
N,K,\Omega,R,Q_{\max},\varepsilon_{\bf u}
and
\int_{B_{R}(x)}|{\bf u}|{\rm d}\mu
, such that

(5.27)

\sup_{B_{R/2}(x)}\Lip u_{i}(x)\leqslant L,\qquad\forall\ i=1,2,\cdots,m.

Take any
y,z\in B_{R/4}(x).
Let
\gamma:[0,d(x,y)]\to\Omega
be a geodesic from
y
to
z
. The triangle inequality implies that
\gamma\subset B_{R/2}(x)
. Noted that
\Lip u_{i}
is one of the upper gradient of
u_{i}
(see
[
Che99
]
), the estimate (
5.27
) implies that

|u_{i}(y)-u_{i}(z)|\leqslant\int_{0}^{d(y,z)}\Lip u_{i}\circ\gamma(s){\rm d}s\leqslant L\cdot d(y,z),

for each
i=1,2,\cdots,m
. The proof is finished.
∎

6.
Local finiteness of perimeter for the free boundary

We continue to assume that
(X,d,\mu)
is an
ncRCD(K,N)
metric measure space with
K\leqslant 0
and
N\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
\Omega\subset X
be a bounded domain and let
Q\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy (
1.3
) for two positive numbers
Q_{\min}
and
Q_{\max}
. Let

{\bf u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})

be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with a boundary data
{\bf g}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,[0,+\infty)^{m})
, i.e., there exists
\varepsilon_{\bf u}>0
such that
J_{Q}({\bf u})\leqslant J_{Q}({\bf v})
for all
{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\bf g}
with
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
, where the
\mathscr{A}_{\bf g}
and
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})
are given in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), respectively.
From Theorem
1.4
, we know that
{\bf u}
is locally Lipschitz continuous in the interior of
\Omega
.

We begin with the nondegeneracy of the local minimizer
{\bf u}
near the free boundary.

6.1.
Nondegeneracy

Theorem 6.1

(Nondegeneracy)
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with
Q
satisfying (
1.3
), and let
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega.
Then there is a constant
R_{1}>0
(depending only on
N,K,\Omega^{\prime},Q_{\max},\varepsilon_{\bf u}
and the Lipschitz constant of
{\bf u}
on
\Omega^{\prime}
) such that for any ball
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
with
x_{0}\in\partial\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\}\cap\Omega^{\prime}
and
r<R_{1}
, it holds

(6.1)

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|{\bf u}\right|\geqslant c\sqrt{Q_{\min}}\cdot r\text{,}

where the positive constant
c
depends only on
N,K,\Omega^{\prime},\varepsilon_{\bf u}
and the Lipschitz constant of
{\bf u}
on
\Omega^{\prime}
.

Proof.

In the Euclidean setting, this assertion was established in
[
CSY18
, Theorem 3]
and
[
MTV17
]
. It was extended to smooth Riemannian manifolds in
[
LS20
]
. Here we will extend their arguments to nonsmooth setting. Without loss of the generality, we can assume that
K<0
.

Fix any
r\in(0,1)
and let
M=\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|{\bf u}\right|
. Since
{\bf u}
is Lipschitz continuous on
\Omega^{\prime}
(see Theorem
1.4
) and
{\bf u}(x_{0})=0
, we get
M\leqslant L
, the Lipschitz constant of
{\bf u}
on
\Omega^{\prime}
. Given any
\theta\in(0,1)
, as in
[
CSY18
]
, we consider the map
{\bf v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
, where

(6.2)

v_{i}(y)=\begin{cases}\min\left\{u_{i}(y),M\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho(y)}{r})\right\}&\text{if }y\in B_{r}(x_{0})\text{,}\\
u_{i}(y)&\text{if }y\not\in B_{r}(x_{0})\text{,}\end{cases}

for all
i=1,\ldots,m
and
y\in\Omega
,
where
\rho(\cdot)=d(\cdot,x_{0})
,

(6.3)

\psi_{\theta}(t)=\frac{(\phi_{N,K}(t)-\phi_{N,K}(\theta))^{+}}{\phi_{N,K}(1)-\phi_{N,K}(\theta)}

and

\phi_{N,K}(s)=-\int^{1}_{s}\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{-K/(N-1)}t)}{\sqrt{-K/(N-1)}}\right)^{1-N}\mathrm{d}t.

Then it is clear that
{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
and
u_{i}-v_{i}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
for all
i=1,2,\cdots,m
.

(i) We first check that
\dist({\bf v},{\bf u})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
provided both
r
and
\theta
are sufficiently small.
The co-area formula gives

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r})\right|^{2}{\rm d}\mu&\leqslant\int_{\theta r}^{r}\left|\frac{1}{r}\psi^{\prime}_{\theta}(\frac{s}{r})\right|^{2}\cdot\frac{d^{+}}{dr}\mu(B_{s}(x_{0})){\rm d}s.\end{split}

Since
(X,d,\mu)
is non-collapsed, substituting (
2.14
) into the above inequality, we obtain

(6.4)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r})\right|^{2}{\rm d}\mu&\leqslant C_{1}r^{N-2}\cdot\left(\int_{\theta}^{1}s^{1-N}{\rm d}s\right)^{-1},\end{split}

where we have used
|\psi_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)|\leqslant C_{N,K}\frac{t^{1-N}}{|\phi_{N,K}(1)-\phi_{N,K}(\theta)|}\leqslant\frac{C^{\prime}_{N,K}\cdot t^{1-N}}{\int_{\theta}^{1}s^{1-N}{\rm d}s}
for all
t\in(\theta,1)
.
Here and in the following of this proof, all constants
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots
depend only on
N,K,\Omega^{\prime}
.

Noticed that
|\psi_{\theta}|\leqslant 1
and
u_{i}-v_{i}=(u_{i}-M\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r}))^{+}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, we get

(6.5)

\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{i}-v_{i}\right|^{2}\leqslant\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}((u_{i}-M\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r}))^{+})^{2}\leqslant M^{2}\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))

and (by the fact that
[
Che99
]
, for any

(6.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(u_{i}-v_{i})\right|^{2}

From the combination of (

,

(ii) Fixed any

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}\right|>0\right\}})-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x)}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}\right|>0\right\}})-\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla v_{i}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2})\text{.}

Let

(6.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla v_{i}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}\Big(-2\left\langle{\nabla v_{i}},{\nabla w_{i}}\right\rangle-\left|\nabla w_{i}\right|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle-2\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla v_{i}},{\nabla w_{i}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla(M\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r}))},{\nabla w_{i}}\right\rangle,

for each
i=1,\ldots,m
,
where for the last equality, we have used that
\left|\nabla w_{i}\right|=0

\mu
-a.e. on

(see
[

]
or Proposition
2.1
(3)) and that
v_{i}=M\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r})
on

. Remark that
\{w_{i}\not=0\}
is open by the continuity of
w_{i}
.

(iii) Next we want to estimate
\sum_{i=1,}^{m}I_{\theta r,r}(w_{i}),
where we denote

(6.10)

I_{r_{1},r_{2}}(w_{i}):=-\int_{B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\left\langle{\nabla(M\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r}))},{\nabla w_{i}}\right\rangle

for any

with
r_{1}<r_{2}
.

By the Laplacian comparison theorem (see (
2.7
) in Theorem
2.5
) and that the space

satisfies

, we conclude that

\boldsymbol{\Delta}\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r})\leqslant 0\quad\mathrm{on}\ B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\overline{B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}

in the sense of distributions. From Remark
5.2
, we have for almost all
r_{1},r_{2}\in(\theta r,r)
with
r_{1}<r_{2}
, that

(6.11)

\displaystyle I_{r_{1},r_{2}}(w_{i})=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})\setminus B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}w_{i}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}(M\psi_{\rho}(\frac{\rho}{r}))

\displaystyle+M\frac{\psi_{\theta}^{\prime}(\frac{r_{1}}{r})}{r}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}w_{i}-M\frac{\psi_{\theta}^{\prime}(\frac{r_{2}}{r})}{r}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{2}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}w_{i}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{N,K,\theta}\frac{M}{r}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}w_{i},

where we have used that
0\leqslant\phi^{\prime}_{N,K}(t)\leqslant C_{N,K,\theta}
for all
t\in(\theta,1)
and that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}w_{i}\geqslant 0
for almost all
s\in(\theta r,r).

For almost every
r_{1}\in(\theta r,r)
such that both
s\mapsto\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}w_{i}

s\mapsto\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u_{i}

(6.12)

\begin{split}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}w_{i}-\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u_{i}=&\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{A_{r_{1},r_{1}+\delta}}\big(u_{i}-w_{i}\big)\\
\leqslant&\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{A_{r_{1},r_{1}+\delta}}\big|u_{i}-w_{i}\big|,\end{split}

where
A_{r_{1},r_{1}+\delta}:=B_{r_{1}+\delta}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})
.

From the definition
w_{i}=(u_{i}-M\psi_{\theta}(\frac{\rho}{r}))^{+}
,
M\leqslant L
and
|\psi^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)|\leqslant C_{N,K,\theta}
in
(\theta,1)

with a Lipschitz constant

By using

(6.13)

\sup_{A_{r_{1},r_{1}+\delta}}\big|u_{i}-w_{i}\big|\leqslant C_{L,N,K,\theta}\cdot(r_{1}+\delta-\theta r).

Substituting this into (
6.12

(6.14)

\begin{split}&\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}w_{i}-\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u_{i}\\
\leqslant&\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}C_{L,N,K,\theta}\cdot(r_{1}+\delta-\theta r)\cdot\frac{\mu(B_{r_{1}+\delta}(x_{0})\backslash B_{r_{1}}(x_{0}))}{\delta}\\
\leqslant&C^{\prime}_{L,N,K,\theta,\Omega^{\prime}}\cdot(r_{1}-\theta r),\end{split}

where for the last inequality we have used (

On the other hand, by using Lemma

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r_{1}}\left(\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}u_{i}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\Delta}(\rho^{2})+\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\left\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\rho^{2}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C_{N,K}}{2r_{1}}\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}u_{i}+\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C_{N,K,\theta}}{r}\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|{\bf u}\right|+\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|,

where we have used

and
r_{1}\geqslant\theta r

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u_{i}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{N,K,m,\theta}\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\Big(\frac{1}{r}\left|{\bf u}\right|+\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{N,K,m,\theta}\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\Big(\frac{1}{r}\left|{\bf u}\right|\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}+\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{N,K,m,\theta}\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\Big(\frac{M}{r}\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}(Q_{\min}\chi^{2}_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}+\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{N,K,m,\theta}\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\Big(\frac{M}{Q_{\min}r}Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}(Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}+\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{N,K,m,\theta}\Big(\frac{M}{Q_{\min}r}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}\Big)\int_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\Big(Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}+\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}\Big)\text{,}

where for the second inequality we have used
|\nabla{\bf u}|=0

\mu
-a.e. in
\{|{\bf u}|=0\}
(
[
Che99
]
), and for the third inequality we have used
\sup_{B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\left|{\bf u}\right|\leqslant M
. From the combination of (
6.11
), (
6.14
), (
6.15
) and the fact

\lim_{r_{1}\to\theta r}I_{\theta r,r_{1}}(w_{i})=0,\qquad\lim_{r_{2}\to r}I_{r_{2},r}(w_{i})=0,

by letting

(6.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}I_{\theta r,r}(w_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(I_{\theta r,r_{1}}(w_{i})+I_{r_{1},r_{2}}(w_{i})+I_{r_{2},r}(w_{i})\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{r_{1}\to\theta r}\sum_{i=1}^{m}C_{N,K,\theta}\frac{M}{r}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=r_{1}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}u_{i}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{N,K,m,\theta}\frac{M}{r}\Big(\frac{M}{Q_{\min}r}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}\Big)\int_{B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}\Big(Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}+\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}\Big).

(iv) At last, the assumption
x_{0}\in\partial\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\}
and the continuity of

implies

\int_{B_{\theta r}(x_{0})}\Big(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}}\Big)\geqslant Q_{\min}\cdot\mu\big(B_{\theta r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\big)>0.

Thus, by combining this with (
6.8
), (
6.9
), (
6.10
) and (

1\leqslant C^{\prime}_{N,K,m,\theta}\frac{2M}{r}\Big(\frac{M}{Q_{\min}\cdot r}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}\Big).

This implies

\frac{M}{\sqrt{Q_{\min}}\cdot r}\geqslant\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2C^{\prime}_{N,K,m,\theta}}\right\}\text{.}

This is the desired estimate (

and
\theta
is given in (

6.2.
Density estimates near the free boundary

In the subsection, we will show that both
\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
and
\{|{\bf u}|=0\}
have positive density along the free boundary.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with
Q
satisfying (
1.3
), and let
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega.
Then for any ball
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega^{\prime}
with
x_{0}\in\Omega^{\prime}\cap\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
and
r<2R_{1}
, where
R_{1}
is given in Theorem
6.1
, we have

(6.17)

\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\big)\geqslant c_{1}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))

for some constant
c_{1}
depending on
m,N,K,\Omega^{\prime},Q_{\min},\varepsilon_{\bf u}
and
L
, the Lipschitz constant of
{\bf u}
on
\Omega^{\prime}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
6.1
, there exists some
i_{0}\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
such that

\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u_{i_{0}}\geqslant\frac{c\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}{m}\cdot r/2

for all
r/2<R_{1}
, where
R_{1}
and
c
are given in Theorem
6.1
. Choose
y_{0}\in B_{r/2}(x_{0})
such that
u_{i_{0}}(y_{0})\geqslant c_{2}r/2
, where
c_{2}:=\frac{c\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}{2m}
. Since
u_{i_{0}}
is Lipschitz continuous on
B_{r/2}(y_{0})\subset\Omega^{\prime}
with a Lipschitz constant
L
, we have

\inf_{B_{c_{3}r}(y_{0})}u_{i_{0}}\geqslant u_{i_{0}}(y_{0})-c_{3}Lr\geqslant c_{2}r/4,\qquad c_{3}:=\min\big\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{c_{2}}{8L}\big\}.

In particular, this yields
B_{c_{3}r}(y_{0})\subset\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
.
It follows

\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\big)\geqslant\mu(B_{c_{3}r}(y_{0})).

By combining this and the Bishop-Gromov inequality

\mu(B_{c_{3}r}(y_{0}))\geqslant c_{N,K}{(c_{3}/2)^{N}}\mu(B_{2r}(y_{0}))\geqslant c_{N,K}{(c_{3}/2)^{N}}\mu(B_{r}(x_{0})),

we get the desired estimate (
6.17
).
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of
J_{Q}
in (
1.2
) with
Q
satisfying (
1.3
), and let
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega.

Then there exists a number
R_{0}\in(0,1)
depending only on
N,K,\Omega^{\prime},Q_{\max}
and
{\bf u}
, such that for any ball
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega^{\prime}
with
x_{0}\in\Omega^{\prime}\cap\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}
and
r<R_{0}
, we have

(6.18)

\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|=0\}\big)\geqslant c_{4}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))

for some constant
c_{4}
depending on
m,N,K,\Omega^{\prime},Q_{\min},Q_{\max},\varepsilon_{\bf u}
and the Lipschitz constant of
{\bf u}
on
\Omega^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
{\bf v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{m})\in W^{1,2}(B_{r}(x_{0}),\mathbb{R}^{m})
be the map such that each component
v_{i}
is the (unique) solution of the following (relaxed) Dirichlet problem
[
Che99
, Theorem 7.12]
:

(6.19)

\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\Delta}v_{i}=0\quad\text{ on }\ B_{r}(x_{0}),\\
v_{i}-u_{i}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(B_{r}(x_{0})).\end{cases}

After extending
{\bf v}
by
{\bf u}
on
\Omega\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})
, we have

{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{g}}
because all
v_{i}\geqslant 0

on
B_{R}(x_{0})
by the maximum principle (see
[
Che99
, Theorem 7.17]
).
In the proof of Lemma
4.3
, it was showed that there is
R_{0}\in(0,1)
(depending only on
N,K,\Omega^{\prime}
and
{\bf u}
) such that
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
for all
r\in(0,R_{0})
.

The local minimality of
{\bf u}
implies

(6.20)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2})\leqslant\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}Q(\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}\right|>0\right\}}-\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})

for all
r\in(0,R_{0})
. From the harmonicity of
{\bf v}
, the same argument in (
4.9
) gives

(6.21)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}(|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2})=\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\right|^{2}.

Note that
|{\bf v}|
is not identical zero on
B_{r}(x_{0})
by Theorem
6.1
. We get from the strong maximum principle (see, for example,
[
KS01
, Corollary 6.4]
) that
|{\bf v}|>0
on
B_{r}(x_{0})
. Hence,

(6.22)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}Q(\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf v}\right|>0\right\}}-\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>0\right\}})&=\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}Q\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|=0\right\}}\\
&\leqslant Q_{\max}\cdot\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|=0\}\big).\end{split}

By combining (
6.20
)–(
6.22
) and the Poincaré inequality (see Proposition
2.1
(5)), we get

(6.23)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|{\bf u}-{\bf v}|^{2}\leqslant C_{P}\cdot r^{2}\cdot Q_{\max}\cdot\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|=0\}\big),

where the Poincaré constant
C_{P}
depends only on
N,K
and
\Omega^{\prime}
.

From the nondegeneracy Theorem
6.1
, there exists some
i_{0}\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
such that

\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u_{i_{0}}\geqslant c\sqrt{Q_{\min}}r/2.
Recalling
{\bf\Delta}(u_{i_{0}}-v_{i_{0}})\geqslant 0
on
B_{r}(x_{0})
in the sense of distributions, the maximum principle implies that
v_{i_{0}}\geqslant u_{i_{0}}
on
B_{r}(x_{0})
. Hence, from this and the Harnack inequality, we have

(6.24)

\inf_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}v_{i_{0}}\geqslant C_{1}\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}v_{i_{0}}\geqslant C_{1}\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u_{i_{0}}\geqslant C_{1}c\sqrt{Q_{\min}}r/2,

where the constant
C_{1}
depends only on
N,K,\Omega^{\prime}.
Since
u_{i_{0}}(x_{0})=0
and that
u_{i_{0}}
is Lipschitz continuous on
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega^{\prime}
with a Lipschitz constant
L
, we have

\sup_{B_{c_{1}r}(x_{0})}u_{i_{0}}\leqslant Lc_{1}r,\quad{\rm with}\ \ c_{1}:=\min\Big\{\frac{C_{1}c\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}{4L},\frac{1}{4}\Big\}.

Combining this with (
6.24
), we conclude that

\inf_{B_{c_{1}r}(x_{0})}(v_{i_{0}}-u_{i_{0}})\geqslant\frac{C_{1}c\sqrt{Q_{\min}}}{4}r:=C_{2}\sqrt{Q_{\min}}\cdot r,

where the constant
C_{2}
depends on
m,N,K,\Omega^{\prime},\varepsilon_{\bf u}
and
L
.
This yields

(6.25)

\int_{B_{c_{1}r}(x_{0})}|{\bf u}-{\bf v}|^{2}\geqslant\int_{B_{c_{1}r}(x_{0})}(v_{i_{0}}-u_{i_{0}})^{2}\geqslant C_{2}^{2}Q_{\min}\cdot r^{2}\cdot\mu(B_{c_{1}r}(x_{0})).

From (
6.23
), (
6.25
) and that
\mu(B_{c_{1}r}(x_{0}))\geqslant C_{N,K}c_{1}^{N}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, it follows

C_{P}Q_{\max}\cdot\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|=0\}\big)\geqslant C_{2}^{2}Q_{\min}C_{N,K}c_{1}^{N}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x_{0})).

This is the desired (
6.18
) with the constant
c_{4}:=C_{P}^{-1}C^{2}_{2}C_{N,K}c_{1}^{N}\cdot Q_{\min}/Q_{\max}
. The proof is finished.
∎

, Lemma 2.4]
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an
RCD(K,N)
space with
K\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in(1,+\infty)
, and let
D\subset X
be an open subset.
Suppose that
0\leqslant\psi\in\Lip_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)\cap W^{1,1}(D)
.
If there are positive constants
\bar{\varepsilon}
and
C
such that

(6.26)

\int_{\left\{0<\psi<\varepsilon\right\}\cap D}\left|\nabla\psi\right|\leqslant C\varepsilon

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{\varepsilon})
, then

(6.27)

\mathcal{P}(\left\{\psi>0\right\},D)\leqslant 2C\text{.}

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{\varepsilon}/2)
,
then, by the coarea formula
[
Amb01
, Theorem 3.3]
(see also Proposition
2.15
(2)) to
\chi_{D}
, we have

(6.28)

\displaystyle\int_{\varepsilon}^{2\varepsilon}\mathcal{P}(\left\{\psi>t\right\},D)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon\leqslant\psi<2\varepsilon}\chi_{D}{\rm d}|D\psi|=\int_{\left\{\varepsilon\leqslant\psi<2\varepsilon\right\}\cap D}\left|\nabla\psi\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\left\{0<\psi<2\varepsilon\right\}\cap D}\left|\nabla\psi\right|\leqslant 2C\varepsilon.

For each integer
j
so large that
2^{-j}\leqslant\bar{\varepsilon}/2
, by setting
\varepsilon_{j}=2^{-j}
,
there exists some
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{j}\in(\varepsilon_{j},2\varepsilon_{j})
such that

(6.29)

\mathcal{P}(\left\{\psi>\varepsilon^{\prime}_{j}\right\},D)\leqslant\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\int_{\varepsilon_{j}}^{2\varepsilon_{j}}\mathcal{P}(\left\{\psi>t\right\},D)\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}{2C\varepsilon_{j}}\leqslant{2C}\text{.}

It is clear that

\chi_{\left\{\psi>\varepsilon^{\prime}_{j}\right\}}\xrightarrow{L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1}(D)}\chi_{\left\{\psi>0\right\}},
by Lebesgue’s dominated converge theorem.
From (
6.29
) and the lower semicontinuity of perimeter
[
Amb01
]
, it follows that

(6.30)

\mathcal{P}(\left\{\psi>0\right\},D)\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{P}(\left\{\psi>\varepsilon^{\prime}_{j}\right\},D)\leqslant{2C}\text{.}

This finishes the proof. ∎

Now let us check the condition (

) of the above lemma for the local minimizers of

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be a local minimizer of

in (
1.2
) with

satisfying (
1.3
), and let
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
. Then there exist
\bar{r}>0,\bar{\varepsilon}>0
and

such that

(6.31)

\int_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}+\mu\big(\{0<|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r/2}(x_{0})\big)\leqslant C\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\big)\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{r^{2}}

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{\varepsilon})
,
r\in(0,\bar{r})
and
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega^{\prime}
. Here the constants
\bar{r},\bar{\varepsilon},C
depend only on
N,K,\Omega^{\prime},{\rm diam}(\Omega),Q_{\min},\varepsilon_{\bf u}
and
L
, the Lipschitz constant of
{\bf u}
on
\Omega^{\prime}

Proof.

Fix any ball
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega^{\prime}
such that
r\in(0,\bar{r})
, where
\bar{r}<1
will be determined later. Let
\phi:M\to\left[0,1\right]
be a cutoff function with

\phi\equiv 1
on
B_{r/2}(x_{0})

\supp(\phi)\subset B_{r}(x_{0})
,
and
r\left|\nabla\phi\right|\leqslant C_{N}

For each
\varepsilon>0
, we set

(6.32)

{\bf v}:=\begin{cases}(1-\phi){\bf u}&\text{if }\left|{\bf u}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\text{,}\\
(1-\varepsilon\frac{\phi}{\left|{\bf u}\right|}){\bf u}&\text{if }\left|{\bf u}\right|>\varepsilon\text{.}\end{cases}

It is easy to check
{\bf v}\in\mathscr{A}_{\bf g}
and
{\bf v}-{\bf u}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}),\mathbb{R}^{m})

(i) We will first show that

r
and
\varepsilon

2.1
(5)) and
{\bf v}-{\bf u}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{r}(x_{0}),\mathbb{R}^{m})

(6.33)

\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})\leqslant C_{P}\Big(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}+\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\big),

where
C_{P}
depends on
N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega).

From
{\bf u}-{\bf v}=\phi{\bf u}
on
\{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})

[
Che99
, Corollary 2.25]

|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|=|\nabla(\phi{\bf u})|\leqslant L+\varepsilon\cdot\frac{C_{N}}{r},\quad\mu{\rm-a.e.\ \ on}\ \ \{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0}),

where
L
is a Lipschitz constant of
{\bf u}
on
\Omega^{\prime}
, and we have used
|\nabla\phi|\leqslant\frac{C_{N}}{r}.

(6.34)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}}|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|^{2}\leqslant\big(L+C_{N}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\big)^{2}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x_{0})).

On
\{|{\bf u}|>\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})

h:=\frac{\varepsilon\phi}{|{\bf u}|}.
Then, from the Chain rule (see
[
Gig15
]

|\nabla h|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{|\bf u|}\cdot\frac{C_{N}}{r}+\frac{\varepsilon\phi|\nabla|{\bf u}||}{|{\bf u}|^{2}}\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{|\bf u|}\cdot\frac{C_{N}}{r}+\frac{L}{|{\bf u}|}

\mu{\rm-a.e.}
on
\{|{\bf u}|>\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})

|\nabla|{\bf u}||\leqslant|\nabla{\bf u}|\leqslant L
,
\varepsilon\phi\leqslant|{\bf u}|
and
|\nabla\phi|\leqslant\frac{C_{N}}{r}.

From this and
{\bf u}-{\bf v}=h{\bf u}

[
Che99

|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|=|\nabla(h{\bf u})|\leqslant L+\varepsilon\cdot\frac{C_{N}}{r}+h|\nabla{\bf u}|\leqslant 2L+\varepsilon\cdot\frac{C_{N}}{r},

\mu{\rm-a.e.}
on
\{|{\bf u}|>\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
. Thus, we obtain

(6.35)

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|>\varepsilon\}}|\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})|^{2}\leqslant\big(2L+C_{N}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\big)^{2}\cdot\mu(B_{r}(x_{0})).

From (
6.33
), (
6.34
) and (
6.35
), it follows that

(6.36)

\begin{split}\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})&\leqslant C_{1}\Big(L+\frac{\varepsilon}{r}\Big)\cdot\sqrt{\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))}+\mu(B_{r}(x_{0})),\end{split}

where
C_{1}
depending only on
N,K
and
{\rm diam}(\Omega)
. By the non-collapsing property of
X
, we have
N\geqslant 2
(since
N
is an integer and
N>1
), and then
\mu(B_{r}(x_{0}))\leqslant C_{2}r^{N}\leqslant C_{2}r^{2}
for
r<1
, where
C_{2}
depends only on
N,K
(see (
2.13
)). Therefore, we conclude
\dist({\bf u},{\bf v})<\varepsilon_{\bf u}
provided

(6.37)

\varepsilon\leqslant\bar{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\varepsilon_{\bf u}}{4C_{1}C_{2}^{1/2}}\qquad r\leqslant\bar{r}:=\min\Big\{1,\frac{\varepsilon_{\bf u}}{4(C_{1}LC^{1/2}_{2}+C_{2})}\Big\}.

(ii) Fix any
r\in(0,\bar{r})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{\varepsilon})
given in (
6.37
). From the local minimality of
{\bf u}
, we obtain

(6.38)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}Q\big(\chi_{\{|{\bf v}|>0\}}-\chi_{\{|\bf u|>0\}}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}}Q\big(\chi_{\{|{\bf v}|>0\}}-\chi_{\{|\bf u|>0\}}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\int_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}Q\chi_{\{0<|\bf u|\leqslant\varepsilon\}}

\displaystyle\leqslant-Q_{\min}\cdot\mu\big(B_{r/2}(x_{0})\cap\{0<|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\big),

where for the second inequality we have used
\chi_{\{|{\bf v}|>0\}}-\chi_{\{|\bf u|>0\}}\leqslant 0
on
B_{r}(x_{0})
, and for the third inequality we have used
{\bf v}=0
on
B_{r/2}(x_{0})\cap\{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}
.

On the one hand, from
{\bf v}=(1-\phi){\bf u}
on
\{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
and
[
Che99
, Corollary 2.25]
,
it follows
|\nabla{\bf v}|=|\nabla((1-\phi){\bf u})|

\mu
-a.e. in
\{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
. Thus, we have

(6.39)

\displaystyle\int_{\left\{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla((1-\phi){\bf u})\right|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{|{\bf u}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}((2\phi-\phi^{2})|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}+2(1-\phi)\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}\left\langle{\nabla\phi},{\nabla u_{i}}\right\rangle-\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}\left|{\bf u}\right|^{2})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(\chi_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}\big)-\int_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(2\frac{C_{N}L}{r}|{\bf u}|+\frac{C^{2}_{N}}{r^{2}}|{\bf u}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\cap B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}-C_{N,L}\frac{\varepsilon}{r^{2}}\mu\big(B_{r}(x_{0})\big),

where for the first inequality we have used
(2\phi-\phi^{2})=1
on
B_{r/2}(x_{0})
,
|\nabla{\bf u}|\leqslant L
and
|\nabla\phi|\leqslant C_{N}/r
on
B_{r}(x_{0})
.

On the other hand, we denote
h=\frac{\varepsilon\phi}{|{\bf u}|}
. Then
{\bf v}=(1-h){\bf u}
on
\{|{\bf u}|>\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
, and by
[
Che99
, Corollary 2.25]
,
we have that, for
\mu
-a.e. in
\{|{\bf u}|>\varepsilon\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})
,

\begin{split}&|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2}=|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla((1-h){\bf u})|^{2}\\
=&(2h-h^{2})|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla h|^{2}|{\bf u}|^{2}+2(1-h)\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}\left\langle{\nabla h},{\nabla u_{i}}\right\rangle\\
=&(2h-h^{2})|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla h|^{2}|{\bf u}|^{2}+(1-h)\left\langle{\nabla h},{\nabla(|{\bf u}|^{2})}\right\rangle\\
=&(2h-h^{2})|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla h|^{2}|{\bf u}|^{2}+2(1-h)|{\bf u}|\left\langle{\nabla h},{\nabla|{\bf u}|}\right\rangle\\
=&(2h-h^{2})|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla(h|{\bf u}|)|^{2}+2\left\langle{\nabla|{\bf u}|},{\nabla(h|{\bf u}|)}\right\rangle+(h^{2}-2h)|\nabla|{\bf u}||^{2}\\
=&(2h-h^{2})\big(|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-|\nabla|{\bf u}||^{2}\big)-|\nabla(\varepsilon\phi)|^{2}+2\left\langle{\nabla|{\bf u}|},{\nabla(\varepsilon\phi)}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Thus, by
|\nabla{\bf u}|\geqslant|\nabla|{\bf u}||

\mu
-almost everywhere and

, we have

(6.40)

\displaystyle\int_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}(\left|\nabla{\bf u}\right|^{2}-\left|\nabla{\bf v}\right|^{2})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\cap B_{r}(x_{0})}\big(-\varepsilon^{2}\left|\nabla\phi\right|^{2}+2\varepsilon\left\langle{\nabla|{\bf u}|},{\nabla\phi}\right\rangle\big)

where we have used

and

. The inequality (
6.31

7.
Compactness and the Euler-Lagrange equation

In this section, we consider the compactness of local minimizers of
J_{Q}
living in a sequence of
pmGH
-converging
ncRCD
-spaces, under some uniformity assumptions.
Let
K\leqslant 0,N\in(1,+\infty)
and let
(X_{j},d_{j},\mu_{j})
be a sequence of
ncRCD(K,N)
metric measure spaces. Fix
p_{j}\in X_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that

(X_{j},d_{j},\mu_{j},p_{j})\overset{pmGH}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\mu_{\infty},p_{\infty}).

According to
[
DPG16
]
, the limit
(X_{\infty},d_{\infty},\mu_{\infty})
is still an
ncRCD(K,N)
metric measure space.

Fix
R>0
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Q_{j}\in C(\overline{B_{R}(p_{j})})
and let

{\bf u}_{j}:=(u_{j,1},u_{j,2},\cdots,u_{j,m})\in W^{1,2}(B_{R}(p_{j}),\mathbb{R}^{m})

be a local minimizer of
J_{Q_{j}}
on
B_{R}(p_{j})
with size
\varepsilon_{{\bf u}_{j}}>0
. That is, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a data
{\bf g}_{j}\in W^{1,2}(B_{R}(p_{j}),[0,\infty)^{m})
such that

J_{Q_{j}}({\bf u}_{j})\leqslant J_{Q_{j}}({\bf v}_{j})
for all
{\bf v}_{j}\in\mathscr{A}_{{\bf g}_{j}}
with
\dist({\bf u}_{j},{\bf v}_{j})<\varepsilon_{{\bf u}_{j}}
, where the
\mathscr{A}_{{\bf g}_{j}}
and
\dist({\bf u}_{j},{\bf v}_{j})
are given in (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), respectively.

Theorem 7.1

(Compactness)
.

Let
R,Q_{j},{\bf u}_{j}
be as above. Let
Q_{\infty}\in C(\overline{B_{R}(p_{\infty})})
such that

\lim_{j\to\infty}Q_{j}(x_{j})=Q_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\quad{\rm whenever}\ \ x_{j}\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}x_{\infty}\in B_{R}(p_{\infty}).

(
Recall that
x_{j}\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}x_{\infty}
means
\Phi_{j}(x_{j})\to\Phi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})
in
Z
, where
\Phi_{j},\Phi_{\infty}
and
Z
are given in the Definition
2.7
(3).
)
Assume that
\{{\bf u}_{j}\}
are uniformly bounded on
\overline{B_{R}(p_{j})}
,

(7.1)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\varepsilon_{{\bf u}_{j}}=+\infty,

and there exist positive constants
Q_{\min},Q_{\max}
and
L
such that

(7.2)

0<Q_{\min}\leqslant Q_{j}\leqslant Q_{\max}<\infty\ \ {\rm on}\ B_{R}(p_{j}),\ \ \forall j\in\mathbb{N},

and

(7.3)

|\nabla{\bf u}_{j}|\leqslant L\quad{\rm on}\ B_{R}(p_{j}),\ \ \forall j\in\mathbb{N}.\qquad

Then there exist a subsequence, denoted by
\{{\bf u}_{j}\}_{j}
again, and a map
{\bf u}_{\infty}\in Lip(\overline{B_{R}(p_{\infty})})
such that
{\bf u}_{j}\to{\bf u}_{\infty}
uniformly over
B_{R}(p_{j})
as
j\to\infty
and that for any
R^{\prime}\leqslant R
, the limit map
{\bf u}_{\infty}
is a minimizer of
J_{Q_{\infty}}
on
B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})
. Moreover, for any
R^{\prime}\leqslant R,
the followings hold:

(7.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}_{j}\right|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}_{\infty}\right|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\infty},

(7.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})}Q_{j}\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}_{j}\right|>0\right\}}{\rm d}\mu_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})}Q_{\infty}\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}_{\infty}\right|>0\right\}}{\rm d}\mu_{\infty},

(7.6)

\displaystyle\partial\{|{\bf u}_{j}|>0\}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})

\displaystyle\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}\partial\{|{\bf u}_{\infty}|>0\}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty}),

(7.7)

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{j}|>0\}\big)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mu_{\infty}\big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{\infty}|>0\}).

Proof.

From (
7.3
) and the Arzela-Ascoli theorem, there exist a subsequence of
\{{\bf u}_{j}\}_{j}
converging uniformly to some

{\bf u}_{\infty}\in\Lip(\overline{B_{R}(p_{\infty})})

(i) Fix any

{\bf u}_{\infty}
on

[

, Lemma 2.12]
) gives

(7.8)

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}_{j}\right|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{j}\geqslant\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})}\left|\nabla{\bf u}_{\infty}\right|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\infty}\text{.}

Since
\left\{\left|{\bf u}_{\infty}\right|>0\right\}
is an open set, it is easy to check that

x_{j}\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}x_{\infty}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\liminf_{j\to\infty}Q_{j}\chi_{\{{\bf u}_{j}>0\}}(x_{j})\ \geqslant Q_{\infty}\chi_{\{{\bf u}_{\infty}>0\}}(x_{\infty}).

By the Fatou’s lemma (see
[
DPG18
, Lemma 2.5]
or
[
ZZ19
, Appendix A]
, for the Fatou’s lemma for functions defined on varying spaces), we have

(7.9)

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})}Q_{j}\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}_{j}\right|>0\right\}}{\rm d}\mu_{j}\geqslant\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})}Q_{\infty}\chi_{\left\{\left|{\bf u}_{\infty}\right|>0\right\}}{\rm d}\mu_{\infty}\text{.}

Let
{\bf v}_{\infty}:B_{R^{\prime}}(x_{\infty})\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a minimizer of
J_{Q_{\infty}}
with
{\bf v}_{\infty}-{\bf u}_{\infty}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty}),\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
We first claim that

(7.10)

J_{Q_{\infty}}({\bf v}_{\infty})\geqslant\limsup_{j\to\infty}J_{Q_{j}}({\bf u}_{j}).

If this claim holds, by combining (
7.8
), (
7.9
), the minimality of
{\bf v}_{\infty}
and (
7.10
), then we have

\liminf_{j\to\infty}J_{Q_{j}}({\bf u}_{j})\geqslant J_{Q_{\infty}}({\bf u}_{\infty})\geqslant J_{Q_{\infty}}({\bf v}_{\infty})\geqslant\limsup_{j\to\infty}J_{Q_{j}}({\bf u}_{j})\text{.}

This yields

Thus, we conclude that

Now let us prove the claim (

Suppose not, there exists some

\{{\bf u}_{j}\}_{j}

\{{\bf u}_{j}\}_{j}

For each

.
It is clear that
\mathbf{w}_{j}

{\bf u}_{j}
in (

From the definition of

\displaystyle J_{Q_{\infty}}({\bf v}_{\infty})+\frac{\delta_{0}}{2},

where we have used (

) and
\mu_{j}\big(A_{R^{\prime}-\delta,R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\big)\to\mu_{\infty}\big(A_{R^{\prime}-\delta,R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})\big)
. This contradicts with (

{\bf u}_{\infty}
and the equalities (

), (
7.5

(ii) Next we will prove (
7.6
). On the one hand, let
x_{j}\in\partial\{|{\bf u}_{j}|>0\}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})
such that

. Fixing any

, we have
B_{r}(x_{j})\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}B_{r}(x_{\infty})
. By Theorem
6.1
, we get
\sup_{B_{r}(x_{j})}|{\bf u}_{j}|\geqslant Cr
for a constant
C>0
independent of
j
. Since
{\bf u}_{j}\to{\bf u}_{\infty}
over

uniformly, we have
|{\bf u}_{\infty}(x_{\infty})|=0
and
\sup_{B_{r}(x_{\infty})}|{\bf u}_{\infty}|\geqslant Cr
. By the arbitrariness of

, we conclude
x_{\infty}\in\partial\{|{\bf u}_{\infty}|>0\}.
On the other hand, for each
y_{\infty}\in\partial\{|{\bf u}_{\infty}|>0\}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})
. We can find a sequence
y_{j}\in B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})
such that
y_{j}\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}y_{\infty}
and
|{\bf u}_{j}(y_{j})|\to 0
. By the nondegeneracy, we get

d\big(y_{j},\partial\{|{\bf u}_{j}|>0\}\big)\to 0\quad{\rm as}\quad j\to\infty.

Therefore, there exists a sequence
\{z_{j}\}
such that
z_{j}\in\partial\{|{\bf u}_{j}|>0\}\cap B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})
and

as

. So we have
z_{j}\overset{GH}{\longrightarrow}y_{\infty}
. This proves the assertion (
7.6
).

(iii) The assertion (
7.7
) follows from Lemma
6.6
. Since all sets
B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{j}|>0\}
are open, and
\mu_{j}\rightharpoonup\mu_{\infty}
, we have

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\mu_{j}\Big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{j}|>0\}\Big)\geqslant\mu_{\infty}\Big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{\infty}|>0\}\Big).

Similarly, given any
\varepsilon>0
, the fact that all sets
B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{j}|\geqslant\varepsilon\}
are closed implies

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\mu_{j}\Big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{j}|\geqslant\varepsilon\}\Big)&\leqslant\mu_{\infty}\Big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{\infty}|\geqslant\varepsilon/2\}\Big)\\
&\leqslant\mu_{\infty}\Big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{\infty})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{\infty}|>0\}\Big).\end{split}

From Lemma
6.6
, there exists a constant
C>0
(independent of

) such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\bar{\varepsilon})
, we have

\begin{split}&\mu_{j}\Big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{j}|>0\}\Big)-\mu_{j}\Big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\cap\{|{\bf u}_{j}|\geqslant\varepsilon\}\Big)\\
&\leqslant\mu_{j}\Big(B_{R^{\prime}}(p_{j})\cap\{0<|{\bf u}_{j}|\leqslant\varepsilon\}\Big)\leqslant C\varepsilon,\qquad\forall\ j\in\mathbb{N}.\end{split}

By combining these three inequality and the arbitrariness of

We now apply Theorem
7.1
to the special case of blow-up limits, to get the Euler-Lagrange equation of local minimizers.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
{\bf u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{m})

on
\Omega

Recall that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}q^{2}_{i}(x_{0})=Q(x_{0}),\quad\mathscr{H}^{N-1}{\rm-a.e.}\ x_{0}\in\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega.

Proof.

Since
\Omega_{\bf u}=\Omega\cap\{|{\bf u}|>0\}

6.7
), we know from Proposition
2.19

2.11
)). We denote by

the ball in

(7.19)

\displaystyle\int_{B^{(j)}_{R}(x_{0})}|{\bf v}_{j}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{j}

\begin{split}{\rm d}_{B_{R}^{(j)}}({\bf v}_{j},{\bf w}_{j}):&=\left\|{\bf v}_{j}-{\bf w}_{j}\right\|_{W^{1,2}(B_{R}^{(j)},\mathbb{R}^{m})}+\left\|\chi_{\left\{|{\bf v}_{j}|>0\right\}}-\chi_{\left\{|{\bf w}_{j}|>0\right\}}\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}^{(j)})}\\

we get

\begin{split}\limsup_{j}r_{j}c_{j}{\bf\Delta}u_{i_{0}}(B_{r_{j}}(x_{0}))&\leqslant-\int_{B^{e}_{1+\delta}(0)}\left\langle{\nabla u_{0,i_{0}}},{\nabla\phi_{\infty}}\right\rangle{\rm d}\mu_{\infty}\\
&=c_{N}\cdot{\Delta}u_{0,i_{0}}(\phi_{\infty})\leqslant c_{N}\cdot{\Delta}u_{0,i_{0}}(B^{e}_{1+\delta}(0)).\end{split}

By combining with the fact

By replacing the Lipschitz cut-off

by
another

\psi_{j}(x):=\begin{cases}\min\big\{1,\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{d_{j}(x,x_{0})}{\delta}\big\},&\quad x\in B_{1}^{(j)}(x_{0})\subset X_{j}\\
0&\quad x\not\in B_{1}^{(j)}(x_{0})\subset X_{j},\end{cases}

the same argument implies that

and then

(7.29)

\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\frac{r_{j}{\bf\Delta}u_{i_{0}}(B_{r_{j}}(x_{0}))}{\mu(B_{r_{j}}(x_{0}))}\geqslant\frac{1}{\omega_{N}}{\Delta}u_{0,i_{0}}(B^{e}_{1-\delta}(0)).

By combining the two inequalities,
{\bf u}_{0,i_{0}}=\xi_{i_{0}}\cdot u
, (

&\geqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\frac{{\bf\Delta}u_{0,i_{0}}(B^{e}_{r_{j}}(0))}{\omega_{N-1}r_{j}^{N-1}}\geqslant\xi_{i_{0}}\sqrt{Q_{0}}(1-\delta)^{N-1}.\end{split}

Since

8.

Suppose that
(X,d,\mu:=\mathscr{H}^{N})

RCD(K,N)
metric measure space with some
K\leqslant 0
,
N\in(1,+\infty)
, and that
{\bf u}
is a minimizer of
J_{Q}
on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset X
and that

and satisfies (

In this section, we consider the regularity of free boundary
\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega

Let
x_{0}\in\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega
and
R>0
with
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. We have known that for almost all
s\in(0,R)
, the ball
B_{s}(x_{0})
has finite perimeter.
We define the
Weiss’ density
by

(8.1)

\begin{split}W_{{\bf u}}(x_{0},s,Q):=&\frac{1}{s^{N}}\int_{B_{s}(x_{0})}\!\!\left(\left|\nabla\mathbf{u}\right|^{2}+Q\chi_{\left\{\left|\mathbf{u}\right|>0\right\}}\right){\rm d}\mu\\
&-\frac{1}{s^{N+1}}\int_{X}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}{\rm d}|D\chi_{B_{s}(x_{0})}|,\end{split}

for almost all
s\in(0,R)
.

Lemma 8.1
.

For every
x_{0}\in\partial\{|{\bf u}|>0\}\cap\Omega

is in

provided

.

Proof.

From the Lipschitz continuity of
{\bf u}
, we know that
|\nabla{\bf u}|(x)\leqslant L
and
|{\bf u}|(x)\leqslant Lr
for all
x\in B_{r}(x_{0})
, since
{\bf u}(x_{0})=0
. By combining (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), we have

-C_{N,K}L^{2}\leqslant W_{{\bf u}}(x_{0},r,Q)\leqslant C_{N,K}\big(L^{2}+Q_{\max}\big)

provided
R\leqslant 1.
This finishes the proof.

Let us recall some properties of Weiss’ density in the special case where

W_{{\bf u}}(x_{0},r,Q)\geqslant W_{{\bf u}}(x_{0},s,Q)-C_{N}Q_{\max}\cdot\int_{s}^{r}\frac{{\rm osc}_{B_{t}(x_{0})}Q}{t}{\rm d}t

x_{0}{\rm\ is\ regular}\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \lim_{r\to 0}W_{{\bf u}}(x_{0},r,Q)<\frac{\omega_{N}}{2}Q(x_{0})(1+\epsilon_{N}).

The properties of Weiss’ density in the Euclidean case

Lemma 8.5
.

For any
\varepsilon>0

Proof.

-spaces. For the completeness we include a proof here.

Suppose that the statement is not true, then there are a sequence of

-spaces

&=\frac{1}{c^{y_{\infty}}_{1}\int_{B_{r_{j}}(0^{N})}\big(1-r_{j}^{-1}|z|\big){\rm d}\mathscr{H}^{N}(z)}=\frac{1}{r^{N}_{j}},\end{split}

we conclude that
\overline{W}_{r^{-1}_{j}{\bf u}_{\infty}}(y_{\infty},s,Q_{\infty})=\frac{c_{N}}{2}Q_{\infty}\omega_{N}
for all
s\in(0,1)
, and hence
\overline{W}_{\overline{\bf u}_{\infty}}(y_{\infty},s,Q_{\infty})=\frac{c_{N}}{2}Q_{\infty}\omega_{N}
for all
s\in(0,1)
. By integrating on
(0,1)
, it contradicts with (
8.11
), and hence, the proof is completed.
∎

The first assertion is similar to the case of Euclidean space. For the completeness, we give the details in the Appendix

By taking a subsequence of

Lemma 8.11

Let

Lemma 8.13
.

The map
{\bf u}_{00}|_{Z\times\{0\}}
is a global minimizer of
J_{Q_{0}}
on
Z
.

Proof.

We first claim that
{\bf u}_{00}(z,t)={\bf u}_{00}(z,0)
for any
z\in Z
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. It can be intuitively observed from homogeneity of
{\bf u}_{0}
on each
C_{s_{j}}
and the converging of
C_{s_{j}}\overset{pmGH}{\longrightarrow}Z\times\mathbb{R}
. Here, for clarity, we include the details for the realization of this observation as follows.

Let
\gamma:[0,+\infty)\to C(Y)
is the ray with
\gamma(0)=o_{Y}
and
\gamma(L)=x_{1}
, where
L=d_{C}(x_{1},o_{Y})>0
. On each
C_{s_{j}}
, the curve
\gamma_{j}(t):=\gamma(s_{j}\cdot t+L)\in C_{s_{j}}
is one of the shortest on every sub-interval
[a,b]\subset[-L/s_{j},+\infty)
.
We first consider the functions
f_{j}
on
C_{s_{j}}
given by

\begin{split}f_{j}(x):&=d_{j}(x_{1},o_{Y})-d_{j}(x,o_{Y})\\
&=s^{-1}_{j}\big(d_{C}(x_{1},o_{Y})-d_{C}(x,o_{Y})\big).\end{split}

Since
{\bf u}_{0}=(u_{0}^{1},u^{2}_{0},\cdots,u^{m}_{0})
is homogeneous of degree one on
C(Y)
, we have for all
s_{j}
, that

(8.18)

\left\langle{\nabla{u}^{\alpha}_{0,j}},{\nabla f_{j}}\right\rangle(x)=\left\langle{\nabla(s^{-1}_{j}u^{\alpha}_{0})},{\nabla d_{j}(x,o_{Y})}\right\rangle=0\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ C_{s_{j}},

for all
\alpha=1,2,\cdots,m.

Letting
s_{j}\to 0^{+}
, up to a subsequence, the curves
\gamma_{j}:[-L/s_{j},+\infty)\subset C_{s_{j}}
converge to a line
\gamma_{\infty}
on the limit space
C_{\infty}
. According to the splitting theorem in
[
Gig13
]
, we know that
C_{\infty}
splits isometrically to a product space
Z\times\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, letting
b
be the Busemann function with respect to the ray
\gamma_{\infty}|_{(-\infty,0]}
on
C_{\infty}
, then
Z=b^{-1}(0)
and that the gradient flow of
b
exists, denoted by
\Phi_{t}
, and furthermore
(z,t)=\Phi_{t}(z,0)
for any
z\in Z
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. On the other hand,
from the definition of
f_{j}(x)=d_{j}(x_{1},o_{Y})-d_{j}(x,o_{Y})=d_{j}\big(\gamma_{j}(0),\gamma_{j}(-L/s_{j})\big)-d_{j}\big(x,\gamma_{j}(-L/s_{j})\big)
and the fact that
f_{j}
is 1-Lipschitz on
C_{s_{j}}
, it is clear that, up to a subsequence, the functions
f_{j}
converge uniformly on each compact set to the Busemann function
b(x)
. Notice that
|\nabla f_{j}|(x)=1
a.e.
x\in C_{s_{j}}
and
|\nabla b|=1
a.e. on
C_{\infty}
. In particular, we get that
|\nabla f_{j}|\to|\nabla b|
in the
L^{2}_{\rm loc}
as
j\to\infty
. By combining this and (
7.4
), we conclude that
\left\langle{\nabla{u}^{\alpha}_{0,j}},{\nabla f_{j}}\right\rangle\to\left\langle{\nabla{u}^{\alpha}_{00}},{\nabla b}\right\rangle
in
L^{1}_{\rm loc}
, for all
\alpha=1,2,\cdots,m
. From (
8.18
), we get that

\left\langle{\nabla{u}^{\alpha}_{00}},{\nabla b}\right\rangle=0\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ C_{\infty}=Z\times\mathbb{R},\qquad\forall\alpha=1,2,\cdots,m.

This implies for almost all
z\in Z
,
{\bf u}_{00}\circ\Phi_{t}
is a constant map, where
\Phi_{t}
is the gradient flow of
b
. Recalling
\Phi_{t}(z,0)=(z,t)
for all
z\in Z
, this gives

{\bf u}_{00}(z,t)={\bf u}_{00}(z,0)
for almost all
z\in Z
. Finally, from the fact that
{\bf u}_{00}
is Lipschitz continuous, we know that
{\bf u}_{00}(z,t)={\bf u}_{00}(z,0)
for all
z\in Z
.
This claim is proved.

With the help of the fact that
{\bf u}_{00}(z,t)={\bf u}_{00}(z,0)
for any
z\in Z
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we will use the argument in
[
Wei99
]
to prove this Lemma
8.13
. Suppose that
{\bf u}_{00}
is not a minimizer of
J_{Q_{0}}
on a ball
B\subset Z
. Then there exists a map
{\bf v}\in W^{1,2}(B,\mathbb{[}0,+\infty)^{m})
such that
{\bf v}-{\bf u}_{00}|_{Z\times\{0\}}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and

\int_{B}(|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{|{\bf v}|>0}){\rm d}\mu_{Z}\leqslant\int_{B}(|\nabla{\bf u}_{00}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{|{\bf u}_{00}|>0}){\rm d}\mu_{Z}-\epsilon_{0}

for some
\epsilon_{0}>0.
We define a map
{\bf v}_{T}
on
B\times(-T,T)
, for any
T>1
, by

{\bf v}_{T}(z,t):=\begin{cases}{\bf v}(z),&|t|\leqslant T-1\\
(T-|t|){\bf v}(z)+(|t|-T+1){\bf u}_{00}(z,0),&T-1\leqslant|t|\leqslant T\\
{\bf u}_{00}(z,0),&|t|\geqslant T.\end{cases}

It is clear that
{\bf v}_{T}-{\bf u}_{00}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(B\times(-T,T),[0,+\infty)^{m})
, by using the fact
{\bf u}_{00}(z,t)={\bf u}_{00}(z,0)
for all
z\in Z
. Note that

\begin{split}&\int_{B\times(-T,T)}(|\nabla{\bf v}_{T}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{|{\bf v}_{T}|>0}){\rm d}\mu_{Z}{\rm d}t-\int_{B\times(-T,T)}(|\nabla{\bf u}_{00}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{|{\bf u}_{00}|>0}){\rm d}\mu_{Z}{\rm d}t\\
\leqslant&2(T-1)\ \Big(\int_{B}(|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{|{\bf v}|>0}){\rm d}\mu_{Z}-\int_{B}(|\nabla{\bf u}_{00}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{|{\bf u}_{00}|>0}){\rm d}\mu_{Z}\Big)\\
&\ \ +\int_{B\times\big((-T,-T+1)\cap(T-1,T)\big)}\Big(|\nabla{\bf v}_{T}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{|{\bf v}_{T}|>0})\Big){\rm d}\mu_{Z}{\rm d}t\\
\leqslant&-2(T-1)\epsilon_{0}+4\int_{B}(|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2}+|{\bf v}|^{2}+|\nabla{\bf u}_{00}|^{2}+|{\bf u}_{00}|^{2}){\rm d}\mu_{Z}+2Q_{0}\mu_{Z}(B),\end{split}

which contradicts the fact that
{\bf u}_{00}
is a minimizer on
B\times(-T,T)
when
T
is large enough. Therefore, the Lemma
8.13
is proved.
∎

We now come back to the proof of Theorem
8.12
. From the assumption
\partial X\cap\Omega=\emptyset
, we know that both
C(Y)
and
Z\times\mathbb{R}
have no boundary. Thus,
Z
has no boundary.

If
N-1\geqslant 3
, by the combination of the above Lemma

, (
8.17
) and the fact that
{\bf u}_{00}(z,t)={\bf u}_{00}(z,0)
for all

, we obtain
that there exists an

-dimensional

-space without boundary,

, and a minimizer of

on

,

, such that

Iterating this procedure we conclude that there exists a

ncRCD(0,3)
-space without boundary, denoted by
\bar{X}

\mathscr{H}^{\eta}\left(\mathcal{S}^{\Omega_{\bar{\bf u}}}_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{1}(\bar{x})\right)>0.

We claim that the singular set of

must contain only isolated points. Suppose that a sequence

Appendix A
Weiss-type monotonicity on cones

A Weiss-type monotonicity for minimizers
{\bf u}
of
J_{Q}
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
has been obtained in
[
CSY18
]
and
[
MTV17
]
. The same argument can be extended to the case where
{\bf u}
is defined on a metric measure cone. We will provide the details as follows.

Let
N\geqslant 2
and let
(C(\Sigma),d_{C},\mu_{C})
be a metric measure cone, with the vertex
p
, over
(\Sigma,d_{\Sigma},\mu_{\Sigma})
, and assume that
(C(\Sigma),d_{C},\mu_{C})
satisfies
RCD(0,N)
.

Lemma A.1
.

For
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, it holds

(A.1)

\int_{C(\Sigma)}g{\rm d}|D\chi_{B_{r}(p)}|=r^{N-1}\int_{\Sigma}g_{r}{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma},

for any Borel function
g(t,\xi)
on
C(\Sigma)
, where
g_{r}(\xi):=g(r,\xi)
.

Proof.

For
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the set
B_{r}(p)
has finite perimeter and that, by coarea formula,

\int_{s}^{t}\int_{C(\Sigma)}g{\rm d}|D\chi_{B_{r}(p)}|{\rm d}r=\int_{B_{t}(p)\backslash B_{s}(p)}g{\rm d}\mu_{C}=\int_{[s,t)\times\Sigma}gr^{N-1}{\rm d}r{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}

for all
0\leqslant s<t<\infty
and all Borel function
g
, where we have used
d\mu_{C}(t,\xi)=t^{N-1}dt\times d\mu_{\Sigma}(x)
. It follows that the function
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\int_{C(\Sigma)}g{\rm d}|D\chi_{B_{r}(p)}|
is absolutely continuous, and then the desired assertion (
A.1
) holds. ∎

Let
Q_{0}>0
be a constant and let
\mathbf{u}=\left(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\right)
be a global minimizer of
J_{Q_{0}}
on
(C(\Sigma),d_{C},\mu_{C})
. I.e., for each
R>0
,
{\bf u}
is minimizer of
J_{Q_{0}}
on
B_{R}(p).
For any
r\in(0,+\infty)
, we denote by
{\bf u}_{r}(\xi)={\bf u}(r,\xi),\forall\xi\in\Sigma.

Lemma A.2
.

For each
r\in(0,\infty)
, we have

(A.2)

\begin{split}\frac{N}{r^{N-2}}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\left(|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{\{|{\bf u}|>0\}}\right)\leqslant&\int_{\Sigma}\Big(|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}+|{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}\Big){\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}\\
&\ +r^{2}\int_{\Sigma}\Big(Q_{0}\chi_{\{|{\bf u}_{r}|>0\}}\Big){\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma},\end{split}

where
\nabla_{\Sigma}v
is the weak upper gradient of
v\in W^{1,2}(\Sigma).

Proof.

Fix any
r\in(0,\infty)
. We set the function
{\bf v}:=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{m}):B_{r}(p)\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
by

v_{i}(t,\xi):=\frac{t}{r}u_{r,i}(\xi)=\frac{t}{r}u_{i}(r,\xi),\quad\ \forall\ t\in(0,r),\ \xi\in\Sigma,\quad\forall\ i\in\{1,2,\cdots,m\}.

Then we first have
{\bf v}\in Lip(B_{r}(p))
and
{\bf v}={\bf u}
on
\partial B_{r}(p)
. By the minimizer of
{\bf u}
, we have

(A.3)

\int_{B_{r}(p)}\left(|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{\{|{\bf u}|>0\}}\right)\leqslant\int_{B_{r}(p)}\left(|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{\{|{\bf v}|>0\}}\right).

Following Proposition 3.4 of
[
Ket15
]
,
we know that for any
v\in W^{1,2}(B_{r}(p))
, it holds, for almost all
(t,x)\in C(\Sigma)
,

|\nabla v|^{2}(t,x)=|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}v_{\xi}|^{2}(t)+t^{-2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}v_{t}|^{2}(\xi),

where
v_{t}(\cdot):=v(t,\cdot)
and
v_{\xi}(\cdot):=v(\cdot,\xi)
. By applying this to each component of
{\bf v}
, we get

(A.4)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(p)}|\nabla{\bf v}|^{2}&=\int_{B_{r}(p)}\Big(\frac{1}{t^{2}}|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\bf v}_{t}|^{2}(\xi)+|\nabla_{t}{\bf v}_{\xi}|^{2}(t)\Big){\rm d}\mu_{C}\\
&=\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\Sigma}\Big(\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}(\xi)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}(\xi)\Big)t^{N-1}{\rm d}t{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}\\
&=\frac{r^{N-2}}{N}\int_{\Sigma}\Big(|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}(\xi)+|{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}(\xi)\Big){\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}.\end{split}

Noticing that
|{\bf v}|(t,\xi)>0\Longleftrightarrow|{\bf u}_{r}|(\xi)>0,
we have

(A.5)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{r}(p)}Q_{0}\chi_{\{|{\bf v}|>0\}}&=\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\Sigma}\Big(Q_{0}\chi_{\{|{\bf v}|>0\}}\Big)t^{N-1}{\rm d}t{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}\\
&=\frac{r^{N}}{N}\int_{\Sigma}\Big(Q_{0}\chi_{\{|{\bf u}_{r}|>0\}}\Big){\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}.\end{split}

Now the desired estimate (
A.2
) follows from the combination of (
A.3
)-(
A.5
), and the proof is finished.
∎

Now we give the monotonicity of
W_{{\bf u}}(p,r,Q_{0})
.

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose the cone
C(\Sigma)
is non-collapsed. Then the function
r\mapsto W_{{\bf u}}(p,r,Q_{0})
is non-decreasing. Moreover, if
W_{{\bf u}}(p,r,Q_{0})
is a constant then
{\bf u}
is homogeneous of degree one.

Proof.

Since
{\bf u}
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
C(\Sigma)
, it is clear that
W_{{\bf u}}(p,r,Q_{0})
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
(0,\infty)
, and then it is differentiable at
\mathscr{L}^{1}
-a.e.
r\in(0,\infty)
. At a such
r
, we have

(A.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}W_{{\bf u}}(p,r,Q_{0})=

\displaystyle-\frac{N}{r^{N+1}}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\left(|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{\{|\mathbf{u}|>0\}}\right){\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{r^{N}}\int_{C(\Sigma)}\left(|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+Q_{0}\chi_{\{\mathbf{u}|>0\}}\right){\rm d}|D\chi_{B_{r}(p)}|

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{r^{3}}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathbf{u}_{r}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\int_{\Sigma}|{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}{\bf u}_{\xi}|^{2}(r){\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}+\frac{1}{r^{3}}\int_{\Sigma}|{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\int_{\Sigma}|{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma},

where we have used (
A.2
), (
A.1
) and

|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}(r,x)=|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}{\bf u}_{\xi}|^{2}(r)+r^{-2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}(\xi)

(by Proposition 3.4 of
[
Ket15
]
). Since
C(\Sigma)
is assumed to be non-collapsed, we know that
{\bf u}
is locally Lipschitz on
C(\Sigma)
. Thus, we get

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}\int_{\Sigma}|{\bf u}_{r}|^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\int_{\Sigma}u^{2}_{i}(r,\xi){\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Sigma}2u_{i}(r,\xi)\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial r}(r,\xi){\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}.

Putting this into (
A.6
), we get

(A.7)

\displaystyle r\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}W_{{\bf u}}(p,r,Q_{0})

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Sigma}\bigg(|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}u_{i,\xi}|^{2}+\frac{u^{2}_{i,r}}{r^{2}}-2\frac{u_{i,r}}{r}|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}u_{i,\xi}|\bigg){\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Sigma}\bigg(|\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}u_{i,\xi}|-\frac{u_{i,r}}{r}\bigg)^{2}{\rm d}\mu_{\Sigma}\geqslant 0,

where
u_{i,\xi}(\cdot):=u_{i}(\cdot,\xi)
and
u_{i,r}(\cdot):=u_{i}(r,\cdot).
It follows that
W_{{\bf u}}(p,r,Q_{0})
is non-decreasing. Moreover, if
W_{{\bf u}}(p,r,Q_{0})
is a constant, then one have

\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial r}(r,\xi)=\frac{u_{i,r}(\xi)}{r}=\frac{u_{i}(r,\xi)}{r}

for almost all
(r,\xi)
in
C(\Sigma)
. This implies for almost
\xi\in\Sigma
that
u_{i,\xi}(r)=ru_{i,\xi}(1).
Therefore, in this case
{\bf u}
is homogeneous of degree one. The proof is finished.
∎

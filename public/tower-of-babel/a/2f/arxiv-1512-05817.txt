Hierarchies of Manakov–Santini Type by Means of Rota–Baxter and Other Identities
Błażej M. SZABLIKOWSKI

Abstract
The Lax–Sato approach to the hierarchies of Manakov–Santini type is formalized in order to extend it to a more general class of integrable systems. For this purpose some linear operators are introduced, which must satisfy some integrability conditions, one of them is the Rota–Baxter identity. The theory is illustrated by means of the algebra of Laurent series, the related hierarchies are classified and examples, also new, of Manakov–Santini type systems are constructed, including those that are related to the dispersionless modified Kadomtsev–Petviashvili equation and so called dispersionless r r -th systems.

2
Generalized hierarchy

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a commutative associative unital algebra
1

1

We assume, for simplicity, that all structures in this work are defined over the field of complex numbers.
. We define the generalized Lax hierarchy of evolution equations by

\displaystyle\Psi_{t_{n}}=A_{n}\Psi_{x}-B_{n}\partial\Psi,\qquad\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}L\\
M\end{pmatrix},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

(2.1)

where
L,M\in\mathbb{A}
are Lax and Orlov operators (functions), respectively. The independent variables are evolution
parameters (times)
t_{n}
and the spatial variable
x
. We assume that
\partial
is some (auxiliary) derivation in the algebra
\mathbb{A}
invariant with respect to all
t_{n}
and
x
. The hierarchy is generated by the functions

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\mathcal{P}\bigl(J^{-1}\partial X_{n}\bigr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{n}:=\mathcal{R}\bigl(J^{-1}(X_{n})_{x}\bigr),\qquad X_{n}:=L^{n},

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{R}
are some linear maps
\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
and

\displaystyle J:=\{L,M\}\equiv\partial LM_{x}-L_{x}\partial M.

(2.3)

We assume that the endomorphisms
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{R}
are invariant with respect to times
t_{n}
and the spatial variable
x
, that is
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{R}
commute with derivatives related to
t_{n}
and
x
.

In all the following proofs we will skip most of straightforward computations, however we will exhibit all the crucial intermediate steps.

Proposition 2.1
.

The evolution equations from the hierarchy (
2.1
) pairwise commute
if the following pair of zero-curvature type equations is satisfied by the generating functions
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
:

\displaystyle(A_{n})_{t_{m}}-(A_{m})_{t_{n}}+\langle A_{n},A_{m}\rangle_{x}+B_{m}\partial A_{n}-B_{n}\partial A_{m}=0

(2.4a)

and

\displaystyle(B_{n})_{t_{m}}-(B_{m})_{t_{n}}+A_{n}(B_{m})_{x}-A_{m}(B_{n})_{x}-\langle B_{n},B_{m}\rangle_{\partial}=0,

(2.4b)

where

\displaystyle\langle a,b\rangle_{x}:=ab_{x}-ba_{x},\qquad\langle a,b\rangle_{\partial}:=a\partial b-b\partial a.

Proof 2.2
.

The commuting of the respective flows means that
(\Psi_{t_{n}})_{t_{m}}=(\Psi_{t_{m}})_{t_{n}}
.
Comparing the coefficients of both sides with respect to the independent variables
\Psi_{x}

and
\partial\Psi
we obtain the required pair of zero-curvature conditions.

Theorem 2.3
.

The following set of conditions on the endomorphisms
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{R}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(a)b+a\mathcal{P}(b)\bigr)-\mathcal{P}(a)\mathcal{P}(b)=\kappa_{1}\mathcal{P}(ab),

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(a)\partial b+a\partial\mathcal{P}(b)\bigr)-\mathcal{R}(a)\partial\mathcal{P}(b)=\kappa_{1}\mathcal{P}(a\partial b),

(2.5b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(a)b+a\mathcal{R}(b)\bigr)-\mathcal{P}(a)\mathcal{R}(b)=\kappa_{2}\mathcal{R}(ab),

(2.5c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(a)\partial b+a\partial\mathcal{R}(b)\bigr)-\mathcal{R}(a)\partial\mathcal{R}(b)=\kappa_{2}\mathcal{R}(a\partial b),

(2.5d)

where
a,b\in\mathbb{A}
and
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, is sufficient for the zero-curvature equations (
2.4
) to be identically fulfilled
by the generating functions
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
. This means that the above equations are sufficient conditions
for mutual commutation of the flows from the hierarchy (
2.1
).

Proof 2.4
.

Let’s define

\displaystyle a_{n}:=J^{-1}\partial X_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad b_{n}:=J^{-1}(X_{n})_{x},

so that
A_{n}=\mathcal{P}(a_{n})
and
B_{n}=\mathcal{R}(b_{n})
. Using (
2.1
) one finds that

\displaystyle(X_{n})_{t_{m}}=A_{m}(X_{n})_{x}-B_{m}\partial X_{n}

and

\displaystyle J_{t_{n}}=(A_{n}J)_{x}-\partial(B_{n}J)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\big(J^{-1}\big)_{t_{n}}=\langle A_{n},J^{-1}\rangle_{x}-\langle B_{n},J^{-1}\rangle_{\partial}.

Hence,

\displaystyle(a_{n})_{t_{m}}=\langle A_{m},a_{n}\rangle_{x}+b_{n}\partial A_{m}-B_{m}\partial a_{n},\qquad(b_{n})_{t_{m}}=A_{m}(b_{n})_{x}-a_{n}(B_{m})_{x}-\langle B_{m},b_{n}\rangle_{\partial}.

Now substituting
(A_{n})_{t_{m}}\equiv\mathcal{R}((a_{n})_{t_{m}})
and
(B_{n})_{t_{m}}\equiv\mathcal{R}((a_{n})_{t_{m}})

into the pair of zero-curvature equations (
2.4
) and taking advantage of the relations (
2.5
) one finds that

\displaystyle(A_{n})_{t_{m}}+A_{n}(A_{m})_{x}+B_{m}\partial A_{n}-n\leftrightarrow m

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\mathcal{P}\bigl(A_{m}(a_{n})_{x}+a_{m}(A_{n})_{x}\bigr)-A_{m}(A_{n})_{x}-\mathcal{P}\bigl(B_{m}\partial a_{n}+b_{m}\partial A_{n}\bigr)+B_{m}\partial A_{n}-n\leftrightarrow m

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\kappa_{1}\mathcal{P}\bigl(a_{m}(a_{n})_{x}-a_{n}(a_{m})_{x}-b_{m}\partial a_{n}+b_{n}\partial a_{m}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\kappa_{1}\mathcal{P}\bigl(J^{-1}\partial(J^{-1}\{X_{m},X_{n}\})\bigr)=0

and

\displaystyle(B_{n})_{t_{m}}-A_{m}(B_{n})_{x}-B_{n}\partial B_{m}-n\leftrightarrow m

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\mathcal{R}\bigl(A_{m}(b_{n})_{x}+a_{m}(B_{n})_{x}\bigr)-A_{m}(B_{n})_{x}-\mathcal{R}\bigl(B_{m}\partial b_{n}+b_{m}\partial B_{n}\bigr)+B_{m}\partial B_{n}-n\leftrightarrow m

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\kappa_{2}\mathcal{R}\bigl(a_{m}(b_{n})_{x}-a_{n}(b_{m})_{x}-b_{m}\partial b_{n}+b_{n}\partial b_{m}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\kappa_{2}\mathcal{R}\bigl(J^{-1}(J^{-1}\{X_{m},X_{n}\})_{x}\bigr)=0.

In the above
n\leftrightarrow m
stands for all the remaining terms arising from the permutation of the indices
m

and
n
in the preceding terms. Since
\{X_{m},X_{n}\}=0
we see that the zero-curvature equations (
2.4
) are indeed satisfied identically.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that the endomorphisms
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{R}
satisfy the following relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\partial a)=\partial\mathcal{R}(a)

(2.6)

for arbitrary
a\in\mathbb{A}
. Then, under the constraint

\displaystyle J\equiv\{L,M\}=L^{s},

(2.7)

where
s
is some fixed integer, the generalized hierarchy (
2.1
) reduces to the standard hierarchy
of the form

\displaystyle L_{t_{n}}=\frac{n}{n-s}\{\mathcal{R}(L^{n-s}),L\},

(2.8)

where the Poisson bracket is defined through the formula (
2.3
), that is
\{,\}:=\partial\wedge\partial_{x}
.

On the other hand, if we assume that
L
is invariant with respect to times
t_{n}
and the variable
x
, that is

L_{t_{n}}=L_{x}=0
, then the hierarchy reduces to

\displaystyle M_{t_{n}}=n\mathcal{P}\bigl(M_{x}^{-1}L^{n-1}\bigr)M_{x}.

(2.9)

Proof 2.6
.

Taking advantage of the constraint (
2.7
) and the relation (
2.6
) we have

\displaystyle A_{n}=\mathcal{P}\bigl(J^{-1}\partial X_{n}\bigr)=\frac{n}{n-r}\mathcal{P}\bigl(\partial L^{n-r}\bigr)=\frac{n}{n-r}\partial\mathcal{R}(L^{n-r})

and

\displaystyle B_{n}=\mathcal{R}\bigl(J^{-1}(X_{n})_{x}\bigr)=\frac{n}{n-r}\mathcal{R}\bigl((L^{n-r})_{x}\bigr)=\frac{n}{n-r}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(L^{n-r})\bigr)_{x}.

The last relation follows from the assumption that
\mathcal{R}
commutes with
\partial_{x}
.
As result the hierarchy (
2.1
) reduces into

\displaystyle\Psi_{t_{n}}=\frac{n}{n-r}\bigl[\partial\mathcal{R}(L^{n-r})\Psi_{x}-\bigl(\mathcal{R}(L^{n-r})\bigr)_{x}\partial\Psi\bigr]\equiv\frac{n}{n-r}\{\mathcal{R}(L^{n-r}),\Psi\},

where
\Psi=(L,M)^{\rm T}
. The first equation on
L
coincides with (
2.8
). The second equation
gives evolution of
M
consistent with the constraint (
2.7
).

If
L_{t_{n}}=L_{x}=0
, then
B_{n}=0
and
J=\partial LM_{x}
. Hence

\displaystyle A_{n}=\mathcal{P}\bigl(J^{-1}\partial X_{n}\bigr)=n\mathcal{P}\bigl(M_{x}^{-1}L^{n-1}\bigr).

In this case, the first equation in (
2.1
) for
\Psi=L
is satisfied identically and the second equation
for
\Psi=M
takes the form (
2.9
).

3
Rota–Baxter and other identities

Consider some algebra
(\mathbb{A},\cdot)
. The Rota–Baxter identity
[
1
,
26
,
27
]
for some linear operator

\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\mathcal{P}(b))-\mathcal{P}(a)\cdot\mathcal{P}(b)=\kappa\mathcal{P}(a\cdot b),

(3.1)

where
a,b,c\in\mathbb{A}
and
\kappa
is some fixed scalar weight. Alternatively we can write the identity as

\displaystyle\ell(\ell(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\ell(b))-\ell(a)\cdot\ell(b)=\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{2}a\cdot b,

where
\ell:=\mathcal{P}-\frac{1}{2}\kappa
. For an operator
\mathcal{P}
satisfying (
3.1
) there is always associated operator

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}:=\kappa-\mathcal{P}
, which satisfies the identity (
3.1
) for the same weight
\kappa
.

There is a source of simple solutions to the Rota–Baxter identity (
3.1
). Assume that the algebra
\mathbb{A}

can be decomposed into direct sum of subalgebras, that is

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{+}\oplus\mathbb{A}_{-},\qquad\mathbb{A}_{\pm}\cdot\mathbb{A}_{\pm}\subset\mathbb{A}_{\pm},\qquad\mathbb{A}_{+}\cap\mathbb{A}_{-}=\{0\}.

Then, the projections
P_{+}
and
P_{-}
on the subalgebras
\mathbb{A}_{+}
and
\mathbb{A}_{-}
satisfy the identity (
3.1
)
for the weight
\kappa=1
. Notice that
P_{+}+P_{-}=1
.

The main feature of the Rota–Baxter identity is that in the case of associative algebra
\mathbb{A}
the identity (
3.1
) is a sufficient condition for associativity of another multiplication in
\mathbb{A}
given by

\displaystyle a*_{\mathcal{P}}b:=\mathcal{P}(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\mathcal{P}(b)-\kappa a\cdot b\equiv\ell(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\ell(b).

For more information on the Rota–Baxter algebras see
[
12
,
13
]
.

The special case of the Rota–Baxter identity (
3.1
), for a Lie algebra
(\mathbb{A},[\cdot,\cdot])
, is the modified Yang–Baxter equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl([\mathcal{R}(a),b]+[a,\mathcal{R}(b)]\bigr)-[\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b)]=\kappa\mathcal{R}\bigl([a,b]\bigr).

(3.2)

The equation (
3.2
) is a sufficient condition for the bracket

\displaystyle[a,b]_{\mathcal{R}}:=[\mathcal{R}(a),b]+[a,\mathcal{R}(b)]-\kappa[a,b]

to define second Lie bracket in
\mathbb{A}
, in this case the linear map
R
is called the classical
r
-matrix. The more standard convention is to consider the endomorphism
r:=\mathcal{R}-\frac{1}{2}\kappa
instead of
\mathcal{R}
. The classical
r
-matrix formalism
[
28
,
29
]
is known to be very useful in the construction of very broad classes of integrable systems, see also the survey
[
5
]
and references therein.

Let’s now consider commutative and associative algebra
(\mathbb{A},\cdot)
. We define Poisson bracket in
\mathbb{A}

be means of two commuting derivations
\partial,\partial_{x}\in\Der\mathbb{A}
:

\displaystyle\{a,b\}:=\partial a\cdot\partial_{x}b-\partial_{x}a\cdot\partial b.

(3.3)

Here, the derivation
\partial_{x}
is a counterpart of the derivative with respect to a spatial variable
x
and the derivation
\partial

is a counterpart of the derivative with respect to some auxiliary variable.
The following identity on the endomorphism
\mathcal{R}
turns out to be important:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(a)\partial b\bigr)+\mathcal{R}\bigl(a\partial\mathcal{R}(b)\bigr)-\mathcal{R}(a)\partial\mathcal{R}(b)=\kappa\mathcal{R}(a\partial b),

(3.4)

where
\kappa
is some constant. Analogically as before,
R^{\prime}=\kappa-R
solves (
3.4
) for the same weight
\kappa
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let us assume that the endomorphism
\mathcal{R}
commutes with the derivation
\partial_{x}
, that is
\mathcal{R}\partial_{x}=\partial_{x}\mathcal{R}
.
Then, the identity (
3.4
) is a sufficient condition for
\mathcal{R}
to satisfy the modified Yang–Baxter equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl(\{\mathcal{R}(a),b\}+\{a,\mathcal{R}(b)\}\bigr)-\{\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b)\}=\kappa\mathcal{R}\bigl(\{a,b\}\bigr),

(3.5)

and so to be a classical
r
-matrix for
\mathcal{R}
with respect to the Poisson bracket (
3.3
). This means that

\displaystyle\{a,b\}_{\mathcal{R}}:=\{\mathcal{R}(a),b\}+\{a,\mathcal{R}(b)\}-\kappa\{a,b\}

is a Lie bracket when the relation (
3.4
) holds.

The proof is straightforward expanding the formula (
3.4
) by means of (
3.3
). In fact, when
\mathcal{R}

also commutes with the derivation
\partial
, the Rota–Baxter identity (
3.1
) is sufficient for
\mathcal{R}
to solve the equation (
3.4
). However, in general (
3.1
) is more restrictive than (
3.4
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that, on a commutative associative algebra
(\mathbb{A},\cdot)
, there exists non-degenerate bilinear
product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathbb{A}}\colon\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, such that the Frobenius condition holds:

\displaystyle(a\cdot b,c)_{\mathbb{A}}=(a,b\cdot c)_{\mathbb{A}}

and the product is invariant with respect to the derivation
\partial\in\Der\mathbb{A}
:

\displaystyle(\partial a,b)_{\mathbb{A}}+(a,\partial b)_{\mathbb{A}}=0.

Then, the Rota–Baxter identity (
3.1
) is equivalent to the ‘dual’ relation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(a)b+a\mathcal{R}(b)\bigr)-\mathcal{P}(a)\mathcal{R}(b)=\kappa\mathcal{R}(ab),\qquad\mathcal{R}:=\kappa-\mathcal{P}^{*},

where
(\mathcal{P}^{*}a,b)_{\mathbb{A}}:=(a,\mathcal{P}b)_{\mathbb{A}}
.

Let additionally assume that the following relation is valid:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\partial a)=\partial\mathcal{R}(a).

(3.6)

Then, from the Rota–Baxter identity (
3.1
) we obtain the relation (
3.4
)
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\bigl(\mathcal{R}(a)\partial b+a\partial\mathcal{P}(b)\bigr)-\mathcal{R}(a)\partial\mathcal{P}(b)=\kappa\mathcal{P}(a\partial b).

For proof see Proposition 3.3 and Lemma 5.3 in
[
31
]

4
Application to the algebra of Laurent series

Consider the algebra of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{A}:=\mathbb{C}[p,p^{-1}]
, it is commutative and associative.
When necessary the algebra
\mathbb{A}
can be extended to the algebra of formal Laurent series at
\infty
:
\mathbb{A}^{\infty}:=\mathbb{C}(\!(p^{-1})\!)
or the algebra of formal Laurent series at
0
:
\mathbb{A}^{0}:=\mathbb{C}(\!(p)\!)
.

Consider
decomposition of
\mathbb{A}
in the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{\geqslant l}\oplus\mathbb{A}_{<l},\qquad A_{\geqslant l}:=p^{l}\mathbb{C}[p],\qquad A_{<l}:=p^{l-1}\mathbb{C}\big[p^{-1}\big].

The related projections are defined by

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i}a_{i}p^{i}\right]_{\geqslant l}:=\sum_{i\geqslant l}a_{i}p^{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left[\sum_{i}a_{i}p^{i}\right]_{<l}:=\sum_{i<l}a_{i}p^{i}.

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=[\cdot]_{\geqslant l}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\mathcal{P}=[\cdot]_{<l}\quad\text{if}\quad l=0,1.

(4.1)

Remember that
[\cdot]_{\geqslant l}+[\cdot]_{<l}=1

We will look now for solutions of the identity (

\displaystyle\partial:=p^{r}\partial_{p},\qquad r\in\mathbb{Z}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=[\cdot]_{\geqslant k-r}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\mathcal{R}=[\cdot]_{<k-r}

\displaystyle\{,\}_{r}:=p^{r}\partial_{p}\wedge\partial_{x}.

The solutions (

\displaystyle p^{\prime}=p^{-1}\qquad\text{with}\quad l^{\prime}=1-l,\quad k^{\prime}=3-k,\quad r^{\prime}=2-r.

(4.6)

Proof 4.2
.

The first two points are consequence of straightforward verification. To show the third point consider the solutions
(
4.4a
) and let
L\in\mathbb{A}^{\infty}
. Then, the generating functions (
2.2
) are

\displaystyle A_{n}=\left[\frac{p^{r}(L^{n})_{p}}{\{L,M\}_{r}}\right]_{\geqslant l}=\left[\frac{(L^{n})_{p}}{\{L,M\}_{0}}\right]_{\geqslant l}

and

\displaystyle B_{n}=\left[\frac{(L^{n})_{x}}{\{L,M\}_{r}}\right]_{\geqslant k-r}=p^{-r}\left[\frac{(L^{n})_{x}}{\{L,M\}_{0}}\right]_{\geqslant k},

where the Poisson bracket is defined by (

). Hence, the related hierarchy (
2.1
) takes the form (
4.5a
),
since the factors
p^{-r}
and
p^{r}
cancel out. For the solutions (
4.4b
) and
\tilde{L}\in\mathbb{A}^{0}
the reasoning is similar. The last point follows from simple verification.

For fixed parameters
k,r
and
l
we can consider the Lax functions
L
and
\tilde{L}
to be analytic extensions
of some function outside and inside, respectively, unit circle on the complex plane. Thus
L
and
\tilde{L}

are defined near
\infty
and

As result we can consider two families of related hierarchies together and postulate
\Psi=(L,M,\tilde{L},\tilde{M})^{\rm T}

in (
4.5
). This is, inter alia, consequence of the fact that the solutions (
4.4a
) and (
4.4b
) are mutually associated through the decomposition of unity:

\displaystyle 1=[\cdot]_{\geqslant l}+[\cdot]_{<l}=[\cdot]_{\geqslant k-r}+[\cdot]_{<k-r}.

The compatibility equations (
2.4
) for the hierarchies (
4.5
) takes the following, independent of
r
, form:

\displaystyle\bigl(A_{n}^{\lambda}\bigr)_{t_{m}^{\mu}}-\bigl(A_{m}^{\mu}\bigr)_{t_{n}^{\lambda}}+\bigl\langle A_{n}^{\lambda},A_{m}^{\mu}\bigr\rangle_{x}+B_{m}^{\mu}\bigl(A_{n}^{\lambda}\bigr)_{p}-B_{n}^{\lambda}\bigl(A_{m}^{\mu}\bigr)_{p}=0

(4.7a)

and

\displaystyle\bigl(B_{n}^{\lambda}\bigr)_{t_{m}^{\mu}}-\bigl(B_{m}^{\mu}\bigr)_{t_{n}^{\lambda}}+A_{n}^{\lambda}\bigl(B_{m}^{\mu}\bigr)_{x}-A_{m}^{\mu}\bigl(B_{n}^{\lambda}\bigr)_{x}-\bigl\langle B_{n}^{\lambda},B_{m}^{\mu}\bigr\rangle_{p}=0,

(4.7b)

where
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
;
\mu,\lambda=\infty,0
and
\langle f,g\rangle_{\xi}:=fg_{\xi}-gf_{\xi}
for
\xi=x,p
. The related generating functions are defined as

\displaystyle A_{n}^{\infty}:=\left[\frac{(L^{n})_{p}}{\{L,M\}_{0}}\right]_{\geqslant l},\qquad B_{n}^{\infty}:=\left[\frac{(L^{n})_{x}}{\{L,M\}_{0}}\right]_{\geqslant k},

and

\displaystyle A_{n}^{0}:=\left[\frac{(\tilde{L}^{n})_{p}}{\{\tilde{L},\tilde{M}\}_{0}}\right]_{<l},\qquad B_{n}^{0}:=\left[\frac{(\tilde{L}^{n})_{x}}{\{\tilde{L},\tilde{M}\}_{0}}\right]_{<k}.

Notice that if we treat

)

\displaystyle\{L,M\}_{r}=L^{r}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\{\tilde{L},\tilde{M}\}_{r}=\tilde{L}^{2-r}.

When Leibniz algebras are Nijenhuis? Thanks: * Corresponding author
Haiying Li, Tianshui Ma *, Shuanhong Wang

Abstract
. Leibniz algebras can be seen as a “non-commutative” analogue of Lie algebras. Nijenhuis operators on Leibniz algebras introduced by Cariñena, Grabowski, and Marmo in [J. Phys. A: Math. Gen. 37(2004)] are (1, 1)-tensors with vanishing Nijenhuis torsion. Recently triangular Leibniz bialgebras were introduced by Tang and Sheng in [J. Noncommut. Geom. 16(2022)] via the twisting theory of twilled Leibniz algebras. In this paper we find that Leibniz algebras are very closely related to Nijenhuis operators, and prove that a triangular symplectic Leibniz bialgebra together with a dual triangular structure must possess Nijenhuis operators, which makes it possible to study the applications of Nijehhuis operators from the perspective of Leibniz algebras. At the same time, we regain the classical Leibniz Yang-Baxter equation by using the tensor form of classical r r -matrics. At last we give the classification of triangular Leibniz bialgebras of low dimensions.

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

A (left)
Leibniz algebra
is a pair
(\mathfrak{L},[,])
consisting of a vector space
\mathfrak{L}
and a bilinear map
[,]:\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathfrak{L}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{L}
(write
[,](x\otimes y)=[x,y]
) such that for all

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]].

(1)

This notion was introduced by Bloh in
[
B
]
under the name D-algebras and rediscovered under the name Leibniz algebras by Loday (see
[
L
,
LP
]
), which can be seen as a “non-commutative” analogue of Lie algebras.

For a given algebra, a coalgebra structure can be obtained by a dual method. It is interesting and important to give a bialgebra structure on it by some compatibility conditions between the multiplication and the comultiplication, such as Lie bialgebras (
[
Dr
,
Maj
]
), Hopf algebras (
[
Maj
]
), infinitesimal Hopf algebras (
[
Ag99
]
), associative D-bialgebras (or balanced infinitesimal bialgebras, antisymmetric infinitesimal bialgebras) (
[

,
Ag00b
,
Bai1
]
), Leibniz bialgebras (
[
TS
]
), dendriform-Nijenhuis bialgebras (
[

,

]
), etc. The bialgebraic structures for Rota-Baxter associative algebras were investigated by the first named author and Liu in
[

]
and by Bai, Guo and the first named author in
[

]
. Recently, triangular Leibniz bialgebras were introduced by Tang and Sheng in
[

]
by using the twisting theory of twilled Leibniz algebras. The authors also presented the equivalence between matched pairs of Leibniz algebras, Manin triples of Leibniz algebras, and Leibniz bialgebras.

A Leibniz algebra together with a Nijenhuis operator is called a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra, which is introduced by Cariñena, Grabowski and Marmo in
[
CGM1
]
. Concretely, a
Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{L},[,],N)
consisting of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{L},[,])
and a Nijenhuis operator
N
, i.e., a linear map
N:\mathfrak{L}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{L}
satisfies the equality below

\displaystyle[N(x),N(y)]+N^{2}([x,y])=N([N(x),y])+N([x,N(y)]),\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathfrak{L}.

Nijenhuis operators are very useful in the deformation theory of algebras, bialgebras theory, integrable systems, almost complex structures, Nijenhuis geometry, preHamiltonian and Hamiltonian operators, etc (see
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
).

In this paper, we find a new phenomenon that Leibniz algebras are closely related to Nijenhuis operators, and prove that a triangular symplectic Leibniz bialgebra together with a dual triangular structure must be a Nijenhuis Leibniz algebra (see Theorem
3.10

to be a Leibniz bialgebra, where the coproduct
\delta_{r}
is defined by Eq.(

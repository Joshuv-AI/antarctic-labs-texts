Preliminaries

An algebraic structure
(E,+,\circ)
is said to be a

(1)

Notice that the identity element

For a left skew brace

\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+(a\circ b)

for all

We have the following result for skew braces .

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be a left skew brace, then for each

\lambda_{a}
is an automorphism of
(E,+)
and the map
\lambda:(E,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(E,+)
given by

is a group homomorphism.

A sub skew brace

is said to be a

of

\lambda_{a}(y)\in I
for all

and

is said to be an

if

is a normal subgroup of

. The Socle of a skew brace

is defined as
\operatorname{Soc}(E)=\operatorname{Ker}\lambda

\cap

\operatorname{Z}(E,+)
, where

represents the centre of the group

and the annihilator of

The following is an easy but important observation, which will be used several times in what follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be a left skew brace. Then for all

, the following hold:

(i)
a+b=a\circ\lambda^{-1}_{a}(b)
.

(ii)
a\circ b=a+\lambda_{a}(b)

Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{Z}_{N}^{1}(H,I)=\Bigg\{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{is a map from}\hskip 2.84544ptH\hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{to}\hskip 2.84544ptI\hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{such that }\hskip 2.84544pt\theta(h_{1}\circ h_{2})=\sigma_{h_{2}}(\theta(h_{1})+\theta(h_{2})\hskip 2.84544pt\\
\mbox{and}\hskip 2.84544pt\nu_{h_{1}+h_{2}}(\theta(h_{1}+h_{2}))=\mu_{h_{2}}(\nu_{h_{1}}(\theta(h_{1}))+\nu_{h_{2}}(\theta(h_{2}))\end{subarray}\Bigg\},

the set
\operatorname{Z}_{N}^{1}(H,I)
is called as the set of derivations, and

\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I):=\operatorname{Z}_{N}^{2}(H,I)/\operatorname{B}_{N}^{2}(H,I)

is the second cohomology group of
H
by
I
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
H
be a skew brace and let
I
be an abelian group equiped with trivial brace structure. Let
\mathcal{E}:=0\to I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}H\to 0
be an extension. Then the coupling and action are same, and there is a bijection between
\operatorname{Ext}_{(\nu,\mu,\sigma)}(H,I)
and
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,I)
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
[\mathcal{E}]\in\operatorname{Ext}_{\alpha}(H,I)
and
(\chi,\beta,\tau)
be an associated triplet of
\mathcal{E}
. Then for
[(\beta_{1},\tau_{1})]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,\operatorname{Ann}(I))
, the operation

[(\beta_{1},\tau_{1})][\mathcal{E}(\chi,\beta,\tau)]=[\mathcal{E}(\chi,\beta_{1}+\beta,\tau_{1}+\tau)]

defines a free action of the group
\operatorname{H}^{2}_{N}(H,\operatorname{Ann}(I))
on the set
\operatorname{Ext}_{\alpha}(H,I)
. If
I
is trivial skew brace, then this action becomes transtivite.

Proof.

It is easy to check that the action under consideration is well defined. Let
[\mathcal{E}(\chi,\beta,\tau)]\in Ext_{\alpha}(H,I)
and
[(\beta_{1},\tau_{1})]\in\operatorname{H}_{N}^{2}(H,\operatorname{Ann}(I))
be such that
[(\beta_{1},\tau_{1})][\mathcal{E}(\chi,\beta,\tau)]=[\mathcal{E}(\chi,\beta,\tau)]
. Then

[(\chi,\beta_{1}+\beta,\tau_{1}+\tau)]=[(\chi,\beta,\tau)],

and therefore there exist a map
\theta:H\rightarrow I
such that
\theta(0)=0
and
\bar{\chi}\approx\bar{\chi}
by
\theta
, which implies that
\theta(h)\in\operatorname{Ann}(I)
for all
h\in H
, and

-\nu_{h_{1}+h_{2}}(\theta(h_{1}+h_{2}))+\beta(h_{1},h_{2})+\mu_{h_{2}}(\nu_{h_{1}}(\theta(h_{1})))+\nu_{h_{2}}(\theta(h_{2}))=\beta(h_{1},h_{2})+\beta_{1}(h_{1},h_{2})

and

\theta(h_{1}\circ h_{2})^{-1}\circ\tau(h_{1},h_{2})\circ\sigma_{h_{2}}(\theta(h_{1}))\circ\theta(h_{2})=\tau(h_{1},h_{2})+\tau_{1}(h_{1},h_{2})

for all
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\in H
(using the fact that
\theta(h)\in\operatorname{Ann}(I)
for all
h\in H
).

We have

\displaystyle\beta_{1}(h_{1},h_{2})

\displaystyle=\nu_{h_{1}+h_{2}}(-\theta(h_{1}+h_{2}))+\mu_{h_{2}}((\nu_{h_{1}}(\theta(h_{1})))+\nu_{h_{2}}(\theta(h_{2})),

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(h_{1},h_{2})

\displaystyle=-\theta(h_{1}\circ h_{2})+\sigma_{h_{2}}\theta(h_{1}))+\theta(h_{2}).

Thus
[(\tau_{1},\beta_{1})]=1
, and hence the action is free.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be two elements in
Ext_{\alpha}(H,I)
. Then for
i=1,2
,
[\mathcal{E}_{i}]=[\mathcal{E}_{i}(\chi_{i},\beta_{i},\tau_{i})]
for some associated trilpet
(\chi_{i},\beta_{i},\tau_{i})
, where
\chi_{i}=(\prescript{}{i}{\nu},\prescript{}{i}{\mu},\prescript{}{i}{\sigma})
. By Theorem
6.2
, we can construct
(\beta^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
such that
(\chi_{2},\beta^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
is an associated triplet with

[\mathcal{E}_{1}(\chi_{1},\beta_{1},\tau_{1})]=[\mathcal{E}_{1}(\chi_{2},\beta^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})].

We set

\beta_{3}(h_{1},h_{2})=\beta^{\prime}(h_{1},h_{2})-\beta_{2}(h_{1},h_{2})

and

\tau_{3}(h_{1},h_{2})=\tau^{\prime}(h_{1},h_{2})\circ\tau_{2}(h_{1},h_{2})^{-1}.

Now
(\beta^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
and
(\beta_{2},\tau_{2})
are
2
-cocycles with the same action
\chi_{2}
. Then from (
9
), (
10
) and (
11
) it follows that
\beta_{3}(h_{1},h_{2})\in Z(I,+)
and
\tau_{3}(h_{1},h_{2})\in Soc(I)
for all
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
. If we take
I
to be trivial skew brace, then
\operatorname{Z}(I,+)=\operatorname{Soc}(I)=\operatorname{Ann}(I)
. Finally we get
\beta_{3},\tau_{3}:H\rightarrow Ann(I)
. It is easy to see that
(\beta_{3},\tau_{3})
is
2-cocycle
with respect to the action
\chi_{2}
and
(\beta_{3},\tau_{3})[\mathcal{E}(\chi_{2},\beta_{2},\tau_{2})]=[\mathcal{E}(\chi_{2},\beta^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})]=[\mathcal{E}(\chi_{1},\beta_{1},\tau_{1})]
. Hence the action is transtive, which completes the proof.

\Box

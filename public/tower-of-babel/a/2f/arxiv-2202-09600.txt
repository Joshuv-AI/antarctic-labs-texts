Equivariant vector bundles on varieties with codimension-one orbits
Lucas Mason-Brown and James Tao

Abstract
. Let G G be an algebraic group and let X X be a smooth G G -variety with two orbits: an open orbit and a a closed orbit of codimension 1 1 . We give an algebraic description of the category of G G -equivariant vector bundles on X X under a mild technical hypothesis. We deduce simpler classifications in the special cases of line bundles and vector bundles which are generically local systems. We apply our results to the study of admissible representations of semisimple Lie groups. Our main result gives a new set of constraints on the associated cycles of unipotent representations.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Summary

Work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero. Consider a variety
X
acted on by an algebraic group
G
. Make the following assumptions:

•

X
is smooth.

•

X
consists of one open
G
-orbit and one closed
G
-orbit of codimension one.

Then our main result (Theorem
3.7.2
) gives an algebraic description of the category of
G
-equivariant vector bundles on
X
, provided that
X
satisfies a technical hypothesis called ‘fastenedness’ (Definition
2.2.1
).

In the rest of the paper, we deduce simpler classifications in the special cases of line bundles (Proposition
4.1.1
) and vector bundles which are generically
G
-equivariant local systems (Corollary
4.3.1
). We also use the main result to describe
G
-equivariant vector bundles on
weakly normal

G
-varieties obtained by gluing together those of the form described above (Section
5
). As an application, we study the coherent sheaves which arise as associated graded modules of
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules, when the pair
(\mathfrak{g},K)
arises from a real reductive group (Section

).

In the rest of this introduction, we discuss Theorem
3.7.2
in the larger context of ‘gluing of categories,’ and we describe some of these applications in more detail.

1.2.
The idea of gluing

Let
X
be as the first paragraph of
1.1
, and write
X=U\cup Z
where
U
is the open
G
-orbit and
Z
is the closed
G
-orbit of codimension one, equipped with the reduced subscheme structure. Upon choosing basepoints
z\in Z
and
u\in U
, it is easy to describe the categories of
G
-equivariant vector bundles on
Z
and
U
separately:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vect}^{G}(Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Rep}(G^{z})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vect}^{G}(U)

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{Rep}(G^{u}),

where
G^{z}
and
G^{u}
are the stabilizer groups. It is natural to ask whether
\mathrm{Vect}^{G}(X)
can be constructed by ‘gluing’ the two categories displayed above.

Motivation for this idea comes from Beilinson’s description
[
7
]
of perverse (constructible) sheaves on a scheme
X
which is decomposed as
U\cup Z
where
Z=V(f)
for some regular function
f\in\mathscr{O}_{X}
. That description says that a perverse sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
can be encoded as a quadruple
(\mathscr{F}_{U},\mathscr{F}_{Z},u,v)
such that

•

\mathscr{F}_{U}
and
\mathscr{F}_{Z}
are perverse sheaves on
U
and
Z
, respectively.

•

u
and
v
are maps

\Psi_{f}(\mathscr{F}_{U})\xrightarrow{u}\mathscr{F}_{Z}\xrightarrow{v}\Psi_{f}(\mathscr{F}_{U})

where
\Psi_{f}(-)
is the nearby cycles functor. (We have ignored the Tate twist.) The composition
u\circ v
must equal
1-(\text{monodromy endomorphism})
.

This description has the good feature that it involves only the categories of interest on
U
and
Z
, along with a functor between them (and an endomorphism of this functor).

For vector bundles, the situation is not as nice. The standard approaches to gluing quasicoherent sheaves (e.g. recollement of categories) require knowing about sheaves on
X_{\widehat{Z}}
(the formal completion of
X
along
Z
). For example, Beauville–Laszlo gluing
[
6
]
implies that a
G
-equivariant vector bundle on
X
can be encoded as a triple
(\mathscr{F}_{U},\mathscr{F}_{\widehat{Z}},\alpha)
where

•

\mathscr{F}_{U}
and
\mathscr{F}_{\widehat{Z}}
are
G
-equivariant vector bundles on
U
and
X_{\widehat{Z}}
, respectively.

•

\alpha
is an isomorphism between their restrictions to
X_{\widehat{Z}}\smallsetminus Z
.

Since
X_{\widehat{Z}}
is not easy to compute with in practice, we sought a different description of
\mathrm{Vect}^{G}(X)
which does not involve
X_{\widehat{Z}}
.

In some special cases, knowledge about
X_{\widehat{Z}}
can be gleaned from looking at the action of
G
on the normal bundle
\mathscr{N}_{Z/X}
. For instance, if
G
is reductive and
X
is affine, then the Luna Slice Theorem provides a one-dimensional
G^{z}
-invariant locally closed subvariety
\ell\subset X
which contains
z
and is transverse to
Z
, together with a
G^{z}
-equivariant étale map
\ell\to\mathscr{N}_{Z/X}|_{z}
. Combined with the previous paragraph, this essentially reduces the problem to studying
G
-equivariant vector bundles on
\mathscr{N}_{Z/X}
. However, in our desired application
X
is usually not affine, so this reduction does not apply.

In our desired application, something weaker is true (Proposition
6.3.7
): there is a line
\ell\subset X
which intersects
Z
transversely, is invariant under a one-parameter subgroup
\gamma:\mathbb{G}_{m}\hookrightarrow G^{z}
, and is acted upon nontrivially by
\gamma(\mathbb{G}_{m})
. The existence of such a pair
(\ell,\gamma)
is equivalent to the ‘fastenedness’ hypothesis of Theorem
3.7.2
.

Starting from a choice of
(\ell,\gamma)
as above, we arrive at a description of
\mathrm{Vect}^{G}(X)
which bears little resemblance to ‘gluing.’ Instead, it should be thought of in the following terms. If we pull back
\mathscr{F}\in\mathrm{Vect}^{G}(X)
along
\ell\hookrightarrow X
, two algebraic structures emerge:

•

\mathscr{F}|_{\ell}
is equivariant with respect to a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action which comes from the subgroup
\gamma
. Recall that the category of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant vector bundles on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces equipped with exhausting filtrations, via the Artin–Rees construction (Proposition
3.1.1
).

•

Each fiber of
\mathscr{F}|_{\ell}
is acted on by the stabilizer group of the corresponding point in
\ell
.

The classifying data defined in Theorem
3.7.2
are a combination of these two bullet points.

However, in the case of line bundles, it is possible to reformulate the main result in terms which more closely resemble ‘gluing via nearby cycles’ see Lemma
5.3.2
. The possibility of doing so reflects that equivariant quasicoherent sheaves are more ‘rigid’ than arbitrary quasicoherent sheaves.

1.3.
Applications

Suppose
G_{\mathbb{R}}
is a real reductive group and
K_{\mathbb{R}}\subset G_{\mathbb{R}}
is a maximal compact subgroup. Let
K
be the complexification of
K_{\mathbb{R}}
and let
\mathcal{N}
be the cone of nilpotent elements in the complexified Lie algebra of
G_{\mathbb{R}}

The determination of the irreducible unitary representations of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
is one of the major unsolved problem in representation theory. There is evidence to suggest that every such representation can be constructed through a sequence of well-understood operations from a finite set of building blocks, called the
unipotent representations
. These representations are ‘attached’ (in a certain mysterious sense) to the nilpotent orbits of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
on the dual space of its Lie algebra.

Attached to every irreducible admissible representation
V
of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
(for example, every irreducible unitary representation) is a Harish-Chandra module
M
, which has the structure of an irreducible
K
-equivariant
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Taking its associated graded gives a well-defined class
[\operatorname{gr}(M)]
in the Grothendieck group of
K
-equivariant coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{N}
. This class provides valuable information about
V
, including, for example, its
K_{\mathbb{R}}
-types. Determining the classes
[\operatorname{gr}(M)]
associated to unipotent representations is a fundamental problem with origins in the seminal paper (
[
15
]
) of Vogan.

In Section
6
, we define the
K
-chain
\overline{\mathrm{Ch}}(M)
associated to
M
. This is the variety with
K
-action obtained by first deleting the orbits of codimension
\geq 2
from the reduced support of
\operatorname{gr}(M)
and then passing to the projectivization (see
6.2
for details). The class
[\operatorname{gr}(M)]
gives rise to a class
[\overline{\operatorname{gr}}(M)]
on
\overline{\mathrm{Ch}}(M)
, and when
V
is unipotent, we conjecture that
[\overline{\mathrm{gr}}(M)]
determines
[\operatorname{gr}(M)]
. Our main result in Section
6
is that
\overline{\mathrm{Ch}}(M)
is fastened (Proposition
6.3.7
). To prove this remarkable fact, we use in an essential way the special structure of
\mathcal{N}
(e.g. the existence of ‘real’ Slodowy slices with nice contracting
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-actions). Since
\overline{\mathrm{Ch}}(M)
is fastened, we can understand
\overline{\mathrm{gr}}(M)
(and by extension
\operatorname{gr}(M)
and
V
) using Theorem
3.7.2
(and its corollaries).

3.
Equivariant sheaves on smooth simple chains

3.1.
Overview

In this section,
X=U\cup Z
is a smooth
G
-chain with one open and one closed orbit. Assume that
X
is fastened, and let
(\gamma,\ell)
be a fastening datum (Definition
2.3.1
).

Given this datum, we will describe the category
\operatorname{QCoh}^{G}_{\mathrm{tf}}(X)
of
G
-equivariant torsion-free quasicoherent sheaves on
X
in algebraic terms (Theorem
3.7.2
). The main idea is that the line
\ell:\mathbb{A}^{1}\to X
functions as a ‘basepoint’ which sees both the open and the closed orbit of
X
. More precisely, we will explain how
X
is related to the ‘homogeneous space’ given by
G\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
modulo the stabilizer scheme of
\ell(\mathbb{A}^{1})
, see Lemma
3.3.1
.

The special case when
G\curvearrowright X
is the standard (weight one) action
\mathbb{G}_{m}\curvearrowright\mathbb{A}^{1}
will feature prominently in our analysis. Here, the desired classification is elementary and well-known:

Proposition 3.1.1 (Artin–Rees).

Let
\mathrm{Vect}^{\mathrm{filt}}
be the category of vector spaces equipped with exhausting (but not necessarily separating)
\mathbb{Z}
-filtrations. There is a natural equivalence of monoidal categories:

\operatorname{QCoh}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{\mathrm{tf}}(\mathbb{A}^{1})\simeq\mathrm{Vect}^{\mathrm{filt}}

Proof.

The category
\operatorname{QCoh}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathbb{A}^{1})
is equivalent (via global sections) to the category of graded modules over the algebra
k[x]
, where the generator
x
is placed in degree 1. Each such module is encoded by a diagram

\cdots\to V_{i}\to V_{i+1}\to\cdots

of vector spaces, where the maps are arbitrary. The module is torsion-free if and only if the maps are all injective. In this case, the diagram of vector spaces is equivalent to the datum of a single vector space
V=\colim_{i}V_{i}
equipped with the exhausting filtration
F
defined by
F^{\geq i}V=V_{i}
. The reverse direction is clear.
∎

We will often refer to this equivalence as the Artin–Rees construction. Since filtrations already appear here, it is unsurprising that they also appear in the statement of the main classification result (Theorem
3.7.2
).

Remark 3.1.2.

We now explain how we propose to get ‘algebraic’ descriptions of categories of equivariant sheaves. The appearance of the word ‘monoidal’ in the proposition implies that we get analogous equivalences involving algebro-geometric objects defined over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
. For example, considering algebra objects on both sides yields the equivalence

\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{aff},\mathrm{tf}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}\simeq(\text{filtered rings})^{\mathrm{op}}

where the left hand side is the category of affine schemes over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
for which the structure map
S\to\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
is flat.

This equivalence intertwines the Cartesian monoidal structure on the left hand side with the
\otimes
monoidal structure on the right hand side. Passing to group objects with respect to these monoidal structures, we obtain a (contravariant) equivalence between affine group schemes flat over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
and filtered Hopf algebras. This list of equivalences continues indefinitely, but essentially we will only need one more observation: given such a group scheme

corresponding to a filtered Hopf algebra

, the category of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant torsion-free quasicoherent sheaves on the stack

is equivalent to the category of filtered comodules for

. This is applied in Definition

.

Lemma 6.3.5.

Let
X
be a
K
-variety which consists of an open orbit
U
and a closed orbit
Z
. Let
Y\hookrightarrow X
be a locally closed subscheme with the following properties:

•

The codimension of
Y\cap Z\subset Z
equals the codimension of
Y\subset X
.

•

The scheme-theoretic intersections
Y\cap Z
and
Y\cap U
are smooth.

•

Y
is Cohen–Macaulay.

Then the base change of the normalization map of
X
along
Y\hookrightarrow X
is the normalization map of
Y
.

Proof.

We first show that the
K
-saturation map
K\times Y\xrightarrow{a}X
is smooth.

(1)

The fiber over a closed point
u\in U
is given by the Cartesian product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a^{-1}(u)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\cap U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k\mapsto k^{-1}\cdot u}

The right vertical map is smooth (since the fibers are isomorphic to the stabilizer group
K^{u}
, which is smooth because we are working in characteristic zero), and

is smooth, so

is smooth as well.

(2)

The fiber over a closed point
z\in U
is given by the Cartesian product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a^{-1}(z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\cap Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k\mapsto k^{-1}\cdot z}

By the same argument as in (1), we know that

is smooth.

(3)

The first bullet point and the Cartesian squares in (1) and (2) imply that
a^{-1}(u)
and
a^{-1}(z)
have the same dimensions, for any

and
z
. Because
Y
is Cohen–Macaulay and

is smooth, the Miracle Flatness Theorem implies that

is flat.

(4)

The fibers of
a
are smooth by (1) and (2). Because

is also flat (by (3)) and finitely presented, it follows that
a
is smooth.

Since normalization is preserved by smooth base change, the two squares in the following diagram are Cartesian:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(K\times Y)^{\sim}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K\times Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1_{K}\times\operatorname{id}_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}

where the vertical maps are normalization maps, and the upper horizontal maps arise via functoriality of normalization. (To see that the first square is Cartesian, apply the smooth base change result to the map

and use that

∎

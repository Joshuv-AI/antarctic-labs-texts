Pinned simplices and connections to product of sets on paraboloids
Alex Iosevich, Minh-Quy Pham, Thang Pham, Chun-Yen Shen

Abstract
In this paper we obtain improved dimensional thresholds for dot product sets corresponding to compact subsets of a paraboloid. As a direct application of these estimates, we obtain significant improvements to the best known dimensional thresholds that guarantee that a given compact subset of Euclidean space determines a positive proportion of all possible congruence classes of simplexes. In many regimes this improves the results previously obtained by Erdogan-Hart-Iosevich ( [ 7 ] ), Greenleaf-Iosevich-Liu-Palsson ( [ 13 ] ) and others.



1
Introduction

In a very recent paper, a connection between isosceles dot-product triangles in a subset of a paraboloid and isosceles distance triangles in a subset of lower dimensional vector space over finite fields has been discovered by Chang, Mohammadi, Pham, and Shen in
[
2
]
. More precisely, assume that we have three points
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d},a_{d+1}),b=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{d},b_{d+1}),c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d},c_{d+1})
on the paraboloid defined by
x_{d+1}=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d}^{2}
, the equation

a\cdot b=a\cdot c,

can be written as

\left|\frac{-\overline{a}}{2|\overline{a}|^{2}}-\overline{b}\right|=\left|\frac{-\overline{a}}{2|\overline{a}|^{2}}-\overline{c}\right|,

(1)

where
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
for any
x
on the paraboloid, and
|\overline{x}|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d}^{2}
.

This tells us that if
a\cdot b=a\cdot c
, then we have an isosceles triangle with the apex
\frac{-\overline{a}}{2|\overline{a}|}
and the base with two vertices
\overline{b}
and
\overline{c}
in the vector space of one dimension lower. As a consequence of this identity, they are able to provide a link between the Erdős-Falconer distance problem and the problem of products of sets in paraboloids. As a consequence, several improvements of well-known results in the literature have been obtained.

The main purpose of this paper is to study applications of the connection described above in the continuous (Euclidean) setting. More precisely, we will focus on the distribution of dot-product values determined by sets on the truncated paraboloid and the structures of pinned simplices in arbitrary sets, where the truncated paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by

P_{d}:=\{(\overline{x},|\overline{x}|^{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\overline{x}|\leq 1\},

where
|\overline{x}|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{d-1}^{2}
.

1.1
Products of sets on paraboloids

Before stating our main results, let us recall the following result due to Erdoğan, Hart, and Iosevich in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be the unit sphere centered at the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
E\subset\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
satisfy

\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d}{2}
. Assume that
\mu_{E}
is a Frostman measure on
E
. Then

\mathcal{L}^{1}(\{x\cdot y\colon x,y\in E\})=\mathcal{L}^{1}(\{|x-y|=\sqrt{(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x_{d}-y_{d})^{2}}\colon x\in E\})>0

for almost
\mu
-every
y\in E
.

It is natural to expect the same dimensional threshold
\frac{d}{2}
to hold for other varieties, for instance, paraboloids or cones. In the following results, using the above connection and recent developments on the Falconer distance problem, we are able to obtain improvements for paraboloids in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x\in E
, we define the product set and its pinned analog formed by
E
at
x
, respectively, by

\displaystyle\Pi(E):=E\cdot E=\{x\cdot y:x,y\in E\},~\Pi_{x}^{1}(E):=\{x\cdot y:y\in E\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set on
P_{d}
,
d\geq 3
. Assume that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d-1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{8d-12},

then there exist
x\in E
and a measure
\nu_{x}
supported on
\Pi_{x}^{1}(E)
such that
||\nu_{x}||_{L^{2}}<\infty
. In particular,
\Pi_{x}^{1}(E)
has positive Lebesgue measure.

In three dimensions, we are able to obtain a better dimensional threshold.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a compact set on
P_{3}
. Assume that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{5}{4},

then there exists
x\in E
such that
\Pi_{x}^{1}(E)
has positive Lebesgue measure.

It is worth noting that, in general, one can not go lower than
\frac{d-1}{2}
. A construction will be provided in Section
6
. If we want to say something about the Hausdorff dimension of the pinned product sets or the property of having a non-empty interior, the next theorem provides some dimensional thresholds in this direction.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set on
P_{d}
,
d\geq 3
.

(i)

If
\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d^{2}-2d+d\beta}{2(d-1)}
for any
\beta>0
, then there exists
x\in E
such that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\Pi_{x}^{1}(E))\geq\beta.

(ii)

If
\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d^{2}}{2(d-1)}
, then there exists
x\in E
such that

\displaystyle\textrm{Int}(\Pi_{x}^{1}(E))\neq\emptyset,

where
\textrm{Int}(A)
denotes the interior of
A
.

1.2
Pinned simplex problem

Definition 1.5
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
1\leq k\leq d
. Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the set of distinct congruent
k
-simplices with vertices in
E
to be

\displaystyle T_{k}(E):=E^{k+1}/\sim,

where
(x^{1},\dots,x^{k+1})\sim(y^{1},\dots,y^{k+1})
if and only if there exist an element
g
of the orthogonal group
O(d)
and a translation
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle y^{j}=gx^{j}+z,\quad\forall\,1\leq j\leq k+1.

Note that we may view
T_{k}(E)
as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{\binom{k+1}{2}}
via the map

\displaystyle[(x^{1},\dots,x^{k+1})]\mapsto\big(|x^{i}-x^{j}|\big)_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}.

The question of finding the smallest dimensional threshold
\alpha
such that the set
T_{k}(E)
has positive Lebesgue measure whenever
\dim_{H}(E)>\alpha
has received much attention during the two recent decades. When
k=1
, this is the Falconer distance problem, and the best current dimensional thresholds are due to Guth, Iosevich, Ou, and Wang
[
14
]
in two dimensions with
\frac{5}{4}
, Du, Iosevich, Ou, Wang and Zhang
[
5
]
in higher even dimensions with
\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{4}
, and Du and Zhang
[
4
]
in odd dimensions with
\frac{d}{2}+\frac{d}{4d-2}
.

When
k>1
, we have three following results, where the first is due to Erdoğan, Hart, and Iosevich
[
7
]
, the second is due to Grafakos, Greenleaf, Iosevich and Palsson in
[
11
]
, and the third is due to Greenleaf, Iosevich, Liu, and Palsson in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 1.6

(pinned simplex,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. Assume that

\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d+k+1}{2},

then there exists
x\in E
such that the set of
k
-simplices pinned at
x
has positive Lebesgue measure.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. Assume that

\dim_{H}(E)>d-\frac{d-1}{2k},

then
\mathcal{L}^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(T_{k}(E))>0
.

Theorem 1.8

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. Assume that

\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{dk+1}{k+1},

then
\mathcal{L}^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(T_{k}(E))>0
.

In this paper, we are able to establish the following improvement in which the ideas described above play a crucial role.

Theorem 1.9

(pinned simplex)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set. Assume that

\dim_{H}(E)>\frac{d+k}{2},

then there exists
x\in E
such that the set of
k
-simplices pinned at
x
has positive Lebesgue measure.

Remark 1.1
.

For comparison, the exponent
\frac{d+k}{2}
in Theorem
1.9
is clearly smaller than the exponent
\frac{d+k+1}{2}
obtained in
[
7
]
, is better than exponent
d-\frac{d-1}{2k}
in
[
11
]
for
k<d-1
, and is lower than exponent
\frac{dk+1}{k+1}
in
[
13
]
when
k<d-2
. For the remaining cases that
k\geq d-2
, the exponent in
[
13
]
is still the best known.

Remark 1.2
.

It is not difficult to see that for the
k
-simplex problem, the dimensional exponent
k-1
cannot be exceeded since the underlying set
E
may be contained in a copy of
{\mathbb{R}}^{k-1}
where a non-degenerate
k
-simplex cannot be constructed.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some notation and results about Frostman measures,
L^{2}
spherical averages, and pinned distance set which are need for our proofs in later sections.

2.1
Frostman measures and spherical averages

Lemma 2.1

(Frostman’s lemma, Theorem 2.7,
[
20
]
)
.

Let
0\leq s\leq n
. For a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
s-
dimensional Hausdorff measure of
E
is positive if and only if there exists a probability measure
\mu
on
E
such that

\displaystyle\mu(B(x,r))\lesssim r^{s},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\,r>0.

In particular, Frostman’s lemma implies that given any exponent
s_{\mu}<\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(E)
, there exists a probability measure
\mu
on
E
such that

\displaystyle\mu(B(x,r))\lesssim r^{s_{\mu}},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r>0.

(2)

A measure satisfying (
2
) is often called an
s_{\mu}
-Frostman measure.

Given a probability measure
\mu
with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, we define the
L^{2}
spherical averages of Fourier transform of
\mu
by

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}|\widehat{\mu}(r\omega)|^{2}d\omega,

where
\omega
is the surface measure on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
. In
[
19
]
, Mattila developed a machinery to study Falconer’s distance problem, by reducing the orginial problem to the study of the decay rates of spherical averages of fractal measures, namely, the supremum of the numbers
\beta
for which:

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}|\widehat{\mu}(r\omega)|^{2}d\omega\lesssim r^{-\beta},\quad r>1.

(3)

It is proved that for any
s
-Frostman measure
\mu
, (
3
) holds with

\beta(s):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}s,&s\in\big(0,\frac{n-1}{2}\big],&(\text{Mattila \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Mattila87}{}{}]}})\\
\frac{n-1}{2},&s\in\big[\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n}{2}\big],&(\text{Mattila \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Mattila87}{}{}]}})\\
\frac{n+2s-2}{4},&s\in\big[\frac{n}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}\big],&(\text{Wolff \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Wolff}{}{}]} \text{ and } Erdo\u{g}an \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Erdogan05}{}{}]}})\end{array}\right..

Recently, the estimate (
3
) was improved by Du–Guth–
Ou–Wang–Wilson–Zhang
[
3
]
when
n\geq 3
and Du-Zhang
[
4
]
when
n\geq 4
, namely,

\displaystyle\beta(s)=\frac{(n-1)s}{n},\quad s\in\bigg[\frac{n}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}\bigg].

When
s
is large, see Lucà and Rogers
[
18
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

There are certain arithmetic condition that limit the exponents one can obtain for spherical averages. See, for example,
[
16
]
and
[
1
]
and the references contained therein.

2.2
Pinned distance set problem

Given
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the pinned distance set of
E
at
x
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}(E)=\{|x-y|:y\in E\}.

The pinned distance set problem is a more restrictive version of the Falconer problem, which asks for the smallest dimensional threshold of a given compact set
E
such that there exists
x\in E
with
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\Delta_{x}(E))>0
. This first investigation was made by Peres and Schlag in
[
22
]
. In a recent work
[
17
]
, Liu proved the following result for pinned distance set problem, using an
L^{2}
identity and the estimates of the Fourier decay (
3
) above.

Proposition 2.2

(Theorem 1.4,
[
17
]
)
.

Suppose
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathcal{L}^{1}(\Delta_{x}(E))=0\})\leq\inf\{s:\dim_{H}(E)+\beta(s)>n\}.

In particular, if
\dim_{H}(E)+\beta(\dim_{H}(E))>n
, there exists
x\in E
such that
\Delta_{x}(E)
has positive Lebesgue measure.

In two dimensions, we recall the following result due to Guth, Iosevich, Ou and Wang
[
14
]
, which is the current best known dimensional threshold of the Falconer problem. The following variant is taken from
[
21
]
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a pair of compact sets with positive
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure for some
s>\frac{5}{4}
. Further, suppose that there exist Frostman probability measures
\mu_{E}
and
\mu_{F}
on
E
and
F
respectively, with exponent
s
. Then

\displaystyle\mu_{F}(y\in F:\mathcal{L}^{1}(\Delta_{y}(E))>0)>0.

We also recall the following result, which is proved in
[
15
]
by Iosevich and Liu.

Proposition 2.4

(Theorem 1.3,
[
15
]
)
.

Let
E,F\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be compact sets. Then there exists a probability measure
\mu_{F}
on
F
such that for
\mu_{F}-
a.e.
x\in F
,

(i)

\dim_{H}(\Delta_{x}(E))\geq\beta
if
\dim_{H}(E)+\frac{n-1}{n+1}\dim_{H}(F)>n-1+\beta,

(ii)

\mathcal{L}^{1}(\Delta_{x}(E))>0
if
\dim_{H}(E)+\frac{n-1}{n+1}\dim_{H}(F)>n
,

(iii)

\textrm{Int}(\Delta_{x}(E))\neq\emptyset
if
\dim_{H}(E)+\frac{n-1}{n+1}\dim_{H}(F)>n+1
.

3
Connection between product of sets on paraboloids and simplex problem

The main goal of this section is to set up the problem and outline a connection between the pinned dot-product and pinned simplex problems.

Assume that
E
and
F
are compact sets on
P_{n}
, with
n\geq 3
. By pigeon-holing, we may assume that

\displaystyle|x|,\,|y|\geq c>0,\quad\forall\,x\in E,y\in F,

for some constant
c>0
. Denote
P_{n}^{\ast}=P_{n}\setminus\{0\}
. We consider the following maps

\displaystyle H_{1}:P_{n}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\to(\mathbb{R}^{\ast})^{n-1},\quad x\mapsto H_{1}(x)=\overline{x},

\displaystyle H_{2}:P_{n}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\to(\mathbb{R}^{\ast})^{n-1},\quad y\mapsto H_{2}(y)=\frac{-\overline{y}}{2|\overline{y}|^{2}},

where for each point
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. The images of
E
and
F
via
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, will be denoted by
E^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}
, respectively, i.e.

\displaystyle E^{\prime}:=H_{1}(E)\quad\text{ and }\quad F^{\prime}:=H_{2}(F).

(4)

Let’s fix some point
y\in F
and put
y^{\prime}=H_{2}(y)
. Then using a similar argument as in the introduction, one can check that for all
x\in E
,

\displaystyle x\cdot y=t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad|y^{\prime}-\overline{x}|=|y^{\prime}|\sqrt{1+4t},

(5)

where
\overline{x}=H_{1}(x)
. Geometrically, the projection of all points
x
satisfying the left hand side to one lower dimensional space is on a sphere centered at
y^{\prime}
of radius
|y^{\prime}|\sqrt{1+4t}
. As we mentioned in the introduction, this geometric observation plays a vital role in our work.

Motivated by this, let us consider more general case. Assume that
k
is an integer such that
1\leq k\leq n
. Given any
k-
tuple
y=(y^{1},\dots,y^{k})\in F^{k}
, we define

\displaystyle\Pi_{y}^{k}(E):=\big\{\,(x\cdot y^{1},\dots,x\cdot y^{k})\,\colon\,x\in E\,\big\}.

Put
y^{\prime}=(y^{1^{\prime}},\ldots,y^{k^{\prime}})\in(F^{\prime})^{k}
, where
y^{j^{\prime}}=H_{2}(y^{j})
for each
1\leq j\leq n
, and define the set of
k-
stars determined by
E^{\prime}
pinned at
y^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{y^{\prime}}^{k}(E^{\prime}):=\big\{\,(\,|y^{1^{\prime}}-\overline{x}|\,,\ldots,|y^{k^{\prime}}-\overline{x}|\,)\,\colon\,\overline{x}\in E^{\prime}\,\big\}.

For each tuple
y\in F^{k}
, we define the corresponding map
\Gamma_{y}
by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{y}:\big[-1/4,\infty\big)^{k}

\displaystyle\to\big[0,\,\infty\,\big)^{k}

\displaystyle(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(\,|y^{1^{\prime}}|\sqrt{1+4t_{1}},\ldots,|y^{k^{\prime}}|\sqrt{1+4t_{k}}\,\big).

Then it is straightforward to see that the set
\Delta_{y^{\prime}}^{k}(E)
is the image of
\Pi_{y}^{k}(E)
via
\Gamma_{y}
, and vice versa, i.e.

\displaystyle\Delta_{y^{\prime}}^{k}(E^{\prime})=\Gamma_{y}\big(\Pi_{y}^{k}(E)\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad\Pi_{y}^{k}(E)=\Gamma_{y}^{-1}\big(\Delta_{y^{\prime}}^{k}(E^{\prime})\big).

Moreover, one can see that
\Gamma_{y}
is a homeomorphism, and hence bi-Lipschitz over compact sets. This implies that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\Delta_{y^{\prime}}^{k}(E^{\prime}))=\dim_{H}(\Pi_{y}^{k}(E)).

(6)

In terms of Lebesgue measures, one also has

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{k}\big(\Delta_{y^{\prime}}^{k}(E^{\prime})\big)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal{L}^{k}\big(\Pi_{y}^{k}(E)\big)>0.

(7)

Furthermore, Lemma
3.1
below provides us stronger information: an equivalent between
L^{2}
norm of natural measures supported on
\Pi_{y}^{k}(E)
and
\Delta_{y^{\prime}}^{k}(E^{\prime})
.

Assume that
\mu_{E}
and
\mu_{F}
are corresponding Frostman probability measures supported on
E
and
F
, with exponents
s_{E}<\dim_{H}(E)
and
s_{F}<\dim_{H}(F)
, respectively. Then we may define measures which supported on
E^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime}
, by

\displaystyle\mu_{E^{\prime}}:=H_{1\sharp}\mu_{E}\quad,\quad\mu_{F^{\prime}}:=H_{2\sharp}\mu_{F}.

(8)

The bi-Lipschitz property of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
ensures that
\mu_{E^{\prime}}
and
\mu_{F^{\prime}}
are Frostman probability measures with exponents
s_{E}
and
s_{F}
, respectively.

With these notations in hand, let us define a natural measure
\eta_{y^{\prime}}
, supported on
\Delta_{y^{\prime}}^{k}(E^{\prime})
, by the following relation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}f(t)d\eta_{y^{\prime}}(t)=\int_{E^{\prime}}f(\,|y^{1^{\prime}}-\overline{x}|,\ldots,|y^{k^{\prime}}-\overline{x}|\,)d\mu_{E^{\prime}}(\overline{x})\,,\quad\forall\,f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{k}).

(9)

Then we may define measure
\nu_{y}
, supported on
\Pi_{y}^{k}(E)
, by relation

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}g(u)d\nu_{y}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}g(\,\Gamma_{y}^{-1}(t)\,)d\eta_{y^{\prime}}(t),\quad\forall\,g\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{k}).

(10)

Lemma 3.1
.

With the notations above, for each
y=(y^{1},\dots,y^{k})\in F^{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nu_{y}\|_{L^{2}}\approx\|\eta_{y^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}}.

(11)

Proof.

We will show that

\|\nu_{y}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\eta_{y^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}}.

The opposite inequality can be obtained by the same argument.

By duality, it enough to show that

\displaystyle\langle g,\nu_{y}\rangle\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|\eta_{y^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}}.

From the definition of
\nu_{y}
, the left-hand side equals

\displaystyle\int g(u)d\nu_{y}(u)=\int g(\Gamma^{-1}(t))d\eta_{y^{\prime}}(t).

Apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and using the fact that
\Gamma_{y}^{-1}
has a bounded Jacobian, one gets

\displaystyle\langle g,\nu_{y}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int|(g\circ\Gamma^{-1})(t)|^{2}dt\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int|\eta_{y^{\prime}}(t)|^{2}dt\bigg)^{1/2}\lesssim\|g\circ\Gamma^{-1}\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|\eta_{y^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|\eta_{y^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}}.

The proof of lemma is completed.
∎

4.1
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Theorem
1.2
is an immediate consequence of the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
E,F
be a pair of compact sets on
P_{d}^{\ast}
,
d\geq 3
. Assume that
\mu_{E}
and
\mu_{F}
are Frostman probability measures supported on
E
and
F
respectively, with corresponding exponents
s_{\mu_{E}}<\dim_{H}(E)
,
s_{\mu_{F}}<\dim_{H}(F)
. Assume that

\displaystyle s_{\mu_{E}}+\beta(s_{\mu_{F}})>d-1.

(12)

Then for
\mu_{F}
a.e.

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\Pi(E))\leq 2\dim_{H}(E)/(d-1).

Consider the map

\displaystyle\Phi:[1,2]^{d-1}\times[1,2]^{d-1}\to\big[0,\infty\big)\,\,,\quad(\overline{x},\overline{y})\mapsto\bigg|\frac{\overline{y}}{|\overline{y}|}+2|\overline{y}|\overline{x}\bigg|^{2}.

For all
x,y\in E
, we have the following equivalence

\displaystyle x\cdot y=t\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})=1+4t.

In particular, this implies that
\dim_{H}(\Pi(E))=\dim_{H}(\Phi(E^{\prime},E^{\prime}))
. So it suffices to give an upper bound for
\dim_{H}(\Phi(E^{\prime},E^{\prime}))
.

Clearly, one has
\Phi(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})\subset\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\Phi(E_{k}^{\prime},E_{k}^{\prime})
, so any cover of
\Phi(E_{k}^{\prime},E_{k}^{\prime})
would also a cover
\Phi(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})
as well. From definition, the set
E_{k}^{\prime}
consist of cubes of side length
q_{k}^{-(d-1)/s}
, whose centered at points of the form
(p_{1}/q_{k},\dots,p_{d-1}/q_{k})
. Thus given any
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in E_{k}^{\prime}
, we must have
\overline{x}
and
\overline{y}
are contained in some cubes centered at
p_{\overline{x}}
and
p_{\overline{y}}
, respectively. Moreover, one can check that the map
\Phi
has continuous derivative, which is bounded on compact set. This yields that
\Phi
is Lipschitz with some Lipschitz constant
L>0
. Combining these facts, one has

\displaystyle\big|\Phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})-\Phi(p_{\overline{x}},p_{\overline{y}})\big|\lesssim Lq_{k}^{-(d-1)/s},\quad\forall\overline{x},\overline{y}\in E_{k}^{\prime}.

Let
p=(p_{1}/q_{k},\dots,p_{d-1}/q_{k})
and
p^{\prime}=(p_{1}^{\prime}/q_{k},\dots,p_{d-1}^{\prime}/q_{k})
, where
q_{k}\leq p_{j},p_{j}^{\prime}\leq 2q_{k}
. Using the fact that
\sqrt{d}\leq|p^{\prime}|\leq 2\sqrt{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(p,p^{\prime})=\bigg|\frac{p^{\prime}}{|p^{\prime}|}+2|p^{\prime}|p\bigg|^{2}\leq\frac{r}{q_{k}^{2}},

for some integer
r
satisfying
0\leq r\lesssim d^{2}q_{k}^{2}
.

Roughly speaking, the above discussion tells us that for each
k
, we can cover
\Phi(E_{k}^{\prime},E_{k}^{\prime})
by at most
Cd^{2}q_{k}^{2}
intervals of length
cLq_{k}^{-(d-1)/s}
for some constants
C,c>0
. This indeed implies that

\displaystyle\dim_{H}(\Phi(E^{\prime},E^{\prime}))\leq\dim_{B}(\Phi(E^{\prime},E^{\prime}))\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log Cd^{2}q_{k}^{2}}{-\log(cLq_{k}^{-(d-1)/s})}=\frac{2s}{d-1}=\frac{2\dim_{H}(E^{\prime})}{d-1}.

We complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

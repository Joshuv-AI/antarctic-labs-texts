Stability and passivity for a class of distributed port-Hamiltonian networks
Hannes Gernandt, Dorothea Hinsen

Abstract
We consider a class of infinite dimensional (distributed) pH systems which is invariant under Kirchhoff-type interconnections and prove exponential stability and a power balance equation for classical solutions. The results are illustrated for power networks that incorporate distributed transmission line models based on the telegraph equations.

Introduction

In the past years, the port-Hamiltonian (pH) modeling approach to systems theory has received a lot of attention. It is well known to guarantee passivity and stability but it has various other benefits such as its flexibility allowing for multiphysical modeling, availability of structure preserving model order reductions, as well as efficient numerical methods, see
[
MU23
]
for a recent overview.

In this note, we study pH models for partial differential equations (PDEs) or also called distributed parameter pH (dpH) systems in the literature, see e.g. the monograph
[
JZ12
]
for a concise introduction as well as the recent survey
[
RCvdSS20
]
which summarizes twenty years of developments in dpH systems.

We consider the dpH system formulation from
[
JZ12
,
Vil07
]
which is the following class of abstract Cauchy problems on the Hilbert space
X=L^{2}([a,b],\mathbb{C}^{n})
of all measurable and square-integrable functions
x:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
equipped with the scalar product
\langle x,y\rangle_{L^{2}}:=\int^{b}_{a}\overline{y(\xi)}^{\top}x(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tfrac{d}{dt}z(t)=AHz(t),\quad z(0)=z_{0},\quad\text{ with }z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n},\end{split}

(1)

where
A:X\supset\dom A\rightarrow X
is a closed, densely defined linear operator with domain
\dom A
and
H
is a bounded uniformly positive operator which are given by

\displaystyle Ax(\xi):=P_{1}\frac{d}{d\xi}x(\xi)+P_{0}x(\xi),\quad(Hx)(\xi):=\mathcal{H}(\xi)x(\xi),

(2)

where
P_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is Hermitian and invertible,
P_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
skew-adjoint i.e.
P_{0}=P^{H}_{0}
with
P_{0}^{H}
is the conjugate transpose of
P_{0}
and the function
\mathcal{H}:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is assumed to be measurable and satisfies
mI_{n}\leq\mathcal{H}(\xi)\leq MI_{n}
for some constants
0<m<M
for almost all
\xi\in[a,b]
. One key idea of pH systems is the concept of
ports
which open the system for control, observation or interconnection. To include ports in (
1
) it is common to introduce the
boundary flow

f_{\partial}
and
boundary effort

e_{\partial}
which are given in terms of
z(t,\xi):=(z(t))(\xi)
by

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}f_{\partial}(t)\\
e_{\partial}(t)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}P_{1}&-P_{1}\\
I_{n}&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{H}(b)z(t,b)\\
\mathcal{H}(a)z(t,a)\end{bmatrix},

see
[
JZ12
,
Vil07
]
and
[
LGZM05
, Definition 3.5]
. Using these flow and effort variables, we obtain the following boundary control system, see e.g.
[
JZ12
, Section 11.3]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}z(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=P_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}(\mathcal{H}(\xi)z(t,\xi))+P_{0}(\mathcal{H}(\xi)z(t,\xi)),\quad W_{B,2}\begin{bmatrix}f_{\partial}(t)\\
e_{\partial}(t)\end{bmatrix}=0,

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=W_{B,1}\begin{bmatrix}f_{\partial}(t)\\
e_{\partial}(t)\end{bmatrix},\quad\quad y(t)=W_{C}\begin{bmatrix}f_{\partial}(t)\\
e_{\partial}(t)\end{bmatrix},

(3)

with
W_{B,1},W_{B,2},W_{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times 2n}
. In the study of the existence and solutions of boundary control systems, one considers the operator
A
given by (
2
) with domain

\dom A:=\left\{x\in L^{2}([a,b],\mathbb{C}^{n})\penalty\ \bigg|\penalty\ Hx\in H^{1}([a,b],\mathbb{C}^{n}),\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}W_{B,1}\\
W_{B,2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\begin{bmatrix}P_{1}&-P_{1}\\
I_{n}&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{H}(b)x(b)\\
\mathcal{H}(a)x(a)\end{bmatrix}=0\right\}.

Here
H^{1}([a,b],\mathbb{C}^{n})
denotes the Sobolev space of functions whose weak derivative exists and is square integrable. Note that there are characterizations for
A
being the generator of a contraction or an exponentially stable semigroup using only the matrices
W_{B,1}
and
W_{B,2}
, see e.g
[
JZ12
, Theorem 7.2.4, Lemma 9.1.4]
. Furthermore, power-balance equations for twice continuously differentiable input functions

were provided in
[
JZ12
,
Vil07
]
.

The main contribution of this work is to provide a general class of operators for which we can guarantee contractivity or exponential stability, as well as passivity for classical solutions and these properties are preserved for typical classes of Kirchhoff-type interconnections. The introduced class of distributed pH networks appears in several applications ranging from gas transport networks
[
EKLS+18
]
, to interconnections of beams as well as electrical transmission line networks, see
[
JZ12
]
.

Besides our extension of the pH system class some recent extensions of the pH system class were done in the following directions:
[

,
KZ15
]
study spatially higher dimensional systems,
[

]
consider higher order spatial derivatives, extension of Dirac structures to infinite dimensional spaces were studied in

In our class of distributed pH systems, compared to (
2
), the considered operators
A
have the following block diagonal structure

\mathcal{H}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{H}_{1}&0\\
0&\mathcal{H}_{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad P_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-I_{d}\\
-I_{d}&0\end{bmatrix},\quad P_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-D\\
-D&0\end{bmatrix},\quad n=2d,

and the derivative operator
D
is a closed and densely defined operator in
L^{2}([a,b],\mathbb{C}^{d})
. The domain of
D
lies between the domain of the
minimal realization

D_{\min}
of the spatial derivative and the

D_{\max}
given by

\displaystyle D_{\min}x=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}x,\quad\dom D_{\min}:=\{x\in H^{1}([a,b],\mathbb{C}^{d})\penalty\ |\penalty\ x(a)=x(b)=0\},

(4a)

\displaystyle D_{\max}x=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}x,\quad\dom D_{\max}:=H^{1}([a,b],\mathbb{C}^{d}).

(4b)

Recall, that the

A^{*}
of a closed densely define linear operator

\dom A^{*}:=\{y\in X\penalty\ |\penalty\ \langle Ax,y\rangle_{X}=\langle x,w\rangle_{X}\penalty\ \text{for some $w\in X$ and all $x\in\dom A$}\},\quad A^{*}y:=w.

Then it is an easy consequence that for the operators in (

.

In the applications mentioned above, incorporating dissipation requires a replacement of the skew-adjoint matrix

by some bounded non-positive diagonal operator

where

.

This motivates the following modifications and generalizations of the pH model (
3

(M1)

Instead of

given by (

X
with scalar product

and define on

the block operator

where

. Furthermore, we assume that

are bounded and fulfill

and
\langle Gx,x\rangle\geq 0
for all

;

(M2)

For

Instead of specifying the domain of

\{\mathcal{X},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}

\displaystyle\dot{z}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}-G&D^{*}\\
D^{*}&-R\end{bmatrix}Hz(t),\quad\Gamma_{0}Hz(t)=u(t),\quad\Gamma_{1}Hz(t)=y(t),

\displaystyle\langle Hz(t),z(t)\rangle-\langle Hz(0),z(0)\rangle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\re\langle u(\tau),y(\tau)\rangle d\tau-\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}R&0\\
0&G\end{bmatrix}Hz(t),Hz(t)\right\rangle.

[

Closely related to this are recent derivations of power balance equations for weak solutions
[

Besides passivity, Section
2

D^{*}&-R\end{bmatrix}H|_{\ker(\Gamma_{0}H)}.

It is shown that the generated semigroups are exponentially stable if

is

and if either

For classical distributed pH systems (

[

In Section
3
, we study structure preserving interconnections of boundary control systems (

Here we consider networks consisting of finitely many nodes and interconnecting edges such that for every edge there is an operator satisfying (M1)-(M3) and we consider then direct sums of these operators. It is shown that Kirchhoff-like interconnection conditions for this direct sum operator fulfill the assumptions (M1)-(M3). Hence, the results of Section

That Kirchhoff-type interconnections preserves impedance passivity of classical solutions of boundary nodes was shown in

Stability and passivity of the system class

First, we recall some basic notations. Let
X
be a Hilbert space with scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}
. Then
X\times X
equipped with the scalar product
\langle\left[\begin{smallmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}y_{1}\\
y_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\rangle:=\langle x_{1},y_{1}\rangle_{X}+\langle x_{2},y_{2}\rangle_{X}
is a Hilbert space. When it is clear from the context we will neglect the subscript and also write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
instead of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}
.
The set of bounded operators mapping a Hilbert space
X
into itself will be denoted by
\mathcal{L}(X)
.

Then we consider the following boundary control system

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}z(t)=\mathfrak{A}Hz(t),\quad u(t)=\Gamma_{0}Hz(t),\quad y(t)=\Gamma_{1}Hz(t),\quad Hz(0)=Hz_{0}

(7)

in the Hilbert space
X\times X
, mappings
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:\dom\mathfrak{A}\rightarrow X
and assume that the following conditions hold:

(A1)

Let
\hat{D}
be closed densely defined and skew-symmetric and
R,G\in\mathcal{L}(X)
with
\langle Rx,x\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle Gx,x\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in X

then
\mathfrak{A}
is a closed and densely defined operator on
X\times X
given by

\mathfrak{A}:=\begin{bmatrix}-G&\hat{D}^{*}\\
\hat{D}^{*}&-R\end{bmatrix};

(A2)

H\in\mathcal{L}(X\times X)
and there exist

such that
m\|x\|^{2}\leq\langle Hx,x\rangle\leq M\|x\|^{2}
holds for all

;

(A3)

A:=\mathfrak{A}|_{\ker(\Gamma_{0})}=\begin{bmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{bmatrix}
for some closed and densely defined operator
D
in
X
.

In the following definition, we recall typical notions of stability of semigroups, see e.g.
[
EN00
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup in a Hilbert space
X
. Then this semigroup is said to be

(a)

unitary
if
\|T(t)x\|=\|x\|
holds for all
x\in X
and all
t\geq 0
;

(b)

bounded
if there exists
M>0
such that
\|T(t)\|\leq M
holds for all
t\geq 0
and
contractive
if the latter holds with
M=1
;

(c)

strongly stable
if
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|T(t)x_{0}\|=0
holds for all
x_{0}\in X
;

(d)

exponentially stable
if there exist
\alpha,M>0
such that
\|T(t)\|\leq Me^{-\alpha t}
holds for all
t\geq 0
.

Strongly continuous semigroups satisfy the following relationship

\text{exponentially stable}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{strongly stable}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\text{contractive}\quad\Leftarrow\quad\text{unitary}.

In the following, we use the
resolvent set

\rho(A)
which is the set of all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
for which
A-\lambda
maps
\dom A
bijectively into
X
. It follows from the closed graph theorem that
(A-\lambda)^{-1}
is a bounded operator. Furthermore, the
spectrum
is defined as
\sigma(A):=\mathbb{C}\setminus\rho(A)
.

Below we recall well-known characterizations of unitarity due to Stone, see
[
EN00
, Theorem II.3.24]
,
contractivity due to Lumer and Philips, see
[
EN00
, Theorem II.3.15]
, strong stability due to Arendt, Batty, Lyubich, and Vũ, see
[
EN00
, Theorem V.2.21]
and exponential stability from Gearhart, Prüss, and Huang, see
[
EN00
, Theorem V.1.11]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup in a Hilbert space
X
with generator
A
then the following holds:

(a)

The semigroup is unitary if and only if
A
is skew-adjoint.

(b)

The semigroup is contractive if and only if

\displaystyle\langle Ax,x\rangle+\langle x,Ax\rangle\leq 0,\quad\text{for all $x\in\dom A$,}

(8)

i.e.
A
is
dissipative
and
A-\lambda
is surjective for some
\lambda>0
.

(c)

The semigroup is strongly stable if it is bounded,
A^{*}
has no eigenvalues on
{\rm i}\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma(A)\cap{\rm i}\mathbb{R}
is at most countable.

(d)

The semigroup is exponentially stable if and only if

\sigma(A)\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \re z<0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}\|({\rm i}\beta-A)^{-1}\|<\infty.

Remark 2.3
.

(a)

Since
A
given by (A3) is a bounded perturbation of a skew-adjoint operator, it generates a strongly continuous semigroup, see e.g.
[
EN00
, Corollary III.1.5]
.

(b)

The assertions (c) and (d) of Proposition
2.2
also hold in Banach spaces. If we replace (
8
) by

\|(\lambda-A)x\|\geq\lambda\|x\|\quad\text{for all $\lambda>0$ and all $x\in\dom A$},

then Proposition
2.2
(b) holds in Banach spaces as well.

(c)

For Hilbert spaces the surjectivity condition in (b) can be replaced by the dissipativity of
A^{*}
, see
[
JZ12
, Theorem 6.1.8]
.

A useful characterization of exponential stability of semigroups in Hilbert spaces is based on Lyapunov inequalities for their generators, see e.g.
[
JZ12
, Theorem 8.1.3]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a strongly continuous semigroup in a Hilbert space
X
. Then the semigroup is exponentially stable if and only if there exists
P\in\mathcal{L}(X)
with
\langle Px,x\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in X
satisfying the following
Lyapunov inequality

\displaystyle\langle Ax,Px\rangle+\langle Px,Ax\rangle\leq-\langle x,x\rangle,\quad\text{for all $x\in\dom A$.}

(9)

Using the positivity assumption (A2) on the Hamiltonian
H
we immediately obtain the following stability characterization for our class of operators.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
A
be a closed densely defined operator in the Hilbert space
X
given by (A3). Then the following holds:

(a)

If
R=G=0
then
A
generates a unitary semigroup.

(b)

If
\langle Gx,x\rangle,\langle Rx,x\rangle\geq 0

generates a contraction semigroup.

(c)

If there exist some

such that
\langle Rx,x\rangle\geq r_{0}
and
\langle Gx,x\rangle\geq g_{0}
holds for all

Note that operators

and

.

This assumption appears for instance in the context of beam equations and gas transport networks

As a preliminary result, we show that the above assumption allows us to exclude eigenvalues on the imaginary axis.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
D
be a closed densely defined operator in a Hilbert space
X
and consider
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{smallmatrix}\right]
with
R,G\in\mathcal{L}(X)
satisfying (A2) and (A4) for some
r_{0}>0
. Then all eigenvalues
\lambda=r+{\rm i}\omega\in\sigma(A)
with
\omega\neq 0
satisfy

\displaystyle\re\lambda\leq\frac{r_{0}}{2}.

(10)

Furthermore, it holds that

\displaystyle\ker A=\ker A^{*}=\ker\begin{bmatrix}G\\
D\end{bmatrix}\times\{0\}.

(11)

Proof.

Let
\lambda=r+{\rm i}\omega
for some
r,\omega\in\mathbb{R}
with
\omega\neq 0
be an eigenvalue of
A
, then there exists
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in X\times X
satisfying
Ax=(r+{\rm i}\omega)x
.
This equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle Dx_{1}-Rx_{2}=(r+{\rm i}\omega)x_{2},\quad-Gx_{1}-D^{*}x_{2}=(r+{\rm i}\omega)x_{1}.

(12)

Multiplying the first and the second equation with
x_{2}
and
x_{1}
, respectively, implies

\displaystyle\langle Dx_{1},x_{2}\rangle-\langle Rx_{2},x_{2}\rangle=(r+{\rm i}\omega)\|x_{2}\|^{2},\quad-\langle Gx_{1},x_{1}\rangle-\langle D^{*}x_{2},x_{1}\rangle=(r+{\rm i}\omega)\|x_{1}\|^{2}.

(13)

The second equation implies

(r-{\rm i}\omega)\|x_{1}\|^{2}=-\langle Gx_{1},x_{1}\rangle+\overline{\langle-D^{*}x_{2},x_{1}\rangle}=-\langle Gx_{1},x_{1}\rangle-\overline{\langle x_{2},Dx_{1}\rangle}=-\langle Gx_{1},x_{1}\rangle-\langle Dx_{1},x_{2}\rangle

and combining this with the first equation in (

\displaystyle(r-{\rm i}\omega)\|x_{1}\|^{2}+\langle Gx_{1},x_{1}\rangle=-(r+{\rm i}\omega)\|x_{2}\|^{2}-\langle Rx_{2},x_{2}\rangle.

(14)

Since
\omega\neq 0
, a comparison of the imaginary parts in (

\|x_{1}\|=\|x_{2}\|.

Hence
x_{2}\neq 0
because otherwise
x_{1}=0
and therefore
x=(x_{1},x_{2})=0
would not be an eigenvector. Therefore, (A4) and (
14
) imply

-r_{0}\|x_{2}\|^{2}\geq-\langle Rx_{2},x_{2}\rangle\geq-\langle Rx_{2},x_{2}\rangle-\langle Gx_{1},x_{1}\rangle=2r\|x_{2}\|^{2}

which leads to (
10
).

We continue with the proof of (
11
). Clearly,
\supseteq
holds. Conversely, let
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\ker A
and assume that
x_{2}\neq 0
(otherwise it would be in the desired set). Then, rewriting the left hand side equation in (
12

implies

x_{2}=R^{-1}Dx_{1}\quad\Rightarrow\quad(-G-D^{*}R^{-1}D)x_{1}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x_{1}^{*}(-G-D^{*}R^{-1}D)x_{1}=0.

The nonnegativity of
G
and
D^{*}R^{-1}D
yields
x_{1}\in\ker D^{*}R^{-1}D
which implies
x_{1}\in\ker R^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}D=\ker D
, but then

holds which proves the claim. Applying (

\ker A^{*}=\ker\begin{bmatrix}G\\
-D\end{bmatrix}\times\{0\}=\ker\begin{bmatrix}G\\

∎

The following lemma gives a criterion for the invertibility of block matrices having unbounded off diagonal entries. In the case of bounded entries this result was obtained in
[
Sta05
, Section A.4]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
D
be closed and densely defined in a Hilbert space
X
. We consider for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
the block operator matrix of the form

A_{\alpha,\beta}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha I_{X}&-D^{*}\\
D&\beta I_{X}\end{bmatrix}.

If
\alpha\beta\notin(-\infty,0]
then
A_{\alpha,\beta}
is boundedly invertible and its inverse is given by

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\beta I_{X}&D^{*}\\
-D&\alpha I_{X}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha\beta+D^{*}D)^{-1}&0\\
0&(\alpha\beta+DD^{*})^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(15)

Proof.

By
[
Kat00
, Theorem V.3.24]
,
D^{*}D
and
DD^{*}
are self-adjoint and nonnegative and we have
\sigma(D^{*}D),\sigma(DD^{*})\subseteq[0,\infty)
. Hence if
\alpha\beta\notin(-\infty,0]
we have, by definition of the spectrum, invertibility of
\alpha\beta+D^{*}D
and
\alpha\beta+DD^{*}
. We first conclude the injectivity of
A_{\alpha,\beta}
. Assume that there exists
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
with
A_{\alpha,\beta}x=0
. Then

\alpha x_{1}-D^{*}x_{2}=0,\quad Dx_{1}+\beta x_{2}=0.

Since
\alpha\beta\neq 0
we have
\alpha\neq 0
and hence combining the two equations gives

\alpha^{-1}DD^{*}x_{2}+\beta x_{2}=0.

If
\alpha\beta\notin(-\infty,0]
then
x_{2}=0
since
\alpha\beta+DD^{*}
is invertible and therefore
x_{1}=0
. Hence
A_{\alpha,\beta}
is injective.

On the other hand a right inverse of
A_{\alpha,\beta}
is given by

A_{r}^{-1}:X\times X\rightarrow\dom D\times\dom D^{*},\quad A_{r}^{-1}:=\begin{bmatrix}\beta I_{X}&D^{*}\\
-D&\alpha I_{X}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha\beta+D^{*}D)^{-1}&0\\
0&(\alpha\beta+DD^{*})^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Hence,
A_{\alpha,\beta}
is surjective and, since it is also injective,
A_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}
exists and is bounded. Moreover, using the equation
A_{\alpha,\beta}A_{r}^{-1}=I=A_{\alpha,\beta}A_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}
and multiplying it with
A_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}
from the left we see that
A_{r}^{-1}=A_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}
holds which proves (
15
).
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
D:X\supseteq\dom D\rightarrow X
be closed and densely defined, let
R,G\in\mathcal{L}(X)
nonnegative and
H\in\mathcal{L}(X\times X)
uniformly positive and consider
A=\begin{bmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{bmatrix}
. Then
AH
generates a strongly continuous semigroup. Furthermore,
A
generates a contraction (exponentially stable) semigroup if and only if
AH
generates a contraction (exponentially stable) semigroup.

Proof.

Decompose

AH=\begin{bmatrix}0&-D^{*}\\
D&0\end{bmatrix}H-\begin{bmatrix}G&0\\
0&R\end{bmatrix}H,

where the first summand is skew-adjoint in the weighted space
\langle H\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and therefore generates a strongly continuous semigroup. Furthermore,
AH
is a bounded perturbation of this generator which implies that
AH
generates a semigroup as well, see Remark
2.3
(a).

The left-hand side of the Lyapunov inequality (
9
) can be rewritten as

\langle Az,Pz\rangle+\langle Pz,Az\rangle=\langle AHH^{-1}z,H^{-1}HPHH^{-1}z\rangle+\langle H^{-1}HPHH^{-1}z,AHH^{-1}z\rangle.

Hence we consider the weighted product
\langle v,w\rangle_{H-}:=\langle H^{-1}v,w\rangle
for all
v,w\in X\times X
. Moreover, we have

-\langle z,z\rangle=-\langle HH^{-1}z,HH^{-1}z\rangle\leq-\|H^{-1}\|^{-2}\langle H^{-1}z,H^{-1}z\rangle\leq-\|H^{-1}\|^{-3}\langle H^{-1}z,H^{-1}H^{-1}z\rangle.

This implies for
\hat{P}:=\|H^{-1}\|^{3}HPH
and
w=H^{-1}z
for
z\in\dom A
that

\langle AHw,\hat{P}w\rangle_{H-}+\langle\hat{P}w,AHw\rangle_{H-}\leq-\langle w,w\rangle_{H-}.

This proves that
AH
generates an exponentially stable semigroup. The converse can be proven by repeating the above steps for
AH
and with
H^{-1}
instead of
H
. Finally, that
A
generates a contraction semigroup if and only if
AH
has this property follows from Proposition
2.2
(b) and Remark
2.3
(c).
∎

Note that there are examples of semigroup generator
A
and bounded operators
H
such that
AH
does not generate a semigroup
[
ZLGMV09
, Section 6]
.

The following proposition is one of the main results of this section.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
D
be a closed densely defined operator in a Hilbert space
X
, let
R,G\in\mathcal{L}(X)
be as in (A1) and assume that (A4) holds. Further, let
H\in\mathcal{L}(X\times X)
be uniformly positive then for
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{smallmatrix}\right]
the following holds:

(a)

If
D^{*}
is onto then
AH
generates an exponentially stable semigroup.

(b)

If
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}G\\
D\end{smallmatrix}\right]
is injective then
A
generates a strongly stable semigroup.

Proof.

First, note that by Lemma
2.8
we can assume that
H
is the identity. To show exponential stability, we first consider the case that
G=0
and
R=r_{0}I_{X}
, i.e.

\displaystyle A=\begin{bmatrix}0&-D^{*}\\
D&-r_{0}I_{X}\end{bmatrix}.

In this case, we verify the characterization of exponential stability given by Proposition
2.2
(d). By Lemma
2.7
we have that
z\in\rho(A)
if
z(z+r_{0})\notin(-\infty,0]
. This holds in particular if
\re z\geq 0
and
z\neq 0
. Thus,

\sigma(A)\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \re z<0\}\cup\{0\}.

Next, we show that
0\notin\sigma(A)
holds. Since
D^{*}
is onto
\ker D=(\ran D^{*})^{\perp}=\{0\}
implies that
D
is injective. Hence, by Lemma
2.6
,
A
is injective. Furthermore, using the closed range theorem we have that
\ran D
is closed, and using the orthogonal decomposition
X=\ran(D)\oplus\ker D^{*}
it follows that
\ran(D^{*}D)=\ran D^{*}=X
. This can be used to conclude the surjectivity of
A
. To this end let
(y_{1},0)\in X\times X
be given. Then there exists
Dx_{1}\in\dom D^{*}
such that
y_{1}=-D^{*}Dx_{1}
. Hence for all
y_{1}\in X
there exists
x_{1}\in X
such that
A(r_{0}x_{1},Dx_{1})=(y_{1},0)
holds. This implies
\ran A\supseteq X\times\{0\}
. On the other hand,
X=\ran D\oplus\ker D^{*}
implies that for
y_{2}=y_{2}^{1}+y_{2}^{2}
with
y_{2}^{1}\in\ran D
we have
A(y_{2}^{1},-r_{0}^{-1}y_{2}^{2})=(0,y_{2})
. Hence
A
is surjective which finally leads to
0\notin\sigma(A)
.

Furthermore, the resolvent along the imaginary axis for
\beta\neq 0
is given by

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \begin{bmatrix}({\rm i}\beta+r_{0})I_{X}&D^{*}\\
-D&{\rm i}\beta I_{X}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\rm i}\beta&-D^{*}\\
D&({\rm i}\beta+r_{0})I_{X}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}((-\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta r_{0})I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}&0\\
0&((-\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta r_{0})I_{X}+DD^{*})^{-1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\rm i}\beta((-\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta r_{0})I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}&-D^{*}((-\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta r_{0})I_{X}+DD^{*})^{-1}\\
D((-\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta r_{0})I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}&({\rm i}\beta+r_{0})((-\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta r_{0})I_{X}+DD^{*})^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(16)

We proceed by estimating the four block entries of the operator in (
16
). First, the self-adjointness of
D^{*}D
implies the resolvent estimate, see e.g.
[
Kat00
, Chapter V, §3.5]

\|{\rm i}\beta((\beta^{2}-{\rm i}\beta r_{0})I_{X}-D^{*}D)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{|{\rm i}\beta|}{\text{dist}(\beta^{2}-{\rm i}\beta r_{0},\sigma(D^{*}D))}\leq\frac{|\beta|}{|\beta|r_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{r_{0}},

where
\rm{dist}(\beta^{2}-{\rm i}\beta r_{0},\sigma(D^{*}D))
denotes the distance between the point
\beta^{2}-{\rm i}\beta r_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
and the closed set
\sigma(D^{*}D)\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. Similarly, by replacing
D
with
D^{*}
, we can estimate

\|({\rm i}\beta+r_{0})((\beta^{2}-{\rm i}\beta r_{0})I_{X}-DD^{*})^{-1}\|\leq\frac{|{\rm i}\beta+r_{0}|}{\text{dist}(\beta^{2}-{\rm i}\beta r_{0},\sigma(DD^{*}))}\leq\frac{|{\rm i}\beta+r_{0}|}{|\beta|r_{0}}\leq\frac{1+|\beta|^{-1}r_{0}}{r_{0}},

which is bounded for large values of
\beta
. It remains to bound the off-diagonal entries. To this end, consider
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
with
-\gamma\in\rho(D^{*}D)
and we have for all
x\in X

\displaystyle\|D(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle D(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x,D(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle D^{*}D(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x,(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x,(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x\rangle-\gamma\langle(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x,(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x\rangle.

To estimate the off-diagonal entries, we choose
\gamma=-\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta r_{0}
. Then we already showed that
\|(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}\|
is bounded for sufficiently large
\beta
and independently of
\beta
. Furthermore, we have according to the resolvent estimate for all
x\in X

\displaystyle\left|\gamma\langle(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x,(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}x\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\gamma|\|(\gamma I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}\|^{2}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|-\beta^{2}+{\rm i}\beta r_{0}|}{|\beta|^{2}r_{0}^{2}}\|x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+|\beta|^{-1}r_{0}}{r_{0}^{2}}\|x\|^{2}.

In combination, this shows that the lower off-diagonal entry is uniformly bounded for large values of
\beta
. Similarly, one can show the uniform boundedness of the upper off-diagonal entry of (
16
) for large values of
\beta
. From this, we can conclude the boundedness of the resolvent uniformly in
\beta
. Hence, Proposition
2.2
(d) provides us the exponential stability in this case.

Therefore, by Proposition
2.4
there exists a solution
P\in\mathcal{L}(X)
to the Lyapunov inequality (
9
) satisfying

\langle Ax,Px\rangle+\langle Px,Ax\rangle=\langle\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-D^{*}\\
D&-r_{0}I_{X}\end{smallmatrix}\right],Px\rangle+\langle Px,\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-D^{*}\\
D&-r_{0}I_{X}\end{smallmatrix}\right]x\rangle\leq-\langle x,x\rangle.

This, together with assumption (A4) and
G\geq 0
implies

\langle\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{smallmatrix}\right],Px\rangle+\langle Px,\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{smallmatrix}\right]x\rangle\leq-\langle x,x\rangle

and therefore by Proposition
2.4
the operator
A
generates an exponentially stable semigroup.

We continue with the proof of (b). By Proposition
2.5
,
A
generates a contraction semigroup. Applying Lemma
2.8
shows that
A
generates a contraction semigroup, which is in particular bounded. To prove the strong stability of the semigroup generated by
A
we apply Proposition
2.2
(c).
From the proof of part (a) it follows that the intersection
\sigma(A)\cap{\rm i}\mathbb{R}
contains at most
0
and is therefore countable. Furthermore, this implies for all
\lambda\in{\rm i}\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
that the folllowing holds

(\ker(A^{*}-\lambda))^{\perp}=\overline{\ran(A-\overline{\lambda})}=X\times X.

This implies
\ker(A^{*}-\lambda)=\{0\}
and therefore
A^{*}
has no eigenvalues
\lambda\in{\rm i}\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. To show the strong stability of the semigroup generated by
A
it remains to show that
A^{*}
is injective. If
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}G\\
D\end{smallmatrix}\right]
is injective then by Lemma
2.6
,
A^{*}
is injective. Hence, by Proposition
2.2
(c),
A
generates a strongly stable semigroup.
∎

Another sufficient condition for exponential stability can be obtained under compact resolvent assumptions, see also
[
Skr21
]
for related results. Here we will say that an eigenvalue
\lambda
of an operator
A
is

if it is an isolated point of
\sigma(E,A)
. Furthermore, an eigenvalue
\lambda
of

has

if
\dim\ker(A-\lambda I)<\infty
holds.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
D
be a closed densely defined operator in a Hilbert space
X
such that
(I_{X}+DD^{*})^{-1}
and
(I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}
are compact and let
R,G\in\mathcal{L}(X)
be nonnegative and
H\in\mathcal{L}(X\times X)
uniformly positive. Then the spectrum of
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{smallmatrix}\right]
consists of isolated eigenvalues with finite multiplicity. If
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}G\\
D\end{smallmatrix}\right]
is injective then
AH
generates an exponentially stable semigroup.

Proof.

It is well-known, see e.g.
[
Kat00
, Theorem 3.49]
, that
(I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1/2}
is compact and that
D(I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1/2}
is bounded see
[
Sch12
, Lemma 5.8]
and hence
D(I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}
is compact. Replacing
D
by
D^{*}
yields the compactness of
D^{*}(I_{X}+DD^{*})^{-1}
. In summary, this implies the compactness of each of the block entries of

\begin{bmatrix}-I_{X}&-D^{*}\\
D&-I_{X}\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}-I_{X}&D^{*}\\
-D&-I_{X}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}(I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}&0\\
0&(I_{X}+DD^{*})^{-1}\end{bmatrix}

and hence of the whole block operator.
Hence
\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-D^{*}\\
D&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]-\lambda\right)^{-1}
is compact for all
\lambda\in\rho\left(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&-D^{*}\\
D&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right)
. This together with the resolvent identity

\displaystyle\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&-D^{*}\\
D&0\end{bmatrix}-\lambda\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(A+\begin{bmatrix}G&0\\
0&R\end{bmatrix}-\lambda\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(A-\lambda)^{-1}-\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&-D^{*}\\
D&0\end{bmatrix}-\lambda\right)^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}G&0\\
0&R\end{bmatrix}(A-\lambda)^{-1}

implies that
A
has compact resolvent. By
[
Kat00
, Theorem III.6.29]
it follows that the spectrum of
A
consists of isolated eigenvalues with finite multiplicity. Furthermore,
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}G\\
D\end{smallmatrix}\right]
injective implies with Lemma
2.6
that
A
is injective and therefore
0\in\rho(A)
. Hence the exponential stability follows as in the proof of Proposition
2.9
.
∎

In the following, we consider the case where
D
is not injective or
D^{*}
is not surjective and restrict the operator
A
to the Hilbert space
\ker A^{\perp}=\overline{\ran\begin{bmatrix}G&D^{*}\end{bmatrix}}\times X
. We define

\hat{A}:=A|_{\dom A\cap\ker A^{\perp}}

and show that the restriction
\hat{A}
also generates a semigroup
(\hat{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
.

Furthermore, if
A
fulfills (A4), then
\hat{A}
fulfills (A4). Hence, if
D
is not injective, this restriction allows us to conclude stability for the semigroup generated by
\hat{A}
. To this end, we will use the orthogonal projector
P_{\ker A}:X^{2}\rightarrow\ker A
onto the kernel of
A
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{smallmatrix}\right]
be given as above satisfying (A4) and generating the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
. Then
\hat{A}:=A|_{\dom A\cap\ker A^{\perp}}
generates a strongly stable semigroup
(\hat{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
. Furthermore, if one of the following conditions holds

(i)

D^{*}
has closed range, or

(ii)

(I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1/2}
and
(I_{X}+DD^{*})^{-1/2}
are compact

then
(\hat{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is exponentially stable and there exist
\alpha,M>0
such that for all
x_{0}\in X\times X
,
t\geq 0
the following holds

\|T(t)x_{0}-P_{\ker A}x_{0}\|\leq Me^{-\alpha t}\|(I-P_{\ker A})x_{0}\|.

Proof.

First it is shown that the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
generated by
A
fulfills

\displaystyle T(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{T}(t)&0\\
0&I_{\ker A}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{in $X\times X=\ker A^{\perp}\oplus\ker A$,}

(17)

where
\tilde{T}(t):=T(t)|_{\ker A^{\perp}}
is again strongly continuous. If
x\in\ker A
, then the function
t\mapsto T(t)x
is a classical solution
x(\cdot)
with
x(0)=x
. Furthermore,
\ker A
is a closed
A
-invariant subspace which implies
x(t)=T(t)x\in\ker A
meaning that
\dot{x}(t)=Ax(t)=0
and therefore
x(t)=x(0)=x
. Hence
T(t)x=x
holds for all
x\in\ker A
. Furthermore,
A^{*}
generates the strongly continuous semigroup
\{T(t)^{*}\}_{t\geq 0}
. If we apply the previous argument to
\{T(t)^{*}\}_{t\geq 0}
with generator
A^{*}
we obtain

T(t)^{*}y\in\ker A^{*}\quad\text{for all $y\in\ker A^{*}$.}

By Lemma
2.6
we have
\ker A^{*}=\ker A
which implies for all
x\in(\ker A)^{\perp}=(\ker A^{*})^{\perp}
that

\langle T(t)x,y\rangle=\langle x,T(t)^{*}y\rangle=0

holds. Hence
T(t)x\in(\ker A)^{\perp}
holds for all
x\in(\ker A)^{\perp}
. Finally, this proves (
17
). Then by the generator definition, this implies that
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\hat{A}&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
with respect to the decomposition
X\times X=\ker A^{\perp}\oplus\ker A
and
\tilde{T}(t):=\hat{T}(t)
.

To show strong and exponential stability, we consider again the case
G=0
. In this case
\ker A=\ker D
and therefore
\hat{A}
is given in the Hilbert space
\hat{X}=\overline{\ran D^{*}}\times X
by

\hat{A}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\begin{bmatrix}-D^{*}|_{\overline{\ran D}}&0\end{bmatrix}\\
\begin{bmatrix}D|_{\overline{\ran D^{*}}}\\
0\end{bmatrix}&-R\end{bmatrix}.

In particular,
D|_{\overline{\ran D^{*}}}
is injective and therefore Proposition
2.9
(b) implies that
\hat{A}
generates a strongly stable semigroup. If the condition (i) holds, then

is onto and therefore Proposition
2.9
(a) implies that
\hat{A}
generates an exponentially stable semigroup. Furthermore, if (ii) is fulfilled then Proposition
2.10
yields the exponential stability of
\hat{A}
. As we argued before this then implies strong stability and exponential stability of
\hat{A}
for arbitrary nonnegative

.

Finally, using the decomposition
x_{0}=P_{\ker A}x_{0}+P_{\ker A^{\perp}}x_{0}
we find that

\|T(t)x_{0}-P_{\ker A}x_{0}\|=\|\hat{T}(t)P_{\ker A^{\perp}}x_{0}\|\leq Me^{-\alpha t}\|P_{\ker A^{\perp}}x_{0}\|

for some
M,\alpha>0
and all

since
\hat{T}
is exponentially stable by assumption.
∎

In the following we study the solvability of the boundary control problem for non-zero input functions

. To guarantee the existence of solutions to the boundary control problem (

(A5)

The triplet

\langle S^{*}f,g\rangle-\langle f,S^{*}g\rangle=\langle\Gamma_{1}f,\Gamma_{0}g\rangle-\langle\Gamma_{0}f,\Gamma_{1}g\rangle,\quad f,g\in\dom S^{*}.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

In the following, we obtain a power-balance equation for the classical solutions of (

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
and
H
be given by (A1)-(A3) and let
\{\mathcal{X},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}

(18)

\displaystyle\re\langle\mathfrak{A}Hz(t),Hz(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\re\langle u(t),y(t)\rangle-\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}G&0\\
0&R\end{bmatrix}Hz(t),Hz(t)\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle Hz(t),z(t)\rangle-\langle Hz(0),z(0)\rangle

0&R\end{bmatrix}Hz(t),Hz(t)\right\rangle.

(20)

Proof.

First, we consider the operator

\mathfrak{J}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&D^{*}\\
D^{*}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]H

and the boundary control system

\displaystyle\tfrac{d}{dt}x(t)={\rm i}\mathfrak{J}Hx(t),\quad u(t)=\Gamma_{1}Hx(t),\quad y(t)=\Gamma_{0}Hx(t).

By Lemma
2.12
the triplet
\{\mathcal{X},\Gamma_{0}H,\Gamma_{1}H\}
is a boundary triplet for
{\rm i}\mathfrak{J}H
. From
[
MS07
, Theorem 5.2]
and since

is a bounded perturbation of
\mathfrak{J}H
and
[

, Lemma 2.6]
we have that for

and

with
\Gamma_{1}Hz_{0}=u(0)

z\in C^{1}([0,\infty),X)\cap C([0,\infty),\dom(\mathfrak{A}H))

satisfying (

y(t):=\Gamma_{1}Hz(t)
is continuous. Invoking the abstract Green identity

\langle i\mathfrak{J}x_{1},x_{2}\rangle-\langle x_{1},i\mathfrak{J}x_{2}\rangle=\langle\Gamma_{1}x_{1},\Gamma_{0}x_{2}\rangle-\langle\Gamma_{0}x,\Gamma_{1}y\rangle,\quad x_{1},x_{2}\in\dom\mathfrak{J}.

The following dissipation inequality holds for all

\displaystyle\re\langle\mathfrak{A}Hz(t),Hz(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\re\langle\Gamma_{1}Hz(t),\Gamma_{0}Hz(t)\rangle-\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}G&0\\
0&R\end{bmatrix}Hz(t),Hz(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\re\langle u(t),y(t)\rangle-\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}G&0\\
0&R\end{bmatrix}Hz(t),Hz(t)\right\rangle.

The power balance equation (
20
) follows after replacing
\tfrac{d}{dt}z(t)=\mathfrak{A}Hz(t)
in (
19
) and integrating this equation.
∎

In the context of electrical circuits there is not only a vertex based definition of the Kirchhoff laws but also a loop based definition, see e.g.

-1&\text{if $e$ has a different orientation as $\mathcal{C}$,}\\
0&\text{if $e$ is not contained in $\mathcal{C}$.}\end{cases}

For electrical circuits, the Kirchhoff voltage law implies that the sum of all cycle voltages with appropriate signs in each fundamental cycle is zero. Furthermore, similar to the vertex based formulation, we have a corresponding condition for the adjoint operator which means here that the currents at the nodes of every fundamental cycle

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}^{\oplus}x(\mathcal{C})=\Gamma_{1,k}^{l}x_{2},\quad\text{for all $(k,l)\sim e\in\mathcal{C}$}.

4
Application to distributed transmission line networks

In this section, we apply our results to study the stability and passivity of electrical networks having distributed transmission lines. In the literature, the transmission lines are often modeled in form of
\pi
-equivalent models which provide accurate lumped models. However, distributed line models are necessary to accurately model higher frequency behavior, longer transmission lines or transmission lines with impurities.

To study a line of length
\ell>0
we consider a spatial interval
[0,\ell]\subset\mathbb{R}
. Then the voltage and current in a single line are given as solutions to the telegraph equations

\displaystyle C(\xi)\tfrac{\partial U}{\partial t}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\partial I}{\partial\xi}(t,\xi)-G(\xi)U(t,\xi),

\displaystyle L(\xi)\tfrac{\partial I}{\partial t}(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{\partial U}{\partial\xi}(t,\xi)-R(\xi)I(t,\xi),

(24)

\displaystyle U(0,\xi)=U^{0}(\xi),\quad I(0,\xi)=I^{0}(\xi),\quad t\geq 0,

where
I:[0,\infty)\times[0,\ell]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the current and
U:[0,\infty)\times[0,\ell]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the voltage across the transmission line. We allow values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d>1
to include three-phase models of transmission lines as well. Furthermore, the underlying Hilbert space is
X=L^{2}([0,\ell],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and we assume that the matrix-valued functions
C,G,L,R:[0,\ell]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
standing for the capacitance, conductance, inductance and resistance have values in the positive semi-definite matrices and fulfill
\langle C(\cdot)x,x\rangle\geq c\|x\|^{2}
and
\langle L(\cdot)x,x\rangle\geq l\|x\|^{2}
with
c,l>0
,
\langle R(\cdot)x,x\rangle\geq 0
and
\langle G(\cdot)x,x\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in X
and
R(\cdot),G(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}([0,\ell],\mathbb{C}^{d\times d})
.

Stability and passivity

The telegraph equations (
24
) can be written as an abstract Cauchy problem in the following way

\tfrac{d}{dt}z(t)=\mathfrak{A}Hz(t),\quad Hz(0)=(U^{0},I^{0})

with
X\times X=L^{2}([0,\ell],\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}([0,\ell],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\mathfrak{A}=\begin{bmatrix}-G&D^{*}\\
D^{*}&-R\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathcal{H}(\xi)=\begin{bmatrix}C(\xi)^{-1}&0\\
0&L(\xi)^{-1}\end{bmatrix},\quad z(t,\xi)=\begin{bmatrix}Q(t,\xi)\\
\phi(t,\xi)\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathcal{H}(\xi)z(t,\xi)=\begin{bmatrix}U(t,\xi)\\
I(t,\xi)\end{bmatrix},

where
Q
is the charge and
\phi
is the flux and the skew-symmetric operator
D
is given by

\displaystyle Dx=\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}x,\quad\dom D:=\{x\in L^{2}([0,\ell],\mathbb{C}^{d})\penalty\ |\penalty\ x\in H^{1}([0,\ell],\mathbb{C}^{d}),\penalty\ x(0)=x(\ell)=0\}.

(25)

Below we show that for the transmission line, there exists a boundary triplet of the desired form.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
D
be given by (
25
) and
X_{1}=X_{2}=L^{2}([0,\ell],\mathbb{C}^{d})
, and let

with

,

with respect to the scalar product given by
\langle H\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and

\left\{\mathbb{C}^{2d},(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}x_{1}(0)\\

{\rm i}H_{2}x_{2}(\ell)\end{bmatrix}\right\}.

Proof.

We compute with the weighted scalar product
\langle H\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\displaystyle\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}0&{\rm i}D\\
{\rm i}D&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}x_{1}\\
H_{2}x_{2}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}y_{1}\\
H_{2}y_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\rm i}DH_{2}x_{2},H_{1}y_{1}\rangle+\langle{\rm i}DH_{1}x_{1},H_{2}y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle{\rm i}H_{2}x_{2}(\ell),H_{1}y_{1}(\ell)\rangle-\langle{\rm i}H_{2}x_{2}(0),H_{1}y_{1}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle{\rm i}H_{2}x_{2},DH_{1}y_{1}\rangle-\langle{\rm i}H_{1}x_{1},DH_{2}y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle{\rm i}H_{1}x_{1}(\ell),H_{2}y_{2}(\ell)\rangle-\langle{\rm i}H_{1}x_{1}(0),H_{2}y_{2}(0)\rangle\

and

\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}x_{1}\\
H_{2}x_{2}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}0&{\rm i}D\\
{\rm i}D&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}y_{1}\\
H_{2}y_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle=\langle H_{1}x_{1},{\rm i}DH_{2}y_{2}\rangle+\langle H_{2}x_{2},{\rm i}DH_{1}y_{1}\rangle.

On the other hand it holds

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \left\langle\begin{bmatrix}-{\rm i}H_{2}x_{2}(0)\\
{\rm i}H_{2}x_{2}(\ell)\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}y_{1}(0)\\
H_{1}y_{1}(\ell)\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle-\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}x_{1}(0)\\
H_{1}x_{1}(\ell)\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}-{\rm i}H_{2}y_{2}(0)\\
{\rm i}H_{2}y_{2}(\ell)\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle{\rm i}H_{2}x_{2}(0)H_{1}y_{1}(0)\rangle+\langle H_{1}x_{1}(0),{\rm i}H_{2}y_{2}(0)\rangle+\langle{\rm i}H_{2}x_{2}(\ell),x_{1}y_{1}(\ell)\rangle-\langle H_{1}x_{1}(\ell),{\rm i}H_{2}y_{2}(\ell)\rangle.

which shows the Green identity. The surjectivity is clear since each of the mappings is surjective and the ranges use independent variables.
∎

There are now several choices for boundary triplets, each resulting in a different boundary control system. Two possible choices are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(x):=\begin{bmatrix}U(0)\\
U(\ell)\end{bmatrix},\quad\Gamma_{1}(x):=\begin{bmatrix}-{\rm i}I(0)\\
{\rm i}I(\ell)\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(x):=\begin{bmatrix}I(0)\\
I(\ell)\end{bmatrix},\quad\hat{\Gamma}_{1}(x):=\begin{bmatrix}-{\rm i}U(0)\\
{\rm i}U(\ell)\end{bmatrix}.

This implies that the telegraph equations (
24
) with either current or voltage boundary inputs is a boundary control system of the form

\dot{z}(t)=\mathfrak{A}Hz(t),\quad u(t)=\Gamma_{0}z(t),\quad y(t)=\Gamma_{1}y(t)

and a power-balance equation similar to (
19
) holds for control inputs
u\in C^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Proposition
2.2
implies that the underlying semigroups are contraction semigroups. However, they are not strongly stable in general. The block operator
A
associated with (
24
) for
G=0
and the boundary conditions
I(0)=I(\ell)=0
is not injective. If
R=0
then the boundary conditions
U(0)=U(\ell)=0
would imply a non-trivial kernel for
A
.

Consider now a network of transmission lines described by a graph
\mathcal{G}=(V,E)
. Then for each line
e\in E
with spatial domain
[0,\ell_{e}]
for some
\ell_{e}>0
, we consider in
X_{e}\times X_{e}=L^{2}([0,\ell_{e}],\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}
the following system

\mathfrak{A}_{e}=\begin{bmatrix}-G_{e}&-D_{e}^{*}\\
-D_{e}^{*}&-R_{e}\end{bmatrix},\quad(H_{e}x_{e})(\xi):=\mathcal{H}_{e}(\xi)x_{e}(\xi)=\begin{bmatrix}C_{e}(\xi)^{-1}&0\\
0&L_{e}(\xi)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}Q_{e}(\xi)\\
\phi_{e}(\xi)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}U_{e}(\xi)\\
I_{e}(\xi)\end{bmatrix}

and the skew-symmetric operator

D_{e}x:=\tfrac{d}{d\xi}x,\quad\dom D_{e}:=\{x_{e}\in H^{1}([0,\ell_{e}])\penalty\ |\penalty\ x_{e}(0)=x_{e}(\ell_{e})=0\}.

The following boundary triplet is given by Lemma
4.1

\Gamma_{0}^{e}(x_{e}):=\begin{bmatrix}U_{e}(0)\\
U_{e}(\ell_{e})\end{bmatrix},\quad\Gamma_{1}^{e}(x_{e}):=\begin{bmatrix}-{\rm i}I_{e}(0)\\
{\rm i}I_{e}(\ell_{e})\end{bmatrix}.

This triplet fulfills for the operators
S_{j}H
assumption (A5’), which was used in Section
3
for Kirchhoff type interconnections. In particular, the boundary mappings are given by

\displaystyle x_{j}=(x_{0}^{j},x_{1}^{j})\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\Gamma_{0,0}^{e}Hx_{0}^{e}\\
\Gamma_{0,1}^{e}Hx_{0}^{e}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}U_{e}(0)\\
U_{e}(\ell_{e})\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle x_{j}=(x_{0}^{j},x_{1}^{j})\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\Gamma_{1,0}^{e}Hx_{1}^{e}\\
-\Gamma_{1,1}^{e}Hx_{1}^{j}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-{\rm i}I_{e}(0)\\
{\rm i}I_{e}(\ell_{e})\end{bmatrix}.

If we consider now the operators based on establishing continuity of
\Gamma_{0}^{e}
at every vertex
v\in V
, then this leads to the node-type boundary triplet

\Gamma_{0}^{V}((x_{e})_{e\in E})=(U(v))_{v\in V},\quad\Gamma_{1}^{V}((x_{e})_{e\in E}):=\big\{-{\rm i}\sum_{e\sim v,(e,k)\sim v}{\rm sgn}(e,v)I_{e}(k\ell_{e})\big\}_{v\in V},

where the sum is taken over all edges
e
which are adjacent to
v
and
k=0,1
. Hence the voltage controlled network of transmission lines can be written as the following boundary control system

\displaystyle\dot{z}(t)=\mathfrak{A}Hz(t),\quad u(t)=\Gamma_{0}^{V}z(t),\quad y(t)=-{\rm i}\Gamma_{1}^{V}z(t)

(26)

where

\displaystyle z=(z_{e})_{e\in E},\quad\mathfrak{A}:=\begin{bmatrix}-\bigoplus_{e\in E}G_{e}&\bigoplus_{e\in E}D_{e}^{*}\\
\bigoplus_{e\in E}D_{e}^{*}&-\bigoplus_{e\in E}R_{e}\end{bmatrix},\quad H:=\begin{bmatrix}\bigoplus_{e\in E}C_{e}(\cdot)^{-1}&0\\
0&\bigoplus_{e\in E}L_{e}(\cdot)^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(27)

This can be viewed as having a voltage input at every node. Furthermore, the operator in (A3) is given by

A=\mathfrak{A}H|_{\ker\Gamma_{0}^{V}}=\begin{bmatrix}-G&-D^{*}\\
D&-R\end{bmatrix}

and has the property that
D
is the direct sum of derivatives with domain

\dom D=\{(U_{e})_{e\in E}\in\oplus_{e\in E}H^{1}([0,\ell_{e}]),U_{e}(v)=0\},\quad D(U_{e})_{e\in E}=(\tfrac{d}{d\xi}U_{e})_{e\in E}.

Therefore,
D
is injective and both operators
(I_{X}+D^{*}D)^{-1}
and
(I_{X}+DD^{*})^{-1}
are compact by the Sobolev embedding theorem. Hence, by Proposition
2.10
,
\mathfrak{A}H|_{\ker\Gamma_{0}^{V}}
generates an exponentially stable semigroup.
Furthermore, by Proposition
2.13
the following power balance equation holds for all
u\in C^{2}([0,\infty),\mathbb{C}^{|V|})

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}\re\langle\mathfrak{A}_{e}H_{e}z_{e}(t),H_{e}z_{e}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\re\langle u(t),y(t)\rangle-\sum_{e\in E}\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}-G_{e}&0\\
0&-R_{e}\end{bmatrix}H_{e}z_{e}(t),H_{e}z_{e}(t)\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}(\langle H_{e}z_{e}(t),z_{e}(t)\rangle-\langle H_{e}z_{e}(0),z_{e}(0)\rangle)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}\re\langle u(\tau),y(\tau)\rangle d\tau-\sum_{e\in E}\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}G_{e}&0\\
0&R_{e}\end{bmatrix}H_{e}z_{e}(t),H_{e}z_{e}(t)\right\rangle.

To study current controlled systems, we interchange the boundary triplet mappings which results in the following boundary triplet

\hat{\Gamma}_{0}^{V}((x_{e})_{e\in E})=\big\{\sum_{e\sim v,(e,k)\sim v}{\rm sgn}(e,v)I_{e}(k\ell_{e})\big\}_{v\in V},\quad,\quad\hat{\Gamma}_{1}^{V}((x_{e})_{e\in E}):=(-{\rm i}U(v))_{v\in V}.

Here the resulting operator

\hat{A}:=\mathfrak{A}H|_{\ker\hat{\Gamma}_{0}^{V}}=\begin{bmatrix}-G&-\hat{D}^{*}\\
-\hat{D}&-R\end{bmatrix}

does in general not generate an exponentially stable semigroup, since
\hat{D}

has a one-dimensional kernel spanned by the function
c\in(\oplus_{e\in E}H^{1}([0,\ell_{e}]))^{2}
which is constant in the first
|E|
entries which correspond to the voltages and zero in the remaining entries which correspond to the currents. Hence if
\ker G_{e}(\xi)
does not contain the constant function for all
\xi
from some non-empty interval and all edges
e\in E
, then
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}G\\
\hat{D}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
is injective and therefore Proposition
2.9
implies the exponential stability of the semigroup
\{\hat{T}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
generated by
\hat{A}
. Otherwise,
\ker\hat{A}=\Span\{c\}
and Corollary
2.11
yields the existence of
M,\alpha>0
such that the following holds:

\left\|\hat{T}(t)x_{0}-\frac{\langle x_{0},c\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\|c\|_{L^{2}}}c\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Me^{-\alpha t}\left\|x_{0}-\frac{\langle x_{0},c\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\|c\|_{L^{2}}}c\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Alternatively, for the current controlled case, we assume that
G
is uniformly positive instead of
R
. Then one can use the loop based formulation which requires a set of fundamental circles
\mathcal{C}
and constant currents
I(C)
for all
C\in\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}^{E}((x_{e})_{e\in E})=\big\{I(C)\big\}_{C\in\mathcal{C}},\quad\quad\hat{\Gamma}_{1}^{E}((x_{e})_{e\in E}):=\left({\rm i}\sum_{e\in C,(k,l)\sim e}\sgn(e,C)U(k\ell_{e})\right)_{C\in\mathcal{C}}.

(28)

However, in comparison to the voltage controlled case, we cannot directly conclude the injectivity of the resulting operator

\tilde{A}:=\mathfrak{A}H|_{\ker\hat{\Gamma}_{0}^{E}}=\begin{bmatrix}-G&-\tilde{D}^{*}\\
-\tilde{D}&-R\end{bmatrix}.

To achieve injectivity we need the additional assumption that a zero current on each fundamental cycle implies that the current is identically zero on every edge. Sufficient for this is that every edge is contained in a fundamental cycle. Furthermore,
\tilde{A}
has a compact resolvent and hence, it generates an exponentially stable semigroup.

We consider now networks with
R_{e}=G_{e}=0
for all
e\in E
together with the following boundary conditions

-{\rm i}\sum_{e\sim v,(e,k)\sim v}{\rm sgn}(e,v)I_{e}(k\ell_{e})=-{\rm i}r_{v}U(v)

for some
r_{v}\geq 0
and all
v\in V
, which describe loads attached to the network nodes. Then for
L:=\diag(-{\rm i}r_{v})_{v\in V}
the matrices
-{\rm i}L=\diag(-r_{v})_{v\in V}={\rm i}L^{*}
are dissipative. Hence, by Proposition
3.1
, these boundary conditions lead to generators of contraction semigroups. This implies the generation of contraction semigroups also for all networks satisfying
R_{e}=G_{e}\geq 0
.

We conclude this section by applying our modeling approach to the small example network shown in Figure

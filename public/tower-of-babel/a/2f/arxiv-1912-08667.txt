Global well-posedness of the two-dimensional stochastic nonlinear wave equation on an unbounded domain
Leonardo Tolomeo

Abstract
. We study the two-dimensional wave equation with cubic nonlinearity posed on ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , with space-time white noise forcing. After a suitable renormalisation of the nonlinearity, we prove global well-posedness for this equation for initial data in ℋ s \mathcal{H}^{s} , s > 4 5 s>\frac{4}{5} .

1.
Introduction

We consider the following stochastic nonlinear wave equation (SNLW) with additive space-time white noise forcing

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}=\Delta u-{u}^{3}+3\infty\cdot u+\mathrm{d}W,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\in H^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}},\\
u_{t}(0,\cdot)=u_{1}\in H^{s-1}_{\mathrm{loc}},\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{d}W
denotes a space-time white noise on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The term “
-3\infty\cdot u
” in the equation denotes a time-dependent renormalisation, which has been first introduced by Gubinelli, Koch and Oh in
[
4
]
for a family of stochastic wave equations with power nonlinearities. In this work, the authors prove local well-posedness for renormalised stochastic wave equations posed on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
with certain polynomial nonlinearities.

The necessity to apply such a renormalisation in oder to get nontrivial solutions was highlighted in a series of works by Albeverio, Haba, Oberguggenberger, and Russo
[
1
,
8
,
9
,
14
]
, and more recently by Oh, Okamoto and Robert
[
10
]
, who showed that without the renormalisation term, solutions to (
1.1
) must satisfy a linear wave equation.

The renormalisation that we apply in (
1.1
) is better described as follows. Recall that
\mathrm{d}W
is defined to be a distribution-valued random
variable such that for every test function
\phi
,
\left\langle\phi,\mathrm{d}W\right\rangle
is a gaussian random variable with mean
0
and variance

(1.2)

\mathbb{E}[|\left\langle\phi,\mathrm{d}W\right\rangle|^{2}]=\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Ignoring the term with
\infty
in (
1.1
), we consider a perturbative expansion
u=v+\psi
, where

(1.3)

\psi:=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}\mathrm{d}W(t^{\prime})

solves the linear wave equation

\psi_{tt}=\Delta\psi+\mathrm{d}W.

Formally, the term
v
would then solve the equation

v_{tt}-\Delta v=-(\psi+v)^{3}=-\psi^{3}-3\psi^{2}v-3\psi v^{2}-v^{3}.

However, because of the roughness of
\mathrm{d}W
, it can be shown that the terms
\psi^{3}
,
\psi^{2}
do not make sense as space-time distributions. Therefore, we introduce the Wick renormalisation

(1.4)

\displaystyle:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt:

\displaystyle=\psi^{2}-\mathbb{E}[|\psi|^{2}],

\displaystyle:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:

\displaystyle=\psi^{3}-3\mathbb{E}[|\psi|^{2}]\psi,

and
define

v=u-\psi
to solve the equation

(1.5)

\begin{cases}v_{tt}-\Delta v=-:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:-3:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt:v^{2}-3\psi v-v^{3}\\
v(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\in H^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\\
v_{t}(0,\cdot)=u_{1}\in H^{s-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).\end{cases}

While both terms on the right hand side of (
1.4
) diverge (for both definitions), it is actually possible to give a meaning to the renormalised terms
:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt:,:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:
by first taking a smooth approximation of the noise
\mathrm{d}W
and then taking a limit in the space
W^{-\varepsilon,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. This will be carried out explicitly in Section 2.
Denoting (formally)
:\hskip-2.0pt{u^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:\,=u^{3}-3\mathbb{E}[|\psi|^{2}]u
, solving the equation (
1.5
) for
v
corresponds to solving the equation

(SNLW)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}=\Delta u-:\hskip-2.0pt{u}\hskip-2.0pt:^{3}+\mathrm{d}W,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\in H^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}},\\
u_{t}(0,\cdot)=u_{1}\in H^{s-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}\end{cases}

for
u
. Since
\mathbb{E}[|\psi|^{2}]=+\infty
for every
t>0
, by inserting this into (
SNLW
) we obtain the formula (
1.1
). This kind of renormalisation is exactly the same that appears in
[
4
]
for the cubic wave equation on the torus.

Before stating our main result, we need to define what we mean by solutions of (
1.1
). As we already discussed, we write
u=\psi+v
, and we require
v
to solve the mild formulation of (
1.5
),

(1.6)

v=\cos(t|\nabla|)u_{0}+\frac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}\Big(-:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:(t^{\prime})-3:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt:(t^{\prime})v(t^{\prime})-3\psi v^{2}(t^{\prime})-v^{3}(t^{\prime})\Big)\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the equation (
SNLW
) on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and let
1>s>\frac{4}{5}
. Then (
SNLW
) is almost surely globally well-posed.
More precisely, for every
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, there exists a unique
\psi+C(\mathbb{R};H^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
-valued random variable
u
such that almost surely

•

for every stopping time
T>0
,
u|_{[-T,T]}
is the unique solution to (
SNLW
) in the space
\psi+C([-T,T];H^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}})
,

•

for every time
0<T<\infty
,
u
is continuous in the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})
.

In the recent years, there have been many developments in the study of global solutions for parabolic stochastic SPDEs, both on a compact domain (see for instance
[
6
,
11
,
12
]
for a study of
\Phi^{4}_{d}
models), and more recently, on the euclidian space (
[
2
,
3
]
).
However, the dispersive nature of the wave equation does not allow to extend the techniques developed for the study of stochastic quantisation equation to the study of equation
1.1
.

On the other hand, there are not many global well-posedness results available for stochastic dispersive equations, and this result is the first one the author is aware of that deals with a non compact domain. In
[
7
]
, the author and his collaborators showed global well-posedness for the nonlinear stochastic beam equation posed on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. In
[
5
]
, the author together with the authors of
[
4
]
, showed a similar result to Theorem
1.1
, by extending the solutions of the stochastic wave equation with cubic nonlinearity posed on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
built in
[
4
]
for infinite time. The main difference between this result and Theorem
1.1
is that we do not have a local well-posedness result available for (
SNLW
). Indeed, due to the unboundedness of the domain, one can show that for every
t>0
, the stochastic convolution
\psi(t)
satisfies
\left\|{\psi(t)}\right\|_{X}=+\infty

for every translation invariant norm
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{X}
defined on a subspace
X
of distributions.
This in turn implies that given
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,
t,R>0
, a typical solution of (
1.6
) could satisfy
a priori

\left\|{u(t)}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}(B)}\geq R
on some ball
B
. Therefore, any perturbative argument for local well-posedness (such as a Banach fixed point argument) is bound to fail.

We therefore follow a different strategy. We take a cutoff function
\rho
with compact support with
\rho\equiv 1
on a big ball
B=B(0,R)
,
and consider a localised version of the equation for
v
given by

(1.7)

\tilde{v}_{tt}-\Delta\tilde{v}+\tilde{v}^{3}+3\tilde{v}^{2}\rho\psi+3\tilde{v}\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt:+\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:\,\,=0.

Notice that in the ball
B
, this equation is exactly the same as (
1.5
). Because of finite speed of propagation, we expect
\tilde{v}(t)=v(t)
in the ball
B(0,R-t)
1
1

1

This will be made more rigorous in Section 5.
. Therefore, we reduce the problem of showing global well-posedness for
(
SNLW
), to showing global well-posedness for (
1.7
), independently of the cutoff
\rho
.

In order to do so, we show an energy estimate. We would like to estimate the functional

E(\tilde{v}(t),\partial_{t}\tilde{v}(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\int|\partial_{t}\tilde{v}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int(\tilde{v})^{4},

since it is conserved by the flow of cubic wave equation with no forcing. However, as we will see in Section 2,
\psi\notin L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
so we expect
\tilde{v}\notin H^{1}
, hence
E(\tilde{v},\partial_{t}\tilde{v})=+\infty
.

In order to deal with the lack of regularity of
\tilde{v}
, we
make use of the I-method developed by Colliander, Keel, Staffilani, Takaoka and Tao in 2002 to show global well-posedness for dispersive equations with initial data of regularity below the energy space. We consider an operator
I=I_{N}
given by the Fourier multiplier
m_{N}
with

m_{N}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }|\xi|\leq N,\\
\Big(\frac{N}{\xi}\Big)^{1-s}&\text{ for }|\xi|\geq 3N.\end{cases}

For every
N
, one has that
I\tilde{v}\in H^{1}
if and only if
\tilde{v}\in H^{s}
, and
I
is the identity at low frequencies. Therefore,
we can bound
\left\|{\tilde{v}}\right\|_{H^{s}}
by making use of the functional

F=E(I\tilde{v}(t),I\partial_{t}\tilde{v}(t)).

Of course, the functional
F
will not be time independent, but we can bound it by making use of a Gronwall argument. We have that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}F=\text{ commutator terms }+\text{ forcing terms}

The commutator terms are typical of the I-method, and we can estimate them by
making use of simple harmonic analytic techniques.

The terms coming from the forcing are more subtle to estimate, and in particular the term

-3\int Iv_{t}(Iv)^{2}I(\rho\psi)

requires a very sharp large deviation estimate for the
L^{p}
norm of the term
I(\rho\psi)
.

Putting the estimates together, for fixed
N
, allows us to show existence of
\tilde{v}
for a random time
O(1)
. Iterating this procedure
on a sequence
N_{0}\ll N_{1}\ll N_{2}\ll\dotsb
, we get global existence for
\tilde{v}
.

We would like to point out that, as far as the author is aware, this is the first instance of an application of the
I
- method that requires to change the cutoff parameter
N
in a time-dependent way.

In section 5, we then conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
by showing that when
\rho\to 1
, the solutions
\tilde{v}
converge to a limit which is continuous in the initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})
,
and that every solution
v
of (
1.5
) must be equal to this limit.

2.
Stochastic Convolution

In this section, we establish relevant estimates on the stochastic convolution

\psi(t,\cdot){:=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}}\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

Let
\psi_{N}(t,\cdot):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}\chi_{N}(\nabla)\mathrm{d}W_{t}
, where

\chi_{N}
is the indicator function of the ball of radius
N
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

,

\displaystyle:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{0}_{N}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x,t)

\displaystyle:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{2}_{N}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x,t)

\displaystyle:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3}_{N}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x,t)

\left\|{\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{q}_{T}W_{x}^{-\varepsilon,r}}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{T,p,q,\varepsilon,r}.

Because of the arbitrariness of

(iv)

\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}W_{x}^{-\varepsilon,r}}<\infty

(v)

Let

m(r)=1

\partial_{r}^{n}m(r)\lesssim_{n}r^{-\varepsilon-n}

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|{I_{N}(\rho\psi)}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{2})}>\lambda p^{\frac{1}{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}N\right)\leq C_{T,\rho,m}^{p}\lambda^{-p}

In order to prove this proposition, we need some tools. Recall the Hermite polynomials generating function

(2.1)

F(t,x;\sigma):=e^{tx-\frac{1}{2}\sigma t^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}H_{k}(x;\sigma).

For simplicity, let
F(t,x):=F(t,x;1)
, and let
H_{k}(x):=H_{k}(x;\sigma)
.
Fix
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the Hilbert space

, where
\mathrm{d}\mu_{d}:=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp(-|x|^{2}/2).

Then Hermite polynomials satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{k}(x)H_{m}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x)=k!\delta_{km}

for all
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Define the homogeneous Wiener chaos of order

to be an element of the form

\prod_{j=1}^{d}H_{k_{j}}(x_{j})
, where

Consider the operator

Moreover, we have the following hypercontractivity of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup

Lemma 2.2

\left\|{U(t)u}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu_{d})}\leq\left\|{u}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mu_{d})}.

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\rho(\cdot)\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)}\right\|_{W^{-\varepsilon,p}}^{p}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{{\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle}^{-\varepsilon}\rho(\cdot)\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)}\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\right]<+\infty

for any
\varepsilon>0
,
t>0
, and
p\geq 1
, uniformly in
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

By the properties of Wick products
[
15
, Theorem I.22]
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}(t,x)}\hskip-2.0pt::\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}(t,y)}\hskip-2.0pt:\right]

\displaystyle\sim\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\psi_{N}(t,x)\psi_{N}(t,y)\right]\right\}^{l}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\xi_{1}|,\dotsc,|\xi_{l}|\leq N}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(t,\xi_{j})e^{-2\pi i\xi_{j}\cdot(x-y)}\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\xi_{1}|,\dotsc,|\xi_{l}|\leq N}e^{-2\pi i\left(\sum_{1}^{l}\xi_{j}\right)\cdot(x-y)}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(t,\xi_{j})\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}.

Therefore, proceeding as before,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|{\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle}^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}(t,\cdot)}\hskip-2.0pt:(x)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{|\xi_{1}|,\dotsc,|\xi_{l}|\leq N}\left\langle{\xi_{1}+\dotsb+\xi_{l}}\right\rangle^{-2\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(t,\xi_{j})\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{t}\int_{\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{l}}\left\langle{\xi_{1}+\dotsb+\xi_{l}}\right\rangle^{-2\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\left\langle{\xi_{j}}\right\rangle^{-2}\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}<\infty.

for any
\varepsilon>0
,
t>0
, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
N
.
Hence we have

\left[\left\|{\rho(\cdot):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{H^{-\varepsilon}}^{2}\right]\lesssim_{\rho,t}1,

and by hypercontractivity

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\rho(\cdot):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{W^{-\varepsilon,p}}^{p}\right]\lesssim_{p,\rho,t}1.

Integrating in time, we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}H_{x}^{-\varepsilon}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\right]\lesssim_{\rho,T}1,

and by hypercontractivity,

(2.7)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}W_{x}^{-\varepsilon,p}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{p}\right]\lesssim_{p,\rho,T}1.

Moreover, since
\rho(\cdot):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:
has compact support,
T<+\infty
, if

q,r,s\leq p
, it follows that

\left\|{\left\|{\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{r}_{t}W_{x}^{-\varepsilon,s}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{p,\rho,T}1.

(iii)

Following (ii), we have that for
N\leq M
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\psi_{N}(s,x)\psi_{M}(t,y)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{B_{N}}\frac{\sin((s-t^{\prime})|\xi|)\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot(x-y)}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\psi_{N}(s,x)\psi_{N}(t,y)].

Therefore, using the properties of Wick products
[
15
, Theorem I.22]
again,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{M}^{l}(t,x)}\hskip-2.0pt:-:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}(t,x)}\hskip-2.0pt:\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left|:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{M}^{l}(t,x)}\hskip-2.0pt:\right|^{2}-2\mathbb{E}\left[:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{M}^{l}(t,x)}\hskip-2.0pt::\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}(t,x)}\hskip-2.0pt:\right]+\mathbb{E}\left|:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}(t,x)}\hskip-2.0pt:\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\psi_{M}(t,x)^{2}\right]\right\}^{l}-2\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\psi_{M}(t,x)\psi_{N}(t,x)\right]\right\}^{l}+\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\psi_{N}(t,x)^{2}\right]\right\}^{l}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\psi_{M}(t,x)^{2}\right]\right\}^{l}-\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[\psi_{N}(t,x)^{2}\right]\right\}^{l}

\displaystyle=\int_{N\leq|\xi_{1}|,\dotsc,|\xi_{l}|\leq M}e^{-2\pi i\left(\sum_{1}^{l}\xi_{j}\right)\cdot(x-y)}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(t,\xi_{j})\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|{\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle}^{-\varepsilon}\left(:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{M}^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:-:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:\right)(t,x)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{N\leq|\xi_{1}|,\dotsc,|\xi_{l}|\leq M}\left\langle{\xi_{1}+\dotsb+\xi_{l}}\right\rangle^{-2\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(t,\xi_{j})\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{t}\int_{|\xi_{1}|,\dotsc,|\xi_{l}|\geq N}\left\langle{\xi_{1}+\dotsb+\xi_{l}}\right\rangle^{-2\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\left\langle{\xi_{j}}\right\rangle^{-2}\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}\lesssim N^{-2\theta}.

for every
0<\theta<\varepsilon
. Integrating in space and time, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{M}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:-\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}H_{x}^{-\varepsilon}}^{2}\right]\lesssim_{p,\rho,t}N^{-2\theta},

from which, by hypercontractivity

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{M}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:-\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{W^{-\varepsilon,p}}^{p}\right]\lesssim_{p,\rho,t}N^{-p\theta},

and, arguing as in (ii),

\left\|{\left\|{\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{M}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:-\rho(x):\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t}W_{x}^{-\varepsilon,q}([-T,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\lesssim_{\max(p,q,r),\rho,T}N^{-\theta}.

(iv)

Using the formula (
2.5
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(s,x)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt::\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,y)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:\right]

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{E}[\psi_{N}(s,x)\psi_{N}(t,y)]^{l}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{s\wedge t}\int_{B_{N}}\frac{\sin((s-t^{\prime})|\xi|)\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot(x-y)}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\right)^{l}.

Therefore, calling

\gamma_{N}(s,t;\xi):=\int_{0}^{s\wedge t}\frac{\sin((s-t^{\prime})|\xi|)\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\lesssim_{s,t}\left\langle{\xi}\right\rangle^{-2}

and proceeding as in (iii), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(s,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x)\right)\left(\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\sim\int_{|\xi_{1}|,\dotsc,|\xi_{l}|\leq N}\left\langle{\xi_{1}+\dotsb+\xi_{l}}\right\rangle^{-2\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(s,t;\xi_{j})\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\left(\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t+h,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x)\right)-\left(\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x)\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\begin{multlined}\sim\int_{|\xi_{1}|,\dotsc,|\xi_{l}|\leq N}\left\langle{\xi_{1}+\dotsb+\xi_{l}}\right\rangle^{-2\varepsilon}\\
\times\left(\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(t+h,t+h;\xi_{j})-2\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(t,t+h;\xi_{j})+\prod_{j=1}^{l}\gamma(t,t;\xi_{j})\right)\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}.\end{multlined}.

Interpolating between

\gamma(t+h,t+h;\xi)-\gamma(t,t+h;\xi),\gamma(t,t+h;\xi)-\gamma(t,t;\xi)\lesssim_{t}|h|\left\langle{\xi}\right\rangle^{-1}

and

\gamma(t+h,t+h;\xi)-\gamma(t,t+h;\xi),\gamma(t,t+h;\xi)-\gamma(t,t;\xi)\lesssim_{t}\left\langle{\xi}\right\rangle^{-2},

we get

\gamma(t+h,t+h;\xi)-\gamma(t,t+h;\xi),\gamma(t,t+h;\xi)-\gamma(t,t;\xi)\lesssim_{t}|h|^{\theta}\left\langle{\xi}\right\rangle^{-2+\theta}

for every
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, so choosing
\theta<2\varepsilon
, by the discrete Liebnitz formula,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|(\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t+h,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:)(x)-\left(\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi_{N}(t,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x)\right)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{t,l}\int_{\xi_{1},\dotsc,\xi_{l}}\left\langle{\xi_{1}+\dotsb+\xi_{l}}\right\rangle^{-2\varepsilon}|h|^{\theta}\left\langle{\xi_{1}}\right\rangle^{-2+\theta}\prod_{j=2}^{l}\left\langle{\xi_{j}}\right\rangle^{-2}\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}\lesssim_{t,l}|h|^{\theta}.

Since this inequality passes to the limit, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\left(\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi(t+h,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x)\right)-\left(\left\langle{\nabla}\right\rangle^{-\varepsilon}:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi(t,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:(x)\right)\right|^{2}\lesssim_{t}|h|^{\theta}

for a.e.
\!t
. Integrating and using hypercontractivity, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\rho(\cdot)\left(:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi(t+h,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:-:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi(t,\cdot)^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:\right)}\right\|_{W^{-\varepsilon,p}}^{p}\right]\lesssim_{t,p}|h|^{\frac{p\theta}{2}}.

Moreover, if
|t|\leq T
, the implicit constant
C
can be chosen as
C=C(T,p)
. Therefore, by Kolmogorov Continuity Theorem, for
|t|<T
,
we have that
\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:\in C^{\alpha}W^{-\varepsilon,p}
, for every

\alpha<\frac{p\theta}{2(p-1)}
, so
\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{l}}\hskip-2.0pt:\in C_{t}W^{-\varepsilon,p}
a.s..

(v)

In order to prove this, we just need to prove

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{I_{N}(\rho\psi)}\right\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\right]\leq C(T,\rho,m)^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\log^{\frac{p}{2}}N,

then (v) will follow from a straightforward application of Chebishev inequality.

Proceeding as in (
2.5
), we have that

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\mathbb{E}[|I_{N}(\rho\psi)|^{2}(t,x)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\iint m_{N}(\xi+\eta)\hat{\psi}(\xi)\hat{\rho}(\eta)e^{i2\pi(\xi+\eta)\cdot x}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta\Big|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\iiint m_{N}(\xi+\eta_{1})\overline{m_{N}(\xi+\eta_{2})}\gamma(t,\xi)\hat{\rho}(\eta_{1})\overline{\hat{\rho}(\eta_{2})}e^{i2\pi(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})\cdot x}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}\eta_{1}\mathrm{d}\eta_{2},

which is the inverse Fourier transform of the function
F:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
,

F(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\hat{\rho}(\eta_{1})\overline{\hat{\rho}(\eta_{2})}\int m_{N}(\xi+\eta_{1})\overline{m_{N}(\xi+\eta_{2})}\gamma(t,\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,

restricted to the plane
\{(x,-x)\}
. We have that
\gamma(t,\xi)\lesssim_{t}\left\langle{\xi}\right\rangle^{-2}
, and
that for
n\geq 1
,
\partial_{\eta}^{n}m_{N}(\xi+\eta)\lesssim_{m,n}\left\langle{\xi+\eta}\right\rangle^{-n}
, therefore calling

\phi(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}):=\int m_{N}(\xi+\eta_{1})\overline{m_{N}(\xi+\eta_{2})}\gamma(t,\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi,

we have that

\left\|{\nabla^{n}_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}\phi}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{n,m}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\log N&\text{if }n=0\\
1&\text{if }n\geq 1\end{array}\right.,

hence
\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{C^{k}}\lesssim_{t,k}\log N
. Since
\hat{\rho}(\eta_{1})\overline{\hat{\rho}(\eta_{2})}
is a Schwartz function of
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
, this implies that

(2.8)

\mathbb{E}[|I_{N}(\rho\psi)|^{2}(t,x)]\lesssim_{T,\rho,m}\frac{\log N}{\left\langle{x}\right\rangle^{4}}.

By hypercontractivity, since
I_{N}(\rho\psi)
is gaussian, one gets that

(2.9)

\mathbb{E}[|I_{N}(\rho\psi)|^{p}(t,x)]\leq C(T,\rho,m)^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\frac{\log^{\frac{p}{2}}N}{\left\langle{x}\right\rangle^{4p}},

hence, integrating,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{I_{N}(\rho\psi)}\right\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}^{p}\right]\leq C(T,\rho,m)^{p}p^{\frac{p}{2}}\log^{\frac{p}{2}}N.

∎

3.
Local well-posedness for the localized equation

Take a compactly supported
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and consider the equation

(3.1)

\begin{gathered}v_{tt}-\Delta v+v^{3}+3v^{2}\rho\psi+3v\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt:+\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:=0,\\
v(t_{0},x)=u_{0}(x)\hskip 28.45274pt\partial_{t}v(t_{0},x)=u_{1}(x).\end{gathered}

Notice that (at least formally)

v^{3}+3v^{2}\rho\psi+3v\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt:+\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:=:\hskip-2.0pt{(v+\psi)^{3}}\hskip-2.0pt:

whenever
\rho=1
.

Consider as well the mild formulation of (
3.1
)

(3.2)

v(t)=\cos((t-t_{0})|\nabla|)u_{0}+\frac{\sin((t-t_{0})|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}\\
\times\left(v^{3}+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\binom{3}{j}v^{j}\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3-j}}\hskip-2.0pt:\right).

The following local well-posedness result holds:

Proposition 3.1

(Local Well-Posedness)
.

Let
1>s\geq\frac{2}{3}
, and let
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{H}^{s}
. Than there exists a time

\tau=\tau(t_{0},\omega,\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})>0
a.s., nonincreasing in
\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}
, such that the equation (
3.2
) has a unique solution
in the space
C([t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}+\tau];H^{s})
. Moreover, a.s. in
\omega
, we have that
\inf_{|t_{0}|<T}\tau(t_{0},\omega,\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})>0

for every
T<\infty
.

Remark 3.2
.

For the sake of simplicity, in this work we will use just Sobolev embeddings to get the required estimates, obtaing the local well-posedness result
in the space
H^{s}
for
s\geq\frac{2}{3}
. Since the constraint coming from Section 5 is stronger than this one, it is enough for our purposes. However, improving the proof by making use of the Strichartz estimates for the wave equation, it is possible to relax the condition to

s>\frac{1}{4}
. See
[
4
]
for this analysis, which can be applied with very little modifications to this problem.

Proof.

This proposition will follow by a standard fixed point argument. Consider the map

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{(u_{0},u_{1})}v

\displaystyle:=\cos((t-t_{0})|\nabla|)u_{0}+\frac{\sin((t-t_{0})|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-t^{\prime})|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}\left(v^{3}+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\binom{3}{j}v^{j}\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3-j}}\hskip-2.0pt:\right).

defined on functions
v\in C_{t}([t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}+\tau];H^{s})
.
By the Sobolev embedding
H^{s}\to H^{\frac{2}{3}}\to W^{0+,6-}\to L^{6}
, and using that
s-1<0
and the fact that
\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{k}}\hskip-2.0pt:
is compactly supported, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|{\Gamma_{(u_{0},u_{1})}v}\right\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+2\tau\left\|{v^{3}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s-1}_{x}}+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\binom{3}{j}\left\|{v^{j}\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3-j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s-1}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+2\tau\left\|{v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{6}}^{3}+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\binom{3}{j}\left\|{v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}W^{0+,6-}}^{j}\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3-j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}W^{0-,6+}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+2\tau\left\|{v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{3}+\sum_{j=0}^{2}\binom{3}{j}\left\|{v}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H^{s}_{x}}^{j}\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{3-j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}W^{0-,6+}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\tau\left\|{v}\right\|_{C_{t}H^{s}}^{3}+(1+\left\|{v}\right\|_{C_{t}H^{s}}^{2})\max_{1\leq j\leq 3}\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}W^{0-,6+}_{x}}.

By Proposition
2.1
,
\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}W^{0-,6+}_{x}}
is finite a.s. (locally in time), so it is
possibile to find
\tau\ll 1
such that
\max_{1\leq j\leq 3}\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}W^{0-,6+}_{x}}\ll 1
.
Therefore, for
\tau
small enough, we have that if
\left\|{v}\right\|_{C_{t}H^{s}}\leq 2\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}
,
then
\left\|{\Gamma_{u_{0},u_{1}}v}\right\|_{C_{t}H^{s}}\leq 2\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}
.

Proceeding similarly, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|{\Gamma_{u_{0},u_{1}}v-\Gamma_{u_{0},u_{1}}w}\right\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau\left\|{v-w}\right\|_{C_{t}H^{s}}(\left\|{v}\right\|^{2}_{C_{t}H^{s}}+\left\|{w}\right\|^{2}_{C_{t}H^{s}})

\displaystyle+\left\|{v-w}\right\|_{C_{t}\mathcal{H}^{s}}(1+\left\|{v}\right\|_{C_{t}\mathcal{H}^{s}}+\left\|{w}\right\|_{C_{t}\mathcal{H}^{s}})\max_{j=1,2}\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}W^{0-,6+}_{x}}.

for the same reason, choosing
\tau\ll 1
we have that
\Gamma_{(u_{0},u_{1})}
is a contraption on
B_{2\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}}\subseteq C_{t}\mathcal{H}^{s}
.

In order to finish the proof of this proposition, we just need to show that

\inf_{|t_{0}|<T}\tau(t_{0},\omega,\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})>0

for every
T>0
. Notice that, by the previous argument, in order to have that

\Gamma_{u_{0},u_{1}}
is a contraption, we just need that
\tau,\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}\mathcal{H}^{s-1}_{x}}<\delta

for a certain fixed
\delta
. By Proposition
2.1
, we have that

\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}W^{0-,6+}_{x}([-T-1,T+1]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}<+\infty
a.s.. Therefore, we have that (when
\tau<1
),

\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}W^{0-,6-}_{x}([t_{0}-\tau,t_{0}+\tau]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|{\rho:\hskip-2.0pt{\psi^{j}}\hskip-2.0pt:}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}W^{0-,6+}_{x}([-T-1,T+1]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},

therefore for fixed
T
,
\tau
can be chosen independently from
t_{0}
, and we have
\inf_{|t_{0}|<T}\tau(t_{0},\omega,\left\|{(u_{0},u_{1})}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}})>0
.
∎

Proposition 3.3

(Blow up condition)
.

Let
T^{*}
be the maximal time for which the solution to (
3.2
) exists in the interval
[0,T^{*})
, in the sense that
(v,v_{t})\in C([0,T^{*});\mathcal{H}^{s})
and for every
\delta>0
, there is no function
(\tilde{v},\tilde{v}_{t})
such that
(v,v_{t})\in C([0,T^{*}+\delta);H^{s})
which solves (
3.2
). Suppose that
T^{*}<+\infty
. Then we have

(3.4)

\lim_{s\to T^{*}}\left\|{(v(s),v_{t}(s))}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}=+\infty.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
\lim_{s\to T^{*}}\left\|{v(s),v_{t}(s)}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}<+\infty
. Then by continuity in time,

\sup_{s<T^{*}}\left\|{v(s),v_{t}(s)}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}=M<+\infty.

Let
\tau^{\prime}=\inf_{|t_{0}|<T^{*}}\tau(t_{0},\omega,M),
which is positive by Proposition
3.1
. Consider the equation (
3.2
) starting from time
t_{0}=T^{*}-\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{2}
, with initial data
(v(t_{0}),v_{t}(t_{0}))
. By Proposition
3.1
again, this will have a unique solution

(\bar{v},\bar{v}_{t})\in C([t_{0}-\tau^{\prime},t_{0}+\tau^{\prime}];\mathcal{H}^{s})=C\Big(\Big[T^{*}-\frac{3}{2}\tau^{\prime},T^{*}+\frac{1}{2}\tau^{\prime}\Big];\mathcal{H}^{s}\Big),

which by uniqueness will coincide with
v
in the interval
[T^{*}-\frac{3}{2}\tau^{\prime},T^{*})
.
Therefore, defining

(\tilde{v},\tilde{v}_{t})(s)=\left\{\begin{aligned} (v,v_{t})(s)&&\text{ for }0\leq s<T^{*},\\

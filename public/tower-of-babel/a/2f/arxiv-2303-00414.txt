Singularity Models for High Codimension Mean Curvature Flow in Riemannian Manifolds
Huy T. Nguyen and Artemis A. Vogiatzi

Abstract
We study the mean curvature flow of smooth n n -dimensional compact submanifolds with quadratic pinching in a Riemannian manifold 𝒩 n + m \mathcal{N}^{n+m} . Our main focus is on the case of high codimension, m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 . We establish a codimension estimate that shows in regions of high curvature, the submanifold becomes approximately codimension one in a quantifiable way. This estimate enables us to prove at a singular time of the flow, there exists a rescaling that converges to a smooth codimension-one limiting flow in Euclidean space. Under a cylindrical type pinching, this limiting flow is weakly convex and moves by translation. Our approach relies on the preservation of the quadratic pinching condition along the flow and a gradient estimate that controls the mean curvature in regions of high curvature. These estimates allow us to analyse the behaviour of the flow near singularities and establish the existence of the limiting flow.

Introduction

Let
F_{0}\colon\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{n+m}
be a smooth immersion of a compact manifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
. The mean curvature flow starting from
F_{0}
is the following family of submanifolds

\displaystyle F\colon\mathcal{M}^{n}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{n+m}

such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}F(p,t)&=H(p,t),\ \ \text{for}\ \ p\in\mathcal{M},t\in[0,T)\\
F(p,0)&=F_{0}(p)\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(1.1)

where
H(p,t)
denotes the mean curvature vector of
\mathcal{M}_{t}=F_{t}(p)=F(p,t)
at
p
. It is well known this is a system of quasilinear weakly parabolic partial differential equations for
F
. Geometrically, the mean curvature flow is the steepest descent flow for the area functional of a submanifold and hence it is a natural curvature flow.

In the case of codimension one, a crucial step in the study of singularity formation in the mean convex mean curvature flow is the convexity estimate. This states that in regions of large mean curvature, the second fundamental form is almost positive definite.

In the paper Huisken
[
9
]
proved that closed convex hypersurfaces under the mean curvature flow evolve into spherical singularities, using Stampacchia iteration, the Michael–Simons–Sobolev inequality together with recursion formulae for symmetric polynomials. In
[
10
]
, Huisken then generalises this theorem to Riemannian background curvature spaces with strict convexity depending on the background curvature.

In contrast, White
[
33
,
34
]
uses compactness theorems of geometric measure theory together with the rigidity of strong maximum principle for the second fundamental form. Haslhofer-Kleiner
[
8
]
developed an alternative approach to White’s results based on Andrews’s non-collapsing
[
2
]
result for the mean curvature flow.

The case of mean curvature flow of mean convex hypersurfaces in Euclidean space has been investigated by White
[
33
]
and Huisken-Sinestrari
[

[

Therefore, we will focus on conditions on the norm of the second fundamental form. In high codimension, the mean curvature flow is more complex than in the hypersurface case, where there is only one normal direction. In the hypersurface setting, the second fundamental form is a symmetric real-valued two-tensor, and the mean curvature is a real-valued function, which simplifies the analysis of the flow. However, the presence of normal curvature complicates reaction terms in the evolution equations for the second fundamental form, making the analysis of high codimension mean curvature flow more challenging.

An alternative condition was introduced by Andrews–Baker in
[
3
]
. On a compact submanifold, if
|H|>0
, there exists a
c>0
, such that

|A|^{2}\leq c|H|^{2},

(1.2)

which is preserved by codimension one mean curvature flow. Also, this condition makes sense for all codimensions. In fact, Andrews–Baker showed that for

for
n<4

) is preserved along the mean curvature flow. For

, remarkably they were able to prove convergence to a round sphere. We note the condition
|A|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-1}|H|^{2}<0,H>0
implies convexity in codimension one. This lead Andrews–Baker to consider the pinching condition:

|A|^{2}-c_{n}|H|^{2}+d_{n}\leq 0,

(1.3)

which, is preserved by mean curvature flow, for
c_{n}\leq\frac{4}{3n}
and
d_{n}>0
. In the paper
[
23
]
, a surgery construction was developed allowing high codimension mean curvature flow with cylindrical pinching to pass through singularities. This generalised the codimension one result of
[
13
]
(see also
[
8
]
) to high codimension. A key aspect of this surgery procedure is the codimension estimate presented in
[
20
]
, which shows that near regions of high curvature, singularities become approximately codimension one. Another crucial component is the cylindrical estimate, which shows that nears regions of high curvature, the submanifold becomes approximately cylindrical of the form
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. These estimates are essential for the surgery to work and allow us to control the geometry of the submanifold in regions of high curvature.

In this paper, we study singularity formation in high codimension mean curvature flow in Riemannian manifolds and will consider the following curvature pinching condition of the length of the second fundamental form

\displaystyle|A|^{2}-c_{n}|H|^{2}\leq-d_{n}(K_{1},K_{2},L)

for some positive constant

depending on the background curvature, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}-K_{1}\leq K_{\mathcal{N}}\leq K_{2},\quad|\bar{\nabla}\bar{R}|\leq L,\quad\inj(\mathcal{N})\geq\imath_{\mathcal{N}}.\end{split}

and

\displaystyle c_{n}:=\min\left\{\frac{4}{3n},\frac{1}{n-2}\right\},\quad\text{ if }n\geq 5.

This was shown to be preserved in the paper of
[

and represents a natural generalisation of Huisken’s condition in
[

]
to high codimension background Riemannian manifolds. We will show in regions of high curvature where the mean curvature is large, the submanifold becomes approximately codimension one in a quantifiable sense. In particular, we will prove a theorem that extends of the main theorem of
[
20
]
to Riemannian background spaces.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
F:\mathcal{M}^{n}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{n+m}
be a smooth solution to mean curvature flow (
1.1
) so that

F_{0}(p)=F(p,0)
is compact and quadratically pinched.
Then
\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists H_{0}>0
, such that if
f\geq H_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\left|A^{-}\right|^{2}\leq\varepsilon f+C_{\varepsilon}

\forall t\in[0,T)
where
C_{\varepsilon}=C_{\varepsilon}(n,m)
.

Assuming the quadratic pinching condition, we prove singularity models for the pinched flow must always have codimension one, regardless of the original flow’s codimension.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In section 2, we give all the technical tools needed for our work and set up our notation. In section 3, we give the proof for the preservation of the quadratic pinching condition along the mean curvature flow. In section 4, we prove the gradient estimate. The importance of the gradient estimate is that it allows us to control the mean curvature and hence the full second fundamental form on a neighbourhood of fixed size. In section 5, we prove the codimension estimate, which is the main theorem of this paper. This means that in regions of high curvature, the submanifold becomes codimension one quantitatively. In section 6, we show how the codimension estimate in Riemannian manifolds actually falls into the Euclidean case. Finally, in section 7, we prove the codimension estimate in the case of constant negative curvature.

4
Gradient Estimate

This section presents a proof of the gradient estimate for the mean curvature flow. We establish this estimate directly from the quadratic curvature bound
|A|^{2}<c_{n}|H|^{2}-d_{n}
, where
c\leq\frac{4}{3n}
, without relying on the asymptotic cylindrical estimates. In fact, we demonstrate the cylindrical estimates follow as a consequence of the gradient estimates we derive here. These estimates are pointwise gradient estimates that rely solely on the mean curvature (or, equivalently, the second fundamental form) at a point and not on the maximum of curvature, as is the case with more general parabolic-type derivative estimates. Specifically, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{3}{n+2}-c>0.

This inequality enables us to combine the derivative terms in the evolution equation of
|A|^{2}-c_{n}|H|^{2}+d_{n}
with the Kato-type inequality from Lemma
2.3
.

Theorem 4.1

(cf.
[
13
]
, Section 6)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{t},t\in[0,T)
be a closed
n
-dimensional quadratically bounded solution to the mean curvature flow in the Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{N}^{n+m}
, with
n\geq 8
, that is

\displaystyle|A|^{2}-c|H|^{2}+d<0,|H|>0

with
c=\frac{1}{n-2}
.
Then, there exists a constant
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{1}(n,\mathcal{M}_{0})
and a constant
\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{2}(n,\mathcal{M}_{0})
, such that the flow satisfies the uniform estimate

\displaystyle|\nabla A|^{2}\leq\gamma_{1}|A|^{4}+\gamma_{2},

for every
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

We choose here
\kappa_{n}=\left(\frac{3}{n+2}-c\right)>0
. Since
n\geq 8
,
\kappa_{n}
is strictly positive. We will consider here the evolution equation for

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}},

where
g=c|H|^{2}-|A|^{2}-d>0
. Since
|A|^{2}-c|H|^{2}<0,|H|>0
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is compact, there exists an
\eta(\mathcal{M}_{0})>0,C_{\eta}(\mathcal{M}_{0})>0
, so that

\displaystyle\left(c-\eta\right)|H|^{2}-|A|^{2}\geq C_{\eta}>0.

(4.1)

Hence, we set

\displaystyle g=c|H|^{2}-|A|^{2}\geq\eta|H|^{2}>\frac{\eta}{c}|A|^{2}>\varepsilon_{1}|A|^{2}+\varepsilon_{2},

where
\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{\eta}{c}
and
\varepsilon_{2}>0
. From (
3.3
) and (
2.3
) in Theorem
3.1
and a suitable constant
d
, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g

\displaystyle=\Delta g-2\left(c|\nabla H|^{2}-|\nabla A|^{2}\right)+2\left(cR_{2}-R_{1}\right)+P_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\Delta g-2\left(\Big(\frac{3}{n+2}-\eta\Big)^{-1}c-1\right)|\nabla A|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\Delta g-2\Big(\frac{n+2}{3}c-1\Big)|\nabla A|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta g+2\kappa_{n}\frac{n+2}{3}|\nabla A|^{2},

for a suitable positive constant
\eta
. The evolution equation for
|\nabla A|^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)|\nabla A|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+c|A|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+d|\nabla A|^{2}.

Let
w,z
satisfy the evolution equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w=\Delta w+W,\quad\partial_{t}z=\Delta z+Z

then, we find

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\frac{w}{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{z}\left\langle\nabla\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),\nabla z\right\rangle+\frac{W}{z}-\frac{w}{z^{2}}Z

\displaystyle=2\frac{\langle\nabla w,\nabla z\rangle}{z^{2}}-2\frac{w|\nabla z|^{2}}{z^{3}}+\frac{W}{z}-\frac{w}{z^{2}}Z.

Furthermore, for any function
g
, we have by Kato’s inequality

\displaystyle\langle\nabla g,\nabla|\nabla A|^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\nabla g||\nabla^{2}A||\nabla A|\leq\frac{1}{g}|\nabla g|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+g|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}.

We then get

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{g}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+\frac{2}{g}\left\langle\nabla g,\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}\right)\right\rangle\leq-\frac{2}{g}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}-\frac{2}{g^{3}}|\nabla g|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+\frac{2}{g^{2}}\langle\nabla g,\nabla|\nabla A|^{2}\rangle\leq 0.

Then, if we let
w=|\nabla A|^{2}
and
z=g
, with
W\leq-2|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+c|A|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+d|\nabla A|^{2}
and
Z\geq 2\kappa_{n}\frac{n+2}{3}|\nabla A|^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{g}\left\langle\nabla g,\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}\right)\right\rangle+\frac{1}{g}(-2|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+c|A|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}

\displaystyle+d|\nabla A|^{2})-2\kappa_{n}\frac{n+2}{3}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{g^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c|A|^{2}\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}+d\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}-2\kappa_{n}\frac{n+2}{3}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{g^{2}}.

We repeat the above computation with
w=\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g},z=g,

\displaystyle W\leq c|A|^{2}\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}+d\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}-2\kappa_{n}\frac{n+2}{3}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{g^{2}}

and
Z\geq 0
, to get

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{g}\left\langle\nabla g,\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{g}\left(c|A|^{2}\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}+d\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}-2\kappa_{n}\frac{n+2}{3}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{g^{2}}\right).

The nonlinearity then is

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}\left(c|A|^{2}+d-\frac{2\kappa_{n}(n+2)}{3}\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}\right).

Since

\displaystyle g>\varepsilon_{1}|A|^{2}+\varepsilon_{2},

there exists a constant
N
, such that

\displaystyle Ng\geq c|A|^{2}+d.

Hence, by the maximum principle, there exists a constant (with
\eta,\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}
chosen sufficiently small so that N is sufficiently large, this estimate holds at the initial time), such that

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}\leq\frac{3N}{2\kappa_{n}(n+2)}.

Therefore, we see there exists a constant
\mathcal{C}=\frac{3N}{2\kappa_{n}(n+2)}=\mathcal{C}(n,\mathcal{M}_{0})
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}\leq\mathcal{C}

and from the definition of
g
, we get the result of the lemma.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{t},t\in[0,T)
be a solution of the mean curvature flow with surgery and normalised initial data. Then there exists constants
\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4}
depending only on the dimension, so that

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}\leq\gamma_{3}|A|^{6}+\gamma_{4},

(4.2)

for any
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

We have the following evolution equation

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2|\nabla^{3}A|^{2}+k_{1}|A|^{2}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+k_{2}|A||\nabla A|^{2}|\nabla^{2}A|

\displaystyle\leq-2|\nabla^{3}A|^{2}+\left(k_{1}+\frac{k_{2}}{2}\right)|A|^{2}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+\frac{k_{2}}{2}|\nabla A|^{4}.

We now consider the evolution equation of the term
\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{5}}
. Firstly we see

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|H|^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\Delta|H|^{\alpha}+\alpha|H|^{\alpha-1}(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|H|-\alpha(\alpha-1)|H|^{\alpha-2}|\nabla H|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\Delta|H|^{\alpha}-\alpha(\alpha-1)|H|^{\alpha-2}|\nabla H|^{2},

since
|\nabla|H||^{2}\geq|\nabla H|^{2}
and
|H|>0
. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{5}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|H|^{5}}\left(-2|\nabla^{3}A|^{2}+\left(k_{1}+\frac{k_{2}}{2}\right)|A|^{2}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+\frac{k_{2}}{2}|\nabla A|^{4}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{20|H|^{3}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}}{|H|^{10}}+\frac{2}{|H|^{10}}\left\langle\nabla|H|^{5},\nabla|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{2|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{15}}|\nabla|H|^{5}|^{2}.

We have the terms

\displaystyle\frac{20|H|^{3}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}}{|H|^{10}}-\frac{2|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{15}}|\nabla|H|^{5}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{20|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}-\frac{50|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}|\nabla|H||^{2}}{|H|^{7}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{20|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}|\nabla H|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}.

and

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|H|^{10}}\left\langle\nabla|H|^{5},\nabla|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{10\left\langle\nabla|H|,\nabla|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}\right\rangle}{|H|^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{20\langle|\nabla H|,|\nabla^{2}A||\nabla^{3}A|\rangle}{|H|^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|H|^{5}}|\nabla^{3}A|^{2}+\frac{100|\nabla H|^{2}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}.

Together with the gradient estimate, Theorem
4.1
this gives the following evolution equation

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{5}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{|\nabla^{3}A|^{2}}{|H|^{5}}+k_{3}\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{3}}+\frac{120|\nabla H|^{2}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}+\frac{k_{2}}{2}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{|H|^{5}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{|\nabla^{3}A|^{2}}{|H|^{5}}+k_{4}\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{3}}+C_{1}\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}+\frac{k_{5}|H|^{8}+C_{2}}{|H|^{5}}.

Similar computations give us

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{|H|^{3}}\leq-\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{3}}+\frac{k_{6}|H|^{8}+C_{3}}{|H|^{5}},

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}\leq-\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}+\frac{k_{7}|H|^{8}+C_{4}}{|H|^{9}}.

We now set

\displaystyle f=\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{5}}+N\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{|H|^{3}}+M\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}-\kappa\sqrt{c|H|-|A|^{2}},

and so we have

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)f

\displaystyle\leq k_{4}\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{3}}+k_{5}|H|^{3}+C_{1}\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}+\frac{C_{2}}{|H|^{5}}

\displaystyle-N\frac{|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{3}}+Nk_{6}|H|^{3}+\frac{NC_{3}}{|H|^{5}}

\displaystyle-\frac{M|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}}{|H|^{7}}+\frac{k_{7}M}{|H|}+\frac{C_{4}M}{|H|}-\kappa\varepsilon_{0}|H|^{3}.

Therefore, we choose

\displaystyle N>k_{4},\quad\varepsilon_{0}\kappa>Nk_{6}+k_{5},\quad M>C_{1}.

Since
H>0
, there exists a constant
\alpha_{1}
, such that
|H|\geq\alpha_{1}
and we find

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)f\leq C_{5}

which implies

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{M}_{t}}f\leq\max_{\mathcal{M}_{t_{0}}}f+C_{5}\left(t-t_{0}\right).

Given the bound on the maximal time of existence, we have

\displaystyle f\leq C(n),

which implies

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}\leq c(n)|H|^{6}+C(n)|H|^{5}.

Applying the quadratic pinching, we get (
4.2
). ∎

Higher order estimates on
\left|\nabla^{m}A\right|
for all

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}A\right|=|\Delta A+A*A*A|\leq C|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+C|A|^{3}\leq c_{1}|A|^{3}+c_{2}.

5
Codimension Estimates

In this section, we want to show in regions of high curvature, the submanifold becomes approximately codimension one in a quantifiable sense. Our goal is to separate the second fundamental form in the principal direction and the second fundamental form in the other directions and compute their evolution equations separately. Later, we find estimates for the reaction and gradient terms as well as for the lower order terms, which appear due to the Riemannian ambient space. Then, we start by computing the evolution equation of the quantity
\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}
, which since in the limit the background space is Euclidean, the result will follow from the maximum principle. The theorem we will prove is the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
F:\mathcal{M}^{n}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{n+m}
be a smooth solution to mean curvature flow so that

F_{0}(p)=F(p,0)
is compact and quadratically pinched.
Then
\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists H_{0}>0
, such that if
f\geq H_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\left|A^{-}\right|^{2}\leq\varepsilon f+C_{\varepsilon}

\forall t\in[0,T)
where
C_{\varepsilon}=C_{\varepsilon}(n,m)
.

5.1
The Evolution Equation of
|A^{-}|^{2}

We start by computing the evolution equation of
|A^{-}|^{2}
. We define the tensor
A^{-}
by

\displaystyle A^{-}(X,Y)=A(X,Y)-\frac{\langle A(X,Y),H\rangle}{|H|^{2}}H,

for vector fields
X,Y
tangent to
\mathcal{M}_{t}
. The tensor
A^{-}
is well defined, since
|H|>0
. Therefore, we will need to compute the evolution equations of
|A|^{2}
and
\frac{|\langle A,H\rangle|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}.
Using (
2.5
) and the quotient rule, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}

\displaystyle-\Delta\Big)\frac{\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}=|H|^{-2}\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|H|^{-2}\sum_{k}\Big\langle\nabla_{k}|H|^{2},\nabla_{k}\frac{\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle-|H|^{-4}\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}\Big(-2|\nabla^{\bot}H|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}+2\sum_{k,\alpha,\beta}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}H^{\alpha}H^{\beta}\Big).

Before computing the evolution equation of
\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}
, we simplify the other terms. In particular, using
\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}=|H|^{2}|h|^{2}
and

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\bot}H|^{2}=|H|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla|H||^{2},

we write

\displaystyle 2|H|^{-4}\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}H|^{2}=2|h|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}+2|H|^{-2}|h|^{2}|\nabla|H||^{2},

\displaystyle-2|H|^{-4}\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{4}=-2|h|^{4},

\displaystyle 2|H|^{-4}\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}\sum_{k,\alpha,\beta}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}H^{\alpha}H^{\beta}=2|h|^{2}|H|^{-2}\sum_{k,\alpha,\beta}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}H^{\alpha}H^{\beta}.

As for the remaining gradient terms, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}|H|^{2}=2\langle\nabla_{k}^{\bot}H,H\rangle

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}(|H|^{-2}\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2})=\nabla_{k}|h|^{2}=2\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}\nabla_{k}h_{ij}.

Therefore, since
H=|H|\nu_{1}
and
\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1},\nu_{1}\rangle=0
, we have

\displaystyle 2|H|^{-2}\sum_{k}\Big\langle\nabla_{k}|H|^{2},\nabla_{k}\frac{\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=8|H|^{-2}\sum_{i,j,k}\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}H,H\rangle h_{ij}\nabla_{k}h_{ij}

\displaystyle=8|H|^{-1}\sum_{i,j,k}\nabla_{k}|H|h_{ij}\nabla_{k}h_{ij}.

To summarise, we have shown so far that

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\frac{\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}

\displaystyle=|H|^{-2}\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}-2|h|^{4}+2|h|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|H|^{-2}|h|^{2}|\nabla|H||^{2}+8|H|^{-1}\sum_{i,j,k}\nabla_{k}|H|h_{ij}\nabla_{k}h_{ij}

\displaystyle-2|h|^{2}|H|^{-2}\sum_{k,\alpha,\beta}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}H^{\alpha}H^{\beta}.

For the evolution equation of
\langle A_{ij},H\rangle
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

The evolution equation of
|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}
is

\displaystyle|H|^{-2}\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=4|\mathring{h}_{ij}A_{ij}^{-}|^{2}+2|R_{ij}^{\bot}(\nu_{1})|^{2}+4|h|^{4}-4|H|^{-1}\mathring{h}_{ij}\nabla_{k}|H|\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij}^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle-4\mathring{h}_{ij}\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij}^{-},\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1}\rangle-4|h|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1}|^{2}-2|H|^{-2}|h|^{2}|\nabla|H||^{2}

\displaystyle-8|H|^{-1}\nabla_{k}|H|h_{ij}\nabla_{k}h_{ij}-2|\nabla h|^{2}+2B^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}-4\langle\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1}),\mathring{h}_{ip}A^{-}_{jp}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A^{-}_{ip}\rangle,

where

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=2|H|^{-2}\bar{R}_{ipjq}\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2|H|^{-2}\bar{R}_{kjkp}\langle A_{pi},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle+|H|^{-2}A^{\alpha}_{ij}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+|H|^{-2}H^{\alpha}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}A_{ij}^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2|H|^{-2}A^{\alpha}_{jp}\bar{R}_{ip\alpha\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2|H|^{-2}A^{\alpha}_{ip}\bar{R}_{jp\alpha\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle+|H|^{-2}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{R}_{kij\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-|H|^{-2}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{R}_{jkk\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle.

\displaystyle-2\langle A_{ip},A_{jq}\rangle\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+2\bar{R}_{ipjq}\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2\bar{R}_{kjkp}\langle A_{pi},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+A^{\alpha}_{ij}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}\langle\nu_{\beta},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2A^{\alpha}_{jp}\bar{R}_{ip\alpha\beta}\langle\nu_{\beta},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2A^{\alpha}_{ip}\bar{R}_{jp\alpha\beta}\langle\nu_{\beta},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

Putting the above equations together and keeping in mind that

we have,

\displaystyle\Big(\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\langle A_{ij},H\rangle\Big)\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle=2\langle A_{ij},A_{pq}\rangle\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+2\langle A_{iq},A_{pq}\rangle\langle A_{pj},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2\langle A_{ip},A_{jq}\rangle\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+2\bar{R}_{ipjq}\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2\bar{R}_{kjkp}\langle A_{pi},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+A^{\alpha}_{ij}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2A^{\alpha}_{jp}\bar{R}_{ip\alpha\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2A^{\alpha}_{ip}\bar{R}_{jp\alpha\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle+\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{R}_{kij\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{R}_{jkk\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle+H^{\alpha}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}A_{ij}^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij},\nabla^{\bot}_{k}H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle.

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=2\bar{R}_{ipjq}\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2\bar{R}_{kjkp}\langle A_{pi},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle+A^{\alpha}_{ij}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+H^{\alpha}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}A_{ij}^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2A^{\alpha}_{jp}\bar{R}_{ip\alpha\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2A^{\alpha}_{ip}\bar{R}_{jp\alpha\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle+\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{R}_{kij\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{R}_{jkk\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle.

We use the Uhlenbeck’s trick to suppose that we are in an orthogonal frame. That is, suppose
g^{ij}=\delta_{ij}
remains orthogonal along the flow. More precisely, for any
e_{i},e_{j}
orthonormal, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g^{ij}=\partial_{t}\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle=0.

Therefore, excluding the time derivative of the inverse of the metric, which is the term

\displaystyle 2\big(\partial_{t}g^{ij}\big)g^{pq}\langle A_{ip},H\rangle\langle A_{jq},H\rangle,

we have

\displaystyle\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\Big(\Big(\partial_{t}-\Delta\Big)\langle A_{ij},H\rangle\Big)\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2|\nabla\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=4\langle A_{ij},A_{pq}\rangle\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+4\langle A_{iq},A_{pq}\rangle\langle A_{pj},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-4\langle A_{ip},A_{jq}\rangle\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-4\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\bot}H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2|\nabla\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}+2B.

(5.1)

To finish the proof, we multiply
|H|^{-2}
and then rewrite each of the remaining terms using
A=A^{-}+h\nu_{1}
. For the first term on the first line of (
5.1
), we have

\displaystyle 4|H|^{-2}\langle A_{ij},A_{pq}\rangle\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle=4|H|^{-2}|H|^{2}h_{ij}h_{pq}\langle A_{ij},A_{pq}\rangle

\displaystyle=4|h|^{4}+4h_{ij}h_{pq}\langle A_{ij}^{-},A_{pq}^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=4|h|^{4}+4\mathring{h}_{ij}\mathring{h}_{pq}\langle A_{ij}^{-},A_{pq}^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=4|h|^{4}+4|\mathring{h}_{ij}A_{ij}^{-}|^{2}.

(5.2)

Also, B can be rewritten as

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=2|H|^{-2}\bar{R}_{ipjq}\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2|H|^{-2}\bar{R}_{kjkp}\langle A_{pi},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle+|H|^{-2}A^{\alpha}_{ij}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle+|H|^{-2}H^{\alpha}\bar{R}_{k\alpha k\beta}A_{ij}^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle-2|H|^{-2}A^{\alpha}_{jp}\bar{R}_{ip\alpha\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-2|H|^{-2}A^{\alpha}_{ip}\bar{R}_{jp\alpha\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle+|H|^{-2}\bar{\nabla}_{k}\bar{R}_{kij\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-|H|^{-2}\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{R}_{jkk\beta}H^{\beta}\langle A_{ij},H\rangle.

In higher codimension, the fundamental Gauss, Codazzi and Ricci equations on Riemannian manifold in local frame take the form

\displaystyle R_{ijpq}=\bar{R}_{ijpq}+A^{\alpha}_{ip}A^{\alpha}_{jq}-A^{\alpha}_{jp}A^{\alpha}_{iq},

\displaystyle(\nabla^{\bot}_{i}A)^{\alpha}_{jp}-(\nabla^{\bot}_{j}A)^{\alpha}_{ip}=-\bar{R}_{ijp\alpha},

and

\displaystyle R^{\bot}_{ij\alpha\beta}=\bar{R}_{ij\alpha\beta}+A^{\alpha}_{ip}A^{\beta}_{jp}-A^{\beta}_{ip}A^{\alpha}_{jp}.

Define a vector-valued version of the normal curvature by

\displaystyle R^{\bot}_{ij}(\nu_{\alpha})=R^{\bot}_{ij\alpha\beta}\nu_{\beta}=\bar{R}_{ij\alpha\beta}\nu_{\beta}+A^{\alpha}_{ip}A^{\beta}_{jp}-A^{\beta}_{ip}A^{\alpha}_{jp}\nu_{\beta}.

(5.3)

In particular, we note that
R^{\bot}_{ij}(\nu_{1})=\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})+h_{ip}A^{\beta}_{jp}-{h}_{jp}A^{\beta}_{ip}
, which in view of

\displaystyle A_{ij}=A^{-}_{ij}+h_{ij}\nu_{1}=A^{-}_{ij}+\mathring{h}_{ij}\nu_{1}+\frac{1}{n}|H|g_{ij}\nu_{1},

gives

\displaystyle R^{\bot}_{ij}(\nu_{1})=\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})+\mathring{h}_{ip}A^{-}_{jp}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A^{-}_{ip}.

(5.4)

For the difference of second and third term of (
5.1
), we notice the resemblance to
|R_{ij}^{\bot}(\nu_{1})|^{2}
in (
5.4
). We compute

\displaystyle|\mathring{h}_{ip}A_{jp}^{-}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A_{ip}^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|h_{ip}A_{jp}-h_{jp}A_{ip}|^{2}=\langle h_{ip}A_{jp}-h_{jp}A_{ip},h_{iq}A_{jq}-h_{jq}A_{iq}\rangle

\displaystyle=2h_{ip}h_{iq}\langle A_{jp},A_{jq}\rangle-2h_{ip}h_{jq}\langle A_{jp},A_{iq}\rangle

\displaystyle=2|H|^{-2}\big(\langle A_{jp},A_{jq}\rangle\langle A_{ip},H\rangle\langle A_{iq},H\rangle-\langle A_{jp},A_{iq}\rangle\langle A_{ip},H\rangle\langle A_{jq},H\rangle\big).

(5.5)

Therefore,

\displaystyle|R_{ij}^{\bot}(\nu_{1})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}+2|H|^{-2}\big(\langle A_{jp},A_{jq}\rangle\langle A_{ip},H\rangle\langle A_{iq},H\rangle-\langle A_{jp},A_{iq}\rangle\langle A_{ip},H\rangle\langle A_{jq},H\rangle\big)

\displaystyle+2\langle\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1}),\mathring{h}_{ip}A^{-}_{jp}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A^{-}_{ip}\rangle.

After reindexing (e.g.
j\to p\to q\to i\to j
on the second term and
j\to i\to q\to j,p\to p
on the third term), this gives

\displaystyle 2|R_{ij}^{\bot}(\nu_{1})|^{2}

\displaystyle=2|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}+4|H|^{-2}\big(\langle A_{ip},A_{pq}\rangle\langle A_{jq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle-\langle A_{ip},A_{jq}\rangle\langle A_{pq},H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle\big)

\displaystyle+4\langle\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1}),\mathring{h}_{ip}A^{-}_{jp}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A^{-}_{ip}\rangle.

Thus, we have shown the reaction terms of our lemma statement are correct. For the gradient terms, it follows from the identities

\displaystyle\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij},\nu_{1}\rangle=\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij}^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle+\nabla_{k}h_{ij},

\displaystyle\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij},\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1}\rangle=\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij}^{-},\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1}\rangle+h_{ij}|\nabla_{k}^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2},

(5.6)

\displaystyle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}H=\nabla_{k}|H|\nu_{1}+|H|\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle-4|H|^{-2}\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij},\nabla^{\bot}_{k}H\rangle\langle A_{ij},H\rangle

\displaystyle=-4|H|^{-1}h_{ij}\nabla_{k}|H|\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij},\nu_{1}\rangle-4|H|^{-1}h_{ij}\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij},\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-4|H|^{-1}\mathring{h}_{ij}\nabla_{k}|H|\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij}^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle-4|H|^{-1}h_{ij}\nabla_{k}|H|\nabla_{k}h_{ij}

\displaystyle-4\mathring{h}_{ij}\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}A_{ij}^{-},\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1}\rangle-4|h|^{2}|\nabla_{k}^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2},

(5.7)

\displaystyle-2|H|^{-2}|\nabla\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2|H|^{-2}|\nabla(|H|h_{ij})|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2|H|^{-2}|h|^{2}|\nabla|H||^{2}-2|\nabla h|^{2}-4|H|^{-1}h_{ij}\nabla_{k}|H|\nabla_{k}h_{ij},

since
A^{-}_{ii}=0
, meaning that it’s trace free. Combining (

Substituting the result of the above lemma into our equation for the evolution of

where

(5.9)

As for the gradient terms, taking the form of

\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{3(n+1)}{2n(n+2)}
, then

\displaystyle 2\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)\geq\frac{3}{2}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+6|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Proof.

Using

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\bot}A|^{2}=\sum_{i,j,k}|\hat{\nabla}^{\bot}_{i}A^{-}_{jk}+h_{jk}\nabla^{\bot}_{i}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{i}A^{-}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle+\nabla_{i}h_{jk}|^{2}

and

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\bot}H|^{2}=|H|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}+|\nabla|H||^{2},

(5.30)

we have

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}A^{-}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle+\nabla_{i}h_{jk}|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla|H\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j,k}|\hat{\nabla}_{i}^{\perp}A^{-}_{jk}+h_{jk}\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}-c_{n}|H|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}|\nabla_{i}h_{jk}+\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{i}A^{-}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{i,j,k}|\nabla_{i}\mathring{h}_{jk}+\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{i}A^{-}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}|\nabla|H||^{2}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{i}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{i,j,k}|\nabla_{i}\mathring{h}_{jk}+\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{i}A^{-}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}.

Also, since

\displaystyle\frac{2(n-1)}{n(n+2)}|\nabla|H||^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{i}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2},

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}A^{-}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle+\nabla_{i}h_{jk}|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla|H||^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1})|^{2}-\frac{nc_{n}-1}{n}|\nabla|H||^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{(n+2)\left(nc_{n}-1\right)}{2(n-1)}\right)\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}.

In view of (
5.19
) and (
5.27
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}|\hat{\nabla}_{i}^{\bot}A^{-}_{jk}+h_{jk}\nabla_{i}^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle-c_{n}|H|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}\geq\left(\frac{3}{n+2}-c_{n}\right)|H|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{nc_{n}-1}\left(\frac{3}{n+2}-c_{n}\right)(f+|A^{-}|^{2}+|\mathring{h}|^{2}+d_{n})|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{nc_{n}-1}\left(\frac{3}{n+2}-c_{n}\right)f|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Thus, by the three previous computations, we have

\displaystyle 2\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(2-\frac{(n+2)\left(nc_{n}-1\right)}{n-1}\right)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2n}{nc_{n}-1}\left(\frac{3}{n+2}-c_{n}\right)|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

If
c_{n}\leq\frac{4}{3n}
, then
nc_{n}-1\leq\frac{1}{3}
and

\displaystyle 2-\frac{(n+2)\left(nc_{n}-1\right)}{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq 2-\frac{n+2}{3(n-1)}=\frac{5n-8}{3(n-1)},

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{nc_{n}-1}\left(\frac{3}{n+2}-c_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 6n\left(\frac{9n-4(n+2)}{3n(n+2)}\right)=\frac{10n-16}{n+2}.

This establishes the first inequality of the lemma.
If
c_{n}\leq\frac{3(n+1)}{2n(n+2)}
, then
nc_{n}-1\leq\frac{n-1}{2(n+2)}
and

\displaystyle 2-\frac{(n+2)\left(nc_{n}-1\right)}{n-1}\geq 2-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{3}{2},

\displaystyle\frac{2n}{nc_{n}-1}\left(\frac{3}{n+2}-c_{n}\right)\geq\frac{4n(n+2)}{n-1}\left(\frac{6n-3(n+1)}{2n(n+2)}\right)=6.

This establishes the second inequality of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.10

(Upper bound for gradient term of
\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)|A^{-}|^{2}
)
.

1.

If
\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{4}{3n}
, then

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{5n-9}{3(n-1)}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{3(n-1)}{n-3}f|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2(n+2)}{n+3}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

2.

If
\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{3(n+1)}{2n(n+2)}-\varepsilon_{0}
and
\varepsilon=\frac{2n(n+2)}{3(n-1)}\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+(1-\varepsilon)\frac{3}{2}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+4f|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+2|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Proof.

Using the definition of
Q_{ijk}
, we get

\displaystyle|Q|\leq|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|+|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|+|H|^{-1}|\mathring{h}||\nabla|H||

\displaystyle\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}|\nabla|H||^{2}\leq\frac{n(n+2)}{2(n-1)}\frac{1}{3n}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{n+2}{6(n-1)}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}.

\displaystyle|\langle A^{-},\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla^{\bot}_{i}\nu_{1}\rangle^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j,k}\sum_{\beta\geq 2}(A^{\beta}_{ij})^{2}\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1},\nu_{\beta}\rangle^{2}

a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}>0

\displaystyle 4|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle||A^{-}||\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq 2a_{1}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{1}}|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2},

5.33

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2a_{1}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\left(2a_{2}+2a_{3}\frac{n+2}{6(n-1)}\right)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{a_{1}}|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{2}}f|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{3}}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

In this case,

Plugging these into (
5.1

Lemma 5.11

(Gradient term estimate)
.

Suppose either
n\geq 8,\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{4}{3n}
and
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{5n-8}
or
\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{3(n+1)}{2n(n+2)}-\varepsilon_{0}
, and
0<\delta\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{2n(n+2)}{3(n-1)}\varepsilon_{0}\right\}
. Then in either case,

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle\leq 2|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right).

Proof.

First suppose
n\geq 8,\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{4}{3n}
and
0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{5n-8}
. Expanding
|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}
using

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\bot}A^{-}|^{2}=|\hat{\nabla}^{\bot}A^{-}|^{2}+|\langle\nabla^{\bot}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

(5.36)

and using the inequality (
5.30
) in Lemma
5.8
gives us

\displaystyle 2|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2|\hat{\nabla}^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{4n-10}{n+2}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{6(n-1)}{n+2}(|A^{-}|^{2}+f+d_{n})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Multiplying the first result in Lemma
5.9
by
(1-\delta)
and using that
1-\delta\geq\frac{1}{2}
on the coefficient of
|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}
gives

\displaystyle 2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}(\left|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\delta)\frac{5n-8}{3(n-1)}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{5n-8}{n+2}|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Putting these together, we get

\displaystyle 2|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle+2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)\geq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\delta)\frac{5n-8}{3(n-1)}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{11n-14}{n+2}|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{6(n-1)}{n+2}(f+d_{n})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{4n-10}{n+2}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

On the other hand, the first result of Lemma
5.10
gives us that

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{5n-9}{3(n-1)}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{3(n-1)}{n-3}f|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2(n+2)}{n+3}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Therefore, it only remains to compare the coefficients of like terms in the two inequalities above. For the coefficients of
\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}
, we need

\displaystyle\frac{5n-9}{3(n-1)}\leq(1-\delta)\frac{5n-8}{3(n-1)}\Longleftrightarrow\delta\leq\frac{1}{5n-8}.

Comparing the coefficients of the remaining terms implies we need

\displaystyle 2n+4\leq 11n-14\Longleftrightarrow 2\leq n,

\displaystyle n+2\leq 2(n-3)\Longleftrightarrow 8\leq n

and

\displaystyle 2(n+2)^{2}\leq(4n-10)(n+3)\Longleftrightarrow 19\leq n(n-3).

Each of these inequalities is true if
n\geq 8
completing the proof for the first case.
Now suppose
\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}<\frac{3(n+1)}{2n(n+2)}
and
0<\delta<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{2n(n+2)}{3(n-1)}\varepsilon_{0}\right\}
. Arguing as before, this time using the second result in Lemma
5.8
and the second result in Lemma
5.9
yields

\displaystyle 2\left|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-c_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)\geq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\delta)\frac{3}{2}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+7|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+4(f+d_{n})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Note we again used
\delta\leq\frac{1}{2}
to simplify the coefficient of
|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}
. On the other hand, by the second result in Lemma
5.10
, we have

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+(1-\delta)\frac{3}{2}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+f|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle+4(f+d_{n})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+2|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

where recall
\varepsilon=\frac{2n(n+2)}{3(n-1)}\varepsilon_{0}
. Using the assumption that
\delta\leq\varepsilon
, this completes the proof of the lemma for the second case.
∎

. Let

\frac{|A|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}<c_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n-2}
we can only have

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}

which gives
\frac{|A|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}=\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n-1}\neq\frac{1}{n-1}+\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon>0
which gives a contradiction.

∎

The case of Constant Curvature

In this chapter, we prove Theorem
5.1
in the case of constant curvature. In the case of constant negative curvature, the proof is more straightforward and more quantitative so we give a direct proof of the statement.

8.1
Evolution equations

We start by stating the evolution equations for the length and the squared length of the second fundamental form and the mean curvature vector in the case of constant curvature. We denote
\bar{K}
to be the sectional curvature and with respect to local orthonormal frames
\{e_{i}\}
and
\{\nu_{\alpha}\}
for the tangent and normal bundles,

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)A_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,q,\beta}A_{ij}^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\beta}A_{pq}+\sum_{p,q,\beta}A_{iq}^{\beta}A_{qp}^{\beta}A_{pj}+\sum_{p,q,\beta}A_{jq}^{\beta}A_{qp}^{\beta}A_{pi}-2\sum_{p,q,\beta}A_{ip}^{\beta}A_{jq}^{\beta}A_{pq}

\displaystyle+2\bar{K}Hg_{ij}-n\bar{K}A_{ij},

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)H

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,q,\beta}H^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\beta}A_{pq}+n\bar{K}H.

From these equations we can compute

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|A|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2|\nabla A|^{2}+2|\langle A,A\rangle|^{2}+2|R^{\perp}|^{2}+4\bar{K}|H|^{2}-2n\bar{K}|A|^{2},

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|H|^{2}

\displaystyle=-2|\nabla H|^{2}+2|\langle A,H\rangle|^{2}+2n\bar{K}|H|^{2}.

Here we show the preservation of pinching for high codimension submanifolds of hyperbolic space. To prove the codimension estimate we need good estimates for the reaction terms in this equation. These are proven following Andrews–Baker.

We assume throughout
H\not=0
. We first observe

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,p,q}|\langle A_{ij},A_{pq}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=|h|^{4}+2\sum_{i,j}|h_{ij}A^{-}_{ij}|^{2}+|A^{-}|^{4}

\displaystyle=|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{4}+\frac{2}{n}|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|H|^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}|H|^{4}+2\sum_{i,j}|h_{ij}A^{-}_{ij}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j,p,q}|\langle A^{-}_{ij},A^{-}_{pq}\rangle|^{2}

and recall the identity

\displaystyle|R^{\perp}|^{2}=2\sum_{i,j,p}|h_{ip}A^{-}_{pj}-h_{jp}A^{-}_{pi}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j,p}|A^{-}_{ip}\otimes A^{-}_{pj}-A^{-}_{jp}\otimes A^{-}_{pi}|^{2}

in order to express

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{4}+\frac{2}{n}|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|H|^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}|H|^{4}+2\sum_{i,j}|h_{ij}A^{-}_{ij}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j,p,q}|\langle A^{-}_{ij},A^{-}_{pq}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i,j,p}|h_{ip}A^{-}_{pj}-h_{jp}A^{-}_{pi}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j,p}|A^{-}_{ip}\otimes A^{-}_{pj}-A^{-}_{jp}\otimes A^{-}_{pi}|^{2}.

Andrews–Baker establish the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}|h_{ij}A^{-}_{ij}|^{2}+\sum_{i,j,p}|h_{ip}A^{-}_{pj}-h_{jp}A^{-}_{pi}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|A^{-}|^{2},

and also observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,p,q}|\langle A^{-}_{ij},A^{-}_{pq}\rangle|^{2}+\sum_{i,j,p}|A^{-}_{ip}\otimes A^{-}_{pj}-A^{-}_{jp}\otimes A^{-}_{pi}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}|A^{-}|^{4}

by setting
B^{\alpha}=A^{-,\alpha}
for
\alpha\geq 2
in the following result
[
16
, Theorem 1]
:

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\{B^{\alpha}\}
be a finite set of symmetric
(n\times n)
-matrices. Then we have

\sum_{i,j,\alpha,\beta}(B^{\alpha}_{ij}B^{\beta}_{ij})^{2}+\sum_{i,j,p,\alpha,\beta}|B^{\alpha}_{ip}B^{\beta}_{pj}-B^{\alpha}_{jp}B^{\alpha}_{pi}|^{2}\leq\frac{3}{2}\Big(\sum_{\alpha}|B^{\alpha}|^{2}\Big)^{2}.

Putting these estimates together we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{4}+\frac{2}{n}|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|H|^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}|H|^{4}+4|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|A^{-}|^{2}+\frac{3}{2}|A^{-}|^{4}.

We may also expand

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}|\langle A_{ij},H\rangle|^{2}=|h|^{2}|H|^{2}=|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|H|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}|H|^{4},

hence

\displaystyle 2R_{1}-2c_{n}R_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{4}+8|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|A^{-}|^{2}+3|A^{-}|^{4}-2(c_{n}-2/n)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|H|^{2}-\frac{2}{n}(c_{n}-1/n)|H|^{4}.

Now we express

\mathcal{Q}=|\accentset{\circ}{A}|^{2}-(c_{n}-1/n)|H|^{2}-d_{n}\bar{K}

and rearrange to obtain

|H|^{2}=\frac{1}{c_{n}-1/n}(|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+|A^{-}|^{2}-\mathcal{Q}-d_{n}\bar{K}).

Substituting this back in gives

\displaystyle 2R_{1}-2c_{n}R_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{4}+8|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|A^{-}|^{2}+3|A^{-}|^{4}-2(c_{n}-1/n)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|H|^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{n}|A^{-}|^{2}|H|^{2}+\frac{2}{n}(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|H|^{2}

\displaystyle=6|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|A^{-}|^{2}+3|A^{-}|^{4}-\frac{2}{n}|A^{-}|^{2}|H|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{n}(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|H|^{2}+2(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(6-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|A^{-}|^{2}+\bigg(3-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|A^{-}|^{4}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{n}(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|H|^{2}+2(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|A^{-}|^{2}.

The terms on the last line can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|H|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\mathcal{Q}(|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+|A^{-}|^{2}-\mathcal{Q})-\frac{4/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}\bar{K}\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle+\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}\bar{K}(|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+|A^{-}|^{2})-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}^{2}\bar{K}^{2},

hence

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|H|^{2}+2(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}(\mathcal{Q}+d_{n}\bar{K})|A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\mathcal{Q}(2|A^{-}|^{2}-\mathcal{Q})-\frac{4/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}\bar{K}\mathcal{Q}+\bigg(\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}+2\bigg)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}+2\bigg)d_{n}\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+\frac{4/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}\bar{K}|A^{-}|^{2}-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}^{2}\bar{K}^{2}

and we have

\displaystyle 2R_{1}-2c_{n}R_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(6-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|A^{-}|^{2}+\bigg(3-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|A^{-}|^{4}

\displaystyle+\bigg(\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}+2\bigg)d_{n}\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+\frac{4/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}\bar{K}|A^{-}|^{2}-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}^{2}\bar{K}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\mathcal{Q}(2|A^{-}|^{2}-\mathcal{Q})-\frac{4/n}{c_{n}-1/n}d_{n}\bar{K}\mathcal{Q}+\bigg(\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}+2\bigg)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}\mathcal{Q}.

Next we compute

\displaystyle-2n\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{A}|^{2}-2n\bar{K}(c_{n}-1/n)|H|^{2}

\displaystyle=-4n\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}-4n\bar{K}|A^{-}|^{2}+2nd_{n}\bar{K}^{2}+2n\bar{K}\mathcal{Q},

and so obtain the following estimate for the zeroth-order terms in the evolution of
\mathcal{Q}
:

\displaystyle 2R_{1}-2

\displaystyle c_{n}R_{2}-2n\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{A}|^{2}-2n\bar{K}(c_{n}-1/n)|H|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(6-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}|A^{-}|^{2}+\bigg(3-\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|A^{-}|^{4}

\displaystyle+2\bigg(\frac{d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}+d_{n}-2n\bigg)\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+4\bigg(\frac{d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}-n\bigg)\bar{K}|A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\bigg(n-\frac{d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)d_{n}\bar{K}^{2}+2\bigg(1+\frac{1/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}Q

\displaystyle+\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\mathcal{Q}(2|A^{-}|^{2}-\mathcal{Q})+2\bigg(n-\frac{2d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)\bar{K}\mathcal{Q}.

(8.1)

Suppose
\bar{K}<0
. In this case, if
d_{n}>0
then the condition
\mathcal{Q}\leq 0
implies
|H|^{2}>0
. As above, for
c_{n}\leq\frac{4}{3n}
we have

\displaystyle 2R_{1}-2

\displaystyle c_{n}R_{2}-2n\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{A}|^{2}-2n\bar{K}(c_{n}-1/n)|H|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\frac{d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}+d_{n}-2n\bigg)\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}+4\bigg(\frac{d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}-n\bigg)\bar{K}|A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\bigg(n-\frac{d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)d_{n}\bar{K}^{2}+2\bigg(1+\frac{1/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}Q

\displaystyle+\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\mathcal{Q}(2|A^{-}|^{2}-\mathcal{Q})+2\bigg(n-\frac{2d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)\bar{K}\mathcal{Q}.

The first term on the left is nonpositive for

so we have

\displaystyle 2R_{1}-2

\displaystyle c_{n}R_{2}-2n\bar{K}|\accentset{\circ}{A}|^{2}-2n\bar{K}(c_{n}-1/n)|H|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4(2/c_{n}-n)\bar{K}|A^{-}|^{2}+2(n-2/c_{n})d_{n}\bar{K}^{2}+2\bigg(1+\frac{1/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)|\accentset{\circ}{h}|^{2}Q

\displaystyle+\frac{2/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\mathcal{Q}(2|A^{-}|^{2}-\mathcal{Q})+2\bigg(n-\frac{2d_{n}/n}{c_{n}-1/n}\bigg)\bar{K}\mathcal{Q}.

All of the terms on the right are either nonpositive or carry a factor

8.2
The evolution of
h

From the equations for
A
and
H
, we have that the projection
\langle A,H\rangle
satisfies

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)A_{ij}^{\alpha}H^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{p,\alpha}\nabla_{p}A_{ij}^{\alpha}\nabla_{p}H^{\alpha}+2\sum_{p,q,\alpha,\beta}H^{\alpha}A_{ij}^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\sum_{p,q,\alpha,\beta}H^{\alpha}(A_{iq}^{\beta}A_{qp}^{\beta}A_{pj}^{\alpha}+A_{jq}^{\beta}A_{qp}^{\beta}A_{pi}^{\alpha}-2A_{ip}^{\beta}A_{jq}^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\alpha})

The first of the reaction terms can be split into a hypersurface and a codimension component, as follows:

\displaystyle 2\sum_{p,q,\alpha,\beta}H^{\alpha}A_{ij}^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)A_{ij}^{\alpha}H^{\alpha}

(8.2)

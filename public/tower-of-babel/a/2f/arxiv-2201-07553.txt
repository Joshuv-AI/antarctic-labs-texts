Internal and external partial difference families and cyclotomy
Sophie Huczynska, Laura Johnson

Abstract
We introduce the concept of a disjoint partial difference family (DPDF) and an external partial difference family (EPDF), a natural generalization of the much-studied disjoint difference family (DDF), external difference family (EDF) and partial difference set (PDS). We establish properties and indicate connections to other recently-studied combinatorial structures. We show how DPDFs and EPDFs may be formed from PDSs, and present various cyclotomic constructions for DPDFs and EPDFs. As part of this, we develop a unified cyclotomic framework, which yields some known results on PDSs, DDFs and EDFs as special cases.

Introduction

Difference sets and difference families (sets, or families of sets, in which every non-zero group element occurs with constant frequency as an internal difference within the sets) were introduced in the 1930s and have been very well-explored in the literature. Difference families are useful for constructing balanced incomplete block designs via development (see
[
9
]
,
[
34
]
). Disjoint difference families (difference families whose sets are disjoint) have recently received particular attention
[
5
]
: there is design theory motivation for asking whether it is always possible to find disjoint base blocks for designs (see Novak’s conjecture and its generalization in
[
27
]
,
[
13
]
), and there are information theory applications (
[
26
]
). In the early 2000s, external difference families were introduced (
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
), in which each non-zero element occurs with constant frequency as an external difference between distinct sets. These structures have strong links to cryptography. Finally, the concept of a partial difference set (a set in which each non-zero element occurs with one of two frequencies according to whether it lies in the set) is a natural generalization of a difference set, which arises naturally and has various useful applications (
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
)).

However the partial analogue of a difference family has not previously been studied. Related ideas are present in the literature; for example, work has been done on structures called almost difference families (
[
12
]
), where the non-identity elements occur with two frequencies
\lambda
and
\lambda+1
; here the sets need not be disjoint and there is no structural condition determining the sets of elements which occur with each frequency. A specialized version of an external partial difference family was introduced in
[
10
]
, in which the sets partition the non-zero group elements and the two frequencies are associated with membership/non-membership of unions of the sets in the family. Finally, ideas of the type explored in this paper occur implictly in
[
25
]
, where the authors seek bounded versions of external difference families in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
(called difference systems of sets) and use conditions on internal differences to guarantee lower-bounded external differences. We set up a more general version of both disjoint difference families and external difference families, precisely analogous to the definition of partial difference set. We will see these objects arise naturally.

Much of our approach will be using cyclotomy in finite fields. There is a long history of using cyclotomic methods to construct internal and external difference families, dating back to
[
34
]
; see for example
[
1
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
33
]
and
[
35
]
. Difference families whose sets are cyclotomic classes are known as radical (
[
4
]
).

Throughout, we let
G
be a group, written additively, and let
G^{*}
denote
G\setminus\{0\}
. For a subset
D
of
G
, we define the multiset
\Delta(D)=\{x-y:x\neq y\in D\}
and for disjoint sets
D_{1},D_{2}\subseteq G
, we define the multiset
\Delta(D_{1},D_{2})=\{x-y:x\in D_{1},y\in D_{2}\}
. For a subset
D
of
G
and non-negative integer
\lambda
, we denote by
\lambda D
the multiset comprising
\lambda
copies of each element of
D
.

The following definitions are well-known (see
[
29
]
,
[
22
]
); we follow the notation of
[
29
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
.

(i)

A subset
D
of
G
, with cardinality
k
, forms an
(n,k,\lambda)
-Difference Set (DS) if the following multiset equation holds:

\Delta(D)=\lambda(G^{*}).

(ii)

A subset
P
of
G
, with cardinality
k
, forms an
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
-Partial Difference Set (PDS) if the following multiset equation holds:

\Delta(P)=\lambda(P)+\mu(G^{*}\backslash{P}).

Note this is a Difference Set if
\lambda=\mu
. We shall say that a PDS is
proper
if
\lambda\neq\mu
.

(iii)

A collection of
m
disjoint
k
-subsets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},...,D_{m}\}
in
G
forms an
(n,m,k,\lambda)
-Disjoint Difference Family (DDF) of
G
if the following multiset equation holds;

\cup_{i=1}^{m}\Delta(D_{i})=\lambda(G^{*}).

If we do not require the subsets to be pairwise disjoint, this structure is called a Difference Family (DF).

(iv)

A collection of
m
disjoint
k
-subsets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
in G forms an
(n,m,k,\lambda)
-External Difference Family (EDF) of G if the following multiset equations holds;

\cup_{i,j:i\neq j}\Delta(D_{i},D_{j})=\lambda(G^{*}).

(v)

A collection of
m
disjoint
k
subsets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
in G forms an,
(n,m,k,\lambda)
-Strong External Difference Family (SEDF) of G if the following multiset equation holds for each
D_{i}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
;

\cup_{j:j\neq i}\Delta(D_{i},D_{j})=\lambda(\rm{G}^{*}).

If the set-sizes are allowed to vary, with
|A_{i}|=k_{i}
, and
\lambda
is replaced by
\lambda_{i}
in the above equation, then the structure is called a Generalised Strong External Difference Family (GSEDF).

(vi)

A collection of
m
disjoint subsets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
in G, comprising
c_{h}
subsets of size
k_{h}
(
1\leq h\leq\ell
) with
m=\sum_{i}c_{i}
, forms an
(n,m;c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell};k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell};\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
-Partitioned External Difference Family (PEDF) of G if the following multiset equations holds for each
1\leq h\leq\ell
:

\cup_{i:|A_{i}|=k_{h}}\cup_{j:j\neq i}\Delta(D_{i},D_{j})=\lambda_{i}(G^{*}).

Both EDFs and GSEDFs are special cases of PEDFs.

We make the following new definitions.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be a collection of
m
disjoint subsets
\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
in
G^{*}
, where each
D_{i}
has cardinality
k
, and let
S=\cup_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}
. Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is said to form an
(n,m,k,\lambda,\mu)
Disjoint Partial Difference Family (DPDF) of
G
if the following multiset equation holds:

\cup_{i=1}^{m}\Delta(D_{i})=\lambda S+\mu(G^{*}\setminus S).

If
\lambda\neq\mu
then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is called
proper
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be a collection of
m
disjoint subsets
\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
in
G^{*}
, where each
D_{i}
has cardinality
k
, and let
S=\cup_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}
. Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is said to form an
(n,m,k,\lambda,\mu)
External Partial Difference Family (EPDF) of
G
if the following multiset equation holds:

\cup_{i,j:i\neq j}\Delta(D_{i},D_{j})=\lambda S+\mu(G^{*}\setminus S).

If
\lambda\neq\mu
then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is called
proper
.

In general we consider
S\neq G^{*}
but we adopt the convention that taking
S=G^{*}
corresponds to the situation when
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a DDF or EDF.

If
\lambda=\mu
, we obtain a DDF and EDF respectively in the above definitions. If
m=1
we obtain a PDS in Definition
1.2
. Some special cases of DPDFs give examples of almost difference families (see
[
12
]
), while EPDFs give examples of bounded external difference families (see
[
29
]
). Finally, these objects (and the cyclotomic construction approach) are also related to supplementary difference sets
[
32
]
. We note that the internal and external partial difference families defined in this paper are not related to the concept of a difference family being partial due to having a short base block.

Example 1.4
.

Let
G=(GF(13),+)
. Consider the subsets
D_{1}=\{1,3,9\}
and
D_{2}=\{4,10,12\}
and let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},D_{2}\}
, so
S=\{1,3,4,9,10,12\}
. Then the multiset
\Delta(D_{1})\cup\Delta(D_{2})
consists of two copies of
\{2,5,6,7,8,11\}
(the complement of
S
in
GF(13)^{*}
). The multiset of external differences
\Delta(D_{1},D_{2})\cup\Delta(D_{2},D_{1})
comprises two copies of
S
and one copy of its complement. Hence
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a
(13,2,3,0,2)
-DPDF and a
(13,2,3,2,1)
-EPDF.

In this paper, we establish properties and exhibit constructions for DPDFs and EPDFs and show connections to other recently-studied combinatorial structures. Section 2 presents basic results. From Section 3 onwards, we focus on DPDFs and EPDFs in finite fields
GF(q)
. We develop a cyclotomic framework which allows us to obtain structural information and thereby derive results beyond those obtainable solely from specific values of cyclotomic numbers. Let
C_{i}^{e}
denote the cyclotomic class of order
e
; we focus on two main questions:

(i)

Is (a subset of)
\{C_{0}^{e},C_{1}^{e},\ldots C_{e-1}^{e}\}
a DPDF or EPDF if
C_{0}^{e}
is a PDS?

(ii)

Is
\{C_{0}^{e},C_{\epsilon}^{e},\ldots C_{e-\epsilon}^{e}\}
a DPDF or EPDF if
C_{0}^{\epsilon}=\cup_{j=0}^{e/\epsilon-1}C_{j\epsilon}^{e}
is a PDS (including DS)?

We also investigate conditions under which a collection of disjoint
\cup_{i\in I}C_{i}^{e}
forms a DPDF or EPDF. We prioritise structures which are simultaneously DPDFs and EPDFs; where possible, we aim to establish DPDF/EPDF results whose parameters may be obtained without direct field element calculation. In so doing, we prove a generalization of a result on PDSs by Calderbank and Kantor
[
6
]
. We also obtain various DPDF/EPDF results for specific values of
e
using values of cyclotomic numbers.

We will indicate in the text when known results occur as special cases of our approach. While none of our proofs depend on computation, the computer algebra system GAP
[
14
]
was used to find and verify examples.

2
Basic results

We begin by establishing some basic definitions and results.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},D_{2},...,D_{m}\}
denote a collection of
m
pairwise disjoint subsets of
G
. We define:

(i)

{\rm Int}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\bigcup\limits^{m}_{i=1}\Delta(D_{i})

(ii)

{\rm Ext}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\bigcup\limits^{m}_{i,j:i\neq{j}}\Delta(D_{i},D_{j})
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
S
be a subset of a group
G
partitioned by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},...,D_{m}\}
, where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a collection of
m
disjoint
k
-element subsets. Then the following multiset equation holds:

{\rm Int}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cup{\rm Ext}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\Delta(S).

It is proved in
[
7
]
that if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
partitions
G
(respectively,
G^{*}
) then it is an EDF if and only if it is a DDF. In fact this holds when
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
partitions any difference set.

Theorem 2.3
.

If
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
partitions an
(n,mk,\lambda)
-difference set
D
then it is an
(n,m,k,\mu)
-DDF if and only if it is an
(n,m,k,\lambda-\mu)
-EDF.

We now ask: what is the situation when an EDF or DDF partitions a PDS? We see that the concepts of DPDF and EPDF arise naturally here:

Theorem 2.4
.

(i)

Let
P
be an
(n,mk,\sigma,\mu)
-Partial Difference Set partitioned by the sets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
. Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,k,\lambda)
-EDF if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a proper
(n,m,k,\sigma-\lambda,\mu-\lambda)
-DPDF.

(ii)

Let
P
be an
(n,mk,\sigma,\mu)
-Partial Difference Set partitioned by the sets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
. Then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,k,\lambda)
-DDF if and only if
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a proper
(n,m,k,\sigma-\lambda,\mu-\lambda)
-EPDF.

Proof.

(i)

Since
P
is an
(n,mk,\sigma,\mu)
-Partial Difference Set partitioned by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
, by Remark
2.2
, this means:

{\rm{Int}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})\cup{\rm Ext}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\sigma(P)+\mu(G^{*}\backslash{P}).

(1)

If
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
forms an
(n,m,k,\lambda)
-EDF, this then implies
\lambda\leq min(\sigma,\mu)
and:

{\rm{Ext}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=\bigcup\limits^{m}_{i,j:i\neq{j}}\Delta(D_{i},D_{j})=\lambda(G^{*}).

(2)

Hence

{\rm{Int}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})=(\sigma-\lambda)(P)+(\mu-\lambda)(G^{*}\backslash{P}).

(3)

Thus
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a proper
(n,m,k,\sigma-\lambda,\mu-\lambda)
-DPDF. The reverse direction is similar.

(ii)

Similar to part (i).

∎

A similar result holds in the more general situation for DPDFs and EDPFs whose union form a difference set or partial difference set.

Theorem 2.5
.

(i)

Let
D
be an
(n,mk,\lambda)
-DS and let
S^{\prime}=\{D_{1},...,D_{m}\}
be a partition of
D
. Then
S^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,k,\sigma,\mu)
-EPDF if and only if
S^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,k,\lambda-\sigma,\lambda-\mu)
-DPDF.

(ii)

Let
P
be an
(n,mk,\lambda,\mu)
-PDS and let
S^{\prime}=\{D_{1},...,D_{m}\}
be a partition of
P
. Then
S^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,k,\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})
-EPDF if and only if
S^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,k,\lambda-\lambda^{*},\mu-\mu^{*})
-DPDF.

An illustration of DPDF and an EPDF partitioning a partial difference set is shown in Example
1.4
, while examples partitioning a difference set are given in Remark
3.25
.

Finally, we observe that an object which is simultaneously a DPDF and an EPDF has the following structural requirement:

Theorem 2.6
.

If
S^{\prime}=\{D_{1},...,D_{m}\}
is a partition of a set
S
, such that
S^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,k,\lambda^{*},\mu^{*})
-DPDF and an
(n,m,k,\lambda,\mu)
-EPDF, then
S
is an
(n,mk,\lambda+\lambda^{*},\mu+\mu^{*})
-PDS when
\lambda+\lambda^{*}\neq\mu+\mu^{*}
and an
(n,mk,\sigma)
-DS when
\lambda+\lambda^{*}=\mu+\mu^{*}=\sigma
.

This motivates our approach to finding structures which are simultaneously DPDFs and EPDFs.

Clearly partial difference sets are of crucial importance when constructing and understanding DPDFs. PDSs have received attention in the literature and various results are known - see
[
22
]
and
[
23
]
. We give a summary of basic results from
[
22
]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
and let
D
be a subset of
G
.

(i)

If D is a difference set then it is a partial difference set; its complement
G\setminus D
is also a difference set and hence a partial difference set.

(ii)

If D is a partial difference set which is not a difference set then
D=-D
.

(iii)

If
D
is a partial difference set with
D=-D
, then
D\setminus\{0\},D\cup\{0\},G\setminus D,(G\setminus D)\setminus\{0\}
and
(G\setminus D)\cup\{0\}
are partial difference sets.

(iv)

If
D
is a non-trivial subgroup of
G
, of order
k
, then it is a
(n,k,k,0)
-PDS.

(v)

If
D
is an
(n,k,\lambda,0)
-PDS with
\lambda\neq 0
then
D\cup\{0\}
is a subgroup of
G
.

A partial difference set
D
in a group
G
is traditionally called
regular
if
D=-D
and
0\not\in D
.

We end this section with another situation where DPDFs naturally arise. In
[
29
]
, it was proven that

Theorem 2.8
.

Suppose
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
is a partition of
G
(where
|G|=n
) such that there are
c_{h}
subsets of size
k_{h}
for

. Then
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
is an
(n,m;c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell};k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell};\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
-PEDF if and only if the subsets of cardinality
k_{h}
form an
(n,c_{h},k_{h},c_{h}k_{h}-\lambda_{h})
-DDF in G for
1\leq h\leq\ell
.

We have the following new result:

Theorem 2.9
.

Suppose
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
is a partition of
{G}^{*}
(where
|{G}|=n
) such that there are
c_{h}
subsets of size
k_{h}
for
1\leq h\leq\ell
. Then
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
is an
(n,m;c_{1},\ldots,c_{\ell};k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell};\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
-PEDF if and only if the subsets of cardinality
k_{h}
form an
(n,c_{h},k_{h},c_{h}k_{h}-\lambda_{h}-1,c_{h}k_{h}-\lambda_{h})
-DPDF in G for

(

]

(

]

\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
partitions

(respectively

is an

(n,k_{i},\lambda_{i},\mu_{i})

2.1
Constructing DPDFs and EPDFs as collections of PDSs

Various types of EDF-like object are obtainable by taking the sets in the family to be appropriate difference sets or partial difference sets. The approach of taking a suitable collection of partial difference sets may be used to construct DPDFs, and if their union has appropriate properties, then an EPDF is also obtained.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
G
be a group of order
n
and let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
be a collection of disjoint
k
-subsets of
G
such that each
D_{i}
is an
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
-PDS. Denote
S=\cup_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}
. Then

(i)

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,\lambda+(m-1)\mu,m\mu)
-DPDF;

(ii)

If
S
forms an
(n,mk,\sigma,\chi)
-PDS (or
(n,mk,\sigma)
-DS) then
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is an
(n,m,k,\sigma-(\lambda+(m-1)\mu),\chi-m\mu)
-EPDF (or
(n,m,k,\sigma-(\lambda+(m-1)\mu),\sigma-m\mu)
-EPDF).

Proof.

(i)

By definition, for each
D_{i}
, we have
\Delta(D_{i})=\lambda(D_{i})+\mu(\rm{G}^{*}\backslash\{D_{i}\})
.
Hence,

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Delta(D_{i})=\Delta(D_{1})\cup\ldots\cup\Delta(D_{m})=(\lambda(D_{1})+\mu(G^{*}\backslash\{D_{1}\}))\cup\ldots(\lambda(D_{m})+\mu(G^{*}\backslash\{D_{m}\}))

=\lambda(D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{m})+(m-1)\mu(D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{m})+m\mu({G}^{*}\backslash\{D_{1}\cup\ldots\cup D_{m}\})

=\lambda(S)+(m-1)\mu(S)+m\mu({G}^{*}\backslash{S})=(\lambda+(m-1)\mu)(S)+m\mu({G}\backslash{S}).

Hence
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
forms an
(n,m,k,\lambda+(m-1)\mu,m\mu)
-DPDF.

(ii)

This follows from Theorem
2.5
.

∎

Example 2.11
.

By Theorem
2.7
, if
H
is a non-trivial subgroup of a group
G
, then
H
and
H\setminus\{0\}
are PDSs. So, if
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
are disjoint equal-sized subgroups of a group

which pairwise intersect only in
\{0\}
, then taking any collection of sets from
\{H_{1}\setminus\{0\},\ldots,H_{r}\setminus\{0\}\}
gives a DPDF.

Various examples of this set-up exist in the literature. In a group
G
of order
n^{2}
, a collection of order-

subgroups
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
such that
H_{i}\cap H_{j}=\{0\}
for
i\neq j
is called an
(n,r)
partial congruence partition of degree
r
in
G
(see
[
17
]
). More generally, for any group
G
, a collection of subgroups
H_{1},\ldots,H_{r}
of
G
such that the
H_{i}\setminus\{0\}
partition

is called a partition of
G
(see
[
16
]
). The groups which admit a subgroup partition have been characterized in
[
36
]
. Subgroup partitions were used in
[
16
]
to construct EDFs.

We now focus on objects which are simultaneously DPDFs and EPDFs.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{S^{\prime}}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}\}
be a family of disjoint

-subsets in a group

such that each

is an
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
-PDS. Let

. If any of the following hold:

(i)

G\setminus S
is a difference set;

(ii)

G\setminus S
is a proper partial difference set;

(iii)

G^{*}\setminus S
is a proper partial difference set;

then
\mathcal{S^{\prime}}
is a DPDF which is also an EPDF.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
2.7
and Theorem
2.10
, since in all cases
S
is either a difference set or a proper partial difference set.
∎

Example 2.14
.

Let

of
i
sets from
A
, where
1\leq i\leq 3
, forms a DPDF and EPDF. Note the union of the sets in
B
is a PDS which is not a DS.

3
Developing a cyclotomic framework

In the rest of the paper, we will obtain constructions for DPDFs and EPDFs via cyclotomy. For further background on cyclotomic definitions and results, see
[
30
]
. Throughout,
q
will denote a prime power and will be expressed as
q=ef+1
where
e,f
are positive integers greater than 1. Note that
q
may be even or odd. The element
\alpha
will denote a primitive element of
GF(q)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let GF
(q)
be a finite field of order
q=ef+1
, where
q
is an arbitrary prime power. Let
\alpha
be a primitive element of GF
(q)
.

(i)

The
cyclotomic classes
of order
e
of GF
(q)^{*}
are defined:

C_{i}^{e}=\alpha^{i}\langle\alpha^{e}\rangle

for
0\leq i\leq e-1
. Each cyclotomic class is of size
f
.

(ii)

For each pair of cyclotomic classes
C_{i}^{e}
and
C_{j}^{e}
of order
e
, we define the
cyclotomic number

(i,j)_{e}
of order
e
to be the number of solutions to the following equation:

z_{i}+1=z_{j}

where
z_{i}\in C_{i}^{e}
and
z_{j}\in C_{j}^{e}
.

The following result from literature directly motivates our approach (see e.g.
[
10
]
,
[
34
]
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
q=ef+1
be a prime power. Then the set of all cyclotomic classes
\{C_{0}^{e},\ldots,C_{e-1}^{e}\}
is a
(q,e,f,f-1)
-DDF and
(q,e,f,q-1-f)
-EDF.

We give the following useful relationship between cyclotomic classes:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
q=ef+1
. If
\epsilon|e
, then for
0\leq j\leq\epsilon-1
,

C_{j}^{\epsilon}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\frac{e}{\epsilon}-1}C_{i\epsilon+j}^{e}.

In particular
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
is partitioned by the cosets
\{C_{i\epsilon}^{e}:0\leq i\leq\frac{\epsilon}{e}-1\}
of
C_{0}^{e}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
C_{0}^{e}
is a subgroup of index
\frac{e}{\epsilon}
in
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
. Hence its cosets in
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
partition
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
, i.e.
C_{0}^{\epsilon}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\frac{e}{\epsilon}-1}C_{i\epsilon}^{e}
.
Then for
0\leq j\leq\epsilon-1
,
C_{j}^{\epsilon}=\alpha^{j}(\bigcup_{i=0}^{\frac{e}{\epsilon}-1}C_{i\epsilon}^{e})=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\frac{e}{\epsilon}-1}C_{i\epsilon+j}^{e}
.
∎

In what follows, we will often consider the situation when the prime power
q=ef+1
is also expressible as
q=\epsilon\rho+1
where
\epsilon|e
(
\epsilon,\rho>1
). For many DPDF and EPDF constructions, we will be interested in the case when
e>\epsilon
, but our results will hold for
e=\epsilon
, and this is useful in analysing the PDS situation.

We will denote the family of sets
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}:=\{C_{i\epsilon}^{e}:0\leq i\leq\frac{\epsilon}{e}-1\}
. Here, the sets of
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
are certain cyclotomic classes of order
e
which partition
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
, where
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
is a cyclotomic class of order
\epsilon
(of larger cardinality if
e>\epsilon
), where
\epsilon
is a divisor of
e
. Note that any coset
\alpha^{i\epsilon}C_{0}^{e}
(
i\in\mathbb{Z}
) represents a set in
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
.

3.1
Internal differences: PDSs and DPDFs

We introduce the following notation and results.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
q=ef+1
and let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
GF(q)
.

(i)

For each
1\leq r\leq f-1
, define

T_{r}:=\{\alpha^{ne}-\alpha^{me}:n-m=r\mod f,\,0\leq n\neq{m}\leq f-1\}.

Clearly,
T_{r}\subseteq\Delta(C_{0}^{e})
; we refer to the set
T_{r}
as a
transversal
of
\Delta(C_{0}^{e})
. Note
|T_{r}|=f
.

(ii)

For each
1\leq r\leq f-1
, let
a_{r}\in\{0,...,e-1\}
be such that
\alpha^{re}-1\in C_{a_{r}}^{e}=\alpha^{a_{r}}C_{0}^{e}
.

This result summarizes some cyclotomic relationships using our notation.

Lemma 3.5
.

(i)

For
1\leq r\leq f-1
, each transversal
T_{r}=(\alpha^{re}-1)C_{0}^{e}
is a copy of the cyclotomic class
C_{a_{r}}^{e}
where
a_{r}\in\{0,...,e-1\}
.

(ii)

\Delta(C_{0}^{e})=\bigcup\limits^{f-1}_{r=1}{T_{r}}=\bigcup\limits_{r=1}^{f-1}C_{a_{r}}^{e}=\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{e-1}(i,0)_{e}(C_{i}^{e})

(iii)

For
0\leq j\leq e-1
,
\Delta(C_{j}^{e})=\alpha^{j}\Delta(C_{0}^{e})=\bigcup\limits^{f-1}_{r=1}\alpha^{j}T_{r}=\bigcup\limits^{f-1}_{r=1}\alpha^{j}C_{a_{r}}^{e}=\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{e-1}(i,0)_{e}(\alpha^{j}C_{i}^{e})
.

From these, together with Theorem
2.10
, we immediately have:

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
q=ef+1
be a prime power.

(i)

For
0\leq i\leq e-1
, each
C_{i}^{e}
is a
(q,\frac{q-1}{e},A,B)
-PDS if and only if there exist
A,B
such that
A=(0,0)_{e}
and
B=(i,0)_{e}
for all
1\leq i\leq e-1
. The PDS is proper precisely when
A\neq B
.

(ii)

Suppose there exist
A,B
such that
A=(0,0)_{e}
and
B=(i,0)_{e}
for all
1\leq i\leq e-1
. Let
I\subset\{0,1,\ldots,e-1\}
(
|I|=u
) and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\{C_{i}^{e}\}_{i\in I}
. Then
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a
(q,u,\frac{q-1}{e},A+(u-1)B,uB)
-DPDF, which is proper precisely when
A\neq B
.

We make a definition whose motivation will be clear from the subsequent proposition.

Definition 3.7
.

For
1\leq r\leq f-1
, we define

D_{r}:=\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{e/\epsilon-1}\alpha^{i\epsilon}T_{r}

We will refer to this as a
diagonal of a transversal
.

Proposition 3.8
.

(i)

{\rm Int}({C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime})=\bigcup\limits_{r=1}^{f-1}D_{r}
.

For each
1\leq r\leq f-1
,

(ii)

D_{r}=(\alpha^{re}-1)C_{0}^{\epsilon}
;

(iii)

D_{r}=\alpha^{i}C_{0}^{\epsilon}=C_{i}^{\epsilon}
, where
\alpha^{re}-1\in C_{i}^{\epsilon}
(
0\leq i\leq\epsilon-1
).

Note that, by (ii) of the above result, our diagonals of transversals of
\Delta(C_{0}^{e})
are themselves transversals of
\Delta(C_{0}^{\epsilon})
.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
q=\epsilon\rho+1=ef+1
where
\epsilon\mid{e}
. Let
\alpha
be a fixed primitive element of GF
(q)
and let
C_{0}^{\epsilon}=\langle\alpha^{\epsilon}\rangle
. We define, for
0\leq i\leq\epsilon-1
,

\Phi_{i}:=\{x\in C_{0}^{e}:x\neq{1},x-1\in\alpha^{i}C_{0}^{\epsilon}\}\mbox{ and }\phi_{i}:=|\Phi_{i}|.

The following result establishes the relationship between
\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{\epsilon-1}
and cyclotomic numbers. The quantities
\phi_{j}
are hard to evaluate in the general case.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
q=\epsilon\rho+1=ef+1
such that
\epsilon\mid e
, then for
0\leq j\leq\epsilon-1
,

\phi_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{e/\epsilon-1}(\epsilon{i}+j,0)_{e}.

We have the following conditions for DDFs and DPDFs.

Theorem 3.11
.

(i)

If
\phi_{1}=\ldots=\phi_{\epsilon-1}
, then
\rm{Int}({C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime})=\phi_{0}(C_{0}^{\epsilon})+\phi_{1}(\rm{G}^{*}\backslash{C_{0}^{\epsilon}})
, i.e.
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
is a DPDF (or PDS when
\epsilon=e
).

(ii)

If
\phi_{0}=\phi_{1}=\ldots=\phi_{\epsilon-1}
, then
\rm{Int}({C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime})=\phi_{0}(\rm{G}^{*})
, i.e.
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
is a DDF (or DS when
\epsilon=e
).

We now look at a useful relationship between transversals and diagonals of transversals:

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
q=ef+1
. For each
1\leq r\leq f-1
,

(i)

T_{f-r}=-T_{r}

(ii)

D_{f-r}=-D_{r}

Proof.

(i)

By Lemma
3.5
(i),
-T_{r}=-(\alpha^{re}-1)C_{0}^{e}=(1-\alpha^{re})C_{0}^{e}
for each
1\leq r\leq f-1
. Since
\alpha^{ef}=1
,
-T_{r}=(\alpha^{ef}-\alpha^{re})C_{0}^{e}=\alpha^{re}(\alpha^{e(f-r)}-1)C_{0}^{e}=(\alpha^{e(f-r)}-1)C_{0}^{e}=T_{f-r}
.

(ii)

Since by Proposition
3.8
(ii),
D_{r}=(\alpha^{re}-1)C_{0}^{\epsilon}
, the proof is analogous to part (i).

∎

Since
-T_{r}=(-1)T_{r}
and
-C_{0}^{\epsilon}=(-1)C_{0}^{\epsilon}
, we may exploit this relationship between a transversal and its negative by determining the cyclotomic class containing the element
-1
. Note that, for an odd prime power
q=\epsilon\rho+1
,
q\equiv 1\mod 2\epsilon
precisely when
\rho
is even, and
q\equiv\epsilon+1\mod 2\epsilon
precisely when
\rho
is odd; moreover when
\epsilon
is even and
\rho
is odd,
e
must be an odd multiple of
\epsilon
. The following result summarises results from
[
30
]
:

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
q=\epsilon\rho+1
.

(a)

Let
\epsilon
be an even integer. Then

(i)

when
q\equiv 1\mod 2\epsilon
,
-1\in C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

(ii)

when
q\equiv\epsilon+1\mod 2\epsilon
,
-1\in\alpha^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

(b)

Let
\epsilon
be an odd integer. Then,
-1\in C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

We now identify precisely how
D_{r}
and
D_{f-r}
are related. Note that, when
f
is even,
T_{\frac{f}{2}}
and
D_{\frac{f}{2}}
occur. These form their own negatives; as such, both will be treated separately to other transversals/diagonals of transversals in later counting arguments.

Proposition 3.14
.

(a)

Let
D_{r}=\alpha^{i}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
, where
1\leq r\leq f-1
(
r\neq\frac{f}{2}
) and
0\leq i\leq\epsilon-1
.

(i)

Let
q=\epsilon\rho+1
and let
\epsilon
be an even integer. Then

(I)

when
q\equiv 1\mod 2\epsilon
,
D_{f-r}=\alpha^{i}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

(II)

when
q\equiv\epsilon+1\mod 2\epsilon
,
D_{f-r}=\alpha^{i+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

(ii)

Let
q=\epsilon\rho+1
, and let
\epsilon
be an odd integer. Then,
D_{f-r}=\alpha^{i}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

(b)

If
f
is even,
T_{\frac{f}{2}}=(-2)C_{0}^{e}
and
D_{\frac{f}{2}}=(-2)C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

In the case when
\epsilon=2
, we have the following useful result.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let GF
(q)
be a finite field
q=ef+1
, with
e
even. Let
f
be even.

(i)

If
q\equiv 1\mod 8
,
D_{\frac{f}{2}}=C_{0}^{2}
.

(ii)

If
q\equiv 5\mod 8
,
D_{\frac{f}{2}}=C_{1}^{2}
.

Proof.

It is known (see
[
30
]
) that for a finite field
GF(q)
of odd order, if
q\equiv 1,7\mod 8
then
2\in C_{0}^{2}
, while if
q\equiv 3,5\mod 8
then
2\in C_{1}^{2}
. The result follows by combining this with Proposition
3.14
and Lemma
3.13
.
∎

Finally, we prove the following consequences of the structural results we have established, which will be useful in what follows. For
0\leq i\leq\epsilon-1
we define

\Psi_{i}:=\{1\leq r<\frac{f}{2}:\alpha^{re}\in\Phi_{i}\}\mbox{ and }\psi_{i}:=|\Psi_{i}|.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
q=ef+1=\epsilon\rho+1
(
\epsilon|e
).

(a)

Let
q\equiv 1\mod 2\epsilon
(i.e.
\rho
is even).
Suppose that
\phi_{1}=\phi_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq\epsilon-1
, i.e.
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
is a DPDF (or PDS when
e=\epsilon
).

(i)

If
f
is odd then
\phi_{0}=2\psi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}=2\psi_{1}
.

(ii)

If
f
is even and
\epsilon>2
then
\phi_{0}=2\psi_{0}+1
and
\phi_{1}=2\psi_{1}
.

(iii)

If
f
is even,
\epsilon=2
and
q\equiv 1\mod 8
then
\phi_{0}=2\psi_{0}+1
and
\phi_{1}=2\psi_{1}
.

(iv)

If
f
is even,
\epsilon=2
and
q\equiv 5\mod 8
then
\phi_{0}=2\psi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}=2\psi_{1}+1
.

(b)

Let
q\equiv\epsilon+1\mod 2\epsilon
(i.e.
\rho
is odd). Let
\epsilon
be odd. Suppose that
\phi_{1}=\phi_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq\epsilon-1
. Then
\phi_{0}=2\psi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}=2\psi_{1}
.

(c)

Let
q\equiv\epsilon+1\mod 2\epsilon
. If
\epsilon
is even, then
\phi_{0}=\phi_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}
.

Proof.

(a)

Since
q\equiv 1\mod 2\epsilon
, we have
\rho
even. If
f\neq\rho
then
f
can be odd or even.

Suppose
f
is odd. Consider the diagonals of transversals of
\Delta(C_{0}^{e})
. There is no central diagonal
D_{\frac{f}{2}}
. By Proposition
3.14
,
D_{r}=D_{f-r}
. Hence if
r\in\Psi_{i}
, i.e.
\alpha^{re}\in\Phi_{i}
for some
i
then also
\alpha^{(f-r)e}\in\Phi_{i}
, i.e. the
f-1
diagonals of
{\rm Int}({C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime})
pair up into
\frac{f-1}{2}
disjoint pairs, each pair contributing two copies of a given
\alpha^{i}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
. Hence
\phi_{0}=2\psi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}=2\psi_{1}
.

Now suppose
f
is even (note this case holds when
e=\epsilon
and
f=\rho
). There is one central diagonal
D_{\frac{f}{2}}
and
\frac{f-2}{2}
pairs of diagonals which pair up according to the rule
D_{r}=D_{f-r}
. Finally, consider
D_{\frac{f}{2}}
. For
\epsilon>2
, if
\alpha^{\frac{f}{2}e}\in\Phi_{i}
where
i\in\{0,\ldots,\epsilon-1\}
, i.e.
D_{\frac{f}{2}}=(\alpha^{\frac{f}{2}e}-1)C_{0}^{\epsilon}=\alpha^{i}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
, then
\phi_{i}=2\psi_{i}+1
, while
\phi_{j}=2\psi_{j}
for all
j\neq i
. If
i\neq 0
, this contradicts the assumption that
\phi_{1}=\phi_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq\epsilon-1
, hence
i=0
. For
\epsilon=2
, this argument does not apply; instead we apply Lemma
3.15
.

(b)

Similar to (a).

(c)

Since
q\equiv\epsilon+1\mod 2\epsilon
, we have
\rho
odd. Since
\rho=\frac{e}{\epsilon}f
,
\epsilon|e
and
\rho
is odd,
f
must be odd. Consider the diagonals of transversals of
\Delta(C_{0}^{e})
. Since
f
is odd, there is no central diagonal
D_{\frac{f}{2}}
. By Proposition
3.14
, if
D_{r}=\alpha^{i}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
then
D_{f-r}=\alpha^{i+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}C_{0}^{\epsilon}
. Hence the
f-1
diagonals of
{\rm Int}({C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime})
pair up into
\frac{f-1}{2}
disjoint pairs, and there are the same number of
\alpha^{re}\in\Phi_{i}
as there are in
\Phi_{i+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}
. Hence, for all
0\leq i\leq\epsilon-1
,
\phi_{i}=\phi_{i+\frac{\epsilon}{2}}
, so in particular
\phi_{0}=\phi_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}
.

∎

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
q=ef+1=\epsilon\rho+1
(
\epsilon|e
).

(a)

Let
q\equiv 1\mod 2\epsilon
.

(i)

If
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
is a PDS, then it must be proper (i.e.
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
cannot be a difference set).

(ii)

Let
f
be even. If
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
is a DPDF, then it must be proper.

Let
\epsilon>2
.

(iii)

If
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
is a (proper) PDS then
-2,2\in C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

(iv)

Let
f
be even. If
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
is a (proper) DPDF then
-2,2\in C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

(b)

Let
q\equiv\epsilon+1\mod 2\epsilon
and suppose
\epsilon
is even.

(i)

C_{0}^{\epsilon}
cannot be a proper PDS.

(ii)

If
\epsilon<e
, then
{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
cannot be a proper DPDF.

Proof.

(a)

We apply Theorem
3.16
(a). For
\epsilon>2
, by assumption, in both the PDS and DPDF cases, we have
\phi_{1}=\phi_{j}
(
1\leq j\leq\epsilon-1
). For (i), we take
e=\epsilon
and
f=\rho
; as
\rho
is even,
\phi_{0}
is of opposite parity to
\phi_{1}
, so
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
is not a difference set. Part (ii) follows similarly by taking
e>\epsilon
in the lemma. For
\epsilon=2
and
f
even, it is also the case that
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
have opposite parity. For (iii) and (iv), from the proof of Theorem
3.16
(a)(ii), we have that
-1=\alpha^{\frac{f}{2}e}\in\Phi_{0}
, so
-2\in C_{0}^{\epsilon}
, and hence
2\in C_{0}^{\epsilon}
.

(b)

Apply Theorem
3.16
(c) with
e=\epsilon
for (i) and
e>\epsilon
for part (ii). Since
\phi_{0}=\phi_{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}
and
\frac{\epsilon}{2}\in\{1,\ldots,\epsilon\}
, it is impossible for
\phi_{0}
to take a distinct value from all other
\phi_{j}
,
j\in\{1,\ldots,\epsilon\}
.

∎

In (a), the
\epsilon=2
case is different to the
\epsilon>2
case due to the absence of
\phi_{j}
with
2\leq j\leq\epsilon-1
. We note that parts (iii) and (iv) of Corollary
3.17
(a) do not hold for
\epsilon=2
.

3.2
External differences: EPDFs

For the external case, we may make analogous definitions to those of the previous subsection.

Definition 3.18
.

Let
q=ef+1
and let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
GF(q)
.

(i)

For each
1\leq r\leq f-1
and
1\leq j\leq e-1
, define

T_{(r,j)}:=\{\alpha^{ne+j}-\alpha^{me}:n-m=r\mod f,\,0\leq n,m\leq f-1\}.

Clearly,
T_{(r,j)}\subseteq\Delta(C_{j}^{e},C_{0}^{e})
; we refer to the set
T_{(r,j)}
as an
external transversal
of
\Delta(C_{j}^{e},C_{0}^{e})
. Note
|T_{(r,j)}|=f
.

(ii)

For each
1\leq r\leq f-1
and
1\leq j\leq e-1
, let
a_{(r,j)}\in\{0,...,e-1\}
be such that
\alpha^{re+j}-1\in C_{a_{(r,j)}}^{e}=\alpha^{a_{(r,j)}}C_{0}^{e}
.

This result summarizes some cyclotomic relationships using our notation.

Lemma 3.19
.

(i)

For
1\leq r\leq f-1
and
1\leq j\leq e-1
, each external transversal
T_{(r,j)}=(\alpha^{re+j}-1)C_{0}^{e}
is a copy of the cyclotomic class
C_{a_{(r,j)}}
.

(ii)

\Delta(C_{j}^{e},C_{0}^{e})=\bigcup\limits_{r=1}^{f}T_{(r,j)}=\bigcup\limits_{r=1}^{f}C_{a_{(r,j)}}^{e}=\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{e-1}(i,j)_{e}(C_{i}^{e})
.

(iii)

For
0\leq l\leq e-1
,
\Delta(C_{j+l}^{e},C_{l}^{e})=\alpha^{l}\Delta(C_{j}^{e},C_{0}^{e})=\bigcup\limits_{r=1}^{f}\alpha^{l}T_{(r,j)}=\bigcup\limits_{r=1}^{f}\alpha^{l}C_{a_{(r,j)}}^{e}=\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{e-1}(i,j)_{e}(\alpha^{l}C_{i}^{e})
.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
\rm{GF}(q)
be a finite field, where
q
is a prime power and
e\geq 3
is a divisor of
q-1
.
Let
I\subset\{0,1,\ldots,e-1\}
(
|I|=u,2\leq u\leq e-1
) and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\{C_{i}^{e}\}_{i\in I}
.

If there exist
B,X
such that
B=(0,i)_{e}=(i,i)_{e}
and
X=(i,j)_{e}
for all
1\leq i\neq{j}\leq e-1
, then

(i)

\Delta(C_{s}^{e},C_{t}^{e})=B(C_{s}^{e}\cup C_{t}^{e})+X(G^{*}\setminus(C_{s}^{e}\cup C_{t}^{e}))
for all
0\leq s\neq{t}\leq e-1
;

(ii)

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a
(q,u,\frac{q-1}{e},2B(u-1)+X(u-1)(u-2),Xu(u-1))
-EPDF (proper when
B\neq X
).

Proof.

For (i), apply Lemma
3.19
; note
\Delta(C_{s}^{e},C_{t}^{e})=\alpha^{t}\Delta(C_{j}^{e},C_{0}^{e})
where
j\equiv s-t\mod e
.

For (ii), the multiset
{\rm Ext}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})=\bigcup\limits_{l\neq{j};l,j\in I}\Delta(C_{l}^{e},C_{j}^{e})
is the union of
u(u-1)
multisets of the form
\Delta(C_{l}^{e},C_{j}^{e})
(since
|I|=u
). For a fixed
l\in I
, there are
u-1
multisets of the form
\Delta(C_{l}^{e},C_{j}^{e})
in
{\rm Ext}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
, where
j\in I
and
j\neq l
, and
u-1
multisets of the form
\Delta(C_{j}^{e},C_{l}^{e})
. There are
u(u-1)-2(u-1)=(u-1)(u-2)
multisets of the form
\Delta(C_{s}^{e},C_{j}^{e})
, where
s\neq{j}\neq{l}\in I
. Applying (i), we see that
C_{l}^{e}
occurs precisely
2B(u-1)+X(u-1)(u-2)
times in the multiset
{\rm Ext}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
. Finally, again by (i), for any
t\not\in I
,
C_{t}^{e}
occurs precisely
u(u-1)X
times in
{\rm Ext}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})=\bigcup\limits_{l\neq{j}:l,j\in I}\Delta(C_{l}^{e},C_{j}^{e})
. The EPDF result follows.
∎

3.3
A combined approach: uniform cyclotomy

Observe that, by combining the conditions in Lemma
3.6
and Proposition
3.20
, we could obtain a class of objects which are both DPDFs and EPDFs. The following useful definition was introduced by Baumert, Mills and Ward in
[
2
]
.

Definition 3.21
.

The cyclotomic numbers
(i,j)_{e}
over
GF(q)
are
uniform
if
(0,i)_{e}=(i,0)_{e}=(i,i)_{e}=(0,1)_{e}
for
i\neq 0
, and
(i,j)_{e}=(1,2)
for
0\neq i\neq j\neq 0
.

So, if the cyclotomic numbers are uniform, we would have a family of DPDFs which are also EPDFs. The following result from
[
2
]
is important here.

Theorem 3.22
.

Let
\rm{GF}(q)
be a finite field where
q
is a power of a prime
p
and let
e\geq 3
be a divisor of
q-1
. The cyclotomic numbers of order
e
over
{\rm GF}(q)
are uniform if and only if
-1
is a power of
p
modulo
e
. If this holds, then either
p=2
or
f=\frac{q-1}{e}
is even;
q=s^{2}
with
s\equiv 1\mod e
; and

(0,0)_{e}=\left(\frac{s-1}{e}\right)^{2}-(e-3)\left(\frac{s-1}{e}\right)-1

(0,i)_{e}=(i,0)_{e}=(i,i)_{e}=\left(\frac{s-1}{e}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{s-1}{e}\right)\,\rm{for}\,i\neq{0},

(i,j)_{e}=\left(\frac{s-1}{e}\right)^{2}\,\rm{for}\,0\neq i\neq j.

We present the following condition which is equivalent to the key condition in Theorem
3.22
:

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
q^{\prime}=p^{n}
where
p
is prime, and let
e\geq 3
be a divisor of
q^{\prime}-1
. Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

-1
is a power of
p
modulo
e

(ii)

there exists a prime power
q
such that
q^{\prime}=q^{2b}
(
b\in\mathbb{N}
) and
e|q+1
.

Proof.

For the forward direction: let
s
be the smallest positive integer such that
p^{s}\equiv-1\mod e
. Then
2s|n
and so
n=2sb
for some
b\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.
q^{\prime}=p^{n}=p^{2sb}=(p^{s})^{2b}
, and
e|p^{s}+1
. The reverse direction is immediate as
q=p^{a}
for some
a\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

This leads to the following result. Observe that, as a special case of part (iii) (taking

), we obtain the result of Calderbank and Kantor in Section 9 of
[
6
]
which is presented in different notation in Section 10 of
[
23
]
.

Theorem 3.24
.

Let
{\rm GF}(q^{\prime})
be a finite field of order

, where
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
and
q
is a power of a prime p. Let

, (

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\{C_{i}^{e}\}_{i\in I}

(i)

each

is a (regular)
(q^{\prime},\frac{q^{\prime}-1}{e},\eta^{2}-(e-3)\eta-1,\eta^{2}+\eta)
-PDS;

(ii)

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is a
(q^{\prime},u,\frac{q^{\prime}-1}{e},u\eta^{2}+(u+2-e)\eta-1,u(\eta^{2}+\eta))
-DPDF and a
(q^{\prime},u,\frac{q^{\prime}-1}{e},u(u-1)\eta^{2}+2(u-1)\eta,u(u-1)\eta^{2})
-EPDF.

(iii)

or

Proof.

By Lemma
3.23

are satisfied, and so the cyclotomic numbers are uniform with the given values, where (in the notation of Theorem

∎

4
Cyclotomic PDSs and applications to DPDFs/EPDFs

In this paper, we will use cyclotomic PDSs in two distinct ways to construct DPDFs and EPDFs: as the constituent sets, or by partitioning. Both of these require a knowledge of when
C_{0}^{e}
forms a partial difference set (or difference set). Throughout,
q
is a prime power which we express as
q=ef+1
.

4.1
When is

a DS or PDS?

It is possible to determine the conditions under which

is a DS or PDS for specific values of small

by using cyclotomic numbers directly. In general, explicit evaluation of cyclotomic numbers is a difficult problem. Selected values for
e=2,3,4,6
and

are given in
[
30
]
; further results have been obtained for

(see
[
20
]
and
[
11
]
) and certain values up to

(see
[
3
]
).

For difference sets, various results are given in
[
30
]
. The recent paper
[
35
]
describes current progress on the question of when

forms a difference set and establishes new results, including the case when

is even. The key results from
[
30
]
and
[
35
]
relevant to this paper are given below. Note that for

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

be a prime power.

(i)

If

is even then

is not a difference set for any value of

.

(ii)

If

is odd and

is a difference set then

is even and

is odd.

(iii)

If

is a difference set.

(iv)

If
q=4f+1
then
C_{0}^{4}
is a difference set if and only if
q=1+4t^{2}
and

is odd (here

is odd).

(v)

If

, there is no

which is a difference set.

(vi)

If

then

is a difference set if and only if

admits the simultaneous representations

(here

is odd).

For partial difference sets, recall that by Corollary
3.17
, for

with

is not a proper
(q,f,\lambda,\mu)
-PDS if

]

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

(and a

.

We establish a comparable result for other small values of

.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let

be a finite field, where

.

(iii)

If

Proof.

(i)

For the forward direction, suppose that

(ii)

Similar to (i); this result was stated without proof in

]
.

(iii)

Similar to (i). By Corollary

, when

(iv)

∎

We end this section with two results (not derived from cyclotomic numbers), which give a characterization of

in terms of the relationship between

and

.

Proposition 4.4
.

(i)

Let
q=p^{\beta}
,
\beta>1
. For
r|\beta
(
r\neq\beta
), let
F_{r}
be the subfield
GF(p^{r})
of
GF(q)
; then
F_{r}\setminus\{0\}
is the cyclotomic class
C_{0}^{e}
of
GF(q)
where
e=\frac{q-1}{p^{r}-1}
.

(ii)

Let
q=ef+1
. Then
C_{0}^{e}
is a
(q,f,f-1,0)
-PDS if and only if
C_{0}^{e}\cup\{0\}
is a subfield of
GF(q)
. Here,
q
is a prime power which is not a prime,
e=\frac{q-1}{p^{r}-1}
and
f=p^{r}-1
.

Proof.

(i) Since
F_{r}
is a subfield of order
p^{r}
,
F_{r}\backslash\{0\}
is a multiplicative subgroup of GF
(q)^{*}
with cardinality
p^{r}-1
. Each subgroup of the cyclic group GF
(q)^{*}
of given order is unique up to isomorphism. Hence
F_{r}\backslash\{0\}=C_{0}^{e}
, where
e=\frac{q-1}{p^{r}-1}
.

(ii) Suppose
C_{0}^{e}
is a
(q,f,f-1,0)
-PDS. By Theorem
2.7
,
C_{0}^{e}\cup\{0\}
is an additive subgroup of
GF(q)
and by definition
C_{0}^{e}
is a multiplicative sugroup of
GF(q)^{*}
, so
C_{0}^{e}\cup\{0\}
is a subfield. Conversely, suppose
C_{0}^{e}\cup\{0\}
is a subfield and hence an additive subgroup of
GF(q)
; then by Theorem
2.7
, it is a
(q,f+1,f+1,0)
-PDS. By subfield properties,
C_{0}^{e}\cup\{0\}
and
C_{0}^{e}
are closed under the taking of negatives. Hence removal of
\{0\}
yields a
(q,f,f-1,0)
-PDS. The parameters
e
and
f
are as in part (i).
∎

In fact the subfield situation is the only possibility unless
e<f
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
q=p^{\beta}=ef+1
. Suppose
C_{0}^{e}
is a proper
(q,f,\lambda,\mu)
-PDS. Then

(i)

if
e>f
,
C_{0}^{e}\cup\{0\}
is a subfield of
GF(q)
;

(ii)

if
e<f
then
C_{0}^{e}\cup\{0\}
is not a subfield of
GF(q)
and
\mu\geq 1
;

(iii)

the case
e=f
cannot occur for

.

Proof.

As
C_{0}^{e}
is a
(q,f,\lambda,\mu)
-PDS then the following multiset equation must hold;

\Delta(C_{0}^{e})=\lambda(C_{0}^{e})+\mu(G^{*}\backslash{C_{0}^{e}}).

The set
C_{0}^{e}
has cardinality
f
, so
|\Delta(C_{0}^{e})|=f(f-1)
and
|G^{*}\setminus{C_{0}^{e}}|=q-1-f=f(e-1)
. We can therefore write;

f(f-1)=\lambda(f)+\mu(f(e-1))

and so

f-1=\lambda+\mu(e-1).

(4)

(i) When
e>f
, since
\lambda,\mu
are non-negative integers, we must have
\mu=0
and
\lambda=f-1
in the above equation. By Proposition
4.4
, the result follows.

(ii)If
C_{0}^{e}\cup\{0\}
is a subfield then
|C_{0}^{e}|=p^{r}-1=f
and

for some

; say
\beta=kr
(

). Then

. The result follows.

(iii) Let
e=f>2
. From Equation
4
above, we have
f-1=\lambda+\mu(f-1)
, so either
\mu=0
or
\mu=1
. When
\mu=0
, then
\lambda=f-1
, and
C_{0}^{e}
is a
(q,f,f-1,0)
-PDS, i.e a subfield
GF(p^{r})
with 0 removed. The parameters are

, contradicting
e=f
. So this case cannot occur. Otherwise
\mu=1
and
\lambda=0
. The set
C_{0}^{e}
will only form a
(q,f,0,1)
-PDS if the multiset
\Delta(C_{0}^{e})
comprises of precisely 1 copy of each cyclotomic class
C_{i}^{e}
,

(and no copies of
C_{0}^{e}
). By Lemma
3.5
, the multiset
\Delta(C_{0}^{e})=\bigcup_{r=1}^{f-1}T_{r}
, where for each
1\leq r\leq f-1
,

. As

is a proper PDS, by Corollary
3.17

q\equiv 1\mod 2e
and by Lemma
3.13
(a)(i) and (b),
-1\in C_{0}^{e}
, hence

, so for any

, there are at least two copies of the cyclotomic class

in the multiset
\Delta(C_{0}^{e})
, a contradiction. We note that for

, i.e.

, the class

does form a

-PDS.
∎

Note that if
q=ef+1
is prime and

, then

cannot be a proper PDS, as any such PDS must be a subfield of

with

4.2
Forming DPDFs and EPDFs from cyclotomic PDSs

In this subsection, we will exhibit constructions of DPDFs and EPDFs by directly taking the sets to be cyclotomic classes, or unions of classes, which themselves form PDSs.

The following is immediate from Proposition
4.4
, Theorem
2.10
and Theorem

.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
GF(q)
be a finite field of order
q=p^{\beta}

\beta>1
. For

(ii)

In the rest of this section, we focus on constructions which simultaneously guarantee DPDFs and EPDFs.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\rm{GF}(q)
be a finite field of order

, where

. Further, let

(where

) and

.

If

is a PDS (with parameters
(q,\frac{q-1}{e},\eta^{2}-(e-3)\eta-1,\eta^{2}+\eta)
) then
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is both a proper
(q,u,\frac{q-1}{e},u\eta^{2}+(u+2-e)\eta-1,u(\eta^{2}+\eta))

(q,u,\frac{q-1}{e},u(u-1)\eta^{2}+2(u-1)\eta,u(u-1)\eta^{2})
-EPDF (where
\eta=\frac{(-p)^{m}-1}{e}
).

Proof.

By Theorem

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

(q)
be a finite field of order

Proof.

By Remark
2.2
,

{\rm Int}({C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime})\cup{\rm Ext}({C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime})=\lambda{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}+\lambda({G}^{*}\backslash{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}).

By basic difference set properties,

(i)

By Theorem
3.11
,
\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=\cdots=\phi_{\epsilon-1}

{C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime}
to be a DPDF, and hence an EPDF (since
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
is a difference set).

(ii)

Since
C_{0}^{\epsilon}
is a difference set, by Theorem
4.1
,

must be odd,
\epsilon
must be even and
\rho
must be odd. By assumption,
\phi_{1}=\phi_{j}

is a DS,

is an EDF.

There are precisely
f-1
diagonals of transversals in Int
({C_{0}^{\epsilon}}^{\prime})

By Remark
2.2

(i)

6
Further Work

It would be of interest to find new constructions for DPDFs and EPDFs. For the cyclotomic approach, one natural direction would be to further investigate theoretical constructions where the sets in the family are formed from unions of
C_{i}^{e}
. An initial investigation was begun in this paper via Theorem
4.9
, and there are various examples of such DDF/EDF constructions in the literature which are not covered by this theorem.

We may also ask about non-cyclotomic construction methods. Packings of PDSs offer a method of obtaining DPDFs, and when they have the additional property that the complement of the union is a subgroup then we are guaranteed an EPDF. For example, in the recent paper
[
17
]
, the authors define a
(c,t)
LP-packing
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{t}\}
in an abelian group
G
of order
t^{2}c^{2}
relative to a subgroup
U
of order
tc
, and prove an existence result. Here the
P_{i}
’s are disjoint, each of order
c(tc-1)
, and the complement of their union is a subgroup
U
of
G
. Any such
(c,t)
LP-packing will form a DPDF which is also an EPDF. It would be of interest to explore such approaches further.

There are various ways in which the definitions of DPDF and EPDF could naturally be generalized. The condition on disjointness of the sets in the DPDF could be relaxed, and the set sizes could be allowed to vary. We could also generalize our DPDF/EPDF definitions in such a way that the two frequencies correspond to elements inside/outside an arbitrary subset of
G
, rather than the union of the sets of the family.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
T
be a subset of
G^{*}
.

(i)

A collection of
m
disjoint subsets
S=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
in
G^{*}
, where each
D_{i}
has cardinality
k_{i}
, forms an
(n,m,k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};\lambda,\mu)
Disjoint Partial Difference Family of
G
(relative to
T
) if the following multiset equation holds:

\cup_{i}\Delta(D_{i})=\lambda(T)+\mu(G^{*}\setminus T)

(ii)

A collection of disjoint
m
subsets
S=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{m}\}
in
G^{*}
, where each
D_{i}
has cardinality
k_{i}
, forms an
(n,m,k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};\lambda,\mu)
External Partial Difference Family (relative to
T
) if the following multiset equation holds:

\cup_{i\neq j}\Delta(D_{i},D_{j})=\lambda(T)+\mu(G^{*}\setminus T)

Observe that Definitions
1.2
and
1.3
correspond to taking
T=\cup_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}
in Definition
6.1
. This is also closer to the definition given in
[
10
]
. Construction methods for these would be of interest.

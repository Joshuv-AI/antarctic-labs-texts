Abstract
As a result of impressive research [ gdelrma ] , D. García-Lucas, Á. del Río and L. Margolis defined an infinite series of non-isomorphic 2 2 -groups G G and H H , whose group algebras 𝔽 ​ G \mathbb{F}G and 𝔽 ​ H \mathbb{F}H over the field 𝔽 = 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{2} are isomorphic, solving negatively the long-standing Modular Isomorphism Problem (MIP). In this note we give a different perspective on their examples and show that they are special cases of a more general construction. We also show that this type of construction for p > 2 p>2 does not provide a similar counterexample to the MIP.



Let
p
be a prime number,
G
and
H
finite
p
-groups and
\mathbb{F}
a field
of characteristic
p
. The long-standing conjecture, due to R. Brauer called the Modular Isomorphism
Problem (MIP), states that if
\mathbb{F}G
and
\mathbb{F}H
are isomorphic algebras then
G
and
H

are isomorphic groups. The problem is settled in the positive in many cases but recently for
p=2

an infinite series of counterexamples was given (
[
gdelrma
]
). In this paper we show that
these counterexamples are special cases of a more general construction for
p=2
. We show also that
for
p>2
the analogous construction does not provide a similar counterexample to the MIP. Therefore the
case
p>2
remains open.

For a survey of known results concerning the MIP see an excellent paper by L. Margolis
[
margolis2022
]
.
The terminology used in the paper is standard. Our research was supported by extensive use of GAP
software (
[
GAP
]
).

We begin with easy folklore observations.
Let
K
be a dihedral group of order
2^{k+1}
,

K=\langle t,\ s\,|\ t^{2}=s^{2}=(ts)^{2^{k}}=1\rangle.

(1)

If we put
r=ts
, then this presentation can be replaced by the following one

K=\langle t,\ r\,|\ t^{2}=r^{2^{k}}=1,r^{t}=r^{-1}\rangle.

(2)

Let
C=\langle c\,|\ c^{2^{n}}=1\rangle
,
D=\langle d\,|\ d^{2^{m}}=1\rangle
,
where
n>m\geqslant k\geqslant 3
.
In the group

P=K\times C\times D,

we define three elements
x=tc
,
y=sd
,
z=tsd=rd

and two subgroups

\displaystyle G=\langle x,y\rangle,

\displaystyle H=\langle x,z\rangle.

Lemma 1
.

The following properties are satisfied:

(i)

|G|=|H|=2^{n+m+k-1}
;

(ii)

G^{\prime}=H^{\prime}=P^{\prime}=K^{\prime}=\langle(ts)^{2}\rangle
and the nilpotency class of groups
G,H,P,K
is equal to
k
;

(iii)

\Phi(G)=\Phi(H)=\Phi(P)=\langle(ts)^{2},\,c^{2},\,d^{2}\rangle
;

(iv)

The subgroup
M=<ts,c,d>
is abelian and maximal in
P
;

(v)

The subgroup
G\cap M=\langle xy,c^{2},d^{2}\rangle
is maximal in
G
;

(vi)

The subgroup
H\cap M=\langle z,c^{2},d^{2}\rangle
is maximal in
H
;

(vii)

The groups
G
and
H
are not isomorphic.

Proof.

(i) The projection of
P
onto
K
restricted to
G
maps
G
onto
K
. The kernel of this
restricted projection is equal to
G\cap(C\times D)=\langle c^{2},\,d^{2}\rangle=\langle x^{2},\,y^{2}\rangle
.
Therefore
|G|=|K|\cdot|\langle c^{2},\,d^{2}\rangle|=2^{n+m+k-1}
. The same projection restricted to
H

is also a map of
H
onto
K
with the kernel
H\cap(C\times D)=\langle c^{2},\,d^{2}\rangle=\langle x^{2},\,(x^{-1}z)^{2}\rangle
, because
(x^{-1}z)^{2}=(c^{-1}t^{-1}tsd)^{2}=c^{-2}d^{2}
. Thus also

|H|=2^{n+m+k-1}
.

(ii) The commutator subgroup
K^{\prime}
of
K
is cyclic and generated
by

{\left[t,s\right]=t^{-1}s^{-1}ts=\left(ts\right)^{2}=r^{2}=\left[t,r\right]}.

But
[t,s]=[x,y]
and
[t,r]=[x,z]
, so
K^{\prime}=G^{\prime}=H^{\prime}
. It is also obvious by construction
of
P
, that
K^{\prime}=P^{\prime}
. It follows analogously that the next terms of the lower central series of these groups are equal.
More precisely,
\gamma_{i}\left(X\right)=\left(ts\right)^{2^{i-1}}
for
i\geqslant 2
and
X\in\left\{G,H,P,K\right\}.

(iii) It is clear that
\Phi\left(P\right)=\Phi\left(K\right)\Phi\left(C\right)\Phi\left(D\right)=\langle(ts)^{2},\,c^{2},\,d^{2}\rangle
. Further
\left[x,y\right]=\left[tc,sd\right]=\left[t,s\right]=\left(ts\right)^{2}
,
x^{2}=\left(tc\right)^{2}=c^{2}
,
y^{2}=(sd)^{2}=d^{2}
,
hence
{\Phi\left(G\right)=\Phi\left(P\right)}
.
As
\left[x,z\right]=\left[tc,tsd\right]=\left[t,ts\right]=t\left(st\right)t\left(ts\right)=\left(ts\right)^{2}
,

x^{2}=c^{2}
,

z^{2}=\left(tsc\right)^{2}=\left(ts\right)^{2}c^{2}
, we have
\Phi\left(H\right)=\Phi\left(P\right)
.

(iv-vi)
M
is abelian, because
c
and
d
are central in
P
. Moreover

|M|=o\left(ts\right)\cdot o\left(c\right)\cdot o\left(d\right)=2^{k+m+n}
,
which means that
|P:M|=2
. The maximality of
G\cap M
in
G
and
H\cap M
in
H
is obvious as
M
is abelian.

(vii) By the assumption
n>m\geqslant k
, the order of
xy
is equal to
2^{n}
,
as
(xy)^{2}=(tcsd)^{2}=(ts)^{2}c^{2}d^{2}
. This is maximal order of generators of
G\cap M
, therefore

\exp\left(G\cap M\right)=2^{n}
.
Similarly, the element
z
has order
2^{m}
, so
c^{2}
has maximal order among generators of
H\cap M

and then
\exp\left(H\cap M\right)=2^{n-1}
.
It is well-known that if a non-abelian
p
-group
X
has two different abelian subgroups of index
p
, then
\Phi(X)
is in the center of
X
, in particular
X
is of class of
2
.
Since
G
and
H
have nilpotency class
k\geqslant 3
the subgroups
G\cap M
and
H\cap M
are the unique abelian subgroups of index
2
in
G
and
H
respectively.
Now groups
G\cap M
and
H\cap M
have different exponents, therefore they are not isomorphic. Hence
G
and
H
are not isomorphic as well.
∎

Lemma 2
.

The groups
G
and
H
can be described in terms of generators and relations in the following way:

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\langle x,\,y,\ u\,\mid x^{2^{n}}=y^{2^{m}}=u^{2^{k-1}}=1,\ y^{x}=yu,\ u^{x}=u^{-1},\ u^{y}=u^{-1}\rangle;

(3)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\langle x,\,z,\ u\,\mid x^{2^{n}}=z^{2^{m}}=u^{2^{k-1}}=1,\ z^{x}=zu,\ u^{x}=u^{-1},\ u^{z}=u\rangle.

Proof.

The correspondence
x\rightarrow tc
,
y\rightarrow sd
can be extended to a homomorphism of
G
described
by (
3
) into
P
because
tc
and
sd
satisfy the relations (
3
) for
G
. Since

\langle tc,\,sd\rangle
generate a subgroup of order
2^{k+m+n-1}
this homomorphism is an embedding.
One can use a similar argument for the group
H
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
A
be a
2
-group generated by elements
a
and
b
of order
2^{n}
and
2^{m}
respectively such
that
a^{2}
and
b^{2}
are central in
A
,
|A^{\prime}|=2^{k-1}
and
\langle a^{2},\,b^{2}\rangle\cap A^{\prime}=1
.
Then
A
is isomorphic to
G
.

Proof.

Note that
A/\langle a^{2},b^{2}\rangle
as a group generated by two elements of order
2
is dihedral of order
2^{k+1}
.
Since
x^{2}=c^{2},y^{2}=d^{2}
are central in
G
,
|G^{\prime}|=2^{k-1}
and
\langle x^{2},y^{2}\rangle\cap G^{\prime}=1
.
Therefore the correspondence
a\rightarrow x
,
b\rightarrow y
extends in a natural way to an isomorphism of
A
onto
G
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

In the group algebra
\mathbb{F}H
let
\beta=1+x\left(1+z\right)
.
Then

(i)

\beta
has order
2^{k}
in the group of units of
\mathbb{F}H
and
\beta^{2}
is a central element of
\mathbb{F}H
;

(ii)

the subgroup
\langle x,\,\beta\rangle
is isomorphic to
G
and spans
\mathbb{F}H
.

Proof.

(i)

Note first that

\displaystyle\beta^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1+x\left(1+z\right)\right)^{2}=1+x\left(1+z\right)x\left(1+z\right)=1+x^{2}\left(1+z^{x}\right)\left(1+z\right)=

\displaystyle=1+x^{2}\left(1+\left(z+z^{x}\right)+\left(z^{x}z\right)\right).

Further

z+z^{x}=tsd+tsd^{tc}=tsd+ts^{t}d=td(s+s^{t})=tsd(1+(st)^{2})=z(1+[z,t])
and

z^{x}z=(tsd)^{tc}(tsd)=(ts^{t}d)(tsd)=d^{2}=z^{2}
, so

\beta^{2^{m-1}}=1+x^{2^{m-1}}(1+z^{2^{m-2}}(1+(st)^{2^{m-1}})+z^{2^{m-1}})\neq 1\\

and

\beta^{2^{m}}=1+x^{2^{m}}(1+z^{2^{m-1}}(1+(st)^{2^{m}})+z^{2^{m}})=1,

because
{\left(st\right)^{2^{m}}}=z^{2^{m}}=1.

We have also
(\beta^{2})^{x}=1+x^{2}(1+(z^{x}+z)+zz^{x})=\beta^{2}
, because
z^{x}z=zz^{x}=d^{2}
.

(ii)

Consider the group
\tilde{G}=\langle x,\beta\rangle
.
Since
x^{2},\beta^{2}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{F}H)
the factor group
\tilde{G}/\langle x^{2},\beta^{2}\rangle
is a dihedral group.
The commutator subgroup of
\tilde{G}
, generated by the element

\displaystyle\left[\beta,x\right]

\displaystyle=1+\beta^{-1}x^{-1}(\beta x+x\beta)=1+\beta^{-1}(\beta^{x}+\beta)

\displaystyle=1+\beta^{-1}x(z^{x}+z)=1+\beta^{-1}xz([z,x]+1)=1+\beta^{-1}xz((ts)^{2}+1),

has order
2^{k-1}
and has trivial intersection with
\langle x^{2},\,\beta^{2}\rangle
.

It follows from Lemma
3
that
\tilde{G}
is isomorphic to
G
.

At the very end, we observe that
x
and
\beta=1+x\left(1+z\right)=z+\left(x+1\right)\left(z+1\right)

are linearly independent modulo
A^{2}(\mathbb{F}H)
, where
A(\mathbb{F}H)
is the augmentation ideal of

\mathbb{F}H
.
Therefore by Jennings theory
[
passman1977
, Chapter 3.3]
both generate the whole algebra
\mathbb{F}H
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of the lemmas we obtain the following

Theorem 5
.

For every
m,\ n,\ k
,
n>m\geqslant k\geqslant 3
there exist non-isomorphic
2
-groups
G
and
H
of
order
2^{m+n+k-1}
and cyclic commutator subgroup of order
2^{k-1}
whose group algebras
over the
2
-element field are isomorphic.

Remark 6
.

(i)

There are many elements in
\mathbb{F}H
that, together with the element
x
, generate a group isomorphic to
G
spanning
\mathbb{F}H
.
For instance, if
\zeta
is a central unit of
\mathbb{F}H
of order
<2^{m}
,
\tilde{x}
is an element of
H
such that
z^{\tilde{x}}\neq z
, then

\tilde{\beta}=\zeta+\tilde{x}(1+z)
is such an element.

(ii)

If we put
k=3
, we get the examples from
[
gdelrma
]
with
\beta=d^{2}+zx[z,x](1+z)
.

(iii)

If, in the above construction, we replace the dihedral group
K
with the semidihedral or the generalized quaternion group of order
2^{k+1}
, we get the same groups (up to isomorphism) as the groups
G
and
H
.

It follows from Lemma 1, that in the counterexample the abelian subgroups of index
2
in

G
and
H
are not isomorphic. Let us consider analogous construction as in the counterexample taking
p>2
.
As a basis of the construction take a
p
-group
K=\langle s,s_{1}\rangle
of maximal class
(similarly as in the case
p=2
) having an abelian subgroup of index
p
(all they are described; see for instance
[
blackburn
, Section 4 and Theorem 4.3]
).
Then in the group
K\times\langle c\mid c^{p^{m}}=1\rangle\times\langle d\mid d^{p^{n}}=1\rangle
, where
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
are arbitrarily fixed,
take a subgroup
G=\langle sc,s_{1}d\rangle
.
In this group, the subgroup
N=C_{G}\left(G^{\prime}/\Phi\left(G^{\prime}\right)\right)
is abelian and has index
p
in
G
.

The following lemma is a consequence of Proposition 1.4
[
bagincaranti
]
and Lemma 1
[
bbaginski1988
]
.

Lemma 7
.

If the subgroup
N
of
G
defined by equation
N=C_{G}\left(G^{\prime}/\Phi\left(G^{\prime}\right)\right)
has index
p
in
G
, then the subgring
I\left(N\right)+I\left(G^{\prime}\right)\mathbb{F}G
is determined by the structure of
\mathbb{F}G
.
In particular the ideal
I\left(N\right)\mathbb{F}G
of
\mathbb{F}G
and the orders of factors
\mathcal{M}_{i}(N)/\mathcal{M}_{i+1}(N)
of the Brauer-Jennings-Zassenhaus series of
N
are determined.

Proposition 8
.

Let
G
be a finite
2
-generated
p
-group in which the subgroup

N=C_{G}\left(G^{\prime}/\Phi\left(G^{\prime}\right)\right)

is abelian and has index
p
then

N
is determined by
\mathbb{F}G
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a
p
-group such that
\mathbb{F}G\cong\mathbb{F}H
, then
H
is also
2
-generated and contains
a subgroup
M=C_{H}\left(H^{\prime}/\Phi\left(H^{\prime}\right)\right)
of index
p
.
Suppose first that
M
is abelian. It is well-known, see the proof of
[
passman1977
, Lemma 14.2.7]
, that
the series
\left\{|\mathcal{M}_{i}(M)/\mathcal{M}_{i+1}(M)|,i\geqslant 1\right\}
determines
the ismorphism class of
M
. Since

|\mathcal{M}_{i}(M)/\mathcal{M}_{i+1}(M)|=|\mathcal{M}_{i}(N)/\mathcal{M}_{i+1}(N)|,\ i\geqslant 1,

by Lemma
7
, we obtain
M\cong N
.
Therefore to complete the proof we need to show that
M
is abelian.

It is true, if we assume additionally that
G
is a
p
-group of maximal class, by

[
bagincaranti
, Theorem 3.2]
. We employ the proof of this theorem in the context of our case.

The set
\Phi(N)-Z(N)
consists of all elements
g\in G
that are the
p
-th powers of the non-central elements of
G
.
Since
N
is abelian and
|G:N|=p
, the
G
-class sums
\widehat{C_{g}}
of these elements are the
only
p
-th powers of other
G
-class sums in
\mathbb{F}G.
It follows from
[
parmenter1981
]

that the number of such class sums is determined by
\mathbb{F}G
, so the number of
H
-class sums

\widehat{C_{h}}
,
h\in H
, which are
p
-th powers is the same in
\mathbb{F}H
. Since

N/\Phi(G^{\prime})\cong M/\Phi(H^{\prime})
this property have exactly the class sums
\widehat{C_{h}}
, if

h\in\Phi(M)-Z(H)
. The number of
G
-class sums which are
p
-th powers is as large as possible,
because all they have cardinality
p
. Therefore all
H
-class sums of noncentral elements
of
\Phi(M)
have cardinality
p
, because
|\Phi(N)-Z(G)|=|\Phi(M)-Z(H)|
.

Let
H=\langle h,y\rangle
, where
y\in M
. If
\Phi(H)=1
, then
M
is abelian, so assume that

\Phi(M)\neq 1
. Now it is clear that
y^{p}\notin\Phi(H^{\prime})
, which implies that
y^{p}
is not central,
because
|H^{\prime}\cap Z(H)|=p
and then
H^{\prime}\cap Z(H)\leqslant\Phi(H^{\prime})
. Thus
\widehat{C_{y^{p}}}=\widehat{C_{y}}^{p}
and then
H:C_{H}(y)|=|H:C_{H}(y^{p})|=p
, by
[
sehgal1967
]
. Therefore
C_{H}(y)=M
,
or equivalently
y\in Z(M)
. Now,
M
is generated by
Z(H)
and all conjugates of
y
, which are
also contained in
Z(M)
. Hence
M
is abelian. This ends the proof.

∎

In the counterexample the subgroup
C_{G}\left(G^{\prime}/\Phi\left(G^{\prime}\right)\right)=G
hence Proposition
8
can not be applied for

.
Simultanously, Proposition
8
shows that there are no similiar counterexamples for odd primes.

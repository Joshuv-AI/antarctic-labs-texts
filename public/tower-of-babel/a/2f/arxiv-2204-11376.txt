Model Repair via Symmetry
Paul Attie, William Cocke

Abstract
The symmetry of a Kripke structure ℳ \mathcal{M} has been exploited to replace a model check of ℳ \mathcal{M} by a model check of the potentially smaller structure 𝒩 \mathcal{N} obtained as the quotient of ℳ \mathcal{M} by its symmetry group G G . We extend previous work to model repair: identify a substructure that satisfies a given temporal logic formula. We show that the substructures of ℳ \mathcal{M} that are preserved by G G form a lattice that maps to the substructure lattice of 𝒩 \mathcal{N} . We also show the existence of a monotone Galois connection between the lattice of substructures of 𝒩 \mathcal{N} and the lattice of substructures of ℳ \mathcal{M} that are ”maximal” w.r.t. an appropriately defined group action of G G on ℳ \mathcal{M} . These results enable us to repair 𝒩 \mathcal{N} and then to lift the repair to ℳ \mathcal{M} . We can thus repair symmetric finite-state concurrent programs by repairing the corresponding 𝒩 \mathcal{N} , thereby effecting program repair while avoiding state-explosion.

4
Substructures of Kripke structures

Symmetry-based repairs are motivated by the correspondence between certain substructures of two related Kripke structures which we introduce as Theorem
5.16
. A substructure of a Kripke structure is defined as follows:

Definition 4.1

(Substructure)
.

Given Kripke structures
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
, we say that

\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
is a
substructure
of
\mathcal{M}
, denoted

\mathcal{M^{\prime}}\leq\mathcal{M}
, iff the following all hold:

1.

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{S}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{S}
.

2.

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{S_{0}}=\mathcal{M}_{S_{0}}\cap\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{S}
.

3.

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{T}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{T}
.

4.

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{AP}=\mathcal{M}_{AP}
.

5.

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{L}=\mathcal{M}_{L}\!\upharpoonright\!S^{\prime}
(where

\!\upharpoonright\!
denotes domain restriction).

We require substructures to be total. To reiterate, a substructure
\mathcal{N}
of a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
is
total
. For mathematical necessity in what follows, we allow for the ‘empty’ substructure. Since substructures must be total, some Kripke structures contain only two substructures, i.e., the empty substructure and the whole structure.

We return briefly to our examples.

(
Example 2-mutex
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the Kripke structure correspond to our running example 2-mutex (see Figure

).

We can form a number of substructures of
\mathcal{M}
. For example, there is a substructure of
\mathcal{M}
consisting only of states
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}
and
s_{5}
along with all edges from
\mathcal{M}
. This substructure corresponds to computations wherein each process must return to neutral before the other process can enter the trying state. The states
s_{0},s_{1},s_{3}
also form a substructure.

As seen in the above examples, even a simple Kripke structure can have many different substructures, even on the same set of states.

We can define a partially ordered set of substructures of a Kripke structure. This partially ordered set is a lattice i.e., it has a join and a meet operation. Before we define this lattice, we investigate a few more basic properties regarding substructures of Kripke structures.

Given a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
, a substructure
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{M}
, and a substructure
\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
of
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
it is immediate that
\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
is a substructure of
\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and suppose that
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
are substructures of
\mathcal{M}
. Then

\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{N}_{S}\cup\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{S},\,\mathcal{N}_{S_{0}}\cup\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{S_{0}},\,\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{R}\cup\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{R},\,\mathcal{M}_{L}\!\upharpoonright\!(\mathcal{N}_{S}\cup\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{S}),\,\mathcal{M}_{X})

is the smallest substructure of
\mathcal{M}
containing both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We will write
\mathcal{N}_{\vee}
for
\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. By construction
\mathcal{N}_{\vee}
contains both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be any substructure of
\mathcal{M}
containing both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. Clearly
\left(\mathcal{N}_{\vee}\right)_{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{S}
,
\left(\mathcal{N}_{\vee}\right)_{S_{0}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{S_{0}}
,
\mathcal{T}_{R}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{R}
, and
\mathcal{T}_{L}
extends
\left(\mathcal{N}_{\vee}\right)_{L}
.

It remains to show that
\mathcal{N}_{\vee}
is a substructure, i.e., that
\mathcal{N}_{\vee}
is total. Given a state
s_{1}
in
\left(\mathcal{N}_{v}ee\right)_{S}
, we know that either
s\in\mathcal{N}_{S}
or
s\in\mathcal{N}_{S}^{\prime}
. If
s\in\mathcal{N}_{S}
, then there is an
t\in\mathcal{N}_{S}
such that
(s,t)\in\mathcal{N}_{T}\subseteq\left(\mathcal{N}_{\vee}\right)_{T}
. By symmetry if
s\in\mathcal{N}_{S}^{\prime}
then there is an
t\in\mathcal{N}_{S}^{\prime}
with
(s,t)\in\left(\mathcal{N}_{\vee}\right)_{T}
.
∎

Given a finite set
X=\{X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
of substructures of
\mathcal{M}
, we can define the structure
\bigvee X=X_{0}\vee X_{1}\vee\dots\vee X_{n}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and suppose that
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
are substructures of
\mathcal{M}
. Then there is a largest substructure of
\mathcal{M}
contained in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The empty structure is contained in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. Let
X
be the collection of structures contained in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. Then
\bigvee X
is contained in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
and is the largest substructure of
\mathcal{M}
contained in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
.
∎

Definition 4.6

(Join, Meet of substructures)
.

Let
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
be two substructures of
\mathcal{M}
. The
join
of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, written
\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, is the smallest substructure of
\mathcal{M}
containing both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. The
meet
of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, written
\mathcal{N}\wedge\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, is the largest substructure of
\mathcal{M}
contained in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
.

We note that for two substructures
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{M}
, the join
\mathcal{N}\lor\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
has the simple description as given in Lemma
4.4
. However, the meet
\mathcal{N}\land\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
, while well-defined, does not have such a simple description. It is possible that for two substructure
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
of a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
, there are no substructures contain in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
. Hence the largest substructure contained in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
could be empty.

We can now define a lattice of substructures
\Lambda_{\mathcal{M}}
for a given structure
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 4.7

(Lattice of Substructures)
.

Given a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
the
lattice of substructures of
\mathcal{M}
is

\Lambda_{\mathcal{M}}=\left(\left\{\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{N}\text{ is a substructure of }\mathcal{M}\right\},\leq\right),

where the meet and join in

There is another lattice of substructures related to
\mathcal{M}
. Given a subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{S}
we can define the substructure
\mathcal{N}=\langle S^{\prime}\rangle
of
\mathcal{M}
generated by
S^{\prime}
as the largest substructure
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}_{S}\subseteq S^{\prime}
, equivalently

\mathcal{N}=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N^{\prime}}\leq\mathcal{M}\\

5
Quotient structures.

Broadly speaking, a symmetry of an object captures the concept of what we mean for two objects to be equal. For Kripke structures, we are in general not interested in full symmetry. Instead, we are interested in a concept of partial symmetry that allows for certain computations to take place. The reason for this is because true symmetry of a Kripke structure would have to act on not only the underlying state-space, but the set of atomic propositions as well. We introduce a related concept, which we call a state-mapping. Some authors call a ‘state-mapping’ a ‘symmetry’ of
\mathcal{M}
. Due to the mathematical meaning of the word symmetry, we do not follow this tradition.

5.1
Groups Acting on Kripke Structures

Definition 5.1
.

A state-mapping of
\mathcal{M}
is a graph isomorphism of the state-space of
\mathcal{M}
such that its restriction to the initial states is also an isomorphism, e.g., takes initial states to initial states. Formally, for a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
, a
state-mapping
of
\mathcal{M}
is a bijection
f:\mathcal{M}_{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{S}
such that:

•

f(\mathcal{M}_{S_{0}})=\mathcal{M}_{S_{0}};

•

For states
s,t\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
we have that
(s,t)\in\mathcal{M}_{T}\iff(f(s),f(t))\in\mathcal{M}_{T}
.

The set of all state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
forms a group.

Definition 5.2

(
G
-closed)
.

For a group
G
of state-mappings of a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
, a substructure
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}
is called
G
-closed
, if
G
is a group of state-mappings of
\mathcal{N}
, i.e., for every
g\in G
and
s\in\mathcal{N}_{S}
we have
g(s)\in\mathcal{N}_{S}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and let
G
be a group of state mappings of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
be two
G
-closed substructures of
\mathcal{M}
. Then

\mathcal{N}\lor\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{N}\land\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
are both
G
-closed.

Proof.

We first show that
\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
is
G
-closed. Since
(\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N^{\prime}})_{S}=\mathcal{N}_{S}\cup\mathcal{N^{\prime}}_{S}
, the group
G
acts on the states of
\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N^{\prime}}.
We will show that every element of
G
is a state-mapping on
\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N^{\prime}}.

Let
s,t\in(\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N^{\prime}})_{S}
. If
g(s)=g(t)
then
s=t
since
g
is bijection on
\mathcal{M}_{S}
. Similarly, for any
s\in(\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N^{\prime}})_{S}
, since
g^{-1}(s)\in(\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N^{\prime}})_{S}
.

If
(s,t)\in(\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N^{\prime}})_{T}
, then
(s,t)
is in one of
\mathcal{N}_{T}
or
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}_{T}
. In either case,
(g(s),g(t))
is contained in the same set of transitions, and thus contained in
(\mathcal{N}\vee\mathcal{N^{\prime}})_{T}
.

We now consider
\mathcal{N}\land\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
. Recall that
\mathcal{N}\wedge\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
, is the largest substructure contained in both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
. If
s\in(\mathcal{N}\wedge\mathcal{N^{\prime}})_{S}
, and
g\in G
, then there is a substructure of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
containing
g(s)
. Moreover as the largest substructure of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
,
\mathcal{N}\wedge\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
must contain this substructure, hence
g(s)\in\mathcal{N}\wedge\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
.

Consider
(s,t)\in(\mathcal{N}\land\mathcal{N^{\prime}})_{T}
. Since
G
is a group of state mappings of
\mathcal{N}\land\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
there is a substructure of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
containing
(g(s),g(t))
. Since
\mathcal{N}\land\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
is the join of all substructures contained in
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
, we conclude that it must contain
(g(s),g(t))
as well.
∎

By Lemma
5.3
, we see that the
G
-closed substructures of
\mathcal{M}
forms a sublattice of
\Lambda_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Definition 5.4

(Lattice of
G
-closed substructures)
.

Given a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
and a group
G
of state mappings of
\mathcal{M}
, the poset of
G
-closed substructures of
\mathcal{M}
forms a lattice. We call this lattice the

-closed substructures of

(Example Box)

Let
\mathcal{M}
be Example Box, i.e., the Kripke structure described in Example

be the map that simultaneously switches

and

, and switches

5.2
Constructing the Quotient Model

Give a group
G
of state-mappings of a model
\mathcal{M}
, we want to construct a quotient model
\mathcal{M}_{G}
. However, as noted, state-mappings do not contain any information about
\mathcal{M}_{L}
. To remedy this situation, we need a function that assigns a representative to each orbit of
G
.

Definition 5.6

(Representative map)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and suppose that
G
is a group of state-mappings of
\mathcal{G}
. A
representative map
of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
G
is a function
\vartheta_{G}:\mathcal{M}_{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{S}
satisfying the following:

•

For all
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
, if there is a
g\in G
such that
g(s)=s^{\prime}
then
\vartheta_{G}(s)=\vartheta_{G}(s^{\prime})
. (respects orbits)

•

For all
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
, if there is no
g\in G
such that
g(s)=s^{\prime}
then

\vartheta_{G}(s)\neq\vartheta_{G}(s^{\prime})
. (separates orbits)

•

For all
s\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
, we have that
\vartheta_{G}(\vartheta_{G}(s))=\vartheta_{G}(s)
, i.e., each orbit has a stable representative. (idempotent)

We are now ready to define the quotient structure.

Definition 5.7

(Quotient structure)
.

Given a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
, a group
G
of state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
, and a representative map
\vartheta_{G}
of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
G
, we define the
quotient structure

\mathcal{M}/\vartheta_{G}
of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to
G
and
\vartheta_{G}
as follows:

The set of states of
\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
consists of the image of
\vartheta_{G}
. We borrow the quotient notation from group theory and write
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
for
\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
and
\overline{s}
for
\vartheta_{G}(s)
.

We have
(\overline{s},\overline{t})\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{T}
if and only if there is some
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
with
\vartheta_{G}(s^{\prime})=\overline{s}
and some
t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
with
\vartheta_{G}(t^{\prime})=\overline{t}
such that
(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{T}
.

An initial state of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
is the image under
\vartheta_{G}
of an initial state of
\mathcal{M}
.

We define
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{L}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{S}\rightarrow 2^{AP}
by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{L}(\overline{s})=\mathcal{M}_{L}(\vartheta_{G}(s))
.

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{AP}=\mathcal{M}_{AP}.

We note that the states of a quotient structure correspond exactly to the orbits of states of the original structure. For transitions, we have a slightly more subtle correspondence.
Consider the following example:

Example 5.8

(Example Box)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
and

\vartheta_{G}
be defined by
\vartheta(x_{i})=x_{1}
for

. Then the quotient structure

has exactly 2 states,
s_{1}=\vartheta_{G}(s_{1})=\vartheta_{G}(s_{2})

, will map to this structure via

Many of these substructures do no have

As shown in Example
5.8
, the existence of a transition in the quotient structure, does not imply the existence of “the same” transition in the original structure. However, it does imply the existence of a corresponding transition in the original structure. Hence it is true that for every path through a quotient structure there is a corresponding path through the original Kripke structure. This property is what allows for model checking of the original structure via model checking of the quotient. This path correspondence is formalized in the below theorem which has appeared in many texts. See for example the work by Emerson and Sistla
[
26
,
3.1
]
.

Theorem 5.9

(
Path Correspondence Theorem
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and let
G
be a group of state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\vartheta_{G}
be any representative map of
\mathcal{M}
with respect of
G
. Write
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
for
\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
. There is a bidirectional correspondence between paths of
\mathcal{M}
and paths of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
. Formally we have the following:

1.

If
x=s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},\dots
is a path in
\mathcal{M}
, then
\overline{x}=\overline{s_{0}},\overline{s_{1}},\overline{s_{2}},\dots
is a path in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
where
\overline{s_{i}}=\vartheta_{G}(s_{i})
.

2.

If
\overline{x}=\overline{s_{0}},\overline{s_{1}},\overline{s_{2}},\dots
is a path in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
, then for every state
s^{\prime}_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{S}
such that
\vartheta_{G}(s^{\prime}_{0})=\overline{s_{0}}
there is a path
s^{\prime}_{0},s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2},\dots
such that
\vartheta_{G}(s^{\prime}_{i})=\overline{s_{i}}
.

Proof.

1. Given
(s_{i},s_{i+1})\in\mathcal{M}_{R}
, then
(\overline{s_{i}},\overline{s_{i+1}})\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{R}.

2. Suppose that
(\overline{s_{i}},\overline{s_{i+1}})\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{R}
. Then there is some
(t,t^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{R}
with
t\in\overline{s_{i}}
and
t^{\prime}\in\overline{s_{i+1}}.
Suppose that we have a path
s_{0}^{\prime},\dots,s_{i}^{\prime}
such that
s_{0}^{\prime}\in\overline{s_{0}}
,
s_{1}^{\prime}\in\overline{s_{1}},\dots,s_{i}^{\prime}\in\overline{s_{i}}
and
(s_{0}^{\prime},s_{1}^{\prime}),(s_{1}^{\prime},s_{2}^{\prime}),\dots,(s_{i-1}^{\prime},s_{i}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{R}.
Since both
t,s_{i}^{\prime}\in\overline{s_{i}}
, there is a group element
g\in G
such that
g(t)=s_{i}^{\prime}
. Then
g(s_{0}^{\prime}),g(s_{1}^{\prime}),\dots,g(s_{i}^{\prime})=t,s_{i+1}
is a partial path in
\mathcal{M}
Continuing in this manner we see the existence of the desired path in
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

We would like to extend the path correspondence between
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}/\left(G,\vartheta_{G}\right)
, for a group
G
of state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
and a representative map
\vartheta_{G}
to one of substructures of Kripke structures. To do this, we need to identify a correspondence between
G
-closed substructures of
\mathcal{M}
and substructures of
\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
. For
\mathcal{N}
a
G
-closed substructure of
\mathcal{M}
, there is a natural map
\mathcal{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
, which we call the quotient map. The quotient map establishes a lattice homomorphism between
\Lambda_{\mathcal{M},G}
and
\Lambda_{\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})}.

We prove this property in a series of lemmas.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and
G
a group of state mappings of
\mathcal{M}
with representative map
\vartheta_{G}
. Then for every substructure
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
, there is a
G
-closed substructure
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})=\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

We will construct a
G
-closed substructure
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})=\mathcal{N}
. For the states of
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
, we select exactly the
G
-orbits of the representative states of
\mathcal{N}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}_{S}=\{g(s):\forall g\in G,s\in\mathcal{N}_{S}\}
. For transitions, we note that for every transition
(s,t)\in\mathcal{N}_{T}
there is at least one transition
(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{T}
such that
\vartheta(s^{\prime})=s
and
\vartheta(t^{\prime})=t
. We add this transition and its orbit under
G
to
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
. Hence for any state
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}_{S}
there is a transition
(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})
for some
t^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}_{S}
. We conclude that
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
is total. Since
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
is determined by its states and transitions as a substructure, the lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma
5.10
establishes that the quotient map is surjective. The next lemma demonstrates that it is a homomorphism of the join-semilattices
\Lambda_{M,G}
and
\Lambda_{M/(G,\vartheta_{G})}
. We note that it is not a homomorphism of the lattices themselves, because the meet of two
G
-closed structures mapping might be empty due to the requirement that Kripke structures are total.

Lemma 5.11

(Quotient map respects join)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and
G
a group of state mappings of
\mathcal{M}
with representative map
\vartheta_{G}
. Let
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N^{\prime}}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{M},G}
. Then

(\mathcal{N}\lor\mathcal{N^{\prime}})/(G,\vartheta_{G})=\mathcal{N}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\lor\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G}).

Proof.

Clearly, by definition a state
\vartheta_{G}(s)\in\left(\mathcal{N}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\lor\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\right)_{S}
if and only if it is in at least one of
\left(\mathcal{N}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\right)_{S}
or
\left(\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\right)_{S}
which occurs if and only if
\vartheta_{G}(s)
is in one of the
G
-closed substructures
\mathcal{N}
or
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
.

For transitions, we note that a transition in
\left(\mathcal{N}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\lor\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\right)_{T}
is entirely contained in one of
\left(\mathcal{N}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\right)_{T}
or
\left(\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\right).
Hence the transition must come from either
\mathcal{N}
or
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
.
∎

As seen in Example
5.8
, for a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
with a group of state-mappings
G
of
\mathcal{M}
and representative map
\vartheta_{G}
, it is possible for multiple
G
-closed substructures of
\mathcal{M}
to map to the same substructure of the quotient structure
\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
.

Recall that the join of
G
-closed substructures of
M
is
G
-closed.

Definition 5.12

(
G
-maximal)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure with group
G
of state-mappings and representative map
\vartheta_{G}
.
A
G
-closed substructure
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}
is

-maximal if

\mathcal{N}=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{N^{\prime}}\in\Lambda_{\mathcal{M},G}\\
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\leq\mathcal{N}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N^{\prime}}.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and let
G
be a group of state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
with representative map
\vartheta_{G}
. Let
\mathcal{M^{\prime}},\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
be two
G
-maximal substructures of
\mathcal{M}
. Then
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}\lor\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
is
G
-maximal.

Proof.

Consider a
G
-closed substructure
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
such that
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\leq(\mathcal{M^{\prime}}\lor\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}})/(G,\vartheta_{G})
. Let
(s,t)\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}_{T}
. Since
(\vartheta_{G}(s),\vartheta_{G}(t))\in(\mathcal{M^{\prime}}\lor\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}})/(G,\vartheta_{G})_{T}
we conclude that
(\vartheta_{G}(s),\vartheta_{G}(t))
is in either
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
or
\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
. In either case, since
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
are both
G
-maximal the transitions
(g(s),g(t))
are already contained in
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
or
\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
.
∎

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and let
G
be a group of state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
with representative map
\vartheta_{G}
. Let
\mathcal{M^{\prime}},\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
be two
G
-maximal substructures of
\mathcal{M}
. Then
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}\land\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
is
G
-maximal.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{N}
is the
G
-maximal substructure of
\mathcal{M}
corresponding to
(\mathcal{M^{\prime}}\land\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}})/(G,\vartheta_{G})
. We note that
\mathcal{N}/(G,\vartheta_{G})\leq\mathcal{M^{\prime}}/(G,\vartheta_{G}).
Hence
\mathcal{N}\leq\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
. By symmetry
\mathcal{N}\leq\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}
and thus
\mathcal{N}\leq\mathcal{M^{\prime}}\land\mathcal{M^{\prime\prime}}.

∎

Combing Lemma

Definition 5.15

(
G
-maximal lattice of substructures)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and let
G
be a group of state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
with representative map
\vartheta_{G}
. Then the set of
G

forms a sublattice
\Lambda_{\mathcal{M},G-\text{max}}

While in general the quotient map from

to

is always surjective, when restricted to

the map is injective and a lattice isomorphism.

Theorem 5.16

(
G
-Maximal Lattice Correspondence
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Kripke structure and suppose that
G
is a group of state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
with a representative map
\vartheta_{G}
. Then the map
f(\mathcal{N})=\mathcal{N}/\left(G,\vartheta_{G}\right)
is an isomorphism between the lattice of
G
-maximal substructures of
\mathcal{M}
and the lattice of structures of
\mathcal{M}/\left(G,\vartheta_{G}\right)
. That is

is an isomorphism from
\Lambda_{M,G-\text{max}}
to
\Lambda_{M/(G,\vartheta_{G})}.

Proof.

As noted the
G
-maximal lattice of substructures is in bijection with the lattice of substructures of
\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
. Since the meet and join of two

So far, our correspondence between models

and

5.3
Semantic relationships between structures and quotient structures.

Let
G
be a symmetry group of
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 5.17
.

A CTL formula
f
is
G
-invariant
over
\mathcal{M}
, if for every state
s
, every
g\in G
, for all maximal propositional subformulae
P
of
f
, we have

\mathcal{M},s\models P\iff\mathcal{M},g(s)\models P.

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
G
be a group of state-mappings for a Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
. If
f
is
G
-invariant, then the valuation of
f
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}/\left(G,\vartheta_{G}\right)
does not depend on the choice of representative map
\vartheta_{G}
.

Proof.

Let
\vartheta_{G},\varphi_{G}
be two representative maps with respect to
G
. The quotients
\mathcal{M}/\left(G,\vartheta_{G}\right)
and
\mathcal{M}/\left(G,\varphi_{G}\right)
have corresponding transition maps. Moreover, for any maximal propositional subformula,
P
of
f
we have that

\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G}),\vartheta_{G}(s)\models P\iff\mathcal{M}/\left(G,\varphi_{G}\right),\varphi_{G}(s)\models P.

The valuation of
f
depends only on the valuation of its maximal propositional subformulae at all other states of the model. Since
\vartheta
and
\varphi
have the same evaluation of the maximal propositional subformulae at all states, the evaluation of
f
at all states is the same.
∎

This allows us to connect semantic statements about
\mathcal{M}
with semantic statements about
\mathcal{M}/(G,\vartheta_{G})
for formulae that are
G
-invariant.

If

and

is a representative map of

6
Repair of concurrent programs

We now apply the repair correspondence theorem to realize our goal: the repair of concurrent programs.
A concurrent program
P=P_{1}\!\parallel\!\ldots\!\parallel\!P_{n}
consists of
n
sequential processes executing in parallel. Each process
P_{i}

is a set of
i
-actions
, i.e., tuples

(s_{i},B\rightarrow A,t_{i})
, where
s_{i},t_{i}
are
local states
of
P_{i}
,
B
is a guard, and
A
is an assignment to shared variables.
We say
action
when we wish to ignore the identity of the process that executes the action.
Each
P_{i}
has a local set of atomic propositions
AP_{i}
which it alone can modify, but other processes can read. Each local state of
P_{i}
determines as assignment of truth values to the atomic propositions in
AP_{i}
. We set
AP=AP_{1}\cup\ldots\cup AP_{n}
.
A
global state

s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
of
P
is a tuple consisting of local states
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}

for processes
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
respectively, and values

v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
for shared variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
.
Define
s\!\upharpoonright\!i=s_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n

and
s(x_{j})=v_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
.
The truth values that atomic propositions in
AP_{i}
have in
s
must be the same as those in
s_{i}
.
The states of Kripke structures that we have treated above are global states as described here.

Given a concurrent program
P
and a CTL formula
f
, we wish to modify
P
to produce a
P^{\prime}
such that

\mathcal{M}\models f
, where
\mathcal{M}
is the global state transition graph of
P^{\prime}
. The modification is ”subtractive”, that is, it only removes behaviors, it does not add behaviors (which would be, in essence, a synthesis, and not a repair, algorithm).
We proceed as follows:

1.

Generate the symmetry-reduced state transition graph
\mathcal{N}
of
P
. We use the
algorithm given by Figure 1 in
[
26
]
, which is reproduced in Appendix ?.

2.

Repair
\mathcal{N}
w.r.t. the given CTL specification
f
. Let
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
be the repaired structure, so that
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}\models f
.

3.

Extract a repaired concurrent program
P^{\prime}
from
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}

Steps 2 and 3 are given in the subsections below.

6.1
Repair of symmetry-reduced structures

We apply the model repair algorithm of
[
2
]

to the symmetry-reduced state transition graph
\mathcal{N}
of
P
, and the specification
f
of
P
. This algorithm is sound and complete, so that if
\mathcal{N}
has some substructure that satisfies
f
, then the algorithm will return such a substructure
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
. If not, the algorithm will report that no repair exists, in which case there is no
subtractive modification of
P
which satisfies
f
.

6.2
Extraction of concurrent programs from symmetry-reduced structures

We extract concurrent program
P^{\prime}
from
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
following
[
15
]
, by projecting each transition in
s\mbox{$\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\,$}t\in\mathcal{N}_{T}
onto the process
P_{i}
making the transition.
For each
s\mbox{$\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\,$}t\in\mathcal{N}_{T}
we define

action{(s\mbox{$\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\,$}t)}
to be the

i
-action
(s\!\upharpoonright\!i,B\rightarrow A,t\!\upharpoonright\!i)
as follows.
The start, end states of this action are the projections of
s,t
respectively onto
P_{i}
.
We define the guard
B=``(\and_{Q\in\mathcal{N}_{L}(s)}Q)\land(\and_{Q\not\in\mathcal{N}_{L}(s)}\neg Q)\land(\and_{x\in\mbox{$\cal SH$}}x=s(x))\mbox{\rm''}

where
Q
ranges over
AP-AP_{i}
.
When
P_{i}
is in local state
s_{i}
, the guard
B
checks that the current global state is actually
s
.
We define the assignment
A=``/\!/x:=t(x)\mbox{\rm''}

where
x
ranges over all shared variables such that

t(x)\neq s(x)
. That is,
A
makes the necessary updates to the shared variables in moving from state
s
to state
t
.

Let
AC=\{action{(s\mbox{$\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\,$}t)}~|~s\mbox{$\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\,$}t\in\mathcal{N}_{T}\}
,
i.e.,
AC
is the set of actions generated by all the transitions of
\mathcal{N}
.
In principle, each process
P^{\prime}_{i}
of the repaired program
P^{\prime}
can be set to be the set of
i
-actions in
AC
. It is straightforward to show that
P^{\prime}
thus constituted generates exactly
\mathcal{N}
as its global state transition diagram. Since
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}\models f
, we also have
P^{\prime}\models f
as desired.
However, even though
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
is total, it is possible for some processes in
P^{\prime}
to have ”dead end” local states from which they have no outgoing actions.
We therefore repeatedly apply some
g\in G
to
AC
, until all such ”dead end” local states acquire at least one outgoing action.
One can continue this application of
g\in G
to
AC
until no more actions are generated, which produces a ”
G
-closed” program
P^{\prime}
. This is the ”largest” correct program that can be produced as a repair of
P
. Using the largest program may increase the available concurrency, but may produce a large number of actions, and so increase the complexity of the repair procedure.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
be a repaired Kripke structure as above,
P^{\prime}
be a concurrent program extracted from
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
as above,
and
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
be the global state transition diagram of
P^{\prime}
. Then every initial state of
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
is
G
-bisimilar to some initial state of
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}

Proof.

Let
\ \sim\
consist of the pairs

such that
s\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}_{S}
,
t\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{S}
, and
t=g(s)
for some
g\in G
.
The proof that
\ \sim\
is a
G

follows from the definitions of program extraction and
program execution semantics.
By the definitions of program extraction and
program execution semantics,
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
have isomorphic sets of initial states, with corresponding states having the same labels. It follows that these states are bisimilar.
Hence
\ \sim\
is a
G
-bisimulation which moreover relates each initial state of
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
to an initial state of
\mathcal{M^{\prime}}
and vice-versa.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{N^{\prime}}

\mathcal{M^{\prime}}

8
Conclusions

We presented a theory for the substructures of a given Kripke structure
\mathcal{M}
, their organization into lattices, and how these substructures can interact with a group of state-mappings of
\mathcal{M}
. We showed the existence of an isomorphism between the lattice
\Lambda_{M,G-\text{max}}
of
G
-maximal substructures of
\mathcal{M}
and the lattice of substructures of
\mathcal{M}(G,\vartheta_{G})
. Using this theory we were able to devise a method for repairing concurrent programs which exploits symmetry.

Definition A.1
.

\mathcal{M},s\models\varphi

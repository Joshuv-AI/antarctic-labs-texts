The L p L_{p} Gauss image problem
Chuanxi Wu, Di Wu, Ni Xiang

Abstract
. In this paper we study the L p L_{p} Gauss image problem, which is a generalization of the L p L_{p} Aleksandrov problem and the Gauss image problem in convex geometry. We obtain the existence result for the L p L_{p} Gauss image problem in two cases (i) p > 0 p>0 or (ii) p < 0 p<0 with the given even measures.

: the
L_{p}
Gauss image problem,
Surface area measure,
Curvature measure,
Existence of solution,
Blaskchke selection theorem.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
denote the set of convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which contains the
origin in its interior. For
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, its support function,
h_{K}
, is defined by

h_{K}(x):=\max_{y\in K}\langle y,x\rangle,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

where
\langle y,x\rangle
is the standard inner product of
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Suppose
K,L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
and
t\geq 0
, the Minkowski combination,
K+tL\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, is given by

h_{K+tL}=h_{K}+th_{L};

for negative
t
,
K+tL
can be defined if
|t|
is sufficient small.
Aleksandrov’s variational formula shows that

\frac{d}{dt}V(K+tL)\bigg|_{t=0}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L}(x)dS(K,x),\quad\forall L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0},

where
V(\cdot)
denotes the volume functional and
S(K,\cdot)
is the surface area measure.

A nature extension is
L_{p}
Minkowski sum, which was first defined by Firey in case
p\geq 1
.
Suppose
K,L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
and
t\geq 0
, the
L_{p}
Minkowski combination,
K+_{p}t\cdot L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, is defined by

h^{p}_{K+_{p}t\cdot L}=h^{p}_{K}+th^{p}_{L}.

In the early 1900’s,
K+_{p}t\cdot L
can be defined for negative
t
if
|t|
is sufficient small. Lutwak
[
36
]
showed the variational formula

\frac{d}{dt}V(K+_{p}t\cdot L)\bigg|_{t=0}=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h^{p}_{L}(x)dS_{p}(K,x),\quad\forall L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0},

where
S_{p}(K,\cdot)
is the
L_{p}
surface area measure, satisfying

(1.1)

dS_{p}(K,\cdot)=h_{K}^{1-p}dS(K,\cdot).

Thus by (
1.1
), the
L_{p}
surface area measure can be defined for any
p\in\mathbb{R}
.

Suppose
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
,
K^{\ast}
stands for the polar body of
K
, which is given by

(1.2)

K^{\ast}=\bigcap_{y\in K}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1\}.

By combining the concept of
L_{p}
Minkowski combination with that of polarity, we obtain another kind of combination, the
L_{p}
harmonic combination,
K\hat{+}_{p}t\cdot L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, is defined by

K\hat{+}_{p}t\cdot L=(K^{*}+_{p}t\cdot L^{*})^{*}.

Huang, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
25
]
considered the entropy functional

(1.3)

\mathcal{E}(K)=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log h_{K}(x)dx,

and got the variational formula

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(K\hat{+}_{p}t\cdot L)\bigg|_{t=0}=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho^{-p}_{L}(u)dJ_{p}(K,u),\quad\forall L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0},

where
\rho_{L}
is the radial function of
L
, given by

\rho_{L}(u):=\max\{t>0:tu\in L\},\quad\forall\,u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

They proved the
L_{p}
integral curvature
J_{p}(K,\cdot)
satisfies

(1.4)

dJ_{p}(K,\cdot)=\rho_{K}^{p}dJ(K,\cdot),

where
J(K,\cdot)
is the Aleksandrov integral curvature.

For any Borel measurable subset
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the radial Gauss image of
\omega
,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)
, is given by

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle\rho_{K}(u)u,x\rangle=h_{K}(x)\text{ for some }u\in\omega\}.

Then
J(K,\cdot)
is the spherical Lebesgue measure of the radial Gauss image
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}
, that is,

(1.5)

J(K,\omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)).

Recently, Boroczky, Lutwak, Yang, Zhang and Zhao
[
5
]

proposed the Gauss image measure
\lambda(K,\omega)
, which is a generalization of the Aleskandrov integral measure. We state it more precisely below.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
.
Then the Gauss image measure of
\lambda
via
K
is defined by

(1.6)

\lambda(K,\omega)=\lambda(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)),

where
\omega
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Based on (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), it is nature to consider the functional

(1.7)

G(K)=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log h_{K}(x)d\lambda(x).

And its variational formulas is

\frac{d}{dt}G(K\hat{+}_{p}t\cdot L)\bigg|_{t=0}=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{L}^{-p}(u)d\lambda_{p}(K,u),\quad\forall L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0},

where
\lambda_{p}(K,\cdot)
is the
L_{p}
Gauss image measure, satisfying

(1.8)

d\lambda_{p}(K,\cdot)=\rho_{K}^{p}d\lambda(K,\cdot).

It is clear that
\lambda_{0}(K,\cdot)=\lambda(K,\cdot)
, and (
1.8
) becomes (
1.4
) if
\lambda(K,\cdot)
is

. Therefore, the
L_{p}
Aleksandrov measure and the Gauss image measure
are special cases of the
L_{p}
Gauss image measure.

The existence result for the
L_{p}
Gauss image problem when
p>0
will be presented in the followong. The idea goes back as for as
[
25
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
p>0
. If
\lambda
and
\mu
are two finite Borel measures on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
which satisfy

(1)
\lambda
is absolutely continuous with respect to spherical Lebesgue measure;

(2)
\lambda(A)>0
for any nonempty open set
A\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
;

(3)
\mu
is not concentrated in any closed hemisphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Then there exists a convex body
K
such that
\mu=\lambda_{p}(K,\cdot)
.

If the given measures are even, the third condition in Theorem
1.2
is naturally satisfied, and we can get the existence result for
p<0
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
p<0
. If
\lambda
and
\mu
are two finite, even Borel measures on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
which satisfy

(1)
\lambda
is absolutely continuous with respect to spherical Lebesgue measure;

(2)
\lambda(A)>0
for any nonempty open set
A\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
;

(3)
\mu
is vanishes on great sub-spheres of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Then there exists an origin-symmetric convex body
K
such that
\mu=\lambda_{p}(K,\cdot)
.

In particular, when
\mu
has a density, say
f
, and
\lambda
has a density, say
g
, the
L_{p}
Gauss image problem asks: Under what conditions on the two given functions
f,g:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, does there exist a solution
h:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
, that is the support function of a convex body
K^{*}
, to the Monge-Ampère equation

(1.9)

g\bigg(\frac{\nabla h+hx}{|\nabla h+hx|}\bigg)\frac{h^{1-p}}{(|\nabla h|^{2}+h^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}}\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)=f(x)\quad\text{on}\quad\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Remark 1.4
.

The weak solution of the equation (
1.9
) for
g\equiv 1
was solved by Huang, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
25
]
.

It is worth pointing out that if
\lambda
and
\mu
have densities, the weak solution of the equation
(
1.9
) can be obtained according to Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
.

Remark 1.5
.

Assume
g>0
.

(1) For
p>0
, if for any hemisphere
\Theta
,

\int_{\Theta}f(x)dx>0,

then the equation (
1.9
) has a strictly positive solution.

(2) For
p<0
, if
f
and
g
are even functions, and if

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(x)dx>0,

then the equation (
1.9
) has a strictly positive even solution.

Let

, which means

.

Suppose
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, recall
h_{K}
and
\rho_{K}
denote the support function and the radial function of
K
, respectively,

(2.1)

h_{K}(x):=\max_{y\in K}\langle y,x\rangle,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

and

(2.2)

\rho_{K}(u):=\max\{t>0:tu\in K\},\quad\forall\,u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Note that

(2.3)

\partial K=\{\rho_{K}(u)u:u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}.

By (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), the support function
h_{K}
and the radial function
\rho_{K}
have the following relationship:

h_{K}(x)=\max_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle u,x\rangle\rho_{K}(u),\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},

and

\rho_{K}(u)=\max_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\langle u,x\rangle/h_{K}(x),\quad u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

The definition of polar body, i.e.(
1.2
), shows that

(2.4)

\rho_{K}=1/h_{K^{*}},\quad h_{K}=1/\rho_{K^{*}}.

From (
2.4
), it is clear that

(2.5)

K^{**}=K.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be a closed set that is not contained in any closed hemisphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. And
h,\rho:\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty)
are continuous functions. The Wulff shape determined by
h
, is denoted by

[h]=\bigcap_{x\in\Omega}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot y\leq h(x)\},

and the convex hull
\langle\rho\rangle
generated by
\rho
, is denoted by

\langle\rho\rangle=\text{conv}\{\rho(u)u:u\in\Omega\}.

And a useful fact is that, see
[
24
]
,

(2.6)

[h]^{*}=\langle 1/h\rangle.

If
h_{K}
is the support function of a convex body
K
, then

[h_{K}]=K,

and if
\rho_{K}
is the radial function of a convex body
K
, then

(2.7)

\langle\rho_{K}\rangle=K.

Assume
h_{t}:\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is a continuous function defined for
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
by

(2.8)

\log h_{t}(x)=\log h(x)+tf(x)+o(t,x),

where

is continuous,
\delta>0
and

is continuous and for

,
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{o(t,x)}{t}=0
.
We shall write
[h_{t}]
as
[h,f,t]
. And if
h
is the support function of a convex body
K
, write
[h_{t}]
as
[K,f,t]
.

Suppose
\rho_{t}:\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is a continuous function defined for
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
by

(2.9)

\log\rho_{t}(u)=\log\rho(u)+tg(u)+o(t,u),

where
g:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous and

is continuous and for

,
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{o(t,u)}{t}=0
. We shall write
\langle\rho_{t}\rangle
as
\langle\rho,g,t\rangle
. And if
\rho
is the radial function of a convex body
K
, write
\langle\rho_{t}\rangle
as
\langle K,g,t\rangle
.

The
L_{p}
Minkowski sum is the key role in the
L_{p}
Brunn-Minkowski theory.
For fixed
p\in\mathbb{R}
, suppose
K
,
L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
and
a
,
b\geq 0
, the
L_{p}
Minkowski combination,
a\cdot K+_{p}b\cdot L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, is defined by the Wulff shape

a\cdot K+_{p}b\cdot L=[(ah_{K}^{p}+bh_{L}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}],\quad p\neq 0,

and for
p=0
,

a\cdot K+_{0}b\cdot L=[h_{K}^{a}h_{L}^{b}].

The Wulff shape allows us to consider the case
a
or

is negative, with strictly positive
ah_{K}^{p}+bh_{L}^{p}
. The
L_{p}
harmonic combination,
a\cdot K\hat{+}_{p}b\cdot L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, is defined by

a\cdot K\hat{+}_{p}b\cdot L=(a\cdot K^{*}+_{p}b\cdot L^{*})^{*}.

If
\mu
is a fixed non-zero finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
then we define

(2.10)

||f:\mu||_{p}=\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mu|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f^{p}d\mu\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad p\neq 0,

and

(2.11)

||f:\mu||_{0}=\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{|\mu|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log fd\mu\bigg),\quad\forall f\in C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

For any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
0<\delta<1
,
\omega_{\delta}(x_{0})
and
\omega^{\prime}_{\delta}(x_{0})
are defined by

(2.12)

\omega_{\delta}(x_{0})=\{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\langle u,x_{0}\rangle\geq\delta\},

(2.13)

\omega^{\prime}_{\delta}(x_{0})=\{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:|\langle u,x_{0}\rangle|\geq\delta\}.

It is obvious that

\omega^{\prime}_{\delta}(x_{0})=\omega_{\delta}(x_{0})\cup\omega_{\delta}(-x_{0}).

And
x_{0}^{\bot}
denotes the hyperplane whose normal is
x_{0}
and the origin
0\in x_{0}^{\bot}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose
K
,
L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, their Hausdorff metric is given by

d(K,L)=\max\{\sup_{x\in K}dist(x,L),\sup_{y\in L}dist(K,y)\}.

where

and

is the distance of

and

in

.

, which means

.

.

,

3.
L_{p}
Gauss image problem

In this section, we review some of the standard facts on the radial Gauss map
\alpha_{K}
and reverse radial Gauss map
\alpha_{K}^{*}
.

Suppose
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
and
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the supporting hyperplane of
K
in direction
x
is given by

H_{K}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle=h_{K}(x)\},

and
x
is called the normal vector of
K
at
y
.

For
\sigma\subset\partial K
, the spherical image of
\sigma
,
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{K}(\sigma):\partial K\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, is defined by

\boldsymbol{\nu}_{K}(\sigma)=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:y\in H_{K}(x)\text{ for some }y\in\sigma\}.

Let
\sigma_{K}\subset\partial K
be the set consisting of
y\in\partial K
, for which the set
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{K}(\{y\})
contains more than a single element.
Define the spherical image map

\nu_{K}:\partial K\backslash\sigma_{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n-1}

such that
\nu_{K}(y)
is the unique element of
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{K}(\{y\})
.

For
\eta\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the reverse spherical image of
\eta
,
y
{}_{K}(\eta):\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\partial K
, is defined by

\textbf{y}_{K}(\eta)=\{y\in\partial K:y\in H_{K}(x)\text{ for some }x\in\eta\}.

The set
\eta_{K}
is made up of
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, for which the set
\textbf{y}_{K}(\{x\})
contains more than a single element. Define the reverse spherical image map

y_{K}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\backslash\eta_{K}\rightarrow\partial K

such that
y_{K}(x)
is the unique element of
\textbf{y}_{K}(\{x\})
. It is well known that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma_{K})=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\eta_{K})=0
and
\nu_{K}
,
y_{K}
are continuous functions,
the details can be found in
[
41
]
.

Suppose
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, the radial map of
K
,
r_{K}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\partial K
, is defined by

r_{K}(u)=\rho_{K}(u)u,

its reverse map
r^{-1}_{K}:\partial K\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is given by

r^{-1}_{K}(y)=\frac{y}{|y|}.

With the above preparation, we can define the radial Gauss image and reverse radial Gauss image. Specifically, let
\omega
and
\eta
be subsets of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the radial Gauss image of
\omega
,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)
, is defined by

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\rho_{K}(u)u\in H_{K}(x)\text{ for some }u\in\omega\};

and the reverse radial Gauss image of
\eta
,
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\ast}_{K}(\eta)
, is defined by

\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\ast}_{K}(\eta)=\{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\rho_{K}(u)u\in H_{K}(x)\text{ for some }x\in\eta\}.

Assume
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, the radial Gauss map of
K
,
\alpha_{K}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\backslash\omega_{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, is given by

\alpha_{K}=\nu_{K}(r_{K}),

where
\omega_{K}=r^{-1}_{K}(\sigma_{K})
, and the reverse radial Gauss map of
K
,
\alpha^{*}_{K}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\backslash\eta_{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, is given by

\alpha^{*}_{K}=r^{-1}_{K}(y_{K}).

It was shown in
[
24
]
that the reverse radial Gauss
image of
K
and the radial Gauss image of
K^{*}
are identical, that is

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K^{\ast}}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}^{\ast}.

And the definitions of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}^{*}
yield that

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{tK}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K},\quad\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}_{tK}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{*}_{K},\quad\forall t>0.

Recall the Gauss image
measure
\lambda(K,\cdot)
of
\lambda
via
K
is defined by

\lambda(K,\omega)=\lambda(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)).

When
\lambda
is a Borel measure which is absolutely continuous with respect to the spherical Lebusgus measure, the Gauss image measure is
a Borel measure. The integral representation of
\lambda(K,\cdot)
can be found in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
\lambda
is a Borel measure which is absolutely continuous with respect to spherical Lebesgue measure and
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
, then

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(u)d\lambda(K,u)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(\alpha^{\ast}_{K}(x))d\lambda(x),\quad\forall f\in C(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}).

Proof.

Note that for any
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\backslash\omega_{K}
,

\alpha_{K}^{*}(x)\in\omega\Leftrightarrow x\in\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega),

which implies that

(3.2)

\chi_{\omega}(\alpha_{K}^{*}(x))=\chi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)}(x).

We first show (
3.1
) holds for simple function

\varphi=\sum_{i}c_{i}\chi_{\omega_{i}},

where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\chi_{\omega_{i}}
is the characteristic function of Borel subset
\omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Since
\lambda
is absolutely continuous with respect to spherical Lebesgue measure, (
3.2
) shows that

(3.3)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\chi_{\omega}(\alpha_{K}^{*}(x))d\lambda(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\chi_{\alpha_{K}(\omega)}(x)d\lambda(x),

then (
1.6
) and (
3.3
) mean

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(u)d\lambda(K,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sum_{i}c_{i}\chi_{\omega_{i}}(u)d\lambda(K,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}c_{i}\lambda(K,\omega_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}c_{i}\lambda(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sum_{i}c_{i}\chi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{K}(\omega_{i})}(x)d\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\sum_{i}c_{i}\chi_{\omega}(\alpha_{K}^{*}(x))d\lambda(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\varphi(\alpha_{K}^{*}(x))d\lambda(x).

Let
f:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function, then there exists a sequence of simple functions
\{\varphi_{k}\}
such that
\varphi_{k}\rightarrow f
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. By the dominated convergence theorem, it holds

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(u)d\lambda(K,u)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f(\alpha_{K}^{\ast}(x))d\lambda(x).

This finishes the proof.
∎

By (

It is worth to point out the Gauss image measure as a functional from

4.
Variational formulas for entropy of convex bodies

In this section, variational formulas for the general entropy of convex bodies will be given. Recall the general entropy

G(K)=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log h_{K}(x)d\lambda(x),

and the dual general entropy, defined by

it is clear that for any

As will be shown, the following Lemma in
[

turns out to be a critical property.

Lemma 4.1

Let

Furthermore, there exist

and

|\log h_{\langle\rho_{t}\rangle}(x)-\log h_{\langle\rho_{0}\rangle}(x)|\leq M|t|,\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times(-\delta,\delta).

It is sufficient to make the following Lemma together with Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
\lambda
is a Borel measure which is absolutely continuous with respect to spherical Lebesgue measure. Let

and

.

\lambda

5.
Existence of solutions to the
L_{p}
Gauss image problem

For given Borel measures
\lambda
and
\mu
, and
p\neq 0
, define the functional
\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}:C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, for any
f\in C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\log h_{\langle f\rangle}(x)d\lambda(x)-\frac{1}{p}\log(\frac{1}{|\mu|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f^{-p}(u)d\mu(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\langle f\rangle)/|\lambda|+\log||f:\mu||_{-p}.

It is easy to check that
\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(f)
is homogeneous of degree 0, that is

(5.2)

\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(tf)=\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(f),\quad\forall t>0,f\in C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

The maximization problem is:

(5.3)

\sup\{\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(f):f\in C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\}.

The following Lemma shows that the solution of maximization problem (
5.3
) must be radial function of a convex body
K
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
p\neq 0
, then a convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
is a solution of the maximization problem

\sup\{\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(\rho_{K}):K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}\}

if and only if
\rho_{K}
is a solution of the maximization problem

\sup\{\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(f):f\in C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\}.

Proof.

The convex hull is given by

\langle f\rangle=\text{conv}\{f(u)u:u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\},\quad\forall f\in C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),

then it is easily seen that

\rho_{\langle f\rangle}\geq f,

which implies that

(5.4)

||\rho_{\langle f\rangle}:\mu||_{-p}\geq||f:\mu||_{-p}.

By (
2.7
), that is

\langle\rho_{\langle f\rangle}\rangle=\langle f\rangle,

hence

(5.5)

G(\langle\rho_{\langle f\rangle}\rangle)=G(\langle f\rangle).

Applying (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) we conclude that

\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(f)\leq\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,p}(\rho_{\langle f\rangle}).

∎

for some

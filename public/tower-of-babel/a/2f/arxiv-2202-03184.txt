Abstract
. Let G G be a finite pseudoreflection group and Ω ⊆ ℂ d \Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d} be a bounded domain which is a G G -space. We establish identities involving Toeplitz operators on the weighted Bergman spaces of Ω \Omega and Ω / G \Omega/G using invariant theory and representation theory of G . G. This, in turn, provides techniques to study algebraic properties of Toeplitz operators on the weighted Bergman space on Ω / G . \Omega/G. We specialize on the generalized zero-product problem and characterization of commuting pairs of Toeplitz operators. As a consequence, more intricate results on Toeplitz operators on the weighted Bergman spaces on some specific quotient domains (namely symmetrized polydisc, monomial polyhedron, Rudin’s domain) have been obtained.

Let
\Omega

\mathbb{C}^{d}

For

Toeplitz operator

(T_{u}f)=P_{\omega}(uf),

where

Then

G

G

G

[

[

1.1.
Generalized zero-product problem

Let
\omega:\Omega\to(0,\infty)
be a
G
-invariant continuous function. For each one-dimensional representation
\varrho
of
G,
we define the relative invariant subspace of the weighted Bergman space
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
by

\displaystyle R^{G}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega))=\{f\in\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega):\sigma(f)=\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)f~{\rm for~~all~}\sigma\in G\},

where
\chi_{\varrho}
denotes the character of
\varrho.
A characterization of the relative invariant subspace is that every
f\in R^{G}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega))
is divisible by some polynomial
\ell_{\varrho}
and the quotient is in the ring of
G
-invariant holomorphic functions on
\Omega

[
20
, p. 8, Lemma 2.8]
. An explicit expression for
\ell_{\varrho}
has been obtained from the representation
\varrho

[
32
, p. 139, Theorem 3.1]
(cf. Lemma
2.4
). For each one-dimensional representation
\varrho
of
G,
we set

\displaystyle\omega_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}))=\frac{|\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{z})|^{2}}{|J_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{z})|^{2}}\omega(\boldsymbol{z}),

( 1.2)

where
J_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
is the determinant of the complex jacobian of the basic polynomial map
\boldsymbol{\theta}
associated to the group
G.
Then
R^{G}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega))
is isometrically isomorphic to the weighted Bergman space
\mathbb{A}_{\omega_{\varrho}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))
with weight function
\omega_{\varrho}

[
20
, p. 2, Theorem 1.1]
.

We also obtain that for a
G
-invariant function
\widetilde{u}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega),
there exists

such that
\widetilde{u}=u\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}
(cf. Remark
2.2
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite pseudoreflection group, the bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a
G
-space and
\boldsymbol{\theta}:\Omega\to\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)
is a basic polynomial map associated to the group

Let

and
\widetilde{q}
be

such that
\widetilde{u}=u\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}
,
\widetilde{v}=v\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}
and
\widetilde{q}=q\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

1.

Suppose that for a one-dimensional representation
\mu
of
G,

on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\mu}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)),
then

(i)

T_{u}T_{v}=T_{q}
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))
for every one-dimensional representation
\varrho
of
G,
and

(ii)

T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}=T_{\widetilde{q}}
on
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega),

where the weight function

is
G

\omega_{\varrho}:\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)\to(0,\infty)
is as defined in Equation (

) for every one-dimensional representation

.

2.

Conversely, if

\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))

1.2.
Characterization of commuting pairs of Toeplitz operators

The following theorem states that a pair of commuting Toeplitz operators on the weighted Bergman space on a quotient domain
\Omega/G
can be characterized by a pair of commuting Toeplitz operators on
\mathbb{A}^{2}(\Omega)
whenever
G
is a finite pseudoreflection group.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
G
is a finite pseudoreflection group, the bounded domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
is a
G
-space and
\boldsymbol{\theta}:\Omega\to\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)
is a basic polynomial map associated to the group
G.
Let
\widetilde{u}
and
\widetilde{v}
be
G
-invariant functions in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\widetilde{u}=u\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}
and
\widetilde{v}=v\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}
.

1.

If for a one-dimensional representation
\mu
of
G,

T_{u}T_{v}=T_{v}T_{u}
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\mu}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)),
then

(i)

T_{u}T_{v}=T_{v}T_{u}
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))
for every one-dimensional representation
\varrho
and

(ii)

T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}=T_{\widetilde{v}}T_{\widetilde{u}}
on
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega),

where the weight function
\omega:\Omega\to(0,\infty)
is
G
-invariant and continuous and
\omega_{\varrho}:\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)\to(0,\infty)
is as defined in Equation (
1.2
) for every one-dimensional representation
\varrho
.

2.

Conversely, if
T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}=T_{\widetilde{v}}T_{\widetilde{u}}
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega),

\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))

Using the above results, we provide characterizations of a pair of commuting Toeplitz operators on the quotient domains such as symmetrized polydisc and Rudin’s domains in Section

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by recalling a number of useful definitions and standard results about pseudoreflection groups.

2.1.
Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem

Let
G
be a finite pseudoreflection group acting on the set of complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
by the action defined in Equation (
1.1
). A function
f
is said to be
G
-invariant if
\sigma(f)=f
for all
\sigma\in G.
We denote the ring of all complex polynomials in
d
-variables by
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
. The set of all
G
-invariant polynomials, denoted by
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}
, forms a subring and coincides with the relative invariant subspace
R^{G}_{tr}(\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])
associated to trivial representation of
G
. Chevalley, Shephard and Todd characterized finite pseudoreflection groups in the following theorem.

Theorem

(Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem)
.

[
13
, p. 112, Theorem 3]

The invariant ring
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}
is equal to
{\mathbb{C}}[\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{d}]
, where
\theta_{i}
’s are algebraically independent
homogeneous polynomials if and only if
G
is a finite pseudoreflection group.

We abbreviate it as CST theorem for further references. The collection of homogeneous polynomials
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
is called a homogeneous system of parameters (hsop) or a set of basic polynomials associated to the pseudoreflection group
G
.
The map
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:{\mathbb{C}}^{d}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, defined by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{z})=\big(\theta_{1}(\boldsymbol{z}),\ldots,\theta_{d}(\boldsymbol{z})\big),\,\,\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}

( 2.1)

is called a basic polynomial map associated to the group
G.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite pseudoreflection group and
\Omega
be a
G
-space. For a basic polynomial map
\boldsymbol{\theta}:\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
associated to the group
G,

(i)

\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)
is a domain and

(ii)

\boldsymbol{\theta}:\Omega\to\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)
is a proper map.

(iii)

The quotient
\Omega/G
is biholomorphically equivalent to the domain
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)

Proof of (i) and (ii) can be found in
[
34
, Proposition 1, p.556]
. The remaining part of the Proposition follows from
[
8
, Proposition 1]
, see also
[
12
, Subsection 3.1.1]
,
[
27
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

We note a few relevant properties of a basic polynomial map associated to
G
.

1.

Although a set of basic polynomials associated to
G
is not unique but the degrees of
\theta_{i}
’s are unique for
G
up to order.

2.

Let
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime}:\mathbb{C}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
be another basic polynomial map of
G.
Then
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\prime}(\Omega)
is biholomorphically equivalent to
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)

[

3.

Clearly, any function on
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)
can be associated to a
G
-invariant function on
\Omega.
Conversely, any
G
-invariant function

for a function
\widehat{u}
on
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega).
Let

be the quotient map. Since the function

for some function

\Omega/G
. It is known that

Clearly, if

then
\widehat{u}\in L^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)).

2.2.
One-dimensional representations of pseudoreflection groups

Since the one-dimensional representations of a finite pseudoreflection group

G

\widehat{G}_{1}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A hyperplane
H
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
is called reflecting if there exists a pseudoreflection in
G
acting trivially
on
H
.

For a pseudoreflection
\sigma\in G,
define
H_{\sigma}:=\ker(I_{d}-\sigma).
By definition, the subspace
H_{\sigma}
has dimension

. Clearly,
\sigma

H_{\sigma}

H_{\sigma}
is a reflecting hyperplane. By definition,
H_{\sigma}
is the zero set of a non-zero homogeneous linear polynomial

on
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
, determined up to a non-zero constant multiple, that is,
H_{\sigma}=\{\boldsymbol{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{d}:L_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{z})=0\}
. Moreover, the elements of
G
acting trivially on a reflecting hyperplane forms a cyclic subgroup of
G
.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{t}
denote the distinct reflecting hyperplanes associated to the group
G
and the corresponding cyclic subgroups are
G_{1},\ldots,G_{t},
respectively. Suppose
G_{i}=\langle a_{i}\rangle
and the order of each
a_{i}
is
m_{i}
for

For every one-dimensional representation
\varrho,
there exists a unique
t
-tuple of non-negative integers
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}),
where
c_{i}
’s are the least non-negative integers that satisfy the following:

\displaystyle\varrho(a_{i})=\big(\det(a_{i})\big)^{c_{i}},\,\,i=1,\ldots,t.

( 2.2)

The
t
-tuple
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})
solely depends on the representation
\varrho.
The character of the one-dimensional representation
\varrho,

\chi_{\varrho}:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
coincides with the representation
\varrho.
The set of polynomials relative to the representation
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle R^{G}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])=\{f\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]:\sigma(f)=\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)f,~{\rm for~~all~}\sigma\in G\}.

( 2.3)

The elements of the subspace
R^{G}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])
are said to be
\varrho
-invariant polynomials. Stanley proves a fundamental property of the elements of
R^{G}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])
in
[
32
, p. 139, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
32
, p. 139, Theorem 3.1]

Suppose that the linear polynomial
\ell_{i}
is a defining function of
H_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,t.
The homogeneous polynomial
\ell_{\varrho}=\prod_{i=1}^{t}\ell_{i}^{c_{i}}
forms a basis of the module

over the ring
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G},
where

’s are unique non-negative integers as described in Equation (

).

We call
\ell_{\varrho}
by
generating polynomial
of
R^{G}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])
over
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}.
It follows that
\sigma(\ell_{\varrho})=\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)\ell_{\varrho}.
The sign representation of a finite pseudoreflection group

{\rm sgn}:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*},
defined by

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}(\sigma)=(\det(\sigma))^{-1},

( 2.4)

is given by
{\rm sgn}(a_{i})=\big(\det(a_{i})\big)^{m_{i}-1}=(\det(a_{i}))^{-1}
,
i=1,\ldots,t,

[
32
, p. 139, Remark (1)]
and it has the following property.

.

[

, p. 616, Lemma]

Let

denote the distinct reflecting hyperplanes associated to the group

and let

be the orders of the corresponding cyclic subgroups

Consequently,

3.
Decomposition of weighted
L^{2}
-spaces

In this section, we define the relative invariant subspaces of
L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
associated to
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1}.
Then we show that those subspaces can be naturally identified to some weighted
L^{2}
-spaces and weighted Bergman spaces on
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega),
respectively. We also include some necessary details of the relative invariant subspaces of
L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
.

3.1.
Isotypic decomposition and projection operators

Given a continuous
G
-invariant weight function
\omega:\Omega\to(0,\infty),

L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
denotes the Hilbert space of Lebesgue measurable functions (equivalence classes of functions) on
\Omega
which are square integrable with respect to the measure
\omega(\boldsymbol{z})dV(\boldsymbol{z}),
where
dV
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\Omega.
The weighted Bergman space
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
is the closed subspace consisting of holomorphic functions in
L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
. For
\omega\equiv 1,

\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
reduces to the Bergman space
\mathbb{A}^{2}(\Omega).
We consider the natural action (sometimes called regular representation) of
G
on
L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega),
given by
\sigma(f)(z)=f(\sigma^{-1}\cdot z).
This action is a unitary representation of
G
(as the weight
\omega
is
G
-invariant) and consequently the space
L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
decomposes into isotypic components.

Now we define the projection operator onto the isotypic component associated to an irreducible representation
\varrho\in\widehat{G}
in the decomposition of the regular representation on
L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)

[
25
, p. 24, Theorem 4.1]
. For
\varrho\in\widehat{G},
the linear operator
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}:L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)\to L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}\phi=\frac{{\rm deg}(\varrho)}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma^{-1})~\phi\circ\sigma^{-1},\,\,\,\phi\in L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega),

where
\chi_{\varrho}
denotes the character of
\varrho
and
|G|
denotes the order of the group
G.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
\varrho\in\widehat{G}
, the operator
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}:L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)\to L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
is an orthogonal projection.

Proof.

An application of Schur’s Lemma implies that
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}^{2}=\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}

[
25
, p. 24, Theorem 4.1]
.
We now show that
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}
is self-adjoint. Using change of variables formula, we get that for all
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)\text{~and~}\sigma\in G,

\displaystyle\langle\sigma\cdot\phi,\sigma\cdot\psi\rangle=\langle\phi,\psi\rangle,

( 3.1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega).
For
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega),
we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}^{*}\phi,\psi\rangle=\langle\phi,\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\phi,\frac{{\rm deg}(\varrho)}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma^{-1})~\psi\circ\sigma^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm deg}(\varrho)}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)\langle\phi,\psi\circ\sigma^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm deg}(\varrho)}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)\langle\phi\circ\sigma,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}\phi,\psi\rangle,

where the penultimate equality follows from Equation (
3.1
).
∎

Therefore,
L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
can be decomposed into an orthogonal direct sum as follows:

\displaystyle L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)=\oplus_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)).

( 3.2)

Remark 3.2
.

The space
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
is clearly
G
-invariant and hence admits a decomposition as above.
The linear map
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}:\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega),
defined by,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}\phi=\frac{{\rm deg}(\varrho)}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma^{-1})~\phi\circ\sigma^{-1},\,\,\,\phi\in\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)

is the orthogonal projection onto the isotypic component associated to the irreducible representation
\varrho
in the decomposition of the regular representation on
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)

[
25
, p. 24, Theorem 4.1]

[
12
, Corollary 4.2]
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)=\oplus_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)).

( 3.3)

For every one-dimensional representation
\varrho,
we provide a characterization of the subspace
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))
in next lemma. Generalizing the notion of a relative invariant subspace, defined in Equation (
2.3
), we define the relative invariant subspace of
L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
associated to a one-dimensional representation
\varrho
of
G,
by

\displaystyle R^{G}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))=\{f\in L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega):\sigma(f)=\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)f~{\rm for~~all~}\sigma\in G\}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite pseudoreflecion group and
\Omega
be a
G
-invariant domain in
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}.
Then for every
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1},

\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))=R^{G}_{\varrho}(L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)).

Proof.

For any
\tau\in G,

\phi\in\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\tau(\phi)=\tau(\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma^{-1})~\phi\circ\sigma^{-1}\tau^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\eta\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\eta^{-1}\tau)~\phi\circ\eta^{-1}\,\,(\text{~taking~}\eta=\tau\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{\varrho}(\tau)~\phi.

Conversely, for
\phi\in L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
with the property
\sigma(\phi)=\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)~\phi,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\phi)=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma^{-1})~\phi\circ\sigma^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma^{-1})\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)~\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\phi=\phi.

∎

The elements of
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))
are called
\varrho
-invariant elements of
L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
. For the trivial representation,
\tr:G\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\tr(\sigma)=1,\,\,\sigma\in G,

( 3.4)

we have
\mathbb{P}_{\tr}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))=\{f\in L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega):\sigma(f)=f\text{~ for all ~}\sigma\in G\}.
We refer to the elements of
\mathbb{P}_{\tr}(L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega))
as
G
-invariant elements of
L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
.

Suppose that
\ell_{\varrho}\widehat{\phi}\in L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
for some
G
-invariant
\widehat{\phi},
then
\sigma(\ell_{\varrho}\widehat{\phi})=\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)\ell_{\varrho}\widehat{\phi},
that is,
\ell_{\varrho}\widehat{\phi}\in R^{G}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)),
consequently,
\ell_{\varrho}\widehat{\phi}\in\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))
by Lemma
3.3
.
The following lemma ensures that
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))=\{\phi\in L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega):\phi=\ell_{\varrho}\widehat{\phi},\text{~where~}\widehat{\phi}\text{~is~}G\text{-invariant}\}.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1}
and
f\in\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)).
Then
f=\ell_{\varrho}~\widehat{f},
where
\ell_{\varrho}
is a generating polynomial of
R^{G}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])
over the ring
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}
and
\widehat{f}
is
G
-invariant.

Proof.

Since
\ell_{\varrho}
vanishes only on a set of measure zero, we can write any
f\in\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))

as

f=\widehat{f}\ell_{\varrho}

where
\widehat{f}=\frac{f}{\ell_{\varrho}}.
Clearly,
\widehat{f}
is
G
-invariant.

∎

We recall an analytic version of Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem which allows us to state a few additional properties of the elements of the weighted Bergman space
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
. First note that
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
is a free
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}
module of rank
|G|

[
13
, Theorem 1, p. 110]
. Further, one can choose a basis of
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]

consisting of homogeneous polynomials. We choose the basis in the following manner. For each
\varrho\in\widehat{G}
,
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}({\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])
is a free module over
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}
of rank
{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}

[
29
, Proposition II.5.3., p.28]
. Clearly,
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]=\oplus_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}({\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]),
where the direct sum is orthogonal direct sum borrowed from the Hilbert space structure of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)
. Then we can choose a basis
\{\ell_{\varrho,i}:1\leq i\leq{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}\}
of
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}({\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])
over
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}
for each
\varrho\in\widehat{G}
such that together they form a basis
\{\ell_{\varrho,i}:\varrho\in\widehat{G}\text{~and~}1\leq i\leq{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}\}
of
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]
over
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}
. We work with such a choice of basis for the rest of discussion.

Theorem 3.5

(Analytic CST)
.

[
12
, p. 12, Theorem 3.12]

Let
G
be a finite group generated by pseudoreflections on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
be a
G
-space. For every holomorphic function
f
on
\Omega
, there exist unique
G
-invariant holomorphic functions
\{f_{\varrho,i}:1\leq i\leq{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}\}_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}
such that

f=\sum_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{i=1}^{{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}}f_{\varrho,i}\ell_{\varrho,i}.

Remark 3.6
.

With such a choice of basis, we have the following:

1.

For any element
f
in
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega),
there exist unique
G
-invariant holomorphic functions
\{f_{\varrho,i}:1\leq i\leq{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}\}_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}

and

for every
\varrho\in\widehat{G}.

2.

For simplicity, we write
\ell_{\varrho}
in the place of
\ell_{\varrho,1}
if
\varrho
is in
\widehat{G}_{1}.
For every
\psi\in\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)),
there exists a unique
G
-invariant holomorphic function
\widehat{\psi}
such that
\psi=\ell_{\varrho}~\widehat{\psi}

[
20
, Lemma 3.1, Remark 3.3]
. A description of
\ell_{\varrho}
is given in Lemma
2.4
.

3.

For
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1},
any other choice of basis of
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}({\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}])
as a free module over
{\mathbb{C}}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}]^{G}
is a constant multiple of
\ell_{\varrho}.

Weighted
L^{2}
-spaces on
\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)
:
Consider a
G
-invariant continuous weight function
\omega:\Omega\to(0,\infty).
We write
\omega=\widetilde{\omega}\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}
for a continuous map
\widetilde{\omega}:\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)\to(0,\infty).
For
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1},
we set

\omega_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}))=\frac{|\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{z})|^{2}}{|J_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{z})|^{2}}~\widetilde{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z})),

and define the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))
as following:

L^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))=\{\phi:\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{C}~|\int_{\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)}|\phi(\boldsymbol{u})|^{2}\omega_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{u})dV(\boldsymbol{u})<\infty\}.

Lemma 3.7
.

For every
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1}
, the linear operator
\Gamma_{\varrho}:L^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))\to\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)),
defined by,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\varrho}\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}(\phi\circ\boldsymbol{\theta})\ell_{\varrho},\,\,\,\,\phi\in L_{\omega_{\varrho}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)),

( 3.5)

is unitary.

Proof.

The polynomial
\ell_{\varrho}
is in
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))
, that is,
\sigma(\ell_{\varrho})=\chi_{\varrho}(\sigma)\ell_{\varrho}
for all
\sigma\in G
from Lemma
3.3
. From the explicit expression of
\ell_{\varrho}
in Lemma
2.4
, we conclude that there exists a unique positive integer, say
m_{\varrho},
such that
\ell_{\varrho}^{m_{\varrho}}
is
G
-invariant. Using CST theorem,
\ell_{\varrho}^{m_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{z})=f_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}))
for a unique polynomial
f_{\varrho}
. The function
\omega_{\varrho}
can be written as

\displaystyle\omega_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{u})=\frac{|f_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{u})|^{\frac{2}{m_{\varrho}}}}{|f_{\rm sgn}(\boldsymbol{u})|^{\frac{2}{m_{\rm sgn}}}}\widetilde{\omega}(\boldsymbol{u}),\,\,\boldsymbol{u}\in\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)\setminus N,

( 3.6)

where
N=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:J_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{z})=0\}.
Then
\Gamma_{\varrho}
is an isometry because for
\psi\in L^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\left\|{\Gamma_{\varrho}\psi}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\int_{\Omega}|\psi\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z})|^{2}|\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{z})|^{2}\omega(\boldsymbol{z})dV(\boldsymbol{z})

( 3.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|G|}\int_{\Omega\setminus\boldsymbol{\theta}^{-1}(N)}|\psi\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z})|^{2}\frac{|f_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}))|^{\frac{2}{m_{\varrho}}}}{|f_{\rm sgn}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}))|^{\frac{2}{m_{\rm sgn}}}}|J_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(\boldsymbol{z})|^{2}\widetilde{\omega}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}))dV(\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)\setminus N}|\psi(\boldsymbol{u})|^{2}\omega_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{u})dV(\boldsymbol{u})=\left\|{\psi}\right\|^{2},

the last equality follows since the set
N
has Lebesgue measure
0.

Let
\phi
be in
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)).
Then there exists a
\widehat{\phi}
such that
\phi=\ell_{\varrho}~\widehat{\phi}\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}
(cf. Lemma
3.4
). We are to show that
\widehat{\phi}\in L^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)).
From Equation (
3.7
), we get the norm of
\widehat{\phi}
is equal to the norm of
\phi.
Since the later is finite, so is the norm of
\widehat{\phi}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
be the set of all holomorphic functions on
\Omega.
We denote the weighted Bergman space with weight function
\omega_{\varrho}
by

\mathbb{A}_{\omega_{\varrho}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))=\{\phi\in\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)):\int_{\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)}|\phi(\boldsymbol{u})|^{2}\omega_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{u})dV(\boldsymbol{u})<\infty\}.

The restriction operator
\Gamma_{\varrho}:\mathbb{A}_{\omega_{\varrho}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))\to\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))
is surjective. Thus the weighted Bergman space
\mathbb{A}_{\omega_{\varrho}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))
is isometrically isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))
via the unitary operator
\Gamma_{\varrho}

[
20
]
. This enables us to make the following observations which are essential.

Remark 3.8
.

1.

For each
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1},
we have the following identity involving the reproducing kernel

of

and the reproducing kernel

of

[

For a fixed
\boldsymbol{w}\in\Omega,
we have

\displaystyle\overline{\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{w})}\Gamma_{\varrho}\big({\mathfrak{K}}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{w}))\big)(\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{z}){\mathfrak{K}}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}),\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{w})\big)\overline{\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{w})}

( 3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{|G|}\mathcal{B}_{\omega}^{(\varrho)}(\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{w}).

2.

Recall that the polynomial

(cf. Corollary

). Therefore, for

in Equation (

), we get

4.
Toeplitz operators and the Weighted Bergman spaces

In this section, we establish a number of identities involving Toeplitz operators on
\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
and Toeplitz operators on
\mathbb{A}_{\omega_{\varrho}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))
which enables us to study algebraic properties of Toeplitz operators on
\mathbb{A}_{\omega_{\varrho}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)).
We start with two very useful lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every one-dimensional
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1},
the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{\omega_{\varrho}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\varrho}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{\varrho}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\varrho}}

where
P_{\omega_{\varrho}}
and
\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\varrho}
are the associated orthogonal projections and
\Gamma_{\varrho}
is as defined in Equation (
3.5
).

Proof.

Note that for
f\in L^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega)),
we have

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{\varrho}P_{\omega_{\varrho}}f)(\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}(P_{\omega_{\varrho}}f\circ\boldsymbol{\theta})(\boldsymbol{z})\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{z})\langle f,{\mathfrak{K}}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\ell_{\varrho}(\boldsymbol{z})\langle\Gamma_{\varrho}f,\Gamma_{\varrho}\big({\mathfrak{K}}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{\theta}(\boldsymbol{z}))\big)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\langle\Gamma_{\varrho}f,\sqrt{|G|}\mathcal{B}_{\omega}^{(\varrho)}(\cdot,\boldsymbol{z})\rangle=(\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\varrho}\Gamma_{\varrho}f)(\boldsymbol{z}),

where the penultimate equality follows from Equation (
3.8
).
∎

If
f\in\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)),
then
\widetilde{u}f\in\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)).
It follows from the orthogonal decomposition of
L_{\omega}^{2}(\Omega)
in Equation (
3.2
) that

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\widetilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a
G
-invariant function such that
\widetilde{u}=u\circ\boldsymbol{\theta}.
For every
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1},
the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega_{\varrho}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}(\Omega))}

4.1.
Generalized zero-product problem

Let
\widetilde{u},\widetilde{v}
and
\widetilde{q}
be
G
-invariant functions in
L^{\infty}(\Omega).
It is evident that if
T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}=T_{\widetilde{q}}
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega),
then the restriction operator
T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}=T_{\widetilde{q}}
on
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))
for every
\varrho\in\widehat{G}
. Interestingly enough, the converse holds as well with a weaker hypothesis.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}=T_{\widetilde{q}}
on
\mathbb{P}_{\varrho}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega))
for at least an irreducible representation
\varrho\in\widehat{G},
then
T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}=T_{\widetilde{q}}
on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega).

Proof.

Suppose that the assumption holds for the representation
\varrho\in\widehat{G}.
Consider an element
f=\sum_{i=1}^{{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}}\ell_{\varrho,i}\widehat{f}_{\varrho,i}
such that
\widehat{f}_{\varrho,i}\in\mathbb{P}_{\tr}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)).
Then from Equation (
4.1
), it is clear that
T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}f=T_{\widetilde{q}}f
implies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}}\ell_{\varrho,i}\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{u}~\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{v}\widehat{f}_{\varrho,i}))=\sum_{i=1}^{{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}}\ell_{\varrho,i}\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{q}\widehat{f}_{\varrho,i}).

In case,
\varrho\in\widehat{G}_{1},
we get

\ell_{\varrho,1}\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{u}~\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{v}\widehat{f}_{\varrho,1})=\ell_{\varrho,1}\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{q}\widehat{f}_{\varrho,1})

for
\widehat{f}_{\varrho,1}\in\mathbb{P}_{\tr}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)),
equivalently,
\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{u}~\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{v}\widehat{f}))=\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{q}\widehat{f})
for every
\widehat{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{\tr}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)).
If
{\rm deg}(\varrho)>1,
we take
\widehat{f}_{\varrho,i}=0
for
i=2,\ldots,{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}
and then repeat the above argument to observe the same.

Let
f=\sum_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{i=1}^{{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}}\ell_{\varrho,i}f_{\varrho,i},
for some
f_{\varrho,i}\in\mathbb{P}_{\tr}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega)).
Then we get

T_{\widetilde{u}}T_{\widetilde{v}}f=\sum_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{i=1}^{{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}}\ell_{\varrho,i}\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{u}~\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{v}f_{\varrho,i}))=\sum_{\varrho\in\widehat{G}}\sum_{i=1}^{{\rm deg}(\varrho)^{2}}\ell_{\varrho,i}\widetilde{P}_{\omega,\tr}(\widetilde{q}f_{\varrho,i})=T_{\widetilde{q}}f

on a dense subset of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\omega}(\Omega).
Hence, the result follows.
∎

6.2.

Let

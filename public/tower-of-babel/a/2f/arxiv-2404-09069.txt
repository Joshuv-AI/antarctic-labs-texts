Turán numbers for non-bipartite graphs and applications to spectral extremal problems
Longfei Fang, Michael Tait, Mingqing Zhai

Abstract

Abstract

Given a graph family
\mathcal{H}

with
\min_{H\in\mathcal{H}}\chi(H)=r+1\geq 3
.
Let
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})
and
{\rm spex}(n,\mathcal{H})

be the maximum number of edges
and the maximum spectral radius of the adjacency matrix
over all
\mathcal{H}
-free graphs of order
n
, respectively.
Denote by
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})
(resp.
{\rm SPEX}(n,\mathcal{H})
)
the set of extremal graphs with respect to
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})

(resp.
{\rm spex}(n,\mathcal{H})
).

In this paper, we use a decomposition family defined by Simonovits to give a characterization of which graph families
\mathcal{H}
satisfy
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})<e(T_{n,r})+\lfloor\frac{n}{2r}\rfloor
. Furthermore, we completely determine

{\rm EX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)
for
n
sufficiently large,
where
\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})
denotes a finite graph family
which consists of
k
edge-disjoint
(r+1)
-chromatic
color-critical graphs
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
. This result strengthens a theorem of Győri, who settled the case that
F_{1}=\cdots=F_{k}=K_{r+1}
.

Wang, Kang and Xue proved that
{\rm SPEX}(n,H)\subseteq{\rm EX}(n,H)

for
n
sufficiently large and any graph
H
with
{\rm ex}(n,H)=e(T_{n,r})+O(1)
. As an application of our first theorem, we show that
{\rm SPEX}(n,\mathcal{H})\subseteq{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})

for
n
sufficiently large and any finite family
\mathcal{H}
with
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})<e(T_{n,r})+\lfloor\frac{n}{2r}\rfloor
. As an application of our second theorem we completely determine
{\rm SPEX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)
for
n
sufficiently large.

Finally, related problems
are proposed for further research.

Keywords:
extremal graph; spectral radius;
edge-disjoint; color-critical graph

05C35; 05C50

Introduction

A graph family
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
finite

if
|H|
is bounded above by a fixed constant for any member
H\in\mathcal{H}
.
We call a graph
\mathcal{H}
-free

if it does not contain any copy of
H\in\mathcal{H}
as a subgraph.
The
Turán number
of
\mathcal{H}
,
denoted by
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})
,
is the maximum number of edges over all

n
-vertex
\mathcal{H}
-free graphs.
Denote by
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})

the set of extremal graphs
with respect to
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})
,
that is,
\mathcal{H}
-free graphs with
n
vertices
and
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})
edges.
If
\mathcal{H}=\{H\}
, we use
{\rm ex}(n,H)

(resp.
{\rm EX}(n,H)
) instead of
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})

(resp.
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})
) for simplicity.

Turán’s famous theorem
states that
{\rm EX}(n,K_{r+1})=\{T_{n,r}\}
,
where
T_{n,r}
denotes the
r
-partite Turán graph on
n
vertices.
Replacing
K_{r+1}
with a finite graph family
\mathcal{H}
,
the famous Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem
[
15
]

shows that

{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})=\Big(1+o(1)\Big)\Big(1-\frac{1}{r}\Big){{n}\choose{2}},

where
r=\min_{H\in\mathcal{H}}(\chi(H)-1)

and
\chi(H)
denotes the chromatic number of
H
.
Since then, the study of the Turán number for various
\mathcal{H}

has become one of the most important topics in extremal graph theory.
For more information on this topic,
we refer the readers to
[

,
13
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

.

Let
F+H
be the join and
F\cup H
be the disjoint union,
of
F
and
H
, respectively.
Let
M_{2k}
be the disjoint union of
k
edges.
As usual, we denote by
K_{n},C_{n},P_{n},S_{n},E_{n}
and

W_{n}
the complete graph, the cycle, the path, the star,
the empty graph and the wheel graph on
n
vertices, respectively.
Given a graph family
\mathcal{H}
, set

\phi(\mathcal{H})=\max\{|H|:H\in\mathcal{H}\}

and
\chi(\mathcal{H})=\min\{\chi(H):H\in\mathcal{H}\}
.
The Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem implies that

\chi(\mathcal{H})=r+1
if
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})=e(T_{n,r})+O(n)
.
In order to study the Turán number of
\mathcal{H}
,
we introduce the decomposition family
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H})
,
which was given by Simonovits. Simonovits first defined a similar family in
[
40
]
without using the name “decomposition family”. The name was given in
[
39
]
(see also
[
41
]
). We note that the family defined in these papers is slightly different than how we have defined it, but captures the same information. The family defined as we have was given by Liu
[
29
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Given a graph family
\mathcal{H}
with
\chi(\mathcal{H})=r+1\geq 3
and
\phi(\mathcal{H})=\phi
.
Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H})
be
the family of minimal graphs
M
satisfying that
there exists an
H\in\mathcal{H}
such that

H\subseteq(M\cup E_{\phi})+T_{(r-1)\phi,r-1}
.
We call
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H})

the decomposition family of
\mathcal{H}
.

We first use the decomposition family to give a characterization of which graph families
\mathcal{H}
satisfy

\chi(\mathcal{H})=r+1\geq 3
and
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})<e(T_{n,r})+\lfloor\frac{n}{2r}\rfloor
,
and further obtain structural
properties of extremal graphs in
EX(n,\mathcal{H})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite graph family
with
\chi(\mathcal{H})=r+1\geq 3
and
\phi(\mathcal{H})=\phi
.
Then for
n
sufficiently large,
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})<e(T_{n,r})+\lfloor\frac{n}{2r}\rfloor
;

(ii) there exist two integers
\nu\leq\lfloor\frac{\phi}{2}\rfloor

and
\Delta\leq\phi
such that
M_{2\nu},S_{\Delta+1}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H})
;

(iii) every graph in
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})

is obtained from
T_{n,r}
by adding and deleting
O(1)
edges.

For an edge subset
M\subseteq E(H)
,
we define
H-M=(V(H),E(H)\setminus M)
.
A graph
H
is called
color-critical

if there exists an edge
e\in E(H)

such that
\chi(H-\{e\})<\chi(H)
.
Simonovits
[
38
]
showed that
{\rm EX}(n,H)=\{T_{n,r}\}

when
n
is sufficiently large and
H
is a color-critical graph with
\chi(H)=r+1
.
Let
edk_{r}(G)
denote the maximum number of edge-disjoint copies of
K_{r}
in
G
,
and
edk_{r}(n,m)
denote the minimum of
edk_{r}(G)

for the graphs
G
of order
n
and size

Győri
[
20
]
determined the maximum
k

such that

edk_{r+1}\big(n,e(T_{n,r})+k\big)=k
for
r\geq 2
and
n
sufficiently large.
Let
\Gamma_{k,r}
be the family of graphs
which consist of
k
edge-disjoint copies of
K_{r}
.
Then, Győri’s result implies that

{\rm ex}(n,\Gamma_{k,r+1})=e(T_{n,r})+k-1
for
r\geq 2
and
n
sufficiently large.

Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
be color-critical graphs with
\chi(F_{i})=r+1\geq 3
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
and let
\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})
denote the family of graphs
which consists of
k
edge-disjoint
copies of
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
.
As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we determine

{\rm ex}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)
and characterize all graphs in

{\rm EX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)
for
n
sufficiently large.
Our result extends the Győri’s theorem on the Turán number of
\Gamma_{k,r+1}
by taking
F_{1}=\cdots=F_{k}=K_{r+1}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
be color-critical graphs with
\chi(F_{i})=r+1\geq 3
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. For sufficiently large
n
,
a graph
G\in{\rm EX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)

if and only if
G
is obtained from
T_{n,r}
by embedding
k-1
edges within
its partite sets.

We use Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to study spectral extremal graph theory problems. Let
A(G)
be the adjacency matrix
and
\rho(G)
be the spectral radius of a graph

The spectral extremal value of a graph family

.

{\rm SPEX}(n,\mathcal{H})

{\rm spex}(n,\mathcal{H})
.
For simplicity, we use
{\rm spex}(n,H)

(resp.
{\rm SPEX}(n,H)

{\rm spex}(n,\mathcal{H})

(resp.
{\rm SPEX}(n,\mathcal{H})

\mathcal{H}=\{H\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite graph family

\chi(\mathcal{H})=r+1\geq 3

(1)

Claim 2.1
.

Define
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
such that they form a partition
V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}

where
\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}e(V_{i},V_{j})
attains the maximum. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(V_{i})\leq\varepsilon n^{2}
and
\big||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{r}\big|\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}n

for each
i\in[r],
where
[r]=\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
.

Proof.

By Definition
2.1
, we have

e(G)\geq e(T_{n,r})-\varepsilon n^{2}\geq\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-\frac{r}{8}-\varepsilon n^{2}>\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-2\varepsilon n^{2},

(2)

and there exists a partition
V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}U_{i}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(U_{i})\leq\varepsilon n^{2}

and
\big\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\big\rfloor\leq|U_{i}|\leq\big\lceil\frac{n}{r}\big\rceil
for each
i\in[r]
.

Now we select a new partition
V(G)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}

such that
\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}e(V_{i},V_{j})

is maximized.
Equivalently
\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(V_{i})

is minimized, and hence

\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(V_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(U_{i})\leq\varepsilon n^{2}.

Set
\alpha=\max\Big\{\big||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{r}\big|:i\in[r]\Big\}
.
We may assume that

\alpha=\big||V_{1}|-\frac{n}{r}\big|
.
By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
we get that

(r-1)\sum_{i=2}^{r}|V_{i}|^{2}\geq\big(\sum_{i=2}^{r}|V_{i}|\big)^{2}=(n-|V_{1}|)^{2}
.
Thus,

2\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq r}|V_{i}||V_{j}|=\Big(\sum_{i=2}^{r}|V_{i}|\Big)^{2}-\sum_{i=2}^{r}|V_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{r-2}{r-1}\Big(n-|V_{1}|\Big)^{2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}|V_{i}||V_{j}|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}e(V_{i})\leq|V_{1}|(n-|V_{1}|)+\!\!\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq r}\!\!|V_{i}||V_{j}|+\varepsilon n^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|V_{1}|(n-|V_{1}|)+\frac{r-2}{2(r-1)}\big(n-|V_{1}|\big)^{2}+\varepsilon n^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{r}{2(r-1)}\alpha^{2}+\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}+\varepsilon n^{2},

where the last equality holds as
\alpha=\big||V_{1}|-\frac{n}{r}\big|
.
Combining (
2

\frac{r}{2(r-1)}\alpha^{2}<3\varepsilon n^{2}

Therefore,

as

by (

∎

|G_{in}|\leq 2r(\nu-1)\Delta<\frac{n}{2r}.

Moreover,
|V_{i}|\geq(\frac{1}{r}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})n

by Claim
2.1
.
Then for each
i\in[r]
,

V_{i}
admits a subset
V_{i}^{\prime}
of size

\lfloor\frac{n}{2r}\rfloor
such that

\big(V_{i}\cap V(G_{in})\big)\subseteq V_{i}^{\prime}
.
Combining Claim
4.2
, we have

V(G_{cr})\subseteq V(G_{in})\subseteq\cup_{i\in[r]}V_{i}^{\prime}.

Thus,

\displaystyle e\big(G[\cup_{i\in[r]}V_{i}^{\prime}]\big)=e\big(T_{r\lfloor\frac{n}{2r}\rfloor,r}\big)+e(G_{in})-e(G_{cr}).

By Lemma
4.4
,

{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})=e(T_{n,r})+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}
.
Since
G
is
\mathcal{H}
-free,
G[\cup_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}^{\prime}]
is too.
Hence,
e\big(G[\cup_{i\in[r]}V_{i}^{\prime}]\big)\leq e\big(T_{r\lfloor\frac{n}{2r}\rfloor,r}\big)+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2},

which implies
e(G_{in})-e(G_{cr})\leq\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}.

∎

Note that
\mathbf{x}
is a unit vector.
Then
\sum_{u\in V(G)}x_{u}^{2}=1
.
By Claim
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle nx_{u^{*}}^{2}\geq\sum_{u\in V(G)}x_{u}^{2}=1\geq n\Big(1-\frac{4\varphi^{3}}{n}\Big)^{2}x_{u^{*}}^{2}\geq(n-8\varphi^{3})x_{u^{*}}^{2},

which yields that
\frac{1}{n}\leq x_{u^{*}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n-8\varphi^{3}}\leq\frac{1}{n}+\frac{9\varphi^{3}}{n^{2}}
.
By (
16
) and Claim
4.2
,
we have

|G_{cr}|\leq|G_{in}|\leq 2r(\nu-1)\Delta
;
and by (
1
) we get
r(\nu-1)\Delta\leq\varphi^{3}
.
Thus,
e(G_{cr})\leq\binom{|G_{cr}|}{2}\leq 2\varphi^{6}
and

\displaystyle e(G_{cr})\Big(1-\frac{4\varphi^{3}}{n}\Big)^{2}\geq e(G_{cr})-e(G_{cr})\frac{8\varphi^{3}}{n}\geq e(G_{cr})-\frac{16\varphi^{9}}{n}.

(18)

Denote
\beta=e(G_{cr})-e(G_{in})
.
Recall that
E(G)=E(K)-E(G_{cr})+E(G_{in})
.
Consequently,
E(G)=E(K)-\beta
.
Combining (
18
) and Claim
4.3
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(K)-\rho(G)

\displaystyle\geq\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(G_{cr})}\!\!2x_{u}x_{v}-\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(G_{in})}\!\!2x_{u}x_{v}\geq 2e(G_{cr})\Big(1-\frac{4\varphi^{3}}{n}\Big)^{2}x_{u^{*}}^{2}-2e(G_{in})x_{u^{*}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2\Big(\beta-\frac{16\varphi^{9}}{n}\Big)x_{u^{*}}^{2}.

Notice that
\frac{1}{n}\leq x_{u^{*}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}+\frac{9\varphi^{3}}{n^{2}}
.
If
\beta\geq 1
, then

\rho(K)-\rho(G)\geq 2\big(\beta-\frac{16\varphi^{9}}{n}\big)\frac{1}{n}=\frac{2\beta}{n}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{n^{2}}
,
where
\beta_{1}=32\varphi^{9}

\rho(K)-\rho(G)\geq 2\big(\beta-\frac{16\varphi^{9}}{n}\big)(\frac{1}{n}+\frac{9\varphi^{3}}{n^{2}}),

4.4

\beta\geq\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}
.

\rho(K)-\rho(G)\geq\frac{2\beta}{n}-\frac{\beta_{2}}{n^{2}}

\beta_{2}

Recall that
G^{*}\in{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})

and

.
Then
\rho(G)\geq\rho(G^{*}).

Combining Lemma
4.4

\rho(G)-\rho(T_{n,r})\geq\frac{2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}-\frac{6\alpha_{1}}{n^{2}}
.
Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\rho(K)-\rho(T_{n,r})=\Big(\rho(K)-\rho(G)\Big)+\Big(\rho(G)-\rho(T_{n,r})\Big)\geq\frac{2(\beta+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{n^{2}},

(19)

\gamma_{1}=\max\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\}+6\alpha_{1}

\beta\geq\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}

|V_{1}|\geq\cdots\geq|V_{r}|
.

,

,
and thus

.
Now (

0\geq\frac{2(\beta+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})}{n}-\frac{\gamma_{1}}{n^{2}}
,
which implies that

.
Therefore,

By Lemma
4.4
,

{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})=e(T_{n,r})+\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}
.
Thus,
G\in{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})

as
G
is
\mathcal{H}
-free.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we characterize

\mathrm{SPEX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)
.
Combining Theorems
1.4
and
1.2
,
we immediately have the following result.

Lemma 5.1
.

Every graph in
\mathrm{SPEX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)

is obtained from
T_{n,r}
by embedding
k-1
edges
within its partite sets.

Before proceeding,
we shall introduce two more lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

(​
[
46
]
)

Assume that
G
is a connected graph with
u,v\in V(G)

and
w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{s}

\in N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(u)
.
Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
be the Perron vector of
G
,
and
G^{\prime}=G-\{vv_{i}:i\in[s]\}+\{uv_{i}:i\in[s]\}
.
If
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
, then
\rho(G^{\prime})>\rho(G)
.

Lemma 5.3
.

(​
[
35
]
)

Let
G
be a graph with
m
edges. Then

\sum_{v\in V(G)}d^{2}_{G}(v)\leq m^{2}+m.

Now we provide the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof.

Let
G^{*}\in\mathrm{SPEX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)

with
E(G^{*})\setminus E(T_{n,r})=E_{0}
(where
|E_{0}|=k-1\geq 1
).
Since
G^{*}-E_{0}\cong T_{n,r}
, we may assume that

V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}

are
r
partite sets of
G^{*}-E_{0}
.
Obviously,
\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor\leq|V_{i}|\leq\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil
for
i\in[r]
.

Let
\rho=\rho(G^{*})
and

\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
be the Perron vector of
G^{*}
.
Moreover,
let
H
be the subgraph of
G^{*}
induced by the edges in
E_{0}
,
and
H_{i}
be the subgraph induced by the vertex subset
V(H)\cap V_{i}

for
i\in[r]
.
For convenience,
we use
x_{V_{i}}
to denote
\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}
,
and let
x_{V}=x_{V(G^{*})}
for simplicity.
We now prove a series of claims.

Claim 5.1
.

Denote
\overline{V_{i}}=V(G^{*})\setminus V_{i}

for
i\in[r]
.
Then

\frac{\rho x_{V}}{\rho+|V_{i}|+\frac{2e(H_{i})}{\rho-k+1}}\leq x_{\overline{V_{i}}}\leq\frac{\rho x_{V}}{\rho+|V_{i}|+\frac{2e(H_{i})}{\rho}}.

(20)

Moreover, if
|V_{i}|=|V_{j}|
and
e(H_{i})<e(H_{j})
,
then
0<x_{\overline{V_{i}}}-x_{\overline{V_{j}}}\leq\frac{1}{\Theta({n^{3}})}\rho x_{V}.

Proof.

If
V(H_{i})=\varnothing
, then

V_{i}
is an independent set of
G^{*}
, and hence

\rho x_{v}=\sum_{u\in N(v)}x_{u}=x_{\overline{V_{i}}}

for each
v\in V_{i}
.
Thus,
\rho x_{V_{i}}=|V_{i}|x_{\overline{V_{i}}}

and
\rho x_{V}=\rho(x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+x_{V_{i}})=(\rho+|V_{i}|)x_{\overline{V_{i}}}.

Note that now
e(H_{i})=0
.
Therefore, (
20
) holds in equality.

Now assume that
V(H_{i})\neq\varnothing.

Then
\rho x_{v}=x_{\overline{V_{i}}}

for each
v\in V_{i}\setminus V(H_{i})
,
and
\rho x_{v}=x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+\sum_{u\in N_{H_{i}}(v)}x_{u}

for each
v\in V(H_{i})
.
It follows that

\rho x_{V_{i}}=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}\rho x_{v}=|V_{i}|x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+\sum_{v\in V(H_{i})}d_{H_{i}}(v)x_{v}.

(21)

It is easy to see that

\min_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=\frac{x_{\overline{V_{i}}}}{\rho}.

On the one hand,
let
v^{*}\in V_{i}
with
x_{v^{*}}=\max_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}
.
Then
v^{*}\in V(H_{i})
and

\rho x_{v^{*}}=x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+\sum_{u\in N_{H_{i}}(v^{*})}x_{u}\leq x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+(k-1)x_{v^{*}}.

It follows that
x_{v^{*}}\leq\frac{x_{\overline{V_{i}}}}{\rho-k+1}.

Combining (
21
), we obtain

|V_{i}|x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+2e(H_{i})\frac{x_{\overline{V_{i}}}}{\rho}\leq\rho x_{V_{i}}\leq|V_{i}|x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+2e(H_{i})\frac{x_{\overline{V_{i}}}}{\rho-k+1}.

(22)

Note that
\rho x_{V_{i}}=\rho(x_{V}-x_{\overline{V_{i}}})
.
In view of (
22
), we get (
20
) immediately.

Observe that
e(H_{i})+e(H_{j})\leq k-1

and
\rho\geq\rho(T_{n,r})=\Theta(n).

If
e(H_{i})<e(H_{j})
, that is,
e(H_{i})\leq e(H_{j})-1
,
then
\frac{2e(H_{i})}{\rho-k+1}<\frac{2e(H_{j})}{\rho}.

Combining (
20
) and
|V_{i}|=|V_{j}|
, we can see that

x_{\overline{V_{i}}}>x_{\overline{V_{j}}}.

On the other hand,
Combining (
20
) and
|V_{i}|=|V_{j}|
also gives that

x_{\overline{V_{i}}}-x_{\overline{V_{j}}}\leq\frac{\rho x_{V}}{\rho+|V_{i}|+\frac{2e(H_{i})}{\rho}}-\frac{\rho x_{V}}{\rho+|V_{j}|+\frac{2e(H_{j})}{\rho-k+1}}=\frac{1}{\Theta({n^{3}})}\rho x_{V}.

Hence, the claim holds.
∎

In the proof of Claim
5.1
,
we do not use the condition that
G^{*}
is a spectral extremal graph.
Therefore, Claim
5.1
holds for every graph
G
in
\mathrm{EX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)
.

Claim 5.2
.

If
V(H_{i})\neq\varnothing
, then
|V_{i}|=\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor
.

Proof.

Recall that
\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor\leq|V_{i}|\leq\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil

for each
i\in[r]
.
If
\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor=\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil
,
then we are done.
Now consider the case
\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil>\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor,

and suppose to the contrary that
|V_{i}|=\lceil\frac{n}{r}\rceil
.
Then we can find some
j\in[r]
such that

|V_{j}|=\lfloor\frac{n}{r}\rfloor=|V_{i}|-1
.
Furthermore, we define
G^{\prime}
to be the graph obtained
from
G^{*}-E(H_{i})
by embedding a copy
H
of
H_{i}
into
V_{j}\setminus V(H_{j}).

Let
\rho^{\prime}=\rho(G^{\prime})
and

\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})^{T}
be the Perron vector of
G^{\prime}
.
Clearly,
G^{\prime}\in\mathrm{EX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)
,
and thus
\rho\geq\rho^{\prime}\geq\rho(T_{n,r})=\Theta(n)
.

Since
H_{i}\cong H
,
they have the same degree sequence.
Thus we can set

\sum_{v\in V(H_{i})}d_{H_{i}}(v)=\sum_{v\in V(H)}d_{H}(v)=a

and
\sum_{v\in V(H_{i})}d^{2}_{H_{i}}(v)=\sum_{v\in V(H)}d^{2}_{H}(v)=b
.
Choose
v^{*}\in V_{i}
such that
x_{v^{*}}=\max_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}.

Then
x_{v^{*}}\leq\frac{x_{\overline{V_{i}}}}{\rho-k+1}.

Moreover,
\sum_{uv\in E(H_{i})}(x_{u}+x_{v})=\sum_{v\in V(H_{i})}d_{H_{i}}(v)x_{v},

and
\rho x_{v}\leq x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+d_{H_{i}}(v)x_{v^{*}}

for each
v\in V(H_{i}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E(H_{i})}\rho(x_{u}+x_{v})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V(H_{i})}d_{H_{i}}(v)x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+\sum_{v\in V(H_{i})}d^{2}_{H_{i}}(v)x_{v^{*}}

\displaystyle=ax_{\overline{V_{i}}}+bx_{v^{*}}\leq\Big(a+\frac{b}{\rho-k+1}\Big)x_{\overline{V_{i}}}.

(23)

Choose an arbitrary
v_{0}\in V_{j}\setminus\big(V(H)\cup V(H_{j})\big).

Then
N_{G^{\prime}}(v_{0})=\overline{V_{j}}
,
and thus
\rho y_{v_{0}}\geq\rho^{\prime}y_{v_{0}}=y_{\overline{V_{j}}}.

A similar argument gives

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(H)}\!\!\rho^{\prime}(y_{u}+y_{v})\geq\!\!\sum_{v\in V(H)}\!\!d_{H}(v)y_{\overline{V_{j}}}+\!\!\sum_{v\in V(H)}\!\!d^{2}_{H}(v)y_{v_{0}}=ay_{\overline{V_{j}}}+by_{v_{0}}\geq\Big(a+\frac{b}{\rho}\Big)y_{\overline{V_{j}}}.

(24)

On the other hand, since
V(H)\subseteq V_{j}\setminus V(H_{j})
,
we see that
N_{G^{*}}(v)=\overline{V_{j}}
,
and thus
\rho x_{v}=x_{\overline{V_{j}}}
, for each
v\in V(H)
.
Similarly, we have
\rho^{\prime}y_{v}=y_{\overline{V_{i}}}

for each
v\in V(H_{i})
.

Now by a double-eigenvector technique
which was first introduced by Rowlinson (see
[
36
]
),
we can obtain that

\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{y}(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)=\mathbf{x}^{T}\Big(A(G^{\prime})-A(G^{*})\Big)\mathbf{y}=\!\!\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(G^{\prime})}\!\!\!\!(x_{u}y_{v}+x_{v}y_{u})-\!\!\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(G^{*})}\!\!\!\!(x_{u}y_{v}+x_{v}y_{u}).

(25)

Multiplying both sides of (
25
) by
\rho\rho^{\prime}
,
we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{y}\rho\rho^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{uv\in E(H)}\!\!\rho\rho^{\prime}(x_{u}y_{v}+x_{v}y_{u})-\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(H_{i})}\!\!\rho\rho^{\prime}(x_{u}y_{v}+x_{v}y_{u})

\displaystyle=\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(H)}\!\!\rho^{\prime}(y_{u}+y_{v})x_{\overline{V_{j}}}-\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(H_{i})}\!\!\rho(x_{u}+x_{v})y_{\overline{V_{i}}}.

(26)

Furthermore,
combining (
23
), (
24
) and (
26
),
we get that

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{y}\rho\rho^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)\geq\Big(a+\frac{b}{\rho}\Big)y_{\overline{V_{j}}}x_{\overline{V_{j}}}-\Big(a+\frac{b}{\rho-k+1}\Big)x_{\overline{V_{i}}}y_{\overline{V_{i}}}.

(27)

Notice that
2e(H_{i})=\sum_{v\in V(H_{i})}d_{H_{i}}(v)=a

and
\rho\geq\rho^{\prime}=\Theta(n).

In view of (
11
), we have

x_{\overline{V_{j}}}y_{\overline{V_{j}}}\geq\frac{\rho x_{V}}{\rho+|V_{j}|+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\rho^{\prime}y_{V}}{\rho^{\prime}+|V_{j}|+\frac{1}{2}}

and

x_{\overline{V_{i}}}y_{\overline{V_{i}}}\leq\frac{\rho x_{V}}{\rho+|V_{i}|}\frac{\rho^{\prime}y_{V}}{\rho^{\prime}+|V_{i}|}.

Recall that
|V_{j}|=|V_{i}|-1
.
Combining (
27
),
we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{y}(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)\geq\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{d_{1}}-\frac{c_{2}}{d_{2}}\Big)x_{V}y_{V},

where
c_{1}=a+\frac{b}{\rho}
,

c_{2}=a+\frac{b}{\rho-k+1}
,

d_{1}=(\rho+|V_{i}|-\frac{1}{2})(\rho^{\prime}+|V_{i}|-\frac{1}{2})

and
d_{2}=(\rho+|V_{i}|)(\rho^{\prime}+|V_{i}|)
.
It is easy to check that
c_{2}-c_{1}=\frac{1}{\Theta(n^{2})}

and
\frac{1}{d_{1}}-\frac{1}{d_{2}}=\frac{1}{\Theta(n^{3})}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}}{d_{1}}=\Big(c_{2}-\frac{1}{\Theta(n^{2})}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{d_{2}}+\frac{1}{\Theta(n^{3})}\Big)=\frac{c_{2}}{d_{2}}+\frac{1}{\Theta(n^{3})}-\frac{1}{\Theta(n^{4})}-\frac{1}{\Theta(n^{5})},

\frac{c_{1}}{d_{1}}>\frac{c_{2}}{d_{2}}
,
and thus
\rho^{\prime}>\rho

∎

Claim 5.3
.

If
V(H_{i})\neq\varnothing
, then
H_{i}\cong K_{3}
for

H_{i}\cong S_{e(H_{i})+1}

v^{*}\in V_{i}

Then
v^{*}\in V(H_{i})
.
We can further see that

v^{*}
is a dominating vertex of
H_{i}
.
Otherwise, there exists a vertex
u\in V(H_{i})\setminus N(v^{*})
.
Since
H_{i}
is an edge-induced subgraph of
G^{*}

Now we define

Then
G^{\prime}\in\mathrm{EX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)

However,
\rho(G^{\prime})>\rho(G^{*})
by Lemma

,

Since
v^{*}
is a dominating vertex,
if

is a star,

Furthermore, if

is either a triangle

S_{4}
.
We now show

Suppose to contrary that

Define

\rho^{\prime}=\rho(G^{\prime})
and

\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})^{T}
be the Perron vector of

.
Since

,
it is easy to see that
\rho^{\prime}\leq\rho

On the one hand,
we have
x_{v_{1}}=x_{v_{2}}=x_{v_{3}}
by symmetry.
Moreover,
\rho x_{v^{*}}=x_{\overline{V}_{i}}+3x_{v_{1}}

and
\rho x_{v_{1}}=x_{\overline{V}_{i}}+x_{v^{*}}
.
Hence, we can deduce that

\rho x_{v^{*}}-\rho x_{v_{1}}=3x_{v_{1}}-x_{v^{*}}
,

On the other hand,
since
V(H_{i})
induces a copy of
K_{3}\cup K_{1}
in
G^{\prime}
,
we can see that
y_{v^{*}}=y_{v_{1}}=y_{v_{2}}
.
Moreover,
\rho^{\prime}y_{v^{*}}=y_{\overline{V_{i}}}+2y_{v^{*}}

and
\rho^{\prime}y_{v_{3}}=y_{\overline{V_{i}}}
.
Hence,

\rho^{\prime}y_{v^{*}}-\rho^{\prime}y_{v_{3}}=2y_{v^{*}}
,
which yields that

y_{v_{3}}=\frac{\rho^{\prime}-2}{\rho^{\prime}}y_{v^{*}}\leq\frac{\rho-2}{\rho}y_{v_{1}}.

In view of (
25
), we can see that

\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{y}(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)=(x_{v_{1}}y_{v_{2}}+x_{v_{2}}y_{v_{1}})-(x_{v_{3}}y_{v^{*}}+x_{v^{*}}y_{v_{3}})=x_{v_{1}}y_{v_{1}}-x_{v^{*}}y_{v_{3}}.

we have
\rho^{\prime}>\rho
, a contradiction.
Therefore,
H_{i}\cong K_{3}
,
and the case
e(H_{i})=3
is proved.

Next it remains the case that
e(H_{i})\geq 4
,
which implies that
d_{H_{i}}(v^{*})\geq 3
.
Write
a=d_{H_{i}}(v^{*})
and
b=e(H_{i}^{\prime})
, where
H_{i}^{\prime}=H_{i}-\{v^{*}\}.

If

, then
H_{i}
is a star, and we are done.
Now, suppose that
b\geq 1
.
Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G^{*}

by deleting
b
edges in
H_{i}^{\prime}

and adding
b
edges from
v^{*}
to
w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{b}\in V_{i}\setminus V(H_{i})
.
It is easy to see that
G^{\prime}\in\mathrm{EX}\big(n,\mathbb{G}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{k})\big)
,
and thus
\rho(G^{\prime})\leq\rho(G^{*})
.
Let
\rho^{\prime}=\rho(G^{\prime})
and

\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})^{T}
be the Perron vector of
G^{\prime}
.

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{y}(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j\leq b}(x_{v^{*}}y_{w_{j}}+x_{w_{j}}y_{v^{*}})-\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(H_{i}^{\prime})}\!\!(x_{u}y_{v}+x_{v}y_{u})

Clearly,

\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E(H_{i}^{\prime})}\rho(x_{u}+x_{v})

\displaystyle\leq 2bx_{\overline{V_{i}}}+(b^{2}+3b)x_{v^{*}}.

(29)

On the other hand,

and
N_{G^{*}}(w_{1})=\overline{V_{i}}\subseteq N_{G^{*}}(v)
for each

,
we have
\rho x_{w_{1}}=x_{\overline{V_{i}}}

\rho x_{v^{*}}\geq x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+ax_{w_{1}}.

Furthermore,

\frac{\rho+a+b}{\rho+1}\rho x_{w_{1}}\geq(\rho+a+b-2)x_{w_{1}}=x_{\overline{V_{i}}}+(a+b-2)x_{w_{1}}.

Combining with (

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{T}\mathbf{y}(\rho^{\prime}-\rho)\frac{\rho}{y_{w_{1}}}

\displaystyle\geq b\Big(\rho x_{v^{*}}+\frac{\rho+a+b}{\rho+1}\rho x_{w_{1}}\Big)-\!\!\sum_{uv\in E(H_{i}^{\prime})}\!\!\rho(x_{u}+x_{v})

\displaystyle\geq b(2a+b-2)x_{w_{1}}-(b^{2}+3b)x_{v^{*}}.

Recall that

.
As in the proof of Claim

,
we know that

.
Moreover, since

Combining (

),
we can deduce that

is a star.
∎

.

6
Concluding remarks

In 1974, Simonovits gave a result
(see Theorem 1.a.,
[
40
]
), which implies that:
Given a finite graph family
\mathcal{H}
with
\chi(\mathcal{H})=r+1
,
if
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H})
contains a linear forest,
then there exist integers
k=k(\mathcal{H})
,

n_{0}=n(\mathcal{H})
and a graph
G\in{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})
such that
after deleting at most
k
vertices of
G
,
the remaining graph contains a complete
r
-partite spanning subgraph
K_{r}(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{r})
for
n\geq n_{0}
,
where
|n_{i}-\frac{n}{r}|\leq k
for all
i\in[r]
.

In this paper,
we used the decomposition family to characterize which graph families
\mathcal{H}
satisfy
\chi(\mathcal{H})=r+1\geq 3
and
{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})<e(T_{n,r})+\lfloor\frac{n}{2r}\rfloor
(see Theorem
1.1
),
where the third sufficient and necessary condition states that
every graph in
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})

is obtained from
T_{n,r}
by adding and deleting
O(1)
edges.
It may be interesting to investigate
whether Theorem
1.1
can be strengthened. A natural first thought is that perhaps one does not need to remove edges. That is, perhaps extremal graphs are all obtained by adding edges to
T_{n,r}
. This is the case for many kinds of forbidden subgraphs
(see
K_{r+1}
, color-critical graphs
[
38
]
,
friendship graphs
[
13
]
,
intersecting cliques
[
2
]
,
and intersecting odd cycles
[
21
,
49
]
). However, the following example shows that this is not true in general.

Example 6.1
.

There exists a finite family
\mathcal{H}
with
\chi(\mathcal{H})=3
and
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{H})=e(T_{n,2})+\Theta(1)
such that there are no graphs in
\mathrm{EX}(n,\mathcal{H})
containing
T_{n,2}
as a subgraph.

Proof.

Let
s\geq 3
and let
H=\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
, where
H_{1}
is obtained from

K_{2s,2s}
by embedding two isolated edges in one partite set,

H_{2}
is obtained from
K_{2s,2s}
by embedding
K_{1,s+2}
in one partite set,
and
H_{3}
is obtained from
K_{2s,2s}
by embedding a copy of
K_{1,s}
in each partite set.
The chromatic number of
H_{i}
is 3 for
i=1,2,3
. Note that a matching on
2
edges and
K_{1,s+2}
are both in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{H})
, and hence by Theorem
1.1
we have that
\mathrm{e}x(n,\mathcal{H})=e(T_{n,2})+O(1)
.

Assume that
G
is an
\mathcal{H}
-free graph on
n
vertices where
n
is sufficiently large, and assume that
T_{n,2}
is a subgraph of
G
. Then because
G
is
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
free, each partite set may induce at most
s+1
edges with equality if and only if they each induce a star. Furthermore, since
G
is
H_{3}
-free, both partite sets may not induce stars with at least
s
edges, and therefore we have
e(G)\leq e(T_{n,2})+2s
with equality if and only if one part induces
K_{1,s-1}
and one part induces
K_{1,s+1}
.

On the other hand, the graph
G^{\prime}
obtained by removing one edge from
T_{n,2}
and embedding a star on
s+1
vertices into each part where the two centers are the endpoints of the removed edge is an
\mathcal{H}
-free graph with
e(G^{\prime})=e(T_{n,r})+2s+1
.
∎

Many scholars are interested in graph families

\mathcal{H}
with

{\rm SPEX}(n,\mathcal{H})\subseteq{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})
.
Based on previous extremal results,
we present the following problems.

Problem 6.1
.

For
r\geq 2
and
n
sufficiently large,
if a finite graph family
\mathcal{H}

satisfies that
e(G)=e(T_{n,r})+\Theta(n)

and
T_{n,r}\subseteq G
for every
G\in{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})
,
determine whether or not we have

{\rm SPEX}(n,\mathcal{H})\subseteq{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})
.

Problem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite graph family
with
\chi(\mathcal{H})=r+1\geq 3
.
Determine the maximum constant
c
such that for all
positive
\varepsilon<c
and sufficiently large
n
,

{\rm ex}(n,\mathcal{H})\leq e(T_{n,r})+(c-\varepsilon)n

implies
{\rm SPEX}(n,\mathcal{H})\subseteq{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{H})
.

By Theorem
1.4
, we can see that

.

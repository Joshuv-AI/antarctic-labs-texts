Exponential Formulas, Normal Ordering and the Weyl–Heisenberg Algebra
Stjepan MELJANAC a and Rina ŠTRAJN b

Abstract
We consider a class of exponentials in the Weyl–Heisenberg algebra with exponents of type at most linear in coordinates and arbitrary functions of momenta. They are expressed in terms of normal ordering where coordinates stand to the left from momenta. Exponents appearing in normal ordered form satisfy differential equations with boundary conditions that could be solved perturbatively order by order. Two propositions are presented for the Weyl–Heisenberg algebra in 2 dimensions and their generalizations in higher dimensions. These results can be applied to arbitrary noncommutative spaces for construction of star products, coproducts of momenta and twist operators. They can also be related to the BCH formula.

2
Exponential formula for the Weyl–Heisenberg algebra

in two dimensions

In this section we consider a class of exponentials in the Weyl–Heisenberg algebra in 2 dimensions (generated with one coordinate and the corresponding momentum) with exponents of type at most linear in the coordinate and arbitrary functions of momentum. They are expressed in terms of normal ordering in which powers of the coordinate stand to the left of the powers of momentum. Exponents appearing in normal ordered form satisfy differential equations with boundary conditions that could be solved perturbatively order by order. Proposition 2.1
.

If
[p,x]=-{\rm i}
, then

i)

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}={}{:}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x\phi(k,p)}{:}
,
k\in\mathbb{R}
,
where
{:}\,{:}
denotes normal ordering, with the
x

p

ii)

\phi(k,p)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}p{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}-p=J(k,p)-p
, i.e.,
J(k,p)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}p{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}
,

iii)

J(k,p)
is a unique solution of the partial differential equation

\frac{\partial J(k,p)}{\partial k}=\varphi(J(k,p)),

(2.1)

with boundary condition
J(0,p)=p
.

Proof.

Expanding
{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}({\rm i}kx\varphi(p))^{n}
, and performing normal ordering of each term
(x\varphi(p))^{n}
, we are led to a general Ansatz

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\rm i}x)^{n}}{n!}\phi_{n}(k,p),

for some
\phi_{n}(k,p)
,

(\operatorname{ad}_{p})^{m}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{({\rm i}x)^{n-m}}{(n-m)!}\phi_{n}(k,p),

where
\operatorname{ad}_{p}(x^{n})=[p,x^{n}]=-{\rm i}nx^{n-1}
, and

\big((\operatorname{ad}_{p})^{m}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\big)\big|_{x=0}=\phi_{m}(k,p).

Hence,

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\rm i}x)^{n}}{n!}\big((\operatorname{ad}_{p})^{n}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\big)\big)\big|_{x=0}.

Let us introduce

J(k,p)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}p{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}=p+\phi(k,p).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ad}_{p}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\big)

\displaystyle=\big[p,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\big]={\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\big({\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}p{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}-p\big)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}(J(k,p)-p)={\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\phi(k,p).

Similarly,

(\operatorname{ad}_{p})^{n}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\big)={\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}(\phi(k,p))^{n}.

It follows

\phi_{n}(k,p)=(\operatorname{ad}_{p})^{n}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\big)\big|_{x=0}=(\phi(k,p))^{n}.

Hence

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\rm i}x)^{n}}{n!}(\phi(k,p))^{n}={:}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x\phi(k,p)}{:}.

The differential equation for
J(k,p)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial J(k,p)}{\partial k}

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}(-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)p+{\rm i}px\varphi(p)){\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\varphi(p){\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}

\displaystyle=\varphi\big({\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}p{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\big)=\varphi(J(k,p)),

with boundary condition
J(0,p)=p
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

If
[p,x]=-{\rm i}
, then

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)+{\rm i}k\chi(p)}={\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}h(k,p)}={:}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x\phi(k,p)}{:}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}h(k,p)},\qquad k\in\mathbb{R},

where

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}h(k,p)}={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)+{\rm i}k\chi(p)},

(2.2)

and

\frac{\partial h(k,p)}{\partial k}=\chi(J(k,p)),

with boundary condition

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h(k,p)}{\partial k}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}h(k,p)}

\displaystyle=-{\rm i}\frac{\partial}{\partial k}\left({\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)+{\rm i}k\chi(p)}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)}\left(-x\varphi(p)+x\varphi(p)+\chi(p)\right){\rm e}^{{\rm i}kx\varphi(p)+{\rm i}x\chi(p)}

3
Exponential formula for the Weyl–Heisenberg algebra

in higher dimensions

Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
can be generalized to the Weyl–Heisenberg algebra in higher dimensions, which leads to two theorems. Here the Weyl–Heisenberg algebra is generated with coordinates
x_{\mu}
and momenta
p_{\nu}
with Minkowski metric
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,\dots,1)
and
\mu,\nu=0,1,\dots,n-1
. Summation over repeated indices is assumed. Generalization to arbitrary signature is straightforward.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
[x_{\mu},x_{\nu}]=0
,
[p_{\mu},p_{\nu}]=0
and
[p_{\mu},x_{\nu}]=-{\rm i}\eta_{\mu\nu}
, then

i)

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}k_{\beta}x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(p)+{\rm i}k_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}(p)}={:}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}(k,p)}{:}\,{\rm e}^{{\rm i}h(k,p)}
,
k=(k_{\mu})\in M_{n}
, where
M_{n}
denotes Minkowski space.

ii)

\phi_{\mu}(k,p)=\big({\rm e}^{O}\big)(p_{\mu})-p_{\mu}=\big(\frac{{\rm e}^{O}-1}{O}\big)(k_{\beta}\varphi_{\mu\beta}(p))=J_{\mu}(k,p)-p_{\mu}
,
where

O=k_{\alpha}O_{\alpha}
and
O_{\alpha}=\operatorname{ad}_{(-{\rm i}x_{\beta}\varphi_{\beta\alpha}(p))}
.

iii)

J_{\mu}(k,p)={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}k_{\beta}x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(p)}p_{\mu}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}k_{\beta}x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\beta(p)}}=\big({\rm e}^{k_{\alpha}O_{\alpha}}\big)(p_{\mu})
,

J_{\mu}(k,p)
is the unique solution of the partial differential equation

k_{\beta}\frac{\partial J_{\mu}(k,p)}{\partial k_{\beta}}=k_{\beta}\varphi_{\mu\beta}(J(k,p)),\qquad J_{\mu}(0,p)=p_{\mu}.

iv)

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}h(k,p)}={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}k_{\beta}x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(p)}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}k_{\beta}x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(p)+{\rm i}k_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}(p)}
,

h(k,p)=\big(\frac{{\rm e}^{k_{\alpha}O_{\alpha}}-1}{k_{\alpha}O_{\alpha}}\big)(k_{\beta}\chi_{\beta}(p))
.

h(k,p)
is the unique solution of the partial differential equation

k_{\beta}\frac{\partial h(k,p)}{\partial k_{\beta}}=k_{\beta}\chi_{\beta}(J(k,p)),\qquad h(0,p)=0.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
follows from Propositions
2.1

\displaystyle\hphantom{J_\mu(k,p)}{}=p_{\mu}+k_{\alpha}\varphi_{\mu\alpha}(p)+\frac{1}{2}k_{\alpha^{\prime}}\varphi_{\beta^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime}}(p)\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\beta^{\prime}}}(k_{\alpha}\varphi_{\mu\alpha}(p))

(3.1)

If we perform a transformation

,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, Theorem
3.1
becomes

Theorem 3.2
.

i)

{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda k_{\beta}x_{\alpha}\varphi_{\alpha\beta}(p)+{\rm i}\lambda k_{\alpha}\chi_{\alpha}(p)}={:}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{\alpha}\phi_{\alpha}(\lambda k,p)}{:}\,{\rm e}^{h(\lambda k,p)}

Corollary 3.5
.

Let

and let

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda x_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}^{(1)}(p)}={:}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{\alpha}(J_{\alpha}^{(1)}(\lambda,p)-p_{\alpha})}{:},

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda x_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}^{(2)}(p)}={:}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{\alpha}(J_{\alpha}^{(2)}(\lambda,p)-p_{\alpha})}{:},

\displaystyle J_{\mu}^{(1)}(\lambda,p)=\big({\rm e}^{\lambda O^{(1)}}\big)(p_{\mu}),\qquad O^{(1)}=\operatorname{ad}_{-{\rm i}x_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}^{(1)}(p)},

\displaystyle J_{\mu}^{(2)}(\lambda,p)=\big({\rm e}^{\lambda O^{(2)}}\big)(p_{\mu}),\qquad O^{(2)}=\operatorname{ad}_{-{\rm i}x_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}^{(2)}(p)}.

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda x_{\alpha}F_{\alpha}^{(1)}(p)}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda x_{\beta}F_{\beta}^{(2)}(p)}={\rm e}^{{\rm i}\lambda x_{\gamma}F_{\gamma}^{(3)}(p)}={:}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}x_{\gamma}(J_{\gamma}^{(3)}(\lambda,p)-p_{\gamma})}{:},

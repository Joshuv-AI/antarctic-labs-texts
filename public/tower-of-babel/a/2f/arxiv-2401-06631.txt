Generalized exponential pullback attractor for a nonautonomous wave equation
Matheus C. Bortolan, Tomás Caraballo, Carlos Pecorari Neto

Abstract
. In this work we introduce the concept of generalized exponential 𝔇 \mathfrak{D} -pullback attractor for evolution processes, where 𝔇 \mathfrak{D} is a universe of families in X X , which is a compact and positively invariant family that pullback attracts all elements of 𝔇 \mathfrak{D} with an exponential rate . Such concept was introduced in [ 1 ] for the general case of decaying functions (which include the exponential decay), but for fixed bounded sets rather than to universe of families. We prove a result that ensures the existence of a generalized exponential 𝔇 𝒞 ∗ \mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}} -pullback attractor for an evolution process, where 𝔇 𝒞 ∗ \mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}} is a specific universe. This required an adaptation of the results of [ 1 ] , which only covered the case of a polynomial rate of attraction, for fixed bounded sets. Later, we prove that a nonautonomous wave equation has a generalized exponential 𝔇 𝒞 ∗ \mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}} -pullback attractor. This, in turn, also implies the existence of the 𝔇 𝒞 ∗ \mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}} -pullback attractor for such problem. Keywords : Generalized exponential pullback attractors. Exponential pullback κ \kappa -dissipativity. Nonautonomous wave equation. Evolution processes. Pullback attractors. MSC2020: 35B41, 35L20, 37L25.

Now, we extend the theory presented in

For what follows, unless clearly stated otherwise,
(X,d)

\displaystyle\kappa(B)=\inf\{\delta>0\colon B

\displaystyle\hbox{ admits a finite cover by sets of diameter less than or equal to }\delta\}.

.

A function

are precompact on
B_{t-T}
and
\psi_{1}\in\operatorname{contr}(B_{t-T})
, we have

(2.2)

\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}g_{1}(\rho_{1}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}}),\cdots,\rho_{m}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}}))=0\hbox{ and }\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}\psi_{1}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}})=0.

Joining (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and hypothesis
(iv)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}d(S_{1}x_{i_{k}},S_{1}x_{i_{l}})^{r}=\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}d(S(t,t-T)x_{i_{k}},S(t,t-T)x_{i_{l}})^{r}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}[\mu d(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}})^{r}+g_{1}(\rho_{1}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}}),\cdots,\rho_{m}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}}))+\psi_{1}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}})]\leqslant\mu(\kappa(A)+\varepsilon)^{r},

and since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that for any sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset A
we have

\liminf_{k,\ell\to\infty}d(S_{1}x_{k},S_{1}x_{\ell})^{r}\leqslant\mu\kappa(A)^{r}.

Now, let
A\subset B_{t-2T}
,
\varepsilon>0
and
\left\{x_{i}\right\}\subset A
. As before, there exists a subsequence
\left\{x_{i_{k}}\right\}
for which
d(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}})<\kappa(A)+\varepsilon
for all
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{m}
are precompact on
B_{t-T}
and
B_{t-2T}
,
\psi_{1}\in\operatorname{contr}(B_{t-T})
and
\psi_{2}\in\operatorname{contr}(B_{t-2T})
, and
S_{2}B_{t-2T}\subset B_{t-T}
, we obtain

\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}g_{2}(\rho_{1}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}}),\cdots,\rho_{m}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}}))=0\hbox{ , }\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}\psi_{2}(x_{i_{k}},x_{i_{l}})=0,

\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}g_{1}(\rho_{1}(S_{2}x_{i_{k}},S_{2}x_{i_{l}}),\cdots,\rho_{m}(S_{2}x_{i_{k}},S_{2}x_{i_{l}}))=0\hbox{ , }\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}\psi_{1}(S_{2}x_{i_{k}},S_{2}x_{i_{l}})=0.

Since for any
x,y\in B_{t-2T}
,

\displaystyle d(S(t,t-2T)x,S(t,t-2T)y)^{r}=d(S_{1}S_{2}x,S_{1}S_{2}y)^{r}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\mu d(S_{2}x,S_{2}y)^{r}+g_{1}(\rho_{1}(S_{2}x,S_{2}y),\ldots,\rho_{m}(S_{2}x,S_{2}y))+\psi_{1}(S_{2}x,S_{2}y)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\mu[\mu d(x,y)^{r}+g_{2}(\rho_{1}(x,y),\ldots,\rho_{m}(x,y))+\psi_{2}(x,y)]

\displaystyle\qquad+g_{1}(\rho_{1}(S_{2}x,S_{2}y),\ldots,\rho_{m}(S_{2}x,S_{2}y))+\psi_{1}(S_{2}x,S_{2}y),

we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{k,l\rightarrow\infty}d(S(t,t-2T)x_{i_{k}},S(t,t-2T)x_{i_{l}})^{r}\leqslant\mu^{2}(\kappa(A)+\varepsilon)^{r},

and, again, since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that for any sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset A
,

\liminf_{m,p\rightarrow\infty}d(S(t,t-2T)x_{m},S(t,t-2T)x_{p})^{r}\leqslant\mu^{2}\kappa(A)^{r}.

Inductively, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
A\subset B_{t-nT}
and
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset A
we obtain

(2.3)

\liminf_{m,p\rightarrow\infty}d(S(t,t-nT)x_{m},S(t,t-nT)x_{p})^{r}\leqslant\mu^{n}\kappa(A)^{r}.

Since
\hat{B}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
, there exists
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
such that
\kappa(B_{t-s})\leqslant\delta(t-s)
for all
s\leqslant t
. We claim that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\subset B_{t-nT}
we have

(2.4)

\kappa(S(t,t-nT)A)^{r}\leqslant 2^{r}\mu^{n}\kappa(A)^{r}\leqslant 2^{r}\mu^{n}\delta^{r}(t-nT).

Assume that the first inequality in (
2.4
) fails. Then we can choose
a>0
such that

2^{r}\mu^{n}\kappa(A)^{r}<a<\kappa(S(t,t-nT)A)^{r}.

Thus implies that

\displaystyle\beta(S(t,t-nT)A)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\kappa(S(t,t-nT)A)>\frac{a^{1/r}}{2},

that is,
S(t,t-nT)A
has no finite cover of balls of radius less than or equal to
\frac{a^{1/r}}{2}
. Take an arbitrary
x_{1}\in A
. Then, there exists
x_{2}\in A
such that
d(S(t,t-nT)x_{1},S(t,t-nT)x_{2})>\frac{a^{1/r}}{2}
, for otherwise
S(t,t-nT)A\subset\overline{B}_{\frac{a^{1/r}}{2}}(S(t,t-nT)x_{1})
. Following this idea, there exists
x_{3}\in A
such that
d(S(t,t-nT)x_{3},S(t,t-nT)x_{i})>\frac{a^{1/r}}{2}
for
i=1,2
, for otherwise
S(t,t-nT)A
would be contained in the union of two balls of radius
\frac{a^{1/r}}{2}
. This process gives us a sequence
\left\{x_{i}\right\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset A
such that
d(S(t,t-nT)x_{i},S(t,t-nT)x_{j})>\frac{a^{1/r}}{2}
for all
i\neq j
. Therefore

\displaystyle d(S(t,t-nT)x_{i},S(t,t-nT)x_{j})^{r}>\frac{a}{2^{r}}>\mu^{n}\kappa(A)^{r},

which contradicts (
2.3
). The second inequality of (
2.4
) is trivial.

In particular, we conclude from (
2.4
) that

\kappa(S(t,t-nT)B_{t-nT})\leqslant 2\delta(t-nT)\mu^{\frac{n}{r}}\quad\hbox{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\delta(t-nT)\mu^{\frac{n}{2r}}\leqslant 1
for
n\geqslant n_{0}
. Thus, for
n\geqslant n_{0}
we obtain

(2.5)

\kappa(S(t,t-nT)B_{t-nT})\leqslant 2\mu^{\frac{n}{2r}}.

Finally, for
s\geqslant(n_{0}+1)T
, if
n\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
\frac{s}{T}-1<n\leqslant\frac{s}{T}
, we have
n\geqslant n_{0}
and
nT\leqslant s
. From the positive invariance of
\hat{B}
and (
2.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle(S(t,t-s)B_{t-s})=\kappa\left(S\left(t,t-nT\right)S\left(t-nT,t-s\right)B_{t-s}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa\left(S\left(t,t-nT\right)B_{t-nT}\right)\leqslant 2\mu^{\frac{n}{2r}}\leqslant 2\mu^{\frac{s}{2rT}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2r}}\leqslant C\mu^{\omega s},

where
C=2\mu^{-\frac{1}{2r}}
and
\omega=\frac{1}{2rT}
. It follows from Proposition
2.4
that
S
is exponentially
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
-pullback
\kappa
-dissipative, and therefore, from Proposition
2.2
there exists a generalized exponential
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
-pullback attractor
\hat{M}
for
S
with
\hat{M}\subset\hat{B}
. Since
\hat{B}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
is a universe, we obtain
\hat{M}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
.
∎

We point out that this result, although specific to
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
, holds true for any universe
\mathfrak{D}
such that for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\kappa(B_{t-nT})\leqslant\delta(t-nT)
, where
\delta
is a function such that
\mathbb{N}\ni n\mapsto\delta(t-nT)\mu^{\alpha n}
is bounded for all
n
sufficiently large, for some
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{r}
. In particular, it holds for the universe of
backwards bounded families

\mathfrak{D}_{bb}=\{\hat{D}\colon\cup_{s\leqslant t}D_{s}\hbox{ is bounded for each }t\in\mathbb{R}\},

and for the universe of
uniformly bounded families

\mathfrak{D}_{ub}=\{\hat{D}\colon\cup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}D_{t}\hbox{ is bounded}\}.

,

,

]
and adapting the results of

]
, we study the nonautonomous wave equation with non-local weak damping and anti-damping (

3.1.
Auxiliary estimates

Now we present a few estimates, regarding the functions
f
and
F
, that will help us in the results to come. They are analogous to the ones presented in
[
1
]
, and for this reason, we omit their proofs. To simplify the notation, we also omit the
(\Omega)
from the subscript of the norms. For instance, we write
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and henceforth.

For
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we have

\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)v(y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant K_{0}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

For all
t,v,w\in\mathbb{R}
we have:

\displaystyle|f(t,v)|\leqslant 2c_{0}(t)(1+|v|^{3});

\displaystyle|f(t,v)-f(t,w)|\leqslant 2c_{0}(t)(1+|v|^{2}+|w|^{2})|v-w|;

(3.1)

\displaystyle|F(t,v)|\leqslant 4c_{0}(t)(1+|v|^{4});

\displaystyle|F(t,v)-F(t,w)|\leqslant 8c_{0}(t)(1+|v|^{3}+|w|^{3})|v-w|;

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(t,v)-\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(t,w)\right|\leqslant 2c_{0}(t)|v-w|.

In what follows, we use
\hookrightarrow
to denote continuous inclusions. With the estimates above, Hölder’s inequality, and the continuous inclusion
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\Omega)
, we can show that there exists a constant
c>0
such that for
L_{0}(t)=cc_{0}(t)
we have

(3.2)

\|f(t,v)-f(t,w)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant L_{0}(t)(1+\|v\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2}+\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2})\|v-w\|_{H^{1}_{0}}\quad\hbox{ for all }v,w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Also, from the definition of
\liminf
, it follows that given
0<\mu_{0}<\lambda_{1}
there exists
M=M(\mu_{0})>0
such that

(3.3)

\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial v}(t,v)>-\mu_{0}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{f(t,v)}{v}>-\mu_{0}\quad\hbox{ for all }|v|>M.

It is also clear that for each
M>0
we have

\int_{-M}^{M}|f(t,v)|dv\leqslant 8(1+M^{4})c_{0}(t)\hbox{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Now, fixing
0<\mu_{0}<\lambda_{1}
and considering
M=M(\mu_{0})>0
given by (
3.3
), we obtain

(3.4)

F(t,v)\geqslant-\tfrac{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{1}}{4}v^{2}-8(1+M^{4})c_{0}(t)\quad\hbox{ for }|v|>M\hbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R},

and

(3.5)

|F(t,v)|\leqslant 8(1+M^{4})c_{0}(t)\quad\hbox{ for }|v|\leqslant M\hbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, we can prove that there exists a constant
e_{0}>0
such that

F(t,v)\leqslant vf(t,v)+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}v^{2}+e_{0}\quad\hbox{ for }|v|>M\hbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}.

Translation of the problem.

Fixing
s\in\mathbb{R}
and setting
v(t,x):=u(t+s,x)
for
t\geqslant 0
and
x\in\Omega
, we formally have

\displaystyle v_{tt}(t,x)-\Delta v(t,x)+k_{s}(t)v_{t}(t,x)+f_{s}(t,v(t,x))-\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)v_{t}(t,y)dy-h(x)

\displaystyle=u_{tt}(t+s,x)-\Delta u(t+s,x)+k_{s}(t)u_{t}(t+s,x)+f_{s}(t,u(t+s,x))-\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)u_{t}(t+s,y)dy-h(x)=0,

where the boundary and initial conditions become

\displaystyle v(t,x)=u(t+s,x)=0\quad\hbox{ for }(t,x)\in\left[0,\infty\right)\times\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle v(0,x)=u(s,x)=u_{0}(x),\ v_{t}(0,x)=u_{t}(s,x)=u_{1}(x)\quad\hbox{ for }x\in\Omega.

Thus, we will study the
translated
boundary and initial conditions problem

(tNWE)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{tt}(t,x)\!-\!\Delta v(t,x)\!+\!k_{s}(t)v_{t}(t,x)\!+\!f_{s}(t,v(t,x))\!=\!\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)v_{t}(t,y)dy\!+\!h(x),\ (t,x)\!\in\![0,\infty)\!\times\!\Omega,\\
&v(t,x)=0,\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
&v(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\ v_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ x\in\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

instead of (
NWE
). This problem is equivalent to the initial one, but with the nonautonomous terms being
k_{s}(\cdot)=k(\cdot+s)
and
f_{s}(\cdot,\cdot)=f(\cdot+s,\cdot)
instead of
k
and
f
. Additionally, we denote
F_{s}(\cdot,\cdot)=F(\cdot+s,\cdot)
.

Taking
w=v_{t}
in (
tNWE
), we obtain

w_{t}=v_{tt}=\Delta v-k_{s}(t)v_{t}-f_{s}(t,v)+\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)v_{t}(t,y)dy+h(x),

and thus

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}v_{t}\\
w_{t}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}w\\
\Delta v-k_{s}(t)v_{t}-f_{s}(t,v)+\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)v_{t}(t,y)dy+h(x)\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\
\Delta&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)w(t,y)dy+h(x)-f_{s}(t,v)-k_{s}(t)w\end{bmatrix}.

Setting
X:=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
,
V=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}v\\
w\end{smallmatrix}\right]
,
V_{0}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}u_{0}\\
u_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
,
\mathcal{A}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\
\Delta&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
and
\mathcal{G}_{s}(t,V)=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
G_{s}(t,V)\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, where

G_{s}(t,V)=\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)w(t,y)dy+h(x)-f_{s}(t,v)-k_{s}(t)w,

we can represent (
tNWE
) by an abstract Cauchy problem

(ACP)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{dV}{dt}=\mathcal{A}V+\mathcal{G}_{s}(t,V),\ t>0\\
&V(0)=V_{0}\in X.\end{aligned}\right.

Clearly
X
is a Hilbert space with the inner product defined by

\left\langle\left[\begin{smallmatrix}v_{1}\\
w_{1}\end{smallmatrix}\right],\left[\begin{smallmatrix}v_{2}\\
w_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right\rangle_{X}=\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

with associate norm

\left\|\left[\begin{smallmatrix}v\\
w\end{smallmatrix}\right]\right\|_{X}^{2}=\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}}+\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Using Lumer-Phillips’ Theorem and the results of
[
7
]
for semilinear evolution equations, we obtain the following:

Proposition 3.1
.

Given
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
V_{0}\in X
there exists a unique maximal weak solution
V(\cdot,s,V_{0})\colon[0,\tau_{m})\to X
of (
ACP
), that is, a continuous function such that

V(t,s,V_{0})=e^{\mathcal{A}t}V_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mathcal{A}(t-\tau)}\mathcal{G}_{s}(\tau,V(\tau,s,V_{0}))d\tau\quad\hbox{ for }t\in[0,\tau_{m}),

such that either
\tau_{m}=\infty
, or
\tau_{m}<\infty
and
\limsup_{t\to\tau_{m}^{-}}\|V(t,s,V_{0})\|_{X}=\infty
, where
\{e^{\mathcal{A}t}\colon t\geqslant 0\}
is the
C^{0}
-semigroup of contractions generated by
\mathcal{A}
in
X
.

Writing
V(t,s,V_{0})=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}v(t)\\
w(t)\end{smallmatrix}\right]
we can see that

v\in C([0,\tau_{m}),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\hbox{ and }w\in C([0,\tau_{m}),L^{2}(\Omega)).

Since
w=v_{t}
, we conclude that

v\in C([0,\tau_{m}),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap C^{1}([0,\tau_{m}),L^{2}(\Omega))

is the unique maximal weak solution to (
tNWE
). Furthermore,
v_{tt}\colon(0,\tau_{m})\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
is well-defined and

\|v_{tt}(t)\|_{H^{-1}}\leqslant\|v(t)\|_{H^{1}_{0}}+k_{s}(t)\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{s}(t,v(t))\|_{L^{2}}+K_{0}\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|v_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\lambda_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}h_{0}.

Later we show that
\tau_{m}=\infty
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
V_{0}\in X
(see Remark
3.4
). For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
V_{0}:=(u_{0},u_{1})\in X
, defining

S(t,s)(u_{0},u_{1})=V(t-s,s,V_{0})\quad\hbox{ for }t\geqslant s,

setting
(u(t),y(t))=S(t,s)(u_{0},u_{1})
, then
u(t)=v(t-s)
and
y(t)=v_{t}(t-s)=u_{t}(t)
. Thus
(u(t),u_{t}(t))
is the weak solution of (
NWE
), and
S=\{S(t,s)\colon t\geqslant s\}
defines an evolution process in
X
associated with (
NWE
), provided we have continuity with respect to initial data (which we also show, see Theorem
3.7
).

3.2.
Properties of uniform pullback absorption

In this section we prove the existence of a closed, positively invariant and bounded pullback absorbing family. Recall that
0<\mu_{0}<\lambda_{1}
is fixed and we consider
M=M(\mu_{0})>0
given by (
3.3
). For
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
we define

\Omega_{1}:=\{x\in\Omega\colon|v(x)|>M\}\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\Omega_{2}:=\{x\in\Omega\colon|v(x)|\leqslant M\}.

From (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), for
t\in\mathbb{R}
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle F(t,v)dx=\int_{\Omega_{1}}F(t,v)dx+\int_{\Omega_{2}}F(t,v)dx

\displaystyle\geqslant-\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left[\tfrac{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{1}}{4}|v|^{2}+8c_{0}(t)(1+M^{4})\right]dx-\int_{\Omega_{2}}8c_{0}(t)(1+M^{4})dx\geqslant-\tfrac{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{1}}{4}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-C_{0}(t),

where
C_{0}(t):=8(1+M^{4})|\Omega|c_{0}(t)
. This means that

(3.6)

\int_{\Omega}F(t,v)dx+\tfrac{\mu_{0}+\lambda_{1}}{4}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C_{0}(t)\geqslant 0,

and it is clear that
C_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
.

For each
V_{0}=(u_{0},u_{1})\in X
, we define the function
E_{s}(\cdot,V_{0})\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(3.7)

E_{s}(t,V_{0})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2})+\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(t,v)dx+\tfrac{\lambda_{1}+\mu_{0}}{4}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C_{0}(t+s),

where
V(t,s,V_{0})=(v(t),v_{t}(t))
for
t\geqslant 0
. For simplicity, we write
E_{s}(t)
instead of
E_{s}(t,V_{0})
, but we must keep in mind that this function depends on
V_{0}
, since it depends on the solution
V(t,s,V_{0})
. Note that for
t\geqslant s
and
V_{0}=(u_{0},u_{1})\in X
, from (
3.7
) and (
3.6
) we have

\displaystyle\|S(t,s)(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|V(t-s,s,V_{0})\|_{X}^{2}\leqslant 2E_{s}(t-s).

Hence, the study of the function
E_{s}
is paramount, since this function bounds the norm of
S(t,s)(u_{0},u_{1})
for
t\geqslant s
.

For
\varepsilon>0
and
(v,v_{t})=V(t,s,V_{0})
we define an auxiliary function
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\cdot,V_{0})\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(t,V_{0})=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}}+\|v_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(t,v)dx-\int_{\Omega}hvdx+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}v_{t}vdx.

As we did for the function
E_{s}
, to simplify the notation we simply write
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(t)
instead of
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(t,V_{0})
. Adapting the results of
[
1
]
, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

For all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\geqslant 0
we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}f_{s}(t,v)vdx

\displaystyle\leqslant-\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(t,v)dx-\tfrac{\lambda_{1}+\mu_{0}}{4}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-C_{0}(t+s)+\tfrac{1}{4}\left(\tfrac{3\mu_{0}}{\lambda_{1}}+1\right)\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}}+g_{0}(t+s),

where
g_{0}(t):=e_{0}|\Omega_{1}|+8c_{0}(t)(1+M^{4})|\Omega_{2}|+8Mc_{0}(t)(1+M^{4})+C_{0}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore, there exists a constant
K^{\ast}>0
such that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\geqslant 0
and
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle-k_{s}(t)\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\varepsilon k_{s}(t)\int_{\Omega}v_{t}vdx\leqslant-k_{0}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon K^{\ast}k_{1}}{k_{0}}\right)+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{12}\left(1-\tfrac{\mu_{0}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\|v\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2};

Also, for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\geqslant 0
and

\mathcal{E}_{s}(\cdot)\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
by

\mathcal{E}_{s}(t)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|Z(t)\|^{2}_{X}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\|z\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}}+\|z_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}),

where
Z(t,Z_{0})=(z(t),z_{t}(t))
for
t\geqslant 0
. In order to obtain a suitable estimate for
\mathcal{E}_{s}
, we use the auxiliary function
\mathcal{F}_{s}
defined by

\mathcal{F}_{s}(t)=\mathcal{E}_{s}(t)+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\langle z,z_{t}\rangle,

where
\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}},k_{0}\right\}>0
. For
t\geqslant 0
it is clear that

(3.17)

\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_{s}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{F}_{s}(t)\leqslant\tfrac{3}{2}\mathcal{E}_{s}(t).

Formally multiplying (
3.15
) by
z_{t}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}z
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we obtain

(3.18)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{s}(t)+\varepsilon_{0}\mathcal{F}_{s}(t)=-k_{s}(t)\|z_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon_{0}\|z_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{2}\langle z,z_{t}\rangle-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}k_{s}(t)\langle z,z_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle f_{s}(t,v)-f_{s}(t,w),z_{t}\rangle-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\langle f_{s}(t,v)-f_{s}(t,w),z\rangle

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)z_{t}(t,x)dydx+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)z(t,x)dydx.

Now observe that for
t\in[0,T]
we have

-k_{s}(t)\|z_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon_{0}\left\|z_{t}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leqslant 0,

\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{2}\langle z,z_{t}\rangle\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}}{2}\left\|z_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}\|z\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\Xi_{T}(s)\|z\|_{L^{2}},

-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}k_{s}(t)\langle z,z_{t}\rangle\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}k_{1}\Xi_{T}(s)\|z\|_{L^{2}},

-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\langle f_{s}(t,v)-f_{s}(t,w),z\rangle\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}L_{0}(t+s)\Xi_{T}(s)(1+2\Xi_{T}^{2}(s))\|z\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}L_{0}(t+s)\Big)\Xi_{T}(s)(1+2\Xi_{T}^{2}(s))\|z\|_{L^{2}},

\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)z_{t}(t,x)dydx\leqslant 2\Xi_{T}(s)\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}},

\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)z(t,x)dydx\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}K_{0}\Xi_{T}(s)\|z\|_{L^{2}}.

Taking

\displaystyle\varphi_{T}(s)=\Xi_{T}(s)\big[2+K_{0}\varepsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{0}^{2}+k_{1}\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}L_{0}(t+s)(1+2\Xi_{T}^{2}(s))\big],

we can see that
\varphi_{T}\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
and inserting the previous estimates into (
3.18
), for
t\in[0,T]
we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}_{s}(t)+\varepsilon_{0}\mathcal{F}_{s}(t)\leqslant\varphi_{T}(s)\|z\|_{L^{2}}-\langle f_{s}(t,v)-f_{s}(t,w),z_{t}\rangle+\varphi_{T}(s)\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
T>0
fixed. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{s}(T)

\displaystyle\leqslant 3e^{-\varepsilon_{0}T}\mathcal{E}_{s}(0)+2\varphi_{T}(s)T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|z(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}+2\varphi_{T}(s)\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\quad+2\left|\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(t-T)}\langle f_{s}(t,v)-f_{s}(t,w),z_{t}(t)\rangle dt\right|.

Proof.

For every
t\geqslant 0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(e^{\varepsilon_{0}t}\mathcal{F}_{s}(t))

\displaystyle=e^{\varepsilon_{0}t}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{F}_{s}(t)+\varepsilon_{0}\mathcal{F}_{s}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\varepsilon_{0}t}\varphi_{T}(s)\left\|z\right\|_{L^{2}}-e^{\varepsilon_{0}t}\langle f_{s}(t,v)-f_{s}(t,w),z_{t}\rangle+e^{\varepsilon_{0}t}\varphi_{T}(s)\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Now, an integration from
0
to
T
provides

\displaystyle e^{\varepsilon_{0}T}\mathcal{F}_{s}(T)-\mathcal{F}_{s}(0)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{\varepsilon_{0}T}\varphi_{T}(s)\int_{0}^{T}\left\|z(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}dt+e^{\varepsilon_{0}T}\varphi_{T}(s)\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}t}\langle f_{s}(t,v)-f_{s}(t,w),z_{t}(t)\rangle dt\right|,

which implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{s}(T)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-\varepsilon_{0}T}\mathcal{F}_{s}(0)+\varphi_{T}(s)\int_{0}^{T}\left\|z(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}dt+\varphi_{T}(s)\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}}dt

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(t-T)}\langle f_{s}(t,v)-f_{s}(t,w),z_{t}(t)\rangle dt\right|,

and the proof is done if we apply (
3.17
) to the previous inequality and consider that
\int_{0}^{T}\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}dt\leqslant T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.
∎

From (
3.16
) and Lemma
3.8
, we conclude that for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
T>0
, writing
S_{n}=S(t-(n-1)T,t-nT)
, we have,

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle(S_{n}V_{1},S_{n}V_{2})^{2}\leqslant 3e^{-\varepsilon_{0}T}d(V_{1},V_{2})^{2}+4T\varphi_{T}(t-nT)\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+4\varphi_{T}(t-nT)\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(t,y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}}dt+4\left|\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(t-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(t,v)-f_{t-nT}(t,w),z_{t}(t)\rangle dt\right|.

Note that
\mu:=3e^{-\varepsilon_{0}T}\in(0,1)
if we take
T>\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\ln 3
. For instance, taking
T=1+\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}\ln 3
, we have

\mu=3e^{-\varepsilon_{0}(\varepsilon_{0}^{-1}ln3+1)}=3e^{-ln3}e^{-\varepsilon_{0}}=e^{-\min\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}},k_{0}\right\}}.

Consider the functions
g_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
g_{n}(\alpha,\beta)=4\varphi_{T}(t-nT)\alpha+4T\varphi_{T}(t-nT)\beta
. It is clear that each
g_{n}
is non-decreasing with respect to each variable,
g_{n}(0,0)=0
and
g_{n}
is continuous at
(0,0)
. Additionally, define the maps
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\psi_{n}\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
by

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(V_{1},V_{2})=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\int_{\Omega}K(x,y)z_{t}(\tau,y)dy\right\|_{L^{2}}d\tau,

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(V_{1},V_{2})=\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\|z(\tau)\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(V_{1},V_{2})=4\left|\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v)-f_{t-nT}(\tau,w),z_{t}(\tau)\rangle dt\right|,

where
(v(\tau),v_{t}(\tau))=V(\tau,t-nT,V_{1})
,
(w(t),w_{t}(t))=V(\tau,t-nT,V_{2})
and
(z(\tau),z_{t}(\tau))=Z(\tau)=V(\tau,t-nT,V_{1})-V(\tau,t-nT,V_{2})
.

We already know that the evolution process
S
associated with (
NWE
) satisfies the Lipschitz condition required in Theorem
2.5
(see Theorem
3.7
). We need to verify that
\psi_{n}\in\operatorname{contr}(C_{t-nT})
, and also that
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are precompact in
C_{t-nT}
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We begin by proving that
\psi_{n}\in\operatorname{contr}(C_{t-nT})
. Let
\left\{V_{k}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{t-nT}
and denote
V(t,t-nT,V_{k})=(v^{(k)}(t),v_{t}^{(k)}(t))
. As in
[
1
]
, we have the following result:

Lemma 3.9
.

For a fixed
\gamma\in(0,1)
, up to a subsequence, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(v^{(k)},v_{t}^{(k)})\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}(v,v_{t})\hbox{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;X),\\
&v^{(k)}\rightarrow v\hbox{ in }C([0,T];H^{\gamma}(\Omega)).\end{aligned}\right.

Also for each
\ell\in[0,T]
and
\psi\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
it holds that

\int_{\ell}^{T}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,w^{(k)}(\tau))-f_{t-nT}(\tau,w(\tau)),\psi(\tau)\rangle d\tau\rightarrow 0

as
k\rightarrow\infty
, and

f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))\hbox{ in }L^{\infty}(t,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\hbox{ as }k\to\infty.

Now, we have:

Lemma 3.10
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau)),v_{t}^{(m)}(\tau)\rangle d\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau)),v_{t}(\tau)\rangle d\tau,

and

\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(m)}(\tau)),v_{t}^{(k)}(\tau)\rangle d\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau)),v_{t}^{(k)}(\tau)\rangle d\tau.

Proof.

For the first item, note that defining
\xi\colon[0,T]\to L^{2}(\Omega)
by
\xi(\tau)=e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau))
for each
\tau\in[0,T]
, we have
\xi\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau))\right\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\underbrace{\left|e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\right|}_{\leqslant 1}\left\|f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau))\right\|_{L^{2}}d\tau\leqslant T\delta(t-nT)<\infty,

for some
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
. Since, by Lemma
3.9
,
v_{t}^{(m)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}v_{t}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
as
m\to\infty
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\xi(\tau),v^{(m)}_{t}(\tau)-v_{t}(\tau)\rangle d\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

For the second item, define
\xi\colon[0,T]\to L^{2}(\Omega)
by
\xi(\tau)=e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}v_{t}^{(k)}(\tau)
for each
\tau\in[0,T]
. Clearly
\xi\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, since for some
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
we have

\int_{0}^{T}\|\xi(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}v_{t}^{(k)}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\underbrace{|e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}|}_{\leqslant 1}\|v_{t}^{(k)}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau\leqslant T\delta(t-nT)<\infty,

and hence, since
f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(m)}(\tau))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
as
m\to\infty
by Lemma
3.9
, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(m)}(\tau))-f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau)),\xi(\tau)\rangle d\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle m\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

and the result is proven.
∎

Analogously, we can show the following:

Lemma 3.11
.

\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau)),v_{t}(\tau)\rangle d\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau)),v_{t}(\tau)\rangle d\tau,

and

\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{s}(\tau,v(\tau)),v^{(k)}_{t}(\tau)\rangle d\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle k\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{s}(\tau,v(\tau)),v_{t}(\tau)\rangle d\tau.

Using these two lemmas, we obtain:

Lemma 3.12
.

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau)),v_{t}^{(m)}(\tau)\rangle d\tau

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(m)}(\tau)),v_{t}^{(k)}(\tau)\rangle d\tau

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}F_{t-nT}(T,v(T))dx-e^{-\varepsilon_{0}T}\int_{\Omega}F_{t-nT}(0,v(0))dx

\displaystyle\qquad-\varepsilon_{0}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}F_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))d\tau dx-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}F_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))d\tau dx.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau)),v_{t}^{(m)}(\tau)\rangle d\tau

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(m)}(\tau)),v_{t}^{(k)}(\tau)\rangle d\tau=\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau)),v_{t}(\tau)\rangle d\tau.

Since for
\tau\in[0,T]
we have

\int_{\Omega}|e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))v_{t}(\tau)|dx\leqslant\|f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\delta(t-nT)<\infty,

for some
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
, we can apply Fubini’s Theorem to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))v_{t}(\tau)dxd\tau=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))v_{t}(\tau)d\tau dx.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\tau}\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}F_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))\right]

\displaystyle=e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}F_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}F_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))v_{t}(\tau),

which, integrating from
0
to
T
, implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}f_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))v_{t}(\tau)d\tau=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{d}{d\tau}[e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}F_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))]d\tau

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon_{0}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}F_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))d\tau-\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}F_{t-nT}(\tau,v(\tau))d\tau.

Now the conclusion follows immediately.
∎

Thus, we obtain:

Corollary 3.13
.

We have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\varepsilon_{0}(\tau-T)}\langle f_{t-nT}(\tau,v^{(k)}(\tau))-f_{s}(\tau,v^{(m)}(\tau)),v_{t}^{(n)}(\tau)-v_{t}^{(m)}(\tau)\rangle d\tau=0.

We can finally prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

With these previous results we conclude the contractiveness of
\psi_{n}
in
C_{t-nT}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying similar changes to the proof of the results presented in
[
1
]
, we can prove that
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
are precompact on
C_{t-nT}
.

Therefore, Theorem
2.5
ensures the existence of a generalized exponential
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
-pullback attractor
\hat{M}
for the evolution process
S
associated with (
NWE
), with
\hat{M}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
. Furthermore, from Proposition
2.3
, it follows that
S
has a
\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}^{\ast}}
-pullback attractor with
\hat{A}\subset\hat{M}
.
∎

Appendix A
Auxiliary results

In this brief appendix, we present two results regarding properties of
\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
.

Lemma A.1
.

If
\eta>0
and
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
, then the map

\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\mu(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta(t-u)e^{-\eta u}du

is also in
\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. Since
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
, given
\alpha>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such that for all
\tau\geqslant\tau_{0}
and
\xi\leqslant t
we have

\delta(\xi-\tau)e^{-\alpha\tau}<\eta\epsilon.

Thus for
s\leqslant t

\mu(s-\tau)e^{-\alpha\tau}=e^{-\alpha\tau}\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta(s-\tau-u)e^{-\eta u}du=\int_{0}^{\infty}\underbrace{\delta(s-u-\tau)e^{-\alpha\tau}}_{<\eta\epsilon}e^{-\eta u}du<\eta\epsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\eta u}du=\epsilon,

which implies that

\limsup_{\tau\to\infty}\sup_{s\leqslant t}\mu(s-\tau)e^{-\alpha\tau}\leqslant\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\mu\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

If
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
and
T>0
then the map
\mu\colon\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
, defined by

\mu(s)=\sup_{\ell\in[0,T]}\delta(\ell+s)\hbox{ for }s\in\mathbb{R},

is in
\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Since
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
, given
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha>0
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\tau_{0}\geqslant 0
such that
\delta(s-\tau)e^{-\alpha\tau}<\epsilon
for all
\tau\geqslant\tau_{0}
and
s\leqslant t+T
. Hence if
\ell\in[0,T]
and
s\leqslant t
we have
\ell+s\leqslant t+T
and

\delta(\ell+s-\tau)e^{-\alpha\tau}<\epsilon,

which implies that

\mu(s-\tau)e^{-\alpha\tau}\leqslant\epsilon\hbox{ for all }\tau\geqslant\tau_{0}\hbox{ and }s\leqslant t.

Thus
\mu\in\mathcal{C}^{\ast}
as claimed.
∎

An a posteriori error estimate for a 0D/2D coupled model Thanks:
Hussein Albazzal, Alexei Lozinski, Roberta Tittarelli

Abstract
This work is motivated by the need of efficient numerical simulations of gas flows in the serpentine channels used in proton-exchange membrane fuel cells. In particular, we consider the Poisson problem in a 2D domain composed of several long straight rectangular sections and of several bends corners. In order to speed up the resolution, we propose a 0D model in the rectangular parts of the channel and a Finite Element resolution in the bends. To find a good compromise between precision and time consuming, the challenge is double: how to choose a suitable position of the interface between the 0D and the 2D models and how to control the discretization error in the bends. We shall present an a posteriori error estimator based on an equilibrated flux reconstruction in the subdomains where the Finite Element method is applied. The estimates give a global upper bound on the error measured in the energy norm of the difference between the exact and approximate solutions on the whole domain. They are guaranteed, meaning that they feature no undetermined constants. (global) Lower bounds for the error are also derived. An adaptive algorithm is proposed to use smartly the estimator for aforementioned double challenge. A numerical validation of the estimator and the algorithm completes the work.

Introduction

The present work is motivated by models of the serpentine cathode-anode flow channels in Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cells (PEMFC), cf.
[
9
]
.
One of the computationally intensive tasks in this modeling is to solve the steady-state incompressible Navier-Stokes equations describing the gas flow in the long channels, characterised by very stretched rectangular regions linked by relatively small bends, cf.
fig.

1
.
Typically, one prescribes the Poiseuille flow as boundary conditions on the inlet and outlet boundaries of the channel, and no-slip boundary condition on the wall, cf. for example
[
15
]
.
In order to speed up the computations, the idea is to develop a coupled model as follows (here, in the 2D setting):
in the rectangular regions of the domain, the flow is approximated by simple analytical solutions, namely the Poiseuille flow which is very accurate sufficiently far from the bends (we call this 0D model), while keeping the original governing equations (the 2D model) in the bend regions. We refer to this as the 0D/2D model.
There exists different ways to derive a coupled model.
In
[
12
,
5
,
4
]
, 0D/3D coupling is obtained for the time dependent Navier-Stokes system by integrating the governing equations on a section and substituting an appropriate closure approximation.
In
[
6
]
and in
[
10
]
an asymptotic analysis is used to get respectively the 1D/3D and 1D/2D coupled simplified models for time dependent Navier-Stokes equations.

Figure 1
:
Gaz channels in a flow plate of a fuel cell
[
16
]
.

In this paper, we consider a simple toy model, replacing the steady Navier-Stokes system by the Poisson equation, to test the basic ideas of the 0D/2D model coupling.
A 0D/2D coupled model for Poisson equation is derived in
[
13
]
and
[
11
]
by the asymptotic analysis. In the stretched rectangular portion of the domain
\Omega
, which we call in this paper
\Omega^{\prime}
and refer to as the 0D domain, cf. Fig.
2
, the solution is approximated by a simple explicit expression (the analogue of the Poiseuille velocity). In the remaining part of the domain, denoted by
\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
and referred to as the 2D domain, we approximate the solution of the original Poisson equation (the 2D model) using the Finite Element (FE) method.
The 0D and 2D domains are separated by an interface
\Gamma
.
We use a simple coupling condition on the interface
\Gamma
: we impose the continuity of the coupled solution on
\Gamma
.
The error between the “original 2D model” and the “0D/2D coupled discretized model” comes thus from two sources: the FE discretization in
\widetilde{\Omega}
and the coupling error provoked by the simple approximation in
\Omega^{\prime}
and controlled by the position of the interface
\Gamma
.

In the present paper, we propose an
a posteriori
error estimator for the 0D/2D coupled model which can be interpreted as a sum of two contributions: the first one measuring the error due to the simplification introduced by the 0D domain
\Omega^{\prime}
and the second one measuring the discretization error in the 2D domain
\widetilde{\Omega}
. Equilibrating the two contributions, under a given tolerance, enables us to determine the position of the interface
\Gamma
, reducing the size of the 2D domain
\widetilde{\Omega}
to be discretized, and to construct an optimized mesh on
\widetilde{\Omega}
.
Our error estimator is based on the flux reconstruction technique as in
[
3
,
2
]
. The originality of our work consists in a new flux reconstruction
\sigma_{h}
, which is defined on the whole domain
\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Gamma\cup\widetilde{\Omega}
, i.e. both on the discretized (2D) and the non-discretized (0D) regions.

The article is organized as follows. In the next section, we introduce the governing equations, the geometry of the domain, and advocate for coupled simplified model.
Our
a posteriori
error is presented in
section

3
. In
section

4
, we prove the global upper and lower bounds for the error with respect to the estimator, called respectively the global reliability and efficiency of the estimator. The reliability is guaranteed, i.e. the upper bound does not contain any unknown constants. Its proof is quite straightforward. The main technical hurdle is in the proof of the global efficiency of the estimator. Finally, we propose in
section

5
an algorithm that uses our estimator both to choose the interface position and to make the local mesh refinement. The section is concluded by numerical tests. In
section

6
we provide conclusions and forthcoming works.

3
A posteriori
error estimator

In this section, we introduce an
a posteriori
error estimator with guaranteed reliability and provable efficiency for the error defined by

e=\left\|\nabla u-\nabla u_{h}^{c}\right\|_{\Omega}\,

(9)

in the spirit of
[
3
]
.
To this end, we should define a reconstructed flux
\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}\in H(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
such that
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}=f
in
\Omega
and such that its construction be local. This is done below, cf. (
15
), as the sum of local contributions from the patches around the mesh nodes and a patch attached to the interface
\Gamma
. Then, the error estimator will be defined as

\eta=\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}+\nabla u_{h}^{c}\right\|\,.

(10)

Let
\mathcal{V}_{h}
and
\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\Gamma}
be the sets respectively of all the nodes of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\widetilde{\Omega}
and of all the nodes lying on the interface
\Gamma
.
Let
\psi^{\Gamma}
be the continuous function on
\Omega
defined on rectangular portion
[0,x_{\Gamma}+R]\times[0,R]
of
\Omega
, supposing that
x_{\Gamma}+R\leq L
, by

\psi^{\Gamma}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\text{for }x<x_{\Gamma}\,,0\leq y\leq R\,,\\
\frac{x_{\Gamma}+R-x}{R},&\text{for }x\in[x_{\Gamma},x_{\Gamma}+R]\,,0\leq y\leq R\,,\\
0,&\text{for }x>x_{\Gamma}+R\,,0\leq y\leq R\,,\end{array}\right.

and extended by 0 everywhere else.
We also introduce a piecewise affine on mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
version of
\psi^{\Gamma}
, defining

\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}}\psi^{\Gamma}(\mathrm{a})\psi^{\mathrm{a}}(x,y)&\text{ if }(x,y)\in\widetilde{\Omega}\cup\Gamma\,,\\
1&\text{ if }(x,y)\in\Omega^{\prime}\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\psi^{\mathrm{a}}
is the hat function associated to node
\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, i.e. the piecewise
\mathbb{P}_{1}
function taking the value 1 at
\mathrm{a}
and vanishing at all the other mesh nodes.
It follows that we have the partition of unity on
\Omega

1=\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}+\sum_{\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\Gamma}}(1-\psi^{\Gamma}(\mathrm{a}))\psi^{\mathrm{a}}\,.

(11)

Remark 1
.

If
x_{\Gamma}+R>L
, then the function
\psi^{\Gamma}
defined above becomes discontinuous and this prevents us from proving the optimality estimate (
21
) in the manner of Theorem
5
. However,
\psi_{h}^{\Gamma}
remains continuous even if
x_{\Gamma}+R>L

x_{\Gamma}+R\leq L
is not necessary for the implementation of our error estimator. The numerical results show that it remains robust even if this theoretical assumption is violated.

Let us define the patches
\omega^{\mathrm{a}}=\operatorname{supp}\psi^{\mathrm{a}}
for all the nodes
\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
and the patch attached to
\Gamma

\omega^{\Gamma}=\operatorname{supp}(\psi_{h}^{\Gamma})\cap\widetilde{\Omega}

We remind that

, we define

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{h}^{\mathrm{a}}=\{\mathbf{\tau}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathrm{div},\omega^{\mathrm{a}});{\mathbf{\tau}_{h}}_{|T}\in\mathbf{RT}_{k}(T)\ \forall T\in\omega^{\mathrm{a}},\mathbf{\tau}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\text{ on }\partial\omega^{\mathrm{a}}\}\,,\\

For the case where

Upper and lower bounds

Theorem 5
(Global reliability and efficiency).

Let
u
be the weak solution of (
3
),
u_{h}^{c}
be the solution (
8
) of the coupled 0D/2D problem,
e
and
\eta
be the error and the error estimator defined respectively by (
9
) and (
10
). Then

e\leq\eta

(20)

and, assuming
x_{\Gamma}+R\leq L
,

\eta\leq Ce

(21)

with a constant
C
depending only on the mesh regularity.

Proof.

The proof of the upper bound (
20
) is completely standard:
we set
e=u-u_{h}^{c}
, observe that
e\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
use the weak formulation (
3
),
proposition

4
and integration by parts (with the fact that
e=0
on
\partial\Omega
), to get

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla u-\nabla u_{h}^{c}\right\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u-\nabla u_{h}^{c})\cdot(\nabla u-\nabla u_{h}^{c})=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u-\nabla u_{h}^{c})\cdot\nabla e=\int_{\Omega}fe-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{h}^{c}\cdot\nabla e

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}e-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{h}^{c}\cdot\nabla e=\int_{\Omega}(-\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}-\nabla u_{h}^{c})\cdot\nabla e\leq\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}+\nabla u_{h}^{c}\right\|_{\Omega}\left\|\nabla e\right\|_{\Omega}\,,

where for the inequality we apply Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. Hence (
20
).

The proof of the lower bound (
21
) needs the following four steps. In this proof, we shall use the letter
C
for various constants that depend only on the mesh regularity (in particular, independent of the geometrical parameter
R
and of the polynomial degree
k
).

Step 1: error caused by the interface, prior to discretization

Let us begin with a “continuous” version of the coupled 0D/2D model:

\displaystyle-\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}\tilde{u}=f\text{ in }\widetilde{\Omega}\,,

(23a)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{\prime}&&\text{ on }\Gamma\,,\\
&0&&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{wall}}\,,\\
&u_{\mathrm{out}}&&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{out}}\,,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(23b)

and set

u^{c}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u^{\prime}\text{ on }\Omega^{\prime}\,,\\
\tilde{u},\text{ on }\widetilde{\Omega}\,.\end{array}\right.

We want to study
\left\|\nabla u-\nabla u^{c}\right\|_{\Omega}
which represents
the error introduced by the interface alone, without discretizing the problem
in
\widetilde{\Omega}
.
We also introduce the continuous version of
\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}
:

\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}\in H_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{div},\omega^{\Gamma})
,
p^{\Gamma}\in L^{2}(\omega^{\Gamma})
with
H_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{div},\omega^{\Gamma})=\{\tau\in H(\mathrm{div},\omega^{\Gamma}):\tau\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\text{ on }\Gamma\}
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}\cdot\tau^{\Gamma}+\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}p^{\Gamma}\mathrm{div}\tau^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\tau^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\forall\tau^{\Gamma}\in H_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{div},\omega^{\Gamma})\,,

(24a)

\displaystyle\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}q^{\Gamma}\mathrm{div}\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}fq^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\forall q^{\Gamma}\in L^{2}(\omega^{\Gamma})\,.

(24b)

Let us prove

\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}+\nabla\tilde{u}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\leq C\left\|\nabla u-\nabla u^{c}\right\|_{\Omega}\,.

(25)

Let
\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}=\operatorname{supp}(\psi^{\Gamma})\cap\widetilde{\Omega}
and

\Gamma_{R}=\partial\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}\cap\{x=x_{\Gamma}+R\}
. In Lemma

6
we show that there exists
\theta\in H^{1}(\omega_{R}^{\Gamma})

such that

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}\theta

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\omega^{\Gamma}_{R}\,,

(26)

\displaystyle\nabla\theta\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=-\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma\,,

\displaystyle\nabla\theta\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma_{R}\,,

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{on }\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{wall}}\cap\partial\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}\,,

and
\left\|\nabla\theta\right\|_{\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}}\leq C_{1}\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\|_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\Gamma}
, with
C_{1}
independent of
R
. Let

\tau^{c}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nabla\theta&&\text{in }\omega^{\Gamma}_{R}\,,\\
&0&&\text{on }\omega^{\Gamma}\setminus\omega^{\Gamma}_{R}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

By construction,
\mathrm{div}\tau^{c}=0
in
\omega^{\Gamma}
. This implies

\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}+\nabla\tilde{u}+\tau^{c})=0\quad\text{in }\omega^{\Gamma}\,

thanks to (
24b
), and

(\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}+\nabla\tilde{u}+\tau^{c})\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma

(27)

since
\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
and
\tau^{c}\cdot\mathbf{n}=-\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}
by (
26
) on
\Gamma
. This
allows us to take
\tau^{\Gamma}=\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}+\nabla\tilde{u}+\tau^{c}
as a test function in (
24a
). Hence, by Cauchy-Schwartz,

\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}+\nabla\tilde{u}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\leq\left\|\tau^{c}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\leq C_{1}\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\|_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\Gamma}\,.

(28)

Coming back to the error, denoting by
[\cdot]
the jump on
\Gamma
, we have that for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u-u^{c})\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\Gamma}[\nabla(u-u^{c})\cdot\mathbf{n}]v=\int_{\Gamma}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}v\,,

where we have split the domain
\Omega
in
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\Omega}
, integrated by parts, using
\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}(u-u^{c})=0
on
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{\Omega}
,
v\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
, and
\nabla u^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
on
\Gamma
.
Now, from
lemma

7
we know that for each
w\in H^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma)
there exists
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

v_{|\Gamma}=w
and such that
\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{\Omega}\leq C_{2}\left\|w\right\|_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\Gamma}
; with
C_{2}
independent of geometrical parameters. We can take
v=\theta
in
\omega^{\Gamma}_{R}
, with
\theta
the solution of
lemma

7
and extend it to all the domain taking
e.g.

v
the mirror of
\theta
in
\Omega^{\prime}
.
Thus

\int_{\Gamma}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}v=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u-u^{c})\cdot\nabla v\leq\left\|\nabla(u-u^{c})\right\|_{\Omega}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{\Omega}\leq C_{2}\left\|w\right\|_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\Gamma}\left\|\nabla(u-u^{c})\right\|_{\Omega}

that implies

\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\mathbf{n}\right\|_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\Gamma}\leq C_{2}\left\|\nabla(u-u^{c})\right\|_{\Omega}\,.

Coming back to (
28
) and using this latter inequality, we get the desired estimate (
25
) with
C=C_{1}C_{2}
.

Step 2: error caused by the interface, adding the discretization in
\widetilde{\Omega}

Here we prove that

\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\leq C(\left\|\nabla(\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{h})\right\|_{\omega_{\Gamma}}+\left\|\nabla(u-u^{c})\right\|_{\Omega})\,.

(29)

This can be viewed as a discrete analogue of (
25
).
The proof is based on Theorem 1.2 of
[
2
]
, but the latter cannot be applied directly due to a mismatch in boundary conditions:
\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}
is required to vanish on both
\Gamma
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}
, while
\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}
vanishes only on
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.
To circumvent this difficulty, we enlarge
\omega^{\Gamma}
to
\omega^{\Gamma,m}
by adding to
\omega^{\Gamma}
its
mirror image with respect to
\Gamma
.
Similarly, we extend
\psi^{\Gamma}_{h},\tilde{u}_{h},\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}
from
\omega^{\Gamma}
to
\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h},\tilde{u}_{h}^{m},\sigma_{h}^{\Gamma,m}
on
\omega^{\Gamma,m}
as functions symmetric with respect to
\Gamma
.
Let
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{m}
denote the mirror image of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
.
Theorem 1.2 of
[
2
]
can be formulated
on
\omega^{\Gamma,m}
as

\hskip-22.76219pt\min_{\scriptsize{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{v}_{h}\in H(\operatorname{div},\omega^{\Gamma,m})\cap\operatorname{RT}_{k}\\
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}_{h}=\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h}f-\nabla\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}^{m}\\
\mathbf{v}_{h}=0\operatorname{on}\tilde{\Gamma}\cup\widetilde{\Gamma}^{m}\end{array}}}\hskip-8.53581pt\left\|\mathbf{v}_{h}+\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h}\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma,m}}\leqslant C(\omega^{\Gamma,m},\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h})\min_{\scriptsize{\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{v}\in H(\operatorname{div},\omega^{\Gamma,m})\\
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{v}=f\end{array}}}\left\|\mathbf{v}+\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}^{m}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma,m}}

(30)

where
C(\omega^{\Gamma,m},\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h})\leq C(\|\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h}\|_{\infty}+C_{P}\|\nabla\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h}\|_{\infty})
with
C_{P}
the
Poincaré constant of the space
H^{1}(\omega^{\Gamma,m})
under the
constraint of functions vanishing on
\widetilde{\Gamma}\cup\widetilde{\Gamma}^{m}
,
i.e.
\|v\|_{\omega^{\Gamma,m}}\leq C_{P}\|\nabla v\|_{\omega^{\Gamma,m}}

for all
v\in H^{1}(\omega^{\Gamma,m})
with
v=0
on
\widetilde{\Gamma}\cup\widetilde{\Gamma}^{m}
(note that these constraints are imposed on the part of

\partial\omega^{\Gamma,m}
where
\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h}
vanishes). By our
geometrical assumptions,
C_{P}
is of order
R
and
\|\nabla\psi_{h}^{\Gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{C}{R}
so that
C(\omega^{\Gamma,m},\psi^{\Gamma,m}_{h})\leq C
. Comparing with the definition of
\sigma_{h}^{\Gamma}
we see that
the minimum on the left-hand side of (
30
) is attained on

\sigma_{h}^{\Gamma,m}
(note in particular that
\sigma_{h}^{\Gamma,m}\cdot n=0
on
\Gamma
by symmetry). In order to identify the minimum on the other
side, we introduce
\mathbf{\hat{\sigma}}^{\Gamma}\in H_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{div},\omega^{\Gamma})
,

\hat{p}^{\Gamma}\in L^{2}(\omega^{\Gamma})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\mathbf{\hat{\sigma}}^{\Gamma}\cdot\tau^{\Gamma}+\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\hat{p}^{\Gamma}\mathrm{div}\tau^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\cdot\tau^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\forall\tau^{\Gamma}\in H_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{div},\omega^{\Gamma})\,,

(31a)

\displaystyle\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}q^{\Gamma}\mathrm{div}\mathbf{\hat{\sigma}}^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega^{\Gamma}}fq^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\forall q^{\Gamma}\in L^{2}(\omega^{\Gamma})\,.

(31b)

We see then that the minium on the right-hand side of (
30
) is
attained on
\hat{\sigma}^{\Gamma,m}
, which is the mirror extension of

\mathbf{\hat{\sigma}}^{\Gamma}
to
\omega^{\Gamma,m}
. Going back in (
30
) to the
subdomain
\omega^{\Gamma}
of
\omega^{\Gamma,m}
and using the symmetry
gives

\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\leqslant C\left\|\mathbf{\hat{\sigma}}^{\Gamma}+\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\,.

This entails by the triangle inequality

\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\leq C(\left\|\mathbf{\hat{\sigma}}^{\Gamma}-\sigma^{\Gamma}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}+\left\|\sigma^{\Gamma}+\nabla\tilde{u}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}+\left\|-\nabla\tilde{u}+\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}).

(32)

To bound the first term in (

), we take the difference
between (
24
) and (
31
) setting
\tau^{\Gamma}=\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}-\mathbf{\hat{\sigma}}^{\Gamma}
. Noting that
\mathrm{div}\tau^{c}=0
, this yields

\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}^{\Gamma}-\mathbf{\hat{\sigma}}^{\Gamma}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\leq\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\,.

(33)

The second term in (
32
) is bounded by (
25
).
Finally, (
32
) gives (
29
).

Step 3: discretization error inside
\widetilde{\Omega}

We have at all the nodes
\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\Gamma}

\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\mathrm{a}}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\mathrm{a}}\right\|_{\omega^{\mathrm{a}}}\leq C\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\mathrm{a}}}\,.

(34)

This well known estimate follows, for example, from Theorem 1.2 of
[
2
]
applied on each patch

.

Step 4: putting everything together

From definition of
\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}
, see (
15
), (
16
) (
17
), and the partition of unity (
11
) we obtain on every mesh element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

\left\|\mathbf{\tilde{\sigma}}_{h}+\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{T}\leq\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}\right\|_{T}+\sum_{\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\Gamma}}(1-\psi^{\Gamma}(\mathrm{a}))\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\mathrm{a}}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\mathrm{a}}\right\|_{T}

where
\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}
and
\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\mathrm{a}}
are extended by 0 outside of their domains of definitions
\omega^{\Gamma}
and
\omega^{\mathrm{a}}
respectively. The number of non-zero terms in the sum above is thus uniformly bounded by a constant that depends only on the regularity of the mesh. Taking the squares on both sides of the inequality above leads to

\left\|\mathbf{\tilde{\sigma}}_{h}+\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{T}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}\right\|_{T}^{2}+\sum_{\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\mathrm{a}}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\mathrm{a}}\right\|_{T}^{2}\right)\,.

Taking the sum over
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, noting
\mathbf{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}}+\nabla u^{\prime}=0
on
\Omega^{\prime}
, and then using the bounds (
29
), (
34
) leads to

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}+\nabla u_{h}^{c}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\sum_{\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\mathrm{a}}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\mathrm{a}}\right\|_{\omega^{\mathrm{a}}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\nabla(u-u^{c})\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\sum_{\mathrm{a}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\Gamma}}\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\mathrm{a}}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\nabla(u-u^{c})\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{\widetilde{\Omega}}^{2}\right)

since the number of possible overlaps between the different patches
\omega^{\Gamma}
and
\omega^{\mathrm{a}}
is uniformly bounded.

By integration by parts,

\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(\nabla u-\nabla\tilde{u})\cdot(\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})=-\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}(\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}u-\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}\tilde{u})(\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{h})+\int_{\partial\widetilde{\Omega}}(\nabla u-\nabla\tilde{u})\cdot\mathbf{n}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{h})=0\,.

Hence

\left\|\nabla(u-u_{h}^{c})\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}=\left\|\nabla(u-u^{\prime})\right\|_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla(u-\tilde{u})+\nabla(\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{h})\right\|_{\widetilde{\Omega}}^{2}=\left\|\nabla(u-u^{c})\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{\widetilde{\Omega}}^{2}\,,

so that

\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{c}+\nabla u_{h}^{c}\right\|_{\Omega}^{2}\leq C\left\|\nabla(u-u_{h}^{c})\right\|_{\Omega}\,,

i.e. (
21
).

5.2
Adaptive algorithm

We now inspire ourselves from the construction of the estimator (
10
) to detect a suitable interface position
x_{\Gamma}
. Guided by the proof of the upper and lower bounds for the error (Steps 2 and 3 of the proof of Theorem
5

\eta^{\Gamma}\coloneqq\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\Gamma}+(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})\psi^{\Gamma}_{h}\right\|_{\omega^{\Gamma}}\,,

(35)

can indicate the modelling error, caused by the replacement of the 2D model by the 0D one, and can thus drive the choice of the interface position. A theoretical motivation for this conjecture stems from the estimate (

\eta^{\Gamma}
is bounded there by two terms, the second one being precisely the part of the error caused by the introduction of the coupling interface. This contribution is global. The first contribution in (

\omega^{\Gamma}

A numerical evidence for the pertinence of
\eta^{\Gamma}
is provided in

vs. the interface position (using quasi-uniform meshes of the same size), and
\eta^{\Gamma}
vs. the mesh size, once the interface position has been fixed. We observe that
\eta^{\Gamma}
is indeed almost independent of the mesh refinement, and the range of interface positions where it grows abruptly coincides with the similar region for the actual error, cf.
figs.

4
and
5
. We conclude that
\eta^{\Gamma}
can be used to detect the part of the error caused by the interface position.

6
Conclusions

In this paper we have proposed a coupled approach to solve a 2D Poisson problem in a domain with long channels, splitting the domain into a portion where the solution is approximated by a simple analytical expression and another one where it is approximated numerically.
We have developed an
a posteriori
error estimator that, for a given tolerance on the error, allows us both to choose the position of the coupling interface and to adapt the mesh. Our theoretical results, namely that the estimator is guaranteed and efficient, are confirmed and enhanced by numerical experiments on automatically adapted meshes. Some partial results for the Stokes equation in a similar setting are available in
[
1
]
. We hope to adapt this approach to more complex governing equations in a future work.

Appendix A
Some technical Lemmas

We recall here two well-known lemmas, needed for the proof of
theorem

5
, and give their proofs for completeness of exposition.
Let
H_{00}^{s}(\Gamma)
, with
s\in\{-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\}
be the spaces of functions (distributions) on
\Gamma
of the form

\eta=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\eta_{k}\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}y\right)\,,

(37)

with the norm
\left\|\eta\right\|_{s,\Gamma}=\left(\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{R}{2}\eta_{k}^{2}\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}\right)^{2s}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
.

Lemma 6
.

For any
\eta\in H_{00}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma)
, let
\theta
be the solution to

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}\theta

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ in }\omega^{\Gamma}_{R}\,,

\displaystyle\nabla\theta\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=\eta

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma\,,

\displaystyle\nabla\theta\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{R}\,,

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ on }\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{wall}}\cap\partial\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}\,.

Then

\left\|\nabla\theta\right\|_{\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}}\leqslant C_{1}\left\|\eta\right\|_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\Gamma}

with
C_{1}>0
which does not depend on
R
.

Proof.

For any
\eta\in H_{00}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma)
written as (
37
), by direct calculation,
\theta
is given by

\theta=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\eta_{k}\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}y\right)\frac{\cosh\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}(x-x_{\Gamma}-R)\right)}{\frac{k\pi}{R}\sinh(k\pi)}\,.

Thus, denoting
x_{\Gamma}^{R}=x_{\Gamma}+R
,

\left\|\nabla\theta\right\|_{\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}}^{2}=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\left(\frac{\eta_{k}}{\sinh(k\pi)}\right)^{2}\times\\
\left(\left\|\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}y\right)\sinh\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}(x-x_{\Gamma}-R)\right)\right\|_{\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}}^{2}+\left\|\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}y\right)\cosh\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}(x-x_{\Gamma}^{R})\right)\right\|_{\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}}^{2}\right)\\
=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\eta_{k}^{2}\frac{R^{2}}{2k\pi\tanh(k\pi)}\leqslant\frac{1}{\tanh(\pi)}\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\eta_{k}^{2}\frac{R^{2}}{2k\pi}\coloneqq C_{1}^{2}\|\eta\|_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\Gamma}^{2}\,,

since
\tanh(k\pi)\geqslant\tanh(\pi)
for
k\geq 1
.

Lemma 7
.

For any
\eta\in H_{00}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma)
, let
\theta
be the solution to

\displaystyle\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}\theta=0\text{ in }\omega^{\Gamma}_{R}\,,

\displaystyle\theta=\eta\text{ on }\Gamma\,,

\displaystyle\theta=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{R}\cup(\widetilde{\Gamma}_{\mathrm{wall}}\cap\partial\omega_{R}^{\Gamma})\,.

Then

\left\|\nabla\theta\right\|_{\omega_{R}^{\Gamma}}\leqslant C_{2}\left\|\eta\right\|_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},\Gamma}
with
C_{2}>0

which does not depend on
R
.

Proof.

For any
\eta\in H_{00}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(\Gamma)
written as (
37
), by direct calculation the solution
\theta
is given by

\theta=\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\eta_{k}\sin\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}y\right)\frac{\sinh\left(\frac{k\pi}{R}(x_{\Gamma}+R-x)\right)}{\sinh(k\pi)}\,.

Calculations similar to those in the proof of Lemma
6
lead to the desired result with
C_{2}=C_{1}
of the previous lemma.

Appendix B
Mesh adaptation in FreeFEM

The traditional strategy for the mesh adaptation (see, for example,
[
14
, Chapter 2]
for a review) consists in marking certain mesh cells to refine according to the error indicator, and then splitting them in smaller cells. The library FreeFEM, used in our numerical experiments, does not provide tools for such a procedure: one cannot ask it to split only some specific cells, without touching the others. FreeFEM proposes instead the function
adaptmesh

that creates an entirely new mesh with the mesh size prescribed (approximately) at every point of the entire computation domain (it is also possible to generate anisotropic meshes, but we deal here only with the isotropic version, option
IsMetric=1
, where all the mesh cells are close to equilateral triangles).

We describe here an alternative mesh adaptation strategy from
[
7
]
, which we name “hopt” (for “
h
optimal”). We start from the following (admittedly not always realistic) assumption: the error between the exact solution
\tilde{u}
and the approximate solution
\tilde{u}_{h}
on
any
mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of
\widetilde{\Omega}
is approximately given by

\left\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\widetilde{\Omega}}^{2}\approx\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}{h}^{2\delta}(x)c^{2}(x)\operatorname{dx}

(38)

where
h(x)
is the mesh size distribution, i.e.
h(x)=h_{T}
on any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
\delta
is a fixed parameter, and
c(x)
is some
a priori
unknown function.
Note also that the number of DoF is approximately given in the 2D case by

N_{\operatorname{DoF}}\sim\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\frac{\operatorname{dx}}{h^{2}(x)}\,,

since a regular triangle of diameter
h
occupies the area of order
h^{2}
.
Let us imagine momentarily that we know
c(x)
and we want to construct an optimal mesh with the minimal possible
N_{\operatorname{DoF}}
to achieve a given error
\epsilon
, i.e.
\left\|u-\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\widetilde{\Omega}}=\epsilon
. This is a
constrained minimization problem for the mesh size distribution
h(x)
, i.e. minimize
\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\frac{\operatorname{dx}}{h^{2}(x)}
under

\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}{h}^{2\delta}(x)c^{2}(x)\operatorname{dx}=\epsilon^{2}
, which gives the following optimal mesh size distribution

h_{\operatorname{opt}}(x)=\frac{\epsilon^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\delta}}}}{\left(\int_{\tilde{\Omega}}c^{2/(\delta+1)}(x)\operatorname{dx}\right)^{{}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{(2\delta)}}}}}\frac{1}{(c(x))^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{(\delta+1)}}}}\,.

Of course,
c(x)
is not known in practice, but, on a given mesh
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
,
we have the estimators
\eta^{\mathrm{D}}_{T}
, see (
36
). It seems thus reasonable to expect that

\left\|\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{1,\widetilde{\Omega}}^{2}\approx(\eta^{\mathrm{D}})^{2}:=\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}(\eta_{T}^{\mathrm{D}})^{2}=\sum_{T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\left\|\mathbf{\sigma}_{h}^{\mathrm{D}}+\nabla\tilde{u}_{h}\right\|_{T}^{2}\,.

Reinterpreting the error in the form
eq.

38
, and localizing to each triangle of the current mesh
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, suggests

\int_{T}h^{2\delta}(x)c^{2}(x)\operatorname{dx}\sim(\eta_{T}^{\mathrm{D}})^{2},\quad\forall T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}\,.

We can thus approximate
c(x)
on any triangle
T\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
by

c(x)\approx\frac{\eta_{T}^{\mathrm{D}}}{h_{T}^{\delta}\sqrt{|T|}}
, which gives

h_{\operatorname{opt}}(x)=\frac{\epsilon^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\delta}}}}{\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\eta_{T}^{\mathrm{D}})^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{(\delta+1)}}}h_{T}^{\nicefrac{{-2\delta}}{{(\delta+1)}}}|T|^{\nicefrac{{\delta}}{{(\delta+1)}}}\right)^{{}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{(2\delta)}}}}}\frac{h_{T}^{\nicefrac{{\delta}}{{(\delta+1)}}}|T|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{(2\delta+2)}}}}{(\eta_{T}^{\mathrm{D}})^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{(\delta+1)}}}}\quad\operatorname{for}x\in T\,.

(39)

In practice, given a mesh
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}_{h}}
, we want to reduce the error
R_{e}
times (with
R_{e}>1
a parameter to be specified). We thus put
\epsilon=\eta^{D}/R_{e}
in (
39
) and give the resulting
h_{\operatorname{opt}}
mesh size distribution to the mesh-generating function of FreeFEM. This is the procedure we have used for mesh adaptation in
algorithm

1
. There are two parameters (
\delta
and
R_{e}
) to be specified. Based on some numerical experimentation (not reported here, but given in
[
1
]
) we have chosen
\delta=1
and
R_{e}=4
.

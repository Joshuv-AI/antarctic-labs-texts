Index-like Theorem for Massless Fermions in Spherically Symmetric Monopole Backgrounds
T. Daniel Brennan

Abstract
In this paper we study massless fermions coupled to spherically symmetric S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) monopoles without Yukawa couplings between the Higgs and fermion fields. The corresponding Dirac operator is not Fredholm and the associated eigenfunctions are not L 2 L^{2} -normalizable. Here we derive a formula for the dimension of the plane-wave normalizable kernel of such a Dirac operator for fermions of any representation of S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) in the presence of any spherically symmetric monopole background. Notably, our results also apply to fermions coupled to monopoles that preserve non-abelian gauge symmetry.

Introduction and Summary

In this paper we are concerned with interactions of monopoles with fermions in 4D
SU(N)
gauge theories. An important feature of these interactions is the spectrum of fermion zero-modes. Fermions admit
L^{2}
-normalizable zero-modes when the spectrum of eigenfunctions of the Dirac operator has an energy gap which typically comes from either a mass term or from coupling to the Higgs field via a Yukawa interaction.
This energy gap implies that the Dirac operator is Fredholm and the number of fermion zero-modes is given by its index
Callias:1977kg
.
Let us denote this energy gap
E_{\rm gap}
.
In the case of
E_{\rm gap}>0
, the fermion zero-modes decay exponentially away from the monopole as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi(r,\theta,\phi)\sim\frac{1}{r}e^{-|E_{\rm gap}|r}\psi_{0}(\theta,\phi)~,\end{split}

(1)

confining the zero-modes to the world volume of the monopole.
The low energy dynamics of the fermions then reduces to the quantum mechanics of these zero-modes which can impart the monopole with charges under global symmetries and can affect monopole-monopole interactions
Jackiw:1975fn
;
Gauntlett:1993sh
;
Sethi:1995zm
;
Gauntlett:1999vc
;
Gauntlett:2000ks
;
Brennan:2016znk
;
Brennan:2018ura
.

Here, we would like to study the limit
E_{\rm gap}\to 0
arising from sending the strength of the Yukawa interaction to zero. In this case, generically some collection of
zero-modes become non-
L^{2}
-normalizable.
In sending
E_{\rm gap}\to 0
, these zero-modes become no longer localized on the monopole world volume, but rather describe free propagating fields that can tunnel into the core of the monopole and interact with the non-abelian gauge degrees of freedom that are trapped there. This is known as the Callan-Rubakov effect and results in monopoles that can catalyze proton decay in grand unified theories such as the
SU(5)
Georgi-Glashow model
Georgi:1974sy
;
Callan:1982ah
;
Callan:1982au
;
Callan:1982ac
;
Rubakov:1982fp
.

In this paper, we will be concerned with the case where all zero-modes become non-
L^{2}
-normalizable from decoupling the fermions frmo the Higgs field.
In this situation, where
E_{\rm gap}\to 0
, the zero-energy solutions belong to a continuous spectrum of spherical scattering states that goes down to zero-energy. These scattering solutions
fall into families based on the zero-energy solutions
of the Dirac equation.
Thus, the mathematically relevant question is to determine the dimension of the kernel of the Dirac operator when restricted to solutions that are “plane-wave normalizable”:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}r\psi<\infty~,\end{split}

(2)

and smooth.

The problem of understanding the spectrum of non-Fredholm Dirac operators is also closely related to the phenomenon of wall crossing in supersymmetric gauge theories. In 4D
{\cal N}=2
gauge theories, the stability of BPS states can be determined by solving the Dirac equation on monopole moduli space (
{\cal M}
) coupled to a Killing vector field
Gauntlett:1993sh
;
Gauntlett:1999vc
;
Gauntlett:2000ks
;
Brennan:2016znk
;
Brennan:2018ura
. Approaching a wall of marginal stability corresponds to shifting the Killing vector so that the Dirac operator on
{\cal M}
becomes non-Fredholm and the solutions to the Dirac equation are no longer
L^{2}
-normalizable. This is analogous to solving the Dirac equation in the presence of a monopole
with a Yukawa coupling
\lambda_{Yuk}
in the limit
\lambda_{Yuk}\to 0
(sends
E_{\rm gap}\to 0
). In fact, in the asymptotic limit of moduli space near a wall of marginal stability where the BPS states separate into two relatively unstable clusters,
the wall crossing is controlled by a 4D Dirac operator coupled to an abelian monopole that becomes non-Fredholm on the wall of marginal stability. In this case, extra data is needed to understand the regular solutions of the Dirac operator in the limit
r\to 0
.

Summary and Conclusion

In this paper, we derive a formula to enumerate the number of plane-wave normalizable zero-energy solutions of the Dirac equation in the presence of a spherically symmetric monopole.

The setting for this paper is four-dimensional
SU(N)
gauge theory with gauge field

We would like to consider the time-independent Dirac equation for the fermion

\displaystyle\begin{split}i\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi_{R}=0~,\end{split}

(3)

where
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+iR(A_{\mu})
and
\mu=1,2,3

in the presence of a spherically symmetric BPS monopole.

A (BPS) monopole is a Yang-Mills-Higgs configuration that satisfies the Bogomolny equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}B_{\mu}=D_{\mu}\Phi~,\end{split}

(4)

with the asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\vec{B}&=\frac{\gamma_{m}}{r^{2}}\hat{r}+O(1/r^{2+\delta})\quad,\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}\Phi=\Phi_{\infty}-\frac{\gamma_{m}}{r}+O(1/r^{1+\delta})\quad\delta>0~,\end{split}

(5)

where

A spherically symmetric monopole is a special class of monopole that is rotationally invariant under

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{i}\in\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{u}(N)\quad,\quad[I_{i},I_{j}]=i\epsilon_{ijk}I_{k}\quad,\quad i=1,2,3~,\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma_{m}=T_{3}-I_{3}\quad,\quad[\gamma_{m},I_{i}]=0~.\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\vec{T}}=\bigoplus_{i}{\vec{T}}^{(i)}~,\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\vec{I}}=\bigoplus_{i,a}{\vec{I}}^{\,(i,a)}\quad,\quad[{\vec{T}}^{(i)},{\vec{I}}^{\,(j,a)}]=0~,~i\neq j~,\end{split}

(9)

{\vec{I}}^{(i,a)}\neq{\vec{T}}^{(i)}

.
This data specifies the vacuum expectation value of the Higgs field

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi_{\infty}=\sum_{i}v_{i}\left(T_{3}^{(i)}-\sum_{a}I_{3}^{(i,a)}\right)+\phi_{\infty}~,\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta^{+}_{R}[T_{3},I_{3}]=\left\{\mu\in\Delta_{{R}}^{+}~\big|~\langle\mu,T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\langle w(\mu),T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0\right\}~,\end{split}

is the set of positive weights of

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm ker}_{R}[i\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}]=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Delta^{+}_{R}[T_{3},I_{3}]}\left[\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\right]^{\oplus n_{R}(\mu)}~,\end{split}

(14)

where
[j]

This work is directly applicable to understanding the Callan-Rubakov effect in which low energy, massless fermions scatter off of a monopole in a way that is sensitive to the UV physics inside the monopole.

\langle\mu_{a},T_{3}\rangle>\langle\mu_{a+1},T_{3}\rangle
and
\langle\mu_{a},\gamma_{m}\rangle\geq\langle\mu_{a+1},\gamma_{m}\rangle
. Then, if we decompose

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{R_{A}}=\sum_{a}\psi^{a}_{R_{A}}v_{\mu_{a}}\quad,\quad{R_{A}}(T_{3})\cdot v_{\mu_{a}}=q_{a}v_{\mu_{a}}\quad,\quad q_{a}=\langle\mu_{a},T_{3}\rangle~,\end{split}

(45)

we find that the eigenfunctions of
J^{2},J_{3}
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi^{a}_{R_{A}}&=\sum_{s=\pm}f_{s,a}(r){\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a},s}(\theta,\phi)\\
&=e^{i(m-q_{a})\phi}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a,+}(r)\,\,d^{j}_{-m,q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}}(\theta)\\
f_{a,-}(r)\,d^{j}_{-m,q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a,+}(r)\,e^{i\phi/2}\,{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
f_{a,-}(r)\,e^{-i\phi/2}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(46)

where

d^{j}_{m,q}(\theta)
are small Wigner
d
-functions and
{\cal D}^{(j)}_{m,q}(\theta,\phi):={\cal D}^{(j)}_{m,q}(\phi,\theta,\phi)
are Wigner
D
-functions. The above representation has eigenvalues

\displaystyle\begin{split}J^{2}\,{\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a},s}(\theta,\phi)=j(j+1){\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a},s}(\theta,\phi)\quad,\quad J_{3}\,{\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a},s}(\theta,\phi)=m{\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a},s}(\theta,\phi)~.\end{split}

(47)

This follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\begin{split}D^{\pm}_{q_{a}}\left[e^{i(m-q_{a})\phi}d^{j}_{-m,q_{a}}(\theta)v_{\mu_{a}}\right]&=\mp\sqrt{j(j+1)-q_{a}(q_{a}\pm 1)}\left[e^{i(m-q_{a})\phi}d^{j}_{-m,q_{a}\pm 1}(\theta)v_{\mu_{a}}\right]~.\end{split}

(48)

The form of these eigenfunctions are restricted by the fact that
d^{j}_{m,q}(\theta),{\cal D}^{(j)}_{m,q}(\theta,\phi)
are only well defined for
|m|,|q|\leq j
and
m-j,q-j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. These restrictions imply that the allowed set of
j,m
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}j&=q_{{R_{A}}}+\frac{1}{2}+n_{j}\geq 0\quad,\quad n_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}~,\\
m&=-j,-j+1,...,j-1,j~.\end{split}

(49)

where
q_{{R_{A}}}=max_{\mu\in\Delta_{R_{A}}}\big\{\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle\big\}
. Note that when
n_{j}<0
that we must impose additional restrictions on the
f_{a,\pm}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{a,+}(r):=0~\text{ if }~\left|q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\right|>j~,\\
f_{a,-}(r):=0~\text{ if }~\left|q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\right|>j~,\end{split}

(50)

which give
SU(2)_{J}
-representations a nested structure.

Because of this nested structure, it is useful to introduce additional notation. Let us define the spin-
j

R
-weight space
\Delta_{R}^{(j)}
which is a restriction of the
R
-representation space to the subspace that contributes to the spin-
j
representation of
\psi_{R}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{R}^{(j)}=\left\{\mu\in\Delta_{R}~\Big|~|\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle|\leq j+\frac{1}{2}\right\}~.\end{split}

(51)

Analogously, we can define the spin-
j
restriction of the
SU(2)_{T}
-restricted
R_{A}
-weight spaces

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{R_{A}}^{(j)}=\left\{\mu\in\Delta_{R_{A}}~\Big|~|\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle|\leq j+\frac{1}{2}\right\}~.\end{split}

(52)

For each
R_{A}
and fixed
j
we can also define an ordered basis
\{\mu_{a}\}
of
\Delta_{R_{A}}^{(j)}
such that
\langle\mu_{a},T_{3}\rangle>\langle\mu_{a+1},T_{3}\rangle
and
\langle\mu_{a},\gamma_{m}\rangle\geq\langle\mu_{a+1},\gamma_{m}\rangle
.
Notably, this defines a top and bottom weight
\mu_{top}=\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{bot}=\mu_{2j+1}
with respect to
T_{3}
.

The restriction to
\Delta_{R}^{(j)}
allows us to define the basis of the spin-
j
eigenfunctions of
\psi_{R}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\{\text{spin-}j\big\}_{R}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\big\{{\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a},s}\big\}\quad,\quad m=-j,...,j~,~s=\pm~,~\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R}^{(j)}~,\end{split}

(53)

which can be decomposed into irreducible
SU(2)_{T}
representations as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\{\text{spin-}j\big\}_{R_{A}}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\big\{{\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a},s}\big\}\quad,\quad m=-j,...,j~,~s=\pm~,~\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}^{(j)}~,\end{split}

(54)

where we omit the
{\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a},s}
for
\langle\mu_{a},T_{3}\rangle=s\times\left(j+\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Additionally, note that
\Delta_{R_{A}}^{(j)}=\Delta_{R_{A}}
if
j>q_{R_{A}}
.

We can also further decompose the
SU(2)_{J}
representation into
{\vec{S}},{\vec{K}}
representations by computing the value of
2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{K}}
on the above
\psi_{R_{A}}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}(2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{K}})\,\psi_{R_{A}}&=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{3}-\frac{1}{2}&~-D_{T_{3}-1/2}^{+}\\
D_{T_{3}+1/2}^{-}&~-T_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\end{array}\right)\,\psi_{R_{A}}~.\end{split}

(55)

We see that acting on
{\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}\,e^{i\phi/2}\,{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
g_{a}\,e^{-i\phi/2}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)
,
2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{K}}
is given by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}(2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{K}})\,{\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}&\sqrt{(j+1/2)^{2}-q_{a}^{2}}\\
\sqrt{(j+1/2)^{2}-q_{a}^{2}}&-q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}\end{array}\right){\cal Y}^{j}_{m,q_{a}}~,\end{split}

(56)

which has eigenvalues

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm Eig}[2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{K}}]=\begin{cases}j&j=k+\frac{1}{2}\\
-j-1&j=k-\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}\end{split}

(57)

where
k
is the spin of the
SU(2)_{K}
representation which is generated by the
{\vec{K}}
.
This matches the standard equations for addition of angular momentum. This means that we can further decompose the representations

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal Y}^{j=k\pm\frac{1}{2}}_{m,q_{a}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(2j+1+2q_{a})}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}(j+1/2\pm q_{a})\,e^{i\phi/2}\,{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
\pm\sqrt{(j+1/2)^{2}-q_{a}^{2}}\,e^{-i\phi/2}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)~.\end{split}

(58)

3.2
Zero-Modes for General Spherically Symmetric Monopole

Now we wish to solve the Dirac equation for a Weyl fermion in a representation
R
of
SU(N)
in the presence of a spherically symmetric monopole specified by
\{{\vec{T}},{\vec{I}}\,\}
. The spherical symmetry of the monopole configuration implies that the associated Dirac operator commutes with
{\vec{J}}
, and thus that we can expand the fermion zero-mode solutions in
SU(2)_{J}
representations.

Let us first restrict
R
to a
SU(2)_{T}
-irreducible component
R_{A}
and correspondingly take
\psi_{R_{A}}
which we can expand with respect to a basis of the representation space

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{R_{A}}=\sum_{a}\psi_{R_{A}}^{a}(r,\theta,\phi)\,v_{\mu_{a}}\quad,\quad\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}~.\end{split}

(59)

Now consider the Dirac operator. Explicitly, it can be written

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}=&\sigma^{\hat{r}}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\left(\sigma^{\hat{\theta}}\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\sigma^{\hat{\phi}}}{\sin\theta}\Big((\partial_{\phi}+iT_{3})-iT_{3}\cos\theta\Big)\right)\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}M_{+}e^{-i\phi}(\sigma^{\hat{\theta}}-i\sigma^{\hat{\phi}})+\frac{1}{2}M_{-}e^{i\phi}(\sigma^{\hat{\theta}}+i\sigma^{\hat{\phi}})~,\end{split}

(60)

where here we use the notation

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sigma^{\hat{r}}=\sigma^{3}\cos\theta+(\sigma^{1}\cos\phi+\sigma^{2}\sin\phi)\sin\theta~,\\
&\sigma^{\hat{\theta}}=-\sigma^{3}\sin\theta+(\sigma^{1}\cos\phi+\sigma^{2}\sin\phi)\cos\theta~,\\
&\sigma^{\hat{\phi}}=-\sigma^{1}\sin\phi+\sigma^{2}\cos\phi~.\end{split}

(61)

If we now perform a frame rotation

\psi_{R_{A},\alpha}=U_{\alpha}^{~\beta}\hat{\psi}_{R_{A},\beta}\quad,\quad U^{~\beta}_{\alpha}=e^{\frac{i\phi}{2}\sigma^{0}\bar{\sigma}^{3}}e^{\frac{i\theta}{2}\sigma^{0}\bar{\sigma}^{2}}~,

(62)

the Dirac equation simplifies

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Bigg[&\sigma^{3}\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r}\right)+\frac{{\cal K}}{r}-\left(\sigma^{-}e^{-i\phi}M_{+}-\sigma^{+}e^{i\phi}M_{-}\right)\Bigg]\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}=0~.\end{split}

(63)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal K}&=\sigma^{1}\left(\partial_{\theta}+\frac{1}{2}{\rm cot}(\theta)\right)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\sin\theta}\Big((\partial_{\phi}+iT_{3})-i\cos(\theta)T_{3}\Big)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&D^{+}_{T_{3}-\frac{1}{2}}\\
D^{-}_{T_{3}+\frac{1}{2}}&0\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(64)

where again

\displaystyle\begin{split}D^{\pm}_{q}=\partial_{\theta}\pm\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\Big(-i(\partial_{\phi}+iT_{3})-q\cos\theta\Big)~.\end{split}

(65)

Now, we can restrict to a solution of fixed spin-
j=q_{R_{A}}+\frac{1}{2}+n_{j}\geq 0
for
n_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
which restricts
\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}^{(j)}
. In the representation basis, the spin-
j
representation can be written

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}=\sum_{\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}^{(j)}}\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}^{a}v_{\mu_{a}}\quad,\quad\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}^{a}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}(r)\,e^{i\phi/2}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
g_{a}(r)\,e^{-i\phi/2}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(66)

where
f_{a}(r)=0
for
|q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}|>j
and
g_{a}(r)=0
for
|q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}|>j
. With this ansatz,
{\cal K}
acts as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal K}\cdot\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}^{a}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\sqrt{(j+1/2)^{2}-q_{a}^{2}}\\
\sqrt{(j+1/2)^{2}-q_{a}^{2}}&0\end{array}\right)\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}^{a}~.\end{split}

(67)

Upon restricting to the spin-
j
representation,
the Dirac equation reduces

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}=\frac{1}{r}\sigma^{3}\left[r\partial_{r}+\sigma^{3}{{\cal K}}+e^{-i\phi}\sigma^{-}M_{+}+e^{i\phi}\sigma^{+}M_{-}+1\right]\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}=0~.\end{split}

(68)

This reduces to a first order matrix ODE for
f_{a}(r),g_{a}(r)
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[r\partial_{r}+{\cal D}\right]F=0\quad,\quad F=\sum_{\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}(r)\\
g_{a}(r)\end{array}\right)v_{\mu_{a}}~,\end{split}

(69)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal D}=\sigma^{3}{{\cal K}}\big|_{j}+\sigma^{-}M_{+}+\sigma^{+}M_{-}+1~,\end{split}

(70)

and
\sigma^{3}{{\cal K}}\big|_{j}
is a symmetric, constant matrix acting diagonally on the spin-
j
restriction of
\hat{\psi}_{R_{A}}
. Now, using the fact that
{M}_{\pm}(r)
is only dependent on
r
we find the solutions:

\displaystyle\begin{split}F(r)={\cal P}\,{\rm exp}\left\{-\int_{0}^{r}{\cal D}(r^{\prime})\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}\right\}F_{0}~,\end{split}

(71)

where
F_{0}
is a constant vector and
{\cal P}\,{\rm exp}
is the path ordered exponential. Since
M_{\pm}(r)
are smooth, bounded solutions, the smooth plane-wave normalizable solutions now reduces to finding the vectors
F_{0}
that lead to solutions which fall off at least as fast as
1/r
at
r\to\infty
and are bounded as
r\to 0
.

Using the fact that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to 0}M_{\pm}=T_{\pm}\quad,\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}M_{\pm}=I_{\pm}~,\end{split}

(72)

we can identify the asymptotics
{\cal D}(r)
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to 0}{\cal D}(r)={\cal D}_{0}:=\sigma^{3}{\cal K}+\sigma^{-}T_{+}+\sigma^{+}T_{-}+1~,\\
\lim_{r\to\infty}{\cal D}(r)={\cal D}_{\infty}:=\sigma^{3}{\cal K}+\sigma^{-}I_{+}+\sigma^{+}I_{-}+1~.\end{split}

(73)

We can then reexpress
{\cal D}_{0}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal D}_{0}=-2\vec{S}\cdot\vec{L}~.\end{split}

(74)

Thus, solving for the regular solutions of the Dirac equation at
r\to 0
reduces to solving for the decomposition of the solutions of
\psi_{R_{A}}
into
{\vec{L}},{\vec{S}}
representations with non-negative
{\vec{L}}\cdot{\vec{S}}
.

Similarly, in the limit
r\to\infty
, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal D}_{\infty}=-2{\vec{S}}\cdot({\vec{K}}-{\vec{I}})-2S_{3}(K_{3}-I_{3})~.\end{split}

(75)

Thus, solving for the plane-wave normalizable solutions of the Dirac equation at
r\to\infty
reduces to solving for the decomposition into
{\vec{S}},({\vec{K}}-{\vec{I}})
representations with non-positive
{\vec{S}}\cdot({\vec{K}}-{\vec{I}})
.

Finding the plane-wave normalizable solutions then reduces to computing the intersection between the non-negative eigenspaces of
{\vec{L}}\cdot{\vec{S}}
and non-positive eigenspaces
{\vec{S}}\cdot({\vec{K}}-{\vec{I}})
.

3.2.1
Solutions of
r\to 0

Let us now study solutions of the Dirac equation in the limit
r\to 0
. Here we can solve for the spectrum of the solutions to the Dirac equation exactly.

Let us
restrict to the spin-
j=q_{R_{A}}+\frac{1}{2}+n_{j}\geq 0
for
n_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Since
{\cal D}_{0}=-{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{L}}
, we can compute the eigenvalues of
{\cal D}_{0}
by decomposing the spin-
j

SU(2)_{J}
representation as a tensor product of
of
{\vec{S}}
-,
{\vec{L}}
-, and
{\vec{T}}
- representations. We can then compute the eigenvalues of
2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{L}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm Eig}[2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{L}}]=\begin{cases}\ell&j\in q_{R_{A}}\otimes(\ell+\frac{1}{2})\\
-(\ell+1)&j\in q_{R_{A}}\otimes(\ell-\frac{1}{2})\end{cases}\end{split}

(76)

which correspond to solutions of the Dirac equation with the asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to 0}\psi_{R_{A}}\sim r^{2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{L}}}\psi_{0}~.\end{split}

(77)

In particular, the regular solutions at
r\to 0
are those for which
2{\vec{S}}\cdot{\vec{L}}\geq 0
which constrains:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ell=\begin{cases}n_{j},n_{j}+1,...,2q_{R_{A}}+n_{j}&n_{j}\geq 0\\
-n_{j}-1,-n_{j},...,2q_{R_{A}}+n_{j}&n_{j}<0\end{cases}\end{split}

(78)

Now we can solve for the eigenvectors of
{\cal D}_{0}
. First, recall that the space of spin-
j
representations arises from the tensor product of
{\vec{A}}={\vec{L}}+{\vec{S}}
and
{\vec{T}}
-representations.
In the vector gauge, the
A^{2}
eigenfunctions with
A^{2}=(\ell+1/2)(\ell+3/2)
are of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\psi}^{a}_{R_{A}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}(r)\,e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{-m_{a+},1/2}(\theta,\phi)\\
f_{a}(r)\,e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{-m_{a-},-1/2}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(79)

which we can see from (
58
) by taking
q_{a}=0
.

To relate this to the “canonical gauge” we have to gauge transform

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\psi}^{a}_{R_{A}}\to g\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}(r)\,e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{-m_{a+},1/2}(\theta,\phi)\\
f_{a}(r)\,e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{-m_{a-},-1/2}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)\quad,\quad g=e^{-i\phi T_{3}}e^{-i\theta T_{2}}e^{i\phi T_{3}}~.\end{split}

(80)

Since
g
is a gauge transformation, we can consider the spin up and spin down transformations separately:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\psi}_{{R_{A}}\pm}^{a}=e^{\pm i\frac{\phi}{2}}\sum_{b}{\cal D}^{(q_{R_{A}})}_{q_{R_{A}}-a,q_{R_{A}}-b}(\theta,\phi)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{0}(r)~{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{m_{0\pm},\pm\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
f_{1}(r)~{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{m_{1\pm},\pm\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
f_{2}(r)~{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{m_{2\pm},\pm\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
\vdots\\
f_{2q_{R_{A}}}(r)~{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{m_{2q_{R_{A}}\pm},\pm\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)^{b}~.\end{split}

(81)

Note here that the gauge transformation takes the form of a Wigner-
D
matrix. This follows from the fact that the gauge transformation is a rotation in
SU(N)
along the embedding
SU(2)_{T}\hookrightarrow SU(N)
.

Now using fact that Wigner
D
-functions multiply as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal D}^{(j_{1})}_{m,m^{\prime}}{\cal D}^{(j_{2})}_{n,n^{\prime}}=\sum_{J,M,M^{\prime}}\langle J,M|j_{1},m;j_{2},n\rangle\langle J,M^{\prime}|j_{1},m^{\prime};j_{2},n^{\prime}\rangle{\cal D}^{(J)}_{M,M^{\prime}}~,\end{split}

(82)

where
\langle J,M|j_{1},m_{1};j_{2},m_{2}\rangle
are Clebsch-Gordon coefficients,
we can determine the
\{f_{a}(r)\}
that lead to a uniform spin-
j
representation upon gauge transformation. To do so, first note that the transformation is only a
J_{3}
-eigenfunction if

\displaystyle\begin{split}m_{a\pm}=-m-(q_{R_{A}}-a)~.\end{split}

(83)

Now want to restrict to
J^{2}=j(j+1)
eigenfunctions.
For each
\ell=|j-q_{R_{A}}|-\frac{1}{2},|j-q_{R_{A}}|+\frac{1}{2},...,j+q_{R_{A}}-\frac{1}{2}
, there exists a unique choice of
\{f_{a}(r)\}
so that the resulting solution has purely spin-
j
components. To find these solutions we must solve

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\sum_{b=0}^{m+q_{R_{A}}-\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\left\langle J,-m\big|\ell+\frac{1}{2},-m-q_{R_{A}}+b;q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-b\right\rangle f_{b}(r)\right)\\
&\qquad\times\left\langle J,q_{R_{A}}-a\pm\frac{1}{2}\big|\ell+\frac{1}{2},\pm\frac{1}{2};q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\right\rangle=\begin{cases}0&J\neq j\\
1&J=j\end{cases}\end{split}

(84)

Note that the
a
-dependence factors out.

For fixed
m,\ell,q_{R_{A}}
, the Clebsch-Gordon coefficients
\langle J,-m|\ell+\frac{1}{2},-m-q_{R_{A}}+a;q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\rangle
define an orthogonal square matrix

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{J,a}^{(m,\ell)}=\langle J,-m|\ell+\frac{1}{2},-m-q_{R_{A}}+a;q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\rangle~.\end{split}

(85)

Since each
M_{J,a}^{(m,\ell)}
has an inverse,
we can pick

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{b}(r)=f_{m}(r)\,(M^{(m,\ell)})^{-1}_{b,J}~\delta^{J,j}~.\end{split}

(86)

This leads to the solution

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\psi}^{(\ell)a}_{R_{A}}=f(r)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\langle j,q_{{R_{A}}}-a+\frac{1}{2}|\ell+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\rangle\,e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{-m,q_{{R_{A}}}-a+1/2}(\theta,\phi)\\
\langle j,q_{{R_{A}}}-a-\frac{1}{2}|\ell+\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2};q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\rangle\,e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(\ell+\frac{1}{2})}_{-m,q_{{R_{A}}}-a-1/2}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(87)

which has the corresponding asymptotic behavior in the limit
r\to 0
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to 0}f(r)\sim r^{\ell}+O(r^{\ell+\delta})\quad,\quad\delta>0~.\end{split}

(88)

We can reorganize the corresponding
F_{0}
into the the vector

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{F}_{0}^{(\ell)}&:=v_{a+}^{(\ell)}\oplus v_{a-}^{(\ell)}~,\\
v_{a+}^{(\ell)}&=\left\langle j,q_{R_{A}}-a+\frac{1}{2}\Big|\ell+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\right\rangle~,\\
v_{a-}^{(\ell)}&=\left\langle j,q_{R_{A}}-a-\frac{1}{2}\Big|\ell+\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2};q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\right\rangle\quad,\quad a=0,...,2q_{R_{A}}~.\end{split}

(89)

3.2.2
Solutions at
r\to\infty

Now we want to solve for the normalizable solutions at
r\to\infty
. Here we are instead looking at the eigenfunctions of

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\cal D}_{\infty}=-2{\vec{S}}\cdot({\vec{K}}-{\vec{I}})-2S_{3}(K_{3}-I_{3})~.\end{split}

(90)

To do this, we can take a different approach.
In the limit
r\to\infty
there exists a gauge transformation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\omega=e^{i\phi I_{3}}e^{i\theta I_{2}}e^{-i\phi I_{3}}~,\end{split}

(91)

which takes the asymptotic gauge field

\displaystyle\begin{split}A=2T_{3}A_{Dirac}+\frac{i}{2}I_{+}e^{-i\phi}(d\theta-i\sin\theta d\phi)-\frac{i}{2}I_{-}e^{i\phi}(d\theta+i\sin\theta d\phi)+O(1/r)~,\end{split}

(92)

to the abelian gauge

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{ab.}=\omega^{-1}A\omega-i\omega^{-1}d\omega=2(T_{3}-I_{3})A_{Dirac}+O(1/r)~.\end{split}

(93)

Since we are looking for the asymptotic solutions to the Dirac operator, it is sufficient to solve the Dirac equation in the abelian gauge at leading order in
1/r
. We are then reduced to solving the Dirac equation in the presence of an abelian monopole at long range, which has a simple, known solution.

Now let us consider an irreducible component of the embedding of
SU(2)_{I}\subset SU(N)
of spin-
s_{I}
. Further, let
R_{A,s_{I}}
be the restriction of the
R_{A}
representation to an irreducible spin-
s_{I}

SU(2)_{I}
representation (which we will also refer to by
s_{I}
) with corresponding weight system
\Delta_{R_{A,s_{I}}}
.

Consider the restriction of
\psi_{R_{A}}
to
R_{A,s_{I}}
and let us decompose

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{R_{A,s_{I}}}=\sum_{\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A,s_{I}}}}\psi_{R_{A,s_{I}}}^{a}v_{\mu_{a}}~.\end{split}

(94)

As before, the gauge transformation to the abelian gauge and frame rotation changes
{\vec{J}}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\vec{J}}\to&-i\hat{r}\times\Big(\vec{\partial}+i(T_{3}-I_{3}){\vec{A}}_{Dirac}-\frac{i}{2}\sigma^{3}{\rm cot}\theta\hat{\phi}\Big)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}I_{+}(\hat{\theta}+i\hat{\phi})+\frac{1}{2}I_{-}(\hat{\theta}-i\hat{\phi})+\left(T_{3}+\frac{\sigma^{3}}{2}\right)\hat{r}~.\end{split}

(95)

After performing the same frame rotation as before, we find that the Dirac equation here simplifies to

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[r\partial_{r}+\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}+1\right]F=0\quad,\quad F=\sum_{a}v_{\mu_{a}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}(r)\\
g_{a}(r)\end{array}\right)\quad,\quad\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A,s_{I}}}~,\end{split}

(96)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}=\omega^{-1}{\cal D}_{\infty}\omega-1=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-D^{+}_{T_{3}-I_{3}-\frac{1}{2}}\\
D^{-}_{T_{3}-I_{3}+\frac{1}{2}}&0\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(97)

which again allows us to decompose the asymptotic eigenfunctions of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
in terms of Wigner-
D
functions. Explicitly, we can take the ansatz

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\psi}^{a}_{R_{A,s_{I}}}=\frac{1}{r}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{f}_{a}(r)\,e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}_{-m,Q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}}^{(n)}(\theta,\phi)\\
{g}_{a}(r)\,e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}_{-m,Q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}}^{(n)}(\theta,\phi)\\
\end{array}\right)\quad,\quad\langle\gamma_{m},\mu_{a}\rangle=Q_{a}~,\end{split}

(98)

where
n
is a fixed integer. Now, using the fact that
[{\vec{I}},\gamma_{m}]=0
we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{a}=\langle\mu_{a}\gamma_{m}\rangle=Q_{s_{I}}~,\end{split}

(99)

is independent of
\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A,s_{I}}}
.

Acting on
\hat{\psi}^{a}_{R_{A,s_{I}}}
with quantum number
n
, we see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}\,\hat{\psi}^{a}_{R_{A,s_{I}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\sqrt{(n+1/2)^{2}-\frac{Q_{s_{I}}^{2}}{4}}\\
\sqrt{(n+1/2)^{2}-\frac{Q_{s_{I}}^{2}}{4}}&0\end{array}\right)\hat{\psi}^{a}_{R_{A,s_{I}}}\end{split}

(100)

which has eigenvalues
\pm\sqrt{(n+1/2)^{2}-\frac{Q_{s_{I}}^{2}}{4}}
and corresponding eigenvectors
f_{a}=\pm g_{a}
.

Now we wish to undo the gauge transformation to relate these solutions back to the spin-
j
eigenfunctions in the canonical gauge. To do so, we must act by the gauge transformation
\omega^{-1}
which is explicitly given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{s_{I}}(\omega^{-1})_{ab}={\cal D}^{(s_{I})}_{s_{I}-a,s_{I}-b}(\theta,\phi)\quad,\quad a,b=0,...,2s_{I}+1~,\end{split}

(101)

where
{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,m^{\prime}}(\theta,\phi)
is the Wigner
D
-matrix and
\rho_{s_{I}}:SU(2)_{I}\to SU(N)
is the embedding associated to the spin-
s_{I}
representation. Due to the multiplication of Wigner
D
-functions, we find that given a fixed
s_{I}
, that the allowed values for
n
that lead to a spin-
j
representation are
n=|j-s_{I}|,...,j+s_{I}
.

After projecting onto the spin-
j
representation, we find that the solutions of the asymptotic Dirac equation are of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi^{a}_{R_{A,s_{I}}}=U(\theta,\phi)f(r)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\langle j,q_{a}+1/2|n,Q_{s_{I}}+1/2;s_{I},s_{I}-a\rangle~e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}\,{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}+1/2}(\theta,\phi)\\
\pm\langle j,q_{a}-1/2|n,Q_{s_{I}}-1/2;s_{I},s_{I}-a\rangle~e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}\,{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}-1/2}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(102)

where
q_{a}=Q_{s_{I}}+s_{I}-a=\langle\mu_{a},T_{3}\rangle
.

Thus, for each quantum number
n=|j-s_{I}|,...,j+s_{I}
there are two linearly independent spin-
j
eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
with
eigenvalues

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{\pm}^{(n)}=\pm\sqrt{(n+1/2)^{2}-\frac{Q_{s_{I}}^{2}}{4}}~.\end{split}

(103)

This implies that the plane-wave normalizable modes at
r\to\infty
are the solutions:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{R_{A,s_{I}}}^{(n)}=U(\theta,\phi)f(r)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\langle j,q_{a}+1/2|n,Q_{s_{I}}+1/2;s_{I},s_{I}-a\rangle~e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}+1/2}\\
\langle j,q_{a}-1/2|n,Q_{s_{I}}-1/2;s_{I},s_{I}-a\rangle~e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}-1/2}\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(104)

where
q_{a}=Q_{s_{I}}+s_{I}-a

which have asymptotic fall-off

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{R_{A,s_{I}}}^{(n)i}\sim r^{-1-\sqrt{(n+1/2)^{2}-\frac{Q_{s_{I}}^{2}}{4}}}\psi_{0}~.\end{split}

(105)

Note that as opposed to the eigenvectors of
{\cal D}_{0}
that are generically non-zero on the spin-
j
restriction of
R_{A}
, the eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
are generically non-zero on the restrictions of
R_{A}
to the irreducible
{\vec{I}}
-representations. Because of this decomposition, the only total angular momentum representations that come from an irreducible
{\vec{I}}
component of spin-
s_{I}
are those of total spin-
j
for
j^{(s_{I})}_{min}\leq j\leq j^{(s_{I})}_{max}
where

\displaystyle\begin{split}j^{(s_{I})}_{max}=max_{\mu\in\Delta_{R_{A},s_{I}}}[\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle]-\frac{1}{2}\quad,\quad j^{(s_{I})}_{min}=min_{\mu\in\Delta_{R_{A},s_{I}}}[\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle]-\frac{1}{2}~.\end{split}

(106)

Note that the only spin up/down solutions are those for which
n<\left|Q_{s_{I}}\mp\frac{1}{2}\right|
which corresponds to the choices

\displaystyle\begin{split}n=\begin{cases}Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}&Q_{s_{I}}>0~\Rightarrow\text{ spin down}\\
-Q_{s_{I}}+\frac{1}{2}&Q_{s_{I}}<0~\Rightarrow\text{ spin up}\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

(107)

with no solution for
Q_{s_{I}}=0
.

We can again identify this solution with the vector

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{F}_{0}^{(n)}&=u_{+,a}^{(n)}\oplus u_{-,a}^{(n)}~,\\
u_{\pm,a}^{(n)}&=\begin{cases}\pm\langle j,Q_{s_{I}}\pm 1/2+s_{I}-a|n,Q_{s_{I}}\pm 1/2;s_{I},s_{I}-a\rangle&\mu_{a}\in s_{I}\\
0&else\end{cases}\end{split}

(108)

3.2.3
Normalizable Solutions

Now let us go on to construct the plane-wave normalizable solutions of the zero-energy Dirac equation. This requires matching the solutions at
r\to\infty
that fall off at least as fast as
1/r
with the solutions at
r\to 0
that are finite.

Now we will show that any non-trivial, plane-wave normalizable, zero-energy solution of the Dirac equation falls off like
1/r
at
r\to\infty
. Given a plane-wave normalizable zero-energy solution of the Dirac equation for
\psi_{R_{A}}
, we can construct the vector fields

\displaystyle\begin{split}J_{R_{A}}^{~\,\mu}=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\psi_{R_{A}}\quad,\quad\widetilde{J}_{R_{A}}^{~\,\mu}=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\Phi\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\psi_{R_{A}}~,\end{split}

(109)

where
\Phi
is the Higgs field that makes up the monopole configuration.
By assumption
\psi_{R_{A}}
is smooth and goes to zero at
r\to\infty
at least as fast as
1/r
and
\Phi
is smooth and has the asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\Phi=\Phi_{\infty}-\frac{\gamma_{m}}{r}+O(1/r^{1+\delta})\quad,\quad\delta>0~.\end{split}

(110)

Because of this, it is clear that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\partial_{\mu}J^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}\,d^{3}x=\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}J^{~\,r}_{R_{A}}\,d^{2}x<\infty\quad,\quad\int\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{J}^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}\,d^{3}x=\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\widetilde{J}^{~\,r}_{R_{A}}\,d^{2}x<\infty~.\end{split}

(111)

Further, if either of these quantities are non-zero, then it is clear that
\psi_{R_{A}}
falls of as
1/r
at
r\to\infty
.

Using the fact that
\psi_{R_{A}}
is a solution of the Dirac equation, we see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\mu}J^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}=0\quad,\quad\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{J}^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}&=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}(D_{\mu}\Phi)\psi_{R_{A}}~.\end{split}

(112)

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}(D_{\mu}\Phi)\psi_{R_{A}}\,d^{3}x\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}r\psi_{R_{A}}\neq 0~.\end{split}

(113)

Now, applying the Bogomolny equation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}D_{\mu}\Phi=B_{\mu}~,\end{split}

(114)

we can rewrite

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{J}^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}(D_{\mu}\Phi)\psi_{R_{A}}&=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}B_{\mu}\psi_{R_{A}}=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}\psi_{R_{A}}~.\end{split}

(115)

Using

\displaystyle\begin{split}[D_{\mu},D_{\nu}]=F_{\mu\nu}\quad,\quad\sigma^{[\mu\nu]}=\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\sigma}^{\nu}+\bar{\sigma}^{0}\delta^{\mu\nu}~,\end{split}

(116)

and the fact that
\psi_{R_{A}}
is annihilated by
\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}
, we can further simplify:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{J}^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\Big((\sigma^{\mu}D_{\mu})(\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu})-\bar{\sigma}^{0}D^{2}\Big)\psi_{R_{A}}=-\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{0}D^{2}\psi_{R_{A}}~.\end{split}

(117)

Then, by integration by parts:

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\int\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{0}D^{2}\psi_{R_{A}}\,d^{3}x&=-\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{0}D^{r}\psi_{R_{A}}\,d^{2}x+\int D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{0}D^{\mu}\psi_{R_{A}}\,d^{3}x\\
&=\int D_{\mu}\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{0}D^{\mu}\psi_{R_{A}}\,d^{3}x=\int|D_{\mu}\psi_{R_{A}}|^{2}d^{3}x>0~,\end{split}

(118)

for any non-trivial
\psi_{R_{A}}
solving the Dirac equation. Here we used the fact that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{0}D^{r}\psi_{R_{A}}\,d^{2}x=0~,\end{split}

(119)

due to the fall off of at least
1/r
of
\psi_{R_{A}}
at
r\to\infty
.

Therefore, for any
\psi_{R_{A}}\neq 0
solving the massless Dirac equation in the presence of a spherically symmetric monopole

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{J}^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}\,d^{3}x>0~.\end{split}

(120)

Hence, any such
\psi_{R_{A}}
falls off like
1/r
at
r\to\infty
.

We can also use the fact that
J^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}
is divergenceless to determine further constraints on
\psi_{R_{A}}
. If we decompose

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{{R_{A}}}=\sum_{\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}}\psi^{a}_{{R_{A}}}v_{\mu_{a}}\quad,\quad\psi^{a}_{{R_{A}}}=U(\theta,\phi)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}(r)\,e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
g_{a}(r)\,e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)~,\end{split}

(121)

then integrating the divergence of
J_{R_{A}}^{~\,\mu}
, implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\partial_{\mu}J^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}d^{3}x=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\sum_{a}\left(|f_{a}|^{2}-|g_{a}|^{2}\right)=0~.\end{split}

(122)

4
Proof of Formula

Now we will prove the main result of this paper:

Theorem
Given a fermion
\psi_{R}
that transforms in the representation
R
of
SU(N)
, the number of plane-wave normalizable solutions to the Dirac equation in the presence of a spherically symmetric monopole field configuration specified by the embeddings
{\vec{T}},{\vec{I}}:\mathfrak{su}(2)_{T,I}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{su}(N)
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\rm ker}_{R}[\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}]\big]=\sum_{\mu\in\Delta_{R}}n_{R}(\mu)\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle~sign\big(\langle\mu,T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\big)~.\end{split}

(123)

To prove this we will need to make use of the following lemmas. In the following we will always assume that we decompose
\psi_{R}
into irreducible
SU(2)_{T}
representations and restrict to a single irreducible component
\psi_{R_{A}}
which transforms in the spin-
q_{R_{A}}
representation of
SU(2)_{T}
.

Lemma 1
When
j>q_{R_{A}}
there are no zero-eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
.

proof.
Spin-
j
zero-eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
only occur if there exists fermions with weight
\mu\in\Delta_{R_{A,s_{I}}}
that have quantum number
n=|\langle\mu,T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle|-\frac{1}{2}>0.
These only exist if
j+s_{I}\geq|\langle\mu,T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle|-\frac{1}{2}\geq|j-s_{I}|
where
v_{\mu}
belongs to some irreducible
{\vec{I}}^{(i,a)}
-component of spin-
s_{I}
. Now, using the fact that
|\langle\mu,T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle|\leq q_{R_{A}}-s_{I},

\forall\mu\in\Delta_{R_{A,s_{I}}}
and the fact that
j\geq q_{R_{A}}+\frac{1}{2}>s_{I}
we see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}j-s_{I}\geq q_{R_{A}}-s_{I}+\frac{1}{2}>|\langle\mu,T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle|-\frac{1}{2}\quad,\quad\forall\mu\in\Delta_{R_{A,s_{I}}}~,\end{split}

(124)

and
therefore there are no zero-eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
when
j>q_{R_{A}},s_{I}
. ∎

Lemma 2
Pick
j<q_{R_{A}}
and let
\mu_{top}
(
\mu_{bot}
) be the top (resp. bottom) weight of
\Delta_{R_{A}}^{(j)}
and let us take the convention
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}\rangle>0
. There exists one single spin down zero-eigenvector of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
with total spin-
j
if
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle>0
and one spin up zero-eigenvector of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
with total spin-
j
if
\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0
. This means that the eigenvectors satisfy either:
f_{a}=0
or
g_{a}=0
respectively. There are no other spin-
j
zero-eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
when restricted to
R_{A}
.

proof.
Let us fix
j<q_{R_{A}}
which defines a highest and lowest weight
\mu_{top},\mu_{bot}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}
. First, consider the highest weight component. In general, this belongs to a spin-
s_{I}
representation of
SU(2)_{I}
. The fermions
\psi^{a}_{R_{A,s_{I}}}
for
\mu_{a}\in\Delta^{(j)}_{R_{A,s_{I}}}
are of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{R_{A,s_{I}}}^{a}=\sum_{b}{\cal D}^{(s_{I})}_{s_{I}-a,s_{I}-b}(\theta,\phi)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{b}(r)\,e^{i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(n)}_{-m-s_{I}+b,Q_{s_{I}}+\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\
g_{b}(r)\,e^{-i\frac{\phi}{2}}{\cal D}^{(n)}_{-m-s_{I}+b,Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\end{array}\right)~.\end{split}

(125)

Since the
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
eigenvalues of such a solution are
\lambda_{\pm}^{(n)}=\pm\sqrt{(n+1/2)^{2}-Q_{s_{I}}^{2}}
, it follows that there exists a spin down zero-eigenvector of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
with
n=Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}
iff
Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}\geq 0
and it is the unique zero-eigenvector belonging to this
{\vec{I}}
-representation. Conversely, if
Q_{s_{I}}\leq 0
, then there is no spin down zero-eigenvector from the irreducible
{\vec{I}}
representation.

Thus, let us assume that
Q_{s_{I}}>0
. This condition combined with
q_{R_{A}}>j,s_{I}
implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}+s_{I}\geq j\geq Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}-s_{I}\geq 0~.\end{split}

(126)

Now, let us pick
n=Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}\geq 0
. We now must show that the solutions constructed above have a non-trivial total spin-
j
solution which requires showing that
[j]\in[n]\otimes[s_{I}]
.

By using the standard multiplication of Wigner
D
-functions, we can expand the product of Wigner
D
-functions in (
125
) as representations with total spin:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}\right]\otimes\left[s_{I}\right]=\left[Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}-s_{I}\right]\oplus\left[Q_{s_{I}}+\frac{1}{2}-s_{I}\right]\oplus...\oplus\left[Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2}+s_{I}\right]~,\end{split}

(127)

where
[p]
is the representation of total spin-
p
. It then follows from (
126
) that
[j]\in\left[Q_{s_{I}}-1/2\right]\otimes\left[s_{I}\right]
and that there is a single spin down zero-eigenvector of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
in the irreducible
{\vec{I}}
component containing
\mu_{top}
when
\langle\mu_{top},\gamma_{m}\rangle>0
. The explicit solution is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{a}=0\quad,\quad g_{a}=\begin{cases}\left\langle j,Q_{s_{I}}+s_{I}-a-\frac{1}{2}\big|Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2},Q_{s_{I}}-\frac{1}{2};s_{I},s_{I}-a\right\rangle&\mu_{a}\in s_{I}\\
0&else\end{cases}\end{split}

(128)

where we have chosen a basis
\{\mu_{a}\}_{a=0}^{2s_{I}}
of
\Delta_{R_{A,s_{I}}}

Now, since the eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
decompose with respect to irreducible
{\vec{I}}
-representations, it follows that the unique spin down zero-eigenvector of
\psi_{R_{A}}
with total spin-
j
of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
is the one constructed above containing
\mu_{top}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}
.
The reason is that any spin-
j
eigenvector
F_{0}^{(j)}
of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
that belongs to a different
{\vec{I}}
-irreducible component of spin-
s_{I}^{\prime}
(i.e. the spin-
s_{I}^{\prime}
representation does not contain
v_{\mu_{top}}
) will not be spin polarized as we have shown in Section
3.1
by explicit construction. This follows from the fact that
[\gamma_{m},{\vec{I}}]=0
which, when restricted to an irreducible
SU(2)_{T}
representation implies
Q_{s_{I}}\neq Q_{s_{I}^{\prime}}
even when
s_{I}=s_{I}^{\prime}
.

Then, since the gauge transformation
\omega
that maps the abelian, asymptotic gauge to the canonical gauge commutes with
\vec{S}
, any such
F_{0}^{(j)}
will necessarily correspond to a solution that is not polarized and hence with quantum number
n
in the asymptotic gauge such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}n\neq\begin{cases}Q_{s_{I}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}&Q_{s_{I}^{\prime}}>0\\
-Q_{s_{I}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}&Q_{s_{I}^{\prime}}<0\end{cases}\end{split}

(129)

Using the fact that
\lambda^{(n)}_{\pm}=\pm\sqrt{(n+1/2)^{2}-Q_{s_{I}^{\prime}}^{2}}
, we see that any such
F_{0}^{(j)}
cannot be a spin-
j
zero-eigenvector of
{\cal D}_{\infty}
and that the only spin-
j
zero-energy solution is the one constructed above.
Therefore, we see that there is only a single spin down zero-eigenvector of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
with total spin-
j
iff
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle>0
coming from the irreducible
{\vec{I}}
component containing
\mu_{top}
as constructed above.

A nearly identical argument shows that there only exists a spin up zero-eigenvector of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
when
\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0
. ∎

Lemma 3
If
\mu_{top},\mu_{bot}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}
are the highest/lowest weight of
\Delta_{R_{A}}^{(j)}
for
j<q_{R_{A}}
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle=\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\langle w(\mu_{top}),T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0~,\end{split}

(130)

then there exsits 2 zero-eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
which are oppositely spin polarized. Conversely, if

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle=\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\langle w(\mu_{top}),T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\geq 0~,\end{split}

(131)

then there is at most one zero-eigenvalue of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}

where
w
is the unique element of the Weyl group of
SU(2)_{T}\subset SU(N)
.

proof.
From Lemma 2, we know that if
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle>0
and
\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0
then there are a pair of zero-eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
that are oppositely spin polarized.

Conversely, we see that if
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle,\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\geq 0
or
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle,\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\leq 0
there is at most a single spin polarized eigenvalue.

Now, using the fact that
q_{R_{A}}>s_{I}
and
[{\vec{I}},\gamma_{m}]=0
for irreducible representations
R_{A}
, we see that if
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0
then
\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0
and similarly if
\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle>0
then
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}\rangle>0
. This implies that if

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0~,\end{split}

(132)

then
\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle>0
and
\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0
and
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
has two spin-
j
zero-eigenvectors.

Further, since we are considering
R_{A}
as a representation of
SU(2)_{T}
, the unique non-trivial element
w
of the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{T}
exchanges the top and bottom weight so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu_{bot}=w(\mu_{top})~.\end{split}

(133)

Explicitly, in terms of Dynkin indices for weights:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu=[m_{1},...,m_{N-1}]~~\Rightarrow~~w(\mu)=[-m_{N-1},...,-m_{1}]~.\end{split}

(134)

Therefore, there exists two zero-eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
if

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\langle\mu_{bot},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle=\langle\mu_{top},T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\langle w(\mu_{top}),T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle<0~.\end{split}

(135)

∎

Lemma 4
When there are no zero-eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
, there are no zero-energy plane-wave normalizable solutions of the Dirac equation.

proof.
First let us recall that a zero-eigenvalue of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
corresponds to a solution of the Dirac equation with asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\psi_{R_{A}}\sim\frac{1}{r}\psi_{R_{A}}^{(0)}~.\end{split}

(136)

And therefore, if there are no zero-eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}r\psi_{R_{A}}=0~.\end{split}

(137)

Now using the fact that for any non-trivial solution of the Dirac equation
\psi_{R_{A}}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{J}_{R_{A}}^{~\,\mu}\,d^{3}x=\int|D_{\mu}\psi_{R_{A}}|^{2}\,d^{3}x>0\quad,\quad\widetilde{J}^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{a}\Phi\psi_{R_{A}}~,\end{split}

(138)

we see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{S^{2}}\widetilde{J}^{r}_{R_{A}}\,\,r^{2}\,d^{2}\Omega\neq 0~~\Rightarrow~~\lim_{r\to\infty}r\,\psi_{R_{A}}(r)\neq 0~.\end{split}

(139)

Thus, any solution of the Dirac equation
\psi_{R_{A}}
must have leading order
1/r
as
r\to\infty
. Therefore, if
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
has no zero-eigenvalues, then all asymptotic solutions that fall off faster than
1/r
and there can be no solution to the Dirac equation.
∎

Lemma 5
When
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
has a single pair of zero-eigenvalues, there is at most a single, plane-wave normalizable, zero-energy solution to the Dirac equation. Conversely, if
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
has less than 2 zero-modes, then there is no normalizable solution of the Dirac equation.

proof.
From Lemma 4, we see that any non-trivial solutions to the Dirac equation has

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}r\psi_{R}\neq 0~.\end{split}

(140)

From our explicit solutions to the Dirac equation, we see that these solutions correspond to the zero-eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
. From Lemma 2, we know that there are at most two such eigenvectors and that they are polarized.

Then, for any time-independent solution of the Dirac equation
\psi_{R_{A}}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{\mu}J^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}=0\quad,\quad J_{R_{A}}^{~\,\mu}=\bar{\psi}_{R_{A}}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}\psi_{R_{A}}~.\end{split}

(141)

This implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\int\partial_{\mu}J^{~\,\mu}_{R_{A}}\,d^{3}x=\lim_{r\to\infty}\int J^{r}_{R_{A}}\,r^{2}\,d^{2}\Omega=\lim_{r\to\infty}r^{2}\sum_{a}\left(|f_{a}|^{2}-|g_{a}|^{2}\right)~.\end{split}

(142)

This constraint then implies that the two polarized zero-eigenvectors of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
can at most combine into one non-trivial solution of the Dirac equation.

Further, if there is only a single zero-eigenvalue of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
, then from Lemma 2 it must be spin polarized. The condition (
142
) then cannot be satisfied except by the trivial solution. Additionally, if there are no zero-eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}
, then there are no solutions that go like
1/r
at
r\to\infty
and hence from Lemma 4, we see that there can be no zero energy plane-wave normalizable solutions of the Dirac equation.
∎

4.1

\psi_{R}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{R}=\sum_{A}\psi_{{R_{A}}}\quad,\quad R=\bigoplus_{A}{R_{A}}~.\end{split}

From Lemma 4 and 5, we know that there is at most a single solution to the Dirac equation if there are a pair of zero-eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\cal D}_{\infty}

where

Let us recall that the spin-

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{R_{A}}=U(\theta,\phi)\sum_{\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}(r)\,e^{i\phi/2}{\cal D}^{(j)}_{-m,q_{a}+\frac{1}{2}}(\theta,\phi)\\

(145)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&F(r)=\sum_{\mu_{a}\in\Delta_{R_{A}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{a}(r)\\
g_{a}(r)\end{array}\right)v_{\mu_{a}}\quad,\quad F(r)={\cal P}\,e^{-\int_{0}^{r}{\cal D}(r^{\prime})\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}}F_{0}~,\\
&{\cal D}(r)=\sigma^{3}{\cal K}\big|_{j}+\sigma^{-}M_{+}+\sigma^{+}M_{-}+1~.\end{split}

(146)

and

{\cal K}\big|_{j}
is the differential operator
{\cal K}
in (

) acting on the spin-
j
representation.

Any constant vector
F_{0}
that leads to a vector

is smooth and plane-wave normalizable. This follows from the fact that
{\cal D}(r)

which is due to the fact that we are considering a smooth monopole configuration and

is a constant.

To any such

which is simply a reorganization into spin up plus spin down components:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{F}(r)&=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f_{1}(r)\\
\vdots\\
f_{2q_{R_{A}}+1}(r)\\
g_{1}(r)\\
\vdots\\

(148)

Above, we have solved explicitly for the set of
\hat{F}_{0}
that correspond to the set of

which provide solutions that obey condition
I.)
and that obey condition

separately. Explicitly, they are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}I.)~\hat{F}_{0}^{(\ell)}&:=v_{a+}^{(\ell)}\oplus v_{a-}^{(\ell)}~,\\
v_{a+}^{(\ell)}&=\left\langle j,q_{R_{A}}-a+\frac{1}{2}\Big|\ell+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\right\rangle~,\\
v_{a-}^{(\ell)}&=\left\langle j,q_{R_{A}}-a-\frac{1}{2}\Big|\ell+\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2};q_{R_{A}},q_{R_{A}}-a\right\rangle\quad,\quad a=1,...,2q_{R_{A}}~,\\
II.)~\hat{F}_{0}^{(n)}&=u_{+,a}^{(n)}\oplus u_{-,a}^{(n)}~,\\

(149)

These can be truncated to form

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\rm ker}_{{R_{A}}}[\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}]\big]&=\sum_{\mu\in\Delta^{+}_{R_{A}}[T_{3},I_{3}]}2\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle\\

&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\sum_{\mu\in\Delta_{R}}n_{R}(\mu)\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle\,{\rm sign}\left(\langle\mu-\epsilon w(\mu),T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\right)~.\end{split}

(153)

∎

Corollary
The plane-wave normalizable kernel of the Dirac operator in the presence of a spherically symmetric monopole decomposes into total angular momentum representations as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm ker}_{R}[i\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}]=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Delta^{+}_{R}[T_{3},I_{3}]}\left[\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\right]^{\oplus n_{R}(\mu)}~,\end{split}

(154)

where
[j]
is the spin-
j
representation.

proof.
From the above analysis, we see that the kernel of the Dirac operator when restricted to the
R_{A}
-irreducible representation of
SU(2)_{T}
decomposes into total angular momentum representations as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm ker}_{{R_{A}}}[\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}]=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Delta^{+}_{R_{A}}[T_{3},I_{3}]}\left[\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\right]~,\end{split}

(155)

where
[j]
is the spin-
j
representation. Summing over the irreducible components
R_{A}
, we find

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm ker}_{R}[\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}]&=\bigoplus_{A}\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Delta^{+}_{R_{A}}[T_{3},I_{3}]}\left[\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\right]=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Delta^{+}_{R}[T_{3},I_{3}]}\left[\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\right]^{\oplus n_{R}(\mu)}~.\end{split}

(156)

∎

5
Example:
SU(5)
Monopoles with a Fundamental Fermion

Here we will illustrate the above index theorem with a simple example. Let us consider
SU(5)
gauge theory with a Weyl fermion
\psi_{R_{f}}
in the fundamental representation
R_{f}
. We would like to compute the number of fermion zero-energy solutions in the presence of a spherically symmetric monopole and determine their structure. Let us pick a spherically symmetric monopole with
{\vec{T}}
embedding specified by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{f}(T_{3})={{\rm diag}}(2,1,0,-1,-2)~.\end{split}

(157)

Let us further specify a monopole with asymptotic magnetic charge

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{f}(\gamma_{m})=\frac{1}{2}{{\rm diag}}(1,1,1,1,-4)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R_{f}(I_{3})=\frac{1}{2}{{\rm diag}}(3,1,-1,-3,0)~.\end{split}

(158)

Using the fact that the weight multiplicity
n_{f}(\mu)=1
for the fundamental representation, we find that the number of zero-energy solutions is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\big[{\rm ker}_{{R_{f}}}[\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}]\big]&=\sum_{\mu\in\Delta_{{R_{f}}}}\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle~sign\big(\langle\mu,T_{3}-I_{3}\rangle\big)\\
&=(2\times 1)+(1\times 1)+0+(-1\times 1)+(-2\times-1)=4~,\end{split}

(159)

which decomposes in terms of total angular momentum representations as

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm ker}_{R_{f}}[i\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}D_{\mu}]=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Delta^{+}_{R_{f}}[T_{3},I_{3}]}\left[\langle\mu,T_{3}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\right]^{\oplus n_{f}(\mu)}=\left[2-\frac{1}{2}\right]=\left[\frac{3}{2}\right]~.\end{split}

(160)

5.1
Spin-1/2 Solution

Let us first show that there is no spin-1/2 zero-energy solution. First let us introduce a basis of the representation and weight space

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{f}={\rm span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{v_{a}\}_{a=1}^{5}~,~\{\mu_{a}\}_{a=1}^{5}\in\Delta_{R_{f}}\quad,\quad R_{f}(T_{3})v_{a}=\langle T_{3},\mu_{a}\rangle v_{a}=(3-a)v_{a}~,\end{split}

where

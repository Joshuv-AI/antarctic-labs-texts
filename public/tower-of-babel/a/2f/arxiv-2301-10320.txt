The angular momentum of electron radiation in a uniform magnetic field
Vladimir Epp, Ulyana Guselnikova

Abstract
We study theoretically by means of quantum electrodynamics the vortex radiation of a relativistic electron in a uniform magnetic field. The exact expressions for the probability of emission of a photon with a certain angular momentum are found. The classical asymptotics ℏ → 0 \hbar\to 0 of this probability does not match the angular momentum flux density calculated by the classical method using the symmetrized energy-momentum tensor. Although the flux of angular momentum integrated over the radiation directions is the same in both cases. We found the angular momentum flux of the radiation field using the canonical (not symmetrized) energy-momentum tensor and showed that the flux obtained in this way coincide with the classical limit for the probability of photon emission.

Introduction

Electromagnetic radiation carrying an angular momentum of more than one Planck constant per photon attracts considerable attention of researchers due to the possibility of application in various fields of physics: information transfer, interaction with atoms, high-energy particle collisions and radiation processes. Vortex light beams have opened up a wide range of applications such as spatial optical confinement of atoms or microscopic objects and phase contrast microscopy
Barnett et al. 2017
,
Andrews and Babiker 2012
. The first theoretical and experimental studies of twisted light or vortex radiation were devoted to laser radiation modified by an astigmatic optical system or numerically calculated holograms
Allen et al. 1992
,
Padgett et al. 2004
.
These publications stimulated extensive research of the vortex optical beams. A more extensive bibliography on the history in this area can be found in the reviews
Barnett et al. 2017
,
Hernández-García
et al. 2017
.

One of the most promising sources of vortex radiation in the X-ray and the gamma range of the spectrum are the charged particles moving in a spiral or vortex beams of charged particles
Karlovets 2015
,
Silenko et al. 2017
. In particular, spiral motion is realized in a uniform magnetic field and in a helical undulator. Various schemes for obtaining a beam of twisted photons in undulators
Sasaki and McNulty 2008
,
Bordovitsyn et al. 2012
,
Matsuba et al. 2018
and free electron lasers
Hemsing et al. 2009
,
Hemsing et al. 2011
,
Hemsing and Marinelli 2012
have been proposed. Analytical calculations have shown that a particle moving in a spiral emits photons carrying an average angular momentum equal to
n\hbar
with
n
being the harmonic number
Katoh et al. 2017
,
Epp and Guselnikova 2019
,
Salehi and Katoh 2021
.
These theoretical results have been confirmed by a number of experimental studies of radiation in helical undulators
Bahrdt et al. 2013
,
Kaneyasu et al. 2017
,
Katoh et al. 2017
.

In the works listed above the angular momentum of the radiation of a particle moving along a spiral was studied within the framework of classical electrodynamics. The quantum aspects of this problem were studied in
Bogdanov et al. 2018
,
Bogdanov et al. 2019
by use of semiclassical approach.

In the present work, we calculated the probability of radiation of photons with a high angular momentum using the exact solution of the Dirac equation for an electron in a uniform magnetic field. This article is a continuation of our work
Epp and Guselnikova 2019
, where the angular momentum of radiation at spiral motion was studied using classical methods.

The article is organized as follows. The section
1
contains the well-known solution of the Dirac equation for an electron in a uniform magnetic field. The main purpose of this section is to remind the wave function of the electron and to introduce the notation that will be used in what follows. In the section
2

\hbar\to 0
) is discussed in section
3

The result obtained here differs from the angular momentum flux density calculated earlier by the classical method. Therefore, in section
4
, we find the canonical angular momentum flux density in the Coulomb gauge of the vector potential by use of the classical method and show that this angular momentum flux is consistent with the quantum calculation. Finally, in the last section, we discuss the obtained results.

2
Probability of the transitions
l\to l^{\prime}

Let us find the probability of emission of a photon with a wave vector
\bm{k}
and a
z
-component of angular momentum equal to
L_{z}
. By virtue of the angular momentum conservation law, this is the probability of transition from the state with the quantum number
l
to the state with the quantum number
l^{\prime}=l-L_{z}/\hbar
. We will specify the photon polarization by the basis

\bm{e}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\bm{e}_{1}\pm i\bm{e}_{2}),

(9)

where
\bm{e}_{1}
and
\bm{e}_{2}
are mutually orthogonal real vectors orthogonal to the wave vector
\bm{k}
, so that the vectors listed in the order
\bm{e}_{1},\bm{e}_{2},\bm{k}
follow the right-hand rule. The vector
\bm{e}_{+}
corresponds to the right-handed polarization, and
\bm{e}_{-}
to the left-handed one.

The average value of

\frac{dL_{z}}{{\rm d}t}=\sum_{m^{\prime}}\hbar(l-l^{\prime})w_{\pm},

(10)

The transition probability

was calculated in
Ternov et al. 1966
,
Sokolov and Ternov 1986
,
Bordovitsyn 1999
. Here we briefly repeat the main steps of the calculations.

w_{\pm}=\frac{e_{0}^{2}}{2\pi\hbar}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}k}{k}\delta(K-K^{\prime}-k)S_{\pm}.

(11)

The matrix elements

in the basis (
9

S_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}[((\bm{n}\times\langle\bm{\alpha}\rangle^{*})(\bm{n}\times\langle\bm{\alpha}\rangle))\pm((\bm{n}(\langle\bm{\alpha}\rangle\times\langle\bm{\alpha}\rangle^{*}))],

(12)

The asterisk denotes complex conjugate. The vector
\langle\bm{\alpha}\rangle
represents the matrix elements of the Dirac matrix
\bm{\alpha}

\langle\bm{\alpha}\rangle=\int\psi_{n^{\prime}l^{\prime}k^{\prime}_{z}\zeta}l^{-(\bm{k}\bm{r})}\bm{\alpha}\Psi_{nl\zeta k_{z}}{\rm d}^{3}x,

(13)

The vector

\bm{n}

(n_{x},n_{y},n_{z})=(0,\sin\theta,\cos\theta)

Integration in (
11

In the spherical coordinate system

\displaystyle S_{\pm}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[|\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle|^{2}+|\langle\alpha_{2}\rangle\cos\theta-\langle\alpha_{3}\rangle\sin\theta|^{2}\mp iS_{\parallel}\right],

(15)

\displaystyle S_{\parallel}=

\displaystyle\bigl(\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle^{*}\langle\alpha_{2}\rangle-\langle\alpha_{2}\rangle^{*}\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle\bigr)\cos\theta

\displaystyle-\bigl(\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle^{*}\langle\alpha_{3}\rangle-\langle\alpha_{3}\rangle^{*}\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle\bigr)\sin\theta.

Integration in (
13

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=i[(c^{\prime}_{1}c_{4}+c^{\prime}_{3}c_{2})I_{n,n^{\prime}-1}-(c_{1}c^{\prime}_{4}+c_{3}c^{\prime}_{2})I_{n-1,n^{\prime}}]f,

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle

Finally, substituting (

\frac{{\rm d}L_{z}}{{\rm d}t}=e_{0}^{2}\sum_{m^{\prime}}(l-l^{\prime})\int S_{\pm}\frac{k\sin\theta{\rm d}\theta}{1-\beta_{||}\cos\theta}.

This expression differs from the formula for the intensity of radiation

3
The classic limit

Next, we find the asymptotic expression for the angular momentum flux (
18
) in the limit
\hbar\to 0
and
n,n^{\prime}\gg 1
. In this approximation, the wave vector (
21
) is equal to

\displaystyle k\approx\frac{(n-n^{\prime})\omega_{0}}{c(1-\beta_{\parallel}\cos\theta)},

(22)

where
\omega_{0}=eHc/E
is the frequency of electron motion in a circle. It can be seen from (
5
) that the linear momentum component orthogonal to the
z
axis is equal to
p_{\perp}=2\hbar\displaystyle\sqrt{\gamma n}
. In the classical limit
p_{\perp}=Ev_{\perp}/c^{2}
, where
v_{\perp}
is the velocity part orthogonal to the
z
axis. Hence, it is convenient to introduce the notation

\displaystyle\beta_{\perp}=\frac{v_{\perp}}{c}=\frac{2c\hbar\sqrt{\gamma n}}{E}

(23)

Substituting (
22
) and (
23
) into the argument of the Laguerre functions (
17
), we obtain

x\approx\frac{(n-n^{\prime})^{2}\beta_{\perp}\sin^{2}\theta}{4n(1-\beta_{\parallel}\cos\theta)}.

(24)

In the classical limit, the photon energy is much less than the electron energy, i.e.
n-n^{\prime}\ll n
. Therefore, for
n\to\infty
and fixed
n-n^{\prime}
the variable
x
decreases as
1/n
. The asymptotic expression for Laguerre polynomials of the form
Q_{n}^{l}(z/n)
for large
n
and fixed
l
and
z
has the form

Sokolov et al. 1955
,
Szegö 1975

Q_{n}^{l}\left(\frac{z}{n}\right)\approx n^{l}e^{z/2n}z^{-l/2}J_{l}(2\sqrt{z}),

(25)

where
J_{l}
are the Bessel functions. Hence,

\displaystyle I_{n,n^{\prime}}\approx J_{n-n^{\prime}}(\xi),\quad\xi=\frac{\nu\beta_{\perp}\sin\theta}{1-\beta_{\parallel}\cos\theta}.

(26)

This asymptotics does not apply to the Laguerre functions
I_{s,s^{\prime}}(x)
, since in the latter case
x
tends to zero regardless of the indices
s
and
s^{\prime}
. Assuming
s
and
s^{\prime}
to be fixed and letting
x
tend to zero, we obtain for the Laguerre polynomials

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}Q_{n}^{l}(x)=\frac{1}{n!}(l+1)(l+2)\dots(l+n).

(27)

Substituting this into the Laguerre function, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}I_{s,s^{\prime}}(x)=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}.

(28)

Thus, we can the neglect the change in quantum number
s
in transitions between states with large numbers
n
. In this case
n-n^{\prime}=l-l^{\prime}
.
This means that the
z
-component of the photon angular momentum can only be positive (if
z
-axis is directed along the magnetic field).
Substituting the resulting expansions into (
15
), averaging the coefficients
c_{i}
over the initial spin states, and summing up
c_{i}^{\prime}
over the final states, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\beta_{\perp}J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\xi)\pm\frac{\cos\theta-\beta_{\parallel}}{\sin\theta}J_{\nu}(\xi)\right]^{2}.

(29)

As a result, the flux of angular momentum in the radiation field (
18
) takes the form

\frac{{\rm d}L_{z\pm}}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{e_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}}{2c}\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\left[\beta_{\perp}J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\xi)\pm\frac{\cos\theta-\beta_{\parallel}}{\sin\theta}J_{\nu}(\xi)\right]^{2}\frac{\sin\theta{\rm d}\theta}{(1-\beta_{\parallel}\cos\theta)^{2}}.

(30)

This expression was obtained in
Bogdanov et al. 2018
by use of semiclassical method.

The integrand in Eq. (
30
) differs from the flux density of angular momentum obtained by use of classical electrodynamics
Epp and Guselnikova 2019
, which reads (for simplicity, we put
\beta_{\parallel}=0
):

\frac{{\rm d}L_{z}}{{\rm d}\Omega{\rm d}t}=\frac{e_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}\beta\sin\theta}{2\pi c}\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu\left\{\xi\left[{J^{\prime}_{\nu}}^{2}(\xi)+\cot^{2}\theta\,J^{2}_{\nu}(\xi)\right]+J_{\nu}(\xi)J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\xi)\right\}.

(31)

The main difference between the Eqs (
30
) and (
31
) is that the angular momentum flux (
31
) in the direction of the
z
axis is equal to zero, while the flux (
30
) is not.
According to standard electrodynamics, the flux of angular momentum component
(\bm{L}\bm{n})
in the direction of the vector
\bm{n}
is equal to zero
Jackson 1999
,
Sokolov 1991

The angular momentum flux (
31
) satisfies this condition. But the angular momentum flux of radiation in the direction of the
z
axis, obtained by quantum theory, is not equal to zero. In particular, if we put
\theta=0,\pi
in Eq. (
30
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}L_{z\pm}}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{e_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}\beta_{\perp}}{8\pi c(1-\beta_{\parallel})^{2}}\left[1\pm\frac{\cos\theta-\beta_{\parallel}}{1-\beta_{\parallel}\cos\theta}\right]_{\theta=0,\pi}.

(32)

This means that in the direction of the
z
axis only right-handed photons are emitted, and only left-handed ones are emitted in the opposite direction.

More detailed information can be obtained from the exact quantum equations (
15
) – (
18
). For
\theta=0,\pi
the argument of the Laguerre functions (
17
) vanishes and
I_{ss^{\prime}}(0)=\delta_{ss^{\prime}}
. Provided that
n^{\prime}<n
, the matrix elements (
16
) take the form

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-i(c_{1}c^{\prime}_{4}+c_{3}c^{\prime}_{2})\delta_{n-1,n^{\prime}}\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\delta_{k^{\prime}_{z},k_{z}-k\cos\theta},

(33)

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=i\langle\alpha_{1}\rangle,\quad\langle\alpha_{3}\rangle=0.

(34)

As we can see, the matrix elements are nonzero only for
n^{\prime}=n-1
and
s^{\prime}=s
and, consequently, for
l^{\prime}=l-1
. Thus, in the
\theta=0,\pi
directions, photons are emitted only at transitions
|n,l,\zeta\rangle\to|n-1,l-1,\zeta^{\prime}\rangle
. Decrease the quantum number
l
by one means that the angular momentum of these photons is
\hbar
, and the projection on the direction of radiation is either
\hbar
at
\theta=0
, or
-\hbar
at
\theta=\pi
.
Hence, the angular momentum of photons emitted in the direction of the
z
axis is represented only by spin. Their orbital momentum is zero. Which, in fact, is consistent with the classical representation (
30
), but not consistent with (

). Note again that the results of integration over the angles of the Eqs (
30
) and (

) are the same. The reasons for this discrepancy are discussed in the next section.

4
Canonical angular momentum

The disagreement between the quantum and classical results obtained in the previous section is due to the fact that the angular momentum density in classical electrodynamics is defined ambiguously. The straightforward application of
the Noether theorem to electromagnetic field yields the proper integral conservation laws.
In this case, the integrands can be transformed in such a way that the integral over the entire space does not change. In particular, the symmetrization of the energy-momentum tensor of the electromagnetic field is based on this
Belinfante 1939
,
Landau and Lifshitz 1975
.

When calculating the angular momentum flux in our paper
Epp and Guselnikova 2019
, we proceeded from the symmetrized energy-momentum tensor, which leads to the well-known expression for the angular momentum flux in the wave zone
Jackson 1999
,
Panofsky and Phillips 1962

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{L}}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{r^{2}}{c}\int(\bm{r}\times\bm{P}){\rm d}\Omega,

(35)

where

\bm{P}=\frac{c}{4\pi}(\bm{E}\times\bm{H})

is the Poynting vector, and
\bm{E}
and
\bm{H}
are the electric and magnetic fields respectively.

Meanwhile, an immediate consequence of Noether’s theorem is the conservation of the nonsymmetric canonical energy-momentum tensor
Jackson 1999
,
Landau and Lifshitz 1975

T^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}g^{\mu\alpha}F_{\alpha\rho}\partial^{\nu}A^{\rho}+\frac{1}{16\pi}g^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}

The corresponding density of the canonical angular momentum is represented by the third rank tensor
Bjorken and Drell 1965

M^{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\left(x^{\alpha}T^{\beta\gamma}-x^{\beta}T^{\alpha\gamma}\right)+\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(-A^{\alpha}F^{\beta\gamma}+A^{\beta}F^{\alpha\gamma}\right).

In this case, the angular momentum density of the electromagnetic field is given by the component
M^{0\beta\gamma}
or by the vector
M_{i}=\varepsilon_{ijk}M^{0jk}
, where
j,k=1,2,3
, and
\varepsilon_{ijk}
is the unit antisymmetric tensor of the third rank. Accordingly, the angular momentum flux density in the wave zone is equal to
c\bm{M}
, and the total flux is determined by the integral

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{L}}{{\rm d}t}=cr^{2}\int\bm{M}{\rm d}\Omega.

(36)

The vector
\bm{M}
in the Coulomb gauge
\bm{\nabla}\bm{A}=0
can be represented as the sum
Jackson 1999
,
Bliokh et al. 2013
,
Afanasev et al. 2022

\bm{M}=\bm{\mathcal{L}}+\bm{\mathcal{S}},

(37)

where
\bm{\mathcal{L}}
and
\bm{\mathcal{S}}
are the “orbital” and “spin” parts respectively

\bm{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{1}{4\pi c}E_{i}(\bm{r}\times\bm{\nabla})A_{i},\quad\bm{\mathcal{S}}=\frac{1}{4\pi c}(\bm{E}\times\bm{A}).

(38)

The result of integration over the solid angle in Eqs (
35
) and (
36
) is the same, however the integrands differ significantly.
Indeed, let us calculate the angular momentum flux due to (
36
), using the expressions for the Fourier components of the electric field and vector potential obtained in
Epp and Guselnikova 2019

\displaystyle A_{\nu r}

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{r}J_{\nu}(\xi),\quad A_{\nu\theta}=\frac{B}{r}\cot\theta J_{\nu}(\xi),

\displaystyle A_{\nu\varphi}

\displaystyle=i\frac{B\beta}{r}J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\xi),\quad B=ee^{i(kr+\nu\varphi-\nu\pi/2)}

(39)

\displaystyle E_{\nu r}

\displaystyle=0,\quad E_{\nu\theta}=\frac{iB\nu\omega\cot\theta}{rc}J_{\nu}(\xi),\quad E_{\nu\varphi}=-\frac{B\nu\omega\beta}{rc}J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\xi).

When using the vector potential, it suffices to restrict ourselves to quantities of the first order in powers of
1/r
. In the Coulomb gauge one should put
A_{\nu r}=0
.
Let us substitute these values into (
38
). In order to find the time-averaged values for the
\nu
-th harmonic, we need to make the substitution
\bm{E}\to\bm{E}^{*}_{\nu},\,\bm{A}\to\bm{A}_{\nu}

\bm{\mathcal{L}}_{\nu}
has only a transverse component (

), while the vector

is directed radially (
\bm{\hat{r}}=\bm{r}/r

\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\nu}=\frac{e_{0}^{2}\nu\omega_{0}\beta}{2\pi r^{2}c^{2}}\cot\theta J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\xi)J_{\nu}(\xi)\bm{\hat{r}}.

The
z
-component of the angular momentum flux is equal to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\nu z}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{0}^{2}\nu^{2}\omega_{0}}{4\pi r^{2}c^{2}}\left[\beta^{2}{J^{\prime}}^{2}_{\nu}(\xi)+\cot^{2}\theta J^{2}_{\nu}(\xi)-\frac{2\beta}{\nu}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin\theta}J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\xi)J_{\nu}(\xi)\right],

(40)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\nu z}

\displaystyle=\frac{e_{0}^{2}\nu\omega_{0}\beta}{2\pi r^{2}c^{2}}\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{\sin\theta}J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\xi)J_{\nu}(\xi)

(41)

Substituting these expressions into (
36
) and (
37
), we obtain the total flux of the

-component of angular momentum

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}L_{z}}{{\rm d}t}=\frac{e_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}}{2c}\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}\nu^{2}\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\left[\beta^{2}{J^{\prime}}^{2}_{\nu}(\xi)+\cot^{2}\theta J^{2}_{\nu}(\xi)\right]\sin\theta{\rm d}\theta

in full accordance with Eq. (

) obtained from the probability of photon emission in the limit

.

In the direction
\theta=0
, the
z
-component of the “orbital” angular momentum is equal to zero, and in the

\bm{\mathcal{S}}_{\nu}\Big|_{\theta=0}=\frac{e_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}\beta^{2}}{8\pi r^{2}c^{2}}\delta_{1\nu}\bm{\hat{r}}.

Wherein, the average angular momentum per one photon is equal to
\hbar
. In the direction
\theta=\pi/2
, which lies in the plane of the electron orbit, on the contrary, the “spin” angular momentum
{\mathcal{S}}_{\nu z}
is equal to zero, and the “orbital” angular momentum
{\mathcal{L}}_{\nu z}
takes on a maximum value. The absence of “spin” angular momentum is due to the fact that in the classical limit the probability of emission of left-handed photons is equal to the probability of emission of right-handed ones in this direction. In this case, the “orbital” angular momentum is associated with the momentum transfer of the electromagnetic field.

5
Conclusion

Using the solution to the Dirac equation for an electron in a uniform magnetic field, we have found exact expressions for the probability of emission of a photon with a certain projection of its angular momentum onto the direction of the magnetic field. We have also found the asymptotic expression for the angular momentum flux density in radiation field in the limit
\hbar\to 0
. The expressions obtained in this way do not coincide with the previously found equations for the angular momentum flux density derived from the symmetrized energy-momentum tensor of the electromagnetic field.
This discrepancy is explained by the fact that the density of the angular momentum of the field and, accordingly, the flux of the angular momentum are determined ambiguously. For comparison, we found the angular momentum flux in radiation using the canonical (not symmetrized) energy-momentum tensor and showed that the formulas obtained in this way coincide with the classical limit of equations for the probability of photon emission. In this case, the flux of angular momentum integrated over the radiation directions following from the canonical and symmetrized energy-momentum tensor, as expected, coincide.

. This problem is closely
related to the determination of the angular momentum density of the field and to the separation of spin and orbital degrees of freedom.
Obviously, the applicability of certain formulas to specific physical measurements is determined by the specific conditions of the light–matter interactions.

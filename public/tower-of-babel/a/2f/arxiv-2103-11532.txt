Faithfullness of geometric action of skein algebras Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Classification: Primary 57N10. Secondary 57M25. Key words and phrases: Kauffman bracket skein module, geometric action.
Thang T. Q. Lê

Abstract
. We show that the action of the Kauffman bracket skein algebra of a surface Σ \Sigma on the skein module of the handlebody bounded by Σ \Sigma is faithful if and only if the quantum parameter is not a root of 1.

Introduction

1.1.
Kauffman bracket skein modules and algebras

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be a commutative domain with unit, equipped with a distinguished invertible element
q
. Suppose
M
is an oriented 3-manifold with possibly non-empty boundary
\partial M
. The skein module
\mathscr{S}(M)
, introduced by Przytycki
[
Pr
]
and Turaev
[
Tu1
,
Tu2
]
, is the
{\mathcal{R}}
-module generated by isotopy classes of framed unoriented links in
M
modulo the Kauffman relations
[
Kau
]

(1)

\displaystyle=\ q\raisebox{-8.0pt}{{\includegraphics[height]{draws/resoP.eps}}}+q^{-1}\raisebox{-8.0pt}{{\includegraphics[height]{draws/resoN.eps}}}

(2)

\displaystyle=\ (-q^{2}-q^{-2})\raisebox{-8.0pt}{{\includegraphics[height]{draws/empty.eps}}}.

For details, see Section

.

For an oriented surface
\Sigma
let
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma):=\mathscr{S}(\Sigma\times(0,1))
. Then
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
has an
{\mathcal{R}}
-algebra structure where the product of two framed links
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
is the result of stacking
\alpha_{1}
above
\alpha_{2}
. The skein algebras and modules have played an important role in low-dimensional topology and quantum topology and they serve as a bridge between classical topology and quantum topology. The skein modules have connections to the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character variety
[
Bul
,
PS1
]
, the quantum group of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, the Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev topological quantum field theory
[
BHMV
]
, the quantum Teichmüller spaces, see e.g.
[
CF
,
Kas
,
BW1
,
Le2
]
, and the quantum cluster algebra theory
[
Mu
]
.

1.2.
Main result

When
\Sigma
is the boundary
\partial M
of an oriented 3-manifold
M
, there is an action of
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
on
\mathscr{S}(M)
given by
\alpha*\beta=\alpha\cup\beta
whenever
\alpha
and
\beta
are framed links, see the details in Section
3
. This action plays an important role in the theory. For example, various topological quantum field theories are constructed based on this type of actions, see e.g.
[
BHMV
,
BW3
]
. An important question is when this action is faithful. Even when
M
is a handlebody, the question is important and was open.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
H
is handlebody of genus
g\geq 1
and
\Sigma
is the boundary of
H
. Assume that the ground ring
{\mathcal{R}}
is a commutative domain with unit and equipped with a distinguished invertible element
q
.

The action of
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
on
\mathscr{S}(H)
is faithful if and only if
q
is not a root of 1, ie if and only if
q^{n}\neq 1
for all positive integers
n
.

The result has already found application in the work of J. Cooke and P. Samuelson in their study of double affine Hecke algebras
[
CS
]
.

To prove that when
q
is a root of unity the action of
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
on
\mathscr{S}(H)
is not faithful we rely on a result of
[
BW2
,
Le1
]
which states that if one threads a framed knot in
H
by the Chebyshev polynomial
T_{N}
of type 2, where
N
is the order of
q
, then the result is transparent with respect to disjoint union, see Section
4
. This allows to construct a non-zero element in
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
which acts trivially on
\mathscr{S}(H)
.

To show that when
q
is not root of 1 the action of
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
on
\mathscr{S}(H)
is faithful we use filtrations of
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
and
\mathscr{S}(H)
coming from a pair of pants decomposition of
\Sigma
and the Dehn-Thurston coordinates of curves on
\Sigma
. The action of the corresponding associated algebra on the associated module is much easier to work with and can be calculated explicitly in “stable” cases which are enough to prove the faithfulness of the action. R. Detcherry informed me that he has an independent proof of the faithfulness for the case when
1-q^{n}
is invertible in
{\mathcal{R}}
for all positive integers
n
.

Organization of the paper

We recall the definitions and basic facts of skein modules in Section
2
. In Section
3
we introduce filtrations on skein algebras and recall a result from
[
FKL
]
calculating the product of triangular simple diagrams, introduced in
[
PS2

2.
Skein modules and algebras

We recall here the definition of the Kauffman bracket skein module and algebra and their basic properties, including a description of
the basis in the case of surfaces.

2.1.
Ground ring
{\mathcal{R}}

Throughout the paper the ground ring
{\mathcal{R}}
is a commutative domain with unity 1, equipped with a distinguished invertible element
q
. The reader might think of
{\mathcal{R}}
as the field
\mathbb{C}
of complex number and
q
is a non-zero complex number, or
{\mathcal{R}}=\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
, where
\mathbb{Z}
is the set of integers. We use
\mathbb{N}
to denote the set of non-negative integers. Thus our
\mathbb{N}
contains 0.

2.2.
Skein module

Let
M
be an oriented 3-manifold, with possibly non-empty boundary.
A
framed link
in
M
is an embedding of a disjoint union of oriented annuli in
M
.
By convention the empty set is considered as a framed link with 0 components and is isotopic only to itself.
The orientation of a component of a framed link corresponds to choosing a preferred side to the annulus.

The Kauffman bracket skein module of
M
, denoted by
\mathscr{S}(M)
, is the
{\mathcal{R}}
-module freely spanned by all isotopy classes of framed links in
M
subject to the following
relations

(3)

\displaystyle=\ q\raisebox{-8.0pt}{{\includegraphics[height]{draws/resoP.eps}}}+q^{-1}\raisebox{-8.0pt}{{\includegraphics[height]{draws/resoN.eps}}}

(4)

\displaystyle=\ (-q^{2}-q^{-2})\raisebox{-8.0pt}{{\includegraphics[height]{draws/empty.eps}}}.

Here the framed links in each expression are identical outside the balls pictured in the diagrams. The skein relations were introduced by Kauffman
[
Kau
]
where he showed that when
M=\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the three-space, one has
\mathscr{S}(M)={\mathcal{R}}
, and the value of a framed link is in
{\mathcal{R}}
is the framed version of the Jones polynomial
[
Jo
]
. Kauffman bracket skein modules for oriented 3-manifolds were introduced independently by Turaev and Przytycki
[
Pr
,
Tu1
]
in an attempt to generalize the Jones polynomial to general 3-manifolds. Turaev
[
Tu2
]
used the algebra structure, see below, to quantize the Goldman bracket of the surface.

2.3.
Skein algebra of surfaces

Suppose
F
is an oriented surface, with possibly non-empty boundary. Define
\mathscr{S}(F)=\mathscr{S}(F\times(0,1))
. The
{\mathcal{R}}
-module
\mathscr{S}(F)
has an algebra structure coming from stacking. More precisely, the product of two links is defined by placing the first link above the second in the direction given by the interval
(0,1)
. The empty link is the unit.

If
D
is a non-oriented link diagram on
F
then a regular neighborhood of
D
is a framed link, defined uniquely up to isotopy. We identify
D
with the isotopy class of the framed link it defines. Any framed link in
F\times(0,1)
is isotopic to a framed link determined by a link diagram. A
simple diagram
on
F
is a link diagram with no crossings and no
trivial loops
, i.e. a curve bounding a disk. We consider the empty set as a simple diagram which is isotopic only to itself.

Let
B(F)
be the set of all isotopy classes of simple diagrams on
F
. It is known

[
PS1
]
that
\mathscr{S}(F)
is a free
{\mathcal{R}}
-module with basis
B(F)
.
Every non-zero
\alpha\in\mathscr{S}(F)
has a unique
standard presentation
, which is a finite sum

(5)

\alpha=\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}\alpha_{j},

where
c_{j}\neq 0
and
\alpha_{j}\in B(F)
are distinct.

If a crossing of a link diagram
D
is as in the left hand side of (
3
), then
the first (resp. second) diagram on the right hand side will be referred to as the
+1
(resp.
-1
)
smooth resolution
of the left hand side diagram at the crossing.

Suppose
D
is a link diagram on
F
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of all crossings. For every map
\sigma:\mathcal{C}\to\{\pm 1\}
let
D_{\sigma}
be the non-crossing diagram obtained from
D
by doing the
\sigma(c)
smooth resolution at every crossing
c
.
Let
|\sigma|=\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\sigma(c)
and
l(\sigma)
be the number of trivial components of
D_{\sigma}
. Let
D_{\sigma}^{\prime}
be the simple diagram obtained from
D_{\sigma}
by removing all the trivial components. Using the two skein relations, we have

(6)

D=\sum_{\sigma:\mathcal{C}\to\{\pm 1\}}q^{|\sigma|}(-q^{2}-q^{-2})^{l(\sigma)}\,D_{\sigma}^{\prime}\quad\text{in }\mathscr{S}(F).

From the defining relations, one can easily derive the following relation:

3.
Curves on surfaces and coordinates

We recall how to coordinatize the system of simple diagrams on surfaces in both cases when the surface has boundary and when the surface is closed. We introduce filtrations on the skein algebra of a surface using a pair of pants decomposition, and explain how the product in the associated algebra look like for a class of so-called triangular curves.

3.1.
Edge coordinates and Thurston form on compact non-closed surfaces

Suppose
F
is a compact connected oriented surface with non-empty boundary
\partial F
and
\chi(F)<0
, where
\chi(F)
is the Euler characteristic.
Then
\mathring{F}=F\setminus\partial F
is known as a
hyperbolic punctured surface
. We will now introduce the notion of
\partial F
-triangulation
of
F
, which is essentially the ideal triangulation of
\mathring{F}
.

A
\partial F
-arc
is a proper embedding
[0,1]\hookrightarrow F
.
Isotopies of
\partial F
-arcs
are considered in the class of
\partial F
-arcs. A
\partial F
-arc is
trivial
if it can be isotoped into a small neighborhood of the boundary
\partial F
. A
\partial F
-triangulation

\mathcal{E}
of
F
is a maximal collection of pairwise disjoint and pairwise non-isotopic non-trivial
\partial F
-arcs. An element
a\in\mathcal{E}
is called an
edge
of the triangulation.
It is easy to show that
|\mathcal{E}|=-3\chi(F)
. A connected component of
F\setminus\left(\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal{E}}e\right)
is called a
face
of the triangulation. An edge
e\in\mathcal{E}
is a
side
of a face
\tau
if the topological closure of
\tau
contains
e
. An edge is
self-folded
if it is the side of only one face.
There are always triangulations of
F
without self-folded edges. For simplicity we will assume that the triangulation
\mathcal{E}
does not have any self-folded edge, although all the facts in this paper can be easily adapted to the case when the triangulation has self-folded edges.

For an edge
e\in\mathcal{E}
and a simple diagram
\alpha
on
F
the
geometric intersection number

I(e,\alpha)
is the minimum of the cardinalities of
\alpha^{\prime}\cap e
, where
\alpha^{\prime}
run the set of simple diagrams isotopic to
\alpha
.

Three distinct edges
a,b,c\in\mathcal{E}
are
triangular
if they are sides of a face. Recall that
B(F)
is the set of isotopy classes of simple diagrams on
F
. We use the notation
X^{Y}
for the set of all functions from
Y
to
X
.
Define the map

\nu:B(F)\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{E}}\quad\text{
by}\ \nu(\alpha)(e)=I(\alpha,e).

It is known that
\nu
is injective, and its image is the submonoid
\Lambda=\Lambda(F,\mathcal{E})\subset\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{E}}
consisting of functions
f:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
whenever
a,b,c
are triangular, we have

f(a)+f(b)+f(c)
is even and

f(a)\leq f(b)+f(c)
. For details, see e.g.
[
Mat
]
. We will denote

S:\Lambda(F,\mathcal{E})\to B(F)

the inverse function of
\nu
.

Associated to the triangulation
\mathcal{E}
is an anti-symmetric function

Q:\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z},

known as the Thurston form, which determines the Poisson structure on the Teichmüller space of
\mathring{F}
. For details see e.g.
[
BW1
,
FoG
,
FST
,
Pen
]
. Although the exact definition is not important for us, let us recall
the definition of
Q
. Each face
\tau
of the triangulation is planar, hence there is defind a cyclic order of the 3 sides of
\tau
, using the counter clockwise orientation of the boundary of
\tau
. For two edges
a,b\in\mathcal{E}
and a face
\tau
define
Q_{\tau}(a,b)=0
if one of
a,b
is not a side of
\tau
, and otherwise define

Q_{\tau}(a,b)=1
or
-1
according as
b
follows
a
along the cyclic order or not. Then

Q(a,b)=\sum_{\tau:\ \text{ faces }}Q_{\tau}(a,b).

The bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{Q}:\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathbb{Z}
defined by

(8)

\langle\mathbf{n},{\mathbf{m}}\rangle_{Q}:=\sum_{a,b\in\mathcal{E}}Q(a,b)\mathbf{n}(a){\mathbf{m}}(b)

plays an important role in the theory of Teichmüller spaces of
\mathring{F}
.

3.2.
Dehn-Thurston coordinates for curves on closed surfaces

Suppose
\Sigma
is a closed oriented surface of genus
g>1
. Then
\Sigma
is the double of a compact surface
F
with
\chi(F)=1-g
with non-empty boundary
\partial F
. This means
\Sigma=F\sqcup F^{\prime}/\sim
, where
F^{\prime}
is a copy of
F
and
x\sim x^{\prime}
for
x\neq x^{\prime}
if and only if
x\in\partial F
and
x^{\prime}
is the corresponding point of
x
in
\partial F^{\prime}
. By a
Dehn-Thurston datum
of
\Sigma
we mean a pair
(F,\mathcal{E})
, where
\Sigma
is the double of
F
and
\mathcal{E}
is a
\partial F
-triangulation of
F
.

For each
e\in\mathcal{E}
let
e^{\prime}\subset F^{\prime}
be the corresponding
\partial F^{\prime}
-arc of
F^{\prime}
.
Then
{\tilde{e}}=e\cup e^{\prime}
is a simple closed curve in
\Sigma
. The collection
{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}}=\{{\tilde{e}}\mid e\in\mathcal{E}\}
is a
pair of pants decomposition
of
\Sigma
.

Recall that
B(\Sigma)
is the set of isotopy classes of simple diagrams on
\Sigma
. The Dehn-Thurston datum
(F,\mathcal{E})
gives rise to the Dehn-Thurston coordinate map

\mu:B(\Sigma)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Gamma\subset\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{E}\cup\mathcal{E}^{*}},

where
\mathcal{E}^{*}=\{e^{*}\mid e\in\mathcal{E}\}
is a copy of
\mathcal{E}
. The set
\Gamma
is a submonoid of
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathcal{E}\cap\mathcal{E}^{*}}
. By definition
\mu(\alpha)(e)=I(\alpha,{\tilde{e}})
for
e\in\mathcal{E}
. The definition of
\mu(\alpha)(e^{*})
for
e^{*}\in\mathcal{E}^{*}
, called the
twist coordinate corresponding to

{\tilde{e}}
, is more involved, and since we we don’t need the exact definition we refer the reader to
[
LS
]
for details. We will recall here a couple of properties of the Dehn-Thurston coordinates that we will need later.

Firstly suppose
\alpha\subset F
. One can consider
\alpha
is a simple diagram on
F
and define
\nu(\alpha):\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{N}
as in Subsection
3.1
. Then the restriction of
\mu(\alpha)
onto
\mathcal{E}
is equal to
\nu(\alpha)
while the restriction of
\mu(\alpha)
onto
\mathcal{E}^{*}
is the 0 function. In other words,

\mu(\alpha)(e)=\nu(\alpha)(e)
for all
e\in\mathcal{E}
and
\mu(\alpha)(e^{*})=0
for all
e^{*}\in\mathcal{E}^{*}
, i.e. all the twist coordinates of
\alpha
are 0.

Secondly suppose
\alpha
is a simple diagram on
\Sigma
and
e\in\mathcal{E}
such that
I(\alpha,{\tilde{e}})=n>0
.
One defines a simple digram
T_{e}(\alpha)
as follows. A closed tubular neighborhood
A({\tilde{e}})
of the simple closed curve
{\tilde{e}}
is homeomorphic to an annulus
S^{1}\times[0,1]
where
{\tilde{e}}
is identified with
S^{1}\times\{1/2\}
. A segment of the form
z\times[0,1]
, where
z\in S^{1}
, is called a vertical line in
A({\tilde{e}})
.
We assume that
\alpha
intersects
A({\tilde{e}})
in
n
vertical lines.
Then
T_{e}(\alpha)
is the the result of twisting the
n
vertical lines along

{\tilde{e}}
by
2\pi/n
, see Figure
1
.
The twist coordinates are defined so that

(9)

\mu(T_{e}(\alpha))(e^{*})=\mu(\alpha)(e^{*})+1.

Figure 1
.
The map
T_{e}
. Here
n=4
.The cylinder is a tubular neighborhood
A({\tilde{e}})
of
{\tilde{e}}
.
The red lines are part of
\partial F
.

Let
S:\Gamma\to B(\Sigma)
be the inverse of
\mu
.

Following
[
PS2
]
, a simple diagram
\alpha
is called
\mathcal{E}
-triangular
if it can be obtained from a simple diagram
\beta
in
F
by repeatedly applying the map
T_{e}
or
(T_{e})^{-1}
, with possibly many
e\in\mathcal{E}
. In other words,
\alpha
is triangular if

•

whenever
a,b,c\in\mathcal{E}
are triangular one has
\mu(\alpha)(a)\leq\mu(\alpha)(b)+\mu(\alpha)(c)
, and

•

whenever
\mu(\alpha)(e)=0
one has
\mu(\alpha)(e^{*})=0
.

We will see later that the product of triangular diagrams behaves well in the filtration defined by the system
\{{\tilde{e}}\}
.

3.3.
Torus

Suppose
\Sigma
is the torus, i.e.
g=1
. The skein algebra
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
in this case is well understood; it is a subalgebra of a quantum torus
[
FrG
]
and many of its properties can be deduced from this fact.

Note that
\Sigma
is the double of an annulus
F=S^{1}\times[0,1]
. We excluded this case in the previous discussion since technically there is no triangulation of
F
. However, in order to uniformly treat all closed surfaces, we use the following convention. A
\partial F
-triangulation

\mathcal{E}=\{e\}
of
F
consists of one
\partial F
-arc
e
of the form
\{x\}\times[0,1]
where
x\in S^{1}
. The simple closed curve
{\tilde{e}}
will be called a
meridian
of
\Sigma
, while the curve
S^{1}\times\{1/2\}
on the annulus
F
will be called a
longitude
of
\Sigma
. Again a Dehn-Thurston datum of
\Sigma
is the pair
(F,\mathcal{E})
.

The set
B(F)
of isotopy classes of simple diagrams on
F
is parameterized by
\Lambda=\mathbb{N}
, where
S(m)
is the diagram consists of
m
parallel copies of the longitudes. The anti-symmetric form
Q:\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{Z}
is defined by
Q(e,e)=0
.

The twist
T_{e}
is defined exactly as in the previous case.
The set
B(\Sigma)
is parameterized by

\Gamma=\{(n,t)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{Z}\mid\text{if}\ n=0\ \text{then}\ t\geq 0\}.

Here
S(n,t)=(T_{e})^{t}(S(n))
if
n>0
and
S(0,t)
is the disjoint union of
t
copies of the meridian
{\tilde{e}}
. The simple diagram
S(n,t)
is defined to be
triangular
if
n>0
or
n=0
and
t=0
.

3.4.
Action of
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
on
\mathscr{S}(H)

Suppose
\Sigma
is the boundary of an compact oriented 3-manifold
M
.
We identify a closed tubular neighborhood
\mathcal{N}(\partial M)
of
\partial M
with
\Sigma\times[0,1]
so that
\partial M=\Sigma\times\{0\}
. Every framed link in
M
can be isotoped off
\mathcal{N}(\partial M)
.
If
\alpha
is a framed link in
\Sigma\times(0,1)
and
\beta
is a framed link in
M\setminus\mathcal{N}(\partial M)
then define
\alpha*\beta:=\alpha\cup\beta
as an element of
\mathscr{S}(M)
. This defines an action of
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
on
\mathscr{S}(M)
.

Suppose now
F
is a compact oriented connected surface with non-empty boundary
\partial F
and with Euler characteristic
\chi(F)=1-g
. Then
H=F\times[0,1]
is a handlebody of genus
g
. By definition, we identify
\mathscr{S}(H)
with
{\mathscr{S}(F)}
.

Note that
\Sigma:=\partial H
is the double of
F
. The embedding
\iota:F\hookrightarrow\Sigma
gives rise to an algebra embedding
\iota_{*}:{\mathscr{S}(F)}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
.
For
\alpha\in\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
and
\beta\in{\mathscr{S}(F)}=\mathscr{S}(H)
, from
the definition one has

(10)

\alpha*\beta=[\alpha\,\iota_{*}(\beta)]*1.

3.5.
Filtrations

Return to a closed surface of genus
g\geq 1
, equipped with a Dehn-Thurston datum
(F,\mathcal{E})
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
let
F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))\subset\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
be the
{\mathcal{R}}
-submodule spanned by
\alpha\in B(\Sigma)
such that

\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}I(\alpha,{\tilde{e}})\leq k.

Then
\{F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is an exhaustive increasing filtration of
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
, compatible with the product structure. This type of filtrations of skein aglebras and modules are used in many works, for example
[
Le1
,
Mac
,
FKL
,
PS2
]
.

We consider
\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
as an
\mathbb{N}
-filtered algebra with the filtration
\{F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. Let
\mathrm{Gr}\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
denote the
\mathbb{N}
-graded associated algebra. By definition

\mathrm{Gr}\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))/F_{k-1}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)),

where by convention
F_{-1}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))
is the 0 module. Suppose
x\in\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)
is non-zero. Then there is
k\in\mathbb{N}
, called the
degree of
x
, such that
x\in F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))\setminus F_{k-1}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))
.
Let

\bar{x}\in F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))/F_{k-1}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))\subset\mathrm{Gr}\mathscr{S}(\Sigma)

be the image of
x
under the natural projection
F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))\to F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))/F_{k-1}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))
.

Let
B_{k}(\Sigma)\subset B(\Sigma)
be the subset consisting of isotopy classes of simple diagrams
\alpha
with
\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}I(\alpha,e)\leq k
. Then

F_{k}(\mathscr{S}(\Sigma))
is free
{\mathcal{R}}

4.2.
A lemma on root of 1

The following lemma is where we use the fact that
q
is not a root of 1.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
r,k
be positive integers, and
q\in{\mathcal{R}}
is not a root of 1. Suppose that

c_{i}\in{\mathcal{R}}
and
\mathbf{n}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
for
i=1,2,\dots,k
, where the
\mathbf{n}_{i}
are pairwise distinct.
Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{r}
be a submonoid generating a subgroup of rank
r
in
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. If

(14)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}q^{\mathbf{n}_{i}\cdot{\mathbf{m}}}=0

for all
{\mathbf{m}}\in\Lambda
then
c_{i}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

For a non-zero element
x
in the standard Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{r}
let

x^{\perp}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\mid x\cdot y=0\},

which is a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
of dimension
r-1
. The finite collection
(\mathbf{n}_{i}-\mathbf{n}_{j})^{\perp},i\neq j
, cannot cover the whole
\mathbb{R}^{r}
. The condition on the rank of
\Lambda
shows that there is
{\mathbf{m}}\in\Lambda
not belonging to any of
(\mathbf{n}_{i}-{\mathbf{m}}_{j})^{\perp}
. This means the numbers
s_{i}:=\mathbf{n}_{i}\cdot{\mathbf{m}}
are pairwise distinct.

Replace
{\mathbf{m}}
by
j{\mathbf{m}}
for
j=0,1,\dots,k-1
in (
14
), we get

\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}(s_{i})^{j}=0,\qquad j=0,1,\dots,k-1.

One can solve for
c_{i}
from the above linear system. The determinant is the
Vandermonde determinant, which is non-zero since the
s_{i}
are disctinct. It follows that all
c_{i}
are 0.
∎

Mixing in an anharmonic potential well
Matías Moreno, Paola Rioseco, Hanne Van Den Bosch

Abstract
. We prove phase-space mixing for solutions to Liouville’s equation for integrable systems. Under a natural non-harmonicity condition, we obtain weak convergence of the distribution function with rate ⟨ time ⟩ − 1 \langle\mathrm{time}\rangle^{-1} . In one dimension, we also study the case where this condition fails at a certain energy, showing that mixing still holds but with a slower rate. When the condition holds and functions have higher regularity, the rate can be faster.

Introduction

We study Liouville’s equation in action-angle coordinates

Liouville’s theorem guarantees that, if the motion of a single particle in this system is integrable, there exist coodinates

In classical mechanics, this occurs for instance in a potential well in one space-dimension

Theorem 1.1

Let

2.
The one-dimensional case.

The main tool in the proof of this theorem is the
vector field

W:=\omega^{\prime}(k)t\partial_{q}+\partial_{k}.

(5)

A straightforward calculation shows that
W
commutes with the Liouville operator

L:=\partial_{t}+\omega(k)\partial_{q}.

Therefore, if
f
solves the transport equation (
1
), the same goes for
W^{n}f
(and
|W^{n}f|
), for any

and thus,

\iint\left|W^{n}f\right|(t,q,k)g(k)\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}q=\iint\left|W^{n}f_{0}\right|(q,k)g(k)\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}q,

(6)

for sufficiently regular functions
f
and
g
.
As usual, we will use this property to obtain time-indepent bounds.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f
denote the solution to (
1
) with initial data
f_{0}\in L^{1}
and fix
\phi\in L^{\infty}
. Assume that either
f
or
\phi
have compact support in
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times K
.
Then, provided all terms on the right-hand-side are finite,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2\pi}{t}\left(\left\|\frac{\bar{\phi}}{\omega^{\prime}}\partial_{k}f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|\bar{f}_{0}\partial_{k}\frac{\phi}{\omega^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)

(7)

Remark 3
.

The hypotheses on
f
,
\phi
and
\omega
of Theorem
1.1
imply directly that the terms in the upper bound are indeed finite. Since it is sufficient to prove the decay for large values of

1.1

Remark 4
.

The hypothesis on compact support is only needed to ensure the absence of boundary terms when integrating by parts. It can be weakened by adding the value(s) of
\frac{\bar{f}_{0}\bar{\phi}}{\omega^{\prime}}
at
\partial K
to the right-hand-side, provided these values are well-defined.

Proof.

First, note that as a limiting case of (
6
) or by using the exact time evolution and a change of variables

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{0}(k)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{0}(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{0}(q-\omega(k)t,k)\,\mathrm{d}q=:\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t,q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q.

We insert this in the expression that we need to estimate and use the fundamental theorem of calculus to write

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-f(t,q^{\prime},k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{q^{\prime}}^{q}\partial_{q}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\int_{K}\partial_{q}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\right|\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}q.

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\int_{K}\left[\partial_{q}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\right]\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\right|\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}q.

To obtain this inequality, we first used Fubini’s theorem to perform the
k

\tilde{q}
(which requires inserting the absolute value). The last line is just the observation that the

-dependence has disappeared from the integrand.

We now use

5

\partial_{q}=(\omega^{\prime}(k)t)^{-1}(W-\partial_{k})
. The first term will have the required form to apply (
6

) to bring the second term in this form as well. This gives

\displaystyle\int_{K}\left[\partial_{q}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\right]\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\qquad=t^{-1}\int_{K}\left[Wf\right](t,\tilde{q},k)\frac{\phi(q,k)}{\omega^{\prime}(k)}\,\mathrm{d}k+t^{-1}\int_{K}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\partial_{k}\left[\frac{\phi(q,k)}{\omega^{\prime}(k)}\right]\,\mathrm{d}k.

Inserting this in the bound, we found

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq t^{-1}\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\left|Wf\right|(t,\tilde{q},k)}{|\omega^{\prime}(k)|}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\phi(q,k)\right|\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\qquad+t^{-1}\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{k}\frac{\phi(q,k)}{\omega^{\prime}(k)}\right|\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}k.

In both terms we can apply (

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq t^{-1}\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\left|\partial_{k}f_{0}\right|(\tilde{q},k)}{|\omega^{\prime}(k)|}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\phi(q,k)\right|\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\qquad+t^{-1}\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{0}(\tilde{q},k)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{k}\frac{\phi(q,k)}{\omega^{\prime}(k)}\right|\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}k.

This can be rewritten in terms of the averages over

to give (

2.1.
Localization argument

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
. We use the explicit rate of decay and the expression for the upper bound obtained in Proposition
2.1
allows for extensions when
\omega^{\prime}(k)
vanishes at some
energies
in the support of
\phi
.
We use a simple localization argument to treat the case where
\omega^{\prime}(k)
vanishes linearly.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
0<\epsilon<1
to be fixed later. We fix a smooth cutoff function
\chi
with support in
(-1,1)
, values in
[0,1]
, and such that
\chi\equiv 1
in
[-1/2,1/2]
.
We define
\chi_{i,\epsilon}:=\chi(\frac{k-k_{i}}{\epsilon})
and

\eta_{\epsilon}:=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-\chi_{i,\epsilon})
.
Then, we write

\phi(q,k)=\eta_{\epsilon}(k)\phi(q,k)+(1-\eta_{\epsilon}(k))\phi(q,k)
.
Note that
\eta_{\epsilon}(k)\phi(q,k)
satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition
2.1
.
Thus, (
7
) gives

\displaystyle\left|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-\bar{f}_{0}(k))\eta_{\epsilon}(k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{2\pi}{t}\left\|\frac{\eta_{\epsilon}}{\omega^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\left\|\bar{\phi}\partial_{k}f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|\bar{f}_{0}\partial_{k}\phi\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{2\pi}{t}\left\|\partial_{k}\left(\frac{\eta_{\epsilon}}{\omega^{\prime}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\phi\bar{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}.

Now, we need to extract the
\epsilon
-dependence from the
L^{\infty}
-norms. Since
\omega^{\prime\prime}(k_{i})\neq 0
, for some
C>0
and all
\epsilon\leq 1
, we have

\inf_{\supp(\eta_{\epsilon})\cap\supp(f)}|\omega^{\prime}(k)|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{C}.

This gives the bounds

\left\|\frac{\eta_{\epsilon}}{\omega^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{\epsilon},\qquad\left\|\partial_{k}\left(\frac{\eta_{\epsilon}}{\omega^{\prime}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{\epsilon^{2}}.

We have obtained

\left|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-\bar{f}(k))\eta_{\epsilon}(k)\phi(q,k)\right|\leq\frac{C}{t\epsilon^{2}}.

(8)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-\bar{f}_{0}(k))(1-\eta_{\epsilon}(k))\phi(q,k)\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{+}}(1-\eta_{\epsilon}(k))\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(t,q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\epsilon\left\|\bar{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

We sum with (
8
), evaluate at some
T>1
and pick
\epsilon=T^{-1/3}
, to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(T,q,k)-\bar{f}(k))\phi(q,k)\right|\leq CT^{-1/3},

since for small
T
, both terms are bounded, this implies the result.
∎

2.2.
Improved decay

If the initial condition is more regular, we can improve the estimate on the decay.
To this end, we use the following
L^{1}
-version of Poincaré’s inequality.

Lemma 2.2

(Poincaré’s inequality)
.

Assume that
g:\mathbb{T}\mapsto{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}
is a periodic function of class
C^{l}
and
g(x)=0
for some
x\in[0,2\pi)
. Then, for all
l\in{\mathord{\mathbb{N}}}
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g(q)|\,\mathrm{d}q\leq(\pi)^{l}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{(l)}(q)|\,\mathrm{d}q.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x=0
.
Then,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}|g(q)|\,\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|\int_{0}^{s}g^{\prime}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\right|\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|g^{\prime}(r)\right|\int_{r}^{\pi}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|g^{\prime}(r)\right|\,\mathrm{d}r.

Treating the contribution to the
L^{1}
-norm of the interval
[\pi,2\pi]
analogously, we find that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g(q)|\,\mathrm{d}q\leq\pi\int_{\mathbb{T}}|g^{\prime}(q)|\,\mathrm{d}q.

For the case
l\geq 2
, we proceed by induction. By periodicity
\int_{\mathbb{T}}g^{(l-1)}=0
, so
g^{(l-1)}(x)=0
for some
x\in\mathbb{T}
, and we can iterate the argument.
∎

As a consequence, we can obtain a faster rate of decay for more regular initial data and observables. For the sake of readability, we assume that the support of is
\phi
compact (bounded away from the boundary of
K
), though it is possible to relax this to suitable decay of the functions and their derivatives.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

As in the proof of Proposition
2.1
, we bound

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{\tilde{q}}\int_{K}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\right|\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}q.

We then use Lemma
2.2

to insert
l-1
additional derivatives:

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\pi^{l-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\partial_{\tilde{q}}^{l}\int_{K}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\right|\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}q

\displaystyle\leq t^{-l}\pi^{l-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\int_{K}\left(\frac{W-\partial_{k}}{\omega^{\prime}(k)}\right)^{l}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\right|\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}\,\mathrm{d}q.

In the previous expression, we keep in mind that the operator
W
only affects the variables denoted by
k
and
\tilde{q}
, not
q
.
Expanding the product makes appear
2^{l}
terms. In order to integrate by parts, we iterate the identities

[\partial_{K},W]=\omega^{\prime\prime}(k)t\partial_{q}=\frac{\omega^{\prime\prime}(k)}{\omega^{\prime}(k)}\left(W-\partial_{k}\right)

and for any sufficiently regular function
g(k)
,

[W,g(k)]=[\partial_{K},g(k)]=g^{\prime}(k).

This allows to obtain an identity of the form

\left(\frac{W-\partial_{k}}{\omega^{\prime}(k)}\right)^{l}=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}g_{j,m}(k)\partial_{k}^{j}W^{m},

where each of the functions
g_{n,m}(k)
is a complicated expression containing powers of
(\omega^{\prime})^{-1}
and its derivatives up to order
l-(m+j)
.
In each term, we integrate by parts in
K
to obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{K}\left(\frac{W-\partial_{k}}{\omega^{\prime}(k)}\right)^{l}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left|\int_{K}W^{m}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\partial_{k}^{j}(g_{j,m}(k)\phi(k))\,\mathrm{d}k\right|.

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\|\partial_{k}^{j}(g_{j,m}\phi)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{K}\left|W^{m}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\right|\,\mathrm{d}k.

Finally, we apply
(
6
)
to bound

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq t^{-l}\pi^{l}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\|\partial_{k}^{j}(g_{j,m}\phi)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{K}\left|W^{m}f(t,\tilde{q},k)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{q}

\displaystyle=t^{-l}\pi^{l}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\left\|\partial_{k}^{j}(g_{j,m}\phi)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{k}^{m}f_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}\qed

3.
Proofs for
d\geq 2

In this section we prove mixing for the
d
-dimensional case.
In this case, we define the
d
vector fields

W=tD\omega(k)\nabla_{q}+\nabla_{k}\qquad\text{ or }\quad W_{j}=t\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\partial_{k_{j}}\omega_{i}(k))\partial_{q_{i}}+\partial_{k_{j}}.

(9)

As before,
W
commutes with the Liouville operator.
If
D\omega
is an invertible matrix, most of the proof goes through as before. For the sake of completeness, we state Theorem
1.1
with an explicit bound on the right-hand-side.
To this end, we define the matrix norm

|M|_{\infty}=\max_{i,j}|M_{i,j}|.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f(t,q,k)
be the solution to (
1
) with initial datum
f_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times K)
. Assume that
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times K)
, and that

\det D\omega(k)\neq 0,\quad\text{ for all }k\in K.

Then,
M:=(D\omega(k))^{-1}
is well-defined and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(f(t,q,k)-\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{2\pi d}{t}\left(\left\||M|_{\infty}\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\partial_{k_{i}}\bar{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|\nabla_{k}\cdot M\phi\hat{e_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\bar{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}\right).

Proof.

As before, we express the left-hand-side as

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(f(t,q,k)-\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{K}(f(t,q,k)-f(t,q^{\prime},k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}\right|.

We then write

\displaystyle f(t,q,k)-f(t,q^{\prime},k)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{q^{\prime}_{j}}^{q_{j}}\partial_{q_{j}}f(t,q_{1},\cdots q_{j-1},s,q^{\prime}_{j+1},\cdots,q^{\prime}_{d},k)\,\mathrm{d}s

and bound

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(f(t,q,k)-\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\int_{K}\partial_{q_{j}}f(t,q_{1},\cdots q_{j-1},s,q^{\prime}_{j+1},\cdots,q^{\prime}_{d},k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\right|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}q^{\prime}.

We now write

\partial_{q_{j}}=t^{-1}[M(W-\nabla_{k})]_{j}
.
Thus, by using the divergence theorem for the second term (and using the compact support of
\phi
to conclude the absence of boundary terms), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{K}\partial_{q_{j}}f(t,q_{1},\cdots q_{j-1},s,q^{\prime}_{j+1},\cdots,q^{\prime}_{d},k)\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{t}\int_{K}\left|[M(k)W]_{j}f(t,q_{1},\cdots q_{j-1},s,q^{\prime}_{j+1},\cdots,q^{\prime}_{d},k)\phi(q,k)\right|\,\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{t}\int_{K}\left|f(t,q_{1},\cdots q_{j-1},s,q^{\prime}_{j+1},\cdots,q^{\prime}_{d},k)(\nabla_{k}\cdot M(k)^{\top}\phi(q,k)\hat{e_{j}})\right|\,\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\left\||M|_{\infty}\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}_{+}^{d}}\left|W_{i}f(t,q_{1},\cdots q_{j-1},s,q^{\prime}_{j+1},\cdots,q^{\prime}_{d},k)\right|\,\mathrm{d}k

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\left\|\nabla_{k}\cdot M^{\top}\phi\hat{e_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}}{t}\int_{K}\left|f(t,q_{1},\cdots q_{j-1},s,q^{\prime}_{j+1},\cdots,q^{\prime}_{d},k)\right|\,\mathrm{d}k.

Inserting this in the previous bound and using (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(f(t,q,k)-\bar{f}_{0}(k))\phi(q,k)\,\mathrm{d}q\,\mathrm{d}k\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi d}{t}\left(\left\||M|_{\infty}\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left\|\partial_{k_{i}}\bar{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\|\nabla_{k}\cdot M^{\top}\phi\hat{e_{j}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\bar{f}_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}}\right)\qed

Finally, we prove Corollary
1.4
.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

For fixed
x_{0}\in{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}
, we write
\phi_{x_{0}}
for the fundamental solution to Poisson’s equation in dimension
d
.
In particular,

\phi_{x_{0}}(x)\begin{cases}(x-x_{0})\mathbb{I}_{[-\infty,x_{0}]}+\max(0,x_{0})&\text{ if }d=1\\
-(2\pi)^{-1}\ln(|x-x_{0}|)&\text{ if }d=2\\
\kappa_{d}|x-x_{0}|^{-d+2}&\text{ if }d=3,\end{cases}

for a suitable constant
\kappa_{d}
.
So we can write

\displaystyle V_{F}(x_{0})=\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}}\phi_{x_{0}}(x)F(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{K}\varphi_{x_{0}}(q,k)f(t,q,k)\,\mathrm{d}k\,\mathrm{d}q,

where we used the notation
f=F\circ N
and
\varphi_{x_{0}}=\phi_{x_{0}}\circ N
.
Now, the integral is in a suitable form to apply the arguments in the proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
3.1
, provided that
\varphi_{x_{0}}
is sufficiently regular. The Coulomb kernel
\phi_{x_{0}}
belongs to the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}({\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d}\times{\mathord{\mathbb{R}}}^{d})
, since the integral of its derivative in a ball is finite. Outside a sufficiently large ball, the function and its derivatives are bounded. Since we assume that
N
is of class
C^{1}
,
\varphi_{x_{0}}
inherits these properties. Thus,
\nabla_{k}\varphi_{x_{0}}\in L^{1}+L^{\infty}
. For the
L^{\infty}
-part, we can apply Proposition
3.1
directly, and for the
L^{1}
part we switch the roles of
f
and
\phi
in the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

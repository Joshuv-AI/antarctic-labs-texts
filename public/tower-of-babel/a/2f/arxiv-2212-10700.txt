Stuffle regularized multiple Eisenstein series revisited
Henrik Bachmann

Abstract
. Multiple Eisenstein series are holomorphic functions in the complex upper-half plane, which can be seen as a crossbreed between multiple zeta values and classical Eisenstein series. They were originally defined by Gangl-Kaneko-Zagier in 2006, and since then, many variants and regularizations of them have been studied. They give a natural bridge between the world of modular forms and multiple zeta values. In this note, we give a new algebraic interpretation of stuffle regularized multiple Eisenstein series based on the Hopf algebra structure of the harmonic algebra introduced by Hoffman.

Introduction

In this note, we will give an overview of the regularization of multiple Eisenstein series and present a new Hopf algebraic approach for the construction of stuffle regularized multiple Eisenstein series. Multiple Eisenstein series were introduced in the depth two case by Gangl-Kaneko-Zagier in
[
GKZ
]
, and for higher depth, they were studied in
[
B0
]
,
[
B1
]
,
[
B2
]
and
[

]
. Similar to multiple zeta values, there exist two regularizations, the shuffle and stuffle regularizations, which were introduced in
[

]
and
[

]
, respectively. We will focus on the stuffle regularisation in this work and give a new algebraic interpretation for them.

As the main building block for all the objects appearing in this note, we define for an index
{\bf k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{r}
of depth
r\geq 1
,
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{Z}_{<0}
and

the
truncated multiple Hurwitz zeta function
by

\displaystyle\zeta_{N}({\bf k};x):=\zeta_{N}(k_{1},\dots,k_{r};x):=\sum_{N>n_{1}>\dots>n_{r}>0}\frac{1}{(x+n_{1})^{k_{1}}\dots(x+n_{r})^{k_{r}}}\,.

(1.1)

The index
{\bf k}
is also allowed to be empty (i.e.
r=0
), in which case we set
\zeta_{N}(\emptyset;x):=1
. We write
\zeta_{N}({\bf k})=\zeta_{N}({\bf k};0)
for the
truncated multiple zeta values
. In the case
k_{1}\geq 2
or
{\bf k}=\emptyset
the index
{\bf k}
is called admissible and we can take the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
to obtain the

\zeta({\bf k})=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\zeta_{N}({\bf k})
, which specialize to the Riemann zeta values
\zeta(k)
in the depth

we denote the

-vector space spanned by all multiple zeta values.

The Riemann zeta values also appear as the constant term in the Fourier expansion of the Eisenstein series
\mathbb{G}(k)
, which can also be constructed out of (
1.1
) in the following way: For
k,N\geq 1
and
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{Z}
we define

\displaystyle\Psi_{N}(k;x):=\zeta_{N}(k;x)+\frac{1}{x}+(-1)^{k}\zeta_{N}(k;-x)=\sum_{N>n>-N}\frac{1}{(x+n)^{k}}\,.

(1.2)

Let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Im(z)>0\}
be an element in the upper-half plane and assume
k\geq 2
. In this case, the limit
\Psi(k;\tau):=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{N}(k;\tau)
exists and we get by the Lipschitz formula

\displaystyle\Psi(k;\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(\tau+n)^{k}}=\frac{(-2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{d>0}d^{k-1}q^{d}\,,

(1.3)

where
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. In particular, we can also replace
\tau
by

and take the sum over all

to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 1}\Psi(k;m\tau)=\frac{(-2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{d>0}\frac{d^{k-1}q^{d}}{1-q^{d}}\,,

which is, up to the constant term
\zeta(k)
, exactly the Fourier expansion of the Eisenstein series of weight
k
. This shows that the truncated multiple Hurwitz zeta function (
1.1
) can be used to construct both multiple zeta values as well as the classical Eisenstein series. The purpose of this note is to show that they can also be used to construct multiple Eisenstein series. This will then be used to show that the regularization of the multiple Hurwitz zeta function gives a, in some sense, natural way of defining stuffle regularized multiple Eisenstein series. This construction is based on the Hopf algebra structure of quasi-shuffle algebras, which we will recall in Section
3
after recalling the basic calculation of multiple Eisenstein series in Section
2
. In Section
4
, we then show how (truncated) multiple Eisenstein series can be constructed out of the truncated multiple Hurwitz zeta function (
1.1
) using the convolution product and standard regularization techniques in the harmonic algebra. In the end, we mention some new results on the comparison between the stuffle and shuffle regularized multiple Eisenstein series, which will be discussed in more detail in the master thesis of Turan in
[
T
]
. Finally, we mention that the construction we present in Section
4
is similar to the one used in
[
BB
]
to construct combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series.

3.1.
Regularization

In this section, we will recall some results from
[
IKZ
]
. For
\bullet\in\{\shuffle,\ast\}
any element

can be written as a polynomial in

with coefficients in
\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{\bullet}
, i.e. there exist some

and

such that

. Since this representation is unique, we obtain algebra isomorphisms

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}_{\bullet}:\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{\bullet}[T],

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}(w)T^{j}=\operatorname{reg}_{\bullet}(w)\,.

In particular, any algebra homomorphism
f:\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{\bullet}\rightarrow A
into some
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

can be lifted to an algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle f^{\bullet}:\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{\bullet}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow A[T],

(3.8)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{j=1}^{m}f(c_{j}(w))T^{j}\,,

(3.9)

i.e. we set
f^{\bullet}=f\circ\operatorname{reg}_{\bullet}
after extending
f
to
\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{\bullet}[T]
coefficient-wise. In the case of the multiple zeta values map (
3.5
), this gives the
shuffle regularized multiple zeta values

\zeta^{\shuffle}
and the
stuffle regularized multiple zeta values

\zeta^{\ast}
. By the work,
[
IKZ
]
, these two regularizations differ, but their difference can be described explicitly: Define the
\mathbb{R}
-linear map
\rho:\mathbb{R}[T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}[T]
by

\displaystyle\rho(e^{Tu}):=\exp\left(Tu+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\zeta(n)u^{n}\right)\,.

(3.10)

Then we have
\zeta^{\shuffle}=\rho\circ\zeta^{\ast}
(
[
IKZ
, Theorem 1]
). The relations among multiple zeta values obtained from this comparison together with the finite double shuffle relations are the
extended double shuffle relations
, which conjecturally give all relations among multiple zeta values.

3.2.
Hopf algebra structures

By the work of Hoffman (
[
H
]
,
[
HI
]
), any quasi-shuffle algebra can be equipped with the structure of a Hopf algebra
[
H
, Section 3]
, where the coproduct is given for
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
by
deconcatenation coproduct

\displaystyle\Delta(w)=\sum_{uv=w}u\otimes v\,.

(3.11)

The antipode in this Hopf algebra can be described explicitly (
[
H
, Theorem 3.2]
). For example, if
\diamond
is the trivial product, then the corresponding antipode for
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\in L
is given by

\displaystyle S(a_{1}\cdots a_{r})=(-1)^{r}a_{r}\dots a_{1}\,.

(3.12)

For any Hopf algebra
A
with coproduct
\Delta
and an
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
B
with multiplication
m
and
f,g\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
the
convolution product
is defined by

\displaystyle f\star g=m\circ(f\otimes g)\circ\Delta\,.

As a simple fact from the theory of Hopf algebras, we get the following lemma, which will play an important role in the construction in the next section.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
f,g\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
then
f\star g\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,B)
.

The antipode
S:A\rightarrow A
is the inverse of
\operatorname{Id}
with respect to
\star
, i.e.

\displaystyle(S\star\operatorname{Id})(w)=\begin{cases}1,w=\emptyset\\
0,\text{else}\end{cases}\,.

As a direct consequence of (
3.12
) one obtains that for any non-empty word
w=a_{1}\dots a_{m}
in
A=\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}a_{i}a_{i-1}\dots a_{1}\shuffle a_{i+1}a_{i+2}\dots a_{m}=0.

(3.13)

This can be used to prove the following relations among the shuffle regularized multiple zeta values.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
and
k=k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq r\\
l_{1}+\dots+l_{j-1}+l_{j+1}+\dots+l_{r}=k-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{e_{j}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq r\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\binom{l_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}\zeta^{\shuffle}(l_{1},\dots,l_{j-1})\zeta^{\shuffle}(l_{r},l_{r-1},\dots,l_{j+1})=0\,,

where
e_{j}=l_{1}+\dots+l_{j-1}+k_{j}
.

Proof.

By induction and the definition of the shuffle product, one can show that for any
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\zeta^{\shuffle}(x^{k_{1}-1}y\dots x^{k_{r}-1}yx^{n})=(-1)^{n}\sum_{l_{1}+\dots+l_{r}=k_{1}+\dots+k_{r}+n}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{l_{i}-1}{k_{i}-1}\zeta^{\shuffle}(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})\,.

The statement then follows by using the following relation, which is a consequence of (
3.13
) for
a_{m}\dots a_{1}=x^{k_{1}-1}y\dots x^{k_{r}-1}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\zeta^{\shuffle}(a_{1}\dots a_{i})\zeta^{\shuffle}(a_{m}a_{m-1}\dots a_{i+1})=0\,.

∎

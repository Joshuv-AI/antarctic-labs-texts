Navarro vertices and lifts in solvable groups Thanks: *Corresponding author
Lei Wang, Ping Jin*

Abstract
. Let Q Q be a p p -subgroup of a finite p p -solvable group G G , where p p is a prime, and suppose that δ \delta is a linear character of Q Q with the property that δ ⁡ ( x ) = δ ⁡ ( y ) \delta(x)=\delta(y) whenever x , y ∈ Q x,y\in Q are conjugate in G G . In this situation, we show that restriction to p p -regular elements defines a canonical bijection from the set of those irreducible ordinary characters of G G with Navarro vertex ( Q , δ ) (Q,\delta) onto the set of irreducible Brauer characters of G G with Green vertex Q Q . Also, we use this correspondence to examine the behavior of lifts of Brauer characters with respect to normal subgroups.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed prime, and let
G
be a finite
p
-solvable group.
For each irreducible complex character
\chi\in\Irr(G)
,
Navarro
[
17
]
introduced a canonical pair
(Q,\delta)
associated to
\chi

which is uniquely defined up to
G
-conjugacy, where
Q
is a
p
-subgroup of
G
and
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
.
Such a pair
(Q,\delta)
is called a
Navarro vertex
of
\chi
,
and has been shown to be quite useful in many
problems in the character theory of solvable groups
(see, for example,
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
18
,
19
]

and the references therein).

Following Navarro, we use
\Irr(G|Q,\delta)
to denote the set of all members of
\Irr(G)
with Navarro vertex
(Q,\delta)
, and write
\IBr_{p}(G|Q)
for the set of irreducible
p
-Brauer characters of
G
with vertex
Q
(in the sense of Green).
Also, we denote by
G_{\delta}
the elements of
N_{G}(Q)
that stabilize
\delta
.

One of the important properties of Navarro vertices is that
if we take the vertex character
\delta
to be the principal character
1_{Q}
,
then restriction
\chi\mapsto\chi^{0}
defines a canonical bijection

\Irr(G|Q,1_{Q})\to\IBr_{p}(G|Q),

where
\chi^{0}
denotes the restriction of
\chi
to the set of
p
-regular elements of
G
;
see Theorem A of
[
17
]
.

Our motivation is to extend the above result
by replacing the principal character
1_{Q}
with certain linear characters
\delta
of
Q
.
We mention that if
\delta
is linear then
\chi^{0}
has vertex
Q
for each
\chi\in\Irr(G|Q,\delta)
with
\chi^{0}\in\IBr_{p}(G)
,
and this is the reason why we only consider linear vertex characters.
(Perhaps it is worthy mentioning that Navarro vertices
(Q,\delta)
with
\delta(1)=1
also appeared in Theorem B of
[
22
]
.)
For convenience, we say that
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is a
lift
if
\chi^{0}\in\IBr_{p}(G)
,
and that
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
is
stable
in
G
if
\delta(x)=\delta(y)
whenever
x,y\in Q
are conjugate in
G
. It is easy to see that if
\delta
is stable in
G
, then
\delta
is invariant in
N_{G}(Q)
, so that
G_{\delta}=N_{G}(Q)
.

With the notation as above, our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
Q
be a
p
-subgroup of a
p
-solvable group
G
, where
p
is a prime,
and suppose that
\delta
is a linear character of
Q
.
If
\delta
is stable in
G
,
then restriction to
p
-regular elements defines a canonical bijection
\Irr(G|Q,\delta)\to\IBr_{p}(G|Q)
.

Actually, we will prove Theorem A in the more general setting of Isaacs’
\pi
-partial characters
(see Theorem
3.4
).
The following Corollary B, which is an important special case of Theorem 2.1 of
[
20
]
,
is useful for applications.
We will establish the
\pi
-version of this result (see Corollary
3.5
),
which can also be used to prove theorems about lifts of
\pi
-partial characters.
For instance, we may replace Theorem 2.3 of
[
8
]
by our Corollary
3.5

to obtain the
\pi
-analogues of Theorems 1.1 and 1.3 of that paper.

Corollary B
.

Let
\varphi\in\IBr_{p}(G|Q)
, where
G
is a
p
-solvable group and
Q
is a
p
-subgroup of
G
. If
Q\vartriangleleft G
and
\delta
is a
G
-invariant linear character of
Q
,
then there is a unique lift
\chi\in\Irr(G)
of
\varphi
having Navarro vertex
(Q,\delta)
.

The next result is another immediate consequence of Theorem A.
In
[
18
]
, Navarro proved that

|\Irr(G|Q,\delta)|\leq|\Irr(G_{\delta}|Q,\delta)|,

and equality holds if either
\delta=1_{Q}
or
\delta
is linear and
Q\in\Syl_{p}(G)
;
see also
[
19
]
.
(We mention that it is very hard to prove that equality holds under these two conditions,
because they are essentially the strong forms of the Alperin weight conjecture (see
[
11
]
) and the McKay conjecture (see
[
12
]
) only for
p
-solvable groups.)
Here, we still have equality whenever
\delta
is a
G
-stable linear character of
Q
;
see Corollary
3.6
for a more general
\pi
-version.

Corollary C
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group for a prime
p
,

Q
a
p
-subgroup of
G
, and
\delta
a linear character of
Q
.
If
\delta
is stable in
G
, then
|\Irr(G|Q,\delta)|=|\Irr(N_{G}(Q)|Q,\delta)|
.

Now we consider lifts of Brauer characters in a
p
-solvable group
G
.
For
N\vartriangleleft G
, it is known that if
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is a lift,
then the irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
can fail,
so some additional conditions are necessary
(see
[
1
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
15
,
21
]
, for example).
The following is an easy application of Theorem A, which should be compared with Corollary 1.3 of
[
7
]
and Theorem 1.1 of
[
8
]
, and we also prove its
\pi
-version as Theorem

.

Theorem D
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
p>2
, let
N\vartriangleleft G
and let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be a lift with Navarro vertex
(Q,\delta)
.
If
\delta_{Q\cap N}
is stable in

, then every irreducible constituent
\psi
of
\chi_{N}

is also a lift. Moreover, if
\lambda
is stable in

, then

.

2.
Preliminaries

For the reader’s convenience, we will briefly review the definitions of both the Navarro vertices
for ordinary characters and the vertices for
\pi
-partial characters in
\pi
-separable groups;
see
[
5
,
17
]
for more details.
We begin by introducing some notation.

Given a finite group
G
, we say that
(H,\theta)
is a
character pair
of
G

if
H\leq G
and
\theta\in\Irr(H)
, and we define a partial order on the set of character pairs of
G
by setting
(H,\theta)\leq(K,\psi)
if
H\leq K
and
\theta
lies under
\psi
.
Observe that
G
acts on character pairs via conjugation,
that is,
(H,\theta)^{g}=(H^{g},\theta^{g})
for
g\in G
,
where
\theta^{g}\in\Irr(H^{g})
is defined by
\theta^{g}(x^{g})=\theta(x)
for
x\in H
.

Suppose that
G
is
\pi
-separable for a set
\pi
of primes.
A
\pi
-factored normal pair
of
G
is a character pair
(H,\theta)
,
where
H\vartriangleleft G
and
\theta
is
\pi
-factored (see
[
13
]
for the definition and properties).
We write
\mathcal{N}(G)
for the set of
\pi
-factored normal pairs of
G
,
and let
\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
be the set of maximal members of
\mathcal{N}(G)
.
We have the following fundamental result (see Sect. 4 of
[
5
]
),
which is very similar to Theorem 4.1 of
[
13
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
, where
G
is a
\pi
-separable group. Then the following hold.

(a)
There exists a unique normal subgroup
N
of
G
maximal with the property that
every irreducible constituent
\theta
of
\chi_{N}
is
\pi
-factored.
In particular,
(N,\theta)\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
.

(b)
Let
N
and
\theta
be as in (a), and assume that
\theta
is invariant in
G
.
Then
N=G
and
\theta=\chi
, so
\chi
is
\pi
-factored.

Now we can define the
norma nucleus

(W,\gamma)
for any
\chi\in\Irr(G)
recursively, where
G
is a
\pi
-separable group. If
\chi
is
\pi
-factored, let
(W,\gamma)=(G,\chi)
.
If
\chi
is not
\pi
-factored, let
(N,\theta)
be as in the above Lemma.
Then
\theta
is not
G
-invariant, so
(G_{\theta},\chi_{\theta})<(G,\chi)
, where
G_{\theta}
is the inertia group of
\theta
in
G
and
\chi_{\theta}
is the Clifford correspondent of
\chi
over
\theta
.
We define
(W,\gamma)
to be any normal nucleus for
\chi_{\theta}
.
Notice that
(W,\gamma)
is uniquely determined by
\chi
up to conjugacy in
G
,
and that
\gamma
is
\pi
-factored with
\gamma^{G}=\chi
.
Moreover, let
Q
be a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
W
and let
\delta
be the restriction to
Q
of the
\pi^{\prime}
-special factor
\gamma_{\pi^{\prime}}
of
\gamma
.
Then we say that
(Q,\delta)
is a
Navarro vertex
for
\chi
.
It is clear that all of the Navarro vertices for
\chi
are conjugate in
G

and as before, we write
\Irr(G|Q,\delta)
for the set of all members of
\Irr(G)
having Navarro vertex
(Q,\delta)
.

Continuing to assume that
G
is
\pi
-separable.
Write
G^{0}
to denote the set of
\pi
-elements of
G
,
and let
\chi^{0}
be the restriction of
\chi
to
G^{0}
, where
\chi
is any character of
G
.
In this situation, we say that
\chi^{0}
is a
\pi
-partial character
of
G
.
A
\pi
-partial character of
G
is
irreducible
if it cannot be written as a sum of two
\pi
-partial characters, and we write
\I(G)
for the set of irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G
. Also, a character
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is said to be a
lift
if
\chi^{0}\in\I(G)
,
and
\chi
is called a lift of
\varphi
if
\chi^{0}=\varphi\in\I(G)
.

Furthermore, we define a
vertex
for
\varphi\in\I(G)
to be any Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of some subgroup
U\leq G
such that there exists
\mu\in\I(U)
, where
\mu^{G}=\varphi
and
\mu(1)
is a
\pi
-number. We use
\I(G|Q)
to denote the set of those
irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G
with vertex
Q
.
It is known that the vertex for
\varphi
is unique up to
G
-conjugacy
(see Theorem 5.17 of
[
13
]
),
and that if
\pi=p^{\prime}
, then
\I(G)=\IBr_{p}(G)
by the Fong-Swan theorem, so that the vertices for irreducible
\pi
-partial characters are the same as the Green vertices for irreducible Brauer characters.

As expected, the vertices for
\pi
-partial characters behave well with respect to normal subgroups.

.

Suppose that
G
is
\pi
-separable and
\varphi\in\I(G)
.
Let

be a normal subgroup of

. If
\theta\in\I(N)
is a constituent of

,
then there is a vertex

for

.

Proof.

See Theorem 3.2 of

]
.

3.
Main results

We begin with a result concerning stable characters, which is fundamental for our proofs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\in\Irr(W)
be
\pi
-factored, where
W
is a subgroup of a
\pi
-separable group
G
, and suppose that
\theta\in\Irr(N)
lies under
\gamma
, where
N\vartriangleleft G
with
N\leq W
.
Let
Q
be a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
W
, and write
\delta=(\gamma_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}
.
If
\delta
is a
G
-stable linear character, then
\theta
is
\pi
-factored, and its
\pi^{\prime}
-special factor
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
is a
G
-invariant linear character.

Proof.

By the basic properties of
\pi
-characters, we see that
\theta
is
\pi
-factored, and thus
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
lies under
\gamma_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
Let
P=Q\cap N
, so that
P
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
N
.
Write
\beta=\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
. Note that
\gamma_{\pi^{\prime}}(1)=\delta(1)=1
,
so
(\gamma_{\pi^{\prime}})_{N}=\beta
, and thus
\delta_{P}=\beta_{P}
.
In particular,
\beta
is linear.
To show that
\beta
is invariant in
G
, it suffices to prove that
\beta(x)=\beta(y)
,
where
x,y\in N
are conjugate in
G
.
Since
\beta
has
\pi^{\prime}
-order in the group of linear characters of
N
,
we have
\beta(x)=\beta(x_{\pi^{\prime}})
and
\beta(y)=\beta(y_{\pi^{\prime}})
.
Clearly the
\pi^{\prime}
-part
x_{\pi^{\prime}}
of
x
is also conjugate to the
\pi^{\prime}
-part
y_{\pi^{\prime}}
of
y
,
so it is no loss to assume that
x,y
are
\pi^{\prime}
-elements of
N
.
In this case, we see that each of
x
and
y
is conjugate to some element of
P
,
and thus we may assume further that
x,y\in P
. Now
\beta(x)=\delta(x)=\delta(y)=\beta(y)
, as required.
∎

We need a sufficient condition for a
\pi
-factored normal pair to be maximal.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\I(G)
, and suppose that
N
is the unique maximal normal subgroup of
G
such that the irreducible constituents of
\varphi_{N}
have
\pi
-degree.
Assume that
\mu\in\I(N)
is an irreducible constituent of
\varphi_{N}

and
\alpha\in\Irr(N)
is the
\pi
-special lift of
\mu
.
Then
(N,\alpha\beta)\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
for every
\pi^{\prime}
-special character
\beta\in\Irr(N)
.

Proof.

We first show that
(N,\alpha)\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
.
Otherwise, we can choose some
(M,\psi)\in\mathcal{N}(G)
with
N<M
.
Since any normal constituent of
\pi
-factored characters is
\pi
-factored,
it is no loss to assume that
M/N
is a chief factor of
G
,
so that
M/N
is either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group.
By Corollary 4.5 of
[
13
]
, we see that every member of
\Irr(M|\alpha)
is
\pi
-factored.

We will use
\B
-characters to derive a contradiction (see
[
13
]
for the definition and properties). Let
\chi\in\B(G)
be a lift of
\varphi
. Then
\chi
lies over
\alpha
as
\chi^{0}=\varphi
lies over
\alpha^{0}=\mu
,
and hence there exists
\rho\in\Irr(M)
satisfying
(N,\alpha)\leq(M,\rho)\leq(G,\chi)
.
Then
\rho\in\B(M)
, which implies that
\rho
is
\pi
-special.
It follows that
\rho^{0}
is irreducible with
\pi
-degree,
so
\rho^{0}
lies under
\varphi
.
Now the maximality of
N
forces
M\leq N
, and this contradiction proves that
(N,\alpha)\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
.

Now, let
(M,\sigma)\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
be such that
(N,\alpha\beta)\leq(M,\sigma)
.
Then
(N,\alpha)\leq(M,\sigma_{\pi})\in\mathcal{N}(G)
, and
by the maximality of
(N,\alpha)
, we have
N=M
. The result follows.
∎

The following result, in some sense, can be viewed as the converse of the above lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(N,\theta)\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
, where
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
is invariant in
G
,
and let
\varphi\in\I(G)
. Suppose that
\chi\in\Irr(G|\theta)
is a lift of
\varphi
,
and that
\chi
has a Navarro vertex
(Q,\delta)
with
\delta(1)=1
.
Then
N
is the unique maximal normal subgroup of
G
such that the irreducible constituents of
\varphi_{N}
have
\pi
-degree.

Proof.

By the construction of Navarro vertices discussed in Section 2,
we see that
\theta
has
\pi
-degree,
and thus
\theta^{0}=(\theta_{\pi})^{0}
, which is clearly irreducible and lies under
\varphi
.
Suppose that the lemma is false. Then there is a chief factor
M/N
of
G
such that
the irreducible constituents of
\varphi_{M}
have
\pi
-degree.
Let
\eta\in\I(M)
lie under
\varphi
and over
\theta^{0}
.
Since
\eta(1)
is a
\pi
-number, there exists a
\pi
-special character
\hat{\eta}
of
M
such that
\hat{\eta}^{0}=\eta
(see Theorem 3.14 of
[
13
]
).
It is clear that
\hat{\eta}
lies over
\theta_{\pi}
, and since
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
is invariant in
G

and
M/N
is either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group, it follows that there exists a
\pi^{\prime}
-special character
\beta
of
M
lying over
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
(see Theorem 2.4 of
[
13
]
).
Now
\hat{\eta}\beta\in\Irr(M)
is
\pi
-factored, and it lies over
\theta_{\pi}\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}=\theta
, contradicting the maximality of
(N,\theta)
. The proof is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove the following result, which is the
\pi
-version of Theorem A.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group,
Q
a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
,
and
\delta
a linear character of
Q
. If
\delta
is stable in
G
,
then restriction
\chi\mapsto\chi^{0}
defines a bijection
\Irr(G|Q,\delta)\to\I(G|Q)
.

Proof.

We will carry out the following steps to complete the proof.

Step 1. For any
\chi\in\Irr(G|Q,\delta)
, we have
\chi^{0}\in\I(G|Q)
,
so the restriction map is well defined.

Observe that once we establish that
\chi^{0}
is irreducible,
then
Q
is clearly a vertex of
\chi^{0}
as
\delta
is linear.
So it suffices to show that
\chi^{0}\in\I(G)
, and we do this by induction on
|G|
.

By definition, there exists a normal nucleus
(W,\gamma)
of
\chi
such that

Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
W
and
(\gamma_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}=\delta
.
Also, there is some
(N,\theta)\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
satisfying

(N,\theta)\leq(W,\gamma)\leq(T,\psi)\leq(G,\chi),

where
T=G_{\theta}
and
\psi\in\Irr(T)
is the Clifford correspondent of
\chi
with respect to
\theta
. Then
(W,\gamma)
is also a normal nucleus of
\psi
, so
\psi\in\Irr(T|Q,\delta)
.
If
T=G
, then
(N,\theta)=(G,\chi)
by Lemma
2.1
, and thus
\chi
is
\pi
-factored.
Moreover, note that
\gamma(1)=\gamma_{\pi}(1)
is a
\pi
-number, so
\chi
has
\pi
-degree. It follows that
\chi^{0}
is irreducible, and we are done in this case.

We can therefore assume that
T<G
. By induction, we have
\psi^{0}\in\I(T)
.
By Lemma
3.1
,
we see that
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
is a
G
-invariant linear character. This implies that
T=G_{\theta}=G_{\theta_{\pi}}=G_{\theta^{0}}
. Notice that
\psi^{0}
lies over
\theta^{0}
,
so the Clifford correspondence for
\pi
-partial characters tells us that
(\psi^{0})^{G}
is irreducible. Now we have
\chi^{0}=(\psi^{G})^{0}=(\psi^{0})^{G}
,
and hence
\chi^{0}\in\I(G)
, as required.

Step 2. The map
\chi\mapsto\chi^{0}
is injective.

With the notation as above, we fix
\tilde{\chi}\in\Irr(G|Q,\delta)
with
\tilde{\chi}^{0}=\chi^{0}=\varphi\in\I(G)
, and we want to show that
\tilde{\chi}=\chi
.
As in Step 1, we can find the following character pairs

(\tilde{N},\tilde{\theta})\leq(\tilde{W},\tilde{\gamma})\leq(\tilde{T},\tilde{\psi})\leq(G,\tilde{\chi}),

where
(\tilde{N},\tilde{\theta})\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
,

(\tilde{W},\tilde{\gamma})
is a normal nucleus of
\tilde{\chi}
,

\tilde{T}
is the inertia group of
\tilde{\theta}
in
G
,
\tilde{\psi}
is the Clifford correspondent of
\tilde{\chi}
over
\tilde{\theta}
,

Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
\tilde{W}
and
(\tilde{\gamma}_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}=\delta
.
By Lemma
3.1
again, we see that
\tilde{\theta}_{\pi^{\prime}}
is invariant in
G
,
and by Lemma
3.3
, we have
\tilde{N}=N
.
Furthermore, both
(\tilde{\theta}_{\pi})^{0}
and
(\theta_{\pi})^{0}
are irreducible constituents of
\varphi_{N}
, so they are conjugate in
G
. Replacing
\tilde{\theta}
by a suitable conjugate,
we can assume that
(\tilde{\theta}_{\pi})^{0}=(\theta_{\pi})^{0}
, which forces

\tilde{\theta}_{\pi}=\theta_{\pi}
.
Also, it is easy to see that
(\tilde{\theta}_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q\cap N}=\delta_{Q\cap N}=(\theta_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q\cap N}
(see the proof of Lemma
3.1
),
which yields
\tilde{\theta}_{\pi^{\prime}}=\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
by Lemma
2.5
,
and thus
\tilde{\theta}=\theta
. In particular, we have
\tilde{T}=T
.
In this case, we obtain
\tilde{\psi},\psi\in\Irr(T|Q,\delta)
.
Note that both
\tilde{\psi}^{0}
and
\psi^{0}
induce
\varphi
,
so they are irreducible and thus lie over
\theta^{0}
.
Then the Clifford correspondence for
\pi
-partial characters deduce that

\tilde{\psi}^{0}=\psi^{0}
. If
T<G
, then
\tilde{\psi}=\psi
by induction on
|G|
,
and hence
\tilde{\chi}=\tilde{\psi}^{G}=\psi^{G}=\chi
, as wanted.
So we assume that
T=G
. Then
N=G
by Lemma
2.1
,
which implies that both
\tilde{\chi}
and
\chi
are
\pi
-factored with
\pi
-degree,
and that
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
. Furthermore, we have
(\tilde{\chi}_{\pi})^{0}=\varphi=(\chi_{\pi})^{0}

and
(\tilde{\chi}_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}=\delta=(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}
.
It follows that

\tilde{\chi}_{\pi}=\chi_{\pi}
and
\tilde{\chi}_{\pi^{\prime}}=\chi_{\pi^{\prime}}
(by Lemma
2.5
),
and thus we have
\tilde{\chi}=\chi
, as required.

Step 3. The map
\chi\mapsto\chi^{0}
is surjective.

Let
\varphi\in\I(G|Q)
. By Lemma
2.3
,
there exists a unique maximal normal subgroup
N
such that the irreducible constituents of
\varphi_{N}
have
\pi
-degree.
We can choose
\mu\in\I(N)
lying under
\varphi
, such that
\eta\in\I(T|Q)
, where
T=G_{\mu}
and
\eta
is the Clifford correspondent of
\varphi
over
\mu

(see Lemma 6.33 of
[
13
]
or Lemma 6.33 of
[
13
]
).
In particular, we have
\eta^{G}=\varphi
.

Suppose first that
T<G
. By induction on
|G|
, there exists
\psi\in\Irr(T|Q,\delta)
with
\psi^{0}=\eta
. As before, we have the following character pairs:

(M,\sigma)\leq(W,\gamma)\leq(T,\psi),

where
(M,\sigma)\in\mathcal{N}^{*}(T)
,
(W,\gamma)
is a normal nucleus for
\psi
,

Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
W
and
(\gamma_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}=\delta
.
Then
\sigma_{\pi^{\prime}}
is invariant in

by Lemma
3.1
,
and applying Lemma
3.3
, we conclude that
M
is the unique maximal normal subgroup of

such that the irreducible constituents of
\eta_{M}
have
\pi
-degree.
Observe that
\eta_{N}=e\mu
for some integer

\mu(1)
is a
\pi
-number,
so
N\leq M
.

Furthermore, let
P=Q\cap N
and
\lambda=\delta_{P}

Then

is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
N
and

\lambda

2.5

\pi^{\prime}
-special character
\hat{\lambda}
of

\hat{\mu}

Finally, we prove the
\pi
-version of Theorem D with a somewhat stronger form.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group with
2\in\pi
, and suppose that
N\vartriangleleft G
.
Let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be a lift with Navarro vertex
(Q,\delta)
, and write
P=Q\cap N
and
\lambda=\delta_{P}
. Assume that
\lambda
is stable in
N
.
Then every irreducible constituent
\psi
of
\chi_{N}
is a lift,
and if in addition
\lambda
is invariant in
N_{G}(P)
, then
G_{\psi}=G_{\psi^{0}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
, there exists an irreducible constituent
\psi
of
\chi_{N}
having
(P,\lambda)
as a Navarro vertex,
and then Theorem
3.4
implies that
\psi^{0}\in\I(N)
.

Assume further that
\lambda
is invariant in
N_{G}(P)
.
Writing
\eta=\psi^{0}
, we have
G_{\psi}\leq G_{\eta}
.
To obtain the reverse containment, let
g\in G_{\eta}
.
Then both
P^{g}
and
P
are clearly vertices for
\eta
, so they are conjugate in
N
.
It follows that
g\in N_{G}(P)N
, and hence
g=xn
for some
x\in N_{G}(P)
and
n\in N
.
Observe that
x=gn^{-1}\in G_{\eta}
and
N\leq G_{\psi}
.
To prove that
g\in G_{\psi}
, therefore, it suffices to show that
x\in G_{\psi}
.
Now
\lambda
is
N_{G}(P)
-invariant, so
\psi,\psi^{x}\in\Irr(N|P,\lambda)
.
By Theorem
3.4
again, we obtain
\psi=\psi^{x}
, so
x\in G_{\psi}
, as desired.
∎

1.
Introduction

After the discovery of contraction mapping theorem by Stefan Banach in 1922, it became apparent that it was a very useful result. This prompted other scholars towards finding similar results, and thus the vast field of metric fixed point theory was born. For a detailed discussion on such theorems, one may refer to
[
10
]
and the references therein.

One of the recent generalizations of contraction mapping theorem was given by Wardowski
[
11
]
, who defined the so called
F
-contraction mappings. He not only proved that every
F
-contraction satisfies the conclusion of contraction mapping theorem, but also produced an example of a mapping which is an
F
-contraction but does not satisfy the hypothesis of contraction mapping theorem. Many scholars tried to generalize the concept of an
F
-contraction in many directions. The recent survey by Karapinar
et al.

[
9
]
contains a detailed discussion of various generalizations of
F
-contractions.

Suppose that
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are nonempty subsets of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
and
\phi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
be a mapping. If
S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\emptyset
then we cannot talk about a fixed point of
\phi
. In such a case, we search for points in
S_{1}
such that the error
d(x,\phi x)
is as small as possible. The initial breakthrough in this direction was obtained by Ky Fan in 1969, who proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
S
be a nonempty compact convex set in a locally convex real topological vector space
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\phi:S\to\mathcal{M}
be a continuous mapping. Then either
\phi
has a fixed point in
S
or there exists a point
x
in
S
and a continuous seminorm
p
on
\mathcal{M}
such that

0<p(x-\phi x)=\inf_{y\in S}p(y-\phi x).

In the modern terminology, theorems which guarantee a conclusion similar to that of Theorem
1
are called best approximation theorems. On the other hand, the theorems which guarantee the existence of a point
x\in S_{1}
such that
d(x,\phi x)=d(S_{1},S_{2})=\inf\{d(s_{1},s_{2}):s_{1}\in S_{1},s_{2}\in S_{2}\}
are called best proximity point theorems. Some early work in this direction was due to Anuradha and Veeramani
[
2
]
, Saddiq Basha
[
3
]
and Eldred
et al.

[
7
]
.

Recently, R. Batra
[
5
]
defined the so called proximally
F
-dominated pair of mappings and proved a common best proximity point theorem for such mappings. In doing so, he generalized the earlier work of Saddiq Basha
[
4
]
. The main objective of our paper is to considerably weaken the notion of proximally
F
-dominated pair of mappings and to prove common best proximity point theorems for such mappings.

2.
Preliminaries

We shall use the symbols
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R_{+}}
to denote the set of non-negative integers, the set of real numbers and the set of positive real numbers, respectively. Convergence of a sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to
x
as
i\to\infty
in a metric space will often be denoted by
x_{i}\to x
. The value of a function
\phi
at
x
will be denoted by
\phi x
instead of
\phi(x)
when there is no possibility of confusion. If
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are sets, then the expression
S_{1}\subset S_{2}
implies that
S_{1}
is a subset of
S_{2}
. If
\phi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
and
\psi:S_{3}\to S_{4}
are functions such that
S_{2}\subset S_{3}
, then,
\psi\circ\phi:S_{1}\to S_{4}
denotes the composition of functions, defined by
\psi\circ\phi x=\psi(\phi x)
for all
x\in S_{1}
. The composition of a function
\phi:S\to S
with itself
n
times will be denoted by
\phi^{n}
, with the understanding that
\phi^{0}
is the identity mapping of
S
. If
\phi:S_{1}\to\ S_{2}
is a function, and
S\subset S_{1}
then we denote by
\phi_{|S}
the mapping
\phi_{|S}:S\to\phi(S)
defined by
\phi_{|S}x=\phi x
for all
x\in S
.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be nonempty subsets of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
. Following S. Basha
[
4
]
, we shall use the notations:
d(S_{1},S_{2})
as the infimum of the set of all possible distances between two points, one each from
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
; and
S_{1}^{0}
(respectively,
S_{2}^{0}
) as the set of all
s_{1}\in S_{1}
(respectively,
s_{2}\in S_{2}
) such that
d(S_{1},S_{2})
is attained by
d
at
(s_{1},s_{2})
for some
s_{2}\in S_{2}
(respectively,
s_{1}\in S_{1}
).

Now, we recall a well known notion of a closed mapping.

Definition 1

(
[
1
]
p. 51)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M}_{1},d_{1})
and
(\mathcal{M}_{2},d_{2})
be metric spaces and
\phi:\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
be a mapping. We say that
\phi
is closed if for every sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{M}_{1}
,

[x_{i}\to x\in\mathcal{M}_{1}\text{ and }\phi x_{i}\to y\in\mathcal{M}_{2}]\Rightarrow\phi x=y.

Remark 1

(
[
1
]
p. 51)
.

It is well known that every continuous mapping is closed, but the converse is not true.

The notion of proximal commutativity will play a prominent role throughout the paper.

Definition 2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be nonempty subsets of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
. A pair of mappings
\phi,\psi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
is said to be proximally commuting, if

\left[d(a,\phi x)=d(b,\psi x)=d(S_{1},S_{2})\right]\Rightarrow\phi b=\psi a

for all
a,b,x\in S_{1}
.

It is easy to see that if
S_{1}=S_{2}
, then
\phi
and
\psi
are proximally commuting if and only if they are commuting. Therefore, proximal commutativity is a generalization of usual commutativity.

Now, we recall the definition of common best proximity point.

Definition 3

(
[
4
]
)
.

Given any pair of nonempty subsets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
and a pair of mappings
\phi,\psi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
, a point
x^{*}\in S_{1}
is called a common best proximity point of
\phi
and
\psi
if
d(x^{*},\phi x^{*})=d(S_{1},S_{2})
and
d(x^{*},\psi x^{*})=d(S_{1},S_{2})
.

Since, the mappings
x\to d(x,\phi x)
and
x\to d(x,\psi x)
are generally nonlinear and
d(S_{1},S_{2})\leq d(x,\phi x)
for all
x\in S_{1}
, hence, a common best proximity point problem may be regarded as a global nonlinear optimization problem.
In
[
11
]
, Wardowski used a class of functions
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following properties to define the so called
F
-contractions.

(F1)

F
is strictly increasing i.e.
x<y\Rightarrow Fx<Fy
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
;

(F2)

for every sequence
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive real numbers,
\alpha_{i}\to 0\iff F\alpha_{i}\to-\infty
;

(F3)

there exists
k\in(0,1)
such that
\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}\alpha^{k}F\alpha=0.

We denote by
\mathcal{F}
the class of all mappings
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the conditions (F1)-(F3).

Example 1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Consider the mappings
F_{i}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}(i=1,2,3,4)
defined by

(i)

F_{1}\alpha=\ln{\alpha}
;

(ii)

F_{2}\alpha=\ln{\alpha}+\alpha
;

(iii)

F_{3}\alpha=\frac{-1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}
;

(iv)

F_{4}\alpha=\ln(\alpha^{2}+\alpha).

It was ensured by Wardowski that
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4}\in\mathcal{F}
.

R. Batra
[
5
]
defined the concept of proximally
F
-dominated pair of mappings as follows:

Definition 4

(
[
5
]
)
.

Given any
F\in\mathcal{F}
and two nonempty subsets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
, a function
\psi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
is said to
F
-dominate another function
\phi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
proximally, if there exists a real number
\tau>0
such that

\left.\begin{array}[]{rll}d(a_{1},\phi x_{1})=d(a_{2},\phi x_{2})&=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
d(b_{1},\psi x_{1})=d(b_{2},\psi x_{2})&=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&a_{1}\neq a_{2}\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&b_{1}\neq b_{2}\\
&\tau+F(d(a_{1},a_{2}))\leq F(d(b_{1},b_{2}))\end{array}\right.

for all
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2},x_{1},x_{2}\in S_{1}
.

For all
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\in S_{1}
, let us define

M_{d}(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})=\max\left\{d(b_{1},b_{2}),d(a_{2},b_{2}),d(a_{1},b_{1}),\frac{d(a_{2},b_{1})+d(a_{1},b_{2})}{2}\right\}.

In
[
12
]
, Wardowski and Dung introduced the concept of
F
-weak contractions as follows:

Definition 5

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},d)
be a metric space and
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Let
\phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
. We say that
\phi
is an
F
-weak contraction if there exists
\tau>0
such that

\phi x_{1}\neq\phi x_{2}\Rightarrow\tau+F(d(\phi x_{1},\phi x_{2}))\leq F(M_{d}(\phi x_{1},\phi x_{2},x_{1},x_{2}))

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
.

The following important generalization of contraction mapping theorem was also proved in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2

(
[
12
]
)
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
(\mathcal{M},d)
be a complete metric space. Let
\phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
be an
F
-weak contraction. If
\phi
or
F
is continuous, then
\phi
has a unique fixed point
x^{*}
in
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, for any
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
, we have
\lim_{i\to\infty}\phi^{i}x_{0}=x^{*}
.

3.
Main Results

Taking inspiration from
[
12
]
, we define the concept of proximally
F
-weakly dominated pair of mappings as follows:

Definition 6
.

Given any
F\in\mathcal{F}
and two nonempty subsets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
, a function
\psi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
is said to
F
-weakly dominate another function
\phi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
proximally, if there exists a real number
\tau>0
such that

\left.\begin{array}[]{rll}d(a_{1},\phi x_{1})=d(a_{2},\phi x_{2})&=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
d(b_{1},\psi x_{1})=d(b_{2},\psi x_{2})&=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&a_{1}\neq a_{2}\end{array}\right\}\\
\Rightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&M_{d}(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\neq 0\\
&\tau+F(d(a_{1},a_{2}))\leq F(M_{d}(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}))\end{array}\right.

for all
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2},x_{1},x_{2}\in S_{1}
.

Proposition 1
.

For nonempty subsets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
, if
\phi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
is
F
-dominated by another mapping
{\psi:S_{1}\to S_{2}}
proximally, then
\phi
is also
F
-weakly dominated by
\psi
proximally.

Proof.

The proposition follows trivially from the fact that
F
is strictly increasing and that
M_{d}(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\geq d(b_{1},b_{2})
for all
{a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}\in S_{1}}
.
∎

Example
6
shows that the converse of Proposition
1
may not be true. Hence, for a fixed pair of subsets
(S_{1},S_{2})
of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
, the class of all pairs of mappings
(\phi,\psi)
such that
\phi
is
F
-dominated by
\psi
proximally for some
F\in\mathcal{F}
is generally a proper subclass of the class of all pairs of mappings
(\phi,\psi)
such that
\phi
is
F
-weakly dominated by
\psi
proximally for some
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

If
S_{1}=S_{2}=\mathcal{M}
, then the concept of best proximity point reduces to the concept of fixed point. The concept of
F
-weakly dominated pair of mappings proximally as introduced in Definition
6
reduces to the following.

Definition 7
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},d)
be a metric space and
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Let
\phi
and
\psi
be functions mapping
\mathcal{M}
into
\mathcal{M}
. Then,
\psi
is said to
F
-weakly dominate
\phi
if there exists
\tau>0
such that

\phi x_{1}\neq\phi x_{2}\Rightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&M_{d}(\phi x_{1},\phi x_{2},\psi x_{1},\psi x_{2})\neq 0\\
&\tau+F(d(\phi x_{1},\phi x_{2}))\leq F(M_{d}(\phi x_{1},\phi x_{2},\psi x_{1},\psi x_{2}))\end{array}\right.

for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
.

A definition similar to the Definition
7
was first introduced by Zhou
et al.
in
[
13
]
under the name “Ćirić-type

-contraction”, however, the authors assume the continuity of
F
in their definition. So, Definition

is more general and is also an improvement of Definition 3.2 in
[
6
]
, which is a special case of Definition
4
with
S_{1}=S_{2}=\mathcal{M}.

The following proposition gives the relationship between
F
-weak contractions and
F
-weakly dominated pair of mappings.

Proposition 2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},d)
be a metric space and
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Let
\phi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
be a mapping and
I:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
be the identity mapping defined by
Ix=x
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Then,
\phi
is
F
-weakly dominated by
I
if and only if
\phi
is an
F
-weak contraction.

Therefore, the concept of
F
-weakly dominated pair of mappings can be considered a broad generalization of the concept of
F
-weak contractions.

Before proving the best proximity point theorems, we prove the following technical lemmas to avoid the repetition of arguments later.

Lemma 1
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
and
(\mathcal{M},d)
be a complete metric space. Consider any two nonempty subsets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of
\mathcal{M}
with
S_{1}^{0}
as nonempty and closed. Let
{\phi,\psi:S_{1}\to S_{2}}
be a pair of proximally commuting mappings which satisfy the following conditions:

(a)

\psi
is such that it
F
-weakly dominates
\phi
proximally;

(b)

\phi(S_{1}^{0})\subset S_{2}^{0}
;

(c)

\phi(S_{1}^{0})\subset\psi(S_{1}^{0})

then, there exists a sequence
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
S_{1}^{0}
which satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\phi a_{i}=\psi a_{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
;

(2)

if
a_{i}\neq a_{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, then there exists
a\in S_{1}^{0}
such that
a_{i}\to a
.

Proof.

Choose
x_{0}\in S_{1}^{0}
arbitrarily. Since
\phi(S_{1}^{0})\subset\psi(S_{1}^{0})
, we may define a sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of points of
S_{1}^{0}
inductively satisfying

(1)

\phi x_{i}=\psi x_{i+1}

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\phi(S_{1}^{0})\subset S_{2}^{0}
, we can find a sequence
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
S_{1}^{0}
such that

(2)

d(\phi x_{i},a_{i})=d(S_{1},S_{2})

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. The equations (
1
) and (
2
) give the following system of equations:

(3)

\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}&d(\phi x_{i+1},a_{i+1})=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&d(\psi x_{i+1},a_{i})=d(S_{1},S_{2})\end{array}\right\}

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
The system (
3
) along with the proximal commutativity of
\phi
and
\psi
gives

\phi a_{i}=\psi a_{i+1}

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Assume that
a_{i}\neq a_{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. We claim that
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence. Suppose that
i\geq 1
. The equations (
1
) and (
2
) give

(4)

\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}&d(\phi x_{i+1},a_{i+1})=d(\phi x_{i},a_{i})=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&d(\psi x_{i+1},a_{i})=d(\psi x_{i},a_{i-1})=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&\quad\quad a_{i}\neq a_{i+1}.\end{array}\right\}

The system (
4
) along with the hypothesis (a) imply that there exists
\tau>0
such that

\begin{split}&F(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))\leq F(d(M_{d}(a_{i+1},a_{i},a_{i},a_{i-1})))-\tau\\
&=F\biggl(\max\biggl\{d(a_{i},a_{i-1}),d(a_{i},a_{i-1}),d(a_{i+1},a_{i}),\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\quad\left.\left.\frac{d(a_{i},a_{i})+d(a_{i+1},a_{i-1})}{2}\right\}\right)-\tau\\
&=F\left(\max\left\{d(a_{i},a_{i-1}),d(a_{i-1},a_{i}),d(a_{i},a_{i+1}),\frac{d(a_{i+1},a_{i-1})}{2}\right\}\right)-\tau\\
&=F\left(\max\left\{d(a_{i},a_{i-1}),d(a_{i},a_{i+1}),\frac{d(a_{i+1},a_{i-1})}{2}\right\}\right)-\tau\\
&\leq F\left(\max\left\{d(a_{i},a_{i-1}),d(a_{i},a_{i+1}),\frac{d(a_{i},a_{i+1})+d(a_{i-1},a_{i})}{2}\right\}\right)-\tau\\
&=F\left(\max\left\{d(a_{i},a_{i+1}),d(a_{i},a_{i-1})\right\}\right)-\tau.\end{split}

In above calculations we took the advantage of the monotonicity of
F
and the triangle inequality. Hence,

(5)

F(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))\leq F(\max\{d(a_{i},a_{i+1}),d(a_{i},a_{i-1})\})-\tau.

If there exists
i\in\mathbb{N},i\geq 1
such that

\max\{d(a_{i},a_{i+1}),d(a_{i},a_{i-1})\}=d(a_{i},a_{i+1}),

then, by inequality (

) we obtain

F(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))\leq F(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))-\tau.

Which is not true.
Hence, we must have

(6)

\max\left\{d(a_{i},a_{i-1}),d(a_{i},a_{i+1})\right\}=d(a_{i},a_{i-1}).

The inequality (
5
) and the equation (

\begin{split}F(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))&\leq F(d(a_{i-1},a_{i}))-\tau\\
&\leq F(d(a_{i-2},a_{i-1}))-2\tau\\
&\leq F(d(a_{i-3},a_{i-2}))-3\tau\\
&\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\ldots\\
&\leq F(d(a_{0},a_{1}))-i\tau\end{split}

for all

.
The inequality (

) implies that

\lim_{i\to\infty}F(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))=-\infty.

Using (F2) it follows that

\lim_{i\to\infty}d(a_{i},a_{i+1})=0.

By (F3) there exists

such that

(8)

\lim_{i\to\infty}(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))^{k}F(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))=0.

Note that

(9)

\begin{split}&d(a_{i},a_{i+1})^{k}F(d(a_{0},a_{1}))-d(a_{i},a_{i+1})^{k}F(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))\\

&=id(a_{i},a_{i+1})^{k}\tau\geq 0.\end{split}

Using (

) and (

\lim_{i\to\infty}i(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))^{k}=0.

Therefore, we can find

\begin{split}&i(d(a_{i},a_{i+1}))^{k}\leq 1\text{ for all }i\geq i_{0}\\
&\Rightarrow d(a_{i},a_{i+1})\leq\frac{1}{i^{1/k}}\text{ for all }i\geq i_{0}.\end{split}

Let

such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}=a.

∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be nonempty subsets of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d)
. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}
and the mappings
\phi,\psi:S_{1}\to S_{2}
be a pair of proximally commuting mappings which satisfy the following conditions:

(a)

\psi
is such that it
F
-weakly dominates
\phi
proximally;

(b)

\phi(S_{1}^{0})\subset S_{2}^{0}
;

(c)

there exists
x\in S_{1}^{0}
such that
\phi x=\psi x

then,
\phi
and
\psi
have a unique common best proximity point.

Proof.

By hypothesis (b), hypothesis (c) and the definition of
S_{2}^{0}
, there exists some
a\in S_{1}^{0}
such that

(10)

\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}&d(a,\phi x)=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&d(a,\psi x)=d(S_{1},S_{2}).\end{array}\right\}

The proximal commutativity of
\phi
and
\psi
along with the system (
10
) implies

\phi a=\psi a.

Again, as
\phi(S_{1}^{0})\subset S_{2}^{0}
, we can find
b\in S_{1}^{0}
such that

(11)

\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}&d(b,\phi a)=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&d(b,\psi a)=d(S_{1},S_{2}).\end{array}\right\}

We claim that
b=a
. Suppose not, hence from (
10
) and (
11
) we obtain the following system

(12)

\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}&d(a,\phi x)=d(b,\phi a)=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&d(a,\psi x)=d(b,\psi a)=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&\quad\quad a\neq b.\end{array}\right\}

System (
12
) along with hypothesis (a) implies that there exists some
\tau>0
such that

\tau+F(d(a,b))\leq F(M_{d}(a,b,a,b))=F(d(a,b))

which is not true. So
a=b
. Hence, equations (
11
) give

d(a,\phi a)=d(a,\psi a)=d(S_{1},S_{2}).

Thus,
a
is a common best proximity point of
\phi
and
\psi
.

For uniqueness, let
a_{i}
(i=1,2) be common best proximity points of
\phi
and
\psi
such that
a_{1}\neq a_{2}
. Thus, we obtain the following system of equations:

(13)

\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}&d(a_{1},\phi a_{1})=d(a_{2},\phi a_{2})=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&d(a_{1},\psi a_{1})=d(a_{2},\psi a_{2})=d(S_{1},S_{2})\\
&\quad\quad a_{1}\neq a_{2}.\end{array}\right\}

System (
13
) along with hypothesis (a) implies that

\tau+F(d(a_{1},a_{2}))\leq F(M_{d}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{1},a_{2}))=F(d(a_{1},a_{2}))

which is not true. Hence, we must have

.

An arc-search interior-point algorithm for nonlinear constrained optimization
Yaguang Yang

Abstract
This paper proposes a new arc-search interior-point algorithm for the nonlinear constrained optimization problem. The proposed algorithm uses the second-order derivatives to construct a search arc that approaches the optimizer. Because the arc stays in the interior set longer than any straight line, it is expected that the scheme will generate a better new iterate than a line search method. The computation of the second-order derivatives requires to solve the second linear system of equations, but the coefficient matrix of the second linear system of equations is the same as the first linear system of equations. Therefore, the matrix decomposition obtained while solving the first linear system of equations can be reused. In addition, most elements of the right-hand side vector of the second linear system of equations are already computed when the coefficient matrix is assembled. Therefore, the computation cost for solving the second linear system of equations is insignificant and the benefit of having a better search scheme is well justified. The convergence of the proposed algorithm is established. Some preliminary test results are reported to demonstrate the merit of the proposed algorithm.



Keywords:
Arc-search, interior-point algorithm, nonlinear optimization.

(

denote the space of nonnegative vectors (positive vectors, respectively),
and use

In this paper, we consider the following nonlinear optimization problem.

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\min&:&f({\bf x})\\
\textrm{s.t.}&:&{\bf h}({\bf x})={\bf 0},\\
&&{\bf g}({\bf x})\geq{\bf 0},\end{array}

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
{\bf h}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and

{\bf g}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
. To ensure that the system has a solution, we assume

n>m\geq 0
. Without loss of generality, we assume
p\geq 1
, that is needed
for interior-point method and simplifies the discussion. The decision vector is
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We introduce a nonnegative relaxation vector
{\bf s}\geq{\bf 0}
to rewrite (
2
) as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\min&:&f({\bf x})\\
\textrm{s.t.}&:&{\bf h}({\bf x})={\bf 0},\\
&&{\bf g}({\bf x})-{\bf s}={\bf 0},\\
&&{\bf s}\geq{\bf 0}.\end{array}

For problem (
2
), the Lagrangian function is given by

L({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf w},{\bf s},{\bf z})=f({\bf x})-{\bf y}^{{\rm T}}{\bf h}({\bf x})-{\bf w}^{{\rm T}}({\bf g}({\bf x})-{\bf s})-{\bf z}^{{\rm T}}{\bf s},

(8)

where
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},{\bf w}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
, and
{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}_{+}
are multipliers.
To simplify the notation, we use
{\bf v}=({\bf x},{\bf y},{\bf w},{\bf s},{\bf z})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m+3p}
to
denote a stacked column vector composed of decision variables, relaxation
variables, and multipliers.
The gradients of the Lagrangian function with respect to
{\bf x}
and
{\bf s}
are
given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bf x}}L({\bf v})=\nabla f({\bf x})-\nabla{\bf h}({\bf x}){\bf y}-\nabla{\bf g}({\bf x}){\bf w},

(9a)

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bf s}}L({\bf v})={\bf w}-{\bf z},

(9b)

respectively, where
\nabla{\bf h}({\bf x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
and

\nabla{\bf g}({\bf x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
are Jacobians given by

\displaystyle\nabla{\bf h}({\bf x})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{\partial h_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial h_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial h_{m}}{\partial x_{1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial h_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}&\frac{\partial h_{2}}{\partial x_{n}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial h_{m}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right]=[\nabla h_{1}({\bf x}),\cdots,\nabla h_{m}({\bf x})],

and

\displaystyle\nabla{\bf g}({\bf x})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{\partial g_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial g_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial g_{p}}{\partial x_{1}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\partial g_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}&\frac{\partial g_{2}}{\partial x_{n}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial g_{p}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right]=[\nabla g_{1}({\bf x}),\cdots,\nabla g_{p}({\bf x})].

Then, the KKT conditions of (
2
) are given by (see
[
27
]
):

\displaystyle{\bf k}({\bf v})=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla_{{\bf x}}L({\bf v})\\
{\bf h}({\bf x})\\
{\bf g}({\bf x})-{\bf s}\\
{\bf w}-{\bf z}\\
{\bf Z}{\bf s}\end{array}\right]={\bf 0},\ ({\bf w},{\bf s},{\bf z})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3p},

where
{\bf k}:\mathbb{R}^{n+m+3p}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+m+3p}
. It is worthwhile to note that
\nabla_{{\bf x}}L({\bf v})

depends on the choice of the multiplier vector
{\bf y}
which has no restriction as long as its elements
are real numbers. In addition, the multiplier
{\bf y}
is unrelated to any other components of
{\bf k}({\bf v})
.
Moreover,
{\bf s}>{\bf 0}
is unrelated to any components of
{\bf k}({\bf v})
except
{\bf g}({\bf x})-{\bf s}={\bf 0}
.
These features will be used while we introduce a warm-restart scheme to minimize
\|{\bf k}({\bf v})\|
.
For the vector
{\bf k}({\bf v})
, its value at the
k
-th iteration is expressed by

\displaystyle{\bf k}({\bf v}^{k})=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla f({\bf x}^{k})-\nabla{\bf h}({\bf x}^{k}){\bf y}^{k}-\nabla{\bf g}({\bf x}^{k}){\bf w}^{k}\\
{\bf h}({\bf x}^{k})\\
{\bf g}({\bf x}^{k})-{\bf s}^{k}\\
{\bf w}^{k}-{\bf z}^{k}\\
{\bf Z}^{k}{\bf s}^{k}\end{array}\right].

Therefore, the Jacobian of
{\bf k}({\bf v})
is given by

\displaystyle{\bf k}^{\prime}({\bf v})=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{2}L({\bf v})&-\nabla{\bf h}({\bf x})&-\nabla{\bf g}({\bf x})&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}\\
\left(\nabla{\bf h}({\bf x})\right)^{{\rm T}}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}\\
\left(\nabla{\bf g}({\bf x})\right)^{{\rm T}}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&-{\bf I}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf I}&{\bf 0}&-{\bf I}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf Z}&{\bf S}\end{array}\right],

where

\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{2}L({\bf v})=\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{2}f({\bf x})-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{2}h_{i}({\bf x})y_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{2}g_{i}({\bf x})w_{i}.

(25)

Let the index set of the active inequality constraints
{\bf g}({\bf x})
at
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

be denoted by

I({\bf x})=\left\{i\in\{1,\ldots,p\}:g_{i}({\bf x})=0\right\}.

(26)

For convergence analysis, we need the definition of Lipschitz continuity. Let

\alpha({\bf x})
and
\beta({\bf x})
be real functions of
{\bf x}
. Let an open set

\mathcal{M}
contain the level set

\mathcal{L}=\{{\bf x}~|~\alpha({\bf x})\leq\alpha({\bf x}_{0})\}
, we say

is
Lipschitz continuous if there is a constant

such that

\|\beta({\bf x})-\beta({\bf y})\|\leq L\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|,

(27)

for all
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathcal{M}
.
Under some mild assumptions used in
[
7
,
27
,
41
]
, similar to the proof of

[
48
]
, it is straightforward to show the following result.

Proposition 2.1

Assume existence, smoothness, regularity, sufficiency, and strict complementarity
for problem (
2
) described as follows:

(A1)

Existence. There exists an optimal solution and its associate
multipliers
{\bf v}^{*}=({\bf x}^{*},{\bf y}^{*},{\bf w}^{*},{\bf s}^{*},{\bf z}^{*})
of (
2
).
Clearly, the KKT conditions (
2
) hold for any optimal solution.

(A2)

Smoothness.
f({\bf x})
,
{\bf h}({\bf x})
and
{\bf g}({\bf x})
are differentiable up to
the third order. In addition,
\nabla_{{\bf x}}L({\bf x})
,
{\bf g}({\bf x})
, and
{\bf h}({\bf x})

are Lipschitz continuous.

(A3)

Regularity. The set
\{\nabla h_{j}({\bf x}^{*}):j=1,\ldots,m\}\cup\{\nabla g_{i}({\bf x}^{*}):i\in I({\bf x}^{*})\}
is linearly independent.

(A4)

Sufficiency. For all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
,
we have
\eta^{{\rm T}}\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{2}L({\bf v}^{*})\eta>0
.

(A5)

Strict complementarity. For each

, we have
z_{i}^{*}+s_{i}^{*}>0
and
z_{i}^{*}s_{i}^{*}=0
.

Then the Jacobian matrix at the optimal
solution
{\bf v}^{*}
is nonsingular, i.e.,
[k^{\prime}({\bf v}^{*})]^{-1}
exists and is bounded.

Remark 2.1

Assuming the continuity of

, Proposition
2.1
implies that
all local optimizers are isolated.

3
The arc-search interior-point algorithm

The main idea of the proposed arc-search interior-point algorithm is to extend
a similar algorithm for linear programming presented in
[
46
]
to the
nonlinear programming problem. Given the current iterate

{{\bf v}^{k}}=({{\bf x}^{k}},{{\bf y}^{k}},{{\bf w}^{k}},{{\bf s}^{k}},{{\bf z}^{k}})
with
({{\bf w}^{k}},{{\bf s}^{k}},{{\bf z}^{k}})>{\bf 0}

and
t\geq 0
, we consider the solution
{\bf v}(t)=({\bf x}({t}),{\bf y}({t}),{{\bf w}}({t}),{{\bf s}}({t}),{{\bf z}}({t}))

of the following nonlinear system of equations defined for
t\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\nabla_{{\bf x}}L({{\bf v}}({t}))\\
{\bf h}({{\bf x}}({t}))\\
{\bf g}({{\bf x}}({t}))-{\bf s}({t})\\
\nabla_{{\bf s}}L({{\bf v}}({t}))\\
{{\bf Z}}({t}){{\bf s}}({t})\end{array}\right]=t\cdot\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\nabla_{{\bf x}}L({{\bf v}^{k}})\\
{\bf h}({{\bf x}^{k}})\\
({\bf g}({{\bf x}^{k}})-{{\bf s}^{k}})\\
\nabla_{s}L({{\bf v}^{k}})\\
{{\bf Z}^{k}}{{\bf s}^{k}}\end{array}\right],\hskip 7.22743pt({{\bf w}}({t}),{{\bf s}}({t}),{{\bf z}}({t}))\geq{\bf 0}.

Clearly, when
t=1
, the system has a solution of
{\bf v}(1)={\bf v}^{k}
; and
when
t\rightarrow 0
, the system approaches the solution of the KKT system.
Under some mild conditions to be defined in (B1)-(B4), and using the inverse function
theorem
[
21
]
, Yamashita et al.
[
41
]
showed the following result.

Proposition 3.1
(
[
41
]
)

Let

be defined in (

(B1)-(B4) given in Section

, then the solution
{\bf v}(t)
of the system of
the nonlinear equations (
3

Therefore,
{\bf v}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}

is an arc that starts at
{\bf v}^{k}
and guides the current iterate to a candidate of a local
optimal solution of (
2

3

when

However, solving the system of nonlinear equations of (

Similar to the idea used in
[
41

]
, our strategy is to approximate the arc

by part of an ellipse which is defined as:

where
\vec{{\bf a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m+3p}

are

\vec{{\bf c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m+3p}

Then, we search the optimizer along the ellipse
\mathcal{E}
to get a
new iterate
{\bf v}^{k+1}
and repeat the process until an
\epsilon

is found. To construct the ellipse of (

the first and second derivatives of (
3

are the same at current iterate

\left(\nabla{\bf g}({{\bf x}^{k}})\right)^{{\rm T}}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&-{\bf I}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf I}&{\bf 0}&-{\bf I}\\
{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf 0}&{\bf Z}^{k}&{\bf S}^{k}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{{\bf x}}\\
\dot{{\bf y}}\\
\dot{{\bf w}}\\
\dot{{\bf s}}\\
\dot{{\bf z}}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla_{{\bf x}}L({{\bf v}^{k}})\\
{\bf h}({{\bf x}^{k}})\\
{\bf g}({{\bf x}^{k}})-{{\bf s}^{k}}\\

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed an arc-search interior-point algorithm for the nonlinear
optimization problem. The algorithm searches the optimizer along an arc that is part of
an ellipse instead of a traditional straight line. Since the arc stays in the interior set
longer than any straight line, the arc step is longer than a line step, thereby producing
a better iterate and improving the efficiency of the algorithm. Convergence of the
algorithm is established under some mild conditions. Preliminary test results show that
the algorithm is very promising. A follow-on paper is under preparation to provide
details of the algorithm implementation and to report extensive test result.

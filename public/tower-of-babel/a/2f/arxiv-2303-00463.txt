Mixed local and nonlocal elliptic equation with singular and critical Choquard nonlinearity
G.C. Anthal , J. Giacomoni and K. Sreenadh

Abstract
In this article, we study a class of elliptic problems involving both local and nonlocal operators with different orders and a singular nonlinearity in combination with critical Hartree type nonlinearity (see problem ( P λ ) (P_{\lambda}) below). Using variational methods together with the critical point theory of nonsmooth analysis, we show the existence, the regularity and the multiplicity of weak solutions with respect to the parameter λ \lambda . Key words: Local-nonlocal mixed operators, singular nonlinearity, Choquard equation, nonsmooth analysis, existence results, Sobolev regularity, comparison principle, asymptotic behaviour. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35A01, 35A15, 35B33, 35D30, 35J15, 35J75, 35M10, 49J52.

Introduction

This article investigates the existence and the multiplicity of weak solutions to the following problem:

(P_{\lambda})\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}u=u^{-\gamma}+\lambda\left(\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|u|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\right)|u|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}-2}u,~&u>0~\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\gamma>0
,
n\geq 3
,
s\in(0,1)
,
0<\mu<n
,
2_{\mu}^{\ast}=\frac{2n-\mu}{n-2}
and
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary. The operator
\mathcal{M}
in
(P_{\lambda})
is defined by

\mathcal{M}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}~\text{for some}~s\in(0,1),

(1.1)

i.e., composed by a local operator, Laplacian
(-\Delta)
, and a nonlocal operator, the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, given for a fixed parameter
s\in(0,1)
, by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u=C(n,s)P.V.\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy.

The term
``P.V"
stands for the Cauchy’s principal value and
C(n,s)
is a normalizing constant, whose explicit expression is given by

C(n,s)=\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1-\cos(z_{1})}{|z|^{n+2s}}dz\right)^{-1}.

The study of mixed type operators of the form
\mathcal{M}
in (
1.1
) is motivated by a wide range of applications. Indeed these operators arise naturally in the applied sciences, to study the impact caused by a local and a nonlocal change in a physical phenomenon. In particular, these operators model diffusion patterns with different time scales (roughly speaking, the higher order operator leads the diffusion for small scales times and the lower order operator becomes predominant for large times). They appear for instance in bi-modal power law distribution processes, see
[
36
]
. Further applications occur in the theory of optimal searching, biomathematics and animal forging, see
[
16
,
17
]
and the references therein.

Due to these various important applications, the study of elliptic problems involving mixed type operators having both local and nonlocal features has attracted more and more attention. In particular, the current research work on problems involving this type of operators has investigated several issues about existence and regularity of solutions. In this mater, using probability theory, Foondum
[
18
]
, Chen et. al
[
10
]
, studied the regularity of solutions to the equation

\mathcal{M}u=0.

More recently, using a purely analytic approach, Biagi, Dipierro, Valdonoci and Veechi, in their series of papers
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, have carried out a broad investigation of problems involving mixed operators, proving a number of results, concerning regularity and qualitative behaviour of solutions, maximum principles and related variational principles. The question of Hölder regularity was investigated by De Filippis-Mingione in
[
13
]
for a large class of mixed local and nonlocal operators. Under some suitable assumptions, the authors proved almost Lipschitz continuity and local gradient Hölder regularity (see Theorem 3, 6 and 7 respectively there).

There is a large literature available for the problems with Choquard nonlinearity due to its vast application in physical modeling, see for instance the works of Pekar

[

and references therein). In
[

, a Schrödinger type problem involving a Hartree type nonlinearity and the fractional laplacian is studied. Existence, nonexistence and properties of solutions are proved in this paper.

For the Brezis-Niremberg type problems involving Choquard nonlinearities, we refer to

in the local setting, to

Problems involving singular nonlinearities have a very long history. One of the seminal breakthrough in the study of singular elliptic problems was the work of Crandall, Rabinowitz and Tartar
[

In this work, the authors proved the existence of solutions to a class of elliptic equations involving a singular nonlinearity by using the classical method of sub and supersolutions and passing to the limit on approximated problems. Subsequently, a large amount of works have been done discussing further issues about regularity, mutilplicity and qualitative properties of solutions to quasilinear elliptic and parabolic singular problems, see for instance the review articles

Without giving an exhaustive list of contributions, we quote some key references:

Motivated by the above discussion, in the present paper, we consider the singular doubly nonlocal problem
(P_{\lambda})
with any
\gamma>0
. To the best of our knowledge, there is no previous contribution dealing with critical Choquard type problems involving singular nonlinearities and mixed diffusion operators. In the spirit of
[
31

, using the theory of nonsmooth analysis together with convexity properties of the associated energy functional, we prove the existence, multiplicity (under some additional restrictions on

and

) asymptotic behavior and regularity of weak solutions to
(P_{\lambda})
for all
\gamma>0
. Precisely, we prove the following main result :

Theorem 1.1

Let
\mu<\min\{4,n\}
and
\mathcal{G}
be defined as in Theorem
2.7
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

(Existence/nonexistence)
There exists
\Lambda>0
such that
(P_{\lambda})
admits at least one weak solution for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda]
and no solution for
\lambda>\Lambda
.

(ii)

(Asymptotic behavior and regularity) Any weak solution
u
to
(P_{\lambda})
is bounded, satisfies
u\in\mathcal{G}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and for any
\nu>\max\{1,\frac{\gamma+1}{4}\}
,
u^{\nu}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, if
\gamma<3
, then
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\beta}_{loc}(\Omega)
for some
0<\beta<1
.

(iii)

(Multiplicity) Assuming
n+2s<6
, there exists at least two distinct weak solutions for any
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
.

Remark 1.2

With additional restrictions, we can further show the following regularity results (proved at the end of Section 3):

(1)

Suppose
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{n})
and
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Then infact
u\in C^{2,\alpha-2s}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
with any
\alpha<1
.

(2)

If
\gamma\in(0,1)
and

, then we have

with

.

An important step to prove Theorem
1.1
is to prescribe the asymptotic behaviour of weak solutions to
(P_{\lambda})
near the boundary
\partial\Omega
. To this goal, we prove a crucial comparison principle (see Lemma

) in the spirit of
[

]
. In addition, we show that weak solutions are bounded by using Lemma

and bootstrap techniques applied on the equivalent problem
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
obtained by translating
(P_{\lambda})
with the solution to the pure singular problem
(P_{0})
, defined in section 2. Next we establish the existence of energy solutions to
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
trapped in a conical shell defined by sub and supersolutions with suitable asymptotic behaviour near boundary. From this, regularity and asymptotic behaviour of weak solutions follow. Finally, using variational arguments, we prove the global multiplicity result of problem thanks to convex properties of the singular part of the nonlinearity and accurate estimates about energy levels associated to
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
. We want to highlight that our approach can be apply also in other situations, in particular when considering local critical perturbation instead of critical Choquard nonlinearity. The corresponding results are new for large
\gamma
according to former contributions.

Organization of the paper
: In Section
2
we define the function spaces, give some preliminaries of nonsmooth analysis and state technical results used in the subsequent sections. In Section

2
Preliminaries and auxilary results

In this section we give the functional settings and collect the notations and preliminary results required in the rest of the paper.

Let
s\in(0,1)
. For a measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define

[u]_{s}=\left(\frac{C(n,s)}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

the so-called Gagliardo seminorm of
u
of order
s
.

We define the space
X_{0}
as the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the global norm

\displaystyle\|u\|:=\left(\ll u\gg^{2}+[u]_{s}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},~u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where we define

\displaystyle\ll u\gg=\left(\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The norm
\|\cdot\|
is induced by the scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle:=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u\nabla vdx+\frac{C(n,s)}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy,~u,v\in X_{0}.

Clearly,
X_{0}
is a Hilbert space.

Remark 2.1

Note that in the definition of
\|\cdot\|
the
L^{2}
-norm of
\nabla u
is considered on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in spite of
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
(identically vanishes outside
\Omega
). This is to point out that the elements in
X_{0}
are functions defined on the entire space and not only on
\Omega
. The benefit of having this global functional setting is that these functions can be globally approximated on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|
by smooth functions with support in
\Omega
.

We see that this global definition of
\|\cdot\|
implies that the functions in
X_{0}
naturally satisfy the nonlocal Dirichlet type condition prescribed in problem
(P_{\lambda})
, that is,

u\equiv 0~\text{a.e. in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega~\text{for every}~u\in X_{0}.

(2.1)

In order to verify (
2.1
), we know (see
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
) that
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is continuously embedded into
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(with
s\in(0,1)
)
i.e.
there exists a constant
k=k(s)>0
such that, for every
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
one has

[u]_{s}^{2}\leq k(s)\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=k(s)(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}+\ll u\gg^{2}).

This, together with the classical Poincaré inequality, implies that
\|\cdot\|
and the full
H^{1}-norm
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are actually equivalent in the space
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, and hence

\displaystyle X_{0}=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u\arrowvert_{\Omega}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)~\text{and}~u\equiv 0~\text{a.e. in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}.

Now we recall the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality which is the first brick in study of the Choquard type problems.

Proposition 2.2

Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
Let
r,q>1
and
0<\mu<n
with
1/r+1/q+\mu/n=2
,
g\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),h\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, there exists a sharp constant
C(r,q,n,\mu)
independent of
g
and
h
such that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{g(x)h(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\leq C(r,q,n,\mu)|g|_{r}|h|_{q}.

In particular, let
g=h=|u|^{p}

then by Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality we see that,

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy

is well defined if
|u|^{p}\in L^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\nu=\frac{2n}{2n-\mu}>1
.
Thus, from Sobolev embedding theorems, we must have

\frac{2n-\mu}{n}\leq p\leq\frac{2n-\mu}{n-2}.

From this, for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we have

\left(\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}|u(y)|^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}\leq C(n,\mu)^{\frac{1}{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}|u|_{2^{*}}^{2}.

We denote by
S_{H,L}
the best constant associated to Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, i.e,

\displaystyle S_{H,L}=\inf\limits_{u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}}{\|u\|_{HL}^{2}}.

The best constant
S_{M}
of the mixed Sobolev embedding is defined by

S_{M}=\inf_{u\in X_{0}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|u\|^{2}}{|u|^{2}_{2^{*}}}.

We also define ’

From

(iii)

For any
\gamma>0
, we have the following asymptotic behavior:

\displaystyle\hat{u}\in\mathcal{G}(\Omega)~\text{where}~\mathcal{G}(\Omega)=\begin{cases}u:u\sim\delta~&\text{if}~\gamma<1,\\
u:u\sim\delta\ln^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{d_{\Omega}}{\delta}\right)~&\text{if}~\gamma=1,\\
u:u\sim\delta^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}~&\text{if}~\gamma>1,\end{cases}

and the Sobolev regularity

\displaystyle\hat{u}^{\nu}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)~\text{with}~\nu\begin{cases}=1~&\text{if}~\gamma<3,\\
>\frac{\gamma+1}{4}~&\text{if}~\gamma\geq 3.\end{cases}

Now we consider the following translated problem:

(\hat{P}_{\lambda})\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}u+\hat{u}^{-\gamma}-(u+\hat{u})^{-\gamma}=\lambda\left(\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{(u+\hat{u})^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\right)(u+\hat{u})^{2_{\mu}^{\ast}-1},~u>0~\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u=0~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Define the function

by

f(x,\tau)=\begin{cases}(\hat{u}(x))^{-\gamma}-(\tau+\hat{u}(x))^{-\gamma}~&\text{if}~\tau+\hat{u}(x)>0,\\
-\infty&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Also we define
\displaystyle F(x,\tau)=\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}f(x,r)dr
. Note that

is nonnegative and nondecreasing in
\tau
. Next we define the notion of subsolution and supersolution for problem
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
.

Definition 2.8

A nonnegative function
u\in X_{0}
is a subsolution (resp. a supersolution) of

if the following hold:

(i)

;

(ii)

For any nonnegative

Definition 2.9

A function

is a weak solution to

Our first lemma is the following implication.

Suppose

(P_{\lambda})

Proof.

for some

2.1
Notion of Nonsmooth analysis

To obtain the existence of nontrivial solutions to problem
(P_{\lambda})
, we use some nonsmooth analysis tools. In this subsection we collect some basic definitions, observations and recall a version of the linking theorem adapted to nonsmooth functionals. We begin with the following definition:

Definition 2.13

Let
V
be a Hilbert space and
I:V\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper (i.e.
I\not\equiv\infty)
lower semicontinuous functional.

(i)

Let
D(I)=\{u\in V:I(u)<\infty\}
be the domain of
I
. For every
u\in D(I)
, we define the Fréchet subdifferential of
I
at
u
as the set

\partial^{-}I(u)=\left\{z\in V:\liminf_{v\rightarrow u}\frac{I(v)-I(u)-\langle z,v-u\rangle_{V}}{\|v-u\|_{V}}\geq 0\right\}.

(ii)

For each
u\in V
, we define

|||\partial^{-}I(u)|||=\begin{cases}\min\{\|z\|_{V}:z\in\partial^{-}I(u)\}~&\text{if}~\partial^{-}I(u)\neq\emptyset,\\
\infty&\text{if}~\partial^{-}I(u)=\emptyset.\end{cases}

We know that
\partial^{-}I(u)
is a closed convex set which may be empty. If
u\in D(I)
is a local minimizer for
I
, then it can be seen that
0\in\partial^{-}I(u)
.

Remark 2.14

We remark that if
I_{0}:V\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper, lower semicontinuous, convex functional,
I_{1}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}
functional, and
I=I_{1}+I_{0}
, then
\partial^{-}I(u)=\nabla I_{1}(u)+\partial I_{0}(u)
for every
u\in D(I)=D(I_{0})
, where
\partial I_{0}
denotes the usual subdifferential of the convex functional
I_{0}
. Thus,
u
is said to be a critical point of
I
if
u\in D(I_{0})
and for every
v\in V
, we have
\langle\nabla I_{1}(u),v-u\rangle_{V}+I_{0}(v)-I_{0}(u)\geq 0
.

Definition 2.15

For a proper, lower semicontinuous functional
I:V\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
, we say that
I
satisfies Cerami’s variant of the Palais-Smale condition at a level
d
(in short,
I
satifies
(CPS)_{d}
), if any sequence
\{w_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset D(I)
such that
I(w_{k})\rightarrow d
and
(1+w_{k})|||\partial^{-}I(w_{k})|||\rightarrow 0
has a strongly convergent subsequence in
V
.

Analogous to the mountain pass theorem, we have the following linking theorem for nonsmooth functionals.

Theorem 2.16

[
40
]
Let
V
be a Hilbert space. Assume
I=I_{0}+I_{1}
, where
I_{0}:V\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper, lower semicontinuous, convex functional and
I_{1}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}-
functional. Let
B^{n},~S^{n-1}
denote the closed unit ball and its boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively. Let
\varphi:S^{n-1}\rightarrow D(I)
be a continuous function such that

\Sigma=\{\psi\in C(B^{n},D(I)):\psi\arrowvert_{S^{n-1}}=\varphi\}\neq\emptyset.

Let
A
be a relatively closed subset of
D(I)
such that

A\cap\varphi(S^{n-1})=\emptyset,~A\cap\psi(B^{n})\neq\emptyset~\text{for all}~\psi\in\Sigma,~\text{and}~\inf I(A)\geq\sup I(\varphi(S^{n-1})).

Define
d=\inf_{\psi\in\Sigma}\sup_{x\in B^{n}}I(\psi(x))
. Assume that
d
is finite and that
I
satisfies
(CPS)_{d}
. Then there exists
u\in D(I)
such that
I(u)=d
and
0\in\partial^{-}I(u)
. Furthermore, if
\inf J(A)=d
, then there exists
u\in A\cap D(I)
such that
I(u)=d
and
0\in\partial^{-}I(u)
.

3
Regularity of weak solutions and comparison principle

In this section, we prove regularity results about nonnegative weak solutions to
({P}_{\lambda})
. For this, we first investigate the regularity of nonnegative weak solution to
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
. We start with the
L^{\infty}
estimates obtained by Moser type iterations:

Lemma 3.1

Any nonnegative solution to
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
belongs to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
u
be a nonnegative solution to
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
. We define
u_{\tau}=\min\{u,\tau\}
for
\tau>0
. Let
\psi=u(u_{\tau})^{q-2}
,
q\geq 3
be a test function to problem
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
. Now

\displaystyle\nabla(uu_{\tau}^{\frac{q}{2}-1})=u_{\tau}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\nabla u+\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)u_{\tau}^{{\frac{q}{2}-2}}u\nabla u_{\tau}.

This implies

\displaystyle\left|\nabla(u(u_{\tau})^{\frac{q}{2}-1})\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(u_{\tau}^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}+\left(\frac{q}{2}-1\right)u_{\tau}^{{\frac{q}{2}-2}}u\frac{\partial u_{\tau}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)^{2}\leq 2\left(u_{\tau}^{q-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{q^{2}}{4}u_{\tau}^{q-4}u^{2}|\nabla u_{\tau}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{2}\left(u_{\tau}^{q-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+u_{\tau}^{q-4}u^{2}|\nabla u_{\tau}|^{2}\right).

Thus,

\int\limits_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(u(u_{\tau})^{\frac{q}{2}-1})\right|^{2}\leq\frac{q^{2}}{2}\left(\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{\tau}^{q-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int\limits_{\{u<\tau\}}u^{q-2}|\nabla u_{\tau}|^{2}\right).

(3.1)

Also we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\psi=\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(u(u_{\tau})^{q-2})=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{\tau}^{q-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+(q-2)\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{\tau}^{q-3}u\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{\tau}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}u_{\tau}^{q-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int\limits_{\{u<\tau\}}u^{q-2}|\nabla u|^{2}.

(3.2)

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3
), we get

\int\limits_{\Omega}\left|\nabla(u(u_{\tau})^{\frac{q}{2}-1})\right|^{2}\leq Cq^{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\psi.

(3.3)

Now from
[
24
, Lemma 3.5]
, we have the following inequality:

\frac{4(q-1)}{q^{2}}\left(a|a_{\tau}|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}-b|b_{\tau}|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\right)^{2}\leq(a-b)(a|a_{\tau}|^{q-2}-b|b_{\tau}|^{q-2}).

(3.4)

where
a,~b\in\mathbb{R}
,
q\geq 2
,
a_{\tau}=\min\{a,\tau\}
and
b_{\tau}=\min\{b,\tau\}
. Using (
3.4
) with
a=u(x)
and
b=u(y)
, we obtain

[u(u_{\tau})^{\frac{q}{2}-1}]_{s}^{2}\leq\frac{Cq^{2}}{q-1}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)(\psi(x)-\psi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy.

(3.5)

Using (
3.3
), (
3.5
) and Sobolev inequality, we get

\displaystyle|u(u_{\tau})^{\frac{q}{2}-1}|_{2^{*}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\ll u(u_{\tau})^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\gg^{2}+[u(u_{\tau})^{\frac{q}{2}-1}]_{s}^{2}\right)\leq Cq^{2}\langle u,\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cq^{2}\left(-\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x,u)u(u_{\tau})^{q-2}dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{(u+\hat{u})^{2_{\mu}^{*}}(u+\hat{u})^{2_{\mu}^{*}-1}u(u_{\tau})^{q-2}}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\right).

The rest of the proof follows similarly as the proof of
[
24
, Lemma 4.1]
.
\square

Lemma 3.2

Let
q>0
and let
v\in L^{(q+1)/q}(\Omega)
be a positive function and
u\in X_{0}\cap L^{q+1}(\Omega)
a positive weak solution to

\mathcal{M}u+f(x,u)=v~\text{in}~\Omega,~u=0~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.

Then
(u+\hat{u}-\epsilon)^{+}\in X_{0}
for every
\epsilon>0
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon_{1},~\epsilon_{2}>0
and set
\varphi=\min\{u,\epsilon_{1}-(\hat{u}-\epsilon_{2})^{+}\}\in X_{0}
. Note that
u-\varphi=(u+(\hat{u}-\epsilon_{2})^{+}-\epsilon_{1})^{+}\in X_{0}
. Since

0\leq v(u-\varphi)\leq vu+v\hat{u}\in L^{1}(\Omega),

and using the arguments as in the proof of Lemma
2.12
, we can show that
f(\cdot,u)(u-\varphi)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\langle u,u-\varphi\rangle+\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x,u)(u-\varphi)dx-\int\limits_{\Omega}v(u-\varphi)=0.

Now using the following inequality for the fractional Laplacian:

(-\Delta)^{s}g(u)\leq g^{\prime}(u)(-\Delta)^{s}u,

where
g
is a convex piecewise

with bounded derivative function, we have

\displaystyle\langle(\hat{u}-\epsilon_{2})^{+},\psi\rangle\leq\langle\hat{u},\psi\rangle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\hat{u}^{-\gamma}\psi dx,~\text{for every}~0\leq\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

So, arguing as in the proof of Lemma
2.12
, we can show that

\langle(\hat{u}-\epsilon_{2})^{+},u-\varphi\rangle\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}\hat{u}^{-\gamma}(u-\varphi)dx.

We note that
u+\hat{u}\geq\epsilon_{1}
when
u\neq\varphi
,
(u+\hat{u})^{-\gamma}(u-\varphi)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and
\hat{u}(u-\varphi)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|(u+(\hat{u}-\epsilon_{2})^{+}-\epsilon_{1})^{+}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\langle(u+(\hat{u}-\epsilon_{2})^{+}-\epsilon_{1})^{+},u-\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\hat{u}^{-\gamma}(u-\varphi)dx-\int\limits_{\Omega}f(x,u)(u-\varphi)dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}v(u-\varphi)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}(u+\hat{u})^{-\gamma}(u-\varphi)dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}v(u-\varphi)dx

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}^{-\gamma}\int\limits_{\Omega}(u-\psi)dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}v(u-\varphi)dx.

Thus for any
\epsilon_{1}>0
, we have that
(u+(\hat{u}-\epsilon_{2})^{+}-\epsilon_{1})^{+}
is bounded in

as
\epsilon_{2}\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Hence, we conclude that

for every

2.7
.
\square

Corollary 3.3

Let
v\in L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)
be a positive function and assume
g(x,v)=\left(\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{v^{2_{\mu}^{*}}(y)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dy\right)v^{2_{\mu}^{*}-1}
. Assume that
u\in X_{0}
be a positive weak solution to

\mathcal{M}u+f(x,u)=g(x,v)~\text{in}~\Omega,~u=0~\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega.

(3.6)

Then
(u+\hat{u}-\epsilon)^{+}\in X_{0}
for every
\epsilon>0
.

Now we establish a crucial comparison principle. It states as follows:

Lemma 3.4

Let
\mathcal{H}\in X_{0}^{*}
(the dual of
X_{0}
) and let
v,~w\in H^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
be such that
v,~w>0
a.e in
\Omega
,
v,~w\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
v^{-\gamma},~w^{-\gamma}\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
,
(v-\epsilon)^{+}\in X_{0}
for all
\epsilon>0
,
z\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\langle v,\psi\rangle\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}v^{-\gamma}\psi dx+(\mathcal{H},\psi),~\langle w,\psi\rangle\geq\int\limits_{\Omega}w^{-\gamma}\psi dx+(\mathcal{H},\psi),

(3.7)

for all compactly supported function
0\leq\psi\in X_{0}\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then
v\leq w
a.e in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let us denote
\Psi_{k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as the primitive of the function

\tau\mapsto\begin{cases}\max\{-\tau^{-q},-k\}~&\text{if}~\tau>0,\\
-k~&\text{if}~\tau\leq 0\end{cases}

such that
\Psi_{k}(1)=0
. Next we define a proper lower semicontinuous, strictly convex functional
\widetilde{G}_{0,k}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\widetilde{G}_{0,k}(u)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}+\int\limits_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}(u)dx~&\text{if}~u\in X_{0},\\
\infty~&\text{if}~u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus X_{0}.\end{cases}

As we know, primitives are usually defined up to an additive constant, to prevent a possible unlikely choice we consider
G_{0,k}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

G_{0,k}(u)=\widetilde{G}_{0,k}(u)-\min\widetilde{G}_{0,k}=\widetilde{G}_{0,k}(u)-\widetilde{G}_{0,k}(u_{0,k}),

where
u_{0,k}\in X_{0}
is the minimum of
\widetilde{G}_{0,k}
. In general, for
\mathcal{H}\in X_{0}^{*}
we set

\widetilde{G}_{\mathcal{H},k}(u)=\begin{cases}G_{0,k}(u)-(\mathcal{H},u-u_{0,k})~&\text{if}~u\in X_{0}\\
\infty~&\text{if}~u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\setminus X_{0}.\end{cases}

Let
\epsilon>0
,
k>\epsilon^{-\gamma}
, and let
z
be the minimum of the functional
\widetilde{G}_{\mathcal{H},k}
on the convex set
K=\{\psi\in X_{0}:0\leq\psi\leq w~\text{a.e in}~\Omega\}
. Then for all
\psi\in K
we get

\langle z,\psi-z\rangle\geq-\int\limits_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}^{\prime}(z)(\psi-z)dx+(\mathcal{H},\psi-z).

(3.8)

Let
0\leq\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
t>0
. Define
\psi_{t}:=\min\{z+t\psi,w\}
. Noting that
w\in H^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
,
z\in X_{0}
,
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have
\psi_{t}\in X_{0}
. Next we claim that
\psi_{t}
is uniformly bounded in
X_{0}
for all
t<1
. Using the continuous embedding of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
into
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it is sufficient to show that
\ll\psi_{t}\gg
is uniformly bounded in
t
. We have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi_{t}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\{z+t\psi\leq w\}}|\nabla z+t\nabla\psi|^{2}+\int\limits_{\{z+t\psi>w\}}|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla z|^{2}+t^{2}\int\limits_{\Omega}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+2t\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla z\nabla\psi+\int\limits_{\text{supp}\psi}|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ll z\gg^{2}+\ll\psi\gg^{2}+\ll z\gg\ll\psi\gg+\int\limits_{\text{supp}\psi}|\nabla w|^{2}<\infty.

This proves the claim. Considering the subsequence (still denoted by
\psi_{t}
) such that
\psi_{t}\rightharpoonup z
weakly in
X_{0}
and taking
\psi=\psi_{t}
in (

\langle z,\psi_{t}-z\rangle\geq-\int\limits_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}^{\prime}(z)(\psi_{t}-z)dx+(\mathcal{H},\psi_{t}-z).

(3.9)

Since
w
is a supersolution and
w^{-\gamma}\geq-\Psi_{k}^{\prime}(w)
, we infer that

satisfies

\langle w,\psi\rangle\geq-\int\limits_{\Omega}\Psi_{k}^{\prime}(w)\psi dx+(\mathcal{H},\psi).

(3.10)

Using the facts that
\psi_{t}\leq w
,
\psi_{t}-z-t\psi\leq 0
and
\psi_{t}-z-t\psi\neq 0
only if
\psi_{t}=w
, we observe that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\nabla\psi_{t}\nabla(\psi_{t}-z-t\psi)+\frac{C(n,s)}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\psi_{t}(x)-\psi_{t}(y))((\psi_{t}-z-t\psi)(x)-(\psi_{t}-z-t\psi)(y))}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq

r\in[0,1]

\|\Psi(r)-v\|=\zeta
. Thus by Theorem
2.16
, we get there exists
w\in D(\Phi_{K_{v}})
such that
w\neq v
,
\Phi_{K_{v}}(w)=d
and
0\in\partial^{-}\Phi_{K_{v}}(w)
. Using Proposition
4.2
, we obtain that
w
is a positive weak solution to
(\hat{P}_{\lambda})
.
\square

End of Proof of Theorem
1.1
:
Combining Lemma
3.6
, Theorem
4.8
and Proposition
4.14
, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.
\square

Hypergeometric sheaves with tannakian monodromy group G 2 G_{2}
Beat Zurbuchen

Abstract
. Based on a suggestion by Katz, we determine the Tannakian monodromy group of certain ℓ \ell -adic hypergeometric sheaves to be the exceptional group G 2 G_{2} . Using the smoothing properties of the Fourier transform over the integers, known as uniformity theorems, we prove that the fourth moment is constant on an open locus of the family of hypergeometric sheaves. In our example, this implies a comparison theorem for the Tannakian monodromy groups which determines these groups if the characteristic is large.



1.
Introduction

The equidistribution of Frobenius conjugacy classes has been a cornerstone of number theory at least since Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions. In a geometric context, this study was successfully initiated by Deligne in
[
1
]
. The central object of this theory is a compact Lie group, the so-called monodromy group, in which Frobenius conjugacy classes acting on a local system on a variety over a finite field naturally occur and then equidistribute. This provides a formalism to derive concrete equidistribution results in large generality. These results can be applied to local systems whose Frobenius traces parametrize a family of exponential sums. Such an application implies equidistribution results for the family of exponential sums.

These monodromy groups were determined for a large class of
\ell
-adic local systems, among them the so-called hypergeometric sheaves (see
[
7
, Ch. 8]
), by Katz. For example, consider a finite field
k
of odd characteristic
p
and a prime
\ell\neq p
. Let
\psi
be a non-trivial
\ell
-adic additive character of
k
. Let
\lambda_{2}
denote the unique non-trivial
\ell
-adic multiplicative character of order 2 of
k
and denote the associated Gauss sums by

A_{\psi,k}:=-\sum_{x\in k^{*}}\lambda_{2}(x)\psi(x).

For each
a\in k^{*}
and each
\ell
-adic multiplicative character
\chi
of
k
define

\text{Hyp}(a,\chi):=A_{\psi,k}^{-7}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\cdots x_{7}=x_{8}a\\
x_{1},\ldots,x_{8}\in k^{*}\end{subarray}}\psi(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{7}-x_{8})\chi\big(x_{4}x_{5}(x_{6}x_{7})^{-1}\big)\lambda_{2}(x_{8}).

Define the complex of constructible
\ell
-adic sheaves on
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}
(see
[
7
, 8.2.2]
for the notation)

\mathcal{F}(\chi,\psi,k):=(A_{\psi,k}^{-7})^{\text{deg}}\otimes\textnormal{Hyp}(!,\psi;1,1,1,\chi,\chi,\overline{\chi},\overline{\chi};\lambda_{2})

where
(A_{\psi,k}^{-7})^{\text{deg}}
denotes the pullback along the structure map
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}\rightarrow\textnormal{Spec}(k)
of the
\ell
-adic character which maps
\text{Fr}_{k}\mapsto A_{\psi,k}^{-7}
. This complex has the property

\text{Tr}(\text{Fr}_{k}|\mathcal{F}(\chi,\psi,k)_{{a}})=\text{Hyp}(a,\chi)

for all
a\in k^{*}
and all multiplicative characters
\chi.
Relations such as this, which are consequences of the Lefschetz fixed point formula for constructible
\ell
-adic sheaves, are the primary reason for the appearance of cohomological methods in the theory of exponential sums.

Fix a multiplicative character
\chi
of
k
and consider the algebraic group
G_{2}
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Fix an isomorphism
\tau\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\cong\mathbb{C}
. This isomorphism allows us to base change
G_{2}
to
\mathbb{C}
to obtain a complex algebraic group
G_{2,\mathbb{C}}
. Pick a maximal compact subgroup
UG_{2}\subset G_{2,\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C})
and let
UG_{2}^{\natural}
be the space of conjugacy classes in
UG_{2}
. Following
[
8
, Thm. 8.1]
, there are semisimple conjugacy classes
\theta_{a,\chi}\in UG_{2}^{\natural}
such that the trace of
\theta_{a,\chi}
acting on the unique irreducible seven-dimensional representation of
UG_{2}
is given by

\text{Tr}(\theta_{a,\chi})=\tau(\text{Hyp}(a,\chi)).

Denote by
k_{n}
a finite extension of
k
of degree
n\geq 1
and for a multiplicative character
\chi
on
k
define
\chi_{n}(x):=\chi(\text{Nm}_{k_{n}/k}(x))
for all
x\in k_{n}^{*}
. When
p\geq 17,
Deligne’s equidistribution theorem
[
7
, Thm. 7.11.1]
applied to the determination of the monodromy group of
\mathcal{F}(\chi,\psi,k)
in
[
8
, Thm. 8.1]
implies

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|k_{n}^{*}|}\sum_{a\in k_{n}^{*}}f(\theta_{a,\chi_{n}})=\int_{UG_{2}}f(g)dg

for any continuous, central function
f\in C(UG_{2})
where

denotes the probability Haar measure on
UG_{2}.
We say that the set
\{\theta_{a,\chi}:a\in k_{n}^{*}\}
, where
\chi
remains fixed, equidistributes in
UG_{2}^{\natural}
for the pushforward of the probability Haar measure from
UG_{2}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

The equidistribution result by Deligne is not able to describe the distribution of the set of conjugacy classes
\{\theta_{a,\chi}:\chi\in\widehat{k_{n}^{*}}\}
for fixed
a
and variable
\chi
because the multiplicative characters are not representable by a variety. In
[
9
, Ch. 4]
, a subcategory of the category of
\ell
-adic perverse sheaves on
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}
is equipped with the structure of a neutral
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-linear Tannakian category, which associates an arithmetic Tannakian monodromy group to each object in this category. By design, this formalism proves and describes the equidistribution of such sets. Katz explains in the book
[
9
, Ch. 25]
(see also Theorem
2.7
) how to apply this formalism to the above sums and determines the Tannakian monodromy group for most of the perverse sheaves occurring in the family defined by the variable
a
.

Theorem

(
[
9
, Thm. 25.1]
)
.

The set of
a\in k^{*}
such that the set of conjugacy classes
\{\theta_{a,\chi}:\chi\in\widehat{k_{n}^{*}}\}
equidistributes in

as

.

Based on a suggestion in
[

, Rmk. 25.8]
, we improve this theorem to the following result.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.2
)
.

Suppose the characteristic of
k
is large enough and let
a\in k^{*}
. The set of conjugacy classes
\{\theta_{a,\chi}:\chi\in\widehat{k^{*}_{n}}\}
equidistributes in
UG_{2}^{\natural}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

We are not able to use our method to give an effective lower bound on the characteristic of
k
. The reason lies in the application of Theorem
5.1
. This theorem provides an ineffective non-empty open subset of the spectrum of the integers such that for each finite field
k
whose spectrum maps into the open subset the fourth moment is constant in the family defined by
a\in k^{*}
. We do not know how to provide such an open subspace without appealing to Theorem
5.1
, thus we cannot provide an effective lower bound on the characteristic. For an exposition of uniformity results, such as Theorem
5.1
, the reader is referred to
[
10
, Ch. V]
.

Furthermore, it is not clear whether the Tannakian monodromy group associated with hypergeometric sheaves remains
G_{2}
over fields of small characteristic. For monodromy groups of hypergeometric sums, it is a well-known phenomenon (see
[
8
, Thm 8.1]
and
[
7
, Thm. 14.10]
) that (geometric) monodromy groups become uniform only for fields of large characteristic. For example, it is proven in
[
8
, Thm. 8.1]
that when the characteristic satisfies
p<17
the monodromy group of
\mathcal{F}(\chi,\psi,k)
becomes a finite group contained in
G_{2}
for some multiplicative characters
\chi
. The Tannakian monodromy group in our example could degenerate to the smaller group
\textnormal{SL}_{2}
(see Theorem
2.7
). On the other hand, Katz demonstrated in a small number of cases – via computer calculations – that the Tannakian monodromy group is
G_{2}
(see
[
9
, Rem. 25.8]
).

Our method should extend to more general hypergeometric families, such as those constructed in
[
5
]
. Consider
n>1
and a perverse !-hypergeometric sheaf
\mathscr{H}
on
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}^{n}
in the sense of
[
5
, Def. 8.1.2]
. In favorable cases, a group morphism
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
induces a hypergeometric family by restricting
\mathscr{H}
to the fibers. The perverse sheaves studied in this paper are of this form up to negligible composition factors – that is, factors whose Euler-Poincaré characteristic vanishes. In future work, we plan to apply this method to determine the Tannakian monodromy groups for other members of such families.

Our proof relies heavily on Katz’s determination of the Tannakian monodromy group for a generic member in the family parametrized by
a
. We study the sum
\textnormal{Hyp}(a,\chi)
, where
a\in k^{*}
. Lemma
3.4
shows that varying the base point
a
is equivalent to varying the additive character
\psi
. The key step is to formulate the fourth moment of the Tannakian monodromy group as a weighted Euler-Poincaré characteristic (see Theorem
4.5
). We then show that this fourth moment – and hence the Tannakian monodromy group itself – is independent of
\psi
, if the characteristic is large enough, by appealing to the uniformity result of Theorem
5.1
. This implies that the Tannakian monodromy group is independent of the parameter
a
, provided the characteristic of
k
is large enough. In particular, the Tannakian monodromy group for any parameter
a
has to agree with the Tannakian monodromy group of a generic member, which is
G_{2}
.

Definition 2.2

(

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
be a perverse sheaf on
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}
which is pure of weight

and
\chi

. The cohomology groups

H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},M\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\chi})

are concentrated in degree zero and pure of weight

Proof.

The Leray spectral sequence implies that the map

{H}_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},M\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\chi})\rightarrow{H}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},M\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\chi})

is an isomorphism. Since the complex

is perverse, Artin’s vanishing theorem
[

says that the cohomology groups on the left are concentrated in non-negative degrees and the cohomology groups on the right are concentrated in non-positive degrees since

is affine. Thus they are concentrated in degree zero. Note that
[
1
, Var. 6.2.3]
says that the left side is of weight
\leq w
and the right side is of weight

. Thus the cohomology group is pure of weight

.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The vanishing is recorded in

The formalism of
[
9
, Ch. 4]
(see also
[

Let

(2)

(3)

There exists

and

such that the monodromy group of

is
G_{2}
.

(4)

The fourth moment satisfies

The points 1. and 2. are proven in

3.
The fourth moment

In the previous section, we proved that it is sufficient to compute the fourth moment of
\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}
to determine its Tannakian monodromy group. The goal of this section is to derive a concrete formula for the fourth moment as a limit using the equidistribution results for Tannakian monodromy groups.

Definition 3.1
.

Define the Laurent polynomial

P(x_{i,j},y_{i,j}):=\prod_{j\in\{1,2\}}x_{4,j}x_{5,j}y_{6,j}y_{7,j}(y_{4,j}y_{5,j}x_{6,j}x_{7,j})^{-1}

as an element of the ring

P\in\mathbb{Z}[(x^{\pm 1}_{i,j})_{(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,8\}\times\{1,2\}},(y_{i,j}^{\pm 1})_{(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,8\}\times\{1,2\}}].

Let
k
be a finite field,
\psi
a non-trivial additive character of
k
, and
a\in k^{*}
. Define

\displaystyle f(\psi,k,a):=|k|^{-15}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1,j}\dots x_{7,j}=ax_{8,j}\\
y_{1,j}\dots y_{7,j}=ay_{8,j}\\
P(x_{i,j},y_{i,j})=1\end{subarray}}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\psi\Bigg(\sum_{j\in\{1,2\}}\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{7}(x_{i,j}-y_{i,j})-x_{8,j}+y_{8,j}\Bigg)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\times\lambda_{2}\Bigg(\prod_{j\in\{1,2\}}x_{8,j}y_{8,j}^{-1}\Bigg)\Bigg)

where we sum over all values
x_{i,j}\in k^{*}
and
y_{i,j}\in k^{*}
which satisfy the condition in the sum.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
k
be a finite field,
\psi
a non-trivial additive character of
k
, and
a\in k^{*}
. We have

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(\psi_{n},k_{n},a).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.5
, all characters are good for
\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}
. Thus
[
7
, Thm. 7.3]
and
[
2
, Eqn. 9.2]
imply

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|k_{n}|-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{k_{n}^{*}}}|\text{Tr}(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}|H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\chi}))|^{4}.

Remark that the terms in this sum are represented by hypergeometric sums

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}

\displaystyle|H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\chi}))=

\displaystyle(A_{\psi,k})^{-7n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\cdots x_{7}=ax_{8}\\
x_{1},\ldots,x_{8}\in k_{n}^{*}\end{subarray}}\psi_{n}(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{7}-x_{8})\chi(x_{4}x_{5}(x_{6}x_{7})^{-1})\lambda_{2}(x_{8})

for all multiplicative characters
\chi\neq\lambda_{2}
of
k_{n}
by Theorem
2.5
and
[
7
, 8.2.7]
. A priori, we can not simply replace the terms in the above formula for the moment by hypergeometric sums because of the term with
\chi=\lambda_{2}
. We replace the terms in this sum by hypergeometric sums by first excluding the term with
\chi=\lambda_{2}
. Afterwards, we replace the terms with hypergeometric sums, and then we complete the sum by adding the hypergeometric sum with
\chi=\lambda_{2}
.

The complex
\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}
is pure of weight zero. Lemma
2.3
implies that the cohomology groups
H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\lambda_{2}})
are pure of weight zero and concentrated in degree zero. This implies that the term

|\text{Tr}(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}|H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\lambda_{2}}))|^{4}\leq\chi_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}))^{4}

is bounded as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Hence we can exclude the character
\lambda_{2}
from the limit and write

\displaystyle M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|k_{n}|-1}\sum_{\chi\neq\lambda_{2}}|\text{Tr}(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}|H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\chi}))|^{4}.

We apply the expression of the terms as hypergeometric sums to obtain

\displaystyle M_{4}(

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|A_{\psi,k}|^{-28n}}{|k_{n}|-1}\sum_{\chi\neq\lambda_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1,j}\dots x_{7,j}=ax_{8,j}\\
y_{1,j}\dots y_{7,j}=ay_{8,j}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\chi(P(x_{i,j},y_{i,j}))

\displaystyle\times\lambda_{2}\Bigg(\prod_{j\in\{1,2\}}x_{8,j}(y_{8,j})^{-1}\Bigg)\psi_{n}\Bigg(\sum_{j\in\{1,2\}}\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{7}(x_{i,j}-y_{i,j})-x_{8,j}+y_{8,j}\Bigg)\Bigg)\Bigg).

It follows from the cancellation theorem
[
7
, 8.4.7]
that we have an exact sequence of perverse sheaves

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow V\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\lambda_{2}}[1]\rightarrow

\displaystyle\text{Hyp}(!,\psi;1,1,1,\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2};\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\text{Hyp}(!,\psi;1,1,1,\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2};\emptyset)\rightarrow 0

where
V:=H_{c}^{0}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},\text{Hyp}(!,\psi;\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2},1,1,1;\emptyset))
. Note that
V
is pure of weight 6 by Künneth’s formula and
\text{Hyp}(!,\psi:1,1,1,\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2};\emptyset)
is pure of weight 6 by
[
7
, Thm. 8.4.2 (4)]
. By
[
7
, Thm. 8.4.2 (6)]
, we get the estimate

\Bigg|(A_{\psi,k})^{-7}\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{7}=ax_{8}}\psi(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{7}-x_{8})\lambda_{2}(x_{4}x_{5}(x_{6}x_{7})^{-1})\lambda_{2}(x_{8})\Bigg|\leq 7.

Thus we can complete the above sum to

\displaystyle M_{4}(

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|A_{\psi,k_{n}}|^{-28}}{|k_{n}|-1}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{k_{n}^{*}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1,j}\dots x_{7,j}=ax_{8,j}\\
y_{1,j}\dots y_{7,j}=ay_{8,j}\end{subarray}}\Bigg(\chi(P(x_{i,j},y_{i,j}))

\displaystyle\times\lambda_{2}\Bigg(\prod_{j\in\{1,2\}}x_{8,j}(y_{8,j})^{-1}\Bigg)\psi\Bigg(\sum_{j\in\{1,2\}}\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{7}(x_{i,j}-y_{i,j})-x_{8,j}+y_{8,j}\Bigg)\Bigg)\Bigg).

The term
\frac{|A_{\psi,k_{n}}|^{-28}}{|k_{n}|-1}
is asymptotically equivalent to
|k_{n}|^{-15}
.
We change the order of summation and then the orthogonality of characters of
{k_{n}^{*}}
implies

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(\psi_{n},k_{n},a).

This is the statement of the theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
k
be a finite field,
\psi
a non-trivial additive character of
k
,
a\in k^{*}
, and
n\geq 1.
Then

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{n},k_{n},a}).

Proof.

Theorem
3.2
implies

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}f(\psi_{m},k_{m},a)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}f(\psi_{mn},k_{mn},a)=M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{n},k_{n},a}).

This is the statement of the corollary.
∎

The following formula for the change of the additive character lies at the heart of our argument.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k
be a finite field,
\psi
a non-trivial additive character of
k
, and
a,\lambda\in k^{*}
. Define the additive character
\psi_{\lambda}(x):=\psi(\lambda x)
for all
x\in k
. Then

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{\lambda},k,a})=M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,\lambda^{6}a}).

Proof.

Just as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
we have

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{\lambda},k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|k_{n}|-1}\sum_{\chi\neq\lambda_{2}}|\text{Tr}(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}|H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{k}},\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{\lambda},k,a}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\chi}))|^{4}.

We define
\psi_{\lambda,n}:=(\psi_{\lambda})_{n}
. Using Theorem
2.5
, we can write the above limit in the form

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{\lambda},k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|k_{n}|-1}\sum_{\chi\neq\lambda_{2}}|\text{Tr}\big(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}|\mathcal{F}(\chi,\psi_{\lambda,n},k_{n})_{{a}}\big)|^{4}.

Note that
A_{\psi_{\lambda},k}=\lambda_{2}(\lambda)A_{\psi,k}
. Moreover, substitution implies the formula

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\cdots x_{7}=ax_{8}\\
x_{1},\ldots,x_{8}\in k_{n}^{*}\end{subarray}}\psi_{\lambda,n}(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{7}-x_{8})\lambda_{2}(x_{8})=\lambda_{2}(\lambda)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y_{1}\cdots y_{7}=\lambda^{6}ay_{8}\\
y_{1},\ldots,y_{8}\in k_{n}^{*}\end{subarray}}\psi_{n}(y_{1}+\cdots+y_{7}-y_{8})\lambda_{2}(y_{8}).

These formulas for changing the character
\psi
imply

|\text{Tr}\big(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}|\mathcal{F}(\chi,\psi_{\lambda,n},k_{n})_{{a}}\big)|^{4}=|\text{Tr}\big(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}|\mathcal{F}(\chi,\psi_{n},k_{n})_{{\lambda^{6}a}}\big)|^{4}

for all multiplicative characters
\chi\neq\lambda_{2}
of
k_{n}^{*}
by Theorem
2.5
. We use this transformation law in the formula for the fourth moment to get

\displaystyle M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{\lambda},k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|k_{n}|-1}\sum_{\chi\neq\lambda_{2}}|\text{Tr}\big(\text{Fr}_{k_{n}}|\mathcal{F}(\chi,\psi_{n},k_{n})_{{\lambda^{6}a}}\big)|^{4}=M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,\lambda^{6}a}).

This is what we wanted to prove.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The equality of moments reflects an isomorphism of sheaves on
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}^{2}
. More precisely, this equality follows from a change of characters formula for !-hypergeometric sheaves on
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}^{4}
as in
[
5
, Def. 8.1.2]
. For a hypergeometric sheaf on
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}
, this formula is given by
[
7
, Lem. 8.7.2]
.

4.
Weighted Euler-Poincaré characteristics and the moment

Informally speaking, this section expresses the function
f
from Definition
3.1
as the trace function of an additive Fourier transform of a sheaf on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The equality of the geometric and the arithmetic Tannakian monodromy group implies a formula for the moment in terms of the weight filtration on the stalk of the Fourier transform at 1. This expression, in turn, can be controlled by appealing to the uniformity properties of the Fourier transform. We introduce an ad-hoc notion for a function which is the “Fourier transform” of a trace function of an
\ell
-adic complex on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
g(\psi,k)
be a complex-valued function that takes as an input a finite field
k
and a non-trivial additive character
\psi
of
k
. Let
U\subset\text{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
be a dense open subset. We say that the function
g

is representable by a Fourier transform over
U
if there exists a mixed complex
K
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{U}
such that

g(\psi,k)=\text{Tr}(\text{Fr}_{k}|H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\overline{k}},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi}))

for all finite extensions
k/\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
p\in U
and all additive characters
\psi
on
k
. The complex
K
is said to be a
representing complex for the function
f
.

Theorem 4.2
.

The function
(\psi,k)\mapsto f(\psi,k,1)
is representable by a Fourier transform over
\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell]
.

Proof.

The squaring map
[2]:\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell]}\to\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell]}
is a finite étale Galois cover with Galois group
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore, the pushforward
[2]_{\ast}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
decomposes as a direct sum
[2]_{\ast}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\cong\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\oplus\mathscr{L}
, where
\mathscr{L}
is a non-trivial rank one local system on
\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell]}
. By
[
4
, Cor. 5.3.9]
, for any closed point
k
of
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell])
, the local system
\mathscr{L}
restricted to the fiber
\mathbb{G}_{m,k}
is isomorphic to the direct factor
\mathscr{L}_{\lambda_{2}}
in the lisse sheaf
[2]_{*}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
. Consider the closed subscheme
Z\subset\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell]}^{32}
defined by the equations

\displaystyle x_{1,j}\dots x_{7,j}=x_{8,j},\ y_{1,j}\dots y_{7,j}=y_{8,j},\ P(x_{i,j},y_{i,j})=1

for all
j\in\{1,2\}.
Define the maps
\varphi_{1}\colon Z\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell]}
and
\varphi_{2}\colon Z\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell]}^{1}
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(x_{i,j},y_{i,j})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\in\{1,2\}}x_{8,i}y_{8,i}^{-1}

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(x_{i,j},y_{i,j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\{1,2\}}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{7}(x_{i,j}-y_{i,j})-x_{8,j}+y_{8,j}\bigg).

Put
K:=R\varphi_{2!}(\varphi_{1}^{*}(\mathscr{L}(-15))).
Consider a finite extension
k/\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
p
coprime to
2\ell
and a non-trivial additive character
\psi
. Denote by
Z_{\overline{k}}
the fiber of
Z
over the geometric point
\overline{k}
in the spectrum of
\mathbb{Z}[1/2\ell]
. The projection formula implies that there is a Frobenius-equivariant isomorphism

H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\overline{k}},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi})\cong H^{\bullet}_{c}(Z_{\overline{k}},\varphi_{1}^{*}(\mathscr{L}_{\lambda_{2}})\otimes\varphi_{2}^{*}(\mathscr{L}_{\psi}))(-15).

The Lefschetz trace formula and the twist in the definition of
K
imply

\text{Tr}(\text{Fr}_{k}|H^{\bullet}_{c}(Z_{\overline{k}},\varphi_{1}^{*}(\mathscr{L}_{\lambda_{2}})\otimes\varphi_{2}^{*}(\mathscr{L}_{\psi}))(-15))=f(\psi,k,1).

The previous two equations combined imply that
K
represents the function from the lemma.
∎

To get the most out of Theorem
4.2
, we use the following well-known proposition. We delay the proof of this proposition to Section
6
because it is unrelated to the rest of the article.

Proposition 4.3
.

Consider
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
such that the limit
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}^{N}
exists. Then it is given by

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}^{N}=\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|=1}\alpha_{i}.

Recall the definition of the weighted Euler-Poincaré characteristic.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
a prime power. Consider a
\mathbb{C}[T]
-module
V
which is finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{C}
. Define
V_{w}\subset V
to be the sum of all generalized eigenspaces of
T
acting on
V
with respect to eigenvalues
\lambda
whose absolute value satisfies
|\lambda|=q^{w/2}.

Let
M
be a bounded complex of
\mathbb{C}[T]
-modules, whose cohomology groups
H^{i}(M)
are finite-dimensional over
\mathbb{C}
. Define the
weighted Euler-Poincaré characteristic of
M
with weight
w
to be (see
[
6
, p. 92]
)

\chi_{w}(M):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}\text{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(H^{i}(M)_{w}).

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
k
be a finite field of characteristic coprime to
2\ell
,
K
a representing complex for the function
(\psi,k)\mapsto f(\psi,k,1)
,
a\in k^{*}
, and
\psi
a non-trivial additive character of
k
. Then we have

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\chi_{0}(H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\overline{k}},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi})).

Proof.

Theorem
3.2
and the definition of representability imply

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\textnormal{Tr}(\textnormal{Fr}_{k}^{n}|H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\overline{k}},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi})).

For all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\lambda_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}
where
1\leq j\leq d_{i}
be the eigenvalues of Frobenius acting on the cohomology group
H^{i}_{c}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\overline{k}},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi})
. We have

\textnormal{Tr}(\textnormal{Fr}_{k}^{n}|H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\overline{k}},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi}))=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{j}}(-1)^{i}\lambda_{i,j}^{n},

so Proposition
4.3
implies

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=\sum_{|\lambda_{i,j}|=1}(-1)^{i}=\chi_{0}\big(H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\overline{k}},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi})\big).

∎

5.
Determination of the Tannakian monodromy group

We recall a theorem on the uniformity of the Fourier transform by Katz. For an exposition of uniformity results for the Fourier transform, the reader is referred to
[
10
, Ch. V]
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
6
, p. 92, Cor. 1]
)
.

Consider a ring
R\subset\mathbb{C}
that is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}
, a constructible, mixed complex of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-sheaves
K
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}
and an integer
w\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then there exists a non-zero
r\in R
such that for all ring morphisms
R[1/r\ell]\rightarrow k
into a finite field
k
and all non-trivial additive characters
\psi
of
k
, the integer

\chi_{w}(H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{k}}^{1},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi}{})),

where the restriction is taken along the map
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{R}
induced by the ring morphism, is independent of the ring morphism and the character.

We have collected all the required results to prove the main theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C>2
such that for all primes
p>C
, all finite extensions
k/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, all non-trivial additive characters
\psi
of
k
, and all
a\in k^{*}
the perverse sheaf
\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}
has Tannakian monodromy group
G_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
K
be a representing complex for the function
(\psi,k)\mapsto f(\psi,k,1)
. Remark that Theorem
5.1
implies that there is a constant
C>2
such that for each prime
p>C
, each finite extension
k/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and each non-trivial additive character
\psi
of
k
the integer

\chi_{0}(H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{k}}^{1},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi}{}))

does neither depend on
\psi
nor on
k
.

Let
p>C
be a prime,
k/\mathbb{F}_{p}
a finite extension,
\psi
a non-trivial additive character, and
a\in k^{*}
. There exists a finite extension
k_{n}
of
k
and an element
b\in k_{n}
such that
M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{n},k_{n},b})=4
by Theorem
2.7
. Define the field
k_{m}:=k_{n}(a^{1/6},b^{1/6})
and the non-trivial additive character
\psi^{\prime}(x):=\psi_{m}(a^{1/6}x)
of
k_{m}.
Corollary
3.3
, Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
4.5
imply

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{{\psi^{\prime}},k_{m},1})=\chi_{0}(H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{k}}^{1},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi^{\prime}})).

Define the additive character
\psi^{\prime\prime}(x):=\psi_{m}(b^{1/6}x)
of
k_{m}
. Corollary
3.3
, Lemma
3.4
and Theorem
4.5
imply

4=M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi_{m},k_{m},b})=M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi^{\prime\prime},k_{m},1})=\chi_{0}(H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{k}}^{1},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi^{\prime\prime}})).

The uniformity result says

\chi_{0}(H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{k}}^{1},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi^{\prime\prime}}))=\chi_{0}(H^{\bullet}_{c}(\mathbb{A}_{\overline{k}}^{1},K\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\psi^{\prime}})),

so we can chain all the equations to get

M_{4}(\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a})=4.

Hence Theorem
2.7
implies that the Tannakian monodromy group of
\mathscr{H}_{\psi,k,a}
is
G_{2}.

∎

6.
Sums of powers

In this section, we prove the remaining Proposition
4.3
. This proposition and the following lemmas are well-known but we prove them here because we were not able to find a reference. Define

\mathbb{T}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and define
A\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
to be the closure of the subgroup generated by
x.
A continuous function
f\colon A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}f(x^{N})

exists is constant.

This is a consequence of the Kronecker–Weyl theorem, see for example
[
11
, Thm. B.6.5 (1)]
. The Kronecker–Weyl theorem implies that the set
\{x^{n}:n\geq N\}
is everywhere dense in
A
for any
N\geq 0
(since the indicator function of a non-empty open subset can never have measure zero with respect to a Haar measure), thereby proving Lemma
6.1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Define the functions
\phi_{z}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

\phi_{z}(n)=z^{n}

for each
z\in\mathbb{C}.
These functions are linearly independent in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Suppose

\lambda_{0}\phi_{z_{0}}+\ldots+\lambda_{m}\phi_{z_{m}}=0

for pairwise distinct
z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
and arbitrary
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Note that the Vandermonde matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
z_{0}&z_{1}&\cdots&z_{m}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
z_{0}^{m}&z_{1}^{m}&\cdots&z_{m}^{m}.\end{pmatrix}

is invertible because of the well-known formula for the determinant. The linear relation implies

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\lambda_{i}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
z_{i}\\
\vdots\\
z_{i}^{m}\end{pmatrix}=0.

The invertibility of the Vandermonde matrix implies
\lambda_{i}=0
which implies that the
\phi_{z}
are linearly independent.
∎

We prove Proposition
4.3
. Suppose
|\lambda_{i}|\leq 1.
We have

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}^{N}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda_{i}|=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}^{N}

because the terms with
|\lambda_{i}|<1
converge to zero as
N\rightarrow\infty.
Let
\mathbf{\lambda}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the tuple of all
\lambda_{i}
with
|\lambda_{i}|=1
and consider the subgroup
A
generated by
\mathbf{\lambda}
. The function

f\colon(\gamma_{i})_{i}\in A\mapsto\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|=1}\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i}

is continuous. The existence of the limit
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}f(\mathbf{\gamma}^{N})
implies that the function is constant by Lemma
6.1
. Hence

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda_{i}|=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}^{N}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}f(\mathbf{\gamma}^{N})=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}f(\mathbf{\gamma}^{0})=f(\mathbf{\gamma}^{0})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda_{i}|=1\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}^{0}=\sum_{|\lambda_{i}|=1}\alpha_{i}.

Consider arbitrary
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
and define

\beta_{z}:=\sum_{\lambda_{i}=z}\alpha_{i}

for each
z\in\mathbb{C}
. We can write

\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}\lambda_{i}^{N}=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\beta_{z}z^{N}

for each
N\geq 0.
Let
M:=\max(\{|z|:\beta_{z}\neq 0\}\cup\{1\})
and suppose
M>1.
Then

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\beta_{z}(z/M)^{N}=\Big(\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\beta_{z}z^{N}\Big)\cdot\Big(\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}M^{-N}\Big)=0.

Thus

0=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{|z|=M}\beta_{z}(z/M)^{N}.

The powers all have absolute value
\leq 1
, by definition of
M
, so the above argument implies

0=\sum_{|z|=M}\beta_{z}(z/M)^{N}

for all
N\geq 0
. Lemma
6.2
implies
\beta_{z}=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|=M
. This is a contradiction to the definition of
M
, and therefore
M\leq 1.
Thus we are in the special case treated above.
∎

Abstract
. We study embeddability of superschemes into Π \Pi -projective spaces and into supergrassmannians G ( 1 | 1 , n | n ) G(1|1,n|n) . We give some criteria based on the relation with 𝔸 0 | 1 {\mathbb{A}}^{0|1} -torsors and 𝔸 0 | 1 {\mathbb{A}}^{0|1} -fibrations. We also prove the existence of nice quotients for free actions of 𝔸 0 | 1 {\mathbb{A}}^{0|1} on superschemes.



1.
Introduction

A striking difference between supergeometry and the usual geometry is the absence of (super)projective embeddings for many natural proper superschemes,
e.g., for most supergrassmannians. This is explained by the fact that by considering bosonic quotients of superschemes (i.e., considering only even functions)
one often gets nontrivial nilpotent extensions of the corresponding reduced schemes, which tend to be nonprojective.

In some respects an adequate analog of projective schemes in supergeometry is provided by schemes embeddable into supergrassmannians.
Recall that the supergrassmannian
G(a|b,m|n)
parametrizes subspaces of dimension
a|b
in a supervector space of dimension
m|n
.
Thus, by fixing possible values of
a
and
b
we get a hierarchy of spaces in supergeometry: for each
a|b
we can consider
a|b
-embeddable schemes
, i.e.,
those embeddable into
G(a|b,m|n)
for some
m|n
. Note that the usual projectivity corresponds to
1|0
-embeddability.
One can also consider embeddability into other homogeneous superspaces. For example, Manin considered in
[
7
, V.6]

\Pi
-projective spaces

{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{\Pi}
(aka
\Pi
-symmetric superprojective space
) which are homogeneous spaces for the simple supergroups of type
Q
.
More generally, one has the
\Pi
-grassmannian
G\Pi(a|a,n|n)
which is a closed subscheme in
G(a|a,n|n)
, parametrizing
\Pi
-symmetric subspaces in a superspace
with a
\Pi
-symmetry (see
[
7
, V.6]
). Note that
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{\Pi}=G\Pi(1|1,n+1|n+1)
.

Even the first few stages of this hierarchy, namely
\Pi
-projectivity and
1|1
-embeddability are still poorly understood.
In the present paper we will contribute to their study by providing some general criteria and some new examples.
Previous works in this direction mostly construct examples of non-projective, and sometimes non-
\Pi
-projective, superschemes (see e.g.,
[
3
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
).

It is well known that projectivity is equivalent to the existence of an (even) line bundle whose restriction to the bosonization is ample (see
[
6
]
,
[
4
, Prop. A.2]
).
The natural approach to understanding embeddability into
\Pi
-projective spaces and into
G(1|1,n|n)

is by studying
1|1
-bundles (equipped with a
\Pi
-symmetry if we are interested in morphisms to
\Pi
-projective spaces).
Our main idea is to study
1|1
-bundles by looking at their projectivizations which are locally trivial
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibrations (since
{\mathbb{P}}^{1|1}={\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
).
Similarly, to a
\Pi
-symmetric
1|1
bundle one can associate an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor.
Thus, we are led to the study of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibrations (resp.,
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsors).
More generally, to a vector bundle of rank
a|b
one can associate an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|ab}
-fibration, however,
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
-fibrations are much harder to study for
n>1
.

Some of the results of this paper are inspired by the result proved in
[
2
, Sec. 4.9]
, stating that a superscheme is
embeddable into some supergrassmannian if and only if there exists an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
-fibration whose total space is projective.
Furthermore, any
1|1
-embeddable superscheme admits an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration with projective total space. Using this we will get criteria for a superscheme not to be

1|1
-embeddable. For example, we prove that
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\Pi}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\Pi}
and

for

,

, are
not
1|1
-embeddable.

On the other hand, we show that there is a close relation between
\Pi
-projectivity and the existence of an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor with projective total space.
This is due to the existence of a natural map

{\mathbb{P}}^{n|n+1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{\Pi}

which has a structure of an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor. Namely, let

be an

-dimensional space with a

-symmetry, then this

-dimensional
\Pi
-symmetric subspace

.
Thus, given an embedding of

into a
\Pi
-projective space, we get an

-torsor over

which has projective total space. We show that

-torsor implies
\Pi
-projectivity (see Theorem

This gives criteria for
\Pi
-projectivity.
For example, we show that the quotient of
{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1|m}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1|n}

We also consider the natural class of CY supervarieties of dimension

One question that naturally arises in this context is the existence of nice quotients by actions of

Even for

2.1.
Free actions of

We denote by
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
the odd affine line with the standard group structure. This super group scheme is also often denoted as
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{-}
.

Let
S
be a base superscheme. We work in the category of superschemes over
S
.
By definition, an action of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}_{S}
on
X
is given by a morphism of superschemes

\sigma:{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}_{S}\times_{S}X\to X

satisfying the usual axioms. Since such a morphism is identity on the underlying topological spaces,
we see that the notion of an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-action is local. It is well known that an
A^{0|1}
-action is determined
by the corresponding homological vector field, which is an odd derivation
v
on
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, trivial on functions pulled back from
S
, such that
v^{2}=0
.
Namely, for
X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
, the action map
\sigma
is given by
\sigma^{*}(a)=a+\theta\cdot v(a)
, where
\theta
is the
coordinate on
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
. Equivalently, we can think of the vector field
v
as an automorphism of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}\times X
. Then
the corresponding action is the composition of this automorphism with the projection to
X
.

2.2.
v
-connections

Let
v
be a homological vector field on a superscheme
X
, and let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a quasicoherent sheaf on
X
.
A
v
-connection
on
{\mathcal{F}}
is a map of sheaves of abelian groups
\nabla:{\mathcal{F}}\to\Pi{\mathcal{F}}

satisfying

\nabla(fs)=v(f)s+(-1)^{|f|}f\nabla(s),

for a function
f
.

The curvature
c(\nabla)
of a
v
-connection is the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear operator
\nabla^{2}:{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{F}}
.
A
v
-connection is called
flat
if
c(\nabla)=0
.

Note that
c(\nabla)
commutes with
\nabla
. In particular, if
\nabla_{L}
is a
v
-connection on a line bundle
L

then
c(\nabla_{L})
can be viewed as an even function which satisfies

v(c(\nabla_{L}))=0.

If
\nabla
is a
v
-connection on
{\mathcal{F}}
then any other
v
-connection is given by

\nabla^{\prime}(s)=\nabla(s)+\phi(s),

where
\phi
is an
{\mathcal{O}}
-linear map
{\mathcal{F}}\to\Pi{\mathcal{F}}
.
In particular, the set of
v
-connections on a line bundle
L
(if non-empty) is a principal homogeneous space for
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{-}
.

It is easy to check that for a
v
-connection
\nabla
on a line bundle
L
and an odd global function
\phi

one has

c(\nabla+\phi)=c(\nabla)+v(\phi).

The tensor product of
v
-connections is defined in the usual way (taking into account that
\nabla
is odd in the sign convention).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
v
be a homological vector field on
X
.

(i) If the map
H^{1}(X,\ker(v))^{-}\to H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{-}
is zero (e.g., if
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{-}=0
) then every line bundle on
X
admits a
v
-connection.

(ii) Assume that
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{-}=0
.
Let

be a line bundle on
X
admitting a
v
-connection
\nabla
.
Then
L
has a unique
v
-connection.
In particular, if
c(\nabla)\neq 0
then

does not admit a flat

-connection.

Proof.

(i) A standard calculation shows that the obstruction to the existence of a
v
-connection on a line bundle

is the image of
[L]\in H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{*,+})
under the map

H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{*,+})\to H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{-},

induced by the morphism of sheaves

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{*,+}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{-}:f\mapsto v(f)f^{-1}.

It remains to observe that this morphism factors through
\ker(v)^{-}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{-}
since
v(v(f)f^{-1})=0
.
Thus, if the latter embedding induces the zero map on

,
the obstruction vanishes.

(ii) Any other

-connection has form
\nabla+\phi
where
\phi
is a global odd function on
X
, so
\phi=0
by the assumption.
∎

. The
\Pi
-symmetric

-bundle on

corresponding to this

-action on
{\mathcal{O}}(-1)
by Proposition

is nothing else but the universal
\Pi
-symmetric
1|1
-bundle on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{\Pi}
.

For arbitrary

, the embedding of
GQ(1)
into

gives a weight-

-action of

on

.
The corresponding action of

a
\Pi
-symmetric

. However, neither this

Remark 2.11
.

The exact sequence (
2.1
) leads to a long exact sequence

\ldots\to H^{1}(X,GQ(1))\to H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1})\rTo{\delta}H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\to\ldots

The connecting map

[

): it is given by the composition

(actually one can make sense of the composition in any characteristic).
For example, for

over an algebraically closed field

,
we have identifications

,

.

2.4.
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibrations

It is easy to see that all automorphisms of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
are affine transformations
\theta\mapsto a\theta+\psi
, where
a
is an even invertible parameter and
\psi
is an odd parameter.

Proposition 2.12
.

There is an equivalence between the groupoid
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibrations over
X
and that of the following data: an even line bundle
L
on
X

together with a
\Pi L
-torsor over
X
. The latter groupoid can be also thought of as that of
1|1
-vector bundles
V
on
X
equipped with an
embedding
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to V
such that
V/{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
is a line bundle of rank
0|1
.

Proof.

The bijection of the corresponding isomorphism classes follows from the general yoga of noncommutative cohomology: we have an exact sequence of group schemes

0\to{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}\to\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{0|1})\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to 1,

admitting a (non-normal) splitting
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{0|1})
.
This implies that the corresponding map
H^{1}(X,\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}))\to H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is surjective and the fiber over

[c]\in H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is identified with
H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}_{c})
, where
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}_{c}
is the twist of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}_{X}
by the
1
-cocycle
c
.
Thus, if
[c]
corresponds to a line bundle
L
then
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}_{c}=\Pi L
.

The functor between the corresponding groupoids is defined as follows.
Given an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
we define the line bundle
L
on
X
by

\Pi L^{-1}:=\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}/{\mathcal{O}}_{X}.

Furthermore, the set of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-linear splittings of the exact sequence

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}\to\Pi L^{-1}\to 0

is a
\Pi L
-torsor over
X
.

Conversely, given an extension of supervector bundles

0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to V\to M\to 0\to 0

where
M
has rank
0|1
, there is a unique structure of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-algebra on
V
defined uniquely by the conditions that the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module structure on
M

is induced by the mutliplication in
V
and that for any local odd section
s
of
V
one has

s^{2}=0
. Indeed, locally we can choose an odd section
\theta
of
V
projecting to a generator of
M
. Then the natural map
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}[\theta]\to V
is an isomorphism of
algebras. These structures glue into a global structure of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-algebra on
V
.
Hence, we have
V=\pi_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}
for a canonically defined
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration
\widetilde{X}\to X
.
∎

3.
Quotients by free
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
-actions

3.1.
Existence of quotients by a free action

Everywhere in this subsection we fix a base superscheme
S
and work with superschemes over
S
.
We use the obvious superanalogs of the basic notions about group actions. Recall that an action of a subgroup scheme
G
on a subsperscheme
X
(over
S
)
is called
{\it free}
if the morphism
\alpha_{G,X}:G\times_{S}X\to X\times_{S}X:(g,x)\mapsto(gx,x)
is a closed embedding.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}_{S}
act freely on a superscheme
X
over
S
. Then there exists a categorical quotient
\pi:X\to Y=X/{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
for this action.
Furthermore,
\pi
is an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
-torsor with respect to Zariski topology. If
X
is of finite type (resp., smooth) over
S
then so is
Y
.

The proof is based on the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v
be an odd derivation of a superring
A
such that
v^{2}=0
and there exists an odd element
\theta
such that
v(\theta)=1
. Set
A^{v}=\ker(v:A\to A)
.
Then the natural ring homomorphism

A^{v}[\theta]\to A

is an isomorphism (where on the left we view
\theta
as an independent variable).
If
A
is finitely generated as an algebra over a sub-superring
C\subset A^{v}
then so is
A^{v}
.

Proof.

We have to prove that every element of
A
can be written uniquely in the form
a+b\theta
with
a,b\in A^{v}
.
The fact that
a
and
b
can be recovered from
a+b\theta
follows immediately from the identity

v(a+b\theta)=\pm b.

On the other hand, we have

a=v(a\theta)\pm v(a)\theta,

where
v(a\theta)
and
v(a)
are in
A^{v}
.

For the last assertion, suppose
x_{i}
is a finite set of generators of
A
over
C
. Write each
x_{i}
in the form
a_{i}+b_{i}\theta
with
a_{i},b_{i}\in A^{v}
.
Then it is easy to see that
(a_{i})
and
(b_{i})
generate
A^{v}
over
C
.
∎

We also need the following general statement.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a supergroup scheme acting freely on a superscheme
X
, and let
H\subset G
be a normal subgroup, flat over
S
, such that the quotient
G/H
exists as a supergroup scheme and the sequence

1\to H(T)\to G(T)\to(G/H)(T)\to 1

is exact for any
S
-superscheme
T
.
Assume that the categorical quotient
X^{\prime}=X/H
exists as a superscheme and the map
X\to X^{\prime}
is an
H
-torsor (in fppf topology).
Then

(i)
G^{\prime}=G/H
acts freely on
X^{\prime}
.

(ii) If the categorical quotient
X^{\prime}/G^{\prime}
exists then the map
X\to X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}/G^{\prime}
gives a categorical quotient
X/G
.

(iii) Under the assumption of (ii), if in addition the map
X\to X^{\prime}
is a Zariski
H
-torsor and the map
X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}/G^{\prime}
is a Zariski
G^{\prime}
-torsor,
then the map
X\to X^{\prime}/G^{\prime}=X/G
is a Zariski
G
-torsor.

Proof.

(i) It is enough to prove that the diagram

{diagram}

is cartesian, where the vertical maps are the natural projections.
It is clear that the diagram

{diagram}

is cartesian, where
\alpha^{\prime}:G^{\prime}\times_{S}X\to X^{\prime}
is induced by
(g,x)\mapsto gx
. Hence, it is enough to show that the diagram

\begin{diagram}

(3.1)

is cartesian. It is enough to show this after the faithfully flat base change
G\times_{S}X\to G^{\prime}\times_{S}X
. Since the natural diagram

{diagram}

is cartesian, it remains to check that the diagram

{diagram}

is cartesian, where the bottom arrow is the composition of
(g,x)\mapsto gx
with the projection
X\to X^{\prime}
.
But we can present it as the composition of two cartesian squares

{diagram}

where the right square is cartesian due to the fact that
X\to X^{\prime}
is an
H
-torsor.

(ii) This is a straightforward consequence of the definitions.

(iii) Combining Zariski local sections of the projections
X\to X^{\prime}
and
X^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}/G^{\prime}
, we obtain that
X\to X^{\prime}/G^{\prime}
admits Zariski local sections.
Hence, it remains to show that the diagram

is cartesian. We have

X\times_{X^{\prime}/G^{\prime}}X\simeq(X\times_{X^{\prime}/G^{\prime}}X^{\prime})\times_{X^{\prime}}X.

Since

is a

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We use induction on
n
.

Case
n=1
.
Let
v
be the homological vector field on
X
corresponding to this action. Let
{\mathcal{O}}^{v}_{X}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
denote the subsheaf of
v
-horizontal functions.
By Proposition
2.1
, locally there exists an odd function
\theta
on
X
such that
v(\theta)=1
. Suppose

\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is an affine open in
X
with such a function
\theta
. We claim that for any even
f\in A
, the ring
(A[f^{-1}])^{v}
is naturally a localization of
A^{v}
.
Indeed, this is clear if
v(f)=0
. Now we observe that the localization by
f
does not change if we replace
f
by

\widetilde{f}:=v(f\theta)=v(f)\theta+f,

where
v(\widetilde{f})=0
. This easily implies that
Y:=(|X|,{\mathcal{O}}^{v}_{X})
is a superscheme. The fact that
X\to Y
is a categorical quotient immediately reduces to the affine case,
and in the affine case it is clear that the categorical quotient boils down to passing from a ring
A
to the subring
A^{v}
.

By Lemma
3.2
, we also see that the map
X\to Y
is an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor.

Case
n>1
.
Consider a subgroup
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n-1}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
. By the induction assumption, there exists a quotient
X^{\prime}=X/{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n-1}
. Furthermore, by Lemma
3.3
,

{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}={\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}/{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n-1}
acts freely on
X^{\prime}
, and the quotient
X^{\prime}/{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
, which we know exists, is also the quotient
X/{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
. The fact that
X\to X/{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
is an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|n}
-torsor
also follows from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

It is easy to see that the condition that
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-action is free is necessary in Theorem
3.1
.
For example, for the homological vector field
v=\theta\partial_{z}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1|1}_{k}
with coordinatees
(z,\theta)
, the subring of
v
-invariants is
k+\theta\cdot k[z]\subset k[z,\theta]
, which is not
finitely generated over
k
.

3.2.
The action on supergrassmannians

Recall that in Example
2.10
we defined an action of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
on supergrassmannians
G(a|b,n|n)

associated with a
\Pi
-symmetry on a supervector space
V
of superdimension
n|n
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The above action of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
on
G(a|b,n|n)
is free if and only if
a\neq b
.

Proof.

In the case
a=b
we have a nonempty subscheme consisting of
W
such that
p(W)=W
. The action of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
on this subscheme is trivial.

To prove freeness in the case
a\neq b
we have to prove that the corresponding homological vector field is non-vanishing everywhere.
Consider the covering of
G(a|b,n|n)=G(a|b,V)
by open affine cells associated with decompositions
V=\Lambda_{1}\oplus\Lambda_{2}
into coordinate subspaces,
where
\Lambda_{1}
has dimension
a|b
, so that subspaces in the corresponding cell are the graphs of maps
\Lambda_{1}\to\Lambda_{2}
.

Let
p_{ij}:\Lambda_{i}\to\Pi\Lambda_{j}
be the components of the
\Pi
-symmetry
p:V\to\Pi V
. The action of
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
is given by
W\mapsto(\operatorname{id}+\psi p)(W)
, so
if
W=(\operatorname{id}+f)(\Lambda_{1})
for some
f:\Lambda_{1}\to\Lambda_{2}\otimes R
(where
R
is a test superring), then it maps to

(\operatorname{id}_{V}+\psi p)(\operatorname{id}_{\Lambda_{1}}+f)(\Lambda_{1})=(\operatorname{id}_{\Lambda_{1}}+f^{\prime})(\Lambda_{1})\ \text{ with}

f^{\prime}=[f+\psi(p_{21}+p_{22}f)]\cdot[1+\psi(p_{11}+p_{12}f)]^{-1}=f+\psi[p_{21}+p_{22}f-fp_{11}-fp_{12}f]=0.

It remains to prove that there does not exist
f
such that

p_{21}+p_{22}f-fp_{11}-fp_{12}f=0.

Indeed, composing with
p_{12}
we get

-\operatorname{id}_{\Lambda_{1}}-p_{12}fp_{11}-p_{12}fp_{12}f=0,

(p_{12}f)(p_{11}+p_{12}f)=-\operatorname{id}_{\Lambda_{1}}.

But
p_{12}f
, viewed as a map from
\Pi\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda_{1}
, cannot be surjective since
a\neq b
, so we get a contradiction.
∎

4.
\Pi
-projectivity

4.1.
Criterion for
\Pi
-projectivity

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
n
, there exists a closed embedding
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1|n}\hookrightarrow\Pi^{2n-1}_{\Pi}
. Hence, every projective superscheme is
\Pi
-projective.

Proof.

Given a supervector space
V
, we can equip the space
V\oplus\Pi V
with a natural
\Pi
-symmetry.
Then to every family of
1|0
-dimensional subspaces
L\subset V
we associate naturally a family of
\Pi
-symmetric
1|1
-subspaces
L\oplus\Pi L\subset V\oplus\Pi V
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

(i) If
X
is
\Pi
-projective then there exists an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor over
X
whose total space is projective.

(ii) Assume that the base is
\operatorname{Spec}(k)
where
k
is an algebraically closed field.
Assume that
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}})^{+}=k
and there exists an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor over
X
whose total space
\widetilde{X}
is projective and such that the morphism

H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{-}\to H^{1}(\widetilde{X},{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}})^{-}
is zero. Then
X
is
\Pi
-projective.

Proof.

(i) If
X
is embedded into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{\Pi}
then we can consider the pullback to
X
of the standard
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1|n}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{\Pi}
. Its total space embeds into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1|n}
.

(ii) Let
L
be a very ample line bundle on
\widetilde{X}
and let
v
be the homological vector field on
\widetilde{X}
corresponding to the
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-action.
Since
\ker(v)={\mathcal{O}}_{X}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}
, Lemma
2.4
(i) implies that
L
admits a
v
-connection
\nabla
.
Next, we look at the curvature
c(\nabla)
.

Case 1
:
c(\nabla)\neq 0
. Then we can rescale
v
, so that
c(\nabla)=1
. Let
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
be the basis of global sections of
L
. Then
using
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n},\nabla(s_{1}),\ldots,\nabla(s_{n})
get a
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-equivariant embedding of
\widetilde{X}
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1|n}
. Passing to
quotients get an embedding of
X
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{\Pi}
.

Case 2
:
c(\nabla)=0
. Then
L
descends to
X
. Since its restriction to
X_{red}
is very ample, it follows that
X
is projective (see e.g.,
[
4
, Prop. A.2]
), hence it is
\Pi
-projective.
∎

Example 4.3
.

Consider
{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1|m}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1|n}
with diagonal
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-action. Then by Theorem
4.2
(ii), the quotient is
\Pi
-projective. On the other hand, this quotient is not projective
for
m\geq 3
,
n\geq 3
, as we can find an embedding
{\mathbb{P}}^{2|3}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{m-1|m}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1|n}
compatible with
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-actions, which implies that the quotient contains
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{\Pi}
as a sub-superscheme.

Example 4.4
.

Consider
G(a|0,n|n)
with the
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-action defined in Example
2.10
. Note that
G(a|0,n|n)
is projective since the restriction of
\operatorname{Ber}({\mathcal{U}})
to the reduced space

G(a,n)
is ample (see
[
4
, Prop. A.2]
). It is easy to check
H^{1}(G(a|0,n|n),{\mathcal{O}})=0
for
n\geq a+2
. Namely, one checks this by induction on
a

using the fibration
F(a-1|0,a|0,n|n)\to G(a|0,n|n)
. Hence, by Theorem
4.2
(ii), the quotient
G(a|0,n|n)/{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
is
\Pi
-projective.

4.2.
Criterion for non-
\Pi
-projectivity

The following criterion for non-
\Pi
-projectivity is similar to the one used in
[
3
, Sec. 7]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

If
X
is non-projective and
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}})^{-}=0
then
X
is not
\Pi
-projective.

Proof.

Suppose
X
is
\Pi
-projective. Then there exists an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor
\widetilde{X}\to X
such that
\widetilde{X}
is projective. But if
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}})^{-}=0
then
there exists a section
X\to\widetilde{X}
, so

is also projective, which is a contradiction.
∎

Example 4.6
.

Using this criterion we can easily check that

is not
\Pi
-projective. Hence,
G(1|1,n|n)
is not
\Pi
-projective for

.

5.

-embeddability

5.1.
General criteria for non-

-embeddability

Proposition 5.1
.

If
X
is
1|1
-embeddable then there exists an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration over
X
whose total space is projective.

Proof.

If
X
is embedded into
G(1|1,n|n)
, then the total space of the pullback of the
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration
F(1|0,1|1,n|n)\to G(1|1,n|n)

embeds into
F(1|0,1|1,n|n)
which is projective.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume that
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=0
. If
X
is
1|1
-embeddable then there exists an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor over
X
with projective total space.

Proof.

Indeed, by Proposition
2.12
, if
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=0
then every
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration over
X
is an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
X
be a proper superscheme over
k
of positive dimension.

(i) Assume that
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=0
and
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}})^{-}=0
. Then
X
is not
1|1
-embeddable.

(ii) Assume that
H^{1}(X,L)^{-}=0
for every even line bundle
L
on
X
and
X
is not projective. Then
X
is not
1|1
-embeddable.

Proof.

(i) If
X
were
1|1
-embeddable we would deduce from Corollary
5.2

that there exists an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor
\widetilde{X}\to X
with projective
\widetilde{X}
.
But any such torsor is trivial since
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}})^{-}=0
. Hence, we would get that
X
is projective
which is impossible if
X
has trivial
\operatorname{Pic}
.

(ii) By Proposition
2.12
, any
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration
\widetilde{X}\to X

has a structure of a
\Pi L
-torsor, for some even line bundle
L
on
X
. If
H^{1}(X,L)^{-}=0
then this torsor is necessarily trivial,
hence, there exists a section
X\to\widetilde{X}
. By Proposition
5.1
, if
X
were
1|1
-embeddable then there would exist
an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration with projective
\widetilde{X}
. Since
X
is embedded into
\widetilde{X}
, it would be projective which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

It is instructive to take
X=G(1|1,2|2)
and to see which assumptions of Proposition
5.3
are not satisfied in this case.
We know that
X
is not projective and we have

H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}})^{-}=H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},({\mathcal{O}}(-1)\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}(-1))^{\oplus 2})=0.

However,
\operatorname{Pic}(X)\neq 0
: we have a nontrivial line bundle

, where
{\mathcal{S}}
is the tautological bundle of rank

. We have
L|_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}={\mathcal{O}}(-1)\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}}(1)
.
Hence,

H^{1}(X,L)^{-}=H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},({\mathcal{O}}(-2)\boxtimes{\mathcal{O}})^{2})\neq 0.

Example 5.5
.

For any smooth variety
X_{0}
over a field

, and a class

, we can define a superscheme

as follows. Let

be the square zero extension of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}
by
\bigwedge^{2}V
with the class

,
and set
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{-}=V
. The multiplication of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{+}
on
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{-}
is given by the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}
-module structure on
V
via the projection
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{+}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{0}}
, while
the multiplication

{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{-}\times{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{-}\to\bigwedge^{2}V\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{+}

is given by the wedge product.

Now let us specialize to the case when
X_{0}=S
is a smooth projective surface,

has rank
2
and
\bigwedge^{2}V\simeq\omega_{S}
.
Then

is a smooth CY supervariety of dimension
2|2
(this construction is used in
[

]
in the case

).

Assume now that in addition
\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\Z
,
H^{1}(S,V)=0
,
k
has characteristic zero, and

e=c_{1}(L)\in H^{1}(S,\Omega^{1}_{S})\simeq H^{1}(S,T_{S}\otimes\omega_{S})

where

is an ample line bundle on

.
Then Proposition
5.3
implies that
X=X(S,V,c_{1}(L))
is not
1|1
-embeddable. Indeed, by assumption we have
H^{1}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{-})=0
. The triviality of
\operatorname{Pic}(X)

is established similarly to
[

: we use the exact sequence

1\to 1+\omega_{S}\to({\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{+})^{*}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*}\to 1

and observe that the connecting homomorphism
H^{1}(S,{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*})\to H^{2}(S,\omega_{S})
sends the class of a line bundle

to
c_{1}(M)\cup c_{1}(L)
. Since
\operatorname{Pic}(S)=\Z
, this map
is injective, so no line bundle on

extends to
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{+}
.

One can try to relax the condition of vanishing of

in Proposition

. We give an example of this in the situation when we have an

-torsor

gives rise to a long exact sequence

\ldots\to H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}})^{-}\rTo{v}H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{+}\to H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})^{-}\to H^{1}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}})^{-}\to\ldots

(5.1)

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\widetilde{X}\to X
be an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor. Assume that
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=0
,

(of characteristic zero),
the space

has dimension

(e.g.,

-embeddable.

First, we see that any
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-fibration over

is an

5.2.
More examples

Recall that in Example
5.5
we considered CY supervarieties of dimension
2|2
of the form

, where

is a rank
2
vector bundle on a smooth projective
surface
S
,
such that
\bigwedge^{2}V\simeq\omega_{S}
and

is a nonzero class.
In the case

it was proved in
[

]
that every

is embeddable into some supergrassmannian. For other surfaces

the question of embeddability of

Proposition 5.7
.

(i) Assume
V=\Omega^{1}_{S}
. Then for any ample line bundle
L
on
S
, the supervariety
X(S,V,c_{1}(L))
is
\Pi
-projective.

(ii) For any nontrivial
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor
\widetilde{X}\to X(S,V,e)
, the total space
\widetilde{X}
is projective. In particular, if
H^{1}(S,V)\neq 0
then

X(S,V,e)
is embeddable into some supergrassmannian.

Proof.

(i) Let us specialize the construction of the superscheme
X(X_{0},V,e)
(see Example
5.5
) to the case
V=\Omega^{1}_{X_{0}}
and

e\in H^{1}(X_{0},T_{X_{0}}\otimes\Omega^{2}_{X_{0}})
comes from a class
c\in H^{1}(X_{0},\Omega^{1}_{X_{0}})
via the natural map

\Omega^{1}_{X_{0}}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\Omega^{1}_{X_{0}},\Omega^{2}_{X_{0}})\simeq T_{X_{0}}\otimes\Omega^{2}_{X_{0}}

induced by the wedge product. Let us call the resulting superscheme
X(X_{0},c)
.
It is easy to see that if
f:Y_{0}\to X_{0}
is a morphism, and the class
c^{\prime}\in H^{1}(Y_{0},\Omega^{1}_{Y_{0}})
is obtained from
c\in H^{1}(X_{0},\Omega^{1}_{X_{0}})

via the natural restriction map

then

extends to a morphism

\widetilde{f}:X(Y_{0},c^{\prime})\to X(X_{0},c)
. Furthermore, if

is a closed embedding then so is
\widetilde{f}
.

It is well known that for
X_{0}={\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and
c=c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}(1))
, the superscheme
X({\mathbb{P}}^{n},c_{1}({\mathcal{O}}(1)))
is the truncation of the
\Pi
-projective space

,
i.e., obtained by replacing the structure sheaf with

, where

is the ideal generated by odd functions (see e.g.,
[
11
]
).
In particular,

is
\Pi
-projective.

We are interested in the superscheme
X(S,c_{1}(L))
for an ample line bundle
L
on
S
. Replacing
L
by a high power leads to rescaling

,
hence, does not change this superscheme up to an isomorphism. Hence, we can assume that

embeds into

, hence it is
\Pi
-projective.

(ii) Let us fix a nonzero class
\alpha\in H^{1}(S,V)
, and let
\widetilde{X}\to X=X(S,V,e)
be the corresponding
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}
-torsor. Then
{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}^{+}
is
an extension of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
by a square zero ideal
{\mathcal{I}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}^{+}
. Furthermore, since
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{+}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}^{+}
, we have an exact sequence
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}
-modules

and the class

defining the extension

is the image of

under the natural map
H^{1}(S,T_{S}\otimes\omega_{S})\to H^{1}(S,T_{S}\otimes{\mathcal{I}})
.
Furthermore, it is easy to see that the class
\alpha^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(V,\omega_{S})
of the extension (
5.2
) is the image of
\alpha
under the isomorphism

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(V,\omega_{S})\simeq H^{1}(S,V^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{S})\simeq H^{1}(S,V)
.

By
[
4
, Prop. A.2]
, to prove that
\widetilde{X}
is projective, it is enough to show that some (even) ample line bundle on

extends to a line bundle on
\widetilde{X}
. The standard argument using
the exact sequence

0\to 1+{\mathcal{I}}\to({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}^{+})^{*}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{S}^{*}\to 1

shows that the obstacle to extending a line bundle

on
S
to

is given by the product
c_{1}(M)\cup\widetilde{e}\in H^{2}(S,{\mathcal{I}})
. This means that this obstacle is the image of
the product
c_{1}(M)\cup e\in H^{2}(S,\omega_{S})
under the natural map

. We claim that the latter map is zero.
Indeed, the exact sequence of cohomology associated with (

) shows that it is enough to prove surjectivity of the connecting homomorphism

which is given by the cup product with

and the fact that the Serre duality pairing

H^{1}(S,V)\otimes\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(V,\omega_{S})\to H^{2}(S,\omega_{S})

is nondegenerate.

Hence, if

then there exists an
{\mathbb{A}}^{0|1}

\widetilde{X}\to X
such that
\widetilde{X}
is projective, and by
[
2
, Prop. 4.28]
,

is embeddable into some supergrassmannian.
∎

Composite Adaptive Lyapunov-Based Deep Neural Network (Lb-DNN) Controller
Omkar Sudhir Patil, Emily J. Griffis, Wanjiku A. Makumi, Warren E. Dixon

Abstract
Recent advancements in adaptive control have equipped deep neural network (DNN)-based controllers with Lyapunov-based adaptation laws that work across a range of DNN architectures to uniquely enable online learning. However, the adaptation laws are based on tracking error, and offer convergence guarantees on only the tracking error without providing conclusions on the parameter estimation performance. Motivated to provide guarantees on the DNN parameter estimation performance, this paper provides the first result on composite adaptation for adaptive Lyapunov-based DNN controllers, which uses the Jacobian of the DNN and a prediction error of the dynamics that is computed using a novel method involving an observer of the dynamics. A Lyapunov-based stability analysis is performed which guarantees the tracking, observer, and parameter estimation errors are uniformly ultimately bounded (UUB), with stronger performance guarantees when the DNN’s Jacobian satisfies the persistence of excitation (PE) condition. Comparative simulation results demonstrate a significant performance improvement with the developed composite adaptive Lb-DNN controller in comparison to the tracking error-based Lb-DNN.



I
Introduction

Deep neural network (DNN)-based controllers have empirically demonstrated
significant performance improvements over shallow neural network (NN)
methods for uncertain nonlinear dynamic systems. Traditional DNN-based
control techniques involve initial offline system identification based
on datasets gathered from experimental trials
[
1
, Sec. 6.6]
.
Subsequently, the identified DNN is implemented as a feedforward term
aimed at compensating for the system uncertainties. However, the weight
estimates of the DNN are fixed and not updated during task execution,
raising questions about the model’s reliability and adaptability.

Recent advancements in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]

offer real-time weight adaptation to continually update the DNN-based
controllers for improved tracking error convergence. Specifically,
in
[
5
]
, Lyapunov-based adaptation laws are developed
for all layers of a fully-connected DNN (i.e., so-called Lb-DNN methods).
More recent Lb-DNN results develop Lyapunov-based adaptation laws
for more complex architectures, specifically, deep residual networks
(ResNets)
[
9
]
and long short-term memory (LSTM)
networks
[
10
]
. However, the adaptation laws
in these results are primarily based on tracking error feedback, and
thus lack guarantees on the parameter estimation performance. Since
the time-derivative of the adaptive estimate converges upon convergence
of the tracking error, it is difficult to draw conclusions regarding
the accuracy of the parameter estimate. To address this problem, incorporating
a prediction error, i.e., a measure of the discrepancy between the
actual dynamics and their DNN-based estimate, into the adaptation
law can help with parameter estimation. It is desirable to estimate
the DNN parameters to achieve system identification in addition to
trajectory tracking, where the identified model can be used to perform
new tasks. However, the prediction error is difficult or often impossible
to obtain since the dynamics are unknown and the state-derivative
is typically either unavailable or noisy.

The classical result in
[
11
]
develops adaptive controllers
with a composite adaptation law that includes both tracking and prediction
errors for nonlinear systems with linear-in-parameters (LIP) uncertainties.
The result in
[
11
]
constructs a form of the prediction
error using the swapping technique (also known as input or torque
filtering), where a low-pass filter is applied on both sides of the
dynamics to eliminate the unknown state-derivative term. However,
the development in
[
11
]
is restricted to uncertainties
that have a LIP model without any modeling error or disturbances,
and is therefore not applicable for nonlinear-in-parameters (NIP)
approximate models such as DNNs. The challenge in constructing a prediction
error for NIP models stems from the fact that the model parameters
are embedded within a nonlinear function. Thus, when a low-pass filter
is applied to the dynamics, the resultant expression is not separable
in terms of the model parameters. Moreover, even if the model parameters
could be separated from the filtered dynamics, the presence of modeling
errors or disturbances would destroy the prediction error formulation.

More recent results in
[
12
]
introduce a robust integral
of the sign of the error (RISE)-based swapping technique to formulate
the prediction error and design composite adaptive controllers for
LIP uncertainties with additive disturbances. The RISE-based swapping
technique is extended in
[
13
]
for NN-based models, but
the development is restricted to single-hidden-layer NNs. Extending
this for DNNs is mathematically challenging due to their nested NIP
structure. Moreover, using RISE-based swapping requires additional
RISE-based terms in the control input, which can debilitate the learning
performance of the adaptive feedforward term. Notably, the results
in
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
only ensure asymptotic
tracking error convergence, and no guarantees are provided on the
parameter estimates under the persistence of excitation (PE) condition.

This paper provides the first result on composite adaptation for Lb-DNN
adaptive controllers. To construct the composite adaptation law, a
new prediction error formulation is provided using a dynamics observer-based
approach, where the observer of the dynamics provides a secondary
estimate of the system dynamics. The secondary estimate of the dynamics
is used to construct a least squares composite adaptation law. To
address the challenges posed by the nested and NIP structure of the
DNNs, the Jacobian of the DNN is used in a composite adaptation law
based on the development in
[
9
]
. Then, a first-order
Taylor series expansion of the DNN is used in the analysis to express
the prediction error in terms of the parameter estimation error. Subsequently,
a Lyapunov-based stability analysis is performed which guarantees
the tracking, dynamics observer, and parameter estimation errors are
uniformly ultimately bounded (UUB). Moreover, the stability analysis
yields stronger performance guarantees when the Jacobian of the DNN
satisfies the PE condition. Comparative simulation results demonstrate
a significant performance improvement with the developed composite
adaptive Lb-DNN controller in comparison to the tracking error-based
Lb-DNN in
[
5
]
.

III-B

Composite Adaptation Laws

Recent results in
[
5
,
6
]
, and
[
9
]

develop DNN-based adaptive controllers where the adaptation laws are
based on tracking error feedback. Although tracking error-based adaptation
assists in tracking error regulation, it does not provide guarantees
on parameter estimation performance. The lack of parameter estimation
guarantees is because the rate of change of the adaptive estimate
converges upon convergence of the tracking error. Thus, it is desirable
to incorporate a prediction error of the unknown dynamics into the
adaptation law that would assist in parameter estimation error convergence.
The classical result in
[
11
]
develops composite adaptation
laws that use tracking and prediction errors for robot manipulators
that involve linearly parameterized uncertainties in the absence of
exogenous disturbances. However, for NIP uncertainties such as DNNs,
it is difficult to obtain the prediction error for constructing the
adaptation laws. Therefore, we develop a new prediction error formulation
based on an observer of the dynamics that provides a secondary estimate
of the dynamics. The dynamics observer is given by

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(x,\dot{x})u-\ddot{x}_{d}+\alpha_{1}\left(r-\alpha_{1}e\right)+\hat{f}+\alpha_{2}\tilde{r},

(13)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{f}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{f}\left(\dot{\tilde{r}}+\alpha_{2}\tilde{r}\right)+\tilde{r},

(14)

where
\hat{r},\hat{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the observer estimates of

and
f

\tilde{r},\tilde{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote

\tilde{r}\triangleq r-\hat{r}

\alpha_{2},k_{f}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}

Taking the time-derivative of
\tilde{r}

\displaystyle\tilde{f}-\alpha_{2}\tilde{r},

The DNN described in (

To facilitate the subsequent analysis, the prediction error

VI
Conclusion and Future Work

This paper provides the first composite adaptation result for DNN-based
adaptive controllers. The composite adaptation law uses the Jacobian
of the DNN, the tracking error, and a prediction error based on a
novel formulation using a dynamics observer-based approach. A Lyapunov-based
stability analysis guarantees the tracking, dynamics observer, and
parameter estimation errors are UUB. Moreover, stronger performance
guarantees are obtained when the Jacobian of the DNN satisfies the
PE condition. Comparative simulation results demonstrate a significant
improvement in tracking, function approximation and generalization
capabilities with the developed composite adaptive Lb-DNN controller
in comparison to the tracking error-based Lb-DNN in
[
5
]
.
Future work can involve relaxing the PE condition requirement for
guarantees parameter estimation. Additionally, composite adaptive
Lb-DNNs can be developed to also approximate the control effectiveness
matrix
g(x)
, potentially using insights from
[
7
]
.

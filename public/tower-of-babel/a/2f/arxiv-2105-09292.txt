Conformal ( σ , τ ) (\sigma,\tau) -Derivations on Lie conformal superalgebras
Tianqi Feng, Jun Zhao, Liangyun Chen

Abstract
In this paper, we focus on the ( σ , τ ) (\sigma,\tau) -derivation theory of Lie conformal superalgebras. Firstly, we study the fundamental properties of conformal ( σ , τ ) (\sigma,\tau) -derivations. Secondly, we mainly research the interiors of conformal G G -derivations. Finally, we discuss the relationships between the conformal ( σ , τ ) (\sigma,\tau) -derivations and some generalized conformal derivations of Lie conformal superalgebras.



Key words
: Lie conformal superalgebra, conformal
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivation, conformal

(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivation.

Introduction

Lie conformal superalgebras, introduced by Kac in
[
5
,
7
]
, encodes the singular part of the operator product expansion of chiral fields in two-dimensional quantum field theory. Furthermore, the category of Lie conformal superalgebras
\mathcal{R}
is equivalent to the category of formal distribution Lie superalgebras
(Lie\mathcal{R},\mathcal{R})
, which is essentially an infinite-dimensional Lie superalgebras. Namely, they are closely connected to the notion of a formal distribution Lie superalgebra
(g,\mathcal{F})
, which means that a Lie superalgebra
g
spanned by the coefficients of a family
\mathcal{F}
of mutually local formal distributions. See
[
3
]
in details.

The derivation theory of Lie conformal (super)algebras was introduced in
[
2
,
3
]
. Lately, the generalized derivation theory of Lie conformal (super)algebras was developed in
[
4
,
9
,
10
]
.
[
8
,
11
]
studied the
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations of Lie (super)algebras.
[
1
]
studied a kind of new generalized derivations of Lie algebras, that is the
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivation theory of Lie algebras. In the present paper, we aim to do same in
[
1
,
4
]
for Lie conformal superalgebras, extend the
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivations of Lie algebras and conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations of Lie conformal algebras to the Lie conformal superalgebras.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we recall several basic definitions of Lie conformal superalgebras and introduce the concept of conformal
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivations of Lie conformal superalgebras. In Section
2
, we obtain some fundamental properties of conformal
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivations. In Section
3

G

4

(\sigma,\tau)
-derivations and some generalized conformal derivations. What’s more, we introduce the concept of conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations of Lie conformal superalgebras and obtain some connection between the generalized conformal derivations and the conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations.

Preliminaries

Let
V
be a superspace that is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded linear space with a direct sum

V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
. The elements of
V_{j}
,
j=\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
, are said to be homogenous and of parity
j
.
The parity of
a homogeneous element
x
is denoted by
|x|
. Throughout what follows, if
|x|
occurs
in an expression, then it is assumed that
x
is homogeneous and that the expression
extends to the other elements by linearity.

The following notion was due to
[
5
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

[
5
]

A
Lie conformal superalgebra

\mathcal{R}
is a left
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module, and for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
there is a
family of
\mathbb{C}
-linear
n
-products from
\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R}
to
\mathcal{R}
satisfying the following conditions

(\rm C0)

For any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
,
a_{(n)}b=0
for
n\gg 0
,

(\rm C1)

For any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
(\partial a)_{(n)}b=-na_{(n-1)}b
,

(\rm C2)

For any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

a_{(n)}b=-(-1)^{|a||b|}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j+n}\frac{1}{j!}\partial^{j}(b_{(n+j)}a),

(\rm C3)

For any
a,b,c\in\mathcal{R}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

a_{(m)}(b_{(n)}c)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(_{j}^{m})(a_{(j)}b)_{(m+n-j)}c+(-1)^{|a||b|}b_{(n)}(a_{(m)}c).

(Convention:
a_{(n)}b=0
if
n<0
). Note that if we define
\lambda
-bracket
[-_{\lambda}-]
:

\displaystyle[a_{\lambda}b]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}a_{(n)}b,\,\forall a,b\in\mathcal{R}.

That is,
\mathcal{R}
is a Lie conformal superalgebra if and only if
[-_{\lambda}-]
satisfies the following axioms

\displaystyle(\rm C1)_{\lambda}\ \ \ \ {\rm Conformal\ sesquilinearity}:\ \,[(\partial a)_{\lambda}b]=-\lambda[a_{\lambda}b];

\displaystyle(\rm C2)_{\lambda}\ \ \ \ {\rm Skew-supersymmetry}:\ \,[a_{\lambda}b]=-(-1)^{|a||b|}[b_{-\partial-\lambda}a];

\displaystyle(\rm C3)_{\lambda}\ \ \ \ {\rm Jacobi\ identity}:\ \,[a_{\lambda}[b_{\mu}c]]=[[a_{\lambda}b]_{\lambda+\mu}c]+(-1)^{|a||b|}[b_{\mu}[a_{\lambda}c]].

A Lie conformal superalgebras are called
finite
if
\mathcal{R}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module. The
rank
of a conformal algebra
\mathcal{R}
is its rank as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module (recall that this is the dimension over
\mathbb{C}(\partial)
, the field of fractions of
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
, of
\mathbb{C}(\partial)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[\partial]}\mathcal{R}
).

Throughout this paper, we assume that
\mathcal{R}
is finite.

Definition 1.2
.

[
5
]

An
associative conformal superalgebra

\mathcal{R}
is a left
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module endowed with a
\lambda
-product
from
\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R}
to
\mathbb{C}[\lambda]\otimes\mathcal{R}
, for any
a,b,c\in\mathcal{R}
,
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(\partial a)_{\lambda}b=-\lambda a_{\lambda}b,\ a_{\lambda}(\partial b)=(\partial+\lambda)(a_{\lambda}b)
,

(2)

a_{\lambda}(b_{\mu}c)=(a_{\lambda}b)_{\lambda+\mu}c
.

Definition 1.3
.

[
3
]

Let
M
and
N
be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules. A
conformal linear map
of degree
\theta
from
M
to
N
is a sequence
f=\{f_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
of
f_{(n)}\in\rm Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(M,N)
satisfying that

\partial_{N}f_{(n)}-f_{(n)}\partial_{M}=-nf_{(n-1)},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ f_{(n)}(M_{\mu})\subseteq N_{\mu+\theta},\ \mu,\theta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Set
f_{\lambda}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}f_{(n)}
. Then

f=\{f_{(n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is a conformal linear map of degree
\theta
if and only if

f_{\lambda}\partial_{M}=(\partial_{N}+\lambda)f_{\lambda}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ f_{\lambda}(M_{\mu})\subseteq N_{\mu+\theta}[\lambda],\ \mu,\theta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Let
\rm Chom(M,N)_{\theta}
denote the set of conformal linear maps of degree
\theta
from
M
to
N
. Then
\rm Chom(M,N)=\rm Chom(M,N)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\rm Chom(M,N)_{\bar{1}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module via:

\displaystyle\partial f_{(n)}=-nf_{(n-1)},\ {\rm equivalently},\ \partial f_{\lambda}=-\lambda f_{\lambda}.

The composition
f_{\lambda}g:L\rightarrow N\otimes\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
of conformal linear maps
f:M\rightarrow N
and
g:L\rightarrow M
is given by

\displaystyle(f_{\lambda}g)_{\lambda+\mu}=f_{\lambda}g_{\mu},\ \ \ \forall\,f,g\in\rm Chom(M,N).

If
M
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module, then
{\rm Cend}(M):=\rm Chom(M,M)
is an associative conformal superalgebra with respect to the above composition. Thus,
{\rm Cend}(M)
becomes a Lie conformal superalgebra, called the general linear Lie conformal superalgebra, denoted as
gc(M)
, with respect to the following
\lambda
-bracket(see
[
3
, Example1.1]
):

\displaystyle[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}=f_{\lambda}g_{\mu-\lambda}-(-1)^{|f||g|}g_{\mu-\lambda}f_{\lambda}.

(1.1)

Hereafter all
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules are supposed to be finitely generated.

Definition 1.4
.

[
3
]

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a Lie conformal superalgebra.
d\in{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
is a
conformal derivation
of degree
\theta
if for any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
it holds that

\displaystyle d_{(m)}(a_{(n)}b)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(_{j}^{m})(d_{(j)}a)_{(m+n-j)}b+(-1)^{|a||\theta|}a_{(n)}(d_{(m)}(b));

equivalently,

\displaystyle d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}b]+(-1)^{|a||\theta|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))].

For any
r\in\mathcal{R}
,
d^{r}_{\lambda}
is called an
inner
conformal derivation of
\mathcal{R}
if
d^{r}_{\lambda}(r^{\prime})=[r_{\lambda}r^{\prime}]
,
\forall r^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
.

Define
{\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})_{\it{\theta}}
is the set of conformal derivations of degree
\theta
of
\mathcal{R}
, and
{\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})=\rm{CDer}_{\bar{0}}(\mathcal{R})\oplus{\rm CDer}_{\bar{1}}(\mathcal{R})
. Then it is obvious that
{\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
is a subalgebra of
{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
.

.

Definition 1.6
.

[
6
]

Let
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{R}^{{}^{\prime}}
be two Lie conformal superalgebras. A
homomorphism

\phi
from
\mathcal{R}
to
\mathcal{R}^{{}^{\prime}}
of Lie conformal superalgebras is a homogeneous
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-linear homomorphism if for any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
it holds that

\phi(a_{(n)}b)=(\phi(a)_{(n)}\phi(b));

equivalently,

\phi[a_{\lambda}b]=[\phi(a)_{\lambda}\phi(b)].

We call
\phi
an isomorphism if it is bijective. We call
\phi
an endomorphism if
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{{}^{\prime}}
. We call
\phi
an automorphism if it is bijective and if
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{{}^{\prime}}
.

In the following, we denote
\rm{Aut}(\mathcal{R})
the automorphism group of
\mathcal{R}
.

Definition 1.7
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{R}
is a Lie conformal superalgebra and
G
is a subgroup of
\rm{Aut}(\mathcal{R})
. Then
d\in{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
is a
conformal
G
-derivation
of degree
\theta
of
\mathcal{R}
if there exist two element
\sigma,\tau
in
G
such that

d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma(b))]+(-1)^{|a||\theta|}[(\tau(a))_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))],\quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal{R}.

In this case,
\sigma
and
\tau
are called the
associated
automorphisms of
d
.

Denote by
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
the set of all conformal
G
-derivations of
\mathcal{R}
. It is clear that
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
if
G
is a trivial group, that is
G=\left\{\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\right\}
. Thus, conformal
G
-derivations can be viewed as a generalization of conformal derivations. What’s more, if
G\leq H
are two subgroups of
\rm{Aut}(\mathcal{R})
, then we have
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{H}(\mathcal{R})
and
{\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
is contained in
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
for any subgroup
G
of
\rm{Aut}(\mathcal{R})
.

Fix two automorphisms
\sigma,\tau\in G
, we denote by
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma,\tau}(\mathcal{R})
the set of all conformal
G
-derivations associated to
\sigma
and
\tau
, called the
conformal
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivation
.
It is clear that
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma,\tau}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module and
{\rm CDer}_{\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}},\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
. For convenience, we denote
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma,\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}}(\mathcal{R})
by

.

In the following,

always denotes a subgroup of
\rm{Aut}(\mathcal{R})
.

2
Fundamental properties

In this section, we aim to show several fundamental properties of conformal
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivations.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{R}
is a Lie conformal superalgebra and
\sigma,\tau
are two elements in
G
, then
\rm{rank}(CDer_{\it{\sigma,\tau}}(\mathcal{R}))=\rm{rank}(CDer_{\it{\tau^{-\rm{1}}\sigma}}(\mathcal{R}))
.

Proof.

Define a map
\varphi_{\tau}:{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\tau}}(\mathcal{R})\rightarrow{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau^{-\rm{1}}\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
by

\varphi_{\tau}(d)=\tau^{-1}d,\quad\forall d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\tau}}(\mathcal{R}).

For any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~\tau^{-1}(d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b]))=\tau^{-1}([(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma(b))]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[(\tau(a))_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))])

\displaystyle=[(\tau^{-1}(d_{\lambda}(a)))_{\lambda+\mu}(\tau^{-1}(\sigma(b)))]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(\tau^{-1}(d_{\lambda}(b)))],

which means that
\tau^{-1}d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau^{-\rm{1}}\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
, i.e., the map
\varphi_{\tau}
is well-defined.
What’s more

\varphi_{\tau}(d_{1}+d_{2})=\tau^{-1}(d_{1}+d_{2})=\tau^{-1}d_{1}+\tau^{-1}d_{2}=\varphi_{\tau}d_{1}+\varphi_{\tau}d_{2},

\varphi_{\tau}(\partial d)=\varphi_{\tau}(-\lambda d_{\lambda})=-\lambda\varphi_{\tau}(d_{\lambda})=-\lambda\tau^{-1}(d_{\lambda})=\partial\varphi_{\tau}(d_{\lambda}).

That is to say,
\varphi_{\tau}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism.

In the following, it still needs to show that
\varphi_{\tau}
is an isomorphism. So we try to see its inverse. We can define a map
\psi_{\tau}:{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau^{-1}\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})\rightarrow{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
by
\psi_{\tau}(d)=\tau d
for any
d
in
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau^{-1}\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
. Similarly, we can verify that
\psi_{\tau}
is a well defined
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism.
What’s more, we have
\psi_{\tau}\varphi_{\tau}=\rm{id}_{CDer_{\it{\sigma,\tau}}(\mathcal{R})}
and
\varphi_{\tau}\psi_{\tau}=\rm{id}_{CDer_{\it{\tau^{-1}\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})}
, which means
\psi_{\tau}
is the inverse of
\varphi_{\tau}
. Therefore,
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
and
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau^{-1}\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules, and we have
\rm{rank}(CDer_{\it{\sigma,\tau}}(\mathcal{R}))=\rm{rank}(CDer_{\it{\tau^{-\rm{1}}\sigma}}(\mathcal{R}))
.
∎

Proposition
2.1
means that the study of
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
with two parameters
\sigma,\tau
can be reduced to the study of
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}}}(\mathcal{R})
with one parameter
\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}=\tau^{-1}\sigma
. Particularly, if we take
\tau=\sigma
, then
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
and
{\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules. Moreover, we may extend this isomorphic relation to the level of Lie conformal superalgebras.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{R}
is a Lie conformal superalgebra and
\sigma
is an element in
G
. Then
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
can be viewed as a Lie conformal superalgebra and
{\rm Der}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})\cong{\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
as Lie conformal superalgebra.

Proof.

Define a
\lambda
-bracket
[-_{\lambda}-]^{\sigma}
on
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})\times{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
as follow:

[f_{\lambda}g]^{\sigma}=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))]),\quad\forall f,g\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R}),

where
\varphi_{\sigma}
is defined as Proposition
2.1
. It’s obvious that
[-_{\lambda}-]^{\sigma}
is well-defined since
\varphi_{\sigma}
is a bijective map. And it is bilinear because both
\varphi_{\sigma}
and
\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}
are isomorphic as
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules.

For any
f,g
in
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~[\partial f_{\lambda}g]^{\sigma}=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(\partial f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))])

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(-\lambda f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))])=-\lambda\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))])=-\lambda[f_{\lambda}g]^{\sigma},

and

[f_{\lambda}g]^{\sigma}=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))])=-(-1)^{|f||g|}\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))_{-\partial-\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))])=-(-1)^{|f||g|}[g_{-\partial-\lambda}f]^{\sigma}.

For any
f,g,h
in
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~[[f_{\lambda}g]^{\sigma}_{\lambda+\mu}h]^{\sigma}=[\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))])_{\lambda+\mu}h]^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([\varphi_{\sigma}(\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))]))_{\lambda+\mu}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))])

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([[(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))]_{\lambda+\mu}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))]).

Similarly, we have

[f_{\lambda}[g_{\mu}h]^{\sigma}]^{\sigma}=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}[(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))_{\mu}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))]]),

[g_{\mu}[f_{\lambda}h]^{\sigma}]^{\sigma}=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))_{\mu}[(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))]]).

So we have

\displaystyle~~~~[[f_{\lambda}g]^{\sigma}_{\lambda+\mu}h]^{\sigma}-[f_{\lambda}[g_{\mu}h]^{\sigma}]^{\sigma}+(-1)^{|f||g|}[g_{\mu}[f_{\lambda}h]^{\sigma}]^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([[(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))]_{\lambda+\mu}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))])-\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}[(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))_{\mu}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))]])

\displaystyle~~~~+(-1)^{|f||g|}\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))_{\mu}[(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))]])

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([[(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))]_{\lambda+\mu}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))]-[\varphi_{\sigma}(f)_{\lambda}[(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))_{\mu}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))]]

\displaystyle~~~~+(-1)^{|f||g|}[(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))_{\mu}[(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(h))]])

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}(0)=0,

which means that

[f_{\lambda}[g_{\mu}h]^{\sigma}]^{\sigma}=[[f_{\lambda}g]^{\sigma}_{\lambda+\mu}h]^{\sigma}+(-1)^{|f||g|}[g_{\mu}[f_{\lambda}h]^{\sigma}]^{\sigma}
.
Hence,
({\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R}),[-_{\lambda}-]^{\sigma})
is a Lie conformal superalgebra.

To complete the proof, it still needs to show that
\varphi_{\sigma}
is a Lie conformal superalgebras homomorphism. Actually,

\varphi_{\sigma}([f_{\lambda}g]^{\sigma})=\varphi_{\sigma}(\varphi_{\sigma}^{-1}([(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))]))=[(\varphi_{\sigma}(f))_{\lambda}(\varphi_{\sigma}(g))]

and
\varphi_{\sigma}
is a Lie conformal superalgebras homomorphism between
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma,\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
and
{\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
, as desired.
∎

Recall that the center of a Lie conformal superalgebra
\mathcal{R}
is the set
\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})=\{\it{a}\in\mathcal{R}\;|\;[\it{a}_{\lambda}\it{b}]=\rm{0},\,\forall\it{b}\in\mathcal{R}\}
, and the centralizer of
a
in
\mathcal{R}
is the set
\rm{Z}_{\it{a}}(\mathcal{R})=\{\it{b}\in\mathcal{R}\;|\;[\it{a}_{\lambda}\it{b}]=\rm{0}\}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
\sigma,\tau
are two elements in
G
such that
(\sigma-\tau)(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})
, then
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
. In addition, if
(\sigma-\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}})(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})
, then
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
is a Lie conformal subalgebra of
{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

For any
d
in
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
and
since
(\sigma-\tau)(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})
, we have

[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}((\sigma-\tau)(b))]=0,\quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal{R},

that is
[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma(b))]=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\tau(b))]
.
So we can get

d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma(b))]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))]=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\tau(b))]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))],

which implies that
d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
and
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
.
By switching the roles of
\sigma
and
\tau
, we have
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
. Consequently, we obtain
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
.

In particular, if we take
\tau=\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}
, then
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
is a Lie conformal subalgebra of
{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that
\sigma,\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}
are two elements in
G
such that
f\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
and
g\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}}}(\mathcal{R})
. If
\sigma
and
\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}
commute,
f
commutes with
\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}
, and
g
commutes with
\sigma
, then
[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}\it{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}}}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

For any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b]))=f_{\lambda}([(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(b))]+(-1)^{|a||g|}[a_{\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b))])

\displaystyle=[(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a)))_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(b)))]+(-1)^{(|a|+|g|)|f|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~+(-1)^{|a||g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b)))]+(-1)^{|a||g|+|a||f|}[a_{\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b)))],

and

\displaystyle~~~~g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b]))=g_{\mu-\lambda}([(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma(b))]+(-1)^{|a||f|}[a_{\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(b))])

\displaystyle=[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(a)))_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(\sigma(b)))]+(-1)^{(|a|+|f|)|g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(\sigma(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~+(-1)^{|a||f|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(f_{\lambda}(b)))]+(-1)^{|a||f|+|a||g|}[a_{\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(b)))].

Since
\sigma
and
\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}
commute,
f
commutes with
\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}
, and
g
commutes with
\sigma
, it follows that

\displaystyle~~~~[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}([a_{\gamma}b])=(f_{\lambda}g_{\mu-\lambda}-(-1)^{|f||g|}g_{\mu-\lambda}f_{\lambda})([a_{\gamma}b])=f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b]))-(-1)^{|f||g|}g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b]))

\displaystyle=[(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a)))_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(b)))]+(-1)^{(|a|+|g|)|f|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~+(-1)^{|a||g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b)))]+(-1)^{|a||g|+|a||f|}[a_{\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~-(-1)^{|f||g|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(a)))_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(\sigma(b)))]-(-1)^{|a||g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(\sigma(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~-(-1)^{|a||f|+|f||g|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(f_{\lambda}(b)))]-(-1)^{|a||f|+|a||g|+|f||g|}[a_{\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(b)))]

\displaystyle=[([f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(a))_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma(\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}(b)))]+(-1)^{|a|(|f|+|g|)}[a_{\gamma}([f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(b))],

which shows that
[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}\it{\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}}}(\mathcal{R})
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Suppose that
\sigma
is an involutive automorphism of
G
. If
\sigma
commutes with every element of
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
, then
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
is a Lie conformal superalgebra.

Proof.

Note that
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module, thus it suffices to verify that
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
is closed under the
\lambda
-bracket, that is

[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}=f_{\lambda}g_{\mu-\lambda}-(-1)^{|f||g|}g_{\mu-\lambda}f_{\lambda}\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R}),\quad\forall f,g\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R}).

With a similar discussion as that in the proof of Proposition
2.4
, we have

f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b]))=[([f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(a))_{\mu+\gamma}\sigma^{2}(b)]+(-1)^{|a|(|f|+|g|)}[a_{\gamma}([f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(b))],\quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal{R}.

Since
\sigma^{2}=\sigma
, it follows that
[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}\in{\rm CDer}_{\rm{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})[\lambda]
. Therefore,
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
is a Lie conformal superalgebra.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that
\sigma,\tau
are two elements in
G
and
d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
. Then

\tau d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}\it{\sigma},\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
and
d\tau\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}\it{\tau},\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

For any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~\tau d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=\tau([(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma(b))]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))])

\displaystyle=\tau([(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma(b))])+(-1)^{|a||d|}\tau([a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))])

\displaystyle=[(\tau(d_{\lambda}(a)))_{\lambda+\mu}(\tau(\sigma(b)))]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[(\tau(a))_{\mu}(\tau(d_{\lambda}(b)))],

which means that

\tau d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}\it{\sigma},\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
.

Similarly, we can obtain that
d\tau\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}\it{\tau},\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that
\sigma,\tau
are two elements in
G
such that
c-\sigma^{-1}\tau(c)\notin\rm{Z}_{\it{c}}(\mathcal{R})
for any nonzero element
c
in
\mathcal{R}
. Then
{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})=\{\rm{0}\}
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a nonzero element
d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{\it{\tau}}(\mathcal{R})
. Then there exists an element
a_{0}\in\mathcal{R}
such that
d_{\lambda}(a_{0})\neq 0
.
So we have

[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma(b))]=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\tau(b))],\quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal{R},

which implies that

[(\sigma^{-1}(d_{\lambda}(a)))_{\lambda+\mu}(b-\sigma^{-1}\tau(b))]=0.

If we take
a=a_{0}
and
b=b_{0}:=\sigma^{-1}d_{\lambda}(a_{0})
,
then we can get

[b_{0_{\lambda+\mu}}(b_{0}-\sigma^{-1}\tau(b_{0}))]=0,

which means that
b_{0}-\sigma^{-1}\tau(b_{0})\in\rm{Z}_{\it{b_{\rm{0}}}}(\mathcal{R})
. Since
c-\sigma^{-1}\tau(c)\notin\rm{Z}_{\it{c}}(\mathcal{R})
with
c\in\mathcal{R}
and
c\neq 0
, we have
b_{0}=0
. According to the assumption,
d_{\lambda}(a_{0})\neq 0
and
\sigma^{-1}
is an isomorphism, we have
b_{0}=\sigma^{-1}d_{\lambda}(a_{0})\neq 0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose that
d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})
is an element such that
(d_{\lambda}\sigma-\sigma d_{\lambda})(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})
. Then
[\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}\mathcal{R}]
is contained in the kernel of
d_{\lambda}\sigma-\sigma d_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

For any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

d_{\lambda}(\sigma([a_{\mu}b]))=d_{\lambda}([(\sigma(a))_{\mu}(\sigma(b))])=[(d_{\lambda}(\sigma(a)))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma^{2}(b))]+(-1)^{|a|+|d|}[(\sigma(a))_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(\sigma(b)))],

and

\displaystyle~~~~\sigma(d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b]))=\sigma([(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma(b))])+(-1)^{|a|+|d|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))]

\displaystyle=[(\sigma(d_{\lambda}(a)))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma^{2}(b))]+(-1)^{|a|+|d|}[(\sigma(a))_{\mu}(\sigma(d_{\lambda}(b)))].

Since
(d_{\lambda}\sigma-\sigma d_{\lambda})(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~(d_{\lambda}\sigma-\sigma d_{\lambda})([a_{\mu}b])=d_{\lambda}\sigma([a_{\mu}b])-\sigma d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])

\displaystyle=[d_{\lambda}(\sigma(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma^{2}(b))]+(-1)^{|a|+|d|}[(\sigma(a))_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(\sigma(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~-[(\sigma(d_{\lambda}(a)))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma^{2}(b))]-(-1)^{|a|+|d|}[(\sigma(a))_{\mu}(\sigma(d_{\lambda}(b)))]

\displaystyle=[((d_{\lambda}\sigma-\sigma d_{\lambda})(a))_{\lambda+\mu}(\sigma^{2}(b))]+(-1)^{|a|+|d|}[(\sigma(a))_{\mu}((d_{\lambda}\sigma-\sigma d_{\lambda})(b))]

\displaystyle=0.

Consequently,
[\mathcal{R}_{\lambda}\mathcal{R}]
is contained in the kernel of
d_{\lambda}\sigma-\sigma d_{\lambda}
.
∎

3
The interiors of conformal
G
-derivations

In this section, we will investigate the structures of
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
and
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
. To understand this, we focus on a special class of
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
called the
interiors
of
G
-derivations,
{\rm CDer}_{G}^{\star}(\mathcal{R})
. In addition, we study the rationality of the Hilbert series for the direct sum of these interiors of conformal
G
- derivations when
G
is a cyclic subgroup.

Set

{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}^{+}(\mathcal{R})=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;d_{\lambda}\sigma=\sigma d_{\lambda}\},\;{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}^{-}(\mathcal{R})=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;d_{\lambda}\tau=\tau d_{\lambda},\;\forall\tau\in G\}.

It is obvious that

{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}^{-}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}^{+}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R})

and they are all
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-modules. We now consider some kind of ”sum” of them respectively and observe how close from these sum to
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
.

Define

{\rm CDer}_{G}^{+}(\mathcal{R}):=\oplus_{\sigma\in G}{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}^{+}(\mathcal{R}),\;{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R}):=\oplus_{\sigma\in G}{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}),

called the
big interior
and the
small interior
of
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
respectively. Besides, we may define

{\rm CDer}_{G}^{\star}(\mathcal{R}):=\oplus_{\sigma\in G}{\rm CDer}_{\it{\sigma}}(\mathcal{R}),

called the
interior
of
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
. Obviously, we have

{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{G}^{+}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{G}^{\star}(\mathcal{R})
.

Example 3.1
.

Let
G=\{\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}\}
, the trivial group. Since
{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{G}^{+}(\mathcal{R})
, we have
{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{G}^{+}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{G}^{\star}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
G
is a cyclic group and
\sigma
is its generator. If
*\in\{-,+,\star\}
, then

{\rm CDer}_{\it{G}}^{*}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{\langle\sigma\rangle}^{*}(\mathcal{R})=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{*}(\mathcal{R})

where
\sigma^{0}=\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}
,
\sigma^{1}=\sigma
and
\sigma^{k}=\sigma^{k-1}\sigma
. For convenience, we denote
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{\star}(\mathcal{R})
by
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}(\mathcal{R})
. In this case,
{\rm CDer}_{\langle\sigma\rangle}^{*}(\mathcal{R})
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module and recall that the
Hilbert series
of
{\rm CDer}_{\langle\sigma\rangle}^{*}(\mathcal{R})
is defined as follow:

H({\rm CDer}_{\langle\sigma\rangle}^{*}(\mathcal{R}),t):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{*}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}.

If
\sigma
is of finite order, then
H({\rm CDer}_{\langle\sigma\rangle}^{*}(\mathcal{R}),t)
is a polynomial function in
\mathbb{Z}[t]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
G
is an abelian group. Then
{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R})
is a Lie conformal superalgebra with the
\lambda
-bracket
[-_{\lambda}-]
.

Proof.

Since
{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R})
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module, it is sufficient to show that
{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R})
is closed under the
\lambda
-bracket. For any
f\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}^{-}(\mathcal{R})
and
g\in{\rm CDer}_{\tau}^{-}(\mathcal{R})
with
\sigma,\tau\in G
and
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b]))=f_{\lambda}([(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(\tau(b))]+(-1)^{|a||g|}[a_{\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b))])

\displaystyle=[(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a)))_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma(\tau(b)))]+(-1)^{(|a|+|g|)|f|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(\tau(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~+(-1)^{|a||g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b)))]+(-1)^{|a||g|+|a||f|}[a_{\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b)))],

and

\displaystyle~~~~g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b]))=g_{\mu-\lambda}([(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma(b))]+(-1)^{|a||f|}[a_{\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(b))])

\displaystyle=[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(a)))_{\mu+\gamma}(\tau(\sigma(b)))]+(-1)^{(|a|+|f|)|g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(\sigma(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~+(-1)^{|a||f|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(\tau(f_{\lambda}(b)))]+(-1)^{|a||f|+|a||g|}[a_{\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(b)))].

Since
f_{\lambda}\tau=\tau f_{\lambda}
,
g_{\mu-\lambda}\sigma=\sigma g_{\mu-\lambda}
and
G
is abelian, we have

\displaystyle~~~~[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}([a_{\gamma}b])=(f_{\lambda}g_{\mu-\lambda}-(-1)^{|f||g|}g_{\mu-\lambda}f_{\lambda})([a_{\gamma}b])

\displaystyle=[(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a)))_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma(\tau(b)))]+(-1)^{(|a|+|g|)|f|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(\tau(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~+(-1)^{|a||g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b)))]+(-1)^{|a||g|+|a||f|}[a_{\gamma}(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~-(-1)^{|f||g|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(a)))_{\mu+\gamma}(\tau(\sigma(b)))]-(-1)^{|a||g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(\sigma(b)))]

\displaystyle~~~~-(-1)^{|a||f|+|f||g|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\mu-\lambda+\gamma}(\tau(f_{\lambda}(b)))]-(-1)^{|f||g|+|a||f|+|a||g|}[a_{\gamma}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(b)))].

\displaystyle=[(f_{\lambda}g_{\mu-\lambda}-(-1)^{|f||g|}g_{\mu-\lambda}f_{\lambda})(a)_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma(\tau(b)))]+[a_{\gamma}((f_{\lambda}g_{\mu-\lambda}-(-1)^{|f||g|}g_{\mu-\lambda}f_{\lambda})(b))]

\displaystyle=[([f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(a))_{\mu+\gamma}(\sigma(\tau(b)))]+[a_{\gamma}([f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(b))],

which implies that
[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma\tau}(\mathcal{R})[\lambda]
. Obviously,
[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}
commutes with every element in
G
, and so
[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma\tau}^{-}(\mathcal{R})[\lambda]\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R})[\lambda]
. Consequently,
{\rm CDer}_{G}^{-}(\mathcal{R})
is a Lie conformal superalgebra.
∎

According to the above results, we can see that
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
may be very large and complicated. In the following, we will focus on the interiors of
{\rm CDer}_{G}(\mathcal{R})
where
G
is an infinite cyclic group. Particularly, we will investigate the
important invariant, the Hilbert series, which encodes the ranks of submodules into an infinite series.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G=\langle\sigma\rangle
be an infinite cyclic group. If there exists
l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{l_{\rm{0}}}}}(\mathcal{R})
such that
\phi_{d}
is invertible restricted to
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{i}}}(\mathcal{R})
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{l_{0}\}
, then
H({\rm CDer}_{\it{G}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}),t)
is a rational function.

Proof.

Since
G
is an infinite cyclic group generated by
\sigma
, we have
\phi_{d}:{\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R})\rightarrow{\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k+l_{\rm{0}}}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R})
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module isomorphism for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{l_{0}\}
by Proposition
3.3
. Hence,
\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))=\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k+l_{\rm{0}}}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))=\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k-l_{\rm{0}}}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{l_{0}\}
. It is obvious that,

H({\rm CDer}_{\it{G}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}),t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))t^{k}=\sum_{\it{k}=\it{l}_{\rm{0}}+\rm{1}}^{\infty}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}+\sum_{-\infty}^{k=\it{l}_{\rm{0}}}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}.

In addition,

\displaystyle~~~~\sum_{k=l_{\rm{0}}+\rm{1}}^{\infty}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}

\displaystyle=(m_{0}t^{l_{0}+1}+m_{1}t^{l_{0}+2}+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{2l_{0}})+(m_{0}t^{2l_{0}+1}+m_{1}t^{2l_{0}+2}+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{3l_{0}})+\cdots

\displaystyle=(m_{0}+m_{1}t+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{l_{0}-1})t^{l_{0}+1}+(m_{0}+m_{1}t+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{l_{0}-1})t^{2l_{0}+1}+\cdots

\displaystyle=t^{l_{0}+1}(m_{0}+m_{1}t+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{l_{0}-1})(1+t^{l_{0}}+t^{2l_{0}}+\cdots)

\displaystyle=t^{l_{0}+1}(m_{0}+m_{1}t+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{l_{0}-1})\frac{1}{1-t^{l_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{l_{0}+1}\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}-1}m_{i}t^{i}}{1-t^{l_{0}}}

where
m_{i}=\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{l_{\rm{0}}+\rm{1}+i}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))
for
0\leq i\leq l_{0}-1
. Similarly,

\displaystyle~~~~\sum_{-\infty}^{k=l_{\rm{0}}}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{l_{0}}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}+\sum_{-\infty}^{k=\rm{0}}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{l_{0}}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}+\sum_{k=\rm{0}}^{\infty}\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma^{\it{-k}}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}))\it{t}^{\it{k}}

\displaystyle=m_{0}t+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{l_{0}}+(m_{0}t^{-l_{0}+1}+m_{1}t^{-l_{0}+2}+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1})

\displaystyle~~~~+(m_{0}t^{-2l_{0}+1}+m_{1}t^{-2l_{0}+2}+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{-l_{0}})+\cdots

\displaystyle=m_{0}t+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{l_{0}}+(m_{0}t^{-l_{0}+1}+m_{1}t^{-l_{0}+2}+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1})(1+t^{-l_{0}}+\cdots)

\displaystyle=(m_{0}+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{l_{0}-1})t+\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}-1}m_{i}t^{-(l_{0}-1-i)}}{1-t^{-l_{0}}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle~~~~H({\rm CDer}_{\it{G}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}),t)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{l_{0}+1}\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}-1}m_{i}t^{i}}{1-t^{l_{0}}}+(m_{0}+\cdots+m_{l_{0}-1}t^{l_{0}-1})t+\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}-1}m_{i}t^{-(l_{0}-1-i)}}{1-t^{-l_{0}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{1-t^{l_{0}}}\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}-1}m_{i}t^{i}+\frac{1}{1-t^{-{l_{0}}}}\sum_{i=0}^{l_{0}-1}m_{i}t^{-(l_{0}-1-i)}.

Consequently,
H({\rm CDer}_{\it{G}}^{-}(\mathcal{R}),t)
is a rational function.
∎

4
Applications

In this section, we study the relation between conformal
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivation and some (generalized) conformal derivations of a Lie conformal superalgebra
\mathcal{R}
, such as centroids and conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations.

4.1
Relation with centroids

Recall that
\rm{ad}:\mathcal{R}\it{\rightarrow}{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
denotes the adjoint map sending
a
to
\rm{ad}(\it{a})
with
\rm{ad}(\it{a})_{\it{\lambda}}(\it{b})=[a_{\lambda}b]
, where
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
. And we denote
\rm{ad}(\mathcal{R})=\{\rm{ad}(\it{a})|a\in\mathcal{R}\}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\sigma\in G
and
d\in{\rm C}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
. Then
\rm{ad}(\it{d_{\lambda}(a)})_{\it{\mu}}=\rm{0}
for any
a\in\mathcal{R}
. In addition, if
\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})=\{\rm{0}\}
, then
{\rm C}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})=\{\rm{0}\}
.

Proof.

For any
d\in{\rm C}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
and
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

d_{\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b])=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma(b))]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\gamma}(d_{\lambda}(b))],

and

(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\gamma}(d_{\lambda}(b))]=d_{\lambda}([a_{\lambda}b]),

which implies

[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\gamma}(\sigma(b))]=d_{\lambda}([a_{\gamma}b])-(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\gamma}(d_{\lambda}(b))]=0.

Since
\sigma
is a bijective map, we can obtain

d_{\lambda}(a)\in\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})[\lambda]=\rm{Ker}(\rm{ad})[\lambda]
. Hence,
\rm{ad}(\it{d_{\lambda}(a)})_{\it{\mu}}=\rm{0}
for any
a\in\mathcal{R}
.

Particularly, if
\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})=\{\rm{0}\}
, then
d=0
. Thus,
{\rm C}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})=\{\rm{0}\}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
\sigma\in G
and
d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
. Then for any
a\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

[d_{\lambda}(\rm{ad}(\it{a}))]_{\mu}=\sigma\rm{ad}(\it{\sigma^{-1}}\it{d}_{\it{\lambda}}(\it{a}))_{\it{\mu}}.

Proof.

For any
b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~[d_{\lambda}(\rm{ad}(\it{a}))]_{\mu}(b)=(d_{\lambda}(\rm{ad}(\it{a}))_{\mu-\lambda}-(-1)^{|a||d|}(\rm{ad}(\it{a}))_{\mu-\lambda}d_{\lambda})(b)

\displaystyle=d_{\lambda}((\rm{ad}(\it{a}))_{\mu-\lambda}(b))-(-1)^{|a||d|}(\rm{ad}(\it{a}))_{\mu-\lambda}(d_{\lambda}(b))=d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu-\lambda}b])-(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\mu-\lambda}(d_{\lambda}(b))]

\displaystyle=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\mu}(\sigma(b))]=\sigma([(\sigma^{-1}(d_{\lambda}(a)))_{\mu}b])=\sigma(\rm{ad}(\it{\sigma^{-1}}\it{d}_{\it{\lambda}}(\it{a}))_{\it{\mu}}(b)).

Therefore,
[d_{\lambda}\rm{ad}(\it{a})]_{\mu}=\sigma\rm{ad}(\it{\sigma^{-1}}\it{d}_{\it{\lambda}}(\it{a}))_{\it{\mu}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{R}
and
\sigma\in G
. Define a map
\phi_{a}^{\sigma}:{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\rightarrow\rm{ad}(\mathcal{R})_{\gamma}
, given by
d\mapsto\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}\it{d}_{\lambda}(a))
.
Then
\phi_{a}^{\sigma}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism.

Proof.

For any
f,g\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
and
b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}(f_{\lambda}+g_{\mu}))(b)=\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}((\it{f}_{\lambda}+\it{g}_{\mu})(a)))_{\gamma}(b)=[(\sigma^{-1}((f_{\lambda}+g_{\mu})(a)))_{\gamma}b]

\displaystyle=\sigma^{-1}([((f_{\lambda}+g_{\mu})(a))_{\gamma}(\sigma(b))])=\sigma^{-1}([(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\gamma}(\sigma(b))]+[(g_{\mu}(a))_{\gamma}(\sigma(b))])

\displaystyle=\sigma^{-1}([(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\gamma}(\sigma(b))])+\sigma^{-1}([(g_{\mu}(a))_{\gamma}(\sigma(b))])=[(\sigma^{-1}(f_{\lambda}(a)))_{\gamma}b]+[(\sigma^{-1}(g_{\mu}(a)))_{\gamma}b]

\displaystyle=\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}(\it{f}_{\lambda}(\it{a})))_{\gamma}(\it{b})+\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}(\it{g}_{\mu}(\it{a})))_{\gamma}(\it{b})=(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}(f_{\lambda})+\phi_{a}^{\sigma}(g_{\mu}))_{\gamma}(b),

and

\displaystyle~~~~(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}(\partial f_{\lambda}))_{\gamma}(b)=\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}(\partial\it{f}_{\lambda})(a))_{\gamma}(b)=\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}(-\lambda\it{f}_{\lambda})(a))_{\gamma}(b)

\displaystyle=\rm{ad}(-\lambda\sigma^{-1}\it{f}_{\lambda}(a))_{\gamma}(b)=[-\lambda\sigma^{-1}\it{f}_{\lambda}(a)_{\gamma}b]=-\lambda[\sigma^{-1}\it{f}_{\lambda}(a)_{\gamma}b]

\displaystyle=-\lambda\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}(\it{f}_{\lambda}(a)))_{\gamma}(b)=\partial\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}(\it{f}_{\lambda}(a)))_{\gamma}(b)=(\partial(\phi_{a}^{\sigma}(f_{\lambda})))_{\gamma}(b).

Therefore,
\phi_{x}^{\sigma}
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module homomorphism.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

If
a\in\mathcal{R}
and
\sigma\in G
, then

\rm{Ker}(\it{\phi_{a}^{\sigma}})=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;d_{\lambda}(a)\in\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})[\lambda]\}.

What’s more,
\rm{Ker}(\it{\phi_{a}^{\sigma}})
is a subalgebra of
{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

According to Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\rm{Ker}(\it{\phi_{a}^{\sigma}})

\displaystyle=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}(\it{d}_{\lambda}(a)))_{\mu}(b)=\rm{0},\;\forall\it{b\in\mathcal{R}}\}

\displaystyle=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;\sigma(\rm{ad}(\sigma^{-1}\it{d}_{\lambda}(a))_{\mu}(b))=\rm{0},\;\forall\it{b\in\mathcal{R}}\}

\displaystyle=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;[d_{\lambda}(\rm{ad}(\it{a}))]_{\mu}(b)=\rm{0},\;\forall\it{b\in\mathcal{R}}\}

\displaystyle=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\mu}(\sigma(b))]=\rm{0},\;\forall\it{b\in\mathcal{R}}\}

\displaystyle=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\mu}b]=\rm{0},\;\forall\it{b\in\mathcal{R}}\}

\displaystyle=\{d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\;|\;d_{\lambda}(a)\in\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})[\lambda]\}.

Moreover, it is obvious that
\rm{Ker}(\it{\phi_{a}^{\sigma}})
is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module. We only need to show
\rm{Ker}(\it{\phi_{a}^{\sigma}})
is a Lie conformal superalgebra. For any
f,g\in\rm{Ker}(\it{\phi_{a}^{\sigma}})
and
b\in\mathcal{R}
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~[([f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(a))_{\gamma}(\sigma(b))]=[(f_{\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a)))_{\gamma}(\sigma(b))]-(-1)^{|f||g|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(a)))_{\gamma}(\sigma(b))]

\displaystyle=f_{\lambda}([(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\gamma-\lambda}b])-(-1)^{(|a|+|g|)|f|}[(g_{\mu-\lambda}(a))_{\gamma-\lambda}(f_{\lambda}(b))]

\displaystyle~~~~-(-1)^{|f||g|}g_{\mu-\lambda}([(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\gamma-\mu+\lambda}b])+(-1)^{(|a|+|f|)|g|+|f||g|}[(f_{\lambda}(a))_{\gamma-\mu+\lambda}(g_{\mu-\lambda}(b))]

\displaystyle=0.

Since
\sigma
is an isomorphism, it follows that
[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(a)\in\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})[\lambda]
, which implies that
[f_{\lambda}g]_{\mu}(a)\in\rm{Ker}(\it{\phi_{a}^{\sigma}})[\lambda]
. Therefore,
\rm{Ker}(\it{\phi_{x}^{\sigma}})
is a subalgebra of
{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
.
∎

As a corollary, we can obtain the following main result.

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{R}
be a centerless Lie conformal superalgebra. If there exists an element
a_{0}\in\mathcal{R}
such that
d_{\lambda}(a_{0})\neq 0
for all
d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
, then
\rm{rank}({\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R}))\leq\rm{rank}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

Note that
\rm{ad}:\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\rm{ad}(\mathcal{R})
is an isomorphism. Since
Z(\mathcal{R})=\{0\}
, we have
\phi_{a_{0}}^{\sigma}
is injective by Proposition
4.4
. Hence, as a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module,
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
can be embedded into
\mathcal{R}
.
∎

4.2
Relation with conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations

Similarly to the investigation of conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations on Lie conformal algebra, we shall define it on Lie conformal superalgebra.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an arbitrary Lie conformal superalgebra. We call a conformal linear map
d\in{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
a
conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivation
of degree
\theta
of
\mathcal{R}
if there exist
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
such that for any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, the following relation is satisfied:

\alpha d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=[(\beta d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}b]+(-1)^{|a||\theta|}[a_{\mu}(\gamma d_{\lambda}(b))].

For any given
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, we denote the set of all conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations of degree
\theta
by
{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})_{\theta}
, i.e.

{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})_{\theta}=\{d\in{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})\,~|~\,\alpha d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=[(\beta d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}b]+(-1)^{|a||\theta|}[a_{\mu}(\gamma d_{\lambda}(b))],\;\forall~a,b\in\mathcal{R}\}.

Denote by
{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})_{\bar{1}}
the set of all conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations of
\mathcal{R}
.

Obviously, we have the following conclusions.

Proposition 4.6
.

(1)

{\rm CDer}_{(1,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
.

(2)

{\rm CDer}_{(0,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm QC}(\mathcal{R})
.

(3)

{\rm CDer}_{(1,0,0)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(0,1,0)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm ZDer}(\mathcal{R})
.

(4)

{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(\triangle\alpha,\triangle\beta,\triangle\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})
,
for any
\triangle\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Furthermore, the following proposition has many useful applications.

Proposition 4.7
.

{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(0,\beta-\gamma,\gamma-\beta)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(2\alpha,\beta+\gamma,\beta+\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})
, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

For any
d\in{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})
and
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, we get the following equality:

\alpha d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=[(\beta d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}b]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(\gamma d_{\lambda}(b))].

(4.1)

By the skew symmetry and (
4.1
), for any
a,b\in\mathcal{R}
, we obtain

(-1)^{|a||b|}\alpha d_{\lambda}([b_{-\mu-\partial}a])=(-1)^{(|a|+|d|)|b|}[b_{-\lambda-\mu-\partial}(\beta d_{\lambda}(a))]+(-1)^{|a||d|+(|b|+|d|)|a|}[(\gamma d_{\lambda}(b))_{-\mu-\partial}a].

(4.2)

By the conformal sesquilinearity and replacing
-\lambda-\mu-\partial
by
\mu^{{}^{\prime}}
in (
4.2
), we get

\alpha d_{\lambda}([b_{\mu^{{}^{\prime}}}a])=(-1)^{(|b||d|}[b_{\mu^{{}^{\prime}}}(\beta d_{\lambda}(a))]+[(\gamma d_{\lambda}(b))_{\lambda+\mu^{{}^{\prime}}}a].

(4.3)

Then by changing the place of
a,b
and replacing
\mu^{{}^{\prime}}
by
\mu
in (
4.3
), we have

\alpha d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=(-1)^{(|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(\beta d_{\lambda}(b))]+[(\gamma d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}b].

(4.4)

By (
4.1
) and (
4.4
), we can get

0=(\beta-\gamma)([(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}b]-(-1)^{(|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))]),

(4.5)

and

2\alpha d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=(\beta+\gamma)([(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}b]+(-1)^{(|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))]).

(4.6)

Therefore,
{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm CDer}_{(0,\beta-\gamma,\gamma-\beta)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(2\alpha,\beta+\gamma,\beta+\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})
.
Similarly, starting with the equation (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we can also conclude that (
4.1
) holds.

Hence,

{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(0,\beta-\gamma,\gamma-\beta)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(2\alpha,\beta+\gamma,\beta+\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})
.
∎

Using similar methods in
[
4
]
and
[
8
]
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.8
.

For any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, there exists
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
such that the subspace
{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})\subseteq{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
is equal to one of the following four cases:
(1)
{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,0,0)}(\mathcal{R})
, (2)
{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})
, (3)
{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,0)}(\mathcal{R})
, (4)
{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})
.

Similarly, we can get the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.9
.

For any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, then
{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})
is equal to one of the following subspaces of
{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
:

(i)

{\rm CDer}_{(0,0,0)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})
.

(ii)

{\rm CDer}_{(1,0,0)}(\mathcal{R})=\{d_{\lambda}\in{\rm Cend}(\mathcal{R})\,~|~\,d_{\lambda}([R,R])=0\}
.

(iii)

{\rm CDer}_{(0,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm QC}(\mathcal{R})
.

(iv)

{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(0,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(1,0,0)}(\mathcal{R})
.

(v)

{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})
,
\delta\in\mathbb{C}
.

(vi)

{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,0)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(0,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(2\delta,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})
.

We turn now to the problem of relation between conformal
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivation and conformal
(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
-derivations.

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta+\lambda},1,0)}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
4.7
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~{\rm CDer}_{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle={\rm CDer}_{(0,\beta-\gamma,\gamma-\beta)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(2\alpha,\beta+\gamma,\beta+\gamma)}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle={\rm CDer}_{(0,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(\frac{2\alpha}{\beta+\gamma},1,1)}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle={\rm CDer}_{(\frac{\alpha}{\beta+\lambda},1,0)}(\mathcal{R}).

This lemma is proved.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Suppose that
\sigma\in G
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
such that
(\sigma-\alpha\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}})(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})
. If
\alpha\neq 1
, then
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(\frac{1}{\alpha+1},1,0)}(\mathcal{R})
;
if
\alpha=1
, then
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(1,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

Assume that
d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})
.
Since
(\sigma-\alpha\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}})(\mathcal{R})\subseteq\rm{Z}(\mathcal{R})
, we have

[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\mu}(\sigma(b))]=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\mu}(\alpha b)],\quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal{R}.

Notice that

d\in{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})\Leftrightarrow d_{\lambda}([(a_{\mu}b])=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\mu}(\alpha b)]+(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))]\Leftrightarrow d\in{\rm CDer}_{(1,\alpha,1)}(\mathcal{R}),

which implies that
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(1,\alpha,1)}(\mathcal{R})
. If
\alpha\neq 1
, then
{\rm CDer}_{(1,\alpha,1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(\frac{1}{\alpha+1},1,0)}(\mathcal{R})
by Lemma
4.10
. Therefore,
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(\frac{1}{\alpha+1},1,0)}(\mathcal{R})
.
If
\alpha=1
, then
{\rm CDer}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{(1,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}(\mathcal{R})
.
∎

Proposition 4.12
.

If
\delta\neq 0
, then

{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}},-\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}}(\mathcal{R})
and
{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{\frac{1}{\delta}\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}},\frac{1}{\delta}\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}}(\mathcal{R})
.

Proof.

According to Proposition
4.7
, we have

\displaystyle~~~~{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle={\rm CDer}_{(0,2,-2)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(2\delta,0,0)}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle={\rm CDer}_{(0,2,-2)}(\mathcal{R})\cap{\rm CDer}_{(2,0,0)}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle={\rm CDer}_{(1,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R}).

For any
d\in{\rm CDer}_{(1,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})
, we have

d_{\lambda}([a_{\mu}b])=[(d_{\lambda}(a))_{\lambda+\mu}b]-(-1)^{|a||d|}[a_{\mu}(d_{\lambda}(b))],

which means
d
is a
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivation with
\sigma=\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}
and
\tau=-\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}
.
Therefore,
{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,-1)}(\mathcal{R})={\rm CDer}_{\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}},-\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}}(\mathcal{R})
.

Similarly,
{\rm CDer}_{(\delta,1,1)}(\mathcal{R})
is a
(\sigma,\tau)
-derivation with
\sigma=\frac{1}{\delta}\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}
and
\tau=\frac{1}{\delta}\rm{id}_{\mathcal{R}}
.
∎

1d Conformal Field Theory and Dispersion Relations
Dean Carmi, Sudip Ghosh, Trakshu Sharma

Abstract
We study conformal field theory in d = 1 d=1 space-time dimensions. We derive a dispersion relation for the 4-point correlation function of identical bosons and fermions, in terms of the double discontinuity. This extends the conformal dispersion relation of [ 1 ] , which holds for CFTs in dimensions d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , to the case of d = 1 d=1 . The dispersion relation is obtained by combining the Lorentzian inversion formula with the operator product expansion of the 4-point correlator. We perform checks of the dispersion relation using correlators of generalised free fields and derive an integral relation between the kernel of the dispersion relation and that of the Lorentzian inversion formula. Finally, for 1 1 - d d holographic conformal theories, we analytically compute scalar Witten diagrams in A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} at tree-level and 1 1 -loop.

Quantum field theories that have scale and conformal symmetry, i.e. conformal field theories (CFT) find applications in diverse areas of physics such as in the study of critical phenomena, statistical mechanics and string theory. 1.1
Conformal dispersion relation for
d\geq 2

A dispersion relation is a formula that gives a function in terms of an integral over its discontinuities. The simplest type of dispersion relations are single-variable Cauchy-type dispersion relations. For example, the 2-to-2 relativistic scattering amplitude
\mathcal{M}(s,t)
can be shown to obey a Cauchy-type dispersion relation
1
1

1

For a Cauchy-type dispersion relation in the context of CFT 4-point correlators, see
[
51
]
.

\mathcal{M}(s,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\frac{Disc_{s^{\prime}}[\mathcal{M}(s^{\prime},t)]}{s^{\prime}-s}+(s\leftrightarrow u)

(1)

where the integral is along the branch cuts of the amplitude
\mathcal{M}
, and the (single) discontinuity across the cut is:

iDisc_{s^{\prime}}[\mathcal{M}(s^{\prime},t)]\equiv\mathcal{M}(s^{\prime}+i0,t)-\mathcal{M}(s^{\prime}-i0,t)\ .

(2)

The standard way to derive the Cauchy-type dispersion relation of Eq. (
1
) is via a contour deformation argument. Specifically, one starts by using Cauchy’s residue theorem for the function
\frac{\mathcal{M}(s^{\prime},t)}{s^{\prime}-s}
, then the contour is deformed to wrap the branch cuts, which results in the dispersion relation Eq. (
1
). For the derivation to work, the amplitude
\mathcal{M}(s,t)
has to be an analytic function apart from cuts, and it must decay fast enough as
s\to\infty
.

An alternative derivation of the dispersion relation starts by assuming the Froissart-Gribov inversion formula and inserting it in the partial-wave expansion of
\mathcal{M}(s,t)
. For a review of this procedure we refer the reader to section 2.1 of
[
1
]
. Instead, we will now review details of the derivation of the conformal dispersion relation, which has a similar essence.

A conformal dispersion relation for the 4-point correlation functions of a CFT
d≥2
, was derived in
[
1
]
. This formula gives the 4-point correlator
\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})
in terms of an integral over its double discontinuity. This formula was derived by combining the Lorentzian inversion formula and the OPE expansion of the correlator
\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})
. We now summarize the main steps of this derivation. The Lorentzian inversion formula of
[
7
]
is:

c(J,\Delta)=c^{t}(J,\Delta)+(-1)^{J}c^{u}(J,\Delta)

(3)

where

c^{t}(J,\Delta)=\frac{\kappa_{J+\Delta}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}dwd\bar{w}\mu(w,\bar{w})G_{\Delta+1-d,J+d-1}(w,\bar{w})\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}(w,\bar{w})].

(4)

Here
c^{t}(J,\Delta)
is the
t
-channel OPE function,
G_{\Delta+1-d,J+d-1}(w,\bar{w})
is the “inverted” conformal block, and
\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})]
is the double discontinuity around
\bar{w}=1
, defined as:

\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})]\equiv\cos(\pi(a+b))\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})-\frac{1}{2}e^{i\pi(a+b)}\Big(\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z}^{\circlearrowleft})+\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z}^{\circlearrowright})\Big).

(5)

The conformal partial wave expansion of
\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})
can be written as:

\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{d}{2}-i\infty}^{\frac{d}{2}+i\infty}\frac{d\Delta}{2\pi i}\ c(J,\Delta)F_{J,\Delta}(z,\bar{z})

(6)

where
F_{J,\Delta}(z,\bar{z})
is the conformal partial wave, defined as the combination of the conformal block and its shadow
2
2

2

The coefficient of the shadow block is not important for our purposes.
:

F_{J,\Delta}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(G_{J,\Delta}(z,\bar{z})+\#G_{J,d-\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\Big).

(7)

To derive the conformal dispersion relation, we insert the Lorentzian inversion formula Eq. (
3
) and (
4
) inside the conformal partial wave expansion Eq. (
6
). The result is:

\mathcal{G}^{t}(z,\bar{z})=\int_{0}^{1}dwd\bar{w}\ K(z,\bar{z},w,\bar{w})\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}(w,\bar{w})]

(8)

where the kernel is:

K(z,\bar{z},w,\bar{w})=\frac{\mu(w,\bar{w})}{8\pi i}\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}\int_{\frac{d}{2}-i\infty}^{\frac{d}{2}+i\infty}d\Delta\ \kappa_{J+\Delta}F_{J,\Delta}(z,\bar{z})G_{\Delta+1-d,J+d-1}(w,\bar{w}).

(9)

The full correlator is the sum of the
t
and
u
-channel contributions:

\mathcal{G}(z,\bar{z})=\mathcal{G}^{t}(z,\bar{z})+\mathcal{G}^{u}(z,\bar{z})\ .

(10)

The dispersion kernel
K(z,\bar{z},w,\bar{w})
is a function of 4 cross-ratios, and a major goal of
[
1
]
was to compute it analytically in terms of these variables. The result is that
K
can be written as a hypergeometric
{}_{2}F_{1}
function, and we refer to
[
1
]
for its detailed form.

Consider a CFT in
d=1
space-time dimensions, containing a primary operator
\phi
with scaling dimension
\Delta_{\phi}
.
The goal of this section is to derive the dispersion relation for the four-point function
\langle\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\phi(x_{3})\phi(x_{4})\rangle
of identical primary operators
\phi
. This four-point function is fixed by conformal symmetry, up to a function of a single cross-ratio
z

\langle\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})\phi(x_{3})\phi(x_{4})\rangle=\frac{\mathcal{G}(z)}{|x_{12}|^{2\Delta_{\phi}}|x_{34}|^{2\Delta_{\phi}}}\ ,\quad\textrm{where, }\ \ z=\frac{x_{12}x_{34}}{x_{13}x_{24}}\ .

(11)

Crossing symmetry of the correlator implies the following constraint on the function
\mathcal{G}(z)
,

\begin{split}z^{-2\Delta_{\phi}}\mathcal{G}(z)=(1-z)^{-2\Delta_{\phi}}\mathcal{G}(1-z)\quad\text{for}\ z\in(0,1)\ .\end{split}

(12)

The function
\mathcal{G}(z)
can be expanded using the s-channel OPE (i.e.
x_{2}\rightarrow x_{1}
) in terms of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
conformal blocks (
G_{\Delta}(z)
),

\mathcal{G}(z)=\sum_{\Delta_{\mathcal{O}}}\left(c_{\phi\phi\mathcal{O}}\right)^{2}G_{\Delta_{\mathcal{O}}}(z)

(13)

where
c_{\phi\phi\mathcal{O}}
are the OPE coefficients. The conformal partial wave expansion that will be useful for deriving the dispersion relation using the Lorentzian inversion formula (LIF) is
[
52
,
46
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(z)=\int_{\frac{1}{2}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+i\infty}\frac{d\Delta}{2\pi i}\frac{I_{\Delta}}{2K_{\Delta}}G_{\Delta}(z)+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{m\in 2N}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(m)}{\Gamma(2m-1)}\tilde{I}_{m}G_{m}(z)\quad\textrm{for }\ z\in(0,1)

(14)

where the first term corresponds to the principal series representation of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, the second sum involves the discrete series representation of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, and
K_{\Delta}
is defined as,

\displaystyle K_{\Delta}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\Delta-\frac{1}{2})\Gamma^{2}(\frac{1-\Delta}{2})}{\Gamma(1-\Delta)\Gamma^{2}(\frac{\Delta}{2})}\ .

(15)

The coefficients
\left(c_{\phi\phi\mathcal{O}}\right)^{2}
in the conformal block decomposition Eq. (
13
) are given by the residue of the simple pole of the function
\frac{I_{\Delta}}{2K_{\Delta}}
at
\Delta=\Delta_{O}
where
\Delta_{O}
is the scaling dimension of the operator
\mathcal{O}
exchanged in the s-channel OPE.

The Lorentzian inversion formula (LIF) for the discrete series coefficient is
[
52
,
46
]
:

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\Gamma^{2}(m)}{\Gamma(2m)}\int_{0}^{1}dww^{-2}G_{m}(w)\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}(w)]

(16)

where
G_{m}(w)
is the conformal block with
m
being an integer. The Lorentzian inversion formula (LIF) for the principal series OPE coefficient function is
[
46
]
:

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}dww^{-2}H_{\Delta}(\Delta_{\phi},w)\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}(w)]\ .

(17)

In the LIF above,
\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}(w)]
is the double discontinuity of the 4-point function, defined for boson and fermions as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{dDisc}_{B}\left[\mathcal{G}(z)\right]=\mathcal{G}(z)-\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\left(\frac{1}{z}+i\epsilon\right)+\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\left(\frac{1}{z}-i\epsilon\right)}{2}

\displaystyle\mathrm{dDisc}_{F}\left[\mathcal{G}(z)\right]=\mathcal{G}(z)+\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\left(\frac{1}{z}+i\epsilon\right)+\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\left(\frac{1}{z}+i\epsilon\right)}{2}

(18)

where
z\in(0,1)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\left(z\right)=z^{-2\Delta_{\phi}}\mathcal{G}\left(z\right)
.

The inversion formula kernel
H_{\Delta}(\Delta_{\phi},w)
depends on whether the
\phi
’s are bosons or fermions. It also depends explicitly on the scaling dimension
\Delta_{\phi}
. We will subsequently use the notation
H^{B}_{\Delta}(\Delta_{\phi},w)
for bosons, and
H^{F}_{\Delta}(\Delta_{\phi},w)
for fermions. The inversion kernel was explicitly computed in
[
46
]
for bosons with scaling dimensions
\Delta_{\phi}
being integers, and for fermions with scaling dimensions
\Delta_{\phi}
being half-integers. As opposed to the inversion formula in higher

[
46
]
.

As shown in
[
1
]

1
-
d

16

\displaystyle\boxed{\mathcal{G}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}dw\ K(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)\ \mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}(w)]}

(19)

Here the dispersion kernel
K(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)
consists of the following two components:

K(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)=K_{dis}(z,w)+K_{princ}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)

(20)

with

and

\displaystyle K_{dis}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}w^{-2}\sum_{m\in 2N}\frac{\Gamma^{4}(m)}{\Gamma(2m)\Gamma(2m-1)}G_{m}(w)G_{m}(z)

(21)

\displaystyle K_{princ}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}w^{-2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{2}+i\infty}\frac{d\Delta}{2\pi i}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\Delta)}{\Gamma(2\Delta-1)}\frac{\cos[\frac{\pi\Delta}{2}]}{\sin[\frac{\pi\Delta}{2}]}H_{\Delta}(\Delta_{\phi},w)G_{\Delta}(z)\ .

(22)

Note that the discrete series kernel does not depend on the function

\begin{split}z^{-2\Delta_{\phi}}K(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)=(1-z)^{-2\Delta_{\phi}}K(\Delta_{\phi},1-z,w)\ .\end{split}

(23)

2.1
The discrete series kernel

The kernel corresponding to the discrete series is Eq. (

\displaystyle K_{dis}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}w^{-2}\sum_{m\in 2N}\frac{\Gamma^{4}(m)}{\Gamma(2m)\Gamma(2m-1)}G_{m}(w)G_{m}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}w^{-2}\sum_{m\in 2N}(2m-1)Q_{m-1}(\hat{w})Q_{m-1}(\hat{z})

where, in the second line we wrote the conformal block in terms of a Legendre function of the second type (LegendreQ’s) as follows:

\displaystyle G_{\Delta}(w)=w^{\Delta}{}_{2}F_{1}(\Delta,\Delta,2\Delta,w)=\frac{2\Gamma(2\Delta)}{\Gamma^{2}(\Delta)}Q_{\Delta-1}(\hat{w})

\displaystyle K_{dis}(z,w)=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}w^{-2}\bigg(\frac{\log\Big[\frac{\hat{w}+1}{\hat{w}-1}\frac{\hat{z}-1}{\hat{z}+1}\Big]}{\hat{z}-\hat{w}}+\frac{\log\Big[\frac{-\hat{w}+1}{-\hat{w}-1}\frac{\hat{z}-1}{\hat{z}+1}\Big]}{\hat{w}+\hat{z}}\bigg)

\displaystyle K_{dis}(z,w)=\frac{z}{\pi^{2}w}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{w-z}+\frac{1}{w+(1-w)z}\bigg)\log\big(1-z\big)

\displaystyle+\bigg(-\frac{1}{w-z}+\frac{1}{w+(1-w)z}\bigg)\log\big(1-w\big)\bigg]

The discrete kernel is independent of the external scaling dimensions

\displaystyle K^{B}_{princ}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)

2.3
Improved bosonic kernel

The kernel in the bosonic Lorentzian OPE inversion formula, which was used in the previous section to derive the dispersion relation, has the property:

\begin{split}H_{\Delta}^{B}(\Delta_{\phi},z)=O(z^{0}),\quad\text{as}\ z\rightarrow 0.\end{split}

As shown in

\begin{split}|z^{-2\Delta_{\phi}}\mathcal{G}(z)|=O(|z|^{-1-\epsilon}),\quad\text{as}\ z\rightarrow\infty,\text{with}\ \epsilon>0.\end{split}

2.5
Dispersion relation from Polyakov bootstrap

In this section, we briefly review the approach, based on the Polyakov bootstrap, taken in
[
47

for deriving dispersion relations for

-

The Polyakov bootstrap in the case of
1d
CFTs is the statement that the expansion of a four-point CFT correlator
\hat{\mathcal{G}}(z)=\mathcal{G}(z)/z^{2\Delta_{\phi}}
in terms of conformal blocks

is equivalent to expanding the correlator in terms of Polyakov blocks
\mathcal{P}_{\Delta_{O}}(\Delta_{\phi},z)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{G}}(z)=\sum_{\Delta_{O}}a_{\Delta_{O}}\frac{G_{\Delta}(z)}{z^{2\Delta_{\phi}}}=\sum_{\Delta_{O}}a_{\Delta_{O}}\mathcal{P}_{\Delta_{O}}(\Delta_{\phi},z).

(57)

Here
a_{\Delta_{O}}
denote squared OPE coefficients. In
[
47
]
, it was shown that Polyakov blocks admit the following integral representations in terms of the double-discontinuity of the conformal blocks,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{B,\Delta_{O}}(\Delta_{\phi},z)=-\int_{0}^{1}dw\ \widehat{g}_{B}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)\ \mathrm{dDisc}_{B}\left[\frac{G_{\Delta}(w)}{w^{2\Delta_{\phi}}}\right],\quad\text{for}\ \Delta_{O}>2\Delta_{\phi},

(58)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{F,\Delta_{O}}(\Delta_{\phi},z)=\int_{0}^{1}dw\ \widehat{g}_{F}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)\ \mathrm{dDisc}_{F}\left[\frac{G_{\Delta}(w)}{w^{2\Delta_{\phi}}}\right],\quad\text{for}\ \Delta_{O}>2\Delta_{\phi}-1,

(59)

where the subscripts

on the Polyakov blocks refer to the bosonic and fermionic cases respectively and
\widehat{g}_{B}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w),\ \widehat{g}_{F}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)
are certain kernels. The above integral representations were obtained in
[
47
]
by defining the Polyakov blocks in terms of a set of master functionals. These are extremal functionals which yield rigorous upper and lower bounds on the values of the four-point correlator
\mathcal{G}(z)
. The action of such master functionals on conformal blocks
G_{\Delta}(z)
can be expressed via contour integrals involving the kernels
\widehat{g}_{B}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w),\ \widehat{g}_{F}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)
in the bosonic and fermionic cases respectively. Then, one can arrive at Eqs. (
58
), (
59
) via an appropriate contour deformation. We refer the reader to
[
47
]
for the details of this derivation. In particular, they showed that these kernels satisfy following integral equation.

\displaystyle\widehat{g}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)

\displaystyle=R_{\Delta_{\phi}}(z,w)-\int_{0}^{1}dz^{\prime}R_{\Delta_{\phi}}(z,z^{\prime})\ \widehat{g}(\Delta_{\phi},z^{\prime},w)

(60)

with,

\displaystyle R_{\Delta_{\phi}}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{z(1-z)}{w(1-w)}}\frac{1+w}{\left(z-\frac{w}{w-1}\right)\left(\frac{1}{1-w}-z\right)}(1-w)^{2\Delta_{\phi}-\frac{3}{2}}

(61)

in Eq. (

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}(z)=-\int_{0}^{1}dw\ \widehat{g}_{B}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)\ \mathrm{dDisc}_{B}\left[\overline{\mathcal{G}}(w)\right],

(62)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(z)=\int_{0}^{1}dw\ \widehat{g}_{F}(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)\ \mathrm{dDisc}_{F}\left[\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(w)\right],

(63)

where we have defined,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}(z)=\hat{\mathcal{G}}(z)-\sum_{0\leq\Delta_{O}\leq 2\Delta_{\phi}}a_{\Delta_{O}}\mathcal{P}_{B,\Delta_{O}}(\Delta_{\phi},z),

(64)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}(z)=\hat{\mathcal{G}}(z)-\sum_{0\leq\Delta_{O}\leq 2\Delta_{\phi}-1}a_{\Delta_{O}}\mathcal{P}_{F,\Delta_{O}}(\Delta_{\phi},z).

(65)

We expect the kernels in the above dispersion relations to be identical to the kernels we have obtained in the previous section using the

-

Lorentzian OPE inversion formula. In particular, we have checked that the kernel in the fermionic case for
\Delta_{\phi}=1/2

Thus only the term involving the double discontinuity in the dispersion relation Eq. (

4
Relation between the LIF kernel and the dispersion kernel

In this section, we obtain a relation between the inversion formula kernel
H_{\Delta}(\Delta_{\phi},w)
and the dispersion relation kernel
K(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)
. We start with the Euclidean OPE inversion formula for the OPE coefficient function

which is given by,

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dzz^{-2}\ \psi_{\Delta}(z)\mathcal{G}(z)\ ,

is the

-

CFT correlator defined in Eq. (

as the following linear combination of the conformal block

and the shadow conformal block

\displaystyle\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}(z)=K_{1-\Delta}G_{\Delta}(z)+K_{\Delta}G_{1-\Delta}(z)\quad\text{for}\ z\in(0,1)\ ,

(87)

where

\displaystyle K_{\Delta}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\Delta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\Delta}{2}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(1-\Delta)\Gamma\left(\frac{\Delta}{2}\right)^{2}}\ .

(88)

For
z\in(-\infty,0)
and
z\in(1,\infty)
the conformal partial waves are given by
[
46
]
,

\displaystyle\psi^{(-)}_{\Delta}(z)=\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{z}{z-1}\right)\quad\text{for}\ z\in(-\infty,0)\ ,

\displaystyle\psi^{(+)}_{\Delta}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}(z+i\epsilon)+\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}(z-i\epsilon)\right]\quad\text{for}\ z\in(1,\infty)\ .

(89)

We will now rewrite
I_{\Delta}
above as an integral over the region
z\in(0,1)
as done in
[
46
]
. For this, let us split the integral into three regions as follows:

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{0}dzz^{-2}\ \psi^{(-)}_{\Delta}(z)\mathcal{G}^{(-)}(z)+\int_{0}^{1}dzz^{-2}\ \psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}(z)\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(z)

\displaystyle+\int_{1}^{\infty}dzz^{-2}\ \psi^{(+)}_{\Delta}(z)\mathcal{G}^{(+)}(z)\ .

(90)

In the above expression,
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(z)
is the definition of the correlation function
\mathcal{G}(z)
for
z\in(0,1)
, and
\mathcal{G}^{(\pm)}(z)
are given in terms of
\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(z)
via
[
46
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(-)}(z)=\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(z)\left(\frac{z}{z-1}\right)\quad\text{for}\ z\in(-\infty,0)\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(+)}(z)=\pm z^{2\Delta_{\phi}}\mathcal{G}^{(0)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\quad\text{for}\ z\in(1,\infty)\ ,

(91)

where the
\pm
sign in the last relation in Eq. (
91
) refers to the bosonic (fermionic) case. Then using the relations Eqs. (
89
),(
91
) and employing a change of variables to recast all the integrals to the region

86

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}=\int_{0}^{1}dzz^{-2}\left[2\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}(z)\pm\frac{\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{z}+i\epsilon\right)+\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{z}-i\epsilon\right)}{2z^{2\Delta_{\phi}-2}}\right]\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(z).

(92)

where again the relative
\pm
sign in Eq. (
92
) refers to the bosonic (fermionic) case. This representation of

was previously obtained in
[

, which was useful for deriving the kernel in the Lorentzian OPE inversion formula.

We can now insert the dispersion relation Eq. (

\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(z)
in Eq. (

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dzz^{-2}\left[2\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}(z)\pm\frac{\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{z}+i\epsilon\right)+\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{z}-i\epsilon\right)}{2z^{2\Delta_{\phi}-2}}\right]\int_{0}^{1}dw\ K(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(w)]

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}dww^{-2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}dzz^{-2}\left[2\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}(z)\pm\frac{\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{z}+i\epsilon\right)+\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{z}-i\epsilon\right)}{2z^{2\Delta_{\phi}-2}}\right]\frac{w^{2}}{2}K(\Delta_{\phi},z,w)\right)\times

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(w)].

(93)

where in the last equality we changed the order of integrations. Now Eq. (

\displaystyle I_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}dww^{-2}\ H_{\Delta}(\Delta_{\phi},w)\mathrm{dDisc}[\mathcal{G}^{(0)}(w)]\ .

(94)

Comparing the previous two equations gives our desired formula:

\displaystyle H_{\Delta}(\Delta_{\phi},w)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dzz^{-2}\left[2\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}(z)\pm\frac{\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{z}+i\epsilon\right)+\psi^{(0)}_{\Delta}\left(\frac{1}{z}-i\epsilon\right)}{2z^{2\Delta_{\phi}-2}}\right]\frac{w^{2}}{2}K(\Delta_{\phi},z,w).

This relation shows that the inversion kernel

against the the dispersion kernel

[
46
]
.

To compute the above sum we will use the following identity,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dm^{\prime}}\Big[(2m^{\prime}+1)P_{m^{\prime}}(x)Q_{m^{\prime}}(y)\Big]\bigg|_{m^{\prime}=m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-y}\frac{d}{d\alpha}\bigg[\alpha\Big(P_{\alpha-1}(x)Q_{\alpha}(y)-P_{\alpha}(x)Q_{\alpha-1}(y)\Big)\bigg]_{\alpha\to 0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{x-y}\log\Big[\frac{y+1}{x+1}\Big]\ .

(147)

where, in the second equality we have computed the rhs of the first line explicitly. Again using the property of LegendreP’s under parity i.e.
P_{n}(-x)=(-1)^{n}P_{n}(x)
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\textrm{odd}}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dm^{\prime}}\Big[(2m^{\prime}+1)P_{m^{\prime}}(x)Q_{m^{\prime}}(y)\Big]\bigg|_{m^{\prime}=m}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{x-y}\log\Big[\frac{y+1}{x+1}\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{x+y}\log\Big[\frac{y-1}{x+1}\Big]\ .

(148)

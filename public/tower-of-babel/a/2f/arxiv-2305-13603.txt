Volterra integral operators and general linear integral operators representing polynomial covariance type commutation relations on L p L_{p} spaces
Domingos Djinja, Sergei Silvestrov, Alex Behakanira Tumwesigye

Abstract
Conditions for linear integral operators on L p L_{p} over measure spaces to satisfy the polynomial covariance type commutation relations are described in terms of defining kernels of the corresponding integral operators. Representation by integral operators are studied both for general polynomial covariance commutation relations and for important classes of polynomial covariance commutation relations associated to arbitrary monomials and to affine functions. Representations of the covariance type commutation relations by integral Volterra operators and integral convolution operators are investigated. MSC 2020: 47A62, 47L80, 47L65, 47G10, 45D05

Lemma 1

Then the following statements are equivalent

1.

For some

Q_{G_{1}}(f,x)=\int\limits_{G_{1}}f(t)x(t)d\mu=\int\limits_{G_{2}}g(t)x(t)d\mu=Q_{G_{2}}(g,x).

2
\Rightarrow
1

\Sigma

\textstyle\int\limits_{G_{1}}f(t)x(t)d\mu=\int\limits_{G_{1}\setminus G}f(t)x(t)d\mu+\int\limits_{G}f(t)x(t)d\mu=\int\limits_{G}f(t)x(t)d\mu\\
\textstyle=\int\limits_{G}g(t)x(t)d\mu=\int\limits_{G_{2}\setminus G}g(t)x(t)d\mu+\int\limits_{G}g(t)x(t)d\mu=\int\limits_{G_{2}}g(t)x(t)d\mu.

1
\Rightarrow

Then

\int\limits_{G_{1}\setminus G}f(t)d\mu=0.

Analogously by taking

we get

\int\limits_{G_{2}\setminus G}g(t)d\mu=0.

\int\limits_{S_{i}}f(t)d\mu=0.

Analogously,

g(t)=0\ \mbox{ for almost every }t\in G_{2}\setminus G.

Theorem 3.1

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and let
A:L_{p}(X,\mu)\to L_{p}(X,\mu)
and
B:\,L_{p}(X,\mu)\to L_{p}(X,\mu)
be nonzero linear operators defined, for almost every
t
, by

\displaystyle(Ax)(t)=\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,s)x(s)d\mu_{s},\quad(Bx)(t)=\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)x(s)d\mu_{s},

(
the subscript
s
in
\mu_{s}
indicates the variable of integration
)

where
G_{A},\,G_{B}\in\Sigma
,
\mu(G_{A})<\infty
,
\mu(G_{B})<\infty
, and
k_{A}(t,s):X\times G_{A}\to\mathbb{R}
and
{k}_{B}(t,s):X\times G_{B}\to\mathbb{R}
are measurable functions.
Let

F(z)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\delta_{j}z^{j}
, where
\delta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
j=0,\ldots,n
. Set
G=G_{A}\cap G_{B}
and

\displaystyle k_{0,A}(t,s)=k_{A}(t,s),\quad k_{m,A}(t,s)=\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,\tau)k_{m-1,A}(\tau,s)d{\mu_{\tau}},\quad m=1,\ldots,n,

\displaystyle F_{0}(k_{A}(t,s))=0,\quad F_{n}(k_{A}(t,s))=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}k_{j-1}(t,s),\mbox{ if }n\geq 1.

Then
AB=BF(A)

if and only if the following conditions are fulfilled:

1.

for almost every
(t,\tau)\in X\times G
,

\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,s){k}_{B}(s,\tau)d\mu_{s}-\delta_{0}{k}_{B}(t,\tau)=\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)F_{n}(k_{A}(s,\tau))d\mu_{s};

2.

for almost every
(t,\tau)\in X\times(G_{B}\setminus G)
;

\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,s){k}_{B}(s,\tau)d\mu_{s}=\delta_{0}{k}_{B}(t,\tau),

3.

for almost every
(t,\tau)\in X\times(G_{A}\setminus G)
,

\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)F_{n}(k_{A}(s,\tau))d\mu_{s}=0.

Proof

By applying Fubini theorem from
AdamsG
and iterative kernels from
KrasnolskZabreyko
we have
for all
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle(A^{m}x)(t)=\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{{m-1},A}(t,s)x(s)d\mu_{s},\quad\mbox{ where }

\displaystyle k_{m,A}(t,s)=\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,\tau)k_{m-1,A}(\tau,s)d{\mu_{\tau}},\quad m=1,\ldots,n,\quad k_{0,A}(t,s)=k_{A}(t,s).

It follows that

\displaystyle(F(A)x)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{0}x(t)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}(A^{j}x)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{0}x(t)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{j-1,A}(t,s)x(s)d\mu_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{0}x(t)+\int\limits_{G_{A}}F_{n}(k_{A}(t,s))x(s)d\mu_{s},\ \mbox{ where }

\displaystyle F_{0}(k_{A}(t,s))=0,

\displaystyle\quad F_{n}(k_{A}(t,s))=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}k_{j-1,A}(t,s),\mbox{ if }n\geq 1.

Computation of
BF(A)x
and
(AB)x
yields

\displaystyle(BF(A)x)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)(F(A)x)(s)d\mu_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)\big(\delta_{0}x(s)+\int\limits_{G_{A}}F_{n}(k_{A}(s,\tau)x(\tau)d{\mu_{\tau}})\big)d\mu_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{0}\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)x(s)d\mu_{s}+\int\limits_{G_{A}}\big(\ \int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)F_{n}(k_{A}(s,\tau))d\mu_{s}\big)x(\tau)d\mu_{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{0}\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)x(s)d\mu_{s}+\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{BF(A)}(t,\tau)x(\tau)d\mu_{\tau},

\displaystyle\quad\text{where}\quad k_{BF(A)}(t,\tau)=\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)F_{n}(k_{A}(s,\tau))d\mu_{s},

\displaystyle(ABx)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{G_{B}}k_{A}(t,s)(Bx)(s)d\mu_{s}=\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,s)\big(\ \int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(s,\tau)x(\tau)d\mu_{\tau}\big)d\mu_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{G_{B}}\big(\ \int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,s){k}_{B}(s,\tau)d\mu_{s}\big)x(\tau)d\mu_{\tau}=\int\limits_{G_{B}}k_{AB}(t,\tau)x(\tau)d\mu_{\tau},

\displaystyle\quad\text{where}\quad k_{AB}(t,\tau)=\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,s){k}_{B}(s,\tau)d\mu_{s}.

We thus have

for all

if and only if

\int\limits_{G_{B}}(k_{AB}(t,\tau)-\delta_{0}{k}_{B}(t,\tau))x(\tau)d\mu_{\tau}=\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{BF(A)}(t,\tau)x(\tau)d\mu_{\tau}.

By applying Lemma

, we conclude that

if and only if
the following conditions hold:

for almost every

,

for almost every

,

\int\limits_{G_{A}}k_{A}(t,s){k}_{B}(s,\tau)d\mu_{s}=\delta_{0}{k}_{B}(t,\tau);

3.

for almost every
(t,\tau)\in X\times(G_{A}\setminus G)
,

\int\limits_{G_{B}}{k}_{B}(t,s)F_{n}(k_{A}(s,\tau))d\mu_{s}=0.

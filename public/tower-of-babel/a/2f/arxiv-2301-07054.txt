Powerful 3-Engel groups
Iker de las Heras, Marialaura Noce, Gunnar Traustason

Abstract
. In this paper we study powerful 3-Engel groups. In particular, we find sharp upper bounds for the nilpotency class of powerful 3 3 -Engel groups and the subclass of powerful metabelian 3 3 -Engel groups.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20F45, 20D15, 20F19

1.
Introduction

Let
n
be a positive integer. A group
G
is said to be an
n
-Engel group if for all
x,y\in G
we have
[[y,x],\overset{n}{\ldots}\,,x]=1.

It is a long-standing question whether a finitely generated
n
-Engel group is nilpotent.
By Wilson
[
12
]
, a finite
r
-generator
n
-Engel group is nilpotent of
(n,r)
-bounded class and hence any finitely generated residually finite
n
-Engel group is nilpotent.
Whereas the class of a finite
n
-Engel groups depends on the number of generators when
n\geq 3
, the situation is different if we furthermore assume that
G
is a powerful
p
-group.

Recall that a finite
p
-group
G
is powerful if
[G,G]\leq G^{p}
, when
p
is odd, and
[G,G]\leq G^{4}
, when
p=2
. The reason why the nilpotency class of a powerful
n
-Engel group is only bounded in
n
comes from a well known theorem of Burns and Medvedev
[
1
]
, according to which there exist positive integers
m
and
k
only depending on
n
such that any finite
n
-Engel group satisfies
\gamma_{m}(G)^{k}=1
.

Let
G
be a powerful
m
-Engel
p
-group. It is a well known property of powerful
p
-groups that
\gamma_{m+l}(G)\leq\gamma_{m}(G)^{p^{l}}
. Thus if we take
m=m(n)
and
k=k(n)
as in the theorem of Burns and Medvedev, then picking
l
such that
p^{l}\geq k
for all
p
we see that every powerful
n
-Engel
p
-group is nilpotent of class at most
c=m+l-1
.
Notice that the class is bounded only by
n
and not by
r
or
p
.

In
[
7
]
and
[
10
]
it is shown that the best upper bound for the nilpotency class of powerful
2
-Engel groups is
3
. In this case we get the same upper bound as holds for
2
-Engel groups in general. It is though interesting to note that it is shown that every powerful

3
-generator
2
-Engel group is nilpotent of class at most
2
and thus satisfies the law
[x,y,z]=1
. This may seem counter intuitive but one should remember that the class of powerful
p
-groups is not closed under taking subgroups.

In this paper we will turn our focus on powerful
3
-Engel groups. The latter and, more precisely, the behaviour of 3-Engel elements in groups has been developed and studied recently
[
9
,
11
,
6
,
4
]
.

We now describe our main results. We start with a general result on powerful
n
-Engel groups. Note that every powerful group
G
of rank
2
has a cyclic derived subgroup. In such a case, if
G
is also
n
-Engel, we show that the nilpotency class of
G
can be at most
n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and let
G
be an
n
-Engel group with cyclic derived subgroup.
Then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
n
.
Moreover, this bound is best possible.

Remark 1.2
.

If
G
is a
2
-generator non-powerful
3
-Engel
p
-group, then its derived subgroup need not be cyclic.
In that case the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
3
if
p
is odd and
4
if
p=2
(this was shown in
[
5
]
and it is summed up in Theorem 2.1 of
[
3
]
).
These bounds are also best possible.

Our main result is determining the best upper bound for the nilpotency class of powerful
3
-Engel groups.
From
[
3
]
we know that every
3
-Engel group satisfies
\gamma_{5}(G)^{20}=1
, and thus
\gamma_{5}(G)=1
when
G
is a
p
-group with
p\neq 2,5
,
\gamma_{5}(G)^{5}=1
when
p=5
and
\gamma_{5}(G)^{4}=1
when
p=2
.
From the general analysis above we thus know that when
G
is furthermore powerful then
\gamma_{6}(G)=1
.
We will show that
5
is the best upper bound for the nilpotency class.
The following theorem gives a more detailed analysis.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a powerful
3
-Engel
p
-group. If
p\neq 2,5
, then
G
is nilpotent of class at most
4
.
Furthermore, if
G
has rank
r\geq 3
, then:

(i)

If
r=3
, then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
3
;

(ii)

If
r\geq 4
, then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
4
.

If
p=2
or
5
, then
G
is nilpotent of class at most
5
.
Furthermore:

(iii)

If
r=3
and
p=5
, then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
3
;

(iv)

If
r=3
and
p=2
, then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
4
;

(v)

If
4\leq r\leq 5
, then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
4
;

(vi)

If
r\geq 14
, then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
5
.

Moreover, these upper bounds are best possible.

Remark 1.4
.

For
6\leq r\leq 13
, we only know that the best upper bound for the class is
4
or
5
.

Finally, when we furthermore know that
G
is metabelian then we can get the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a powerful metabelian
3
-Engel
p
-group of rank
r\geq 3
.
If
p
is odd, then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
3
.
If
p=2
, then the nilpotency class of
G
is at most
4
.
Moreover, these upper bounds are best possible.

Remark 1.6
.

In Theorem
1.5
, if
p
is odd or if
r=3
, then the assumption that
G
is powerful is not necessary, as shown in Theorem
3.2
and Lemma
3.4
.

Notation and organisation.

Our notation is mostly standard.
For a group
G
and elements
x,y\in G
, the commutator of
x
and
y
is the element
[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy
.
We use left-normed notation for commutators of length
\geq 3
(for example,
[x,y,z]=[[x,y],z]
).
A commutator of length
n\in\mathbb{N}
in a subset
X\subseteq G
is a commutator of the form
[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
.
We denote the derive subgroup of
G
by
G^{\prime}
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we will recall and develop some useful tools about commutators in 3-Engel groups.
In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.5
which gives a bound for the nilpotency class of a powerful metabelian 3-Engel group. In Section
4
we establish Theorem
1.3
(i), (iii) and (iv) for powerful
3
-Engel groups of rank
3
.
Finally, in Section
5
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
and, as a consequence, the description of the nilpotency class of powerful 3-Engel groups.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, the following identities due to Gupta and Newman
[
3
]
will have a prominent role.

Lemma 2.1

(Gupta and Newman
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a
3
-Engel group and let
a,b,c\in G
.
Then, modulo
\gamma_{5}(G)
, we have the following relations:

(i)

[c,a,a,b][c,a,b,a][c,b,a,a][c,a,b,b][c,b,a,b][c,b,b,a]\equiv 1
;

(ii)

[c,a,a,b]^{2}[c,a,b,a]^{2}[c,b,a,a]^{2}\equiv 1
;

(iii)

[b,a,a,c]\equiv[c,b,a,a]^{-3}[c,a,a,b]^{-3}
;

(iv)

[c,a,a,b]^{4}[c,b,a,a]^{6}\equiv 1
;

(v)

[c,b,a,a]^{2}\equiv[c,a,b,a]^{4}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Gupta and Newman
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a 3-Engel group and let
a,b,c\in G
.
Let also
w_{1}=[c,a,a,b,b]
,
w_{2}=[c,a,b,a,b]
,
w_{3}=[c,a,b,b,a]
,
w_{4}=[c,b,a,a,b]
,

w_{5}=[c,b,a,b,a]
and
w_{6}=[c,b,b,a,a]
.
Then, modulo
\gamma_{6}(G)
, we have the following relations:

(i)

[c,a,b,a,a]\equiv 1
;

(ii)

[c,a,a,b,a]\equiv 1
;

(iii)

w_{3}\equiv w_{4}\equiv w_{1}^{-1}w_{2}^{-1}
,
w_{2}\equiv w_{5}
,
w_{1}\equiv w_{6}
;

(iv)

[b,a,a,b,c]\equiv 1
;

(v)

w_{2}\equiv w_{1}^{3}
,
w_{3}\equiv w_{1}^{-4}
;

(vi)

w_{1}^{10}\equiv 1
.

The following well-known lemma will be useful throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a group, and let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y\in G
.
Then,

[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y]\in\langle[y,x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}]\mid\sigma\in S_{n}\rangle\gamma_{n+2}(G),

where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group of degree
n
.

3.
Powerful
3
-Engel metabelian groups

This section is devoted to study powerful
3
-Engel metabelian groups.
We start by proving our first main result for
n
-Engel groups with cyclic derived subgroups.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
G=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\rangle
and
G^{\prime}=\langle y\rangle
, where
r\geq 2
.
For
1\leq i<j\leq r
and
1\leq k\leq r
, write

(1)

[x_{i},x_{j}]=y^{p^{\gamma(i,j)}\delta(i,j)}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ [y,x_{k}]=y^{p^{\alpha(k)}\beta(k)},

where
\gamma(i,j),\alpha(k)\geq 0
and
p
does not divide
\delta(i,j)
and
\beta(k)
.
We assume
\gamma_{n+2}(G)=1
, and claim that

[x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n+1}}]=1

for any
1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{n+1}\leq r
.
The relations in (
1
) give

(2)

[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{n+1}}]=y^{p^{\gamma(i_{1},i_{2})+\alpha(i_{3})+\dots+\alpha(i_{n+1})}\delta(i_{1},i_{2})\beta(i_{3})\cdots\beta(i_{n+1})}.

Let
1\leq j\leq n
be such that
\alpha(i_{j})=\min\{\alpha(i_{1}),\alpha(i_{2}),\dots,\alpha(i_{n+1})\}
.
We will distinguish two cases in turn:
j\in\{1,2\}
and
j\geq 3
.

Suppose first
j=1
. Since the group
G
is
n
-Engel, it follows that

1=[x_{i_{2}},x_{i_{1}},\overset{n}{\ldots},x_{i_{1}}]=y^{-p^{\gamma(i_{1},i_{2})+(n-1)\alpha(i_{1})}\delta(i_{1},i_{2})\beta(i_{1})^{n-1}},

so that
y^{p^{\gamma(i_{1},i_{2})+(n-1)\alpha(i_{1})}}=1
.
Since
(n-1)\alpha(i_{1})\leq\alpha(i_{3})+\cdots+\alpha(i_{n+1})
, we deduce that
[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{n+1}}]=1
, as claimed. If
j=2
, then
[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{n+1}}]=[x_{i_{2}},x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{n+1}}]^{-1}
, which follows from the case
j=1
.

Now, suppose
j\geq 3
. It immediately follows from (
2
) that

(3)

[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{n+1}}]=[x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},x_{i_{\sigma(1)+2}},\dots,x_{i_{\sigma(n-1)+2}}]

for any
\sigma\in S_{n-1}
, so we may further assume that
j=3
.
Now, since
\gamma_{n+2}(G)=1
, the Hall-Witt identity gives

[x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},x_{i_{3}},\ldots,x_{i_{n+1}}]=[x_{i_{3}},x_{i_{2}},x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{n+1}}][x_{i_{3}},x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{n+1}}]^{-1},

and the result follows from the previous case.

This bound cannot be further sharpened due to Example
3.1
below.
∎

Example 3.1
.

Let
p
be either an odd prime or
4
, and define
G=\langle a,b\mid[a,b]=a^{p},a^{p^{n}}=b^{p^{n-1}}=1\rangle
.
Then
G
is a powerful
n
-Engel group with cyclic derived subgroup of nilpotency class
n
.

If the derived subgroup of our
3
-Engel group
G
is not cyclic, then the search of best upper bounds for the nilpotency class of
G
is much more involved.
However, the following result due to Gupta and Newman gives some insights for the structure of
G
when
G^{\prime}
is abelian.

Theorem 3.2

(Gupta and Newman
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a metabelian
3
-Engel group.
Then we have

\gamma_{4}(G)^{16}=\gamma_{5}(G)^{2}=1.

As a direct consequence of this theorem, if
G
is a
3
-Engel metabelian
p
-group, then it is nilpotent of class at most
3
unless
p=2
.
This bound is clearly best possible, as all groups of nilpotency class
3
are
3
-Engel.
The study of the nilpotency class of metabelian
3
-Engel powerful groups will be then reduced to the case
p=2
.
In such a case, we have

\gamma_{6}(G)=[\gamma_{5}(G),G]\leq\gamma_{5}(G)^{4}=1,

where the last inequality holds by Shalev’s Interchange Lemma
[
8
]
. Therefore, the nilpotency class of
G
can be at most 5.
As we show in Theorem
1.5
, this bound can be further improved.
Before proving it, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a metabelian
3
-Engel
2
-group.
Then for all
a,b,c,d\in G
we have

[a,b,c,d]^{2}[a,c,b,d]^{2}[a,d,c,b]^{2}\in\gamma_{5}(G).

Proof.

We may assume
\gamma_{5}(G)=1
.
Then, Lemma
2.1
(i) yields

1=[a,bc,bc,d]^{2}[a,d,bc,bc][a,bc,d,d][a,d,d,bc]^{2}.

Expanding the commutators and applying Lemma
2.1
(i) again, we obtain

\begin{split}1=&[a,b,b,d]^{2}[a,d,b,b][a,b,d,d][a,d,d,b]^{2}[a,c,c,d]^{2}[a,d,c,c][a,c,d,d][a,d,d,c]^{2}\\
&[a,b,c,d]^{2}[a,c,b,d]^{2}[a,d,b,c][a,d,c,b]=[a,b,c,d]^{2}[a,c,b,d]^{2}[a,d,c,b]^{2},\end{split}

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By Theorem
3.2
, we only need to show the result for
p=2
.
Therefore, since
G
is powerful, we can find generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
of
G
such that
G^{\prime}=\langle x_{1}^{4\alpha_{1}},\dots,x_{k}^{4\alpha_{k}}\rangle
, with
1\leq k\leq n
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Also, we can assume
k>1
by Theorem
1.1
.
Thus, it suffices to show that
[x_{i}^{4\alpha_{i}},a,b,c]=1
for all
a,b,c,\in G
.
For that purpose, we may assume
\gamma_{5}(G)^{2}=\gamma_{6}(G)=1
, so by the Hall-Petresco identity, we obtain

[x_{i}^{4\alpha_{i}},a,b,c]=[x_{i},a,b,c]^{4\alpha_{i}}

for all
1\leq i\leq k
.
Now, from Lemma
3.3
, we deduce

\displaystyle[x_{i},a,b,c]^{4\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=([a,x_{i},b,c]^{2})^{-2\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=([a,b,x_{i},c]^{-2}[a,c,b,x_{i}]^{-2}g)^{-2\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=[a,b,x_{i},c]^{4\alpha_{i}}[a,c,b,x_{i}]^{4\alpha_{i}}

for some
g\in\gamma_{5}(G)
.
Since, again by the Hall-Petresco identity,

[a,b,x_{i},c]^{4\alpha_{i}}[a,c,b,x_{i}]^{4\alpha_{i}}=[a,b,x_{i}^{4\alpha_{i}},c][a,c,b,x_{i}^{4\alpha_{i}}]=1,

the theorem follows.
∎

As pointed out in Remark
1.6
, we now show that the upper bound obtained for the nilpotency class of powerful
3
-Engel metabelian
2
-groups remains the same when the rank of
G
is
3
, even if we relax the condition that
G
is powerful.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G=\langle a,b,c\rangle
be a metabelian 3-Engel 2-group.
Then
\gamma_{5}(G)=1
.

Proof.

We are going to show that all commutators of length 5 in
\{a,b,c\}
are trivial. Since
G
is
3
-Engel, Lemma
2.2
already shows that any commutator with a triple entry is trivial.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
, and since
G
is metabelian, it remains to show that commutators of the form
[x,y,y,z,z]
with
x,y,z\in\{a,b,c\}
are trivial.
Since
[x,y,y,z,z]=[x,y,z,z,y]
, and, additionally, by Lemma
2.2
, we have
[x,y,z,z,y]=[x,y,y,z,z]^{-4}
, it follows that
[x,y,y,z,z]^{5}=1
.
Since
G
is a
2
-group, the result follows.
∎

We end this section by noting that this bound is best possible, as the following example shows.

Example 3.5
.

Let
p=2
and let
G=\langle a,b,c\rangle
be a group with the following presentation:

[a,b]=a^{-p^{5}}
,

[a,c]=b^{3p^{6}}a^{p^{4}}
,

[b,c]=b^{p^{7}}a^{p^{2}}
,

[a^{p^{2}},b^{p^{6}}]=1
,

[a^{p^{2}},b]=a^{-p^{7}}
,

[a^{p^{2}},c]=a^{-p^{2}}b^{3p^{8}}a^{17p^{2}}
,

[b^{p^{6}},a]=a^{p^{11}}
,

[b^{p^{6}},c]=a^{p^{8}}
,

a^{p^{12}}=1
,

b^{p^{11}}=1
,

c^{p^{10}}=1
.

Then,
G
is a powerful
3
-Engel
2
-group of nilpotency class
4
.

4.
Powerful
3
-Engel groups of rank
3

The aim of this section is proving Theorem
1.3
(i), (iii) and (iv).
For that purpose, let
G
be a

(4)

\text{powerful }3\text{-Engel }p\text{-group such that }\left\{\begin{aligned} \gamma_{5}(G)=\gamma_{4}(G)^{p}=1&\text{ if }p\neq 2\\
\gamma_{6}(G)=\gamma_{5}(G)^{2}=1&\text{ if }p=2.\end{aligned}\right.

We will fix for the rest of the section
a,b,c\in G
and
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
i\leq j\leq k
such that
G=\langle a,b,c\rangle
and
G^{\prime}=\langle a^{p^{i}},b^{p^{j}},c^{p^{k}}\rangle
.
Also, since
G
is powerful, let
r,s,t,u,v,w,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that

(5)

[a,b]=a^{rp^{i}}b^{sp^{j}}c^{tp^{k}},\quad[c,a]=a^{up^{i}}b^{vp^{j}}c^{wp^{k}},\quad[c,b]=a^{\alpha p^{i}}b^{\beta p^{j}}c^{\gamma p^{k}}.

The following remark will be frequently used.

Remark 4.1
.

Suppose that
\gamma_{n}(G)^{p}=\gamma_{n+1}(G)=1
for some
n\geq 2
.
If
x^{p^{i}}\in G^{\prime}
for some
x\in G
and
i\geq 0
, then

1=[x^{p^{i}},y_{1},{\ldots},y_{n-2}]^{p}=[x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-2}]^{p^{i+1}}

for all
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-2}\in G
.

First we will establish Theorem
1.3
(i) and (iii), i.e., we will show that for
G
as in (
4
) with
p\neq 2
, we have
\gamma_{4}(G)=1
.
For that purpose, we first need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be as in (
4
) with
p\neq 2
.
If
i<j\leq k
, then
\gamma_{4}(G)=1
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
, commutators of length
4
in
\{a,b,c\}
with only two different entries are all trivial.
We focus, then, on commutators of length
4
in
\{a,b,c\}
with
3
different entries.

Observe that

[x,y,a,z]=[a^{\sigma_{1}p^{i}}b^{\sigma_{2}p^{j}}c^{\sigma_{3}p^{k}},a,z]=[a,b,z]^{-\sigma_{2}p^{j}}[a,c,z]^{-\sigma_{3}p^{k}}

for every
x,y,z\in G
, where
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
i<j,k
and
n=4
, it follows from Remark
4.1
that
[x,y,a,z]=1
, so that all commutators of length
4
with
a
in the third entry are trivial.
In particular,
[c,b,a,b]
,
[c,b,a,a]
, and
[b,c,a,c]
are trivial, and so, from (iv) and (v) of Lemma
2.1
, it follows that all the possible combinations with
a,b
and
c
produce trivial commutators, as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be as in (
4
) with
p\neq 2
.
If
i=j<k
, then
\gamma_{4}(G)=1
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
, commutators of length
4
in
\{a,b,c\}
with only two different entries are all trivial.

Let us suppose first that all commutators of length
4
in
\{a,b,c\}
with exactly one entry in
c
are trivial.
Then, by (iv) and (v) of Lemma
2.1
, we only need to show that one of
[c,a,b,c]
,
[c,b,a,c]
or
[a,b,c,c]
is trivial.
Following the notation in (
5
), we have

(6)

\displaystyle[c,a,b,c]

\displaystyle=[a,b,c]^{up^{i}}[b,c,c]^{-wp^{k}}=[a,b,c]^{up^{i}},

(7)

\displaystyle[c,b,a,c]

\displaystyle=[b,a,c]^{\beta p^{i}}[a,c,c]^{-\gamma p^{k}}=[b,a,c]^{\beta p^{i}},

(8)

\displaystyle[a,b,c,c]

Feasible route to high-temperature ambient-pressure hydride superconductivity
Kapildeb Dolui, Lewis J. Conway, Christoph Heil, Timothy A. Strobel, Rohit Prasankumar, Chris J. Pickard

Abstract
A key challenge in materials discovery is to find a viable high temperature superconductor. Hydrogen and hydride materials have long been considered promising materials displaying conventional phonon-mediated superconductivity. However, the high pressures required to stabilise these materials have restricted their application. Here, we present results from high-throughput computation, considering a wide range of high-symmetry ternary hydrides from across the periodic table at ambient-pressure. This large composition space is then reduced by considerations of thermodynamic, dynamic and magnetic stability, before direct estimations of the superconducting critical temperature. This approach, combined with first-principles structure prediction has revealed a metastable ambient-pressure hydride superconductor, Mg 2 IrH 6 , with a predicted critical temperature of 160 K, comparable to the highest temperature superconducting cuprates. We propose a synthesis route via a structurally related insulator, Mg 2 IrH 7 , which is thermodynamically stable above 15 GPa and discuss the potential challenges in doing so. This supports a long-held hypothesis that, hydrogen, in the right chemical environment, can promote high-temperature superconductivity at ambient pressure.

The aim of computational approaches is to identify crystal structures possessing a high
T_{\rm c}
s with thermodynamic and dynamic stability.

Thermodynamic
stability indicates a resilience to transform into competing phases or decomposition into constituent species.
This is calculated by Maxwell construction; how far a structure is from the convex hull formed of free energy and composition coordinates.
A structure on the convex hull is considered thermodynamically stable.
A structure close to the convex hull can be described as metastable.

Dynamic
stability indicates a resilience to small fluctuations in atomic positions and is determined through phonon dispersion calculations.
A structure with no ‘imaginary’ (or zero-frequency) phonon modes is considered dynamically stable.

Most of the predicted high-
T_{\rm c}
hydride structures are stabilized at extremely high pressures (typically over 100 GPa)

However, most theoretically predicted hydrides have not yet been synthesized.
This may in part be because many of these predicted structures are metastable.
Since there are significantly more crystal structures off the convex hull than on it, it is to be expected that there are a multitude of energetically plausible metastable high-
T_{\rm c}
hydrides.

Many functional materials are in fact ‘metastable’ states obtained through a well-designed synthesis pathway. Indeed, a recent survey of the Inorganic Crystal Structure Database found that half of all registered structures are metastable

Given the large number of predicted high-
T_{\rm c}
hydrides, it is difficult to decide which of these are worth pursuing experimentally. This situation could improve if increased emphasis was placed on the feasibility of synthetic pathways when proposing new metastable structures.

Although most of the synthesized superconducting hydrides are only stable at impractically high pressures, synthesis routes exploiting high pressures may be viable to obtain metastable, ambient-pressure superconducting hydrides.
A structure may form at high pressure where it is thermodynamically stable and remain in a metastable state on recovery to lower pressures due to its dynamic stability and resilience to kinetic barriers.
Several ternary hydrides structures such as those with clathrate hydrogen structures, have recently been predicted to have

In this Letter, we have presented a promising result from a wide search for hydrides exhibiting phonon-mediated BCS superconductivity. The tendency for electrons to form Cooper pairs — the mechanism behind BCS superconductivity — must compete with other instabilities resulting from electron–electron and electron–phonon coupling; magnetism, charge-density waves, disorder and structural distortions.
By employing
AIRSS
across the periodic table, high-throughput calculations and DFPT, we have filtered a wide compositional and structural space (albeit a high-symmetry subspace) to extract candidates with only the Cooper pair instability.
In doing so, we have predicted the existence of cubic Mg
2
IrH
6
, an ambient-pressure, phonon-mediated, superconducting hydride with a
T_{\rm c}
of about 160 K.
We demonstrate that there may exist a viable high-pressure synthesis route
via
an intermediate cubic Mg
2
IrH
7
phase, thermodynamically stable above 15 GPa. The extraction of non-bonded, interstitial hydrogen from Mg
2
IrH
7
represents a plausible route to create superconducting Mg
2
IrH
6
, although kinetic barriers between competing phases warrant careful consideration.

By studying the electronic structure of cubic Mg
2
IrH
6
, we suggest there may be a route to increase either the thermodynamic stability or
T_{\rm c}
by doping this prototype crystal structure. This work demonstrates the feasibility of high-
T_{\rm c}
hydrides at ambient-pressure and reflects a positive outlook for hydride superconductivity.

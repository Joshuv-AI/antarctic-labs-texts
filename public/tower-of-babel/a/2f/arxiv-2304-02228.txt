Galerkin-Koornwinder approximations of delay differential equations for physicists
Mickaël D. Chekroun, Honghu Liu

Abstract
Formulas for Galerkin-Koornwinder (GK) approximations of delay differential equations are summarized. The functional analysis ingredients (semigroups, operators, etc.) are intentionally omitted to focus instead on the formulas required to perform GK approximations in practice.



I
Galerkin-Koornwinder (GK) approximations of DDEs

We consider nonlinear
scalar
differential delay equations (DDEs) of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x(t)}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=ax(t)+bx(t-\tau)+c\int_{t-\tau}^{t}x(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

(I.1)

\displaystyle+F\Big(x(t),x(t-\tau),\int_{t-\tau}^{t}x(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big),

where
a
,
b
and
c
are real numbers,
\tau>0
is the delay parameter, and
F

is a nonlinear function.
We restrict ourselves to the scalar case to simplify the maths, but the approach extends
to systems of nonlinear DDEs involving possibly several delays as detailed in
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
)
and illustrated in
(
Chekroun et al. 2020a
)
for the cloud-rain delay model of
(
Koren and Feingold 2011
)
.

I.1
Koornwinder polynomials

First, let us recall that Koornwinder polynomials
K_{n}
are obtained from
Legendre polynomials
L_{n}
, for any nonnegative integer
n
, according to the relation

\displaystyle

\displaystyle K_{n}(s)=-(1+s)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}L_{n}(s)+(n^{2}+n+1)L_{n}(s),

(I.2)

\displaystyle s\in[-1,1];

see
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
, Eq. (3.3))
.

Koornwinder polynomials are known to form an orthogonal set for the following weighted inner product on
[-1,1]
with a point-mass,
\mu(\,\mathrm{d}x)=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta_{1},

where
\delta_{1}
denotes the Dirac point-mass at the right endpoint
x=1
; see
Koornwinder 1984
. In other words, the following orthogonality property holds:

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}K_{n}(s)K_{p}(s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}K_{n}(s)K_{p}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+K_{n}(1)K_{p}(1)

(I.3)

\displaystyle=0,\,\mbox{ if }p\neq n.

It is also worthwhile noting that Koornwinder polynomials augmented by the right endpoint values as follows

\mathcal{K}_{n}=(K_{n},K_{n}(1)),

(I.4)

form an orthogonal basis of the product space

\mathcal{E}=L^{2}([-1,1);\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R},

endowed with the inner product:

\Big\langle(f,a),(g,b)\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}f(s)g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+ab.

(I.5)

The norm induced by this inner product is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
. The basis function
\mathcal{K}_{n}
has then its
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
-norm given by
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
, Prop. 3.1)
:

\|\mathcal{K}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{E}}=\sqrt{\frac{(n^{2}+1)((n+1)^{2}+1)}{2n+1}}.

(I.6)

This is a useful property for calculating the GK approximations, as it will come apparent below.

Finally, since the original Koornwinder basis is given on the interval
[-1,1]
and the state space of a DDE such as Eq. (
I.1
) is made of functions defined on
(-\tau,0)
, we will work mainly with the following rescaled version of Koornwinder polynomials.
The rescaled Koornwinder polynomials
K_{n}^{\tau}
are defined as follows

\displaystyle K^{\tau}_{n}\colon[-\tau,0]

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

(I.7)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\mapsto K_{n}\Bigl(1+\frac{2\theta}{\tau}\Bigr).

They form orthogonal polynomials on the interval
[-\tau,0]
for the
L^{2}
-inner product on
(-\tau,0)
with a Dirac point-mass at the right endpoint
0
.

The following family of rescaled Koornwinder polynomials augmented with a right endpoint value,

\mathcal{K}_{n}^{\tau}=(K_{n}^{\tau},K_{n}^{\tau}(0)),

(I.8)

forms then an orthogonal basis for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}([-\tau,0);\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}
endowed with the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
given by (
I.5
) in which the integral is taken on
(-\tau,0)
and weighted by
1/\tau
. Note that since
K_{n}(1)=1

(
Chekroun et al. 2016
, Prop. 3.1)
, we have also

K_{n}^{\tau}(0)=1.

(I.9)

Finally, observe that
\|\mathcal{K}_{n}^{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\|\mathcal{K}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{E}}
.

I.2
Space-time representation and GK approximations

Unlike ordinary differential equations (ODEs), a DDE such as Eq. (
I.1
) is an infinite-dimensional dynamical system in which a history segment has to be specified over the interval
[-\tau,0)
to apprehend properly the existence and computation problems of its solutions.

Thus, given a function of time,
x
, solving Eq. (
I.1
) one distinguishes between the
history segment

\{x(t+\theta):\theta\in[-\tau,0)\}
and the
current state
,
x(t)
. Denoting by
u(t,\theta)
the history segment, one can then rewrite the DDE (
I.1
) as the transport equation

\partial_{t}u=\partial_{\theta}u,\quad-\tau\leq\theta<0,

(I.10)

subject to the
nonlocal and nonlinear boundary condition

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}u|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=au(t,0)+bu(t,-\tau)+c\int_{t-\tau}^{t}u(s,0)\,\mathrm{d}s

(I.11)

\displaystyle+F\Big(u(t,0),u(t,-\tau),\int_{t-\tau}^{t}u(s,0)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big),

for
t\geq 0
.
This reformulation is often called the space-time representation in the literature; see Fig.
1
for an illustration.

Figure 1
:
Panel A shows a solution
u(t,\theta)
to a transport problem associated with a DDE. In this example, the boundary value
u(t,0)
at
\theta=0
is shown in panel B as the time series
H(t)
(from the Supplementary Material of
(
Chekroun et al. 2022
)
). This time series at the right endpoint
\theta=0
, coincides, in the deterministic setting, with the time series
x(t)
obtained by solving the original DDE.

Using rescaled Koornwinder polynomials (
I.7
), one can then seek for approximations of
u(t,\theta)
solving Eqns. (
I.10
)-(
I.11
) under the form

u_{N}(t,\theta)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}y_{j}(t)K_{j}^{\tau}(\theta).

(I.12)

Given the property (
I.9
), the current state
x(t)=u(t,0)
is approximated
by

x_{N}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}y_{j}(t).

(I.13)

Note that in (
I.12
) the Koornwinder polynomials are indexed according to their degree
j
.

The question arises then of determining the equations that the coefficients
y_{j}(t)
in (
I.12
) must satisfy in order to have that
u_{N}(t,\theta)
provides a rigorous approximation that converges to
u(t,\theta)
as
N\rightarrow\infty.

This nontrivial problem has been solved in
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
)
. The next section recalls the structure of these equations forming what we call a GK system.

I.3
The GK approach in action

The analysis of
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
)
shows that
y_{j}(t)
solving the
N
-dimensional system of ODEs (
I.14
) provides a rigorous method for approximating the solutions to a broad class of DDEs.
Such systems are given by:

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}y_{j}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{K}_{j}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\bigg(a+bK_{n}(-1)+c\tau(2\delta_{n,0}-1)

(I.14)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}\left(\delta_{j,k}\|\mathcal{K}_{j}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}-1\right)\bigg)y_{n}(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{K}_{j}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}F\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}y_{n}(t),\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}y_{n}(t)K_{n}(-1),\tau y_{0}(t)-\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}y_{n}(t)\right),

\displaystyle 0\leq j\leq N-1.

Here the Kronecker symbol
\delta_{j,k}
has been used, and the coefficients
a_{n,k}
are obtained by solving a triangular linear system in which the right-hand side has
explicit coefficients depending on
n

(
Chekroun et al. 2016
, Prop. 5.1)
; see Appendix
A
.
The values of the
K_{n}(-1)
’s are known and recalled in (
A.1
) below.

We rewrite now the GK system (
I.14
) into the following compact form:

\boxed{\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=A_{N}(\tau)\bm{y}+F_{N}(\bm{y},\tau),}

(I.15)

where
\bm{y}=(y_{0},\cdots,y_{N-1})^{T}
,
and in which the
A_{N}(\tau)\bm{y}
-term and
F_{N}(\bm{y},\tau)
-term group the linear and nonlinear terms in Eq. (
I.14
), respectively.
The entries of the
N\times N
matrix
A_{N}(\tau)
are given by

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \Big(A_{N}(\tau)\Big)_{i,j}&=\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{K}_{i}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}\Big(a+bK_{j}(-1)+c\tau(2\delta_{j,0}-1)\\
&\hskip 80.00012pt+\frac{2}{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}a_{j,k}\left(\delta_{i,k}\|\mathcal{K}_{i}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}-1\right)\Big),\;\;i,j=0,\cdots,N-1.\end{aligned}}

(I.16)

Here, the coefficients
a_{j,k}
are independent of the DDE model and are determined by
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
, Prop. 5.1)
; see Proposition
A.1
below.
Re-writing
A_{N}(\tau)
as
A_{N}(\tau)=2/\tau P_{N}+Q_{N}
, one observes that only
Q_{N}
depends on the model’s parameters. More generally, the matrix
A_{N}(\tau)
accounts for the approximation of the transport equation
\partial_{t}u=\partial_{\theta}u
and the contribution of the linear terms arising in the nonlocal boundary condition (
I.11
); see
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
)
.

The nonlinear mapping
F_{N}
has its components
F_{N}^{j}
given by

\boxed{F_{N}^{j}(\bm{y},\tau)=\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{K}_{j}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}F\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}y_{n}(t),\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}y_{n}(t)K_{n}(-1),\tau y_{0}(t)-\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}y_{n}(t)\right),}

(I.17)

for each
0\leq j\leq N-1
.

Due to rigorous convergence results of GK approximations
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
)
, the GK formulas above provide a powerful apparatus to analyze DDEs by means of ODE approximations.

I.4
Example: GK approximations of the Suarez and Schopf model

The Suarez and Schopf model is given by the following DDE
(
Suarez and Schopf 1988
)

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}T}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=T(t)-\alpha T(t-\tau)-T^{3}(t),

(I.18)

where
\tau
and
\alpha
are positive constants; and the physically relevant range of
\alpha
used in
(
Suarez and Schopf 1988
)
is
(0,1)
. We refer to
(
Suarez and Schopf 1988
)
for the physical interpretation of this model in relationship with the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) phenomenon, and to
(
Chekroun et al. 2018a
)
and references therein for other ENSO models with delays.

It is clear that for this given range of
\alpha
, Eq. (
I.18
) admits three fixed points:

T_{0}=0,\qquad T_{+}=\sqrt{1-\alpha},\qquad T_{-}=-\sqrt{1-\alpha}.

Let us first introduce the perturbed variable

x=T-T_{+}.

(I.19)

Equation (
I.18
), when written in the perturbed variable
x
, reads

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}x}{\,\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=(1-3T_{+}^{2})x(t)

(I.20)

\displaystyle-\alpha x(t-\tau)-3T_{+}x^{2}(t)-x^{3}(t).

The above equation fits into Eq. (
I.1
) with

\displaystyle

\displaystyle a=1-3T_{+}^{2},\quad b=-\alpha,\quad c=0,

(I.21)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad F(x(t))=-3T_{+}x^{2}(t)-x^{3}(t).

The corresponding
N
-dimensional GK approximation can then be obtained by using Eqns. (
I.15
)–(
I.17
), and it reads

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=A(\tau)\bm{y}+G(\bm{y}),

(I.22)

dropping the dependence on
N
.

The entries of the matrix
A(\tau)
are given by

\displaystyle(A(\tau))_{j,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{K}_{j}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\Bigl(1-3T_{+}^{2}-\alpha K_{n}(-1)

(I.23)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}\left(\delta_{j,k}\|\mathcal{K}_{j}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}-1\right)\Bigr),

for
j,n=0,\cdots,N-1
. The
a_{n,k}
are determined thanks to Proposition
A.1
.

The nonlinearity
G
is given as the following sum of
N
-dimensional mappings

G(\bm{y})=G_{2}(\bm{y})+G_{3}(\bm{y}),

with

\displaystyle G_{2}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle=-3T_{+}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}y_{n}\right)^{2}\bm{\nu}_{N},

\displaystyle G_{3}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle=-\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}y_{n}\right)^{3}\bm{\nu}_{N},

where
\bm{\nu}_{N}
denotes the
N
-dimensional column vector

\bm{\nu}_{N}=\Big(\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{K}_{0}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}},\cdots,\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{K}_{N-1}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}\Big)^{T}.

(I.24)

Note that here only the linear terms in the GK-approximations depend on
\tau
as the DDE model (
I.20
) contains delay terms only in its linear ones. If delay terms would be present in the nonlinear terms as well then
G
would depend on
\tau
according to (
I.17
).

We set
\alpha=0.75
. The steady state is then given by
T_{+}=\sqrt{1-\alpha}=0.5
.
Setting
N=6
, the 6-D GK approximation system of the form (
I.22
) reads here as:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\bm{y}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}=\left(M_{1}+\frac{1}{\tau}M_{2}\right)\bm{y}

(I.25)

\displaystyle-\left(\sum_{n=1}^{6}y_{n}\right)^{2}\left(3T_{+}+\sum_{n=1}^{6}y_{n}\right)\bm{\nu}_{6},

where
\bm{y}=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{6})^{T}
,
\bm{\nu}_{6}
is given by (
I.24
) with
N=6
, and the matrix
A(\tau)
in (
I.22
) is given by
M_{1}+\frac{1}{\tau}M_{2}
with

M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-0.25&1.25&-2.5&5&-7.75&11.75\\
-0.15&0.75&-1.5&3&-4.65&7.05\\
-0.05&0.25&-0.5&1&-1.55&2.35\\
-0.0206&0.1029&-0.2059&0.4118&-0.6382&0.9676\\
-0.0102&0.0509&-0.1018&0.2036&-0.3156&0.4785\\
-0.0057&0.0286&-0.0572&0.1143&-0.1772&0.2687\end{pmatrix},

(I.26)

M_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&2&-3&7&-10&16\\
0&-1.2&7.8&-10.2&20.4&-26.4\\
0&-0.4&-2.4&11.6&-12.2&24.2\\
0&-0.1647&-0.9882&-3.4588&14.7412&-13.0941\\
0&-0.0814&-0.4887&-1.7104&-4.4796&17.7557\\
0&-0.0457&-0.2744&-0.9605&-2.5156&-5.4886\end{pmatrix}.

(I.27)

We refer to Appendix
A
for the computation of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}.

Numerical results show that the GK system (
I.25
) with
N=6
exhibits already very good skills in approximating the solutions to the DDE model (
I.20
); see Fig. 2 in Supplementary Material of
(
Chekroun et al. 2020b
)
. In general, a few Koornwinder polynomials are needed to capture the dynamics in absence of time-dependent forcings; see also Fig. 4 in
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
)
and
(
Chekroun et al. 2018b
, Sec. 4.3.3)
.

Appendix A
Determination of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
in Eq. (
I.25
)

The calculations of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are based on the RHS of (
I.23
). First, the terms

\frac{1}{\|\mathcal{K}_{j}\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(1-3T_{+}^{2}-\alpha K_{n}(-1)),

when collected for each
j
and
n
allow us to build up
M_{1}
readily once one recalls (
I.6
) and that due to (
I.2
),

K_{n}(-1)=(n^{2}+n+1)(-1)^{n},

(A.1)

since
L_{n}(-1)=(-1)^{n}
from the properties of the Legendre polynomials.

The determination of
M_{2}
which results from the collection of the terms
2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}\left(\delta_{j,k}\|\mathcal{K}_{j}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}-1\right)
, requires the knowledge of the coefficients
a_{n,k}
.
These coefficients arise in the expression of the derivatives of the Koornwinder polynomials in terms of these polynomials themselves; see
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
, Appendix B)
for more details.

The coefficients
a_{n,k}
are then obtained by simply solving the triangular system (
A.3
) described in the following proposition.

Proposition A.1
.

The Koornwinder polynomial
K_{n}
of degree
n
defined in (
I.2
) satisfies the differential relation

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}K_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}K_{k}(s),\quad s\in(-1,1),

(A.2)

where the vector
\bm{a}_{n}
made of the
a_{n,k}
-coefficients,

\bm{a}_{n}=(a_{n,0},\cdots,a_{n,n-1})^{T},

solves the following upper triangular system

\mathbf{T}\bm{a}_{n}=\bm{b}_{n},

(A.3)

with
\mathbf{T}=(\mathbf{T}_{i,j})_{n\times n}
and
\bm{b}_{n}=(b_{n,0},\cdots,b_{n,n-1})^{T}
given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\;\text{ if }j<i,\\
i^{2}+1,&\;\text{ if }j=i,\\
-(2i+1),&\;\text{ if }j>i,\end{cases}\;\quad\text{ where }\quad 0\leq i,j\leq n-1,

(A.4)

\displaystyle b_{n,i}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}(2i+1)(n+i+1)(n-i),&\text{ if $n+i$ is even},\\
(n^{2}+n)(2i+1)-\frac{i}{2}(n+i)(n-i+1)&\\
\hskip 20.00003pt-\frac{1}{2}(i+1)(n-i-1)(n+i+2),&\text{ if $n+i$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Finally, the rescaled Koornwinder polynomials satisfy

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}K^{\tau}_{n}}{\,\mathrm{d}\theta}(\theta)=\frac{2}{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{n,k}K^{\tau}_{k}(\theta),\quad\theta\in(-\tau,0).

(A.5)

Remark A.1
.

We note that the formula for
b_{n,i}
that appeared in
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
)
and in
(
Chekroun et al. 2020a
)
contains two typos (a sign error and an extra factor
i
in one of the terms) that are here rectified in the formula (
A.4
). We would like to express our gratitude to Tetsushi Saburi for pointing out these typos to us. We emphasize though that it is only a typo as the correct formulas were implemented in our codes, thus not questioning the diverse numerical results published in
(
Chekroun et al. 2016
)
,
(
Chekroun et al. 2018b
)
,
(
Chekroun et al. 2020a
)
,
(
Chekroun et al. 2022
)
, and in the Supplementary Material of
(
Chekroun et al. 2020b
)
.

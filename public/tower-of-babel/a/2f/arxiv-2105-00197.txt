skew-product dynamical systems for crossed product C ∗ C^{*} -algebras and their ergodic properties
Simone Del Vecchio, Francesco Fidaleo, Stefano Rossi

Abstract
. Starting from a discrete C ∗ C^{*} -dynamical system ( 𝔄 , θ , ω o ) (\mathfrak{A},\theta,\omega_{o}) , we define and study most of the main ergodic properties of the crossed product C ∗ C^{*} -dynamical system ( 𝔄 ⋊ α ℤ , Φ θ , u , ω o ∘ E ) (\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega_{o}\circ E) , E : 𝔄 ⋊ α ℤ → 𝔄 E:\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{A}} being the canonical conditional expectation of 𝔄 ⋊ α ℤ \mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z} onto 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} , provided α ∈ Aut ( 𝔄 ) \alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}}) commute with the ∗ * -automorphism θ \theta up tu a unitary u ∈ 𝔄 u\in{\mathfrak{A}} . Here, Φ θ , u ∈ Aut ( 𝔄 ⋊ α ℤ ) \Phi_{\theta,u}\in\mathop{\rm Aut}(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}) can be considered as the fully noncommutative generalisation of the celebrated skew-product defined by H. Anzai for the product of two tori in the classical case.

1.
Introduction

Classical ergodic theory is a well-established topic whose early motivations came undoubtedly from an attempt to
give a sound foundation to statistical mechanics.
It is exactly in this spirit that the first ergodic theorems were proved.

Noncommutative ergodic theory is a decidedly younger area of research, whose motivation is more mathematical in character. However, when it comes to producing truly new examples, the noncommutative theory turns out to be much harder to deal with. On the contrary, in the classical theory it is not too difficult to come up with suitably chosen classes of dynamical systems featuring quite a wide spectrum of phenomena.

The so-called Anzai skew-products are certainly a case in point.
First introduced in
[
3
]
, and later dealt with in far more generality in
[
18
]
and called there
processes on the torus
, these are also known simply as skew-products, and are homeomorphisms defined on a product space of the type
X_{o}\times\mathbb{T}
, where
X_{o}
is a compact metrizable space and
\mathbb{T}
the one-dimensional torus.
Corresponding to any homeomorphism
\theta\in\textrm{Homeo}(X_{o})
and a continuous function

f\in C(X_{o};\mathbb{T})
, one can define a homeomorphism
\Phi_{\theta,f}
on the product
X_{o}\times\mathbb{T}
acting as
\Phi_{\theta,f}(x,z):=(\theta(x),f(x)z)
,
(x,z)\in X_{o}\times\mathbb{T}
.
If
\theta
is assumed to be uniquely ergodic, that is there exists only one probability
regular Borel measure (
i.e.
a Radon probability measure)
\mu_{o}
on
X_{o}
which is invariant under
\theta
, then the properties of
\Phi_{\theta,f}
can be analised
thoroughly. The behaviour of the skew-product
(X_{o}\times\mathbb{T},\Phi_{\theta,f})
is in fact ruled
by the so-called cohomological equations
g(\theta(x))f^{n}(x)=g(x)
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, in the unknown function

g
. More precisely,
the system will be ergodic with respect to the product measure
\mu:=\mu_{o}\times\lambda
, where
\lambda
is
the Lebesgue-Haar measure on the torus, if and only if, for any integer
n
different
from zero, the above equations only have null solutions.
Moreover, in this case the product measure is the only
invariant measure of the skew-product. Not having continuous solutions corresponds to what is usually referred to as
topological ergodicity, namely that the constant functions are the only continuous functions.
Anzai skew-product dynamical systems were then intensively studied for several applications to many branches of mathematics, and were used to produce examples of dynamical systems enjoying very special ergodic and spectral properties.

It is then natural to address the systematic investigation of the skew-product construction in a totally noncommutative setting. The first noncommutative version of the skew-product was defined in
[
24
]
in order to invesitigate the so-called Rokhlin property for the seminal example of the noncommutative 2-torus, whereas the main ergodic properties for such an example were recently investigated in
[
9
]
.

The primary aim of this paper is to extend the study initiated in
[
18
]
for the classical case and in
[
9
]
for the noncommutative torus, to the general situation relative to crossed products. In fact, the key-point is that the noncommutative torus can also be obtained as a crossed product
of
C(\mathbb{T})
by the discrete group
\mathbb{Z}
acting on the one-dimensional torus through a rotation
by
2\pi\alpha
. No wonder, crossed products by
\mathbb{Z}
provide a natural framework where the sought
generalization can be set. More precisely, the data we start from is a triple
(\mathfrak{A},\theta,\alpha)
, with
{\mathfrak{A}}
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
\theta,\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
are
*
-automorphisms of
{\mathfrak{A}}
.
In order to investigate the ergodic properties of the arising skew-product systems, we suppose that

(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
is a uniquely ergodic
C^{*}
-dynamical system whose unique invariant state is
\omega_{o}
. In order to construct the skew-product dynamical system on the
crossed product
C^{*}
-algebra, we need that the
*
-automorphism

\alpha

The output is the crossed product

The existence/non existence of continuous, or measurable non continuous solutions of the nontrivial solutions of (

We first recall that, concerning the classical situation when

In Section

5.
On the canonical cone associated to standard von Neumann algebras

Let
({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{H}})
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with a cyclic and separating vector
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
, that is a standard vector. A
standard von Neumann algebra
will be then a triplet
({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{H}},\xi)
made of a von Neumann algebra
({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{H}})
, and unit vector
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
which is standard for
{\mathcal{M}}
.

For the standard von Neumann algebra
({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{H}},\xi)
, since
\xi
is separating, for
a\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
a\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}\mapsto a^{*}\xi
uniquely defines a densely defined, closable anti-linear map

S_{o}(a\xi)=a^{*}\xi\,,\quad a\in{\mathcal{M}}\,,

whose closure, denoted by
S
, has polar decomposition
S=J\Delta^{1/2}
. The operators
S
,
\Delta
and
J
are called the
Tomita involution, modular operator and conjugation
, respectively. The main results of Tomita-Takesaki theory are the remarkable fact that
\mathop{\rm ad}_{\Delta^{\imath t}}
leaves
{\mathcal{M}}
globally invariant and therefore defines a one-parameter group of
*
-automorphisms of
{\mathcal{M}}
called
the modular group
, and that
{\mathcal{M}}
acts in standard form on
{\mathcal{H}}
with conjugation
J
:

(i)

J{\mathcal{M}}J={\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
,

(ii)

JzJ=z^{*}
,
z\in Z({\mathcal{M}})
.

A canonical self-dual cone
{\mathcal{P}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}
(
e.g.
Haa) can be defined as follows:

{\mathcal{P}}:=\overline{\{JaJa\xi\mid a\in{\mathcal{M}}\}}={\mathcal{P}}=\overline{\{\Delta^{1/4}a\xi\mid a\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}\}}\,.

For the standard von Neumann algebra
({\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{H}},\xi)
, we include the following result without proof (
cf.

[
26
]
, E.10.20) relative to the
polar decomposition
of any vector

\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the above notations, for each
\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}
we have
\eta=v_{\eta}|\eta|
, uniquely determined by the conditions:
|\eta|\in{\mathcal{P}}
,
v_{\eta}\in{\mathcal{M}}
and
v_{\eta}^{*}v_{\eta}=p_{|\eta|}
, the self-adjoint projection onto the closed subspace
[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}|\eta|]
.

We collect some results, which will come in useful in the next sections.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}
and

an isometry, we have
|v\eta|=|\eta|
.

Proof.

We have
v\eta=vv_{\eta}|\eta|
, and
(vv_{\eta})^{*}(vv_{\eta})=v_{\eta}^{*}v^{*}vv_{\eta}=v_{\eta}^{*}v_{\eta}=p_{|\eta|}
because
v
is an isometry. The assertion now follows by Proposition
5.1
.
∎

For the remaining part of the section, we consider

such that
u{\mathcal{M}}u^{*}={\mathcal{M}}
and
u\xi=\xi
.

Lemma 5.3

We have

.

Proof.

By
[

]
, Lemma 3.3,
[u,J]=0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle u{\mathcal{P}}\equiv u\overline{\{JaJa\xi\mid a\in{\mathcal{M}}\}}=

\displaystyle\overline{\{J(uau^{*})J(uau^{*})\xi\mid a\in{\mathcal{M}}\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\{JaJa\xi\mid a\in{\mathcal{M}}\}}={\mathcal{P}}\,.

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For
\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we have
u|\eta|=|u\eta|
.

Proof.

We have
u\eta=uv_{\eta}|\eta|=(uv_{\eta}u^{*})(u|\eta|)
. Since

by Lemma

5.1
it is enough to show that
{\mathcal{M}}\ni uv_{\eta}^{*}v_{\eta}u^{*}=p_{u|\eta|}

We first get

and thus
u[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}|\eta|]^{\perp}=[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(u|\eta|)]^{\perp}
because

Consequently,

uv_{\eta}^{*}v_{\eta}u^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(u|\eta|)]=uv_{\eta}^{*}v_{\eta}[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}|\eta|]=u[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}|\eta|]=[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(u|\eta|)]\,,

which means

uv_{\eta}^{*}v_{\eta}u^{*}[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(u|\eta|)]^{\perp}=[{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}(u|\eta|)]^{\perp}

If

Proof.

Since
\eta\in E_{1}^{u}{\mathcal{H}}\iff\eta=z\xi
for some
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
, the unicity of the polar decomposition of
(e^{\imath{\rm arg}(z)}{1}\!\!{\rm I}_{H})(|z|\xi)=\eta=v_{\eta}|\eta|
leads to
|\eta|=|z|\xi
and
v_{\eta}=e^{\imath{\rm arg}(z)}{1}\!\!{\rm I}_{H}
.
∎

6.
The noncommutative skew-product

The aim of the present section is to provide the quantum generalisation of the Anzai skew-product, first appeared in
[
3
]
, see also
[
24
,
9
]
, for the quantum case on the noncommutative torus.

We start with a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
made of the unital
C^{*}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
, the automorphism

\theta\in\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
of
\mathfrak{A}
. We next consider a second automorphism
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
.

To this end, we start by extending the action of
\theta
on
{\mathfrak{A}}
to an automorphism acting on the crossed product

\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
, which is well known when
\theta
and
\alpha
commute, see
e.g.

[
6
]
, Section 2.1. The construction is also
possible in more generality, for instance when
\theta
and
\alpha
commute up to a unitary
in the
C^{*}
-tensor category
{\rm End}(\mathfrak{A})
.
More explicitly, we assume that there exists
u\in\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{A}})
such that

(6.1)

u(\alpha\circ\theta)(a)=(\theta\circ\alpha)(a)u\,,\quad a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,.

Now, associated with any intertwining unitary
u
as above,
it is possible to define an automorphism
\Phi_{\theta,u}\in\rm{Aut}(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
, whose action on the generators is

(6.2)

\begin{split}&\Phi_{\theta,u}(a):=\theta(a)\,,\quad a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,,\\
&\Phi_{\theta,u}(V):=uV\,.\end{split}

For the convenience of the reader, we include a proof that the above formulae do in fact yield a well-defined automorphism
of
\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
. Before doing that, we also note that

\Phi_{\theta,u}(V^{*})=V^{*}u^{*}=\alpha^{-1}(u^{*})V^{*}\,.

Proposition 6.1
.

The map
\Phi_{\theta,u}
in (
6.2
) uniquely extends to a unital
*
-automorphism denoted, with a slight abuse of notation, again with
\Phi_{\theta,u}
, whose inverse is

\Phi_{\theta,u}^{-1}=\Phi_{\theta^{-1},\theta^{-1}(u^{*})}
.

Proof.

Thanks to the universal property enjoyed by crossed products and the definition for
\Phi_{\theta,u}(V^{*})
in (
6.2
), in order to obtain a unital
*
-endomorphism, for
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
we should satisfy

\Phi_{\theta,u}(V)\Phi_{\theta,u}(a)\equiv\Phi_{\theta,u}(Va)=\Phi_{\theta,u}(\alpha(a)V)\equiv\Phi_{\theta,u}(\alpha(a))\Phi_{\theta,u}(V)\,.

Therefore, we have to check
\Phi_{\theta,u}(V)\Phi_{\theta,u}(a)=\Phi_{\theta,u}(\alpha(a))\Phi_{\theta,u}(V)
,
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Indeed, by the assumption (
6.1
),

\displaystyle\Phi_{\theta,u}(V)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\theta,u}(a)=uV\theta(x)=u(\alpha\circ\theta)(a)V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\theta\circ\alpha)(a)uV=\Phi_{\theta,u}(\alpha(a))\Phi_{\theta,u}(V)\,.

We now notice that
\theta^{-1}
satisfies the same condition (
6.1
) w.r.t. the unitary
\theta^{-1}(u^{*})
:

\theta^{-1}(u^{*})(\alpha\circ\theta^{-1})(a)=(\theta^{-1}\circ\alpha)(a)\theta^{-1}(u^{*})\,,\quad a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,,

and therefore
\Phi_{\theta^{-1},\theta^{-1}(u^{*})}
, defined as in (
6.2
), provides another unital
*
-endomorphism of

{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
which realises a left and right inverse of
\Phi_{\theta,u}
.
∎

To ease the computations we will make in the sequel, we need to re-express powers
(uV)^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, in a more
convenient way, which is done below.

Lemma 6.2
.

With the notation established above, the equality
(uV)^{n}=u_{n}V^{n}
holds for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where the double sequence

\{u_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset\mathfrak{A}
is given by

(6.3)

u_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}I\,,\,\,\,n=0\,,&\\[4.30554pt]
u\alpha(u)\ldots\alpha^{n-1}(u)\,,\,\,\,n>1\,,&\\[4.30554pt]
\alpha^{-1}(u^{*})\alpha^{-2}(u^{*})\ldots\alpha^{n}(u^{*})\,,\,\,\,n<0\,.&\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It is a straightforward induction, which can be safely left out.
∎

Analogously, we can write

(6.4)

(uV)^{n}=V^{n}\alpha^{-n}(u_{n})\,,\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

Suppose now that a state
\omega_{o}
is invariant under the action of
\theta
, that is
\omega_{o}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\theta}
. Therefore, we have a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta,\omega_{o})
. Correspondingly,
we want to exhibit the
skew-product dynamical system

(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
where,

\omega\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
is simply defined by (
4.2
) as

\omega:=\omega_{o}\circ E
.

Concerning the invariance of
\omega
w.r.t.
\Phi_{\theta,u}
, we have

Proposition 6.3
.

With the notations set above, the state
\omega\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})
is
\Phi_{\theta,u}
-invariant, i.e.

\omega\circ\Phi_{\theta,u}=\omega
.

Proof.

Notice that it is enough to check the equality on the generators of the form
aV^{n}
,
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
On the one hand, by (
4.3
) we have
\omega(aV^{n})=\omega_{o}(a)\delta_{n,0}
.
By Lemma
6.2
and the invariance of
\omega_{o}
under
\theta
, we also have

\displaystyle\omega(\Phi_{\theta,u}(aV^{n}))=

\displaystyle\omega(\Phi_{\theta,u}(a)\Phi_{\theta,u}(V^{n}))=\omega(\theta(a)(uV)^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(\theta(a)u_{n}V^{n})=\omega_{o}(\theta(a)u_{n})\delta_{n,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{o}(\theta(a))\delta_{n,0}=\omega_{o}(a)\delta_{n,0}\,.

Collecting everything together, we have

\omega(\Phi_{\theta,u}(aV^{n}))=\omega_{o}(a)\delta_{n,0}=\omega(aV^{n})\,,

which ends the proof.
∎

To sum up, starting with a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta,\omega_{o})
and
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
, such that

\theta\circ\alpha
and
\alpha\circ\theta
are unitarily equivalent in
{\rm End}(\mathfrak{A})
by the unitary
u\in{\mathcal{U}}({\mathfrak{A}})
, we can define a
skew-product dynamical system associated to
u
, extending the Anzai skew-product constructed in the classical case, and therefore providing the
C^{*}
-dynamical system

(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
for the associated
C^{*}
-crossed product.

7.
A comparison with alpha-induction

The extensions considered above have already appeared in the literature with the name
alpha-induction
, even if in some very particular cases arising from the theory of factor-subfactor inclusions. Alpha-induction is designed to produce extensions of endomorphisms in the context of quantum field theory or, more generally, subfactors as long as they belong to an appropriate braided tensor category.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\hbox{id})
be a strictly associative
C^{*}
tensor category. It is said to be
braided
if it is equipped with a unitary braiding
\varepsilon^{+}
, that is a family

\{\varepsilon^{+}_{X,Y}\in\text{Hom}(X\otimes Y,Y\otimes X)\mid X,Y\in\mathop{\rm Obj}({\mathcal{C}})\}

of unitary isomorphisms
satisfying

(i)

naturality
: for any
X,X^{\prime},Y,Y^{\prime}\in\mathop{\rm Obj}(\mathcal{C})
,
f\in\text{Hom}(X,X^{\prime})
and
g\in\text{Hom}(Y,Y^{\prime})
,

(7.1)

(g\otimes f)\circ\varepsilon^{+}_{X,Y}=\varepsilon^{+}_{X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}\circ(f\otimes g)\,;

(ii)

tensoriality
: for every
X,Y,Z\in\mathop{\rm Obj}(\mathcal{C})
,

(7.2)

\begin{split}&\varepsilon^{+}_{X\otimes Y,Z}=(\varepsilon^{+}_{X,Z}\otimes\hbox{id}_{Y})\circ(\hbox{id}_{X}\otimes\varepsilon^{+}_{Y,Z})\,,\\
&\varepsilon^{+}_{X,Y\otimes Z}=(\hbox{id}_{Y}\otimes\varepsilon^{+}_{X,Z})\circ(\varepsilon^{+}_{X,Y}\otimes\hbox{id}_{Z})\,.\end{split}

Note that, if
\varepsilon^{+}
is a unitary braiding for
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\hbox{id})
, then also

\{\varepsilon^{-}_{X,Y}:=(\varepsilon^{+}_{Y,X})^{*}\mid X,Y\in\mathop{\rm Obj}({\mathcal{C}})\}

is a unitary braiding for
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\hbox{id})
.

In the rest of the present section, we assume that

I:={1}\!\!{\rm I}_{{\mathcal{H}}}\in\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}\subset{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}})

is a factor-subfactor inclusion of von Neumann algebra acting on the separable Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, with
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
of type
\mathop{\rm{III}}
.

Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be a standard vector for both
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
which exists by the Dixmier-Maréchal Theorem (
cf.

[
11
]
),
J_{\xi}^{\mathcal{N}}
and
J_{\xi}^{\mathcal{M}}
being the modular conjugations w.r.t.
\xi
of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
, respectively.

The
canonical endomorphism
(
e.g.

[
21
]

is defined for the subfactor
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}

\gamma_{\xi}(m):=\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{(J_{\xi}^{\mathcal{N}}J_{\xi}^{\mathcal{M}})}(m)\,,\quad m\in{\mathcal{M}}\,.

If
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
is another standard vector for both
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}

(
cf.

[
21

such that

\gamma_{\eta}=\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{u}\circ\gamma_{\xi}\,.

Since the reference vector
\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}
is fixed once for all, for the canonical endomorphism we write

\gamma:=\gamma_{\xi}
.

Let
\hat{\gamma}\in\mathop{\rm End}(\mathcal{N})
be the restriction of
\gamma
to
\mathcal{N}
. It is referred to as the dual canonical endomorphism, and provides a canonical endomorphism associated to the inclusion
\gamma(M)\subset N
.

Suppose that there is a tensor subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathop{\rm End}(\mathcal{N})
which admits unitary braidings
\varepsilon^{\pm}
, is full, and contains as objects the canonical endomorphism

\hat{\gamma}
and its sub-objects. In this situation, we say that the subfactor
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
is
braided
.
Then, for every
\sigma\in{\rm Obj}(\mathcal{C})

via

(

[

]

(7.3)

\alpha^{\pm}_{\sigma}:=\gamma^{-1}\circ\mathop{\rm ad}{}_{\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\rho}}\circ\sigma\circ\gamma.

In

]
, (

) is understood in the context of local nets, where

is a localizable, transportable endomorphism of the local net, and

-algebra of the local net.

If
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
has finite Jones index, in the context of braided subfactors outlined above,

.

Since we are here interested in crossed products by the action of
\mathbb{Z}
, we show for completeness that alpha-induction from (

) is actually well defined and yields an element of
\mathop{\rm End}(\mathcal{M})
, under the hypothesis that
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
is an irreducible (

), discrete, and braided inclusion. For such a purpose, we first recall the relevant definitions.

Suppose that there exists a normal faithful conditional expectation

of
{\mathcal{M}}
onto
{\mathcal{N}}
, and consider the Jones projection
e_{\mathcal{N}}
for

\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
associated to
E
.
Namely, fix the faithful normal state
\omega_{\xi}\circ E:=\langle E(\,{\bf\cdot}\,)\xi,\xi\rangle
, and consider the standard vector

for

\mathcal{M}
which induces
\omega_{\xi}\circ E
. The Jones projection

is defined as the self-adjoint
projection onto
\overline{\mathcal{N}\Omega}\subset\mathcal{H}
.

A
Pimsner-Popa basis
for

w.r.t.

is a countably family of elements

(ii)

\sum_{i}m_{i}^{*}e_{\mathcal{N}}m_{i}=I

Definition 7.1
.

An irreducible subfactor
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
of type
\mathop{\rm{III}}
is said to be discrete if it has a (unique) normal faithful conditional expectation

, and the dual canonical endomorphism

is a countable direct sum of finite-index endomorphisms (i.e.
[\rho_{i}({\mathcal{N}}):{\mathcal{N}}]<+\infty

\hat{\gamma}=\sum_{i}v_{i}\rho_{i}(\,{\bf\cdot}\,)v_{i}^{*}\,,

where
v_{i}\in\mathcal{N}
mutually orthogonal isometries satisfying
\sum_{i}v_{i}v_{i}^{*}=\hbox{id}_{\mathcal{H}}
, and the convergence is understood in the strong operator topology.

Proposition 7.2

(
cf.

[
10
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
be an irreducible inclusion of type
\mathop{\rm{III}}
factors. Then
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
is discrete if and only if, there exists a, necessarily unique, normal, faithful conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}

w.r.t.

whose elements

m_{i}n=\hat{\gamma}(n)m_{i}\,,\quad n\in\mathcal{N}\,,\,\,i\in I\,.

In order to check that (
7.3
) yields a well-defined
\alpha_{\sigma}^{\pm}\in\mathop{\rm End}(\mathcal{M})
in the context of the present paper, we report the following

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
be an irreducible inclusion of type
\mathop{\rm{III}}
factors.
Suppose that there is a braided tensor subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathop{\rm End}(\mathcal{N})
which is full and contains
\hat{\gamma}
.
Then for any
\sigma\in\mathop{\rm Obj}(\mathcal{C})
,

\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}}}\circ\sigma\circ\gamma(\mathcal{M})\subset\gamma(\mathcal{M}).

Proof.

For
n\in\mathcal{N}
, we have
\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}}}\circ\sigma\circ\gamma(n)=\hat{\gamma}\circ\sigma(n)
.
We must check that

\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}}}\circ\sigma\circ\gamma(m_{i})=\hat{\gamma}(\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}})^{*}\gamma(m_{i})\,,

where the
m_{i}
are elements of the Pimsner-Popa basis from Proposition
7.2
.

We first note that
\gamma(m_{i})^{*}\in{\rm Hom}(\hat{\gamma}^{2},\hat{\gamma})
. Hence, by naturality and tensoriality of the braidings
\varepsilon^{\pm}
(
cf.
(
7.1
) and (
7.2
)),

\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}}\sigma(\gamma(m_{i}^{*}))=\gamma(m_{i})^{*}\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}^{2}}=\gamma(m_{i})^{*}\hat{\gamma}(\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}})\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}}\,,

and thus

\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}}}\circ\sigma\circ\gamma(m_{i})=\hat{\gamma}(\varepsilon^{\pm}_{\sigma,\hat{\gamma}})^{*}\gamma(m_{i})\,.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is generated as a von Neumann algebra by
\mathcal{N}
and
\{m_{i}\}_{i\in I}
(
cf.

[
15
]
, Proposition 3.6), and since
\gamma
is normal, we get the claim.
∎

When
\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{N}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
, with
\alpha
an outer automorphism in
\mathop{\rm Obj}(\mathcal{C})
, the alpha-induction extension
\alpha^{\pm}_{\sigma}
coincides with
\Phi_{\sigma,\epsilon^{\mp}_{\alpha,\sigma}}
, as the following proposition shows explicitly.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a type
\mathop{\rm{III}}
factor with separable predual,
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm End}(\mathcal{\mathcal{N}})
be an outer automorphism of
\mathcal{N}
, and
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a full tensor subcategory of
\mathop{\rm End}(\mathcal{N})
containing
\alpha
and
\hat{\gamma}
, which is braided with unitary braidings
\varepsilon^{\pm}
. Then for any
\sigma\in\mathop{\rm Obj}(\mathcal{C})
,

\alpha^{\pm}_{\sigma}=\Phi_{\sigma,\epsilon^{\mp}_{\alpha,\sigma}}.

Proof.

It is enough to show that

\alpha^{\pm}_{\sigma}(n)=\sigma(n)
for all
n\in\mathcal{N}
,

\alpha^{\pm}_{\sigma}(V)=\epsilon^{\mp}_{\alpha,\sigma}V
.

The first equality is straightforward, recalling that
\hat{\gamma}=\gamma\lceil_{\mathcal{N}}
.
For the second equality, note that, since
Vn=\alpha(n)V
for all

, we have
\gamma(V)\in{\rm Hom}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{\gamma}\circ\alpha)

that is

Applying

∎

8.
General ergodic properties of the skew-product

We start with the following preliminary result concerning the topological ergodicity of
(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z},\Phi_{\theta,u})
, resulting from that of
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
.

Proposition 8.1
.

For the
C^{*}
-dynamical systems
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
and
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u})
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}={\mathfrak{A}}^{\theta}
;

(ii)

with the
u_{n}
given in (
6.3
), the equation

(8.1)

\alpha^{-n}(u_{n})\theta(a)=a\,,\quad a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,,

has no nontrivial solutions for each
n\neq 0
.

Consequently, suppose that
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is topologically ergodic. Then
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u})
is also topologically ergodic if and only if
(ii)
holds true.

Proof.

As shown in
[
8
]
, Theorem VIII.2.2, any element
x\in{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
can be decomposed into its Fourier series

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V^{n}c_{n}(x)
, with
c_{n}(x):=E(V^{-n}x)
, and the convergence being understood in norm
in the sense of Cesàro.
But then, for
x\in{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\Phi_{\theta,u}(x)-x=\theta(c_{o}(x))-c_{o}(x)+\sum_{n\neq 0}V^{n}\big(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n})\theta(c_{n}(x))-c_{n}(x)\big)\,,

and therefore
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}={\mathfrak{A}}^{\theta}
turns out to be equivalent to the fact that
\alpha^{-n}(u_{n})\theta(a)=a
has no nontrivial solutions for
n\neq 0
.

The last assertion is an immediate consequence of the previous part.
∎

The next proposition provides more information on the structure of
W_{n}
, the set of all solutions of the cohomological equations (
8.1
) at level
n
when
(\mathfrak{A},\theta)
is topologically ergodic.

Proposition 8.2
.

Suppose that the
C^{*}
-dynamical systems
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is topologically ergodic. Then the sets
W_{n}
of the solutions of the cohomological equations,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, are generated by a single unitary: if (
8.1
) admits a nonnull solution, then

W_{n}={\mathbb{C}}w_{n}
, for some
w_{n}\in{\mathfrak{A}}
with
w^{*}_{n}w_{n}=I=w_{n}w^{*}_{n}
.
7
7

7

Since
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is topologically ergodic,
W_{o}={\mathbb{C}}I
.
In addition, if for some
n\neq 0
, (
8.1
) has no nontrivial solution,
W_{n}
is the zero-dimensional subspace generated by any unitary in
{\mathfrak{A}}
.

Proof.

We first prove that all solutions are a, possibly trivial, multiple of a unitary.

If
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
satisfies the equation

\alpha^{-n}(u_{n})\theta(a)=a
, then it also satisfies
\theta(a^{*})\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}^{*})=a^{*}
. Multiplying the latter by the former equation, we find the equality
\theta(a^{*}a)=a^{*}a
. Hence, by topological ergodicity we must have
a^{*}a=\lambda I
, for some, necessarily positive, scalar
\lambda
. Since
a
is a solution of the equation,
V^{n}a=\alpha^{n}(a)V^{n}
is

\Phi_{\theta,u}
- invariant, but then the equation
\theta(\alpha^{n}(a))u_{n}=\alpha^{n}(a)
must be satisfied as well.
Taking its adjoint, it yields
u_{n}^{*}\theta(\alpha^{n}(a^{*}))=\alpha^{n}(a^{*})
. Again, if we multiply the former by the latter equality,
we now find
\theta(\alpha^{n}(aa^{*}))=\alpha^{n}(aa^{*})
, whence
\alpha^{n}(aa^{*})
is a scalar. But then
aa^{*}
is a scalar as well, say
aa^{*}=\mu I
. Since
\|a^{*}a\|=\|aa^{*}\|
, we have
\lambda=\mu
, which means
a
is a multiple of a unitary.

All is left to do is show that any two (non-trivial) solutions associated with the same
n
, say
a,b\in{\mathfrak{A}}
, are linearly dependent. From

\alpha^{-n}(u_{n})\theta(a)=a
and
\theta(b^{*})\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}^{*})=b^{*}
, we see that
\theta(b^{*}a)=b^{*}a
, hence

b^{*}a=\lambda I
. Thanks to the previous part, we can safely assume that
b
is a unitary, which means
b=\lambda a
.
∎

Let
({\mathcal{H}}_{\omega},\pi_{\omega},V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}},\xi_{\omega})
be the GNS covariant representation of
{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}

associated to the invariant state
\omega
. Consider the orthogonal projection
P
onto the closed subspace

[\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})\xi_{\omega}]:=\overline{\pi_{\omega}(\mathfrak{A})\xi_{\omega}}
. It is easily seen that
P\in\big(\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})\bigcup\{V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}}\}\big)^{\prime}
, and therefore
(P{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega},P\pi_{\omega},PV_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}},\xi_{\omega})
provides, up to unitary equivalence, the GNS covariant representation
({\mathcal{H}}_{\omega_{o}},\pi_{\omega_{o}},V_{\omega_{o},\theta},\xi_{\omega_{o}})
of
{\mathfrak{A}}
associated to the invariant state
\omega
.
Under the above identification of the covariant GNS representation of
\omega_{o}
, we also note that
E_{1}^{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}\leq E_{1}^{V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}}}
.

If we set

{\mathcal{H}}_{o}:=[\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})\xi_{\omega}]={\mathcal{H}}_{\omega_{o}}\,,\,\,{\mathcal{H}}_{n}:=\pi_{\omega}(V)^{n}[\pi_{\omega}({\mathfrak{A}})\xi_{\omega}]\,,\,\,\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

we have the following elementary, yet
useful, result.

Lemma 8.3
.

The Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
decomposes as
\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{H}_{n}
.

Proof.

By cyclicity, all we need to prove is
\mathcal{H}_{m}\perp\mathcal{H}_{n}
if
m
and
n
differ. To this end, it is enough
to show that any two vectors
\pi_{\omega}(V^{m}a)\xi_{\omega}
and
\pi_{\omega}(V^{n}b)\xi_{\omega}
,
a,b\in\mathfrak{A}
, are orthogonal in
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
. But this is seen by an easy computation, for

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{\omega}(V^{m}a)\xi_{\omega},\pi_{\omega}(V^{n}b)\xi_{\omega}\rangle=\omega(b^{*}V^{m-n}a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(b^{*}\theta^{m-n}(a)V^{m-n})=\omega_{o}(b^{*}\theta^{m-n}(a))\delta_{m,n}\,.

∎

Remark 8.4
.

The subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{n}
are all invariant under the restriction of
\pi_{\omega}
to
\mathfrak{A}
:

\pi_{\omega}(\mathfrak{A})\mathcal{H}_{n}\subset\mathcal{H}_{n}\,,\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

Furthermore, as noticed above, the restriction
of
\pi_{\omega}
, thought of as a representation of
\mathfrak{A}
, to the cyclic subspace
\mathcal{H}_{o}
is
unitarily equivalent to the GNS representation
\pi_{\omega_{o}}
of
\omega_{o}
.

The following result is the version of Proposition
8.1
at the Hilbert space level.

Proposition 8.5
.

For the
C^{*}
-dynamical systems
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta,\omega_{o})
and
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

E_{1}^{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}=E_{1}^{V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}}}
;

(ii)

with the
u_{n}
given in (
6.3
), the equation

(8.2)

\pi_{\omega_{o}}(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}))V_{\omega_{o},\theta}\eta=\eta\,,\quad\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega_{o}}\,,

has no nontrivial solutions for each
n\neq 0
.

Consequently, suppose that
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta,\omega_{o})
is weakly clustering. Then
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
is also weakly clustering if and only if
(ii)
holds true.

Proof.

By using the decomposition of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
into orthogonal components in Lemma
8.3
, we have

{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}\ni\xi=\xi_{o}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\pi_{\omega}(V^{n})\xi_{n}\,,

for a sequence

(\xi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega_{o}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega}
. Consequently,

V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}}\xi-\xi=V_{\omega_{o},\theta}\xi_{o}-\xi_{o}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\pi_{\omega}(V^{n})\big(\pi_{\omega_{o}}(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}))V_{\omega_{o},\theta}-{1}\!\!{\rm I}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega_{o}}}\big)\xi_{n}\,,

and therefore
E_{1}^{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}=E_{1}^{V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}}}
turns out to be equivalent to the fact that
\pi_{\omega_{o}}(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}))V_{\omega_{o},\theta}\eta=\eta
has no nontrivial solutions in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega_{o}}
for
n\neq 0
.

The weak clustering property of the invariant state
\omega_{o}
means that
\dim\big(E_{1}^{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}\big)=1
, and thus
\dim\big(E_{1}^{V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}}})=1
if and only if (ii) holds true.
∎

9.
Unique ergodicity and the cohomological equation

In the present section, we suppose that the
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta,\omega_{o})
is uniquely ergodic if it is not differently stated.
In analogy with what was found in dealing with classical Anzai flows and skew-product on the noncommutative torus (
cf.

[
18
,
9
]
), unique ergodicity
is actually automatic for the
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
as soon as it is ergodic.

Proposition 9.1
.

The
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u})
is uniquely ergodic with
\omega\in\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})

its unique invariant state, if and only if
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
is ergodic.

Proof.

We need only prove that ergodicity implies unique ergodicity.
We start by noticing that for the gauge automorphisms,

\rho_{z}\circ\Phi_{\theta,u}=\Phi_{\theta,u}\circ\rho_{z}\,,\quad z\in{\mathbb{T}}\,,

and thus
\rho_{z}\big(\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}\big)=\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}
,
z\in{\mathbb{T}}
.

Now, we assume that
\omega
is an extreme invariant state.
The key property to point out is that the equality

\oint\varphi\circ\rho_{z}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi\imath z}=\omega

holds true for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}
. By a standard approximation argument,
it suffices to check the claimed
equality only on elements of the form
V^{n}a
,
a\in\mathfrak{A}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
To this end, first we note that
\varphi\lceil_{\mathfrak{A}}=\omega_{o}
by unique ergodicity. Then we have

\displaystyle\oint\varphi\big(\rho_{z}(V^{n}a)\big)\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi\imath z}=\varphi(V^{n}a)\oint z^{n}\frac{{\rm d}z}{2\pi\imath z}=\varphi(V^{n}a)\delta_{n,0}=\omega_{0}(a)\delta_{n,0}

Now the conclusion follows by the same argument employed in
[
9
]
, Theorem 4.5.
∎

We can now move onto the main theorem of the present section.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
be uniquely ergodic with
\omega_{o}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathfrak{A}})
as the unique invariant state. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

the cohomological equation (
8.2
) has no nontrivial solution for
n\neq 0
;

(ii)

the
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)

(iii)

the
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
is ergodic;

(iv)

the
C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u})
is uniquely ergodic with
\omega\in\mathcal{S}({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})

as its unique invariant state.

Suppose in addition that
s(\omega_{o})\in Z({\mathfrak{A}}^{**})
, then one of the previous conditions
(i)-(iv)
turns out to be equivalent to

(v)

with the
u_{n}
given in (
6.3
), the equation

(9.1)

\pi_{\omega_{o}}(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}))\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}(v)=v\,,\quad v\in\pi_{\omega_{o}}({\mathfrak{A}})^{\prime\prime}\,,

has no nontrivial solutions for each
n\neq 0
.

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) Since
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta,\omega_{o})
is uniquely ergodic, as we have seen in (
3.1
), we have

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\omega_{o}(a\theta^{k}(b))\Big)=\omega_{o}(a)\omega_{o}(b)\,,\quad a,b\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,,

and therefore it is weakly clustering. But this means
\dim\big(E_{1}^{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}\big)=1
. If (i) holds true, then by Proposition
8.5
,

\dim\big(E_{1}^{V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}}}\big)=1
as well, and therefore
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
is weakly clustering.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) is clear since weak clustering implies ergodicity.

(iii)
\iff
(iv) is nothing else than the content of Proposition
9.1
.

(iv)
\Longrightarrow
(i) Suppose that
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u},\omega)
is uniquely ergodic. Then

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\omega(a\Phi_{\theta,u}^{k}(b))\Big)=\omega(a)\omega(b)\,,\quad a,b\in{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}\,,

and therefore it is weakly clustering. But this means
\dim\big(E_{1}^{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}\big)=1=\dim\big(E_{1}^{V_{\omega,\Phi_{\theta,u}}}\big)
. Thus (i) holds true again by Proposition
8.5
.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(v) is trivial, without any further assumption on the support of
\omega_{o}
. Suppose now
s(\omega_{o})\in Z({\mathfrak{A}}^{**})
. In this situation, if
\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega_{o}}\smallsetminus\{0\}
satisfies (

), then by Lemmata
5.2
and
5.4

|\eta|=\big|\pi_{\omega_{o}}(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}))V_{\omega_{o},\theta}\eta\big|=\big|V_{\omega_{o},\theta}\eta\big|=V_{\omega_{o},\theta}|\eta|\,,

and therefore

is invariant. By Lemma
5.5

Therefore, if

\big(\pi_{\omega_{o}}(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}))\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}(v_{\eta})-v_{\eta}\big)\xi_{\omega_{o}}=0\,,

and thus (v)
\Longrightarrow

is separating for

∎

Remark 9.4
.

For a uniquely ergodic

Much more than pointed out in Remark

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta,\omega_{0})
be a weakly clustering
C^{*}
-dynamical system.
If
s(\omega_{0})\in Z({\mathfrak{A}}^{**})
, then the solutions of (
9.1

of a unitary.

Proof.

We start by claiming that, under our hypotheses, any

such that

must be
a scalar multiple of the identity,

Now let

be a solution of
\pi_{\omega_{o}}(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}))\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}(v)=v

\mathop{\rm ad}\!{}_{V_{\omega_{o},\theta}}(v^{*})\pi_{\omega_{o}}(\alpha^{-n}(u_{n}^{*}))=v^{*}
. If we now multiply the latter by the former

for some positive
constant. Proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
8.2

must be a multiple of the identity as well, which gives the conclusion.
All is left to do is prove the claim made at the beginning.

Indeed, if

is central, the vector

is also separating
for the von Neumann algebra
\pi_{\omega_{0}}({\mathfrak{A}})^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
, which means the above equality is possible if and only
if

, as maintained.
∎

E

10.
Unique ergodicity with respect to the fixed-point subalgebra

In analogy with the terminology used in
[
9
,
13
]
, we start with the following definition relative to the splitting of the solutions of the cohomological equations (
8.2
) in
“continuous” and “measurable non-continuous” ones.

Definition 10.1
.

Let
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta,\omega_{o})
be
C^{*}
-dynamical system,
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
satisfying (
6.1
) for some
u\in\mathcal{U}({\mathfrak{A}})
, and for each fixed
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
consider the cohomological equation (
8.2
).

The solutions
\eta\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\omega_{o}}

of (
8.2
) of the form
\eta=\pi_{\omega_{o}}(a)\xi_{\omega_{o}}
for some
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
, satisfying (
8.1
), are called
“continuous”, whereas the remaining ones are called “measurable non-continuous”.

For
n_{o}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define
S_{n_{o}}\subset{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
as

S_{n_{o}}:=V^{n_{o}}\big\{a\mid a\in{\mathfrak{A}}\,\,\text{satisfying \eqref{comc*} for $n=n_{o}$}\big\}=V^{n_{o}}W_{n_{o}}\,,

where the

are made of all solutions of the coomological equation (
8.1
) for the given

. We note that if
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is topologically ergodic, then
W_{n_{o}}={\mathbb{C}}V^{n_{o}}w_{n_{o}}

is generated by a single unitary
w_{n_{o}}
(
cf.
Proposition
8.2
), as long as it is not trivial.

Proposition 10.2
.

For the

C^{*}
-dynamical system
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}},\Phi_{\theta,u})
, we have for
m,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(i)

S_{m}^{*}=S_{-m}
,

;

(ii)

\big({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}\big)^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}=\overline{{\rm span}\{S_{n}\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}}^{\|\,\,\,\|}
.

In addition, if
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is topologically ergodic, which happens when
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
uniquely ergodic, then

G_{\theta,u}:=\big\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid S_{n}\neq\{0\}\big\}

is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

(ii) directly follows by the lines in Proposition
8.1
.
We also note that if
V^{m}a\in S_{m}
and
V^{n}b\in S_{n}
, then

\displaystyle(V^{m}a)^{*}

\displaystyle=V^{-m}(V^{m}a^{*}V^{-m})=V^{-m}\alpha^{m}(a^{*})\,,

\displaystyle V^{m}aV^{n}b

\displaystyle=V^{m+n}(V^{-n}aV^{n})b=V^{m+n}\alpha^{-n}(a)b\,,

and
\alpha^{m}(a^{*})
,
\alpha^{-n}(a)b
satisfy (
8.1
) for
-m
and
m+n
, respectively. Therefore, (i) holds true.

Suppose now that
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is topologically ergodic. By Proposition
8.2
, if
S_{n}\neq\{0\}
, it is generated by the single unitary
V^{n}w_{n}
and therefore
G_{\theta,u}
, seen as a subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
, is closed by taking sums and opposite by (i), that is it is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

The previous considerations tell us that if
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is topologically ergodic, then
G_{\theta,u}=l_{\theta,u}{\mathbb{Z}}
for some

. Note that

is nothing but

\min\{n>0\mid S_{n}\neq\{0\}\}
.

In the case when
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is topologically ergodic, we define a linear map

E_{\Phi_{\theta,u}}
from the crossed product
C^{*}
-algebra to itself by setting

(10.1)

E_{\Phi_{\theta,u}}(x):=\sum_{l\in G_{\theta,u}}\omega_{o}\big(w_{-l}E(V^{-l}x)\big)V^{l}w_{l}\,,\quad x\in\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}\,,

where

is the conditional expectation in (

), the

are the unique unitary generating the

, and the convergence of the infinite sum is meant in norm in the sense of Cesàro.

If we define

\widetilde{w}_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w_{n}&\!\!\text{if}\,\,n\in G_{\theta,u}\,,\\
0&\!\!\text{otherwise}\,,\end{array}\right.

(

) becomes

E_{\Phi_{\theta,u}}(x):=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\omega_{o}\big(\widetilde{w}_{-n}E(V^{-n}x)\big)V^{n}\widetilde{w}_{n}\quad x\in\mathfrak{A}\rtimes{{}_{\alpha}}\mathbb{Z}\,.

We also define

{\mathfrak{A}}_{\theta,u}:=\overline{{\rm span}\big\{V^{m}\mathfrak{A}\mid m\in G_{\theta,u}\big\}}^{\|\,\,\|}\,.

It is obvious that
{\mathfrak{A}}_{\theta,u}
is a a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
containing the fixed-point subalgebra
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}
of the automorphism
\Phi_{\theta,u}
.

Proposition 10.3
.

The map in (
10.1
) is a conditional expectation onto the fixed-point subalgebra
(\mathfrak{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z})^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}
satisfying
E_{\Phi_{\theta,u}}\circ\Phi_{\theta,u}=E_{\Phi_{\theta,u}}
.

If in addition
({\mathfrak{A}},\theta)
is uniquely ergodic, for each
x\in{\mathfrak{A}}_{\theta,u}
we have

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Phi^{k}_{\theta,u}(x)\Big)=E_{\Phi_{\theta,u}}(x)\,,

in the norm topology.

Proof.

We leave to the reader the task to check that
E_{\Phi_{\theta,u}}
is a well-defined conditional expectation onto
({\mathfrak{A}}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\Phi_{\theta,u}}
.

Concerning the invariance, by a standard approximation argument, it is enough to consider
x=V^{m}a
for a fixed
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
a\in{\mathfrak{A}}
. For such an
x
we have

E_{\Phi_{\theta,u}}(x)=\sum_{l\in G_{\theta,u}}\delta_{l,m}\omega_{o}(w_{-l}a)V^{l}w_{l}\,.

Taking into account that, for general skew-products,
V^{-m}\Phi^{k}_{\theta,u}(V^{m})\in{\mathfrak{A}}
and, by (
6.4
), for
l\in G_{\theta,u}
,
V^{-l}\Phi^{k}_{\theta,u}(V^{l})=w_{l}\theta^{k}(w_{-l})
,
we get

be a process on the torus based on the topologically ergodic

-dynamical system

the fixed-point subalgebra

is

-isomorphic with either
\mathbb{C}

C(\mathbb{T})
,
the first case occurring when
(X_{o}\times\mathbb{T},\Phi_{\theta,f})

Proof.

The case
C(X_{o}\times\mathbb{T})^{\Phi_{\theta,f}}={\mathbb{C}}I
is trivial and occurs when the cohomological equations (
8.1
) have no nontrivial solutions for
n\neq 0
.

The remaining case occurs when the cohomological equations (
8.1
) have nontrivial solutions for all and only
n
in the subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}
of the form
\{n_{o}l\mid l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. In this case, the solutions are multiple of a single unitary

g_{n_{o}l}
(
cf.
Proposition
8.2
) satisfying
\overline{g_{n_{o}l}}=g_{-n_{o}l}
, and in addition one can choose the
g_{n_{o}l}
such that

g_{n_{o}l}g_{n_{o}m}=g_{n_{o}(l+m)}
, see Proposition
10.2
.

We point out that the continuous functions
G_{l}(x,z):=g_{n_{o}l}(x)z^{n_{o}l}
on
X\times{\mathbb{T}}
are linearly independent and
their linear span provides a (nontrivial)
*
-subalgebra of
C(X_{o}\times\mathbb{T})^{\Phi_{\theta,f}}
.

We now observe that

\mathcal{A}:=\Big\{\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{l}G_{l}\mid\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{l}|<\infty\Big\}\,,

endowed with the
\ell_{1}
-type norm

\Big[\!\!\Big]\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{l}G_{l}\Big[\!\!\Big]:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{l}|\,,

is also contained in
C(X_{o}\times\mathbb{T})^{\Phi_{\theta,f}}
since any series of that type
converges uniformly to a continuous
\Phi_{\theta,f}
-invariant function.

Thanks to the equality
G_{l}G_{m}=G_{l+m}
,

\mathcal{A}
is seen at once to be isometrically isomorphic with the Banach algebra
\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
understood
as the convolution algebra of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Since the latter has only one
C^{*}
-completion, that is
C(\mathbb{T})
, we finally see
that the fixed-point subalgebra must be
*
-isomorphic with
C(\mathbb{T})
.

Examples

We intend to provide some illustrative, perhaps simple, examples of commutative and noncommutative skew-products.

Example 11.1
.

The double rotation on the two-dimensional torus
R_{\theta,\varphi}\in\textrm{Homeo}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})

given by

(11.1)

R_{\theta,\varphi}(z,w):=(e^{\imath\theta}z,e^{\imath\varphi}w)\,,\quad(z,w)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}\,,

with

\frac{\theta}{2\pi}
irrational and
e^{\imath\varphi}
a root of unity, provides the simplest example
of an Anzai skew-product that is uniquely ergodic w.r.t the fixed-point subalgebra while not being uniquely ergodic.

Indeed, if
\varphi=\frac{2\pi}{l}
, for some positive natural number
l
, then the associated cohomological equation (
10.3
) at the level
n
is

g(e^{\imath\theta}z)e^{\frac{2\pi\imath n}{l}}=g(z)
,
a.e.
w.r.t. the Lebesgue-Haar measure of
\mathbb{T}
.

Let
U\in{\mathcal{U}}(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}},\lambda))
be the Koopman operator associated to the rotation of the angle
\theta
. It is well-known that
\sigma_{\rm pp}(U)=\{e^{\imath m\theta}\mid m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
.
13
13

13

For a bounded operator
A
acting on a Hilbert space,
\sigma_{\rm pp}(A)
is denoting its pure-point spectrum.

Since
\frac{\theta}{2\pi}
is irrational and
e^{-\frac{2\pi\imath n}{l}}
should belong to
\sigma_{\rm pp}(U)
if the cohomological equation would admit a nontrivial solution, it can happen if and only if
e^{\frac{2\pi\imath n}{l}}=1
, providing the “continuous” (according to Definition
10.1
) solutions which are the constants.

Therefore, the fixed-point algebra is nontrivial (
cf.
Proposition
8.1
), and (
cf.
Theorem
10.7
)

({\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}},R_{\theta,\varphi})
is uniquely ergodic w.r.t. the fixed-point subalgebra.

Example 11.2
.

The situation described in Remark

occurs for instance in the following classical case.

Indeed, we consider the Anzai skew-product on
{\mathbb{T}}\times{\mathbb{T}}

given in (

), with

being irrational. The corresponding cohomological equation for

is

g(e^{\imath\theta}z)e^{-\imath\theta}=g(z)
,

is seen at once to be a continuous solution of it. Therefore, this dynamical system is uniquely ergodic w.r.t.
the fixed-point subalgebra. Interestingly, this is true for the quantum analogue of this system as well.

More precisely, denoting by
R_{\theta}:z\mapsto e^{\imath\theta}z

\theta\in[0,2\pi)

Since for

, the

-dynamical system

\big(\mathbb{A}_{\alpha},\Phi_{R_{\theta},e^{-\imath\theta}I}\big)

In the remaining part, for the skew-product

Example 11.3
.

We deal with
X_{o}={\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty}
, the one-point compactification of the integers
{\mathbb{Z}}
, together with the homeomorphism

\theta:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty}
given by

\theta(l):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}l+1&\!\!\text{if}\,\,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,,\\
\infty&\!\!\text{if}\,\,l=\infty\,.\end{array}\right.

The dynamical system
({\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty},\theta)
is uniquely ergodic (but neither minimal, nor strictly ergodic) with the unique invariant measure

\mu_{o}(f):=f(\infty)\,,\quad f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty})\,.

We also note that

C({\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty})^{\theta}:=\{f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty})\mid f\circ\theta=f\}={\mathbb{C}}1\sim L^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty},\mu_{o})\,,

with
1
being the function identically equal to one.

For
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty};{\mathbb{T}})
, we can associate the process on the torus
\Phi_{\theta,f}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty}\times{\mathbb{T}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty}\times{\mathbb{T}}
given by
(\Phi_{\theta,f})(l,w)=(\theta(l),f(l)w)
. We now particularise the situation for

f(l):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta&\!\!\text{if}\,\,l=0\,,\\
1&\!\!\text{otherwise}\,,\end{array}\right.

for some
\beta\in{\mathbb{T}}
.

It is immediate to show that for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\text{const}f(l)^{n}=\text{const}\,,\,\,\mu_{o}\,\text{-}\,\textrm{a.e.}\,,

and therefore the (
10.3
) admit nontrivial solutions for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

On the other hand, if the two-sided sequence
(g(l))_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
satisfies (
10.4
) for some
n
, then

g(l):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g(0)\beta^{-n}&\!\!\text{if}\,\,l>0\,,\\
g(0)&\!\!\text{if}\,\,l\leq 0\,,\end{array}\right.

Imposing continuity, we are led to

g(0)=\lim_{l\to-\infty}g(l)=\lim_{l\to+\infty}g(l)=\beta^{-n}g(0)\,.

If
\beta=e^{2\pi\imath\alpha}
for some irrational number
\alpha
, then the unique continuous solution of (
10.4
) for some
n\neq 0
is accordingly the null sequence
g(l)=0
. Therefore, by Theorem
9.2
,

(X_{o}\times{\mathbb{T}},\Phi_{\theta,f})
provides a simple example of a (classical)
C^{*}
-dynamical system which is topologically ergodic but not uniquely ergodic.

If
\beta=-1
, (
10.4
) admits nontrivial solutions, provided
n
is even, and therefore
we can exhibit a
C^{*}
-dynamical system which is neither topologically ergodic (
i.e.

C(X_{o}\times{\mathbb{T}})^{\Phi_{\theta,f}}\supsetneq{\mathbb{C}}1
), nor uniquely ergodic w.r.t. the fixed-point subalgebra, see Theorem
10.7
.

The above assertions can however be explicitly checked as follows.
Indeed, in both the above examples for which
f(l)=1
,
l\neq 0
, we easily compute for the function
h(l,z)=z
,
l\in{\mathbb{Z}},z\in{\mathbb{T}}
,

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}h(\Phi_{\theta,f}^{k}(l,z))\Big)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z&\!\!\text{if}\,\,l>0\,,\\
f(0)z&\!\!\text{if}\,\,l\leq 0\,,\end{array}\right.

and therefore the Cesàro averages of
h(l,z)
do not converge to a function in
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{\infty},{\mathbb{T}})
if
f(0)\neq 1
.

Example 11.4

We provide as elementary an example as possible of a uniquely ergodic skew-product, where
\mathfrak{A}

To this aim, we choose
\mathfrak{A}:=\mathbb{A_{\gamma}}

UV=e^{2\pi\imath\gamma}VU
, where the deformation parameter
\gamma

{\mathbb{A}}_{\gamma}
is simple with a unique tracial state
\tau

Corresponding to this choice of the initial
C^{*}
-algebra, we consider the

\theta\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{A}_{\gamma})

\theta(U)=e^{i\theta}U\quad{\rm and}\quad\theta(V)=UV\,.

In other words,
\theta

\alpha(U)=U\quad{\rm and}\quad\alpha(V)=e^{i\alpha}V\,.

Phrased differently, the automorphism
\alpha

\alpha

We set
\mathfrak{B}:={\mathbb{A}}_{\gamma}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathbb{Z}

\theta

By considering the Koopman operator

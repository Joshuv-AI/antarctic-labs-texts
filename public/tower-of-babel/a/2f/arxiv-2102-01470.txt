Lax Connections in T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed Integrable Field Theories
Bin Chen, Jue Hou, Jia Tian

Abstract
In this work, we try to construct the Lax connections of T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed integrable field theories in two different ways. With reasonable assumptions, we make ansatz and find the Lax pairs in the T ​ T ¯ T\bar{T} -deformed affine Toda theories and the principal chiral model by solving the Lax equations directly. This way is straightforward but maybe hard to apply for general models. We then make use of the dynamical coordinate transformation to read the Lax connection in the deformed theory from the undeformed one. We find that once the inverse of the transformation is available, the Lax connection can be read easily. We show the construction explicitly for a few classes of scalar models, and find consistency with the ones in the first way.

Introduction

The
T\bar{T}
-deformation of two-dimensional field theories
[
1
,
2
]
has recently attracted much attention. It is a kind of solvable irrelevant deformation, and induces a flow in the space of field theories that satisfies the differential equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}^{(t)}=\text{det}\left({T_{\mu\nu}^{(t)}}\right),

(1)

where
T_{\mu\nu}^{(t)}
is the stress-energy tensor and
T\bar{T}=-\pi^{2}\text{det}{T_{\mu\nu}^{(t)}}
. A remarkable property of this flow is that it preserves integrability if the undeformed theory is integrable. In the original paper
[
1
]
, the preservation of integrability under a
T\bar{T}
-deformation or its generations has been supported by showing that the infinite conserved charges of the undeformed theory are still conserved under the flow. Another piece of evidence for integrability is from the fact that the S-matrix in the deformed theory is only modified by adding a CDD-like factor
[
1
,
3
]
. A word of caution is that the solvability of the deformed theories does not rely on integrability crucially and it can be understood from various aspects
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. Nevertheless, integrability may provide additional convenient handles on the theory.

As it is well-known, integrability can be described in other frameworks, such as the Lax pair formulation and the Bäcklund transformation formulation. In particular, the existence of the Lax connection is usually taken as the hallmark of classical integrability, and it also paves the way to quantization
[
4
]
.
However the Lax connection is notoriously difficult to find. Most of the time it needs the art of guess and trial and error. In this work, we will derive the Lax connections of several
T\bar{T}
-deformed integrable theories with two different methods. The first method is kind of straightforward. We start from some reasonable ansatz, and find the connection by imposing equations of motion and solving the Lax equation. This method is suggestive but could be limited to specific models. The second method is more systematic, and it relies on the fact that the
T\bar{T}
-deformation could be realized as a dynamical coordinate transformation
[
10
]
. It is reminiscent of the method in deriving the Lax connections of
\gamma
-deformed superstring theory
[
12
]
. This similarity is also expected, considering the fact that the holographic
T\bar{T}
-deformation
[
13
]
, or known as the single trace
T\bar{T}
-deformation, as well as the
\gamma
deformations, can be related to a TsT deformation
[
14
,
15
]
. The difference is that the single trace
T\bar{T}
-deformation is a field redefinition while the
T\bar{T}
-deformation of field theories is a change of coordinates.

The paper is organized as follows: In Sect. 2 and Sect. 3, we derive the Lax connection of (affine) Toda field theories and principle chiral model directly with reasonable ansatz; in Sect. 4 we first review the dynamical coordinate transformation approach of
T\bar{T}
-deformation, and then reproduce the results obtained in section 2 and 3, and in the end we derive the Lax connection for a
T\bar{T}
-deformed non-relativistic non-linear Schrödinger theory; in Sect. 5 we end up with conclusions.

2.1
Undeformed theories

The Lagrangian of a rank-
r
affine Toda field theory
1
1

1

[
18
]
.
is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{(0)}&\equiv\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}+V\end{split}

(2)

with

\begin{split}V=-\frac{m^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{r}n_{i}e^{\beta\vec{\alpha}_{i}\cdot\vec{\phi}},\end{split}

(3)

where
\vec{\phi}
is a vector field of
r
components, the set of integer number
\{n_{i}\}
characterizes the theory,
\{\vec{\alpha}_{i},~i=1,\dots,r\}
are positive simple roots of the underlying Lie algebra and
\vec{\alpha}_{0}=-\sum_{i}^{r}n_{i}\vec{\alpha}_{i}
.
If in the summation the term
i=0
is omitted then the theory reduces to the conformal Tada field theory.
The generators of the Cartan subalgebra
\vec{H}=\{H_{a},\ a=1,2,...,r\}
, and the simple roots
\{E_{\vec{\alpha}_{i}},E_{-\vec{\alpha}_{i}},\ i=0,1,...r\}
satisfy the standard commutation relations

\begin{split}&\left[H_{a},H_{b}\right]=0,\quad\left[\vec{H},E_{\pm\vec{\alpha}_{i}}\right]=\pm\vec{\alpha}_{i}E_{\pm\vec{\alpha}_{i}},\quad\left[E_{\vec{\alpha}_{i}},E_{-\vec{\alpha}_{j}}\right]=\delta_{ij}\frac{2\vec{\alpha}_{j}\cdot\vec{H}}{|\vec{\alpha}_{j}|^{2}},\\
&\left[E_{\vec{\alpha}_{i}},E_{\vec{\alpha}_{j}}\right]=\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{N}_{\vec{\alpha}_{i}+\vec{\alpha}_{j}}E_{\vec{\alpha}_{i}+\vec{\alpha}_{j}},\text{\ \ \ \ if $\vec{\alpha}_{i}+\vec{\alpha}_{j}$ is a root},\\
0,\quad\text{\quad\quad\quad\quad if $\vec{\alpha}_{i}+\vec{\alpha}_{j}$ is not a root}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(4)

The equations of motion are simply given by

\begin{split}2\partial\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}-\frac{\delta V}{\delta\vec{\phi}}=0,\end{split}

(5)

and the Lax connections are

\begin{split}L&=-\frac{\beta}{2}\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\vec{H}-\lambda\sum_{i=0}^{r}m_{i}e^{\beta\vec{\alpha}_{i}\cdot\vec{\phi}/2}E_{\vec{\alpha}_{i}},\\
\bar{L}&=\frac{\beta}{2}\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\cdot\vec{H}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{i=0}^{r}m_{i}e^{\beta\vec{\alpha}_{i}\cdot\vec{\phi}/2}E_{-\vec{\alpha}_{i}},\end{split}

(6)

where
\lambda\in\mathbf{C}
is the spectral parameter and
m_{i}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}|\vec{\alpha}_{i}|^{2}m^{2}n_{i}
. For a classical integrable system, the equations of motion are equivalent to the Lax equation

\begin{split}\partial\bar{L}-\bar{\partial}L-\left[L,\bar{L}\right]=0.\end{split}

(7)

For later convenience we introduce two new combinations

\begin{split}&E_{+}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}m_{i}e^{\beta\vec{\alpha}_{i}\cdot\vec{\phi}/2}E_{\vec{\alpha}_{i}},\quad E_{-}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}m_{i}e^{\beta\vec{\alpha}_{i}\cdot\vec{\phi}/2}E_{-\vec{\alpha}_{i}}\end{split}

(8)

satisfying

\begin{split}\left[E_{+},E_{-}\right]=-\frac{\beta}{2}\vec{\nabla}{V}\cdot\vec{H},\hskip 12.91663pt\left[\vec{H},E_{\pm}\right]=\pm\frac{2}{\beta}\vec{\nabla}{E_{\pm}}\end{split}

(9)

where
\vec{\nabla}{f}
denotes
\frac{\delta f}{\delta\vec{\phi}}
. Then the Lax connections (
6
) can be rewritten as

\begin{split}L&=-\frac{\beta}{2}\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\vec{H}-\lambda E_{+},\\
\bar{L}&=\frac{\beta}{2}\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\cdot\vec{H}+\frac{1}{\lambda}E_{-}.\end{split}

(10)

2.2
T\bar{T}
-deformed theories

The
T\bar{T}
-deformed Lagrangian of a
N
-scalar theory is
[
16
,
17
]

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{(t)}=\frac{V}{1-tV}+\frac{1}{2t(1-tV)}(\Omega_{T}-1)\end{split}

(11)

where

\begin{split}&\Omega_{T}=\sqrt{1+Y+Z},\quad Y=4t(1-tV)\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right),\\
&Z=-4t^{2}(1-tV)^{2}\left[\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\partial\vec{\phi}\right)\left(\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)-\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)^{2}\right].\end{split}

(12)

The equations of motion are given by

\begin{split}\vec{A_{e}}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{(t)}}{\delta\partial_{\mu}\vec{\phi}}-\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{(t)}}{\delta\vec{\phi}}=0.\end{split}

(13)

Substituting (
11
) and (
12
) into (
13
), one can get

\begin{split}\vec{A}_{e}=&\partial\left\{\frac{1}{\Omega_{T}}\left[\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}-2t(1-tV)\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\left(\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)-\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)\right)\right]\right\}\\
&+\bar{\partial}\left\{\frac{1}{\Omega_{T}}\left[\partial\vec{\phi}-2t(1-tV)\left(\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\partial\vec{\phi}\right)-\partial\vec{\phi}\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)\right)\right]\right\}\\
&-\frac{\vec{\nabla}{V}}{4\Omega_{T}(1-tV)^{2}}\left[\left(\Omega_{T}+1\right)^{2}-Z\right].\end{split}

(14)

Given these equations of motions we propose a simple ansatz for the Lax connection:

\begin{split}&L=-\frac{\beta}{2}\vec{a}_{1}\cdot\vec{H}-\lambda b_{1}E_{+}+\frac{1}{\lambda}c_{1}E_{-},\\
&\bar{L}=\frac{\beta}{2}\vec{a}_{2}\cdot\vec{H}-\lambda b_{2}E_{+}+\frac{1}{\lambda}c_{2}E_{-},\\
\end{split}

(15)

where
\vec{a}_{1},b_{1},c_{1},\vec{a}_{2},b_{2},c_{2}
are the functions of
\vec{\phi}
and their derivatives, and will be determined by imposing the Lax equation and the equations of motion. Notice that in our ansatz (
15
) the Lax connection depends uniformly on the simple roots
E_{\vec{\alpha}}
.
Plugging (
15
) into (
7
) directly gives rise to a set of linear differential equations
\vec{A}_{H},A_{+}^{\prime},A_{-}^{\prime},
corresponding to the components
\vec{H}
,
E_{+}
and
E_{-}
, respectively. In principle
\vec{A}_{H},A_{+}^{\prime},A_{-}^{\prime},
should vanish separately. However because the terms like
\vec{\nabla}{E}_{\pm}
are not uniformly dependent on the simple roots
E_{\vec{\alpha}}
, we would require that the coefficients before the terms like
\vec{\nabla}{E}_{\pm}
vanish separately. Consequently we obtain five sets of linear equations

\begin{split}\left\{\begin{aligned} &\vec{A}_{H}\equiv\partial\vec{a}_{2}+\bar{\partial}\vec{a}_{1}-\vec{\nabla}{V}(b_{1}c_{2}-b_{2}c_{1})=0\\
&A_{+}\equiv-\partial b_{2}+\bar{\partial}b_{1}=0\\
&A_{-}\equiv\partial c_{2}+-\bar{\partial}c_{1}=0\\
&\vec{A}_{p+}\equiv-\partial\vec{\phi}b_{2}+\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}b_{1}-(\vec{a}_{1}b_{2}+\vec{a}_{2}b_{1})=0\\
&\vec{A}_{p-}\equiv\partial\vec{\phi}c_{2}-\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}c_{1}-(\vec{a}_{1}c_{2}+\vec{a}_{2}c_{1})=0\\
\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

(16)

To solve these equations we make another assumption that they can be written as linear combinations of the equations of motion, i.e.

\begin{split}\vec{A}_{H}=f_{H}\vec{A}_{e},\quad A_{+}=\vec{f}_{+}\cdot\vec{A}_{e},\quad A_{-}=\vec{f}_{-}\cdot\vec{A}_{e},\quad\vec{A}_{p+}=f_{p+}\vec{A}_{e},\quad\vec{A}_{p-}=f_{p-}\vec{A}_{e}.\end{split}

(17)

To ensure the equivalence between the Lax equation (
16
) and the equations of motion (
13
), there should be no common zero of
f_{H},\vec{f}_{+},\vec{f}_{-},f_{p+},f_{p-}
.
Indeed we are making quite strong assumptions here, but we will show that the consistent solution does exist.

For the undeformed theory, by (
10
), one can find that

\begin{split}f_{H}=1,\quad f_{p+}=0,\quad f_{p-}=0,\end{split}

(18)

and there is no
\vec{f}_{+},\vec{f}_{-}
terms. We assume that (
18
) is still true for the deformed theory and observe that if we take

\begin{split}\vec{f}_{+}=-t\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi},\end{split}

(19)

then

\begin{split}\vec{f}_{+}\cdot\vec{A}_{e}=-\partial\left[\frac{t}{\Omega_{T}}\left(\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)\right]+\bar{\partial}\left[\frac{(\Omega_{T}+1)^{2}-Z}{4\Omega_{T}(1-tV)}\right]\end{split}

(20)

suggesting that we can identify

\begin{split}&b_{1}=\frac{(\Omega_{T}+1)^{2}-Z}{4\Omega_{T}(1-tV)},\quad b_{2}=\frac{t}{\Omega_{T}}\left(\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)\end{split}

(21)

up to some constants which can be fixed to be zero after considering other equations. Similarly, by taking
\vec{f}_{-}=-t\partial\vec{\phi},
we can read off
c_{1}
and
c_{2}

\begin{split}&c_{1}=\frac{t}{\Omega_{T}}\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\partial\vec{\phi}\right),\\
&c_{2}=\frac{(\Omega_{T}+1)^{2}-Z}{4\Omega_{T}(1-tV)}.\\
\end{split}

(22)

Finally from
\vec{f}_{H}\vec{A}_{e}
, we fix all the remaining functions in our ansatz

\begin{split}&\vec{a}_{1}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{T}}\left[\partial\vec{\phi}-2t(1-tV)\left(\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\partial\vec{\phi}\right)-\partial\vec{\phi}\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)\right)\right],\\
&\vec{a}_{2}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{T}}\left[\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}-2t(1-tV)\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\left(\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)-\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\left(\partial\vec{\phi}\cdot\bar{\partial}\vec{\phi}\right)\right)\right].\\
\end{split}

(23)

Plugging (
21
) (
22
) and (
23
) into (
16
), one can check that (
16
) is indeed equivalent to the equations of motion (
13
).

To summarize, the Lax connections of the
T\bar{T}
-deformed (affine) Toda field theories are of the forms (
15
) with the functions being given by (
21
) (
22
) and (
23
). We want to stress that after we assume (
18
) and (
19
) the solutions can be read off directly without solving any other equations.

In this section we consider the principal chiral model (PCM) which is an integrable sigma model. The

3.1
Undeformed theory

A principal chiral model (PCM) is a field theory whose field takes values in some Lie group manifold. Its action is
2
2

2

In the section, we consider the theory in the flat space and take the Euclidean signature, that is,
g^{\mu\nu}=diag(1,1)
. The coordinate is
(x^{0},x^{1})
and the Levi-Civita symbol is
\epsilon^{0,1}=-\epsilon^{1,0}=1
.

\begin{split}S_{0}=\int dx^{2}g^{\mu\nu}\text{Tr}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}gg^{-1}\partial_{\nu}g\right),\quad g\in G.\end{split}

(35)

Usually the Lie group is chosen to be semisimple but we will leave it to be arbitrary since our interest is on integrability. The model has symmetry group
G_{L}\times G_{R}
.
The equation of motion of PCM is just

\begin{split}\partial^{\mu}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g\right)=0,\end{split}

(36)

which is equivalent to the current conservation equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}=0,\quad j^{\mu}\equiv g^{-1}\partial^{\mu}g.

(37)

Here
j^{\mu}
is the conserved current corresponding to the
G_{R}
symmetry. In addition to (
37
), the conserved current also satisfies the flatness condition:

\begin{split}\partial_{0}j_{1}-\partial_{1}j_{0}=-\left[j_{0},j_{1}\right].\end{split}

(38)

The equations (
37
) and (
38
) are equivalent to the Lax equation with the Lax connections

\begin{split}&L_{0}=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+1}(\lambda j_{1}+j_{0}),\\
&L_{1}=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+1}(-\lambda j_{0}+j_{1}),\\
\end{split}

(39)

where
\lambda\in\mathbf{C}
is the spectral parameter.

3.2
T\bar{T}
-deformed theory

The
T\bar{T}
-deformed Lagrangian of PCM is given by
[
16
]

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{(t)}_{PCM}=\frac{1}{2t}\left(-1+\Omega_{P}\right),\end{split}

(40)

where

\begin{split}\Omega_{P}&=\sqrt{1+4t\text{Tr}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}gg^{-1}\partial_{\mu}g\right)+8t^{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\epsilon^{\rho\sigma}\text{Tr}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{\mu}gg^{-1}\partial_{\rho}g\right)\text{Tr}\left(g^{-1}\partial_{\nu}gg^{-1}\partial_{\sigma}g\right)}\\
&=\sqrt{1+4t\text{Tr}\left(j_{\mu}j^{\mu}\right)+8t^{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\epsilon^{\rho\sigma}\text{Tr}\left(j_{\mu}j_{\rho}\right)\text{Tr}\left(j_{\nu}j_{\sigma}\right)}.\\
\end{split}

(41)

The equation of motion
A_{ePCM}=0
, can also be cast into a form of conservation law:

\begin{split}A_{ePCM}&\equiv\partial_{\mu}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{(t)}_{PCM}}{\delta\partial_{\mu}\vec{\phi}}-\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{(t)}_{PCM}}{\delta\vec{\phi}}=2(\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu})g^{-1}\end{split}

(42)

Here the conserved current
J^{\mu}
is defined as

\begin{split}J^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\Omega_{P}}\left(j^{\mu}+4t\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\epsilon^{\rho\sigma}j_{\rho}\text{Tr}\left(j_{\nu}j_{\sigma}\right)\right),\end{split}

(43)

which satisfies the following useful identities

\begin{split}&[J_{0},J_{1}]=[j_{0},j_{1}],\\
&[J_{0},j_{0}]=\frac{1}{\Omega_{P}}4t\text{Tr}\left(j_{0}j_{1}\right)[j_{0},j_{1}],\\
&[J_{0},j_{1}]=\frac{1}{\Omega_{P}}\left(1+4t\text{Tr}\left(j_{1}j_{1}\right)\right)[j_{0},j_{1}],\\
&[J_{1},j_{0}]=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{P}}\left(1+4t\text{Tr}\left(j_{0}j_{0}\right)\right)[j_{0},j_{1}],\\
&[J_{1},j_{1}]=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{P}}4t\text{Tr}\left(j_{0}j_{1}\right)[j_{0},j_{1}].\\
\end{split}

(44)

Notice that the current
j_{\mu}
still satisfies the flatness condition (
38
) so the reasonable ansatz for the Lax connections could be that they are the linear combination of the new conserved current (
43
) and
j_{\mu}
:

\begin{split}&L_{0}=a_{0}J_{1}+b_{0}j_{0}+c_{0}j_{1},\\
&L_{1}=a_{1}J_{0}+b_{1}j_{0}+c_{1}j_{1},\\
\end{split}

(45)

where
a_{0},a_{1},b_{0},b_{1},c_{0},c_{1}
are some constants to be determined. Again we assume that the Lax equation is linearly dependent on the equation of motion:

\begin{split}A_{LPCM}\equiv&\partial_{0}L_{1}-\partial_{1}L_{0}-\left[L_{0},L_{1}\right],\quad A_{LPCM}=A_{ePCM}\cdot f_{PCM}\\
\end{split}

(46)

For the undeformed theory, using (

Lax connections from dynamical coordinate transformation

The solvability of

-deformation can be understood in various ways. From the point of view of integrability

d\bar{z}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathcal{J}=\begin{pmatrix}\partial w&\partial\bar{w}\\
\bar{\partial}w&\bar{\partial}\bar{w}\end{pmatrix}.

4.1
Free scalar

Consider the free scalar with Lagrangian

\displaystyle L(\mathbf{w})=\partial_{w}\phi\partial_{\bar{w}}\phi.

\displaystyle T_{2}(\mathbf{w})=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{w}\phi)^{2},\quad\Theta_{0}(\mathbf{w})=0,\quad\Delta=1-4t^{2}T_{2}(\mathbf{w})\bar{T}_{2}(\mathbf{w}),

T\bar{T}
-deformed free scalar:

\displaystyle\partial\left(\frac{\bar{\partial}\phi}{\Omega_{T}}\right)+\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\Omega_{T}}\right)=0.

5
Conclusion

In this work we constructed the Lax connections of several
T\bar{T}
-deformed integrable models in two different ways, and found consistent picture. The first way is based on proper ansatz, which assumes that the Lax equation is linearly dependent on the equation of motion. In the discussion, we also assumed that some proportional functions or parameters are invariant under the deformation. We obtained the Lax connections for the affine Toda theories and the principal chiral model. The method is suggestive, but its potential is not clear to us.

The other way relies on the dynamical coordinate transformation between the
T\bar{T}
-deformed theory and its ancestor. The method is systematic but maybe difficult to implement in some models due to the complexity of the dynamical coordinate transformation. We showed the power of the coordinate transformation in several models, including the free scalar theory, sine-Gordon model, Liouville field theory,
N
-scalar theory and non-linear Schrödinger model.

We want to stress that the dynamical coordinate transformation is not a diffeomorphism. Because the coordinate transformation depends on the dynamical fields, the inverse of the transformation could not be obtained in a closed form. Actually we tried to derive the Lax connection of
T\bar{T}
-deformed KdV equation. In this case the coordinate transformation depends on the higher order derivatives so the closed form of the inverse of the transformation is unlikely to exist. It is interesting to investigate the effectiveness of the coordinate transformation in other models, for example, the fermionic ones
[
24
]
. Besides the two methods discussed in this work, it would be interesting to study the Lax connection from other aspects on
T\bar{T}
-deformation, say from the light–cone gauge approach in
[
8
]
.

Given the explicit form of the Lax connection, there are various of applications. The first one is to construct infinite conserved charges as we show for the PCM. The expression (
52
) indicates the conserved charges get deformed in a very complicated way and it would be very interesting to study how the algebra is deformed. The other application is to construct the solitonic surfaces following
[
10
]
. Most importantly we hope our construction of Lax connection can shed light on the quantization of the
T\bar{T}
-deformation.

“Tattered” membrane
Pierre Le Doussal, Leo Radzihovsky

Abstract
Ideal crystalline membranes, realized by graphene and other atomic monolayers, exhibit rich physics – a universal anomalous elasticity of the critical “flat” phase characterized by a negative Poisson ratio, universally singular elastic moduli, order-from-disorder and a crumpling transition. We formulate a generalized D D -dimensional field theory, parameterized by an O ⁡ ( d ) × O ⁡ ( D ) O(d)\times O(D) tensor field with an energetic longitudinal constraint. For a soft constraint the resulting field theory describes a new class of a fluctuating “tattered” membranes, exhibiting a nonzero density of topological connectivity defects - slits, cracks and faults at an effective medium level. For hard, infinite-coupling constraint, the model reproduces the conventional crystalline membrane and its crumpling transition, and thereby demonstrates the essence of the difference between an elastic membrane and conventional field theories. Two additional fixed points emerge within the critical manifold, (i) globally attractive, ”isotropic” O ⁡ ( d ) × O ⁡ ( D ) O(d)\times O(D) , and (ii) ”transverse”, which in D = 2 D=2 is the exact ”dual” of the elastic membrane. Their properties are obtained in general D , d D,d from the renormalization group and the self-consistent screening analyses.

Summary and conclusion.

We introduced a generalized
O(d)\times O(D)
field-theoretic description of the
statistical mechanics of fluctuating manifolds –”tattered”
membranes, that allow for a finite density of unbound connectivity
defects, such as slits, cracks and faults,
in an elastic sheet. The model is formulated in
terms of a tensor field
\vec{t}_{\alpha}
controlled by an energetic constraint,
which, for a hard constraint recover known results for the crumpling
transition of an elastic membrane.

For a soft constraint, i.e.
g=O(1)
,
we have shown that
the criticality of the tattered membrane ultimately behaves at large scale
as a critical
O(d)\times O(D)
magnet. This suggests

D_{lc}=2
as the lower-critical dimension, implying that
the unbound connectivity defects destroy the crumpling
transition for physical 2D membranes at large scale,
while there is a non trivial critical point for
2<D<4
.
In addition, our study has unveiled an interesting transverse ”dual”
analog of elastic membranes,
which can be thought as a multi-component generalization
of the dipolar Heisenberg model. We calculated
its non trivial critical exponent for any
D
,
and predicted that it exhibits an ordered phase
that extends down to a lower critical dimension
D_{lc}(d)<2

that we computed. Finally, we
have shown that for
g\ll 1
or
\kappa\ll 1
the defected
membrane, or its dual, survives until a scale
that we determined. We hope these predictions
stimulate numerical and experimental studies on this
new class of defected
membranes.

II. SCSA analysis and large

In this section we give some of the details of the SCSA calculation, as well as of
the large-
d
expansion. Some more technical details will be given in
following sections. The method was pioneered for the

O(N)
model by Bray in
Bray
. For membranes it was
introduced in
LRprl
to describe the flat phase and
the crumpling transition. We will use similar methods and notations
as in Refs.
LRReview
and
OurBuckling
to which
we refer for more details on the method (see in particular
the Appendices and Supp. Mat there).

II.1
Starting model: bare propagator and bare vertex

We will consider again the model at the critical point
\tau_{R}=0
.
Since in the SCSA the (dressed) quartic vertex acquires a momentum dependence,
we extend the notations and write the bare effective Hamiltonian as

H[\vec{r}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf k}}\left(\kappa P^{L}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})+gP^{T}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})\right)k^{2}t_{\alpha}({\bf k})t_{\beta}(-{\bf k})+{1\over{4d}}\int_{{\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2},{\bf k}_{3}}R_{\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta}(\hbox{\bf q})~{\vec{t}}_{\alpha}({\bf k}_{1})\cdot{\vec{t}}_{\beta}({\bf k}_{2})~{\vec{t}}_{\gamma}({\bf k}_{3})\cdot{\vec{t}}_{\delta}({\bf k}_{4})

(89)

with
\hbox{\bf q}={\bf k}_{1}+{\bf k}_{2}
and
{\bf k}_{1}+{\bf k}_{2}+{\bf k}_{3}+{\bf k}_{4}={\bf 0}
, and we
use
\int_{{\bf k}}
to denote
\int d^{D}k/(2\pi)^{D}
. As before the bare tangent correlator reads

\displaystyle\langle t^{i}_{\alpha}({\bf k})t^{j}_{\beta}({\bf k})\rangle_{0}=\delta_{ij}G_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}({\bf k}+{\bf k}^{\prime})

(90)

\displaystyle G^{-1}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})=\left(\kappa P^{L}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})+gP^{T}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})\right)k^{2}\quad,\quad G_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})=\frac{P_{\alpha\beta}^{L}({\bf k})}{\kappa k^{2}}+\frac{P_{\alpha\beta}^{T}({\bf k})}{gk^{2}}

(91)

We choose the bare quartic tensorial interaction to be local, as in the main text, and of the form:

R_{\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}(\delta_{\alpha\gamma}\delta_{\beta\delta}+\delta_{\alpha\delta}\delta_{\beta\gamma})+\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{\gamma\delta}.\quad,\quad\mu_{0}=u\quad,\quad\lambda_{0}=2v

(92)

where we use the natural notations in the context of membranes.

II.2
SCSA equations: dressed propagator and dressed vertex

The dressed tangent correlator is denoted as

\langle t^{i}_{\alpha}({\bf k})t^{j}_{\beta}({\bf k})\rangle=\delta_{ij}{\cal G}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}({\bf k}+{\bf k}^{\prime})\quad,\quad{\cal G}^{-1}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})=G^{-1}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})+\sigma_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})

(93)

where
\sigma_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})
is the self-energy.
The SCSA gives two self-consistent
Dyson type equations for the self-energy and the dressed vertex, which allow to determine the dressed propagator
{\cal G}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})
. The infrared behavior of the resulting dressed propagator
(e.g. the exponent
\eta
, see below) is by construction exact to first order in the
large
d
expansion, i.e. to order
O(1/d)
in any
D
dimension.
Similarly the dressed vertex is exact as
d\to\infty
.
The SCSA equations ”improve” on the large-
d
expansion by
making it self-consistent through the use of dressed propagators and vertices.
It amounts to a resumation of a
1/d
expansion. It thereby provides predictions
for any
D,d
. Since it accounts for the physics of
screening of the interaction by fluctuations, it is known to give accurate results for membranes
in physical dimensions
d=3

LRReview
.

The first SCSA ”self-energy” equation takes the form

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}({\bf k})={2\over d}\int_{q}\tilde{R}_{\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta}(\hbox{\bf q}){\cal G}_{\beta\delta}({\bf k}-\hbox{\bf q})

(94)

where everywhere repeated indices are summed over.
Note that in principle it should be symmetrized in
\alpha,\gamma
,
however the only two-index tensors which appear
here are symmetric.
The complete Dyson equation for the self-energy contains an
additional (UV divergent)
k
-independent ”tadpole” diagram contribution.
The integral in (
94
) takes also some value at
k=0
. Both contributions have been
subtracted by tuning the bare ”mass” term (reduced temperature)
\tau
to its
critical value (so that
\tau_{R}=0
). This is the standard
procedure when dealing with a critical theory, where a parameter
(distance to crumpling transition parameters) must be tuned.
Henceforth, the integral in the r.h.s. of (
94
)
is to be understood with its value at
k=0
substracted.

In (
94
)
\tilde{R}_{\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta}(\hbox{\bf q})
is the (momentum dependent)
dressed quartic vertex. It is a four index tensor which is
symmetric in
\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta
, in
\gamma\leftrightarrow\delta
and under the
exchange
(\alpha,\beta)\leftrightarrow(\gamma,\delta)
. It is determined by the
second SCSA equation, which reads

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta}(\hbox{\bf q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta}(\hbox{\bf q})-R_{\alpha\beta,\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\hbox{\bf q})\Pi_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}}(\hbox{\bf q})\tilde{R}_{\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime},\gamma\delta}(\hbox{\bf q})

(95)

where
\Pi_{\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta}(\hbox{\bf q})
is the four index vacuum polarization tensor, which is also a symmetric tensor

\Pi_{\alpha\beta,\gamma\delta}(\hbox{\bf q})={\rm sym}_{\alpha\beta}{\rm sym}_{\delta\gamma}\int_{p}{\cal G}_{\alpha\gamma}({\bf p}){\cal G}_{\beta\delta}(\hbox{\bf q}-{\bf p})

(96)

where
{\rm sym}_{\alpha\beta}A_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}(A_{\alpha\beta}+A_{\beta\alpha})
.

To solve these equations we first parameterize the momentum-dependent tensors
\Pi
and
\tilde{R}
in an appropriate basis of projectors. We now recall how this is done
(see
ALGolubovic

II.3
Projectors and tensor multiplication, inversion of the second SCSA equation

Here we consider four index tensors, such as

II.4
Ansatz for the propagator at the critical point

We now look for a solution of the SCSA equations which describes the
critical manifold, i.e., for a propagator which behaves as a power law at small momentum.
A priori one could choose

{\cal G}^{-1}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})\simeq Z_{\kappa}k^{2-\eta}P^{L}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})+Z_{g}k^{2-\eta_{g}}P^{T}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})\quad,\quad{\cal G}_{\alpha\beta}({\bf k})\simeq\frac{P_{\alpha\beta}^{L}({\bf k})}{Z_{\kappa}k^{2-\eta}}+\frac{P_{\alpha\beta}^{T}({\bf k})}{Z_{g}k^{2-\eta_{g}}}

i.e. two different exponents for the transverse and longitudinal components.
However we will restrict our search to the case
\eta_{g}=\eta
, but
take into account that there are a priori two
distinct amplitudes

and
Z_{g}
for each component.
While in SCSA each is non-universal, their ratio may be universal
and in this section we will call

since it corresponds to a ”dressed” (or renormalized) version of

Anticipating that

(117)

\frac{d}{2}=-\frac{(D+1)(D-\eta+2)(D+2\eta-4)(D+2\eta-2)\Gamma(2-\eta)\Gamma\left(2-\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\Gamma(D+\eta)}{8(\eta-2)^{2}(D+\eta-1)\Gamma\left(-\frac{D}{2}-\eta+2\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}(D-\eta+4)\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}+\eta\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D+\eta}{2}\right)}

which can equivalently be written in a slightly shorter form as

The vanishing of

of the tattered membrane remains an open problem
that we leave for future studies.

The above results can be compared with the predictions by Bray

For general

II.8
RG version of SCSA and flow of
s=\kappa/g

To understand why there are only three critical fixed points discussed above,
we now use SCSA to derive the RG flow of the parameter
s=s(\ell)=\kappa/g

at scale
k=\Lambda e^{-\ell}
.

As was explained in details in
OurBuckling
(see section C.3. of the Supp. Mat. there)
one can recast the SCSA equations into an RG flow. It can be done so as
to recover either (i) only the RG functions to first order in
1/d
, or (ii) within the full self-consistent scheme.
We will only consider here the first approach, which is exact to
O(1/d)
.

Refering to
OurBuckling
for details, one defines within the SCSA
the natural dimensionless coupling for the RG as

\hat{w}_{i}:=\tilde{w}_{i}(q)Z_{\kappa}^{-2}q^{-\epsilon}

(163)

In terms of these couplings we obtain the RG equation for
d=+\infty
, exact for any
\epsilon=4-D
,
by taking a derivative
\partial_{\ell}=-q\partial_{q}
on both sides
of (
112
) and using (
118
). This gives

\displaystyle\partial_{\ell}\hat{w}_{i}=\epsilon\hat{w}_{i}-\epsilon a_{i}(0,D,s)\hat{w}_{i}^{2}\quad,\quad i=1,2

(164)

\displaystyle\partial_{\ell}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{w}_{3}&\hat{w}_{4}\\
\hat{w}_{4}&\hat{w}_{5}\end{pmatrix}=\epsilon\begin{pmatrix}\hat{w}_{3}&\hat{w}_{4}\\
\hat{w}_{4}&\hat{w}_{5}\end{pmatrix}-\epsilon\begin{pmatrix}\hat{w}_{3}&\hat{w}_{4}\\
\hat{w}_{4}&\hat{w}_{5}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{3}(0,D,s)&a_{4}(0,D,s)\\
a_{4}(0,D,s)&a_{5}(0,D,s)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{w}_{3}&\hat{w}_{4}\\
\hat{w}_{4}&\hat{w}_{5}\end{pmatrix}

(165)

where the functions
a_{i}(\eta,D,s)
are those defined in (
118
) and given in (
119
),
(
120
). Here, since
\eta=O(1/d)
, they have to be evaluated at
\eta=0
,
where they reach finite values. Whenever
s
can be considered as fixed, the
fixed point of these RG equations is
\hat{w}_{i}=\hat{w}_{i}^{*}
with

\hat{w}_{i}^{*}=\frac{1}{a_{i}(0,D,s)}\quad,\quad i=1,2\quad,\quad\begin{pmatrix}\hat{w}^{*}_{3}&\hat{w}^{*}_{4}\\
\hat{w}^{*}_{4}&\hat{w}^{*}_{5}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{3}(0,D,s)&a_{4}(0,D,s)\\
a_{4}(0,D,s)&a_{5}(0,D,s)\end{pmatrix}^{-1}

(166)

However,
s
, defined as the renormalized value of

\kappa/g
has a non-trivial RG flow.
To obtain this flow we
apply
-k\partial_{k}
to the equation for the
self-energy as explained in
OurBuckling
.
Here it is convenient to define
the transverse and longitudinal components
of the self-energy, within the SCSA, and
write them as

\displaystyle\sigma_{T}(k)\simeq Z_{g}k^{2-\eta}=Z_{g}(k)k^{2}

(167)

\displaystyle\sigma_{L}(k)\simeq Z_{\kappa}k^{2-\eta}=Z_{\kappa}(k)k^{2}

(168)

We then define the two ”
\eta
functions” or anomalous dimensions of

\kappa
and
g
respectively as

\displaystyle\eta_{g}=-k\partial_{k}\log Z_{g}(k)=-\frac{1}{Z_{g}(k)}k\partial_{k}(\sigma_{T}(k)/k^{2})

(169)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{Z_{g}(k)}k\partial_{k}(\frac{2}{d}Z_{\kappa}(k)\sum_{i=1}^{5}\hat{w}_{i}b_{i}^{1}(\eta,D,s))\simeq\frac{2}{d}s\sum_{i=1}^{5}\hat{w}_{i}\tilde{b}_{i}^{1}(D,s)\quad,\quad\tilde{b}_{i}^{1}(D,s)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}(\eta b_{i}^{1}(\eta,D,s)))

(170)

Similarly we define

\displaystyle\eta_{\kappa}=-k\partial_{k}\log Z_{\kappa}(k)\simeq\frac{2}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{5}\hat{w}_{i}\tilde{b}_{i}(D,s)\quad,\quad\tilde{b}_{i}(D,s)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}(\eta b_{i}^{1}(\eta,D,s)))

(171)

where we substitute the fixed point values for
\hat{w}_{i}
in (
166
)
for a given
s
, and we work to first order in
O(1/d)
.
Using the definitions in (
131
) it can also be written as

\displaystyle\eta_{g}=\frac{2}{d}f_{1}(D,s)\quad,\quad f_{1}(D,s)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\eta F_{1}(\eta,D,s)

(172)

\displaystyle\eta_{\kappa}=\frac{2}{d}f(D,s)\quad,\quad f(D,s)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\eta F(\eta,D,s)

(173)

We can now write the flow of
s=\kappa/g
as

\partial_{\ell}s=(\eta_{\kappa}-\eta_{g})\,s=\frac{2}{d}\left[f(D,s)-f_{1}(D,s)\right]\,s

(174)

Its expression as a function of general
s,D
is quite cumbersome, so we focus on
the main features.

First we find that
f(D,s=1)=f_{1}(D,s=1)
for any
D
in (
174
)
so that for
\kappa=g
,
s
does not flow, hence it corresponds to a fixed point
which describes the critical point for an isotropic
tattered membrane. One obtains
the explicit expressions in
D=3
, near
D=2
and near
D=4

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2}(\eta_{\kappa}-\eta_{g})=-\frac{16(s-1)\left(495s^{5}+377s^{4}+238s^{3}+246s^{2}+43s+9\right)}{15\pi^{2}(s+1)^{2}(3s+1)(7s+1)\left(5s^{2}+2s+1\right)}\quad,\quad D=3

(175)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(D-2)(s-1)\left(3s^{2}-2s+3\right)}{8s(s+1)}+O((D-2)^{2})

(176)

\displaystyle=-\frac{8(s-1)s\left(49s^{4}+2s^{3}+21s^{2}+8s+4\right)\epsilon^{2}}{3\left(3s^{2}+1\right)\left(7s^{2}+4s+1\right)^{2}}+O(\epsilon^{3})\quad,\quad D=4-\epsilon

(177)

One sees that the flow of
s
vanishes in
D=2
for any fixed
s>0

and finite, as in fact both exponents
\eta_{\kappa}
and
\eta_{g}
vanish
in that limit. However the limits
s\to 0
and
D\to 2
,
as well as
s\to+\infty
and
D\to 2
do not commute,
as the points
s=0,+\infty
are special (and dual to each others
in
D=2
, as discussed above).

We now extract the behavior of the flow of
s
around its three fixed points

s=0,1,+\infty
.

(i) Linearizing (
174
) around
s=1
, one finds, for general
D
, up to
O(1/d^{2})
terms

\displaystyle\eta_{\kappa}-\eta_{g}=\omega_{1}(D,d)(s-1)+O\left((s-1)^{2}\right)

(178)

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(D,d)=-\frac{2}{d}\frac{(4-D)(D-2)\left(D^{3}-D^{2}+4D-8\right)\Gamma(D)}{16(D-1)^{2}\Gamma\left(2-\frac{D}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}+2\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^{2}}

(179)

This defines an eigenvalue exponent
\omega_{1}=\omega_{1}(D,d)
, such that
s-1
flows as
k^{-\omega_{1}}

for
k\to 0
.
We find that
\omega_{1}(D,d)
vanishes for
D=2
as

\omega_{1}(D,d)\simeq-(D-2)/(2d)
, and for
D=4

as
\omega_{1}(D=4-\epsilon,d)\simeq-7\epsilon^{2}/(9d)
,
and is negative in between
with
\omega_{1}(D=3,d)=-44/(15d\pi^{2})
.
Hence
s
flows to
1
at large scale, and the isotropic tattered membrane
fixed point
s=1
is stable.

(ii) Linearizing (
174
) around
s=0
one finds, up to
O(1/d^{2})

\displaystyle\eta_{\kappa}-\eta_{g}=\omega_{0}(D,d)+O(s)\quad,\quad\omega_{0}(D,d)=\frac{2}{d}\frac{(D-4)^{2}D(D+1)(2D-3)\Gamma(D)}{4(5-D)(D-1)\Gamma\left(2-\frac{D}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}+2\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^{2}}

(180)

\displaystyle\omega_{0}(D=3,d)=\frac{96}{5d\pi^{2}}\quad,\quad\omega_{0}(D=2,d)=\frac{2}{d}\quad,\quad\omega_{0}(D=4-\epsilon,d)\simeq\frac{25\epsilon^{3}}{3d}

(181)

It turns out that to this order in
1/d
this exponent is nothing but the crumpling exponent

\omega_{0}(D,d)=\eta_{cr}(D,d)

(182)

This is because
\eta_{g}
vanishes at
s=0
. Since

\omega_{0}(D,d)>0
, it shows that any initial
small transverse perturbation of the tangent fields
with
s=\kappa/g>0
grows as
k^{-\omega_{0}(D,d)}

at large scale. This gives information on
how these ”tattering” or defects destroy the
purely longitudinal constraint of an elastic membrane at large scale.

(iii) Finally, linearizing (
174
) around
s=+\infty
one finds, up to
O(1/d^{2})

\displaystyle\partial_{\ell}s=\omega_{\infty}(D,d)s\quad,\quad\partial_{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)=-\omega_{\infty}(D,d)\frac{1}{s}

(183)

\displaystyle\omega_{\infty}(D,d)=-\frac{2}{d}C_{\rm tr}(D)

(184)

where
C_{\rm tr}(D)
was given in (
157
), with its value in
D=3
and

D=2
were given, as well at its expansion for
D=4-\epsilon
.
The exponent
\omega_{\infty}(D,d)

is negative, which means that any small positive
value of
1/s=g/\kappa>0
grows as
k^{\omega}_{\infty}(D,d)
.
In
D=2
the behavior around
s=0
and
s=+\infty
are
dual of each others (by the transformation
s\to 1/s
).
In general dimensions
D<4
both of these fixed points
are unstable towards the isotropic one,
s=1
.

performed

We will first identify the fixed points.

Similarly, we know that to one-loop order

In terms of the dimensionless couplings

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{1}=\hat{w}_{2}\simeq\frac{1}{2A(s(7s+4)+1)}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\hat{w}_{3}&\hat{w}_{4}\\
\hat{w}_{4}&\hat{w}_{5}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{A}\frac{1}{\left(3s^{2}+1\right)(s(7s+4)+1)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{4}(s(5s+2)+1)&-\frac{(s-1)^{2}}{4\sqrt{3}}\\

Remarkably, comparing with (

(

Chiral homology of the projective line and extensions of vertex algebra modules
Thadeu Henrique Cardoso, Jethro van Ekeren,

Abstract




Abstract.
The purpose of this note is to establish an isomorphism from the vector space of extensions between two modules over a vertex algebra, to an appropriate first chiral homology of one dimensional projective space with coefficients in the corresponding chiral algebra.

1
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional semisimple Lie algebra over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
. A classic theorem of Weyl asserts complete reducibility of all finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. The proof has two ingredients. The first is a general construction of homological algebra, namely an isomorphism from the vector space
\Ext^{1}(C,A)
of equivalence classes of extensions of
C
by
A
(where
A
and
C
are
\mathfrak{g}
-modules), to a cohomology space
H^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\Hom(C,A))
which may be described explicitly in terms of Chevalley-Eilenberg cocycles. The second is a proof of vanishing of cohomology using the semisimplicity hypothesis.

Vertex algebras are certain, usually infinite dimensional, algebraic objects with important applications in representation theory and theoretical physics. Homology and cohomology theories of vertex algebras have been introduced and studied by various authors, see
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
12
]
for example, as well as the series of articles starting with
[
1
]
. In these approaches, first cohomology
H^{1}(V,M)
recovers the appropriate notion of outer derivation of a module
M
over a vertex algebra
V
, parallel to the case of Lie algebras.

Meanwhile Beilinson and Drinfeld
[
2
]
have introduced the notion of chiral algebra over an algebraic curve
X
, as well as a definition of chiral homology of
X
with coefficients in a chiral algebra
\mathcal{A}
over
X
. Chiral homology generalises the construction of conformal blocks, which is in turn recovered in degree
0
. A conformal vertex algebra
V
gives rise to a chiral algebra
\mathcal{A}_{V}
over each algebraic curve
X
, see
[
6
]
. A key feature of chiral homology is that multiple modules can be ‘inserted’ at distinct points of
X
.

In this note we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
V
be a conformal vertex algebra and
A
and
C
two admissible
V
-modules. Then

\Ext^{1}(C,A)\cong H_{1}^{\textup{ch}}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{A}_{V},\mathcal{M}_{C,0},\mathcal{M}_{A^{\vee},\infty})^{*}.

We remark that Theorem
1.1
actually holds more generally for Möbius vertex algebras, see Remark
3.3
below. As an essentially immediate corollary of Theorem
1.1
we deduce

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
V
be a conformal vertex algebra. Then
V
is rational if and only if

H^{\textup{ch}}_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{A}_{V},\mathcal{M}_{C,0},\mathcal{M}_{A^{\vee},\infty})=0

for all admissible
V
-modules
A
and
C
.

Background on vertex algebras

A standard reference for this material is
[
10
]
, see also
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
. A quantum field on a vector space
V
is a formal series

A(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}

with coefficients
a_{(n)}\in\en(V)
, such that for each
b\in V
one has
A(z)b\in V((z))
the vector space of formal Laurent series.

Let
\mathbb{C}(z,w)
denote the fraction field of the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[z,w]
. We define
i_{z,w}:\mathbb{C}(z,w)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}((z))((w))
to be the unique morphism extending the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[z,w]\subset\mathbb{C}((z))((w))
, and
i_{w,z}
similarly.

We use the symbol
\res_{z=0}f(z)
to denote the coefficient of
z^{-1}
of a formal power series
f(z)\in U[[z^{\pm 1}]]
, and the symbol
\res_{z_{1}=z_{2}}f(z_{1},z_{2})
to denote the coefficient of
(z_{1}-z_{2})^{-1}
of the image of
f(z_{1},z_{2})
under the natural map

U[[z_{1},z_{2}]][z_{1}^{-1},z_{2}^{-1},(z_{1}-z_{2})^{-1}]\rightarrow U((z_{2}))[(z_{1}-z_{2})^{-1}],

obtained by expanding powers of
z_{1}=z_{2}+(z_{1}-z_{2})
in positive powers of
z_{1}-z_{2}
.

The
n^{\text{th}}
product of quantum fields
A(w)
and
B(w)
on a vector space
V
is

A(w)_{(n)}B(w)=\res_{z=0}\left(A(z)B(w)i_{z,w}-B(w)A(z)i_{w,z}\right)(z-w)^{n},

which is well-defined and again a quantum field on
V
.

Definition 2.1
.

A vertex algebra consists of a vector space
V
, for each
a\in V
a quantum field

Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}

on
V
, as well as a distinguished vector
\mathbf{1}\in V
(the vacuum vector) such that the following conditions are satisfied.

•

For all
a,b,c\in V
and
f(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},z_{2}^{\pm 1},(z_{1}-z_{2})^{-1}]
the following equality holds

\displaystyle\res_{z_{2}=0}\res_{z_{1}=z_{2}}

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})Y(Y(a,z_{1}-z_{2})b,z_{2})c

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\res_{z_{1}=0}\res_{z_{2}=0}f(z_{1},z_{2})Y(a,z_{1})Y(b,z_{2})c

\displaystyle-\res_{z_{1}=0}\res_{z_{2}=0}f(z_{1},z_{2})Y(b,z_{2})Y(a,z_{1})c,

•

Y(\mathbf{1},z)=I_{V}
, and

•

for each
a\in V
we have
Y(a,z)\mathbf{1}\in V[[z]]
and
Y(a,z)\mathbf{1}|_{z=0}=a
.

The translation operator
T\in\en(V)
is recovered as follows:
Y(a,z)\mathbf{1}=a+(Ta)z+\cdots
, and satisfies

\displaystyle[T,Y(a,z)]=Y(Ta,z)=\partial_{z}Y(a,z)

(2.1)

for all
a\in V
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
V
be a a vertex algebra. A
V
-module is a vector space
M
and for each
a\in V
a quantum field

Y_{M}(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a^{M}_{(n)}z^{-n-1}

on

, such that the following conditions are satisfied.

•

For all
a,b,\in V
and
m\in M
, and
f(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1}^{\pm 1},z_{2}^{\pm 1},(z_{1}-z_{2})^{-1}]
the following equality holds

\displaystyle\res_{z_{2}=0}\res_{z_{1}=z_{2}}

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})Y_{M}(Y(a,z_{1}-z_{2})b,z_{2})m

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\res_{z_{1}=0}\res_{z_{2}=0}f(z_{1},z_{2})Y_{M}(a,z_{1})Y_{M}(b,z_{2})m

\displaystyle-\res_{z_{1}=0}\res_{z_{2}=0}f(z_{1},z_{2})Y_{M}(b,z_{2})Y_{M}(a,z_{1})m,

•

Y_{M}(\mathbf{1},z)=I_{M}
.

Remark 2.3
.

The definition of
V
-module given above is one of two variants. In the other variant, an endomorphism
T\in\en(M)
is also given, for which the obvious analogue of (
2.1
) is required to hold. In this article we shall primarily be concerned with conformal vertex algebras (Definition
2.4
below) and their modules, and in this context there is no difference between the two definitions.

Definition 2.4
.

A conformal vertex algebra is a vertex algebra
V
together with a grading
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{n}
and a conformal vector
\omega\in V_{2}
whose associated quantum field
L(w)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}w^{-n-2}
equips
V
with a representation of some central charge
c
of the Virasoro Lie algebra

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\delta_{m,-n}\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}cI_{V},

compatible with the vertex algebra structure in the sense that (1)
L_{0}|_{V_{n}}=nI_{V_{n}}
and (2)
L_{-1}=T
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
V
be a conformal vertex algebra. A
V
-module
M
is said to be admissible if it carries a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-grading
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}M_{n}
such that
a_{(m)}(M_{n})\subseteq M_{r+n-m-1}
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
r,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
a\in V_{r}
. The vertex algebra
V
is said to be rational if every admissible
V
-module is completely reducible.

Remark 2.6
.

If
V
is a conformal vertex algebra, then any
V
-module
M
is in particular a representation of the Virasoro Lie algebra via the quantum field
Y_{M}(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}z^{-n-2}

[
11
, Prop. 4.1.5]
. If
M
is admissible, then the action of the Lie subalgebra
\der_{+}(\mathbb{C}[[z]])
topologically generated by the

with

is locally nilpotent, since
L_{k}(M_{n})\subseteq M_{n-k}
. So we can exponentiate this action to obtain an action of the Lie group
G_{+}=\aut_{+}(\mathbb{C}[[z]])
on
M
.
However, the action of
\der(\mathbb{C}[[z]])=\mathbb{C}L_{0}\oplus\der_{+}(\mathbb{C}[[z]])
cannot always be exponentiated to an action of the bigger group
G=\aut(\mathbb{C}[[z]])
on
M
. This can only be done if the eigenvalues of
L_{0}
are integral.

We denote by
\Ext^{1}(C,A)
the set of isomorphism classes of extensions of
C
by
A
, that is of short exact sequences

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

in the category of admissible
V
-modules. This set is naturally a vector space since the category of admissible
V
-modules is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear abelian category.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
V
be a conformal vertex algebra and
M
an admissible
V
-module. The contragredient
V
-module
M^{\vee}
is by definition the restricted dual vector space
M^{\vee}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}M_{n}^{*}
, equipped with the
V
-action

\displaystyle[Y(a,z)\varphi](m)=\varphi\left(Y(e^{zL_{1}}(-z^{-2})^{L_{0}}a,z^{-1})m\right).

(2.2)

Remark 2.8
.

The contragredient module construction is usually applied to
V
-modules whose graded pieces are finite-dimensional, such as ordinary
V
-modules (see
[
7
, Section 5.2]
). For an arbitrary admissible
V
-module with possibly infinite-dimensional graded pieces we find that the contragredient dual
M^{\vee}
is still well-defined and (
2.2
) continues to furnish
M^{\vee}
with the structure of a
V
-module. In this case, each dual space
M_{n}^{*}
acquires the structure of a topological vector space such that annihilators of finite dimensional vector spaces of
M_{n}
form a base of open neighbourhoods of
\{0\}
, and
M_{n}^{*}
is linearly compact with this topology
[
3
, Chapter 1.2]
. The action of
V
on
M^{\vee}
is now continuous.

3
Chiral algebras and chiral modules

Let
X
be a smooth complex algebraic curve and
V
a conformal vertex algebra. Following Beilinson and Drinfeld
[
2
]
, a chiral algebra on
X
is a right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module together with a morphism

\mu:j_{*}j^{*}(\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\Delta_{*}(\mathcal{A})

of right
\mathcal{D}_{X^{2}}
-modules satisfying analogues of the skew symmetry and Jacobi identities of Lie algebras. Here
\Delta:X\rightarrow X^{2}
and
j:X^{2}\backslash X\rightarrow X^{2}
are the inclusions of the diagonal and its complement. In general for a right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{A}
one has a canonical morphism

\displaystyle j_{*}j^{*}(\omega_{X}\boxtimes\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\Delta_{*}(\mathcal{A})

(3.1)

whose kernel is
\omega_{X}\boxtimes\mathcal{A}
. Here
\omega_{X}
is the canonical sheaf with its natural right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module structure. Then
\mathcal{A}=\omega_{X}
with (
3.1
) for
\mu
is an example of a chiral algebra on
X
. A chiral algebra
\mathcal{A}
is said to be unital if it comes with an inclusion
\omega_{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
compatible with (
3.1
).

For
V
a conformal vertex algebra, Frenkel and Ben Zvi present in
[
6
]
a construction of a unital chiral algebra
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{V}
over
X
. As a
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{V}\otimes\omega_{X}
is obtained via side-changing from a left
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{V}
. As an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module,
\mathcal{V}
is the colimit of finite dimensional holomorphic vector bundles constructed from truncations
V_{\leq n}
of
V
via an associated bundle construction.

In particular the fibres of
\mathcal{V}
have the following description. Let
G=\aut(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
and let
\coord_{p}
denote the
G
-torsor of formal coordinates at the point
p\in X
. The conformal structure of
V
endows it with a left
G
-action, and the fibre of
\mathcal{V}
at
p
is the vector space

\mathcal{V}_{p}=\coord_{p}\times_{G}V.

A choice of local coordinate at
p
furnishes a linear isomorphism between
\mathcal{V}_{p}
and
V
. An element of
\mathcal{V}_{p}
may thus be written in the form
(z,a)
, and a section of
\mathcal{V}
over a trivialising open
U\subset X
with coordinate
z
may be written as a linear combination of terms of the form
f(z)(z,a)
where by abuse of notation the value of
(z,a)
at
p\in U
is the element of
\mathcal{V}_{p}
represented by
(z-z(p),a)
.

A chiral
\mathcal{A}
-module on
X
is a right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}
and a morphism

\alpha_{\mathcal{M}}:j_{*}j^{*}(\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\Delta_{*}(\mathcal{M})

of
\mathcal{D}_{X^{2}}
-modules satisfying a natural condition of compatibility with
\mu
. We are interested in chiral
\mathcal{A}
-modules supported at a point
p\in X
. Such modules can be constructed in the following manner: if
M
is an admissible
V
-module such that the eigenvalues of
L_{0}
are integral, then we have a well-defined action of
G
on
M
by Remark
2.6
. Now a construction parallel to that of
\mathcal{A}_{V}
yields a chiral
\mathcal{A}_{V}
-module
\mathcal{M}_{M}
. Let
p
be a point of
X
and
j_{p}:X\backslash p\rightarrow X
the inclusion of its complement. We may now form the
\mathcal{A}_{V}
-module
\mathcal{M}_{M,p}=\coker\left(\mathcal{M}_{M}\rightarrow j_{p*}j_{p}^{*}\mathcal{M}_{M}\right)
.

The construction described above does not work for more general
V
-modules
M
, as the
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{M}_{M}
is not well-defined. We do have, however, the following construction from
[
6
]
. Let
M
be any admissible
V
-module, with possibly non-integral eigenvalues of
L_{0}
. Then we still have an action of the subgroup
G_{+}=\aut_{+}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
of
G
on
M
, and we may use it to construct

\mathcal{M}_{M,p,\xi}=\coord_{p,\xi}\times_{G_{+}}M,

where
\coord_{p,\xi}\subset\coord_{p}
consists of those coordinates with first order differential
\xi\in T_{p}X
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{M,p,\xi}
acquires an action
\alpha_{\mathcal{M}_{M,p,\xi}}
of local sections of
\mathcal{A}_{V}

[
6
, Theorem 7.3.10]
. To explicitly describe this action, we make a choice of local coordinate at
z
with first jet equal to
\xi
. This induces an identification of
\mathcal{M}_{M,p,\xi}
with
M
, and a local section
\sigma=f(z)(z,a)\,dz
of
\mathcal{A}_{V}
then acts as

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{M}_{M,p,\xi}}(\sigma)m=\res_{z=0}f(z)Y_{M}(a,z)m.

(3.2)

In the sequel we shall be concerned with
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
the Riemann sphere, which we equip with its two standard charts
(U_{1},z)
and
(U_{2},x)
, each isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
, and coordinates related by
x=1/z
on the intersection. We consider
\mathcal{A}_{V}
-modules supported at the points
z=0
and
z=\infty
(which is to say
x=0
). The choice of coordinates
z
and
x=1/z
induce tangent vectors
\xi
at
0
and
\infty
(which we will henceforth omit from the notation), and identifications
\mathcal{M}_{M,0}\rightarrow M
and
\mathcal{M}_{M,\infty}\rightarrow M
. The relationship between this setup and the definition of the contragredient dual is encapsulated in Proposition
3.2
below. In its proof we will use the following result from
[
7
]
(see Lemma 5.2.3, and equation (5.3.1) of
loc. cit.
)

Proposition 3.1
.

The identity

(-t^{-2})^{L_{0}}e^{t^{-1}L_{1}}(-t^{-2})^{L_{0}}=e^{-tL_{1}}

holds in
\en(V)[[t,t^{-1}]]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
V
be a conformal vertex algebra,
M
an admissible
V
-module, and
M^{\vee}
its contragredient. Under the induced identifications
\mathcal{M}_{M,0}\rightarrow M
and
\mathcal{M}_{M^{\vee},\infty}\rightarrow M^{\vee}
, we have

[\alpha_{\mathcal{M}_{M^{\vee},\infty}}(\sigma)\varphi](m)=-\varphi(\alpha_{\mathcal{M}_{M,0}}(\sigma)m).

for all
\sigma\in\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{\times},\mathcal{A}_{V})
,
m\in M
and
\varphi\in M^{\vee}
.

Proof.

It suffices to take
z^{n}(z,a)\,dz
for
\sigma
, for some
a\in V
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We first write
\sigma
in terms of the trivialisation associated with

. At the point
x=x_{0}
we have local coordinates
x-x_{0}
and
z-x_{0}^{-1}
related by
z-x_{0}^{-1}=g(x-x_{0})
where

g(t)=-x_{0}^{-1}t(x_{0}+t)^{-1}=e^{-x_{0}^{-1}t^{2}\partial_{t}}\cdot\left((-x_{0}^{2})^{-t\partial_{t}}\cdot t\right).

The linear automorphism of
V
corresponding to

is

R(g)=e^{x_{0}^{-1}L_{1}}(-x_{0}^{2})^{L_{0}},

and we have
(z-x_{0}^{-1},a)=(x-x_{0},R(g)a)
. Since
dz=-x^{-2}dx
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma=-x^{-n-2}(x,e^{x^{-1}L_{1}}(-x^{2})^{L_{0}}a)\,dx.

(3.3)

We now turn to the proof of the proposition. As per formula (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle[\alpha_{\mathcal{M}_{M^{\vee},\infty}}(\sigma)\varphi](m)

\displaystyle=-[\res_{x=0}x^{-n-2}Y_{M^{\vee}}(e^{x^{-1}L_{1}}(-x^{2})^{L_{0}}a,x)\varphi](m)

\displaystyle=-\varphi\left(\res_{x=0}x^{-n-2}Y(e^{xL_{1}}(-x^{-2})^{L_{0}}e^{x^{-1}L_{1}}(-x^{2})^{L_{0}}a,x^{-1})m\right)

\displaystyle=-\varphi\left(\res_{x=0}x^{-n-2}Y(a,x^{-1})m\right),

where in the first line we have used (
3.3
), to pass to the second line we have used Definition
2.7
, and to pass to the third line we have used Proposition
3.1
above.

But clearly

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\res_{x=0}x^{-n-2}Y(a,x^{-1})m\right)=\varphi(a_{(n)}m)=\varphi\left(\res_{z=0}z^{n}Y(a,z)m\right)=\varphi(\alpha_{\mathcal{M}_{M,0}}(\sigma)m)

and so we are done.
∎

We will abuse notation slightly and write

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{M}_{M^{\vee},\infty}}(\sigma)\varphi=\res_{z=\infty}f(z)Y_{M^{\vee}}(\mathcal{R}(z)a,z^{-1})\varphi

where
\mathcal{R}(z)=z^{2}e^{zL_{1}}(-z^{-2})^{L_{0}}
and
\res_{z=\infty}
means to substitute

and apply

\displaystyle\res_{z=\infty}f(z)\left(Y_{M^{\vee}}(\mathcal{R}(z)a,z^{-1})\varphi\right)(m)=-\varphi(\res_{z=0}f(z)Y_{M}(a,z)m).

Remark 3.3
.

Let
X
be a curve with a choice of projective structure, i.e., atlas relative to which transition functions are restrictions of Möbius transformations. This data yields a principal
PGL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-bundle on
X
with flat connection, and a reduction of the structure group to a fixed Borel
B

[
6
, Section 8.2]
. We denote this principal
B
-bundle as
\mathcal{P}
. In particular
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
comes equipped with such structures.

A vertex algebra
V
is said to be Möbius, roughly speaking, if it possesses a conformal grading and a compatible action of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}=\left<L_{-1},L_{0},L_{1}\right>
, see
[
10
, Definition 4.9]
for details. In particular a conformal vertex algebra is Möbius.

Exponentiating the action of
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
on the Möbius vertex algebra
V
and applying the associated bundle construction as in
[
6
]
produces a left
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{P}\times_{B}V
and a chiral algebra structure on the corresponding right
\mathcal{D}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{V}\otimes\omega_{X}
, with the chiral operation locally given by the vertex algebra state-field correspondence
[
2
]
. All the results proved below go through at this greater level of generality.

4
Chiral homology

Let
A
and
C
be a pair of admissible
V
-modules. We now introduce a chain complex
C_{\bullet}
that will compute chiral homology
H_{1}^{\textup{ch}}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{A}_{V},\mathcal{M}_{C,0},\mathcal{M}_{A^{\vee},\infty})
.

We write
(U_{1},z)
and
(U_{2},x)
for the affine charts of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as in Section
3
, and we write
X
for
U_{1}\cap U_{2}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,\infty\}
. Let us denote by
\Gamma_{n}
the coordinate ring of the configuration space
F(X,n)=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}\colon x_{i}\neq x_{j}\text{ for all }i\neq j\}
, i.e.,

\Gamma_{n}=\text{Spec }\mathbb{C}[z_{i},z_{i}^{-1},(z_{i}-z_{j})^{-1}\colon 1\leq i<j\leq n].

For each
i=1,\dots,n
we will denote by
\partial_{i}
the derivation of
\Gamma_{n}
defined to be the pullback of
\partial_{z}
along the
i^{\text{th}}
projection map.

We now define

\widetilde{C}_{n}=\Gamma_{n}\otimes V^{\otimes n}\otimes A^{\vee}\otimes C\quad\text{and}\quad C_{n}=\widetilde{C}_{n}/\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}+T_{i})\widetilde{C}_{n},

where
T_{i}
denotes the action of the translation operator on the
i^{\text{th}}
tensor factor of
V^{\otimes n}
.

The differential
d_{n}\colon C_{n}\rightarrow C_{n-1}
will be defined first as a map
d_{n}\colon\widetilde{C}_{n}\rightarrow\widetilde{C}_{n-1}
, which in fact will descend to the quotient
C_{\bullet}
and endow it with a chain complex structure (see
[
5
]

We define

In the arguments above we have assumed the graded pieces of

Suppose
A
and
C
are admissible
V
-modules. Each component

of

, being the full dual of a discrete vector space, may be equipped with the linearly compact topology, which has the property that its continuous dual
(A_{n}^{*})^{*\text{, cont}}
(i.e., the space of continuous linear functionals
A_{n}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
) is isomorphic to the original space
A_{n}
.

We have an isomorphism

\theta\colon\Hom^{\text{gr}}(C,A)=\Pi_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\Hom(C_{n},A_{n})\xlongrightarrow{\simeq}\Pi_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(A_{n}^{*}\otimes C_{n})^{*\text{, cont}}

which recovers (

) in case the graded pieces of
A
and

are finite dimensional and hence discrete.

As noted in Remark
2.8
, the action of
V
on

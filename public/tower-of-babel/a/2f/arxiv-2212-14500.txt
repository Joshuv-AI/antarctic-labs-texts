Hybrid measure differential equations: existence of solutions and continuous dependence on parameters.
Claudio A. Gallegos, Hernán R. Henríquez, Jaqueline G. Mesquita

Abstract
This paper is devoted to study the qualitative properties of hybrid measure differential equations (HMDEs, for short). We establish several results on the existence of global solutions, including the existence of regulated, continuous, differentiable and S–asymptotically ω \omega –periodic solutions. Furthermore, we present a result on continuous dependence of solutions in terms of parameters. Our results are based on extensions of Kasnoselskii’s fixed-point theorem. Keywords: Measure differential equations; Hybrid differential equations; Kurzweil–Stieltjes integral; existence of solutions; S–asymptotically ω \omega –periodic functions; continuous dependence on parameters. MSC 2020 subject classification: Primary: 34A12; 34A06. Secondary: 34A38; 34C25.

This work is dedicated to the study of qualitative properties of the hybrid measure differential equation (abbreviated, HMDE) given by:

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some basic concepts of Kurzweil–Stieltjes integration theory. For more details, the reader can see

[
33
,
36
]
. Throughout this text,
X
will always denote a Banach space endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|
and
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
is a compact interval. In particular, we consider
\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|
.

A function
\delta\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is called a
gauge
on
[a,b]
. If
\delta
is a gauge on
[a,b]
, a

tagged partition
of the interval
[a,b]
with subdivision points

a=s_{0}\leqslant s_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant s_{k}=b
, and
tags

\tau_{i}\in[s_{i-1},s_{i}]
,
i=1,...,k
, is called
\delta
-
fine
if

[s_{i-1},s_{i}]\subset\left(\tau_{i}-\delta(\tau_{i}),\tau_{i}+\delta(\tau_{i})\right),\;\;i=1,\ldots,k.

It is important to mention that given a gauge
\delta\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, it is always possible to obtain a
\delta
-fine tagged partition of the interval
[a,b]
, see
[
33
, Cousin’s lemma 6.1.3]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
33
, Definition 6.1.2]
)
We say that a function
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
Kurzweil–Stieltjes integrable
on
[a,b]
with respect to a function

g\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
if there is a vector
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for every
\varepsilon>0,
there exists a gauge
\delta\colon[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}f(\tau_{i})\left(g(s_{i})-g(s_{i-1})\right)-\mathcal{I}\right\|<\varepsilon

for all
\delta
–fine tagged partition of
[a,b].
In this case,
\mathcal{I}
is called the
Kurzweil–Stieltjes integral of
f
with respect to
g
over
[a,b]
and it will be denoted by
\int^{b}_{a}f(s){\rm d}g(s),
or just by
\int^{b}_{a}f{\rm d}g
.

As it should be expected, the Kurzweil–Stieltjes integral satisfies the usual properties of linearity, integrability on subintervals, additivity with respect to adjacent intervals, among others usual properties in integration theory (see
[
33
]
).

When
g
is the identity function, R. Henstock in 1961 (see
[
27
]
) introduced another concept of integral for real valued functions
which is equivalent to the integral in the sense of Kurzweil. In the technical literature, due to its equivalence with the integral
of Kurzweil, this integral is known as
Henstock–Kurzweil integral
or
gauge integral
(see
[
28
]
).
The relationship between different integration concepts can be summarized in the following chain of inclusions

\mathcal{R}([a,b],\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{L}_{1}([a,b],\mathbb{R})\subset H([a,b],\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{K}([a,b],\mathbb{R}),

where
\mathcal{R}([a,b],\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of Riemann integrable functions,
\mathcal{L}_{1}([a,b],\mathbb{R})
stands for
the space of Lebesgue integrable functions,
H([a,b],\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of Henstock integrable functions,
and
\mathcal{K}([a,b],\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of Kurzweil integrable functions.

To complete these general comments on the Kurzweil-Stieltjes integral,
we point out that the Kurzweil–Stieltjes definition allows us to integrate a broad class of functions.
For instance, as it was explained previously, if
g(t)\equiv t
, then we obtain the Henstock–Kurzweil integral,
allowing us to integrate highly oscillating functions.
On the other hand, the Kurzweil–Stieltjes integral allows us to integrate a function
f
with respect to
g
even when
both of them have points of discontinuity in common, which we know that is not possible for the integral in the Riemann–Stieltjes sense.
Therefore, for all these reasons, it seems most convenient to work with the Kurzweil–Stieltjes integral when continuity of both
functions
f
and
g
is not required. The relation among other types of Stieltjes integrals and the
Kurzweil–Stieltjes can be found in
[
33
]
.

In the rest of this section, we develop in detail some fundamental technical aspects to justify the results presented in
the following sections. Initially, we recall the concept of regulated function which plays an important role in this work.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
f\colon[a,b]\to X
is called
regulated
if the limits below exist

\lim_{s\to t^{-}}f(s)=f(t^{-})\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ t\in(a,b]\ \ \textrm{and}\ \ \lim_{s\to t^{+}}f(s)=f(t^{+})\ \ \textrm{for}\ \ t\in[a,b).

The space consisting of all regulated functions
f\colon[a,b]\to X
will be denoted by
G([a,b],X)
, and it is a Banach space
under the usual norm of the uniform convergence
\|f\|_{\infty}=\displaystyle\sup_{a\leqslant t\leqslant b}\|f(t)\|
.

In addition, we recall that the vector space consisting of all functions
f\colon[a,b]\to X
of bounded variation on

[a,b]
, denoted by
BV([a,b],X)
, is a Banach space endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{BV}=\|f(a)\|+\mathrm{var}_{a}^{b}(f),\;f\in BV([a,b],X),

where
\mathrm{var}_{a}^{b}(f)
denotes the variation of the function
f
on
[a,b]
. In the case
X=\mathbb{R}
, we
abbreviate the notation by writing
BV([a,b])
instead of
BV([a,b],\mathbb{R})
. It is well known that
BV([a,b],X)\subset G([a,b],X)
.

The next result yields sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of the Kurzweil–Stieltjes integral.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
36
, Corollary 1.34]
)

Let
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a regulated function and let
g\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded variation function.
Then the integral
\int_{a}^{b}f{\rm d}g
exists, and

\left\|\int_{a}^{b}f(s){\rm d}g(s)\right\|\leqslant\|f\|_{\infty}\mathrm{var}_{a}^{b}(g).

The following results establish important properties of the Kurzweil–Stieltjes integral.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
33
, Corollary 6.5.4]
)

Let
f\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a pair of functions such that
g
is regulated, and the
integral
\int_{a}^{b}f{\rm d}g
exists. Then the function
p\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

p(t)=\int_{a}^{t}f(s){\rm d}g(s),\;\;t\in[a,b],

is regulated, and satisfies

\displaystyle p(t^{+})

\displaystyle=p(t)+f(t)\Delta^{+}g(t),\;\;t\in[a,b),

\displaystyle p(t^{-})

\displaystyle=p(t)-f(t)\Delta^{-}g(t),\;\;t\in(a,b],

where
\Delta^{+}g(t)=g(t^{+})-g(t)
and
\Delta^{-}g(t)=g(t)-g(t^{-})
.

Remark 2.5
.

In the case that
g\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
is a regulated function, the Kurzweil–Stieltjes integral
\int_{a}^{b}f{\rm d}g
exists
for
f\in BV([a,b],\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see
[
33
, Theorem 6.3.11]
.

The next definition will allow us to appropriately manage sets of regulated functions.

Definition 2.6
.

(
[
20
]
)
A set
\mathcal{F}\subset G([a,b],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be
equiregulated
if for every
\varepsilon>0

the following conditions hold:

(i)

For every
\tau_{0}\in(a,b]
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|x(t)-x(\tau_{0}^{-})\|<\varepsilon
for all
\tau_{0}-\delta<t<\tau_{0}
and all

.

(ii)

For every
\tau_{0}\in[a,b)
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\|x(s)-x(\tau_{0}^{+})\|<\varepsilon
for all
\tau_{0}<s<\tau_{0}+\delta
and all
x\in\mathcal{F}
.

Next we present a type of Arzelà–Ascoli Theorem to characterize the compact sets in the space of regulated functions.

Lemma 2.7
.

(
[
20
, Corollary 2.4]
)

A set
\mathcal{F}\subset G([a,b],\mathbb{R}^{n})
is relatively compact if and only if
for every
t\in[a,b]
the set
\{x(t):x\in\mathcal{F}\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

and
\mathcal{F}
is equiregulated.

We finish this section with a generalization of Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

(
[
33
, Theorem 6.8.10]
)

Let
g\in BV([a,b])
and let
f,f_{k}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, be functions such that the integral

\int_{a}^{b}f_{k}{\rm d}g

exists for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}f_{k}(t)=f(t)
for each
t\in[a,b]
. Assume that there exists a
positive constant
K>0
such that for every subdivision
a=\sigma_{0}<\sigma_{1}<\ldots<\sigma_{l}=b
of the interval
[a,b]
and
every finite subset
\{m_{1},m_{2},...,m_{l}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
, the inequality

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{\sigma_{j-1}}^{\sigma_{j}}f_{m_{j}}(s){\rm d}g(s)\right\|<K

holds. Then the integral
\int_{a}^{b}f{\rm d}g

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{a}^{b}f_{k}{\rm d}g=\int_{a}^{b}f{\rm d}g.

3
Existence results

In this section, we present some results on the existence of solutions for problem HMDE (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). In order to state our results, we will make use of some additional concepts and a variant of Krasnoselskii’s fixed-point theorem.

Definition 3.1
.

A continuous nondecreasing function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is said to be a
\mathfrak{D}
–function
if
\varphi(0)=0

and
\varphi(s)>0
for
s>0
.

Definition 3.2
.

An operator
T:X\to X
is called
nonlinear
\mathfrak{D}
–contraction
if there is a
\mathfrak{D}
–function

\varphi
satisfying
\varphi(t)<t
for
t>0
, and

\|Tx-Tz\|\leqslant\varphi(\|x-z\|),

for all
x,z\in X
.

The Definition
3.2
is taken from
[
34
]
. Originally, the concept of nonlinear contraction appears in
[
8
]
, in which the function
\varphi
is considered upper semicontinuous from the right and satisfying
\varphi(t)<t
for
t>0
.

Next, we recall some known concepts of compactness for nonlinear operators.

Definition 3.3
.

An operator
T\colon X\to X
is said to be

•

Compact
if
T(X)
is a relatively compact subset of
X
.

•

Totally bounded
if
T(E)
is a relatively compact subset of
X
for any bounded subset
E\subset X
.

•

Completely continuous
if it is continuous and totally bounded.

We next recall an extension of Krasnoselskii’s fixed–point theorem, which is essential to establish our
results in this section. This extension was achieved using an improvement of the Banach contraction mapping principle.
The reader can see
[
8
]
for a discussion about this issue.

Theorem 3.4
.

(
[
34
, Theorem 1]
)

Let
S
be a closed, convex, and bounded subset of a Banach space X and let
A:S\to X
,
B:S\to X
be two
operators which satisfy the following conditions:

(a)

A
is a nonlinear
\mathfrak{D}
–contraction.

(b)

B
is completely continuous.

(c)

Au+Bv\in S
for all
u,v\in S
.

Then the operator equation
Au+Bu=u
has a solution in
S
.

In the rest of this section, we assume that
J=[t_{0},t_{0}+a]
with
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
a>0
, and the function
g\colon J\to\mathbb{R}
is nondecreasing
and left–continuous. To state our results, we introduce a set of additional conditions about

h,f\colon J\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(A0)

For every
t\in J
, the function
f(t,\cdot)
is continuous.

(A1)

For every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the function
f(\cdot,u)
is Kurzweil–Stieltjes integrable w.r.t.
g
on
J
.

(A2)

There exists a function
M\colon J\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
which is Kurzweil–Stieltjes integrable w.r.t.
g
such that

\left\|\int_{c}^{d}f(s,u){\rm d}g(s)\right\|\leqslant\int_{c}^{d}M(s){\rm d}g(s),

for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
[c,d]\subseteq J
.

(A3)

For every
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the function
h(\cdot,v)
is regulated on
J
, and there exists a
\mathfrak{D}
–function

\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\varphi(t)<t
such that

\|h(t,u)-h(t,v)\|\leqslant\varphi\left(\|u-v\|\right)

for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
t\in J
.

Remark 3.5
.

It is immediate to note that condition (A3) implies that for a fixed
x\in G(J,\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the function
h(\cdot,x(\cdot))
is regulated on
J
.

In order to illustrate a situation in which the condition (A1) is satisfied, we will present a property to ensure that a function

s\mapsto f(s,x(s))
is regulated on
J
for each
x\in G(J,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For a function
x:J\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by

Im(x)=\{x(t):t\in J\}
the range set of
x
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
x\in G(J,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Assume that
f:J\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a function
such that for each
v\in\overline{Im(x)}
, the function
f(\cdot,v)
is regulated on
J
. Let
y(s):J\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
the function given by
y(s)(t)=f(s,x(t))
. Assume that
\{y(s)(\cdot):s\in J\}

is an equiregulated set of functions. Then the function
z:J\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by
z(t)=f(t,x(t))
is regulated.

Proof.

We will start by showing that the function
z
has limits on the right. For this purpose, we will consider the set

J_{1}=[t_{0},t_{0}+a)
and we define the function
L:J_{1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

L(s)=\lim_{t\to s^{+}}f(t,x(s^{+})).

Let
s_{0}\in J_{1}
. We claim that
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to s_{0}^{+}}z(t)=L(s_{0})
.
In fact, for
t\in J_{1}
, we can write

z(t)-L(s_{0})=z(t)-f(t,x(s_{0}^{+}))+f(t,x(s_{0}^{+}))-L(s_{0}).

Since the functions
y(\xi)=f(\xi,x(\cdot))
are equiregulated for
\xi\in J
, it follows from Definition
2.6
that
for every
\varepsilon>0
, we can choose
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\|f(\xi,v)-f(\xi,x(s_{0}^{+}))\|<\varepsilon/2,

for all
\xi\in J
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\|v-x(s_{0}^{+})\|<\delta_{0}
. In addition, since
x(\cdot)
is a regulated function,
there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that
\|x(t)-x(s_{0}^{+})\|<\delta_{0}

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume that hypotheses
(A0)-(A3)
hold. Assume further that

\liminf_{r\to\infty}\dfrac{\varphi(r)}{r}<1.

(3.2)

Then there exists a solution for problem HMDE (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) defined on
J
.

Proof.

Using condition (
3.2
), we infer that there exists
N>0
large enough such that

\frac{\varphi(N)}{N}+\frac{\|x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|+H_{0}+K_{0}}{N}<1,

where
\displaystyle H_{0}=\sup\{\|h(t,0)\|:t\in J\}
and
K_{0}=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+a}M(s){\rm d}g(s)
.

Let
S
be the subset of

defined by

S:=\{x\in G(J,\mathbb{R}^{n}):x(t_{0})=x_{0},\;\|x\|_{\infty}\leqslant N\}.

Clearly,

is a closed, convex and bounded subset of

. We define the operators
A:S\to G(J,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

Ax(t)=h(t,x(t)),\;\;t\in J,

(3.3)

and

Bx(t)=x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f(s,x(s)){\rm d}g(s),\;\;t\in J.

(3.4)

Using these definitions, the integral equation (

(3.5)

From (A3), it follows that the operator
A
is a nonlinear
\mathfrak{D}
–contraction. In fact, for every

\|Ax(t)-Az(t)\|=\|h(t,x(t))-h(t,z(t))\|\leqslant\varphi(\|x(t)-z(t)\|)\leqslant\varphi(\|x-z\|_{\infty})

\|Ax-Az\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varphi(\|x-z\|_{\infty})

Next, we show that

is a completely continuous operator from
S
into

that

is continuous on

. Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

, for every finite subset

, we obtain

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to+\infty}Bx_{n}(t)&=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\left[x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f(s,x_{n}(s)){\rm d}g(s)\right]\\

&=x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f(s,x(s)){\rm d}g(s)=Bx(t),\end{split}

for all

is a continuous map on
S
.

Next we prove that

\begin{split}\|Bx(t)\|&\leqslant\|x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f(s,x(s)){\rm d}g(s)\right\|\\
&\leqslant\|x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|+K_{0},\end{split}

for all

, for all

is

.

On the other hand, let

with

. For any

, we have

\begin{split}\|Bx(t_{2})-Bx(t_{1})\|=\left\|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}f(s,x(s)){\rm d}g(s)\right\|\leqslant\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}M(s){\rm d}g(s)=p(t_{2})-p(t_{1}),\end{split}

where

is the function given by

for

by Theorem
2.4

is an equiregulated

.

To finish this proof, we show that condition (c) of Theorem

holds. Let

be arbitrary. Clearly,

Au(t_{0})+Bv(t_{0})=h(t_{0},u(t_{0}))+x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})=x_{0}
. Also, we estimate

\begin{split}\|Au(t)+Bv(t)\|&\leqslant\|h(t,u(t))\|+\|x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|+\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f(s,v(s)){\rm d}g(s)\right\|\\
&\leqslant\|h(t,u(t))-h(t,0)\|+\|h(t,0)\|+\|x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}M(s){\rm d}g(s)\\

for all

The preceding development shows that all the hypotheses of Theorem

The proof of this statement is standard. For completeness of the text, we are going to include here an outline of it.

Proposition 4.2

In the remaining of this section, we will study the existence of a type of solution that resembles a
\omega
–periodic function.
In

–periodic functions
for bounded continuous functions defined on
[0,\infty)

and the authors studied the main properties of this class of functions. In what follows, we will extend the definition
considered in those works to include regulated functions.

Definition 4.4
.

A bounded regulated function
x:[0,\infty)\to X
is called
S–asymptotically
\omega
–periodic

if there exists
\omega>0
such that

x(t+\omega)-x(t)\to 0,\;t\to\infty.

Before establishing the concepts necessary to study this topic, inspired by
[
25
, Example 3.2]
, we exhibit an example
to illustrate the concept of S–asymptotically
\omega
–periodic for regulated functions.

Example 4.5
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded sequence of real numbers consisting of numbers different
from each other and such that
a_{n+1}-a_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Let
x:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be the function given by

x(t)=\begin{cases}a_{1}+(a_{1}-a_{0})(t-1),&\text{if}\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant 1,\\
a_{n+1}+(a_{n+1}-a_{n-1})(t-n-1),&\text{if}\ n<t\leqslant n+1,\;n\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

Clearly the function
x
is continuous on every subinterval of type
(n,n+1)
with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

and left–continuous at every
t=n\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
x(n)=a_{n}
and

\Delta^{+}x(n)=a_{n-1}-a_{n}
for all

. Consequently,

is a regulated
function with a countable set of discontinuities on

and
x(\cdot)
is not periodic. The

x(\cdot)
follows
from the properties of the sequence
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

In addition, for every

Therefore,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}[x(t+1)-x(t)]=0
, which shows that the function

is
S
–asymptotically
1
–periodic.

We denote by

the space consisting of all S–asymptotically
\omega
–periodic functions from

to

. It is clear that

is a Banach space provided with the norm of uniform convergence.

To establish our result on the existence of S–asymptotically

Lemma 4.7

x\in W
and we can define
the map
U:W\to W
given by
Uy=(I-A)^{-1}By
.
Combining steps (ii) and (iv) we derive that
U
is a completely continuous map. Moreover, we can show that
\overline{U(W)}\subseteq W
. In fact,
for
y\in\overline{U(W)}
we can select a sequence
(x_{k})_{k}
in
W
such that
U(x_{k})\to y
as
k\to\infty
. It follows from Step (iv) that there exists a subsequence of
(x_{k})_{k}
, that we will continue to denote with the same index, such that
Bx_{k}\to z
as
k\to\infty
with
z\in W
.
Applying again Step (ii), we obtain that
U(x_{k})=(I-A)^{-1}Bx_{k}\to(I-A)^{-1}z\in V_{N}
as
k\to\infty
. This implies that

y=(I-A)^{-1}z
and
y=Ay+z
. Arguing as before to get the estimates (
4.6
), we infer that
y\in S_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which
in turn implies that
y\in W
. Applying Schauder–Tychonoff’s Theorem

[
23
, Theorem II.7.1.13]
, we obtain that
U
has a fixed point
x
in
W
. Therefore,
x
is a fixed point of
A+B
and

x(\cdot)\in S_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a solution of problem HMDE (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) on
[0,\infty)
.
∎

5
Continuous dependence on parameters

In this section, we present a continuous dependence result on parameters for the problem HMDE (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
on
J=[t_{0},t_{0}+a]
for functions
h,f:J\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g:J\to\mathbb{R}
. We assume that
f

satisfies conditions (A0)-(A1) and that
g
is a nondecreasing left–continuous function. To simplify the presentation, we will
restrict ourselves to consider the type of
conditions considered in Theorem
3.10
. Hence, in order to present our statement,
let
h_{k},f_{k}:J\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, be functions which satisfy conditions
(A0)–(A3) with respect to functions
\varphi_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
M_{k}:J\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, respectively. We denote by
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
the operators
associated to functions
h_{k},f_{k}
by mean of expressions (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively. We assume that

\varphi_{k}
are
\mathfrak{D}
–functions that satisfy the condition
\varphi_{k}(t)<t
uniformly on bounded intervals.
To specify this requirement, we introduce the following condition:

(I)

For every
0<c<d
, there is
\delta>0
such that
\varphi_{k}(t)<t-\delta
for all
t\in[c,d]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

As a consequence, since the hypotheses involved in the statement of Theorem
3.10
are fulfilled, we can affirm that the following problem

\displaystyle x_{k}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{k}(t_{0})-h_{k}(t_{0},x_{k}(t_{0}))+h_{k}(t,x_{k}(t))+\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{k}(s,x_{k}(s)){\rm d}g(s),\;\;t\in J,\vskip 5.69054pt

(5.1)

\displaystyle x_{k}(t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{x}_{k}

(5.2)

has a solution
x_{k}(\cdot)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

We are in a position to present the following result of continuous dependence on parameters.

Theorem 5.1
.

Under the above conditions, assume further that condition (I) holds and that the
following conditions are fulfilled:

(i)

The sequence
\widehat{x}_{k}\to x_{0}
as
k\to\infty
.

(ii)

The sequence
h_{k}(t,u)\to h(t,u)
as
k\to\infty
uniformly for
t\in J
and
u
in bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(iii)

The sequence
f_{k}(t,u)\to f(t,u)
as
k\to\infty
for
t\in J
and uniformly for
u
in bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the sequence of functions
(M_{k})_{k}
converges pointwise on
J
to a function
M\colon J\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and there exists a constant
C>0
having the following property: for every subdivision
d=\{\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{l}\}

of the interval
J
and every finite subset
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{l}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
the inequality

\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{\sigma_{j-1}}^{\sigma_{j}}M_{m_{j}}(s){\rm d}g\leqslant C
holds.

If there exists
R>0
such that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\sup_{r\geqslant R}\frac{\varphi_{k}(r)}{r}<1,

(5.3)

then there is a subsequence of
(x_{k})_{k}
that converges uniformly on
J
to a solution
x(\cdot)
of problem
HMDE (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

Proof.

We separate the proof in four steps.

Step 1.
We show that the set of functions
\{x_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded on
J
. First, we observe that

h_{k}(t_{0},\widehat{x}_{k})\to h(t_{0},x_{0})
as
k\to\infty
. In fact,

\displaystyle\|h_{k}(t_{0},\widehat{x}_{k})-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|h_{k}(t_{0},\widehat{x}_{k})-h_{k}(t_{0},x_{0})\|+\|h_{k}(t_{0},x_{0})-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(\|\widehat{x}_{k}-x_{0}\|)+\|h_{k}(t_{0},x_{0})-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|\widehat{x}_{k}-x_{0}\|+\|h_{k}(t_{0},x_{0})-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|\to 0,\;k\to\infty.

Moreover, from (
5.1
)–(
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|x_{k}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|\widehat{x}_{k}-h_{k}(t_{0},\widehat{x}_{k})\|+\|h_{k}(t,x_{k}(t))-h_{k}(t,0)\|+\|h_{k}(t,0)\|+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+a}M_{k}(s){\rm d}g(s)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|\widehat{x}_{k}-h(t_{0},x_{0})\|+\|h(t_{0},x_{0})-h_{k}(t_{0},\widehat{x}_{k})\|+\|h_{k}(t,0)\|+\varphi_{k}(\|x_{k}(t)\|)+\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{0}+a}M_{k}(s){\rm d}g(s)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{1}+\varphi_{k}(\|x_{k}(t)\|)

for certain constant
C_{1}\geqslant 0
and all
t\in J
. Let
{\displaystyle N_{k}=\sup_{t\in J}\|x_{k}(t)\|}
. It follows from the
above estimates that

1\leqslant\frac{C_{1}}{N_{k}}+\frac{\varphi_{k}(\|x_{k}(t)\|)}{N_{k}}\leqslant\frac{C_{1}}{N_{k}}+\frac{\varphi_{k}(N_{k})}{N_{k}}

(5.4)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that the set
\{N_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is unbounded. It follows from the estimates (
5.4
)
that
{\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\varphi_{k}(N_{k})}{N_{k}}\geqslant 1}
.
On the other hand, it follows from (
5.3
) that

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{\varphi_{k}(N_{k})}{N_{k}}\leqslant\liminf_{k\to\infty}\sup_{r\geqslant R}\frac{\varphi_{k}(r)}{r}<1

which contradicts our previous assertion. Hence, we get that there is a constant
N>0
independent of
k
such that

\|x_{k}(t)\|\leqslant N
for all
t\in J
.

Step 2.
For each
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the function
h(\cdot,v)
is regulated because it is the uniform limit
of the sequence of regulated functions
h_{k}(\cdot,v)
as
k\to\infty
. For the same reason, the set

\{h_{k}(\cdot,v):k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equiregulated on
J
. In addition,
for a bounded set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the set of functions
\{h_{k}(\cdot,v):v\in K,\;k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equiregulated on
J
.
In order to prove that the left and right limits of the functions
h_{k}(\cdot,v)
exist uniformly for

v\in K
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it suffices to show that we can approximate the functions
h_{k}(\cdot,v)
by functions taken
from an equiregulated set of functions. Specifically, for every
\varepsilon>0
, since
K
is a relatively compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there
exist
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
such that
for each
v\in K
there is
i=1,\ldots,m
with
\|v-v_{i}\|\leqslant\varepsilon
. By our previous remark, the set

\{h_{k}(\cdot,v_{i}):k\in\mathbb{N},\;i=1,\ldots,m\}
is equiregulated on
J
. Moreover, for
v\in K
, by choosing

i=1,\ldots,m
such that
\|v-v_{i}\|\leqslant\varepsilon
, we have

\|h_{k}(t,v)-h_{k}(t,v_{i})\|\leqslant\varphi_{k}(\|v-v_{i}\|)\leqslant\varepsilon

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in J
. This shows that
\{h_{k}(\cdot,v):v\in K,\;k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equiregulated on
J
.

Step 3.
The set of functions
\{x_{k}(\cdot):k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equiregulated on
J
.
It follows from (
5.1
) that

x_{k}(t)=A_{k}x_{k}(t)+B_{k}x_{k}(t).

First we prove that the set
\{B_{k}x_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equiregulated on
J
. Let

p_{k}(t)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{k}(s,x_{k}(s)){\rm d}g(s),\;t\in J.

It follows from (iii) that the set
\{p_{k}(t):k\in\mathbb{N},t\in J\}
is bounded. Moreover,
for
t_{0}\leqslant t<t_{0}+a
, from (
3.4
) and applying Theorem
2.4
, we have that

B_{k}x_{k}(t^{+})-B_{k}x_{k}(t)=f_{k}(t,x_{k}(t))\Delta^{+}g(t)

which shows that
\{B_{k}x_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is right equiregulated at
t
. In similar way, we can show that

\{B_{k}x_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is left equiregulated at
t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+a]
.

On the other hand, for
t_{1},t_{2}\in J
, we can write

x_{k}(t_{2})-x_{k}(t_{1})=A_{k}x_{k}(t_{2})-A_{k}x_{k}(t_{1})+B_{k}x_{k}(t_{2})-B_{k}x_{k}(t_{1})

from which we have

\displaystyle\|x_{k}(t_{2})-x_{k}(t_{1})\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|A_{k}x_{k}(t_{2})-A_{k}x_{k}(t_{1})\|+\|B_{k}x_{k}(t_{2})-B_{k}x_{k}(t_{1})\|

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(\|x_{k}(t_{2})-x_{k}(t_{1})\|+\|B_{k}x_{k}(t_{2})-B_{k}x_{k}(t_{1})\|.

Assume that
\{x_{k}(\cdot):k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is not equiregulated. Specifically, to fix ideas, suppose that condition
(i) from Definition
2.6
is not verified at point
\tau_{0}
. Then we can affirm that there exists
\varepsilon>0
and two sequences

(t_{1}^{k})_{k}
and
(t_{2}^{k})_{k}
that converge to
\tau_{0}^{-}
and
\|x_{k}(t_{2}^{k})-x_{k}(t_{1}^{k})\|\geqslant\varepsilon
for all

k\in\mathbb{N}
. Using (
5.5
) and condition (I) with
\varepsilon
instead of
c
and
2N
instead

, we can choose
\delta>0

such that

\|x_{k}(t_{2}^{k})-x_{k}(t_{1}^{k})\|\leqslant\|x_{k}(t_{2}^{k})-x_{k}(t_{1}^{k})\|-\delta+\|B_{k}x_{k}(t_{2}^{k})-B_{k}x_{k}(t_{1}^{k})\|

and taking the limit above as

, we derive that

, which is a contradiction.

Step 4.
From Step 1, Step 3, and Lemma
2.7
, we infer that the set

\{x_{k}(\cdot):k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively compact in the space
G(J,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Consequently,
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
contains a subsequence which is uniformly convergent on
J
.
Without loss of generality, we can denote this subsequence again by
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
x=\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}
. In particular,

x(t_{0})=\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}(t_{0})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\widehat{x}_{k}={x}_{0}.

Moreover, we point out that
h_{k}(t,x_{k}(t))\to h(t,x(t))
as
k\to\infty
uniformly on
J
. In fact,

\displaystyle\|h_{k}(t,x_{k}(t))-h(t,x(t))\|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|h_{k}(t,x_{k}(t))-h_{k}(t,x(t))\|+\|h_{k}(t,x(t))-h(t,x(t))\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|x_{k}(t)-x(t)\|+\|h_{k}(t,x(t))-h(t,x(t))\|

and the assertion is consequence of hypothesis (ii).
In addition,
{\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{k}(s,x_{k}(s)){\rm d}g\to\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f(s,x(s)){\rm d}g}
as
k\to\infty
.
Indeed, in the first place, we can see that

f_{k}(s,x_{k}(s))\to f(s,x(s))
as
k\to\infty
for
s\in J
. As a matter of fact,
gathering the hypothesis (iii) with the continuity of the function

, we obtain that

\|f_{k}(t,x_{k}(t))-f(t,x(t))\|\leqslant\|f_{k}(t,x_{k}(t))-f(t,x_{k}(t))\|+\|f(t,x_{k}(t))-f(t,x(t))\|\to 0,\;k\to\infty.

Furthermore, let
d=\{\sigma_{0},\ldots,\sigma_{l}\}
be a subdivision
of the interval
[t_{0},t_{0}+a]
and let
\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{l}\}
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}

applying our hypothesis (iii), we have

\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{\sigma_{j-1}}^{\sigma_{j}}f_{m_{j}}(s,x_{m_{j}}(s)){\rm d}g(s)\right\|\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{l}\int_{\sigma_{j-1}}^{\sigma_{j}}M_{m_{j}}(s){\rm d}g(s)<C.

Thus, from Theorem

, we obtain the assertion.

Finally, collecting the properties established above, taking limit in (
5.1
) as

x(t)=x_{0}-h(t_{0},x_{0})+h(t,x(t))+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f(s,x(s)){\rm d}g(s),\;\;t\in J,

which shows that
x(\cdot)
is a solution of problem HMDE (
1.1
)–(
1.2

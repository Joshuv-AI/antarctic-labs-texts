∞ \infty -type theories
Hoang Kim Nguyen, Taichi Uemura

Abstract
We introduce ∞ \infty -type theories as an ∞ \infty -categorical generalization of the categorical definition of type theories introduced by the second named author. We establish analogous results to the previous work including the construction of initial models of ∞ \infty -type theories, the construction of internal languages of models of ∞ \infty -type theories, and the theory-model correspondence for ∞ \infty -type theories. Some structured ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -categories are naturally regarded as models of some ∞ \infty -type theories. Thus, since every (1-categorical) type theory is in particular an ∞ \infty -type theory, ∞ \infty -type theories provide a unified framework for connections between type theories and ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -categorical structures. As an application we prove Kapulkin and Lumsdaine’s conjecture that the dependent type theory with intensional identity types gives internal languages for ( ∞ , 1 ) (\infty,1) -categories with finite limits.



1
Introduction

Type theory
and
higher category theory
are closely
related: dependent type theories with intensional identity types
provide a syntactic way of reasoning about

(\infty,1)
-categories. This is known as the family of

internal language conjectures
and has led for example to syntactic developments of classical material in homotopy theory such as the homotopy groups of spheres
(
Brunerie 2016
;
Brunerie 2019
;
Licata & Shulman 2013
)
and the Blakers-Massey Theorem
(
Hou (Favonia) et al. 2016
)
, just to name a few. These proofs often lead to new perspectives on classical material and their nature makes them applicable to a wider class of
(\infty,1)
-categories, importing ideas from the homotopy theory of spaces to other
(\infty,1)
-categories, see for example
(
Anel et al. 2018
)
and
(
Anel et al. 2020
)
. One of the main appeals of type theory for higher category theory and homotopy theory is thus the usage of this type theoretic language to reason in a synthetic way.
On the other hand, higher categories will be useful for the
study of type theories. For example, one can expect a conceptual proof
of Voevodsky’s homotopy canonicity conjecture that any closed term of the type of natural
numbers is homotopic to a numeral using a higher dimensional analogue of
the Freyd cover
(
Lambek & Scott 1986
)
.

However, internal language conjectures are still open problems in
homotopy type theory

(
The Univalent Foundations Program 2013
)
.
The advantage of type-theoretic languages, that a lot of equations
strictly
hold in type theories so that
a lot of trivial homotopies in
(\infty,1)
-categories can be
eliminated, is at the same time the main difficulty of
internal language conjectures. One has to justify interpreting strict
equality in type theories as homotopies in

(\infty,1)
-categories. This is an
\infty
-dimensional version
of the
coherence problem
in the categorical semantics of type
theories.

An internal language conjecture should be formulated as an equivalence
between an
(\infty,1)
-category of theories and an

(\infty,1)
-category of structured

(\infty,1)
-categories. Currently, only a few internal language
conjectures have been made precise.
Kapulkin & Lumsdaine 2018

made precise formulations of the simplest cases and conjectured that
the
(\infty,1)
-category of theories over Martin-Löf type
theory with intensional identity types (and dependent function types
with function extensionality) is equivalent to the

(\infty,1)
-category of small
(\infty,1)
-categories with finite
limits (and pushforwards). In this paper, we prove

Kapulkin & Lumsdaine 2018
’s conjecture by introducing a novel

\infty
-dimensional generalization of type theories which we call

\infty
-type theories
.

The basic strategy for proving
Kapulkin & Lumsdaine 2018
’s
conjecture is to decompose the equivalence to be proved into smaller
pieces. An existing approach is to introduce
1
-categorical
presentations of
(\infty,1)
-categories with finite limits. It had
already been shown by
Szumiło 2014
that

(\infty,1)
-categories with finite limits are equivalent to
categories of fibrant objects in the sense of

Brown 1973
.
Kapulkin & Szumiło 2019
then
proved that categories of fibrant objects are equivalent to

Joyal 2017
’s tribes

(
Joyal 2017
)
. Tribes are considered as
1
-categorical
models of the type theory, but a full proof of the equivalence between
tribes and theories has not yet been achieved.

Although this approach is natural for those who know the homotopical
interpretation of intensional identity types

(
Awodey & Warren 2009
;
Arndt & Kapulkin 2011
;
Shulman 2015
)
,

1
-categorical models of intensional identity types are not
convenient to work with. A problem is that
1
-categories of

1
-categorical models of type theories need not be rich enough to
calculate the
(\infty,1)
-categories they present. It is also
unclear if this approach can be generalized to internal language
conjectures for richer type theories.

In this paper we seek another path. The key idea is to introduce a
notion of
\infty
-type theories
, an
\infty
-dimensional
generalization of type theories. Intuitively, an
\infty
-type
theory is a kind of type theory but equality is like homotopies rather
than strict one. Ordinary type theories are considered as truncated

\infty
-type theories in the sense that all homotopies are
trivial.

Our proof strategy is as follows. Let
\mathbb{I}
denote the type theory
with intensional identity types. We introduce an
\infty
-type
theory
\mathbb{I}_{\infty}
which is analogous to
\mathbb{I}
but without
truncation. Because
\mathbb{I}_{\infty}
is already a higher dimensional
object, it is straightforward to interpret
\mathbb{I}_{\infty}
in

(\infty,1)
-categories with finite limits. The internal language
conjecture is then reduced to a coherence problem between
\mathbb{I}

and
\mathbb{I}_{\infty}
: how to interpret
\mathbb{I}
in models of

\mathbb{I}_{\infty}
. Although this coherence problem is as difficult as
the original internal language conjecture, this reduction is an
important step. Since the problem is now formulated in the language of

\infty
-type theories and related concepts, our proof strategy is
easily generalized to internal language conjectures for richer type
theories. When we extend
\mathbb{I}
by some type constructors, we just
extend
\mathbb{I}_{\infty}
in the same way.

A solution to coherence problems in the
1
-categorical semantics of
type theories given by
Hofmann 1995
is to
replace a “non-split” model, in which equality between types is up
to isomorphism, by an equivalent “split” model, in which equality
between types is strict. In our approach, models of
\mathbb{I}_{\infty}

are like non-split models of
\mathbb{I}
, so we consider replacing a
model of
\mathbb{I}_{\infty}
by an equivalent model of

\mathbb{I}
. Splitting techniques for
(\infty,1)
-categorical
structures have not yet been fully developed except for some
presentable
(\infty,1)
-categories considered by

Gepner & Kock 2017
;
Shulman 2019
. Since we have to
split small
(\infty,1)
-categories which are usually
non-presentable, their results cannot directly apply. However, as he
already mentioned in
(
Shulman 2019
, Remark 1.4)
,

Shulman 2019
’s result on splitting presentable

(\infty,1)
-toposes can be used for splitting small

(\infty,1)
-categories by embedding them into presheaf

(\infty,1)
-toposes.

Organization

In
Section

2
we fix notations and remember some
concepts in
(\infty,1)
-category theory. Relevant concepts to this
paper are
\infty
-cosmoi

(
Riehl & Verity 2022
)
,

compactly generated

(\infty,1)
-categories,

exponentiable
arrows, and
representable maps
between
right fibrations.

We introduce the notion of an
\infty
-type theory in

Section

3
. It is defined as an

(\infty,1)
-category with a certain structure, generalizing the
categorical definition of type theories introduced by the second named
author
(
Uemura 2019
)
. There are two important
notions around
\infty
-type theories:
models
and

theories
. The notion of models we have in mind is a
generalization of categories with families

(
Dybjer 1996
)
and, equivalently, natural models

(
Awodey 2018
;
Fiore 2012
)
. The notion of
theories is close to the essentially algebraic definitions of theories
given by

Garner 2015
;
Isaev 2018
;
Voevodsky 2014
.

In
Section

4
we prove
\infty
-analogue of the
main results of the previous work

(
Uemura 2019
)
. Given an
\infty
-type theory

\mathbb{T}
, we construct a functor that assigns to each model of

\mathbb{T}
a
\mathbb{T}
-theory called the
internal language
of the
model. The internal language functor has a fully faithful left adjoint
which constructs a
syntactic model
from a
\mathbb{T}
-theory. We
further characterize the image of the left adjoint.

We study some concrete
\infty
-type theories in

Section

5
. The most basic example is

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
, the
\infty
-analogue of Martin-Löf type
theory with
extensional
identity types. We show that

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-theories are equivalent to

(\infty,1)
-categories with finite limits (
Theorem

5.15
),
which is an
\infty
-analogue of the result of

Clairambault & Dybjer 2014
. This is to be an
intermediate step toward
Kapulkin & Lumsdaine 2018
’s
conjecture, but it also has an interesting corollary. One can derive a
new universal property of the
(\infty,1)
-category of small

(\infty,1)
-categories with finite limits from a universal property
of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
(
Corollary

5.21
). We also study a couple of
examples of
\infty
-type theories with dependent function
types. Finally in
Section

6
we prove

Kapulkin & Lumsdaine 2018
’s conjecture.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
\infty
-categories

For concreteness, we will work with
\infty
-categories
, also called
quasicategories
in the literature,
(
Joyal 2008
;
Lurie 2009a
;
Cisinski 2019
)
as models for
(\infty,1)
-categories. An
\infty
-category is a simplicial set satisfying certain horn filling conditions. We recollect some standard definitions and notations.

Definition 2.1
.

1.

Given an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
and a simplicial set
A
, we denote
\mathbf{Fun}(A,\mathcal{C})
the internal hom of simplicial sets, which is itself an
\infty
-category and models the
\infty
-category of functors and natural transformations.

2.

For an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\simeq}
the largest
\infty
-groupoid (Kan complex) contained in
\mathcal{C}
. Furthermore we write
\mathrm{k}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}):=\mathbf{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})^{\simeq}
.

3.

For an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
C^{\triangleright}
the join
\mathcal{C}\star\Delta^{0}
.

4.

We denote by
\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}
the
\infty
-category of small
\infty
-categories. This is obtained as the homotopy coherent nerve of the simplicial category with objects given by small
\infty
-categories and hom simplicial sets given by
\mathrm{k}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
.

5.

We denote by
\mathbf{CAT}_{\infty}
the
\infty
-category of (possibly large)
\infty
-categories obtained in a similar way.

6.

We denote by
\mathbf{S}
the
\infty
-category of small
\infty
-groupoids obtained as the homotopy coherent nerve of the simplicial category with objects small Kan complexes and hom simplicial sets given by the internal hom of simplicial sets.

Although we chose to work with
\infty
-categories, we will primarily use the language of the formal category theory of
\infty
-categories as expressed by
\infty
-cosmoi. Therefore, most of our constructions, statements and proofs are independent of the model.

2.2
\infty
-cosmoi

An
\infty
-cosmos

(
Riehl & Verity 2022
)
is, roughly,
a complete
(\infty,2)
-category with enough structure to do formal
category theory. More concretely, an
\infty
-cosmos
\mathcal{K}
is
a simplicially enriched category such that for any pair of objects

\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{K}
, the hom simplicial set

\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
is an
\infty
-category.
\mathcal{K}
is also
equipped a class of morphisms called isofibrations, and all small

(\infty,1)
-categorical limits are constructible from products and
pullbacks of isofibrations. Moreover,
\mathcal{K}
has cotensors with
small simplicial sets
A\pitchfork\mathcal{C}
characterized by the
equivalence (isomorphism, in fact) of
\infty
-categories

\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{D},A\pitchfork\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathbf{Fun}(A,\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{C})).

Given an
\infty
-category, we may take its
homotopy category
, which is just an ordinary category. Applying this to the hom spaces of an
\infty
-cosmos gives rise to a 2-category. Adjunctions and equivalences in
\infty
-cosmoi are then defined in the usual way using this 2-category.

Example 2.2
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{CAT}_{\infty}
the
\infty
-cosmos of (possibly large)
\infty
-categories. That is,
\mathfrak{CAT}_{\infty}
is the simplicial category with objects (possibly large)
\infty
-categories and hom simplicial sets
\mathbf{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
. The cotensor
A\pitchfork\mathcal{C}
in
\mathfrak{CAT}_{\infty}
is given by the functor
\infty
-category
\mathbf{Fun}(A,\mathcal{C})
and adjunctions and equivalences agree with the standard notions of
\infty
-categories.

Cartesian fibrations and right fibrations in
\infty
-cosmoi are
characterized by analogy with those in complete 2-categories. Here we
prefer to work with versions of these concepts that are invariant
under equivalence. The following definition coincides with

Riehl & Verity 2022
’s when
F
is an isofibration.

Definition 2.3
.

A functor
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
in an
\infty
-cosmos
\mathcal{K}
is said to be a
cartesian fibration
if the functor

(\mathrm{ev}_{1},F_{\ast})\colon\Delta^{1}\pitchfork\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}\times_{\mathcal{D}}\Delta^{1}\pitchfork\mathcal{D}

has a right adjoint with invertible counit. A
fibred functor

between cartesian fibrations is a morphism in
\mathcal{K}^{\to}

that commutes with the right adjoint of
(\mathrm{ev}_{1},F_{\ast})
. A
cartesian fibration is a
right fibration
if

(\mathrm{ev}_{1},F_{\ast})
is an equivalence.

For a small
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by

\mathbf{CartFib}_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}/\mathcal{C}
the
\infty
-category of
cartesian fibrations over
\mathcal{C}
and fibred functors over

\mathcal{C}
. We denote by
\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbf{CartFib}_{\mathcal{C}}
the
full subcategory spanned by the right fibrations over
\mathcal{C}
. Note
that any functor between right fibrations over
\mathcal{C}
is
automatically a fibred functor, so
\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a full
subcategory of
\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}/\mathcal{C}
. We
write
\mathbf{RFib}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\infty}^{\to}
for the full subcategory
spanned by the right fibrations.

2.3
Compactly generated
\infty
-categories

Definition 2.4

(
Lurie 2009a
)
.

An
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be

compactly generated
if it is an

\omega
-accessible localization of
\mathbf{Fun}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{S})
,
that is, a reflective full subcategory of
\mathbf{Fun}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{S})
closed under filtered colimits,
for some small
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
. The subcategory of
\mathbf{CAT}_{\infty}
spanned by the compactly generated
\infty
-categories and
\omega
-accessible right adjoints is denoted by
\mathbf{Pr}^{R}_{\omega}
. We will moreover denote by
\mathfrak{Pr}^{R}_{\omega}\subset\mathfrak{CAT}_{\infty}
the locally full subcategory spanned by the compactly
generated
\infty
-categories and
\omega
-accessible right
adjoints.

Recall
(
Lurie 2009a
, Proposition 5.5.7.6)
that
\mathbf{Pr}^{R}_{\omega}\subset\mathbf{CAT}_{\infty}
is closed under small limits. By definition, compactly generated
\infty
-categories are closed in
\mathfrak{CAT}_{\infty}
under cotensors with small simplicial sets. Hence, the subcategory

\mathfrak{Pr}^{R}_{\omega}\subset\mathfrak{CAT}_{\infty}
is an
\infty
-cosmos, and the inclusion

\mathfrak{Pr}^{R}_{\omega}\to\mathfrak{CAT}_{\infty}
preserves the structures of

\infty
-cosmoi and reflects equivalences.

Example 2.5
.

The
\infty
-category
\mathbf{S}
of small spaces is compactly
generated. The
\infty
-category
\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}
of small

\infty
-categories is compactly generated, and the functor

\mathrm{k}(\Delta^{n},{-}):\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}\to\mathbf{S}
sending an

\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
to the space of
n
-cells of
\mathcal{C}

is an
\omega
-accessible right adjoint. This is because

\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}
is regarded as an
\omega
-accessible
localization of
\mathbf{Fun}(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{S})
using the equivalence
of quasicategories and complete Segal spaces

(
Joyal & Tierney 2007
)
.

Example 2.6
.

For a small
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, the
\infty
-category

\mathbf{CartFib}_{\mathcal{C}}
of cartesian fibrations over
\mathcal{C}
and fibred
functors over
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated as

\mathbf{CartFib}_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq\mathbf{Fun}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty})
. The
forgetful functor
\mathbf{CartFib}_{\mathcal{C}}\to\mathbf{Cat}_{\infty}/\mathcal{C}
is an

\omega
-accessible right adjoint. To see this, observe that this
forgetful functor is the right derived functor of the forgetful
functor

\mathbf{SSet}^{+}/\mathcal{C}^{\sharp}\to\mathbf{SSet}/\mathcal{C}

which is a right Quillen functor with respect to the cartesian model
structure and the slice model structure of the Joyal model structure
on
\mathbf{SSet}
(
Lurie 2009a
, Proposition 3.1.5.2)
or
(
Nguyen 2019
, Proposition
3.1.18)
. The functor

\mathbf{SSet}^{+}/\mathcal{C}^{\sharp}\to\mathbf{SSet}/\mathcal{C}
preserves filtered
colimits, and filtered colimits are homotopy colimits in both model
structures, from which it follows that the right derived functor
preserves filtered colimits.

For a compactly generated

3
\infty
-type theories

We introduce notions of an
\infty
-type theory
, a

theory
over an
\infty
-type theory and a
model
of an

\infty
-type theory, translating the previous work of the second
author
(
Uemura 2019
)
into the language of

\infty
-categories. The idea is to extend the functorial semantics
of algebraic theories
(
Lawvere 1963
)
. Algebraic
theories are identified with categories with finite products, and
models of an algebraic theory are identified with functors into the
category of sets preserving finite products. For type theories, it is
natural to identify models of a type theory with functors into
presheaf categories, because (extensions of) natural models

(
Awodey 2018
)
and categories with families

(
Dybjer 1996
)
are diagrams in presheaf
categories. Since representable maps of presheaves play a special role
in the natural model semantics, some arrows in the source category
should be specified to be sent to representable maps. This motivates
the following definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

An
\infty
-category with representable maps
is a pair

(\mathcal{C},R)
where
\mathcal{C}
is an
\infty
-category and

R\subseteq k(\Delta^{1},\mathcal{C})
is a subspace of the space of arrows
of
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the conditions below. Arrows in
R
are
called
representable arrows
.

1.

\mathcal{C}
has finite limits.

2.

All the identities are representable and representable arrows
are closed under composition.

3.

Representable arrows are stable under pullbacks.

4.

Representable arrows are exponentiable.

A
morphism of
\infty
-categories with representable maps

is a functor preserving representable arrows, finite limits and
pushforwards along representable arrows.

Example 3.2
.

For a small
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, the
\infty
-category

\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{C}}
of small right fibrations over
\mathcal{C}
is an

\infty
-category with representable maps in which a map is
representable if it has a right adjoint.

Definition 3.3
.

An
\infty
-type theory
is an
\infty
-category with
representable maps whose underlying
\infty
-category is small. A

morphism of
\infty
-type theories
is a morphism of

\infty
-categories with representable maps.
By an

-type theory
for

, we mean an

\infty
-type theory whose underlying
\infty
-category is an

n
-category.

Example 3.4
.

The type theories in the sense of the previous work

(
Uemura 2019
)
are the
1
-type theories.

.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an
\infty
-type theory.

•

\infty
-category

is the composite

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{M}({\star})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{S}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1^{*}}

where

is the fiber over

1\in\mathcal{M}({\star})
for a right fibration

over

\mathcal{M}({\star})
. As the functor

lies in

, it has a left adjoint

.

Definition 4.1
.

For a model
\mathcal{M}
of

\mathbb{T}
, the
\mathbb{T}
-theory

is called the

.

, we call

-theory

.

In this section, we prove the following:

is

is

2.

is the class of democratic models
of

defined below.

Consequently, the adjunction
\mathcal{F}\dashv\mathrm{L}
induces an equivalence
between
\mathbf{Th}(\mathbb{T})
and the full subcategory of
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbb{T})

spanned by the democratic models of
\mathbb{T}
. We define the notion of

. The components of
the unit

are completely determined by the

\mathbb{T}
-theories

, because

is the cocompletion of

\mathbb{T}^{\mathrm{op}}
under filtered colimits and the right adjoint

\mathrm{L}:\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbb{T})\to\mathbf{Th}(\mathbb{T})
preserves filtered colimits. We
thus study in details the syntactic model generated by a representable

\mathbb{T}
-theory. In

we concretely describe

\mathbb{T}
which is the syntactic model generated

-theory

. We then

to a description of the

\mathbb{T}
-theory. Finally we prove the main results in

Section

.

4.1
Democratic models

For a model
\mathcal{M}
of an
\infty
-type theory, we think of an
object
\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}({\star})
as a context (see

Example

3.6
), but contexts from the syntax of type theory
satisfy an additional property: every context is obtained from the
empty context by context comprehension. A model of an
\infty
-type
theory satisfying this property is said to be
democratic
,
generalizing the notion of a democratic category with families

(
Clairambault & Dybjer 2014
)
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a model of
\mathbb{T}
,
u:x\to y
a
representable arrow in
\mathbb{T}
,
\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}({\star})
an object
and
b:\mathcal{M}({\star})/\Gamma\to\mathcal{M}(y)
a section. Let

\delta_{u}:\mathcal{M}(y)\to\mathcal{M}(x)
be the right
adjoint of
\mathcal{M}(u)
. Then the counit

\mathrm{p}_{u}(b):\mathcal{M}(u)(\delta_{u}(b))\to b
is a pullback square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}({\star})/\{b\}_{u}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}({\star})/\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(y).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{u}(b)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{p}_{u}(b)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(u)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}

We refer to the object
\{b\}_{u}
the
context
comprehension of
b
with respect to
u
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a model of
\mathbb{T}
. The class of
contextual
objects of
\mathcal{M}
is the smallest replete class of objects of

\mathcal{M}({\star})
containing the terminal object and closed under
context comprehension.

In other words, the contextual objects of

\mathcal{M}
are inductively defined as follows:

•

the terminal object
1\in\mathcal{M}({\star})
is
contextual;

•

if
\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}({\star})
is a contextual object,

u:x\to y
is a representable arrow in
\mathbb{T}
and

b:\mathcal{M}({\star})/\Gamma\to\mathcal{M}(y)
is a section, then
the context comprehension
\{b\}_{u}
is contextual;

•

if
\Gamma\in\mathcal{M}({\star})
is a contextual object and

\Gamma\simeq\Delta
, then
\Delta
is contextual.

Definition 4.4
.

We call a model
\mathcal{M}

democratic
if all the objects of

\mathcal{M}({\star})
are contextual. We denote by
\mathbf{Mod}^{\mathrm{dem}}(\mathbb{T})
the
full subcategory of
\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbb{T})
spanned by the democratic models.

One can always find a largest democratic model contained in an
arbitrary model of
\mathbb{T}
.

Definition 4.5
.

For a model
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathbb{T}
, we define a model

\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}
of
\mathbb{T}
called the
heart of

\mathcal{M}
as follows:

•

the base
\infty
-category
\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}({\star})
is
the full subcategory of
\mathcal{M}({\star})
spanned by the contextual
objects;

•

the functor

\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(*)}

is the composite with the pullback along the inclusion

\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}({\star})\to\mathcal{M}({\star})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{M}({\star})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}({\star})}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}}

\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}
is indeed a model of
\mathbb{T}
, and the
inclusion
\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a
morphism of models of
\mathbb{T}
. By definition, the functor

\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(*)}

preserves finite limits. Since
\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}({\star})
is
closed under context comprehension, for a representable arrow

u:x\to y
in
\mathbb{T}
, the composite

\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(y)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}(y)\overset{\delta_{u}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{M}(x)
factors
through
\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(x)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}(x)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(x).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta_{u}}

This means that
\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(u):\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(x)\to\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(y)
has a
right adjoint, and the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(u)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}(u)}

satisfies the Beck-Chevalley condition. Since the pushforward along

\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}(u)
is given by the pullback along its right
adjoint
\delta_{u}
, we see that

\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathcal{M}^{\heartsuit}({\star})}

preserves pushforwards along representable maps.

Proposition 4.6
.

For a democratic model
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathbb{T}
and an arbitrary model

\mathcal{N}
of
\mathbb{T}
, the inclusion

\mathcal{N}^{\heartsuit}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{N}
induces an
equivalence of spaces

\mathbf{Mod}^{\mathrm{dem}}(\mathbb{T})(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}^{\heartsuit})\simeq\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbb{T})(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}).

In other words,
({-})^{\heartsuit}
is a right adjoint of the
inclusion
\mathbf{Mod}^{\mathrm{dem}}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbb{T})
.

Proof.

Because any morphism of models of
\mathbb{T}
preserves contextual
objects, any morphism
\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
from a democratic
model
\mathcal{M}
factors through
\mathcal{N}^{\heartsuit}
.
∎

4.2
The initial model

Definition 4.7
.

Recall that the Yoneda embedding

to be the heart of the

The goal of this subsection is to show that

By definition, the model
\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{T})

•

\infty

•

along the

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}_{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{RFib}_{\mathbb{T}_{r}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{T})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

.

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G({\star})}

4.4
The equivalence of theories and democratic models

Proposition 4.10
.

The unit of the adjunction

\mathcal{F}\dashv\mathrm{L}:\mathbf{Th}(\mathbb{T})\to\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbb{T})
is
invertible. Consequently, the left adjoint

is fully faithful.

Proof.

Since both functors
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathrm{L}
preserves filtered colimits,
it suffices to show that the unit

\eta_{K}:K\to\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{F}(K))
is invertible
for every representable functor

. From
the description of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{y}(x))
in

Section

4.3
, we have that

\mathrm{L}(\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{y}(x)))(y)\simeq\mathbb{T}(x,y)=\mathbf{y}(x)(y)
and
\eta_{\mathbf{y}(x)}
is just the
identity.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

The functor
\mathcal{F}:\mathbf{Th}(\mathbb{T})\to\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbb{T})
factors through

\mathbf{Mod}^{\mathrm{dem}}(\mathbb{T})\subset\mathbf{Mod}(\mathbb{T})

Proof.

Since

is a coreflective

is

in

4.3
.
∎

5
Correspondence between type-theoretic structures and categorical structures

We discuss a correspondence between type-theoretic structures and
categorical structures. Given an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
whose
objects are small
\infty
-categories equipped with a certain
structure and morphisms are structure-preserving functors, we try to
find an
\infty
-type theory
\mathbb{T}
such that

\mathbf{Th}(\mathbb{T})\simeq\mathcal{C}
. Such an
\infty
-type theory
\mathbb{T}
can
be understood in a couple of ways. Type-theoretically,
\mathbb{T}

provides
internal languages
for
\infty
-categories in

\mathcal{C}
. We will find type-theoretic structures corresponding to
categorical structures like finite limits and
pushforwards. Categorically,
\mathbb{T}
gives a
presentation
of
the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
as a localization of a presheaf

\infty
-category. Such a presentation has the advantage that the

\infty
-type theory
\mathbb{T}
often has a simple universal property
from which one can derive a universal property of
\mathcal{C}
(see

Corollary

5.21
for example).

The fundamental example of such an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is

\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{Lex}_{\infty}
, the
\infty
-category of small left exact

\infty
-categories. In
Section

5.3
, we introduce an

\infty
-type theory
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
which is an

\infty
-analogue of Martin-Löf type theory with extensional identity
types. The main result of this section is to establish an equivalence

\mathbf{Th}(\mathbb{E}_{\infty})\simeq\mathbf{Lex}_{\infty}
, and this is a higher analogue of the
result of
Clairambault & Dybjer 2014
. To do this, we
need two preliminaries: one is the
representable map classifier

of right fibrations over a left exact
\infty
-category
(
Section

5.1
) which is used for constructing a
democratic model of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
out of a left exact

\infty
-category; the other is the notion of
univalence
in

\infty
-categories with representable maps (
Section

5.2
)
which for example makes a type constructor unique up to contractible
choice. We also give two other examples
\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{LCCC}_{\infty}
, the

\infty
-category of small locally cartesian closed

\infty
-categories (
Section

5.4
), and

\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{TT}_{\infty}
, the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-type theories
(
Section

5.5
). The latter example shows that the
notion of
\infty
-type theories itself can be written in the

\infty
-type-theoretic language.

because of the following

We end this section with discussion about generalizations of

this approach, coherence problems are solved by rewriting techniques,

\infty
-type theories, and this is not
obvious.

7
Conclusion and future work

We introduced
\infty
-type theories as a higher dimensional
generalization of type theories and as an application proved

Kapulkin & Lumsdaine 2018
’s conjecture that the

\infty
-category of small left exact
\infty
-categories is a localization
of the category of theories over Martin-Löf type theory with
intensional identity types
(
Kapulkin & Lumsdaine 2018
)
. The
technique developed in this paper also works for the internal language
conjecture for locally cartesian closed
\infty
-categories, but further
generalization including (higher) inductive types is left as future
work.

7.1
Syntax for
\infty
-type theories

Coherence problems are often solved by syntactic arguments

(
Curien 1993
)
. Therefore, syntactic presentations
of
\infty
-type theories will be helpful for solving internal
language conjectures for structured
\infty
-categories. We have not
figured out syntax for
\infty
-type theories. Here we consider one
possibility based on
logical frameworks
.

In the previous work
(
Uemura 2019
)
, the author
introduced a logical framework to define type theories. For every
signature
\Sigma
in that logical framework, the syntactic category

\mathbb{R}(\Sigma)
is naturally equipped with a structure of a category
with representable maps and satisfies a certain universal property. To
define
\infty
-type theories syntactically, we modify the logical
framework as follows:

•

the new logical framework has intensional identity types instead
of extensional identity types;

•

dependent product types indexed over representable types satisfy
the function extensionality axiom.

Remark 7.1
.

A similar kind of framework is used by
Bocquet 2020
to represent space-valued models of a type
theory.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a signature in this new logical framework.

1.

The syntactic category
\mathbb{R}(\Sigma)
is
equipped with a structure of a fibration category.

2.

The localization
L(\mathbb{R}(\Sigma))
is
equipped with a structure of an
\infty
-category with
representable maps.

Proof.

It is known
(
Avigad et al. 2015
)
that the syntactic
category of a type theory with intensional identity types is a
fibration category. The second claim is proved in the same way as
the fact that the localization of a locally cartesian closed
fibration category is a locally cartesian closed
\infty
-category

(
Kapulkin 2017
;
Cisinski 2019
)
.
∎

We expect that the syntactic
\infty
-category with representable maps

L(\mathbb{R}(\Sigma))
satisfies a universal property analogous to

(
Uemura 2019
, Theorem 5.17)
so that the logical
framework with intensional identity types provides syntactic
presentations of
\infty
-type theories.

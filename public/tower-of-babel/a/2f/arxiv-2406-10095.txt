Calculation of the chiral Lagrangian coefficients with light vector mesons
Zi-Kan Geng 1 , Qing Wang 1,2

Abstract
We calculate the coefficients in the effective chiral Lagrangian from QCD, which includes pseudo-scalar mesons and vector mesons (with hidden symmetry), up to 𝒪 ⁡ ( p 4 ) \mathcal{O}(p^{4}) . This encompasses both the normal and anomalous parts. Our work builds on a previous study that derived the chiral Lagrangian from first principles of QCD, where the low-energy coefficients are defined in terms of specific Green’s functions in QCD. This research extends our earlier efforts that focused on calculating the low-energy coefficients of the chiral Lagrangian for pure pseudo-scalar mesons. This marks the first calculation of chiral Lagrangian coefficients for vector mesons from QCD, particularly for the important parameters a a and g g , which are typically considered inputs in existing literature. Notably, the regularization method used previously is inadequate for this broader scope. We find that cut-off regularization yields reasonable results for both pseudo-scalar mesons and vector mesons, though it has certain limitations. Finally, we demonstrate that our method aligns with the Weinberg sum rules.

Although our calculation for the LECs of pseudo-scalar mesons was successful, extending these calculations to vector mesons is nontrivial. The key difference lies in the introduction of HLS and the unique properties of vector mesons. Pseudo-scalar mesons are treated as Goldstone bosons following spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking. In the HLS model, vector mesons are considered gauge fields, with their masses arising from the Higgs mechanism. In previous papers
[
24
,
36
]
, we used Schwinger proper time regularization to calculate the LECs for pseudo-scalar mesons, yielding results that matched those in the literature
[
37
]
and appeared reasonable
[
24
,
25
]
. However, when this regularization was applied to the HLS model to calculate the LECs for vector mesons, the decay constant for vector mesons significantly deviated from experimental values. We explored the impact of different regularizations on the
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})

\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
LECs are insensitive to the regularizations, and there was minimal change in the LECs for pseudo-scalar mesons. References
[
38
,
39

also used cut-off regularization to calculate the LECs in the chiral Lagrangian for heavy-light mesons, obtaining the reasonable results. This suggests that cut-off regularization may be suitable for higher energy regions. In summary, the
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
LECs are sensitive to regularizations, and Schwinger proper time regularization, previously used, is inadequate for vector mesons. While the new regularization method aligns with empirical data, it still presents some issues. Nonetheless, we demonstrate that our method is consistent with the Weinberg sum rules
[
40
]
at the end of this article.

It is worth noting that no new parameters are introduced for vector mesons in this paper, even though the number of LECs is larger than those for pure pseudo-scalar mesons. The parameters in the calculation are entirely fixed by pseudo-scalar mesons, as in Refs.

25

II
The hidden local symmetry model of Vector Mesons

The hidden local symmetry model describing the vector mesons is based on the
G_{\text{global}}\times H_{\text{local}}
symmetry, where
G=SU(3)_{L}\times SU(3)_{R}
is the global chiral symmetry of the ChPT and
H=SU(3)_{V}
is the HLS. The entire symmetry is spontaneously broken down to a diagonal sum
H
which is the flavor symmetry. The gauge bosons of HLS acquire masses by the Higgs mechanism, and are then identified as the light vector mesons,
\rho
meson and its flavor partners.

In the HLS model, the building block
U
in the ChPT is split into two variables
\xi_{L}
and
\xi_{R}
:

U=\xi_{R}^{\dagger}\xi_{L}.

(1)

The ambiguity in this split is known as the hidden local symmetry,
H_{local}
. Under the chiral symmetry and the HLS, these two variables transfrom as

\xi_{L,R}\to\xi^{\prime}_{L,R}=h(x)\cdot\xi_{L,R}(x)\cdot g^{\dagger}_{L,R},

(2)

where
h(x)\in[SU(3)_{V}]_{local},~g_{L,R}\in[SU(3)_{L}\times SU(3)_{R}]_{global}.
These variables are parameterized as

\xi_{L,R}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\sigma/F_{\sigma}}\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\pi/F_{\pi}},~~~\pi=\pi^{a}T_{a},~\sigma=\sigma^{a}T_{a},

(3)

where
\pi
denote the Goldstone bosons associated with the spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking and
\sigma
denote the Goldstone bosons associated with the spontaneous breaking of HLS which are absorbed into the gauge bosons.

When gauging the chiral symmetry, we can construct two important building blocks that are chiral invariant and covariant under HLS:

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{\bot\mu}

\displaystyle=(D_{\mu}\xi_{R}\cdot\xi_{R}^{\dagger}-D_{\mu}\xi_{L}\cdot\xi_{L}^{\dagger})/(2\mathrm{i}),

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{\parallel\mu}

\displaystyle=(D_{\mu}\xi_{R}\cdot\xi_{R}^{\dagger}+D_{\mu}\xi_{L}\cdot\xi_{L}^{\dagger})/(2\mathrm{i}),

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{\bot\mu}

\displaystyle\to h(x)\hat{\alpha}_{\bot\mu}h^{\dagger}(x),~\hat{\alpha}_{\parallel\mu}\to h(x)\hat{\alpha}_{\parallel\mu}h^{\dagger}(x).

The covariant derivatives are defined as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\xi_{L}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\xi_{L}-\mathrm{i}V_{\mu}\xi_{L}+\mathrm{i}\xi_{L}\mathcal{L}_{\mu},

(5)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\xi_{R}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\xi_{R}-\mathrm{i}V_{\mu}\xi_{R}+\mathrm{i}\xi_{R}\mathcal{R}_{\mu},

where
V_{\mu}=g\rho_{\mu}
are the gauge fields of HLS,
g
is the coupling of HLS, and
\mathcal{L}_{\mu},\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
are the external gauge fields of the chiral symmetry. These gauge fields transform as

\displaystyle V_{\mu}

\displaystyle\to h(x)(V_{\mu}+\mathrm{i}\partial_{\mu})h^{\dagger}(x),

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\to g_{L}(\mathcal{L}_{\mu}+\mathrm{i}\partial_{\mu})g^{\dagger}_{L},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\to g_{R}(\mathcal{R}_{\mu}+\mathrm{i}\partial_{\mu})g^{\dagger}_{R}.

The order assignment of the new variables is

\xi_{L}\sim\xi_{R}\sim\mathcal{O}(1),~~~V_{\mu}\sim g\sim\mathcal{O}(p).

(7)

The leading order Lagrangian is given by
[
10
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{A}+\mathcal{L}_{\chi}+a\mathcal{L}_{V}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{kin}}(V_{\mu})

(8)

\displaystyle=F^{2}_{\pi}\mathrm{tr}[\hat{\alpha}_{\bot\mu}\hat{\alpha}^{\mu}_{\bot}]+\frac{F_{\pi}^{2}}{4}\mathrm{tr}[\hat{\chi}+\hat{\chi}^{\dagger}]+F^{2}_{\sigma}\mathrm{tr}[\hat{\alpha}_{\parallel\mu}\hat{\alpha}^{\mu}_{\parallel}]-\frac{1}{2g^{2}}\mathrm{tr}[V_{\mu\nu}V^{\mu\nu}],

where
\hat{\chi}
is the adjoint representation of the HLS for external source field
\chi
using the ”converters”
\xi_{L}
and
\xi_{R}
as
\hat{\chi}=\xi_{R}\chi\xi_{L}^{\dagger}=2B_{0}\xi_{R}(s+\mathrm{i}p)\xi_{L}^{\dagger}
, and
V_{\mu\nu}
is the gauge field strength of the HLS gauge boson defined by

V_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}V_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}V_{\mu}-\mathrm{i}[V_{\mu},V_{\nu}].

(9)

When the parameter
a\equiv F_{\sigma}^{2}/F_{\pi}^{2}
is specifically chosen as 2, the above Lagrangian can reproduce several phenomenological facts, such as the universality of the
\rho
-coupling
g_{\rho\pi\pi}=g
, the
\rho
meson dominance of the pion’s electromagnetic form factor
g_{\gamma\pi\pi}=0
and the KSRF formulas
[
41
,
42
]
:

g_{\rho}=2F_{\pi}^{2}g_{\rho\pi\pi},~~~m_{\rho}^{2}=2g^{2}_{\rho\pi\pi}F_{\pi}^{2}.

(10)

Similarly to the square of the pseudoscalar meson mass, HLS model assigns
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
to the square of the vector meson mass:
m_{\rho}^{2}\sim\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
, which is inconsistent with experimental values. However, it is emphasized in the Section 4.1 of Ref.
[
10
]
that, “thanks to the gauge invariance, the HLS makes possible the systematic expansion including the vector meson loops, particularly when the vector meson mass is light. It turns out that such a limit can actually be realized in QCD when the number of massless flavors
n_{f}
becomes large as was demonstrated in Refs.
[
48
,
49
]
. Then one can perform the derivative expansion with including the vector mesons under such an extreme condition where the vector meson masses are small, and extrapolate the results to the real world
n_{f}=3
where the vector meson masses take the experimental values.” The detailed discussion on the power counting in the HLS model can be found in Ref.
[
10
]
. The
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
Lagrangian in HLS model can be found in the Appendix
A
.

III
chiral effective Lagrangian including light vector mesons from QCD

In this section, we review our previous derivation of the effective chiral
Lagrangian with vector mesons from QCD
[
28
]
. The start point of the derivation is the generating functional of QCD, with an external source
J(x)
introduced for the composite light quark fields, given in Ref.
[
23
]
:

\displaystyle Z[J]

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi\exp\left\{\mathrm{i}\int d^{4}x[\bar{\psi}(i\not{\partial}+J)\psi]\right\}\int\mathcal{D}\Psi\mathcal{D}\bar{\Psi}\mathcal{D}A_{\mu}\Delta_{F}(A_{\mu})

(11)

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{\mathrm{i}\int d^{4}x\left[\mathcal{L}_{QCD}(A)-\frac{1}{2\xi}[F^{i}(A_{\mu})]^{2}-g\mathcal{I}_{i}^{\mu}A_{\mu}^{i}+\bar{\Psi}(\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}-M-g\not{A})\Psi\right]\right\},

The definitions and conventions for fields in Eq. (
11
) are the same as those in Ref.
[
23
]
. After integrating out the quark and gluon fields, and integrating in the bilocal auxiliary field
\Phi^{\sigma\rho}(x,y)\sim(1/N_{c})\bar{\psi}_{\alpha}^{\sigma}(x)\psi_{\alpha}^{\rho}(y)
, the result is

Z[J]=\int\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{0}[J,\Phi,\Pi]},

(12)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}[J,\Phi,\Pi]

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}N_{c}\mathrm{Tr}\ln[\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}+J-\Pi]+N_{c}\int d^{4}xd^{4}x^{\prime}\Phi^{\sigma\rho}(x,x^{\prime})\Pi^{\sigma\rho}(x,x^{\prime})

(13)

\displaystyle+N_{c}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\int d^{4}x_{1}\cdots d^{4}x_{n}d^{4}x^{\prime}_{1}\cdots d^{4}x^{\prime}_{n}\frac{(-\mathrm{i})^{n}(N_{c}g^{2})^{n-1}}{n!}

\displaystyle\times\bar{G}^{\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{n}}_{\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{n}}(x_{1},x^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n})\Phi^{\sigma_{1}\rho_{1}}(x_{1},x^{\prime}_{1})\cdots\Phi^{\sigma_{n}\rho_{n}}(x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n}).

The detail of this derivation can be found in Ref.
[
23
]
.

Further by integrating in the local pseudo-scalar mesons and vector meson fields, and integrating in the corresponding auxiliary field, the result can be expressed as

Z=\int\mathcal{D}\xi_{R}\mathcal{D}\xi_{L}\mathcal{D}V\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi\delta(\xi_{L}^{\dagger}\xi_{L}-1)\delta(\xi_{R}^{\dagger}\xi_{R}-1)\delta(\det\xi_{R}-\det\xi_{L})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]},

(14)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]=\Gamma_{0}[J,\Phi,\Pi]+\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{I}[\Phi]

(15)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}N_{c}\int d^{4}x\mathrm{tr}_{f}\left\{\Xi\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{N_{f}}}\xi_{R}\mathrm{tr}_{l}(P_{R}\Phi^{T})\xi_{L}^{\dagger}-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{N_{f}}}\xi_{L}\mathrm{tr}_{l}(P_{L}\Phi^{T})\xi_{R}^{\dagger}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}N_{c}\int d^{4}x\left\{V_{\mu}^{ab}+\left[(\xi_{L}P_{R}+\xi_{R}P_{L})\left(\frac{\Pi}{4{\mu}^{4}}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}}{4}\right)(\xi^{\dagger}_{R}P_{R}+\xi^{\dagger}_{L}P_{L})\right]^{(a\xi)(b\zeta)}\gamma_{\mu}^{\zeta\xi}\right\}\tilde{V}^{\mu,ba}.

After formally finishing the integration over the auxiliary fields
\Phi,\Pi,\Xi
and
\tilde{V}
, we obtain

Z=\int\mathcal{D}\xi_{R}\mathcal{D}\xi_{L}\mathcal{D}V\delta(\xi_{L}^{\dagger}\xi_{L}-1)\delta(\xi_{R}^{\dagger}\xi_{R}-1)\delta(\det\xi_{R}-\det\xi_{L})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}S_{\text{eff}}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi_{c},\tilde{V}_{c},\Phi_{c},\Pi_{c}]},

(16)

where

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}S_{\text{eff}}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi_{c},\tilde{V}_{c},\Phi_{c},\Pi_{c}]}=\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}.

(17)

The fields
\Xi_{c},\tilde{V}_{c},\Phi_{c},\Pi_{c}
are the corresponding classical field defined by

\displaystyle\Phi_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi~\Phi~\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}}{\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}},

(18)

\displaystyle\Pi_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi~\Pi~\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}}{\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}},

\displaystyle\Xi_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi~\Xi~\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}}{\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}},

\displaystyle\tilde{V}^{\mu}_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi~\tilde{V}^{\mu}~\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}}{\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi\mathcal{D}\Pi\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[\xi_{R},\xi_{L},V,J,\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi,\Pi]}},

which satisfy the classical field equations:

\frac{\partial S_{\text{eff}}}{\partial\Phi^{\sigma\rho}_{c}}=\frac{\partial S_{\text{eff}}}{\partial\Pi^{\sigma\rho}_{c}}=\frac{\partial S_{\text{eff}}}{\partial\Xi^{\sigma\rho}_{c}}=\frac{\partial S_{\text{eff}}}{\partial\tilde{V}^{\mu}_{c}}=0.

(19)

To simplify the result, we define the rotated source and fields denoted with subscript
\xi
:

\displaystyle J_{\xi}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv[\xi_{L}(x)P_{R}+\xi_{R}(x)P_{L}][J(x)+\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}][\xi^{\dagger}_{R}(x)P_{R}+\xi^{\dagger}_{L}(x)P_{L}],

(20)

\displaystyle\Phi^{T}_{\xi}(x,y)

\displaystyle\equiv[\xi_{R}(x)P_{R}+\xi_{L}(x)P_{L}]\Phi^{T}(x,y)[\xi^{\dagger}_{L}(y)P_{R}+\xi^{\dagger}_{R}(y)P_{L}],

\displaystyle\Pi^{T}_{\xi}(x,y)

\displaystyle\equiv[\xi_{L}(x)P_{R}+\xi_{R}(x)P_{L}]\Pi^{T}(x,y)[\xi^{\dagger}_{R}(y)P_{R}+\xi^{\dagger}_{L}(y)P_{L}],

\displaystyle V^{\mu}_{\xi}

\displaystyle\equiv V^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}[\xi_{R}\mathrm{i}\partial^{\mu}\xi_{R}^{\dagger}+\xi_{L}\mathrm{i}\partial^{\mu}\xi^{\dagger}_{L}].

After this rotation, there is no explicit
\xi_{L},\xi_{R}
-dependence in Eq. (
15
), and all
\xi_{L},\xi_{R}
-dependence is absorbed into variables with subscript
\xi
. The final expression for the generating functional becomes

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\xi_{R}\mathcal{D}\xi_{L}\mathcal{D}V\delta(\xi_{L}^{\dagger}\xi_{L}-1)\delta(\xi_{R}^{\dagger}\xi_{R}-1)\delta(\det\xi_{R}-\det\xi_{L})

(21)

\displaystyle\times\exp\{\mathrm{i}S_{\text{eff}}[1,1,V_{\xi},J_{\xi},\Xi_{c},\tilde{V}_{c},\Phi_{\xi c},\Pi_{\xi c}]\},

where

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}S_{\text{eff}}[1,1,V_{\xi},J_{\xi},\Xi_{c},\tilde{V}_{c},\Phi_{\xi c},\Pi_{\xi c}]}=\int\mathcal{D}\Xi\mathcal{D}\tilde{V}\mathcal{D}\Phi_{\xi}\mathcal{D}\Pi_{\xi}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{2}[1,1,V_{\xi},J_{\xi},\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi_{\xi},\Pi_{\xi}]},

(22)

and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}[1,1,V_{\xi},J_{\xi},\Xi,\tilde{V},\Phi_{\xi},\Pi_{\xi}]=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}[J_{\xi},\Phi_{\xi},\Pi_{\xi}]+\mathrm{i}\Gamma_{I}[\Phi_{\xi}]

(23)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}N_{c}\int d^{4}x\mathrm{tr}_{f}\left\{\Xi\left[\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{N_{f}}}\mathrm{tr}_{l}(P_{R}\Phi^{T}_{\xi})-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\theta}{N_{f}}}\mathrm{tr}_{l}(P_{L}\Phi^{T}_{\xi})\right]\right\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}N_{c}\int d^{4}x\left[V_{\xi\mu}^{ab}+\frac{1}{4{\mu}^{4}}\Pi_{\xi}^{(a\xi)(b\zeta)}\gamma_{\mu}^{\zeta\xi}\right]\tilde{V}^{\mu,ba}.

\mathrm{Tr}\ln[\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}+\not{J}-\Pi]=\mathrm{Tr}\ln[\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}+\not{J}_{\xi}-\Pi_{\xi}]+\Gamma_{WZ}[\xi_{R}^{\dagger}\xi_{L},l,r].

]
.

The details of this derivation can be found in Ref.
[

]
, where the definition of the vector mesons was given by

V_{\mu}^{ab}=-\left[(\xi_{L}P_{R}+\xi_{R}P_{L})\left(\frac{\Pi}{4{\mu}^{4}}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}}{4}\right)(\xi^{\dagger}_{R}P_{R}+\xi^{\dagger}_{L}P_{L})\right]^{(a\xi)(b\zeta)}\gamma_{\mu}^{\zeta\xi},

(27)

which transforms inhomogeneously under the HLS, confirming that it is indeed the corresponding gauge boson.

IV
the calculations of the
p^{2}
order and

order LECs

Under the large-
N_{c}
limit, the field integrations in Eq.(
22
) can be carried out using the saddle point approximation with the classical field equations:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\mu^{4}}\delta^{(4)}(x-y)\widetilde{\not{V}}_{c}^{\sigma\rho}(x)+\Phi^{\sigma\rho}_{\xi c}(x,y)=-\mathrm{i}[(\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}+J_{\xi}-\Pi_{\xi c})^{-1}]^{\rho\sigma}(y,x),

(28)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Xi}^{\sigma\rho}(x)\delta^{(4)}(x-y)+\Pi^{\sigma\rho}_{\xi c}(x,y)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int d^{4}x_{1}\cdots d^{4}x_{n}d^{4}x^{\prime}_{1}\cdots d^{4}x^{\prime}_{n}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(-\mathrm{i})^{n+1}(N_{c}g^{2})^{n}}{n!}\bar{G}(x_{1},x^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n})\Phi_{\xi c}(x_{1},x^{\prime}_{1})\cdots\Phi_{\xi c}(x_{n},x^{\prime}_{n})=0,

\zeta
-function regularization, but the quadratic divergence in
F_{0}^{2}B_{0}
is positive and dominant. As emphasized in the Chapter 6 of Ref.
[
33
]
, “The chiral symmetry is spontaneously broken by the quadratic divergence. In a regulator that does not have the quadratic divergence, like dimensional regularization, one always works in the phase where chiral symmetry is explicitly realized in the spectrum. In the ENJL model this means that we treat it as being in a phase with weakly interacting massive quarks.” This discussion applies to QCD as well. The Schwinger proper time regularization, whether assuming a constant quark mass
[
33
]
or using a dynamical quark self-energy
[
24
,
25
]
has indeed provided reasonable LECs for pseudo-scalar mesons.

However if the calculation for pseudo-scalar mesons in Ref.
[
24
,
25
]
is generalized to vector mesons, the obtained parameter
a\equiv F_{\sigma}^{2}/F_{0}^{2}\simeq 0.35
. This result is significantly smaller than the phenomenological value
a_{\text{ph}}\sim 2
, and violates many phenomenological facts discussed in Sec.
II
. The Schwinger proper time regularization with a constant quark mass even yields a zero result for
F_{\sigma}^{2}
, as shown in TABLE
1
. Thus, Schwinger proper time regularization previously used does not work for vector mesons. In cut-off regularization, the divergence in
F_{0}^{2}B_{0}
and the logarithmic divergence in
F_{0}^{2}
are same as in the Schwinger proper time regularization, but the dominant contribution for
F_{0}^{2}
and
F_{\sigma}^{2}
becomes the quadratic divergence, which is absent in other regularizations. In this case, the obtained values of
F_{0}^{2}
and
F_{\sigma}^{2}
are reasonable, although their signs are negative. As emphasized in the Section 4.5 of Ref.
[
10
]
, “In the usual phenomenological study in the ChPT of the pseudoscalar mesons as well as the calculations in the early stage of the ChPT with HLS, only the logarithmic divergence was included. As far as the bare theory is not referred to, the quadratic divergence is simply absorbed into redefinitions of the parameters.
\cdots
However, as was shown in Refs.
[
48
,
49
,
50
]
, the inclusion of the quadratic divergence is essential to studying the phase transition with referring to the bare theory. Moreover, it was shown
[
51
]
that inclusion of the quadratic divergence is needed to match the HLS with the underlying QCD even for phenomenological reason.” Quadratic divergence also appear in the bare parameters determined by Wilsonian matching of the EFT with the underlying QCD in Ref.
[
51
]
. Specifically, the
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
LECs are sensitive to the regularizations used. This suggests that the current handling of high-energy degrees of freedom in such research is still relatively crude. Most
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
LECs, on the other hand, diverge logarithmically, and are therefore less sensitive to regularizations.

VI
Weinberg sum rules

Although the signs of obtained
F_{0}^{2}
and
F_{\sigma}^{2}
are negative, our method is consistent with the Weinberg sum rules
[
40
]
which relate the spectral functions of the vector and axial-vector currents. These spectral functions,
\rho_{V}(p^{2})
and
\rho_{A}(p^{2})
, are defined by the quark current Green functions:

\displaystyle\langle V_{i}^{\mu}(x)V_{j}^{\nu}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-3}\delta_{ij}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}p\theta(p^{0})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}p\cdot x}\rho_{V}(p^{2})[-g^{\mu\nu}+p^{\mu}p^{\nu}/p^{2}],

(67)

\displaystyle\langle A_{i}^{\mu}(x)A_{j}^{\nu}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-3}\delta_{ij}\int\mathrm{d}^{4}p\theta(p^{0})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}p\cdot x}\{\rho_{A}(p^{2})[-g^{\mu\nu}+p^{\mu}p^{\nu}/p^{2}]+F_{\pi}^{2}\delta(p^{2})p^{\mu}p^{\nu}\},

where
V_{i}^{\mu}(x)
and
A_{i}^{\mu}(x)
are vector and axial-vector currents respectively. The first and second Weinberg sum rules are given by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[\rho_{V}(\mu^{2})-\rho_{A}(\mu^{2})]\mu^{-2}d\mu^{2}=F_{\pi}^{2},

(68)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[\rho_{V}(\mu^{2})-\rho_{A}(\mu^{2})]d\mu^{2}=0,

respectively, where
p^{2}=\mu^{2}
.

In this section, we will derive the Weinberg sum rules using our method. We start with Eq.(
13
), which can be regarded as the deformed action of QCD after integrating out the quark and gluon fields, and integrating in the bilocal auxiliary fields
\Phi^{\sigma\rho}(x,y)
and
\Pi^{\sigma\rho}(x,y)
. In this case, there is no need to integrate in the local pseudo-scalar mesons and vector meson fields. After formally completing the integration over the auxiliary fields
\Phi,\Pi
, taking the large-
N_{c}
limit and the leading order in dynamical perturbation, the generating functional of quark current Green functions simplifies to

W[J]=-\mathrm{i}N_{c}\mathrm{Tr}\ln[\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}+J-\Pi_{c}],

(69)

where
J(x)=\not{v}(x)+\not{a}(x)\gamma^{5}-s(x)+\mathrm{i}p(x)\gamma^{5}
is the external source for quark current, and
\Pi_{c}
is the classical field of auxiliary field
\Pi^{\sigma\rho}(x,y)
which satisfies the classical field equation:

\Phi^{\sigma\rho}_{c}(x,y)=-\mathrm{i}[(\mathrm{i}\not{\partial}+J-\Pi_{c})^{-1}]^{\rho\sigma}(y,x).

(70)

Firstly, we calculte the vector current Green function:

\displaystyle\langle V_{i}^{\mu}(x)V_{j}^{\nu}(y)\rangle

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\delta^{2}W}{\delta v^{i}_{\mu}(x)\delta v^{j}_{\nu}(y)}\right|_{J=0}

(71)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}N_{c}\mathrm{d}^{4}z\mathrm{d}^{4}z^{\prime}\mathrm{tr}_{lf}\left.\left[\gamma^{\mu}\lambda^{i}\Phi_{c}(x,z)\frac{\delta\Phi_{c}^{-1}(z,z^{\prime})}{\delta v^{j}_{\nu}(y)}\Phi_{c}(z^{\prime},x)\right]\right|_{J=0}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}N_{c}\mathrm{d}^{4}z\mathrm{d}^{4}z^{\prime}\mathrm{tr}_{lf}\left[\gamma^{\mu}\lambda^{i}\Phi_{c}(x,z)\delta(z-z^{\prime})\delta(z-y)\gamma^{\nu}\lambda^{j}\Phi_{c}(z^{\prime},x)\right]|_{J=0}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}N_{c}\mathrm{tr}_{lf}\left[\gamma^{\mu}\lambda^{i}\Phi_{c}(x,y)\gamma^{\nu}\lambda^{j}\Phi_{c}(y,x)\right]|_{J=0},

where
\lambda^{i}
is the Gell-Mann matrix in flavor space.
As in section
IV
,
\Pi_{c}
can be further approximated as the quark self-energy:

\Pi_{c}(x,y)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}p\cdot(x-y)}\Sigma(-p^{2}).

(72)

Then the vector current Green function is

\displaystyle\langle V_{i}^{\mu}(x)V_{j}^{\nu}(y)\rangle

(73)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}N_{c}\delta^{ij}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{8}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(p-q)\cdot(x-y)}\mathrm{tr}_{l}\left[\gamma^{\mu}\frac{1}{\not{p}-\Sigma(-p^{2})}\gamma^{\nu}\frac{1}{\not{q}-\Sigma(-q^{2})}\right]

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}N_{c}\delta^{ij}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}p\cdot(x-y)}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathrm{tr}_{l}\left[\gamma^{\mu}\frac{1}{\not{p}+\not{q}-\Sigma(-(p+q)^{2})}\gamma^{\nu}\frac{1}{\not{q}-\Sigma(-q^{2})}\right]

\displaystyle=4\mathrm{i}N_{c}\delta^{ij}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}p\cdot(x-y)}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}

\displaystyle\times\left[-g^{\mu\nu}\Sigma(-(p+q)^{2})\Sigma(-q^{2})+g^{\mu\nu}p\cdot q+g^{\mu\nu}q^{2}-q^{\mu}p^{\nu}-p^{\mu}q^{\nu}-2q^{\mu}q^{\nu}\right]f(p\cdot q,q^{2},p^{2}),

where we shift integral momentum
p^{\mu}
in the second equal sign, and

f(p\cdot q,q^{2},p^{2})\equiv\frac{1}{(p+q)^{2}-\Sigma^{2}(-(p+q)^{2})}\frac{1}{q^{2}-\Sigma^{2}(-q^{2})}.

(74)

We define some integral functions as

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\Sigma(-(p+q)^{2})\Sigma(-q^{2})f(p\cdot q,q^{2},p^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv F_{1}(p^{2}),

(75)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}q^{\mu}f(p\cdot q,q^{2},p^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv p^{\mu}F_{2}(p^{2}),

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}q^{\mu}q^{\nu}f(p\cdot q,q^{2},p^{2})

\displaystyle\equiv g^{\mu\nu}G_{1}(p^{2})+p^{\mu}p^{\nu}G_{2}(p^{2}).

Finally the vector current Green function becomes

\langle V_{i}^{\mu}(x)V_{j}^{\nu}(y)\rangle=4\mathrm{i}N_{c}\delta^{ij}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}p\cdot(x-y)}T^{\mu\nu},

(76)

where

T^{\mu\nu}\equiv\left[g^{\mu\nu}(-F_{1}+p^{2}F_{2}+2G_{1}+p^{2}G_{2})-p^{\mu}p^{\nu}(2F_{2}+2G_{2})\right].

(77)

The conservation of vector current requires the following subtraction, as in Ref.
[
33
]
:

T^{{}^{\prime}\mu\nu}=T^{\mu\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}T^{\rho\sigma}\frac{p_{\rho}p_{\sigma}}{p^{2}}

(78)

Then the conservation of vector current is satisfied:
p_{\mu}T^{{}^{\prime}\mu\nu}=0
.
Compared to Eq.(
67
), we obtain the spectral function
\rho_{V}(p^{2})
:

\rho_{V}=-\frac{2\mathrm{i}N_{c}}{\pi}p^{2}(2F_{2}+2G_{2}).

(79)

Similarly, we can obtain the spectral function
\rho_{A}(p^{2})
as

\displaystyle\rho_{A}=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\mathrm{i}N_{c}}{\pi}(F_{1}+p^{2}F_{2}+2G_{1}+p^{2}G_{2}),

(80)

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{A}}{p^{2}}+F_{\pi}^{2}\delta(p^{2})=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\mathrm{i}N_{c}}{\pi}(2F_{2}+2G_{2})

There is a relation between these two spectral functions:

\frac{\rho_{V}}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{A}}{\mu^{2}}=F_{\pi}^{2}\delta(\mu^{2}).

(81)

where
p^{2}=\mu^{2}
. After performing the integral, we obtain the Weinberg sum rules

\displaystyle\int_{0^{-}}^{\infty}[\rho_{V}(\mu^{2})-\rho_{A}(\mu^{2})]\mu^{-2}d\mu^{2}=\int_{0^{-}}^{\infty}F_{\pi}^{2}\delta(\mu^{2})d\mu^{2}=F_{\pi}^{2},

(82)

\displaystyle\int_{0^{-}}^{\infty}[\rho_{V}(\mu^{2})-\rho_{A}(\mu^{2})]d\mu^{2}=\int_{0^{-}}^{\infty}F_{\pi}^{2}\mu^{2}\delta(\mu^{2})d\mu^{2}=0.

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we review our original derivation of effective chiral Lagrangian involving pseudo-scalar mesons and vector mesons (with hidden symmetry) from QCD. With proper approximations, we successfully obtain the analytical expressions for the
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
and
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
LECs in both the normal and anomalous parts of the chiral Lagrangian based on the quark self-energy. Using
|F_{0}^{2}|=(87\text{MeV})^{2}
to fix the
\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}
in the running coupling constant and choosing a cutoff
\Lambda=1000^{+100}_{-100}
MeV, we calculate the LECs numerically up to
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
. This marks the first time that LECs for vector mesons have been calculated directly from QCD, particularly for the critical parameters
a
and
g
, which are typically treated as input values in the literature. The obtained
a
is consistent with the phenomenological value, allowing us to reproduce many phenomenological results in our calculation. The obtained
g
is slightly larger than the phenomenological value, but the non-Trln term in the action might compensate for this discrepancy
[
35
]
. This aspect of the study is ongoing. The
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
LECs we derive are comparable to those found in the literature, and we anticipate more experiment values or model calculation for the
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
LECs of HLS model in the future.

The methodology for deriving the chiral effective Lagrangian of vector mesons and calculating the LECs from QCD can be straightforwardly extended to axial-vector mesons, and such studies are also in progress.

In the studies focused on calculating the LECs in ChPT from underlying theory or corresponding approximate models, there are regularization dependence and sign issues for the
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
LECs. The choice of regularization is generally guided by phenomenological reasons. Despite these challenges, we have demonstrated that our method is consistent with Weinberg sum rules. Further research is needed to address these issues comprehensively.

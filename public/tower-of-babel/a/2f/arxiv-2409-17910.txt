On the tails of log-concave density estimators
Didier B. Ryter, Lutz Dümbgen

Abstract




Abstract.

It is shown that the nonparametric maximum likelihood estimator of a univariate log-concave probability density satisfies desirable consistency properties in the tail regions. Specifically, let
P
and
f
denote the true underlying distribution and density, respectively. If
\hat{f}_{n}
is the estimated log-concave density, and
\hat{\varphi}_{n}=\log\hat{f}_{n}
, then we specify sequences
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
P([b_{n},\infty))\to 0
at a specific speed, ensuring that the absolute errors or absolute relative errors of
\hat{f}_{n},\ \hat{\varphi}_{n}
and
\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}
converge to zero uniformly on sets
[a,b_{n}]
. The main tools, besides characterizations of
\hat{f}_{n}
, are exponential and maximal inequalities for truncated moments of log-concave distributions, which are of independent interest.

AMS subject classification:

62G05, 62G07, 62G20, 62G32

Key words:

Chernov bounds, consistency, exponential inequalities, log-linear densities.

1
Introduction

Let
f
be a log-concave probability density on the real line, that is,
f(x)=\exp(\varphi(x))
for some concave and upper semicontinuous function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to[-\infty,\infty)
. Suppose we observe independent random variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
with density
f
and corresponding distribution function
F
. As noted by
Walther (2002)
and
Pal et al. (2007)
, for any sample size
n\geq 2
, there exists a unique maximum-likelihood estimator (MLE)
\hat{f}_{n}=\exp(\hat{\varphi}_{n})
of
f
, where the MLE
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
of
\varphi
maximizes

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi(X_{i})

over all concave functions
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to[-\infty,\infty)
such that
\int e^{\psi(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. Denoting the order statistics of
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
with
X_{(1)}<\cdots<X_{(n)}
, this estimator
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
is piecewise linear on
[X_{(1)},X_{(n)}]
with changes of slope only at observations, and
\hat{\varphi}_{n}=-\infty
outside of
[X_{(1)},X_{(n)}]
.

Concerning consistency of
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
, let
\{x\in\mathbb{R}:0<F(x)<1\}=:(a_{o},b_{o})
with
-\infty\leq a_{o}<b_{o}\leq\infty
. It was shown by
Dümbgen and Rufibach (2009)
that for any fixed interval
[a,b]\subset(a_{o},b_{o})
, the supremum of
|\hat{\varphi}_{n}-\varphi|
over
[a,b]
is of order
O_{\mathrm{p}}(\rho_{n}^{1/3})
, where
\rho_{n}:=\log(n)/n
. If
\varphi
is Hölder-continuous on a neighborhood of
[a,b]
with exponent
\beta\in(1,2]
, this rate improves to
O_{\mathrm{p}}(\rho_{n}^{\beta/(2\beta+1)})
. Uniform consistency of
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
on arbitrary compact subintervals of
(a_{o},b_{o})
implies that
\int\bigl|\hat{f}_{n}(x)-f(x)\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}x\to_{\mathrm{p}}0
. (Throughout this paper, asymptotic statements refer to
n\to\infty
.) Pointwise limiting distributions at a single point
x_{o}\in(a_{o},b_{o})
have been derived by
Balabdaoui et al. (2009)
, assuming that
\varphi
is twice continuously differentiable in a neighborhood of
x_{o}
.
Kim and Samworth (2016)
showed that the expected squared Hellinger distance between
\hat{f}_{n}
and
f
is of order
O(n^{-4/5})
. Numerous further results about
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
and
\hat{f}_{n}
have been derived thereafter, including multivariate settings, see the review of
Samworth (2018)
. In the present univariate setting, fast algorithms for the computation of
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
and related objects are provided by
Dümbgen and Rufibach (2011)
and
Dümbgen et al. (2021)
. Experiments with simulated data show that even in the tail regions, that is, close to
a_{o}
and
b_{o}
, the estimator
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
is surprisingly accurate. In view of this empirical finding,
Müller and Rufibach (2009)
developed new estimators for extreme value analysis with excellent empirical performance. However, the currently available theory about the asymptotic properties of
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
does not explain its good performance in the tail regions.

In what follows, several results about
\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)
and the right-sided derivative
\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)
for
x
close to
b_{o}
are derived. By symmetry, these findings carry over to results in the left tail region. The main results are presented in Section
2
while Sections
3
and
4
provide the proofs. Moreover, the results in Section
3.4
are of independent interest.

2
Main results

We start with a simple consequence of the pointwise consistency of
\hat{f}_{n}
,
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
and concavity of
\varphi,\hat{\varphi}_{n}
.

Theorem 1
.

The estimator
\hat{f}_{n}
does not overestimate
f
in the sense that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\,\bigl(\hat{f}_{n}(x)-f(x)\bigr)^{+}\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

Moreover, for any sequence
(b_{n})_{n}
in
(a_{o},b_{o})
with limit
b_{o}
,

\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}+)\ \begin{cases}\leq\ \varphi^{\prime}(b_{o}-)+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1)&\text{if}\ \varphi^{\prime}(b_{o}-)>-\infty,\\
\to_{\mathrm{p}}\ -\infty&\text{if}\ \varphi^{\prime}(b_{o}-)=-\infty,\end{cases}

where
\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+):=-\infty
for
x\geq X_{(n)}
.

The remaining goal is to show that the right tails are not “severely underestimated”, and for this task we distinguish the cases
b_{o}=\infty
and
b_{o}<\infty
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
b_{o}<\infty
.

(a)
Let
f(b_{o})=0
. Then for any fixed
a\in(a_{o},b_{o})
,

\sup_{x\geq a}\bigl|\hat{f}_{n}(x)-f(x)\bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

Moreover, for any given sequence
(b_{n})_{n}
in
(a_{o},b_{o})
with limit
b_{o}
,

\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}+)\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ \varphi^{\prime}(b_{o}-)=-\infty.

(b)
Let
f(b_{o})>0
. Then for arbitrary fixed intervals
[a,b_{n}]\subset(a_{o},b_{o})
such that
b_{n}\uparrow b_{o}
and
n(1-F(b_{n}))\to\infty
,

\sup_{x\in[a,b_{n}]}\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

(c)
Let
f(b_{o})>0
and
\varphi^{\prime}(b_{o}-)>-\infty
. Then for any given sequence
(b_{n})_{n}
in
(a_{o},b_{o})
such that
b_{n}\uparrow b_{o}
and
\rho_{n}^{-1/3}(1-F(b_{n}))\to\infty
,

\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}+)\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ \varphi^{\prime}(b_{o}-).

.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
b_{o}=\infty
. Let
(b_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
(a_{o},\infty)
such that
b_{n}\to\infty
and
(1-F(b_{n}))/\rho_{n}\to\infty
.

(a)
With asymptotic probability one,
\hat{f}_{n}(b_{n})>0
, and

\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}+)\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ \varphi^{\prime}(\infty-).

(b)
Suppose that
\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)>-\infty
. Then for any fixed
a\in(a_{o},\infty)
,

\max_{x\in[a,b_{n}]}\,\frac{\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr|}{1+|\varphi(x)|}\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

(c)
Suppose that
\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)=-\infty
and
\varphi
is differentiable on some halfline
(a_{*},\infty)\subset(a_{o},\infty)
with Lipschitz-continuous derivative
\varphi^{\prime}
. Then, for arbitrary fixed
a\in(a_{*},\infty)
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(a)<0
,

\sup_{x\in[a,b_{n}]}\,\Bigl(\frac{\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)}{\varphi^{\prime}(x)}-1\Bigl)^{+}\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0

and

\sup_{x\in[a,b_{n}]}\,\bigl(\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr)^{+}\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0,\quad\sup_{x\in[a,b_{n}]}\,\frac{\bigl(\varphi(x)-\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\bigr)^{+}}{1+|\varphi(x)|}\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

Parts (a) and (b) apply, for instance, if
P
is a logistic distribution or a gamma distribution with shape parameter in
[1,\infty)
. Parts (a) and (c) explain why the estimator
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
is remarkably accurate in the tails if, for instance,
P
is a Gaussian distribution. In particular, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(\hat{f}_{n}(x)\leq(1+\varepsilon)f(x)\ \text{for all}\ x\in[a,b_{n}]\bigr)\ \to\ 1,

which is substantially stronger than the first conclusion of Theorem
1
. Furthermore, since
\varphi^{\prime}<0
on
[a,b_{n}]
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\varphi^{\prime}(x)\ \text{for all}\ x\in[a,b_{n}]\bigr)\ \to\ 1,

which complements the second conclusion of Theorem
1
.

3
Auxiliary results

In what follows, let
P
and
\hat{P}_{n}
be the distribution with density
f
and
\hat{f}_{n}
, respectively. The corresponding distribution functions are denoted by
F
and
\hat{F}_{n}
, respectively. In addition, let
\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}
and
\hat{F}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}
be the empirical distribution and the empirical distribution function, respectively, of the observations
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
.

3.1
More about
\hat{f}_{n}
and
\hat{\varphi}_{n}

We mentioned already some properties of
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
and
\hat{f}_{n}
. Recall that for any fixed
[a,b]\subset(a_{o},b_{o})
,

\sup_{x\in[a,b]}\,\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0,

(1)

and since
\varphi
is bounded on
[a,b]
, this implies that

\sup_{x\in[a,b]}\,\bigl|\hat{f}_{n}(x)-f(x)\bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

(2)

An important consequence of the latter result is that

\sup_{B\in\mathrm{Borel}(\mathbb{R})}\bigl|\hat{P}_{n}(B)-P(B)\bigr|\ =\ 2^{-1}\int\bigl|\hat{f}_{n}(x)-f(x)\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}x\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0,

(3)

see
Dümbgen and Rufibach (2009)
. The latter paper also provides the following key inequalities: Let

\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\ :=\ \{X_{(1)},X_{(n)}\}\cup\bigl\{x\in(X_{(1)},X_{(n)}):\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x-)>\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x+)\bigr\},

the set of kinks of
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
. Then for arbitrary
b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\int(x-b)^{+}\,\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(\mathrm{d}x)\ \begin{cases}\displaystyle\geq\ \int(x-b)^{+}\,\hat{P}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x),\\
\displaystyle=\ \int(x-b)^{+}\,\hat{P}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x)&\text{if}\ b\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}.\end{cases}

(4)

Moreover, for
b\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}
,

\hat{F}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(b-)\ \leq\ \hat{F}_{n}(b)\ \leq\ \hat{F}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(b)

(5)

Finally,

\int x\,\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(\mathrm{d}x)\ =\ \int x\,\hat{P}_{n}(\mathrm{d}x).

3.2
Inequalities for
\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}

Concerning
\hat{F}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}
, note the following useful inequality: For any
b<b_{o}
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\biggl(\sup_{x\leq b}\,\Bigl|\frac{\hat{F}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(x)-F(x)}{1-F(x)}\Bigr|^{2}\biggr)\ \leq\ \frac{4}{n(1-F(b))}.

This follows from the well-known fact that
M_{x}:=[\hat{F}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(x)-F(x)]/[1-F(x)]
defines a martingale
(M_{x})_{x<b_{o}}
and from one of Doob’s martingale inequalities
(
Shorack and Wellner, 1986
;
Hall and Heyde, 1980
)
. In particular, for any sequence of numbers
b_{n}\in(a_{o},b_{o})
,

\sup_{x\leq b_{n}}\Bigl|\frac{1-\hat{F}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(x)}{1-F(x)}-1\Bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0\quad\text{if}\ n(1-F(b_{n}))\to\infty.

(6)

Combining this with (
5
) leads to the fact that

\max_{x\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}:x\leq b_{n}}\Bigl|\frac{1-\hat{F}_{n}(x)}{1-F(x)}-1\Bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0\quad\text{if}\ n(1-F(b_{n}))\to\infty.

(7)

Here is another useful result about
\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)-P(I)
over real intervals
I
.

Proposition 1

(Consistency of
\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}
).

For any constant
\tau>2
, with asymptotic probability one,

\bigl|\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)-P(I)\bigr|\ \leq\ \sqrt{2\tau\rho_{n}\min\{\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I),P(I)\}}+(\tau+2)\rho_{n}

for arbitrary intervals
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

Note first that for arbitrary indices
0\leq j<k\leq n+1
with
k-j\leq n
, the random variable
P(X_{(j)},X_{(k)})
follows a beta distribution with parameters
k-j
and
n+1-k+j
, see Chapter 3.1 of
Shorack and Wellner (1986)
. Here we set
X_{(0)}:=a_{o}
and
X_{(n+1)}:=b_{o}
. In particular, its mean equals

p_{njk}\ :=\ \frac{k-j}{n+1},

and it follows from Proposition 2.1 of
Dümbgen (1998)
that for any
\eta>0
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl[\Psi\bigl(P(X_{(j)},X_{(k)}),p_{njk}\bigr)\geq\eta\rho_{n}\bigr]\ \leq\ 2\exp(-(n+1)\eta\rho_{n})\ <\ 2n^{-\eta},

where
\Psi(x,p):=p\log(p/x)+(1-p)\log[(1-p)/(1-x)]
. Moreover, for
c>0
, the inequality
\Psi(x,p)<c
implies that
|x-p|<\sqrt{2cp(1-p)}+|1-2p|c
. Furthermore, since
\Psi(x,p)=K_{0}(x,p)=K_{1}(p,x)
in the notation of
Dümbgen and Wellner (2023)
, Lemma S.12 in the latter paper shows that
|x-p|<\sqrt{2cx}+(2/3)|1-2x|c
whenever
\Psi(x,p)<c
. Consequently, the probability that

\bigl|P(X_{(j)},X_{(k)})-p_{njk}\bigr|\ \leq\ \sqrt{2\eta\rho_{n}\min\{P(X_{(j)},X_{(k)}),p_{njk}\}}+\eta\rho_{n}

(8)

for all indices
0\leq j<k\leq n+1
is at least
1-2\binom{n+2}{2}n^{-\eta}=1-(1+3/n+2/n^{2})n^{2-\eta}
, and this converges to
1
if
\eta>2
.

It remains to be shown that in case of
\eta\in(2,\tau)
, inequality (
8
) implies that
with asymptotic probability one,

\bigl|P(I)-\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)\bigr|\ \leq\ \sqrt{2\tau\rho_{n}\min\{P(I),\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)\}}+(\tau+2)\rho_{n}

for all intervals
I\subset\mathbb{R}
. Note first that

\max_{\ell=1,\ldots,n+1}P(X_{(\ell-1)},X_{(\ell)})\ \leq\ \rho_{n}+O_{\mathrm{p}}(n^{-1}).

(9)

This can be deduced from the well-known representation
P(X_{(\ell-1)},X_{(\ell)})=E_{\ell}/\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}E_{i}
with independent, standard exponential random variables
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n+1}
. Now, for an arbitrary nonvoid interval
I
, let
j=j_{n}(I)\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
be maximal and
k=k_{n}(I)\in\{1,2,\ldots,n+1\}
be minimal such that
I\subset[X_{(j)},X_{(k)}]
. If
k-j\leq 2
, then

\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)-P(I)\ \leq\ \hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)\ \leq\ 3/n\ =\ o(\rho_{n})

and

P(I)-\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)\ \leq\ P(I)\ \leq\ 2\max_{\ell=1,\ldots,n+1}P(X_{(\ell-1)},X_{(\ell)})\ \leq\ (2+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1))\rho_{n}.

In case of
k-j\geq 3
, let
\tilde{I}:=[X_{(j+1)},X_{(k-1)}]\subset I
with

\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(\tilde{I})=\frac{k-j-1}{n}\ >\ \frac{k-j-2}{n+1}=p_{n,j+1,k-1}.

Consequently, it follows from (
8
) and (
9
) that

\displaystyle P(I)-\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)\

\displaystyle\leq\ P(\tilde{I})-\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(\tilde{I})+2\max_{\ell=0,\ldots,n}P(X_{(\ell-1)},X_{(\ell)})

\displaystyle\leq\ P(\tilde{I})-p_{n,j+1,k-1}+(2+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1))\rho_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\ \sqrt{2\eta\rho_{n}\min\{P(\tilde{I}),p_{n,j+1,k-1}\}}+(\eta+2+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1))\rho_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\ \sqrt{2\eta\rho_{n}\min\{P(I),\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)\}}+(\eta+2+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1))\rho_{n},

and

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)-P(I)

\displaystyle\leq\ 3/n+(1+1/n)p_{n,j+1,k-1}-P(\tilde{I})

\displaystyle\leq\ 4/n+(1+1/n)\bigl(p_{n,j+1,k-1}-P(\tilde{I})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\ o(\rho_{n})+(1+1/n)\Bigl(\sqrt{2\eta\rho_{n}\min\{P(I),\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)\}}+\eta\rho_{n}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\ \sqrt{2(\eta+o(1))\rho_{n}\min\{P(I),\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(I)\}}+(\eta+o(1))\rho_{n},

where the terms
o_{\mathrm{p}}(1)
and
o(1)
depend only on
n
, not on the interval
I
.

3.3
Truncated and conditional means of
P

For
-\infty<a<b\leq\infty
, let

\displaystyle M(a,b)\

\displaystyle:=\ \int_{(a,b)}(x-a)\,P(\mathrm{d}x),

\displaystyle\mu(a,b)\

\displaystyle:=\ \begin{cases}P(a,b)^{-1}M(a,b)&\text{if}\ P(a,b)>0,\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

For
-\infty\leq a<b<\infty
, we set

W(a,b)\ :=\ \int_{(a,b)}(b-x)\,P(\mathrm{d}x).

Further, let

\mu(a)\ :=\ \mu(a,\infty).

(10)

The univariate function
\mu
is known as the mean excess function or mean residual lifetime in fields such as extreme value theory and actuarial science.

To formulate various approximations and inequalities for these functions
M
,
\mu
and
W
, we need three auxiliary functions and some properties thereof.

Proposition 2
.

Let
N,\nu,V:\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
be given by

N(t)\ :=\ \int_{0}^{1}ue^{tu}\,\mathrm{d}u,\quad\nu(t)\ :=\ N(t)\Big/\int_{0}^{1}e^{tu}\,\mathrm{d}u,\quad V(t)\ :=\ \int_{0}^{1}(1-u)e^{tu}\,\mathrm{d}u.

These functions are continuously differentiable with
N^{\prime},\nu^{\prime},V^{\prime}>0
, where
N(0)=\nu(0)=V(0)=1/2
,
N^{\prime}(0)=1/3
,
\nu^{\prime}(0)=1/12
and
V^{\prime}(0)=1/6
. Moreover,

\lim_{t\to-\infty}\,t^{2}N(t)\ =\ \lim_{t\to-\infty}\,|t|\nu(t)\ =\ \lim_{t\to-\infty}\,|t|V(t)\ =\ 1.

This proposition follows from elementary calculus. The limits of
t^{2}N(t)
,
|t|\nu(t)
and
|t|V(t)
as
t\to-\infty
follow from the explicit formulae

N(t)\ =\ \frac{1-(1-t)e^{t}}{t^{2}},\quad\nu(t)\ =\ \frac{1-(1-t)e^{t}}{t(e^{t}-1)},\quad V(t)\ =\ \frac{-1+(e^{t}-1)/t}{t}

for

.

The next proposition summarizes several useful properties of the functions

,
\mu
and

and their relation to

and

. The monotonicity property of

in part (a) was noted already by

. In the proposition’s proof and later on, we use repeatedly well-known results about the stochastic and likelihood ratio orders between probability distributions on the real line, see
Shaked and Shanthikumar (2007)
for the foundations. Specifically, let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be probability distributions on the real line with densities
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, respectively. If
f_{1}\geq f_{2}
on
(-\infty,x_{o})
and
f_{1}\leq f_{2}
on
(x_{o},\infty)
for some real number
x_{o}
, then
P_{1}\leq_{\rm st}P_{2}
, where
\leq_{\rm st}
denotes stochastic order. In particular, if
f_{2}/f_{1}
is non-decreasing on
\{f_{1}+f_{2}>0\}
, then such a number
x_{o}
has to exist, whence
P_{1}\leq_{\rm st}P_{2}
.

Proposition 3

(Properties of
M
and
\mu
).

(a)
The function
\mu
given by (

) is non-increasing and Lipschitz-continuous with constant one.

(b)
Let
-\infty<a<b<c\leq\infty
such that

. Then,

\frac{1}{p}\ \leq\ \frac{M(a,c)}{M(a,b)}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{p+(1-p)\log(1-p)},

where

and
0\log 0:=0
.

(c)
For arbitrary real numbers
a<b
in

f(a)(b-a)^{2}V\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(b-)(b-a)\bigr)\ \leq\ W(a,b)\ \leq\ f(a)(b-a)^{2}V\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr),

where
N(-\infty),V(-\infty):=0
and
N(\infty),V(\infty):=\infty
. Moreover,

(b-a)\nu\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(b-)(b-a)\bigr)\ \leq\ \mu(a,b)\ \leq\ (b-a)\nu\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr)

where
\nu(-\infty):=0
,

.

(d)
Suppose that
b_{o}=\infty
. Then for arbitrary real

with

\frac{f(a)}{\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)^{2}}\ \leq\ M(a,\infty)\ \leq\ \frac{f(a)}{\varphi^{\prime}(a+)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{|\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)|}\ \leq\ \mu(a)\ \leq\ \frac{1}{|\varphi^{\prime}(a+)|}.

(e)
Suppose that
\varphi
is differentiable on some interval
(a,b)\subset[a_{o},b_{o}]
with

and
\varphi^{\prime}(a+)<0
. Further, let
\varphi^{\prime}
be Lipschitz-continuous with constant

M(a,b)\ \geq\ \exp\bigl(-3L\varphi^{\prime}(a+)^{-2}\bigr)\cdot\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{f(a)}{\varphi^{\prime}(a+)^{2}}&\text{if}\ b=\infty,\\[8.61108pt]
f(a)(b-a)^{2}N\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr)&\text{if}\ b<\infty,\end{cases}

and

\mu(a,b)\ \geq\ \exp\bigl(-3L\varphi^{\prime}(a+)^{-2}\bigr)\cdot\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\varphi^{\prime}(a+)|}&\text{if}\ b=\infty,\\[8.61108pt]
(b-a)\nu\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr)&\text{if}\ b<\infty.\end{cases}

Proof of Proposition
3
.

As to part (a), by means of Fubini’s theorem we may write

\mu(a)\ =\ (1-F(a))^{-1}\int\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{[r<y-a]}\,\mathrm{d}r\,P(\mathrm{d}y)\ =\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-F(a+r)}{1-F(a)}\,\mathrm{d}r

for
a\in\mathbb{R}
with
F(a)<1
. For
a<a^{\prime}
with
F(a^{\prime})=1
,

\mu(a)-\mu(a^{\prime})\ =\ \mu(a)\ =\ \int_{0}^{a^{\prime}-a}\frac{1-F(a+r)}{1-F(a)}\,\mathrm{d}r\ \in\ [0,a^{\prime}-a].

For
a<a^{\prime}
with
F(a^{\prime})<1
,

\displaystyle\mu(a)-\mu(a^{\prime})\

\displaystyle=\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-F(a+r)}{1-F(a)}\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-F(a^{\prime}+r)}{1-F(a^{\prime})}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-F(a+r)}{1-F(a)}\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-F(a^{\prime}+r)}{1-F(a)}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\ \int_{a}^{a^{\prime}}\frac{1-F(a+r)}{1-F(a)}\,\mathrm{d}r\ \leq\ a^{\prime}-a.

That
\mu(a)\geq\mu(a^{\prime})
was noted already by
Bagnoli and Bergstrom (2005)
, but for the reader’s convenience, we provide an argument here. For
\xi\in\{a,a^{\prime}\}
, one may write

\mu(\xi)\ =\ \int_{[0,\infty)}zf_{\xi}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z

with the probability density
f_{\xi}
on
[0,\infty)
given by
f_{\xi}(z)\ :=\ \exp\bigl(\varphi(\xi+z)-\log(1-F(\xi))\bigr)
. By concavity of
\varphi
,
f_{a}/f_{a^{\prime}}
is non-decreasing on
\{f_{a}>0\}=[0,b_{o}-a)\supset\{f_{a^{\prime}}>0\}
. This implies that the distribution with density
f_{a}
is stochastically greater than (or equal to) the distribution with density
f_{a^{\prime}}
. In particular, the mean
\mu(a)
of the former is not smaller than the mean
\mu(a^{\prime})
of the latter.

To prove part (b), it suffices to consider the nontrivial case that
P(b,c)>0
, i.e.
p\in(0,1)
. Note that the ratios
M(a,c)/M(a,b)
,
\mu(a,c)/\mu(a,b)
and
p=P(a,b)/P(a,c)
remain the same if we replace
P
with the conditional distribution
P(a,c)^{-1}P(\cdot\cap(a,c))
. Thus we may assume that
P(a,c)=1
, and we may replace
c
with
\infty
. With a random variable
X
with this (modified) distribution
P
, we may write

\frac{M(a,c)}{M(a,b)}\ =\ \frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(X-a)}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(1_{[X\leq b]}(X-a))},\quad\frac{\mu(a,c)}{\mu(a,b)}\ =\ \frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(X-a)}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(X-a\,|\,X\leq b)}\ =\ \frac{pM(a,c)}{M(a,b)}.

Now let
\tilde{f}(x):=1_{[x>a]}\lambda\exp(-\lambda(x-a))
with
\lambda>0
such that
\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=p
, that is,

\lambda\ =\ \frac{-\log(1-p)}{b-a}.

By concavity of
\log f
and linearity of
\log\tilde{f}
on
(a,\infty)
, either
f\equiv\tilde{f}
, or there exist numbers
a\leq x_{1}<x_{2}\leq\infty
such that

f\ \begin{cases}<\tilde{f}\ \text{on}\ (a,x_{1})\cup(x_{2},\infty),\\
>\tilde{f}\ \text{on}\ (x_{1},x_{2}).\end{cases}

Note that
x_{1}=a
would imply that
f>\tilde{f}
on
(a,b)
or
f<\tilde{f}
on
(b,\infty)
, and in both cases we would end up with
\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\neq\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{f}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
. Similarly one can exclude the cases
x_{2}=\infty
,
x_{1}\geq b
and
x_{2}\leq b
. Consequently, we know that there exist constants
a<x_{1}<b<x_{2}<\infty
such that

f\ \begin{cases}\leq\ \tilde{f}\ \ \text{on}\ (a,x_{1})\cup(x_{2},\infty),\\
\geq\ \tilde{f}\ \ \text{on}\ (x_{1},x_{2}).\end{cases}

In particular, if
\tilde{X}
is a random variable with density
\tilde{f}
, then

\mathcal{L}(X\,|\,X\leq b)\ \geq_{\rm st}\ \mathcal{L}(\tilde{X}\,|\,\tilde{X}\leq b)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{L}(X\,|\,X>b)\ \leq_{\rm st}\ \mathcal{L}(\tilde{X}\,|\,\tilde{X}>b),

where
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
stands for ‘distribution’. In particular,

\displaystyle\frac{M(a,c)}{M(a,b)}\

\displaystyle=\ 1+\frac{(1-p)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(X-a\,|\,X>b)}{p\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(X-a\,|\,X\leq b)}

\displaystyle\leq\ 1+\frac{(1-p)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(\tilde{X}-a\,|\,\tilde{X}>b)}{p\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(\tilde{X}-a\,|\,\tilde{X}\leq b)}\ =\ \frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(\tilde{X}-a)}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(1_{[\tilde{X}\leq b]}(\tilde{X}-a))}.

Elementary calculations reveal that the latter ratio is equal to
1/[p+(1-p)\log(1-p)]
, which yields our upper bound for
M(a,c)/M(a,b)
. Concerning the lower bound, note that

\frac{M(a,c)}{M(a,b)}\ =\ 1+\frac{(1-p)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(X-a\,|\,X>b)}{p\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(X-a\,|\,X\leq b)}\ \geq\ 1+\frac{(1-p)}{p}\ =\ \frac{1}{p}.

To prove part (c), we consider up to three functions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3}:[0,b-a]\to[-\infty,\infty]
given by

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(z)\

\displaystyle:=\ \varphi^{\prime}(b-)z\qquad(\text{if}\ \varphi^{\prime}(b_{o}-)>-\infty),

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(z)\

\displaystyle:=\ \varphi(a+z)-\varphi(a),

\displaystyle\psi_{3}(z)\

\displaystyle:=\ \varphi^{\prime}(a+)z\qquad(\text{if}\ \varphi^{\prime}(a+)<\infty).

Concavity of
\varphi
implies that
\psi_{1}\leq\psi_{2}\leq\psi_{3}
, so

\frac{M(a,b)}{f(a)}\ =\ \int_{(a,b)}(x-a)\exp(\psi_{2}(x-a))\,\mathrm{d}x\ =\ \int_{0}^{b-a}z\exp(\psi_{2}(z))\,\mathrm{d}z

lies between

\int_{0}^{b-a}z\exp(\psi_{1}(z))\,\mathrm{d}z\ =\ (b-a)^{2}N\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(b-)(b-a)\bigr)

and

\int_{0}^{b-a}z\exp(\psi_{3}(z))\,\mathrm{d}z\ =\ (b-a)^{2}N\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr).

Analogously, one can show that
W(a,b)/f(a)
lies between
(b-a)^{2}V\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(b-)(b-a)\bigr)
and
(b-a)^{2}V\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr)
.

Concerning the inequalities for
\mu(a,b)
, note that in case of
\varphi^{\prime}(b-)>-\infty
,

\psi_{2}(z)-\psi_{1}(z)\ =\ \int_{0}^{z}\bigl[\varphi^{\prime}((a+t)+)-\varphi^{\prime}(b-)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t

is non-decreasing in
z\in[0,b-a]
, so the probability distribution
P_{1}
on
[0,b-a]
with density proportional to
\exp(\psi_{1})
is stochastically smaller than (or equal to) the probability distribution
P_{2}
on
[0,b-a]
with density proportional to
\exp(\psi_{2})
. Thus,

\mu(a,b)\ =\ \int_{[0,b-a]}z\,P_{2}(\mathrm{d}z)\ \geq\ \int_{[0,b-a]}z\,P_{1}(\mathrm{d}z)\ =\ (b-a)\nu\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(b-)(b-a)\bigr).

Analogously, if
\varphi^{\prime}(a+)<\infty
, then
\psi_{3}-\psi_{2}
is non-decreasing on
[0,b-a]
, and this implies that

\mu(a,b)\ \leq\ (b-a)\nu\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr).

Part (d) is verified similarly as part (c). Here we consider two or three probability densities
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
on
[0,\infty)
given by
f_{1}(z):=\lambda_{1}\exp(-\lambda_{1}z)
with
\lambda_{1}:=-\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)
(if
\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)>-\infty
),
f_{2}(z):=\exp\bigl(\varphi(a+z)-\log(1-F(a))\bigr)
and
f_{3}(z):=\lambda_{3}\exp(-\lambda_{3}z)
with
\lambda_{3}:=-\varphi^{\prime}(a+)
. If
\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)>-\infty
, then
f_{2}/f_{1}
is non-decreasing, whence
\mu(a)\geq 1/\lambda_{1}=1/|\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)|
. And
f_{3}/f_{2}
is non-decreasing too, so
\mu(x)\leq 1/\lambda_{3}=1/|\varphi^{\prime}(a+)|
.

As to part (e), it suffices to prove the inequalities for
M(a,b)
and
\mu(a,b)
with
b<\infty
, because
t:=\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\to-\infty
and
t^{2}N(t)\to 1
,
|t|\nu(t)\to 1
as

, see Proposition
2
. For
z\in[0,b-a]
, let
\psi_{2}(z)=\varphi(a+z)-\varphi(a)
as before and

\psi_{4}(z)\ :=\ \varphi^{\prime}(a+)z-Lz^{2}/2.

The difference
\psi_{2}-\psi_{4}
is non-negative and non-decreasing, because

\psi_{2}(z)-\psi_{4}(z)\ =\ \int_{0}^{z}\bigl[\varphi^{\prime}(a+t)-\varphi^{\prime}(a+)+Lt\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t,

and the integrand is non-negative by Lipschitz-continuity of
\varphi^{\prime}
on
(a,b)
with constant
L
. Hence,
M(a,b)
is not smaller than

\displaystyle f(a)\int_{0}^{b-a}z\exp(\psi_{4}(z))\,\mathrm{d}z\

\displaystyle=\ f(a)\int_{0}^{b-a}z\exp(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)z-Lz^{2}/2)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\ f(a)(b-a)^{2}\int_{0}^{1}u\exp\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)u-cu^{2})\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\ f(a)(b-a)^{2}N\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\exp(-cU^{2}),

where
c:=L(b-a)^{2}/2
, and
U
denotes a nonnegative random variable with density proportional to
1_{[u<1]}u\exp(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)u)
. Similarly,
\mu(a,b)
is not smaller than

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{b-a}

\displaystyle z\exp(\psi_{4}(z))\,\mathrm{d}z\Big/\int_{0}^{b-a}\exp(\psi_{4}(z))\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\geq\ \int_{0}^{b-a}z\exp(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)z-Lz^{2}/2)\,\mathrm{d}z\Big/\int_{0}^{b-a}\exp(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\ (b-a)\int_{0}^{1}u\exp\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)u-cu^{2})\,\mathrm{d}u\Big/\int_{0}^{1}\exp(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)u)\,\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\ (b-a)\nu\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(a+)(b-a)\bigr)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\exp(-cU^{2}).

But the random variable
U
is stochastically smaller than a gamma random variable
Y
with shape parameter
2
and rate parameter
|\varphi^{\prime}(a+)|(b-a)
. Thus it follows from Jensen’s inequality and this comparison that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\exp(-cU^{2})\ \geq\ \exp(-c\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(U^{2}))\ \geq\ \exp(-c\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(Y^{2}))\ =\ \exp\bigl(-3L\varphi^{\prime}(a+)^{-2}\bigr),

because
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}(Y^{2})\ =\ 6\varphi^{\prime}(a+)^{-2}(b-a)^{-2}
.
∎

3.4
Exponential and maximal inequalities

In what follows, let
\hat{M}_{n},\hat{\mu}_{n},\hat{W}_{n}
and
\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}},\hat{\mu}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}},\hat{W}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}
be defined as

with
\hat{P}_{n}
and
\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}
, respectively, in place of

. The following basic result will be our key to bound the ratios
\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}/M
and
\hat{\mu}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}/\mu
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
Q
be a distribution on
[0,\infty)
with log-concave density and mean
\mu_{Q}>0
. Consider an i.i.d. sample
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
drawn from
Q
. Then, for arbitrary
t<1
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\exp\Bigl(t\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{Y_{i}}{\mu_{Q}}\Bigr)\ \leq\ (1-t)^{-m}.

Proof of Proposition
4
.

If
Q
follows an exponential distribution, it is well known that the above inequality holds with equality. Now suppose that
Q
is an arbitrary distribution with log-density
\varphi
. Let
\tilde{\varphi}
be the log-density of the exponential distribution
\tilde{Q}
with mean
\mu_{Q}
, that is,
\tilde{\varphi}(x)=-x/\mu_{Q}-\log(\mu_{Q})
for
x\geq 0
and
\tilde{\varphi}(x)=-\infty
for
x<0
. Concavity of
\varphi
and linearity of
\tilde{\varphi}
on
[0,\infty)
together with equality of the means imply that for suitable real numbers
0<a<b
,
\varphi(x)\leq\tilde{\varphi}(x)
for
x\not\in[a,b]
and
\varphi(x)\geq\tilde{\varphi}(x)
for
x\in(a,b)
. By Lemma b in
Karlin and Novikoff (1963)
(see also Theorem 3.A.44 in
Shaked and Shanthikumar (2007)
), we obtain

\int\Psi\,\mathrm{d}Q\ \leq\ \int\Psi\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{Q}\quad\text{for all convex}\ \Psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

(11)

Applying (
11
) to
\Psi(x)=\exp(tx/\mu_{Q})
for arbitrary
t<1
, and using independence of
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
concludes the proof.
∎

Our next key results are simultaneous inequalities for the univariate versions of

.

(a)

Proof of Proposition
6
.

As to part (a), we first note that Proposition
4
yields the inequality

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\exp\Bigl(t\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{M(a,b)}\Bigr)\

\displaystyle=\ \operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\exp\Bigl(\frac{t}{nP(a,b)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}1_{(a,b)}(X_{i})\frac{X_{i}-a}{\mu(a,b)}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\ \operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\Bigl(\exp\Bigl(\frac{t}{nP(a,b)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}1_{(a,b)}(X_{i})\frac{X_{i}-a}{\mu(a,b)}\Bigr)\,\Big|\,\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n}{k}P(a,b)^{k}(1-P(a,b))^{n-k}\Bigl(1-\frac{t}{nP(a,b)}\Bigr)^{-k}

\displaystyle=\ \biggl(1+\frac{P(a,b)t}{nP(a,b)-t}\biggr)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\ \exp\Bigl(\frac{nP(a,b)t}{nP(a,b)-t}\Bigr)\ =\ \exp\Bigl(t+\frac{t^{2}}{nP(a,b)-t}\Bigr)

for arbitrary
t<nP(a,b)
, so

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\exp\Bigl(t\Bigl(\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{M(a,b)}-1\Bigr)\Bigr)\ \leq\ \exp\Bigl(\frac{t^{2}}{nP(a,b)-t}\Bigr).

A standard application of Markov’s inequality shows that for
\eta\geq 0
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\biggl(\Bigl|\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{M(a,b)}-1\Bigr|\geq\eta\biggr)\ \leq\ 2\exp\Bigl(\frac{t^{2}}{nP(a,b)-t}-t\eta\Bigr)

for all
t\in[0,nP(a,b))
. This bound is minimized for
t=nP(a,b)\bigl(1-1/\sqrt{1+\eta}\bigr)
, which leads to

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\biggl(\Bigl|\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{M(a,b)}-1\Bigr|\geq\eta\biggr)\ \leq\ 2\exp\Bigl(-nP(a,b)\bigl(\sqrt{1+\eta}-1\bigr)^{2}\Bigr).

Thus, for
\eta=2\sqrt{\tau/P(a,b)}+\tau/P(a,b)
we obtain the asserted inequality.

Concerning part (b), let
\mathcal{D}_{n}=\bigl\{j/n:j\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[-n^{2},n^{2}]\bigr\}\cup\{\infty\}
. Then it follows from part (a) that for
D>4
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\biggl(

\displaystyle\Bigl|\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{M(a,b)}-1\Bigr|\geq 2\sqrt{\frac{D\rho_{n}}{P(a,b)}}+\frac{D\rho_{n}}{P(a,b)}\ \text{for some}\ a,b\in\mathcal{D}_{n},P(a,b)>0\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\ (2n^{2}+1)2n^{2}n^{-D}\ \to\ 0.

In particular,

\Delta_{n,M}\ :=\ \max_{a,b\in\mathcal{D}_{n}:P(a,b)\geq\delta_{n}/2}\,\Bigl|\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{M(a,b)}-1\Bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

Note also that by Proposition
1
,

\Delta_{n,P}\ :=\ \max_{a,b\in\mathcal{D}_{n}:P(a,b)\geq\delta_{n}/2}\,\Bigl|\frac{\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{P(a,b)}-1\Bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

For an arbitrary pair
(a,b)\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
, let
(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}]
and
(b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime}]
be minimal intervals with endpoints in

containing
a
and
b

and

because

has a bounded density and subexponential tails. In particular,
P(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime})\geq\delta_{n}-2\gamma_{n}\geq\delta_{n}/2
for sufficiently large

\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{M(a,b)}\ \leq\ \frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b^{\prime\prime})}{M(a,b^{\prime\prime})}\cdot\frac{M(a,b^{\prime\prime})}{M(a,b)}

and

\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b)}{M(a,b)}\ \geq\ \frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b^{\prime})}{M(a,b^{\prime})}\cdot\frac{M(a,b^{\prime})}{M(a,b)},

and by Proposition

are larger than one but not larger than

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b^{\prime\prime})}{M(a,b^{\prime\prime})}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\int_{a}^{a^{\prime\prime}}\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(x,b^{\prime\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x+\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})}{\int_{a}^{a^{\prime\prime}}P(x,b^{\prime\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x+M(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{(1+\Delta_{n,P})\int_{a}^{a^{\prime\prime}}P(x,b^{\prime\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x+(1+\Delta_{n,M})M(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})}{\int_{a}^{a^{\prime\prime}}P(x,b^{\prime\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x+M(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a,b^{\prime})}{M(a,b^{\prime})}\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{\int_{a}^{a^{\prime\prime}}\hat{P}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(x,b^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x+\hat{M}_{n}^{\mathrm{emp}}(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime})}{\int_{a}^{a^{\prime\prime}}P(x,b^{\prime\prime})\,\mathrm{d}x+M(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\ -1/\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(Y_{n}+)\ =\ -1/\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}+)\ \leq\ -1/(\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)-\varepsilon),

whence

\frac{\hat{\mu}_{n}(Y_{n})}{\mu(Y_{n})}\ \leq\ \frac{\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)}{\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)-\varepsilon}\ =\ \bigl(1+\varepsilon/|\varphi^{\prime}(\infty-)|\bigr)^{-1}.

According to Proposition
5
(b), the latter inequality holds true with asymptotic probability zero.

To prove the claim in part (b), recall that for any compact interval
I\subset(a_{o},\infty)
,

\Delta_{n}(I)\ :=\ \max_{x\in I}\,\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

Consequently, there exists a sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
of numbers
a_{n}\in[a,b_{n}]
converging to
\infty
such that even
\Delta_{n}[a,a_{n}]\to_{\mathrm{p}}0
. Hence, it suffices to show that

\max_{x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]}\,\frac{\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr|}{1+|\varphi(x)|}\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\varphi(a_{n})\leq 0
and
\varphi^{\prime}(a_{n}+)<0
. Applying part (a) to
(b_{n})_{n}
and to
(a_{n})_{n}
in place of
(b_{n})_{n}
implies that

\Gamma_{n}\ :=\ \max_{t\in[a_{n},b_{n}]}\,\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(t+)/\varphi^{\prime}(t+)-1\bigr|\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

Thus, uniformly in
x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
,

\displaystyle\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr|\

\displaystyle\leq\ \bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}(a_{n})-\varphi(a_{n})\bigr|+\int_{a_{n}}^{x}\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(t+)-\varphi^{\prime}(t+)\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\ o_{\mathrm{p}}(1)+\Gamma_{n}\int_{a_{n}}^{x}|\varphi^{\prime}(t+)|\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\ o_{\mathrm{p}}(1)+\Gamma_{n}\bigl(\varphi(a_{n})-\varphi(x)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\ o_{\mathrm{p}}(1)+\Gamma_{n}|\varphi(x)|.

Now we prove the claims in part (c). If
a<b<\infty
and and
0<\delta<a-a_{o}
, then for
x\in[a,b]
,

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)-\varphi^{\prime}(x)\

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x-\delta)}{\delta}-\varphi^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\ 2\Delta_{n}[a-\delta,b]/\delta+\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{x-\delta}^{x}\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(s)-\varphi^{\prime}(x)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\ 2\Delta_{n}[a-\delta,b]/\delta+L\delta/2,

where
L
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
\varphi^{\prime}
on
(a_{*},\infty)
. Analogously,

\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)-\varphi^{\prime}(x)\ \geq\ -2\Delta_{n}[a,b+\delta]/\delta-L\delta/2,

Hence,
\max_{x\in[a,b]}\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)-\varphi^{\prime}(x)\bigr|\to_{\mathrm{p}}0
. Consequently, there exist sequences
(a_{n})_{n}
and
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n}
of numbers
a_{n}\in[a,b_{n}]
and
\varepsilon_{n}>0
such that
a_{n}\to\infty
,
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, and with asymptotic probability one,

\max_{x\in[a,a_{n}]}\,\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr|\ <\ \varepsilon_{n},\quad\sup_{x\in[a,a_{n}]}\,\bigl|\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)-\varphi^{\prime}(x)\bigr|\ <\ \varepsilon_{n}.

(15)

Consequently, it suffices to verify the claims of part (c) with
[a_{n},b_{n}]
in place of
[a,b_{n}]
.

At first we show that

\Gamma_{n}\ :=\ \sup_{x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]}\,\Bigl(\frac{\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)}{\varphi^{\prime}(x)}-1\Bigr)^{+}\ \to_{\mathrm{p}}\ 0.

(16)

It follows from Proposition
3
(e,d,a,d) that

\sup_{x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]}\,\frac{\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)}{\varphi^{\prime}(x)}\ =\ (1+o(1))\sup_{x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]}\,\mu(x)|\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)|\ \leq\ (1+o(1))\max_{y\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}:y\leq b_{n}}\,\frac{\mu(y)}{\hat{\mu}_{n}(y)},

because for any
x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
, the point
y:=\max(\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\cap(a_{o},x])
satisfies
y\leq x
and
\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(y+)=\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)\leq\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(a_{n}+)<0
with asymptotic probability one, whence

\mu(x)|\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)|\ \leq\ \mu(y)|\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(x+)|\ =\ \mu(y)|\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(y+)|\ \leq\ \mu(y)/\hat{\mu}_{n}(y).

Now (
16
) follows from the fact that the maximum of
\mu/\hat{\mu}_{n}
over
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\cap(a_{o},b_{n}]
equals
1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1)
, see Proposition
5
(b).

Secondly, with (
16
) at hand, the claim about
(\hat{\varphi}_{n}-\varphi)^{+}/(1+|\varphi|)
can be verified easily. Uniformly in
x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
,

\displaystyle\varphi(x)-\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)\

\displaystyle=\ \varphi(a_{n})-\hat{\varphi}_{n}(a_{n})+\int_{a_{n}}^{x}\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(t)-\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(t+)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\ \varepsilon_{n}+\Gamma_{n}\int_{a_{n}}^{x}|\varphi^{\prime}(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\ \varepsilon_{n}+\Gamma_{n}\bigl(\varphi(a_{n})-\varphi(x)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\ o_{\mathrm{p}}(1)\bigl(1+|\varphi(x)|\bigr).

Finally, we derive an upper bound for
(\hat{\varphi}_{n}-\varphi)^{+}
on
[a_{n},b_{n}]
. To this end we augment the boundary
a_{n}
by a number
a_{n}^{\prime}\in[a,a_{n}]
such that

\varphi^{\prime}(a_{n}^{\prime})\ =\ \min\{\varphi^{\prime}(a),\varphi^{\prime}(a_{n})+1\}.

Since
\varphi^{\prime}(a_{n}^{\prime})\to-\infty
, the sequence
(a_{n}^{\prime})_{n}
converges to
\infty
too. Moreover, assuming that (
15
) holds true, as soon as
\varphi^{\prime}(a_{n}^{\prime})=\varphi^{\prime}(a_{n})+1
and
\varepsilon_{n}<1/2
,

\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(a_{n}^{\prime}+)-\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(a_{n}+)\ \geq\ 1-2\varepsilon_{n}\ >\ 0,

so

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\cap[a_{n}^{\prime},a_{n}]\neq\emptyset)\ \to\ 1.

(17)

For any
x\geq a
let

y(x)\ :=\ x+|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|^{-1}.

This defines an increasing function
y:[a,\infty)\to(a,\infty)
such that

\frac{P(x,y(x))}{P(x,\infty)}\ \to\ 1-e^{-1}\quad\text{as}\ x\to\infty.

(18)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{P(x,y(x))}{P(x,\infty)}\

\displaystyle=\ \int_{0}^{y(x)-x}\exp\bigl(\varphi(x+s)-\log\bar{F}(x)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\ |\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\int_{0}^{y(x)-x}\exp(-|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\ 1-e^{-1},

because the distribution with density
s\mapsto\exp\bigl(\varphi(x+s)-\log\bar{F}(x)\bigr)
on
(0,\infty)
is stochastically smaller than the exponential distribution with rate
|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|
. On the other hand, since
-|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|s\geq\varphi(x+s)-\varphi(x)\geq-|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|s-Ls^{2}/2
for
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\frac{P(x,y(x))}{P(x,\infty)}\

\displaystyle\leq\ \int_{0}^{y(x)-x}\exp\bigl(-|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|s\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s\Big/\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\bigl(-|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|s-Ls^{2}/2\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\ (1-e^{-1})\Big/\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y}\exp(-L\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{-2}y^{2}/2\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\ \exp(L\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{-2})(1-e^{-1})

by Jensen’s inequality.

Now we define
b_{n}^{\prime}:=y(b_{n})
and
b_{n}^{\prime\prime}:=y(b_{n}^{\prime})
. Then it follows from (
18
) that

\frac{1-F(b_{n}^{\prime\prime})}{\rho_{n}}\ =\ (e^{-2}+o(1))\frac{1-F(b_{n})}{\rho_{n}}\ \to\ \infty

Hence, (
16
) remains valid with
[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime\prime}]
in place of
[a_{n},b_{n}]
. Furthermore, (
18
) implies that

\inf_{x\in[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime}]}\frac{P(x,y(x))}{\rho_{n}}\ =\ (1-e^{-1}+o(1))\frac{1-F(b_{n}^{\prime})}{\rho_{n}}\ \to\ \infty.

Consequently, by Corollary
7
,

\max_{x\in[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime}]\cap\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}}\,\frac{\hat{W}_{n}(x,y(x))}{W(x,y(x))}\ \leq\ 1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1).

On the other hand, Proposition
3
(c) implies that for
x\in[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime}]
,

\displaystyle\hat{W}_{n}(x,y(x))\

\displaystyle\geq\ \hat{f}_{n}(x)\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{-2}V\bigl(-\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(y(x)+)/\varphi(x)\bigr),

\displaystyle W(x,y(x))\

\displaystyle\leq\ f(x)\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{-2}V(-1),

because
\varphi^{\prime}<0
on
[a,\infty)
, and it follows from (
16
) with
[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime\prime}]
in place of
[a_{n},b_{n}]
, Lipschitz-continuity of
\varphi^{\prime}
on
[a,\infty)
with constant
L
and Proposition
2
that

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime}]}\,\frac{V\bigl(-\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(y(x)+)/\varphi(x)\bigr)}{V(-1)}\

\displaystyle\geq\ \inf_{x\in[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime}]}\,\frac{V\bigl(-(1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1))\varphi^{\prime}(y(x))/\varphi^{\prime}(x)\bigr)}{V(-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\ \frac{V\bigl(-(1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1))(1+L\varphi^{\prime}(a_{n}^{\prime})^{-2})\bigr)}{V(-1)}

\displaystyle=\ 1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1),

because
\varphi^{\prime}(y(x))/\varphi^{\prime}(x)\geq-1-L\varphi^{\prime}(x)^{-2}\geq-1-L\varphi^{\prime}(a_{n}^{\prime})^{-2}\to-1
. Consequently,

\max_{x\in[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime}]\cap\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}}\,\frac{\hat{f}_{n}(x)}{f(x)}\ \leq\ 1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1),

which is equivalent to

\max_{x\in[a_{n}^{\prime},b_{n}^{\prime}]\cap\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}}\,\bigl(\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr)^{+}\ =\ o_{\mathrm{p}}(1).

Since
\hat{\varphi}_{n}-\varphi
is convex between consecutive knots in
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}
, and since
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\cap[a_{n}^{\prime},a_{n}]\neq\emptyset
with asymptotic probability one by (
17
), this implies that

\max_{x\in[a_{n},\hat{b}_{n}]}\,\bigl(\hat{\varphi}_{n}(x)-\varphi(x)\bigr)^{+}\ =\ o_{\mathrm{p}}(1),

where
\hat{b}_{n}:=\max(\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\cap(-\infty,b_{n}^{\prime}])
. This proves already our claim in case of
\hat{b}_{n}\geq b_{n}
. So it remains to show that

\frac{\hat{f}_{n}(b_{n})}{f(b_{n})}\ \leq\ 1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1)\quad\text{if}\ \hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\cap[b_{n},b_{n}^{\prime}]=\emptyset.

With
\check{b}_{n}:=\min(\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{n}\cap[b_{n}^{\prime},\infty))
, the inequality
\hat{b}_{n}\leq b_{n}
implies that
\hat{\varphi}_{n}
is linear on
[b_{n},\check{b}_{n}]
, and
P(b_{n},\check{b}_{n})/\rho_{n}\geq P(b_{n},b_{n}^{\prime})/\rho_{n}\geq(1-e^{-1}+o(1))(1-F(b_{n}))/\rho_{n}\to\infty
. Hence Proposition
6
(b) implies that

\frac{\hat{M}_{n}(b_{n},\check{b}_{n})}{M(b_{n},\check{b}_{n})}\ \leq\ 1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1).

On the other hand, Proposition
3
(c,e) implies that

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{n}(b_{n},\check{b}_{n})\

\displaystyle\geq\ \hat{f}_{n}(b_{n})(\check{b}_{n}-b_{n})^{2}N\bigl(\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}+)(\check{b}_{n}-b_{n})\bigr),

\displaystyle M(b_{n},\check{b}_{n})\

\displaystyle\leq\ f(b_{n})(\check{b}_{n}-b_{n})^{2}N\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(b_{n})(\check{b}_{n}-b_{n})\bigr),

and it follows from (
16
), Lipschitz-continuity of
\varphi^{\prime}
on
[a,\infty)
with constant
L
and Proposition
2
that

\frac{N\bigl(\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{\prime}(b_{n}+)(\check{b}_{n}-b_{n})\bigr)}{N\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(b_{n})(\check{b}_{n}-b_{n})\bigr)}\ \geq\ \frac{N\bigl((1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1))\varphi^{\prime}(b_{n})(\check{b}_{n}-b_{n})\bigr)}{N\bigl(\varphi^{\prime}(b_{n})(\check{b}_{n}-b_{n})\bigr)}\ =\ 1+o_{\mathrm{p}}(1).

The latter conclusion follows from the fact that for arbitrary sequences
(t_{n})_{n}
in
(-\infty,0]
,
N\bigl((1+o(1))t_{n}\bigr)/N(t_{n})=1+o(1)
. For a bounded sequence
(t_{n})_{n}
this follows from continuity of
N
. If
t_{n}\to-\infty
, this follows from the expansion
N\bigl((1+o(1))t_{n}\bigr)=(1+o(1))t_{n}^{-2}
.

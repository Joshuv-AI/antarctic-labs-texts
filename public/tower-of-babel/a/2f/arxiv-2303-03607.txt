1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
\textsf{Irr}(G)
be the set of complex irreducible characters of
G
. Denote the set of character degrees of
G
by
\mathsf{cd}(G)
, and when the context allows us the set of irreducible character degrees will be referred to as the set of character degrees. There are various examples showing that the set of character degrees of
G
cannot completely determine the structure of
G
even for nilpotent and solvable groups.
For example, the non-isomorphic groups
\mathrm{D}_{8}
and
\mathrm{Q}_{8}
not only have the same set of character degrees, but also share the same character table and
\mathsf{cd}(\mathrm{Q}_{8})=\mathsf{cd}(\mathrm{S}_{3})=\{1,2\}
. However, in the late 1990s, Huppert
[
15
]
posed a conjecture which, if true, would sharpen the connection between the character degree set of a non-abelian simple group and the structure of the group.

Conjecture 1.1

(Huppert)
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and let
H
be a finite non-abelian simple group such that the sets of character degrees of
G
and
H
are the same. Then
G\cong H\times A
, where
A
is an abelian group.

The conjecture asserts that the non-abelian simple groups are essentially characterized by the set of their character degrees. This conjecture is verified for alternating groups, sporadic simple groups, some finite simple groups of Lie type including the Ree groups
{}^{2}\mathrm{G}_{2}(q)
, see for example
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
15
,
16
,
24
,
30
,
31
]
. Since 2016, we have started to study the structure of groups with the same character degrees as almost simple groups including those with socle
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)

[
12
,
2
]
, Suzuki groups
[
6
]
, Ree groups
[
3
]
and sporadic simple groups
[
4
,
5
]
. There are several examples provided in
[
4
,
5
,
12
]
which show that Huppert’s conjecture cannot be extended to almost simple groups. If
H
is an almost simple group with socle
H_{0}=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(2^{f})
with
f
prime, Daneshkhah
[
12
]
proved that there is an abelian group
A
such that
G/A
is isomorphic to
H
. In particular, she proved that
G^{\prime}=H_{0}
and
A=\mathbf{Z}(G)
with only one exception, namely, when
H=\textsf{Aut}(\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(4))
in which
G^{\prime}=H_{0}
or
2\cdot H_{0}
, see
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
. It seems that if
G
is a group with the same character degrees as an almost simple group
H
with socle
H_{0}
, then
G^{\prime}=H_{0}
and
G/\mathbf{Z}(G)
is isomorphic to
H
with a few exceptions for which
G^{\prime}
is an extension of an abelian normal subgroup of
G
by
H_{0}
in which cases we still have
G/A\cong H
with
A
central in
G
.
In this paper, we continue our investigation to support this statement which is the almost simple group version of Huppert’s conjecture, and study groups whose character degrees are the same as those of almost simple groups with socle Ree groups:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
H
is an almost simple group
with socle
H_{0}=\,^{2}\mathrm{G}_{2}(q)
, where
q=3^{f}
with
f\geqslant 3
odd. Let
G
be a finite group with
\mathsf{cd}(G)=\mathsf{cd}(H)
. Then
G
is nonsolvable and the chief factor
G^{\prime}/M
of
G
is isomorphic to
H_{0}
. If
f
is coprime to
3
, then
G^{\prime}\cong H_{0}
and
G/\mathbf{Z}(G)
is isomorphic to
H
.

Note that Theorem
1.1
for
H=\,^{2}\mathrm{G}_{2}(q)
is proved by Wakefield
[
31
]
, and so we need only to focus on the case where
{}^{2}\mathrm{G}_{2}(q)\neq H\leqslant\textsf{Aut}(^{2}\mathrm{G}_{2}(q))
. In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, it is worth noting that we do not need to determine all the character degrees of
H
. We use several useful facts proved in Lemma
3.2
on the character degrees of
G
. For example, one of the key result is the fact that the
2
-part of the character degrees of
G
divides
2^{3}
.
It is unfortunate to state that our method does not work when
3\mid f
, in which case, we think that
G^{\prime}
may not be isomorphic to
H_{0}
but we still believe that
G/A
is isomorphic to
H
, for some abelian subgroup
A
of
G
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some useful results to prove Theorem
1.1
. We first establish some definitions and notation. Throughout this paper all groups are finite. A finite group of order

We use
\mathrm{C}_{n}

\mathrm{A}_{n}

A group

with socle

H_{0}\leqslant H\leqslant\textsf{Aut}(H_{0})

in

of

Lemma 2.1

(
[
14
, Theorems 19.5 and 21.3]
)
.

Suppose that
N\unlhd G
and
\chi\in\textsf{Irr}(G)
.

(i)

If
\chi_{N}=\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{k}
with
\theta_{i}\in{\rm{\textsf{Irr}}}(N)
, then

divides
|G/N|
. In particular, if
\chi(1)
is prime to
|G/N|
, then
\chi_{N}\in\textsf{Irr}(N)
.

(ii)

(Gallagher’s Theorem) If
\chi_{N}\in\textsf{Irr}(N)
, then
\chi\psi\in\textsf{Irr}(G)
for all
\psi\in\textsf{Irr}(G/N)
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
14
, Theorems 19.6 and 21.2]
)
.

Suppose that
N\unlhd G
and
\theta\in{\rm{\textsf{Irr}}}(N)
. Let
I=\mathsf{I}_{G}(\theta)
.

(i)

If
\theta^{I}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\phi_{i}
with
\phi_{i}\in{\rm{\textsf{Irr}}}(I)
, then
\phi_{i}^{G}\in{\rm{\textsf{Irr}}}(G)
. In particular,
\phi_{i}(1)|G:I|\in\mathsf{cd}(G)
.

(ii)

If
\theta
extends to
\varphi\in{\rm{\textsf{Irr}}}(I)
, then
(\varphi\tau)^{G}\in{\rm{\textsf{Irr}}}(G)
for all
\tau\in{\rm{\textsf{Irr}}}(I/N)
. In particular,
\theta(1)\tau(1)|G:I|\in{\rm{\mathsf{cd}}}(G)
.

A character
\chi\in\textrm{{Irr}}(G)
is said to be
isolated
in
G
if
\chi(1)
is divisible by no proper non-trivial character degree of
G
and no proper multiple of
\chi(1)
is a character degree of
G
. In this situation, we also say that
\chi(1)
is an
isolated degree
of
G
.
We define a
proper power
degree of
G
to be a character degree of
G
of the form
c^{a}
for integers

with
a>1
. A
mixed
degree of
G
is a character degree which is divisible by at least two distinct primes.

(

)
.

Let

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
G^{\prime}
being a non-abelian finite simple group. Then

,
\mathbf{C}_{G}(G^{\prime})=\mathbf{Z}(G)
and
G/\mathbf{Z}(G)
is an almost simple group with socle
G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let

and

. Since

is a non-abelian simple group,

, and hence

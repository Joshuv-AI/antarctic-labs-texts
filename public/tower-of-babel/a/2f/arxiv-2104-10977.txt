Abstract
Intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRSs) enable multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) transmitters to modify the communication channels between the transmitters and receivers. In the presence of eavesdropping terminals, this degree of freedom can be used to effectively suppress the information leakage towards such malicious terminals. This leads to significant potential secrecy gains in IRS-aided MIMO systems. This work exploits these gains via a tractable joint design of downlink beamformers and IRS phase-shifts. In this respect, we consider a generic IRS-aided MIMO wiretap setting and invoke fractional programming and alternating optimization techniques to iteratively find the beamformers and phase-shifts that maximize the achievable weighted secrecy sum-rate. Our design concludes two low-complexity algorithms for joint beamforming and phase-shift tuning. Performance of the proposed algorithms are numerically evaluated and compared to the benchmark. The results reveal that integrating IRSs into MIMO systems not only boosts the secrecy performance of the system, but also improves the robustness against passive eavesdropping.

Contributions

As mentioned, the results in the literature imply that integrating

•

First the secrecy performance of an

To cope with the non-convexity, we initially derive a parameterized variational problem whose solution returns the optimal design. We then invoke

(

)
[
49
,
50
]
to transform the variational problem into a quadratic problem.

[
51
]
, in which the authors use

for weighted sum-rate maximization in an
irs
-aided

system. Although the derivations in
[
51
]
consider a generic
irs
-aided

system, extending them to a wiretap setting is not straightforward. We address this issue by dividing the variational optimization into multiple

problems and by

bounding the objective of each marginal problem. The final solution is then found by following the
ao
(
ao
) strategy.

•

Using

•

To confirm the validity of our derivations, we investigate the performance of the proposed algorithms by running several numerical experiments. The results are compared to multiple reference scenarios, e.g., the scenarios with no

\displaystyle\mathrm{SINR}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)=\frac{|\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}({\boldsymbol{\phi}}){\mathbf{w}}_{k}|^{2}}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,i\neq k}^{K}|\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}({\boldsymbol{\phi}}){\mathbf{w}}_{i}|^{2}+\sigma_{k}^{2}}.

(17)

•

\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{e}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)
is an upper bound on the maximum information leakage to the eavesdroppers and is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{e}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)=\log\left(1+\mathrm{ESNR}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)\right)

(18)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ESNR}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)=\left\lVert\mathbf{\Upsilon}_{\mathrm{e}}\mathbf{\tilde{G}^{\mathsf{H}}}({\boldsymbol{\phi}}){\mathbf{w}}_{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

(19)

By plugging (

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{s}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)=\left[\log\left(\dfrac{1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)}{1+\mathrm{ESNR}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)}\right)\right]^{+}.

The above inner bound is derived by considering the

In the sequel, we utilize this inner bound to quantify the secrecy throughput of the system. To this end, we define the

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ssr}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{s}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)

III
Jointly Optimal Precoding and Phase-Shifting

Considering the problem formulation, the design parameters in this system are the precoding vectors at the
bs
and phase-shifts applied by the
irs
-unit. As a result, the ultimate goal of this work is to design a joint precoding and phase-shifting algorithm that maximizes the weighted secrecy sum-rate.

With respect to the given performance metric, the optimal design for the precoding matrix and phase shifts is given via the following constrained optimization
\mathcal{P}_{1}
:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}\left.\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ssr}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)\right.

(
\mathcal{P}_{1}
)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}

(22a)

\displaystyle\phantom{\text{subject to }}\lvert\phi_{n}\rvert=1,~\forall n\in\left[N\right].

(22b)

Here, (
22a
) ensures that the maximum transmit power is kept below the
bs

P_{\mathrm{max}}
, and (
22b
) restricts the unit modulus constraints imposed by the physical characteristics of the
irs
elements.

The optimization problem
\mathcal{P}_{1}
contains two analytical challenges:

(a)

The objective function is not differentiable with respect to the optimization variables. This follows from the fact that the non-negative operator, i.e.,
f\left(x\right)=\left[x\right]^{+}
, is not differentiable.

(b)

Even by replacing the non-negative operators with their arguments in
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ssr}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)
,
\mathcal{P}_{1}
is still
non-convex
. This is due to non-convexity of the objective function and the unit modulus constraints in (
22b
).

The above challenges indicate that the global optimum of
\mathcal{P}_{1}
can not be computed via classical convex programming techniques. In fact, it requires a brute-force search that is not a computationally tractable option even for not-so-large dimensions.

We address these issues as follows: First, a variational optimization problem is derived whose objective is differentiable and whose solution coincides with the solution of
\mathcal{P}_{1}
. We then develop a two-tiers algorithm that uses
bcd
(
bcd
) and

to approximate the solution of the variational problem.

The Variational Problem

Theorem 1

(The variational problem)
.

Let

. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\star}=

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\mathbf{b}}\left.\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ssr}}_{\mathrm{q}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\mathbf{b}\right)\right.

(
\mathcal{P}_{2}
)

\displaystyle\text{subject to }\left\lVert\mathbf{W}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\leq P_{\mathrm{max}}

(23a)

\displaystyle\phantom{\text{subject to }}\lvert\phi_{n}\rvert=1,~\forall n\in\left[N\right],

(23b)

\displaystyle\phantom{\text{subject to }}\mathbf{b}\in\left[0,1\right]^{K}

(23c)

where the objective function

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84526pt\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ssr}}_{\mathrm{q}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\mathbf{b}\right)\hskip-2.84526pt=\hskip-2.84526pt\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}b_{k}\log\left(\dfrac{1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)}{1+\mathrm{ESNR}_{k}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)}\right)

The equivalency of

We start the derivations with the first tier: Considering the optimization problem

\mathbf{W}

{\boldsymbol{\phi}}
, and
\mathbf{b}

]

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, two major iterative algorithms for secure precoding and phase-shift tuning in
irs
-aided
mimo
systems were proposed, and their applications were investigated through multiple numerical experiments. The proposed algorithms determine the system parameters in terms of the
csi
. This means that the proposed algorithms are required to be run, once per a coherence time interval. From the computational viewpoint, this is a tractable task in many practical scenarios.

Since the proposed algorithms consider the weighted secrecy sum-rate as the objective, the information leakage suppression is implicitly applied. This is confirmed by the numerical investigations which show similar behavior as the one observed in
srzf
precoding
[
63
]
. The results further indicate that
irs
can boost the secrecy performance of the system significantly. These two findings, along with the cost-efficiency of integrating
irs
, suggest that
irs
ia a good candidate for establishing secure communications in
mimo
systems.

The results of this study are given under several idealistic assumptions and can be extended in various aspects. A natural direction is to extend the derivations to more realistic scenarios with imperfect
csi
acquisition. In this respect, one can adopt the proposed scheme to more realistic settings, e.g., by considering the study in
[
67
]
. Robustness of the proposed scheme against the unavailability of eavesdroppers’
csi
is also an interesting direction for future work. Another line of work is to develop the proposed approach for settings in which the eavesdroppers increase their obtained leakage rate by actively contaminating the uplink pilots.

Now, we define the difference term

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ssr}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)-\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ssr}}_{\mathrm{t}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\mathbf{b}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}\left(1-b_{k}\right)\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\mathrm{s}}\left(\mathbf{W},{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right).

Noting that

(103)

\displaystyle f\left(\mathbf{x}_{0}\right)

Lemma 3

(Lemma 1 of

In this appendix, we tackle the non-convex unit modulus optimization

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{\left(t\right)}_{n}\left(\varphi\right)=\left[\phi_{1}^{\left(t-1\right)},\ldots,\phi_{n-1}^{\left(t-1\right)},\varphi,\phi_{n+1}^{\left(t-1\right)},\ldots,\phi_{N}^{\left(t-1\right)}\right]^{\mathsf{T}}

(125)

with

Defining the function

\mathbf{U}

\displaystyle f^{\left(t\right)}\left(\varphi\right)=\left[\mathbf{U}\right]_{nn}\lvert\varphi\rvert^{2}+2\left.\Re\left\{a_{n}^{\left(t\right)}\varphi^{*}\right\}\right.+c_{n}^{\left(t\right)}

\displaystyle a_{n}^{\left(t\right)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{v}_{n}+\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{\mathsf{H}}\tilde{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}}_{n}^{\left(t-1\right)}

Following the notation of Algorithm
3
, let

t
and
t+1
, respectively. We define the following functions:

\displaystyle f_{Q}\left(\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{W}\right)

\displaystyle=Q_{1}^{\mathrm{m}}\left(\mathbf{W},\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)+Q_{1}^{\mathrm{e}}\left(\mathbf{W},\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right)+\log B_{1U}^{\mathrm{e}}

(130a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rm M}^{\mathrm{ssr}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{q}}^{\mathrm{ssr}}\left(\mathbf{W},\bar{{\boldsymbol{\phi}}},\bar{\mathbf{b}}\right)

(130b)

with
Q_{1}^{\mathrm{m}}\left(\mathbf{W},\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)
,
Q_{1}^{\mathrm{e}}\left(\mathbf{W},\boldsymbol{\gamma}\right)
, and
B_{1U}^{\mathrm{e}}
as defined in Section
V-A
. Moreover, let us define vectors

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\beta}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\beta_{1}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right),\ldots,\beta_{K}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)\right],

(131a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\gamma_{1}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right),\ldots,\gamma_{K}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)\right]

(131b)

for a given precoding matrix
\mathbf{W}
, where

\displaystyle\beta_{k}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\displaystyle\sqrt{\omega_{k}\bar{b}_{k}\left(1+\frac{A_{1k}^{\rm m}\left({\mathbf{W}}\right)}{B_{1k}^{\rm m}\left({\mathbf{W}}\right)}\right)}\left.\tilde{\mathbf{h}}^{\mathsf{H}}_{k}\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right){\mathbf{w}}_{k}\right.}{A_{1k}^{\rm m}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)+B_{1k}^{\rm m}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)},

(132a)

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\displaystyle\omega_{k}\bar{b}_{k}\left(1+\frac{A_{1k}^{\rm e}\left({\mathbf{W}}\right)}{B_{1k}^{\rm e}\left({\mathbf{W}}\right)}\right)}{B_{1U}^{\rm e}}}\sqrt{A_{1k}^{\rm e}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)}.

(132b)

By substitution, it is straightforward to show that in iteration
t
of Algorithm
3
,

and
\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\bar{\mathbf{W}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rm M}^{\mathrm{ssr}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right)=f_{Q}\left(\boldsymbol{\beta}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right),\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathrm{F}}\left(\mathbf{W}\right),\mathbf{W}\right).

\mathbf{W}

\boldsymbol{\beta}
and

\mathbf{W}

\displaystyle f_{Q}\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},\mathbf{W}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\dagger}}{{=}}f_{Q}\left(\bar{\boldsymbol{\beta}},\bar{\boldsymbol{\gamma}},\bar{\mathbf{W}}\right)

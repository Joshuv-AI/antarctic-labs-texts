Gradient inequality and convergence of the normalized Ricci flow
Nikos I. Kavallaris, Takashi Suzuki

Abstract
. We study the problem of convergence of the normalized Ricci flow evolving on a compact manifold Ω \Omega without boundary. In [ 11 , 12 ] we derived, via PDE techniques, global-in-time existence of the classical solution and pre-compactness of the orbit. In this work we show its convergence to steady-states, using a gradient inequality of Łojasiewicz type. We have thus an alternative proof of [ 7 ] , but for general manifold Ω \Omega and not only for unit sphere. As a byproduct of that approach we also derive the rate of convergence according to this steady-sate being either degenerate or non-degenerate as a critical point of a related energy functional.

Theory of Critical Manifolds

Under the change of variables

\displaystyle\frac{\partial e^{w}}{\partial t}=\Delta w+\lambda\left(\frac{e^{w}}{\int_{\Omega}e^{w}\,dx}-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\right),\quad x\in\Omega,\;\;t>0,

(2.2)

Hence it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial e^{w}}{\partial t}=\Delta w+e^{w}-\frac{{\lambda}}{|\Omega|},\quad x\in\Omega,\;\;t>0

(2.4)

In relation to the Gel’fand triple

the first variation of

is given by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{E}(w)=-\Delta w-e^{w}+\frac{{\lambda}}{|{\Omega}|}

and thus (
2.3
)-(

\displaystyle\frac{\partial e^{w}}{\partial t}=-\delta\mathcal{E}(w),\quad x\in\Omega,\;\;t>0

\displaystyle w(x,0)=w_{0}(x),\quad x\in\Omega,\quad\int_{\Omega}e^{w_{0}}\,dx=\lambda.

Each steady-state

to (

)-(

This section is devoted to the proof of the following inequality, which casts a basis for proving Theorem

as in the standard theory of gradient inequality, cf.

Theorem 2.1
.

Given
w^{*}\in V
satisfying
\delta\mathcal{E}(w^{\ast})=0
, there exist
0<\theta\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
such that

(2.9)

w\in V,\ ||w-w^{*}||_{V}<{\varepsilon}_{0}\ \Rightarrow\ |\mathcal{E}(w)-\mathcal{E}(w^{*})|^{1-\theta}\leq C||\delta\mathcal{E}(w)||_{V^{*}}.

To prove this result we decompose
\mathcal{E}(w)
as in

\mathcal{E}(w)=\mathcal{E}_{1}(w)-\mathcal{E}_{2}(w)

for

\mathcal{E}_{1}(w)=\int_{{\Omega}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}+\frac{{\lambda}}{|{\Omega}|}w\ dx,\quad\mathcal{E}_{2}(w)=\int_{{\Omega}}e^{w}\,dx.

The first functional
\mathcal{E}_{1}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
is analytic, and it holds that

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{E}_{1}(w_{\ast})[w]=\int_{{\Omega}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla w_{\ast}+\frac{{\lambda}}{|{\Omega}|}\ dx

\displaystyle\delta^{2}\mathcal{E}_{1}(w_{\ast})[w,w]=\int_{{\Omega}}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx=(\nabla w,\nabla w)

\displaystyle\delta^{k}\mathcal{E}_{1}(w_{\ast})[\overbrace{w,w,\cdots,w}^{k}]=0,\ k\geq 3,\quad w,w_{\ast}\in V,

where
(\ ,\ )
denotes the
L^{2}
-inner product. The second functional
\mathcal{E}_{2}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
is also analytic by the Trudinger-Moser-Fontana inequality (
1.24
), which assures

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{{\Omega}}e^{w_{\ast}}|w|^{k}\,dx=\int_{{\Omega}}e^{w^{\ast}+|w|}\,dx<+\infty,\quad w,w_{\ast}\in V.

Then

\mathcal{E}_{2}(w+w_{\ast})-\mathcal{E}_{2}(w_{\ast})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\int_{{\Omega}}e^{w_{\ast}}w^{k}\,dx

and hence

\delta^{k}\mathcal{E}_{2}(w_{\ast})[\overbrace{w,w,\cdots,w}^{k}]=\int_{\Omega}e^{w_{\ast}}w^{k}\ dx,\quad k\geq 1.

Given
w^{\ast}\in V
satisfying
\delta\mathcal{E}(w^{\ast})=0

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\equiv\delta^{2}\mathcal{E}(w^{*})=-\Delta-e^{w^{*}}:V\to V^{*}

is realized as a self-adjoint operator in

with domain

. To develop the theory of critical manifold, cf.
[

]

\displaystyle X_{1}\equiv\mbox{Ker}\ \mathcal{L}=\{v\in D(\mathcal{L})\big|\mathcal{L}v=0\}\subset V=H^{1}(\Omega).

Let

\mathcal{P}:X\to X_{1}

\mathcal{P}v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(v,\phi_{i})\phi_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\phi_{i},v\rangle_{V,V^{\ast}}\phi_{i}.

We next recall the following theorem, derived from the implicit function theorem applied to

(I-\mathcal{P})\delta\mathcal{E}(v)=0.

The local manifold

Theorem 2.2

(

5

Lemma 2.3
.

It holds that

|\mathcal{E}(w)-\mathcal{E}(Qw)|\leq C||w-Qw||^{2}_{V},\quad w\in U.

Proof.

First, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(w)-\mathcal{E}(Qw)

(2.15)

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle w-Qw,\delta\mathcal{E}(Qw)\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\mathcal{E}(Qw)[w-Qw,w-Qw]+o\left(||w-Qw||_{V}^{2}\right).

Second, there arises

(I-\mathcal{P})(w-Qw)=w-Qw

by (

), and therefore,
Qw\in\mathcal{S}
implies

\displaystyle\langle w-Qw,\delta\mathcal{E}(Qw)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(I-\mathcal{P})(w-Qw),\delta\mathcal{E}(Qw)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle w-Qw,(I-\mathcal{P})\delta\mathcal{E}(Qw)\rangle=0.

Then (

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}(w)-\mathcal{E}(Qw)|\leq C||w-Qw||^{2}_{V}.

∎

Lemma 2.5

\displaystyle C_{1}\left|\left|(I-\mathcal{P})\delta^{2}\mathcal{E}(Qw)(w-Qw)\right|\right|_{V^{*}}

Lemma 2.6

Lemma 3.1

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}J_{\lambda}(v^{\ast}+s\phi)\right|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi-\frac{{\lambda}e^{v^{*}}}{\int_{{\Omega}}e^{v^{*}}\,dx}\phi+\frac{{\lambda}\int_{{\Omega}}e^{v^{*}}\phi\,dx}{\left(\int_{{\Omega}}e^{v^{*}}\,dx\right)^{2}}\ e^{v^{*}}

Projections of modular forms on Eisenstein series and its application to Siegel’s formula
Zafer Selcuk Aygin

Abstract
. Let k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 and N N be positive integers and let χ \chi be a Dirichlet character modulo N N . Let f ⁡ ( z ) f(z) be a modular form in M k ​ ( Γ 0 ​ ( N ) , χ ) M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi) . Then we have a unique decomposition f ⁡ ( z ) = E f ​ ( z ) + S f ​ ( z ) f(z)=E_{f}(z)+S_{f}(z) , where E f ​ ( z ) ∈ E k ​ ( Γ 0 ​ ( N ) , χ ) E_{f}(z)\in E_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi) and S f ​ ( z ) ∈ S k ​ ( Γ 0 ​ ( N ) , χ ) S_{f}(z)\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi) . In this paper we give an explicit formula for E f ​ ( z ) E_{f}(z) in terms of Eisenstein series. Then we apply our result to certain families of eta quotients and to representations of positive integers by 2 ​ k 2k –ary positive definite quadratic forms in order to give an alternative version of Siegel’s formula for the weighted average number of representations of an integer by quadratic forms in the same genus. Our formula for the latter is in terms of generalized divisor functions and does not involve computation of local densities.

4.
Orthogonal relations

In this section we prove some orthogonal relations involving the functions
\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(d,c)
and

defined in (

) and (
1.8

of Theorem

. Throughout the section we assume

and

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p\mid N
be a prime and let
t\mid N/p^{v}
, where
v=v_{p}(N)
, then for
0\leq i\leq v
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k,N,\epsilon,\psi}(t\cdot p^{i},d)=\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})\mathcal{S}_{k,N/p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(t,d/p^{v_{p}(d)}).

Proof.

Since
t\mid N/p^{v}
we have
\gcd(t,p)=1
. Using the multiplicative properties of the Möbius function we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k,N,\epsilon,\psi}(t\cdot p^{i},d)=\mu\left(\frac{t\cdot p^{i}\cdot d}{\gcd(t\cdot p^{i},d)^{2}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\mid\gcd(t\cdot p^{i},d),\\
0<v_{p_{2}}(t\cdot p^{i})=v_{p_{2}}(d)<v_{p_{2}}(N)\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{p_{2}^{k}+\epsilon(p_{2})\overline{\psi}(p_{2})}{p_{2}^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu\left(\frac{p^{i}\cdot p^{v_{p}(d)}}{\gcd(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})^{2}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\mid\gcd(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)}),\\
0<v_{p_{2}}(p^{i})=v_{p_{2}}(p^{v_{p}(d)})<v_{p_{2}}(N)\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{p_{2}^{k}+\epsilon(p_{2})\overline{\psi}(p_{2})}{p_{2}^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\mu\left(\frac{t\cdot d/p^{v_{p}(d)}}{\gcd(t,d/p^{v_{p}(d)})^{2}}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}\mid\gcd(t,d/p^{v_{p}(d)}),\\
0<v_{p_{2}}(t)=v_{p_{2}}(d/p^{v_{p}(d)})<v_{p_{2}}(N/p^{v_{p}(d)})\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{p_{2}^{k}+\epsilon(p_{2})\overline{\psi}(p_{2})}{p_{2}^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})\mathcal{S}_{k,N/p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(t,d/p^{v_{p}(d)}).

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\gcd(t,p^{i})=1
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t\cdot p^{i})

\displaystyle=\epsilon(-1)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c/p^{v_{p}(c)},t),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(t\cdot p^{i},d)

\displaystyle=\epsilon(-1)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(t,d/p^{v_{p}(d)}).

Proof.

By elementary manipulations we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t\cdot p^{i})

\displaystyle=\epsilon\left(\frac{-c}{\gcd(c,t\cdot p^{i})}\right)\overline{\psi}\left(\frac{t\cdot p^{i}}{\gcd(c,t\cdot p^{i})}\right)\left(\frac{\gcd(c,t\cdot p^{i})}{t\cdot p^{i}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\left(\frac{-c/p^{v_{c}}}{\gcd(c/p^{v_{c}},t)}\right)\overline{\psi}\left(\frac{t}{\gcd(c/p^{v_{p}(c)},t)}\right)\left(\frac{\gcd(c/p^{v_{p}(c)},t)}{t}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad\times\epsilon\left(\frac{p^{v_{p}(c)}}{\gcd(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})}\right)\overline{\psi}\left(\frac{p^{i}}{\gcd(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})}\right)\left(\frac{\gcd(p^{v_{c}},p^{i})}{p^{i}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=\epsilon(-1)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c/p^{v_{p}(c)},t).

Proof of the second equation is similar.

∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

, then

\displaystyle\sum_{t\mid N}\mathcal{S}_{k,N,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(t,d)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if $c\neq d$,}\\
\displaystyle\prod_{p\mid N}\frac{p^{k}-\epsilon(p)\overline{\psi}(p)}{p^{k}}&\mbox{ if $c=d$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
p\mid N
be prime and
v_{p}(N)=v

4.1
and
4.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t\mid N}\mathcal{S}_{k,N,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(t,d)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\sum_{t\mid N/p^{v}}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{S}_{k,N/p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(c/p^{v_{p}(c)},t)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\epsilon(-1)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c/p^{v_{p}(c)},t)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\epsilon(-1)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(t,d/p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\sum_{t\mid N/p^{v}}\mathcal{S}_{k,N/p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(c/p^{v_{p}(c)},t)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c/p^{v_{p}(c)},t)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(t,c/p^{v_{p}(d)}).

\displaystyle\sum_{t\mid N}\mathcal{S}_{k,N,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(t,d)

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{p\mid N}\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)}).

Now we prove for all

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\frac{p^{k}-\epsilon(p)\overline{\psi}(p)}{p^{k}}&\mbox{ if $v_{p}(c)=v_{p}(d)$.}\end{cases}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if $|v_{p}(c)-i|>1$,}\\
\displaystyle\frac{p^{k}+\epsilon(p)\overline{\psi}(p)}{p^{k}}&\mbox{ if $i=v_{p}(c)$ and $v>v_{p}(c)>0$,}\\

1&\mbox{ if $i=v_{p}(c)$ and $v_{p}(c)=0$,}\\
-1&\mbox{ if $i=v_{p}(c)-1$ and $v_{p}(c)>0$,}\\

i

|v_{p}(c)-i|>1
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})=0.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)-1})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)-1})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)-1},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)+1})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)+1})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)+1},p^{v_{p}(d)}),

which, by (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), equals to

\displaystyle=-1\cdot\epsilon(-p)\cdot\epsilon(-1)\overline{\psi}(p^{v_{p}(d)-v_{p}(c)+1})\left(\frac{1}{p^{v_{p}(d)-v_{p}(c)+1}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{p^{k}+\epsilon(p)\overline{\psi}(p)}{p^{k}}\cdot\epsilon(-1)\cdot\epsilon(-1)\overline{\psi}(p^{v_{p}(d)-v_{p}(c)})\left(\frac{1}{p^{v_{p}(d)-v_{p}(c)}}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)\cdot\epsilon(-1)\overline{\psi}(p)\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k}\cdot\epsilon(-1)\overline{\psi}(p^{v_{p}(d)-v_{p}(c)-1})\left(\frac{1}{p^{v_{p}(d)-v_{p}(c)-1}}\right)^{k}.

By using multiplicative properties of Dirichlet characters we conclude that this expression is equal to
0
.

Case 2:
Let
0=v_{p}(c)<v_{p}(d)\leq v
, then by employing (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(1,1)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(1,1)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(1,p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(1,p)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(1,p)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p,p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}(p^{v_{p}(d)})\left(\frac{1}{p^{v_{p}(d)}}\right)^{k}-\overline{\psi}(p^{v_{p}(d)})\left(\frac{1}{p^{v_{p}(d)}}\right)^{k},

which equals to
0
.

Case 3:
Let
v>v_{p}(c)>v_{p}(d)\geq 0
, then by employing (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)+1})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)+1})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)+1},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle=-\epsilon(-p)\epsilon(-p^{v_{p}(c)-1-v_{p}(d)})+\frac{p^{k}+\epsilon(p)\overline{\psi}(p)}{p^{k}}\cdot\epsilon(-1)\cdot\epsilon(-p^{v_{p}(c)-v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\quad-\epsilon(-1)\overline{\psi}(p)\frac{1}{p^{k}}\cdot\epsilon(-p^{v_{p}(c)+1-v_{p}(d)}).

By using multiplicative properties of Dirichlet characters we conclude that this expression is equal to

.

Case 4:

, then by employing (

) and (

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(d)})

), (

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i\leq v}\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{i})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{i},p^{v_{p}(d)})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)-1})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)-1})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)-1},p^{v_{p}(c)})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{S}_{k,p^{v},\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)})\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(p^{v_{p}(c)},p^{v_{p}(c)})

5.
Constant terms of expansions of Eisenstein series at the cusps

Recall that
E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz)
is defined by (
1.3
) and we have

\displaystyle{E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz)}\in E_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\mbox{ when $(k,\epsilon,\psi)\neq(2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1})$},

and

\displaystyle L_{d}(z):=E_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};z)-dE_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};dz)\in E_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi_{1}).

The constant terms of Eisenstein series in the expansion at the cusp
a/c
with
\gcd(a,c)=1
are given by

(5.1)

\displaystyle[0]_{a/c}E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz)=\displaystyle\overline{\psi}(a)\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c,Md)\mbox{ when $(k,\epsilon,\psi)\neq(2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1})$ and}

(5.2)

\displaystyle[0]_{a/c}L_{d}(z)=\mathcal{R}_{2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1}}(c,1)-d\mathcal{R}_{2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1}}(c,d),

where
\mathcal{R}_{k,\epsilon,\psi}(c,t)
is defined by (
1.7
). For (
5.1
) see
[
3
, (6.2)]
,
[
5
, Proposition 8.5.6 and Ex. 8.7 (i) on pg. 308]
. The formula (
5.2
) is proved later in this section.

The structure of the terms
[0]_{a/c}E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz)
is complicated and difficult to work with. We observe that taking the average
[0]_{c,{\psi}}E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz)
gives constant terms a very nice structure which is easier to work with, see (
6.5
). Throughout the section we assume
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\epsilon
and
\psi
are primitive Dirichlet characters with conductors
L
and
M
, respectively, such that
LM\mid N
and
(k,\epsilon,\psi)\neq(2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1})
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
c\mid N
and
LMd\mid N
. If
M\nmid c
, or
M\mid c
and
L\nmid N/c
, then

\displaystyle[0]_{a/c}E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz)=0.

Proof.

First we let
M\nmid c
. Then
M\nmid\gcd(Md,c)
. Thus
\displaystyle\gcd\left(\frac{Md}{\gcd(Md,c)},M\right)\mid M
, which implies
\displaystyle\overline{\psi}\left(\frac{Md}{\gcd(Md,c)}\right)=0
since the conductor of
\psi
is
M
. Therefore the result follows from (
5.1
).

Second, we let
M\mid c
,
c_{1}=M/c
, thus
c_{1}\mid N/M
. Assume
L\nmid N/c
, thus
c_{1}\nmid N/LM
. Since
\frac{(N/M)}{L}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\frac{(N/M)}{c_{1}}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\frac{(N/M)/c_{1}}{L}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\gcd(c_{1},L)\neq 1
. Additionally, there exists a prime
p
dividing
c_{1}
such that

(5.3)

\displaystyle v_{p}(c_{1})>v_{p}(N)-v_{p}(M)-v_{p}(L).

Since
c_{1}\mid N/M
, for all
p\mid c_{1}
we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle v_{p}(c_{1})\leq v_{p}(N)-v_{p}(M).

By (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) we have

\displaystyle v_{p}(N)-v_{p}(M)>v_{p}(N)-v_{p}(M)-v_{p}(L).

Therefore

(5.5)

\displaystyle v_{p}(L)>0.

Since
d\mid N/LM
we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle v_{p}(N)-v_{p}(M)-v_{p}(L)\geq v_{p}(d)\geq 0.

Inequalities (
5.3
) and (
5.6
) together implies
v_{p}(c_{1})>v_{p}(d)
. Therefore by employing (
5.5
) we have

\displaystyle p\mid\gcd\left(\frac{p^{v_{p}(c_{1})}}{\gcd(p^{v_{p}(c_{1})},p^{v_{p}(d)})},p^{v_{p}(L)}\right).

That is,

\displaystyle p\mid\gcd\left(\frac{c_{1}}{\gcd(c_{1},d)},L\right).

This implies
\displaystyle\epsilon\left(\frac{c}{\gcd(Md,c)}\right)=0
since conductor of
\epsilon
is
L
. Therefore the result follows from (
5.1
).

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
be two primitive Dirichlet characters with conductors
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, respectively. Let both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
divide
c
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1,\\
\gcd(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}^{c}\overline{\psi_{1}}(a){\psi_{2}}(a)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if $\psi_{1}\neq\psi_{2}$,}\\
\displaystyle\phi(c)&\mbox{if $\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The modulus of the character
\overline{\psi_{1}}{\psi_{2}}
is
\lcm(M_{1},M_{2})
, see
[
13
, pg. 80]
. We let
A=\lcm(M_{1},M_{2})
, then
A\mid c
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1,\\
\gcd(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}^{c}\overline{\psi_{1}}(a){\psi_{2}}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{c}\overline{\psi_{1}}(a){\psi_{2}}(a)\sum_{s\mid\gcd(a,c)}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\mid c}\mu(s)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1,\\
s\mid a\end{subarray}}^{c}\overline{\psi_{1}}(a){\psi_{2}}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\mid c}\mu(s)\overline{\psi_{1}}(s){\psi_{2}}(s)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\end{subarray}}^{c/s}\overline{\psi_{1}}(t){\psi_{2}}(t).

We have
\overline{\psi_{1}}(s){\psi_{2}}(s)=0
whenever
\gcd(A,s)>1
, therefore we obtain

(5.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1,\\
\gcd(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}^{c}\overline{\psi_{1}}(a){\psi_{2}}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\mid c,\\
\gcd(A,s)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(s)\overline{\psi_{1}}(s){\psi_{2}}(s)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\end{subarray}}^{c/s}\overline{\psi_{1}}(t){\psi_{2}}(t).

Now if
A\mid c
,
s\mid c
and
\gcd(A,s)=1
, then
A\mid c/s
. First we consider
\psi_{1}\neq\psi_{2}
. Then

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\end{subarray}}^{c/s}\overline{\psi_{1}}(t){\psi_{2}}(t)=0.

Therefore if
\psi_{1}\neq\psi_{2}
by (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1,\\
\gcd(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}^{c}\overline{\psi_{1}}(a){\psi_{2}}(a)=0.

Second we consider the case when
\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}
. Noting that in this case
A=M_{2}
, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=1\end{subarray}}^{c/s}\overline{\psi_{1}}(t){\psi_{2}}(t)=\frac{c}{sM_{2}}\phi(M_{2}).

Therefore if
\psi_{1}=\psi_{2}
by by (
5.7
) and (
5.9
) we have

(5.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1,\\
\gcd(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}^{c}\overline{\psi_{1}}(a){\psi_{2}}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\mid c,\\
\gcd(M_{2},s)=1\end{subarray}}\mu(s)\frac{c}{sM_{2}}\phi(M_{2})=c\cdot\frac{\phi(M_{2})}{M_{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\mid c,\\
\gcd(M_{2},s)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(s)}{s}.

Noting that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(s)}{s}=\frac{\phi(c)}{c}=\prod_{p\mid c}\frac{p-1}{p},

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\mid c,\\
\gcd(M_{2},s)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(s)}{s}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid c,\\
\gcd(p,M_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{p-1}{p}=\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid c\end{subarray}}\frac{p-1}{p}}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid M_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{p-1}{p}}=\frac{\phi(c)/c}{\phi(M_{2})/M_{2}}.

Putting this in (
5.10
) completes the proof.

∎

Before we prove the main result of this section we prove (
5.2
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\gcd(a,c)=1
, then we have

\displaystyle[0]_{a/c}L_{d}(z)=\mathcal{R}_{2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1}}(c,1)-d\mathcal{R}_{2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1}}(c,d).

Proof.

Since
\gcd(a,c)=1
, there exist
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&\beta\\
c&\gamma\end{bmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then by
[
12
, (1.21)]
we have

(5.11)

\displaystyle E_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};A(z))=(cz+\gamma)^{2}E_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};z)-\frac{6ic}{\pi}(cz+\gamma),

where
A(z)
is the usual linear fractional transformation. Let
e=\frac{ad}{\gcd(c,ad)}
and
g=\frac{c}{\gcd(c,ad)}
. Then since
\gcd(e,g)=1
there exist
f,h
such that
\begin{bmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{bmatrix}\in SL_{2}(Z)
. Hence we have

\displaystyle E_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};dA(z);)

\displaystyle=E_{2}\left(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};\begin{bmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}ahd-cf&\beta hd-\gamma f\\
-agd+ce&-\beta gd+\gamma e\end{bmatrix}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=E_{2}\left(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};\begin{bmatrix}\frac{ad}{\gcd(c,ad)}&f\\
\frac{c}{\gcd(c,ad)}&h\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}ahd-cf&\beta hd-\gamma f\\
0&\frac{d}{\gcd(c,ad)}\end{bmatrix}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\gcd(c,d)}{d}\right)^{2}(cz+\gamma)^{2}E_{2}\left(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};\begin{bmatrix}ahd-cf&\beta hd-\gamma f\\
0&\frac{d}{\gcd(c,ad)}\end{bmatrix}(z)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{6ic}{\pi d}(cz+\gamma),

where in the last line we used (
5.11
). Thus we obtain

\displaystyle[0]_{a/c}L_{d}(z)

\displaystyle=[0]_{a/c}(E_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};z)-dE_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};dz))=\frac{d-\gcd(c,d)^{2}}{d}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1}}(c,1)-d\mathcal{R}_{2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1}}(c,d).

∎

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
c\mid N
and let

0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

If
(k,\epsilon,\psi)\neq(2,\chi_{1},\chi_{1})
by (

) we have

\displaystyle[0]_{c,{\psi_{2}}}E(\epsilon_{1},\psi_{1};dz)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi(c)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1,\\

Therefore by Lemma

6.
Proof of the Main Theorem

Recall that
E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz)
is defined by (
1.3
) and the set

(6.1)

\displaystyle\{E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz):(\epsilon,\psi)\in\mathcal{E}(k,N,\chi),d\mid N/LM\}

constitutes a basis for
E_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
whenever

and the set

\displaystyle\{E_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};z)-dE_{2}(\chi_{1},\chi_{1};dz):1<d\mid N/LM\}

\displaystyle\qquad\cup\{E_{2}(\epsilon,\psi;dz):(\epsilon,\psi)\in\mathcal{E}(2,N,\chi_{1}),(\epsilon,\psi)\neq(\chi_{1},\chi_{1}),d\mid N/LM\}

constitutes a basis for
E_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi_{1})
, see
[
5
, Theorems 8.5.17 and 8.5.22]
, or
[
22
, Proposition 5]
.

Now we prove the main theorem whenever
(k,\chi)\neq(2,\chi_{1})
. Let
f(z)\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
where
N,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 2
and
(k,\chi)\neq(2,\chi_{1})
. Then by (
6.1
) we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle E_{f}(z)=\sum_{(\epsilon,\psi)\in\mathcal{E}(k,N,\chi)}\sum_{d\mid N/LM}a_{f}(\epsilon,\psi,d)E_{k}(\epsilon,\psi;dz),

for some
a_{f}(\epsilon,\psi,d)\in\mathbb{C}
. Our strategy for the proof is, using the interplay between the constant terms of Eisenstein series, to create sets of linear equations (see (

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

and

and

. If

\displaystyle{\psi}(a)[0]_{a/c}f={\psi}(a^{\prime})[0]_{a^{\prime}/c}f.

Let
a/c
and

be equivalent cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, then there exists a matrix

\gamma&\delta\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\

Then using transformation properties of modular forms we have

\displaystyle\psi(a^{\prime})[0]_{a^{\prime}/c}f

\displaystyle=\psi(a^{\prime})\lim_{z\rightarrow i\infty}(cz+d^{\prime})^{-k}f\left(\frac{a^{\prime}z+b^{\prime}}{cz+d^{\prime}}\right)

Gopakumar-Vafa Invariants and Macdonald Formula
Lutian Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we present an investigation of the Gopakumar-Vafa (GV) invariant, a curve-counting integral invariant associated with Calabi-Yau threefolds, as proposed by physicists. Building upon the conjectural definition of the GV invariant in terms of perverse sheaves, as formulated by Maulik-Toda in 2016, we focus on the total space of the canonical bundle of ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} and compute the relevant invariants. We establish a conjectural correspondence between the Gopakumar-Vafa and Pandharipande-Thomas invariants at the level of perverse sheaves, drawing inspiration from the work of Migliorini, Shende, and Viviani. This work serves as a significant step towards validating the conjecture and deepening our understanding of the GV invariant and its connections to algebraic geometry and physics.

Introduction

1.1.
Gopakumar-Vafa and Pandharipande-Thomas Invariants

Algebraic geometers have long been interested in curve counting theory, which involves calculating various curve counting invariants associated with a given Calabi-Yau threefold
X
and an element
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. There are three primary theories used for this purpose: Gromov-Witten (GW) theory, Donaldson-Thomas (DT) theory, and Pandharipande-Thomas (PT) theory, each with its own set of definitions and comparisons. While the review by Pandharipande and Thomas
[
28
]
provides an overview of these theories, the work by Maulik and Toda
[
20
]
defines the Gopakumar-Vafa (GV) theory, which is believed to represent the same numerical invariants as the other three theories, albeit under certain assumptions.

This article focuses on the equivalence between the GV and PT theories. We explore the generating series of these invariants and examine how they can be represented as motivic invariants within the K-group of motives or as cohomological invariants within the K-group of perverse sheaves. We provide several computational examples and theoretical analyses to support our findings. Ultimately, our results shed new light on the relationship between the GV and PT theories and have important implications for the broader field of enumerative geometry.

In order to better understand the differences between the theories, we consider the simplest case of
n
smooth isolated curves of genus
g
. In this case, the GV moduli space
\mathrm{Sh}
corresponds to
n
copies of complex tori of dimension
g
, while the PT moduli space
\mathbb{C}^{[k]}
corresponds to
n
copies of the Hilbert scheme of
k
points. We can determine the value of
n
using either the Poincaré polynomial for
\mathrm{Sh}
, given by
P(\mathrm{Sh},tx)=n(1+tx)^{2g}
, or the Poincaré polynomial for
\mathbb{C}^{[k]}
, given by
\sum_{k}P(\mathbb{C}^{[k]},x)t^{k}=n\frac{(1+tx)^{2g}}{(1-t)(1-tx^{2})}
, which is essentially the MacDonald formula
[
18
, (4.3)]
.

This will give a naive GV-PT correspondence: Let
Z_{PT}=\sum_{k}P(\mathbb{C}^{[k]},x)t^{k}
,
Z_{GV}=P(\mathrm{Sh},tx)
, then the following formula holds:

Z_{PT}=\frac{Z_{GV}}{(1-t)(1-tx^{2})}.

In a more general setting, we may find evidence for the correspondence between Gopakumar-Vafa invariants and Pandharipande-Thomas invariants. Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau threefold and
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
be a curve class. Consider the moduli space of Gieseker-stable sheaves with one-dimensional support
\beta
and Euler characteristic
1
, denoted by
\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)
. We define the Hilbert-Chow morphism
\pi^{\beta}:\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)\to\mathrm{Chow}_{\beta}(X)
by sending a sheaf to its Fitting support.

Assuming that the moduli space
\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)
is smooth, we can define the Gopakumar-Vafa (GV) invariant as follows. For a given
\beta
, let
n_{\beta}^{g}
be integers such that the following equality holds:

\sum_{g\geq 0}n_{\beta}^{g}(q^{1/2}+q^{-1/2})^{2g}=\sum_{i}\chi(\mathrm{Chow}_{\beta}(X),\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{i}\pi^{\beta}_{*}\mathcal{IC}_{\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)})q^{i},

where
\chi
is the Euler characteristic,
\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{i}
is the perverse cohomology for the pushforward, and
\mathcal{IC}_{\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)}
is the intersection cohomology complex on
\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)
.

If the moduli space
\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)
is not smooth, the definition of the GV invariant remains an open question as is stated by Maulik-Toda
[
20
]
. In this case, the
\mathcal{IC}
sheaf should be replaced by a more general perverse sheaf, which requires gluing vanishing cycles depending on a choice of orientation data for moduli of sheaves. The work of
[
20
]
showed that if the moduli space admits a structure called a Calabi-Yau orientation, then we have a well-defined GV invariant.

In order to present a more general definition of the GV invariant, we can refer to the work by Toda in
[
33
]
. Toda replaces Gieseker stability with Bridgeland stability and defines the perverse sheaf to be pushforwarded from the moduli stack of sheaves to the coarse moduli space. However, for the purposes of this paper, we will only be considering the case of smooth moduli spaces of Gieseker-stable sheaves. Interested readers can find more information on singular moduli spaces in the references by Maulik and Toda
[
20
,
33
]
.

To state the general conjecture, we need to first define stable pair invariants, which were introduced by Pandharipande and Thomas in
[
29
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

For a Calabi-Yau threefold
X
and a class
\beta\in X
, we define a
stable pair
with class
\beta
to be a pair
(F,s)
where
F
is a
1
-dimensional pure sheaf with
[F]=\beta
and
s:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to F
is a section of
F
with
0
-dimensional cokernel.

Let
P_{n}(X,\beta)
denote the moduli space of stable pairs with class
\beta
and with Euler characteristic
\chi(F)=n
. Then
P_{n}(X,\beta)
admits a symmetric obstruction theory
[
29
, section 2.3]
, which defines a virtual fundamental class
[P_{n}(X,\beta)]^{vir}\in A_{0}(P_{n}(X,\beta),\mathbb{Z})
. We define a stable pair invariant as the degree of the virtual cycle:

\displaystyle P_{n,\beta}:=\int_{[P_{n}(X,\beta)]^{vir}}1.

With this definition, we can introduce the following conjecture, which relates stable pair invariants to the Gopakumar-Vafa invariants introduced earlier.

Conjecture 1.2

(The Gopakumar-Vafa/Pandharipande-Thomas Correspondence)
.

We define the generating function for stable pair invariants to be

\displaystyle Z_{PT}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})}P_{n,\beta}q^{n}Q^{\beta}.

Then, following an argument in physics from
[
15
]
, one may introduce the following conjecture, first formulated by Katz in
[
13
]
:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Z_{PT}=\prod_{\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})}\Biggl(&\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+(-1)^{j+1}q^{j}Q^{\beta})^{jn_{\beta}^{0}}\cdot\prod_{g=1}^{\infty}\prod_{k=0}^{2g-2}\left(1+(-1)^{g-k}q^{g-1-k}Q^{\beta}\right)^{(-1)^{k+g}n^{g}_{\beta}\binom{2g-2}{k}}\Biggr),\end{split}

where
n_{\beta}^{g}
are the Gopakumar-Vafa invariants for the class
\beta

1.2.
Refined Invariant

We promote the GV invariant to a
refined
theory, that is, replacing the Euler characteristic by the Poincaré polynomial. We must suppose the following to get a valid refinement: we have an identification:

\mathbb{H}^{j}(\mathrm{Chow}_{\beta}(X),\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{i}\pi_{*}^{\beta}\mathcal{IC}_{\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)})\cong\mathbb{H}^{-j}(\mathrm{Chow}_{\beta}(X),\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{i}\pi_{*}^{\beta}\mathcal{IC}_{\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)})
. This is always true when
\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}
is projective, according to the Hard Lefschetz theorem. Then this allows us to define the refined GV invariant:

Definition 1.4

(Refined Gopakumar-Vafa Numbers)
.

Given a
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, the
refined Gopakumar-Vafa (GV) numbers
are defined to be integers
N_{j_{L},j_{R}}
with
j_{L},j_{R}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
such that the following equality holds:

\sum_{j_{L},j_{R}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}}N_{j_{L},j_{R}}\frac{r^{2j_{L}+1}-r^{-2j_{L}-1}}{r-r^{-1}}\cdot\frac{q^{2j_{R}+1}-q^{-2j_{R}-1}}{q-q^{-1}}=\sum_{i}P(\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{i}\pi^{\beta}_{*}\mathcal{IC}_{\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)},r)q^{i}.

Here
P(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet},r)
is the Poincaré polynomial associated to the constructible complex
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
with variable
r
,
\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{i}\pi_{*}^{\beta}
is the perverse cohomology for pushforward, and
\mathcal{IC}_{\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)}
is the intersection cohomology complex on
\mathrm{Sh}_{\beta}(X)
.

To define the corresponding PT part, we need to look at a conjectural definition of refined stable pair invariant. Recall that the stable pair invariant is defined by a symmetric obstruction theory. In order to categorify that, we need to introduce a special structure called
d-critical loci
on our moduli space of stable pairs. Before giving the exact definition, we first recall some results of Joyce
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
11
, theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a scheme. Then there exists a canonical
sheaf
\mathcal{S}_{X}
of vector spaces on
X
, such that if
R\subset X
is Zariski
open and
i:R\hookrightarrow U
is a closed embedding of
R
into a smooth
scheme
U
, and
I_{R,U}\subset\mathcal{O}_{U}
is the ideal vanishing on
i(R)
, then

\mathcal{S}_{X}|_{R}=\ker\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{U}}{I_{R,U}^{2}}\overset{d_{DR}}{\to}\frac{\Omega_{U}}{I_{R,U}\cdot\Omega_{U}}\right)

Also
\mathcal{S}_{X}
splits naturally as
\mathcal{S}_{X}=\mathcal{S}_{X}^{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}_{X}
, where
\mathbb{C}_{X}
is the sheaf of locally constant functions.

Then we may define the d-critical loci as follows:

Definition 1.6
.

An (algebraic) d-critical locus
(X,s)
is a classical scheme
X
and
a global section
s\in H^{0}(\mathcal{S}_{X}^{0})
such that
X
may be covered by
Zariski open
R\subset X
with an isomorphism

for some regular

on a smooth scheme

such that

.

We call the pair
(R,U,f)
a
critical chart
.

Then there exists a canonical bundle on
(X,s)
characterized by the following result:

Theorem 1.7
.

[
11
, Proposition 2.25]

Let
(X,s)
be the d-critical locus and let

the reduced subscheme of

. There exists a line bundle
K_{X,s}
on
X
called the canonical bundle of

, such that if locally
R=Z(df)
on
U
, then there is a canonical isomorphism
K_{X,s}|_{R^{red}}\cong K_{U}^{\otimes 2}|_{R^{red}}
for
K_{U}
the usual canonical bundle of

.

Then we may define the associated orientation data:

Definition 1.8
.

Let
(X,s)
be the d-critical locus, a choice of
orientation data
is a choice of a square root bundle
K_{X,s}^{1/2}
of
K_{X,s}
on
X^{red}
.

When we have an orientation data on a moduli space of sheaves, we get the categorification of the invariant from the perfect obstruction by the gluing of vanishing cycles.

Proposition 1.9
.

[
4
, Theorem 6.9]

Let
(X,s)
be a d-critical locus, and suppose we have an orientation data
K_{X,s}^{1/2}
. Then there exists a perverse sheaf
P_{X,s}^{\bullet}
such that if locally

, then
P_{X,s}^{\bullet}|_{R}\cong\phi_{f}|_{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}Q_{R,U,f,i}
where
\phi_{f}
is the perverse sheaf of vanishing cycles associated to

and
\pi_{R,U,f,i}:Q_{R,U,f,i}\to R
is the principal

-bundle parametrizing local isomorphisms
\alpha:K_{X,s}^{1/2}\cong i^{*}(K_{U})|_{R_{red}}
.

Now we may define the perverse sheaf we are interested in on the moduli of PT stable pairs.

Conjecture 1.10
.

Let
P_{n}(X,\beta)
be the moduli space of Pandharipande-Thomas stable pairs. Then there exists a canonical oriented d-critical locus structure on
P_{n}(X,\beta)
which induces a perverse sheaf
\phi_{n,\beta}
. We denote
\Phi_{n,\beta}=\phi_{n,\beta}[-\dim(P_{n}(X,\beta))]
, and such that
\Phi_{n,\beta}=\mathbb{Q}
when

is smooth.

Remark 1.11
.

A recent paper by Joyce and Upmeier
[
12
]
proved the existence of a canonical orientation structure on the moduli space of sheaves on a Calabi-Yau threefold. This provides a hint for the existence for such orientation data.

Assuming this conjecture, then we may define our refined stable pair invariant in the same way. By sending a sheaf to its support one can also define the projection
\pi^{n,\beta}:P_{n}(X,\beta)\to\mathrm{Chow}_{\beta}
.

We may now refine the virtual class
[P_{n,\beta}]^{vir}
to a polynomial by the following:

Definition 1.12
.

Let
\phi_{n,\beta}
be the perverse sheaves associated to
P_{n}(X,\beta)
. We define the
refined stable pair
polynomial to be

\mathbb{P}_{n,\beta}(r,t)=\sum_{k,l}h^{l}(\mathrm{Chow}_{\beta},\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{k}\pi_{*}^{n,\beta}\phi_{n,\beta})r^{k}t^{l}.

Here
h^{l}(-,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})=\dim\mathbb{H}^{l}(-,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
. Then the
refined virtual class
is defined to be

[P_{n}(X,\beta)]_{vir}=\mathbb{P}_{n,\beta}(\mathbb{L}^{-1/2},\mathbb{L}^{-1/2})

for some formal variable
\mathbb{L}^{-1/2}
.

Then we have the refined GV-PT correspondence, following the paper of Choi, Katz and Klemm
[
5
]
.

Conjecture 1.13

(The refined Gopakumar-Vafa/Pandharipande-Thomas Correspondence)
.

Let
Z_{PT}^{r}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})}[P_{n}(X,\beta)]_{\mathrm{vir}}q^{n}Q^{\beta}
be a generating series of motivic stable pair. Then

(1.14)

\displaystyle Z_{PT}^{r}=\prod_{j_{L},j_{R}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{m_{L/R}=-j_{L/R}}^{j_{L/R}}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(1-(-q)^{m-2m_{L}}\mathbb{L}^{-m/2+1/2+j-m_{R}}\right)^{(-1)^{2j_{L}+2j_{R}}N_{j_{L},j_{R}}^{\beta}}.

Here
\prod_{m_{L/R}=-j_{L/R}}^{j_{L/R}}
means
\prod_{m_{L}=-j_{L}}^{j_{L}}\prod_{m_{R}=-j_{R}}^{j_{R}}
.

Remark 1.15
.

Here the class
\mathbb{L}=[\mathbb{A}^{1}]
is chosen to match the notation in
[
5
]
for the motivic PT theory. Since the motivic invariant is defined only if we have an orientation data, and there is no general description for this orientation, we use our refined numbers instead. Examples of the definition of motivic stable pair invariants can be found in the K3 case
[
14
]
. where an orientation can be chosen canonically.

Main Results

2.
Perverse Sheaves and their Symmetric Products

We have Grothendieck’s six functors
f_{*},f_{!},f^{*},f^{!},\mathrm{Hom}
and
{\otimes}
on the category of constructible sheaves. We can derive this category and give analogously six functors:
direct image

\mathbf{R}f_{*}
,
direct image with compact support

\mathbf{R}f_{!}
,
pullback

f^{*}
,
exceptional inverse image

f^{!}
,
derived homomorphism

\mathbf{R}\mathrm{Hom}
and
left tensor product

\overset{L}{\otimes}
on
D(X)
. For simplicity we denote
\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{H}}^{i}(\mathbf{R}f_{*})
and
\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{H}}^{i}(\mathbf{R}f_{!})

\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{i}f_{*}

\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{R}}^{i}f_{!}

[

The necessary properties of these functors will be reviewed in later sections. Since we only consider the left tensor product in later sections, we will abbreviate
\overset{L}{\otimes}

Definition 2.1
.

The shifting operator
[r]
is a morphism
D^{b}_{c}(X)\to D^{b}_{c}(X)
by setting
[r]:\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}[r]
. Note that this shifting operator acts on the right.

We may now study the intersection cohomology complexes, which are the simple objects in the derived category of perverse sheaves according to
[
1
, Proposition 4.3.1]
. Here by
simple
we mean there is no nontrivial sub-object of intersection cohomology complex. Given a
\mathbb{C}

Let

Let

Lemma 3.28

The Euler characteristic for symmetric and alternating products satisfies the following generating series:

(3.29)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\chi\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(X),\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\right)q^{n}=(1-q)^{-\chi(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\chi\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(X),\mathrm{Alt}^{n}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\right)q^{n}=(1+q)^{\chi(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}.

Proof.

We only prove this for the alternating product since the proof for the symmetric product is completely analogous. By Theorem

we know that it only depends on the symmetric and alternating product of the even and odd parts. Since the sign only cares about the odd part, we let

so that

\displaystyle\chi(\mathrm{Alt}^{n}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}))=\sum_{i+j=n}(-1)^{j}\binom{d_{0}}{i}\binom{d_{1}+j-1}{j}=\binom{\chi(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}{n}.

The last equality is true since
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\binom{d_{1}+j-1}{j}x^{j}=(1+q)^{-d_{1}}
and

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{d_{0}}{i}q^{i}=(1+q)^{d_{0}}

(1+q)^{d_{0}-d_{1}}=(1+q)^{\chi(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}

Thus the summation by binomial identity is

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\chi\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(X),\mathrm{Alt}^{n}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\right)q^{n}=(1+q)^{\chi(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})}.

as the dimension of the symmetric product of constructible sheaves only depends on the dimension of its cohomology group. Thus

\displaystyle S_{r}\left(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\right)=S_{r}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}[-i]^{h^{i}}\right)=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}S_{r}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}[-i])^{h^{i}},

which proves the lemma.
∎

We define the

Lemma 4.11
.

For an element

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{G}_{2},p,r)=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_{1},p,r)\cdot\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{G}_{2},p,r)

Proof.

We first observe that
P(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2},r)=P(\mathcal{F}_{1})\cdot P(\mathcal{F}_{2})
with
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}F_{1,k}q^{k}\in D(X_{m})[\![q]\!]

\mathcal{F}_{2}=\sum_{k\geq 0}F_{2,l}q^{l}\in D(X_{n})(X)[\![q]\!]

\displaystyle P(\mathcal{F}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{2},r)=\sum_{k,l}P(i_{[m,n]*}(F_{1,k}\boxtimes F_{2,l}),r)q^{k+l}

5.
Perverse Sheaves of Geometric Origin

We review a series of mixed Hodge module, talking about the famous decomposition theorem, following Saito’s work
[
31
, Theorem 4.5.4]

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a proper morphism and let
\mathcal{M}\in MHM(Y)
, then

f_{*}\mathcal{M}\cong\bigoplus_{i}H^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{M}

If
\mathcal{M}
is pure, then
f_{*}\mathcal{M}
is pure of the same weight. Here
H^{j}f_{*}
is a cohomological pushforward defined by
[
31
, (2.13)]
. It can be thought of as the cohomological functor defined by t-structure of
[
31
, (4.6.2)]
.

So that we have the definition of geometric origin.

Definition 5.2
.

We call
P\in\mathrm{MHM}(X)

of geometric origin
if it is a direct summand of
f_{*}\mathcal{IC}_{Y}(\mathbb{Q})
for some proper morphism
f:Y\to X
.

The following theorem is a restatement of
[
1
, Corollaire 5.3.11]
in the category of mixed Hodge modules. We will see that in the setting of mixed Hodge module this theorem follows directly from the strict support theorem.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
P
be a mixed Hodge module of geometric origin on
X
and let
j:U\to X
be an open dense subset and
i:Z\to X
be its complement. Then the perverse sheaf has a natural decomposition,

P=j_{!*}j^{*}P\oplus i_{*}P_{0},

where
P_{0}
is a mixed Hodge module of geometric origin supported on
Z
.

Proof.

If
P
is a mixed Hodge module of geometric origin, it will be a summand of the pushforward of
IC_{Y}(\mathbb{Q})
, which is a pure complex by Theorem
5.1
. Thus
f_{*}\mathcal{M}
is pure, and by the strict support theorem
P
is a direct sum of
IC
sheaves.

When
P
is pure, we know that it is a direct sum of simple Hodge modules. In particular,
P=\oplus_{W\in\mathcal{P}}P_{W}
for some Whitney partition of
X
. If
W\cap U\not=\varnothing
then we claim that
j_{!*}j^{*}P_{W}=P_{W}
. This is because
j^{*}P_{W}
is simple, and
P_{W}
is an extension of
j^{*}P_{W}
that is simple. With the same idea as in Proposition
2.5
in the derived category of mixed Hodge module, we know that
j_{!*}j^{*}P_{W}=P_{W}
since such kind of extension is unique.

Thus we have the equality

(5.4)

\displaystyle P=j_{!*}j^{*}\left(\bigoplus_{W\cap U\not=\varnothing}P_{W}\right)\oplus i_{*}\left(\bigoplus_{W\cap U=\varnothing}P_{W}\right).

∎

Since all of our perverse sheaves in future sections are of geometric origin. So we can upgrade all of them to a mixed Hodge module of geometric origin and use this lemma for free.

Appendix A
Ngô’s Support Theorem

In this section, we review the theorem by Ngô that gives us an upper bound for the support of the perverse sheaves. We want to review the support theorem directly in complex numbers. But due to a lack of definition of the polarizability of Tate module in the complex case, we only have some partial results. When the author was working on his thesis, a paper by Maulik and Shen
[
21
]
appeared considering similar problem where interested readers can find a clearer presentation of the inequality in
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
.

First of all, we may recall the classical Goresky-Macpherson inequality.

Theorem A.1

(Goresky-MacPherson Inequality)
.

Let
L
be a local system defined on an open subset of
X
. Let
f:X\to Y
be proper with
X
of relative dimension
d
. Assuming that a subvariety
S
appears in the decomposition theorem for
\mathbf{R}f_{*}\mathcal{IC}_{X}(L)
, then
\mathrm{codim}(S)\leq d
.

Proof.

Let
V
be the dense open subset of
X
where
L
is defined, and let
f(V)
be the image of
V
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
be the maximal possible number such that there exists the term
\mathcal{IC}_{Z}(M)[-k]
appearing in the decomposition for some local system
M
. We may choose
k\geq 0
by the hard Lefschetz Theorem
. Since
M
is defined on some open subset
Z^{o}\subset Z
, we can choose
U\subset Y
to be an open subset such that
U\cap Z=Z^{o}
. Let
i:Z\hookrightarrow Y
be the closed embedding, thus
i_{*}M[\dim Z][-k]
is a direct summand of
\mathbf{R}f_{*}\mathcal{IC}_{X}(L)|_{U}
. Then we know that
i_{*}M
is a non-trivial summand of
(\mathbf{R}^{\dim X-\dim Z+k}f_{*}L)|_{U\cap f(V)}
. Recall that each fiber has dimension
d
and
k\geq 0
. Also by the vanishing theorem we know that we can only have nontrivial
\mathbf{R}^{i}f_{*}L
if
i\leq 2d
. So in summary we have the following inequality

\dim X-\dim Z\leq\dim X-\dim Z+k\leq 2d

and thus
\mathrm{codim}Z=\dim Y-\dim Z=\dim X-\dim Z-d\leq d
.
∎

Ngô’s theorem is a vast generalization of this idea to the case of Abelian fibration.

The difference between our attempt and Ngô’s original theorem
[
27
, Proposition 7.2.3]
is that we are considering a direct proof for characteristic

without resorting to the spreading out techniques.

Let
Y
be a scheme with
f:X\to Y
a smooth proper morphism and
g:P\to Y
a smooth commutative group scheme and an action

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{act}:P\times_{Y}X\to X

We set the following

Definition A.3
.

We say this pair
(f,g)
is an

if

(1)

The morphisms
f
and
g
have the same relative dimension
d
.

(2)

The action of
P
on
X
has affine stablizers, i.e. for any point
x\in X
over
y\in Y
, the stabilizer of
x
by action of
P_{y}=g^{-1}(y)
is an affine subgroup of
P_{y}
.

For each point
y\in Y
we can describe the Chevalley devissage. Let
g^{0}:P^{0}\to Y
be the component of
P
containing the identity, then there is a decomposition of
P^{0}_{y}=(g^{0})^{-1}(y)
into the abelian part and affine algebraic part by

(A.4)

\displaystyle 1\to R_{y}\to P^{0}_{y}\to A_{y}\to 1

where
A_{y}
is an Abelian variety and
R_{y}
is a commutative affine algebraic group. We can define the function
\delta:Y\to\mathbb{Z}
to be
\delta(y)=\dim R_{y}
. For every

, irreducible closed subset of

, we define
\delta_{Z}
to be

(A.5)

\displaystyle\delta_{Z}=\min_{z\in Z}(\delta(z)).

Now we will always consider the connected part of this
P
and by an abuse of notation, our

will always be
g^{0}
.
On the other hand, by the decomposition theorem,
\mathbf{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}[\dim M]
is
semisimple. Let
Z
be an irreducible closed subset of
S
and we consider
\mathrm{occ}(Z)
to be the integers

such that there exists an irreducible direct factor

of
\prescript{p}{}{\mathbf{H}}^{n}(\mathbf{R}f_{*}\mathbb{C}[\dim M])
that is supported on

. We can define another function called the amplitude to be

(A.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{amp}(Z)=\max(\mathrm{occ}(Z))-\min(\mathrm{occ}(Z)).

The following is the celebrated Ngô inequality that gives a bound on the possible amplitude for the perverse sheaves that may appear in the decomposition.

Theorem A.7

(Ngô’s Support Inequality)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
and
g:P\to Y
be the abelian fibration. Then for any

that is the support of
\mathbf{R}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}[\dim X]
we have the inequality

(A.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{amp}(Z)\geq 2(d-\delta_{Z})

Since
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{amp}(Z)\leq d-\mathrm{codim}(Z)
, this implies

(A.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{codim}(Z)\leq\delta_{Z}.

In order to prove the amplitude inequality, we need to use the action
\mathrm{act}:P\times_{Y}X\to X
. We consider the complex of compactly supported cohomology of
P
over
Y
by the formula

(A.10)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{P}:=g_{!}\mathbb{Q}[2d](d),

and we need to find a map
\Lambda_{P}\otimes f_{!}\mathbb{Q}\to f_{!}\mathbb{Q}

The adjunction morphism gives us

(A.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{act}_{!}\mathrm{act}^{!}\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}

We get
\mathrm{act}^{!}\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}[2d](d)
as a mixed Hodge module. By pushing forward via
f_{!}
we obtain a morphism

(A.12)

\displaystyle(g\times_{Y}f)_{!}\mathbb{Q}[2d](d)\to f_{!}\mathbb{Q}

Using the Künneth isomorphsm we get the map

(A.13)

\displaystyle g_{!}\mathbb{Q}[2d](d)\otimes f_{!}\mathbb{Q}\to f_{!}\mathbb{Q}

which is exactly

(A.14)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{P}\otimes f_{!}\mathbb{Q}\to f_{!}\mathbb{Q}

Now we look at the action pointwise, we see this is an action of

on

. As a vector space with Hodge structure,

. Therefore we have actually proven the following lemma.

Lemma A.15

There is an action of

To obtain the Hodge structure of

Milnor-Witt K-theory and Witt K-theory of a field
Robin Carlier

Abstract
We recall some basic computations in the Milnor-Witt K-theory of a field, following Morel. We then focus on the Witt K-theory of a field of characteristic two and give an elementary proof of the fact that it is isomorphic as a graded ring to the Rees algebra of the fundamental ideal of the Witt ring of symmetric bilinear forms using Kato’s solution to Milnor’s conjecture on quadratic form.

Basics of Milnor-Witt K-theory of fields, after Morel

Definition 2.1.

Let
F
be a field. The
Milnor-Witt
K
-theory of
F
, denoted by
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)
, is the associative graded ring presented by generators and relations in the following way:

•

A set of generator is given by the set of symbols
\left\{[u],\,u\in F^{\times}\right\}\cup\{\eta\}
.
The symbol
\eta
is of degree
-1
, and for
u\in F^{\times}
the symbol
[u]
is of degree
1
.

•

The generators are subject to the following set of relations:

\mathbf{KMW1}

For all
a\in F^{\times}
,
a\neq 1
,
[a][1-a]=0
.

\mathbf{KMW2}

For all
a,b\in F^{\times}
,
[ab]=[a]+[b]+\eta\cdot[a][b]
.

\mathbf{KMW3}

For all
u\in F^{\times}
,
\eta[u]=[u]\eta
.

\mathbf{KMW4}

Let
h\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\eta[-1]+2
. Then
\eta h=0
.

Relation

KMW1

is usually called the
Steinberg relation
.
It is immediate from the definition that the quotient
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)/\eta
is isomorphic to the Milnor
K
-theory
\mathrm{K}^{M}_{*}(F)
of the field
F
.
One defines the Witt
K
-theory of
F
, denoted by
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
, as the quotient
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)/h
.
It follows immediately from relation

KMW3

that
\eta
is a central element in
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)
, so that one gets the following easy presentation of the
n
-the graded piece of
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)
.

Lemma 2.2.

Let
F
be a field and let
n
be an integer. Let
\widetilde{\mathrm{K}}^{MW}_{n}(F)
be the abelian group presented by generators and relations in the following way:

•

A set of generators is the set of symbols
\left[\eta^{m},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\right]
where
r
is an integer,
m=r-n\geq 0
, and
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}
are elements of
F^{\times}
.

•

These generators satisfy the following set of relations:

–

\left[\eta^{m},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}\right]=0
if there exists
i
such that
1\leq i<r
and
u_{i}+u_{i+1}=0
.

–

For all integers
r
, for all pairs
a,b\in F^{\times}
and for all
1<i<r
,

\displaystyle\left[\eta^{m},\ldots,u_{i-1},ab,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{r}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\eta^{m},\ldots,u_{i-1},a,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{r}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[\eta^{m},\ldots,u_{i-1},b,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{r}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left[\eta^{m+1},\ldots,u_{i-1},a,b,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{r}\right].

–

For all integers
i
,

\displaystyle\left[\eta^{m},u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},-1,u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{r}\right]+2\left[\eta^{m-1},u_{1},\ldots,u_{i-1},u_{i+1},\ldots,u_{r}\right]=0.

The canonical morphism
\widetilde{\mathrm{K}}^{MW}_{n}(F)\to\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{n}(F)
that sends
[\eta^{m},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r}]
to
\eta^{m}[u_{1}]\cdots[u_{r}]
is an isomorphism.

For an element
a\in F^{\times}
, we set
\langle a\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}1+\eta[a]\in\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{0}(F)
.
We now give a few relations that hold in the ring
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)
. The proofs are extracted from
[
Mor12
]
, chapter 3.1 and are reproduced here for convenience.

Lemma 2.3.

Let
a,b\in F^{\times}
.

(a)

[ab]=[a]+\langle a\rangle[b]
and
[ab]=[a]\langle b\rangle+[b]
.

(b)

\langle ab\rangle=\langle a\rangle.\langle b\rangle
and
\langle a\rangle
is central in
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)
.

(c)

\langle 1_{F}\rangle=1_{\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)}
and
[1]=0
.

(d)

\langle a\rangle
is a unit of
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)
whose inverse is
\langle a^{-1}\rangle
.

(e)

\left[\frac{a}{b}\right]=[a]-\left\langle\frac{a}{b}\right\rangle[b]
. In particular
\left[a^{-1}\right]=-\left\langle a^{-1}\right\rangle[a]
.

Proof.

One has

\displaystyle[ab]

\displaystyle=[a]+[b]+\eta[a][b]

\displaystyle=[a]+(1+\eta[a])[b]

\displaystyle=[a]+\langle a\rangle[b],

with the first equality being relation

KMW2

, and the last being the definition of
\langle a\rangle
.
Since
[ab]=[ba]
, one also gets
[ab]=[a]+[b]\langle a\rangle
and
[ab]=[b]+\langle b\rangle[a]
, which proves the first item.

For the proof of
(b)
, using again relation

KMW2

, we have

\displaystyle\langle ab\rangle

\displaystyle=1+\eta[ab]

\displaystyle=1+\eta\left([a]+[b]+\eta[a][b]\right)

\displaystyle=1+\eta[a]+\eta[b]+\eta^{2}[a][b]

\displaystyle=(1+\eta[a])(1+\eta[b])

\displaystyle=\langle a\rangle\langle b\rangle.

Using property
(a)
, this gives
\langle a\rangle[b]=[b]\langle a\rangle
for all
b
, thus
\langle a\rangle
is central as claimed.

For the third item, notice that
h=\langle-1\rangle+1
and that
\eta[1]=\langle 1\rangle-1
. Thus, relation

implies

(\langle 1\rangle-1)(\langle-1\rangle+1)=0,

and since

(\langle 1\rangle-1)(\langle-1\rangle+1)=\langle 1\rangle\langle-1\rangle+\langle 1\rangle-\langle-1\rangle-1,

we have
\langle 1\rangle\langle-1\rangle=\langle-1\rangle
by
(b)
, so that

(\langle 1\rangle-1)(\langle-1\rangle+1)=\langle 1\rangle-1.

Thus the fact that the right-hand side is zero implies the desired relation. Now by part
(a)
,

\displaystyle[1]

\displaystyle=[1]+\langle 1\rangle[1]

\displaystyle=[1]+[1]

so that
[1]=0
as desired.

For the fourth point, by part
(c)
and
(b)
we get

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\langle 1\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle aa^{-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle a\rangle\langle a^{-1}\rangle,

and similarly
\langle a^{-1}\rangle\langle a\rangle=1
, which is
(d)
.

Finally, one has

\displaystyle[a]

\displaystyle=\left[ab^{-1}b\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{a}{b}\right]+\left\langle\frac{a}{b}\right\rangle[b]

which proves the equality
\left[\frac{a}{b}\right]=[a]-\left\langle\frac{a}{b}\right\rangle[b]
. Setting
a=1
in this equality and using that
[1]=0
, we get the expected particular case.
∎

Lemma 2.4.

(1)

For
n\geq 1
, the abelian group
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{n}(F)
is generated by elements of the form
[u_{1}]\cdots[u_{n}]
.

(2)

For

, the abelian group
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{n}(F)
is generated by elements of the form

is again generated by elements of the form

. Notice first that

\displaystyle\eta^{m}[u_{1}]\cdots[u_{r}]=\eta^{n}\eta^{r}[u_{1}]\cdots[u_{r}].

By part

of the previous lemma,

\displaystyle\eta[a]

\displaystyle=\langle a\rangle-1

\displaystyle=\langle a\rangle-\langle 1\rangle

.

3
Witt K-theory in characteristic 2

In this section, we bring a particular attention to Witt K-theory in characteristic two. Recall that
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
is defined to be the quotient
\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)/(h)
.
Recall also that we set

Given an element

in
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)

Although the signs are important when working in a general setting to have well-defined morphisms, we will mostly ignore them when working in a characteristic

Our goal is to prove the following:

Theorem 3.1.

be a field of characteristic

, there is a unique isomorphism

The main interest of this result is that through the explicit generators and relations of

One of the main input is Milnor’s conjecture that

]
as done in
[

]

In the characteristic 2 case, the input is again that

.

For the first point, one has

\displaystyle\left\lBrack a^{2}b\right\rBrack=\left\lBrack a^{2}\right\rBrack+\lBrack b\rBrack+\eta\left\lBrack a^{2}\right\rBrack\lBrack b\rBrack

and the claim exactly follows from Lemma
3.4
.

For the last point, first, setting
\alpha=\frac{a}{a+b}
, one sees that
1-\alpha=\frac{b}{a+b}
, so that
\alpha(1-\alpha)=\frac{ab}{{(a+b)}^{2}}
, which differs multiplicatively from
ab
by a square. Hence by the first point,

\lBrack\alpha(1-\alpha)\rBrack=\lBrack ab\rBrack.

On the other hand

\lBrack\alpha(1-\alpha)\rBrack=\lBrack\alpha\rBrack+\lBrack 1-\alpha\rBrack+\eta\lBrack\alpha\rBrack\lBrack 1-\alpha\rBrack.

The last summand of the right hand side is zero by the Steinberg relation, so that

\lBrack ab\rBrack=\left\lBrack\frac{a}{a+b}\right\rBrack+\left\lBrack\frac{b}{a+b}\right\rBrack.

Since one has

\left\lBrack\frac{a}{a+b}\right\rBrack=\lBrack a\rBrack-\left\langle\frac{a}{a+b}\right\rangle\lBrack a+b\rBrack

and

\left\lBrack\frac{b}{a+b}\right\rBrack=\lBrack b\rBrack-\left\langle\frac{b}{a+b}\right\rangle\lBrack a+b\rBrack

by
(e)
of Lemma
2.3
, the above expression of
\lBrack ab\rBrack
yields the equality

\lBrack ab\rBrack=\lBrack a\rBrack+\lBrack b\rBrack-(\langle\alpha\rangle+\langle 1-\alpha\rangle)\lBrack a+b\rBrack

As
x\mapsto\langle x\rangle
extends to a morphism
\mathrm{GW}(F)\to\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
(as it already extends to a morphism
\mathrm{GW}(F)\to\mathrm{K}^{MW}_{*}(F)
), one gets
\langle\alpha\rangle+\langle 1-\alpha\rangle=\langle 1\rangle+\langle\alpha(1-\alpha)\rangle
, which is also
1+\langle ab\rangle
as
\alpha(1-\alpha)
and
ab
differ multiplicatively by a square so that by reorganizing the terms of the above equality, it becomes

\lBrack a\rBrack+\lBrack b\rBrack=\lBrack a+b\rBrack+\lBrack ab\rBrack+\langle ab\rangle\lBrack a+b\rBrack

and the last two summands sum to

\lBrack ab(a+b)\rBrack

by point
(a)
of Lemma
2.3
, which gives exactly the expected equality.
∎

Proposition 3.7.

There is a morphism

\mathrm{I}(F)\to\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)

that sends the class of a Pfister form
\lAngle a\rAngle
to
\lBrack a\rBrack
.

Proof.

Proposition
3.6
tells us that
a\mapsto\lBrack a\rBrack
extends to a morphism
\mathrm{GW}(F)\to\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
. Furthermore, as
h=0
in
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
, this map factors as a map
f:\mathrm{W}(F)\to\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
.
There is a short exact sequence of abelian groups

0\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{I}(F)\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{W}(F)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{rk}\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{}0

As
F
is of characteristic
2
, the element
h
of
\mathrm{GW}(F)
becomes
2
, so that the unit of the ring structure of
\mathrm{W}(F)
factors as a map
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to\mathrm{W}(F)
that splits the previous exact sequence.
Thus, the subgroup
\mathrm{I}(F)
identifies as a quotient of
\mathrm{W}(F)
, namely the quotient by the subgroup spanned by
\langle 1\rangle
. As
\lBrack 1\rBrack=0
in
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
, the map
f:\mathrm{W}(F)\to\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
factors through
\mathrm{I}(F)
in a way that, by definition, the class of the Pfister form
\lAngle a\rAngle=1+\langle a\rangle
is mapped to
\lBrack a\rBrack
.
∎

Corollary 3.8.

There is an isomorphism
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{1}(F)\simeq\mathrm{I}(F)
.

Proof.

The morphism
\mathrm{I}(F)\to\mathrm{K}^{W}_{1}(F)
defined in the last proposition is surjective as
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{1}(F)
is spanned linearly by the elements
\lBrack u\rBrack
for
u\in F^{\times}
according to Lemma
2.4
.
Essentially by construction, the degree
1
part of the morphism
\theta
from Proposition
3.5
is a right inverse to this morphism and it is thus injective.
∎

We now engage the proof that
\theta
is an isomorphism in all degrees. Our argument will rely on the two following lemmas.

Lemma 3.9.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
2
and
a,b,c\in F^{\times}
.

(i)

One has
\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack b\rBrack=\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack ab\rBrack
in
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
.

(ii)

If there exists
u,v\in F
such that
c=u^{2}+v^{2}a
, then

\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack b\rBrack=\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack bc\rBrack

in
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
.

(iii)

If there exists
u,v\in F
such that
c=u^{2}a+v^{2}b
, then

\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack b\rBrack=\lBrack ab\rBrack\lBrack c\rBrack

in
\mathrm{K}^{W}_{*}(F)
.

Proof.

For the first point, compute that

\displaystyle\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack ab\rBrack

\displaystyle=\lBrack a\rBrack\left(\lBrack a\rBrack+\lBrack b\rBrack+\eta\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack b\rBrack\right)

\displaystyle=\lBrack a\rBrack^{2}+\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack b\rBrack+\eta\lBrack a\rBrack^{2}\lBrack b\rBrack

\displaystyle=\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack b\rBrack,

the last equality following from the fact that
\lBrack a\rBrack^{2}=0
by Lemma
3.2
.

For the second point, in the case
u=0
, then as
\lBrack a\rBrack=\left\lBrack av^{2}\right\rBrack
by Proposition
3.6
, we are done by the previous computation. Assume now that
u\neq 0
.
One has,

\displaystyle\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack bc\rBrack

\displaystyle=\lBrack a\rBrack\left\lBrack b\left(u^{2}+av^{2}\right)\right\rBrack

\displaystyle=\lBrack a\rBrack\left(\lBrack b\rBrack+\left\lBrack u^{2}+v^{2}a\right\rBrack+\eta\lBrack b\rBrack\left\lBrack u^{2}+v^{2}a\right\rBrack\right)

but using again Proposition
3.6
, one sees that

\displaystyle\lBrack a\rBrack\left\lBrack u^{2}+v^{2}a\right\rBrack

\displaystyle=\lBrack a\rBrack\left\lBrack u^{2}\left(1+{\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)}^{2}a\right)\right\rBrack

\displaystyle=\lBrack a\rBrack\left\lBrack 1+{\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)}^{2}a\right\rBrack

\displaystyle=\left\lBrack{\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)}^{2}a\right\rBrack\left\lBrack 1+{\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)}^{2}a\right\rBrack

\displaystyle=0

the last equality coming from the Steinberg relation and the characteristic 2 assumption. Thus

\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack bc\rBrack=\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack ab\rBrack

which is indeed
\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack b\rBrack
by the first point.

Finally, assume
c=u^{2}a+v^{2}b
. Then as
c=u^{2}a\left(1+{\left(\frac{v}{ua}\right)}^{2}ab\right)
, one gets

\displaystyle\lBrack c\rBrack\lBrack ab\rBrack

\displaystyle=\left(\left\lBrack u^{2}a\right\rBrack+\left\lBrack\left(1+{\left(\frac{v}{ua}\right)}^{2}ab\right)\right\rBrack\right)\lBrack ab\rBrack

\displaystyle=\left\lBrack u^{2}a\right\rBrack\lBrack ab\rBrack+\left\lBrack\left(1+{\left(\frac{v}{ua}\right)}^{2}ab\right)\right\rBrack\lBrack ab\rBrack

Using again Proposition
3.6
, characteristic
2
and the Steinberg relation, the second summand of the right hand side is zero while the first summand is
\lBrack a\rBrack\lBrack ab\rBrack
, so we are done using the first point.
∎

Theorem 3.12.

Let

. The map

It suffices to show the assertion when

Indeed, any

such that

By definition of
\theta

implies that this lift of

in

is an element of the subgroup spanned by the Witt relation, and one can thus write it as a sum of elements that generates the Witt relations.
All of this only involves a finite number of elements of

and thus actually happens in the free abelian group on the nonzero elements of the subfield of

Now, such a field is finitely generated over

[

and for such field, it is known

that

Let us first show that

Let

.
Again by Lemma

Because of the dimension assertion, the family of all nonempty products of the

. This is precisely saying that the pure subspace of the Pfister inner product space

\lBrack 1\rBrack
is zero. This at least establishes that
\theta
is an isomorphism in high enough degrees, as both source and targets are zero. Now, assuming that
\theta_{k}

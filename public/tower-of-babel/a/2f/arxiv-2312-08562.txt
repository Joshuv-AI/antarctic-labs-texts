The covariant functoriality of graph algebras
Piotr M. Hajac, Mariusz Tobolski

Abstract
. In the standard category of directed graphs, graph morphisms map edges to edges. By allowing graph morphisms to map edges to finite paths (path homomorphisms of graphs), we obtain an ambient category in which we determine subcategories enjoying covariant functors to categories of algebras given by constructions of path algebras, Cohn path algebras, and Leavitt path algebras, respectively. Thus we obtain new tools to unravel homomorphisms between Leavitt path algebras and between graph C*-algebras. In particular, a graph-algebraic presentation of the inclusion of the C*-algebra of a quantum real projective plane into the Toeplitz algebra allows us to determine a quantum CW-complex structure of the former. It comes as a mixed-pullback theorem where two ∗ * -homomorphisms are covariantly induced from path homomorphisms of graphs and the remaining two are contravariantly induced by admissible inclusions of graphs. As a main result and an application of new covariant-induction tools, we prove such a mixed-pullback theorem for arbitrary graphs whose all vertex-simple loops have exits, which substantially enlarges the scope of examples coming from noncommutative topology.

1.
Introduction

The standard category of directed graphs and graph homomorphism found spectacular application in solving problems concerning
finitely generated free groups
[
20
]
. Herein, prompted by interesting examples and the flexibility afforded by the Cuntz–Krieger relations
[
8
]
,
we consider a much larger category of directed
graphs whose morphisms are path homomorphisms of graphs. The main idea is that path homomorphisms need not preserve the lengths
of paths, which is well suited to study
*
-homomorphisms between graph C*-algebras
[
5
,
10
]
that are not gauge equivariant.

To systematically understand different layers of the covariant induction
[
19
]
, first we observe that the construction of a path algebra defines a covariant functor
on the subcategory given by path homomorphisms that are injective on vertices. Then we propose a new monotonicity condition for
such path homomorphisms (generalizing
[
7
, Lemma 3.3(1)]
), and prove that it defines a further restricted subcategory which yields a covariant functor
via the construction
of a Cohn path algebra
[
6
]
. Finally, we unravel a general regularity condition, vastly generalizing its earlier incarnation
[
11
, Section 2.3]
(cf.
[
1
, Definition 1.6.2]
),
custom-designed to handle the second-type Cuntz–Krieger relation, and thus arrive at the subcategory bringing about a covariant functor via
the contruction of a Leavitt path algebra and its enveloping graph C*-algebra.

As a key application, we use the aforementioned covariant functor into graph C*-algebras to prove a mixed-pullback theorem (Theorem
5.2
)
substantially generalizing the mixed-pullback theorem
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
. In particular, we replace the acyclicity assumption in the latter by “all vertex-simple
loops have exits” in the former (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 2]
). We instantiate our theorem by the graph of a quantum projective plane
[
12
,
13
,
16
]
, which has a vertex-simple loop
with an exit. The thus obtained pullback
structure of its C*-algebra can be interpreted as a quantum CW-complex structure
[
9
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The standard category of graphs

A (directed)
graph
is a quadruple
E:=(E^{0},E^{1},s_{E},t_{E})
, where:

•

E^{0}
is the set of
vertices
,

•

E^{1}
is the set of
edges
,

•

E^{1}\overset{s_{E}}{\to}E^{0}
is the
source
map assigning to each edge its beginning,

•

E^{1}\overset{t_{E}}{\to}E^{0}
is the
target
(range) map assigning to each edge its end.

Let
v
be a vertex in a graph
E
. It is called a
sink
iff
s^{-1}_{E}(v)=\emptyset
, it is called a
source
iff
t^{-1}_{E}(v)=\emptyset
, and it is called
regular
iff it is not a sink and
|s_{E}^{-1}(v)|<\infty
. The subset of regular vertices of a graph

E
is denoted by
{\rm reg}(E)
. A
finite path

p
in
E
is a vertex or a finite collection
e_{1}\ldots e_{n}
of edges satisfying

(2.1)

t_{E}(e_{1})=s_{E}(e_{2}),\quad t_{E}(e_{2})=s_{E}(e_{3}),\quad\ldots,\quad t_{E}(e_{n-1})=s_{E}(e_{n}).

We denote the set of all finite paths in
E
by
FP(E)
. The beginning
s_{PE}(p)
of
p
is
s_{E}(e_{1})
and the end
t_{PE}(p)
of
p
is
t_{E}(e_{n})
. The
beginning and the end of a vertex is the vertex itself. We thus extend the source and target maps to maps
s_{PE},\,t_{PE}\colon FP(E)\rightarrow E^{0}
.
Vertices are considered as finite paths of length
0
. Every edge is a path of
length
1
. The
length
of a finite path that is not a vertex is the size of the tuple. The set of all paths in
E
of length
n

is denoted by
FP_{n}(E)
.
A
loop
is a positive-length path that begins and ends at the same vertex.

Definition 2.1
.

A homomorphism from a graph
E:=(E^{0},E^{1},s_{E},t_{E})
to a graph
F:=(F^{0},F^{1},s_{F},t_{F})

is a pair of maps

(f^{0}:E^{0}\to F^{0},f^{1}:E^{1}\to F^{1})

satisfying the conditions:

s_{F}\circ f^{1}=f^{0}\circ s_{E}\,,\qquad t_{F}\circ f^{1}=f^{0}\circ t_{E}\,.

We call
(f_{0},f_{1})
injective or surjective if both
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
are injective or surjective, respectively.

One can easily check that graphs with homomorphisms of graphs form a category. We refer to this category as the

standard category of graphs
.

2.2.
Different types of graph algebras

Let
E
be a graph and
R_{E}
be the set of all subsets of
FP(E)
. Equiping the latter with
the set union
\cup
as addition and the concatenation of paths as multiplication renders
R_{E}
a unital
semiring. Here the neutral element for addition is
\emptyset
and the neutral element for multiplication
is
E^{0}
. Restricting this structure to the set
R^{f}_{E}
of all finite subsets of
FP(E)
yields a subsemiring
of
R_{E}
. The semiring
R^{f}_{E}
is unital if and only if
|E^{0}|<\infty
, in which case the unit is
E^{0}
.

Next, let
E
be a non-empty graph and let
k
be any field.
The following construction can be thought of as a linearization of
R^{f}_{E}
.
Consider the vector space

(2.2)

kE:=\{f\in{\rm Map}(FP(E),k)~|~f(p)\neq 0\text{ for finitely many }p\in FP(E)\},

where the addition and scalar multiplication are pointwise. Then the set of functions
\{\chi_{p}\}_{p\in FP(E)}

given by

(2.3)

\chi_{p}(q):=\delta_{p,q}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{for }p=q\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

is a linear basis of
kE
. By checking the associativity, one can prove that the formulas

(2.4)

m:kE\times kE\longrightarrow kE,\qquad m(\chi_{p},\chi_{q}):=\begin{cases}\chi_{pq}&\text{if }t(p)=s(q)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

define a multiplication on
kE
.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
3
, Definition II.1.2]
)

Let
E
be a non-empty graph.
The above constructed algebra
(kE,+,0,m)
is called the path algebra of
E
.
If
E=\emptyset
, then
kE:=0
.

To define Cohn and Leavitt path algebras, we need extended graphs.
The
extended graph

\bar{E}:=(\bar{E}^{0},\bar{E}^{1},s_{\bar{E}},t_{\bar{E}})

of the graph
E
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\bar{E}^{0}:=E^{0},\quad\bar{E}^{1}:=E^{1}\sqcup(E^{1})^{*},\quad(E^{1})^{*}:=\{e^{*}~|~e\in E^{1}\},

\displaystyle\forall\;e\in E^{1}:\quad s_{\bar{E}}(e):=s_{E}(e),\quad t_{\bar{E}}(e):=t_{E}(e),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\forall\;e^{*}\in(E^{1})^{*}:\quad s_{\bar{E}}(e^{*}):=t_{E}(e),\quad t_{\bar{E}}(e^{*}):=s_{E}(e).

Given a path
p=e_{1}e_{2}\ldots e_{n}\in FP(E)
, we put
p^{*}:=e_{n}^{*}\ldots e_{1}^{*}
for the corresponding path in
FP(\overline{E})
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
1
, Definition 1.5.9]
)

Let
E
be a graph,
k
be a field, and
X
be a subset of the set
\mathrm{reg}(E)

of all regular vertices in
E
. The relative Cohn path algebra
C_{k}^{X}(E)
of
E

is the path algebra
k\bar{E}
of the extended graph
\bar{E}
divided by the ideal generated
by the union of the following sets:

(1)

\{\chi_{e^{*}}\chi_{f}-\delta_{e,f}\chi_{t_{E}(e)}~|~e,f\in E^{1}\}
,

(2)

\{\sum_{e\in s^{-1}_{E}(v)}\chi_{e}\chi_{e^{*}}-\chi_{v}~|~v\in X\}
.

The relative Cohn algebra
C_{k}^{\emptyset}(E)=:C_{k}(E)
is called the Cohn path algebra
of
E
, and the relative Cohn algebra
C_{k}^{\mathrm{reg}(E)}(E)=:L_{k}(E)

is called the Leavitt path algebra of
E
.

The algebra
C_{k}^{X}(E)
is isomorphic to the universal algebra generated by the elements

\chi_{v}
,
\chi_{e}
,
\chi_{e^{*}}
,
v\in E^{0}
,
e\in E^{1}
, subject to the above relations (1) and (2) and
the standard path-algebraic relations
\chi_{v}\chi_{w}=\delta_{v,w}\chi_{v}
,
v,w\in E^{0}
, and
\chi_{s_{E}(e)}\chi_{e}=\chi_{e}=\chi_{e}\chi_{t_{E}(e)}
,
e\in E^{1}
.

In the case
k=\mathbb{C}
, one defines an anti-linear involution on
L_{\mathbb{C}}(E)
by the formulas

(2.6)

([\chi_{v}])^{*}:=[\chi_{v}],\quad([\chi_{e}])^{*}:=[\chi_{e^{*}}],\quad([\chi_{e^{*}}])^{*}:=[\chi_{e}],\quad v\in E^{0},\quad e\in E^{1},

to render
L_{\mathbb{C}}(E)
a complex
*
-algebra.
This brings us to the following key definition:

Definition 2.4
.

(
[
1
, Definition 5.2.1]
)

Let
E
be a graph. The graph C*-algebra
C^{*}(E)
of
E
is
the universal enveloping C*-algebra of the complex
*
-algebra
L_{\mathbb{C}}(E)
.

Definition
2.4
is equivalent to
[
10
, Definition 1]
defining
C^{*}(E)
as the universal C*-algebra
generated by mutually orthogonal projections
P_{v}
,
v\in E^{0}
, and partial isometries
S_{e}
,
e\in E^{1}
, with mutually
orthogonal ranges, satisfying

(1)

S^{*}_{e}S_{e}=P_{t_{E}(e)}
for all
e\in E^{1}
,

(2)

P_{v}=\sum_{e\in s^{-1}_{E}(v)}S_{e}S_{e}^{*}
for all
v\in{\rm reg}(E)
,

(3)

S_{e}S_{e}^{*}\leq P_{s_{E}(e)}
for all
e\in E^{1}
.

In what follows, we will need the notaion
S_{p}:=S_{e_{1}}S_{e_{2}}\ldots S_{e_{n}}
,
where
p=e_{1}e_{2}\ldots e_{n}
is a finite path in
E
, and
S_{v}:=P_{v}
, where
v\in E^{0}
.

3.
Subcategories in the category of graphs and path morphisms of graphs

Let us now consider a more general category of graphs.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
E
and
F
be graphs.
A path homomorphism from
E
to
F
is a map
f\colon FP(E)\to FP(F)
satisfying:

(1)

f(E^{0})\subseteq F^{0}
,

(2)

s_{PF}\circ f=f\circ s_{PE}\,,\quad t_{PF}\circ f=f\circ t_{PE}\,
,

(3)

\forall\;p,q\in FP(E)\text{ such that }t_{PE}(p)=s_{PE}(q)\colon f(pq)=f(p)f(q)
.

If
(f^{0},f^{1})\colon E\to F
is a homomorphism of graphs, then
f\colon FP(E)\to FP(F)
defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\forall\;v\in E^{0}\colon f(v):=f^{0}(v),\quad\forall\;e\in E^{1}\colon f(e):=f^{1}(e),

\displaystyle\forall\;e_{1}\ldots e_{n}\in FP(E)\colon f(e_{1}\ldots e_{n}):=f^{1}(e_{1})\ldots f^{1}(e_{n})\in FP(F),

is a path homomorphism of graphs. The thus induced path homomorphism of graphs preserves the length of
paths. Vice versa, if a path homomorphism of graphs preserves the length of
paths, then it is induced by a homomorphism of graphs. Therefore, if a path homomorphism of graphs maps
edges to paths longer than one or to vertices, then it
does not come from a homomorphism of graphs. Note also that if we treat a graph as a small category, where vertices are objects and
finite paths are morphisms between them, then path homomorphisms are functors between these categories.
Therefore, it is clear that path homomorphisms yield a category:

Definition 3.2
.

We denote the category of graphs and path homomorphisms by
PG
and its subcategory of graphs and path homomorphisms which
are injective on the set of vertices by
IPG
. The subcategory of
IPG
obtained by restricting objects to graphs with finitely many
vertices and morphisms to path homomorphisms that are bijective on vertices is denoted by
BPG
.

To deal with the condition Definition
2.3
(1), we introduce the following relation on the set of finite paths:

(3.2)

\alpha\preceq\beta\iff\exists\;\gamma\in FP(E)\colon\beta=\alpha\gamma.

Note that
\preceq
is a partial order. Indeed, reflexivity holds because
\alpha=\alpha t_{E}(\alpha)
,
transitivity holds because

(3.3)

(\beta=\alpha\gamma\;\text{and}\;\delta=\beta\gamma^{\prime})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\delta=\alpha\gamma\gamma^{\prime},

and antisymmetry holds because

(\beta=\alpha\gamma\;\text{and}\;\alpha=\beta\gamma^{\prime})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\beta=\beta\gamma^{\prime}\gamma\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma^{\prime}=t_{PE}(\beta)=\gamma\quad\Rightarrow\quad\alpha=\beta t_{PE}(\beta)=\beta.

Lemma 3.3
.

Restricting morphisms in the category
IPG
to the morphisms satisfying the monotonicity condition

(3.4)

f(e)\preceq f(e^{\prime})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e=e^{\prime},\quad

where
e
and
e^{\prime}
are edges, yields a subcategory of
IPG
.

Proof.

For starters, for each graph
E
, (
3.4
) is satified by
f=\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
. Assume now that both
morphisms
f\colon E\to F
and
g\colon F\to G
satisfy (
3.4
) and
g(f(e))\preceq g(f(e^{\prime}))
.
If
f(e)
is a vertex,
then
g(f(e))
is also a vertex, so

(3.5)

g(f(e))=s_{PG}\big(g(f(e^{\prime}))\big)=g\big(s_{PF}(f(e^{\prime}))\big).

Hence,
f(e)=s_{PF}(f(e^{\prime}))
by the vertex injectivity of
g
. Consequently,

f(e)\preceq f(e^{\prime})
, which implies
e=e^{\prime}
.
Much in the same way, if
f(e^{\prime})
is a vertex, then also
g(f(e^{\prime}))
is a vertex, so

(3.6)

g(f(e^{\prime}))=g(f(e))=s_{PG}\big(g(f(e))\big)=g\big(s_{PF}(f(e))\big).

Hence,
f(e^{\prime})=s_{PF}(f(e))
by the vertex injectivity of
g
. Consequently,

f(e^{\prime})\preceq f(e)
,
which implies
e=e^{\prime}
.

Assume now that

f(e)=e_{1}\ldots e_{m}
and
f(e^{\prime})=e^{\prime}_{1}\ldots e^{\prime}_{n}\,
,
where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m},e^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,e^{\prime}_{n}
are edges.
Then

g(e_{1})\ldots g(e_{m})\preceq g(e^{\prime}_{1})\ldots g(e^{\prime}_{n})
implies that
g(e_{1})\preceq g(e^{\prime}_{1})

or
g(e^{\prime}_{1})\preceq g(e_{1})
, whence
e_{1}=e^{\prime}_{1}
. It follows that

g(e_{2})\ldots g(e_{m})\preceq g(e^{\prime}_{2})\ldots g(e^{\prime}_{n})
. We can iterate this argument to conclude that

e_{i}=e^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,\mathrm{min}\{m,n\}\}
. Therefore,
f(e)\preceq f(e^{\prime})
or
f(e^{\prime})\preceq f(e)
,
so
e=e^{\prime}
.
Summarizing, we have shown that

(3.7)

(f\circ g)(e)\preceq(f\circ g)(e^{\prime})\quad\Rightarrow\quad e=e^{\prime},

so (
3.4
) determines a subcategory.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

The subcategory of the category
IPG
given by Lemma
3.3
is denoted by
MIPG
and its subcategory given by restricting
its objects and morphisms to
BPG
is denoted by
MBPG
.

Finally, to deal with the condition Definition
2.3
(2), we further restrict our morphisms.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
f\colon FP(E)\to FP(F)
be a morphism in the category
PG
and let

{\rm reg}_{0}(E):=\{v\in{\rm reg}(E)~|~s_{E}^{-1}(v)=\{e\}\text{ and }t_{E}(e)=v\}

denote the set of 0-regular vertices. We call
f

regular
whenever the following conditions hold:

(1)

For any
v\in\mathrm{reg}(E)\setminus{\rm reg}_{0}(E)
, we require that:

(a)

f
restricted to
s_{E}^{-1}(v)
be injective;

(b)

p\in f(s_{E}^{-1}(v))
if and only if

(i)

\exists\;n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}\colon p=e_{1}\ldots e_{n},\,e_{i}\in F^{1}\text{ for all }i
,

(ii)

pq\in f(s_{E}^{-1}(v))\,\Rightarrow\,q=t_{PE}(p)
,

(iii)

\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},e\in s_{F}^{-1}(s_{F}(e_{i}))\,\exists\,r\in FP(F):e_{1}\ldots e_{i-1}er\in f(s_{E}^{-1}(v))
.

(2)

For any
v\in{\rm reg}_{0}(E)
, either the above condition holds or
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))=f(v)
.

Note that all vertices in the paths belonging to
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
, possibly except for their endpoints, are regular. Also, observe that
the set

f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
can be viewed as constructed in the following way: we take
x\in s_{F}^{-1}(f(v))

and either
set it aside as a length-one element of
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
, or extend it by all edges emitted from
t_{F}(x)
. Any thus
obtained path of length two, we either set aside as an element of

f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
, or extend it by all edges emitted from its end. Then we iterate this procedure until we obtain the
set
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
f\colon E\to F
be a path homomorphism of graphs such that the restriction

s^{-1}_{E}(v)\longrightarrow s^{-1}_{F}(f(v))

is bijective for all
v\in\mathrm{reg}(E)
. Then
f
is regular. Observe that the above condition was considered in
[
11
, Section 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Restricting morphisms in the category
MIPG
to the morphisms
satisfying the regularity condition of Definition
3.5

yields a subcategory of
MIPG
.

Proof.

To begin with, it is clear that, for each graph
E
,
f=\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{E}
satisfies the regularity condition. Assume now that

f\colon E\to F
and
g\colon F\to G
are morphisms in
MIPG
. Then, by Lemma
3.3
,

g\circ f

is injective on edges.
Next, let us observe that regular vertices that are not 0-regular must be mapped to regular vertices that are not 0-regular by
any morphism in
MIPG
that satisfies the regularity condition.
Indeed, if
v\in\mathrm{reg}(E)\setminus\mathrm{reg}_{0}(E)
,
then
f(v)\in\mathrm{reg}(E)
. Suppose now that
f(v)\in\mathrm{reg}_{0}(E)
.
Then, since
v
emits more than one edge, or emits an edge that is not a loop, and
f(v)

emits only one edge that is a loop, we immediately obtain a
contradiction with the regularity condition or the injectivity-on-vertices condition.

Now, let us consider the composition of morphisms restricted to regular vertices that are not 0-regular.
From the regularity of
f
and the monotonicity of
g
, we infer that

(3.8)

g\circ f\colon s_{E}^{-1}(v)\to g(f(s^{-1}_{E}(v)))

is bijective for any such a vertex
v
.
Indeed, arguing as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
,
we see that the only different
paths which
g
can glue are paths such that one extends the other, and there are no such pairs of different paths in
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
.
Furthermore, since
f(v)
is regular but not
0
-regular, and
g
is regular, all elements of
g(f(s_{E}^{-1}(v)))
are positive-length paths. Better still, the implication
p
,
pq\in g(f(s^{-1}_{E}(v)))

\Rightarrow

q=t_{PE}(p)
follows immediately from the monotonicity of
g\circ f
guaranteed by Lemma
3.3
. Next, assume that
e_{1}\ldots e_{n}\in g(f(s^{-1}_{E}(v)))
and
e\in s^{-1}_{G}(s_{G}(e_{i}))
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then
e_{1}\ldots e_{i}=g(x_{1}\ldots x_{m})
, where
x_{j}\in F^{1}
,
1\leq j\leq m
. As
x_{1}\ldots x_{m}\in f(s_{E}^{-1}(v))
, the vertex
s_{F}(x_{m})
is regular. If it is not
0
-regular, then
g(x_{m})=e_{k}\ldots e_{i}
for some
k
. Now, there exists
r\in FP(G)
such that
e_{k}\ldots e_{i-1}er=g(x)
for
x\in s_{F}^{-1}(s_{F}(x_{m}))
. Also, there exists
r^{\prime}\in FP(F)
such that
x_{1}\ldots x_{m-1}xr^{\prime}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
. Hence,

(3.9)

g(x_{1}\ldots x_{m-1}xr^{\prime})=g(x_{1}\ldots x_{m-1})g(x)g(r^{\prime})=e_{1}\ldots e_{k-1}e_{k}\ldots e_{i-1}erg(r^{\prime}),

so
e_{1}\ldots e_{i}erg(r^{\prime})\in g(f(s^{-1}_{E}(v)))

s_{F}(x_{m})\in{\rm reg}_{0}(F)

e_{1}\ldots e_{i}=g(x_{1}\ldots x_{m-1})
. Reasoning inductively, we arrive at
e_{1}\ldots e_{i}=g(x_{1})
, which ends the argument because
s_{F}(x_{1})=f(v)\notin{\rm reg}_{0}(F)
.

Finally, if
v\in\mathrm{reg}_{0}(E)
, then
f(s_{E}^{-1}(v))
is either
f(v)
or a loop at
f(v)
such that every vertex of this loop emits only one edge.
In the former case we are done as
g\circ f
satisfies the regularity condition at
v
. The latter case splits into two subcases: either the loop at

f(v)
has only one vertex or more than one vertex. In the former subcase,
f(v)\in\mathrm{reg}_{0}(F)
, so
g
either maps
s_{F}^{-1}(f(v))

g(f(v))
or to a loop at
g(f(v))

(3.10)

g(f(s_{E}^{-1}(v)))=g((s_{F}^{-1}(f(v)))^{n})=g(f(v)),

where
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}

maps the loop

g

g(f(s^{-1}_{E}(v)))

as morphisms. One can easily check that the
assignment

\displaystyle\mathrm{Obj}(IPG)\ni E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\longmapsto}}R_{E}\in\mathrm{Obj}(SR),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mor}(IPG)\ni(f\colon E\to F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle R}}{{\longmapsto}}(f_{*}\colon R_{E}\to R_{F})\in\mathrm{Mor}(SR),

(4.1)

\displaystyle R_{E}\ni A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*}}}{{\longmapsto}}f(A)\in R_{F}\,,

defines a covariant functor. As the image of a finite subset is a finite subset, the same assignment
but with
R
replaced by
R^{f}
also defines a covariant functor to the category
SR
. In what follows, we explore such covariant functoriality for different types of algebras given by graphs.

Let
KA
be the category
of algebras over a field
k
with algebra homomorphisms as morphisms, and let
UKA
be the category
of unital algebras over a field
k
with unital algebra homomorphisms as morphisms.

Proposition 4.1
.

(cf.

[
11
, Section 2.2]
)
The assignment

\displaystyle\mathrm{Obj}(IPG)\ni E

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longmapsto}}kE\in\mathrm{Obj}(KA),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mor}(IPG)\ni(f\colon E\to F)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longmapsto}}(f_{*}\colon kE\to kF)\in\mathrm{Mor}(KA),

\displaystyle\forall\;p\in FP(E)\colon kE\ni\chi_{p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*}}}{{\longmapsto}}\chi_{f(p)}\in kF\,,

defines a covariant functor. Furthermore, the same assignment restricted to the subcategory
BPG

yields a covariant functor to the category
UKA
.

Proof.

For starters,
f_{*}\colon kE\to kF
is, clearly, a linear map. To check that it is a homomorphism of algebras,
using the injectivity of
f\colon FP(E)\to FP(F)
restricted to
E^{0}
, we compute:

\displaystyle f_{*}(\chi_{p}\chi_{q})

\displaystyle=\delta_{t_{PE}(p),s_{PE}(q)}f_{*}(\chi_{pq})=\delta_{t_{PE}(p),s_{PE}(q)}\chi_{f(pq)}=\delta_{t_{PE}(p),s_{PE}(q)}\chi_{f(p)f(q)}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\delta_{t_{PF}(f(p)),s_{PF}(f(q))}\chi_{f(p)f(q)}=\chi_{f(p)}\chi_{f(q)}=f_{*}(\chi_{p})f_{*}(\chi_{q}).

Hence,
f_{*}
is an algebra homomorphism, as claimed.
The covariance is obvious because

(4.3)

(f\circ g)_{*}=f_{*}\circ g_{*}.

Finally, if
E
and
F
are graphs with finitely many vertices, then
kE
and
kF
are unital algebras
with
\sum_{v\in E^{0}}\chi_{v}
and
\sum_{w\in F^{0}}\chi_{w}
as respective units, and the unitality of
f_{*}

follows from the bijectivity of
f\colon FP(E)\to FP(F)

restricted to vertices:

(4.4)

f_{*}\left(\sum_{v\in E^{0}}\chi_{v}\right)=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}\chi_{f(v)}=\sum_{w\in F^{0}}\chi_{w}\,.\vskip-17.07164pt

∎

4.2.
Cohn path algebras

To pass to the functoriality of the construction of Cohn and Leavitt path algebras, first we need to establish the
functoriality of the extended graph construction. We leave the routine proof of the following lemma to the reader.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f\colon E\to F
be any path homomorphism of graphs. Then the formulas

\displaystyle\forall\;v\in E^{0}\colon\bar{f}(v):=f(v),\quad\forall\;e\in E^{1}\colon\bar{f}(e):=f(e),\,\bar{f}(e^{*}):=f(e)^{*},

\displaystyle\forall\;p:=x_{1}\ldots x_{n}\in FP(\bar{E}),\,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\bar{E}^{1}\colon\bar{f}(p):=\bar{f}(x_{1})\ldots\bar{f}(x_{n}),

define a path homomorphism
\bar{f}\colon\bar{E}\to\bar{F}
of extended graphs such that the assignment

E\longmapsto\bar{E}
,
f\longmapsto\bar{f}
,
yields a
covariat endofunctor of the category of graphs and path homomorphisms of graphs.
Furthermore, this endofunctor restricts to an endofunctor of the subcategory
BPG
.

We are now ready to determine the covariant functoriality of assigning Cohn path algebras to graphs.

Proposition 4.3
.

The assignment

\displaystyle\mathrm{Obj}(MIPG)\ni E

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longmapsto}}C_{k}(E)\in\mathrm{Obj}(KA),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mor}(MIPG)\ni(f\colon E\to F)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longmapsto}}(f_{*}^{C}\colon C_{k}(E)\to C_{k}(F))\in\mathrm{Mor}(KA),

\displaystyle\forall\;p\in FP(\bar{E})\colon C_{k}(E)\ni[\chi_{p}]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*}^{C}}}{{\longmapsto}}[\chi_{\bar{f}(p)}]\in C_{k}(F)\,,

defines a covariant functor. Furthermore, the same assignment restricted to the subcategory
MBPG

yields a covariant functor to the category
UKA
.

Proof.

Combining Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
with the fact that
MIPG
is a subcategory
of
IPG
, we conclude that the assignment

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Obj}(MIPG)\ni E

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longmapsto}}k\bar{E}\in\mathrm{Obj}(KA),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mor}(MIPG)\ni(f\colon E\to F)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longmapsto}}(\bar{f}_{*}\colon k\bar{E}\to k\bar{F})\in\mathrm{Mor}(KA),

\displaystyle\forall\;p\in FP(\bar{E})\colon k\bar{E}\ni\chi_{p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{f}_{*}}}{{\longmapsto}}\chi_{\bar{f}(p)}\in k\bar{F}\,,

defines a covariant functor. Next, let us check that for any graph
E
and
e\in E^{1}
such that
f(e)

is of positive length (
f(e)=:(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
) we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle[\bar{f}_{*}(\chi_{e^{*}}\chi_{e})]

\displaystyle=[\chi_{\bar{f}(e^{*}e)}]=[\chi_{f(e)^{*}}][\chi_{f(e)}]=[\chi_{f_{n}^{*}}]\ldots[\chi_{f_{1}^{*}}][\chi_{f_{1}}]\ldots[\chi_{f_{n}}]

\displaystyle=[\chi_{f_{n}^{*}}]\ldots[\chi_{f_{2}^{*}}][\chi_{t_{F}(f_{1})}][\chi_{f_{2}}]\ldots[\chi_{f_{n}}]=[\chi_{f_{n}^{*}}]\ldots[\chi_{f_{2}^{*}}][\chi_{f_{2}}]\ldots[\chi_{f_{n}}]

\displaystyle=[\chi_{t_{F}(f_{n})}]=[\bar{f}_{*}(\chi_{t_{E}(e)})].

Note that, if
f(e)
is a vertex, the above calculation is trivial.

Assume now that
e,e^{\prime}\in E^{1}
,
e\neq e^{\prime}
and
s_{E}(e)=s_{E}(e^{\prime})
.
Then, by the condition (
3.4
), we know that

f(e)\not\preceq f(e^{\prime})
and
f(e^{\prime})\not\preceq f(e)
.
Hence, neither
f(e)
nor
f(e^{\prime})
can be a vertex. Indeed, suppose that
f(e)
is a vertex. Then

(4.7)

f(e)=s_{PF}(f(e))=f(s_{PE}(e))=f(s_{PE}(e^{\prime}))=s_{PF}(f(e^{\prime})),

so
f(e^{\prime})=f(e)f(e^{\prime})
contradicting
f(e)\not\preceq f(e^{\prime})
. The path
f(e^{\prime})
cannot be a vertex by the symmetric
argument. Consequently, both
f(e)
and
f(e^{\prime})
are paths of positive length.
Let
f(e)=:f_{1}\ldots f_{n}
and
f(e^{\prime})=:f^{\prime}_{1}\ldots f^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}
.
Now, the condition
f(e)\not\preceq f(e^{\prime})
and
f(e^{\prime})\not\preceq f(e)
implies that there exists an index

i\leq\min\{n,n^{\prime}\}
such that
f_{i}\neq f^{\prime}_{i}
.
It follows that

(4.8)

[\bar{f}_{*}(\chi_{e^{*}}\chi_{e^{\prime}})]=[\chi_{\bar{f}(e^{*}e^{\prime})}]=[\chi_{f(e)^{*}}][\chi_{f(e^{\prime})}]=[\chi_{f_{n}^{*}}]\ldots[\chi_{f_{1}^{*}}][\chi_{f^{\prime}_{1}}]\ldots[\chi_{f^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}}]=0.

Note that, if
s_{E}(e)\neq s_{E}(e^{\prime})
, the above calculation is trivial.

Summarizng, we infer that
f_{*}^{C}
is a well defined
algebra homomorphism. The functoriality
(f\circ g)_{*}^{C}=f_{*}^{C}\circ g_{*}^{C}
follows
immediately from the functoriality
\overline{(f\circ g)}_{*}=\bar{f}_{*}\circ\bar{g}_{*}
.
Finally, the unitality of
f^{C}_{*}
for
f\in{\rm Mor}(MBPG)
follows immediately from (
4.4
).
∎

4.3.
Leavitt path algebras and graph C*-algebras

Finally, we consider the covariant functoriality of the construction of the Leavitt path algebra.

Theorem 4.8
.

The assignment

\displaystyle\mathrm{Obj}(RMIPG)\ni E

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longmapsto}}L_{k}(E)\in\mathrm{Obj}(KA),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Mor}(RMIPG)\ni(f\colon E\to F)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longmapsto}}(f_{*}^{L}\colon L_{k}(E)\to L_{k}(F))\in\mathrm{Mor}(KA),

\displaystyle\forall\;p\in FP(\bar{E})\colon L_{k}(E)\ni[\chi_{p}]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{*}^{L}}}{{\longmapsto}}[\chi_{\bar{f}(p)}]\in L_{k}(F)\,,

defines a covariant functor.
Furthermore, the same assignment restricted to the subcategory
RMBPG

yields a covariant functor to the category
UKA
.

Proof.

Since one can view the Leavitt path algebras as quotients of Cohn path algebras, we infer from Proposition
4.3
that it suffices to show that

(4.14)

\forall\;v\in\mathrm{reg}(E)\colon\sum_{e\in s_{E}^{-1}(v)}[\chi_{\bar{f}(ee^{*})}]=[\chi_{\bar{f}(v)}].

To this end, we need to use the regularity of
f
. For starters, let us observe that (
4.14
) is easily satisfied
for any
v\in\mathrm{reg}_{0}(E)
. Indeed, if
f(e)=w\in F^{0}
, then

(4.15)

[\chi_{\bar{f}(ee^{*})}]=[\chi_{w}]=[\chi_{\bar{f}(v)}].

If
f(e)=:p_{1}\ldots p_{n}
,
p_{1}\,,\ldots\,,p_{n}\in F^{1}
, is a loop whose all vertices emit exactly one edge, then we obtain

(4.16)

[\chi_{\bar{f}(ee^{*})}]=[\chi_{p_{1}}]\ldots[\chi_{p_{n}}][\chi_{p_{n}^{*}}]\ldots[\chi_{p_{1}^{*}}]=[\chi_{\bar{f}(v)}].

Now, let us consider
v\in\mathrm{reg}(E)\setminus\mathrm{reg}_{0}(E)
.
Then, by the bijectivity assumption, we have

(4.17)

\sum_{e\in s_{E}^{-1}(v)}[\chi_{\bar{f}(ee^{*})}]=\sum_{e\in s_{E}^{-1}(v)}[\chi_{f(e)}][\chi_{f(e)^{*}}]=\sum_{p_{i}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))}[\chi_{p_{i}}][\chi_{p_{i}^{*}}].

Next, let
n
be the length of a longest path in
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
. Then we can partition the set
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
by path lengths
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{i}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))}[\chi_{p_{i}}][\chi_{p_{i}^{*}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\;\sum_{p_{i,j}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))\cap FP_{j}(F)}[\chi_{p_{i,j}}][\chi_{p_{i,j}^{*}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{p_{i,j}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))\cap FP_{j}(F)}[\chi_{p_{i,j}}][\chi_{p_{i,j}^{*}}]\right)+\sum_{p_{i,n}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))\cap FP_{n}(F)}[\chi_{p_{i,n}}][\chi_{p_{i,n}^{*}}].

Writing
p_{i,n}=:q_{i,n-1}x_{i,n}
, where
x_{i,n}\in F^{1}
, we obtain

(4.18)

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{i,n}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))\cap FP_{n}(F)}[\chi_{p_{i,n}}][\chi_{p_{i,n}^{*}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{i,n-1}\in E_{f(v)}^{n-1}}\;\sum_{x_{i,n}\in s_{F}^{-1}(t_{F}(q_{i,n-1}))}[\chi_{q_{i,n-1}}][\chi_{x_{i,n}}][\chi_{x_{i,n}^{*}}][\chi_{q_{i,n-1}^{*}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{i,n-1}\in E_{f(v)}^{n-1}}[\chi_{q_{i,n-1}}][\chi_{q_{i,n-1}^{*}}].

Here
E_{f(v)}^{n-1}
stands for all paths of length
n-1
that where extended to paths of length
n

in the construction of
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))
, and the first step follows from Definition
3.5
(1). Furthermore,
combining the sets
f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))\cap FP_{n-1}(F)
and
E_{f(v)}^{n-1}
we obtain all paths of length
n-1
that
are given by extending paths of length
n-2
by all possible edges.
Hence, using an analogous notation, we infer that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{i,n-1}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))\cap FP_{n-1}(F)}[\chi_{p_{i,n-1}}][\chi_{p_{i,n-1}^{*}}]+\sum_{q_{i,n-1}\in E_{f(v)}^{n-1}}[\chi_{q_{i,n-1}}][\chi_{q_{i,n-1}^{*}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{i,n-2}\in E_{f(v)}^{n-2}}\;\sum_{x_{i,n-1}\in s_{F}^{-1}(t_{F}(q_{i,n-2}))}[\chi_{q_{i,n-2}}][\chi_{x_{i,n-1}}][\chi_{x_{i,n-1}^{*}}][\chi_{q_{i,n-2}^{*}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{q_{i,n-2}\in E_{f(v)}^{n-2}}[\chi_{q_{i,n-2}}][\chi_{q_{i,n-2}^{*}}].

Iterating this procedure, we conclude that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\sum_{p_{i}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))}[\chi_{p_{i}}][\chi_{p_{i}^{*}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{p_{i,1}\in f(s^{-1}_{E}(v))\cap FP_{1}(F)}[\chi_{p_{i,1}}][\chi_{p_{i,1}^{*}}]+\sum_{q_{i,1}\in E^{1}_{f(v)}}[\chi_{q_{i,1}}][\chi_{q_{i,1}^{*}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in s_{F}^{-1}(f(v))}[\chi_{e}][\chi_{e^{*}}]=[\chi_{\bar{f}(v)}].

Combining this with (
4.17
), we complete the proof.
∎

5.
Mixed-pullback theorem

In
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
, the authors together with A. Chirvasitu proved a pullback theorem for graph C*-algebras (cf. pushout-to-pullback theorem
[
14
,
15
]
). The theorem was motivated by
examples coming from noncommutative topology such as the quantum CW complex structure of the standard Podleś quantum sphere. Since the result
involves both covariant and contravariant functors from certain categories of graphs, we call it a mixed-pullback theorem. Herein, using
Corollary
4.9
, we substantially
improve this result by allowing loops, and restricting the monotonicity condition from arbitrary finite paths to edges (see (
3.4
)).
Thus, the quantum CW-complex structure of the quantum real projective plane (Example
5.3
) and the basic Example
4.4
are now
within the scope of the theorem. To simplify notation, in this section, given a morphism
f:FP(E)\to FP(F)
in the category RMIPG, we write
f_{*}
for the induced C*-algebra homomorphism given by Corollary
4.9
.

First, we recall the notions of a saturated subset and an admissible inclusion. A subset
H\subseteq E^{0}
is called
saturated
if there does not exist a
regular vertex
v\in E^{0}\setminus H
such that
t_{E}(s^{-1}_{E}(v))\subseteq H
. An injective graph homomorphism
(\pi^{0},\pi^{1}):F\hookrightarrow E
is called
an
admissible inclusion
(cf.
[
7
, Definition 3.1]
) if the following conditions are satisfied:

(A1)

E^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}(F^{0})
is saturated,

(A2)

t_{E}^{-1}(\pi^{0}(F^{0}))\subseteq\pi^{1}(F^{1})
.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that in
[
7
, Definition 3.1]
it is assumed that
E^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}(F^{0})
is hereditary and that we have the equality rather than the
inclusion of sets in the condition (A2). However, the reverse inclusion to the one in (A2) is automatically true for any graph homomorphism, and one can
show that (A2) implies that
E^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}(F^{0})
is hereditary (e.g., see
[
15
]
).

Although the concept of admissibility allows the existence of breaking vertices, we need to control their behavior to formulate Theorem
5.2
.
Recall that, for
H\subseteq E^{0}
, we say that
v\in E^{0}\setminus H
is a
breaking vertex
of
H
whenever

(5.1)

|s^{-1}_{E}(v)|=\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0<|s^{-1}_{E}(v)\cap t^{-1}_{E}(E^{0}\setminus H)|<\infty.

By
B_{H}
we denote the set of all breaking vertices of
H
.
Note that a breaking vertex of
H
becomes regular in the subgraph obtained by removing all vertices
in
H
and all edges ending in
H
.

Next, recall that, by
[
4
, Corollary 3.5]
,
if
(\pi^{0},\pi^{1}):F\hookrightarrow E
is an admissible inclusion, then there exists a surjective gauge-equivariant
*
-homo
morphism
\pi^{*}:C^{*}(E)\to C^{*}(F)
given by

(5.2)

\pi^{*}(P_{v})=\begin{cases}P_{v}&v\in\pi^{0}(F^{0}),\\
0&v\notin\pi^{0}(F^{0}),\end{cases}\quad v\in E^{0},\qquad\pi^{*}(S_{e})=\begin{cases}S_{e}&e\in\pi^{1}(F^{1}),\\
0&e\notin\pi^{1}(F^{1}),\end{cases}\quad e\in E^{1},

and whose kernel can be explicitely written as follows:

\displaystyle\ker(\pi^{*})

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\{P_{v}~|~v\in E^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}(F^{0})\}\cup\Big\{P_{w}-\hskip-34.1433pt\sum_{e\in\pi^{1}\big((\pi^{0}\circ s_{F})^{-1}(w)\big)}\hskip-34.1433ptS_{e}S_{e}^{*}\;\Big|\;w\in B_{E^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}(F^{0})}\Big\}\Big\rangle

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\left\{S_{\alpha}S_{\beta}^{*},\;S_{\mu}\Big(P_{w}-\hskip-34.1433pt\sum_{e\in\pi^{1}\big((\pi^{0}\circ s_{F})^{-1}(w)\big)}\hskip-34.1433ptS_{e}S_{e}^{*}\Big)S_{\nu}^{*}~\Big|~\begin{matrix}\alpha,\,\beta,\,\mu,\,\nu\in FP(E),\\
t(\alpha)=t(\beta)\in E^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}(F^{0}),\\
t(\mu)=t(\nu)=w\in B_{E^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}(F^{0})}\\
\end{matrix}\right\}.

Finally, recall that a
vertex-simple loop
is a loop whose each vertex emits only one edge belonging to the loop. We say that a loop has an
exit

iff one of its vertices emits an edge not belonging to the loop.
We are now ready to prove the main result of this section,
which generalizes
[
7
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(\pi^{0}_{i},\pi^{1}_{i}):F_{i}\hookrightarrow E_{i}
,
i=1,2
, be admissible inclusions of graphs. Also, assume that
E_{1}
is a graph whose every vertex-simple
loop has an exit. Furthermore, let
f\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
be a morphism in the category
RMIPG
mapping only breaking vertices to breaking vertices:

(5.4)

f^{-1}(B_{E_{2}^{0}\setminus\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0})})\subseteq B_{E_{1}^{0}\setminus\pi_{1}^{0}(F_{1}^{0})}

and such that

(5.5)

f^{-1}(\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0}))\subseteq\pi_{1}^{0}(F_{1}^{0}).

Finally, assume that
f
restricts to a morphism
f|:F_{1}\to F_{2}
in the same category such that

(5.6)

f|\big((\pi^{0}_{1}\circ s_{F_{1}})^{-1}(B_{E_{1}^{0}\setminus\pi_{1}^{0}(F_{1}^{0})})\big)\subseteq F^{1}_{2}\,.

Then, if all the finite paths ending in
(E_{2}^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}_{2}(F_{2}^{0}))\cup B_{E_{2}^{0}\setminus\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0})}
are in the image of
f
,
the induced
*
-homo
morphisms exist and render the diagram

(5.7)

\textstyle{C^{*}(E_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{1}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{f_{*}}
\textstyle{C^{*}(F_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f|_{*}}
\textstyle{C^{*}(E_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{2}^{*}}
\textstyle{C^{*}(F_{2})}

a pullback diagram in the category of C*-algebras and
*
-homomorphisms.

Proof.

Since
(\pi_{i}^{0},\pi^{1}_{i}):F_{i}\hookrightarrow E_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are admissible, the induced
*
-homomorphisms
\pi^{*}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, exist and are given
by (
5.2
). Next, since
f
and
f|
are morphisms in the category
RMIPG
, the induced
*
-homomorphisms
f_{*}
and
f|_{*}
exist by
Corollary
4.9
.

Let us check that (
5.7
) is a commutative diagram. Pick a vertex
v\in f^{-1}(\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0}))\subseteq E_{1}^{0}
. It follows that

\pi_{2}^{*}(f_{*}(P_{v}))=\pi_{2}^{*}(P_{f(v)})=P_{f(v)}
. Now, we conclude from (
5.5
) that
\pi_{1}^{*}(P_{v})=P_{v}
and,
consequently,
f|_{*}(\pi_{1}^{*}(P_{v}))=f|_{*}(P_{v})=P_{f(v)}
. On the other hand, if
v\notin f^{-1}(\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0}))
, then
\pi_{2}^{*}(f_{*}(P_{v}))=\pi_{2}^{*}(P_{f(v)})=0
.
Furthermore, since
f
restricts
to
f|
, we infer the inclusion
f(\pi_{1}^{0}(F_{1}^{0}))\subseteq\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0})
. Hence,

\pi_{1}^{0}(F^{0}_{1})\subseteq f^{-1}(\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0}))
, so
\pi_{1}^{*}(P_{v})=0
. Consequently,

(5.8)

(f|_{*}\circ\pi_{1}^{*})(P_{v})=0=(\pi_{2}^{*}\circ f_{*})(P_{v}).

An analogous argument shows that
\pi_{2}^{*}\circ f_{*}
agrees with
f|_{*}\circ\pi_{1}^{*}
on the elements
\{S_{e}\}_{e\in E^{1}}
.

To prove that (
5.7
) is a pullback diagram of C*-algebras, we check the conditions of
[
17
, Proposition 3.1]
. First, observe that, since
every vertex-simple loop of
E_{1}
has an exit and
f_{*}(P_{v})\neq 0
for all
v\in E_{1}^{0}
, the
*
-homomorphism
f_{*}
is injective
(e.g., see
[
21
, Corollary 1.3]
).
Combining this with surjectivity of
\pi^{*}_{1}
and
\pi^{*}_{2}
and the commutativity of the diagram (
5.7
),
we infer that it suffices to check whether

(5.9)

\ker(\pi_{2}^{*})\subseteq f_{*}(\ker(\pi_{1}^{*})).

Next, since
f_{*}(\ker(\pi_{1}^{*}))
is a closed vector space, we can reduce proving the above inclusion to showing that all spanning elements of both types
appearing in the second line of (
5
) are in
f_{*}(\ker(\pi_{1}^{*}))
.

By assumption, finite paths
\alpha
and
\beta
with
t_{PE_{2}}(\alpha)=t_{PE_{2}}(\beta)\in E_{2}^{0}\setminus\pi^{0}_{2}(F_{2}^{0})

are in the image of
f
,
so we can write
\alpha\beta^{*}=f\big(\widetilde{\alpha}\widetilde{\beta}^{*}\big)

and
S_{\alpha}S_{\beta}^{*}=f_{*}\big(S_{\widetilde{\alpha}\widetilde{\beta}^{*}}\big)
. Furthermore, as

(5.10)

f(\pi_{1}^{0}(F_{1}^{0}))\subseteq\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0}),

we infer that
f|_{*}(\pi_{1}^{*}(P_{t_{PE_{1}}(\widetilde{\alpha})}))=0
by the commutativity of the diagram.
However, since
f|_{*}
is injective on vertex projections, we conclude that

t_{PE_{1}}(\widetilde{\alpha})\notin\pi^{0}_{1}(F^{0}_{1})
, so
\pi_{1}^{*}(S_{\widetilde{\alpha}})=0
.
Hence,
S_{\widetilde{\alpha}}\in\ker\pi_{1}^{*}
, as needed.

Also by assumption, finite paths
\mu
and
\nu
with
t_{PE_{2}}(\mu)=t_{PE_{2}}(\nu)=w\in B_{E_{2}^{0}\setminus\pi_{2}^{0}(F_{2}^{0})}

are in the image of
f
. In particular, there exists
u\in E^{0}_{1}
such that
f(u)=w
. It follows from (
5.4
) that

u\in B_{E_{1}^{0}\setminus\pi_{1}^{0}(F_{1}^{0})}
.
The commutativity of the diagram combined with the regularity assumption for
f|
and (
5.6
) allows us to compute:

(5.11)

f_{*}\Big(P_{u}-\hskip-22.76219pt\sum_{y\in\pi^{1}_{1}\big((\pi^{0}_{1}\circ s_{F_{1}})^{-1}(u)\big)}\hskip-22.76219ptS_{y}S_{y}^{*}\Big)\quad=\quad P_{w}-\hskip-28.45274pt\sum_{x\in\pi^{1}_{2}\big((\pi^{0}_{2}\circ s_{F_{2}})^{-1}(w)\big)}\hskip-22.76219ptS_{x}S_{x}^{*}\quad\in C^{*}(E_{2}).

Therefore, for finite paths
\widetilde{\mu}
and
\widetilde{\nu}
such that
f(\widetilde{\mu})=\mu
and
f(\widetilde{\nu})=\nu
, we obtain

(5.12)

f_{*}\Big(S_{\widetilde{\mu}}\big(P_{u}-\hskip-22.76219pt\sum_{y\in\pi^{1}_{1}\big((\pi^{0}_{1}\circ s_{F_{1}})^{-1}(u)\big)}\hskip-22.76219ptS_{y}S_{y}^{*}\big)S_{\widetilde{\nu}}^{*}\Big)\quad=\quad S_{\mu}\big(P_{w}-\hskip-28.45274pt\sum_{x\in\pi^{1}_{2}\big((\pi^{0}_{2}\circ s_{F_{2}})^{-1}(w)\big)}\hskip-22.76219ptS_{x}S_{x}^{*}\big)S_{\nu}^{*}

and

(5.13)

\pi_{1}^{*}\Big(S_{\widetilde{\mu}}\big(P_{u}-\hskip-22.76219pt\sum_{y\in\pi^{1}_{1}\big((\pi^{0}_{1}\circ s_{F_{1}})^{-1}(u)\big)}\hskip-22.76219ptS_{y}S_{y}^{*}\big)S_{\widetilde{\nu}}^{*}\Big)\;=\;\pi_{1}^{*}(S_{\widetilde{\mu}})\pi_{1}^{*}\big(P_{u}-\hskip-22.76219pt\sum_{y\in\pi^{1}_{1}\big((\pi^{0}_{1}\circ s_{F_{1}})^{-1}(u)\big)}\hskip-22.76219ptS_{y}S_{y}^{*}\big)\pi_{1}^{*}(S_{\widetilde{\nu}}^{*})=0,

as required. Here the last step follows from the regularity of
(\pi^{0}_{1})^{-1}(u)
and the second
Cuntz–Krieger relation.
∎

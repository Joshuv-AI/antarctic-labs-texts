FLPR Model: Supervariable approach and a model for Hodge theory
Ansha S. Nair

Abstract




Abstract:

We procure the complete set of (anti-)BRST as well as (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations of the Friedberg-Lee-Pang-Ren (FLPR) model within the framework of the supervariable approach. Additionally, we capture the nilpotency and absolute anti-commutativity aspects of these symmetries, along with the invariance of the Lagrangian in terms of translational generators along the Grassmannian directions in the context of the supervariable approach. The anti-commutator of the (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetries generates a novel bosonic symmetry that retains the ghost part of the Lagrangian invariant. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the system under consideration respects the ghost
scale and discrete symmetries, in addition to other symmetries. We show that the generators of all these symmetries cling to the algebra obeyed by the de Rham cohomological operators of differential geometry - thus, the FLPR model presents a toy model for the Hodge theory.

PACS:
02.40.-k, 11.10.Ef, 11.30.-j

Keywords:
FLPR model; Supervariable approach; (anti-)BRST symmetries; (anti-)co-BRST symmetries; Hodge theory

1
Introduction

Gauge symmetries play a pivotal role in making the fundamental theories of nature comprehensible. These gauge theories are characterized by the presence of first-class constraints which are the generators of the gauge symmetries
[
1
,
2
]
. The Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin (BRST) formalism is one of the sophisticated ways of covariant quantization of gauge theories. Based on the BRST formalism, we rewrite a gauge invariant theory as a quantum theory that possesses off-shell nilpotent and absolutely anti-commuting global symmetries known as (anti-)BRST symmetries
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
. This quantum theory respects another set of off-shell nilpotent and absolutely anti-commuting symmetries called (anti-)co-BRST symmetries. The (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations leave the kinetic terms invariant in the Lagrangian whereas the gauge-fixing term remains invariant under the (anti-)co-BRST transformations.

The geometrical origin and interpretation of (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST transformations have been clearly elucidated using the supervariable approach
[
8
,
9
]
. In this approach, a
D
-dimensional gauge theory is generalized onto a
(D,2)
-dimensional theory parametrized by an additional pair of Grassmannian superspace variables
(\eta
,
\bar{\eta})
. By exploiting the (dual-)horizontality conditions together with the gauge invariant [or (anti-)BRST] restrictions and (anti-)co-BRST restrictions, we derive the proper (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations of the system. A wide variety of gauge systems such as the Christ-Lee model, rigid rotor, vector Schwinger model, particle on a torus knot and FLRW model have been studied within the framework of the supervariable approach
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

The Friedberg-Lee-Pang-Ren (FLPR) model is a
U(1)
gauge invariant solvable model of a single non-relativistic particle of unit mass with the characteristics of Gribov ambiguity
[
15
]
. This model is endowed with two first-class constraints
à la
Dirac formalism. A delineated BRST analysis of the model, by summing over all Gribov copies, was carried out in
[
16
]
. A quantization technique using physical projector operators, which abstain from the need of gauge fixing, was explored for the FLPR model in
[
17
]
. Recently, we performed Faddeev-Jackiw quantization and BRST analysis of this model using an admissible gauge condition
[
18
]
. Moreover, the presence of (anti-)co-BRST symmetries, in Cartesian coordinates, was demonstrated in
[
19
]
.

Our primary motive for the present endeavor is to derive the (anti-)BRST as well as (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations of the FLPR model by employing the supervariable approach. We also intend to provide a geometrical meaning to these fermionic symmetries. Second, we aspire to reveal the existence of novel symmetries of this model. Finally, yet importantly, we wish to establish that the algebra of these symmetry transformations (and their conserved charges), in the operator form, is analogous to the algebra obeyed by the de Rham cohomological operators of differential geometry. Thus, our aim is to establish the FLPR model as a toy model for the Hodge theory.

The structure of our manuscript is organised as follows. Section
2
briefly discusses the gauge invariant theory of the FLPR model. We also provide an account of the off-shell nilpotent and absolutely anti-commuting (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetries and their corresponding conserved charges. In section
3
, we derive the (anti-)BRST symmetries within the framework of supervariable approach and provide their geometrical interpretation in terms of the Grassmannian translational generators. A similar analysis is being performed in the case of (anti-)co-BRST symmetries in section
4
. In our subsequent section
5
, we recapitulate the off-shell nilpotency and absolute anti-commutativity of the (anti-)BRST as well as (anti-)co-BRST transformations in terms of the translational generators along the Grassmannian directions. Moreover, we capture the (anti-)BRST as well as (anti-)co-BRST invariance of the Lagrangian within the framework of supervariable approach. Our section
6
is devoted to the derivation of bosonic symmetry transformations. The continuous ghost scale and discrete symmetries are listed in section
7
. In section
8
, we demonstrate that the generators corresponding to the continuous symmetry transformations adhere to an algebra that is obeyed by the de Rham cohomological operators of the differential geometry. Finally, we summarize our results in section
9
.

2
Prelimineries: Novel Symmetries

The first-order gauge invariant Lagrangian describing the dynamics of FLPR model, in polar coordinates, is given by
[
18
]

L_{f}=P_{r}\,\dot{r}+P_{\theta}\,\dot{\theta}+P_{z}\,\dot{z}-\frac{1}{2}\,P_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\,P_{\theta}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\,P_{z}^{2}-\zeta\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)-V(r),

(1)

where
P_{r}
,
P_{\theta}
,
P_{z}
are canonical momenta corresponding to the generalised coordinates
{r}
,
{\theta}
and
{z}
, respectively. The
\zeta
is a gauge variable and
g
denotes the coupling constant. This Lagrangian respects
the following gauge symmetry transformations
(\delta_{g})
:

\begin{split}&{\delta}_{g}r=0,\qquad{\delta}_{g}P_{r}=0,\qquad{\delta}_{g}\theta=g\lambda(t),\\
&{\delta}_{g}P_{\theta}=0,\qquad{\delta}_{g}z=\lambda(t),\qquad{\delta}_{g}P_{z}=0,\qquad{\delta}_{g}\zeta=\dot{\lambda}(t),\end{split}

(2)

where
\lambda
is the time dependent gauge parameter. The BRST invariant first-order Lagrangian
(L_{b})
can be constructed by adding a BRST invariant function (i.e. gauge-fixing and Faddeev Popov ghost terms) into the first-order Lagrangian
L_{f}
in (
1
) (cf.
[
18
]
for details), as

\displaystyle{L}_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{r}\,\dot{r}+P_{\theta}\,\dot{\theta}+P_{z}\,\dot{z}-\frac{1}{2}\,p_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{2r^{2}}\,p_{\theta}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\,P_{z}^{2}-\zeta\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)

(3)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle V(r)+b\Big(\dot{\zeta}-z-\frac{1}{2}\,b\Big)+{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\,\dot{\cal{C}}+\bar{\cal{C}}\,{\cal{C}}.

Here,
(\bar{\cal{C}}){\cal{C}}
are the Faddeev-Popov (anti-)ghost variables and
b
is the Nakanishi-Lautrup auxiliary variable. The off-shell nilpotent and absolutely anti-commuting (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations
(s_{(a)b})
can be given as

\begin{split}&s_{b}r=0,\qquad s_{b}P_{r}=0,\qquad s_{b}\theta=g\,{\cal{C}},\qquad s_{b}P_{\theta}=0,\qquad s_{b}z={\cal{C}},\\
&s_{b}P_{z}=0,\qquad s_{b}\zeta=\dot{\cal{C}},\qquad s_{b}{\cal{C}}=0,\qquad s_{b}{\cal\bar{C}}=b,\qquad s_{b}b=0,\end{split}

(4)

and

\begin{split}&s_{ab}r=0,\qquad s_{ab}P_{r}=0,\qquad s_{ab}\theta=g\,{\cal\bar{C}},\qquad s_{ab}P_{\theta}=0,\qquad s_{ab}z={\cal\bar{C}},\\
&s_{ab}P_{z}=0,\qquad s_{ab}\zeta={\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}},\qquad s_{ab}{\cal\bar{C}}=0,\qquad s_{ab}{\cal{C}}=-b,\qquad s_{ab}b=0.\end{split}

(5)

The Lagrangian
(L_{b})
in (
3
) remains quasi-invariant under the above set of (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations as one can check

s_{b}L_{b}=\frac{d}{dt}\big(b\,\dot{\cal{C}}\big),\qquad s_{ab}L_{b}=\frac{d}{dt}\big(b\,\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}\big).

(6)

According to the Noether’s theorem, these continuous symmetries lead to the following conserved (anti-)BRST charges
(Q_{(a)b})

\displaystyle Q_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\,\dot{\cal C}+\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)\,{\cal{C}}\equiv b\,\dot{\cal C}-{\dot{b}}\,{\cal C},

\displaystyle Q_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\,{\dot{\bar{\cal C}}}+\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)\,\bar{\cal{C}}\equiv b\,{\dot{\bar{\cal C}}}-{\dot{b}}\,{\bar{\cal C}},

(7)

which are the generators of the (anti-)BRST transformations. These conserved charges are nilpotent of order two
(\text{i.e.}\;Q_{(a)b}^{2}=0)
and absolutely anti-commuting
(\text{i.e.}\;Q_{b}\,Q_{ab}+Q_{ab}\,Q_{b}=0)
in nature. The Lagrangian
(L_{b})
in (
3
) also respects other nilpotent and anti-commuting continuous symmetries known as (anti-)co-BRST symmetries or (anti-)dual-BRST symmetries. The following (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations
(s_{(a)d})

\begin{split}&s_{d}r=0,\qquad s_{d}P_{r}=0,\qquad s_{d}\theta=g\,{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}},\qquad s_{d}P_{\theta}=0,\qquad s_{d}z={\dot{\bar{\cal C}}},\\
&s_{d}P_{z}=0,\qquad s_{d}\zeta={\bar{\cal C}},\qquad s_{d}{\bar{\cal{C}}}=0,\qquad s_{d}{\cal C}=-\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big),\qquad s_{d}b=0,\end{split}

(8)

\begin{split}&s_{ad}r=0,\qquad s_{ad}P_{r}=0,\qquad s_{ad}\theta=g\,{\cal\dot{C}},\qquad s_{ad}P_{\theta}=0,\qquad s_{ad}z={\cal\dot{C}},\\
&s_{ad}P_{z}=0,\qquad s_{ad}\zeta={\cal{C}},\qquad s_{ad}{\cal\bar{C}}=\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big),\qquad s_{ad}{\cal{C}}=0,\qquad s_{ad}b=0,\end{split}

(9)

leave the Lagrangian
(L_{b})
quasi-invariant as:

\displaystyle s_{d}L_{b}=\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big(\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big){\dot{\bar{\cal C}}}\Big),\qquad s_{ad}L_{b}=\dfrac{d}{dt}\Big(\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)\dot{\cal{C}}\Big).

(10)

The conserved charges corresponding to the (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations are obtained as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\,{\bar{\cal{C}}}+\big(g\,P_{\theta}+P_{z}\big){\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\equiv b\,{\bar{\cal C}}-\dot{b}\,{\dot{\bar{\cal C}}},

\displaystyle Q_{ad}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\,{\cal C}+\big(g\,P_{\theta}+P_{z}\big){\dot{\cal{C}}}\equiv b\,{\cal C}-\dot{b}\,{\dot{\cal C}}.

(11)

These nilpotent and anti-commuting charges are the generators of the (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations. The (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST transformations differ primarily in a sense that (anti-)BRST transformations preserve the invariance of kinetic terms, whereas (anti-)co-BRST transformations leave the gauge-fixing term invariant.

3
(Anti-)BRST Symmetries: Supervariable Approach

In this section, we investigate the FLPR model within the framework of augmented supervariable approach, in which we utilize the horizontality condition together with the gauge invariant restrictions, to procure the complete set of (anti-)BRST symmetries of the system.
We begin with defining the exterior derivative
(d)
and a one-form connection
(\zeta^{(1)})
on
(0+1)
-dimensional ordinary space parametrized by time evolution parameter
t
as follows:

d=dt\,\partial_{t},\qquad\zeta^{(1)}=dt\,\zeta(t).

(12)

Thus, the action of exterior derivative on a one-form yields two-form

d\,\zeta^{(1)}=0,\qquad\text{since}\;\big(dt\wedge dt\big)=0.

(13)

Now, to make use of supervariable approach, we generalize the exterior derivative
(d)
and one-form
(\zeta^{(1)})
to their corresponding counterpart super-exterior derivative
(\widetilde{d})
and super-one-form
(\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)})
, respectively on the
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace parameterized by time evolution parameter
t
and a pair of Grassmannian variables
(\eta
and
\bar{\eta})
. Thus, on
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace, we have the following generalizations, namely;

\begin{split}&~~~\widetilde{d}=dt\,\partial_{t}+d\eta\,\partial_{\eta}+d\bar{\eta}\,\partial_{\bar{\eta}},\\
&\widetilde{{\zeta}}^{(1)}=dt\,{\Xi}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})+d\eta\,{\cal{\bar{F}}}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})+d\bar{\eta}\,{\cal{F}}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta}),\end{split}

(14)

where
\partial_{\eta}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}
and
\partial_{\bar{\eta}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}
are the Grassmannian derivatives, which are nilpotent of order two
({\text{i}.e.}\;\partial_{\eta}^{2}=\partial_{\bar{\eta}}^{2}=0)
and absolutely anti-commuting
({\text{i}.e.}\;\partial_{\eta}\partial_{\bar{\eta}}+\partial_{\bar{\eta}}\partial\eta=0)
. Expanding the components of super-one-form (or super multiplets) along the Grassmannian directions
\eta
and
\bar{\eta}
as

\begin{split}&{\Xi}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=\zeta(t)+\eta\,\bar{f_{1}}(t)+\bar{\eta}\,f_{1}(t)+\eta\bar{\eta}\,B(t),\\
&{\cal{F}}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})={\cal{C}}(t)+\eta\,{\bar{b}}_{1}(t)+\bar{\eta}\,b_{1}(t)+\eta\bar{\eta}\,s(t),\\
&{\cal{\bar{F}}}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})={\cal{\bar{C}}}(t)+\eta\,{\bar{b}}_{2}(t)+\bar{\eta}\,b_{2}(t)+\eta\bar{\eta}\,\bar{s}(t),\end{split}

(15)

where
f_{1}
,
\bar{f}_{1}
,
s
and
\bar{s}
are the fermionic secondary variables and
B
,
b_{1}
,
{\bar{b}}_{1}
,
b_{2}
and
{\bar{b}}_{2}
are bosonic secondary variables. Now, to express all supervariables in terms of the basic and auxiliary variables, we enforce the so called horizontality condition. The horizontality condition demands that the curvature two-form should be independent of the Grassmannian variables
\eta
and
\bar{\eta}
. i.e.,

\widetilde{d}\,\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)}=d\,\zeta^{(1)}.

(16)

Thus, the two-form in (1,2)-dimensional superspace can be written as

\displaystyle\widetilde{d}\,\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(dt\wedge d\eta)\big(\partial_{t}\bar{\cal{F}}-\partial_{\eta}\Xi\big)+(dt\wedge d\bar{\eta})\big(\partial_{t}{\cal{F}}-\partial_{\bar{\eta}}\Xi\big)

(17)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(d\eta\wedge d\bar{\eta})\big(\partial_{\eta}{\cal{F}}+\partial_{\bar{\eta}}\bar{\cal{F}}\big)-(d\eta\wedge d\eta)\partial_{\eta}\bar{\cal{F}}-(d\bar{\eta}\wedge d\bar{\eta})\partial_{\bar{\eta}}{\cal{F}}.

Using the horizontality condition (
16
) and expansion of supermultiplets (
15
), we obtain following expression for secondary variables in terms of the basic dynamical and auxiliary variables as

\begin{split}&\bar{f}_{1}={\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}},\qquad f_{1}=\dot{\cal{C}},\qquad b_{1}=0,\qquad b_{2}+{\bar{b}}_{1}=0,\\
&\dot{b}_{2}=B,\qquad\bar{b}_{2}=0,\qquad s=0,\qquad\bar{s}=0.\end{split}

(18)

Now, we make a choice
b_{2}=-{\bar{b}}_{1}=b
for the Nakanishi-Lautrup type auxiliary variable and substituting the above relations in (
15
) leads to the following desired expressions for the supervariables

\begin{split}&{\Xi}^{(h)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=\zeta(t)+\eta\,{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}+\bar{\eta}\,\dot{\cal{C}}+\eta{\bar{\eta}}\,{\dot{b}},\\
&{\cal{F}}^{(h)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})={\cal{C}}(t)-\eta\,b,\\
&{\cal{\bar{F}}}^{(h)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})={\cal{\bar{C}}}(t)+\bar{\eta}\,b,\end{split}

(19)

where the superscript
(h)
indicates that the superexpansions are being obtained after utilizing the horizontality condition. So far, we have attained the complete form of supervariables to procure the (anti-)BRST transformations for the variables
\zeta,{\cal{C}}
and
\bar{\cal{C}}
.

To procure the (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations for the dynamical variable
z
and
\theta
, we exploit the gauge invariant restrictions. Specifically, the gauge invariant quantities would be independent of the Grassmannian variables when generalized on superspace. To this end, the quantities
(\zeta-\dot{z})
and
(g\,\zeta-\dot{\theta})
remain invariant under the gauge transformations (cf. (
2
)) and it will accomplish our purpose of getting the off-shell nilpotent (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations of the variables
z
and
\theta
, respectively. Thus, we express these quantities in the language of differential forms as

\displaystyle\zeta^{(1)}-dz^{(0)}=dt\;\big(\zeta(t)-\partial_{t}z(t)\big),

\displaystyle g\,\zeta^{(1)}-d\theta^{(0)}=dt\;\big(g\zeta(t)-\partial_{t}\theta(t)\big),

(20)

where
\zeta^{(1)}
is a one-form and
z^{(0)}
,
\theta^{(0)}
define the zero-forms. Generalizing these one-form quantities onto (1,2)-dimensional superspace in the following fashion:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)}-\widetilde{d}\,\widetilde{z}^{(0)}=\zeta^{(1)}-dz^{(0)},

\displaystyle g\,\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)}-\widetilde{d}\,\widetilde{\theta}^{(0)}=g\zeta^{(1)}-d\theta^{(0)}.

(21)

These super-zero-forms can be expanded along the Grassmannian directions as

\displaystyle\widetilde{z}^{(0)}={\cal{Z}}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}})=z(t)+\eta\,\bar{f}_{2}(t)+\bar{\eta}f_{2}(t)+\eta\bar{\eta}\,\bar{B}(t),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\theta}^{(0)}=\Theta(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=\theta(t)+\eta\,\bar{f}_{3}(t)+\bar{\eta}\,f_{3}(t)+\eta\bar{\eta}\,b_{3}(t).

(22)

Here
f_{2}
,
\bar{f_{2}}
,
f_{3}
,
\bar{f_{3}}
are fermionic secondary variables and
\bar{B}
,
b_{3}
are bosonic secondary variables. The gauge [or (anti-)BRST] invariant quantities in (
3
) can be explicitly expressed as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)}-\widetilde{d}\,\widetilde{z}^{(0)}=dt\,\big(\Xi^{(h)}-\partial_{t}{\cal{Z}}\big)+d\eta\,\big(\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}-\partial_{\eta}{\cal{Z}}\big)+d\bar{\eta}\big({\cal{F}}^{(h)}-\partial_{\bar{\eta}}{\cal{Z}}\big),

\displaystyle g\,\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)}-\widetilde{d}\,\widetilde{\theta}^{(0)}=dt\,\big(g\,\Xi^{(h)}-\partial_{t}\Theta\big)+d\eta\,\big(g\,\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}-\partial_{\eta}\Theta\big)+d\bar{\eta}\,\big(g\,{\cal{F}}^{(h)}-\partial_{\bar{\eta}}\Theta\big).

(23)

Using Eqs. (
19
)–(
3
), we deduce the values of all secondary variables as follows:

\displaystyle f_{2}={\cal{C}},\qquad\bar{f_{2}}=\bar{{\cal{C}}},\qquad\bar{B}=b,

\displaystyle f_{3}=g\,{\cal{C}},\qquad\bar{f}_{3}=g\,\bar{{\cal{C}}},\qquad b_{3}=g\,b.

(24)

Thus, the super expansion for the supervaribles
{\cal{Z}}
and
\Theta
, can be respectively given as

\displaystyle{\cal{Z}}^{(h)}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}})=z(t)+\eta\,\bar{{\cal{C}}}(t)+\bar{\eta}\,{\cal{C}}(t)+\eta\bar{\eta}\,b(t),

\displaystyle\Theta^{(h)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=\theta(t)+g\eta\,\bar{{\cal{C}}}(t)+g\bar{\eta}\,{\cal{C}}(t)+g\eta\bar{\eta}\,b(t).

(25)

Moreover, the dynamical variables
r,P_{r},P_{\theta}\;\text{and}\;P_{z}
are gauge invariant. Therefore, these variables remain unaffected by the presence of the Grassmannian variables in superspace, i.e.;

\displaystyle{\cal{R}}^{(h)}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}})=r(t),\quad

\displaystyle{\cal{P}}_{r}^{(h)}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}})=P_{r}(t),

(26)

\displaystyle{\cal{P}}_{\theta}^{(h)}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}})=P_{\theta}(t),\quad

\displaystyle{\cal{P}}_{z}^{(h)}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}})=P_{z}(t).

In view of the above super-expansions, we can deduce the nilpotent and absolutely anti-commuting (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations for any generic variables
(\phi)
in the theory from its corresponding supervariables
\Phi^{(h)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})
as

\displaystyle s_{b}\phi(t)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\Phi^{(h)}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}})\bigg|_{\eta=0},\qquad s_{ab}\phi(t)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\Phi^{(h)}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}})\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0},

\displaystyle s_{b}s_{ab}\phi(t)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\Phi^{(h)}(t,\eta,{\bar{\eta}}).

(27)

These expressions elucidate that the (anti-)BRST symmetry transformation of any generic variable is equivalent to translation of corresponding supervariable in the direction of Grassmannian variable
(\eta)\bar{\eta}
on the
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace while keeping other direction fixed. Consequently, we derived the (anti-)BRST transformations for all the variables as listed in Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
), except for the Nakanishi-Lautrup variable
b
, via the supervariable approach. The transformations of latter variable can be obtained by the requirements of anti-commutativity and nilpotency of the (anti-)BRST transformations.

4
(Anti-)co-BRST Symmetries: Supervariable Approach

In this section, we derive the off-shell nilpotent and absolutely anti-commuting (anti-)co-BRST symmetries of the FLPR model by making use of the dual-horizontality condition together with the (anti-)co-BRST invariant restrictions. We notice that the total gauge-fixing terms i.e.
b\big(\dot{\zeta}-z\big)-\frac{b^{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\dot{\zeta}-z)^{2}
remain invariant under the (anti-)co-BRST transformations (cf. Eqs.(
8
) and (
9
)). In terms of differential forms, the term
(\dot{\zeta}-z)
can be written as

\delta\zeta^{(1)}-z^{(0)}=\dot{\zeta}(t)-z(t),

(28)

where
\delta=*\,d\,*
is the co-exterior derivative and
*
is the Hodge duality operation defined on one-dimensional ordinary space. Thus, we can say that the gauge-fixing term has its origin in the co-exterior derivative. The co-exterior derivative acting on a one-form
\zeta^{(1)}
results in

\delta\zeta^{(1)}=\big(*\,d\,*\big)\zeta^{(1)}=\dot{\zeta}(t).

(29)

In order to derive the complete set of (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations, we generalize the co-exterior derivative
(*\;d\;*)
and one-form
(\xi^{(1)})
to corresponding super-co-exterior derivative
(\star\;\tilde{d}\;\star)
and super-one-form
(\tilde{\xi}^{(1)})
on the
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace. Consequently, the dual-horizontality condition implies that

\big(\star\,\widetilde{d}\,\star\big)\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)}-\widetilde{z}^{(0)}=\big(*\,d\,*\big)\zeta^{(1)}-z^{(0)},

(30)

where
\star
is the Hodge duality operation defined on
(1,2)
-dimensional supermanifold. Now expanding in terms of supervariables, we obtain

\big(\partial_{t}\Xi+\partial_{\eta}\bar{{\cal{F}}}+\partial_{\bar{\eta}}{\cal{F}}\big)+S^{\eta\eta}\big(\partial_{\eta}{\cal{F}}\big)+S^{\bar{\eta}\bar{\eta}}\big(\partial_{\bar{\eta}}\bar{{\cal{F}}}\big)-{\cal{Z}}=\dot{\zeta}-z(t),

(31)

where
S^{\eta\eta}
,
S^{\bar{\eta}\bar{\eta}}
and
S^{\eta\bar{\eta}}
are symmetric in
\eta
and
\bar{\eta}
. Here we used the working principles for the Hodge duality
\star
operation on a
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace
[
10
,
20
]
;

\displaystyle\star\,dt=(d\eta\wedge d\bar{\eta}),\qquad\star\,(dt\wedge d\eta\wedge d\bar{\eta})=S^{\eta\bar{\eta}}=1,\qquad(dt\wedge dt\wedge d\eta)=0,

\displaystyle\star\,d\eta=(dt\wedge d\bar{\eta}),\qquad\star\,(dt\wedge d\eta\wedge d\eta)=S^{\eta\eta},\qquad\qquad(d\eta\wedge d\bar{\eta}\wedge d\bar{\eta})=0,

\displaystyle\star\,d\bar{\eta}=(dt\wedge d{\eta}),\qquad\star\,(dt\wedge d\bar{\eta}\wedge d\bar{\eta})=S^{\bar{\eta}\bar{\eta}},\qquad\qquad(d\eta\wedge d\eta\wedge d\bar{\eta})=0.

(32)

Exploiting the Eqs. (
15
) and (
31
) yields the following relationships:

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{f}}_{1}=\bar{f}_{2},\qquad\dot{f}_{1}=f_{2},\qquad\dot{B}=\bar{B},\qquad b_{1}=-\bar{b}_{2},

\displaystyle\bar{b}_{1}=0,\qquad\;\;b_{2}=0,\qquad\;\;s=0,\qquad\;\;\bar{s}=0.

(33)

Substituting these relations in Eqs. (
15
) and (
3
) and making the choice
b_{1}=-\bar{b}_{2}={\cal{B}}
, we obtain the super-expansion for the supervariables as

\displaystyle{\cal{Z}}^{(r)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z(t)+\eta\,\dot{\bar{f}}_{1}+\bar{\eta}\,\dot{f_{1}}+\eta\bar{\eta}\,\dot{B},

\displaystyle{\cal{F}}^{(r)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal{C}}(t)+\bar{\eta}\,{\cal{B}},

\displaystyle\bar{{\cal{F}}}^{(r)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\cal{C}}(t)-\eta\,{\cal{B}},

(34)

where superscript
(r)
denotes the reduced form of the supervariables. Nevertheless, we are unable to derive the super-expansion of the supervariable
\Theta(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})
. Accordingly, we exploit (anti-)co-BRST invariant term
(g\,\dot{\zeta}-\theta)
, which would be independent of the Grassmannian variables upon the generalization on superspace. Explicitly, it can be written as

g\big(\star\,\widetilde{d}\,\star\big)\,\widetilde{\zeta}^{(1)}-\widetilde{\theta}^{(0)}=g\big(*\,d\,*\big)\zeta^{(1)}-\theta^{(0)}.

(35)

Expanding using co-exterior derivative and supervariables, we have

g\big(\partial_{t}\Xi+\partial_{\eta}\bar{{\cal{F}}}+\partial_{\bar{\eta}}{\cal{F}}\big)+g\,S^{\eta\eta}\big(\partial_{\eta}{\cal{F}}\big)+g\,S^{\bar{\eta}\bar{\eta}}\big(\partial_{\bar{\eta}}\bar{{\cal{F}}}\big)-\Theta=g\,\dot{\zeta}-\theta(t),

(36)

where we have again used Eq. (

\displaystyle g\dot{\bar{f}}_{1}=\bar{f}_{3},\qquad g\dot{f}_{1}=f_{3},\qquad g\dot{B}=b_{3},\qquad b_{1}=-\bar{b}_{2},

\displaystyle\bar{b}_{1}=0,\qquad\quad b_{2}=0,\qquad\quad s=0,\qquad\quad\;\bar{s}=0.

(37)

Thus, we have derived the reduced form of supervariable
\Theta(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})

\Theta^{(r)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=\theta(t)+g\,\eta\,\dot{\bar{f}}_{1}+g\,\bar{\eta}\,\dot{f_{1}}+g\,\eta\bar{\eta}\,\dot{B}.

(38)

However, it is evident that we have not yet obtained the cherished super-expansion for the supervariables in terms of basic and auxiliary variables present in the theory. To obtain the exact super-expansion, we use the following (anti-)co-BRST invariant restrictions on the dynamical variables;

\displaystyle s_{(a)d}\big[\zeta\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)-\bar{\cal{C}}\,{\cal{C}}\big]=0.

(39)

We generalize the above (anti-)co-BRST invariant restriction on to
(1,2)
-dimensional supermanifold and demand that these restrictions must be independent of the Grassmannian variables
\eta
and

. Mathematically, they can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle\Xi\big({\cal{P}}_{z}+g\,{\cal{P}}_{\theta}\big)-\bar{\cal{F}}^{(r)}{\cal{F}}^{(r)}=\zeta\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)-\bar{\cal{C}}\,{\cal{C}}.

(1,2)
-dimensional supermanifold, i.e.;

\displaystyle{\cal R}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=r(t),\qquad{\cal P}_{r}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=P_{r}(t),

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{z}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=P_{z}(t),\qquad{\cal P}_{\theta}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=P_{\theta}(t).

(41)

Substituting the above super-expansion of supervariables listed in Eqs. (

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{1}\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)+{\cal{B}}\,{\cal{C}}=0,\quad f_{1}\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)+{\cal{B}}\,\bar{\cal{C}}=0,\quad B\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)+{\cal{B}}\,{\cal{B}}=0.

(42)

We notice that this is not sufficient to determine the secondary variables in terms of dynamic and auxiliary variables. Therefore, we invoke co-BRST and anti-co-BRST invariant restrictions:
s_{d}(\zeta\,\bar{\cal{C}})=0
and
s_{ad}(\zeta\,{\cal{C}})=0

(1,2)
-dimensional superspace as

\displaystyle\Xi\,\bar{\cal{F}}^{(r)}=\zeta\,\bar{\cal{C}},\qquad\Xi\,{\cal{F}}^{(r)}=\zeta\,{\cal{C}}.

This leads to the following set of expressions:

\displaystyle f_{1}\,\bar{\cal{C}}=0,\hskip 88.2037pt\bar{f_{1}}\,{\cal{C}}=0,

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{1}\,\bar{\cal{C}}-\zeta\,{\cal{B}}=0,\hskip 56.9055ptf_{1}\,{\cal{C}}+{\cal{B}}\,\zeta=0,

\displaystyle B\,\bar{\cal{C}}-f_{1}\,{\cal{B}}=0,\hskip 56.9055ptB\,{\cal{C}}-\bar{f}_{1}\,{\cal{B}}=0.

\displaystyle{\cal{Z}}^{(d)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})

5
Key Features of (Anti-)BRST and (Anti-)co-BRST Symmetries: Supervariable Approach

In this section, we encapsulate the key properties such as nilpotency and anti-commutativity of the (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations in terms of supervariables and Grassmannian translational generators. Moreover, we capture the invariance of the Lagrangian under (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations within the framework of the supervariable approach.

5.1
Nilpotency and Anti-commutativity

The Nilpotency of order two and absolute anti-commutativity are the two basic features of the (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations. In a
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace, the nilpotency property for any generic variable can be expressed in the following manner

\displaystyle s_{b}^{2}\phi(t)=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\Phi^{(h)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=0,

\displaystyle s_{ab}^{2}\phi(t)=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\Phi^{(h)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=0,

\displaystyle s_{d}^{2}\phi(t)=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\Phi^{(d)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=0,

\displaystyle s_{ad}^{2}\phi(t)=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\Phi^{(d)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=0,

(48)

where
\phi(t)
is any generic variable,
\Phi^{(h)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})
and
\Phi^{(d)}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})
are its corresponding supervariable [cf. Eqs.(
19
), (
3
), (
26
) and (
4
)]. In a similar fashion, the absolute anti-commutativity reads

\displaystyle\big(s_{b}s_{ab}+s_{ab}s_{b}\big)\phi(t)=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\bigg)\Phi^{h}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=0,

\displaystyle\big(s_{d}s_{ad}+s_{ad}s_{d}\big)\phi(t)=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\bigg)\Phi^{d}(t,\eta,\bar{\eta})=0.

(49)

In addition, we also explore the nilpotency and anti-commutativity of (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST charges and provide their geometrical interpretation in terms of Grassmannian translational generators. To begin with, the BRST and anti-BRST charges can be expressed in terms of BRST and/or anti-BRST transformations as

\displaystyle Q_{b}=s_{b}\big(\bar{\cal{C}}\,\dot{\cal{C}}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\,{\cal{C}}\big)=s_{ab}\big(\dot{\cal{C}}\,{\cal{C}}\big)=-s_{b}s_{ab}\big(\zeta\,{\cal{C}}\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\,s_{b}s_{ab}\big({\dot{z}}\,{\cal{C}}-z\,{\cal{\dot{C}}}\big),

\displaystyle Q_{ab}=-s_{ab}\big(\bar{\cal{C}}\,\dot{\cal{C}}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\,{\cal{C}}\big)=-s_{b}\big({\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\,\bar{\cal{C}}\big)=-s_{b}s_{ab}\big(\zeta\,\bar{\cal{C}}\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\,s_{b}s_{ab}\big({\dot{z}}\,{\bar{\cal{C}}}-z\,{\cal{\dot{\bar{C}}}}\big).

(50)

To provide an analogy in
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace, we have

\displaystyle Q_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\Big(\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}{\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(h)}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(h)}{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\bigg|_{\eta=0}\equiv\int d\bar{\eta}\;\Big(\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}{\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(h)}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(h)}{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\bigg|_{\eta=0}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big({\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(h)}\,{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}\equiv\int d\eta\,\Big({\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(h)}\,{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big(\Xi^{(h)}\,{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\equiv-\int d\bar{\eta}\int d\eta\,\Big(\Xi^{(h)}\,{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big({\dot{\cal Z}}^{(h)}{\cal F}^{(h)}-{\cal Z}^{(h)}{\dot{\cal F}}^{(h)}\Big)\equiv-\dfrac{1}{2}\int d{\bar{\eta}}\int d\eta\Big({\dot{\cal Z}}^{(h)}{\cal F}^{(h)}-{\cal Z}^{(h)}{\dot{\cal F}}^{(h)}\Big),

and

\displaystyle Q_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\Big(\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}{\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(h)}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(h)}{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}\equiv-\int d{\eta}\;\Big(\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}{\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(h)}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(h)}{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\Big({\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(h)}\bar{{\cal{F}}}^{(h)}\Big)\bigg|_{\eta=0}\equiv-\int d\bar{\eta}\,\Big({\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(h)}\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\bigg|_{\eta=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big(\Xi^{(h)}\,\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)\equiv-\int d\bar{\eta}\int d\eta\,\Big(\Xi^{(h)}\,\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\,\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big({\dot{\cal Z}}^{(h)}{\bar{\cal F}}^{(h)}-{\cal Z}^{(h)}{\dot{\bar{\cal F}}}^{(h)}\Big)\equiv-\dfrac{1}{2}\int d{\bar{\eta}}\int d\eta\Big({\dot{\cal Z}}^{(h)}{\bar{\cal F}}^{(h)}-{\cal Z}^{(h)}{\dot{\bar{\cal F}}}^{(h)}\Big).\qquad

It is noteworthy that, we can directly prove from the above expressions that
s_{b}Q_{b}=0
and

, which sequentially imply the nilpotency of (anti-)BRST charges
({\text{i}.e.}\;Q_{b}^{2}=Q_{ab}^{2}=0)

and
s_{ab}Q_{b}=0
lead to the anti-commutativity
({\text{i}.e.}\;Q_{b}Q_{ab}+Q_{ab}Q_{b}=0)
of the charges. Consequently, we write the nilpotency and anti-commutativity of (anti-)BRST charges in terms of the Grassmannian translational generators in the
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}Q_{b}=s_{b}Q_{b}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Q_{b}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}Q_{ab}=s_{ab}Q_{ab}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Q_{ab}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}Q_{ab}=s_{b}Q_{ab}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Q_{b}\,Q_{ab}+Q_{ab}\,Q_{b}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}Q_{b}=s_{ab}Q_{b}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Q_{b}\,Q_{ab}+Q_{ab}\,Q_{b}=0.

(53)

In an exact similar fashion, we can demonstrate the nilpotency and absolute anti-commutativity of the (anti-)co-BRST charges. In a one-dimensional space, (anti)-co-BRST charges takes the following form

\displaystyle Q_{d}=-s_{d}\big(\bar{\cal{C}}\,\dot{\cal{C}}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}\,{\cal{C}}}\big)=-s_{ad}\big({\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\,\bar{\cal{C}}\big)=s_{d}s_{ad}\big(z\,\bar{\cal{C}}\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\,s_{d}s_{ad}\big(\zeta\,\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}-\dot{\zeta}\,\bar{\cal{C}}\big),

\displaystyle Q_{ad}=s_{ad}\big(\bar{\cal{C}}\,\dot{\cal{C}}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}\,{\cal{C}}}\big)=s_{d}\big({\dot{{\cal{C}}}}\,{\cal{C}}\big)=s_{d}s_{ad}\big(z\,{\cal{C}}\big)=-\frac{1}{2}\,s_{d}s_{ad}\big(\zeta\,\dot{{\cal{C}}}-\dot{\zeta}\,{\cal{C}}\big).

(54)

These co-BRST and anti-co-BRST charges can be expressed in terms of the Grassmannian translational generators in the superspace as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\Big(\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}{\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(d)}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\bigg|_{\eta=0}\equiv-\int d\bar{\eta}\,\Big(\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}{\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(d)}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\bigg|_{\eta=0}

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big({\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}\equiv-\int d\eta\,\Big({\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big({\cal{Z}}^{(d)}\,\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\equiv\int d\bar{\eta}\int d\eta\,\Big({\cal{Z}}^{(d)}\,\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big({\Xi}^{(d)}\,{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}-{\dot{\Xi}}^{(d)}\,\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\int d\bar{\eta}\int d\eta\,\Big({\Xi}^{(d)}\,{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}-{\dot{\Xi}}^{(d)}\,\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big),\qquad

and

\displaystyle Q_{ad}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\Big(\bar{\cal F}^{(d)}{\dot{\cal F}}^{(d)}-{\dot{\bar{\cal F}}}^{(d)}{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}\equiv\int d{\eta}\;\Big(\bar{\cal{F}}^{(d)}{\dot{\cal{F}}}^{(d)}-{\dot{\bar{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\Big({\dot{{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\bigg|_{\eta=0}\equiv\int d\bar{\eta}\;\Big({\dot{{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\bigg|_{\eta=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big({\cal{Z}}^{(d)}{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\equiv\int d\bar{\eta}\int d\eta\,\Big({\cal{Z}}^{(d)}{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big({\Xi}^{(d)}\,{\dot{{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}-{\dot{\Xi}}^{(d)}\,{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big)\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\int d\bar{\eta}\int d\eta\,\Big({\Xi}^{(d)}\,{\dot{{\cal{F}}}}^{(d)}-{\dot{\Xi}}^{(d)}\,{\cal{F}}^{(d)}\Big).\qquad

It is straightforward to illustrate the nilpotency and absolute anti-commutativity of (anti-)co-BRST charges within the framework of the supervariable approach as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}Q_{d}=s_{d}Q_{d}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Q_{d}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}Q_{ad}=s_{ad}Q_{ad}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Q_{ad}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}Q_{ad}=s_{d}Q_{ad}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Q_{d}\,Q_{ad}+Q_{ad}\,Q_{d}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}Q_{d}=s_{ad}Q_{d}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;Q_{d}\,Q_{ad}+Q_{ad},Q_{d}=0,

(57)

where we have used the nilpotency
(\partial_{\eta}^{2}=\partial_{\bar{\eta}}^{2}=0)
and anti-commutativity
(\partial_{\eta}\partial_{\bar{\eta}}+\partial_{\bar{\eta}}\partial_{\eta}=0)
of the Grassmannian derivatives
\partial_{\eta}
and
\partial_{\bar{\eta}}

5.2
Invariance of the Lagrangian

In this subsection, we consider the invariance of the Lagrangian in the language of Grassmannian translational generators
(\partial_{\eta},\partial_{\bar{\eta}})
. We begin by generalizing the first-order gauge invariant Lagrangian
L_{f}
on to
(1,2)

\displaystyle L_{f}\longrightarrow{\cal{L}}_{f}

(58)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\Xi^{(h)}\big(g\,P_{\theta}+P_{z}\big)-V(r)=L_{f}.

This Lagrangian is independent of the Grassmannian variables, which leads to the (anti-)BRST invariance of the Lagrangian in supersace. More precisely

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}{\cal{L}}_{f}\bigg|_{\eta=0}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;s_{b}L_{f}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}{\cal{L}}_{f}\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;s_{ab}L_{f}=0.

(1,2)

{\cal{L}}_{b}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}{\cal{L}}_{b}\bigg|_{\eta=0}=\frac{d}{dt}\big(b\,{\dot{\cal{C}}}\big)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;s_{b}L_{b}=\frac{d}{dt}\big(b\,{\dot{\cal{C}}}\big),

(61)

We represent the (anti-)BRST invariant super-Lagrangian in following different ways

\displaystyle{\cal{L}}_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal{L}}_{f}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}\Big[\bar{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big({\dot{\Xi}}^{(h)}-{\cal{Z}}^{(h)}-\frac{1}{2}\,b\Big)\Big],

(62)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal{L}}_{f}-\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}\Big[{\cal{F}}^{(h)}\Big({\dot{\Xi}}^{(h)}-{\cal{Z}}^{(h)}-\frac{1}{2}\,b\Big)\Big].

This can be encoded in terms of the Grassmannian translational generators as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}{\cal{L}}_{b}\bigg|_{\eta=0}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;s_{b}L_{b}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}{\cal{L}}_{b}\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}=0\;\Longleftrightarrow\;s_{ab}L_{b}=0,

(63)

where we have used the nilpotency property
(\partial_{\eta}^{2}=0,\partial_{\bar{\eta}}^{2}=0)
of the Grassmannian derivatives.

\displaystyle+

which is unaffected by the presence of Grassmannian variables in superspace. Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\eta}}{\cal{L}}_{b}\bigg|_{\eta=0}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\big(P_{z}+gP_{\theta}\big)\,{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\Big)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;s_{d}L_{b}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\big(P_{z}+gP_{\theta}\big)\,{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial{\eta}}{\cal{L}}_{b}\bigg|_{\bar{\eta}=0}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\big(P_{z}+gP_{\theta}\big)\,{\dot{\cal{C}}}\Big)\;\Longleftrightarrow\;s_{ad}L_{b}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\big(P_{z}+gP_{\theta}\big)\,{\dot{\cal{C}}}\Big).

Bosonic Symmetries

In this section, in addition to the fermionic symmetries, we explore the possibility of bosonic symmetries. To this end, we observed that the anti-commutator between BRST and co-BRST as well as anti-BRST
and anti-co-BRST lead to unique novel symmetries of the system - known as bosonic symmetries. Specifically, we interpret bosonic symmetry in terms of fermionic symmetry, as

\{s_{b},s_{d}\}=s_{w},\qquad\{s_{ab},s_{ad}\}=s_{\bar{w}},

(66)

where
s_{w},s_{\bar{w}}
denote the bosonic symmetries. Moreover, the following anti-commutators are identically zero

\displaystyle\{s_{b},s_{ab}\}=0,\qquad\{s_{d},s_{ad}\}=0,\qquad\{s_{b},s_{ad}\}=0,\qquad\{s_{d},s_{ab}\}=0.

(67)

Thus, the bosonic symmetry transformations, for the variables of the system, can be explicitly given as below:

\displaystyle s_{w}r=0,\qquad s_{w}P_{r}=0,\qquad s_{w}\theta=g{\dot{b}}-g(P_{z}+gP_{\theta}),\qquad s_{w}P_{\theta}=0,

\displaystyle s_{w}z=\dot{b}-(P_{z}+gP_{\theta}),\qquad s_{w}P_{z}=0,\qquad s_{w}\zeta=b-(\dot{P}_{z}+g\dot{P_{\theta}}),

\displaystyle s_{w}{\bar{\cal{C}}}=0,\qquad s_{w}{\cal{C}}=0,\qquad s_{w}b=0,

(68)

and

\displaystyle s_{\bar{w}}r=0,\qquad s_{\bar{w}}P_{r}=0,\qquad s_{\bar{w}}\theta=-g{\dot{b}}+g(P_{z}+gP_{\theta}),\qquad s_{\bar{w}}P_{\theta}=0,

\displaystyle s_{\bar{w}}z=-\dot{b}+(P_{z}+gP_{\theta}),\qquad s_{\bar{w}}P_{z}=0,\qquad s_{\bar{w}}\zeta=-b+(\dot{P}_{z}+g\dot{P_{\theta}}),

\displaystyle s_{\bar{w}}{\bar{\cal{C}}}=0,\qquad s_{\bar{w}}{\cal{C}}=0,\qquad s_{\bar{w}}b=0.

(69)

From Eqs.(
6
) and (
6
), we observe that
s_{w}+s_{\bar{w}}=0
, which implies that bosonic symmetries are dependent on each other, i.e.:

\{s_{b},s_{d}\}=s_{w}=-\{s_{ab},s_{ad}\}.

(70)

The first-order Lagrangian given in Eq.(
3
) remains quasi-invariant under these symmetry transformations as

\displaystyle s_{w}L_{b}=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\dot{b}(P_{z}+gP_{\theta})-b(\dot{P}_{z}+g\dot{P_{\theta}})\Big)=-s_{\bar{w}}L_{b}.

(71)

The corresponding conserved bosonic charges, derived using Noether’s theorem,

\displaystyle Q_{w}=b^{2}-\big(P_{z}+g\,P_{\theta}\big)^{2}=-Q_{\bar{w}},

(72)

are generators of bosonic transformations
s_{w}
and
s_{\bar{w}}
, respectively.

Ghost Scale and Discrete Symmetries

In addition to the four fermionic and two bosonic symmetries, we observe that the Lagrangian respects another set of symmetry referred as ghost scale symmetry, as listed below

\displaystyle{\cal{C}}\rightarrow e^{+1.\Lambda}{\cal{C}},\qquad\bar{{\cal{C}}}\rightarrow e^{-1.\Lambda}\bar{{\cal{C}}},

\displaystyle(r,P_{r},\theta,P_{\theta},z,P_{z},\zeta,b)\rightarrow e^{0.\Lambda}(r,P_{r},\theta,P_{\theta},z,P_{z},\zeta,b),

(73)

where
\Lambda
is the global independent scale parameter. The numerals in the exponential refer to the ghost number of the variables,
(\bar{\cal{C}}){\cal{C}}
has ghost number
(-1)+1
whereas rest of the variables have zero ghost number. Making
\Lambda
infinitesimal, we outline the above transformations as:

\displaystyle s_{g}{\cal{C}}={\cal{C}},\qquad s_{g}\bar{\cal{C}}=-\bar{\cal{C}},\qquad

\displaystyle s_{g}(r,P_{r},\theta,P_{\theta},z,P_{z},\zeta,b)=0.

(74)

The Lagrangian in Eq.(
3
) remains unaffected by these symmetry transformations. Furthermore, we obtain the following conserved charge

Q_{g}=\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}\,{\cal{C}}-\bar{\cal{C}}\,\dot{\cal{C}}.

(75)

It is straightforward to prove the conservation of the ghost scale charge
(Q_{g})
using Euler-Lagrange equations of motion and the charge
Q_{g}
is the generator of the infinitesimal continuous symmetry transformation in Eq.(
74
).

In addition, the ghost sector of the Lagrangian
L_{b}
in Eq.(
3
) also remains invariant under the following discrete symmetries

{\cal{C}}\rightarrow\pm{\bar{\cal{C}}},\qquad{\bar{\cal{C}}}\rightarrow\mp{\cal{C}}.

(76)

These discrete symmetries help us obtain the (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST transformations from BRST and co-BRST transformations respectively.

8
Extended BRST Algebra and Cohomological Aspects

So far, we have derived six continuous symmetries of the FLPR model. We now demonstrate a connection between conserved charges corresponding to continuous symmetry transformations and de Rham cohomological operators of differential geometry
[
11
,
21
]
.
The conserved charges corresponding to the respective symmetries satisfy the following algebra

\displaystyle Q_{(a)b}^{2}=0,\qquad Q_{(a)d}^{2}=0,\qquad\{Q_{b},Q_{ab}\}=0,\qquad\{Q_{d},Q_{ad}\}=0,

\displaystyle\{Q_{b},Q_{ad}\}=0,\qquad\{Q_{ab},Q_{d}\}=0,\qquad[Q_{g},Q_{b}]=Q_{b},\qquad[Q_{g},Q_{ab}]=-Q_{ab},

\displaystyle\left[Q_{g},Q_{d}\right]=-Q_{d},\qquad\left[Q_{g},Q_{ad}\right]=Q_{ad},\qquad\{Q_{b},Q_{d}\}=-\{Q_{ab},Q_{ad}\}=Q_{{w}},

\displaystyle\left[Q_{w},Q_{r}\right]=0,\qquad r=b,ab,d,ad,g,

(77)

here we used the canonical brackets
[r,P_{r}]=i,[\theta,P_{\theta}]=i,[z,P_{z}]=i,[\zeta,b]=i,\{{\cal{C}},{\dot{\bar{\cal{C}}}}\}=-1
and
\{\dot{\cal{C}},\bar{\cal{C}}\}=1
.
The algebraic structures in Eq.(
8
) are redolent of the algebra of the de Rham cohomological operators of differential geometry. To be precise, these operators comprise the exterior derivative
(d)
, co-exterior derivative
(\delta)
and Laplacian operator
(\Delta)
and the algebra satisfied amongst them is explicitly given as
[
22
]

d^{2}=0,\qquad\delta^{2}=0,\qquad\{d,\delta\}=\Delta,\qquad\left[\Delta,d\right]=0,\qquad\left[\Delta,\delta\right]=0.

(78)

It is noteworthy that the algebra given in Eq.(
8
) and (
78
) entails an exact mapping between cohomological operators and conserved charges (and their corresponding symmetry transformations). More precisely, from the nilpotency of (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST charges (their corresponding symmetry transformations), we can map them to exterior derivative or co-exterior derivative. Moreover, the anti-commutator
\{Q_{b},Q_{d}\}=Q_{w}
is correlated to anti-commutator of the cohomological operators
\{d,\delta\}=\Delta
, which evokes the mapping of conserved charges
Q_{b},Q_{d},Q_{w}
to the exterior derivative, co-exterior derivative and Laplacian operator, respectively. Therefore, we have the following two-to-one mapping between the conserved charges and cohomological operators:

\displaystyle(Q_{b},Q_{ad})\longrightarrow d,\qquad(Q_{ab},Q_{d})\longrightarrow\delta,\qquad(Q_{w},-Q_{w})\longrightarrow\Delta.

(79)

As a result, the continuous symmetry transformations and their corresponding conserved charges furnish the physical realization of abstract mathematical quantities such as de Rham cohomological operators. Thus, the theory of the FLPR model brings forth a toy model for the Hodge theory.

9
Conclusions

In the present study, we have derived the off-shell nilpotent and absolutely anti-commuting (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations within the framework of the supervariable approach. We generalized the one dimensional ordinary space onto
(1,2)
-dimensional superspace and exploited the horizontality condition along with the gauge invariant restrictions to derive the (anti-)BRST symmetry transformations, whereas dual horizontality condition together with the (anti-)co-BRST invariant restrictions have been exploited to obtain the (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations. We have established a relation to procure the (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetries of any generic variable from its corresponding supervariable in superspace. Moreover, we provided a geometrical interpretation of the key features of these symmetries such as nilpotency and anti-commutativity in terms of the translational generators
\partial_{\eta}
and
\partial_{\bar{\eta}}
. The invariance of Lagrangian is also captured with the aid of supervariables in the superspace.

Furthermore, we have demonstrated that the (anti-)BRST invariant Lagrangian respects another novel symmetry known as bosonic symmetry, which is derived from the anti-commutator of (anti-)BRST and (anti-)co-BRST symmetry transformations. We also procured the ghost scale and discrete symmetry transformations and corresponding charges. Subsequently, we established that all these symmetry transformations and their generators adhere to a mathematically abstract algebra which is analogous to the algebra obeyed by the de Rham cohomological operators of differential geometry. Thus, we presented the FLPR model as a prototype for the Hodge theory.

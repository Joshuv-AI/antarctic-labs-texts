The tropical critical points of an affine matroid
Federico Ardila-Mantilla, Christopher Eur, Raul Penaguiao

Abstract
We prove that the number of tropical critical points of an affine matroid ( M , e ) (M,e) is equal to the beta invariant of M M . Motivated by the computation of maximum likelihood degrees, this number is defined to be the degree of the intersection of the Bergman fan of ( M , e ) (M,e) and the inverted Bergman fan of N = ( M / e ) ⟂ N=(M/e)^{\perp} , where e e is an element of M M that is neither a loop nor a coloop. Equivalently, for a generic weight vector w w on E − e E-e , this is the number of ways to find weights ( 0 , x ) (0,x) on M M and y y on N N with x + y = w x+y=w such that on each circuit of M M (resp. N N ), the minimum x x -weight (resp. y y -weight) occurs at least twice. This answers a question of Sturmfels.

2.1
The lattice of set partitions

A set partition

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell}\}

We write

\boldsymbol{\lambda}
.
If

We define the

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\textrm{span}\{{\mathsf{e}}_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,{\mathsf{e}}_{\lambda_{\ell}}\}\,\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\mathsf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{E}\,\,|\,x_{i}=x_{j}\text{ whenever }i,j\text{ are in the same block of }\boldsymbol{\lambda}\},

The map

If

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\models[0,n]
and
\boldsymbol{\mu}\models[n]
be set partitions and
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
be their intersection graph.

1.

If

has a cycle, then
\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})|_{x_{0}=0}\cap(\mathsf{w}-\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))=\emptyset

2.

If

is disconnected, then
\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})|_{x_{0}=0}\cap(\mathsf{w}-\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
is not a point for any

3.

If
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is a tree, then
\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})|_{x_{0}=0}\cap(\mathsf{w}-\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
is a point for any
\mathsf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{x}\in\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
and
\mathsf{y}\in\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
such that
\mathsf{x}+\mathsf{y}=\mathsf{w}
.
Write
x_{\lambda(i)}\coloneqq x_{i}
and
y_{\mu(i)}\coloneqq y_{i}
for

.
The subspace

\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})|_{x_{0}=0}\cap(\mathsf{w}-\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
,
can be naturally regarded as living in
\mathbb{R}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\sqcup\boldsymbol{\mu}}
, where it
is cut out by the equalities

\displaystyle x_{\lambda(i)}+y_{\mu(i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{i}\qquad\textrm{ for }i\in[n],

\displaystyle x_{\lambda(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

This system has
n+1
equations and
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|+|\boldsymbol{\mu}|
independent unknowns.
The linear dependences among these equations are controlled by the cycles of the graph
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
. More precisely, the first
n
linear functionals
\{x_{\lambda(i)}+y_{\mu(i)}\,:\,i\in[n]\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\sqcup\boldsymbol{\mu}}
give a realization of the graphical matroid of
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
. The last equation is clearly linearly independent from the others.

If
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
has a cycle with edges

in that order, then the above equalities imply that
w_{i_{1}}-w_{i_{2}}+w_{i_{3}}-\cdots-w_{i_{2k}}=0
. For generic
\mathsf{w}
this equation does not hold, so we have
\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})|_{x_{0}=0}\cap(\mathsf{w}-\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))=\emptyset
.

If
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is disconnected, let

be the set of edges in a connected component not containing the vertex
\lambda(0)
. If
\mathsf{x}\in\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})

\mathsf{y}\in\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu})

and

, then
\mathsf{x}+r\mathsf{e}_{A}\in\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})
and
\mathsf{y}-r\mathsf{e}_{A}\in\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
also satisfy those equations for any real number

. Therefore
\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\lambda})|_{x_{0}=0}\cap(\mathsf{w}-\mathsf{L}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
is not a point.

Finally, if
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is a tree, then its number of vertices is one more than the number of edges, that is,
n+1=|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|+|\boldsymbol{\mu}|
, so the system of equations has equally many equations and unknowns. Also, these equations are linearly independent since
\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}}
is a tree. It follows that the system has a unique solution.
∎

[-\Sigma_{(M/e)^{\perp}}]^{T}=c_{r}^{T}(\mathcal{S}^{\vee}_{M/e\,\oplus\,U_{0,e}})
, or explicitly,

[\Sigma_{(M,e)}]^{T}_{\sigma}=\prod_{i\in E\setminus B_{\sigma}(M)}(-t_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad[-\Sigma_{(M/e)^{\perp}}]^{T}_{\sigma}={\prod_{i\in B_{\sigma}(M/e\,\oplus\,U_{0,e})}}t_{i}\quad\text{for all $\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{E}$}.

Then, their images in the quotient
A^{\bullet}(\Sigma_{E})
are exactly
[\Sigma_{(M,e)}]
and
[-\Sigma_{(M/e)^{\perp}}]
, respectively.

Proof.

The first equality is a restatement of
[
BEST21
, Theorem 7.6]
when one notes that the choice of
e\in E
induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{R}^{E}/\mathbb{R}(1,\ldots,1)\simeq\mathbb{R}^{E-e}
. The second statement also follows from that theorem when one combines it with
[
BEST21
, Propositions 5.11, 5.13]
, which describe how tautological Chern classes behave with respect to matroid duality and direct sums, respectively.
∎

Proof of
1.2
.2.

We begin with

[
BEST21
, Theorem 6.2]
which states that

\deg_{\Sigma_{E}}\big([\Sigma_{(M,e)}]\cdot c_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee})\big)=\beta(M).

In light of (
3
), the desired statement
\deg(\Sigma_{(M,e)}\cap-\Sigma_{(M/e)^{\perp}})=\beta(M)
will follow once we show that
[\Sigma_{(M,e)}]\cdot\big(c_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee})-[-\Sigma_{(M/e)^{\perp}}]\big)
= 0 in
A^{\bullet}(\Sigma_{E})
.

Towards this end, we look at the distinguished representative of this product in
A_{T}^{\bullet}(\Sigma_{E})
, and
show that the variable
t_{e}
divides
[\Sigma_{(M,e)}]_{\sigma}^{T}\cdot\big(c^{T}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee})_{\sigma}-[-\Sigma_{(M/e)^{\perp}}]^{T}_{\sigma}\big)
for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{E}
, as follows.

•

If
e\notin B_{\sigma}(M)
, then
\displaystyle[\Sigma_{(M,e)}]^{T}_{\sigma}=\prod_{i\in E\setminus B_{\sigma}(M)}(-t_{i})
is divisible by
t_{e}
.

•

If
e\in B_{\sigma}(M)
, then
B_{\sigma}(M/e\oplus U_{0,e})=B_{\sigma}(M)\setminus e
, and hence

\displaystyle c^{T}_{r}(\mathcal{S}_{M}^{\vee})_{\sigma}-[-\Sigma_{(M/e)^{\perp}}]^{T}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Elem}_{r}(\{t_{k}\,:\,k\in B_{\sigma}(M))-\prod_{j\in B_{\sigma}(M)\setminus e}t_{j}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in B_{\sigma}(M)}\Big(\prod_{j\in B_{\sigma}(M)\setminus i}t_{j}\Big)-\prod_{j\in B_{\sigma}(M)\setminus e}t_{j}

∎

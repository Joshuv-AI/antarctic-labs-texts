Sofic actions on graphs
David Gao, Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, Gregory Patchell

Abstract
. We develop a theory of soficity for actions on graphs and obtain new applications to the study of sofic groups. We establish various examples, stability and permanence properties of sofic actions on graphs, in particular soficity is preserved by taking several natural graph join operations. We prove that an action of a group on its Cayley graph is sofic if and only if the group is sofic. We show that arbitrary actions of amenable groups on graphs are sofic. Using a graph theoretic result of E. Hrushovski, we also show that arbitrary actions of free groups on graphs are sofic. Notably we show that arbitrary actions of sofic groups on graphs, with amenable stabilizers, are sofic, settling completely an open problem from [ GKEP24 ] . We also show that soficity is preserved by taking limits under a natural Gromov-Hausdorff topology, generalizing prior work of the first author [ Gao24 ] . Our work sheds light on a family of groups called graph wreath products, simultaneously generalizing graph products and generalized wreath products. Extending various prior results in this direction including soficity of generalized wreath products [ GKEP24 ] , B. Hayes and A. Sale [ HS18 ] , and soficity of graph products [ CHR14 , CC21 ] , we show that graph wreath products are sofic if the action and acting groups are sofic. These results provide several new examples of sofic groups in a systematic manner.

Introduction

This work builds a theory of soficity for group actions in the general setting of actions on graphs, significantly extending our previous work
[
GKEP24
]
, and also settles certain important problems left open there. Our work has applications to the study of sofic groups, in particular in identifying new examples of sofic groups which is a very timely and interesting activity due to the utility in resolution of certain important problems and conjectures
[

,

,

,

,

,

]
. The main results of this paper are listed as Theorems in the introduction.

Our definition of soficity for group actions on graphs uses the concept of orbit approximations introduced in
[
GKEP24
]
adapted to consider graph embeddings. In this paper graphs
\Gamma=(V,E)
will be understood to be undirected and with no multi-edges and no self-loops. Vertices with infinite degree are permitted. In other words,
E\subset V\times V
is any symmetric relation that excludes the diagonal. The graph complement
\Gamma^{c}
has vertex set
V
and edge set
E^{c}
where
E^{c}=\{(v,w):v\neq w\text{ and }(v,w)\not\in E\}
. A graph embedding
\pi:\Gamma_{1}=(V_{1},E_{2})\to\Gamma_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
is an injection from
V_{1}
to
V_{2}
such that for all
v,w\in V_{1},

(v,w)\in E_{1}
if and only if
(\pi(v),\pi(w))\in E_{2}
. The group of automorphisms of
\Gamma
will be denoted
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
and will consist of all surjective graph embeddings
\pi:\Gamma\to\Gamma.
An action of a group
G
on a graph
\Gamma
is a homomorphism of
G
to
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma).

In the following,
d
, when denoting a metric on a symmetric group of a finite set, shall always be understood to be the normalized Hamming distance, unless specified otherwise. The following is our finitary definition of sofic actions on graphs:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group,
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a countable graph,
\alpha:G\curvearrowright\Gamma
be an action,

be a map (not necessarily a homomorphism):

(1)

is called

\varphi(1_{G})=1
;

(2)

For a finite subset

and
\epsilon>0
,

-

;

(3)

For finite subsets

,

, and
\epsilon>0
,

is called an
(F,W,\epsilon)
-

and for each

,

,

(4)

Recall that

(5)

\epsilon>0

The infinitary definition is based on a commutative diagram in an ultraproduct construction involving the random graph on

Permanence properties of sofic actions on graphs

We document several basic and intuitive permamence results about sofic actions on graphs (for proofs see Proposition

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright\Gamma=(V,E)

(1)

If

(2)

Let

(3)

(4)

If

is sofic, then

(5)

We show a general result involving permanence of soficity for products and coproducts of sofic actions on graphs.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
be two graphs. Let
\Gamma_{1}\prod\Gamma_{2}=(V,E)
denote any product between
\Gamma_{1}
and

of the following form:

(i)

, and

(ii)

The truth value of

v_{2}=w_{2},

(v_{1},w_{1})\in E_{1},
and
(v_{2},w_{2})\in E_{2}.

The Cartesian, tensor, lexicographical, strong, co-normal, modular, and homomorphic products of graphs are all examples of graph products in our sense
[
Gra17
]
. For example, if

the Cartesian product, then
((v_{1},v_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2}))\in E
if and only if
(v_{1},v_{2})\in E_{1}
and
w_{1}=w_{2}
or
v_{1}=v_{2}
and
(w_{1},w_{2})\in E_{2}
.

We remark that if
G_{i}\curvearrowright\Gamma_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
,

are actions of groups on graphs then the product action
G_{1}\times G_{2}\curvearrowright\Gamma_{1}\prod\Gamma_{2}

is also a graph action, since the action of each

preserves the truth value of equality (by acting by injections) and the truth value of being an edge (by definition of graph embedding).

Let
\Gamma_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
be two graphs. The coproduct of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
is denoted
\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
and has vertex set
V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
and has edge set
E=\{(v,w):\exists i\ v,w\in V_{i}\text{ and }(v,w)\in E_{i}\}
.

Proposition 1.4

(see Propositions
2.8
,
2.9
)
.

If
G_{i}\curvearrowright^{\alpha_{i}}\Gamma_{i}=(V_{i},E_{i})
,

and so is

The previous result applies to countable infinite (co)products by induction and the fact that for each finite set
W\subset\bigsqcup V_{i},

W
only intersects finitely many of the

We also note that in the case
G=G_{1}=g_{2}
we can view

via the diagonal embedding, giving an action of

and

We also obtain that sofic actions on graphs imply the acting group has a large quotient which is sofic:

Proposition 1.5

(see Proposition

)

If

Examples of sofic actions on graphs

By using a rather standard argument involving Folner sets used prior in our work
[
GKEP24
]
, the main difference being checking certain graph embedding criteria, we obtain that any action of an amenable group on a graph is sofic. Another family of groups that satisfy all actions are sofic, are free groups. This however, is quite tricky to obtain, in particular we needed a technical graph theoretic result of E. Hrushovski
[
Hru92
]
.

Proposition 1.6

(see Theorems
2.11
,
2.14
)
.

Any action of an amenable group or a free group on a graph is sofic.

The following is perhaps the most important result of this paper, and settles one of the important open question from
[
GKEP24
]
.

Theorem 1.7

(see Theorem
2.20
)
.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright\Gamma=(V,E)
be an action of a sofic group such that
G\curvearrowright V
is transitive and
\operatorname{Stab}(v)
is amenable for some/all
v\in V.
Then
\alpha
is sofic.

We now explain the sketch of the proof of the above. The argument uses the technique of Følner tilings, i.e., partition of an amenable group into translations of finitely many tiles, each of which can be assumed to be appropriately invariant under the action of given finitely many elements of group, up to some
\epsilon
. By choosing an appropriately invariant Følner set and enlarging it to include all tiles touching it, we can show that there is a Følner sequence s.t. each set in the sequence is a union of prefixed tiles. It is known that any two sofic approximations of an amenable group are conjugate
[
ES11
]
. Using this, if
H<G
is an infinite amenable subgroup of a sofic group, then we can obtain a sequence of sofic approximations of
G
whose underlying sets are a given Følner sequence of
H
, and on which
H
acts by left multiplication.

Now, applying the above results, we may choose a sequence of sofic approximations of
G
whose underlying sets are a Følner sequence of
H
, each one a union of tiles appropriately invariant under the action of the finite set
K=\{\sigma(x)^{-1}g\sigma(g^{-1}x):g\in F,x\in W\}
for some finite subset
F\subset G
and
W\subset V
, and where
\sigma:V=G/H\to G
is some fixed lifting map. The finite approximating graph
B
is then formed by collapsing each tile into a single point and connecting tiles in such a way as to approximate how vertices are connected in
\Gamma
. For some large enough subset
S
of the Følner set on which everything “behaves well”,
\pi_{s}
sends
x\in W
to the tile corresponding to
\sigma(x)^{-1}s
. The tiles being appropriately invariant under
K
ensures
\pi_{\varphi(g)s}(v)=\pi_{s}(\alpha(g^{-1})v)
for all
s\in S
,
g\in F
,
v\in W
, whenever
\varphi(g)s\in S
and
\alpha(g^{-1})v\in W
. And having a sequence of sofic approximations ensures for large enough index,
g\in G\setminus H
behaves sufficiently differently from elements of
H
, which are the only ones that can send a tile to itself. This ensures
\pi_{s}
are injective. Følner tilings are needed as this step requires having a prefixed finite subset of
H
s.t. behaving sufficiently differently from them are enough to ensure injectivity, and this finite subset comes from the fixed finitely many tiles. This shows the soficity of the action.

As a corollary of the previous result we immediately obtain the following result:

Corollary 1.8
.

A group is sofic if and only if its left multiplication action on any/all Cayley graph is sofic.

A further rich source of examples can be obtained by considering limits under a natural Gromov-Hausdorff topology:

Let

We prove the following result:

Let

Corollary 1.11

(see Corollary

Let

Graph wreath products

One can construct a natural family of groups arising from group actions on graphs, which we call graph wreath products. The reason for the terminology is because this simultaneously generalizes graph products and generalized wreath products. First recall from See
[
Gre90
]
:
Let
H
be a group and
\Gamma=(V,E)
a graph. For each
v\in V
denote by
H_{v}
an isomorphic copy of
H
. The graph product
*^{\Gamma}H
is the group with presentation

\langle H_{v}:v\in V\mid[h_{v},h_{w}]=e\text{ if }h_{v}\in H_{v},h_{w}\in H_{w},\text{ and }(v,w)\in E\rangle.

Let
G\curvearrowright\Gamma
be an action. Then any graph product
*^{\Gamma}H
is acted on by
G
via automorphisms that permute the vertices. The graph wreath product is then the semi-direct product
*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G
.

The graph product of a tracial von Neumann algebra
M
over a graph
\Gamma
is defined in
[
CF17
]
; it also admits an action of
G
by automorphisms which permute the vertices, and we can again form the graph wreath product
*^{\Gamma}M\rtimes G.
If
H
is a sofic group (resp. if
M
is Connes-embeddable) then
*^{\Gamma}H
(resp.
*^{\Gamma}M
) is sofic
[
CHR14
]
(resp. Connes-embeddable
[
Cas15
,
CC21
]
).

We prove the following result, which extends several prior works including
[
HS18
,
GKEP24
,
CHR14
,
CC21
]
:

Theorem 1.12
.

Fix a sofic group
H.
Consider a sofic action of a sofic group
G\curvearrowright^{\alpha}\Gamma=(V,E).
Then the graph wreath product
*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G
is sofic. Analogously, fix a Connes-embeddable tracial von Neumann algebra
M.
Consider a sofic action of a hyperlinear group
G\curvearrowright\Gamma=(V,E).
Then the graph wreath product
*^{\Gamma}M\rtimes G
is Connes-embeddable.

2.1.
Ultraproduct framework

We obtain below an ultraproduct definition for sofic actions and discuss the relation with the finitary definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X_{n})
be a sequence of sets,
\mathcal{U}
be a free ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Then
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
, the
algebraic ultraproduct
of
(X_{n})
, is defined as
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}=\prod X_{n}/\sim
where
f\sim g
iff
\{n:f(n)=g(n)\}\in\mathcal{U}
. We shall write
(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}
to mean the element of
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
represented by
(x_{n})\in\prod X_{n}
. If
(A_{n}\subset X_{n})
is a sequence of subsets, then we shall write
(A_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}
to mean,

(A_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}=\{(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}:\{n:x_{n}\in A_{n}\}\in\mathcal{U}\}

We may similarly define ultraproducts of graphs. If
(\Gamma_{n}=(V_{n},E_{n}))
is a sequence of graphs, then
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\Gamma_{n}
has vertex set
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}V_{n}
and
((v_{n}),(w_{n}))
define an edge if
\{n:(v_{n},w_{n})\in E_{n}\}\in\mathcal{U}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X_{n},d_{n})
be a sequence of metric spaces,
\mathcal{U}
be a free ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Then
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(X_{n},d_{n})
, the
metric ultraproduct
of
(X_{n},d_{n})
, is defined to have the underlying set
\prod X_{n}/\sim
where
f\sim g
iff
\lim_{\mathcal{U}}d_{n}(f(n),g(n))=0
. We shall write
(x_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}
to mean the element of
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
represented by
(x_{n})\in\prod X_{n}
. Then the metric on this space is defined by

.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma_{u}
be a countable graph that contains a copy of every finite graph. For example, we may take
\Gamma_{u}=(V_{u},E_{u})
to be the disjoint union of all finite graphs, or to be the random graph on
\aleph_{0}
vertices. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a free ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}.
For each

let

be the collection of all maps

Let
\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{U}}=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(V_{n},d_{n})

\rho_{n}(f,g)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s\in[n]}\rho(f(s),g(s)),

1/2&\text{ if }(v,w)\in E_{u},\\

Then

has two metrics:

and

We remark that if

2.2.
Properties of Sofic Actions on Graphs

In this subsection we state some basic results about sofic actions on graphs, prove soficity for products and coproducts of sofic actions on graphs, prove that sofic actions on graphs imply the acting group has a large quotient which is sofic, and that the action of any amenable group on a graph is sofic.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

(1)

]

Proof.

.

(1)

s=(s_{1},s_{2})\in S,

g=(g_{1},g_{2})\in F\subset F_{1}\times F_{2},
and
(v_{1},v_{2})\in W.
Assume
\varphi(g)s\in S
and
\alpha(g^{-1})v\in W.
Then

\displaystyle\pi_{\varphi(g)s}(v)

\displaystyle=\pi_{(\varphi_{1}(g_{1})s_{1},\varphi_{2}(g_{2})s_{2})}(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=(\pi^{1}_{\varphi_{1}(g_{1})s_{1}}(v_{1}),\pi^{2}_{\varphi_{2}(g_{2})s_{2}}(v_{2}))

\displaystyle=(\pi^{1}_{s_{1}}(\alpha_{1}(g_{1}^{-1})v_{1}),\pi^{2}_{s_{2}}(\alpha_{2}(g_{2}^{-1})v_{2}))

\displaystyle=\pi_{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\alpha_{1}(g_{1}^{-1}v_{1}),\alpha_{2}(g_{2}^{-1})v_{2})

\displaystyle=\pi_{s}(\alpha(g^{-1}v)),

finishing the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

If
G\curvearrowright^{\alpha}\Gamma=(V,E)
is sofic, then there is
H\trianglelefteq G
such that
H\leq\bigcap_{v\in V}\operatorname{Stab}(v)
and
G/H
is sofic.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.6
, there are maps
\varphi:G\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(S_{n},d)
and
\pi:V\to\mathbb{V}_{\mathcal{U}}
such that
\mathbb{S}(\varphi(g))(\pi(v))=\pi(\alpha(g)v)
for all
g\in G
and
v\in V
. Define
H=\ker(\varphi)
. Then
G/H
is sofic since it embeds into
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(S_{n},d).
It remains to show that
H\subset\operatorname{Stab}(v)
for all
v\in V.
Let
g\in H.
Then
\varphi(g)=1,
so that
\pi(\alpha(g)v)=\mathbb{S}(\varphi(g))(\pi(v))=\pi(v)
. But
\pi
is an isometry, so it is injective, and thus
v=\alpha(g)v
. In other words,
g\in\operatorname{Stab}(v)
.
∎

The key point of the following is verifying the maps
\pi_{s}
are graph embeddings.

Theorem 2.11
.

Any action of an amenable group on a graph is sofic.

Proof.

Let
G
be the acting group,
\Gamma=(V,E)
the graph, and
\alpha
the action. Fix finite subsets
F\subset G,

W\subset V
, and
\varepsilon>0.
Assume without loss of generality
F
is symmetric and contains the identity of
G.
Let
F^{\prime}=F\cdot F
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2|F|}
. Since
G
is amenable, there is a finite set
A\subset G
such that
|A\vartriangle gA|<\varepsilon^{\prime}|A|
for all
g\in F^{\prime}.
We now construct a unital,
(F,\varepsilon)
-multiplicative,
(F,W,\varepsilon)
-orbit approximation
\varphi
of
\alpha.

For
g\in G,
choose
\varphi(g)\in\textrm{Sym}(A)
to be any element such that
\varphi(g)a=ga
whenever
a,ga\in A.
It follows that
\varphi
is unital. We now claim it is
(F,\varepsilon)
-multiplicative. Indeed, let
g,h\in F
. For
a\in A
such that
ha,gha\in A
, by definition we have
\varphi(g)\varphi(h)a=\varphi(gh)a.
So, since
h^{-1},h^{-1}g^{-1}\in F\cdot F
,

\displaystyle|A|\cdot d(\varphi(g)\varphi(h),\varphi(gh))

\displaystyle\leq|\{a\in A:ha\not\in A\}|+|\{a\in A:gha\not\in A\}|

\displaystyle\leq|A\vartriangle h^{-1}A|+|A\vartriangle h^{-1}g^{-1}A|

\displaystyle<2\varepsilon^{\prime}|A|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|A|.

That is,
d(\varphi(g)\varphi(h),\varphi(gh))<\varepsilon.

We now show
\varphi
is a
(F,W,\varepsilon)
-orbit approximation of
\alpha.
Let
S=\{s\in A:gs\in A\ \forall g\in F\}
. Since
F
is symmetric,
S=\cap_{g\in F}(A\cap gA)
. Since
|A\cap gA|>(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})|A|,
we have
|S|>(1-|F|\varepsilon^{\prime})|A|>(1-\varepsilon)|A|.
Now let
B=(V_{B},E_{B})
where
V_{B}=\alpha(A^{-1})\cdot W
and
E_{B}=E|_{V_{B}\times V_{B}}
. Define
\pi_{s}:W\to V_{B}
by
\pi_{s}(v)=\alpha(s^{-1})v.
Each
\pi_{s}
is clearly a graph embedding since
\alpha(s^{-1})\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
and
B
is a subgraph of
\Gamma
containing all vertices in
\alpha(s^{-1})W
and all edges between such vertices.

We also have, for all
s\in S,

g\in F,
and
v\in W
such that
\varphi(g)s\in S
and
\alpha(g^{-1})v\in W,

\pi_{\varphi(g)s}(v)=\pi_{gs}(x)=\alpha(s^{-1}g^{-1})v=\alpha(s^{-1})\alpha(g^{-1})v=\pi_{s}(\alpha(g^{-1})v),

where we used the fact that, since
gs\in A,

\varphi(g)s=gs.
This concludes the proof.

We now present two main results. The first is that any action of a free group on a graph is sofic. This proof requires a graph theoretic result due to Hrushovski

Hru92
]
. The second is a proof proving soficity of actions with amenable stabilizers. This is a substantial generalization of Theorem 2.14 in
[
GKEP24
]
and can be considered an optimal result in this setting.

We begin with actions of free groups. The following result is a direct consequence of
[
Hru92
]
. We thank Henry Wilton for pointing us to this reference.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a graph,
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
be finitely many automorphisms of
\Gamma
,
\Gamma_{0}=(V_{0},E_{0})\subset\Gamma
be a finite induced subgraph. Then there exists a finite graph
\Gamma^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
, a graph embedding
\pi:\Gamma_{0}\to\Gamma^{\prime}
, and automorphisms
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma^{\prime})
s.t., if for some
i
and
v\in F_{0}
, we have
\alpha_{i}(v)\in F_{0}
, then
\pi(\alpha_{i}(v))=\phi_{i}(\pi(v))
.

Theorem 2.14
.

Any action of a free group on a graph is sofic.

Proof.

By item 3 of Proposition
2.7
, we may assume the acting group
G
is a finitely generated free group, i.e.,
G=\mathbb{F}_{n}
and we write
g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}
for the free generators of
G
. We denote the graph being acted on as
\Gamma=(V,E)
and the action be
\alpha:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
. Let
\alpha_{i}=\alpha(g_{i})\in\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
F\subset G
,
W\subset V
be finite subsets,
\epsilon>0
. Let
F^{-1}=\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\}
.

We write
f_{j}=g_{i_{j,1}}^{\epsilon_{j,1}}\cdots g_{i_{j,k_{j}}}^{\epsilon_{j,k_{j}}}
, where
\epsilon
’s are in
\{\pm 1\}
. Let
W^{\prime}\subset V
be a finite set containing
W
and all elements of
V
of the form
\alpha(g_{i_{j,l}}^{\epsilon_{j,l}}\cdots g_{i_{j,k_{j}}}^{\epsilon_{j,k_{j}}})v=\alpha_{i_{j,l}}^{\epsilon_{j,l}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{j,k_{j}}}^{\epsilon_{j,k_{j}}}v
for all
v\in W
,
1\leq j\leq m
,
1\leq l\leq k_{j}
. Then by Lemma
2.13
, there exists a finite graph
B
, a graph embedding
\pi:(W^{\prime},E|_{W^{\prime}\times W^{\prime}})\hookrightarrow B
, and automorphisms
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
s.t.
\pi(\alpha_{i}(v))=\phi_{i}(\pi(v))
whenever
v\in W^{\prime}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, and
\alpha_{i}(v)\in W^{\prime}
. Clearly, this also implies
\pi(\alpha_{i}^{-1}(v))=\phi_{i}^{-1}(\pi(v))
whenever
v\in W^{\prime}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, and
\alpha_{i}^{-1}(v)\in W^{\prime}
. By sending
g_{i}
to
\phi_{i}
, we have a group homomorphism
\psi:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(B)
, which induces another group homomorphism
\varphi:G\to\textrm{Sym}(\operatorname{Aut}(B))
given by
\varphi(g)s=\psi(g)s
.

Note that
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
is a finite set. Since
\varphi
is a group homomorphism, it is clearly unital and
(F,\epsilon)
-multiplicative. Thus, it suffices to show it is an
(F,W,\epsilon)
-orbit approximation of
\alpha
. Let
S=\operatorname{Aut}(B)
. For each
s\in S
, let
\pi_{s}:(W,E|_{W\times W})\hookrightarrow B
be given by
\pi_{s}(v)=s^{-1}(\pi(v))
, which is clearly a graph embedding. Now, for
s\in S
,
g\in F
,
v\in W
, we have
\pi_{\varphi(g)s}(v)=\pi_{\psi(g)s}(v)=s^{-1}\psi(g^{-1})\pi(v)
. Then
g^{-1}=f_{j}=g_{i_{j,1}}^{\epsilon_{j,1}}\cdots g_{i_{j,k_{j}}}^{\epsilon_{j,k_{j}}}
for some
j
, so,

\psi(g^{-1})\pi(v)=\phi_{i_{j,1}}^{\epsilon_{j,1}}\cdots\phi_{i_{j,k_{j}}}^{\epsilon_{j,k_{j}}}\pi(v)=\phi_{i_{j,1}}^{\epsilon_{j,1}}\cdots\phi_{i_{j,k_{j}-1}}^{\epsilon_{j,k_{j}-1}}\pi(\alpha_{i_{j,k_{j}}}^{\epsilon_{j,k_{j}}}v)=\cdots=\pi(\alpha_{i_{j,1}}^{\epsilon_{j,1}}\cdots\alpha_{i_{j,k_{j}}}^{\epsilon_{j,k_{j}}}v)=\pi(\alpha(g^{-1})v)

Thus, whenever
\alpha(g^{-1})v\in E
, we have
\pi_{\varphi(g)s}(v)=s^{-1}\psi(g^{-1})\pi(v)=s^{-1}\pi(\alpha(g^{-1})x)=\pi_{s}(\alpha(g^{-1})x)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.16

(Theorem 4.3 of

]
)
.

Let
G
be an infinite amenable group,

be a finite set,
\epsilon>0
. Then there exists a tiling

of

s.t. every shape

in

is

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
G
be an infinite amenable group. Fix a tiling
T
of
G
. Then there exists a Følner sequence
A_{n}
of
G
s.t.
|A_{n}|\to\infty
and each
A_{n}
is a union of tiles in
T
.

Proof.

Fix an increasing sequence of finite sets
F_{n}\subset G
with
\cup_{n}F_{n}=G
. For each
n
, we shall construct a finite set
A_{n}
s.t.
|A_{n}|\geq n
,
\frac{|gA_{n}\triangle A_{n}|}{|A_{n}|}\leq\frac{1}{n}
for all
g\in F_{n}
, and
A_{n}
is a union of tiles in
T
. Then such
A_{n}
clearly satisfies the desired conclusion.

Let
\{S_{1},\cdots,S_{n}\}
be the shapes of
T
. Let
M=\max\{|S_{1}|,\cdots,|S_{n}|\}
. Let,

E=(\cup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}S_{i}^{-1})\cup F_{n}

which is a finite set. Let
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2nM|E|+n}
. Since
G
is an infinite amenable group, there exists
B
which is
(E,\epsilon)
-invariant and furthermore

. Let
A_{n}
be the union of all tiles intersecting

. Since the tiles are all finite and they form a partition of

, we observe that
|A_{n}|\geq|B|\geq n
and
A_{n}
is finite.

We now calculate an upper bound on
|A_{n}\setminus B|
. Observe that, if
s\in B
belongs to a tile

and
Es\subset B
, then as
t
is of the form
S_{i}c
for some

,

, and as
\cup_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}S_{i}^{-1}\subset E
, we have

. So any tile that intersects

but is not fully contained in

But then for any

\begin{split}|gA_{n}\triangle A_{n}|&=|gA_{n}\setminus A_{n}|+|A_{n}\setminus gA_{n}|\\

&\leq|gB\setminus B|+|B\setminus gB|+2|A_{n}\setminus B|\\
&\leq\epsilon|B|+2M\epsilon|E||B|\\
&\leq(2M|E|+1)\epsilon|A_{n}|\\
&=\frac{|A_{n}|}{n}\end{split}

Lemma 2.18
.

Let

be an amenable subgroup,
\psi_{n}:H\to\textrm{Sym}(A_{n})

and a sequence of sofic approximations
\varphi_{k}:G\to\textrm{Sym}(A_{n_{k}})

\psi_{n_{k}}

Since
G
is sofic, fix a sequence of sofic approximations
\varphi_{k}^{\prime}:G\to\textrm{Sym}(B_{k})
of
G
. Choose
\{n_{k}\}_{k}
s.t.
|A_{n_{k}}|\geq k|B_{k}|
. Then we may write
A_{n_{k}}=(B_{k}\times Q_{k})\sqcup R_{k}
, where
|C_{k}|\geq k
and
R_{k}<|B_{k}|
. We may then define
\varphi_{k}^{\prime\prime}:G\to\textrm{Sym}(A_{n_{k}})

\varphi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(g)(b,q)=(\varphi_{k}^{\prime}(g)b,q)

. Since
\frac{|R_{k}|}{|A_{n_{k}}|}<\frac{|B_{k}|}{k|B_{k}|}=\frac{1}{k}\to 0

is a sequence of sofic approximations for

. Then there are two sequences of sofic approximations for

on the sets

\psi_{n_{k}}
and

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright\Gamma=(V,E)
be an action of a sofic group such that
G\curvearrowright V
is transitive and
\operatorname{Stab}(v)
is finite for some/all
v\in V.
Then
\alpha
is sofic.

Proof.

Fix some
v\in V
and let
H=\operatorname{Stab}(v)
. Then we may take
V=G/H
and the action
\alpha
being the left-multiplication action of
G
on
V=G/H
. Let
F\subset G
,
W\subset G/H
be finite subsets. Fix
\epsilon>0
. Let
q:G\rightarrow G/H
be the natural quotient map. Fix
\sigma:G/H\rightarrow G
an arbitrary section of
q
. Let,

F^{\prime}=F\cup H\cup[H\cdot(\sigma(W)\cup\sigma(W)^{-1})\cdot H]

We observe that since
H
is a subgroup,
1\in H
, so,

\begin{split}\sigma(W)\subset H\cdot(\sigma(W)\cup\sigma(W)^{-1})\cdot H\subset F^{\prime}\\
\sigma(W)^{-1}\subset H\cdot(\sigma(W)\cup\sigma(W)^{-1})\cdot H\subset F^{\prime}\\
\sigma(W)\cdot H\subset H\cdot(\sigma(W)\cup\sigma(W)^{-1})\cdot H\subset F^{\prime}\\
H\cdot\sigma(W)^{-1}\subset H\cdot(\sigma(W)\cup\sigma(W)^{-1})\cdot H\subset F^{\prime}\end{split}

We also observe that, as
H
is finite, so is
F^{\prime}
. Let
\epsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\epsilon}{|W|+1}
. Since
F^{\prime}
is finite and as
G
is sofic, there is a finite set
A
and a unital,
(F,\epsilon)
-multiplicative map
\varphi:G\rightarrow\textrm{Sym}(A)
and a subset
S^{\prime}\subset A
s.t.
|S^{\prime}|>(1-\epsilon^{\prime})|A|
, and,

(1)

\varphi(g)s\neq\varphi(h)s
for all
g,h\in F^{\prime}\cdot F^{\prime}
,
g\neq h
,
s\in S^{\prime}
(we observe here that
F^{\prime}\subset F^{\prime}\cdot F^{\prime}
as
1\in H\subset F^{\prime}
);

(2)

\varphi(gh)s=\varphi(g)\varphi(h)s
for all
g,h\in F^{\prime}
,
s\in S^{\prime}
;

(3)

\varphi(g^{-1})s=\varphi(g)^{-1}s
for all
g\in F^{\prime}
,
s\in S^{\prime}
.

Now, on the set
S^{\prime}
, we define a relation
s_{1}\sim s_{2}
if there exists
h\in H
s.t.
s_{1}=\varphi(h)s_{2}
. Since
\varphi
is unital,
\sim
is reflexive. As
H
is a group,
H\subset F^{\prime}
, and by the conditions on
S^{\prime}
, we see that
\sim
is symmetric and transitive. Hence,
\sim
is an equivalence relation. Let
B
be the graph with vertex set
V^{\prime}=S^{\prime}/\sim
, which is finite. We let
B
have the edge set,

E^{\prime}=\{([s_{1}]_{\sim},[s_{2}]_{\sim}):s_{1},s_{2}\in S^{\prime}\,\text{s.t. }\exists x,y\in W\,\begin{cases}x,y\text{ are connected in }\Gamma\text{, and}\\
\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1}\sigma(y))s_{2}=s_{1}\end{cases}\}

We need to show this is well-defined, i.e.,
E^{\prime}
is a symmetric relation excluding the diagonal. Indeed, if
([s_{1}]_{\sim},[s_{2}]_{\sim})\in E^{\prime}
, i.e., there exists
x,y\in W
s.t.
x,y
are connected in
\Gamma
and
\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1}\sigma(y))s_{2}=s_{1}
. But then
y,x
are connected in
\Gamma
, and by conditions on
S^{\prime}
,

\begin{split}s_{1}=\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1}\sigma(y))s_{2}=\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})\varphi(\sigma(y))s_{2}&\Rightarrow\varphi(\sigma(y))s_{2}=\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})^{-1}s_{1}\\
&\Rightarrow\varphi(\sigma(y)^{-1})^{-1}s_{2}=\varphi(\sigma(x))s_{1}\\
&\Rightarrow s_{2}=\varphi(\sigma(y)^{-1})\varphi(\sigma(x))s_{1}\end{split}

So
([s_{2}]_{\sim},[s_{1}]_{\sim})\in E^{\prime}
and
E^{\prime}
is symmetric. To show it excludes the diagonal, let
v\in V^{\prime}
. Suppose there exists
s_{1},s_{2}\in v
,
x,y\in W
s.t.
x,y
are connected in
\Gamma
and
\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1}\sigma(y))s_{2}=s_{1}
. As
s_{1}\sim s_{2}
, we have
s_{2}=\varphi(h)s_{1}
for some

. Again, by conditions on
S^{\prime}
,

\begin{split}s_{1}=\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1}\sigma(y))s_{2}=\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})\varphi(\sigma(y))s_{2}&\Rightarrow\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})^{-1}s_{1}=\varphi(\sigma(y))\varphi(h)s_{1}\\
&\Rightarrow\varphi(\sigma(x))s_{1}=\varphi(\sigma(y)h)s_{1}\\
&\Rightarrow\sigma(x)=\sigma(y)h\\
&\Rightarrow x=y\end{split}

But
x,y
are connected in
\Gamma
, so
x\neq y
, a contradiction. Thus,
(v,v)\notin E^{\prime}
.

Having shown that
B
is well-defined, we now define,

S=S^{\prime}\cap\bigcap_{x\in E}\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})^{-1}S^{\prime}

We observe that as
|S^{\prime}|>(1-\epsilon^{\prime})|A|
and
\epsilon^{\prime}=\frac{\epsilon}{|W|+1}
, we have
|S|>(1-\epsilon)|A|
. Now, for
s\in S
, we define
\pi_{s}:W\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}
to be
\pi_{s}(x)=[\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})s]_{\sim}
. We observe that this is injective. Indeed, assume
\pi_{s}(x)=\pi_{s}(y)
, i.e.,
\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})s\sim\varphi(\sigma(y)^{-1})s
. Then there exists
h\in H
s.t.
\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})s=\varphi(h)\varphi(\sigma(y)^{-1})s
. By conditions on
S^{\prime}
and noting that
s\in S\subset S^{\prime}
, we have
\varphi(\sigma(x)^{-1})s=\varphi(h)\varphi(\sigma(y)^{-1})s=\varphi(h\sigma(y)^{-1})s
, so

. But then
\sigma(x)=\sigma(y)h^{-1}\in\sigma(y)H=y
, i.e.,

. This shows that

Definition 2.21
.

A Cayley graph of a group
G
is a graph
\Gamma=(G,E)
where, for some fixed (not necessarily finite) symmetric subset
S\subset G\setminus\{1_{G}\}
,
(g,h)\in E
if and only if exists
k\in S
such that
gk=h.

Corollary 2.22
.

A group is sofic if and only if its left multiplication action on any/all Cayley graph is sofic.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
Suppose
G
is sofic. The left multiplication
G\curvearrowright G
induces a graph action
G\curvearrowright\Gamma
on any Cayley graph
\Gamma
of
G.
This action is clearly transitive and has trivial stabilizers, so by Lemma
2.19
the action is sofic.

(\Leftarrow)
Suppose
G\curvearrowright\Gamma
is sofic for some Cayley graph
\Gamma
of
G.
Since all stabilizers are trivial, by Theorem
2.10
,
G=G/\{1_{G}\}
is sofic.
∎

w\in W
. Let,

F^{\prime}=\sigma(W)^{-1}\sigma(W)\cup\{\sigma(w)^{-1}g\sigma(\alpha(g^{-1})w):g\in F,w\in W\}

Then by definition of the Gromov-Hausdorff topology, there exists
n>0
s.t. for any
g\in F^{\prime}
, we have
g\in\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha_{n}}(v_{n})
iff
g\in\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha}(v)
; and
v_{n}
is connected to
\alpha_{n}(g)v_{n}
iff
v
is connected to
\alpha(g)v
.

Now, let
W_{n}=\{\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w))v_{n}:w\in W\}
. As
\alpha_{n}
is sofic, there exists
\varphi:G\to\textrm{Sym}(A)
which is unital,
(F,\epsilon)
-multiplicative, and an
(F,W_{n},\epsilon)
-orbit approximation, i.e., there exists a finite graph
B
, a subset
S\subset A
with
|S|>(1-\epsilon)|A|
, and for each
s\in S
, a graph embedding
\pi_{s}^{\prime}:(W_{n},E_{n}|_{W_{n}\times W_{n}})\hookrightarrow B
s.t.
\pi_{\varphi(g)s}^{\prime}(x)=\pi_{s}^{\prime}(\alpha(g^{-1})x)
for all
s\in S
,
g\in F
,
x\in W_{n}
, whenever
\varphi(g)s\in S
and
\alpha(g^{-1})x\in W_{n}
.

It suffices to show
\varphi
is also an
(F,W,\epsilon)
-orbit approximation. For each
s\in S
,
w\in W
, we define,

\pi_{s}(w)=\pi_{s}^{\prime}(\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w))v_{n})

To show this is a graph embedding, as
\pi_{s}^{\prime}
is one, it suffices to show the map
\psi:(W,E|_{W\times W})\to(W_{n},E_{n}|_{W_{n}\times W_{n}})
,
\psi(w)=\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w))v_{n}
, is a graph isomorphism. It is clearly surjective. For
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
, since
\sigma(W)^{-1}\sigma(W)\subset F^{\prime}
, we have,

\begin{split}\alpha(\sigma(w_{1}))v=\alpha(\sigma(w_{2}))v&\Leftrightarrow v=\alpha(\sigma(w_{1})^{-1}\sigma(w_{2}))v\\
&\Leftrightarrow v_{n}=\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w_{1})^{-1}\sigma(w_{2}))v_{n}\\
&\Leftrightarrow\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w_{1}))v_{n}=\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w_{2}))v_{n}\end{split}

i.e.,
w_{1}=w_{2}
iff
\psi(w_{1})=\psi(w_{2})
, so
\psi
is injective. By the same argument,
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are connected iff
\psi(w_{1})
and
\psi(w_{2})
are connected. This shows that
\psi
is a graph isomorphism, whence
\pi_{s}
is a graph embedding.

Now, let
s\in S
,
g\in F
,
w\in W
s.t.
\varphi(g)s\in S
and
\alpha(g^{-1})w\in W
. This implies
\alpha(\sigma(\alpha(g^{-1})w))v=\alpha(g^{-1})w=\alpha(g^{-1}\sigma(w))v
, so,

\sigma(w)^{-1}g\sigma(\alpha(g^{-1})w)\in\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha}(v)

By definition of
F^{\prime}
,
\sigma(w)^{-1}g\sigma(\alpha(g^{-1})w)\in F^{\prime}
, so
\sigma(w)^{-1}g\sigma(\alpha(g^{-1})w)\in\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha_{n}}(v_{n})
, i.e.,

v_{n}=\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w)^{-1}g\sigma(\alpha(g^{-1})w))v_{n}\Rightarrow\alpha_{n}(g^{-1})\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w))v_{n}=\alpha_{n}(\sigma(\alpha(g^{-1})w))v_{n}

Thus, both
\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w))v_{n}\in W_{n}
and
\alpha_{n}(g^{-1})\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w))v_{n}\in W_{n}
. We therefore have,

\begin{split}\pi_{\varphi(g)s}(w)&=\pi_{\varphi(g)s}^{\prime}(\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w))v_{n})\\
&=\pi_{s}^{\prime}(\alpha_{n}(g^{-1})\alpha_{n}(\sigma(w))v_{n})\\
&=\pi_{s}^{\prime}(\alpha_{n}(\sigma(\alpha(g^{-1})w))v_{n})\\
&=\pi_{s}(\alpha(g^{-1})w)\end{split}

∎

The following is immediate:

Proposition 2.24
.

If
(\Gamma_{n},v_{n},\alpha_{n})\to(\Gamma,v,\alpha)
in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology, then
\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha_{n}}(v_{n})\to\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha}(v)
in the Chabauty topology. If
\Gamma_{n}
and
\Gamma
are either all fully disconnected or all complete, then the converse also holds.

Combining the above with item 4 of Proposition
2.7
, we immediately retrieve,

Corollary 2.25

(Propostion 5 of
[
Gao24
]
)
.

Let
(H_{n})
be a sequence of subgroups of
G.
If
G\curvearrowright G/H_{n}
is sofic for each
n
and
H_{n}\to H
in the Chabauty topology, then
G\curvearrowright G/H
is sofic.

We observe the following easy lemma, which leads to a more algebraic characterization of Gromov-Hausdorff convergence:

Lemma 2.26
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group. A transitive action of
G
on a graph with a distinguished vertex is specified by,

(1)

A subgroup
H\leq G
;

(2)

A symmetric subset
S\subset G
, which we shall call the
characteristic set of edges
, s.t.
HSH\subset S
and
S\cap H=\varnothing
. Equivalently, a subset
S^{\prime}\subset H\backslash G/H
of the double coset space which is invariant under the involution
HgH\mapsto Hg^{-1}H
and
H\notin S^{\prime}
. (
S^{\prime}=\{HgH:g\in S\}
and
S=\bigcup S^{\prime}
.)

The graph being acted on is then isomorphic to
\Gamma=(G/H,E)
, where
E
is s.t.
(gH,kH)\in E
iff
k=gs
for some
s\in S
, while the distinguished vertex is sent to
H
by the isomorphism and the action of
G
is carried over by the isomorphism to the left multiplication action of
G
on
G/H
. Conversely, any such choice of
H
and
S
(or equivalently
S^{\prime}
) yields a transitive action of
G
on a graph with distinguished vertex
H
, as described above.

Proof.

We first note here how to retrieve
S
from an action
G\curvearrowright\Gamma=(G/H,E)
, where the action on the vertex set is the left-multiplication action.
S
is simply given by,

S=\bigcup\{gH:(H,gH)\in E\}

Clearly
SH\subset S
. To show
HS\subset S
as well, we observe that if
(H,gH)\in E
, then left multiplying by any
h\in H
yields
(H=hH,hgH)\in E
. To show
S
is symmetric, we observe that if
(H,gH)\in E
, then left multiplying by
g^{-1}
yields
(g^{-1}H,H)\in E
, so as
\Gamma
is undirected,
(H,g^{-1}H)\in E
. That
S\cap H=\varnothing
follows from
(H,H)\notin E
.

To show
(gH,kH)\in E
iff
k=gs
for some
s\in S
, we note that left multiplying by
g^{-1}
yields
(gH,kH)\in E
iff
(H,g^{-1}kH)\in E
iff
g^{-1}k\in S
iff
k=gs
for some
s\in S
.

For the converse, we simply note the graph and the action is well-defined. Indeed, note that as
HSH\subset S
, the relation
k=gs
for some
s\in S
between
gH
and
kH
does not depend on the choice of representatives of cosets
g
and
k
. And, as
S
is symmetric and
S\cap H=\varnothing
,
\Gamma=(G/H,E)
is indeed a well-defined simple, undirected graph. Finally, the relation
k=gs
is clearly preserved by left multiplication, i.e., for any
a\in G
,
k=gs
iff
ak=ags
.
∎

The following proposition, which can be understood as an improvement on Proposition
2.24
, is now a easy consequence of the proposition above and the definition of Gromov-Hausdorff topology:

Proposition 2.27
.

For each
n
, let
(\Gamma_{n},v_{n},\alpha_{n})\in\mathcal{C}
(where
\mathcal{C}
is as in Definition
1.9
). Let
(\Gamma,v,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}
. For each
n
, let
H_{n}=\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha_{n}}(v_{n})
and let
S_{n}
be the characteristic set of edges as given by Lemma
2.26
. Similarly, let
H=\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha}(v)
and
S
be the characteristic set of edges as given by Lemma
2.26
. Then
(\Gamma_{n},v_{n},\alpha_{n})\to(\Gamma,v,\alpha)
iff
1_{H_{n}}\to 1_{H}
pointwise (i.e.,
H_{n}\to H
in the Chabauty topology) and
1_{S_{n}}\to 1_{S}
pointwise.

We now have the following corollary of the proposition above and Theorem
2.23
,

Corollary 2.28
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of all
H\leq G
s.t. every transitive action of
G
on a graph with the stabilizer of some vertex being
H
is sofic. Then
\mathcal{H}
is closed under taking increasing unions.

Proof.

Let
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence in
\mathcal{H}
and
H=\cup_{n}H_{n}
. Let
(\Gamma,v,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}
with
\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha}(v)=H
. Let
S
be the characteristic set of edges as given by Lemma
2.26
. Then
S
is symmetric;
HSH\subset S
, so
H_{n}SH_{n}\subset S
as
H_{n}\subset H
; and
S\cap H=\varnothing
, so
S\cap H_{n}=\varnothing
as
H_{n}\subset H
. Thus,
S
and
H_{n}
determines
(\Gamma_{n},v_{n},\alpha_{n})\in\mathcal{C}
with
\operatorname{Stab}_{\alpha_{n}}(v_{n})=H_{n}
and the characteristic set of edges being
S
. As
1_{H_{n}}\to 1_{H}
and
1_{S}\to 1_{S}
, by Proposition
2.27
,
(\Gamma_{n},v_{n},\alpha_{n})\to(\Gamma,v,\alpha)
. By assumptions,
\alpha_{n}
is sofic for all
n
, so by Theorem
2.23
,
\alpha
is sofic. Since
\alpha
is an arbitrary action with the stabilizer of a vertex being
H
, this shows
H\in\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Using Remark
2.12
and the corollary above, we have an immediate corollary, in analogy with the result in

]
that all actions of LERF groups are sets on sofic:

Corollary 2.29
.

Let
(\Gamma,v,\alpha)\in\mathcal{C}
. Let
S
be the characteristic set of edges as given by Lemma
2.26
. If there exists a decreasing sequence of finite index subgroups

and a sequence of symmetric subset

with
H_{n}S_{n}H_{n}\subset S_{n}

, s.t.

is sofic. In particular, if

is a group for which: whenever

Question 2.30
.

We observe that groups that satisfy the conditions in the “in particular” part of the corollary above must be LERF (i.e., all finitely generated subgroups are decreasing intersections of finite index subgroups). Are there nontrivial examples of such groups except finite groups?

2.3.
Graph Wreath Products

In this subsection we see that, in analogy to
[
GKEP24
]
, graph wreath products over sofic groups with a sofic action are sofic.

Definition 2.31
.

Let
H
be a group and
\Gamma=(V,E)
a graph. For each
v\in V
denote by
H_{v}
an isomorphic copy of
H
. The graph product
*^{\Gamma}H
is the group with presentation

\langle H_{v}:v\in V\mid[h_{v},h_{w}]=e\text{ if }h_{v}\in H_{v},h_{w}\in H_{w},\text{ and }(v,w)\in E\rangle.

See
[
Gre90
]
.

Let
G\curvearrowright\Gamma
be an action. Then any graph product
*^{\Gamma}H
is acted on by
G
via automorphisms that permute the vertices. The graph wreath product is then the semi-direct product
*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G
.

The graph product of a tracial von Neumann algebra
M
over a graph
\Gamma
is defined in
[
CF17
]
; it also admits an action of
G
by automorphisms which permute the vertices, and we can again form the graph wreath product
*^{\Gamma}M\rtimes G.

Remark 2.32
.

If
H
is a sofic group (resp. if
M
is Connes-embeddable) then
*^{\Gamma}H
(resp.
*^{\Gamma}M
) is sofic
[
CHR14
]
(resp. Connes-embeddable
[
Cas15
,
CC21
]
).

Definition 2.33
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group and
A
be a finite set. Let
\alpha_{A}:\textrm{Sym}(A)\curvearrowright A
be the natural permutation action. The generalized wreath product
G\wr_{\alpha_{A}}\textrm{Sym}(A)
is defined to be the semi-direct product
G^{\oplus A}\rtimes_{\beta_{A}}\textrm{Sym}(A)
where
\beta_{A}
is the action
\beta_{A}:\textrm{Sym}(A)\curvearrowright G^{\oplus A}
induced by
\alpha_{A}
. We define a metric
d_{G,A}
on this group by

d_{G,A}(g_{1}\sigma_{1},g_{2}\sigma_{2})=\frac{1}{|A|}|\{a\in A:\sigma_{1}(a)\neq\sigma_{2}(a)\text{ or }g_{1}(\sigma_{1}(a))\neq g_{2}(\sigma_{2}(a))\}|,

where
\sigma_{i}\in\textrm{Sym}(A)
and
g_{i}\in G^{\oplus A}
are regarded as functions from
A
to
G
. The metric
d_{G,A}
is invariant under both left and right multiplication.

Lemma 2.34

(Corollary 3.5 in
[
GKEP24
]
)
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group. Suppose there exists a free ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N},
a sequence of sofic groups
G_{i},
and a sequence of finite sets
A_{i}
such that
G
embeds into
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(G_{i}^{\oplus A_{i}}\rtimes_{\beta_{A_{i}}}\textrm{Sym}(A_{i}),d_{G_{i},A_{i}}).
Then
G
is sofic.

A key step in the following proof is that we need to check that the maps
q_{s}^{i}
are always group homomorphisms, which will follow from the maps
\pi_{s}^{i}
being graph embeddings.

Theorem 2.35
.

Fix a sofic group
H.
Consider a sofic action of a sofic group
G\curvearrowright^{\alpha}\Gamma=(V,E).
Then the graph wreath product
*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G
is sofic.

Proof.

Fix increasing sequences of finite subsets
F_{i}\nearrow G
and
W_{i}\nearrow V.
Fix a decreasing sequence
\varepsilon_{i}\searrow 0.
For each
i,
let
\varphi_{i}:H\to\textrm{Sym}(A_{i})
be a unital,
(F_{i},\varepsilon_{i})
-multiplicative,
(F_{i},W_{i},\varepsilon_{i})
-orbit approximation of
\alpha.
Then there exist finite graphs
B_{i}=(V_{B_{i}},E_{B_{i}})
and subsets
S_{i}\subset A_{i}
such that
|S_{i}|>(1-\varepsilon_{i})|A_{i}|
and for each
s\in S_{i}
there is a graph embedding
\pi_{s}^{i}:W_{i}\to V_{B_{i}}
such that
\pi^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g)s}(v)=\pi_{s}^{i}(\alpha(g^{-1})v)
for all
s\in S_{i},

g\in F_{i},
and
v\in W_{i}
, whenever
\varphi_{i}(g)s\in S_{i}
and
\alpha(g^{-1})v\in W_{i}.

Denote
\Gamma_{i}=(W_{i},E|_{W_{i}\times W_{i}}).
Let
H_{i}=*^{B_{i}}H
, which is sofic since
H
is. Let
p_{i}:*^{\Gamma}H\to*^{\Gamma_{i}}H
be the map which is identity on
*^{\Gamma_{i}}H
and takes all other vertices to the identity. Let
q^{i}_{s}:*^{\Gamma_{i}}H\hookrightarrow*^{B_{i}}H=H_{i}
be the homomorphism induced by the graph embedding
\pi_{s}^{i}:\Gamma_{i}\hookrightarrow B_{i}.
Let
P^{i}_{s}=q^{i}_{s}\circ p_{i}.

Let the action of
G
on
*^{\Gamma}H
via
\alpha
be denoted by
\beta
, i.e., we wish to show
*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes_{\beta}G
is sofic. We define
\rho_{i}:*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes_{\beta}G\to H_{i}^{\oplus A_{i}}\rtimes_{\beta_{A_{i}}}\textrm{Sym}(A_{i})
as follows:

\rho_{i}(hg)=\left(\bigoplus_{s\in S_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(h)\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus S_{i}}1_{H_{i}}\right)\cdot\varphi_{i}(g)

where
h\in*^{\Gamma}H
and
g\in G.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a free ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}.
Let
\rho:*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(H_{i}^{\oplus A_{i}}\rtimes_{\beta_{A_{i}}}\textrm{Sym}(A_{i}),d_{H_{i},A_{i}})
be given by
\rho(hg)=(\rho_{i}(hg))_{\mathcal{U}}.

It now suffices to show that
\rho
is a homomorphism and that the map
\iota:*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G\to(*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G)/\ker(\rho)\times G
defined by
\iota(hg)=(hg\ker(\rho),g)
where
h\in*^{\Gamma}H
and
g\in G,
is injective. This is because the previous lemma would imply
(*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G)/\ker(\rho)\hookrightarrow\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(H_{i}^{\oplus A_{i}}\rtimes_{\beta_{A_{i}}}\textrm{Sym}(A_{i}),d_{H_{i},A_{i}})
is sofic.

We start by showing
\rho
is a homomorphism. Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in*^{\Gamma}H
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G.
The set of vertices on which each
h_{j},

j=1,2
is supported is finite, and so for large enough
i,

\operatorname{supp}(g_{2})\subset W_{i},

\alpha(g_{1})\operatorname{supp}(h_{2})\subset W_{i},
and
g_{1},g_{1}^{-1},g_{2}\in F_{i}.
Furthermore, since
\varphi_{i}
is
(F_{i},\varepsilon_{i})
-multiplicative, we have

d(\varphi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1})^{-1},\varphi_{i}(g_{1}))<\varepsilon_{i}.

Let
\tilde{S_{i}}=\{s\in S_{i}:\varphi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1})^{-1}s=\varphi_{i}(g_{1})s\}
. Then for large enough
i,

|\tilde{S}_{i}|>(1-2\varepsilon_{i})|A_{i}|.
Now for large enough
i,

\begin{split}\rho_{i}(h_{1}g_{1}h_{2}g_{2})&=\rho_{i}(h_{1}\beta_{g_{1}}(h_{2})g_{1}g_{2})\\
&=\left(\bigoplus_{s\in\tilde{S}_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(h_{1}\beta_{g_{1}}(h_{2}))\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus\tilde{S}_{i}}1_{H_{i}}\right)\cdot\varphi_{i}(g_{1}g_{2})\\
&=\left(\bigoplus_{s\in\tilde{S}_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(h_{1})P^{i}_{s}(\beta_{g_{1}}(h_{2}))\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus\tilde{S}_{i}}1_{H_{i}}\right)\cdot\varphi_{i}(g_{1}g_{2})\end{split}

We now claim that
P^{i}_{s}(\beta_{g_{1}}(h_{2}))=P^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1}s}(h_{2})
for all
s\in\tilde{S}_{i}\cap\varphi_{i}(g_{1})\tilde{S}_{i}
and for all
i
sufficiently large. Since
h\in*^{\Gamma}H,
we may write
h_{2}=h_{v_{1}}\cdots h_{v_{n}}
where
h_{v_{k}}\in H_{v_{k}}
, and
H_{v_{k}}
is the copy of
H
at the vertex
v_{k}\in V.
For
i
large enough,
\alpha(g_{1})\operatorname{supp}(h_{2})
and
\operatorname{supp}(h_{2})
are both contained in
W_{i},
so that
p_{i}(\beta(g_{1})h_{2})=\beta(g_{1})h_{2}
and
p_{i}(h_{2})=h_{2}.

Since
q_{s}^{i}
is a homomorphism, it suffices to consider
h_{2}\in H_{v}
for some
v\in W_{i}\cap\alpha(g_{1}^{-1})W_{i}.
We compute that
P_{s}^{i}(\beta(g_{1})h_{2})=q_{s}^{i}(\beta(g_{1})h_{2})=h^{\prime}\in H_{\pi_{s}^{i}(\alpha(g_{1})v)}
, where
h^{\prime}
is the element in
H_{\pi_{s}^{i}(\alpha(g_{1})v)}
corresponding to
h_{2}
via our isomorphism of
H_{v}
and
H_{\pi_{s}^{i}(\alpha(g_{1})v)}
.

On the other hand,
P^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1})s}(h_{2})=q^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1})s}(h_{2})=h^{\prime\prime}\in H_{\pi^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1})s}}(v)
where again
h^{\prime\prime}
corresponds to
h_{2}
via the isomorphism of all copies of
H.
But for our choices of
s
and
i,
we have that
\pi^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1})s}(v)=\pi^{i}_{s}(\alpha(g_{1})v).
Therefore we actually have
h^{\prime}=h^{\prime\prime}
and hence we have shown our claim.

So,

\rho_{i}(h_{1}g_{1}h_{2}g_{2})=\left(\bigoplus_{s\in\tilde{S}_{i}\cap\varphi_{i}(g_{1})\tilde{S}_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(h_{1})P^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g_{1}^{-1})s}(h_{2})\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus(\tilde{S}_{i}\cap\varphi_{i}(g_{1})\tilde{S}_{i})}\kappa_{a}\right)\cdot\varphi_{i}(g_{1}g_{2})

for some
\kappa_{a}\in H_{i}
. On the other hand,

\begin{split}\rho_{i}(h_{1}g_{1})\rho_{i}(h_{2}g_{2})&=\left(\bigoplus_{s\in S_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(h_{1})\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus S_{i}}1_{H_{i}}\right)\cdot\varphi_{i}(g_{1})\cdot(\bigoplus_{s\in S_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(h_{2})\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus S_{i}}1_{H_{i}})\cdot\varphi_{i}(g_{2})\\
&=\left(\bigoplus_{s\in\tilde{S}_{i}\cap\varphi_{i}(g_{1})\tilde{S}_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(h_{1})P^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g_{1})^{-1}s}(h_{2})\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus(\tilde{S}_{i}\cap\varphi_{i}(g_{1})\tilde{S}_{i})}\lambda_{a}\right)\cdot\varphi_{i}(g_{1})\varphi_{i}(g_{2})\end{split}

for some
\lambda_{a}\in H_{i}
. Thus, for large enough
i
,

\begin{split}d_{H_{i},A_{i}}(\rho_{i}(h_{1}g_{1}h_{2}g_{2}),\rho_{i}(h_{1}g_{1})\rho_{i}(h_{2}g_{2}))&\leq d(\varphi_{i}(g_{1}g_{2}),\varphi_{i}(g_{1})\varphi_{i}(g_{2}))+\frac{|A_{i}\setminus(\tilde{S}_{i}\cap\varphi_{i}(h_{1})\tilde{S}_{i})|}{|A_{i}|}\\
&<\varepsilon_{i}+4\varepsilon_{i}\\
&=5\varepsilon_{i}\\
&\rightarrow 0\end{split}

This proves that
\rho
is a group homomorphism. Set
N=\textrm{ker}(\rho)
. Recall we defined
\iota:*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G\rightarrow(*^{\Gamma}H\rtimes G)/N\times H
by
\iota(hg)=(hgN,g)
where
h\in*^{\Gamma}H
and
g\in G
. It now suffices to prove
\iota
is injective.

Clearly,
\textrm{ker}(\iota)\subset*^{\Gamma}H
. Assume to the contrary that
\textrm{ker}(\iota)
is not trivial and let
h\in\textrm{ker}(\iota)\setminus\{1\}
. Then
\rho_{i}(h)=(\oplus_{s\in S_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(h)\bigoplus\oplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus S_{i}}1_{H_{i}})
. Since
h\neq 1
, for all
i
sufficiently large
P_{s}^{i}(h)\neq 1_{H_{i}}
for all
s\in S_{i}.
(This happens when
\operatorname{supp}(h)\subset W_{i}
.) Then we have

d_{G_{i},A_{i}}(\rho_{i}(h),1)=\frac{|S_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}=1-\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 1

In particular,
\rho(h)\neq 1
, so
h\notin N
. But then
\iota(h)\neq 1
, a contradiction. This proves the claim.

∎

The following result can be proved in a very similar way. As such, we only provide an outline of the proof here:

Theorem 2.36
.

Fix a Connes-embeddable tracial von Neumann algebra
M.
Consider a sofic action of a hyperlinear group
G\curvearrowright\Gamma=(V,E).
Then the graph wreath product
*^{\Gamma}M\rtimes G
is Connes-embeddable.

Proof sketch.

The proof follows the same outline as that of Theorem
2.35
. One first observes the analogy of Lemma
2.34
clearly holds in the von Neumann algebra setting by changing direct sums to tensor products and semidirect products with symmetric groups to tensoring with matrix algebras, i.e.,
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{i}^{\otimes A_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{|A_{i}|}(\mathbb{C})
, where
M_{i}
is a sequence of Connes-embeddable tracial von Neumann algebras and
A_{i}
is a sequence of finite sets, is Connes-embeddable. Now, again fix increasing sequences of finite subsets
F_{i}\nearrow G
and
W_{i}\nearrow V.
Fix a decreasing sequence
\varepsilon_{i}\searrow 0.
For each
i,
let
\varphi_{i}:H\to\textrm{Sym}(A_{i})
be a unital,
(F_{i},\varepsilon_{i})
-multiplicative,
(F_{i},W_{i},\varepsilon_{i})
-orbit approximation of
\alpha.
Then there exist finite graphs
B_{i}=(V_{B_{i}},E_{B_{i}})
and subsets
S_{i}\subset A_{i}
such that
|S_{i}|>(1-\varepsilon_{i})|A_{i}|
and for each
s\in S_{i}
there is a graph embedding
\pi_{s}^{i}:W_{i}\to V_{B_{i}}
such that
\pi^{i}_{\varphi_{i}(g)s}(v)=\pi_{s}^{i}(\alpha(g^{-1})v)
for all
s\in S_{i},

g\in F_{i},
and
v\in W_{i}
, whenever
\varphi_{i}(g)s\in S_{i}
and
\alpha(g^{-1})v\in W_{i}.

Denote
\Gamma_{i}=(W_{i},E|_{W_{i}\times W_{i}}).
Let
M_{i}=*^{B_{i}}M
, which is Connes-embeddable since
M
is. Let
E_{i}:*^{\Gamma}M\to*^{\Gamma_{i}}M
be the conditional expectation and let
q^{i}_{s}:*^{\Gamma_{i}}M\hookrightarrow*^{B_{i}}M=M_{i}
be the embedding induced by the graph embedding
\pi_{s}^{i}:\Gamma_{i}\hookrightarrow B_{i}.
Let
P^{i}_{s}=q^{i}_{s}\circ E_{i}.
Then there is an embedding
\iota:*^{\Gamma}M\rtimes G\to(\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{i}^{\otimes A_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{|A_{i}|}(\mathbb{C}))\otimes L(G)
defined by,

\iota(xg)=[(\bigoplus_{s\in S_{i}}P^{i}_{s}(x)\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A_{i}\setminus S_{i}}0)\cdot\varphi_{i}(g)]_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\otimes g

where we regard
\varphi_{i}(g)\in\textrm{Sym}(A_{i})
as a permutation matrix in
\mathbb{M}_{|A_{i}|}(\mathbb{C})
. That this is a well-defined embedding can be proved by the same methods as in the proof of Theorem
2.35
. As both
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}M_{i}^{\otimes A_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{M}_{|A_{i}|}(\mathbb{C})
and
L(G)
are Connes-embeddable, this implies
*^{\Gamma}M\rtimes G
is Connes-embeddable.
∎

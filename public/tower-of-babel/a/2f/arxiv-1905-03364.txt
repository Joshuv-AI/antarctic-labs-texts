Gaudin model modulo p p , Tango structures, and dormant Miura opers
Yasuhiro Wakabayashi

Abstract
. In the present paper, we study the Bethe ansatz equations for Gaudin model and Miura opers in characteristic p > 0 p>0 . Our study is based on a work by E. Frenkel, in which solutions to the Bethe ansatz equations are described in terms of Miura opers on the complex projective line. The main result of the present paper provides a positive characteristic analogue of this description. We pay particular attention to the case of Miura PGL 2 \mathrm{PGL}_{2} -opers because dormant generic Miura PGL 2 \mathrm{PGL}_{2} -opers correspond bijectively to Tango structures, which bring various sorts of exotic phenomena in positive characteristic, e.g., counter-examples to the Kodaira vanishing theorem. As a consequence, we construct new examples of Tango structures by means of solutions to the Bethe ansatz equations modulo p p .

Introduction

The
Bethe ansatz equations
for a simple finite-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
(cf.
[
Fre1
, § 3.2, (3.5)]
) are the system of equations on the set of distinct complex numbers
z_{1},\cdots,z_{m}
(
m\geq 1
) of the form

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{\langle\alpha_{j},\check{\lambda}_{i}\rangle}{z_{j}-x_{i}}-\sum_{s\neq j}\frac{\langle\alpha_{j},\check{\alpha}_{s}\rangle}{z_{j}-z_{s}}=0\hskip 28.45274pt\left(j=1,\cdots,m\right),

where

\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m}
are simple positive roots,

\check{\alpha}_{1},\cdots,\check{\alpha}_{m}
are the corresponding coroots,

x_{1},\cdots,x_{r}
(
r\geq 0
) are distinct complex numbers,
and
\check{\lambda}_{1},\cdots,\check{\lambda}_{r}
are dominant coweights of
\mathfrak{g}
.
It is well-known that each solution to the Bethe ansatz equations (
1
)
specifies
an eigenvector of the Hamiltonian of
a certain spin model called the
Gaudin model
.
It gives an effective way to solve the problem of simultaneous diagonalization of the Gaudin Hamiltonian.

On the other hand, the Bethe Ansatz equations may also be interpreted as a “monodromy-free” condition on Miura opers.
A
Miura oper
(cf. §
1.3
) is, by definition, an oper equipped with additional data, or more precisely,
a principal bundle over an algebraic curve equipped with two Borel reductions and a flat connection satisfying some conditions (including a certain transversality condition).
For example,
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers and Miura
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers on a complex algebraic curve
may be identified with, respectively, projective and affine structures on the associated Riemann surface.

In a work by E. Frenkel (cf.
[
Fre1
]
and
[
Fre2
]
),
a canonical correspondence between solutions to the Bethe ansatz equations and certain Miura opers with trivial monodromy on the complex projective line
was constructed.
This may be regarded as an example of the geometric Langlands correspondence for the projective line.

The present paper discusses Frenkel’s correspondence

in characteristic
p
, where
p
is a prime number.
(The previous study of the Bethe ansatz equations in positive characteristic can be found in
[
Var
]
.)
Note that we cannot adopt directly the results and their proofs in
[
Fre1
]
or
[
Fre2
]

because some of them are based on properties and techniques inherent in characteristic
0
.
For example, on a flat connection in characteristic
p
, the condition of having trivial monodromy is not sufficient to conclude the local triviality of that connection.

Taking this into account,
we establish a positive-characteristic version of Frenkel’s correspondence
(cf. Theorem
A
below), asserting that mod
p
solutions to the Bethe ansatz equations of certain types
correspond bijectively to
generic Miura
opers on a pointed projective line with conditions imposed on their
p
-curvatures.
(For the previous works concerning such Miura opers, we refer to
[
Wak1
]
and
[
Wak4
]
.)

In what follows, we shall describe the main theorems in the present paper.
Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
,
G
a connected simple algebraic group over
k
of adjoint type satisfying the condition
(*)_{G}
introduced in §
1.1
.
Let
{\bf x}:=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{r+1})
(
r\geq 0
) be an ordered collection of
r+1
distinct closed points of the projective line
\mathbb{P}
over
k
with
x_{r+1}=\infty
.
Also, let
\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}:=(\check{\lambda}_{1},\cdots,\check{\lambda}_{r+1})
be an element of
(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{F})^{r+1}
(cf. (
11
) for the definition of
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{F}
) and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}:=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m})
an element of
\Gamma^{m}
, where
m\geq 1
and
\Gamma
denotes the set of simple positive roots with respect to a fixed pair of a maximal torus and a Borel subgroup of
G
.
Suppose that these collections of data satisfy the following equality

(2)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}\check{\lambda}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\check{\alpha}_{j}=2\check{\rho}

(cf. (
45
)), where
\check{\rho}
denotes the sum of the fundamental coweights of simple positive roots.

Denote by
C^{m}({\bf x})
(cf. (
28
)) the set of ordered collections of
m
distinct closed points in
\mathbb{P}\setminus\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{r+1}\}
.
Also, denote by

(3)

\displaystyle G\text{-}\mathrm{BA}_{\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}

(cf. (
47
)) the subset of
C^{m}({\bf x})
consisting of elements
{\bf z}:=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{m})
satisfying the Bethe ansatz equations (
1
) (considered as a system of equations with coefficients in
k
) associated to the fixed triple
({\bf x},\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\boldsymbol{\alpha})
.
On the other hand, we have a certain set

(4)

\displaystyle G\text{-}\mathrm{MOp}(\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf x}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}};-\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}-\check{\rho})_{\mathrm{triv},+\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{W}}^{{}^{p\text{-}\mathrm{nilp}}}\ \left(\text{resp.,}\ G\text{-}\mathrm{MOp}(\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf x}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}};-\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}-\check{\rho})_{\mathrm{gen},+\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{W}}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\right)

consisting of
p
-nilpotent generic Miura
G
-opers satisfying a monodromy-free condition (resp., dormant generic
G
-opers) on
\mathbb{P}
equipped with a log structure;
the precise definition of this set can be seen in (
73
) (resp., (
38
)).
Since any connection with vanishing
p
-curvature is locally trivial, the dormancy condition is stronger than the monodromy-free condition.
This implies that there exists a canonical inclusion

(5)

\displaystyle G\text{-}\mathrm{MOp}(\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf x}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}};-\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}-\check{\rho})_{\mathrm{gen},+\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{W}}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}\hookrightarrow G\text{-}\mathrm{MOp}(\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf x}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}};-\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}-\check{\rho})_{\mathrm{triv},+\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{W}}^{{}^{p\text{-}\mathrm{nilp}}}

(cf. Propositions
2.2
, (i), and
2.9
).

Then, the following theorem
tells us relationships between these sets of Miura
G
-opers and the solutions to the Bethe ansatz equations modulo

.

Theorem A

(cf. Theorem
2.5
, Proposition
2.9
)
.

(i)

There exists a canonical bijection of sets

(6)

\displaystyle G\text{-}\mathrm{BA}_{\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}G\text{-}\mathrm{MOp}(\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf x}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}};-\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}-\check{\rho})_{\mathrm{triv},+\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{W}}^{{}^{p\text{-}\mathrm{nilp}}}.

(ii)

If,
\check{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=0^{r+1}\left(:=(0,0,\cdots,0)\right)
, then the injection (
5
) becomes bijective. In particular,
we obtain a bijection of sets

(7)

\displaystyle G\text{-}\mathrm{BA}_{0^{r+1},\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}G\text{-}\mathrm{MOp}(\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf x}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}};(-\check{\rho})^{r+1})_{\mathrm{gen},+\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{W}}^{{}^{\mathrm{Zzz...}}}.

The above theorem for
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
provides
an application to the problem of constructing (pre-)Tango structures.
A
(pre-)Tango structure
is a certain line bundle on an algebraic curve (cf. Definition
3.1
, (i), or Definition
3.3
for the precise definition) and has an important feature in that
it brings various sorts of exotic phenomena in positive characteristic.
For example, each Tango structure on a proper smooth curve in characteristic
p
associates an algebraic surface violating the Kodaira vanishing theorem, as well as other reasonable theorems valid in characteristic
0
(cf.
[
Wak1
, § 7.2]
for the construction of such surfaces using Tango structures).
To the author’s knowledge,
there are very few previous examples of Tango structures described explicitly.
In fact, the existence of a Tango structure implies a strong restriction on the genus
g
of the underlying curve, i.e.,
p
must divide
2g-2
.

However, by combining Theorem
A
with the correspondence between Tango structures and dormant generic Miura
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers proved in
[
Wak1
]
,
we can construct (cf. Theorem
B
below) infinitely many examples of Tango curves, i.e., algebraic curves admitting a Tango structure (cf. Definition
3.1
, (ii)).
The well-known examples given
by M. Raynaud (cf.
[
Ray
, Example]
or
[
Muk
, Example 1.3]
)
may be thought of as special cases
of our construction, and we obtain
other new Tango curves.

Theorem B

(cf. Theorem
3.9
)
.

Suppose that
r=0
and that we are given
a pair of positive integers
(a,b)

with
\mathrm{gcd}(a,bp-1)=1
and
m=ap
.
Let
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{ap})
be a solution to the Bethe ansatz equation classified by
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}\text{-}\mathrm{BA}_{0,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
.
Denote by
Y

the desingularization of the plane curve defined by the equation

(8)

\displaystyle y^{bp-1}=\prod_{j=1}^{ap}(x-z_{j}).

Then,
Y

is a Tango curve.

1.
Opers and Miura opers

In this section, we recall the definitions of an oper and a (generic) Miura oper.

1.1.
Algebraic groups and Lie algebras

Throughout the present paper, let us fix a prime
p
and an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p
.
Also, let
G
be
a connected simple algebraic group over
k
of adjoint type satisfying
the
condition
(*)_{G}
described as follows.

(*)_{G}
:

is either equal to
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}

1<n<p

, where

denotes the Coxeter number of

Let us fix a maximal torus

of

and
a Borel subgroup
B

of
G
containing
T
.
Write

, i.e., the unipotent radical of
B
, and write

for the Weyl group of
(G,T)

Denote by
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{b}
,
\mathfrak{n}
, and
\mathfrak{t}

G
,

, and

).
Denote by

\Phi^{+}
the set of positive roots in
B
with respect to

and by
\Phi^{-}
the set of negative roots.
Also, denote by
\Gamma
(
\subseteq\Phi^{+}
) the set of simple positive roots.
Given each character
\varphi:T\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
(resp., each cocharacter
\check{\varphi}:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow T
), we shall use, by abuse of notation, the same notation

(resp.,
\check{\varphi}
) to denote its differential
d\varphi\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
(resp.,
d\check{\varphi}\in\mathfrak{t}
).

For each

, we write

(9)

\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}:=\big\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\ \big|\ \text{$\mathrm{ad}(t)(x)=\alpha(t)\cdot x$ for all $t\in T$}\big\}.

Each
\mathfrak{g}^{-\alpha}
(
\alpha\in\Gamma
)
may be thought of as a subspace of
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{b}
closed under the adjoint

-action.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is equipped with the principal gradation
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=-\mathrm{rk}(\mathfrak{g})}^{\mathrm{rk}(\mathfrak{g})}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, which
restricts to identifications

,
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}
,
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\mathfrak{g}^{-\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
, and

.

We shall write

\check{\rho}
for the

(as well as its differential)
defined as the sum
\sum_{\alpha\in\Gamma}\check{\omega}_{\alpha}
such that each
\check{\omega}_{\alpha}
(
\alpha\in\Gamma
) denotes the fundamental coweight of

.
We fix a generator

of

\alpha\in\Gamma
.
Hence, we obtain

.

If

denotes the set of regular elements in

, then
it follows from
[

, Chap. VI, Theorem 7.2]
that

(10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{reg}}=\left\{\check{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{t}\ |\ \alpha(\check{\lambda})\neq 0\ \text{for any root $\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\cup\Phi^{-}$}\right\}.

We obtain the set of
F

in
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{reg}}
, where

denotes the Frobenius endomorphism of
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{reg}}
viewed as a

-scheme, and
write

(11)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{dom}}^{F}:=\left\{\check{\lambda}\in\mathfrak{t}\,\big|\,\check{\lambda}+\check{\rho}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathrm{red}}^{F}\right\}.

Proposition 1.1
.

The maps of sets

(16)

\displaystyle G\text{-}\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow G\text{-}\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{D}^{\times}),\hskip 28.45274ptG\text{-}\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{D}^{\mathrm{log}};\chi(-\check{\rho}))\rightarrow G\text{-}\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{D}^{\times})

and the resulting injection

(17)

\displaystyle G\text{-}\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{D})\hookrightarrow G\text{-}\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{D}^{\mathrm{log}};\chi(-\check{\rho}))

\check{\rho}\circ t\in T(k(\!(t)\!))
(for any uniformizer

Proof.

To prove the former assertion, we only consider the injectivity of

G\text{-}\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow G\text{-}\mathrm{Op}(\mathbb{D}^{\times})

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\spadesuit}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\spadesuit}_{2}
be

-opers on
\mathbb{D}
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\spadesuit}_{1}\cong\mathcal{E}^{\spadesuit}_{2}

\mathbb{D}^{\times}

After choosing a uniformizar
t
of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}
and apply a suitable gauge transformation, we can express the connection
\nabla_{l}
defining
\mathcal{E}^{\spadesuit}_{i}
(for each

) as

(18)

\displaystyle\nabla_{l}=\partial_{t}+p_{-1}+{\bf u}_{l}(t)

for some
{\bf u}_{l}(t)\in\mathfrak{b}[\![t]\!]
.
By assumption, there exists an element

satisfying the equality

\nabla_{2}=(\nabla_{1})_{b}
, where

denotes the connection obtained from

by carrying out the gauge transformation by

The problem is reduced to proving that

Suppose, on the contrary, that

.
Since the entry lying in
\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\left(\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\right)
of
\nabla_{1}

is the same as that of
\nabla_{2}

(i.e., coincides with

),

.
By the assumption

, we have the exponential map
\mathrm{exp}:\mathfrak{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}N

[

In particular,

for some
{\bf v}(t)\in\mathfrak{n}(\!(t)\!)
.
It follows from
[
Wak2
, Corollary 1.34]
that the equality
\nabla_{2}=(\nabla_{1})_{b}

.
In particular, the pole order of

is

.
But, by comparing the respective pole orders of the both sides of (

, which contradicts the inequality

induced by
the assumption
(*)_{G}
.
Therefore,

must be contained in
\mathfrak{n}[\![t]\!]
, and hence,

\nabla^{\prime}
forms a connection on
\mathbb{D}\times G
.
It follows that

\langle\alpha,{\bf u}(t)\rangle=0
, as desired.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Tango structures

In this section, we recall the notion of a Tango structure and consider a bijective correspondence between Tango structures and solutions to the Bethe ansatz equations for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
.
Also, by means of this correspondence,
we construct new examples of Tango structures.
Hereinafter, we suppose that

.

3.1.
Tango structures

Let

be a connected
proper
smooth curve over

Denote by

the Frobenius twist of

k

Also, denote by

relative to
k

The direct image

of

of rank

The sheaf

may be considered, via the underlying homeomorphism of

of rank

Now, let
\mathcal{L}
be a line subbundle of
\mathcal{B}_{X/k}

Consider the
\mathcal{O}_{X^{(1)}}
-linear composite

(78)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\hookrightarrow F_{*}(\mathcal{B}_{X/k})\hookrightarrow F_{*}(\Omega_{X/k}),

where the first arrow denotes the natural inclusion and the second arrow denotes the morphism obtained by applying the functor

to the natural inclusion

This composite corresponds to a morphism

(79)

\displaystyle\xi_{\mathcal{L}}:F^{*}(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\Omega_{X/k}

Definition 3.1
.

(i)

We shall say that

on

is an isomorphism.

(ii)

A

is a connected proper smooth curve over

Remark 3.2

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line subbundle of
\mathcal{B}_{X/k}

Since

In particular,

admits no Tango structure when

Y. Hoshi proved that there exists a Tango curve of genus

if and only if

(cf.
[
Hos

Next, we shall recall the definition of a pre-Tango structure in the sense of
[

, Definition 5.3.1]

Let

.
(We take

if

If there is no fear of confusion, we shall write

instead of

, where

The data

induces, via base-change, a collection of closed points

in

, which determines a log structure on

; we denote the resulting log scheme by

Moreover, let

(80)

\displaystyle C_{X^{\mathrm{log}}/k}:F_{*}(\Omega_{X^{\mathrm{log}}/k})\rightarrow\Omega_{X^{(1)\mathrm{log}}/k}

of

To be precise,

is a unique

-linear morphism
whose composite with the injection

Definition 3.3

is a

-log connection

Remark 3.4
.

According to
[

, Proposition 5.3.2]
,

in the above sense correspond bijectively to Tango structures on

Indeed, let
\nabla

The
\mathcal{O}_{X^{(1)}}

is

the

Namely,

.
Moreover,
the condition that

Conversely, let
\mathcal{L}
be a Tango structure on

.
The line bundle

has uniquely a connection determined by the condition that the sections in

if

whose monodromy operator

for every

.

Write
\boldsymbol{l}^{\prime}:=(l_{q(1)}R_{1},\cdots,l_{q(s)}R_{s})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{s}
.
Then, the assignment
\nabla\mapsto\pi^{*}(\nabla)
discussed above defines an injection

(87)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tang}(X;{\bf x};\boldsymbol{l})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Tang}(Y;{\bf y};\boldsymbol{l}^{\prime}).

Proof.

Since the injectivity may be immediately verified, it suffices to prove that the monodromy operator of the pre-Tango structure
\pi^{*}(\nabla)
at
y_{j}
(where
\nabla\in\mathrm{Tang}(X;{\bf x};\boldsymbol{l})
,
j\in\{1,\cdots,s\}
) is
l_{q(j)}R_{j}
.
For simplicity, we write
x:=x_{q(j)}
,
y:=y_{j}
,
R:=R_{j}
, and
l:=l_{q(j)}
.
The formally local description of
\pi
at
y
may be given by
\mathbb{D}_{y}:=\mathrm{Spec}(k[\![t^{1/R}]\!])\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(k[\![t]\!]):=\mathbb{D}_{x}
corresponding to the natural inclusion
k[\![t]\!]\hookrightarrow k[\![t^{1/R}]\!]
, where
t
denotes a formal coordinate in
X
at
x
.
By a suitable trivialization
\Omega_{X^{D_{{\bf x}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k}|_{\mathbb{D}_{x}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{D}_{x}}
,
the restriction
\nabla|_{\mathbb{D}_{x}}
of
\nabla
to
\mathbb{D}_{x}

is expressed as
\nabla=d+l\cdot\frac{dt}{t}
(cf.
[
Oss
, Corollary 2.10]
).
Since

(88)

\displaystyle\frac{dt}{t}=\frac{d(t^{1/R})^{R}}{(t^{1/R})^{R}}=\frac{R\cdot(t^{1/R})^{R-1}\cdot dt^{1/R}}{(t^{1/R})^{R}}=R\cdot\frac{dt^{1/R}}{t^{1/R}},

the restriction
\pi^{*}(\nabla)|_{\mathbb{D}_{y}}
of
\pi^{*}(\nabla)
to
\mathbb{D}_{y}
satisfies
\pi^{*}(\nabla)|_{\mathbb{D}_{y}}=d+lR\cdot\frac{dt^{1/R}}{t^{1/R}}
.
It follows that the monodromy operator of
\pi^{*}(\nabla)
at
y
coincides with
lR
.
This completes the proof of the assertion.
∎

Example 3.8
.

We focus on the case where
X=\mathbb{P}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{l}:=(l_{1},\cdots,l_{r+1})
(

) be an element of

and
{\bf x}:=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{r+1})

r+1
closed points of
\mathbb{P}
with

.

(89)

\displaystyle y^{n}=a\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}(x-x_{i}),

Write
{\bf y}:=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{r+r^{\prime}})
.
According to
[

, the ramification index of

is given by
\frac{(n-1)(r-1)}{2}-\frac{\mathrm{gcd}(n,r)-1}{2}

Now, let us fix

3.3.
Examples of Tango structures

Let us show that
the solutions of the Bethe ansatz equations of a certain type come from
dormant Miura
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
-opers.

To this end, we apply the bijective correspondence (
56
) in the case of
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
and
p\geq 3

(i.e., the condition
(*)_{\mathrm{PGL}_{2}}
is satisfied).
Recall that

\check{\rho}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}
,
\Gamma=\{\alpha\}
, and
\check{\alpha}=2\check{\rho}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
.
If
r=0
, then the equality (
45
) holds if and only if
m\equiv 1
mod
p
.
Now, let us take a positive integer
l
(and consider the case of
m=lp+1
).
We combine Theorems
2.5
,
3.6
, and Proposition
3.5
, (i), to obtain the following composite bijection

(92)

\displaystyle\mathrm{PGL}_{2}\text{-}\mathrm{BA}_{0,\alpha^{lp+1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\coprod_{{\bf z}\in C^{lp+1}(\infty)}\mathrm{PGL}_{2}\text{-}\mathrm{MOp}(\mathbb{P}^{D_{(\infty,{\bf z})}};(-\check{\rho},\check{\rho}^{lp+1}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\coprod_{{\bf z}\in C^{lp+1}(\infty)}\mathrm{Tang}(\mathbb{P};(\infty,{\bf z});(-1,1^{lp+1}))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\coprod_{{\bf z}\in C^{lp+1}(\infty)}\mathrm{Tang}(\mathbb{P};{\bf z};1^{lp+1}).

In particular, it follows from the discussion in Remark
2.4
that

(\mathbb{P},{\bf z})
admits a pre-Tango structure whose monodromy operators are given by
1^{lp+1}
if and only if
the equality
f^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
holds, where
f(x):=\prod_{i=1}^{lp+1}(x-z_{i})
.

Next, let
a
,
b
be positive integers with
\mathrm{gcd}(a,bp-1)=1

and
h(x)\left(\in k[x]\right)

a monic polynomial of degree
ap
with

\mathrm{gcd}(h(x),h^{\prime}(x))=1
.
Denote by

(93)

\displaystyle Y

the smooth curve defined by
the equation

(94)

\displaystyle y^{bp-1}=h(x).

(If
a>b
, then the point at infinity is singular, and hence, we need to replace
this plane curve
by its desingularization to obtain a smooth curve.)
Since
\mathrm{gcd}(ap,bp-1)=\mathrm{gcd}(a,bp-1)=1
, there is only one point
\infty_{Y}
at infinity in
Y
(cf.
[
Sti
, Proposition 6.3.1]
).
Here, let us take
an ordered collection
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{ap})
of elements of
k
(i.e., closed points in
\mathrm{Spec}(k[x])=\mathbb{P}\setminus\{\infty\}
)
satisfying the equality
h(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{ap}(x-z_{i})
.
The assumption
\mathrm{gcd}(h(x),h^{\prime}(x))=1
implies that
z_{i}\neq z_{i^{\prime}}
if
i\neq i^{\prime}
.
Denote by
\pi:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}
the natural projection
(x,y)\mapsto x
, which is tamely ramified.
Also, denote by
{\bf y}:=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{ap+1})
, where
{\bf z}:=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{ap+1})
and
z_{ap+1}:=\infty
, the ordered collection of distinct points in
Y
determined uniquely by
\pi(y_{i})=z_{i}
for any
i=1,\cdots,ap+1
.
Moreover, denote by
\nabla
the
D_{{\bf z}}
-log connection on
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf z}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k}
defined as

(95)

\displaystyle\nabla=\partial_{x}+\sum_{i=1}^{ap}\frac{1}{x-z_{i}}

under the identification
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf z}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k}|_{\mathbb{P}\setminus\{\infty\}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}\setminus\{\infty\}}
given by
dx\mapsto 1
.
By passing to the isomorphism
\pi^{*}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{D_{{\bf z}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\Omega_{Y^{D_{{\bf y}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k}
induced by
\pi
,
we obtain a
D_{{\bf y}}
-log connection

(96)

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\nabla):\Omega_{Y^{D_{{\bf y}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k}\rightarrow\Omega_{Y^{D_{{\bf y}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k}^{\otimes 2}

on
\Omega_{Y^{D_{{\bf y}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k}

defined to be the pull-back of
\nabla
.
In particular, we obtain an
\mathcal{O}_{Y^{(1)}}
-submodule

(97)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ker}(\pi^{*}(\nabla))

of
F_{Y/k*}(\Omega_{Y^{D_{{\bf y}}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k})
, where
F_{Y/k}:Y\rightarrow Y^{(1)}
denotes the relative Frobenius morphism of
Y
relative to
k
.
Then, the following assertion, i.e., Theorem
B
, holds.

Theorem 3.9
.

Suppose that
h^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
, or equivalently,
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{ap})
specifies an element of
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}\text{-}\mathrm{BA}_{0,\alpha^{ap}}
.
Then,
\mathrm{Ker}(\pi^{*}(\nabla))
forms a Tango structure on
Y
.
In particular,
Y
is a Tango curve.

Proof.

Let us fix an element
\gamma\in k
with
h(\gamma)\neq 0
.
Consider the automorphism
\iota
of
\mathbb{P}
given by
x\mapsto\frac{1}{x-\gamma}
.
Then,
\iota({\bf z}):=(\iota(z_{1}),\cdots,\iota(z_{ap+1})))
are a collection of distinct points in
\mathbb{P}\setminus\{\infty\}
.
Since
f(x):=\prod_{i=1}^{ap+1}(x-\iota(z_{i}))
coincides with
h(\gamma)^{-1}\cdot h(\frac{1}{x}+\gamma)\cdot x^{ap+1}
,
we see that
\mathrm{gcd}(f(x),f^{\prime}(x))=1
and
f^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
.
It follows from Theorem
A

that
the
D_{\iota({\bf z})}
-log connection on
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{D_{\iota({\bf z})}\text{-}\mathrm{log}}/k}
determined by
\partial_{x}+\sum_{i=1}^{ap+1}\frac{1}{x-\iota(z_{i})}
forms a pre-Tango structure on
(\mathbb{P},\iota({\bf z}))
.
By pulling-back via
\iota
,
we obtain a pre-Tango structure on
(\mathbb{P},{\bf z})

whose monodromy operators are
1^{ap+1}
;
by construction, it coincides with
\nabla
.
Hence, (since
bp-1\equiv-1
mod
p
)
the pull-back
\pi^{*}(\nabla)
forms a pre-Tango structure on
(Z,{\bf z})
whose monodromy operators are given by
(-1)^{ap+1}
(cf. Proposition
3.7
).
It follows from Proposition

3.5
(and its proof) that

\mathrm{Ker}(\pi^{*}(\nabla))
specifies a Tango structure.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

A well-known example of a Tango structures can be found in some literature (cf., e.g.,
[
Ray
, Example]
and
[
Muk
, Example 1.3]
).
This is constructed as follows.
Let
l
be an integer with
lp\geq 4
.
Also, let
f(x)
be a polynomial of degree
l
in one variable
x
and let
Y

be the plane curve defined by

(98)

\displaystyle y^{lp-1}=f(x^{p})-x.

One verifies that
Y
is a smooth curve having only one point
\infty
at infinity and
\Omega_{Y/k}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(lp(lp-3)\cdot\infty)
.
Then, the base-change of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(l(lp-3)\cdot\infty)
via the absolute Frobenius morphism of
\mathrm{Spec}(k)

turns out to specify a Tango structure.

Notice that
(f(x^{p})-x)^{\prime}=-1
(which implies
\mathrm{gcd}(f(x^{p})-x,(f(x^{p})-x)^{\prime})=1
) and
(f(x^{p})-x)^{\prime\prime}=0
.
Hence,

Y
is
a specific type of
Tango curves constructed in the discussion preceding Theorem
3.9
.
On the other hand, the curve defined, e.g., by the equation
y^{2p-1}=x^{2p}+x^{p+1}+ax^{p}+bx+c
with
c\neq b(a-b)
is
a Tango curve (by Theorem
3.9
), which gives
a new example.
Thus, our result allows us to obtain infinitely many new (explicit!) examples of Tango curves.

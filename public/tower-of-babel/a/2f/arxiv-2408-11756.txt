Semilinear damped wave equations with data from Sobolev spaces of negative order: the critical case in Euclidean setting and in the Heisenberg space
Marcello D’Abbicco

Abstract
. In this note, we prove the global existence of solutions to the semilinear damped wave equation in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≤ 6 n\leq 6 , with critical nonlinearity under the assumption that the initial data are small in the energy space H 1 × L 2 H^{1}\times L^{2} and under the vanishing condition that the initial data belong to H ˙ − γ \dot{H}^{-\gamma} for some γ ∈ ( 0 , n / 2 ) \gamma\in(0,n/2) . A similar result also applies to the damped wave equation in the Heisenberg group ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} , with n = 1 , 2 n=1,2 .



1.
Introduction

In this note, we prove that global-in-time solutions to the damped wave equation

(1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u_{t}=f(u),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\ t>0,\\
(u,u_{t})(0,x)=(u_{0},u_{1}),&x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\end{cases}

with small initial data in the energy space
H^{1}\times L^{2}
and in
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
exist for
f(u)=|u|^{p}
(or, more in general, for
f
as in (
3
)) in the critical case

(2)

p=1+\frac{4}{n+2\gamma},\qquad\gamma\in(0,n/2),

in space dimension
n=1,2
and, with some restrictions on
\gamma
, in space dimension
n=3,4,5,6
. This answers the question posed in the recent paper of W. Chen and M. Reissig
[
2
, Remark 5]
, who proved the global existence of small data solutions in the supercritical case,
f(u)=|u|^{q}
with
q>p
(see
[
2
, Theorem 1]
). Here

H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}=\{u\in H^{s}:{\mathrm{|\xi|}}^{-\gamma}\hat{u}\in L^{2}\},

equipped with norm

\|u\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}=\|(\langle\xi\rangle^{s}+{\mathrm{|\xi|}}^{-\gamma})\hat{u}\|_{L^{2}},

where
\hat{u}=\mathscr{F}u(t,\cdot)
is the Fourier transform with respect to
x
. The assumption of initial data in homogeneous potential spaces of negative order
\dot{H}^{-\gamma,p}
is sometimes called
vanishing condition
; it plays a crucial role in several models, as in the Boussinesq equation
[
3
]
.

.

Let

if

f(0)=0,\quad|f(u)-f(v)|\leq C\,|u-v|\big(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1}\big),

and

is as in (
2
). Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any

(4)

\begin{split}u_{0}\in H^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma},\quad u_{1}\in L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma},\quad\text{with}\\
\varepsilon=\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\leq\varepsilon_{0},\end{split}

there exists a unique solution
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{1})
to (

f
verifies
Moreover,

\begin{split}\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}&\leq C\,\varepsilon\,(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma+1}{2}},\\

Remark 1
.

By dual Sobolev’s embeddings (or Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality),
L^{m}\cap H^{s}\hookrightarrow H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
, for some
m\in(1,2)
, where

(6)

\frac{1}{m}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{n}.

Therefore, the critical exponent in (
2
) is a generalization of the second critical exponent, in the sense of Lee and Ni
[
12
]
, derived assuming
L^{m}
initial data with
m>1
. Indeed,

1+\frac{4}{n+2\gamma}=1+\frac{2m}{n}.

The global existence of small data solutions for supercritical powers
p>1+2m/n
for the damped wave equation (
1
) has been proved in
[
11
]
, while the global existence of small data solutions in the critical case
p=1+2m/n
has been proved in
[
10
]
. The nonexistence of global solutions for subcritical powers
p<1+2m/n
has been proved in
[
4
, Theorem 3]
.

We mention that when
m=1
, the critical exponent is Fujita exponent
1+2/n
, and nonexistence of global solutions holds in the critical case, on the contrary of what happens when
m\in(1,2]
, under a suitable sign assumption on the data
[
15
]
. On the other hand, there are several other models in which the critical exponent belongs to the existence range, even when
L^{1}
data are considered, see, for instance,
[
5
]
. A sharp integral condition on
f(u)
in (
1
) which determines whether global existence of small data solutions holds, or nonexistence of global solutions follows under suitable data sign assumption, has been obtained for the
L^{1}
theory in
[
8
]
.

The counterpart of nonexistence of global solution for subcritical powers is given by Theorem 3 in
[
4
]
, for the more general model of damped
\sigma
-evolution equations; in
[
4
]
it is proved that even weak solution
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to (
1
) with
f(u)=|u|^{q}
, do not exist when
q\in(1,p)
, assuming a growth condition on the data

u_{0}\equiv 0,\qquad u_{1}(x)\geq\varepsilon\,(1+|x|)^{-\mu},\quad 1<q<1+\frac{2}{\mu-1}\,;

for any given
q<p
, it is sufficient to choose

1+\frac{2}{q}<\mu<\gamma+\frac{n}{2},

to obtain the nonexistence of global solutions for any
q<p
. We stress that the growth condition with
\mu
as above is compatible with the assumption
u\in L^{m}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
, namely,
m\mu>n
, where
m
is as in (
6
). The lifespan estimate

T\leq C\,\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{2(p^{\prime}-1)-\mu}}

is also provided in
[
4
, Theorem 3]
. A variant of this result with an additional logarithmic term in the growth condition is proved in
[
2
]
; also the sharpness of the lifespan estimate is proved in
[
2
, Theorem 3]
.

H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{2p}

Moreover, with a strategy similar to the one employed in the Euclidean case, we may study the damped wave equation on the Heisenberg group
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
. Namely, we may prove the global existence of solutions to the damped wave equation

(7)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta_{H}u+u_{t}=f(u),&g\in{\mathbb{H}}^{n},\ t>0,\\

assuming small initial data in
H_{\mathcal{L}}^{1}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
and in

where
{\mathcal{Q}}=2n+2
represents the homogeneous dimension of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
,

, and
\tilde{\gamma}
is the positive solution to

2\gamma^{2}+{\mathcal{Q}}\gamma-2{\mathcal{Q}}=0.

Here

and

]
) on the Heisenberg group

is the Sobolev space of

functions

such that

, and

is the space of
H_{\mathcal{L}}^{1}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})

such that the distribution

belongs to
L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. Similarly for
L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\cap\dot{H}_{\mathcal{L}}^{-\gamma}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
. We use the notation in
[

]
for
(-{\mathcal{L}})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}
and for the Sobolev spaces

and
L^{2}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})\cap\dot{H}_{\mathcal{L}}^{-\gamma}({\mathbb{H}}^{n})
.

This answers the question in
[

whether global existence holds or not in the critical case; indeed, in

both the global existence of small data solutions in the supercritical case, and the nonexistence of global solutions in the subcritical case (under a growth assumption analogous to the one in

Proof.

For any
T>0
, let
X
be the Banach space of functions in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],H^{1})
equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{X}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}t^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\big(\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\big).

Thanks to Proposition 1 in
[
2
]
, the solution

u_{\mathrm{lin}}=K_{0}(t)\ast u_{0}+K_{1}(t)\ast u_{1}

to the linear problem (
1
) with
f=0
is in
X
and

(11)

\|u\|_{X}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon,

for some
C_{1}>0
, independent on
T
, where
\varepsilon
is as in (
4
). Thanks to Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, for any
u\in X
it holds

(12)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}}\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\|u\|_{X},

for any
q\in[2,\infty]
if
n=1
, for any
q\in[2,\infty)
if
n=2
and for any
q\in[2,2^{*}]
, where

\frac{1}{2^{*}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n},\quad\text{i.e.}\qquad 2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2},

if
n\geq 3
. We now consider the Duhamel’s integral operator

Nu(t,\cdot)=\int_{0}^{t}K_{1}(t-\tau)\ast f(u(\tau,\cdot))\,d\tau.

We claim that for any
u,v\in X
, it holds

(13)

\|Nu-Nv\|_{X}\leq C_{2}D(u,v),\qquad D(u,v)=\|u-v\|_{X}\big(\|u\|_{X}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X}^{p-1}\big),

for some
C_{2}>0
, independent on
T
. To prove (
13
), we use the well-known estimates (see Lemma 1 in
[
13
]
):

\|\nabla^{j}K_{1}(t)\ast g\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\,(1+t)^{-n\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{q}\right)-\frac{j}{2}}\,\|g\|_{L^{m}\cap L^{2}},\quad\|g\|_{L^{m}\cap L^{2}}=\|g\|_{L^{m}}+\|g\|_{L^{2}},

that hold for
m\in[1,2]
and
j=0,1
. For
j=0,1
, we get

\begin{split}&\|\nabla^{j}(Nu-Nv)(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{j}{2}}\,\|(f(u)-f(v))(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{m}\cap L^{2}}d\tau\\
&\qquad+\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|(f(u)-f(v))(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau,\end{split}

where
m=1
if
p\geq 2
or
m=2/p
otherwise (we use
m=2
in
[t/2,t]
). Due to (
3
) and (
12
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\|(f(u)-f(v))(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\qquad\leq C\,\|(u-v)(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2p}}\big(\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2p}}^{p-1}+\|v(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2p}}^{p-1}\big)\\
&\qquad\leq C^{\prime}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{4}(p-1)-\frac{\gamma}{2}p}\,D(u,v)=C^{\prime}(1+\tau)^{-1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,D(u,v),\end{split}

so that we find

\begin{split}&\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{j}{2}}\|(f(u)-f(v))(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau\\
&\qquad\leq C\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{j}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,d\tau\,D(u,v)\\
&\qquad\leq C^{\prime}(1+t)^{-1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{j}{2}}\,d\tau\,D(u,v)\\
&\qquad\leq C^{\prime\prime}\,(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma+j}{2}}\,D(u,v).\end{split}

We now consider the first integral. First, let
p\geq 2
, so that
m=1
. In this case, due to (
3
) and (
12
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\|(f(u)-f(v))(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}\\
&\qquad\leq C\,\,\|(u-v)(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\big(\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}+\|v(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\big)\\
&\qquad\leq C^{\prime}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{n}{4}(p-2)-\frac{\gamma}{2}p}D(u,v)=C^{\prime}(1+\tau)^{\frac{n}{4}-1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}D(u,v),\end{split}

so that we get

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{j}{2}}\,\big(\|(f(u)-f(v))(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{2}}\big)d\tau\\
&\qquad\leq C\,(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}-\frac{j}{2}}\,\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+\tau)^{\frac{n}{4}-1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,d\tau\,D(u,v)\\
&\qquad\leq C^{\prime}\,(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma+j}{2}}\,D(u,v).\end{split}

where we used that

\frac{n}{4}-1-\frac{\gamma}{2}>-1,

as a consequence of
\gamma<n/2
, to estimate the integral. Now let
p\leq 2
so that
m=2/p
. In this case, due to (
3
) and (
12
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\|(f(u)-f(v))(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{2}{p}}}\\
&\qquad\leq C\,\|(u-v)(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|u(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{p-1}+\|v(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{p-1}\big)\\
&\qquad\leq C^{\prime}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}p}\,D(u,v),\end{split}

so that we get

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{n}{4}(p-1)-\frac{j}{2}}\,\|f(u)(\tau,\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{2}{m}}\cap L^{2}}d\tau\\
&\qquad\leq C\,(1+t)^{-\frac{n}{4}(p-1)-\frac{j}{2}}\,\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}p}\,d\tau\,\|u\|_{X}^{p}\\
&\qquad\leq C^{\prime}\,(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma+j}{2}}\,\|u\|_{X}^{p},\end{split}

where we used that

\frac{\gamma}{2}p=\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(1+\frac{4}{n+2\gamma}\right)<1,

as a consequence of the assumption
\gamma<\tilde{\gamma}
when
n\geq 3
, to estimate the integral.

This concludes the proof of (
13
).

Let

(14)

R=2C_{1}\,\varepsilon,

where
C_{1}
is as in (
11
), and fix
\varepsilon_{0}
such that
C_{2}R^{p-1}\leq 1/4
, for any
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, where
C_{2}
is as in (
13
). Then the operator

\Lambda:u\in X\mapsto\Lambda u=u_{\mathrm{lin}}+Nu\in X

maps the closed ball of radius
R
in
X
into itself, since

\|Nu+u_{\mathrm{lin}}\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{4}\,\|u\|_{X}+\|u_{\mathrm{lin}}\|_{X}\leq R.

Moreover, the operator
\Lambda
is a contraction, due to

\|\Lambda u-\Lambda v\|_{X}=\|Nu-Nv\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\|u-v\|_{X}.

Therefore, in the closed ball of radius
R
in
X
, there exists a unique fixed point
u
for the operator
\Lambda
, for sufficiently small data, i.e.,
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
as in (
4
). This fixed point is the unique solution to (
1
) in
\mathcal{C}([0,T],H^{1})
. Moreover, the fixed point verifies the estimate

(15)

\|u\|_{X}\leq R=2C_{1}\,\varepsilon.

Due to the fact that no constant depends on
T
, this argument gives the existence of a unique global-in-time solution in
\mathcal{C}([0,\infty),H^{1})
, and (
15
) provides the desired decay estimate (

). This concludes the proof.

This concludes the proof.

An extension theory for partial groups
Carles Broto and Alex Gonzalez

Abstract
. Partial groups are a natural generalization of discrete groups recently introduced by Chermak in connection with the theory of fusion systems. In this paper we develop an extension theory for partial groups based in the classical theory of fibre bundles of simplicial sets.



The notion of partial group was introduced by Chermak
[
Ch
]
as an alternative to linking
and transporter systems
[
BLO2
,
OV1
]
that might be more appealing to group theorists.
Roughly speaking, a partial group is a set with an associative product which is only defined on
certain tuples of elements and an inversion. The aim of this paper is to establish an
extension theory for partial groups that will be useful in order to understand more sophisticated
structures
[
BG2
,
Go
]
.

The extension theory of partial groups will be based on the theory
of fibre bundles of simplicial sets
[
BGM
,
Cu
,
GJ
,
M
]
.
Indeed, to a partial group one may associate a simplicial set,
built as a bar construction (cf. Example
1.6
)
which in turn determines explicitly the original partial group.
Simplicial sets obtained in this way are
easily characterized. For this reason we have found it appropriate for this paper
to define a partial group as a simplicial set
with a unique vertex and higher simplices uniquely determined by their spines
(see Definition
2.1
for details). Likewise, homomorphisms between partial groups are arbitrary
simplicial maps.
It becomes clear that the bar construction provides an
equivalence between this definition and Chermak’s definition
[
Ch
, 2.1]
.

Given a partial group
𝕄
we identify two characteristic subgroups,
N(\text{\bbfamily M})
and
Z(\text{\bbfamily M})
. The first
consists of all elements that define inner automorphisms of
𝕄
, while
Z(\text{\bbfamily M})
is the subgroup of those
for which the given inner automorphism is the identity map.
Z(\text{\bbfamily M})
is the center
of
𝕄
and we call
N(\text{\bbfamily M})
the normalizer of
𝕄
.
We identify the outer automorphisms

\operatorname{Out}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})
with the automorphisms of
𝕄
modulo

N(\text{\bbfamily M})
, and we get an exact sequence of finite groups

1\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}Z(\text{\bbfamily M})\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}N(\text{\bbfamily M})\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\operatorname{Out}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}1

(see Proposition
2.11
).
This allows the description of the simplicial monoid of self-equivalences of a partial group
𝕄
, that
we denote
\operatorname{aut}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})
. A self-equivalence is really an automorphism of
𝕄
and we obtain

\pi_{i}(\operatorname{aut}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M}))\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\operatorname{Out}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M}),&i=0\\
Z(\text{\bbfamily M}),&i=1\\
\{0\},&i\geq 2\\
\end{array}\right.

(0.1)

In this more general case, as it happens in the case of finite groups, the simplicial set of self-equivalences has at most two non-trivial
homotopy groups. This fact is the ultimate reason why the classification of extensions of partial groups resembles the classical results
for extensions of finite groups.

We define an extension of partial groups to be a fibre bundle
\text{\bbfamily M}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily E}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily H}
.
We first obtain a description
of
𝔼
in terms of
𝕄
and
ℍ
and then show that
𝔼
is indeed a partial
group if
𝕄
and
ℍ
were so (see Theorem
4.5
).
Fixing
𝕄
,
ℍ
, and an outer action of
ℍ
on
𝕄
, that is, a homomorphism

\alpha\colon\text{\bbfamily H}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\operatorname{Out}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})
,
we show that the set
\mathcal{E}(\text{\bbfamily M},\text{\bbfamily H},\alpha)
of equivalence classes of extensions is nonempty
provided a certain obstruction class
[\kappa]\in H^{3}(\text{\bbfamily H};Z(\text{\bbfamily M}))
vanishes.
Furthermore, assuming
\mathcal{E}(\text{\bbfamily M},\text{\bbfamily H},\alpha)\neq\emptyset
, the group

H^{2}(\text{\bbfamily H};Z(\text{\bbfamily M}))
acts freely and transitively on
\mathcal{E}(\text{\bbfamily M},\text{\bbfamily H},\alpha)

(see Theorem
4.11
).

We also establish a classification theorem for sections of extensions. We distinguish
between arbitrary sections and regular sections. Given an extension

\text{\bbfamily M}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily E}\overset{\tau}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily H}
, a section is called regular if it has image in
N(\text{\bbfamily E})
.
An extension that admits a regular section is called regular split. Regular split extensions are
isomorphic to semi-direct products.
We show that equivalence classes of sections of regular split extensions are classified by the first non-abelian cohomology group

H^{1}(\text{\bbfamily H};\text{\bbfamily M})
.

Organization of the paper.
In Section
2
we review some basic concepts related to simplicial sets.
Most of the work in this section is devoted to analyze homotopies between homomorphisms of partial groups,
as they play a key role in this paper. In Section 3 we analyze the space of self-homotopy equivalences of a partial
group and we prove equation (
0.1
).
Section
4
develops the theme of fibre bundles and extensions of partial groups.
Finally, in Section
5
we introduce regular split extensions of partial groups and
we prove the classification theorem for sections.

1.
Simplicial sets

This section contains a brief discussion about simplicial sets focused on our needs. We refer the interested reader to
[
M
,
GJ
]
for the general theory of simplicial sets. Let
\Delta
be the category with objects the sets
[n]=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, for
n\geq 0
. Morphisms among them are compositions of the non-decreasing maps:
d^{i}\colon[n-1]\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}[n]
,
0\leq i\leq n
, defined
d^{i}(j)=j
if
j<i
and
d^{i}(j)=j+1
if
j\geq i
, and
s^{i}\colon[n+1]\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}[n]
defined
s^{i}(j)=j
if
j\leq i
and
s^{i}(j)=j-1
if
j>i
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
simplicial set
is a functor
X\colon\Delta^{\textup{op}}\longrightarrow\EuR{Sets}
. Equivalently, a simplicial set is a sequence of sets
\{X_{n}=X([n])\}_{n\geq 0}
, together with structural maps

d_{i}=X(d^{i})\colon X_{n}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}X_{n-1}\,,\quad\mbox{and}\quad s_{i}=X(s^{i})\colon X_{n}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}X_{n+1}\,

called
face maps
and
degeneracies
respectively, satisfying the following
simplicial identities
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}d_{i}d_{j}=d_{j-1}d_{i}&i<j\\
d_{i}s_{j}=s_{j-1}d_{i}&i<j\\
d_{j}s_{j}=1=d_{j+1}s_{j}&\\
d_{i}s_{j}=s_{j}d_{i-1}&i>j+1\\
s_{i}s_{j}=s_{j+1}s_{i}&i\leq j\,.\end{array}

The elements of
X_{n}
are called the
n
-simplices
of
X
. Maps between simplicial sets are natural transformations
f\colon X\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}Y
, in other words, collections of maps
f_{n}\colon X_{n}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}Y_{n}
that commute with face maps and
degeneracies.

Example 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 0
. Associated to
[n]
there is a simplicial set
\Delta[n]
, called the
standard
n
-simplex
, which is defined as the functor
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\Delta}(-,[n])\colon\Delta^{{}^{\textup{op}}}\to\EuR{Sets}
, see
[
GJ
, Example I.1.7]
. Equivalently,
\Delta[n]
is characterized by the existence of a distinguished
n
-simplex, denoted by
\iota_{n}
(corresponding to the identity map
[n]\to[n]
), and by the fact that every other simplex in
\Delta[n]
is obtained from
\iota_{n}
by applying to it a composition of face and degeneracy operations.

For instance, the standard
1
-simplex
\Delta[1]
plays a crucial role in the definition of homotopy below. It has a unique non-degenerate
1
-simplex
\iota
and two vertices
v_{0}
and
v_{1}
obtained as

v_{1}=d_{0}(\iota)
and
v_{0}=d_{1}(\iota)
. It has the homotopy type of the unit interval. The vertices can be represented by simplicial maps

v_{0}\colon\Delta[0]\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\Delta[1]\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad v_{1}\colon\Delta[0]\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\Delta[1].

More generally, given a simplicial set
X
and
n\geq 0
, one can represent each
n
-simplex
\omega\in X_{n}
as the simplicial map
\omega\colon\Delta[n]\to X
, which maps the unique non-degenerate
n
-simplex
\iota_{n}
of
\Delta[n]
to the
n
-simplex
\omega\in X_{n}
(note that this completely determines the map
\omega
). We shall tacitly use this fact throughout the rest of the paper.

Example 1.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be simplicial sets. The
cartesian product of
X
and
Y
, denoted by
X\times Y
, is the simplicial set with
n
-simplices
(X\times Y)_{n}=X_{n}\times Y_{n}
. Face and degeneracies on
X\times Y
are defined componentwise. That is, given
(\omega,\sigma)\in(X\times Y)_{n}
and
0\leq i\leq n
, we define
d_{i}(\omega,\sigma)=(d_{i}^{X}(\omega),d_{i}^{Y}(\sigma))
and
s_{i}(\omega,\sigma)=(s_{i}^{X}(\omega),s_{i}^{Y}(\sigma))
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be simplicial sets, and let
f,g\colon Y\to X
be simplicial maps. A
homotopy from
g
to
f
is a map

F\colon Y\times\Delta[1]\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}X

such that
f=F\mathchoice{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}(\textup{Id}\times v_{0})
and
g=F\mathchoice{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}(\textup{Id}\times v_{1})
(cf.
[
GJ
, pag. 24]
).

The following operators are of special interest to us. Some definitions are borrowed from
[
BLO6
, Section 6]
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
X
be a simplicial set. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
e_{i}^{n}\colon X\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}X_{1}
be the
i
th edge map
induced by the non-decreasing function
[1]\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}[n]
with image
{i-1,i}
.

•

The
spine operator

\boldsymbol{e}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, is defined as

\boldsymbol{e}^{n}=(e^{n}_{1},e^{n}_{2},\dots,e^{n}_{n})\colon X_{n}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{1}\,.

•

The
back edge
or
product operator

\Pi^{n}
,
n\geq 1
, is the map

\Pi^{n}\colon X_{n}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}X_{1}\,,\qquad n\geq 1\,,

induced by the function
[1]\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}[n]
with image
\{0,n\}
.
Equivalently,
\Pi^{1}=\textup{Id}
and
\Pi^{n}=d_{1}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}d_{1}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}\dots\mathchoice{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}d_{1}
,
n-1
times. We simply write
\Pi=\Pi^{n}
when there is no possible
confusion about the degree
n
.

The following example illustrates and motivates the above definitions.

Example 1.6
.

The bar construction on a group
G
, denoted by
BG
, is a simplicial set with
n
-simplices

B_{n}G=\bigl\{\,{[g_{1}|g_{2}|\dots|g_{n}]}\,\bigm|\,{g_{i}\in G}\,\bigr\}=G^{n}
, face maps

d_{i}([g_{1}|\dots|g_{n}])=\begin{cases}[g_{2}|\ldots|g_{n}]&\text{$i=0$, }\\
[g_{1}|\ldots|g_{i}g_{i+1}|\ldots|g_{n}]&\text{$1\leq i\leq n-1$,}\\
[g_{1}|\ldots|g_{n-1}]&\text{$i=n$.}\end{cases}

and degeneracies

s_{i}([g_{1}|\ldots|g_{n}])=[g_{1}|\ldots|g_{i}|1|g_{i+1}|\ldots|g_{n}]\mbox{, }i=0,\ldots,n\,.

In this case, the edges operator is
\boldsymbol{e}^{n}([g_{1}|g_{2}|\dots|g_{n}])=(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n})
, while the product operator is
\Pi([g_{1}|g_{2}|\dots|g_{n}])=g_{1}g_{2}\dots g_{n}
. Thus for each simplex, the product operator is precisely the product of its front edges. Notice that in this case the edges operator is actually a bijection for every
n\geq 2
.

The concepts of homotopy groups and homology groups are defined in the setting of simplicial sets.
There is a realization functor that associates a topological space
|X|
to a simplicial set
X
. In particular,
homotopy groups and homology groups of
X
coincide with that of
|X|
, defined geometrically.
A simplicial map

is a weak equivalence if it induces isomorphisms on homotopy groups.
Details can be easily find in the literature (cf.
[
GJ
, Ch. I]
[
M
, Ch.I, II, and III]
).

Example 1.7
.

The nerve of a small category

is a simplicial set

with

and degneracies

2.
Partial groups

We now introduce the concept of partial group, from a simplicial point of view. As we show, our definition is equivalent to Chermak’s
[
Ch
, definition 2.1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
partial monoid
is a nonempty simplicial set
𝕄
satisfying

(PM1)

𝕄
is reduced (
\text{\bbfamily M}_{0}
consists of a unique vertex).

(PM2)

The spine operator
\boldsymbol{e}^{n}\colon\text{\bbfamily M}_{n}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}(\text{\bbfamily M}_{1})^{n}
is injective for all
n\geq 1
.

A
homomorphism of partial monoids
is a simplicial map.

One can easily check that the above definition is equivalent to
[
Ch
, Definition 2.1]
. Indeed, using Chermak’s notation, we can regard the partial monoid
𝕄
as having underlying set
\mathcal{M}=\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}
, the set of edges of
𝕄
, and the domain of multiplication is given by

\mathbf{D}=\{(\emptyset)\}\cup\coprod_{n\geq 1}\{\,{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\text{\bbfamily M}_{1})^{n}}\,|\,{\exists\sigma\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{n},\,,\boldsymbol{e}^{n}(\sigma)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})}\,\}\,.

The multiplication
\Pi
corresponds to the product operator of
𝕄
, and the
unit is
1=s_{0}(v)\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}
, where
v
stands for the unique vertex of
𝕄
.
Conversely, given a partial monoid defined as in
[
Ch
]
, the bar construction,
defined exactly as in Example
1.6
, is a simplicial set that satisfies conditions (PM1) and (PM2).

The above correspondence suggests the following notational conventions that we use henceforth.
For a partial monoid
𝕄
, a simplex
\boldsymbol{x}\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{n}
with spine

\boldsymbol{e}^{n}(\boldsymbol{x})=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})

is denoted

\boldsymbol{x}=[x_{1}|x_{2}|\dots|x_{n}]\,,

thus using the same notation as for the bar construction on a group
G
.
Concerning the multiplication, we write
1=s_{0}(v)
, where
v
is the vertex of
𝕄
, and

\Pi[x_{1}|x_{2}|\dots|x_{n}]=x_{1}\cdot x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}\,.

To be more precise, the symbol
x_{1}\cdot x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}
means that the simplex

[x_{1}|x_{2}|\dots|x_{n}]
exists in
𝕄
and

\Pi[x_{1}|x_{2}|\dots|x_{n}]=x_{1}\cdot x_{2}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
inversion
in a partial monoid
𝕄
is an anti-involution
\nu\colon\text{\bbfamily M}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}
(that is, a simplicial map
\nu\colon\text{\bbfamily M}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}^{\textup{op}}
with inverse
\nu^{-1}=\nu^{\textup{op}}
), satisfying the following conditions for each
\boldsymbol{u}\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
:

(I1)

There is a simplex
[\nu(\boldsymbol{u})|\boldsymbol{u}]\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{2n}
; and

(I2)

\Pi[\nu(\boldsymbol{u})|\boldsymbol{u}]=1
.

A
partial group
is a partial monoid together with an inversion. A
partial subgroup
is a simplicial subset
\text{\bbfamily H}\subseteq\text{\bbfamily M}
satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

If
\boldsymbol{x}=[x_{1}|x_{2}|\dots|x_{n}]\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{n}
, and each
[x_{i}]
is in
\text{\bbfamily H}_{1}
, then
\boldsymbol{x}\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{n}
.

(2)

The inversion and the product of
𝕄
are internal in
ℍ
.

In this case, we denote
\text{\bbfamily H}\leq\text{\bbfamily M}
. If
\text{\bbfamily H}=BG
is the classifying space of a group
G
, we simply say that
G
is a subgroup of
𝕄
, and loosely write
G\leq\text{\bbfamily M}
, instead of
BG\leq\text{\bbfamily M}
.

Continuing with our notational conventions, we won’t ususally specify the map
\nu

providing the inversion of a partial group, and instead, we write
\boldsymbol{u}^{-1}=\nu(\boldsymbol{u})
.
The simplicial identities imply that
[u_{1}|\ldots|u_{n}]^{-1}=[u_{n}^{-1}|\ldots|u_{1}^{-1}]
.

It follows from simplicial identities that if a partial monoid
𝕄
admits an inversion, then this is unique.
Also, a homomorphism of partial groups
f\colon\text{\bbfamily M}\to\text{\bbfamily H}
satisfies

f(1_{\text{\bbfamily M}})=1_{\text{\bbfamily H}}
and
f(\boldsymbol{w}^{-1})=(f(\boldsymbol{w}))^{-1}
. These properties are also shown in

[
Ch
, 2.2, 2.3]
.

Remark 2.3
.

Partial groups are not generally Kan complexes, that is, fibrant objects in the Quillen model category of simplicial sets.
It turns out that a partial group is really a group if and only if the spine operators are bijections
for all
n\geq 1
, in which case it is a Kan complex. (See
[
M
, 1§1]
[
GJ
, I.3]
.)
As a consequence, the homotopy relation between homomorphisms fails to be transitive in general.

We start here a careful analysis of homotopies between homomorphisms of partial groups and their relation with the
concept of conjugation. This leads at the end of this section to the definition of the groups of automorphisms and
outer automorphisms of partial groups.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f\colon X\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}
be a simplicial map where
𝕄
is a partial monoid. Then,
f
is determined by its restriction to the
1
-skeleton of
X
.

Proof.

Let
g\colon X\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}
be another simplicial map with
g_{1}=f_{1}
.
Since there is a unique vertex in
𝕄
, it is also clear that
g_{0}=f_{0}
.
Assume
n\geq 2
and let
\omega\in X_{n}
be an arbitrary
n
-simplex with

\boldsymbol{e}(\omega)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{1}
.
Since both
f
and
g
are simplicial, we have

\boldsymbol{e}^{n}(f_{n}(\omega))=(f_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,f_{1}(x_{n}))=(g_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,g_{1}(x_{n}))=\boldsymbol{e}^{n}(g_{n}(\omega))\,,

hence
f_{n}(\omega)=g_{n}(\omega)
by (PM2), and

.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be two homomorphisms of partial monoids. Then, the following holds.

(a)

A homotopy

In addition, if

and
ℍ

(c)

By Examples

and

, the map

is determined by the images of these

We only need to check that this last is also determined by

and

Notice that each

is also determined by

and

Definition 2.8
.

The
normalizer

N(\text{\bbfamily M})
of a partial group
𝕄
is the set of elements

\eta\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}
that define a homotopy of the identity on
𝕄
:

N(\text{\bbfamily M})=\{\,\eta\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}\,|\,\exists\ \boldsymbol{c}_{\eta}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\;{\leftarrow\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar}\;}\limits^{\eta}}}1_{\text{\bbfamily M}}\,\}\,.

The
center
of
𝕄
is the subset
Z(\text{\bbfamily M})\subseteq N(\text{\bbfamily M})
of elements
\eta
defining a self-homotopy of the identity (
\boldsymbol{c}_{\eta}=1_{\text{\bbfamily M}}
).

For any
\eta\in N(\text{\bbfamily M})
, the map
\boldsymbol{c}_{\eta}
can be seen as a standard conjugation map and it shares many properties with conjugation maps in honest groups.
Indeed,
\boldsymbol{c}_{\eta}
is a well defined automorphism, characterized by the formula
\boldsymbol{c}_{\eta}(x)=\eta\cdot x\cdot\eta^{-1}
, for each
x\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}
.
This is shown in Lemma
2.9
below. It turns out that
N(\text{\bbfamily M})
is a group and

\boldsymbol{c}
defines a group homomorphism
\boldsymbol{c}\colon N(\text{\bbfamily M})\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})
. By analogy with the case of
groups, the automorphisms in the image of
\boldsymbol{c}
are called inner, and the cokernel is defined to be the
outer automorphsims. Proposition
2.11
below formalizes this idea.

Notice that if
\text{\bbfamily M}=BG
for some group
G
, then
N(BG)=G
. In general though,
we can only guarantee that the unit belongs to
N(\text{\bbfamily M})
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
𝕄
be a partial group and let
\eta\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}
. Then, the following holds.

(a)

if and only if

(b)

[\eta_{1}|\ldots|\eta_{n}]\in\text{\bbfamily M}

(a). According to Lemma

3.
The space of self-equivalences of a partial group

In this section we analyze in detail the structure of the simplicial group of automorphisms of a partial group.

We show that this complex can be completely described in terms of the algebraic structure of the partial group. We start with a quick review on the definition of the automorphism complex of a simplicial set, which we then specialize to partial groups.

Given simplicial sets

In particular, when

Each

\textstyle{X\times\Delta[n]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

5.1.
Classification of sections

We present a classification of sections of regular split extensions, based on an adapted version of non-abelian cohomology of groups (cf.
[
Ser
]
).

Fix a regular split extension

\text{\bbfamily M}\overset{\iota}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily E}\overset{\tau}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily H}
. We denote by

\Gamma(\tau)
the set of all sections of
\tau
.
Two sections
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\tau)
are
vertically homotopic

if there is a homotopy
\sigma{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\;{\leftarrow\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar}\;}\limits^{j(y)}}}\sigma^{\prime}
, with
y\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}
.
As in the classical case of Hurewicz fibrations, two sections that are homotopic are indeed
vertically homotopic. This is proved in Lemma
5.6
, below.
But there is an additional problem.
Partial groups are simplicial sets that fail to be Kan complexes, unless they are actual groups.
In this situation homotopy does not generally define an equivalence relation.
In particular, homotopy or vertical homotopy as described above, is reflexive and symmetric,
but it is not generally transitive. We are therefore forced to take the equivalence relation
that it generates.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\text{\bbfamily M}\overset{\iota}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily E}\overset{\tau}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily H}
be an extension of partial groups. We say that two sections
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Gamma(\tau)
are equivalent,

\sigma\approx\sigma^{\prime}
, if there is a string of sections and homotopies

\sigma_{0}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\;{\leftarrow\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar}\;}\limits^{x_{1}}}}\sigma_{1}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\;{\leftarrow\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar}\;}\limits^{x_{2}}}}\ldots{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\;{\leftarrow\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar}\;}\limits^{x_{n}}}}\sigma_{n}

with

,

. We define
\mathfrak{X}(\tau)
as the set of equivalence classes

.

. Then, we can apply the same argument repeatedly and show that (b) holds.

Suppose next that condition (b) holds. By assumption, we have a string of homotopies

\sigma_{i-1}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\;{\leftarrow\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar}\;}\limits^{j(y_{i})}}}\sigma_{i}

Composing the

\tau

\textup{Id}_{\text{\bbfamily H}}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\;{\leftarrow\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar}\;}\limits^{\tau(x_{i})}}}\textup{Id}_{\text{\bbfamily H}}

g=\tau(x_{i})^{-1}

ℍ

.
Notice

\sigma_{i}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}{\mathbin{\scriptscriptstyle\circ}}\boldsymbol{c}_{g}=\sigma_{i}

By an iteration of this argument, we prove that (c) holds. Finally, it is clear that if condition (a)

5.2.
Derivations

Let
ℍ
and
𝕄
be partial groups.
Given an action
\rho\colon\text{\bbfamily H}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}B\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})
of
ℍ
on
𝕄
,
we will write
{}^{h}\kern-1.0pt{}\boldsymbol{x}=\rho(h)(\boldsymbol{x})
, for simplicity, without further mention of
\rho
, when no confusion is possible (see Example
5.2
for a
justification of this notation).

Definition 5.7
.

A
derivation

\theta\colon\text{\bbfamily H}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}
is a
collection of maps
\{\theta_{n}\colon\text{\bbfamily H}_{n}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfying

(i)

d_{0}(\theta_{n}(\boldsymbol{h}))={}^{h_{1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta_{n-1}(d_{0}\boldsymbol{h})
and

d_{i}(\theta_{n}(\boldsymbol{h}))=\theta_{n-1}(d_{i}\boldsymbol{h})
,
1\leq i\leq n
,

(ii)

s_{i}(\theta_{n}(\boldsymbol{h}))=\theta_{n+1}(s_{i}\boldsymbol{h})
,
0\leq i\leq n
,

for all
\boldsymbol{h}=[h_{1}|\dots|h_{n}]\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{n}
,
n\geq 0
.
We denote by
\operatorname{Der}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily H},\text{\bbfamily M})
the set of derivations from
ℍ
to
𝕄
.

\operatorname{Der}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily H},\text{\bbfamily M})
is a pointed set, with canonical base point defined as the constant derivation
c
,

c(h)=1
, for all
h\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{1}
.
It turns out that a derivation is uniquely determined by its effect in dimension 1,

\theta=\theta_{1}\colon\text{\bbfamily H}_{1}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}
by the formula

\theta_{n}([h_{1}|h_{2}|\dots|h_{n}])=\bigl[\theta(h_{1})|{}^{h_{1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{2})|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{3})|\dots|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{n-1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{n})\bigr]\,.

Following the classical definitions, we
say that an element
y\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{1}
defines an equivalence
\theta\sim\theta^{\prime}
between
two derivations

\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Der}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily H},\text{\bbfamily M})
,
if for any simplex

\boldsymbol{h}=[h_{1}|h_{2}|\dots|h_{n}]\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{n}
,
n\geq 0
, there are simplices

\boldsymbol{v}_{k}=\bigl[\theta(h_{1})|{}^{h_{1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{2})|\dots|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{k-1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{k})|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{k}}\kern-1.0pt{}y|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{k}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta^{\prime}(h_{k+1})|\dots|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{n-1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta^{\prime}(h_{n})\bigr]\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{n+1}\,,

k=0,\dots,n
, with
\Pi(\boldsymbol{v}_{0})=\Pi(\boldsymbol{v}_{1})=\dots=\Pi(\boldsymbol{v}_{n})
.

We extend
\sim
to an equivalence relation and define

H^{1}(\text{\bbfamily H},\text{\bbfamily M})=\operatorname{Der}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily H},\text{\bbfamily M})/{\sim}\,.

Definition 5.8
.

A
regular derivation
is a derivation
\theta\colon\text{\bbfamily H}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}
with image in
BN(\text{\bbfamily M})
.

The characterization of regular derivations is simplified by the fact that

N(\text{\bbfamily M})
is a group. Indeed, a
regular derivation reduces to a map
\theta\colon\text{\bbfamily H}_{1}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}N(\text{\bbfamily M})
satisfying

\theta(g\cdot h)=\theta(g)\cdot{}^{g}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h)

for all
[g|h]\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{2}
.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\text{\bbfamily M}\overset{\imath}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily E}\overset{\tau}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily H}
be an extension of partial groups.

(a)

\tau
admits a regular section if and only if a given obstruction class

[\eta]\in H^{2}(\text{\bbfamily H};N(\text{\bbfamily M}))
vanishes.

(b)

A regular section defines an action
\rho\colon\text{\bbfamily H}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})
, given by conjugation in
𝔼
, and a bijective correspondence between
the sets
H^{1}(\text{\bbfamily H};\text{\bbfamily M})
and
\mathfrak{X}(\tau)
. Furthermore, under this bijection regular derivations correspond to regular sections.

Proof.

According to Theorem
4.5
(b), there is an
ℍ
-twisting function

\phi=(t,\eta)
and an equivalence of extensions
\text{\bbfamily E}\cong\text{\bbfamily M}\times_{\phi}\text{\bbfamily H}
.
The obstruction class to the existence of a regular section is represented by
\eta
, the second component of the
ℍ
-twisting pair.
This is already implicit in the proof of Lemma
5.3
, where equation
(
5.3
) presents
\eta
as a coboundary if and only if a regular section exists. This proves (a).
Furthermore (
5.4
) defines a lift of
t
to an action

\rho\colon\text{\bbfamily H}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily M})
. Then Lemma
5.3
provides a fibrewise
equivalence of
𝔼
with the semidirect product

\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}
. Hence, to prove (b), we can compute the equivalence classes of
sections of the projection

\operatorname{proj}\nolimits\colon\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily H}
.

We define maps

\textstyle{\Gamma(\operatorname{proj}\nolimits)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Der}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily H},\text{\bbfamily M})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}

as follows.
Let
\sigma\in\Gamma(\operatorname{proj}\nolimits)
. Write
\sigma([h])=[(\theta(h),h)]\in\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}
,
for each
h\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{1}
. Then, given a simplex
\boldsymbol{h}[h_{1}|\dots|h_{n}]\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{n}
,

\sigma(\boldsymbol{h})
is a simplex of
\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}
, written
[(\theta(h_{1}),h_{1})|\dots|(\theta(h_{n}),h_{n})]
.
This implies the existence of a simplex

[\theta(h_{1})|{}^{h_{1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{2})|\dots|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{n-1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{n})]\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{n}

(see Example
5.2
).
So,

\theta
extends to a collection of maps

\theta=\{\theta_{n}\colon\text{\bbfamily H}_{n}\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, with

\theta_{n}([h_{1}|\dots|h_{n}])=[\theta(h_{1})|{}^{h_{1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{2})|\dots|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{n-1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{n})]
.
It is a routine to check that this is a well defined derivation, so we can define
\delta(\sigma)=\theta
.

Conversely, fix
\theta=\{\theta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\in\operatorname{Der}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily H},\text{\bbfamily M})
and define

\text{\bbfamily H}\overset{\sigma_{\theta}}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}

in degree one by
\sigma_{\theta}(g)=(\theta(g),g)
for all
g\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{1}
. Again, by
Example
5.2
, it is easy to check that this extends to a well defined homomorphism of partial groups
which is indeed a section of
\operatorname{proj}\nolimits
. Thus we can define

\gamma(\theta)=\sigma_{\theta}
.

These maps are inverse to each other.
Moreover, regular sections clearly correspond to regular
derivations. This follows at once from the identification

\text{\bbfamily N}_{\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}}(\text{\bbfamily M})\cong BN(\text{\bbfamily M})\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}
. Indeed, recall from Lemma
4.8
that there is an induced fibre bundle

BN(\text{\bbfamily M})\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily N}_{\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}}(\text{\bbfamily M})\overset{}{\;{\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\rightarrow}\;}\text{\bbfamily H}.

Moreover, for any
h\in\text{\bbfamily H}_{1}
, one can easily check that

(1,h_{1})\in N_{\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}}(\text{\bbfamily M})
, thus
N(\text{\bbfamily M})\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}\leq N_{\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}}(\text{\bbfamily M})
, hence
N(\text{\bbfamily M})\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}=N_{\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}}(\text{\bbfamily M})
.

It only remains to check that
\delta
and
\gamma=\delta^{-1}
preserve
the given equivalence relations
on
\Gamma(\operatorname{proj}\nolimits)
and on
\operatorname{Der}\nolimits(\text{\bbfamily H},\text{\bbfamily M})
.
It is enough to show that given sections
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
and a homotopy

\sigma{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\;{\leftarrow\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar\mathrel{\mkern-6.0mu}\relbar}\;}\limits^{\eta}}}\sigma^{\prime}
, then
\theta=\delta{\sigma}
and
\theta^{\prime}=\delta(\sigma^{\prime})
are
equivalent derivations, and vice versa.

According to Lemma
5.6
, we can assume that
\eta=(\xi,1)\in\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}
.
Now, according to Lemma
2.5
, a homotopy determined by
\eta
exists if and only if
there are simplices

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}_{k}

\displaystyle=[\sigma(h_{1})|\dots|\sigma(h_{k})|\eta|\sigma^{\prime}(h_{k+1})|\dots|\sigma^{\prime}(h_{n})]

\displaystyle=[(\theta(h_{1}),h_{1})|\dots|(\theta(h_{k}),h_{k})|(\xi,1)|(\theta^{\prime}(h_{k+1}),h_{k+1})|\dots|(\theta^{\prime}(h_{n}),h_{n})]\in\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}

for
k=0,\dots,n
, such that
\Pi(\boldsymbol{w}_{0})=\dots=\Pi(\boldsymbol{w}_{n})
.
These, in turn, are well defined simplices of
\text{\bbfamily M}\rtimes\text{\bbfamily H}
if and only if
there are simplices

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}_{k}=[\theta(h_{1})|{}^{h_{1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{2})|\dots|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{k-1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta(h_{k})|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{k}}\kern-1.0pt{}\xi|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{k}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta^{\prime}(h_{k+1})|\dots|{}^{h_{1}h_{2}\dots h_{n-1}}\kern-1.0pt{}\theta^{\prime}(h_{n})]\in\text{\bbfamily M}_{n+1}

for
k=0,\dots,n
, such that
\Pi(\boldsymbol{v}_{0})=\dots=\Pi(\boldsymbol{v}_{n})
.
(Notice that
\Pi(\boldsymbol{w}_{k})=(\Pi(\boldsymbol{v}_{k}),\Pi(\boldsymbol{h}))
.)
Finally, this last condition is by definition equivalent to say that
\xi
defines an
equivalence
\theta\sim\theta^{\prime}
.
∎

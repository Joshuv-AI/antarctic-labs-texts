Tamás László

Abstract
. The canonical polynomial is an important output of the multivariable topological Poincaré series associated with a normal surface singularity. It can be considered as a multivariable polynomial generalization of the Seiberg–Witten invariant of the link. In the case of elliptic germs, another key topological invariant was considered, the elliptic sequence, which mirrors the specific structure of the elliptic germs and guides several properties of them. In this note we study the relationship of these two objects. First of all, we describe the structure of the exponents of the canonical polynomial and prove that they determine the elliptic sequence. For the converse problem, we consider an inductive setup of elliptic germs via natural extension of their graphs and compare the corresponding sets of exponents. This leads to the definition of a good extension which can be characterized by an inclusion type formula for the corresponding canonical polynomials. This reflects in a compatible way the ‘flag structure’ of the elliptic sequence.

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Classification of normal surface singularities and investigation of their analytic invariants via topological data is a classical research program in singularity theory. In such a study
certain fundamental cycles (ie. special divisors supported on the exceptional set of a fixed resolution space) play a crucial role since they identify important analytical and topological properties of the singularity.

The first results of this program can be attributed to Artin
[
A62
,
A66
]
, who defined topologically a special cycle
Z_{min}
(called the minimal cycle or Artin’s fundamental cycle), and characterized the rational singularities and their Hilbert-Samuel function via this cycle. Laufer
[
La72
,
La77
]
extended Artin’s results
to the case of elliptic singularities: he introduced the class of minimally elliptic singularities as the Gorenstein singularities having geometric genus
1
. On one hand, Laufer proved that they can be characterized topologically and their Hilbert-Samuel function can be computed topologically. On the other hand, he noticed that for ‘more complicated’ elliptic singularities (eg. having
p_{g}\geq 1
without the Gorenstein condition, or Gorenstein elliptic singularities with
p_{g}\geq 2
)
all these fail without extra assumptions. An important new step in the study of
(arbitrary) elliptic singularities was done by S. S.-T. Yau
[
Y79
,
Y80
]

who defined the elliptic sequence for any elliptic germ (inspired by constructions of Laufer).
They constitute some kind of ‘topological skeleton’ for the topological type of an elliptic singular germ. Motivated by the results of Laufer and Yau, Némethi
[
N99a
]
showed that for Gorenstein singularities with rational homology sphere link the so-called ‘Artin-Laufer’ program of topological characterization of the geometric genus and of the Hilbert-Samuel function can be continued.

In the case of elliptic germs, the main topological invariant, which guides most of the topological and analytical properties of elliptic singularities is the
elliptic sequence
. The first versions of the elliptic sequence were introduced by Laufer and Yau
[
Y79
,
Y80
]
. It is a sequence of integral cycles. Recently, Nagy and Némethi
[
NN18
,
NN19
]
constructed another version, which coincides with Yau’s sequence in the numerically Gorenstein case, however it differs from it otherwise. In this second case it is a sequence of rational cycles and it captures differently the failure of the numerically Gorenstein property. In this article we will rely on this second approach.

The aim of the present work is to show how the elliptic sequence can be extracted from the exponents of certain topological-combinatorial Poincaré series
(zeta-function) associated with the topology of the singularity.
Such series can be defined for any surface singularity with rational homology sphere link. Several results of the last two decades show that they are extremely strong invariants of the singularities,
not only that they code deep topological invariants of the link, but by their nature they constitute a key bridge with the analytical Poincaré series (associated with divisorial filtrations). For more details see the next subsection.

The motivation of the present work is two folded. On one hand, we believe this study gives a hint for defining such a ‘characteristic’ sequences in the general case of normal surface singularities, as a generalization of the elliptic sequence (with all its benefits).
On the other hand, it highlights (once again) by a new concrete way how the exponents of the Poincaré series can encode key information. The present connection was completely hidden before this work.

1.2.

In order to provide some details regarding the results, we will introduce the following terminology.

Let
(X,o)
be a normal surface singularity and we will assume that its link
M
is a rational homology sphere (
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
for short). We fix a good resolution
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
of
X
. In particular, the exceptional divisor
E:=\pi^{-1}(0)
is a simple normal-crossing divisor. Let
\Gamma
be the dual resolution graph with set of vertices
{\mathcal{V}}
.

One considers the usual combinatorial package of the resolution (for details see sect.
2.1
) and define the topological Poincaré series as follows. Let
\cup_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}E_{v}
be the decomposition of
E
into irreducible components,

L=H_{2}(\tilde{X},\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\langle E_{v}\rangle_{v}
is the lattice associated with
\pi
( or with
\Gamma
) endowed with the negative definite intersection form
(E_{v},E_{w})_{v,w}
.
L^{\prime}
is the dual lattice identified with the
rational cycles
l^{\prime}\in L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
for which
(l^{\prime},l)\in\mathbb{Z}
for any
l\in L
.
Then
L^{\prime}/L
is the finite group
H:=H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
and
[l^{\prime}]
denotes the class of
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
in
H
. Given two cycles
l^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{v}l^{\prime}_{i,v}E_{v}\in L^{\prime}\subset L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
(
i\in\{1,2\}
) one defines a natural partial ordering:
l^{\prime}_{1}\geq l^{\prime}_{2}
if and only if
l^{\prime}_{1,v}\geq l^{\prime}_{2,v}
for any
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
.
We consider the (anti)dual basis
\{E^{*}_{v}\}
of
L^{\prime}
, see section
2.1.1
. Using these dual cycles one defines a ‘zeta-type’ rational function
\prod_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}(1-{\mathbf{t}}^{E^{*}_{v}})^{\delta_{v}-2}
, where
\delta_{v}
is the valency of the vertex
v
and
{\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}:=\prod_{v}t_{v}^{l^{\prime}_{v}}
if
l^{\prime}=\sum_{v}l^{\prime}_{v}E_{v}
. Its Taylor expansion at the origin
Z({\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}}z(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}
is called the
topological Poincaré series
associated with
\Gamma
.

The theory of topological Poincaré series was started with the work of Campillo, Delgado and Gusein-Zade (see eg.
[
CDG03
,
CDGZ04
,
CDGZ08
,
CDGZ13
]
and the references therein) and Némethi
[
N08
]
, see also
[
NN02
]
. In the last decade several developements have been made regarding its applications, cf.
[
NN02
,
N11
,
N12
,
LN14
,
LNN17
,
LSz16
,
LSz17
,
LNN19
,
CMLN21a
,
CMLN20
,
CMLN21b
]
.

The series
Z({\mathbf{t}})
decomposes into
\sum_{h\in H}Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}})
, where
Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}}):=\sum_{[l^{\prime}]=h}z(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}
. Furthermore, for any
h\in H
and subset
I\subset{\mathcal{V}}
we can define the reduced series
Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I}):=Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}})|_{t_{i}=1,i\notin I}
. For each
Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
there exists a unique decomposition
Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})=P_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})+Z^{neg}_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})

[
LSz16
,
LSz17
,
LNN19
]
guided by the following properties:
P_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
is a Laurent polynomial with exponents
l^{\prime}
, where

l^{\prime}\not\leq 0
(with respect to the partial ordering),
and
Z^{neg}_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
is a rational function of negative degree in all variables. In general, the sum of the coefficients
P_{h,I}({\mathbf{1}})
of
P_{h,I}
is equal to the periodic constant of
Z_{h,I}
(see
2.5
), measuring the ‘regularity’ of the series. In fact, this associated
numerical invariant in the case
I={\mathcal{V}}
provides the normalized Seiberg–Witten invariants of the link associated with the corresponding
spin^{c}
-structure indexed by the group element
h\in H
, cf.
[
Lim00
,
N05
,
N11
]
. This invariant is realized for certain smaller sets
I\subset{\mathcal{V}}
as well, thanks to a reduction procedure, see
[
LN14
,
LN15
]
.

In particular, associated with
h=0
and
I={\mathcal{V}}
the ‘polynomial part’
P_{0}({\mathbf{t}})
of the above decomposition is called the
canonical polynomial
. (For simplicity, the index
I={\mathcal{V}}
is always omitted from
P_{0,{\mathcal{V}}}({\mathbf{t}})
.)
This polynomial can be thought as the analog of the Alexander polynomial associated with knots/links in
S^{3}
. In fact, a similar procedure can be applied to the Poincaré series associated with a plane curve singularity as well, when the Alexander polynomial of the corresponding knot/link appears in such decomposition, see eg.
[
CDG03
]
and
[
LNN19
, Sect. 5.4]
.

1.3.

Now, let
(X,o)
be an elliptic singularity with
\mathbb{Q}HS^{3}
link. Note that by a result of Yau
[
Y80
]
there are very few pathological cases for which the minimal and the minimal good resolution do not coincide. For these cases the results of the present article can be adapted easily to the level of the minimal good resolution. Therefore, we will omit these cases from our discussion and always assume that the minimal resolution is good.

We consider the elliptic sequence defined by Nagy and Némethi
[
NN18
]
(NN-elliptic sequence for short). It is linked to a sequence of
subgraphs
\{B_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
, where
B_{-1}:=\Gamma
and
B_{m}
is the support of the minimal elliptic cycle associated with the resolution, see
3.1.1
. We say that the length of the sequence is
m+1
. One also associates to this sequence of subgraphs a sequence of special cycles
\{C_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
which play a crucial role in our study. They agree with the NN-elliptic sequence.
One knows by
[
NN19
]
(see also
[
N08b
]
) that the length of the elliptic sequence equals to the normalized canonical Seiberg–Witten invariant of the link of the singularity. Hence, it equals to
P_{0}(\mathbf{1})
as well. This result was one of the motivations
of the present manuscript.

The overall goal of this work is to understand the role of the exponents of the canonical polynomial. In this context, first we determine the structure of the exponents of
P_{0}({\mathbf{t}})
and prove that they determine the elliptic sequence
\{B_{j},C_{j}\}
. More precisely, one proves that the non-negative entries of an exponent determine
B_{j+1}
for some
j
and the cycle formed from these components is exactly the special cycle
C_{j}
, see Theorem
4.1.5
and the discussion therein.

Conversely, we study in what extent can the exponents be reconstructed from the elliptic sequence. For that, we consider an extension (an inclusion)
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
of elliptic graphs and we study the projections of the exponents of
\Gamma
to
\Gamma^{\prime}
(Theorem
5.2.8
). On the other hand, there might be exponents of
\Gamma^{\prime}
which do not come from such projections, that is, they can not be ‘extended’ to
\Gamma
. Nevertheless, in Theorem
6.5.2
we prove that whenever an exponent of
\Gamma^{\prime}
is extendable to
\Gamma
, the coefficient of its monomial in
P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
equals to the sum of the coefficients in
P_{0}^{\Gamma}
corresponding to its extensions. Furthermore, we construct a step by step algorithm which builds up all the exponents of
\Gamma
from the elliptic sequence.

The study of extendability of the exponents leads to the definition of a ‘good extension’ of elliptic graphs which can be characterized by a natural inclusion type formula for the corresponding cannonical polynomials, as presented in Theorem
7.1.5
. This formula is in accordance with the flag structure
B_{m}\subset B_{m-1}\subset\dots\subset\Gamma
provided by the elliptic sequence.

1.4.

The structure of the article is as follows. Section
2
presents some preliminaries regarding the topology of normal surface singularities, Seiberg–Witten invariants, Poincaré series and the canonical polynomial. Section
3
is a short review of elliptic singularities with a high emphasis on the NN-elliptic sequence. Section
4
explains how (the exponents of) the canonical polynomial determines the elliptic sequence. We illustrate our result on detailed examples as well. In Section
5
we study the projection of the exponents to elliptic subgraphs. Reversely, in Section
6
we discuss extensions of elliptic graphs and their corresponding exponents. We define the notion of ‘good extension’ which, in some sense, ‘behaves’ nicely with respect to a natural recursivity of canonical polynomials in the elliptic case. Finally, Section
7
proves that this natural recursive (surgery) formula for the corresponding canonical polynomials is valid exactly when we have a good extension.

2.
Preliminaries: invariants of normal surface singularities

2.1.
Plumbing/resolution graphs

2.1.1.

We fix a connected plumbing graph
\Gamma
whose associated intersection matrix is negative definite. We denote the corresponding oriented
plumbed 3–manifold by
M=M(\Gamma)
. Any such
M(\Gamma)
is the link of a complex normal surface singularity

(X,o)
, which has a resolution
\widetilde{X}\to X
with
resolution graph
\Gamma
(see e.g.
[
N99b
]
),
and the complex analytic smooth surface
\widetilde{X}
as a smooth manifold is the plumbed 4–manifold associated with
\Gamma
, with boundary
\partial\widetilde{X}=M
.

In this article we always assume that
M
is a
rational homology sphere
,
equivalently,
\Gamma
is a tree with all genus decorations zero.

We will use the notation
\mathcal{V}
for the set of vertices of
\Gamma
,
\delta_{v}
for the valency of a vertex
v
,

\mathcal{N}
for the set of nodes (vertices with
\delta_{v}\geq 3
), and

\mathcal{E}
for the set of end–vertices (ie. vertices with
\delta_{v}=1
).

As in section
1.2
, we consider the lattice
L:=H_{2}(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\langle E_{v}\rangle
endowed with the negative definite intersection form
(\,,\,)
. We set the notation
e_{v}:=(E_{v},E_{v})
. One also considers the dual lattice
L^{\prime}:={\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(L,{\mathbb{Z}})=\{l^{\prime}\in L\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\,:\,(l^{\prime},L)\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
which is generated over
\mathbb{Z}
by the (anti)dual classes
\{E^{*}_{v}\}_{v\in\mathcal{V}}
defined by
(E^{*}_{v},E_{w})=-\delta_{vw}
, the opposite of the Kronecker symbol. Then one has the inclusions
L\subset L^{\prime}\subset L\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=:L_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and the intersection form extends to
L_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Furthermore, we get the finite group
H:=L^{\prime}/L\simeq H_{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

For a cycle
l^{\prime}=\sum_{v}l^{\prime}_{v}E_{v}
we define its support as
|l^{\prime}|:=\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}\ :\ l^{\prime}_{v}\neq 0\}
. Usin the partial ordering defined in section
1.2
, we say that
l^{\prime}
is an effective cycle if
l^{\prime}\geq 0
. For any
l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2}\in L^{\prime}
with
l^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{v}l^{\prime}_{i,v}E_{v}
(
i=\{1,2\}
) one can set
\min\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2}\}:=\sum_{v}\min\{l^{\prime}_{1,v},l^{\prime}_{2,v}\}E_{v}
and
analogously
\min\{F\}
for a finite subset
F\subset L^{\prime}
. We will also use the notation
l^{\prime}_{1}\prec l^{\prime}_{2}
if
l^{\prime}_{1,v}<l^{\prime}_{2,v}
for all
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
. (Note that
\not\geq
differs from
\prec
.)

2.1.2.
The Lipman cone and generalized Laufer algorithm

The lattice
L^{\prime}
admits a partition parametrized by the group
H
, where for any
h\in H
one sets
L^{\prime}_{h}=\{\ell^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}\mid[\ell^{\prime}]=h\}\subset L^{\prime}
. Note that
L^{\prime}_{0}=L
.
Given a class
h\in H
one can define the unique cycle

r_{h}:=\sum_{v}l^{\prime}_{v}E_{v}\in L^{\prime}_{h}
with the property
0\leq l^{\prime}_{v}<1
for any
v
.

We define the
rational Lipman cone
by

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\{\ell^{\prime}\in L_{\mathbb{Q}}\ |\ (\ell^{\prime},E_{v})\leq 0\ \mbox{for all}\ v\in{\mathcal{V}}\},

which is generated over
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
by
E^{*}_{v}
.
On also defines
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}:={\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cap L^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\langle E^{*}_{v}\rangle
as the semigroup (monoid) of anti-nef cycles in
L^{\prime}
.
Since
\{E^{*}_{v}\}_{v}
have positive entries,
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus\{0\}
is in the open first quadrant.

The
Lipman cone

{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
also admits a natural equivariant partition
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}={\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\cap L^{\prime}_{h}
.
Furthermore, we have the following properties:

(a)

if
l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
then
l^{\prime}_{2}-l^{\prime}_{1}\in L
and
\min\{l^{\prime}_{1},l^{\prime}_{2}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
;

(b)

for any
s\in L^{\prime}
the set
\{l^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\mid l^{\prime}\not\geq s\}
is finite;

(c)

for any
h\in H
there exists a
unique minimal cycle

s_{h}:=\min\{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
.

A given class
h\in H
will be represented wither with
r_{h}
or with
s_{h}
, depending on the situation. The minimal cycle is determined by the
generalized Laufer algorithm

[
La72
]
,
[
N05
, Lemma 7.4]
which is as follows.

We fix
h\in H
. Then for any
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}_{h}
there exists a unique
minimal element
s(l^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
satisfying
s(l^{\prime})\geq l^{\prime}
which can be obtained by the following algorithm.
Set
x_{0}:=l^{\prime}
. Then one constructs a computation sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i}
as follows.
If
x_{i}
is already constructed and
x_{i}\not\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
then there exits some
E_{v_{i}}
such that
(x_{i},E_{v_{i}})>0
.
Then take
x_{i+1}:=x_{i}+E_{v_{i}}
(for some choice of
E_{v_{i}}
). The procedure stops after finitely many steps,
say at
x_{t}
, and necessarily
x_{t}=s(l^{\prime})
.

In particular, if we start with
l^{\prime}=E_{v}
with an arbitrarily chosen
v\in{\mathcal{V}}
then
s(l^{\prime})=\min\{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}\}
is the
minimal (or Artin’s fundamental) cycle
Z_{min}\in L
, see
[
A62
,
A66
,
La72
]
. In this case, the algorithm is the classical
‘Laufer algorithm targeting
Z_{min}
’
.

In fact
s(r_{h})=s_{h}
and
r_{h}\leq s_{h}
, however, in general
r_{h}\neq s_{h}
. Note that this fact does not contradict the minimality of
s_{h}
in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
since
r_{h}
might not sit in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
.

2.1.3.

Let
Z_{K}\in L^{\prime}
be the anti-canonical cycle defined by the adjunction formulae
(Z_{K},E_{v})=e_{v}+2
for all
v
.
We say that the germ
(X,o)
, or its topological type, is
numerically Gorenstein
if
Z_{K}\in L
. Note that this property
is independent of the resolution, since it means that the line bundle
\Omega^{2}_{X\setminus\{o\}}
of holomorphic 2–forms on
X\setminus\{o\}
is topologically trivial, cf.
[
D78
]
. Furthermore, one says that
(X,o)
is
Gorenstein
if
Z_{K}\in L
and
\Omega^{2}_{\widetilde{X}}
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}}(-Z_{K})
(or,
equivalently, if
\Omega^{2}_{X\setminus\{o\}}
is holomorphically trivial).

From the adjunction formulae one follows the following useful expression

(2.1.1)

Z_{K}-E=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}(\delta_{v}-2)E^{*}_{v},

where we set
E:=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}E_{v}
.

2.2.
Rational singularities

We recall that a germ
(X,o)
is rational if its geometric genus
p_{g}(X,o)=\dim H^{1}(\widetilde{X},{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{X}})=0
. This vanishing was characterized topologically by Artin
[
A62
,
A66
]
and Laufer
[
La72
]
as follows.

Let us define the Riemann–Roch function
\chi:L^{\prime}\to\mathbb{Q}
,
\chi(l^{\prime}):=-(l^{\prime},l^{\prime}-Z_{K})/2
. Then,
(X,o)
is rational if and only if
\chi(l)>0
for any non-zero effective cycle
l
, or, equivalently,
\chi(Z_{min})=1
. These are also equivalent with the fact that along the Laufer algorithm targeting
Z_{min}
the
\chi
function is constant, or, at every step in the algorithm one has
(x_{i},E_{v_{i}})=1
. In fact, this implies that for all the vertices of a resolution graph of a rational singularity one has the following inequality:

-e_{v}\geq\delta_{v}-1.

For simplicity, we also say that a negative definite plumbing graph is a
rational
if one of the above properties is satisfied.

2.3.
The topological Poincaré series

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}[[L^{\prime}]]
be the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module generated by the monomials
\mathbf{t}^{l^{\prime}}:=\prod_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}t_{v}^{l^{\prime}_{v}}
,
where
l^{\prime}=\sum_{v}l^{\prime}_{v}E_{v}\in L^{\prime}
. Then
the
multivariable topological Poincaré series

Z({\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}}z(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[[L^{\prime}]]
is the Taylor expansion at the origin of the rational function

(2.3.1)

\prod_{v\in\mathcal{V}}(1-\mathbf{t}^{E^{*}_{v}})^{\delta_{v}-2}.

The expression (
2.3.1
) shows that
Z(\mathbf{t})
is supported in the Lipman cone
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Moreover, the exponents are expressed as
l^{\prime}=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{N}\cup{\mathcal{E}}}l^{*}_{v}E^{*}_{v}
for some
l^{*}_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfying
0\leq l^{*}_{v}\leq\delta_{v}-2
if
v\in\mathcal{N}
. Hence, the coefficients are

(2.3.2)

z(l^{\prime})={\prod_{v\in\mathcal{N}}(-1)^{l^{*}_{v}}{{\delta_{v}-2}\choose{l^{*}_{v}}}}.

The series decomposes as
Z(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{h\in H}Z_{h}(\mathbf{t})
where
Z_{h}(\mathbf{t})=\sum_{[l^{\prime}]=h}z(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}
, and we will use the following notation for the support:

Supp(Z_{h})=\{l^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}\ :\ z(l^{\prime})\neq 0\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}.

Note that, however the first exponent of
Z_{0}
is always
l^{\prime}=0
, in the case of
h\neq 0
the minimal element
s_{h}
of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{h}
might not be in
Supp(Z_{h})
as shown by the following example.

Example 2.3.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a star-shaped graph with
4
vertices with the associated base elements
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{4}
. Consider the following decorations:
E_{1}^{2}=-3
for the central vertex and
E_{i}^{2}=-2
for the other vertices. Then one computes that
r_{[2E_{1}^{*}]}=(1/3)E_{1}+(2/3)E_{2}+(2/3)E_{3}+(2/3)E_{4}
and
s_{[2E_{1}^{*}]}=r_{[2E_{1}^{*}]}+E_{1}=2E_{1}^{*}
. Hence, by the above discussion,
s_{[2E_{1}^{*}]}
is not in the support of
Z_{[2E_{1}^{*}]}
.

2.4.
Counting functions and periodic constants of multivariable series

In this section we will adopt the setting of section
2.1.1
in a slightly more general context.
Namely,
L
will be a lattice freely generated by base elements
\{E_{v}\}_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}
,

L^{\prime}
is an overlattice of the same rank (but not necessarily dual of
L
), and we consider the finite abelian group
H:=L^{\prime}/L
.
One also defines the partial ordering on
L^{\prime}
as in section
1.2
.

Consider a multivariable series
S({\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}}a(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[[L^{\prime}]]
,
let
Supp(S):=\{l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}:a(l^{\prime})\neq 0\}
be its support and we assume the following condition:

(2.4.1)

\{l^{\prime}\in Supp(S)\mid l^{\prime}\not\geq x\}\ \ \mbox{is finite for any}\ x\in L^{\prime}.

For any subset of vertices
\emptyset\neq I\subset{\mathcal{V}}
we can define the projection of
L^{\prime}
via the map

{\mathrm{pr}}_{I}:L_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\to\oplus_{v\in I}{\mathbb{Q}}\langle E_{v}\rangle
as
L^{\prime}_{I}={\mathrm{pr}}_{I}(L^{\prime})
, and one can consider multivariable series in the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
{\mathbb{Z}}[[L^{\prime}_{I}]]
. For example, if
S({\mathbf{t}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[[L^{\prime}]]
then
S({\mathbf{t}}_{I}):=S({\mathbf{t}})|_{t_{v}=1,v\notin I}
is called the
reduced series
, which is an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}[[L^{\prime}_{I}]]
.
In the sequel we use the notation
l^{\prime}_{I}=l^{\prime}|_{I}:={\rm pr}_{I}(l^{\prime})
and
{\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}_{I}:={\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}|_{t_{v}=1,v\notin I}
for any
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
.
Each coefficient
a_{I}(x)
of
S({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
is obtained as a summation of certain coefficients
a(y)
of
S({\mathbf{t}})
, where
y
runs over

\{l^{\prime}\in Supp(S)\mid l^{\prime}_{I}=x\}

(which is finite by (
2.4.1
)). Moreover,
S({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
satisfies the finiteness property (
2.4.1
) too.

We can consider the decomposition
S({\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{h}S_{h}({\mathbf{t}})
,
where
S_{h}({\mathbf{t}}):=\sum_{[l^{\prime}]=h}a(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}^{l^{\prime}}
. Here one has to emphasize that
the
H
-decomposition of the reduced series
S({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
is not well defined. That is,
the reduced series
S_{h}({\mathbf{t}})|_{t_{v}=1,v\notin I}
cannot be recovered from
S({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
in general,
since the class of
l^{\prime}
cannot be recovered from
l^{\prime}|_{I}
. Hence, the notation
S_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
, defined as
S_{h}({\mathbf{t}})|_{t_{v}=1,v\notin I}
, is not ambiguous, but requires certain caution.

Associated with
S_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
the the
counting function
of its coefficients is defined by

(2.4.2)

Q^{(S)}_{h,{I}}:L^{\prime}_{I}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\ \ \ \ x_{I}\mapsto\sum_{l^{\prime}_{I}\ngeq x_{I}}a(l^{\prime}_{I}).

It is well defined by (
2.4.1
), and can be extended to
x\in L^{\prime}
via the projection
L^{\prime}\to L^{\prime}_{I}
as
Q^{(S)}_{h,{I}}(x)=Q^{(S)}_{h,{I}}(x_{I})
. The same definition provides the counting function
Q{(S({\mathbf{t}}_{I}))}
for
S({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
too, and this case we get
Q{(S({\mathbf{t}}_{I}))}=\sum_{h\in H}Q^{(S)}_{h,{I}}
.

Now, we fix
h\in H
and for simplicity we set
{I}:={\mathcal{V}}
. Thus we look at only
S_{h}({\mathbf{t}})
.
Let
\mathcal{K}\subset L^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{R}
be a real closed cone whose affine closure is top dimensional.
Assume that there exist
l^{\prime}_{*}\in\mathcal{K}
and a finite index sublattice
\widetilde{L}
of
L
and a
quasi-polynomial
\mathfrak{Q}^{{\mathcal{K}},(S)}_{h,{\mathcal{V}}}(x)

defined on
\widetilde{L}
such that

(2.4.3)

\mathfrak{Q}_{h,{\mathcal{V}}}^{{\mathcal{K}},(S)}(l)=Q_{h,{\mathcal{V}}}^{(S)}(r_{h}+l)

whenever
l\in(l^{\prime}_{*}+\mathcal{K})\cap\widetilde{L}
(
r_{h}\in L^{\prime}
is defined as in
2.1.2
).
Then we say that the counting function
Q_{h,{\mathcal{V}}}^{(S)}
(or just
S_{h}(\mathbf{t})
)

admits a quasi-polynomial
in
\mathcal{K}
, namely
\widetilde{L}\ni\l\mapsto\mathfrak{Q}_{h,{\mathcal{V}}}^{\mathcal{K},(S)}(l)
.
In this case, we can define the
periodic constant
(
[
LN14
]
) of
S_{h}({\mathbf{t}})
associated with
\mathcal{K}
by

(2.4.4)

\mathrm{pc}^{\mathcal{K}}(S_{h}({\mathbf{t}})):=\mathfrak{Q}_{h,{\mathcal{V}}}^{{\mathcal{K}},(S)}(0)\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Note that
\mathrm{pc}^{\mathcal{K}}(S_{h}({\mathbf{t}}))
is independent of the
choice of
\l^{\prime}_{*}
and of the finite index sublattice

\widetilde{L}\subset L
.

Given any
{I}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
the natural group homomorphism
{\rm pr}_{I}:L^{\prime}\to L^{\prime}_{I}
preserves the lattices
L\to L_{I}

and induces a homomorphism
H\to H_{I}:=L^{\prime}_{I}/L_{I}
. However, note that even if

L^{\prime}
is the dual of
L
associated with a form
(\,,\,)
,

L_{I}^{\prime}
usually is not a dual lattice of
L_{I}
, it is just an overlattice. This fact motivates the general setup of the present subsection.
Nevertheless, one can define the periodic constant associated with the reduced series
S_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
too by exchanging

{\mathcal{V}}
(resp.
{\mathbf{t}}
,
r_{h}
) by
{I}
(resp.
{\mathbf{t}}_{I}
,
(r_{h})_{I}
).

The concept of periodic constants for one-variable series was defined by Némethi and Okuma in
[
O08
,
NO09
]
, the multivariable case was clarified in
[
LN14
]
. For more details we invite the reader to consult with
[
N08
,
N11
,
LSz16
]
.

2.5.
The case of topological Poincaré series and Seiberg–Witten invariants

In this section we consider the case of topological Poincaré series
Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}})
, and recall some results emphasizing how it encodes important topological invariant of the link.

The positivity of the dual base elements
E_{v}^{*}
implies that for any
x\in L^{\prime}

Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
(
\emptyset\neq I\subset{\mathcal{V}}
) satisfies the finiteness condition (
2.4.1
), hence the corresponding counting functions are well-defined. For simplicity, throughout the paper we will simplify the notation to
Q_{h,I}(x):=Q^{(Z)}_{h,I}(x)
for the counting function of
Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
. In particular we write
Q_{h}(x):=Q^{(Z)}_{h,{\mathcal{V}}}(x)
if there is no danger of ambiguity.

By the result of
[
N11
]
we know that for any
\l^{\prime}\in Z_{K}+int({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})
(where
int({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime})=\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\langle E^{*}_{v}\rangle_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}
) the
counting function of
Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}})
has the following form

(2.5.1)

{Q}_{h}(l^{\prime})=\chi(l^{\prime})-\chi(r_{h})+\mathfrak{sw}_{h}^{norm}(M),

where
\mathfrak{sw}_{h}^{norm}(M)
denotes the
normalized Seiberg–Witten invariant
of the link

M
indexed by
h\in H
. (For more details see eg.
[
N05
,
N11
,
LN14
,
LNN17
]
.) In particular, for
h=0
,
\mathfrak{sw}_{0}^{norm}(M)
is called the canonical Seiberg–Witten invariant of
M
.
Furthermore, one deduces that
Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}})
admits the (quasi-)polynomial

\mathfrak{Q}_{h}(l)=\chi(l+r_{h})-\chi(r_{h})+\mathfrak{sw}_{h}^{norm}(M)

in the cone
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}:={\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{R}
and

\mathrm{pc}^{{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}}(Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}}))=\mathfrak{sw}_{h}^{norm}(M).

Remark 2.5.2
.

(1) If
M
is the link of a rational singularity then the Seiberg–Witten invariant can be expressed as
\mathfrak{sw}^{norm}_{h}(M)=\chi(r_{h})-\chi(s_{h})
, cf.
[
N08
,
LN14
]
. In particular,
\mathfrak{sw}^{norm}_{0}(M)=0
.

(2) We also emphasize that the normalized Seiberg–Witten invariant appears as the ‘Euler characteristic’ of several homology theories associated with the link, such as lattice homology and Heegaard Floer homology. This also supports the vanishing of the canonical Seiberg–Witten invariant in case (1), since the simplest manifolds from the viewpoint of these homology theories are the negative definite L-spaces, or, equivalently, the links of rational surface singularities.

For Seiberg–Witten invariants of links of normal surface singularities, there exists a general surgery type formula in terms of the graph, developed in
[
LNN17
]
. Here we state only the
h=0
case which will be used in the sequel.

Let
I\subset{\mathcal{V}}
be an arbitrary non–empty subset of
{\mathcal{V}}
as before. Consider the subgraph spanned by the vertices
{\mathcal{V}}\setminus I
(ie. we take out from
\Gamma
the vertices
I
and their associated edges) and write it as the union of full
connected subgraphs
\cup_{i}\Gamma_{i}
. Denote by
M(\Gamma_{i})
the plumbed 3-manifold associated with
\Gamma_{i}
. Then the surgery formula is as follows:

(2.5.3)

\mathfrak{sw}_{0}^{norm}(M)-\sum_{i}\ \mathfrak{sw}_{0}^{norm}(M(\Gamma_{i}))={\rm pc}^{{\rm pr}_{I}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{R}})}(Z_{0}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})).

2.6.
Polynomials from the topological Poincaré series

Assume again that we are in the situation of a negative definite plumbing graph and its associated
topological Poincaré series
Z({\mathbf{t}})
. Fix
h\in H
and a subset
\emptyset\neq I\subset{\mathcal{V}}
, and write the expression
{\mathbf{t}}_{I}^{Z_{K}-E}Z_{[Z_{K}]-h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I}^{-1})

\sum_{l^{\prime}\in Z_{K}-E-{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}}w(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}_{I}^{l^{\prime}}

[l^{\prime}]=h

(2.6.1)

P_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I}):=\sum_{l^{\prime}|_{I}\nprec 0|_{I}}w(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}_{I}^{l^{\prime}}

and also the series

Z^{neg}_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I}):=\sum_{l^{\prime}|_{I}\prec 0|_{I}}w(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}_{I}^{l^{\prime}}
.
Set also the notation
P_{h,I}({\mathbf{1}}):=P_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})|_{t_{i}=1,\forall\,i}
.
Then one has the following result.

Theorem 2.6.2

(
[
LNN19
]
)
.

(a) There is a unique decomposition

Z_{h}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})=P_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})+Z^{neg}_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})
satisfying the following properties:
(i)

is a finite sum (Laurent polynomial) with non-negative exponents;
(ii)
Z^{neg}_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I})

(b)

In particular, for

we get

(For simplicity, we will always omit the index
{\mathcal{V}}
and simply write
P_{h}({\mathbf{t}})
for the polynomial associated with

.)
On the other hand, in the case of

part (b) of the above theorem specializes to

(2.6.3)

{\rm pc}^{\pi_{I}({\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{{\mathbb{R}}})}(Z_{0}({\mathbf{t}}_{I}))=Q_{[Z_{K}],I}(Z_{K})\ \ \mbox{for any }\ I\subset{\mathcal{V}}.

Definition 2.6.4
.

For

is called the

associated with
\Gamma
.

Sometimes is very convenient to consider the

\check{P}_{h,I}({\mathbf{t}}_{I}):=\sum_{[l^{\prime}]=[Z_{K}]-h\atop l^{\prime}|_{I}\nsucc(Z_{K}-E)|_{I}}z(l^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}_{I}^{l^{\prime}}.

Then using the defining identity (

3.
Elliptic singularities and the NN-elliptic sequence

3.1.
Elliptic singularities

[
La77
,
Wa70
]

We recall that a normal surface singularity
(X,o)
is
elliptic
if
\min_{l\in L_{>0}}\chi(l)=0
, or, equivalently,
\chi(Z_{min})=0
. In this case, we say that the dual resolution graph
\Gamma
is an elliptic graph.
It is known that if we decrease the decorations (Euler numbers), or we take a full subgraph of an elliptic graph, then we get either elliptic or a rational graph.

One defines the minimally elliptic cycle
C

[
La77
]
satisfying the conditions
\chi(C)=0
and

\chi(l)>0
for any
0<l<C
. It is unique with these properties, and if
\chi(l)=0
(
l\in L_{>0}:=\{l\in L:l>0\}
) then necessarily
C\leq l
. In particular,
C\leq Z_{min}
. Moreover, if
|C|
is its support (cf.
2.1
), then the uniqueness implies that the connected components of
\Gamma\setminus|C|
are rational graphs. In particular, any connected subgraph of
\Gamma\setminus|C|
is rational.

If we assume further, that
\Gamma
is the minimal resolution, then
Z_{K}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. Hence, in the numerically Gorenstein case we have

C\leq Z_{min}\leq Z_{K}.

In particular, an elliptic germ which, on the minimal resolution, realizes the equalities
C=Z_{min}=Z_{K}
is called
minimally elliptic
, cf. Laufer
[
La77
]
. They are characterized by the Gorenstein and
p_{g}=1
property, see
[
La77
,
N99a
,
N99b
]
. In fact for any elliptic singularity
|C|
supports a resolution graph of a minimally elliptic singularity.

In the sequel we always assume that the minimal resolution of the elliptic singularity
(X,o)
is also good
. Hence, in this article an elliptic graph
\Gamma
will be the dual graph of such a resolution.

Most of the properties of an elliptic singularity is guided by the
elliptic sequence
. For Gorenstein singularities it was defined by Laufer, then a definition for any elliptic germ was given by Yau
[
Y79
,
Y80
]
. Recently, Nagy and Némethi in
[
NN18
]
have constructed another version based on the unique minimal cycle
s_{[}Z_{K}]
. Note that all these versions coincide for numerically Gorenstein elliptic graphs.
More details and comparisons of the elliptic sequences can be found in
[
Y79
,
Y80
,
N99a
,
N99b
,
O05
,
NN18
,
NN19
]
.

In this article, we will only recall and discuss the properties of the sequence from
[
NN18
]
, and we will refer to it as the
NN-elliptic sequence
.

3.1.1.
Construction of the NN-elliptic sequence

Let
\Gamma
be an elliptic graph as in the previous section, ie. it is the minimal resolution graph of an elliptic singularity
(X,o)
which is also good.

The elliptic sequence consists of a sequence of cycles
\{Z_{B_{j}}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
, or a sequence of connected subgraphs
\{B_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
, where
Z_{B_{j}}
is a certain ‘minimal’
cycle associated with
B_{j}
.
\{B_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
are defined inductively as follows.

By the minimality of the resolution one has
Z_{K}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
and
Z_{K}\geq s_{[Z_{K}]}
. Moreover, since the graph is not rational, we get
Z_{K}>s_{[Z_{K}]}
, cf.
[
NN18
, Lemma 2.1.4]
. Therefore, as a ‘pre-step’ of the construction one sets the following:

B_{-1}:=\Gamma,\ \ Z_{B_{-1}}:=s_{[Z_{K}]}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ B_{0}:=|Z_{K}-s_{[Z_{K}]}|.

Then, one proves that if
Z_{K}\in L^{\prime}\setminus L
then
|C|\subset B_{0}\subsetneq\Gamma
,
B_{0}
is connected and supports a numerically Gorenstein elliptic graph with anti-canonical cycle
Z_{K}-s_{[Z_{K}]}
. Moreover, any numerical Gorenstein subgraph is contained in
B_{0}
, cf.
[
NN19
, Prop. 3.4.4]
.

Now, let
Z_{B_{0}}
be the Artin’s minimal cycle of
B_{0}
. Then
C\leq Z_{B_{0}}\leq Z_{K}-s_{Z_{K}}
. If
Z_{B_{0}}=Z_{K}-s_{[Z_{K}]}
then we stop at index
m=0
. Otherwise, the procedure continues and at the
j
-th step (
j\geq 1
) one defines
B_{j}:=|Z_{K}-s_{[Z_{K}]}-Z_{B_{0}}-\dots-Z_{B_{j-1}}|
which will be a connected numerically Gorenstein elliptic graph with anti-canonical cycle
Z_{K}-s_{[Z_{K}]}-Z_{B_{0}}-\dots-Z_{B_{j-1}}
and satisfies
|C|\subseteq B_{j}\subsetneq B_{j-1}
. Then, one sets
Z_{B_{j}}:=Z_{min}(B_{j})
for which we have
C\leq Z_{B_{1}}\leq Z_{K}-s_{[Z_{K}]}-Z_{B_{0}}-\dots-Z_{B_{j-1}}
.
After finite steps the coincidence of the minimal cycle and the canonical cycle on

B_{m}
will stop the inductive procedure. This means that
B_{m}
supports a minimally elliptic singularity with
Z_{B_{m}}=C
.

We say that the NN-elliptic sequence
\{Z_{B_{j}}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
has length
m+1
with ‘pre-term’
Z_{B_{-1}}=s_{Z_{K}}\in L^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]}
.

Remark 3.1.1
.

In particular, if
\Gamma
is a numerically Gorenstein elliptic graph then
s_{[Z_{K}]}=0
, hence
Z_{B_{-1}}=0
,
B_{0}=\Gamma
and the algorithm starts with constructing the numerically Gorenstein subgraph
B_{1}
.

3.2.
The cycles
C_{j}
and
C^{\prime}_{j}

Using the elliptic sequence, it is convenient to define the following cycles:

(3.2.1)

C_{j}=\sum_{i=-1}^{j}Z_{B_{i}}\in L^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ C^{\prime}_{j}=\sum_{i=j}^{m}Z_{B_{i}}\ \ \ (-1\leq j\leq m).

In particular,
C_{-1}=s_{[Z_{K}]}
,
C^{\prime}_{m}=C
and
C_{m}=C^{\prime}_{-1}=Z_{K}
.

We will rather use the sequence of cycles
\{C_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
instead of the minimal ones, since they hide a ‘geometric’ universal property which will be crucial in this article.

First of all, in the next lemma we summarize the main properties of the elliptic sequence and its associated cycles. The properties from (a)-(e) can be found in
[
N99a
,
NN18
,
NN19
]
. The observation from (f) is new and it will be used for our purposes.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

(a)
B_{-1}=\Gamma,\ B_{0}=|Z_{K}-s_{[Z_{K}]}|,\ B_{1}=|Z_{K}-s_{[Z_{K}]}-Z_{B_{0}}|,\ \ldots,\ B_{m}=|C|;

each
B_{j}
is connected and the inclusions
B_{j+1}\subset B_{j}
are strict for any
j\geq 0
. For
j=-1
one has
B_{0}\subsetneq B_{-1}
whenever
Z_{K}\notin L
, otherwise
B_{0}=B_{-1}
.
Furthermore,
Z_{B_{-1}}=s_{[Z_{K}]}\in L^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]}
and the other cycles
Z_{B_{0}}\supset Z_{B_{1}}\supset\cdots\supset Z_{B_{m}}=C
are integral.

(b)
(E_{v},Z_{B_{j}})=0
for any
E_{v}\subset B_{j+1}
and
-1\leq j\leq m
.
In particular,
(Z_{B_{i}},Z_{B_{j}})=(C_{i},Z_{B_{j}})=0
for all
-1\leq i<j\leq m
.

(c)
Z_{K}=\sum_{i=-1}^{m}Z_{B_{i}}
.

(d)
(E_{v},C^{\prime}_{j})=(E_{v},Z_{K})
for any
E_{v}\subset|C^{\prime}_{j}|
. In other words,

C^{\prime}_{j}=Z_{K}^{B_{j}}
, where
Z_{K}^{B_{j}}
is the anti-canonical cycle of the graph
|C^{\prime}_{j}|=B_{j}
. Furthermore,
\chi(Z_{B_{j}})=\chi(C_{j})=\chi(C_{j}^{\prime})=0
.

(e)
C_{j}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
.

(f)
(C_{j},E_{v})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }v\in{\mathcal{V}}(B_{j+1})\\
e_{v}+2&\text{if }v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1})\text{ and }(E_{B_{j+1}},E_{v})=0\\
e_{v}+1&\text{if }v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1})\text{ and }(E_{B_{j+1}},E_{v})=1\\
\end{cases}
.

Proof.

(a)-(d) follows from
[
N99a
, 2.11]
(numerically Gorenstein case) and
[
NN18
, Lemma 3.2.3]
. The proof of (e) can be found in
[
NN19
, Lemma 3.3.1]
.

(f) If
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(B_{j+1})
the statement follows from (b). Assume now that
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1})
. Note that we have
Z_{K}-C_{j}=Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}}
, hence
(C_{j},E_{v})=e_{v}+2-(Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}},E_{v})
. Therefore, we get
(C_{j},E_{v})=e_{v}+2
whenever
(E_{B_{j+1}},E_{v})=0
.

The case
(E_{B_{j+1}},E_{v})=1
will be a consequence of Lemma
6.1.1
, see Corollary
6.1.4
.
∎

Remark 3.2.3
.

Note that in general (eg. for non-numerically Gorenstein case) the cycle
s_{[Z_{K}]}\in L^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]}
is determined by the generalized Laufer algorithm, cf. sect.
2.1.2
. However, in the elliptic case it is given by the explicit formula
s_{[Z_{K}]}=Z_{K}-Z_{K}^{B_{0}}
.

The crucial fact regarding the cycles
C_{j}
is that they are the only cycles in
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]}
below the anti-canonical cycle.

Lemma 3.2.4
.

[
NN18
, Lemma 3.3.1]

Assume that
l^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]}
and
l^{\prime}\leq Z_{K}
. Then
l^{\prime}\in\{C_{-1},C_{0},\dots,C_{m}\}
.

This also means that the elliptic sequence
\{B_{j}\}
has some universal property which can be formulated as follows:

Corollary 3.2.5
.

Any numerically Gorenstein connected subgraph of the elliptic graph
\Gamma
must be one of the
B_{j}
for
j\geq 0
.

As a closure of this section, we recall the main result of
[
NN19
]
which identifies the canonical Seiberg–Witten invariant of the link of an elliptic germ with the length of the elliptic sequence. Namely, using the previous notations, we have

(3.2.6)

\mathfrak{sw}^{norm}_{0}(M(\Gamma))=m+1.

4.
The shape of the canonical polynomial for elliptic singularities

We fix an elliptic graph
\Gamma
. Associated with
\Gamma
we consider the canonical polynomial
P_{0}:=\sum_{\ell\in L}w(\ell){\mathbf{t}}^{\ell}
, cf. sect.
2.6
. Define its
support

Supp(P_{0})\subset L
as the finite set of exponents
\ell\in L
for which
w(\ell)\neq 0
.

4.1.
The structure of the exponents

4.1.1.

For any exponent
\ell=\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}}\ell_{v}E_{v}\in Supp(P_{0})
we associate the following subset of vertices

(4.1.1)

{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell):=\{v\in{\mathcal{V}}\ :\ \ell_{v}<0\}.

Note that by (
2.6.1
)
\ell
satisfies
\ell\nprec 0
, hence

(4.1.2)

{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)\subsetneq{\mathcal{V}}=:{\mathcal{V}}(B_{-1})\mbox{ \ is a proper subset.}

Since, by (
2.6.6
), one knows that
\ell\in Z_{K}-E-{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]}
, we consider the unique decomposition

(4.1.3)

\displaystyle\ell=Z_{K}-E-l^{\prime}-\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(l)}m_{v}E_{v}\mbox{ \ satisfying the following properties:}

\displaystyle(1)\ l^{\prime}\leq Z_{K},\quad\ \ \ (2)\ (Z_{K}-l^{\prime})|_{{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)}=0|_{{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)}\quad\mbox{ \ and \ }\quad\ (3)\ m_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Definition 4.1.4
.

For any exponent
\ell\in Supp(P_{0})
the unique cycle
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]}
from (
4.1.3
) will be called
the associated cycle of

\ell
.

We will also set the notation
\check{\ell}:=l^{\prime}+\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)}m_{v}E_{v}\in Supp(\check{P}_{0})
and call
\check{\ell}
the
dual exponent associated with

\ell
, where
Supp(\check{P}_{0})
denotes the set of the exponents of the dual polynomial
\check{P}_{0}({\mathbf{t}})
obtained by truncation of the series
Z_{[Z_{K}]}({\mathbf{t}})
(cf. (
2.6.6
) and (
2.6.5
)), ie. they are elements from
Supp(Z_{[Z_{K}]})
and satisfy
\check{\ell}\ngeq Z_{K}
.

4.1.2.

In the next result we prove that in the case of an elliptic graph the set of associated cycles are exactly
\{C_{j}\}
provided by the NN-elliptic sequence.

Theorem 4.1.5
.

If
l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
is the associated cycle of an exponent
\ell\in Supp(P_{0})
, then

(1)

l^{\prime}\in\{C_{-1},C_{0},\dots,C_{m-1}\}
;

(2)

{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)={\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1})
for any
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0})
, where we denote by
Supp_{j}(P_{0})\subset Supp(P_{0})
the subset of those exponents whose associated cycle is
C_{j}
.

Proof.

(1) Since
[l^{\prime}]=[Z_{K}]
and
l^{\prime}\leq Z_{K}
by construction, in order to apply Lemma
3.2.4
we only have to show that
l^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
, ie.
(l^{\prime},E_{v^{\prime}})\leq 0
for any
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}
.

First of all, in the case
v^{\prime}\notin{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(l)
we have
(E_{v^{\prime}},\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)}m_{v}E_{v})\geq 0
. Then, together with the fact that
l^{\prime}+\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(l)}m_{v}E_{v}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
one deduces the following inequalities

(l^{\prime},E_{v^{\prime}})\leq-(E_{v^{\prime}},\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)}m_{v}E_{v})\leq 0.

For
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)
, first we decompose
(l^{\prime},E_{v^{\prime}})
as

(l^{\prime},E_{v^{\prime}})=(l^{\prime}|_{{\mathcal{V}}\setminus{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)},E_{v^{\prime}})+(l^{\prime}|_{{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(l)},E_{v^{\prime}}).

Then, (
4.1.3
)(2) gives
(l^{\prime}|_{{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)},E_{v^{\prime}})=(Z_{K}|_{{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)},E_{v^{\prime}})
. Moreover (
4.1.3
)(1) and the assumption
v^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)
imply
(l^{\prime}|_{{\mathcal{V}}\setminus{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)},E_{v^{\prime}})\leq(Z_{K}|_{{\mathcal{V}}\setminus{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)},E_{v^{\prime}})
.
Therefore, one gets
(l^{\prime},E_{v^{\prime}})\leq(Z_{K},E_{v^{\prime}})\leq 0
where the last inequality is implied by the minimality of the resolution, cf. sect.
3.1
.
This proves
l^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
.

Finally, by applying Lemma
3.2.4
we get
l^{\prime}\in\{C_{-1},C_{0},\dots,C_{m-1}\}
.

Note that
C_{m}
does not appear in our list by the properness of the subset
{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)
, ie. there exists
v\in{\mathcal{V}}\setminus{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)
such that
l^{\prime}_{v}<Z_{K,v}
(
E_{v}
-coefficient of
Z_{K}
), see (
4.1.2
).

(2) Lemma
3.2.2
(d) says that for any
j\in\{-1,0,\dots,m-1\}

C^{\prime}_{j+1}=Z_{K}-C_{j}
is the cannonical cycle on its support
B_{j+1}
. Then, by using the definition (
4.1.1
) of
{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)
and the decomposition
\ell=Z_{K}-C_{j}-E-\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}^{<0}(\ell)}m_{v}E_{v}
with its properties from (
4.1.3
), the result follows.
∎

Corollary 4.1.6
.

For any
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0})
(
j\in\{-1,\dots,m-1\}
) one has
\ell|_{B_{j+1}}\geq 0
and
\ell|_{\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1}}\prec 0
.

We note that Theorem
4.1.5
induces the following decomposition:

Supp(P_{0})=\sqcup_{j=-1}^{m-1}Supp_{j}(P_{0}).

Similarly, one writes
Supp(\check{P}_{0})=\sqcup_{j=-1}^{m-1}Supp_{j}(\check{P}_{0})
. On the other hand, the dual exponent associated with
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0})
is expressed uniquely as

\check{\ell}=C_{j}+\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1})}m_{v}E_{v}

for some

. Moreover, it satisfies

for any

, which contradicts to

.

Regarding the coefficients

we have the following observation.

.

If

then

is not adjacent to

.

Proof.

For every

one has

by the previous discussion. Moreover,
(\check{\ell},E_{v^{\prime}})\leq 0
since the series

is supported on

, which implies the result.
∎

4.2.
Counting coefficients of the canonical polynomial and its consequences

Consider the coefficients
w(\ell)
corresponding to the exponents
\ell\in Supp(P_{0})
in the canonical polynomial
P_{0}({\mathbf{t}})
. These are the coefficients
z(\check{\ell})
of the series
Z_{[Z_{K}]}({\mathbf{t}})
.

Then, the first key observation of this section is that counting coefficients corresponding to the exponents in
Supp_{j}(P_{0})
is very special. Namely, we prove the following.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

(a)

For every
j\in\{0,\dots,m\}
and
-1\leq i<j
one has the identity

Q_{[Z_{K}],B_{i}\setminus B_{j}}(Z_{K})=j.

In particular,
Q_{[Z_{K}],\Gamma\setminus B_{j}}(Z_{K})=j
.

(b)

\sum_{\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0})}w(\ell)=1
for any
-1\leq j\leq m-1
.

Proof.

(a) Following (
2.5.3
) and (
2.6.3
), the counting function
Q_{[Z_{K}],B_{i}\setminus B_{j}}(Z_{K})
can be expressed via the surgery formula of the canonical Seiberg–Witten invariant as follows. Let
{\mathcal{I}}={\mathcal{V}}(B_{i}\setminus B_{j})
and denote by
\{\Gamma_{k}\}_{k}
the connected components of the subgraph
\Gamma\setminus(B_{i}\setminus B_{j})
. Then, (
2.5.3
) reads as

(4.2.2)

Q_{[Z_{K}],B_{i}\setminus B_{j}}(Z_{K})=\mathfrak{sw}^{norm}_{0}(M(\Gamma))-\sum_{k}\mathfrak{sw}^{norm}_{0}(M(\Gamma_{k})).

One of the connected components is
B_{j}
and the others are rational graphs by section
3.1
.
Moreover, by (
3.2.6
) one has
\mathfrak{sw}^{norm}_{0}(M(\Gamma))=m+1
and
\mathfrak{sw}^{norm}_{0}(M(B_{j}))=m+1-j
, while for the rational graphs we have always
\mathfrak{sw}^{norm}_{0}=0
, see Remark
2.5.2
(1). Thus, one implies that
Q_{[Z_{K}],B_{i}\setminus B_{j}}(Z_{K})=j
.

(b) By definition
Q_{[Z_{K}],\Gamma\setminus B_{j+2}}(Z_{K})=\sum_{l^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}]},\ l^{\prime}|_{\Gamma\setminus B_{j+2}}\ngeq Z_{K}|_{\Gamma\setminus B_{j+2}}}z(l^{\prime})
. For any
l^{\prime}
contributing in the previous sum one has
\ell=Z_{K}-E-l^{\prime}\in Supp(P_{0})
since there exists
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+2})
with
l_{v}\geq 0
. Moreover, Corollary
4.1.6
implies that

Q_{[Z_{K}],\Gamma\setminus B_{j+2}}(Z_{K})=\sum_{t=-1}^{j}\sum_{\ell\in Supp_{t}(P_{0})}w(\ell).

Hence, using (a) one can conclude with the following expression:

\sum_{\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0})}w(\ell)=Q_{[Z_{K}],\Gamma\setminus B_{j+2}}(Z_{K})-Q_{[Z_{K}],\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1}}(Z_{K})=1.

∎

As we have already announced in the previous section, Lemma
4.2.1
(b) deduces immediately the following:

Corollary 4.2.3
.

Supp_{j}(P_{0})\neq\emptyset
for every
-1\leq j\leq m-1
.

This ensures that

(4.2.4)

\mbox{\it the polynomial }P_{0}\ \mbox{\it completely determines the elliptic sequence }\{B_{j};C_{j}\}_{j},

except maybe
C_{-1}
, see Example
4.2.6
and Remark
4.2.7
after therein.

Note that, however
P_{0}({\mathbf{t}})
might be complicated, we can get a simplified shape if we reduce it to the variables associated with the support of the minimally elliptic cycle
|C|
.

Theorem 4.2.5
.

For any elliptic graph one has

P_{0}({\mathbf{t}}_{|C|})=\sum_{j=-1}^{m-1}{\mathbf{t}}_{|C|}^{Z_{K}-E-C_{j}}.

Proof.

Since
|C|\subseteq B_{j+1}
for any
j\in\{-1,\dots,m-1\}
, Theorem
4.1.5
implies that for any exponent
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0})
one has
\ell|_{|C|}=(Z_{K}-E-C_{j})|_{|C|}
. Moreover, the coefficient of
(Z_{K}-E-C_{j})|_{|C|}
in
P_{0}({\mathbf{t}}_{|C|})
is given by the sum of all the coefficients of the exponents from
Supp_{j}(P_{0})
, which equals
1
by Lemma
4.2.1
(b).
∎

5.
Projections of the exponents

The forthcomming sections are devoted to study how the canonical polynomial can be built up from the elliptic sequence. For that, we consider an extension
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
of elliptic graphs and, first of all, we discuss the projection of exponenets of
\Gamma
to
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

5.1.
The index of the extension

In this section we will introduce the notations
P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}({\mathbf{t}})
,
L(\Gamma^{\prime})
,
L^{\prime}(\Gamma^{\prime})
,
l^{\prime\Gamma^{\prime}}
etc., where it is needed to specify the associated graph for the corresponding objects. We will also adopt all the settings from the previous sections.

Associated with
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
we consider the projection/restriction map

(5.1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}:L(\Gamma)\ &\longrightarrow\ L(\Gamma^{\prime})\\
\ell\ \ \ \ \ \ &\longmapsto\ \ \ \ell|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}.\end{split}

In the sequel, we will study how does the map
\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}
behave on the set of exponents of the canonical polynomial
P_{0}^{\Gamma}
associated with
\Gamma
.

Let
\{B_{j}^{\Gamma}\}
be the NN-elliptic sequence of
\Gamma
together with the cycles
C_{j}^{\Gamma}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma}]}
, where
Z_{K}^{\Gamma}
is the anti-canonical cycle associated with
\Gamma
. Since an exponent
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})
can be written uniquely as
\ell=Z_{K}^{\Gamma}-E^{\Gamma}-C_{j}^{\Gamma}-\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1}^{\Gamma})}m_{v}E_{v}
for some
m_{v}\geq 0
, its image equals with

(5.1.2)

\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\ell)=\ell|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=(Z_{K}^{\Gamma}-C_{j}^{\Gamma})|_{\Gamma^{\prime}}-E^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus B_{j+1}^{\Gamma})}m_{v}E_{v}.

Consider also the NN-elliptic sequence
\{B_{t}^{\Gamma^{\prime}},C_{t}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}\}
of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Then, by the universal property Corollary
3.2.5
follows that any of the numerically Gorenstein subgraphs
B_{t}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
for
t\geq 0
must be equal to one of the
B_{i}^{\Gamma}
for some
i\geq 0
. In particular, there exists an
i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}\geq 0
, called the
index of extension
, such that

(5.1.3)

B_{i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma}\subseteq\Gamma^{\prime}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ B_{i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1}^{\Gamma}\nsubseteq\Gamma^{\prime}.

In this case
\{B_{-1}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}=\Gamma^{\prime},B_{i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma},B_{i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}+1}^{\Gamma}\,\dots,B_{m}^{\Gamma}\}
is exactly the elliptic sequence of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Therefore, we are interested to consider those exponents
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})
for which
j\geq i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1
. In this case, the canonical cycle on
B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}\subset B_{i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma}
is expressed as
Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}}=Z_{K}^{\Gamma}-C_{j}^{\Gamma}=Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-C_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
. Hence, according to (
5.1.2
), we get

(5.1.4)

\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\ell)=Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-E^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-C_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus B_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}+1}^{\Gamma^{\prime}})}m_{v}E_{v},

using also the identification
B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}=B_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}+1}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.

In the followings,
we will prove that with certain conditions,
\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\ell)
will be an exponent of
\Gamma^{\prime}
belonging to
Supp_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}(P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}})
.

5.2.
The ‘dualized map’
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}

It will be useful to introduce the ‘dualized map’ of
\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}
defined by
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}:L^{\prime}(\Gamma)_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma}]}\to L^{\prime}(\Gamma^{\prime})_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}]}
,
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(l^{\prime}):=Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-E^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(Z_{K}^{\Gamma}-E^{\Gamma}-l^{\prime})
, and understand its behaviour on the set of dual exponents
Supp_{j}(\check{P}_{0}^{\Gamma})
for
j\geq i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1
. For this reason, we make first the following preparation.

5.2.1.

In general, we consider any good resolution graph
\Gamma
and fix a subset
I\subset{\mathcal{V}}
of its vertices.
The set of vertices
{\mathcal{V}}\setminus I
determines the connected full subgraphs
\{\Gamma_{k}\}_{k}
with vertices

{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma_{k})
, i.e.
\cup_{k}{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma_{k})={\mathcal{V}}\setminus I
. We associate with any
\Gamma_{k}
the lattices

L(\Gamma_{k})
and
L^{\prime}(\Gamma_{k})
as well, endowed with the corresponding intersection forms.
Then for each
k
one considers the inclusion operator
j_{k}:L(\Gamma_{k})\to L(\Gamma)
,

E_{v}^{\Gamma_{k}}\mapsto E_{v}^{\Gamma}
, identifying naturally the corresponding
E
-base elements. This preserves the intersection forms.

Let
j_{k}^{*}:L^{\prime}(\Gamma)\to L^{\prime}(\Gamma_{k})
be the dual operator, defined by

j_{k}^{*}(E_{v}^{*\Gamma})=E_{v}^{*\Gamma_{k}}
if
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma_{k})
, and
j_{k}^{*}(E_{v}^{*\Gamma})=0
otherwise.
Note that
j^{*}_{k}(E_{v}^{\Gamma})=E_{v}^{\Gamma_{k}}
for any
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma_{k})
.
Futhermore, we have the projection formula

(5.2.1)

(j^{*}_{k}(l^{\prime}),\ell)_{\Gamma_{k}}=(l^{\prime},j_{k}(\ell))_{\Gamma}

for any
\l^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}(\Gamma)
and
\ell\in L(\Gamma_{k})
,
which also implies that

(5.2.2)

j^{*}_{k}(Z_{K}^{\Gamma})=Z_{K}^{\Gamma_{k}}.

Similarly, this identification holds for the minimal cycles
s_{h}
as well:

Lemma 5.2.3

(
[
LSz16
]
)
.

For any
h\in H
one has
j^{*}_{k}(s_{h}^{\Gamma})=s_{[j^{*}_{k}(s_{h}^{\Gamma})]}^{\Gamma_{k}}\in L^{\prime}(\Gamma_{k})
.

5.2.2.
Description of
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}|_{Supp_{j}(\check{P}_{0}^{\Gamma})}

Let
\check{\ell}=C_{j}^{\Gamma}+\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1}^{\Gamma})}m_{v}E_{v}\in Supp_{j}(\check{P}_{0}^{\Gamma})
be a dual exponent for some
j\geq i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1
. Then, by (
5.1.4
) one has

(5.2.4)

\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell})=C_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}+\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma^{\prime}\setminus B_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}+1}^{\Gamma^{\prime}})}m_{v}E_{v}.

On the other hand, one proves that the dual operator
j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
gives the concrete relationship between the cycles
C_{j}^{\Gamma}
and
C_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
as follows.

Lemma 5.2.5
.

j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(C_{j}^{\Gamma})=C_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
for any
j\geq i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1
.

Proof.

Since
Z_{K}^{\Gamma}-C_{j}^{\Gamma}=Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}}
is supported on
B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}\subset B_{i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
, it follows that
j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}})=Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}}=j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(Z_{K}^{\Gamma})-j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(C_{j}^{\Gamma})
. Furthermore, by (
5.2.2
) we get
j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(C_{j}^{\Gamma})=Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}}
. This, composed with the identity
Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}^{\Gamma}}=Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-C_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
, implies that
j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(C_{j}^{\Gamma})=C_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.
∎

Next, we define the following subsets of vertices:
\partial_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})
is the set of vertices of
\Gamma^{\prime}
which are connected by an edge to
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\Gamma
. Similarly,
\partial_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
denotes the set of vertices
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime})
which are connected to
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since both
\Gamma^{\prime}
and
\Gamma
are connected and trees,
\partial_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
can be decomposed into
\sqcup_{v\in\partial_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})}\partial_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)_{v}
, where
\partial_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)_{v}
contains all the neighbours of
v
in
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}
.

By
5.2.1
we know that
j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(E_{u})=0
for any
u\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime})\setminus\partial_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)
, while
j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(E_{u})=-E^{*}_{v}
for any
u\in\partial_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)_{v}
and
v\in\partial_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})
. Therefore, a straightforward calculation implies that for any
\check{\ell}=C_{j}^{\Gamma}+\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1}^{\Gamma})}m_{v}E_{v}
we have

(5.2.6)

\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell})=j^{*}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\check{\ell})+\sum_{v\in\partial_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})}\big(\sum_{u\in\partial_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)_{v}}m_{u}\big)\cdot E^{*}_{v}.

This description of
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}
and the projection formula (
5.2.1
) immediately gives us the following identities.

Lemma 5.2.7
.

For any
j\geq i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1
and
\check{\ell}\in Supp_{j}(\check{P}^{\Gamma}_{0})
one has

(1)
(\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell}),E_{v})_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=(\check{\ell},E_{v})_{\Gamma}
for
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma^{\prime})\setminus\partial_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})
and

(2)
(\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell}),E_{v})_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=(\check{\ell},E_{v})_{\Gamma}-\sum_{u\in\partial_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(\Gamma)_{v}}m_{u}
for every
v\in\partial_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})
.

Now, we can prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.2.8
.

If every
v\in\partial_{\Gamma}(\Gamma^{\prime})
is an end-vertex of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, then
\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\ell)\in Supp_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}(P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}})
whenever
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})
(
j\geq i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1
).

Proof.

Let
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})
( for some
j\geq i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1
) be an exponent of
\Gamma
and
\check{\ell}=C_{j}^{\Gamma}+\sum_{v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1}^{\Gamma})}m_{v}E_{v}
be its dual exponent. We have to show that
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell})
is a dual exponent of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, that is an exponent of
Z_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}]}
and satisfies
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell})\ngeq Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
, where
Z_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}]}
is the
[Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}]
-part of the topological Poincaré series associated with
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

The latter is immediate from the identities (
5.2.4
) and
Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-C_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}=Z_{K}^{B_{j-i_{\tiny(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}+1}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}}
. On the other hand, we know that
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell})\in Supp(Z_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}]})
if and only if
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell})\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}]}
and for every node

of
\Gamma^{\prime}
(ie.

) the inequality
-(\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell}),E_{v})_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\leq\delta^{\Gamma^{\prime}}_{v}-2
is satisfied.
By assumption one has
\check{\ell}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma}]}
and
-(\check{\ell},E_{v})\leq\delta_{v}^{\Gamma}-2
for every

. Therefore, Lemma

implies that

for any

, hence
\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell})\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}]}
. Moreover, by the assumption on

and Lemma
5.2.7
(1), for every node

of
\Gamma^{\prime}
we have
-(\check{\pi}_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}(\check{\ell}),E_{v})_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=-(\check{\ell},E_{v})_{\Gamma}\leq\delta_{v}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}-2
, since in this case

.

6.
Extensions of elliptic graphs and their exponents

In this part we consider the converse of the previous section and study how the exponents of
\Gamma^{\prime}
extend to the exponents of
\Gamma
.

6.1.
Small extensions of elliptic graphs

In the following lemma we recall and slightly extend the statement of
[
NN18
, Lemma 3.2.7]
.

Lemma 6.1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an elliptic graph and
\{B_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
the NN-elliptic sequence. Assume that
\Gamma
can be extended to a new elliptic graph
\Gamma_{new}
by attaching new vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{s}
to a fixed vertex
v_{0}\in\mathcal{V}(\Gamma)
. Then

(a)

v_{0}\in{\mathcal{V}}(B_{-1}\setminus B_{1})
,

(b)

E_{v_{0}}
-multiplicity of
Z_{min}^{\Gamma}
is
1
and

(c)

(Z_{min}^{\Gamma},E_{v_{0}})_{\Gamma}\leq 1-s
.

Furthermore, if
v_{0}\in{\mathcal{V}}(B_{0}\setminus B_{1})
then
v_{0}
is an end-vertex of
B_{0}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
v_{0}\in{\mathcal{V}}(B_{1})
. Then the
E_{v_{0}}
-multiplicity of the cycle
Z_{B_{0}}+Z_{B_{1}}
, denoted by
m_{v_{0}}(Z_{B_{0}}+Z_{B_{1}})
, is at least
2
and
\chi_{\Gamma}(Z_{B_{0}}+Z_{B_{1}})=0
implied by Lemma
3.2.2
. Hence,
\chi_{\Gamma_{new}}(Z_{B_{0}}+Z_{B_{1}}+E_{v_{i}})<0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
which contradicts to the ellipticity of
\Gamma_{new}
. Thus,
v_{0}\in{\mathcal{V}}(B_{-1}\setminus B_{1})
.

Consider the Laufer computation sequence
\{z_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
of
Z_{min}^{\Gamma}
in
\Gamma
(ie.
z_{n}=Z_{min}^{\Gamma}
). This can be extended in
\Gamma_{new}
to a Laufer’s computation sequence (†)
z_{0},\dots,Z_{min}^{\Gamma},z_{n+1},\dots,Z_{min}^{\Gamma_{new}}
. Since
(Z_{min}^{\Gamma},E_{v})_{\Gamma_{new}}=(Z_{min}^{\Gamma},E_{v})_{\Gamma}\leq 0
for any
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma)
and
(Z_{min}^{\Gamma},E_{v_{i}})_{\Gamma_{new}}=m_{v_{0}}(Z_{min}^{\Gamma})>0
, part of the (†) computation sequence can be chosen in such a way that

(6.1.2)

z_{n+t}=Z_{min}^{\Gamma}+\sum_{j=1}^{t}E_{v_{j}}\ \ \mbox{for}\ 1\leq t\leq s.

On the other hand, we know that
\chi_{\Gamma_{new}}(Z_{min}^{\Gamma})=\chi_{\Gamma_{new}}(Z_{min}^{\Gamma_{new}})=0
and
\chi_{\Gamma_{new}}
is decreasing along the sequence (†). Hence for any
t\geq n
we must have
(z_{t},E_{v(t)})_{\Gamma_{new}}=1
where
z_{t+1}=z_{t}+E_{v(t)}
. In particular,
m_{v_{0}}(Z_{min}^{\Gamma})=1
which proves (b). We get the inequality
(z_{n+s},E_{v(n+s)})_{\Gamma_{new}}\leq 1
too.

Moreover, if the new decorations associated with
v_{i}
are
e_{i}
, then (b) shows that for any
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}

(z_{n+s},E_{v_{i}})_{\Gamma_{new}}=e_{v_{i}}+1
, which is stricly negative. Therefore, the only candidate for
E_{v(n+s)}
would be
E_{v_{0}}
and we get
(z_{n+s},E_{v_{0}})_{\Gamma_{new}}\leq 1
, or, equivalently,
(Z_{min}^{\Gamma},E_{v_{0}})_{\Gamma}\leq 1-s
, thereby proving (c).

Now, assume
v_{0}\in{\mathcal{V}}(B_{0}\setminus B_{1})
. Then
(Z_{B_{0}},E_{v_{i}})_{\Gamma_{new}}=m_{v_{0}}(Z_{B_{0}})\geq 1
for any
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
. Moreover, the facts
\chi_{\Gamma_{new}}(Z_{B_{0}})=0
and
\chi_{\Gamma_{new}}(Z_{B_{0}}+E_{v_{i}})\geq 0
imply
m_{v_{0}}(Z_{B_{0}})=1
, which gives also the equality
m_{v_{0}}(Z_{K}^{B_{0}})=1
since
v_{0}\in{\mathcal{V}}(B_{0}\setminus B_{1})
. This shows that
v_{0}
must be an end-vertex of
B_{0}
. Indeed, by the adjunction formula
v_{0}
is either an end-vertex of
B_{0}
or it has two neighbours in
B_{0}
both with multiplicity
1
in
Z_{K}^{B_{0}}
. But the last case would generate by repeating the argument an infinite string, all with multiplicity one, which cannot happen.
∎

Definition 6.1.3
.

For the ease of terminology, an extension of elliptic graphs with properties as in Lemma
6.1.1
will be called a
small
extension.

Corollary 6.1.4

(Part of Lemma
3.2.2
(f))
.

Let
\Gamma
be an elliptic graph,
\{B_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
its associated
NN
-elliptic sequence and

the corresponding cycles. Then in the case
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma\setminus B_{j+1})
and
(E_{B_{j+1}},E_{v})=1
one has
(C_{j},E_{v})=e_{v}+1
.

Proof.

As we have seen in the proof of Lemma
3.2.2
one has the identity
(C_{j},E_{v})=e_{v}+2-(Z_{K}^{B_{j+1}},E_{v})
. By assumption, the numerically Gorenstein elliptic graph

has a small extension by attaching to it the vertex

. We denote the neighbour of

∎

Now assume

By Theorem

\{v_{1},\dots,v_{s}\}
to an end-vertex
v_{0}
of
\Gamma
.

Let
\check{\ell}\in Supp_{j}(\check{P}_{0}^{\Gamma})
be a dual exponent of
\Gamma
which can be extended to
\Gamma_{new}
and consider all its extensions
\check{\ell}_{new}\in\check{\pi}^{-1}_{(\Gamma,\Gamma_{new})}(\check{\ell})\subset Supp_{j+i(\Gamma,\Gamma_{new})}(\check{P}_{0}^{\Gamma_{new}})
. Then, the aim of this section is to prove the following identity for the coefficients.

Theorem 6.3.1
.

(6.3.2)

\sum_{\check{\ell}_{new}\in\check{\pi}^{-1}_{(\Gamma,\Gamma_{new})}(\check{\ell})}z(\check{\ell}_{new})=z(\check{\ell}).

Proof.

We may write the fixed dual exponent in the following form:
\check{\ell}=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{E}}\check{\ell}^{*}_{v}E_{v}^{*}
, where
\check{\ell}^{*}_{v}=-(\check{\ell},E_{v})_{\Gamma}
,
\mathcal{N}
denotes the set of nodes and
{\mathcal{E}}
is the set of end-vertices of
\Gamma
. Then, by (
2.3.2
) its coefficient in
Z_{[Z_{K}^{\Gamma}]}
equals
z(\check{\ell})=(-1)^{\sum_{v\in\mathcal{N}}\check{\ell}_{v}^{*}}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{N}}\binom{\delta_{v}-2}{\check{\ell}^{*}_{v}}
. Note that
v_{0}
does not contribute to
z(\check{\ell})
, since by assumption
v_{0}
is an end-vertex of
\Gamma
. On the other hand, by Lemma
5.2.7
one has
(\check{\ell},E_{v})_{\Gamma}=(\check{\ell}_{new},E_{v})_{\Gamma_{new}}
for any
v\in\mathcal{V}(\Gamma)\setminus v_{0}
and
\check{\ell}_{new}\in\check{\pi}^{-1}_{(\Gamma,\Gamma_{new})}(\check{\ell})
. Moreover, the vertices
v_{i}

(i\in\{1,\dots,s\})
do not contribute to
z(\check{\ell}_{new})
since they are end-vertices of
\Gamma_{new}
. Therefore,
z(\check{\ell}_{new})=z(\check{\ell})\cdot z_{v_{0}}(\check{\ell}_{new})
, where

(6.3.3)

z_{v_{0}}(\check{\ell}_{new})=(-1)^{-(\check{\ell}_{new},E_{v_{0}})_{\Gamma_{new}}}\cdot\binom{s-1}{-(\check{\ell}_{new},E_{v_{0}})_{\Gamma_{new}}}

is the contribution of
v_{0}
to
z(\check{\ell}_{new})
. This reduces (
6.3.2
) to the following identity:

(6.3.4)

\sum_{\check{\ell}_{new}\in\check{\pi}^{-1}_{(\Gamma,\Gamma_{new})}(\check{\ell})}z_{v_{0}}(\check{\ell}_{new})=1.

Now, consider the cycles
\ell^{\prime},s(\ell^{\prime})\in L^{\prime}(\Gamma_{new})
associated with
\check{\ell}
by the algorithm developed in section
6.2.1
. We have also proved that
(s(\ell^{\prime}),E_{v})_{\Gamma_{new}}=(\check{\ell},E_{v})_{\Gamma}
for every
v\in\mathcal{V}(\Gamma)\setminus v_{0}
. In the case of
v_{0}
, we set the notation
m:=-(s(\ell^{\prime}),E_{v_{0}})_{\Gamma_{new}}
for simplicity. Then, by (
6.2.2
) the extensions are given by
\check{\ell}_{new}(N,m_{i})=s(\ell^{\prime})+\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}E_{v_{i}}
for all possible
m_{i}\geq 0
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}=N
and
N\in[m-s+1,m]\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Hence, for fixed
N
and
m_{i}
, (
6.3.3
) reads as

z_{v_{0}}(\check{\ell}_{new}(N,m_{i}))=(-1)^{m-N}\cdot\binom{s-1}{m-N}.

On the other hand, the number of partitions
\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}=N
for a fixed
N
is counted by the Ehrhart polynomial
\mathcal{L}_{\Delta_{s-1}}(N)
of the standard
(s-1)
-simplex
\Delta_{s-1}
, whose explicit expression is given by
\mathcal{L}_{\Delta_{s-1}}(N)=\binom{N+s-1}{s-1}
, see
[
BR15
, Theorem 2.2]
for more details.
This implies that (
6.3.4
) is equivalent with the identity

\sum_{N\in[m-s+1,m]\cap\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}(-1)^{m-N}\cdot\binom{s-1}{m-N}\cdot\binom{N+s-1}{s-1}=1,

which can be deduced by part (a) of the following lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.3.5
.

(a) For any non-negative integers
d,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
one has

S_{d,m}=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{d,m\}}(-1)^{i}\cdot\binom{d}{i}\cdot\binom{m+d-i}{d}=1.

(b) Assume
m\leq d
. Then

S^{\prime}_{d,m}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\cdot\binom{d}{i}\cdot\binom{m+d-i-1}{d-1}=0.

Proof.

By using the binomial expression
\binom{p}{r}=\binom{p-1}{r}+\binom{p-1}{r-1},p>r
consequently, it can be proved that
S_{d,m}=S_{d,m-1}+S_{d-1,m}-S_{d-1,m-1}
. Then, in case (a) one can proceed easily by induction. Using again the same binomial expression, (b) follows from (a).
∎

6.5.2

\Gamma_{i}

\Gamma^{\prime}

\Gamma

7.
Good extensions and an inclusion type formula for canonical polynomials

In this section we will introduce the notion of good extensions and characterize them with a natural inclusion type formula for the corresponding canonical polynomials.

7.1.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
be an extension of elliptic graphs such that (
6.5.1
) holds, ie.
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}
is attached to
\Gamma^{\prime}
through end-vertices of
\Gamma^{\prime}
, cf. sect.
6.5
.

Definition 7.1.1
.

We say that
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
is a
good extension
if every
\ell^{\prime}\in Supp(P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}})
can be extended to an exponent of
\Gamma
.

Example 7.1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be an elliptic graph and
\{B_{j};C_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
its NN-elliptic sequence. Then the extension
B_{m}\subset\Gamma
is always good. Indeed, the only exponent of the minimal elliptic graph
B_{m}
is
Z_{K}^{B_{m}}-E^{B_{m}}=C-E^{B_{m}}
which can be realized as the projection of any of the exponents from
Supp_{m-1}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})
.

Now, let
\Gamma
be an elliptic graph,
\{B_{j};C_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
its associated NN-elliptic sequence and let
P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{\ell}w(\ell){\mathbf{t}}^{\ell}
be its canonical polynomial. Then for any
i\in\{0,\dots,m\}
we define the following truncated polynomials:

(7.1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{i}P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}}):=\sum_{\ell\in Supp(P_{0}^{\Gamma})\atop\ell|_{\Gamma\setminus B_{i}}<0}w(\ell){\mathbf{t}}^{\ell}.

(One can also set
{\mathcal{F}}_{-1}P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}}):=P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}})
for convenience.)

By Corollary
4.1.6
, this means that for a fixed
i\geq 0
,
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}P_{0}^{\Gamma}
consists of the monomials with exponents
\ell\in Supp_{j}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})
where
i-1\leq j\leq m-1
. In particular, this implies that

(7.1.4)

{\mathcal{F}}_{0}P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{0}^{\Gamma}.

Then we prove the following statement.

Theorem 7.1.5
.

The extension
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
is good if and only if the corresponding canonical polynomials satisfy the following identity:

{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}})=P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}({\mathbf{t}}),

where
i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}
denotes the index of the extension as in section
5.1
.

Proof.

First of all, note that the identity implies that any exponent of
P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
is realized in
P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}})
as the projection of at least one exponent of
\Gamma
, hence the extension is good.

Conversely, assume
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
is a good extension. Then by Theorem
5.2.8
the map

\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}|_{Supp_{j}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})}:Supp_{j}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})\to Supp_{j-i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}(P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}})

is well defined and surjective for any
j\geq i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1
. Hence, the extensions of the exponents of
P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
to
\Gamma
are the exponents in the set
\cup_{j\geq i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}-1}Supp_{j}(P_{0}^{\Gamma})
. Thus, in order to compare the two canonical polynomial, one has to consider only those monomials in
P_{0}^{\Gamma}
which are exactly in the truncated part
{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}P_{0}^{\Gamma}
.

On the other hand, for any
\ell^{\prime}\in Supp(P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}})
we have proved in Theorem
6.5.2
that

\sum_{\ell\in\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}^{-1}(\ell^{\prime})}w(\ell)=w(\ell^{\prime}).

This implies that the sum of monomials
\sum_{\ell\in\pi_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}^{-1}(\ell^{\prime})}w(\ell){\mathbf{t}}^{\ell}_{\Gamma}
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{i_{(\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma)}}P_{0}^{\Gamma}
reduced to the variables of the vertices of
\Gamma^{\prime}
gives exactly the monomial
w(\ell^{\prime}){\mathbf{t}}^{\ell^{\prime}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
in
P_{0}^{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.
∎

Corollary 7.1.6
.

Let
\{B_{j};C_{j}\}_{j=-1}^{m}
be the NN-elliptic sequence of some
\Gamma
. If
B_{j}\subset\Gamma
is a good extension, then

{\mathcal{F}}_{j}P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}}_{B_{j}})=P_{0}^{B_{j}}({\mathbf{t}}).

Remark 7.1.7
.

(1) In particular, if
B_{0}\subset\Gamma
is good, then Corollary
7.1.6
and (
7.1.4
) imply that the canonical polynomial of
\Gamma
reduced to the vertices of
B_{0}
is the same as the canonical polynomial of
B_{0}
, ie.
P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}}_{B_{0}})=P_{0}^{B_{0}}({\mathbf{t}})
.

(2) Note that for
j=m
one deduces the equality
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}}_{B_{m}})=P_{0}^{B_{m}}({\mathbf{t}})={\mathbf{t}}^{C-E}
, where
B_{m}=|C|
is the support of the minimal elliptic cycle
C
. (See also Theorem
4.2.5
.)

7.2.
An example

Let
\Gamma
be the subgraph of the elliptic graph considered in Figure
2
, generated by the vertices
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{12}
and the corresponding edges. Then
\Gamma
is non-numerically Gorenstein and its NN-elliptic sequence consists of the following subgraphs:

B_{-1}^{\Gamma}=\Gamma\supset B_{0}^{\Gamma}=\langle E_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{10}\supset B_{1}^{\Gamma}=\langle E_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{6},

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes the subgraph generated by the corresponding vertices.

These informations can also be extracted from the canonical polynomial of
\Gamma
which is as follows:

\displaystyle P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}})=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{t}}^{(1,3,1,3,1,1,1,0,0,0,-1,-3)}-2{\mathbf{t}}^{(0,1,0,1,0,0,-1,-1,-2,-1,-1,-2)}+{\mathbf{t}}^{(0,1,0,1,0,0,-1,-1,-2,-2,-3,-7)}

\displaystyle-2{\mathbf{t}}^{(0,1,0,1,0,0,-1,-1,-1,-2,-3,-7)}+{\mathbf{t}}^{(0,1,0,1,0,0,-1,-1,-1,-3,-5,-12)}

\displaystyle+{\mathbf{t}}^{(0,1,0,1,0,0,-1,-2,-2,-1,-1,-2)}+{\mathbf{t}}^{(0,1,0,1,0,0,-1,-1,-1,-1,-1,-2)}

\displaystyle+{\mathbf{t}}^{(0,1,0,1,0,0,-1,-2,-1,-2,-3,-7)}+{\mathbf{t}}^{(0,1,0,1,0,0,-1,-1,-3,-1,-1,-2)}

In the case of the extension
B_{0}^{\Gamma}\subset\Gamma
we have
i_{(B_{0}^{\Gamma},\Gamma)}=0
. Moreover, since
B_{0}^{\Gamma}
is the graph for which we have calculated the canonical polynomial in Example

, one can see that

P_{0}^{\Gamma}({\mathbf{t}}_{B_{0}^{\Gamma}})=P_{0}^{B_{0}^{\Gamma}}({\mathbf{t}})
, which says that the extension
B_{0}^{\Gamma}\subset\Gamma
is good.

Aman Sangal

Abstract
We measure the branching fraction for the Singly Cabibbo-suppressed decay D 0 → K S 0 ​ K S 0 ​ π + ​ π − D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-} , and we search for CP violation via a measurement of the CP asymmetry A C ​ P A_{CP} and also the T T -odd triple-product asymmetry a C ​ P T a_{CP}^{T} . The later two measurements are complementary. We use 922 fb -1 of data recorded by the Belle experiment, which ran at the KEKB asymmetric-energy e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider. The branching fraction is measured relative to the Cabibbo-favored normalization channel D 0 → K S 0 ​ π + ​ π − D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-} . Singly Cabibbo-suppressed charm decays are expected to have especially good sensitivity to new physics effects.



I
Introduction

Equal amounts of matter and antimatter
existed in the early Universe
Allahverdi et al. 2021
.
For such an initial state to evolve into our current matter dominated universe
Canetti et al. 2012
;
Farrar and Shaposhnikov 1994
violation of
CP
(charge-conjugation and parity) symmetry
Sakharov 1967
is required.
The amount of
CP
violation (
CPV
) present in the Standard Model (SM) fails to account for the observed imbalance
between matter and antimatter
Farrar and Shaposhnikov 1994
;
Huet and Sather 1995
. Thus, it is important to search for new sources
of
CPV
.

In this note, we present the measurement of the branching fraction and search for
CPV
in the singly Cabibbo-suppressed (SCS) decay
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

cha
;
Collaboration 2022
.
The branching fraction measurement gives an order of magnitude improvement in the precision over PDG world average
Zyla et al. 2020
. In the SM framework,
CPV
is expected to be very small (
\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})
or smaller) in the charm meson decays
Grossman et al. 2007
. Any significant deviation from SM expectation will probe new physics effects beyond the SM. SCS decays are expected to be especially sensitive to physics beyond the SM, as their amplitudes receive contributions
from QCD “penguin” operators and also chromomagnetic
dipole operators
Grossman et al. 2007
.
The SCS decays
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{+}\,K^{-}
and
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

Aaij et al. 2019

are the only decay modes in which
CPV
has been
observed in the charm sector.
The
CP
asymmetry measured,

\displaystyle A_{CP}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!f)-\Gamma(\overline{D}{}^{\,0}\!\rightarrow\!\bar{f})}{\Gamma(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!f)+\Gamma(\overline{D}{}^{\,0}\!\rightarrow\!\bar{f})}\,,

(1)

is small, at the level of 0.1%.

For our analysis of
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays, we search for
CPV
in two
complementary ways. We first measure the asymmetry
A_{CP}
; a nonzero
value results from interference between contributing
decay amplitudes. The
CP
-violating interference term
is proportional to
\sin(\phi)\sin(\delta)
for
D^{0}
decays,
where
\phi
and
\delta
are the weak and strong
phase differences, respectively, between the amplitudes. Thus, to observe
A_{CP}\neq 0
,
\delta
must be nonzero.

To avoid the need for
\delta\neq 0
, we also search for

CPV
by measuring the asymmetry in the triple-product

C_{T}=\vec{p}_{K^{0}_{S}}\cdot(\vec{p}_{\pi^{+}}\times\vec{p}_{\pi^{-}})
,
where
\vec{p}_{K^{0}_{S}}
,
\vec{p}_{\pi^{+}}
, and
\vec{p}_{\pi^{-}}
are
the three-momenta of the
K^{0}_{S}
,
\pi^{+}
, and
\pi^{-}

daughters, defined in the
D^{0}
rest frame. From the two

K^{0}_{S}
in final state we choose the
K^{0}_{S}
with the higher momentum for this calculation.
The asymmetry is defined as

\displaystyle A_{T}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{N(C_{T}\!>\!0)-N(C_{T}\!<\!0)}{N(C_{T}\!>\!0)+N(C_{T}\!<\!0)}\,,

(2)

where
N(C_{T}\!>\!0)
and
N(C_{T}\!<\!0)
correspond to the yields of
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

decays having
C_{T}\!>\!0
and
C_{T}\!<\!0
, respectively.
For
\overline{D}{}^{\,0}
decays, we define the analogous
CP
conjugate quantity

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{T}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{N}(-\overline{C}_{T}>0)-\overline{N}(-\overline{C}_{T}<0)}{\overline{N}(-\overline{C}_{T}>0)+\overline{N}(-\overline{C}_{T}<0)}\,,

(3)

The difference is a
CP
violating observable

\displaystyle a_{CP}^{T}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{A_{T}-\bar{A}_{T}}{2}

(4)

proportional to
\sin(\phi)\sin(\delta)
Durieux and Grossman 2015
;
Valencia 1989
;
Bensalem and London 2001
, and, unlike
A_{CP}
,

\delta=0
results in the largest
CP
asymmetry. The observable
a_{CP}^{T}
is advantageous to measure experimentally,
as any production or detection related non
CP
asymmetry contribution cancels out.

II
Reconstruction and data sample

To measure the branching fraction and search for
CP
violation, we use 922 fb
-1
data collected by the Belle experiment
Abashian et al. 2002
running at KEKB asymmetric-energy
e^{+}e^{-}
collider
Kurokawa and Kikutani 2003
. We use Monte Carlo (MC) simulated events to optimize event selection criteria, calculate reconstruction efficiencies, and study sources of background.

We reconstruct the decay chain
D^{*+}\!\rightarrow\!D^{0}\pi^{+}_{s}
,
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
where the charge of slow pion (
\pi_{s}
) is used to tag the flavor of
D
meson. We start the reconstruction by selecting charged tracks that originate near
e^{+}e^{-}
interaction point (IP) by requiring that the impact parameter
\delta z
of a track along the
z
direction (anti-parallel to the
e^{+}
beam) satisfies
|\delta z|<5.0
cm, and that the impact parameter transverse to the
z
axis satisfies
\delta r<2.0
cm. Tracks are identified as
\pi^{\pm}
using likelihood based particle identification utilizing information from Belle subdetectors ACC (Aerogol cherenkov counter), TOF (Time of flight counter) and CDC (Central drift chamber).
K_{S}^{0}
are reconstructed as
K^{0}_{S}\!\rightarrow\!\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
using standard Belle neutral network based method
Collaboration 2022
. The invariant mass of the two pions is required to satisfy
|M(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})-m_{K_{S}^{0}}|<0.010
GeV/
c^{2}
, where
m_{K^{0}_{S}}
is the
K^{0}_{S}
mass
Zyla et al. 2020
. This range corresponds to three standard deviations in the mass resolution.

After identifying
\pi^{\pm}
and
K_{S}^{0}
candidates,
we reconstruct
D^{0}
candidates by requiring that the
four-body invariant mass
M(K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-})\equiv M

satisfy
1.810~{\rm GeV}/c^{2}<M<1.920~{\rm GeV}/c^{2}
.
We remove
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}
decays, which have the same
final-state particles, by requiring
|M(\pi^{+}\pi^{-})-m_{K^{0}_{S}}|>0.010
GeV/
c^{2}
. This criterion removes 96% of these decays. To improve the mass resolution, we apply mass-constrained vertex fits for the
K_{S}^{0}
candidates. To ensure the
D^{0}
daughters originate from a common decay vertex we perform a vertex fit using the
\pi^{\pm}
tracks and the momenta of the
K_{S}^{0}
candidates; the resulting fit quality (
\chi^{2}
) must satisfy a loose requirement to ensure that the tracks and
K^{0}_{S}
candidates are consistent with originating from a common decay vertex.

To reconstruct
D^{*+}\!\rightarrow\!D^{0}\pi^{+}_{s}
decays, we combine
D^{0}
candidates with
\pi^{+}_{s}
candidates and require that the mass difference
M(K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}\pi^{+}_{s})-M\equiv\Delta M
be less than 0.15 GeV/
c^{2}
. To reduce the combinatorial backgorund and remove the
D^{*+}
candidates coming from
B
decays, we also require that the momentum
of the
D^{*+}
candidate in the CM frame be greater than 2.5 GeV/
c
; A vertex fit is performed for
D^{*+}
, constraining the
D^{0}
and
\pi^{+}_{s}
to originate from the IP. We subsequently require
\sum(\chi^{2}/\rm{ndf})<100
, where the sum runs over the two mass-constrained
K^{0}_{S}
vertex fits, the
D^{0}
vertex fit, and the IP-constrained
D^{*+}
vertex fit, and “ndf” is the number of degrees of freedom in each fit. After applying all selection criteria, for events with
multiple
D^{*+}\!\rightarrow\!D^{0}\pi^{+}_{s},\,D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
signal candidates we retain a single candidate by choosing that with the lowest value of
\sum(\chi^{2}/\rm{ndf})
.

We measure the branching fraction relative to Cabbibbo favored
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays
observed in the same data set. The branching fraction for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
is calculated as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-108.405pt\left(\frac{N_{K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}}{N_{K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}}\right)\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}}{\varepsilon_{K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}}\right)\times\frac{\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})}{\mathcal{B}(K^{0}_{S}\!\rightarrow\!\pi^{+}\pi^{-})}\,,

where
N
is the fitted yield for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
or
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

decays;

\varepsilon
is the corresponding reconstruction efficiency,
given that
K^{0}_{S}\!\rightarrow\!\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
;
and
\mathcal{B}(K^{0}_{S}\!\rightarrow\!\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
and
\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
are the world average branching fractions
for
K^{0}_{S}\!\rightarrow\!\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}

Zyla et al. 2020
.
The selection criteria for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
are the same as
those used for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, except that only one
K^{0}_{S}

is required.

After applying all the selection criteria, the resulting reconstruction efficiencies are
\varepsilon_{K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}=(6.92\pm 0.02)\%
and
\varepsilon_{K^{0}_{S}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}=(14.97\pm 0.03)\%
, We calculate reconstruction efficiencies using MC simulation, so we consider data-MC correction factors of

0.930\pm 0.014
for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
and

0.899\pm 0.007
for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
.

D^{*+}

D^{0}

IV
Measurement of
A_{CP}

We measure the
CP
asymmetry
A_{CP}
from the difference
in signal yields for
D^{0}
and
\overline{D}{}^{\,0}
decays:

A^{\rm{det}}_{CP}{}=\frac{N(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!f)-N(\overline{D}{}^{\,0}\!\rightarrow\!\overline{f})}{N(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!f)+N(\overline{D}{}^{\,0}\!\rightarrow\!\overline{f})}\,.

(6)

The observable
A^{\rm{det}}_{CP}
includes asymmetries in production and reconstruction:

\displaystyle A^{\rm{det}}_{CP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{CP}+A_{\rm FB}+A_{\epsilon}^{\pi_{s}}\,,

(7)

where
A_{\rm FB}
is the “forward-backward” production asymmetry
Berends et al. 1973
between

D^{*+}
and
D^{*-}
due to
\gamma^{*}-Z^{0}
interference in
e^{+}e^{-}\!\rightarrow\!c\overline{c}
;
and
A_{\epsilon}^{\pi_{s}}
is the asymmetry in reconstruction efficiencies for
\pi^{\pm}_{s}
tracks.

We correct for
A_{\epsilon}^{\pi_{s}}
in
K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
events by separately weighting
D^{0}
and
\overline{D}{}^{\,0}
decays:

\displaystyle w_{D^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-A_{\epsilon}^{\pi_{s}}(p_{\rm T},\,\cos\theta_{\pi_{s}})

(8)

\displaystyle w_{\overline{D}{}^{\,0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+A_{\epsilon}^{\pi_{s}}(p_{\rm T},\,\cos\theta_{\pi_{s}})\,.

(9)

where
A_{\epsilon}^{\pi_{s}}
is the asymmetry in
\pi_{s}
detection in bins of
p_{\rm T}
and
\cos\theta_{\pi_{s}}
of
the
\pi^{\pm}_{s}
, where
p_{\rm T}
is the transverse momentum and

\theta_{\pi_{s}}
is the polar angle with respect to the
z
-axis, both evaluated in the laboratory frame.

After correcting for
A_{\epsilon}^{\pi_{s}}
, we obtain
A_{CP}^{\rm{cor}}=A_{CP}+A_{\rm FB}
. The asymmetry
A_{\rm FB}
is an
odd function of
\cos\theta^{*}
, and
A_{CP}
is an even function, where
\theta^{*}
is the polar angle
between the
D^{*\pm}
momentum and the
+z
axis in the CM frame. We thus extract

A_{CP}
and
A_{\rm FB}
via

\displaystyle A_{CP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A_{CP}^{\rm{cor}}(\cos\theta^{*})+A_{CP}^{\rm{cor}}(-\cos\theta^{*})}{2}

(10)

\displaystyle A_{\rm FB}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{A_{CP}^{\rm{cor}}(\cos\theta^{*})-A_{CP}^{\rm{cor}}(-\cos\theta^{*})}{2}\,.

(11)

We calculate
A_{CP}^{\rm{cor}}
in four bins of
\cos\theta^{*}
:

(-1.0,-0.4)
,
(-0.4,0)
,
(0,0.4)
and
(0.4,1.0)
.
We determine
A_{CP}^{\rm{cor}}
for each bin by simultaneously fitting
for
D^{0}
and
\overline{D}{}^{\,0}
signal yields for weighted events in that bin. The results for
A_{CP}^{\rm{cor}}
are combined according to Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
) to obtain
A_{CP}
and
A_{\rm FB}
. Fitting the
A_{CP}
values in bins of
\cos\theta^{*}
to a constant, we obtain
A_{CP}=[-2.51\,\pm 1.44\,({\rm stat})\,^{+0.35}_{-0.52}\,({\rm syst})]\%
, where the first uncertainty is statistical and second is systematic
Collaboration 2022
.

V
Measurement of
a_{CP}^{T}

To measure
a_{CP}^{T}
, we divide the data into four subsamples:

D^{0}
decays with
C_{T}>0
(
{\rm yield}\!=\!N_{1}
) and

C_{T}<0
(
{\rm yield}\!=\!N_{2}
); and

\overline{D}{}^{\,0}
decays with
-\overline{C}_{T}>0
(
N_{3}
) and

-\overline{C}_{T}<0
(
N_{4}
).
Thus,
A_{T}=(N_{1}-N_{2})/(N_{1}+N_{2})
,

\bar{A}_{T}=(N_{3}-N_{4})/(N_{3}+N_{4})
, and

a_{CP}^{T}=(A_{T}-\bar{A}_{T})/2
.
We fit the four subsamples simultaneously and take the
fitted parameters to be
N_{1}
,
N_{3}
,
A_{T}
, and
a_{CP}^{T}
.
The fit gives
a_{CP}^{T}=[-1.95\pm 1.42\,({\rm stat})\,^{+0.14}_{-0.12}\,({\rm syst})]\%
, where the first uncertainty is statistical and second is systematic
Collaboration 2022
.

VI
Conclusion:

In summary, using 922 fb
-1
of Belle data we report the world’s most precise branching fraction measurement for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decays. The branching fraction, measured relative to that for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, is:

\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
/
\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=[1.72\pm 0.03\,({\rm stat})\pm{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}0.04}\,({\rm syst})\,]\times 10^{-2}
. Inserting the world average value
\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=(2.80\pm 0.18)\%

Zyla et al. 2020
gives
\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=[4.82\pm 0.08\,({\rm stat})\pm^{+0.10}_{-0.11}\,({\rm syst})\pm 0.31\,({\rm norm})]\times 10^{-4}
where the last uncertainty is due to
\mathcal{B}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})
.

We report the first
CP
violation search for
D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
using
A_{CP}
and
a_{CP}^{T}
. The time-integrated
CP
asymmetry is measured to be
A_{CP}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=[-2.51\pm 1.44\,({\rm stat})\,^{+0.35}_{-0.52}\,({\rm syst})]

The
CP
-violating asymmetry
a_{CP}^{T}
is measured to be

a_{CP}^{T}(D^{0}\!\rightarrow\!K^{0}_{S}\,K^{0}_{S}\,\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=[-1.95\,\pm 1.42\,({\rm stat})\,^{+0.14}_{-0.12}\,({\rm syst})]
.
Both
A_{CP}
and
a_{CP}^{T}
measurements are consistent with zero
CP
violation.

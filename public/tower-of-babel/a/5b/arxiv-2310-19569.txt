Ehrhart theory on periodic graphs II: Stratified Ehrhart ring theory
Takuya Inoue, Yusuke Nakamura

Abstract
. We investigate the “stratified Ehrhart ring theory” for periodic graphs, which gives an algorithm for determining the growth sequences of periodic graphs. The growth sequence ( s Γ , x 0 , i ) i ≥ 0 (s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i\geq 0} is defined for a graph Γ \Gamma and its fixed vertex x 0 x_{0} , where s Γ , x 0 , i s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i} is defined as the number of vertices of Γ \Gamma at distance i i from x 0 x_{0} . Although the sequences ( s Γ , x 0 , i ) i ≥ 0 (s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i\geq 0} for periodic graphs are known to be of quasi-polynomial type, their determination had not been established, even in dimension two. Our theory and algorithm can be applied to arbitrary periodic graphs of any dimension. As an application of the algorithm, we determine the growth sequences in several new examples.

1.
Introduction

An
n
-dimensional periodic graph

(\Gamma,L)
is a pair of a directed graph
\Gamma
(that may have loops and multiple edges) and a free abelian group
L
of rank
n
such that
L
freely acts on
\Gamma
and its quotient graph
\Gamma/L
is finite (see Definition
3.2
).
For a vertex
x_{0}
of
\Gamma
, the
growth sequence

(s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i\geq 0}

(resp.
cumulative growth sequence

(b_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i\geq 0}
) is defined as the number of vertices of
\Gamma
whose distance from
x_{0}
is
i
(resp. at most
i
).
Periodic graphs naturally appear in crystallography, also appear as periodic tilings in combinatorics, and as Cayley graphs of virtually abelian groups in geometric group theory (see
[
11
]
*Section 13).
Furthermore, it is shown in
[
16
]
that the theory of growth sequence of periodic graphs potentially includes the Ehrhart theory of rational polytopes.

In
[
12
]
, Grosse-Kunstleve, Brunner and Sloane conjectured
that the growth sequences of periodic graphs are of quasi-polynomial type, i.e.,
there exist an integer
M
and a quasi-polynomial
f_{s}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that

s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}=f_{s}(i)
holds for any
i\geq M
(see Definition
3.9
).
In
[
21
]
, the second author, Sakamoto, Mase, and Nakagawa prove that this conjecture is true for any periodic graphs (Theorem
3.8
).
Although it was proved to be of quasi-polynomial type, determining the explicit formulae of growth sequences is still difficult.
Thus, the following natural question arises.

Question 1.1

(cf.
[
16
]
*Question 1.1)
.

Find an effective algorithm to determine the explicit formulae of the growth sequences.

So far, various computational methods have been established for several special classes of periodic graphs.
In
[
7
]
, Conway and Sloane study the growth sequences of the contact graphs of some lattices from the viewpoint of the Ehrhart theory, and they give explicit formulae for the root lattices
A_{d}
.
In
[
3
]
, Bacher, de la Harpe, and Venkov give the proof for the conjectural formulae for the root lattices
B_{d}
,
C_{d}
and
D_{d}
(cf.
[
2
]
).
We note that the periodic graphs
(\Gamma,L)
obtained from the lattices satisfy
\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)=1
, where
V_{\Gamma}
denotes the set of vertices of
\Gamma
.
When
\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)=1
,
V_{\Gamma}
has a monoid structure, and the growth sequence can be directly studied by the Hilbert series of the corresponding graded monoid.
However, when
\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)>1
, it is more difficult to study the growth sequence.
In
[
11
]
, Goodman-Strauss and Sloane proposed “the coloring book approach”
and obtained the growth sequence for some periodic tilings.
In
[
22
,
23
]
, Shutov and Maleev obtained the growth sequences for
tilings satisfying certain conditions that contain the 20 2-uniform tilings.
In
[
16
]
, the authors introduce a class of periodic graphs called “well-arranged”, and they give an algorithm to calculate their growth sequences. However, as far as we know, no algorithm has been proposed that can be applied to any periodic graph, even in dimension two.

The purpose of this paper is to give an algorithm for determining
the growth sequences that can be applied to all periodic graphs in all dimensions.
In Subsection
4.1
, we define invariants
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and

\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
from combinatorial information of
\Gamma
.
In Corollary
4.23
, we prove that the growth sequence
(s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i\geq 0}
is a quasi-polynomial on

i>\beta
and
\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}
is its quasi-period.
More precisely, we prove that its generating function
G_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)
is given by

G_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}t^{i}=\frac{Q(t)}{(1-t^{\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}})^{n}}

with some polynomial
Q(t)
of
\deg Q\leq\beta+n\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}
.
On the other hand, with the help of a computer program (breadth-first search algorithm),
we can compute the first few terms of
(s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i\geq 0}
.
After we compute the first
\lfloor\beta\rfloor+n\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}+1
terms,
we can determine the generating function as follows: For
\gamma:=\lfloor\beta\rfloor+n\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}
,
we have

G_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)=\frac{\bigl(\text{The terms of $(1-t^{\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}})^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{\gamma}s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}t^{i}$ of degree $\gamma$ or less}\bigr)}{(1-t^{\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}})^{n}}.

Corollary
4.23
follows from Theorem
4.20
, which we call the “stratified Ehrhart ring theory”.
Theorem
4.20
gives an algebraic meaning to the growth sequence, and by using it,
Corollary
4.23
can be proved by a standard technique in algebraic combinatorics.
In what follows, we will outline the statement of Theorem
4.20
.

First, we briefly review the Ehrhart ring of a rational polytope and its structure.
Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a rational polytope.
Then, the Ehrhart ring of a rational polytope
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as the group ring
k[A]
corresponding to the (commutative) monoid

A:=\left\{(d,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ \middle|\ y\in dQ\right\}.

This
A
has a graded monoid structure with respect to the degree function
\operatorname{deg}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:(d,y)\mapsto d
.
When
0\in\operatorname{int}(Q)
, the monoid
A
has a nice algebraic structure as follows:

Fact 1.2

(cf.
[
4
]
*Section 3.2)
.

Suppose
0\in\operatorname{int}(Q)
.
For each
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(Q)
, we take a triangulation
T_{\sigma}
of
\sigma
satisfying

V(\Delta)\subset V(\sigma)
for any
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
(see Section
2
for the notation of triangulations).
For
v\in V(Q)
, let
a_{v}
be the minimum positive integer satisfying
a_{v}v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
For
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(Q)
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta)
, we define

L_{\Delta^{\prime}}:=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Delta^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad A\left(L_{\Delta^{\prime}}\right):=A\cap\left(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times L_{\Delta^{\prime}}\right).

We also define a free submonoid
M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime}}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by

M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime}}:=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}(1,0)+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime})}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}(a_{v},a_{v}v).

Then, for
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(Q)
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
and
\Delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta)
, it follows that

•

each
A\left(L_{\Delta^{\prime}}\right)
is a free
M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime}}
-module.
Furthermore,
A\left(L_{\Delta^{\prime}}\right)
is freely generated by finitely many elements whose degrees are less than
\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime})}a_{v}
.

By using this structure (and the inclusion-exclusion principle), we can prove the counting function

\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0};\ d\mapsto\#\left\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ \middle|\ y\in dQ\right\}=\#\left\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ \middle|\ (d,y)\in A\right\}

is a quasi-polynomial.

Next, we shall explain the “stratified Ehrhart ring theory” for periodic graphs, which is an analogy of Fact
1.2
.
Let
(\Gamma,L)
be an
n
-dimensional strongly connected periodic graph, and let
x_{0}
be a vertex of
\Gamma
.
Let
V_{\Gamma}
denote the set of vertices of
\Gamma
.
We define

B:=\left\{(d,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times V_{\Gamma}\ \middle|\ d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)\leq d\right\}.

Unlike the case of the Ehrhart ring,
B
itself does not have a monoid structure when
\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)>1
, and the situation is more complicated.
In the statement of Theorem
4.20
, the
growth polytope

P_{\Gamma}
plays an important role.
The growth polytope
P_{\Gamma}\subset L_{\mathbb{R}}:=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is a rational polytope and is canonically defined from the periodic graph
\Gamma
(Definition
3.10
).
Furthermore, we fix a
periodic realization

\Phi:V_{\Gamma}\to L_{\mathbb{R}}
, that is a map satisfying
\Phi(u+y)=u+\Phi(y)
for any
y\in V_{\Gamma}
and
u\in L
(see Definition
3.4
).

Theorem 1.3

(cf. Theorem
4.20
)
.

There exist

•

\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,

•

a triangulation
T_{\sigma}
for each
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P_{\Gamma})
, and

•

\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
\beta_{\Delta,v}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P_{\Gamma})
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
and
v\in V(\Delta)

with the condition (
\spadesuit
) below:
For
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P_{\Gamma})
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
,
\Delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta)
and
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta^{\prime})
, we define
L_{\Delta^{\prime}},L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\subset L_{\mathbb{R}}
by

L_{\Delta^{\prime}}:=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Delta^{\prime},\quad L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}:=\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}(\beta_{\Delta,v},\infty)\cdot v+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime})\setminus V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}[0,\beta_{\Delta,v}]\cdot v.

(Note that we have
L_{\Delta^{\prime}}=\bigsqcup\limits_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta^{\prime})}L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
.)
For a subset
F\subset L_{\mathbb{R}}
, we define
B\left(F\right):=B\cap\left(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\Phi^{-1}\left(F\right)\right)
.
We also define a free submonoid
M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime}}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times L
by

M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime}}:=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}(1,0)+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime})}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\left(\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v),\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)v\right).

Then, for
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P_{\Gamma})
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
,
\Delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta)
and
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta^{\prime})
, it follows that

(
\spadesuit
)

each
B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
is a free
M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
-module. Furthermore,
B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
is freely generated by finitely many elements whose degrees are at most
\beta+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)
.

We define

\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}:=\operatorname{LCM}\bigl\{\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)\ \big|\ \sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P_{\Gamma}),\ \Delta\in T_{\sigma},\ v\in V(\Delta)\bigr\}.

In Corollary
4.23
, by using the algebraic structure in Theorem
1.3
(and the inclusion-exclusion principle), we prove that the generating function
G_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)
is given by the form

G_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)=\frac{Q(t)}{(1-t^{\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}})^{n}}

with some polynomial
Q(t)
of
\deg Q\leq\beta+n\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}
.

In
[
23
]
and
[
11
]
,
similar ideas of calculating the growth sequence by partitioning
V_{\Gamma}
into finite regions, as in Theorem
1.3
, also appear (although in both papers, the construction is explained only for a specific class of planar graphs and not for general graphs in arbitrary dimension).
Theorem
1.3
can be said to be a rigorous formulation and a generalization of their ideas.

In
[
16
]
, the authors introduce a class of periodic graphs called “well-arranged”.
For well-arranged periodic graphs, they prove that
\beta=0
and
\beta_{\Delta,v}=0
satisfy the statement of Theorem
1.3
(see
[
16
]
*Claim 4.10). As a consequence, they prove that their growth sequences are quasi-polynomial on
i>0
.

In the two-dimensional case,
it is relatively simple to implement our algorithm into a computer program
since there is no need to consider the complicated triangulations of the facets of
P_{\Gamma}
(see Subsection
4.2
and Remark
5.1
).
In fact, using the computer program, the growth sequences of the examples treated in
[
11
]
can be computed automatically without separate consideration (see Subsection
5.1
).
In Subsection
5.1
, we also examine a
6
-uniform tiling.
As far as we know, this is the first time the growth series of this example has been determined with a proof.
In Subsection
5.2
, we illustrate the algorithm for a
3
-uniform tiling.
In higher dimensions, although it is possible to implement the algorithm in a computer program,
it is practical to give the triangulation by hand.
In Subsections
5.3
and
5.4
, we illustrate the algorithm for the periodic graphs corresponding to two carbon allotropes called K6 and
CFS
.
There, the triangulation and other calculations are partially given by hand, and the rest is done by a computer program.
As far as we know, this is the first time the growth series of these two examples have been determined with proofs.

3.
Preliminaries

Following
[
16
]
, we introduce notations related to periodic graphs.

3.1.
Graphs and walks

In this paper, a
graph
means a directed weighted graph which may have loops and multiple edges.
A graph
\Gamma=(V_{\Gamma},E_{\Gamma},s_{\Gamma},t_{\Gamma},w_{\Gamma})

consists of the set
V_{\Gamma}
of vertices, the set
E_{\Gamma}
of edges,
the source function
s_{\Gamma}:E_{\Gamma}\to V_{\Gamma}
,
the target function
t_{\Gamma}:E_{\Gamma}\to V_{\Gamma}
, and
the weight function
w_{\Gamma}:E_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
We often abbreviate
s_{\Gamma}
,
t_{\Gamma}
and
w_{\Gamma}
to
s
,
t
and
w

when no confusion can arise.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(V_{\Gamma},E_{\Gamma},s,t,w)
be a graph.

(1)

\Gamma
is called to be
unweighted
if
w(e)=1
holds for any
e\in E_{\Gamma}
.

\Gamma
is called to be
undirected
when
there exists an involution
E_{\Gamma}\to E_{\Gamma};\ e\mapsto e^{\prime}

such that
s(e)=t(e^{\prime})
,
t(e)=s(e^{\prime})
and
w(e)=w(e^{\prime})
.

(2)

A
walk

p
in
\Gamma
is a sequence
e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{\ell}
of edges
e_{i}
of
\Gamma
satisfying

t(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for each
i=1,\ldots,\ell-1
.
We define

s(p):=s(e_{1}),\quad t(p):=t(e_{\ell}),\quad w(p):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w(e_{i}),\quad\operatorname{length}(p):=\ell.

Note that we have
w(p)=\operatorname{length}(p)
if
\Gamma
is unweighted.

We say that “
p
is a walk from
x
to
y
” when
x=s(p)
and
y=t(p)
.
We also define the
support

\operatorname{supp}(p)\subset V_{\Gamma}
of
p
by

\operatorname{supp}(p):=\{s(e_{1}),t(e_{1}),t(e_{2}),\ldots,t(e_{\ell})\}\subset V_{\Gamma}.

By convention, each vertex
v\in V_{\Gamma}
is also considered as a walk of length
0
.
This is called the
trivial walk
at
v
and denoted by
\emptyset_{v}
: i.e.,
we define

s(\emptyset_{v}):=v,\ \ t(\emptyset_{v}):=v,\ \ w(\emptyset_{v}):=0,\ \ \operatorname{length}(\emptyset_{v}):=0,\ \ \operatorname{supp}(\emptyset_{v}):=\{v\}.

(3)

A
path
in
\Gamma
is a walk
e_{1}\cdots e_{\ell}
such that
s(e_{1}),t(e_{1}),t(e_{2}),\ldots,t(e_{\ell})
are distinct.
A walk of length
0
is considered as a path.

(4)

A
cycle
in
\Gamma
is a walk
e_{1}\cdots e_{\ell}
with
s(e_{1})=t(e_{\ell})

such that
t(e_{1}),t(e_{2}),\ldots,t(e_{\ell})
are distinct.
A walk of length
0
is
NOT
considered as a cycle.

\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma}
denotes the set of cycles in
\Gamma
.

(5)

For
x,y\in V_{\Gamma}
,
d_{\Gamma}(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
denotes the smallest weight
w(p)
of any walk
p
from
x
to
y
.
By convention, we define
d_{\Gamma}(x,y)=\infty
when there is no walk from
x
to
y
.
A graph
\Gamma
is said to be
strongly connected

when we have
d_{\Gamma}(x,y)<\infty
for any
x,y\in V_{\Gamma}
.
When
\Gamma
is undirected, we have
d_{\Gamma}(x,y)=d_{\Gamma}(y,x)
for any
x,y\in V_{\Gamma}
.

(6)

C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
denotes the group of
1
-chains on
\Gamma
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e.,

C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
is a free abelian group generated by
E_{\Gamma}
.
For a walk
p=e_{1}\cdots e_{\ell}
in
\Gamma
, let
\langle p\rangle
denote the
1
-chain

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}e_{i}\in C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
.

H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})\subset C_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
denotes the
1
-st homology group, i.e.,

H_{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
is a subgroup generated by
\langle p\rangle
for
p\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma}
.
We refer the reader to
[
25
]
for more detail.

3.2.
Periodic graphs

Definition 3.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer.
An
n
-dimensional periodic graph

(\Gamma,L)
is a graph
\Gamma
and
a free abelian group
L\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
of rank
n
with the following two conditions:

•

L
freely acts on both
V_{\Gamma}
and
E_{\Gamma}
,
and their quotients
V_{\Gamma}/L
and
E_{\Gamma}/L
are finite sets.

•

This action preserves the edge relations and the weight function, i.e., for any
u\in L
and
e\in E_{\Gamma}
,
we have
s_{\Gamma}(u(e))=u(s_{\Gamma}(e))
,
t_{\Gamma}(u(e))=u(t_{\Gamma}(e))
and

w_{\Gamma}(u(e))=w_{\Gamma}(e)
.

If
(\Gamma,L)
is an
n
-dimensional periodic graph,
then the
quotient graph

\Gamma/L=(V_{\Gamma/L},E_{\Gamma/L},s_{\Gamma/L},t_{\Gamma/L},w_{\Gamma/L})
is defined by

V_{\Gamma/L}:=V_{\Gamma}/L
,
E_{\Gamma/L}:=E_{\Gamma}/L
,
and the functions
s_{\Gamma/L}:E_{\Gamma}/L\to V_{\Gamma}/L
,

t_{\Gamma/L}:E_{\Gamma}/L\to V_{\Gamma}/L
, and

w_{\Gamma/L}:E_{\Gamma}/L\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
induced from
s_{\Gamma}
,
t_{\Gamma}
, and
w_{\Gamma}
.
Note that the functions
s_{\Gamma/L}
,
t_{\Gamma/L}
and
w_{\Gamma/L}

are well-defined due to the second condition in Definition
3.2
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\Gamma,L)
be an
n
-dimensional periodic graph.

(1)

Since
L
is an abelian group, we use the additive notation:
for
u\in L
,
x\in V_{\Gamma}
,
e\in E_{\Gamma}
and a walk
p=e_{1}\cdots e_{\ell}
,

u+x:=u(x),\quad u+e:=u(e),\quad u+p:=u(e_{1})\cdots u(e_{\ell})

denote their translations by
u
.

(2)

For any
x\in V_{\Gamma}
and
e\in E_{\Gamma}
,
let
\overline{x}\in V_{\Gamma/L}
and
\overline{e}\in E_{\Gamma/L}
denote their images in

V_{\Gamma/L}=V_{\Gamma}/L
and
E_{\Gamma/L}=E_{\Gamma}/L
.
For a walk
p=e_{1}\cdots e_{\ell}
in
\Gamma
, we define its image in
\Gamma/L
by
\overline{p}:=\overline{e_{1}}\cdots\overline{e_{\ell}}

3.3.
Growth sequences of periodic graphs

Let
\Gamma
be a locally finite graph, and let
x_{0}\in V_{\Gamma}
.
For
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define subsets
B_{\Gamma,x_{0},i},S_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}\subset V_{\Gamma}
by

B_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}:=\{y\in V_{\Gamma}\mid d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)\leq i\},\quad S_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}:=\{y\in V_{\Gamma}\mid d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)=i\}.

Let
b_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}:=\#B_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}
and
s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}:=\#S_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}
denote their cardinarities.
The sequences
(s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i}
and
(b_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i}
are called the
growth sequence
and the
cumulative growth sequence
of
\Gamma
with the start point
x_{0}
, respectively.

The
growth series

G_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t)
of
\Gamma
with the start point
x_{0}
is the generating function

G_{\Gamma,x_{0}}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}t^{i}

of the growth series
(s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i})_{i}
.

The growth sequences of periodic graphs are known to be of quasi-polynomial type.

Theorem 3.8

(
[
21
]
*Theorem 2.2)
.

Let
(\Gamma,L)
be a periodic graph, and let
x_{0}\in V_{\Gamma}
.
Then, the functions
b:i\mapsto b_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}
and
s:i\mapsto s_{\Gamma,x_{0},i}
are of quasi-polynomial type (see Definition
3.9
below).
In particular, its growth series is rational.

Definition 3.9

(cf.
[
24
]
*Chapter 0)
.

(1)

A function
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a

if there exist a positive integer
N
and polynomials
Q_{0},\ldots,Q_{N-1}\in\mathbb{C}[x]
such that

f(n)=\begin{cases}Q_{0}(n)&\text{if $n\equiv 0\pmod{N}$, }\\
Q_{1}(n)&\text{if $n\equiv 1\pmod{N}$, }\\
\hskip 14.22636pt\vdots&\\
Q_{N-1}(n)&\text{if $n\equiv N-1\pmod{N}$. }\end{cases}

(2)

A function
g:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
is called to be of
quasi-polynomial type
if
there exists a non-negative integer
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and a quasi-polynomial

such that

g(n)=f(n)
holds for any
n>M
.
The positive integer

is called a

of

when

is of the form in (

).
Note that the notion of quasi-period is not unique.
The minimum quasi-period is called the

of

We say that the function

is a

if

g(n)=f(n)
holds for

.

\mathbb{R}

\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)
and

satisfy condition (S).

4.7
, we have

\beta_{\Delta,v}=\alpha^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}-\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)

by Lemma
4.12
below.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P)
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
,
v\in V(\Delta)
and
F\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,v}
.
Suppose that
\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)
,
r_{\sigma,v}^{F}
and
s_{\sigma,v}^{F}
are the invariants constructed in the proof of Lemma
4.7
.
Then, we have

\alpha^{\prime F}_{\Delta,v}-\alpha^{F}_{\Delta,v}\geq\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)
,
and hence,
\beta_{\Delta,v}=\alpha^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}-\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)
.

Proof.

Note that we set
r_{\sigma,v}^{F}=0
in the construction. Therefore, it is sufficient to show the inequality

\frac{a_{\Delta,v}^{F}(v)}{1-a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)}\cdot\frac{1-h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}\cdot s_{\sigma,v}^{F}\geq\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v).

When
v\in F
, we have
s_{\sigma,v}^{F}\geq m_{\sigma,v}(v)=\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)
by the construction.
Since
h_{\Delta,v}^{F}\leq a_{\Delta,v}^{F}(v)<1
, we obtain the desired inequality (i).

In what follows, we assume
v\not\in F
.
Take any
v_{F}\in F
.
For each
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,v}\setminus\{F\}
, we take any
v_{F^{\prime}}\in F^{\prime}\setminus F
.
Then by Lemma
4.4
, we have

v\in\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{v_{F^{\prime}}\mid F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,v}\}\bigr).

Take a minimal subset
I\subset\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,v}
satisfying
v\in\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{v_{F^{\prime}}\mid F^{\prime}\in I\}\bigr)
.
Then, by the minimality of
I
, the elements of
\{v_{F^{\prime}}\mid F^{\prime}\in I\}
are
\mathbb{R}
-linear independent.
Furthermore, by the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{\sigma,v}
, we conclude
F\in I
since
v_{F^{\prime}}\not\in F
holds for each
F^{\prime}\in I\setminus\{F\}
.

Since
v\in\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\{v_{F^{\prime}}\mid F^{\prime}\in I\}\bigr)
,
there exist
w\in\operatorname{conv}\bigl(\bigl\{v_{F^{\prime}}\ \big|\ F^{\prime}\in I\setminus\{F\}\bigr\}\bigr)
and
t\in[0,1]

such that

v=tv_{F}+(1-t)w.

We have
t\neq 1
by the assumption
v\not\in F
.
We also have
t\neq 0
by the minimality of
I
.
Therefore, we conclude that
t\in(0,1)
.
Furthermore, by the construction of
s_{\sigma,v}^{F}
in the proof of Lemma
4.7
, we have

s_{\sigma,v}^{F}\geq m_{\sigma,v}(v_{F})\geq t\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v).

Let
H:=H\bigl(\bigl\{a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v^{\prime})v^{\prime}\ \big|\ v^{\prime}\in V(\Delta)\bigr\}\bigr)
be the half-space defined in Definition
4.8
.
By the conditions (A1) and (A2) on
a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v^{\prime})
’s in Subsection
4.1.4
, we have

v\not\in H,\qquad w\in\operatorname{conv}\bigl((\operatorname{Im}(\nu)\cap\sigma)\setminus F\bigr)\subset H.

Therefore,
\partial H
intersects with the line through
v
and
w
.
Let
x
be the intersection point.
Then the points
v_{F}
,
v
,
x
, and
w
are on the line in this order.
Let
b\in\mathbb{R}
be the unique real number such that
bv_{F}\in\partial H
.
Then, we have
h^{F}_{\Delta,v}\leq b<1
since
v_{F}\not\in H
and
h^{F}_{\Delta,v}v_{F}\in H
.
Therefore, we have

(iii)

\displaystyle\frac{a_{\Delta,v}^{F}(v)}{1-a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)}\cdot\frac{1-h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left\lvert a_{\Delta,v}^{F}(v)v-0\right\rvert}{\left\lvert v-a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)v\right\rvert}\cdot\frac{\left\lvert v_{F}-bv_{F}\right\rvert}{\lvert bv_{F}-0\rvert}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lvert v_{F}-x\rvert}{\lvert x-v\rvert}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lvert v_{F}-w\rvert}{\lvert w-v\rvert}=\frac{1}{t}.

Here, we used Menelaus’s theorem for the first equality.
From (ii) and (iii), we get the desired inequality (i).
∎

Definition 4.14
.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P)
and
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
.

x\in L_{\mathbb{R}}
and

, we define
\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
by the unique expression

.

\alpha_{\Delta,v}
and

4.1.5
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P)
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
and
v\in V(\Delta)
.
Let
y\in V_{\Gamma}\cap\Phi^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\Delta)
,
and let
p
be a shortest walk in
\Gamma
from
x_{0}
to
y
.
By applying Lemma
3.7
(1) to
\overline{p}
,
we take a path
q_{0}
in
\Gamma/L
and a function
a:\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

such that
\langle\overline{p}\rangle=\langle q_{0}\rangle+\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}}a(q)\langle q\rangle
.
Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

We fix any
F\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,v}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
If
\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))>f^{F}(t)
holds for

f^{F}(t):=\frac{a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)}{1-a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)}\left(\frac{C_{1}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}+C^{\prime}_{2}+\frac{1-h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}\bigl(t+W(\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)-1)\bigr)\right),

then we have

\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q)>t.

(2)

In particular, if
\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))>\alpha_{\Delta,v}
(resp.
\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))>\alpha^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}
) holds, then we have

\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q)>r^{F}_{\sigma,v}\qquad(\text{resp.\ $\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q)>s^{F}_{\sigma,v}$})

for each
F\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,v}
.

Proof.

We prove (1).
Let
F\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,v}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
We assume

\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q)\leq t.

First, we have

•

\mu(\langle q\rangle)\in w(q)\cdot H\bigl(\bigl\{a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v^{\prime})v^{\prime}\mid v^{\prime}\in V(\Delta)\bigr\}\bigr)

for
q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}\setminus\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)
,

•

\mu(\langle q\rangle)\in w(q)\cdot P
for

q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}
, and

•

\mu(\langle q_{0}\rangle)\in(C_{1}+w(q_{0}))P
.

For simplicity, we put

\displaystyle\ell_{0}

\displaystyle:=w(q_{0}),

\displaystyle\ell_{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q),

\displaystyle\ell_{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}\setminus\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q),

\displaystyle H^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=H\bigl(\bigl\{a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v^{\prime})v^{\prime}\ \big|\ v^{\prime}\in V(\Delta)\bigr\}\bigr).

Then, we have

\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot\mu(\langle q\rangle)\in\ell_{1}P,\qquad\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}\setminus\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot\mu(\langle q\rangle)\in\ell_{2}H^{\prime}.

Furthermore, we have

•

\ell_{0}+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}=w(p)=d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)
,

•

\ell_{0}\leq W\cdot\operatorname{length}(q_{0})\leq W(\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)-1)
, and

•

\ell_{1}=\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q)\leq t
.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(y)

\displaystyle=\mu(\langle\overline{p}\rangle)

\displaystyle=\mu(\langle q_{0}\rangle)+\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot\mu(\langle q\rangle)+\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}\setminus\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot\mu(\langle q\rangle)

\displaystyle\in(C_{1}+\ell_{0})P+\ell_{1}P+\ell_{2}H^{\prime}.

Since we have
P\subset(h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}H^{\prime}
by the choice of
h^{F}_{\Delta,v}
, we have

\Phi(y)\in\bigl((h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}(C_{1}+\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})+\ell_{2}\bigr)H^{\prime}.

Since we have

\displaystyle(h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}(C_{1}+\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})+\ell_{2}

\displaystyle=(h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}C_{1}+\bigl((h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}-1\bigr)(\ell_{0}+\ell_{1})+d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)

\displaystyle\leq(h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}C_{1}+\bigl((h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}-1\bigr)\bigl(t+W(\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)-1)\bigr)+d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y),

we have

\Phi(y)\in\Bigl((h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}C_{1}+\bigl((h^{F}_{\Delta,v})^{-1}-1\bigr)\bigl(t+W(\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)-1)\bigr)+d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)\Bigr)H^{\prime}.

Therefore, we have

\sum_{v^{\prime}\in V(\Delta)}\frac{\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v^{\prime}}(\Phi(y))}{a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v^{\prime})}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}+\frac{1-h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}\bigl(t+W(\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)-1)\bigr)+d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y).

On the other hand, by the definition of
C_{2}
, we have

d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)\leq C^{\prime}_{2}+d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y)=C^{\prime}_{2}+\sum_{v^{\prime}\in V(\Delta)}\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v^{\prime}}(\Phi(y)).

Since
a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v^{\prime})\leq 1
holds for any
v^{\prime}\in V(\Delta)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1-a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)}{a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)}\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{v^{\prime}\in V(\Delta)}\frac{\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v^{\prime}}(\Phi(y))}{a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v^{\prime})}\right)-\left(\sum_{v^{\prime}\in V(\Delta)}\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v^{\prime}}(\Phi(y))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}+C^{\prime}_{2}+\frac{1-h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}\bigl(t+W(\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)-1)\bigr).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)}{1-a^{F}_{\Delta,v}(v)}\left(\frac{C_{1}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}+C^{\prime}_{2}+\frac{1-h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}{h^{F}_{\Delta,v}}\bigl(t+W(\#(V_{\Gamma}/L)-1)\bigr)\right)

\displaystyle=f^{F}(t),

which completes the proof of (1).

(2) follows from (1) and the definitions of
\alpha_{\Delta,v}
and
\alpha^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}
.
∎

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P)
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
and

.

Suppose that

satisfies
\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))>\alpha_{\Delta,v}
.
Let
p

\Gamma
from
x_{0}
to

3.7
(1) to
\overline{p}
,

q_{0}
in
\Gamma/L
and a function
a:\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

such that
\langle\overline{p}\rangle=\langle q_{0}\rangle+\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}}a(q)\langle q\rangle
.
Then, by Lemma
4.15
, we have

\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q)>r^{F}_{\sigma,v}

\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(F)}a(q)\cdot w(q)>s^{F}_{\sigma,v}

for each
F\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,v}
.
Then, by the condition (S) in Subsection
4.1.3
,
there exists a function
b:\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(\sigma)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that

•

b(q)\leq a(q)
holds for any

,

•

\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(\sigma)}b(q)\cdot\mu(\langle q\rangle)=\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)v
, and

•

for any

,
there exists
q^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(\sigma)

such that
b(q^{\prime})<a(q^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{supp}(q)\subset\operatorname{supp}(q^{\prime})
.

Since any
q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(\sigma)
satisfies
\frac{\mu(\langle q\rangle)}{w(q)}\in\sigma
,
we also have

\sum_{q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(\sigma)}b(q)\cdot w(q)=\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v).

We define a function

by

a^{\prime}(q):=\begin{cases}a(q)-b(q)&\text{(if $q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(\sigma)$)}\\
a(q)&\text{(if $q\in\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}\setminus\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Gamma/L}(\sigma)$)}.\end{cases}

above,

Hence, there exists a walk

\displaystyle w(p^{\prime})

Theorem 4.20
.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P)
,
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
,

\Delta^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta)
and
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Face}(\Delta^{\prime})
.
Then, the following assertions hold.

(1)

B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
is a free
M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
-module with basis

S\left(\overline{L}_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
.

(2)

For any
(d,y)\in S\left(\overline{L}_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
,
we have
d\leq C^{\prime}_{2}+\beta_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}}+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)
.

Proof.

We prove (1). First, we prove that
B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
is an
M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
-module.
By definition, it is easy to see that
B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)+(1,0)\subset B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)

We fix
(d,y)\in B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
and
v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})
.
By the definition of
L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
, we have
\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))>\beta_{\Delta,v}
.
Therefore, By Theorem
4.18
, we have

d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y+\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)v)=d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)+\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)\leq d+\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v).

Furthermore, we have
\Phi(y+\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)v)\in L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
.
Hence, we conclude that

(d,y)+\bigl(\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v),\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)v\bigr)\in B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right).

We fix
(d,y)\in B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
.
By the definitions of
L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
and
\overline{L}_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
,
there exist
y^{\prime}\in V_{\Gamma}\cap\Phi^{-1}\left(\overline{L}_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
and

b_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for
v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})
such that

y=y^{\prime}+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}b_{v}\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)\cdot v.

By Theorem
4.18
, we have

d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)=d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y^{\prime})+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}b_{v}\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v).

Therefore, for
d^{\prime}:=d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y^{\prime})
, we have
(d^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in S\left(\overline{L}_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
and

\displaystyle(d,y)

\displaystyle=\left(d^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right)+\left(d-d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)\right)\cdot(1,0)+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}b_{v}\cdot\bigl(\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v),\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)v\bigr)

\displaystyle\in\left(d^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right)+M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime\prime}}.

We have proved that
B\left(L_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
is an
M_{\sigma,\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
-module generated by

S\left(\overline{L}_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
.

Suppose that
(d,y),(d^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in S\left(\overline{L}_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\right)
satisfies

\displaystyle(d,y)+a\cdot(1,0)+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}b_{v}\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)

\displaystyle=(d^{\prime},y^{\prime})+a^{\prime}\cdot(1,0)+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})}b^{\prime}_{v}\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)

with some
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
b_{v},b^{\prime}_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for
v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})
.
Then, for each
v\in V(\Delta^{\prime\prime})
, we have

\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))+b_{v}\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)=\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y^{\prime}))+b^{\prime}_{v}\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v).

Here, by the definition of
\overline{L}_{\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime}}
,
we have

\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y)),\ \operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y^{\prime}))\in\left(\beta_{\Delta,v},\beta^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}\right].

Since
\beta^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}-\beta_{\Delta,v}=\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)

y=y^{\prime}
and

Corollary 4.23
.

Let
(\Gamma,L)
be a strongly connected
n
-dimensional periodic graph, and let
x_{0}\in V_{\Gamma}
.
Let
(b(d))_{d}
and
(s(d))_{d}

\Gamma
with the start point

(see Subsection
3.3
).

\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be as in Subsection
4.1
.
Then, the following assertions hold.

(1)

The generating function of
b:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0};d\mapsto b(d)
is given by

\sum_{i\geq 0}b(i)t^{i}=\frac{Q(t)}{(1-t)(1-t^{\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}})^{n}},

where
Q(t)
is a polynomial with
\deg Q\leq\beta+n\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}

The generating function of

\sum_{i\geq 0}s(i)t^{i}=\frac{Q(t)}{(1-t^{\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}})^{n}},

where
Q(t)
is a polynomial with
\deg Q\leq\beta+n\cdot\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}
.
In particular,
the function
s:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0};d\mapsto s(d)

d>\beta
, and

\operatorname{cpx}_{\Gamma}

(3) divides

.
Therefore,
(1) follows from Theorem
4.22
(3) since

(2) follows from (1).

Theorem 4.26
.

For
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P)
and
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
,
we define

L_{\Delta}^{\leq\beta^{\prime}}:=\left\{z\in L_{\Delta}\ \middle|\ \text{$\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(z)\leq\beta^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}$ holds for any $v\in V(\Delta)$}\right\}.

We also define

L^{\leq\beta^{\prime}}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P),\\
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}\end{subarray}}L_{\Delta}^{\leq\beta^{\prime}}.

Then, we have

C_{2}(\Gamma,\Phi,x_{0})=\max\left\{d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)-d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y)\ \middle|\ y\in V_{\Gamma}\cap\Phi^{-1}\left(L^{\leq\beta^{\prime}}\right)\right\}.

In particular, we have

C_{2}(\Gamma,\Phi,x_{0})=\max\left\{d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)-d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y)\ \middle|\ y\in B_{\lfloor\beta^{\prime}\rfloor}\right\},

where we define

\beta^{\prime}:=C^{\prime}_{2}+\max\Biggl\{\sum_{v\in V(\Delta)}\beta^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}\ \Bigg|\ \sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P),\ \Delta\in T_{\sigma}\Biggr\}.

Proof.

Let
y\in V_{\Gamma}
.
Take
\sigma\in\operatorname{Facet}(P)
and
\Delta\in T_{\sigma}
such that
\Phi(y)\in L_{\Delta}
.
For each
v\in V(\Delta)
, we define

b_{v}:=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta,v}(\Phi(y))-\beta^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}}{\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)}\right\rceil\right\}.

We define

y^{\prime}:=y-\sum_{v\in V(\Delta)}b_{v}\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v)v.

Then, we have
\Phi(y^{\prime})\in L_{\Delta}^{\leq\beta^{\prime}}
. By Theorem
4.18
, we have

d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y^{\prime})=d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)-\sum_{v\in V(\Delta)}b_{v}\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v).

On the other hand, we have

d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y^{\prime})=d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y)-\sum_{v\in V(\Delta)}b_{v}\operatorname{cpx}_{\sigma}(v).

Therefore, we have

d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y^{\prime})-d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y^{\prime})=d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)-d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle C_{2}(\Gamma,\Phi,x_{0})

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)-d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y)\ \big|\ y\in V_{\Gamma}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)-d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y)\ \middle|\ y\in V_{\Gamma}\cap\Phi^{-1}\left(L^{\leq\beta^{\prime}}\right)\right\},

which proves the first assertion.

For
y\in V_{\Gamma}
with
\Phi(y)\in L_{\Delta}^{\leq\beta^{\prime}}
, we have

d_{\Gamma}(x_{0},y)\leq C^{\prime}_{2}+d_{P,\Phi}(x_{0},y)\leq C^{\prime}_{2}+\sum_{v\in V(\Delta)}\beta^{\prime}_{\Delta,v}.

Therefore, we have
V_{\Gamma}\cap\Phi^{-1}\left(L^{\leq\beta^{\prime}}\right)\subset B_{\lfloor\beta^{\prime}\rfloor}
,
which proves the second assertion.
∎

,
(1,\mathbf{0})
, …,

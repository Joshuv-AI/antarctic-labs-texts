Counting the Numbers of Paths of all Lengths in Dendrimers and its Applications
Hafsah Tabassum, Syed Ahtsham Ul Haq Bokhary, Thiradet Jiarasuksakun, and Pawaton Kaemawichanurat

Abstract
For positive integers n n and k k , the dendrimer T n , k T_{n,k} is defined as the rooted tree of radius n n whose all vertices at distance less than n n from the root have degree k k . The dendrimers are higly branched organic macromolecules having repeated iterations of branched units that surroundes the central core. Dendrimers are used in a variety of fields including chemistry, nanotechnology, biology. In this paper, for any positive integer ℓ \ell , we count the number of paths of length ℓ \ell of T n , k T_{n,k} . As a consequence of our main results, we obtain the average distance of T n , k T_{n,k} which we can establish an alternate proof for the Wiener index of T n , k T_{n,k} . Further, we generalize the concept of medium domination, introduced by Vargör and Dündar in 2011, of T n , k T_{n,k} .

\displaystyle|\mathcal{S}_{x,i}|=(k-2)(k-1)^{i-1}

which implies that

\displaystyle|S_{x}|

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{S}_{x,\ell-n+1}|+\cdots+|\mathcal{S}_{x,\lfloor\frac{\ell-1}{2}\rfloor}|

\displaystyle=(k-2)(k-1)^{\ell-n}+(k-2)(k-1)^{\ell-n+1}+\cdots+(k-2)(k-1)^{\lfloor\frac{\ell-3}{2}\rfloor}

\displaystyle=(k-2)\Big(\frac{(k-1)^{\lfloor\frac{\ell-1}{2}\rfloor}-1}{k-2}-\frac{(k-1)^{\ell-n}-1}{k-2}\Big)

\displaystyle=(k-1)^{\lfloor\frac{\ell-1}{2}\rfloor}-(k-1)^{\ell-n}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{P}_{x}|=|\mathcal{R}_{x}|+|\mathcal{S}_{x}|=(k-1)^{\lfloor\frac{\ell-1}{2}\rfloor}

and this proves Case 2.

In both cases, we have that
|\mathcal{P}_{x}|=(k-1)^{\lfloor\frac{\ell-1}{2}\rfloor}
. As
x
is an arbitrary leaf of
T_{n,k}
and
T_{n,k}
has
k(k-1)^{n-1}
leaves, it follows that

n^{1}_{\ell}(T_{n,k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}k(k-1)^{n+\frac{\ell}{2}-2}&\text{when }\ell\textnormal{ is even},\\[15.0pt]
k(k-1)^{n+\frac{\ell-1}{2}-1}&\text{when }\ell\textnormal{ is odd}\end{array}\right.

and this proves Lemma
1
.

Lemma 2

Let
n^{2}_{\ell}(T_{n,k})
be the number of paths of length
\ell
that starts and end on a leaf vertex of the graph
T_{n,k}
. Then, for
n,k\geq 1
,

n^{2}_{\ell}(T_{n,k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}k(k-1)^{n+\frac{\ell}{2}-3}\binom{k-1}{2}&\text{when }2\leq\ell\leq 2n-2\\[15.0pt]
(k-1)^{\ell-2}\binom{k}{2}&\text{when }\ell=2n.\end{array}\right.

Proof.
First, we let

\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}:
the family of paths of length
\ell
of
T_{n,k}
whose both end vertices are leaves of
T_{n,k}
.

Clearly,
\ell
must be even. For a path
P\in\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}
, we let
x_{P}
be the center of
P
which the distance from
x_{p}
to the end vertices of
P
are both equal to
\frac{\ell}{2}
. We distinguish
2
cases according to the value of
\ell
.

Case 1:

2\leq\ell\leq 2n-2
.

It can be observed that every path in
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}
has the center in
L_{n-\frac{\ell}{2}}
. Let
x
be a vertex in
L_{n-\frac{\ell}{2}}
. There are
k-1
neighbors of
x
in
L_{n-\frac{\ell}{2}+1}
. Each pair of these
k-1
neighbors can be passed by a path in
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}
. Hence, there are
\binom{k-1}{2}
possibilities for the paths in
\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}
. We may let
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
be a pair among these
\binom{k-1}{2}
possibilities. There are
(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-1}
paths from each of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
to the leaves of
T_{n,k}
. Hence, there are

\binom{k-1}{2}(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-1}(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-1}=(k-1)^{\ell-2}\binom{k-1}{2}

paths whose center is
x
and both end vertices are leaves. Since
x
is arbitraty and there are
k(k-1)^{n-\frac{\ell}{2}-1}
vertices in
L_{n-\frac{\ell}{2}}
, it follows that

\displaystyle n^{2}_{\ell}(T_{n,k})=|\mathcal{Q}_{\ell}|=k(k-1)^{n-\frac{\ell}{2}-3}\binom{k-1}{2}.

Case 2:

\ell=2n

In this case, the root
r
is the center of all paths in
\mathcal{Q}_{2n}
. There are
\binom{k}{2}
possibilities for the paths in
\mathcal{Q}_{2n}
to pass these vertices. Similarly, we let
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
be a pair among these
\binom{k}{2}
possibilities. There are
(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-1}
paths from each of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
to the leaves of
T_{n,k}
. Hence,

n^{2}_{\ell}(T_{n,k})=|\mathcal{Q}_{2n}|=(k-1)^{\ell-2}\binom{k}{2}

and this proves Lemma
2
.

Proof of Theorem
1
Recall that the graph
T_{n,k}
can be constructed from
T_{n-1,k}
by introducing
k-1
vertices to each leaf, and joining these
k-1
vertices to the leaf.
We have considered two cases.

Case 1:

\ell
is an even number.

Every path of length
\ell
in this case is either (
i
) lies completely in
T_{n-1,k}
, (
ii
) can be formed from a path of length
\ell-1
whose exactly one end vertex is a leaf of
T_{n-1,k}
or (
iii
) can be formed from a path of length
\ell-2
whose both end vertices are at the leaves of
T_{n-1,k}
. The Case (
i
) gives
n_{\ell}(T_{n-1,k})
paths of length
\ell
while the Case (
ii
) gives
(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell}(T_{n-1,k})
paths of length
\ell
as the end vertex at a leaf of
T_{n-1,k}
can be extended with
k-1
ways. Finally, the Case (
iii
) gives
(k-1)^{2}n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{n-1,k})
paths as every path of length
\ell-2
whose both end vertices are at the leaves of
T_{n-1,k}
can be extended to the path of length
\ell
by
(k-1)^{2}
ways,
k-1
for each end vertex.
Thus, we have the following recursive formula

n_{\ell}(T_{n,k})=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-1,k})+(k-1)^{2}n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{n-1,k})+n_{\ell}(T_{n-1,k}).

This proves Case 1.

Case 2:

\ell
is an odd number

Similarly, every path of length
\ell
in this case is either (
i
) lies completely in
T_{n-1,k}
, (
ii
) can be formed from a path of length
\ell-1
whose exactly one end vertex is a leaf of
T_{n-1,k}
or (
iii
) can be formed from a path of length
\ell-1
whose both end vertices are at the leaves of
T_{n-1,k}
. The Case (
i
) gives

ii
) gives

in

k-1

n_{\ell}(T_{n,k})=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-1,k})+2(k-1)n^{2}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-1,k})+n_{\ell}(T_{n-1,k}).

This proves Case 2 and completes the proof of our theorem.

4.2
Proof of Corollary
1

We distinguish two cases according to the parity of
\ell
.

Case 1:

\ell
is an even number.

By Theorem
1
, we have that

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(T_{n,k})

\displaystyle=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-1,k})+(k-1)^{2}n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{n-1,k})+n_{\ell}(T_{n-1,k})

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(T_{n-1,k})

\displaystyle=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-2,k})+(k-1)^{2}n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{n-2,k})+n_{\ell}(T_{n-2,k})

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(T_{n-2,k})

\displaystyle=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-3,k})+(k-1)^{2}n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{n-3,k})+n_{\ell}(T_{n-3,k})

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(T_{\frac{\ell}{2}+1,k})

\displaystyle=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{\frac{\ell}{2},k})+(k-1)^{2}n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{\frac{\ell}{2},k})+n_{\ell}(T_{\frac{\ell}{2},k})

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(T_{\frac{\ell}{2},k})

\displaystyle=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{\frac{\ell-2}{2},k})+(k-1)^{2}n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{\frac{\ell-2}{2},k})+n_{\ell}(T_{\frac{\ell-2}{2},k}).

As
\frac{\ell-2}{2}<\frac{\ell}{2}
, we have
n_{\ell}(T_{\frac{\ell-2}{2},k})=0
. Further,
n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{\frac{\ell-2}{2},k})=0
because
\frac{\ell-2}{2}<\frac{\ell-1}{2}
. Thus, summing the above equations we have

n_{\ell}(T_{n,k})=(k-1)\sum^{n-1}_{i=\frac{\ell}{2}}{n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{i,k})}+(k-1)^{2}\sum^{n-1}_{j=\frac{\ell-2}{2}}{n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{j,k})}.

(3)

By Lemma
1
when
\ell-1
is odd, we have that

\displaystyle\sum^{n-1}_{i=\frac{\ell}{2}}{n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{i,k})}

\displaystyle=\sum^{n-1}_{i=\frac{\ell}{2}}{k(k-1)^{i+\frac{\ell-2}{2}-1}}

\displaystyle=k(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-2}[\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}{(k-1)^{i}}-\sum^{\frac{\ell}{2}-1}_{i=0}{(k-1)^{i}}].

By Geometric Series, we have that

\sum^{n-1}_{i=\frac{\ell}{2}}{n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{i,k})}=k(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-2}[\frac{(k-1)^{n}-(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}{k-2}].

(4)

For the sum
\sum^{n-1}_{j=\frac{\ell-2}{2}}{n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{j,k})}
, we may split the first term as

\sum^{n-1}_{j=\frac{\ell-2}{2}}{n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{j,k})}=n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{\frac{\ell-2}{2},k})+\sum^{n-1}_{j=\frac{\ell}{2}}{n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{j,k})}.

By Lemma
2
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum^{n-1}_{j=\frac{\ell-2}{2}}{n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{j,k})}

\displaystyle=(k-1)^{\ell}{k\choose 2}+\sum^{n-1}_{j=\frac{\ell}{2}}{k(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-4+j}}{k-1\choose 2}

\displaystyle=(k-1)^{\ell}{k\choose 2}+k(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-4}{k-1\choose 2}\sum^{n-1}_{j=\frac{\ell}{2}}(k-1)^{j}.

By Geometric Series, we have that

\sum^{n-1}_{j=\frac{\ell-2}{2}}{n^{2}_{\ell-2}(T_{j,k})}=(k-1)^{\ell-4}{k\choose 2}+k(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}-4}{k-1\choose 2}[\frac{(k-1)^{n}-(k-1)^{\frac{\ell}{2}}}{k-2}].

(5)

Putting values from Equation (
4
) and (
5
) into Equation (
3
) and simplifying, we get

n_{\ell}(T_{n,k})=\frac{k(k-1)^{\ell-1}}{2}[\frac{k(k-1)^{n-\frac{\ell}{2}}-2}{k-2}].

(6)

This proves Case 1.

Case 2:

\ell
is an odd number

By Theorem
1
, we have that

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(T_{n,k})

\displaystyle=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-1,k})+2(k-1)n^{2}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-1,k})+n_{\ell}(T_{n-1,k})

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(T_{n-1,k})

\displaystyle=(k-1)n^{1}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-2,k})+2(k-1)n^{2}_{\ell-1}(T_{n-2,k})+n_{\ell}(T_{n-2,k})

\displaystyle n_{\ell}(T_{n-2,k})

4.3
Proof of Corollary
2

We let
\binom{V(T_{n,k})}{2}
be the set of all sets of two vertices of
T_{n,k}
. Namely,

\displaystyle\binom{V(T_{n,k})}{2}=\{\{u,v\}:u,v\in V(T_{n,k})\}

and

\displaystyle\Big|\binom{V(T_{n,k})}{2}\Big|=\binom{|V(T_{n,k})|}{2}=\binom{1+\frac{k[(k-1)^{n}-1]}{k-2}}{2}.

Construct the
(0,1)
-matrix whose rows are the pairs
\{u,v\}
of
\binom{V(T_{n,k})}{2}
, columns are the path length
\ell
for all
1\leq\ell\leq 2n
and the entries
a_{\{u,v\},\ell}
are defined as follows:

a_{\{u,v\},\ell}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1&\text{if }d_{T}(u,v)=\ell,\\[15.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We first consider Row
\{u,v\}
. There is exactly one column,
\ell
say, such that

a_{\{u,v\},\ell}=1

but

a_{\{u,v\},j}=0

for all
j\in\{1,...,2n\}\setminus\{\ell\}
. Thus, the summation of all entries in this matrix is

\displaystyle\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(T)}1=\Big|\binom{V(T_{n,k})}{2}\Big|=\binom{1+\frac{k[(k-1)^{n}-1]}{k-2}}{2}.

We then consider Column
\ell
. By the definition of
n_{\ell}(T_{n,k})
, there are
n_{\ell}(T_{n,k})
rows whose entries are equal to
1
while the entries of the other rows are all
0
. Hence, the summation of all entries of Column
\ell
is equal to
n_{\ell}(T)
implying that the summation of all etries in this matrix is
\sum^{2n}_{\ell=1}n_{\ell}(T)
.

By the counting two way principle, we have that

\binom{1+\frac{k[(k-1)^{n}-1]}{k-2}}{2}=\sum^{2n}_{\ell=1}n_{\ell}(T_{n,k}).

This proves Corollary
2
.

4.4
Proof of Theorem
2

We prove this theorem by similar argument as in the proof of Corollary
2
. First, we let
T
be a tree with the diameter
diam(T)=t
. We let
\binom{V(T)}{2}
be the set of all sets of two vertices of
T
. Construct the matrix whose rows are the pairs
\{u,v\}
of
\binom{V(T)}{2}
, columns are the path length
\ell
for all
1\leq\ell\leq t
and the entries
a_{\{u,v\},\ell}
are defined as follows:

a_{\{u,v\},\ell}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\ell&\text{if }d_{T}(u,v)=\ell,\\[15.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Thus, in the Column
j
, all the entries are either
j
or
0
.

We first consider Row
\{u,v\}
. There is exactly one column,
\ell
say, such that

a_{\{u,v\},\ell}=\ell=d_{T}(u,v)

but

a_{\{u,v\},j}=0

\ell=d_{T}(u,v)

\ell

n_{\ell}(T)

\ell

\ell n_{\ell}(T)

Weighted composition operators between weighted Hardy spaces on rooted trees Thanks: File: 2112.08323.tex, printed: 8-8-2026, 11.30
P. Muthukumar, Ajay K. Sharma, Vivek Kumar

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a discrete analogue of weighted Hardy spaces on rooted trees and study weighted composition operators between them in detail. In particular, we characterize bounded and compact weighted composition operators between discrete Hardy spaces. We also consider isometric weighted composition operators between these spaces.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 47B38, 47B33, 37E25, 05C05; Secondary: 30H10, 46B50

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a linear space consisting of complex-valued maps on a non-empty set
\Omega
.
For a given self map
\varphi
of
\Omega
and a complex-valued map
\psi
on
\Omega
, the corresponding weighted
composition operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is defined by

W_{\psi,\varphi}(f)=\psi(f\circ\varphi)\mbox{~for~}f\in X.

If
\psi\equiv 1
, then
W_{\psi,\varphi}
reduced to a composition operator
C_{\varphi}\,(
defined by
C_{\varphi}(f)=f\circ\varphi)
and if
\varphi(x)=x
for all
x\in\Omega
, then
W_{\psi,\varphi}

reduced to a multiplication operator
M_{\psi}\,(
defined by
M_{\psi}(f)=\psi f)
.

The study of weighted composition operators were begun by Banach in early nineteenth century. The classical Banach-Stone theorem says that the surjective isometries between the spaces of continuous functions on some intervals, are certain weighted composition operators (see
[
5
]
).
When
p\neq 2
, the isometric isomorphisms of the Hardy spaces
H^{p}
and Bergman spaces
A^{p}

are also weighted composition operators
[
9
,
10
]
.

Further, weighted composition operators appeared in other branches of mathematics
such as dynamical systems and evolution equations. For example, refer
[
6
]
to see the connection between weighted composition operators and classification of dichotomies in certain dynamical systems. Weighted composition transformations are also considered on some
compact convex family of analytic functions on the unit disk of the complex plane in order to solve some extremal problems in geometric function theory. For more details, refer
[
4
,
13
]
.

In recent years, studying operators on various function spaces defined on trees
becomes an interesting topic of research. See

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
16
]
and references therein.
In
[
11
]
, the authors introduced the discrete Hardy spaces on trees and considered
multiplication operators on them. Composition operators on discrete Hardy spaces were
studied in
[
12
,
14
]
. Recently, multiplication operators between these function spaces were investigated in
[
15
]
. As an extension of all these works, in this paper, we introduce a discrete analogue of weighted Hardy spaces
on trees and study weighted composition operators between them.

The paper is organized as follows. We refer to the Sections
2
and
3
for basic definitions and preliminaries about the discrete weighted Hardy spaces
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
such as inclusion properties, growth estimate, etc. In Sections
4
and
5
, we characterize bounded and compact weighted composition operators between
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
spaces, respectively.
We discuss about isometric weighted composition operators between
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
spaces in Section
6
. Finally, in Section
7
, we compare the boundedness and compactness of the operators
M_{\psi},C_{\varphi}
with the boundedness and compactness of the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{N},\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\mathbb{C}
denote the set of all positive
integers, the set of all non-negative integers and the set of all complex numbers,
respectively. An infinite tree (graph)
T
with a special vertex (known as a root),
is called a
rooted tree
. Using the edge counting distance,
T
can be regarded
as a metric space. For the basic graph theory, we refer the readers to
[
8
]
.
By abuse of language, we denote
x\in T
when
x
is in the
vertex set of
T
and we call a function defined on the vertex set of
T
by a
function defined on
T
. For each vertex
x\in T
,
|x|
denotes the distance between
the root
o
and the vertex
x
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, let
D_{n}
and
c_{n}
denote the
set of all vertices
x\in T
with
|x|=n
and the number of vertices
x\in T
with

|x|=n
, respectively.

A positive-valued function
T
is called a
weight function
on
T
. Fix a weight
function
\sigma
on
T
and fix
p>0
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and a complex-valued function
f
on

T
, we introduce

M_{\sigma,p}(n,f)=\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum\limits_{|x|=n}\sigma^{p}(x)|f(x)|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Now, we define discrete weighted Hardy spaces
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
as follows.
For
p>0
,

\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}=\left\{f:T\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}:\|f\|_{\sigma,p}:=\sup\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}M_{\sigma,p}(n,f)<\infty\right\},

and

\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,\infty}=\left\{f:T\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}:\|f\|_{\sigma,\infty}:=\sup\limits_{x\in T}\sigma(x)|f(x)|<\infty\right\}.

In particular, if
\sigma\equiv 1
, then the space
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
becomes
the discrete Hardy space
\mathbb{T}_{p}
with
\|\cdot\|_{p}=\|\cdot\|_{1,p}
(see
[
11
]
). For a complex-valued
function
f
on
T
, it is evident that
\|f\|_{\sigma,p}=\|\sigma f\|_{p}
. Thus,

f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
is equivalent to
\sigma f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
.

Throughout the paper,
T
denotes an infinite rooted tree,
\sigma
denotes a weight function on
T
.

3.
Basic Properties

We begin with the inclusion properties of
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
spaces.

Proposition 1
.

(1)

If
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}c_{n}=\infty
, then
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,q}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
for
0<p<q
.

(2)

If
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}c_{n}<\infty
, then
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}=\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,\infty}
for all
p>0
.

Proof of this proposition is similar to the case of
\mathbb{T}_{p}
spaces.
See
[
15
, Section 3]
for a proof of later case. Also, see Corollary
6

below for different weight functions.
In view of Proposition
1
, throughout this article,
we assume that
\{c_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}
is unbounded to avoid the triviality.

Theorem 1
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty,\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
is a Banach space.

As the proof is straight forward, we omit the details here. Refer

[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
for case of
\mathbb{T}_{p}
spaces.

Now, fix
x\in T
. For any
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
, we obtain

\sigma^{p}(x)|f(x)|^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{|y|=n}\sigma^{p}(y)|f(y)|^{p}\leq c_{n}\|f\|^{p}_{\sigma,p},

where
n=|x|
. Thus, we get the following growth estimate.

Proposition 2
.

For every
x\in T
, we have

|f(x)|\leq\frac{\left(c_{|x|}\right)^{1/p}}{\sigma(x)}\|f\|_{\sigma,p},\,\,f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}.

Sharpness of this estimate can be easily verified by using characteristic functions.

Corollary 1
.

For every
x\in T
, the evaluation map
ev_{x}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
defined
by
ev_{x}(f):=f(x)
is a bounded linear functional on
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
.

Corollary 2
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty,\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
is a functional Banach space.

Recall that Banach space
X
consisting of functions defined on a set
\Omega

is called
functional Banach space
if for each
x\in\Omega
, the point
evaluation map
ev_{x}:f\in X\mapsto f(x)
is a bounded linear functional on
X

and
f(x)=f(y)
for each
f\in X
implies
x=y.

Theorem 2
.

\mathbb{T}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
if and only if
\sigma
is a bounded map.

Proof.
Suppose
\sigma
is a bounded map and
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}.
From the definition of

\|\cdot\|_{p}
, it is trivial to see that

\|\sigma f\|_{p}\leq\|\sigma\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{p}.

This yields that
\sigma f\in\mathbb{T}_{p},
that is,
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}.

Therefore,
\mathbb{T}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}.

For the converse part, we suppose that
\sigma
is unbounded. For each
n\in\mathbb{N},

choose
v_{n}\in T
such that
\sigma(v_{n})>n.
Define
f:T\to\mathbb{C}
by

f(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\left(c_{|v|}\right)^{1/p}&\mbox{ if }v=v_{n}\mbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Thus,

M_{p}(k,f)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if }k=v_{n}\mbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore,
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
with
\|f\|_{p}=1.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\sigma,p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\sigma(v_{n})|f(v_{n})|}{\left(c_{|v_{n}|}\right)^{1/p}}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sigma(v_{n}),

which is not finite. Hence
f\notin\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3
.

\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{p}
if and only if
\sigma
is bounded away
from zero, that is, there is a constant
m>0
such that
m\leq\sigma(x)
for all

x\in T.

Proof.
Suppose that
\sigma
is bounded away from zero. Then,
1/\sigma
is a
bounded function.
Let
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
, i.e.,
\sigma f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}.
Consequently,
f=\left(1/\sigma\right)(\sigma f)\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
.
Hence
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{p}.

For the other way inclusion, suppose that
\sigma
is not away from zero. Choose

v_{n}\in T
such that
\sigma(v_{n})<1/n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Define
f
by

f(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}n\left(c_{|v_{n}|}\right)^{1/p}&\mbox{ if }v=v_{n}\mbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

As
M_{p}(|v_{n}|,f)=n\to\infty
, we have
f\notin\mathbb{T}_{p}.

But,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\sigma,p}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{\sigma,p}(|v_{n}|,f)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\sigma(v_{n})|f(v_{n})|}{\left(c_{|v_{n}|}\right)^{1/p}}\leq 1,

that is,
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
. The desired result follows.
∎

Corollary 3
.

\mathbb{T}_{p}=\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
if and only if there exist
m,M>0
such that

0<m\leq\sigma(x)\leq M\text{ for all }x\in T.

In this case,

m\|f\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{\sigma,p}\leq M\|f\|_{p}\text{ for all }f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}(=\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}).

Remark 1
.

The inclusion
\mathbb{T}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
is equivalent to saying that

\sigma f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
whenever
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
; that is, the multiplication
operator
M_{\sigma}
maps
\mathbb{T}_{p}
to
\mathbb{T}_{p}
. In turn, this is same as

\sigma
is bounded (see
[
15
, Theorem D]
). Similarly, the inclusion

\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{p}
is equivalent to the multiplication
operator
M_{1/\sigma}
maps
\mathbb{T}_{p}
to
\mathbb{T}_{p}
.

4.
Bounded weighted composition operators

Throughout this article,
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},
denote weight functions on
T
,

\varphi
denotes a self map of
T
and
\psi
denotes a complex-valued map on
T
.
In this section, we will discuss about the boundedness of the weighted composition operator

W_{\psi,\varphi}:f\mapsto\psi(f\circ\varphi)
between
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
spaces.
When we discuss an operator
A:X\to Y
, it is understood that the operator norm

\|A\|_{X\to Y}
will be simply denoted by
\|A\|
.

Theorem 4
.

For
0<p\leq\infty
, the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is bounded if and only if
\Psi\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}.

In this case,

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|=\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\sigma_{2},p},

where
\Psi=\psi/(\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi)
.

Proof.
Suppose that
\Psi\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}.
First,
we consider the case
p=\infty
.
For
x\in T
and
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty},

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(f\circ\varphi)(x)|=\sigma_{2}(x)\frac{|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))|f(\varphi(x))|\leq\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}.

Therefore,

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}\leq\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\text{ for all }f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}.

Now, we consider the case
p<\infty
.
For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,W_{\psi,\varphi}f)=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{|x|=n}\frac{\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}}{\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))}|f(\varphi(x))|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))\leq\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}^{p}\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}.

Thus,

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f\|_{\sigma_{2},p}\leq\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\sigma_{2},p}\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\text{ for all }f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}.

Hence,
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is a bounded operator with

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|\leq\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\sigma_{2},p}.

Conversely, suppose that
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is a bounded operator. Then,

\Psi=W_{\psi,\varphi}\left(1/\sigma_{1}\right)\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}\;\text{ as }\;1/\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}.

Since
\displaystyle\left\|1/\sigma_{1}\right\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}=1,

we also have that

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|\geq\left\|\Psi\right\|_{\sigma_{2},p}.

Thus, the desired result follows.
∎

Upon taking
\psi(x)=1
and
\varphi(x)=x,\,x\in T
, in Theorem
4
, we get
the following results, respectively.

Corollary 4
.

(i)

Let
0<p\leq\infty
. Then, the composition operator
C_{\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is bounded if and only if
1/(\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi)\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
.
In this case,

\|C_{\varphi}\|=\left\|1/(\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi)\right\|_{\sigma_{2},p}.

(ii)

Let
0<p\leq\infty
. Then, the multiplication operator
M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is bounded if and only if

\psi/\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}.

Moreover,

\|M_{\psi}\|=\left\|\psi/\sigma_{1}\right\|_{\sigma_{2},p}.

Theorem 5
.

For
0<p<\infty
, the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is bounded if and only if

M=\sup\limits_{x\in T}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|\left(c_{|\varphi(x)|}\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Furthermore,
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|=M.

Proof.
Suppose that
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is a bounded operator. Fix
x\in T.
Define
f
by

f(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\left(c_{|x|}\right)^{1/p}\displaystyle/\sigma_{1}(x)&\mbox{ if }y=x,\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then,
\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1
and

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|\geq\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(f)\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}\geq\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{1/p}.

Since
x
is arbitrary, it is evident that

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|\geq\sup\limits_{x\in T}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|\left(c_{|\varphi(x)|}\right)^{1/p}.

Conversely, suppose that

M=\sup_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{1/p}<\infty.

For
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p},
by the growth estimate (Proposition
2
),
we have

\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)f(\varphi(x))|\leq\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|\frac{c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{1/p}\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}.

Thus,

\sigma_{2}(x)|W_{\psi,\varphi}f(x)|\leq M\,\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}\;\text{ for all }x\in T.

Therefore,
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}\leq M\,\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}

for all
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}.

This yields that,

W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

is a bounded operator with
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|\leq M.

This completes the proof.
∎

As a special case of Theorem
5
, we have the following results.

Corollary 5
.

(i)

For
0<p<\infty
, the composition operator

C_{\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

is bounded if and only if

\|C_{\varphi}\|=\sup\limits_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}\left(c_{|\varphi(x)|}\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

(ii)

For
0<p<\infty
, the multiplication operator

M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

is bounded if and only if

\|M_{\psi}\|=\sup\limits_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(x)}\left(c_{|x|}\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Before we discuss about boundedness of the operator

W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
,
let us introduce few notations. The characteristic (indicator) function at a vertex

x\in T
will be denoted by
\chi_{x}
.
Recall that

D_{n}=\{x\in T:|x|=n\},n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. We denote the set
\{|\varphi(x)|:x\in D_{n}\}

by
[\varphi(D_{n})]
.
For
w\in T,n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, let
N_{\varphi}(n,w)
denote the number of elements in
\varphi^{-1}(w)\cap D_{n}
(the number of elements in
D_{n}
that are mapped to
w

under
\varphi
). For
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, define

N_{m,n}:=\displaystyle\max_{|w|=m}N_{\varphi}(n,w),

the maximum number of elements in
D_{n}
that are mapped to a single element in
D_{m}.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
there exist
v_{m,n}\in D_{m}
such that
N_{\varphi}(n,v_{m,n})=N_{m,n},
equivalently, there are
N_{m,n}
vertices of
D_{n}
those are mapped into
v_{m,n}\in D_{m}
under
\varphi.

Theorem 6
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
choose
v_{m,n}\in D_{m}
as above.

(i)

If

W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is a bounded operator, then

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m\in[\varphi(D_{n})]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
\varphi(x)=v_{m,n}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{m}\right)\leq\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}.

(ii)

If

M=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m\in[\varphi(D_{n})]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{m}\right)<\infty,

then
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is a bounded operator with
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}\leq M
.

Proof.
Suppose
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is a bounded operator.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},
consider a function
f_{n}
on
T
, defined by

f_{n}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{m}^{1/p}}{\sigma_{1}(v_{m,n})}\chi_{v_{m,n}}(x).

Thus,
M_{\sigma_{1},p}(k,f_{n})=1
for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and hence

\|f_{n}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1
all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Note that
f_{n}(\varphi(x))\neq 0
and
|\varphi(x)|=m
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
forces that
\varphi(x)=v_{m,n}
.
Now,

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,\psi(f_{n}\circ\varphi))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}|f_{n}(\varphi(x))|^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m\in[\varphi(D_{n})]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
\varphi(x)=v_{m,n}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{m}.

As

M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,\psi(f_{n}\circ\varphi))\leq\|\psi(f_{n}\circ\varphi)\|^{p}_{\sigma_{1},p}\leq\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

we see that

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m\in[\varphi(D_{n})]}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
\varphi(x)=v_{m,n}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{m}\right)\leq\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}.

Hence the result
(i)
follows.

Now, we prove the result
(ii)
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.
For any

f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
, growth estimate gives that

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,\psi f\circ\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m\in[\varphi(D_{n})]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}|f(\varphi(x))|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m\in[\varphi(D_{n})]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}\frac{c_{m}\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}^{p}}{\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))}.

Thus, for every
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
, one has

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(f)\|^{p}_{\sigma_{2},p}\leq\left(\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m\in[\varphi(D_{n})]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{m}\right)\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}^{p}.

This completes the proof of
(ii)
.
∎

Remark 2
.

Though we do not have a necessary and sufficient condition for the boundedness of the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
, the upper and lower
bounds for
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|
that we get in Theorem
6
, are very close to each other.

Proposition 3
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. If

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}N_{m,n}c_{m}\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}\right)<\infty,

then
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is a bounded operator.

Proof.
Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}.
Then,

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,\psi(f\circ\varphi))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}|f(\varphi(x))|^{p}\frac{\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))}{\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))|f(\varphi(x))|^{p}.

Since every element of
D_{m}
has atmost
N_{m,n}
pre-images in
D_{n},
we have

M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,\psi(f\circ\varphi))\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}N_{m,n}\,c_{m}\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}^{p}.

This yields that

W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\to\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is a bounded
operator with

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|=m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}N_{m,n}\,c_{m}\right).

∎

Remark 3
.

If we take
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=\psi\equiv 1
, then

\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}=\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}=\mathbb{T}_{p}
and

W_{\psi,\varphi}=C_{\varphi}.
As a special case of the previous theorem,
we get

\|C_{\varphi}\|^{p}\leq\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}N_{m,n}\,c_{m}.

Indeed,

\|C_{\varphi}\|^{p}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}N_{m,n}\,c_{m}.

See
[
14
, Theorem 4]
for a proof.

Theorem 7
.

Let
0<p\leq\infty
. Then, the multiplication operator
M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is bounded
if and only if

\psi/\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
. Further, we also have

\|M_{\psi}\|=\left\|\psi/\sigma_{1}\right\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}.

Proof.
Suppose the operator

M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is bounded. For the case
p=\infty
, see Corollary
4
. Now, assume that

p<\infty
. Fix
y\in T.

Define

f_{y}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{c_{|y|}^{1/p}}{\sigma_{1}(y)}&\mbox{ if }x=y,\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then, it is trivial to see that
\|f_{y}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1
and

\|\psi f\|_{\sigma_{2},p}=\frac{\sigma_{2}(y)}{\sigma_{1}(y)}|\psi(y)|.

As
y\in T
was arbitrary, we see that

\left\|\psi/\sigma_{1}\right\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}=\sup_{y\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(y)}{\sigma_{1}(y)}|\psi(y)|\leq\|M_{\psi}\|,

that is,
\psi/\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}.

Conversely, suppose that
\psi/\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}.

For any
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we easily have

M_{\sigma_{2},p}(n,\psi f)\leq\sup_{y\in T}\left(\frac{\sigma_{2}(y)}{\sigma_{1}(y)}|\psi(y)|\right)M_{\sigma_{1},p}(n,f).

Therefore,
\|\psi f\|_{\sigma_{2},p}\leq\|\psi/\sigma_{1}\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}\,\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}
for all
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}.

This gives that,
M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is a bounded operator. From this proof, it is also clear that

\|M_{\psi}\|=\left\|\psi/\sigma_{1}\right\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}.

∎

By taking
\psi(x)=1
in the previous theorem, we have the following interesting result.

Corollary 6
.

For
0<p\leq\infty
, define the identity operator
I:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
by
I(f)=f
. Then,
I

is a bounded operator
(
or equivalently,
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p})
if and only if

\sup_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}<\infty.

5.
Compact weighted composition operators

This section is devoted to compactness of weighted composition operators. For any function

f
on
T
, supremum of
|f|
over an empty set is regarded as zero, that is,

\sup_{x\in\emptyset}|f(x)|=0.

We now begin the section with the following characterization of compact weighted
composition operators between
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
spaces.

Theorem 8
.

Let
0<p,q\leq\infty
. Then, the weighted composition operator

W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},q}

is compact if and only if for every bounded sequence
\{f_{n}\}
in

\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
that converges to
0
pointwise, the sequence
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(f_{n})\|_{\sigma_{2},q}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty.

Proof is similar to that of
[
15
, Theorem 6]
and so we omit its proof.

Theorem 9
.

The operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is compact if and only if

\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{ as }\;\;|y|\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.
Suppose that
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is compact.
For
y\in T
, define
f_{y}
as

f_{y}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}\chi_{y}.
Then,

\displaystyle\|f_{y}\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}=\sup_{x\in T}|\chi_{y}(x)|=1\text{ for all }y\in T.

It is obvious that
f_{y}\rightarrow 0
as
|y|\rightarrow\infty
and

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(f_{y})\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in T}|\sigma_{2}(x)\psi(x)f_{y}(\varphi(x))|=\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|.

As
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is compact, by Theorem
8
, we get that

\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{ as }\;\;|y|\rightarrow\infty.

Conversely, assume that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{ as }\;\;|y|\rightarrow\infty.

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. Then, there exists an
M>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}\hskip 5.0pt\text{whenever}\hskip 5.0pt|y|>M.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle\sup_{|\varphi(x)|>M}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Let
{f_{n}}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}
such that

\|f_{n}\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{n}\rightarrow 0

pointwise as
n\rightarrow\infty.
As
\{y\in T:|y|\leq M\}
is a finite set, there
exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{|y|\leq M}|f_{n}(y)\sigma_{1}(y)|<\frac{\epsilon}{2\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for all}\hskip 5.0ptn>N.

For
n>N
,

\displaystyle\sup_{|\varphi(x)|\leq M}|\sigma_{2}(x)\psi(x)f_{n}(\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|\varphi(x)|\leq M}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|\sup_{|\varphi(x)|\leq M}|f_{n}(\varphi(x))\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle<\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|\frac{\epsilon}{2\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|}=\frac{\epsilon}{2},

and

\displaystyle\sup_{|\varphi(x)|>M}|\sigma_{2}(x)\psi(x)f_{n}(\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|\varphi(x)|>M}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|\sup_{|\varphi(x)|>M}|f_{n}(\varphi(x))\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|f_{n}\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(f_{n})\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}=\sup_{x\in T}|\sigma_{2}(x)\psi(x)f_{n}(\varphi(x))|<\epsilon\hskip 5.0pt\text{for all}\hskip 5.0ptn>N.

Consequently,
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(f_{n})\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Hence,
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is compact by Theorem
8
.
∎

Though we have characterization for compactness of
C_{\varphi}
and
M_{\psi}
by above
theorem,
we state it for
M_{\psi}
because of its independent interest and simplicity.

Corollary 7
.

The multiplication operator

M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

is compact if and only if

\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}|\psi(x)|\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty.

Theorem 10
.

The operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is compact if and only if

\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}\left(c_{|\varphi(x)|}\right)^{1/p}\rightarrow 0\;\;\mbox{ as }\;\;|y|\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.
Suppose that
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is compact. For each
y\in T,
consider a function

f_{y}
defined by

f_{y}(x)=\frac{\left(c_{|y|}\right)^{{1}/{p}}}{\sigma_{1}(y)}\chi_{y}(x).

Then, it is easy to see that
\|f_{y}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1
for all
y\in T
and

f_{y}\rightarrow 0
pointwise as
|y|\rightarrow\infty.
Now,

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(f_{y})\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in T}|\sigma_{2}(x)\psi(x)f_{y}(\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in T}\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|\frac{\left(c_{|y|}\right)^{{1}/{p}}}{\sigma_{1}(y)}\chi_{y}(\varphi(x))

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{{1}/{p}}.

Thus, the desired result follows from Theorem
8
.

Conversely, assume that

\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{{1}/{p}}\rightarrow 0\;\;\text{ as }\;\;|y|\rightarrow\infty.

Then, for a given
\epsilon>0
, there exists an
M>0
such that

\sup_{|\varphi(x)|>M}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{{1}/{p}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Choose a sequence
{(f_{n})}
in
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
such that
\|f_{n}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
pointwise as
n\rightarrow\infty.
As
\{y\in T:|y|\leq M\}
is a finite set, there exists an

N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{|y|\leq M}\frac{|f_{n}(y)|\sigma_{1}(y)}{\left(c_{|y|}\right)^{{1}/{p}}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|}\;\;\text{ for }\;\;n>N.

For any
n>N
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{|\varphi(x)|\leq M}|\sigma_{2}(x)\psi(x)f_{n}(\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|\varphi(x)|\leq M}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{{1}/{p}}\sup_{|\varphi(x)|\leq M}\frac{|f_{n}(\varphi(x))|\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}{c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{{1}/{p}}}

\displaystyle<\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|\frac{\epsilon}{2\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|}=\frac{\epsilon}{2},

and by growth estimate, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{|\varphi(x)|>M}|\sigma_{2}(x)\psi(x)f_{n}(\varphi(x))|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|\varphi(x)|>M}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{{1}/{p}}\|f_{n}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|f_{n}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Thus,

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{n}\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}=\sup_{x\in T}|\sigma_{2}(x)\psi(x)f_{n}(\varphi(x))|<\epsilon

for all
n>N
. This gives that

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{n}\|\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore,

W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a compact operator by Theorem
8
.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we have the following result.

Corollary 8
.

For
0<p<\infty
, the multiplication operator
M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is compact if and only if

\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}|\psi(x)|\left(c_{|x|}\right)^{1/p}\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty.

Theorem 11
.

For
0<p<\infty
, the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is compact if and only if

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}\rightarrow 0\;\text{ as }m\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.
Suppose
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is compact.
For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define
g_{m}
as follows:

g_{m}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mbox{if}\;|x|<m,\\
\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}(x)}&\mbox{if}\;|x|\geq m.\end{array}\right.

Then,
\|g_{m}\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}=1
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
g_{m}\rightarrow 0
pointwise as
m\rightarrow\infty.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,W_{\psi,\varphi}(g_{m}))=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq m\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)^{p}|\psi(x)|^{p}}{\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))}.

Hence,

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(g_{m})\|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }m\rightarrow\infty,

by Theorem
8
.

Conversely, suppose that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }m\rightarrow\infty.

Let
\epsilon>0
be given. Then, there exists an
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq M\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Let
\{f_{k}\}
be a sequence in the unit ball of
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}
such that
f_{k}\rightarrow 0
pointwise as
k\rightarrow\infty.
Since
\{y\in T:|y|<M\}
is a finite set, there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{|y|<M}|f_{k}(y)|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(y)<\frac{\epsilon}{2\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}}\text{ for all }k>N.

Thus, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
k>N
,

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq M\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|<M\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}.

Therefore, for all
k>N
,

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{k}\|_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq M\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))+

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|<M\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f_{k}\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}^{p}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq M\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}+\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}\sup_{|\varphi(x)|<M}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}\frac{\epsilon}{2\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}}=\epsilon.

Thus,
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{k}\|\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence, by Theorem
8
,
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a compact operator. It completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 9
.

The multiplication operator
M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
,
p<\infty
is compact if and only if

M_{\sigma_{2},p}\left(n,\frac{\psi}{\sigma_{1}}\right)\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty.

Theorem 12
.

Consider the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
,
0<p<\infty
. Then,

(i)

the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is compact if

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{|\varphi(x)|}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }m\rightarrow\infty.

(ii)

if
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a compact operator, then

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }m\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.
Consider the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
,
0<p<\infty
.
Note that the function
1/\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
with
\|1/\sigma_{1}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1.

\psi/(\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi)\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
and
\|\psi/(\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi)\|_{\sigma_{2},p}\leq\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{|x|=n}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}\leq\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}.

Now, we prove
(i)
. Assume that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{|\varphi(x)|}\rightarrow 0\mbox{ as }m\rightarrow\infty.

For a given
\epsilon>0,
there exists an
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq M\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{|\varphi(x)|}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Let
{f_{k}}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
such that
\|f_{k}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}\leq 1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f_{k}\rightarrow 0
pointwise as
k\rightarrow\infty.

As
\{y\in T:|y|<M\}
is a finite set, there exists an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{|y|<M}|f_{k}(y)|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(y)<\frac{\epsilon}{2\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}}

for all
k>N
.
Thus, for any
k>N
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}(n,W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq M\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|<M\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)|\psi(x)|^{p}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}.

Therefore, for all
k>N
,

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{k}\|_{\sigma_{2},p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|<M\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))+

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq M\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{|\varphi(x)|}\frac{|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))}{c_{|\varphi(x)|}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}\sup_{|\varphi(x)|<M}|f_{k}(\varphi(x))|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(\varphi(x))+

\displaystyle\|f_{k}\|^{p}_{\sigma_{2},p}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq M\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}c_{|\varphi(x)|}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}\frac{\epsilon}{2\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|^{p}}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}=\epsilon.

This implies that
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{k}\|\rightarrow 0\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

Hence, by Theorem
8
,
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a compact operator.

Next, we prove
(ii)
. Suppose that
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a compact operator.
For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

g_{m}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\mbox{if}\;|x|<m,\\
\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}(x)}&\mbox{if}\;|x|\geq m.\end{array}\right.

Then,
\|g_{m}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
g_{m}\rightarrow 0
pointwise as
m\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence,

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}(g_{m})\|=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|=n\\
|\varphi(x)|\geq m\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|^{p}\rightarrow 0

as
m\rightarrow\infty
, by Theorem
8
. It completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 13
.

For
0<p\leq\infty
, the multiplication operator
M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is compact if and only if

\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}|\psi(x)|\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.
For the case
p=\infty
, refer Corollary
7
. So, it is enough to consider
the case
p<\infty
.

Suppose
M_{\psi}
is compact.
For each
y\in T
, take
f_{y}
as

f_{y}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{\left(c_{|y|}\right)^{{1}/{p}}}{\sigma_{1}(y)}&\mbox{ if }x=y,\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

It is evident that
\|f_{y}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1
for every
y\in T
.
As
f_{y}\rightarrow 0
pointwise as
|y|\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\|M_{\psi}(f_{y})\|=\frac{\sigma_{2}(y)}{\sigma_{1}(y)}|\psi(y)|\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }|y|\rightarrow\infty.

Conversely, suppose that

\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}|\psi(x)|\rightarrow 0\mbox{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the map

\psi_{n}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\psi(x)&\mbox{ if }|x|<n,\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Thus,
M_{\psi_{n}}
is a finite rank operator and hence compact. Further,

\displaystyle\|M_{\psi}-M_{\psi_{n}}\|

\displaystyle=\|M_{\psi-\psi_{n}}\|=\sup_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}|(\psi-\psi_{n})(x)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{|x|\geq n}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}|\psi(x)|\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

As
M_{\psi}
is a limit of a sequence of compact operators,
M_{\psi}
is also a
compact operator.
∎

Corollary 10
.

For
0<p\leq\infty
, the identity operator
I:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is compact if and only if

\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}\rightarrow 0\hskip 5.0ptas\hskip 5.0pt|x|\rightarrow\infty
.

6.
Isometric weighted composition operators

In this section, we will discuss about isometric weighted
composition operators between
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
spaces. Isometric multiplication
and isometric composition operators on
\mathbb{T}_{p}
spaces were characterized in

[
11
, Theorem 4.10]
and
[
14
, Theorems 13 and 14]
, respectively.
When
p\neq q
, there are no isometric multiplication operators from
\mathbb{T}_{p}
to
\mathbb{T}_{q}
(see
[
15
, Theorem 5]
).

Theorem 14
.

The weighted composition operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is isometry if and only if
\varphi
is onto and

\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|=1\;\text{ for all }\;y\in T.

Proof.
Suppose that
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
is an isometry.
We first show that
\varphi
is onto.
For the contrary, assume that
\varphi
is not onto. Choose
y\in T\setminus\varphi(T)
.
Then,
f=\chi_{y}\neq 0,
but
\psi(f\circ\varphi)\equiv 0
. Thus,
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
cannot be isometry. This contradiction yields that
\varphi
is onto.

Next, for
y\in T
, take
f_{y}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}}\chi_{y}
. Then,
\|f_{y}\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}=1
for all
y\in T
.

\displaystyle\|\psi(f_{y}\circ\varphi)\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}\chi_{y}(\psi(x))=\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|.

As
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is an isometry, we see that

\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|=1\;\text{ for all }y\in T.

Conversely, assume that
\varphi
is onto and

\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\left|\frac{\sigma_{2}\psi}{\sigma_{1}\circ\varphi}(x)\right|=1\;\text{ for all }y\in T.

As
\varphi
is onto,
T
can be written as disjoint union of the sets

\varphi^{-1}(y),\,y\in T.
For any
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}
,

\displaystyle\|\psi(f\circ\varphi)\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}|f(\varphi(x))|\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in T}\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}|f(y)|\sigma_{1}(y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in T}|f(y)|\sigma(y)=\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},\infty}.

Therefore,
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is an isometry. Hence the theorem.
∎

Theorem 15
.

For
0<p<\infty
, there are no isometric weighted composition operators
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
.

Proof.
Suppose there exists an isometric operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},\infty}
. By using same arguments as in previous theorem,
we get
\varphi
is onto.
For
y\in T
, consider

f_{y}(x)=\frac{\left(c_{|y|}\right)^{1/p}}{\sigma_{1}(x)}\chi_{y}(x).

Then,
\|f_{y}\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1
for all
y\in T
. As
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is an isometry,
we have

\|\psi(f_{y}\circ\varphi)\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}=\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{1/p}=1

for all
y\in T
.
Now for any
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p},

\displaystyle\|\psi(f\circ\varphi)\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{1/p}\frac{|f(\varphi(x)|\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}{c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{1/p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in T}\left(\sup_{x\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)|\psi(x)|}{\sigma_{1}(\varphi(x))}c_{|\varphi(x)|}^{1/p}\right)\frac{|f(y)|\sigma_{1}(y)}{\left(c_{|y|}\right)^{1/p}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in T}\frac{|f(y)|\sigma_{1}(y)}{\left(c_{|y|}\right)^{1/p}}.

Choose
y_{1},y_{2}\in T
with
|y_{1}|=|y_{2}|
(say
m
). Take
g
as

g(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{1}{2\sigma_{1}(x)}&\mbox{ if }x=y_{1},\\
\frac{1}{3\sigma_{1}(x)}&\mbox{ if }x=y_{2},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then,

\|g\|_{\sigma_{1},p}^{p}=M_{\sigma_{1},p}^{p}(m,g)=\frac{1}{c_{m}}\left(\frac{1}{2^{p}}+\frac{1}{3^{p}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}g\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{y\in T}\frac{|g(y)|^{p}\sigma_{1}^{p}(y)}{c_{|y|}}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{2^{p}c_{m}},\frac{1}{3^{p}c_{m}}\right\}=\frac{1}{2^{p}c_{m}}.

Therefore,
\|g\|_{\sigma_{1},p}\neq\|W_{\psi,\varphi}g\|_{\sigma_{2},\infty}.

Hence,
W_{\psi,\varphi}
cannot be an isometry.
∎

As there are no isometric multiplication operators from
\mathbb{T}_{\infty}
to
\mathbb{T}_{p}
when
p<\infty
(see
[
15
, Theorem 5]
),
we suspect that there are no isometric weighted composition operators
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

when
p<\infty
.
But, it is still an open problem.

7.
Examples

Consider the operators

M_{\psi},C_{\varphi},W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
. From the definition, it is clear that

W_{\psi,\varphi}=M_{\psi}C_{\varphi},\mbox{~~i.e.,~~}W_{\psi,\varphi}(f)=M_{\psi}(C_{\varphi}(f)),\,f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}.

Suppose
M_{\psi},C_{\varphi}
are bounded operators on
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma,p}
.
Then, it is well-known that the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}(=M_{\psi}C_{\varphi})
is
also bounded with
\|W_{\psi,\varphi}\|\leq\|M_{\psi}\|\,\|C_{\varphi}\|
.
Moreover, if at least one of these operators

M_{\psi},C_{\varphi}
is compact, then the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is
also compact.

In this section,
we will compare the boundedness and compactness of operators

M_{\psi},C_{\varphi}
with the boundedness and compactness of the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}
. Throughout the section, we will assume that
0<p<\infty.

Example 1
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, choose
x_{n}\in T
with
|x_{n}|=n
. Define

\psi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{ if }x=x_{n}\mbox{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and
\varphi(x)=x_{n}
when
|x|=n
.

Since
\psi
is a bounded function,
M_{\psi}
is a bounded operator on

\mathbb{T}_{p}
. Consider the function

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}c_{n}^{1/p}&\mbox{ if }x=x_{n},\;n\in\mathbb{{\mathbb{N}}}_{0},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then,
\|f\|_{p}=1
as
M_{p}(n,f)=1
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

\displaystyle M_{p}^{p}(n,C_{\varphi}f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{|x|=n}|f(\varphi(x))|^{p}=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{|x|=n}|f(x_{n})|^{p}=c_{n}.

Therefore,

C_{\varphi}f\notin\mathbb{T}_{p}
as the sequence
(c_{n})
is unbounded.
Thus,
C_{\varphi}
is not a bounded operator on
\mathbb{T}_{p}
.

Next, for
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,

\displaystyle M_{p}^{p}(n,\psi(f\circ\varphi))=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{|x|=n}|\psi(x)f(\varphi(x))|^{p}=\frac{1}{c_{n}}|\psi(x_{n})f(\varphi(x_{n}))|^{p}=\frac{1}{c_{n}}|f(x_{n})|^{p}\leq\|f\|^{p}.

Thus,
\|\psi(f\circ\varphi)\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}
for all
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}.

Therefore,
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a bounded operator on
\mathbb{T}_{p}.

This example shows that
W_{\psi,\varphi}
can be a bounded operator, though
C_{\varphi}
is not.

Example 2
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, fix
x_{n}\in T
with
|x_{n}|=n
.
Define

\psi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}c_{n}^{{1}/{p}}&\mbox{ if }x=x_{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and
\varphi(x)\equiv\textsl{o}
, where
o
is the root of T.
As
\{c_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
is unbounded,
M_{\psi}
is not a bounded operator
on
\mathbb{T}_{p}
. Note that,

\varphi\equiv\textsl{o}
implies
C_{\varphi}=
evaluation at
o
, which is
clearly a bounded linear functional.

For
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

\displaystyle M_{p}^{p}(n,\psi(f\circ\varphi))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{|x|=n}|\psi(x)|^{p}|f(o)|^{p}=|f(o)|^{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

Therefore,
\|\psi(f\circ\varphi)\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}
for all
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
,
which in turn implies that
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a bounded operator on
\mathbb{T}_{p}
.

This example shows that
W_{\psi,\varphi}
can be a bounded operator though

M_{\psi}
is not a bounded operator.

Example 3
.

As before, for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, fix
x_{n}\in T
with
|x_{n}|=n
.
Choose a subsequence
\{c_{n_{k}}\}
of
\{c_{n}\}
such that
c_{n_{k}}\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. In particular,
c_{n_{2k+1}}\mbox{~and~}c_{n_{2k}}\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Consider the maps

\psi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}c_{n}^{{1}/{p}}&\mbox{ if }x=x_{n},n=n_{2k+1},\;k\in\mathbb{N},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and

\varphi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}x_{n_{2k}}&\mbox{ if }|x|={n_{2k}},\;k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\
\textsl{o }&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Since
c_{n_{2k+1}}\rightarrow\infty
as
k\rightarrow\infty,
we get that

\psi
is an unbounded function and hence
M_{\psi}
is not a bounded operator
on
\mathbb{T}_{p}
.
Take

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}c_{n}^{{1}/{p}}&\mbox{ if }x=x_{n},\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then,
\|f\|_{p}=1
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle M_{p}^{p}(n_{2k},C_{\varphi}f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n_{2k}}}\sum_{|x|=n_{2k}}|f(\varphi(x))|^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n_{2k}}}\sum_{|x|=n_{2k}}|f(x_{n_{2k}})|^{p}=c_{n_{2k}}\rightarrow\infty\mbox{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

Thus
C_{\varphi}f\notin\mathbb{T}_{p}
, and hence
C_{\varphi}
is not a bounded
operator on
\mathbb{T}_{p}
.

For
n=n_{2k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p},
it is obvious that

M_{p}^{p}(n,\psi(f\circ\varphi))=0,

as
\psi(x)=0
for all
x
with
|x|=n_{2k}.

For
n\neq n_{2k},k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle M_{p}^{p}(n,\psi(f\circ\varphi))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{|x|=n}|\psi(x)|^{p}|f(o)|^{p}=\frac{1}{c_{n}}|\psi(x_{n})|^{p}|f(o)|^{p}=|f(o)|^{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

Therefore,
\|\psi(f\circ\varphi)\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}
for all
f\in\mathbb{T}_{p}
.
Hence
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a bounded operator on
\mathbb{T}_{p}
.

This example shows that
W_{\psi,\varphi}
can be a bounded operator even though both
M_{\psi}
and
C_{\varphi}
are not bounded operators.

Example 4
.

Choose the weights
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
such that
\sigma_{2}/\sigma_{1}

is unbounded. For example,
\sigma_{1}(x)=|x|+1
and
\sigma_{2}(x)=(|x|+1)^{2}
.
Take
\varphi(x)=x
,
x\in T.

As
\sigma_{2}/\sigma_{1}
is unbounded, the operator
C_{\varphi}
is not bounded,
by Corollary
6
.
Define

\psi(x)=\frac{1}{1+|x|}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}(x)}{\sigma_{2}(x)}\right),

so that
\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}|\psi(x)|\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty.

Thus,
W_{\psi,\varphi}=M_{\psi}
is a compact operator from
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
to
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
, by Theorem
13
.

The above example shows that the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
can be compact, even if
C_{\varphi}
is an unbounded operator.

Example 5
.

Choose
\sigma_{1}
such that
1/\sigma_{1}
is unbounded
(
eg.
\sigma_{1}(x)=1/(|x|+1))
. Take
\varphi
to be a constant map. That is,
\varphi(x)=y
for all
x\in T
and for some

y\in T
. Define
\psi=1/\sigma_{2}
, so that
\psi\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
with
\|\psi\|_{\sigma_{2},p}=1.

Consider the operator
M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}.

As
\sigma_{2}\psi/\sigma_{1}
is unbounded,
M_{\psi}
is not a bounded operator by
Theorem
7
.
For any
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
,

\displaystyle\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f\|_{\sigma_{2},p}

\displaystyle=\|\psi f(y)\|_{\sigma_{2},p}=|f(y)|\,\|\psi\|_{\sigma_{2},p}=|f(y)|\leq\frac{\left(c_{|y|}\right)^{{1}/{p}}}{\sigma_{1}(y)}\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}.

Thus,
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}

is a bounded operator.

To prove the compactness, start with a bounded sequence
\{f_{n}\}
in
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
such that
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
pointwise. Then,

\|W_{\psi,\varphi}f_{n}\|_{\sigma_{2},p}=|f_{n}(y)|\,\|\psi\|_{\sigma_{2},p}=|f_{n}(y)|\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore,
W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a compact operator, by Theorem
8
.

This example shows that the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
can be compact even if
M_{\psi}
is an unbounded operator.

Example 6
.

For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, fix
x_{n}\in T
with
|x_{n}|=n
. Choose
\sigma_{1}
such that
\sigma_{1}(x_{n})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty\,(
eg.
\sigma_{1}(x_{n})=\frac{1}{n+1})
, and
\sigma_{2}
such that

\sum_{|x|=n}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)\geq 1
if
n
is even
(
eg.

\sigma_{2}(x)=1
for all
x\in T)
.
Take

\psi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1/\sigma_{2}(x)&\mbox{ if }x=x_{2k-1},k\in\mathbb{N},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Since

\sup_{x\in T}\frac{\sigma_{2}(x)}{\sigma_{1}(x)}|\psi(x)|=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}(x_{2k-1})}

is not finite,
M_{\psi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is not a bounded operator, by Theorem
7
.

Let us now define,

\varphi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}x_{n}&\mbox{ if }|x|\mbox{ is even},\\
\textsl{o}&\mbox{ if }|x|\mbox{ is odd}.\end{array}\right.

Consider the map

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{\left(c_{n}\right)^{{1}/{p}}}{\sigma_{1}(x_{n})}&\mbox{ if }x=x_{n},n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then,
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
with
\|f\|_{\sigma_{1},p}=1
. For
n=2k
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{2},p}(n,C_{\varphi}(f))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}^{{1}/{p}}}\left(\sum_{|x|=n}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)\right)^{{1}/{p}}|f(x_{n})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}(x_{n})}\left(\sum_{|x|=n}\sigma_{2}^{p}(x)\right)^{{1}/{p}}\geq\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}(x_{n})}\rightarrow\infty

as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Therefore,
C_{\varphi}f\notin\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
.
That is,
C_{\varphi}
is not a bounded operator from
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
to
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
.

Now consider the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
.
For
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
and
n=2k
,
k\in N_{0}
,

M_{\sigma_{2},p}(n,\psi(f\circ\varphi))=0\mbox{~as $\psi(x)=0$ if $|x|$ is even.}

For
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
and
n=2k-1
,
k\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle M_{\sigma_{2},p}(n,\psi(f\circ\varphi))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{n}^{{1}/{p}}}\sigma_{2}(x_{n})|\psi(x_{n})f(\textsl{o})|=\frac{|f(\textsl{o})|}{c_{n}^{{1}/{p}}}\leq|f(\textsl{o})|.

Therefore,
\|\psi(f\circ\varphi)\|_{\sigma_{2},p}\leq|f(\textsl{o})|\leq\|f\|
for all
f\in\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}.

Thus ,
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
is a bounded operator.

To see the compactness of
W_{\psi,\varphi}
, let us begin with a bounded sequence

\{f_{n}\}
in
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
with
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
pointwise.
As
\|\psi(f_{n}\circ\varphi)\|_{\sigma_{2},p}\leq|f_{n}(\textsl{o})|\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty,

W_{\psi,\varphi}
is a compact operator by Theorem
8
.

This example shows that
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
can be a compact operator even if both the operators
M_{\psi}
and
C_{\varphi}
are unbounded.

As concluding remarks, we leave some unsolved questions:

(i)

Characterize the bounded, compact, isometric weighted composition
operators from
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}
to
\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},p}
,
0<p<\infty
.

(ii)

The same questions for the operator
W_{\psi,\varphi}:\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{1},p}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\sigma_{2},q}
,

p\neq q
and
0<p,q<\infty
, are also open.

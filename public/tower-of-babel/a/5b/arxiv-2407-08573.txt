2.
Familial descent

One of the main insights behind our main result, Theorem
3.1
, is
that we can reduce the study of effective descent morphisms (respectively,
stable regular epimorphisms) in
\mathsf{Ord}//X
to the study of effective
descent morphisms (respectively, stable regular epimorphisms) in
\mathsf{Fam}(X)

and
\mathsf{Ord}
. This latter problem in
\mathsf{Fam}(X)
has been considered before
in
[
17
, Lemma 4.4]
(see also
[
18
, Lemma 3.17]
), from which we
proceed to recall the relevant details.

For a fixed ordered set
X
, we denote by
\mathsf{Fam}(X)
the category of
set-indexed
families of elements
in
X
. It consists of:

–

Objects: families
(\alpha_{j})_{j\in J}
of elements
\alpha_{j}\in X

indexed by a set
J
,

–

Morphisms
(\alpha_{j})_{j\in J}\to(\beta_{k})_{k\in K}
: a function

f\colon J\to K
such that
\alpha_{j}\leq\beta_{f(j)}
for all
j\in J
.

We will assume that
X

locally has binary meets
, that is,

\downarrow x
has binary meets for all
x
. When
X
is seen as a thin
category, this condition is equivalent to saying that
X
has pullbacks.
Thus, it follows that
\mathsf{Fam}(X)
is a category with pullbacks (see, e.g.

[
2
, Sections 6.2, 6.3]
).

We also recall that an ordered set
X
with finite meets is said to
be
cartesian closed
if there is an assignment
(y,z)\mapsto z^{y}
, which satisfies

x\wedge y\leq z\iff x\leq z^{y}

for every
x\in X
. When
X
is complete and the underlying order is
antisymmetric, this is equivalent to
X
being a
frame
.
Likewise, an ordered set
X
with locally binary meets is said to be

locally cartesian closed
if
\downarrow x
is cartesian closed
for all
x\in X
.

While the results of
[
17
,
18
]
study (effective) descent morphisms
in
\mathsf{Fam}(X)
when
X
has a top element – due to the pertinence of the
work carried out within – the results plainly extend to the setting where

X
does not admit a top element.

The characterization

Having reviewed the necessary details, we may proceed to prove our main

Theorem 3.1

Let

(3.i)

\forall\,b\in B,\;\;\forall\,a^{\prime}\in f^{-1}(b),\quad\alpha(a^{\prime})\wedge\Join_{a\in f^{-1}(b)}\sigma(a)\cong\sigma(a^{\prime}).

Corollary 3.3
.

Let

be a locally cartesian closed, locally complete ordered set with a

we have

Proof.

for each

.

(f_{i}\colon(A_{i},\alpha_{i})\to(B_{i},\beta_{i}))_{i\in I}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Ord}//X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{i\in I}\mathsf{Ord}//X_{i}}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a locally complete ordered set. Its connected components are
locally complete ordered sets with bottom element. Moreover, if

is
locally cartesian closed, then so is each component.

Proof.

For

It is enough to confirm that if we have

in

calculation shows that we have a chain

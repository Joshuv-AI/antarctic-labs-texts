Abstract
. The notion of P P -algebra due to Margolis, building on work of Moore & Peterson, was motivated by the case of the Steenrod algebra at a prime and its modules. We develop aspects of this theory further, focusing especially on coherent modules and finite dimensional modules. We also discuss the dual Hopf algebra of P P -algebra and its comodules. One of our aims is provide a collection of techniques for calculating cohomology groups over P P -algebras and their duals, in particular giving vanishing results. Much of our work is implicit in that of Margolis and others but we are unaware of systematic discussions in the literature. We give some examples illustrating topological applications which follow easily from our results.

Proposition 1.5

Let

be a finite

\Ext_{A}^{*}(M,N)=0.

The left exact functor

.

Pseudo-coherent modules over
a coherent ring

In Bourbaki
[
5
]
*ex. §3.10,
as well as coherent modules, pseudo-coherent
modules are considered, where a module is

if every finitely
generated submodule is finitely presented
(so over a coherent ring it is coherent).
The reader is warned that pseudo-coherent
is sometimes used in a different sense; for
example, over a coherent ring the definition
of Weibel
[
20
]
*example II.7.1.4
corresponds to our coherent.

Examples of pseudo-coherent modules over
a coherent ring

include coproducts
of coherent modules such as the finitely
related modules defined by

9
]
*chapter 2§4.
However pseudo-coherent modules do not form
a full abelian subcategory of

since
cokernels of homomorphisms between pseudo-coherent
modules need not be pseudo-coherent. Nevertheless,
pseudo-coherent modules do occur quite commonly
when working with coherent modules over
P
-algebras,
for example as tensor products over a Hopf
algebra.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
A
be a ring and
M
a pseudo-coherent

A
-module. Then

is the union of its
coherent submodules and therefore their
colimit.

Proof.

Every element
m\in M
generates a cyclic
submodule which is coherent.
∎

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
A
be a coherent ring and
B
an Artinian
subring where
A
is flat as a right
B
-module.
Then every extended
A
-module
A\otimes_{B}N

is pseudo-coherent.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq A\otimes_{B}N
be a finitely
generated submodule. Taking a finite generating
set and expressing each element as a sum of basic
tensors we find that
U\subseteq A\otimes_{B}U^{\prime}

where
U^{\prime}\subseteq N
is a finitely generated
submodule. As
B
is Artinian and so Noetherian,
U^{\prime}

is finitely presented, so by flatness of
A
,

A\otimes_{B}U^{\prime}
is a finitely presented
A
-module.
Since
A
is coherent,
U
is also finitely
presented.
∎

An important special case of this occurs when
A

is a coherent algebra over a field
\Bbbk
and
B

is a finite dimensional subalgebra. If

is
a
B
-module then it is locally finite, i.e.,
every element is contained in a finite
dimensional submodule.

In general, the tensor product of two coherent
modules over a Hopf algebra that is a

is not a coherent module. However, as we will
soon see, it turns out that it is pseudo-coherent.

Assumption 1.9

We will assume from now on that

is a cocommutative

is

Now given two left

Pseudo-coherence itself is probably of limited
practical use, but stronger versions are perhaps
more likely to be of use for computational

Definition 1.12
.

A module over a coherent ring is

The dual of a
P
-algebra and its comodules

The theory of

-algebras can be dualised: given
a finite type

-algebra
A

dual
A_{*}
by setting

(doing this the finite

-algebra
; although
this is not standard terminology it seems appropriate
and convenient.

When working with comodules over a

-algebra

we will use homological grading. For left

of finite type there is no significant difference
between working with them or their (degree-wise)

\Cohom_{A_{*}}(M_{*},N_{*})\cong\Hom_{A}(N^{*},M^{*}),

where
M^{n}=\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(M_{n},\mathbb{F}_{2})

is
made into a left

-module using the antipode.
More generally,

\Coext^{s,*}_{A_{*}}(M_{*},N_{*})\cong\Ext^{s,*}_{A}(N^{*},M^{*}),

where
\Coext^{s,*}_{A_{*}}(M_{*},-)
denotes the
right derived functor of
\Cohom_{A_{*}}(M_{*},-)
,
which can be computed using extended comodules
which are injective comodules here since we
are working over a field.

Here are the dual versions of
Propositions
1.1

and
1.2
. Recall that a
cofree

or

A_{*}
-comodule is one of the
form

is a graded

.

Suppose that

a bounded below cofree

Proposition 2.2
.

For a surjective morphism of

\Coext^{*,*}_{A_{*}}(A_{*},A_{*}\square_{B_{*}}\Bbbk)\cong\Coext^{*,*}_{B_{*}}(A_{*},\Bbbk)=\Cohom_{B_{*}}(A_{*},\Bbbk)=0.

.

If

3.
Some homological algebra

In this section we describe some Cartan-Eilenberg
spectral sequences for comodules over a commutative
Hopf algebra over a field. Some of these are
similar to other examples in the literature
such as that for computing
\Cotor
for Hopf
algebroids in
[
16
]
.

To ease notation, in this section we suppress
internal gradings and assume that all our
objects are connective and of finite type
over a field
\Bbbk
. We refer to the classic

[
13
]
as well as the more
recent
[
12
]
for notation
and basic ideas about graded Hopf algebras.

Before discussing cohomology for comodules,
we will recall the dual theory to modules
over algebras, where there are classical
Cartan-Eilenberg spectral sequences of
[
6
]

for a normal sequence of Hopf algebras
over a field
\Bbbk
,

(3.1)

R\to S\to S/\!/R

where
S
is a free
R
-module. Then for a left

S/\!/R
-module
L
and a left
S
-module

there is a spectral sequence of the form

(3.2)

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}=\Ext^{s}_{S/\!/R}(L,\Ext^{t}_{R}(\Bbbk,M))\Longrightarrow\Ext^{s+t}_{S}(L,M).

There is another similar spectral sequence
for a left
S
-module
M
and a left

S/\!/R
-module
N
which has the form

(3.3)

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}=\Ext^{s}_{S/\!/R}(\Tor^{t}_{R}(\Bbbk,M),N)\Longrightarrow\Ext^{s+t}_{S}(M,N).

Since
\Ext
and
\Tor
are balanced functors,
one approach to setting these spectral sequences
is by resolving both variables and using double
complex spectral sequences. However, they can
instead be viewed as composite functor spectral
sequences obtained using injective or projective
resolutions of the
S
-module
M
. When the
algebras and modules are graded
\Bbbk
-vector spaces,
these spectral sequences are tri-graded; also,
in topological applications, (
3.1
)
is often a sequence of cocommutative Hopf algebras.

Now suppose we have a sequence of homomorphisms
of connected commutative graded Hopf algebras
over
\Bbbk
,

K\backslash\!\backslash H\rightarrowtail H\twoheadrightarrow K,

where in the notation of
[
13
]
*definition 3.5,

K\backslash\!\backslash H=\Bbbk\square_{K}H=H\square_{K}\Bbbk\subseteq H.

We also assume given a left

K\backslash\!\backslash H
-comodule

M
and a left
H
-comodule
N
. Of course

M
and
N
inherit structures of
H
-comodule
and
K
-comodule respectively, where

is trivial as a

-comodule. Our aim is
to calculate

derived functor of

\Cohom_{H}(M,-)\colon\mathbf{Comod}_{K\backslash\!\backslash H}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\Bbbk};\quad N\mapsto\Cohom_{H}(M,N).

Following Hovey
[
8

will write

\textstyle{U\otimes V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu\otimes\mu}
\textstyle{(H\otimes U)\otimes(H\otimes V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{(H\otimes H)\otimes(U\otimes V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\varphi}\otimes\Id}

For a vector space

-comodule with

\textstyle{U\otimes W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

If

K\backslash\!\backslash H\overset{H}{\wedge}L=(H\square_{K}\Bbbk)\overset{H}{\wedge}L\cong H\square_{K}L,

We will use two more functors

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

be a left

-comodule and

a left

-comodule. Then there is a first

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}=\Coext_{K\backslash\!\backslash H}^{s}(M,\Cotor_{K}^{t}(\Bbbk,N))\Longrightarrow\Coext_{H}^{s+t}(M,N).

is a trivial
K
-comodule then

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}\cong\Coext_{K\backslash\!\backslash H}^{s}(M,\Cotor_{K}^{t}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)\overset{K\backslash\!\backslash H}{\wedge}N).

There is another spectral sequence that
we will use whose construction requires
that one of the Hopf algebras involved
is a
P_{*}
-algebra. The reason for this
is discussed in Remark

:
in the category of finite type connected
comodules, extended comodules are projective

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that

are

-algebras. Let

be a left

-comodule which admits a projective resolution

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}=\Coext_{K\backslash\!\backslash H}^{s}(\Cotor_{K}^{t}(\Bbbk,M),N)\Longrightarrow\Coext_{H}^{s+t}(M,N).

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}\cong\Coext_{K\backslash\!\backslash H}^{s}(\Cotor_{K}^{t}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)\overset{K\backslash\!\backslash H}{\wedge}M,N).

The construction is similar to the other one,
and involves expressing

as a composition

\Cohom_{K\backslash\!\backslash H}(-,N)\circ(K\backslash\!\backslash H\square_{H}(-))=\Cohom_{K\backslash\!\backslash H}(K\backslash\!\backslash H\square_{H}(-),N)\cong\Cohom_{H}(-,N).

The functor

Of course the condition that

5.
Recollections on the Steenrod algebra
and its dual

The theory of
P
-algebras applies to many
situations involving sub and quotient Hopf
algebras of the Steenrod algebra and its
dual for a prime. We will focus attention
on the prime
2
but the methods are
applicable for all primes.

To illustrate this, here is a simple application
involving the mod
2
Steenrod algebra; this
result appears in
[
15
]
*corollary 4.10.
We denote the mod
2
Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum
by
H=H\mathbb{F}_{2}
and recall that

\mathcal{A}_{*}=H_{*}(H)=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\ldots]=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n},\ldots]

where
\zeta_{n}=\chi(\xi_{n})\in\mathcal{A}_{2^{n}-1}

and the coproduct is given by the equivalent
formulae

\psi\xi_{n}=\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\xi_{n-i}^{2^{i}}\otimes\xi_{i},\quad\psi\zeta_{n}=\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant n}\zeta_{i}\otimes\zeta_{n-i}^{2^{i}}.

Doubling

The operation of
doubling
has been
used frequently in studying
\mathcal{A}
-modules.
The reader is referred to the account of
Margolis
[
11
]
*section 15.3
which we will use as background.

Since the dual
\mathcal{A}_{*}
is a commutative
Hopf algebra, it admits a Frobenius
endomorphism
\mathcal{A}_{*}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}
which
doubles degrees and has Hopf algebra
cokernel

\mathcal{E}_{*}=\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}=\Lambda_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\overline{\zeta}_{s}:s\geqslant 1),

where
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\zeta_{s}^{2}:s\geqslant 1]
.
Dually, there is a Verschiebung
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}

which halves degrees and satisfies

\Sq^{r}\mapsto\begin{cases}\Sq^{r/2}&if $r$ is even,\\
\phantom{\;\;\;}0&if $r$ is odd.\end{cases}

The kernel of this Verschiebung is the
ideal generated by the Milnor primitives

\mathrm{P}^{0}_{t}
(
t\geqslant 1
), hence there
is a degree-halving isomorphism of Hopf
algebras
\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{A}
,
where
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is the
subHopf algebra generated by the primitives

\mathrm{P}^{0}_{t}
and dual to the exterior
quotient Hopf algebra
\mathcal{E}_{*}
.

Given a left (graded)
\mathcal{A}
-module
M
, we
can induce an
\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}
-module

M_{(1)}
where

M_{(1)}^{n}=\begin{cases}M^{n/2}&if $n$ is even,\\
0&if $n$ is odd,\end{cases}

and we write
x_{(1)}
to indicate the
element
x\in M
regarded as an element
of
M_{(1)}
; the module structure is
given by

\Sq^{r}(x_{(1)})=\begin{cases}(\Sq^{r/2}x)_{(1)}&if $r$ is even,\\
0&if $r$ is odd,\end{cases}

Using this construction, the category of
left
\mathcal{A}
-modules
\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}
admits
an additive functor to the category of
evenly graded
\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}
-modules,

\Phi\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{ev}};\quad M\mapsto M_{(1)}

which is an isomorphism of categories.
The quotient homomorphism
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}

also induces an additive isomorphism of categories

\rho^{*}\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{ev}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{ev}}

and it is often useful to consider the
composition

\rho^{*}\circ\Phi\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathrm{ev}}
.

By iterating
\Phi^{(1)}=\Phi
we obtain
isomorphisms

\Phi^{(s)}=\Phi\circ\Phi^{(s-1)}\colon\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathbf{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}^{(s-1)}}^{(s)};\quad M\mapsto M_{(s)}

where the codomain is the category of

\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}^{(s-1)}
-modules concentrated
in degrees divisible by
2^{s}
and

\mathcal{E}^{(s-1)}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is the subHopf
algebra multiplicatively generated by the
elements

(5.1)

\mathrm{P}^{a}_{b}\quad(s-1\geqslant a\geqslant 0,\;b\geqslant 1),

and
\mathcal{E}^{(0)}=\mathcal{E}
.

By doubling all three of the variables involved
the following homological result is immediate
for
e\geqslant 1
and two
\mathcal{A}
-modules
M,N
:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Ext_{\mathcal{A}_{(e)}}^{s,2^{e}t}(M_{(e)},N_{(e)})\cong\Ext_{\mathcal{A}}^{s,t}(M,N).

Because doubling is induced using a grade
changing Hopf algebra endomorphism, the
double
\mathcal{A}_{(1)}
is also a Hopf algebra
isomorphic to the quotient Hopf algebra

\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}
and dual to the
subHopf algebra of squares

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{*}
which is
also given by

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}=\mathcal{A}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{2}\square_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}\mathcal{A}_{*}=(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*})\backslash\!\backslash\mathcal{A}_{*}.

More generally, for any
s\geqslant 1
,
\mathcal{A}_{(s)}

is isomorphic to the quotient Hopf algebra
of
\mathcal{A}/\!/\mathcal{E}^{(s)}
dual to the subalgebra
of
2^{s}
-th powers

\mathcal{A}^{(s)}_{*}=(\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(s)}_{*})\backslash\!\backslash\mathcal{A}_{*}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{*}.

In many ways, doubling is more transparent
when viewed in terms of comodules. For
an
\mathcal{A}_{*}
-comodule
M_{*}
, we can define
a
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}
-coaction

\mu^{(1)}\colon M_{*}^{(1)}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}\otimes M_{*}^{(1)}

where
M_{*}^{(1)}
denotes
M_{*}
with its
degrees doubled; this is given on elements
by the composition

\textstyle{M_{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu}
\scriptstyle{\mu^{(1)}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{A}_{*}\otimes M_{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(-)^{2}\otimes\Id}
\textstyle{\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}\otimes M_{*}.}

By iterating we also obtain a
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(s)}
-coaction

\mu^{(s)}\colon M_{*}^{(s)}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(s)}\otimes M_{*}^{(s)}
.

Then the comodule analogue of (
5.2
)
is

(5.3)

\displaystyle\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(e)}}^{s,2^{e}t}(M_{*}^{(e)},N_{*}^{(e)})\cong\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(M_{*},N_{*}).

We can use iterated doubling combined with
Proposition
1.2
to show that
for any
d\geqslant 1
,

(5.4)

\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}_{*},\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(d)})\cong\Ext_{\mathcal{A}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}_{(d)},\mathcal{A})=0.

By doubling all three of the variables involved
here we can also prove that for
e\geqslant 0
,

(5.5)

\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(e)}}^{s,2^{e}t}(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(e)},\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(d+e)})\cong\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}_{*},\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(d)})=0.

Some families of quotient
P_{*}
-algebras
of
\mathcal{A}_{*}

We will begin by describing some quotients
of the dual Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}_{*}
. For
any
n\geqslant 1
,
(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})\lhd\mathcal{A}_{*}

is a Hopf ideal so there is a quotient Hopf
algebra
\mathcal{A}_{*}/(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})

together with the subHopf algebra

\mathcal{P}(n)_{*}=\mathcal{A}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n})}\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{*}

and in fact

\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)_{*}=\mathcal{A}_{*}/(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n}).

Similarly, for any
s\geqslant 0
, the ideal

(\zeta_{1}^{2^{s}},\ldots,\zeta_{n}^{2^{s}})\lhd\mathcal{A}_{*}

is a Hopf ideal and there is a quotient
Hopf algebra

\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}=\mathcal{A}_{*}/(\zeta_{1}^{2^{s}},\ldots,\zeta_{n}^{2^{s}})

with associated subHopf algebra

\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}=\mathcal{A}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\zeta_{1}^{2^{s}},\ldots,\zeta_{n}^{2^{s}}]\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{*}.

For each
t\geqslant 0
there is a finite quotient
Hopf algebra

\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}/(\zeta_{1}^{2^{s+t}},\zeta_{2}^{2^{s+t-1}},\ldots,\zeta_{t}^{2^{s+1}},\zeta_{t+1}^{2^{s}},\ldots,\zeta_{n}^{2^{s}})

and we have

\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}=\lim_{t}\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}/(\zeta_{1}^{2^{s+t}},\zeta_{2}^{2^{s+t-1}},\ldots,\zeta_{t}^{2^{s+1}},\zeta_{t+1}^{2^{s}},\ldots,\zeta_{n}^{2^{s}})

where the limit is computed degree-wise. The
graded dual Hopf algebra

\mathcal{P}(n)_{(s)}=(\mathcal{P}(n)_{*}^{(s)})^{*}=\Hom(\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2})

is the colimit of the finite dual Hopf algebras

\Hom(\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}/(\zeta_{1}^{2^{s+t}},\zeta_{2}^{2^{s+t-1}},\ldots,\zeta_{t}^{2^{s+1}},\zeta_{t+1}^{2^{s}},\ldots,\zeta_{n}^{2^{s}}),\mathbb{F}_{2}),

i.e.,

\mathcal{P}(n)_{(s)}=\colim_{t}\Hom(\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}/(\zeta_{1}^{2^{s+t}},\zeta_{2}^{2^{s+t-1}},\ldots,\zeta_{t}^{2^{s+1}},\zeta_{t+1}^{2^{s}},\ldots,\zeta_{n}^{2^{s}}),\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Therefore
\mathcal{P}(n)_{(s)}
is a
P
-algebra and

\mathcal{P}(n)^{(s)}_{*}
is a
P_{*}
-algebra.

6.
Some comodules and their cohomology

Later we will need to determine various
cohomology groups such as

\Coext^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2}).

using the Cartan-Eilenberg spectral sequence
of Proposition
3.1
,

(6.1)

\mathrm{E}^{s,t}_{2}=\Coext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}))\Longrightarrow\Coext^{s+t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2}),

where we have suppressed the internal grading.
In fact,
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}
is a projective

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}
-comodule, so
\mathrm{E}^{s,t}_{2}=0

when
s>0
, therefore we only need to consider

\mathrm{E}^{0,t}_{2}=\Cohom_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})).

Here the
\Cotor
term is bigraded with

\Cotor^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})=\mathbb{F}_{2}[q_{n}:n\geqslant 0]

for
q_{n}=[\overline{\zeta_{n+1}}]\in\Cotor^{1,2^{n+1}-1}

represented in the cobar construction by the
residue class of

\overline{\zeta_{n+1}}\in\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}
.
We need to understand the comodule structure
on this and similar
\Cotor
groups.

Now we can consider the adjoint coaction
for
\mathcal{A}_{*}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The left adjoint coaction of
\mathcal{A}_{*}
is
given by

\mu\colon\mathcal{A}_{*}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{*};\quad\mu(\zeta_{n})=\sumop\displaylimits_{i\geqslant 0}\sumop\displaylimits_{j\geqslant 0}\zeta_{i}\xi_{n-i-j}^{2^{i+j}}\otimes\zeta_{j}^{2^{i}}.

Proof.

This follows by iterating the coaction
and using the formulae

\psi\zeta_{n}=\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leqslant k\leqslant n}\zeta_{k}\otimes\zeta_{n-k}^{2^{k}},\quad\xi_{r}=\chi(\zeta_{r}).\qed

The left coaction on
q_{n}
can be deduced from
that on
\zeta_{n+1}
where we can ignore
all terms in the sum for
\mu(\zeta_{n+1})

with
i>0
, thus giving

(6.2)

\mu q_{n}=\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leqslant j\leqslant n}\xi_{n-j}^{2^{j+1}}\otimes q_{j}.

This extends to polynomials in the
q_{n}
using
multiplicativity.

This coaction is visibly defined over

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{*}
, and in
practise we will only require the coaction over
the quotient
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(3)}_{*}

we will denote this coaction by

\overline{\mu}\colon\Cotor^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(3)}_{*}\otimes\Cotor^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}),

where for

,

\overline{\mu}q_{n}=\overline{\xi_{n}^{2}}\otimes q_{0}+\overline{\xi_{n-1}^{4}}\otimes q_{1}+1\otimes q_{n}

and

\overline{\mu}q_{1}=\overline{\xi_{1}^{2}}\otimes q_{0}+1\otimes q_{1}.

In what follows we will make use of this

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(3)}_{*}
-comodule structure
and also the induced
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*}
-comodule
structure.

Proposition 6.2
.

For
k\geqslant 0
there are no non-trivial

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*}
-comodule homomorphisms

Hence

Before giving the proof, we will define for
each

, an increasing filtration of
the
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*}
-comodule

\mathrm{F}^{k,0}=\mathbb{F}_{2}\{q_{0}^{k}\},\ \quad\mathrm{F}^{k,k}=\mathbb{F}_{2}\{q_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{r_{\ell}}:\sumop\displaylimits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant\ell}r_{i}=k\}.

is a non-trivial comodule homomorphism. Choose

.
Then the composition

(6.3)

\textstyle{M_{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\scriptstyle{\overline{f}}

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}
-comodules

\mathcal{P}(n)^{(1)}_{*}\cong\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(1)}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2}

so by a standard ‘change of rings’ isomorphism,

\mathrm{E}^{s,t}_{2}\cong\Coext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})).

Again the Milnor-Moore theorem shows that

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}
is a cofree

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(1)}_{*}
-comodule
and since this is a
P_{*}
-algebra,

\mathrm{E}^{s,t}_{2}=0
for
s>0
. Now
a similar argument to that in the proof
of Proposition
6.2

shows that
\mathrm{E}^{0,t}_{2}=0
.
∎

Before passing on to discuss other examples,
we note that in
\mathcal{A}_{*}
there is a subcomodule

{\vphantom{\mathcal{A}}}{}^{\leqslant k}\mathcal{A}_{*}
spanned by the
monomials in the
\xi_{i}
of degree at most
k
.
Under the doubling isomorphism this corresponds
to a subcomodule
{\vphantom{\mathcal{A}}}{}^{\leqslant k}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}

of
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}
spanned by monomials in the

\xi_{i}^{2}
of degree at most
k
.

Proposition 6.5
.

For
k\geqslant 0
there is an isomorphism of

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}
-comodules

\Cotor^{k,*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\xrightarrow{\cong}{\vphantom{\mathcal{A}}}^{\leqslant k}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)};\quad q_{0}^{r_{0}}q_{1}^{r_{1}}\cdots q_{\ell}^{r_{\ell}}\leftrightarrow\xi_{1}^{2r_{1}}\cdots\xi_{\ell}^{2r_{\ell}}.

The following result is an analogue of
Proposition
6.2
whose
proof can be adapted using the filtration
of
{\vphantom{\mathcal{A}}}{}^{\leqslant k}\mathcal{A}_{*}
based
on polynomial degree.

Proposition 6.6
.

For
k\geqslant 0
,

\Coext^{*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}_{*},{\vphantom{\mathcal{A}}}^{\leqslant k}\mathcal{A}_{*})=0.

We will also require some other vanishing results.

Proposition 6.7
.

For
n\geqslant 0
,

\Coext^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*})=0

and

\Coext^{*,*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*})=0.

Proof.

By setting
\mathcal{P}(\infty)^{(2)}_{*}=\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*}

we can present the proofs of these in a uniform
faahion.

There is a Cartan-Eilenberg spectral sequence

\mathrm{E}^{s,t}_{2}=\Coext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}))\Longrightarrow\Coext^{s+t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}).

Here

\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*})\cong\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\overset{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}{\wedge}\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}

and

\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}\cong\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}\square_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}}\mathbb{F}_{2}

so

\mathrm{E}^{s,t}_{2}\cong\Coext^{s}_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})).

Since
\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}
is a cofree

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}
-comodule,

\mathrm{E}^{s,t}_{2}=0
when
s>0
. Also the
change of coalgebra homomorphism

\Cohom_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{P}(n)^{(2)}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}))\to\Cohom_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\Cotor^{t}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}_{*}^{(1)}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}))

is injective. By Proposition
6.2
,
the codomain is trivial so
\mathrm{E}^{0,t}_{2}=0
.
∎

7.
Some topological applications

We illustrate our theory with a few calculations
of homotopy groups of spectra. Some of the
following examples where found in response
to questions of John Rognes. Results of this
type were proved by Lin, Margolis, Ravenel
and others. The interested reader will be
able to give others, especially at odd primes.

For simplicity we assume that all spectra
are
2
-completed. We are interested in
determining when
[X,Y]^{*}=0
for two
connective finite type spectra
X,Y
. Here
the Adams spectral sequence

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}(X,Y)=\Ext_{\mathcal{A}}^{s,t}(H^{*}(Y),H^{*}(X))=\Coext_{\mathcal{A}}^{s,t}(H_{*}(X),H_{*}(Y))\Longrightarrow[X,Y]^{s-t}

converges by work of Boardman
[
4
]
,
so we are interested in examples where

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{*,*}(X,Y)=0
. The general
result of Proposition
1.5

applies to many interesting examples.

•

For each the spectra
X
where
H=H\mathbb{F}_{2}
,

H\mathbb{Z}_{2^{s}}
(
s\geqslant 2
),
H\mathbb{Z}
,
{k\mathrm{O}}
,

{k\mathrm{U}}
,
\mathrm{tmf}
, and
BP\langle n\rangle

(
n\geqslant 1
),
[X,S]^{*}=0
since
H^{*}(X)

is a coherent
\mathcal{A}
-module and

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{*,*}(X,S)=0
.

•

For each of the above spectra
X
,

[X,BP]^{*}=0
. This follows since

H^{*}(BP)\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathcal{E}}\mathbb{F}_{2}

where
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is the
subHopf algebra generated by the
Milnor primitives and this is
also a
P
-algebra. So by
Proposition
1.6
,

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{*,*}(X,BP)\cong\Ext_{\mathcal{E}}^{*,*}(\mathbb{F}_{2},H^{*}(X))=0.

•

The case of
[BP,S]^{*}
involves more work.
We will use the dual version of the Adams
spectral sequence,

\mathrm{E}_{2}^{*,*}(BP,S)=\Coext_{\mathcal{A}_{*}}^{s,t}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\mathbb{F}_{2})\Longrightarrow[BP,S]^{s-t}.

We can calculate the
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-term
using the Cartan-Eilenberg spectral
sequence of (
6.1
),
and by Corollary
6.3

this
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-term

\Coext^{*}_{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*},\Cotor^{*}_{\mathcal{A}_{*}/\!/\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}))

is trivial. Therefore
\mathrm{E}_{2}^{*,*}(BP,S)=0

and
[BP,S]^{*}=0
.

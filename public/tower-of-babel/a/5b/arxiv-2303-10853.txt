On an exponential power sum
Neha Elizabeth Thomas, K Vishnu Namboothiri

Abstract
. Using combinatorial techniques, we derive a recurrence identity that expresses an exponential power sum with negative powers in terms of another exponential power sum with positive powers. Consequently, we derive a formula for the power sum of the first k k natural numbers when the power is odd, which when used in combination with Faulhaber’s formula for computing power sums helps us to retrieve the Bernoulli numbers in certain cases.

05A18, 11B37, 11B75, 11L03

1.
Introduction

A Dirichlet character
\chi
modulo
k
(where
k
is a positive integer) is defined to be
odd
if
\chi(-1)=-1
and
even
if
\chi(-1)=1
. The
Dirichlet
L
-function

L(s,\chi)
is defined by the infinite series
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}
, where
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
Re\,(s)>1
.
The
Gauss sum

G(z,\chi)
for any complex number
z
is defined as

\displaystyle G(z,\chi):=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\chi(m)e^{\frac{2\pi imz}{k}}.

E. Alkan established the following identity connecting
L(r,\chi)
with Gauss sums and Bernoulli numbers:

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{v+1}kr!}{i^{r}2^{r-1}\pi^{r}}L(r,\chi)=\sum\limits_{q=0}^{2[\frac{r}{2}]}\binom{r}{q}B_{q}S(r-q,\chi)\text{ \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{alkan2011values}{}{}, Theorem 1]}},

where

\displaystyle S(m,\chi):=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\frac{j}{k}\right)^{m}G(j,\chi).

and
B_{q}
are the
q
th
Bernoulli numbers. See
[
5
, Chapter 12]
for the definition and other properties of Bernoulli numbers and Bernoulli polynomials.

E. Alkan derived formulae in
[
1
]
for the sums
\sum\limits_{\chi\,\text{odd}}|L(1,\chi)|^{2}
and
\sum\limits_{\chi\,\text{even}}|L(2,\chi)|^{2}
using Identity (
1
).
His computations involved the simplification of sums of the form
\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}e^{\frac{-2\pi ims}{k}}
. He mainly tried to express such sums in terms of positive arguments of the exponential function. It seems that the complexity of simplifying these exponential power sums prevented him from establishing an exact formula for computing the value of
\sum\limits_{\chi,r\text{ with same parity}}|L(r,\chi)|^{2}
in general.

In this paper, we derive an identity that expresses the exponential power sum
\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}e^{\frac{-2\pi ims}{k}}
in terms of another exponential power sum with positive powers. Consequently, we derive a recursive formula for the power sum of the first
k
natural numbers
h(p,k):=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}s^{p}
when
p
is odd.

We note that Schaumberger and Faulhaber gave two different techniques for finding the power sums of natural numbers. In
[
9
]
, Schaumberger used the exponential generating function

\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\infty}h(p,k)\frac{x^{k}}{k!}=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{p}e^{rx}

and L’Hôspital’s rule for evaluating
h(p,k)
.

Faulhaber’s formula
[
7
, Identity 1]
is a non-recursive formula for finding
h(p,k)
. It involves the Bernoulli numbers
B_{s}
and is given by

(2)

\displaystyle h(p,k)=\frac{1}{(p+1)}\left(k^{p+1}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(-1)^{j}\binom{p+1}{j}B_{j}k^{p-j+1}\right).

In
[
10
]
, J. Singh defined the power sum
\psi_{p}(k)=a_{1}^{p}+a_{2}^{p}+\cdots+a_{\varphi(k)}^{p}
, where
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
\cdots
,
a_{\varphi(k)}
are positive integers each of which is less than or equal to
n
and relatively prime to
n
. There he gave a relation between
h(p,k)
and
\psi_{p}(k)
. In
[
2
]
, E. Alkan used the results from
[
10
]
to obtain a formula for the weighted average
\frac{1}{k^{r+1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}j^{r}c_{k}(j)
, where
c_{k}(j)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}{m=1}\\
(m,k)=1\end{subarray}}^{r}e^{\frac{2\pi imj}{k}}

is the Ramanujan sum. Later in
[
11
]
, J. Singh studied the sum of products of power sums via the multiple products of their exponential generating functions.

For non integral power sums, some approximate results only seem to exist in the literature. See, for example Theorem 1 in
[
8
]
.

2.
The main result and proof

We state and prove our main identity in this section. To begin with, we introduce the terms which we use in this paper. An
ordered partition
or
composition
of a positive integer
n
is an
m
-tuple
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
with
j_{1}+\ldots+j_{m}=n
, where
1\leq j_{l}\leq n
. The
j_{l}
are called
parts
of the composition and
m
, the number of parts in it, is called the
length
of the composition. We use
C(n)
to denote the set of all compositions of
n
. For
1\leq m\leq n
,
C(m,n)
will denote the set of all compositions of
n
with length
m
. The number of elements in
C(m,n)
is
\binom{n-1}{m-1}
(
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
). More properties and results related to compositions can be found, for example, in
[
4
, Chapter 4]
.

In this paper, we mainly derive the identity given in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
k,p
positive integers with
k\nmid m
and
p\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}e^{\frac{-2\pi ims}{k}}=-k^{p}+\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}k^{a}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p-a}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}.

We state and prove a lemma which is essential in the proof of the main proposition.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r\geq 1
. The set
\{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}):i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{r}\in\{1,\cdots,p-1\},i_{1}>i_{2}>\cdots>i_{r}\}
is in one to one correspondence with the set
C(r+1,p)
.

Proof.

Write
p-i_{1}=j_{1}
,
i_{1}-i_{2}=j_{2}
,
\cdots
,
i_{r-1}-i_{r}=j_{r},i_{r}-0=j_{r+1}
. Since
j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{r+1}=p
and
j_{l}
are all positive,
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{r+1})\in C(r+1,p)
. Let
(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r})
and
(i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,i_{r}^{\prime})
be distinct. Let
l
be the largest index such that
i_{l}\neq i_{l}^{\prime}
(with the convention that
i_{l+1}=0
if
l=r
). Thus
j_{l+1}=i_{l}-i_{l+1}
and
j_{l+1}^{\prime}=i_{l}^{\prime}-i_{l+1}^{\prime}
will be distinct. Hence
(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r+1})
and
(j_{1}^{\prime},j_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,j_{r+1}^{\prime})
will be distinct. On the other hand, if
(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r+1})\in C(r+1,p)
, let
i_{1}=p-j_{1},i_{2}=p-(j_{1}+j_{2}),\ldots
so that
i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
and they satisfy
i_{1}>\ldots>i_{r}
, with
r=0
giving the empty set. This shows that this correspondence is onto as well.
∎

Since
C(r+1,p)
has
\binom{p-1}{r}
elements, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

The set defined in the above proposition has
\binom{p-1}{r}
elements.

We state a result from
[
6
]
which we will be using in the proof of our main proposition.

Lemma 2.4
.

(
[
6
, Lemma 1]
)
If
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},\ldots
is any sequence of numbers, then the coefficient of
x^{n}
in
\left(1-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}u_{i}x^{i}\right)^{-1}
is
\sum\limits_{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in C(n)}u_{a_{1}}u_{a_{2}}\cdots u_{a_{k}}
.

Now we prove Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

We start the proof using the binomial expansion:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}(k-s)^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi im(k-s)}{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}(k-s)^{p}e^{-\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{p}s^{p}e^{-\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}+\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{a}\binom{p}{a}k^{p-a}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{a}e^{\frac{-2\pi ims}{k}}.

Hence

(3)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}e^{-\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}=(-1)^{p}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}+\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{p+a+1}\binom{p}{a}k^{p-a}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{a}e^{\frac{-2\pi ims}{k}}.

For notational convenience, we write
f(p)=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}e^{\frac{-2\pi ims}{k}}
and
g(p)=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}
. With this notation, we rewrite Equation (
3
) and expand it recursively as

\displaystyle f(p)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{p}g(p)+\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{p+a+1}k^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}f(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{p}g(p)+(-1)^{p+1}\binom{p}{p-1}kg(p-1)

\displaystyle+\left[(-1)^{p+1}\binom{p}{p-2}+(-1)^{p+2}\binom{p}{p-1}\binom{p-1}{p-2}\right]k^{2}g(p-2)

\displaystyle+\Bigg[(-1)^{p+1}\binom{p}{p-3}+(-1)^{p+2}\binom{p}{p-2}\binom{p-2}{p-3}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p+2}\binom{p}{p-1}\binom{p-1}{p-3}+(-1)^{p+3}\binom{p}{p-1}\binom{p-1}{p-2}\binom{p-2}{p-3}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\times k^{3}g(p-3)+\cdots.

It can be seen by induction that for
a\geq 2
,
g(p-a)
has coefficient as the sum

(4)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{r}\in\left\{p-a+1,\cdots,p-1\right\}\\
i_{1}>i_{2}>\cdots>i_{r}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p+r+1}\binom{p}{i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{2}}\ldots\binom{i_{r-1}}{i_{r}}\binom{i_{r}}{p-a}k^{a},

with the convention that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}
can also be the empty set to allow the binomial coefficient
\binom{p}{p-a}
.
Thus the coefficient of
k^{p}g(0)
is

(5)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{r}\in\left\{1,\cdots,p-1\right\}\\
i_{1}>i_{2}>\cdots>i_{r}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p+r+1}\binom{p}{i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{2}}\ldots\binom{i_{r-1}}{i_{r}}\binom{i_{r}}{0}.

Now

\displaystyle\binom{p}{i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{2}}\ldots\binom{i_{r-1}}{i_{r}}\binom{i_{r}}{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{p!}{(p-i_{1})!i_{1}!}\frac{i_{1}!}{(i_{1}-i_{2})!i_{2}!}\cdots\frac{i_{r-1}!}{(i_{r-1}-i_{r})!i_{r}!}\frac{i_{r}!}{(i_{r}-0)!0!}

\displaystyle=\frac{p!}{(p-i_{1})!(i_{1}-i_{2})!\cdots(i_{r}-0)!0!}.

Now we use Lemma
2.2
. The binomial product sum in Equation (
5
) consists of the summand
(-1)^{p+1}\binom{p}{0}
. This term corresponds to the empty subset of
\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
. Corresponding to this empty set we may take the single available element
(p)
from
C(1,p)
.
Hence,
\binom{p}{i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{2}}\cdots\binom{i_{r-1}}{i_{r}}\binom{i_{r}}{0}
and
\binom{p}{0}
can be replaced with the multinomial coefficient
\binom{p}{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{r+1}}
and so the coefficient of
k^{p}g(0)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{r=0}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r+1})\in C(r+1,p)\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle(-1)^{p+r+1}\binom{p}{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{r+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r=1}^{p}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r})\in C(r,p)\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p+r}\binom{p}{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{r}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}p!\sum\limits_{r=1}^{p}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r})\in C(r,p)\end{subarray}}(-1)^{r}\frac{1}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\cdots j_{r}!}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}p!\times\text{ coefficient of }x^{p}\text{ in }\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\left(\text{obtained by taking }u_{i}=\dfrac{-1}{i!}\text{ in Lemma \ref{gessellemma}}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}p!\times\text{ coefficient of }x^{p}\text{ in }\left(e^{x}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=1.

Now we compute the coefficient of
k^{a}g(p-a)
. We take the product of binomial coefficients appearing in Equation (
4
) and simplify it further:

\displaystyle\binom{p}{i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{2}}\ldots\binom{i_{r-1}}{i_{r}}\binom{i_{r}}{p-a}

\displaystyle=\binom{p}{p-i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{1}-i_{2}}\ldots\binom{i_{r-1}}{i_{r-1}-i_{r}}\binom{i_{r}}{i_{r}-p+a}

\displaystyle=\frac{p!}{(p-i_{1})!(i_{1}-i_{2})!\cdots(i_{r}-p+a)!(p-a)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{a!}{(p-i_{1})!(i_{1}-i_{2})!\cdots(i_{r}-p+a)!}\binom{p}{a}.

Write
p-i_{1}=j_{1}
,
i_{1}-i_{2}=j_{2}
,
\cdots,i_{r-1}-i_{r}=j_{r},i_{r}-(p-a)=j_{r+1}
. So
j_{1}+j_{2}+\cdots+j_{r+1}=a
. As in the above case, we can see that
(j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{r+1})
is in a one to one correspondence with
(i_{1}
,
i_{2}
,
\cdots
,
i_{r}
), where
i_{1}>\ldots>i_{r}
. So we have

\binom{p}{i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}}{i_{2}}\cdots\binom{i_{r-1}}{i_{r}}\binom{i_{r}}{p-a}=\binom{p}{a}\binom{a}{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{r+1}}.

Hence we get the coefficient of
k^{a}g(p-a)
as

\displaystyle(-1)^{p-a}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{r=0}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r+1})\in C(a,r+1)\end{subarray}}(-1)^{a+r+1}\binom{p}{a}\binom{a}{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{r+1}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{p-1}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r+1})\in C(a,r+1)\end{subarray}}(-1)^{a+r+1}\binom{a}{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{r+1}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}.

Thus

\displaystyle f(p)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}g(p)+(-1)^{p-1}\binom{p}{p-1}kg(p-1)+\sum\limits_{a=2}^{p}(-1)^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}k^{a}g(p-a)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}k^{a}g(p-a)+k^{p}g(0)

\displaystyle=-k^{p}+\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}k^{a}g(p-a)

which is what we claimed. In the last step above, we used the fact that
g(0)=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}=-1
when
e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}\neq 1
.
∎

Let us see an application of the computations above. When
k|m
,
e^{\frac{2\pi ims}{k}}=1
and
g(0)=k-1
in the above so that we get

(6)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p}=k^{p}(k-1)+\sum\limits_{a=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}k^{a}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k-1}s^{p-a}.

Let
h(p,k)=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}s^{p}
. If
p
is odd, from the above, we get a recursive formula for the sum of
p
th
powers of the first
k
natural numbers as

\displaystyle h(p,k)=\frac{1}{2}\left((k+1)^{p}k+\sum\limits_{a=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{p-a}\binom{p}{a}(k+1)^{a}h(p-a,k)\right).

On replacing
p-a
with
j
, we get the following.

Corollary 2.5
.

For
p,k
positive integers with
p
odd, we have

(7)

\displaystyle h(p,k)=\frac{1}{2}\left((k+1)^{p}k+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{j}\binom{p}{j}(k+1)^{p-j}h(j,k)\right).

Such a formula cannot be derived when
p
is even because of the cancellation of
h(p,k)
taking place from both sides of Identity (
6
).

To illustrate the use of the above identity, let us compute
\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}s^{3}
. Note that the knowledge of
h(1,k)=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}s
and
h(2,k)=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}s^{2}
is necessary for this computation. We have
h(1,k)=\frac{k(k+1)}{2}
and
h(2,k)=\frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6}
.
Thus

\displaystyle h(3,k)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((k+1)^{3}k+\sum\limits_{a=1}^{2}(-1)^{3-a}\binom{3}{a}(k+1)^{a}h(3-a,k)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((k+1)^{3}k+(-1)^{2}\binom{3}{1}(k+1)h(2,k)+(-1)^{1}\binom{3}{2}(k+1)^{2}h(1,k)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((k+1)^{3}k+3(k+1)\frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6}-3(k+1)^{2}\frac{k(k+1)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(k(k+1)^{2}(2k+1)-(k+1)^{3}k\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{k(k+1)}{2}\right]^{2}.

We cannot compute
h(4,k)
using Identity (
7
). But if we use the known formula
h(4,k)=\frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)(3k^{2}+3k-1)}{30}
, we get
h(5,k)
as
\frac{k^{2}(k+1)^{2}(2k^{2}+2k-1)}{12}
if we proceed as in the above computation for
h(3,k)
.

Another application of Identity (
7
) is that we can retrieve the Bernoulli numbers using Identities (
2
) and (
7
). For example, if we put
p=1
in the right-hand side of both the Equations (
2
) and (
7
) and equate them, we get

\displaystyle\frac{k(k+1)}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(k^{2}+(-1)\binom{2}{1}B_{1}k\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(k^{2}-2B_{1}k\right).

On comparing the coefficients of
k
on both sides, we get
B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
.

Similarly, if we put
p=3
in the right-hand side of both identities (
2
) and (
7
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{k^{4}}{4}-B_{1}k^{3}+\frac{3}{2}B_{2}k^{2}-4B_{3}k=

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)^{3}k}{2}-\frac{3}{2}(k+1)^{2}\frac{k(k+1)}{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{2}(k+1)\frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6}.

By comparing the coefficients of
k^{2}
on both sides in the above, we get
B_{2}=\frac{1}{6}
.
Proceeding like this we can find the values of
B_{4}
,
B_{6}
,
\cdots
. Note that
B_{2n-1}=0
for all positive integers
n
except when
n=1
.

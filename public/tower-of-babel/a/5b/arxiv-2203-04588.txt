Unsupervised Domain Adaptation across fmcw Radar Configurations Using Margin Disparity Discrepancy
Rodrigo Hernangómez 1 , Igor Bjelaković 1 3 , Lorenzo Servadei 2 4 , and Sławomir Stańczak 1 3

Abstract
Commercial radar sensing is gaining relevance and ml algorithms constitute one of the key components that are enabling the spread of this radio technology into areas like surveillance or healthcare. However, radar datasets are still scarce and generalization cannot be yet achieved for all radar systems, environment conditions or design parameters. A certain degree of fine tuning is, therefore, usually required to deploy machine-learning-enabled radar applications. In this work, we consider the problem of unsupervised domain adaptation across radar configurations in the context of deep-learning human activity classification using fmcw . For that, we focus on the theory-inspired technique of mdd , which has already been proved successful in the area of computer vision. Our experiments extend this technique to radar data, achieving a comparable accuracy to few-shot supervised approaches for the same classification problem.

Introduction

Radar, a well-established technology for several industrial
areas, has recently gained
attention for other commercial applications
like human monitoring,
presence detection or
gesture sensing
[
1
,
2
,
3
]

due to the production of
small and compact radar sensors
[
4
]
.
Here radar offers
some advantages in comparison with
computer vision approaches, such as
good performance under poor-lighting conditions
or privacy protection (due to the difficulty to
identify individuals from radar images).

Similarly as in the realm of computer vision,
the use of radar
often comes hand in hand with

ml
(
ml
) techniques,
including deep learning,
to overcome the burden of handcrafted
feature engineering
[
5
]
.
Due to the variety
of system design parameters at hand,
such as modulation techniques or bandwidth,
these
ml
algorithms are required to generalize
well under different radar setups.
This need for inter-domain generalization
is common to several
ml
problems and
it has been studied in recent years under
the paradigm of

da
(
da
)
[
6
,
7
]
.

Considered a special case of transfer learning,

da

involves modifying
an
ml
estimator that
can be trained with enough data
from a
source domain
,
so that its performance
increases when evaluated
with data
originated from a different
target domain
.
Unlike other transfer learning approaches,
here
the mismatch between source and target domain
lies merely in a distinct probability measure
over data rather than in different input or
output spaces
[
7
]
.

The reasons for
da
are usually related with insufficient
or incomplete data in the target domain, which
can be overcome with the help of data from
the source domain. In

ml
classification,
the missing information
is often the labels;
this case is referred to as
unsupervised

da
.
If target data are labeled, we can apply

supervised

da
techniques
instead
[
6
]
.

Both supervised and unsupervised
da
methods have already been
investigated in the Radar-
ml
community
to overcome several problems,
including individual patient differences
[
8
]
,
aspect angle variations
[
9
]
,
synthetic-to-real adaptation
[
10
]

or environmental differences
[
11
]
.
In the case of cross-configuration adaptation,

Khodabakhshandeh et al. 2021
use supervised techniques
such as
fada
(
fada
)
[
13
]

or
dsne
(
dsne
)
[
14
]
to
adapt their trained human activity classifier to new

fmcw
(
fmcw
) radar setups using few data.

In this paper, we build on the work in
[
12
]

by applying
mdd
(

)
[

]
.
In that way, we confirm that
this unsupervised technique, which delivers

II
Problem Statement

Radar-
ml
classification
deals with the evaluation of a group of
radar features
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}
obtained from a target
to find the underlying class
y\in\mathcal{Y}
that best
describes some property of the said target.
The input space

\mathcal{X}\subset\mathds{R}^{m}

is characterized by a dimension

m
that depends on the radar technology and preprocessing
steps, while the label space is defined as

\mathcal{Y}=\left\{1,\ldots,k\right\}
,
with
k
being the number of classes.

In order to achieve this classification, one has first
to find a classifier
h
that maps
\boldsymbol{x}

into
y
.
The
ml
approach assumes a sufficiently
large amount
of data available conveying information both about the inputs
and the class so that it can be used to train

h
among a restricted hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}
.
This dataset consists of a sequence of pairs of
features and labels, i.e.

\left\{\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{i},y_{i}\right)\right\}^{n}_{i=1}
,
that have been previously sampled
from a certain domain
\mathcal{D}
,
defined to be

\mathcal{D}=\left(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},p_{\mathcal{D}}\right),

(1)

with an associated probability measure

p_{\mathcal{D}}
over

\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
.
Here and hereafter, we write
\mathbf{x}

and
\mathrm{y}
in their upright form whenever
we refer to the random variables related to
p_{\mathcal{D}}
,
and not to its realizations.

By choosing an objective loss function

\ell:\;\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathds{R}_{0+}

(2)

and minimizing it over the hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}

with a suitable optimization method,
we can train an
h
that performs well for the
available data.
The performance of
h

can thus be measured by the
risk
associated with the loss for
a domain
\mathcal{D}
. This risk
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}}

represents the expected
value of the loss of
h
for
p_{\mathcal{D}}
:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}}\left(h\right)=\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\ell\left(h,\left(\mathbf{x},\mathrm{y}\right)\right).

(3)

By assuming the indicator function

\mathds{1}_{h\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\neq y}

to be the loss, we obtain the
0-1 error

\textrm{err}_{\mathcal{D}}\left(h\right)\triangleq\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathds{1}_{h\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\neq\mathrm{y}}
.
In practice, we do not have access to
p_{\mathcal{D}}
,
so we resort to its empirical approximation

\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}}\left(h\right)\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\ell\left(h,\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{i},y_{i}\right)\right)}/n

for a dataset
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}

with
n
samples drawn from
\mathcal{D}
.

If generalization is achieved,

h
will also behave well for
unseen data as long as it is drawn from the same domain.
Unfortunately, this assumption cannot always be guaranteed.
It is often the case that training data have been drawn from
a
source domain

\mathcal{S}
but we would like to leverage
the trained classifier for a different
target domain

\mathcal{T}
.
Depending on how dissimilar
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are,
the performance of the trained classifier can degrade
significantly.
In our specific problem, this
domain shift

is given by the choice of different

fmcw
settings
and presents an additional challenge
in the lack of the labels
for the training data from
\mathcal{T}
.
The absence of
labeled target data
makes it necessary to apply

unsupervised

da
.
In this paper, we explore
this possibility by using

mdd
(
mdd
)
[
15
]
.

II-A

mdd

In order to use
mdd
,
we assume a
hypothesis class induced by a space
\mathcal{F}

of
scoring functions

f:\>\mathcal{X}\mapsto\mathds{R}^{k}
.
We also introduce the shorthand
f_{y}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)

to refer to the
y
-th component of
f\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)
.
The hypothesis class is given by

\mathcal{H}\triangleq\left\{h_{f}:\>\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\argmax_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}{f_{y}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)}\mid f\in\mathcal{F}\right\}\,.

(4)

mdd
has been developed by

Zhang et al. 2019

as a practical algorithm
based on the concept of discrepancy distance by

Mansour et al. 2009
.
For that,
they define the
margin loss

\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(f\right)
as

\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(f\right)\triangleq\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{D}}{\Phi^{\left(\rho\right)}\circ\phi_{f}\left(\mathbf{x},\mathrm{y}\right)}\,,

(5)

\phi_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{y}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)-\max_{y^{\prime}\neq y}{f_{y^{\prime}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)}\right)\,,

(6)

\Phi^{\left(\rho\right)}\left(x\right)\triangleq\begin{cases}0&\rho\leq x\\
1-x/\rho&0\leq x\leq\rho\\
1&x\leq 0\end{cases}\,,

(7)

and the true and empirical

margin disparity

between two scoring functions
f^{\prime}
and
f
as

\textrm{disp}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{{\mathcal{D}}}\left(f^{\prime},f\right)\triangleq\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{D}}{\Phi^{\left(\rho\right)}\circ\phi_{f^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{x},h_{f}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\right)}\,,

(8)

\textrm{disp}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\widehat{\mathcal{D}}}\left(f^{\prime},f\right)\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\Phi^{\left(\rho\right)}\circ\phi_{f^{\prime}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{i},h_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\right)\right)}\,,

(9)

to finally formulate the following minimax
optimization problem:

\begin{gathered}\min_{f\in\mathcal{F}}{\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}\left(f\right)+d^{\left(\rho\right)}_{f,\mathcal{F}}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{S}},\widehat{\mathcal{T}}\right)},\\
d^{\left(\rho\right)}_{f,\mathcal{F}}\left({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{T}}\right)\triangleq\sup_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}{\left(\textrm{disp}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{{\mathcal{T}}}\left(f^{\prime},f\right)-\textrm{disp}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{{\mathcal{S}}}\left(f^{\prime},f\right)\right)}\,.\end{gathered}

(10)

Following the principles
of unsupervised

da
,
the
mdd
term

d^{\left(\rho\right)}_{f,\mathcal{F}}

does not make use of
any labels
y_{i}
.
Furthermore, the solution to (
10
)
minimizes the 0-1 error of

h_{f}
in the target domain,
as
the authors of
[
15
]

prove with the following theoretical bound:

\textrm{err}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(h_{f}\right)\leq\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(f\right)+d^{\left(\rho\right)}_{f,\mathcal{F}}\left({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{T}}\right)+\lambda\,,

(11)

where
\lambda
is the ideal combined
margin loss:

\lambda=\min_{f^{*}\in\mathcal{F}}{\left\{\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(f^{*}\right)+\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\mathcal{T}}\left(f^{*}\right)\right\}}\,.

(12)

The bound in (
11
) can also be expressed in terms
of empirical measures rather than true probability measures
by the addition of
Rademacher complexity terms
[
17
, Chapter 3]
.

Despite the interesting properties
of
mdd
,

\Phi^{\left(\rho\right)}\circ\phi_{f}
is non-smooth, non-convex
and its training causes vanishing and exploding gradients, which leads
Zhang et al. 2019

to work with
the cross-entropy loss instead.
For this, they map

f\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)
to the

k
-simplex via the softmax function

\sigma
, as it is customary in deep learning,
where the elements of
\sigma\left(\boldsymbol{z}\right)

are given by

\sigma_{j}\left(\boldsymbol{z}\right)\triangleq\frac{\exp{z_{j}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\exp{z_{i}}}},\;\;\text{for }j=1,\ldots,k\,.

(13)

The composition
of the cross-entropy loss with the softmax yields the

lse
(
lse
)
loss
\mathsterling_{f}
:

\mathsterling_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\triangleq H\left(\mathds{1}_{j=y},\sigma\left(f\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)\right)=-\log{\sigma_{y}\left(f\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)}\\
=\log{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}{\exp{\left(f_{y^{\prime}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)-f_{y}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)}}}\,.

(14)

Zhang et al. 2019
propose to use

\mathsterling_{f}
instead of

\Phi^{\left(\rho\right)}\circ\phi_{f}

for

\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}

and

\textrm{disp}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}

in (
10
).
As for

\textrm{disp}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\widehat{\mathcal{T}}}
,
they use the adversarial loss

\widetilde{\mathsterling}_{f}
proposed by

Goodfellow et al. 2014
, i.e.

\widetilde{\mathsterling}_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\triangleq\log{\left(1-\sigma_{y}\left(f\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)\right)}\,,

(15)

so that their
mdd
ultimately becomes

\widetilde{d}^{\left(\gamma\right)}_{f,\psi,\mathcal{F}}\left({\widehat{\mathcal{S}}},{\widehat{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\triangleq\max_{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathds{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}^{t}\sim\widehat{\mathcal{T}}}{\widetilde{\mathsterling}_{f^{\prime}}\left(\psi\left(\boldsymbol{x}^{t}\right),h_{f}\left(\psi\left(\boldsymbol{x}^{t}\right)\right)\right)}\\
-\gamma\mathds{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}^{s}\sim\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathsterling_{f^{\prime}}\left(\psi\left(\boldsymbol{x}^{s}\right),h_{f}\left(\psi\left(\boldsymbol{x}^{s}\right)\right)\right)}

(16)

for a
margin factor

\gamma>0

and a feature extractor
\psi
that levels
the min-player to the max-player
[
15
]

(A concrete example of
\psi
is given
in (
27
),
Section

III
).
The authors explain
that this is equivalent to
the use of the margin loss with a margin

\rho=\log{\gamma}

and that the problem is still
solved for
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}

[
15
]
.

In addition to the results
in
[
15
]
,
we observe that the use
of the recently proposed soft-margin softmax

[
19
]
instead of

\sigma

in (
14
) provides
an upper bound for

\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\mathcal{S}}
.
The entries of the soft-margin softmax

\sigma^{\left(\rho\right)}\left(\boldsymbol{z}\right)

are defined as

\sigma^{\left(\rho\right)}_{j}\left(\boldsymbol{z}\right)\triangleq\frac{\exp{\left(z_{j}-\rho\right)}}{\exp{\left(z_{j}-\rho\right)}+\sum_{i\neq j}{\exp{z_{i}}}},\\
\text{for }j=1,\ldots,k;\;\;\rho\in\mathds{R}_{+}

(17)

and this induces the soft-margin cross-entropy loss
\mathsterling_{f}^{\left(\rho\right)}
:

\mathsterling_{f}^{\left(\rho\right)}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\triangleq-\log{\sigma^{\left(\rho\right)}_{y}\left(f\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)}\\
=\log{\sum_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}{\exp{\left(f_{y^{\prime}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)-f_{y}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)+\rho\cdot\mathds{1}_{y^{\prime}\neq y}\right)}}}\,.

(18)

Likewise, a soft-max adversarial loss can also
be defined as

\widetilde{\mathsterling}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\triangleq\log{\left(1-\sigma^{\left(\rho\right)}_{y}\left(f\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)\right)\right)}\,.

(19)

We prove this soft-margin-based
bound with the help of the following Lemma,
which motivates us to
investigate

\mathsterling^{\left(\rho\right)}_{f}

further in
Section

III
.

Lemma 1
.

The soft-max cross entropy bounds the margin loss as

\Phi^{\left(\rho\right)}\circ\phi_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\rho}\mathsterling^{\left(2\rho\right)}_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\,.

(20)

Proof.

First, let us recall the generalized hinge
loss
[
20
]
:

\hslash_{f}^{\left(\theta\right)}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\triangleq\max_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}{\left(f_{y^{\prime}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)-f_{y}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)+\theta\cdot\mathds{1}_{y^{\prime}\neq y}\right)}\,.

(21)

Noting that the term within
\max_{y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Y}}

is null
\forall y^{\prime}=y
, we have

\hslash_{f}^{\left(2\rho\right)}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)=\max\left\{0,\max_{y^{\prime}\neq y}{f_{y^{\prime}}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)-f_{y}\left(\boldsymbol{x}\right)+2\rho}\right\}=\\
\max\left\{0,2\rho-2\phi_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\right\}=2\rho\max\left\{0,1-\phi_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)/\rho\right\}\,.

(22)

The last expression in (
22
)
can be derived from (
7
)
if we set
1-x/\rho
instead of 1
for
x\leq 0
, hence

\Phi^{\left(\rho\right)}\circ\phi_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\rho}\hslash^{\left(2\rho\right)}_{f}\left(\boldsymbol{x},y\right)

(23)

and the proof is concluded using the fact that

\log{\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}{\exp{a}}}\geq\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}{{a}}

(24)

for any finite set

\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Taking the expectation w.r.t.

p_{\mathcal{S}}
in (
20
),
we finally obtain

\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(f\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\rho}\mathds{E}_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathsterling^{\left(2\rho\right)}_{f}\left(\mathbf{x},\mathrm{y}\right)}\,.

(25)

Despite the gap introduced by
Eqs.

23
and
24
, we note that
Eq.

25
delivers convincing bounds
for a small
\textrm{err}^{\left(\rho\right)}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(f\right)
,
which can be achieved by training

f
under enough samples from
\mathcal{S}
.

IV
Conclusion

In this work, we confirm that
the
mdd
algorithm,
which has already shown promising results
for unsupervised
da
in
the area of computer vision, is also suitable
for radar data across different
fmcw
parameters.
The obtained accuracy can become as high
as for some supervised techniques
[
12
]

while using a much more limited dataset, paving thus the way for a prompt deployment of
radar-based deep learning applications with
custom configurations.

In our experiments,
we observe that
the use of the soft-margin cross entropy loss provides similar results as the original implementation by

Zhang et al. 2019
.
Since the motivation of
mdd
is
to bring the algorithms closer to the analytical
performance bounds of
da
, we see
potential in this alternative loss function
to bridge the gap between theory and practice.

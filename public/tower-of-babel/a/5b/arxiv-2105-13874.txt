Abstract
. The finite dual H ∘ H^{\circ} of an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra H H is studied. Such a Hopf algebra H H is an extension of an affine commutative Hopf algebra A A by a finite dimensional Hopf algebra H ¯ \overline{H} . The main theorem gives natural conditions under which H ∘ H^{\circ} decomposes as a crossed or smash product of H ¯ ∗ \overline{H}^{\ast} by the finite dual A ∘ A^{\circ} of A A . This decomposition is then further analysed using the Cartier-Gabriel-Kostant theorem to obtain component Hopf subalgebras of H ∘ H^{\circ} mapping onto the classical components of A ∘ A^{\circ} . The detailed consequences for a number of families of examples are then studied.

1.
Introduction

1.1.

This is the second in a series of papers about affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebras and their duals, the first in the series being
[
4
]
. In that paper we defined a Hopf algebra
H
over the algebraically closed field
k
to be
affine commutative-by-finite
if
H
is a finitely generated module over a normal commutative finitely generated Hopf subalgebra
A
. (To say that

is

, recalled at the start of

denote the augmentation ideal of

ensures that
A^{+}H
is a Hopf ideal of

, so that

is a finite dimensional quotient Hopf algebra of
H
, and it is natural to view

as an extension of

by
\overline{H}
.

Our aim in this paper is to obtain, for an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra

, a description of the finite dual
H^{\circ}
of

which generalises the classical description valid when
H=A
given by the Cartier-Gabriel-Kostant structure theorem
[
26
, Corollary 5.6.4, Theorem 5.6.5]
. Recall that in that case, assuming that

has characteristic 0,

is a smash product

, where

is the algebra of polynomial functions

of the affine algebraic group

,

is the enveloping algebra of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and

is the group algebra of

. The description we obtain combines (generalisations of) these ingredients with the dual
\overline{H}^{\ast}
of
\overline{H}
. It comes closest to replicating the classical cocommutative picture when

satisfies two additional hypotheses. Currently we know of no examples where these hypotheses fail to hold. We briefly explain the additonal hypotheses in
\lx@sectionsign
1.2

\lx@sectionsign

.

1.2.
Coideal splitting and orbital semisimplicity

Let
H
and its Hopf subalgebra
A
satisfy the hypotheses of
\lx@sectionsign
1.1
, and assume that
A
is semiprime or central, or that
H
is pointed. It is known, as we’ll recall in Theorem
2.2
, that

is a direct summand of

as right
A
-modules, say

(1.1)

H\;=\;A\oplus X.

Our first additional hypothesis is:

\textbf{(CoSplit)}\quad X\textit{ in (\ref{split}) can be chosen to be a coideal of }H.

To explain the second hypothesis, note first that the left adjoint action of
H
on
A
factors through
\overline{H}
, so that for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
A
we can define
\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}
to be the unique largest
\overline{H}
-invariant ideal of

contained in
\mathfrak{m}
. By work of Skryabin
[
33

, which we recall in
\lx@sectionsign

is a finite dimensional algebra. The second hypothesis is:

\textbf{(OrbSemi)}\quad\textit{For every }\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Maxspec}A,\;A/\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}\,\textit{is semisimple.}

1.3.
Main theorem

Here is the main result stated here in the simplest case, namely when
k
has characteristic 0; the full result is Theorem
6.1

is used to denote the smash product of the
k
-algebra

[

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
have characteristic 0 and let
H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf
k
-algebra, finite over the normal commutative Hopf subalgebra
A
. (So
A=\mathcal{O}(G_{H})
for an affine algebraic
k
-group
G_{H}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.) Assume that
A\subseteq H
satisfies
\mathrm{\mathbf{(CoSplit)}}
and
\mathrm{\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}}
. Denote the finite dual of
H
by
H^{\circ}
and recall that
\overline{H}
denotes the finite dimensional Hopf algebra
H/A^{+}H
.

(1)

As a vector space,
H^{\circ}
decomposes as
U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes kG_{H}
; as an algebra it decomposes as a smash product,

(1.2)

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\,A^{\circ}\;\cong\;(\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\,U(\mathfrak{g}))\#kG_{H}.

(2)

H^{\circ}
contains three distinguished Hopf subalgebras, namely
\overline{H}^{\ast}
, a differential smash product

(1.3)

W(H^{\circ})\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\,U(\mathfrak{g}),

and a skew group algebra

(1.4)

\widehat{kG_{H}}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\,kG_{H}.

(3)

Suppose in addition that
\overline{H}
is semisimple. Then the action of
U(\mathfrak{g})
on
\overline{H}^{\ast}
is inner, so that

(1.5)

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;(\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes U(\mathfrak{g}))\#kG_{H},

a skew group algebra with coefficient ring the Hopf subalgebra
W(H^{\circ})
.

We currently know no counterexample to the possibility that, given any affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra
H
, there is a choice of normal commutative Hopf subalgebra

and
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}

2.
Commutative-by-finite Hopf algebras

In this section we briefly recall the most basic properties of affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebras. At the same time notation will be fixed which will remain in force throughout the paper. Full details of the results stated here can be found in
[
4

2.1.
Definition and basic properties

Recall
[
26
, §3.4]
that a subalgebra
K
of a Hopf algebra
H
is
normal
if it is invariant under the left and right adjoint actions of
H
; that is, for all
k\in K
and
h\in H
,

(\mathrm{ad}_{\ell}h)(k)=\sum h_{1}kS(h_{2})\in K\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\mathrm{ad}_{r}h)(k)=\sum S(h_{1})kh_{2}\in K.

The following class of Hopf algebras will be the focus of this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

A Hopf
k
-algebra
H
is
affine commutative-by-finite
if it is a finite (left or right) module over a commutative normal Hopf subalgebra
A
which is finitely generated as a
k
-algebra.

For full proofs and references for the following see
[
4
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra, finite over the normal commutative Hopf subalgebra
A
with augmentation ideal
A^{+}
.

(1)

A
and
H
are Noetherian
k
-algebras.

(2)

A^{+}H=HA^{+}
is a Hopf ideal of
H
, and

\overline{H}\;:=\;H/A^{+}H

is a finite dimensional quotient Hopf algebra of
H
. We denote the Hopf algebra surjection by
\pi:H\longrightarrow\overline{H}
.

(3)

The left adjoint action of
H
on
A
factors through
\overline{H}
, so that
A
is a left
\overline{H}
-module algebra.

(4)

The antipode
S
of
H
is bijective.

(5)

H
is a finite module over its centre
Z(H)
, which is affine.

(6)

\GKdim H=\GKdim A<\infty.

Assume for (7)-(10) that
A
is semiprime or central, or that
H
is pointed.

(7)

H
is a finitely generated projective generator as left and right
A
-module.

(8)

A
is a left (resp. right)
A
-module direct summand of
H
.

(9)

A\subseteq H
is a faithfully flat
\overline{H}
-Galois extension.

(10)

A
equals the right and the left
\overline{H}
-coinvariants of the
\overline{H}
-comodule
H
; that is,

H^{{\rm co\,}\pi}\;=\;\,^{{\rm co\,}\pi}H\;=\;A.

Remarks 2.3
.

Keep the notation and hypotheses of Theorem

(1)
(Radford

(2)

(3)
Since Theorem

(4)
In seeking to apply Theorem

3.
The finite dual

3.1.
Commutative recap

When
A
is a commutative affine Hopf
k
-algebra the structure of
A^{\circ}
is well understood - it is cocommutative and decomposes as a smash product

(3.6)

A^{\circ}\;\cong\;A^{\prime}\#kG,

by the classical structure theorem for cocommutative pointed Hopf algebras, due to Cartier-Gabriel-Kostant
[
26
, Theorem 5.6.4(3)]
. Here,
A^{\prime}
is a connected Hopf subalgebra of
A
, and, as discussed in
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
,
G=G(A^{\circ})
is the group of characters of
A
. Moreover, when
k
has characteristic 0,
A^{\prime}
is the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
,
[
26
, Theorem 5.6.5]
. That is,
\mathfrak{g}=(A^{+}/{A^{+}}^{2})^{\ast}
, consisting of the primitive elements of
A^{\circ}
, the functionals vanishing at 1 and
{A^{+}}^{2}
. Thus, by
[
26
, Proposition 9.2.5]
,

(3.7)

A^{\prime}=\{f\in A^{\circ}:f((A^{+})^{n})=0,\text{ for some }n>0\}=U(\mathfrak{g}),

where the first equality holds in general and the second when
k
has characteristic 0.
Similarly, as can easily be checked, with no condition on the characteristic of
k
,

(3.8)

kG=\{f\in A^{\circ}:f(\mathfrak{m}_{1}\cap\ldots\cap\mathfrak{m}_{r})=0,r\geq 1,\mathfrak{m}_{i}\in\maxspec(A)\},

the functionals vanishing on some finite intersection of maximal ideals of
A
.

3.2.
Subspaces and quotient spaces of
H^{\circ}

The following lemma gathers together some basic facts concerning the components
A^{\circ}
and
\overline{H}^{\ast}
of
H^{\circ}
. Fix throughout the rest of the paper the Hopf embedding and the Hopf surjection

\iota:A\hookrightarrow H\;\textit{ and }\;\pi:H\longrightarrow\overline{H},

and the projection of right

-modules along

,

(3.9)

\Pi:H=A\oplus X\twoheadrightarrow A:h=a+x\mapsto a,

afforded by Theorem

is semiprime or central, or

is pointed.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra and assume that
A
semiprime or central, or
H
is pointed.

(1)

There is an embedding of Hopf algebras

\pi^{\circ}:\overline{H}^{\ast}\hookrightarrow H^{\circ}:\beta\mapsto\beta\circ\pi.

(2)

\pi^{\circ}(\overline{H}^{\ast})
, which we identify with
\overline{H}^{\ast}
, is a normal Hopf subalgebra of
H^{\circ}
.

(3)

H^{\circ}
is a free (right and left)
\overline{H}^{\ast}
-module.

(4)

There is a map of right
A^{\circ}
-comodules

\Pi^{\circ}:A^{\circ}\longrightarrow H^{\circ}:\alpha\mapsto\alpha\circ\Pi.

(5)

There is a Hopf algebra map

\iota^{\circ}:H^{\circ}\longrightarrow A^{\circ}:f\mapsto f\circ\iota,

with
\iota^{\circ}\circ\Pi^{\circ}={\mathrm{id}}_{A^{\circ}}.
Thus
\Pi^{\circ}
is injective and
\iota^{\circ}
is surjective.

(6)

Via
\iota^{\circ}
,
H^{\circ}
is canonically a right and left
A^{\circ}
-comodule algebra, with coinvariants

(H^{\circ})^{co\,\iota^{\circ}}={{}^{co\,\iota^{\circ}}(H^{\circ})}=\overline{H}^{*}.

(7)

The Hopf ideal
(\overline{H}^{\ast})^{+}H^{\circ}
of

is contained in
{\rm ker\,}\iota^{\circ}
, so that

(3.10)

(\overline{H}^{\ast})^{+}H^{\circ}\oplus\Pi^{\circ}(A^{\circ})\subseteq{\rm ker\,}\iota^{\circ}\oplus\Pi^{\circ}(A^{\circ})=H^{\circ}.

Proof.

(1),(2) Define
T:=\{f\in H^{\circ}:f(A^{+}H)=0\}
. Since
A^{+}H
is a Hopf ideal of
H
, it is clear that
T
is a Hopf subalgebra of
H^{\circ}
which is isomorphic as a Hopf algebra to
\overline{H}^{*}
, via the map
\pi^{\circ}:\overline{H}^{\ast}\longrightarrow H^{\circ}:\beta\mapsto\beta\circ\pi
.

Since
HA^{+}=A^{+}H
by Theorem
2.2
(2), it is very easy to check that

(3.11)

T=\{f\in H^{\circ}:f(ah)=\epsilon(a)f(h)=f(ha),\forall a\in A,\forall h\in H\}.

To deduce normality of

, let
f\in\overline{H}^{*}
and
\varphi\in H^{\circ}
. For any

\displaystyle(\mathrm{ad}_{r}\varphi)(f)(ah)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\sum S^{\circ}(\varphi_{1})f\varphi_{2}\right](ah)=\sum S^{\circ}(\varphi_{1})(a_{1}h_{1})f(a_{2}h_{2})\varphi_{2}(a_{3}h_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\varphi_{1}(S(h_{1})S(a_{1}))\epsilon(a_{2})f(h_{2})\varphi_{2}(a_{3}h_{3})=\sum\varphi(S(h_{1})S(a_{1})a_{2}h_{3})f(h_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon(a)\sum\varphi(S(h_{1})h_{3})f(h_{2})=0.

Similarly, one shows that

).

(3) Since

is a finite-dimensional and normal Hopf subalgebra of

,

is a free
\overline{H}^{\ast}
-module by
[

(4),(5) Clearly, the restriction map

is a Hopf algebra map whose image lies in

.

Let

, so
f(J)=0
for an ideal

In view of (

by
\widehat{f}(X)=0
and
\widehat{f}_{\mid A}=f
. Since
\widehat{f}(HJ)=0
and

,
\widehat{f}\in H^{\circ}
by
[

. By construction,

; that is,
\Pi^{\circ}(f)=\widehat{f}
. It is clear that

It remains to prove that

\displaystyle\left((\rho\circ\Pi^{\circ})(f)\right)(h\otimes a)

\displaystyle=

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf

H

(1)

Suppose that

satisfies
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
. Then

decomposes as a smash product

(3.13)

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#A^{\circ},

the isomorphism (
3.13
) being as left
\overline{H}^{*}
-modules and right
A^{\circ}
-comodules as well as of algebras.

(2)

Suppose that as an algebra

is a crossed product whose cleaving map

is a coalgebra map. (For example, this is the case if

decomposes as a smash product

with

a Hopf subalgebra of

on

is trivial, so that

(3.14)

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes A^{\circ}

as left
\overline{H}^{*}
-modules, right

-comodules and algebras.

Proof.

(1) Since
X={\rm ker\,}\Pi
is a coideal of

,
\Pi
is a coalgebra map, and so the right

-comodule map

of Lemma

(4) is an algebra map. In particular, it is a cleaving map with convolution inverse
\Pi^{\circ}\circ S_{A^{\circ}}
. By Remark

is isomorphic to
\overline{H}^{*}\#_{\sigma}A^{\circ}
for some action of
A^{\circ}
on
\overline{H}^{*}
and cocycle
\sigma
. Since the cleaving map
\Pi^{\circ}
is an algebra map, the cocycle

is trivial. By
[

, Proposition 7.2.3]
the isomorphism carries the structures as stated.

(2) Since
\gamma
is a coalgebra map,

and

is a coideal of

, so (1) applies. It remains to prove the action is trivial, that is
\Pi^{\circ}(f^{\prime})\pi^{\circ}(f)=\pi^{\circ}(f)\Pi^{\circ}(f^{\prime}),
for all

Let

\displaystyle\pi^{\circ}(f)\Pi^{\circ}(f^{\prime})(a\gamma(\bar{h}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\pi^{\circ}(f)(a_{1}\gamma(\bar{h_{1}}))\Pi^{\circ}(f^{\prime})(a_{2}\gamma(\bar{h_{2}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum f\pi(a_{1}\gamma(\bar{h_{1}}))f^{\prime}\Pi(a_{2}\gamma(\bar{h_{2}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\epsilon(a_{1})f\pi\gamma(\bar{h_{1}})f^{\prime}(a_{2})\epsilon(\gamma(\bar{h_{2}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\epsilon(a_{1})f(\bar{h_{1}})f^{\prime}(a_{2})\epsilon(\bar{h_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\bar{h})f^{\prime}(a),

since

Remarks 3.3
.

(1) In general the decompositions (

) are not isomorphisms of coalgebras. For instance, consider the group algebra

However the functional

(2) Note that the action of the smash product in (

(3) The following question seems on the face of it to be rather optimistic, but we currently do not know any example of an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra

4.
The tangential component
W(H^{\circ})

4.1.
Definition and basic properties

Throughout this section the hypotheses and notation of
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
remain in force. We define an analogue for the finite dual of a commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra of the irreducible component
A^{\prime}
appearing in (
3.6
), as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf
k
-algebra. The
tangential component
of
H^{\circ}
is

W(H^{\circ}):=\{f\in H^{\circ}:f((A^{+}H)^{n})=0,\text{ for some }n>0\}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Retain the notation and hypotheses of Definition
4.1
.

(1)

W(H^{\circ})
is a normal Hopf subalgebra of
H^{\circ}
.

(2)

\overline{H}^{*}
is a normal Hopf subalgebra of
W(H^{\circ})
.

Proof.

First,
W(H^{\circ})
is a Hopf subalgebra because
A^{+}H
is a Hopf ideal of
H
and, for any Hopf ideal
I
of
H
,

\{f\in H^{\circ}:f(I^{n})=0\textit{ for some }n>0\}

is a Hopf subalgebra of
H^{\circ}
by
[
26
, Lemma 9.2.1]
. Clearly,
W(H^{\circ})
contains
\overline{H}^{*}
, by the definition of the latter subalgebra in Lemma
3.1
(1). Since
\overline{H}^{\ast}
is normal in
H^{\circ}
by Lemma
3.1
(2), it is
a fortiori
normal in the Hopf subalgebra
W(H^{\circ})
.

To prove normality of
W(H^{\circ})
let
\varphi\in H^{\circ}
and
f\in W(H^{\circ})
, so
f
vanishes at
(A^{+}H)^{n}=(A^{+})^{n}H
for some positive integer
n
. Take
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in A^{+}
and
h\in H
. Then

\displaystyle\left[\sum S^{\circ}(\varphi_{1})f\varphi_{2}\right](a_{1}\ldots a_{n}h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum S^{\circ}(\varphi_{1})(a_{1,1}\ldots a_{n,1}h_{1})f(a_{1,2}\ldots a_{n,2}h_{2})\varphi_{2}(a_{1,3}\ldots a_{n,3}h_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\varphi_{1}(S(h_{1})S(a_{n,1})\ldots S(a_{1,1}))f(a_{1,2}\ldots a_{n,2}h_{2})\varphi_{2}(a_{1,3}\ldots a_{n,3}h_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\varphi(S(h_{1})S(a_{n,1})\ldots S(a_{1,1})a_{1,3}\ldots a_{n,3}h_{3})f(a_{1,2}\ldots a_{n,2}h_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\varphi(S(h_{1})S(a_{n,1})a_{n,3}\ldots S(a_{1,1})a_{1,3}h_{3})f(a_{1,2}\ldots a_{n,2}h_{2}),

since
A
is commutative. For each
i
we write
a_{i,2}=\epsilon(a_{i,2})1+a_{i,2}^{\prime}
, where
a_{i,2}^{\prime}\in A^{+}
. Writing
[1,n]
for
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, we now have

f(a_{1,2}\ldots a_{n,2}h_{2})=\sum_{\mathcal{P}\subseteq[1,n]}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon(a_{i,2})f\left(\prod_{i\in[1,n]\setminus\mathcal{P}}a_{i,2}^{\prime}h_{2}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left[\sum S^{\circ}(\varphi_{1})f\varphi_{2}\right](a_{1}\ldots a_{n}h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\sum_{\mathcal{P}\subseteq[1,n]}\varphi(S(h_{1})S(a_{n,1})a_{n,3}\ldots S(a_{1,1})a_{1,3}h_{3})\prod_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon(a_{i,2})f\left(\prod_{i\in[1,n]\setminus\mathcal{P}}a_{i,2}^{\prime}\,h_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\sum_{\mathcal{P}\subseteq[1,n]}\varphi\left(S(h_{1})\prod_{i\in\mathcal{P}}S(a_{i,1})\epsilon(a_{i,2})a_{i,3}\prod_{i\in[1,n]\setminus\mathcal{P}}S(a_{i,1})a_{i,3}\,h_{3}\right)f\left(\prod_{i\in[1,n]\setminus\mathcal{P}}a_{i,2}^{\prime}h_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\sum_{\mathcal{P}\subseteq[1,n]}\prod_{i\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon(a_{i})\,\varphi\left(S(h_{1})\prod_{i\in[1,n]\setminus\mathcal{P}}S(a_{i,1})a_{i,3}\,h_{3}\right)f\left(\prod_{i\in[1,n]\setminus\mathcal{P}}a_{i,2}^{\prime}h_{2}\right).

The summands where
\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
vanish due to
\epsilon(a_{i})=0
for all
i\in[1,n]
and the summands where
\mathcal{P}=\emptyset
vanish because
f
is zero on
(A^{+}H)^{n}={A^{+}}^{n}H
. The proof for the left adjoint action is similar, so normality of
W(H^{\circ})
in
H^{\circ}
is proved.
∎

4.2.
Decomposition of
W(H^{\circ})

To analyse the structure of
W(H^{\circ})
we use the Hopf algebra surjection
\iota^{\circ}
from
H^{\circ}
to
A^{\circ}
of Lemma
3.1
(5), given by restriction of functionals from
H
to
A
. In the following result we decompose
W(H^{\circ})
into the crossed product
\overline{H}^{*}\#_{\sigma}A^{\prime}
. Notice that the isomorphism (
4.15
) below does not require hypothesis
\mathrm{\mathbf{(CoSplit)}}
, in contrast to Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra with
A
semiprime or central, or
H
pointed. Recall that
A^{\circ}
decomposes as
A^{\prime}\#kG_{H}
as in (
3.6
).

(1)

\iota^{\circ}(W(H^{\circ}))=A^{\prime}
.

(2)

W(H^{\circ})
decomposes as a crossed product for a cocycle
\sigma
and an action of
A^{\prime}
on
\overline{H}^{*}
. That is, as algebras, left
\overline{H}^{\ast}
-modules and right
A^{\prime}
-comodules,

(4.15)

W(H^{\circ})\cong\overline{H}^{*}\#_{\sigma}A^{\prime}.

(3)

W(H^{\circ})^{{\rm co\,}\iota^{\circ}_{\mid_{W(H^{\circ})}}}=\overline{H}^{\ast}.

(4)

Suppose that
k
has characteristic 0. Then
{\rm ker\,}\iota^{\circ}_{\mid_{W(H^{\circ})}}=(\overline{H}^{\ast})^{+}W(H^{\circ}).

(5)

Suppose that
A\subseteq H
satisfies
\mathrm{\mathbf{(CoSplit)}}
. Then
\sigma
is trivial - that is, (
4.15
) is a decomposition of
W(H^{\circ})
as a smash product. When
k
has characteristic 0, so that
A^{\prime}=U(\mathfrak{g})
, the action of
U(\mathfrak{g})
on
\overline{H}^{*}
is given by

f\cdot\varphi=\Pi^{\circ}(f)\varphi-\varphi\Pi^{\circ}(f),

for
f\in\mathfrak{g},\varphi\in\overline{H}^{*}
. That is,
W(H^{\circ})
is a differential operator ring over
\overline{H}^{*}
.

Proof.

(1) Since
\iota^{\circ}
is just restriction of functionals to the subdomain
A
, it is clear that
\iota^{\circ}(W(H^{\circ}))\subseteq A^{\prime}
. To see that
{\rm im\,}\iota^{\circ}_{\mid_{W(H^{\circ})}}=A^{\prime}
, note the definition (
3.7
) of
A^{\prime}
, and then observe that, in the proof of Lemma
3.1
(4), if
f\in A^{\prime}\subseteq A^{\circ}
, then the map
\widehat{f}\in H^{\circ}
constructed there, with
\Pi^{\circ}(f)=\widehat{f}
, is actually in
W(H^{\circ})
. Since
\iota^{\circ}\circ\Pi^{\circ}={\mathrm{id}}_{A^{\circ}}
by Lemma
3.1
(5),
\iota^{\circ}(\widehat{f})=f
and the result follows.

(2),(3): By Lemma
3.1
(6),
H^{\circ}
is a right
A^{\circ}
-comodule algebra with
\rho=({\mathrm{id}}\otimes\iota^{\circ})\Delta
. In particular, since
W(H^{\circ})
is a Hopf subalgebra of
H^{\circ}
by Lemma
4.2
(2), and using (1) of the present theorem,

(4.16)

\rho(W(H^{\circ}))\subseteq W(H^{\circ})\otimes\iota^{\circ}(W(H^{\circ}))=W(H^{\circ})\otimes A^{\prime}.

That is, the structure of right
A^{\circ}
-comodule of
H^{\circ}
restricts to a structure of right
A^{\prime}
-comodule on
W(H^{\circ})
.

We have noted in the proof of (1) that the right
A^{\circ}
-comodule map
\Pi^{\circ}:A^{\circ}\longrightarrow H^{\circ}
maps
A^{\prime}
into
W(H^{\circ})
. Thus, in light of (
4.16
),
\Pi^{\circ}_{\mid_{A^{\prime}}}:A^{\prime}\longrightarrow W(H^{\circ})
is an injection of right
A^{\prime}
-comodules.

A linear map from a coalgebra to an algebra is convolution invertible if and only if its restriction to the coradical is convolution invertible, see
[
35
, Lemma 14]
or
[
26
, Lemma 5.2.10]
. But
A^{\prime}
is connected by its definition as the irreducible component of the coalgebra
A^{\circ}
containing
\varepsilon
,
[
26
, page 78]
; that is, its coradical is
k
. Therefore, in the language of Remark
2.3
(3),
\Pi^{\circ}_{\mid_{A^{\prime}}}
is a cleaving map and by the result of Doi and Takeuchi recalled in Remark
2.3
(3),

W(H^{\circ})\;\cong\;W(H^{\circ})^{{\rm co\,}\iota^{\circ}_{\mid_{W(H^{\circ})}}}\#_{\sigma}A^{\prime}.

Since the
A^{\prime}
-comodule structure of
W(H^{\circ})
is the restriction of the
A^{\circ}
-comodule structure of
H^{\circ}
, it follows from Lemma
3.1
(6) that

W(H^{\circ})^{{\rm co\,}\iota^{\circ}_{\mid_{W(H^{\circ})}}}=W(H^{\circ})\cap(H^{\circ})^{{\rm co\,}\iota^{\circ}}=W(H^{\circ})\cap\overline{H}^{*}=\overline{H}^{*}.

(4) Since
\mathrm{char}k=0
,
A^{\prime}=U(\mathfrak{g})
by (
3.7
). It is clear from (
4.15
) that

W(H^{\circ})/(\overline{H}^{\ast})^{+}W(H^{\circ})\quad\cong\quad U(\mathfrak{g}).

Moreover we know from Lemma
3.1
(7) that

(4.17)

(\overline{H}^{\ast})^{+}W(H^{\circ})\subseteq{\rm ker\,}\iota^{\circ}_{\mid_{W(H^{\circ})}}.

By (1),
{\rm im\,}\iota^{\circ}_{\mid_{W(H^{\circ})}}=U(\mathfrak{g})
. Since
A
is affine,
\mathfrak{g}
is finite dimensional, and hence
U(\mathfrak{g})
is a noetherian algebra. In particular
U(\mathfrak{g})
cannot be isomorphic to a proper factor of itself, and hence (
4.17
) must be an equality, proving (4).

(5) This follows from (2) and Theorem
3.2
(1), since the cocycle
\sigma
in (2) is in this case trivial by the latter result. The last part is a special case of Remark
3.3
(2).
∎

Recall that, in characteristic 0, a finite dimensional Hopf algebra is semisimple if and only if cosemisimple
[
22
]
, so the following improvement of Theorem

(5) applies when

is semisimple if

has characteristic 0.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf

-algebra with
A
semiprime or central, or
H
pointed. Retain the additional notation of Theorem
4.3
. Assume that
A\subseteq H
satisfies hypothesis
\mathrm{\mathbf{(CoSplit)}}
, and that

is cosemisimple. Then the action of

on
\overline{H}^{\ast}
is inner, so that, as algebras,

W(H^{\circ})\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes U(\mathfrak{g}).

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
(5)

is a smash product of algebras,

(4.18)

\overline{H}^{\ast}\#U(\mathfrak{g}).

By our hypothesis,
\overline{H}^{\ast}
is semisimple, and so by Jacobson’s theorem, (see for example
[
26
, Example 6.1.6, Proposition 6.2.1 and discussion following]
), since

is algebraically closed,

acts by inner derivations on

. That is, there is a copy

of
\mathfrak{g}
contained in
\overline{H}^{\ast}
, (where we view
\overline{H}^{\ast}
as a Lie algebra via the commutator bracket), such that for all

and

,

Replacing the copy of
\mathfrak{g}
appearing in (

) with the isomorphic Lie algebra

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\;:=\;\sum_{i}k(x_{i}-u(x_{i})),

where

is a

-basis of

, we see that

as claimed.
∎

5.
The subcoalgebra

5.1.
Orbits in
\maxspec(A)

Recall the description (
3.8
) in
\lx@sectionsign
3.1
of the Hopf subalgebra

of
A^{\circ}
. In this section we extend this viewpoint to

, defining a subcoalgebra
\widehat{kG_{H}}
of

which is a Hopf subalgebra under mild additional hypotheses and which reduces to

when
H=A
. We saw in Theorem
2.2
(3) that the left adjoint action of
H
on
A
factors over the ideal
A^{+}H
, so that
A
is a left
\overline{H}
-module algebra. An ideal
I
of
A
is said to be
\overline{H}
-stable
if

(\mathrm{ad}_{\ell}h)(a)\in I,\;\text{for all }h\in\overline{H},a\in I.

Since the extension
A\subseteq H
is faithfully flat by Theorem
2.2
(4),
I
is
\overline{H}
-stable if and only if
HI=IH
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
T
be a Hopf algebra and
R
a left
T
-module algebra, with
V
a subspace of
R
. The
T
-
core
of
V
is the subspace

V^{(T)}\;=\;\{v\in V:t\cdot v\in V,\,\forall t\in T\}.

Note that if
V
is a right or left ideal of
R
in Definition
5.1
then so is
V^{(T)}
. The crucial parts (2) and (3) of the following result are essentially due to Skryabin
[
33
, Theorems 1.1, 1.3]
. For the proofs of Proposition
5.2
and Corollary
5.4
, see
[
4
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra and let
\mathfrak{m}\in\maxspec(A)
.

(1)

\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}
is the largest
\overline{H}
-stable ideal of
A
contained in
\mathfrak{m}
.

(2)

A/\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}
is an
\overline{H}
-simple algebra - that is, the
\overline{H}
-core of each of its proper ideals is
\{0\}
.

(3)

A/\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}
is a Frobenius algebra. In particular,
A/\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}
is finite dimensional.

Following still Skryabin, we can use Proposition
5.2
(2) to extend the fundamental idea that a group of automorphisms of the algebra
A
defines orbits in
\maxspec(A)
, to the present setting where the group is replaced by a Hopf algebra.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra.

(1)

For each
\mathfrak{m}\in\maxspec(A)
, its
\overline{H}
-orbit is the set of maximal ideals with the same
\overline{H}
-core,

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}}=\{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\maxspec(A):{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}^{(\overline{H})}=\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}\}.

(2)

Define an equivalence relation
\sim^{(\overline{H})}
on
G_{H}=\maxspec(A)
as follows: for
g,h\in G_{H}
,
g\sim^{(\overline{H})}h
if and only if
\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}=\mathfrak{m}_{h}^{(\overline{H})}.

It’s clear that
\sim^{(\overline{H})}
is an equivalence relation; but it is far from obvious, although it follows easily from Proposition
5.2
(2), that the
\overline{H}
-orbits have the following simple description.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra.

(1)

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}}=\{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in\maxspec(A):\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\}
.

(2)

{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is finite.

5.2.
Orbital semisimplicity

The results in
\lx@sectionsign

lead naturally to:

Definition 5.5

Let

Note that, in view of Corollary

is orbitally semisimple if and only if

(5.19)

\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}.

Since we have used the
left
adjoint action in Definition
5.5
, one might choose to call the above property
left
orbital semisimplicity; but in fact it is shown in
[
4
, Lemma 4.10(2)]
that
A\subseteq H
is left orbitally semisimple if and only if it is right orbitally semisimple.

We know no examples of affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebras that are not orbitally semisimple. When
A
is central in
H
orbital semsimplicity is trivial; the next result lists other positive cases. In characteristic 0 there are overlaps between these, thanks to the result of Etingof and Walton
[
15
]
implying that the action of
\overline{H}
factors through a group algebra when
\overline{H}
is semisimple and cosemisimple and
A
is a domain.

Theorem 5.6
.

(
[
4
, Theorem 4.8]
)
The affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra
A\subseteq H
satisfies
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
in the following cases.

(1)

The adjoint action of
\overline{H}
on
\maxspec(A)
factors through a group.

(2)

\overline{H}
is cosemisimple.

(3)

\overline{H}
is involutory and either
{\rm char\,}k=0
or
{\rm char\,}k>\dim A/\mathfrak{m}^{(\overline{H})}
for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\maxspec(A)
.

We discuss particular families of examples of affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebras, and their status with respect to orbital semisimplicity, in
\lx@sectionsign
7
.

5.3.
The character component of
H^{\circ}

Here is the definition we have been working towards:

Definition 5.7
.

Let
H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra, finite over the normal commutative Hopf subalgebra
A
. Recall from
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
that
G_{H}
denotes the affine algebraic
k
-group such that
A/N(A)\cong\mathcal{O}(G_{H})
.

(1)

The character component of
H^{\circ}
is

\widehat{kG_{H}}=\left\{f\in H^{\circ}:f\left(H\mathfrak{m}_{g_{1}}^{(\overline{H})}\cap\ldots\cap H\mathfrak{m}_{g_{r}}^{(\overline{H})}\right)=0,\text{ for some }g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in G_{H}/\sim^{(\overline{H})}\right\}.

(2)

For
g\in G_{H}
, write
\widehat{g}
to denote the subspace
(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})^{\ast}
of
\widehat{kG_{H}}
.

Here are some basic properties of these spaces.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf
k
-algebra. Retain the notation of
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
and Definition
5.7
.

(1)

If
g,h\in G_{H}
with
g\sim^{(\overline{H})}h
, then
\widehat{g}=\widehat{h}
.

(2)

\widehat{kG_{H}}=\bigoplus_{g\in G_{H}/\sim^{(\overline{H})}}\;\widehat{g}
.

(3)

\widehat{kG_{H}}
is a subcoalgebra of
H^{\circ}
.

(4)

For all
g\in G_{H}
,
\widehat{g}
is a subcoalgebra of
\widehat{kG_{H}}
.

(5)

For all
g\in G_{H}
,

\widehat{g}\supseteq\bigoplus_{h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g}\left(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{h}\right)^{\ast},

and

\left(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{g}\right)^{\ast}\supseteq\overline{H}^{\ast}\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})+\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})\overline{H}^{\ast}.

(6)

For
g\in G_{H}
,
S^{\circ}(\widehat{g})\;=\;\widehat{g^{-1}}
.

(7)

\widehat{kG_{H}}\cap W(H^{\circ})\;=\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\;=\;\widehat{1_{G}}
.

(8)

Assume that
A
is a domain and let
d_{A}(H)
be the minimal number of generators of
H
as a right or left
A
-module. For
g\in G_{H}
,

(5.20)

\mathrm{dim}_{k}(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{g})=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(H/\mathfrak{m}_{g}H)=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\overline{H}),

and

(5.21)

\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\widehat{g})\;=\;\mathrm{dim}_{k}(A/\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\overline{H})\leq d_{A}(H)\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\overline{H}).

If
H
is prime (which implies that
A
is a domain), then

(5.22)

\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\widehat{g})\;\leq\;\left(\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\overline{H})\right)^{2}.

Proof.

(1) This follows from the definition of
\sim^{(\overline{H})}
.

(2) If
g,h\in G_{H}
with
{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}\neq{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{h}}
, then the ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{h}^{(\overline{H})}
of
A
are comaximal, by Corollary
5.4
(1). The same is thus true of the ideals
H\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}
and
H\mathfrak{m}_{h}^{(\overline{H})}
of
H
. Therefore, if
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in G_{H}
are representatives of distinct
\sim^{(\overline{H})}
-orbits, the Chinese Remainder Theorem shows that

\left.H\middle/\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}H\mathfrak{m}_{g_{i}}^{(\overline{H})}\right.\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H/H\mathfrak{m}_{g_{i}}^{(\overline{H})}.

Taking duals on both sides proves (2).

(3),(4): Let
g\in G_{H}
. That
\widehat{g}
is a subcoalgebra of
H^{\circ}
is simply a special case of the elementary fact that, for an ideal
I
of finite codimension in an algebra
R
,
(R/I)^{\ast}
is a subcoalgebra of
R^{\circ}
. So (3) and (4) follow from this observation together with (2).

(5) The first inclusion is clear. For the second, note first that

(5.23)

\Delta(H\mathfrak{m}_{g})=\Delta(H)\Delta(\mathfrak{m}_{g})\subseteq H\mathfrak{m}_{1_{G_{H}}}\otimes H+H\otimes H\mathfrak{m}_{g},

by the definition of the coproduct in
A/N(A)={\mathcal{O}}(G_{H})
, combined with the fact that
N(A)
is a Hopf ideal of
A
,
[
4
, Lemma 5.2(1)]
. Let
f\in\overline{H}^{\ast}
. Then, using (
5.23
),

(f\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g}))(H\mathfrak{m}_{g})\subseteq f(H\mathfrak{m}_{1_{G_{H}}})\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})(H)+f(H)\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})(H\mathfrak{m}_{g})=0.

So
\overline{H}^{\ast}\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})\subseteq(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{g})^{\ast}
. The proof of the remaining inclusion is similar.

(6) Let
g\in G_{H}
. To prove that
S^{\circ}(\widehat{g})=\widehat{g^{-1}}
it suffices to show that
S(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})=\mathfrak{m}_{g^{-1}}^{(\overline{H})}
. It is easy to see that

\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}=\mathrm{r-Ann}_{A}(H/\mathfrak{m}_{g}H)=\mathrm{l-Ann}_{A}(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{g}).

Since
S
is an algebra anti-isomorphism of
H
with
S(A)=A
, it follows that

S(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})=\mathrm{l-Ann}_{A}(H/HS(\mathfrak{m}_{g}))=\mathrm{l-Ann}_{A}(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{g^{-1}}).

This proves (6).

(7) The final equality is clear from the definitions, and this also shows that
\overline{H}^{\ast}\subseteq\widehat{kG_{H}}
. Thus, by Lemma
4.2
(2),
\overline{H}^{\ast}\subseteq W(H^{\circ})\cap\widehat{kG_{H}}
.

For the reverse inclusion, suppose
f\in H^{\circ}
with
f
vanishing on
(A^{+}H)^{n}={A^{+}}^{n}H
and also on
I:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{m}_{g_{i}}^{(\overline{H})}H
, for some
n,r\geq 1
and distinct
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in G_{H}/\sim^{(\overline{H})}
. We claim that

(5.24)

A^{+}H\subseteq I+{A^{+}}^{n}H.

If
g_{i}\neq 1_{G_{H}}
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
, then
I
and
{A^{+}}^{n}H
are comaximal by the discussion in the proof of (2), so that (
5.24
) is clear. Suppose on the other hand that, say,
g_{1}=1_{G_{H}}
. Then
I+{A^{+}}^{n}H\subseteq A^{+}H
, and the left
A
-module
A^{+}H/(I+{A^{+}}^{n}H)
is annihilated by both of the comaximal ideals
{A^{+}}^{n-1}
and by
\bigcap_{i=2}^{r}\mathfrak{m}_{g_{i}}^{(\overline{H})}
. Hence
A^{+}H/(I+{A^{+}}^{n}H)=\{0\}
and again (
5.24
) is proved. Thus (
5.24
) holds in all cases and so
f\in\overline{H}^{\ast}
, as required.

(8) Assume that
A
is a domain. Localise
A
at the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{g}
, and consider the left
A_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}
-module
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}:=A_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}\otimes_{A}H
. Since
H
is a finitely generated projective
A
-module by Theorem
2.2
(6), and
A_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}
is local,
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}
is a free
A_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}
-module of finite rank
r
. Let
Q
denote the quotient field of
A
. Since
H
is a torsion free
A
-module,

r\;=\;\mathrm{dim}_{Q}(Q\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}}H_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}})\;=\;\mathrm{dim}_{Q}(Q\otimes_{A}H),

so
r
is constant as
g
varies through
G_{H}
. Taking
g=1_{G_{H}}
and applying Nakayama’s lemma,

(5.25)

r=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(H/\mathfrak{m}_{1_{G_{H}}}H)=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\overline{H}),

proving the second equality in (
5.20
). The first equality is a consequence of the second, using the antipode. Since the algebra
H/\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}H
is artinian, it is isomorphic as an
A/\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}
-module to the factor
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}/\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}H_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}
of
H_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}
. But the latter is a free
A/\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}
-module of rank
r
. Combining this with (
5.25
) proves the equality in (
5.21
), and the inequality is then given by
[
4
, Theorem 6.1(2)]
. If
H
is prime then
A
is a domain and the inequality (
5.22
) follows from
[
4
, Corollary 6.2 and its proof]
.

∎

5.4.
Subalgebras and subcoalgebras of
\widehat{kG_{H}}

In order to better control the structure of
\widehat{kG_{H}}
we need to impose on the inclusion
A\subseteq H
one or both of the additional hypotheses
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
and
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
defined respectively in
\lx@sectionsign
1.1
and Definition
5.5
. First we assume
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
in Proposition
5.9
, then
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
in Proposition
5.10
, before combining these in Theorem
5.13
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf
k
-algebra satisfying
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
. Assume also that
A
is semiprime or central, or that
H
is pointed. Keep the notation introduced in
\lx@sectionsign\lx@sectionsign
5.3
and
2.2
and in Lemma
3.1
. Define

\widetilde{G_{H}}=\{\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g}):g\in G_{H}\}\subseteq H^{\circ}.

(1)

\widetilde{G_{H}}
is a subgroup of the group of units of
H^{\circ}
, and the algebra injection
\Pi^{\circ}:A^{\circ}\to H^{\circ}
restricts to an isomorphism of groups from the group-likes
G_{H}
of
A^{\circ}
to
\widetilde{G_{H}}
.

(2)

The subalgebra of
H^{\circ}
generated by
\widetilde{G_{H}}
is the group algebra
k\widetilde{G_{H}}
, and
\Pi^{\circ}
restricts to an algebra isomorphism from
kG_{H}
to
k\widetilde{G_{H}}
.

(3)

For all
g\in G_{H}
,
\overline{H}^{\ast}\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})=\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})\overline{H}^{\ast}
is free of rank 1 as an
\overline{H}^{\ast}
-bimodule. Hence
\langle\overline{H}^{\ast},\widetilde{G_{H}}\rangle
is a skew group algebra
\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\widetilde{G_{H}}
; that is,

\langle\overline{H}^{\ast},\widetilde{G_{H}}\rangle\;=\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\widetilde{G_{H}}\;=\;\oplus_{g\in G_{H}}\overline{H}^{\ast}\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})

is a subalgebra of
H^{\circ}
contained in the subcoalgebra
\widehat{kG_{H}}
.

(4)

There are inclusions

\widehat{kG_{H}}=\bigoplus_{g\in G_{H}/\sim^{(\overline{H})}}\widehat{g}\supseteq\bigoplus_{g\in G_{H}/\sim^{(\overline{H})}}\left(\bigoplus_{h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g}(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{h})^{\ast}\right)\supseteq\bigoplus_{g\in G_{H}/\sim^{(\overline{H})}}\left(\bigoplus_{h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g}\overline{H}^{\ast}\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{h})\right)=\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\widetilde{G_{H}}.

If
A
is a domain then the second inclusion is an equality.

Proof.

(1),(2) The
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
hypothesis ensures that
\Pi
is a coalgebra map, so
\Pi^{\circ}
is an algebra homomorphism, and is an injection by Lemma
3.1
(5). Thus
\Pi^{\circ}
maps
G_{H}
isomorphically to a subgroup
\widetilde{G_{H}}
of the group of units of
H^{\circ}
, and

k\widetilde{G_{H}}=\Pi^{\circ}(kG_{H})\cong kG_{H}.

(3) By Remark
3.3
(2), the action of
kG_{H}\subset A^{\circ}
on
\overline{H}^{*}
is

\chi_{g}\cdot f=\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})f\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g^{-1}})\in\overline{H}^{*},

for any
f\in\overline{H}^{*},g\in G_{H}
, hence
\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})f\in\overline{H}^{*}\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})
, proving
\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})\overline{H}^{*}\subseteq\overline{H}^{*}\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})
and the converse is proved similarly. Both these
\overline{H}^{\ast}
-modules are free of rank 1 since
\Pi^{\circ}(G_{H})
consists of units of
H^{\circ}
. From Theorem
3.2
(1) and its proof we know that
H^{\circ}
is a free left and right
\overline{H}^{\ast}
-module with any vector space basis of
\Pi^{\circ}(A^{\circ})
as free basis. In particular,
\{\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g}):g\in G_{H}\}
spans a free left and right
\overline{H}^{\circ}
-submodule of
\widehat{kG_{H}}
.

(4) The first equality and first inclusion are immediate from Definition
5.7
and Lemma
5.8
(5). The second inclusion follows from Lemma
5.8
(5) and the third equality is part (3) of the present lemma. If
A
is a domain the second inclusion is seen to be an equality by comparing vector space dimensions using Lemma
5.8
(8).

∎

We examine next the effect of the
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
hypothesis on
\widehat{kG_{H}}
.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra satisfying
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
.

(1)

The subcoalgebra
\widehat{kG_{H}}
of
H^{\circ}
is a Hopf subalgebra.

(2)

For
g,h\in G_{H}
,

(5.26)

\widehat{g}\widehat{h}\subseteq\sum_{g^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g,h^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}h}\widehat{g^{\prime}h^{\prime}}.

(3)

For
g\in G_{H}
,
\widehat{g}
is a left and right
\overline{H}^{\ast}
-module.

Assume further that
A
is a domain. Then, for any
g\in G_{H}
:

(4)

\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\widehat{g})=\left|{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}\right|\,\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\overline{H}).

(5)

\widehat{g}=\bigoplus_{h\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g}\left(H/H\mathfrak{m}_{h}\right)^{\ast}=\bigoplus_{h\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g}\left(H/\mathfrak{m}_{h}H\right)^{\ast}.

Proof.

Note that (3) is a special case of (2), since
\overline{H}^{\ast}=\widehat{1_{G_{H}}}
. By Lemma
5.8
(3),(6), (1) will follow if
\widehat{kG_{H}}
is closed under multiplication. Moreover, by Lemma
5.8
(2), closure under multiplication will follow at once from (2). We claim that to prove (
5.26
) amounts to showing that

(5.27)

\Delta\left(\bigcap_{g^{\prime}\sim^{(\overline{H})}g,h^{\prime}\sim^{(\overline{H})}h}\mathfrak{m}_{g^{\prime}h^{\prime}}\right)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}\otimes A+A\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{h}^{(\overline{H})}=:J.

For, the flatness of
H
over
A
together with the Chinese remainder theorem implies that
\bigcap_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}(H\mathfrak{m}_{g^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{(\overline{H})})=H\left(\bigcap_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}\mathfrak{m}_{g^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{(\overline{H})}\right)
. Hence, given (
5.27
),

(5.28)

\Delta\left(\bigcap_{g^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g,h^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}h}H\mathfrak{m}_{g^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{(\overline{H})}\right)\subseteq H\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}\otimes H+H\otimes H\mathfrak{m}_{h}^{(\overline{H})}.

Consider now
\beta\gamma
for
\beta\in\widehat{g}
and
\gamma\in\widehat{h}
. It follows from (
5.28
) that

\beta\gamma\left(\bigcap_{g^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g,h^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}h}H\mathfrak{m}_{g^{\prime}h^{\prime}}^{(\overline{H})}\right)\subseteq\beta(H\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})\gamma(H)+\beta(H)\gamma(H\mathfrak{m}_{h}^{(\overline{H})})=0,

proving part (2) of the proposition.

Let us prove (
5.27
). By the definition of the coproduct of
A
,

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\bigcap_{g^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g,h^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}h}\mathfrak{m}_{g^{\prime}h^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\bigcap_{g^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g,h^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}h}\Delta\left(\mathfrak{m}_{g^{\prime}h^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\bigcap_{g^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g,h^{\prime}\sim^{({\overline{H}})}h}(\mathfrak{m}_{g^{\prime}}\otimes A+A\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{h^{\prime}})=:I.

Thus (
5.27
) will follow if it can be shown that
I=J
. It is clear that
J\subseteq I
. To prove equality, let
r=|{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}|
and
s=|{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{h}}|
. Then it follows from
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
that

\mathrm{dim}_{k}((A\otimes A)/J)=rs=\mathrm{dim}_{k}((A\otimes A)/I).

This completes the proof of (
5.27
).

(4) Since
H
is orbitally semisimple,
\left|{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{g}}\right|=\mathrm{dim}_{k}(A/\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})
by (
5.19
), hence (4) is (
5.21
).

(5) Since
H
is orbitally semisimple,
H/H\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}=\bigoplus_{h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g}H/H\mathfrak{m}_{h}
, so this follows from the definition of
\widehat{g}
.

Remark 5.11
.

The formula (
5.26
) cannot be further simplified. To see this, consider the group algebra

of the dihedral group as in Remark

(1).
Recall that

5.5.
Decomposition of
\widehat{kG_{H}}

Before uniting the conclusions of Propositions
5.9
and
5.10
in Theorem
5.13
we need one more lemma. Recall from §
2.2
and Lemma
3.1
the Hopf surjection
\iota^{\circ}:H^{\circ}\longrightarrow A^{\circ}
, so that
H^{\circ}
is a right
A^{\circ}
-comodule algebra via the map
\rho:=({\mathrm{id}}\otimes\iota^{\circ})\circ\Delta
.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra satisfying
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
. Assume that
A
is semiprime or central, or that
H
is pointed. In the notation of §§
2.2
,
5.1
, Definition
5.7
and Lemma
3.1
, the following hold for each
g\in G_{H}
:

(1)

\qquad\iota^{\circ}(\widehat{g})=\bigoplus_{h\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g}k\chi_{h}
.

(2)

\qquad\widehat{g}\;\supseteq\;\{f\in H^{\circ}:\rho(f)\in f\otimes\sum_{h\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g}k\chi_{h}\}
.

(3)

\qquad\widehat{1_{G_{H}}}\;=\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\;=\;\{f\in H^{\circ}:\rho(f)=f\otimes 1\}
.

Proof.

(1) Let
g\in G_{H}
and take
f\in\widehat{g}
, so that
\left.f\right|_{A}(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})=0
. Since
A\subseteq H
satisfies
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
the Chinese remainder theorem implies that

\iota^{\circ}(f)=\left.f\right|_{A}\in\left(A/\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}\right)^{\ast}\cong\bigoplus_{h\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g}(A/\mathfrak{m}_{h})^{\ast}=\bigoplus_{h\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g}k\chi_{h}.

Conversely, let
\alpha=\sum_{h\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g}\lambda_{h}\chi_{h}\in A^{\circ}
for some
\lambda_{h}\in k
. Then
\alpha=\iota^{\circ}(\Pi^{\circ}(\alpha))
by Lemma
3.1
(5). Recall that
H=A\oplus X
as right
A
-modules by (
1.1
) in
\lx@sectionsign
1.2
. Thus

\Pi^{\circ}(\alpha)(H\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})=\Pi^{\circ}(\alpha)(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}\oplus X\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}))=\alpha(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})=\sum_{h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g}\lambda_{h}\chi_{h}(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})=0,

as
\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{h}
for every
h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g
. Thus
\Pi^{\circ}(\alpha)\in\widehat{g}
and
\alpha\in\iota^{\circ}(\widehat{g})
, proving (1).

(2) Let
f\in H^{\circ}
and suppose
\rho(f)=f\otimes\sum_{h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g}\lambda_{h}\chi_{h}
for some
\lambda_{h}\in k
. Then

\displaystyle f(H\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum f_{1}(H)f_{2}(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})=\sum f_{1}(H)\left.f_{2}\right|_{A}(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(f)(H\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})=f(H)\left(\sum_{h\sim^{({\overline{H}})}g}\lambda_{h}\chi_{h}(\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})})\right)=0,

as
\mathfrak{m}_{g}^{(\overline{H})}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{h}
for every
h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g
. Hence
f\in\widehat{g}
.

(3) That the left side contains the right is a special case of (2). For the reverse inclusion, let
f\in\overline{H}^{*}=\widehat{1_{G}}
. By part (1)

\rho(f)\in({\mathrm{id}}\otimes\iota^{\circ})(\overline{H}^{*}\otimes\overline{H}^{*})=\overline{H}^{*}\otimes k\varepsilon_{A},

say
\rho(f)=f^{\prime}\otimes\varepsilon_{A}
for some
f^{\prime}\in\overline{H}^{*}
. By the counit axiom of right comodules,
f=\varepsilon_{A}(1_{A})f^{\prime}=f^{\prime}
, hence
\rho(f)=f\otimes 1_{A^{\circ}}
, proving (3).
∎

We can now show that when
A\subseteq H
satisfies
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}

\widehat{kG_{H}}
decomposes as a crossed or smash product whenever
H^{\circ}
does, generalising the presence of the group algebra
kG_{H}
in the dual of a commutative semiprime Hopf algebra.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
A\subseteq H
be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra satisfying
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
, and assume that
A
is semiprime or central, or that
H
is pointed.

(1)

Suppose that
H^{\circ}\cong\overline{H}^{\ast}\#_{\sigma}A^{\circ}
decomposes as a crossed product for some cocycle
\sigma
and action of
A^{\circ}
on
\overline{H}^{*}
. Then

(5.31)

\widehat{kG_{H}}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{*}\#_{\left.\sigma\right|_{kG_{H}\otimes kG_{H}}}kG_{H}

as algebras, left
\overline{H}^{\ast}
-modules and right
kG_{H}
-comodules.

(2)

Suppose
A\subseteq H
satisfies
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
. Then, in (
5.31
),
\sigma
is trivial, so
\widehat{kG_{H}}
is a smash product of
\overline{H}^{\ast}
by
\widetilde{G_{H}}
,

(5.32)

\widehat{kG_{H}}\;=\;\overline{H}^{*}\#k\widetilde{G_{H}},

with action given, for
g\in G_{H},\varphi\in\overline{H}^{*}
, by

\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})\cdot\varphi=\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g})\varphi\Pi^{\circ}(\chi_{g^{-1}}).

Proof.

(1) Suppose that
H^{\circ}\cong\overline{H}^{*}\#_{\sigma}A^{\circ}
. By
[
14
]
(see Remark
2.3
(4)), there exists a convolution invertible right
A^{\circ}
-comodule map
\gamma:A^{\circ}\to H^{\circ}
such that the isomorphism is given by
f\#\varphi\mapsto f\gamma(\varphi)
. The core of this proof is showing that

(5.33)

\widehat{g}=\bigoplus_{h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g}\overline{H}^{*}\gamma(\chi_{h}).

We first claim that
\gamma(\chi_{h})\in\widehat{g}
. Since
\gamma:A^{\circ}\to H^{\circ}
is a right
A^{\circ}
-comodule map and
\chi_{h}
is a grouplike element of
A^{\circ}
, then

\rho\gamma(\chi_{h})=(\gamma\otimes{\mathrm{id}})\Delta_{A}(\chi_{h})=\gamma(\chi_{h})\otimes\chi_{h}

and the claim follows from Lemma
5.12
(2). Therefore, by Proposition
5.10
(3)

\overline{H}^{*}\gamma(\chi_{h})\subseteq\overline{H}^{*}\widehat{g}\subseteq\widehat{g},

proving the inclusion of the right of (
5.33
) in the left.

For the reverse inclusion, let
f\in\widehat{g}
. By the second sentence of the proof,
f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\gamma(u_{i}\#\chi_{g_{i}})
, where
f_{i}\in\overline{H}^{*},u_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g}),g_{i}\in G_{H}
. Since
\rho
is an algebra homomorphism and
\gamma:A^{\circ}\to H^{\circ}
is a right
A^{\circ}
-comodule map, and since
\chi_{g_{i}}\in A^{\circ}
is grouplike and
\rho(f_{i})=f_{i}\otimes 1
by Lemma
5.12
(2) we have

\displaystyle\rho(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(f_{i})\rho\gamma(u_{i}\#\chi_{g_{i}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho(f_{i})(\gamma\otimes{\mathrm{id}})\Delta(u_{i}\#\chi_{g_{i}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{(u_{i})}f_{i}\gamma(u_{i,1}\#\chi_{g_{i}})\otimes(u_{i,2}\#\chi_{g_{i}}).

But by Lemma
5.12
(1)

\rho(f)\in\widehat{g}\otimes\iota^{\circ}(\widehat{g})=\bigoplus_{h\sim^{(\overline{H})}g}\widehat{g}\otimes\chi_{h}.

Moreover, since each
u_{i}
is a polynomial on primitive elements, we must have

for every

, and each

. Hence

has the required form, and (

) follows.

Therefore,

\widehat{kG_{H}}=\overline{H}^{*}\gamma(kG_{H})\cong\overline{H}^{*}\otimes kG_{H}

as vector spaces, left
\overline{H}^{*}
-modules and right
kG_{H}
-comodules, since by the calculations above
\rho(\widehat{kG_{H}})\subseteq\widehat{kG_{H}}\otimes kG_{H}
, that is the

-comodule structure of

restricts to a

-comodule structure for

. In particular, by Lemma

\widehat{kG_{H}}^{co\,kG_{H}}=\widehat{kG_{H}}\cap(H^{\circ})^{co\,A^{\circ}}=\widehat{kG_{H}}\cap\overline{H}^{*}=\overline{H}^{*}.

Also, since
\gamma:A^{\circ}\to H^{\circ}
is convolution invertible, then so is its restriction to the coradical
\left.\gamma\right|_{kG_{H}}:kG_{H}\to\widehat{kG_{H}}
,
[

. Therefore,

\widehat{kG_{H}}\cong\overline{H}^{*}\#_{\tau}kG_{H}

and by inspecting the formulae for the cocyles

and

.

(2) If
X
can be chosen to be a coideal, then

by Theorem

(1), hence the cocycle

decomposes as a smash product. The action of

on

is a special case of Remark

(2).

∎

6.
H^{\circ}
under the auspices of
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
and
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}

Let

be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra. Recall that the subspaces

of

were introduced in Definition

the following generalisation of the description of the Hopf dual of a commutative Hopf algebra via the Cartier-Gabriel-Kostant theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let

be an affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebra and assume that
A
is semiprime or central, or that
H
is pointed. Suppose that
A\subseteq H
satisfies

\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
and
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
. Retain the notation of
\lx@sectionsign
2.2
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.1
and of Definitions
4.1
and
5.7
.

(1)

H^{\circ}
is a smash product

(6.34)

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\,A^{\circ}\;\cong\;(\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\,A^{\prime})\#kG_{H},

where
A/N(A)=\mathcal{O}(G_{H})
, where

is the nilradical of

. If

, where

is the Lie algebra of

(2)

contains three Hopf subalgebras:

,

(6.35)

W(H^{\circ})\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\,A^{\prime}

and

(6.36)

\widehat{kG_{H}}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#\,kG_{H},

with

if
k
has characteristic 0.

(3)

Suppose that
\overline{H}
is semisimple and

has characteristic 0. Then the action of

on

is inner, so that

(6.37)

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;(\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes U(\mathfrak{g}))\#kG_{H},

a skew group algebra with coefficient ring the Hopf subalgebra

.

Proof.

(1),(2) By the isomorphism (

) in
\lx@sectionsign
3.1
there is a decomposition

(6.38)

with

and

Hopf subalgebras of

. Substituting (

) into the isomorphism (

) of Theorem

(1) shows that, as algebras,

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#A^{\circ}\;=\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#(A^{\prime}\#kG_{H}).

More precisely, the proof of Theorem

(1), using the fact that

is an algebra homomorphism, shows that we can rewrite (

Moreover, by Proposition

(3) This is Corollary

7.
Examples

We list here a number of classes of examples of affine commutative-by-finite Hopf algebras and discuss in each case what our results tell us about their Hopf duals.

7.1.
Enveloping algebras of Lie algebras in positive characteristic

Assume that
k
has positive characteristic
p
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
k
-Lie algebra with basis
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Then
H:=U(\mathfrak{g})
is a free module of finite rank over a central polynomial Hopf subalgebra

A:=k[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}],

where each
y_{i}
is a
p
-polynomial in
x_{i}
and hence is primitive; see
[
18
, Proposition 1]
. When
\mathfrak{g}
is restricted, with restriction map
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
, one takes
y_{i}:=x_{i}^{p}-x_{i}^{[p]}
for all
i
, and in this case the Hopf quotient
\overline{H}
is the restricted enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, usually denoted by
u^{[p]}(\mathfrak{g})
, with
\mathrm{dim}_{k}(u^{[p]}(\mathfrak{g}))=p^{n}
. In the non-restricted case it is still the case by
[
18
, Proposition 2]
that
U(\mathfrak{g})
is a free
A
-module, with basis

(7.41)

\{x_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{j_{n}}:0\leq j_{i}<p^{d_{i}}\},

where
y_{i}
is a linear combination of
x_{i}^{p^{j}}
for
j=0,\ldots,d_{i}
. Thus, in the general non-restricted case,

\mathrm{dim}_{k}(\overline{H})\;=\;p^{\sum_{i}d_{i}}.

Since
A
is central
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
is trivially true for
H=U(\mathfrak{g})
. Moreover it is clear from (
7.41
) that, as an
A
-module complement to
A
in
H
, we can choose

X\;:=\;\sum Ax_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{j_{n}},\;0\leq j_{i}<p^{d_{i}},\;\textit{ not all }j_{i}=0.

Thus
X
is a coideal of
H
and so
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
is also valid. We can therefore apply Theorem
6.1
(1). Note that

k[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]\;\cong\;\mathcal{O}((k,+)^{n}),

and that
H^{\circ}
is commutative since
H
is cocommutative, so that the actions in the smash products composing
H^{\circ}
are all trivial. We conclude that, as algebras,

U(\mathfrak{g})^{\circ}\;\cong\;A^{\prime}\otimes\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes k(k,+)^{n},

with
\overline{H}^{\ast}
,
W(H^{\circ})\,:=\,A^{\prime}\otimes\overline{H}^{\ast}
and
\widehat{k(k,+)^{n}}\,:=\,\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes k(k,+)^{n}
each being a Hopf subalgebra of
U(\mathfrak{g})^{\circ}
. Here, as an algebra,
A^{\prime}
is a divided powers algebra in
n
variables,

A^{\prime}\;=\;k[f^{(m)}_{1},\ldots,f^{(m)}_{n}:m\geq 0],

as defined and discussed at
[
26
, Examples 5.6.8 and 9.1.7]
. Modulo the Hopf ideal
(\overline{H}^{\ast})^{+}A^{\prime}
of
W(H^{\circ})
the coproducts of the elements of
A^{\prime}
take their classical cocommutative forms. Similarly, the elements of
(k,+)^{n}
are group-like
modulo
(\overline{H}^{\ast})^{+}k(k,+)^{n}
.

7.2.
Group algebras of finitely generated abelian-by-finite groups

Let
G
be a finitely generated group with an abelian normal subgroup

of finite index. For convenience we may as well choose

to be torsion free, and hence free abelian of rank
n

and

,
\overline{H}=k(G/N)
. Clearly

is a free
A
-module with basis a set of coset representatives of

\overline{H}^{\ast}
,

and
\widehat{k((k^{\times})^{n})}\,:=\,\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes k((k^{\times})^{n})

7.3.

In this subsection and the next we assume that

k
algebraically closed of characteristic 0 there are 2 finite families and 3 infinite families, as follows.

(I)

The commutative algebras
k[x]
and
k[x^{\pm 1}]
.

(II)

The unique noncommutative cocommutative example, the group algebra
H=kD
of the infinite dihedral group

(III)

The infinite dimensional Taft algebras

\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+g^{t}\otimes x

is a commutative normal Hopf subalgebra.

(IV)

The generalised Liu algebras
B(n,w,q)
, where
n
and

q
is a primitive
n
th root of 1. Here,

B(n,w,q)\;:=\;k\langle x^{\pm 1},g^{\pm 1},y\,:\,x\textit{ central},\,yg=qgy,\,g^{n}=x^{w}=1-y^{n}\rangle,

with

and

. One can show that

is a central Hopf subalgebra over which

is free of rank

.

(V)

Let

root of 1 in

[

.

The above algebras are all free over the listed normal commutative Hopf subalgebras. Families (I)-(IV) are pointed and decompose as crossed products

H\;\cong\;A\#_{\sigma}\overline{H};

but

is not pointed,
[
12
, Proposition 4.9]
. Applying Theorems
3.2
,
4.3
and
5.13
to these families yields the following information about their Hopf duals, (omitting the algebras in (I) and (II), which are already covered by
\lx@sectionsign\lx@sectionsign
3.1
and

). In the proposition, for coprime integers

and

with
1\leq s<m
and a primitive

root of unity

denotes the finite dimensional Taft algebra in these parameters; that is,
T_{f}(m,s,q):=T(m,s,q)/\langle x^{m}\rangle
. For a positive integer

For reasons of space we omit some details in the proofs for (III) and (IV), and omit the entire proof of (V). Full proofs can be found in

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, and let
H
be an affine prime regular Hopf
k
-algebra of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension one, as listed in cases (III), (IV) and (V) above. Then
\mathbf{CoSplit)}
and
\mathbf{OrbSemi)}
hold in all cases, and the finite dual of
H
takes the following forms.

(III)

Let
d:=\mathrm{gcd}(n,t),\,n^{\prime}:=n/d,\,t^{\prime}:=t/d
. Then, as algebras,

T(n,t,q)^{\circ}\;\cong\;kC_{d}\otimes T_{f}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime},q^{d})\otimes k[f]\otimes k(k,+),

with Hopf subalgebras
kC_{d}
,
\overline{H}^{\ast}=kC_{d}\otimes T_{f}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime},q^{d})
,

W(H^{\circ})\;=\;kC_{d}\otimes T_{f}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime},q^{d})\otimes k[f]

and

\widehat{kG_{H}}\;=\;kC_{d}\otimes T_{f}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime},q^{d})\otimes k(k,+).

(IV)

When
H=B(n,w,q)
,

H^{\circ}\;=\;T_{f}(n,1,q)\#(k[f]\otimes k(k^{\times})),

with Hopf subalgebras
\overline{H}^{\ast}=T_{f}(n,1,q)
,
W(H^{\circ})=T_{f}(n,1,q)\#k[f]
and

\widehat{kG_{H}}\;=\;T_{f}(n,1,q)\#k(k,+).

(V)

When
H=D(m,d,q)
,

H^{\circ}\;=\;(kC_{2}\#_{\sigma}T_{f}(m,1,q^{2}))\#(k[f]\otimes k(k^{\times})),

with Hopf subalgebras
\overline{H}^{\ast}=kC_{2}\#_{\sigma}T_{f}(m,1,q^{2})
,
W(H^{\circ})=\overline{H}^{\ast}\#k[f]
and

\widehat{kG_{H}}\;=\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#k(k^{\times}).

Proof.

(III) Let
A=k[x^{n^{\prime}}]
as noted above. Thus

\overline{H}\;:=\;H/x^{n^{\prime}}H\;=\;k\langle\overline{x},\overline{g}\,:\,\overline{g}^{n}=1,\,\overline{x}^{n^{\prime}}=0,\,\overline{x}\overline{g}=q\overline{g}\overline{x}\rangle.

Thus, as a right
A
-module

, where
X
is the free right
A
-module on basis
\mathcal{P}
, where

\mathcal{P}\;:=\;\{g^{i}x^{j}:0\leq i<n,1\leq j<n^{\prime}\}\cup\{g^{i}-1:1\leq i<n\}.

One checks that
X
is a coideal of
H
, so that

satisfies
\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
. Moreover, it is clear that, for
0\leq i<n,\,0\leq j<n^{\prime}
, the map

\gamma:\overline{H}\longrightarrow H:\overline{g}^{i}\overline{x}^{j}\mapsto g^{i}x^{j}

is a cleaving map of coalgebras, so that Theorem
3.2
(2) applies, yielding the isomorphism of algebras

(7.43)

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\otimes A^{\circ}.

It is now straightforward to calculate that, as an algebra,

\overline{H}\;\cong\;T_{f}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime},q^{d})\#_{\tau}kC_{d},

where
\tau
is a cocycle, while as coalgebra

\overline{H}\;\cong\;T_{f}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime},q^{d})\otimes kC_{d}.

Since
\mathrm{gcd}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime})=1

T_{f}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime},q^{d})
is self-dual by
[
28
, Exercise 7.4.3]
, as also is

, so we deduce that, as an algebra,

(7.44)

\overline{H}^{\ast}\;\cong\;kC_{d}\otimes T_{f}(n^{\prime},t^{\prime},q^{d}).

Combining (
7.43
), (
7.44
) and the fact that
A^{\circ}=k[f]\otimes k(k,+)
, we deduce the algebra isomorphism in (III), with

a Hopf subalgebra by Lemma

(1). Moreover
A\subseteq H
also satisfies
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
since the action of
\overline{H}
on
A
factors through the group algebra

. Thus the claims regarding
W(H^{\circ})
and
\widehat{kG_{H}}
follow respectively from Theorems
4.3
(5) and
5.13
(2).

(IV) Let
H=B(n,w,q)
, so
H=\bigoplus_{0\leq i,j<n}y^{i}g^{j}A
is a free
A
-module, and

(7.45)

\overline{H}\;=\;H/A^{+}H\;\cong\;T_{f}(n,1,q).

As a complement
X
to
A
in
H
choose the
A
-module generated by

\{g^{i}y^{j}:0\leq i<n,1\leq j<n\}\cup\{g^{i}-1:1\leq i<n\}.

One checks routinely that
X

H

\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
holds, and Theorem

(1) implies that

H^{\circ}\;\cong\;\overline{H}^{\ast}\#A^{\circ}\;\cong\;T_{f}(n,1,q)\#(k[f]\otimes k(k^{\times})),

by (

Since

(V) The fact that

7.6.
Noetherian PI Hopf domains of Gelfand-Kirillov dimension two

Let
k
be algebraically closed of characteristic 0 and let
H
be a noetherian Hopf
k
-algebra domain with
\GKdim(H)=2
. Such Hopf algebras were classified in
[
17
]
under the additional assumption that
H
has an infinite dimensional commutative factor, or equivalently by
[
17
, Proposition 3.8(c)]
that
\Ext_{H}^{1}({{}_{H}k},{{}_{H}k})\neq 0.

The algebras in this classification which satisfy a polynomial identity (PI) are all easily seen to be commutative-by-finite, as we now indicate. Omitting the 2 group algebras and the rank 2 polynomial algebra, since these have already been discussed, the remaining PI families are as follows, using the numbering scheme of
[
17
]
:

(III)

Hopf algebras
A(\ell,n,q)
, where
\ell
and
n
are integers with
\ell>1
and
n>0
, and
q
is a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of 1 in
k
. As an algebra

A(\ell,n,q)\;:=\;k\langle x^{\pm 1},y:xy=qyx\rangle,

the localised quantum plane, with
x
group-like and
\Delta(y)=y\otimes 1+x^{n}\otimes y
. These algebras are free of finite rank over the normal commutative Hopf subalgebra
A:=k[x^{\pm\ell},y^{\ell^{\prime}}]
, where
\ell^{\prime}:=\ell/\gcd(n,\ell)
.

(IV)

Hopf algebras
B(n,p_{0},\dots,p_{s},q)
, where
s\geq 2
,
n,p_{0},\ldots,p_{s}
are positive integers with
p_{0}\mid n
and
\{p_{i}:i\geq 1\}
strictly increasing and pairwise relatively prime, and
q
is a primitive
\ell^{\mathrm{th}}
root of 1 where
\ell:=(n/p_{0})p_{1}\ldots p_{s}
. Then
B(n,p_{0},\dots,p_{s},q)
is the subalgebra of the localised quantum plane from (III) generated by
\{x^{\pm 1},y^{m_{i}}:1\leq i\leq s\}
, where
m_{i}:=\Pi_{j\neq i}p_{j}.
This is a Hopf subalgebra of
A(\ell,n,q)
, with
x
group-like and
\Delta(y^{m_{i}})=y\otimes 1+x^{m_{i}n}\otimes y
. One can easily check that
A:=k[y^{p_{1}\cdots p_{s}},x^{\pm\ell}]
is a normal commutative Hopf subalgebra over which
B(n,p_{0},\ldots,p_{s},q)
is a finite module.

We proceed to describe the Hopf duals of these algebras. Once again we leave some calculations to the reader in order to save space; details of the omitted arguments can be found in
[
19
]
or
[
11
]
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, and let
H
be a noetherian Hopf
k
-algebra domain of GK-dimension 2 with
\mathrm{Ext}_{H}(k,k)\neq 0

\mathbf{(CoSplit)}
and
\mathbf{(OrbSemi)}
, and so the Hopf dual

of

(III)

When

Then

,

(IV)

When

Proof.

(III) As noted above,

for a cocycle

\overline{H}\;\cong\;T_{f}(\ell^{\prime},n^{\prime},q^{-d})\otimes kC_{d}.

Since

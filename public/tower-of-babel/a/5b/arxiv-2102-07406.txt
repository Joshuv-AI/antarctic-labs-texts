Stable Systems with Power Law Conditions for Poisson Hail
Thomas Mountford, Zhe Wang

Abstract
We consider Poisson hail models and characterize up to boundaries the collection of critical moments which guarantee stability. In particular, we treat the case of infinite speed of propagation.

1.1
Model and dynamics

In this article we treat the Poisson hail model introduced in
[

We now define

From (

In this case, we also say
\tilde{P}

We are interested in the stability of systems. We stress that the notion of stability depends on the law
\tilde{P}
and not on the existence of a queueing model.
It is not clear that a queueing system can be defined when our dual model explodes. But for such a model to exist meaningfully it must have its workload given by (

By translation invariance
\tilde{P}
is stable if and only if for any

is tight.
The central question in this article is to understand the stability of the system.
It is natural to expect that if

does not grow fast in time, and it decreases due to no arrivals of job but if a job of large spatial size follows a job of large temporal size, the system has many sites with large workload.

\displaystyle e^{g_{1}b_{n}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}\mathbb{E}\left[(12^{d}e^{g_{1}}CS^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d}+\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d}}{K}}_{n+1})^{N_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}\right](12^{d}e^{g_{1}}CS^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d}+\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d}}{K}}_{n+1})^{L}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{g_{1}b_{n}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}\exp\left((12^{d}e^{g_{1}}CS^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d}+\frac{d}{K}}_{n+1})\lambda_{n+1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}+S_{n+1}^{-\alpha}\ln(12^{d}e^{g_{1}}CS_{n+1}^{d+\frac{d}{K}}){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}\right),

(5.37)

where in the last line, we use the fact that

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{bN_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}}\right]=\exp\left((e^{b}-1)\lambda_{n+1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}\right)\leq\begin{cases}\exp\left(3b\lambda_{n+1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}\right),\text{ when $b\leq 1$}\\
\exp\left(e^{b}\lambda_{n+1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}\right),\text{ when $b>1$}\end{cases}.

Since
\lambda_{n+1}S_{n+1}^{d+\frac{d}{K}}=\lambda S_{n+1}^{-(\alpha-\frac{d}{K})}<\lambda_{d,2}S_{n+1}^{-(\alpha-\frac{d}{K})}
, and
\ln(12^{d}e^{g_{1}}CS_{n+1}^{d+\frac{d}{K}})<C_{d,2}S_{n+1}^{\frac{d}{K}}
for some
C_{d,2}
depending on
d
, we bound (
5.37
) by

\exp\left((g_{1}b_{n}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{d,2}}S_{n+1}^{-(\alpha-\frac{d}{K})}){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}\right).

(5.38)

Since
X^{(n+1)}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}t}}
is super-additive, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{g_{1}X^{(n+1)}_{r+s}}\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[e^{g_{1}X^{(n+1)}_{r}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[e^{g_{1}X^{(n+1)}_{s}}\right]
, for any
r,s\geq 0
. Therefore, we also have for any
t>0
,

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{g_{1}X^{(n+1)}_{t}}\right]\leq\exp\left(\left(g_{1}b_{n}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{d,2}}S_{n+1}^{-(\alpha-\frac{d}{K})}\right)t\right),

for some
C_{d,2}
depending on
d
when
K>3
. Lastly, we use a change of variables to replace
g_{1}
by
a\cdot g_{1}
.

\square

A monotonicity version of a concavity theorem of Lieb
Eric A. Carlen

Abstract
We give a simple proof of a strengthened version of a theorem of Lieb that played a key role in the proof of strong subadditivity of the quantum entropy.

Introduction

We write
M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
to denote the
n\times n
complex matrices,
M_{n}^{+}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
the subset consisting of positive semidefinite matrices, and
M_{n}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
the
subset consisting of positive definite matrices. The following theorem was proved by Lieb in 1973
[
7
, Theorem 6]
.

1.1 THEOREM

(Lieb)
.

For all self-adjoint
H\in M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
the function

Y\mapsto{\rm Tr}\left[\exp(H+\log Y)\right]

(1.1)

is concave on
M_{n}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
.

As a simple consequence of this, Lieb deduced his
triple matrix inequality
, a generalization of the Golden-Thompson inequality to
three self adjoint matrices. This played a fundamental role in the proof of strong subadditivity of the quantum entropy
[
8
]
. For more recent applications of Theorem
1.1
, see the influential paper of Tropp
[
14
]
.

We now prove a stronger version of this theorem in terms of monotonicity instead of concavity.
Recall that a linear map
\Phi:M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\to M_{m}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
is
positive
if
\Phi(A)\in M_{m}^{+}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
whenever
A\in M_{n}^{+}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
, and is unital if
\Phi(I)=I
, and

trace preserving
if
{\rm Tr}[\Phi(X)]={\rm Tr}[X]
for all
X\in M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
. We equip
M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
with the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product, and we use
\Phi^{\dagger}
to denote the corresponding adjoint of a linear map on
M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
. Note that
\Phi
is unital if and only if
\Phi^{\dagger}
is trace preserving. Our main result is:

1.2 THEOREM
.

Let
\Phi:M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\to M_{m}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
be unital and positive. Then for all self-adjoint
H\in M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
and all
Y\in M_{m}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
,

{\rm Tr}\left[\exp(H+\log\Phi^{\dagger}(Y))\right]\geq{\rm Tr}\left[\exp(\Phi(H)+\log Y)\right]\ .

(1.2)

Before giving the very simple proof, we explain how Theorem
1.2
implies Theorem
1.1
. Let
\Phi:M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\to M_{2n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
be defined by

\Phi(H)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}H&0\\
0&H\end{array}\right]\quad{\rm and\ hence}\quad\Phi^{\dagger}\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}Y_{1}&0\\
0&Y_{2}\end{array}\right]\right)=Y_{1}+Y_{2}\ .

(1.3)

Thus,

{\rm Tr}[e^{H+\log(Y_{1}+Y_{2})}]\geq{\rm Tr}[e^{H+\log Y_{1}}]+{\rm Tr}[e^{H+\log Y_{2}}]\ .

Since
Y\mapsto{\rm Tr}[e^{H+\log Y}]
is homogeneous of degree one, this is the same as concavity.
Note that this particular map
\Phi
is not only positive; it is completely positive.

Our proof is based on the well-known and elementary Gibbs variational principle for the free energy in terms of the entropy
S(X)=-{\rm Tr}[X\log X]
of a density matrix
X
. This states that for all self-adjoint
K\in M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})

\log({\rm Tr}[e^{K}])=\sup\{{\rm Tr}[XK]-{\rm Tr}[X\log X]\ :\ X\in M_{n}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\ ,{\rm Tr}[X]=1\ \}\ .

(1.4)

A short proof from scratch can be found in Appendix A of
[
5
]
. There is a simple variant involving the relative entropy

D(X||Y)={\rm Tr}[X(\log X-\log Y)]

of two density matrices:
For
W\in M_{n}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
replace
K
with
K+\log W
in (
1.4
) to conclude that
for all self-adjoint
K\in M_{n}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
and all
W\in M_{m}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})
,

\log({\rm Tr}[e^{K+\log W}])=\sup\{{\rm Tr}[XK]-D(X||W)\ :\ X\in M_{n}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\ ,{\rm Tr}[X]=1\ \}\ .

(1.5)

We shall also use the result due to Müller-Hermes and Reeb
[
10
]
that the relative entropy is monotone under positive trace-preserving maps

\Phi^{\dagger}
.
That is, for all such maps
\Phi^{\dagger}
, and all density matrices
X,Y

D(\Phi^{\dagger}(X)||\Phi^{\dagger}(Y))\leq D(X||Y)\ .

(1.6)

A somewhat weaker result is due to Uhlmann, who proved in 1977 that (
1.6
) is true whenever

\Phi^{\dagger}
is the dual of a unital
Schwarz map
; i.e., a unital map
\Phi
such that
\Phi(A^{*}A)\geq\Phi(A)^{*}\Phi(A)
. Earlier still in 1973,
Lindblad had shown that (
1.6
) was valid unital completely positive
\Phi
. Lindblad’s proof relied on the Lieb Concavity Theorem
[
7
]
.
It is well known that every completely positive unital map is a Schwarz map, and it is evident that Schwarz maps are positive.
By now, quite simple proofs of the monotonicity of the relative entropy under the dual of a unital Schwarz map are known; see e.g.,
[
11
]
. The deep result in
[
10
]
turns on ideas introduced by Beigi
[
2
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By (
1.5
),

\displaystyle\log({\rm Tr}[e^{H+\log\Phi^{\dagger}(Y)}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{{\rm Tr}[XH]-D(X||\Phi^{\dagger}(Y))\ :\ X\in M_{n}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\ ,{\rm Tr}[X]=1\ \}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup\{{\rm Tr}[\Phi^{\dagger}(W)H]-D(\Phi^{\dagger}(W)||\Phi^{\dagger}(Y))\ :\ W\in M_{m}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\ ,{\rm Tr}[W]=1\ \}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup\{{\rm Tr}[W\Phi(H)]-D(W||Y)\ :\ W\in M_{m}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\ ,{\rm Tr}[W]=1\ \}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log{\rm Tr}\left[e^{\Phi(H)+\log Y}\right]\ .

where we used
\{\ \Phi^{\dagger}(W)\ :\ W\in M_{m}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\ ,{\rm Tr}[W]=1\ \}\subset\{\ X\in M_{n}^{++}({\mathord{\mathbb{C}}})\ ,{\rm Tr}[X]=1\ \}
and (
1.6
).
Exponentiating both sides of the inequality yields (
1.2
).
∎

While Theorem
1.2
is strictly stronger than Theorem
1.1
, the greatest interest in it may lie in the very simple proof that its proof provides of Theorem
1.1
. This is an interesting example of how it may be easiest to prove a concavity result by first proving a monotonicity result, and then applying that to the particular map
\Phi
that is defined in (
1.3
).

It is interesting to observe another advantage of the monotonicity approach to convexity or concavity inequalities. A duality method for proving convexity and concavity inequalities was introduced by myself and Lieb in
[
4
]
which uses the lemma:

1.3 LEMMA
.

If
f(x,y)
is jointly convex in
x,y
, then
g(x):=\inf_{y}f(x,y)
is convex. If
f(x,y)
is jointly concave in
x,y
, then
g(x):=\sup_{y}f(x,y)
is concave.

This may be found in
[
12
, Theorem 1]
, and the simple proof is also given in
[
4
]
. Since
(X,W)\mapsto D(X||W)
, is jointly convex,
for fixed
K
,

(X,W)\mapsto{\rm Tr}[XK]-D(X||W)
, is jointly concave. Then by Lemma
1.3
and (
1.5
),
W\mapsto\log{\rm Tr}[e^{K+\log W}]
is concave.
However, this is a weaker statement than Theorem
1.1
since if
g(x)=\log(f(x))
with
f
positive and twice continuously differentiable.

g^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(x)}{f(x)}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{f(x)}f^{\prime\prime}(x)\ .

Thus, concavity of
f
implies concavity of
\log f
, but not the other way around. However, monotonicity of
f
is equivalent to monotonicity of
\log f
.
For this reason, we could simply use the Gibbs variational principle to prove our Theorem
1.2
. Tropp
[
13
]
found an ingenious variational representation of
W\mapsto{\rm Tr}[e^{K+\log W}]
which allowed him to give a proof of Theorem
1.1
using the joint convexity of the relative entropy and Lemma
1.3
, along the lines of
[
4
]
. However, when using duality to to prove the monotonicity theorem, the logarithm is not an issue because log monotonicity is the same as monotonicity, and we do not need Lemma
1.3
. Already in 1973 Epstein
[
6
]
gave a second proof of Theorem
1.1
using the theory of Herglotz functions, but this is considerably more involved than the present proof. For more infromation, see
[
3
]
.

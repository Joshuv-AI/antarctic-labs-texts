Radiative corrections to di-meson tau decays
A. Miranda \from ins:x

Abstract
We review radiative corrections to tau decays into two mesons discussing their impact in new physics searches.

Introduction

Radiative corrections are needed to make new physics tests more precise and required to get the most from experimental data with below percent accuracy.

The tau is the only lepton massive enough to decay into mesons, giving thus access to CKM unitarity tests (through
V_{us}

V_{us}/V_{ud}

One-meson processes depend only on the corresponding meson decay constant, which is best determined on the lattice (without any pollution from possible new physics)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}=&\frac{eG_{F}V^{*}_{ud}}{\sqrt{2}}\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}\bigg[(V^{\mu\nu}-A^{\mu\nu})\bar{u}(q^{\prime})\gamma_{\nu}(1-\gamma^{5})u(P)\\[4.30554pt]

(1)

H^{\nu}(p_{-},p_{0})=C_{V}F_{+}(t)Q^{\nu}+C_{S}\frac{\Delta_{{-}{0}}}{t}q^{\nu}F_{0}(t)\ ,

(2)

Decay rate and spectra

g^{\mu\nu}
part in Eq. (

K^{-}\pi^{0}

We emphasize the importance of any measurement of these decays to reduce the uncertainty on the radiative corrections and therefore increase the reach on new physics searches (as

4
Radiative corrections

The
G_{\text{EM}}(t)
function encapsulating the long-distance electromagnetic corrections (from on- and off-shell photons) is defined in the usual way
[
29
]

\begin{split}\frac{d\Gamma}{dt}\Bigg|_{PP(\gamma)}&=\frac{G_{F}^{2}\left|V_{uD}F_{+}(0)\right|^{2}S_{\rm EW}m_{\tau}^{3}}{768\pi^{3}t^{3}}\left(1-\frac{t}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)^{2}\lambda^{1/2}(t,m_{-}^{2},m_{0}^{2})\\[4.30554pt]
&\quad\times\left[C_{V}^{2}|\tilde{F}_{+}(t)|^{2}\left(1+\frac{2t}{m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)\lambda(t,m_{-}^{2},m_{0}^{2})+3C_{S}^{2}\Delta_{-0}^{2}|\tilde{F}_{0}(t)|^{2}\right]G_{\rm EM}(t)\ ,\end{split}

(4)

where
S_{EW}
encodes the universal short-distance electroweak corrections
[
30
,
31
]
,
V_{uD}\,(D=d,s)
is the appropriate CKM matrix element and
G_{F}
is the Fermi constant. We also divide
G_{\text{EM}}(t)
into a part including the non-radiative plus the virtual contributions together with the leading Low approximation,
G_{\text{EM}}^{(0)}(t)
, and
\delta G_{\text{EM}}(t)
, which has all remaining pieces.

G_{\text{EM}}^{(0)}(t)

[

8
]

K\pi

\delta_{\rm EM}^{PP}

\Gamma_{PP(\gamma)}=\frac{G_{F}^{2}S_{\rm EW}m_{\tau}^{5}}{96\pi^{3}}\left|V_{uD}F_{+}(0)\right|^{2}I^{\tau}_{PP}\left(1+\delta_{\rm EM}^{PP}\right)^{2}\ ,

(5)

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm EM}^{K^{-}K^{0}}=-\left(0.030^{+0.026}_{-0.179}\right)\%\ ,\qquad\delta_{\rm EM}^{\pi^{-}\pi^{0}}=-\left(0.186^{+0.024}_{-0.169}\right)\%\ ,

\tau^{-}\to\bar{u}d\nu_{\tau}

\Lambda\gtrsim[2,4]

90\%
C.L., and radiative corrections do not yet affect these limits significantly
[

6
Conclusions

We have first computed the complete radiative corrections to the
\tau^{-}\to(PP^{\prime})^{-}\nu_{\tau}
decays, including the structure-dependent corrections, complying with the constraints from chiral symmetry at low
t
and with Low’s and Burnett-Kroll theorems at low
E_{\gamma}
. Our main results are Eqs. (
4
), complementing previous evaluations for the one-meson modes by our group
[
2
,
3
]
, enabling more precise new physics searches.

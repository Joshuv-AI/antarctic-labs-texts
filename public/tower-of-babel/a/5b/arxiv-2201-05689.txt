New fixed point theorems for ( ϕ , F ) − (\phi,F)- contraction on rectangular b-metric spaces
Mohamed Rossafi 1∗ and Abdelkarim Kari 2

Abstract
. The Banach contraction principle is the most celebrated fixed point theorem, it has been generalized in various directions. In this paper, inspired by the concept of ( ϕ , F ) − (\phi,F)- contraction in metric spaces, introduced by Wardowski. We present the notion of ( ϕ , F ) − (\phi,F)- contraction in b − b- rectangular metric spaces to study the existence and uniqueness of fixed point for the mappings in this spaces. Our results improve many existing results.

3.
Main result

Corollary 3.3
.

Let

be a complete b-rectangular metric space with parameter

and let
T
be a self mapping on
\ X
. If for all

Proof.

\displaystyle ln(sd\left(Tx,Ty\right))

In this section, we apply our results to solve the following nonlinear integral equation problem:

4.
Application to nonlinear integral equations

(4.1)

x(t)=\lambda\int_{a}^{b}K(t,r,x(r))ds,

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in C(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R})
and
K:\left[a,b\right]^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a given continuous function.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider the nonlinear integral equation problem:
(4.1)
and assume that the kernel function
K
satisfies the condition

|K(t,r,x(r))-K(t,r,y(r))|\leq\frac{1}{s^{2+s}}e^{-\frac{1}{|x(t)-y(t)|+1}}\left(|x(t)-y(t)|\right)

for all
t,r\in\left[a,b\right]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the equation
(4.1)
has a unique solution
x\in C(\left[a,b\right]
for some constant
\lambda
depending on the constants
a,b\ and\ s
.

Proof.

Let
X=C(\left[a,b\right]
and
T:X\rightarrow X
defined by

T(x)(t)=\lambda\int_{a}^{b}K(t,r,x(r))ds,

for all
x\in X
. Clearly,
X
with the metric
d:X\times X\rightarrow\left[0,+\infty\right[
given by

d(x,y)=\left(\max_{t\in\left[a,b\right]}|x(t)-y(t)|\right)^{s},

for all
x,y\in X
. It is clear that
X,d
is a complete
b
-rectangular metric space.

We will find the condition on
\lambda
under which the operator has a unique fixed point which will the solution of the integral equation
(4.1)
. Assume that,
x,y\in X
and
t,r\in\left[a,b\right]
. Then we get

\displaystyle|Tx(t)-Ty(t)|^{s}

\displaystyle=|\lambda|^{s}\left(|\int_{a}^{b}K(t,r,x(r))dr-\int_{a}^{b}K(t,r,y(r))dr|\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=|\lambda^{s}||\int_{a}^{b}K(t,r,x(r))-K(t,r,y(r))dr|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|^{s}\int_{a}^{b}|K(t,r,x(r))-K(t,r,y(r))dr|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|^{s}\int_{a}^{b}\left(\frac{1}{s^{2+s}}e^{-\frac{1}{|x(r)-y(r)|+1}}\left(|x(r)|-|y(r)|\right)dr\right)^{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s^{2}}|\lambda|^{s}\int_{a}^{b}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{|x(r)-y(r)|+1}}\left(|x(r)|-|y(r)|\right)\right)dr.

Which implies that

\displaystyle\max_{t\in\left[a,b\right]}\left(|Tx(t)-Ty(t)|\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{t\in\left[a,b\right]}|\lambda|^{s}\int_{a}^{b}|K(t,r,x(r))-K(t,r,y(r))dr|^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{t\in\left[a,b\right]}\frac{1}{s^{2}}|\lambda|^{s}\int_{a}^{b}\left(e^{-\frac{1}{|x(r)-y(r)|+1}}\left(|x(r)-y(r)|\right)dr\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda|^{s}\frac{1}{s^{2}}\int_{a}^{b}\left(e^{-\max_{s\in\left[a,b\right]}\frac{1}{|x(r)-y(r)|+1}}\left(\max_{r\in\left[a,b\right]}|x(r)-y(r)|\right)dr\right)^{s}.

b

\displaystyle=ln\left[s^{2}|\lambda|^{s}\max_{t\in\left[a,b\right]}\int_{a}^{b}|K(t,r,x(r))-K(t,r,y(r))dr|^{s}\right]

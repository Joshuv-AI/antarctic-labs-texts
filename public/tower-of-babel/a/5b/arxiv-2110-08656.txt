Newton Polygons of Sums on Curves I: Local-to-Global Theorems
Joe Kramer-Miller and James Upton

Abstract
The purpose of this article is to study Newton polygons of certain abelian L L -functions on curves. Let X X be a smooth affine curve over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} and let ρ : π 1 ​ ( X ) → ℂ p × \rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times} be a finite character of order p n p^{n} . By previous work of the first author, the Newton polygon NP ⁡ ( ρ ) \NP(\rho) lies above a ‘Hodge polygon’ HP ⁡ ( ρ ) \HP(\rho) , which is defined using local ramification invariants of ρ \rho . In this article we study the touching between these two polygons. We prove that NP ⁡ ( ρ ) \NP(\rho) and HP ⁡ ( ρ ) \HP(\rho) share a vertex if and only if a corresponding vertex is shared between the Newton and Hodge polygons of ‘local’ L L -functions associated to each ramified point of ρ \rho . As a consequence, we determine a necessary and sufficient condition for the coincidence of NP ⁡ ( ρ ) \NP(\rho) and HP ⁡ ( ρ ) \HP(\rho) .

1.1
Motivation

Let
p

be a power of
p

of the étale fundamental group of

A fundamental problem in arithmetic geometry is to understand the character sums

\displaystyle S_{k}(X,\rho)=\sum_{x\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}})}\rho(\text{Frob}_{x}),

where
\text{Frob}_{x}
denotes the Frobenius element at

. By the Weil conjectures, there exist algebraic integers
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d_{1}},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{d_{2}}

such that

\displaystyle S_{k}(X,\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}\beta_{i}^{k}-\sum_{i=1}^{d_{2}}\alpha_{i}^{k}.

What can we say about these algebraic integers? The Riemann Hypothesis states
that the
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{i}
are
q
-Weil numbers, i.e. that there exist positive integers

a_{1},\dots,a_{d_{1}},b_{1},\dots,b_{d_{2}}

|\cdot|_{\infty}

we have
|\alpha_{i}|_{\infty}=q^{\frac{a_{i}}{2}}

We also understand what happens at the finite places away from

\alpha_{i}
and the

are
\ell
-adic units for any prime
\ell\neq p

The

-adic properties of these numbers are less definitive.
There is no exact formula as in the
\ell
-adic and Archimedean cases.
In general, the best one can hope for are lower bounds on the
p
-adic valuations, i.e.,
that the Newton polygon of
\rho
lies above some sort of Hodge polygon. Under additional
congruence conditions involving

-adic valuations (i.e. that the Newton and Hodge
polygons coincide). The classical approach to ‘Newton over Hodge’ results for exponential sums has been restricted to the
case where

is an algebraic torus. This wonderful theory was developed by Robba, Adolphson-Sperber, Wan,
and others, building off of ideas of Dwork.
For instance, Adolphson-Sperber prove a general ‘Newton over Hodge’ result for exponential sums over
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}

(see
[
1
]
) and Wan establishes a criterion for equality of the two polygons (see
[
27
]
).
Outside of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d}
case nothing was known until recent work of the first author (see
[
16
]

and
[
17
]
). In these articles Kramer-Miller establishes a ‘Newton over Hodge’ theorem for
abelian
L
-functions on curves of any genus. Unfortunately, this work ignores the question of touching or equality
between these two polygons.

The purpose of this article is to study touching between the Newton and Hodge polygons
of character sums on higher genus curves. Our main result establishes a local criterion that is necessary and sufficient for the Newton and Hodge polygons to touch at a vertex. It roughly states the following: Assume the compactification of
X
is an ordinary curve
\overline{X}
.

Main results

16

.

.

1.3
Outline of proof

The starting point of our proof is the method developed by the first author in
[
17
]
and
[
16
]
.
However, the methods in this earlier work only produce lower bounds on
\NP_{q}(\rho)
, and are woefully inadequate
for pinpointing exact vertices. The necessary new ingredient is a perturbation theory for
p
-adic operators, which allows us to relate the Newton polygons
\NP_{q}(\rho)
and
\NP_{q}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})

Characters and Liftings

Our general approach is to study
L(\rho,s)
via the Monsky trace formula, which expresses
L(\rho,s)
as a ratio of Fredholm determinants of operators. This requires
us to lift the Frobenius on
U
to characteristic
0
. We use the lifting construction developed by the first author in
[
17
]
. Choose a tame Belyi map
\eta:\overline{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
for which
\eta(P)=0
for all
P\in S
. Let
U\subseteq X
denote the étale locus of
\eta
. Let
A
be a smooth lifting of the coordinate ring of
U
to characteristic
0
, and write
A^{\dagger}
for the
p
-adic weak completion of
A
(see §
2.1
). Using
\eta
we construct a lifting of Frobenius
\sigma:A^{\dagger}\to A^{\dagger}
whose local expression at each
P\in S
is particularly simple, see §
4.3
.

Let
\rho_{U}
denote the restriction of
\rho
to
\pi_{1}(U)
. Let
\pi\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\rho]
be a uniformizer, and consider the base change

A_{\pi}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\rho]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}A^{\dagger}.

In this setting, the Monsky trace formula states that there is a
\mathbb{Z}_{q}[\rho]
-linear endomorpshism
\Theta_{q}:A_{\pi}^{\dagger}\to A_{\pi}^{\dagger}
for which

\displaystyle L(\rho_{U},s)

\displaystyle=\frac{\det(1-s\Theta_{q}|A_{\pi}^{\dagger}[\tfrac{1}{\pi}])}{\det(1-qs\Theta_{q}|A_{\pi}^{\dagger}[\tfrac{1}{\pi}])}.

The Space
A_{\pi}^{\dagger}

It follows from the trace formula that the truncated Newton polygon
\NP_{q}^{<1}(\rho_{U})
agrees with that of the characteristic series
\det(1-s\Theta_{q}|A_{\pi}^{\dagger}[\tfrac{1}{\pi}])
. Thus, our goal in this paper is to estimate the Newton polygon of this series. In the classical situation
X=\mathbb{G}_{m}
, the ring
A^{\dagger}
may be identified with the
p
-adic weak completion of the ring of Laurent polynomials
\mathbb{Z}_{q}[t^{\pm}]
. The
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-module structure of
A^{\dagger}
is quite explicit, as the elements

for

form a natural topological basis. Choosing a suitable basis for the lifting

is a key point of the papers
[
17
]
and
[
16
]
, where a Riemann-Roch argument is used to construct a basis with prescribed poles at each branch point
P
of
\eta
.

For each such
P
, choose a local parameter
t_{P}
at
P
and consider the ring
\mathcal{A}_{P}=\mathbb{Z}_{q}(\!(t_{P})\!)
. This ring is a lifting of the local field

to characteristic

, and there is a unique map
A^{\dagger}\to\mathcal{A}_{P}^{\dagger}
given by “expanding functions in the parameter

”. The Riemann-Roch theorem implies that there is an exact sequence of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-modules

0\to L\to A^{\dagger}\to\bigoplus_{P}\mathcal{A}_{P}^{\dagger,\mathrm{tr}}\to 0,

where
L
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-module of finite rank, and
\mathcal{A}_{P}^{\dagger,\mathrm{tr}}
is a space of truncated Laurent series in

with prescribed poles. The space

admits a topological basis of the form

, where
\mu(P)
is a parameter depending on the branching of the map

P
. Is is then straightforward to “lift” these bases to produce a global basis

.

1.4
Applications to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers of curves

Our primary motivation for this article is to study the
p
-adic variation of
L
-functions
along
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers of curves. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower

X_{\infty}/X
is a sequence of finite Galois coverings

\cdots\to X_{2}\to X_{1}\to X_{0}=X,

together with compatible identifications
\mathrm{Gal}(X_{n}/X)\cong\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
for all
n
. Equivalently, a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower over
X
corresponds to a continuous and surjective map
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We will typically regard
X_{\infty}/X
as a family of characters of
\pi_{1}(X)
as follows: For every continuous
p
-adic character
\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
, we obtain a composite character

\rho_{\chi}=\chi\circ\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}.

Daqing Wan has devised a program (see
[
28
]
) to understand how the Newton polygon
\NP_{q}(\rho_{\chi})
varies as a function of
\chi
. The general philosophy is that for well behaved towers we expect
the Newton polygons to exhibit a certain degree of regularity as
\chi
varies. Recently, there has been much progress
surrounding the case
X=\mathbb{A}^{1}
(see e.g.
[
6
]
for one of the first articles on the topic or
[
14
]
for results on a general class of towers). However, essentially nothing is known for higher genus curves
or when multiple points along the tower are ramified. The local-to-global results of this article allow us to
approach these questions when the base curve is ordinary. This is the content of the sequel articles
[
18
]
and
[
25
]
.

1.4.1
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Towers with Strictly Stable Monodromy

Let
\bm{\delta}=(\delta_{P})_{P\in S}
be a tuple of positive rational numbers in
\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac{1}{p}]
. We say that the tower
X_{\infty}/X
has
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy
if for each
P\in S
, the highest ramification break of

at
P
is of the form
p^{n-1}\delta_{P}
for all

Theorem 1.6

(
[
18
]
)
.

Suppose that
\overline{X}
is ordinary. Let
X_{\infty}/X
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of curves with
\bm{\delta}
-stable monodromy. Then for a finite character
\chi:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times}
of order
p^{n}
:

1.

(Slope Uniformity) The
q
-adic Newton slopes of
\rho_{\chi}
are equidistributed in the interval
[0,1]
as
n\to\infty
.

2.

(Slope Stability) Suppose that
\delta_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}
and that
\delta_{P}\equiv 1\pmod{p}
for all

. Then
\NP_{q}(\rho_{\chi})
has slope set

\{\underbrace{0,...,0}_{g-1+|S|}\}\sqcup\{\underbrace{1,...,1}_{g-1+|S|}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{P\in S}\left\{\frac{1}{\delta_{P}p^{n-1}},\dots\frac{\delta_{P}p^{n-1}-1}{\delta_{P}p^{n-1}}\right\}.

In particular, the invariants
\delta_{1},...,\delta_{P}
completely determine the Newton polygon of each curve
X_{n}
.

This theorem generalizes work of Kosters-Zhu (see
[
14
]
) for
\bm{\delta}
-stable towers over
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
.
To the best of our knowledge, there were no prior examples of slope uniform or slope stable towers beyond the
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
case. Using the methods of
[
16
]
we obtain similar theorems for
\bm{\delta}
-stable towers twisted by a tame finite character
\psi:\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

1.4.2
Overconvergent
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Towers

Any tower
X_{\infty}/X
is determined by an Artin-Schreier-Witt equation

, where

is an element of the

-typical Witt vectors of the coordinate ring of

-stable for some

-stable towers are not overconvergent.

A notable class of examples are the towers over

studied in work of Davis-Wan-Xiao
[
6
]
. Those authors prove that a special class of towers are slope-uniform and slope-stable. In fact, they prove a beautiful
spectral halo theorem
which describes the
p
-adic variation of the zero locus of
L(\rho_{\chi},s)
as
\chi
varies through the character space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
. In the final part of this series of articles
[
25
]
, the second author shows that the spectral halo theorem holds for a general overconvergent tower
X_{\infty}/\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
. By combining this result with our local-to-global theorems, we obtain the following stability result for overconvergent towers over an ordinary curve:

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose that
X
is ordinary. Let
X_{\infty}/X
be an overconvergent
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of curves. There exist a non-negative integer
m_{0}
and postivie rational numbers
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{d}
with the following property: For every
\chi
of order
p^{n}
with
n>m_{0}
, the slope set of
\NP_{q}(\rho_{\chi})
is

\{\underbrace{0,...,0}_{g-1}\}\sqcup\{\underbrace{1,...,1}_{g-1+|S|}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{P\in S}\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{p^{n-m_{0}}-1}\left\{\frac{i}{p^{n-m_{0}-1}},\frac{\alpha_{1}+i}{p^{n-m_{0}-1}},...,\frac{\alpha_{d}+i}{p^{n-m_{0}-1}}\right\}.

As a byproduct of our approach, we obtain similar estimates for the Newton polygon
\NP_{q}^{<1}(\rho_{\chi})
for
any

\chi
(finite or infinite) near the boundary of the character space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mathbb{C}_{p}^{\times})
. This may be regarded as a “small-slope” analogue of the spectral halo theorem of Davis-Wan-Xiao. Unfortunately, our perturbation approach does not produce any information regarding the zeroes of
L(\rho_{\chi},s)
outside of the disk
v_{p}(s)>-v_{p}(q)
. It seems likely that new techniques are necessary to study zeros in this region.

2
Growth Conditions

Throughout this article, our basic coefficient ring will be a complete discrete valuation ring
R
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(we do not assume that
R
has characteristic
0
). We fix a non-zero topologically nilpotent element
\pi\in R
. We will frequently work with algebras of functions over
R
, which are
\pi
-adically overconvergent. In this section we spell out our conventions regarding these “growth conditions” and introduce some basic constructions.

2.1
Weak Completions

Let
A
be an
R
-algebra equipped with the
\pi
-adic topology. We will write
A^{\infty}
for the
\pi
-adic completion of
A
. Given real numbers
m
,
b
with
m>0
, let
A^{m}(b)
denote the set of
a\in A^{\infty}
with the following property: there exist
a_{1},...,a_{n}\in A
and polynomials
p_{j}\in\pi^{j}R[X_{1},...,X_{n}]
such that

a=\sum_{j}p_{j}(a_{1},...,a_{n})

(2)

and
\deg(p_{j})<mj+b
. Then each
A^{m}(b)
is a
\pi
-adically complete submodule of
A^{\infty}
. The following basic properties are immediate:

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
m>0
,

1.

A^{m}(b_{1})\cdot A^{m}(b_{2})\subseteq A^{m}(b_{1}+b_{2})
.

2.

\pi^{k}A^{m}(b)\subseteq A^{m}(b-km)
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
weak completion

A^{\dagger}
of
A
is the union of the submodules
A^{m}(b)
.

It is clear from Lemma
2.1
that
A^{\dagger}
is a subring of
A^{\infty}
and is independent of the choice of
\pi\in R
. By
[
21
, Theorem 1.4]
, the natural map
A/\pi A\to A^{\dagger}/\pi A^{\dagger}
is an isomorphism. Let us say that a subset
S\subseteq A^{\dagger}
is a set of
weak generators
for
A^{\dagger}
if every
a\in A^{\dagger}
has the form (
2
), where the
a_{i}
are elements of
S
. For our purposes we will only consider weak completions in two situations:

1.

Let
A
be an
R
-algebra such that
A^{\dagger}
admits a finite set of weak generators. In this case a theorem of Fulton
[
8
]
guarantees that
A^{\dagger}
is Noetherian.

2.

Let
A=R_{q}(\!(t)\!)
be the ring of formal Laurent series over
R_{q}
. Then

A^{\dagger}\cong(R_{q}\llbracket t\rrbracket\otimes_{R}R[X]^{\dagger})/(tX-1).

This is once again a Noetherian ring. In this case we have:

A^{m}(b)=\left\{\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{k}t^{-k}:v_{\pi}(a_{k})\geq\frac{k-b}{m}\text{ for }k>0\right\}.

2.2
Weak Base Change

A typical situation for us will be as follows: Let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra, equipped with the
p
-adic topology. We define the base change
A_{\pi}=R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}A
, which we equip with the
\pi
-adic topology. We will always assume that the modules
A^{m}(b)
are given by
p
-adic growth conditions, whereas the
A_{\pi}^{m}(b)
are given by
\pi
-adic growth conditions (hence the choice of subscript).

Since many of our constructions will be given initially over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, it is convenient to have a “comparison” between the weak completions
A^{\dagger}
and
A_{\pi}^{\dagger}
. First, we require a basic result from commutative algebra:

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider an exact sequence of
R
-modules

0\to L\to M\xrightarrow{f}N.

(3)

Let
\overline{f}:\overline{M}\to\overline{M}
denote the reduction of
f
modulo
\pi
. Then:

1.

If
M
is
\pi
-adically complete,
N
is
\pi
-adically separated, and
\overline{f}
is surjective, then
f
is surjective.

2.

If
N
is
\pi
-torsion free and
f
is surjective, then
\overline{L}=\ker(\overline{f})
.

3.

If
M
and
N
are
\pi
-torsion free,
M
is
\pi
-adically separated, and
\overline{f}
is injective, then
f
is injective.

Proof.

Let
n\in N
. Since
\overline{f}
is surjective, there exists
m_{0}\in M
such that
n=f(m_{0})+\pi n_{1}
, for some
n_{1}\in N
. Repeating this procedure, we inductively construct
m_{i}\in M
such that
n_{i}=f(m_{i})+\pi n_{i+1}
. Since
M
is
\pi
-adically complete, the sum

m=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}m_{i}\pi^{i}

converges in
M
. The difference
f(m)-n
vanishes because
N
is
\pi
-adically separated, proving (
1
). Since
R
is a discrete valuation ring, if
N
is
\pi
-torsion free then
N
is flat over
R
. Claim (
2
) then follows by tensoring (
3
) with
R/\pi R
.

Suppose then that
M
and
N
are
\pi
-torsion free,
M
is
\pi
-adically separated, and that
\overline{f}
is injective. Since
M
and
N
are flat over
R
, by tensoring with the exact sequence

0\to\pi^{j+1}R\to\pi^{j}R\to\pi^{j}R/\pi^{j+1}R\to 0,

we have an identification
\pi^{j}M/\pi^{j+1}M\cong\pi^{j}R/\pi^{j+1}R\otimes_{R/\pi R}\overline{M}
and similarly for
N
. Since
R
is a discrete valuation ring,
\pi^{j}R/\pi^{j+1}R
is flat over
R/\pi R
for all
j\geq 0
. By tensoring
\overline{f}
with
\pi^{j}R/\pi^{j+1}R
, and using the above identification, we see that
f
induces an injective map

\pi^{j}M/\pi^{j+1}M\to\pi^{j}N/\pi^{j+1}N.

It follows that the kernel of
f
lies in
\bigcap_{j}\pi^{j}M=0
. Since
M
is
\pi
-adically separated, this proves (
3
).
∎

By base change, the natural map
A\to A^{\dagger}
induces a map
A_{\pi}\to(A^{\dagger})_{\pi}
. Passing to weak completions, we obtain a map

A_{\pi}^{\dagger}\to(A^{\dagger})_{\pi}^{\dagger}.

(4)

Our “comparison” between
A^{\dagger}
and
A_{\pi}^{\dagger}
is provided by the following:

Lemma 2.4

(Weak Base Change)
.

Suppose that
A
is flat over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then the map (
4
) is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The flatness condition guarantees that
A_{\pi}^{\dagger}
and
(A^{\dagger})_{\pi}^{\dagger}
are
\pi
-torsion free and
\pi
-adically separated. By Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to prove that (
4
) is surjective. Since
(A^{\dagger})_{\pi}
is a set of weak generators for
(A^{\dagger})_{\pi}^{\dagger}
, and since the image of (
4
) is weakly complete, we only need to show that the image contains
(A^{\dagger})_{\pi}
. Now
A
constitutes a set of weak generators for
A^{\dagger}
, so every element of
(A^{\dagger})_{\pi}
is a finite sum of elements of the form

r\otimes\sum_{j}p_{j}(a_{1},...,a_{n}),

where
r\in R
,
a_{1},...,a_{n}\in A
and
p_{j}\in p^{j}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[X_{1},...,X_{n}]
has degree
\leq mj+b
. Since
p
is topologically nilpotent in
R
, we see that this is exactly the image of the convergent sum

\sum_{j}(r\otimes p_{j})(a_{1},...,a_{n})\in A_{\pi}^{\dagger},

where we regard
r\otimes p_{j}
as a polynomial in
R[X_{1},...,X_{n}]
.
∎

3
Fredholm Theory

Let
K=R[\tfrac{1}{\pi}]
denote the field of fractions of
R
. If
q
is a power of
p
, then we define the ring
R_{q}=\mathbb{Z}_{q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}R
and its field of fractions

. We will now discuss the Fredholm theory of nuclear operators acting on
K_{q}
-vector spacse and the corresponding theory of Newton and Hodge polygons of such operators.

3.1
Spectral Theory of Completely Continuous Operators

3.1.1
Normed Vector Spaces

Definition 3.1
.

A
normed vector space
over
K_{q}
is a
K_{q}
-vector space of the form
V=K\otimes_{R}M
, where
M
is a
\pi
-adically separated
R_{q}
-module. We say that
V
is a
Banach space
over
K_{q}
if
M
is
\pi
-adically complete.

Let
V=K\otimes_{R}M
be a normed vector space over
K_{q}
. We topologize

by taking the
R_{q}
-submodules
\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
to be a fundamental system of neighborhoods of

in
V
. As the terminology suggests, the choice of

uniquely determines a norm
\|\cdot\|_{\pi}
on
V
defined as follows: If
\pi R=\mathfrak{m}^{d}R
, then we define the
\pi
-adic valuation
on
V
via

v_{\pi}(x)=\frac{1}{d}\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:x\in\mathfrak{m}^{n}M\}.

The condition that
M
is
\pi
-adically separated guarantees that
\|x\|_{\pi}=p^{-v_{\pi}(x)}
defines a norm on
V
. Since we can recover
M
as the
R_{q}
-submodule of
x\in V
with
\|x\|_{\pi}\leq 1
, the choice of
M
is equivlent to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\pi}
or the valuation
v_{\pi}
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
I
be a set. We define
b(I)
to be the
K_{q}
-vector space of
I
-families

of elements of
K_{q}
which are
\pi
-adically bounded, in the sense that

\|x\|_{\pi}=\sup_{i\in I}\|x_{i}\|<\infty.

Then

is a Banach space over
K_{q}
. For each
i\in I
, let
e_{i}\in b(I)
denote the element whose
i
th component is 1, and whose other components are all
0
. Then we may represent an element

as a formal sum

x=\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}e_{i}.

(5)

Let
c(I)
be the subspace of
b(I)
consisting of those
x
for which
x_{i}\to 0
in the finite-complement topology on
I
. Then
c(I)
is a Banach space over
K_{q}
, and for each
x\in c(I)
the formal sum (
5
) converges to
x
in the norm topology on
c(I)
.

Let
V=K\otimes_{R}M
and
W=K\otimes_{R}N
be normed vector spaces over
K_{q}
. Let
M\hat{\otimes}_{R_{q}}N
denote the
\pi
-adic completion of the
R_{q}
-module
M\otimes_{R_{q}}N
. We define the
completed tensor product
of
V
and
W
to be the
K_{q}
-Banach space
V\hat{\otimes}_{K_{q}}W=K\otimes_{R}(M\hat{\otimes}_{R_{q}}N)
. Similarly, for each
n\geq 0
we let
\wedge^{n}M
denote the
\pi
-adic completion of the
n
th exterior power of
M
(over
R_{q}
). We define the
n
th
completed exterior power
of
V
to be the
K_{q}
-Banach space
\wedge^{n}V=K\otimes_{R}\wedge^{n}M
.

We write
\mathrm{Hom}(V,W)
for the
K_{q}
-vector space of
continuous

K_{q}
-linear maps
V\to W
, equipped with the usual norm

\|\psi\|_{\pi}=\sup_{x\in M}\|\psi(x)\|_{\pi}<\infty.

In particular, we will write
V^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}(V,K)
for the
continuous
dual space of

.

3.1.2
Completely Continuous Maps

Definition 3.3
.

Let
V
and
W
be Banach spaces over
K_{q}
. We say that a continuous
K_{q}
-linear map
\psi:W\to V
is
completely continuous
if
\psi
is a limit of operators of finite rank. Equivalently,
\psi
is completely continuous if and only if
\psi
lies in the image of the natural map

W^{*}\hat{\otimes}_{K_{q}}V\to\mathrm{Hom}(W,V).

In particular, we identify the space of completely continuous operators
V\to V
with the Banach space
V^{*}\hat{\otimes}_{K_{q}}V
. The
trace map
on this space is defined in the usual way:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}:V^{*}\hat{\otimes}_{K_{q}}V

\displaystyle\to K_{q}

\displaystyle f\otimes v

\displaystyle\mapsto f(v).

If
\psi:V\to V
is completely continuous, then for each
n\geq 0
the
n
th exterior power
\wedge^{n}\psi
is a completely continuous operator on
\wedge^{n}V
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\psi:V\to V
be a completely continuous operator. For each
n\geq 0
we define the
n
th
Fredholm coefficient
to be

c_{n}(\psi)=(-1)^{n}\mathrm{Tr}(\wedge^{n}\psi).

The
Fredholm determinant
of
\psi
is the power series

\det(I-s\psi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}(\psi)s^{n}\in 1+sK_{q}\llbracket s\rrbracket.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
10
, Proposition 15]
)
.

Let
\psi:V\to V
be a completely continuous operator. Then the dual map
\psi^{*}:V^{*}\to V^{*}
is completely continuous, and there is an equality of Fredholm series

\det(I-s\psi)=\det(I-s\psi^{*}).

3.1.3
Orthonormal and Integral Bases

Definition 3.6
.

Let
V
be a Banach space over
K_{q}
. Let
I
be a set and let
B=\{e_{i}:i\in I\}
be a family of elements of
V
indexed by
I
. We say that
B
is an
orthonormal basis
for
V
if, for every
x\in V
there exists a unique
(x_{i})\in c(I)
such that
\|x\|_{\pi}=\sup_{i\in I}|x_{i}|_{\pi}
and

x=\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}e_{i}.

Let
V=K_{q}\otimes_{R_{q}}M
be a Banach space over
K_{q}
and let
B=\{e_{i}:i\in I\}
be subset of
V
. By
[
10
, Proposition 1]
,
B
is an orthonormal basis for
V
if and only if
B\subseteq M
and
B
reduces to an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis for
\mathbb{F}_{q}\otimes_{R_{q}}M
. It follows that every Banach space over
K_{q}
admits an orthonormal basis. An orthonormal basis for
V
indexed by
I
is equivalent to an isometric isomorphism of Banach spaces
\iota:V\xrightarrow{\sim}c(I)
, sending each
x\in V
to the corresponding
I
-family
(x_{i})\in c(I)
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
V
be a normed vector space over
K_{q}
. A
formal basis
for
V
is a pair
(B,\iota)
, where
B=\{e_{i}:i\in I\}
is a subset of
V
, and
\iota:V\to b(I)
is an isometric embedding sending
e_{i}\mapsto e_{i}
for all
i\in I
.

Typically we will abuse notation and refer to a formal basis
(B,\iota)
simply by
B
, leaving the embedding into

implicit.

Example 3.8
.

Let
V
be a Banach space and let
B=\{e_{i}:i\in I\}
be an orthonormal basis for
V
. By the preceding discussion,

is a formal basis for

in a natural way. For each

, consider the continuous linear functional

. Let

. If

is any continuous linear functional, then we define

\iota(f)=(f(e_{i}))\in b(I).

Then
\iota:V^{*}\cong b(I)
is an isometric isomorphism of Banach spaces over
K_{q}
, and the pair
(B^{*},\iota)
is a formal basis for

.

Let
I
be a set. Let
\Psi=(\psi_{i,j})
be an

. We say that
\Psi
is of

\psi_{i,i}\to 0

. In this case, we define the

of

\mathrm{Tr}(\Psi)=\sum_{i\in I}\psi_{i,i}\in K_{q}.

Choose any ordering on

. For each

, the exterior power
\wedge^{n}\Psi
is a matrix with entries in
K_{q}
indexed by the set

\wedge^{n}I=\{(i_{1},...,i_{n}):i_{1}<\cdots<i_{n}\}.

For each subset

, let
\Psi_{J}
denote the

submatrix of
\Psi
corresponding to

.

Definition 3.9
.

Suppose that
\wedge^{n}\Psi
is of trace class for all

. We define the

th
Fredholm coefficient
of
\Psi
to be

c_{n}(\Psi)=(-1)^{n}\mathrm{Tr}(\wedge^{n}\Psi)=(-1)^{n}\sum_{|J|=n}\det(\Psi_{J}).

(6)

The

of
\Psi
is the power series

\det(I-s\Psi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}(\Psi)s^{n}\in 1+sK_{q}\llbracket s\rrbracket.

Lemma 3.10

(
[
10
, §5]
)
.

Let
V
be a Banach space over

and let

Definition 3.11
.

We say that an

For each

\Psi_{i}\to 0

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\Psi
be a tight
I\times I

\psi:b(I)\to b(I)

-linear operator. Then

is completely continuous, and there is an equality of Fredholm series

Let

Proposition 3.13
.

Let

3.2
Nuclear Operators

Let
V
be a
K_{q}
-vector space and let
\psi:V\to V
be a
K_{q}
-linear operator. For every polynomial
g\in 1+sK_{q}[s]
, we define the subspace

V_{g}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\ker(g(\psi)^{n}).

Let
\overline{K}_{q}
be an algebraic closure of
K_{q}
. For any
\lambda\in\overline{K}_{q}^{\times}
, let
g_{\lambda}=1+sK_{q}[s]
be the irreducible polynomial of
\lambda
over
K_{q}
. We say that
\lambda
is a
non-zero eigenvalue
if
V_{g_{\lambda}}\neq 0
.

Definition 3.14
.

A
K_{q}
-linear operator
\psi:V\to V
is
nuclear
if:

1.

For every
g\in 1+sK_{q}[s]
there is a
\psi
-equivariant decomposition
V=F_{g}\oplus V_{g}
, where
g(\psi):F_{g}\to F_{g}
is bijective and
V_{g}
is finite-dimensional.

2.

The non-zero eigenvalues of
\psi
tend to
\infty
in the finite-complement topology.

Let
\psi:V\to V
be a nuclear operator. For each real number
r
define the subspace

V^{<r}=\sum_{v_{\pi}(\lambda)>-r}V_{g_{\lambda}},

Since
\psi
is nuclear, each
V^{<r}
is a finite-dimensional subspace of
V
. The
characteristic series
of
\psi
is defined to be the power series

C(\psi|V,s)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\det(I-s\psi|V^{<r})\in 1+K_{q}[[s]].

Over
\overline{K}_{q}
, this series factors as a product
C(\psi|V,s)=\prod_{\lambda}(1-s/\lambda)
, where
\lambda
runs through the non-zero eigenvalues of
\psi
counted with multiplicity. It follows that
C(\psi|V,s)
is a
\pi
-adic entire function on
K_{q}
.

Example 3.15
.

The following basic examples of nuclear operators, which we will refer to frequently, are due to Monsky
[
20
]
:

1.

Let
V
be a Banach space over
K_{q}
. Let
\psi:V\to V
be a completely continuous operator. Then by
[
20
, Theorem 1.3]
,
\psi
is nuclear and

C(\psi|V,s)=\det(I-s\psi).

2.

Let
V
be a
K_{q}
-vector space, and let
\psi:V\to V
be a nuclear operator. Let
W\subseteq V
be a
\psi
-invariant subspace such that
\psi
restricts to a nuclear operator on
W
. Then by
[
20
, Theorem 1.4]
the action of
\psi
on
V/W
is nuclear and

C(\psi|V,s)=C(\psi|W,s)\cdot C(\psi|V/W,s).

3.

Let
V
be a
K_{q}
-vector space. Let
I
be a linearly ordered set, and let
\{V_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of
K_{q}
-subspaces of
V
such that
V_{j}\subseteq V_{j}
whenever
i\leq j
, with
V=\bigcup_{i\in I}V_{i}
. Let
\psi:V\to V
be a
K_{q}
-linear operator. Suppose that for all
i\gg 0
,
\psi
restricts to a nuclear operator on
V_{i}
, and that the characteristic series
C(\psi|V_{i},s)
is independent of
i
. Then by
[
20
, Theorem 1.6]
,
\psi
is a nuclear operator on
V
and for such
i
we have

C(\psi|V,s)=C(\psi|V_{i},s).

3.3
Newton and Hodge Polygons

Definition 3.16
.

Let
S
be a countable multiset of real numbers. We say that
S
is a
slope set
if for all
r\in R
, the multiset
S^{<r}=\{s\in S:s<r\}
is finite.

Let
S
be a slope set. If
S
is finite of cardinality
n
, then there is a unique convex function
f:[0,n]\to\mathbb{R}\sqcup\{\infty\}
such that:

1.

f(0)=0

2.

For each
0\leq i<n
, the restriction of
f
to
[i,i+1]
is a linear function.

3.

S
is equal to the multiset
\{f(i+1)-f(i):0\leq i<n\}
.

In this case, we define the
Newton polygon

\NP(S)
of
S
to be the graph of
f
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. If
S
is not finite, then we define the
Newton polygon
of
S
to be the union

\NP(S)=\bigcup_{r}\NP(S^{<r}).

If
S^{\prime}
is another slope set, we define the
concatenation
of
S
and
S^{\prime}
to be the slope set
S\sqcup S^{\prime}
. In particular, we will write
S^{\times n}
for the
n
-fold concatenation of
S
with itself. The basic operations on slope sets carry over to their Newton polygons as well. We define a partial order on slope sets (or their Newton polygons) by writing
S_{1}\succeq S_{2}
whenever the Newton polygon of
S_{1}
lies on or above the Newton polygon of
S_{2}
.

Example 3.17
.

1.

Let
P(s)=1+c_{1}s+\cdots+c_{d}s^{d}
be a polynomial with coefficients in
K_{q}
. We define the
\pi
-adic Newton polygon

\NP_{\pi}P(s)
of
P(s)
to be the lower convex hull in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the points
(n,v_{\pi}(c_{n}))
for
0\leq n\leq d
. Then
\NP_{\pi}P(s)
is the Newton polygon of a unique slope set which we call the
\pi
-adic
Newton slopes
of
P(s)
. If we factor

P(s)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(1-\alpha_{i}s)

over
\overline{K}_{q}
, then the
\pi
-adic Newton slopes of
P(s)
are precisely the
v_{\pi}(\alpha_{i})
counted with multiplicity.

2.

Let
C(s)\in 1+sK_{q}\llbracket s\rrbracket
be a power series converging in the disk
v_{\pi}(s)>-r
. By the Weierstrass preparation theorem there is a factorization

C(s)=P(s)\cdot C^{\prime}(s),

where
P(s)\in 1+sK_{q}[s]
has all
\pi
-adic Newton slopes
<r
, and
C^{\prime}(s)
converges and is non-zero in the disk
v_{\pi}(s)>-r
. In this case we define
\NP_{\pi}^{<r}C(s)=\NP_{\pi}P(s)
. In particular, if
C(s)
is entire then we may write

\NP_{\pi}C(s)=\bigcup_{r}\NP_{\pi}^{<r}C(s).

3.

Let
V
be a
K_{q}
-vector space and let
\psi:V\to V
be a nuclear operator. Then we define the
\pi
-adic Newton polygon
of
\psi
to be
\NP_{\pi}(\psi)=\NP_{\pi}C(\psi,s)
.

Definition 3.18
.

Let
V
be a Banach space over
K_{q}
. Let
\psi:V\to V
be a completely continuous operator. The
\pi
-adic
Hodge polygon

\HP_{\pi}(\psi)
of
\psi
is the lower convex hull in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of the points
(n,v_{\pi}\wedge^{n}\psi)
.

Let
I
be a set, and let
\Psi
be a tight
I\times I
matrix with entries in
K_{q}
. Then we may write
\HP_{\pi}(\Psi)
for the Hodge polygon of the induced operator
b(I)\to b(I)
. For tight matrices, the following “naive” Hodge polygon is often useful for making explicit estimates:

Definition 3.19
.

The
column Hodge polygon

\cHP_{\pi}(\Psi)
of
\Psi
is the convex polygon with slope set

\{v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{i}):i\in I\}.

It is straightforward to show that
\HP_{\pi}(\Psi)
always lies on or above
\cHP_{\pi}(\psi)
, and therefore that
\HP_{\pi}(\Psi)
is the Newton polygon of a unique slope set. We will frequently make use of the following much stronger estimate:

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
\Psi
be a (finite)
n\times n
matrix with entries in
K_{q}
. Then every slope of
\HP_{\pi}(\Psi)
is greater than or equal to the corresponding slope of
\cHP_{\pi}(\Psi)
. Consequently, if both polygons pass through a point
(n,m)
then they agree on the interval
[0,n]
.

Proof.

Recall that the “invariant factor theorem” states that
\Psi
can be put in diagonal form by applying finitely many of the following operations: (1) Permuting the rows or columns of
\Psi
, (2) Adding an
R_{q}
-multiple of one row of
\Psi
to another, and (3) Given a column with one non-zero entry, adding an
R_{q}
-multiple of this column to clear other entries in the same row. It is well known that these operations do not affect the Hodge polygon of
\Psi
. On the other hand, each of these operations can only increase the column slopes
v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{i})
. Since
\HP_{\pi}(\Psi)=\cHP_{\pi}(\Psi)
when
\Psi
is diagonal, this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
V
be a Banach space, and let
\psi:V\to V
be a completely continuous operator. Then

1.

\HP_{\pi}(\psi)
is the Newton polygon of a unique slope set.

2.

\NP_{\pi}(\psi)\succeq\HP_{\pi}(\psi)
.

3.

If
V
is finite-dimensional and
\det(\psi)\neq 0
, then
\NP_{\pi}(\psi)
and
\HP_{\pi}(\psi)
have the same terminal point.

Proof.

Let
B=\{e_{i}:i\in I\}
be an orthonormal basis for
V
. Let
\Psi=\psi|B
and let
\Psi^{\prime}
denote the transpose of
\Psi
. Then
\Psi^{\prime}
is tight, and
\HP_{\pi}(\psi)=\HP_{\pi}(\Psi^{\prime})
. Claim
1
then follows from the preceding discussion. Evidently
v_{\pi}\mathrm{Tr}\wedge^{n}\psi\geq v_{\pi}\wedge^{n}\psi
for all
n
, proving
2
. For
3
, simply note that the terminal point of both polygons is
(n,v_{\pi}\det(\psi))
.
∎

3.4
Iteration

Let
F
denote the canonical Frobenius endomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
. By base change,
F
induces an
R
-linear endomorphism
F:R_{q}\to R_{q}
.

Definition 3.22
.

Let
V
be a vector space over
K_{q}
. We say that a
K
-linear operator
\psi:V\to V
is
F^{-1}
-linear
if

\psi(F(r)x)=r\psi(x)

for all
r\in R
and
x\in V
. In this case, the iterate
\psi_{q}=\psi^{v_{p}(q)}
is a
K_{q}
-linear operator.

Definition 3.23
.

Let
V
be a normed vector space over
K_{q}
. Let
B_{q}
be a formal basis for
V
. An
associated
K
-Basis
for
V
is a formal basis for
V
(as a normed vector space over
K
) of the form

B=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{v_{p}(q)-1}F^{i}(\xi)\otimes B_{q},

(7)

where
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is any element such that the
F^{i}(\xi)
generate
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.

Let
\psi:V\to V
be an
F^{-1}
-linear operator. Via the partition (
7
), the matrix
\psi_{q}|B
is block diagonal of the form

\begin{bmatrix}\xi(\psi_{q}|B_{q})&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&F^{v_{p}(q)-1}(\xi)(\psi_{q}|B_{q})\end{bmatrix}.

Consider the parameter
\pi_{q}=\pi^{v_{p}(q)}
. We have the following relation between the Newton polygons of the operators
\psi
and
\psi_{q}
:

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
V
be a Banach space over
K_{q}
. Let
\psi:V\to V
be a completely continuous
F^{-1}
-linear operator. Then
\psi_{q}
is completely continuous as a
K_{q}
-linear operator and

\NP_{\pi_{q}}(\psi_{q})^{\times v_{p}(q)}=\NP_{\pi}(\psi).

Proof.

Note that
\psi_{q}
is completely continuous as a
K
-linear operator. Let
B_{q}
be an orthonormal
K_{q}
-basis for
V
, and let
B
be an associated
K
-basis for
V
. From the above block matrix, we see that the rows of
\psi_{q}|B_{q}
tend to
0
in the finite-complement topology, thus
\psi_{q}
is completely continuous as a
K_{q}
-linear operator. We see moreover that
\det(I-s\psi_{q}|B)=\det(I-s\psi_{q}|B_{q})^{v_{p}(q)}
. It follows that

\det(I-s^{v_{p}(q)}\psi_{q}|B_{q})^{v_{p}(q)}=\det(I-s^{v_{p}(q)}\psi_{q})=\prod_{\zeta^{v_{p}(q)}=1}\det(I-s\zeta\psi|B).

The Newton polygons of the matrices
\zeta\psi|B
do not depend on
\zeta
. Thus,

\NP_{\pi_{q}}(\psi_{q}|B_{q})^{\times v_{p}(q)}=\NP_{\pi}\det(I-s^{v_{p}(q)}\psi_{q}|B_{q})=\NP_{\pi}(\psi|B).

The claim follows from Lemma
3.10
.
∎

4
Geometric Setup

Fix a smooth projective curve
\overline{X}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of genus
g
. Let
S
be a finite set of closed points of
\overline{X}
and let
X=\overline{X}\backslash S
. In this section we construct the global lifting
(A^{\dagger},\sigma)
, which we will use to study abelian
L
-functions over
X
. We will give our constructions over the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, with
\pi=p
. Later, we will pass to general
R
and
\pi
by weak base change.

4.1
Mapping to
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}

The construction of our global lifting relies on an analogue of Belyi’s theorem in characteristic
p
. For this we require:

Theorem 4.1

(Fulton
[
9
]
)
.

After extending the base field, there exists a finite, separable, tamely ramified morphism
\eta_{0}:\overline{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
.

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
23
]
, Sugiyama and Yasuda extend Fulton’s result to the case
p=2
. We have omitted this case for other reasons (see Remark
6.5
). By a recent theorem of Kedlaya-Litt-Witaszek,
\eta
exists even without extending the base field
[
13
]
.

From now on we fix a choice of
\eta_{0}
as in Theorem
4.1
. As in
[
13
, Theorem 9.3]
, the existence of
\eta_{0}
implies the existence of a tamely ramified map
\overline{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
with three ramified points. We shall need some control over the branching of this map, so we modify the construction slightly.

By extending the base field, we assume that all branch points of
\eta_{0}
and every point in
S
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational. We assume moreover that
q
is large enough so that there are two
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational points of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
which are disjoint from the branch points of
\eta_{0}
and the image of
S
. Label these points as
0
and
\infty
, and let
1
denote any other
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
. Consider the composition

\eta_{q}:\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}\xrightarrow{q-1}\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}\xrightarrow{p-1}\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}.

Here, the first and third maps denote the
(q-1)
- and
(p-1)
-power maps, respectively. The second map is a linear transformation fixing
1
and
\infty
and sending
0
to any other
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
. The final map is also a linear transformation which fixes
\infty
and swaps
0
with
1
. Then
\eta_{q}
is ramified over
\{0,1,\infty\}
and all branch points of
\eta_{q}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational. We have:

Proposition 4.3
.

The composite
\eta=\eta_{q}\circ\eta_{0}:\overline{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}
is a tame Belyi map such that

1.

\eta(P)=0
for each
P\in S
.

2.

If
\eta(P)=1
then the ramification index of
\eta
at
P
is
p-1
.

For each
Q\in\{0,1,\infty\}
, let
r_{Q}
denote the cardinality of the fiber
\eta^{-1}(Q)
. If
\eta(P)=Q
, then we let
e_{P}
denote the ramification index of
P
over
Q
. In particular, if
Q=1
then
e_{P}=p-1
. It follows that that
r_{1}(p-1)=\deg(\eta)
. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives

2(g-1)+r_{0}+r_{1}+r_{\infty}=\deg(\eta).

(8)

4.2
The Global Lifting

Let
V=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}\backslash\{0,1,\infty\}
. Let
u_{0}
denote a parameter at
0
, and define
u_{1}=u_{0}-1
,
u_{\infty}=u_{0}^{-1}
. The coordinate ring of
V
is a generated by
u_{0}
,
u_{\infty}
, and
u_{1}^{-1}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We consider the lifting

B=\mathbb{Z}_{q}[u_{0},u_{\infty},u_{1}^{-1}].

(9)

We define
\sigma
to be the unique Frobenius endomorphism of
B^{\dagger}
satisfying
\sigma(u_{0})=u_{0}^{p}
.

Let
U=\eta^{-1}(V)
. Then
U
is an affine open subset of
X
, which is finite étale over
V
. We define
S_{\eta}=X-V
. We will lift
U
to characteristic
0
rather than all of
X
, because the étale property of
\eta
is essential for constructing our Frobenius endomorphism: First, a theorem of Elkik
[
7
]
states that there exists a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-algebra
A
such that
U=\mathrm{Spec}(A/pA)
. By
[
26
, Theorem 2.4.4]
, there is a (necessarily unique) lifting of
\eta
to an étale map
B^{\dagger}\to A^{\dagger}
.

Definition 4.4
.

The
global lifting
is the ring
A^{\dagger}
equipped with the unique extension of
\sigma
along
\eta
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The Frobenius
\sigma:A^{\dagger}\to A^{\dagger}
is injective, and
A^{\dagger}
is a finite projective
\sigma(A^{\dagger})
-module of rank
p
.

Proof.

The first statement is
[
21
, Theorem 3.2(3)]
. The second follows from that fact that
A^{\dagger}
is finite
[
21
, Theorem 6.2]
and flat
[
21
, Theorem 2.1(2)]
over
\sigma(A^{\dagger})
.
∎

Let
\Omega_{A^{\dagger}}
denote the
A^{\dagger}
-module of continuous differentials of
A^{\dagger}/\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, and similarly for
B^{\dagger}
. Then
\Omega_{B^{\dagger}}
is a free
B^{\dagger}
-module of rank
1
generated by the differential
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}
. Since
\eta:B^{\dagger}\to A^{\dagger}
is étale, we have

\Omega_{A^{\dagger}}=A^{\dagger}\otimes_{B^{\dagger}}\Omega_{B^{\dagger}}=A^{\dagger}\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}.

The Frobenius
\sigma
induces an injective
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-linear endomorphism
\sigma_{1}
of
\Omega_{A^{\dagger}}
. Explicitly,

\sigma_{1}\left(a\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}\right)=\sigma(a)p\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}.

Thus
\sigma_{1}(\Omega_{A^{\dagger}})=\Omega_{\sigma(A^{\dagger})}
is a free
\sigma(A^{\dagger})
-module of rank
1
, generated by
p\tfrac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}
. By Lemma
4.5
, there is a
\sigma(A^{\dagger})
-linear
trace map

\mathrm{Tr}:A^{\dagger}\to\sigma(A^{\dagger}).

Given
a\in A^{\dagger}
, the trace
\mathrm{Tr}(a)
may be identified with the coefficient of
X^{p-1}
in the minimal polynomial
g_{a}(X)
of
a
over
\sigma(A^{\dagger})
. Note that
g_{a}\equiv X^{p}-a^{p}\pmod{p}
, so that
\mathrm{Tr}(a)
in fact lies in
p\sigma(A^{\dagger})
. The trace map induces a
\sigma(A^{\dagger})
-linear map
\mathrm{Tr}_{1}:\Omega_{A^{\dagger}}\to\sigma_{1}(\Omega_{A^{\dagger}})
. Explicitly,

\mathrm{Tr}_{1}\left(a\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}\right)=\mathrm{Tr}(a)\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}=\frac{\mathrm{Tr}(a)}{p}p\frac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}.

Definition 4.6
.

The
global
U_{p}
operator
is the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linear endomorphism

U_{p}=\sigma_{1}^{-1}\circ\mathrm{Tr}_{1}:\Omega_{A^{\dagger}}\to\Omega_{A^{\dagger}}.

The global
U_{p}
operator is a
p
-Dwork operator on
\Omega_{A^{\dagger}}
and the iterate
U_{q}=U_{p}^{v_{p}(q)}
is a
q
-Dwork operator. If we identify
\Omega_{A^{\dagger}}
with
A^{\dagger}
using the basis
\tfrac{\mathrm{d}u_{0}}{u_{0}}
, then
U_{p}
is identified with the operator

U_{p}=\frac{1}{p}\sigma^{-1}\circ\mathrm{Tr}:A^{\dagger}\to A^{\dagger}.

7.1
Newton-Hodge Interaction and Perturbation Theory

Let
I
be a countable set. Let
\Psi
be a tight matrix with entries in
R
indexed by
I
, regarded as a completely continuous operator
\psi:b(I)\to b(I)
. We will now discuss the interaction between the Newton, Hodge, and column Hodge polygons of
\Psi
. For every
r>0
, let

I^{<r}(\Psi)=\{i\in I:v_{\pi}\psi(e_{i})<r\}.

Let
\Psi^{<r}
denote the finite diagonal submatrix of
\Psi
indexed by
I^{<r}(\Psi)
. We are primarily interested in the following strong type of Newton-Hodge interaction:

Lemma 7.1
.

The following are equivalent:

1.

\NP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
and
\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
have the same terminal point.

2.

\NP_{\pi}(\Psi^{<r})
and
\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
have the same terminal point.

Proof.

Choose an ordering on
I
so that the column slopes
v_{\pi}\psi(e_{i})
are increasing. Label the elements
I^{<r}=\{i_{1},...,i_{n}\}
in increasing order, and let
(n,m)
denote the terminal point of
\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
so that

m=v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{i_{1}})+\cdots+v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{i_{n}}).

Let
J=\{j_{1},...,j_{n}\}
be any subset of
I
of cardinality
n
, with elements labelled in increasing order. Then for every
1\leq k\leq n
we have

v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{j_{k}})\geq v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{i_{k}}).

If
J\neq I^{<r}(\Psi)
, then the inequality is
strict
for
k=n
, since
e_{j_{n}}\notin I^{<r}(\Psi)
. Since
\NP_{\pi}(\Psi_{J})\succeq\cHP_{\pi}(\Psi_{J})
, we see that every
J\times J
minor of
\Psi
has
\pi
-adic valuation
\geq v_{\pi}\det(\Psi^{<r})
, with strict inequality when
J\neq I^{<r}(\Psi)
. Thus
\NP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
and
\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
have the same terminal point if and only if
v_{\pi}\det(\Psi^{<r})=m
, i.e. if and only if
\NP_{\pi}(\Psi^{<r})
and
\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
have the same terminal point
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose that
\NP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
and
\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
have the same terminal point. Then the polygons

\HP_{\pi}(\Psi^{<r}),\ \cHP_{\pi}(\Psi^{<r}),\ \HP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi),\ \cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)

are all equal.

Proof.

Observe that each of the listed polygons lies on or below
\HP_{\pi}(\Psi^{<r})
and on or above
\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
. By Lemma
7.1
, all of these polygons share the same terminal point. Since each column slope of
\Psi^{<r}
is greater than or equal to the corresponsing column slope of
\Psi
, we must have that
\cHP_{\pi}(\Psi^{<r})=\cHP_{\pi}(\Psi)
. It suffices then to consider the case that
\Psi=\Psi^{<r}
is a finite matrix whose
\pi
-adic column slopes are
<r
. But this is immediate from Lemma
3.20
.
∎

Definition 7.3
.

Let
\Psi^{\prime}=\Psi+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon
is a matrix with entries in
R
indexed by
I
. We say that
\Psi^{\prime}
is a
\pi
-adic
r
-perturbation
of
\Psi
if:

1.

v_{\pi}\varepsilon(e_{i})>v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{i})
for all
i\in I^{<r}(\Psi)
.

2.

v_{\pi}\varepsilon(e_{i})\geq r
for all
i\notin I^{<r}(\Psi)
.

If
\Psi^{\prime}
is a tight
\pi
-adic
r
-perturbation of
\Psi
, then the truncated column Hodge polygons
\cHP^{<r}_{\pi}(\Psi)
and
\cHP^{<r}_{\pi}(\Psi^{\prime})
of Lemma
3.20
necessarily agree. The stronger perturbation condition guarantees the following interaction between the Newton polygons of both matrices:

Lemma 7.4

(Perturbation Lemma)
.

Suppose that
\Psi^{\prime}=\Psi+\varepsilon
is a tight
\pi
-adic
r
-perturbation of
\Psi
. If
\NP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
has the same terminal point as
\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)=\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)
, then the same is true of
\NP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
7.1
and
7.2
, we immediately reduce to the case that
\Psi
and
\Psi^{\prime}
are finite matrices whose column slopes are all
<r
. We must show that
v_{\pi}\det(\Psi)=v_{\pi}\det(\Psi^{\prime})
. Choose an ordering of
I
so that the sequence
v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{i})
is increasing. Given an index
(i_{1},...,i_{k})\in\wedge^{n}I
, we will abbreviate
e_{i_{1},...,i_{k}}=e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{k}}
. Let us write

\wedge^{k}\Psi^{\prime}=\wedge^{k}\Psi+\varepsilon_{k}

for all
k\geq 0
. A straightforward computation shows that

\varepsilon_{k+1}(e_{i_{1},...,i_{k+1}})=\wedge^{k}\Psi(e_{i_{1},...,i_{k}})\wedge\varepsilon(e_{i_{k+1}})+\varepsilon_{k}(e_{i_{1},...,i_{k}})\wedge\Psi^{\prime}(e_{i_{k+1}}).

Since
\Psi^{\prime}
is an
r
-perturbation of
\Psi
, by induction on
k
we see that

v_{\pi}(\varepsilon_{n})>v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{1})+\cdots+v_{\pi}\Psi(e_{n}).

By assumption, the latter is exactly
\det(\Psi)
. It follows that

v_{\pi}\det(\Psi^{\prime})=v_{\pi}(\det(\Psi)+\varepsilon_{n})=v_{\pi}\det(\Psi).

∎

\varepsilon({e}_{P,k}^{\mathbf{m}_{e}})=-\pi^{kp/\delta_{P}}\tilde{\Theta}(c_{k}).

It follows that

v_{\pi}(\varepsilon({e}_{P,k}^{\mathbf{m}_{e}}))\geq\frac{kp}{\delta_{P}}.

On the other hand, the assumption that
\rho
is
\pi
-adically
\bm{\delta}
-Hodge implies that

v_{\pi}(\tilde{\Theta}({e}_{P,k}^{\mathbf{m}_{e}}))=\frac{k(p-1)}{\delta_{P}}.

∎

7.4
Main results

We are now ready to state and prove the general form of Theorem
1.1
. Let
r\leq v_{\pi}(p)
, so that
L(\phi,s)
is analytic in the region
v_{\pi_{q}}(s)>-r
by the Monsky trace formula. We define the
r
-truncated
\pi_{q}
-adic Newton polygon
of
(M,\phi)
to be

\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\phi)=\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\Theta_{q}|V_{\pi}^{\dagger}).

Lemma 7.12
.

If
\overline{X}
is ordinary, then
\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\phi)
has
N
segments of slope
0
.

Proof.

Observe that
(M,\phi)
is congruent to the trivial
\sigma
-module
(A_{\pi}^{\infty},\sigma)
modulo
\pi
. In particular, if we write
P(U,s)/(1-qs)
for the zeta function of
U
, then

L(\phi,s)\equiv P(U,s)\pmod{\pi}.

The result follows from the Deuring-Shafarevich formula.
∎

Assume now that
\rho
is
\pi
-adically
\bm{\delta}
-Hodge. We define the
r
-truncated
\pi_{q}
-adic Hodge polygon
of
(M,\phi)
to be

\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\phi)=\NP\{\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{N}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{P\in S}\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}}).

Theorem 7.13
.

Suppose that
\overline{X}
is ordinary and that
\rho
is
\pi
-adically
\bm{\delta}
-Hodge. Then
\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})
and
\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})
have the same terminal point for each
P\in S
if and only if
\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\phi)
and
\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\phi)
have the same terminal point.

Proof.

Since
r\leq v_{\pi}(p)
, we may enlarge
e
as necessary and assume that
r\leq e
. By Lemma
7.11
the matrix
\Psi^{\prime}
of
7.3
is a
\pi
-adic
r
-perturbation of
\Psi
. Since
\overline{X}
is assumed to be ordinary, we know that
\NP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi)=\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\phi)^{\times{v_{p}(q)}}
must have
Nv_{p}(q)
segments of slope
0
. By the perturbation lemma, the same is true of
\Psi^{\prime}
. Thus

\NP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi^{\prime})=\NP\{\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{Nv_{p}(q)}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{P\in S}\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})^{\times{v_{p}(q)}}.

and

\cHP_{\pi}^{<r}(\Psi^{\prime})=\NP\{\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{Nv_{p}(q)}\}\sqcup\bigsqcup_{P\in S}\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})^{\times v_{p}(q)}.

The claim is immediate from the perturbation lemma.
∎

Corollary 7.14
.

Suppose that
\overline{X}
is ordinary and that
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to R^{\times}
is
\pi
-adically
\bm{\delta}
-Hodge. Let
r\leq v_{\pi}(p)
. If
\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})
and
\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})
have the same terminal point for each
P\in S
, then
\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho)
and
\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho)
have the same terminal point.

Proof.

Let
(M,\phi)
be the unit-root
\sigma
-module over
A_{\pi}^{\infty}
corresponding to
\rho_{U}
. From the equality (
14
), the Newton polygon
\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\phi)
is the concatenation of
\NP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho)
with
r_{0}+r_{1}+r_{\infty}-|S|
segments of slope
0
. By definition, the Hodge polygon
\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\phi)
is a concatenation of
\HP_{\pi_{q}}^{<r}(\rho)
with
r_{0}+r_{1}+r_{\infty}-|S|
segments of slope
0
. The result follows by canceling out these extra slope-
0
segments.
∎

Corollary 7.15
.

Theorem
1.1
holds.

Proof.

By Theorem
7.10
we know that any finite character of order
p^{n}

is
\bm{\delta}
-Hodge, where
\delta_{P}
is the Swan conductor at
P
divided by
p^{n-1}
.
The result is then an immediate consequence of Corollary
7.14
.
∎

Corollary 7.16
.

Theorem
1.3
holds for finite characters of order
p
.

Proof.

By Remark
1.4
we only need to show the “if” direction. By our assumption on the order
of
\rho
we have
d_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
p\equiv 1\mod d_{P}
. By
[
22
]
(see also
[
2
, Remark 4.1]
)
we know
\NP_{q}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})=\HP_{q}(\rho_{P}^{\mathrm{ext}})
for each
P\in S
. The result follows from
Theorem
7.10
.
∎

8
Glossary of notation

The spaces studied in this paper are numerous and intricate. For this reason,
we thought the reader would benefit from a notation glossary.

8.1
Basic definitions

R

A complete discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and residue field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

\pi

A non-zero topologically nilpotent element of
R
.

K

The fraction field of
R
, i.e.
K=R[\frac{1}{\pi}]
.

q

A power of
p
.

R_{q}

The unramified extension of
R
whose residue field is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

K_{q}

The fraction field of
R_{q}
, i.e.
K_{q}=R_{q}[\frac{1}{\pi}]
.

\pi_{q}

The
v_{p}(q)
-th power of
\pi
: i.e.
\pi_{q}=\pi^{v_{p}(q)}
.

X

A smooth affine curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

\overline{X}

The smooth compactification of
X
.

S

The points of
\overline{X}
at infinity, i.e.
S=\overline{X}\backslash X
.

\eta

A tame morphism
\overline{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}^{1}

that is only ramified over
\{0,1,\infty\}
. See §
4.1
for more details.

S_{\eta}

The branch points of
\eta
, i.e.
S_{\eta}=\eta^{-1}(\{0,1,\infty\})
.

\mu

A function from
S_{\eta}
to
\mathbb{Z}
. More precisely, if
\eta(P)\in\{0,\infty\}
we
have
\mu(P)=0
and if
\eta(P)=1
we have
\mu(P)=p-1
.

A

A smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-algebra whose special fiber is the coordinate ring of

\overline{X}-S_{\eta}
.

8.2
The space
A
, its modifications, and some associated notation

In this article, we frequently work with modifications to the space of functions
A
.
These modified spaces are notated using combinations of superscripts, subscripts, and font changes. These modifiers are occasionally applied to spaces other than
A
. However,
for expository purposes, it seems prudent to use
A
as our ‘running example’. We will now
explain these notation modifiers, as well as explain some other notation for objects associated to
A
.

A_{\pi}

The ring
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}A
.

A_{\pi}^{\infty}

The
\pi
-adic completion of
A_{\pi}
.

A_{\pi}^{m}

The elements of
A_{\pi}^{\infty}
that
‘overconverge’
\pi
-adically with a radius of
m
. See §
2.1
for more details.

A_{\pi}^{\dagger}

The elements of
A_{\pi}^{\infty}
that ‘overconverge’
\pi
-adically. In particular, it is the union of all
A_{\pi}^{m}
.

V_{\pi}^{\dagger}

The
K
-vector space
K\otimes_{R}A_{\pi}^{\dagger}
. More
generally, when
A
is replaced with
V
, this signifies tensoring with
K
.

\tilde{A}_{\pi}^{\dagger}

The semi-local ‘twist’ of
A_{\pi}^{\dagger}
.

\mathcal{A}_{\pi,P}

The completion of
A_{\pi}
along the ideal defining
P
.
We use the superscripts
m
,
\dagger
, and
\infty
for the appropriate
\pi
-adic
growth conditions.

\mathbf{m}

A tuple
(m_{P})_{P\in S_{\eta}}
of rational numbers indexed by
S_{\eta}
.

\mathcal{A}_{\pi,P}^{\mathbf{m}}

This is
a subspace of
\mathcal{A}_{\pi,P}^{\dagger}
with very precise growth conditions
depending on
\eta(P)
and
\mathbf{m}
. See §
6.1.1
and §
6.1.2
for the precise definitions.

\mathcal{A}_{\pi,P}^{\mathrm{tr}}

The subspace of elements in
\mathcal{A}_{P}

of ‘truncated’ power series. More precisely, elements
that can be written as
\sum\limits_{k=\mu(P)}^{\infty}a_{k}t_{P}^{-k}
.
This will frequently be combined with another ‘growth’ superscript, such as

\dagger
,
\infty
, or
\mathbf{m}
.

\mathcal{A}_{\pi}

The product
\prod\limits_{P\in S_{\eta}}\mathcal{A}_{\pi,P}
.
We use the superscripts
\mathbf{m}
,
\dagger
, and
\infty
for the appropriate
\pi
-adic
growth conditions. We also use the
\mathrm{tr}
superscript to denote the ‘truncated’ power series in each summand.

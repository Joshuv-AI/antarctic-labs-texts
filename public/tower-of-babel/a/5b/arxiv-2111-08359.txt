A change of measure formula for recursive conditional expectations
Luca Di Persio, Alessandro Gnoatto, Marco Patacca

Abstract
We derive a representation for the value process associated to the solutions of forward-backward stochastic differential equations in a jump-diffusion setting under multiple probability measures. Motivated by concrete financial problems, the latter representations are then applied to devise a generalization of the change of numéraire technique allowing to obtain recursive pricing formulas in the presence of non-linear funding terms due to e.g. collateralization agreements. Keywords Pricing ⋅ \cdot Change of measure ⋅ \cdot BSDE ⋅ \cdot recursive conditional expectation ⋅ \cdot non-linear valuation JEL Classification C63 ⋅ \cdot G12 ⋅ \cdot G13



1
Introduction and Motivation

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be two probability measures on the measurable space
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}\right)
. From the Radon-Nikodym theorem, it is well known that
\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}
on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F}\right)
if and only if there exists an
\mathcal{F}
-measurable and non negative random variable
\mathcal{E}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathcal{E}\right]=1
, such that, for any random variable
X\in L^{1}\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{Q}\right)
we have

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[X\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathcal{E}X\right]\;,\;\mathbb{Q}-a.s.

(1.1)

The previous result can be generalized to conditional expectations, which is sometimes referred to as the “abstract Bayes rule”.
More specifically, if
\mathcal{G}
is a
\sigma
-algebra with
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
we have

\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[X|\mathcal{G}\right]=\frac{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathcal{E}X|\mathcal{G}\right]}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{G}\right]}\;,\;\mathbb{Q}-a.s.

(1.2)

The above relations play a relevant role in several applications, particularly in Mathematical Finance, see e.g.
Bjork 2020
. Within this setting, the likelihood ratio
\mathcal{E}
, due to the Girsanov theorem, is usually given by the Doléans-Dade stochastic exponential driven by possibly discontinuous semimartingales. Such tools are well understood for standard conditional expectations, while the case when the conditional expectation has a recursive structure is less clear. For the sake of clarity, let us first introduce such situation in an informal way.

By recursive expectation, we mean an expression of the following form

Y_{t}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[g(X_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,X_{s},Y_{s},Z_{s})\mathrm{d}s|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right],

(1.3)

where the functions
g,f
and the processes
X,Y
and
Z
are
regular enough
, also having implicitly introduced the filtration
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
,
T>0
being a fixed time horizon. Such filtration is generated by the process
X\coloneqq\{X_{t},t\in[0,T]\}
, typically assumed to be an Itô semimartingale.

Expectations like (
1.3
) naturally arise from the study of Forward-Backward Stochastic Differential Equations (FBSDEs) of the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=\mu(t,X_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t,X_{t})\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\quad,\quad X_{0}=x

(1.4)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{d}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=f(t,X_{t},Y_{t},Z_{t})\mathrm{d}t-Z_{t}\mathrm{d}Z_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\quad,\quad Y_{T}=g(X_{T})\,,

(1.5)

where all vector field functions are assumed to be
sufficiently regular
, while
W^{\mathbb{Q}}
is a standard Brownian motion under the reference measure
\mathbb{Q}
. For a background on FBSDEs, we refer the reader to
Bismut 1973
,
Pardoux and Peng 1990
,
El Karoui et al. 1997
,
Ma and Yong 1999
and
Delong 2013
among others.

The aim of this work is to provide a change of measure formula for conditional expectations of the form (
1.3
) associated to FBSDEs of the form (
1.4
) within a Markovian Jump Diffusion setting. We derive such result in Theorem
3.3
which constitutes the main result of the present paper.

Our motivation to consider this type of recursive conditional expectations is mainly steered by financial applications. As a response to the financial crisis, the valuation of contingent claim has been generalized along several directions.
For example
Piterbarg 2010
obtained, starting from a Black-Scholes type replication argument and applying the Feynman-Kač formula, the following recursive expression for the price of a contingent claim when collateral is exchanged:

Y_{t}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[e^{-\int_{t}^{T}r_{u}^{c}\mathrm{d}u}Y_{T}-\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\int_{t}^{T}r_{s}^{c}\mathrm{d}s}(r_{u}^{f}-r_{u}^{c})(C_{u}-Y_{u})\mathrm{d}u\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\,,

(1.6)

where the collateral
C
and the short rates
r^{c},r^{f}
are sufficiently regular, such that the above expression is well posed: in applications,
C
is assumed to be a Lipschitz function of
Y
.
Expressions of the aforementioned form have been then justified via BSDE-techniques in, e.g.,
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
,
Bichuch et al. 2018
,
Brigo et al. 2019
,
Biagini et al. 2021
and
Gnoatto and Seiffert 2021
.
Such recursive expectations are now well understood, and are at least approximately implemented
in several industry-standard software packages via a combination of quadrature formulas for the time integral, and Monte Carlo simulations for the expectation.
To the best of our knowledge, a comprehensive study of the change of measure/change of numéraire technique applied to such conditional expectations, is still missing.
It is worth mentioning that in
Cohen and Elliott 2015
the authors observed that linear BSDEs can be used to represent a measure change, also providing the formulation of a linear BSDE under two probability measures. The conditional expectation they obtained does not exhibit a recursive structure since the integrand is a predictable process not depending on the value process nor on the controls. We take this idea one step further. Indeed, we consider a fairly general structure of the driver which is relevant for the aforementioned applications, allowing us to generalize the “change of numéraire” technique, see
Geman et al. 1995
, so as to also cover the recursive conditional expectation case.

The paper is organised as follows: in Section
2
we present the probabilistic setup, whereas in Section
3
we derive our main result, i.e. Theorem
3.3
, subsequently, in Section
4
, we provide some financial applications.
Concerning these latter, we exploit our Theorem
3.3
to analyse the market model introduced in
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
focusing on the change from the physical to the risk-neutral measure, allowing us also to emphasize the link between our results and the benchmark approach proposed by
Platen and Heath 2006
. We also show how our method can be used to revisit the valuation of exchange options in a setting including collateral, permitting us to deduce a general procedure to perform changes of numéraire in non-linear market models. A simple example of market driven by a jump process is also provided.

Notations and setting

Let
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

, associated to

\tilde{N}(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)\coloneqq N(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)-\vartheta(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z),

and we introduce the following space of processes

•

•

||\phi||^{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|\phi_{t}|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right]<+\infty

As previously mentioned, the filtration
\mathbb{F}
supports both the Brownian motion
W
and the Poisson random measure
N

.

where

are
\mathcal{F}
-predictable processes integrable with respect to both

For the sake of completeness, let us also further

Assumption 2.2
.

N
is an integer-valued random measure with compensator

\vartheta(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)=Q(t,\mathrm{d}z)\eta(t)\mathrm{d}t

(2.3)

where

is a predictable process and

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{Y_{s}^{t,x}}{B_{s}}H_{s}\delta(z)\tilde{N}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle\left.-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{B_{s-}}H_{s-}\delta(z)U_{s-}^{t,x}N(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}z)\pm\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{B_{s-}}H_{s-}\delta(z)U_{s-}^{t,x}\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}s\right)

(3.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{B_{s}}f\left(s,X_{s-}^{t,x},Y_{s-}^{t,x},Z_{s}^{t,x},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\delta(z)\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\right)\mathrm{d}s+\frac{Z_{s}^{t,x}}{B_{s}}\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{\mathbb{P}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle-\frac{Z_{s}^{t,x}}{B_{s}}\beta(X_{s}^{t,x})\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{B_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta(z)U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{B_{s}}U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\tilde{N}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}z),

(3.19)

and since we also have

\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{Y_{s}^{t,x}}{B_{s}}\right)=\frac{1}{B_{s}}\mathrm{d}Y_{s}^{t,x}-\frac{Y_{s}^{t,x}}{B_{s}^{2}}\mathrm{d}B_{s},

(3.20)

then

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}Y_{s}^{t,x}

\displaystyle=B_{s}\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{Y_{s}^{t,x}}{B_{s}}\right)+\frac{Y_{s}^{t,x}}{B_{s}}\mathrm{d}B_{s}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f\left(s,X_{s-}^{t,x},Y_{s-}^{t,x},Z_{s}^{t,x},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\delta(z)\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\right)\mathrm{d}s

(3.22)

\displaystyle+Z_{s}^{t,x}\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{\mathbb{P}}-Z_{s}^{t,x}\beta(X_{s}^{t,x})\mathrm{d}s

(3.23)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta(z)U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\tilde{N}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}z)+\alpha(X_{s}^{t,x})Y_{s}^{t,x}\mathrm{d}s\;\,.

(3.24)

Summing up the previous points we recover
(
3.3
), namely:

-\mathrm{d}Y_{s}^{t,x}=\left(f\left(s,X_{s-}^{t,x},Y_{s-}^{t,x},Z_{s}^{t,x},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\delta(z)\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\right)+Z_{s}^{t,x}\beta(X_{s}^{t,x})\right.\\
\left.+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\delta(z)U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)-\alpha(X_{s}^{t,x})Y_{s}^{t,x}\right)\mathrm{d}s-Z_{s}^{t,x}\mathrm{d}W_{s}^{\mathbb{P}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U_{s}^{t,x}(z)\tilde{N}(\mathrm{d}s,\mathrm{d}z)\;.

(3.25)

Finally, since
\mathcal{E}^{T}
is a true
(\mathbb{P},\mathcal{F})
-martingale, we can apply the Girsanov Theorem as in Theorem 2.5.1 in
Delong 2013
. By repeating the previous steps under the equivalent measure
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we have that the
new
representation of the process
Y_{s}^{t,x}
under
\mathbb{P}^{2}
reads as follows:

Y_{t}^{t,x}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\left[g(X_{T}^{t,x})e^{-\int_{t}^{T}\alpha(X_{r}^{t,x})\mathrm{d}r}+\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\int_{t}^{r}\alpha(X_{s}^{t,x})\mathrm{d}s}\right.\\
\left.\cdot f\left(r,X_{r-}^{t,x},Y_{r-}^{t,x},Z_{r}^{t,x},\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}U_{r}^{t,x}(z)\frac{\delta(z)}{1+\delta(z)}\nu^{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(\mathrm{d}z)\right)\mathrm{d}r\,\big|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\;\mathbb{P}^{2}\,\mbox{a.s.}\,.

(3.26)

∎

We notice that changing the measure in (
3.10
) results in the appearance of the density process
\mathcal{E}^{T}
both next to the terminal condition (which is standard) and inside the integral term which is an uncommon feature of the non-linear and recursive context we are considering.

4
Financial Applications

In what follows, we adopt the setup of
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
, see also
Gnoatto and Seiffert 2021
. In particular, we fix a positive but finite time horizon
T
. All stochastic processes introduced in the sequel are defined on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
where the filtration
\mathbb{F}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{F}_{t},t\in\left[0,T\right]\}
satisfies usual assumptions, while
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is the trivial
\sigma-
algebra at initial time. All processes introduced in the sequel are assumed to be
\mathbb{F}
-adapted and we work with càdlàg versions.

We denote by
S^{i}
the price (ex-dividend price) of the i-th risky asset with cumulative dividend stream
A^{i}
with
i=1,\ldots,d
. We take the perspective of the hedger, i.e., the entity that sells a certain contract and constructs a hedging portfolio against the liability stemming from the contract. Let
\xi_{t}^{i}
denote the number of the hedger’s positions in asset
S^{i}
at time
t
. We let
B^{0}=B
be the cash account for unsecured funding and introduce
B^{1},\ldots,B^{d}
cash accounts allowing for the financing of repurchase agreements (repo trading) on the risky assets.
We assume that:

i)

S^{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
are semimartingales;

ii)

A^{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
are finite variation processes with
A_{0}^{i}=0
;

iii)

B^{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,d
are strictly positive and continuous processes of finite variation with
B_{0}^{j}=1
.

We introduce the cumulative dividend price
S^{i,cld}
, given by

S_{t}^{i,cld}\coloneqq S_{t}^{i}+B_{t}^{i}\int_{(0,t]}(B_{u}^{i})^{-1}\mathrm{d}A_{u}^{i}\;,\quad t\in[0,T]\;,

(4.1)

together with its discounted version

\hat{S}_{t}^{i,cld}\coloneqq\hat{S}_{t}^{i}+\int_{(0,t]}(B_{u}^{i})^{-1}\mathrm{d}A_{u}^{i}\;,

(4.2)

with
\hat{S}_{t}^{i,cld}=(B^{i})^{-1}S^{i}
.

A trading strategy, or dynamic portfolio, is composed of positions in risky assets
\xi^{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
, positions in unsecured funding repo accounts
\psi^{j}
,
i=0,\ldots,d
, and the cash account related to lending and borrowing collateral
\psi^{c,l}
,
\psi^{c,b}

\varphi=(\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{d},\psi^{0},\ldots,\psi^{d},\psi^{c,l},\psi^{c,b}).

(4.3)

Processes
(\xi^{1},\ldots,\xi^{d})
are assumed to be
\mathbb{F}
-predictable, while
(\psi^{0},\ldots,\psi^{d},\psi^{c,l},\psi^{c,b})
are
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, allowing the stochastic integrals we then introduce to be well defined. Trading strategies are subject to admissibility constraints. A contract is represented by means of its cumulative stream of cash flows, hence by a process
A
of finite variation with càdlàg paths, such that
A_{0^{-}}=0
. As to provide an example, if the hedger sells a standard call option written on
S^{i}
, with maturity time
T
and strike price
\mathcal{K}
, at a price
p
, then
A_{t}=p\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}(t)-(S_{T}^{i}-\mathcal{K})^{+}\mathbbm{1}_{[T]}(t)
.

To prevent arbitrage opportunities via trivial positions in the cash accounts, we impose the repo constraint

\psi_{t}^{i}B_{t}^{i}+\xi_{t}^{i}S_{t}^{i}=0\;,\;\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-a.s.

(4.4)

meaning that the holdings in the i-th cash account is instrumental to the purpose of financing the position in the i-th risky asset only.

In the setting of
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
, the meaning of a martingale measure is that of a measure such that all processes
\hat{S}^{i,cld}
are local martingales.

Proposition 4.1
.

[See Proposition 3.1 in
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
] Assume that all strategies available to the hedger are admissible and satisfy the repo constraint (
4.4
). If
\exists
a probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T})
such that
\mathbb{Q}\sim\mathbb{P}
and the processes
\hat{S}^{i,cld}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
are local
(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{F})
martingales, then the market model is free of arbitrage opportunities for the hedger.

Let us now specialize the above introduced setting by means of the following assumptions:

1.

All processes
B^{j}
are absolutely continuous, meaning that they can be written as
\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{j}=r_{t}^{j}B_{t}^{j}\mathrm{d}t
for some
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes
r^{j}
,
j=0,\ldots,d
. To simplify the treatment of existence and uniqueness of BSDEs, we assume that such rates are bounded.

2.

We let
W^{1,\mathbb{P}},\ldots,W^{d,\mathbb{P}}
be standard mutually independent Brownian motions and we define
W^{\mathbb{P}}=\begin{pmatrix}W^{1,\mathbb{P}}\\
\vdots\\
W^{d,\mathbb{P}}\end{pmatrix}
.

3.

We introduce vector fields
\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{d},\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{d}
which are assumed to be bounded stochastic process, and we write

\mu=\begin{pmatrix}\mu^{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mu^{d}\end{pmatrix}\;,\;\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{1}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\sigma^{d}\end{pmatrix}\;.

(4.5)

Each asset is then characterized by the following
dividend process

A_{t}^{i}=\int_{0}^{t}k_{u}^{i}S_{u}^{i}\mathrm{d}u\;,

(4.6)

where
k^{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
are
\mathbb{F}
-adapted bounded stochastic processes,

while the risky assets evolve according to

\mathrm{d}S=\mathrm{d}\begin{pmatrix}S_{t}^{1}\\
\vdots\\
S_{t}^{d}\end{pmatrix}=diag(S)\left(\mu_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}\right)\;,

(4.7)

where
\bar{\rho}_{t}
is a matrix such that
\rho_{t}=\bar{\rho}_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}^{\intercal}
represents the correlation matrix between

W^{1},\ldots,W^{d}
. Moreover,
\bar{\rho}
is such that
\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}
is invertible, for every
t\in[0,T]
.
Given the previous setting, let us recall the shape of the martingale measure
\mathbb{Q}
as stated in Lemma 5.2 in
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the measure
\mathbb{Q}
, risky assets evolve according to

\mathrm{d}\begin{pmatrix}S_{t}^{1}\\
\vdots\\
S_{t}^{d}\end{pmatrix}=diag(S)\left(\begin{pmatrix}r_{t}^{1}-k_{t}^{1}\\
\vdots\\
r_{t}^{d}-k_{t}^{d}\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{d}t+\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right),

(4.8)

where the multivariate Brownian motion
W^{\mathbb{Q}}
is given as

W^{\mathbb{Q}}\coloneqq W_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\Sigma_{u}\bar{\rho}_{u}\right)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{u}^{1}-r_{u}^{1}+k_{u}^{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{u}^{d}-r_{u}^{d}+k_{u}^{d}\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{d}u.

(4.9)

Given the assumptions
1
and
3
and denoting

\displaystyle\theta_{u}\coloneqq\left(\Sigma_{u}\bar{\rho}_{u}\right)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{u}^{1}-r_{u}^{1}+k_{u}^{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{u}^{d}-r_{u}^{d}+k_{u}^{d}\end{pmatrix},

(4.10)

it is immediate to conclude that the stochastic exponential

\mathcal{E}_{0}^{t}=\exp{-\int_0^t\theta_u^\intercal\ud W_u-\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t\theta_u^\intercal\theta_u\ud u}

(4.11)

is a true martingale such that
W^{\mathbb{Q}}
defined in (
4.9
) is a Brownian motion according to the Girsanov theorem.

4.1
Wealth Dynamics and Pricing under Different Measures

In what follows, we analyse the situation where collateral is exchanged in continuous time between the hedger and the counterparty to reduce possibly outstanding credit exposures.

Under the previously introduced probability space, we denote by
C
an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted stochastic process representing the exchanged collateral.

C^{+}
represents the collateral received while
C^{-}
is the collateral posted by the hedger. Collateral posted, resp. received, earns, resp. pays, an interest at a rate
r^{c,l}
, resp.
r^{c,b}
, and we set
C_{T}=0
, meaning that all collateral is returned at the terminal time when trading stops. The interest rates
r^{c,l},r^{c,b}
are assumed to be
\mathbb{F}
-adapted and bounded processes. When collateral is posted via cash and the receiver is allowed to use it to fund own trading activity, then, according to equations 5.23 and 5.24 in
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
, the wealth dynamics are as follows:

\mathrm{d}V_{t}(\varphi)=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi_{t}^{i}(\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{i}-r_{t}^{i}S_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}t+k_{t}^{i}S_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}t)+\mathrm{d}\hat{F}_{t}^{h}+\mathrm{d}A_{t},

(4.12)

where

\hat{F}_{t}^{h}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}C_{u}^{+}(r_{u}-r_{u}^{c,b})\mathrm{d}u-\int_{0}^{t}C_{u}^{-}(r_{u}-r_{u}^{c,l})\mathrm{d}u.

(4.13)

We are then in a position to apply the representation stated in Section
3.2
so as to obtain pricing formulas under different measures.

Assumption 4.3
.

A_{t}=p\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}(t)+X\mathbbm{1}_{[T]}(t)
for
X\in\mathcal{F}_{T}=\mathcal{F}_{T}^{S}
, where by
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}^{S}
we mean that the filtration is generated by the risky assets, and
p
is the initial price.

The collateral process is of the form
C=c(V)
, where
c
is a Lipschitz function.

Let us now apply Theorem
3.3
. We can substitute the
\mathbb{P}
dynamics in (
4.7
) inside (
4.12
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}V_{t}(\varphi)&=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi_{t}^{i}(\mu_{t}^{i}S_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}t+S_{t}^{i}\left(\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}\right)_{i}-r_{t}^{i}S_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}t+k_{t}^{i}S_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}t)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathrm{d}\hat{F}_{t}^{h}+\mathrm{d}A_{t}\end{split}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+\left\langle\xi_{t}diag(S_{t})\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t},\left(\Sigma_{u}\bar{\rho}_{u}\right)^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{u}^{1}-r_{u}^{1}+k_{u}^{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{u}^{d}-r_{u}^{d}+k_{u}^{d}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left\langle\xi_{t}diag(S_{t})\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t},\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}\right\rangle+\mathrm{d}\hat{F}_{t}^{h}+\mathrm{d}A_{t}\;.\end{split}

(4.15)

We set
Z\coloneqq\xi diag(S)\Sigma\bar{\rho}\,\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\theta
as in (
4.10
),therefore, we are left with a
BSDE providing a non-linear pricing rule, i.e.:

\mathrm{d}V_{t}(\varphi)=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+Z_{t}^{\intercal}\theta_{t}\mathrm{d}t+Z_{t}^{\intercal}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}+(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,b})C_{t}^{+}\mathrm{d}t\\
-(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,l})C_{t}^{-}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}A_{t}.

(4.16)

Therefore, by the results in Section
3.2
, we obtain the following version of Proposition 5.4 in
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
under
\mathbb{P}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X
be
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{S}
-measurable with
X\in\mathbb{L}_{T}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T}^{S})
, then there exists a unique solution
(V,Z)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{T}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{H}^{2}_{T}(\mathbb{R})
to the BSDE (
4.16
). The contract
A_{t}=p\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}(t)+X\mathbbm{1}_{[T]}(t)

\xi

We have

and the process

p_{t}=V_{t}(\varphi)=-\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{B_{t}}{B_{T}}X\mathcal{E}_{t}^{T}+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{B_{t}}{B_{u}}\mathcal{E}_{t}^{u}\right.\\
\left.\left[(r_{u}-r_{u}^{c,b})C_{u}^{+}-(r_{u}-r_{u}^{c,l})C_{u}^{-}\right]\mathrm{d}u|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

(4.17)

Remark 4.5
.

In the formula above, we recognize the term
\frac{B_{t}}{B_{T}}\mathcal{E}_{t}^{T}
as the stochastic discount factor or state price density in the terminology of financial economics, see e.g.
Duffie 2001
,
Barucci and Fontana 2017
.

Next, by exploiting the result derived in Theorem
3.3

as

\mathrm{d}V_{t}(\varphi)=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+Z_{t}^{\intercal}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}+(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,b})C_{t}^{+}\mathrm{d}t-(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,l})C_{t}^{-}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}A_{t}\;,

(4.18)

allowing us to reformulate Proposition 5.4 in
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
X
be
\mathcal{F}_{T}^{S}
-measurable with
X\in\mathbb{L}_{T}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T}^{S})
, then
\exists!

(V,Z)\in\mathbb{S}^{2}_{T}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{H}^{2}_{T}(\mathbb{R})

4.18

p_{t}=V_{t}(\varphi)=-\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\left[\frac{B_{t}}{B_{T}}X+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{B_{t}}{B_{u}}\left[(r_{u}-r_{u}^{c,b})C_{u}^{+}-(r_{u}-r_{u}^{c,l})C_{u}^{-}\right]\mathrm{d}u|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

(4.19)

Remark 4.7
.

A classical example for
X\in\mathbb{L}_{T}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T}^{S})

4.2
Benchmark approach interpretation

In what follows we further analyse (
4.17
), from the
Benchmark Approach
point of view.
Let us explicitly state the form of the product of the ratio of cash-accounts times the density process

\displaystyle\frac{B_{t}}{B_{T}}\mathcal{E}_{t}^{T}

\displaystyle=\exp{-\int_t^T r_s\ud s -\frac{1}{2}\int_t^T\theta_s^\intercal\theta_s\ud s-\int_t^T\theta_s^\intercal\ud W^\P}

(4.20)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp{\int_0^t r_s\ud s +\frac{1}{2}\int_0^t\theta_s^\intercal\theta_s\ud s+\int_0^t\theta_s^\intercal\ud W^\P}}{\exp{\int_0^T r_s\ud s +\frac{1}{2}\int_0^T\theta_s^\intercal\theta_s\ud s+\int_0^T\theta_s^\intercal\ud W^\P}}.

(4.21)

We recognize in this ratio the solution to the SDE satisfied by the
Growth Optimal Portfolio
(GOP) in the benchmark approach of
Platen and Heath 2006
, namely

\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}}{S_{t}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}}=r_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\langle\theta_{t},\theta_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}\rangle.

(4.22)

It is worth recalling that the GOP is the portfolio maximizing the expected logarithmic utility. In
Platen and Heath 2006
, the GOP plays a central role in financial evaluation. Indeed, it has the role of the numéraire for the physical probability measure
\mathbb{P}
, allowing for the derivation of a pricing formula for contingent claims under
\mathbb{P}
, i.e. the so-called
real-world pricing formula
.
The latter generalizes the risk-neutral valuation formula since it can be applied also when a risk-neutral measure does not exist.
Exploiting our previous calculations, we deduce that (
4.17
) can be written as in the following corollary, hence extending the real world pricing formula derived in
Platen and Heath 2006
in the presence of collateral and multiple interest rates.

Corollary 4.7.1
.

[The real World Pricing Formula - Case with collateral] The price of the claim
A_{t}=p\mathbbm{1}_{[0,T]}(t)+X\mathbbm{1}_{[T]}(t)
with
X\in\mathbb{L}_{T}^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{T}^{S})
is given by

p_{t}=V_{t}(\varphi)=-\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\left[\frac{S_{t}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}}{S_{T}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}}X+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{S_{t}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}}{S_{u}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}}\left[(r_{u}-r_{u}^{c,b})C_{u}^{+}-(r_{u}-r_{u}^{c,l})C_{u}^{-}\right]\mathrm{d}u|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\;,

(4.23)

where
S_{t}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}
is the growth optimal portfolio with dynamics

\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}}{S_{t}^{\delta^{\text{*}}}}=r_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\langle\theta_{t},\theta_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{P}}\rangle.

(4.24)

4.3
Exchange option example

In this subsection we reconsider the classical example of an exchange option, see
Geman et al. 1995
, but in the presence of collateral and multiple interest rates.
We define
\tilde{V}=\frac{V}{B}
the discounted wealth process. From Equation (
4.18
), the dynamics under the measure
\mathbb{Q}
are given by

\mathrm{d}\tilde{V}_{t}(\varphi)=\frac{Z_{t}^{\intercal}}{B_{t}}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\left((r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,b})C_{t}^{+}-(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,l})C_{t}^{-}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\mathrm{d}A_{t}.

(4.25)

From Proposition
4.1
we know that the ratio
\frac{S^{2}}{B^{2}}
is a
(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{F})
-local martingale.
Such ratio can be used to construct a new probability measure. Let us first compute the dynamics of
\frac{B^{2}}{S^{2}}
under
\mathbb{Q}
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\right)={}

\displaystyle\frac{B_{t}^{2}r_{t}^{2}\mathrm{d}t}{S_{t}^{2}}+B_{t}^{2}\left(-\frac{1}{(S_{t}^{2})^{2}}\left(r_{t}^{2}S_{t}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+S_{t}^{2}\left(\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{2}\right.\right.

(4.26)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\left.+\frac{1}{2}\frac{2}{(S_{t}^{2})^{3}}(S_{t}^{2})^{2}(\sigma^{2})^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)\right)

(4.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left((\sigma_{t}^{2})^{2}\mathrm{d}t-\sigma_{t}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{\rho}_{2,j}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q},j}\right)=\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left((\sigma_{t}^{2})^{2}\mathrm{d}t-\sigma_{t}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right)\;,

(4.28)

where
\left(\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{2}
refers to the second coordinate of the vector
\left(\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right)
and
\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}
is the second row of
\bar{\rho}_{t}
. We can write

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\right)={}

\displaystyle\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left((\sigma_{t}^{2})^{2}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\bar{\rho}_{\cdot,2,t}}{\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\bar{\rho}_{\cdot,2,t}}\mathrm{d}t-\sigma_{t}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}\right)

(4.29)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left(-\sigma_{t}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\left(\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}-\sigma_{t}^{2}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\cdot,2,t}}{\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\bar{\rho}_{\cdot,2,t}}\mathrm{d}t\right)\right)\;\,,

(4.30)

where we have
\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\bar{\rho}_{\cdot,2,t}=\rho_{2,2}=1
, since
\bar{\rho}
is a Cholesky factorization of the correlation matrix.

Define

•

\frac{\partial\mathbb{Q}^{S^{2}}}{\partial\mathbb{Q}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}\coloneqq\frac{S_{T}^{2}}{B_{T}^{2}}\frac{B_{0}^{2}}{S_{0}^{2}}\;;

•

\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}^{S^{2}}}\coloneqq\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}-\sigma_{t}^{2}\frac{\left(\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\right)^{\intercal}}{\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\bar{\rho}_{\cdot,2,t}}\mathrm{d}t\;.

Since

\mathrm{d}\tilde{V}_{t}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{B_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{t}^{1}S_{t}^{i}\sigma_{t}^{i}\bar{\rho}_{i,j}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q},j}\\
+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\left((r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,b})C_{t}^{+}-(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,l})C_{t}^{-}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\mathrm{d}A_{t}\;,

(4.31)

then the dynamics of the product
\frac{V}{B}\frac{B^{2}}{S^{2}}
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{V}{B}\frac{B^{2}}{S^{2}}\right)_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{B_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\xi_{t}^{1}S_{t}^{i}\sigma_{t}^{i}\bar{\rho}_{i,j}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q},j}\right.

(4.32)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\left.+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\left((r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,b})C_{t}^{+}-(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,l})C_{t}^{-}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\mathrm{d}A_{t}\right)

(4.33)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{V_{t}}{B_{t}}\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left((\sigma_{t}^{2})^{2}\mathrm{d}t-\sigma_{t}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bar{\rho}_{2,j}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q},j}\right)

(4.34)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\mathrm{d}\left\langle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{\cdot}\xi_{u}^{1}S_{u}^{i}\sigma_{u}^{i}\bar{\rho}_{i,j,u}\mathrm{d}W_{u}^{\mathbb{Q},j},-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{2,j,u}\mathrm{d}W_{u}^{\mathbb{Q},j}\right\rangle_{t}\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{B_{t}S_{t}^{2}}\;.

(4.35)

Concerning the quadratic covariation term we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left\langle\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{\cdot}\xi_{u}^{1}S_{u}^{i}\sigma_{u}^{i}\bar{\rho}_{i,j,u}\mathrm{d}W_{u}^{\mathbb{Q},j},-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{\cdot}\sigma_{u}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{2,j,u}\mathrm{d}W_{u}^{\mathbb{Q},j}\right\rangle_{t}\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{B_{t}S_{t}^{2}}

(4.36)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi_{t}^{1}S_{t}^{i}\sigma_{t}^{i}\sigma_{t}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\bar{\rho}_{i,j,t}\bar{\rho}_{2,k,t}\mathrm{d}\langle W_{\cdot}^{\mathbb{Q},j},\mathrm{d}W_{\cdot}^{\mathbb{Q},k}\rangle_{t}\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{B_{t}S_{t}^{2}}

(4.37)

\displaystyle=-\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{B_{t}S_{t}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi_{t}^{1}S_{t}^{i}\sigma_{t}^{i}\sigma_{t}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\bar{\rho}_{i,k,t}\bar{\rho}_{2,k,t}\mathrm{d}t

(4.38)

\displaystyle=-\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{B_{t}S_{t}^{2}}\left(\xi_{t}diag(S_{t})\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}\right)\sigma_{t}^{2}(\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t})^{\intercal}\mathrm{d}t\;,

(4.39)

where we recall that
\xi_{t}diag(S_{t})\Sigma_{t}\bar{\rho}_{t}=Z_{t}
and
\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}
denotes the second row of
\bar{\rho}_{t}
. Collecting all terms we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\left(\frac{V}{B}\frac{B^{2}}{S^{2}}\right)_{t}&=\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{B_{t}}Z_{t}^{\intercal}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\left((r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,b})C_{t}^{+}-(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,l})C_{t}^{-}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\mathrm{d}A_{t}\right)\\
&\qquad\quad+\frac{V_{t}}{B_{t}}\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left(-\sigma_{t}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\left(\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}-\sigma_{t}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\cdot,2,t}\mathrm{d}t\right)\right)-\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{B_{t}S_{t}^{2}}Z_{t}^{\intercal}\sigma_{t}^{2}(\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t})^{\intercal}\mathrm{d}t\;,\end{aligned}

(4.40)

therefore, after rearranging, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathrm{d}\left(\frac{V}{B}\frac{B^{2}}{S^{2}}\right)_{t}&=\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{B_{t}}Z_{t}^{\intercal}\left(\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}-\sigma_{t}^{2}(\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t})^{\intercal}\mathrm{d}t\right)\right.\\
&\qquad\quad\left.+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\left((r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,b})C_{t}^{+}-(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,l})C_{t}^{-}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{B_{t}}\mathrm{d}A_{t}\right]\\
&\qquad\quad-\frac{V_{t}}{B_{t}}\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\sigma_{t}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}\left(\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}}-\sigma_{t}^{2}(\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t})^{\intercal}\mathrm{d}t\right)\;.\end{aligned}

(4.42)

If we now define
\tilde{Z}\coloneqq Z_{t}-V_{t}\sigma_{t}^{2}\bar{\rho}_{2,\cdot,t}
, then, by the Girsanov Theorem, we have:

\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{V_{t}}{B_{t}}\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}}\right)=\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}B_{t}}\tilde{Z}^{\intercal}\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{\mathbb{Q}^{S^{2}}}+\frac{B_{t}^{2}}{S_{t}^{2}B_{t}}\left[\left((r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,b})C_{t}^{+}-(r_{t}-r_{t}^{c,l})C_{t}^{-}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}A_{t}\right]\;.

(4.44)

We omit the conditional expectation representation of the BSDE above, since it is immediate. Now, under the further assumption
r^{2}=r
, by applying Theorem
3.3
we recover, the usual change of Numéraire, but in the presence of a non-linear, recursive integral term:

p_{t}=-S_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{S^{2}}}\left[\frac{X}{S_{T}^{2}}+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{1}{S_{s}^{2}}\left[(r_{s}-r_{s}^{c,b})C_{s}^{+}-(r_{s}-r_{s}^{c,l})C_{s}^{-}\right]\mathrm{d}s|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\;.

(4.45)

In the present example,
X
is the payoff of the exchange option, i.e.

X=(S_{T}^{1}-S_{T}^{2})^{+}.

(4.46)

In summary, we obtain

p_{t}=-S_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{S^{2}}}\left[\frac{(S_{T}^{1}-S_{T}^{2})^{+}}{S_{T}^{2}}+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{1}{S_{s}^{2}}\left[(r_{s}-r_{s}^{c,b})C_{s}^{+}-(r_{s}-r_{s}^{c,l})C_{s}^{-}\right]\mathrm{d}s|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\;,

(4.47)

i.e.

p_{t}=-S_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}^{S^{2}}}\left[\left(\frac{S_{T}^{1}}{S_{T}^{2}}-1\right)^{+}+\int_{t}^{T}\frac{1}{S_{s}^{2}}\left[(r_{s}-r_{s}^{c,b})C_{s}^{+}-(r_{s}-r_{s}^{c,l})C_{s}^{-}\right]\mathrm{d}s\big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\;.

(4.48)

When there is no collateral, we recover the classical Margrabe’s formula under the standard Black-Scholes multivariate model.

4.4
Pure jump setting example

We assume that the risky asset evolves now according to

\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{i}}{S_{t-}^{i}}=\mu^{i}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{z}-1)(N-\nu^{\mathbb{P}})(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)\;.

(4.49)

which corresponds to the SDE associated to the class of exponential Lévy models.
For the sake of the present example, we assume that
N
has compensator
\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}t=\lambda\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}t
, i.e. we consider a Poisson process with constant intensity
\lambda
and fixed jump size
\alpha
. We construct the martingale measure along the following steps. In line with
Bielecki and Rutkowski 2015
, our objective is to guarantee that the process

\tilde{S}_{t}^{i}\coloneqq\left(\frac{S^{i}}{B^{i}}\right)_{0\leq t\leq T}

(4.50)

is a local martingale.
Consider next

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\tilde{S}_{t}^{i}

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}_{t-}^{i}\left((\mu^{i}-r^{i})\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{z}-1)(N-\nu^{\mathbb{P}})(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)\right)

(4.51)

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}_{t-}^{i}\left((e^{\alpha}-1)N(\mathrm{d}t,\{\alpha\})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{z}-1)\lambda\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(z)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t)+(\mu^{i}-r^{i})\mathrm{d}t\right)

(4.52)

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}_{t-}^{i}\left((e^{\alpha}-1)N(\mathrm{d}t,\{\alpha\})-\left((e^{\alpha}-1)\lambda-(\mu^{i}-r^{i})\right)\mathrm{d}t\right)

(4.53)

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}_{t-}^{i}\left((e^{\alpha}-1)N(\mathrm{d}t,\{\alpha\})-(e^{\alpha}-1)\left(\lambda-\frac{\mu^{i}-r^{i}}{e^{\alpha}-1}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right)

(4.54)

\displaystyle=\tilde{S}_{t-}^{i}\left((e^{\alpha}-1)N(\mathrm{d}t,\{\alpha\})-(e^{\alpha}-1)\lambda\left(1-\frac{\mu^{i}-r^{i}}{(e^{\alpha}-1)\lambda}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right)\;.

(4.55)

Recalling the notation of Theorem
3.3
, this means that we have

\delta(z)=\delta=-\frac{\mu-t}{(e^{\alpha}-1)\lambda}\;,

(4.56)

which is the Girsanov kernel in the present setting. Under
\mathbb{Q}
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{i}}{S_{t-}^{i}}=r^{i}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{z}-1)(N-\nu^{\mathbb{P}})(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)\;,

(4.57)

with

\displaystyle\nu^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle=(1+\delta(z))\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)

(4.58)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\mu-r}{(e^{\alpha}-1)\lambda}\right)\lambda\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(\mathrm{d}z)\;.

(4.59)

In summary we have:

Under
\mathbb{P}

\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{i}}{S_{t-}^{i}}=\mu^{i}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{z}-1)(N-\nu^{\mathbb{P}})(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)\;,\;i.e.

(4.60)

S_{t}^{i}=S_{0}^{i}\exp{\mu t-\int_\bR(e^z-1-z)\nu(\ud z)t + \int_\bR z(N-\nu^\P)(\ud t, \ud z)}\;.

(4.61)

Under
\mathbb{Q}

\frac{\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{i}}{S_{t-}^{i}}=r^{i}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{z}-1)(N-\nu^{\mathbb{Q}})(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)\;,\;

(4.62)

\nu^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{d}z)=(1+\delta(z))\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)=\left(1-\frac{\mu-r}{(e^{\alpha}-1)\lambda}\right)\lambda\mathbbm{1}_{\alpha}(\mathrm{d}z)\;.

(4.63)

Density process
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{T}

\mathcal{E}_{t}^{T}=\exp\left\{\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ln(1+\delta(z))\right.\\
\left.-\delta(z)\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{t}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ln(1+\delta(z))\tilde{N}(\mathrm{d}r,\mathrm{d}z)\right\}\,.

(4.64)

4.4.1
Portfolio dynamics with jumps

We start again from the wealth dynamics

\mathrm{d}V_{t}(\varphi)=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi_{t}^{i}(\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{i}-r_{t}^{i}S_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}t+k_{t}^{i}S_{t}^{i}\mathrm{d}t)+\mathrm{d}\hat{F}_{t}^{h}+\mathrm{d}A_{t}\;,

(4.65)

then, substituting the
\mathbb{P}
dynamics of
S
, and setting
d=1
, we have

\mathrm{d}V_{t}(\varphi)=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+\xi_{t}^{1}\left(S_{t-}^{1}\left(\mu^{1}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{z}-1)(N-\nu^{\mathbb{P}})(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)\right)-r^{1}S^{1}\mathrm{d}t\right)\\
+\mathrm{d}\hat{F}_{t}^{h}+\mathrm{d}A_{t}\;,

(4.66)

it follows that, setting

U^{1}\coloneqq\xi^{1}S_{t-}^{1}

(4.67)

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}V_{t}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+U_{t}^{1}(\mu^{1}-r^{1})\mathrm{d}t+U_{t-}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{z}-1)(N-\nu^{\mathbb{P}})(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)

(4.68)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{d}\hat{F}_{t}^{h}+\mathrm{d}A_{t}

\displaystyle=r_{t}V_{t}(\varphi)\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{t-}^{1}\frac{\mu^{1}-r^{1}}{(e^{\alpha}-1)\lambda}(e^{z}-1)\nu^{\mathbb{P}}(\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}}U_{t-}^{1}(e^{z}-1)(N-\nu^{\mathbb{P}})(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}z)+\mathrm{d}\hat{F}_{t}^{h}+\mathrm{d}A_{t}\;,

allowing us to apply
Theorem
3.3
in the present pure jump setting.

4.5
A generalization of the change of numéraire technique

The reasoning we illustrated in Section
4.1
provides us with a generalization of the well-known change of numéraire technique that covers recursive conditional expectations.
The technique involves the following steps:

1.

Start by considering wealth dynamics under a starting probability measure
\mathbb{Q}
, for example as in Equation
4.18
for
\tilde{V}
;

2.

Consider a traded asset, suitably discounted so as to treat a
(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{F})
-martingale (the ratio
\frac{S^{2}}{B^{2}}
in Section
4.3
) and compute its (inverse) dynamics via the Itô formula;

3.

Identify the stochastic drivers under the new measure
\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}
;

4.

Compute the dynamics of the product between
\tilde{V}
and the (inverse) density process;

5.

Apply Theorem
3.3
to obtain the expression for the price under the new probability measure
\mathbb{Q}^{\prime}
.

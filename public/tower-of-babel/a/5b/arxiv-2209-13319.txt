1.
Introduction

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.
A sequence of ideals
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called an
I
-admissible filtration
if
(i)

I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n}
,

(ii)

I_{m}I_{n}\subseteq I_{m+n}
and
(iii)~I^{n}\subseteq I_{n}\subseteq I^{n-k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. A
reduction
of
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}
is an ideal
J\subseteq I_{1}
such that
JI_{n}=I_{n+1}
for
n\gg 0
and it is called
minimal reduction
if it is minimal with respect to containment among all reductions. A Minimal reduction of
\mathcal{I}
exists and is generated by
d
elements if
R/\mathfrak{m}
is infinite. Minimal reductions are important in the study of Hilbert functions and blow-up algebras. For a minimal reduction
J
of
\mathcal{I}
, we define

r_{J}(\mathcal{I})=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid I_{n}\not=JI_{n-1}\}\mbox{ and }r(\mathcal{I})=\min\{r_{J}(\mathcal{I})~|~J\mbox{ is a minimal reduction of }\mathcal{I}\},

known as
reduction number of
\mathcal{I}
with respect to
J
and
reduction number of
\mathcal{I}
respectively.
We write
r_{J}(I)
and
r(I)
in place of
r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
and
r(\mathcal{I})
respectively for
\mathcal{I}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Reduction number is an important data associated to an ideal which contains information about the depth and structural properties of the associated graded ring
G(\mathcal{I})=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}I_{n}/I_{n+1}
. The number
r_{J}(I)
can be seen as a measure of how closely
J
and
I
are related. In general, it may be hard to compute
r_{J}(I)
. We look for bounding
r_{J}(I)
in terms of other invariants of the ring or the ideal such as embedding dimension, Hilbert coefficients etc.
The Hilbert coefficients of
\mathcal{I}
are the unique integers
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
,
0\leq i\leq d,
such that the function
H_{\mathcal{I}}(n):=\lambda(R/I_{n})
coincides with the following polynomial for
n\gg 0:

P_{\mathcal{I}}(x)=e_{0}(\mathcal{I}){x+d-1\choose d}-e_{1}(\mathcal{I}){x+d-2\choose d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}(\mathcal{I}).

Here
\lambda(*)
denotes the length function. The function
H_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
and the polynomial
P_{\mathcal{I}}(x)
are known as the Hilbert-Samuel function and the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of
\mathcal{I}
respectively. For
\mathcal{I}=\{I^{n}\}
, we write
e_{i}(I)
instead of
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
. We refer to
[
19
]
for the related background material.

It is well known that if
0ptG(I)\geq d-1
, then
r_{J}(I)
does not depend on the minimal reduction
J
. Further, if
R
is a one dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring then
r(I)\leq e_{0}(I)-1
. In
[
20
, Theorem 2.45]
, Vasconcelos proved that in a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
,

(1)

r(I)\leq\frac{d.e_{0}(I)}{o(I)}-2d+1

where
o(I)
is the largest positive integer
n
such that
I\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{n}
.

A non-Cohen-Macaulay version of the above result can be found in
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
. Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension at most two. In
[
16
, Corollary 1.5]
, Rossi proved that

(2)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1

for a minimal reduction
J\subseteq I.

Since then many attempts have been made to achieve a bound of similar character in higher dimension. For instance, the bound in (
2
) holds in all dimensions if
0ptG(I)\geq d-2

[
19
, Theorem 4.3]
or if
e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)=1

[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
. Another case is when
I\subseteq k[x,y,z]
is of codimension
3
generated by five quadrics
[
6
, Theorem 2.1 and Proposition 2.4]
. However, no example is known to counter the
relation in (
2
)
in higher dimension. In this paper, our objective is to find bounds for
r_{J}(I)
in dimension three involving higher Hilbert coefficients. We prove the following generalization of Rossi’s result
[
19
, Theorem 4.3]
in dimension three:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension three and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with
0ptG(I^{t})>0
for some
t\geq 1.
Let
J\subseteq I
be a minimal reduction of
I
. Then

(3)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+t.

Furthermore, if
r_{J}(I)\equiv k\mod t
,
1\leq k\leq t-1
, then

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+k.

As a consequence, if
r_{J}(I)
is odd and
0ptG(I^{2})>0
then

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1.
Furthermore, we prove the following bound in dimension
d\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
- primary ideal with
0ptG(I)\geq d-3
. Then

(4)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

Though the bound in (
4
) is quadratic in
e_{2}(I)
, we illustrate various examples where it is tighter than earlier known bounds. For small values of
e_{2}(I)
in dimension three, we obtain linear bounds for
r_{J}(I)
in terms of
e_{i}(I)
,
0\leq i\leq 3
, in Corollary
4.3
.

It is worth asking if we can find similar bounds for
r_{J}(I)
in Noetherian local rings. In
[
5
]
, Ghezzi et. al. proved a nonlinear bound in terms of Hilbert coefficients in two dimensional Buchsbaum local rings of positive depth. We prove the following results:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Buchsbaum local ring of dimension
d\leq 2
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.

(1)

Let
d=1
. Then
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{1}(J)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+2
.

(2)

Let
d=2
and
0ptG(I^{t})>0
for some
t\geq 1
. Then
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{1}(J)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+t+1
.

The main difficulty in generalizing the bound in (
2
) in higher dimension is that
r_{J}(I)
does not behave well with respect to superficial elements. This fact is closely related to Ratliff-Rush closure
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}
of powers of
I
, see Lemma
3.1
. We recall the definition more generally for an
I
-admissible filtration
\mathcal{I}
. The Ratliff-Rush filtration of
\mathcal{I}
is the filtration
\{\widetilde{I_{n}}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{t\geq 0}(I_{n+t}:I^{t})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
For a minimal reduction
J
of
\mathcal{I}
, we set

\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I}):=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}|\widetilde{I_{n}}\not=J\widetilde{I_{n-1}}\}.

We write
\widetilde{r}_{J}(I)
if
\mathcal{I}=\{I^{n}\}
. Note that if
0ptG(I)>0
, then
\widetilde{r}_{J}(I)=r_{J}(I)
. In
[
17
]
, Rossi and Swanson proved that in a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension two
\widetilde{r}_{J}(I)\leq r_{J}(I)
. It follows that
\widetilde{r}_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1
in dimension two. We extend the result of Rossi and Swanson for any
I
-admissible filtration in Proposition
2.1
.

It is natural to ask if
r_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq e_{1}(\mathcal{I})-e_{0}(\mathcal{I})+\lambda(R/I_{1})+1
for an
I
-admissible filtration
\mathcal{I}
. This is largely unknown, even in smaller dimensions.
In Theorem
2.2
, we prove that
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq e_{2}(\mathcal{I})+1
and subsequently discuss the cases when
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})+1
and
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})
.

This paper is organised in four sections.
In Section
2
, we prove
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I})

and discuss bounds on
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})
in two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings. In Section
3
, we establish Theorem
1.1
and its consequences. Then we gather various cases when the bound
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1
holds in dimension three.
We also prove Theorem
1.3
in this section.
In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
Examples are given in support of our bounds being better than the earlier known bounds.

2.
Bound for
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})
in dimension two

For this section, let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension two. Inspired by Rossi’s bound in (
2
), one can ask whether
r_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq e_{1}(\mathcal{I})-e_{0}(\mathcal{I})+\lambda(R/I_{1})+1
for an
I
-admissible filtration
\mathcal{I}
? In general, it is not known. Suppose
I=\tilde{I}
. Then for the case
\mathcal{I}=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, the above question has affirmative answer which follows from a result of Rossi and Swanson
[
17
, Proposition 4.5]
. They proved that
\widetilde{r}_{J}(I)\leq r_{J}(I)
. We generalize
[
17
, Proposition 4.5]
for an
I
admissible filtration. Further in this section, we prove certain bounds for
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension two,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}
an
I
-admissible filtration. Then, for a minimal reduction
J
of
\mathcal{I}
,
\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
.

Proof.

Let
r=r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
and
J=(x,y)
with
x,y
a regular sequence in
R
. We show that for all
n\geq r
,
\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I_{n}}.
For
k\gg 0
, we may write
\widetilde{I_{m}}=(I_{m+k}:(x^{k},y^{k}))
for
m=n-1,n,n+1
. Let
a\in\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=(I_{n+1+k}:(x^{k},y^{k}))=(J^{k+1}I_{n}:(x^{k},y^{k}))
. Then
ax^{k}\in J^{k+1}I_{n}\subseteq x^{k}xI_{n}+yI_{n+k}.
Let
ax^{k}=x^{k}b+yc
where
b\in xI_{n}
and
c\in I_{n+k}.
This gives
x^{k}(a-b)=yc
. Since
x,y
is a regular sequence we have
a-b=dy
and
c=x^{k}d
for some
d\in R
. As
c\in I_{n+k}
, we get
d\in(I_{n+k}:x^{k})
and
a-b=dy\in y(I_{n+k}:x^{k})
. Therefore,

a\in xI_{n}+y(I_{n+k}:x^{k}).

By similar arguments, we can show that
a\in yI_{n}+x(I_{n+k}:y^{k})
. Now let
a=xr_{1}+ys_{1}=yr_{2}+xs_{2}
where
r_{1},r_{2}\in I_{n}
,
s_{1}\in(I_{n+k}:x^{k})
and
s_{2}\in(I_{n+k}:y^{k})
. Then
x(r_{1}-s_{2})=y(r_{2}-s_{1})
which implies
r_{2}-s_{1}=\alpha x
and
r_{1}-s_{2}=\alpha y
for some
\alpha\in R
. Then
\alpha x^{k+1}=r_{2}x^{k}-s_{1}x^{k}\in I_{n+k}
and
\alpha y^{k+1}=r_{1}y^{k}-s_{2}y^{k}\in I_{n+k}
. These give
\alpha\in(I_{n+k}:(x^{k+1},y^{k+1}))=\widetilde{I_{n-1}}.
Therefore
r_{1}-s_{2}=\alpha y\in J\widetilde{I_{n-1}}\subseteq\widetilde{I_{n}}
and
r_{2}-s_{1}=\alpha x\in J\widetilde{I_{n-1}}\subseteq\widetilde{I_{n}}
. This gives
s_{1},s_{2}\in\widetilde{I_{n}}
and
a=xr_{1}+ys_{1}\in JI_{n}+J\widetilde{I_{n}}\subseteq J\widetilde{I_{n}}
. Therefore
\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for all
n\geq r
.
∎

, we see a class of examples with

. In the next theorem, we give an upper bound for

in dimension two. We may assume from now on that

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}
an

,

\widetilde{r}_{J}({\mathcal{I}})\leq e_{2}(\mathcal{I})+1.

Furthermore, consider the following statements:

(i)

\widetilde{r}_{J}({\mathcal{I}})=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})+1
;

(ii)

\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for all
n\neq 0,e_{2}(\mathcal{I})
;

\lambda(\widetilde{I_{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I_{n}})=1
for

We have
(iv)

\implies

(iii)

\implies

(i)

\Longleftrightarrow

(ii)
and all four are equivalent if
e_{2}(\mathcal{I})\neq 0.

Proof.

Since
0ptG(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})\geq 1,
we have

by
[
19
, Theorem 2.5]
, where
v_{n}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})=\lambda(\widetilde{I_{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I_{n}})
. This gives
nv_{n}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})=0
for all

. Hence
\widetilde{{I}_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{{I}_{n}}
for all
n\geq e_{2}(\mathcal{I})+1
, i.e.,
\widetilde{r}_{J}({\mathcal{I}})\leq e_{2}(\mathcal{I})+1
.

Now we show
(i)
\Longleftrightarrow

(ii)
. Suppose
\widetilde{r}_{J}({\mathcal{I}})=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})+1
. Then
\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for all
n\geq e_{2}(\mathcal{I})+1
and
\widetilde{I_{n+1}}\neq J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for

. This gives
(ii)
when
e_{2}(\mathcal{I})=0
. When

. For the converse, suppose the assumption in

holds. When

,

\implies

(iii)
and
(ii)
\implies

(iii)

.

Suppose

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. If
\widetilde{r}_{J}({I})=e_{2}(I)+1\neq 1
then

1\leq e_{2}(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1\leq\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)+1.

Moreover, if
I
is Ratliff-Rush closed then the following statements hold:

(i)

r_{J}(I)=2

(ii)

e_{2}(I)=1

(iii)

0ptG(I)\geq 1

Proof.

We have

(5)

\displaystyle e_{2}(I)+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}_{J}(I)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r_{J}(I)\hskip 28.45274pt(\mbox{by Proposition \ref{mafi}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1\mbox{ (by }\eqref{rossi-bound})

By Theorem
2.2
,
e_{1}(I)=e_{0}(I)-\lambda(R/\widetilde{I})+1
. Substituting the value in equation (

), we get
e_{2}(I)+1\leq r_{J}(I)\leq\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)+2
which implies

1\leq e_{2}(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1\leq\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)+1.

Moreover if

is Ratliff-Rush closed, then we obtain

and

. Then by
[

, Theorem 3.3]
we have

.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}
an
I
-admissible filtration.
For a minimal reduction
J\subseteq I
,
if
\widetilde{r}_{J}({\mathcal{I}})=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})

then the following statements hold:

(i)

\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for
n\neq 0,1,e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1
;

(ii)

\lambda(\widetilde{I_{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I_{n}})=\begin{cases}2\text{ for }n=1\text{ if }e_{2}(\mathcal{I})=2,\\
1\text{ for }n=1,e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1\text{ if }e_{2}(\mathcal{I})\neq 2,\end{cases}

(iii)

e_{1}(\mathcal{I})=e_{0}(\mathcal{I})-\lambda(R/\widetilde{I_{1}})+2
.

Proof.

Note that
\widetilde{r}_{J}({\mathcal{I}})=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})
if and only if
\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for all
n\geq e_{2}(\mathcal{I})
and
\widetilde{I_{n+1}}\neq J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for
n=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1
. Since
0ptG(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})\geq 1,
we have
e_{2}(\mathcal{I})=\sum_{n=0}^{e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1}nv_{n}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})
. Therefore
e_{2}(\mathcal{I})\neq 1
.
Now suppose
e_{2}(\mathcal{I})=2.
Then
\lambda(\widetilde{I_{2}}/J\widetilde{I_{1}})=2
and
\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for
n\geq 2
. For the case
e_{2}(\mathcal{I})\geq 3
, we get

2(e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1)>e_{2}(\mathcal{I})\geq(e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1)v_{e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}).

So,
v_{e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})=1
. This gives
1=e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-(e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1)v_{e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})=\sum_{n=0}^{e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-2}nv_{n}(\widetilde{\mathcal{I}})
which implies
\lambda(\widetilde{I_{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I_{n}})=1
for
n=1
and
\widetilde{I_{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I_{n}}
for all
2\leq n\leq e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-2.
This proves
(i)
and
(ii)
.
To see

3.
Rossi’s bound in dimension three

In general, reduction number does not behave well with respect to a superficial element
x\in I
, i.e.,
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))=r_{J}(I)
may not hold.
When
0ptG(I)\geq 1
, then
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))=r_{J}(I)
, see
[
11
, Lemma 2.14]
.
However, there are examples when
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))=r_{J}(I)
and
0ptG(I)=0.

Note that
0ptG(I)\geq 1
is equivalent to the condition that
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. In the lemma below, we state a necessary condition for
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))<r_{J}(I)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
and
0ptR>0
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
a minimal reduction of
I
. If
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))<r_{J}(I)
for a superficial element
x\in I
, then
\widetilde{I^{n}}\neq I^{n}
for all
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))\leq n<r_{J}(I)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for some
n
with
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))\leq n<r_{J}(I)
. Then
I^{n}\subseteq(I^{n+1}:x)\subseteq(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}:x)=\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
. Thus
(I^{n+1}:x)=I^{n}
which implies
I^{n+1}\cap(x)=xI^{n}
. On the other hand,
(I/(x))^{n+1}=J/(x)(I/(x))^{n}
which implies
I^{n+1}\subseteq JI^{n}+(x)
.
Hence
I^{n+1}\subseteq JI^{n}+(x)\cap I^{n+1}=JI^{n}+xI^{n}=JI^{n}
. So
r_{J}(I)\leq n
which is a contradiction.
∎

We define

\rho(I)=\min\{i\geq 1|\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}\text{ for all }n\geq i\}.

As an interesting application of Lemma
3.1
, we see that Rossi’s bound holds in dimension three for those
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals
I
for which
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d=3
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
primary ideal. For a minimal reduction
J
of
I
, if
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1
, then
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1.

Proof.

Let
x\in I
be a superficial element. Suppose
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1
. Then
I^{r_{J}(I)-1}=\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)-1}}
which implies
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))=r_{J}(I)
by Lemma
3.1
. Now, using the bound in (
2
), we get that
r_{J}(I)=r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)+1
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with
0ptG(I^{t})>0
for some

Let
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I
and

Furthermore, if
r_{J}(I)\equiv k\mod t

, then

(6)

r_{J}(I)\leq r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))+k-1.

Proof.

Since
0ptG(I^{t})>0
, we have
0ptR>0
. We first consider the case when
r_{J}(I)\equiv k\mod t
for

and prove (

for

\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

the Rees algebra of

H(\mathcal{I},z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\lambda(I_{n}/I_{n+1})z^{n}
, is rational, i.e., there exists a unique rational polynomial
h_{\mathcal{I}}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}[z]
with
h_{\mathcal{I}}(1)\neq 0
such that

H(\mathcal{I},z)=\frac{h_{\mathcal{I}}(z)}{(1-z)^{d}}.

For every
i\geq 0
, we define
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})=\frac{h_{\mathcal{I}}^{(i)}(1)}{i!}
, where
h_{\mathcal{I}}^{(i)}(1)
denotes the
i
-th formal derivative of the polynomial
h_{\mathcal{I}}(z)
at
z=1
. The integers
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
are called the Hilbert coefficients of
\mathcal{I}
and for
0\leq i\leq d
, these are same as defined earlier in the Introduction, see
[
3
]
for more details.

Let us recall the modified Koszul complex in dimension two defined in
[
11
]
as follows:

C_{.}({\mathcal{I}},n):~~~~~0\longrightarrow R/{{I}_{n-2}}\overset{\begin{pmatrix}-y\\
~x\end{pmatrix}}{\longrightarrow}(R/{I}_{n-1})^{2}\overset{(x,y)}{\longrightarrow}R/{I}_{n}\longrightarrow 0,

where
(x,y)
is a minimal reduction of
I
. Let
H_{i}(C_{.}(\mathcal{I},n))
denote the
i
-th homology module of the complex
C_{.}(\mathcal{I},n)
. The relation between the homology of this complex and Hilbert coefficients is used in the proof of the next theorem. For a numerical function
f:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, we put
\triangle f(n)=f(n+1)-f(n)
and recursively we can define
\triangle^{i}f(n):=\triangle(\triangle^{i-1}f(n))
for all
i\geq 1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
- primary ideal with
0ptG(I)\geq d-3
. Then

(9)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

Proof.

Suppose
d=3
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}
,
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I
and
J=(x,y,z)
a minimal reduction of
I
. Then
x
is also superficial for the filtration
\mathcal{F}
. Let
\overline{R}=R/(x)
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}=\{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n}=\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}\}
. Since
0ptG(\mathcal{F})\geq 1
, we have
v_{n}(\mathcal{F})=v_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})
.
By the proof of
[
7
, Proposition 2.9]
, we have

(10)

\displaystyle e_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\triangle^{2}(P_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(n)-H_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(n))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big(e_{0}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})-\sum_{i=0}^{2}(-1)^{i}\lambda(H_{i}(C_{.}(\overline{\mathcal{F}},n)))\Big)\mbox{ (by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{marley}{}{}, Proposition 3.2]})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}v_{n}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})-\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda(H_{2}(C_{.}(\overline{\mathcal{F}},n)))\mbox{ (by the proof of \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{marley}{}{}, Theorem 3.6]})}.

Since
x
is a superficial element for
\mathcal{F}
,
e_{1}(I)=e_{1}(\mathcal{F})=e_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})
.
Therefore, by using (
7
) and (
10
), we get

(11)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda(H_{2}(C_{.}(\overline{\mathcal{F}},n)))-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1.

From the modified Koszul complex
C_{.}(\overline{\mathcal{F}},n)
, we have
H_{2}(C.(\overline{\mathcal{F}},n))=\frac{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}:(\overline{y},\overline{z})}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n-2}}
.
Since
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n-1}:(\overline{y},\overline{z})\subseteq\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{n-2}
,

\lambda(H_{2}(C.(\overline{\mathcal{F}},n))\leq\lambda\left(\frac{\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{n-2}}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n-2}}\right).

Therefore, for large
m
we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda(H_{2}(C.(\overline{\mathcal{F}},n))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda\left(\frac{\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{n-2}}{\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n-2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda(\overline{R}/\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{n-2})-\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda(\overline{R}/\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}_{n-2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{3}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})-e_{3}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{3}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})-e_{3}(\mathcal{F})~~\mbox{ (by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{gr}{}{}, Proposition 1.5]})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{3}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})-e_{3}(I)

This gives

(12)

0\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\lambda(H_{2}(C_{.}(\overline{\mathcal{F}},n)))\leq e_{3}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})-e_{3}(I).

From (
11
) and (
12
), we get

(13)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1+e_{3}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})-e_{3}(I).

By the difference formula in
[
1
, Proposition 4.4]
, we have
for all
n\geq-1
,

(14)

P_{\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}(n)-H_{\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}}(n)=\lambda((H^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})))_{n+1}).

Now taking sum for large
m
on both sides of the above equation, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda((H^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})))_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}P_{\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}(n)-\sum_{n=0}^{m}H_{\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}P_{\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}(n)-H^{2}_{\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}}(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{0}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}){m+3\choose 3}-e_{1}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}){m+2\choose 2}+e_{2}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}){m+1\choose 1}-P^{2}_{\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}}}(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{3}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}).

As
\overline{R}
is a
2
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, we have
\lambda((H^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})))_{n})\leq\lambda((H^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})))_{n-1})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
by
[
1
, Lemma 4.7]
. Now in equation
(\ref{diff})
, we substitute
n=-1
to get

\lambda((H^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})))_{0})=e_{2}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})=e_{2}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})=e_{2}(\mathcal{F})=e_{2}(I)

Therefore,

(15)

e_{3}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})=\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda((H^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})))_{n+1})\leq\sum_{n=0}^{a_{2}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}))-1}\lambda((H^{2}_{\mathcal{R}_{+}}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})))_{0})=a_{2}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}))e_{2}(I)

where
a_{2}(\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}))\leq a_{2}(G(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}))=s
(say). Now using (
13
) and (
15
), we have

(16)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1+se_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

By
[
11
, Corollary 5.7(2)]
, we have
s=a_{2}(G(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}))=r(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})-2
and
by Theorem
2.2
,
r_{J}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})-1\leq e_{2}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})
. This gives

(17)

s=r_{J}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})-2\leq e_{2}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})-1=e_{2}(I)-1.

Now by (
16
) and (
17
), we get the conclusion.

Suppose
d\geq 4.
Let
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I
. Then
e_{i}(I/(x))=e_{i}(I)
for
0\leq i\leq 3
. Also,
0ptG(I)\geq 1
implies
I^{n}=\widetilde{I^{n}}
for
n\geq 1.
This gives
r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))=r_{J}(I)
by Lemma
3.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Example 4.2
.

(1)

We refer to Example
3.10
to note that our bound
r_{J}(\mathfrak{m})\leq e_{1}(\mathfrak{m})-e_{0}(\mathfrak{m})+\lambda(R/\mathfrak{m})+1+(e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})-1)e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})-e_{3}(\mathfrak{m})=17
is better than Vasconcelos’ bound
\frac{de_{0}(\mathfrak{m})}{o(\mathfrak{m})}-2d+1=19.

(2)

Example
2.5
provides a number of three dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings with
e_{1}(\mathfrak{m})-e_{0}(\mathfrak{m})+\lambda(R/\mathfrak{m})+1+(e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})-1)e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})-e_{3}(\mathfrak{m})=17
and

\frac{3e_{0}(\mathfrak{m})}{o(\mathfrak{m})}-2.3+1=3m+19
, where
m\geq 0
.

(3)

Let
R=Q[|x,y,z|]
and
I=(x^{4},y^{4},z^{4},x^{3}y,xy^{3},y^{3}z,yz^{3})
. Note that
0ptG(I)=0
as
x^{2}y^{2}z^{2}\in I^{2}:I\subseteq\widetilde{I}
but
x^{2}y^{2}z^{2}\notin I
. By
[
10
, Example 3.7]
,
e_{0}(I)=64
,
e_{1}(I)=48
,
e_{2}(I)=4
and
e_{3}(I)=0
. Using Macaulay 2,
J=(\frac{3}{4}x^{4}+x^{3}y+\frac{5}{6}xy^{3}+\frac{1}{2}y^{4}+5y^{3}z+3yz^{3}+\frac{1}{5}z^{4},~\frac{4}{3}x^{4}+\frac{5}{3}x^{3}y+\frac{1}{2}xy^{3}+\frac{3}{2}y^{4}+\frac{4}{5}y^{3}z+\frac{5}{3}yz^{3}+\frac{10}{7}z^{4},~\frac{5}{3}x^{4}+\frac{4}{5}x^{3}y+\frac{7}{2}xy^{3}+y^{4}+\frac{3}{2}y^{3}z+\frac{8}{9}yz^{3}+\frac{3}{4}z^{4})
is a minimal reduction of
I
and
3=r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I)=32
whereas
\frac{de_{0}(I)}{o(I)}-2d+1=43.

Next we show that in dimension three, for certain values of
e_{2}(I)
, we get linear upper bound on
r(I)
in terms of Hilbert coefficients. We write
v_{n}
for
v_{n}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a three dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If
e_{2}(I)=0
or
1
then

r(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1-e_{3}(I)
.

(2)

If
e_{2}(I)=1
and
I
is asymptotically normal then

r(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1

(3)

If
e_{2}(I)=2
then

r(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+2-e_{3}(I)
.

Proof.

(1)

If
e_{2}(I)=0
or
1
then
r(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1-e_{3}(I)
by Theorem
4.1
.

(2)

If
e_{2}(I)=1
then
s\leq 0
by (
17
) and hence
e_{3}({\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}})\leq 0
using (
15
). Then by equation (
12
),
e_{3}(I)\leq 0
. Since
I
is asymptotically normal,
e_{3}(I)\geq 0
by
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
which implies
e_{3}(I)=0
. Hence we have
r(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1
.

(3)

As
0ptG(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})\geq 1
, we have
2=e_{2}(I)=e_{2}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})=\sum_{n\geq 1}nv_{n}
(by
[
19
, Theorem 2.5]
),
which implies either
v_{1}=0,v_{2}=1,v_{n}=0
for all
n\geq 3
or
v_{1}=2,v_{n}=0
for all
n\geq 2
. Hence
e_{3}(\widetilde{\overline{\mathcal{F}}})=\sum_{n\geq 2}{n\choose 2}v_{n}=v_{2}\leq 1
. Using (
13
), we get
r(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+2-e_{3}(I)
.

∎

Generalized parton distributions of sea quarks in the proton from nonlocal chiral effective theory Preprint: JLAB-THY-22-3553, ADP-22-1/T1172
Fangcheng He, Chueng-Ryong Ji, W. Melnitchouk, A. W. Thomas, P. Wang

Abstract
We calculate the spin-averaged generalized parton distributions (GPDs) of sea quarks in the proton at zero skewness from nonlocal covariant chiral effective theory, including one-loop contributions from intermediate states with pseudoscalar mesons and octet and decuplet baryons. A relativistic regulator is generated from the nonlocal Lagrangian where a gauge link is introduced to guarantee local gauge invariance, with additional diagrams from the expansion of the gauge link ensuring conservation of electric charge and strangeness. Flavor asymmetries for sea quarks at zero and finite momentum transfer, as well as strange form factors, are obtained from the calculated GPDs, and results compared with phenomenological extractions and lattice QCD.

I
Introduction

Reconstructing the three-dimensional structure of the nucleon and other hadrons in terms of their fundamental quark and gluon (or parton) constituents is one of the defining problems in modern nuclear physics, and one which is a major driver of experimental programs at facilities such as Jefferson Lab and the future Electron-Ion Collider (EIC)
Accardi et al. 2016
;
Avakian 2019
.
A central element of this endeavor is the extraction of generalized parton distributions (GPDs), which, as Fourier transforms of nonforward (and nondiagonal) matrix elements of nonlocal operators, contain rich information on the partonic structure of the nucleon.
GPDs interpolate between exclusive form factors, when integrated over parton momentum fraction
x
, and parton distribution functions (PDFs) in the forward limit, and contain considerably more information about the nucleon’s internal structure than do PDFs or form factors alone (for reviews of GPDs see,
e.g.
, Refs.
Goeke et al. 2001
;
Belitsky and Radyushkin 2005
).

The mapping of nucleon GPDs requires a comprehensive program of experimental studies of hard exclusive processes, such as deeply-virtual Compton scattering (DVCS) and hard exclusive meson production (HEMP), over a broad kinematic range.
While theoretical tools have been developed to formally factorize GPDs from the process-dependent, hard scattering amplitudes
Ji 1998
;
Ji 1997
;
Collins and Freund 1999
, the reconstruction of the full functional dependence of the GPDs, including their flavor and spin dependence, from limited experimental data is a formidable challenge
Kumericki et al. 2016
.
Experimental data were obtained at the HERA collider by the H1
Adloff et al. 2001
;
Aktas et al. 2005
and ZEUS
Breitweg et al. 1999
;
Chekanov et al. 2003
collaborations and by the HERMES
Airapetian et al. 2001
;
Airapetian et al. 2011
;
Airapetian et al. 2012
fixed target experiment, as well as by COMPASS at CERN
d’Hose et al. 2004
;
Fuchey 2015
.
A rich program of DVCS and HEMP measurements is also underway at Jefferson Lab with the 12 GeV energy upgraded, high-luminosity CEBAF accelerator
Stepanyan et al. 2001
;
Defurne et al. 2015
;
Jo et al. 2015
;
Seder et al. 2015
.

In addition to the experimental efforts, considerable progress has also been made on the theoretical front.
Because of the complex, nonperturbative properties of QCD, it is extremely challenging to calculate GPDs from first principles.
Since parton distributions and other light-cone correlation functions are defined in Minkowski space, it has also been very difficult to simulate GPDs on the Euclidean lattice.
Recent breakthroughs, however, have enabled the
x

As with PDFs, the simulation of GPDs on the lattice is still at a relatively early stage of development.
Much of the work on GPDs has focused on finding effective ways to parametrize their dependence on kinematic variables

.
From more phenomenological perspectives, characteristics of GPDs have been studied within nonperturbative approaches, such as the MIT and cloudy bag models

In addition to the phenomenological models, more systematic approaches using heavy baryon and relativistic chiral effective field theory (EFT) have been widely used to study hadron structure at small momentum transfer

Perturbative calculations in chiral EFT expand observables as series in the pseudoscalar meson mass

Historically, most formulations of EFT have been based on dimensional or infrared regularization.

;

The nonlocal interaction generates both the regulator which makes the loop integral convergent and the momentum dependence of the form factors at tree level.
The electromagnetic and strange form factors of the nucleon obtained in this approach have been found to be in excellent agreement with experiment up to values of the four-momentum transfer squared of
\approx 1
GeV
2

;
He and Wang 2018b
.
Recently, the method has also been applied to calculate the

In this paper we apply the nonlocal chiral effective theory for the first time to GPDs of sea quarks in the proton.
The study is timely, given the ongoing experimental program of DVCS and HEMP measurements at Jefferson Lab, and plans for future studies of high-

We begin in Sec.
II
by introducing the local and nonlocal chiral Lagrangian, including a derivation of the currents which couple to the external vector field.
The GPDs may be written as convolutions of splitting functions, describing the nucleon to meson plus baryon process, with the GPDs of the bare hadrons.
The one-loop nucleon

Taking moments of the nonforward splitting functions and expanding in powers of the pseudoscalar meson mass, in Sec.

The convolution formalism is discussed in Sec.
V

Numerical results are presented in Sec.

Finally, Sec.
VII

Explicit formulas for splitting function integrals are compiled in Appendix

II.2
Nonlocal chiral Lagrangian

In this section we outline the generalization of the effective local chiral Lagrangian to the case of nonlocal interactions.
Taking the traces in Eqs. (
1
), (
19
) and (
23
) in Sec.

, we can write the local Lagrangian density in the form

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\rm(local)}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{B}(x)(i\gamma^{\mu}\mathscr{D}_{\mu}-M_{B})B(x)+\frac{C_{B\phi}}{f}\big[\bar{p}(x)\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}B(x)\,\mathscr{D}_{\mu}\phi(x)+{\rm H.c.}\big]

(30)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\overline{T}_{\mu}(x)(i\gamma^{\mu\nu\alpha}\mathscr{D}_{\alpha}-M_{T}\gamma^{\mu\nu})\,T_{\nu}(x)+\frac{C_{T\phi}}{f}\left[\,\bar{p}(x)\Theta^{\mu\nu}T_{\nu}(x)\,\mathscr{D}_{\mu}\phi(x)+{\rm H.c.}\right]

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\mu}\phi(x)(\mathscr{D}_{\mu}\phi)^{\dagger}(x)\,+\frac{iC_{\phi\phi^{\dagger}}}{2f^{2}}\bar{p}(x)\gamma^{\mu}p(x)\left[\phi(x)(\mathscr{D}_{\mu}\phi)^{\dagger}(x)-\mathscr{D}_{\mu}\phi(x)\phi^{\dagger}(x)\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{iC_{\phi\phi^{\dagger}}^{\prime}}{2f^{2}}\bar{p}(x)\sigma^{\mu\nu}p(x)\mathscr{D}_{\mu}\phi(x)(\mathscr{D}_{\nu}\phi)^{\dagger}(x)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{\mu}-ie^{q}_{B}\,\mathscr{A_{\mu}}(x)\right]B(x),

III
Nonforward splitting functions

The GPDs for a quark flavor
q
in a proton with initial momentum
p
and final momentum
p^{\prime}
are defined by the Fourier transform of the matrix elements of the quark bilocal field operators
\psi_{q}
as
Ji 1997

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\differential{\lambda}}{2\pi}e^{-ix\lambda}\langle p^{\prime}|\bar{\psi}_{q}(\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda n)\not{n}\,\psi_{q}(-\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda n)|p\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big[\not{n}H^{q}(x,\xi,t)+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}n_{\mu}\Delta_{\nu}}{2M}\,E^{q}(x,\xi,t)\Big]u(p),

where
n_{\mu}
is the light-cone vector which projects the “plus” component of momenta and
\lambda
is a dimensionless parameter.
From Lorentz invariance, the Dirac (
H^{q}
) and Pauli (
E^{q}
) GPDs are typically written as functions of the light-cone momentum fraction
x
of the proton carried by the initial quark with momentum
k_{q}
and the skewness parameter
\xi
, which are defined as

x\,\equiv\,\frac{k_{q}^{+}}{P^{+}},\qquad\xi\,\equiv\,-\frac{\Delta^{+}}{2P^{+}},

(44)

where

P\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\big(p+p^{\prime}\big),\qquad\Delta\,=\,p^{\prime}-p,

(45)

are the average of the initial and final proton momenta and the momentum difference, respectively. The light-cone components
k^{+}
and
k^{-}
of any four-vector
k^{\mu}
are defined
as
k^{+}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(k^{0}+k^{3})
and
k^{-}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(k^{0}-k^{3})
.
The GPDs are also functions of the hadronic four-momentum transfer squared,
t\equiv\Delta^{2}
. The dependence of the GPDs on the fourth variable, typically taken to be the four-momentum transfer squared from the incident lepton,
Q^{2}
, is suppressed.

Integrating the
H^{q}
and
E^{q}
GPDs over
x
, one obtains the Dirac and Pauli form factors for a given quark flavor
q
, respectively,

\displaystyle F_{1}^{q}(t)=\int_{-1}^{1}\,\differential{x}H^{q}(x,\xi,t),\qquad F_{2}^{q}(t)=\int_{-1}^{1}\,\differential{x}E^{q}(x,\xi,t),

(46)

and summing over the quark flavors gives the nucleon Dirac,
F_{1}^{N}
, and Pauli,
F_{1}^{N}
, form factors,

\displaystyle F_{1,2}^{N}(t)=\sum_{q}e_{q}\,F_{1,2}^{\,q}(t).

(47)

The combination of these form factors can generate the usual Sachs electric and magnetic form factors as

\displaystyle G_{E}^{N}(t)=F_{1}^{N}(t)+{t\over{4M^{2}}}F_{2}^{N}(t),\qquad G_{M}^{N}(t)=F_{1}^{N}(t)+F_{2}^{N}(t).

(48)

In our calculations we consider only non-skewed GPDs, and henceforth set
\xi=0
, in which case the hadron momenta are parametrized as
Brodsky et al. 1998

\displaystyle p^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(P^{+},P^{-},-\frac{1}{2}{\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}}\Big),\qquad p^{\prime\mu}\,=\,\Big(P^{+},P^{-},\frac{1}{2}{\bm{\Delta}_{\perp}}\Big),

(49)

where the momentum transfer
\Delta^{\mu}
is purely in the transverse direction.

In the application of our nonlocal EFT framework to GPDs, we need to compute the nonforward splitting functions defined by the matrix elements of the hadronic level currents derived in the previous section.
The electromagnetic vertex is given by

\langle N(p^{\prime})|J^{\mu}|N(p)\rangle\,=\,\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big[\gamma^{\mu}F_{1}^{N}(t)+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\nu}}{2M}F_{2}^{N}(t)\Big]u(p)\,\equiv\int\!\differential[4]{k}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}(k),

(50)

where the integrand
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\mu}(k)
depends on the internal meson momentum,
k
.
Defining the light-cone momentum fraction of the target nucleon carried by the interacting hadron,
y=k^{+}/P^{+}
, the Dirac-like splitting function
f(y,t)
and Pauli-like splitting function
g(y,t)
are related to the vertex by

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big[\gamma^{+}f(y,t)+\frac{i\sigma^{+\nu}\Delta_{\nu}}{2M}\,g(y,t)\Big]u(p)=\int\differential[4]{k}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{+}(k)\,\delta\Big(y-\frac{k^{+}}{P^{+}}\Big)\,\equiv\,\Gamma^{+}.

(51)

One can easily verify that the integral of the splitting functions over
y
leads to the corresponding form factors in (
50
).

IV
Nonanalytic behavior of splitting functions

While certain features of the hadronic splitting functions derived in the previous section depend on short-distance physics that in the current framework is controlled by the regulator function
\widetilde{F}
, some aspects of their calculation are in fact model independent.
In particular, the moments of the splitting functions, which can be expanded as a series in the pseudoscalar meson mass,
m_{\phi}
, contain terms that do not depend on the regularization method
Thomas et al. 2000
;
Arndt and Savage 2002
;
Chen and Ji 2001
;
Salamu et al. 2019a
.
These are the coefficients of the leading nonanalytic (LNA) terms, that are determined by the low-energy properties of the nucleon, such as the hadronic couplings and masses.
Since the LNA behavior is derived solely from the long-distance characteristics of the chiral effective theory, understanding these can place constraints on models of the splitting functions consistent with the symmetries of QCD.

To explore the LNA terms further, we define the lowest moments of the splitting functions
f^{\rm(x)}(y,t)
and
g^{\rm(x)}(y,t)
for the diagram in Fig.
1
(“x”), where “x” = “a”,
\ldots
“u”, as

\displaystyle F^{\rm(x)}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential{y}f^{\rm(x)}(y,t),

(93a)

\displaystyle F^{\rm(x)}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential{y}g^{\rm(x)}(y,t),

(93b)

which correspond to the Dirac and Pauli electromagnetic form factors of the nucleon, respectively.
Taking the values at zero four-momentum transfer squared,
t=0
, we expand the form factors
F^{\rm(x)}_{1,2}(t\!=\!0)
in
m_{\phi}
, keeping only the nonanalytic (NA) terms in
m_{\phi}^{2}
, from which the LNA behavior can be extracted.

For the rainbow diagram in Fig.
1
(a), the NA contributions can be written as

\displaystyle F_{1}^{\rm(a)}(0)\Big|_{\rm NA}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3C^{2}_{B\phi}}{(4\pi f)^{2}}\bigg[\big(m_{\phi}^{2}-2\Delta_{B}^{2}\big)\log m_{\phi}^{2}+2\Delta_{B}R_{B}\log\frac{\Delta_{B}-R_{B}}{\Delta_{B}+R_{B}}\bigg],

(94a)

\displaystyle F_{2}^{\rm(a)}(0)\Big|_{\rm NA}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4C^{2}_{B\phi}}{(4\pi f)^{2}}\bigg[\big(M\Delta_{B}+2\Delta^{2}_{B}+2R_{B}^{2}\big)\log m_{\phi}^{2}

(94b)

\displaystyle\hskip 105.2751pt-\,\big(M+4\Delta_{B}\big)R_{B}\,\log\frac{\Delta_{B}-R_{B}}{\Delta_{B}+R_{B}}\bigg],

where
R_{B}=\sqrt{\Delta_{B}^{2}-m_{\phi}^{2}}
.
If

,

will be an imaginary number and the log term will become an arctangent, according to its definition
Salamu et al. 2019a
.
The NA terms of

V
Convolution formalism

Having derived the full set of splitting functions for the diagrams in Fig.
1
involving the SU(3) octet and decuplet baryon intermediate states, in this section we discuss the calculation of the GPDs in the proton arising from these contributions.
We derive expressions for the GPDs in terms of convolutions of the splitting functions and GPDs of quarks in the various hadronic configurations.
Using flavor symmetry constraints, we discuss relations for the GPDs in the hadronic configurations among the various SU(3) baryons.

V.1
GPDs as convolutions

The
n
-th Mellin moments of the generalized quark distributions
H^{q}(x,\xi,t)
and
E^{q}(x,\xi,t)
are given by
Ji 1998

\displaystyle H_{q}^{(n)}(\xi,t)\equiv\int_{-1}^{1}\differential{x}x^{n-1}H^{q}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0,\,{\rm even}}^{n-1}\big(\!-2\xi\big)^{i}A^{(n)i}_{q}(t)+\big(\!-2\xi\big)^{n}C_{q}^{(n)}(t)\Big|_{n\,{\rm even}},~~~~~~~~

(107a)

\displaystyle E_{q}^{(n)}(\xi,t)\equiv\int_{-1}^{1}\differential{x}x^{n-1}E^{q}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0,\,{\rm even}}^{n-1}\big(\!-2\xi\big)^{i}B^{(n)i}_{q}(t)-\big(\!-2\xi\big)^{n}C_{q}^{(n)}(t)\Big|_{n\,{\rm even}},~~~~~~~~

(107b)

where
A_{q}^{(n)i}(t)
,
B_{q}^{(n)i}(t)
and
C_{q}^{(n)}(t)
are the generalized form factors of rank
n
.
The generalized form factors can be related to the matrix elements of local twist-2 operators
{\cal O}_{q}^{\{\mu\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n-1}\}}
between nucleon states
Ji 1998
,

\displaystyle\langle N(p^{\prime})|\mathcal{O}_{q}^{\{\mu\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n-1}\}}|N(p)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\bigg[\ \sum_{i=0,\,\rm even}^{n-1}A_{q}^{(n)i}(t)\,\gamma^{\{\mu}\Delta^{\mu_{1}}\cdots\Delta^{\mu_{i}}P^{\mu_{i}+1}\cdots P^{\mu_{n-1}\}}

(108)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt-\frac{i}{2M}\sum_{i=0,\,\rm even}^{n-1}B_{q}^{(n)i}(t)\,\Delta_{\nu}\sigma^{\nu\{\mu}\Delta^{\mu_{1}}\cdots\Delta^{\mu_{i}}P^{\mu_{i}+1}\cdots P^{\mu_{n-1}\}}~~~~~

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt+\frac{1}{M}\ C_{q}^{(n)}(t)\Big|_{n\,{\rm even}}\ \Delta^{\{\mu}\cdots\Delta^{\mu_{n-1}\}}\bigg]u(p),

where the symmetric and traceless operators are defined as

\mathcal{O}^{\{\mu\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n-1}\}}_{q}=i^{n-1}\,\bar{\psi}_{q}\,\gamma^{\{\mu}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu_{n-1}\}}\,\psi_{q},

(109)

with
\overleftrightarrow{D}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\overrightarrow{D}-\overleftarrow{D}\big)
.
The braces
\{\cdots\}
represent symmetrization over the indices
\mu\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}
and subtraction of traces.

In an effective field theory, these quark operators are matched to hadronic operators with the corresponding set of quantum numbers
Chen and Ji 2001
,

\mathcal{O}^{\{\mu\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n-1}\}}_{q}=\sum_{j}Q^{(n-1)}_{j}\,\mathcal{O}^{\{\mu\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n-1}\}}_{j},

(110)

where the subscript
j
labels different types of hadronic operators.
The coefficients
Q^{(n-1)}_{j}
can be defined through the
n
-th moments of the generalized parton distributions in the hadronic configuration
j
(see Sec.
V.2
below).
Matrix elements of the hadronic operators
\mathcal{O}^{\{\mu\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n-1}\}}_{j}
can be used to define the moments of the Dirac-like and Pauli-like hadronic splitting functions
f_{j}
and
g_{j}
, respectively, introduced in Eq. (
51
),

\displaystyle f^{(n)}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{-1}\differential{y}y^{(n-1)}f_{j}(y,t),

(111a)

\displaystyle g^{(n)}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{-1}\differential{y}y^{(n-1)}g_{j}(y,t),

(111b)

where
y
is the light-cone momentum fraction of the nucleon carried by the hadronic state
j
.
Taking the matrix elements of the matched operators in Eq. (
110
) between nucleon states with unequal initial and final momenta, and contracting both sides with light-cone vector
n_{\mu}n_{\mu_{1}}\cdots n_{\mu_{n-1}}
, we then arrive at a relation for the GPD moments in terms of the moments of the hadronic splitting functions,

\displaystyle H_{q}^{(n)}\,\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\not{n}u(p)+\frac{i}{2M}E_{q}^{(n)}\,\bar{u}(p^{\prime})n_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\nu}\,u(p)

\displaystyle\hskip-66.86414pt=\sum_{j}Q^{(n-1)}_{j}\bigg[f^{(n)}_{j}\,\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\not{n}u(p)+\frac{i}{2M}\,g^{(n)}_{j}\ \bar{u}(p^{\prime})n_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\nu}\,u(p)\bigg].

(112)

Since Eq. (
V.1
) is valid for all moments
n
, we deduce that the corresponding convolution relation exists also for the GPDs as a function of
x
.
Specializing to the case of zero skewness,
\xi=0
, we can write the contributions to the
H^{q}
and
E^{q}
GPDs from the hadronic configurations in the form

\displaystyle H^{q}(x,t)\,\equiv\,H^{q}(x,\xi\!=\!0,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\big[f_{j}\otimes q_{j}^{v}\big](x,t)

(113a)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{y}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{z}\delta(x-yz)\,f_{j}(y,t)\,q_{j}^{v}(z,t),

\displaystyle E^{q}(x,t)\,\equiv\,E^{q}(x,\xi\!=\!0,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\big[g_{j}\otimes q_{j}^{v}\big](x,t)

(113b)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{y}\int_{0}^{1}\differential{z}\delta(x-yz)\,g_{j}(y,t)\,q_{j}^{v}(z,t),

where we define

q^{v}_{j}(x,t)\equiv q_{j}(x,\xi\!=\!0,t)-\bar{q}_{j}(x,\xi\!=\!0,t)

as the GPD of the valence quark
q
in the hadronic configuration
j
evaluated at zero skewness. We should mention that the above convolutions are only valid when
\xi=0
. The nonzero skewness GPDs have additional properties, such as
H^{q}(x,\xi,t)
and
E^{q}(x,\xi,t)
being even under the transformation
\xi\to-\xi
. The proof of the polynomiality properties in Eq. (
107
) will also be an important check for the convolution equations in the nonzero skewness case. Such a proof has been demonstrated in some nonperturbative approaches
Schweitzer et al. 2002
.

Since the total
H^{q}
and
E^{q}
GPDs can each receive contributions from both Dirac-like and Pauli-like GPDs of the hadronic configurations, the sum over
j
in Eqs. (
113
) includes both electric and magnetic couplings,
q_{j}\to H^{q}_{j}
or
E^{q}_{j}
.
Note also that crossing symmetry,

q_{j}(-x,\xi\!=\!0,t)=-\bar{q}_{j}(x,\xi\!=\!0,t)
,
in direct analogy to that for forward (
t=0
) parton distributions, has been used to write the integrals in Eqs. (
113
) over the interval 0 to 1.

To illustrate the application of Eqs. (
113
) to chiral loop contributions to sea quark GPDs in the proton, in this paper we focus on the asymmetries between the GPDs for
\bar{d}
and
\bar{u}
quarks, and between the
s
and
\bar{s}
quark flavors.
Assuming that the intermediate state octet and decuplet baryons are flavor symmetric, with mesons
\phi
the only source of antiquarks, the convolution form for the antiquark electric and magnetic GPDs in the proton involves contributions only from the diagrams in Fig.

(a), (k), (l) and (m).
Specifically, for the

and

\displaystyle H^{\bar{q}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\phi BT}\Big[\big(f_{\phi B}^{(\rm rbw)}+f_{\phi T}^{(\rm rbw)}+f^{(\rm bub)}_{\phi}\big)\otimes H^{\bar{q}}_{\phi}\Big](x,t),

(114a)

\displaystyle E^{\bar{q}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\phi BT}\Big[\big(g_{\phi B}^{(\rm rbw)}+g_{\phi T}^{(\rm rbw)}+g^{\prime(\rm bub)}_{\phi}\big)\otimes H^{\bar{q}}_{\phi}\Big](x,t),

(114b)

where
H^{\bar{q}}_{\phi}
is the electric GPD for quark flavor
\bar{q}
in the meson
\phi

The splitting functions

f_{\phi B}^{(\rm rbw)}
and
g_{\phi B}^{(\rm rbw)}

for Fig.

(a) are given in Eqs. (

) and (

for Fig.

) and (

f^{(\rm bub)}_{\phi}

) and (

The convolution form for the quark GPDs received contributions from all other diagrams in Fig.
1
, and so has a more complicated structure,

\displaystyle H^{q}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{2}\,H^{q}_{0}(x,t)\ +\ \sum_{\phi BT}\Big[\big(f_{\phi B}^{(\rm rbw)}+f_{\phi T}^{(\rm rbw)}+f^{(\rm bub)}_{\phi}\big)\otimes H^{q}_{\phi}

\displaystyle\hskip 93.89418pt+\ \bar{f}_{B\phi}^{(\rm rbw)}\otimes H^{q}_{B}\ +\ \bar{f}^{(\rm KR)}_{B\phi}\otimes H^{q({\rm KR})}_{B}\ +\ \delta\bar{f}^{(\rm KR)}_{B}\otimes H^{q({\rm KR})}_{B}\

\displaystyle\hskip 93.89418pt+\ \bar{f}_{T\phi}^{(\rm rbw)}\otimes H^{q}_{T}\ +\ \bar{f}^{(\rm KR)}_{T\phi}\otimes H^{q({\rm KR})}_{T}\ +\ \delta\bar{f}^{(\rm KR)}_{T\phi}\otimes H^{q({\rm KR})}_{T}\

\displaystyle\hskip 93.89418pt+\ \bar{f}_{B\phi}^{(\rm rbw\,mag)}\otimes E^{q}_{B}\ +\ \bar{f}_{T\phi}^{(\rm rbw\,mag)}\otimes E^{q}_{T}\ +\ \bar{f}_{BT}^{(\rm rbw\,mag)}\otimes E^{q}_{BT}~~~~~~

\displaystyle\hskip 93.89418pt+\ \bar{f}^{(\rm tad)}_{\phi}\otimes H^{q({\rm tad})}_{\phi\phi^{\dagger}}\ +\ \delta\bar{f}^{(\rm tad)}_{\phi}\otimes H^{q({\rm tad})}_{\phi\phi^{\dagger}}\Big](x,t),

\displaystyle E^{q}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{2}\,E^{q}_{0}(x,t)\ +\ \sum_{\phi BT}\Big[\big(g_{\phi B}^{(\rm rbw)}+g_{\phi T}^{(\rm rbw)}+g^{\prime(\rm bub)}_{\phi}\big)\otimes H^{q}_{\phi}

The relevant operator here can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{q\,B}^{\mu\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0,\rm even}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\alpha_{A}^{(n)i}\,{\rm Tr}\left[\bar{B}\gamma^{\mu}\left\{(u^{\dagger}\lambda_{q}u+u\lambda_{q}u^{\dagger}),B\right\}\right]

(116)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.77039pt+\ \beta_{A}^{(n)i}\,{\rm Tr}\left[\bar{B}\gamma^{\mu}\left[(u^{\dagger}\lambda_{q}u+u\lambda_{q}u^{\dagger}),B\right]\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.77039pt+\ \sigma_{A}^{(n)i}\,{\rm Tr}\left[\bar{B}\gamma^{\mu}B\right]{\rm Tr}\left[(u^{\dagger}\lambda_{q}u+u\lambda_{q}u^{\dagger})\right]\Big)\,\Delta^{\mu_{1}}\cdots\Delta^{\mu_{i}}P^{\mu_{i+1}}\cdots P^{\mu_{n-1}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0,\rm even}^{n-1}\frac{i}{4M_{B}}\Big(\alpha_{B}^{(n)i}\,{\rm Tr}\left[\bar{B}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\left\{(u^{\dagger}\lambda_{q}u+u\lambda_{q}u^{\dagger}),B\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2M_{B}}\Big(\alpha_{C}^{(n)}\big|_{n=\rm even}\,{\rm Tr}\left[\bar{B}\left\{(u^{\dagger}\lambda_{q}u+u\lambda_{q}u^{\dagger}),B\right\}\right]

\displaystyle n_{\mu}n_{\mu_{1}}\cdots n_{\mu_{n-1}}{\cal O}_{q\,B}^{\mu\mu_{1}\ldots\mu_{n-1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{(n)}\,{\rm Tr}\left[\bar{B}\not{n}B\right]{\rm Tr}\left[(u^{\dagger}\lambda_{q}u+u\lambda_{q}u^{\dagger})\right]

\displaystyle\frac{i\alpha_{\rm mag}^{(n)}}{8M_{B}}\,{\rm Tr}\left[\bar{B}n_{\mu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\Delta_{\nu}\left\{(u^{\dagger}\lambda_{q}u+u\lambda_{q}u^{\dagger}),B\right\}\right]

\displaystyle H^{s(\rm KR)}_{\Sigma^{*+}}=H^{s(\rm KR)}_{\Sigma^{*0}}=\frac{1}{2D}\big(\widetilde{H}^{u}-2\widetilde{H}^{d}+\widetilde{H}^{s}\big),

(136)

where
\widetilde{H}^{q}\equiv\widetilde{H}^{q}(x,\xi=0,t)
are the corresponding spin-dependent GPDs in the proton.
Finally, for the antiquark GPDs in pions and kaons that enter in the convolution formulas, we have the relations

\displaystyle H^{\bar{s}}_{K^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{\bar{s}}_{K^{0}}=H^{\bar{d}}_{\pi^{+}}=H^{d}_{\pi^{-}}=H^{\bar{u}}_{\pi^{-}}=2H^{\bar{u}}_{\pi^{0}}=2H^{\bar{d}}_{\pi^{0}}.

(137)

In our numerical calculations, we will assume valence quark dominance for the undressed states, so that for the proton
H^{s}=E^{s}=0
.
The decuplet and transition GPDs
H^{q}_{\Delta}
,
E^{q}_{\Delta}
and
E^{q}_{p\Delta^{+}}
can be related to the proton GPDs
H^{q}
and
E^{q}
using SU(3) flavor symmetry, which constrains the coefficients according to

\theta_{\rm mag}^{(n)}=\alpha_{\rm mag}^{(n)}+3\beta_{\rm mag}^{(n)}
.
Since
H^{q}
and
E^{q}
have the same flavor symmetry, the decuplet GPDs can then be written as

\displaystyle H^{u}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2H^{d}_{\Delta}=\frac{4}{3}H^{u}-\frac{2}{3}H^{d},

(138)

\displaystyle E^{u}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2E^{d}_{\Delta}=\frac{4}{3}E^{u}-\frac{2}{3}E^{d}.

(139)

Similarly, the constraint
\omega_{\rm mag}^{(n)}=4\alpha_{\rm mag}^{(n)}
leads to the relations for the transition GPDs,

E^{u}_{p\Delta^{+}}=-E^{d}_{p\Delta^{+}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\big(E^{u}-2E^{d}\big).

(140)

With these relations, all the GPDs used in the calculation in the next section can be expressed in terms of the GPDs in the proton.
Note that because the magnetic coefficients
C_{j}^{\rm mag}
(
j=B,T,BT
) are not included in the splitting functions, the GPDs
E^{q}_{j}(x,t=0)
need to be normalized to their magnetic moments with unit charge obtained from
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.

VI
Numerical results for sea quark GPDs

In this section we present the numerical results of our calculation of the meson loop contributions to the sea quark GPDs of the proton.
We begin first by summarizing the inputs used in the calculation, followed by discussions of the results for the light antiquark contributions and the strange quark contributions to the GPDs.

VI.1
Theoretical inputs

For the meson–baryon couplings in our numerical calculations we use the values
D=0.76
and
F=0.5
for the octet baryon couplings (with
g_{A}=D+F=1.26
), and
{\cal C}=-2D
for the octet-decuplet transition coupling under the assumption of SU(6) symmetry.
The loop integrals are regularized using a covariant regulator of dipole form,

\widetilde{F}(k)=\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}-m^{2}_{\phi}}{\Lambda^{2}-k^{2}}\right)^{2},

(141)

with
\Lambda
a mass parameter.
Such a regulator can suppress the short distance physics and improve the chiral convergence
Young et al. 2003
.
On the other hand, the Taylor expansion of
\widetilde{F}(k)
is a series in
k^{2}
, which can be regarded as the resummation of contributions from higher order meson–baryon interactions.
From previous analyses of nucleon electromagnetic and strange from factors, we take
\Lambda=1.0(1)
GeV
He and Wang 2018a
;
He and Wang 2018b
.
The parameters
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are determined by fitting to the nucleon anomalous magnetic moments, and we find
c_{1}=1.40
and
c_{2}=0.54
reproduce the empirical values
\mu_{p}=2.79\,\mu_{N}
and
\mu_{n}=-1.91\,\mu_{N}
in units of the nucleon magneton,
\mu_{N}=e\hbar/2M
.

For the valence quark GPDs in the proton, we follow Diehl
et al.

Diehl et al. 2005
and parametrize the GPDs as products of forward distributions and
t
-dependent exponential factors,

\displaystyle H^{q}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{v}(x)\,\exp[t\,f_{q}(x)\big],

(142a)

\displaystyle E^{q}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{q}(x)\,\exp[t\,f_{q}(x)\big],

(142b)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{q}(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta q_{v}(x)\,\exp[t\,\tilde{f}_{q}(x)\big].

(142c)

Here the unpolarized
q_{v}(x)
, helicity-flip
e_{q}(x)
, and helicity-dependent
\Delta q_{v}(x)
PDFs for the valence quarks are taken from the parametrizations in Refs.
Martin et al. 1998
;
Diehl et al. 2005
;
Leader et al. 2007
.
The profile functions
f_{q}(x)
and
\tilde{f}_{q}(x)
parametrize the
x
dependence of the average impact parameter of the corresponding quark distribution, which can be seen after performing a Fourier transform to coordinate space
Diehl et al. 2005
.

The valence quark GPD in the pion is parametrized as a simple factorized product of a pion valence PDF and a
t
-dependent elastic form factor,

\displaystyle H^{q}_{\pi}(x,t)=q^{\pi}_{v}(x)\,F_{\pi}(t),

(143)

where
q^{\pi}_{v}(x)
is the pion valence quark PDF.
For illustration purposes we use the parametrization of
q^{\pi}_{v}
from Ref.
Aicher et al. 2010
, while more recent analyses have studied the large-
x
behavior in the presence of next-to-leading-log threshold resummation effects
Barry et al. 2021
.
For the pion elastic electromagnetic from factor
F_{\pi}(t)
we use a monopole form,

\displaystyle F_{\pi}(t)=\frac{1}{1-t/\Lambda_{\pi}^{2}}.

(144)

The cutoff parameter
\Lambda_{\pi}
is tuned to be 0.79 GeV, corresponding to the average of the charge radii for the pion and kaon
Tanabashi et al. 2018
(since we use the same inputs for all the meson valence quark GPDs).
The valence quark GPDs in other hadronic configurations are obtained through the SU(3) symmetry relations in Sec.
V.2
.
With the calculated splitting functions and the valence quark distributions as input, we can proceed to evaluate the GPDs of the sea quarks from the convolution expressions (
114
)–(
115
).

VII
Summary

This paper has presented a detailed account of unpolarized GPDs of sea quark and antiquarks in the proton arising from pseudoscalar meson loops whose interactions with octet and decuplet baryons are described within a nonlocal chiral effective theory with a finite range regularization.
We have restricted this initial study to the special case of zero skewness,
\xi=0
, although the calculation can be straightforwardly extended to the
\xi>0
case.
Within the convolution formulation, the dependence of the electric
H^{q}\equiv H^{q}(x,\xi\!=\!0,t)
and magnetic
E^{q}\equiv E^{q}(x,\xi\!=\!0,t)
sea quark GPDs on the parton momentum fraction
x
and four-momentum transfer squared
t
has been computed from the derived nonforward hadronic splitting functions and input GPDs of hadronic configurations constrained by SU(3) flavor symmetry.

For all light and strange quark flavors the electric, spin-nonflip GPDs
H^{q}
are positive.
For the magnetic spin-flip GPDs, the
E^{\bar{d}}
and
E^{\bar{s}}
distributions are positive, while the
E^{\bar{u}}
GPD is negative.
The strange magnetic GPD
E^{s}
, on the other hand, displays nontrivial
x
dependence with changes of sign as a function of
x
.
The electric and magnetic sea quark flavor asymmetries for the light quarks consequently remain positive across all
x
values, decreasing in magnitude with increasing momentum transfer squared
-t
.
Interestingly, the magnetic asymmetry
E^{\bar{d}-\bar{u}}
is some four times greater than the corresponding electric asymmetry
H^{\bar{d}-\bar{u}}
, which presents opportunities for phenomenological studies of this function with future experiments.
The shape of the electric asymmetry is constrained at
t=0
by Drell-Yan and other measurements, and is quite comparable with the
\bar{d}-\bar{u}
PDF asymmetry from global QCD analysis
Cocuzza et al. 2021
.

For the strange quark GPDs, both the electric and magnetic
s-\bar{s}
asymmetries are significantly smaller than for the nonstrange case, and change sign with
x
.
The integral of
xH^{s-\bar{s}}
favors positive values, and has a magnitude at finite
t
that may be even larger than the value at
t=0
.
The results are also qualitatively consistent with current phenomenological determinations from global QCD analyses, although within rather large uncertainties.
The electric and magnetic
s-\bar{s}
GPD asymmetries, integrated over
x
, are also broadly consistent with the strange electromagnetic form factors as a function of
t
, obtained from recent lattice QCD simulations
Sufian et al. 2017
as well as from direct calculations within nonlocal chiral effective theory
He and Wang 2018b
.

While the present analysis has been performed at zero skewness,
\xi=0
, in future it will be important to extend the GPD calculations to nonzero skewness.
Such calculations will naturally be rather more complicated, but should provide further insights into the three-dimensional structure of the nucleon.
The current analysis can also be easily extended to the case of spin-dependent GPDs of sea quarks in the proton, where we know from similar studies of helicity PDFs
Wang et al. 2020
that chiral loops play a somewhat different role for polarized and unpolarized distributions.
Experimentally, while facilities such as Jefferson Lab are expected to provide information on GPDs in the valence quark region at larger values of
x
, distributions of sea quarks will be ideally suited for study at the future Electron-Ion Collider
Abdul Khalek et al. 2021
.

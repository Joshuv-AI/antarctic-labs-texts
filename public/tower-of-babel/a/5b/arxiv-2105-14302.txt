Comment on “ Ab initio calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
Jae-Mo Lihm

Abstract
In a recent paper, Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. [Phys. Rev. B 97 , 245143 (2018)] derive a band-truncation-error-free formula for calculating the generalized derivative of the interband dipole matrix using Wannier interpolation. In practice, the denominators involving intermediate states are regularized by introducing a finite broadening parameter. In this Comment, I show that when a finite broadening parameter is used, a correction term must be added to the generalized derivative to obtain results that are independent of the phase convention for the Bloch sums.



Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
derive a truncation-error-free formula for the Wannier interpolation of the generalized derivative of the interband dipole matrix. This result enables an efficient and accurate calculation of the shift-current response.

In practice, to avoid numerical problems due to near-degenerate states, the energy denominator involving intermediate states is regularized by introducing a broadening parameter
\eta
(see Eq. (38) of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
).
Within the Wannier-interpolation scheme, the regularized version of the generalized derivative of the interband dipole matrix element is given from Eqs. (36) and (38) of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)=\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(0)+\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\Bigg\{\real\left[\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{pm}(\omega_{pm}+i\eta)}\right](\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{a}+i\omega_{nm}\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{a})\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{b}

\displaystyle\quad-\real\left[\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{np}(\omega_{np}+i\eta)}\right]\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{b}(\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{a}+i\omega_{nm}\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{a})\Bigg\},

(1)

where
M
is the number of Wannier functions.
Here,
\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(0)
refers to the case without regularization, which equals to
r^{a;b}_{nm}
in Eq. (36) of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
.
I use tilde to distinguish the quantity obtained from the Wannier interpolation scheme of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
from a direct calculation using Kohn-Sham wavefunctions.

In this Comment, I show that when
\eta
is nonzero,
\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)
defined in Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
), as well as the corresponding shift-current spectrum, depends on the phase convention for the Bloch sums.
I derive a correction term that should be added to Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
) to obtain a phase-convention-independent result.

I consider two phase conventions, the tight-binding (TB) convention [Eq. (14) of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
]

\ket{u_{\mb{k}j}^\mathrm{(W,\,TB)}}=\sum_{{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}}e^{-i{\bm{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot(\hat{{\bm{\mathbf{r}}}}-{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}-{\bm{\mathbf{\tau}}}_{j})}\ket{\mb{R}j}

(2)

and the Wannier90 (W90) convention

\ket{u_{\mb{k}j}^\mathrm{(W,\,W90)}}=\sum_{{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}}e^{-i{\bm{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot(\hat{{\bm{\mathbf{r}}}}-{\bm{\mathbf{R}}})}\ket{\mb{R}j}.

(3)

Here,
{\bm{\mathbf{\tau}}}_{j}
is the position of the
j
-th atom of the unit cell.
The “internal” matrix elements that appear in Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
) such as
\mathbbm{v}
and
\mathbbm{a}
depend on the phase convention.
In the tight-binding (TB) convention, one finds

\partial^{a}H_{ij}^{\rm(W,TB)}=\sum_{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}e^{i{\bm{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot({\bm{\mathbf{R}}}+{\bm{\mathbf{\tau}}}_{j}-{\bm{\mathbf{\tau}}}_{i})}i(R^{a}+\tau^{a}_{j}-\tau^{a}_{i})H_{ij;{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}},

(4)

A_{ij}^{a\mathrm{(W,TB)}}=\sum_{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}e^{i{\bm{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot({\bm{\mathbf{R}}}+{\bm{\mathbf{\tau}}}_{j}-{\bm{\mathbf{\tau}}}_{i})}r^{a}_{ij;{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}}-\tau^{a}_{i}\delta_{ij}.

(5)

In the W90 convention, one finds

\partial^{a}H_{ij}^{\rm(W,W90)}=\sum_{{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}}e^{i{\bm{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}}iR^{a}H_{ij;{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}},

(6)

A_{ij}^{a\mathrm{(W,W90)}}=\sum_{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}e^{i{\bm{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}}r^{a}_{ij;{\bm{\mathbf{R}}}}.

(7)

The unitary matrices that diagonalize the Hamiltonians in the two conventions can be related as

U^{(\mathrm{TB})}_{in}=e^{-i{\bm{\mathbf{k}}}\cdot{\bm{\mathbf{\tau}}}_{i}}U^{(\mathrm{W90})}_{in}.

(8)

Comparing Eqs. (
6
,
7
) with Eqs. (
4
,
5
), one can show that the internal matrix elements in the two conventions are related as

\mathbbm{v}^{a,(\mathrm{TB})}_{nm}=\mathbbm{v}^{a,(\mathrm{W90})}_{nm}+i\omega_{nm}\bbtau^{a,(\mathrm{TB})}_{nm}

(9)

and

\mathbbm{a}^{a,(\mathrm{TB})}_{nm}=\mathbbm{a}^{a,(\mathrm{W90})}_{nm}-\bbtau^{a,(\mathrm{TB})}_{nm}

(10)

where

\bbtau^{a,(\mathrm{TB})}_{nm}\equiv\sum_{i}U^{(\mathrm{TB})\dagger}_{ni}\tau^{a}_{i}U^{(\mathrm{TB})}_{im}.

(11)

Thus, if there is an atom that is not at the origin of the unit cell, the two phase conventions give different internal matrix elements.

Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
showed that when
\eta=0
, the additional terms involving
\bbtau
cancel out so that the two conventions give identical shift-current spectrum.
Here, I show that such cancellation is incomplete when
\eta\neq 0
.

First, let us define the generalized derivative with finite broadening
\eta
based on Bloch wavefunctions as follows:

\displaystyle r^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)=\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\Bigg\{\frac{v_{nm}^{a}\Delta_{nm}^{b}+v_{nm}^{b}\Delta_{nm}^{a}}{\omega_{nm}}-w^{ab}_{nm}

\displaystyle+\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{\infty}\Big[\real\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{pm}+i\eta}\right)v_{np}^{a}v_{pm}^{b}

\displaystyle\quad-\real\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{np}+i\eta}\right)v_{np}^{b}v_{pm}^{a}\Big]\Bigg\}.

(12)

When
\eta=0
,
r^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)
reduces to Eq. (12) of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
.
Our goal is to obtain a Wannier interpolation formula for
r^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)
which holds true at finite
\eta
.

We find

\displaystyle r^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)-r^{a;b}_{nm}(0)=\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{\infty}A_{nm,p}^{a,b}(\eta)

(13)

where we defined

\displaystyle A_{nm,p}^{a,b}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\real\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{pm}+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega_{pm}}\right)v_{np}^{a}v_{pm}^{b}

\displaystyle-\real\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{np}+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega_{np}}\right)v_{np}^{b}v_{pm}^{a}.

(14)

In principle, Eq. (
13
) requires a sum over an infinite number of unoccupied bands and thus cannot be computed using Wannier interpolation.
However, note that

\displaystyle\real\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{pm}+i\eta}-\frac{1}{\omega_{pm}}\right)

\displaystyle=\real\left[\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{pm}(\omega_{pm}+i\eta)}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\omega_{pm}(\omega_{pm}^{2}+\eta^{2})}

(15)

is very small as long as
\absolutevalue{\omega_{pm}}
is much larger than
\eta
.
Also, when computing the shift-current response,
r^{a;b}_{nm}
is computed only for the low-energy states
n
and
m
which are a pair of occupied and unoccupied states that can be resonantly excited by an external electric field.
Thus, Eq. (
13
) converges rapidly with the number of states included in the sum.
We exploit this rapid convergence to approximate the infinite sum in Eq. (
13
) by a finite sum over Wannier-interpolated bands

r^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)-r^{a;b}_{nm}(0)\approx\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}A_{nm,p}^{a,b}(\eta),

(16)

where
A_{nm,p}^{a,b}(\eta)
[Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
)] is calculated using the Wannier interpolation of energy and velocity matrix elements.
Later, I demonstrate the validity of this approximation using numerical calculation (see Fig.
3
).

) and Eq. (

The velocity matrix element can be written as

v_{nm}^{a}=\mathbbm{v}_{nm}^{a}+i\omega_{nm}\mathbbm{a}_{nm}^{a}.

(17)

This expression can be obtained from Eqs. (11), (22), and (23) of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
.
From Eqs. (

,

), one can easily show that

does not depend on the phase convention.
By using Eqs. (

,

Ab initio

), we find

\displaystyle\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}A_{nm,p}^{a,b}(\eta)=

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)-\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(0)+\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\real\left[\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{pm}(\omega_{pm}+i\eta)}\right]\left[v^{a}_{np}v^{b}_{pm}-(\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{a}+i\omega_{nm}\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{a})\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{b}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 72.00008pt-\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\real\left[\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{np}(\omega_{np}+i\eta)}\right]\left[v^{b}_{np}v^{a}_{pm}-\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{b}(\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{a}+i\omega_{nm}\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{a})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)-\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(0)+\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\real\left[\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{pm}(\omega_{pm}+i\eta)}\right]\left[i\omega_{mp}\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{a}\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{b}+v^{a}_{np}i\omega_{pm}\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{b}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 72.00008pt-\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\real\left[\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{np}(\omega_{np}+i\eta)}\right]\left[i\omega_{pn}\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{b}\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{a}+i\omega_{np}\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{b}v^{a}_{pm}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)-\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(0)+\frac{1}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\real\left(\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{pm}+i\eta}\right)\left[\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{a}\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{b}-(\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{a}+i\omega_{np}\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{a})\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{b}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 72.00008pt-\frac{1}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\real\left(\frac{-i\eta}{\omega_{np}+i\eta}\right)\left[\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{b}\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{a}-\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{b}(\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{a}+i\omega_{pm}\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{a})\right].

(18)

Using Eqs. (

) and

which is proven in

, we find

\displaystyle r^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)\approx\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\frac{\eta^{2}}{\omega_{pm}^{2}+\eta^{2}}\left[\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{a}\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{b}-(\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{a}+i\omega_{np}\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{a})\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{b}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{M}\frac{\eta^{2}}{\omega_{np}^{2}+\eta^{2}}\left[\mathbbm{v}_{np}^{b}\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{a}-\mathbbm{a}_{np}^{b}(\mathbbm{v}_{pm}^{a}+i\omega_{pm}\mathbbm{a}_{pm}^{a})\right].

The right-hand side of Eq. (

Ab initio

) is the desired phase-convention-independent Wannier interpolation formula for the generalized derivative of the interband dipole matrix element at finite
\eta
.
This expression is independent of the phase convention.
The reason is as follows.
Equation (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
) is phase-convention independent because
A^{a,b}_{nm,p}(\eta)
[Eq. (

Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
)] is defined only in terms of phase-convention independent matrix elements.
Also,
\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(0)
is phase-convention independent because it equals
r^{a;b}_{nm}(0)
which is defined in terms of Kohn-Sham wavefunctions.
Since the right-hand side of Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
) is the sum of Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
) and
\widetilde{r}^{a;b}_{nm}(0)
, it is also independent of the phase convention.

When
\eta=0
, the correction terms in Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
) vanish as expected.
When
\eta
is nonzero, the correction terms are finite.
Also, we find that these correction terms depend on the phase convention used for the Bloch sums.
Thus, if this correction is not applied, the calculated
r^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)
matrix elements, as well as the shift-current spectrum, becomes dependent on the phase convention.

Now, I demonstrate these findings with numerical calculations.
I implemented the correction formula Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
) in
Wannier90
and calculated the shift-current spectrum of monolayer GeS.
For the density functional theory calculation, I used fully relativistic ONCV pseudopotentials
Hamann 2013
taken from the PseudoDojo library (v0.4)
van Setten et al. 2018
and a wavefunction kinetic energy cutoff of 80 Ry.
Other computational parameters are the same as in
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
unless otherwise noted.

Figure
1
shows the difference between the shift-current spectra calculated using the two conventions.
Without the correction term, there is a small but nonzero discrepancy between the two spectra.
The discrepancy increases with
\eta
.
When the correction term is added, the shift-current spectra calculated using the TB and the W90 conventions perfectly agree for all values of
\eta
.
This result demonstrates the phase-convention independence of Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
).

Figure
2
shows the change in the shift-current spectrum due to the correction term.
For
\eta
=40 meV, which is used in
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
, the change in the shift-current spectrum due to the correction is below 0.3
\mathrm{\mu A/V^{2}}
.
This correction is nonzero but will have little effect on the calculated shift-current spectrum [Fig. 5(a) of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
] whose magnitude is around 50
\mathrm{\mu A/V^{2}}
.

Finally, I test the validity of approximating the infinite sum over bands in Eq. (
16
) by a finite sum over Wannier-interpolated bands as Eq. (
16
).
Let us define the following approximant of Eq. (
13
):

f_{n,m}^{a;b}(E_{\rm cutoff};\eta)=\frac{i}{\omega_{nm}}\sum_{p\neq n,m}^{\varepsilon_{p}\leq E_{\rm cutoff}}A_{nm,p}^{a,b}(\eta).

(20)

Here, I replaced the infinite sum over
p
in Eq. (
13
) with a finite sum for states with
\varepsilon_{p}\leq E_{\rm cutoff}
.
Equation (
13
) is recovered in the limit
E_{\rm cutoff}\rightarrow\infty
.
In a Wannier interpolation, only the bands inside the frozen window for Wannierization are accurately interpolated.
So, to use Wannier interpolation, we choose
E_{\rm cutoff}
to be
E_{\rm froz.}
, the upper bound of the frozen window for Wannierization.
Then, the truncation error in evaluating Eq. (
13
) is

\delta r^{a;b}_{nm}(\eta)=f_{n,m}^{a;b}(\infty;\eta)-f_{n,m}^{a;b}(E_{\rm froz.};\eta).

(21)

Therefore, we can safely approximate the infinite sum in Eq. (

Note that the use of
\eta
is necessary to avoid numerical problems due to near-degenerate states.
As one uses smaller
\eta
, a larger number of
k
points need to be used to converge the calculated shift-current spectrum.
In other words, the number of
k
points used gives the lower bound of
\eta
for the calculated spectrum to remain stable.

I also note that I fixed a small inconsistency between the shift current implementation of
Wannier90
and the formalism of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
.
In the
Wannier90
implementation, the broadening
\eta
was included in denominators that do not contain any intermediate states, such as
1/\omega_{nm}
in Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
), while the formalism
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
does not include
\eta
in such cases.
I changed the
Wannier90
code so that it is consistent with
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
.
Without this change, the exact agreement between the two phase conventions was not reached.
All results in this Comment were obtained after this change.

To summarize, I showed that when using a finite broadening parameter
\eta
to calculate the generalized derivative of the interband dipole matrix, one needs to add a correction term [Eq. (
Comment on “
Ab initio
calculation of the shift photocurrent by Wannier interpolation”
)] to Eq. (36) of
Ibañez-Azpiroz et al. 2018
.
Otherwise, the calculated shift-current spectrum becomes dependent on the phase convention for the Bloch sums.

Variations on the Maiani-​Testa approach and the inverse problem Preprint: CERN-TH-2020-412
M. Bruno, and M. T. Hansen

Abstract
We discuss a method to construct hadronic scattering and decay amplitudes from Euclidean correlators, by combining the approach of a regulated inverse Laplace transform with the work of Maiani and Testa Maiani:1990ca . Revisiting the original result of ref. Maiani:1990ca , we observe that the key observation, i.e. that only threshold scattering information can be extracted at large separations, can be understood by interpreting the correlator as a spectral function, ρ ⁡ ( ω ) \rho(\omega) , convoluted with the Euclidean kernel, e − ω ​ t e^{-\omega t} , which is sharply peaked at threshold. We therefore consider a modification in which a smooth step function, equal to one above a target energy, is inserted in the spectral decomposition. This can be achieved either through Backus-Gilbert-like methods or more directly using the variational approach. The result is a shifted resolution function, such that the large t t limit projects onto scattering or decay amplitudes above threshold. The utility of this method is highlighted through large t t expansions of both three- and four-point functions that include leading terms proportional to the real and imaginary parts (separately) of the target observable. This work also presents new results relevant for the un-modified correlator at threshold, including expressions for extracting the N ​ π N\pi scattering length from four-point functions and a new strategy to organize the large t t expansion that exhibits better convergence than the expansion in powers of 1 / t 1/t .

Introduction

Over the last decades, numerical lattice QCD has become a high-precision tool for predicting several non-perturbative strong-force observables, including hadronic masses, decay constants and form factors. Looking beyond these quantities, each defined in terms of single-hadron states, lattice QCD has also shown outstanding progress in calculating multi-hadron observables including
2\to 2
scattering and
1\to 2
decay amplitudes. The successful determination of such amplitudes is especially impressive due to the presence of the Euclidean metric, required in order to apply Monte Carlo methods in estimating the lattice QCD path integral. While the analytic continuation to Minkowski correlators is formally guaranteed by the Osterwalder-Schrader theorem
Osterwalder:1973dx
, in practice the limited knowledge of correlation functions on a finite set of points, together with the presence of statistical uncertainties, implies that direct extraction is a numerically ill-posed inverse problem, see e.g. ref.
Bertero
.

Offering another perspective on this challenge, Maiani and Testa
Maiani:1990ca
showed that (for energies above production threshold) asymptotically separating individual pion fields in Euclidean time leads to correlators dominated by off-shell contributions. As we review in more detail in section
2
, this means that matrix elements of the form
\langle\pi|\hat{\pi}(0)|\pi\pi\rangle
contribute, where
\langle\pi|
represents a single pion state and
|\pi\pi\rangle
a two-pion asymptotic in- or out-state. The infamous off-shellness refers to the fact that the four momentum associated with the operator
\hat{\pi}
does not satisfy
q^{2}=m_{\pi}^{2}
, where
m_{\pi}
is the physical particle mass. As a result the matrix element depends on the details of the operator used and gives no useful information.

This limitation was circumvented by Lüscher
Luscher:1985dn
;
Luscher:1986pf
, who showed that the values of the low-lying two-pion energies in a finite periodic spatial volume are determined by the on-shell
2\to 2
scattering amplitude (up to corrections falling faster than any power of
1/L
where
L
denotes the box length). Thus, the scattering amplitude can be extracted indirectly, from discrete spectra determined in lattice calculations. In more recent years, due especially to efficient methods for evaluating quark-field Wick contractions
Peardon:2009gh
and an improved understanding of the importance of a large operator basis
Blossier:2009kd
, this method has proven to be extremely successful in the determination of elastic two-hadron scattering amplitudes.

The main limitation of the finite-volume formalism, besides the technical challenge of measuring several excited-state energy levels and matrix elements, is the proliferation of multi-particle channels as the scattering energy increases. Here it is important to note that any finite-volume method must formally treat all open multi-hadron channels (or argue that they are irrelevant) in order to reach a prediction about any individual scattering, decay, or transition amplitude. This is because finite-volume energies and matrix elements generically depend on a mixture of all physically allowed scattering processes.

For these reasons, recent work has revisited prospects for the direct analytic continuation of numerical correlation functions in the context of inverting the Laplace transform

G(t,L)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,e^{-\omega t}\,\rho(\omega,L)\qquad\qquad(t>0)\,,

(1)

where
G(t,L)
represents a general Euclidean correlator and the right-hand side defines the spectral function,
\rho(\omega,L)
. A regulated inverse of this simple relation could potentially unlock an alternative method in extracting inclusive quantities, such as heavy-particle lifetimes or the hadronic tensor
Hansen:2017mnd
, as well as scattering amplitudes
Bulava:2019kbi
, directly from spectral functions convoluted with a known resolution function

\widehat{\rho}(\overline{\omega},\Delta,L)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\widehat{\delta}_{\Delta}(\omega,\overline{\omega})\,\rho(\omega,L)\,,

(2)

where
\widehat{\delta}_{\Delta}(\omega,\overline{\omega})
is peaked at
\omega=\overline{\omega}
with characteristic width
\Delta
. Encouraging progress has recently been made in developing improved strategies to regulate the inverse problem and systematically target a resolution function
Hansen:2019idp
;
Bailas:2020qmv
to extract eq. (
2
) from eq. (
1

L\to\infty
) is taken before the resolution width is sent to zero (
\Delta\to 0
). Such ideas might prove useful also in the context of QED corrections to semi-leptonic decays, and similar processes,

In this manuscript, we present a strategy for combining the ideas summarized by eqs. (

2

We present the idea both in the context of three- and four-point functions and, as a side benefit, we also reach new results for extracting threshold information from standard correlators.

Although we focus in this work on three- and four-point functions, the basic idea can already be expressed with a two point function of a scalar current,
J(t,\bm{x})
,

G(t)=\int d^{3}\bm{x}\,\langle J(t,\bm{x})J(0)\rangle\,,

(3)

where we assume the
L\to\infty
limit has been taken. We then define the modified correlator

G^{\Theta}(t|s)=\int d^{3}\bm{x}\,\langle J(t,\bm{x})\Theta(\hat{H}-\sqrt{s},\Delta)J(0)\rangle\,,

(4)

where
\Theta(z,\Delta)
is a smoothened Heaviside function, interpolating from zero for
z<0
to one for
z>0
. A specific definition of
\Theta(z,\Delta)
is given in eq. (
15
) below, but any function can be used provided it is smooth and becomes the usual Heaviside step function for
\Delta\to 0
. Defining the spectral function as

\rho(\omega)=\int d^{3}\bm{x}\,\langle J(0,\bm{x})\delta(\hat{H}-\omega)J(0)\rangle\,,

(5)

note that the following relations hold:

G(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,e^{-\omega t}\,\rho(\omega)\,,\qquad\qquad G^{\Theta}(t|s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\big[\Theta(\omega-\sqrt{s},\Delta)e^{-\omega t}\big]\,\rho(\omega)\,.

(6)

These two simple results form the basis of this work. In the expression for
G(t)
, the kernel
e^{-\omega t}
becomes sharply peaked at threshold for large
t
, leading to the threshold dominance famously identified by Maiani and Testa
Maiani:1990ca
. For
G^{\Theta}(t|s)
, by contrast, the peak forms at
\omega=\sqrt{s}
and one can extract time-like observables away from threshold.

2
Time-like form factors

Following Maiani and Testa we begin by defining the Euclidean correlator

\displaystyle G_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\big\langle\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{1}}(t_{1})\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{2})J(0)\big\rangle\,,

(11)

where
\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}}(t)=\int d^{3}\bm{x}\,\pi(t,\bm{x})e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{x}}
is a generic single-pion interpolating field and
J(0)
is a local scalar current. Then, taking
t_{1}
large and positive, we write

\displaystyle G_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=Z_{\pi}\,\frac{e^{-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}t_{1}}}{2\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}}\frac{e^{-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}t_{2}}}{2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}}c_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{2})+\mathcal{O}\Big(e^{-\sqrt{9m_{\pi}^{2}+\bm{q}_{1}^{2}}t_{1}}\Big)\,,

(12)

\displaystyle c_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{2})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}e^{\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}t_{2}}\,\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{1}|\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{2})J(0)|0\rangle}{\sqrt{Z_{\pi}}}\,,

(13)

where
\omega_{\bm{q}}=\sqrt{\bm{q}^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}}
and
Z_{\pi}=\langle\pi,\bm{p}|\pi(0)|0\rangle^{2}
. Here the single particle state has the usual relativistic normalization,
\langle\pi,\bm{q}|\pi,\bm{q}^{\prime}\rangle=2\omega_{\bm{q}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\bm{q}-\bm{q}^{\prime})
.

As was shown in ref.
Maiani:1990ca
, for
\bm{q}_{1}\neq\bm{q}_{2}
[which, for vanishing total momentum (
\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2}=\bm{0}
), translates to
\bm{q}_{1}\neq\bm{0}
], the quantity
c_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{2})
contains growing exponentials as
t_{2}\to\infty
, with coefficients depending on scattering amplitudes for which one of the external legs is off the mass shell. To better understand the limitations associated with this, we define the modified quantity

\displaystyle c^{\Theta}_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}e^{\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}t_{2}}\,\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{1}|\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{2}}(0)\,\Theta(\hat{M}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,e^{-(\hat{H}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}})t_{2}}\,J(0)|0\rangle}{\sqrt{Z_{\pi}}}\,,

(14)

where
\Theta(\omega,\Delta)
is a smooth Heaviside function, e.g.

\Theta(\omega,\Delta)=\frac{\tanh(2\omega/\Delta)+1}{2}\,,

(15)

and
\hat{M}=\sqrt{\hat{H}^{2}-\hat{P}^{2}}
is an operator giving the center-of-momentum-frame energy. In section
4
, we describe strategies for accessing this object in a numerical lattice calculation, including the role of the finite-volume boundary conditions. In this section we take
c^{\Theta}
as given and show that it can be used to access the real and imaginary parts of

f\big(s(\bm{q})\big)\equiv\langle\pi\pi,\text{out},\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}|J(0)|0\rangle\,,

(16)

where we have introduced

s(\bm{q})\equiv(\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}+\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})^{2}-(\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2})^{2}\,.

(17)

In the same spirit as the inverse methods described in refs.
Hansen:2017mnd
;
Bulava:2019kbi

s(\bm{q})
, provided the correlator can be estimated with an appropriate range of
\Delta

\omega_{0}

\displaystyle B_{k}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle\equiv\langle k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}|J(0)|0\rangle\,,

(27)

where
k
is a channel index (referring e.g. to
\pi\pi
,
K\overline{K}
,
\pi\pi\pi\pi
),
n_{k}
is the number of particles in the channel and
S_{k}
the number of permutations of identical particles. We define
k=1
as the
\pi\pi
channel, with internal quantum numbers matching
\pi(0)|\pi,\bm{q}\rangle
.

The final key step is to express
A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})
in terms of an off-shell amplitude
\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(\eta|\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p}))
. We use the bold notation here whenever the amplitude has at least one off-shell external leg. This object is defined in more detail in appendix
A.1
and for the present argument it suffices to note that
\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}(0|\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p}))=\mathcal{M}_{k}(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p}))
is the usual on-shell two-to-
n_{k}
scattering amplitude as a function of Lorentz invariants organized in the vector
\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})
. For example in the case of a two-to-two amplitude

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\big(s,t\big)\,,

(28)

\displaystyle=\Big((\omega_{\bm{p}_{1}}+\omega_{\bm{p}_{2}})^{2}-(\bm{p}_{1}+\bm{p}_{2})^{2}\,,\ (\omega_{\bm{p}_{1}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}})^{2}-(\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{q}_{1})^{2}\Big)\,.

(29)

The matrix element
A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})
admits a simple decomposition in terms of the off-shell amplitude

A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})=\delta_{k,1}(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\big[\delta^{3}(\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{q}_{1})+\delta^{3}(\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{q}_{1})\big]-2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}}{\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})-i\epsilon}\,,

(30)

where the distance of the
\pi(0)
leg from the mass shell is given by

\displaystyle\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}})^{2}-(\bm{P}-\bm{q}_{1})^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}\,.

(31)

Combining Eqs. (
23
) and (
30
) gives the generalization of Maiani and Testa’s result to the case of non-zero total momenta and to the
\Theta
-function modification of the correlator. In appendix
A.2
we give explicit expressions in the case of general kinematics and discuss their utility. Here we focus on the role of the Heaviside function and set
\bm{q}_{1}=-\bm{q}_{2}=\bm{q}
to reach

c^{\Theta}_{\bm{q},-\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})=\Theta(2\omega_{q}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,f\big(s(q^{2})\big)\\
-2\omega_{q}\int_{2m_{\pi}}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{2\pi}\,e^{-(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{q})t_{2}}\,\Theta(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,\frac{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})}{(\mathcal{E}-\omega_{q})^{2}-\omega_{q}^{2}-i\epsilon}\,,

(32)

where we have replaced
\bm{q}\to q
in quantities that only depend on
\bm{q}^{2}=q^{2}
and have introduced

\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})=\sum_{k}\int d\Phi_{k}\,2\pi\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-E(\bm{p})\big)\,\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}B_{k}(\bm{p})\,.

(33)

For example, in the case where only the
\pi\pi
channel contributes, i.e. for
\mathcal{E}^{2}<(4m_{\pi})^{2}
, this becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})

\displaystyle=2\pi\frac{1}{2}\frac{4\pi}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\!dp\,p^{2}\,\frac{1}{4\omega_{p}^{2}}\,\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{p}\big)\,\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}\big(\eta(q,p)|\,\mathcal{E}^{2}\big)^{\!*}f(\mathcal{E}^{2})\,,

(34)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{2}/4-m_{\pi}^{2}}}{8\pi\mathcal{E}}\,\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}\big((\mathcal{E}-\omega_{q})^{2}-\omega_{q}^{2}|\,\mathcal{E}^{2}\big)^{\!*}f(\mathcal{E}^{2})\,,

(35)

where
\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}
is the scattering amplitude projected to zero orbital angular momentum

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}\big(\eta(q,p)|\,\mathcal{E}^{2}\big)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d\Omega\,\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)\,.

(36)

The on-shell limit is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}/2)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{2}/4-m_{\pi}^{2}}}{8\pi\mathcal{E}}\frac{16\pi\mathcal{E}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{2}/4-m_{\pi}^{2}}}\frac{e^{-2i\delta_{\sf s}(\mathcal{E})}-1}{-2i}e^{i\delta_{\sf s}(\mathcal{E})}|f(\mathcal{E}^{2})|\,,

(37)

\displaystyle=2\sin\delta_{\sf s}(\mathcal{E})|f(\mathcal{E}^{2})|=2\,\text{Im}f(\mathcal{E}^{2})\,.

(38)

Remarkably, the relation between the on-shell
\mathcal{G}
function and
\text{Im}f(\mathcal{E}^{2})
in fact holds for all
\mathcal{E}^{2}
, as we prove in appendix
A.3
.

Returning to the general case, the final step is to perform the change of variables
x=(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{q})t_{2}
to reach

c^{\Theta}_{\bm{q},-\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})=\Theta(2\omega_{q}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,\text{Re}\Big[f\big(s(q^{2})\big)\Big]-\int_{-(2\omega_{q}-2m_{\pi})t_{2}}^{\infty}\!dx\,\kappa(t_{2},x)\,\mathcal{G}(x/t_{2}+2\omega_{q},\omega_{q})\,,

(39)

where

\displaystyle\kappa(t_{2},x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{q}}{\pi}\,e^{-x}\,\frac{\Theta(x/t_{2}+2\omega_{q}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)}{x/t_{2}+2\omega_{q}}\,\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\,,

(40)

and
\mathcal{P}
indicates the principal-value pole prescription. To reach this expression, one uses the fact that
c^{\Theta}
is real so that one can take the real part of the right-hand side for free. As
\mathcal{G}
is also real this simply replaces:
f(s(q^{2}))\to\text{Re}f(s(q^{2}))
and
1/{(\eta-i\epsilon)}\to\mathcal{P}[1/\eta]
. Alternatively, one can demonstrate that the imaginary part of
1/{(\eta-i\epsilon)}
explicitly cancels that of
f(s(q^{2}))
, as we review in appendix
A.4
, following ref.
Maiani:1990ca
.

Equations (
33
), (
39
) and (
40
) summarize the main new technical results of this section. In the following subsections we explore their implications for extracting scattering information.

2.1
Threshold kinematics

As a first step we set
\omega_{0}=\omega_{q}-\epsilon=m_{\pi}-\epsilon
to reach the threshold case already considered in ref.
Maiani:1990ca
. For these choices (together with
\Delta\to 0
) the
\Theta
-function has no effect and only the two-pion channel contributes in
\mathcal{G}
. We reach

c^{\Theta}_{\,\bm{0},\bm{0}}(t_{2}|m_{\pi})=f(4m_{\pi}^{2})\bigg[1-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}\,\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(2m_{\pi}t_{2})\bigg]\,,

(41)

or, to give an explicit expression in terms of the Euclidean correlator,

\big\langle\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{0}}(t_{1})\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{0}}(t_{2})J(0)\big\rangle=Z_{\pi}\,\frac{e^{-m_{\pi}(t_{1}+t_{2})}}{4m_{\pi}^{2}}f(4m_{\pi}^{2})\bigg[1-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}\,\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(2m_{\pi}t_{2})\bigg]\,,

(42)

where
e^{-3m_{\pi}t_{1}}
has been dropped. Here the
g_{n}
coefficients arise in an expansion of the amplitudes

\displaystyle\begin{split}g_{n}&\equiv\frac{1}{n!}m_{\pi}^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{d\omega^{n}}\bigg[\frac{f(4\omega^{2})}{f(4m_{\pi}^{2})}\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}\big((2\omega-m_{\pi})^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}|\,4\omega^{2}\big)^{\!*}\bigg]_{\omega=m_{\pi}}\,,\end{split}

(43)

and the functions
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(b)
are integrals of the kernel
\kappa
times the phase space,
\sqrt{\mathcal{E}^{2}/4-m_{\pi}^{2}}/(8\pi\mathcal{E})
, both in the limiting case of the threshold amplitude. They can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(b)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{b^{n+1/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\,x^{n}\,\frac{e^{-x}}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{2+x/b}}{\sqrt{x}(1+x/b)^{2}}\,,

(44)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{b^{n+1/2}}\Gamma(n+1/2)\Big[1+\mathcal{O}\big(1/b\big)\Big]\,.

(45)

Expanding in powers of
1/b=1/(2m_{\pi}t_{2})
generates the original series presented in ref.
Maiani:1990ca
. However, as we illustrate in figure
2
, the leading terms converge slowly to the full integrals and the latter may prove to be a better basis in describing the correlation function.

:

\mathcal{I}^{(n)}(b)
functions (solid lines), defined in eq. (

) of the main text. The dashed lines show the large

as shown in eq. (

).

Combining
g_{0}=\mathcal{M}_{\sf s}(4m_{\pi}^{2})=32\pi m_{\pi}a_{\pi\pi}
with
\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}
gives the known result
6
6

6

Here we differ from eq. (10) of ref.
Maiani:1990ca
by a factor of 2 in the
1/\sqrt{t_{2}}
-dependent term. We are currently in correspondence with the authors of that work to clarify this difference. Note also that, following ref.
Maiani:1990ca
, we have set our convention for the scattering-length such that
a_{\pi\pi}<0
for repulsive interactions at threshold.

\displaystyle c^{\Theta}_{\,\bm{0},\bm{0}}(t_{2}|m_{\pi})

\displaystyle=f\big(4m_{\pi}^{2}\big)\bigg[1-a_{\pi\pi}\sqrt{\frac{m_{\pi}}{\pi t_{2}}}+\mathcal{O}\big(t_{2}^{-3/2}\big)\bigg]\,,

(46)

and thus

\big\langle\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{0}}(t_{1})\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{0}}(t_{2})J(0)\big\rangle=Z_{\pi}\,\frac{e^{-m_{\pi}(t_{1}+t_{2})}}{4m_{\pi}^{2}}f\big(4m_{\pi}^{2}\big)\bigg[1-a_{\pi\pi}\sqrt{\frac{m_{\pi}}{\pi t_{2}}}+\mathcal{O}\big(t_{2}^{-3/2}\big)\bigg]\,,

(47)

where
a_{\pi\pi}
is the two-particle scattering length. Our expressions allow one to easily reach generalizations of this, for example

c^{\Theta}_{\,\bm{0},\bm{0}}(t_{2}|m_{\pi})=f\big(4m_{\pi}^{2}\big)\bigg[1-32\pi m_{\pi}a_{\pi\pi}\mathcal{I}^{(0)}(2m_{\pi}t_{2})-g_{1}\mathcal{I}^{(1)}(2m_{\pi}t_{2})+\mathcal{O}\big(\mathcal{I}^{(2)}\big)\bigg]\,,

(48)

with

g_{1}=256\pi m_{\pi}^{3}a_{\pi\pi}\frac{\partial_{s}f(s)}{f(4m_{\pi}^{2})}+8m_{\pi}^{2}\,\partial_{s}\mathcal{M}_{\sf s}(s)^{*}+4m_{\pi}^{2}\,\partial_{\eta}\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}(\eta|4m_{\pi}^{2})^{*}\bigg|_{s=4m_{\pi}^{2}\,,\ \eta=0}\,.

(49)

This gives a more explicit expression for the leading off-shellness contaminating this correlator. The
\eta
derivative represents a small variation in the virtuality of one external leg away from the mass shell. The quantity is perfectly well defined but depends on the details of the operator
\pi(x)
and has no physical meaning for pion scattering.

2.2
General kinematics

Next we take
\omega_{0}=\omega_{q}>m_{\pi}
and write

c^{\Theta}_{\,\bm{q},-\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{q})=\Theta(0,\Delta)\,\text{Re}\Big[f\big(4\omega_{q}^{2}\big)\Big]-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}(t_{2},\omega_{q},\Delta)\,,

(50)

where
\Theta(0,\Delta)=1/2
for the specific choice given in eq. (
15
). For the second term we have substituted the expansion

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\big(2\omega_{q}[1+x/(2\omega_{q}t_{2})],\,\omega_{q}\big)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{(2\omega_{q}t_{2})^{n}}\,,

(51)

and have introduced the basis of integrals

\mathcal{J}^{(n)}(t_{2},\omega_{q},\Delta)\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(2\omega_{q}t_{2})^{n}}\int_{-2(\omega_{q}-m_{\pi})t_{2}}^{\infty}dx\,x^{n}e^{-x}\,\frac{\Theta(x,t_{2}\Delta)}{1+x/(2\omega_{q}t_{2})}\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\,.

(52)

Note here that the principal value is only required for
n=0
. Substituting
g_{0}=2\,\text{Im}f\big(4\omega_{q}^{2}\big)
, demonstrated in appendix
A.3
, then gives

c^{\Theta}_{\,\bm{q},-\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{q})=\Theta(0,\Delta)\,\text{Re}\Big[f\big(4\omega_{q}^{2}\big)\Big]-2\,\text{Im}\Big[f\big(4\omega_{q}^{2}\big)\Big]\mathcal{J}^{(0)}(t_{2},\omega_{q},\Delta)-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}(t_{2},\omega_{q},\Delta)\,.

(53)

for fixed values of

, as a function of

. We stress that these expressions require

in order for the principal-value pole prescription to properly regulate the integral.

We close this subsection by addressing a final subtlety concerning the fact that the sum over
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
in eq. (
53
) [as well as that over
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}
in eq. (
41
)] is, in fact, a divergent asymptotic series. The detailed properties of this series depend on the coefficients
g_{n}
, but the divergence is expected as
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}\sim n!
for large
n
. We stress however that the task here is to estimate the difference between
c^{\Theta}
and the first two terms on the right-hand side of eq. (
53
). This difference is finite and the divergent asymptotic series provides a representation of it in the usual way, i.e.

c^{\Theta}_{\,\bm{q},-\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{q})-\Theta(0,\Delta)\,\text{Re}\Big[f\big(4\omega_{q}^{2}\big)\Big]+2\,\text{Im}\Big[f\big(4\omega_{q}^{2}\big)\Big]\mathcal{J}^{(0)}(t_{2},\omega_{q},\Delta)\\
=-\sum_{n=1}^{N}g_{n}\mathcal{J}^{(n)}(t_{2},\omega_{q},\Delta)+\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{J}^{(N+1)})\,.

(54)

Given the clear hierarchy in the
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
functions, it should be feasible to keep the first few terms, with the
g_{n}
as fit parameters and remove the contamination in order to extract the form factor.

3
Two-to-two scattering amplitudes

We now imitate the results of the previous section, but focusing here on a four-point function built from two nucleon and two pion fields. We begin with the four-point analog of eq. (
14
), defining

c^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})\equiv\frac{2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}e^{\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}t^{\prime}}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}e^{-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}t}}{Z_{N}}C_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}C_{ab}\,\\
\times\langle\pi^{a^{\prime}},\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}|\widetilde{N}^{b^{\prime}}_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}(0)\,\Theta(\hat{M}-M_{0},\Delta)\,e^{-\hat{H}(t^{\prime}-t)}e^{\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}t^{\prime}}e^{-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}t}\,\widetilde{N}^{\dagger b}_{-\bm{q}_{2}}(0)|\pi^{a},\bm{q}_{1}\rangle\,.

(55)

Here
a
and
a^{\prime}
are isospin indices for the pions and
b
and
b^{\prime}
are combined spin and isospin indices. The coefficients
C_{ab}
and
C_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}
are chosen to project to
N\pi
states with definite spin and isospin. As above, here a sum over the repeated indices is implied and the tildes on the operators indicate the projection to definite spatial momentum. Note that momenta with a
1
subscript correspond to pion fields and those with a
2
to nucleons, such that

\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}=\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+\bm{q}_{1}^{2}}\,,\ \ \ \ \ \ \omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}=\sqrt{m_{N}^{2}+\bm{q}_{2}^{2}}\,,

(56)

where
m_{N}
is the nucleon mass. Up to the
\Theta
function, this quantity is directly extractable from a standard Euclidean correlator

C_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}\,\langle\widetilde{\pi}^{a^{\prime}}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}(t^{\prime\prime})\,\widetilde{N}^{b^{\prime}}_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\,\widetilde{N}^{\dagger b}_{-\bm{q}_{2}}(t)\,\widetilde{\pi}^{b}_{\bm{q}_{1}}(0)\rangle\,C_{ab}\,.

(57)

The strategy for implementing
\Theta(\hat{M}-M_{0},\Delta)
is discussed in the next section.
7
7

7

\Theta
by
M_{0}
rather than

Inserting a complete set of states then yields

c^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})=\sum_{k}\int\!d\Phi_{k}\,e^{-(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}})t^{\prime}}e^{+(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})t}\\[-5.0pt]

(58)

in direct analog to eq. (

) above, here with

\displaystyle A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime},\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime};\bm{p})

\displaystyle\equiv 2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}\frac{C_{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}\,\langle\pi^{a^{\prime}},\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}|\,N^{b^{\prime}}(0)\,|k,\mathrm{out},\bm{p}_{1}\dots\bm{p}_{n}\rangle}{\sqrt{Z_{N}}}\,,

(59)

where

represents the
N\pi

and only a single delta function arises, since only a single contraction can appear in the disconnected contribution. Note that this decomposition assumes that no two-particle bound state appears in the channel of interest, an assumption we have already noted in footnote 3. Substituting eq. (

58
) yields four terms: one doubly disconnected, two singly disconnected, and the final term proportional to
|\mathcal{M}|^{2}

\bm{q}_{1}=-\bm{q}_{2}=\bm{q}

\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}=-\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}=\bm{q}^{\prime}

(61)

where we have suppressed the
k=1
index, introduced mass labels on the

\mathcal{E}(q)=\omega_{\pi,q}+\omega_{N,q}

\displaystyle\eta(q^{\prime},q)

\displaystyle\equiv\big(\mathcal{E}(q)-\omega_{\pi,q^{\prime}}\big)^{2}-(\omega_{N,q^{\prime}})^{2}\,,

(62)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})

(63)

.

In the next paragraph we discuss the final term in eq. (

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0ptc^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{q}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})_{\sf c}

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,{\mathcal{M}}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)\Big[\Theta\big(\mathcal{E}(q)-M_{0},\Delta\big)(t^{\prime}-t+1/\omega_{N,q})-\Theta^{(1,0)}\big(\mathcal{E}(q)-M_{0},\Delta\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt-4\omega_{N,q}\Theta\big(\mathcal{E}(q)-M_{0},\Delta\big)\Big[\frac{\mathcal{E}(q)}{2\omega_{N,q}}\text{Re}{\mathcal{M}}^{(1,0)}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)+\text{Re}\bm{\mathcal{M}}^{(1,0,0)}\big(0|\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)\Big]

(64)

where the superscript numbers indicate derivatives with respect to the arguments, i.e.
\eta,s,t
in the case of
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
. This is a striking result: up to the contaminations from the final term, to which we turn shortly, the correlator gives an estimator of the on-shell
N\pi\to N\pi
scattering amplitude. This is contaminated by an off-shell term with superscript
(1,0,0)
, but with a different time dependence, such that the physical result can be disentangled. This result holds for
q=q^{\prime}
and
\text{Re}\,\mathcal{M}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\big)
simply represents the real part of on-shell amplitude for
N(\bm{q})+\pi(-\bm{q})\to N(\bm{q}^{\prime})+\pi(-\bm{q}^{\prime})
.

We turn now to
\mathcal{C}
, the contamination resulting from the connected parts of both
A_{k}
and
A^{*}_{k}
. For the simplifying case of
q^{\prime}=q
, the fully connected quantity is given by

\mathcal{C}(t^{\prime},t|\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q},M_{0},\Delta)=\int_{m_{\pi}+m_{N}}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{2\pi}\,e^{-(\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}(q))(t^{\prime}-t)}\Theta(\mathcal{E}-M_{0},\Delta)\\[-2.0pt]
\times\,\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{E},\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\,\frac{(2\omega_{N,q})^{2}}{(\mathcal{E}-\omega_{\pi,q}+\omega_{N,q})^{2}}\bigg[\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}(q)}\bigg]^{2}\,,

(65)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{E},\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int d\Phi_{k}\,2\pi\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-E(\bm{p})\big)\,\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})\big)\,.

(66)

Applying the change of variables
x=(\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{E}(q))(t^{\prime}-t)
then yields

\mathcal{C}(t^{\prime},t|\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q},M_{0},\Delta)=\int_{-(\mathcal{E}(q)-m_{\pi}-m_{N})(t^{\prime}-t)}^{\infty}\!\!\!dx\,\mathcal{H}\big(x/(t^{\prime}-t)+\mathcal{E}(q),\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q}\big)\,\kappa(x,t^{\prime}-t)\,,

(67)

where

\kappa(x,t^{\prime}-t)=\frac{(t^{\prime}-t)}{2\pi}e^{-x}\,\Theta\big(x/(t^{\prime}-t)+\mathcal{E}(q)-M_{0},\Delta\big)\frac{(2\omega_{N,q})^{2}}{[x/(t^{\prime}-t)+2\omega_{N,q}]^{2}}\bigg[\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\bigg]^{2}\,.

(68)

Equations (
64
), (
66
) (
67
), and (
68
) summarize the main new technical results of this section. In the following subsections we explore their implications for extracting scattering information. In direct analogy to our treatment in section
2
, we divide the discussion into threshold and general kinematics.

3.1
Threshold kinematics

For
\mathcal{E}
near threshold and
\bm{q}=\bm{q}^{\prime}=\bm{0}
, the function
\mathcal{H}
takes the form

\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{E},\bm{0},\bm{0})=\int\!\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}}{2\omega_{N,\bm{p}}(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2\omega_{\pi,\bm{p}}}\,2\pi\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-\omega_{\pi,\bm{p}}-\omega_{N,\bm{p}}\big)|\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\big(\eta(\bm{0},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{0},\bm{p})\big)|^{2}\,,

(69)

where the coordinates in the scattering amplitude are given explicitly by

\displaystyle\eta(\bm{0},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=(\omega_{\pi,\bm{p}}+\omega_{N,\bm{p}}-m_{\pi})^{2}-m_{N}^{2}\,,

(70)

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}(\bm{0},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=(s,t)=\Big((\omega_{\pi,\bm{p}}+\omega_{N,\bm{p}})^{2}\,,\ (\omega_{\pi,\bm{p}}-m_{\pi})^{2}-\bm{p}^{2}\Big)\,.

(71)

Note here that, although the angular dependence is absent for
\bm{q}=\bm{q}^{\prime}=\bm{0}
, the amplitude nonetheless depends on the Mandelstam variable
t
as an artifact of the off-shell kinematics.
Next, we use the fact that the Dirac delta function sets
\bm{p}^{2}=p^{2}=k_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})^{2}
, with the magnitude of back-to-back momenta for non-degenerate particles given by

k_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})^{2}=\frac{\mathcal{E}^{2}}{4}-\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}+m_{N}^{2}}{2}+\frac{(m_{N}^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2})^{2}}{4\mathcal{E}^{2}}\,.

(72)

Introducing the shorthand

\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})=\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\big(\eta(\bm{0},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{0},\bm{p})\big)\bigg|_{\bm{p}^{2}=k_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})^{2}}\,,

(73)

and evaluating the remaining phase-space integral, we reach

\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{E},\bm{0},\bm{0})=\big|\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})\big|^{2}\frac{k_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})}{4\pi\mathcal{E}}\,.

(74)

Substituting these simplifications into the definition of
\mathcal{C}
, the four-point function reduces to

\hskip-10.0ptc^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{0}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})_{\sf c}=8\pi(m_{N}+m_{\pi})\,a_{N\pi}\,(t^{\prime}-t+1/m_{N})-2(m_{N}+m_{\pi})\text{Re}\,\partial_{s}{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}(s)-4m_{N}\text{Re}\,\partial_{\eta}\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}(\eta|s)\\[5.0pt]
+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!dx\,\,\frac{(t^{\prime}-t)e^{-x}}{[1+x/[2m_{N}(t^{\prime}-t)]]^{2}}\bigg[\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\bigg]^{2}\big|\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})\big|^{2}\frac{k_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})}{4\pi\mathcal{E}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{E}=x/(t^{\prime}-t)+m_{N}+m_{\pi}}\,.

(75)

In this result, the top line depends only on
\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}
, the off-shell scattering amplitude projected to zero orbital angular momentum. This differs from
\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{N\pi}
because, for terms that are linear in the scattering amplitude, both the incoming and outgoing spatial momenta are set to zero. As a result the Mandelstam
t
is set identically to zero and no higher partial waves can contribute. Here we have also used the relation between the threshold scattering amplitude and scattering length for the case of non-degenerate particles

\mathcal{M}_{\sf s}\big((m_{N}+m_{\pi})^{2}\big)=8\pi(m_{N}+m_{\pi})a_{N\pi}\,.

(76)

In direct analog to our analysis of the three-point function, the final step is to expand the final term of eq. (
75
). We reach

c^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{0}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})_{\sf c}=8\pi(m_{N}+m_{\pi})\,a_{N\pi}\,(t^{\prime}-t+1/m_{N})-2(m_{N}+m_{\pi})\text{Re}\,\partial_{s}{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}(s)\\[5.0pt]
-4m_{N}\text{Re}\,\partial_{\eta}\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{\sf s}(\eta|s)+\frac{4(t^{\prime}-t)}{(1+m_{\pi}/m_{N})^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n}\,\mathcal{K}^{(n)}\big(b,\delta m\big)\,,

(77)

where in the final term we have introduced
b=(m_{N}+m_{\pi})(t^{\prime}-t)
and
\delta m=(m_{N}-m_{\pi})/(m_{N}+m_{\pi})
. We have also introduced

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(n)}(b,\delta m)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}b^{n+1/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dx\,x^{n}\,e^{-x}\sqrt{\frac{x(x/b+2)(x/b+1-\delta m)}{(x/b+1+\delta m)^{3}(x/b+1)^{4}}}\bigg[\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\bigg]^{2}\,,

(78)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{16\pi^{2}b^{n+1/2}}\Gamma(n-1/2)\frac{(1-\delta m)^{1/2}}{(1+\delta m)^{3/2}}\Big[1+\mathcal{O}\big(1/b\big)\Big]\,,

(79)

\displaystyle h_{n}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{n!}\ (m_{N}+m_{\pi})^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{d\mathcal{E}^{n}}\big|\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{N\pi}(\mathcal{E})\big|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}=m_{N}+m_{\pi}}\,.

(80)

In the case of degenerate, non-identical particles the kernel simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(n)}(b,0)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}b^{n+1/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dx\,x^{n}\,e^{-x}\frac{\sqrt{x(x/b+2)}}{(x/b+1)^{3}}\bigg[\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\bigg]^{2}\,,

(81)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{16\pi^{2}b^{n+1/2}}\Gamma(n-1/2)\Big[1+\mathcal{O}\big(1/b\big)\Big]\,,

(82)

and in the case of identical particles one requires an additional factor of
(1/2)
in these expressions.

Expanding
\mathcal{K}^{(0)}(b,\delta m)
in powers of
t^{\prime}-t
gives the direct analog of ref.
Maiani:1990ca
for the four-point function

c^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{0}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})_{\sf c}=8\pi(m_{N}+m_{\pi})\,a_{N\pi}\,(t^{\prime}-t)\\
-16\,a_{N\pi}^{2}\sqrt{2\pi(m_{N}+m_{\pi})m_{N}m_{\pi}(t^{\prime}-t)}+\mathcal{O}\big((t^{\prime}-t)^{0}\big)\,.

(83)

This was already highlighted in the introduction, see eq. (
10
). The relation appears promising as two separate time dependences, together with
a_{N\pi}
and
a^{2}_{N\pi}
coefficients, may give a strong constraint of this threshold observable. As with the three-point function, one reaches a better description by working in the basis of
\mathcal{K}
integrals. See figure
4
.

3.2
General kinematics

Finally, the general-energy result for the four-point function, with
M_{0}=\mathcal{E}(q)
, reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hskip-20.0ptc^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{q}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})_{\sf c}&=\text{Re}\,{\mathcal{M}}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)\Big[\frac{t^{\prime}-t+1/\omega_{N,q}}{2}-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\Big]\\[5.0pt]
&\hskip 0.0pt-\mathcal{E}(q)\text{Re}{\mathcal{M}}^{(1,0)}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)-2\omega_{N,q}\text{Re}\bm{\mathcal{M}}^{(1,0,0)}\big(0|\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)\\[3.0pt]
&\hskip 0.0pt+(t^{\prime}-t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n}\mathcal{L}^{(n)}(t^{\prime}-t,q,\Delta)\,,\end{split}

(84)

where we have used
\Theta(0,\Delta)=1/2
and also substituted the derivative of the theta function. The final term is built from the integrals

\mathcal{L}^{(n)}(t^{\prime}-t,q,\Delta)\equiv\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{[\mathcal{E}(q)(t^{\prime}-t)]^{n}}\int_{-(\mathcal{E}(q)-m_{\pi}-m_{N})(t^{\prime}-t)}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!dx\,x^{n}e^{-x}\\
\times\frac{\Theta\big(x,\Delta(t^{\prime}-t)\big)}{\big(1+x/[2\omega_{N,q}(t^{\prime}-t)]\big)^{2}}\bigg[\mathcal{P}\frac{1}{x}\bigg]^{2}\,,

(85)

together with the coefficients
h_{n}
, defined via

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\big(x/(t^{\prime}-t)+\mathcal{E}(q),\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q}\big)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{[\mathcal{E}(q)(t^{\prime}-t)]^{n}}\,.

(86)

Substituting
h_{0}=\text{Im}\,\mathcal{M}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\big)
, demonstrated in appendix
A.5
, then gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}c^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{q}}(t^{\prime},t|\,\omega_{q})_{\sf c}&=\text{Re}\,{\mathcal{M}}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)\Big[\frac{t^{\prime}-t+1/\omega_{N,q}}{2}-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\Big]\\[3.0pt]
&+\text{Im}\,\mathcal{M}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\big)\,(t^{\prime}-t)\,\mathcal{L}^{(0)}(t^{\prime}-t,q,\Delta)\\[7.0pt]
&-\mathcal{E}(q)\text{Re}{\mathcal{M}}^{(1,0)}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)-2\omega_{N,q}\text{Re}\bm{\mathcal{M}}^{(1,0,0)}\big(0|\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q})\big)\\[0.0pt]
&+(t^{\prime}-t)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}\mathcal{L}^{(n)}(t^{\prime}-t,q,\Delta)\,.\end{split}

(87)

The first two lines represent physical information and the final two represent the contaminations that one must remove via fitting.

This completes our presentation of strategies for extracting scattering information from Euclidean correlators, at both threshold and general kinematics. We now turn to discussing how the modified correlation function, defined with a smooth
\Theta
function inserted with the interpolating operators, might be extracted in practice, in a numerical lattice calculation.

4
Implementation strategies

In this section we discuss two possibilities to estimate the modified quantities
c^{\Theta}
and
c^{\Theta,N\pi}
, defined in eqs. (
14
) and (
55
) respectively. We make one adjustment relative to the previous section by replacing the
N\pi\to N\pi
four-point function with the simplified version built only out of pion fields. We denote the latter by
c^{\Theta,\pi\pi}
. With straightforward adjustments of the previous section, in particular setting
\delta m=0
and including the
(1/2)
for identical particles, this can be used to extract the
\pi\pi\to\pi\pi
scattering amplitude.

In the previous sections, we have not addressed the fact that numerical lattice calculations are necessarily confined to a finite space-time volume. In this section we consider the effects of a finite cubic spatial volume, with periodicity
L
in each of the three spatial directions, but continue to neglect the thermal effects of a finite temporal extent. Within this set-up, suppose that one has extracted the matrix element

M_{3}^{i}(t)\equiv\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i},L|\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(t)\widetilde{J}_{\bm{0}}(0)|0\rangle\,,

(88)

from the relevant three-point function. Here the index
i
labels the values of non-equivalent back-to-back momenta. In practice the combined pion state and pion field should be projected to a definite two-pion isospin along the lines of the
N\pi
projections involving the
C_{ab}
coefficients. For the scalar current,
\widetilde{J}_{\bm{0}}(t)=\int d^{3}\bm{x}\,J(x)
, the total isospin must be
0
or
2
to give a non-zero result.

Similarly, we consider the two-point function and also matrix elements from four-point functions:

M_{2}(t)\equiv\langle\widetilde{J}_{\bm{0}}(t)\,\widetilde{J}_{\bm{0}}(0)\rangle\,,\quad M_{4}^{ij}(t)\equiv\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i},L|\,\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(t)\,\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{j}}(0)\,|\pi,\bm{q}_{j},L\rangle\,.

(89)

It is then useful to assemble these quantities into a matrix of correlation functions

M(t)\equiv\begin{pmatrix}M_{2}(t)&M_{3}^{T}(t)\\
M_{3}(t)&M_{4}(t)\\
\end{pmatrix}\,,

(90)

with rank dictated by the number of pion momenta available in the calculation. Following refs.
WilsonGEVP
;
Blossier:2009kd
, from the solution of the generalized eigenvalue problem on this matrix, one can extract a series of finite-volume energies,
E_{n}(L)
, and matrix elements

\displaystyle M_{3}^{i}[n]

\displaystyle=\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i},L|\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(0)|n,L\rangle\,\langle n,L|\widetilde{J}_{\bm{0}}(0)|0\rangle\,,

(91)

\displaystyle M_{4}^{ij}[n]

\displaystyle=\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i},L|\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(0)|n,L\rangle\,\langle n,L|\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{j}}(0)|\pi,\bm{q}_{j},L\rangle\,,

(92)

up to a maximal value of
E_{N}(L)
, depending on the size of the operator basis.

Then the matrix element used in Eq. (
14
) to define the modified quantity
c^{\Theta}_{\bm{q},-\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{q})
can be constructed as

c^{\Theta}_{\bm{q}_{i},-\bm{q}_{i}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})\equiv\frac{2\omega_{q_{i}}e^{\omega_{q_{i}}t_{2}}}{\sqrt{Z_{\pi}}}\times\\[-5.0pt]
\bigg[\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i},L|\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(t_{2})\widetilde{J}_{\bm{0}}(0)|0\rangle-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Theta\big(2\omega_{q_{i}}-E_{n}(L),\Delta\big)\,M_{3}^{i}[n]\,e^{-(E_{n}(L)-\omega_{q_{i}})t_{2}}\bigg]\,,

(93)

provided that the given value of
2\omega_{q_{i}}
is smaller than
E_{N}(L)
to avoid systematic errors due to the truncated sum.

The additional effort in measuring the various correlation functions required to construct
M(t)
is compensated by the fact that we can study several kinematic points for the
2\to 1
transitions, together with the
2\to 2
amplitudes

\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i}|\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(0)\,\Theta(\hat{M}-2\omega_{q_{i}},\Delta)e^{-(\hat{H}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{i}})t}\,\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{j}}(0)|\pi,\bm{q}_{j}\rangle=\\
\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i}|\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(t)\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{j}}(0)|\pi,\bm{q}_{j}\rangle-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Theta\big(2\omega_{q_{i}}-E_{n}(L),\Delta\big)\,M_{4}^{ij}[n]\,e^{-(E_{n}(L)-\omega_{q_{i}})t}\,.

(94)

Note that this procedure in fact relies on finite-
L
, in order to ensure that a finite (and manageable) number of states appear in the regime where
\Theta\big(2\omega_{q_{i}}-E_{n}(L),\Delta\big)
has support. Once the re-construction is achieved, the resulting
c^{\Theta}
and
c^{\Theta,\pi\pi}
are expected to have residual volume effects, scaling as
\mathcal{O}(e^{-L\Delta})
. Thus, exactly as with the methods described in refs.
Hansen:2017mnd
;
Bulava:2019kbi
, it is important that
L\Delta
is sufficiently large that these can be removed or included in the systematic uncertainty. Future work, perhaps along the lines of refs.
Hansen:2019rbh
;
Hansen:2020whp
is needed to fully analyze the finite-
L
corrections to these correlators as a function of the
\Theta
width as well as the time and energy coordinates.

Alternatively, for situations where an exclusive study is not possible, e.g. for higher center-of-mass energies where a variational approach would be impractical, approximate solutions to the inverse problem represent a viable alternative. The approach is to define coefficients,
w^{\Theta}(t,\Delta,\omega_{q_{i}}|t^{\prime})
, satisfying

\sum_{t^{\prime}}\,w^{\Theta}(t,\Delta,\omega_{q_{i}}|t^{\prime})\,e^{-(\mathcal{E}-\omega_{q_{i}})t^{\prime}}=\Theta(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{q_{i}},\Delta)\,e^{-(\mathcal{E}-\omega_{q_{i}})t}\,,

(95)

where the kernels
w
are found numerically, for each value of
t
,
\Delta
and
\omega_{q_{i}}
, using Backus-Gilbert-like methods
Backus
;
Hansen:2019idp
, or Chebyshev polynomials
Bailas:2020qmv
. These then satisfy

\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i}|\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(0)\,\Theta(\hat{M}-2\omega_{q_{i}},\Delta)e^{-(\hat{H}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{i}})t}\,\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{j}}(0)|\pi,\bm{q}_{j}\rangle=\\[3.0pt]
\sum_{t^{\prime}}\,w^{\Theta}(t,\Delta,\omega_{q_{i}}|t^{\prime})\,\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{i}|\widetilde{\pi}_{-\bm{q}_{i}}(t^{\prime})\widetilde{\pi}_{\bm{q}_{j}}(0)|\pi,\bm{q}_{j}\rangle\,.

(96)

While a large
\Delta
improves the stability of the numerical solutions, the change of basis from
e^{-Et}
to
\Theta(E-2\omega_{q_{i}},\Delta)e^{-Et}
may also simplify the complexity of the inverse problem (compared to a target Gaussian or Breit-Wigner peak, for example) thereby potentially reducing systematic errors.

Finally, we note that it may also be useful to construct the coefficients to reproduce
1-\Theta(\cdots)
instead, i.e. the reflected
\Theta
-function that decreases with increasing
\mathcal{E}
. The resulting estimator can then be subtracted from the original correlator to reach the desired quantity. This seems like a trivial adjustment as the two sets of coefficients should formally be related as

w^{\Theta}(t,\Delta,\omega_{q_{i}}|t^{\prime})=\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}-w^{1-\Theta}(t,\Delta,\omega_{q_{i}}|t^{\prime})\,.

(97)

In practice, however, the algorithms used to determine the coefficients depend non-trivially on the target functions and
w^{1-\Theta}
may give a better overall estimate.

5
Summary and outlook

In this article we have considered extensions of the work of Maiani and Testa
Maiani:1990ca
. These divide into two main categories:
(i)
generalizing the results for threshold correlators and
(ii)
proposing modified correlators that allow one to extend the reach of scattering extractions above threshold.

In the case of threshold kinematics, the key results are presented in sections
2.1
and
3.1
. In the former we review the analysis of the
\pi\pi J
three-point function (with
J
a scalar current and
\pi
a single-particle interpolator) and consider an alternative expansion in kinematic functions that replaces the original expansion of ref.
Maiani:1990ca
, in powers of inverse time (
1/t
), and appears to exhibit better convergence at small to moderate
t
values. We also give explicit expressions for the leading off-shellness contaminating the correlator. In section
3.1
, we extend these considerations to four-point functions, focusing on
N\pi\to N\pi
. This gives a tool to extract the
N\pi
scattering length,
a_{N\pi}
, from a non-standard fit to the Euclidean time dependence, with terms scaling as
a_{N\pi}t
and
a_{N\pi}^{2}\sqrt{t}
; see also eq. (
10
) in the introduction.

The second class of extensions, targeting scattering information away from threshold, are detailed in sections
2.2
and
3.2
, again with a focus on three- and four-point functions, respectively. The key idea is to consider a modified correlator, in which a smooth step function, denoted by
\Theta(\hat{M}-M_{0},\Delta)
, is inserted with the operators. Here
\Delta
denotes the width of the step,
\hat{M}
is the hamiltonian boosted to zero-momentum, and
M_{0}
a free parameter. This modification effectively shifts the threshold to higher energies, so that the Maiani-Testa approach can be used to extract the form factor and scattering amplitude for general kinematics. As we stress in the main text, this method is analogous to those based in reconstructing the spectral function
Hansen:2017mnd
;
Bulava:2019kbi
. The key distinction here is that the target energy range is isolated by the combined effect of the
\Theta
-function and the Euclidean-time translation operator,
e^{-\hat{H}t}
. In particular, the latter naturally provides the damping of contributions above the target energy, see also figure
1
. This is formalized by the large time expansions presented in sections
2.2
and
3.2
. At leading order these give the real and imaginary parts of the physical observables, separated by coefficients with different time dependencies.

In section
4
we briefly describe the implementation strategies for extracting the
\Theta
-modified correlator from numerical lattice calculations. We outline two methods there, one based on the generalized eigenvalue problem (GEVP) and the other on reconstruction methods such as that due to Backus and Gilbert
Backus
. In both cases, this approach may have an advantage over the other methods because only the low-energy modes of the correlator need to be modified. In particular, the contamination of higher energies is encoded in the large
t
expansion of the correlation function and therefore profits from knowledge of the functional form (ultimately arising from Lorentz invariance and the form of the time-translation operator and threshold singularities). This information is not directly used, for example, in refs.
Hansen:2017mnd
;
Bulava:2019kbi
. In section
4
we also briefly discuss the role of finite volume, and the importance of
\Delta
in suppressing volume effects.

Looking forward, the next steps are to test these methods in a lattice calculation. The most likely initial applications might include extracting scattering lengths from standard three- and four-point functions, especially on lattice ensembles where this information is available from a finite-volume analysis and can provide a direct comparison. This can be used to test, for example, whether one can achieve a more precise determination due to the fact that the observable appears at leading order in the matrix element, rather than as a shift to the energies. This will set the stage for the more ambitious implementation away from threshold, with ultimate target observables including electroweak decay amplitudes, QED corrections to hadronic matrix elements and long-range matrix elements. This will require some extensions of the formal results presented here, but these are expected to be relatively straightforward, along the lines of ref.
Bulava:2019kbi
.

Matrix element decomposition

In this section we prove eq. (

A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})

(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(q_{1}+q_{2}-P)A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})=\frac{2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}}{\sqrt{Z_{\pi}}}\int d^{4}x\,e^{iq_{2}^{0}x^{0}-i\bm{q}_{2}\cdot\bm{x}}\,\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{1}|\pi(x)|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle\,,

Next we decompose the matrix element on the right-hand side into disconnected and connected components to reach

(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(q_{1}+q_{2}-P)A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})=\\[5.0pt]
\hskip 80.0pt\delta_{k,1}(2\pi)^{7}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\big[\delta^{3}(\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{q}_{1})\delta^{4}(p_{2}-q_{2})+\delta^{3}(\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{q}_{1})\delta^{4}(p_{1}-q_{2})\big]\\[5.0pt]
+(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(q_{1}+q_{2}-P)2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}}{\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})-i\epsilon}\,.

(100)

This result serves to define the off-shell amplitude
\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}
as the connected component of the matrix element with a simple pole factor removed. The latter is defined using

\displaystyle\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})

\displaystyle=(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}})^{2}-(\bm{P}-\bm{q}_{1})^{2}-m_{\pi}^{2}\,.

(101)

Note that the self-energy contributions from the external leg are absorbed into the off-shell definition.

From the Lehmann-Symanzik-Zimmermann (LSZ) reduction formula then immediately follows

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)={\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)\,,

(102)

where the right-hand side here denotes the
2\to n_{k}
scattering amplitude, a function of
3n_{k}-4
Lorentz invariants arranged in the vector
\bm{\mu}
.

We conclude by cancelling the common delta function in eq. (
100
) to reach

A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})=\delta_{k,1}(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\big[\delta^{3}(\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{q}_{1})+\delta^{3}(\bm{p}_{2}-\bm{q}_{1})\big]-2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}}{\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})-i\epsilon}\bigg|_{q_{1}+q_{2}=P}\,,

(103)

matching eq. (
30
) of the main text.

A.2
General kinematics in the three-point function

In this section we extend our expressions for the three-point function, presented in section
2
, to arbitrary kinematics. We begin by rewriting eq. (
23
) as

c^{\Theta}_{\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})=\sum_{k}\int\!d\Phi^{\star}_{k}\,e^{-(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})t_{2}}\,\Theta\big(s(\bm{p}^{\star})^{1/2}-2\omega_{0},\Delta\big)\,A^{\star}_{k}(\bm{q}_{1}^{\star},\bm{q}_{2}^{\star};\bm{p}^{\star})B^{\star}_{k}(\bm{p}^{\star})\,,

where the

\bm{\beta}=-(\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2})/(\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}+\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})

\Lambda(\bm{\beta})

{\Lambda(\bm{\beta})^{\mu}}_{\nu}\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}+\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\\
\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\big(\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}+\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\big)^{2}-\big(\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2}\big)^{2}}\\
\bm{0}\end{pmatrix}^{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}^{\star}+\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}^{\star}\\
\bm{0}\end{pmatrix}^{\mu}\,.

(105)

We then define

{\Lambda(\bm{\beta})^{\mu}}_{\nu}\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{\bm{q}_{i}}\\
\bm{q}_{i}\end{pmatrix}^{\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{\bm{q}_{i}}^{\star}\\
\bm{q}_{i}^{\star}\end{pmatrix}^{\mu}\,,\qquad\qquad{\Lambda(\bm{\beta})^{\mu}}_{\nu}\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{\bm{p}_{i}}\\
\bm{p}_{i}\end{pmatrix}^{\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{\bm{p}_{i}}^{\star}\\
\bm{p}_{i}^{\star}\end{pmatrix}^{\mu}\,,

(106)

which in turn allows us to introduce

\displaystyle\begin{split}A^{\star}_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})&=\delta_{k,1}(2\pi)^{3}2\omega^{\star}_{\bm{q}_{1}}2\omega^{\star}_{\bm{q}_{2}}\big[\delta^{3}(\bm{p}^{\star}_{1}-\bm{q}^{\star}_{1})+\delta^{3}(\bm{p}^{\star}_{2}-\bm{q}^{\star}_{1})\big]\\

(107)

\displaystyle B^{\star}_{k}(\bm{p}^{\star})

\displaystyle=B_{k}(\bm{p})\,.

(108)

Then eq. (
104
) holds because
d\Phi_{k}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}
,
(2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})^{-1}A_{k}
and
B_{k}
are each Lorentz scalars.

It remains only to rewrite the
t_{2}
dependence in the exponential. To do so we substitute

(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})t_{2}=(P-q_{1}-q_{2})\cdot x_{2}=(E^{\star}(\bm{p})-\omega^{\star}_{\bm{q}_{1}}-\omega^{\star}_{\bm{q}_{2}})t^{\star}_{2}\,,

(109)

where we have introduced the four-vector
x_{2}^{\mu}=(t_{2},\bm{0})
. The final line holds because
\bm{q}_{1}^{\star}+\bm{q}_{2}^{\star}=\bm{0}
and the Delta function inside
d\Phi_{k}
then sets
\bm{P}^{\star}=\bm{0}
.

Thus, we conclude that eqs. (
32
), (
39
), (
41
), (

), (

), (

), (
53
), (
54
) hold, also for

provided the following replacements are made

\displaystyle\bm{q}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\bm{q}_{1}^{\star}\,,

(110)

\displaystyle t_{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow t_{2}^{\star}=\gamma t_{2}\,,

(111)

where

\gamma=\frac{\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}+\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}}{\sqrt{\big(\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}+\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\big)^{2}-\big(\bm{q}_{1}+\bm{q}_{2}\big)^{2}}}\,.

(112)

A.3
Optical theorem for the three-point function

In this section we demonstrate eq. (
38
) of the main text. Beginning with the definition of
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}/2)

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}/2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int d\Phi_{k}\,2\pi\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-E(\bm{p})\big)\,{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}B_{k}(\bm{p})\,,

(113)

\displaystyle\hskip-30.0pt=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{S_{k}}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{p}_{1}}}\cdots\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{n_{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{p}_{n_{k}}}}\,(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{3}(\bm{P})\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-E(\bm{p})\big)\,{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}B_{k}(\bm{p})\,,

(114)

we first apply the trick of ref.
Maiani:1990ca
to write the scattering amplitude as a difference of inner-products

-i(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{3}(\bm{P})\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-E(\bm{p})\big)\,{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}\\
=\langle\pi\pi,\text{in},\bm{q},-\bm{q}|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle-\langle\pi\pi,\text{out},\bm{q},-\bm{q}|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle\,.

(115)

Inserting this into eq. (
114
) then gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}/2)&=i\sum_{k}\frac{1}{S_{k}}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{p}_{1}}}\cdots\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{n_{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{p}_{n_{k}}}}\,\langle k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}|J(0)|0\rangle\\
&\hskip 10.0pt\times\Big[\langle\pi\pi,\text{in},\bm{q},-\bm{q}|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle-\langle\pi\pi,\text{out},\bm{q},-\bm{q}|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle\Big]\,.\end{split}

(116)

Finally we identify the combination of the integrals and the outer-product as an insertion of the identity on the Hilbert space

\mathbb{I}=\sum_{k}\frac{1}{S_{k}}\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{p}_{1}}}\cdots\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{n_{k}}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{p}_{n_{k}}}}\,|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle\langle k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}|\,,

(117)

such that eq. (
116
) reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}/2)

\displaystyle=i\Big[\langle\pi\pi,\text{in},\bm{q},-\bm{q}|J(0)|0\rangle-\langle\pi\pi,\text{out},\bm{q},-\bm{q}|J(0)|0\rangle\Big]\,,

(118)

\displaystyle=2\,\text{Im}\,\langle\pi\pi,\text{out},\bm{q},-\bm{q}|J(0)|0\rangle\,,

(119)

thereby completing the proof.

A.4
Cancellation of imaginary parts in the three-point function

In this section we review the result of ref.
Maiani:1990ca
, that the imaginary parts cancel between the first and second terms of eq. (
32
), which we repeat here for convenience

\displaystyle c^{\Theta}_{\bm{q},-\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})

\displaystyle=\Theta(2\omega_{q}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,f\big(s(q^{2})\big)+\Xi_{\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})\,,

(120)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv-2\omega_{q}\int_{2m_{\pi}}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{2\pi}\,e^{-(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{q})t_{2}}\,\Theta(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,\frac{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})}{(\mathcal{E}-\omega_{q})^{2}-\omega_{q}^{2}-i\epsilon}\,.

(121)

As a first step in the proof, we need to demonstrate that
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})
is real, a result that we have shown in the previous section for
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}/2)
and now extend for all values of the arguments. Beginning with the definition

\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})=\sum_{k}\int d\Phi_{k}\,2\pi\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-E(\bm{p})\big)\,\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}B_{k}(\bm{p})\,.

(122)

We then substitute

-i(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{3}(\bm{P})\delta\big(\mathcal{E}-E(\bm{p})\big)\,\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}\\
=\langle\pi,\bm{q};\tilde{\pi}(q),\text{in}|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle-\langle\pi,\bm{q};\tilde{\pi}(q),\text{out}|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle\,,

(123)

where we have introduced the shorthand

\langle\pi,\bm{q};\tilde{\pi}(q),\text{in}|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle\equiv\\
i\frac{[\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})-i\epsilon]}{\sqrt{Z_{\pi}}}\int d^{4}x\,e^{iq^{0}x^{0}-i(-\bm{q})\cdot\bm{x}}\langle\pi,\bm{q}|\pi(x)|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle\,.

(124)

This notation then allows us to directly imitate the derivation above to conclude

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})

\displaystyle=i\Big[\langle\pi,\bm{q};\tilde{\pi}(-q),\text{in}|J(0)|0\rangle-\langle\pi,\bm{q};\tilde{\pi}(-q),\text{out}|J(0)|0\rangle\Big]\,,

(125)

\displaystyle=2\,\text{Im}\,\langle\pi,\bm{q};\tilde{\pi}(-q),\text{out}|J(0)|0\rangle\,,

(126)

from which directly follows
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})\in\mathbb{R}
.

Returning to eq. (
120
), we now evaluate the imaginary part of
\Xi_{\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})
to reach

\displaystyle\text{Im}\,\Xi_{\bm{q}}(t_{2}|\omega_{0})

\displaystyle=-2\omega_{q}\text{Im}\int_{2m_{\pi}}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{2\pi}\,e^{-(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{q})t_{2}}\,\Theta(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,\frac{\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})}{(\mathcal{E}-\omega_{q})^{2}-\omega_{q}^{2}-i\epsilon}\,,

(127)

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt=-2\pi\omega_{q}\int_{2m_{\pi}}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{2\pi}\,e^{-(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{q})t_{2}}\,\Theta(\mathcal{E}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{E},\omega_{q})\delta\Big((\mathcal{E}-\omega_{q})^{2}-\omega_{q}^{2}\Big)\,,

(128)

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt=-\Theta(2\omega_{q}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,\text{Im}f\big(s(q^{2})\big)\,.

(129)

Thus, the imaginary part of
\Xi
cancels the imaginary part of
\Theta(2\omega_{q}-2\omega_{0},\Delta)\,f\big(s(q^{2})\big)
, as claimed.

A.5
Decomposition of the four-point function

In this section, we demonstrate eq. (
61
) of the main text. We begin by recalling the basic definitions

c^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})\equiv\sum_{k}\int\!d\Phi_{k}\,e^{-(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}})t^{\prime}}\,e^{+(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})t}\\[-5.0pt]
\times\Theta\big(s(\bm{p})^{1/2}-M_{0},\Delta\big)\,A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime},\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime};\bm{p})A^{*}_{k}(\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2};\bm{p})\,,

(130)

where
\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}=\sqrt{m_{\pi}^{2}+\bm{q}_{1}^{2}}
,
\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}=\sqrt{m_{N}^{2}+\bm{q}_{2}^{2}}
, and

\displaystyle A_{k}(\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime},\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime};\bm{p})

\displaystyle=\delta_{k,1}(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}\delta^{3}(\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime})-2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}}{\eta(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})-i\epsilon}\,.

(131)

The product of
A_{k}
and
A_{k}^{*}
generates four terms, schematically represented by

\Big(\delta+\frac{1}{\eta+i\epsilon}\Big)\Big(\delta+\frac{1}{\eta-i\epsilon}\Big)=\delta^{2}+\delta\frac{1}{\eta-i\epsilon}+\frac{1}{\eta+i\epsilon}\delta+\frac{1}{\eta+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{\eta-i\epsilon}\,.

(132)

The doubly disconnected term is discarded and, for the single delta function term, the integral is removed so that one can send
\epsilon\to 0
to recover

\Big(\delta+\frac{1}{\eta+i\epsilon}\Big)\Big(\delta+\frac{1}{\eta-i\epsilon}\Big)\ \longrightarrow\ \delta\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\eta}\delta+\frac{1}{\eta+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{\eta-i\epsilon}\,.

(133)

As the final term is inside the integral, the
i\epsilon
must be treated carefully. The first step is to break each pole into real and imaginary parts

\delta\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\eta}\delta+\frac{1}{\eta+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{\eta-i\epsilon}=\delta\frac{1}{\eta}+\frac{1}{\eta}\delta+\bigg[\text{Re}\frac{1}{\eta+i\epsilon}+i\text{Im}\frac{1}{\eta+i\epsilon}\bigg]\bigg[\text{Re}\frac{1}{\eta-i\epsilon}+i\text{Im}\frac{1}{\eta-i\epsilon}\bigg]\,.

(134)

In the analysis below we will consider
q\neq q^{\prime}
and only take the limit
q\to q^{\prime}
on combinations of terms for which we know this is safe. Indeed, for many of the individual terms here the limit does not exist.

After multiplying out the product of binomials on the right-hand side, one has a total of six contributions to
c^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}
. We find it most instructive to introduce some additional notation for this decomposition

c^{\Theta,N\pi}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})_{\sf c}=\sum_{\bm{n}=\bm{1}}^{\bm{6}}c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{n}]}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}(t^{\prime},t|\,M_{0})\,,

(135)

where schematically the correspondence is given by

\bigg\{[\bm{1}]=\delta\frac{1}{\eta},\ \ [\bm{2}]=\frac{1}{\eta}\delta,\ \ [\bm{3}]=\text{Im}\times\text{Re},\ \ [\bm{4}]=\text{Re}\times\text{Im},\ \ [\bm{5}]=\text{Im}\times\text{Im},\ \ [\bm{6}]=\text{Re}\times\text{Re}\bigg\}\,.

(136)

For example,
c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{1}]}
is given explicitly by

\begin{split}c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{1}]}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}&\equiv-\int\!\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{p}_{1}}2\omega_{\bm{p}_{2}}}\,e^{-(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}})t^{\prime}}e^{+(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})t}\\[-5.0pt]
&\hskip 0.0pt\times\Theta\big(s(\bm{p})^{1/2}-M_{0},\Delta\big)\,(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}\delta^{3}(\bm{p}_{1}-\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime})\,2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)}{\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})+i\epsilon}\,,\end{split}

(137)

where the
\delta_{k,1}
projects us into a single two-particle channel and we drop the
k=1
subscript. Using the Dirac delta function to evaluate the remaining integrals yields

c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{1}]}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}=-e^{+(\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}+\omega_{\bm{q}^{\prime}_{2}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})t}\Theta\big(s(\bm{q}^{\prime})^{1/2}-M_{0},\Delta\big)2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\big)}{\eta(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})}\,,

(138)

or, in the center-of-mass frame with
\bm{q}_{1}=-\bm{q}_{2}=\bm{q}
,

c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{1}]}_{\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q}}=-e^{(\mathcal{E}(q^{\prime})-\mathcal{E}(q))t}\,\Theta\big(\mathcal{E}(q^{\prime})-M_{0},\Delta\big)\,2\omega_{N,q}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}\big(\eta(q,q^{\prime})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\big)}{\eta(q,q^{\prime})}\,.

(139)

This matches the second line of eq. (
61
), except for the fact that here we have the complex valued
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
rather than only its real part. This is because
c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{3}]}
generates the exact cancellation of the imaginary part, as we now show.

For general kinematics,
c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{3}]}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{3}]}_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}\bm{q}_{1}\bm{q}_{2}}&\equiv i\pi\sum_{k}\int\!d\Phi_{k}\,e^{-(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}^{\prime}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}})t^{\prime}}e^{+(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\bm{q}_{1}}-\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}})t}\,\Theta\big(s(\bm{p})^{1/2}-M_{0},\Delta\big)\\[-5.0pt]
&\hskip 40.0pt\times\,2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}^{\prime}}\delta\big(\eta(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})\big)\mathcal{M}_{k}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})\big)2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\mathcal{P}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})|\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}}{\eta(\bm{q},\bm{p})}\,,\end{split}

(140)

and, in the case of back-to-back momenta, this reduces to

\displaystyle\begin{split}c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{3}]}_{\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q}}&=i\pi\sum_{k}\int\!d\Phi_{k}\,e^{-(E(\bm{p})-\mathcal{E}(q^{\prime}))t^{\prime}}e^{+(E(\bm{p})-\mathcal{E}(q))t}\,\Theta\big(\mathcal{E}(q^{\prime})-M_{0},\Delta\big)\\[-10.0pt]
&\hskip 60.0pt\times\,\delta\big(E(\bm{p})-\mathcal{E}(q^{\prime})\big)\mathcal{M}_{k}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})\big)2\omega_{N,q}\mathcal{P}\frac{\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(q,p)|\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}}{\eta(q,p)}\,,\end{split}

(141)

\displaystyle=\frac{i2\omega_{N,q}\,\Theta\big(\mathcal{E}(q^{\prime})-M_{0},\Delta\big)\,e^{+(\mathcal{E}(q^{\prime})-\mathcal{E}(q))t}}{\eta(q,q^{\prime})}\,\Xi(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\,,

(142)

where we have used
\eta(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})=(E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\pi,q^{\prime}})-\omega_{N,q^{\prime}}^{2}=[E(\bm{p})-\omega_{\pi,q^{\prime}}+\omega_{N,q^{\prime}}][E(\bm{p})-\mathcal{E}(q^{\prime})]
for the case of back-to-back momenta. In addition we have defined

\Xi(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\equiv\pi\sum_{k}\int\!d\Phi_{k}\,\delta(E(\bm{p})-2\omega_{q^{\prime}})\mathcal{M}_{k}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{p})\big)\bm{\mathcal{M}}_{k}\big(\eta(q,p)|\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{p})\big)^{\!*}\,.

(143)

Next, following the original argument of ref.
Maiani:1990ca
, reviewed in appendix
A.3
, one can show that
\Xi
is just the imaginary part of
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Xi(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})&=-i\pi\sum_{k}\int\!d\Phi_{k}\delta(2\omega_{q^{\prime}}-E(\bm{p}))\,\eta(q,p)\,2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}\frac{\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{1}|\pi(0)|k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}\rangle}{\sqrt{Z_{\pi}}}\\
&\hskip 20.0pt\times\Big[\langle k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}|N\pi,\text{in},\bm{q},-\bm{q}\rangle-\langle k,\text{out},\bm{p}_{1}\cdots\bm{p}_{n_{k}}|N\pi,\text{out},\bm{q},-\bm{q}\rangle\Big]\,,\end{split}

(144)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\frac{\eta(q,p)2\omega_{\bm{q}_{2}}}{\sqrt{Z_{\pi}}}\Big[\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{1}|\pi(0)|N\pi,\text{in},\bm{q},-\bm{q}\rangle-\langle\pi,\bm{q}_{1}|\pi(0)|N\pi,\text{out},\bm{q},-\bm{q}\rangle\Big]\,,

(145)

\displaystyle=\text{Im}\,\bm{\mathcal{M}}\big(\eta(q,q^{\prime})|\,\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\big)\,.

(146)

Note that strictly one should substitute only the connected part of the matrix element for
\bm{\mathcal{M}}
. As the disconnected part is pure real, however, the final line holds as written. Setting
\eta(q,q^{\prime})=0
on both sides of this equation also yields the result

h_{0}=\text{Im}\,\mathcal{M}\big(\bm{\mu}(\bm{q},\bm{q}^{\prime})\big)\,,

(147)

used to reach eq. (
87
) of the main text.

\displaystyle c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{1}]}_{\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q}}+c^{\Theta,N\pi,[\bm{3}]}_{\bm{q}^{\prime},\bm{q}}

Spectral Monic Chebyshev Approximation for Higher Order Differential Equations
M. Abdelhakem, Aya Ahmed, M. El-kady

Abstract
This paper is focused on performing a new method for solving linear and nonlinear higher-order boundary value problems (HBVPs). This direct numerical method based on spectral method. The trial function of this method is the Monic Chebyshev polynomials (MCPs). This method was relying on derivative of MCPs which explicit in the series expansion. The advantage of this method is solved HBVPs without transforming it to a system of lower-order ordinary differential equations (ODEs). This method supported by examples of HBVPs in wide application. The mentioned examples showed that the proposed method is efficient and accurate.



Keywords:
High-order Boundary value problems, Monic Chebyshev polynomial, Spectral method, Galerkin method

Preliminaries

In this section, we shall present the base formula for MCPs and the relation between Chebyshev polynomials and MCPs.
The Chebyshev polynomials of first kind
[
26
]
are denoted by
"T_{n}(z);\quad n=1,2,3,\dots\quad on\quad z\in[-1,1]"
, where

T_{n}(z)=\cos(n\arccos(z))

(1)

The fundamental recurrence relation is:

T_{n}(z)=2zT_{n-1}(z)-T_{n-2}(z);\quad n\geq 2

(2)

where
T_{0}(z)=1,\quad T_{1}(z)=z.

The Chebyshev polynomials
"T_{n}(z)"
can be expanded in power series:

T_{n}(z)=\frac{n}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k}(n-k-1)!(2z)^{n-2k}}{(n-2k)!(k)!}

(3)

where
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
is the integer part of
n/2
.
This explicit representation allows us to derive many useful formulas respective to the Chebyshev polynomials
[
27
]

2T_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{n+1}T^{{}^{\prime}}_{n+1}(z)-\frac{1}{n-1}T^{{}^{\prime}}_{n-1}(z)

(4)

The Chebyshev polynomials form a complete orthogonal set on the interval
[-1,1]
w.r.t. the weighting function
\varpi(z)=(1-z^{2})^{-1/2}

\langle T_{i},T_{j}\rangle=\int_{-1}^{1}T_{i}(z)T_{j}(z)\varpi(z)dz=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&i\neq j\\[5.69046pt]
\frac{\pi}{2},&i=j\neq 0\\[5.69046pt]
\pi,&i=j=0\end{array}\right.

(5)

Consider MCPs of the first kind
[
28
]
denoted by
"Q_{n}(z)\quad;\quad n=0,1,2,....\quad on\quad z\in[-1,1]"
is given by:

Q_{n}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&n=0\\[5.69046pt]
2^{1-n}T_{n}(z),&n\geq 1\end{array}\right.

(6)

Using the previous relation, we have:

Q_{1}(z)=z,\quad Q_{2}(z)=z^{2}-\frac{1}{2}

(7)

The recursive formulae of MCPs as follows

Q_{n}(z)=zQ_{n-1}(z)-\frac{1}{4}Q_{n-2}(z);\quad n\geq 3

(8)

The MC polynomials constitutes an orthogonal basis w.r.t.
\varpi(z)
:

\langle Q_{i},Q_{j}\rangle=\int_{-1}^{1}Q_{i}(z)Q_{j}(z)\varpi(z)dz=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0,&i\neq j\\[5.69046pt]
2^{1-2i}\pi,&i=j\neq 0\\[5.69046pt]
\pi,&i=j=0\end{array}\right.

(9)

3
Monic Chebychev polynomials

In this section, some important relations and properties of the MCPs have presented and introduced. These relations are essential to setup the Monic Chebychev differentiation expansions.
The next lemma introduced the recurrence relation for MCPs. This recurrence relation is different from the recurrence relation Chebychev polynomials. This illustrates MCPs is not a copy of Chebychev polynomials. Also, this deviation leads us the totally different differentiation expansion rather those in Chebychev polynomials.

Lemma 3.1

Let
Q_{n}
, be a MC of degree n, then

Q_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{n+1}Q^{{}^{\prime}}_{n+1}(z)-\frac{1}{4(n-1)}Q^{{}^{\prime}}_{n-1}(z);\quad n\geq 2

Proof.

The proof is straight forward from Eq. (
4
) and Eq. (
6
).

Theorem 3.1

The m-th derivative of MCPs is expanded as follows:

Q^{(m)}_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-m)/2\rfloor}b_{nk}c^{(m)}z^{n-2k-m}

(10)

where

b_{nk}=\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{-2k}n\varGamma(n-k)}{\varGamma(k+1)\varGamma(n-2k+1)}

(11)

c^{(m)}=\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}(n-2k-l),\quad m\geq 1

(12)

Proof.

The following formula is important for proving theorem.
From eq. (
3
), by replacing
z
with
2^{1-n}z
then MCPs
Q_{n}(z)
can be expanded in power series:

Q_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}b_{nk}z^{n-2k},\quad n>0

(13)

where
\lfloor n/2\rfloor
is the integer part of
n/2
and
b_{nk}
as equation (
11
)
The first derivative of the formula (
13
) is the following:

Q^{(1)}_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}(n-2k)b_{nk}z^{n-2k-1},\quad n>0

(14)

Then, using the mathematical induction to prove the theorem:
For
m=1
, we have

Q^{(1)}_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}(n-2k)b_{nk}z^{n-2k-1},\quad n>0

(15)

which leads to the same result as the direct first differentiation.
Now, assume that theorem is satisfied at
m=j

Q^{(j)}_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-j)/2\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{-2k}n\varGamma(n-k)}{\varGamma(k+1)\varGamma(n-2k+1)}z^{n-2k-j},

(16)

For
m=j+1
, we have

\displaystyle Q^{(j+1)}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}Q^{(j)}_{n}(z)=\frac{d}{dz}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-j)/2\rfloor}\left(\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{-2k}n\varGamma(n-k)}{\varGamma(k+1)\varGamma(n-2k+1)}z^{n-2k-j}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-(j+1)}{2}\rfloor}\Big(\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{-2k}n\varGamma(n-k)}{\varGamma(k+1)\varGamma(n-2k+1)}(n-2k-j)\,c^{(j)}z^{n-2k-j-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-(j+1)}{2}\rfloor}\big(\frac{(-1)^{k}2^{-2k}n\varGamma(n-k)}{\varGamma(k+1)\varGamma(n-2k+1)}(n-2k-j)\,c^{(j+1)}z^{n-2k-j-1}\big)

Hence the theorem is satisfied for every positive integer m.

Proposed method

For solving the problems in the next section, we need to calculate the
y(z_{i})
such that
z_{i}
is Chebyshev Gauss Lobatto points where
z_{i}=\cos(\pi i/N);\quad 0\leq i\leq N

Consider the linear HBVPs:

\displaystyle f_{m}(z)y^{(m)}(z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f_{m-1}(z)y^{(m-1)}(z)+f_{m-2}(z)y^{(m-2)}(z)

(18)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\dots+f_{0}(z)y(z)=g(z);a\leq x\leq b

Subject to the boundary conditions:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}y(a)=\alpha_{0},\quad y(b)=\beta_{0},\\[5.69046pt]
y^{(1)}(a)=\alpha_{1},\quad y^{(1)}(b)=\beta_{1},\\[5.69046pt]
\vdots\\[5.69046pt]
y^{(q)}(a)=\alpha_{q},\quad y^{(q)}(b)=\beta_{q}.\end{array}\right.

(19)

such that
f(z)
and
g(z)
is a function of
z
or constant,
\alpha_{0}
and
\beta_{0}
are constants.
The number of boundary conditions is equal to the order of the problem.
Consider the non-linear HBVPs as:

y^{(m)}_{n}=g(z,y,y^{{}^{\prime}});\quad a\leq x\leq b

(20)

Such that
g(z,y,y^{{}^{\prime}})
is a non-linear function. For examples,
e^{y^{(m)}(z)}
and
(y^{(m)}(z))^{n},n>1\quad etc\dots
,
Subject to the boundary conditions as in (
19
).
Furthermore, by approximating the unknown function
y(z_{i})
as

y(z_{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q_{i}(z_{j}),\quad z_{j}=\cos(\frac{\pi j}{N});0\leq j\leq N

(21)

Where
A_{i}
denotes constants. Realizing that
Q_{i}(z_{j})
is square matrix. i.e. the elements of this matrix are the values of Monic Chebyshev polynomials at
z_{j}

The derivatives can be expressed in differential equation as the following form:

\frac{d^{m}y}{dz^{m}}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q_{i}(z)

(22)

Such that m is the order of the derivative. At m=0, the form (
22
) become equivalent to (
21
).
Substituting from (
22
) into (
18
) yields:

\displaystyle f_{m}\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(m)}_{i}(z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f_{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(m-1)}_{i}(z)

(23)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f_{m-2}\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(m-2)}_{i}(z)+\dots

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f_{0}\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q_{i}(z)=g(z);\quad a\leq x\leq b

By shifting the interval
[a,b]
such that,
z
will be belongs to the interval from -1 to 1 and shifting the boundary conditions (20) as the following:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q_{i}(-1)=\alpha_{0},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q_{i}(1)=\beta_{0},\\[5.69046pt]
\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(1)}_{i}(-1)=\alpha_{1},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(1)}_{i}(1)=\beta_{1}\\[5.69046pt]
\vdots\\[5.69046pt]
\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(q)}_{i}(-1)=\alpha_{q},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(q)}_{i}(1)=\beta_{q}.\end{array}\right.

(24)

Substituting (
21
), (
22
) in (
18
)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(m)}_{i}(z_{j})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f_{m-1}\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(m-1)}_{i}(z_{j})

(25)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f_{m-2}\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q^{(m-2)}_{i}(z_{j})+\dots

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f_{0}\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i}Q_{i}(z_{j})=g(z_{j});\quad 0\leq j\leq N

then equations (
25
) construct a system of algebraic equations. Particularly, linear or non-linear system of equations depend on formulation of HBVPs ”(
18
) and (
20
)” of the problem. Hint, we shall be replacing some rows of
D_{ij}
by the conditions (
24
). In case of the non-linearity, the non-linear algebraic system may be treated by numerous methods like newton method, secant method and SOR- Steffensen-Newton method for finding solution of systems of nonlinear equations
[
29
]
.
A_{i}
can be approximated by solving the previous system. Finally we can get the approximate solution expanded depend on MCPs.

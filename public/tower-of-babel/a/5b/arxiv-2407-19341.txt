Bollobás - Nikiforov Conjecture for graphs with not so many triangles
Hitesh Kumar, Shivaramakrishna Pragada

Abstract
Bollobás and Nikiforov [ 1 ] conjectured that for any graph G ≠ K n G\neq K_{n} with m m edges λ 1 2 + λ 2 2 ≤ ( 1 − 1 ω ⁡ ( G ) ) ​ 2 ​ m \lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}\leq\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\omega(G)}\bigg)2m where λ 1 \lambda_{1} and λ 2 \lambda_{2} denote the two largest eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix A ⁡ ( G ) A(G) , and ω ⁡ ( G ) \omega(G) denotes the clique number of G G . This conjecture was recently verified for triangle-free graphs [ 4 ] and regular graphs [ 8 ] . In [ 2 ] , a generalization of this conjecture was proposed. In this note, we verify this generalized conjecture for the family of graphs on m m edges, which contain at most O ⁡ ( m 1.5 − ε ) O(m^{1.5-\varepsilon}) triangles for some ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 . In particular, we show that the conjecture is true for planar graphs, book-free graphs and cycle-free graphs.

Introduction

For a simple graph
G
on
n
vertices and
m
edges, let
A(G)
denote its
adjacency matrix
. We denote the
eigenvalues
of
A(G)
by

\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n-1}\geq\lambda_{n}.

Let
(n^{+},n^{0},n^{-})
denote the
inertia
of
G
, i.e.
G
has
n^{+}
positive eigenvalues, eigenvalue
0
with multiplicity
n^{0}
, and
n^{-}
negative eigenvalues. We denote the
clique number
of
G
by
\omega(G)
. Let
t(G)
denote the number of triangles in
G
. Let
s_{k}(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}^{2}
, i.e.
s_{k}(G)
is the square sum of first
k
eigenvalues of
G
. For a graph
G
define

\Lambda_{k}(G)=\frac{s_{k}(G)}{m}.

In 2002, Nikiforov
[
6
]
showed the following result, which implies the classical Turán’s Theorem on cliques.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every graph
G
, we have

\Lambda_{1}(G)\leq 2\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\omega(G)}\bigg).

Later, in 2007, Bollobás and Nikiforov
[
1
]
proposed that the following stronger conjecture might be true.

Conjecture 1.2

(Bollobás-Nikiforov Conjecture)
.

For every graph
G\neq K_{n}
, we have

\Lambda_{2}(G)\leq 2\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\omega(G)}\bigg).

The above conjecture has been verified for triangle-free graphs by Lin, Ning and Wu
[
4
]
and for regular graphs by Zhang
[
8
]
. Elphick, Linz and Wocjan
[
2
]
, after some computational investigation, suggested that the following generalization might be true.

Conjecture 1.3
.

For every graph
G
,

\Lambda_{\ell}(G)\leq 2\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\omega(G)}\bigg)

where
\ell=\min\{n^{+},\omega(G)\}
.

Elphick, Linz and Wocjan
[
2
]
verified the conjecture for some graph families, including weakly perfect graphs and Kneser graphs. Also, see the recent survey by Liu and Ning
[
5
]
for progress on these and related conjectures.

In this note, we verify the above conjectures in a strong way for graphs with not so many triangles. To make our claims precise, we introduce some notation. For positive constants
\varepsilon
and
c
let
\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon,c)
denote the family of graphs
G
with
t(G)\leq cm^{1.5-\varepsilon}
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}(\varepsilon,c)
denote the subfamily of graphs in
\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon,c)
whose clique number equals fixed constant
\omega
. Now, we can state our main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\omega\geq 3
. Let
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}(\varepsilon,c)
with
m
edges and let
k\geq 1
. If
m\geq\big(2.2c\omega^{2k}\big)^{1/\varepsilon}
then

\Lambda_{\ell}(G)<2\bigg(\frac{\sqrt[3]{\omega}}{1+\sqrt[3]{\omega}}+\frac{1}{\omega^{k}}\bigg)

where
\ell=\min\{n^{+},\omega\}
.

Note that taking
k=3
in the above theorem implies Conjecture
1.3
for graphs in
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}(\varepsilon,c)
with sufficiently many edges. We emphasize that our bound outperforms the conjectured bound for any fixed
\omega
and large
m
. In other words, there is no hope for equality in Conjecture
1.3
for graphs with not so many triangles.

Since there are many interesting graph classes with not so many triangles, our result expands the set of all known graph classes for which Conjecture
1.3
is true. In particular, it is true for planar graphs,
B_{k}
-free graphs, and
C_{k}
-free graphs where
B_{k}
denotes the book on
k+2
vertices and
C_{k}
denotes the
k
-cycle. Recall that a graph
G
is said to be
H
-free if
G
does not contain
H
as a subgraph.

In this note, we also observe that the bound in the Bollobás-Nikiforov Conjecture can be improved for graphs with not so many triangles. The upper bound in Conjecture

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon,c)
. Then

\Lambda_{2}(G)\leq 1+o(1).

In conclusion, our results suggest that the bottleneck in proving Conjectures
1.2
and
1.3
is when the graph
G
has many triangles, i.e. when
t(G)=\Omega(m^{1.5}).

2
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We first recall a consequence of Holder’s inequality. For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq p<\infty
, define
\|x\|_{p}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x
be a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq p\leq q
. Then

||x||_{q}\leq||x||_{p}\leq n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}||x||_{q}.

Using the above lemma, we estimate the number of triangles in a graph in terms of its eigenvalues.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
t(G)
denote the number of triangles in a graph
G
. Let
s_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}^{2}
. Then,

6t(G)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\big(s_{k}\big)^{3/2}-\big(2m-s_{k}\big)^{3/2},

for all
1\leq k\leq n^{+}
.

Proof.

Consider the vectors
x=(\lambda_{1}^{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k}^{2})
and
y=(\lambda_{n}^{2},\lambda_{n-1}^{2},\dots,\lambda^{2}_{n-n^{-}+1})
. By Lemma
2.1
with
p=2
and
q=3
, we obtain that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}^{3}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\big(s_{k}\big)^{3/2},

and

\sum_{i=n-n^{-}+1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|^{3}\leq\bigg(\sum_{i=n-n^{-}+1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}\bigg)^{3/2}\leq\big(2m-s_{k}\big)^{3/2}.

It follows that

6t(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{3}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}^{3}-\sum_{i=n-n^{-}+1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|^{3}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\big(s_{k}\big)^{3/2}-\big(2m-s_{k}\big)^{3/2}.\qed

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
. Our proof is via contradiction. It relies on the fact that if the square sum of eigenvalues is too large, then the number of triangles in the graph has to be
\Omega(m^{1.5})
, contrary to what we assumed.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\delta=\dfrac{\Lambda_{\ell}}{2}
. Then, by Lemma
2.2
,

\displaystyle 6cm^{1.5-\varepsilon}\geq 6t(G)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}\big(s_{\ell}\big)^{3/2}-\big(2m-s_{\ell}\big)^{3/2}\geq(2m)^{3/2}\bigg(\frac{\delta^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\omega}}-\big(1-\delta\big)^{3/2}\bigg),

since
\ell\leq\omega
. Suppose that Theorem
1.4
does not hold. Then
\delta\geq\big(\frac{\sqrt[3]{\omega}}{1+\sqrt[3]{\omega}}+\frac{1}{\omega^{k}}\big)
, and we have

\displaystyle 6cm^{1.5-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq(2m)^{3/2}\bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega}}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt[3]{\omega}}{1+\sqrt[3]{\omega}}+\frac{1}{\omega^{k}}\bigg)^{3/2}-\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\sqrt[3]{\omega}}-\frac{1}{\omega^{k}}\bigg)^{3/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle>(2m)^{3/2}\bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega}}\bigg(\frac{\sqrt[3]{\omega}}{1+\sqrt[3]{\omega}}\bigg)^{3/2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega^{k}}\bigg)^{3/2}-\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\sqrt[3]{\omega}}\bigg)^{3/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle>(2m)^{3/2}\frac{1}{\omega^{1.5k+0.5}}.

The above inequalities hold since
\omega\geq 3
and
\omega^{k}\geq\omega\geq\sqrt[3]{\omega}+1
. This gives us
m<(2.2c\omega^{2k})^{1/\varepsilon}
which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

In the proof above, if
\delta>(1-\frac{1}{\omega})
then

\displaystyle 6cm^{1.5-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle>(2m)^{3/2}\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega}}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\omega}\bigg)^{3/2}-\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega}\bigg)^{3/2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2m)^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\omega}}\bigg(\bigg(1-\frac{1}{\omega}\bigg)^{3/2}-\frac{1}{\omega}\bigg)

\displaystyle>\frac{(2m)^{3/2}}{3\sqrt{3}\sqrt{\omega}}\big(2\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{3}\big).

The function
\big(1-\frac{1}{\omega}\big)^{3/2}-\frac{1}{\omega}
is increasing in
\omega
, so the last inequality follows by taking
\omega=3
. This gives us
m<(10.06c\sqrt{\omega})^{1/\varepsilon}
. We conclude that if
G\in\mathcal{G}_{\omega}(\varepsilon,c)
has
m\geq(10.06c\sqrt{\omega})^{1/\varepsilon}
edges then Conjecture
1.3
holds for
G
.

We use the above remark to prove Corollary
1.5
. We require the following lemma, which is not difficult to prove.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every graph
G
,

t(G)=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{v\in V(G)}m(G[N(v)]),

where
m(G[N(v)])
denotes the number of edges in the subgraph induced by
N(v)
.

We finish this section with a proof of Corollary
1.5
.

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

The following claims are well known. We give the proofs for completeness using Lemma
2.3
.

(i)

If
G
is a planar graph then for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, the induced subgraph
G[N(v)]
is outerplanar and has at most
2\deg(v)-3
edges. This gives
t(G)\leq\frac{4m-3n}{3}
and since
m\leq 3n-6
it follows that
t(G)\leq m-2
. As planar graphs are
K_{5}
-free,
\omega(G)\leq 4
.

(ii)

If
G
is an outerplanar graph then for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, the induced subgraph
G[N(v)]
is acyclic and has at most
\deg(v)-1
edges. This gives
t(G)\leq\frac{2m-n}{3}
. Since
m\leq 2n-3
, it follows that
t(G)\leq\frac{m-1}{2}
. As outerplanar graphs are
K_{4}
-free,
\omega(G)\leq 3
.

(iii)

If
G
is a
B_{k}
-free graph then for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, the induced subgraph
G[N(v)]
is
K_{1,k}
-free and has at most
\deg(v)\frac{(k-1)}{2}
edges. Thus
t(G)\leq\frac{(k-1)m}{3}
and clearly
\omega(G)\leq k+1
.

(iv)

If
G
is a
C_{k}
-free graph then for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, the induced subgraph
G[N(v)]
is
P_{k-1}
-free and by Erdös-Gallai Theorem
[
3
]
, it has at most
\deg(v)\frac{(k-3)}{2}
edges. Thus
t(G)\leq\frac{(k-3)m}{3}
and clearly
\omega(G)\leq k
.

Using the above claims and Remark
2.1
, the Corollary
1.5
is immediate.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6

In
[
4
]
, Li, Ning and Wu proved Conjecture
1.2
for triangle-free graphs and characterized the graphs which attain equality. In
[
7
, Remark 4.1]
, Kannan and Pragada gave an upper bound on spectral radius in terms of the number of triangles in the graph. In the following theorem, we obtain a bound for
s_{2}=\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}
in terms of number of triangles in the graph. This generalizes the above-mentioned results.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every graph
G
with
m\geq 2
edges,

\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}\leq m+\big(3t(G)\big)^{2/3}

and the inequality is strict if
t(G)>0
.

The proof of the above theorem relies on majorization. To that end, we first recall some definitions and results. For a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x^{\downarrow}
denote the vector obtained by rearranging the entries of
x
in the non-increasing order. Given two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we say that
x
is
weakly majorized
by
y
, denoted by
x\prec_{w}y
, if

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}{x_{j}}^{\downarrow}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}{y_{j}}^{\downarrow}

for all
1\leq k\leq n.

If
x\prec_{w}y\ \text{and}\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\downarrow}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{\downarrow},

then we say that
x
is
majorized
by
y
and denoted by
x\prec y
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}),\ y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, such that
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
are in non-increasing order. If
y\prec_{w}x
, then

\|y\|_{p}\leq\|x\|_{p}

for any real number
p>1
, and equality holds if and only if
x=y
.

We now give a proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The statement is true for complete graphs
K_{n}
for
n\geq 3
. So assume
G\neq K_{n}
and hence
\lambda_{2}\geq 0
. Now if
\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}\leq m
, then we are done. So suppose
\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}>m
. Let
\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}=m+\delta
, for some
\delta>0
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}=2m
, we have

2(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2})=2m+2\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}+2\delta

which implies

\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=3}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}+2\delta\geq\sum_{i=n-n^{-}+1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}+2\delta.

(3.1)

Take
x=(\lambda_{1}^{2},\lambda_{2}^{2},0,\dots,0)^{T}
and
y=(\lambda_{n}^{2},\lambda_{n-1}^{2},\dots,\lambda^{2}_{n-n^{-}+1},\delta,\delta)^{T}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n^{-}+2}
. We claim that
y\prec_{w}x
. Clearly,
\lambda_{1}^{2}\geq\max\{\lambda_{n}^{2},\delta\}
by (
3.1
). Again by (
3.1
),
\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=n-n^{-}+1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}+2\delta
. Thus
y\prec_{w}x
. Moreover,
x\neq y
because
y
has at least three non-zero entries.

Using Theorem
3.2
with
p=\frac{3}{2}
, we get

\|x\|^{3/2}_{3/2}>\|y\|^{3/2}_{3/2},

that is,

\lambda_{1}^{3}+\lambda_{2}^{3}>\sum_{i=n-n^{-}+1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|^{3}+2\delta^{3/2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle 6t(G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{3}\geq\lambda_{1}^{3}+\lambda_{2}^{3}-\sum_{i=n-n^{-}+1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|^{3}

\displaystyle>2\delta^{3/2}=2\big(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}-m\big)^{3/2}.

Upon rearrangement we get the desired inequality.
∎

Now if
G\in\mathcal{G}(\varepsilon,c)
then
t(G)\leq cm^{1.5-\varepsilon}
. By Theorem
3.1
we get

\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}\leq m+\big(3cm^{1.5-\varepsilon}\big)^{2/3}.

Theorem
1.6
is now immediate.

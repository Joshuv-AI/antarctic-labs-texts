Abstract
. We prove a converse of Fatou type result for certain eigenfunctions of the Lalplace-Beltrami operator on Harmonic NA groups relating sectorial convergence and admissible convergence of Poisson type integrals of complex (signed) measures. This result extends several results of this kind proved eariler in the context of the classical upper half space ℝ + n + 1 \mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1} . Similar results are also obtained in the degenerate case of the real hyperbolic spaces.

1.
Introduction

In the celebrated article
[
11
]
, Fatou posed and solved the classical problem which relates the differentiability of a suitable Borel measure (possibly complex valued)
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
with the boundary behaviour of the harmonic function
u=P[\mu]
on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
obtained as the Poisson integral of
\mu
. More precisely, it was proved that the harmonic function
u
will have non-tangential limit at some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
if
\mu
is differentiable at
x_{0}
. Loomis
[
16
]
proved that the converse is true when
\mu
is assumed to be positive. It was also shown by an example that the converse is false in the absence of positivity of
\mu
.

Ramey and Ullrich
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
obtained higher dimensional generalizations of the result of Loomis by introducing the notion of strong derivative of a measure. Precisely, it was proved in
[
17
]
that the non-tangential convergence of the Poisson integral
P[\mu]:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}\to[0,\infty)
, to a boundary point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equivalent to the existence of the strong derivative of the measure
\mu
at
x_{0}
. Among other things, their proof crucially uses the positivity of
\mu
. We refer the reader to
[
5
]
for a remarkable generalization of this result which deals with a more general class of measures.
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
has recently been extended to the class of Harmonic
NA
groups in
[
18
]
, for certain positive eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\mathcal{L}
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
18
]
, Theorem 4.2)
.

Suppose that
u
is a positive eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
in
S
with eigenvalue
\beta^{2}-\rho^{2}
, where
\beta>0
, with boundary measure
\mu
, and that
n_{0}\in N,L\in[0,\infty)
. Then
\mu
has strong derivative at
n_{0}
, equal to
L
if and only if the function
(n,a)\mapsto a^{\beta-\rho}u(n,a)
has admissible limit
L
at
n_{0}
.

Motivated by a result of Gehring
[
13
]
, Ramey and Ullrich also related the sectorial convergence and non-tangential convergence of Poisson integral of positive measure. We will need to introduce some notation to explain this result.
In
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
the non-tangential cone with vertex at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and aperture
\alpha>0
is defined by

\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{0})=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}:\|x-x_{0}\|<\alpha y\}.

The topological boundary
\partial\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{0})
of
\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{0})
with respect to
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
is denoted by

\partial\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{0})=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}:\|x-x_{0}\|=\alpha y\}.

Then a sector
\mathscr{S}
of
\partial\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{0})
is defined to be the portion of
\partial\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{0})
bounded by two rays in
\partial\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{0})
emanating from
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, that is

\mathscr{S}=\bigcup\limits_{\theta\in(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})}\gamma_{x_{0},\alpha,\theta}(0,\infty),\>\>\>\>\text{for some $\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,2\pi)$,}

where
\gamma_{x_{0},\alpha,\theta}:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
is defined by,

\gamma_{x_{0},\alpha,\theta}(y)=(x_{0}+(\alpha y\cos\theta,\alpha y\sin\theta),y).

A function
u:\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to have a sectorial limit
L\in\mathbb{C}
at
x_{0}
if for some
\mathscr{S}\subset\partial\Gamma_{\alpha}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to 0\\
z\in\mathscr{S}\end{subarray}}u(z)=L.

In
[
17
, Theorem 3.3]
it was proved that if a positive harmonic function
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
has a sectorial limit
L\in[0,\infty)
at
x_{0}
then
u
has non-tangential limit
L
at
x_{0}
. Although the result was proved for
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
, it can be easily generalized to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
for
n\geq 3
. A variant of this result was proved by Saeki in
[
19
, Corollary 3.4]
. For a class of measures (not necessarily nonnegative), Saeki proved that if the Poisson integral
P[\mu]
converges to
L
along rays (emanating from a boundary point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
) which form a dense subset of the topological boundary of some non-tangential cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
then
P[\mu]
has non-tangential limit
L
at
x_{0}
.

A comparison between the result of Ramey and Ullrich regarding sectorial convergence of the Poisson integral and that of Saeki is as follows. Ramey and Ullrich dealt with nonnegative measures
\mu
and assumed convergence of
P[\mu]
on some sector of the topological boundary of a non-tangential cone which is not a dense subset of this topological boundary. However, the result of Saeki accommodates a larger class of measures
\mu
but assumes convergence of
P[\mu]
along rays on some dense subset of the topological boundary of a non-tangential cone.

In this paper we wish to prove a result which generalizes both the results stated above in the context of Damek-Ricci spaces or the so called Harmonic
NA
groups. Instead of Poisson integral of nonnegative measures we deal with certain generalized Poisson integral (related to positive eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator) of measures on the boundary
N
which satisfies a condition similar to that of Saeki. We will also introduce a notion analogous to that of a sector (see Definition
2.3
) suitable for our context and prove that the boundary behaviour of the corresponding convolution integral along a sector is sufficient to conclude the existence of its admissible limit (as introduced in
[
14
]
). We refer the reader to Section
2
for unexplained notations and terminologies. The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\beta>0
, suppose
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<t_{0}}\frac{|\mu|(B(n_{0},r))}{m(B(n_{0},r))}<\infty\>,

for some
t_{0}>0
, for
n_{0}\in N
. If
u=Q_{i\beta}[\mu]
has a sectorial limit
L\in\mathbb{C}
at
n_{0}
, then
u
has admissible limit
L
at
n_{0}
.

We refer the reader to Remark
4.1
regarding the condition imposed on the boundary measure in the theorem above which shows that this condition accommodates all the cases dealt with in
[
17
,
5
]
for Euclidean spaces. This paper is organised as follows. In section
2
, we will discuss the preliminaries about Harmonic NA groups, generalized Poisson kernel, Poisson integral on these groups and introduce the notion of sector and the sectorial limit. In section
3
we prove some auxiliary results which play a crucial role in the proof of our main result. The proof of the main result is given in Section
4
. In Section
5
we discuss some related results for the Real hyperbolic spaces.

2.
Preliminaries of Harmonic NA groups

In this section we will discuss the necessary preliminaries and fix our notations. Most of this material can be found in
[
1
,
2
,
12
,
18
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be an
H
-type Lie algebra.
\mathfrak{z}
and
\mathfrak{v}
will denote the center of
\mathfrak{n}
and its orthogonal complement (with respect to the underlying inner product) respectively. The connected and simply connected Lie group
N
corresponding to
\mathfrak{n}
is called an
H
-type group. Since
\mathfrak{n}
is nilpotent, the exponential map defines an analytic diffeomorphism and hence one gets the parametrization
N=\exp\mathfrak{n}
by
(X,Z)
for
X\in\mathfrak{v},\>Z\in\mathfrak{z}
. The group law of
N
in the exponential coordinates is given by

(X,Z)(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime})=(X+X^{\prime},Z+Z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}[X,X^{\prime}]).

The Haar measure of
N
is given by
dXdZ
and will be denoted by
m
. Let
S=NA
be the semi-direct product of
N
and the multiplicative group
A=(0,\infty)
with respect to the nonisotropic dilation:

(2.1)

\delta_{a}(n)=\delta_{a}(X,Z)=(\sqrt{a}X,aZ),\>\>a\in A,\>n=(X,Z)\in N.

Hence the multiplication on
S
is given by

(X,Z,a)(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},a^{\prime})=(X+\sqrt{a}X^{\prime},Z+aZ^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{a}[X,X^{\prime}],aa^{\prime}).

Then
S
turns out to be a solvable Lie group having Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathbb{R}
with Lie bracket

[(X,Z,u),(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},u^{\prime})]=(\frac{1}{2}uX^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}u^{\prime}X,uZ^{\prime}-u^{\prime}Z+[X,X^{\prime}],0).

We shall write
(n,a)=(X,Z,a)
for the element
\left(\exp(X+Z),a\right),\>a\in A,\>X\in\mathfrak{v},\>Z\in\mathfrak{z}
and thus identify
S
with the upper-half space
\mathbb{R}^{2p+k+1}_{+}
topologically, where
dim\>\mathfrak{v}=2p,\>dim\>\mathfrak{z}=k
. Setting
Q=p+k
, one can see that

(2.2)

m\left(\delta_{a}(E)\right)=a^{Q}m(E),

is true for all measurable sets
E\subseteq N
and
a\in A
. This
Q
is called the homogeneous dimension of
N
. The group
S
is equipped with the left-invariant Riemannian metric induced by

\langle(X,Z,u),(X^{\prime},Z^{\prime},u^{\prime})\rangle_{S}=\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle+\langle Z,Z^{\prime}\rangle+uu^{\prime}

on
\mathfrak{s}
. Then the associated left-invariant Haar measure
dx
on
S
is given by

dx=a^{-Q-1}dXdZda,

where
dX,\>dZ,\>da
are the Lebesgue measures on
\mathfrak{v},\>\mathfrak{z},\>A
respectively. Let
\rho=Q/2
and we note that
\rho
corresponds to the half-sum of positive roots when
S=G/K
, is a rank one symmetric space of noncompact type. It is noteworthy however that rank one symmetric spaces of noncompact type form a very small subclass of the class of Harmonic
NA
groups.
We will use
e
to denote the identity element
(\underline{0},1)
of
S
, where
\underline{0}
and
1
are the identity elements of
N
and
A
respectively.

N
being a stratified Lie group, admits homogeneous norms with respect to the family of dilations
\{\delta_{a}\mid a\in A\}

[
12
, P.8-10]
. We recall that a continuous function
d:N\to[0,\infty)
is said to be a homogeneous norm on
N
with respect to the family of dilations
\{\delta_{a}\mid a\in A\}
if
d
satisfies the following
[
12
, P.8]
:

(i)

d
is smooth on
N\setminus\{0\}
;

(ii)

d(\delta_{a}(n))=ad(n)
, for all
a>0,\>n\in N
;

(iii)

d(n^{-1})=d(n)
, for all
n\in N
;

(iv)

d(n)=0
if and only if
n=\underline{0}
.

From
[
12
, Proposition 1.6]
, we know that for a homogeneous norm
d
on
N
, there exists a positive constant
\tau_{d}\geq 1
, such that

(2.3)

d(nn_{1})\leq\tau_{d}\left[d(n)+d(n_{1})\right],\;\>\>\>n\in N,n_{1}\in N.

A homogeneous norm
d
defines a left invariant quasi-metric on
N
, denoted by
\bf{d}
, as follows:

{\bf d}(n_{1},n_{2})=d(n_{1}^{-1}n_{2}),\>\>\>\>n_{1}\in N,\>n_{2}\in N.

From the definition of
d
and from (
2.3
), the following properties of the associated quasi-metric easily follow:

i)

{\bf d}(n_{1},n_{2})={\bf d}(n_{2},n_{1}),\>\>\>\text{for all}\>\>n_{1},\>n_{2}\in N
.

ii)

{\bf d}(nn_{1},nn_{2})={\bf d}(n_{1},n_{2}),\>\>\>\text{for all}\>\>n_{1},\>n_{2},\>n\in N.

iii)

For all
n_{1},\>n_{2},\>n\in N

(2.4)

{\bf d}(n_{1},n_{2})\leq\tau_{d}\left[{\bf d}(n_{1},n)+{\bf d}(n,n_{2})\right].

Any two homogeneous norms
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
on
N
are equivalent
[
4
, P.230]
. The homogeneous we will be working with is the following
[
7
, P.1918]
:

(2.5)

d(n)=d(X,Z)=(\|X\|^{4}+16\|Z\|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},\>\>\>\>\>n=(X,Z)\in N,

where
\|X\|
,
\|Z\|
are the usual Euclidean norms of
X\in\mathfrak{v}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2p}
and
Z\in\mathfrak{z}\cong\mathbb{R}^{k}
respectively. For
n\in N
and
r>0
, the
d
-ball centered at
n
with radius
r
is defined as

B(n,r)=\{n_{1}\in N\mid{\bf d}(n,n_{1})<r\}=\{n_{1}\in N\mid d(n_{1}^{-1}n)<r\}.

Then
\overline{B}(n,r)=\{n_{1}\in N\mid{\bf d}(n,n_{1})\leq r\}
, is a compact subset of
N

[
12
, Lemma 1.4]
.
So
B(n,r)
is the left translate by
n
of the ball
B(\underline{0},r)
, which is the image under the dilation
\delta_{r}
of the unit ball
B(\underline{0},1)
. It is also easy to observe that if
B=B(n,t)
for some
n\in N
,
t>0
, then

\delta_{r}(B)=B(\delta_{r}(n),rt),\>\>\>\>\>\text{for all $r>0$}.

For a function
\psi
defined on
N
, we define for
a>0
,

(2.6)

\psi_{a}(n)=a^{-Q}\psi\left(\delta_{{\frac{1}{a}}}(n)\right),\>\>\>\>\>n\in N.

If
g
is a measurable function on
N
and
\mu
is a measure on
N
, their convolution
\mu\ast g
is defined by

\mu\ast g(n)=\int_{N}g(n_{1}^{-1}n)\>d\mu(n_{1}),

provided the integral converges.

We now describe the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\mathcal{L}
on
S
. Let
\{e_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq 2p\}
,
\{e_{r}\mid 2p+1\leq r\leq 2p+k\}
,
\{e_{0}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{s}
corresponding to the decomposition
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathbb{R}
.
E_{l}
will denote the left-invariant vector field on
S
determined by
e_{l}
,
0\leq l\leq 2p+k
. From (
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
8
, P. 234]
) it is known that the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\mathcal{L}
associated to the left-invariant metric
\langle,\rangle_{S}
is the form

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{l=0}^{2p+k}E_{l}^{2}-QE_{0}.

Let
\partial_{i},\>\partial_{r},\>\partial_{a}
be the partial derivatives for the system of coordinates
(X_{i},Z_{r},a)
corresponding to
(e_{i},e_{r},e_{0})
. From the definition of left-invariant vector fields it can be shown that

\displaystyle E_{0}

\displaystyle=a\partial_{a},

\displaystyle E_{i}

\displaystyle=a\partial_{i}+\frac{a}{2}\sum_{r=2p+1}^{2p+k}\langle[X,e_{i}],e_{r}\rangle\partial_{r},

\displaystyle E_{r}

\displaystyle=a^{2}\partial_{r},\>\>\text{ for }1\leq i\leq 2p,\>\>2p+1\leq r\leq 2p+k.

Then
\mathcal{L}
can be written as
[
8
, P.234]

(2.7)

\mathcal{L}=a^{2}\partial_{a}^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{a}+(1-Q)a\partial_{a},

where

\mathcal{L}_{a}=a(a+\frac{1}{4}\|X\|^{2})\sum_{r=2p+1}^{2p+k}\partial_{r}^{2}+a\sum_{i=1}^{2p}\partial_{i}^{2}+a^{2}\sum_{2p+1}^{2p+k}\sum_{i=1}^{2p}\langle[X,e_{i}],e_{r}\rangle\partial_{r}\partial_{i}\>.

The Poisson kernel
\mathcal{P}:S\times N\to\mathbb{C}
, corresponding to
\mathcal{L}
is given by
[
2
, P.409]

\mathcal{P}(x,n)=P_{a}(n_{1}^{-1}n),\>\>\>\>\>x=(n_{1},a)\in S,\>\>n\in N,

where
P
is the function on
N
defined by

P(n)=P(X,Z)=\frac{c_{p,k}}{\left(\left(1+\frac{\|X\|^{2}}{4}\right)^{2}+\|Z\|^{2}\right)^{Q}},\>\>\>\>n=(X,Z)\in N,

and
c_{p,k}
is a positive constant so that
\|P\|_{L^{1}(N)}=1
. Then dilation of
P
(see (
2.6
)) is given by,

(2.8)

P_{a}(n)=P_{a}(X,Z)=\frac{c_{p,k}\>a^{Q}}{\left(\left(a+\frac{\|X\|^{2}}{4}\right)^{2}+\|Z\|^{2}\right)^{Q}},\>\>\>\>n=(X,Z)\in N,\>\>a>0.

Simplifying the expression (
2.8
), we get that

(2.9)

P_{a}(n)=P_{a}(X,Z)=\frac{16^{Q}c_{p,k}\>a^{Q}}{\left(16a^{2}+8a\|X\|^{2}+d(X,Z)^{2}\right)^{Q}},\>\>\>\>n=(X,Z)\in N,\>\>a>0.

The more general eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}
can be obtained in the following manner: for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, the
\lambda
-Poisson kernel is defined as

(2.10)

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(x,n)=\left[\frac{\mathcal{P}(x,n)}{P(\underline{0})}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i\lambda}{Q}}=\left[\frac{P_{a}(n_{1}^{-1}n)}{P(\underline{0})}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i\lambda}{Q}},\>\>\>\>x=(n_{1},a)\in S,\>\>n\in N.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, the function
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(.,n)
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
with eigenvalue
-(\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2})
, for each fixed
n\in N
,
[
1
, P.654]
.
One can normalize
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
, for
\text{Im}(\lambda)>0
, to define

(2.11)

\tilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(x,n)=C_{\lambda}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(x,n),\>\>\>x\in S,\>n\in N,

where
C_{\lambda}=c_{p,k}{\bf c}(-\lambda)^{-1}
and
c
is the generalization of the Harish-Chandra

-function. For
\text{Im}(\lambda)>0
, the
\lambda
-Poisson transform of a measure
\mu
on
N
is defined by

(2.12)

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}[\mu](n,a)=\int_{N}\tilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}((n,a),n^{\prime})\>d\mu(n^{\prime}),

whenever the integral converges absolutely for every
(n,a)\in S
. In this case, we say that the
\lambda
-Poisson transform
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}[\mu]
of
\mu
is well-defined. Since for each
n\in N
,
\tilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(.,n)
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
with eigenvalue
-(\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2})
, it follows that
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}[\mu]
is also an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
with the same eigenvalue provided
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}[\mu]
is well-defined. Using the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
given in (
2.10
) and the relation (
2.11
), it can be easily shown that for
\text{Im}(\lambda)>0
,
x=(n_{1},a)\in S
and
n\in N
,

(2.13)

\tilde{\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}}(x,n)=a^{\frac{Q}{2}+i\lambda}q^{\lambda}_{a}(n_{1}^{-1}n)

where

q^{\lambda}(n):=C_{\lambda}\left[P(\underline{0})^{-1}P(n)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{i\lambda}{Q}},\>\>\>q^{\lambda}_{a}(n)=a^{-Q}q^{\lambda}\left(\delta_{a^{-1}}(n)\right),

\text{Im}(\lambda)>0
,
n\in N
and

. An explicit expression of the function
q_{a}^{\lambda}
,
\text{Im}(\lambda)>0
, obtained from (

) is given by,

q^{\lambda}_{a}(n)=c_{\lambda}\>\frac{a^{-2i\lambda}}{\left(16a^{2}+8a\|X\|^{2}+d(n)^{2}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}-i\lambda}},\>\>\>n=(X,Z)\in N,\>a\in A,

where
c_{\lambda}=16^{\rho-i\lambda}c_{p,k}{\bf c}(-\lambda)^{-1}
.
If
\text{Im}(\lambda)>0
, then it is easily follows that

\int_{N}q^{\lambda}(n)\>dm(n)=1.

For a measure
\mu
on
N
and
\text{Im}(\lambda)>0
, we define the convolution integral

\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}[\mu](n,a):=\mu\ast q^{\lambda}_{a}(n)=a^{-Q}\int_{N}q^{\lambda}\left(\delta_{a^{-1}}(n_{1}^{-1}n)\right)\>d\mu(n_{1}),

whenever the integral converges absolutely for every

and say that
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}[\mu]
is well-defined. From the definition of the

-Poisson integral (

) and (

), it follows that for a measure

(2.14)

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}[\mu](n,a)=a^{\frac{Q}{2}+i\lambda}\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}[\mu](n,a),\>\>\>\text{for all}\>\>(n,a)\in S.

In this paper we will be interested only in the case
\lambda=i\beta
, for
\beta>0
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
\beta>0
,
\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
is defined to be the set of all radon measures

on

\mathcal{P}_{i\beta}[|\mu|]
(equivalently

) is well-defined.

If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}
then for all

,

, we have the explicit expression

(2.15)

\mathcal{Q}_{i\beta}[\mu](n,a)=c_{\beta}\>a^{2\beta}\int_{N}\frac{1}{\left(16a^{2}+8a\|X-X_{1}\|^{2}+d\left((X_{1},Z_{1})^{-1}(X,Z)\right)^{2}\right)^{\rho+\beta}}\>d\mu(X_{1}Z_{1}),

where

c_{\beta}=16^{\rho+\beta}C_{i\beta}=16^{\rho+\beta}c_{p,k}{\bf c}(-i\beta)^{-1}>0.

We now state some important definitions.

Definition 2.2
.

i)

A function
u
defined on
S
is said to have admissible limit
L\in\mathbb{C}
, at
n_{0}\in N
, if for each
\alpha>0

\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\to 0\\
(n,a)\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n_{0})\end{subarray}}u(n,a)=L,

where

(2.16)

\Gamma_{\alpha}(n_{0}):=\{(n,a)\in S\mid{\bf d}(n_{0},n)<\alpha a\}=\{(n,a)\in S\mid d(n_{0}^{-1}n)<\alpha a\}

is called the admissible domain with vertex at
n_{0}
and aperture
\alpha
.

ii)

Given a measure
\mu
on
N
, we say that
\mu
has strong derivative
L\in[0,\infty)
at
n_{0}\in N
if

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(n_{0}\delta_{r}(B))}{m(n_{0}\delta_{r}(B))}=L,

holds for every
d
-ball
B\subset N
. The strong derivative of
\mu
at
n_{0}
, if it exists, is denoted by
D\mu(n_{0})
.

iii)

A sequence of complex valued functions
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
defined on
S
is said to converge normally to a function
u

5.
The real hyperbolic space

As
N
has been assumed to be noncommutative, the class of harmonic
NA
groups does not include the degenerate case of the real hyperbolic space. We view the real hyperbolic space in the upper half-space model

\mathbb{H}^{l}=\{(x,y)|x\in\mathbb{R}^{l-1},y\in(0,\infty)\}\>,\>l\geq 2\;,

equipped with the standard hyperbolic metric

ds^{2}=\frac{1}{y^{2}}\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}\right)\>,

with the boundary being identified with
\mathbb{R}^{l-1}
. Note that the notion of admissible convergence here reduces to the usual non-tangential convergence and the rays defined as in definition
2.3
become usual rays in the setting of euclidean spaces. Also note that in this case the homogeneous dimension is
Q=2\rho=l-1
, and the corresponding differential operators are given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=y^{2}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{l-1}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}\right)-(l-2)y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\>,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\beta}=y^{2}\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{l-1}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}\right)-(2\beta-1)y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\>,\>\text{ for }\>\beta>0\>.

Proceeding as in section
4
, we can obtain the obvious analogues of all the results. Now, when
l=2
, corollary
4.2
can be proved in the following form,

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
u
is a positive eigenfunction of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with eigenvalue
\beta^{2}-\frac{1}{4}
,  for
\beta>0
, and boundary measure
\mu
, and let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. If the function

(x,y)\mapsto y^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}u(x,y)\>,

admits a radial limit
L
along two rays at
x_{0}
, then it has non-tangential limit
L
at
x_{0}
.

Some similar results were obtained in the context of
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
regarding the Poisson integral and the Gauss-Weierstrass integral of measures on
\mathbb{R}
, in
[
19
]
. Now the most natural question is in the setting of Theorem
5.1
, what can be said if two different values are assumed along two rays. Of course there does not exist any non-tangential limit, but as it turns out, we can conclude about radial limits along any such ray for
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Analogous result holds for Poisson integral of positive measures on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
(see
[
17
]
).

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that
u
is a positive eigenfunction of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with eigenvalue
\beta^{2}-\frac{1}{4}
,  for
\beta>0
, and boundary measure
\mu
, and let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be two distinct real numbers and
L_{1},L_{2}\in[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}u(x_{0}+\alpha_{1}y,y)=L_{1}\>,\>\>\text{and}\>\>\displaystyle\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}u(x_{0}+\alpha_{2}y,y)=L_{2}\>,

then for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to 0}y^{\beta-\frac{1}{2}}u(x_{0}+\alpha y,y)=\frac{L_{2}-L_{1}}{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}(\alpha-\alpha_{1})+L_{1}\>.

Proof.

We set

L(\alpha)=\frac{L_{2}-L_{1}}{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}(\alpha-\alpha_{1})+L_{1}\>,\>\>\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

Just as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we only work out the case when
x_{0}=0
and
\mu
is finite. As in the proof of corollary
4.2
, we transfer the problem in terms of
\mathcal{L}^{\beta}
-harmonic functions. Again we argue by contradiction. So suppose there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that there exists a sequence
\{(\alpha y_{j},y_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, converging to
(0,0)
, but the sequence
\mathcal{Q}_{i\beta}[\mu](\alpha y_{j},y_{j})
does not converge to
L(\alpha)
. Then proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we get hold of an
\mathcal{L}^{\beta}
-harmonic function
v
, obtained as a normal limit of some subsequence of dilates of
\mathcal{Q}_{i\beta}[\mu]
. Now we consider the change of coordinates

(5.1)

(x,y)\mapsto(\eta y,y)\>\>\text{(for }\eta\text{ varying over }\mathbb{R})\>.

In the above transformed coordinates, the differential operator
\mathcal{L}^{\beta}
takes the form

\mathcal{L}^{\beta}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\eta^{2}}+y^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}-(2\beta-1)y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\>.

Then we note that in the transformed coordinates (
5.1
), the function

F(\eta y,y):=\frac{L_{2}-L_{1}}{\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}}(\eta-\alpha_{1})+L_{1}\>,

is an
\mathcal{L}^{\beta}
-harmonic function in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. Thus

\tilde{v}(x,y):=v(x,y)-F(x,y)\>,\>(x,y)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}\>,

is an
\mathcal{L}^{\beta}
-harmonic function in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, which is by construction, non-tangentially bounded and vanishes on the two rays given in the hypothesis. Then an argument exactly analogous to that of the proof of Theorem
1.2
yields a contradiction.
∎

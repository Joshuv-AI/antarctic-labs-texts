Existence of minimizers and convergence of critical points for a new Landau-de Gennes energy functional in nematic liquid crystals
Zhewen Feng and Min-Chun Hong

Abstract
. The Landau-de Gennes energy in nematic liquid crystals depends on four elastic constants L 1 L_{1} , L 2 L_{2} , L 3 L_{3} , L 4 L_{4} . In the case of L 4 ≠ 0 L_{4}\neq 0 , Ball and Majumdar (Mol. Cryst. Liq. Cryst., 2010) found an example that the original Landau-de Gennes energy functional in physics does not satisfy a coercivity condition, which causes a problem in mathematics to establish existence of energy minimizers. At first, we introduce a new Landau-de Gennes energy density with L 4 ≠ 0 L_{4}\neq 0 , which is equivalent to the original Landau-de Gennes density for uniaxial tensors and satisfies the coercivity condition for all Q Q -tensors. Secondly, we prove that solutions of the Landau-de Gennes system can approach a solution of the Q Q -tensor Oseen-Frank system without using energy minimizers. Thirdly, we develop a new approach to generalize the Nguyen and Zarnescu (Calc. Var. PDEs, 2013) convergence result to the case of non-zero elastic constants L 2 L_{2} , L 3 L_{3} , L 4 L_{4} .

1.
Introduction

A liquid crystal is a state of matter between isotropic liquid and crystalline solid.
Based on the molecular positional and orientational order of liquid crystals, there are three main types: sematic, cholesterics and nematic. The nematic liquid crystal is the most common type in which the general phases are uniaxial and biaxial.
In 1971, de Gennes
[
11
]
used
Q
-tensor order parameters to formulate the elastic energy of liquid crystals with Landau’s bulk energy.
The Landau-de Gennes theory has been verified in physics as a successful theory describing both uniaxial and biaxial phases in nematic liquid crystals.
Indeed,
Pierre-Gilles de Gennes was awarded a Nobel prize for physics in 1991 for his discoveries in liquid crystals and polymers.

In the Landau-de Gennes framework, the space of
Q
-tensors in the Landau-de Gennes theory is a space of symmetric, traceless
3\times 3
matrices defined by

S_{0}:=\left\{Q\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}:\quad Q^{T}=Q,\,\mbox{tr }Q=0\right\},

where
\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}
denotes the space of
3\times 3
matrices.
When
Q\in S_{0}
has two equal non-zero eigenvalues, a nematic liquid crystal is said to be uniaxial. When
Q
has three unequal non-zero eigenvalues, a nematic liquid crystal is said to be biaxial. For material constants
a,b,c
,
we define the constant order parameter

s_{+}:=\frac{b+\sqrt{b^{2}+24ac}}{4c}

and denote the identity matrix by
I
. The subspace of uniaxial
Q
-tensors is given by

S_{*}:=\left\{Q\in S_{0}:\quad Q=s_{+}(u\otimes u-\frac{1}{3}I),\quad u\in S^{2}\right\}.

In this paper, we only consider the case of positive constants
a
,
b
,
c
, which corresponds to a lower temperature regime in liquid crystals (the constant
a
could also be negative; see
[
34
]
,
[
35
]
).

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For a tensor
Q\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;S_{0})
,
the Landau-de Gennes energy is defined by

(1.1)

E_{LG}(Q;\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}f_{LG}(Q,\nabla Q)\,dx:=\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{E}(Q,\nabla Q)+f_{B}(Q)\right)\,dx,

where
f_{E}
is the elastic energy density with elastic constants
L_{1},...,L_{4}
of the form

(1.2)

f_{E}(Q,\nabla Q):=\frac{L_{1}}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial Q_{ik}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{L_{3}}{2}\frac{\partial Q_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{L_{4}}{2}Q_{lk}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}

and
f_{B}(Q)
is the bulk energy density defined by

(1.3)

f_{B}(Q):=-\frac{a}{2}\tr(Q^{2})-\frac{b}{3}\tr(Q^{3})+\frac{c}{4}\left[\tr(Q^{2})\right]^{2}

with positive material constants
a
,
b
and
c
. Here and in the sequel, we adopt the Einstein summation convention for repeated indices.

In
[
11
]
, de Gennes discovered the first two terms of the elastic energy density in (
1.2
) with
L_{3}=L_{4}=0
. Since both the Oseen-Frank theory and the Landau-de Gennes theory should unify for modeling uniaxial liquid crystals,
Schiele and Trimper
[
38
]
pointed out that the
early attempt of de Gennes’ work
[
11
]
was incomplete since it would require the splay and bend Frank constants to be equal (i.e.
k_{1}=k_{3}
) in the Oseen-Frank density (as defined below in (
1.5
)) of uniaxial tensors
Q=s_{+}(u\otimes u-\frac{1}{3}I)
. However, some experiments on liquid crystals showed that
k_{3}>k_{1}
, so they added a third order term to original de Gennes’ elastic energy density by

\frac{L_{1}}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial Q_{ik}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{L_{4}}{2}Q_{lk}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}

with
L_{4}=\frac{1}{2s_{+}^{3}}(k_{3}-k_{1})>0
. Later, Berreman and Meiboom
[
6
]
observed that the above two groups
discarded the surface energy density in the Oseen-Frank density,
which correlates the blue phase theory for liquid crystals, so
they proposed to recover a second order term
\frac{L_{3}}{2}\frac{\partial Q_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}
with four third order terms, but their density is over-determined with the Oseen-Frank density.
Later, Longa et al.
[
31
]
gave an extension of the Landau-de Gennes density with 22 independent parameters.
Finally, combining the work of Schiele and Trimper
[
38
]
with Berreman and Meiboom
[
2
]
, Dickmann
[
13
]
found the full density
(
1.2
), which is consistent with
the Oseen-Frank density in (
1.5
) for uniaxial nematic liquid crystals. Since then, the general form (
1.2
) of the Landau-de Gennes energy density
has been widely used in the study of nematic liquid crystals (e.g.
[
33
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
1
]
). Under the relation that

\displaystyle s_{+}^{2}L_{1}=-\frac{1}{6}k_{1}+\frac{1}{2}k_{2}+\frac{1}{6}k_{3},\,s_{+}^{2}L_{2}=k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{4},\,s_{+}^{2}L_{3}=k_{4},\,s_{+}^{3}L_{4}=\frac{k_{3}-k_{1}}{2},

one can show (c.f.
[
33
]
) that for a uniaxial tensor
Q=s_{+}(u\otimes u-\frac{1}{3}I)
with
u\in S^{2}
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle f_{E}(Q,\nabla Q)=W(u,\nabla u),

where the Oseen-Frank density
W(u,\nabla u)
is defined by

(1.5)

\displaystyle W(u,\nabla u)=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{1}}{2}(\div u)^{2}+\frac{k_{2}}{2}(u\cdot\curl u)^{2}+\frac{k_{3}}{2}|u\times\curl u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{k_{2}+k_{4}}{2}(\tr(\nabla u)^{2}-(\div u)^{2})

for a unit director
u\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;S^{2})
. In (
1.5
),
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
,
k_{3}
are the Frank constants for molecular distortion of splay, twist and bend respectively and
k_{4}
is the Frank constant for the surface energy (c.f.
[
12
]
).
In 1937, Zvetkov established
numerical values for p-azoxyanisole (PAA) at
120^{\circ}C
(with the unit
10^{-12}\,m/J
) as follows:

\displaystyle k_{1}=5,\;k_{2}=3.8,\;k_{3}=10.1.

Therefore, according to physical experiments on nematic liquid crystals, the elastic constant
L_{4}=\frac{1}{2s_{+}^{3}}(k_{3}-k_{1})
is not equal to zero in general (c.f.
[
12
]
).

A fundamental problem in mathematics on the Landau-de Gennes theory is to establish existence of a minimizer of the energy functional
E_{LG}(Q,\Omega)
in
W^{1,2}_{Q_{0}}(\Omega;S_{0})
with
L_{4}\neq 0
.
If the functional density
f_{LG}(Q,\nabla Q)
satisfies the coercivity condition, one can prove existence of a minimizer of the functional
E_{LG}(Q,\Omega)
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;S_{0})
. In 2010, Ball and Majumdar
[
2
]
found an example where for
Q\in S_{0}
, the general Landau-de Gennes energy density (
1.2
) with
L_{4}\neq 0
does not satisfy the
coercivity condition.
Very recently, Golovaty et al.
[
22
]
emphasized that “From the
standpoint of energy minimization, unfortunately, such a version of Landau-de Gennes becomes
problematic, since the inclusion of the cubic term leads to an energy which is unbounded
from below”. Therefore, the Landau-de Gennes density (
1.2
) causes a knowledge gap between mathematical and physical theories on liquid crystals, since the energy functional
E_{LG}(Q,\Omega)
in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;S_{0})
does not satisfy the coercivity condition and violates the existence theorem of minimizers (e.g.
[
18
]
,
[
1
]
). In physics, concerning the third order term
\frac{L_{4}}{2}Q_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{\alpha}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{\beta}}
with
L_{4}\neq 0
in (
1.2
), Longa et al.
[
31
]
questioned that “In the presence of biaxial fluctuations the general third order
theory in
Q_{\alpha\beta}
becomes unstable and thus is thermodynamically incorrect”.
In order to overcome the difficulty, they extended Landau-de Gennes densities through 22 independent second, third, fourth order terms,
to preserve the stability of the free energy. Although their result is very interesting, all energy densities in
[
31
]
are complicated and have not addressed the above coercivity problem for all
Q
-tensors. In 2020, following similar spirit in
[
31
]
, Golovaty et al.
[
22
]
proposed a new physical interpretation of the density through fourth order terms to address the above coercivity problem for all
Q
-tensors. We would like to point out that the new density form in
[
22
]
is completely different from the original Landau-de Gennes density (
1.2
) although the density in
[
22
]
can recover the Oseen-Frank density for uniaxial
Q
-tensors.

In this paper, we will propose a new Landau-de Gennes energy density to solve the above coercivity problem with
L_{4}\neq 0
. At first, we observe in Lemma
2.1
that for uniaxial tensors
Q\in S_{*}
,
the original
third order term on
L_{4}
in (
1.2
), proposed by
Schiele and Trimper
[
38
]
*p. 268 in physics, is a linear combination of a fourth order term and a second order term in the following:

(1.6)

\displaystyle Q_{lk}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=\frac{3}{s_{+}}(Q_{ln}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}})(Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}})-\frac{2s_{+}}{3}|\nabla Q|^{2}.

In the case of
L_{4}\geq 0
, we introduce a new elastic energy density

(1.7)

\displaystyle f_{E,1}(Q,\nabla Q)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{L_{1}}{2}-\frac{s_{+}L_{4}}{3}\right)|\nabla Q|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial Q_{ik}}{\partial x_{k}}

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{3}}{2}\frac{\partial Q_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{3L_{4}}{2s_{+}}Q_{ln}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}

for all
Q\in S_{0}
.
We should point out that the fourth order term
Q_{ln}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}
in (
1.7
) is a non-negative square term, so our new Landau-de Gennes density (
1.7
) for
Q\in S_{0}
satisfies the coercivity condition in mathematics under suitable conditions on
L_{1},\cdots,L_{4}
. The first three terms in (
1.7
) keep the original form (
1.2
) for
Q\in S_{0}
and the new Landau-de Gennes density (
1.7
) is equivalent to the original density (
1.2
) for
Q\in S_{*}
. We also remark that our fourth order term
Q_{ln}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}
is a linear combination
of three fourth order terms
L^{(4)}_{5},L^{(4)}_{6},L_{7}^{(4)}
in
[
31
]
; i.e., we verify in Lemma
2.2
that

Q_{ln}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=\frac{8}{5}L^{(4)}_{5}-\frac{2}{5}L^{(4)}_{6}+\frac{2}{5}L_{7}^{(4)}.

For a
Q\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,S_{0})
, we introduce a new Landau-de Gennes energy functional

(1.8)

E_{L}(Q;\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}f_{LG}(Q,\nabla Q)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{E,1}(Q,\nabla Q)+\frac{1}{L}\tilde{f}_{B}(Q)\right)\,dx,

where
f_{E,1}(Q,\nabla Q)
has the form (
1.7
),

\tilde{f}_{B}(Q):=f_{B}(Q)-\min_{Q\in S_{0}}f_{B}(Q)\geq 0
and
L>0
is a parameter to drive all elastic constants to zero
[
35
,
5
,
17
]
.

Although there are many differences between the Oseen-Frank theory and the Landau-de Gennes theory, it is of great interest in mathematics whether minimizers of the Landau-de Gennes energy functional can approach a minimizer of the Oseen-Frank energy functional.

, where

is a minimizer of the Dirichlet energy functional in

. Sine then, there exist many developments on the one-constant approximation (c.f.
[

]

,

,

(
[

]

[

[

[

, we first prove

.

Let

,

,

\displaystyle L_{1}-\frac{s_{+}L_{4}}{6}>0,\quad-L_{1}-\frac{s_{+}L_{4}}{6}<L_{3}<2L_{1}-\frac{s_{+}L_{4}}{3},

\displaystyle\,L_{1}-\frac{s_{+}L_{4}}{6}+\frac{5}{3}L_{2}+\frac{1}{6}L_{3}>0,\quad L_{4}\geq 0.

Then, for each

Furthermore,

Remark 1
.

In the case of
L_{4}<0
, for each
Q\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,S_{0})
, we introduce an elastic energy density by

(1.10)

\displaystyle f_{E,-}(Q,\nabla Q):=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{L_{1}}{2}+\frac{s_{+}L_{4}}{3}\right)|\nabla Q|^{2}+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial Q_{ik}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{L_{3}}{2}\frac{\partial Q_{ik}}{\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{s_{+}}L_{4}\left(|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}-Q_{ln}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}\right).

It is clear that
|Q|^{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}-Q_{ln}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}\geq 0
for each
Q\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,S_{0})
, and that the elastic energy density

in (

Next, we discuss critical points of the Landau-de Gennes energy functional (
1.7
) in
W^{1,2}_{Q_{0}}(\Omega;S_{0})
.
One can write
f_{E}(Q,\nabla Q):=\frac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+V(Q,\nabla Q)
for some

for all
Q\in W^{1,2}_{Q_{0}}(\Omega;S_{0})

(1.11)

\displaystyle\alpha\Delta Q+\frac{1}{2}{\nabla_{k}(V_{Q_{x_{k}}}+V^{T}_{Q_{x_{k}}})}-\frac{1}{3}I\mbox{ tr}(\nabla_{k}V_{Q_{x_{k}}})-\frac{1}{2}(V_{Q}+V^{T}_{Q})+\frac{1}{3}I\mbox{ tr}(V_{Q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\left(-aQ-b\left(QQ-\frac{1}{3}I\tr(Q^{2})\right)+cQ\tr(Q^{2})\right)

in the weak sense, where
A^{T}
denotes the transpose of
A
,

V_{Q}:=\frac{\partial V(Q,\nabla Q)}{\partial Q}\mbox{ and }V_{Q_{x_{k}}}:=\frac{\partial V(Q,\nabla Q)}{\partial Q_{x_{k}}}.

For general elastic constants
L_{1},\cdots
,
L_{4}
, we cannot find any reference having an explicit form of the
Euler-Lagrange equation of

for
Q=s_{+}(u\otimes u-\frac{1}{3}I)\in S_{*}
with

, so we give an explicit form of the
Euler-Lagrange equation in the following:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\quad\alpha\left(s_{+}\Delta Q-2\nabla_{k}Q\nabla_{k}Q+2s_{+}^{-1}(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)|\nabla Q|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\nabla_{k}\left(V_{Q_{x_{k}}}(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)+(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)V^{T}_{Q_{x_{k}}}-2s_{+}^{-1}(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)\langle Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I,\,V_{Q_{x_{k}}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle-V_{Q_{x_{k}}}\nabla_{k}Q-\nabla_{k}QV^{T}_{Q_{k}}+2s_{+}^{-1}\left[\langle V_{Q_{k}},\nabla_{k}Q\rangle(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)+\langle V_{Q_{x_{k}}},(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)\rangle\nabla_{k}Q\right]

\displaystyle-(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)V^{T}_{Q}-V_{Q}(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)+2s_{+}^{-1}\langle V_{Q},Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I\rangle(Q+\frac{s_{+}}{3}I)=0,

which is equivalent to the harmonic map equation of
u
(c.f.
[

Since the Landau-de Gennes theory has been successfully used for modeling both uniaxial and biaxial states of nematic liquid crystals, it is of great interest whether the
Q
-tensor type of the Oseen-Frank system can be approximated by the Landau-de Gennes system (

) without using minimizers. In general, the problem of the convergence of solutions of the Landau-de Gennes equation (

Gartland

(1.13)

E_{\varepsilon}(u;\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2}\right)\,dx.

The Euler-Lagrange equation for the Ginzburg-Landau functional is

(1.14)

\Delta u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}u_{\varepsilon}(1-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})=0.

Using the cross product of vectors,
Chen
[
7
]
first proved that as
\varepsilon\to 0
, solutions

of the Ginzburg-Landau system (
1.14
) weakly converge to a harmonic map in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Chen and Struwe
[
9
]
proved global existence of partial regular solutions to the heat flow of harmonic maps using the Ginzburg-Landau approximation. In
[
3
]
-
[

]
, Bethuel, Brezis and Hélein obtained many results on asymptotic behavior for minimizers of

]

. Motivated by the above results on the Ginzburg-Landau approximation, it is natural to investigate the converging problem on solutions of the Landau-de Gennes system (

) as

Let

Assume that the solution

For the proof of Theorem

\pi

\displaystyle S_{\delta}:=\left\{Q\in S_{0}:\quad\dist(Q;S_{*})\leq\delta\right\}.

For a sufficiently small

When

of

uniformly converge to

away from the singular set of

Theorem 3
.

For each

be a weak solution to the equation (
1.11
) and let

be the limiting map of

in Theorem

uniformly inside

Q_{L}\to Q_{*}\text{ in }C^{k}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\backslash\Sigma)

for any positive integer

We would like to point out that our proof of Theorem

is new and different from one in
[
36
]
.
We outline main steps as follows:

Step I. For each
Q\in S_{\delta}
, there exists a rotation
R(Q)\in SO(3)
such that
\tilde{Q}=R^{T}(Q)QR(Q)
is diagonal. For any
\xi\in S_{0}
, we prove

(1.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\partial_{\tilde{Q}_{ii}}\partial_{\tilde{Q}_{jj}}f_{B}(\tilde{Q})\xi_{ii}\xi_{jj}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}(\xi_{11}^{2}+\xi_{22}^{2}+\xi_{33}^{2}),

where
\lambda=\min\{3a,s_{+}b\}>0
.
For each smooth

,
R^{T}(Q(x))Q(x)R(Q(x))
is diagonal. Then there exists a measure zero set
\Omega_{0}
such that
Q(x)
has a constant multiplicity of eigenvalues inside subdomains of
\Omega\backslash\Omega_{0}
and

is almost differentiable in
\Omega
. Using the geometric identity

\nabla\left(R^{T}(Q)QR(Q)\right)_{ii}=\left(R^{T}(Q)\nabla QR(Q)\right)_{ii}

with

we apply
(
1.17

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}|\nabla^{2}Q_{L}|^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}|\nabla Q_{L}|^{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx,

for a uniform constant

in
L

Step II. Using Step I

we establish a uniform Caccioppoli inequality for solutions

of (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}|\nabla Q_{L}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}}\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|Q_{L}-Q_{L,x_{0},r}|^{2}\,dx

Step III. Based on the uniform Caccioppoli inequality (

[

Finally, we make some remarks about new forms of the Landau-de Gennes energy density through a strong Ericksen’s condition on the Oseen-Frank density.
Recently, Golovaty et al.
[

proposed a novel form of the Landau-de Gennes energy density through the Oseen-Frank density. In addition to Ericksen’s inequalities that

k_{2}\geq|k_{4}|,k_{3}\geq 0,2k_{1}\geq k_{2}+k_{4}

they also assumed that

\displaystyle k_{1}\geq k_{2}+k_{4},\,k_{3}\geq k_{2}+k_{4},\,k_{4}\leq 0.

However, their result is not optimal. In fact, Golovaty et al.
[

]
made a further remark that their assumption (

[

2.
The coercivity condition and existence of minimizers

At first, we note

Lemma 2.1
.

For a uniaxial
Q=s_{+}(u\otimes u-\frac{1}{3}I)\in S_{*}
with
u\in S^{2}
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle Q_{lk}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=\frac{3}{s_{+}}(Q_{ln}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}})(Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}})-\frac{2s_{+}}{3}|\nabla Q|^{2}.

Proof.

Using the fact that
|u|=1
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle Q_{ln}Q_{kn}=s_{+}^{2}(u_{k}u_{n}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{kn})(u_{l}u_{n}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ln})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{+}^{2}\left(u_{k}u_{l}u_{n}u_{n}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{kn}u_{l}u_{n}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ln}u_{k}u_{n}+\frac{1}{9}\delta_{kn}\delta_{ln}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{2}\left(\frac{1}{3}u_{k}u_{l}+\frac{1}{9}\delta_{kl}\right)=\frac{s_{+}}{3}s_{+}(u_{k}u_{l}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{lk})+\frac{2s_{+}^{2}}{9}\delta_{kl}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s_{+}}{3}Q_{kl}+\frac{2s_{+}^{2}}{9}\delta_{kl}.

Through the identity (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle Q_{lk}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=(\frac{3}{s_{+}}Q_{ln}Q_{kn}-\frac{2s_{+}}{3}\delta_{kl})\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{s_{+}}(Q_{ln}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}})(Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}})-\frac{2s_{+}}{3}|\nabla Q|^{2}.

∎

Recall from Longa et al.
[
31
]
that

\displaystyle L^{(4)}_{5}:=

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\rho}Q_{\rho\beta}\frac{\partial Q_{\alpha\mu}}{\partial x_{\beta}}\frac{\partial Q_{\mu\nu}}{\partial x_{\nu}},\quad L^{(4)}_{6}:=Q_{\alpha\rho}Q_{\rho\beta}\frac{\partial Q_{\alpha\mu}}{\partial x_{\mu}}\frac{\partial Q_{\beta\nu}}{\partial x_{\nu}},

\displaystyle L^{(4)}_{7}:=

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\rho}Q_{\rho\beta}\frac{\partial Q_{\alpha\mu}}{\partial x_{\nu}}\frac{\partial Q_{\beta\mu}}{\partial x_{\nu}}.

Then we have

Lemma 2.2
.

For a uniaxial
Q\in S_{*}
, we obtain

(2.3)

\displaystyle Q_{ln}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=\frac{8}{5}L^{(4)}_{5}-\frac{2}{5}L^{(4)}_{6}+\frac{2}{5}L_{7}^{(4)}.

Proof.

Let
Q=s_{+}(u\otimes u-\frac{1}{3}I)
for
u\in S^{2}
.
Noting that
u_{i}\nabla u_{i}=0
, we calculate

\displaystyle(u\times\curl u)_{1}^{2}=

\displaystyle[u_{2}(\nabla_{1}u_{2}-\nabla_{2}u_{1})-u_{3}(\nabla_{3}u_{1}-\nabla_{1}u_{3})]^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-u_{1}\nabla_{1}u_{1}-u_{2}\nabla_{2}u_{1}-u_{3}\nabla_{3}u_{1})^{2}=[(u\cdot\nabla)u_{1}]^{2}.

Similarly, we can calculate other terms to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i}[(u\cdot\nabla)u_{i}]^{2}=\sum_{i}(u\times\curl u)_{i}^{2}=|u\times\curl u|^{2}.

Moreover, we calculate

(2.4)

\displaystyle Q_{lk}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=s_{+}^{3}(u_{l}u_{k}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{lk})\nabla_{l}(u_{i}u_{j})\nabla_{k}(u_{i}u_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{+}^{3}(u_{l}u_{k}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{lk})(u_{j}\nabla_{l}u_{i}+u_{i}\nabla_{l}u_{j})(u_{j}\nabla_{k}u_{i}+u_{i}\nabla_{k}u_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s_{+}^{3}(u_{l}u_{k}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{lk})(\nabla_{l}u_{i}\nabla_{k}u_{i}+\nabla_{l}u_{j}\nabla_{k}u_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2s_{+}^{3}\sum_{i}[(u\cdot\nabla)u_{i}]^{2}-\frac{2}{3}s_{+}^{3}|\nabla u|^{2}=2s_{+}^{3}|u\times\curl u|^{2}-\frac{2}{3}s_{+}^{3}|\nabla u|^{2}.

It follows from using (
2.1
) and (
2.4
) that

(2.5)

\displaystyle Q_{ln}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}s_{+}Q_{lk}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{2s^{2}_{+}}{9}|\nabla Q|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}s_{+}^{4}|u\times\curl u|^{2}+\frac{2}{9}s_{+}^{4}|\nabla u|^{2}.

We can verify from
[
31
]
that

(2.6)

\displaystyle 4L_{5}^{(4)}-L_{6}^{(4)}=\frac{5s_{+}^{4}}{3}|u\times\curl u|^{2},\quad L_{7}^{(4)}=\frac{5}{9}s_{+}^{4}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Substituting (
2.6
) into (
2.5
), we have

Q_{ln}Q_{kn}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}=\frac{2}{5}(4L^{(4)}_{5}-L^{(4)}_{6})+\frac{2}{5}L_{7}^{(4)}.

∎

Under the condition (
1.9
),
one can verify from Lemma 1.2 in
[
30

that there are two uniform constants
\alpha>0
and
C>0
such that the form
f_{E,1}(Q,p)
also satisfies

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}|p|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f_{E,1}(Q,p)\leq C(1+|Q|^{2})|p|^{2},\quad|\partial_{Q}f_{E,1}(Q,p)|\leq C(1+|Q|)|p|^{2}

for any
Q\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}
and
p\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Since
f_{E,1}(Q,p)
is
quadratic in

and satisfies (
2.7
), it can be checked (c.f.
[
28
]
) that
f_{E,1}(Q,p)
is uniformly convex in

; that is

(2.8)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{p^{i}_{kl}p^{j}_{mn}}f_{E,1}(Q,p)\xi^{i}_{kl}\xi^{j}_{mn}\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Now we give a proof of Theorem 1.

Proof.

Under the condition on
L_{1},\cdots,L_{4}
in Theorem
1
, it is clear from using Lemma 1.2 in
[
30
]
that

f_{E,1}(Q,\nabla Q)\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2},\quad\forall Q\in S_{0}.

By the standard theory in the calculus of variations (c.f.
[
17
]
), there exists a minimizer
Q_{L}
of
E_{L}
in
W_{Q_{0}}^{1,2}(\Omega;S_{0})
.
For each
Q\in W^{1,2}_{Q_{0}}(\Omega;S_{0})
, we set

E(Q;\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}f_{E,1}(Q,\nabla Q)\,dx.

It implies that

E(Q_{L};\Omega)+\int_{\Omega}(f_{B}(Q_{L})-\inf_{S_{0}}f_{B})\,dx\leq E(Q;\Omega)

for any
Q\in W^{1,2}_{Q_{0}}(\Omega;S_{*})
with the fact that
\tilde{f}_{B}(Q)=f_{B}(Q)-\inf_{S_{0}}f_{B}=0
.

As
L\to 0
, minimizers
Q_{L}
converge (possible passing
subsequence) weakly to a tensor
Q_{*}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;S_{0})
with that
\tilde{f}_{B}(Q_{*})=0
, which implies that
Q_{*}\in S_{*}
a.e. in
\Omega
. Then, for any
Q\in W_{Q_{0}}^{1,2}(\Omega;S_{*})
, we have

E(Q_{*};\Omega)\leq\liminf_{L\to 0}E(Q_{L};\Omega)\leq\operatornamewithlimits{lim\,sup}_{L\to 0}E(Q_{L};\Omega)\leq E(Q;\Omega).

Therefore
Q_{*}
is also a minimizer of
E
in
W^{1,2}_{Q_{0}}(\Omega;S_{*})
.
Choosing
Q=Q_{*}
in the above inequality, it implies that

E(Q_{*};\Omega)=\lim_{L\to 0}E_{L}(Q_{L};\Omega),\quad\lim_{L\to 0}\frac{1}{L}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{f}_{B}(Q_{L})\,dx=0.

Moreover, it is known that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla Q_{*}|^{2}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{L\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla Q_{L}|^{2}\,dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}V(Q_{*},\nabla Q_{*})\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{L\to 0}\int_{\Omega}V(Q_{L},\nabla Q_{L})\,dx.

It implies that
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla Q_{*}|^{2}\,dx=\liminf_{L\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla Q_{L}|^{2}\,dx
. Otherwise, there is a subsequence
L_{k}\to 0
such that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla Q_{*}|^{2}\,dx<\lim_{L_{k}\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla Q_{L_{k}}|^{2}\,dx.

Then

\displaystyle E(Q_{*};\Omega)=\lim_{L_{k}\to 0}E_{L_{k}}(Q_{L_{k}};\Omega),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\lim_{L_{k}\to 0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla Q_{L_{k}}|^{2}\,dx+\lim_{L_{k}\to 0}\int_{\Omega}V(Q_{L_{k}},\nabla Q_{L_{k}})\,dx

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle E(Q_{*};\Omega).

This is impossible. Therefore, minimizers
Q_{L_{k}}
converge strongly, up to a subsequence, to a minimizer
Q_{*}=s_{+}(u_{*}\otimes u_{*}-\frac{1}{3}I)
of
E
in

W^{1,2}_{Q_{0}}(\Omega;S_{0})
. Following from Lemma
3.1
,
Q_{*}
satisfies (
1.12
) and
u_{*}
is a minimizer of the Oseen-Frank energy in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;S^{2})
. Due to the well-known result of Hardt, Kinderlehrer and Lin
[
23
]
,
u_{*}
is partially regular in
\Omega
(see also
[
24
]
). Thus
Q_{*}
is partially regular.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
Q
is a minimizer of
E_{L}(Q;\Omega)
from (
1.8
) in
W^{1,2}_{Q_{0}}(\Omega;S_{0})
, it satisfies

\displaystyle-\alpha\Delta Q_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}(V_{p^{k}_{ij}}+V_{p^{k}_{ji}})+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\nabla_{k}V_{p^{k}_{ll}}+\frac{1}{2}(V_{Q_{ij}}+V_{Q_{ji}})-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\sum_{l=1}^{3}V_{Q_{ll}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L}\left(-aQ_{ij}-b(Q_{ik}Q_{kj}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\tr(Q^{2}))+cQ_{ij}\tr(Q^{2})\right)=0

in the weak sense, where
V_{p^{k}_{ij}}:=V_{p^{k}_{ij}}(Q,p)
with
p=(\nabla_{k}Q_{ij})
.

Proof.

For any test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega;S_{0})
, consider
Q_{t}:=Q+t\phi
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega;S_{0})
, we calculate

\displaystyle\left.\int_{\Omega}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\tilde{f}_{E,1}(Q_{t},\nabla Q_{t})+\frac{1}{L}\tilde{f}_{B}(Q_{t})\right)\right|_{t=0}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha\frac{\partial Q_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial\phi_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}+V_{p^{k}_{ij}}\frac{\partial\phi_{ij}}{\partial x_{k}}+V_{Q_{ij}}\phi_{ij}\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L}\int_{\Omega}-aQ_{ij}\phi_{ij}-bQ_{ik}Q_{kj}\phi_{ij}+c(Q_{ij}\tr(Q^{2})\phi_{ij})\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(-\alpha\Delta Q_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}(V_{p^{k}_{ij}}+V_{p^{k}_{ji}})+\frac{1}{2}(V_{Q_{ij}}+V_{Q_{ji}})\right)\phi_{ij}\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L}\int_{\Omega}\left(-aQ_{ij}-bQ_{ik}Q_{kj}+cQ_{ij}\tr(Q^{2})\right)\phi_{ij}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(-\alpha\Delta Q_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{k}(V_{p^{k}_{ij}}+V_{p^{k}_{ji}})-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\sum_{l}\nabla_{k}V_{p^{k}_{ll}}\right)\phi_{ij}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}(V_{Q_{ij}}+V_{Q_{ji}})-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\sum_{l}V_{Q_{ll}}\right)\phi_{ij}\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L}\int_{\Omega}\left(-aQ_{ij}-b(Q_{ik}Q_{kj}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\tr(Q^{2}))+cQ_{ij}\tr(Q^{2})\right)\phi_{ij}\,dx=0,

where we used the fact that
\phi
is traceless.
This proves our claim.
∎

In the case of

, Majumdar and Zarnescu
[

proved that the weak solution of (

are non-zero, the system (

of

in

is bounded and the energy density

in (

. Without this above growth condition on the density, it is a well-known fact that a minimizer

of the Landau-de Gennes energy functional in

may not satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,S_{0})
. To overcome this difficulty, we can introduce a smooth cutoff function
\eta(r)
in
[0,\infty)
so that
\eta(r)=1
for

with a very large constant
M>0
and

for

.
Then for each
Q\in W^{1,2}(\Omega,S_{0})
, one can modify the Landau-de Gennes density by

\widetilde{f}_{E}(Q,\nabla Q):=\frac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+\tilde{V}(Q,\nabla Q)=\frac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+\eta(|Q|)V(Q,\nabla Q)

with the property that

\frac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}\leq\widetilde{f}_{E}(Q,\nabla Q)\leq C|\nabla Q|^{2}.

For a large
M>0
in (
2.9
), we consider a modified Landau-de Gennes functional

(2.10)

\tilde{E}_{L}(Q;\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\widetilde{f}_{E}(Q,\nabla Q)+\frac{1}{L}\tilde{f}_{B}(Q)\right)\,dx.

Then we obtain

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
Q_{L}
be a weak solution to the equation (
1.11
) with the boundary value
Q_{0}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega;S_{*})
associated to the functional
\tilde{f}_{E}(Q,\nabla Q)
in (

). Then,
|Q_{L}|\leq M+1
for a sufficient large

.

Proof.

Recall from the definition of
\tilde{f}_{E}(Q,\nabla Q)
in (

) that for a

with
|Q|\geq M+1
,

\tilde{f}_{E}(Q,\nabla Q)=\frac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}.

Similarly to one in
[

, choose a test function
\phi=Q(1-\min\{1,\frac{M+1}{|Q|}\})
. Multiplying (
1.11
) by the test function

, we have

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{|Q|\geq M+1}|\nabla Q|^{2}(1-\frac{M+1}{|Q|})-(M+1)Q_{ij}\nabla_{k}Q_{ij}\nabla_{k}\frac{1}{|Q|})\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{L}\int_{|Q|\geq M+1}\left(-a|Q|^{2}-bQ_{ik}Q_{kj}Q_{ij}+c|Q|^{4}\right)(1-\frac{M+1}{|Q|})\,dx=0.

Note the fact that

. The above second term is non-negative.
For a sufficiently large

, the third term is positive. This implies that the set

is empty; i.e.,

a.e. in
\Omega
.
∎

4.21
) that

r_{0}\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}|\nabla^{2}Q_{L}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{C}{r_{0}}\int_{B_{\frac{4}{3}r_{0}}(x_{0})}|\nabla Q_{L}|^{2}\,dx.

As a consequence of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation (c.f.
[
15
]
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}|\nabla Q_{L}|^{3}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}r_{0}|\nabla^{2}Q_{L}|^{2}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}|\nabla Q_{L}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{3/2}

\displaystyle+C\left(r_{0}^{-1}\int_{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}|\nabla Q_{L}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{3/2}\leq\varepsilon_{0}^{3}.

Using Lemma 4.5 with any
k\geq 1
, we obtain

(4.47)

\|Q_{L}\|_{W^{k,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\backslash\Sigma)}<C_{k}.

Then,
Q_{L}
converges smoothly to
Q_{*}
in
\Omega\backslash\Sigma
.
∎

5.
The Landau-de Gennes density through the Oseen-Frank density

In this section, we will obtain a new form of the Landau-de Gennes energy density through the Oseen-Frank density. Under the condition (
1.9
), it was shown in
[
33
]
that for each
Q=s_{+}(u\otimes u-\frac{1}{3}I)\in S_{*}
, one has

W(u,\nabla u)=f_{E}(Q,\nabla Q).

Assuming the strong Ericksen condition

(5.1)

k_{2}>|k_{4}|,\quad k_{3}>0,\quad 2k_{1}>k_{2}+k_{4},

it was pointed out in
[
28
]
(see also
[
14
,
1
,
15
]
) that there are positive constants
\lambda
and
C
such that the density
W(u,\nabla u)
is equivalent to a new form that
\widetilde{W}(u,p)
satisfies

\lambda|p|^{2}\leq\widetilde{W}(u,p)\leq C(1+|u|^{2})|p|^{2}

for any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and any
p\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}
. However, there seems no reference for an explicit form of
\widetilde{W}(u,\nabla u)
, so we give an explicit form
\widetilde{W}(u,\nabla u)
here. For
u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, it can be checked that

(5.2)

\displaystyle 2|\nabla u|^{2}-|\curl u|^{2}-(\div u)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla_{1}u_{1}+\nabla_{2}u_{2})^{2}+(\nabla_{2}u_{2}+\nabla_{3}u_{3})^{2}+(\nabla_{1}u_{1}+\nabla_{3}u_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle+(\nabla_{2}u_{3}+\nabla_{3}u_{2})^{2}+(\nabla_{1}u_{3}+\nabla_{3}u_{1})^{2}+(\nabla_{1}u_{2}+\nabla_{2}u_{1})^{2}.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume the Frank constants
k_{1},\cdots,k_{4}
satisfy

(5.3)

\displaystyle k_{2}>|k_{4}|,\quad\min\{k_{1},k_{3}\}>\frac{1}{2}(k_{2}+k_{4}).

Then
the density
W(u,\nabla u)
of the form (
1.5
) for each
u\in S^{2}
is equivalent to the new form

(5.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(u,\nabla u)=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{4}-\alpha}{4}(\div u)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{k_{2}-k_{4}-\alpha}{4}(u\cdot\curl u)^{2}+\frac{2k_{3}-k_{2}-k_{4}-\alpha}{4}|u\times\curl u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{k_{2}+k_{4}-\alpha}{4}\sum_{i\neq j}\left((\nabla_{i}u_{i}+\nabla_{j}u_{j})^{2}+(\nabla_{i}u_{j}+\nabla_{j}u_{i})^{2}\right)

where
\alpha=\min\{k_{2}-|k_{4}|,2k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{4},2k_{3}-k_{2}-k_{4}\}>0
.

Proof.

Note that
W(u,\nabla u)
is rotational invariant (c.f.
[
24
]
); i.e., for each
R\in SO(3)
,
\tilde{x}=R(x-x_{0})
and
\tilde{u}=Ru(x)=Ru
. Then we have

W(\tilde{u},\tilde{}\nabla\tilde{u})=W(Ru,R\nabla uR^{T})=W(u,\nabla u).

Then for any
u\in S^{2}
, we can find some
R=R(u(x_{0}))\in SO(3)
at each point
x_{0}\in\Omega
such that

\tilde{u}(0):=Ru(x_{0})=(0,0,1)^{T}.

Using the relation

\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{3}}{\partial\tilde{x}_{i}}=-(\tilde{u}_{1}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{1}}{\partial\tilde{x}_{i}}+\tilde{u}_{2}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{2}}{\partial\tilde{x}_{i}})=0

for all
i=1,2,3
, we evaluate four terms of the Oseen-Frank energy density at
\tilde{x}_{0}

(5.5)

\displaystyle(\tilde{}\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})^{2}=

\displaystyle(\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}+\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{2})^{2},

\displaystyle(\tilde{u}\cdot\curl\tilde{u})^{2}=

\displaystyle(\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}-\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{1})^{2},

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}\times\curl\tilde{u}|^{2}=

\displaystyle|\tilde{}\nabla_{3}\tilde{u}_{1}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{3}\tilde{u}_{2}|^{2},

\displaystyle(\tr(\tilde{}\nabla\tilde{u})^{2}-(\tilde{}\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})^{2})=

\displaystyle 2\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{1}-2\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{2}.

Substituting above identities into the density, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle W(\tilde{u},\tilde{}\nabla\tilde{u})=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{1}}{2}(\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}+\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{2})^{2}+\frac{k_{2}}{2}(|\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{1}|^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{k_{3}}{2}(|\tilde{}\nabla_{3}\tilde{u}_{1}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{3}\tilde{u}_{2}|^{2})+{k_{4}}\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{1}-(k_{2}+k_{4})(\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{2}|\tilde{}\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}+\frac{2k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{4}-\tilde{\alpha}}{4}(\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}+\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{k_{2}+k_{4}-\tilde{\alpha}}{4}(\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}-\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{2})^{2}+\frac{k_{2}-k_{4}-\tilde{\alpha}}{2}(|\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{1}|^{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{(k_{3}-\tilde{\alpha})}{2}(|\tilde{}\nabla_{3}\tilde{u}_{1}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{3}\tilde{u}_{2}|^{2})+\frac{k_{4}}{2}(\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}+\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{4}-\tilde{\alpha}}{4}(\div u)^{2}+\frac{k_{2}-k_{4}-\tilde{\alpha}}{4}(u\cdot\curl u)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{k_{3}-\tilde{\alpha}}{2}|u\times\curl u|^{2}+\frac{k_{2}+k_{4}-\tilde{\alpha}}{4}\left(|\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}-\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{2}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}+\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{1}|^{2}\right),

where
\tilde{\alpha}
is a positive constant due to the strong Ericksen condition (
5.1
). Using (
5.5
), we find

\displaystyle|\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{1}-\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{2}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{1}\tilde{u}_{2}+\tilde{}\nabla_{2}\tilde{u}_{1}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{3}\tilde{u}_{1}|^{2}+|\tilde{}\nabla_{3}\tilde{u}_{2}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\tilde{}\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}+\tr(\tilde{}\nabla\tilde{u})^{2}-(\tilde{}\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})^{2}=2|\nabla u|^{2}-|\curl u|^{2}-(\div u)^{2}.

If we further assume that
2k_{3}>k_{2}+k_{4}
, then we can rewrite (
5.6
) into

(5.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(u,\nabla u)=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{4}-\alpha}{4}(\div u)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{k_{2}-k_{4}-\alpha}{4}(u\cdot\curl u)^{2}+\frac{2k_{3}-k_{2}-k_{4}-\alpha}{4}|u\times\curl u|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{k_{2}+k_{4}-\alpha}{4}\left(2|\nabla u|^{2}-|\curl u|^{2}-(\div u)^{2}\right).

From (
5.2
), we prove (
5.4
).
∎

It is clear that the new form
\widetilde{W}(u,p)
in (
5.4
) with
p=\nabla u
satisfies

\frac{\tilde{\alpha}}{2}|p|^{2}\leq\widetilde{W}(u,p)\leq C(1+|u|^{2})|p|^{2}

for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p\in\mathbb{M}^{3\times 3}
.

Through the relation (
5.4
), we can have the new Landau-de Gennes energy density satisfying the coercivity in the following:

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
\hat{L}_{1}
,
\hat{L}_{2}
,
\hat{L}_{3}
and
\hat{L}_{4}
satisfy the condition

(5.8)

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{1}:=

\displaystyle s_{+}^{-2}\frac{2k_{1}-k_{2}-k_{4}}{2},

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{2}:=

\displaystyle s_{+}^{-2}\frac{2k_{3}-k_{2}-k_{4}}{2}

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{3}:=

\displaystyle s_{+}^{-2}\frac{k_{2}-k_{4}}{2},

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{4}:=

\displaystyle s_{+}^{-2}\frac{k_{2}+k_{4}}{2}.

Then for each
Q\in S_{*}
, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{E,2}(Q,\nabla Q):=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla Q|^{2}+\frac{\hat{L}_{1}-\tilde{\alpha}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big((s_{+}^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{3}I)_{ij}(\nabla\cdot Q_{j})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\hat{L}_{2}-\tilde{\alpha}}{2}\left|(s_{+}^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{3}I)_{i}\times\curl Q_{i}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\hat{L}_{3}-\tilde{\alpha}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left((s_{+}^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{3}I)_{ij}(\curl Q_{j})_{i}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\hat{L}_{4}-\tilde{\alpha}}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\Big((\nabla_{i}Q_{ik}+\nabla_{j}Q_{jk})^{2}+(\nabla_{i}Q_{jk}+\nabla_{j}Q_{ik})^{2}\Big),

where
Q_{i}
is the
i
-th column of the
Q
matrix and
\tilde{\alpha}
is given by

(5.9)

\displaystyle\alpha=\min\{\hat{L}_{1},\hat{L}_{2},\hat{L}_{3},\hat{L}_{4}\}>0.

Proof.

Due to the fact that
|u|^{2}=1
, a direct calculation yields

\nabla_{k}u_{i}=u_{j}\nabla_{k}(u_{i}u_{j})=s_{+}^{-1}(u_{1}\nabla_{k}Q_{i1}+u_{2}\nabla_{k}Q_{i2}+u_{3}\nabla_{k}Q_{i3})=s_{+}^{-1}u_{j}\nabla_{k}Q_{ij}.

One can verify that

(5.10)

\displaystyle u_{l}\nabla_{k}u_{i}=s_{+}^{-1}(s_{+}^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{3}I)_{lj}\nabla_{k}Q_{ij}.

Here we used the fact that
|Q|=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}s_{+}
.
Then we can derive
f_{E}(Q,\nabla Q)
from (
5.4
) that

(5.11)

\displaystyle s_{+}^{2}(\div u)^{2}=s_{+}^{2}\sum_{i}(u_{i}\div u)^{2}=\sum_{i}\Big((s_{+}^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{3}I)_{ij}(\nabla\cdot Q_{j})\Big)^{2},

(5.12)

\displaystyle s_{+}^{2}|u\times\curl u|^{2}=s_{+}^{2}|u\times(s_{+}^{-1}u_{j}(\curl Q_{j}))|^{2}=\left|(s_{+}^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{3}I)_{i}\times\curl Q_{i}\right|^{2},

(5.13)

\displaystyle s_{+}^{2}(u\cdot\curl u)^{2}=s_{+}^{2}(s_{+}^{-1}u_{i}u_{j}(\curl Q_{j})_{i})^{2}=\left((s_{+}^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{3}I)_{ij}(\curl Q_{j})_{i}\right)^{2}.

It then follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.10
) that

(5.14)

\displaystyle s_{+}^{2}\left(2|\nabla u|^{2}-|\curl u|^{2}-(\div u)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l,k=1}^{3}\sum_{i\neq j}((s_{+}^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{3}I)_{lk})^{2}\left((\nabla_{i}Q_{ik}+\nabla_{j}Q_{jk})^{2}+(\nabla_{i}Q_{jk}+\nabla_{j}Q_{ik})^{2}\right).

Substituting the identities (
5.11
)-(
5.14
) into the equation (
5.4
), we complete a proof.
∎

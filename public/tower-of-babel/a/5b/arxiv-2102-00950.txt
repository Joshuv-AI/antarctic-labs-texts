Virtual elements for Maxwell’s equations
L. Beirão da Veiga, F. Dassi, G. Manzini, and L. Mascotto

Abstract
We present a low order virtual element discretization for time dependent Maxwell’s equations, which allow for the use of general polyhedral meshes. Both the semi- and fully-discrete schemes are considered. We derive optimal a priori estimates and validate them on a set of numerical experiments. As pivot results, we discuss some novel inequalities for de Rahm sequences of nodal, edge, and face virtual element spaces. AMS subject classification: 65N12; 65N15

A recent application for permanent magnet simulations can be found in

We recall the definition of some differential operators that we shall use in the paper.
Let

, and

,

, and

Given a two-dimensional vector-valued field

\displaystyle\divF\mathbf{v}:=\partial_{x}v_{1}+\partial_{y}v_{2},\quad\quad\rotF\mathbf{v}:=\partial_{y}v_{1}-\partial_{x}v_{2},\quad\quad\curlboldF v:=\big(\partial_{y}v,-\partial_{x}v\big)^{T}.

In turn, given a three-dimensional vector-valued field
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}):K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\div\mathbf{v}:=\partial_{x}v_{1}+\partial_{y}v_{2}+\partial_{z}v_{3},\quad\quad\curlbold\mathbf{v}:=\big(\partial_{y}v_{3}-\partial_{z}v_{2},\partial_{z}v_{1}-\partial_{x}v_{3},\partial_{x}v_{2}-\partial_{y}v_{1}\big)^{T}.

For Lipschitz domains

, we introduce the Sobolev

\displaystyle H^{s}(\curlbold,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in[H^{s}(D)]^{3}\mid\curlbold\mathbf{v}\in[H^{s}(D)]^{3}\right\},

\displaystyle H^{s}(\div,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in[H^{s}(D)]^{3}\mid\div\mathbf{v}\in H^{s}(D)\right\}.

If

and
H(\div,D)
.
We denote the unit vector that is orthogonal to the

\partial D

.
Furthermore, we recall the existence of the two trace

\text{tr}_{\textbf{curl}}:H(\curlbold,D)\rightarrow[H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial D)]^{2}

According to the standard notation,

We shall also consider

\displaystyle H_{0}(\curlbold,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in H(\curlbold,D)\mid\text{tr}_{\textbf{curl}}(\mathbf{v})=0\text{~on~}\partial D\right\},

\displaystyle H_{0}(\div,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\mathbf{v}\in H(\div,D)\mid\text{tr}_{\text{div}}(\mathbf{v})=0\text{~on~}\partial D\right\}.

Let

2
The continuous problem

We consider the strong form of Maxwell’s equations on a polyhedral domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
:

Given the initial data
\mathbf{E}^{0}
and
\mathbf{B}^{0}
, find the
electric field
\mathbf{E}
and the magnetic induction field
\mathbf{B}
such
that

\begin{cases}\varepsilon\mathbf{E}_{t}+\sigma\mathbf{E}-\curlbold(\mu^{-1}\mathbf{B})=\mathbf{J}&\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\,\forall t\in[0,T],\\[5.0pt]
\mathbf{B}_{t}+\curlbold\mathbf{E}=\mathbf{0}&\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\,\forall t\in[0,T],\\[5.0pt]
\mathbf{E}(0)=\mathbf{E}^{0},\quad\mathbf{B}(0)=\mathbf{B}^{0}\quad&\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\[5.0pt]
\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}=0,\quad\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}=0&\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where the subscript
t
denotes the first derivative in
time (so throughout the paper we use
q_{t}
instead of
\partial q/\penalty{\partial t}
for a given time-dependent quantity
q(t)
).
Above,
\mathbf{J}
,
\varepsilon
,
\sigma
, and
\mu
denote the electric current density that is externally applied to the system, the electric permittivity, the electric conductivity, and the magnetic permeability.
We consider homogeneous boundary conditions to ease the exposition, since
the nonhomogeneous boundary case presents further complications.
We assume that the initial magnetic induction is a solenoidal field, i.e.,

\div\mathbf{B}^{0}=0.

(2)

Taking the divergence of the second equation in (
1
), we readily deduce that

\displaystyle\div\mathbf{B}(t)=0\quad\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(3)

The weak formulation of Maxwell’s equations reads as follows:

\begin{cases}\text{Find}~(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{B})\in H_{0}(\curlbold,\Omega)\times H_{0}(\div,\Omega)\text{~such~that}\\[5.0pt]
(\varepsilon\mathbf{E}_{t}+\sigma\mathbf{E},\mathbf{w})_{0,\Omega}-(\mu^{-1}\mathbf{B},\curlbold\mathbf{w})_{0,\Omega}=(\mathbf{J},\mathbf{w})_{0,\Omega}&\quad\forall\mathbf{w}\in H_{0}(\curlbold,\Omega),\\[5.0pt]
(\mu^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{t},{\bm{\psi}})_{0,\Omega}+(\mu^{-1}{\bm{\psi}},\curlbold\mathbf{E})_{0,\Omega}=0&\quad\forall{\bm{\psi}}\in H_{0}(\div,\Omega).\end{cases}

(4)

In the sequel, we shall assume that there exist strictly positive constants
\sigma^{\star},\varepsilon_{\star},\varepsilon^{\star},\mu_{\star},\mu^{\star}
such that, for all
x\in\Omega
, the
material parameters satisfy

0\leq\sigma(x)\leq\sigma^{\star}\ ,\quad\varepsilon_{\star}\leq\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^{\star}\ ,\quad\mu_{\star}\leq\mu(x)\leq\mu^{\star}\ .

(5)

On the regularity of the solutions to (
4
).

Under suitable assumptions on the regularity of the data, problem (
4
) admits a unique solution; see, e.g.,
[
45
, Theorem 2.1]
and the references therein.
We here recall sufficient conditions from
[
45
]
leading to extra smoothness in space for the solutions to Maxwell’s equations that will be needed in the following derivations.
To the aim, given
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in\mathcal{H}:=[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}\times[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}
,
we first introduce an operator
\mathcal{A}
with domain

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\
\mathbf{v}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{H}\text{ such that }\mathbf{u}\in H_{0}(\curlbold,\Omega)\text{ and }\curlbold(\mu^{-1}\mathbf{v})\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}\right\},

where the operator
\mathcal{A}
is given by

\mathcal{A}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\
\mathbf{v}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-\varepsilon^{-1}\curlbold(\mu^{-1}\mathbf{v})+\varepsilon^{-1}\sigma\mathbf{u}\\
\curlbold\mathbf{u}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\forall\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{u}\\
\mathbf{v}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}).

Introduce

\mathbf{X_{N}}(\Omega,\varepsilon):=\{\mathbf{u}\in H_{0}(\curlbold,\Omega)\mid\varepsilon\mathbf{u}\in H(\div,\Omega)\},\qquad\mathbf{X_{T}}(\Omega,\mu):=\{\mathbf{u}\in H(\curlbold,\Omega)\mid\mu\mathbf{u}\in H_{0}(\div,\Omega)\}.

Let
\mathbf{E}
and
\mathbf{B}
be the solutions to (
4
).
Assume that
\mu
or
\varepsilon
is a constant function,
\sigma
,
\mu
, and
\varepsilon
are continuous, and
\sigma/\varepsilon\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
p>3
.
Further assume

\begin{split}&\mathbf{J}\in\mathcal{C}^{3}([0,T],[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}),\qquad\div\mathbf{J}\in C^{2}([0,T],[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}),\\
&(\mathbf{J}(0),0)^{T},\;\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{J}(0),0)^{T}+(\mathbf{J}_{t}(0),0)^{T}\in\mathbf{X_{N}}(\Omega,\varepsilon)\times\mu\mathbf{X_{T}}(\Omega,\mu).\end{split}

Then, as in
[
45
, Theorem
2.3
]
,
we have that
\mathbf{E}(t)
,
\mathbf{B}(t)
,
\mathbf{E}_{t}(t)
,
\curlbold\mathbf{E}(t)
, and
\curlbold\mathbf{E}_{t}
belong to
H^{s}(\Omega)
, for some
s>1/2
, for all
t\in[0,T]
.

3.1
Polyhedral meshes and mesh assumptions

Let

\{\Omega_{h}\}_{h}
be a sequence of mesh partitionings of the computational domain
\Omega
labeled by the subscript
h
,
which stands for the
mesh size parameter
.
Every mesh
\Omega_{h}
is a collection of open, bounded, simply connected polyhedral elements
K
such that
\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\overline{K}
.
The mesh elements are nonoverlapping in the sense that the intersection of any possible pair of
them can only be either the empty set, a set of common vertices, or a shared portion of their boundaries (which is a union of edges).
The mesh size parameter is defined as
h=\max_{K\in\Omega_{h}}h_{K}
, where

h_{K}=\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in K}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|
is the

diameter
of
K
.
Other characteristic lengths are the face diameters
h_{F}=\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in F}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|
,
which are defined for any mesh face
F
, and the edge lengths
h_{e}
, which are defined for any mesh edge
e
.
For all
h
, we denote the set of faces and edges by
\mathcal{F}_{h}
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}
.
Moreover, we denote the set of faces of an element
K\in\Omega_{h}
by

\mathcal{F}^{K}
and the set of edges of a face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
by
\mathcal{E}^{F}
.
Consistently with our previous notation,
\mathbf{n}_{K}
is the unit vector
pointing out of element
K
, and
\mathbf{b}_{K}
and
\mathbf{b}_{F}
are the
centroids of
K
and
F
.

Let
\gamma>0
. A face
F
is said to be
\gamma
-shape regular
if there
exists a a two-dimensional ball
B
with diameter
h_{B}
in the interior of
F
such that
h_{B}>\gamma h_{F}
.
Similarly, an element
K
is said to be
\gamma
-shape regular

if there exists a three-dimensional ball
B
with diameter
h_{B}
in the interior of
K
such that
h_{B}>\gamma h_{K}
.

In the rest of the manuscript we assume that all the meshes
\Omega_{h}
of a given sequence

\{\Omega_{h}\}
satisfy these conditions uniformly: there exists a real constant factor
\gamma\in(0,1)
independent of
h
such that

•

all the elements
K\in\Omega_{h}
and faces
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
are
\gamma
-shape regular;

•

\gamma h_{K}\leq h_{F}
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}^{K}
of every element
K\in\Omega_{h}
, and, analogously,
\gamma h_{F}\leq h_{e}

for every edge
e\in\mathcal{E}^{F}
of every face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
.

We assume that the (scalar and real valued) problem coefficients
\varepsilon
,
\sigma
,
and
\mu
in (
1
) and (
4
) are
piecewise continuous over
\Omega_{h}
.
As a consequence, we can approximate them by the three piecewise constant
functions
\widehat{\varepsilon}
,
\widehat{\sigma}
, and
\widehat{\mu}
given by, in every mesh element
K\in\Omega_{h}
,

\widehat{\varepsilon}_{K}:=\varepsilon(\mathbf{b}_{K}),\quad\widehat{\sigma}_{K}:=\sigma(\mathbf{b}_{K}),\quad\widehat{\mu}_{K}:=\mu(\mathbf{b}_{K}).

(6)

To perform the analysis of the method, we also need the additional
regularity condition that, for every element
K\in\Omega_{h}
,

{\mu^{-1}}_{|K},\,\,\,{\sigma}_{|K},\,\,{\varepsilon}_{|K}\in W^{1,\infty}(K).

(7)

On every mesh
\Omega_{h}
, we consider the broken Sobolev space of order
s\geq 0

\displaystyle H^{s}(\Omega_{h}):=\Big\{\mathbf{v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}\mid{\mathbf{v}}_{|K}\in[H^{s}(K)]^{3}\,\,\forall K\in\Omega_{h}\Big\},

endowed with the seminorm

\displaystyle|\mathbf{v}|_{s,\Omega_{h}}^{2}:=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}_{s,K}.

For all elements
K
, we define the local
L^{2}
-orthogonal
projector
\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}:[L^{2}(K)]^{3}\rightarrow[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3}
onto constant vectors as

\begin{split}(\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}\mathbf{E},\mathbf{c})_{0,K}=(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{c})_{0,K}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{E}\in[L^{2}(K)]^{3},\,\forall\mathbf{c}\in[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3}.\end{split}

(8)

Given a function
\mathbf{E}\in[H^{s}(K)]^{3}
,
0<s\leq 1
, we have the standard approximation property

\|\mathbf{E}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}\mathbf{E}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{s}|\mathbf{E}|_{s,K}.

3.2
Nodal virtual element spaces

Consider a mesh face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
and set

\mathbf{x}_{F}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}_{F}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in F.

(9)

The nodal virtual element space on face
F
is

\displaystyle V^{\NODE}_{h}(F):=\bigg\{v_{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{F})\mid\Delta v_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F),\;{v_{h}}_{|e}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(e)\,\,\forall e\in\mathcal{E}^{F},\;\int_{F}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{F}=0\bigg\}.

We use
V^{\NODE}_{h}(F)
in the definition of the nodal virtual element space
on element
K\in\Omega_{h}
, which is given by

\displaystyle V^{\NODE}_{h}(K):=\Big\{v_{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{K})\mid\Delta v_{h}=0,\;{v_{h}}_{|F}\in V^{\NODE}_{h}(F)\,\,\forall F\in\mathcal{E}^{F}\Big\}.

Every virtual element function
v_{h}\in V^{\NODE}_{h}(K)
is uniquely
characterized by the set of its values at the vertices of
K
, which
we take as the degrees of freedom . This unisolvence property is proved in
[
11
]
.
Then, we introduce the global virtual element space of the functions
that are globally defined on the computational domain
\Omega
and
have zero trace on
\partial\Omega
:

\displaystyle V^{\NODE}_{h}:=\Big\{v_{h}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\mid{v_{h}}_{|K}\in V^{\NODE}_{h}(K)\,\,\forall K\in\Omega_{h}\Big\}.

The degrees of freedom of
V^{\NODE}_{h}
are given by an
H^{1}
-conforming coupling of the local degrees of freedom, i.e., collecting all the internal vertex values.

For future reference, we also define the interpolant
v_{I}\in V^{\NODE}_{h}
of a function
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(\overline{\Omega})

as the unique nodal virtual element function with the same vertex values as
v
.
Formally, this definition reads as

\displaystyle v_{I}(\nu):=v(\nu)\quad\quad\text{for all vertices~$\nu$ in~$\Omega_{h}$.}

(10)

Upper bounds for the approximation error
v-v_{I}
are available in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
38
,
15
,
18
]
.

3.3
Edge virtual element spaces

Space definitions.

The edge virtual element space on face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}
is

\begin{split}\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(F):=\bigg\{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in[L^{2}(F)]^{2}\mid&\divF\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F),\;\rotF\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F),\\
&\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{t}_{e}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(e)\,\,\forall e\in\mathcal{E}^{F},\;\int_{F}\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{F}=0\bigg\},\end{split}

(11)

where
\mathbf{x}_{F}
is defined as in (
9
).
Next, we define the edge virtual element space on an element
K\in\Omega_{h}
as

\begin{split}\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K):=\bigg\{\mathbf{v}_{h}&\in[L^{2}(K)]^{3}\mid\div\mathbf{v}_{h}=0,\;\curlbold\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\in[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3},\\
&(\mathbf{n}_{F}\times\mathbf{v}_{h}{}_{|F})\times\mathbf{n}_{F}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(F)\,\,\forall F\in\mathcal{F}^{K},\quad\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{t}_{e}\text{~continuous at each edge~$e$,~}\\[10.00002pt]
&\int_{K}\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{K}\times\mathbf{p}_{0})=0\,\,\forall\mathbf{p}_{0}\in[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3}\bigg\},\end{split}

(12)

where
\mathbf{x}_{K}=\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}_{K}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in K
.
We note that
(\mathbf{n}_{F}\times\mathbf{v}_{h}{}_{|F})\times\mathbf{n}_{F}

corresponds to the projection of
\mathbf{v}_{h}{}_{|F}
onto the tangent plane to
F
.
The last integral constraints in (
11
) and (
12
) are required to allow for the computation of the
L^{2}
projector onto vector constant functions defined in (
8
); see
[
11
, Section 4.1.2]
for more details.

Every virtual element function
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K)
is uniquely characterized by the constant values of
\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{t}_{e}
on the
edges
e
of
K
, which we take as the degrees of freedom.
The unisolvence of this set of degrees of freedom for the space
\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K)
is proved, e.g., in
[
12
]
.
A noteworthy property of the local edge virtual element space is that the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector
\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}
defined
in (
8
) is computable from the degrees of freedom of the edge virtual element functions; see, e.g.,
[
12
]
.
We define the global edge virtual element space as

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}:=\Big\{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in H_{0}(\curlbold,\Omega)\mid\mathbf{v}_{h}{}_{|K}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K)\,\,\forall K\in\Omega_{h}\Big\}.

Bilinear forms.

As customary in the virtual element framework, we introduce local computable bilinear forms

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\EDGE,K}:\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K)\times\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

mimicking the
L^{2}
inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{0,K}

In particular, we first introduce the stabilizing bilinear form

(13)

Then, we define the local discrete counterpart of the
L^{2}
inner product as

\big[\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\big]_{\EDGE,K}:=(\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}\mathbf{u}_{h},\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}\mathbf{v}_{h})_{0,K}+S^{K}_{\EDGE}((\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h})\mathbf{u}_{h},(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h})\mathbf{v}_{h})\quad\forall\mathbf{u}_{h},\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K).

(14)

The local discrete bilinear forms
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\EDGE,K}

satisfies the stability condition

\big[\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}\big]_{\EDGE,K}\approx\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,K}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K)

(15)

and the consistency property

\big[\mathbf{p}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{h}\big]_{\EDGE,K}=(\mathbf{p}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{h})_{0,K}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{p}_{0}\in[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3},\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}(K).

(16)

Whereas property (
16
) follows from
definition (
14
), property (
15
)
requires the design of a suitable stabilization satisfying (
13
).
If we consider the stabilization

S^{K}_{\EDGE}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}):=h_{K}^{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{K}}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}^{F}}(\mathbf{u}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{t}_{e},\mathbf{v}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{t}_{e})_{0,e}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{u}_{h},\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}(K),

(17)

proposed in
[

, then the stability bounds (

) are proven in
[

.

We introduce the global discrete bilinear forms

\displaystyle[\widehat{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}]_{\EDGE}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\widehat{\varepsilon}_{|K}[\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}]_{\EDGE,K}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{u}_{h},\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h},

(18)

and

\displaystyle[\widehat{\sigma}\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}]_{\EDGE}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\widehat{\sigma}_{|K}[\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}]_{\EDGE,K}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{u}_{h},\,\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}.

(19)

In these definitions, we scale the local bilinear forms in the
right-hand side of (

) by
\widehat{\varepsilon}

and
\widehat{\sigma}
.
Moreover, in the forthcoming analysis, we shall employ the mesh-dependent norm

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}^{2}:=[\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}]_{\EDGE},

which is induced by the scalar product defined
in (

) by setting
\widehat{\varepsilon}=1

Interpolation properties.

We denote the interpolation in

of a given vector-valued

3.4
Face virtual element spaces

Space definitions.

Given an element
K\in\Omega_{h}
, we define the face virtual element
space as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K):=\bigg\{\bm{\psi}_{h}&\in[L^{2}(K)]^{3}\mid\div\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K),\;\curlbold\bm{\psi}_{h}\in[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3},\\
&\bm{\psi}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{F}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F)\,\forall F\in\mathcal{E}^{K},\\
&\int_{K}\bm{\psi}_{h}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{K}\times\mathbf{p}_{0})=0\ \forall\mathbf{p}_{0}\in[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3}\bigg\},\end{split}

where we recall that
\mathbf{x}_{K}
is defined as
\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{b}_{K}
for
all
\mathbf{x}\in K

Every virtual element function
\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K)

characterized by the constant values of
\bm{\psi}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{F}
on the
faces
F
of
\mathcal{F}^{K}
, which we take as the degrees of freedom.
The unisolvence of this set of degrees of freedom for the space
\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K)

[
12
]

A noteworthy property of the local face virtual element space is that
the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projector
\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}
defined
in (
8

of the face virtual element functions; see, e.g.,
[
12
]
.
We define the global face virtual element space as

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}:=\bigg\{\bm{\psi}_{h}\in H_{0}(\div,\Omega)\mid\bm{\psi}_{h}{}_{|K}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K)\,\,\forall K\in\Omega_{h}\bigg\}.

This definition includes homogeneous boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
.
The set of degrees of freedom of
\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}
is obtained via an

H(\div)
-conforming coupling of the local ones, i.e., by collecting

Bilinear forms.

As in the edge element case, we introduce local computable bilinear forms

mimicking the

\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K)

(22)

Then, we define the local discrete counterpart of the
L^{2}
inner
product as

\big[\bm{\psi}_{h},\bm{\phi}_{h}\big]_{\FACE,K}:=(\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}\bm{\psi}_{h},\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}\bm{\phi}_{h})_{0,K}+S^{K}_{\FACE}((\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h})\bm{\psi}_{h},(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h})\bm{\phi}_{h})\quad\forall\bm{\psi}_{h},\,\bm{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K).

(23)

The local discrete bilinear forms
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\FACE,K}
satisfies the stability condition

\big[\bm{\psi}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h}\big]_{\FACE,K}\approx\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,K}\quad\quad\forall\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K)

and the consistency property

\big[\mathbf{p}_{0},\bm{\psi}_{h}\big]_{\FACE,K}=(\mathbf{p}_{0},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{0,K}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{p}_{0}\in[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3},\,\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K).

(25)

Whereas (

) follows from
definition (

)
requires the design of a suitable stabilization satisfying (

).
We consider the stabilization, cf.
[
11
, (4.17)]

S^{K}_{\FACE}(\bm{\psi}_{h},\bm{\phi}_{h}):=h_{K}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{K}}(\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\bm{\psi}_{h},\mathbf{n}_{F}\cdot\bm{\phi}_{h})_{0,F}\quad\quad\forall\bm{\psi}_{h},\,\bm{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}(K).

(26)

The stability bounds (

in

Finally, we introduce the global discrete bilinear form

\big[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\bm{\psi}_{h},\bm{\phi}_{h}\big]_{\FACE}:=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\widehat{\mu}^{-1}_{|K}[\bm{\psi}_{h},\bm{\phi}_{h}]_{\FACE,K}\quad\quad\forall\bm{\psi}_{h},\,\bm{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}.

(27)

In the forthcoming analysis, we shall employ the mesh-dependent norm

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\bm{\psi}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\FACE}^{2}:=\big[\bm{\psi}_{h},\bm{\psi}_{h}\big]_{\FACE}\quad\forall\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h},

3.6
Two novel operators

We introduce two novel operators on the spaces
\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}

and
\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}
, which also satisfy a commuting diagram property;
see Proposition
6
below.
We begin by defining the weighted, global projector
\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}:H(\curlbold,\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}
as

\begin{split}&\begin{cases}[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\curlbold(\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}),\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE}=(\mu^{-1}\curlbold\mathbf{E},\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\mathbf{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}\\[5.0pt]
[\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E},\nabla s_{h}]_{\EDGE}=(\mathbf{E},\nabla s_{h})_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\mathbf{E}\in H(\curlbold,\Omega),\,\forall s_{h}\in V^{\NODE}_{h}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(34)

The discrete bilinear form
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\FACE}
appearing in the
first equation of (
34
) is well defined.
In fact, thanks to (
33
),

\curlbold(\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}(\mathbf{E}))
belongs to
\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K)
.
An analogous observation applies for the form
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\EDGE}

appearing in the second equation.

In order to prove the approximation properties of the projector
\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}
, we need two preliminary technical results.

Lemma 3

Consider
K\in\Omega_{h}
. Then it exists a real parameter
1/2<s\leq 1
, depending on the shape regularity constant of
K
, such that the following inverse inequality is valid:

|\bm{\psi}_{h}|_{s,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{-s}\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{0,K}\quad\quad\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}(K).

(35)

Proof.

Define
\Psi
as the solution to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\Psi=0&\text{in }K\\
\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\nabla\Psi=\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\bm{\psi}_{h}&\text{on }\partial K\\
\int_{K}\Psi=0.\end{cases}

Standard regularity results for elliptic problems, see, e.g.,
[
24
, Corollary
23.5
]
, entail that there
exists
1/2<s\leq 1
such that

|\Psi|_{s+1,K}\lesssim\|\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{s-\frac{1}{2},\partial K}.

(36)

Define
\mathbf{z}:=\bm{\psi}_{h}-\nabla\Psi
and observe that
\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\mathbf{z}=0
on
\partial K
.
We recall from
[
5
, Proposition 3.7]
that for all
\mathbf{z}\in H(\curlbold,K)\cap H(\div,K)

with
\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\mathbf{z}{}_{|\partial K}=0
, there exists
1/2<s\leq 1
such that

|\mathbf{z}|_{s,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{-s}\|\mathbf{z}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{1-s}\|\curlbold\mathbf{z}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{1-s}\|\div\mathbf{z}\|_{0,K}.

(37)

Recalling that
\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\mathbf{z}=0
on
\partial K
,
\div\mathbf{z}=\div\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)
,
and
\Psi
has vanishing integral on
K
, an integration by parts yields

(\mathbf{z},\nabla\Psi)_{0,K}=-(\div\mathbf{z},\Psi)_{0,K}+(\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\mathbf{z},\Psi)_{0,\partial K}=0\ .

Therefore

\|\mathbf{z}\|_{0,K}^{2}=(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{z})_{0,K}=(\mathbf{z},\bm{\psi}_{h})_{0,K}\leq\|\mathbf{z}\|_{0,K}\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{0,K}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{.}}

Thus,
(
37
) becomes

|\mathbf{z}|_{s,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{-s}\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{1-s}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{(\|\div\mathbf{z}\|_{0,K}+\|\curlbold\mathbf{z}\|_{0,K})}}.

(38)

We take the minimum
s
such that (
36
) and (
38
) are valid.
Using the triangle inequality, (
36
), (
38
),
the fact that
\curlbold(\nabla\Psi)=\mathbf{0}
and
\div(\nabla\Psi)=0
,
and the fact that
\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\bm{\psi}_{h}{}_{|F}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F)
for all
F\in\mathcal{E}^{K}
,

we easily obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\bm{\psi}_{h}|_{s,K}&\lesssim|\mathbf{z}|_{s,K}+|\Psi|_{s+1,K}\\
&\lesssim h_{K}^{1-s}(\|\div\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{0,K}+\|\curlbold\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{0,K})+\|\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{s-\frac{1}{2},\partial K}+h_{K}^{-s}\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{0,K}.\end{split}

We are left to show a bound for each term on the right-hand side in terms of
h_{K}^{-s}\|\bm{\psi}_{h}\|_{0,K}
.
We can prove such bounds based on employing polynomial inverse inequalities as, e.g., in the proofs of
[
7
, Proposition
4.1
, Proposition
4.2
]
.
The main ingredients are the fact that
\div\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)
,
\curlbold\bm{\psi}_{h}\in[\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)]^{3}
,

\mathbf{n}_{K}\cdot\bm{\psi}_{h}{}_{|F}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(F)
for all
F\in\mathcal{E}^{K}
, and inverse inequalities involving bubbles.
∎

The second auxiliary result is a coercivity property on the kernel of
edge functions

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{h}:=\left\{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}\mid[\mathbf{v}_{h},\nabla v_{h}]_{\EDGE}=0\;\forall v_{h}\in V^{\NODE}_{h}\right\}.

Lemma 4

The following coercivity property on the kernel
\mathbf{Z}_{h}
is valid:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\FACE}\gtrsim\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\quad\quad\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}.

(39)

Proof.

Given
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}
, let
p
be the solution to the following problem:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{find }p\in H^{1}(\Omega)\text{ such that }\int_{\Omega}p=0\text{ and}\\
(\nabla p,\nabla\xi)_{0,\Omega}=(\mathbf{v}_{h},\nabla\xi)_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\xi\in H^{1}(\Omega).\end{cases}

Set
\mathbf{v}:=\mathbf{v}_{h}-\nabla p
. We clearly have that

\div\mathbf{v}=0,\quad\quad\curlbold\mathbf{v}=\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h},\quad\quad\mathbf{n}_{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{|\partial\Omega}=0.

(40)

For a
1/2<s\leq 1
depending on the shape of
\Omega
, using
[
5
, Proposition
3.7
]
gives

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{s,\Omega}\lesssim\|\curlbold\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}\overset{\eqref{properties:function:v}}{=}\|\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(41)

Denote the nodal interpolant of
p
by
p_{I}
; see (
10
).
As in Remark
1
, the edge interpolant of
\nabla p

in the sense of (
20
) is such that
(\nabla p)_{I}=\nabla p_{I}.

Therefore, the edge interpolant of
\mathbf{v}
in the sense of (
20
) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{I}=\mathbf{v}_{h}-(\nabla p)_{I}=\mathbf{v}_{h}-\nabla p_{I}.

Next, recalling that
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}
, we observe that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{\EDGE}=[\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}]_{\EDGE}=[\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{I}+\nabla p_{I}]_{\EDGE}=[\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{I}]_{\EDGE}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{I}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}.

We deduce that

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{I}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}\overset{\eqref{stability:edge}}{\lesssim}\|\mathbf{v}_{I}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(42)

We estimate from above the right-hand side of (
42
) elementwise. Let
K\in\Omega_{h}
.
Using the triangle inequality and (
21
), we write:

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{v}_{I}\|_{0,K}&\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{s}|\mathbf{v}|_{s,K}+h_{K}\|\curlbold\mathbf{v}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{s+1}|\curlbold\mathbf{v}|_{s,K}\\
&\overset{\eqref{properties:function:v}}{=}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{s}|\mathbf{v}|_{s,K}+h_{K}\|\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{s+1}|\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}|_{s,K}.\\
\end{split}

We know that
\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}
belongs to
\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}(K)
; see (
33
).
Therefore, we can apply the inverse estimate (
35
), possibly taking the minimum among the scalar
s
in (
41
)
and the minimum over all elements of the parameter
s
appearing in Lemma
3
, and find that

\|\mathbf{v}_{I}\|_{0,K}\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{s}|\mathbf{v}|_{s,K}+h_{K}\|\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,K}.

(43)

Inserting (
43
) in (
42
) and
summing over all mesh elements yield

\begin{split}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\overset{\eqref{stability:edge}}{\lesssim}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}\|_{0,\Omega}+|\mathbf{v}|_{s,\Omega}+\|\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\overset{\eqref{using:Amrouche-Friedrichs}}{\lesssim}\|\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\overset{\eqref{stability:face}}{\lesssim}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\curlbold\mathbf{v}_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\FACE},\end{split}

which is the assertion.
∎

We are in the position of proving the approximation properties of the projector
\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}
.

Proposition 5

Let
\mathbf{E}\in H^{s}(\curlbold,\Omega)
,
1/2<s\leq 1
, and
\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}
be the projector defined in (
34
).
Then, the following inequality is valid:

\|\mathbf{E}-\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}\|_{\curlbold,\Omega}\lesssim h^{s}(|\mathbf{E}|_{s,\Omega}+h^{1-s}\|\curlbold\mathbf{E}\|_{0,\Omega}+h|\curlbold\mathbf{E}|_{s,\Omega}).

(44)

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}}
denote the interpolant of
\mathbf{E}
in the edge space
\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}
; cf. (
20
).
Then, for a given
\mathbf{E}\in H(\curlbold,\Omega)
,
\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}
is the solution to the following mixed variational problem:

\begin{cases}[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\curlbold(\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}),\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE}+[\mathbf{w}_{h},\nabla p_{h}]_{\EDGE}=(\mu^{-1}\curlbold\mathbf{E},\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega},\\[5.0pt]
[\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E},\nabla s_{h}]_{\EDGE}=(\mathbf{E},\nabla s_{h})_{0,\Omega}\qquad\forall\mathbf{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h},\;\;\forall s_{h}\in V^{\NODE}_{h}.\end{cases}

(45)

Indeed, it can be easily shown that
p_{h}=0
and the coercivity of the bilinear form
[\curlbold\cdot,\curlbold\cdot]_{\FACE}
on the kernel
\mathbf{Z}_{h}
is shown in Lemma
4
.
On the other hand, the discrete inf-sup condition for the bilinear
form
[\cdot,\nabla\cdot]_{\EDGE}:\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}\times V^{\NODE}_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a trivial consequence of
the fact that the virtual element spaces under consideration form an
exact sequence.
Therefore, we can use the standard analysis for mixed problems; see, e.g.,
[
17
]
.
Notably, there exist
\mathbf{w}_{h}
and
s_{h}
in
\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}\times V^{\NODE}_{h}
with

\|\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{\curlbold,\Omega}+\|s_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}\leq 1,

(46)

such that

\begin{split}&\|\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}-\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}}\|_{\curlbold,\Omega}=\|\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}-\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}}\|_{\curlbold,\Omega}+\|p_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}\\[5.0pt]
&\qquad\lesssim[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\curlbold(\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}-\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}}),\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE}+[\mathbf{w}_{h},\nabla p_{h}]_{\EDGE}+[\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}-\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}},\nabla s_{h}]_{\EDGE}\\[5.0pt]
&\qquad\overset{p_{h}=0}{=}[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\curlbold(\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}-\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}}),\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE}+[\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}\mathbf{E}-\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}},\nabla s_{h}]_{\EDGE}\\[5.0pt]
&\qquad\overset{\eqref{curl-projector-edge}}{=}(\mu^{-1}\curlbold\mathbf{E},\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h})_{0,\Omega}-[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\curlbold\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}},\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE}+(\mathbf{E},\nabla s_{h})_{0,\Omega}-[\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}},\nabla s_{h}]_{\EDGE}=:\big[I\big]+\big[II\big].\end{split}

Since the bounds for the two terms on the right-hand side follow
using standard VE calculations, we address them briefly.
Recall the definition of the projector
\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}
in (

\displaystyle\big[I\big]

\displaystyle\overset{\hskip 8.53581pt\eqref{consistency:face}\hskip 8.53581pt}{=}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\bigg((\mu^{-1}(\curlbold\mathbf{E}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}(\curlbold\mathbf{E})),\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h})_{0,K}-[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\curlbold\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}(\curlbold\mathbf{E})),\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE,K}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+((\mu^{-1}-\widehat{\mu}^{-1})\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}(\curlbold\mathbf{E}),\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h})_{0,K}\bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{stability:face},\eqref{living-on-the-ball}}{\lesssim}\Bigg(\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\bigg(\|\curlbold\mathbf{E}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}(\curlbold\mathbf{E})\|_{0,K}^{2}+\|\curlbold(\mathbf{E}-\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}})\|_{0,K}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\|\mu^{-1}-\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\|\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}(\curlbold\mathbf{E})\|_{0,K}^{2}\bigg)\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\hskip 5.69054pt\eqref{assumption:coefficients},\eqref{interpolation-estimates:edge}}{\lesssim}h^{s}|\curlbold\mathbf{E}|_{s,\Omega}+h\max_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|\mu^{-1}|_{W^{1,\infty}(K)}^{2}\|\curlbold\mathbf{E}\|_{0,\Omega}.

We proceed similarly for the term
\big[II\big]

\displaystyle\big[II\big]\overset{\hskip 8.53581pt\eqref{consistency:edge}\hskip 8.53581pt}{=}\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}\left((\mathbf{E}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}\mathbf{E},\nabla s_{h})_{0,\Omega}-[\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}}-\bm{\Pi}^{0}_{h}\mathbf{E},\nabla s_{h}]_{\EDGE}\right)

The assertion follows easily collecting the bounds on the terms
\big[I\big]
,
\big[II\big]
and by the triangle inequality.
∎

3.8
The fully-discrete scheme

We consider the fully-discrete approximation of (
4
) that is obtained by applying the backward Euler time-stepping scheme to the semi-discrete problem in (
50
).
Higher order schemes in time can be built analogously.
As customary, we start by splitting the time integration interval
[0,T]
in
N
equally spaced subintervals
[t^{n-1},t^{n}]
with size
\tau=t^{n}-t^{n-1}
for all

Moreover, let
\mathbf{E}_{h}^{0}=\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{I}}^{0}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}

and
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{0}=\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{I}}^{0}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}
be the virtual element
interpolations of
\mathbf{E}^{0}
and
\mathbf{B}^{0}
,
cf. (
20
) and (
28
), respectively
satisfying inequalities (
15
)
and (
24
).

Let
\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}
and
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}
be the discrete solutions at steps
m=0,\dots,N-1
.
We compute the two discrete vector fields at the time step

using the implicit Euler scheme:
find
\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m+1}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}

and
\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m+1}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}
such that,
for all
\mathbf{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h}
and
\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\tau}[\widehat{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m}),\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\EDGE}+[\widehat{\sigma}\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m+1},\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\EDGE}-[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m+1},\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE}=[\mathbf{J}_{I}^{m+1},\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\EDGE}\\[10.00002pt]
\frac{1}{\tau}[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m},\bm{\psi}_{h})]_{\FACE}+[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\bm{\psi}_{h},\curlbold\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m+1}]_{\FACE}=0.\end{cases}

(51)

The existence and uniqueness of a solution for problem (
51
) follows with standard arguments.

We can simplify (
51
) by rewriting the second equation as

\displaystyle[\widehat{\mu}^{-1}(\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m+1}-\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m}+\tau\curlbold\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m+1}),\bm{\psi}_{h}]_{\FACE}=0\quad\quad\forall\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}^{\FACE}_{h}.

Since
\curlbold(\mathbf{V}^{\EDGE}_{h})=\mathbf{\widetilde{V}}_{h}^{\FACE}
,
cf. (
33
), we deduce that

\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m+1}=\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m}-\tau\curlbold\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m+1}.

(52)

Then, we substitute (
52

\hskip 56.9055pt[(\widehat{\varepsilon}+\tau\,\widehat{\sigma})\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m+1},\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\EDGE}+[\tau^{2}\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\curlbold\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m+1},\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE}\\[5.0pt]
=[\tau\mathbf{J}_{I}^{m+1}+\widehat{\varepsilon}\mathbf{E}_{h}^{m},\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\EDGE}+[\tau\,\widehat{\mu}^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m},\curlbold\mathbf{w}_{h}]_{\FACE}.\hskip 56.9055pt

This reformulation allows us to reduce the computational effort in solving (
51
).

In view of Remark
1
and assumption (
2
), we find that

\div\mathbf{B}_{h}^{0}=\div\mathbf{B}_{\mathbf{I}}^{0}=(\div\mathbf{B}^{0})_{I}=0.

(53)

We use (
52
) and (
53
), and apply the divergence operator to derive the discrete counterpart of (

\displaystyle\div\mathbf{B}_{h}^{m}=0\quad\quad\forall m=0,\dots,M,

which implies that our scheme provides an approximation of
\mathbf{B}

that naturally satisfies the divergence-free constraint.

Thus, we are left to show (
77
), i.e., the upper bounds
on the terms
T_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,4
, in (
72
)-(
75
).
In the following bounds, we shall use the data assumption (
5
) several times.
Therefore, this will not be declared at every instance.
We can deal with the terms
T_{1}
and
T_{3}
as in the semi-discrete
analysis of Theorem
8
.
More precisely, proceeding as in (
59
)
and (
60
), we can write

\displaystyle T_{1}\lesssim h^{s}(|\mathbf{J}|_{s,\Omega}+h^{1-s}\|\curlbold\mathbf{J}\|_{0,\Omega}+h|\curlbold\mathbf{J}|_{s,\Omega}){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}\lesssim h^{s}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\varepsilon}^{1/2}\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{3}&\overset{\eqref{assumption:coefficients}}{\lesssim}h^{s}(|\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1})|_{s,\Omega}+h^{1-s}\|\curlbold(\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1}))\|_{0,\Omega}+h\max_{K\in\Omega_{h}}|\varepsilon|_{W^{1,\infty}(K)}|\curlbold(\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1}))|_{s,\Omega}){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}\\
&\lesssim h^{s}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\varepsilon}^{1/2}\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}.\end{split}

Next, we focus on the two remaining terms and start with
T_{4}
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{4}&:=(\mu^{-1}\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}(t^{m+1})-(\mathbf{B}(t^{m+1})-\mathbf{B}(t^{m}))/\tau,\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1})_{0,\Omega}\\
&\overset{\eqref{stability:edge}}{\lesssim}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{B}(t^{m+1})-(\mathbf{B}(t^{m+1})-\mathbf{B}(t^{m}))/\tau\|_{0,\Omega}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}\\
&=\|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}(s-t^{m})\partial_{tt}\mathbf{B}(s)ds\|_{0,\Omega}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}\\
&\leq\tau\|\partial_{tt}\mathbf{B}\|_{L^{\infty}([t^{m},t^{m+1}],L^{2}(\Omega))}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}\lesssim\tau{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\varepsilon}^{1/2}\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}.\\
\end{split}

As for the term
T_{2}
, we consider the splitting

\displaystyle\begin{split}T_{2}&=[\widehat{\varepsilon}(\bm{\Pi}_{h}^{\text{curl}}(\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1})-\mathbf{E}(t^{m})))/\tau,\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}]_{\EDGE}-(\varepsilon(\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1})-\mathbf{E}(t^{m}))/\tau,\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1})_{0,\Omega}\\
&\quad+(\varepsilon(\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1})-\mathbf{E}(t^{m}))/\tau,\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1})_{0,\Omega}-(\varepsilon\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1}),\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1})_{0,\Omega}=:T_{2,1}+T_{2,2}.\end{split}

The term
T_{2,2}
is dealt with as the term
T_{4}
:

\displaystyle T_{2,2}\leq\tau\|\partial_{tt}\mathbf{E}\|_{L^{\infty}([t^{m},t^{m+1}],L^{2}(\Omega))}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\lesssim\tau{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\varepsilon}^{1/2}\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE}.

Finally, we show an upper bound on the term
T_{2,1}
proceeding as for the term
T_{3}
:

\displaystyle T_{2,1}\lesssim h^{s}\|(\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1})-\mathbf{E}(t^{m}))/\tau\|_{*,\Omega}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\widehat{\varepsilon}^{1/2}\mathbf{e}_{h}^{m+1}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{\EDGE},

where we have used (
7
) again and set

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{*,\Omega}:=|\cdot|_{s,\Omega}+h^{1-s}\|\curlbold(\cdot)\|_{0,\Omega}+h|\curlbold(\cdot)|_{s,\Omega}.

We must prove that the *-norm of the difference quotient is finite. To
this aim, observe

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(\mathbf{E}(t^{m+1})-\mathbf{E}(t^{m})/\tau)\|_{*,\Omega}=\left\|1/\tau\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}(s)ds\right\|_{*,\Omega}\\
&\leq 1/\tau\int_{t^{m}}^{t^{m+1}}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}(s)\|_{*,\Omega}ds\leq\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T),H^{s}(\curlbold,\Omega))}.\end{split}

Collecting the bounds on the terms
T_{1}
,
T_{2,1}
,
T_{2,2}
,
T_{3}
,
and
T_{4}
, we deduce (
77
), whence the assertion
follows.
∎

In this section, we investigate the accuracy of the fully discrete scheme (

).
To this end, we consider three different mesh families:

•

•

•

7
Conclusions

In this paper, we have considered a low order virtual element approximation of Maxwell’s equations
based on a De Rahm sequence.
After developing some interpolation and stability properties of edge and face spaces,
we showed optimal a priori estimates for both the semi- and the fully- discrete schemes
and corroborated the theoretical predictions with numerical experiments.
Future works may cover the approximation of corner singularities and the virtual element approximation of MHD problems.
The extension to high order methods requires high order interpolation estimates and stability properties of edge and face VEM spaces, which is currently a work in progress.

On the interpretation of Λ \Lambda spin polarization measurements
Wojciech Florkowski, Radoslaw Ryblewski

Abstract
The physics interpretation of the recent measurements of the spin polarization of Λ \Lambda hyperons produced in relativistic heavy-ion collisions is discussed. We suggest that the polarization measured in the Λ \Lambda rest frame should be projected along the direction of the total angular momentum that is first transformed to the same frame, and only then averaged over Λ \Lambda ’s with different momenta in the center-of-mass frame. While this procedure does not affect the current measurements done in a broad transverse-momentum range, it may become important (represent a correction of about 10%) for the most energetic hyperons under study (with transverse momenta reaching 4–5 GeV/c). The proposed treatment is generally more appropriate for relativistic Λ \Lambda ’s. Throughout the paper, we deliver explicit expressions for various boosts, rotations, and transformations of angular distributions, which may help to compare model predictions with the experimental results.

To determine the magnitude of the polarization in different directions, however, one first studies distributions of various three-momentum components of protons emitted in the weak decay
\Lambda\to p+\pi^{-}
, which are measured in the
\Lambda
rest frame. As the COM frame and the
\Lambda
rest frame are connected by the Lorentz transformation depending on the three-momentum of
\Lambda
, the spatial directions in these two frames are linked by a non-trivial relation. Consequently, relating the results obtained in the
\Lambda
rest frame to the global angular momentum direction of the system requires that an appropriate Lorentz transformation is done before one describes such results in terms of the COM variables.

The STAR measurements
Adamczyk et al. 2017
;
Adam et al. 2018
;
Acharya et al. 2020
indicate that the proton distributions in the
\Lambda
rest frame are not isotropic and, consequently, unambiguously lead to the conclusion about the non-zero
\Lambda
spin polarization. In our opinion, however, the interpretation of those results in the context of specific correlations between the spin direction of
\Lambda
’s and various directions in COM (in particular, the direction of the total orbital angular momentum
{\bm{L}}
) requires further clarifications because of at least two reasons. First, typically only one component of the polarization vector is measured — the
y
-component in the
\Lambda
rest frame. Second, the
y
direction in the
\Lambda
rest frame is different from the
y
direction in COM.

This effect has been neglected in the experimental analyses of the spin polarization of
\Lambda
’s, with a non-relativistic assumption that these two directions are the same.
Consequently, a complete understanding of the relation between the
\Lambda
spin direction and the direction of the total orbital angular momentum in COM calls for a more detailed study of the effect connected with the boost to the
\Lambda
rest frame.

This is especially important if one interprets the result of the
\Lambda
polarization measurements as an analog of the Einstein-de Haas or the Barnett effect
Einstein and de Haas 1915
;
Barnett 1935
. In this case, we suggest first to measure the projection of the spin polarization along the orbital angular momentum direction that is “seen” by a
\Lambda
in its rest frame, and only then to make averaging over
\Lambda
’s with different momenta in COM. Such a method guarantees that the same physical direction is used for all
\Lambda
’s. We do not expect that such a procedure may change any qualitative conclusions about the global spin polarization but, in our opinion, it is more appropriate to establish the right magnitude of the polarization and its energy dependence.

We note that an alternative method for measurements of the global polarization of
\Lambda
’s has been proposed in Ref.
Siddique et al. 2019
, where one demonstrates that the measurements can use quantities defined in the laboratory frame (instead of the quantities defined in the
\Lambda
’s rest frame). However, this work does not discuss the effects connected with a change of the orbital angular momentum direction due to the boosts, which is the main topic of the present analysis.

In this work, we give several explicit expressions for boosts, rotations, and transformations of angular distributions that can be useful whenever model predictions are compared with the experimental results. In particular, we give an expression for the form of the angular momentum in the
\Lambda
rest frame that can be used to consistently project the polarization of
\Lambda
’s measured in their rest frames.

The canonical boost

We define the rest frame

-\frac{p^{3}_{\Lambda}}{m_{\Lambda}}&\alpha p^{3}_{\Lambda}p^{1}_{\Lambda}&\alpha p^{3}_{\Lambda}p^{2}_{\Lambda}&1+\alpha p^{3}_{\Lambda}p^{3}_{\Lambda}\end{array}\right].

Here

\Lambda
in COM, respectively, while

(6)

In particular, by construction we obtain

It is well known

III.2
Transformation of the system’s angular momentum

The crucial role in the discussion and interpretation of the spin-polarization measurements is played by the total angular momentum of the system described by the tensor
J^{\mu\nu}
. It can be decomposed into the orbital and spin parts,
J^{\mu\nu}=L^{\mu\nu}+S^{\mu\nu}
. In non-central heavy-ion collisions, a substantial non-zero orbital part
L^{\mu\nu}
is generated at the initial stage
Becattini et al. 2008

L^{\mu\nu}
is transferred to the spin part
S^{\mu\nu}

J^{\mu\nu}
being conserved. The generation of a non-zero spin part
S^{\mu\nu}
may be reflected just by the measured spin polarization of the produced particles. We note that
the spin part may be also generated at the very early stages of the collision but one expects anyway that the values of
S^{\mu\nu}
are negligible compared to
L^{\mu\nu}
.

If one works in the COM frame, only the spatial components of
L^{\mu\nu}
are different from zero.

\displaystyle{\bm{P}}^{\prime}

VI
Correlation with total angular momentum

VI.1
Improved formula for the projection

We have discussed above how the magnitude and direction of the spin polarization can be determined in the frame where
\Lambda
’s are at rest. More precisely, we have considered the rest frame of
\Lambda
’s with three-momentum
{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}
, which is obtained by the canonical boost from COM. A natural question at this stage appears, how the direction of the measured polarization is related to the axes of the COM coordinate system.

Equation (
53
) gives the prescription how to measure three independent components of the
\Lambda
polarization in its (canonical) rest frame. Assuming that the measurement of the averages
\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}_{p,x}\rangle
,
\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}_{p,y}\rangle
, and
\langle\hat{p}^{\prime}_{p,z}\rangle
is indeed possible, we may define the projection of the polarization along the direction of the total angular momentum by the expression

\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}=\left(1-({\bm{v}}_{\Lambda}\cdot\hat{\bm{L}})^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\hat{\bm{L}}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}-\frac{\gamma_{\Lambda}}{\gamma_{\Lambda}+1}\,{\bm{v}}_{\Lambda}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}\,\,\,{\bm{v}}_{\Lambda}\cdot\hat{\bm{L}}\right).

(57)

The direction represented by a unit vector
\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}
is the direction of the total angular momentum that is “seen” by the spin of the decaying
\Lambda
that has three-momentum
{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}
in COM. By construction
|\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}|\leq P^{\prime}\leq 1
.

The measurement of the
\Lambda
spin polarization is very often interpreted as an analog of the Einstein-de Haas and/or the Barnett effect
Einstein and de Haas 1915
;
Barnett 1935
. Except for the fact that these two phenomena describe the behavior of a different physical system, one important difference is that for these two effects there exists always a reference frame where all particles are at rest
6
6

6

Although it is typically a non-inertial rotating frame, the non-relativistic treatment allows for simple addition of polarizations of different particles.
. In the case of spin polarization of
\Lambda
’s, such a frame does not exist, since the analyzed
\Lambda
’s have usually different momenta in COM.

So far, our discussion has been concentrated on
\Lambda
’s with a given momentum in COM. For a given colliding system, beam energy, and the centrality class, such
\Lambda
’s can be treated as produced in the same physical environment (even if they are “taken” from different events) so it makes sense to obtain
\hat{\bm{L}}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
or
\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
from the proton distributions in
S^{\prime}({\bm{p}}_{\Lambda})
. The advantage of the expression (
57
) compared to the estimate of just
\hat{\bm{L}}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
is that the spin polarization of each
\Lambda
, irrespectively of its three-momentum
{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}
in COM, is projected on the same physical axis corresponding to
{\bm{L}}
in COM. Hence, Eq. (
57
) is in our opinion the proper object that can be used to study the relation between the polarization of all
\Lambda
’s with
{\bm{L}}
. To do so, one has to simply average
Eq. (
57
) over all
\Lambda
’s with different
{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}
.

VI.2
Numerical estimate of the relativistic effects

To make a numerical estimate of the effects discussed above, we consider the case where
{\bm{P}}^{\prime}=P^{\prime}\hat{\bm{L}}
and

\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}=P^{\prime}\left(1-v_{2}^{2}\right)^{-1/2}\left(1-\frac{v_{2}^{2}}{1+\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}\,\right)\equiv P^{\prime}F_{P}({\bm{v}})

(58)

where
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
are the components of the
\Lambda
velocity in COM and
v=\sqrt{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+v_{3}^{2}}
(to simplify the notation we skip here the subscript
\Lambda
). We further assume that the velocity distribution of Lambdas is thermal and described by the Fermi-Dirac distribution

F_{T}(v)=N\left[\exp\left(\frac{m_{\lambda}}{T_{\rm eff}\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}\right)+1\right]^{-1}.

(59)

Here
T_{\rm eff}
is an effective temperature and
N
is the normalization constant that is irrelevant for our study. The average value of
\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
for Lambdas with the momentum in the range between
m
GeV and
n
GeV is defined as the ratio

\langle\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}\rangle_{m-n}=P^{\prime}\,\,\frac{\int_{v_{(m)}}^{v_{(n)}}dv\int d\Omega\,F_{P}({\bm{v}})F_{T}(v)}{\int_{v_{(m)}}^{v_{(n)}}dv\int d\Omega\,F_{T}(v)},

(60)

where

v_{(n)}=\tanh\left[\sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{n\,\rm GeV}{m_{\Lambda}}\right)\right].

(61)

The numerical calculations performed with
T_{\rm eff}
= 150 MeV give:

\langle\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}\rangle_{2-3}=0.97~P^{\prime}
,

\langle\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}\rangle_{3-4}=0.94~P^{\prime}
,
\langle\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}\rangle_{4-5}=0.92~P^{\prime}
, and
\langle\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}\rangle_{5-6}=0.90~P^{\prime}
. Consequently, the relativistic effects studied in this work may reach 10% for the most energetic Lambdas studied at STAR. However, in the case of momentum-averaged results in the range
0.5-6\mathrm{GeV}/c
studied in Ref.
Adamczyk et al. 2017
one obtains a negligible correction
\langle\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{P}^{\prime}\rangle_{0.5-6}=0.997P^{\prime}
.

VI.3
Replacing the reaction plane angle by the experimentally-determined event plane angle

Equation (
57
) is an algebraic equation involving
\hat{\bm{L}}
, which makes the experimental determination of
\hat{\bm{L}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
more difficult than the measurement of
\hat{\bm{L}}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
alone. This is due to the fact that in the general case the reaction plane is characterized by the angle
{\Psi_{\rm RP}}
that is not necessarily equal to zero and the direction of the orbital angular momentum is defined by the vector

\hat{\bm{L}}=-\left(\cos\left({\Psi_{\rm RP}}+\pi/2\right),\sin\left({\Psi_{\rm RP}}+\pi/2\right),0\right).

(62)

We note that for
{\Psi_{\rm RP}}=0
this formula is reduced to Eq. (
28
) that has been used so far.

To measure
\hat{\bm{L}}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
in the case
{\Psi_{\rm RP}}\neq 0
we first perform averaging over the angles of the emitted protons and get

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{L}}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}=-\frac{8}{\pi\alpha_{\Lambda}}\langle\sin(\phi^{\prime}_{p}-{\Psi_{\rm RP}})\rangle=-\frac{8}{\pi\alpha_{\Lambda}}\int\left(\frac{dN^{\rm pol}_{p}}{d\Omega^{\prime}}\right)\sin\left(\phi^{\prime}_{p}-{\Psi_{\rm RP}}\right)\,d\Omega^{\prime}.

(63)

This equation introduces an explicit dependence of our results on the reaction plane angle
{\Psi_{\rm RP}}
which is not directly measured. To overcome this difficulty, the STAR experiment measures the azimuthal angle of the event plane,
{\Psi_{\rm EP}^{(1)}}
, determined by the directed flow. Consequently, one considers the observable

\displaystyle\langle\sin(\phi^{\prime}_{p}-{\Psi_{\rm EP}^{(1)}})\rangle_{\rm ev.}=\langle\sin(\phi^{\prime}_{p}-{\Psi_{\rm RP}}-({\Psi_{\rm EP}^{(1)}}-{\Psi_{\rm RP}}))\rangle_{\rm ev.}

\displaystyle=\langle\sin(\phi^{\prime}_{p}-{\Psi_{\rm RP}})\rangle_{\rm ev.}\langle\cos{\Delta\Psi}\rangle_{\rm ev.}\equiv\langle\sin(\phi^{\prime}_{p}-{\Psi_{\rm RP}})\rangle_{\rm ev.}R^{(1)}_{\rm EP},

(64)

where we introduced

\phi^{\prime}_{p}
and
{\Psi_{\rm EP}^{(1)}}
are correlated only with the reaction plane angle
{\Psi_{\rm RP}}
and uses the property
\langle\sin{\Delta\Psi}\rangle_{\rm ev.}=0
. The notation

means that one makes first averaging over different protons in one event and then makes averaging over a sample of events. The last equation in (
64

R^{(1)}_{\rm EP}
. Using Eq. (
64

\langle\hat{\bm{L}}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}\rangle_{\rm ev.}=-\frac{8}{\pi\alpha_{\Lambda}}\langle\sin(\phi^{\prime}_{p}-{\Psi_{\rm RP}})\rangle_{\rm ev.}=-\frac{8}{\pi\alpha_{\Lambda}}\frac{\langle\sin(\phi^{\prime}_{p}-{\Psi_{\rm EP}^{(1)}})\rangle_{\rm ev.}}{R^{(1)}_{\rm EP}},

(65)

which is the basis of the experimental approach (for example, see Eq. (19) in Ref.
Becattini and Lisa 2020

VII
Proton-proton collisions

At the end of this work, let us turn to a discussion of polarization measurement in proton-proton collisions
Bunce et al. 1976
;
Panagiotou 1990
. If the proton-proton COM frame corresponds to the case shown in Fig. 2, where the variant with a rotation in the production plane is chosen, the canonical boost is reduced to the form (
III.1
). Then, the four-vector describing the polarization in COM is obtained by the boost
{\cal L}_{3}\left(+v_{\Lambda}\right)
acting on the four-vector
(0,{\bm{P}}^{\prime})
. This leads to the expression

P^{\mu}=P^{\prime}\left(\gamma_{\Lambda}v_{\Lambda}\cos\Theta^{\prime},\sin\Theta^{\prime}\cos\Phi^{\prime},\sin\Theta^{\prime}\sin\Phi^{\prime},\gamma_{\Lambda}\cos\Theta^{\prime}\right).

(73)

At first sight, the interpretation of the spin polarization measurements in proton-proton collisions seems to be easier compared to the heavy-ion case. As the transverse components of
{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
are not affected by the boost one may try simply to add them and average over different
\Lambda
’s. This procedure, however, makes sense only if the
x
and
z
components of
{\bm{P}}^{\prime}
are zero. Otherwise, the results obtained for different
\Lambda
’s depend on the boost and the original transition to a rotated frame.

Consequently, if the spin polarization of
\Lambda
’s has non-zero
x
and
z
components it is suitable to use the frame without the rotation. In this case, we may follow the procedure discussed above for heavy ions, with the total angular momentum direction replaced by one of the other physical directions defined in the non-rotated COM frame that can be measured (for example, the direction perpendicular to the plane determined by the beam and the fastest proton produced). Such a procedure may be also useful in the case if more
\Lambda
’s are produced in one event.

VIII
Conclusions

In this work, we have discussed the interpretation of the recent measurements of the spin polarization of
\Lambda
hyperons produced in relativistic heavy-ion collisions. We have shown that the precise interpretation of the relation between the
\Lambda
spin direction (measured in the
\Lambda
rest frame) and the total angular momentum of the system (measured in the center-of-mass frame) requires that the direction of the angular momentum is boosted to the
\Lambda
rest frame. We have given the necessary formula that, we hope, may find its practical implementation in the polarization measurements. In particular, this expression may be used to average the measured polarization of
\Lambda
’s with different momenta in the center-of-mass frame. Several explicit expressions for boosts and rotations have been written out, which may help to compare model predictions with the experimental results.

Appendix A
Properties of the canonical boost

Of course, the three-momentum of the
\Lambda
hyperon in its frame is zero, however, it is possible to introduce the four-vector in COM that defines the direction of a moving
\Lambda
and has nonvanishing components in the
\Lambda

\lambda_{\parallel}^{\mu}=(\lambda_{\parallel}^{0},{\bm{\lambda}}_{\parallel})=\left(0,\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{\Lambda}\right).

(74)

After the canonical boost to
S^{\prime}({\bm{p}}_{\Lambda})
we obtain

\lambda^{\prime\,\mu}_{\parallel}=(\lambda_{\parallel}^{\prime\,0},{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}_{\parallel})=\gamma_{\Lambda}\left(-\frac{p_{\Lambda}}{E_{\Lambda}},\hat{{\bm{p}}}_{\Lambda}\right).

(75)

The property
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}_{\parallel}=\gamma_{\Lambda}{\bm{\lambda}}_{\parallel}
is usually interpreted as the conservation of the angles between the three-momentum of a moving particle and the frame axes by the canonical boost, as one has
{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}_{\parallel}/\lambda^{\prime}_{\parallel}={\bm{\lambda}}_{\parallel}/\lambda_{\parallel}
. The other two important four-vectors are:

\lambda_{\perp,1}^{\mu}=(\lambda_{\perp,1}^{0},{\bm{\lambda}}_{\perp,1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(p_{\Lambda}^{1})^{2}+(p_{\Lambda}^{2})^{2}}}\left(0,-p_{\Lambda}^{2},p_{\Lambda}^{1},0\right)

(76)

and

\lambda_{\perp,2}^{\mu}=(\lambda_{\perp,2}^{0},{\bm{\lambda}}_{\perp,2})=\frac{1}{p_{\Lambda}\sqrt{(p_{\Lambda}^{1})^{2}+(p_{\Lambda}^{2})^{2}}}\left(0,-p_{\Lambda}^{3}p_{\Lambda}^{1},-p_{\Lambda}^{3}p_{\Lambda}^{2},(p_{\Lambda}^{1})^{2}+(p_{\Lambda}^{2})^{2}\right).

(77)

The four-vectors
\lambda_{\perp,1}^{\mu}
and
\lambda_{\perp,2}^{\mu}
do not change under the canonical boost (
III.1
). The three-vector
{\bm{\lambda}}_{\perp,1}
represents the rotation axis for the rotation
{\cal R}_{\Lambda}
.

For example, any four-vector of the form

n^{\mu}=(0,{\bm{n}})=(0,n^{1},n^{2},n^{3}),

(78)

with the normalization
n^{\mu}n_{\mu}=-1
or, equivalently,
{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{n}}=1
, after the canonical boost we obtain

n^{\prime\,\mu}=\left(n^{\prime\,0},{\bm{n}}^{\prime}\right)=\left(-\frac{{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}\cdot{\bm{n}}}{m_{\Lambda}},\,{\bm{n}}+{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}\,\,\frac{{\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}\cdot{\bm{n}}}{m_{\Lambda}\left(E_{\Lambda}+m_{\Lambda}\right)}\right),

(79)

where
{\bm{n}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{n}}^{\prime}=1+({\bm{p}}_{\Lambda}\cdot{\bm{n}})^{2}/m_{\Lambda}^{2}
. Thus the direction
{\bm{n}}
in COM can be

One can check that

{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}_{\parallel}\cdot{\bm{n}}^{\prime}=\gamma_{\Lambda}^{2}\,{\bm{\lambda}}_{\parallel}\cdot{\bm{n}}\,.

(80)

Since

{\bm{n}}\cdot{\bm{P}}={\bm{n}}^{\prime}\cdot{\bm{P}}^{\prime}.

(81)

Hence, the four-vector

). In this section, we assume that a certain angular distribution of protons
dN_{p}/d\Omega^{\prime}
is known and construct the distribution of the proton projected momentum along an arbitrary direction in
S^{\prime}({\bm{p}}_{\Lambda})
. The obtained formula can be used to determine polarization in a given direction directly from the angular distribution
dN_{p}/d\Omega^{\prime}
. Note that if the distribution
dN_{p}/d\Omega^{\prime}
is isotropic, the proton projected momentum along any direction has a flat distribution that reflects no sign of polarization.

We start with the integral of the angular distribution and rewrite as follows (we are now in the frame
S^{\prime}({\bm{p}}_{\Lambda})
but for clarity of notation we skip the index prime, also the number of protons is normalized to one)

1=\int\left(\frac{dN}{d\Omega}\right)\sin\theta_{p}d\theta_{p}d\phi_{p}=\int\limits_{-1}^{+1}dc\int\left(\frac{dN}{d\Omega}\right)\delta(c^{*}-c)\sin\theta_{p}d\theta_{p}d\phi_{p}.

(82)

Here
\delta
denotes the Dirac delta function and
c^{*}
is the cosine of the angle between the proton direction defined by the angles
\theta_{p}
and
\phi_{p}
and an arbitrary direction defined by the angles
\Theta
and
\Phi
, hence

c^{*}=\cos\theta^{*}=\cos\Theta\cos\theta_{p}+\cos(\Phi-\phi_{p})\sin\Theta\sin\theta_{p}.

(83)

By construction
-1\leq c^{*}\leq+1
.

The distribution of the proton three-momentum direction along the direction specified by the angles
\Theta
and
\Phi
is defined by the integral

\frac{dN}{dc}=\int\left(\frac{dN}{d\Omega}\right)\delta(c^{*}-c)\sin\theta_{p}d\theta_{p}d\phi_{p}.

(84)

To do the integral on the right-hand side we introduce the function

f(c,\Theta,\Phi,\theta_{p},\phi_{p})=c^{*}(\Theta,\Phi,\theta_{p},\phi_{p})-c

(85)

and use the properties of the Dirac delta function to write

\frac{dN}{dc}=\int\limits_{0}^{\pi}\sin\theta_{p}d\theta_{p}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{dN}{d\Omega}(\theta_{p},\phi_{p})\left[\frac{\delta(\phi_{p}-\phi_{p}^{+})}{|f^{\prime}(\phi_{p}^{+})|}+\frac{\delta(\phi_{p}-\phi_{p}^{-})}{|f^{\prime}(\phi_{p}^{-})|)}\right]d\phi_{p}.

(86)

Here

f^{\prime}=\sin(\Phi-\phi_{p})\sin\Theta\sin\theta_{p}

(87)

and

\phi_{p}^{\pm}=\Phi\pm\arccos\left(\frac{c-\cos\Theta\cos\theta_{p}}{\sin\Theta\sin\theta_{p}}\right)

(88)

are the two solutions of the equation
c^{*}-c=0
. We note that it may happen that the solutions defined by Eq. (
88
) are outside of the range
(0,2\pi)
, however, since
f^{\prime}
and
dN/d\Omega
are periodic this does not lead to problems. As a matter of fact, this equation has solutions only if the following condition is satisfied

-1\leq\frac{c-\cos\Theta\cos\theta_{p}}{\sin\Theta\sin\theta_{p}}\leq+1.

(89)

This implies that the range of the integration over
\theta_{p}
must be limited — for given values of
c
and
\Theta
, only those values of
\theta_{p}
contribute to the integral (
86
) which satisfy (
89
). If we introduce the notation
c=\cos\theta
, with
0\leq\theta\leq\pi
, then the limits for the
\theta_{p}
integration are

\theta_{p}^{\rm min}=\max(0,\Theta-\theta,\theta-\Theta)\,\leq\theta_{p}\,\,\leq\,\min(\pi,2\pi-\theta-\Theta,\Theta+\theta)=\theta_{p}^{\rm max}.

(90)

Here we assumed that the range of the angles
\Theta
and
\theta_{p}
is between
0
and
\pi
. Consequently, the final result can be written as

\frac{dN}{dc}=\int\limits_{\theta_{p}^{\rm min}}^{\theta_{p}^{\rm max}}\left[\frac{dN}{d\Omega}(\theta_{p},\phi_{p}^{+})\frac{1}{|f^{\prime}(\phi_{p}^{+})|}+\frac{dN}{d\Omega}(\theta_{p},\phi_{p}^{-})\frac{1}{|f^{\prime}(\phi_{p}^{-})|)}\right]\,\sin\theta_{p}d\theta_{p}.

(91)

If the proton distribution is given by the weak decay law discussed above, the last formula may be interpreted as the inverse of the transformation that leads from Eq. (
50
) to Eq. (
51
). We have checked numerically that this is indeed so.

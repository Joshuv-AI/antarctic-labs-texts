The Two Point Function of the SK Model without External Field at High Temperature
Christian Brennecke, Adrien Schertzer, Changji Xu, Horng-Tzer Yau

Abstract
We show that the two point correlation matrix M = ( ⟨ σ i ​ σ j ⟩ ) 1 ≤ i , j ≤ N \textbf{M}=(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle)_{1\leq i,j\leq N} of the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model with zero external field satisfies lim N → ∞ ‖ M − ( 1 + β 2 − β ​ G ) − 1 ‖ op = 0 \lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{M}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}=0 in probability, in the full high temperature regime β < 1 \beta<1 . Here, G denotes the GOE interaction matrix of the model.

Introduction and Main Result

Consider
N
spins
\sigma_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, with values in
\{-1,1\}
whose interactions are described by the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick
[
11
]
Hamiltonian
H_{N}:\{-1,1\}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}

H_{N}(\sigma)=\beta\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\frac{\beta}{2}(\sigma,\textbf{G}\sigma).

(1.1)

The symmetric interaction
\textbf{G}=(g_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
is a GOE matrix, i.e. its entries are (up to symmetry) i.i.d. centered Gaussians of variance
N^{-1}
for
i\neq j
(we set
g_{ii}:=0
for simplicity), and
\beta\geq 0
denotes the inverse temperature. We assume the
\{g_{ij}\}
to be realized in some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and denote the expectation with respect to them by
\mathbb{E}(\cdot)
. We denote the
L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
norms by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=(\mathbb{E}\,|\cdot|^{p})^{1/p}
, for
p\geq 1
.

In this paper, we are interested in analyzing the behavior of the two point correlation matrix of the model at high temperature. The correlation matrix is given by

\textbf{M}=\big(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N},

(1.2)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes from now on the Gibbs expectation which is defined so that

\langle f\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}f(\sigma)\,e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{with}\hskip 14.22636ptZ_{N}=\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}

(1.3)

for every observable
f:\{-1,1\}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
.

As discussed recently in
[
1
]
(see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.4 & Remark 1.5]
and the references therein), standard mean field heuristics suggests that, at sufficiently high temperature, the correlations
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle,i\neq j
, satisfy the self-consistent equations

Notice that
\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle=0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
by the global spin-flip symmetry of the model.

\begin{split}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle&\approx\beta g_{ij}+\sum_{k:k\neq j}\beta g_{ik}\langle\sigma_{k}\sigma_{j}\rangle^{(i)}\\
&\approx\sum_{k}\beta g_{ik}\langle\sigma_{k}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\beta^{2}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle,\end{split}

(1.4)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle^{(i)}
denotes the Gibbs measure after particle
i
has been removed from the system. The right hand side in the second line of (
1.4
) is analogous to the well-known Thouless-Anderson-Palmer equations
[
13
]
for the magnetizations at high temperature and non-zero external field. It suggests, in a suitable sense to be made precise, that

\textbf{M}\approx\frac{1}{1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G}}.

(1.5)

Since the spectrum of a GOE matrix
G
is contained with high probability in
[-2;2]
, the simple mean field heuristics naturally suggests the (well-known) phase transition at
\beta=1
, without referring to replica arguments or the powerful Parisi theory
[
9
,
10
]
. This is our main motivation and giving a rigorous proof of (
1.5
) for all
\beta<1
is our main result. In the sequel, we denote by
\|\cdot\|
the standard Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
while
\|\textbf{A}\|_{\text{op}}
and
\|\textbf{A}\|_{\text{F}}
, for
\textbf{A}=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, refer to the operator and Frobenius norms, respectively. They are defined by

\|\textbf{A}\|_{\text{op}}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\|v\|=1\end{subarray}}\|\textbf{A}v\|\hskip 14.22636pt \text{ and }\hskip 14.22636pt\|\textbf{A}\|_{\text{F}}:=\bigg(\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}a_{ij}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\beta<1
and denote by
\textbf{P}=(p_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
the matrix whose diagonal entries are equal to one and whose off-diagonal entries are given by

p_{ij}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:\text{ self-avoiding}\\
\text{ path from }i\text{ to }j\end{subarray}}\;\prod_{e\in\gamma}\beta g_{e}

for
i\neq j
, where
g_{e}:=g_{i_{1}i_{2}}
for edges
e=\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
. Then it holds true that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{M}-\textbf{P}\,\|_{\emph{F}}=0\hskip 22.76228pt\text{ and }\hskip 22.76228pt\lim_{N\to\infty} \|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\emph{op}}=0

in the sense of probability. In particular, we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty} \|\textbf{M}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\emph{op}}=0.

(1.6)

Remarks:

1)

As a corollary of Theorem

, it follows that the norm of

is given by

\lim_{N\to\infty} \|\textbf{M}\|_{\text{op}}=\lim_{N\to\infty} \|(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}=(1-\beta)^{-2},

in the sense of probability. This follows, for instance, from
[
4
, Theorem C]
.

2)

Observe that the matrix
P
in Theorem
1.1
, that approximates

to leading order in terms of the Frobenius norm, emerges naturally by iterating the identity in the first line of (
1.4
) (and using that
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i}\rangle=1
for the diagonal entries of
M
).

3)

The boundedness of
\mathbb{E}\|\textbf{M}\|_{\text{op}}
has been conjectured in
[
12

, based on the simple observation that
m_{ij}\approx\beta g_{ij}
to leading order in

(for

). While writing up our manuscript, we noticed that this has been verified quite recently in an independent work
[
8
]
, combining the TAP heuristics (
1.5
) with well established higher-order asymptotics for overlap moments
[
12
,
5
]
. The main result of
[
8
]
shows that
\mathbb{E}\|\textbf{M}\,(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\|_{\text{F}}=O(1)
, implying
\mathbb{E}\|\textbf{M}\|_{\text{op}}=O(1)
. In particular, the entries of
\textbf{M}\,(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
are typically of size
O(N^{-1})
. This information is, however, not enough to conclude the norm convergence (
1.6
) in Theorem
1.1
. Inspired by
[
2
]
, our proof of (
1.6
) is instead based on a microcanonical analysis that determines the entries of
M
explicitly in terms of
G
. This enables us not only to determine the typical size of the entries of
\textbf{M}\,(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
, but also to conclude (
1.6
). In particular, our arguments are independent of
[
12
,
5
,
8
]
.

4)

The recent paper
[
6
]
establishes a logarithmic Sobolev inequality for the Gibbs measure induced by
H_{N}
under the stronger high temperature condition
\beta<1/4
. This implies in particular the boundedness of the norm of
M
. Whether the results of
[
6
]
or, possibly simpler, a spectral gap inequality can be proved for all
\beta<1
remains an interesting open question. Theorem
1.1
may be viewed as an initial step in this direction as it implies a spectral gap inequality on the space of linear combinations of the magnetizations
\sigma_{i}
.

5)

As mentioned earlier, we assume for simplicity that
g_{ii}=0
. Note that Theorem
1.1
remains valid if
G
in (
1.1
) was replaced by a standard GOE matrix
\widetilde{\textbf{G}}
which is such that
\widetilde{g}_{ij}=g_{ij}
for
i\neq j
and which has i.i.d. Gaussian diagonal entries
\widetilde{g}_{ii}
of variance
1/N
. In this case, the diagonal term in the interaction
\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}
is a constant independent of
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}
that does not affect
M
and one has

\lim_{N\to\infty} \|(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\widetilde{\textbf{G}})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}=0

in the sense of probability.

6)

Theorem
1.1
is trivial if
\beta=0
. We therefore assume from now on that
\beta>0
.

Let us briefly motivate the strategy of our proof. As shown in
[
2
]
, which studies the fluctuations
\Phi=\log Z_{N}-\frac{1}{4}\beta^{2}N
of the log partition function around its leading order contribution, one may think of
\log Z_{N}
for
\beta<1
heuristically as

\begin{split}\log Z_{N}&=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\log\cosh(\beta g_{ij})+\log\frac{1}{2^{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\big(1+\tanh(\beta g_{ij})\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\big)\\
&\approx\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\log\cosh(\beta g_{ij})+\sum_{\gamma\;\text{simple loop}}\log(1+w(\gamma)),\end{split}

(1.7)

where by simple loops, we mean simple, connected graphs
\gamma
with vertices in
\{1,\dots,N\}
each having degree two (see section
2
for the details) and with corresponding weights

w(\gamma)=\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\gamma}\tanh(\beta g_{ij}).

(1.8)

As a consequence of (
1.7
) we expect that up to negligible errors, we have for
i\neq j
that

\begin{split}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle=\beta^{-1}\partial_{g_{ij}}\log Z_{N}&\approx\tanh(\beta g_{ij})+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\gamma\;\text{simple loop}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\frac{1}{1+w(\gamma)}\\
&\approx\beta g_{ij}+\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\gamma\;\text{simple loop}}\;\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c} e\in\gamma:e\neq\{i,j\}\end{subarray}}\beta g_{e}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:\text{ self-avoiding}\\
\text{ path from }i\text{ to }j\end{subarray}}\;\prod_{e\in\gamma}\beta g_{e},\end{split}

(1.9)

where we used that removing the edge
\{i,j\}
from a cycle yields a unique self-avoiding path from vertex
i
to vertex
j
(note indeed that there is a one-to-one correspondence between cycles containing
\{i,j\}
and self-avoiding paths from
i
to
j
of length greater or equal to two). In other words, we expect that the correlation between spins
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{j}
can be written as the sum over weights of self-avoiding paths from
i
to
j
.

On the other hand, by standard mean field arguments, we also expect that

\big(\textbf{M}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big)_{ij}\approx 0.

(1.10)

That this is consistent with (
1.9
) corresponds, on a heuristic level, to the equivalence of the two approximations in (
1.4
), and it is indeed easily checked that e.g.

\mathbb{E}\|\textbf{P}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}=O(1).

(1.11)

For completeness, we outline the quick proof of (
1.11
) in Appendix
A
. By Markov, such a bound implies (
1.10
), in the sense that for
i\neq j
we have with high probability that

\big(\textbf{P}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big)_{ij}=O(N^{-1}).

Let us remark that proving the approximate identity (
1.10
) based on Frobenius norm bounds as in (
1.11
) is part of the main strategy of
[
8
]
. More precisely,
[
8
]
verifies (
1.11
) directly with
P
replaced by
M
, based on previously established overlap asymptotics. The quantity
O(1)
is furthermore shown to satisfy
O(1)=C_{\beta}+O(N^{-1/2})
for some explicit value
C_{\beta}>0
and the uniform Frobenius norm bound is used to deduce a uniform operator norm bound on
M
, in the sense that
\mathbb{E}\|\textbf{M}\|_{\text{op}}=O(1)
.

While such a strategy implies the operator norm boundedness of
M
, the information obtained from Frobenius norm bounds as in (
1.11
) is not enough to deduce the operator norm convergence

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{M}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}=0.

(1.12)

To approach this problem, the heuristic remarks from above suggest to analyze the matrix
\textbf{P}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
in more detail, anticipating that
\textbf{M}\approx\textbf{P}
. Following this idea, we derive an exact identity of the form

\textbf{P}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}=\textbf{P}\,\textbf{Q}^{\prime}+\textbf{Q}^{\prime\prime}

for two errors
\textbf{Q}^{\prime},\textbf{Q}^{\prime\prime}
which turn out to have vanishing operator norm in the limit
N\to\infty
if
\beta<1
. Using the boundedness of the resolvent
(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}
for
\beta<1
and rewriting

\textbf{P}\,\textbf{Q}^{\prime}=\big(\textbf{P}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big)(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\,\textbf{Q}^{\prime}+(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\textbf{Q}^{\prime},

this implies the norm convergence (
1.12
) with
M
replaced by
P
. To conclude (
1.12
), our main task is therefore reduced to justifying the approximation
\textbf{M}\approx\textbf{P}
in a sufficiently strong sense. Based on an exact graphical representation of
M
, that follows from the identity
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle=\beta^{-1}\partial_{g_{ij}}\log Z_{N}
and the graphical representation of
Z_{N}
from
[
2
]
, we do this in the Hilbert-Schmidt sense
\|\textbf{M}-\textbf{P}\|_{\text{F}}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. From the technical point of view, this is our main contribution, thereby extending the high temperature analysis of
[
2
]
and providing a direct explanation of the emergence of the resolvent of
G
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we set up the notation, we collect several preliminary results and we explain in detail the reduction step from
M
to
P
. Using this information, we conclude Theorem
1.1
in Section
3
by comparing the matrix
P
with the resolvent
(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}
.

In this section, we set up the notation following
[
2
]
, we collect several preliminary results and we determine the main contribution

We start with some notation. In the sequel,
\langle\cdot\rangle_{0}
denotes the expectation with regards to the law of
N
i.i.d. Bernoulli variables
\sigma_{i}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. Notice that
\langle\cdot\rangle_{0}
is obtained from
\langle\cdot\rangle
, defined in (
1.3
), by setting
\beta=0
in (
1.1
).

We denote by
\Gamma_{\text{s}}=\Gamma_{\text{s}}[N]
the set of simple graphs with vertices in
[N]:=\{1,\dots,N\}
: a graph
\gamma=(\mathcal{V}_{\gamma},\mathcal{E}_{\gamma})\in\Gamma_{\text{s}}=\Gamma_{\text{s}}[N]
is called simple for subsets
\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\subset[N]
(the vertex set of
\gamma
) and
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}\subset\big\{\{i,j\}:i,j\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma},\,i\neq j\big\}
(the set of edges of
\gamma
) if
\gamma
contains no isolated vertices (for every
i\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}
there exists some
e\in\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
with
i\in e
) and if the multiplicity
n_{ij}(\gamma)\in\{0,1\}
is at most one, for each edge
\{i,j\}
. We set
n_{ii}(\gamma):=0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
(self-loops are not allowed).
\Gamma_{\text{sc}}=\Gamma_{\text{sc}}([N])\subset\Gamma_{\text{s}}
denotes the set of simple, closed graphs with vertices in
[N]
: a graph
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
is called a simple, closed graph if it is simple and if the degree

n_{i}(\gamma)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{ij}(\gamma)\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}

of each vertex
i\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}
is even. By convention, we include
\emptyset\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
with weight
w(\emptyset):=1
.

From
[
2
]
, we recall the graphical representation of the log partition function

\begin{split}\log Z_{N}&=\sum_{1\leq u<v\leq N}\log\cosh(\beta g_{uv})+\log\bigg\langle\prod_{1\leq u<v\leq N}(1+\tanh(\beta g_{uv})\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v})\bigg\rangle_{0}\\
&=\sum_{1\leq u<v\leq N}\log\cosh(\beta g_{uv})+\log\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}}w(\gamma)\\
&=:\sum_{1\leq u<v\leq N}\log\cosh(\beta g_{uv})+\log\widehat{Z}_{N},\end{split}

(2.1)

where
w(\gamma)
is defined as in (
1.8
) (for every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{s}
). Analogously, one obtains a graphical representation of the two point correlation functions

\begin{split}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle&=\beta^{-1}\partial_{g_{ij}}\log Z_{N}=\tanh(\beta g_{ij})+(1-\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij}))\;\widehat{Z}_{N}^{-1}\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\!\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\end{split}

(2.2)

for
i\neq j
(recall that
\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i}\rangle=1
).

Besides
\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
, the set
\Gamma_{\text{loop}}\subset\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
of simple loops (or, equivalently, cycles) will be of particular importance. It is defined as the set

\Gamma_{\text{loop}}=\big\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:n_{i}(\gamma)=2\;\forall\;i\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\text{ and }\gamma\text{ is connected}\big\}.

Recall that a graph is connected if for every pair
i,j\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}
of vertices,
\gamma
contains a path from
i
to
j
. That is, there exist vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}
so that

\big(\{i,v_{1}\},\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\dots,\{v_{k-1},v_{k}\},\{v_{k},j\}\big)\in\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}^{k}.

We denote by
\Gamma_{\text{p}}\subset\Gamma_{\text{s}}
the set of simple, self-avoiding paths, that is, the set of connected
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{s}}
in which exactly two vertices
i,j\in[N]
,
i\neq j
, have degree
n_{i}(\gamma)=n_{j}(\gamma)=1
(corresponding to the end points of the path) and the remaining vertices have degree two. The set of self-avoiding paths with endpoints
i\neq j\in[N]
is denoted by
\Gamma_{\text{p}}^{ij}=\Gamma_{\text{p}}^{ji}
. As already mentioned in the introduction, note that every path in
\Gamma_{\text{p}}
with at least two edges can be identified uniquely with a cycle
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
by connecting its end points and, conversely, removing an edge from a given cycle in
\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
defines a unique path
\Gamma_{\text{p}}
with at least two edges and whose end points are the vertices of the edge that has been removed.

An important result that is frequently used below is Veblen’s theorem
[
7
, Theorem 2.7]
which states that every
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
is equal to an edge-disjoint union of a finite number of cycles
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
, which we write as

\gamma=\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}\circ\ldots\circ\gamma_{k}.

(2.3)

If
e=\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
, we set
g_{e}:=g_{ij}
, and from now on, by slight abuse of notation, we frequently write
e\in\gamma
for edges
e\in\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}
. Similarly, we write
|\gamma|:=|\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}|
to denote the number of edges in
\gamma
and by
\gamma\cap\gamma^{\prime}=\emptyset
, we abbreviate that two graphs
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
are edge-disjoint (i.e.
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\gamma^{\prime}}=\emptyset
). Finally, constants independent of
N
are typically denoted by
C,C^{\prime},c,c^{\prime},etc.
and they may vary from line to line in our estimates.

Let us now prepare the analysis of the two point functions by collecting several preliminary results. We first recall a crucial observation of
[
2
]
that tells us that large graphs have exponentially decaying
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm, if
\beta<1
. In the sequel, we frequently use this result with mildly diverging large graph cutoff
(\log N)^{1+\epsilon}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
for suitable
\epsilon>0
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
)
Let
\beta<1
, then there exists a constant
C=C_{\beta}>0
, which is independent of
N
, such that

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\emph{sc}}:|\gamma|\geq k}w(\gamma)\bigg)^{2}\leq C\exp\big(-k\log\beta^{-1}\big)

for every
k\geq 0
. Similarly, for every
i,j\in[N]
with
i\neq j
, it holds true that

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\emph{sc}}:\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma,|\gamma|\geq k\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg)^{2}\leq C\exp\big(-k\log\beta^{-1}\big).

Proof.

For the reader’s convenience, we recall the quick argument together with some useful facts about the weights
w(\gamma)
. We consider two cases.

First, if
k\leq\max\big(\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta^{2})^{-2},(16)^{2}/(\log\beta^{-2})^{2}\big)
, we bound

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\gamma|\geq k}w(\gamma)\bigg)^{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\gamma|\geq k}\mathbb{E}w^{2}(\gamma)\leq\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}}\big(1+\mathbb{E}w^{2}(\gamma)\big)&\leq\exp\bigg(\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\frac{\beta^{2j}}{2j}\bigg)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}}.\end{split}

In the first step, we used that the weights
w(\gamma_{1})
and
w(\gamma_{2})
of two different graphs
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
are orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and in the second step we used the observation from (
2.3
). Finally, in the third step we used the estimate (see
[
2
, Eq. (3.12)]
for the details)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:|\gamma|=j}\mathbb{E}w^{2}(\gamma)\leq\frac{\beta^{2j}}{2j}.

For
k\leq\max\big(\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta^{2})^{-2},(16)^{2}/(\log\beta^{-2})^{2}\big)
, we thus obtain trivially that

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\gamma|\geq k}w(\gamma)\bigg)^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\exp\big(-k\log\beta^{-1}\big),

where
C^{\prime}=(1-\beta^{2})^{-1/2}\max\big(e^{(1-\beta^{2})^{-2}\log\beta^{-1/2}},e^{(16)^{2}/(4\log\beta^{-1}})\big).

On the other hand, assume
k>\max\big(\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta^{2})^{-2},(16)^{2}/(\log\beta^{-2})^{2}\big)
s.t. in particular

\epsilon=\log\beta^{-2}(1-1/\sqrt{2k})>0.

In this case, we bound

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\gamma|\geq k}w(\gamma)\bigg)^{2}&\leq\sum_{l\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{l}\}:\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}},\\
\gamma_{i}\neq\gamma_{j},|\gamma_{1}| +\ldots+|\gamma_{l}|\geq k\end{subarray}}\;\;\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathbb{E}w^{2}(\gamma_{j})\\
&\leq\sum_{l\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{l}\}:\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}},\\
\gamma_{i}\neq\gamma_{j},|\gamma_{1}| +\ldots+|\gamma_{l}|\geq k\end{subarray}}e^{\epsilon(|\gamma_{1}| +\ldots+|\gamma_{l}|-k)}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathbb{E}w^{2}(\gamma_{j})\\
&\leq e^{-\epsilon k}\sum_{l\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{l}\}:\\
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}},\gamma_{i}\neq\gamma_{j}\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}e^{\epsilon|\gamma_{j}|}\mathbb{E}w^{2}(\gamma_{j})\\
&\leq e^{-\epsilon k}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}}\big(1+e^{\epsilon|\gamma|}\mathbb{E}w^{2}(\gamma)\big)\end{split}

and thus obtain that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\gamma|\geq k}w(\gamma)\bigg)^{2}&\leq\beta^{2k}\exp\bigg(-k\log(e^{\epsilon}\beta^{2})+\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\frac{e^{\epsilon j}\beta^{2j}}{2j}\bigg)\leq\exp\big(8\sqrt{k}-k\log\beta^{-2}\big).\end{split}

Since
k>\max\big(\frac{1}{2}(1-\beta^{2})^{-2},(16)^{2}/(\log\beta^{-2})^{2}\big)
, we have that
k\log\beta^{-1}\geq 8\sqrt{k}
and thus

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\gamma|\geq k}w(\gamma)\bigg)^{2}\leq\exp\big(-k\log\beta^{-1}\big).

Finally, setting
C=\max(1,C^{\prime})
with
C^{\prime}>0
from the first step concludes the first bound of the lemma. For the second bound, we use the rough estimate

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\\
|\gamma|=l,\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,\frac{w^{2}(\gamma)}{\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})}\leq N^{-1}\beta^{2l}\leq\beta^{2l}

and proceed similarly as in the first step.
∎

As indicated in the introduction, the proof of Theorem

\Phi:\mathcal{D}_{\Phi}\to\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
by

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{\Phi}&:=\big\{(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}\times\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\gamma\cap\tau=\emptyset\big\},\\
\Phi(\gamma,\tau)&:=\gamma\circ\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}},\forall\,(\gamma,\tau)\in\mathcal{D}_{\Phi}.\end{split}

(2.4)

Note that
\Phi(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
due to
\gamma\cap\tau=\emptyset
. Furthermore, for

, we set

S_{i_{1}i_{2}}:=\big\{(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}\times\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\{i_{1},i_{2}\}\in\gamma,|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\leq 1\big\}\subset\Gamma_{\text{loop}}\times\Gamma_{\text{sc}}.

Observe that

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Phi
be defined as in (
2.4
) and let
S_{i_{1}i_{2}}
be defined as in (
2.5
), for
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
. Then
\Phi_{i_{1}i_{2}}:=\Phi_{|S_{i_{1}i_{2}}}:S_{i_{1}i_{2}}\to\Gamma_{\emph{sc}}
is injective.

Proof.

We prove the more general property that if
\gamma=\Phi(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}
for
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in S_{i_{1}i_{2}}
and if there exist
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
with
\{i_{1},i_{2}\}\in\gamma_{1}^{\prime}
as well as
\gamma_{2}^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
with
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\cap\gamma_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
so that
\gamma
can also be represented as

\gamma=\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\circ\gamma_{2}^{\prime},

then it already follows that
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}=\gamma_{1}
and, as a consequence, that
\gamma_{2}^{\prime}=\gamma_{2}
(because
\gamma_{1}\cap\gamma_{2}=\emptyset
and
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}\cap\gamma_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
). To show that
\gamma_{1}^{\prime}=\gamma_{1}
, we infer from
\gamma=\Phi_{i_{1}i_{2}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
that
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
contains at most one vertex of
\gamma
that has degree greater than two. This happens if and only if
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
share exactly one vertex,
|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma_{1}}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\gamma_{2}}|=1
. Elementary arguments imply that removing this unique vertex disconnects
\gamma
into two connected components which are unique (if
|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma_{1}}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\gamma_{2}}|=0
, on the other hand, the claim is trivial).
∎

Lemma
2.2
shows that
\Phi_{ij}:S_{ij}\to\Phi(S_{ij})
is a bijection and we write

\begin{split}&\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}\in\Phi(S_{ij}):\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}},\\
\gamma_{1}\cap\gamma_{2}=\emptyset,|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma_{1}}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\gamma_{2}}|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{i,j\}\in\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}\setminus\Phi(S_{ij})\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}=:\Lambda_{ij}+R_{ij}^{(1)}.\end{split}

(2.6)

In the following, we analyze
\Lambda_{ij}
and
R_{ij}^{(1)}
separately. We find that the second term on the r.h.s. in (

O(N^{-3/2})
while
\Lambda_{ij}
is close to the r.h.s. in (
1.9
). To see this latter fact, recalling that
\Phi_{ij}:S_{ij}\to\Phi(S_{ij})
is a bijection and that
S_{ij}\subset\Gamma_{\text{loop}}\times\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
, we can split the summation in
\Lambda_{ij}
as

\begin{split}\Lambda_{ij}&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma\circ\tau\in\Phi(S_{ij}):\\

&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\gamma\circ\tau\in\Phi(S_{ij})\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)\bigg).\end{split}

Now, for fixed cycle
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
with
\{i,j\}\in\gamma
, we know that
\gamma\circ\tau\in\Phi(S_{ij})
or, equivalently,
(\gamma,\tau)\in S_{ij}
, if and only if

(implying
\gamma\cap\tau=\emptyset
) so that

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\gamma\circ\tau\in\Phi(S_{ij})\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:(\gamma,\tau)\in S_{ij}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}\end{subarray}}w(\tau)-\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\gamma\cap\tau=\emptyset,\\
|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)-\!\!\!\sum_{\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\gamma\cap\tau\not=\emptyset}\!\!\!w(\tau)\\
&=\widehat{Z}_{N}-\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau).\end{split}

Here, we used in the second step the definition (
2.1
) of
\widehat{Z}_{N}
and the fact that two graphs that share an edge also share at least two vertices.

To connect (
2.2
) with the r.h.s. of (

\begin{split}\Lambda_{ij}&=\widehat{Z}_{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}-R_{ij}^{(2)}-R_{ij}^{(3)}-R_{ij}^{(4)}+R_{ij}^{(5)},\end{split}

where the errors
R_{ij}^{(2)},R_{ij}^{(3)},R_{ij}^{(4)},R_{ij}^{(5)}
are given by

\begin{split}R_{ij}^{(2)}&:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|<k_{N}^{2}\}}}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|<k_{N}^{4}\}}\,,\\
R_{ij}^{(3)}&:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|<k_{N}^{2}\}}}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq k_{N}^{4}\}}\,,\\

(2.7)

for fixed
k_{N}
, specified below. Defining in addition the two errors

\begin{split}R_{ij}^{(6)}&:=\big(\beta g_{ij}-\tanh(\beta g_{ij})\big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c} e\in\gamma:e\neq\{i,j\}\end{subarray}}\beta g_{e}-\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg),\\
R_{ij}^{(7)}&:=\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\end{split}

(2.8)

and denoting the matrix corresponding to the r.h.s. of (

) by
\textbf{P}=(p_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
s.t.

\begin{split}p_{ij}:=&\begin{cases}\beta g_{ij}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c} e\in\gamma:e\neq\{i,j\}\end{subarray}}\beta g_{e}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\Gamma_{\text{p}}^{ij}\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c} e\in\nu\end{subarray}}\beta g_{e}&:i\neq j\\
1&:i=j,\end{cases}\end{split}

(2.9)

we conclude from the identities (

), (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) that

\begin{split}\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle&=p_{ij}+\big(1-\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})\big)\widehat{Z}_{N}^{-1}\big(R_{ij}^{(1)}-R_{ij}^{(2)}-R_{ij}^{(3)}-R_{ij}^{(4)}+R_{ij}^{(5)})-R_{ij}^{(6)}-R_{ij}^{(7)}\end{split}

(2.10)

for

(recall that for the diagonal elements, we trivially have that

).

In the rest of this section, our goal is to show that the errors

. For most of the bounds, it is sufficient to apply Lemma

to graphs which have a mildly growing size of order

. This yields faster than polynomial (in

norm of large graphs while small graphs can be controlled directly using combinatorial arguments. For definiteness, we denote by

from now on the large graph threshold

k_{N}:=(\log N)^{3/2}.

(2.11)

Let us start the analysis by showing that the terms
R_{ij}^{(k)}
for
k\in\{1,3,4,5,6,7\}
are small. To this end, we need some preparation. For
k\in\mathbb{N},l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define

A_{k,l}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|=k,|\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}|=k+l\},\quad A_{l}:=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}|-|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|=l\},

(2.12)

so that
A_{k,l}=\emptyset
if
l>\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)
(the complete graph of
k
vertices has
\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)
edges).

Moreover, for subsets
S\subset\Gamma_{\text{s}}
, let us denote in the sequel by

the sum of weights

w(S):=\sum_{\gamma\in S}w(\gamma).

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{N},l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the number of unlabeled simple closed graphs with
k+l
edges and
k
vertices is bounded by
C(k+l)^{2l}e^{C\sqrt{k+l}}
, for some universal constant
C>0
independent of
k
and
l
. As a consequence, for
A_{k,l}
defined as in (
2.12
), we have

|A_{k,l}|\leq C(k+l)^{2l}e^{C\sqrt{k+l}}N^{k}.

(2.13)

Proof.

The bound (
2.13
) follows from the first claim by noticing that any graph with
k
vertices can be assigned labels in
[N]
in less than
N^{k}
ways. So, let us focus on the claim that the number of unlabeled simple closed graphs with
k+l
edges and
k
vertices is bounded by
C(k+l)^{2l}e^{C\sqrt{k+l}}
for some universal constant
C>0
. To this end, note first that the number of unlabeled simple closed graphs with
k+l
edges and
k+l
vertices is bounded from above by the number of solutions
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k+l})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k+l}
of the equation

x_{1}+2x_{2}+...+(k+l)x_{k+l}=k+l,

(2.14)

which corresponds to the number of unlabeled simple closed graphs with
k+l
edges and
k+l
vertices allowing in addition for self-loops. Here,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
denotes the number of loops of size
i\in\mathbb{N}
contained in the graph. The upper bound via (
2.14
) simply follows from decomposing a given graph into an edge disjoint union of cycles (recall (
2.3
)) and to note that the cycles in the decomposition are in fact vertex disjoint if both the number of edges and vertices equal
k+l
(that is, the cycles characterize the unlabeled graph uniquely). Now, the number of solutions to (
2.14
) corresponds to the number of partitions of
k+l
, which is bounded by
Ce^{C\sqrt{k+l}}
(see e.g.
[
3
, Def. 1.2 & Eq. (5.1.2)]
) for some universal constant
C>0
. To conclude the claim, notice that every unlabeled simple closed graph with
k+l
edges and
k
vertices can be obtained from a simple closed graph of
k+l
edges and
k+l
vertices by merging
l
(not necessarily vertex-disjoint) pairs of vertices which can be done in less than
(k+l)^{2l}
ways.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\beta<1
, let
m,n\in\mathbb{N},l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let
k_{N}
be defined as in (
2.11
). Then, there exists
C=C_{\beta}>0
such that for every
\epsilon>0
, we have that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[w(A_{l})^{2}]&\leq C\max\big(N^{-l(1-\epsilon)},\beta^{k_{N}^{n}+l}\big)\,,\\
\mathbb{E}[w(\{\gamma\in A_{l}:i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\})^{2}]&\leq C\max\big(N^{-l(1-\epsilon)-m},\beta^{k_{N}^{n}+l}\big)\end{split}

(2.15)

for all
l\leq k_{N}^{n}
,
N
large enough and
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}\in[N]
. Similarly, we have that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{w(\{\gamma\in A_{l}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\})^{2}}{\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg]\leq CN^{-1}\max\big(N^{-l(1-\epsilon)},\beta^{k_{N}^{n}/2+l}\big)\end{split}

(2.16)

for all
i,j\in[N]
,
i\neq j
and
N
large enough.

Finally, if the number of vertices is bounded by
k_{N}^{n}
, we have the improved bounds

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[w(\{\gamma\in A_{l}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|\leq k_{N}^{n}\})^{2}]]&\leq CN^{-l(1-\epsilon)}\,,\\
\mathbb{E}[w(\{\gamma\in A_{l}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|\leq k_{N}^{n};i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{m}\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\})^{2}]&\leq CN^{-l(1-\epsilon)-m}\end{split}

(2.17)

for every
\epsilon>0
and
N
large enough.

Proof.

From the
L^{2}(\Omega)
orthogonality of different graphs in
\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
, we get the upper bound

\mathbb{E}[w(A_{l})^{2}]\leq\sum_{k=0}^{k_{N}^{n}}\mathbb{E}[w(A_{k,l})^{2}]+\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\gamma|>k_{N}^{n}+l}w(\gamma)\bigg)^{2}.

Combining Lemma
2.3
with the fact that

\mathbb{E}[w(\gamma)^{2}]\leq(\beta^{2}/N)^{k+l}

for every

, we get

\mathbb{E}[w(A_{k,l})^{2}]\leq C(k+l)^{2l}e^{C\sqrt{k+l}}\,N^{k}\,(\beta^{2}/N)^{k+l}\leq C(k+l)^{Cl}e^{C\sqrt{k+l}-(k+l)\log\beta^{-2}}N^{-l}.

Using this bound and Lemma
2.1
, we get for every
\epsilon>0

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[w(A_{l})^{2}]&\leq C(k_{N}^{n}+l)^{Cl}N^{-l}\sum_{k=0}^{k_{N}^{n}}e^{C\sqrt{k+l}-(k+l)\log\beta^{-2}}+C\beta^{k_{N}^{n}+l}\\
&\leq C(k_{N}^{n}+l)^{Cl}N^{-l}+C\beta^{k_{N}^{n}+l}\\
&\leq N^{-l(1-\epsilon)}+C\beta^{k_{N}^{n}+l}.\end{split}

Here, we used that
l\leq k_{N}^{n}
so that
\log(k_{N}^{n}+l)\ll\epsilon\log N
for every
\epsilon>0
and large
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

The second bound in (
2.15
) follows in the same way if we use that for

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}[w(\{\gamma\in A_{k,l}:i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\})^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}[w(A_{k,l})^{2}]}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\{V\subset[N]:|V|=k,\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}\subset V\}|}{|\{V\subset[N]:|V|=k\}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{(N-m)(N-m-1)\ldots(N-k+1)}{N(N-1)\ldots(N-k+1)}.

This follows from
\mathbb{E}[w(\gamma)^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[w(\gamma^{\prime})^{2}]
for all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in A_{k,l}
and the

orthogonality of different graphs in
\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
. The bound (
2.16
) follows with the same argument used to prove the second bound in (
2.15
) (for

), noting that we lose a decay factor of

in the rate (compared to the second bound in (

)), because we divide

and using that

for

uniformly in

, using that

and

. The second bound in (
2.17

).
∎

\begin{split}\sum_{k=3}^{k_{N}}\sum_{l=2}^{\frac{1}{2}k_{N}(k_{N}-1)}\sum_{\gamma\in A_{k,l}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma}\mathbb{E}\,\frac{w(\gamma)^{2}}{\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})}&\leq\sum_{l=2}^{k_{N}^{2}}\sum_{\gamma\in A_{l}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma}\mathbb{E}\,\frac{w(\gamma)^{2}}{\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})}\\
&=\sum_{l=2}^{k_{N}^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}\,\bigg[\frac{w(\{\gamma\in A_{l}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\})^{2}}{\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg],\end{split}

we apply Lemma
2.4
and conclude that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big(R_{ij}^{(1)}\big)^{2}&\leq CN^{-1}\sum_{l=2}^{k_{N}^{2}}\max\big(N^{-l(1-\epsilon)},\beta^{k_{N}^{2}/2+l}\big)+Ce^{-k_{N}\log\beta^{-1}}\\
&\leq CN^{-3+2\epsilon}+Ce^{-k_{N}\log\beta^{-1}}\leq CN^{-3+2\epsilon}\end{split}

for every
\epsilon>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough.

Next, consider the term
R^{(3)}_{ij}
. Applying Lemma
2.1
and Markov’s inequality for fixed
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
with
\{i,j\}\in\gamma
, we find for large
N
that

\mathbb{P}\bigg(\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq k_{N}^{4}\}}\Big|>N^{-k_{N}^{3}}\bigg)\leq N^{-k_{N}^{3}}\,.

Since the number of those
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
with
|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|<k_{N}^{2}
is bounded by
N^{k_{N}^{2}}
and since

\begin{split}\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\max_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}}|w(\gamma)|\geq N^{-3/2+\epsilon}\big)&=0,\\
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\big(\min_{i,j\in[N]:i\neq j}|g_{ij}|\leq N^{-3}\big)&=0\end{split}

for each
\epsilon>0
(see
[
2
, Eq. (3.5)]
for the first statement; the second follows from standard bounds on Gaussian integrals), a simple union bound implies for large
N
that

\displaystyle\max_{i,j\in[N]:i\neq j}|R_{ij}^{(3)}|

\displaystyle=\max_{i,j\in[N]:i\neq j}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|<k_{N}^{2}\}}}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq k_{N}^{4}\}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq N^{-k_{N}^{3}}\max_{i,j\in[N]:i\neq j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\bigg|\frac{w(\gamma)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|<k_{N}^{2}\}}}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg|\!\leq N^{2+k_{N}^{2}-k_{N}^{3}}\leq\!N^{-\log N}

with probability tending to one in the limit
N\to\infty
.

The bound on
\widehat{Z}_{N}^{-1}R_{ij}^{(4)}
is obtained from Lemma
2.1
, observing that

\widehat{Z}_{N}^{-1}R_{ij}^{(4)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma,|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|\geq k_{N}^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})},

so that for every
\epsilon>0
and large enough
N\in\mathbb{N}

\begin{split}\big\|\widehat{Z}_{N}^{-1}R_{ij}^{(4)}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq k_{N}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma,|\gamma|=n\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\,\frac{w^{2}(\gamma)}{\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})}&\leq CN^{-1}\sum_{n\geq k_{N}^{2}}(\beta^{2})^{n}\leq CN^{-3+\epsilon}.\end{split}

The large graph bounds from Lemma
2.1
imply furthermore that

\begin{split}\big\|R_{ij}^{(5)}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}&=\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|\geq k_{N}^{2}\}}}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\gamma\circ\tau\in\Phi(S_{ij})\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)\bigg)^{2}\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma^{\prime},|\gamma^{\prime}|\geq k_{N}^{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma^{\prime})}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg)^{2}\\
&\leq Ce^{-k_{N}^{2}\log\beta^{-1}}\leq N^{-3+\epsilon},\end{split}

recalling that
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma\circ\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
if
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
and
\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
are such that
\gamma\circ\tau\in\Phi(S_{ij})
and using the
L^{2}(\Omega)
orthogonality of different graphs in
\Gamma_{\text{sc}}
.

Finally, the
L^{2}(\Omega)
estimates on
R^{(6)}_{ij}
and
R^{(7)}_{ij}
are straightforward. Indeed, combining a simple second moment computation with the Taylor expansion

\tanh(\beta g_{uv})-\beta g_{uv}=-\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\tanh^{2}(s\beta g_{uv})\beta g_{uv}=O(g_{uv}^{3}),

we obtain on the one hand that

\begin{split}\big\|R_{ij}^{(6)}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq\big\|\beta g_{ij}-\tanh(\beta g_{ij})\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum_{k=3}^{N}\bigg\|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\\
|\gamma|=n,\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c} e\in\gamma:e\neq\{i,j\}\end{subarray}}\beta g_{e}-\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c} e\in\gamma:e\neq\{i,j\}\end{subarray}}\tanh(\beta g_{e})\bigg)\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq CN^{-3/2}+CN^{-3/2}\sum_{n\geq 3}\beta^{n}\leq CN^{-3/2}.\end{split}

On the other hand, proceeding similarly as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
and using that
\mathbb{E}\tanh^{4}(\beta g_{ij})\leq C\,\mathbb{E}\,g_{ij}^{4}\,\leq CN^{-2}
, we readily find that

\begin{split}\big\|R_{ij}^{(7)}\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}&=\mathbb{E}\bigg(\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg)^{2}\\
&\leq CN^{-2}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\bigg)^{2}\leq CN^{-3}.\qed\end{split}

Let us now switch to the analysis of

. We recall from (

R_{ij}^{(2)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\{i,j\}\in\gamma\end{subarray}}\frac{w(\gamma)}{\tanh(\beta g_{ij})}\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|<k_{N}^{2}\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\!\!\!w(\tau)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|<k_{N}^{4}\}}.

In order to simplify the following discussion, which is based on certain graphical considerations, let us define the related error

\widetilde{R}_{ij}^{(2)}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}},\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma,|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}w(\gamma)w(\tau)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}|<k_{N}^{2},|\mathcal{V}_{\tau}|<k_{N}^{4}\}}.

(2.19)

To relate
\widetilde{R}_{ij}^{(2)}
to
R_{ij}^{(2)}
, denote by
\mathbb{E}_{ij}(\cdot)
the expectation with regards to
g_{ij}
. Then

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{ij}(R_{ij}^{(2)})^{2}&=\mathbb{E}_{ij}\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}},\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma,\{i,j\}\in\tau,\\

&\hskip 14.22636pt+\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}},\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\\

where we used that the odd moments of
\tanh(\beta g_{ij})
vanish. Combining this with Cauchy-Schwarz, which shows that
\big(\mathbb{E}_{ij}\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})\big)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}_{ij}\tanh^{4}(\beta g_{ij})

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{ij}(R_{ij}^{(2)})^{2}&\leq CN\,\mathbb{E}_{ij}\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})\Big(\mathbb{E}_{ij}(R_{ij}^{(2)})^{2}\Big)\\

&\hskip 14.22636pt+CN\,\mathbb{E}_{ij}\tanh^{2}(\beta g_{ij})\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}},\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}:\\
\{i,j\}\in\gamma,\{i,j\}\not\in\tau,\\

for some

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}(R_{ij}^{(2)})^{2}\leq CN\,\mathbb{E}\big(\widetilde{R}_{ij}^{(2)}\big)^{2}.\end{split}

Hence, from now on let us focus on controlling the r.h.s. in (

\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}
and
\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}

(\gamma,\tau)\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}\times\Gamma_{\text{sc}}

\gamma\circ\tau
(edges may have multiplicity greater than one) which are built up from cycles
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}

\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}

\gamma\circ\tau

1.8

\begin{split}\psi:\Gamma_{\text{loop}}\times\Gamma_{\text{sc}}&\to\Gamma_{\text{s}}^{2},\hskip 14.22636pt(\gamma,\tau)\mapsto\psi(\gamma,\tau):=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2}),\end{split}

\psi_{1}=\psi_{1}(\gamma\circ\tau)
and
\psi_{2}=\psi_{2}(\gamma\circ\tau)
are defined so that

\gamma\circ\tau=\psi_{1}\circ\psi_{2}\circ\psi_{2}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{ and }\hskip 14.22636pt\psi_{1}\cap\psi_{2}=\emptyset.

(2.21)

In other words,
\psi_{1}(\gamma\circ\tau)
denotes the simple graph that consists of the edges that have multiplicity one in
\gamma\circ\tau
, and
\psi_{2}(\gamma\circ\tau)
denotes the simple graph consisting of the edges which have multiplicity two in
\gamma\circ\tau

\gamma,\tau\in\Gamma_{\text{sc}}

Proof.

We start with a general bound on

\mathbb{E}[Z^{4}]=3\sigma^{4}
for centered Gaussian random variables

, which implies

\mathbb{E}[w^{2}(\eta_{2})w^{2}(\eta_{2}^{\prime})]=3^{|\eta_{2}\cap\eta_{2}^{\prime}|}\mathbb{E}\big(w^{2}(\eta_{2})\big)\mathbb{E}\big(w^{2}(\eta_{2}^{\prime})\big).

as the set of interior points of the paths in
\Gamma_{\text{p}}

\eta_{2}

\mathcal{V}_{\eta_{1}}

In this section we conclude the proof of Theorem

. Throughout this section, we assume that for some sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, we have that

\|\beta\textbf{G}\|_{\text{op}}\leq 2\beta+(1-\beta)^{2}\epsilon/2.

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
4

) that this holds true on a set of probability at least
1-o(1)

o(1)
(that depends on
\epsilon
) such that
\lim_{N\to\infty}o(1)=0
.

As a consequence of Corollary
2.10
, Theorem
1.1
follows if we prove that
\textbf{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

2.9
), converges in norm to
(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}
. The assumption on
\|\textbf{G}\|_{\text{op}}
implies
\|(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}\leq(1-\beta)^{-2}(1+\epsilon)
s.t. it is enough to prove that the matrix

\textbf{Q}=(q_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}:=\textbf{P}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

(3.1)

converges in norm to zero. Before providing the details on this, let us point out two crucial cancellation mechanisms that readily indicate the smallness of
Q
. To ease the notation, here and from now on all summations over matrix indices run over
[N]
(recall also that
g_{ii}=0
). Using the graphical representation of
p_{ij}
for
i\neq j

\begin{split}q_{ij}&=p_{ij}-\delta_{ij}-\beta g_{ij}+\beta^{2}p_{ij}-\beta\sum_{k:k\neq i}p_{ik}g_{kj}\\

(3.2)

and our main task is to show that the main contributions to the first two terms on the r.h.s. of (
3.2
) are cancelled by the last term on the r.h.s. of (
3.2
), that is, by the sum

\beta\sum_{k:k\neq i}p_{ik}g_{kj}.

(3.3)

These cancellations are based on two mechanisms: for
i\neq k
, the matrix element
p_{ik}
consists of a sum over the weights
\prod_{e\in\gamma}\beta g_{e}
of the self-avoiding paths
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\text{p}}^{ik}
from vertex

to
k
. Such paths
\gamma
either contain the edge

or they do not contain

to vertex

of length greater than two, indicating a cancellation of such contributions with the first term on the r.h.s. of (

from vertex

to

. If we factor out
\beta g_{kj}
from

, the law of large numbers

\beta^{2}\sum_{k:k\neq i}g_{kj}^{2}\approx\beta^{2}

indicates a cancellation of such contributions with the term
\beta^{2}p_{ij}
on the r.h.s. of (

(3.19)

does only contribute to the fourth moment if the multiplicity

of each edge is even, and since

consists of four cycles, we have in this case in fact

Now, notice that the contribution of each multi-graph
\gamma=\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2}\circ\gamma_{3}\circ\gamma_{4}

\pi:[N]\to[N]

\gamma

\gamma^{\prime}

\pi:[N]\to[N]

Now, the number of elements in an equivalence class is bounded by

, because all multi-graphs have vertices in

and because each graph contains

, by assumption. This assumption also implies that

for each

. Together with the fact that the degree

(because
\gamma
is the product of cycles and every edge must occur at least twice to give a non-zero contribution under

), we find

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\big(q_{\leq k,ii}^{(2)}\big)^{4}\leq(3\beta^{2})^{2k}N^{-1}\sum_{[\gamma]}N^{|\mathcal{V}_{[\gamma]}|-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}([\gamma])}\leq(3\beta^{2})^{2k}N^{-2}\sum_{[\gamma]}\leq(3\beta^{2})^{2k}C^{\prime}_{k}N^{-2}.\end{split}

Here, we used that the number of multi-graphs with

edges is bounded by some

determined uniquely by

(in particular independent of

). This proves (
3.17

\displaystyle\leq 4\,\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma_{\text{loop}}:\\

and where we relaxed the large graph constraint to
|\gamma_{2}|\leq k_{N}^{4}
, as all cross terms give positive contributions under taking the expectation, by Wick’s rule. Now, it is clear that the r.h.s. in the last bound can be treated in the same way as
\mathbb{E}(R^{(2)}_{ij})^{2}
in Corollary
2.8
(the differences to
R^{(2)}_{ij}
are only the graph size constraints and the fact that the edge weights
\tanh(\beta g_{e})
are replaced by
\beta g_{e}
; both differences do not affect the arguments in the proof of Corollary
2.8
). Thus

\mathbb{E}\big(\big(q_{\leq k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2},ij}^{(6)}\big)^{2}\big)\leq CN^{-3+\epsilon}

for every
\epsilon>0
small enough and
N
large enough.

Similarly, the contribution
q_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2},ij}^{(6)}
can be controlled like the term
R^{(3)}_{ij}
, defined in (
2.7
), as in the proof of Lemma
2.5
. This yields the probability estimate in (
3.18
).
∎

We are now ready to conclude Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Corollary
2.10
, it is enough to show that

\|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
, in the sense of probability. Using
\|\beta\textbf{G}\|_{\text{op}}<2\beta+(1-\beta^{2})\epsilon/2
, we first bound

\begin{split}\|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}&\leq\|(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}\|\textbf{Q}\|_{\text{op}}\leq(1-\beta)^{-2}(1+\epsilon)\|\textbf{Q}\|_{\text{op}}\end{split}

so that, by (
3.15
) and
\|\textbf{A}\textbf{B}\|_{\text{op}}\leq\|\textbf{A}\|_{\text{op}}\|\textbf{B}\|_{\text{op}}
for all
\textbf{A},\textbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, we obtain that

\begin{split}&\frac{\|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}}{(1-\beta)^{-2}(1+\epsilon)}\\
&\leq\big(\|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}+C\big)\big(\beta^{2}N^{-1}+\|\textbf{Q}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{2}}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}^{(4)}\|_{\text{op}}\big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|\textbf{Q}^{(3)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}^{(5)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{1},k_{2}}^{(1)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{2}}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{1},k_{2}}^{(6)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}_{\leq k_{1},k_{2}}^{(6)}\|_{\text{op}}\\
\end{split}

(3.20)

for
C=(1-\beta)^{-2}(1+\epsilon)
and for fixed
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 3
. Choosing
k_{1}=k_{N}^{4},k_{2}=k_{N}^{2}
, defined in (
2.11
), s.t.
k_{N}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
, we make sure that the
k_{1}
- and
k_{2}
-dependent matrices on the r.h.s. converge to zero, in the sense of probability. Indeed, by (
3.18
), we get

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(6)}\|_{\text{op}}>\delta\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(6)}\|_{\text{F}}>\delta\Big)&\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{i,j\in[N]:i\neq j}|q_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2},ij}^{(6)}|>\delta N^{-1}\Big)\to 0\end{split}

as
N\to\infty
, for every
\delta>0
. Similarly, we obtain from (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(1)}\|_{\text{op}}>\delta\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{2}}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}>\delta\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\textbf{Q}_{\leq k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(6)}\|_{\text{op}}>\delta\Big)\\
&\leq\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(1)}\|_{\text{F}}>\delta\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{2}}^{(2)}\|_{\text{F}}>\delta\Big)+\mathbb{P}\Big(\|\textbf{Q}_{\leq k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(6)}\|_{\text{F}}>\delta\Big)\\
&\leq\delta^{-2}N^{2}\max_{i,j\in[N]:i\neq j}\Big(\|q_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2},ij}^{(1)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|q_{\leq k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2},ij}^{(6)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\Big)+\delta^{-2}N\max_{i\in[N]}\|q_{>k_{N}^{2},ii}^{(2)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\delta^{-2}N^{-1+\epsilon}\end{split}

for fixed
\epsilon>0
small enough, so that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(1)}\|_{\text{op}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{2}}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}_{\leq k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(6)}\|_{\text{op}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}_{>k_{N}^{4},k_{N}^{2}}^{(6)}\|_{\text{op}}=0.

Inserting this into (
3.20
), we conclude that

\begin{split}&\frac{\|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}}{(1-\beta)^{-2}(1+\epsilon)}\\
&\leq\big(\|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}+C\big)\big(\|\textbf{Q}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}^{(4)}\|_{\text{op}}+o_{1}(1)\big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\|\textbf{Q}^{(3)}\|_{\text{op}}+\|\textbf{Q}^{(5)}\|_{\text{op}}+o_{2}(1)\\
\end{split}

(3.21)

for two errors
o_{1}(1)
and
o_{2}(1)
that converge
\lim_{N\to\infty}o_{1}(1)=\lim_{N\to\infty}o_{2}(1)=0
in probability. Now, consider the remaining matrices
\textbf{Q}^{(2)}
,
\textbf{Q}^{(3)}
,
\textbf{Q}^{(4)}
and
\textbf{Q}^{(5)}
. Using that
\|\textbf{Q}^{(3)}\|_{\text{op}}\leq\|\textbf{Q}^{(3)}\|_{\text{F}}
, we obtain with the bounds (
3.16
) for every
\delta>0
that

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\big(\|\textbf{Q}^{(3)}\|_{\text{op}}>\delta\big)&\leq\mathbb{P}\big(\|\textbf{Q}^{(3)}\|_{\text{F}}>\delta\big)\leq\delta^{-2}N^{2}\max_{i,j\in[N]:i\neq j}\|q_{ij}^{(3)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\delta^{-2}N^{-1}\end{split}

so that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}^{(3)}\|_{\text{op}}=0
. The same argument implies
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}^{(5)}\|_{\text{op}}=0
and for
\textbf{Q}^{(4)}
, we notice that, by a standard concentration and union bound, we have

\mathbb{P}\Big(\max_{i\in[N]}\Big|\sum_{u}\big(g_{iu}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\,g_{iu}^{2}\big)\Big|>\delta\Big)\leq Ce^{-cN\delta^{2}}

for suitable
C,c>0
that are independent of
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
. Recalling the definition of
q_{ii}^{(4)}
in (
3.4
), this trivially implies that in the sense of probability we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}^{(4)}\|_{\text{op}}=\beta^{2}\lim_{N\to\infty}\max_{i\in[N]}\Big|\sum_{u}\big(g_{iu}^{2}-\mathbb{E}\,g_{iu}^{2}\big)\Big|=0.

Finally, consider
\textbf{Q}^{(2)}
. For
\delta>0
and fixed
k\geq 3
, we use (
3.17
) to estimate

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\big(\|\textbf{Q}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}>\delta\big)&\leq\mathbb{P}\big(\|\textbf{Q}_{>k}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}>\delta/2\big)+\mathbb{P}\big(\|\textbf{Q}_{\leq k}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}>\delta/2\big)\\
&\leq\delta^{-2}N\max_{i\in[N]}\|q_{>k,ii}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2^{4}\delta^{-4}N\max_{i\in[N]}\|q_{>k,ii}^{(2)}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}^{4}\\
&\leq C\delta^{-2}e^{-k\log\beta^{-2}}+C^{\prime}_{k}\delta^{-4}N^{-1}.\end{split}

Sending first
N\to\infty
and then
k\to\infty
, we conclude that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{Q}^{(2)}\|_{\text{op}}=0
.

Collecting the bounds from above and inserting them into (
3.21
), we conclude that

\begin{split}\|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}&\leq\frac{o_{1}^{\prime}(1)}{(1-\beta)^{2}(1+\epsilon)^{-1}-o_{2}^{\prime}(1)}\end{split}

for two errors
o_{1}^{\prime}(1)
and
o_{2}^{\prime}(1)
that satisfy
\lim_{N\to\infty}o_{1}^{\prime}(1)=\lim_{N\to\infty}o_{2}^{\prime}(1)=0
. In particular, we conclude that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|\textbf{P}-(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})^{-1}\|_{\text{op}}=0
in probability.
∎

Appendix A
Proof of the Identity (

In this appendix, we outline the key steps of the computation for

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\|\textbf{P}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}=O(1).\end{split}

(A.1)

Denoting by
\langle\textbf{A},\textbf{B}\rangle_{\text{F}}=\text{tr}\,\textbf{A}^{T}\textbf{B}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ji}b_{ji}
the standard Hilbert-Schmidt inner product for matrices
\textbf{A},\textbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, we have that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\|\textbf{P}(1+\beta^{2}-\beta\textbf{G})-\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}&=(1+\beta^{2})^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\,\|\textbf{P}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}+\beta^{2}\mathbb{E}\|\textbf{P}\textbf{G}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}+N-2(1+\beta^{2})\,\mathbb{E}\,\text{tr}\,\textbf{P}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-2(1+\beta^{2})\beta\,\mathbb{E}\,\langle\textbf{P},\textbf{P}\textbf{G}\rangle_{\text{F}}+2\beta\,\mathbb{E}\,\text{tr}\,\textbf{P}\textbf{G}.\end{split}

(A.2)

We compute each expectation on the r.h.s. of the previous equation separately.

First, using that
p_{ii}=1
and
\mathbb{E}\,p_{ij}=0
for all
i\neq j\in[N]
, we trivially have
\mathbb{E}\,\text{tr}\,\textbf{P}=N.

Using that the weights of distinct paths are orthogonal in

, we find next that

\mathbb{E}\,\text{tr}\,\textbf{P}\textbf{G}=\sum_{i,j:i\neq j}\mathbb{E}\,p_{ij}g_{ij}=\beta(N-1).

Similarly, we obtain that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\,\|\textbf{P}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}=N+\sum_{i,j:i\neq j}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}^{ij}}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\prod_{e\in\gamma}\beta g_{e}\bigg)^{2}&=N+\sum_{i,j:i\neq j}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}^{ij}}\frac{\beta^{2|\gamma|}}{N^{|\gamma|}}=\frac{N}{(1-\beta^{2})}+O(1).\end{split}

Note that, in the last step, we used that

|\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{p}^{ij}:|\gamma|=k\}|=\frac{(N-2)!}{(N-k-1)!}

for every
k\geq 1
. Computing the remaining two expectations that appear on the r.h.s. of (
A.2

for all

&=(1-\beta^{2})^{-1}\big((1+1-2)+(2\beta^{2}+\beta^{2}-\beta^{2}-4\beta^{2}+2\beta^{2})\\

&=0.\end{split}

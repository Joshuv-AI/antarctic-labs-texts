Some identities and properties on degenerate Stirling numbers
Taekyun Kim, DAE SAN KIM

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is by using generating functions to further study some identities and properties on the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate r r -Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind and the unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind.

1.
Introduction

Carlitz [4] obtained some interesting arithmetical and combinatorial results on the degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials and numbers, which are degenerate versions of the Bernoulli and Euler polynomials and numbers.
In recent years, studying degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials have drawn the attention of many mathematicians with their regained interests not only in combinatorial and arithmetical properties but also in applications to differential equations, identities of symmetry and probability theory (see [7,9,10,13-16,18,20] and the references therein). These degenerate versions include the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first and second kinds, degenerate Bernoulli numbers of the second kind and degenerate Bell numbers and polynomials. Especially, it turns out that the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first and second kind appear very frequently when we study degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials. It is noteworthy that studying degenerate versions is not only limited to polynomials but also extended to transcendental functions. Indeed, the degenerate gamma functions were introduced in connection with degenerate Laplace transforms in [12]. It is also remarkable that the degenerate umbral calculus is introduced as a degenerate version of the classical umbral calculus. Indeed, the Sheffer sequences occupy the central position in the umbral calculus and are characterized by the generating functions for Sheffer pairs where the usual exponential function enters (see [8]). The motivation for the paper [8] started from the question that what if the usual exponential function is replaced by the degenerate exponential functions in (
3
). With this replacement the
\lambda
-Sheffer polynomials are introduced, which are charactered by the generating functions for Sheffer pairs where the degenerate exponential function enters.

The Stirling number of the second
S_{2}(n,k)
is the number of ways to partition a set of
n
objects into
k
nonempty subsets (see (
2
)). The (signed) Stirling number of the first kind
S_{1}(n,k)
is defined such that the number of permutations of
n
elements having exactly
k
cycles is the nonnegative integer
(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k)=|S_{1}(n,k)|
(see (
1
)). The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)
(see (
10
), (
11
)) and of the first kind
S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)
(see (
9
), (
12
)) appear most naturally when we replace the powers of
x
by the generalized falling factorial polynomials
(x)_{k,\lambda}
in the defining equations (see (
1
), (
2
), (
9
), (
10
)).

Degenerate versions of special numbers and polynomials have been explored by various methods, including combinatorial methods, generating functions, umbral calculus techniques,
p
-adic analysis, differential equations, special functions, probability theory and analytic number theory. The aim of this paper is by using generating functions to further study some identities and properties on the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind and the unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 1, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.
The main results are obtained in Section 2.
Some identities on the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are derived in Theorems 1, 10, 13 and 14,
those on the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are stated in Theorem 2, those on the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are obtained in Theorem 4, 5, 7 and 8. Finally, certain identities on the unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are obtained in Theorems 3, 6, 9 and 15.

For
n\geq 0
, the Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(1)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-20]),

where
(x)_{0}=1
,
(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-(n-1)),\ (n\geq 1)
.

As the inversion formula of (
1
) the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(2)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,5,14,17]).

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential functions are defined by

(3)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}t^{k},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12,14]),

where the generalized falling factorials
(x)_{n,\lambda}
are given by

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\ (n\geq 1)
. Note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}\displaystyle
.

For
k\geq 0
, the Lah numbers are given by

(4)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,5,14,17]).

From (
4
), we note that

(5)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,5,14,17]),

where
\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\ \langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

By (
5
), we get

(6)

L(n,k)=\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{n-1}{k-1},\quad(n,k\geq 1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,7,10,11]).

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the generalized Laguerre polynomials
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
are given by

(7)

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}x^{-\alpha}e^{x}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\Big(e^{-x}x^{n+\alpha}\Big)

\displaystyle=x^{-\alpha}\frac{1}{n!}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}-1\bigg)^{n}x^{n+\alpha},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,7,17]).

When
\alpha=0
,
L_{n}(x)=L_{n}^{(0)}(x)
are called the Laguerre polynomials.

Let
\log_{\lambda}t
be the compositional inverse function of
e_{\lambda}(t)
. Then we have

(8)

\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-1}(1)_{n,1/\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7]).

We note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\log_{\lambda}(1+t)=\log(1+t)\displaystyle
.

It is known that the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(9)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7]).

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(10)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7]).

From (
9
) and (
10
), we note that
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{1}(n,k)\displaystyle
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=S_{2}(n,k),\ (n,k\geq 0)\displaystyle
.

From [13], we recall that

(11)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and

(12)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\quad(k\geq 0).

2.
Some identities and properties on degenerate Stirling numbers

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq 0
, we have

(13)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(t)\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}t^{l}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(j,k)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}S_{2,\lambda}(j,k)(1)_{n-j,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand,

(14)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(t)\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1+1\big)\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{k+1}{(k+1)!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k+1}+\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\Big((k+1)S_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1)+S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
13
) and (
14
), we get

(15)

\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}S_{2,\lambda}(j,k)(1)_{n-j,\lambda}=(k+1)S_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1)+S_{2,\lambda}(n,k).

From (
10
), we note that

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=(x)_{n+1,\lambda}=(x)_{n,\lambda}(x-n\lambda)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}-n\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}(x-k+k)-n\lambda\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k-n\lambda)S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k-1)(x)_{k}+\sum_{n=0}^{n}(k-n\lambda)S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\Big(S_{2,\lambda}(n,k-1)+(k-n\lambda)S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\Big)(x)_{k}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
7
), we have

(17)

S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k)=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k-1)+(k-n\lambda)S_{2,\lambda}(n,k).

Replacing
k
by
k+1
, we get

(18)

S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)+n\lambda S_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1)=S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)+(k+1)S_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1).

Therefore, by (
15
) and (
18
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}S_{2,\lambda}(j,k)(1)_{n-j,\lambda}=S_{2,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)+n\lambda S_{2,\lambda}(n,k+1).

It is known that the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(19)

e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,11]).

From (
19
), we note that

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(l,k)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(r)_{m,\lambda}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{2,\lambda}(l,k)(r)_{n-l,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By (
19
) and (
20
), we get

(21)

S_{2,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{2,\lambda}(l,k)(r)_{n-l,\lambda},

where
n,k,r
are nonnegative integers.

From (
20
), we note that

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)(x)_{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\Big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)\bigg)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(x)_{k}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{r}(t)e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x+r)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
22
), we have

(23)

\displaystyle(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)(x)_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14]).

Further, we note that

\displaystyle(x+r)_{n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)(x+r)_{l}

(24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)\sum_{k=0}^{l}\binom{l}{k}(x)_{k}(r)_{l-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{l}{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)(r)_{l-k}(x)_{k}.

From (
21
), (
22
), (
23
) and (
24
), we obtain the next theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
n,k,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq k\geq 0
, and
r\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle S_{2,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{n}{l}S_{2,\lambda}(l,k)(r)_{n-l,\lambda}=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{l}{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)(r)_{l-k},

\displaystyle(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)(x)_{k}.

It is known that the unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(25)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14]).

By (
9
) and (
25
), we easily get

(26)

{n\brack k}_{\lambda}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k),\quad(n,k\geq 0).

Now, we observe that

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{k}\frac{1}{1-t}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}^{-1}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\langle 1\rangle_{l,\lambda}\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+l}\langle 1\rangle_{l,\lambda}\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{l+k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\langle 1\rangle_{l-k,\lambda}\frac{l!}{(l-k)!}\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\langle 1\rangle_{l-k,\lambda}\binom{l}{k}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)(-1)^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}\binom{l}{k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)\langle 1\rangle_{l-k,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand,

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{k}\frac{1}{1-t}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)(-1)^{l}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{S_{1\lambda}(l,k)}{l!}(-1)^{l-k}\Big)t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\Big(n!\sum_{l=k}^{n}(-1)^{l-k}\frac{S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)}{l!}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
27
) and (
28
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{l}{k}\langle 1\rangle_{l-k,\lambda}{n\brack l}_{\lambda}=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{1}{l!}{l\brack k}_{\lambda}.

From (
12
), we note that

(29)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}(1+t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}(1+t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}+\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}nS_{1,\lambda}(n-1,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\big(S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)+nS_{1,\lambda}(n-1,k)\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}e_{\lambda}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{l,\lambda}}{l!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k+l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{l-k,\lambda}l!}{(l-k)!}\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{l}{k}(1)_{l-k,\lambda}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
29
) and (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)+nS_{1,\lambda}(n-1,k)=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{l}{k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)(1)_{l-k,\lambda}.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq 0
, we have

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(1+t)^{\alpha}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\alpha)_{l,\lambda}\frac{1}{l!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{l+k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}\binom{l}{k}(\alpha)_{l-k,\lambda}\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{l}{k}(\alpha)_{n-k,\lambda}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By binomial expansion, we get

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}(1+t)^{\alpha}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{\alpha}{m}t^{m}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l=k}^{n}\frac{S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)}{l!}\binom{\alpha}{n-l}n!\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{\alpha}{n-l}S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)(n-l)!\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
31
) and (
32
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{l}{k}(\alpha)_{l-k,\lambda}S_{1,\lambda}(n,l)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=k}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\binom{\alpha}{n-l}S_{1,\lambda}(l,k)(n-l)!

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n-k}\binom{n}{l}\binom{\alpha}{l}l!S_{1,\lambda}(n-l,k).

From (
12
), we have

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{t}\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{k!}{t}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=k!\sum_{n=k-1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,k)\frac{t^{n}}{(n+1)!}.

Thus, by (
33
), we get

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t}\frac{1-t}{t}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(1+\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}k!\sum_{l=k-1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l+1,k)\frac{\big(\frac{t}{1-t}\big)^{l}}{(l+1)!}

\displaystyle=k!\sum_{l=k-1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l+1,k)\frac{t^{l}}{(l+1)!}\Big(\frac{1}{1-t}\Big)^{l+1}

\displaystyle=k!\sum_{l=k-1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l+1,k)\frac{t^{l}}{(l+1)!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{l+m}{m}t^{m}

\displaystyle=k!\sum_{n=k-1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=k-1}^{n}\frac{S_{1,\lambda}(l+1,k)}{(l+1)!}\binom{n}{l}\bigg)t^{n}.

On the other hand, by (
33
), we see that

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t}\frac{1-t}{t}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(1+\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{t}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\frac{1}{1-t}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\big(-\log_{-\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{k}=(-1)^{k-1}\frac{1}{-t}\big(\log_{-\lambda}(1-t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}k!\sum_{n=k-1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{S_{1,-\lambda}(n+1,k)}{(n+1)!}t^{n}.

Therefore, by (
34
) and (
35
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq k-1
, we have

\frac{1}{(n+1)!}{n+1\brack k}_{-\lambda}=\sum_{l=k-1}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\frac{S_{1,\lambda}(l+1,k)}{(l+1)!}.

We observe that

(36)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=k!\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\frac{1}{t}\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=k!\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{j-1}(1)_{j,1/\lambda}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{l=k-1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(l+1)!}S_{1,\lambda}(l+1,k)t^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\bigg(k!\sum_{l=k-1}^{n-1}\lambda^{n-l-1}(1)_{n-l,1/\lambda}\frac{1}{(n-l)!}\frac{1}{(l+1)!}S_{1,\lambda}(l+1,k)\bigg)t^{n}.

From (
12
), we also note that

(37)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+1)!}{t}\frac{1}{(k+1)!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(k+1)!}{t}\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k+1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=(k+1)!\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\frac{S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)}{(n+1)!}t^{n}.

Therefore, by (
36
) and (
37
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,k+1)=\frac{\lambda^{n-1}}{k+1}(n+1)!\sum_{l=k-1}^{n-1}\frac{\lambda^{-l}}{(n-l)!}(1)_{n-l,1/\lambda}\frac{S_{1,\lambda}(l+1,k)}{(l+1)!}.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, by (
9
), we easily get

(38)

S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,k)=S_{1,\lambda}(n,k-1)+(k\lambda-n)S_{1,\lambda}(n,k).

From (
12
), we note that

(39)

\displaystyle\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{p+1}

\displaystyle=(p+1)!\frac{1}{(p+1)!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{p+1}

\displaystyle=(p+1)!\sum_{n=p+1}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n,p+1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Taking derivatives on both sides of (
39
), we have

(40)

(p+1)\frac{1}{1+t}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{p}(1+t)^{\lambda}=(p+1)!\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,p+1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
31
), we get

(41)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,p+1)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p!}\frac{1}{1+t}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{p}(1+t)^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}t^{j}\sum_{l=p}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(l,p)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(\lambda)_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\sum_{l=p}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}\frac{S_{1,\lambda}(l,p)}{l!}t^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(\lambda)_{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}\sum_{l=p}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}\binom{n}{k}\frac{S_{1,\lambda}(l,p)}{l!}(\lambda)_{n-k}k!\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
41
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

For
n,p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq p\geq 0
, we have

S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,p+1)=\sum_{k=p}^{n}\sum_{l=p}^{k}(-1)^{k-l}\binom{k}{l}\binom{n}{k}(k-l)!S_{1,\lambda}(l,p)(\lambda)_{n-k}.

Note that

S_{1}(n+1,p+1)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}S_{1,\lambda}(n+1,p+1)=\sum_{l=p}^{n}(-1)^{n-l}\binom{n}{l}(n-l)!S_{1}(l,p).

For
p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p\geq 0
, we have

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(1+\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)\Big)^{p}=(-1)^{p}\frac{p!}{1-t}\frac{1}{p!}\Big(\log_{-\lambda}(1-t)\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}\frac{p!}{1-t}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{1,-\lambda}(k,p)(-1)^{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}p!\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{1,-\lambda}(k,p)(-1)^{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\Big((-1)^{p}p!\sum_{k=p}^{n}\frac{S_{1,-\lambda}(k,p)}{k!}(-1)^{k}\Big)t^{n}.

On the other hand, by (
12
), we get

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(1+\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)\Big)^{p}=p!\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\Big(\frac{1}{1-t}\Big)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=p!\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+m}{m}t^{m}

\displaystyle=p!\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)\frac{1}{k!}\binom{n}{k}\bigg)t^{n}.

Therefore, by (
42
) and (
43
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

For
n,p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq p\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=p}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}{k\brack p}_{-\lambda}=\sum_{k=p}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{n}{k}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p).

It is known that the degenerate Bell polynomials are defined by

(44)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13]).

From (
44
), we note that

(45)

\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

By (
3
) and (
12
), we get

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(t)\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{p}=p!e_{\lambda}(t)\frac{1}{p!}\big(\log_{\lambda}(1+t)\big)^{p}

\displaystyle=p!\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(1)_{l,\lambda}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}=p!\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{n}{k}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)(1)_{n-k,\lambda}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
11
), we note that

(46)

\displaystyle x^{p}\frac{1}{p!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{p}e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k+p}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k+p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\binom{k}{p}x^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\binom{k}{p}x^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{k}{p}x^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand,

(47)

\displaystyle x^{p}\frac{1}{p!}(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)^{p}e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}

\displaystyle=x^{p}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=x^{p}\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{n}{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)\phi_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
46
) and (
47
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

For
n,p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq p\geq 0
, we have

x^{p}\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{n}{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)\phi_{n-k,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{k}{p}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)x^{k},

In particular, for
x=1
,

\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{n}{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)\phi_{n-k,\lambda}=\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{k}{p}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k),

where
\phi_{n,\lambda}=\phi_{n,\lambda}(1)
are the degenerate Bell numbers.

For
p=1
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}kS_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{k}\phi_{k,\lambda}(1)_{n-k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 1).

In view of (
7
), we may consider the generalized degenerate Laguerre polynomials given by

(48)

\frac{1}{n!}x^{-\alpha}e_{\lambda}(x)\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{n}\Big(x^{n+\alpha}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(x)\Big)=L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
48
), we can derive the following equation

(49)

L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-k}(-1)^{k}\langle 1\rangle_{k,\lambda}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}.

Now, we observe that

(50)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{p}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)=\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{\big(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\big)^{k}}{(1-t)^{k}}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}\frac{\big(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\big)^{k+p}}{(1-t)^{k+p}}t^{k+p}=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k-p,\lambda}}{(k-p)!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{k}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p!}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\frac{k!(1)_{k-p,\lambda}}{(k-p)!}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}\binom{k}{p}(1)_{k-p,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{k}{p}(1)_{k-p,\lambda}L(n,k)\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Here we note that

(51)

\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha+1}}e_{\lambda}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)t^{n}.

Indeed, from (
49
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\langle 1\rangle_{k,\lambda}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-k}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}(1-t)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-k}t^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k}(1-t)^{k}(1-t)^{-\alpha-k-1}

\displaystyle=(1-t)^{\alpha-1}e_{\lambda}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg).

From (
51
), we note that

(52)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{p}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{p}}{p!}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{p}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{p}}e_{-\lambda}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg(-\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{p}}{p!}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{p}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t^{m}L_{m,-\lambda}^{(p-1)}(-x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{p}\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}t^{n}L_{n-p,-\lambda}^{(p-1)}(-x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{p}\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}n!L_{n-p,-\lambda}^{(p-1)}(-x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
50
) and (
52
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

For
n,p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq p\geq 0
, we have

L_{n-p,-\lambda}^{(p-1)}(-x)=\frac{p!}{n!}\sum_{k=p}^{n}\binom{k}{p}(1)_{k-p,\lambda}L(n,k)\bigg(\frac{x}{1+\lambda x}\bigg)^{k-p}.

We are going to use the following lemma.

Lemma 12
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

a_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)b_{k,\lambda}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ b_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)a_{k,\lambda}

From (
5
) and (
25
), we note that

(53)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}\sum_{l=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,l)(x)_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=l}^{n}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}S_{2,\lambda}(k,l)\bigg)(x)_{l}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (

), we get

(54)

L(n,l)=\sum_{k=l}^{n}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}S_{2,\lambda}(k,l),\quad(n\geq l\geq 0).

From (
11

(55)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{1-e_{\lambda}(t)}{e_{\lambda}(t)}\bigg)^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p!}\big(e_{\lambda}^{-1}(t)-1\big)^{p}=\frac{1}{p!}\big(e_{-\lambda}(-t)-1\big)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}S_{2,-\lambda}(n,p)(-1)^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (

(56)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{1-e_{\lambda}(t)}{e_{\lambda}(t)}\bigg)^{p}=\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{1-e_{\lambda}(t)}{e_{\lambda}(t)-1+1}\bigg)^{p}=\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{1-e_{\lambda}(t)}{1-(1-e_{\lambda}(t))}\bigg)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}L(k,p)(-1)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{k}=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}L(k,p)(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}L(n,k)(-1)^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

55

Theorem 13
.

For
n,p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq p\geq 0
, we have

(-1)^{n}S_{2,-\lambda}(n,p)=\sum_{k=p}^{n}L(n,k)(-1)^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k).

Note that

(57)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\frac{1-e_{\lambda}(t)}{e_{\lambda}(t)}\bigg)^{p}=(-1)^{p}e_{-\lambda}^{p}(-t)\frac{1}{p!}\Big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}e_{-\lambda}^{p}(-t)\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}=(-1)^{p}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(p)_{l,-\lambda}(-1)^{l}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)(-1)^{n-k}(p)_{n-k,-\lambda}\binom{n}{k}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By (
56

57

(58)

\sum_{k=p}^{n}L(k,p)(-1)^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)=(-1)^{p}\sum_{k=p}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)(-1)^{n-k}(p)_{n-k,-\lambda}\binom{n}{k}.

Therefore, by (
58

Theorem 14
.

For
n,p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq p\geq 0

S_{2,-\lambda}(n,p)=(-1)^{p}\sum_{k=p}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(k,p)(-1)^{k}(p)_{n-k,-\lambda}\binom{n}{k}.

Now, we observe that

(59)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\log_{\lambda}\bigg(1+\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)\bigg)^{p}=\frac{1}{p!}\bigg(\log_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)\bigg)^{p}=\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p!}\Big(\log_{-\lambda}(1-t)\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}S_{1,-\lambda}(n,p)(-1)^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}{n\brack p}_{-\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

(60)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p!}\Big(\log_{\lambda}\Big(1+\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)\Big)^{p}=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)\frac{1}{k!}\Big(\frac{t}{1-t}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=p}^{\infty}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=p}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=p}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)L(n,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
59

60

Theorem 15
.

For
n,p\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq p\geq 0
, we have

{n\brack p}_{-\lambda}=\sum_{k=p}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(k,p)L(n,k).

3.
Conclusion

Carlitz initiated the exploration of degenerate Bernoulli and Euler polynomials, which are degenerate
versions of the ordinary Bernoulli and Euler polynomials.
Along the same line as Carlitz’s pioneering work, intensive studies have been done for degenerate versions of quite a few special polynomials and numbers by employing such tools as combinatorial methods, generating functions, umbral calculus techniques,
p
-adic analysis, differential equations, special functions, probability theory and analytic number theory.

In this paper, by using generating functions we further studied some identities and properties on the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind and the unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind.

It is one of our future projects to continue to explore various degenerate versions of many special polynomials and numbers by using aforementioned tools.

Periodic travelling wave solutions of discrete nonlinear Klein-Gordon lattices
Dirk Hennig, Nikos I. Karachalios

Abstract
We prove the existence of periodic travelling wave solutions for general discrete nonlinear Klein-Gordon systems, considering both cases of hard and soft on-site potentials. In the case of hard on-site potentials we implement a fixed point theory approach, combining Schauder’s fixed point theorem and the contraction mapping principle. This approach enables us to identify a ring in the energy space for non-trivial solutions to exist, energy (norm) thresholds for their existence and upper bounds on their velocity. In the case of soft on-site potentials, the proof of existence of periodic travelling wave solutions is facilitated by a variational approach based on the Mountain Pass Theorem. Thresholds on the averaged kinetic energy for these solutions to exist are also derived.



AMS Subject Classifications: 37K40; 37K60; 34C15; 34A33

Keywords: fixed point theorems; variational methods; mountain pass theorem; nonlinear lattices; periodic travelling waves

Introduction

Travelling wave solutions (TWSs) in lattice dynamical systems have attracted considerable interest due to their fundamental importance in numerous physical contexts such as the energy transfer in biomolecules, the mobility of dislocations in crystalline materials and the propagation of pulses in optical systems, to name a few

Braun2004
-
Satn5
. A variety of fundamental models for the description of such phenomena has been studied, including
Fermi-Pasta-Ulam-Tsingou, discrete nonlinear Klein-Gordon (DKG) and discrete nonlinear Schrödinger (DNLS) models

-

, as well as, spatially discrete reaction diffusion systems
Zinner
-

The existence of solitary travelling wave solutions has been rigorously treated with various approaches from nonlinear analysis

-
Zeidler2
, based on direct and minimax variational methods
FWattis
–

, reduction to finite dimensional manifolds and normal form techniques

On the other hand, the problem of the existence of
periodic TWSs
is much less explored, in particular for second order in time lattice dynamical systems, such as DKG lattices. In the present work, by combining a fixed-point approach and variational methods, we study the existence of periodic TWSs for general DKG systems with anharmonic on-site potentials in one-dimensional lattices.

In particular, we study general DKG systems
described by the following set of coupled oscillator equations

\frac{d^{2}q_{n}}{dt^{2}}=\kappa(q_{n+1}-2q_{n}+q_{n-1})-V^{\prime}(q_{n}).

(1.1)

The prime

the coordinate of the oscillator at site

Each oscillator interacts with its neighbours to the left and right and the

This system has a Hamiltonian structure related to the energy

H=\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}p_{n}^{2}+V(q_{n})+\frac{\kappa}{2}(q_{n+1}-q_{n})^{2}\right),

(1.2)

In the case of a finite lattice, the system (

x_{n}=-L+nh,\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots,N.

(1.3)

For finite lattices (
1.1
) we impose
periodic boundary conditions

q_{n}=q_{n+N}.

(1.4)

We consider TWSs of the form:

q_{n}(t)=Q\left(n-c\,t\right)=Q(z),

(1.5)

The solutions (

As the system (

The presentation of the results is as follows:
Section
II
deals with the existence of periodic TWSs in finite lattices with
hard on-site potentials
.
Imposing periodic boundary conditions, the existence of periodic TWSs is proved
by a fixed-point approach, based on Schauder’s fixed point theorem and contraction mapping principles. The Schauder’s fixed point method was made use of in our recent works

,
DNBRS
in order to establish the existence of breather solutions. To our knowledge, the extension of this approach to the problem of the existence of TWs is novel, and as in the case of breathers, can offer a comparatively concise method (if compared to the aforementioned ones), for treating the advance-delay equation (
1.6
) in the suitable functional set-up of Sobolev spaces of periodic functions. The combination with the contraction mapping argument yields the identification of a ring in the energy space where nontrivial solutions exist, together with the derivation of energy thresholds for their existence, and an upper bound for their velocity. For the latter, the contraction mapping argument is applied on a map defined by a suitable auxiliary linear, non-homogeneous problem stemming from (
1.6
), whose solvability is guaranteed by the Friedrichs extension theorem. We should remark that while the current literature on the energy threshold problem concerns mainly breather solutions for DNLS models
ETH1
,
ETH2
(see also
Ch1
,
Ch2
,
NJF
,
ETH3
and references therein), to the best of our knowledge, the results of the present work are the first on energy thresholds for TWSs.

In section
III
we consider the case of

soft on-site potentials
. This time, for the proof of existence of non-trivial periodic TWSs, we implement a variational approach based on the Mountain Pass Theorem. Still in this case, threshold values for the average kinetic energy of TWSs are derived with the aid of the contraction mapping principle.

In both cases of potentials, the estimates on the norms of the solutions and the various thresholds are derived under certain conditions exhibiting a coherent dependence on the lattice parameters, the frequency of the TWs and their velocity which is proved to be physically relevant (e.g. see the concluding remarks of section II for the case of the hard potentials).

assuring by assumptions (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), that indeed,

\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}_{0}.

(2.43)

Furthermore, since it holds that
\mathscr{L}(Q)\in\mathcal{X}_{2}
for all
Q\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{0}
, one has
\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{2}\cap\mathcal{Y}_{0}
,
and as the embedding of
\mathcal{X}_{2}
in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is compact, the operator
\mathscr{L}
is compact.
It remains to prove that
\mathscr{L}
is continuous on
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
:
For arbitrary
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{Y}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle||\mathscr{L}(P_{1})-\mathscr{L}(P_{2})||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||M^{-1}(N(P_{1}))-M^{-1}(N(P_{2}))||_{\mathcal{X}_{2}}\leq||M^{-1}||_{\mathcal{X}_{0},\mathcal{X}_{0}}\,||N(P_{1})-N(P_{2})||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}

(2.44)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ac^{2}}{c^{2}\Omega^{2}-4\kappa}||P_{1}-P_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}<\epsilon,

if

||P_{1}-P_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}<\delta=\frac{c^{2}\Omega^{2}-4\kappa}{Ac^{2}}\epsilon,

(2.45)

for any given
\epsilon>0
, verifying that
\mathscr{L}(Q)
is continuous on
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
. Thus, all the assumptions of Schauder’s fixed point theorem are satisfied, and hence, the fixed point equation
Q=\mathscr{L}(Q)
has at least one solution.

\square

II.3
Upper bound for the velocity

By using a fixed point approach, we derive upper bounds for the velocity
c
of TWSs.
For this purpose, we rewrite equation (
1.6
) in the following operator form:

\displaystyle-Q^{\prime\prime}(z)={\frac{1}{c^{2}}\left\{\kappa\left([Q(z)-Q(z-1)]-[Q(z+1)-Q(z)]\right)+V^{\prime}(Q(z))\right\}}.

(2.51)

We recall some basic auxiliary results, starting with the Friedrichs extension Theorem.

Theorem II.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{0}:D(\mathcal{L}_{0})\subseteq{X}_{0}\rightarrow{X}_{0}
be a linear symmetric operator on the Hilbert space
{X}_{0}
with its domain
D(\mathcal{L}_{0})
being dense in
{X}_{0}
. Assume that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{0}v,v)_{{X}_{0}}\geq c||v||^{2}_{{X}_{0}}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;v\in D(\mathcal{L}_{0}).

Then
\mathcal{L}_{0}
has a self-adjoint extension
\mathcal{L}:D(\mathcal{L})\subseteq{X}_{1}\subseteq{X}_{0}\rightarrow{X}_{0}
where
{X}_{1}
denotes the energetic Hilbert space endowed with the energetic scalar product
(v,w)_{{X}_{1}}=(\mathcal{L}v,w)_{{X}_{0}}
for all
u,v\in{X}_{1}
and the energetic norm
||v||_{{X}_{1}}^{2}=(\mathcal{L}v,v)_{{X}_{0}}
. Furthermore, the operator equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}v=f,f\in{X}_{0},

has a unique solution
v\in D(\mathcal{L})
. In addition, if
\hat{\mathcal{L}}:{X}_{1}\rightarrow{X}_{1}^{*}
denotes the energetic extension of
\mathcal{L}
, then
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
is the canonical isomorphism from
{X}_{1}
to its dual
{X}_{1}^{*}
and the operator equation

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}v=f,f\in{X}_{1}^{*},

has also a unique solution
v\in{X}_{1}
.

With Theorem
II.4
in hand, we discuss the left-hand side of Eq. (
2.51
).
It is well known that Theorem
II.4
is applicable to the operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}:D(\mathcal{L}_{0})\subseteq L^{2}(-L,L)\rightarrow L^{2}(-L,L)
,

\mathcal{L}_{0}Q=-Q^{\prime\prime}(z)
, with domain of definition,
D(\mathcal{L}_{0})
, the space of
C^{\infty}
-functions on
(-L,L)
. Since
D(\mathcal{L}_{0})
is dense in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, and inequality (
2.13
) holds, the Friedrichs extension of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is the operator
\mathcal{L}:D(\mathcal{L})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{0}
where

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{L})=\left\{v\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\;:\;\mathcal{L}v\in L^{2}(-L,L)\right\}.

Consequently, the equation

\displaystyle-Q^{\prime\prime}(z)=f,\;\;\mbox{for every}\;\;f\in L^{2}(-L,L),

(2.52)

has a unique solution in
D(\mathcal{L})
. Thus, we shall consider the right-hand side of Eq. (
2.51
) as a suitable mapping on
L^{2}(-L,L)
. For its linear part, we have the following lemma proved in
(
Smets
, Proposition 1, pg. 268)
.

Lemma II.1
.

The linear operators

A_{1}[Q(z)]=Q(z+1)-Q(z)=\int_{z}^{z+1}Q^{\prime}(s)ds,\;\;A_{2}[Q(z)]=Q(z)-Q(z-1)=\int_{z-1}^{z}Q^{\prime}(s)ds,

are continuous from
\mathcal{X}_{1}
to
L^{2}(-L,L)\cap L^{\infty}(-L,L)
and
||A_{i}Q||_{L^{\infty}}\leq||Q||_{{\mathcal{X}_{1}}}
,
||A_{i}Q||_{{\mathcal{X}_{0}}}\leq||Q||_{{\mathcal{X}_{1}}}
,
i=1,2
.

Using Theorem
II.4
, we treat the following auxiliary linear, non-homogeneous problem

\displaystyle-Q^{\prime\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\left\{\kappa\left([\Psi(z)-\Psi(z-1)]-[\Psi(z+1)-\Psi(z)]\right)+V^{\prime}(\Psi(z))\right\}.

(2.53)

for some arbitrary fixed
\Psi\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
, as an equation of the form (
2.52
). In particular, we have the following result:

Proposition II.2
.

For any
\Psi\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
, the equation (
2.53
) has a unique solution
Q\in D(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{X}_{1}
.

Proof:
Equation (
2.53
) can be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle-Q^{\prime\prime}(z)=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\left\{\kappa{\left([A_{2}[\Psi(z)]-A_{1}[\Psi(z)]\right)}+V^{\prime}(\Psi(z))\right\}:=\mathcal{F}[\Psi(z)].

(2.54)

Due to the continuous embedding
\mathcal{X}_{1}\subset L^{r}(-L,L)
for any
1\leq r\leq\infty
and condition (
2.4
), we have that for some constant
C=C(K,\beta)>0
,

\displaystyle||V^{\prime}(\Psi)||^{2}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}=\int_{-L}^{L}(V^{\prime}(\Psi(z)))^{2}dz\leq K^{2}\int_{-L}^{L}|\Psi(z)|^{2(\beta+1)}dz\leq C||\Psi||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2(\beta+1)}.

Then, for the right-hand side of Eq. (
2.54
) we get

\displaystyle||\mathcal{F}[\Psi]||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c^{2}}\left\{\kappa||A_{2}[\Psi]-A_{1}[\Psi]||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}+||V^{\prime}(\Psi(z))||_{{\mathcal{X}_{0}}}\right\}

(2.55)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c^{2}}\left\{2\kappa||\Psi||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}+C||\Psi||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2(\beta+1)}\right\}.

Thus,

{\mathcal{F}[\Psi]\in L^{2}(-L,L)}
, and by virtue of Theorem
II.4

Eq. (
2.54
)
has a unique solution
Q\in D(\mathcal{L})
.
\square

We now proceed with the implementation of the fixed point argument. To this aim we consider for some
R>0
the closed ball of
\mathcal{X}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{R}:=\left\{\psi\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\,:\,||\psi||_{{\mathcal{X}_{1}}}\leq R\right\}
. Proposition
II.2
shows that the map
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{X}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{1}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}[\Psi]=Q,

where
Q
is the unique solution of the auxiliary problem (
2.54
), is well defined. Hence we introduce
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{R}
such that

Q=\mathcal{T}[\Psi_{1}]\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;P=\mathcal{T}[\Psi_{2}].

Then the difference
Y=Q-P
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle-Y^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\Psi_{1}(z)]-\mathcal{F}[\Psi_{2}(z)]

(2.56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c^{2}}\left\{\kappa\left(A_{1}[\Psi_{2}(z)]-A_{1}[\Psi_{1}(z)]+A_{2}[\Psi_{1}(z)]-A_{2}[\Psi_{2}(z)]\right)\right\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.V^{\prime}(\Psi_{1}(z))-V^{\prime}(\Psi_{2}(z))\right\}.

From Lemma
II.1
the linear operators
A_{i}:\mathcal{X}_{1}\rightarrow L^{2}(-L,L)\cap L^{\infty}(-L,L)
are globally Lipschitz,

\displaystyle||A_{i}\psi_{1}-A_{i}\psi_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\leq||\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}},

(2.57)

\displaystyle||A_{i}\psi_{1}-A_{i}\psi_{2}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq||\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}},\,\,\,{{{i=1,2.}}}

To estimate the difference of the remaining terms in Eq. (
2.56
), we use (
2.4
) and the embedding
\mathcal{X}_{1}\subset L^{\infty}(-L,L)
with its embedding constant
C_{*}
,

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}|V^{\prime}(\Psi_{1}(z))-V^{\prime}(\Psi_{2}(z))|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K^{2}\int_{-L}^{L}(|\Psi_{1}(z)|^{\beta}+|\Psi_{2}(z)|^{\beta})^{2}|\Psi_{1}(z)-\Psi_{2}(z)|^{2}dz

(2.58)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K^{2}\max_{-L\leq z\leq L}(|\Psi_{1}(z)|^{\beta}+|\Psi_{2}(z)|^{\beta})\int_{-L}^{L}|\Psi_{1}(z)-\Psi_{2}(z)|^{2}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2K^{2}C_{*}^{2\beta}R^{2\beta}||\Psi_{1}(z)-\Psi_{2}(z)||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}^{2}.

Hence, for the right-hand side of (
2.56
) we get

\displaystyle||\mathcal{F}[\Psi_{1}(z)]-\mathcal{F}[\Psi_{2}(z)]||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\leq M\||\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}},

(2.59)

where the constant
M
is given by

\displaystyle M=\frac{2}{c^{2}}\left(\kappa+KC_{*}R^{\beta}\right).

(2.60)

Next, by multiplying (
2.56
) in the
L^{2}(-L,L)
-scalar product and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Young’s inequality, we get the estimate

\displaystyle||Y||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\mathcal{F}[\Psi_{1}(z)]-\mathcal{F}[\Psi_{2}(z)]||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\,||Y||_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}

(2.61)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\sqrt{C(L)}||\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}\,||Y||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}||Y||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2}+\frac{M^{2}C(L)}{2}||\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2}.

Note that the Poincaré inequality (
2.13
) has been used. From (
2.61
) we derive

\displaystyle||Y||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2}=||\mathcal{T}[\Psi_{1}]-\mathcal{T}[\Psi_{2}]||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2}\leq M^{2}C(L)||\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2}||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}}^{2}.

(2.62)

We conclude that if the Lipschitz constant satisfies

\displaystyle M\sqrt{C(L)}<1,

(2.63)

then the map
\mathcal{T}:\mathcal{B}_{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{R}
is a contraction.
Hence the map
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the assumptions of the Banach Fixed Point Theorem and has a unique fixed point. By the assumptions we have that
\mathcal{F}(0)=0
. Therefore we deduce that if (
2.63
) holds, then the unique fixed point is the trivial one.

Thus, nontrivial solutions exist only if (
2.63
) is violated, that is, when

\displaystyle M\sqrt{C(L)}>1.

(2.64)

Regarding the upper bound for the velocity we summarise in

Theorem II.5
.

An upper bound for the velocity

to the system (

Remarks on the physical significance of the estimates for the TWSs and their velocity.

The estimates on the TWSs proved in Theorems

,

III
Soft on-site potentials

In this section we study the KG lattice with soft on-site potentials of the form

V(x)=-\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{2}x^{2}+\frac{a}{p+1}x^{p+1},\,\,\,a>0,\,\,p>1,

(3.1)

where we assume that
p+1=2r>0
, with integer

. That is,

We prove the existence of periodic TWSs on finite lattices with imposed periodic boundary conditions
utilising the Mountain Pass Theorem.

III.1
Periodic TWSs: Existence by the Mountain Pass Theorem

We consider periodic TWSs satisfying (
2.5
), that is, solutions
Q(z)
performing oscillations about
Q=0
so that the associated energy
E>0
. Hence, the Poincaré inequality applies.
These periodic TWSs, as solutions of (

S:\,\mathcal{X}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
given by

S(Q)=\int_{-L}^{L}\left[\frac{c^{2}}{2}\left(Q^{\prime}(z)\right)^{2}+\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{2}Q^{2}(z)-\frac{a}{p+1}Q^{p+1}(z)-\frac{\kappa}{2}[Q(z+1)-Q(z)]^{2}\right]dz.

We get

\displaystyle<S^{\prime}(Q),P>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}\left[c^{2}Q^{\prime}(z)P^{\prime}(z)+\omega_{0}^{2}Q(z)P(z)-aQ^{p}(z)P(z)\right.

(3.2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\kappa[Q(z+1)-2Q(z)+Q(z-1)]P(z)\right]dz,\,\,\,P,Q\in\mathcal{X}_{1},

where

is the standard duality bracket between
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and its dual
\mathcal{X}_{1}^{*}
; by the definition of the derivative
S^{\prime}
, for any
Q\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
, the functional

is a linear functional acting on any

as
S^{\prime}(Q)[P]=<S^{\prime}(Q),P>

Chow
.

To prove the existence of TWSs we facilitate the Mountain Pass Theorem (MPT).
We recall [
Chow
, Definition 4.1, p. 130] (Palais-Smale (PS) condition) and [
Chow
, Theorem 6.1, p. 140] (Mountain Pass Theorem (MPT)
of Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz

Definition III.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
{\bf E}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be

. We say that
{\bf E}
satisfies condition (PS) if, for any sequence
\{u_{n}\}\in X
such that
|{\bf E}(u_{n})|
is bounded and
{\bf E}^{\prime}(u_{n})\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in
X^{*}
, there exists a convergent subsequence.

Theorem III.1
.

Let
{\bf E}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be
C^{1}
and satisfy (A)
{\bf E}(0)=0
,
(B)
\exists\rho>0
,
\alpha>0
:
||u||_{X}=\rho
implies
{\bf E}(u)\geq\alpha
, (C)
\exists u_{1}\in X
:
||u_{1}||_{X}\geq\rho
and
{\bf E}(u_{1})<\alpha
.
Define

\Gamma=\left\{\gamma\in C^{0}([0,1],X)\,:\,\gamma(0)=0,\,\gamma(1)=u_{1}\right\}.

(3.3)

Let
F_{\gamma}=\{\gamma(t)\in X\,:\,0\leq t\leq 1\}
and
\mathcal{L}=\{F_{\gamma}\,:\,\gamma\in\Gamma\}.
If
{\bf E}
satisfies (PS), then

\beta:=\inf_{F_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{L}}\,\sup\{{\bf E}(v)\,:\,v\in F_{\gamma}\}\geq\alpha,

(3.4)

is a critical value of the functional
{\bf E}
.

We proceed by proving the validity of the assumptions of the MPT:

Lemma III.1
.

Assume either

(i)
\omega_{0}^{2}\geq 4\kappa
,

or

(ii)
\omega_{0}^{2}<4\kappa
and

.

Then the functional
S
satisfies the PS-condition.

Proof:
Assume
S(Q_{m})
is bounded, i.e.
|S(Q_{m})|\leq M
for all

, and

S^{\prime}(Q_{m})\rightarrow 0,\;\;\mbox{as $m\rightarrow\infty$ in $\mathcal{X}_{1}^{*}$}.

(3.5)

Recall also that

|<S^{\prime}(Q_{m}),Q_{m}>|\leq||S^{\prime}(Q_{m})||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}^{*}}||Q_{m}||_{X_{1}},

(3.6)

by the standard inequality for the duality bracket
(

, Sec. 21.5, pg. 251)
.
Then, using (
3.5
),we deduce that for any

, there exists

such that

||S^{\prime}(Q_{m})||_{\mathcal{X}_{1}^{*}}\leq\epsilon,\;\;\mbox{for $m>N(\epsilon)$}.

(3.7)

Applying (

) for

and using the inequality (

Isochronicity in 1 DOF
Dmitry Treschev

Abstract
Our main result is the complete set of explicit conditions necessary and sufficient for isochronicity of a Hamiltonian system with one degree of freedom. The conditions are presented in terms of Taylor coefficients of the Hamiltonian function.



1
Introduction

1.1
Main question

Consider the Hamiltonian system

\dot{x}=\partial H/\partial y,\quad\dot{y}=-\partial H/\partial x,

(1.1)

where
(x,y)
are coordinates on the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
H=H(x,y)
is a smooth function. We assume that

H=H_{2}+H_{*},\qquad H_{2}=\pi(x^{2}+y^{2}),\quad H_{*}=O(|x|+|y|)^{3}.

(1.2)

Then the point
(x,y)=(0,0)
is an equilibrium position and all solutions of the linearized system (the system with Hamiltonian
H_{2}
) are periodic with period 1. Our main question is as follows.

For which functions
H_{*}
all solutions are periodic with period one?

Such systems are said to be
isochronous
.

1.2
Main theorem

It is convenient to use the complex coordinates
(z,\overline{z})

z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x+iy),\quad\overline{z}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(x-iy).

Then

H_{2}=2\pi z\overline{z}.

(1.3)

Let

H_{*}=\sum_{|\beta|\geq 3}H_{\beta}\mu^{\beta},\qquad\mu^{\beta}=z^{\beta_{1}}\overline{z}^{\beta_{2}},\quad|\beta|=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2},\quad\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2},

(1.4)

where
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. We put

\Sigma^{d}_{k}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{k}}\sum H_{\beta^{(1)}}\cdot\ldots\cdot H_{\beta^{(k)}},

(1.5)

where the summation is performed under the conditions

\beta^{(1)},\ldots,\beta^{(k)}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2},\quad|\beta^{(j)}|\geq 3,\quad\beta^{(1)}+\cdots+\beta^{(k)}=(d+k,d+k).

(1.6)

Remark 1.1

Conditions (
1.6
) imply

3k\leq|\beta^{(1)}+\ldots+\beta^{(k)}|=|(d+k,d+k)|=2(d+k).

Therefore, if the sum
\Sigma_{d}^{k}
contains at least one term, then
1\leq k\leq 2d
.

Theorem 1

Suppose
H_{2}
satisfies (
1.3
), and the series (
1.4
) converges in a neighborhood of the point
(z,\overline{z})=(0,0)
. Then the system (
1.1
) is (locally) isochronous if and only if the following infinite collection of equations hold:

\sum_{s=1}^{d}\frac{(-1)^{s-1}(d+s)!}{s!(d+1)!}\Sigma_{s}^{d}=0,\qquad d=1,2,\ldots

(1.7)

1.3
Comments

1. The problem of isochronicity (or superintegrability) has a long history going back to Huygens who constructed an isochronous cycloidal pendulum. More general results on the existence of isochronous systems are established for some special classes of Hamiltonian systems (
1.1
). One of such classes is the “natural” or “physical” one, where
H=y^{2}/2+V(x)
. Here the function
V
is said to be a potential. Here we mention results, presented in
[
3
]
, concerning construction of isochronous potentials, which generalize all known examples by Piskunov, Urabe, Stillinger, Dorignac, etc. Note that unlike our assumptions the function
V
in
[
3
]
may be only
C^{1}
-smooth.

In
[
5
,
1
]
some results on the existence of globally isochronous systems with 1 degree of freedom are presented. The monograph
[
2
]
contains several constructions which work in the multidimensional case as well.

2. Isochronicity condition means that the normal form of
H
equals
H_{2}
. Equivalently for some generating function
z\overline{w}+S(z,\overline{w})
, we have:

H(z,\overline{w}+\partial_{z}S)=H_{2}(z+\partial_{\overline{w}}S,\overline{w}).

At first glance study of this equation looks simpler than the method we choose below. But we do not know how our main result (equations (
1.7
)) can be obtained in this way.

3. Equations (
1.7
) may be used to solve the following class of problems. Suppose that the Hamiltonian (
1.2
) contains as a parameter a function
\varphi
of one variable:

H=H^{\varphi}(x,y)=\pi(x^{2}+y^{2})+O(|x|+|y|)^{3}.

If the family of functions
H^{\varphi}
is chosen reasonably, Taylor coefficients of
\varphi
are computed uniquely from equations (
1.7
).

If we plug such
\varphi
in
H^{\varphi}
, we obtain an isochronous system provided the Taylor series, that determines
H^{\varphi}
, converges. Convergence or divergence of this series is a separate problem.

Example 1
. Suppose that

H=2\pi z\overline{z}+az^{4}+bz^{3}\overline{z}+\overline{b}z\overline{z}^{3}+\overline{a}\overline{z}^{4}+\varphi(z\overline{z})
,

\varphi(w)=\sum_{j=3}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}w^{j}.

(1.8)

In this case conditions (
1.7
) are satisfied for a unique series (
1.8
). Numeric study gives an evidence that this series diverges.

Example 2
. N. Elfimov,
[
4
]

H=\varphi(y^{2})f(x^{2}),\qquad\varphi(0)=f(0)=1,\quad\varphi^{\prime}(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=1.

Proposition 1.1

For any real-analytic
f
there exists a real-analytic
\varphi
such that the system (
1.1
) is isochronous.

Example 3
. An analogous convergence problem combined with superintegrability phenomenon appears in a slightly another context. Consider a billiard system on a billiard table with a smooth boundary, having two perpendicular symmetry axes. Such a system has two trajectories with period 2. Take one of them.

In
[
6
]
we ask the following question.
Do the above tables exist such that locally near this trajectory the square of the billiard map is conjugated to a rigid rotation on a plane?

Results (partially analytic, partially numeric), essentially confirming the positive answer to the question are contained in
[
6
,
7
,
9
]
. A multidimensional version of this problem is discussed in
[
8
]
.

In this problem the boundary curve plays the role of the function
\varphi
. Under reasonable nonresonance conditions superintegrability (linearizability) of the billiard map fixes uniquely the Taylor expansion of
\varphi
(up to a real parameter). So, the main problem is the convergence of the corresponding series. This problem remains unsolved.

The main equation

We introduce the Poisson bracket

\{f,g\}=i\Big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial z_{j}}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\Big)\quad\mbox{for any two functions $f=f(z,\overline{z})$ and $g=g(z,\overline{z})$}.

[\widehat{F}^{\prime},\widehat{F}^{\prime\prime}]=\widehat{F},\qquad F=\{F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime}\}.

\Phi_{k}

\Phi_{k}

(2.8)

For any point
\xi\in 2\pi i\mathbb{Z}

{\cal K}_{\xi}=\{K\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}:\varphi_{K}(\xi)=0\}.

We say that
{\cal K}_{\xi}

We say that
K\subset\{1,\ldots,k\}

K=I(k^{-},k^{+}):=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:k^{-}\leq j\leq k^{+}\},\qquad 1\leq k^{-}\leq k^{+}\leq k.

(2.9)

First, consider the “simple” case when
{\cal K}_{\xi}
is generated by the intervals

I_{s}=I(k_{s}^{-},k_{s}^{+}),\quad 1\leq k_{s}^{-}\leq k_{s}^{+}\leq k,\qquad s=1,\ldots,m,

This condition means that the intervals

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(m-1)!}\partial_{x}^{m-1}\big|_{x=0}{\bf e}(x)N(x),

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\bf e}(x)=\frac{e^{x}-1}{x},\quad N(x)=\frac{1}{\rho^{-}_{1,k_{1}^{-}-1,-x}\rho^{-}_{k_{1}^{-}+1,k_{1}^{+},-x}\ldots\rho^{-}_{k_{m}^{-}+1,k_{m}^{+},-x}\rho^{+}_{k_{m}^{+}+1,k,x}}.

Abstract
. Exceptional extensions of a class of Laurent biorthogonal polynomials (the so-called Hendriksen-van Rossum polynomials) have been presented by the authors recently. This is achieved through Darboux transformations of generalized eigenvalue problems. In this paper, we discuss the recurrence relations satisfied by these exceptional Laurent biorthogonal polynomials and provide a type of recurrence relations with 3 ​ l 0 + 4 3l_{0}+4 terms explicitly, where the parameter l 0 l_{0} corresponds to the degree of the polynomial part in the seed function used in the Darboux transformation. In the proof of these recurrence relations, the backward operator which maps an exceptional polynomial into a classical one plays a significant role. 3.
Hendriksen-van Rossum polynomials

In this section, we present key properties of HR polynomials,
including both previously known results from references
[
17
,
12
,
29
]
,
and original findings by the authors.

HR polynomials satisfy the recurrence relation:

(3.1)

P_{n+1}(z)+d_{n}P_{n}(z)=z(P_{n}(z)+b_{n}P_{n-1}(z)),

which can be seen as a GEVP with eigenvalue
z
,
and

(3.2)

d_{n}:=d_{n}(\alpha,\beta)=-\dfrac{n+\beta}{n+\alpha+1},\quad b_{n}:=b_{n}(\alpha,\beta)=-\dfrac{n(n+\alpha+\beta)}{(n+\alpha)(n+\alpha+1)}.

HR polynomials are polynomial solutions of the linear second-order differential equation:

(3.3)

\left[z(1-z)\partial_{z}^{2}+(1-\beta-n-(2+\alpha-n)z)\partial_{z}\right]P_{n}(z)=-n(\alpha+1)P_{n}(z).

This equation can also be rewritten into a GEVP:

(3.4)

L_{1}P_{n}(z)=\theta_{n}L_{2}P_{n}(z),\quad\theta_{n}=n,

with

(3.5)

\displaystyle L_{1}=A_{1}(z)\partial_{z}^{2}+B_{1}(z)\partial_{z},\quad L_{2}=B_{2}(z)\partial_{z}+C_{2}(z)I,

where

(3.6)

\displaystyle A_{1}(z)=z(1-z),\hskip 3.41432ptB_{1}(z)=(1-\beta-(2+\alpha)z),\hskip 3.41432ptB_{2}(z)=(1-z),\hskip 3.41432ptC_{2}(z)=-(\alpha+1).

And it turns out that the operators
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
shift the parameters of
P_{n}(z)
as follows:

(3.7)

\displaystyle L_{1}P_{n}(z)=-n(n+\alpha+1)P_{n}(z;\alpha+1,\beta-1),

(3.8)

\displaystyle L_{2}P_{n}(z)=-(n+\alpha+1)P_{n}(z;\alpha+1,\beta-1).

The biorthogonality relation on the unit circle satisfied by HR polynomials is:

(3.9)

\langle w(z)P_{n}(z),Q_{m}(z)\rangle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}P_{n}(e^{ix})Q_{m}(e^{-ix})w(e^{ix})dx=h_{n}\delta_{mn},

where

(3.10)

w(z)=(-z)^{-\beta}(1-z)^{\alpha+\beta},\quad h_{n}=\dfrac{(\alpha+n+1)_{\infty}(\beta+n+1)_{\infty}}{(n+1)_{\infty}(\alpha+\beta+n+1)_{\infty}},

and the branch of
(-z)^{-\beta}
and of
(1-z)^{\alpha+\beta}
is chosen as follow:

\displaystyle(-z)^{-\beta}=|z|^{-\beta}\text{ if arg}z=\pi(0<\text{arg}z<2\pi),

\displaystyle(1-z)^{\alpha+\beta}=|1-z|^{\alpha+\beta}\text{ if arg}(1-z)=0(-\pi<\text{arg}(1-z)<\pi).

Alternatively, the norming constant
h_{n}
can also be expressed in terms of Gamma function:

(3.11)

\displaystyle h_{n}=\dfrac{(1)_{n}\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}.

The positive-definiteness of the linear functional defined by (
3.9
) is equivalent to
h_{n}>0,n=0,1,\ldots
,
which can be guaranteed by a stronger condition:

(3.12)

\alpha>-1,\quad\beta>-1,\quad\alpha+\beta>-1.

The moments related to the weight function
w(z)
are

c_{n}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=1}z^{n}w(z)\dfrac{dz}{z}=\dfrac{\sin{(\beta\pi)}\Gamma(n-b)}{\sin{((\alpha+\beta)\pi)}\Gamma(-(\alpha+\beta))\Gamma(1+n+\alpha)},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots

under the condition
\Re{(\alpha+\beta)}>-1
, which is equivalent to
\alpha+\beta>-1
since
\alpha,\beta
are real.

The coefficients of
L_{1}
and the weight function
w(z)
satisfy the following Pearson equation

(3.13)

(A_{1}(z)w(z))^{\prime}=B_{1}(z)w(z).

The authors have already provided several useful formulas in
[
17
]
.
Here, we introduce two additional formulas and consolidate them into the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following relations are satisfied by the HR polynomials:

(3.14)

\displaystyle z^{n}P_{n}(z^{-1})=\dfrac{(\beta)_{n}}{(\alpha+1)_{n}}P_{n}(z;\beta-1,\alpha+1),

(3.15)

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{n}(z)=nP_{n-1}(z;\alpha+1,\beta),

(3.16)

\displaystyle\dfrac{P^{\prime}_{n}(z;\beta-1,\alpha+1)}{P_{n}(z;\beta-1,\alpha+1)}=\dfrac{n}{z}\left[1-\dfrac{(1+\alpha)}{(n-1+\beta)}\dfrac{P_{n-1}(z;\beta-1,\alpha+2)}{P_{n}(z;\beta-1,\alpha+1)}\right],

(3.17)

\displaystyle\dfrac{P^{\prime}_{n}(z;-\alpha-1,-\beta+1)}{P_{n}(z;-\alpha-1,-\beta+1)}=\dfrac{n}{z}\left[1-\dfrac{P_{n-1}(z^{-1};-\beta+1,-\alpha)}{zP_{n}(z^{-1};-\beta,-\alpha)}\right],

(3.18)

\displaystyle zP_{n}(z)=P_{n+1}(z)+(d_{n}-b_{n})P_{n}(z;\alpha-1,\beta+1),

(3.19)

\displaystyle(z-1)P^{\prime}_{n}(z)=n\left(P_{n}(z)-\frac{n+\alpha+\beta}{n+\alpha}P_{n-1}(z)\right).

By using (
3.15
), the formula (
3.19
) can also be written as:

(3.20)

\displaystyle(z-1)P_{n-1}(z;\alpha+1,\beta)=P_{n}(z)-\frac{n+\alpha+\beta}{n+\alpha}P_{n-1}(z).

Remark 3.2
.

One can immediately find the ladder operators of HR polynomials by using the relations (
3.15
)
and (
3.7
) as follows

\displaystyle\text{Lowering operator: }\quad\partial_{z}P_{n}(z)=nP_{n-1}(z;\alpha+1,\beta),

\displaystyle\text{Raising operator: }\quad\left(A_{1}(z)\partial_{z}+B_{1}(z)\right)P_{n}(z;\alpha+1,\beta)=-(n+\alpha+2)P_{n+1}(z;\alpha+1,\beta-1).

Moreover, it follows from (
3.15
) that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\int P_{n}(z)dz=P_{n+1}(z;\alpha-1,\beta)/(n+1)+Const.

We also found the following type of recurrence relations of HR polynomials, where the coefficients are polynomials in
z

instead of constants.

Lemma 3.3
.

The HR polynomials
P_{n}(z)
satisfy the following recurrence relation:

(3.22)

\displaystyle P_{n+k+1}(z)=D_{k}(z)P_{n+1}(z)+B_{k}(z)P_{n}(z),

where
D_{k}(z)
and
B_{k}(z)
are polynomials of degree
k
which are defined by

(3.23)

\displaystyle D_{k}(z)=(z-d_{n+k})D_{k-1}(z)+b_{n+k}zD_{k-2}(z),

(3.24)

\displaystyle B_{k}(z)=(z-d_{n+k})B_{k-1}(z)+b_{n+k}zB_{k-2}(z),\quad k\geq 1,

where
D_{0}(z)=1
,
D_{i}(z)=0
for
i<0
,
and
B_{0}(z)=0
,
B_{-1}(z)=1
,
B_{j}(z)=0
for
j<-1
.
Moreover, one easily finds that
(b_{n+1}z)^{-1}B_{k}(z)
is a monic polynomial,
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

It follows from the recurrence relation (
3.1
) that

P_{n+2}(z)=(z-d_{n+1})P_{n+1}(z)+b_{n+1}zP_{n}(z),

which can be rewritten as the following matrices multiplication:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+2}(z)\\
P_{n+1}(z)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z-d_{n+1}&b_{n+1}z\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+1}(z)\\
P_{n}(z)\end{pmatrix}.

Inductively, we can conclude that for
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+k+1}(z)\\
P_{n+k}(z)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z-d_{n+k}&b_{n+k}z\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\cdots\begin{pmatrix}z-d_{n+1}&b_{n+1}z\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+1}(z)\\
P_{n}(z)\end{pmatrix}.

If we write

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+k+1}(z)\\
P_{n+k}(z)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{k}(z)&B_{k}(z)\\
D_{k-1}(z)&B_{k-1}(z)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}P_{n+1}(z)\\
P_{n}(z)\end{pmatrix},

then the following relation leads to (
3.23
) and (
3.24
):

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}D_{k}(z)&B_{k}(z)\\
D_{k-1}(z)&B_{k-1}(z)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z-d_{n+k}&b_{n+k}z\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}D_{k-1}(z)&B_{k-1}(z)\\
D_{k-2}(z)&B_{k-2}(z)\end{pmatrix}.

∎

Here we list the first few terms of
D_{k}(z)
and
B_{k}(z)
for reference:

\displaystyle D_{1}(z)=z-d_{n+1},\quad B_{1}(z)=b_{n+1}z;

\displaystyle D_{2}(z)=(z-d_{n+2})(z-d_{n+1})+b_{n+2}z,\quad B_{2}(z)=b_{n+1}z(z-d_{n+2});

\displaystyle D_{3}(z)=(z-d_{n+3})[(z-d_{n+2})(z-d_{n+1})+b_{n+2}z]+b_{n+3}z(z-d_{n+1}),

\displaystyle B_{3}(z)=b_{n+1}z[(z-d_{n+3})(z-d_{n+2})+b_{n+3}z];\quad\cdots

Corollary 3.4
.

For any monic polynomial
C_{k}(z)
of degree
k
,
k\geq 0
,
there exists a degree
k+1
polynomial
Q_{k+1}(z)
, such that

(3.25)

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}(z)P_{n+1}(z)+b_{n+1}(d_{n}-b_{n})C_{k}(z)P_{n}(z;\alpha-1,\beta+1)

\displaystyle\quad\in{\text{span}}\{P_{n+k+2}(z),\cdots,P_{n+2}(z)\}.

Proof.

First, let us introduce the following formula

(3.26)

\displaystyle(d_{n}-b_{n})P_{n}(z;\alpha-1,\beta+1)=zP_{n}(z)-P_{n+1}(z),

which is a straightforward consequence of (
3.18
).
Then we can rewrite the second term on the left-hand side of (
3.25
) as

b_{n+1}(d_{n}-b_{n})C_{k}(z)P_{n}(z;\alpha-1,\beta+1)=b_{n+1}C_{k}(z)(zP_{n}(z)-P_{n+1}(z)).

In view of lemma
3.3
, we assume that

b_{n+1}zC_{k}(z)=B_{k+1}(z)+c_{1}B_{k}(z)+\cdots+c_{k}B_{1}(z),

hence

\displaystyle b_{n+1}zC_{k}(z)P_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=(B_{k+1}(z)+c_{1}B_{k}(z)+\cdots+c_{k}B_{1}(z))P_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=P_{n+k+2}(z)+c_{1}P_{n+k+1}(z)+\cdots+c_{k}P_{n+2}(z)

\displaystyle\quad-(D_{k+1}(z)+c_{1}D_{k}(z)+\cdots+c_{k}D_{1}(z))P_{n+1}(z).

Let
Q_{k+1}(z)
be defined by

Q_{k+1}(z)=(D_{k+1}(z)+c_{1}D_{k}(z)+\cdots+c_{k}D_{1}(z))+b_{n+1}C_{k}(z)

then the proof of (

) is completed.

∎

From the recurrence relation (

Lemma 3.5

For

Lemma 3.6

The twisted HR polynomials

(3.28)

\displaystyle P_{n}(z;\alpha+1,\beta-1)=P_{n}(z)+b_{n}P_{n-1}(z),

(3.29)

\displaystyle zP_{n}(z;\alpha+1,\beta-1)=P_{n+1}(z)+d_{n}P_{n}(z).

(3.31)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(z)=Q_{n}(z;\alpha+1,\beta-1)+b_{n}^{\beta-1,\alpha+1}Q_{n-1}(z;\alpha+1,\beta-1),

3.31
)

Lemma 3.7
.

For
1\leq j\leq n
, it holds that

\displaystyle P_{n}(z;\alpha+j,\beta-j)=P_{n}(z)+\sum^{j}_{l=1}C_{n,j}^{(l)}P_{n-l}(z),

\displaystyle Q_{n}(z)=Q_{n}(z;\alpha+j,\beta-j)+\sum^{j}_{l=1}E_{n,j}^{(l)}Q_{n-l}(z;\alpha+j,\beta-j),

Lemma 3.8
.

There exist constants

(3.40)

\displaystyle a_{n,l_{0}}^{(l)}=C_{n,l_{0}+1}^{(l)},\quad l=1,\ldots,l_{0}+1,

Firstly, for arbitrary constants

(3.41)

\displaystyle z^{-j}w(z^{-1})Q_{n}(z)

\displaystyle\sum^{l_{0}+1}_{l=0}a_{n,l_{0}}^{(l)}P_{n-l}(z)\in\textup{span}\{P_{n}(z;\alpha+j,\beta-j),\cdots,P_{n+j-l_{0}-1}(z;\alpha+j,\beta-j)\}

Concluding remarks

In conclusion, the topic of exceptional-type extensions of COP has received
significant attention from researchers in recent years.
XOP generalizes COP by relaxing constraints on their degree sequence,
and they have demonstrated their potential in various applications,
particularly in deriving new exactly solvable potentials.
Darboux transformations have played a crucial role in the construction of XOP,
while other methods, such as the concept of dual families of polynomials, have also been employed.
Properties such as recurrence relations, zeros, and spectral analysis have been extensively studied
in the context of XOP.
Examples of the exceptional extensions of Laurent biorthogonal polynomials,
such as exceptional HR polynomials, have also been introduced recently by the authors,
and their recurrence relations have been investigated in this paper.
Unlike XOP, XLBP satisfies longer recurrence relations.
By using the expansions of HR polynomials with twisted parameters and
properties of the so-called forward operator and backward operators,
we obtained the recurrence relations satisfied by the exceptional HR polynomials.

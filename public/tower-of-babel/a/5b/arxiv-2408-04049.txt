delayed parabolic regularity for curve shortening flow
Arjun Sobnack, Peter M. Topping

Abstract
Given two curves bounding a region of area A A that evolve under curve shortening flow, we propose the principle that the regularity of one should be controllable in terms of the regularity of the other, starting from time A / π A/\pi . We prove several results of this form and demonstrate that no estimate can hold before that time. As an example application, we construct solutions to graphical curve shortening flow starting with initial data that is merely an L 1 L^{1} function.

Introduction

A smooth embedded curve in the plane is said to evolve under curve shortening flow if we deform it in time with velocity given by its geodesic curvature vector.
In the case that the curve starts as a smooth embedded closed loop,
the work of Gage, Hamilton and Grayson
[
14
,
16
]
tells us that
there is a unique smooth evolution under this flow, which lasts for
a time
T=\frac{A}{2\pi}
, where
A
is the initial enclosed area.

In the case that the curve starts as the graph of a smooth function
y_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
the pioneering work of Ecker-Huisken
[
10
,
11
]
, valid also for higher-dimensional mean curvature flow, tells us that there always exists a solution to the curve shortening flow irrespective of how badly behaved the function
y_{0}
is at spatial infinity. A natural generalisation was given by Chou-Zhu
[
5
]
, who ran the flow starting with an arbitrary proper embedded curve in the plane.

Ecker-Huisken’s flow can be represented as the graph of an evolving function

y:{\mathbb{R}}\times[0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
that solves the equation

y_{t}=\frac{y_{xx}}{1+y_{x}^{2}}\equiv(\arctan(y_{x}))_{x}.

(1.1)

Such a solution is called a graphical curve shortening flow, and uniqueness is known within this class by the work of Chou-Zhu
[
5
]
.

A fundamental question when studying a geometric flow is to determine its regularising properties. This could be phrased in terms of decay estimates of some quantity that measures regularity, or by expressing a class of rough initial data from which we can start the flow.
Ecker-Huisken’s result
[
11
]
could handle initial graphs
y_{0}
that are merely
locally
Lipschitz, yielding smooth solutions
y:{\mathbb{R}}\times(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}

that achieve the initial data locally uniformly as
t\downarrow 0
.
The graphical curve shortening flow equation (
1.1
) implies that the gradient

\alpha:=y_{x}
evolves under the parabolic PDE

\alpha_{t}=(\arctan\alpha)_{xx},

(1.2)

so a bound on
\alpha
induced by a Lipschitz hypothesis on
y
will bring classical parabolic regularity theory into play.
However, Ecker-Huisken needed to prove local estimates to handle the fact that the initial curve is only
locally
Lipschitz; these estimates ultimately inspired Perelman’s pseudolocality theorem for Ricci flow
[
25
, §10]
. The later work of Chou-Zhu
[
5
]
also works at this level of regularity and includes the uniqueness of graphical solutions when the locally Lipschitz initial data is attained locally uniformly by the flow as
t\downarrow 0
; see also the uniqueness results of Chou-Kwong
[
4
]
and
Daskalopoulos-Saez
[
8
]
.

More generally, Evans-Spruck
[
13
]
considered the problem of starting the graphical flow (in arbitrary dimension) with the graph of a
continuous
function.
The essential regularity estimate they prove is that a local
C^{0}
bound on the initial graph turns into an interior
C^{0}
bound on the
gradient
of the solution as we start flowing, so parabolic regularity theory springs into action immediately.
A similar theory can be found in Colding-Minicozzi
[
7
]
and generalisations were given by Andrews-Clutterbuck
[
1
]
and Nagase-Tonegawa
[
22
]
.
See also Lauer
[
21
]
.
The case of
L^{p}_{loc}
initial data for
p>1
was addressed by Chou-Kwong
[
4
]
.
Just as for locally Lipschitz initial data, for continuous or
L^{p}
(
p>1
) initial data these authors are obtaining full quantified regularity instantaneously. That is, their work implies
C^{k}
estimates at time
t
depending only on
t>0
and a bound on the relevant local norm of the initial data.

In this paper we do something that is qualitatively different to the papers above. We weaken the regularity class of the initial data to the extent that no
a priori
regularity estimate could ever be given in terms of
t>0
and the corresponding local norm of the initial data. Instead, our theorems will establish that there is a quantified initial period of the flow in which no estimates
could hold, but after which parabolic regularity is switched on.
The general
delayed parabolic regularity
principle we propose can be loosely phrased as:

Consider two disjoint proper embedded curves
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
in the plane, evolving under curve shortening flow for
t\in[0,T)
and bounding an evolving connected region of finite area
A_{0}

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}

Define the threshold time
\tau
to be
\frac{A_{0}}{\pi}

Then after flowing beyond the threshold time we expect to start being able to control the regularity of
\gamma_{1}
at time
t>\tau
in terms of
t
,
A_{0}
and the regularity of
\gamma_{2}
at time
t
.

We will obtain the uniform upper bound for
y^{n}
at time
\varepsilon
using our delayed height estimate of Theorem
1.5
, in the form of Corollary
1.7
.
First note that the mollification of
y_{0}
(i.e.
y_{0}^{n}
)
is dominated by the mollification of
(y_{0})_{+}
, which in turn has the same
L^{1}
norm
as
(y_{0})_{+}
. Therefore

\|(y^{n}_{0})_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|(y_{0})_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}.

(8.1)

If
\varepsilon\geq\frac{2}{\pi}\|(y_{0})_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
,
we apply Corollary
1.7

not to the flow
y^{n}
starting with
y^{n}_{0}
, but to the Ecker-Huisken solution
\tilde{y}^{n}
starting with
(y^{n}_{0})_{+}
.
We apply it at time
\varepsilon\geq\frac{2}{\pi}A_{0}
where
A_{0}=\|(y^{n}_{0})_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
for this application.
The conclusion is an upper bound

\tilde{y}^{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)\leq K:=C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},

for universal
C
.
By the comparison principle (comparing
\tilde{y}^{n}
to
y^{n}
over the time interval
[0,\varepsilon]
using Corollary
2.5
) we find that

y^{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)\leq\tilde{y}^{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)\leq K,

which provides the desired
n
-independent upper bound for
y^{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)
.

If instead
\varepsilon<\frac{2}{\pi}\|(y_{0})_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
, then we pick
k>0
such that

\varepsilon=\frac{2}{\pi}\|(y_{0}-k)_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},

and note that
k
depends only on
\varepsilon
and
y_{0}
.
We can then repeat the argument above with
\tilde{y}^{n}
now the Ecker-Huisken solution starting with
(y_{0}^{n}-k)_{+}
, with
A_{0}=\|(y^{n}_{0}-k)_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
, and applied now
at time
\varepsilon\geq\frac{2}{\pi}A_{0}
for this different
A_{0}
.
The conclusion is again that

\tilde{y}^{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)\leq K,

for
K
independent of
n
.
This time we complete the argument by comparing
y^{n}
to
k+\tilde{y}^{n}
to give

y^{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)\leq k+\tilde{y}^{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)\leq k+K,

which again provides the desired
n
-independent upper bound for
y^{n}(\cdot,\varepsilon)
.
∎

We have constructed a candidate solution
y
. To complete the proof of Theorem
1.11

we have to show that
it attains the correct initial data, i.e. we can interchange the limits
n\to\infty
and
t\downarrow 0
.

Lemma 8.2

(Initial data attained)
.

The solution
y:{\mathbb{R}}\times(0,\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
constructed in Lemma
8.1
satisfies
y(\cdot,t)\to y_{0}
in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
as
t\downarrow 0
.

Before we can prove this, we need to understand how quickly two nearby solutions can separate under the flow. We abbreviate
y(\cdot,t)
by
y(t)
.

Lemma 8.3

(Separation estimate)
.

Let
y^{1}
,
y^{2}
be two smooth solutions to (
1.1
) on
I\times[0,T)
, where
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is any open interval, finite or infinite.
Then

\|\big(y^{1}(t)-y^{2}(t)\big)_{+}\|_{L^{1}(I)}\leq\|\big(y^{1}(s)-y^{2}(s)\big)_{+}\|_{L^{1}(I)}+2\pi(t-s)

for all
0\leq s\leq t<T
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I,[0,1])
.
It suffices to prove that

\|\big(y^{1}(t)-y^{2}(t)\big)_{+}\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|\big(y^{1}(s)-y^{2}(s)\big)_{+}\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+\pi(t-s)\int|\varphi_{x}|dx

(8.2)

for all
0\leq s\leq t<T
because we could then take a sequence of cut-off functions
\varphi
that increase to
\chi_{I}
, each having
\int|\varphi_{x}|dx=2
.
Moreover, we may assume that
s>0
since otherwise we can apply the result with
s>0
and take a limit
s\downarrow 0
.

The function
(y^{1}-y^{2})\varphi
is smooth on
I\times[s,t]
and so

(y^{1}-y^{2})_{+}\varphi\in W^{1,1}(I\times(s,t))
with weak derivative

{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}(y^{1}-y^{2})_{+}\varphi=\left\{\begin{aligned} (y^{1}_{t}-y^{2}_{t})\varphi&\qquad\text{if }y^{1}>y^{2}\\
0&\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{aligned}\right.

(See, e.g.,
[
15
, Lemma 7.6]
.)
Therefore the function
t\mapsto\|\big(y^{1}(t)-y^{2}(t)\big)_{+}\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}

lies in
W^{1,1}((s,t))
and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\big(y^{1}(t)-y^{2}(t)\big)_{+}\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{y^{1}>y^{2}\}}(y^{1}_{t}-y^{2}_{t})\varphi\,dx

(8.3)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{y^{1}>y^{2}\}}\big(\arctan(y^{1}_{x})-\arctan(y^{2}_{x})\big)_{x}\varphi\,dx

where the integrals are over the time slice at time
t
.
Let
R>0
be large enough so that the support of
\varphi
lies within
(-R,R)
.
The set
\{y^{1}>y^{2}\}\cap(-R,R)
is a countable union of disjoint open intervals

I_{i}=(a_{i},b_{i})
.
Moreover, by definition of
I_{i}
we have
y^{1}_{x}\geq y^{2}_{x}
at
a_{i}
if
a_{i}\neq-R
and

y^{1}_{x}\leq y^{2}_{x}
at
b_{i}
if
b_{i}\neq R
. Because
\arctan
is an increasing function,
this then implies that
\arctan(y^{1}_{x})\geq\arctan(y^{2}_{x})
at
a_{i}
if
a_{i}\neq-R
and

\arctan(y^{1}_{x})\leq\arctan(y^{2}_{x})
at
b_{i}
if
b_{i}\neq R
.
Integrating by parts on the time slice at time
t
, keeping in mind that
\varphi(-R)=\varphi(R)=0
, gives

\displaystyle\int_{I_{i}}\big(\arctan(y^{1}_{x})-\arctan(y^{2}_{x})\big)_{x}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{I_{i}}\big(\arctan(y^{1}_{x})-\arctan(y^{2}_{x})\big)\varphi_{x}\,dx

(8.4)

\displaystyle+\left[\big(\arctan(y^{1}_{x})-\arctan(y^{2}_{x})\big)\varphi\right]_{x=a_{i}}^{x=b_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{I_{i}}\big(\arctan(y^{1}_{x})-\arctan(y^{2}_{x})\big)\varphi_{x}\,dx.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\big(y^{1}(t)-y^{2}(t)\big)_{+}\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\{y^{1}>y^{2}\}}\big(\arctan(y^{1}_{x})-\arctan(y^{2}_{x})\big)\varphi_{x}\,dx

(8.5)

\displaystyle\leq\pi\int|\varphi_{x}|\,dx,

because
|\arctan|
is bounded by
\frac{\pi}{2}
.
This can be integrated to give (
8.2
).
∎

Proof of Lemma
8.2
.

We need to show that for any
\eta>0
we have

\|y(t)-y_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\eta

(8.6)

for sufficiently small
t>0
.
Because mollifications converge in
L^{1}
, for some fixed
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
n\geq m
we have

\|y_{0}^{n}-y_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\eta/16.

(8.7)

In particular, we have

\|y_{0}^{n}-y_{0}^{m}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\eta/8.

(8.8)

We next claim that
y^{m}(t)\to y^{m}_{0}
in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
as
t\downarrow 0
,
and in particular

\|y^{m}(t)-y^{m}_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\eta/4

(8.9)

for sufficiently small
t>0
.
The mollification
y^{m}_{0}
is globally Lipschitz, so the estimates of Ecker-Huisken

[
10
]
tell us that
y^{m}(t)\to y^{m}_{0}
(globally) uniformly as
t\downarrow 0
.
The claim will follow if we can demonstrate that

\|y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\to\|y^{m}_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
as
t\downarrow 0
, or
(equivalently in this case) that

\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\|y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|y^{m}_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})},

(8.10)

i.e., that

y^{m}(t)
does not lose
L^{1}
norm at spatial infinity as
t\downarrow 0
. To prove
this we apply the separation estimate of Lemma
8.3
in the case that
I={\mathbb{R}}
,
s=0
,
y^{2}\equiv 0
and
y^{1}=y^{m}
.
That tells us that

\|\big(y^{m}(t)\big)_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|\big(y^{m}_{0}\big)_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+2\pi t

for all
t\geq 0
. Repeating with
y^{m}
replaced by
-y^{m}
and adding then gives

\|y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\|y^{m}_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+4\pi t

for all
t\geq 0
, which gives (
8.10
) and hence
the claim and (
8.9
).

We now appeal to the separation estimate of Lemma
8.3
in the case that
I={\mathbb{R}}
,
s=0
,
y^{1}=y^{n}
and
y^{2}=y^{m}
.
Together with (
8.8
) this implies that

\displaystyle\|\big(y^{n}(t)-y^{m}(t)\big)_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\big(y^{n}_{0}-y^{m}_{0}\big)_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}+2\pi t

(8.11)

\displaystyle\leq\eta/8+2\pi t

\displaystyle\leq\eta/4

for

t\in(0,\frac{\eta}{16\pi}]
.
By repeating with
n
and
m
switched, we obtain the corresponding estimate

\|\big(y^{m}(t)-y^{n}(t)\big)_{+}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\eta/4
, and so

\|y^{n}(t)-y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\eta/2

for
t\in(0,\frac{\eta}{16\pi}]
.
We would now like to take the limit
n\to\infty
. We know from the construction of
y(t)
in Lemma
8.1
that
y^{n}\to y
smoothly locally on
{\mathbb{R}}\times(0,\infty)
, and in particular
y^{n}(t)\to y(t)
locally uniformly on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
Since

\|y^{n}(t)-y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{1}([-R,R])}\leq\|y^{n}(t)-y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\eta/2

(8.12)

for any
R>0
and any
t\in(0,\frac{\eta}{16\pi}]
, we may take first the limit

n\to\infty
and then
R\to\infty
to conclude that

\|y(t)-y^{m}(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\eta/2

for
t\in(0,\frac{\eta}{16\pi}]
.
Combining with (
8.9
) and (
8.7
) (the latter with
n=m
)
gives (
8.6
) for sufficiently small
t>0
, as required.
∎

(Flowing from measure initial data)

4

4

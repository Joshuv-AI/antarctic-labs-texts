Anisotropic Quasilinear Elliptic Systems with Homogeneous Critical Nonlinearities
Mathew Gluck

Abstract
. In this work we consider a system of quasilinear elliptic equations driven by an anisotropic p p -Laplacian. The lower-order nonlinearities are in potential form and exhibit critical Sobolev growth. We exhibit conditions on the coefficients of the differential operator, the domain of the unknown function, and the lower-order nonlinearities under which nontrivial solutions are guaranteed to exist and conditions on these objects under which a nontrivial solution does not exist.



1.
Introduction

In 1983 Brezis and Nirenberg
[
BN83
]
determined conditions on
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and the bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 3
) for which the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda u+|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(1.1)

admits a positive solution and conditions on these objects under which problem (
1.1
) does not admit a positive solution. They established the following theorem. In the statement of the theorem,
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(-\Delta)
is the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet Laplacian.

Theorem A
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set.

(a)

If
n=3
then there are constants
\lambda_{*}(\Omega)\leq\lambda^{*}(\Omega)
satisfying
0<\lambda_{*}\leq\lambda^{*}<\lambda_{1}
such that problem (
1.1
) admits a positive solution if
\lambda\in(\lambda^{*},\lambda_{1})
and problem (
1.1
) does not admit a positive solution if
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{*}]
.

(b)

If
n\geq 4
then problem (
1.1
) admits a positive solution if and only if
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1})
.

The subtleties under which problem (
1.1
) is solvable, at least in the case that one in interested in positive solutions, are apparent in the statement of Theorem
A
. These subtleties make problem (
1.1
) a natural candidate for further investigations and generalizations and there is a substantial body of mathematical research devoted to such pursuits. With regard to exploring extensions of problem (
1.1
), one theme present in the literature is concerned with replacing
-\Delta
in problem (
1.1
) with a more general operator. Works in this family concern the extension to the
p
-Laplacian
[
GAPA87
,
GV89
]
, the extension to an operator of Hardy type
[
Jan99
,
GG01
,
FG01
]
, the extension to the fractional Laplacian
[
SV15
]
and the extension to more general divergence-form operators
[
Egn88
,
HMPY06
,
HY07
,
dMM14
,
MdM15
,
HM16
]
. A second theme is the extension of problem (
1.1
) to the setting of vector-valued unknown functions
[
AdMFS00
,
ADNM02
,
BG06
,
BIGG+23
]
. In the vector-valued setting, a number of works have investigated the case where the analog of the right-hand side of the differential equation in problem (
1.1
) is replaced by a sum of homogeneous functions
[
MFS99
,
BM11
,
LS20
]
. Such investigations are more interesting in the vector-valued setting than in the scalar-valued setting due to the fact that in the vector-valued setting there are many more homogeneous functions than there are in the scalar-valued setting.

In this work we consider a vector-valued analog of problem (
1.1
) where the Laplacian is replaced by an anisotropic
p
-Laplacian and the right-hand side of the differential equation in (
1.1
) is replaced by the sum of two (vector-valued) homogeneous functions, one of homogeneous degree
p-1
and the other of homogeneous degree
p^{*}-1
, where
p^{*}=np/(n-p)
is the critical exponent for the Sobolev embedding. To describe the problem of interest more specifically, we introduce the following notions of homogenity and anisotropic
p
-Laplacian.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
H:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is homogeneous of degree
q
if
H(\varrho s)=\varrho^{q}H(s)
for all
(\varrho,s)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We say
H
is positively homogeneous of degree
q
if
H
is homogeneous of degree
q
and
H(s)>0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
.

Examples of homogeneous functions of degree
q
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
include
H(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}|s_{j}|^{q}
for
c_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
,
H(s)=|\langle Ms,s\rangle|^{(q-2)/2}\langle Ms,s\rangle
where
M
is a
d\times d
matrix and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the Euclidean inner product,
H(s)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}|s_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}
where
\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}=q
and
H(s)=\pi_{\ell}(|s_{1}|,\ldots,|s_{d}|)^{q/\ell}
, where
\pi_{\ell}
is the
\ell^{\text{th}}
elementary symmetric polynomial in
d
variables.

We define the anisotropic
p
-Laplacian
L_{A,p}
by

\begin{split}L_{A,p}u=\mathrm{div}\left(\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle^{\frac{p-2}{2}}A(x)\nabla u\right),\end{split}

(1.2)

where
p>1
and
A
is an
n\times n
matrix-valued function. In this work we are concerned with establishing conditions under which either the existence or the non-existence of nontrivial solutions to

\begin{cases}-L_{A,p}\bm{u}=f(\bm{u})+g(\bm{u})&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\bm{u}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(1.3)

is guaranteed, where
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a vector-valued function and
L_{A,p}
acts on
\bm{u}
in the coordinatewise sense:

L_{A,p}\bm{u}=(L_{A,p}u_{1},\ldots,L_{A,p}u_{d}).

Problem (
1.3
) is assumed to be in potential form in the sense that the nonlinearities
f
and
g
satisfy

f=\frac{1}{p}\nabla F\qquad\text{ and }\qquad g=\frac{1}{p^{*}}\nabla G

for some
p
-homogeneous function
F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and some
p^{*}
-homogeneous function
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We do not consider positive solutions of problem (
1.3
) (solutions whose coordinate functions are non-negative, non-zero functions). Rather, we aim to establish results with general homogeneous nonlinearities. Under such generality, it is unclear whether the existence of a nontrivial solution implies the existence of a positive solution. See
[
MFS99
,
LS20
,
BIGG+23
]
for some restrictions on
F
and
G
under which such an implication holds. The results in the present work extend some results corresponding to some special cases of problem (
1.3
). For example,
[
MFS99
,
BM11
]
consider problem (
1.3
) with
A\equiv I
,
[
Egn88
,
dMM14
,
HM16
]
consider problem (
1.3
) in the scalar-valued setting with
A(x)=a(x)^{2/p}I
, and
[
BIGG+23
]
considers problem (
1.3
) with
p=2
, for general
A
, and for particular choices of
F
and
G
.

In order that
L_{A,p}
retain the essential properties of the usual
p
-Laplacian we impose the following assumptions on
A
:

A1.

A:\overline{\Omega}\to M(n;\mathbb{R})
is continuous

A2.

A(x)=A^{\top}(x)
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}

A3.

A
is uniformly positive definite in the sense that there is
\tau>0
such that
\tau|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle
for all
(x,\xi)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

where, in item
A1
and throughout the manuscript,
M(n;\mathbb{R})
denotes the space of
n\times n
matrices with real entries. When
\Omega
is bounded, assumptions
A1
,
A2
and
A3
ensure the existence of a constant
\Lambda>0
for which

\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\qquad\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.4)

The following smoothness and homogeneity assumptions will be imposed on the functions
F
and
G
:

H1.

F\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is homogeneous of degree
p

H2.

G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is positively homogeneous of degree
p^{*}
.

In the spirit of the classical Brezis-Nirenberg problem
[
BN83
]
we will establish the existence of solutions to problem (
1.3
) by showing that a suitable energy functional subject to a suitable constraint has sufficiently small infimum. In this context, it is natural to impose both a positivity assumption on
G
and assumptions on
A
and
F
under which the energy functional is coercive. We collect and label these assumptions in (
AFG
) below as they will be used in all of our existence theorems. To state the assumptions we define

M_{F}=\max_{s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}}F(s)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mu_{F}=\min_{s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}}F(s),

(1.5)

where

\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}=\{s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|s_{1}|^{p}+\ldots+|s_{d}|^{p}=1\}

and we let
\lambda_{1}(A,p)
be the first eigenvalue of

\begin{cases}-L_{A,p}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The assumptions to be used in our existence theorems are

\begin{cases}A:\overline{\Omega}\to M(n;\mathbb{R})\text{ satisfies \ref{item:A_continuous}, \ref{item:A_symmetric} and \ref{item:A_positive_definite}},\\
F\text{ satisfies both \ref{item:F_homogeneous} and }M_{F}\in(0,\lambda_{1}(A,p)),\text{ and }\\
G\text{ satisfies \ref{item:G_positively_homogeneous}}.\end{cases}

(AFG)

With these conventions in place we can state the main results of the present work. Generally, both the location of a global minimizer
x_{0}
of
\det A
and the behavior of
A
near
x_{0}
play roles in our sufficient conditions for existence of nontrivial solutions to problem (
1.3
). The first of our existence theorems concerns the case where
A
is not too flat near a global minimizer of
\det A
and there is a positive lower bound on
\mu_{F}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and suppose
A
,
F
and
G
satisfy assumptions (
AFG
). If there exists
x_{0}\in\Omega
that minimizes
\det A
and if there are constants
C_{0}>0
and
\gamma\in(0,p]
such that

\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}\geq\langle A(x_{0})\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}+C_{0}|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}|\xi|^{p}\qquad\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.6)

then there is
\lambda^{*}\in(0,\lambda_{1}(A,p))
such that if
\mu_{F}>\lambda^{*}
then problem (
1.3
) has a nontrivial weak solution.

Our next existence theorem concerns the case where
A
is sufficiently flat near a global minimizer of
\det A
. It does not require strict positivity of
F
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\in(1,\sqrt{n}]
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set. Suppose
A
,
F
and
G
satisfy assumptions (
AFG
) and suppose there is
s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}
for which both

G(s)=\max\{G(t):t\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}\}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad F(s)>0.

(1.7)

If there is
C_{0}>0
,
\gamma>p
, a minimizer
x_{0}\in\Omega
of
\det A
and
\delta>0
such that

\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}\leq\langle A(x_{0}),\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}+C_{0}|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}|\xi|^{p}

(1.8)

for all
(x,\xi)\in B(x_{0},\delta)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then problem (
1.3
) has a nontrivial weak solution.

Theorem
1.6
below concerns the case where a global minimizer
x_{0}
of
\det A
is on
\partial\Omega
and
\partial\Omega
has favorable geometry near
x_{0}
. This case was investigated in the scalar-valued setting and with
A(x)=a(x)^{2/p}I
for some positive function
a
by
[
HM16
]
. To describe the meaning of favorable geometry, we give the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

The boundary of
\Omega
is said to be
interior
\theta
-singular
at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
with
\theta\geq 1
if there is a constant
\delta>0
and a sequence
(x^{i})\subset\Omega
such that
x^{i}\to x_{0}
as

and
B(x^{i},\delta|x^{i}-x_{0}|^{\theta})\subset\Omega
.

Example 1.5
.

For
\theta\geq 1
, the set
\Omega=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y>|x|^{1/\theta}\}
is interior
\theta
-singular at the origin. Indeed, there is
\delta>0
such that for any
0<r<1
, the containment
B((0,r),\delta r^{\theta})\subset\Omega
holds.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\sqrt{n})
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and suppose
A
,
F
and
G
satisfy assumptions (
AFG
). Suppose there are
C_{0}>0
,

\gamma>\max\left\{p^{*},\frac{p(n-p)}{n-p^{2}}\right\},

(1.9)

a global minimizer
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
for
\det A
and
\delta>0
such that (
1.8
) is satisfied for all
(x,\xi)\in(\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\delta))\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If

is

for some
1\leq\theta<\gamma\max\left\{p^{*},\frac{p(n-p)}{n-p^{2}}\right\}^{-1}
then problem (
1.3
) admits a nontrivial weak solution.

Our final theorem is a nonexistence result.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a
C^{1}
bounded domain that is star-shaped with respect to
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Let
A=(a_{ij})\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{x_{0}\};M(n,\mathbb{R}))
satisfy
A1
,
A2
and
A3
and suppose
b_{ij}(x)=\langle x-x_{0},\nabla a_{ij}(x)\rangle
extends continuously to
x_{0}
for all
(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Set
B(x)=(b_{ij}(x))
and let
F
and
G
satisfy
H1
and
H2
respectively. If there are
C_{0}>0
and
\gamma\in(0,p]
for which

(x-x_{0})\cdot\nabla(\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2})\geq\gamma C_{0}|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}|\xi|^{p}\qquad\text{for all }(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.10)

then there is a constant
\lambda_{*}>0
such that if
M_{F}<\lambda_{*}
then problem (
1.3
) has no nontrivial solution
\bm{u}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Remark 1.8
.

In (
1.10
), the factor
\gamma
in the multiplicative constant
\gamma C_{0}
plays no essential role in the proof of Theorem
1.7
. However, writing the multiplicative constant as such facilitates comparison between Theorems
1.2
and
1.7
. Indeed, an elementary computation shows that condition (
1.10
) implies condition (
1.6
). Therefore, for sufficiently smooth
\Omega
and sufficiently smooth

, if (
1.10
) holds for some minimizer
x_{0}
of
\det A(x)
relative to which
\Omega
is star-shaped then combining Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.7
and the elliptic regularity theory we deduce the existence of constants

such that a nontrivial

solution to problem (

) exists whenever

and no such solution exists whenever

. An example of a matrix-valued function for which the hypotheses of Theorem
1.7
are satisfied, for which

minimizes

, and for which the regularity theory applies is
A(x)=M+C|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}I
where

is symmetric and positive definite and where

.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we discuss some preliminary notions including notational conventions, the functional space to be used in the variational argument, the first eigenvalue for
-L_{A,p}
and properties of homogeneous functions. In Section
3
we derive a sharp Sobolev-type inequality. The sharp constant in this inequality will be used in Section
4
to express a sufficient condition for the existence of a nontrivial solution to problem (
1.3
). This condition is expressed as a smallness condition on the infimum of a suitably constrained energy functional. Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.6
(the existence theorems) are proved in Section

by exhibiting that this smallness condition is satisfied. In Section

we prove Theorem

by using a variant of the well-known Pohozaev identity. Section
7
is an appendix where we provide details of some computations that may be useful to some readers but whose inclusion in the main body of the manuscript would detract from the presentation.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, let
p\in(1,n)
and let
A:\overline{\Omega}\to M(n;\mathbb{R})
satisfy
A1
,
A2
and
A3
.
Define the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega)}
on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
by

\|u\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle^{p/2}\;\mathrm{d}x.

(2.1)

With this notation, in the case that
A\equiv I
we recover the usual norm on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
in the sense that
\|u\|_{W_{0,I}^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)}
for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. When the context is clear we write
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}
in place of
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega)}
. The assumptions on
A
guarantee that
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}
is equivalent to the standard norm on
W_{0}^{1,p}
. In particular, denoting the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega)}
by
W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we have
W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega)=W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, where the equality is understood as an equality of sets. Despite this equivalence we retain the notational distinction between
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0}^{1,p}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}
as doing so will be convenient for expressing norms and inner products. For vector-valued functions
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
we consider the product space

\mathcal{W}_{A}:=W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})=W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega)\times\ldots\times W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega).

The equivalence of the norms
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0}^{1,p}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}
ensures that as sets,
\mathcal{W}_{A}=\mathcal{W}_{I}=W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To ease notation, when making statements involving only set-theoretic notions (for example, set containment, membership in a set, etc.) we write
\mathcal{W}
in place of
\mathcal{W}_{A}
or
\mathcal{W}_{I}
. We will use the following norms for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued functions:

\begin{split}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}&=\|u_{1}\|_{p}^{p}+\ldots+\|u_{d}\|_{p}^{p}\\
\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}&:=\|\bm{u}\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}=\|u_{1}\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}^{p}+\ldots+\|u_{d}\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}^{p}.\end{split}

As in the scalar-valued setting we retain the notational distinction between the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}_{I}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}
despite the equality of the sets
\mathcal{W}_{I}=\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{A}
.

From assumption
A2
the operator
-L_{A,p}
may be viewed as the differential of the map
u\mapsto\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}
in the sense that

\langle-L_{A,p}u,\varphi\rangle_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}}}=\int_{\Omega}\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\langle A(x)\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle\;\mathrm{d}x

for all
u,\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}}}
is the dual pairing of
W_{0}^{1,p}
with
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}
. Correspondingly, a weak solution to problem (
1.3
) is a function
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
for which

-L_{A,p}u_{j}=f_{j}(\bm{u})+g_{j}(\bm{u})\qquad\text{ for all }j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},

where the equalities are understood to hold in
W^{-1,p^{\prime}}
. Although our existence theorems for problem (
1.3
) are formulated in terms of weak solutions, the following regularity result illustrates the fact that under sufficient smoothness assumptions on
A
and
\Omega
, the regularity of weak solutions can be improved. The proof is standard except for the initial integrability boosting step. For the convenience of the reader, we overview the proof in Subsection
7.1
of the appendix.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
, let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and suppose
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega\in C^{1,\alpha}
. Assume the entries of
A:\overline{\Omega}\to M(n;\mathbb{R})
are in
C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
and that
A
satisfies both
A2
and
A3
. Let
f=\frac{1}{p}\nabla F
and
g=\frac{1}{p^{*}}\nabla G
for some functions
F
and
G
satisfying
H1
and
H2
respectively. If
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
is a weak solution to problem (
1.3
) then
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
.

2.1.
The Variational Problem

In this subsection we introduce a constrained minimization problem whose solutions (if they exist) yield nontrivial weak solutions to problem (
1.3
). We start by recording the following lemma which lists some basic properties of homogeneous functions that will be used throughout the manuscript. Since the proof is straight-forward we omit the details.

Lemma 2.2

(Properties of homogeneous functions)
.

If
H:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is homogeneous of degree
q>0
then the following properties hold:

(a)

H(0)=0
.

(b)

If
H\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
then for any
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, the partial derivative
\partial_{j}H\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is homogeneous of degree
q-1
.

(c)

If
H\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
then for every
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
\langle s,\nabla H(s)\rangle=qH(s)
.

(d)

If
H\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
then for any
\alpha\in[1,\infty)
there is
s\in\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{d-1}
such that
H(s)=M_{H}(\alpha)=\max\{H(t):t\in\mathbb{S}_{\alpha}^{d-1}\}
. Moreover, the inequality
H(s)\leq M_{H}(\alpha)|s|_{\alpha}^{q}
holds for all
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In Lemma
2.2

(d)
, when
\alpha=p
and
p
is as in the definition of
L_{A,p}
we write
M_{H}
in place of
M_{H}(p)
. This notation is consistence with the notation in (
1.5
). Next we introduce both the energy functional
\Phi_{A,F}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\Phi_{A,F}(\bm{u})=\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}-\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x

(2.2)

and the constraint

\mathcal{M}=\{\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}:\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=1\}.

(2.3)

If
F
and
G
satisfy
H1
and
H2
respectively and if
\Phi_{A,F}
is coercive then the restriction
\Phi_{A,F}\big|_{\mathcal{M}}
is bounded below. In this case, up to a positive constant multiple, any constrained minimizer of
\Phi_{A,F}\big|_{\mathcal{M}}
is a weak solution to problem (
1.3
). In what follows we formulate, under some hypotheses on
A
and
F
, an equivalent condition for the coercivity of
\Phi_{A,F}
.

From assumption
A3
we have

\frac{\|u\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}^{p}}{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}\geq\tau^{p/2}\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{p}^{p}}{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}\geq\tau^{p/2}\lambda_{1}(p)\qquad\text{ for all }u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\},

(2.4)

where
\lambda_{1}(p)>0
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{p}
on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Therefore,
-L_{A,p}
has a first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(A,p)
and this eigenvalue admits the variational characterization

\lambda_{1}(A,p)=\inf\{\|u\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}^{p}:u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\text{ and }\|u\|_{p}=1\}>0,

(2.5)

where the strict positivity follows from (
2.4
) and the strict positivity of
\lambda_{1}(p)
. A standard argument based on the compactness of the embedding
W_{0}^{1,p}\hookrightarrow L^{p}
shows that the infimum in the definition of
\lambda_{1}(A,p)
is attained by some
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}
for which
\|u\|_{p}=1
. In fact,
\lambda_{1}(A,p)
admits a strictly positive first eigenfunction as can be seen by combining the fact that
\||u|\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}=\|u\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}
for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
with Trudinger’s Harnack inequality
[
Tru67
]
.

A simple construction shows that if
F\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a degree-
p
homogeneous function for which
M_{F}>0
, then there is
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
for which
\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x>0
, see Lemma
7.1
in the appendix for details. For any such
F
, and for any
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
for which
\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x>0
, the inequality

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x&\leq M_{F}\int_{\Omega}|\bm{u}|_{p}^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\lambda_{1}(p)^{-1}M_{F}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{j}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\tau^{-p/2}\lambda_{1}(p)^{-1}M_{F}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}\end{split}

guarantees that the quantity

\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)=\inf\{\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}:\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=1\}

(2.6)

is well-defined. The following lemma characterizes
\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)
as the smallest eigenvalue for a suitable eigenproblem. Since the proof follows from a routine argument involving Rellich’s Theorem, we omit the details.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain. If
A
satisfies
A1
,
A2
,
A3
and if
F
satisfies both
H1
and
M_{F}>0
, then
\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)
as defined in (
2.6
) is the smallest positive eigenvalue for the problem

\begin{cases}-L_{A,p}\bm{u}=\lambda f(\bm{u})&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\bm{u}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.7)

where
f=\frac{\nabla F}{p}
.

The following lemma relates the values of
\lambda_{1}(A,p)
and
\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)
defined in (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) respectively.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and let
A
satisfy
A1
,
A2
and
A3
. If
F\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is homogeneous of order
p
and
M_{F}>0
, then the first eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(A,p)
and
\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)
defined in (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) respectively are related by

M_{F}\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)=\lambda_{1}(A,p).

Proof.

For any
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
for which
\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=1
, Lemma
2.2

(d)
gives
1\leq M_{F}\|\bm{u}\|_{p}^{p}
, from which we routinely obtain

\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}\geq\frac{\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}}{M_{F}\|\bm{u}\|_{p}^{p}}\geq M_{F}^{-1}\lambda_{1}(A,p).

Taking the infimum over all
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=1
gives
M_{F}\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)\geq\lambda_{1}(A,p)
. To show the reverse inequality, let
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}
satisfy
M_{F}=F(s)
and let
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a first eigenfunction for
\lambda_{1}(A,p)
. Setting
\bm{u}=(s_{1}\varphi,\ldots,s_{d}\varphi)
we have
\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}=\|\varphi\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}
and

\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=M_{F}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x>0.

Since
\varphi
is assumed to attain the infimum in (
2.5
) we get

\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)\leq\frac{\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}}{\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x}=\frac{\|\varphi\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}^{p}}{M_{F}\|\varphi\|_{p}^{p}}=M_{F}^{-1}\lambda_{1}(A,p).

∎

To close this section we establish the following equivalent condition for coercivity of
\Phi_{A,F}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and suppose
A
satisfies
A1
,
A2
and
A3
. If
F\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is homogeneous of degree
p
and if
M_{F}>0
then the functional
\Phi_{A,F}
as defined in (
2.2
) is coercive if and only if
M_{F}<\lambda_{1}(A,p)
.

Proof.

If
\Phi_{A,F}
is coercive then there is
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that the inequality

\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}-\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\geq\varepsilon\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}

holds for all
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
. In particular, for any such
\varepsilon
and for any
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
for which
\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x>0
we have

1\leq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}}{\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x}.

Taking the infimum over all such
\bm{u}
and in view of the positivity of
\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)
and Lemma
2.4
we have

1\leq(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)<\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)=M_{F}^{-1}\lambda_{1}(A,p).

This shows that the inequality
M_{F}<\lambda_{1}(A,p)
is a necessary condition for the coercivity of
\Phi_{A,F}
. Next we proceed to show that this inequality is also sufficient for the coercivity of
\Phi_{A,F}
. For all
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
for which
\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\leq 0
, the inequality
\Phi_{A,F}(\bm{u})\geq\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}
holds by inspection of
\Phi_{A,F}
. For
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{0\}
satisfying
\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x>0
we have

\begin{split}\Phi_{A,F}(\bm{u})&=\left(1-\frac{\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x}{\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}}\right)\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}\\
&\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1,F}(A,p)}\right)\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}\\
&=\left(1-\frac{M_{F}}{\lambda_{1}(A,p)}\right)\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p},\end{split}

where the final equality holds by Lemma
2.4
. We conclude that the inequality
M_{F}<\lambda_{1}(A,p)
guarantees the coercivity of
\Phi_{A,F}
.
∎

3.
Inequalities of Sobolev Type

In Section
4
, we provide a sufficient condition for the existence of nontrivial solutions to problem (
1.3
). This condition is expressed as a smallness threshold for the infimum of the energy functional
\Phi_{A,F}
constrained to
\mathcal{M}
and this threshold is quantified in terms of a sharp Sobolev type constant. The purpose of this section is to introduce and compute the value of this sharp constant.

For
p\in(1,n)
and an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(not necessarily bounded), the sharp constant in the classical Sobolev inequality is

\mathcal{S}^{-1}=\inf\{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}:\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}=1\}.

(3.1)

It is well-known that
\mathcal{S}^{-1}
depends only on
n
and
p

[
Tal76
]
and that the infimum in the definition of
\mathcal{S}^{-1}
is only attained when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for any
(\varepsilon,x_{0})\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the infimum is attained by

U_{\varepsilon,x_{0}}(x)=\varepsilon^{-\frac{n-p}{p}}U\left(\frac{x-x_{0}}{\varepsilon}\right),

(3.2)

where

U(x)=c_{n,p}\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{n-p}{p}}

(3.3)

and
c_{n,p}>0
is chosen so that
\|U\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
. A direct computation shows that
\|U_{\varepsilon,x_{0}}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
for all
(\varepsilon,x_{0})\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For symmetric positive definite matrices
M\in M(n,\mathbb{R})
we introduce the anisotropic Sobolev constant
\mathcal{S}(M)
defined by

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}(M)^{-1}=\inf\left\{\|u\|_{W_{0,M}^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}:\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}=1\right\},\end{split}

(3.4)

where
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0,M}^{1,p}(\Omega)}
is as in (
2.1
).
With this notation,
\mathcal{S}^{-1}=\mathcal{S}(I)^{-1}
is the sharp Sobolev constant in (
3.1
). The following lemma relates the values of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}(M)
. The proof follows from a routine computation using the change of variable
x\mapsto P^{-1}y
, where
P\in GL(n;\mathbb{R})
satisfies
PMP^{\top}=I_{n}
. See Appendix A of
[
MdM15
]
for details.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. If
M\in M(n;\mathbb{R})
is a symmetric positive definite matrix then the constants
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}(M)
defined in (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) are related via

\mathcal{S}(M)=(\det M)^{-\frac{p}{2n}}\mathcal{S}.

For vector-valued functions
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})
and for any
G\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that is positively homogeneous of degree
p^{*}
we have

\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\leq M_{G}\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|u_{j}|^{p}\right)^{p^{*}/p}\;\mathrm{d}x,

where
M_{G}=\max\{G(s):s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}\}
as in (
1.5
). From this estimate, from Minkowski’s inequality and from the definition of
\mathcal{S}(M)
we have

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}&\leq M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}|^{p^{*}}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\\
&\leq\mathcal{S}(M)M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}.\end{split}

(3.5)

This computation shows that the quantity

\mathcal{S}(M;G)^{-1}=\inf\{\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}:\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=1\}

(3.6)

is well-defined, positive and satisfies
\mathcal{S}(M;G)\leq M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}\mathcal{S}(M)
. The next lemma shows that equality holds in this inequality.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set and let
M\in M(n,\mathbb{R})
be symmetric and positive definite. If
G\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is positively homogeneous of degree
p^{*}
then the Sobolev-type constants
\mathcal{S}(M)
and
\mathcal{S}(M;G)
defined in (
3.4
) and (
3.6
) respectively are related via
\mathcal{S}(M;G)=M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}\mathcal{S}(M)
.

Proof.

The inequality
M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}\mathcal{S}(M)\geq\mathcal{S}(M;G)
was established in the computation preceding the statement of the lemma. To show the reverse inequality, let
(\varphi^{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be a sequence of nonnegative functions for which

\mathcal{S}(M)^{-1}+\circ(1)=\frac{\|\varphi^{k}\|_{W_{0,M}^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|\varphi^{k}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p}}.

Choose
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}
for which
G(s)=M_{G}
and set
\bm{u}^{k}=(s_{1}\varphi^{k},\ldots,s_{d}\varphi^{k})
. Evidently we have both

\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u}^{k})\;\mathrm{d}x=M_{G}\|\varphi^{k}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p^{*}}

and
\|\bm{u}^{k}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}=\|\varphi^{k}\|_{W_{0,M}^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}
. Therefore,

\frac{\|\bm{u}^{k}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}}{\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u}^{k})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}}=M_{G}^{-p/p^{*}}\frac{\|\varphi^{k}\|_{W_{0,M}^{1,p}(\Omega)}^{p}}{\|\varphi^{k}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\Omega)}^{p}}=M_{G}^{-p/p^{*}}\mathcal{S}(M)^{-1}+\circ(1).

The inequality
M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}\mathcal{S}(M)\leq\mathcal{S}(M;G)
follows immediately.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

A simple rescaling argument shows that for all
(x_{0},\delta)\in\overline{\Omega}\times(0,\infty)
, the quantity

\mathcal{S}_{x_{0},\delta}^{-1}(M;G):=\inf\{\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}:\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}(x_{0};\delta)\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=1\}

satisfies
\mathcal{S}_{x_{0},\delta}(M;G)=\mathcal{S}(M;G)
, where

\mathcal{W}(x_{0};\delta):=W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\delta);\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(3.7)

For non-constant coefficient matrices
A
, in place of an inequality of the form
\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq C\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}
as in (
3.5
), we consider the following inequality with lower-order term:

\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq C_{1}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}+C_{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}.

(3.8)

In place of the sharp Sobolev type constant
\mathcal{S}(M;G)^{-1}
defined in (
3.6
) we define

\mathcal{N}(A;G)=\inf\{C_{1}:\text{there exists $C_{2}>0$ such that \eqref{eq:sobolev_with_lower_order} holds for all $\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}_{A}$}\}.

The following proposition gives the explicit value of
\mathcal{N}(A;G)
in terms of
A
,
G
and the classical sharp Sobolev constant in (
3.1
).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and let
A
satisfy
A1
,
A2
and
A3
. If
G\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is positively homogeneous of degree
p^{*}
then

\mathcal{N}(A;G)=m_{A}^{-\frac{p}{2n}}M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}\mathcal{S},

where

m_{A}=\min\{\det A(x):x\in\overline{\Omega}\}.

(3.9)

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

Define

\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)=\max\{\mathcal{S}(A(x_{0});G):x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}\}

and observe that combining Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
gives
\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)=m_{A}^{-\frac{p}{2n}}M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}\mathcal{S}
. In particular, the equality asserted by Proposition
3.4
is precisely the equality
\mathcal{N}(A;G)=\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)
. This equality follows by combining Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
below where the inequalities
\mathcal{N}(A;G)\geq\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)
and
\mathcal{N}(A;G)\leq\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)
are established respectively.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
3.4
, if
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are positive constants for which (
3.8
) holds for all
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
then
C_{1}\geq\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)
. In particular,
\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)\leq\mathcal{N}(A;G)
.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Proceeding by way of contradiction, suppose
C_{1}\in(0,\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G))
and
C_{2}\in(0,\infty)
are constants for which (
3.8
) holds for all
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
. By definition of
\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)
there there is
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
for which

C_{1}<\mathcal{S}(A(x_{0});G).

(3.10)

We fix any such
x_{0}
and, for ease of notation, we set
M=A(x_{0})
. By hypotheses on
A
, for all
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that

(1-\varepsilon)\langle M\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\langle M\xi,\xi\rangle\quad\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in(\overline{\Omega}\cap B(x_{0},\delta))\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon^{1/p})
as such. If
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}(x_{0};\delta)
(as defined in (
3.7
)) then we have both

\begin{split}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}&=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\delta)}\langle A(x)\nabla u_{j},\nabla u_{j}\rangle^{p/2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{p/2}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}\end{split}

and, from the Hölder and Sobolev inequalties,

\begin{split}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}&\leq C\delta^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\delta)}|u_{j}|^{p^{*}}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|u_{j}\|_{p^{*}}^{p}\\
&\leq C_{3}\varepsilon\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p},\end{split}

where
C_{3}
is a positive constant depending only on
n
,
p
, and
\det M
(in particular
C_{3}
is independent of
\varepsilon
and
\bm{u}
). Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
and any
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}(x_{0};\delta)
that satisfies
\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=1
we have

\begin{split}1&\leq C_{1}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}+C_{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\\
&\leq(C_{1}(1+\varepsilon)^{p/2}+C_{2}C_{3}\varepsilon)\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}.\end{split}

(3.11)

Since
C_{1}<\mathcal{S}(M;G)
, we choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small so that

2(C_{1}(1+\varepsilon)^{p/2}+C_{2}C_{3}\varepsilon)<\mathcal{S}(M;G)+C_{1}

and then obtain
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)\in(0,\varepsilon^{1/p})
for which (
3.11
) holds whenever
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}(x_{0},\delta)
satisfies
\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x=1
. For any such
\varepsilon
,
\delta
and
\bm{u}
we obtain

1\leq(C_{1}(1+\varepsilon)^{p/2}+C_{2}C_{3}\varepsilon)\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}<\frac{\mathcal{S}(M;G)+C_{1}}{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{M}}^{p}.

(3.12)

From the definition of
\mathcal{S}(M;G)
and from Remark
3.3
, estimate (
3.12
) implies
C_{1}\geq\mathcal{S}(M;G)
, and this inequality contradicts inequality (
3.10
).
∎

Lemma 3.6

(
\varepsilon
-sharp inequality)
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
3.4
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there is
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that the inequality

\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq(\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)+\varepsilon)\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}+C_{\varepsilon}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}

(3.13)

holds for all
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
. In particular,
\mathcal{N}(A;G)\leq\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)
.

The proof of Lemma
3.6
is similar in spirit to the proof of Theorem 9 of
[
Aub76
]
, an english translation of which is provided in Theorm 4.5 of
[
Heb00
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we provide the details.

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

The proof is a partition of unity argument. Since
A
satisfies
A1
,
A2
and
A3
, and since
\overline{\Omega}
is compact, for every
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, there are
\delta>0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset\overline{\Omega}
for which both
\overline{\Omega}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B(x^{i},\delta)
and

(1-\varepsilon_{0})\langle A(x^{i})\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq(1+\varepsilon_{0})\langle A(x^{i})\xi,\xi\rangle\quad\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in(\overline{\Omega}\cap B_{i})\times\mathbb{R}^{n},

(3.14)

where, for ease of notation, we set
B_{i}=B(x^{i},\delta)
. Fix
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and let
\delta>0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\{x^{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be as such. Let
\{\zeta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a smooth partition of unity subordinate to
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and define

\eta_{i}=\frac{\zeta_{i}^{\lfloor p\rfloor+1}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\zeta_{j}^{\lfloor p\rfloor+1}},

where
\lfloor p\rfloor
is the greatest integer that does not exceed
p
. Evidently
\supp\eta_{i}\subset B_{i}
,
\eta_{i}^{1/p}\in C^{1}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\eta_{i}\equiv 1
on
\overline{\Omega}
. For any
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
we have

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}&=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\eta_{i}G(\bm{u})^{p/p^{*}}\right)^{p^{*}/p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\\
&=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\eta_{i}|\bm{u}|_{p}^{p}G\left(\frac{\bm{u}}{|\bm{u}|_{p}}\right)^{p/p^{*}}\right)^{p^{*}/p}\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\eta_{i}^{1/p}|\bm{u}|_{p}\right)^{p^{*}}G\left(\frac{\bm{u}}{|\bm{u}|_{p}}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\eta_{i}^{1/p}\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{S}(A(x^{i});G)\|\eta_{i}^{1/p}\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A(x^{i})}}^{p}\\
&\leq\overline{\mathcal{N}}(A;G)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\eta_{i}^{1/p}\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A(x^{i})}}^{p}.\end{split}

(3.15)

We proceed to estimate the sum on the right-most side of this string of inequalities. For each
(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\times\{1,\ldots,d\}
, using Cauchy’s inequality and the fact that (
3.14
) holds on
\supp(\eta_{i})
we obtain

0\leq\langle A(x^{i})\nabla(\eta_{i}^{1/p}u_{j}),\nabla(\eta_{i}^{1/p}u_{j})\rangle\leq f_{ij}^{2}+h_{ij}^{2},

where
f_{ij}
and
h_{ij}
are defined by

\begin{split}f_{ij}^{2}&=\eta_{i}^{2/p}\left(\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{0}}\langle A(x)\nabla u_{j},\nabla u_{j}\rangle+\varepsilon^{2}\Lambda^{2}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}\right)\\
h_{ij}^{2}&=u_{j}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}+\Lambda\right)\|\nabla(\eta_{i}^{1/p})\|_{\infty}^{2}\end{split}

(3.16)

and
\Lambda
is as in (
1.4
). According to this estimate and Lemma
7.2
, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we have

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\eta_{i}^{1/p}\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A(x^{i})}}^{p}&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{ij}^{2}+h_{ij}^{2}\right)^{p/2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\left(f_{ij}+h_{ij}\right)^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left((1+\varepsilon)\|f_{ij}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{C(p)}{\varepsilon^{p}}\|h_{ij}\|_{p}^{p}\right).\end{split}

(3.17)

We proceed to separately estimate the sum over
i
and

of the norms
\|f_{ij}\|_{p}^{p}
and
\|h_{ij}\|_{p}^{p}
. From the expression of

in (

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|h_{ij}\|_{p}^{p}&\leq N\max_{i}\|\nabla(\eta_{i}^{1/p})\|_{\infty}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}+\Lambda\right)^{p/2}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}.\end{split}

(3.18)

To estimate the
p
-norm of

we define
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
by

\begin{split}\phi_{j}^{2}&=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{0}}\langle A(x)\nabla u_{j},\nabla u_{j}\rangle\\
\psi_{j}^{2}&=\varepsilon^{2}\Lambda^{2}|\nabla u_{j}|^{2}\end{split}

so that the expression of
f_{ij}^{2}
in (
3.16
) reads

. For each

together with Lemma

(applied with

in place of

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|f_{ij}\|_{p}^{p}&=\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\eta_{i}\right)(\phi_{j}^{2}+\psi_{j}^{2})^{p/2}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega}(\phi_{j}+\psi_{j})^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon^{1/2})\|\phi_{j}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{C(p)}{\varepsilon^{p/2}}\|\psi_{j}\|_{p}^{p}\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon^{1/2}}{(1-\varepsilon_{0})^{p/2}}+C(p)\Lambda^{p}\frac{\varepsilon^{p/2}}{\tau^{p/2}}\right)\|u_{j}\|_{W_{0,A}^{1,p}}^{p},\end{split}

where

is as in assumption

. Summing this estimate over

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|f_{ij}\|_{p}^{p}&\leq\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon^{1/2}}{(1-\varepsilon_{0})^{p/2}}+C(p)\Lambda^{p}\frac{\varepsilon^{p/2}}{\tau^{p/2}}\right)\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}.\end{split}

(3.19)

Using estimates (
3.19
) and (
3.18
) in (
3.17
) gives

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\eta_{i}^{1/p}\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A(x^{i})}}^{p}&\leq(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{1+\varepsilon^{1/2}}{(1-\varepsilon_{0})^{p/2}}+C(p)\Lambda^{p}\frac{\varepsilon^{p/2}}{\tau^{p/2}}\right)\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}\\
&+\frac{C(p)}{\varepsilon^{p}}N\max_{i}\|\nabla(\eta_{i}^{1/p})\|_{\infty}^{p}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}+\Lambda\right)^{p/2}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}.\end{split}

(3.20)

Since both
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and

are arbitrary, using estimate (

) in estimate (

∎

\mathcal{M}

We define

(4.1)

and we observe that when

,

and

satisfy (

), Lemma
2.5
guarantees that

is bounded below by a positive constant. For such

,
F
and

\begin{split}K(A,F,G)^{-1}&=\inf\{\Phi_{A,F}(\bm{u}):\bm{u}\in\mathcal{M}\}\\
&=\inf\{Q_{A,F,G}(\bm{u}):\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}\setminus\{0\}\},\end{split}

is well-defined and positive. For ease of notation, in what follows we do not indicate the dependence on

,

or

in the notations for

,

or

. For example, the notation

Proposition 4.1

(Sufficient condition for minimizer)
.

Let
p\in(1,n)
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and suppose

,
F
and

satisfy assumptions (

). If

\mathcal{N}(A;G)<K(A,F,G)

then there is
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{M}
for which

.

The remainder of this section will be devoted to the proof of Proposition

. It follows from a standard application of Ekeland’s variational principle. For the convenience of the reader, a proof is provided in the appendix.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

for

(4.3)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

Proof.

The result is known in the case

to the case

\begin{split}\lx@add@cssclass{ltx_eqn_lefteqn}$\displaystyle D_{A(x)}(\xi,\zeta)$\mbox{}\hfil\\

where the final estimate follows from the fact that the Lemma is known in the case

j
gives

\|\bm{u}^{k}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}=\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}+\|\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}+\circ(1)

and therefore, by the assumption
\Phi(\bm{u}^{k})\to K^{-1}
, we have

\begin{split}K^{-1}+\circ(1)&=\Phi(\bm{u}^{k})\\
&=\|\bm{u}^{k}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}-\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})+\circ(1)\\
&=\Phi(\bm{u})+\|\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}+\circ(1)\\
&\geq K^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\|\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}+\circ(1).\end{split}

(4.15)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, applying the
\varepsilon
-sharp inequality in Corollary
3.7
to the second term on the right-most side of (
4.15
) we obtain

\begin{split}K^{-1}&+\circ(1)\\
&\geq K^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+(\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon)^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\\
&-C_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon)^{-1}\|\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}+\circ(1)\\
&=K^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+(\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon)^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\circ(1).\end{split}

Rearranging this inequality and applying the Brezis-Lieb type lemma to
\int_{\Omega}G(\cdot)\;\mathrm{d}x
, see Lemma 2.1 of
[
ADNM02
]
, gives

\begin{split}K^{-1}\left(1-\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\right)&\geq\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\circ(1)\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u}^{k})\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\circ(1)\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon}\left(1-\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\circ(1)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon}\left(1-\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\right)+\circ(1).\end{split}

Letting
k\to\infty
we find that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

0\leq\left(1-\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}\right)\left(K^{-1}-(\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon)^{-1}\right).

Choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough so that
\mathcal{N}+\varepsilon<K
yields
\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\geq 1
which, when combined with estimate (
4.8
) gives
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{M}
. Using this in estimate (
4.15
) we obtain
\|\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}\to 0
, so the continuity of
\Phi
gives
\Phi(\bm{u})=K^{-1}
.
∎

Up to a positive constant multiple, any minimizer of
\Phi_{A,F}
subject to the constraint
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{M}
is a nontrivial weak solution to (
1.3
), so we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
4.1
there is a nontrivial solution to problem (
1.3
).

5.
Proofs of Existence Theorems

In this section we give proofs of Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
and
1.6
. In each of the proofs we verify that the energy smallness condition in (
4.2
) is satisfied by establishing the existence of
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{M}
for which
\Phi(\bm{u})<\mathcal{N}(A;G)^{-1}
.

5.1.
Construction of Test Function for
\gamma\leq p

This subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. In the proof we will employ the Hardy-Sobolev inequality which we recall now. For
p\in(1,n)
, for a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
\gamma\in(0,p]
and for
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the Hardy-Sobolev inequality ensures the existence of an optimal positive constant
K_{0}(n,p,\gamma,\Omega,x_{0})
such that

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq K_{0}(n,p,\gamma,\Omega,x_{0})^{p}\int_{\Omega}|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma}|\nabla u|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\qquad\text{ for all }u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

(5.1)

This inequality is a particular case of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities
[
CKN84
]
. When
\gamma=p
, the constant
K_{0}
on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) is independent of
x_{0}
and
\Omega
. More specifically,
K_{0}(n,p,p,\Omega,x_{0})=p/n
, see
[
HLP34
]
for the case
p=2
and
[
ACP05
]
for the case
p\neq 2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

For simplicity we assume
x_{0}=0\in\Omega
, in which case assumption (
1.6
) becomes

\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}\geq\langle A(0)\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}+C_{0}|x|^{\gamma}|\xi|^{p}\qquad\text{ for all }(x,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5.2)

Using this estimate together with the sharp Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
5.1
) we have for any
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
,

\begin{split}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}&\geq\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A(0)}}^{p}+C_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{\gamma}|\nabla u_{j}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\geq\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A(0)}}^{p}+\frac{C_{0}}{K_{0}^{p}}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\\
&\geq\mathcal{S}(A(0);G)^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\frac{C_{0}}{K_{0}^{p}}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p},\end{split}

(5.3)

the final inequality holding by the definition of
\mathcal{S}(A(0);G)^{-1}
given in equation (
3.6
). Define
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}
to be the set of
\lambda>0
for which the inequality

\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}\geq\mathcal{S}(A(0);G)^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\lambda\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}

holds for all
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
. Estimate (
5.3
) shows that
\Theta\neq\emptyset
. It is routine to verify that
\Theta
is bounded above and that

\lambda^{*}:=\sup\Theta

is in
\Theta
. Moreover, the following simple argument shows that
\lambda^{*}<\lambda_{1}(A,p)
. Let
s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}
satisfy
G(s)=M_{G}=\max\{G(t):t\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}\}
and set
\bm{u}=(\varphi s_{1},\ldots,\varphi s_{d})
, where
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}
is an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(A,p)
. For this
\bm{u}
we have

\begin{split}\lambda_{1}(A,p)\|\varphi\|_{p}^{p}&=\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}\\
&\geq\mathcal{S}(A(0);G)^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\lambda^{*}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\\
&=\mathcal{S}(A(0);G)^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}+\lambda^{*}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\\
&>\lambda^{*}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\end{split}

thereby establishing the inequality
\lambda^{*}<\lambda_{1}(A,p)
. From the definition of
\lambda^{*}
, if
\mu_{F}>\lambda^{*}
then there is
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
for which

\begin{split}\Phi(\bm{u})&\leq\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}-\mu_{F}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\\
&<\mathcal{S}(A(0);G)^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p/p^{*}}.\end{split}

(5.4)

We fix any such
\bm{u}
. Lemma
2.5
ensures that
\Phi
is coercive, so the strict inequality in (
5.4
) guarantees that
\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x>0
. Defining
\bm{v}=\left(\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x\right)^{-1/p^{*}}\bm{u}
we have
\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{v})\;\mathrm{d}x=1
and, from (
5.4
) and the degree-
p
homogeneity of
\Phi
,

\Phi(\bm{v})<\mathcal{S}(A(0);G)^{-1}.

(5.5)

Since
x_{0}=0
is a minimizer of
\det A
, combining Proposition
3.4
, Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
yields
\mathcal{N}(A;G)=\mathcal{S}(A(0);G)
. Therefore, combining inequality (
5.5
) and Corollary
4.5
guarantees the existence of a nontrivial solution to problem (
1.3
).
∎

5.2.
Construction of Test Function for
\gamma>p

This subsection is devoted to the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.6
. In each of these proofs we verify the energy smallness condition in (
4.2
) by using a standard bubble-based construction to establish the existence of
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{M}
for which
\Phi(\bm{u})<\mathcal{N}(A;G)^{-1}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We assume for simplicity that
x_{0}=0\in\Omega
in which case condition (
1.8
) reads

\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}\leq\langle A(0)\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}+C_{0}|x|^{\gamma}|\xi|^{p}

(5.6)

for all
(x,\xi)\in B(0,\delta)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By assumptions
A2
and
A3
there is an orthogonal matrix
O\in O(n)
for which

OA(0)O^{\top}=\diag(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=:D,

(5.7)

where
0<a_{1}\leq\ldots\leq a_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
A(0)
. Set
P=D^{-1/2}O
so that
PA(0)P^{\top}=I_{n}
. We also have both
a_{1}|y|^{2}\leq|P^{-1}y|^{2}\leq a_{n}|y|^{2}
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
|P^{\top}\xi|^{2}\leq a_{1}^{-1}|\xi|^{2}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
with
\supp\bm{u}\subset B(0,\delta)
we set

\bm{v}(y)=\bm{u}(P^{-1}y)\qquad\text{ for }y\in P\Omega=\{Px:x\in\Omega\}.

(5.8)

Using the change of variable
y=Px
and since
(\det P)^{-1}=\pm m_{A}^{1/2}
(recall the notational convention in (
3.9
)) we obtain

\begin{split}\|\bm{u}\|_{\mathcal{W}_{A}}^{p}&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\left(\langle A(0)\nabla u_{j},\nabla u_{j}\rangle^{p/2}+C_{0}|x|^{\gamma}|\nabla u_{j}|^{p}\right)\;\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq m_{A}^{1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{P\Omega}\left(1+C_{1}|y|^{\gamma}\right)|\nabla v_{j}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}y,\end{split}

(5.9)

where
C_{1}=C_{0}a_{n}^{\gamma/2}a_{1}^{-p/2}
. Still for
\bm{u}\in\mathcal{W}
satisfying
\supp\bm{u}\subset B(0,\delta)
, using the same change of variable gives both of the following relations:

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}F(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x&=m_{A}^{1/2}\int_{P\Omega}F(\bm{v})\;\mathrm{d}y\\
\int_{\Omega}G(\bm{u})\;\mathrm{d}x&=m_{A}^{1/2}\int_{P\Omega}G(\bm{v})\;\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Combining these equalities with estimate (
5.9
) we find that

\begin{split}Q(\bm{u})&\leq m_{A}^{\frac{p}{2n}}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{P\Omega}\left(1+C_{1}|y|^{\gamma}\right)|\nabla v_{j}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}y-\int_{P\Omega}F(\bm{v})\;\mathrm{d}y}{\left(\int_{P\Omega}G(\bm{v})\;\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p/p^{*}}}\end{split}

(5.10)

whenever
\bm{u}
and
\bm{v}
are related via (
5.8
). If, in addition to
\bm{u}
and
\bm{v}
satisfying this relation,
\bm{v}=(s_{1}w,\ldots,s_{d}w)
for some nonnegative function
w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(P\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
having support contained in
PB(0,\delta)
and some
s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}
satisfying both conditions in (
1.7
), then (
5.10
) gives

m_{A}^{-p/(2n)}M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}Q(\bm{u})\leq\frac{\int_{P\Omega}(1+C_{1}|y|^{\gamma})|\nabla w|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}y-F(s)\|w\|_{L^{p}(P\Omega)}^{p}}{\|w\|_{L^{p^{*}}(P\Omega)}^{p}}.

(5.11)

Now, let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(PB(0,\delta))
with
\eta\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of the origin and, for
\varepsilon>0
small, set

w_{\varepsilon}(y)=\eta(y)U_{\varepsilon}(y),

(5.12)

where
U_{\varepsilon}=U_{\varepsilon,0}
as given in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) with
x_{0}=0
. Recalling that these functions are normalized so that
\|U_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
for all
\varepsilon>0
, we have the following estimates for
w_{\varepsilon}
, see
[
GAPA87
]
:

\begin{split}\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}&=\mathcal{S}^{-1}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}\right)\\
\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{p^{*}}^{p}&=1+O(\varepsilon^{n})\\
\end{split}

(5.13)

and

\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}=\begin{cases}b_{n,p}\varepsilon^{p}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}}\right)&\text{ if }n>p^{2}\\
b_{n,p}\varepsilon^{p}|\log\varepsilon|+O(\varepsilon^{p})&\text{ if }n=p^{2},\end{cases}

(5.14)

where

b_{n,p}=\begin{cases}c_{n,p}^{p}\omega_{n-1}\frac{(p-1)\Gamma\left(\frac{n-p^{2}}{p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{np-n}{p}\right)}{p\Gamma(n-p)}&\text{ if }n>p^{2}\\
c_{n,p}^{p}\omega_{n-1}&\text{ if }n=p^{2},\end{cases}

(5.15)

\omega_{n-1}=|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
is the volume of the
(n-1)
-sphere, and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(n,p)
is the sharp Sobolev constant defined in (
3.1
). Since
\gamma>p
we have

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{\gamma}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}y&=\begin{cases}O(\varepsilon^{\gamma})&\text{ if }\gamma<\frac{n-p}{p-1}\\
O(\varepsilon^{\gamma}|\log\varepsilon|)&\text{ if }\gamma=\frac{n-p}{p-1}\\
O(\varepsilon^{\frac{n-p}{p-1}})&\text{ if }\gamma>\frac{n-p}{p-1}.\end{cases}\\
&=\begin{cases}\circ(\varepsilon^{p})&\text{ if }n>p^{2}\\
O(\varepsilon^{p})&\text{ if }n=p^{2}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(5.16)

In particular, comparing (
5.16
) to (
5.14
) and in view of the assumption
\gamma>p
we find that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{\gamma}|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}y=\circ(1)\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{p}^{p}

independently of whether
n>p^{2}
or
n=p^{2}
. For
s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}
satisfying both conditions in (
1.7
) set
\bm{v}^{\varepsilon}(y)=(w_{\varepsilon}s_{1},\ldots,w_{\varepsilon}s_{d})
and set
\bm{u}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\bm{v}^{\varepsilon}(Px)
. Choosing
\bm{u}=\bm{u}^{\varepsilon}
in (
5.11
) gives

m_{A}^{-\frac{p}{2n}}M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}Q(\bm{u}^{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{\int_{P\Omega}\left(1+C_{1}|y|^{\gamma}\right)|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}y-F(s)\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(P\Omega)}^{p}}{\|w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(P\Omega)}^{p}}.

(5.17)

If
n>p^{2}
then combining estimates (
5.13
), (
5.14
) and (
5.16
) with estimate (
5.17
) gives

\begin{split}m_{A}^{-\frac{p}{2n}}M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}Q(\bm{u}^{\varepsilon})&\leq\frac{\mathcal{S}^{-1}-F(s)b_{n,p}\varepsilon^{p}+\circ(\varepsilon^{p})}{1+O(\varepsilon^{n})}\\
&=\mathcal{S}^{-1}-F(s)b_{n,p}\varepsilon^{p}+\circ(\varepsilon^{p})\\
&<\mathcal{S}^{-1},\end{split}

(5.18)

the final inequality holding for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small. In the case
n=p^{2}
a similar computation shows that for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,

\begin{split}m_{A}^{-\frac{p}{2n}}M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}Q(\bm{u}^{\varepsilon})&\leq\frac{\mathcal{S}^{-1}-F(s)b_{n,p}\varepsilon^{p}|\log\varepsilon|+O(\varepsilon^{p})}{1+O(\varepsilon^{n})}\\
&=\mathcal{S}^{-1}-F(s)b_{n,p}\varepsilon^{p}|\log\varepsilon|+O(\varepsilon^{p})\\
&<\mathcal{S}^{-1}.\end{split}

(5.19)

When combined with Proposition
3.4
, estimates (
5.18
) and (
5.19
) show that
\mathcal{N}<K
whenever
n\geq p^{2}
. Corollary
4.5
now ensures that problem (
1.3
) has a nontrivial weak solution.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we assume for simplicity that
x_{0}=0
and we define
P=D^{-1/2}O
, where
O\in O(n)
and
D
are as in (
5.7
). If
0\leq w\in W_{0}^{1,p}(P\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
is a nonnegative function with support contained in
P(B(0,\delta)\cap\Omega)
and if
s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}
satisfies both conditions in (
1.7
) then for
\bm{u}(x)=(s_{1}w(Px),\ldots,s_{d}w(Px))
, we still have estimate (
5.11
). It is routine to check that the interior
\theta
-singularity assumption on
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}=0
guarantees the interior
\theta
-singularity of
\partial P\Omega
at
y_{0}=Px_{0}=0
. Indeed, if
r>0
and if
(x_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\Omega
with
x_{i}\to 0
and
B(x_{i},r|x_{i}|^{\theta})\subset\Omega
, then for
y_{i}=Px_{i}
we have both
|y_{i}|\to 0
and
B(y_{i},r^{\prime}|y_{i}|^{\theta})\subset P\Omega
, where
r^{\prime}=a_{n}^{-1/2}a_{1}^{\theta/2}r
. For
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
as such, let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta\equiv 1
in
B(0,r^{\prime}/2)
and
\eta\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,r^{\prime})
and consider the test function

\varphi^{i}(y)=\eta\left(\frac{y-y_{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}^{\theta}}\right)U_{\varepsilon_{i}^{\sigma}}(y-y_{i}),

where
\varepsilon_{i}=|y_{i}|
, where
U_{\varepsilon}=U_{\varepsilon,0}
is given in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) with
x_{0}=0
and where
\sigma
satisfies

\max\left\{\theta p^{*},\frac{\theta p(n-p)}{n-p^{2}}\right\}<p\sigma<\gamma.

(5.20)

In particular
\sigma>\theta
. With
w_{\varepsilon}
as in (
5.12
) we have

\varphi^{i}(y)=\varepsilon_{i}^{-\theta(n-p)/p}w_{\varepsilon_{i}^{\sigma-\theta}}\left(\frac{y-y_{i}}{\varepsilon_{i}^{\theta}}\right),

so estimates (
5.13
) and (
5.14
) yield the following estimates

\begin{split}\|\nabla\varphi^{i}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}&=\|\nabla w_{\varepsilon_{i}^{\sigma-\theta}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=\mathcal{S}^{-1}+O(\varepsilon_{i}^{(\sigma-\theta)(n-p)/(p-1)})\\
\|\varphi^{i}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}&=\|w_{\varepsilon_{i}^{\sigma-\theta}}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=1+O(\varepsilon_{i}^{n(\sigma-\theta)})\\
\|\varphi^{i}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}&=\varepsilon_{i}^{p\theta}\|w_{\varepsilon_{i}^{\sigma-\theta}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=b_{n,p}\varepsilon_{i}^{p\sigma}+O(\varepsilon_{i}^{p\theta+(\sigma-\theta)(n-p)/(p-1)}),\end{split}

(5.21)

where
b_{n,p}
is as in (
5.15
).
Moreover, since
\supp\varphi^{i}\subset B(y^{i},r^{\prime}\varepsilon_{i}^{\theta})
and since
\theta\geq 1
, for any
y\in\supp\varphi^{i}
we have

|y|\leq|y-y_{i}|+|y_{i}|\leq r^{\prime}\varepsilon_{i}^{\theta}+\varepsilon_{i}\leq C\varepsilon_{i}.

Therefore, the first estimate in (
5.21
) gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|y|^{\gamma}|\nabla\varphi^{i}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}y\leq C\varepsilon_{i}^{\gamma}\|\nabla\varphi^{i}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=O(\varepsilon_{i}^{\gamma}).

(5.22)

For any small
r>0
, by choosing
i
sufficiently large we have
\supp(\varphi^{i}\circ P)\subset B(0,\delta)
, where
\delta>0
is as in the hypotheses of the theorem, so choosing
s\in\mathbb{S}_{p}^{d-1}
for which both of conditions (
1.7
) hold, and setting
\bm{u}^{i}(x)=(s_{1}\varphi^{i}(Px),\ldots,s_{d}\varphi^{i}(Px))
, we may apply estimate (
5.11
) to obtain

\begin{split}m_{A}^{-p/(2n)}&M_{G}^{p/p^{*}}Q(\bm{u}^{i})\\
&=\frac{\int_{P\Omega}(1+C_{1}|y|^{\gamma})|\nabla\varphi^{i}|^{p}\;\mathrm{d}y-F(s)\|\varphi^{i}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}}{\|\varphi^{i}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}}\\
&=\frac{\mathcal{S}^{-1}-F(s)b_{n,p}\varepsilon^{p\sigma}+O(\varepsilon^{(\sigma-\theta)(n-p)/(p-1)})+O(\varepsilon_{i}^{\gamma})}{1+O(\varepsilon_{i}^{n(\sigma-\theta)})}\\
&=\mathcal{S}^{-1}-F(s)b_{n,p}\varepsilon^{p\sigma}+O(\varepsilon^{(\sigma-\theta)(n-p)/(p-1)})+O(\varepsilon_{i}^{\gamma})+O(\varepsilon_{i}^{n(\sigma-\theta)}),\end{split}

where the second equality is obtained with the aid of estimates (
5.21
) and (
5.22
). Since
\sigma
satisfes (
5.20
), we find that
p\sigma<\min\{\gamma,\frac{(\sigma-\theta)(n-p)}{p-1},n(\sigma-\theta)\}
, so for
i
sufficiently large we obtain

Q(\bm{u}^{i})<m_{A}^{p/(2n)}M_{G}^{-p/p^{*}}\mathcal{S}^{-1}=\mathcal{N}^{-1},

the equality holding by Proposition
3.4
. Finally, Corollary
4.5
now implies that problem (
1.3
) admits a nontrivial solution.
∎

6.
Proof of the Non-Existence Result

For
C^{2}
solutions to problem (
6.1
) below, the following Pohozaev identity follows from the general variational identity of Pucci and Serrin
[
PS86
]
. Because of the strict convexity of the map
\xi\mapsto\mathcal{F}(z,x,\xi)=\frac{1}{p}\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle^{p/2}-H(z)
the results of

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. For each
p\geq 1
there is a constant
C=C(p)>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and all non-negative functions

\int_{\Omega}(f+h)^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{C(p)}{\varepsilon^{p}}\|h\|_{p}^{p}.

Proof.

For any

, the convexity of

ensures that
(1+t)^{p}\leq 1+(2^{p}-1)t
for all

while for

the Mean Value Theorem gives

\begin{split}(1+t)^{p}-1^{p}\leq pt(2t)^{p-1}=2^{p-1}pt^{p}.\end{split}

Combining these two estimates yields

(1+t)^{p}\leq\begin{cases}1+(2^{p}-1)t&\text{ if }t\in[0,1]\\
1+2^{p-1}pt^{p}&\text{ if }t\geq 1.\end{cases}

From this estimate we obtain
(1+t)^{p}\leq 1+2^{p}t+4^{p}t^{p}
for all
t\geq 0
and hence

(a+b)^{p}\leq a^{p}+2^{p}a^{p-1}b+4^{p}b^{p}\qquad\text{ for all }a,b\geq 0.

(7.1)

Now if

and if
\delta\in(0,1)
then applying (
7.1
), Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality gives

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(f+h)^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x&\leq\|f\|_{p}^{p}+2^{p}\int_{\Omega}f^{p-1}h\;\mathrm{d}x+4^{p}\|h\|_{p}^{p}\\
&\leq\|f\|_{p}^{p}+2^{p}\left(\frac{\delta^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{1}{p\delta^{p}}\|h\|_{p}^{p}\right)+4^{p}\|h\|_{p}^{p}\\
&\leq(1+2^{p}\delta^{p^{\prime}})\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\left(4^{p}+2^{p}\delta^{-p}\right)\|h\|_{p}^{p}.\end{split}

Given
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we apply the previous inequality with

to obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(f+h)^{p}\;\mathrm{d}x&\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\left(4^{p}+2^{p}\left(2^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right)^{p}\right)\|h\|_{p}^{p}\\
&\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|f\|_{p}^{p}+4^{p^{2}+1}\varepsilon^{-p}\|h\|_{p}^{p}.\end{split}

∎

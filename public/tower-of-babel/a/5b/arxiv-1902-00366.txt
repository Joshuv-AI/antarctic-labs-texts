Abstract
. The main aim of this paper is to investigate ( H p , L p ) \left(H_{p},L_{p}\right) - type inequalities for the the maximal operators of Nörlund logaritmic means, for 0 < p < 1 . 0<p<1.

42C10.

Key words and phrases:
Vilenkin system, partial sums, Logarithmic
means, martingale Hardy space.

1.
INTRODUCTION

It is well-known that (see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
16
]
) Vilenkin systems do not form bases in the Lebesgue space
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right).
Moreover, there exists a function in the Hardy space
H_{1},
such that the partial sums of
f
are not
bounded in
L_{1}
-norm.

In
[
17
]
(see also
[
21
]
) it was proved that the following is true:

Theorem T1.
Let
0<p<1.
Then the maximal operator

\overset{\sim}{S}_{p}^{*}f:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|S_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}

is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the space
L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right).
Here
S_{n}
denotes the
n
-th partial sum with respect to the Vilenkin system. Moreover, it was proved that the rate of the factor
(n+1)^{1/p-1}
is in a sense sharp.

In the case
p=1
it was proved that the maximal operator
\widetilde{S}^{\ast}
defined by

\widetilde{S}^{\ast}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|S_{n}\right|}{\log\left(n+1\right)}

is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the space
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right).
Moreover, the rate of the factor
\log(n+1)
is in a sense sharp. Similar problems for Nörlund logarithmic means in the case when
p=1
was considered in
[
15
]
.

Móricz and Siddiqi
[
9
]
investigate the approximation properties of
some special Nörlund means of Walsh-Fourier series of
L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
functions in
L_{p}
-norm. Fridli, Manchanda and Siddiqi
[
5
]
improved and extended the results of Móricz and Siddiqi
[
9
]
to Martingale Hardy spaces. However, the
case when
\left\{q_{k}=1/k:k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right\}
was excluded, since
the methods are not applicable to Nörlund logarithmic means. In
[
6
]

Gát and Goginava proved some convergence and divergence properties of
Walsh-Fourier series of the Nörlund logarithmic means of functions in the
Lebesgue space
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right).
In particular, they proved that there exists an
function in the space
L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right),
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{1}=\infty.

In
[
2
]
(see also
[
15
,
20
]
) it was proved that there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right),\quad(0<p<1)
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|L_{n}f\right\|_{p}=\infty.

Analogical problems for Nörlund means with respect to Walsh, Kaczmarz and unbounded Vilenkin systems were considered in
[
7
]
,
[
3
,
4
]
,
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
,
[
13
,
14
]
,
[
18
,
19

]
,
[
22
]
.

In this paper we discuss boundedness of weighted maximal
operators from the Hardy space
H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the Lebesgue space
L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
for
0<p<1.

2.
Definitions and Notation

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of the positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.

Let
m:=(m_{0},m_{1},...)
denote a sequence of the positive integers not
less than
2.

Denote by

Z_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,...m_{k}-1\}

the additive group of integers modulo
m_{k}.

Define the group
G_{m}
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{m_{j}}
with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{m_{j}}

,
s.

The direct product
\mu
of the measures

\mu_{k}\left(\{j\}\right):=1/m_{k}\text{ \qquad}(j\in Z_{m_{k}})

is the Haar measure on
G_{m_{\text{ }}}
with
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

If
\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}m_{n}<\infty
, then we call
G_{m}
a bounded Vilenkin
group. If the generating sequence
m
is not bounded then
G_{m}
is said to
be an unbounded one.
In this paper we discuss bounded
Vilenkin groups only.

The elements of
G_{m}
are represented by sequences

x:=(x_{0},x_{1},...x_{j},...)\qquad\left(x_{k}\in Z_{m_{k}}\right).

It is easy to give a base for the neighborhood of
G_{m}

I_{0}\left(x\right):=G_{m},

I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G_{m}\mid y_{0}=x_{0},...y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\}\text{ }(x\in G_{m},\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N})

Denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right),
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\overset{-}{I_{n}}:=G_{m}

\backslash

I_{n}
.

If we define the so-called generalized number system based on
m
in the
following way :

M_{0}:=1,\text{ \qquad}M_{k+1}:=m_{k}M_{k}\qquad(k\in\mathbb{N})

then every
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as
n=\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{j}M_{j}
where
n_{j}\in Z_{m_{j}}

(j\in\mathbb{N})
and
only a finite number of
n_{j}`
s differ from zero. Let
\left|n\right|:=\max\{j\in\mathbb{N};\quad n_{j}\neq 0\}.

The norm (or quasi-norm) of the space
L_{p}(G_{m})
is defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{G_{m}}\left|f\right|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\qquad\left(0<p<\infty\right).

The space
weak-L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
consists of all measurable
functions
f
for which

\left\|f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}(G_{m})}:=\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\lambda^{p}\mu\left(x:\left|f\left(x\right)\right|>\lambda\right)<+\infty.

Next, we introduce on
G_{m}
an ortonormal system which is called the
Vilenkin system.

At first define the complex valued function
r_{k}\left(x\right):G_{m}\rightarrow C,
the generalized Rademacher functions as

r_{k}\left(x\right):=\exp\left(2\pi ix_{k}/m_{k}\right)\qquad\left(i^{2}=-1,\qquad x\in G_{m},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Now define the Vilenkin system
\psi:=(\psi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
on
G_{m}
as:

\psi_{n}:=\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\Pi}}r_{k}^{n_{k}},\qquad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Specifically, we call this system the Walsh-Paley one if m=2.

The Vilenkin system is ortonormal and complete in
L_{2}\left(G_{m}\right)\,
[
1
,
23
]
.

Now we introduce analogues of the usual definitions in Fourier-analysis.

If
f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
we can establish the the Fourier
coefficients, the partial sums of the Fourier series, the Dirichlet kernels
with respect to the Vilenkin sistem
\psi
in the usual manner:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}}f\overline{\psi}_{k}d\mu,\qquad\left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right),

\displaystyle S_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)\psi_{k},\qquad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\quad S_{0}f:=0\right),

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi_{k},\qquad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).

Recall that (for details see e.g.
[
1
]
)

(1)

D_{M_{n}}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}M_{n}\quad x\in I_{n}\\
0\qquad x\notin I_{n}\end{array}\right.

The
\sigma
-algebra generated by the intervals

\left\{I_{n}\left(x\right):x\in G_{m}\right\}
will be denoted by
\digamma_{n}

\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).
Denote by
f=\left(f_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
a
martingale with respect to
\digamma_{n}

\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).
(for details see e.g.
[
24
,
25
]
). The maximal function of a martingale
f
is defend by

f^{*}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|f_{n}\right|.

In the case when
f\in L_{1},
the maximal function is also be given by

f^{*}\left(x\right)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left|I_{n}\left(x\right)\right|}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)\mu\left(u\right)\right|.

For
0<p<\infty
the Hardy martingale spaces
H_{p}

\left(G_{m}\right)

consist of all martingales for which

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}:=\left\|f^{*}\right\|_{p}<\infty.

If
f\in L_{1},
then it is easy to show that the sequence

\left(S_{M_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is a martingale. If
f=\left(f_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is martingale then the Vilenkin-Fourier coefficients must be defined in a slightly different manner:

\widehat{f}\left(i\right):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{G_{m}}f_{k}\overline{\psi}_{i}d\mu.

The Vilenkin-Fourier coefficients of
f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
are
the same as those of the martingale
\left(S_{M_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
obtained from
f
.

Let {
q_{k}:k>0
} be a sequence of nonnegative numbers. The
n
-th
Nörlund means for the Fourier series of
f
is defined by

\frac{1}{Q_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{n-k}S_{k}f\qquad\text{where}\qquad Q_{n}:=\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}q_{k}.

If
q_{k}=1/k,
then we get Nörlund logarithmic means

L_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}\frac{S_{k}f}{n-k}\qquad\text{where}\qquad l_{n}=\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}\frac{1}{n-k}=\overset{n}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\frac{1}{j}.

A bounded measurable function
a
is
p
-atom, if there exist a dyadic
interval
I,
such that

\int_{I}ad\mu=0,\ \ \left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq\mu\left(I\right)^{-1/p},\ \ \text{supp}\left(a\right)\subset I.

3.
Formulation of Main Results

Theorem 1
.

a) Let
0<p<1.
Then the maximal operator

\overset{\sim}{L}_{p}^{*}f:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}

is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the space
L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right).

b) Let
0<p<1
and
\varphi:\mathbb{N}_{+}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
be a
non-decreasing function satisfying the condition

(2)

\overline{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{n^{1/p-1}}{\log n\varphi\left(n\right)}=+\infty.

Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right),
such that the maximal operator

\text{ }\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\varphi\left(n+1\right)}

is not bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the space

L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right).

4.
Proof of the Theorem

Proof.

Since

\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}\leq\frac{1}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}\underset{1\leq k\leq n}{\sup}\left|S_{k}f\right|\leq\underset{1\leq k\leq n}{\sup}\frac{\left|S_{k}f\right|}{\left(k+1\right)^{1/p-1}}\leq\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\frac{\left|S_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}

if we use Theorem T1 we obtain that

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}\leq\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\frac{\left|S_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}

and

\left\|\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\frac{\left|S_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-1}}\right\|_{p}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}}.

Now, prove part b) of the Theorem. Let

f_{n_{k}}=D_{M_{2n_{k}+1}}-D_{M_{2n_{k}}}.

It is evident

(3)

\widehat{f}_{n_{k}}\left(i\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\text{ }1,\qquad\text{ if }\qquad i=M_{2n_{k}},...,M_{2n_{k}+1}-1,\\
\text{ }0,\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then we can write

(4)

S_{i}f_{n_{k}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}D_{i}-D_{M_{2n_{k}}},\quad\text{ if }\quad i=M_{2n_{k}}+1,...,M_{2n_{k}+1}-1,\\
f_{n_{k}},\qquad\qquad\quad\text{ if }\quad i\geq M_{2n_{k}+1},\\
0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

From (
1
) we get

(5)

\displaystyle\left\|f_{n_{k}}\right\|_{H_{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}S_{M_{n}}f_{n_{k}}\right\|_{p}=\left\|D_{M_{2n_{k}+1}}-D_{M_{{}_{2n_{k}}}}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|D_{M_{2n_{k}+1}}\right\|_{p}+\left\|D_{M_{{}_{2n_{k}}}}\right\|_{p}\leq cM_{{}_{2n_{k}}}^{1-1/p}<c<\infty.

Let
0<p<1
and
\left\{\lambda_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right\}
be an increasing sequence of the positive integers such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{1/p-1}}{\varphi\left(\lambda_{k}\right)}=\infty.

Let
\ \left\{n_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right\}\subset\left\{\lambda_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right\}
such that

\displaystyle\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{\left(M_{{}_{2n_{k}}}+2\right)^{1/p-1}}{\log{\left(M_{2n_{k}}+2\right)}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+2}\right)}\geq c\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{1/p-1}}{\varphi\left(\lambda_{k}\right)}=\infty.

According to (
4
) we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{L_{M_{2n_{k}}+2}f_{n_{k}}}{\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+2}\right)}\right|=\frac{\left|D_{M_{{}_{2n_{k}}}+1}-D_{M_{{}_{2n_{k}}}}\right|}{l_{M_{2n_{k}}+1}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+1}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\psi_{M_{{}_{2n_{k}}}}\right|}{l_{M_{2n_{k}}+2}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+1}\right)}=\frac{1}{l_{M_{2n_{k}}+1}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+2}\right)}.

Hence,

(6)

\mu\left\{x\in G_{m}:\left|L_{M_{2n_{k}}+2}f_{n_{k}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{l_{M_{2n_{k}}+2}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+2}\right)}\right\}=\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

By combining (
5
) and (
6
) we get that

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{l_{M_{2n_{k}}+2}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+2}\right)}\left(\mu\left\{x\in G_{m}:\quad\left|L_{M_{2n_{k}}+2}f_{n_{k}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{l_{M_{2n_{k}}+2}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+2}\right)}\right\}\right)^{1/p}}{\left\|f_{n_{k}}\right\|_{p}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{M_{{}_{2n_{k}}}^{1/p-1}}{l_{M_{2n_{k}}+2}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+2}\right)}\geq\frac{c\left(M_{{}_{2n_{k}}}+2\right)^{1/p-1}}{\log{\left(M_{2n_{k}}+2\right)}\varphi\left(M_{2n_{k}+2}\right)}\rightarrow\infty,\text{\quad as \quad}k\rightarrow\infty.

Theorem is proved.

\overset{\sim}{L}_{p}^{*}f:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\Theta\left(n+1\right)}

is bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the Lebesgue space
L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)

then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right),
such that the maximal operator

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|L_{n}f\right|}{\varphi\left(n+1\right)}

is not bounded from the Hardy space
H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
to the space

Remark 1
.

According to Theorem 1 we can conclude that there exist absolute constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\frac{C_{1}n^{1/p-1}}{\log(n+1)}\leq\Theta\left(n\right)\leq C_{2}n^{1/p-1}.

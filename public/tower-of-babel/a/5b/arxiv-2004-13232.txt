Full Ellipsoid Embeddings and Toric Mutations
Roger Casals, Renato Vianna

Abstract
Abstract: This article introduces a new method to construct volume-filling symplectic embeddings of 4-dimensional ellipsoids by employing polytope mutations in toric and almost toric varieties. The construction uniformly recovers the full sequences for the Fibonacci Staircase of McDuff-Schlenk, the Pell Staircase of Frenkel-Müller and the Cristofaro-Gardiner-Kleinman Staircase, and adds new infinite sequences of ellipsoid embeddings. In addition, we initiate the study of symplectic-tropical curves for almost toric fibrations and emphasize the connection to quiver combinatorics.

1.1
Context and Results

Let
(\R^{4},\omega_{\text{st}})
be standard symplectic 4-space,
a,b\in\R^{>0}
, and consider the symplectic ellipsoid

E(a_{1},a_{2}):=\left\{(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\in\R^{4}:\frac{x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}}{a_{1}}+\frac{x_{2}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}}{a_{2}}\leq 1\right\}\subseteq(\R^{4},\omega_{\text{st}}).

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a 4-dimensional symplectic almost toric domain, as introduced in Section
2
. The first goal of this article is to discuss the existence of infinite staircases for the function

c_{X}(a):=\inf\{\sigma\mbox{ such that }\exists i:(E(1,a),\omega_{\text{st}})\hookrightarrow(X,\sigma\cdot\omega_{\text{st}})\},

where
i
denotes a
symplectic
embedding, i.e.
i^{*}\omega_{X}=\omega_{\text{st}}
. A
symplectic
embedding is volume preserving, and thus the volume bound
\pi^{2}a\leq 2\sigma^{2}\cdot\operatorname{Vol}(X,\omega_{\text{st}})
implies

\frac{\pi\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{2\operatorname{Vol}(X,\omega_{\text{st}})}}\leq c_{X}(a).

The function
c_{X}(a)
is non-decreasing and continuous. The symplectic non-squeezing phenomenon
[
20
]
states the existence of values
a\in\R^{>0}
for which the above inequality is actually strict. The ground-breaking work of D. McDuff and F. Schlenk
[
44
]
establishes for
(X,\omega_{\text{st}})=(E(1,1),\omega_{\text{st}})
the existence of a non-empty interval
I_{X}=[\alpha_{X},\Omega_{X}]\subseteq\R^{>0}
and a convergent sequence
S=\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\N}\subseteq I_{X}
such that
c_{X}(s_{n})
coincides with the volume lower bound and
c_{X}|_{I_{X}\setminus S}
is strictly larger than the volume bound. The exact graph for the function
c_{X}
in this case is depicted in
[
44
, Figure 1.1]
.

Definition
\thethm
.

A 4-dimensional symplectic domain
(X,\omega_{\text{st}})
is said to admit a
sharp infinite staircase
if there exists a non-zero interval
I_{X}=[\alpha_{X},\Omega_{X}]\subseteq\R^{>0}
and an infinite sequence
S=\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\N}\subseteq I_{X}
of distinct points converging to
\Omega_{X}
, such that
c_{X}(s_{n})
coincides with the volume lower bound and
c_{X}|_{(I_{X}\setminus S)}
is strictly greater than the volume bound. The
sharp points
of a sharp infinite staircase
(I_{X},S)
are the points
S=\{s_{n}\}_{n\in\N}
where the volume bound for
c_{X}|_{I_{X}}
is sharp.
\Box

The existence of a sharp infinite staircase for a 4-dimensional symplectic domain
(X,\omega_{\text{st}})
has been a central question in the study of low-dimensional quantitative symplectic geometry, as beautifully developed by D. McDuff, R. Hind, M. Hutchings, F. Schlenk and many others
[

(X,\omega_{\text{st}})=(\mathbb{D}^{2}(1)\times\mathbb{D}^{2}(1),\omega_{\text{st}}\oplus\omega_{\text{st}})
by Frenkel-Müller
[
16
]
, and for the ellipsoid
(X,\omega_{\text{st}})=(E(2,3),\omega_{\text{st}})
by Cristofaro-Gardiner-Kleinman

First, let us state our main result on the construction of ellipsoid embeddings:

Theorem 1
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})\in\mathscr{H}
be a 4-dimensional symplectic domain. Then the non-decreasing function
c_{X}:\R\longrightarrow\R
admits a sequence of sharp points
S\subseteq I_{X}
.
\Box

Theorem
1
establishes the existence of
sharp
points, which is the constructive ingredient towards infinite staircases. Indeed, the existence of sharp infinite staircases consists of two independent arguments. First, a
constructive
result showing the existence of a sequence of
sharp
points
S\subseteq I_{X}
. Second, an
obstructive
result stating that the volume bound is
not
an equality for the non-sharp points
I_{X}\setminus S
. Our Theorem
1
contributes to the constructive part, which is the part that the present manuscript geometrically establishes.

Important point:
It is crucial to emphasize that, in Theorem
1
, we are able to embed an infinite staircase of ellipsoids into the symplectic domains associated to Figure
1
, and not just into the closed (almost toric) symplectic 4-manifolds: we must show that the ellipsoid embeddings can be made to avoid the required configurations of symplectic 2-spheres and Lagrangian 2-spheres, and this is achieved explicitly by using symplectic-tropical curves, see Sections
3
and
4
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we develop notations for the base diagrams of almost toric fibrations, also known as ATFs
[
52
]
, which we use in our description of symplectic ellipsoid embbedings. The present section is focused on understanding combinatorial mutations of polytopes that describe these almost toric fibrations – particularly from the viewpoint of smoothing, and degenerating, toric orbifolds. The more technical aspects of the symplectic topology shall be presented in Subsection

2.1

We start by succinctly discussing

henceforth abbreviated ATF

Definition 2.1

(
[
37
,

]
)
.

An

of a symplectic 4-manifold

a Lagrangian fibration
\pi:(X,\omega)\rightarrow B

(X,\omega)

\pi

\displaystyle\pi(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2}),\hskip 162.18062pt\text{regular point},

\displaystyle\pi(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}),\hskip 137.99603pt\text{elliptic, corank one},

\displaystyle\pi(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}),\hskip 113.81102pt\text{ elliptic, corank two},

\displaystyle\pi(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2},x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1}),\hskip 76.82234pt\text{ nodal or focus-focus},

Definition 2.5

2.3
Polytope Mutations in Almost-Toric Diagrams

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a 4-dimensional closed

symplectic manifold. The moment map

Recall from Definition

Let

3
Existence of Sharp Sequences of Ellipsoid Embeddings

The central idea that this manuscript introduces is the use of
almost toric mutations
in the study of symplectic ellipsoid embeddings. In this section we prove our main result Theorem
1
, assuming Theorem
6
, whose proof will require the tropical symplectic techniques developed in Section
4
.

Let us start by explaining how to construct symplectic ellipsoid embeddings in an almost toric domain
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
with
(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
a relative almost toric base.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P\subseteq\R^{2}
be a convex polygon and
v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\in V(P)
three vertices of
P
. The
triangle

\overline{T}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}\subseteq P
is the convex hull of
v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}
. For
\varepsilon\in\R_{\geq 0}
, the
open
\varepsilon
-triangle

T^{\varepsilon}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}\subseteq P
is the complement
\overline{T}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}\setminus t^{\varepsilon}
, where
t^{\varepsilon}\subseteq\overline{T}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}
is an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the (open) side
\operatorname{conv}(v_{0},v_{2})
connecting
v_{0},v_{2}\in V(P)
.
\Box

Let
(X,\omega)=X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
be symplectic almost toric manifold with polytope
P\subseteq\mathscr{P}
, and assume that
v_{1}\in V(P)
is a smooth toric vertex. Let
v_{0},v_{2}
be the two vertices in
P
closest to
v_{1}
. If the open triangle
T^{\varepsilon}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}\subseteq P
does not contain any critical values for the almost toric fibration
\mathfrak{m}_{X}:X\longrightarrow P
, the pre-image of
T^{\varepsilon}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}
under
\mathfrak{m}
yields a symplectic embedding

i_{v_{1}}:E(a,b)\longrightarrow(X,\omega),

where
a,b
are the affine lengths of the two sides
\operatorname{conv}(v_{0},v_{1})\cap T^{\varepsilon}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}
,
\operatorname{conv}(v_{1},v_{2})\cap T^{\varepsilon}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}
respectively. This construction is a potential method for construction symplectic ellipsoid embeddings, but it has the following two disadvantages.

First, for a given
\varepsilon\in\R^{>0}
, there are only finitely many open triangles in a moment polytope
P\subseteq\R^{2}
for
(X,\omega)
and thus, even if we considered the
\operatorname{GL}(2,\Z)
action, this observation on its own is not sufficient to build an infinite sequence of ellipsoid embeddings. Second, unless
P
is a triangle, i.e.
|V(P)|=3
, the symplectic ellipsoid embeddings of the form
i_{v_{1}}
will not be volume-filling.

The first new geometric idea is that the symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
admits a sequence of
almost toric
fibrations
(\mathfrak{m}_{n})_{n\in\N}:X\longrightarrow\R^{2}
, as introduced in Section
2.3
, which themselves can be used to construct a sequence of symplectic ellipsoid embeddings. The images
\mathfrak{m}_{n}(X)
of these almost toric fibrations are also convex polytopes
P_{n}\subseteq\R^{2}
. In this almost toric case, the symplectic embeddings
i_{v_{1}}
are built as above, where the main condition is that the open triangles
T^{\varepsilon}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}\subseteq P
do not include any interior singular values of
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
. (This condition is always satisfied in the toric case, as there are no interior singular values for the Lagrangian fibration.)

In Subsection
3.1
, we describe the properties for a sequence of almost toric fibrations
(\mathfrak{m}_{n})_{n\in\N}:X\longrightarrow\R^{2}
such that each almost toric fibration admits a volume-filling open triangle
T^{\varepsilon}_{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}}\subseteq P
, for arbitrarily small
\varepsilon\in\R^{>0}
. These open triangles give rise to symplectic embeddings

i^{(n)}_{v_{1}}:E(a_{n},b_{n})\longrightarrow(X,\omega).

The arithmetic of the sequence of pairs
(a_{n},b_{n})\in\N^{2}
is governed by a diophantine equation which depends on the initial choice of
(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
. These equations have featured prominently in birational geometry
[
23
,
30
,
31
]
and the study of coherent sheaves
[
19
,
49
,
50
]
, and we present them systematically in Subsection
3.2
.

The use of almost toric fibrations has many advantages, including the fact that we can build sequences of almost toric fibrations using the theory of
mutations
in algebraic geometry
[
2
,
3
,
23
]
. Nevertheless, the standard techniques have the disadvantage that it requires our symplectic almost toric manifold
(X,\omega)
to be closed. That said, the known tools for polytope mutations and algebraic degenerations do not include the relative case of symplectic divisors and Lagrangian submanifolds. This article starts developing techniques in this direction.

In Theorem
1
, we are interested in open symplectic toric domains
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
, with
\mathscr{S}\neq\varnothing
. In order to address this dissonance, we first compactify
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
to the closed symplectic toric manifold
X(\mathscr{P})
by adding the surface configuration
D(\mathscr{S})\subseteq(X,\omega)
. Then we construct the sequence of almost toric fibrations
(\mathfrak{m}_{n})_{n\in\N}:X(\mathscr{P})\longrightarrow\R^{2}
and, at the same time, keep track of the images of
D(\mathscr{S})\subseteq(X,\omega)
under these almost toric fibrations. We achieve this latter step by developing a new theory of tropical symplectic curves, in Section
4
, and use the uniqueness of certain symplectic isotopy classes in symplectic 4-manifolds
[
34
,
41
,
42
]
and the classification of Lagrangian 2-spheres in the monotone symplectic 4-manifolds
\C\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\C\mathbb{P}^{1},Bl_{3}(\C\mathbb{P}^{2}),Bl_{4}(\C\mathbb{P}^{2})

[
5
,
25
,
38
,
39
]
.

In conclusion, the two main ingredients to execute the above scheme, and thus prove Theorem
1
, are:

(i)

The construction of a sequence
(\mathfrak{m}_{n})_{n\in\N}:X\longrightarrow\R^{2}
of almost toric fibrations, and its associated volume-filling symplectic ellipsoid embeddings
i^{(n)}:E(a_{n},b_{n})\longrightarrow(X,\omega)
. This is the content of Subsections
3.1
and
3.2
.

(ii)

Showing that the images
i^{(n)}(E(a_{n},b_{n}))
of the symplectic embeddings in Part (i) lie in the complement of the compactifying divisor
D(\mathscr{S})\subseteq X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
. This will be shown in Subsection
3.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1
then proceeds in the following three steps. First, the existence and choice of a Symington Sequence, which addresses the first item above. Second, the construction of tropical configurations realizing
D(\mathscr{S})
at each stage of the Symington Sequence, in Section
4
. Third, the study of the Hamiltonian isotopy class of these configurations in
X(\mathscr{P})
, which is the content of Subsection
3.3
. The second and third steps address the second item above.

3.1
Realization of a Symington Sequence

Given an almost toric symplectic 4-manifold
X(\mathscr{P})
, it is possible to mutate an almost toric fibration for
X(\mathscr{P})
as in Subsection
2.3
in many ways. It is
not
true that
any
mutation, even if the underlying polytope is always triangular, will yield a full symplectic ellipsoid embedding.

Example 3.2
.

We can mutate the triangular polytope
P_{\C\mathbb{P}^{2}}
for
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}=X(P_{\C\mathbb{P}^{2}},\varnothing)
to the toric moment polytope for the weighted projective space
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(2,5,29)
. In fact, there are infinitely many (
\Q
-Gorenstein) degenerations of
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}
obtained by mutating the triangular polytope
P_{\C\mathbb{P}^{2}}
, including
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(5,29,433)
and
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(2,29,169)

[
3
,
23
]
. Neither of these weighted projective spaces readily admits a full symplectic ellipsoid embedding, since the three vertices of their toric moment polytopes are singular.
\Box

Example
3.2
illustrates that a specific choice of sequence of mutations is needed in order to construct full ellipsoid embeddings with our method. This leads to the following definition, which we have named after M. Symington, after her exemplary article
[
52
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

A
Symington sequence
for an almost toric symplectic manifold
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
consists of a sequence of pairs
\{(\mathscr{P}_{n},v_{n})\}_{n\in\N}
such that:

(S1)

X(\mathscr{P}_{n})=X(\mathscr{P})
for all
n\in\N
,

(S2)

P_{n}
is a triangle and
v_{n}\in V(P_{n})
is a vertex,

(S3)

P_{n+1}=\mu_{v_{n}}(P_{n})
, where
\mu_{v_{n}}(P_{n})
is the polytope mutation of
P_{n}
at
v_{n}
,

(S4)

There exists
v_{f}\in\R^{2}
such that
v_{f}\in V(P_{n})
is a smooth toric vertex, for all
n\in\N
.

The vertex
v_{f}
in (S4) will be referred to as a
frozen
vertex, as it does not appear in the sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\N}
specifying the sequence of mutations
(\mu_{v_{n}})_{n\in\N}
.
\Box

The crucial geometric properties in Definition
3.3
are (S1) and (S3), and the fact that we are interested in
ellipsoid
embeddings leads to requiring (S2) and (S4).

Proposition 3
.

Let
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
be a symplectic domain with relative almost toric base
(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})\in\mathscr{H}
. Then there exists a Symington sequence for
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
.

Proof 3.4
.

The statement readily holds for those
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
such that
P\in\mathscr{P}
is a triangle with a smooth toric vertex. Indeed, if
P
is a triangle, a Symington sequence is obtained by choosing one of the two vertices in
P
different from
v_{f}
, and iteratively mutating and choosing the only vertex in the mutated polytope which differs from
v_{f}
and the newly created vertex. In case
P\in\mathscr{P}
is not a triangle, we must directly verify that the almost toric diagram
\mathscr{P}
can be mutated to triangular almost toric diagram with a smooth toric vertex. This is explicitly shown in
[
56
]
, and Figure
1
for
Bl_{3}(\C\mathbb{P}^{2})
.

The arithmetic of the vertices of a Symington sequence for each
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})\in\mathscr{H}
is discussed in the subsequent Subsection
3.2
. The usefulness of a Symington sequence in the study of the Ellipsoid Embedding function
c_{X}
comes from the following:

Proposition 4
.

Let
P\subseteq\R^{2}
be a triangular polytope with a smooth toric vertex
v_{f}\in V(P)
, and let
(a,b)\in\Z^{2}
be the affine length of the two sides of
P
incident to
v_{f}
. Then there exists a full symplectic embedding
i:E(a,b)\longrightarrow X(P)
.
\Box

See
[
35
]
for the notion of a full symplectic embedding. Proposition
4
follows by noticing that the complement of the symplectic divisor associated to the side in
P
non-incident to
v_{f}
is the symplectic domain
E(a,b)

[
17
,
26
,
51
]
. In particular, let
\{(\mathscr{P}_{n},v_{n})\}_{n\in\N}
be a Symington sequence and denote by
(a_{n},b_{n})\in\Z^{2}
the affine lengths of the sides of
P_{n}
incident to
v_{f}
. Then there exist a sequence of symplectic ellipsoid embeddings

i_{n}:E(a_{n},b_{n})\longrightarrow X(P_{n}).

This is at the core of the relation between the existence of infinite staircases and polytope mutations. It is a powerful starting point, but it not enough to conclude Theorem
1
since the symplectic varieties
X(P_{n})
are not isomorphic. As emphasized,
X(P_{n})
are typically singular algebraic varieties, and their algebraic isomorphism type strongly depends on
n\in\N
. That said, the fundamental defining property (S1) of a Symington sequence states that
X(\mathscr{P}_{n})=X(\mathscr{P})
for all
n\in\N
.

The difference between the equalities
X(\mathscr{P}_{n})=X(\mathscr{P})
, for all
n\in\N
, and the sequence of algebraic varieties
X(P_{n})
is contained in the cut content
\mathscr{B}_{n}
in
\mathscr{P}_{n}=(P_{n},\mathscr{B}_{n})
. In particular, Proposition
4
implies the following:

Proposition 5
.

Let
P\subseteq\R^{2}
be a triangular polytope with a smooth toric vertex
v_{f}\in V(P)
, and let
(a,b)\in\Z^{2}
be the affine length of the two sides of
P
incident to
v_{f}
. Suppose that
\mathscr{P}=(P,\mathscr{B})
has empty cut content at
v_{f}
. Then there exists a full symplectic embedding
i:E(a,b)\longrightarrow X(\mathscr{P})
.
\Box

Note that introducing the cut content
\mathscr{B}
can be achieved by choosing arbitrarily short cuts at the vertices of

P
. In this manner, we can choose
N\subseteq P
to be a neighborhood of the side in
P
opposite to
v_{f}
containing the
cuts in
\mathscr{B}
, so that the complement in
X(\mathscr{P})
of the pre-image of
N\subseteq P
is the symplectic ellipsoid

E(a-\varepsilon,b-\varepsilon)
for any
\varepsilon\in\R^{>0}
, depending on the choice of
N
; since
\epsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small, the claim follows by taking into account Remark
1.2
.

Given a Symington sequence

\{(\mathscr{P}_{n},v_{n})\}_{n\in\N}
for
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
, Proposition
3
and Proposition

5
allow us to construct a sequence of full symplectic ellipsoid embeddings

i_{n}:E(a_{n},b_{n})\longrightarrow X(\mathscr{P}).

The challenge is now upgrading these absolute ellipsoid embeddings

(i_{n})_{n\in\N}
to a sequence of relative full symplectic ellipsoid embeddings

\iota_{n}:E(a_{n},b_{n})\longrightarrow X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S}).

This would conclude the proof of Theorem
1
once we study the arithmetic properties
of the Symington sequence
(a_{n},b_{n})
associated to
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
as in Proposition

3
. The focus of Subsection
3.3
and Section

4
is the construction of the relative embeddings
\iota_{n}
into
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
from the
absolute embeddings
i_{n}
into
X(\mathscr{P})
.

The central difficulty in showing the existence of
\iota_{n}
is understanding the surface configuration
D(\mathscr{S})\subseteq X(\mathscr{P})
under the identifications
X(\mathscr{P})\cong X(\mathscr{P}_{n})
, for each
n\in\N
. In order to achieve that, we will develop a diagrammatic tropical calculus for surface configurations
D(\mathscr{S})\subseteq X(\mathscr{P})
in almost toric symplectic manifolds. In particular, such a tropical calculus starts with a configuration
D(\mathscr{S})\subseteq X(\mathscr{P}_{n})
and describes a diagram
\Delta_{n}\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{n}
such that the Hamiltonian isotopy class of the configuration
D(\mathscr{S})
admits a representative contained in the pre-image of this diagram
\Delta_{n}
.

Remark 3.5
.

There is an alternative course of action to construct
\iota_{n}
. It should be possible to understand how the surface configuration
D(\mathscr{S})
is explicitly carried along a polytope mutation through almost toric diagrams
[
52
,
56
]
. Starting with
D(\mathscr{S})\subseteq X(\mathscr{P})
, this would yield an understanding of the inclusions
D(\mathscr{S})\subseteq X(\mathscr{P}_{n})
, upon identifying
X(\mathscr{P}_{n})\cong X(\mathscr{P})
along the mutation sequence. This is an interesting line of research, but we shall not discuss it in the present manuscript.
\Box

Let us now provide a detailed and self-contained account of the numerics appearing in Symington sequences for
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
for
(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})\in\mathscr{H}
.

The polytope

is the following:

Lemma 3.6

Each of the polytopes in Figure

Proof 3.7

This is immediate for the first two rows: the toric moment polytope for

is triangular, the square toric moment polytope for the monotone
\C\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\C\mathbb{P}^{1}
is mutation equivalent to the triangular moment polytope for
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,1,2)
, and the hexagonal moment polytope for the monotone

is mutation equivalent to the triangular moment polytope for
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,2,3)
. These triangular polytopes are depicted in Figure

at the rightmost part for each region corresponding to each symplectic 4-manifold. Finally, the almost toric polytope
Bl_{4}(\C\mathbb{P}^{2})
can be mutated to
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,4,5)
as shown in
[

]
.

This allows us to reduce the arithmetic of Symington sequences for the polytopes in Figure

to those for the weighted projective spaces
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,1,1),\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,1,2),\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,2,3)
and
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,4,5)
. The following result covers all the necessary arithmetic for our Theorem
1
:

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
be the toric moment polytope for
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
.

(i)

Suppose that
\alpha
corresponds to a smoothable singularity
of
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
and divides
(\beta+\gamma)^{2}
, with
\alpha\cdot\delta=(\beta+\gamma)^{2}
.
Then the polytope
P(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
admits a polytope mutation to the triangle
P(\beta,\gamma,\delta)
.

(ii)

(
Vieta jumping
) Suppose that
(p,q,r)\in\Z^{3}
solves the Diophantine equation

C_{0}p^{2}+C_{1}q^{2}+C_{2}r^{2}=mpqr.

Suppose that the
C_{i}
divide
m
,
1\leq i\leq 3
. Then either of the three triples
(p,q,mpq/C_{2}-r)
,
(p,mpr/C_{1}-q,r)
and
(mqr/C_{0}-p,q,r)

solves this Diophantine equation.

\Box

Remark 3.9
.

A toric orbifold singularity being smoothable is equivalent to being a
T
-singularity
[
3
,
32
]
. If
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
is smoothable, then it is of the form

\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(C_{0}p^{2},C_{1}q^{2},C_{2}r^{2})
, where
(p,q,r)\in\Z^{3}
solves the Diophantine
equation above. Vieta jumping is then equivalent to the change
\alpha\leftrightarrow\delta
in
Proposition
3.8
(i), with
\delta=C_{0}(mqr/C_{0}-p)
. Note that Proposition
3.8
is proven in
[

]
for more general toric orbifolds with triangular moment map and smoothable corner, and it appears in the context of Del Pezzo surfaces in
[

]
as Lemma 4.2.
\Box

Arithmetic for
(\C\mathbb{P}^{2},\omega_{\text{st}})
. The toric moment polytope for
(\C\mathbb{P}^{2},\omega_{\text{st}})
is mutation equivalent to the toric moment polytope for
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(p^{2},q^{2},r^{2})
if and only if
(p,q,r)\in\Z^{3}
is a Markov triple, i.e.

p^{2}+q^{2}+r^{2}=3pqr.

This explains the numerics in Example
3.2
. An
arbitrary sequence of Markov triples, even if they differ only in one component,
does
not
yield a Symington Sequence, since condition (S4) in Definition

3.3
imposes a non-trivial constraint. Indeed,

\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(p^{2},q^{2},r^{2})
has a smooth toric vertex if and only if one of the
numbers
p,q,r\in\Z
equals one. Thus a Symington Sequence for

(\C\mathbb{P}^{2},\omega_{\text{st}})
can be obtained using the construction in the proof of
Proposition
3
yielding any of the toric
polytopes for
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,q^{2},r^{2})
, if
(q,r)\in\Z^{2}
satisfy

1+q^{2}+r^{2}=3qr.

Let
F_{n}
be the
n
th odd-index Fibonacci number, starting at
F_{1}=1
. The recursion

F_{n+2}=3F_{n+1}-F_{n}
implies that
(1,F_{n},F_{n+1})
is a Markov triple with

p=1
, as required. Hence, a Symington Sequence for
(\C\mathbb{P}^{2},\omega_{\text{st}})
yields
the sequence of toric polytopes
\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,F^{2}_{n},F^{2}_{n+1})
for all
n\in\N
.
Conversely, all positive integral solutions
(1,q,r)
of the Markov equation are
of the form
(1,F_{n},F_{n+1})
. These Fibonacci solutions are directly obtained
by iteratively applying the Vieta jumping in Proposition
3.8

starting with the minimal solution
(p,q,r)=(1,1,1)
. The
ratio
F_{n+1}/F_{n}
is known to converge to

Arithmetic for
(\C\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\C\mathbb{P}^{1},\omega_{\text{st}}\oplus\omega_{\text{st}})
. The toric moment polytope for

As above, these

\C\mathbb{P}^{2}(1,q^{2},2r^{2})

(q,r)\in\Z^{2}
satisfy

1+q^{2}+2r^{2}=4qr.

Let
P_{n},H_{n}
be the
n
th Pell number and the
n
th half-companion Pell number. They are defined by the initial values

P_{0}=0,P_{1}=1,\quad H_{0}=H_{1}=1,

and the recurrence relations

P_{n}=2P_{n-1}+P_{n-2},\quad H_{n}=2H_{n-1}+H_{n-2}.

The triples
(1,q,r)=(1,H_{2n},P_{2n\pm 1})
verify the above Diophantine equation. Indeed, rewriting the Diophantine equation as
1+2r^{2}=q(4r-q)
and
1+q^{2}=2r(2q-r)
, we see that
mutations induce the recurrence relations:

q_{0}=r_{0}=1,\ \ q_{2n+1}=4r_{2n}-q_{2n},\ \ q_{2n+2}=q_{2n+1},\ \ r_{2n+1}=r_{2n},\ \ r_{2n+2}=2q_{2n+1}-r_{2n+1}.

Then, one shows by induction
that the Pell numbers satisfy
P_{n}=H_{n}-P_{n-1}
, and that the relations
H_{2n+2}=4P_{2n+1}-H_{2n}

and
P_{2n+1}=2H_{2n}-P_{2n-1}
follow from the recurrence relations and
P_{n}=H_{n}-P_{n-1}
.
Therefore, setting
q_{2n}=q_{2n-1}=H_{2n}
and
r_{2n+1}=r_{2n}=P_{2n+1}
, we conclude that the triples
(1,q_{n},r_{n})
form solutions to the above Diophantine equation.

Rescaling the ellipsoid
E(q_{n}^{2},2r_{n}^{2})
and setting

c_{2n-1}=\max{\{2P_{2n-1}^{2}/H_{2n}^{2},H_{2n}^{2}/(2P_{2n-1}^{2})\}},\quad c_{2n}=\max{\{2P_{2n+1}^{2}/H_{2n}^{2},H_{2n}^{2}/(2P_{2n+1}^{2})\}},

Theorem 6
.

Let
X(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})
be a symplectic toric domain, with
(\mathscr{P},\mathscr{S})\in\mathscr{H}

represents any embedded (configuration of) symplectic surface(s)

in the same homology class as the (configuration of) embedded symplectic curve(s) in
D(S)
,

(ii)

There exists a neighborhood
{\mathcal{O}}{\it p}\,(\widetilde{S}_{n})\subseteq P_{n}
and a volume-filling symplectic embedding

\iota_{n}:E(a_{n},b_{n})\longrightarrow X(\mathscr{P}_{n})\setminus{\mathcal{O}}{\it p}\,(\widetilde{S}_{n}).

\Box

6

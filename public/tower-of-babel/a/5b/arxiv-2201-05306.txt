Maximal L p L_{p} - L q L_{q} regularity for the Stokes equations with various boundary conditions in the half space
Naoto Kajiwara

Abstract
We prove resolvent L p L_{p} estimates and maximal L p L_{p} - L q L_{q} regularity estimates for the Stokes equations with Dirichlet, Neumann and Robin boundary conditions in the half space. Each solution is constructed by a Fourier multiplier of x ′ x^{\prime} -direction and an integral of x N x_{N} -direction. We decompose the solution such that the symbols of the Fourier multipliers are bounded and holomorphic. We see that the operator norms are dominated by a homogeneous function of order − 1 -1 for x N x_{N} -direction. The basis are Weis’s operator-valued Fourier multiplier theorem and a boundedness of a kernel operator. We give a new simple approach to get maximal regularity in the half space.



Keywords
: resolvent estimate, maximal regularity, Stokes equations.

1
Introduction

This paper is concerned with resolvent
L_{p}
estimates and maximal
L_{p}
-
L_{q}
regularity for the Stokes equations with three types of boundary conditions in the half-space with
1<p,q<\infty
.
The boundary conditions are Dirichlet(so called non-slip), Neumann and Robin(so called Navier-slip).
The resolvent estimate is used for the generation of analytic semigroups, and the maximal regularity is used to solve quasi-linear evolution equations such as free boundary problems of non-linear Navier–Stokes equations.
The standard way to solve the free boundary problems is to transform of a fixed domain.
The typical methods are known as Lagrangian transform or Hanzawa transform.
Then the equations on a fixed domain often become quasi-linear equations.
The maximal regularity for linearized equations is useful for this solvability.
Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a domain with three disjoint boundaries
\Gamma_{D},\Gamma_{N}
and
\Gamma_{R}
, where
N\geq 2
.
We allow that one or two of them are empty.
We keep in mind the following transformed linearized Stokes problem;

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=f&\quad\text{in}~\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
{\rm div}\,u=g&\quad\text{in}~\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\
u=h_{D}&\quad\text{on}~\Gamma_{D}\times(0,\infty),\\
(D(u)-\pi I)n=h_{N}&\quad\text{on}~\Gamma_{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
\alpha u+\beta(D(u)n-\langle D(u)n,n\rangle n)=h_{R},\quad\langle u,n\rangle=h_{Rn}&\quad\text{on}~\Gamma_{R}\times(0,\infty),\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}&\quad\text{in}~\Omega.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Here unknowns are the velocity
u={}^{t}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})
and the pressure
\pi
, while
f,g,h_{D},h_{N},h_{R},h_{Rn}
and
u_{0}
are given functions,
n
denotes the unit normal outward vector,

and
I

N

N

and

are given by

D(u)_{jk}=\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}+\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{j}},\quad\delta_{jk}=\begin{cases}1~(j=k),\\
0~(j\neq k),\end{cases}

and
\alpha\geq 0,\beta>0
.
Note that the end-point case
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,0)
in Robin boundary condition implies Dirichlet boundary condition.
The inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the standard one in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.
For the sake of simplicity, we set the viscosity coefficient and density equal
1
.
Not only this non-stationary Stokes equations but also the following generalized resolvent problem are analyzed;

\left\{\begin{aligned} \lambda u-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=f&\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
{\rm div}\,u=g&\quad\text{in}~\Omega,\\
u=h_{D}&\quad\text{on}~\Gamma_{D},\\
(D(u)-\pi I)n=h_{N}&\quad\text{on}~\Gamma_{N},\\
\alpha u+\beta(D(u)n-\langle D(u)n,n\rangle n)=h_{R},\quad\langle u,n\rangle=h_{Rn}&\quad\text{on}~\Gamma_{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

This resolvent equation is derived from Laplace transform of the equation (
1
).

In this paper we do not treat the domain with curved boundaries so that the domain is the half-space.
However the domain will be allowed more general domains like a bounded domain by cut-off techniques and localizations.
We do not use such procedures since that is common and the analysis of the half space is the most important steps.
Instead of them, we consider the problem with non-homogeneous data, which is a key to treat non-linear problems.
After a reduction to
f=g=0

h
to the solutions

and

Although these solutions are given by a Fourier multiplier of

-variable, we shall use

We decompose the symbols of the solution operators into new symbols and new independent variables.
Since the new symbol of the Fourier multiplier operator are bounded and holomorphic, we are able to use Fourier multiplier theorem with the connection to Mikhlin conditions.
The theorem is used only for the whole space, but we are able to use the theorem in the tangential direction for the half-space
{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}={\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}\times(0,\infty)

We confirm that the operator norm is dominated by a homogeneous function of order

This kernel operator is known as a bounded operator in

Therefore this shows resolvent estimates.
Note that the new decomposed independent variables become suitable right-hand side of the generalized resolvent estimates.

-bounded in

Therefore we are able to use Weis’s operator-valued Fourier multiplier theorem to get maximal

-

Main theorem

We formulate the resolvent and the non-stationary problems in the half-space.

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}:=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\mid x_{N}>0\},

\displaystyle\quad{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{0}}

\displaystyle:=\{x=(x^{\prime},0)=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1},0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\},

\displaystyle Q_{+}:={{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}\times(0,\infty),

\displaystyle\quad Q_{0}

\displaystyle:={{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{0}}\times(0,\infty).

The resolvent problem is as follows;

\left\{\begin{aligned} \lambda u-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=f&\quad\text{in}~{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}},\\

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}U-\Delta U+\nabla\Pi=F&\quad\text{in}~Q_{+},\\

Definition 3.1
.

A Banach space
X
is said to belong to the class
{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{T}}
if the Hilbert transform
{\mathcal{H}}
, defined by

{\mathcal{H}}f:=\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\to+0}\int_{|t-s|>\varepsilon}\frac{f(s)}{t-s}ds,

is a bounded linear operator on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}},X)
for some
1<p<\infty
.
In this case we write

.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces with norms
\|\cdot\|_{X}
and
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
.
Let
{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
denote the set of all bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
, and
{\mathcal{L}}(X):={\mathcal{L}}(X,X)
.

Definition 3.2
.

A family of operators
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
is called
{\mathcal{R}}
-bounded, if there exist constant
C>0
and
1\leq p<\infty
such that for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}},\{T_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset{\mathcal{T}},\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\subset X
and for all sequences
\{\varepsilon_{j}(u)\}_{j=1}^{m}
of independent, symmetric,
\{-1,1\}
-valued random variables on a probability space

the inequality

|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}T_{j}x_{j}|_{L_{p}(\Omega,Y)}\leq C|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}x_{j}|_{L_{p}(\Omega,X)}

is valid.
The smallest such

is called
{\mathcal{R}}
-bound of
{\mathcal{T}}
, which is denoted by
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathcal{T}})
.

Note that when
X
and

are Hilbert spaces,

is

-bounded if and only if

is uniformly bounded.

Let

and
{\dot{{\mathbb{R}}}}^{n}=[{\dot{{\mathbb{R}}}}]^{n}
.
Given

, we define an operator

F=G=0
are enough to consider in theorems
2.1
and
2.3
.

5
Solution formulas from boundary data

We give the solution of the resolvent problem (
1
) with
f=g=0
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
by Fourier multipliers for each boundary condition.
We apply partial Fourier transform with respect to tangential direction
x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}
so that we use the notations

\displaystyle\hat{v}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}):=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}v(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}e^{-ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi^{\prime}}v(x^{\prime},x_{N})dx^{\prime},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\xi^{\prime}}w(x^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N-1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}e^{ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi^{\prime}}w(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})d\xi^{\prime}

for functions
v,w:{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
.
In this section and section
6
the index
j
runs from
1
to
N-1
if we do not indicate.
We use
A:=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\xi_{j}^{2}}
and
B:=\sqrt{\lambda+A^{2}}
with positive real parts.

5.1
Dirichlet boundary

In this subsection we focus on Dirichlet boundary condition.
By partial Fourier transform, we have the following second order ordinary differential equations;

\left\{\begin{aligned} (\lambda+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\partial_{N}^{2})\hat{u}_{j}+i\xi_{j}\hat{\pi}=0\quad&\text{in}~x_{N}>0,\\
(\lambda+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\partial_{N}^{2})\hat{u}_{N}+\partial_{N}\hat{\pi}=0\quad&\text{in}~x_{N}>0,\\
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}i\xi_{j}\hat{u}_{j}+\partial_{N}\hat{u}_{N}=0\quad&\text{in}~x_{N}>0,\\
\hat{u}=\hat{h}\quad&\text{on}~x_{N}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We find the solution of the form

\hat{u}_{j}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\alpha_{j}e^{-Ax_{N}}+\beta_{j}e^{-Bx_{N}}~(j=1,\ldots,N),\qquad\hat{\pi}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\gamma e^{-Ax_{N}}.

Then, the equations are

\left\{\begin{aligned} \{\alpha_{j}(B^{2}-A^{2})+i\xi_{j}\gamma\}e^{-Ax_{N}}=0,\\
\{\alpha_{N}(B^{2}-A^{2})-A\gamma\}e^{-Ax_{N}}=0,\\
(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}i\alpha_{j}\xi_{j}-A\alpha_{N})e^{-Ax_{N}}+(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}i\beta_{j}\xi_{j}-B\beta_{N})e^{-Bx_{N}}=0,\\
\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j}=\hat{h}_{j},\quad\alpha_{N}+\beta_{N}=\hat{h}_{N}.\end{aligned}\right.

By the linear independence of
e^{-Ax_{N}}
and
e^{-Bx_{N}}
, we are able to find the coefficients
\alpha_{j}
,
\beta_{j}
and
\gamma
;

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}=\frac{i\xi_{j}}{A(B-A)}(i\xi^{\prime}\cdot\hat{h}^{\prime}-B\hat{h}_{N}),

\displaystyle\quad\alpha_{N}=-\frac{i\xi^{\prime}\cdot\hat{h}^{\prime}-B\hat{h}_{N}}{B-A},

\displaystyle\beta_{j}=\hat{h}_{j}-\alpha_{j},

\displaystyle\quad\beta_{N}=\hat{h}_{N}-\alpha_{N},\quad\gamma=-\frac{A+B}{A}(i\xi^{\prime}\cdot\hat{h}^{\prime}-B\hat{h}_{N}),

where
\xi^{\prime}\cdot\hat{h}^{\prime}=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\xi_{k}\hat{h}_{k}
.
We introduce the new notation

{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{e^{-Bx_{N}}-e^{-Ax_{N}}}{B-A}

to treat
B-A
in the denominator.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{j}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left\{\delta_{jk}e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{A}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\right\}\hat{h}_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)+\frac{i\xi_{j}B}{A}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\hat{h}_{N}(\xi^{\prime},0),

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{N}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left\{i\xi_{k}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi,x_{N})\right\}\hat{h}_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)+(e^{-Bx_{N}}-B{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}))\hat{h}_{N}(\xi^{\prime},0),

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left\{-\frac{i\xi_{k}(A+B)}{A}e^{-Ax_{N}}\right\}\hat{h}_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)+\frac{(A+B)B}{A}e^{-Ax_{N}}\hat{h}_{N}(\xi^{\prime},0),

To simplify, we define the symbols;

\displaystyle\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\delta_{jk}e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{A}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\quad\phi_{j,N}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{i\xi_{j}B}{A}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\phi_{N,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=i\xi_{k}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi,x_{N}),

\displaystyle\quad\phi_{N,N}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=e^{-Bx_{N}}-B{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=-\frac{i\xi_{j}(A+B)}{A}e^{-Ax_{N}},

\displaystyle\quad\chi_{N}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{(A+B)B}{A}e^{-Ax_{N}},

which derives the solution formula;

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{j}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\hat{h}_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)\quad(j=1,\ldots,N),

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\hat{h}_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0).

In the next step, we use the Volevich trick
a(\xi^{\prime},0)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{N}a(\xi^{\prime},y_{N})dy_{N}
for a suitable decaying function
a
.
We obtain the solution formula;

\displaystyle u_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[(\partial_{N}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N})){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}h_{k}\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{N}h_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right\}\quad(j=1,\ldots,N),

\displaystyle\pi(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[(\partial_{N}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N})){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}h_{k}\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{N}h_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right\}.

Since Laplace transformed non-stationary Stokes equations (
2
) with
F=G=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}
are the resolvent problem (
1
), we have the following formula for Dirichlet boundary condition;

\displaystyle U_{j}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}_{\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[(\partial_{N}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N})){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}H_{k}\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}(\partial_{N}H_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right\}\quad(j=1,\ldots,N),

\displaystyle\Pi(x,t)

\displaystyle=-{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}_{\lambda}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[(\partial_{N}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N})){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}H_{k}\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.+\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}(\partial_{N}H_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right\}.

5.2
Neumann boundary

The corresponding ordinary differential equations are as follows;

\left\{\begin{aligned} (\lambda+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\partial_{N}^{2})\hat{u}_{j}+i\xi_{j}\hat{\pi}=0\quad&\text{in}~x_{N}>0,\\
(\lambda+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\partial_{N}^{2})\hat{u}_{N}+\partial_{N}\hat{\pi}=0\quad&\text{in}~x_{N}>0,\\
\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}i\xi_{j}\hat{u}_{j}+\partial_{N}\hat{u}_{N}=0\quad&\text{in}~x_{N}>0,\\
-(\partial_{N}\hat{u}_{j}+i\xi_{j}\hat{u}_{N})=\hat{h}_{j},\quad-(2\partial_{N}\hat{u}_{N}-\hat{\pi})=\hat{h}_{N}\quad&\text{on}~x_{N}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

The solutions are given by

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{j}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left\{(\frac{\delta_{jk}}{B}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}(3B-A)}{D(A,B)B})e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{2\xi_{j}\xi_{k}B}{D(A,B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\right\}\hat{h}_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{-i(B-A)}{D(A,B)}\xi_{j}e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{i\xi_{j}(A^{2}+B^{2})}{D(A,B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\right)\hat{h}_{N}(\xi^{\prime},0),

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{N}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left\{\frac{i\xi_{k}(B-A)}{D(A,B)}e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{2i\xi_{k}AB}{D(A,B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\right\}\hat{h}_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\frac{A(A+B)}{D(A,B)}e^{-Bx_{N}}-\frac{A(A^{2}+B^{2})}{D(A,B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\right)\hat{h}_{N}(\xi^{\prime},0),

\displaystyle\hat{\pi}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\left\{\frac{-2i\xi_{k}B(A+B)}{D(A,B)}e^{-Ax_{N}}\right\}\hat{h}_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)+\frac{(A+B)(A^{2}+B^{2})}{D(A,B)}e^{-Ax_{N}}\hat{h}_{N}(\xi^{\prime},0),

where
D(A,B)=B^{3}+AB^{2}+3A^{2}B-A^{3}
.
It is known that
D(A,B)\neq 0
for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
,
\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}\setminus\{0\}
in
[
19
, Lemma 4.4]
.
For the details of Neumann boundary condition, see also
[
23
]
.
Let

\displaystyle\phi_{j,k}^{N}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=(\frac{\delta_{jk}}{B}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}(3B-A)}{D(A,B)B})e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{2\xi_{j}\xi_{k}B}{D(A,B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\phi_{j,N}^{N}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{-i\xi_{j}(B-A)}{D(A,B)}e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{i\xi_{j}(A^{2}+B^{2})}{D(A,B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\phi_{N,k}^{N}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{i\xi_{k}(B-A)}{D(A,B)}e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{2i\xi_{k}AB}{D(A,B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\phi_{N,N}^{N}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{A(A+B)}{D(A,B)}e^{-Bx_{N}}-\frac{A(A^{2}+B^{2})}{D(A,B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{N}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{-2i\xi_{j}B(A+B)}{D(A,B)}e^{-Ax_{N}},\quad\chi_{N}^{N}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{(A+B)(A^{2}+B^{2})}{D(A,B)}e^{-Ax_{N}},

then, the solution formula is written as Dirichlet boundary.

5.3
Robin boundary

The symbols of the solutions are given by

\displaystyle\phi_{j,k}^{R}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=(\frac{\delta_{jk}}{\alpha+\beta B}-\frac{\beta\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{(\alpha+\beta B)(\alpha+\beta(A+B))A})e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{(\alpha+\beta(A+B))A}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\phi_{j,N}^{R}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=-\frac{i\beta\xi_{j}B}{(\alpha+\beta(A+B))A}e^{-Bx_{N}}+\frac{i\xi_{j}B(\alpha+\beta B)}{(\alpha+\beta(A+B))A}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})

\displaystyle\phi_{N,k}^{R}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{i\xi_{k}}{\alpha+\beta(A+B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\phi_{N,N}^{R}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=e^{-Bx_{N}}-\frac{B(\alpha+\beta B)}{\alpha+\beta(A+B)}{\mathcal{M}}_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),

\displaystyle\chi_{j}^{R}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{-i\xi_{j}(A+B)}{(\alpha+\beta(A+B))A}e^{-Ax_{N}},\quad\chi_{N}^{R}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})=\frac{B(A+B)(\alpha+\beta B)}{(\alpha+\beta(A+B))A}e^{-Ax_{N}}.

For the details of Robin boundary condition, see
[
18
,
24
]
although they only treated the case
h_{N}=0
.

6
Proof of resolvent estimates and maximal regularity estimates

6.1
A Sufficient condition

We prepare a theorem to prove the main theorems.
This gives an easy way to show a boundedness of an operator.
Let us difine the operators
T
and
\tilde{T}_{\gamma}
by

\displaystyle T[m]f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}[{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\xi^{\prime}}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}f](x,y_{N})dy_{N},

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{\gamma}[m_{\lambda}]g(x,t)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}[{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\xi^{\prime}}m_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{L}}g](x,y_{N})dy_{N},

\displaystyle=[e^{\gamma t}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\tau\to t}T[m_{\lambda}]{\mathcal{F}}_{t\to\tau}(e^{-\gamma t}g)](x,t),

where
\lambda=\gamma+i\tau\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
,
m,m_{\lambda}:{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
are multipliers, and
f:{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

(i)

Let
m
satisfy the following two conditions:

(a)
There exists
\eta\in(0,\pi/2)
such that
\{m(\cdot,x_{N}),x_{N}>0\}\subset H^{\infty}(\tilde{\Sigma}_{\eta}^{N-1})
.

(b)
There exist
\eta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
C>0
such that
\sup_{\xi^{\prime}\in\tilde{\Sigma}^{N-1}_{\eta}}|m(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})|\leq Cx_{N}^{-1}
for all
x_{N}>0
.

Then
T[m]
is a bounded linear operator on
L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}})
for every
1<q<\infty
.

(ii)
Let
\gamma_{0}\geq 0
and let
m_{\lambda}
satisfy the following two conditions:

(c)
There exists
\eta\in(0,\pi/2-\varepsilon)
such that for each
x_{N}>0
and
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
,

\tilde{\Sigma}_{\eta}^{N}\ni(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\mapsto m_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\in{\mathbb{C}}

is bounded and holomorphic.

(d)
There exist
\eta\in(0,\pi/2-\varepsilon)
and
C>0
such that
\sup\{|m_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})|\mid(\tau,\xi^{\prime})\in\tilde{\Sigma}_{\eta}^{N}\}\leq Cx_{N}^{-1}
for all
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
and
x_{N}>0
.

Then
\tilde{T}_{\gamma}[m_{\lambda}]
satisfies

\|e^{-\gamma t}\tilde{T}_{\gamma}g\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}},L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}))}\leq C\|e^{-\gamma t}g\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}},L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}))}

for every
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
and
1<p,q<\infty
.

Proof.

(i) From the assumptions, equivalence of uniformly boundedness and
{\mathcal{R}}
-boundedness on Hilbert space
{\mathbb{C}}
and Theorem
3.7
, we have

{\mathcal{R}}(\{{\xi^{\prime}}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi^{\prime}}^{\alpha}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\mid\xi^{\prime}\in{\dot{{\mathbb{R}}}}^{N-1},\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{N-1}\})\leq C_{\eta}x_{N}^{-1}.

This means that for each
x_{N}>0
,

\displaystyle\|T[m]f(\cdot,x_{N})\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\|{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}m(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}f(\cdot,y_{N})\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1})}dy_{N}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\|f(\cdot,y_{N})\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1})}}{x_{N}+y_{N}}dy_{N}

from Fourier multiplier theorem.
And then, by Lemma
3.9
,

\|T[m]f\|_{L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}})}

for some
C>0
.

(ii) From the assumptions, equivalence of uniformly boundedness and
{\mathcal{R}}
-boundedness on Hilbert space
{\mathbb{C}}\in{\mathcal{H}}{\mathcal{T}}(\alpha)
, and Theorem
3.7
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(\{{\xi^{\prime}}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi^{\prime}}^{\alpha}(\tau\partial_{\tau})^{\beta}m_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N})\mid\xi^{\prime}\in{\dot{{\mathbb{R}}}}^{N-1},\tau\in{\dot{{\mathbb{R}}}},\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{N-1},\beta\in\{0,1\}\})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\eta}x_{N}^{-1},

where
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
.
This means that for each
x_{N}>0,\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
,

\{(\tau\partial_{\tau})^{\beta}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\xi^{\prime}}m_{\lambda}(\xi^{\prime},x_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}\mid\tau\in{\dot{{\mathbb{R}}}},\beta\in\{0,1\}\}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}))

is
{\mathcal{R}}
-bounded and its
{\mathcal{R}}
-norm is less than
Cx_{N}^{-1}
by theorem
3.6
.
Combining theorem
3.8
with
D=(0,\infty),X=Y=L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}),k_{0}(x_{N},y_{N})=(x_{N}+y_{N})^{-1}
and Lemma
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle\{(\tau\partial_{\tau})^{\beta}T[m_{\lambda}]\mid\tau\in{\dot{{\mathbb{R}}}},\beta\in\{0,1\}\}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}))

is
{\mathcal{R}}
-bounded.
We use Fourier multiplier theorem again to get
{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\tau\to t}T[m_{\lambda}]{\mathcal{F}}_{t\to\tau}
is a bounded linear operator on
L_{p}({\mathbb{R}},L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}))
, which conclude

\|e^{-\gamma t}\tilde{T}_{\gamma}g\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}},L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}))}\leq C\|e^{-\gamma t}g\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}},L_{q}({{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}}))}

for every
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
and
1<p,q<\infty
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

By the same proof, we are able to use anisotropic Lebesgue spaces between tangential direction and normal direction in the sense that

\displaystyle\|T[m]f\|_{L_{q_{1}}(0,\infty,L_{q_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q_{1}}(0,\infty,L_{q_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}))},

\displaystyle\|e^{-\gamma t}\tilde{T}_{\gamma}g\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}},L_{q_{1}}(0,\infty,L_{q_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1})))}

\displaystyle\leq C\|e^{-\gamma t}g\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}},L_{q_{1}}(0,\infty,L_{q_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1})))}

with
1<p,q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
and
\|f\|_{L_{q_{1}}(0,\infty,L_{q_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}))}=(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}}|f(x^{\prime},x_{N})|^{q_{2}}dx^{\prime})^{q_{1}/q_{2}}dx_{N})^{1/q_{1}}
.

6.2
Dirichlet boundary

In section
5
, we obtained the solution formulas for Dirichlet boundary condition.
We use the following identity;

B^{2}=\lambda+\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\xi_{m}^{2},\qquad 1=\frac{B^{2}}{B^{2}}=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}}{B^{2}}\lambda^{1/2}-\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\frac{i\xi_{m}}{B^{2}}(i\xi_{m}).

We decompose the solution operator so that the independent variables become the right-hand side of the estimates;

\displaystyle u_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[B^{-2}\partial_{N}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}((\lambda-\Delta^{\prime})h_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[\lambda^{1/2}B^{-2}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}(\lambda^{1/2}\partial_{N}h_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[i\xi_{m}B^{-2}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{m}\partial_{N}h_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right\}\quad(j=1,\ldots,N),

\displaystyle\pi(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[B^{-2}\partial_{N}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}((\lambda-\Delta^{\prime})h_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[\lambda^{1/2}B^{-2}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}(\lambda^{1/2}\partial_{N}h_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{F}}_{\xi^{\prime}}^{-1}\left[i\xi_{m}B^{-2}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}+y_{N}){\mathcal{F}}_{x^{\prime}}(\partial_{m}\partial_{N}h_{k})\right](x,y_{N})dy_{N}\right\}.

Let
S_{u}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})
and
S_{\pi}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})
be any of symbols;

\displaystyle S_{u}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}):=\begin{cases}B^{-2}\partial_{N}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})&\text{or},\\
\lambda^{1/2}B^{-2}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})&\text{or},\\
i\xi_{m}B^{-2}\phi_{j,k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}),&\end{cases}

\displaystyle S_{\pi}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}):=\begin{cases}B^{-2}\partial_{N}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})&\text{or},\\
\lambda^{1/2}B^{-2}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N})~(k\neq N)&\text{or},\\
i\xi_{m}B^{-2}\chi_{k}^{D}(\lambda,\xi^{\prime},x_{N}).&\end{cases}

Generalizations of Hermitian-Einstein equation of cyclic Higgs bundles, their heat equation, and inequality estimates
Natsuo Miyatake

Abstract
We introduce some generalizations of the Hermitian-Einstein equation for diagonal harmonic metrics on cyclic Higgs bundles, including a generalization using subharmonic functions. When the coefficients are all smooth, we prove the existence, uniqueness, and convergence of the solution of their heat equations with Dirichlet boundary conditions. We also generalize two inequality estimates for solutions of the Hermitian-Einstein equation of cyclic Higgs bundles.

[
6
,
7
]
), we see that
H_{\ast}
is bijective. From the implicit function theorem (see
[
6
,
7
]
), we can find a small
0<\epsilon\ll T
and a
\xi^{\#}\in L^{p}_{2}([0,\epsilon)/0\times M)_{\#}
such that
H(\widetilde{\xi}_{t}+\xi^{\#}_{t})=0
. Then from the regularity theorem for parabolic operators (see
[
6
,
7
]
),
\xi^{\#}_{t}
is smooth on
(0,\epsilon)\times M
.

Thirdly, we show the global existence of a solution to equation (
2
) satisfying the boundary condition. Suppose that for a finite
T>0
, we have a solution
(\xi_{t})_{t\in[0,T)}
satisfying the boundary condition. From Lemma
9
and Lemma
15
, by using the same argument as
[
3
, Corollary 15]
, we see that there exists a continuous map
\xi_{T}:M\rightarrow V
satisfying the boundary condition
\xi_{T}\left.\right|_{\partial M}=\eta\left.\right|_{\partial M}
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}\sup_{M}|\xi_{t}-\xi_{T}|=0
. From Lemma
13
and Lemma
15
, we also see that
\sup_{M}|\Delta_{g_{M}}\xi_{t}|
is bounded for
t\in[0,T)
. Then from the
L^{p}
-estimate (see
[
6
, p.96]
) for Laplacian
\Delta_{g_{M}}
, we see
|\xi_{t}|_{L^{p}_{2}}
is bounded for any
1<p<\infty
, and thus
\sup_{M}(|d\xi_{t}|+|\xi_{t}|)
is bounded for
t\in[0,T)
. Then from Lemma
14
, we see that
f\coloneqq|dF(\xi_{t})|^{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dt}+\Delta_{g_{M}}\right)f\leq A(f+1)

for a positive constant
A
. Then from the argument of
[
7
, pp.120-121, proof of (8.15)]
and the existence theorem of the solution of the linear heat equation
[
6
, pp.116-118]
, we see that
\sup_{M}|dF(\xi_{t})|
is bounded for
t\in[0,T)
. Then by repeating the argument above, we see that
\sup_{M}(|\xi_{t}|+|d\xi_{t}|+|\nabla^{2}\xi_{t}|)
is bounded for
t\in[0,T)
. By induction, we see that
\sup_{M}|\nabla^{k}F(\xi_{t})|
and
\sup_{M}(\sum_{j=0}^{k}|\nabla^{j}\xi_{t}|)
are bounded for every
k
. From this, we also easily see that
|\nabla^{l}\frac{d^{k}\xi_{t}}{dt^{k}}|
is bounded for all
k
and
l
. Therefore,
(\xi_{t})_{t\in[0,T)}
converges to
\xi_{T}
in
C^{\infty}
. This implies the global existence of a solution of equation (
2
).

Fourthly, for the solution
(\xi_{t})_{t\in[0,\infty)}
of equation (
2
) satisfying the boundary condition (
3
), we prove (i) of the latter statement of Theorem
1
. Suppose that
\partial M
is not empty. We first note the uniqueness of the solution of equation (
1
) satisfying the same boundary condition follows from Lemma
10
and the maximum principle (see
[
4
, p.94]
). For the rest of the proof of (i) of Theorem
1
, we use the argument of
[
4
]
. Since
F(\xi_{t})\left.\right|_{\partial M}=0
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and we have Lemma
13
, by applying
[
4
, Lemma on p.98]
to
|F(\xi_{t})|^{2}
, we see that there exists a positive constant
C
and
\mu
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{M}|F(\xi_{t})|^{2}\leq Ce^{-\mu t}\ \text{for all $t\in[0,\infty)$}.

From this, we see that
\sup_{M}|\xi_{t}|
is bounded for
t\in[0,\infty)
. Then by the same argument as in the proof of the global existence of
(\ref{heat equation})
above, we see that for all
k=0,1,2,\dots,

\sup_{M}(\sum_{j=0}^{k}|\nabla^{j}\xi_{t}|)
and
\sup_{M}|\nabla^{k}F(\xi_{t})|
are bounded for
t\in[0,\infty)
and thus
|\nabla^{l}\frac{d^{k}\xi_{t}}{dt^{k}}|
is bounded for all
k
and
l
. Therefore we can find a sequence
(t_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
going to infinity and a smooth
\xi:M\rightarrow V
such that
\xi_{t_{i}}\rightarrow\xi
in
C^{\infty}
. This
\xi
solves equation (
1
) and satisfies the Dirichlet boundary condition.

Finally, we prove (ii) of Theorem
1
. Suppose that the boundary
\partial M
is empty and that for each
j=1,\dots,r
,
a_{j}^{-1}(0)
is a measure
0
set and
\log a_{j}
is integrable. Let
E
be the functional introduced in
[
11
]
. From the assumption, we see that
E
is bounded below and the estimate of
[
11
, Lemma 3]
holds. By calculation we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(\xi_{t})=-\int_{M}|F(\xi_{t})|^{2}.

In particular,
E(\xi_{t})
is bounded above and thus
|\xi_{t}|_{L^{2}}
is bounded for
t\in[0,\infty)
. Then from
[
10
, pp.72-73]
(see
[
11
, Lemma 6]
) and the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 5]
, we see that
\sup_{M}|\xi_{t}|
is bounded for
t\in[0,\infty)
. Then by repeating the argument of the proof of (i) of Theorem
1
, we can find a sequence
(t_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
going to infinity and a smooth
\xi:M\rightarrow V
such that
\xi_{t_{i}}\rightarrow\xi
in
C^{\infty}
. This
\xi
solves equation (
1
). The uniqueness of the solution of equation (
1
) follows from
[
11
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

For each
j=1,\dots,r
, let
f_{j}\coloneqq(\xi-\xi^{\prime},u_{j})
. We set
\varphi\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}e^{f_{j}/2}u_{j}
,
\widetilde{\varphi}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}e^{f_{j}}u_{j}
. Then we can calculate
\Delta_{g_{M}}\log|\sum_{j=1}^{r}e^{(\xi-\xi^{\prime},u_{j})}|=\Delta_{g_{M}}\log|\varphi|^{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{M}}\log|\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{g_{M}}|\varphi|^{2}}{|\varphi|^{2}}+\frac{|d|\varphi|^{2}|^{2}}{|\varphi|^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(\Delta_{g_{M}}\varphi,\varphi)}{|\varphi|^{2}}-\frac{2|d\varphi|^{2}}{|\varphi|^{2}}+\frac{4|d\varphi|^{2}}{|\varphi|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(\Delta_{g_{M}}\varphi,\varphi)}{|\varphi|^{2}}+\frac{2|d\varphi|^{2}}{|\varphi|^{2}},

where we have used
d|\varphi|^{2}=2(d\varphi,\varphi)
and the inequality
|(d\varphi,\varphi)|^{2}\leq|d\varphi|^{2}|\varphi|^{2}
. Furthermore,
\Delta_{g_{M}}\varphi
is calculated as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{M}}\varphi=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{g_{M}}f_{j}e^{\frac{f_{j}}{2}}u_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{4}|df_{j}|^{2}e^{\frac{f_{j}}{2}}u_{j}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle(\Delta_{g_{M}}\varphi,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{g_{M}}f_{j}e^{\frac{f_{j}}{2}}u_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{4}|df_{j}|^{2}e^{\frac{f_{j}}{2}}u_{j},\varphi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta_{g_{M}}(\xi-\xi^{\prime}),\widetilde{\varphi})-|d\varphi|^{2}.

Then we have the following:

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{M}}\log|\varphi|^{2}\leq\frac{(\Delta_{g_{M}}(\xi-\xi^{\prime}),\widetilde{\varphi})}{|\varphi|^{2}}.

Since, for each
j=1,\dots,r
, the function

\displaystyle((e^{(v_{j},\xi)}-e^{(v_{j},\xi^{\prime})})v_{j},\widetilde{\varphi})=(e^{(v_{j},\xi)}-e^{(v_{j},\xi^{\prime})})(e^{(\xi-\xi^{\prime},u_{j})}-e^{(\xi-\xi^{\prime},u_{j+1})})

is a positive function, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{M}}\log|\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(\Delta_{g_{M}}(\xi-\xi^{\prime}),\widetilde{\varphi})}{|\varphi|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\varphi|^{2}}\{(\Delta_{g_{M}}\xi+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}e^{(v_{j},\xi)}v_{j}-w,\widetilde{\varphi})-(\Delta_{g_{M}}\xi^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}e^{(v_{j},\xi^{\prime})}v_{j}-w,\widetilde{\varphi})\}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|\widetilde{\varphi}|}{|\varphi|}\{|\Delta_{g_{M}}\xi+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}e^{(v_{j},\xi)}v_{j}-w|+|\Delta_{g_{M}}\xi^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}e^{(v_{j},\xi^{\prime})}v_{j}-w|\}.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\Delta_{g_{M}}\xi+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}e^{(v_{j},\xi)}v_{j}-w|+|\Delta_{g_{M}}\xi^{\prime}+\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}e^{(v_{j},\xi^{\prime})}v_{j}-w|.

Then we have the desired claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
\xi
be a
C^{2}
-solution of (
1
). For each
j=1,\dots,r
, let
f_{j}\coloneqq(v_{j},\xi)+\log(a_{j})
. We set
\varphi\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}e^{\frac{1}{2}f_{j}}u_{j},\Psi\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}e^{f_{j}}v_{j}
. By the same calculation as in the proof of Theorem
2
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{g_{M}}\log|\varphi|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\varphi|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Delta_{g_{M}}f_{j}e^{f_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\varphi|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Delta_{g_{M}}((v_{j},\xi)+\log(a_{j}))e^{f_{j}}.

By assumption, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\varphi|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Delta_{g_{M}}((v_{j},\xi)+\log(a_{j}))e^{f_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\varphi|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\Delta_{g_{M}}\xi-w,v_{j})e^{f_{j}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|\varphi|^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\Psi,v_{j})e^{f_{j}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{|\Psi|^{2}}{|\varphi|^{2}}.

Then we have the desired claim.
∎

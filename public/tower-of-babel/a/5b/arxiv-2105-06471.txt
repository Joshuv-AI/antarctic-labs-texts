Tensor Expander Chernoff Bounds
Shih Yu Chang

Abstract
The Chernoff bound is an important inequality relation in probability theory. The original version of the Chernoff bound is to give an exponential decreasing bound on the tail distribution of sums of independent random variables. Recent years, several works have been done by extending the original version of the Chernoff bound to high-dimensional random objects, e.g., random matrices, or/and to consider the relaxation that there is no requirement of independent assumptions among random objects. In this work, we generalize the matrix expander Chernoff bound studied by Garg et al. [ 8 ] to tensor expander Chernoff bounds . Our main tool is to develop new tensor norm inequalities based on log-majorization techniques. These new tensor norm inequalities are used to bound the expectation of Ky Fan norm of the random tensor exponential function, then tensor expander Chernoff bounds can be established. Compared with the matrix expander Chernoff bound, the tensor expander Chernoff bounds proved at this work contributes following aspects: (1) the random objects dimensions are increased from matrices (two-dimensional data array) to tensors (multidimensional data array); (2) this bound generalizes the identity map of the random objects summation to any polynomial function of the random objects summation; (3) Ky Fan norm, instead only the maximum or the minimum eigenvalues, for the function of the random objects summation is considered; (4) we remove the restriction about the summation of all mapped random objects is zero, which is required in the matrix expander Chernoff bound derivation.

n
nonnegative real vectors with the dimension

\mathbf{b}_{i}=(b_{i_{1}},\cdots,b_{i_{r}})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}

\displaystyle\rho\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}b_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{i}},\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}b_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{i}},\cdots,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}b_{i_{r}}^{\alpha_{i}}\right)\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\rho^{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbf{b}_{i})

(22)

\displaystyle\rho\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}b_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{i}},\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}b_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{i}},\cdots,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}b_{i_{r}}^{\alpha_{i}}\right)=\rho\left(\odot_{i=1}^{m+1}\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\right),

(23)

where
\odot_{i=1}^{m+1}\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
is defined as
\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}b_{i_{1}}^{\alpha_{i}},\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}b_{i_{2}}^{\alpha_{i}},\cdots,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}b_{i_{r}}^{\alpha_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle\left[\rho^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}}\left(\odot_{i=1}^{m}\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}}}\right)\right]\cdot\rho^{\alpha_{m+1}}(\mathbf{b}_{m+1})\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m+1}\rho^{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbf{b}_{i}).

By mathematical induction, this lemma is proved.

Theorem 13

3.1

is the Ky Fan
k
-norm defined by Eq. (
19

f

11

\displaystyle\vec{\lambda}(f(\mathcal{C}))=f(\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{C}))\prec_{w}f\left(\int_{\Omega^{r}}\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu^{r}(\tau)\right).

(34)

\displaystyle f\left(\int_{\Omega^{r}}\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})d\nu^{r}(\tau)\right)\leq\int_{\Omega^{r}}f(\vec{\lambda}(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}))d\nu^{r}(\tau)=\int_{\Omega^{r}}\vec{\lambda}(f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}))d\nu^{r}(\tau).

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho(\vec{\lambda}(f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})))d\nu(\tau)=\int_{\Omega}\left\|f(\mathcal{D}_{\tau})\right\|_{(k)}d\nu(\tau).

\det\nolimits_{H}
definition.

\hfill\Box

3.3
Multivaraite Tensor Norm Inequalities

In this section, we will apply derived majorization inequalities for tensors to multivaraite tensor norm inequalities which will be used to derive tensor expander bounds. We will begin to present a Lie-Trotter product formula for tensors.

Lemma 18
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
(\mathcal{L}_{k})_{k=1}^{m}
be a finite sequence of bounded tensors with dimensions
\mathcal{L}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{m}\exp(\frac{\mathcal{L}_{k}}{n})\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathcal{L}_{k}\right)

(95)

Proof:

We will prove the case for
m=2
, and the general value of
m
can be obtained by mathematical induction.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
be bounded tensors act on some Hilbert space. Define
\mathcal{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\exp((\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2})/n)
, and
\mathcal{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\exp(\mathcal{L}_{1}/n)\star_{M}\exp(\mathcal{L}_{2}/n)
. Note we have following estimates for the norm of tensors
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}\right\|,\left\|\mathcal{D}\right\|\leq\exp\left(\frac{\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|}{n}\right)=\left[\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)\right]^{1/n}.

(96)

From the Cauchy-Product formula, the tensor
\mathcal{D}
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(\mathcal{L}_{1}/n)\star_{M}\exp(\mathcal{L}_{2}/n)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mathcal{L}_{1}/n)^{i}}{i!}\star_{M}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mathcal{L}_{2}/n)^{j}}{j!}

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}n^{-l}\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{i}}{i!}\star_{M}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{l-i}}{(l-i)!},

then we can bound the norm of
\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}
as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left([\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}]/n\right)^{i}}{i!}-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}n^{-l}\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{i}}{i!}\star_{M}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{l-i}}{(l-i)!}\right\|

(98)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}k^{-i}\frac{\left([\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{2}]\right)^{i}}{i!}-\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}n^{-l}\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{i}}{i!}\star_{M}\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{l-i}}{(l-i)!}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left[\exp(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|)+\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}n^{-l}\sum_{i=0}^{l}\frac{\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|^{i}}{i!}\cdot\frac{\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|^{l-i}}{(l-i)!}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left[\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)+\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}n^{-l}\frac{(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|)^{l}}{l!}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)}{n^{2}}.

For the difference between the higher power of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, we can bound them as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}^{n}-\mathcal{D}^{n}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{C}^{m}(\mathcal{C}-\mathcal{D})\mathcal{C}^{n-l-1}\right\|

(99)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\exp(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|)\cdot n\cdot\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}-\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|,

where the inequality
\leq_{1}
uses the following fact

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}\right\|^{l}\left\|\mathcal{D}\right\|^{n-l-1}\leq\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\exp\left(\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right),

(100)

based on Eq. (
96
). By combining with Eq. (
98
), we have the following bound

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{C}^{n}-\mathcal{D}^{n}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\exp\left(2\left\|\mathcal{L}_{1}\right\|+2\left\|\mathcal{L}_{2}\right\|\right)}{n}.

(101)

Then this lemma is proved when
n
goes to infity.
\hfill\Box

Theorem 19
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
be positive Hermitian tensors for
1\leq i\leq n
with Hermitian rank
r
,
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{(k)}
be Ky Fan
k
-norm. For any continous function
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
x\rightarrow\log f(e^{x})
is convex on
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\left(\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)\right)\right\|_{(k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|f\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right|\right)\right\|_{(k)}\beta_{0}(t)dt,

(102)

where
\beta_{0}(t)=\frac{\pi}{2(\cosh(\pi t)+1)}
.

For any continous function
g(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
x\rightarrow g(e^{x})
is convex on
\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|g\left(\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)\right)\right\|_{(k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right|\right)\right\|_{(k)}\beta_{0}(t)dt.

(103)

Proof:

From Hirschman interpolation theorem
[
22
]
and
\theta\in[0,1]
and setting
\sqrt{-1}
as
\iota
, we have

\displaystyle\log\left|h(\theta)\right|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left|h(\iota t)\right|^{1-\theta}\beta_{1-\theta}(t)dt+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left|h(1+\iota t)\right|^{\theta}\beta_{\theta}(t)dt,

(104)

where
h(z)
be uniformly bounded on
S\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:0\leq\Re(z)\leq 1\}
and holomorphic on
S
. The term
\beta_{\theta}(t)
is defined as :

\displaystyle\beta_{\theta}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\frac{\sin(\pi\theta)}{2\theta(\cos(\pi t)+\cos(\pi\theta))}.

(105)

Let
H(z)
be a uniformly bounded holomorphic function with values in
\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}
. Fix some
\theta\in[0,1]
and let
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times 1}
be normalized tensors such that
\langle\mathcal{U},\mathcal{H}(\theta)\star_{N}\mathcal{V}\rangle=\left\|H(\theta)\right\|
. If we define
h(z)
as
h(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\langle\mathcal{U},\mathcal{H}(z)\star_{N}\mathcal{V}\rangle
, we have following bound:
\left|h(z)\right|\leq\left\|H(z)\right\|
for all
z\in S
. From Hirschman interpolation theorem, we then have following interpolation theorem for tensor-valued function:

\displaystyle\log\left\|H(\theta)\right\|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|H(\iota t)\right\|^{1-\theta}\beta_{1-\theta}(t)dt+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|H(1+\iota t)\right\|^{\theta}\beta_{\theta}(t)dt.

(106)

Let
H(z)=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}^{z}_{i}
. Then the first term in the R.H.S. of Eq. (
106
) is zero since
H(\iota t)
is a product of unitary tensors. Then we have

\displaystyle\log\left\|\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right\|\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right\|\beta_{\theta}(t)dt.

(107)

From Lemma
10
, we have following relations:

\displaystyle\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\wedge^{k}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}=\wedge^{k}\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}^{\theta}_{i}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}},

(108)

and

\displaystyle\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\wedge^{k}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right)^{1+\iota t}\right|=\wedge^{k}\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}^{1+\iota t}_{i}\right|.

(109)

If Eq. (
107
) is applied to
\wedge^{k}\mathcal{C}_{i}
for
1\leq k\leq r
, we have following log-majorization relation from Eqs. (
108
) and (
109
):

\displaystyle\log\vec{\lambda}\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)\prec\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\vec{\lambda}\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\beta_{\theta}(t)dt.

(110)

Moreover, we have the equality condition in Eq. (
110
) for
k=r
due to following identies:

\displaystyle\det\nolimits_{H}\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}=\det\nolimits_{H}\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right|=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\det\nolimits_{H}\mathcal{C}_{i}.

(111)

At this stage, we are ready to apply Theorem
16
for the log-majorization provided by Eq. (
110
) to get following facts:

\displaystyle\left\|f\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)\right\|_{(k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log\left\|f\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right|\right)\right\|_{(k)}\beta_{\theta}(t)dt,

(112)

and

\displaystyle\left\|g\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)\right\|_{(k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g\left(\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{1+\iota t}\right|\right)\right\|_{(k)}\beta_{\theta}(t)dt.

(113)

From Lie product formula for tensors given by Lemma
18
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{C}_{i}^{\theta}\right|^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\rightarrow\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathcal{C}_{i}\right).

(114)

By setting
\theta\rightarrow 0
in Eqs. (
112
), (
113
) and using Lie product formula given by Eq. (
114
), we will get Eqs. (
102
) and (
103
).

4
Tensor Expander Chernoff Bounds Derivation by Majorization

In this section, we will begin with the derivation for the expectation bound of Ky Fan
k
-norm for the product of positive Hermitian tensors in Section
4.1
. This bound will play a key role in the next Section
4.2
by establishing tensor expander chernoff bounds.

4.1
Expectation Estimation for Product of Tensors

The main purpose of this section is to bound the expectation of Ky Fan
k
-norm for the product of positive Hermitian tensors. We extend scalar valued expander Chernoff bound proof in
[
10
]
and matrix valued expander Chernoff bound proof in
[
8
]
to context of tensors and remove the restriction that the summation of all mapped tensors should be zero tensor, i.e.,
\sum\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\mathrm{g}(v)=\mathcal{O}
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be the normalized adjacency matrix of the underlying graph
\mathfrak{G}
and let
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{A}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})^{2}}
, where the identity tensor
\mathcal{I}_{(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})^{2}}
has dimensions as
I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\times I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}
. We use
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{\left(n\times I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\right)\times\left(n\times I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\right)}
to represent block diagonal tensor valued matrix where the
v
-th diagonal block is the tensor

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{v}=\exp\left(\frac{t\mathrm{g}(v)(a+\iota b)}{2}\right)\otimes\exp\left(\frac{t\mathrm{g}(v)(a-\iota b)}{2}\right).

(115)

The tensor
\mathcal{F}
can also be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathcal{T}_{v_{1}}&\mathcal{O}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}\\
\mathcal{O}&\mathcal{T}_{v_{2}}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathcal{O}&\mathcal{O}&\cdots&\mathcal{T}_{v_{n}}\\
\end{array}\right].

Then the tensor
\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{\kappa}
is a block tensor valued matrix whose
(u,v)
-block is a tensor with dimensions as
I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\times I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}
expressed as :

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{v_{1},\cdots,v_{\kappa-1}\in\mathfrak{V}}\mathbf{A}_{u,v_{1}}\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\kappa-2}\mathbf{A}_{v_{j},v_{j+1}}\right)\mathbf{A}_{v_{\kappa-1},v}\left(\mathcal{T}_{u}\star_{2M}\mathcal{T}_{v_{1}}\star_{2M}\cdots\star_{2M}\mathcal{T}_{v_{\kappa-1}}\right)

(121)

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\mathbb{I}^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{I}^{2}_{M}}
be the tensor obtained by
\frac{\mathbf{1}}{\sqrt{n}}\otimes\mbox{{col}}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}})
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the all ones vector with size
n
and
\mbox{{col}}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}})\in\mathbb{C}^{I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\times 1}
is the column tensor of the identity tensor
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
. By applying the following relation:

\displaystyle\bigl<\mbox{{col}}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}),\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{B}\star_{M}\mbox{{col}}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}})\bigr>=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\mathcal{C}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}^{T}\right),

(122)

where
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
;
we will have following expectation of
\kappa
steps transition of Hermitian tensors from the vertex
v_{1}
to the vertex
v_{\kappa}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{\kappa}\exp\left(\frac{t\mathrm{g}(v_{i})(a+\iota b)}{2}\right)\star_{M}\prod\limits_{i=\kappa}^{1}\exp\left(\frac{t\mathrm{g}(v_{i})(a-\iota b)}{2}\right)\right)\right]=

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\biggl<\mbox{{col}}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}),\prod_{i=1}^{\kappa}\mathcal{T}_{v_{i}}\star_{M}\mbox{{col}}(\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}})\biggr>\right]=\biggl<\mathbf{u}_{0},\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{\kappa}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}_{0}\biggr>.

(123)

If we define
\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\right)^{\kappa}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}_{0}
as
\mathbf{u}_{\kappa}
, the goal of this section is to estimate
\bigl<\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{u}_{\kappa}\bigr>
.

The trick is to separate the space of
\mathbf{u}
as the subspace spanned by the
(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})^{2}
tensors
\mathbf{1}\otimes e_{i}
denoted by
\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}
, where
1\leq i\leq(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})^{2}
and
e_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\times 1}
is the column tensor of size
(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})^{2}
with 1 in position
i
and 0 elsewhere, and its orthogonal complement space, denoted by
\mathbf{u}^{\perp}
. Following lemma is required to bound how the tensor norm is changed in terms of aforementioned subspace and its orthogonal space after acting by the tensor
\mathcal{F}\star_{2M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}
.

Lemma 20
.

Given paramters
\lambda\in(0,1)
,
a\geq 0
,
r>0
, and
t>0
. Let
\mathfrak{G}=(\mathfrak{V},\mathfrak{E})
be a regular
\lambda
-expander graph on the vetices set
\mathfrak{V}
and
\left\|\mathrm{g}(v_{i})\right\|\leq r
for all
v_{i}\in\mathfrak{V}
. Each vertex
v\in\mathfrak{V}
will be assigned a tensor
\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v}
, where
\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\frac{\mathrm{g}(v)(a+\iota b)}{2}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}+\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}\otimes\frac{\mathrm{g}(v)(a-\iota b)}{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\times I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}}
. Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{C}^{\left(n\times I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\right)\times\left(n\times I^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times I^{2}_{M}\right)}
to represent block diagonal tensor valued matrix where the
v
-th diagonal block is the tensor
\exp(t\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v})=\mathcal{T}_{v}
. For any tensor
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times\mathbb{I}^{2}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{I}^{2}_{M}}
, we have

1.

\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right)^{\parallel}\right\|\leq\gamma_{1}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right\|
, where
\gamma_{1}=\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})
;

2.

\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right)^{\parallel}\right\|\leq\gamma_{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right\|
, where
\gamma_{2}=\lambda(\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})-1)
;

3.

\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right)^{\perp}\right\|\leq\gamma_{3}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right\|
, where
\gamma_{3}=\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})-1
;

4.

\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right)^{\perp}\right\|\leq\gamma_{4}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right\|
, where
\gamma_{4}=\lambda\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})
.

Proof:

For Part 1, let
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
be all ones vector, and let
\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}=\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{v}
for some
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{(\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})^{2}}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right)^{\parallel}=\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right)^{\parallel}=\mathbf{1}\otimes\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\exp(t\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v})\star_{M}\mathbf{v}\right)

(124)

and we can bound
\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\exp(t\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v})\star_{M}\mathbf{v}\right)
further as

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\exp(t\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v})\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{i}\acute{\mathcal{T}}^{i}_{v}}{i!}\right\|

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}+\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{i}\acute{\mathcal{T}}^{i}_{v}}{i!}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{i}\left\|\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v}\right\|^{i}}{i!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})^{i}}{i!}=\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}),

where the last inequality is due to the fact that
\left\|\frac{t\mathrm{g}(v)(a+\iota b)}{2}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}+\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}}\otimes\frac{t\mathrm{g}(v)(a-\iota b)}{2}\right\|\leq 2tr\times\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}}{4}}
.

Then Part 1. of this lemma is established due to

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right)^{\parallel}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\exp(t\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v})\mathbf{v}\right\|

(126)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})=\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right\|.

For Part 2, since
(\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp})^{\parallel}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right)^{\parallel}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|((\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{I})\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp})^{\parallel}\right\|

(127)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{I})\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\left\|\exp(t\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v})-\mathcal{I}\right\|\cdot\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{i}\acute{\mathcal{T}}^{i}_{v}}{i!}\right\|\cdot\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right\|\leq(\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})-1)\lambda\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right\|,

where the last inequality uses that the underlying graph
\mathfrak{G}
is a
\lambda
-expander graph, i.e.,
\left\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\|\leq\lambda\cdot\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|
. Therefore, Part 2 is also valid.

For Part 3, because
(\mathbf{u}^{\parallel})^{\perp}=0
, we have
(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel})^{\perp}=(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel})^{\perp}=((\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{I})\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel})^{\perp}
. Then, we can upper bound as

\displaystyle\left\|((\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{I})\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel})^{\perp}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{I})\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right\|

(128)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathcal{F}-\mathcal{I}\right\|\cdot\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\left\|\exp(t\acute{\mathcal{T}}_{v})-\mathcal{I}\right\|\cdot\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\limits_{v\in\mathfrak{V}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{i}\acute{\mathcal{T}}^{i}_{v}}{i!}\right\|\cdot\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right\|\leq(\exp(tr\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})-1)\left\|\mathbf{u}^{\parallel}\right\|,

hence, Part 3 is also proved.

Finally, for Part 4, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp})^{\perp}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}\star_{M+1}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}\star_{M+1}\mathbf{u}^{\perp}\right\|

(129)

\displaystyle\leq

4.2

We begin with a lemma about a Ky Fan
k
-norm inequality for the sum of tensors.

Lemma 22
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{N}}

\displaystyle\left\|\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}\leq m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}

(140)

where
s\geq 1
and
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,r\}
.

Proof:

Since we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{j}\left(\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\lambda^{s}_{j}\left(\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|\right)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma^{s}_{j}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right).

(141)

where we have orders for eigenvalues as
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots
, and singular values as
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\cdots
.

From Theorem G.1.d. in
[
17
]
and Theorem 5.2 in
[
19
]
, we have Ky Fan singular value majorization inequalities:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma_{j}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i})\leq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right),

(142)

where
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,s\}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\sigma^{s}_{j}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{C}_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right)^{s}\leq m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{s}_{j}(\mathcal{C}_{i})\right)

(143)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma^{s}_{j}(\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|)\right)=m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{j}(\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{s-1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\left|\mathcal{C}_{i}\right|^{s}\right\|_{(k)}

5
Conclusions

In this work, we first build tensor norm inequalities based on the concept of log-majorization, and apply these new tensor norm inequalities to derive the
tensor expander Chernoff bounds
which generalize the matrix expander Chernoff bound by adopting more general norm for tensors, Ky Fan norm, and general convex function, instead of identity function, of random tensors summation.

There are several future directions that can be explored based on the current work. The first is to consider other types of tensor expander Chernoff bounds under other non-independent assumptions among random tensors. The other direction is to characterize random behaviors of other tensor related quantities besides norms or eigenvalues, for example, what is the Hermitian tensor rank behavior for the summation of random tensors.

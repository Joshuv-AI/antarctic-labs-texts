On the Global Well-Posedness and Gevrey Regularity of some Electrodiffusion Models
Elie Abdo, Fizay-Noah Lee, Weinan Wang

Abstract
. We consider the Nernst-Planck equations describing the nonlinear time evolution of multiple ionic concentrations in a two-dimensional incompressible fluid. The velocity of the fluid evolves according to either the Euler or Darcy’s equations, both forced nonlinearly by the electric forces generated by the presence of charged ions. We address the global well-posedness and Gevrey regularity of the resulting electrodiffusion models in the periodic setting.



1.
Introduction

Electrodiffusion of ions in a fluid is governed by three main mechanisms: diffusion driven by the gradient of the ionic concentrations, transport driven by the gradient of the electrical potential due to the presence of ions, and transport due to the fluid. Mathematically, electrodiffusion is modeled by the Nernst-Planck equations, which are mass-balance equations describing the time evolution of local ionic concentrations. The equations relate the ionic fluxes to diffusion and transport by the electrical field and the fluid velocity. In turn, the electrical field is determined nonlocally via a Poisson equation from the ionic concenrations. Real world applications motivate studies of electrodiffusion phenomena. One such model is addressed in
[
21
]
in the context of improving the performance and lifetime of batteries, which has direct applications to the development of electric vehicles, portable electronics, etc. Other applications of electrodiffusion systems arise in semiconductors
[
11
,
20
]
and ion selective membranes
[
9
]
.

The
Nernst-Planck equations
are given by

(1)

\partial_{t}c_{i}+u\cdot\nabla c_{i}-D_{i}\Delta c_{i}=D_{i}z_{i}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\quad i=1,...,n

describing the time evolution of the ionic concentrations
c_{i}
of
n
ionic species, with valences
z_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and diffusivities
D_{i}>0
in a two-dimensional incompressible fluid. Above, the electrical potential
\Phi
is determined by the Poisson equation

(2)

-\Delta\Phi=\rho=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}c_{i}.

In this paper, we consider two electrodiffusion models distinguished by the choice of model used to describe the fluid velocity: the velocity
u
and pressure
p
of the fluid obey either the
Euler equations

(3)

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=-\rho\nabla\Phi,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0

or
Darcy’s law

(4)

u+\nabla p=-\rho\nabla\Phi,\quad\nabla\cdot u=0.

The model described by (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) is called the
Nernst-Plank-Euler system
and will be referred to as NPE, whereas the model described by (
1
), (
2
) and (
4
) is called the
Nernst-Planck-Darcy system
and will be referred to as NPD. Physically, considering Euler equations amounts to considering a region of low viscosity fluid far from any solid boundaries. On the other hand, Darcy’s law corresponds to creeping flow in a porous medium, which is of physical interest, for example, when considering electrodiffusion in porous electrodes. In this paper, we study the global well-posedness and Gevrey regularity of the NPE and NPD systems in the periodic setting on the two-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}=[0,2\pi]^{2}
.

The global well-posedness of the periodic NPE and NPD systems were addressed respectively in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
for two ionic species having valences 1 and -1 and equal diffusivities. The special structure of the two-species NPE and NPD models yields energy estimates that facilitate the analysis. These same bounds also yield precise information on the long time behavior of solutions. In fact, it is shown in
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
that the the two ionic concentrations converge in
L^{2}
towards constant distributions (corresponding to their initial spatial averages) exponentially quickly in time. In this paper, we are interested in the more general models that model the time evolution of
n
ionic species with different valences and diffusivities. At this level of generality, the ideas of
[
14
]
and
[
13
]
no longer apply. Instead, in this paper we adapt ideas from
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
to obtain time dependent
L^{2}
bounds for the velocity and ionic concentrations, and we use them to bootstrap to obtain higher regularity bounds, assuming sufficiently regular initial data. We thus establish global well-posedness of solutions of NPE and NPD in all sufficiently regular Sobolev spaces.

We also consider the analyticity of solutions to the NPE and NPD models. Gevrey regularity techniques were initiated by Foias and Temam in
[
10
]
to study the analyticity of solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations. Their idea uses Fourier series expansions, is restricted to Hilbert spaces, and requires Sobolev regular initial regularity. In
[
12
]
, Grujic and Kukavica introduced a simpler method to obtain the analyticity of the Navier-Stokes system for
L^{p}
initial data based on a mild formulation of the complexified problem. Bardos and Benachour proved in
[
3
]
the persistence of analyticity for the Euler equations on three-dimensional bounded domains and obtained decaying rates for the radius of analyticity. Using the method of Gevrey regularity, Levermore and Oliver obtained the same rates in
[
18
]
for the three-dimensional periodic Euler equations. In
[
17
]
, Kukavica and Vicol improved the aforementioned results and derived lower bounds for the radius of analyticity for solutions of the three-dimensional Euler system that depend algebraically on
\exp\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,ds
.

The NPE and NPD models are closely related to the Nernst-Planck-Navier-Stokes (NPNS) system, where the velocity of the fluid evolves according to Navier-Stokes equations forced nonlinearly by the electrical force
-\rho\nabla\Phi
. The NPNS system was investigated on bounded domains in
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
, and on the 2D periodic torus in
[
1
]
. In
[
2
]
, the authors adapted the technique of
[
12
]
to study the analyticity of the periodic NPNS system, forced by body forces in the fluid and additional charge densities, for
L^{p}
initial data. However, the idea of the proof applies to semi-linear parabolic equations and breaks down in the absence of the fluid dissipation. In this paper, we use ideas from
[
10
]
and
[
17
]
and provide quantitative estimates for the growth in time of the Gevrey norms of the solution to both the NPE and NPD systems. These estimates remain finite on all finite intervals, and thus our results imply global in time spatial analyticity of solutions.

The Nernst-Planck equations have a dissipative structure, which is taken advantage of to estimate the nonlinearities involving ionic concentration terms. The Euler equations, on the other hand, are inviscid and thus nondissipative. Despite the inviscidity of the Euler system and the nonlinearties driving the time evolution of the concentrations and velocity, we successfully adapt the ideas of
[
17
]
and establish the analyticity of solutions of NPE via use of Fourier series techniques. The challenges arising from the analysis of the NPD system are different though. NPD is effectively a system of
n
dissipative partial differential equations with
n
unknowns, namely the ionic concentrations
c_{1},...,c_{n}
, and the fluid velocity is computed from the charge density
\rho
via the nonlinear relation

(5)

u=-\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\Phi)

where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray-Hodge projector onto the space of divergence-free vector fields. Hence, the nonlinear transport terms
u\cdot\nabla c_{i}
are effectively cubic. Controlling these terms is the main source of difficulty and is dealt with using Fourier series techniques, yielding bounds with cubic dependence on the desired Gevrey norm. This gives local analytic solutions, which we then extend globally by establishing uniform control of high regularity Sobolev norms of the solution.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the functional setting and notation conventions that will be frequently used throughout the paper. In Section
3
, we study the global well-posedness of the NPE system on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\times[0,T]
. Indeed, we first prove the existence of a unique local regular solution provided that the initial velocity and ionic concentrations are at least Sobolev
H^{3}
regular, and then we show that this local solution extends to a global regular solution on the time interval
[0,T]
. In Section
4
, we show that the solution to the NPE system is Gevrey regular with a radius of analyticity that depends on the Sobolev regularity of the solution on the whole time interval
[0,T]
under the assumption that the initial data is real analytic. In Section
5
, we show the global well-posedness of the NPD system on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\times[0,T]
and we prove the existence of a global unique regular solution for at least Sobolev
H^{\frac{3}{2}}
regular initial concentrations. Finally, we show in Section
6
that the solution to the NPD system is space analytic, for any initial data that is more regular that Sobolev
H^{2}
, with a radius of analyticity depending only on the initial regularity of the velocity and concentrations.

2.
Functional Settings and Notations

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we denote by
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
the Lebesgue spaces of measurable periodic functions
f
from
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}
(or
{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that

(6)

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}\|f\|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty

if
p\in[1,\infty)
and

(7)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}={\mathrm{esssup}}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}}|f|<\infty

if
p=\infty
. The
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
inner product is denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}}
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the fractional Laplacian
\Lambda^{s}
applied to a mean zero function
f
is defined as a Fourier multiplier with symbol
|k|^{s}
, that is, for
f
given by

(8)

f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}f_{k}e^{ik\cdot x},

and obeying

(9)

\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}|k|^{2s}|f_{k}|^{2}<\infty,

we define

(10)

\Lambda^{s}f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}|k|^{s}f_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}.

For
\tau>0
,
s>0
, and functions
f
obeying

(11)

\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}e^{2\tau|k|^{s}}|f_{k}|^{2}<\infty,

we define

(12)

e^{\tau\Lambda^{s}}f=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}e^{\tau|k|^{s}}f_{k}e^{ik\cdot x}.

We denote by
\mathbb{P}
the Leray-Hodge projection onto the space divergence free vector fields. For a mean-free periodic vector field
v=(v_{1},v_{2})
with Fourier series

(13)

v=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}v_{j}e^{ij\cdot x},

\mathbb{P}v
has the following Fourier representation

(14)

\mathbb{P}v=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\left[v_{j}-(v_{j}\cdot j)\frac{j}{|j|^{2}}\right]e^{ij\cdot x}.

The operator
\mathbb{P}
is bounded on
L^{p}
spaces for any
p\in(1,\infty)
.

For
s>0
, we denote by
H^{s}({\mathbb{T}}^{2})
the Sobolev spaces of measurable periodic functions
f
from
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}
(or
{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
obeying

(15)

\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}(1+|k|^{s})^{2}|f_{k}|^{2}<\infty.

For a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we consider the Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(0,T;X)
of functions
f
from
X
to
\mathbb{R}
(or
{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
satisfying

(16)

\int_{0}^{T}\|f\|_{X}^{p}dt<\infty

with the usual convention when
p=\infty
.

Throughout the paper,
C
denotes a positive constant depending only on the parameters of the problem (namely the diffusivities and valences of the ionic concentrations) and some universal constants, and it changes from line to line along the proofs. The dependency on any other variable will be stated explicitly. We also use the notation convention
A\lesssim B
when
A\leq CB
. Finally, we adopt the notation
[\Lambda^{m},v\cdot\nabla]w
to denote the commutator
\Lambda^{m}(v\cdot\nabla w)-v\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{m}w
.

3.
Global Well-posedness of the Nernst-Planck-Euler system

In this section, we address the global existence and uniqueness of solutions to the NPE system:

Theorem 1
.

(Global well-posedness and regularity of NPE) Let
T>0
be arbitrary and
m\geq 3
. Assume that the initial ionic concentrations
c_{i}(0)\in H^{m}
are nonnegative, and the initial velocity
u_{0}\in H^{m}
is mean-zero and divergence-free. Then the NPE system described by (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) has a unique solution
(u,c_{1},...,c_{n})
on the time interval
[0,T]
with the property that

(17)

(u,c_{1},...,c_{n})\in(L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}))^{n+1}.

The proof of Theorem
1
will be presented in this section. First, we note that the spatial averages of the ionic concentrations are constant in time, that is

(18)

\int c_{i}(t)dx=\int c_{i}(0)dx

for all
t\geq 0
and all
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
. Moreover, the spatial average of the velocity vanishes at any positive time
t
, a fact that follows from integrating the forced Euler equation (
3
) obeyed by
u
and noting that

(19)

\displaystyle\int\rho\nabla\Phi dx=\int\rho\nabla\Lambda^{-2}\rho dx=\int\Lambda^{-2}\rho\nabla\rho dx=-\int(\nabla\Lambda^{-2}\rho)\rho dx=-\int\rho\nabla\Phi dx

from which we conclude that the spatial integral of
\rho\nabla\Phi
vanishes at any time
t\geq 0
.

Now we proceed to prove Theorem
1
. The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1. Local well-posedness for
H^{m}
initial datum.
We sketch a proof of local existence, with a focus on obtaining a priori estimates. Rigorously, we may do the following computations for a mollified version of the NPE system
[
19
]
. We fix
m>2
, and consider initial conditions in
H^{m}
. Then, we apply
\Lambda^{m}
to (
1
), multiply the resulting equation by
\Lambda^{m}c_{i}
, and integrate by parts:

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\left(\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i}),\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\right)_{L^{2}}+D_{i}z_{i}\left(\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\right)_{L^{2}}.

Due to the fact that
\nabla\cdot u=0
, we have that
(\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i}),\Lambda^{m}c_{i})_{L^{2}}=([\Lambda^{m},u\cdot\nabla]c_{i},\Lambda^{m}c_{i})_{L^{2}}
. So it follows from commutator estimates
[
16
]
that

(21)

\displaystyle\left|\left([\Lambda^{m},u\cdot\nabla]c_{i},\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\right)_{L^{2}}\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}})\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1+\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{4},

where the second line follows from the embedding
H^{m}\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}
which holds for
m>2
. We estimate the second term on the right hand side of (
20
) as follows

(22)

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\right)_{L^{2}}\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\right)_{L^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{i}}{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}.

Above, we used the fact that for
m>1
,
H^{m}
is a Banach algebra so that
\|\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}
. Putting together (
20
)-(
22
), and summing in
i
, we have thus far

(23)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim 1+\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}.

Next, we apply
\Lambda^{m}
to (
3
), multiply by
\Lambda^{m}u
, and integrate by parts to obtain

(24)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\left(\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla u),\Lambda^{m}u\right)_{L^{2}}-\left(\Lambda^{m}(\rho\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}u\right)_{L^{2}}.

We estimate the terms on the right hand side of (
24
)

(25)

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla u),\Lambda^{m}u\right)_{L^{2}}\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\left([\Lambda^{m},u\cdot\nabla]u,\Lambda^{m}u\right)_{L^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1+\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}

and

(26)

\displaystyle\left|\left(\Lambda^{m}(\rho\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}u\right)_{L^{2}}\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 1+\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}.

Again, in obtaining (
25
), we used the embedding
H^{m}\hookrightarrow W^{1,\infty}
for
m>2
. Putting together (
24
)-(
26
) with (
23
), we finally have that

(27)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\lesssim 1+\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{2}.

Then from a Gronwall inequality, this last inequality gives us a time
T^{*}
, depending on the
H^{m}
norms of the initial data, and a local solution of NPE on
[0,T^{*})
such that

(28)

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq 2\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{m}u(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Uniqueness follows from similar energy estimates. Suppose
(u,c_{1},...,c_{n})
and
(u,\bar{c}_{1},...,\bar{c}_{n})
are two local
H^{m}
solutions to NPE on some common interval
[0,T^{*})
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
T^{*}
is taken small enough so that both solutions satisfy the local doubling inequality (
28
). Then, the differences
\tilde{c}_{i}=c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}
,
\tilde{\Phi}=\Phi-\bar{\Phi}
,
\tilde{u}=u-\bar{u}
satisfy

(29)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{c}_{i}+u\cdot\nabla\tilde{c}_{i}-D_{i}\Delta\tilde{c}_{i}=

\displaystyle D_{i}z_{i}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\tilde{\Phi}+\tilde{c}_{i}\nabla\bar{\Phi})-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\bar{c}_{i}

(30)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{u}+u\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}-\nabla\tilde{p}=

\displaystyle-\rho\nabla\tilde{\Phi}-\tilde{\rho}\nabla\bar{\Phi}-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\bar{u}.

We multiply the above equations by
\tilde{c}_{i}
and
\tilde{u}
respectively, and integrate by parts to obtain

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\nabla\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\bar{\Phi}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\bar{c}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{i}}{2}\|\nabla\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+G_{1}(t)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

and

(32)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\bar{\Phi}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle G_{2}(t)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

where

(33)

\displaystyle G_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\|c_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\bar{c}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})+\|\nabla\bar{\Phi}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle G_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\bar{\Phi}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Both
G_{1}(t)
and
G_{2}(t)
are controlled by the
H^{m}
norms of
c_{i},\bar{c}_{i},u,\bar{u}
and thus integrable on
[0,T^{*})
due to (
28
). Thus, adding (
31
) to (
32
) and integrating, we find

(35)

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\lesssim\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)e^{C\int_{0}^{T^{*}}G_{1}(s)+G_{2}(s)\,ds}.

The latter inequality proves uniqueness and continuous (in
L^{2}
) dependence on initial conditions.

Step 2. Positivity of the ionic concentrations.
Suppose
(u,c_{1},...,c_{n})
is a solution of the NPE problem with the property that each ionic concentration
c_{i}
belongs to the Lebesgue space
L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})
. Then the positivity of
c_{i}(0)
for
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
is preserved for all positive times, that is
c_{i}(x,t)\geq 0
for all
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
, for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, and for all
t\in[0,T]
. The proof can be found in
[
4
]
.

Step 3. A priori
L^{2}
uniform-in-time bounds.
Suppose
(u,c_{1},...,c_{n})
is a solution of the NPE problem on the time interval
[0,T]
such that
c_{i}(x,t)\geq 0
for all
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
, for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, and for all
t\in[0,T]
. Then there is a positive constant
\Gamma
depending on the initial data, the time
T
, the parameters of the problem, and some universal constants, such that the following bound

(36)

\sup\limits_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\|\nabla c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\Gamma

holds. This a priori bound is proved in Proposition 2 of
[
2
]
. We omit the details here.

Step 4. A priori
H^{1}
uniform-in-time bounds for the velocity
u
.

The evolution of the vorticity
\omega=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u
is described by the equation

(37)

\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=-\nabla^{\perp}\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi.

We take the
L^{2}
inner product of (
37
) with
\omega
and we estimate.
The nonlinear term in
\omega
vanishes due to the divergence-free condition obeyed by
u
.
Elliptic estimates applied to the mean-free function
\rho
yield

(38)

\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

This gives the differential inequality

(39)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int\nabla^{\perp}\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi\omega\,dx&\leq\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

from which we conclude that

(40)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

so that

(41)

\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau.

In view of the boundedness of
\rho
in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
obtained in Step 3, we conclude that
\omega
belongs to
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})
. Since
\|\nabla u||_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}
, Step 4 is complete.

Step 5. A priori
H^{2}
uniform-in-time bounds for the ionic concentrations.
Applying
\Delta
to (
1
), multiplying by
\Delta c_{i}
and integrating by parts, we find that the
L^{2}
norm of
\Delta c_{i}
evolves according to

(42)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int\Delta(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})\Delta c_{i}\,dx-D_{i}z_{i}\int\Delta\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\Delta c_{i}\,dx.

For the nonlinear term in
u
, we integrate by parts and estimate using Sobolev and interpolation estimates. We obtain

(43)

\displaystyle\left|\int\Delta(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})\Delta c_{i}\,dx\right|=\left|\int\nabla(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})\cdot\nabla\Delta c_{i}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{4}})\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}(\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}+\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2})\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

As for the second nonlinear term, we bound

(44)

\displaystyle\left|-D_{i}z_{i}\int\Delta\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\Delta c_{i}\,dx\right|=\left|D_{i}z_{i}\int\Delta(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\cdot\nabla\Delta c_{i}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left[\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

using fractional product estimates
[
15
]
. Putting (
42
)–(
44
) together, we obtain the differential inequality

(45)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left[\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Using the triangle inequality, we bound

(46)

\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\|\Delta c_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We sum over all indices
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
, and we obtain

(47)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\|\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\right]\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right],

and from Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

(48)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}D_{i}\|\nabla\Delta c_{i}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Delta c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{T}\|\omega(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|\nabla\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}(\tau)\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\,d\tau\right).

From the above bounds, (
36
) and (
41
), we conclude that

(49)

c_{i}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3})

for all
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}.

Step 6. A priori
L^{\infty}
uniform-in-time bounds for the vorticity
\omega
.
We multiply the vorticity equation (
37
) by
\omega|\omega|^{p-2}
, integrate in the space variable over
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, and estimate the forcing term in the resulting equation. We obtain

(50)

\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\|\omega\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq\left|\int(\nabla^{\perp}\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi)\omega|\omega|^{p-2}\,dx\right|\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{p}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}

by Hölder’s inequality. In view of Morrey’s inequality

(51)

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{p}}\leq(4\pi^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}(4\pi^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{4}}\leq C_{0}(4\pi^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}

where
C_{0}
is a constant independent of
p
. Therefore, we have

(52)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\omega\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{0}(4\pi^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

We integrate in time from
0
to
t
and let
p\rightarrow\infty
. We obtain

(53)

\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+C_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\Phi(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Delta\rho(s)\|_{L^{2}}ds

for all
t\geq 0
. In view of (
48
), we conclude that
\omega\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty})
.

Step 7. A priori
H^{3}
uniform-in-time bounds for the velocity
u
.

For this step we outline the argument provided in
[
13
]
. We have

(54)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(1+\log(1+\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}})\right)\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}},

from which we conclude that

(55)

\displaystyle\log\left(1+\log(1+\|\nabla\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}})\right)\leq\log\left(1+\log(1+\|\nabla\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})\right)

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\omega(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\Phi(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Delta\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\right)d\tau

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
This gives the double exponential bound

(56)

\|\nabla\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+\|\nabla\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})\exp\left(\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left[\|\omega(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\Delta\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]d\tau\right)\right)

after applications of the Poincaré inequality.
The regularity established in (
48
) allows us to conclude that
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{3})
. We refer the reader to
[
13
, (3.23)–(3.25)]
for the details involved in the preceding computations.

Step 8. A priori
H^{m}
uniform-in-time bounds for the ionic concentrations and velocity.
We take the scalar product in
L^{2}
of each ionic concentration equation (
1
) with
\Lambda^{2m}c_{i}
and the vorticity equation (
37
) with
\Lambda^{2m-2}\omega
. We add the resulting energy equations and obtain

(57)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left[\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int\Lambda^{m-1}(u\cdot\nabla\omega)\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\,dx-\int\Lambda^{m-1}(\nabla^{\perp}\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi)\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\,dx

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\,dx+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}z_{i}\int\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\,dx.

We estimate

(58)

\displaystyle\left|\int\Lambda^{m-1}(u\cdot\nabla\omega)\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\,dx\right|=\left|\int[\Lambda^{m-1},u\cdot\nabla]\omega\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|[\Lambda^{m-1},u\cdot\nabla]\omega\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m-1}u\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^{4}})\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

using standard commutator estimates (see
[
16
]
). We bound

(59)

\displaystyle\left|\int\Lambda^{m-1}(\nabla^{\perp}\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi)\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{a}(t)\left(\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

by applying fractional product estimates and continuous Sobolev embeddings. Above the coefficient
\tilde{a}(t)
is given by

(60)

\tilde{a}(t)=C(\|\nabla\Phi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{4}}).

Using the fact that the fluid velocity
u
is divergence-free, integrating by parts, and applying fractional product, interpolation, and Poincaré inequalities, we estimate

(61)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\,dx\right|=\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int\Lambda^{m}(uc_{i})\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\right)\left(\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Using elliptic regularity, we have

(62)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}z_{i}\int\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\,dx\right|=\left|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}z_{i}\int\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Putting (
57
)–(
62
) together, we obtain the differential inequality

(63)

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\lesssim a(t)\left[\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]

where

(64)

a(t)=\tilde{a}(t)+\|\nabla\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

is integrable over the time interval
[0,T]
due to (
48
) and (
56
). Thus, applying Gronwall’s inequality to (
63
), we obtain

(65)

\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left[\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}a(\tau)\,d\tau\right)

This finishes the proof of Step 8.

Step 9. Extension of the local solution.
The local solution can be extended to the time interval
[0,T]
, a fact that follows from the uniform-in-time boundedness in
H^{m}
obtained in Step 8. This ends the proof of Theorem
1
.

4.
Gevrey Regularity of the Nernst-Planck-Euler system

In this section, we address the propagation of the Gevrey regularity for the NPE system:

Theorem 2
.

(Global analyticity of NPE) Let
T>0
be arbitrary and
m>4
. Assume that the initial ionic concentrations
c_{i}(0)
are nonnegative and real-analytic, and the initial velocity
u_{0}
is mean-zero, divergence-free, and real-analytic. Then the NPE system described by (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) has a unique solution
(u,c_{1},...,c_{n})
on
[0,T]
such that for each time
t\in(0,T)
, the functions
u,c_{1},...,c_{n}
are real analytic in the spatial variable with uniform radius of analyticity
\tau(t)
, depending on the
H^{m}
norm of the solution
(u,c_{1},...,c_{n})
up to time
t
.

In order to prove Theorem
2
, we start by taking the scalar products in
\mathcal{D}(e^{\tau\Lambda})
of the equation obeyed by the ionic concentration
c_{i}
with
\Lambda^{2m}c_{i}
and of the equation obeyed by
\omega=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u
with
\Lambda^{2m-2}\omega
. We add the resulting energy equalities, and we obtain

(66)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tau^{\prime}(t)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{\prime}(t)\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c_{i}})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}z_{i}(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot[(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\nabla\Phi],e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}z_{i}\bar{c}_{i}(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}\Delta\Phi,e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle-(u\cdot\nabla\omega,\Lambda^{2m-2}e^{2\tau\Lambda}\omega)_{L^{2}}-(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot[\rho\nabla\Phi],\Lambda^{2m-2}e^{2\tau\Lambda}\omega)_{L^{2}}.

In order to control the nonlinear terms, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

[
17
]
The following estimate

(67)

\displaystyle|(u\cdot\nabla\omega,\Lambda^{2m-2}e^{2\tau\Lambda}\omega)_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\tau\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\tau^{2}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}+\tau^{2}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}\right)\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m-\frac{1}{2}}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}e^{\tau\Lambda}\omega\|_{L^{2}}+(1+\tau)\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\|\Lambda^{m-1}e^{\tau\Lambda}\omega\|_{L^{2}}

holds for any
m>4
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
. The following estimate

(68)

\displaystyle|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\Lambda^{m+1}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Lambda^{1+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Lambda^{m-1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{2+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\tau^{2}\|\Lambda^{2+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\tau^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{2+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

holds for any
m>0
and
\epsilon>0
.
Consequently, we have

(69)

\displaystyle|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\Lambda^{m+1}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Lambda^{m-1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\tau^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\tau\Lambda}\omega\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for any
m>2
.

Proof.
We set

(70)

u=\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}u_{j}e^{ij\cdot x},

(71)

u^{*}=\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}u_{j}^{*}e^{ij\cdot x},u_{j}^{*}=e^{\tau|j|}u_{j},

(72)

c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}=\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}(c_{i})_{j}e^{ij\cdot x},

and

(73)

(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})^{*}=\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}(c_{i})_{j}^{*}e^{ij\cdot x},(c_{i})_{j}^{*}=e^{\tau|j|}(c_{i})_{j}.

Then

(74)

\left|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}\right|=4\pi^{2}\left|\sum\limits_{j+k+l=0}(u_{j}\cdot k)(c_{i})_{k}(c_{i})_{l}e^{2\tau|l|}|l|^{2m}\right|.

Since
j+k+l=0
, we have
|l|\leq|k|+|j|
, which implies that
e^{\tau|l|}\leq e^{\tau|k|}e^{\tau|j|}
, and so

(75)

\left|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}\right|\leq 4\pi^{2}\sum\limits_{j+k+l=0}e^{\tau|j|}|u_{j}\cdot k||(c_{i})_{k}^{*}||(c_{i})_{l}^{*}||l|^{2m}.

Using the estimate
e^{x}\leq e+xe^{x}
, that holds for any
x\geq 0
, and the estimate
|l|^{m-1}\lesssim(|k|^{m-1}+|j|^{m-1})
, that holds for all
j,k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
j+k+l=0
, we bound

(76)

\displaystyle\left|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{j+k+l=0}(e+\tau|j|e^{\tau|j|})|u_{j}||(c_{i})_{k}^{*}||(c_{i})_{l}^{*}||k||l|^{m+1}(|k|^{m-1}+|j|^{m-1}).

Using Young’s convolution inequality and Plancherel identity, we obtain

(77)

\displaystyle\left|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{j}\|_{\ell^{1}}\||k|^{m}(c_{i})_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\||j|^{m-1}u_{j}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||k|(c_{i})_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle+\tau\||j|u_{j}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}\||k|^{m}(c_{i})_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}+\tau\||j|^{m}u_{j}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||k|(c_{i})_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{1+\epsilon}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{m-1}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{2+\epsilon}c_{i}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\tau\|\Lambda^{2+\epsilon}u^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}+\tau\|\Lambda^{m}u^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{2+\epsilon}c_{i}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}

which gives the desired estimate (
68
). The bound (
69
) is a consequence of Young’s inequality and the bound

(78)

\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}=\|\Lambda\Lambda^{m-1}e^{\tau\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla\Lambda^{m-1}e^{\tau\Lambda}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m-1}e^{\tau\Lambda}\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m-\frac{1}{2}}e^{\tau\Lambda}\omega\|_{L^{2}}

that holds in view of the divergence-free condition obeyed by the velocity
u
. This ends the proof of Lemma
2
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
. The following estimate

(79)

\displaystyle D_{i}|z_{i}||(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot[(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\nabla\Phi],e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\Lambda^{m+1}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Lambda^{m-1}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{1+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Lambda^{\epsilon}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\tau^{2}\|\Lambda^{1+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\tau^{2}\|\Lambda^{1+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

holds for any
m>0
and
\epsilon>0
. Consequently, we have

(80)

(89)

\tilde{B}(t)=\tau^{\prime}(t)+C\tau\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\tau^{2}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}+C\tau^{2}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m-1}\omega\|_{L^{2}}+C\tau^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

Remark 1
.

If the initial ionic concentrations are taken to be zero, then their spatial averages vanish at all positive times, and so they are identically zero on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\times[0,\infty)

2
generalizes the result obtained in
[

]
in the 2D situation. In particular, the Gevrey bound (

) and the radius of analyticity (
107
) coincides with the ones derived in
[
17
]
.

5.
Global Well-posedness of the Nernst-Planck-Darcy system

In this section, we prove Theorem
3
, concerning the global existence and uniqueness of solutions to the NPD system:

Theorem 3
.

(Global well-posedness and regularity of NPD) Let
T>0
be arbitrary and
m\geq\frac{3}{2}
. Assume that the initial ionic concentrations
c_{i}(0)\in H^{m}
are nonnegative. Then the NPD system described by (
1
), (
2
) and (
4
) has a unique solution
(c_{1},...,c_{n})
on the time interval
[0,T]
with the property that

(108)

(c_{1},...,c_{n})\in(L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m}))^{n}.

The proof of Theorem
3
is divided into several steps.

Step 1. Existence of a unique local-in-time solution for
H^{m}
initial datum.

As in Section
3
, we focus on obtaining a priori estimates. We fix
m\geq\frac{3}{2}
. We apply
\Lambda^{m}
to (
1
), multiply the resulting equation by
\Lambda^{m}c_{i}
, and integrate by parts to obtain

(109)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}+D_{i}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle-(\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i}),\Lambda^{m}c_{i})_{L^{2}}+D_{i}z_{i}(\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}c_{i})_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-([\Lambda^{m},u\cdot\nabla]c_{i},\Lambda^{m}c_{i})_{L^{2}}-D_{i}z_{i}(\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i})_{L^{2}}.

We estimate the first integral on the right hand side of (
109
) as follows

(110)

\displaystyle\left|([\Lambda^{m},u\cdot\nabla]c_{i},\Lambda^{m}c_{i})_{L^{2}}\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{4}})\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}.

Then using
\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}
for
m>1
and
\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}
, we obtain

(111)

\displaystyle\left|([\Lambda^{m},u\cdot\nabla]c_{i},\Lambda^{m}c_{i})_{L^{2}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{i}}{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}}^{2})\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now, recalling from (
4
) that
u=-\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\Phi)
where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray projection onto the space of divergence-free vectors, we have
\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\Lambda^{m}(\rho\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}
and thus

(112)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

which holds due to the Banach algebra property of
H^{m}
for
m>1
. Similarly, we have

(113)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

where the first inequality holds for
m\geq\frac{3}{2}
. Then, taking (
112
),(
113
) and returning to (
111
), we have

(114)

\displaystyle\left|([\Lambda^{m},u\cdot\nabla]c_{i},\Lambda^{m}c_{i})_{L^{2}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{i}}{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{3}.

Now we estimate the second integral on the right hand side of (
109
):

(115)

\displaystyle\left|D_{i}z_{i}(\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i})_{L^{2}}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{i}}{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{i}}{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{i}}{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{2}.

Thus, summing (
109
) in
i
and using (
114
) and (
115
), we obtain

(116)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\lesssim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{3}

from which it follows that for a short time
T^{*}
depending on the
H^{m}
norms of the initial data of
c_{i}
, there is a local solution of NPD on
[0,T^{*})
satisfying

(117)

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The uniqueness of local solutions follows from similar energy estimates. Suppose
(c_{1},...,c_{n})
and
(\bar{c}_{1},...,\bar{c}_{n})
are two local
H^{m}
solutions to NPD on some common interval
[0,T*)

T^{*}
is taken small enough so that both solutions satisfy (
117

satisfy

(118)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{c}_{i}+u\cdot\nabla\tilde{c}_{i}-D_{i}\Delta\tilde{c}_{i}=

\displaystyle D_{i}z_{i}\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\tilde{\Phi}+\tilde{c}_{i}\nabla\bar{\Phi})-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\bar{c}_{i}.

Multiplying the above by
\tilde{c}_{i}
and integrating by parts, we obtain exactly as in (
31
)

(119)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\nabla\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D_{i}}{2}\|\nabla\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+G_{1}(t)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

with
G_{1}(t)
uniformly bounded on
[0,T^{*})
(c.f. (
33
)). Next, we estimate, using
\tilde{u}=-\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\Phi-\bar{\rho}\nabla\bar{\Phi})

(120)

\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\tilde{\Phi}\|_{L^{2}}\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim G_{3}(t)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{j}\|_{L^{2}}

where

(121)

G_{3}(t)=\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}}

is uniformly bounded on
[0,T^{*})
. Therefore, from (
119
), we obtain after summing in
i

(122)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim G_{1}(t)\left(1+(G_{3}(t))^{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then, applying Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

(123)

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\tilde{c}_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}e^{C\int_{0}^{T^{*}}G_{1}(s)(1+G_{3}(s))^{2}\,ds}.

Uniqueness follows from this last inequality.

[
4
]
.

L^{2}

such that

for all

Step 4. A priori
L^{4}
uniform-in-time bounds.
We multiply the

1
) by
c_{i}^{3}
and integrate the resulting equation over
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. We obtain

(125)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{4}\frac{d}{dt}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{3D_{i}}{4}\|\nabla c_{i}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=D_{i}z_{i}\int\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi)c_{i}^{3}\,dx=-3D_{i}z_{i}\int c_{i}\nabla\Phi c_{i}^{2}\cdot\nabla c_{i}\,dx\\
&\lesssim\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla c_{i}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\frac{3D_{i}}{4}\|\nabla c_{i}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

Elliptic regularity yields the bound

(126)

\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, the
L^{4}
norm of
c_{i}
obeys

(127)

\frac{d}{dt}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\lesssim\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating in time from
0
to
t
and using the boundedness of
\rho
in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
given by (
124
), we conclude that the ionic concentrations are uniformly bounded on the space
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{4})
:

(128)

\|c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{4}}\leq\|c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{4}}\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau\right).

This finishes the proof of Step 4.

Step 5. A priori
H^{1}
uniform-in-time bounds.
The gradient of the
i
-th ionic concentration evolves according to the energy equality

(129)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=(u\cdot\nabla c_{i},\Delta c_{i})_{L^{2}}-D_{i}z_{i}(\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Delta c_{i})_{L^{2}}.

Using the divergence-free condition obeyed by the fluid velocity
u
and elliptic regularity, we have

(130)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla^{\perp}\rho\cdot\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{4}},

hence

(131)

\displaystyle|(u\cdot\nabla c_{i},\Delta c_{i})_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{3/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now we estimate

(132)

\displaystyle|D_{i}z_{i}(\nabla\cdot(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Delta c_{i})_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\right)\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\right)\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2}

Putting (
129
)–(
132
) together, we obtain the differential inequality

(133)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim(\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}+\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2})\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2}.

Summing over
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
, and bounding the
L^{4}
norm of the density
\rho
by the sum of the
L^{2}
norms of gradients of the ionic concentrations
c_{i}
, the latter energy inequality reduces to

(134)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left(\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}+\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

(135)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\|\nabla\Phi(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{4}+\|\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|c_{i}(\tau)\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\right)d\tau\right)

for all
t\geq 0
,
and so due to (
128
) and (
134
), the ionic concentrations belong to the space

(136)

L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}).

This completes the proof of Step 5.

Step 6. A priori
H^{m}
uniform-in-time bounds.
We multiply the equation obeyed by the
i
-th ionic concentration (
1
) by
\Lambda^{2m}c_{i}
, and we integrate over the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. Integrating by parts, we obtain

(137)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-(\Lambda^{m-1}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i}),\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i})_{L^{2}}-D_{i}z_{i}(\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i})_{L^{2}}.

Using fractional product estimates, the boundedness of the Leray projector on
L^{4}
, the Ladyzhenskaya’s interpolation inequality, the Poincaré inequality, and the continuous embedding of
H^{1}
in
L^{8}
, we estimate

(138)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{m-1}u\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle=\|\Lambda^{m-1}\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{4}}\leq\|\Lambda^{m-1}(\rho\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m-1}\rho\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{8}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{8}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho\|_{L^{4}}\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

In view of the continuous embedding of
H^{2}
in
L^{\infty}
and the algebra property of
H^{2}
, we have

(139)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\Delta\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}.

Consequently, we bound the nonlinear term in
u
as follows

(140)

\displaystyle|(\Lambda^{m-1}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i}),\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i})_{L^{2}}|\leq\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}(u\cdot\nabla c_{i})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Lambda^{m-1}u\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\right).

Now we estimate

(141)

\displaystyle|(\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}\nabla\Phi),\Lambda^{m}\nabla c_{i})_{L^{2}}|\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Lambda^{m}\nabla\Phi\|_{L^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\right).

This gives the energy inequality

(142)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+D_{i}\|\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Delta c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|c_{i}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}.}^{2}

Finally, we use the triangle inequality to bound

(143)

\|\Lambda^{m}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

sum over all indices
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
, integrate in time from
0
to
t
, exploit the regularity obtained in (
136
), and conclude that the ionic concentrations satisfy

(144)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq e^{L(t)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

where

(145)

L(t)=C\left(\sup_{\tau\in[0,t)}\|\nabla\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta c_{i}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\rho(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\|c_{i}(\tau)\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\,d\tau.

Thus for all
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
,
c_{i}
belongs to the space
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{m})
.

Step 7. Extension of the local solution.
The local solution can be extended to the time interval
[0,T]
, a fact that follows from the uniform-in-time boundedness in
H^{m}
obtained in Step 6. This ends the proof of Theorem
3
.

6.
Gevrey Regularity of the Nernst-Planck-Darcy system

In this section, we address the propagation of the Gevrey regularity for the NPD system:

Theorem 4
.

(Global analyticity of NPD) Let
T>0
be arbitrary and
m>2
. Assume that the initial ionic concentrations
c_{i}(0)\in H^{m}
are nonnegative. Then the NPD system described by (
1
), (
2
) and (
4
) has a unique solution
(c_{1},...,c_{n})
on [0,T] such that for each time
t\in(0,T)
, the functions
c_{1},...,c_{n}
are real analytic in the spatial variable with uniform radius of analyticity

(146)

\tau(t)=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{D_{1},...,D_{n}\right\}\min\{t,T_{0}/2\}

where
T_{0}>0
depends only on parameters, the
H^{m}
norms of
c_{i}(0)
, and lower regularity Sobolev norms of
c_{i}
up to time
T
.

In order to prove Theorem
4
, we take the scalar products in
\mathcal{D}(e^{\tau\Lambda})
of the equation obeyed by the ionic concentration
c_{i}
with
\Lambda^{2m}c_{i}
. We obtain

(147)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\tau^{\prime}(t)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m+\frac{1}{2}}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c_{i}})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}z_{i}(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot[(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\nabla\Phi],e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}z_{i}\bar{c}_{i}(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}\Delta\Phi,e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}.

In order to control the nonlinear terms, we need the following lemmas.

Lemma 6
.

Let
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
. The following estimate

(148)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{3}

holds for any
m>2
.

Proof.
We set
c_{i}
and
c_{i}^{*}
as in (
72
) and (
73
) respectively. We write the Fourier expansion of
\rho

(149)

\rho=\sum\limits_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\rho_{v}e^{iv\cdot x},

from which we obtain the Fourier expansion of
\rho\nabla\Phi

(150)

\rho\nabla\Phi=i\sum\limits_{v,z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}\rho_{z}\rho_{v}e^{i(v+z)\cdot x}=i\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\left[\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0,j\right\}}\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}\rho_{z}\rho_{j-z}\right]e^{ij\cdot x}.

In view of the divergence free-condition obeyed by
u
, we have

(151)

u=-\mathbb{P}(\rho\nabla\Phi)

where
\mathbb{P}
is the Leray projection onto the space of divergence-free vectors. Consequently, the Fourier series of
u
is given by

(152)

u=-\sum\limits_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\left[v_{j}-(v_{j}\cdot j)\frac{j}{|j|^{2}}\right]e^{ij\cdot x}

where

(153)

v_{j}=\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0,j\right\}}i\frac{z}{|z|^{2}}\rho_{z}\rho_{j-z}.

For
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, we denote by
u_{j}
the Fourier coefficients of
u
, that is

(154)

u_{j}=-\left[v_{j}-(v_{j}\cdot j)\frac{j}{|j|^{2}}\right],

and we obtain

(155)

\displaystyle(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}=4\pi^{2}i\sum\limits_{j+k+l=0}(u_{j}\cdot k)(c_{i})_{k}(c_{i})_{l}|l|^{2m}e^{2\tau l}

\displaystyle=4\pi^{2}\sum\limits_{j+k+l=0}\sum\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\left\{0,j\right\}}\left[\frac{z\cdot k}{|z|^{2}}-\frac{z\cdot j}{|z|^{2}}\frac{j\cdot k}{|j|^{2}}\right]\rho_{z}\rho_{j-z}(c_{i})_{k}(c_{i})_{l}|l|^{2m}e^{2\tau l}

\displaystyle=4\pi^{2}\sum\limits_{z+\tilde{z}+k+l=0}\left[\frac{z\cdot k}{|z|^{2}}-\frac{z\cdot(z+\tilde{z})}{|z|^{2}}\frac{(z+\tilde{z})\cdot k}{|z+\tilde{z}|^{2}}\right]\rho_{z}\rho_{\tilde{z}}(c_{i})_{k}(c_{i})_{l}|l|^{2m}e^{2\tau l}.

We point out that the last sum is over all indices
z,\tilde{z},k,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
such that
z+\tilde{z}+k+l=0,\tilde{z}+z\neq 0,z\neq 0,k\neq 0
,
l\neq 0
and
\tilde{z}\neq 0
. Using the fact that
z+\tilde{z}+k+l=0
, we have
|l|\leq|k|+|z|+|\tilde{z}|
, hence

(156)

\displaystyle|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(u\cdot\nabla(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})),e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{z+\tilde{z}+k+l=0}|\rho_{z}^{*}||\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}||(c_{i})_{k}^{*}||(c_{i})_{l}^{*}||k||z|^{-1}|l|^{m+1}(|k|^{m-1}+|z|^{m-1}+|\tilde{z}|^{m-1})

where
\rho_{z}^{*}=e^{\tau|z|}\rho_{z}
and
\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}=e^{\tau|\tilde{z}|}\rho_{\tilde{z}}
. We estimate the following three terms

(157)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{z+\tilde{z}+k+l=0}|\rho_{z}^{*}||\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}||(c_{i})_{k}^{*}||(c_{i})_{l}^{*}||k|^{m}|z|^{-1}|l|^{m+1}\lesssim\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||k|^{m}(c_{i})_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||z|^{-1}\rho_{z}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}\|\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||k|^{m}(c_{i})_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||z|^{-1}\rho_{z}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}\|\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m+1}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}\rho\|_{L^{2}},

(158)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{z+\tilde{z}+k+l=0}|\rho_{z}^{*}||\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}||(c_{i})_{k}^{*}||(c_{i})_{l}^{*}||k||z|^{m-2}|l|^{m+1}\lesssim\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||z|^{m-2}\rho_{z}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||k|(c_{i})_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}\|\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m+1}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-2}e^{\tau\Lambda}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{2+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}\rho\|_{L^{2}},

(159)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{z+\tilde{z}+k+l=0}|\rho_{z}^{*}||\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}||(c_{i})_{k}^{*}||(c_{i})_{l}^{*}||k||z|^{-1}|l|^{m+1}|\tilde{z}|^{m-1}\lesssim\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||\tilde{z}|^{m-1}\rho_{\tilde{z}}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||z|^{-1}\rho_{z}^{*}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\||k|(c_{i})_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{m+1}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{m-1}e^{\tau\Lambda}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{2+\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{\epsilon}e^{\tau\Lambda}\rho\|_{L^{2}}

for any
m>0
and any
\epsilon>0
. Putting (
156
)–(
159
) together and summing over all
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
, we obtain (
148
) for any
m>2
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
. The following estimate

(160)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}|z_{i}||(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot[(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\nabla\Phi],e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{D_{i}}{8}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m+1}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{2}

holds for any
m>1.

Proof.
Under the same setting introduced in the proof of Lemma
3
, we have

(161)

\displaystyle\left|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}\nabla\cdot[(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i})\nabla\Phi],e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}\right|=4\pi^{2}\left|\sum\limits_{j+k+l=0}(c_{i})_{j}\rho_{k}(c_{i})_{l}\frac{l\cdot k}{|k|^{2}}|l|^{2m}e^{2\tau|l|}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{j+k+l=0}|(c_{i})_{j}^{*}||\rho_{k}^{*}||(c_{i})_{l}^{*}||l|^{m+1}|k|^{-1}(|k|^{m}+|j|^{m})

\displaystyle\lesssim\||l|^{m+1}(c_{i})_{l}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\left[\||k|^{m-1}\rho_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\|(c_{i})_{j}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}+\||j|^{m}(c_{i})_{j}^{*}\|_{\ell^{2}}\||k|^{-1}\rho_{k}^{*}\|_{\ell^{1}}\right],

which gives the desired estimate (
160
) after applications of Plancherel’s identity and Young’s inequality.

Lemma 8
.

Let
i\in\left\{1,...,n\right\}
. The following estimate

(162)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}D_{i}z_{i}\bar{c}_{i}|(e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}\Delta\Phi,e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}(c_{i}-\bar{c}_{i}))_{L^{2}}|\leq C\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

holds for any
m>0
. Here
C
is a positive constant depending on the parameters of the problem and the initial spatial average of the ionic concentrations.

Proof.
The proof follows from a direct application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
4
. For fixed
T>0
, it follows from (
144
) that

(163)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,d\tau\leq e^{L(T)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=:L_{T}.

Now, choosing
\tau(t)=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{D_{1},...,D_{n}\right\}t
, and putting (
147
), (
148
), (
160
) and (
162
) together, we obtain the energy inequality

(164)

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\lesssim\left[1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]^{3}

from which we deduce the boundedness of the Gevrey norm

(165)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau(0)\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=2\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(0)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

on a short time interval
[0,T_{0}]
, where
T_{0}
depends on the initial magnitude in
L^{2}
of
e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}
, which is simply the
H^{m}
norm of
c_{i}
. In particular, we may choose
T_{0}
to just be a function of parameters and
L_{T}
from (
163
). This yields local Gevrey regularity.
To extend Gevrey regularity to a longer interval, we take this time
\tau(t)=\frac{1}{2}\min\{D_{1},...,D_{n}\}(t-\frac{T_{0}}{2})
and obtain from (
164
) the following bound

(166)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|e^{\tau(T_{0}/2)\Lambda}\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(T_{0}/2)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=2\left(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda^{m}c_{i}(T_{0}/2)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

on the interval
[T_{0}/2,T_{0}/2+T_{1}]
, where
T_{1}
depends on the
H^{m}
norms of
c_{i}
at time
T_{0}/2
. However, due to the uniform bound (
163
), we may choose
T_{1}=T_{0}
. Thus, we have Gevrey regularity on the interval
[0,3T_{0}/2]
, and in the same manner we extend to the whole interval
[0,T]
. Based on this construction, we see that the radius of analyticity

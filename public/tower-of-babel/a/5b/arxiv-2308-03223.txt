Discrete weak duality of hybrid high-order methods for convex minimization problems Thanks: This work has been supported by the European Union’s Horizon 2020 research and innovation programme (project DAFNE, grant agreement No. 891734, and project RandomMultiScales, grant agreement No. 865751
Ngoc Tien Tran

Abstract
. This paper derives a discrete dual problem for a prototypical hybrid high-order method for convex minimization problems. The discrete primal and dual problem satisfy a weak convex duality that leads to a priori error estimates with convergence rates under additional smoothness assumptions. This duality holds for general polyhedral meshes and arbitrary polynomial degrees of the discretization. A novel postprocessing is proposed and allows for a posteriori error estimates on regular triangulations into simplices using primal-dual techniques. This motivates an adaptive mesh-refining algorithm, which performs superiorly compared to uniform mesh refinements.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Given an open bounded polyhedral Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the boundary
\partial\Omega
and unit normal vector
\nu
, we consider the convex minimization problem of the energy

(1.1)

\displaystyle E(v)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}(\Psi(\mathrm{D}v)+\psi(x,v))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{among }v\in V_{\mathrm{D}}\coloneqq W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{D}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),

p\in(1,\infty)
.
Here,
\Psi:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
with
\mathbb{M}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
denotes a proper lower semicontinuous convex energy density,
\psi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is measurable such that
\psi(x,\bullet)
is a proper lower semicontinuous convex function for a. e.
x\in\Omega
, and
V_{\mathrm{D}}=W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{D}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the space of functions in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
with homogenous boundary data on a non-empty closed part
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\subset\partial\Omega
of
\partial\Omega
.
Let
\Psi^{*}
(resp.
\psi^{*}
) denote the convex conjugate of
\Psi
(resp.
\psi
a.e. in
\Omega
) and
p^{\prime}\coloneqq p/(p-1)\in(1,\infty)
with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
.
The dual problem of (
1.1
) maximizes the dual energy

(1.2)

\displaystyle E^{*}(\tau)\coloneqq-\int_{\Omega}(\Psi^{*}(\tau)+\psi^{*}(x,\mathrm{div}\tau))\,\mathrm{d}x

among
\tau\in W_{\mathrm{N}}\coloneqq W^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega;\mathbb{M})
, the Sobolev space of functions
\tau\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{M})
with a distributional divergence
\mathrm{div}\tau
belonging to
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and the normal trace
\tau\nu
vanishes along the Neumann boundary
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\coloneqq\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
in a weak sense.
The weak duality
E^{*}(\tau)\leq E(v)
holds for any
v\in V_{\mathrm{D}}
and
\tau\in W_{\mathrm{N}}
.

It is well-established that duality relations on the discrete level can be utilized in the error analysis of nonconforming methods
[
14
,
5
,
6
,
18
]
.
Only a few methods in the literature are known to preserve the weak duality on the discrete level,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\max E_{h}^{*}(W_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathcal{M}))\leq\min E_{h}(V_{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{M}))

for an appropriate discretization
V_{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{M})
of
V_{\mathrm{D}}
(resp.
W_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathcal{M})
of
W_{\mathrm{N}}
) and discrete energy
E_{h}
(resp. discrete dual energy
E_{h}^{*}
).
Historically, (
1.3
) was first known for a Crouzeix-Raviart finite element method (FEM) for the primal problem and a discrete Raviart-Thomas FEM for the dual problem
[
41
,
14
,
6
]
.
The generalized Marini identity even leads to the strong duality
\max E_{h}^{*}(W_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathcal{M}))=\min E_{h}(V_{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{M}))
under additional assumptions on
\Psi
and
\psi
.
For higher-order approximations, the unstabilized hybrid high-order (HHO) method from
[
1
]
and the classical Raviart-Thomas FEM satisfy (
1.3
) without a discrete strong duality in general
[
18
]
.
Finally, (
1.3
) holds for
the class of lowest-order discontinuous Galerkin (dG) and Raviart-Thomas FEM from
[
5
]
.
The analysis of all three aforementioned methods requires a regular triangulation of
\Omega
into simplices (i.e., no hanging nodes are allowed) because they heavily utilize the structure of Crouzeix-Raviart or Raviart-Thomas functions.

1.2.
Motivation and description of main results

The method from
[
5
]
stands out as the only one with a stabilization.
The later is required for the design of hybridizable methods, e.g., HHO or hybrid discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) methods, on general polyhedral meshes.
The foundation for establishing (
1.3
) is an integration by parts on the discrete level and the resulting integrals on the skeleton of the mesh determine the stabilizations. In
[
5
]
, the dG methodology leads to stabilizations that penalize the average of the trace on the primal and the normal trace on the dual level.
These terms do not possess any convergence rates for smooth functions – an undesired effect that requires overpenalization and projections onto the Crouzeix-Raviart and the Raviart-Thomas finite element space in the error analysis.
Therefore, it only applies to the lowest-order case on regular triangulations into simplices.
Hybridizable methods, on the other hand, provide further flexibility due to additional degrees of freedom on the skeleton, but can they overcome these restrictions?

This paper provides a clear affirmative answer.
In fact, we consider a prototypical HHO method
[
28
,
27
,
24
]
, which is also related to the HDG methodology
[
22
]
.
The ansatz space
V(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})\times P_{k}(\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{m})
features a split of degrees of freedom into piecewise polynomials of degree at most
k+1
on the (polyhedral) mesh
\mathcal{M}
and piecewise polynomials of degree at most
k
on the skeleton
\Sigma
, the set of faces of
\mathcal{M}
.
The discrete scheme replaces the gradient in (
1.1
) by a reconstruction operator
\mathrm{D}_{h}:V(\mathcal{M})\to P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})
. In contrast to
[
18
]
, this operator has a non-trivial kernel and so, a stabilization
\mathrm{s}_{h}:V(\mathcal{M})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is required.
This paper utilizes the nonlinear version of the Lehrenfeld-Schöberl stabilization, but the analysis can be extended to the classical HHO stabilization as well.
Given
r\in(1,\infty)
with Hölder conjugate
r^{\prime}=r/(r-1)
, the discrete primal problem minimizes

(1.4)

\displaystyle E_{h}(v_{h})\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}(\Psi(\mathrm{D}_{h}v_{h})+\psi_{h}(x,\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}v_{\mathcal{M}}))\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathrm{s}_{h}(v_{h})/r

among
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V_{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{M})
,
where
V_{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{M})\subset V(\mathcal{M})
is a discrete analogue to
V_{\mathrm{D}}
and
\psi_{h}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is an approximation of
\psi
.
Our analysis departs from a discrete integration by parts involving three different operators: a gradient reconstruction
\mathrm{D}_{h}:V(\mathcal{M})\to P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})
, a divergence reconstruction
\mathrm{div}_{h}:W(\mathcal{M})\to P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, which maps from the ansatz space
W(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})\times P_{k}(\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{m})
of
W^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega;\mathbb{M})
onto the space of piecewise polynomials of degree
k
, and a potential reconstruction
\mathcal{R}_{h}^{*}:W(\mathcal{M})\to P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})
in the spirit of
[
30
,
29
]
on the dual level.
This leads to a discrete dual problem that maximizes

(1.5)

\displaystyle E_{h}^{*}(\tau_{h})\coloneqq-\int_{\Omega}(\Psi^{*}(\mathcal{R}^{*}_{h}\tau_{h})+\psi_{h}^{*}(x,\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau_{h}))\,\mathrm{d}x-\gamma_{h}(\tau_{h})/r^{\prime}

among
\tau_{h}=(\tau_{\mathcal{M}},\tau_{\Sigma})\in W_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathcal{M})

with a suitable stabilization
\gamma_{h}:W(\mathcal{M})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and a discrete analogue
W_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathcal{M})\subset W(\mathcal{M})
of
W_{\mathrm{N}}
.
We establish that
E_{h}
from (
1.4
) and
E_{h}^{*}
from (
1.5
) satisfy (
1.3
).
This duality leads to a priori error estimates with convergence rates under suitable smoothness assumptions.
Apart from a priori results, this paper proposes a novel
W^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega,\mathbb{M})
conforming postprocessing
\sigma_{0}
for the approximation of the dual variable on regular triangulations into simplices.
Its construction is based on the discrete Euler-Lagrange equations, which allow for the concept of equilibrated tractions in the HHO methodology
[
23
,
1
]
.
This is a cheap alternative to equilibrium techniques
[
11
,
35
,
36
]
in the linear case and
can be used to obtain a posteriori error estimates via primal-dual gap similar to
[
42
,
4
,
14
,
18
,
8
]
.

2.1.
Polytopal Mesh

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite collection of closed polytopes of positive volume with overlap of measure zero that covers
\overline{\Omega}=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}K
.
A face
S
of the mesh
\mathcal{M}
is a closed connected subset of a hyperplane
H_{S}
with positive
(n-1)
-dimensional surface measure such that either (a) there exist
K_{+},K_{-}\in\mathcal{M}
with
S\subset H_{S}\cap K_{+}\cap K_{-}
(interior face) or (b) there exists
K_{+}\in\mathcal{M}
with
S\subset H_{S}\cap K_{+}\cap\partial\Omega
(boundary face).
Let
\Sigma
be a finite collection of faces with overlap of
(n-1)
-dimensional surface measure zero that covers the skeleton
\partial\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\cup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\partial K=\cup_{S\in\Sigma}S
with the split
\Sigma=\Sigma(\Omega)\cup\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
into the set of interior faces
\Sigma(\Omega)
and the set of boundary faces
\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
.
Let
\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}}\coloneqq\{S\in\Sigma:S\subset\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\}
(resp.
\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}}\coloneqq\Sigma(\partial\Omega)\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}}
) denote the set of Dirichlet (resp. Neumann) faces. For
K\in\mathcal{M}
,
\Sigma(K)
is the set of all faces of
K
.
The normal vector
\nu_{S}
of an interior face
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
is fixed beforehand and set
\nu_{S}\coloneqq\nu|_{S}
for boundary faces
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
.
For
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
,
K_{+}(S)\in\mathcal{M}
(resp.
K_{-}(S)\in\mathcal{M}
) denotes the unique cell with
S\subset\partial K_{+}(S)
(resp.
S\subset\partial K_{-}(S)
) and
\nu_{K_{+}(S)}|_{S}=\nu_{S}
(resp.
\nu_{K_{-}(S)}|_{S}=-\nu_{S}
).
For
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
,
K_{+}(S)\in\mathcal{M}
is the unique cell with
S\subset\partial K_{+}(S)
.
The explicit reference to
S
is omitted if there is no likelihood of confusion.
The jump
[v]_{S}
and the average

of any function
v\in W^{1,1}(\mathrm{int}(T_{+}\cup T_{-});\mathbb{R}^{m})
along
S\in\Sigma(\Omega)
are defined by
[v]_{S}\coloneqq v|_{K_{+}}-v|_{K_{-}}\in L^{1}(S;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\{v\}_{S}\coloneqq(v|_{K_{+}}+v|_{K_{-}})/2\in L^{1}(S;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
If
S\in\Sigma(\partial\Omega)
, then
[v]_{S}\coloneqq v|_{S}=v_{K_{+}}|_{S}\eqqcolon\{v\}_{S}
.

For theoretical purposes, let
\vartheta
denote the mesh regularity parameter of
\mathcal{M}
associated with a matching simplicial submesh, cf.
[
26
, Definition 1.38]
for a precise definition. The constants in discrete inequalities such as the trace or inverse inequality depend on this parameter.
The notation
c_{\mathrm{P}}\coloneqq\max_{K\in\mathcal{M}}c_{\mathrm{P}}(K)
denotes the best possible local Poincaré constant on
\mathcal{M}
. For general polyhedral meshes,
c_{\mathrm{P}}
may depend on
\vartheta
.
The differential operators
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}
and
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{pw}}
denote the piecewise version of
\mathrm{D}
and
\mathrm{div}
without explicit reference to the underlying mesh.

2.2.
Finite element spaces

Given a subset
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of diameter
h_{M}
, let
P_{k}(M)
denote the space of polynomials of degree at most
k
.
The vector- and matrix-valued version of
P_{k}(M)
read
P_{k}(M;\mathbb{R}^{m})\coloneqq P_{k}(M)^{m}
and
P_{k}(M;\mathbb{M})\coloneqq P_{k}(M;\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
.
This notation applies to other spaces as well.
For any
v\in L^{1}(M)
,
\Pi_{M}^{k}v\in P_{k}(M)
denotes the
L^{2}
projection of
v
onto
P_{k}(M)
. The space of piecewise polynomials of degree at most
k
with respect to the mesh
\mathcal{M}
or the faces
\Sigma
is denoted by
P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
or
P_{k}(\Sigma)
.
Given
v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, we define the
L^{2}
projection
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}v
of
v
onto
P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
by
(\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}v)|_{K}=\Pi_{K}^{k}v|_{K}
in any cell
K\in\mathcal{M}
.
We recall the stability of the
L^{2}
projection
\|\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\xi\|_{p}\lesssim\|\xi\|_{p}
for any
\xi\in L^{p}(\Omega)

from, e.g.,
[
23
, Lemma 3.2]
. A consequence of this is the quasi-best approximation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k})\xi\|_{p}\leq\|\xi-\varphi_{k}\|_{p}+\|\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}(\varphi_{k}-\xi)\|_{p}\lesssim\|\xi-\varphi_{k}\|_{p}

for any
\varphi_{k}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M})
, where
\varphi_{k}=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\varphi_{k}
is utilized in the penultimate inequality.

For a simplex

,
\mathrm{RT}_{k}(T)\coloneqq P_{k}(T;\mathbb{R}^{n})+xP_{k}(T)
denotes the space of Raviart-Thomas finite element functions. The piecewise version of this on a regular triangulation

into simplices is called
\mathrm{RT}^{\mathrm{pw}}_{k}(\mathcal{T})
, while
\mathrm{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T})\coloneqq\mathrm{RT}_{k}^{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{T})\cap W
denotes the space of globally
W^{p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{div},\Omega)
Raviart-Thomas functions.
The mesh
\mathcal{M}
gives rise to the piecewise constant function
h_{\mathcal{M}}\in P_{0}(\mathcal{M})
with
h_{\mathcal{M}}|_{K}=h_{K}
;
h_{\max}\coloneqq\max_{K\in\mathcal{M}}h_{K}
is the maximal mesh-size of
\mathcal{M}
.
For any
f\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, the oscillation of

reads
\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\|h_{\mathcal{M}}(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k})f\|_{p^{\prime}}
.

2.3.
Discrete ansatz spaces

Given a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
V(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})\times P_{k}(\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{m})
(resp.
W(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})\times P_{k}(\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{m})
) denote the discrete ansatz space for
V
(resp.
W
).
For any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})
, we use the notation
v_{K}\coloneqq v_{\mathcal{M}}|_{K}
and
v_{S}\coloneqq v_{\Sigma}|_{S}
to abbreviate the restriction of
v_{\mathcal{M}}
in a cell
K\in\mathcal{M}
and
v_{\Sigma}
along a face
S\in\Sigma
.
The same notation applies to discrete objects in
W(\mathcal{M})
.
The set of Dirichlet faces
\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}}
(resp. Neumann faces
\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}}
) gives rise to the subspace
P_{k}(\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}};\mathbb{R}^{m})
(resp.
P_{k}(\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}};\mathbb{R}^{m})
) of
P_{k}(\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{m})
with the convention
v_{\Sigma}\in P_{k}(\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}};\mathbb{R}^{m})
(resp.
\tau_{\Sigma}\in P_{k}(\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}};\mathbb{R}^{m})
) if
v_{S}\equiv 0
(resp.
\tau_{S}\equiv 0
) on Dirichlet faces
S\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}}
(resp. Neumann faces
S\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}}
) to model homogenous Dirichlet boundary condition (resp. vanishing normal traces).
This defines the discrete spaces
V_{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})\times P_{k}(\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}};\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
W_{\mathrm{N}}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})\times P_{k}(\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}};\mathbb{R}^{m})

and

The interpolation operators
\mathrm{I}_{V}:V\to V(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathrm{I}_{W}:W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M})\to W(\mathcal{M})
map

to
\mathrm{I}_{V}v\coloneqq(\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k+1}v,(\Pi_{S}^{k}v)_{S\in\Sigma})\in V(\mathcal{M})

.

2.4.
Reconstruction operators and stabilization

The reconstruction operators
\mathrm{D}_{h}
and
\mathrm{div}_{h}
provide an approximation of the differential operators
\mathrm{D}
and
\mathrm{div}
.

Gradient reconstruction.

The gradient reconstruction
\mathrm{D}_{h}v_{h}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})
of
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V(\mathcal{M})
is the unique solution to, for all
\tau_{k}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{h}v_{h}:\tau_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}v_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{pw}}\tau_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\Sigma}\int_{S}v_{S}\cdot[\tau_{k}\nu_{S}]_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Divergence reconstruction.

The divergence reconstruction
\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau_{h}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})
of
\tau_{h}=(\tau_{\mathcal{M}},\tau_{\Sigma})\in W(\mathcal{M})
is the unique solution to, for all
v_{k}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{h}\tau_{h}\cdot\varphi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}\varphi_{k}:\tau_{\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\Sigma}\int_{S}[\varphi_{k}]_{S}\cdot\tau_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Potential reconstruction

The potential reconstruction
\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}\in P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})
of
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{M}},v_{\Sigma})\in V(\mathcal{M})
satisfies, for any
\varphi_{k+1}\in P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}\varphi_{k+1}\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.4)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\Omega}v_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot\Delta_{\mathrm{pw}}\varphi_{k+1}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\Sigma}\int_{S}v_{S}\cdot[\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}\varphi_{k+1}\nu_{S}]_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s.

This defines
\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}
uniquely up to piecewise constants, which are fixed by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathcal{R}_{h}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{K}v_{K}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for any }K\in\mathcal{M}.

The following lemma states the characteristic commuting property of the reconstruction operators in the HHO methodology
[
28
,
27
,
24
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(commuting property)
.

Any
v\in V
and
\tau\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{M})
satisfy
\mathrm{D}_{h}\mathrm{I}_{V}v=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\mathrm{D}v
,
\mathrm{div}_{h}\mathrm{I}_{W}\tau=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\mathrm{div}\tau
, and the orthogonality
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}(v-\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathrm{I}_{V}v)\perp\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}P_{k+1}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})
with respect to the
L^{2}
scalar product.

The key for establishing the discrete weak duality (
1.3
) is the design of a reconstruction operator on the dual level in the spirit of
[
30
,
29
]
.
The latter can be computed explicitly, although in this paper, it is only used for theoretical purposes.

Dual potential reconstruction

Let
Z(\mathcal{M})
denote the

Proof.

\mathrm{D}_{h}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathrm{D}_{h}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h}\cdot v_{\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}}}(v_{S}-\{v_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S})\cdot[\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\nu_{S}]_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\sum_{S\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}}}[v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot(\sigma_{S}-\{\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\nu_{S}\}_{S})\,\mathrm{d}s

.

The combination of this with the discrete Euler-Lagrange equations (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot(\nabla_{u}\psi_{h}(x,\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}u_{\mathcal{M}})-\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}x+\sum_{S\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}}}\int_{S}(v_{S}-\{v_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S})\cdot[\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\nu_{S}]_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s

(5.4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{S\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}[v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\cdot(\sigma_{S}-\{\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}\nu_{S}\}_{S})\,\mathrm{d}s+\mathrm{s}(u_{h};v_{h})=0

.

Let
v_{\Sigma}
be chosen such that
v_{S}\coloneqq\Pi_{S}^{k}\{v_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S}=\{v_{\mathcal{M}}\}_{S}
along
S\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}}
in (

). For any

and

-[v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}

, and
-\frac{\nu_{S}\cdot\nu_{K}|_{S}}{2}[v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}
if

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{s}(u_{h};v_{h})=-\sum_{S\in\Sigma(\Omega)}\frac{h_{S}^{-s}}{2}\int_{S}(T_{K_{+},S}u_{h}-T_{K_{-},S}u_{h})\cdot\Pi_{S}^{k}[v_{\mathcal{M}}]_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle-\sum_{S\in\Sigma_{\mathrm{D}}}h_{S}^{-s}\int_{S}T_{K_{+},S}u_{h}\cdot\Pi_{S}^{k}v_{\mathcal{M}}\,\mathrm{d}s

.

This, the definition of
\sigma_{\Sigma}
in
Lemma

5.2
, and (
5.4
) conclude

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}v_{\mathcal{M}}\cdot(\nabla_{u}\psi_{h}(x,\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}u_{\mathcal{M}})-\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h})\,\mathrm{d}x=0\quad\text{for any }v_{\mathcal{M}}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m}),

whence
\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h}=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\nabla_{u}\psi_{h}(x,\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}u_{\mathcal{M}})
.
Given any
v_{k}\in P_{k}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, a piecewise integration by parts and
\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{k}\in P_{k-1}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{M})
show

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\sigma_{0}\cdot v_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{k}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F},F\subset\partial\mathcal{M}}\int_{F}[v_{k}]_{F}\cdot\sigma_{0}\nu_{F}\,\mathrm{d}s

because the function
v_{k}
only jumps on the skeleton
\partial\mathcal{M}=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\partial K
of
\mathcal{M}
.
This, the definition of
\sigma_{0}
from (
5.3
), and of
\mathrm{div}_{h}
from (

) imply

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\sigma_{0}\cdot v_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}v_{k}+\sum_{S\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{\mathrm{N}}}\int_{S}[v_{k}]_{S}\cdot\sigma_{S}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h}\cdot v_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The assertion then follows from
\mathrm{div}_{h}\sigma_{h}=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}\nabla_{u}\psi_{h}(x,\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{k}u_{\mathcal{M}})
.
∎

In order to derive a posteriori error estimate, we consider throughout the remaining parts of this paper the explicit representation (
4.1
) of the lower-order terms.
In particular,
\varphi\equiv 0
in (

).
The following arguments are well known in the literature

,
4
,

,

and sketched below for later reference.
Let
\widetilde{u}
minimize

\displaystyle E_{\sigma_{0}}(v)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}(\Psi(\mathrm{D}v)+\mathrm{div}\sigma_{0}\cdot v)\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{among }v\in V_{\mathrm{D}}.

The corresponding dual energy reads

\displaystyle E_{\sigma_{0}}^{*}(\tau)\coloneqq-\int_{\Omega}\Psi^{*}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x-\chi_{\mathrm{div}\sigma_{0}}(\mathrm{div}\tau)\quad\text{for any }\tau\in W_{\mathrm{N}}.

Abbreviate

.
The

from Lemma

\displaystyle E(u)-E_{\sigma_{0}}(u)=-\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f}\cdot u\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{\mathrm{P}}c_{\ref{cnst:Du}}\mathrm{osc}(\widetilde{f},\mathcal{M})

Given a conforming postprocessing
v_{0}\in V_{\mathrm{D}}
, this and

prove

\displaystyle\mathrm{LEB}\coloneqq E^{*}_{\sigma_{0}}(\sigma_{0})-c_{\mathrm{P}}c_{\ref{cnst:Du}}\mathrm{osc}(\widetilde{f},\mathcal{M})\leq E(u)\leq E(v_{0}).

In practice,

is the nodal average of
u_{\mathcal{M}}
in a matching simplicial submesh

of
\mathcal{M}
.
If
\Psi
satisfies (

can be bounded as follows. The arguments, that lead to (

\displaystyle e(u,v_{0})\leq E(v_{0})-E(u)\leq E(v_{0})-\mathrm{LEB}.

]

.

Remark 5.3

(data oscillation)

6.1.
Adaptive mesh-refining algorithm

Up to data oscillation, the a posteriori error estimator (

) consists of the duality gap

The localization of this has been discussed in

An integration by parts with

\displaystyle E_{\sigma_{0}}(v_{0})-E^{*}_{\sigma_{0}}(\sigma_{0})=\int_{\Omega}(\Psi(\nabla v_{0})-\sigma_{0}\cdot\nabla v_{0}+\Psi^{*}(\sigma_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}x\eqqcolon\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\eta(K)

\displaystyle\eta(K)\coloneqq\int_{K}(\Psi(\nabla v_{0})-\sigma_{0}\cdot\nabla v_{0}+\Psi^{*}(\sigma_{0}))\,\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\eta(K)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum\nolimits_{K\in\mathfrak{M}}\eta(K).

The convergence history plots display the quantities of interest against the number of degrees of freedom
\mathrm{ndof}
in a log-log plot.
(Recall the scaling

for uniform meshes.)
Solid lines indicate adaptive, while dashed lines are associated with uniform mesh refinements.

The discrete minimization problem (
1.4
) is solved by an iterative solver

from the MATLAB standard library in an extension of the data structures and the short MATLAB programs
[
2
]
.
The first and (piecewise) second derivatives of

have been provided for the trust-region quasi-Newton scheme with parameters of

Given fixed positive parameters

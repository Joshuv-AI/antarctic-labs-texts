On quantum states generated from interacting oscillators
Haqi Ismael Shareef, Fardin Kheirandish

Abstract
In this paper, we study the quantum states generated from two and three linearly interacting quantum harmonic oscillators. We consider the possibility that one of the oscillators be under the influence of a classical external source and obtain the total and reduced density matrices related to the system. We show how the problem can be generalized to n n -linearly interacting oscillators straightforwardly.

Two interacting oscillators

g(t)

2

\tau

t>\tau

g(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g_{0},&t\leq\tau,\\

(1)

\hat{H}(t)=\underbrace{\hbar\omega_{0}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}_{\hat{H}_{a}}+\underbrace{\hbar\Omega\,\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}}_{\hat{H}_{b}}+\underbrace{\hbar g(t)\,(\hat{a}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{b})}_{\hat{H}_{ab}(t)},

\hat{H}(t)=\hbar\,\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{a}^{\dagger}&\hat{b}^{\dagger}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega_{0}&g(t)\\

\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{a}\\
\hat{b}\\
\end{array}\right).

(3)

|\psi(0)\rangle

\displaystyle\hat{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta(t)\,\hat{A}+\sin\theta(t)\,\hat{B},

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sin\theta(t)\,\hat{A}+\cos\theta(t)\,\hat{B},

\displaystyle\lambda_{A}(t)=\bar{\omega}-\sqrt{g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}/4},

(7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{B}(t)=\bar{\omega}+\sqrt{g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}/4},

(8)

\displaystyle\tan(2\theta(t))=2g(t)/\triangle.

(9)

In Eqs. (

8

\theta

\displaystyle\lambda_{B}(t)

2.1
Energy exchange

In Hamiltonian Eq. (
2
), let the
a
-oscillator be prepared in the number state
|n\rangle_{a}
and the
b
-oscillator be prepared in the vacuum state
|0\rangle_{b}
. Note that the vacuum state of the Hamiltonian Eq. (
69
) is
|0\rangle_{A}\otimes|0\rangle_{B}
with zero energy, and the state
|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}
is the vacuum state of the same Hamiltonian expressed as Eq. (
2
). Therefore, due to the uniqueness of the vacuum state, we have
|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}\equiv|0\rangle_{A}\otimes|0\rangle_{B}
. Accordingly, the initial state can be written as

\displaystyle|\psi(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b},

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\,|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\cos(\theta)\hat{A}^{\dagger}+\sin(\theta)\hat{B}^{\dagger})^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\,|0\rangle_{A}\otimes|0\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}\sqrt{\binom{n}{r}}(\cos(\theta))^{n-r}(\sin(\theta))^{r}\,|n-r\rangle_{A}\otimes|r\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\cos(\theta))^{n}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\sqrt{\binom{n}{r}}(\tan(\theta))^{r}\,|n-r\rangle_{A}\otimes|r\rangle_{B},

where
|m\rangle_{A(B)}
belongs to the Hilbert space of
A(B)
-oscillator, respectively. The unitary evolution operator corresponding to Hamiltonian Eq. (
69
) is the tensor product of the unitary operators corresponding to
A(B)
-oscillators

\displaystyle\hat{U}(t)=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{A}(t)\otimes\hat{U}_{B}(t),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-if_{A}(t)\,\hat{A}^{\dagger}\,\hat{A}}\otimes e^{-if_{B}(t)\,\hat{B}^{\dagger}\,\hat{B}},

(13)

where for notational simplicity we have defined

f_{A(B)}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\lambda_{A(B)}(t^{\prime}).

(14)

By applying
\hat{U}(t)
on the initial state Eq. (
12
), we find the evolved state as

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle=[e^{-if_{A}(t)}\cos(\theta)]^{n}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\sqrt{\binom{n}{r}}(\tan(\theta))^{r}\,e^{ir\,\delta f(t)}|n-r\rangle_{A}\otimes|r\rangle_{B},

(15)

where for notational simplicity we have defined

\displaystyle\delta f(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{A}(t)-f_{B}(t)=-2\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\sqrt{g^{2}(t^{\prime})+\triangle^{2}}.

(16)

The state
|\psi(t)\rangle
in Eq. (
15
) is expressed in
A(B)
-oscillator number states or Fock states. By using the inverse of the Bogoliubov transformations Eq. (
4
)

\displaystyle\hat{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta(t)\,\hat{a}-\sin\theta(t)\,\hat{b},

(17)

\displaystyle\hat{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin\theta(t)\,\hat{a}+\cos\theta(t)\,\hat{b},

(18)

and following the same process we used in Eq. (
12
), the state
|\psi(t)\rangle
can be represented in the initial
a(b)
-oscillator states as

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle(\cos(\theta))^{2n}\sum_{r=0}^{n}\sum_{p=0}^{n-r}\sum_{q=0}^{r}\,\frac{(-1)^{p}\,\sqrt{n!\,(n-p-q)!\,(p+q)!}}{p!\,q!\,(n-r-p)!\,(r-q)!}

\displaystyle\times(\tan(\theta))^{2r+p-q}e^{-i(n-r)f_{A}(t)}\,e^{-irf_{B}(t)}\,|n-(p+q)\rangle_{a}\otimes|p+q\rangle_{b}.

2.2
Binomial states

As an example, let us set
n=1
, in Eq. (
2.1
) then

|\psi(t)\rangle=C_{10}(t)\,|1\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}+C_{01}(t)\,|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|1\rangle_{b},

(20)

where

\displaystyle|C_{10}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4g^{2}(t)\,\cos^{2}[\delta f(t)/2]+\triangle^{2}}{4g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}}=q(t),

(21)

\displaystyle|C_{01}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4g^{2}(t)\,\sin^{2}[\delta f(t)/2]}{4g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}}=p(t),

(22)

and
q(t)+p(t)=1
. Also, by setting
n=2
in the same equation, we find

|\psi(t)\rangle=C_{20}(t)\,|2\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}+C_{11}(t)\,|1\rangle_{a}\otimes|1\rangle_{b}+C_{02}(t)\,|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|2\rangle_{b},

(23)

\displaystyle|C_{20}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{4g^{2}(t)\,\cos^{2}[\delta f(t)/2]+\triangle^{2}}{4g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}}\right]^{2}=q^{2}(t),

(24)

\displaystyle|C_{02}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{4g^{2}(t)\,\sin^{2}[\delta f(t)/2]}{4g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}}\right]^{2}=p^{2}(t),

(25)

\displaystyle|C_{11}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4g^{2}(t)\Big[2g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}-2g^{2}(t)\cos^{2}(\delta f(t))-\triangle^{2}\cos(\delta f(t))\Big]}{(4g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2})^{2}},

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2q(t)p(t).

In general, for the initial state
|\psi(0)\rangle=|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}
, one finds the evolved state as a binomial state given by

|\psi(t)\rangle=\sum_{s=0}^{n}C_{n-s,s}(t)\,|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b},

(27)

where the coefficients are given by

\displaystyle C_{n-s,s}(t)=

\displaystyle e^{-inf_{a}(t)}(\cos(\theta))^{2n}\sqrt{\binom{n}{s}}(1+\tan^{2}(\theta)\,e^{i\delta f(t)})^{n-s}\,(\tan(\theta))^{s}(e^{i\delta f(t)}-1)^{s}.

Therefore, the probability that the system be found in the state
|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b}
at time
t
is

\displaystyle|C_{n-s,s}(t)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\binom{n}{s}\,\Bigg[\frac{4g^{2}(t)\,\cos^{2}[\delta f(t)/2]+\triangle^{2}}{4g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}}\Bigg]^{n-s}\,\Bigg[\frac{4g^{2}(t)\,\sin^{2}[\delta f(t)/2]}{4g^{2}(t)+\triangle^{2}}\Bigg]^{s},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{n}{s}\,(q(t))^{n-s}(p(t)^{s}.

(29)

which is a binomial distribution that reminiscences a random walk with the time-dependent right (
p(t)
) and left (
q(t)
) probabilities given in Eqs. (
21
,
22
), respectively.

On the resonance (
\Delta=0
), we can set
\theta=\pi/4
, and the Eqs. (
27
,
2.2
) become

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle_{reso.}=

\displaystyle e^{-in(f_{A}(t)+f_{B}(t))/2}\,(\cos(\delta f(t)/2))^{n}

(30)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sqrt{\binom{n}{s}}\,(i\tan(\delta f(t)/2))^{s}\,|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b},

and

\displaystyle|C_{n-s,s}(t)|_{reso.}^{2}=\binom{n}{s}\,\big(\cos^{2}(\delta f(t)/2)\big)^{n-s}\,\big(\sin^{2}(\delta f(t)/2)\big)^{s},

(31)

respectively. Note that on the resonance we have

\delta f(t)=-2\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,|g(t^{\prime})|=-2G(t).

(32)

For a constant coupling
g(t)=g_{0}
, we have
\delta f(t)=-2g_{0}t
, and at the time
t_{0}=\pi/(2g_{0})
, we have
\cos(\delta f(t_{0})/2)=0
, and from Eq. (
31
) we find

|C_{n-s,s}(t_{0})|_{reso.}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&0\leq s\leq n-1,\\
1,&s=n.\end{array}\right.

(33)

Therefore, by using Eq. (
27
) we deduce that at
t=t_{0}
the initial state
|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}
has transformed to the state
|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|n\rangle_{b}
.

2.3
The reduced density matrix

From Eq. (
27
), the total density matrix at time
t
is

\hat{\rho}(t)=|\psi(t)\rangle\langle\psi(t)|=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sum_{s^{\prime}=0}^{n}C_{n-s,s}\,\bar{C}_{n-s^{\prime},s^{\prime}}|n-s\rangle_{a\,a}\langle n-s^{\prime}|\otimes|s\rangle_{b\,b}\langle s^{\prime}|,

(34)

and the reduced density matrix corresponding to the
a
-mode can be obtained by tracing out the
b
-mode degrees of freedom as

\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)=\mbox{Tr}_{b}\,[\hat{\rho}(t)]=\sum_{s=0}^{n}|C_{n-s,s}|^{2}\,|n-s\rangle_{a\,a}\langle n-s|,

(35)

where
\mbox{Tr}_{b}
is the partial trace over
b
-mode degrees of freedom. Having the reduced density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)
we can find the average energy of the
a
-mode oscillator at time
t
as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{H}_{a}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}[\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)\,\hat{H}_{a}]=\hbar\omega_{0}\,\sum_{s=0}^{n}|C_{n-s,s}|^{2}\,(n-s),

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{0}\,\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}\,(q(t))^{n-s}(p(t))^{s}\,(n-s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{0}\,\Big(n-p\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\Big)(p+q)^{n},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\hbar\omega_{0}q(t),

where on resonance
q(t)=\cos^{2}(\delta f(t)/2)=\cos^{2}(G(t))
.

2.4
The initial state is a coherent state

Let the initial state of the combined system be given by the separable state
|\varphi(0)\rangle=|\alpha\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}
, where
|\alpha\rangle_{a}
is a coherent state of the
a
-oscillator. The state of the system at time
t
is

\displaystyle|\varphi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}(t)|\alpha\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\underbrace{\hat{U}(t)|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}}_{|\psi(t)\rangle\,in\,Eq.(\ref{psit})},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\sum_{s=0}^{n}C_{n-s,s}(t)\,|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Big(\alpha\,\cos^{2}(\theta)[e^{-if_{A}(t)}+\tan^{2}(\theta)\,e^{-if_{B}(t)}]\Big)^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sqrt{\binom{n}{s}}\Big(\frac{\tan(\theta)[e^{i\delta f(t)}-1]}{1+\tan^{2}(\theta)\,e^{i\delta f(t)}}\Big)^{s}\,|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b},

and the probability of finding the oscillators in the state
|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b}
is

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}|\alpha\cos^{2}(\theta)[1+\tan^{2}(\theta)e^{i\delta f(t)}]|^{2n}}{n!}}_{Poisson}\underbrace{\binom{n}{s}\Bigg|\frac{\tan(\theta)[e^{i\delta f(t)}-1]}{1+\tan^{2}(\theta)e^{i\delta f(t)}}\Bigg|^{2}}_{binomial},

(38)

which is the product of a Poisson and a binomial distribution.

On the resonance, Eq. (
2.4
) becomes

\displaystyle|\varphi(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(\alpha\,e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\,\cos(G(t))\big)^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sqrt{\binom{n}{s}}\big(-i\tan(G(t))\big)^{s}\,|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b},

and in this case, one finds the reduced density matrix for the
a
-oscillator as

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)=\mbox{Tr}_{b}\big[|\varphi(t)\rangle\langle\varphi(t)|\big]=

\displaystyle e^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s=0}^{n}\frac{\big(\alpha\,e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\,\cos(G(t))\big)^{n}\,\big(\bar{\alpha}\,e^{i\omega_{0}t}\,\cos(G(t))\big)^{n^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n!n^{\prime}!}}

\displaystyle\times\sqrt{\binom{n}{s}\binom{n^{\prime}}{s}}\big(\tan^{2}(G(t))\big)^{s}\,|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b}.

By making use of Eq. (
2.4
), the average energy of
a
-oscillator is obtained as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{H}_{a}\rangle=\mbox{Tr}[\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)\,\hat{H}_{a}]=\hbar\omega_{0}|\alpha|^{2}\cos^{2}(G(t)).

(41)

For a constant coupling, we have
g(t)=g_{0}
, (
G(t)=g_{0}t
), therefore, the
a
-oscillator loses all its energy at time
t_{0}=\pi/2g_{0}
which is transferred to the
b
-oscillator. By using Eq. (
2.4
), one finds the state of the system at time
t=t_{0}
as
|\varphi(t_{0})\rangle=|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|-i\alpha e^{-i\omega_{0}t_{0}}\rangle_{b}
. If we chose
g_{0}=\omega_{0}/3
, then the final state at time
t_{0}
will be
|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|\alpha\rangle_{b}
, that is, the coherent state has transferred to the
b
-oscillator.

2.6
External classical source

In this section, we consider the effect of an external time-dependent classical source acting linearly on the
b
-oscillator. Here, the main subsystem is the
a
-oscillator. The total Hamiltonian in this case is

\hat{H}^{(k)}(t)=\underbrace{\hbar\omega_{0}\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}_{\hat{H}_{a}}+\underbrace{\hbar\Omega\,\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}}_{\hat{H}_{b}}+\underbrace{\hbar g(t)\,(\hat{a}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{b})}_{\hat{H}_{ab}(t)}+\underbrace{\hbar k(t)\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\hbar\bar{k}(t)\hat{b}}_{\hat{H}_{bs}}.

(43)

By using the same Bogoliubov transformations Eqs. (
4
,
5
), the Hamiltonian can be transformed to the following form

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{(k)}(t)=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\hbar\lambda_{A}(t)\,\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}-\hbar\sin\theta\,(k(t)\hat{A}^{\dagger}+\bar{k}(t)\hat{A})}_{\hat{H}_{A}(t)}

(44)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\hbar\lambda_{B}(t)\hat{B}^{\dagger}\hat{B}+\hbar\cos\theta\,(k(t)\hat{B}^{\dagger}+\bar{k}(t)\hat{B})}_{\hat{H}_{B}(t)},

and the time-evolution operator can be written immediately as

\hat{U}^{(k)}(t)=\hat{U}_{A}(t)\otimes\hat{U}_{B}(t),

(45)

where

i\hbar\,\frac{d\hat{U}_{A(B)}(t)}{dt}=\hat{H}_{A(B)}(t)\,\hat{U}_{A(B)}(t).

(46)

Our aim is to find the unitary operator
\hat{U}_{A}(t)
. Note that the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{B}
can be obtained from the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{A}
using the transformations
\sin\theta\rightarrow-\cos\theta,\,A\rightarrow B
, therefore, one can obtain
\hat{U}_{B}(t)
from
\hat{U}_{A}(t)
using the same transformations. To find
\hat{U}_{A}(t)
let us make the ansatz

\hat{U}_{A}(t)=e^{-if_{0}^{A}(t)/\hbar}\,e^{-if_{1}^{A}(t)\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}/\hbar}\,e^{-if_{2}^{A}(t)\hat{A}^{\dagger}/\hbar}\,e^{-i\bar{f}_{2}^{A}(t)\hat{A}/\hbar}.

(47)

By inserting
\hat{U}_{A}(t)
into Eq. (
46
) we will find the unknown functions
f_{0}(t)
,
f_{1}(t)
and
f_{2}(t)
as

\displaystyle f_{1}^{A}(t)=\hbar\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\lambda_{A}(t^{\prime})=\hbar\,f_{A}(t),

\displaystyle f_{2}^{A}(t)=-\hbar\,\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\sin\theta(t^{\prime})\,k(t^{\prime})\,e^{if_{A}(t^{\prime})},

\displaystyle f_{0}^{A}(t)=-\frac{i}{2\hbar}\,|f_{2}^{A}(t)|^{2}.

(48)

In the case of resonance, we can set
\theta=\pi/4
, and also by assuming a time-independent coupling constant
g(t)=g_{0}
, we find the following explicit forms for the unknown functions

\displaystyle f_{1}^{A}(t)=\hbar(\omega_{0}-g_{0})t,

\displaystyle f_{2}^{A}(t)=-\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2}}\,\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,k(t^{\prime})\,e^{i(\omega_{0}-g_{0})t^{\prime}},

\displaystyle f_{0}^{A}(t)=\frac{-i}{4\hbar}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,k(t^{\prime})\,e^{i(\omega_{0}-g_{0})t^{\prime}}\Big|^{2}.

(49)

Similarly, we can assume the following form for
\hat{U}_{B}

\hat{U}_{B}(t)=e^{-if_{0}^{B}(t)/\hbar}\,e^{-if_{1}^{B}(t)\hat{B}^{\dagger}\hat{B}/\hbar}\,e^{-if_{2}^{B}(t)\hat{B}^{\dagger}/\hbar}\,e^{-i\bar{f}_{2}^{B}(t)\hat{B}/\hbar},

(50)

and find the unknown functions from the previously mentioned transformations
\sin\theta\rightarrow-\cos\theta,\,A\rightarrow B
as

\displaystyle f_{1}^{B}(t)=\hbar\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\lambda_{B}(t^{\prime})=\hbar\,f_{B}(t),

\displaystyle f_{2}^{B}(t)=\hbar\,\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,\cos\theta(t^{\prime})\,k(t^{\prime})\,e^{if_{B}(t^{\prime})},

\displaystyle f_{0}^{B}(t)=-\frac{i}{2\hbar}\,|f_{2}^{B}(t)|^{2}.

(51)

2.7
The evolution of the initial state

let us assume that initially (at
t=0
), each oscillator is prepared in its ground state, then the initial state of the combined system is

|\psi(0)\rangle=|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}\equiv|0\rangle_{A}\otimes|0\rangle_{B}=|0\rangle.

(52)

By applying the time-evolution operator Eq. (
45
) on the initial state, we will find the evolved state as

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{a}|0\rangle_{A}\otimes\hat{U}_{B}|0\rangle_{B},

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(f_{0}^{A}(t)+f_{0}^{B}(t))/\hbar}\,e^{(|f_{2}^{A}(t)|^{2}+|f_{2}^{B}(t)|^{2})/2\hbar^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big|{-\frac{i}{\hbar}f_{2}^{A}(t)\,e^{-if_{1}^{A}(t)/\hbar}}\Big\rangle_{A}\otimes\Big|{-\frac{i}{\hbar}f_{2}^{B}(t)\,e^{-if_{1}^{B}(t)/\hbar}}\Big\rangle_{B}.

For notational simplicity, we define the following time-dependent functions

\mu_{A(B)}(t):=-\frac{i}{\hbar}f_{2}^{A(B)}(t)\,e^{-if_{1}^{A(B)}(t)/\hbar}.

(54)

Now using the definition of a coherent state and Bogoliubov transformations, Eq. (
53
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(f_{0}^{A}(t)+f_{0}^{B}(t))/\hbar}\,\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu_{A})^{n}}{n!}\frac{(\mu_{B})^{m}}{m!}\,|n\rangle_{A}\otimes|m\rangle_{B},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(f_{0}^{A}(t)+f_{0}^{B}(t))/\hbar}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu_{A})^{n}}{n!}\frac{(\mu_{B})^{m}}{m!}\,(\cos\theta\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\sin\theta\,\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{n}(\sin\theta\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\cos\theta\,\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{m}\,|0\rangle,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(f_{0}^{A}(t)+f_{0}^{B}(t))/\hbar}\,e^{(\mu_{A}\cos\theta+\mu_{B}\sin\theta)\hat{a}^{\dagger}}e^{(\mu_{B}\cos\theta-\mu_{A}\sin\theta)\hat{b}^{\dagger}}\,|0\rangle,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(f_{0}^{A}(t)+f_{0}^{B}(t))/\hbar}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\mu_{A}\cos\theta+\mu_{B}\sin\theta)^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\frac{(\mu_{B}\cos\theta-\mu_{A}\sin\theta)^{m}}{\sqrt{m!}}\,|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|m\rangle_{b}.

Therefore, the probability that at time
t
we find the combined system in the product state
|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|m\rangle_{b}
is

|C_{n,m}^{(k)}(t)|^{2}=\frac{|\mu_{A}\cos\theta+\mu_{B}\sin\theta|^{2n}}{n!}\,\frac{|\mu_{B}\cos\theta-\mu_{A}\sin\theta|^{2m}}{m!}\,e^{\frac{2}{\hbar}\mbox{Im}(f_{0}^{A}+f_{0}^{B})},

(56)

where
\mbox{Im}(g)
gives the imaginary part of a complex function
g
. By using Eqs. (
2.6
,
2.6
), one easily finds

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|C_{n,m}^{(k)}(t)|^{2}=1,

(57)

as expected. From Eq. (
2.7
) one also finds
|\psi(t)\rangle
as a separable state of two coherent states

|\psi(t)\rangle=|\mu_{A}\cos\theta+\mu_{B}\sin\theta\rangle_{a}\otimes|\mu_{B}\cos\theta-\mu_{A}\sin\theta\rangle_{b}.

(58)

In the case of resonance (
\theta=\pi/4
) and a time-independent coupling function, we have

|\psi(t)\rangle=|\alpha_{A}\rangle_{a}\otimes|\alpha_{B}\rangle_{b},

(59)

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{B}+\mu_{A}}{\sqrt{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,k(t^{\prime})e^{-i\omega_{0}(t-t^{\prime})}\sin g_{0}(t-t^{\prime}),

(60)

\displaystyle\alpha_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{B}-\mu_{A}}{\sqrt{2}}=-\frac{i}{2}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,k(t^{\prime})e^{-i\omega_{0}(t-t^{\prime})}\cos g_{0}(t-t^{\prime}).

(61)

2.8
The reduced density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)

The elements of the reduced density matrix
\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)
corresponding to the main system (
a
-oscillator) initially prepared in the ground state, can be obtained from the state Eq. (
2.7
) as

\displaystyle{}_{a}\langle p|\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)|q\rangle_{a}=

\displaystyle\mbox{Tr}_{b}\left[|\psi(t)\rangle\langle\psi(t)|\right],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{2}{\hbar}\mbox{Im}[f_{0}^{A}(t)+f_{0}^{B}(t)]}\,e^{|\mu_{B}\cos\theta-\mu_{A}\sin\theta|^{2}}

(62)

\displaystyle\times\,\frac{(\mu_{B}\cos\theta+\mu_{A}\sin\theta)^{p}}{\sqrt{p!}}\frac{(\bar{\mu}_{B}\cos\theta+\bar{\mu}_{A}\sin\theta)^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}.

The probability that the
a
-oscillator be found in the state
|n\rangle_{a}
at time
t
is

\displaystyle P_{a}(n,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{}_{a}\langle n|\hat{\rho}_{a}(t)|n\rangle_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{2}{\hbar}\mbox{Im}[f_{0}^{A}(t)+f_{0}^{B}(t)]}\,e^{|\mu_{B}\cos\theta-\mu_{A}\sin\theta|^{2}}\,\frac{|\mu_{B}\cos\theta+\mu_{A}\sin\theta|^{2n}}{n!},

which is a Poisson distribution as expected from Eq. (

2.9
Time-evolution of an arbitrary state

An arbitrary initial state of the combined system is a linear combination of the tensor product states
|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|m\rangle_{b}
where
|n\rangle_{a}\,(|m\rangle_{b})
is a number state in the Hilbert of the
a\,(b)
-oscillator. Since the evolution is linear, we only need to find the time evolution of the state
|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|m\rangle_{b}
. We have

\displaystyle|\varphi(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}(t)\,|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|m\rangle_{b},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{A}(t)\otimes\hat{U}_{B}(t)\,\frac{(\hat{a}^{\dagger})^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\,\frac{(\hat{b}^{\dagger})^{m}}{\sqrt{m!}}\,|0\rangle,

(64)

by inserting the Bogoliubov transformations Eqs. (
4
,
5
) into Eq. (
2.9
), we find

\displaystyle|\varphi(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!m!}}\sum_{p=0}^{n}\sum_{q=0}^{m}

\displaystyle\binom{n}{p}\binom{m}{q}(-1)^{m-q}\,(\cos\theta)^{n-p+q}(\sin\theta)^{m-q+p}

(65)

\displaystyle\times\hat{U}_{A}(t)(\hat{A}^{\dagger})^{n+m-p-q}\otimes\hat{U}_{B}(t)(\hat{B}^{\dagger})^{p+q}|0\rangle.

To proceed, we make use of the following identities

\displaystyle e^{-i\bar{f}_{2}^{A}\,\hat{A}/\hbar}\hat{A}^{\dagger}\,e^{i\bar{f}_{2}^{A}\,\hat{A}/\hbar}=\hat{A}^{\dagger}-\frac{i}{\hbar}\bar{f}_{2}^{A},

\displaystyle e^{-if_{1}^{A}\,\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}/\hbar}\hat{A}^{\dagger}\,e^{if_{1}^{A}\,\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}/\hbar}=e^{-if_{1}^{A}/\hbar}\,\hat{A}^{\dagger},

(66)

and rewrite Eq. (
65
) as

\displaystyle|\varphi(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!m!}}\sum_{p=0}^{n}\sum_{q=0}^{m}\binom{n}{p}\binom{m}{q}(-1)^{m-q}\,(\cos\theta)^{n-p+q}(\sin\theta)^{m-q+p}

(67)

\displaystyle\Big(e^{-if_{1}^{A}/\hbar}\hat{A}^{\dagger}-\frac{i}{\hbar}\bar{f}_{2}^{A}\Big)^{n+m-p-q}\otimes\Big(e^{-if_{1}^{B}/\hbar}\hat{B}^{\dagger}-\frac{i}{\hbar}\bar{f}_{2}^{B}\Big)^{p+q}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\underbrace{\hat{U}_{A}(t)\otimes\hat{U}_{B}(t)|0\rangle}_{\mbox{see}\,Eq.(\ref{gr2})}\Big].

Therefore, in the coherent state basis
\{|\alpha\rangle_{A}\otimes|\beta\rangle_{B}\equiv|\alpha\rangle_{A}|\beta\rangle_{B}\}
, we find

\displaystyle{}_{A}\langle\alpha|{}_{B}\langle\beta|\varphi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m}}{\sqrt{n!m!}}\sum_{p=0}^{n}\sum_{q=0}^{m}\binom{n}{p}\binom{m}{q}(-1)^{q}\,(\cos\theta)^{n-p+q}(\sin\theta)^{m-q+p}

\displaystyle\times\Big(e^{-if_{1}^{A}/\hbar}\,\bar{\alpha}-\frac{i}{\hbar}\bar{f}_{2}^{A}\Big)^{n+m-p-q}\,\Big(e^{-if_{1}^{B}/\hbar}\,\bar{\beta}-\frac{i}{\hbar}\bar{f}_{2}^{B}\Big)^{p+q}

\displaystyle\times e^{-i(f_{0}^{A}(t)+f_{0}^{B}(t))/\hbar}\,e^{(|f_{2}^{A}(t)|^{2}+|f_{2}^{B}(t)|^{2})/2\hbar^{2}}

\displaystyle\times{}_{A}\langle\alpha|{-\frac{i}{\hbar}f_{2}^{A}(t)\,e^{-if_{1}^{A}(t)/\hbar}}\rangle_{A}\,{}_{B}\langle\beta|{-\frac{i}{\hbar}f_{2}^{B}(t)\,e^{-if_{1}^{B}(t)/\hbar}}\rangle_{B}.

To obtain
|\varphi(t)\rangle
in terms of the standard basis
\{|n\rangle_{a}|m\rangle_{b}\}
, we should replace
\hat{U}_{A}(t)\otimes\hat{U}_{B}(t)|0\rangle
with Eq. (
2.7
) and write
\hat{A}^{\dagger}\,(\hat{B}^{\dagger})
in terms of the
\hat{a}^{\dagger}\,(\hat{b}^{\dagger})
using the inverse Bogoliubov transformations Eqs. (
17
,
18
).

3.1
Evolution of the initial state

Let the initial state of the three oscillators defined by the bosonic modes (
\hat{a},\,\hat{b},\,\hat{c})

|\psi(0)\rangle=|1\rangle_{a}\,|0\rangle_{b}\,|0\rangle_{c}
, then

\displaystyle|\psi(0)\rangle=

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}\,|0\rangle_{a}\,|0\rangle_{b}\,|0\rangle_{c},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{A}^{\dagger}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{C}^{\dagger})\,|0\rangle_{A}\,|0\rangle_{B}\,|0\rangle_{C},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle_{A}\,|0\rangle_{B}\,|0\rangle_{C}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|0\rangle_{A}\,|0\rangle_{B}\,|1\rangle_{C}.

(96)

The evolution operator corresponding to the Hamiltonian Eq. (

\displaystyle\hat{U}(t)=

\displaystyle e^{-if_{-}(t)\,\hat{A}^{\dagger}\hat{A}}\,e^{-i\omega_{0}t\,\hat{B}^{\dagger}\hat{B}}\,e^{-if_{+}(t)\,\hat{C}^{\dagger}\hat{C}},

(97)

where

\displaystyle f_{\pm}(t)=

\displaystyle\omega_{0}t\pm\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\,G(t^{\prime}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{0}t\pm\,\eta(t).

(98)

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-if_{-}(t)}|1\rangle_{A}\,|0\rangle_{B}\,|0\rangle_{C}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-if_{-}(t)}|0\rangle_{A}\,|0\rangle_{B}\,|1\rangle_{C},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg[-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-if_{-}(t)}\bigg(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{2}G(t)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{2}G(t)}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-if_{+}(t)}\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{2}G(t)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{2}G(t)}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\bigg)\Bigg]\,|0\rangle_{A}\,|0\rangle_{B}\,|0\rangle_{C},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}\bigg(e^{-if_{+}(t)}+e^{if_{-}(t)}\bigg)\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\frac{g(t)}{2G(t)}\bigg(e^{-if_{+}(t)}-e^{if_{-}(t)}\bigg)\hat{b}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle+\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{2G(t)}\bigg(e^{-if_{+}(t)}-e^{if_{-}(t)}\bigg)\hat{c}^{\dagger}\Bigg]\,|0\rangle_{ABC},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\cos\eta(t)\,|1\rangle_{a}\,|0\rangle_{b}\,|0\rangle_{c}-\frac{ig(t)}{G(t)}e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\sin\eta(t)\,|0\rangle_{a}\,|1\rangle_{b}\,|0\rangle_{c}

(99)

\displaystyle-\frac{ig^{\prime}(t)}{G(t)}e^{-i\omega_{0}t}\sin\eta(t)\,|0\rangle_{a}\,|1\rangle_{b}\,|1\rangle_{c}.

From Eq. (
3.1
), we find that the probability that the state remains at the initial state is

and the probability that the excitation be transferred to
b
-oscillator or
c
-oscillator is given by

and

respectively.

For the initial state
|\psi(0)\rangle=|2\rangle_{a}|0\rangle_{b}|0\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{-2i\omega_{0}t}(1+\cos(2\eta(t)))\,|2\rangle_{a}|0\rangle_{b}|0\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle+\frac{g^{2}(t)}{2G^{2}(t)}e^{-2i\omega_{0}t}(\cos(2\eta(t))-1)\,|0\rangle_{a}|2\rangle_{b}|0\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle+\frac{g^{\prime 2}(t)}{2G^{2}(t)}e^{-2i\omega_{0}t}(\cos(2\eta(t))-1)\,|0\rangle_{a}|0\rangle_{b}|2\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle+\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{2}G(t)}e^{-2i\omega_{0}t}(-i\sin(2\eta(t)))\,|1\rangle_{a}|1\rangle_{b}|0\rangle_{c}

\displaystyle+\frac{g^{\prime}(t)}{\sqrt{2}G(t)}e^{-2i\omega_{0}t}(-i\sin(2\eta(t)))\,|1\rangle_{a}|0\rangle_{b}|1\rangle_{c}

4
Conclusion

The main result obtained in Sec. (
2
) was Eq. (
2.2
) describing the probability of finding the oscillators in the state
|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b}
at time
t
if the initial state was
|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}
. The generated state Eq. (
27
) was a binomial state and the probability given in Eq. (
2.2
) was like a classical one-dimensional random walk with time-dependent right (
p(t)
) and left (
q(t)
) probabilities depending on detuning
\triangle
and coupling function
g(t)
. For the initial state
|\alpha\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b}
, the evolved state was given in Eq. (
2.4
) and in this case the probability of finding the oscillators in the state
|n-s\rangle_{a}\otimes|s\rangle_{b}
is

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}|\alpha\cos^{2}(\theta)[1+\tan^{2}(\theta)e^{i\delta f(t)}]|^{2n}}{n!}}_{Poisson}\underbrace{\binom{n}{s}\Bigg|\frac{\tan(\theta)[e^{i\delta f(t)}-1]}{1+\tan^{2}(\theta)e^{i\delta f(t)}}\Bigg|^{2}}_{binomial},

which is the product of a Poisson and a binomial distribution.

In the following we assumed an external source acting on the
b
-oscillator and found the evolved state Eq. (
67
) at time
t
for the initial state
|n\rangle_{a}\otimes|m\rangle_{b}
. For the special case
|0\rangle_{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{b}
, the generated state was the product of two coherent states given in Eq. (
58
).

For three linearly-interacting oscillators with time-dependent coupling functions
g_{1}(t)
and
g_{2}(t)
, we found the exact propagator Eq. (
97
) and calculated the probability transition
P_{a\rightarrow a}(t)
that the excitation remains with the
a
-oscillator or be transferred to
b(c)
-oscillator with probability transitions
P_{a\rightarrow b(c)}(t)
respectively.

In the following, based on the results obtained from two and three-linearly interacting oscillators, we could generalize the problem to
n
-linearly interacting oscillators with time-dependent coupling functions
g_{1}(t),g_{2}(t),\cdots,g_{n}(t)
. In this case, one needs to diagonalize the coefficient matrix
\Gamma
in Eq. (
3.2
) to find the corresponding Bogoliubov transformations and follow the same steps we took for two or three-linearly interacting oscillators.

Variation of constants formulae for forward and backward stochastic Volterra integral equations
Yushi Hamaguchi

Abstract
In this paper, we provide variation of constants formulae for linear (forward) stochastic Volterra integral equations (SVIEs, for short) and linear Type-II backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs, for short) in the usual Itô’s framework. For these purposes, we define suitable classes of stochastic Volterra kernels and introduce new notions of the products of adapted L 2 L^{2} -processes. Observing the algebraic properties of the products, we obtain the variation of constants formulae by means of the corresponding resolvent. Our framework includes SVIEs with singular kernels such as fractional stochastic differential equations. Also, our results can be applied to general classes of SVIEs and BSVIEs with infinitely many iterated stochastic integrals. The duality principle between generalized SVIEs and generalized BSVIEs is also proved.



Keywords:
Stochastic Volterra integral equations; backward stochastic Volterra integral equations; variation of constants formulae.

Introduction

In this paper, we provide variation of constants formulae for linear stochastic Volterra integral equations (SVIEs, for short) and linear backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs, for short). First of all, consider the following SVIE:

X(t)=\varphi(t)+\int^{t}_{0}j(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}k(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(0,T),

(1.1)

where
W
is a one-dimensional Brownian motion,
j
and
k
are given deterministic functions called the kernels, and
\varphi
is a given adapted process called the free term. If the kernels
j(t,s)
and
k(t,s)
do not depend on the first time-parameter
t
, and if the free term is of the form
\varphi(t)=x_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}b(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}\sigma(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)
for some constant
x_{0}
and adapted processes
b
and
\sigma
, then SVIE (
1.1
) is reduced to a linear stochastic differential equation (SDE, for short)

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X(t)=\{j(t)X(t)+b(t)\}\,\mathrm{d}t+\{k(t)X(t)+\sigma(t)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\ t\in(0,T),\\
X(0)=x_{0}.\end{cases}

More importantly, SVIE (
1.1
) includes a class of fractional SDEs of the following form:

\begin{cases}{}^{\mathrm{C}}\!D^{\alpha}_{0+}X(t)=j(t)X(t)+b(t)+\{k(t)X(t)+\sigma(t)\}\frac{\mathrm{d}W(t)}{\mathrm{d}t},\ t\in(0,T),\\
X(0)=x_{0},\end{cases}

where
{}^{\mathrm{C}}\!D^{\alpha}_{0+}
denotes the Caputo fractional differential operator of order
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
. Indeed, by the definition, an adapted process
X
is called a solution to the above fractional SDE if it satisfies the integral equation

X(t)=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\{j(s)X(s)+b(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\{k(s)X(s)+\sigma(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(0,T),

where
\Gamma(\alpha)
is the Gamma function (see
[
36
]
). Thus, the above fractional SDE can be seen as SVIE (
1.1
) with the singular kernels

j(t,s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}j(s),\ k(t,s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}k(s),

and the free term

\varphi(t)=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s).

Fractional differential systems are suitable tools to describe the dynamics of systems with memory effects and hereditary properties. There are many applications of fractional calculus in a variety of research fields including mathematical finance, physics, chemistry, biology, and other applied sciences. For detailed accounts of theory and applications of fractional calculus, see for example
[
8
,
10
,
24
,
26
]
and the references cited therein.

It is well-known that a variation of constants formula for deterministic Volterra integral equations is an important tool in many qualitative theory such as the linear perturbation theory (see Chapter 11 of
[
11
]
), the invariant manifold theory (see
[
9
]
), and the linear-quadratic optimal control theory (see
[
15
]
). In the first half of this paper, we extend the variation of constants formula to the stochastic case (
1.1
).

A difficulty to solve SVIE (
1.1
) comes from the term
\int^{t}_{0}k(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)
. In order to treat the stochastic convolution term, Berger and Mizel
[
5
]
introduced an extended stochastic integral for anticipated integrands, and gave a variation of constants formula in terms of the anticipated stochastic integral. Also, Øksendal and Zhang
[
20
,
21
]
studied SVIE (
1.1
), where the free term
\varphi
is not necessarily adapted, and the stochastic integral is taken in the Skorohod sense. They showed variation of constants formulae for (
1.1
) in extended frameworks of the functional process approach and the generalized white noise functional (Hida distribution) approach. Unfortunately, the frameworks of
[
5
,
20
,
21
]
cannot be applied to fractional SDEs due to some strong regularity and boundedness assumptions on the kernels
j
and
k
. For other studies on (nonlinear) SVIEs in the framework of anticipated calculus, see Berger and Mizel
[
3
,
4
]
, Pardoux and Protter
[
22
]
and Alòs and Nualart
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we provide another viewpoint to solve SVIE (
1.1
) in the classical Itô’s framework without the use of anticipated stochastic integrals. Our idea is to regard SVIE (
1.1
) as an algebraic equation in the space of adapted
L^{2}
-processes of the following form:

X=\varphi+J\ast X+K\star X.

(1.2)

Here, the
\ast
-product
J\ast X
with
J=j
corresponds to the convolution
\int^{t}_{0}j(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
in terms of the Lebesgue integral, and the
\star
-product
K\star X
with
K(t)=\int^{t}_{0}k(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)
corresponds to the stochastic convolution
\int^{t}_{0}k(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)
in terms of the stochastic integral. The
\star
-product for general adapted processes is defined by means of the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion (see
3.4
), and it has a representation by infinitely many iterated stochastic integrals in the classical Itô’s sense (see
3.6
). We introduce suitable classes of stochastic Volterra kernels
J
and
K
and show that these spaces become Banach algebras with respect to the
\ast
- and the
\star
-products, respectively (see
3.5
and
3.10
). Then, we show that the solution to the linear equation (
1.2
) is given by the variation of constants formula

X=\varphi+Q\ast\varphi+R\star\varphi,

where
Q
and
R
are resolvents of
J
and
K
in terms of the
\ast
- and the
\star
-products (see
3.22
). The main advantages of our frameworks are three fold. First, we do not need any anticipated calculus in the above expressions, and all stochastic integrals are understood in the usual Itô’s sense. Second, our framework includes linear SVIEs with singular kernels such as fractional SDEs; in
3.25
, we apply our general results to a time-fractional version of the Black–Scholes SDE and give an explicit expression of the solution. Third, we can apply our analysis to a more general class of SVIEs with (infinitely many) iterated stochastic integrals (see equation (
3.1
)), which is beyond the framework of
[
5
,
20
,
21
]
. This class of SVIEs includes fractional SDEs with delay and “noisy memory” (see Example
3.1
).

Next, consider the following equations:

Y(t)=\tilde{\psi}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\{j(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)+k(s,t)^{\top}Z(t,s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int^{T}_{t}Z(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(0,T),

(1.3)

and

Y(t)=\tilde{\psi}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\{j(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)+k(s,t)^{\top}Z(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int^{T}_{t}Z(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(0,T),

(1.4)

where
j
and
k
are given deterministic kernels, and
\tilde{\psi}
is a given (not necessarily adapted) stochastic process. Equations (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) are called linear Type-I and Type-II backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs, for short), respectively. The difference between Type-I and Type-II BSVIEs is the role of the time-parameters of
Z(t,s)
and
Z(s,t)
in the drivers. On the one hand, Type-I BSVIE (
1.3
) is an equation for a pair
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
with
Z(t,s)
defined on
0<t<s<T
, and we call it an adapted solution to (
1.3
) if
Y
is adapted,
Z(t,\cdot)=(Z(t,s))_{s\in(t,T)}
is adapted for each
t\in(0,T)
, and the equality (
1.3
) holds for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, a.s. Nonlinear Type-I BSVIEs were introduced by Lin
[
19
]
and Yong
[
37
,
39
]
. Recently, many authors have applied Type-I BSVIEs to stochastic control and mathematical finance where the time-inconsistency is taken into account. For example, time-inconsistent dynamic risk measures and time-inconsistent recursive utilities can be modeled by the solutions to Type-I BSVIEs (see
[
38
,
18
,
1
,
30
]
). Time-inconsistent stochastic control problems related to Type-I BSVIEs were studied by Wang and Yong
[
31
]
and the author
[
12
]
. On the other hand, Type-II BSVIE (
1.4
) is an equation for
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
with
Z(t,s)
defined on
(t,s)\in(0,T)^{2}
, and we call it an adapted M-solution to (
1.4
) if
Y
is adapted,
Z(t,\cdot)=(Z(t,s))_{s\in(0,T)}
is adapted for each
t\in(0,T)
, and the equality (
1.4
), together with the relation
Y(t)=\mathbb{E}[Y(t)]+\int^{t}_{0}Z(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)
hold for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, a.s. Type-II BSVIEs and the concept of adapted M-solutions were first introduced by Yong
[
39
]
and applied to the duality principle appearing in stochastic control problems for (forward) SVIEs. Since then, Type-II BSVIEs have been found important to study stochastic control problems of SVIEs (see
[
6
,
27
,
28
,
35
,
33
,
34
,
29
,
13
,
14
]
). Specifically, Chen and Yong
[
6
]
and Wang
[
33
]
showed that the optimal controls for linear-quadratic control problems of SVIEs are characterized by using the adapted M-solutions of linear Type-II BSVIEs. See also our previous papers
[
13
,
14
]
for the counterparts in the infinite horizon setting.

For linear Type-I BSVIEs, Hu and Øksendal
[
16
]
, Wang, Yong and Zhang
[
32
]
, Ren, Coulibaly and Aman
[
25
]
and the author
[
13
]
obtained explicit formulae of the solutions in different settings. However, due to the difficulty of the appearance of the term
Z(s,t)
in the driver, explicit solutions of linear Type-II BSVIEs have not been obtained in the literature, except for some concrete examples. In the latter half of this paper, we focus on linear Type-II BSVIEs. We provide a variation of constants formula for a general class of linear Type-II BSVIEs and give explicit expressions of the adapted M-solutions. For this purpose, we introduce new notions of the products which we call the backward
\star
-product and the backward
\ast
-product for adapted processes (see
4.3
and
4.8
). Then, regarding a generalized class of linear Type-II BSVIEs as a simple algebraic equation including (iterated) martingale representation operations, we obtain the variation of constants formula (see
4.12
). Furthermore, we provide a duality principle between linear SVIEs and linear Type-II BSVIEs with infinitely many iterated stochastic integrals, which extends the result of Yong
[
39
]
to a more general framework (see
4.15
).

The established variation of constants formulae and the duality principle in this paper are natural extensions of the counterparts of deterministic Volterra integral equations (see
[
11
]
) to the stochastic one. Our results have potential applications to many qualitative theories such as the linear perturbation theory, the invariant manifold theory, and the linear-quadratic optimal control theory for SVIEs. We leave these applications as the future research.

We summarize the contributions of this paper.

•

We introduce novel kinds of products for adapted
L^{2}
-processes and show their fundamental properties.

•

We introduce and study the corresponding resolvents, which play crucial roles for solving SVIEs and BSVIEs.

•

We prove variation of constants formulae for SVIEs and BSVIEs, which provide explicit expressions of the Wiener–Itô chaos expansions of the solutions.

•

We provide useful sufficient conditions for the existence of the resolvent with respect to the
\star
-product.

Preliminaries

2.1
The Wiener–Itô chaos expansion

For each
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
with
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the iterated stochastic integral

\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\xi(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(S,T),

is well-defined as an element of
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
. Furthermore, the following isometry holds:

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\Bigl|\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\xi(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}=\|\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}.

From the Winer–Itô chaos expansion in Itô
[
17
]
, we see that each adapted process
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
has an orthogonal expansion in terms of iterated stochastic integrals with deterministic integrands. We introduce the following notation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

We define operators
\mathfrak{W}_{n}:L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})\to L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, by

\mathfrak{W}_{0}[f_{0}](t):=f_{0}(t),\ t\in(S,T),

for each
f_{0}\in L^{2}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
, and

\mathfrak{W}_{n}[f_{n}](t):=\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}f_{n}(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(S,T),

for each
f_{n}\in L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

We define operators
\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n}:L^{2}(\Delta_{n+2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})\to L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, by

\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{0}[g_{0}](t,s):=g_{0}(t,s),\ (t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(S,T),

for each
g_{0}\in L^{2}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
, and

\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n}[g_{n}](t,s):=\int^{t}_{s}\int^{t_{1}}_{s}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{s}g_{n}(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n},s)\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ (t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(S,T),

for each
g_{n}\in L^{2}(\Delta_{n+2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the present framework, the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion theorem in Itô
[
17
]
can be stated as follows.

Proposition 2.2

(The Wiener–Itô chaos expansion for stochastic processes)
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

The space
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
is decomposed into the infinite orthogonal sum:

L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})=\bigoplus^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})].

More precisely, for any
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
, there exists a unique sequence
\{\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi]\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi]\in L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}[S,T];\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}<\infty

and

\displaystyle\xi=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi]],

where the infinite sum in the right-hand side converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
. Furthermore, the following isometry holds:

\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}.

(ii)

The space
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
is decomposed into the infinite orthogonal sum:

L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})=\bigoplus^{\infty}_{n=0}\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n}[L^{2}(\Delta_{n+2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})].

More precisely, for any
\Xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
, there exists a unique sequence
\{\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[\Xi]\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[\Xi]\in L^{2}(\Delta_{n+2}[S,T];\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[\Xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+2}(S,T))}<\infty

and

\displaystyle\Xi=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n}[\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[\Xi]],

where the infinite sum in the right-hand side converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
. Furthermore, the following isometry holds:

\|\Xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T))}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[\Xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+2}(S,T))}.

We sometimes write

\mathfrak{W}_{n}[\xi]:=\mathfrak{W}_{n}[\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi]],\ \overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n}[\Xi]:=\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n}[\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[\Xi]],

which are the projections of
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
and
\Xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
on the
n
-th chaos for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, respectively.

2.2
Deterministic Volterra kernels

We introduce the spaces of deterministic Volterra kernels and investigate their fundamental properties.

Definition 2.3
.

Define the spaces
\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, of
(n+1)
-parameters deterministic Volterra kernels
by

\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}):=\{k:\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\,|\,\text{$k$ is measurable and}\ |\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}<\infty\},

where
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{1}(S,T)}:=\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|k(t)|_{\mathrm{op}}
for
n=0
, and

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}:=\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}|k(s,t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/2}

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Here and elsewhere, for each matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
|A|_{\mathrm{op}}
denotes the operator norm of
A
as an linear operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. (Recall that
|A|:=\sqrt{\mathrm{tr}(AA^{\top})}
denotes the Frobenius norm of
A
.)

The following lemma can be easily proved by the definition. We omit the proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
(\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)})
is a Banach space. Furthermore, for each
k\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, it holds that
\|k\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}\leq\sqrt{d(T-S)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}
. In particular,
\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\subset L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.

(ii)

Let
k:\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a measurable map with
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume that there exists a function
a\in L^{2}((0,T-S)^{n};\mathbb{R})
such that

|k(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},{\dots},t_{n})|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq a(t_{0}-t_{1},t_{1}-t_{2},{\dots},t_{n-1}-t_{n})

for a.e.
(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
. Then
k
is in
\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, and it holds that
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}\leq\|a\|_{L^{2}((0,T-S)^{n})}
.

Remark 2.5
.

From the above lemma, we see that
\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
includes (
(n+1)
-parameters) singular kernels. For example, the fractional kernel
k(t,s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}
with
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
is in
\mathcal{V}_{2}(S,T;\mathbb{R})
.

We use the following notation frequently in this paper.

Definition 2.6
.

For each
f:\Delta_{m+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
and
g:\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times d_{3}}
with
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
\triangleright
-product

f\triangleright g:\Delta_{m+n+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}}
by

(f\triangleright g)(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{m+n}):=f(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{m})g(t_{m},t_{m+1},{\dots},t_{m+n})

for
(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{m+n})\in\Delta_{m+n+1}(S,T)
. When
m=0
,
f\triangleright g:\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}}
is defined by

(f\triangleright g)(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n}):=f(t_{0})g(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})

for
(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
. When
n=0
,
f\triangleright g:\Delta_{m+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}}
is defined by

(f\triangleright g)(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{m}):=f(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{m})g(t_{m})

for
(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{m})\in\Delta_{m+1}(S,T)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

(i)

For each
k_{1}\in\mathcal{V}_{m+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
k_{2}\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
with
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the
\triangleright
-product
k_{1}\triangleright k_{2}
is in
\mathcal{V}_{m+n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and satisfies

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{1}\triangleright k_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{m+n+1}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{m+1}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}.

(ii)

For each
k\in\mathcal{V}_{m+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
f\in L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, the
\triangleright
-product
k\triangleright f
is in
L^{2}(\Delta_{m+n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
and satisfies

\|k\triangleright f\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{m+n+1}(S,T))}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{m+1}(S,T)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}.

Proof.

(i)

Noting the inequality
|AB|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq|A|_{\mathrm{op}}|B|_{\mathrm{op}}
for
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and using the Fubini theorem, we see that, for a.e.
t\in(S,T)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{m+n}(t,T)}|(k_{1}\triangleright k_{2})(s,t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}\int_{\Delta_{m}(\max s_{2},T)}|k_{1}(s_{1},\max s_{2})k_{2}(s_{2},t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}\int_{\Delta_{m}(\max s_{2},T)}|k_{1}(s_{1},\max s_{2})|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}|k_{2}(s_{2},t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}\Bigl\{\int_{\Delta_{m}(\max s_{2},T)}|k_{1}(s_{1},\max s_{2})|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Bigr\}|k_{2}(s_{2},t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{m+1}(S,T)}\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}|k_{2}(s_{2},t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{m+1}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}<\infty.

This implies that
k_{1}\triangleright k_{2}\in\mathcal{V}_{m+n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{1}\triangleright k_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{m+n+1}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{m+1}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}
.

(ii)

Noting the inequality
|AB|\leq|A|_{\mathrm{op}}|B|
for
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}}
, we can prove the assertion (ii) by the same way as in (i).

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
k_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Suppose that
\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}<\infty
. Then
\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|k_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}<\infty
. Consequently, the infinite sum of the iterated stochastic integrals
\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[k_{n}]
converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.

Proof.

By
2.4
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{n}
is in
L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, and it holds that
\|k_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}\leq\sqrt{d(T-S)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}
. Furthermore, by the assumption, we have
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}<\infty
. Therefore, we have

\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|k_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}\leq d(T-S)\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}\leq d(T-S)\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|k_{n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}<\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

3
Stochastic Volterra integral equations

In this section, we investigate linear SVIE (
1.1
). More generally, we consider SVIEs with infinitely many iterated stochastic integrals of the following form:

X(t)=\varphi(t)+\int^{t}_{S}J(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}k_{n}(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})X(t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(S,T),

(3.1)

with suitable choices of kernels
J\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
k_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In this paper, we call the above equation a
generalized SVIE
. This class of SVIEs includes fractional SDEs with “noisy memory”, as demonstrated in the following example.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the following fractional equation of order
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
:

\begin{cases}{}^{\mathrm{C}}\!D^{\alpha}_{0+}X(t)=j(t)X(t)+X_{1}(t)+X_{2}(t)+b(t)+\{k(t)X(t)+X_{1}(t)+X_{2}(t)+\sigma(t)\}\frac{\mathrm{d}W(t)}{\mathrm{d}t},\ t\in(0,T),\\
\displaystyle X_{1}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\ell_{1}(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ X_{2}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\ell_{2}(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(0,T),\\
\displaystyle X(0)=x_{0},\end{cases}

(3.2)

where
j(t),k(t),\ell_{1}(t,s),\ell_{2}(t,s)
are
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
-valued, deterministic and bounded coefficients,
b,\sigma\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This is a fractional SDE which has a delay
X_{1}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\ell_{1}(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
and a “noisy memory”
X_{2}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}\ell_{2}(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)
. The term noisy memory was first introduced by Dahl et al
[
7
]
in the classical SDEs framework. By the definition, an adapted process
X
is said to be a solution to (
3.2
) if it satisfies the integral equation

\displaystyle X(t)=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\Bigl\{j(s)X(s)+\int^{s}_{0}\ell_{1}(s,r)X(r)\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{s}_{0}\ell_{2}(s,r)X(r)\,\mathrm{d}W(r)+b(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\Bigl\{k(s)X(s)+\int^{s}_{0}\ell_{1}(s,r)X(r)\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{s}_{0}\ell_{2}(s,r)X(r)\,\mathrm{d}W(r)+\sigma(s)\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}W(s)

for
t\in(0,T)
. Applying the stochastic Fubini theorem, we see that the above integral equation becomes

\displaystyle X(t)=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}\Bigl\{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}j(s)+\int^{t}_{s}(t-r)^{\alpha-1}\ell_{1}(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{t}_{s}(t-r)^{\alpha-1}\ell_{1}(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(r)\Bigr\}X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}\Bigl\{(t-t_{1})^{\alpha-1}k(t_{1})+\int^{t}_{t_{1}}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\ell_{2}(s,t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}X(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}(t-t_{1})^{\alpha-1}\ell_{2}(t_{1},t_{2})X(t_{2})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{2})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(0,T).

Thus, the fractional SDE with noisy memory (
3.2
) can be seen as a generalized SVIE (
3.1
) with the free term

\varphi(t)=x_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}b(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int^{t}_{0}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)

and the kernels

\displaystyle J(t,s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\Bigl\{(t-s)^{\alpha-1}j(s)+\int^{t}_{s}(t-r)^{\alpha-1}\ell_{1}(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r+\int^{t}_{s}(t-r)^{\alpha-1}\ell_{1}(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(r)\Bigr\},

\displaystyle k_{1}(t,t_{1})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\Bigl\{(t-t_{1})^{\alpha-1}k(t_{1})+\int^{t}_{t_{1}}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\ell_{2}(s,t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\},

\displaystyle k_{2}(t,t_{1},t_{2})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(t-t_{1})^{\alpha-1}\ell_{2}(t_{1},t_{2}),\ k_{n}=0,\ n\geq 3.

Note that since
J(t,s)
is
\mathcal{F}^{s}_{t}
-measurable,
J
is in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.

In order to investigate generalized SVIE (
3.1
), we write it as an algebraic equation on
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as follows:

X=\varphi+J\ast X+K\star X,

(3.3)

where
J\ast X
corresponds to the convolution in terms of the Lebesgue integral, and
K\star X
with
K=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[k_{n}]
corresponds to the infinite sum of the stochastic convolutions with respect to the iterated stochastic integrals.

3.1
\star
-product

In this subsection, we introduce a suitable class of
K
and define the
\star
-product
K\star X
appearing in equation (
3.3
).

Definition 3.2
.

We define the space
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
of
\star
-Volterra kernels
by

\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}):=\{K\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\,|\,\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ |\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<\infty\},

where

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}:=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}.

Remark 3.3
.

We emphasize that each
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is an
\mathbb{F}^{S}
-adapted and square-integrable stochastic process (with one time-parameter), see
2.8
. Intuitively speaking, the Volterra structure corresponds to the adaptedness of the process
K
. Since each
(\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\cdot|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)})
is a Banach space, we see that
(\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\cdot|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)})
is a Banach space.

Definition 3.4
.

For each
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
\star
-product

K\star\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
by the following Wiener–Itô chaos expansion:

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\star\xi]:=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi],\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

The following lemma shows that the
\star
-product is well-defined and makes the space
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
a Banach algebra.

Proposition 3.5
.

(\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\cdot|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)},\star)
is a (real) unitary Banach algebra with the unit
I_{d}
, where
I_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
denotes the identity matrix which can be viewed as an element of
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Furthermore,
(L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)})
is a left Banach module over
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
with respect to the
\star
-product. In other words,
(\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\cdot|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)})
is a Banach space, and for each
K,K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the following hold:

\displaystyle K_{1}\star K_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\ \text{and}\ |\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}\star K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)},

\displaystyle(K_{1}\star K_{2})\star K_{3}=K_{1}\star(K_{2}\star K_{3}),

\displaystyle K_{1}\star(K_{2}+K_{3})=K_{1}\star K_{2}+K_{1}\star K_{3},

\displaystyle(K_{1}+K_{2})\star K_{3}=K_{1}\star K_{3}+K_{2}\star K_{3},

\displaystyle\alpha(K_{1}\star K_{2})=(\alpha K_{1})\star K_{2}=K_{1}\star(\alpha K_{2}),

\displaystyle K\star\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{and}\ \|K\star\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)},

\displaystyle(K_{1}\star K_{2})\star\xi=K_{1}\star(K_{2}\star\xi),

\displaystyle K\star(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})=K\star\xi_{1}+K\star\xi_{2},

\displaystyle(K_{1}+K_{2})\star\xi=K_{1}\star\xi+K_{2}\star\xi,

\displaystyle\alpha(K\star\xi)=(\alpha K)\star\xi=K\star(\alpha\xi),

\displaystyle I_{d}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}),\ |\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|I_{d}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}=1,\ K\star I_{d}=I_{d}\star K=K,\ \text{and}\ I_{d}\star\xi=\xi.

Proof.

Let
K,K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. By the triangle inequality,
2.7
and Young’s convolution inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\Big|\hskip-0.56905pt\Big|\hskip-0.56905pt\Big|\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{1}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{2}]\Big|\hskip-0.56905pt\Big|\hskip-0.56905pt\Big|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{n}_{k=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{1}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{2}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{n}_{k=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{1}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n-k+1}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{2}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k+1}(S,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{2}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}

\displaystyle=|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<\infty.

Therefore,
K_{1}\star K_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is well-defined and satisfies

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}\star K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\Bigl\|\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]\Bigr\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\Bigl(\sum^{n}_{k=0}\|\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\Bigl(\sum^{n}_{k=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n-k+1}(S,T)}\|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{k+1}(S,T))}\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}\Bigr)^{2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle=|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<\infty.

Therefore,
K\star\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is well-defined and satisfies

\|K\star\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

Furthermore, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[(K_{1}\star K_{2})\star K_{3}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{1}\star K_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{3}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\Bigl(\sum^{n-k}_{\ell=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k-\ell}[K_{1}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[K_{2}]\Bigr)\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{3}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\sum^{n}_{\ell=k}\mathfrak{F}_{n-\ell}[K_{1}]\triangleright(\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-k}[K_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{3}])

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{\ell=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-\ell}[K_{1}]\triangleright\Bigl(\sum^{\ell}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-k}[K_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{3}]\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{\ell=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-\ell}[K_{1}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[K_{2}\star K_{3}]

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}\star(K_{2}\star K_{3})],

and hence
(K_{1}\star K_{2})\star K_{3}=K_{1}\star(K_{2}\star K_{3})
. Similarly, we can show that
(K_{1}\star K_{2})\star\xi=K_{1}\star(K_{2}\star\xi)
. The remaining assertions are clear.
∎

Although the
\star
-product is defined by means of the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion, it can be represented in terms of iterated stochastic integrals.

Proposition 3.6
.

For each
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

(K\star\xi)(t)=\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)\xi(t)+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\xi(t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(S,T),

(3.4)

where the infinite sum in the right-hand side converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
.

Proof.

We define
\Phi^{K}_{0}[\xi](t):=\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)\xi(t)
,
t\in(S,T)
, and

\Phi^{K}_{n}[\xi](t):=\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\xi(t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(S,T),

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that the integrand in the above iterated stochastic integral is in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
, and thus
\Phi^{K}_{n}[\xi]\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
is well-defined. By using the isometry of the stochastic integral, similar calculations as in
2.7
show that
\|\Phi^{K}_{n}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Thus, each
\Phi^{K}_{n}
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with the operator norm
\|\Phi^{K}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}
. Since
\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}=|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<\infty
, the infinite sum in the right-hand side of (
3.4
) converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
. Noting the continuity of the linear operator
\Phi^{K}_{n}
and considering the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion of
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
, we have

\Phi^{K}_{n}[\xi]=\Phi^{K}_{n}\Bigl[\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\mathfrak{W}_{m}[\xi]\Bigr]=\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\Phi^{K}_{n}[\mathfrak{W}_{m}[\xi]],\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
\mathfrak{W}_{m}[\xi]:=\mathfrak{W}_{m}[\mathfrak{F}_{m}[\xi]]
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Observe that, for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{K}_{n}[\mathfrak{W}_{m}[\xi]](t)

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\Bigl(\int^{t_{n}}_{S}\int^{t_{n+1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n+m-1}}_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{m}[\xi](t_{n},t_{n+1},{\dots},t_{n+m})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n+m}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n+1})\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n+m-1}}_{S}(\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{m}[\xi])(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n+m})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n+m}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{W}_{n+m}[\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{m}[\xi]](t),\ t\in(S,T),

and hence
\Phi^{K}_{n}[\mathfrak{W}_{m}[\xi]]=\mathfrak{W}_{n+m}[\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{m}[\xi]]
for each
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly, these relations hold when
n=0
or
m=0
. Therefore, the right-hand side of (
3.4
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\Phi^{K}_{n}[\mathfrak{W}_{m}[\xi]]

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\mathfrak{W}_{n+m}[\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{m}[\xi]]

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]]

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathfrak{W}_{n}\Bigl[\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\star\xi]]

\displaystyle=K\star\xi.

This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

For each
j\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{n+2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
,
f\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times d_{3}})
with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\mathbb{N}

\ast

j\ast f:\Omega\times\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}}
by

(j\ast f)(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n}):=\int^{t_{n}}_{S}j(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n},s)f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

for
(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)

g\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{m+2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times d_{3}})
with

\ast
-product
j\ast g:\Omega\times\Delta_{n+m+2}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}}
by

(j\ast g)(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n+m+1}):=\int^{t_{n}}_{t_{n+1}}j(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n},s)g(s,t_{n+1},{\dots},t_{n+m+1})\,\mathrm{d}s

for
(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n+m+1})\in\Delta_{n+m+2}(S,T)
.

Recall that
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
is the space of
\Xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
with
\Xi(t,s)
being
\mathcal{F}^{s}_{t}
-measurable for each
(t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(S,T)
. Also, recall the definitions of
\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}
(see
2.1
).

Lemma 3.8
.

For each
\Xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times d_{3}})
with
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
, the
\ast
-product
\Xi\ast\xi
is in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}})
, and the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion satisfies

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\Xi\ast\xi]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n-k}[\Xi]\ast\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi],\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Furthermore, for each
\Xi_{1}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
and
\Xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}\times d_{3}})
, the
\ast
-product
\Xi_{1}\ast\Xi_{2}
is in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F},\ast}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}})
, and the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion satisfies

\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[\Xi_{1}\ast\Xi_{2}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n-k}[\Xi_{1}]\ast\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}[\Xi_{2}],\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Proof.

We prove the first assertion. The second can be proved by the same way. Note that
\Xi(t,s)
is
\mathcal{F}^{s}_{t}
-measurable, and hence it is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
. By using Minkowski’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\Bigl(\int^{t}_{S}|\Xi(t,s)||\xi(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{T}_{S}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{s}|\Xi(t,s)|^{2}|\xi(s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{S}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{s}|\Xi(t,s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,|\xi(s)|^{2}\Bigr]^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int^{T}_{S}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{s}|\Xi(t,s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\xi(s)|^{2}\bigr]^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\int^{T}_{S}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{s}|\Xi(t,s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigl(\int^{T}_{S}\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\xi(s)|^{2}\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle<\infty.

Thus, the
\ast
-product
\Xi\ast\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}})
is well-defined, and the operations
\Xi\mapsto\Xi\ast\xi
and
\xi\mapsto\Xi\ast\xi
are continuous. Noting the adaptedness, we have
\Xi\ast\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{3}})
. Furthermore, by using the stochastic Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle(\Xi\ast\xi)(t)=\Bigl\{\Bigl(\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n}[\Xi]\Bigr)\ast\Bigl(\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}\mathfrak{W}_{m}[\xi]\Bigr)\Bigr\}(t)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{n}_{k=0}(\overline{\mathfrak{W}}_{n-k}[\Xi]\ast\mathfrak{W}_{k}[\xi])(t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{n}_{k=0}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t}_{s}\int^{t_{1}}_{s}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-k-1}}_{s}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n-k}[\Xi](t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n-k},s)\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n-k}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\int^{s}_{S}\int^{t_{n-k+1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t_{n-k+1},{\dots},t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n-k+1})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{n}_{k=0}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t}_{s}\int^{t_{1}}_{s}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-k-1}}_{s}\int^{s}_{S}\int^{t_{n-k+1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n-k}[\Xi](t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n-k},s)\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t_{n-k+1},{\dots},t_{n})

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n-k+1})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n-k}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\sum^{n}_{k=0}\int^{t_{n-k}}_{t_{n-k+1}}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n-k}[\Xi](t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n-k},s)\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t_{n-k+1},{\dots},t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\sum^{n}_{k=0}(\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n-k}[\Xi]\ast\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi])(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),

which implies that
\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\Xi\ast\xi]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n-k}[\Xi]\ast\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. This completes the proof.
∎

- and the
\ast
-resolvents are unique if they exist. More precisely, the following proposition holds.

Proposition 3.14
.

(i)

Let
\star
-Volterra kernels
K,R_{1},R_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be given. Suppose that
R_{1}=K+K\star R_{1}
and
R_{2}=K+R_{2}\star K
. Then
R_{1}=R_{2}
. In particular, each
\star
-Volterra kernel has at most one
\star
-resolvent.

(ii)

Let
\ast
-Volterra kernels
J,Q_{1},Q_{2}\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be given. Suppose that
Q_{1}=J+J\ast Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}=J+Q_{2}\ast J
. Then
Q_{1}=Q_{2}
. In particular, each
\ast
-Volterra kernel has at most one
\ast
-resolvent.

The above proposition holds for any algebra (see Lemma 3.3 in
[
11
]
). Here, we give a proof for readers’ convenience.

Proof of
3.14
.

We prove (i). The assertion (ii) is proved by the same way. By the assumption, we have
R_{2}\star(R_{1}-K)=R_{2}\star K\star R_{1}=(R_{2}-K)\star R_{1}
. Cancelling out
R_{2}\star R_{1}
, one gets
R_{2}\star K=K\star R_{1}
. Thus,
R_{2}=K+R_{2}\star K=K+K\star R_{1}=R_{1}
.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

Let
J\in L^{2}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be a deterministic
\ast
-Volterra kernel, and suppose that
Q\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a
\ast
-resolvent of
J
. Then we see that
\mathbb{E}[Q]
is also a
\ast
-resolvent of
J
. By the uniqueness of the
\ast
-resolvent, we have
Q=\mathbb{E}[Q]
, and thus
Q
is deterministic.

Actually, the
\ast
-resolvent always exists.

Proposition 3.16
.

Every
\ast
-Volterra kernel
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
has a unique
\ast
-resolvent
Q\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.

Proof.

The uniqueness follows from
3.14
. We show the existence. First, we assume that
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|J|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<1
. By
3.10
, we have
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|J^{\ast n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|J|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}^{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
J^{\ast n}\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
denotes the
(n-1)
-fold
\ast
-product of
J
by itself. By the assumption,
Q:=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}J^{\ast n}
converges in
\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Observe that

J+J\ast Q=J+J\ast\Bigl(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}J^{\ast n}\Bigr)=J+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}J\ast J^{\ast n}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}J^{\ast n}=Q.

Similarly, we have
J+Q\ast J=Q
. Thus,
Q
is the
\ast
-resolvent of
J
.

Now we consider the general case. For each
\sigma>0
, define
J_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
by
J_{\sigma}(t,s):=e^{-\sigma(t-s)}J(t,s)
for
(t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(S,T)
. It is easy to see that

\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[J_{\sigma}](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n+1})=e^{-\sigma(t_{0}-t_{n+1})}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{n}[J](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n+1})

for
(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n+1})\in\Delta_{n+2}(S,T)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Noting the definition of
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\cdot|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}
(see
3.9
), by the dominated convergence theorem, we see that
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|J_{\sigma}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}
tends to zero as
\sigma\to\infty
. Let
\sigma>0
be such that
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|J_{\sigma}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<1
. Then there exists a unique
\ast
-resolvent
Q_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
of
J_{\sigma}
. For
(t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(S,T)
, we have

\displaystyle(J_{\sigma}\ast Q_{\sigma})(t,s)

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{s}\!J_{\sigma}(t,r)Q_{\sigma}(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r=\int^{t}_{s}\!e^{-\sigma(t-r)}J(t,r)Q_{\sigma}(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=e^{-\sigma(t-s)}\int^{t}_{s}\!J(t,r)e^{\sigma(r-s)}Q_{\sigma}(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r

and

\displaystyle(Q_{\sigma}\ast J_{\sigma})(t,s)

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{s}\!Q_{\sigma}(t,r)J_{\sigma}(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r=\int^{t}_{s}\!Q_{\sigma}(t,r)e^{-\sigma(r-s)}J(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=e^{-\sigma(t-s)}\int^{t}_{s}\!e^{\sigma(t-r)}Q_{\sigma}(t,r)J(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Therefore, the
\ast
-Volterra kernel
Q\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
defined by
Q(t,s):=e^{\sigma(t-s)}Q_{\sigma}(t,s)
for
(t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(S,T)
satisfies

Q(t,s)=J(t,s)+\int^{t}_{s}J(t,r)Q(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r=J(t,s)+\int^{t}_{s}Q(t,r)J(r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r

for
(t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(S,T)
, and thus it is the
\ast
-resolvent of
J
. This completes the proof.
∎

The case of the
\star
-resolvent is more difficult. We provide useful sufficient conditions for the existence of the
\star
-resolvent in
Section

5
.

The next lemma provides an explicit expression of the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion of the
\star
-resolvent.

Lemma 3.17
.

Suppose that a
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
satisfies
\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K]=0
(which is equivalent to
\mathbb{E}[K]=0
). If
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a
\star
-resolvent of
K
, then the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion is given by
\mathfrak{F}_{0}[R]=0
and

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{i}\leq n\\
m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{i}=n\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{F}_{m_{1}}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{m_{2}}[K]\triangleright\cdots\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{m_{i}}[K],\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

By the definition, the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion of
R
satisfies the following equations:

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]+\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R],\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Since
\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K]=0
, we have
\mathfrak{F}_{0}[R]=0
,
\mathfrak{F}_{1}[R]=\mathfrak{F}_{1}[K]
, and
\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]+\sum^{n-1}_{k=1}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R]
for
n\geq 2
. By the induction, we get the assertion.
∎

Next, we introduce the notion of the
(\ast,\star
)-resolvent.

Definition 3.18
.

For each
\ast
-Volterra kernel
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, we say that a pair
(Q,R)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
is a
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent
of
(J,K)
if it satisfies the following resolvent equations:

\begin{cases}Q=J+J\ast Q+K\star Q=J+Q\ast J+R\star J,\\
R=K+J\ast R+K\star R=K+Q\ast K+R\star K.\end{cases}

The following is concerned with the uniqueness of the
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
(J,K)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be fixed. Suppose that
(Q_{1},R_{1}),(Q_{2},R_{2})\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
satisfy

\begin{cases}Q_{1}=J+J\ast Q_{1}+K\star Q_{1},\\
R_{1}=K+J\ast R_{1}+K\star R_{1},\end{cases}\text{and}\ \begin{cases}Q_{2}=J+Q_{2}\ast J+R_{2}\star J,\\
R_{2}=K+Q_{2}\ast K+R_{2}\star K.\end{cases}

Then
Q_{1}=Q_{2}
and
R_{1}=R_{2}
. In particular, the pair
(J,K)
has at most one
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent.

Proof.

By
3.10
and
3.11
, we have

Q_{2}\ast(Q_{1}-J)=Q_{2}\ast(J\ast Q_{1}+K\star Q_{1})=Q_{2}\ast J\ast Q_{1}+Q_{2}\ast K\star Q_{1}=(Q_{2}-J-R_{2}\star J)\ast Q_{1}+Q_{2}\ast K\star Q_{1}.

Cancelling out
Q_{2}\ast Q_{1}
, we get

Q_{2}\ast J+Q_{2}\ast K\star Q_{1}=J\ast Q_{1}+R_{2}\star J\ast Q_{1}.

(3.10)

Similarly,

Q_{2}\ast(R_{1}-K)=Q_{2}\ast(J\ast R_{1}+K\star R_{1})=Q_{2}\ast J\ast R_{1}+Q_{2}\ast K\star R_{1}=(Q_{2}-J-R_{2}\star J)\ast R_{1}+Q_{2}\ast K\star R_{1}.

Cancelling out
Q_{2}\ast R_{1}
, we get

Q_{2}\ast K+Q_{2}\ast K\star R_{1}=J\ast R_{1}+R_{2}\star J\ast R_{1}.

(3.11)

Also,

R_{2}\star(Q_{1}-J)=R_{2}\star(J\ast Q_{1}+K\star Q_{1})=R_{2}\star J\ast Q_{1}+R_{2}\star K\star Q_{1}=R_{2}\star J\ast Q_{1}+(R_{2}-K-Q_{2}\ast K)\star Q_{1}.

Cancelling out
R_{2}\star Q_{1}
, we get

R_{2}\star J+R_{2}\star J\ast Q_{1}=K\star Q_{1}+Q_{2}\ast K\star Q_{1}.

(3.12)

Lastly,

R_{2}\star(R_{1}-K)=R_{2}\star(J\ast R_{1}+K\star R_{1})=R_{2}\star J\ast R_{1}+R_{2}\star K\star R_{1}=R_{2}\star J\ast R_{1}+(R_{2}-K-Q_{2}\ast K)\star R_{1}.

Cancelling out
R_{2}\star R_{1}
, we get

R_{2}\star K+R_{2}\star J\ast R_{1}=K\star R_{1}+Q_{2}\ast K\star R_{1}.

(3.13)

By (
3.10
) and (
3.12
), we see that

Q_{2}\ast J+R_{2}\star J=J\ast Q_{1}+K\star Q_{1}.

Thus, we obtain

Q_{2}=J+Q_{2}\ast J+R_{2}\star J=J+J\ast Q_{1}+K\star Q_{1}=Q_{1}.

Furthermore, by (
3.11
) and (
3.13
), we see that

Q_{2}\ast K+R_{2}\star K=J\ast R_{1}+K\star R_{1}.

Thus, we obtain

R_{2}=K+Q_{2}\ast K+R_{2}\star K=K+J\ast R_{1}+K\star R_{1}=R_{1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We note that the
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent is defined as a pair of kernels. The next proposition shows that the
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent is constructed by the
\ast
-resolvent and the
\star
-resolvent.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let a
\ast
-Volterra kernel
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and a
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be given.

(i)

Let
Q_{1}\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be the
\ast
-resolvent of
J
, and suppose that
K+Q_{1}\ast K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
has a
\star
-resolvent
R_{1}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Then
(Q,R)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
defined by
Q:=Q_{1}+R_{1}\star Q_{1}
and
R:=R_{1}
is the
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent of
(J,K)
.

(ii)

Suppose that
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
has a
\star
-resolvent
R_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, and let
Q_{2}\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be the
\ast
-resolvent of
J+R_{2}\star J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Then
(Q,R)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
defined by
Q:=Q_{2}
and
R:=R_{2}+Q_{2}\ast R_{2}
is the
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent of
(J,K)
.

Proof.

(i)

Note that
Q_{1}=J+J\ast Q_{1}
and
R_{1}=K+Q_{1}\ast K+(K+Q_{1}\ast K)\star R_{1}
. By
3.5
,
3.10
and
3.11
, we have

\displaystyle J\ast R=J\ast R_{1}

\displaystyle=J\ast K+J\ast Q_{1}\ast K+J\ast K\star R_{1}+J\ast Q_{1}\ast K\star R_{1}

\displaystyle=(J+J\ast Q_{1})\ast K+(J+J\ast Q_{1})\ast(K\star R_{1})

\displaystyle=Q_{1}\ast K+Q_{1}\ast K\star R_{1},

and hence

\displaystyle K+J\ast R+K\star R

\displaystyle=K+Q_{1}\ast K+Q_{1}\ast K\star R_{1}+K\star R_{1}

\displaystyle=K+Q_{1}\ast K+(K+Q_{1}\ast K)\star R_{1}

\displaystyle=R_{1}=R.

Consequently, we get

R=K+J\ast R+K\star R.

(3.14)

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle J+J\ast Q+K\star Q

\displaystyle=J+J\ast(Q_{1}+R_{1}\star Q_{1})+K\star(Q_{1}+R_{1}\star Q_{1})

\displaystyle=J+J\ast Q_{1}+(K+J\ast R_{1}+K\star R_{1})\star Q_{1}

\displaystyle=Q_{1}+R_{1}\star Q_{1}=Q,

and thus

Q=J+J\ast Q+K\star Q.

(3.15)

Similarly, from the equalities
Q_{1}=J+Q_{1}\ast J
and
R_{1}=K+Q_{1}\ast K+R_{1}\star(K+Q_{1}\ast K)
, we have

\displaystyle K+Q\ast K+R\star K

\displaystyle=K+(Q_{1}+R_{1}\star Q_{1})\ast K+R_{1}\star K

\displaystyle=K+Q_{1}\ast K+R_{1}\star(K+Q_{1}\ast K)

\displaystyle=R_{1}=R,

and thus

R=K+Q\ast K+R\star K.

(3.16)

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle J+Q\ast J+R\star J

\displaystyle=J+(Q_{1}+R_{1}\star Q_{1})\ast J+R_{1}\star J

\displaystyle=J+Q_{1}\ast J+R_{1}\star(J+Q_{1}\ast J)

\displaystyle=Q_{1}+R_{1}\star Q_{1}=Q,

and thus

Q=J+Q\ast J+R\star J.

(3.17)

By (
3.14
), (
3.15
), (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), we see that
(Q,R)
is the
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent of
(J,K)
.

(ii)

The claim (ii) can be proved by the same way as (i) by inverting the roles of
J
and
K
,
Q
and
R
, and the
\ast
-product and the
\star
-product.

∎

Remark 3.21
.

From
3.20
(ii) and
3.16
, if
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
has a

-resolvent, then
(J,K)
has a

3.4
A variation of constants formula for generalized SVIEs

Now we are ready to show the variation of constants formula for generalized SVIEs.

Theorem 3.22
.

Let a
\ast
-Volterra kernel
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and a
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be fixed, and suppose that
(J,K)
has a
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent
(Q,R)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Then for any free term
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the generalized SVIE

X=\varphi+J\ast X+K\star X

has a unique solution
X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This solution is given by the variation of constants formula:

X=\varphi+Q\ast\varphi+R\star\varphi.

Proof.

Define
X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
X=\varphi+Q\ast\varphi+R\star\varphi
. By
3.5
,
3.10
and
3.11
, we have

J\ast X=J\ast(\varphi+Q\ast\varphi+R\star\varphi)=(J+J\ast Q)\ast\varphi+(J\ast R)\star\varphi

and

K\star X=K\star(\varphi+Q\ast\varphi+R\star\varphi)=(K\star Q)\ast\varphi+(K+K\star R)\star\varphi.

Combining the above two equalities and using the resolvent equations, we get

J\ast X+K\star X=(J+J\ast Q+K\star Q)\ast\varphi+(K+J\ast R+K\star R)\star\varphi=Q\ast\varphi+R\star\varphi.

This implies that
X=\varphi+J\ast X+K\star X
.

Conversely, if
X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies
X=\varphi+J\ast X+K\star X
, then we have

Q\ast\varphi=Q\ast(X-J\ast X-K\star X)=(Q-Q\ast J)\ast X-(Q\ast K)\star X

and

R\star\varphi=R\star(X-J\ast X-K\star X)=-(R\star J)\ast X+(R-R\star K)\star X.

Combining the above two equalities and using the resolvent equations, we get

Q\ast\varphi+R\star\varphi=(Q-Q\ast J-R\star J)\ast X+(R-Q\ast K-R\star K)\star X=J\ast X+K\star X.

This implies that
X=\varphi+Q\ast\varphi+R\star\varphi
. This completes the proof.
∎

Noting the stochastic integral representation of the
\star
-product (see
3.6
), we immediately get the following corollary.

Corollary 3.23
.

Let
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
K=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[k_{n}]\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
with
k_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be fixed, and suppose that
(J,K)
has a
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent
(Q,R)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Then for any free term
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the generalized SVIE

X(t)=\varphi(t)+\int^{t}_{S}J(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}k_{n}(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})X(t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(S,T),

has a unique solution
X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This solution is given by the variation of constants formula:

X(t)=\varphi(t)+\int^{t}_{S}Q(t,s)\varphi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}r_{n}(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\varphi(t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(S,T),

where
r_{n}:=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Here, the infinite sum in the right-hand side converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Corollary 3.24
.

Let deterministic kernels
j\in L^{2}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
k\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be fixed. Let
q\in L^{2}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be the
\ast
-resolvent of
j
, and assume that
\mathfrak{W}_{1}[k+q\ast k]
has a
\star
-resolvent. Then for any free term
\varphi\in\ L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the SVIE

X(t)=\varphi(t)+\int^{t}_{S}j(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{S}k(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(S,T),

has a unique solution
X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This solution is given by the variation of constants formula:

\displaystyle X(t)

\displaystyle=\varphi(t)+\int^{t}_{S}q(t,s)\varphi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}(k+q\ast k)^{\triangleright n}(t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\Bigl\{\varphi(t_{n})+\int^{t_{n}}_{S}q(t_{n},s)\varphi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928ptt\in(S,T).

Here,
(k+q\ast k)^{\triangleright n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
denotes the
(n-1)
-fold
\triangleright
-product of

by itself, and the infinite sum in the right-hand side converges in

.

Proof.

Note that
\mathfrak{W}_{1}[k]+q\ast\mathfrak{W}_{1}[k]=\mathfrak{W}_{1}[k+q\ast k]
. Thus, by
3.17
, the
\star
-resolvent of

3.20

Example 3.25
.

Consider the following one-dimensional linear fractional SDE of order
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
:

\begin{cases}{}^{\mathrm{C}}\!D^{\alpha}_{0+}X(t)=\mu X(t)+\sigma X(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}W(t)}{\mathrm{d}t},\ t\in(0,T),\\
X(0)=x_{0},\end{cases}

(3.18)

where
\mu,\sigma,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
are constants. When

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X(t)=\mu X(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sigma X(t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t),\ t\in(0,T),\\

and the solution is the geometric Brownian motion
X(t)=x_{0}\exp((\mu-\sigma^{2}/2)t+\sigma W(t))
. Thus, (
3.18
) can be seen as a time-fractional Black–Scholes SDE. Note that (
3.18
) is equivalent to the following SVIE:

X(t)=x_{0}+\int^{t}_{0}j(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int^{t}_{0}k(t,s)X(s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(0,T),

where
j(t,s)=\frac{\mu}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}
and
k(t,s)=\frac{\sigma}{\Gamma(\alpha)}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}
. Here we use some fundamental calculus for the fractional kernel and the Mittag–Leffler function (see Section 1.2 in the textbook
[
23
]
). The
\ast
-resolvent
q\in L^{2}(\Delta_{2}(0,T);\mathbb{R})
of

is given by

, where

E_{\beta,\gamma}(z):=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{z^{k}}{\Gamma(\beta k+\gamma)}
is the Mittag–Leffler function. Furthermore, it holds that
(k+q\ast k)(t,s)=\sigma f(t-s)
for
(t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(0,T)
, and thus the
\star
-resolvent
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
of

R(t)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\sigma^{n}\int^{t}_{0}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{0}f(t-t_{1})f(t_{1}-t_{2}){\cdots}f(t_{n-1}-t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1}),\ t\in(0,T).

Here, the existence of the
\star
-resolvent

follows from the results in
Section

5

1+\int^{t}_{0}q(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s=1+\mu\int^{t}_{0}f(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}s=E_{\alpha,1}(\mu t^{\alpha})=:E_{\alpha}(\mu t^{\alpha})
. Consequently, by
3.24
, the solution
X\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R})
to the fractional Black–Scholes SDE (

) is given by

X(t)=x_{0}\Bigl\{E_{\alpha}(\mu t^{\alpha})+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\sigma^{n}\int^{t}_{0}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{0}f(t-t_{1})f(t_{1}-t_{2}){\cdots}f(t_{n-1}-t_{n})E_{\alpha}(\mu t^{\alpha}_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})\Bigr\}

X(t)=x_{0}\Bigl\{1+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\Bigl(\frac{\sigma}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\Bigr)^{n}\int^{t}_{0}\int^{t_{1}}_{0}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{0}(t-t_{1})^{\alpha-1}(t_{1}-t_{2})^{\alpha-1}{\cdots}(t_{n-1}-t_{n})^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})\Bigr\}

for

.

4
Backward stochastic Volterra integral equations

In this section, we investigate linear Type-II BSVIEs. First of all, consider the following Type-II BSVIE:

Y(t)=\tilde{\psi}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}\{j(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)+k(s,t)^{\top}Z(s,t)\}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int^{T}_{t}Z(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s),\ t\in(S,T),

(4.1)

where
\tilde{\psi}(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a given free term such that
\tilde{\psi}(t)
is
\mathcal{F}^{S}_{T}
-measurable for each
t\in(S,T)
, and
j\in L^{2}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
k\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
are given deterministic kernels. We say that a pair
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
is an adapted M-solution of BSVIE (
4.1
) if
Y\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
Z:\Omega\times(S,T)^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
are measurable,
Z(t,\cdot)
is
\mathbb{F}^{S}
-adapted for a.e.
t\in(S,T)
,
\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{S}\int^{T}_{S}|Z(t,s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}s]<\infty
, and the equation, together with the relation
Y(t)=\mathbb{E}_{S}[Y(t)]+\int^{t}_{S}Z(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)
hold for a.e.
t\in(S,T)
, a.s. (see
[
39
]
). Note that the term
Z(t,s)
for
(t,s)\in\Delta_{2}(S,T)
is determined by
Y
via the martingale representation theorem. Thus, we can write
Z=\mathcal{M}_{1}[Y]
in
\Delta_{2}(S,T)
by means of the martingale representation operator
\mathcal{M}_{1}:L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which will be defined below. Therefore, taking the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{t}
in (
4.1
), we see that the above equation is equivalent to the following equations:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle Y(t)=\mathbb{E}_{t}[\tilde{\psi}(t)]+\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}j(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]+\int^{T}_{t}k(s,t)^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{1}[Y](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(S,T),\\
\displaystyle\int^{T}_{t}Z(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}W(s)=\Theta^{Y}(t)-\mathbb{E}_{t}[\Theta^{Y}(t)],\ t\in(S,T),\end{cases}

where the process
\Theta^{Y}(t):=\tilde{\psi}(t)+\int^{T}_{t}j(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)\,\mathrm{d}s
,
t\in(S,T)
, is in
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and depends on
Y
. Note that the above two equations are decoupled in the sense that if
Y\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
solves the first equation, then the term
Z(t,s)
for
(t,s)\in(S,T)^{2}\setminus\Delta_{2}(S,T)
is determined by
Y
as the integrand of the martingale representation theorem for
\Theta^{Y}(t)
. Consequently, Type-II BSVIE (
4.1
) can be seen as an integral equation for
Y\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
(not for the pair
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot,\cdot))
) including the martingale representation operator:

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}j(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]+\int^{T}_{t}k(s,t)^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{1}[Y](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(S,T),

where
\psi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined by
\psi(t):=\mathbb{E}_{t}[\tilde{\psi}(t)]
for
t\in(S,T)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define the
martingale representation operator

\mathcal{M}_{n}:L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})\to L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
via the martingale representation theorem inductively as follows: for each
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
,

\mathcal{M}_{0}[\xi](t_{0}):=\xi(t_{0})

for
t_{0}\in(S,T)
, and

\mathcal{M}_{n}[\xi](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})=\mathbb{E}_{S}[\mathcal{M}_{n}[\xi](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})]+\int^{t_{n}}_{S}\mathcal{M}_{n+1}[\xi](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n},t_{n+1})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n+1})

for
(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 4.2
.

(i)

For each
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}_{S}[\mathcal{M}_{n}[\xi](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})]=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{M}_{n}[\xi](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n}),\ (t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T).

See (
4.5
) below.

(ii)

Noting the isometry, we have
\|\mathcal{M}_{n+1}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{n+2}(S,T))}\leq\|\mathcal{M}_{n}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
. By the induction, we see that
\mathcal{M}_{n}:L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})\to L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}})
is a bounded linear operator with the operator norm
\|\mathcal{M}_{n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
.

We will consider the following generalized equation including (infinitely many) martingale representation operators:

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}J(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}k_{n}(s,t)^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{n}[Y](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(S,T),

(4.2)

where
\psi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
K:=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[k_{n}]\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. We call this equation a
generalized BSVIE
. The solution we are looking for is the adapted process
Y\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying (
4.2
). In order to investigate generalized BSVIE (
4.2
), we write it as an algebraic equation on
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as follows:

Y=\psi+J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y+K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y,

(4.3)

where
J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y
corresponds to the term
\mathbb{E}_{t}[\int^{T}_{t}J(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)\,\mathrm{d}s]
, and
K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y
corresponds to the infinite sum of the integrals including martingale representation operators. Then, we provide a variation of constants formula for (
4.3
). Furthermore, we show the duality principle between a generalized SVIE and a generalized BSVIE.

4.1

\star
-product

In this subsection, we investigate the term

appearing in equation (

).

Definition 4.3
.

For each
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with

\star

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi](t_{0},t):=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(t_{0},T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s,t_{0})\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t_{0},t)\,\mathrm{d}s

for
(t_{0},t)=(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
and

, where
\int_{\Delta_{0}(t_{0},T)}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}s:=\alpha
for each vector
\alpha
.

The following lemma ensures the well-definedness of the backward
\star
-product.

Lemma 4.4
.

For each
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, the backward
\star
-product
K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
is well-defined and satisfies

\|K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

Furthermore, if
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, then
K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}K_{2}
is in
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and satisfies

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

Proof.

By using Minkowski’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we have, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\{\int_{\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)}\Bigl(\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s,\max t)\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t)|\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\Bigl\{\int_{\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s,\max t)\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t)|\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\Bigl\{\int_{\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s,\max t)|_{\mathrm{op}}\,|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t)|\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\Bigl\{\int_{\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s,\max t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k-n+1}(S,T)}\|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{k+1}(S,T))}.

\|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi]\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k-n+1}(S,T)}\|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{k+1}(S,T))}.

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\Bigl(\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k-n+1}(S,T)}\|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}(\Delta_{k+1}(S,T))}\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}\Bigr)^{2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle=|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{2}_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<\infty.

This implies the first assertion. Similarly, we can show that

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}K_{2}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n+1}[K_{1}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k-n+1}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{2}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k+1}(S,T)}

for each

, and thus

\displaystyle|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}K_{2}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K_{1}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k-n+1}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{2}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k+1}(S,T)}

\displaystyle=|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{1}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{2}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

Hence, the last assertion holds.
∎

The following proposition shows fundamental algebraic properties of the backward
\star
-product.

Proposition 4.5
.

For each
K,K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, the following hold:

\displaystyle I_{d}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi=\xi,

\displaystyle(K_{1}+K_{2})\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi=K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi+K_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi,

\displaystyle K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})=K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi_{1}+K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi_{2},

\displaystyle\alpha(K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi)=(\alpha K)\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi=K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(\alpha\xi),

\displaystyle K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(K_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi)=(K^{\top}_{2}\star K^{\top}_{1})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi.

Proof.

We prove
K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(K_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi)=(K^{\top}_{2}\star K^{\top}_{1})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi
. The others are trivial from the definition. Note that
\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]^{\top}=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K^{\top}]
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(K_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi)](t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K_{1}](s,\max t)\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K_{1}](s,\max t)\Bigl\{\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=k}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-k}(\max s,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-k}[K_{2}](r,\max s)\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](r,s,t)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\sum^{\ell}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-k}(\max s,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K_{1}](s,\max t)\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-k}[K_{2}](r,\max s)\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](r,s,t)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\sum^{\ell}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n}(\max t,T)}(\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-k}[K_{2}]^{\top}\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K_{1}]^{\top})(s,\max t)^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n}(\max t,T)}\Bigl\{\sum^{\ell-n}_{k=0}(\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-n-k}[K^{\top}_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K^{\top}_{1}])(s,\max t)\Bigr\}^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n}(\max t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-n}[(K^{\top}_{2}\star K^{\top}_{1})^{\top}](s,\max t)\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[(K^{\top}_{2}\star K^{\top}_{1})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi](t).

Hence, we have
K_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(K_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi)=(K^{\top}_{2}\star K^{\top}_{1})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi
.
∎

Next, we prove a representation of the backward
\star
-product in terms of martingale representation operators.

Proposition 4.6
.

For each
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with
d_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

(K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi)(t)=\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)\xi(t)+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](s,t)\mathcal{M}_{n}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(S,T),

(4.4)

where the infinite sum in the right-hand side converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
.

Proof.

First, we show that, for any
k,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
(s_{1},{\dots},s_{k},t_{0},t)=(s_{1},{\dots},s_{k},t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+k+1}(S,T)
,

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s_{1},{\dots},s_{k},\cdot)](t_{0},t)=\mathfrak{F}_{n+k}[\xi](s_{1},{\dots},s_{k},t_{0},t).

(4.5)

Clearly, for
k=0
, (
4.5
) holds for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Assume that (
4.5
) holds for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. By the definition of the martingale representation operator
\mathcal{M}_{k+1}
, we have, for
(s,s_{k})=(s_{0},{\dots},s_{k-1},s_{k})\in\Delta_{k+1}(S,T)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,s_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S}[\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,s_{k})]+\int^{s_{k}}_{S}\mathcal{M}_{k+1}[\xi](s,s_{k},t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S}[\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,s_{k})]+\int^{s_{k}}_{S}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{k+1}[\xi](s,s_{k},\cdot)](t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S}[\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,s_{k})]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\int^{s_{k}}_{S}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{k+1}[\xi](s,s_{k},\cdot)](t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{S}[\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,s_{k})]+\int^{s_{k}}_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{0}[\mathcal{M}_{k+1}[\xi](s,s_{k},\cdot)](t)\,\mathrm{d}W(t)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int^{s_{k}}_{S}\int^{t}_{S}\int^{t_{1}}_{S}{\cdots}\int^{t_{n-1}}_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{k+1}[\xi](s,s_{k},\cdot)](t,t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{n}){\cdots}\,\mathrm{d}W(t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}W(t).

From this, together with the assumption of the induction, we have, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
(s,s_{k},t_{0},t)=(s_{0},{\dots},s_{k-1},s_{k},t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+k+2}(S,T)
,

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{k+1}[\xi](s,s_{k},\cdot)](t_{0},t)=\mathfrak{F}_{n+1}[\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,\cdot)](s_{k},t_{0},t)=\mathfrak{F}_{n+k+1}[\xi](s,s_{k},t_{0},t).

By the induction, we see that (
4.5
) holds for any
k,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

We write the right-hand side of (
4.4
) by
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Psi^{K}_{k}[\xi](t)
, where
\Psi^{K}_{0}[\xi](t):=\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)\xi(t)
,
t\in(S,T)
, and

\Psi^{K}_{k}[\xi](t):=\int_{\Delta_{k}(t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K](s,t)\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(S,T),

for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Noting that
\|\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi]\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{k+1}(S,T))}\leq\|\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}
, we see that

\displaystyle\|\Psi^{K}_{k}[\xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\Bigl|\int_{\Delta_{k}(t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K](s,t)\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{k}(t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K](s,t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{k}(t,T)}|\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k+1}(S,T)}\|\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi]\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Delta_{k+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{2}_{\mathcal{V}_{k+1}(S,T)}\|\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

This implies that, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\Psi^{K}_{k}
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
with the operator norm
\|\Psi^{K}_{k}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k+1}(S,T)}
. Noting that
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{k+1}(S,T)}=|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<\infty
, the infinite sum
\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Psi^{K}_{k}[\xi]
converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})
. Furthermore, by the stochastic Fubini theorem and (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\Psi^{K}_{k}[\xi]](t_{0},t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{k}(t_{0},T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K](s,t_{0})\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\mathcal{M}_{k}[\xi](s,\cdot)](t_{0},t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta_{k}(t_{0},T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K](s,t_{0})\mathfrak{F}_{n+k}[\xi](s,t_{0},t)\,\mathrm{d}s

for
(t_{0},t)=(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
and
k,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Thus, we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}\Bigl[\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Psi^{K}_{k}[\xi]\Bigr](t_{0},t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\Psi^{K}_{k}[\xi]](t_{0},t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\int_{\Delta_{k}(t_{0},T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K](s,t_{0})\mathfrak{F}_{n+k}[\xi](s,t_{0},t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(t_{0},T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s,t_{0})\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\xi](s,t_{0},t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi](t_{0},t)

for
(t_{0},t)=(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. This implies that the equality
K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Psi^{K}_{k}[\xi]
holds in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and we complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

For each
\star

\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\langle\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}:=\mathbb{E}[\int^{T}_{S}\langle\xi_{1}(t),\xi_{2}(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t]
denotes the inner product in the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

By the isometry, together with the Fubini theorem, we have

\displaystyle\langle K\star\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\langle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\star\varphi],\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\psi]\rangle_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\Bigl\langle\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\varphi],\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\psi]\Bigr\rangle_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\sum^{\infty}_{n=k}\langle\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\varphi],\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\psi]\rangle_{L^{2}(\Delta_{n+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\sum^{\infty}_{n=k}\int_{\Delta_{k+1}(S,T)}\int_{\Delta_{n-k}(\max t,T)}\langle\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K](s,\max t)\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\varphi](t),\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\psi](s,t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\int_{\Delta_{k+1}(S,T)}\Bigl\langle\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\varphi](t),\sum^{\infty}_{n=k}\int_{\Delta_{n-k}(\max t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K](s,\max t)^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{n}[\psi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\int_{\Delta_{k+1}(S,T)}\langle\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\varphi](t),\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi](t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\langle\mathfrak{F}_{k}[\varphi],\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi]\rangle_{L^{2}(\Delta_{k+1}(S,T))}

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi,K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

Thus, we get the assertion.

for each

and
(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
. This completes the proof.

\displaystyle J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d_{1}})\ \text{and}\ \|J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|J|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)},

(4.7)

\displaystyle J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\ \text{and}\ |\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|J|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)},

(4.8)

and

\displaystyle J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(J_{2}+J_{3})=J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{2}+J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{3},

\displaystyle(J_{1}+J_{2})\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{3}=J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{3}+J_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{3},

\displaystyle\alpha(J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{2})=(\alpha J_{1})\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{2}=J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(\alpha J_{2}),

\displaystyle J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})=J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi_{1}+J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi_{2},

\displaystyle(J_{1}+J_{2})\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi=J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi+J_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi,

\displaystyle\alpha(J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi)=(\alpha J)\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi=J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(\alpha\xi),

\displaystyle J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(J_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{3})=(J^{\top}_{2}\ast J^{\top}_{1})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}J_{3},

(4.9)

\displaystyle J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(J_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi)=(J^{\top}_{2}\ast J^{\top}_{1})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi,

(4.10)

\displaystyle J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi)=(K^{\top}\star J^{\top})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi,

(4.11)

\displaystyle K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi)=(J^{\top}\ast K^{\top})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi.

(4.12)

Proof.

Noting
4.9
, the assertions (
4.6
), (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) can be proved by the same way as in
4.4
, and thus we omit the proofs. We prove (
4.10
), (
4.11
) and (
4.12
). The equality (
4.9
) can be proved by the same way as (
4.10
), and the other assertions are clear from the definition.

First, we prove (
4.10
). Noting the measurability of
J_{1}
and using Fubini’s theorem, we see that, for each
t\in(S,T)
,

\displaystyle(J_{1}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(J_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi))(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}J_{1}(s,t)(J_{2}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}J_{1}(s,t)\mathbb{E}_{s}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{s}J_{2}(r,s)\xi(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}J_{1}(s,t)\int^{T}_{s}J_{2}(r,s)\xi(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}\Bigl(\int^{r}_{t}J_{2}(r,s)^{\top}J_{1}(s,t)^{\top}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{\top}\xi(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}(J^{\top}_{2}\ast J^{\top}_{1})(r,t)^{\top}\xi(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr]

\displaystyle=((J^{\top}_{2}\ast J^{\top}_{1})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi)(t).

Thus, (
4.10
) holds.

Next, we prove (
4.11
). By
4.9
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
(t_{0},t)=(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi)](t_{0},t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n+1}(t_{0},T)}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{k-n}[J](s,t_{0})\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n+1}(t_{0},T)}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{k-n}[J](s,t_{0})\Bigl\{\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=k}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-k}(\max s,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-k}[K](r,\max s)\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](r,s,t)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=k}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n+1}(t_{0},T)}(\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-k}[K^{\top}]\triangleright\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{k-n}[J^{\top}])(s,t_{0})^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n+1}(t_{0},T)}\Bigl\{\sum^{\ell-n}_{k=0}(\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-n-k}[K^{\top}]\triangleright\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{k}[J^{\top}])(s,t_{0})\Bigr\}^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n+1}(t_{0},T)}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{\ell-n}[(K^{\top}\star J^{\top})^{\top}](s,t_{0})\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[(K^{\top}\star J^{\top})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi](t_{0},t).

Thus, (
4.11
) holds.

Lastly, we prove (
4.12
). By
4.9
and
3.8
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi)](t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s,\max t)\mathfrak{F}_{k}[J\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=n}\int_{\Delta_{k-n}(\max t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s_{0},s,\max t)\Bigl\{\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=k}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-k+1}(s_{0},T)}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{\ell-k}[J](r,s_{0})\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](r,s,t)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr\}\,\mathrm{d}(s_{0},s)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n}(\max t,T)}\Bigl\{\sum^{\ell}_{k=n}\int^{\min r}_{\max s}\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{\ell-k}[J](r,s_{0})^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K](s_{0},s,\max t)^{\top}\,\mathrm{d}s_{0}\Bigr\}^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](r,s,t)\,\mathrm{d}(r,s)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n}(\max t,T)}\Bigl\{\sum^{\ell}_{k=n}(\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{\ell-k}[J^{\top}]\ast\mathfrak{F}_{k-n}[K^{\top}])(s,\max t)\Bigr\}^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n}(\max t,T)}\Bigl\{\sum^{\ell-n}_{k=0}(\overline{\mathfrak{F}}_{\ell-n-k}[J^{\top}]\ast\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K^{\top}])(s,\max t)\Bigr\}^{\top}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{\ell=n}\int_{\Delta_{\ell-n}(\max t,T)}\mathfrak{F}_{\ell-n}[(J^{\top}\ast K^{\top})^{\top}](s,\max t)\mathfrak{F}_{\ell}[\xi](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[(J^{\top}\ast K^{\top})^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\xi](t).

Thus, (
4.12
) holds, and we finish the proof.
∎

The following is a
duality principle
with respect to the
\ast
-product and the backward
\ast
-product.

Proposition 4.11
.

For each
\ast
-Volterra kernel
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that

\langle J\ast\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}=\langle\varphi,J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

Proof.

By using Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle\langle J\ast\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\langle(J\ast\varphi)(t),\psi(t)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\Bigl\langle\int^{t}_{S}J(t,s)\varphi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\psi(t)\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\Bigl\langle\varphi(s),\int^{T}_{s}J(t,s)^{\top}\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\Bigl\langle\varphi(s),\mathbb{E}_{s}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{s}J(t,s)^{\top}\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr]\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{S}\langle\varphi(s),(J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi)(s)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi,J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}.

Thus, we get the assertion.
∎

4.3
A variation of constants formula for generalized BSVIEs

Now we show the variation of constants formula for generalized BSVIEs.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let a
\ast
-Volterra kernel
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and a
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be fixed, and suppose that
(J,K)
has a
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent
(Q,R)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Then for any free term
\psi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the generalized BSVIE

Y=\psi+J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y+K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y

has a unique solution
Y\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This solution is given by the variation of constants formula:

Y=\psi+Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi+R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi.

Proof.

Define
Y\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
Y=\psi+Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi+R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi
. By
4.5
,
4.10
and the resolvent equations, we have

\displaystyle J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y

\displaystyle=J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(\psi+Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi+R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi)

\displaystyle=J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi+(Q\ast J)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi+(R\star J)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi

\displaystyle=(J+Q\ast J+R\star J)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi

\displaystyle=Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi

and

\displaystyle K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y

\displaystyle=K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(\psi+Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi+R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi)

\displaystyle=K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi+(Q\ast K)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi+(R\star K)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi

\displaystyle=(K+Q\ast K+R\star K)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi

\displaystyle=R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi.

Thus, we get
Y=\psi+J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y+K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y
.

Conversely, if
Y\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies
Y=\psi+J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y+K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y
, then we have

\displaystyle Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi

\displaystyle=Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(Y-J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y-K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y)

\displaystyle=Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y-(J\ast Q)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y-(K\star Q)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y

\displaystyle=(Q-J\ast Q-K\star Q)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y

\displaystyle=J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y

and

\displaystyle R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi

\displaystyle=R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}(Y-J^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}Y-K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y)

\displaystyle=R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y-(J\ast R)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y-(K\star R)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y

\displaystyle=(R-J\ast R-K\star R)^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y

\displaystyle=K^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}Y.

Thus, we get
Y=\psi+Q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi+R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi
. This completes the proof.
∎

From the above result, together with the stochastic integral representation of the backward
\star
-product (see
4.6
), we immediately get the following corollary.

Corollary 4.13
.

Let
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
K=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\mathfrak{W}_{n}[k_{n}]\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
with
k_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be fixed, and suppose that
(J,K)
has a
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent
(Q,R)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Then for any free term
\psi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the generalized BSVIE

Y(t)=\psi(t)+\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\int^{T}_{t}J(s,t)^{\top}Y(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr]+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}k_{n}(s,t)^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{n}[Y](s,t)\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(S,T),

has a unique solution

. This solution is given by the variation of constants formula:

where
r_{n}:=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]\in\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
for

. Here, the infinite sum in the right-hand side converges in
L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Corollary 4.14
.

Let deterministic kernels
j\in L^{2}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
k\in\mathcal{V}_{2}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be fixed. Let
q\in L^{2}(\Delta_{2}(S,T);\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be the

, and assume that

has a

has a unique solution

\displaystyle Y(t)

\displaystyle=\psi(t)+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}(k+q\ast k)^{\triangleright n}(r,t)^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{n}[\psi](r,t)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\hskip 8.5359pt+\int^{T}_{t}q(s,t)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{t}\Bigl[\psi(s)+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\int_{\Delta_{n}(s,T)}(k+q\ast k)^{\triangleright n}(r,s)^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{n}[\psi](r,s)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr]\,\mathrm{d}s,\ t\in(S,T).

By the arguments in the proof of

-resolvent of

. Thus, by
4.12
, the BSVIE

\displaystyle=\psi+q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}\psi+q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi)+R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi

\displaystyle=\psi+R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi+q^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\ast}(\psi+R^{\top}\overset{\leftarrow}{\star}\psi).

Here, we used
4.10
. Noting
4.6
, we get the assertion.
∎

By the variation of constants formulae, together with the duality principles for the

- and the
\ast
-products (see

and

), we get the following duality principle between generalized SVIEs and generalized BSVIEs.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let a
\ast
-Volterra kernel
J\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and a
\star
-Volterra kernel

(J,K)
has a

(Q,R)\in\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})\times\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. For each
\varphi,\psi\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})

3.22
and
4.12

5
Existence of the
\star
-resolvent

As we have seen in the above sections, the
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent plays crucial roles for solving SVIEs and BSVIEs. Recall that every
\ast
-Volterra kernel has a unique
\ast
-resolvent (see
3.16
). Also, the
(\ast,\star)
-resolvent can be constructed by the
\ast
-resolvent and the
\star
-resolvent (see
3.20
). In this section, we focus on the existence of the
\star
-resolvent. We borrow some ideas from Section 9.3 of the textbook
[
11
]
, where the general theory on deterministic Volterra kernels is discussed.

First, we show the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Every
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
with
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}<1
has a
\star
-resolvent
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Furthermore, it holds that
R=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}K^{\star n}
, where
K^{\star n}
denotes the
(n-1)
-fold
\star
-product of
K
by itself, and the infinite sum converges in
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
.

Proof.

By
3.5
, we have
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K^{\star n}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}\leq|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|^{n}_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T)}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, by the assumption, the infinite sum
R:=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}K^{\star n}
converges in
\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
. Observe that

K+K\star R=K+K\star\Bigl(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}K^{\star n}\Bigr)=K+\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}K\star K^{\star n}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}K^{\star n}=R.

Similarly, we have
K+R\star K=R
. Thus,
R
is the
\star
-resolvent of
K
.
∎

The above lemma is not so useful by its own right for the existence of the
\star
-resolvent on the whole interval
(S,T)
with arbitrary length. In order to ensure the existence of the
\star
-resolvent in more general settings, we introduce the notion of the restriction and the concatenation of
\star
-Volterra kernels with respect to subintervals of
(S,T)
.

Definition 5.2
.

For each
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and a subinterval
(U,V)
of
(S,T)
, we define the
restricted
\star
-Volterra kernel

K_{(U,V)}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U,V;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
of
K
on
(U,V)
by the Wiener–Itô chaos expansion

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{(U,V)}]:=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]|_{\Delta_{n+1}(U,V)},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where, for each function
f:\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the function
f|_{\Delta_{n+1}(U,V)}:\Delta_{n+1}(U,V)\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the restriction of
f
on
\Delta_{n+1}(U,V)
.

Remark 5.3
.

(i)

Note that it does not hold
K_{(U,V)}(t)=K(t)
for
t\in(U,V)
in general.

(ii)

Let
0\leq S\leq U<V\leq T<\infty
. Suppose that a
\star
-Volterra kernel
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
has a
\star
-resolvent
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
on
(S,T)
. By the definition, it is easy to see that the restricted
\star
-Volterra kernel
K_{(U,V)}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U,V;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
has a
\star
-resolvent on
(U,V)
, which is given by the restricted
\star
-Volterra kernel
R_{(U,V)}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U,V;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
of
R
.

We prove the opposite direction of (ii) in the above remark. In other words, we provide a (non-trivial) concatenation procedure for the
\star
-resolvent. The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be a
\star
-Volterra kernel on
(S,T)
with
0\leq S<T<\infty
. Suppose that there exists
\{U_{k}\}^{m}_{k=0}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S=U_{0}<U_{1}<{\cdots}<U_{m}=T
and that, for every
k\in\{0,1,{\dots},m-1\}
, the restricted
\star
-Volterra kernel
K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{k},U_{k+1};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
has a
\star
-resolvent on
(U_{k},U_{k+1})
. Then
K
has a
\star
-resolvent on
(S,T)
.

Proof.

It suffices to show the theorem when
m=2
. Let
S<U<T
be fixed, and assume that
K_{(S,U)}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,U;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
(resp.
K_{(U,T)}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
) has a
\star
-resolvent
R_{(S,U)}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,U;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
on
(S,U)
(resp.
R_{(U,T)}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
on
(U,T)
). Define
\star
-Volterra kernels
K_{i},R_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
,
i=1,2
, on the whole interval
(S,T)
by the Wiener–Itô chaos expansions

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}]:=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{(S,U)}]\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(S,U),\ \mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}]:=0\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\setminus\Delta_{n+1}(S,U),\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{2}]:=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{(U,T)}]\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(U,T),\ \mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{2}]:=0\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\setminus\Delta_{n+1}(U,T),\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{1}]:=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{(S,U)}]\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(S,U),\ \mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{1}]:=0\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\setminus\Delta_{n+1}(S,U),\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}]:=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{(U,T)}]\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(U,T),\ \mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}]:=0\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\setminus\Delta_{n+1}(U,T),\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Define
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
by

R:=K+K\star R_{1}+R_{2}\star K+R_{2}\star K\star R_{1}.

We will show that
R
is the
\star
-resolvent of
K
on
(S,T)
.

First, we show that
R_{i}
is the
\star
-resolvent of
K_{i}
on
(S,T)
for
i=1,2
. Observe that, on
\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}\star R_{1}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{1}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R_{1}]=\Bigl(\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{(S,U)}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R_{(S,U)}]\Bigr)\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{n+1}(S,U)}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{(S,U)}\star R_{(S,U)}]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{n+1}(S,U)}=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{(S,U)}-K_{(S,U)}]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{n+1}(S,U)}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{1}-K_{1}],

and hence
R_{1}=K_{1}+K_{1}\star R_{1}
on
(S,T)
. Similarly, we can show that
R_{1}=K_{1}+R_{1}\star K_{1}
and
R_{2}=K_{2}+K_{2}\star R_{2}=K_{2}+R_{2}\star K_{2}
on
(S,T)
. Thus,
R_{i}
is the
\star
-resolvent of
K_{i}
on
(S,T)
for
i=1,2
.

Second, we show the following claim:

R=K+K\star R_{1}+K_{2}\star R=K+R_{2}\star K+R\star K_{1}.

(5.1)

Since
R_{2}
is the
\star
-resolvent of
K_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle K_{2}\star R

\displaystyle=K_{2}\star K+K_{2}\star K\star R_{1}+\underbrace{K_{2}\star R_{2}}_{=R_{2}-K_{2}}\star K+\underbrace{K_{2}\star R_{2}}_{=R_{2}-K_{2}}\star K\star R_{1}

\displaystyle=R_{2}\star K+R_{2}\star K\star R_{1}.

By inserting this formula into the definition of
R
, we get the first equality in (
5.1
). On the other hand, since
R_{1}
is the
\star
-resolvent of
K_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle R\star K_{1}

\displaystyle=K\star K_{1}+K\star\underbrace{R_{1}\star K_{1}}_{=R_{1}-K_{1}}+R_{2}\star K\star K_{1}+R_{2}\star K\star\underbrace{R_{1}\star K_{1}}_{=R_{1}-K_{1}}

\displaystyle=K\star R_{1}+R_{2}\star K\star R_{1}.

By inserting this formula into the definition of
R
, we get the second equality in (
5.1
).

Third, let us show the following claim:

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}]\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(S,U)\ \text{and}\ \mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{2}]\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(U,T),\\
&\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{1}]\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(S,U)\ \text{and}\ \mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}]\ \text{on}\ \Delta_{n+1}(U,T),\\
\end{split}

(5.2)

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The equalities for
K
are trivial from the definition. We show the equalities for
R
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Observe that, on
\Delta_{n+1}(S,U)
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\star R_{1}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R_{1}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{1}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R_{1}]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}\star R_{1}],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}\star K]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]=0,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}\star K\star R_{1}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K\star R_{1}]=0,

and hence

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}]+\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}\star R_{1}]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{1}+K_{1}\star R_{1}]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{1}].

On the other hand, on
\Delta_{n+1}(U,T)
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{2}],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\star R_{1}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R_{1}]=0,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}\star K]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{2}]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}\star K_{2}],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}\star K\star R_{1}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R_{2}\star K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R_{1}]=0,

and hence

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{2}]+\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}\star K_{2}]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{2}+R_{2}\star K_{2}]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}].

Thus, the claim (
5.2
) holds.

Lastly, we show the following claim:

K\star R_{1}+K_{2}\star R=K\star R\ \text{and}\ R_{2}\star K+R\star K_{1}=R\star K.

(5.3)

By using (
5.2
), for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\star R_{1}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R_{1}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright(\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R_{1}]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{k+1}(S,U)})

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright(\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{k+1}(S,U)})

and, for a.e. on
\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{2}\star R]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}(\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K_{2}]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{n-k+1}(U,T)})\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}(\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{n-k+1}(U,T)})\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright(\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{k+1}(S,T)\setminus\Delta_{k+1}(S,U)}).

Note that the last equality holds except on the set
\bigcup^{n}_{k=0}\{(t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)\,|\,t_{k}=U\}
whose Lebesgue measure is zero on
\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
. Thus, we get

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\star R_{1}+K_{2}\star R]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[K]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[R]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K\star R]\ \text{a.e.\ on $\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)$}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Therefore, the first equality in (
5.3
) holds. Similarly, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}\star K]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R_{2}]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}(\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R_{2}]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{n-k+1}(U,T)})\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}(\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{n-k+1}(U,T)})\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]

and, for a.e. on
\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R\star K_{1}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{1}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R]\triangleright(\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K_{1}]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{k+1}(S,U)})

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R]\triangleright(\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{k+1}(S,U)})

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}(\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R]\mbox{\rm{1}}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{\rm{l}}_{\Delta_{n-k+1}(S,T)\setminus\Delta_{n-k+1}(U,T)})\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K].

Thus, we get

\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R_{2}\star K+R\star K_{1}]=\sum^{n}_{k=0}\mathfrak{F}_{n-k}[R]\triangleright\mathfrak{F}_{k}[K]=\mathfrak{F}_{n}[R\star K]\ \text{a.e.\ on $\Delta_{n+1}(S,T)$}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Therefore, the second equality in (
5.3
) holds.

By (
5.1
) and (
5.3
), we get
R=K+K\star R=K+R\star K
, which implies that
R
is the
\star
-resolvent of
K
on
(S,T)
. This completes the proof.
∎

By
5.1
and
5.4
, we get the following important corollaries.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be a
\star
-Volterra kernel. Suppose that there exists
\{U_{k}\}^{m}_{k=0}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S=U_{0}<U_{1}<{\cdots}<U_{m}=T
and that, for every
k\in\{0,1,{\dots},m-1\}
, the restricted
\star
-Volterra kernel
K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{k},U_{k+1};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
satisfies
|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}<1
. Then
K
has a
\star
-resolvent
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
on
(S,T)
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be a
\star
-Volterra kernel. Assume that

\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)|_{\mathrm{op}}<1

and that there exists a nonnegative function
a\in L^{2}(0,T-S;\mathbb{R})
satisfying

|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\prod^{n}_{i=1}a(t_{i-1}-t_{i}),\ (t_{0},t_{1},{\dots},t_{n})\in\Delta_{n+1}(S,T),

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
K
has a
\star
-resolvent
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
on
(S,T)
.

Proof.

We denote
\delta:=\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be a number such that
\delta+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}<1
. Then there exists
\{U_{k}\}^{m}_{k=0}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S=U_{0}<U_{1}<{\cdots}<U_{m}=T
and that
\|a\|_{L^{2}(0,U_{k+1}-U_{k})}\leq\varepsilon
for every
k\in\{0,1,{\dots},m-1\}
. Fix an arbitrary
k\in\{0,1,{\dots},m-1\}
. Note that

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{1}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}=\underset{t\in(U_{k},U_{k+1})}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\delta.

Furthermore, noting
2.4
, we see that

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}\leq\|a\|^{n}_{L^{2}(0,U_{k+1}-U_{k})}\leq\varepsilon^{n}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we have

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}\leq\delta+\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}<1.

By
5.5
, we get the assertion.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
be a
\star
-Volterra kernel. Assume that

\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)|_{\mathrm{op}}<1

and that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Bigl\{(n!)^{-\frac{p-2}{2p}}\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](s,t)|^{p}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/p}\Bigr\}^{1/n}<\infty

for some
p>2
. Then
K
has a
\star
-resolvent
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(S,T;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
on
(S,T)
.

Proof.

We denote
\delta:=\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)|_{\mathrm{op}}<1
and

C_{p}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Bigl\{(n!)^{-\frac{p-2}{2p}}\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](s,t)|^{p}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/p}\Bigr\}^{1/n}<\infty.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a number such that

C_{p}\varepsilon^{\frac{(p-2)}{2p}}<1\ \text{and}\ \delta+\frac{C_{p}\varepsilon^{\frac{(p-2)}{2p}}}{1-C_{p}\varepsilon^{\frac{(p-2)}{2p}}}<1.

Take
\{U_{k}\}^{m}_{k=0}
with
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S=U_{0}<U_{1}<{\cdots}<U_{m}=T
and that
U_{k+1}-U_{k}<\varepsilon
for every
k\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
. Let
k\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
be fixed. The restricted
\star
-Volterra kernel
K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}\in\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{k},U_{k+1};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
satisfies

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{1}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}=\underset{t\in(U_{k},U_{k+1})}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}|\mathfrak{F}_{0}[K](t)|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\delta

and, by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}

\displaystyle=\underset{t\in(U_{k},U_{k+1})}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,U_{k+1})}|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](s,t)|^{2}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\frac{(U_{k+1}-U_{k})^{n}}{n!}\Bigr)^{\frac{(p-2)}{2p}}\underset{t\in(S,T)}{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Bigl(\int_{\Delta_{n}(t,T)}|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K](s,t)|^{p}_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{p}\varepsilon^{\frac{(p-2)}{2p}})^{n}.

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we have

|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{K}_{\mathbb{F}}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|\mathfrak{F}_{n}[K_{(U_{k},U_{k+1})}]|\hskip-0.56905pt|\hskip-0.56905pt|_{\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(U_{k},U_{k+1})}\leq\delta+\frac{C_{p}\varepsilon^{\frac{(p-2)}{2p}}}{1-C_{p}\varepsilon^{\frac{(p-2)}{2p}}}<1.

By
5.5
, we get the assertion.
∎

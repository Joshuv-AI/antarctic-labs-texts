Periodic minimum in the count of binomial coefficients not divisible by a prime
Hsien-Kuei Hwang, Svante Janson, Tsung-Hsi Tsai

Abstract
The summatory function of the number of binomial coefficients not divisible by a prime is known to exhibit regular periodic oscillations, yet identifying the less regularly behaved minimum of the underlying periodic functions has been open for almost all cases. We propose an approach to identify such minimum in some generality, solving particularly a previous conjecture of B. Wilson [Asymptotic behavior of Pascal’s triangle modulo a prime, Acta Arith. 83 (1998), pp. 105–116].

Introduction

Let
F_{p}(n)
denote the number of binomial coefficients

\binom{m}{k}
,
0\leq k\leq m<n
, that are not divisible by a given
prime
p
. In particular, for
p=2
,

is the number of odd
numbers in the first
n
rows of Pascal’s triangle. The study of the
quantity
F_{p}(n)
has a long history; see, for example, the
historical account in Stolarsky’s paper
[
11
]
. Some sequences of

appear in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
(OEIS)
[
9
]
: A006046 (
p=2
), A006048 (
p=3
), and A194458
(
p=5
).

Fine
[
3
]
proved that “almost all” binomial coefficients are
divisible by a prime
p
, more precisely that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{p}(n)}{\binom{n+1}{2}}=0.

(1.1)

He
[
3
]
also gave the expression (with
n=\sum_{0\leq i\leq s}b_{i}2^{i}
,
b_{i}\in\{0,1\}
)

F_{p}(n+1)-F_{p}(n)=\prod_{0\leq i\leq s}(b_{i}+1)

(1.2)

for the number of binomial coefficients
\binom{n}{k}
,
0\leq k\leq n
,
not divisible by
p
.

Later Stein
[
10
]
observed that, for any prime

\displaystyle F_{p}(pn)=\binom{p+1}{2}F_{p}(n),\qquad n\geq 1,

(1.3)

Thus the sequence
\psi_{p}(n):=F_{p}(n)/n^{\varrho_{p}}
, where

\varrho_{p}=\log_{p}\binom{p+1}{2}
, satisfies
\psi_{p}(pn)=\psi_{p}(n)
,
so that
\psi_{p}
can be extended by this property to all positive

p
-adic rational numbers. Stein
[
10
]
also showed that
\psi_{p}

can be further extended to a continuous function on
(0,\infty)
; in
other words, there exists a continuous 1-periodic function
\mathcal{P}_{p}(t)

on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle F_{p}(n)=n^{\varrho_{p}}\mathcal{P}_{p}(\log_{p}n),\qquad n\geq 1.

(1.4)

It follows immediately from this that

\displaystyle\alpha_{p}

\displaystyle:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{F_{p}(n)}{n^{\varrho_{p}}}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{F_{p}(n)}{n^{\varrho_{p}}}=\max_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{P}_{p}(t)\in[0,\infty),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\beta_{p}

\displaystyle:=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{F_{p}(n)}{n^{\varrho_{p}}}=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{F_{p}(n)}{n^{\varrho_{p}}}=\min_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{P}_{p}(t)\in[0,\infty),

(1.6)

furthermore,
\alpha_{p}=1
for every
p
, and that
\binom{p+1}{2}^{-1}\leq\beta_{p}<1
; see
[
10
]
. The extremal properties of

[
11
]
and Harborth
[

]
.
In particular, Harborth proved that

\beta_{2}\doteq 0.812556
to 6 decimal places; see
A077464 (Stolarsky-Harborth constant) for more information. Further
numerical estimates of
\beta_{p}
for various

of special mention is Chen and Ji’s inequalities
[
1
]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+p^{-r})^{{\varrho_{p}}}}\min_{p^{r}\leq n\leq p^{r+1}}\frac{F_{p}(n)}{n^{{\varrho_{p}}}}\leq\beta_{p}\leq\min_{p^{r}\leq n\leq p^{r+1}}\frac{F_{p}(n)}{n^{{\varrho_{p}}}},

(1.7)

which in principle makes it possible to calculate

For

2
A recurrence and its solutions

In this section, we fix a prime
p\geq 2
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The total number of binomial coefficients
\binom{m}{k}
with
m,k<n
that
are not divisible by
p
satisfies the recurrence

F_{p}(n)=\sum_{0\leq j<p}(p-j)F_{p}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n+j}{p}\right\rfloor\right)\qquad(n\geq p),

(2.1)

with the initial values
\{F_{p}(j)=\binom{j+1}{2}:j=1,\ldots,p-1\}
.
In fact, (
2.1
) holds for all
n\geq 0
, with
F_{p}(0):=0
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\binom{j}{k}
is not divisible by a prime
p
if

k\leq j<p
. Thus the initial values are

F_{p}(j)=\binom{j+1}{2}\quad\textrm{for }j=1,\ldots,p-1.

(2.2)

We use the following expression from Volodin
[
12
]
(there stated
more generally for multinomial coefficients):

F_{p}(n)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\leq j\leq\nu}\binom{p+1}{2}^{j}b_{j}\prod_{j\leq i\leq\nu}(b_{i}+1),

(2.3)

[see also
[
1
]
] where
b_{j}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
are the base

p
digits of
n=b_{0}+b_{1}p+\dotsm+b_{\nu}p^{\nu}
. (The formula
(
2.3
) holds trivially for
n=0
too, with an empty sum.) If

n=kp
, then
b_{0}=0
and
k=b_{1}+b_{2}p+\cdots+b_{\nu}p^{\nu-1}
.
Thus, from (
2.3
),

\displaystyle F_{p}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq\nu}\binom{p+1}{2}^{j}b_{j}\prod_{j\leq i\leq\nu}(b_{i}+1)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\binom{p+1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq\nu}\binom{p+1}{2}^{j-1}b_{j}\prod_{j\leq i\leq\nu}(b_{i}+1)

\displaystyle=\binom{p+1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sum_{0\leq j\leq\nu-1}\binom{p+1}{2}^{j}b_{j+1}\prod_{j\leq i\leq\nu-1}(b_{i+1}+1)

\displaystyle=\binom{p+1}{2}F_{p}(k).

In general, suppose
n=kp+r
, where
k\geq 0
and
0\leq r<p
. Then

b_{0}=r
and (
2.3
) yields

F_{p}(n)-F_{p}(kp)=\frac{r}{2}\prod_{0\leq i\leq\nu}(b_{i}+1)=\frac{r(r+1)}{2}\prod_{1\leq i\leq\nu}(b_{i}+1)

(2.5)

since all but the first term in the sums cancel. By Fine’s result
(
1.2
), this yields

F_{p}(n)-F_{p}(kp)=\binom{r+1}{2}\left(F_{p}(k+1)-F_{p}(k)\right).

(2.6)

Thus we have

\displaystyle F_{p}(n)

\displaystyle=F_{p}(kp)+\left(F_{p}(n)-F_{p}(kp)\right)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\binom{p+1}{2}F_{p}(k)+\binom{r+1}{2}\left(F_{p}(k+1)-F_{p}(k)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq j<p}(p-j)F_{p}(k)+\binom{r+1}{2}\left(F_{p}(k+1)-F_{p}(k)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq j\leq p-r-1}(p-j)F_{p}(k)+\sum_{p-r\leq j<p}(p-j)F_{p}(k+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq j<p}(p-j)F_{p}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n+j}{p}\right\rfloor\right).

This proves the recurrence (
2.1
).
∎

Our approach

In this section,
p
is a fixed odd prime. Recall that

By (

\beta_{p}=\min\{G(s):s\in[p^{-1},1]\}

\displaystyle A^{-\log_{p}s}=p^{-{\varrho_{p}}\log_{p}s}=s^{-{\varrho_{p}}}.

(3.3)

Remark 3.1
.

For any

\displaystyle F_{p}(n)n^{-{\varrho_{p}}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}(\log_{p}n)=\mathcal{P}(\{\log_{p}n\})=G\bigl(p^{\{\log_{p}n\}-1}\bigr)=G\bigl(np^{-\lfloor\log_{p}n\rfloor-1}\bigr).

(3.4)

It follows that if

attains its minimum on
[p^{-1},1]
at
{\hat{s}}
,
then the sequence

satisfies

\displaystyle F_{p}(n_{k})n_{k}^{-{\varrho_{p}}}\to G({\hat{s}})=\beta_{p},

and thus the infimum

is asymptotically reached by the

(n_{k})
. Conversely, Wilson

]
conjectured (in a

\displaystyle\beta_{p}=\lim_{k\to\infty}(F_{p}(n_{k})n_{k}^{-{\varrho_{p}}}),

(3.6)

for a sequence

given by the recursion

for a suitably chosen

is attained at a point

such that all but a finite

. Note

\triangle

Our methods of proof consists of two major techniques:

magnifying mapping
and
piecewise monotonic
majorization
. The former defines first a mapping
\theta
and
magnifies the local difference of
G(\theta(s))-G(\theta(\frac{1}{2}))

in a small neighborhood, say

\hat{s}=\theta(\frac{1}{2})
into
the global difference
\varphi(s)-\varphi(\frac{1}{2})
, justifying that

G(\hat{s})
is a local minimum in

ratio between two monotonic functions by their extreme values in the
targeted interval, which, after partitioning the interval

into proper subintervals, is used
interval-by-interval to check that
G(\hat{s})
is also a minimum in

[0,1]\setminus J
.

3.1
A magnifying mapping

As the minimum
\hat{s}
of
G(s)
we are going to prove all have the
form (
1.11
) whose
p
-ary expansion has an infinity number
of trailing digits of the form
\frac{p-1}{2}
, we construct a linear
mapping as follows.

Fix
M
and
m
with
1\leq m<p^{M}
. Let
\mu
be the middle
point of
\bigl[\frac{m}{p^{M}},\frac{m+1}{p^{M}}\bigr]
:

\displaystyle\mu:=\frac{m+\frac{1}{2}}{p^{M}}.

(3.7)

For every
k\geq 0
, define a linear mapping
\theta_{k}
from
[0,1]

onto the interval

I_{M+k}:=\left[\mu-\frac{1}{2p^{M+k}},\mu+\frac{1}{2p^{M+k}}\right]

(3.8)

by

\theta_{k}(t):=\frac{m}{p^{M}}+\sum_{M<j\leq M+k}\frac{p-1}{2\,p^{j}}+\frac{t}{p^{M+k}},\qquad t\in[0,1].

(3.9)

Thus
\mu=\theta_{k}(\frac{1}{2})
. In terms of the
p
-ary expansion, if

\displaystyle m=a_{1}p^{M-1}+\cdots+a_{M-1}p+a_{M},

(3.10)

then
\mu
has the form
\mu=(0.{\hat{b}}_{1}{\hat{b}}_{2}\cdots)_{p}
, where

{\hat{b}}_{i}=a_{i}\;\textrm{for}\;1\leq i\leq M\quad\textrm{and}\quad{\hat{b}}_{i}=\frac{p-1}{2}\;\textrm{for}\;i\geq M+1.

(3.11)

We prove that the “zoomed” functions
G(\theta_{k}(t))-G(\mu)
,
suitably scaled, converge uniformly on
[0,1]
, and we give a sufficient
condition for

to be a minimum in an explicitly specified

Note that
\varphi(\mu)
has the form,
by (

) and (

),

\displaystyle\varphi(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq M}\frac{a_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j}(1+a_{i})}{A^{j}}+\frac{\tau_{M}}{4}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq j\leq M}\frac{a_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j}(1+a_{i})}{A^{j}}+\tau_{M}\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right),

(3.12)

where

\displaystyle\tau_{M}:=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{M}(1+a_{i})}{A^{M}}.

(3.13)

The construction of the mapping
\theta_{k}
is helpful in bringing the
local difference
\varphi(\theta_{k}(t))-\varphi(\theta_{k}(\frac{1}{2}))
into a global one in terms of

\varphi(t)-\varphi(\frac{1}{2})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

We have

p^{k}\bigl(\varphi(\theta_{k}(t))-\varphi(\mu)\bigr)=\tau_{M}\left(\varphi(t)-\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

(3.14)

Proof.

Let
b_{i}
and
\tilde{b}_{i}
be the base
p
digits of
t
and

\theta_{k}(t)
, respectively. Then (
3.9
) shows that the first

M+k
digits
\tilde{b}_{i}
coincide with those of
\mu
given by
(
3.11
):

\tilde{b}_{i}={\hat{b}}_{i}=a_{i}\;\textrm{for}\;1\leq i\leq M\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tilde{b}_{i}={\hat{b}}_{i}=\frac{p-1}{2}\;\textrm{for}\;M+1\leq i\leq M+k,

(3.15)

and also that the remaining digits of
\tilde{b}_{i}
are the digits of
t
,
i.e.,

\tilde{b}_{i+k+M}=b_{i},\qquad i\geq 1.

(3.16)

Hence, if we compute
\varphi(\theta_{k}(t))
and
\varphi(\mu)
by
(
2.11
), then the first
M+k
terms are equal, and we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(\theta_{k}(t))-\varphi(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq k+M+1}\frac{\tilde{b}_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left(\tilde{b}_{i}+1\right)}{A^{j}}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq k+M+1}\frac{\frac{p-1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{M}\left({a}_{i}+1\right)\prod_{i=M+1}^{j}\left(\frac{p-1}{2}+1\right)}{A^{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{M}\left({a}_{i}+1\right)}{A^{k+M}}\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)^{k}\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{b_{l}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left(b_{i}+1\right)}{A^{l}}-\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{\frac{p-1}{2}\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right)^{l}}{A^{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{M}}{p^{k}}\left(\varphi(t)-\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).\qed

(3.18)

The crucial properties we need of the magnifying mapping
\theta_{k}

are given as follows, the first for large
k
and the second for
finite one.

Theorem 3.3
.

With the notations as above, we have, uniformly for
t\in[0,1]
,

p^{k}\left(G(\theta_{k}(t))-G(\mu)\right)=\mu^{-{\varrho_{p}}-1}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}(t)+O\left(p^{-k}\right)\right),

(3.19)

for large
k
, where the limiting function
\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}(t)
is
given by

\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}(t):=\tau_{M}\mu\left(\varphi(t)-\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)-\frac{{\varrho_{p}}\varphi(\mu)}{p^{M}}\left(t-\frac{1}{2}\right).

(3.20)

Furthermore, if for some
k\geq 0
,

\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}(t)\geq E_{\mu,k}(t)\qquad\text{for all}\quad t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p}\right]\cup\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p},1\right],

(3.21)

where

E_{\mu,k}(t):=\frac{\tau_{M}{\varrho_{p}}}{p^{M+k}}\left(\varphi(t)-\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\left(t-\frac{1}{2}\right),

(3.22)

then
G(\mu)
is the minimum of the function
G
in the interval

(defined in (

)), and this minimum is attained
only at
\mu
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we use the abbreviations (with an abuse of notation):

\nabla G(\theta):=G(\theta_{k}(t))-G(\mu)
,
\nabla\varphi(\theta):=\varphi(\theta_{k}(t))-\varphi(\mu)
,
\nabla\varphi:=\varphi(t)-\varphi(\frac{1}{2})
,
\nabla\theta:=\theta_{k}(t)-\mu
and

\nabla t:=t-\frac{1}{2}
. Observe first that a Taylor expansion yields

\displaystyle\theta^{-{\varrho_{p}}}-\mu^{-{\varrho_{p}}}+{\varrho_{p}}\mu^{-{\varrho_{p}}-1}(\theta-\mu)=J_{\theta,\mu}\mu^{-{\varrho_{p}}-1}(\theta-\mu)^{2},

(3.23)

where

\displaystyle J_{\theta,\mu}:=\mu^{{\varrho_{p}}+1}{\varrho_{p}}({\varrho_{p}}+1)\int_{0}^{1}x(\mu x+\theta(1-x))^{-{\varrho_{p}}-2}\text{d}x

(3.24)

remains positive whenever
\theta,\mu>0
and
\theta\neq\mu

(or

). Applying this expansion, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla G(\theta)

\displaystyle=\theta_{k}(t)^{-{\varrho_{p}}}\bigl(\varphi(\theta_{k}(t))-\varphi(\mu)\bigr)+\varphi(\mu)\bigl(\theta_{k}(t)^{-{\varrho_{p}}}-\mu^{-{\varrho_{p}}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-{\varrho_{p}}-1}\nabla\varphi(\theta)\left(\mu-{\varrho_{p}}\nabla\theta+J_{\theta_{k}(t),\mu}\bigl(\nabla\theta\bigr)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\varphi(\mu)\mu^{-{\varrho_{p}}-1}\left({\varrho_{p}}\nabla\theta-J_{\theta_{k}(t),\mu}\bigl(\nabla\theta\bigr)^{2}\right);

(3.25)

thus

\begin{split}\mu^{{\varrho_{p}}+1}\nabla G(\theta)&=\mu\nabla\varphi(\theta)-{\varrho_{p}}\varphi(\mu)\nabla\theta-{\varrho_{p}}\nabla\varphi(\theta)\nabla\theta+J_{\theta_{k}(t),\mu}\varphi(\theta_{k}(t))\bigl(\nabla\theta\bigr)^{2}.\end{split}

(3.26)

By (
3.9
)

p^{k}\nabla\theta=p^{-M}\nabla t.

(3.27)

This, together with (

), Lemma

, and the definitions
(

) and (
3.22
) of

and

,
gives

(3.28)

where

\displaystyle R_{\mu,k}(t):=p^{k}J_{\theta_{k}(t),\mu}\varphi(\theta_{k}(t))\bigl(\nabla\theta\bigr)^{2}.

(3.29)

We note for later use that, by (
3.29
) and (
3.24
),

\displaystyle R_{\mu,k}(t)>0,\qquad\text{if }\theta_{k}(t)\neq\mu\quad(\text{i.e., }t\neq\tfrac{1}{2}).

(3.30)

Since

,

, and

are fixed, and

is bounded, we see that

\displaystyle R_{\mu,k}(t)=O\left(p^{-k}\right),

(3.31)

Similarly, (

) implies that

(3.32)

Hence, (

), and (

with

3.2
Monotonic majorization

Once we convert the minimality of
G(s)
at
s=\mu
in
I_{M+k}
to
the positivity of
\Delta_{\mu,k}(t):=\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}(t)-E_{\mu,k}(t)
for
t
in the unit interval excluding a small
neighborhood of
t=\frac{1}{2}

(see (
3.21
)),
we then need means of handling the
positivity of the difference of two monotonic functions because

\Delta_{\mu,k}(t)
can be expressed as:

\begin{split}\Delta_{\mu,k}(t)&:=\mathcal{Q}_{\mu}(t)-E_{\mu,k}(t)\\
&=\begin{cases}\frac{{\varrho_{p}}\varphi(\mu)}{p^{M}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-t\right)-\tau_{M}\left(\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)-\varphi(t)\right)\left(\mu+\frac{{\varrho_{p}}}{p^{M+k}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-t\right)\right),&\text{if }t\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}],\\
\tau_{M}\mu\left(\varphi(t)-\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)-\frac{{\varrho_{p}}}{p^{M}}\left(t-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\varphi(\mu)+\frac{\tau_{M}}{p^{k}}\left(\varphi(t)-\varphi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\right),&\text{if }t\in[\frac{1}{2},1],\end{cases}\end{split}

(3.35)

the first being the difference of two positive decreasing functions,
the second that of two increasing functions, and the condition
(
3.21
) then being equivalent to
\Delta_{\mu,k}(t)\geq 0
.

On the other hand,
G(s)=s^{-{\varrho_{p}}}\varphi(s)
can also be regarded
as the ratio of two increasing functions. Thus to show that
G

attains nowhere the minimum value
\beta
for
s
outside a small
neighborhood of
\mu
, we need to handle the ratio of two increasing
functions.

Since the function
\varphi(t)
is of fractal type, we use the
following simple idea.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
f,g
are increasing functions on
[a,b]
with

f\geq 0
and

g>0

there. If
f(a)/g(b)>C
, then
f(x)/g(x)>C
for
x\in[a,b]
.
Similarly, if
f(a)-g(b)>C
, then
f(x)-g(x)>C
for
x\in[a,b]
.

Proof.

By monotonicity, for
x\in[a,b]
,

\displaystyle\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\geq\frac{f(a)}{g(b)}>C.

(3.36)

The difference version is similar:
f(x)-g(x)\geq f(a)-g(b)>C
.
∎

The case when
f,g
are decreasing functions is similar.

In particular, for
\delta>0
, if
(x+\delta)^{-{\varrho_{p}}}\varphi(x)>\beta
, then
G(t)=t^{-{\varrho_{p}}}\varphi(t)>\beta
for
t\in[x,x+\delta]
.

Such a simple idea will be applied numerically to sufficiently small
subintervals after a suitable partition of the target interval.

6.2
Large
p
asymptotics

Assuming that
G
reaches its minimum at
s=\hat{s}_{p}(\xi,\eta)

for some
(\xi,\eta)
,
so that the minimum value
\beta_{p}
is given by (
1.13
),
we give here some simple, not completely rigorous, estimates
of the two parameters
(\xi,\eta)
and the minimum point
\hat{s}_{p}

for a given large
p
.
We note first that

\displaystyle{\varrho_{p}}=\frac{\log(\frac{1}{2}p(p+1))}{\log p}=2+\frac{\log(1+\frac{1}{p})-\log 2}{\log p}=2-\frac{1}{\log_{2}p}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{p\log p}\Bigr).

(6.1)

We see from Table
1
in Section
6.1
that
\xi
and
\eta
both seem to
grow as
p
grows (although not monotonically in case of
\eta
).
In fact, it is easy to see that at least
\xi+\eta
must tend to infinity,
because otherwise there would be an infinite subsequence with some fixed
values of
\xi
and
\eta
, but then it would follow from (
1.13
)
that as
p\to\infty
along this subsequence,
using
\rho_{p}\to 2
from (
6.1
),

\displaystyle\beta_{p}=B_{\xi,\eta}\to\frac{\xi+1}{2\xi+1}>\frac{1}{2},

(6.2)

which contradicts (
1.9
).

We obtain more precise estimates
by regarding
\xi
and
\eta
as continuous variables in (
1.13
)
and setting the partial derivatives of (
1.13
) with
respect to
\xi
and
\eta
equal to 0.
This yields the equations:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(4\xi+3)p^{2}+(4\xi-4\eta+3)p+2\eta(2\eta-1)}{(\xi+1)(2\xi+1)p^{2}+(\xi+1)(2\xi-4\eta+1)p+2\eta(\xi+1)(2\eta-1)}-\frac{2{\varrho_{p}}p}{(2\xi+1)p-2\eta}=0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{{\varrho_{p}}}{(2\xi+1)p-2\eta}-\frac{2p-4\eta+1}{(2\xi+1)p^{2}+(2\xi-4\eta+1)p+2\eta(2\eta-1)}=0.\end{cases}

(6.3)

The positive solution pair, say
(\xi_{+},\eta_{+})
, already gives a
very good approximation to the true values of
(\xi,\eta)
. Empirically,
(\lfloor\xi_{+}+0.5\rfloor,\lfloor\eta_{+}+0.45\rfloor)

is identical to the true pair
(\xi,\eta)
at which
G
attains the
minimum for primes
p
from
11
to
79
, and differs by at most
1

(at either
\xi
or
\eta
but not both) for primes up to
7907
.
For
p
as large as
p=1,000,003
, such a solution pair gives
(9,13206)
,
while the true minimum of
G
is reached at
(\xi,\eta)=(9,13203)
.

For large
p
,
we may approximate the equations (
6.3
) by ignoring all terms
that are
O(\frac{1}{p})
or
O(\frac{\eta}{p\xi})
times the leading terms in the various
numerators and
denominators. (We assume that
\frac{\eta}{p\xi}
is small.)
This gives the equations, using the notation
\Delta:=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\eta}{p\xi}
,

\displaystyle\frac{4\xi+3}{(\xi+1)(2\xi+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2{\varrho_{p}}}{2\xi+1}\bigl(1+O(\Delta)\bigr),

(6.4a)

\displaystyle\frac{{\varrho_{p}}}{2\xi+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2-\frac{4}{p}\eta}{2\xi+1}\bigl(1+O(\Delta)\bigr).

(6.4b)

If we further define
\delta_{p}:=2-{\varrho_{p}}\sim\frac{1}{\log_{2}p}
(see (
6.1
)), then
(
6.4a
) yields

\displaystyle(4-2\delta_{p})(\xi+1)=2{\varrho_{p}}(\xi+1)=(4\xi+3)\bigl(1+O(\Delta)\bigr)=4\xi+3+O(\xi\Delta).

(6.5)

Assuming
\xi,\eta=o(p)
, we have
\xi\Delta=\frac{\xi+\eta}{p}=o(1)
and then
(
6.5
) yields

2\delta_{p}\xi=1+o(1)
and finally, using (
6.1
),

\displaystyle\xi\sim\frac{1}{2\delta_{p}}\sim\frac{\log_{2}p}{2}=\log_{4}p.

(6.6)

Similarly, (
6.4b
) yields

\displaystyle 2-\frac{4\eta}{p}=\rho_{p}+O(\Delta)=2-\delta_{p}+O(\Delta)

(6.7)

leading to the empirical approximation for
\eta
:

\displaystyle\eta\sim\frac{1}{4}p\delta_{p}\sim\frac{p}{4\log_{2}p}.

(6.8)

The values (
6.6
) and (
6.8
) yield by (
1.11
)
the estimate for the minimum point

\displaystyle\hat{s}_{p}\sim\frac{\xi}{p}\sim\frac{\log_{4}p}{p}.

(6.9)

Finally, observe that if

\displaystyle x=\sum_{j\geq 1}b_{j}p^{-j}=(0.b_{1}b_{2}\dots)_{p},

(6.10)

then
b_{1}=\lfloor{px\rfloor}
, and

\displaystyle b_{m}=\lfloor p^{m}x\rfloor-p\lfloor p^{m-1}x\rfloor=p\{p^{m-1}x\}-\{p^{m}x\}\qquad(m\geq 2).

(6.11)

Thus

\displaystyle\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\lfloor p^{j}x\rfloor-p\lfloor p^{j-1}x\rfloor}{A^{j}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j}\left(1+\lfloor p^{i}x\rfloor-p\lfloor p^{i-1}x\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\lfloor{px\rfloor}}{2}\left(\frac{\lfloor px\rfloor}{A}+\sum_{j\geq 2}\frac{p\{p^{j-1}x\}-\{p^{j}x\}}{A^{j}}\prod_{2\leq i\leq j}\left(1+p\{p^{i-1}x\}-\{p^{i}x\}\right)\right)

(6.12)

For large
p
, each term in the sum on the right-hand side is
asymptotic to

\displaystyle\frac{2^{j-1}}{p^{j-1}}\,x\{px\}\cdots\{p^{j-2}x\}\{p^{j-1}x\}^{2}\left(1+O\left(p^{-1}\right)\right),

(6.13)

for
j\geq 2
, and for
j=1
:

\displaystyle\frac{\lfloor px\rfloor(\lfloor px\rfloor+1)}{p(p+1)}=x^{2}+\frac{x(1-x-2\{px\})}{p}+O\left(p^{-2}\right).

(6.14)

We then obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=x^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{x(1-x-2\{px\}+2\{px\}^{2})}{p}+O\bigl(p^{-2}\bigr).

(6.15)

Then

\displaystyle G(x)=x^{2-{\varrho_{p}}}+\frac{x^{1-{\varrho_{p}}}(1-x-2\{px\}+2\{px\}^{2})}{p}+\cdots,

(6.16)

where the piecewise differentiability of the terms on the right-hand side
might be
useful in further identifying the true minimum of
G
for large
p
.

Appendix A
The p-ary recurrence

In this appendix we study a more general recurrence, using the
methods of
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
where binary recurrences are
studied; see also Section 7.1 in the

earlier version of

[
8
]
on arXiv.

Let
p
be any integer larger than
1
. Consider the recurrence

f(n)=\sum_{0\leq j<p}\gamma_{j}f\left(\left\lfloor\frac{n+j}{p}\right\rfloor\right)\qquad\textrm{for $n\geq p$},

(A.1)

with given coefficients
\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{p-1}
and given
initial values
f(1),\ldots,f(p-1)
. We assume for simplicity that

\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{p-1}>0
. Let

A:=\sum_{0\leq j<p}\gamma_{j}.

(A.2)

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\gamma_{0},\ldots,\gamma_{p-1}>0
. Then there exists a unique
strictly increasing continuous function
\varphi
on
[0,1]
such
that
\varphi(0)=0,\varphi(1)=1
and for
j=0,1,\ldots,p-1
,

\varphi(t)=\frac{\gamma_{p-j-1}}{A}\varphi(pt-j)+\frac{\sum_{p-j\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}\qquad\mathrm{if}\quad\frac{j}{p}\leq t\leq\,\frac{j+1}{p}.

(A.3)

Moreover, we have the explicit formula

\varphi\left(\sum_{j\geq 1}b_{j}p^{-j}\right)=\sum_{j\geq 1}A^{-j}\left(\sum_{p-b_{j}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j}\gamma_{p-1-b_{i}}\right),

(A.4)

when
b_{j}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
for
j\geq 1
.

Proof.

Define
\varphi_{0}(t):=t
for
t\in[0,1]
, and recursively let

\varphi_{k+1}(t):=\frac{\gamma_{p-j-1}}{A}\varphi_{k}(pt-j)+\frac{\sum_{p-j\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}\qquad\mathrm{if}\quad\frac{j}{p}\leq t\leq\,\frac{j+1}{p},

(A.5)

for
j=0,1,\ldots,p-1
. (Thus
\varphi_{k+1}
consists of
p

suitably scaled copies of
\varphi_{k}
.) Observe first that

\varphi_{k}(0)=0
and
\varphi_{k}(1)=1
by induction. Note also that if

t_{0}=\frac{j_{0}}{p}
for
j_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
, then the
definition (
A.5
) can be applied with both
j=j_{0}-1
and

j=j_{0}
. The first choice gives

\varphi_{k+1}\left(t_{0}\right)=\frac{\gamma_{p-j_{0}}}{A}\varphi_{k}(1)+\frac{\sum_{p-j_{0}+1\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}=\frac{\sum_{p-j_{0}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A},

(A.6)

and the second one gives

\varphi_{k+1}\left(t_{0}\right)=\frac{\gamma_{p-j_{0}-1}}{A}\varphi_{k}(0)+\frac{\sum_{p-j_{0}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}=\frac{\sum_{p-j_{0}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}.

(A.7)

Since these are equal, the definition (
A.5
) is consistent. It
is now obvious by induction that
\varphi_{k}
is continuous and
strictly increasing.

We claim that

\left|\varphi_{k+1}(t)-\varphi_{k}(t)\right|\leq\left(\frac{\max_{0\leq j<p}\gamma_{j}}{A}\right)^{k}\qquad\textrm{for all $k\geq 0$ and }t\in[0,1].

(A.8)

We prove this by induction. The case
k=0
is clear, since

\left|\varphi_{1}(t)-\varphi_{0}(t)\right|\leq 1
. Assume now that
(
A.8
) holds for
k-1
. If
\frac{j}{p}\leq t\leq\,\frac{j+1}{p}
then

\displaystyle\left|\varphi_{k+1}(t)-\varphi_{k}(t)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{p-j-1}}{A}\left|\varphi_{k}(pt-j)-\varphi_{k-1}(pt-j)\right|

(A.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{p-j-1}}{A}\left(\frac{\max_{0\leq j<p}\gamma_{j}}{A}\right)^{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\max_{0\leq j<p}\gamma_{j}}{A}\right)^{k}.

Thus, (
A.8
) holds, and since
\max_{0\leq j<p}\gamma_{j}/A<1
,
it follows that the sequence
\varphi_{k}
converges uniformly to a
function
\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
. By (
A.5
),
\varphi
satisfies
(
A.3
). Since each
\varphi_{k}
is continuous and strictly
increasing, the limiting function
\varphi
is continuous and
non-decreasing.

We next prove by induction that

\varphi_{N}\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}b_{j}p^{-j}\right)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}A^{-j}\left(\sum_{p-b_{j}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j}\gamma_{p-1-b_{i}}\right),

(A.10)

for any
N\geq 0
, where
b_{j}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
. This is
trivial for
N=0
. Suppose that (
A.10
) holds for
N
. Let

t=\sum_{1\leq j\leq N+1}b_{j}p^{-j}
, where

b_{j}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,p-1\}
. Then, by (
A.5
),

\displaystyle\varphi_{N+1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{p-b_{1}-1}}{A}\varphi_{N}\left(pt-b_{1}\right)+\frac{\sum_{p-b_{1}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{p-b_{1}-1}}{A}\left(\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}A^{-j}\left(\sum_{p-b_{j+1}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j}\gamma_{p-1-b_{i+1}}\right)\right)+\frac{\sum_{p-b_{1}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\leq j\leq N+1}A^{-j}\left(\sum_{p-b_{j}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j}\gamma_{p-1-b_{i}}\right)+\frac{\sum_{p-b_{1}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j\leq N+1}A^{-j}\left(\sum_{p-b_{j}\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}\right)\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j}\gamma_{p-1-b_{i}}\right).

(A.11)

Hence (
A.10
) holds for
N+1
, and thus it holds in general by
induction.

For any
p
-adic rational
t=\sum_{1\leq j\leq M}b_{j}p^{-j}
, let

b_{j}:=0
for
j>M
and apply (
A.10
) with
N\geq M
. Letting

N\to\infty
, we see that (
A.4
) holds for
t
. Since we have
shown that
\varphi
is continuous, it follows that (
A.4
) holds in
general, for any
\sum_{1\leq j<\infty}b_{j}p^{-j}
. Furthermore, it
follows from (
A.10
) that for every
N\geq M
, we have

\varphi_{N}(t)=\varphi_{M}(t).

(A.12)

Thus
\varphi(t)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\varphi_{N}(t)=\varphi_{M}(t)
. Accordingly,

\varphi(t)=\varphi_{N}(t)<\varphi_{N}(t+p^{-N})=\varphi(t+p^{-N}).

(A.13)

This shows that
\varphi
is strictly increasing on
p
-adic
rationals. In general, for
0\leq s_{1}<s_{2}\leq 1
, there exist

p
-adic rationals
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
such that
s_{1}\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq s_{2}
. Then

\varphi(s_{1})\leq\varphi(t_{1})<\varphi(t_{2})\leq\varphi(s_{2}).

(A.14)

Consequently,
\varphi
is strictly increasing.
∎

We next extend
f(n)
to a function of a real variable
x\geq 1
by

f(n+t):=(1-\varphi(t))f(n)+\varphi(t)f(n+1)

(A.15)

for
n\geq 1
and
0\leq t\leq 1
.

Lemma A.2
.

Assume that the recurrence (
A.1
) holds. Then

f(x)=Af\left(\frac{x}{p}\right)\qquad\textrm{for all real }x\geq p.

(A.16)

Proof.

Define

A_{k}^{-}:=\sum_{0\leq i<k}\gamma_{i}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad A_{k}^{+}:=\sum_{k\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}.

(A.17)

Rewrite (
A.1
) as

f(pn+j)=A_{p-j}^{-}f(n)+A_{p-j}^{+}f(n+1)

(A.18)

for
n\geq 1
and
j=0,1,\ldots,p-1
; note that for
j=0
(
A.18
)
is
f(pn)=Af(n)
, and it follows that (
A.18
) holds for
j=p

too. Also rewrite (
A.3
) as

\gamma_{p-1-j}\varphi(pt)=A\varphi\left(\frac{j}{p}+t\right)-A_{p-j}^{+}

(A.19)

for
0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{p}
and
j=0,1,\ldots,p-1
.

Now, for
x\geq p
, write

x=pn+j+pt,

(A.20)

where

n=\left\lfloor\frac{x}{p}\right\rfloor,\quad j=\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor\ \mathrm{mod\ }p,\quad t=\frac{\{x\}}{p}.

(A.21)

Then, by (
A.15
), (
A.18
) and (
A.19
),

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(pn+j+pt)

\displaystyle=(1-\varphi(pt))f(pn+j)+\varphi(pt)f(pn+j+1)

\displaystyle=(1-\varphi(pt))\left(A_{p-j}^{-}f(n)+A_{p-j}^{+}f(n+1)\right)

(A.22)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\varphi(pt)\left(A_{p-j-1}^{-}f(n)+A_{p-j-1}^{+}f(n+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(A_{p-j}^{-}f(n)+A_{p-j}^{+}f(n+1)\right)-\varphi(pt)\gamma_{p-j-1}\left(f(n)-f(n+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(A_{p-j}^{-}f(n)+A_{p-j}^{+}f(n+1)\right)-\left(A\varphi\left(\frac{j}{p}+t\right)-A_{p-j}^{+}\right)\left(f(n)-f(n+1)\right)

\displaystyle=Af(n)-A\varphi\left(\frac{j}{p}+t\right)f(n)+A\varphi\left(\frac{j}{p}+t\right)f(n+1)

\displaystyle=A\left(\left(1-\varphi\left(\frac{j}{p}+t\right)\right)f(n)+\varphi\left(\frac{j}{p}+t\right)f(n+1)\right)

\displaystyle=Af\left(n+\frac{j}{p}+t\right)

\displaystyle=Af\left(\frac{x}{p}\right),

(A.23)

which proves (
A.16
).
∎

Theorem A.3
.

Assume that the recurrence (
A.1
) holds, with

\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{p-1}>0
. Then

f(n)=n^{\varrho}\mathcal{P}\left(\log_{p}n\right)\qquad\textrm{for all }n\geq 1,

(A.24)

where
\varrho:=\log_{p}A
and

\mathcal{P}(t):=A^{-\{t\}}f(p^{\{t\}})

(A.25)

is a continuous
1
-periodic function.

Moreover, if the initial values satisfy

f(j)=\sum_{p-j\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}\qquad\textrm{for }j=1,\ldots,p-1

(A.26)

then

\mathcal{P}(t)=A^{1-\{t\}}\varphi(p^{\{t\}-1}),

(A.27)

where
\varphi
is defined in Lemma
A.1
.

Proof.

Since
f(x)
is continuous,
\mathcal{P}(t)
is continuous on
[0,1)
, and by
(
A.16
)

\lim_{t\nearrow 1}\mathcal{P}(t)=A^{-1}f(p)=f(1)=\mathcal{P}(0)=\mathcal{P}(1),

(A.28)

which shows that
\mathcal{P}(t)
is a continuous 1-periodic
function. For
y\in[1,p)

f(y)=f(p^{\log_{p}y})=A^{\log_{p}y}\left(A^{-\log_{p}y}f(p^{\log_{p}y})\right)=A^{\log_{p}y}\mathcal{P}\left(\log_{p}y\right),

(A.29)

and, for each
x\geq 1
,

p^{-\lfloor\log_{p}x\rfloor}x\in[1,p).

(A.30)

By applying Lemma
A.2
repeatedly
\lfloor\log_{p}x\rfloor
times:

\begin{split}f(x)&=A^{\lfloor\log_{p}x\rfloor}f\left(p^{-\lfloor\log_{p}x\rfloor}x\right)\\
&=A^{\lfloor\log_{p}x\rfloor+\log_{p}\left(p^{-\lfloor\log_{p}x\rfloor}x\right)}\mathcal{P}\left(\log_{p}\left(p^{-\lfloor\log_{p}x\rfloor}x\right)\right)\\
&=A^{\log_{p}x}\mathcal{P}\left(\log_{p}x\right).\end{split}

(A.31)

Thus we get

f(n)=A^{\log_{p}n}\mathcal{P}\left(\log_{p}n\right)=p^{\varrho\log_{p}n}\mathcal{P}\left(\log_{p}n\right)=n^{\varrho}\mathcal{P}\left(\log_{p}n\right),

(A.32)

proving (
A.24
).

Finally, suppose that condition (
A.26
) holds. For
1\leq x<p
, let

j=\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
. Then, using (
A.15
) and (
A.3
),

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(j)+\varphi(x-j)\left(f(j+1)-f(j)\right)

(A.33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p-j\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}+\varphi(x-j)\gamma_{p-j-1}

\displaystyle=A\left(\frac{\gamma_{p-j-1}}{A}\varphi(x-j)+\frac{\sum_{p-j\leq i<p}\gamma_{i}}{A}\right)

\displaystyle=A\varphi\left(\frac{x}{p}\right).

Thus we have

\mathcal{P}(t)=A^{-\{t\}}f(p^{\{t\}})=A^{1-\{t\}}\varphi(p^{\{t\}-1}).\qed

(A.34)

Corollary A.4
.

Assume that the recurrence (
A.1
) holds, with

\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{p-1}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\varrho}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\varrho}}=\max_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{P}(t),

(A.35)

\displaystyle\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\varrho}}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\varrho}}=\min_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{P}(t).

(A.36)

Proof.

By (
A.24
), since
\mathcal{P}(t)
is a continuous 1-periodic function.
∎

1.1.
General Strategy and Relation to Previous Work

Our general strategy is to prove an inverse-type theorem of the following form

Theorem 3

For each

\|\Lambda-\Lambda_{Siegel}\|_{U^{3}([N])}\geq\delta.

\|\mu-\mu_{Siegel}\|_{U^{2}([N])}\geq\epsilon

\|\Lambda-\Lambda_{Siegel}\|_{U^{2}([N])}\geq\epsilon.

From there, since we have quasi-polynomial upper bounds on
\|\mu-\mu_{Siegel}\|_{U^{2}([N])}
and
\|\Lambda-\Lambda_{Siegel}\|_{U^{2}([N])}

is quasi-polynomial in

\delta

1.2.
Remark on the Quantitative Equidistribution Theory of Two-Step Nilsequences

Here, we will briefly explain why the equidistribution estimates considered here are single exponential in dimension and thus quantitatively speaking significantly better than
[
GT3
]
. The reason is that we invoke the geometry of numbers which allows us to eliminate many rational relations at once. In
[
JT
]
, the authors construct, given a locally quadratic form on a Bohr set, a nilsequence on (essentially
1
1

1

The explicit ambient nilmanifold that the construction of
[
JT
]
lies on is a three-step nilmanifold, but the nilsequence that
[
JT
]
constructs lies on a coset of a two-step nilmanifold.
) a two-step nilmanifold that corresponds to it. Viewing the equidistribution theory of locally quadratic forms in this form, we see that the conclusion of
[
GT5
, Proposition 4.11]
or
[
GW
, Corollary 6.2]
implies that the locally quadratic form has roughly rational coefficients when written in local coordinates. This can be combined with Vinogradov’s Lemma (e.g. ?THM?
) to show that the degree one and degree two components of
\text{Poly}_{\leq 2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the construction of the nilsequence (denoted
\Phi(h,\theta)(n)
in
[
JT
]
) are roughly rational, which implies that, modulo some rational relation, the nilsequence lies on (a coset of) an abelian nilmanifold.

This process is more efficient than Green-Tao’s original approach of locating a single horizontal character to quotient the nilpotent Lie group by to lower the dimension, since the horizontal character depends on the complexity of the nilmanifold, which can increase when we take a quotient by the horizontal character. This leads to losses double exponential in dimension.

3.
Fourier Complexity

The purpose of this section is to make rigorous and automate the “Fourier approximation” heuristic argument as mentioned in the introduction. We recall from discussion of the introduction that while analyzing oscillatory sums over objects that aren’t quite abelian groups or arithmetic progressions, we may sometimes end up with cutoff terms while invoking Cauchy-Schwarz. The example mentioned in the introduction was

\mathbb{E}_{\ell\in[L,2L],m\in[M,2M]}\psi(\ell m)e(\phi(\ell m))\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

In a similar looking sum appearing later in our argument, it becomes desirable to eliminate the term
\psi
. The method used here to eliminate such terms is to Fourier approximate such a
\psi
.

A useful notion in additive combinatorics is
Fourier Complexity
, which measures how many Fourier phases
n\mapsto e(\alpha n)
captures the behavior of
n\mapsto f(n)
. Specifically, we say that a function
f\colon[N]\to\mathbb{C}
has
L^{p}

\delta
-Fourier complexity
at most
M
if we can write

f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e(\alpha_{i}n)+g

where
\|g\|_{L^{p}}\leq\delta
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}|a_{i}|\leq M
. If
p=\infty
, we shall refer to
L^{p}

\delta
-Fourier complexity as just
\delta
-Fourier complexity and if
\delta=0
, we will refer
\delta
-Fourier complexity as
Fourier complexity
.

Lemma 3.1

(Fourier/Fejer Expansion lemma)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous function with Lipschitz norm at most
L
, meaning that
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\|f\|_{Lip(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq L
. Then we may write

f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e(n_{i}x)+g

where
\sum_{i}^{k}|a_{i}|\leq C^{d^{2}}L\delta^{-2d^{2}-d}
and
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 3\delta
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a smooth compactly supported function supported in
[-1,1]
and with integral one. Let
Q_{\delta}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\delta^{-1}\phi(x_{i}/\delta)
and let
K=Q_{\delta}*Q_{\delta}
be a Fejer-type kernel. Since
|\hat{\phi}(\xi)|\ll_{k}|\xi|^{-k}
,

|\hat{K}(\xi)|\leq C^{d}\delta^{2d}\xi^{-2}

for some constant
C
it follows that the Fourier coefficients of
K
larger than
M
contributes at most
C^{d}M^{-1}\delta^{2d}
. In addition, for
M=C^{-d}\delta^{-2d-1}\|f\|_{Lip}^{-1}

\|f-f*K\|_{\infty}\leq\int|f(x)-f(y)|K(x-y)dy=\int zK(z)dz\leq 2\delta

since
K
has integral one and is supported on
|x|\leq 2\delta
. Set

h(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|k|\leq M}\hat{f}(k)\hat{K}(k)e(kx).

Then by Fourier inversion formula, it follows that

\|h-f*K\|_{\infty}\leq\delta.

Thus,
\|h-f\|_{\infty}\leq 3\delta
. The sum of the Fourier coefficients of
H
is at most
LC^{-d^{2}}\delta^{-2d^{2}-d}
.
∎

Thus, if
f(n)=F(n\alpha)
where
f\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is Lipschitz continuous and has Lipschitz norm at most
L
, then
f
has
\delta
-Fourier complexity at most
O(L/\delta)^{O(d^{2})}
. Such a sequence
(F(n\alpha))_{n\in[N]}
is known as a
degree one nilsequence
. We say that a sequence

is an
\epsilon
-
approximate degree one nilsequence
if there exists a degree one nilsequence

such that

\mathbb{E}_{n\in[N]}|F(\alpha n)-g(n)|\leq\epsilon.

A frequent example of an approximate degree one nilsequence in this article is an indicator function of a regular Bohr set. Regularity is not strictly necessary but not assuming regularity introduces some unnecessary complications (that can be dealt with using similar arguments to those in

4.
Reducing to Equidistribution Estimates

In this section, we shall turn the condition

\|\mu-\mu_{Siegel}\|_{U^{3}([N])}\geq\delta

and

\|\Lambda-\Lambda_{Siegel}\|_{U^{3}([N])}\geq\delta

into a condition that an oscillatory sum is large, ultimately arriving at the hypotheses of ?THM?
. In order to do so, we must first apply the

This gives us freedom to swap orders of summation and to Cauchy-Schwarz away undesirable terms while still being able to exploit cancellation in phase. In contrast, with a single sum, “Cauchy-Schwarzing” away undesirable terms is still possible, but one cannot hope to exploit any cancellation in phase once we do so.

4.2.
Type I, Twisted Type I, and Type II Sums

A
type I sum
is a sum of the form

n\mapsto\sum_{d\leq R}a_{d}1_{d|n}1_{[N^{\prime}]}(n)

where
a_{n}\ll\tau(n)^{k}
for some absolute constant
k
,
R\geq\exp(O(\log^{1/C}(N)))
is a parameter that is at least quasi-polynomial in
N
, and
\tau(n)
is the number of divisors of
n
. A
twisted type I sum
is a sum of the form

n\mapsto\sum_{d\leq R}a_{d}1_{d|n}\chi_{Siegel}(n/d)1_{[N^{\prime}]}(n).

Note that if
c
is a type I sum, and if

|\mathbb{E}_{n\in[N]}f(n)c(n)|\geq\delta

then

\left|\mathbb{E}_{n}\sum_{d\leq R}f(n)a_{d}1_{d|n}1_{[N^{\prime}]}(n)\right|\geq\delta.

The point of these type I sums is that we may interchange sums and use the triangle inequality:

\sum_{d\leq R}\tau(d)^{k}\left|\sum_{n\in[N^{\prime}],d|n}f(n)\right|\gg\delta N.

Since we have divisor moment estimates

\sum_{d\leq R}\frac{\tau(d)^{k}}{d}\ll\log^{O(1)}(N)

it follows from Cauchy-Schwarz that

\sum_{d\leq R}d\left|\sum_{n\in[N^{\prime}],d|n}f(n)\right|^{2}\gg\frac{\delta^{2}N^{2}}{\log^{O(1)}(N)}.

By dyadic pigeonholing, it follows that there exists
D\leq R/2
such that for
\frac{\delta D}{\log^{O(1)}(N)}
many
d\in[D,2D]
, we have

\left|\sum_{n\in[N^{\prime}],d|n}f(n)\right|\gg\frac{\delta N}{D\log^{O(1)}(N)}.

Thus, if
f
were an oscillatory, one turns a correlation with
a
into an oscillatory sum for which equidistribution theories can apply. A similar calculation occurs for twisted type I sums, obtaining

\left|\sum_{n\in[N^{\prime}],d|n}f(n)\chi(n/d)\right|\gg\frac{\delta N}{D\log^{O(1)}(N)}

with
d\in[D,2D]\leq R/2
.

A
type II sum
is a sum of the form

n\mapsto\sum_{d,w>\sqrt{R},dw=n}a_{d}b_{w}.

where
a_{d},b_{w}\ll\tau(n)^{k}
for some absolute
k
and as above,
R
is a parameter that is at least quasi-polynomial in
N
. Now suppose
c
is a type II sum and that

\left|\sum_{n\in[\delta N,N]}f(n)c(n)\right|\geq\delta N.

The reason we opt to work with
[\delta N,N]
will be clearer later in the argument. In hypotheses such as

\left|\sum_{n\in[N]}f(n)c(n)\right|\geq\delta N

with
c
either
\mu,\mu_{Siegel},\Lambda,\Lambda_{Siegel}
, we may, at the cost of shrinking
\delta
to
\delta^{2}
, replace
[N]
with
[\delta N,N]
. Since
\delta\ll_{A}\log^{-A}(N)
, all of the contribution of the sum for
n\leq\delta^{2}N
will be smaller than
\delta N
. Substituting the type II sum, we obtain

\left|\sum_{n\in[\delta N,N]}\sum_{d,w>\sqrt{R},dw=n}a_{d}b_{w}f(n)\right|\geq\delta N.

The point is that we may use Cauchy-Schwarz to turn this type II sum into a type I sum which can be handled similarly as above. Using Cauchy-Schwarz and the divisor moment bound

\sum_{d\in[\sqrt{R},N/\sqrt{R}]}\tau(d)^{k}\left|\sum_{w\in[\delta N/d,N/d]}f(n)\right|\gg\delta N,

we obtain

\sum_{\sqrt{R}<d<N/\sqrt{R}}d\left|\sum_{\delta N/d\leq w\leq N/d}b_{w}f(dw)\right|^{2}\gg\frac{\delta^{2}N^{2}}{\log^{O(1)}(N)}.

Using dyadic pigeonholing, and divisor bounds once more, we find that there exists
D\in[\sqrt{R},N/\sqrt{R}]
and
W
such that

\sum_{d\in[D/2,D]}\left|\sum_{w\in[W/2,W]}1_{[\delta N/d,N/d]}(w)b_{w}f(dw)\right|^{2}\gg\frac{\delta^{2}N^{2}}{D\log^{O(1)}(N)}

and
\delta/4N\leq DW\leq N
. Fourier approximating
1_{[\delta N/d,N/d]}
via ?THM?
, and expanding, we find that

\sum_{d\in[D/2,D]}\sum_{w,w^{\prime}\in[W/2,W]}e(\alpha(w-w^{\prime}))b_{w}\overline{b_{w^{\prime}}}f(dw)\overline{f(dw^{\prime})}\gg\frac{\delta^{O(1)}N^{2}}{D\log^{O(1)}(N)}.

Thus

\mathbb{E}_{d\in[D/2,D]}\mathbb{E}_{w,w^{\prime}\in[W/2,W]}b(w,w^{\prime})f(dw)\overline{f(dw^{\prime})}\gg\frac{\delta^{O(1)}}{\log^{O(1)}(N)}

for some divisor power bounded function

∎

If the conclusion of Section 4.1 holds, e.g.,

(3)

|\mathbb{E}_{n\in[N]}(\mu-\mu_{Siegel})(n)\psi(n)e(\phi(n))|\geq\delta^{O(|S|)^{O(1)}}

or

for

, and ?THM?

If

In this case, we do not have a twisted type I sum after we apply ?THM?
, and the setting is significantly simpler. In particular, ?THM?
applies but without any twisted type I hypothesis. Thus, we shall assume that

. The below theorem takes the application of ?THM?
to the conclusion of Section 4.1 and the above discussion as hypotheses and outputs a conclusion about the behavior of the locally quadratic form

on

Theorem 4
.

Let
\phi\colon B=B(S,64\rho)\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be a locally quadratic form with
\rho\gg\delta^{C}
,
|S|\leq\log^{C}(1/\delta)

be the associated bilinear form defined above in Section 2. Let

(1)

(Type I/twisted type I case) There exists

(2)

(3)

(4)

(Degenerate Case II)

(5)

(Degenerate Case III)

do not hold, we show ?THM?
. We shall take

with the following lemma combined with ?THM?
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose

-approximate degree one nilsequence with

We may cover

The claim then follows from ?THM?
and ?THM?
.
∎

Thus, we may essentially replace

with a Fourier phase, leading to the conclusion that

Finally, by ?THM?

concluding the proof of ?THM?

.

5.
Controlling the Type I and Twisted Type I Sum

In this section, we will control the type I and twisted type I sums, ultimately turning the property that
e(\phi)
satisfies some oscillatory inequality as in hypotheses (1) and (2) of ?THM?
to a property that
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is small on some subset of a Bohr set. We will use this information in Section 7 to obtain conclusion (1) of ?THM?
. The main tool that allows us to exploit cancellation in the oscillatory sum is ?THM?
which offers a “coordinate-free” method to obtain an equidistribution-like estimate for quadratic phases on Bohr sets rather than an alternate method of
[
GW
]
which passes to a generalized arithmetic progression. Since generalized arithmetic progressions don’t behave well with respect to multiplication, it seems necessary to to use a slightly different approach from
[
GW
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\phi\colon h+B=h+B(S,64\rho)\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be a locally quadratic form with
\rho\gg\delta^{-C}
,
|S|\leq\log^{C}(1/\delta)
and
h\in[N]
. Let
\psi_{h}=\psi(\cdot-h)
be a smooth approximant to
1_{B+h}
with support in
B+h
. Suppose we have the estimate
q_{Siegel}\leq\delta^{-O(|S|)^{O(1)}}
. Let
L\leq\sqrt{R}
. Suppose
N^{\prime}\geq\delta^{2}N
and that for
d\ll L
that

\left|\sum_{n\in[N^{\prime}/d]}\psi_{h}(dn)e(\phi(dn))\right|\geq\delta N/d

or

\left|\sum_{n\in[N^{\prime}/d]}\psi_{h}(dn)\chi_{Siegel}(n)e(\phi(dn))\right|\geq\delta N/d.

Then for
a\in[N]
chosen so that
ad\in B(S,\rho\delta^{O(|S|)^{O(1)}})
,

\|\phi^{\prime\prime}(ad,ad)\|_{Q,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq\delta^{-O(|S|)^{O(1)}}\|ad\|_{S}^{2}\rho^{-2}.

Proof.

As noted before, we shall work with
B
instead of
h+B
since all of the estimates still hold for
h+B
. In particular, ?THM?
holds for
\phi
defined on a translation
h+B
where we note that its second derivative
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is still defined on
B\times B
(rather than
(h+B)\times(h+B)
). We start with the hypotheses

\left|\sum_{n\in[N]}\psi(dn)1_{[N^{\prime}/d]}(n)e(\phi(dn))\right|\geq\delta N/d

and

\left|\sum_{n\in[N]}\psi(dn)1_{[N^{\prime}/d]}(n)\chi_{Siegel}(n)e(\phi(dn))\right|\geq\delta N/d.

The second hypothesis is effectively the same as the first hypothesis because
\chi_{Siegel}
is a major arc because it is periodic with respect to the conductor of the Siegel zero. Since we have reduced to the case of
q_{Siegel}\leq\delta^{-O(|S|)^{O(1)}}
, we may safely handle the second case at the cost of replacing
\delta
with
\delta^{O(|S|)^{O(1)}}
. Let us now consider only the type I case. Let
a\in[N]
be such that
da\in B(S,\delta^{O(|S|)^{O(1)}}\rho/2)
. Let
A=\frac{\|da\|_{S}}{\rho}
. Then

\sum_{n\in[N]}\psi(dn)1_{[N^{\prime}/d]}(n)e(\phi(dn))=\sum_{n\in[N]}\mathbb{E}_{q\in[-A,A]}\psi(d(n+qa))1_{[N^{\prime}/d]}(n+qa)e(\phi(d(n+aq)))\geq\delta N/d.

By the pigeonhole principle, we have for some
n
,

|\mathbb{E}_{q\in[-A,A]}\psi(d(n+qa))1_{[N^{\prime}/d]}(n+qa)e(\phi(d(n+qa)))|\geq\delta.

The next lemma, which is
[
GT4
, Corollary 9.1]
, tells us how to utilize quadratic structure to obtain a quadratic polynomial in
q
.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{T}
such that
\phi(dn+dqa)=\frac{q(q-1)}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(ad,ad)+\alpha q+\beta
.

Proof.

We shall prove this by induction on
q
. From the identity
\phi(n+a+b)-\phi(n+a)-\phi(n+b)+\phi(n)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(a,b)
with
a=b=da
, we obtain

\phi(dn+d(q+2)a)-2\phi(dn+d(q+1)a)+\phi(dn+dqa)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(da,da).

The claim of
q=0,1
follows vacuously from the claim. By induction on
q
, there exists
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{T}
such that

\phi(dn+d(q+2)a)-q(q+1)\phi^{\prime\prime}(da,da)-2\alpha(q+1)-2\beta+\frac{q(q-1)}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(da,da)+\alpha q+\beta=\phi^{\prime\prime}(da,da).

Rearranging, it follows that

\phi(dn+d(q+2)a)=\frac{(q+2)(q+1)}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(ad,ad)+(q+2)\alpha+\beta.

∎

Since by ?THM?
and
1_{[N^{\prime}/d]}
has
\delta^{4}
-Fourier complexity at most
\delta^{-O(1)}
, there exists
\alpha
such that

\left|\mathbb{E}_{q\in[-A,A]}e\left(\frac{1}{2}q(q-1)\phi^{\prime\prime}(ad,ad)+\alpha q\right)\right|\geq\delta^{O(|S|)^{O(1)}}.

Thus, by ?THM?
, it follows that

\|\phi^{\prime\prime}(ad,ad)\|_{Q,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq\delta^{-O(|S|)^{O(1)}}A^{-2}=\delta^{-O(|S|)^{O(1)}}\|ad\|_{S}^{2}\rho^{-2}.

∎

6.
Controlling the Type II sum

In this section, we shall analyze the oscillatory type II as in hypothesis (3) of ?THM?
, ultimately aiming to prove that
\phi^{\prime\prime}
is small on a somewhat regular subset of
B(S,\rho)
. We will use this information to obtain conclusion (2) of ?THM?
. ?THM?
is the main tool to analyze the equidistribution sum in the type II sum similar to how ?THM?
is the main tool to analyze the equidistribution sum of the type I sum. Since type II sums also involve multiplication in the domain of
\phi
, we also similarly cannot straightforwardly pass to a generalized arithmetic progression as in
[
GW
]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
L,M
be chosen such that
\delta N\leq LM\leq N
,
\sqrt{R}\leq L\leq N/\sqrt{R}
with
\delta>0
. Let
\phi:h+B=h+B(S,64\rho)\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be a locally quadratic form and
\psi_{h}=\psi(\cdot-h)
be a smooth approximant to
1_{B+h}
with support in
B+h

|\mathbb{E}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[L,2L],m,m^{\prime}\in[M,2M]}\psi_{h}(m\ell)\psi_{h}(m^{\prime}\ell)\psi_{h}(m\ell^{\prime})\psi_{h}(m^{\prime}\ell^{\prime})e(\phi(m\ell)-\phi(m^{\prime}\ell)-\phi(m\ell^{\prime})+\phi(m^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}))|\geq\delta.

Then there exists an absolute constant
C
such that for
\delta^{-Cr^{C}}\leq K\ll R^{1/8}
,
a\in[L/2K,L/K]
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
\|ab\|_{S}\leq\frac{1}{K^{2}}
and
|ab|\leq N
, we have

\|\phi^{\prime\prime}(ab,ab)\|_{Q,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq\delta^{-O(|S|)^{O(1)}}/K^{4}

for
Q\leq\delta^{-O(|S|)^{O(1)}}
.

Proof.

As before, we will work with the constraint of
h=0
since all of the estimates of
h=0
work for in the more general case of
\phi
being defined on a translation
h+B
; in particular, ?THM?
is proved in
[
GT4
, lemma 9.2]
for translations of Bohr sets rather than Bohr sets so it still holds in this case. We begin with the constraint

|\mathbb{E}_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}\in[L,2L],m,m^{\prime}\in[M,2M]}\psi(m\ell)\psi(m^{\prime}\ell)\psi(m\ell^{\prime})\psi(m^{\prime}\ell^{\prime})e(\phi(m\ell)-\phi(m^{\prime}\ell)-\phi(m\ell^{\prime})+\phi(m^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}))|\geq\delta

By pigeonholing in
\ell,m
(in particular eliminating
\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}
), we obtain

|\mathbb{E}_{\ell\in[L,2L],m\in[M,2M]}\psi(\ell m)e(\phi(m\ell))b(\ell)b(m)|\geq\delta

where in this proof,
b
denotes a function whose absolute value is bounded by
1
. Let
a\in[L/K,L/2K]
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that
\|ab\|_{S}\leq K^{-2}
and
|ab|\leq\frac{N}{2}
. Then since
|ab|\leq N\|ab\|_{S}
(since we specified in Section 2 that we work with the convention that
1\in S
), it follows that
|b|\leq\frac{N\|ab\|_{S}}{a}\leq\frac{N}{KL}\leq\delta^{-1}\frac{M}{K}
. Taking
U=V=\frac{\delta^{2}K}{4}
, it follows that
U|a|\leq\delta L
and
V|b|\leq\delta M
. Thus,
\|ab\|_{S}\leq\frac{\delta^{-3}}{UV}
with
U|a|\leq\frac{\delta}{2}L
,
V|b|\leq\frac{\delta}{2}M
. Our initial goal is to replace an average in
m\ell
with an average in
[U]
and
[V]
over small multiples of
a
and
b
. To see why that may be helpful, we have the following lemma from
[
GT4
, Lemma 9.2]
:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\partial_{y}(f)=f(x+y)-f(x)
and define
f(v,w)=(m+va)(\ell+wb)
. Suppose

UV\|ab\|_{S}\leq\rho

and for all

,

for any choice of
\omega_{i,j}\in\{0,1\}

\partial^{1}_{u_{1},u_{2}}\partial^{2}_{v_{1},v_{2}}\phi(f(u_{0},v_{0}))=2u_{1}u_{2}v_{1}v_{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(ab,ab)

where
\partial^{1}
applies in the first variable and
\partial^{2}
applies in the second variable.

Proof.

By replacing
m
and
\ell
by
m+u_{0}a
and
\ell+v_{0}b
, we may assume that

are zero. Since

and

are in

and

\partial_{v_{1},v_{2}}\phi(f(\omega_{1,1}u_{1}+\omega_{1,2}u_{2},0))=\phi(n+h+k)-\phi(n+h)-\phi(n+k)+\phi(n).

Since

\phi^{\prime\prime}(h_{1},h_{2})=\phi^{\prime\prime}((m+\omega_{1,1}au_{1}+\omega_{1,2}au_{2})bv_{1},(m+\omega_{1,1}au_{1}+\omega_{1,2}au_{2})bv_{2}).

Using bilinearity, and the fact that
UV\|ab\|_{S}\leq\rho
and
u_{i}a\ell
and
v_{i}bm
are in
B(S,2\rho)
we can expand this in terms of

2\omega_{1,1}\omega_{1,2}u_{0}u_{1}v_{0}v_{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(ab,ab)+[\text{Lower Order Terms}]

where
[\text{lower order terms}]
are degree one terms in
\omega_{1,1}
and
\omega_{1,2}
. Taking an alternating sum in
\omega_{1,1}
and
\omega_{1,2}
which varies in

\partial_{u_{1},u_{2}}^{1}\partial_{v_{1},v_{2}}^{2}\phi(f(u_{0},v_{1}))=2u_{1}u_{2}v_{1}v_{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(ab,ab).

∎

This lemma tells us that it would be helpful if we were to convert an average in
L
and
M
into one along arithmetic progressions which are multiplies of

and

so that once we use Cauchy-Schwarz or a van der Corput type estimate sufficiently enough times, we obtain a genuine multidimensional polynomial that we may control. Now we average over

and

|\mathbb{E}_{\ell\in[L,2L],m\in[M,2M]}\psi(\ell m)e(\phi(m\ell))b(\ell)b(m)|\leq

|\mathbb{E}_{\ell\in[L,2L],m\in[M,2M]}\mathbb{E}_{u\in[U],v\in[V]}\psi((\ell+ua)(m+bv))e(\phi((\ell+ua)(m+vb)))b(\ell+ua)b(m+vb)|+\frac{\delta}{2}

and thus there exists

such that

|\mathbb{E}_{u\in[U],v\in[V]}\psi((\ell+ua)(m+vb))e(\phi((\ell+ua)(m+vb)))b(u)b(v)|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to eliminate
u
and

|\mathbb{E}_{u,u^{\prime}\in[P],v,v^{\prime}\in[R]}\psi(h(u,v))\psi(h(u^{\prime},v))\psi(h(u,v^{\prime}))\psi(h(u^{\prime},v^{\prime}))

e(\phi(h(u,v))-\phi(h(u^{\prime},v))-\phi(h(u,v^{\prime}))+\phi(h(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})))|\geq\frac{\delta^{4}}{16}

where

. Making a change of variables

and

|\mathbb{E}_{u\in[U],v\in[V],u_{0}\in[-U,U],v_{0}\in[-V,V]}\psi(h(u,v))\psi(h(u+u_{0},v))\psi(h(u,v+v_{0}))\psi(h(u+u_{0},v+v_{0}))

e(\partial_{u_{0},v_{0}}\phi(h(u,v)))|\geq\frac{\delta^{4}}{32}

where the average over

is over a “tent,” meaning that it is multiplied by
\nu_{U}(u_{0})=\max(0,1-|u_{0}|/|U|)

again, we obtain

|\mathbb{E}_{u\in[U],v\in[V],u_{0},u_{1}\in[-U,U],v_{0},v_{1}\in[-V,V]}\Psi(u,v,u_{0},v_{0},u_{1},v_{1})e(\partial_{u_{0},u_{1},v_{0},v_{1}}(\phi(h(u,v))))|\geq\frac{\delta^{8}}{1024}

where

with
\Delta_{y}f(x):=f(x+y)\overline{f(x)}
and the average over
[-U,U]
and
[-V,V]
is a tent average. The point is that

\partial_{(u_{0},v_{0}),(u_{1},v_{1})}\phi(u,v)=2u_{0}v_{0}u_{1}v_{1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(ab,ab)

so applying ?THM?
and since

|\mathbb{E}_{u,u^{\prime}\in[-U,U],v,v^{\prime}\in[-V,V]}e(2u_{0}v_{0}u_{1}v_{1}\phi^{\prime\prime}(ab,ab)+[\text{Lower Order Terms}])|\geq\delta^{O(|S|)^{O(1)}}

where

indicate degree three or lower multidimensional polynomials in

. By ?THM?

such that

Properties of Gowers Uniformity Norms

In this section, we will state without proof properties of Gowers uniformity norms. For a more comprehensive reference, see e.g.,
[
T2
]
and
[
TV
]
. Let
G
be a finite abelian group and
f\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
a function. As defined in Section 2, the Gowers
U^{s}
norm of
f
is defined as

\|f\|_{U^{s}(G)}^{2^{s}}=\mathbb{E}_{n,h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{s}\in G}\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s}}C^{|\omega|}f(x+\omega\cdot h)

where
|\omega|
denotes the number of ones in
\omega
and
C
denotes the conjugation operation
z\mapsto\bar{z}
. One can verify that the Gowers
U^{s}
norms are seminorms and that for
s\geq 2
, the Gowers
U^{s}
norms are indeed norms. The Gowers norms satisfy the below Cauchy-Schwarz-type inequality

Lemma C.1

(Cauchy-Schwarz-Gowers Inequality)
.

For
(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\colon G\to\mathbb{C}^{\{0,1\}^{s}}
, we define

\langle(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\rangle_{U^{s}(G)}:=\mathbb{E}_{n,h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{s}\in G}\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s}}C^{|\omega|}f_{\omega}(x+\omega\cdot h).

Then

|\langle(f_{\omega})_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\rangle_{U^{s}(G)}|\leq\prod_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{s}}\|f_{\omega}\|_{U^{s}(G)}.

The Gowers norms also satisfy the following properties:

Lemma C.2
.

Let
f\colon G\to\mathbb{C}
.

1.

\|f\|_{U^{i}(G)}\leq\|f\|_{U^{j}(G)}
whenever
i\leq j
.

2.

If
P\in\widehat{G}[x]
has degree at most
s
, then
\|fe(P)\|_{U^{s}(G)}=\|f\|_{U^{s}(G)}

3.

Then
\|f\|_{U^{2}(G)}^{4}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{G})}^{2}
.

The last of these properties is known as the
U^{2}
inverse theorem
. An analogous statement for the

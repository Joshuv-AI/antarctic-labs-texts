Spectral convergence of the Laplace operator with Robin boundary conditions on a small hole
Diana Barseghyan, Baruch Schneider

Abstract
In this paper we study a bounded domain with a small hole removed. Our main result concerns the spectrum of the Laplace operator with the Robin conditions imposed at the hole boundary. Moreover we prove that under some suitable assumptions on the parameter in the boundary condition the spectrum of the Laplacian converges in the Hausdorff distance sense to the spectrum of the Laplacian defined on the unperturbed domain.



Keywords.  Robin Laplacian, spectral convergence, domain with a hole.

Mathematics Subject Classification.  Primary: 58J50;  Secondary: 35P15, 47A10.

1
Introduction

It is a common expectation that small perturbations of the physical situation will lead to only a small change in the spectrum. In the case of domain perturbations, this is largely true for Dirichlet boundary conditions, while the Neumann or Robin case is more delicate. In the recent literature, such questions have already received quite a few answers, starting with the seminal work of Rauch and Taylor
on the spectrum of the Laplace operator of domains with holes
[
13
]
.

An excellent shortcut to the recent work on the asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues of the Laplace operator on the domain with small spherical obstacles imposing the Neumann condition at their boundary and the Dirichlet condition at the rest part of the boundary can be found in
[
11
]
.

Maz’ya, Nazarov and Plamenewskii, see
[
10
, Ch.9, vol.I]
, have considered the Laplace operator on the domain with obstacles, imposing the Dirichlet condition on their boundary and have proved the validity of a complete asymptotic expansion for the eigenvalues.

For a survey on more recent research in this subject, we refer the reader to
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
where authors have considered the Dirichlet Laplacians on Euclidean domains or manifolds with holes and studied the problems of the resolvent convergence.

The problems with small Neumann obstacles of more general geometry can cause abrupt changes in the spectrum. For example, such an effect is observed when the hole has a ”split ring” geometry, see
[
14
]
. The split ring (even if very small) can produce additional eigenvalues that have nothing in common with the eigenvalues of the Neumann Laplacian on the unperturbed domain. The problems with the Neumann obstacles having more general geometry have been studied in
[
5
]
and later in
[
2
]
.

The Robin case for general self-adjoint elliptic operators was considered in
[
8
]
but with the restriction that the boundary of the unperturbed domain is
C^{2}
- smooth. In the mentioned work shrinking the hole and scaling properly the parameter in the boundary condition, the authors obtain an operator family that converges, in the norm-resolvent sense, to an operator with a point interaction in the domain without the hole. Results on resolvent convergence for operators with Robin conditions in domains with small holes in higher dimensions were also considered in
[
3
]
, but in this paper the original domain must again be
C^{2}
-smooth and the Robin condition was used with the coefficient independent of the hole size.

In this paper we will focus our attention on the spectral properties of a Laplacian defined on a two-dimensional bounded domain with
no additional assumptions
on the smoothness of its boundary with a single hole
K_{\varepsilon}
(for a fixed parameter
\varepsilon
) having the Lipschitz boundary. On the boundary of the original domain we impose the Dirichlet or Neumann condition, and on the boundary of the hole we impose the Robin condition with the coefficient depending on the size of the hole.

Our main result is the proof of the spectral convergence, in the Hausdorff distance sense, of the spectrum of the Laplacian defined on the perturbed domain to the spectrum of the Laplacian defined on the original domain.

Main results

\displaystyle q_{2}(u)=\int_{\Omega_{K}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\,dy+\gamma_{K}\int_{\partial K}|u|_{K}^{2}\,d\mu,\quad u\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega_{K})\cap\mathcal{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\,,

3
Main tool of the spectral convergence of operators on varying Hilbert spaces

For the convenience of the reader, this section begins by reviewing some basic facts that ensure spectral convergence for two operators having different domains. For more information we refer the reader to
[
12
]
.
To a Hilbert space
H
with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
and norm
\|\cdot\|
together with a non-negative, unbounded operator
A
we associate the scale of Hilbert spaces

H_{k}:=\mathrm{Dom}((A+I)^{k/2}),\quad\|u\|_{k}:=\|(A+I)^{k/2}u\|,\,\,k\geq 0,

where
I
is the identity operator.

We think of
(H^{\prime},A^{\prime})
as some perturbation of
(H,A)
and want to relax the assumption so that the spectral properties are not the same, but still close.

Definition 1
.

(see
[
12
]
)
Suppose we have linear operators

\displaystyle J:H\longrightarrow H^{\prime},\quad\quad J_{1}:H_{1}\longrightarrow H_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle J^{\prime}:H^{\prime}\longrightarrow H,\quad\quad J_{1}^{\prime}:H_{1}^{\prime}\longrightarrow H_{1}.

Let
\delta>0
and
k\geq 1
.
We say that
(H,A)
and
(H^{\prime},A^{\prime})
are
\delta
-close of order
k
iff the following conditions are fulfilled:

\displaystyle\|Jf-J_{1}f\|_{0}\leq\delta\|f\|_{1},

(3.1)

\displaystyle|(Jf,u)-(f,J^{\prime}u)|\leq\delta\|f\|_{0}\|u\|_{0},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|u-JJ^{\prime}u\|_{0}\leq\delta\|u\|_{1},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\quad\|Jf\|_{0}\leq 2\|f\|_{0},\,\,\,\|J^{\prime}u\|_{0}\leq 2\|u\|_{0},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|(f-J^{\prime}Jf)\|_{0}\leq\delta\|f\|_{1},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|J^{\prime}u-J_{1}^{\prime}u\|_{0}\leq\delta\|u\|_{1}

(3.6)

\displaystyle|a(f,J_{1}^{\prime}u)-a^{\prime}(J_{1}f,u)|\leq\delta\|f\|_{k}\|u\|_{1},

(3.7)

for all
f,u
in the appropriate spaces. Here,
a
and
a^{\prime}
denote the sesquilinear forms associated to
A
and
A^{\prime}
.

We denote by
d_{\mathrm{Haussdorff}}(A,B)
the Hausdorff distance for subsets
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}
:

d_{\mathrm{Haussdorff}}(A,B):=\mathrm{max}\left\{\underset{a\in A}{\mathrm{sup}}\,d(a,B),\,\underset{b\in B}{\mathrm{sup}}\,d(b,A)\right\},

(3.8)

where
d(a,B):=\mathrm{inf}_{b\in B}|a-b|
. We set

\overline{d}(A,B):=d_{\mathrm{Hausdorff}}\left((A+I)^{-1},(B+I)^{-1}\right)

(3.9)

for closed subsets of
[0,\infty)
.

For the next result, which comes from the work of O. Post
[
12
]
, we have the following spectral convergence theorem in terms of the distance
\overline{d}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
12
]

There exists
\eta(\delta)>0
with
\eta(\delta)\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
such that

\overline{d}(\sigma_{\bullet}(A),\,\sigma_{\bullet}(A^{\prime}))\leq\eta(\delta)

(3.10)

for all pairs of non-negative operators and Hilbert spaces
(H,A)
and
(H^{\prime},A^{\prime})
that are
\delta
-close. Here,
\sigma_{\bullet}(A)
denotes either the entire spectrum, the essential or the discrete spectrum of
A
. Also, the multiplicity of the discrete spectrum,

\sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}
, is preserved, i.e. if
\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}(A)
has multiplicity
\mu>0
, then there exist
\mu
eigenvalues (not necessarily all distinct) of the operator
A^{\prime}
belonging to the interval
(\lambda-\eta(\delta),\lambda+\eta(\delta))
.

We now turn to the proof of Theorem
2.4
. Since the proof is almost the same for both the Dirichlet and Neumann cases, we will restrict ourselves to the Neumann case.
The only difference is Lemma
6.3
, but its validity for the Dirichlet case can be easily checked from
[

]

\displaystyle=\biggl|\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}}\nabla f\,\overline{\nabla(J_{1}^{\prime}u)}\,dx\,dy-\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}\backslash K_{\varepsilon}}\nabla f\,\overline{\nabla u}\,dx\,dy-\gamma_{K_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}(J_{1}f)\,\overline{u}\,d\mu\biggr|

Since

(see Appendix) applied with domain
\Omega^{\prime}
such that
\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}\subset\Omega

To proceed further with the proof of an upper bound of (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}}|\nabla J_{1}^{\prime}u|^{2}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}}\left(\left|\frac{\partial(J_{1}^{\prime}u)}{\partial x}\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{\partial(J_{1}^{\prime}u)}{\partial y}\right|^{2}\right)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\epsilon}\int_{0}^{2\pi}r\left|\frac{1}{\epsilon}\tilde{u}(\epsilon,\varphi)\,\cos\varphi-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}}{\partial\varphi}(\epsilon,\varphi)\,\sin\varphi\right|^{2}\,dr\,d\varphi

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\tilde{u}(\epsilon,\varphi)|^{2}d\varphi+4\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{\partial\tilde{u}}{\partial\varphi}(\epsilon,\varphi)\right|^{2}\,d\varphi.

As in the proof of (

Thus, using Lemma

\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\tilde{u}(\epsilon,\varphi)|^{2}\,d\varphi\leq\frac{2K}{\epsilon}\|u\|_{1}^{2}.

Now we come to the second term of (

(see Appendix) used for

Combining this together with (

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{\partial\tilde{u}}{\partial\varphi}(\epsilon,\varphi)\right|^{2}\,d\varphi\leq 4\|u\|_{1}^{2}.

Thus, by virtue of (

4.24

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}}|\nabla J_{1}^{\prime}u|^{2}\,dx\,dy\leq\left(\frac{8K}{\varepsilon}+16\right)\|u\|_{1}^{2}.

(4.25)

Finally, using the above bound and inequality (

), we estimate the right-hand side of (

\left|\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}}\nabla f\,\overline{\nabla(J_{1}^{\prime}u)}\,dx\,dy\right|\leq 2^{13/6}(C_{2}(K+2\varepsilon))^{1/2}\varepsilon^{1/6}\|f\|_{2}\|u\|_{1}.

Let us now consider the second term in (

) and (

) we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}\backslash K_{\varepsilon}}\nabla f\,\overline{\nabla u}\,dx\,dy\right|\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}}|\nabla f|^{2}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}\backslash K_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2/3}(C_{2})^{1/2}\varepsilon^{2/3}\|f\|_{2}\|u\|_{1}.

(4.27)

Finally we move on to the third term in (
4.20
). We have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}(J_{1}f)\,\overline{u}\,d\mu\right|\leq\left(\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}|J_{1}f|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}|f|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{1/2}.

(4.28)

Let us first find the appropriate estimate for the first integral on in the right hand side of (

Let
\Pi(d)=(-d,d)^{2},\,d>0

\Omega
and containing
K_{\varepsilon}

For almost all
x_{0}
belonging to the projection of
K_{\varepsilon}
on the axis
X

y(x_{0})\in(-d,d)

|f(x_{0},y(x_{0}))|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{d}\int_{-d}^{d}|f(x_{0},t)|^{2}\,dt\,.

Let us fix any

y>y(x_{0})

\displaystyle|f(x_{0},y)|^{2}=\left|f(x_{0},y(x_{0}))+\int_{y(x_{0})}^{y}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x_{0},t)\,dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|f(x_{0},y(x_{0}))\right|^{2}+2\left|\int_{y(x_{0})}^{y}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x_{0},t)\,dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{d}\int_{-d}^{d}|f(x_{0},t)|^{2}\,dt+2(y-y(x_{0}))\int_{y_{0}(x_{0})}^{y}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x_{0},t)\right|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{d}\int_{-d}^{d}|f(x_{0},t)|^{2}\,dt+4d\int_{-d}^{d}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x_{0},t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,.

(4.29)

Without loss of generality assume that the boundary of the unperturbed set which is

(x,y_{1}(x)),\,x\in(-1,1)

is some

Integrating

over
\partial K_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}|f(x,y)|^{2}\,d\mu=\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}|f(x,\varepsilon y_{1}(x/\varepsilon))|^{2}\,\left(1+y_{1}^{\prime 2}(x/\varepsilon)\right)^{1/2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\|y_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\biggl(\frac{2}{d}\int_{-d}^{d}|f(x,t)|^{2}\,dt+4d\int_{-d}^{d}\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(x,t)\right|^{2}\,dt\biggr)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathrm{max}\left\{1/d,2d\right\}\left(1+\|y_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(|f|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2})\,dxdy\,,

(4.30)

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times(-d,d)

To proceed with a proof we need the following auxiliary result
[
9
]
:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\Pi^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex set and let
G
and
Q
be arbitrary measurable sets in
\Pi^{\prime}
with
\mu\,(G)\neq 0
.
Then, for all
v\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Pi^{\prime})
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|v|^{2}\,dx\,dy

(4.31)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\mu\,(Q)}{\mu\,(G)}\int_{G}|v|^{2}\,dx\,dy+\frac{C(n)(d(\Pi^{\prime}))^{n+1}(\mu\,(Q))^{1/n}}{\mu\,(G)}\int_{\Pi^{\prime}}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\,dy,

where
d(\Pi^{\prime})
is the parameter of
\Pi^{\prime}
,
\mu
is the Lebesque measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the constant
C(n)
depends only on the dimension of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
G=\Pi^{\prime}=\Pi(d)
and
Q=\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Then using the Lemma
4.4
for the function
f
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|f|^{2}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\varepsilon d}{\mu(\Pi(d))}\int_{\Pi(d)}|f|^{2}\,dx\,dy+\frac{2C(2)(d(\Omega))^{3}\sqrt{\varepsilon d}}{\mu(\Pi(d))}\int_{\Pi(d)}|\nabla f|^{2}\,dx\,dy,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla f|^{2}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\varepsilon d}{\mu(\Pi(d))}\int_{\Pi(d)}|\nabla f|^{2}\,dx\,dy+\frac{2C(2)(d(\Omega))^{3}\sqrt{\varepsilon d}}{\mu(\Pi(d))}\int_{\Pi(d)}\left(\left|\nabla\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)\right|^{2}\right)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\varepsilon d}{\mu(\Pi(d))}\int_{\Pi(d)}|\nabla f|^{2}\,dx\,dy+\frac{2C(2)(d(\Omega))^{3}\sqrt{\varepsilon d}}{\mu(\Pi(d))}\int_{\Pi(d)}\left(\left|\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x^{2}}\right|^{2}+\left|\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial y^{2}}\right|^{2}+2\left|\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x\partial y}\right|^{2}\right)\,dx\,dy\,.

With the above bounds, one can show that sufficiently small values of

(4.32)

where

\tilde{c}=\frac{2C(2)(d(\Omega))^{3}\sqrt{d}}{\mu(\Pi(d))}=\frac{C(2)(d(\Omega))^{3}}{2d\sqrt{d}}

and
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Pi(d))}
means the Sobolev
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Pi(d))
norm of

.

Next we need the following interior regularity theorem
[
1
]

Theorem 4.5
.

(Interior Regularity Theorem.)
Suppose that
h\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of

-\Delta h=w
. Then
h\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and for each
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega

c=c(\Omega_{0})
independent of

\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\leq c\left(\|h\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

(4.33)

In view of the above theorem the right-hand side of (
4.32
) can be estimated as follows

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(|\nabla f|^{2}+|f|^{2})\,dx\,dy\leq c\,\tilde{c}\,\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\int_{\Omega}(|f|^{2}+|\Delta f|^{2})\,dx\,dy,

(4.34)

with some constant

does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

By virtue of inequalities (
4.30
), (
4.34
) and Lemma
6.3
(see Appendix) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}|f(x,y)|^{2}\,d\mu\leq 2c\,\tilde{c}\,\mathrm{max}\left\{1/d,2d\right\}\left(1+\|y_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\,\int_{\Omega}(|f|^{2}+|\Delta f|^{2})\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2c\,\tilde{c}\,\mathrm{max}\left\{1/d,2d\right\}\left(1+\|y_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\|f\|_{2}^{2}\,.

The above combined the fact that

estimates the right-hand side of the inequality (
4.28
) as follows

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial K_{\varepsilon}}(J_{1}f)\,\overline{u}\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2c\,\tilde{c}\,\mathrm{max}\left\{1/d,2d\right\}}{\gamma_{K_{\varepsilon}}}\right)^{1/2}\left(1+\|y_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(-1,1)}^{2}\right)^{1/4}\,\varepsilon^{1/4}\,\|f\|_{2}\|u\|_{1}\,.

By virtue of (
4.26
), (
4.27

\mathrm{r.h.s.}(\ref{7''})=\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{1/6}+\gamma_{K_{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}\varepsilon^{1/4}\right)\,\|f\|_{2}\,\|u\|_{1},\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

which ends the proof.∎

Proof of Lemma

Let
M_{\epsilon}
be the subset of
\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}\setminus\Gamma
such that for every point
(x_{0},y_{0})\in M_{\epsilon}

l_{x_{0}}:=\{x=x_{0}\}

(x_{0},y_{1}(x_{0}))

(x_{0},y_{2}(x_{0}))\in\Omega

(x_{0},y_{1}(x_{0},y_{0}))
and
(x_{0},y_{2}(x_{0},y_{0}))

\Omega
and has an empty intersection with
\Gamma
.
Without loss of generality suppose that
y_{1}(x_{0})<y_{2}(x_{0})
. Then for any

Let
P_{\epsilon}
denote the projection of
M_{\epsilon}
onto the axis
\mathbb{X}
. We get

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\epsilon}}|u(x,y)|^{2}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{M_{\epsilon}}|u(x,y_{1}(x,y))|^{2}\,dx\,dy+2\int_{M_{\epsilon}}\left|\int_{y_{1}(x,y)}^{y}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)\,dt\right|^{2}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=2\int_{P_{\epsilon}}\int_{\{y:\,(x,y)\in M_{\epsilon}\}}|u(x,y_{1}(x))|^{2}\,dxdy

\displaystyle+2\int_{P_{\epsilon}}\int_{\{y:\,(x,y)\in M_{\epsilon}\}}\left|\int_{y_{1}(x,y)}^{y}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)\,dt\right|^{2}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{P_{\epsilon}}\int_{\{y:\,(x,y)\in M_{\epsilon}\}}|u(x,y_{1}(x))|^{2}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle+2\int_{P_{\epsilon}}\int_{\{y:\,(x,y)\in M_{\epsilon}\}}(y-y_{1}(x))\int_{y_{1}(x)}^{y}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)\right|^{2}\,dt\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\leq 4\epsilon\int_{P_{\epsilon}}|u(x,y_{1}(x))|^{2}\,dx+8\epsilon^{2}\int_{P_{\epsilon}}\int_{y_{1}(x)}^{y_{2}(x)}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)\right|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 4\epsilon\int_{P_{\epsilon}}|u(x,y_{1}(x))|^{2}\,dx+8\epsilon^{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}\setminus\Gamma}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,t)\right|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 4\epsilon\int_{P_{\epsilon}}|u(x,y_{1}(x))|^{2}\,dx+8\epsilon^{2}\|u\|_{1}^{2}.

(5.1)

Since the set
\{(x,y_{1}(x))\},\,x\in P_{\epsilon}
, is the part of
\Gamma
then

\displaystyle\int_{P_{\epsilon}}|u(x,y_{1}(x))|^{2}\,dx\leq\int_{P_{\epsilon}}\left(1+y_{1}^{\prime}(x)^{2}\right)^{1/2}|u(x,y_{1}(x))|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma\cap\{(x,y):x\in P_{\epsilon}\}}|u|^{2}\,d\mu\leq\int_{\Gamma}|u|^{2}\,d\mu\,.

Returning to the inequality (
5.1
) and combining the above bound together with the fact that

\int_{\Gamma}|u|^{2}\,d\mu\leq\frac{1}{\gamma_{\Gamma}}\|u\|_{1}^{2}

we get

\int_{M_{\epsilon}}|u(x,y)|^{2}\,dxdy\leq 4\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\gamma_{\Gamma}}+2\epsilon^{2}\right)\|u\|_{1}^{2}\,.

(5.2)

Now let us go to the subset
\left(\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}\setminus\Gamma\right)\setminus M_{\epsilon}
.
For any
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\left(\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}\setminus\Gamma\right)\setminus M_{\epsilon}
let
(x_{0},y_{3}(x_{0}))
with
y_{3}(x_{0})<y_{0}
, be a point of intersection of line
l_{x_{0}}
with the boundary of
\Omega
. One can easily check that there is

y_{4}(x_{0})\in(y_{3}(x_{0}),y_{0})
such that

|u(x_{0},y_{4})|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{y_{0}-y_{3}(x_{0})}}\sqrt{\int_{y_{3}(x_{0})}^{y_{0}}|u(x_{0},z)|^{2}\,dz}.

\displaystyle|u(x_{0},y_{0})|^{2}=\left|u(x_{0},y_{4}(x_{0}))+\int_{y_{4}(x_{0})}^{y_{0}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(x_{0},z)\,dz\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|u(x_{0},y_{4}(x_{0}))|^{2}+2(y_{0}-y_{4}(x_{0}))\int_{y_{4}(x)}^{y}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(x,z)\right|^{2}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{y_{0}-y_{3}(x_{0})}\int_{y_{3}(x_{0})}^{y_{0}}|u(x_{0},z)|^{2}\,dz+2(y_{0}-y_{3}(x_{0}))\int_{y_{3}(x_{0})}^{y_{0}}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(x_{0},z)\right|^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\mathrm{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),\partial\Omega)}\int_{y_{3}(x_{0})}^{y_{0}}|u(x_{0},z)|^{2}\,dz+2\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\int_{y_{3}(x_{0})}^{y_{0}}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(x_{0},z)\right|^{2}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\left(\int_{y_{3}(x_{0})}^{y_{0}}|u(x_{0},z)|^{2}\,dz+\int_{y_{3}(x_{0})}^{y_{0}}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}(x_{0},z)\right|^{2}\,dz\right)\,,

where
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
is the diameter of
\Omega
and
\mathrm{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),\partial\Omega)
is the distance between
(x_{0},y_{0})

\Omega

\widetilde{C}=2\mathrm{max}\left\{\frac{1}{\mathrm{dist}((x_{0},y_{0}),\partial\Omega)},\,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)\right\}.

be the projection of
\left(\mathbb{B}_{\epsilon}\backslash\Gamma\right)\setminus M_{\epsilon}

This together (

Irreducibility and singularities of some nested Quot schemes
Parvez Rasul, Ronnie Sebastian

Abstract
. Let C C be a smooth projective curve over ℂ \mathbb{C} of genus g ⩾ 1 g\geqslant 1 . Let E E be a vector bundle on C C of rank r r and degree e e . Given integers k 1 , k 2 , d 1 , d 2 k_{1},k_{2},d_{1},d_{2} such that r > k 1 > k 2 > 0 r>k_{1}>k_{2}>0 , let 𝒬 d 1 , d 2 k 1 , k 2 ​ ( E ) \mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E) denote the nested Quot scheme which parametrizes pair of quotients [ E ↠ F 1 ↠ F 2 ] [E\twoheadrightarrow F_{1}\twoheadrightarrow F_{2}] such that F i F_{i} has rank k i k_{i} and degree d i d_{i} . We show that these nested Quot schemes are integral, local complete intersection schemes when d 1 ≫ d 2 ≫ 0 d_{1}\gg d_{2}\gg 0 or d 2 ≫ d 1 ≫ 0 d_{2}\gg d_{1}\gg 0 .

1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of genus
g\geqslant 1
.
Let
E
be a vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
and degree
e
.
Let
k
be an integer such that
0<k<r
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E):=\textnormal{Quot}_{C/\mathbb{C}}(E,k,d)
denote the Quot scheme of quotients of
E
of rank
k
and degree
d
on
C
.
Quot schemes are very important objects in the study of geometry of moduli spaces.
The Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus r})
also provides a
compactification of the space of maps from
C
into the Grassmannian.
Thus, Quot schemes also appear in a natural way in enumerative geometry.
In
[
Str87
]
, Stromme proved that the Quot scheme

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{O}^{\oplus r}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is smooth
and irreducible and computed its Picard group.
Let
C
be smooth and projective of genus
g\geqslant 1
.
When
E
is trivial, it is proved in
[
BDW96
]
that
the Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus r})
is
irreducible, generically smooth
and is a local complete intersection for
d\gg 0
.
For any vector bundle
E
on
C
, it is proved in

[
PR03
]
that the Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E)
is
irreducible and generically smooth when
d\gg 0
.
In
[
GS24
]
, the authors compute the
Picard group of
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E)

and show that
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E)
is integral, normal,
local complete intersection and locally factorial
when
d\ \gg 0
.

When
k=0
, the Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{0}_{d}(E)
of torsion
quotients of
E
is a smooth variety of dimension
rd
.
This is a well-studied variety,
and we only mention a few recent works

[
BFP20
]
,
[
OS23
]
,
[
OP21
]
,
[
BGS24
]
.

A natural generalization of the Quot scheme is the nested
Quot scheme.
Given integers
k_{1},k_{2},d_{1},d_{2}
such that
r>k_{1}>k_{2}>0
,
let
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
denote the nested Quot scheme
which parametrizes pair of quotients

[E\twoheadrightarrow F_{1}\twoheadrightarrow F_{2}]

such that
F_{i}
has rank
k_{i}
and degree
d_{i}
.

We may also consider the case when
k_{1}=k_{2}=0
.
When
k_{1}=k_{2}=0
and
E=\mathcal{O}_{C}
,
we get the nested Hilbert schemes of points,
\mathcal{Q}^{0,0}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(\mathcal{O}_{C})
.
In
[
Che94
]
, Cheah proved that the nested Hilbert
scheme over a smooth projective curve
C
is isomorphic to a product
of symmetric products of
C
and hence smooth.
A variation of the nested Hilbert scheme, namely, double
nested Hilbert scheme, which parametrizes flags of
subschemes nestings in two direction, is studied in

[
Mon22
]
and
[
GLM+23
]
. In the latter article, it is proved
that these double nested Hilbert schemes are connected, reduced,
and pure. The components need not be normal, but the normalizations
are isomorphic to a product of symmetric products of
C
.
For a vector bundle
E
, the Quot scheme

\mathcal{Q}^{0,0}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
is smooth of dimension
rd_{1}
.
In
[
MR22
]
, the authors compute the generating
function of the motive of the nested Quot scheme
of torsion quotients, see also
[
BFP20
]
.
The smoothness of nested Quot scheme of torsion
quotients is studied in
[
MR23
]
when the
underlying scheme is higher-dimensional.

In recent years, there has been an increasing focus on
nested Hilbert schemes on surfaces due to its
connection with various areas like moduli of sheaves,
enumerative geometry, representation theory and Lie algebras.
We refer the reader to some recent works
[
RS23
]
,

[
RT22
]
,
[
GRS24
]
,
[
GSY20
]
,
[
GT20
]

and references therein. In
[
RS23
]
, the authors
study the nested Hilbert scheme
S^{[2,n]}
and show
that this is an integral scheme which is normal and
has rational singularities. In particular, it is Cohen-Macaulay.
They further pose the question of studying the singularities
of the nested Hilbert schemes
S^{[n,m]}
, see
[
RS23
, Question 9.5]
.

In view of the above results, it is natural to study
nested Quot schemes over smooth projective curves when the ranks of the quotients
are positive.

Such nested Quot schemes are studied in
[
MR25
]
,
where the authors construct virtual fundamental classes and determine motivic partition functions of the nested Quot schemes.
In this article we prove some results about irreducibility
and singularities of these nested Quot schemes.
We will consider two cases:
d_{1}\gg d_{2}\gg 0
and
0\ll d_{1}\ll d_{2}
.
Writing the nested Quot scheme as a relative Quot scheme,
we get an expected dimension of the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)

(1.1)

\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2}):=[d_{1}r-k_{1}e+k_{1}(r-k_{1})(1-g)]+[d_{2}k_{1}-d_{1}k_{2}+k_{2}(k_{1}-k_{2})(1-g)]\,.

It can be easily checked that the expected dimension, that is, the quantity
\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2})
, coincides with the virtual dimension computed in
[
MR25
, Theorem A]
.
We prove the following results.

Theorem

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

There exists a number
d(E,k_{2})
such that for all
d_{2}\geqslant d(E,k_{2})
,
the following holds. There is a number
\xi(E,k_{1},k_{2},d_{2})

such that if
d_{1}-d_{2}\geqslant\xi(E,k_{1},k_{2},d_{2})

then the nested Quot scheme

\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
is irreducible of dimension

\textnormal{expdim}{(d_{1},d_{2})}
, a local complete intersection, integral
and normal.

Theorem

(Theorem
5.27
, Theorem
5.26
)
.

There exists a number
\gamma(E,k_{1},k_{2})
such that
for all
d_{1}\geqslant\gamma(E,k_{1},k_{2})
, the following holds. There is a number
\beta(E,k_{1},k_{2},d_{1})
,
such that if
d_{2}\geqslant\beta(E,k_{1},k_{2},d_{1})
, then

(1)

The nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
is irreducible
of dimension
\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2})
.

(2)

The natural map
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)\to\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)
is a local complete
intersection morphism. In particular, it follows that
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)

is a local complete intersection.

(3)

The nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
is an integral scheme.

(4)

\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
is normal if
k_{1}+k_{2}>r
and
k_{1}-k_{2}\geqslant 2
.

One of the ingredients used to prove the above results is the following Theorem,
which may be viewed as a generalization of
[
PR03
]
to a family
of vector bundles.
Let
T
be a scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{A}
be
a vector bundle on
C\times T
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A})
denote the relative
Quot scheme
\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d)
.

Theorem

(Theorem
3.16
)
.

Let
T
be an irreducible scheme. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a locally
free sheaf on
C\times T
of rank
r
, such that each
\mathcal{A}_{t}
has
degree
e
. There is a number
\alpha(\mathcal{A},k)
such that if

d\geqslant\alpha(\mathcal{A},k)
then the structure morphism
\pi:\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A})\to T

has the following properties

(1)

The fibers are irreducible of dimension
dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)
.

(2)

The relative Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A})
is
irreducible of dimension
dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)+\dim T
.

(3)

\pi
is a local complete intersection morphism and flat.

(4)

If
T
is reduced, then
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A})
is generically smooth.

(5)

Let
T
be reduced and assume the singular locus of
T

has codimension
\geqslant 2
. There is
\alpha^{\prime}(\mathcal{A},k)

such that for all
d\geqslant\alpha^{\prime}(\mathcal{A},k)
the singular locus
of
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A})
has codimension
\geqslant 2
.

Given integers
l>2,k_{1},\dots,k_{l},d_{1},\dots,d_{l}

such that
r>k_{1}>\dots>k_{l}>0
,
the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{l}}(E)
is defined similarly.
Theorem
4.1
generalises easily
to prove a similar result for the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{l}}(E)

in the case
d_{1}\gg d_{2}\gg\cdots\gg d_{l}\gg 0
.
We explain this in Remark
4.2
.
It would be interesting to know if the results in
Theorem
5.27
can be
generalised to
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{l}}(E)

in the case
0\ll d_{1}\ll d_{2}\ll...\ll d_{l}
.

We briefly discuss the strategy and the organization of the paper.
In Section 2, we prove some preliminary lemmas.
In Section 3, we prove Theorem
3.16
.
We remark that in Section 3, the techniques in
[
PR03
, Section 6]

naturally generalize to the relative setting.
However, for the sake of completeness, we include a detailed proof.
In Section 4, we prove Theorem
4.1
.
In Section 5, our main result is Theorem
5.27
.
Here we write the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
as a
relative Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{2}}_{d_{2}}(\mathcal{F}_{1})
, where

\mathcal{F}_{1}
denotes the universal quotient over
C\times\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)
.
Here
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is not locally free,
so we cannot apply Theorem
3.16
directly.
However we can apply Theorem
3.16

for the open subset of
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)
where
the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is locally free.
This gives us an open subset of the nested Quot scheme

\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
, which is irreducible of expected dimension.
Let
Y
denote the complement of this open locus.
We show that points of
Y
cannot be general in any component of
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
.
Computing the dimension upper bound for
Y

is a crucial step to prove the main result
and this is done through several lemmas.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
of genus

g\geqslant 1
.
Let
T
be an irreducible scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
and
let
\mathcal{A}
be a coherent sheaf on
C\times T

which is flat over
T
.
Then
\chi(\mathcal{A}_{t})
is constant as a function of
t\in T
.
From the Hilbert polynomial we see that the rank and degree of
\mathcal{A}_{t}

are independent of
t\in T
.
Let
r:={\rm rank}(\mathcal{A}_{t})
and let
e:=\deg(\mathcal{A}_{t})
for all
t\in T
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There are numbers
m_{q}(\mathcal{A},k)
and
m_{s}(\mathcal{A},k)
such that the
following happens.

(1)

Let
F
be a sheaf on
C
of rank
k
,
which is a quotient of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
for some
t\in T
.
Then
\deg(F)\geqslant m_{q}(\mathcal{A},k)
.

(2)

Let
F
be a sheaf on
C
of rank
k
which is a subsheaf of
\mathcal{A}_{t}

for some
t\in T
. Then
\deg(F)\leqslant m_{s}(\mathcal{A},k)
.

Proof.

The family of sheaves
\{\mathcal{A}_{t}\}_{t\in T}
is bounded in the sense of
[
HL10
, Definition 1.7.5]
.
Using
[
HL10
, Lemma 1.7.6 (iii)]
, we get a sheaf
A
on
C
such that each
\mathcal{A}_{t}
is a quotient of
A
.
If
F
is a quotient of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
for some
t\in T
, then
F
is also a quotient of
A
.
Let
K_{F}
be the kernel of the quotient
A\to F
.
Then we have
\chi(F)=\chi(A)-\chi(K_{F})\geqslant\chi(A)-\textrm{h}^{0}(K_{F})
.
Using Rieman-Roch formula and the fact
\textrm{h}^{0}(K_{F})\leqslant\textrm{h}^{0}(A)
, we get

\deg(F)\geqslant\deg A+(\text{rank}(A)-k)(1-g)-\textrm{h}^{0}(A)\,.

Define
m_{q}(\mathcal{A},k):=\deg A+(\text{rank}(A)-k)(1-g)-\textrm{h}^{0}(A)
.
This proves the first assertion.

Let
F
be a subsheaf of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
for some
t\in T
and
let
B_{F}
be the cokernel.
So
B_{F}
is a quotient of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
of rank
r-k
.
By the previous part we have
\deg(B_{F})\geqslant m_{q}(\mathcal{A},r-k)
.
We have
\chi(F)=\chi(\mathcal{A}_{t})-\chi(B_{F})\leqslant\chi(\mathcal{A}_{t})-m_{q}(\mathcal{A},r-k)-(r-k)(1-g)
.
Using Riemann-Roch we get

\deg F\leqslant e-m_{q}(\mathcal{A},r-k)\,.

Define
m_{s}(\mathcal{A},k):=e-m_{q}(\mathcal{A},r-k)
.
This proves the Lemma.
∎

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be a sheaf of rank
r
on
C
.
For each
k
with
0<k<r
, define

m(G,k)=\min_{\text{rank}(F)=k}\left\{\deg(F):F\textrm{ is a quotient of }G\right\}

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T
be a scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{G}
be a
coherent sheaf on
C\times T
which is flat over
T
.
Fix an integer
k
such that
0<k<\text{rank}(\mathcal{G})
. Then
the function
t\mapsto m(\mathcal{G}_{t},k)
is lower semicontinuous as a
function from
T
to
\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence the set
\{m(\mathcal{G}_{t},k)\}_{t\in T}
is
finite for a fixed
k
.

Proof.

See Lemma 2.2 of
[
Ras24
]
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Lemma
2.3
proves that the set

\{m(\mathcal{A}_{t},k)\}_{t\in T}
is finite for a fixed
k
.
Define

m_{\max}(\mathcal{A},k)=\max_{t\in T}\left\{m(\mathcal{A}_{t},k)\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad m_{\min}(\mathcal{A},k)=\min_{t\in T}\left\{m(\mathcal{A}_{t},k)\right\}\,.

If
d<m_{\min}(\mathcal{A},k)
then the Quot
scheme
\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d)
is empty.
For the structure map

\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d)\to T
to be surjective on closed points,
we need that
d\geqslant m_{\max}(\mathcal{A},k)
.

From here on, we denote the relative Quot scheme

\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d)
by

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A})\,.

A closed point of
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A})
corresponds to a tuple

(t,[\varphi:\mathcal{A}_{t}\to F])
,
where
t
is a closed point of
T
and

\varphi:\mathcal{A}_{t}\to F
is a quotient in
\textnormal{Quot}_{C/\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{A}_{t},k,d)
.
Let
S_{F}
denote the kernel of
\varphi
. We have the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
T
be irreducible. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a coherent sheaf on
C\times T
which is flat over
T
. Then

(2.6)

\hom(S_{F},F)\geqslant\dim_{(t,\varphi)}\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A})-\dim T\geqslant\hom(S_{F},F)-{\rm ext}^{1}(S_{F},F)\,.

Proof.

Since
T
is irreducible, we may apply
[
Kol96
, Theorem 5.17, Chapter 1]
.
This gives the second inequality.

Next we prove the first inequality.
Let
T_{\rm red}\subset T
denote the reduced subscheme
structure. Using
[
dJ96
, Theorem 4.1]
we may conclude the following.
There is a surjective morphism
\tilde{T}\to T_{\textnormal{red}}

from an irreducible smooth variety
\tilde{T}
such that
\dim({\tilde{T}})=\dim(T)
.
Let
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
denote the pullback
of
\mathcal{A}
to
C\times\tilde{T}
.
Using the base change property of
Quot schemes (for example, see
[
FGI+05
, Chapter 5, Example 5.1.5 (5)]
), we have the following Cartesian square

\textstyle{\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times\tilde{T}/\tilde{T}}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},k,d)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tilde{T}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T}

Clearly, the map
\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times\tilde{T}/\tilde{T}}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},k,d)\to\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d)

is surjective on closed points. Let
\tilde{q}
be a point in

\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times\tilde{T}/\tilde{T}}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},k,d)
and let
q

denote its image in
\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d)
.
Then it is clear that

\dim_{\tilde{q}}(\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times\tilde{T}/\tilde{T}}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},k,d))\geqslant\dim_{q}(\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d))\,.

Applying
[
HL10
, Proposition 2.2.7]
to the point
\tilde{q}

we get that

\displaystyle\hom(S_{F},F)+\dim(\tilde{T})\geqslant

\displaystyle\dim(T_{\tilde{q}}\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times\tilde{T}/\tilde{T}}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},k,d))

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\dim_{\tilde{q}}(\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times\tilde{T}/\tilde{T}}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},k,d))\geqslant\dim_{q}(\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times T/T}(\mathcal{A},k,d))\,.

As
\dim({\tilde{T}})=\dim(T)
the proof of the first inequality
is complete.
∎

3.
Irreducibility of relative Quot scheme

Throughout this section, unless mentioned otherwise,

T
will be an irreducible scheme and
\mathcal{A}
will be a
locally free sheaf of rank
r
on
C\times T
.
The degree of each

\mathcal{A}_{t}
will be denoted
e
.
We may put additional assumptions on
T
if required.
The proofs in this section are very similar to those in

[
PR03
, Section 6]
. We only need to take care that the
degree
d
can be chosen so that it works for all
t\in T
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There is a number
\alpha_{1}(\mathcal{A},k)
, such that for

d\geqslant\alpha_{1}(\mathcal{A},k)
, for any stable bundle
F
on
C

of rank
k
and degree
d
and for any
t\in T
, the
sheaf
\mathscr{H}om(\mathcal{A}_{t},F)
is generated by global sections and

H^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\vee}\otimes F)=0
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that in
[
PR03
, Lemma 6.1]
, except
that we replace the moduli spaces
U_{C}^{s}(k,j)
with the relative moduli spaces

U^{s}_{C\times T/T}(k,j)
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
d\geqslant\alpha_{1}(\mathcal{A},k)
. Fix

t\in T
and a quotient
\varphi:\mathcal{A}_{t}\to F
,
where
F
is a stable bundle on
C
of rank
k

and degree
d
. Let
S_{F}
be the kernel of
\varphi
.
Then
h^{1}(S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F)=0
.
As a consequence

h^{0}(S_{F}^{\vee}\otimes F)=dr-ke+k(r-k)(1-g)\,.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that in
[
PR03
, Lemma 6.2]
.
∎

For a closed point
t\in T
, consider the Quot scheme

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A}_{t})={\rm Quot}_{C/\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{A}_{t},k,d)\,.

Inside
\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A}_{t})
we have the loci

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(\mathcal{A}_{t})^{s}
, consisting of quotients

Proposition 3.18
.

be a coherent sheaf on

of rank

and degree

\mathcal{T}
be the torsion subsheaf of

\mathcal{T}
be

factors through

hence gives a quotient

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E^{\prime})^{0}

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E)^{0}

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E)

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E^{\prime})^{0}

\mathcal{Q}^{k}_{d}(E)

of genus

and
E
be a locally free sheaf on

of rank

and degree
e
.
Let

be integers such that

Let

We will use the following construction in this section.

Using the universal properties of

and

is isomorphic to the

The closed points of the nested Quot scheme

\displaystyle\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2}):=[d_{1}r-k_{1}e+k_{1}(r-k_{1})(1-g)]+[d_{2}k_{1}-d_{1}k_{2}+k_{2}(k_{1}-k_{2})(1-g)]\,.

.

[

,
the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{l}}(E)
is defined similarly.
Theorem
4.1
can be extended to the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{l}}(E)

(for
l>2
)
easily using induction on
l
.
Let
Q^{\prime}
denote the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{2},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{2},\dots,d_{l}}(E)
.
Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
denote the universal kernel over
C\times Q^{\prime}
.
For any closed point
{\bar{q}}=[E\to F_{2}\to\cdots\to F_{l}]
in
Q^{\prime}
,
the fiber
(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})_{\bar{q}}
is the locally free sheaf
\ker(E\to F_{2})
.
Then
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{l}}(E)
is isomorphic to the relative Quot scheme

\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times Q^{\prime}/Q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime},k_{1}-k_{2},d_{1}-d_{2})\,.

By induction hypothesis,
Q^{\prime}=\mathcal{Q}^{k_{2},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{2},\dots,d_{l}}(E)
is irreducible, a local complete intersection, integral and normal when
d_{2}\gg d_{3}\gg\cdots\gg d_{l}\gg 0
.
Applying Theorem
3.16
,
it follows that
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},\dots,k_{l}}_{d_{1},\dots,d_{l}}(E)
is
irreducible, a local complete intersection, integral and normal when
d_{1}\gg d_{2}\gg\cdots\gg d_{l}\gg 0
.

5.
Irreducibility of nested Quot schemes when
0\ll d_{1}\ll d_{2}

As in the previous section,
let
d_{1},d_{2},k_{1},k_{2}
be integers such that
0<k_{2}<k_{1}<r

and we denote by

\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
the nested Quot scheme.
Next we want to show that if
0\ll d_{1}\ll d_{2}
then the nested Quot scheme is irreducible.
We will consider another construction of the nested
Quot scheme. Consider the Quot scheme

\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)=\textnormal{Quot}_{C/\mathbb{C}}(E,k_{1},d_{1})\,.

Let
p_{C}:C\times\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)\to C
be the projection.
Let

p_{C}^{*}E\to\mathcal{F}_{1}\to 0

be the universal quotient on
C\times\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)
.
Consider the relative Quot scheme

(5.1)

\textnormal{Quot}_{C\times\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)/\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)}(\mathcal{F}_{1},k_{2},d_{2})\,.

It is easy to see that this relative Quot scheme is the nested Quot scheme

\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
.

Remark 5.2
.

Using
[
PR03
, Theorem 6.2, Theorem 6.4]
and
[
GS24
, Lemma 6.1, Theorem 6.3]
,
we get a number
d(E,k_{1})
such that
the Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)
is irreducible of dimension

d_{1}r-k_{1}e+k_{1}(r-k_{1})(1-g)
, integral, normal and a local complete intersection when
d_{1}\geqslant d(E,k_{1})
.

Recall the quantity (expected dimension)
\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2})
from (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2}):=[d_{1}r-k_{1}e+k_{1}(r-k_{1})(1-g)]+[d_{2}k_{1}-d_{1}k_{2}+k_{2}(k_{1}-k_{2})(1-g)]\,.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
d_{1}\geqslant d(E,k_{1})
and

be an integer
such that the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
is non-empty.
Let
\mathcal{W}
be any irreducible component of the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
.
Then

\dim\mathcal{W}\geqslant\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2})\,.

Proof.

Let
[E\xrightarrow{q_{1}}F_{1},F_{1}\xrightarrow{q_{2}}F_{2}]

be a closed point of the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
.
Let
S_{12}
denote the kernel of
q_{2}
.
By choice of
d_{1}
, the Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)
is irreducible.
As the nested Quot scheme
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
is a relative Quot scheme,
we can find the dimension bound at any closed point
using Lemma
2.5
,
by taking
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)
as
T
.
Note that we cannot apply
Theorem
3.16
to
conclude the irreducibility of the nested Quot scheme as

\mathcal{F}_{1}
is not locally free on
C\times\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1}}_{d_{1}}(E)
.
Using Lemma

, we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\hom(S_{12},F_{2})\geqslant\dim_{(q_{1},q_{2})}\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)-[d_{1}r-k_{1}e

\displaystyle+k_{1}(r-k_{1})(1-g)]

\displaystyle\geqslant\hom(S_{12},F_{2})-\textrm{ext}^{1}(S_{12},F_{2})\,.

Taking a free resolution of

and using Riemann-Roch formula we easily

\hom(S_{12},F_{2})-\textrm{ext}^{1}(S_{12},F_{2})=d_{2}k_{1}-d_{1}k_{2}+k_{2}(k_{1}-k_{2})(1-g)\,.

So it follows that,

\displaystyle\dim_{(q_{1},q_{2})}\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)

\displaystyle\geqslant[d_{1}r-k_{1}e+k_{1}(r-k_{1})(1-g)]+[d_{2}k_{1}-d_{1}k_{2}+k_{2}(k_{1}-k_{2})(1-g)]

\displaystyle=\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2})\,.

Since this is true for any closed point of
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)

it follows that any irreducible component of
\mathcal{Q}^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{d_{1},d_{2}}(E)
has dimension at least
\textnormal{expdim}(d_{1},d_{2})
.
∎

Let

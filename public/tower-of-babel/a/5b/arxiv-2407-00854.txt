Effects of Internal Resonance and Damping on Koopman Modes
Rahul Das, Anil K Bajaj, Sayan Gupta

Abstract
This study investigates the nonlinear normal modes (NNMs) of a system comprising of two coupled Duffing oscillators, with one oscillator being grounded and with the coupling being both linear and nonlinear. The study utilizes the eigenfunctions of the Koopman operator and validates their connection with the Shaw-Piere invariant manifold framework for NNMs. Furthermore, the study delves into the impact of internal resonance and dissipation on the accuracy of this framework by defining a continuous quantitative measure for internal resonance. The applicability and robustness of the framework for the systems which are very similar qualitatively to that of an ENO, are also observed and discussed about the limitations of the approximation technique.

Introduction

In classical dynamical theory
Perko 2013
, the eigensubspaces
Hoffmann and Kunze 1971
,
Guckenheimer and Holmes 2013
,
Artin 1991
of a linear dynamical system are referred to as Linear Normal Modes (LNMs), which are invariant subspaces in terms of flow. LNMs play a crucial role in decoupling the dynamical system and the general solution is obtained by solving its components separately, relying on the principles of linear independence and linear superposition.
However, when applied to nonlinear dynamical systems, LNMs often fall short, failing to capture important phenomena such as bifurcation, chaos, and nonlinear internal resonance
Das et al. 2023
. Recognizing these limitations, Lyapunov, Kauderer and Rosenberg
Lyapunov 1992
,
Kauderer and Kauderer 1958
,
Rosenberg 1961
,
Rosenberg 1962
,
Rosenberg 1960
pioneered the concept of
Nonlinear Normal Modes
(
NNM

Shaw and Pierre 1991
,
Shaw and Pierre 1993
further advanced the concept for dissipative systems by introducing
invariant manifolds
to compute NNMs and investigate their topology. However, Shaw’s technique involves solving numerous polynomial equations, posing computational challenges for systems with a high degree of freedom. Moreover, the accuracy of the manifold parameterization diminishes significantly for the states (points in state space) further from the equilibrium point amidst strong nonlinear interactions. This motivated the development of various frameworks to study Shaw-Pierre type NNMs. These include an algebraic geometry-based computational framework
Petromichelakis and
Kougioumtzoglou 2021
, the operator-theoretic approach
Cirillo et al. 2016
, and the introduction of the
Spectral Sub-Manifolds
(
SSMs
)
Haller and Ponsioen 2016
,
Ponsioen et al. 2018
,
Li et al. 2023
. A concise overview of the development and contributions of these articles is presented in Sec.
2
, underscoring the motivation to explore NNMs using an operator-theoretic approach.

In contrast to classical dynamical theory, an operator-theoretic approach has been developed by B.O. Koopman
Koopman 1931
, focusing on the spectral properties of the
Koopman operator
. Cirillo
et. al.

Cirillo et al. 2016
developed the framework for studying Shaw-Pierre NNMs (
invariant manifolds
) by adapting the Koopman operator theory. In this method, the parameterization of the manifold is carried out by a nonlinear coordinate transformation, which involves mapping the state variable into an infinite-dimensional vector space. A proof of the uniqueness of this mapping is however not available. Furthermore, the works of Sternberg
Sternberg 1957a
,
Sternberg 1957b
, Mezić
Mezić 2013
and Lan
Lan and Mezić 2013
indicate that this framework is exclusive to systems with only hyperbolic fixed points, and the analytic properties of the nonlinear coordinate transformation are compromised in the case of systems with nonresonating eigenvalues. Cirillo
et. al.

Cirillo et al. 2016
have explored the impact of resonating eigenvalues on the accuracy of the Koopman operator framework, particularly in systems with
linear coupling terms
. For such cases, the presence of strong nonlinearity in coupling leads to strong nonlinear internal resonance, even for systems with non-resonating eigenvalues.

The motivation behind the present study is threefold: first, to provide a proof of the uniqueness of mapping the state vector into the infinite-dimensional vector space (as stated in Proposition
1
); second, to investigate the effects of nonlinear internal resonance and damping on the accuracy of this framework; and finally, to explore the applicability and the accuracy of the Koopman operator framework for systems which are very similar qualitatively to systems with at least one nonhyperbolic eigenvalue.
In this study, we have considered a
2
-dof system comprising of two Duffing oscillators, such that the first oscillator is grounded and the second oscillator is coupled to the first one, where the coupling has additive linear and nonlinear terms. The choice of the system offers flexibility to meet the objectives of this study. The presence of nonlinearity in the coupling ensures the possibility of nonlinear resonance and by making the linear part of the coupling term close to zero, the second oscillator will approach to be
essentially nonlinear
(ENO) (
i.e.
the linear part of the stiffness is zero). The corresponding dynamical system contains at least one non-hyperbolic fixed point.

Review on Nonlinear Normal Modes

The pioneering contributions of Lyapunov, Kauderer, and Rosenberg
Lyapunov 1992
,
Kauderer and Kauderer 1958
,
Rosenberg 1961
,
Rosenberg 1962
have laid the foundation for the theory of NNMs, extending the invariance principle of LNMs to address the complexity arising from nonlinearities in dynamical systems. Rosenberg
Rosenberg 1961
,
Rosenberg 1962
,
Rosenberg 1960
specifically focused on
n
-degree of freedom conservative systems, defining NNMs as vibrations in unison,
i.e.
synchronous periodic motions, where all material points of the system reach their extrema values at the same instant of time and cross the equilibrium simultaneously. These NNMs were categorized into either straight modal lines, known as similar NNMs
Rosenberg 1961
,
Rosenberg 1962
or modal curves for non-similar NNMs
Rosenberg and Kuo 1964a
,
Vakakis 1992
in the configuration space of the system; see Appendix
A
for more detailed explanations.
This representation directly originates from the generalization of Linear Normal Modes (LNMs) for nonlinear cases, where the normal modes are depicted as the relationship between the displacement of the material points of the system. This class of representation is commonly referred to as Kauderer-Rosenberg NNMs.

Similar NNMs occur only in special cases, such as in homogeneous and symmetric systems. These NNMs are qualitatively similar to LNMs as the relationship between the displacement of the material points of the system is linear, facilitating straight modal lines in configuration space
Mikhlin and Avramov 2010
. Conversely, non-similar normal modes represent more general cases, where the relationship between the displacement of the material points of the system is nonlinear, resulting in modal curves in configuration space; see Appendix
A
. This nonlinear relation makes computation of non-similar NNMs, more challenging compared to similar NNMs
Rosenberg and Kuo 1964b
,
Rosenberg and Kuo 1964a
,
Rand 1971
. Manevitch and Mikhlin introduced a power series method
Manevich and Mikhlin 1972
,
Mikhlin 1996
, later developed into
Complexification-Averaging
(
CX-A
) technique by Manevitch
Manevitch 1999
,
Manevitch 2001
,
Manevitch et al. 2003
, for computing non-similar NNMs for conservative systems. Nayfeh
Nayfeh and Nayfeh 1994
,
Nayfeh et al. 1996
extended this technique for continuous systems. Vakakis
Vakakis 2001
,
Vakakis et al. 2003
,
Vakakis et al. 2008
, Gendelman
Gendelman et al. 2001
,
Gendelman et al. 2008
, Kerschen
Kerschen et al. 2009
,
Kerschen 2014
and others
Starosvetsky and
Gendelman 2008a
,
Starosvetsky and
Gendelman 2008b
,
Viguié et al. 2009
,
Viguié and Kerschen 2009
,
Sapsis et al. 2009
conducted detailed investigation of energy transfer mechanism between two substructures of a multi degree of freedom dissipative system, focusing on developing methodologies to compute Kauderer-Rosenberg type NNMs using Manevitch’s CX-A technique for various engineering problems. These investigations involve study of the underlying Hamiltonian structure, despite these systems being essentially non-conservative. This makes the notion of NNMs less robust for non-conservative systems.

The concept of invariant manifolds, based on
centre manifold technique

Carr 1981
,
Guckenheimer and Holmes 2013
,
Glendinning 1994
has been introduced by Shaw and Pierre
Shaw and Pierre 1991
,
Shaw and Pierre 1993
,
Shaw and Pierre 1994
,
Pierre et al. 2006
to define NNMs for non-conservative systems. They defined NNMs as
2
-dimensional invariant manifolds (see Appendix
C
), embedded in
n
-dimensional state space, and are tangent to the LNMs (linear eigenspaces) of the linearized system at the equilibrium points. Shaw’s technique requires solving a large number of nonlinear algebraic equations, depending on the desired degree of approximation and the type of nonlinearity in the system; making this approach computationally challenging for systems with higher degrees of freedom. Furthermore, parameterization works only in the vicinity of equilibrium in the presence of strong nonlinear interactions
Haller and Ponsioen 2016
,
Ponsioen et al. 2018
,
Li et al. 2023
,
Cirillo et al. 2016
. Petromichelakis
et.al.

Petromichelakis and
Kougioumtzoglou 2021
developed a computational algebraic geometrical technique based on the concept of Gröbner basis to address the computational challenges. More recently, Nield
et. al.

Neild et al. 2015
reported that the Shaw–Pierre type invariant surfaces are non-unique in the linearized system, raising uncertainties about the persistence of the parameterization of the manifolds. Haller
et. al.

Haller and Ponsioen 2016
,
Ponsioen et al. 2018
,
Li et al. 2023
introduced a generalized definition of NNMs using
Spectral Sub-Manifolds
(
SSM
s), defined as the smoothest member of an invariant manifold family tangent to a modal sub-bundle along a NNM, to address the uniqueness of the Shaw-Pierre type NNMs. Though these methodologies are well developed, the issue with non-global parameterization remains unresolved.

Cirillo
et. al.

Cirillo et al. 2016
addressed the challenges associated with the non-uniqueness and limited non-global validity of parameterization in Shaw-Pierre NNMs, by adapting an operator theoretic approach, named
Koopman Operator Theory

Koopman 1931
. This approach and related literature
Mezić 2013
,
Lan and Mezić 2013
suggest that only one of these invariant manifolds is infinitely many times differentiable (
i.e.
, is analytic), which is a necessary condition for unique parameterization of the manifold. Without further analysis, it has been stated that there exists a unique, analytic Shaw-Pierre type invariant surface tangent to any two-dimensional modal subspace of a nonlinear system if the eigenvalues of the linearized system are nonresonant. Lan and Mezić
Lan and Mezić 2013
have claimed that global validation of parameterization can be achieved with the Koopman operator framework. However, while this framework accurately predicts the trajectories for nonlinear oscillators with
linear coupling terms
(like the systems considered in
Cirillo et al. 2016
), the presence of any nonlinearity in the coupling often results in strong nonlinear interactions, inducing nonlinear internal resonance. Recognizing these potential challenges, the motivation for further development of the Koopman operator framework and extension of the domain of application appears to be a necessary and promising direction for further research in NNMs.

3
Koopman operator

The Koopman operator theory
Koopman 1931
provides a framework for studying nonlinear dynamical systems by mapping the state space variable to an infinite-dimensional vector space, such as the Hilbert space
Debnath and Mikusinski 2005
. The fundamental principle of this framework in the calculation of NNMs, lies in the spectral properties
Hoffmann and Kunze 1971
,
Artin 1991
of the Koopman operator, with the objective being estimating the eigenfunctions of the operator and representing the dynamics in the Koopman operator spectrum. This is equivalent to decoupling the equations of the nonlinear dynamical system (see Sec.
4
) and representing the dynamical response as a linear combination of NNMs.

Artin 1991

3.2
Eigenfunctions of the Koopman operator

The Koopman operator is a linear transformation
Hoffmann and Kunze 1971
,
Artin 1991
,
\mathcal{K}^{t}\colon\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
, thus have eigenvalues and corresponding eigenfunctions. The eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{K}^{t}
are those observables
s(\mathbb{x})\in\mathcal{G}
, defined as

\centering\mathcal{K}^{t}s(\mathbb{x})=s(\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbb{x},t))=s(\mathbb{x})\exp{(\lambda t)},\@add@centering

(5)

where,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
(in general) is the corresponding eigenvalue of the eigenfunction
s(\mathbb{x})
.
The Koopman eigenfunctions have an interesting property, which enables constructing an eigenfunction from two different eigenfunctions. This can be mathematically stated as

\centering\mathcal{K}^{t}s_{1,2}(\mathbb{x})=s_{1,2}(\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbb{x},t))=s_{1}(\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbb{x},t))s_{2}(\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbb{x},t))=s_{1}(\mathbb{x})s_{2}(\mathbb{x})\exp{((\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})t)},\@add@centering

(6)

where
s_{1,2}(\mathbb{x})=s_{1}(\mathbb{x})s_{2}(\mathbb{x})
is the constructed eigenfunction with eigenvalue
(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})
, from two different eigenfunctions
s_{1}(\mathbb{x})
and
s_{2}(\mathbb{x})
with corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
.
In general, any linear combination of eigenvalues is an eigenvalue of the Koopman operator, and which corresponds to an eigenfunction of the Koopman operator. Thus, in general

\centering s_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}(\mathbb{x})=s_{1}^{k_{1}}(\mathbb{x})\cdots s_{n}^{k_{n}}(\mathbb{x})\@add@centering

(7)

and the corresponding eigenvalue is
\lambda_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}}=k_{1}\lambda_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}\lambda_{n}
; for
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Here
\mathbb{N}_{0}
is the set of all
non-negative integers
, defined as,
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of all natural numbers. These eigenvalues constitute the
spectrum
of the Koopman operator
Kolmogorov and Fomin 1957
.

An additional important property embedded in the Koopman eigenfunctions is the invariance property of the
zero level sets
of these eigenfunctions. Zero level sets are the set of points where a function yields zero values

. Let
s(\mathbb{x})
be an eigenfunction and
\lambda
be the corresponding eigenvalue. Then its zero level set
\{\mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|s(\mathbb{x})=0\}
is invariant for dynamics, as for any initial condition
s(\mathbb{x}_{0})=0
, the corresponding trajectory will satisfy
s(\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbb{x}_{0},t))=\exp{(\lambda t)}s(\mathbb{x}_{0})=0
. Later in this paper, it will be shown that the invariance property of the Koopman eigenfunction is analogous to the invariant definition of NNMs and how this property plays a major role in finding NNMs.

3.3
Spectral expansion of a linear system

The motivation for applying the Koopman operator theory for nonlinear dynamical systems lies in its spectral properties. This enables applying principles of linear algebra to decouple the dynamics and represent the flow in terms of linear combinations of the modal dynamics.
Consider a linear system,

\centering\dot{\mathbb{x}}=\mathbb{A}\mathbb{x},\ \ \mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\@add@centering

(8)

where
\mathbb{A}
is a
n\times n
diagonalizable matrix with distinct eigenvalues. As is well known, the corresponding flow can be represented in terms of the linear combination of the eigenvectors as

\centering\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbb{x},t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathbb{x})\mathbb{v}_{k}\exp({\lambda_{k}t}),\@add@centering

(9)

where
\mathbb{v}_{k}
is an eigenvector of
\mathbb{A}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
and
\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathbb{x})
is the projection of
\mathbb{x}
on to the eigenspace generated by
\mathbb{v}_{k}
, given by

\centering\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathbb{x})=\langle\mathbb{x}|\mathbb{v}_{k}\rangle=\mathbb{w}_{k}^{*}\mathbb{x}.\@add@centering

(10)

Here
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
represents the inner product,
∗
denotes the conjugate transpose and
\mathbb{w}_{k}
are the left eigenvectors of
\mathbb{A}
. In other words, these are also the eigenvectors of
\mathbb{A}^{*}

As
\mathbb{A}
has distinct eigenvalues, it can easily be proved (see Appendix
B

\centering\mathbb{w}_{k}^{*}\mathbb{v}_{j}=\begin{cases}1,&k=j\\
0,&k\neq j.\end{cases}\@add@centering

(11)

This orthonormality condition leads to Eq.(
10

\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathbb{x})
therefore leads to

\centering\mathcal{K}^{t}\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathbb{x})=\mathcal{P}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbb{x},t))=\mathbb{w}_{k}^{*}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{j}(\mathbb{x})\mathbb{v}_{j}\exp({\lambda_{j}t})=\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathbb{x})\exp{(\lambda_{k}t)}.\@add@centering

(12)

Eq.(
12

\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathbb{x})
are indeed the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{K}^{t}
, corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
, of the matrix
\mathbb{A}
. For the rest of this paper, the notation

(as,
\mathcal{P}_{k}(\mathbb{x})\equiv s_{k}(\mathbb{x})

\mathbb{A}
and the eigenfunctions of the Koopman operator. Therefore

\centering\boldsymbol{\phi}(\mathbb{x},t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}s_{k}(\mathbb{x})\mathbb{v}_{k}\exp({\lambda_{k}t}),\@add@centering

(13)

is used to represent the flow.

Consider a nonlinear

Systems with nonlinear internal resonance

The Koopman operator theory appears to be a powerful tool for identifying NNMs with certain limitations, as Theorem
4.2

8
Conclusions

This study focused on the applicability and robustness of the Koopman operator framework across a broad parameter regime. The key highlights of this investigation are as follows:

(a)

The Proposition
1
, which enables unique mapping of the state variables on to the space of observables, is necessary for the further advancement of the procedure. While this was used in
Cirillo et al. 2016
, the uniqueness of the mapping is proved here for the first time.

(b)

A connection of the Koopman eigenfunction with the diffeomorphism stated in the Hartman theorem (Theorem
4.1
) has also been established using the theorems on linearization and from Corollary
1
.

(c)

Further, it has been established the importance of Sternberg’s theorem (Theorem
4.2
), which ensures the existence of the analytic diffeomorphism of the desired invariant manifold in the neighborhood of the hyperbolic fixed point, in the context of the nonlinear state space transformation used in the proposed framework.

(d)

The effects of damping and internal resonance on the Koopman modes have been studied through extensive parametric numerical investigations. As damping contributes to the real part of the eigenvalue whereas the instantaneous frequency contributes to the imaginary part of the eigenvalue, in some sense this is somewhat equivalent to observing the Koopman modes at different regimes of eigenvalues.

(e)

The limitations of the proposed framework for systems with characteristics similar to ENO have been analysed.

It emerges from this study that the accuracy of the Koopman operator framework strongly depends on the damping of the system and the closeness of the system to internal resonance. According to Sternberg theorem, the existence of resonating eigenvalues forfeits the analytic diffeomorphism of the manifold which otherwise naturally exists in the neighbourhood of the origin, leading towards folding of the manifold. In this paper, such cases of resonating eigenvalues have been avoided but the presence of strong nonlinearity ensures nonlinear internal resonance which appears to be beneficial for the proposed framework. Although this framework fails for systems with ENO, the framework is shown to be quite robust for a system that is very similar to ENO.

Equivariantly normal varieties for diagonalizable group actions
Michel Brion

Abstract
Given a finite group scheme G G over a field and a G G -variety X X , we obtain a criterion for X X to be G G -normal in the sense of [ Br24 ] . When G G is diagonalizable, we describe the local structure of G G -normal varieties in codimension 1 1 and their dualizing sheaf. As an application, we obtain a version of the Hurwitz formula for G G -normal varieties, where G G is linearly reductive.



1
Introduction

This paper is a sequel to
[
Br24
]
,
which considered actions of finite group
schemes over fields of positive characteristic.
In this setting, an action on a variety

X
does not necessarily lift to an
action on the normalization
\tilde{X}
;
as a remedy, the notion of equivariant
normalization was introduced and explored.
The present paper focuses on
G
-normal
varieties, where
G
is a finite
diagonalizable group scheme,
with two main motivations in mind:
firstly, finite diagonalizable group
schemes have an especially simple structure
and representation theory, which makes them
very accessible. Secondly, they are quite
ubiquitous; for example, the study of

G
-normal varieties with
G
linearly reductive
reduces to some extent to the diagonalizable
case (see Remark
4.4
for details,
and Section
9
for an illustration).

Given a finite diagonalizable group scheme

G
acting faithfully on a
G
-normal
variety
X
with categorical quotient

\pi:X\to Y
,
the variety
Y=X/G
is known to be normal
and
\pi
is a
G
-torsor over a dense open
subset of
Y
. We may thus view
X
as a
“ramified
G
-cover” of
Y

(as a partial converse,
every variety obtained as a
G
-torsor
over a normal variety is
G
-normal).
When
G
is constant,
\pi
is indeed
a ramified Galois cover with group
G

and
X
is normal; this gives back
the classically studied abelian covers,
see e.g.
[
Pa91
,
AP12
]
. But the general
case has quite different geometric features:
if
G
is infinitesimal, then
\pi
is
purely inseparable,
and hence ramified everywhere. Moreover,

X
is generally singular in codimension
1
,
with cuspidal singularities only. Still,

X
satisfies Serre’s property
(S_{2})
,
as well as an equivariant version of
(R_{1})
:
every
G
-stable divisor is Cartier at its
generic points.

For simplicity, we now state our main results
in the setting of curves over an algebraically
closed field
k
. Let
X
be such a curve,
equipped with a faithful
G
-action with
quotient
\pi:X\to Y
. We assume that

X
is
G
-normal; this is equivalent to
the orbit
G\cdot x\subset X

being a Cartier divisor for any
x\in X(k)
,
and implies that the curve
Y
is smooth.
Denote by
\Stab_{G}(x)\subset G
the stabilizer
of
x
; it acts linearly on the fibre at
x

of the conormal sheaf to
G\cdot x

via a character
\nu(x):\Stab_{G}(x)\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.
We may now describe the equivariant local
structure of
X
at
x
:

Theorem 1
.

(i)

The group scheme

H=\Stab_{G}(x)
is cyclic, and
\nu(x)

generates its character group.

(ii)

There exists
an open
G
-stable neighborhood

U=U(x)\subset X
of
x

such that the quotient morphism

U\to U/G=V
factors as

U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}U/H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}V
,
where
\varphi
is a cyclic cover
of degree
|H|
,
and
\psi
is a
G/H
-torsor.

By a cyclic cover of degree
n
in this
local setting, we mean a morphism of affine
schemes
\varphi:{\rm Spec}(A)\to{\rm Spec}(B)
,
where
A=B[T]/(T^{n}-g)
and
g\in B

is a nonzerodivisor. Then
\varphi

is the quotient by the cyclic group scheme

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
acting on
{\rm Spec}(A)
via the

{\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading

A=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{n-1}B\bar{T}^{m}
;
also,
\varphi
is a
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
-torsor outside
of the zero scheme of
g
.

As a consequence of Theorem

1
, every
G
-normal curve
is a tamely ramified
G
-torsor in
the sense of
[
BB22
, Def. 2.1]
.
But equivariant normality imposes
additional conditions; for example,
a cyclic cover as above is

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}
-normal if and only if

B
is an integrally closed domain
and the zero scheme of
g
is reduced.

As a further consequence, the finite
morphism
\pi
is flat and
a local complete intersection. So
its dualizing sheaf is isomorphic to
the relative canonical sheaf

\omega_{X/Y}
, and hence is invertible.

Theorem 2
.

The sheaf
\omega_{X/Y}
is equipped with
a
G
-linearization and a
G
-invariant
global section
s_{X/Y}
such that

{\rm div}(s_{X/Y})=\sum\big(|\Stab_{G}(x)|-1)\big)\,G\cdot x

(sum over the
G
-orbits of
k
-rational
points of
X
).

For a projective
G
-normal curve
X
,
this readily yields the relation between
arithmetic genera

2p_{a}(X)-2=|G|\,\big(2p_{a}(Y)-2\big)+\sum\big(|\Stab_{G}(x)|-1\big),

(1.1)

where the sum runs again over the orbits.
(We have of course
p_{a}(Y)=g(Y)
as
Y

is smooth; also,
g(X)=g(Y)
if
G
is
infinitesimal, see
[
Br24
, Rem. 5.2]
).

The above results are used in the
preprint
[
FM24
]
by P. Fong and M. Maccan,
which studies a remarkable class
of smooth projective surfaces in positive
characteristics:
relatively minimal surfaces equipped with
an isotrivial elliptic fibration. It turns out that
every such surface
S
is obtained as
a quotient
(E\times X)/G
,
where
E
is an elliptic curve,

G\subset E
a finite subgroup scheme,
and
X
a projective
G
-normal curve.
Thus,
S
is equipped with an action of
the algebraic group

E
for which the quotient is the elliptic
fibration
f:S\to X/G=Y
. When

G
is diagonalizable, the multiple fibers
and relative canonical bundle of
f

are described in terms of the quotient

\pi:X\to Y
in
[
FM24
, §3]
,
based on Theorems
1

and
2
. It would be very
interesting to obtain a global description
of
G
-normal curves in this setting,
by gluing their local models in the spirit of

[
Pa91
]
.

Theorem
1
is well-known if

G
is constant; then
X
is a smooth curve,
the morphism
\psi:U/H\to V
is étale,
and
\pi
is a tamely ramified
G
-cover.
In this setting, Theorem
2

and (
1.1
) follow from
the classical Hurwitz formula.
For an arbitrary finite group scheme
G
,
the morphism
\psi
may well be purely
inseparable. Still, its dualizing sheaf is
equipped with a natural section,
and this reduces the proof of Theorem

2
to a local computation.
In view of this theorem, the branch locus
of
\pi
(i.e., the locus where it fails to
be a
G
-torsor) can be read off the
relative canonical sheaf
\omega_{X/Y}
.

In the general setting of a
G
-normal variety

X
over an arbitrary field, where
G

is diagonalizable, we obtain versions of
Theorems
1
and
2

over an open
G
-stable subset of
X
with
complement of codimension at least
2
;
see Theorems
6.2
and
7.4
.
In this setting, the relative dualizing
sheaf
\omega_{\pi}
is torsion-free and
satisfies
(S_{2})
, so that it suffices
to determine it in codimension
1
;
see
[
Kol22
]
for further aspects of
duality for torsion-free
(S_{2})
sheaves.

The layout of this paper is as follows.
Sections
2
and
3

collect preliminary notions and results
on actions of finite group schemes over
a field, especially
diagonalizable ones. For these, we discuss
torsors and uniform cyclic covers in the
sense of
[
AV04
]
(Remarks
3.2

and
3.3
),
which form the main ingredients of our local
structure results. Our presentation has some
overlap with
[
Hau20
, Sec. 3]
; we have
provided details in order to be self-contained.
The main result of Section
4

is a
G
-normality criterion for a finite
group scheme
G

(Proposition
4.5
), which takes
a simpler form when
G
is diagonalizable.
Under this assumption, we obtain our local
structure theorem by steps: in Section

5
, we first describe the fibres of
the quotient morphism
\pi
at points of
codimension
1
for a
G
-normal variety
X
,
and then the corresponding semi-local rings
of
X
; these may be viewed as equivariant
analogs of discrete valuation rings
(see Propositions

and

5.4
).
From this, we deduce a more global
description of

in codimension
1

(Theorem

, the main result of
Section

). In turn, this
yields in Section
7

a formula à la Hurwitz for the dualizing
sheaf of the quotient morphism

\pi
. Section

contains applications to curves;
in particular, we determine the arithmetic
genus of projective
G
-normal curves,
and its equivariant analog
(Proposition
8.2
).
In Section
9
,
we extend our description of the dualizing

and
Proposition

).

2
Finite group schemes and their actions

Throughout this paper, we work over a field
k

of characteristic
p>0
.
Schemes are assumed to be separated and
of finite type over
k
unless explicitly
mentioned. A
variety

is a geometrically integral scheme.
We say that an open subset
U
of a variety

X
is
big
if its complement

X\setminus U
has codimension at least
2
.

We denote by
G
a finite group scheme and
by
|G|
its order, i.e.,
the dimension of the algebra

{\mathcal{O}}(G)
viewed as a
k
-vector space.
Recall that
G
lies in a unique exact sequence

1\longrightarrow G^{0}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow\pi_{0}(G)\longrightarrow 1,

(2.1)

where
G^{0}
is infinitesimal and
\pi_{0}(G)

is finite and étale. If
k
is perfect, then
this sequence has a unique splitting
(see e.g.
[
DG70
, II.5.2.4]
.

We say that
G
is
linearly reductive

if every
G
-module is semi-simple. By Nagata’s
theorem, this is equivalent to
G^{0}
being of
multiplicative type and
|\pi_{0}(G)|

being prime to
p
(see
[
DG70
, IV.3.3.6]
).

A
G
-
scheme
is a scheme
X

equipped with a
G
-action

\alpha:G\times X\longrightarrow X,\quad(g,x)\longmapsto g\cdot x.

This action is said to be
faithful

if every nontrivial subgroup scheme of
G

acts nontrivially.

For any
G
-scheme
X
and any closed
subscheme
X^{\prime}
, the morphism

G\times X^{\prime}\to X
,
(g,x)\mapsto g\cdot x

is finite. We denote its schematic image by

G\cdot X^{\prime}
; this is the smallest closed

G
-stable subscheme of
X
containing
X^{\prime}
.
Also, we denote by
C_{G}(X^{\prime})
the

centralizer
of
X^{\prime}
, i.e.,
the largest subgroup scheme of
G
that acts
trivially on
X^{\prime}
.
In particular, for any closed point
x\in X
,
we obtain the
orbit

G\cdot x

and the centralizer
C_{G}(x)
.
These will be discussed at the end of the next
paragraph.

Given a field extension
K/k
and a scheme

X
, we denote by
X_{K}
the
K
-scheme
obtained from
X
by the base change

{\rm Spec}(K)\to{\rm Spec}(k)
. Then

G_{K}
is a finite
K
-group scheme
and the formation of (
2.1
)
commutes with such base change.
Moreover, every
G
-action
\alpha
on
X

yields a
G_{K}
-action
\alpha_{K}
on
X_{K}
,
which may be identified with a
G
-action on
the
k
-scheme
X_{K}
via the isomorphism

G_{K}\times_{{\rm Spec}(K)}X_{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}G\times X_{K}.

The canonical projection
{\rm pr}:X_{K}\to X

is equivariant relative
to the homomorphism
G_{K}\to G
.
If
x\in X
is a closed point with residue
field
K=\kappa(x)
, then viewing
x

as a
K
-point of
X_{K}
, the orbit

G_{K}\cdot x\subset X_{K}
has schematic image

G\cdot x
under the above projection. Moreover,
we have an isomorphism of
G_{K}
-schemes

G_{K}\cdot x\simeq G_{K}/C_{G_{K}}(x)

and the equality
C_{G_{K}}(x)=\Stab_{G}(x)
,
the fibre at
x
of the stabilizer

\Stab_{G}\subset G\times X
.

Next, we discuss quotients by
G
; for this,
we only consider
G
-schemes
X
that admit
a covering by open affine
G
-stable subsets.
This assumption is satisfied if
G
is
infinitesimal (then every open subset is

G
-stable), or if
X
is quasi-projective
(e.g., a curve). Then
X
admits
a categorical quotient

\pi:X\to Y=X/G
,
where
Y
is a scheme,
\pi

is finite and surjective, and the morphism

\gamma=(\alpha,{\rm pr}_{2}):G\times X\longrightarrow X\times_{Y}X,\quad(g,x)\longmapsto(g\cdot x,x)

(2.2)

is surjective as well. Therefore,

{\mathcal{A}}=\pi_{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{X})
is a coherent
algebra over
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
, equipped with
a
G
-linearization.
Moreover, the natural map

{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{G}
is an isomorphism
(see
[
DG70
, III.2.6.1]
for these results).
If
X
is a variety, then so is
Y
.

Also, recall that
X
has a largest open

G
-stable subset
X_{{\rm fr}}
on which
G

acts freely. Moreover,
\pi
restricts to
a
G
-torsor
X_{{\rm fr}}\to Y_{{\rm fr}}
, where

Y_{{\rm fr}}
is open in
Y
(see loc. cit.).
We say that a
G
-variety
X
is

generically free
if
X_{{\rm fr}}
is
nonempty.

The quotient morphism
\pi
is the composition

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z=X/G^{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y,

(2.3)

where
\varphi
(resp.
\psi
) denotes the
quotient by
G^{0}
(resp.
\pi_{0}(G)
).
Moreover,
\varphi
is purely inseparable,
and hence a universal homeomorphism.
The formation of
\pi
,
\varphi
and
\psi

commutes with flat base change
Y^{\prime}\to Y
;
in particular, with field extensions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
X
is a
G
-variety. Then
the
G
-action on
X
is faithful
(resp. generically free, free)
if and only if the
G^{0}
-action on
X

is faithful (resp. generically free, free)
and the
\pi_{0}(G)
-action on
X/G^{0}

is faithful (resp. free).

Proof.

By fpqc descent, we may assume
k

algebraically closed. Then

G=G^{0}\rtimes\pi_{0}(G)
and hence

G(k)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi_{0}(G)(k)
; moreover,
\pi_{0}(G)

is constant.

Denote by
H
the kernel of the
G
-action
on
X
. Then also

H\simeq H^{0}\rtimes\pi_{0}(H)
,
and
\pi_{0}(H)
is constant. Moreover,

H^{0}
(resp.
\pi_{0}(H)
) is the kernel
of the action of
G^{0}
(resp.
\pi_{0}(G)
)
on
X
. Since
\varphi
is bijective,
it follows that
\pi_{0}(H)
is the kernel
of the
\pi_{0}(G)
-action on
Z
. This
readily implies the assertion on faithful
actions.

The assertion on free actions is obtained
similarly by considering the stabilizers
of
k
-rational points of
X
.
This assertion implies that the
G
-action
on
X
is generically free if and only if
so are the
G^{0}
-action on
X
and the

\pi_{0}(G)
-action on
Z
. But for an action
of a finite group (that is, a finite constant
group scheme) on a variety,
being generically free is equivalent to being
faithful; this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a
G
-variety with quotient

\pi:X\to Y
, and let
X^{\prime}\subset X
be
a closed
G
-stable subscheme.

(i)

The quotient morphism

\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}=X^{\prime}/G
exists and lies in
a commutative square

\textstyle{X^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\scriptstyle{\pi^{\prime}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{Y^{\prime}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{j}
\textstyle{Y,}

where
i
denotes the inclusion, and

j
is finite and purely inseparable.
If
G
is linearly reductive, then
j
is
a closed immersion.

(ii)

For any open subset

U\subset X
such that
U\cap X^{\prime}

is dense in
X^{\prime}
, there exists an open

G
-stable subset
V\subset U
such that

V\cap X^{\prime}
is dense in
X^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(i) Since
X^{\prime}\subset X
is covered by

G
-stable open affine subsets, it admits
a categorical quotient
\pi^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\to Y^{\prime}
.
Moreover, the universal property of
\pi

yields a unique morphism
j:Y^{\prime}\to Y

such that
j\circ\pi^{\prime}=i\circ\pi
;
as a consequence,
j\circ\pi^{\prime}
is finite.
Since
\pi^{\prime}
is dominant, it follows that

j
is finite. Also, for any algebraically
closed field extension
K/k
,
the map
j(K):Y^{\prime}(K)\to Y(K)
is injective,
since
Y^{\prime}(K)
is the orbit space

X^{\prime}(K)/G(K)
and likewise

Y(K)=X(K)/G(K)
.
So
j
is purely inseparable.

To show the final assertion, we may assume

X
affine. Then the restriction map

{\mathcal{O}}(X)\to{\mathcal{O}}(X^{\prime})
is surjective,
and hence so is

{\mathcal{O}}(X)^{G}\to{\mathcal{O}}(X^{\prime})^{G}

by linear reductivity. Equivalently,

j^{\#}:{\mathcal{O}}(Y)\to{\mathcal{O}}(Y^{\prime})
is surjective
as desired.

(ii) If
G
is constant, then it permutes the
generic points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
of
X^{\prime}
.
By assumption,

U\cap X^{\prime}\supset\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}

and hence
V=\bigcap_{g\in G}g\cdot U

is an open
G
-stable subset of
X

such that

V\cap X^{\prime}\supset\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.
This yields our assertion in this case,
and hence in the case where
G
is étale
by Galois descent. The general case follows
from this by using the factorization
(
2.3
) of
\pi
.
∎

With the assumption of Lemma
2.2
,
we denote by
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
the generic
points of
X^{\prime}
and let

{\mathcal{O}}_{X,X^{\prime}}={\mathcal{O}}_{X,x_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x_{n}}.

(2.4)

This is a semi-local ring, and its maximal
ideals correspond bijectively to

x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

With the above notation, the ring
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,X^{\prime}}

is equipped with a (functorial)
G
-action,
and is a union of finite-dimensional
G
-submodules.

Proof.

We have

{\mathcal{O}}_{X,X^{\prime}}=\varinjlim{\mathcal{O}}(U)
,
where
U
runs over the open subsets of

X
containing
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
By Lemma
2.2
, it follows that

{\mathcal{O}}_{X,X^{\prime}}=\varinjlim{\mathcal{O}}(V)
,
where
V
runs over the above open subsets
which are
G
-stable. This readily yields the
assertions.
∎

In what follows, we will consider
G
-varieties
with a prescribed quotient
Y
, that is,
pairs
(X,\pi)
, where
X
is a
G
-variety and

\pi:X\to Y
is the quotient morphism.
Assuming in addition that the
G
-action is
faithful, we say that
X
is a

G
-variety over
Y
.
Morphisms of such pairs are
G
-equivariant
morphisms of varieties over
Y
; in particular,
the automorphism group of
(X,\pi)
is
the group
{\rm Aut}^{G}_{Y}(X)
of equivariant
relative automorphisms. We now obtain
a description of this group, under
assumptions that will be fulfilled by
diagonalizable groups.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
Y
be a variety, and
X
a
G
-variety
over
Y
.

(i)

The quotient morphism

\varphi:X\to X/G^{0}=Z
induces
an injective homomorphism

\varphi_{*}:{\rm Aut}_{Y}^{G^{0}}(X)\to{\rm Aut}_{Y}(Z)
.

(ii)

The natural homomorphism

\pi_{0}(G)(k)\to{\rm Aut}_{Y}(Z)
is bijective.

(iii)

If
G
is commutative and
the exact sequence (
2.1
) splits,
then

G(k)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Aut}_{Y}^{G^{0}}(X)={\rm Aut}_{Y}^{G}(X).

Proof.

The existence of the homomorphism

\varphi_{*}
follows from the universal
property of the categorical quotient.
Clearly, the kernel of
\varphi_{*}
is
isomorphic to
{\rm Aut}_{Z}(X)
, and hence is
a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{k(Z)}(k(X))
. Since
the extension of function fields

k(X)/k(Z)
is purely inseparable, it follows
that
\varphi_{*}
is injective, proving (i).

By Lemma
2.1
, the action of

\pi_{0}(G)
on
Z
is generically free.
Therefore, we may identify
{\rm Aut}_{Y}(Z)
with
a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}_{Y_{{\rm fr}}}(Z_{{\rm fr}})
,
where
Z_{{\rm fr}}\to Y_{{\rm fr}}
is a

\pi_{0}(G)
-torsor. Moreover,
we have natural isomorphisms

{\rm Aut}_{Y_{{\rm fr}}}(Z_{{\rm fr}})\simeq\Hom(Z_{{\rm fr}},\pi_{0}(G))\simeq\Hom(\pi_{0}(Z_{{\rm fr}}),\pi_{0}(G))\simeq\pi_{0}(G)(k),

where the second one follows from

[
DG70
, I.4.6.5]
, and the third one
holds since
Z_{{\rm fr}}
is a variety
and hence
\pi_{0}(Z_{{\rm fr}})={\rm Spec}(k)
.
This implies (ii).

Under the assumptions of (iii), the group

G(k)
acts on
X
by
G
-equivariant
automorphisms over
Y
, and

G(k)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi_{0}(G)(k)
. This yields the desired
assertion by combining (i) and (ii).
∎

3
Diagonalizable group schemes

We assume that
G
is diagonalizable,
and denote by

\Lambda=\Lambda(G)=\Hom_{{\rm gp}}(G,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})

its character group. Recall that
\Lambda

is a finite abelian group, and we have
a
\Lambda
-grading

{\mathcal{O}}(G)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}k\lambda.

(3.1)

We say that

For an arbitrary diagonalizable group

Given a

Lemma 3.1
.

With the above notation,
\pi
is a
G
-torsor
if and only if
{\rm mult}_{\lambda,\mu}
is an
isomorphism for all
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda
.
Under these assumptions, each

is an isomorphism as well.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from

[
SGA3
, Exp. VIII, Prop. 4.1]
;
we present a proof for completeness.
If
\pi
is the trivial torsor

{\rm pr}_{Y}:G\times Y\to Y
,
then
{\mathcal{A}}
is identified with

{\mathcal{B}}\otimes_{k}{\mathcal{O}}(G)
as a
{\mathcal{B}}
-
G
-algebra,
where

acts on
{\mathcal{O}}(G)
via its action on
itself by multiplication. Using the
decomposition (

),
this identifies each multiplication map

{\rm mult}_{\lambda,\mu}
with the map

{\mathcal{B}}\otimes_{k}k\lambda\otimes_{k}k\mu\to{\mathcal{B}}\otimes_{k}k(\lambda+\mu)

induced by the multiplication in
{\mathcal{O}}(G)
.
As a consequence,
{\rm mult}_{\lambda,\mu}
is an
isomorphism. By fpqc descent, this still holds
if
\pi
is an arbitrary

-torsor.

Conversely, assume that each

{\rm mult}_{\lambda,\mu}
is an isomorphism.
Then the multiplication map induces
an isomorphism

{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}\otimes_{{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{A}}_{-\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{B}}

for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
. In particular,
every
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}
is invertible,
and hence
\pi
is finite and flat.
To show that it is a
G
-torsor, it suffices
to check that the morphism
(

) is an isomorphism.
As in the proof of Lemma

,
we may assume that

and

Y={\rm Spec}(B)
. Then
\gamma
corresponds
to the algebra homomorphism

\gamma^{\#}:A\otimes_{B}A\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(G)\otimes_{k}A,\quad a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\longmapsto(g\mapsto(g\cdot a_{1})a_{2}),

where
{\mathcal{O}}(G)\otimes_{k}A
is identified
with the space of morphisms
G\to A
.
Therefore,

\gamma^{\#}=\bigoplus_{\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda}\gamma^{\#}_{\lambda,\mu}
,
where

\gamma^{\#}_{\lambda,\mu}:A_{\lambda}\otimes_{B}A_{\mu}\to k\lambda\otimes_{k}A_{\lambda+\mu}

satisfies

\gamma^{\#}_{\lambda,\mu}=\lambda\otimes{\rm mult}_{\lambda,\mu}

Thus, each
\gamma^{\#}_{\lambda,\mu}

This completes the proof of the first
assertion ; the second assertion is
obtained by a similar descent argument.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The above lemma takes a much more concrete

Remark 3.3
.

More generally, consider a scheme

.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a
G
-variety.

(i)

Each
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}
is a torsion-free

{\mathcal{B}}
-module of rank

, and

\Lambda(X)=\{\lambda\in\Lambda~|~{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}\neq 0\}

is a subgroup of
\Lambda
.

(ii)

The

-action on

is faithful if and
only if
\Lambda(X)=\Lambda
; under these
assumptions, the action is generically free.

Proof.

(i) We may assume again that
X={\rm Spec}(A)

and
Y={\rm Spec}(B)
. Then

A=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}A_{\lambda}

is a
\Lambda
-graded domain, hence

is a domain and each
A_{\lambda}

is a torsion-free
B
-module. Denoting
by

(resp.

of

(resp.

), we have

[

, Lem. 2.3 (ii)]
) and hence

, where

.
Thus,

if and

is a

(ii) Since

Then

∎

4
Equivariantly normal varieties

Let
G
be a finite group scheme, and

X
a
G
-variety. Following

[
Br24
, Def. 4.1]
, we say that
X
is

G
-
normal
if every finite birational
morphism of
G
-varieties
X^{\prime}\to X

is an isomorphism.

By loc. cit., §4, this notion of equivariant
normality is related to the usual notion of
normality as follows: we may view
G

as a closed subgroup scheme of a smooth
connected algebraic group
G^{\#}

(for example, we may embed
G
in
{\rm GL}_{n}

via the regular representation, where

n=|G|
). Given a
G
-variety

X
, the product
G^{\#}\times X

is a variety equipped with an action of

G^{\#}\times G
via

(a,g)\cdot(b,x)=(abg^{-1},g\cdot x)
.
Since
G^{\#}
and
X
are covered by open
affine
G
-stable subsets, the quotient

X^{\#}=(G^{\#}\times X)/G
exists;
this is a variety equipped with an action
of
G^{\#}
and a
G^{\#}
-equivariant
morphism
\varphi:X^{\#}\to G^{\#}/G
.
The fibre of
\varphi
at the base point
of the homogeneous space
G^{\#}/G

is
G
-equivariantly isomorphic to
X
.
We say that
X^{\#}
is the

induced variety

G^{\#}\times^{G}X
.

Lemma 4.1
.

With the above notation,
X
is
G
-normal
if and only if
X^{\#}
is normal.

This is proved by the same argument as

[
Br24
, Lem. 4.7]
, which deals with
the quotient
(G^{\#}\times X)/G^{0}
.
As an application, we show:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
Y
be a variety, and
u:X\to X^{\prime}

a morphism of
G
-varieties over
Y
.
If
X^{\prime}
is
G
-normal and generically free,
then
u
is an open immersion.

Proof.

Denote by
\eta
the generic point of
Y
;
then
u
induces a
G_{\kappa(y)}
-equivariant
morphism
u_{\eta}:X_{\eta}\to X^{\prime}_{\eta}

between generic fibres. Moreover,
X^{\prime}_{\eta}

is a
G_{\kappa(y)}
-torsor by assumption, and

X_{\eta}
is
G_{\kappa(y)}
-homogeneous in view
of
[
Br24
, Cor. 2.5]
. Thus,
u_{\eta}

is an isomorphism, and hence
u
is birational.
Also,
u
is quasi-finite, since

\pi=\pi^{\prime}\circ u
is finite. We now
consider the induced varieties
X^{\#}
,

X^{\prime\#}
and the induced morphism

u^{\#}:X^{\#}\to X^{\prime\#}
, which is still
birational and quasi-finite. Since
X^{\prime\#}

is normal (Lemma
4.1
),

u^{\#}
is an open immersion by Zariski’s
Main Theorem. Thus,
u
is an open immersion.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

A finite group scheme
G
is diagonalizable
if and only if it is isomorphic to a subgroup
scheme of a torus
T\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
.
Then the assignement

X\mapsto X^{\#}=T\times^{G}X

defines a bijection
between
G
-normal varieties and normal

T
-varieties equipped with an equivariant
morphism
\varphi
to the quotient torus

T/G
, whose fibre at the base point is
geometrically integral. The dimension of
X

is the
complexity
of the
T
-variety

X^{\#}
(the transcendence degree of the
field of
T
-invariant rational functions).

Given a torus
T
, there is a classification
of normal
T
-varieties in terms of objects
mixing convex and algebraic geometry, called
proper polyhedral divisors and divisorial fans,
which generalize the cones and fans associated
with toric varieties. Over an algebraically
closed field
k
of characteristic
0
,
this classification is obtained in
[
AH06
]

for affine varieties; the general case is
treated in
[
AHS08
]
. Over an arbitrary field,
normal affine
T
-varieties of complexity

1
are also classified by proper polyhedral
divisors, as shown in
[
Lan15
]
.
Yet we do not know how to deduce from this
a full classification of
G
-normal varieties,
which incorporates the additional datum of
the morphism
\varphi
.

Remark 4.4
.

Returning to an arbitrary finite group
scheme
G
, note that a
G
-variety
X

is
G
-normal if and only if
X_{K}
is

G^{0}_{K}
-normal for some separable field

(see

[
Br24
, Cor. 4.8, Prop. 4.9]
).
If

is linearly reductive, we may
take for

a finite Galois extension
such that

is diagonalizable
and

is constant. Then
the quotient morphism

factors uniquely as

,
where

and

is a finite Galois cover
with group

, of order prime to

.
This reduces a number of questions on
equivariantly normal varieties under

for an illustration.

Next, we obtain a criterion for

-normality
of a
G
-variety

in terms of
the
{\mathcal{B}}
-algebra

, with the notation
of Section

.
Clearly, we may assume that
G
acts
faithfully on

.

Let
D
be a prime divisor on

.
Then
G\cdot D
is a closed
G
-stable

, of pure codimension
1
.
We denote by

the

and is equipped with a

-action
(Lemma

).
We have

-variety over

(ii)

(iii)

If

is equivalent to

(ii)’

The sheaf

Recall that a coherent sheaf

)

(i) This is a consequence of

Proof of Proposition

.
By
[

if and only if

satisfies (

)
and every

-stable ideal is invertible in

. Together with the above lemma,
it follows that (ii) holds for every

-normal

Conversely, assume (i), (ii) and (iii).
Then

satisfies (

) by Lemma

again. So it suffices to check
that every

-stable ideal
is invertible in codimension

.
In other words, for any prime divisor

on

, every

-stable ideal

of

is principal.

If

is free, we may replace

with

-stable subset

,
and hence assume that

acts freely on

Then by fpqc descent, the assignement

of

of

. Moreover,

If

It remains to prove the equivalence

\Leftrightarrow

Proposition 5.1

(i)

Remark 5.2

(i)

We show (i) and (ii) simultaneously.

-graded ideal

is

).

It remains to show that

(iii) Note that

The relative dualizing sheaf

Let
G
be a finite group scheme acting
on a scheme

with quotient morphism

\pi:X\to Y
. Since
\pi
is finite,
it has a
(0)
-dualizing sheaf

\omega_{\pi}=\pi^{!}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
; this is
the
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
-module that corresponds to
the
{\mathcal{A}}
-module
{{\mathcal{H}}}{\rm om}_{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
.
It is equipped with a trace map

{\rm tr}_{\pi}:\pi_{*}(\omega_{\pi})\to{\mathcal{B}}

via evaluation at
1
.
If
\pi
is flat and a local complete
intersection (l.c.i.), then
\omega_{\pi}

is isomorphic to the canonical sheaf

\omega_{X/Y}
; in particular,

\omega_{\pi}
is invertible (see
e.g.
[
Li10
, §6.4]
, which will be our
reference for duality theory). In any case,

\omega_{\pi}
is equipped with a

G
-linearization for which the trace map
is invariant.

If
G
is linearly reductive, then the
unique
G
-invariant projection

{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
(the “Reynolds operator”)
yields a global section

s_{\pi}\in\Gamma(X,\omega_{\pi})=\Hom_{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{B}})
.
Note that
s_{\pi}
is
G
-invariant
and satisfies
{\rm tr}_{\pi}(s_{\pi})=1
.
Also, the formation of
\omega_{\pi}

and

commutes with flat base
change
Y^{\prime}\to Y
. If the morphism
\pi

is flat l. c. i, then we denote
s_{\pi}
by

.

Given a subgroup scheme
H\subset G

and the corresponding factorization
of
\pi
as

,
we may identify

\omega_{\pi}
with

\omega_{\varphi}\otimes_{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}\varphi^{*}(\omega_{\psi})

(see
[
Li10
, Lem. 6.4.26]
).
If

is linearly reductive, then we have

s_{\pi}=s_{\varphi}\otimes\varphi^{*}(s_{\psi}),

(7.1)

where

s_{\psi}\in\Gamma(Z,\omega_{\psi})=\Hom_{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}^{H},{\mathcal{B}})
is the
projection
{\mathcal{A}}^{H}\to{\mathcal{B}}
. If in addition

is a normal subgroup of

, then

\psi
is the quotient morphism by
G/H
, and

is the corresponding canonical section.

We will need two further observations, certainly
well-known but for which we could not locate
any reference.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group scheme, and

\pi:X\to Y
a
G
-torsor.

(i)

The (finite flat) morphism
\pi
is l.c.i. and satisfies

\omega_{X/Y}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
.

(ii)

If
G
is linearly reductive, then
s_{X/Y}

is a trivializing section of
\omega_{X/Y}
.

Proof.

(i) We use the construction at the beginning of
Section
4
.
Choose an embedding of
G
in some
{\rm GL}_{n}
.
Then
\pi
is the composition

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z={\rm GL}_{n}\times^{G}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y,

where the morphism
\iota
is a closed immersion
with image
G\times^{G}X
, and
\psi

is the projection

{\rm GL}_{n}\times^{G}X\to X/G=Y
.
Thus,
\psi
is a
{\rm GL}_{n}
-torsor.
In particular, it is smooth and its
relative tangent sheaf is isomorphic to

{\rm Lie}({\rm GL}_{n})\otimes_{k}{\mathcal{O}}_{Z/Y}
.
As a consequence,

\det(\Omega^{1}_{Z/Y})\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{Z/Y}
.
Also, recall that
X
is the fibre at the base
point of the other projection

\varphi:{\rm GL}_{n}\times^{G}X\to{\rm GL}_{n}/G
.
Thus,
\iota
is a regular immersion
and its conormal sheaf
{\mathcal{C}}_{X/Z}

is trivial (see
[
Li10
, Prop. 6.3.11]
).
This gives an isomorphism

\omega_{X/Y}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
in view of
loc. cit., Def. 6.4.7.

(ii) We use the commutative diagram of flat morphisms

\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\rm Spec}(k)}
\textstyle{G\times X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\rm pr}_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\scriptstyle{{\rm pr}_{G}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{Y,}

where both squares are cartesian.
Since the formation of
s_{X/Y}
commutes
with flat base change, this yields

\alpha^{*}(s_{X/Y})=s_{G\times X/X}={\rm pr}_{G}^{*}(s_{G})
. By fpqc descent, we are
reduced to checking that

is a free generator of the
{\mathcal{O}}(G)
-module

\omega_{G}
.

In the case where

is diagonalizable,
the

-vector space
\Hom_{k}({\mathcal{O}}(G),k)

has basis
(
e_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

dual to the basis
\Lambda
of
{\mathcal{O}}(G)
,
and the
{\mathcal{O}}(G)
-module structure of

is given by

.
Therefore, this module is freely generated
by

.

In the general case of a linearly reductive
group

, we may assume

algebraically closed by fpqc descent again.
Then

is diagonalizable and

,
where

is constant of order

.
Thus,

for any

,
where
\varphi_{0}
denotes the

relative to

; note that

as

induces an automorphism of

\Lambda(G^{0})
. Moreover, we have
an isomorphism of algebras

{\mathcal{O}}(G)\simeq\prod_{g\in G(k)}{\mathcal{O}}(gG^{0})

and

for any

As

is a trivial

restricts to a nonzero scalar multiple of

∎

\pi^{*}(\pi(x))=|H(x)|\,G\cdot x

(which follows fromTheorem

2p_{a}(X)-2=|G|\,\big(2p_{a}(Y)-2+\sum_{y\in Y\setminus Y_{{\rm fr}}}(1-\frac{1}{n(y)})\,{\rm deg}(y)\big),

(8.2)

where

for any

.
This generalizes (

) to an arbitrary field.

We may also determine the degree of the
invertible sheaves

on

By Remark

the equality in

As a consequence,

(8.3)

Next, we determine the “equivariant arithmetic

-module

.
As

Proposition 8.2
.

With the above notation, we have

{\rm ch}\,H^{0}(X,\omega_{X})-1=\big(p_{a}(Y)-1\big)\,{\rm ch}\,{\mathcal{O}}(G)+\sum_{y\in Y\setminus Y_{fr}}\frac{\gamma(y)}{n(y)}\,{\rm ch}\,{\mathcal{O}}\big(G/H(y)\big)\,{\rm deg}(y),

where

\gamma(y)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n(y)-1}\ell e^{-\ell\nu(y)}
.

Proof.

For any
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we set

h^{0}(\omega_{X})_{\lambda}=\dim\big(H^{0}(X,\omega_{X})_{\lambda}\big)
.
If
G
acts freely on

\omega_{X}=\pi^{*}(\omega_{Y})

by (

), or directly by
Lemma

. As a consequence,
we obtain isomorphisms of
G

H^{0}(X,\omega_{X})\simeq H^{0}(Y,\omega_{Y}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}),\quad H^{1}(X,\omega_{X})\simeq H^{1}(Y,\omega_{Y}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})

by using the projection formula. Moreover,

H^{1}(X,\omega_{X})
is the trivial
G
-module

k

and

h^{0}(\omega_{X})_{\lambda}=\chi(Y,\omega_{Y}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda})

for any nonzero
\lambda

we obtain

by using the Riemann–Roch theorem.
Thus,

{\rm ch}\,H^{0}(X,\omega_{X})-1=\big(p_{a}(Y)-1\big)\,{\rm ch}\,{\mathcal{O}}(G)

as desired.

So we may assume that

does not act

G\cdot\Delta_{X}
and

Denoting by

0\longrightarrow\omega_{X}\longrightarrow\omega_{X}(G\cdot\Delta_{X})\longrightarrow\iota_{*}\iota^{*}\omega_{X}(G\cdot\Delta_{X})\longrightarrow 0

\longrightarrow H^{1}(X,\omega_{X})\longrightarrow H^{1}\big(X,\omega_{X}(G\cdot\Delta_{X})\big)\longrightarrow 0,

where the sum runs over the

nonfree points. Moreover,

In addition, we obtain isomorphisms

H^{0}\big(X,\omega_{X}(G\cdot\Delta_{X})\big)_{\lambda}\simeq H^{0}\big(Y,\omega_{Y}(\Delta_{Y})\otimes{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda}\big)

Thus,

h^{0}\big(\omega_{X}(G\cdot\Delta_{X})\big)_{\lambda}=p_{a}(Y)-1+\sum_{y\in Y\setminus Y_{{\rm fr}}}\big(1-\frac{m(y,\lambda)}{n(y)}\big)\,{\rm deg}(y)

in view of (

) and the
Riemann-Roch theorem again.

We also obtain isomorphisms of

-modules

H^{0}\big(G\cdot x,\omega_{X}(G\cdot\Delta_{X})\big)\simeq{\mathcal{O}}(G\cdot x)

by using (

) and the fact

is contained
in the fibre of

at

.
Moreover,

\dim{\mathcal{O}}(G\cdot x)_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}{\rm deg}(y)&\text{if $m(y,\lambda)=0$,}\\
0&\text{else},\end{cases}

as follows from Proposition

h^{0}\big(\omega_{X}(G\cdot\Delta_{X})\big)_{0}=p_{a}(Y)-1+{\rm deg}(\Delta_{Y})

For

h^{0}(\omega_{X})_{\lambda}=p_{a}(Y)-1+\sum\big(1-\frac{m(y,\lambda)}{n(y)}\big)\,{\rm deg}(y),

where the sum runs over the points

∎

The above proposition expresses the

Of course, it gives back the Hurwitz

9
Linearly reductive group schemes

Throughout this section, we denote by

G
a finite linearly reductive group
scheme, and by
X
a
G
-normal variety
on which

acts faithfully with quotient

\pi:X\to Y
. Our aim is to determine the
relative dualizing sheaf
\omega_{\pi}

as a
G
-linearized sheaf. Using
Lemmas
7.2
and
7.3
,
we may assume that

is regular
and
\pi
is flat l. c. i. Then

\omega_{\pi}=\omega_{X/Y}

is invertible and has a canonical

-invariant
section

, which trivializes it over
the free locus and whose formation commutes
with flat base change on

.

Theorem 9.1
.

With the above assumptions, we have

{\rm div}(s_{X/Y})=\pi^{*}(\Delta_{Y})-G\cdot\Delta_{X}
.

Proof.

We start with a reduction to the case where

G^{0}
is diagonalizable and
\pi_{0}(G)

is constant
.
As in Remark
4.4
, we may choose
a finite Galois extension
K/k
such that

G^{0}_{K}
is diagonalizable and
\pi_{0}(G)_{K}

is constant. Then the
G_{K}
-variety
X_{K}

satisfies our assumptions; moreover,

\Delta_{Y_{K}}=(\Delta_{Y})_{K}

as the base change to

the free locus and reducedness, and

(G\cdot\Delta_{X})_{K}=G_{K}\cdot(\Delta_{X})_{K}

as the formation of the schematic image commutes
with flat base change. By Galois descent,
it thus suffices to prove the theorem for
the
G_{K}
-variety

.

If
G=G^{0}
then the desired assertion
follows from Remark
7.5
.
We now prove this assertion when

, i.e.,
G
is constant
.
Since
G
is linearly reductive, its order is
prime to

; also, recall that

is normal.

We will need a folklore result
for which we could not find any reference.
Consider a point

with centralizer

C_{G}(x)
. Then the quotient morphism

\pi:X\to Y
factors as

X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z=X/C_{G}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y=X/G,\quad x\longmapsto z\longmapsto y.

In turn,
\psi
factors as

Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}W=X/N_{G}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y,\quad z\longmapsto w\longmapsto y,

where the normalizer
N_{G}(x)
is the largest
subgroup of
G
that stabilizes
x
,
and
\tau
is the quotient by
N_{G}(x)/C_{G}(x)
.

Lemma 9.2
.

With the above notation, we have:

(i)

\kappa(z)=\kappa(x)
.

(ii)

\tau
is a torsor under

N_{G}(x)/C_{G}(x)
in a neighborhood of
z
.

(iii)

\eta
induces an isomorphism
of completed local rings

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{Y,y}\simeq\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{W,w}
.

(iv)

\psi
is étale at
z
and
s_{\psi}

is a trivializing section of
\omega_{\psi}

at that point.

(v)

If
x
is the generic point of a prime divisor,
then
C_{G}(x)
is cyclic.

Proof.

(i) This follows from the isomorphism
of local rings

{\mathcal{O}}_{Z,z}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{C_{G}(x)}

in view of the linear reductivity of

C_{G}(x)
.

(ii) Likewise, the linear reductivity of
N_{G}(x)

and the isomorphism

{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{N_{G}(x)}

imply that

\kappa(w)=\kappa(x)^{N_{G}(x)}=\kappa(x)^{N_{G}(x)/C_{G}(x)}
.
As
N_{G}(x)/C_{G}(x)
acts faithfully on

,
the desired assertion follows from
Galois theory.

(iii) This is a special case of

[
SGA1
, Exp. V, Prop. 2.2]
)
and can be proved directly as follows:

\pi
induces an isomorphism

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{Y,y}\simeq\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{X,G\cdot x}^{G}
. Moreover,

G\cdot x
is the disjoint union of the

g\cdot x
, where
g\in G/N_{G}(x)
.
Thus, we have a
G
-equivariant isomorphism
of semi-local rings

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{X,G\cdot x}\simeq\prod_{g\in G/N_{G}(x)}\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{X,g\cdot x}
.
Taking
G
-invariants, we obtain

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{Y,y}\simeq\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{X,x}^{N_{G}(x)}
.
Moreover,

{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}^{N_{G}(x)}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{W,w}
;
this yields the assertion.

(iv)The morphism
\tau
is étale at
z

by (ii), and hence
\omega_{\tau}

is invertible at that point; moreover,

is a trivializing section
in view of Lemma
7.1
.
Also,
\eta
is étale at

by (iii), so that

\omega_{\eta}
is invertible at that point.
Moreover,
s_{\eta}
is a trivializing section,
since its formation commutes with flat base
change. This implies the assertions in view
of the isomorphism

\omega_{\psi}\simeq\omega_{\tau}\otimes\tau^{*}(\omega_{\eta})

identifying

Remark 9.4
.

Still assuming
k
algebraically closed,
we discuss the structure of finite linearly reductive
group schemes
G
acting faithfully on a curve.
Recall that we have

G\simeq\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}\rtimes H
,
where
H
is constant of order prime to
p
.
As the automorphism group of

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}
is isomorphic to

({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\times{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{r-1}{\mathbb{Z}}
,
it follows that the conjugation action of
G
on

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}
is given by a character

\chi:G\longrightarrow({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times},

which may be identified with the weight of the
G
-action
on its Lie algebra. In particular, we have an exact
sequence of finite group schemes

1\longrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}\times F\longrightarrow G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\chi}}{{\longrightarrow}}({\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}})^{\times},

(9.2)

where
F
(the centralizer of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}
in
H
)
is constant of order prime to
p
.

Conversely, given such an exact sequence,
does
G
act faithfully on some curve (which may then
be taken projective and
G
-normal)?
We only know a partial answer to this question,
in the case where
G=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}\times F
with
F

as above. Then by the main result of
[
MV83
]
,
there exists a smooth projective curve
X
such that

F={\rm Aut}(X)
. We first construct a faithful

rational
action of
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}
on
X

that commutes with the
F
-action.

Let
K
be the function field of
X
,
and
L=K^{F}
. Choose

t\in L\setminus L^{p}
; then

t\in K\setminus K^{p}
and hence
we have a decomposition

K=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{p-1}K^{p}\,t^{m},

which is a
{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of the

K^{p}
-algebra
K
by

F
-stable subspaces. In view of

[
Br24
, Cor. 3.4]
, this yields
a faithful rational action of

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p}\times F
on
X
, which extends
the given
F
-action. More generally,
we obtain a decomposition

K=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{p^{r}-1}K^{p^{r}}\,t^{m}

by induction on
r
, and hence
the
F
-action on
X
extends
to a faithful rational action of

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}\times F
. Moreover,
these actions are compatible with
the inclusions

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}\subset\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r+1}}
.

By loc. cit., Cor. 4.4, these rational
actions can be “regularized”, i.e.,
for any
r
, there exists
a
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}\times F
-normal projective
curve
X_{r}
which is equivariantly
birationally isomorphic to
X
; then
we still have
F={\rm Aut}(X_{r})
.
If
g(X)\geq 2
then
X_{r}\neq X
,
as the automorphism group scheme
{\rm Aut}_{X}

is étale. Under this assumption,
there exists no projective curve
Y

that is
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{p^{r}}
-normal for infinitely
many values of
r
, and birational to

X
: otherwise, the connected
automorphism group scheme
{\rm Aut}^{0}_{Y}

is not finite, and hence
Y
must have
infinitely many automorphisms,
a contradiction. In particular,
we obtain infinitely many pairwise
nonisomorphic projective models
X_{r}
.

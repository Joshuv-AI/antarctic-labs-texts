Existence, Smoothness and Uniqueness (in smooth space) of the Navier-Stokes equation by using a new Boundary Integral representation.
Edmund Chadwick, e.a.chadwick@salford.ac.uk

Abstract
Consider an exterior space-time domain where the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation and continuity equation hold with no bodies or force fields present, and smooth velocity at initial time. This is equivalent to the velocity being impulsively instantaneously started into motion and further assume that this force impulse is bounded. A smooth solution with a Stokeslet far-field decay for all subsequent time is sought and found, demonstrating existence and smoothness. This is given by a space-time boundary integral velocity representation by a single layer potential linear distribution of Navier-Stokes fundamental solutions called NSlets. This is obtained by extending the theory of hydrodynamic potentials to also include a non-linear potential that subsequently drops out of the formulation. Zero initial velocity gives the null solution and so there can be only one smooth solution demonstrating uniqueness in smooth space, but this is not to say that there aren’t other possible solutions in the wider class of non-smooth spaces.



1
Introduction

A general solution to the Navier-Stokes equation is one of the
millennium problem challenges
[
19
]
.
This challenge seeks to prove (or otherwise) that there exists a
smooth solution satisfying the Navier-Stokes equation for the particular
problem of a smooth divergence free velocity at initial time and an
incompressible fluid filling all of space.
So, non-smooth solutions such as jumps and bifurcations are not
considered because a solution is sought that is restricted to

C^{\infty}
only.
Existence and smoothness have been proven for the two-dimensional case,
and is demonstrated by Ladyzhenskaya
[
24
]
for the weak
solution by using generalised functions.
However, this approach has not successfully been extended to the
three-dimensional unsteady problem.
This is because of the question of blow-up of the solution due to a
finite-time singularity.
At small scale the inertial (transport) term dominates and the
Navier-Stokes equation becomes supercritical and is demonstrated to
blow up when considering averaged quantities
[
28
]
.
Ramm
[
27
]
gives a velocity representation by a
domain distribution of unsteady Stokeslets,
with the non-linear velocity term within the domain integral itself.
By using Fourier Transforms,
he then is able to give bounds for the velocity and velocity
derivative as well as demonstrate the solution is unique.
However, it is unclear how this approach deals with blow-up and there
has been a challenge to the accuracy of the analysis in the review

[
25
]
that suggests one of the integrals used in
the analysis is non-integrable (unbounded).
For the three-dimensional steady Stokes problem,
Ladyzhenskaya also gives a strong solution by a different approach,
using the theory of hydrodynamic potentials which uses Green’s
boundary integral method.
Oseen
[
26
]
similarly gives solutions for the three
dimensional steady Oseen flow,
and also unsteady Stokes flow using Oseenlet and Stokeslet unsteady
force impulse fundamental solutions.
Chadwick
[
8
]
extends this approach giving a boundary
integral representation for Navier-Stokes flow by introducing its
fundamental solutions called NSlets.
In this paper
[
8
]
the near field of the NSlet is
assumed to be the Stokeslet.
However, this is not the case and so the same difficulty as in

[
27
]
arises as the expected behaviour at small scale
given in
[
28
]
is not replicated,
particularly for turbulent flows associated with high Reynolds number.

Instead, in the present paper the NSlets are shown to reduce to
Eulerlets near to their origin.
This is a crucial difference that also indicates the difficulties in
getting existence and smoothness proofs for the Navier-Stokes
equation,
since the Eulerlets contain unstable domain singularities

[
10
]
consequently giving the possibility of blow up

[
28
]
as well as turbulence.
So by using standard approaches, it is difficult to see how this
singular small scale nature can be treated mathematically to produce
bounds for the solution,
explaining why the Navier-Stokes equation is so difficult to solve.

However, in the present paper it is found that the integral flux of
the Eulerlet in the boundary integral formulation is finite and can be
evaluated removing this difficulty,
and so for smooth initial conditions with no bodies or other force
fields, the boundary integral representation in Chadwick

[
8
]
is able to be used in the present paper despite
the singular and unstable nature of the Eulerlet.
This follows the methodology of Oseen
[
26
]
that uses
the Green’s integral representation theory of hydrodynamic potentials
for the linear problems of Oseen and Stokes flow.
In Oseen’s work, a potential by an integral along the radius variable
(from its origin) is defined.
Similarly, this approach is followed here for the non-linear
Navier-Stokes equation which gives an additional non-linear
potential,
but it is shown that this non-linear potential drops out across the
integral boundary resulting in a velocity representation by a linear
distribution of Navier-Stokes fundamental solutions (NSlets).
Once in this linear form, existence, smoothness and uniqueness are
obtained by following an approach similar to Ladyzhenskaya’s
strong solution for the three-dimensional steady Stokes problem
by extending and developing it for a space-time domain.
The space-time boundary can be moved near to the fluid point,
where the fundamental solution approximates to the Eulerlet,
and so the representation reduces to a linear distribution of
Eulerlets with strength given by the force impulse distribution.
Since the force impulse generating the flow is assumed bounded and the
integral flux of the Eulerlet is bounded even though the Eulerlet
itself is singular at its spatial origin,
it is then shown that the velocity is bounded as well as
its derivatives, so the solution exists and is smooth.
Choosing the space-time boundary to be the space domain at initial time,
the velocity is represented by a boundary integral distribution of
NSlets such that the force impulse strength is given by the initial
velocity.
So, considering zero initial velocity leads to a zero velocity flow
field at all subsequent time, demonstrating uniqueness in
C^{\infty}

only.

Problem statement

This paper seeks to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1

Consider the motion of a fluid in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
described by the
incompressible Navier-Stokes equation and continuity equation,
such that body forces are taken to be zero,
for the unknown velocity field
\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

with initial condition given for the velocity as a
C^{\infty}

divergence free vector field that decays like a Stokeslet in the

Then, the pressure and velocity exist, are smooth and unique at all
subsequent time with bounded energy.

This theorem requires the following two lemmas.

enclosed by a hyper-surface

given by:

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}=X^{2}
,

;

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}\leq X^{2}

;

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}\leq X^{2}
,

;
and another spherinder surface

interval given by:

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}\leq X^{2}
,

where

is co-ordinate position and

and

, see figure
1
.

Figure 1
:
The space-time domain
\Sigma^{+}

The boundary conditions are:
The spherinder hyper-surface

is the volume at initial time over
which an instantaneous smooth force impulse distribution giving a total
finite force impulse is applied;
and over the far-field spherinder hyper-surface
\partial\Sigma_{X}

the flow field behaves like a Stokeslet.

The spherinder hyper-surfaces have defined normals (except in the limit
approaching the edges), are smooth surfaces piece-wise connected,
and so amenable to Green’s boundary integral analysis.
Green’s analysis is performed in the space-time domain enclosed by the
initial time and far-field space-time boundary integrals,

A proof by ansatz is considered.
So, in this domain it is assumed that the velocity and pressure (and
therefore the NSlet velocity and pressure also) are
C^{\infty}

smooth.
This enables a solution for the velocity to be found by a Green’s

From this solution it is then shown that a velocity and pressure which
are
C^{\infty}
smooth exist, completing the proof by ansatz.
(Of course, this proof by ansatz says nothing about whether or not
non-smooth solutions exist as well and so this remains unanswered.)
It is then shown that there can be only one solution, uniqueness,
in the
C^{\infty}
smooth class.
In particular, the Green’s integral representation for the velocity

u_{k}({\bf x},t)
is given by a boundary integral distribution of NSlet
fundamental solutions with strength given by (minus) the initial

V
at the initial
time
t=0
so

u_{k}({\bf x},t)=-\int_{V}u_{i}({\bf x^{\prime}},0)u_{ki}({\bf x}-{\bf x^{\prime}},t)dV^{\prime},

(1)

where
u_{ki}
is the
i
th velocity component of the
k
th NSlet,

dV^{\prime}
is an element of the volume
V
,
and the force impulse
{\bf J}
is given by

J_{i}=-\int_{V}u_{i}({\bf x^{\prime}},0)dV^{\prime},

(2)

where
-u_{i}({\bf x},0)
is also the force impulse distribution.
(It is noted that this automatically reduces to boundary integral

4
NSlet asymptotic approximations

Existence, smoothness and uniqueness is established from the velocity
representation by NSlets.
This representation is obtained from the theory of hydrodynamic
potentials, see for example
[
24
]
.
This requires the evaluation of boundary integrals near to and
far from a fluid point, and so the near-field and far-field asymptotic
approximations of the NSlet.
So in this section, consider approximations to the NSlet near to and
far from its origin.
It shall be shown that near to its origin, the NSlet approximates to
the Eulerlet, and far from its origin, the NSlet approximates to the
Stokeslet.

4.1
Near to the origin: the Eulerlet

First, consider the NSlet close to its origin
x_{I}=x^{\prime}_{I}
.
We shall find that this approximates to the Eulerlet,
and this is demonstrated next.
From the NSlet equation (
8
),
it is seen that if the time dependent term
u_{ki,0}
doesn’t
contribute to the integral flux represented by the term

\delta\delta_{ki}

then the velocity would be multiplied by the factor
\delta(x_{0})

However, such a velocity has no solution for
x_{0}^{\prime}\neq x_{0}
and so is
not a meaningful solution to (

This means that the time-dependent term in the NSlet must contribute
to the integral flux.
(This does not mean that steady state problems cannot be tackled,

4.2
Far from the origin: the Stokeslet

Far from the origin, the velocity decays to zero, and so the quadratic

and not in three-dimensional space.
This fundamental solution for the Stokes equation appears to have been

The Stokeslet solution is given in the appendix from Fourier
Transforms following the approach of Chan and Chwang

5.1
The non-linear potential

The quadratic term

is then given in terms of the divergence of a
vector field

(28)

q_{kj}=W_{k}\hat{R}_{j}+q^{*}_{kj}

\hat{R}_{j}=\frac{x_{j}^{\prime}-x_{j}}{R}

(It is noted that defining the quadratic term is this way doesn’t
imply any loss of generality and can be applied to any differential
equation problem,
but it just happens to work out in this particular case because the
quadratic term disappears across boundaries and because the evaluation
of the integrals involving the quadratic term do not contribute
asymptotically close to and far from the point,
see sections
6

7

This may not necessarily work out for other non-linear partial
differential equations.)
Therefore the tensor

directed along
the radial spoke
\hat{R}_{j}

4

\displaystyle[W_{k}\hat{R}_{j}]_{;j}

\displaystyle u_{j}u_{i;j}u_{ki}+u_{i}u_{{\bf k}j}(u_{{\bf k}i;j}-u_{{\bf k}j;i})

\displaystyle g_{k}(x_{I},x^{\prime}_{I}).

This is the divergence of a four dimensional vector in space-time,
and so expressing in the co-ordinate system
(x_{0},R,\theta,\alpha)

where
(R,\theta,\alpha)
are spherical co-ordinates in space,
gives

(31)

It is noted that in the linear (Stokes and Oseen) approach given by
Oseen
[
26

of physical variables of the fundamental solution. Oseen then
integrates along the radial variable of integration to give an
integral expression for this potential.
Here, we use a similar approach but for this non-linear potential

W_{k}

So, integrating gives

\Sigma

the variable of integration is

So, by putting the results from (

(

we can apply the divergence theorem to get a boundary integral
formulation

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left[f_{i}u_{ki}-f_{ki}u_{i}\right]d\Sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left[u_{i}f_{kiJ}-u_{ki}\tau_{ij}+q_{kj}\right]_{;J}d\Sigma^{\prime}

(33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Sigma_{Coll}}\left[u_{i}f_{kiJ}-u_{ki}\tau_{ij}+q_{kj}\right]n_{J}^{\Sigma}d\sigma^{\prime},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

where

\Sigma
,
and
\partial\Sigma_{Coll}
is the collection of boundaries enclosing both
the exterior and interior domains that constitute
\Sigma

The first term in the integrand yields an integral associated with the
double layer potential,
the second term in the integrand with the single layer potential,
see for example the theory of hydrodynamic potentials given in
Ladyzhenskaya
[
24
]
.
The third term in the integrand is given by the non-linear potential
that depends on integration of quantities within the whole domain.
The ansatz proof assumes that the physical variables are all smooth,
and so the divergence theorem applied to the non-linear term to get
the boundary representation is possible since the term is assumed to
be smooth.
There may be other solutions that are not
C^{\infty}
smooth due to
the non-elliptic nature of the Navier-Stokes equation,
but these other possible solutions are not considered here and instead
it is only investigated whether a smooth solution exists.
This representation also has similarities with the dual-reciprocity
method used for boundary integral representations for non-linear
partial differential equations,
except that the dual-reciprocity method approximates the non-linear
domain term by a series expansion of known functions,
such as radial basis functions,
whose divergence is known and so can then be represented over the
boundary.

The whole concept of the boundary integral framework requires
linearity,
and the non-linear third term in (
33

preclude this.
However, in the subsequent analysis we shall see that a representation
is found such that the non-linear term (as well as the double-layer
potential) drops out thus justifying its use.

11
Conclusion

A Green’s integral representation for the velocity is given over a
four-dimensional space-time hyper-surface with a Green’s function
fundamental solution kernel, called an NSlet,
having strength given by a force impulse distribution over the
hyper-surface.
The force impulse for the problem is assumed to be bounded.
Choosing the integral representation for the velocity to be close to
the fluid point, then the NSlet approximates to the Eulerlet which is
a known smooth function (away from its spatial origin).
From this, it is shown that if the velocity is smooth at an initial
time then it is smooth for all subsequent time as well.
If the velocity exists and is smooth then from the Helmholtz theorem
so is the pressure.
The force impulse distribution is given by the initial velocity,
and if this is taken to be zero then the velocity at all later time is
zero,
demonstrating uniqueness for the smooth class of solutions

C^{\infty}
.

Appendix A
Stokeslet

We follow the approach of Chan and Chwang
[
15
]
to give
the unsteady Stokeslet.
The Stokeslet equation is

u^{S}_{ki,0}+p^{S}_{k,i}-\nu u^{S}_{ki,jj}=-\delta\delta_{ki}\;,\;\;u^{S}_{ki,i}=0.

(66)

Taking the divergence of the first equation in (
66
)
gives
p^{S}_{k,ii}=-\delta_{,k}
.
Letting
p^{S}_{k}=\delta(x_{0})p^{*}_{,k}
gives
p^{*}_{,ii}=-\delta(x_{i})

and so
p^{*}=\frac{1}{4\pi R}
, which gives

p^{S}_{k}=\frac{\delta(x_{0})}{4\pi}\left[\frac{1}{R}\right]_{,k}.

(67)

Substituting this back into the first equation in (
66
)
gives

u^{S}_{ki,0}-\nu u^{S}_{ki,jj}=-\delta\delta_{ki}-\left(\frac{1}{4\pi R}\right)_{,ki}\delta(x_{0}).

(68)

We can split the velocity into two parts, and determine each
separately,
such that

u^{S}_{ki}=u^{A}\delta_{ki}+u^{B}_{,ki}

(69)

where

u^{A}_{,0}-\nu u^{A}_{,jj}=-\delta

(70)

u^{B}_{,0}-\nu u^{B}_{,jj}=-\frac{1}{4\pi R}\delta(x_{0}).

(71)

A.1
Evaluation of

Apply Fourier Transforms to (

\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{4}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}\left(u^{A}_{,0}-\nu u^{A}_{,jj}\right)e^{-\alpha_{J}x_{J}}d\Sigma=-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{4},

(72)

where
\alpha_{I}
are the Fourier Transform variables,
and
\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}

So

\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{4}\left(i\alpha_{0}+\nu\alpha_{R}^{2}\right)\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}u^{A}e^{-\alpha_{J}x_{J}}d\Sigma=-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{4}.

(73)

Let
{\bar{\bar{u}}}^{A}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{4}\int_{\Sigma_{\infty}}u^{A}e^{-\alpha_{J}x_{J}}d\Sigma
,
and
let
{\bar{u}}^{A}=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{3}\int_{V_{\infty}}u^{A}e^{-\alpha_{j}x_{j}}dV
,
where
dV

V_{\infty}
which is an
integration across all of space.
This gives

\displaystyle{\bar{\bar{u}}}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{1}{i}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\alpha-i\nu\alpha_{R}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle{\bar{u}}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{3}H(x_{0})e^{\nu\alpha_{R}^{2}x_{0}}

using the result for the inverse Fourier Transform

F^{-1}\{1/(\alpha-ia)\}=H(x_{0})\sqrt{2\pi}ie^{-\alpha x}
given in standard
transform tables,
for the real variable
x
, transform variable
\alpha

Taking the inverse transforms in the spatial directions by using the

F^{-1}\{e^{-a\alpha^{2}}\}=\left(e^{-x^{2}/(4a)}\right)/\sqrt{2a}

for the Gaussian,
we then get

\displaystyle u^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{3}H(x_{0})e^{-R^{2}/(4\nu x_{0})}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2x_{0}}}\right)^{3}

(75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi\nu}H(x_{0})\left(\frac{{\rm erf}\eta}{R}\right)_{,0}

\displaystyle({\rm erf}\eta)_{,0}

\displaystyle-\frac{R}{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_{0}}^{3}}\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\eta^{2}}.

Evaluation of

We then have

Appendix B
Pressure smooth

By applying the Helmholtz decomposition (fundamental theorem of vector
calculus) it is shown that if the velocity is smooth then the pressure
is smooth as well.
The Navier-Stokes equation is rewritten in terms of the head
function
h
such that

u_{i,0}+h_{i}+p_{,i}-\nu u_{i,jj}=0\;,\;\;h_{i}=u_{j}u_{i,j}.

(80)

Applying the Helmholtz decomposition to the velocity and head
functions

\displaystyle u_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{,i}+w_{i}\;,\;\;w_{i}=\epsilon_{ijk}A_{k,j}

\displaystyle h_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{,i}+h_{i}^{w}\;,\;\;h_{i}^{w}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_{k,j}

for curl free scalar potentials
\phi
and
h
,
and divergence free vectors
w_{i}
and
h_{i}^{w}
,
represented by the vector potentials
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
respectively,
and
\epsilon_{ijk}
is the Levi-Civita symbol.
(This symbol is such that
\epsilon_{ijk}=1
for permutations

(i,j,k)=(1,2,3)
,
(2,3,1)
and
(3,1,2)
,

\epsilon_{ijk}=-1
for permutations
(i,j,k)=(1,3,2)
,
(2,1,3)

and
(3,2,1)
, and zero otherwise.)
The wake velocity divergence is
w_{i,i}=\epsilon_{ijk}A_{k,ji}=0
, and so
u_{i,i}=\phi_{,ii}=0
.
So, the curl free component of the Navier-Stokes equation is

\phi_{,i0}+h_{,i}+p_{,i}=0
since
\nu\phi_{,ijj}=0
.
Integrating this then gives the pressure as

p=-\phi_{,0}-h.

(82)

From the Helmholtz theorem
[
23
]
,
the potentials are given by

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S}\frac{u_{i}n_{i}}{\lvert R-R^{\prime}\rvert}ds^{\prime}

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{V}\frac{u_{j;i}u_{i;j}}{\lvert R-R^{\prime}\rvert}dV^{\prime}+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S}\frac{u_{j}u_{i;j}n_{i}}{\lvert R-R^{\prime}\rvert}ds^{\prime},

for some volume
V
enclosed by a surface
S
with outward pointing
normal
n_{i}
.
So, if the velocity
u_{i}
is smooth then the potentials
\phi
and
h

are smooth, and the pressure
p
is smooth.

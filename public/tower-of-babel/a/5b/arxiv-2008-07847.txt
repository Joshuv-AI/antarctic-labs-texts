Abstract
We use the isomorphisms between the R R -matrix and Drinfeld presentations of the quantum affine algebras in types B B , C C and D D produced in our previous work to describe finite-dimensional irreducible representations in the R R -matrix realization. We also review the isomorphisms for the Yangians of these types and use Gauss decomposition to establish an equivalence of the descriptions of the representations in the R R -matrix and Drinfeld presentations of the Yangians.

Introduction

The Yangians and quantum affine algebras associated with simple
Lie algebras comprise two remarkable families of
infinite-dimensional quantum groups, as introduced by V. Drinfeld
[
9
]

and M. Jimbo
[
17
]
. Both families have since found numerous connections with
many areas in mathematics and physics, they
possess rich and versatile representation theory.

Finite-dimensional irreducible representations of the Yangians were classified
by Drinfeld in his paper
[
11
]
by relying on the pioneering
work by V. Tarasov
[
27
,
28
]
. The classification in
[
11
]
uses
a new presentation of the Yangians
and quantum affine algebras which is now often referred to
as the
Drinfeld presentation
and which was used by V. Chari and A. Pressley
to classify finite-dimensional irreducible representations
of quantum affine algebras
[
6
, Chapter 12]
.

A different kind of presentations of these algebras known as
R
-matrix presentations

goes back to the work of the Leningrad school of L. Faddeev; see, e.g.,

[
12
,
23
,
25
,
26
]
.
In accordance to Drinfeld
[
10
]
,
such presentations can be produced from
the universal
R
-matrix associated with a quantum group
and they can be associated with arbitrary
finite-dimensional representations.
This approach was developed further in
a more recent work
[
29
]
.

Explicit isomorphisms between the
R
-matrix and Drinfeld
presentations of the Yangians in type
A
were given in the original work

[
11
]
, while detailed proofs were produced
by J. Brundan and A. Kleshchev
[
5
]
. An analogous isomorphism
for the type
A
quantum affine algebras is due to
J. Ding and I. Frenkel
[
8
]
.

Despite their importance in representation theory and applications,
such isomorphisms had remained unknown
beyond type
A
until recent work
[
15
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
, where they
were produced for the remaining classical types
B
,
C
and
D
.

Our goal in this paper is to give a brief review
of Yangians and quantum affine algebras in types
B
,
C
and
D

and apply the isomorphisms to describe
finite-dimensional irreducible representations of these algebras
in their
R
-matrix realization.

In the case of Yangians, such a description was already given in
[
1
]
,
so that the isomorphisms connect two sides of the representation theory
and explain how additional symmetries of the representation parameters
arise from the Gauss decomposition of the generator matrices.
However, for the
R
-matrix realization of the quantum affine algebras the isomorphisms are essential
to get a parametrization of their finite-dimensional irreducible representations.

A key step in our arguments relies on a consistency property for
the triangular decomposition of the algebra and
the Gauss decomposition of the generator matrices.
The property ensures that the upper triangular Gaussian generators can be expressed
in terms of the ‘simple root generators’ implying that the annihilation properties of the highest vectors
agree in both presentations of the algebras.
Its Yangian version is well-known
in type
A
(see, e.g.,
[
24
, Section 3.1]
), while proofs in the remaining
classical types follow from
[
19
, Lemma 5.15]
.
This property has also been known for the quantum affine algebras of type
A

[
22
]
, so that
the isomorphism of
[
8
]
connects the descriptions of the representations
given in
[
6
, Section 12.2]
and
[
14
]
. Similar to the approach of
[
22
]
,
we establish the consistency property
in Section
3.2.4
in types
B
,
C
and
D
by deriving it from the defining relations.
An alternative way
can rely on explicit formulas for the universal
R
-matrices
which, however, leads to more
involved calculations.

In Appendix
A
we give a modified version of the Yangian isomorphisms
produced in
[
19
]
, which is based on the opposite
Gauss decomposition of the generator matrix. This version
can be applied to make an alternative connection
between the parameters of representations in the two realizations
of the Yangians.

Representations of Yangians of types
\boldsymbol{B}
,
\boldsymbol{C}
and
\boldsymbol{D}

2.1
Classification results

We will denote by
\mathfrak{g}
one of the simple Lie algebras of type
B_{n}
,
n
or
D_{n}
. That is,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}_{N}
is either the orthogonal Lie algebra with
N=2n+1
and
N=2n
,
or
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{N}
is the symplectic Lie algebra with
N=2n
.
Choose simple roots in the form

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}=\epsilon_{i}-\epsilon_{i+1}\qquad\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,n-1,

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\begin{cases}\epsilon_{n}&\text{if}\ \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}_{2n+1},\\
2\hskip 1.0pt\epsilon_{n}&\text{if}\ \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\\
\epsilon_{n-1}+\epsilon_{n}&\text{if}\ \mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}_{2n},\end{cases}

where

\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{n}
is an orthonormal basis of a Euclidian space with the inner product

(\cdot\,,\cdot)
. The Cartan matrix
[A_{ij}]
for
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\displaystyle A_{ij}=\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}.

(2.2)

We will use the notation

\displaystyle r_{i}=\tfrac{1}{2}\hskip 1.0pt(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}),\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

(2.3)

As defined in
[
11
]
,
the
Drinfeld Yangian

{\rm Y}^{D}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by
elements
\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}
,
\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{+}
and

\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{-}
with
i=1,\dots,n
and
r=0,1,\dots

subject to the defining relations

\displaystyle[\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr},\kappa_{j\hskip 1.0pts}]=0,

\displaystyle\big[\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{+},\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{-}\big]=\delta_{ij}\,\kappa_{i\,r+s},

\displaystyle\big[\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0pt0},\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm}\big]=\pm\,(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})\,\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm},

\displaystyle\big[\kappa_{i\,r+1},\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm}\big]-\big[\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr},\xi_{j\,s+1}^{\pm}\big]=\pm\,\frac{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{2}\big(\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\,\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm}+\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm}\,\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\big),

\displaystyle\big[\xi_{i\,r+1}^{\pm},\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm}\big]-\big[\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{\pm},\xi_{j\,s+1}^{\pm}\big]=\pm\,\frac{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{2}\big(\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{\pm}\,\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm}+\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm}\,\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{\pm}\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\big[\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr_{p(1)}}^{\pm},\big[\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr_{p(2)}}^{\pm},\dots\big[\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr_{p(m)}}^{\pm},\xi_{j\hskip 1.0pts}^{\pm}\big]\dots\big]\big]=0,

where the last relation holds for all
i\neq j
, and we denoted
m=1-a_{ij}
.

By the results of
[
11
]
(they apply to any simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
),
every finite-dimensional irreducible representation
of the algebra
{\rm Y}^{D}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by a nonzero vector
\zeta
(called the
highest vector
)
which is annihilated by all
\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{+}
and is a simultaneous eigenvector for all

\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}
so that

\displaystyle\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\zeta=d_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta,\qquad d_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt.

Furthermore, there exist unique monic polynomials
P_{1}(u),\dots,P_{n}(u)
in
u

such that

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}d_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\hskip 1.0ptu^{-r-1}=\frac{P_{i}(u+r_{i})}{P_{i}(u)},\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

Equivalently, every finite-dimensional irreducible representation
of the algebra
{\rm Y}^{D}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by a nonzero vector
\zeta^{\prime}
(the
highest vector

with respect to the opposite triangular decomposition)
which is annihilated by all
\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{-}
and is a simultaneous eigenvector for all

\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}
so that

\displaystyle\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\zeta^{\prime}=d^{\,\prime}_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{\prime},\qquad d^{\,\prime}_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt.

Furthermore, there exist unique monic polynomials
Q_{1}(u),\dots,Q_{n}(u)
in
u

such that

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}d^{\,\prime}_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\hskip 1.0ptu^{-r-1}=\frac{Q_{i}(u)}{Q_{i}(u+r_{i})},\qquad i=1,\dots,n.

(2.4)

All possible
n
-tuples of monic polynomials
(P_{1}(u),\dots,P_{n}(u))

and
(Q_{1}(u),\dots,Q_{n}(u))

arise in this way.
The equivalence of the two parametrizations is seen by using the automorphism of
the algebra
{\rm Y}^{D}(\mathfrak{g})
defined by

\displaystyle\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{+}\mapsto(-1)^{r+1}\hskip 1.0pt\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{-},\qquad\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{-}\mapsto(-1)^{r+1}\hskip 1.0pt\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{+},\qquad\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\mapsto(-1)^{r+1}\hskip 1.0pt\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}.

2.2
Gaussian generators and isomorphisms

Introduce the following elements of the endomorphism algebra
{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}
:

\displaystyle P=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}e_{ij}\otimes e_{ji}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}\hskip 1.0pt\varepsilon_{j}\,e_{ij}\otimes e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}},

where
e_{ij}\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}
are the matrix units, and we use the notation

i^{\prime}=N+1-i
and
set
\varepsilon_{i}\equiv 1
in the orthogonal case, and

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}=\begin{cases}\phantom{-}1&\text{for}\ i=1,\dots,n,\\
-1&\text{for}\ i=n+1,\dots,2n,\end{cases}

in the symplectic case. Set

\displaystyle\kappa=\begin{cases}N/2-1&\text{in the orthogonal case,}\\
N/2+1&\text{in the symplectic case}.\end{cases}

Following
[
30
]
, consider
the
R
-
matrix

R(u)

\displaystyle R(u)=1-\frac{P}{u}+\frac{Q}{u-\kappa}.

The
extended Yangian

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{g})
is defined as a unital associative algebra with generators

t_{ij}^{(r)}
, where
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant N
and
r=1,2,\dots
, satisfying certain quadratic relations.
Introduce the formal series

\displaystyle t_{ij}(u)=\delta_{ij}+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}t_{ij}^{(r)}\,u^{-r}\in{\rm X}(\mathfrak{g})\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big]

and set

\displaystyle T(u)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}e_{ij}\otimes t_{ij}(u)\in{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\otimes{\rm X}(\mathfrak{g})\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big].

Consider the tensor product algebra
{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\otimes{\rm{End}\,}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt^{N}\otimes{\rm X}(\mathfrak{g})

and introduce the series with coefficients in this algebra by

\displaystyle T_{1}(u)=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{N}e_{ij}\otimes 1\otimes t_{ij}(u)\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{2}(u)=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{N}1\otimes e_{ij}\otimes t_{ij}(u).

The defining relations for the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{g})
are then written in the form

\displaystyle R(u-v)\,T_{1}(u)\,T_{2}(v)=T_{2}(v)\,T_{1}(u)\,R(u-v).

The
Yangian

{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
is defined as the subalgebra of

{\rm X}(\mathfrak{g})
which
consists of the elements stable under
the automorphisms

\displaystyle\mu_{f}\colon\ T(u)\mapsto f(u)\,T(u),

for all series
f(u)=1+f_{1}u^{-1}+f_{2}u^{-2}+\cdots
with
f_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt
.
Equivalently,
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to the quotient of the algebra
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{g})
by the relation

\displaystyle T^{\hskip 1.0pt{\rm t}}(u+\kappa)\,T(u)=1,

(2.5)

where
{\rm t}
denotes the matrix
transposition with
e_{ij}^{{\rm t}}=\varepsilon_{i}\,\varepsilon_{j}\,e_{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}}
.

2.2.1
Isomorphisms

Apply the Gauss decomposition to the matrix
T(u)
,

\displaystyle T(u)=F(u)\,H(u)\,E(u),

(2.6)

where
F(u)
,
H(u)
and
E(u)
are uniquely determined matrices of the form

\displaystyle F(u)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\dots&0\,\\
f_{21}(u)&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
f_{N1}(u)&f_{N2}(u)&\dots&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad E(u)=\begin{bmatrix}\,1&e_{12}(u)&\dots&e_{1N}(u)\,\\
\,0&1&\dots&e_{2N}(u)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&1\end{bmatrix},

and
H(u)={\rm diag}\,[h_{1}(u),\dots,h_{N}(u)]
. Define the series
with coefficients in
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
by

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}(u)=h_{i}(u-(i-1)/2)^{-1}\,h_{i+1}(u-(i-1)/2)

for
i=1,\dots,n-1
, and

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}(u)=\begin{cases}h_{n}(u-(n-1)/2)^{-1}\,h_{n+1}(u-(n-1)/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{o}_{2n+1},\\
\hskip 1.0pth_{n}(u-n/2)^{-1}\,h_{n+1}(u-n/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\\
h_{n-1}(u-(n-2)/2)^{-1}\,h_{n+1}(u-(n-2)/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{o}_{2n}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, set

\displaystyle\xi_{i}^{+}(u)=f_{i+1\,i}(u-(i-1)/2),\qquad\xi_{i}^{-}(u)=e_{i\,i+1}(u-(i-1)/2)

for
i=1,\dots,n-1
,

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{+}(u)=\begin{cases}f_{n+1\,n}(u-(n-1)/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{o}_{2n+1},\\
f_{n+1\,n}(u-n/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\\
f_{n+1\,n-1}(u-(n-2)/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{o}_{2n}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\xi_{n}^{-}(u)=\begin{cases}e_{n\,n+1}(u-(n-1)/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{o}_{2n+1},\\
\frac{1}{2}e_{n\,n+1}(u-n/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\\
e_{n-1\,n+1}(u-(n-2)/2)&\text{for}\ \mathfrak{o}_{2n}.\end{cases}

Introduce elements of
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
by the respective expansions into power series in
u^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}(u)=1+\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}\,u^{-r-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\xi_{i}^{\pm}(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{\pm}\,u^{-r-1}

for
i=1,\dots,n
. According to
[
19
, Main Theorem]
,
the mapping which sends the generators
\kappa_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}
and
\xi_{i\hskip 1.0ptr}^{\pm}
of
{\rm Y}^{D}(\mathfrak{g})
to the elements
of
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
with the same names defines an isomorphism
{\rm Y}^{D}(\mathfrak{g})\cong{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
.

2.3
Highest weight representations

Definition 2.2
.

A representation
V
of the algebra
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
(or
{\rm X}(\mathfrak{g})
)
is called a
highest weight representation
if there
exists a nonzero vector
\zeta\in V
such that
V
is generated by
\zeta

and the following relations hold:

\displaystyle t_{ij}(u)\,\zeta=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad 1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle t_{ii}(u)\,\zeta=\lambda_{i}(u)\,\zeta\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,N,

for some formal series
\lambda_{i}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\hskip 1.0pt\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big]
.
The vector
\zeta
is called the
highest vector
of the representation
V
.

The following classification theorem for finite-dimensional irreducible representations
of the Yangians in types
B
,
C
and
D
was proved in
[
1
]

in terms of their
R
-matrix presentation. We will use
the isomorphisms of
[
19
]
which we recalled in Section
2.2.1
,
to make an explicit connection between this theorem and the results of
[
11
]
.
Note that such a connection
was already established in
[
15
]
, where isomorphisms between
three presentations of the orthogonal and symplectic Yangians were constructed.
However, those results did not use the Gaussian presentation
which we will rely on in our arguments.

Theorem 2.3
.

1.

Any finite-dimensional irreducible representation of the algebra
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})

is a highest weight representation. Its parameters satisfy the relations

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{i}(u)}{\lambda_{i+1}(u)}=\frac{P_{i}(u+1)}{P_{i}(u)},\qquad i=1,\dots,n-1,

and

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{n}(u)}{\lambda_{n+1}(u)}

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{n}(u+1/2)}{P_{n}(u)}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for type $B_{n}$},

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{n}(u)}{\lambda_{n+1}(u)}

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{n}(u+2)}{P_{n}(u)}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for type $C_{n}$},

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{n-1}(u)}{\lambda_{n+1}(u)}

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{n}(u+1)}{P_{n}(u)}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for type $D_{n}$},

for some monic polynomials
P_{i}(u)
in
u
.

2.

Every
n
-tuple
(P_{1}(u),\dots,P_{n}(u))
of monic polynomials in
u
arises in this way.

3.

The series
\lambda_{i}(u)
satisfy the relations

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}(u+\kappa-i)\hskip 1.0pt\lambda_{i^{\prime}}(u)=\lambda_{i+1}(u+\kappa-i)\hskip 1.0pt\lambda_{(i+1)^{\prime}}(u),

(2.8)

where
i=0,1,\dots,n
for
N=2n+1
, and
i=0,1,\dots,n-1
for
N=2n
, and we set
\lambda_{0}(u)=\lambda_{0^{\prime}}(u):=1
.

Proof.

Using the isomorphism
{\rm Y}^{D}(\mathfrak{g})\cong{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})
and the classification results
recalled in Section
2.1
, we find that
any finite-dimensional irreducible representation
V
of the algebra
{\rm Y}(\mathfrak{g})

in types
B_{n}
and
C_{n}

is generated by a vector
\zeta^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle e_{i,i+1}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{\prime}=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,n,

(2.9)

\displaystyle h_{i}(u)\,\zeta^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}(u)\,\zeta^{\prime}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,n+1,

(2.10)

for some formal series
\lambda_{i}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big]
. For type
D_{n}
,
the same conditions hold, except that relation (
2.9
) with
i=n

should be replaced with
e_{n-1,n+1}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{\prime}=0
. Indeed, for all types,
relation (
2.9
)
is clear from the definition of the highest vector
\zeta^{\prime}
, while
(
2.10
) follows from the condition that
\zeta^{\prime}
is an eigenvector
for all series
\kappa_{i}(u)
with
i=1,\dots,n
and
z(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{\prime}=\zeta^{\prime}
.
Now, Lemma
2.1
and
[
19
, Lemma 5.15]
imply that

\displaystyle e_{ij}(u)\hskip 1.0pt\zeta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad i<j

\displaystyle h_{i}(u)\,\zeta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i}(u)\,\zeta^{\prime}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for}\quad i=1,\dots,N,

for certain formal series
\lambda_{i}(u)\in 1+u^{-1}\mathbb{C}\hskip 1.0pt\big[\big[u^{-1}\big]\big]
satisfying
identities (
2.8
). Finally, note that the values of the parameters (
2.3
)
are found by
r_{i}=1
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
, while
r_{n}=1/2
for type
B_{n}
,
r_{n}=2
for type
C_{n}
, and
r_{n}=1
for type
D_{n}
, so that
conditions in Part 1 of the theorem follow from (

3.2.3
Hopf algebra structure

The quantum affine algebra
{\rm U}^{{\rm ext}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
possesses a Hopf algebra structure defined by the coproduct

\displaystyle\Delta\colon\ l^{\pm}_{ij}(u)\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{N}l^{\pm}_{ik}(u)\otimes l^{\pm}_{kj}(u),

(3.19)

the antipode

\displaystyle S\colon\ L^{\pm}(u)\mapsto L^{\pm}(u)^{-1}

(3.20)

and the counit

\displaystyle\varepsilon\colon\ L^{\pm}(u)\mapsto 1.

(3.21)

Proposition 3.4
.

In the Hopf algebra
{\rm U}^{{\rm ext}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
we have

\displaystyle\Delta\colon\ z^{\pm}(u)\mapsto z^{\pm}(u)\otimes z^{\pm}(u)

and

\displaystyle S\colon\ z^{\pm}(u)\mapsto z^{\pm}(u)^{-1}.

(3.22)

In particular,
{\rm U}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Hopf subalgebra of
{\rm U}^{{\rm ext}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})

Moreover,

\displaystyle S^{2}\colon\ L^{\pm}(u)\mapsto\frac{z^{\pm}(u)}{z^{\pm}(u\hskip 1.0pt\xi)}\hskip 1.0ptL^{\pm}\big(u\hskip 1.0pt\xi^{2}\big).

(3.23)

Proof.

The formulas for the images of the series
z^{\pm}(u)
under the maps
\Delta
and
S
follow
easily from the definition of

and the Hopf algebra axioms.
For the proof of (

) use the relation

\displaystyle L^{\pm}(u)^{-1}=z^{\pm}(u)^{-1}\hskip 1.0ptD{L}^{\pm}(u\hskip 1.0pt\xi)^{{\rm t}}D^{-1}

and apply (

for the power series

we have

\displaystyle\Delta\colon\ \zeta^{\pm}(u)\mapsto\zeta^{\pm}(u)\otimes\zeta^{\pm}(u),

and so

\displaystyle\Delta\big({\rm U}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\big)\subset{\rm U}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\rm U}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad S\big({\rm U}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\big)\subset{\rm U}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}),

thus proving that
{\rm U}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Hopf subalgebra of
{\rm U}^{{\rm ext}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
.
∎

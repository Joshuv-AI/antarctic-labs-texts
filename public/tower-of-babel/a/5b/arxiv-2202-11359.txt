Deepfake Detection for Facial Images with Facemasks
Donggeun Ko, Sangjun Lee, Jinyong Park, Saebyeol Shin, Donghee Hong, Simon S. Woo *

Abstract
. Hyper-realistic face image generation and manipulation have given rise to numerous unethical social issues, e.g., invasion of privacy, threat of security, and malicious political maneuvering, which resulted in the development of recent deepfake detection methods with the rising demands of deepfake forensics. Proposed deepfake detection methods to date have shown remarkable detection performance and robustness. However, none of the suggested deepfake detection methods assessed the performance of deepfakes with the facemask during the pandemic crisis after the outbreak of the Covid-19. In this paper, we thoroughly evaluate the performance of state-of-the-art deepfake detection models on the deepfakes with the facemask. Also, we propose two approaches to enhance the masked deepfakes detection: face-patch and face-crop . The experimental evaluations on both methods are assessed through the baseline deepfake detection models on the various deepfake datasets. Our extensive experiments show that, among the two methods, face-crop performs better than the face-patch , and could be a train method for deepfake detection models to detect fake faces with facemask in real world.



1.
Introduction

With the advancement in hyper-realistic face image generation techniques such as Face2Face
(
Thies et al. 2016
)
and Deepfake
(
Perov
et al. 2021
)
, synthesized hyper-realistic images and videos threaten the trustworthiness of society, as they raise unethical issues widely from breaking down political trust to abusing in targeted revenge pornography. To counterpart such problems, many recent deep learning-based deepfake detection models have shown remarkable detection performance and robustness with extensive experiments over the well-known deepfake datasets such as FaceForensics++
(
Rössler et al. 2019
)
and Celeb-DF v2
(
Li
et al. 2020
)
.

Nevertheless, none of the detection models have assessed the performance of deepfake detection over the masked face images. Ever since the outbreak of coronavirus disease (COVID-19), people have often worn facemasks to reduce the spread of the coronavirus. This unprecedented pandemic crisis increased the demands of face recognition under the circumstances of wearing the mask for identification. However, masked face recognition is a challenging task as the facemask covers certain parts of the face.
Detecting synthesized faces with facemasks will be more difficult as the important landmarks (e.g., mouse and nose) are missing. Therefore, it is crucial to assess the face recognition performance of baseline models on a forged face with the facemask.

This paper presents two approaches of generating new training dataset:
face-patch
and
face-crop
. First,
face-patch
trains the models with face patches that remove the mouth and nose part of the face from deepfakes, while
face-crop
trains the models with cropped deepfakes above the facemask.
In addition, we have generated masked deepfake images and real images on various well-known deepfake datasets for testing purposes. The extensive experiments show that the
face-crop
outperforms the
face-patch
. To the best of our knowledge, we believe that this work is the first attempt to generate and detect deepfake images with facemasks. The contributions of our work can be summarized as follows:

•

We generate facemask on deepfakes and real images on well-known deepfake datasets creating new deepfake facemask dataset.

•

We propose the two training methods,
face-patch
, to detect deepfake images with facemask by implementing black face patches on nose and mouth in the image, and
face-crop
, to detect deepfake images with facemask by focusing on the upper part of the facemask to detect deepfake images with facemasks.

•

We conduct experiments using the new deepfake facemask datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed methods in terms of accuracy and AUC value.

2.
Related Works

In this section, we will cover the most relevant works regarding deepfake and deepfake detection methods to date.

Deepfake.
As various advanced algorithms have been used to generate realistic synthetic face images, the majority of these studies have not been opened to the public until the dawn of an open-source DeepFakes
(
Perov
et al. 2021
)
platform, DeepFaceLab. Inspired by the emergence of DeepFakes algorithm to the public, various methods, i.e., FaceSwap
(
torzdf 2019
)
, NeuralTextures
(
Gatys
et al. 2015
)
, Face2Face
(
Thies et al. 2016
)
, and FSGAN
(
Nirkin
et al. 2019
)
, have been proposed to synthesize hyper-realistic deepfake images that are unrecognizable to human eyes. These methods allowed to generate numerous deepfake datasets
(
Khalid
et al. 2021
;
Li
et al. 2020
;
Rössler et al. 2019
)
for public usage in the research community. Furthermore, Wav2Lips
(
Prajwal et al. 2020
)
has shown a lip-synchronization network, generating lip-syncing arbitrary talking face videos with
arbitrary speech.

Deepfake Detection.
Previous methods of deepfake detection mainly utilized facial recognition systems with deep neural network-based models
(
Tarasiou and
Zafeiriou 2020
;
Sun
et al. 2014
)
such as FaceNet
(
Schroff
et al. 2015
)
and VGGFace2
(
Cao
et al. 2017
)
; however, these models were ineffective in detecting face forgeries. Recently, more complex models have been introduced to detect deefake images accurately. Rössler et al.
(
Rössler et al. 2019
)
proposed Xception
(
Chollet 2017
)
that outperformed other models by attempting to learn facial features using shallow networks to capture mesoscopic
(
Afchar
et al. 2018
)
features. Another field of deepfake detection applies domain adaptation
(
Tariq
et al. 2021
;
Cozzolino et al. 2019
)
in which high-quality (HQ) deepfake videos and C23 level compression of deepfake videos were adopted, respectively. Furthermore, Kim et al.
(
Kim
et al. 2021
)
represent a domain adaption framework that can prevent catastrophic forgetting without accessing the source domain data. Aso, Tolosana et al.
(
Tolosana et al. 2020
)
evaluated the comparative analysis on deepfake detection by omitting specific facial regions such as eyes, nose, mouth, and the remaining part one by one from deepfake images.
However, to the best of our knowledge, none of the works attempted to evaluate the performance on the generated masked deepfake dataset, which corresponds to the current pandemic crisis. This work presents two novel methods to detect masked deepfake.

3.
Methodology

In this section, we describe on the methodology and experimental settings. For all the datasets, we implement an identical preprocessing step from CLRNet
(
Tariq
et al. 2020
)
for the extraction of frames from each deepfake video. Then, the frames are further processed to generate the face-patch and face-crop dataset for training purposes.

For the face-patch training method,
we apply face-patch generation to make patches on the nose and mouth portion of the face from deepfakes as shown in Figure
2
. This allows generating trainable deepfake datasets without useless landmarks (nose and mouth) for masked deepfake detection. Before training the models with the generated face-patch dataset, we trained baseline models with original deepfake datasets as pretrained models. Once the models are pretrained by the procedures above, the pretrained models are further trained on face-patch datasets through transfer learning.

Tolosana et al.
(
Tolosana et al. 2020
)
suggested that the eye region provides the most crucial information among the other facial regions to detect deepfakes. Inspired by this work, the face-crop method makes the detection models focus on the upper part of the face, specifically the face above the mask, for the masked deepfake detection. we train the detection models with the generated cropped face dataset by dropping the lower part of the face from the deepfake dataset.

We will describe more specific implementations of generating datasets for training on these 2 methods and for testing by facial images with facemask below.

3.1.
Generating Datasets of Face with Facemask

Facemask Generation.
To the best of our knowledge, we believe that there is no available deepfake dataset entirely composed of masked faces, so we generated real and fake faces with a face mask for the test dataset which is used for evaluation of our approaches. We utilized MaskTheFace
(
Anwar and
Raychowdhury 2020
)
, which is a dlib
(
King 2009
)
based face landmarks detector, to convert the evaluation set of deepfake datasets to masked face datasets. Note that during training datasets, deepfake images are not wearing facemasks since the unnatural placement of the facemask and the face (e.g. contrast in light reflection) can affect the detection models to focus on this.
Figure
1
shows some examples of faces with facemasks we generated.

Figure 1.
An example of masked faces generated by MaskTheFace. Both real and fake images are masked with different mask designs for testing purpose.

Face Patch Generation.

Face-patch generation covers the face patches in the mouth and nose area of deepfakes. Initially, dlib is used to find the face landmarks from deepfakes. After this step, we insert black-colored patches (i.e. set the pixel values to 0) to the location of the detected landmarks of the nose and mouth. Figure
2
illustrates the steps of the patch generation on the deepfake image.

Figure 2.
Steps in generating patches on the image. There are 3 steps in generating face patches. 1) image extraction from a video. 2) Apply landmarks (red circles in the image) on the extracted images. 3) Insert patches on nose and mouth.

Figure 3.
Pipeline of our method. In the face-patch model, each selected model is first trained on normal faces and trained on patched faces respectively. In the face-crop model, model is trained on only the cropped normal faces. The model is tested on unseen generated facemask images to detect deepfake images with facemasks.

Cropped Face Generation.

To crop the bottom part of the face to focus on the eyes from the deepfake images, We utilize Face Alignment Network(FAN)
(
Bulat and
Tzimiropoulos 2017
)
, an open-source landmark detection library that is effective to catch the face landmark on faces with facemask. With this library, we find the face landmark on the masked faces and obtain the coordinates of the eyes landmark. Then, we drop the area below the eyes in the face images for the dataset generation.

4.
Experimental Settings

In this experiment, all models were trained on learning rate 0.001 with Adam
(
Kingma and Ba 2017
)
optimizer.
First, the models were trained for 50 epochs as baseline models with each dataset independently. For the face-patch method, the pretrained weight of baseline models were trained via transfer learning for 50 epochs with the patch-generated faces. Also, we trained other models which focus on cropped face images for 50 epochs that are independent with baseline and transfer-learned models.

Evaluation Metrics.
We used accuracy as well as AUC to evaluate the performance of our models in each dataset since true positives and true negatives are equally important. Furthermore, our data are balanced so we used AUC scores to evaluate our results.

Machine Configuration.
Our experiment is conducted on TITAN RTX GPUs and Nvidia RTX 3090 GPUs with 24GB of video memory, 8 cores of Intel Xeon Gold 6230 CPUs abd 256GB of RAM. Ubuntu OS 18.04.2 LTS 64 bit with PyTorch v1.7.1 with CUDA 11.0 and Python 3.7 was used in all experiments.

Data Preprocessing.

For each dataset, we extracted 750 frames
\times
80 videos for DeepFake, Face2Face, FaceSwap, NeuralTextures and FakeAVCeleb. During the extraction process, the images were preprocessed as mentioned in Section
3
. All dataset is resized and center-cropped to 128
\times
128. Lastly, we applied Pytorch normalization with ([0.485, 0.456, 0.406], [0.229, 0.224, 0.225]).

Experimental Results

In this section, we demonstrate our experimental results for baseline and two proposed methods for different masked datasets. We evaluate our model performances primarily under Faceforensics++ C40 and FakeAVCeleb facemask generated dataset.

presents a summary of our results from all different methods.

5.1.
Baselines Performance Results

We test our baseline models on face with facemask, and to show the upper bound of the detection model, and we also test the models on face without facemask. As demonstrated in Table
2
, baseline models clearly underperform when they encounter facemask in every datasets. In particular, Xception models trained on DeepFakes and FakeAVCeleb images separately shows
24.13\%
,
13.32\%
decrease in accuracy performance when face with facemask were given on test compare to face without facemask. In total, testing on face with facemask shows
19.98\%
and
18.75\%
decrease in average accuracy and AUC performance compare to testing on face without facemask. The decrease of performance on baseline model in uncompressed dataset (FakeAVCeleb) is larger than in compressed dataset(FaceForensics++ C40), showing
22.86\%
,
20.86\%
and
9.6\%
,
11.32\%
decrease in average accuarcy and AUC performance of each. Images applied with NeuralTextures method demonstrate lowest accuracy and AUC for all models compared to other generation methods. In contrast, all models show high performance in detection of fake images in FakeAVCeleb dataset. We hypothesize that FakeAVCeleb is not compressed and it contains some similar source faces in train and test images.
In general, Xception shows the best performance on both face with and without facemask.

Performance of Face-patch Method

To enhance the performance of detection models, we trained our models with images with patches explained in Section
3

8.82\%
respectively compare to baseline model tested on face with facemask. Face-patch method provides an increase of average accuracy and AUC by

3.75\%
in accuracy performance of Xception.

5.3.
Performance of Face-crop Method

With our second proposed method, we aim to evaluate the performance of the models by focusing on the facial features above the mask. We found that the models were able to detect fake facial images with facemasks more accurately compared to our previous method of patch trained method in Section
5.2
. Xception trained on DeepFakes was able to achieve
30.29\%
increase in accuarcy compare to its baseline model. In total, model trained with cropped face shows
17.57\%

13.83\%

Moreover, MobileNetV2 100 trained on cropped FakeAVCeleb dataset outperforms baseline models tested on face without facemask.

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we present two method to detect deepfake facial images with facemask. We demonstrate that learning facial features, while inhibiting nose and mouth features by generating patches on the images improves the baseline models in which they were fully trained on masks. Furthermore, we present the face cropping method, which achieves higher accuracy and AUC score compared with baseline and face-patch method
and could be a deepfake detection method to determine fake faces with facemask in real world. With these two method, we evaluate the performance of the deepfake detection models by recent deepfake datasets with manipulated facemask. Furthermore, this work can
this work can be further developed to create new facemask dataset for deepfake images to generalize the current pandemic state. In conclusion, we hope that our attempt can become a stepping stone for further research in the field of deepfake detection focused on facemask dataset.

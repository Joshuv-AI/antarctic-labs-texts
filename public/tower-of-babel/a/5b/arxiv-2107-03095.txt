Jie Xiao, Han Xu, Minghui Zhao

Abstract
. In [ 17 ] , Lusztig gave an explicit construction of the affine canonical basis by simple perverse sheaves. In this paper, we construct a bar-invariant basis by using a PBW basis arising from representations of the corresponding tame quiver. We prove that this bar-invariant basis coincides with Lusztig’s canonical basis and obtain a concrete bijection between the elements in theses two bases. The index set of these bases is listed orderly by modules in preprojective, regular non-homogeneous, preinjective components and irreducible characters of symmetric groups. Our results are based on the work of Lin-Xiao-Zhang ( [ 14 ] ) and closely related with the work of Beck-Nakajima ( [ 2 ] ). A crucial method in our construction is a generalization of that in [ 8 ] .



1.
Introduction

Let
Q=(I,H)
be a quiver and
{\bf U}
the quantized enveloping algebra associated with the quiver
Q
. Denote by
{\bf f}
the
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
-algebra with unit
{\bf 1}
generated by
\theta_{i},i\in I
subject to the quantum Serre relations.
The algebra
{\bf f}
is isomorphic to the positive (resp. negative) part of
{\bf U}
.

In
[
24
]
, Ringel introduced the Ringel-Hall algebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
and prove that there is an isomorphism between the generic composition subalgebra
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
and the algebra
{\bf f}
.
Inspired by the work of Ringel, Lusztig (
[
15
]
[
16
]
) gave a geometric realization of
{\bf f}
via the category of certain semisimple perverse sheaves on the variety of representations of the corresponding quiver
Q
. The set
{\bf B}
of the isomorphism classes of simple perverse sheaves gives a basis of
{\bf f}
, which is called the canonical basis.

In the case of finite type, Lusztig (
[
15
]
) also gave an elementary algebraic construction of the canonical basis via the Ringel-Hall realization of
{\bf f}
.
The set of isomorphism classes of representations of the corresponding quiver gives a PBW basis of
{\bf f}
. Via this PBW basis, Lusztig construct a bar invariant basis, which is the canonical basis.

Assume that
Q
is a Dynkin quiver.
Denote by
\mathbb{N}^{{\rm ind}\,Q}
the set of all functions
{\bf c}:{\rm ind}\,Q\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, where
{\rm ind}\,Q
is the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in the category
{\rm rep}\,Q
of representations of quiver
Q
. For each
{\bf c}:{\rm ind}\,Q\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, there is a representation

M({\bf c})
, such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
is spanned by the set

\{\langle{M({\bf c})}\rangle|{\bf c}:{\rm ind}\,Q\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\}

as
\mathbb{Q}(v)
-vector space. This set is called a PBW basis of
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
.

For each
{\bf c}:{\rm ind}\,Q\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, there exists a monomial
m_{{\bf c}}
on Chevalley generators satisfying

m_{{\bf c}}=\langle{M({\bf c})}\rangle+\sum_{{\bf c}^{\prime}<{\bf c}}a^{{\bf c}^{\prime}}_{{\bf c}}\langle{M({\bf c}^{\prime})}\rangle,

with
a^{{\bf c}^{\prime}}_{{\bf c}}\in\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
.
Since
\overline{m_{{\bf c}}}=m_{{\bf c}}
, we have

\overline{\langle{M({\bf c})}\rangle}=\langle{M({\bf c})}\rangle+\sum_{{\bf c}^{\prime}<{\bf c}}b^{{\bf c}^{\prime}}_{{\bf c}}\langle{M({\bf c}^{\prime})}\rangle,

with
b^{{\bf c}^{\prime}}_{{\bf c}}\in\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]
.

According to an elementary linear algebra method of Lusztig, we can construct a bar-invariant basis

\{C_{{\bf c}}|{\bf c}:{\rm ind}\,Q\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\}.

Under the isomorphism between
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
and
{\bf f}
, this bar-invariant basis induces a basis of
{\bf f}
, which is the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}
of
{\bf f}
.

In the case of affine type, Lusztig (
[
17
]
) gave an explicit construction of the canonical basis by simple perverse sheaves indexed by the isomorphism classes of aperiodic modules of the corresponding tame quiver and irreducible representations of symmetric groups. It is an important problem to generalize the elementary algebraic construction to the affine canonical basis.

Beck-Chari-Pressley (
[
1
]
) and Beck-Nakajima (
[
2
]
) gave an algebraic construction of the canonical basis by using a PBW basis (see
[
22
]
).

Let
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}
be a symmetric generalized Cartan matrix of affine type, where
I=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
,
0\in I
is the exceptional point and
I_{0}=I\backslash\{0\}
.

Beck-Nakajima defined an infinite sequence

h=(\cdots,i_{-1},i_{0},i_{1},\cdots)

in
I
such that
R=R_{>}\bigsqcup R_{<}
is the set of all positive real roots, where

R_{>}=\{\beta_{0}=\alpha_{i_{0}},\beta_{-1}=s_{i_{0}}(\alpha_{i_{-1}}),\beta_{-2}=s_{i_{0}}s_{i_{-1}}(\alpha_{i_{-2}}),\cdots\}

and

R_{<}=\{\beta_{1}=\alpha_{i_{1}},\beta_{2}=s_{i_{1}}(\alpha_{i_{2}}),\beta_{3}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}(\alpha_{i_{3}}),\cdots\}.

Let

E_{\beta_{k}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}T^{-1}_{i_{0}}T^{-1}_{i_{-1}}\cdots T^{-1}_{i_{k+1}}(E_{i_{k}})&\textrm{if $k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}$},\\
T_{i_{1}}T_{i_{2}}\cdots T_{i_{k-1}}(E_{i_{k}})&\textrm{if $k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$},\end{array}\right.

where
T_{j}
is the Lusztig’s symmetry corresponding to
j\in I
.
Then
E_{\beta_{k}}
is a real root vector for the root
\beta_{k}\in R
.

Also, Beck-Nakajima defined integral imaginary root vectors
\tilde{P}_{i,k}
from real root vectors by a recursive identity.
Denote by
\mathscr{P}
the set of all partitions.
For
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\cdots)\in\mathscr{P}
, define the Schur function

S_{\lambda}=\det(\tilde{P}_{i,\lambda_{k}-k+m})_{1\leqslant k,m\leqslant t},

where
t
is the length of
\lambda
.

For a map
c_{0}:I_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{P}
, denote

S_{c_{0}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}S_{c_{0}(i)}.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of
\bar{c}=(c,c_{0})
, where
c_{0}:I_{0}\rightarrow\mathscr{P}
is a map and
c:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
is a function with finite support.
For any
\bar{c}\in\mathcal{E}
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, let

\displaystyle L(\bar{c},p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(E_{i_{p}}^{(c(p))}T_{i_{p}}^{-1}(E_{i_{p-1}}^{(c(p-1))})T_{i_{p}}^{-1}T_{i_{p-1}}^{-1}(E_{i_{p-2}}^{(c(p-2))})\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle T_{i_{p+1}}T_{i_{p+2}}\cdots T_{i_{0}}(S_{c_{0}})

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\cdots T_{i_{p+1}}T_{i_{p+2}}(E_{i_{p+3}}^{(c(p+3))})T_{i_{p+1}}(E_{i_{p+2}}^{(c(p+2))})E_{i_{p+1}}^{(c(p+1))}\right).

Beck-Nakajima defined a certain partial ordering
\prec_{p}
on
{\mathcal{E}}
for each
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and proved that

\overline{L(\bar{c},p)}=L(\bar{c},p)+\sum_{\bar{c}\prec_{p}\bar{c}^{\prime}}a_{\bar{c},\bar{c}^{\prime}}L(\bar{c}^{\prime},p)

with
a_{\bar{c},\bar{c}^{\prime}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[v,v^{-1}]
.

According to an elementary linear algebra method of Lusztig, Beck-Nakajima constructed a bar-invariant basis

\{b(\bar{c},p)|\bar{c}\in\mathcal{E}\}

and proved that
this is the canonical basis
\mathbf{B}
of
{\bf f}
.

In the case of the Kronecker quiver, Chen (
[
6
]
) and Zhang (
[
31
]
) constructed PBW bases of
{\bf f}
, and McGerty (
[
21
]
) gave the interpretation of PBW bases of
{\bf f}
constructed by Beck-Nakajima in terms of representations of Kronecker quiver.
In the case of cyclic quiver, Deng-Du-Xiao (
[
8
]
) gave an inductive method to construct a PBW basis from the monomial basis. By using the triangular relations between the PBW basis and the monomial basis, they gave an algebraic construction of the canonical basis.
Based on the work of Chen, Zhang and Deng-Du-Xiao, Lin-Xiao-Zhang (
[
14
]
) gave an algebraic construction of the canonical basis by using the representations of tame quiver.

In this paper, we give an algebraic construction of the canonical basis by using a PBW basis arising from representations of the corresponding tame quiver. Our results are based on the work of Lin-Xiao-Zhang (
[
14
]
) and are closely related with the work of Beck-Nakajima (
[
2
]
). A crucial method in our construction is a generalization of that in
[
8
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
be the set consisting of all triples
{\bf c}=({\bf c}_{-},{\bf c}_{0},{\bf c}_{+})
, where
{\bf c}_{-}
arising from preprojective components,
{\bf c}_{+}
arising from preinjective components, and
{\bf c}_{0}
arising from non-homogeneous tubes of
{\rm rep}\,Q
. We define the index set
{\mathcal{G}}
consisting of all pairs
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
with
{\bf c}\in{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}
and
t_{\lambda}
be the character of the irreducible module
S_{\lambda}
of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{|\lambda|}
for a partition
\lambda
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{q}
be the
{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})
-subalgebra of the Ringel-Hall algebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
generated by
u_{i}=u_{[S_{i}]},i\in I
and
u_{[M]}
for all representations
M
in non-homogeneous tubes, where
S_{i}
is the simple module concentrated at
i\in I
.
For
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}
, we define an element

N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{q}
and we prove that

\{N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}\}

is a
{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})
-basis of
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{q}
.
Moreover, this is a ”generic” basis independent of
q
, which allows us to define a generic form
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
and we have
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}\subset{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
.

Let
{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
be the subset of
{\mathcal{G}}
consisting of aperiodic indices.
For
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we define a monomial basis of the composition algebra

\{{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}.

Then we use the method in
[
8
]
to construct a PBW basis

\{E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}

for
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}
inductively, where
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}
is the integral form of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
.

At last, we construct a bar-invariant basis

\{C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}

by using the triangular relation between the PBW basis and the monomial basis.

Let
B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=[\mathrm{IC}(X({\bf c},|\lambda|),{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda})]
, the image of perverse sheaf
\mathrm{IC}(X({\bf c},|\lambda|),{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda})
in the Grothendieck group, where the local system
{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda}
is given by an irreducible module of
\mathfrak{S}_{|\lambda|}
. By Lusztig, the set

{\bf B}=\{B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}

is the canonical basis.

The following theorem is the main result in this paper.

Theorem 1
.

For all
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, it holds that
C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
under the isomorphism between
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
and
{\bf f}
.

Hence the bar-invariant basis constructed in this paper is the canonical basis
{\bf B}
and we give an algebraic construction of the canonical basis.

We write few words here to explain why we need a further investigation upon the work of Lin-Xiao-Zhang (
[
14
]
) and Beck-Nakajima (
[
2
]
).

We hope to use the well-known structure of representations of tame quivers to construct a PBW basis of
{\bf f}
and show its relation with a monomial basis clearly, similarly to the case of finite type. New progresses in this paper are as follows.

(1)

We introduce the reductive form
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
by extending the generic composition algebra
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, which is a
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
-algebra with a natural PBW basis. By using the PBW basis of
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
, we define a monomial basis and construct a PBW basis for the integral form
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}
of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, and obtain a unipotent triangular relation between these two bases over
{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathbb{Z}}[v,v^{-1}]
. We prove also that these two bases are
{\mathcal{Z}}
-bases of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}
. This is an improvement of the main result of
[
14
]
(see Section 6.5 of
[
14
]
).

(2)

Each basis element of the PBW basis we constructed corresponds to an aperiodic module of the quiver
Q
and a partition
\lambda
. This fulfill, in an algebraic way, that Lusztig’s perverse sheaves
\mathrm{IC}(X({\bf c},|\lambda|),{\mathcal{L}}_{\lambda})
with the parameter
{\bf c}
corresponds to the aperiodic representation
M({\bf c})
of
Q
.

(3)

For a partition
\lambda
of homogeneous regular modules
of
{\rm rep}\,Q
, we have the permutation action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{|\lambda|}
. The indices
t_{\lambda}
in the PBW basis elements
E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
are given by irreducible characters of
\mathfrak{S}_{|\lambda|}
, which correspond bijectively to the local systems
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
of Lusztig’s perverse sheaves.

(4)

The final step is the construction of a bar-invariant basis according to an elementary linear algebra method of Lusztig (
[
19
]
), by using the unipotent triangular relation between the monomial basis and the PBW basis. We prove that it coincides with the canonical basis, moreover, each element in the canonical basis is explicitly realized.

In Section 2 and 3, we recall some basic definitions for quiver representations, Ringel-Hall algebras and quantum groups.
In Section 4, we recall the algebraic construction of the canonical basis for the quantized enveloping algebra corresponding to a cyclic quiver given by Deng-Du-Xiao.
The representations of affine quivers are recalled in Section 5.
Then we define the extended composition algebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
, the PBW basis elements and their index set in Section 6, 7, 8.
We give the construction of a monomial basis
\{{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
, a PBW basis
\{E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
and a bar-invariant basis
\{C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}

in Section 9 and prove in Section 11 that the bar-invariant basis is exactly the canonical basis introduced in Section 10.
Based on the proof of Theorem
1
, we give a more direct algorithm to compute
C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
in the last section.

2.
Quivers and representations

2.1.
Quivers

A quiver
Q=(I,H,s,t)
consists of a vertex set
I
, an arrow set
H
, and two maps
s,t:H\rightarrow I
such that an arrow
h\in H
starts at
s(h)
and terminates at
t(h)
.
A quiver
Q
is called acyclic if there is no oriented cycle.
Throughout the paper, we always assume that
Q
is finite, i.e.,
I
and
H
are finite sets.

2.2.
Representations

Let
k
be a field. A representation
(V_{i},V_{h})_{i\in I,h\in H}
of
Q
over
k
is a family of
k
-vector spaces
V_{i},i\in I
and linear maps
V_{h}:V_{s(h)}\rightarrow V_{t(h)},h\in H
.
A representation
(V_{i},V_{h})
is called nilpotent if the map
V_{h_{l}}\circ V_{h_{l-1}}\circ\cdots\circ V_{h_{1}}:V_{s(h_{1})}\rightarrow V_{s(h_{1})}
is nilpotent, for any
h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{l}\in H
with
t(h_{j})=s(h_{j+1}),1\leqslant j<l,s(h_{1})=t(h_{l})
.
Note that all representations are nilpotent if
Q
is acyclic.
In this paper, all modules mentioned will be nilpotent.
By
S_{j}
we denote the simple module
(V_{i},V_{h})
with
V_{j}=k
and
V_{i}=0,i\neq j
.

Let
kQ
be the path algebra of
Q
over
k
.
By
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
we denote the category of finite dimensional left
kQ
-modules.
It is well-known that
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
is equivalent to the category
\mathrm{rep}_{k}Q
of finite dimensional representations of
Q
over
k
.
We shall simply identify
kQ
-modules with representations of
Q
over
k
.

2.3.
Dimension vectors and the Euler form

The set of isomorphism classes of (nilpotent) simple
kQ
-modules is naturally indexed by the vertex set
I
of
Q
.
Then the Grothendieck group
G(kQ)
of
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
is the free Abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}I
. We say
\nu\leqslant\nu^{\prime}
for
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}I
, if
\nu^{\prime}-\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}I
.

For each nilpotent
kQ
-module
M=(M_{i},M_{h})
, the dimension vector

\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M=\sum_{i\in I}(\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,M_{i})i

is an element of
{\mathbb{N}}I
.

The Euler form
\langle-,-\rangle
on
{\mathbb{Z}}I
is defined by

\langle\nu,\nu^{\prime}\rangle=\sum_{i\in I}\nu_{i}\nu^{\prime}_{i}-\sum_{h\in H}\nu_{s(h)}\nu^{\prime}_{t(h)}

for
\nu=\sum_{i\in I}\nu_{i}i
and
\nu^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in I}\nu^{\prime}_{i}i
in
{\mathbb{Z}}I
.
For any two nilpotent
kQ
-modules
M
and
N
, one has

\langle\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M,\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,N\rangle=\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Hom}_{kQ}(M,N)-\mathrm{dim}_{k}\,\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{kQ}(M,N).

The symmetric Euler form is defined as

(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\langle\nu,\nu^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\nu^{\prime},\nu\rangle\,\,\mathrm{for}\,\,\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}I.

This gives rise to a symmetric generalized Cartan matrix
C=((i,j))_{i,j\in I}
.
It is easy to see that
C
is independent of the field
k
and the orientation of
Q
.

2.4.
Root systems and Weyl groups

Since we only consider tame quivers, the root systems can be defined in an easier way (see
[
7
]
).
A nonzero element
\nu
in
{\mathbb{Z}}I
is called a root if
0\leqslant(\nu,\nu)\leqslant 2
.
Denote by
R
the set of all roots.
Let
R_{+}=R\cap{\mathbb{N}}I
and
R_{-}=-R_{+}
.
It is well-known that

R=R_{+}\sqcup R_{-}.

One can check that
(\nu,\nu)\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Thus if
\nu
is a root, then
(\nu,\nu)=0
or
2
.
A root is called real (resp. imaginary) if
(\nu,\nu)=2
(resp. 0).
Denote by
R^{\rm re}_{+}
(resp.
R^{\rm im}_{+}
) the set of all real (resp. imaginary) positive roots.

When
Q
is of affine type, it is well-known that all imaginary positive roots are of the form
m\delta
where
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
and
\delta\in R_{+}
.
The root
\delta=\sum_{i\in I}\delta_{i}i
is called the minimal imaginary positive root. Let
|\delta|=\sum_{i}\delta_{i}
.

Given any
i\in I
, we denote by
s_{i}
the automorphism of
{\mathbb{Z}}I
defined by

s_{i}(\nu)=\nu-(\nu,i)i

for any
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}I
.
Note that
s_{i}
is involutive, i.e.
s_{i}\circ s_{i}=\mathrm{id}_{{\mathbb{Z}}I}
.
The bilinear form
(-,-)
is
s_{i}
-invariant for all
i\in I
.
The Weyl group is the subgroup of
\mbox{\rm Aut}({\mathbb{Z}}I)
generated by
s_{i},i\in I
.

3.
Ringel-Hall algebras and quantum groups

3.1.
Notations

Given
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and any symbol
x
, we define the notation

[n]_{x}=\frac{x^{n}-x^{-n}}{x-x^{-1}}.

We define
[0]_{x}=[0]_{x}^{!}=1
and
[n]_{x}^{!}=[n]_{x}[n-1]_{x}\cdots[1]_{x}
for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
.

3.2.
Ringel-Hall algebras

Let
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements.
Given three modules
L,M,N
in
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
, let
g^{L}_{MN}
be the number of
kQ
-submodules
W
of
L
such that
W\cong N
and
L/W\cong M
in
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
.
Let
v_{q}=\sqrt{q}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
be the set of isomorphism classes of finite dimensional nilpotent
kQ
-modules. We denote by
[M]
the isomorphism class of
M
for any
kQ
-module
M
.

The (twisted) Ringel-Hall algebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}={\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
of
kQ
is by definition the
{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})
-space with basis
\{u_{[M]}|[M]\in{\mathcal{M}}\}
, whose multiplication
*
is given by

u_{[M]}*u_{[N]}=v_{q}^{\langle\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M,\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,N\rangle}\sum_{[L]\in{\mathcal{M}}}g^{L}_{MN}u_{[L]}.

Following
[
26
]
, for any
kQ
-module
M
, we denote

\langle M\rangle=v_{q}^{-\mathrm{dim}\,M+\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{End}_{kQ}M}u_{[M]}.

Note that
g^{L}_{MN}
depends only on the isomorphism classes of
M,N
and
L
, and for fixed isomorphism classes of
M,N
there are only finitely many isomorphism classes
[L]
with
g^{L}_{MN}\neq 0
.
It is clear that
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
is an associative
{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})
-algebra with unit
1=u_{0}
, where
0
denotes the isomorphism class of zero module.
We sometimes use the notation
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}={\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(Q)
instead of
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
to indicate the dependence on
q
when needed.

Here is a property that will be used later.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
26
]
)
.

An indecomposable
kQ
-module
M
is called exceptional if

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{kQ}(M,M)=0.

If
M
is exceptional, then

\langle M\rangle^{(m)}=\langle M^{\oplus m}\rangle.

If
M,N\in\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
with
\mathrm{Hom}_{kQ}(N,M)=0
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{kQ}(M,N)=0
, then

\langle M\rangle*\langle N\rangle=\langle M\oplus N\rangle,

where
x^{(m)}=x^{m}/[m]_{v_{q}}^{!}
.

3.3.
Quantum groups

Let
v
be an indeterminant and
{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathbb{Z}}[v,v^{-1}]
.
Let
{\bf f}
be the
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
-algebra with unit
1
generated by
\theta_{i},i\in I
subject to the quantum Serre relations

\sum_{s+r=1-(i,j)}(-1)^{s}\theta_{i}^{(s)}\theta_{j}\theta_{i}^{(r)}=0,i\neq j.

For any
\nu=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}i\in{\mathbb{N}}I
, we denote by
{\bf f}_{\nu}
the
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
-subspace of
{\bf f}
spanned by the monomials
\theta_{i_{1}}\theta_{i_{2}}\cdots\theta_{i_{r}}
such that
\sum_{j}i_{j}=\nu
.
Then we have a direct sum decomposition
{\bf f}=\oplus_{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}I}{\bf f}_{\nu}
.
We have
{\bf f}_{\nu}{\bf f}_{\nu^{\prime}}\subset{\bf f}_{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}
,
1\in{\bf f}_{0}
and
\theta_{i}\in{\bf f}_{i}
.

An element
x
in
{\bf f}
is said to be homogeneous if it belongs to
{\bf f}_{\nu}
for some
\nu
.
We set
|x|=\nu
.

The integral form
{\bf f}_{\mathcal{Z}}
is by definition the
{\mathcal{Z}}
-subalgebra of
{\bf f}
generated by
\theta_{i}^{(m)},i\in I,m\geqslant 0
.

The quantized enveloping algebra
{\bf U}
associated with a quiver
Q
is an associative algebra over
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
with unit
{\bf 1}
generated by the elements
E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}^{\pm}(i\in I)
subject to the following relations:

K_{0}={\bf 1},K_{\nu}K_{\nu^{\prime}}=K_{\nu+\nu^{\prime}}\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,\,\,\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}I;

K_{\nu}E_{i}K_{-\nu}=v^{(\nu,i)}E_{i}\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,\,\,i\in I,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}I;

K_{\nu}F_{i}K_{-\nu}=v^{-(\nu,i)}F_{i}\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,\,\,i\in I,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}I;

E_{i}F_{j}-F_{j}E_{i}=\delta_{ij}\frac{K_{i}-K_{-i}}{v-v^{-1}}\,\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,\,\,i,j\in I;

\sum^{1-(i,j)}_{k=0}(-1)^{k}E_{i}^{(k)}E_{j}E_{i}^{(1-(i,j)-k)}=0\,\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\,\,i\neq j\in I;

\sum^{1-(i,j)}_{k=0}(-1)^{k}F_{i}^{(k)}F_{j}F_{i}^{(1-(i,j)-k)}=0\,\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\,\,i\neq j\in I.

Here
E_{i}^{(n)}=E_{i}^{n}/[n]_{v}^{!}
,
F_{i}^{(n)}=F_{i}^{n}/[n]_{v}^{!}
.

Let
{\bf U}^{+}
(resp.
{\bf U}^{-}
) be the subalgebra of
{\bf U}
generated by
E_{i}
(resp.
F_{i}
) for
i\in I
, and let
{\bf U}^{0}
be the subalgebra of
{\bf U}
generated by
K_{i}
for
i\in I
.
It is well known that
{\bf U}
has the triangular decomposition

{\bf U}={\bf U}^{-}\otimes{\bf U}^{0}\otimes{\bf U}^{+}.

It is easy to see that there exist well-defined
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
-algebra monomorphisms
{\bf f}\rightarrow{\bf U}(x\mapsto x^{+})
and
{\bf f}\rightarrow{\bf U}(x\mapsto x^{-})
with image
{\bf U}^{+}
and
{\bf U}^{-}
respectively satisfying
E_{i}=\theta_{i}^{+}
and
F_{i}=\theta_{i}^{-}
. We denote
{\bf U}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}={\bf f}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}
,
{\bf U}^{-}_{\mathcal{Z}}={\bf f}^{-}_{\mathcal{Z}}
.

3.4.
Composition subalgebras

Let
{\mathcal{W}}
be an infinite set consisting of numbers of the form
q=p^{s}
with
p
prime and
s
positive integer.
The integral generic composition algebra
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}(Q)
is defined to be the
{\mathcal{Z}}
-subalgebra of
\Pi_{q\in{\mathcal{W}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(Q)
generated by
u_{i}^{(m)}=(u^{(m)}_{[S_{i}]})_{q}=(\langle S_{i}^{\oplus m}\rangle)_{q},i\in I,m\geqslant 0
, where
v
acts by
(v_{q})_{q}
and
v^{-1}
acts by
(v_{q}^{-1})_{q}
.
Then define the generic composition algebra

{\mathcal{C}}^{*}={\mathbb{Q}}(v)\otimes_{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}.

It is well-known (
[
24
]
) that there is an isomorphism of algebras

\displaystyle\Phi:{\bf f}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{*}

\displaystyle\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\longmapsto u_{i},

such that
\Phi({\bf f}_{\mathcal{Z}})={\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Denote by
\Phi^{+}:{\bf U}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
the composition of
\Phi
and the isomorphism
{\bf U}^{+}\rightarrow{\bf f}
.

For a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we denote by
\Phi_{q}
the specializing map given by the composition of
\Phi:{\bf f}_{\mathcal{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow\Pi_{q^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q^{\prime}}(Q)
and the projection
\Pi_{q^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q^{\prime}}(Q)\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(Q)
.

3.5.
Monomials

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be the set of all pairs
\omega=(\underline{i},\underline{a})
, where
\underline{i}=(i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{m})
is a sequence in
I
and
\underline{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{m})
is a sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
of the same length.
For such a pair
\omega=(\underline{i},\underline{a})\in{\mathcal{S}}
, we define a monomial

{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega}=\theta_{i_{1}}^{(a_{1})}\theta_{i_{2}}^{(a_{2})}\cdots\theta_{i_{m}}^{(a_{m})}

in
{\bf f}
.
By definition,
{\bf f}_{\mathcal{Z}}
is
{\mathcal{Z}}
-spanned by
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega}
for all
\omega\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

For simplicity, under the isomorphism
\Phi
, we still denote
\Phi({\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega})=u_{i_{1}}^{(a_{1})}*u_{i_{2}}^{(a_{2})}*\cdots*u_{i_{m}}^{(a_{m})}
by
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega}
.
When specializing to a field

, we still denote
\Phi_{q}({\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega})=u_{[S_{i_{1}}]}^{(a_{1})}*u_{[S_{i_{2}}]}^{(a_{2})}*\cdots*u_{[S_{i_{m}}]}^{(a_{m})}
by
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega}
.

3.6.
A bilinear form and the coproduct

Define an algebra structure on
{\bf f}\otimes{\bf f}
by

(x_{1}\otimes x_{2})(y_{1}\otimes y_{2})=v^{(|x_{2}|,|y_{1}|)}x_{1}y_{1}\otimes x_{2}y_{2},

where
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}
are homogeneous.

The coproduct
r:{\bf f}\rightarrow{\bf f}\otimes{\bf f}
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
-algebra homomorphism such that
r(\theta_{i})=\theta_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes\theta_{i},i\in I
.

There is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
(-,-)
on
{\bf f}
with values in
{\mathbb{Q}}(v)
such that
(1,1)=1
and

(a)

(\theta_{i},\theta_{j})=\delta_{ij}(1-v^{-2})^{-1},i,j\in I
;

(b)

(x,yy^{\prime})=(r(x),y\otimes y^{\prime}),x,y,y^{\prime}\in{\bf f}
,
where the form on
{\bf f}\otimes{\bf f}
is defined by
(x_{1}\otimes y_{1},x_{2}\otimes y_{2})=(x_{1},x_{2})(y_{1},y_{2})
.

Let
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, the inner product
(-,-)
on
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
is given by the formula (
[
11
]
)

(\langle M\rangle,\langle N\rangle)=\delta_{MN}\frac{v_{q}^{2\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{End}(M)}}{a_{M}},

where
a_{M}=|\mbox{\rm Aut}(M)|
.
This inner product is also well-defined on
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, which coincides with the bilinear form on
{\bf f}
under the isomorphism
\Phi
.

Following
[
11
]
and
[
24
]
, under the homomorphism
\Phi_{q}
, the linear operation
r:{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)\otimes{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
given by

r(u_{[L]})=\sum_{[M],[N]}v_{q}^{\langle\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M,\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,N\rangle}g^{L}_{M,N}\frac{a_{M}a_{N}}{a_{L}}u_{[M]}\otimes u_{[N]}

coincides with the coproduct
r:{\bf f}\rightarrow{\bf f}\otimes{\bf f}
.

3.7.
The bar involution and the canonical basis

There is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra automorphism
\bar{\cdot}:x\mapsto\bar{x}
on
{\bf f}
defined by
\bar{\theta}_{i}=\theta_{i}
,
\bar{v}=v^{-1}
, called bar involution.

By
[
19
]
, let
{\mathcal{B}}
be the set of all
x\in{\bf f}_{\mathcal{Z}}
such that
\bar{x}=x
and
(x,x)\in 1+v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[[v^{-1}]]
. The set
{\mathcal{B}}
is called the signed canonical basis of
{\bf f}
and Lusztig defined by induction a particular subset
{\bf B}\subset{\mathcal{B}}
such that
{\mathcal{B}}={\bf B}\sqcup-{\bf B}
(see
[
19
]
, 14.4). The set
{\bf B}
is called the canonical basis of
{\bf f}
. The aim of this paper is to construct
{\bf B}
by Ringel-Hall algebra approach.

3.8.
Lusztig’s symmetries

Lusztig in
[
19
]
introduced the symmetries
T_{i}=T_{i,1}^{\prime\prime}:{\bf U}\rightarrow{\bf U}
for
i\in I
, as algebra automorphisms of
{\bf U}
defined by relations:

T_{i}(K_{\nu})=K_{s_{i}(\nu)};T_{i}(E_{i})=-F_{i}K_{i};T_{i}(F_{i})=-K_{i}^{-1}E_{i},

T_{i}(E_{j})=\sum_{r+s=-(i,j)}(-1)^{r}v^{-r}E_{i}^{(s)}E_{j}E_{i}^{(r)},j\neq i,

T_{i}(F_{j})=\sum_{r+s=-(i,j)}(-1)^{r}v^{r}F_{i}^{(r)}F_{j}F_{i}^{(s)},j\neq i,

and its inverse
T_{i}^{-1}=T_{i,-1}^{\prime}:{\bf U}\rightarrow{\bf U}
defined by relations:

T_{i}^{-1}(K_{\nu})=K_{s_{i}(\nu)};T_{i}^{-1}(E_{i})=-K_{i}^{-1}F_{i};T_{i}^{-1}(F_{i})=-E_{i}K_{i},

T_{i}^{-1}(E_{j})=\sum_{r+s=-(i,j)}(-1)^{r}v^{-r}E_{i}^{(r)}E_{j}E_{i}^{(s)},j\neq i,

T_{i}^{-1}(F_{j})=\sum_{r+s=-(i,j)}(-1)^{r}v^{r}F_{i}^{(s)}F_{j}F_{i}^{(r)},j\neq i.

For
i\in I
, define

{\bf U}^{+}[i]=\{x\in{\bf U}^{+}|T_{i}(x)\in{\bf U}^{+}\},{{}^{\sigma}{\bf U}^{+}}[i]=\{\in{\bf U}^{+}|T_{i}^{-1}(x)\in{\bf U}^{+}\}.

Then
T_{i}:{\bf U}^{+}[i]\rightarrow{{}^{\sigma}{\bf U}^{+}}[i]
is an isomorphism and
T_{i}^{-1}
is its inverse. Moreover, let
{\bf U}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}[i]={\bf U}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}\cap{\bf U}^{+}[i]
and
{{}^{\sigma}{\bf U}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}[i]}={\bf U}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}\cap{{}^{\sigma}{\bf U}^{+}}[i]
, then
T_{i}:{\bf U}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}[i]\rightarrow{{}^{\sigma}{\bf U}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}}[i]
is an isomorphism and
T_{i}^{-1}
is its inverse.

3.9.
BGP reflections functors

A vertex
i\in I
is called a sink (resp. source) of
Q
if there is no arrow
h\in H
with
s(h)=i
(resp.
t(h)=i
).
We define
\sigma_{i}Q
to be the quiver obtained from
Q
by reversing the direction of every arrow connected to the vertex
i
.
In
[
3
]
, one may define the BGP reflection functors:

\sigma^{+}_{i}:\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ\rightarrow\mbox{\rm mod}\,k\sigma_{i}Q

if
i
is a sink and

\sigma^{-}_{i}:\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ\rightarrow\mbox{\rm mod}\,k\sigma_{i}Q

if
i
is a source.
Let
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ[i]
be the subcategory of
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
consisting of all representations which do not have
S_{i}
as a direct summand and
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)[i]
be the subalgebra of
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
generated by
u_{[M]}
with
M\in\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ[i]
.
Therefore we have algebra homomorphisms:

\sigma_{i}^{+}:{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)[i]\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(k\sigma_{i}Q)[i]

if
i
is a sink and

\sigma_{i}^{-}:{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)[i]\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(k\sigma_{i}Q)[i]

if
i
is a source, defined by

\sigma_{i}^{\pm}(\langle M\rangle)=\langle\sigma_{i}^{\pm}(M)\rangle

for any

. Furthermore, they induce the homomorphisms

\sigma_{i}^{+}:{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(Q)[i]\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\sigma_{i}Q)[i]

if
i
is a sink and

\sigma_{i}^{-}:{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(Q)[i]\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(\sigma_{i}Q)[i]

if

is a source.
Here
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(Q)[i]={\mathcal{C}}^{*}(Q)\cap\prod_{q\in{\mathcal{W}}}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}Q)[i]
.

It is known that
\sigma_{i}^{+}=T_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}^{-}=T_{i}^{-1}
under the identification
\Phi:{\bf U}^{+}[i]\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(Q)[i]
when

is a sink and
\Phi:{{}^{\sigma}{\bf U}^{+}[i]}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}^{*}(Q)[i]
when

is a source (see
[
10
]
or
[

]
).

4.
Cyclic quivers and PBW bases

4.1.
Nilpotent representations of cyclic quivers

Let
k
be a field,
\Delta
=
\Delta(n)
be the cyclic quiver

\textstyle{n\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{n-2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{n-1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

with vertex set
I={\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}}=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and arrow set
H=\{i\rightarrow i+1|1\leqslant i\leqslant n\}
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}={\mathcal{K}}_{n}(k)
be the category of nilpotent representations of the path algebra
k\Delta
.

A multisegment is a formal finite sum

\pi=\sum_{i\in I,l\geqslant 1}\pi_{i,l}[i;l),

where
\pi_{i,l}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and there are only finitely many nonzero
\pi_{i,l}
. Let
\Pi=\Pi(n)
denote the set of all multisegments. Each multisegment
\pi=\sum_{i\in I,l\geqslant 1}\pi_{i,l}[i;l)\in\Pi
defines a representation in
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
:

M(\pi)=M_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n}}(\pi)=\bigoplus_{i\in I,l\geqslant 1}S_{i}[l]^{\oplus\pi_{i,l}},

where
S_{i}[l]
is the indecomposable
k\Delta
-module with top
S_{i}
and of length
l
.
This gives a bijection between
\Pi
and the set of isomorphim classes of representations in
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
and the bijection is independent of the field
k
.

4.2.
Aperiodicity

A multisegment
\pi=\sum_{i\in I,l\geqslant 1}\pi_{i,l}[i;l)
is called aperiodic if, for each
l\geqslant 1
, there is some
i\in I
such that
\pi_{i,l}=0
, otherwise,
\pi
is called periodic.
By
\Pi^{a}
we denote the set of aperiodic multisegments.
A representation
M
in
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
is called aperiodic (resp. periodic) if
M\cong M(\pi)
for some
\pi\in\Pi^{a}
(resp.
\pi\in\Pi\setminus\Pi^{a}
).

4.3.
Order of multisegements

For
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}I
, let

\Pi_{\nu}=\{\pi\in\Pi|\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M(\pi)=\nu\},\Pi^{a}_{\nu}=\Pi^{a}\cap\Pi_{\nu}.

Define a partial order
\leqslant_{G}
on
\Pi_{\nu}
.
For
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi_{\nu}
,
\pi^{\prime}\leqslant_{G}\pi
if and only if

\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{Hom}(M,M(\pi^{\prime}))\geqslant\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{Hom}(M,M(\pi))

for all modules (or equivalently, for all indecomposable modules)
M
in
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
.
By
[
5
]
, this ordering coincides with the geometric ordering:
\pi^{\prime}\leqslant\pi
if and only if the orbit of
M(\pi^{\prime})
in the representation space is contained in the closure of the orbit of
M(\pi)
.

4.4.
Generic extensions

For any two modules
M,N\in{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
, there is a unique (up to isomorphism) extension
L
of
M
by
N
with minimal
\mathrm{dim}\,\mathrm{End}\,L
. The module
L
is called the generic extension of
M
by
N
and we denote
L=M\diamond N
or
[L]=[M]\diamond[N]
.

4.5.
Bases of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}

A PBW basis of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
is constructed in
[
8
]
, where
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
is the composition algebra of the category
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}
.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

For each
\pi\in\Pi^{a}
, there is a distinguished monomial
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega_{\pi}}
satisfying

S_{i_{1}}^{\oplus a_{1}}\diamond S_{i_{2}}^{\oplus a_{2}}\diamond\dots\diamond S_{i_{s}}^{\oplus a_{s}}=M(\pi)

and

{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega_{\pi}}=\langle M(\pi)\rangle+\sum_{\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi_{\nu},\pi^{\prime}<_{G}\pi}\xi^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\omega_{\pi}}(v)\langle M(\pi^{\prime})\rangle,

where

.

Since

is a finite set for any

inductively by the following relations.
If

\displaystyle E_{\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{M(\pi)}\rangle+\sum_{\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi_{\nu}\setminus\Pi_{\nu}^{a},\pi^{\prime}<_{G}\pi}\eta_{\omega_{\pi}}^{\pi^{\prime}}(v)\langle{M(\pi^{\prime})}\rangle,

for some
\eta^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\omega_{\pi}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

The set
\{E_{\pi}|\pi\in\Pi^{a}\}
is a
{\mathcal{Z}}
-basis of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\Delta)
and is independent of the choice of distinguished monomials.

5.
Representations of affine quivers

5.1.
Representations of affine quivers

Let
Q=(I,H,s,t)
be an acyclic quiver of affine type.
Let
k
be a finite field and
kQ
be the path algebra.

Denote by
\mathrm{ind}\,{\mathcal{C}}
the set of isomorphism classes of all indecomposable objects in the category
{\mathcal{C}}
. According to
[
23
]
, the set
\mathrm{ind}\,\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
can be divided into

\mathrm{ind}\,\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ=\mathrm{ind}\,{\mathcal{P}}\sqcup\mathrm{ind}\,{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}}\sqcup\mathrm{ind}\,{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}}\sqcup\mathrm{ind}\,{\mathcal{I}},

where
{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{P}}(k)
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}}={\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}}(k)
,
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}}={\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}}(k)
,
{\mathcal{I}}={\mathcal{I}}(k)
denotes the full subcategory of
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
consisting of all preprojective, non-homogeneous regular, homogeneous regular, preinjective modules respectively.
Hence we have

{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)={\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{P}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{I}}),

where
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{P}})
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}})
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}})
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{I}})
are the Ringel-Hall algebras of corresponding subcategories and each of them is closed under multiplication in
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}(kQ)
.

The following lemma gives an easy method to check which subcategory an indecomposable module is in.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
28
]
)
.

Let
M
be an indecomposable
kQ
-module. Then

(a)

\langle\delta,\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M\rangle<0\Leftrightarrow M\in{\mathcal{P}}
;

(b)

\langle\delta,\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M\rangle>0\Leftrightarrow M\in{\mathcal{I}}
;

(c)

\langle\delta,\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M\rangle=0\Leftrightarrow M\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm nh}
or
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h}
;

(d)

M\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h}\Rightarrow\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M=m\delta
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

5.2.
Preprojective and preinjective components

A doubly infinite sequence

\underline{i}=(\dots,i_{-1},i_{0}|i_{1},i_{2},\dots)

in
I
is said to be reduced if for any integers
r\leqslant t
,
s_{i_{r}}s_{i_{r+1}}\dots s_{i_{t}}
is reduced in the Weyl group.

We say a finite sequence (
i_{m},\dots,i_{1}
) is an admissible sink (resp. source) sequence of
Q
if
i_{m}
is a sink (resp. source) of
Q
, and
i_{t}
is a sink (resp. source) of
\sigma_{i_{t+1}}\dots\sigma_{i_{m}}Q
for any
1\leqslant t<m
.
We say that
\underline{i}
is adapted to
Q
if for any
t\leqslant 0
, (
i_{0},i_{-1},\dots,i_{t}
) is an admissible sink sequence and for any
t>0
, (
i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{t}
) is an admissible source sequence. We say
\underline{i}
is admissible if
\underline{i}
is reduced and adapted to
Q
.

Such an admissible
\underline{i}
always exists for any acyclic quiver
Q
. For example, if we write
I=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
such that there are no arrows from
i
to
j
for
i>j
, then let
i_{t}=t
for
1\leqslant t\leqslant n
, and
i_{t+n}=i_{t}
. One can check that this
\underline{i}
is admissible.

For an admissible
\underline{i}
, set

\beta_{t}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &s_{i_{0}}s_{i_{-1}}\dots s_{i_{t+1}}(i_{t}),&\mathrm{if}\,t\leqslant 0,\\
&s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\dots s_{i_{t-1}}(i_{t}),&\mathrm{if}\,t>0.\end{aligned}\right.

in
{\mathbb{N}}I
. Here
\beta_{0}=i_{0},\beta_{1}=i_{1}
.
We order these
\beta_{t},t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
by

(\beta_{0}<\beta_{-1}<\beta_{-2}<\cdots)<(\cdots<\beta_{3}<\beta_{2}<\beta_{1}).

For each
t\leqslant 0
, there is an indecomposable preprojective module

M(\beta_{t})=\sigma_{i_{0}}^{-}\sigma_{i_{-1}}^{-}\dots\sigma_{i_{t+1}}^{-}(S_{i_{t}})

with dimension vector
\beta_{t}
. For each
t>0
, there is an indecomposable preinjective module

5.3.
Non-homogeneous tubes

For
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}}={\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}}(k)
, there are several extension-closed subcategories
J_{1},J_{2},\dots,J_{s}(s\leqslant 3)
of
\mbox{\rm mod}\,kQ
known as non-homogeneous tubes such that
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}}
is the direct product of them. Thus

{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}})={\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(J_{1})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(J_{2})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})}\cdots\otimes_{{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(J_{s})

and

if

and
i\neq j
.

It is well-known that each

is equivalent to the category
{\mathcal{K}}_{r}
of nilpotent representations of the cyclic quiver with
r
vertices for some
r=r(d)
, and we denote
r=\mathrm{rk}\,J_{d}
.
Note that the equivalence is generally not unique when considering the rotation on
{\mathbb{Z}}/r{\mathbb{Z}}
. We choose an equivalence for each
J_{d}

and denote it by

\epsilon_{d}:{\mathcal{K}}_{r(d)}\longrightarrow J_{d}.

Thus
\epsilon_{d}
induces an algebra isomorphism, which is denoted again by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{d}:{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{K}}_{r(d)})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(J_{d})

\displaystyle\langle M\rangle

\displaystyle\longmapsto\langle\epsilon_{d}(M)\rangle.

Using the notations in Section
4
, there is a bijection from
\Pi(r(1))\times\Pi(r(2))\times\dots\times\Pi(r(s))
to the set of isomorphism classes of objects in
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{nh}}
by mapping
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\cdots,\pi_{s})
to
[\oplus_{1\leqslant d\leqslant s}M_{J_{d}}(\pi_{d})]
, where
M_{J_{d}}(\pi_{d})=\epsilon_{d}(M_{{\mathcal{K}}_{r}(d)}(\pi_{d}))
.

5.4.
Homogeneous tubes

For
{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}}={\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}}(k)
, there are extension closed subcategories known as homogeneous tubes, indexed by a subset
Z_{k}
of the projective line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(k)
.

A homogeneous regular module
M\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}}
can be decomposed uniquely as

M=M_{k}(\underline{\lambda},\underline{z})=M_{k}(\lambda^{1},z_{1})\oplus M_{k}(\lambda^{2},z_{2})\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{k}(\lambda^{t},z_{t}),

where
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2},\dots,\lambda^{t})
is a
t
-tuple of partitions and
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{t}\in Z_{k}
are distinct.
The modules of the form
M_{k}((1),z)
will be called regular simple modules. We set
\deg z=d
if
\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M_{k}((1),z)=d\delta
. When
k
is algebraic closed,
\deg z=1
for all
z\in Z_{k}
.

Now let
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The subalgebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}})
of
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(Q)
is isomorphic to

{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(Z_{k}):=\bigotimes_{z\in Z_{k}}{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(z),

where
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}(z)
is the classical Hall algebra of homogeneous tube
z
.
Since there are no non-trivial extensions and homomorphisms between two different homogeneous tubes, the algebra
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}({\mathcal{R}}_{\rm{h}})
is commutative.

5.5.

We define the index set
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}

6.1.
Generators in

Let

{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h}={\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h}(k)
be the homogeneous regular subcategory of

.
When necessary, we require that

For

6.2.
The character of permutation modules arising from
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}({\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h})

We introduce some notions about representation theory of the symmetric groups first and we refer to
[
27
]
for more details.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
be the symmetric group of
m
.
For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\lambda_{s})
of

\lambda
-tabloid is by definition an ordered sequence
(W_{1},W_{2},\dots,W_{s})
of subsets

This definition is equivalent to the one in
[
27
]
.
Then the permutation module
M^{\lambda}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{|\lambda|}
is the
\mathbb{C}
-linear space spanned by all
\lambda
-tabloids.
The natural action of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
on
\{1,2,\dots,m\}
induces the action of

on

.

Let
S^{\lambda}
be the Specht module introduced in Section 2.3 in
[
27
]
. It is known that

where

Let

at
g_{\mu}\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}

of cycle type
\mu
.
Then

is the irreducible character table of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
.
Let

be the character of the permutation module

.
It follows that

.

Let
Z_{K}
be the set of all homogeneous tubes of
\mbox{\rm mod}\,KQ
for any field
K
. Let
k
be a finite field.
For a partition
\mu=(\mu_{1}\geqslant\mu_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\mu_{l})

for all

.

For partitions
\lambda,\mu=(\mu_{1}\geqslant\mu_{2}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\mu_{l})

Fix

Let

We write

S_{\lambda}=\sum_{[M]\atop M\in{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm h},\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,M=m\delta}a^{[M]}_{\lambda}u_{[M]}.

Lemma 6.2

\displaystyle v^{3|\delta|}S_{(3)}

Lusztig’s geometric construction and the canonical basis

We give a review of Lusztig’s geometric construction of the canonical basis. We refer to

]

10.1.
Representation spaces and flag varieties

Let
V=\oplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
be an
I
-graded vector space over an algebraic closed field
k
.
Denote
\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,V=\sum_{i\in I}\mathrm{dim}\,V_{i}i\in{\mathbb{N}}I
.
Similarly to Section
7.3
, we define

{\mathbb{E}}_{V}=\oplus_{h\in H}\mathrm{Hom}(V_{s(h)},V_{t(h)}),\mathrm{GL}_{V}=\oplus_{i\in I}\mathrm{GL}(V_{i}).

The
\mathrm{GL}_{V}
-action on
{\mathbb{E}}_{V}
is also defined by

g.x=((g.x)_{h})_{h\in H},(g.x)_{h}=g_{t(h)}x_{h}g^{-1}_{s(h)}

for any
g=(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathrm{GL}_{V},x=(x_{h})_{h\in H}\in{\mathbb{E}}_{V}
.

Given any
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}I
, let
{\mathcal{S}}_{\nu}
be the set consisting of all pairs of sequences defined in Section
3.5
:

\omega=(\underline{i},\underline{a})=((i_{1},\dots,i_{t}),(a_{1},\dots,a_{t}))

such that
\sum_{m}a_{m}i_{m}=\nu
.

Fix an
I
-graded vector space
V
such that
\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,V=\nu
. For any
\omega\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\nu}
, we say that a flag of subspaces

V^{\bullet}=(V=V^{0}\supseteq V^{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq V^{t}=0)

is of type
\omega
if
\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,V^{m-1}/V^{m}=a_{m}i_{m}
for all
m=1,2,\cdots,t
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}
be the flag variety consisting of all flags of type
\omega
. Then
\mathrm{GL}_{V}
acts on
{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}
naturally.

Given
x\in{\mathbb{E}}_{V},V^{\bullet}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}
, we say that
V^{\bullet}
is
x
-stable if
x_{h}(V^{m}_{s(h)})\subseteq V^{m}_{t(h)}
for any
h\in H
and
m=1,2,\cdots,t
.
We define
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}
to be the variety consisting of all pairs
(x,V^{\bullet})\in{\mathbb{E}}_{V}\times{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}
such that
V^{\bullet}
is
x
-stable. So
\mathrm{GL}_{V}
acts on
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}
naturally.

10.2.
Perverse sheaves

Fix a prime
l
that is invertible in
k
.
Let
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}
be the algebraic closure of the field of
l
-adic numbers.
Given any algebraic variety
X
over
k
,
denote by
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
the bounded derived category of complexes of
l
-adic sheaves on
X
.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}(X)
be the full subcategory of
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
consisting of all perverse sheaves on
X
.

Let
G
be a connected algebraic group. Assume that
G
acts on
X
algebraically.
By
{\mathcal{D}}_{G}(X)
we denote the full subcategory of
{\mathcal{D}}(X)
consisting of all
G
-equivariant complexes over
X
.
By
{\mathcal{M}}_{G}(X)
we denote the full subcategory of
{\mathcal{M}}(X)
consisting of all
G
-equivariant perverse sheaves over
X
.

Let
\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}}
be the constant sheaf on
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}
.
Let
\pi_{\omega}:\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}\rightarrow{\mathbb{E}}_{V}
be the first projection
(x,V^{\bullet})\mapsto x
.
By the decomposition theorem of Beilinson, Bernstein and Deligne (
[
4
]
), the Lusztig sheaf

L_{\omega}:=(\pi_{\omega})_{!}\mathbf{1}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}}[d_{\omega}](\frac{d_{\omega}}{2})

is a
\mathrm{GL}_{V}
-equivariant semisimple complex on
{\mathbb{E}}_{V}
, where
[d]
is the shift by
d
of complexes and
(\frac{d}{2})
is the Tate twist,
d_{\omega}=\mathrm{dim}\,\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\omega}
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{V}
be the subcategory of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{GL}_{V}}({\mathbb{E}}_{V})
consisting of direct sums of simple perverse sheaves, which are direct summands of
L_{\omega}[r](\frac{r}{2})
for some
\omega\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,V}
and
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{V}
be the full subcategory of
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathrm{GL}_{V}}({\mathbb{E}}_{V})
whose objects are the complexes that are isomorphic to finite direct sums of complexes of the form
L[d](\frac{d}{2})
for various
L\in{\mathcal{P}}_{V}
and
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let
K_{\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,V}=K_{V}=K({\mathcal{Q}}_{V})
be the Grothendieck group of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{V}
. Define

(-v)[L]=[L[1](\frac{1}{2})],(-v^{-1})[L]=[L[-1](-\frac{1}{2})].

Then
K_{V}
is a free
{\mathcal{Z}}
-module, where
{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathbb{Z}}[v,v^{-1}]
. Define

K({\mathcal{Q}})=\bigoplus_{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}I}K_{\nu}.

10.3.
Lusztig’s induction functors

Let
W\subset V
be an
I
-graded subspace. Let
T=V/W
and
p:V\rightarrow T
be the natural projection.

Given any
x\in{\mathbb{E}}_{V}
,
W
is called
x
-stable if
x_{h}(W_{s(h)})\subseteq W_{t(h)}
for all
h\in H
.

If
W
is
x
-stable, it induces two elements
x_{W}
and
x_{T}
in
{\mathbb{E}}_{W}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{T}
respectively such that
(x_{W})_{h}
is the restriction of
x_{h}
to
W
and
p_{t(h)}x_{h}=(x_{T})_{h}p_{s(h)}
for all
h\in H
.

We consider the following diagram

{\mathbb{E}}_{T}\times{\mathbb{E}}_{W}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q_{1}}}{{\longleftarrow}}{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle q_{3}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{E}}_{V},

where
{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime\prime}=\{(x,V^{\prime})|V^{\prime}
is
x
-stable,
\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,V^{\prime}=\mathrm{\underline{dim}}\,W\}
;
{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}
is the variety consisting of all quadruples
(x,V^{\prime},r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime})
such that
(x,V^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime\prime}
,
r^{\prime}:V/V^{\prime}\rightarrow T
and
r^{\prime\prime}:V^{\prime}\rightarrow W
are graded linear isomorphisms;
q_{2},q_{3}
are natural projections and
q_{1}(x,V^{\prime},r^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime})=(y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime})
such that
y^{\prime}_{h}=r^{\prime}_{t(h)}(x_{V/V^{\prime}})_{h}(r^{\prime})^{-1}_{s(h)}
and
y^{\prime\prime}_{h}=r^{\prime\prime}_{t(h)}(x_{V^{\prime}})_{h}(r^{\prime\prime})^{-1}_{s(h)}
for all
h\in H
.

Given any
L^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{T},L^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{W}
, we set

L^{\prime}\circ L^{\prime\prime}=(q_{3})_{!}(q_{2})_{\flat}q_{1}^{*}(L^{\prime}\boxtimes L^{\prime\prime})[d_{1}-d_{2}](\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{2}),

where
q_{2}^{*}
is an equivalence and
(q_{2})_{\flat}
is the inverse of
q_{2}^{*}
,
d_{1}
is the dimension of the fibres of
q_{1}
and
d_{2}
is the dimension of the fibres of
q_{2}
.

The operation
\circ
induces a multiplication
\circ
on
K({\mathcal{Q}})
such that
[L^{\prime}]\circ[L^{\prime\prime}]=[L^{\prime}\circ L^{\prime\prime}]
. This makes
K({\mathcal{Q}})
a
{\mathcal{Z}}
-algebra.

Lusztig shows that
K({\mathcal{Q}})
is isomorphic to
{\bf f}_{\mathcal{Z}}
by mapping
L_{\omega}
to
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega}
(Chapter 13,
[
19
]
).

So the image of equation (

10.5.
Trace map

For a perverse sheaf
L
in
{\mathcal{P}}_{V}
, the specialization
L\mapsto\Phi_{q}([L])
is the trace map introduced in
[
12
]
.
Also in
[
18
]
, Lusztig gives a way to compute
\Phi_{q}([L])\in{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}
as follows.

Let the ground field
k=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
. An
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear isomorphism
\mathfrak{f}:V\rightarrow V
on a
k
-vector space
V
is called a Frobenius map if it satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

\mathfrak{f}(yv)=y^{q}\mathfrak{f}(v)
,
v\in V
,
y\in k
.

(2)

For any
v\in V
,
\mathfrak{f}^{t}(v)=v
for some
t\geqslant 1
.

Given a Frobenius map
\mathfrak{f}:V\rightarrow V
,
V^{\mathfrak{f}}=\{v\in V|\mathfrak{f}(v)=v\}
is naturally a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space.

Now let
V=\oplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
be an
I
-graded
k
-vector space and
\mathfrak{f}:V\rightarrow V
be a Frobenius map such that
\mathfrak{f}(V_{i})=V_{i}
.
Then there is a naturally induced Frobenius map
\mathfrak{f}:{\mathbb{E}}_{V}\rightarrow{\mathbb{E}}_{V}
such that
\mathfrak{f}(x)_{h}(\mathfrak{f}(v))=\mathfrak{f}(x_{h}(v))
for any
x\in{\mathbb{E}}_{V},h\in H
and
v\in V_{s(h)}
.

For a perverse sheaf
L
in
{\mathcal{P}}_{V}
,
\mathfrak{f}:{\mathbb{E}}_{V}\rightarrow{\mathbb{E}}_{V}
induces a canonical isomorphism
\mathfrak{f}^{*}L\cong L
.
This induces a linear map
H^{j}(L)|_{x}=H^{j}(L)|_{\mathfrak{f}(x)}=H^{j}(\mathfrak{f}^{*}L)|_{x}\cong H^{j}(L)|_{x}
for
x\in{\mathbb{E}}_{V}^{\mathfrak{f}}
.
Taking the trace of this linear map times
(-1)^{j}
and summing over
j
, we obtain an element
\chi_{L,q}(x)\in\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}
.
We thus have a function
\chi_{L,q}:{\mathbb{E}}_{V}^{\mathfrak{f}}\rightarrow\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}
.

Note that for any
x\in{\mathbb{E}}_{V}^{\mathfrak{f}}
,
(V^{\mathfrak{f}},x)
is a representation of
Q
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
\chi_{L,q}
can be regarded as an element in
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}
by identifying the characteristic function
\chi_{[M]}
of the orbit of
M
with
\langle M\rangle
in
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}_{q}
. Under the field isomorphism
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
, Lusztig showed that
\chi_{L,q}=\Phi_{q}([L])
.

11.
Coincidence of the canonical basis and bar-invariant basis

11.1.

In this section, we always identify
K({\mathcal{Q}})
,
{\bf f}_{\mathcal{Z}}
,
{\bf U}^{+}_{\mathcal{Z}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Lemma 11.1
.

For
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we have

(8)

B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}a^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})

with
a^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.

Proof.

By Section
10.5
, when specialized to
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
,
\Phi_{q}(B({\bf c},t_{\lambda}))
is always a
{\mathbb{Q}}(v_{q})
-sum of some
N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})
with
Y({\bf c}^{\prime},|\lambda^{\prime}|)\cap\overline{X({\bf c},|\lambda|)}\neq\emptyset
. Therefore, by Proposition
7.3
, we have
({\bf c}^{\prime},|\lambda^{\prime}|)\preceq({\bf c},|\lambda|)
. That is, for any
q
, the coefficient of
N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})
with
({\bf c}^{\prime},|\lambda^{\prime}|)\npreceq({\bf c},|\lambda|)
is always zero.
So as an element in
{\mathcal{H}}^{0}_{\mathcal{Z}}
,
B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
is a
{\mathcal{Z}}
-sum of some
N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})
with
({\bf c}^{\prime},|\lambda^{\prime}|)\prec({\bf c},|\lambda|)
.

Therefore by equation (
7
),

{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}-\sum_{\mu\geqslant\lambda}K_{\lambda\mu}B({\bf c},t_{\mu})\in{\mathcal{Z}}-{\rm span}\{N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})|({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}},({\bf c}^{\prime},|\lambda^{\prime}|)\prec({\bf c},|\lambda|)\}.

Recall that

{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}=\sum_{\mu\geqslant\lambda}K_{\lambda\mu}N({\bf c},t_{\mu})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},|\lambda^{\prime}|)\prec({\bf c},|\lambda|)}\phi_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}).

So for all
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we have

B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}a^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

with
a^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.

By the almost orthogonality of
\{B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
and
\{N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}\}
(see Section
8.5
), we have
a^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.

∎

Corollary 11.2
.

For
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we have

E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}{\tilde{a}}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)B({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

with
{\tilde{a}}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.

Proof.

By doing the same operation in Definition
9.12
on equations (
8
) for various
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we can inductively get

\tilde{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}{\tilde{a}}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)B({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})

for some
{\tilde{a}}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
, such that
\{\tilde{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
is a
Q(v)
-basis of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
, and

(9)

\tilde{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}f_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

for some
f_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.

Thus

E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})-\tilde{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in\mathbf{P}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{*}.

By Lemma
9.15
,

E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=\tilde{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda}),

which proves the corollary.
∎

Now we give the main theorem of this paper, showing that the bar-invariant basis
\{C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
is the canonical basis
{\bf B}=\{B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
of
{\bf f}
, and the bijection is clear.

Theorem 11.3
.

For all
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we have
C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

By Section
9.8
and Corollary
11.2
, we already have

C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\tilde{b}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda}^{\prime})}(v)B({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

for some
\tilde{b}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.
Applying bar-involution to both sides, all
\tilde{b}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}=0
and
B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
since all
B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
and
C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
are bar-invariant.
∎

Note that we not only prove that

\{C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}=\{B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}

as sets, but also show that

C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})

for any
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
.

By the almost orthogonality of
\{B({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
, the condition (2) of Theorem
9.20
follows.

Recall that in Lemma
9.13
, for
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we have

E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}_{\nu}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}^{a}_{\nu}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}b_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

with
b_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.
By Corollary
11.2
,
f_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}=b_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}
, so
b_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.
By the almost orthogonality of
\{N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}\}
, the PBW basis
\{E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})|({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\}
is almost orthogonal.
This completes the proof of Proposition
9.19
.

12.
Another algorithm to compute the canonical basis

In this section, we will give an easier algebraic algorithm to compute
C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
, which relies on the proof of Theorem
9.20
.

Back to the construction of the monomial basis, we have for
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
,

{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}=N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\phi_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

For a polynomial
\phi(v)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\phi_{i}v^{i}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
, let
{{}^{+}\phi}(v)=\phi_{0}+\sum_{i>0}\phi_{i}(v^{i}+v^{-i})
, then
\overline{{}^{+}\phi}={{}^{+}\phi}
and
\phi-{{}^{+}\phi}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.

Now fix
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}_{\nu}
. For a maximal
({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in\{({\bf c}^{\prime\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}_{\nu}|({\bf c}^{\prime\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}-{{}^{+}\phi}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v){\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+(\phi_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}-{{}^{+}\phi}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})})(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})

\displaystyle+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda}),({\bf c}^{\prime\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}})\neq({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\varphi_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}),

where the left side is bar-invariant and the coefficient of
N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})
in the right side is in
v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.

Whenever the right side has a term as the form
\tilde{\varphi}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})
for
({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
with
({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
such that
\tilde{\varphi}(v)\not\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
, let
({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})
be maximal with this property, we minus
{}^{+}\tilde{\varphi}(v){\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}
for both sides.
Continue this process until all coefficients of
N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a},({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
in the right side are in
v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.

In this way, we can get

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}r_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v){\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}s_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\tilde{s}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})

where all
r_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
are bar-invariant and all
s_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}
are in
v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
, while
\tilde{s}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
is not necessarily in
v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
for now (although later we can prove that
\tilde{s}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
by Proposition
12.1
and almost-orthogonality).

Denote this element by

G({\bf c},t_{\lambda})={\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}r_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v){\mathfrak{m}}^{\omega({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}.

Proposition 12.1
.

For all
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, we have
G({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

Since
s_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
, by doing the same operation in Definition
9.12
on
G({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
, we can inductively get

\hat{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=G({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\gamma_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)G({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})

with
\gamma_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
, such that
\tilde{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
is in
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
for any
({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}
, and

\hat{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=N({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}\setminus{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\psi_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}(v)N({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})

with
\psi_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Hence,
\hat{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})-E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})\in\mathbf{P}\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{*}
. By Lemma
9.15
, we have
\hat{E}({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
.
Recall that

C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=E({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}g^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}(v)E({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

with
g^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
. Then

C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=G({\bf c},t_{\lambda})+\sum_{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\in{\mathcal{G}}^{a}\atop({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})\prec({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\kappa^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}(v)G({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}}),

with
\kappa^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda^{\prime}})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}\in v^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}[v^{-1}]
.
Applying bar-involution to both sides, all
\kappa^{({\bf c}^{\prime},t_{\lambda}^{\prime})}_{({\bf c},t_{\lambda})}=0
and
G({\bf c},t_{\lambda})=C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
, since all
G({\bf c},t_{\lambda}),C({\bf c},t_{\lambda})
are bar-invariant.
∎

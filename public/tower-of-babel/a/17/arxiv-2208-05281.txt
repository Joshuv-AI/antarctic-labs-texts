Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the consensus models on the sphere with control signals, where both the first and second order systems are considered. We provide the existence of the optimal control-trajectory pair and derive the first order optimality condition taking the form of the Pontryagin Minimum Principle. Numeric simulations are also presented to show that the obtained optimal control can help to accelerate the process of reaching a consensus.

1.
Introduction

Large systems of interacting particles arise in the study of collective behaviours of biological and physical systems on manifolds, such as nematic patterns on a sphere
[
11
]
, application to launching unmanned spacecrafts
[
5
]
, and crystals on a cylinder
[
6
]
. It is receiving lots of attention due to the appearance of a consensus emergence of these particle systems.
This paper addresses centralized control problems for one of the well known consensus models on the sphere, i.e. the swarm sphere model
[
10
]
, whose first order form is given by:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{i}=\Omega_{i}x_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(x_{k}-\frac{\langle x_{i},x_{k}\rangle}{\|x_{i}\|^{2}}x_{i}\right),\\
x_{i}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~i\in[N]:=\{1,2,\cdots,N\},\end{cases}

where
\Omega_{i}\in\mathrm{Skew}(d):=\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:A^{\top}=-A\}
is the natural frequency of the
i
-th particle,
\kappa
is the coupling strength,
x_{i}^{0}
is the initial position of the
i
-th particle,
N
is the number of particles, and
\|\cdot\|
represents the standard Euclidean norm. Adding the inertia (mass) term to system (
1.1
) as in
[
8
]
we can obtain the second order model satisfying:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{i}=v_{i},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}v_{i}=-\frac{\gamma}{m}v_{i}-\frac{\|v_{i}\|^{2}}{\|x_{i}\|^{2}}x_{i}+\frac{1}{m}\Omega_{i}x_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{mN}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(x_{k}-\frac{\langle x_{i},x_{k}\rangle}{\|x_{i}\|^{2}}x_{i}\right),\\
\displaystyle x_{i}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\hskip 9.24994ptv_{i}(0)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{i}\Big|_{t=0+}=v_{i}^{0}\in T_{x_{i}^{0}}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~i\in[N],\end{cases}

where
m
is the mass,
\gamma
is the friction coefficients, and
v_{i}^{0}
is the initial velocity of the
i
-th particle. Actually, the systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can be also seen as generalized Kuramoto models on the sphere.
According to
[
8
]
, if the identical natural frequencies satisfies
\Omega_{i}\equiv O_{d-1}
for all
i\in[N]
, then the emergent dynamics for both systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with generic initial can be observed, namely it satisfies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|x_{i}(t)-x_{j}(t)\|=0\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~i,j\in[N].

In this context the consensus is understood as a travelling formulation in which every particle has the same position. However this behaviour strongly depends on the initial configuration of the particle system. In the present work we are interested in the design of centralized control signals enforcing consensus emergence in systems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) in order to generate an external intervention able to steer the system towards a desired configuration. Very related to this work,
[
1
,
4
,
3
]
study the problem of consensus control for Cucker–Smale type models.
Meanwhile, according to
[
9
]
, the Kuramoto model (position synchronization model) can be derived from the Cucker–Smale model (velocity alignment model). So in this work we are aiming to extend those results on velocity alignment as in
[
1
]
to our position synchronization models (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

2.
Swarm sphere models with controls

In this section we consider both the first order and second order swarm sphere models with control signals.

2.1.
Second order model

We consider a set of
N
particles with state
(x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t))\in T\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
moving on a sphere via system (
1.2
) with
\Omega_{i}\equiv O_{d-1}
for all
i\in[N]
.
We are interested in the study of consensus emergence, i.e. the convergence towards a configuration in which
x_{i}=\bar{x}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j},~\forall\,i\in[N]\,.

In particular we are concerned with inducing the consensus through the synthesis of an external forcing term
\mathbf{u}(t):=(u_{1}(t),\dots,u_{N}(t))^{\top}
in the form of

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}}{dt}=v_{i},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{i}}{dt}=-\frac{\|v_{i}\|^{2}}{\|x_{i}\|^{2}}x_{i}-\frac{\gamma}{m}v_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{mN}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(x_{j}-\frac{\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle}{\|x_{i}\|^{2}}x_{i}\right)+\left(u_{i}-\frac{\langle u_{i},x_{i}\rangle x_{i}}{\|x_{i}\|^{2}}\right)\\
\displaystyle x_{i}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\hskip 9.24994ptv_{i}(0)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{i}\Big|_{t=0+}=v_{i}^{0}\in T_{x_{i}^{0}}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~i\in[N],\end{cases}

where the control signals
u_{i}\in L^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})=:\mathcal{U}
. Formally, for
T>0
and given a set of admissible control signals
\textbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}^{N}
for the entire population, it holds that

\|u_{i}\|_{2}\leq\max_{i\in[N]}\{\|u_{i}\|_{2}\}=:M
for all
i\in[N]
. Our goal is then to seek a solution to the minimization problem

(2.4)

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0)):=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}-\bar{x}\|^{2}dt+\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}\|^{2}dt\,,

with some regularization parameter
\lambda>0
. Here we have used the notation
\mathbf{x}(t):=(x_{1}(t),\dots,x_{N}(t))^{\top}
and
\mathbf{v}(t):=(v_{1}(t),\dots,v_{N}(t))^{\top}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any given
T>0
and
\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}
, assume the initial data
(\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))
and parameters
m,\gamma,\kappa,T
satisfy

\frac{m}{\gamma}\left(\mathcal{V}(0)+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2MT^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\exp\left(\frac{\gamma T}{m}\right)-1\right)<1\,,

then there exists a pathwise unique global solution
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
to the Kuramoto system (
2.3
) up to time
T
. Moreover for all
i\in[N]
and
t\in[0,T]
it holds that

(2.5)

\|x_{i}(t)\|=1,\hskip 9.24994pt\langle x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t)\rangle=0

and

(2.6)

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{V}(t)\leq\frac{\exp\left(\frac{\gamma T}{m}\right)\left(\mathcal{V}(0)+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2MT^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{1-\frac{m}{\gamma}\left(\mathcal{V}(0)+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2MT^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\exp\left(\frac{\gamma T}{m}\right)-1\right)}=:C_{V}\,,

where
\mathcal{V}(t)=\max\limits_{i=1,\dots,N}\|v_{i}(t)\|
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a map on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with bounded derivatives of all orders such that
\phi(x)=\frac{x}{\|x\|^{2}}
for all
x
with
\|x\|\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Then we consider the following regularized system

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=v_{i},\\
\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\phi(x_{i})\|v_{i}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{m}v_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{m}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(x_{j}-\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle\phi(x_{i}))+u_{i}-\langle u_{i},x_{i}\rangle\phi(x_{i})\\
\displaystyle x_{i}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\hskip 9.24994ptv_{i}(0)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{i}\Big|_{t=0+}=v_{i}^{0}\in T_{x_{i}^{0}}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~i\in[N],\end{cases}

It is obvious that the coefficients are local Lipschitz, so one has local existence and uniqueness up to some time
\tau\in[0,T]
.

Next we prove the global existence up to time
T
. Actually, as long as
\|x_{i}\|\geq\frac{1}{2}
one has

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\langle x_{i},v_{i}\rangle=\|v_{i}\|^{2}+\left\langle x_{i},\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right\rangle=\|v_{i}\|^{2}-\|v_{i}\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{m}\langle x_{i},v_{i}\rangle=-\frac{\gamma}{m}\langle x_{i},v_{i}\rangle\,,

which implies
\langle x_{i}(t),v_{i}(t)\rangle=\langle x_{i}(0),v_{i}(0)\rangle e^{-\frac{\gamma}{m}t}=0
for all
t\in[0,T]
since
\langle x_{i}(0),v_{i}(0)\rangle=0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|x_{i}\|^{2}=2\langle x_{i},v_{i}\rangle=0\,,

which following the initial condition
\|x_{i}(0)\|=1
leads to
\|x_{i}(t)\|=1
for all
t\in[0,T]
. In addition, notice that

\displaystyle\|v_{i}(t)\|\leq\|v_{i}(0)\|+\int_{0}^{t}(\|v_{i}(s)\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{m}\|v_{i}(s)\|)ds+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{i}(s)\|ds\,.

Using Gronwall’s lemma one has

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|v_{i}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\exp(\frac{\gamma T}{m})(\|v_{i}(0)\|+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{i}(s)\|ds)}{1-\frac{m}{\gamma}(\|v_{i}(0)\|+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{i}(s)\|ds)(\exp(\frac{\gamma T}{m})-1)}\leq\frac{\exp(\frac{\gamma T}{m})(\|v_{i}(0)\|+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2MT^{\frac{1}{2}})}{1-\frac{m}{\gamma}(\|v_{i}(0)\|+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2MT^{\frac{1}{2}})(\exp(\frac{\gamma T}{m})-1)}\,.

This means that if initially
\frac{m}{\gamma}(\|v_{i}(0)\|+\frac{2\kappa T}{m}+2MT^{\frac{1}{2}})(\exp(\frac{\gamma T}{m})-1)<1
, then we have global existence and pathwise uniqueness for (
2.7
) up to time
T
.

Now the solution to (
2.7
) for the given
\phi
is a solution to (
2.3
) since
\|x_{i}\|=1
for all
i=1,\dots,N
and
t\in[0,T]
, which provides global existence to (
2.3
). If we consider two solutions to (
2.3
) for the same initial data, then they satisfies
\|x_{i}\|=1
. So they are also solutions to the regularized system (
2.7
), for which pathwise uniqueness holds. Hence they are equal, which completes the proof.
∎

From (
2.5
), now we can assume
\|x_{i}(t)\|=1
for all
t\geq 0
and
i\in[N]
if
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
satisfy (
2.3
), and one can prove the existence of the optimal control-trajectory pair:

Theorem 2.2
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
2.1
, there exists some control
u_{i}^{*}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
i\in[N]
and the corresponding
\{(x_{i}^{*},v_{i}^{*})\}_{i=1}^{N}
trajectories solving the optimal control problem (
2.3
)-(
2.4
).

Proof.

For any given
u_{i}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
i\in[N]
, by Theorem
2.1
there exists a corresponding solution
\{(x_{i}^{u},v_{i}^{u})\}_{i=1}^{N}
to (
2.3
). Note that
u_{i}=0\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, so that

\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{0};\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}^{0}-\bar{x}^{0}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq 4T

because
\|x_{i}^{0}\|=1
, where
\{(x_{i}^{0},v_{i}^{0})\}_{i=1}^{N}
is the solution to (
2.3
) with given
u_{i}=0
. Since
\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))\geq 0
for all
\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}
, it holds that

0\leq\min\limits_{\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))\leq\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{0};\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))\leq 4T\,.

By the definition of the infimum, we know that there exists a sequence of controls
(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{U}^{N}
with corresponding
(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{u^{n}},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}^{u^{n}})
solving (
2.3
), such that

0\leq\min\limits_{\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))\leq\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{n}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))\leq\min\limits_{\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))+\frac{1}{n}\,,

and so

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{n}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))=\min\limits_{\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))\,.

Notice that

\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{n}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{n}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))<\infty\,,

and by Banach-Alaoglu theorem, for all
i
there exists a subsequence
(u_{i}^{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
u_{i}^{*}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(2.8)

u_{i}^{k}:=u_{i}^{n_{k}}\underset{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rightharpoonup}u_{i}^{*}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ in }L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})\,.

For the corresponding solutions
(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}:=(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{u^{n_{k}}},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}^{u^{n_{k}}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have from Theorem
2.1
that

\displaystyle\max\limits_{i=1,\dots,N}\|\dot{v_{i}}^{k}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\max\limits_{i=1,\dots,N}\|v_{i}^{k}(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{m}\max\limits_{i=1,\dots,N}\|v_{i}^{k}(t)\|+\frac{2\kappa}{m}+2\max\limits_{i=1,\dots,N}\|u_{i}^{k}(t)\|\leq C_{V}^{2}+\frac{C_{V}\gamma}{m}+\frac{2\kappa}{m}+2M\,.

This combining with (
2.6
) implies the equi-boundedness and equi-absolute continuity of
v_{i}^{k}(t)
uniformly with respect to
k
, for all
i=1,\dots,N
. This also yields the equi-Lispchitzanity of
x_{i}^{k}(t)
. By Ascoli-Arzela theorem there exits a subsequence, again renamed
(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{k},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and an absolutely continuous trajectory
(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{*},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}^{*})
in
[0,T]
such that for
k\to\infty
:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x_{i}^{k}\rightarrow x_{i}^{*},\mbox{ in }[0,T],\mbox{ for all }i=1,\cdots,N,\\
v_{i}^{k}\rightarrow v_{i}^{*},\mbox{ in }[0,T],\mbox{ for all }i=1,\cdots,N,\\
\dot{x}_{i}^{k}\rightarrow\dot{x}_{i}^{*},\mbox{ in }[0,T],\mbox{ for all }i=1,\cdots,N,\\
\dot{v}_{i}^{k}\rightharpoonup\dot{v}_{i}^{*},\mbox{ in }L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mbox{ for all }i=1,\cdots,N\,.\end{cases}

Thus it is easy to see that

(2.10)

\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}^{*}}{\mathrm{d}t}=v_{i}^{*}\mbox{ and }\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}^{*}-\bar{x}^{*}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\,.

Moreover, one notice that for all
\psi\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\psi\dot{v}_{i}^{k}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\frac{\|v_{i}^{k}\|^{2}}{\|x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}}\langle x_{i}^{k},\psi\rangle-\frac{\gamma}{m}\langle v_{i}^{k},\psi\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{m}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\langle x_{j}^{k},\psi\rangle-\frac{\langle x_{i}^{k},x_{j}^{k}\rangle}{\|x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}}\langle x_{i}^{k},\psi\rangle\right)+\langle u_{i}^{k},\psi\rangle-\frac{\langle u_{i}^{k},x_{i}^{k}\rangle\langle x_{i}^{k},\psi\rangle}{\|x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t

Let
k\to\infty
, and by using (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\psi\dot{v}_{i}^{*}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\left(-\frac{\|v_{i}^{*}\|^{2}}{\|x_{i}^{*}\|^{2}}\langle x_{i}^{*},\psi\rangle-\frac{\gamma}{m}\langle v_{i}^{*},\psi\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{m}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\langle x_{j}^{*},\psi\rangle-\frac{\langle x_{i}^{*},x_{j}^{*}\rangle}{|x_{i}^{*}|^{2}}\langle x_{i}^{*},\psi\rangle\right)+\langle u_{i}^{*},\psi\rangle-\frac{\langle u_{i}^{*},x_{i}^{*}\rangle\langle x_{i}^{*},\psi\rangle}{\|x_{i}^{*}\|^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\,,

which leads to

(2.11)

\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{i}^{*}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\frac{\|v_{i}^{*}\|^{2}}{\|x_{i}^{*}\|^{2}}x_{i}^{*}-\frac{\gamma}{m}v_{i}^{*}+\frac{\kappa}{m}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(x_{j}^{*}-\frac{\langle x_{i}^{*},x_{j}^{*}\rangle}{\|x_{i}^{*}\|^{2}}x_{i}^{*}\right)+u_{i}^{*}-\frac{\langle u_{i}^{*},x_{i}^{*}\rangle x_{i}^{*}}{\|x_{i}^{*}\|^{2}}\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{a.e.}\,.

Since
(u_{i}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
converges weakly to
u_{i}^{*}
for all
i\in[N]
, we also have

\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{*}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leq\lim\inf\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{k}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t

by the weak lower-semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
-norm. This implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{*}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}^{*}-\bar{x}^{*}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{*}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim\inf\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|u_{j}^{k}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\inf\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{k}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}^{n}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))=\min\limits_{\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}}\mathcal{J}(\mathbf{u}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0),\mathbf{v}(0))\,.

This together with (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) implies the limit
(\textbf{x}^{*},\textbf{v}^{*},\textbf{u}^{*})
is a solution to the optimal control problem (
2.3
)-(
2.4
).
∎

While the existence of
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{v}^{*},\mathbf{u}^{*})
to the optimal control problem (
2.3
)-(
2.4
) has been obtained in Theorem
2.2
, the Pontryagin Minimum Principle
[
7
]
yields first-order necessary conditions for the optimal control. Let
(p_{i}^{*}(t),q_{i}^{*}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be adjoint variables associated to
(x_{i}^{*},v_{i}^{*})
, and we set
\mathbf{p}^{*}=\{p_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{N}
,
\mathbf{q}^{*}=\{q_{i}^{*}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Then the optimality system consists of a solution
(\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{v}^{*},\mathbf{u}^{*},\mathbf{p}^{*},\mathbf{q}^{*})
satisfying (
2.3
) along with the adjoint equations

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}p_{i}^{*}}{\mathrm{d}t}=-\|v_{i}^{*}\|^{2}q_{i}^{*}+\frac{\kappa}{mN}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(q_{j}^{*}-\langle x_{i}^{*},x_{j}^{*}\rangle q_{i}^{*}-\langle x_{i}^{*},q_{i}^{*}\rangle x_{j}^{*}-\langle x_{j}^{*},q_{j}^{*}\rangle x_{j}^{*}\right)-q_{i}^{*}\langle u_{i}^{*},x_{i}^{*}\rangle-u_{i}^{*}\langle x_{i}^{*},q_{i}^{*}\rangle-\frac{2}{N}(\langle\bar{x}^{*},\bar{x}_{i}^{*}\rangle\bar{x}^{*}-x_{i}^{*})\,,\\
\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}q_{i}^{*}}{\mathrm{d}t}=p_{i}^{*}-2\langle x_{i}^{*},q_{i}^{*}\rangle v_{i}^{*}-\frac{\gamma}{m}q_{i}^{*}\,,\\
p_{i}^{*}(T)=0,q_{i}^{*}(T)=0,\hskip 9.24994pti\in[N]\,,\end{cases}

and the optimality condition

\mathbf{u}^{*}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\left\langle q_{j}^{*},\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{j}^{*}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right\rangle+\frac{\lambda}{N}|w_{j}|^{2}\ \right)=-\frac{N}{2\lambda}\left[q_{j}^{*}-\langle q_{j}^{*},x_{j}^{*}\rangle x_{j}^{*}\right]_{j=1}^{N}.

Recall that the gradient of the functional
\mathcal{J}
introduces in (
2.4
) is given as follows:

(2.13)

\displaystyle\nabla\mathcal{J}=\frac{2\lambda}{N}\mathbf{u}+\left[q_{j}-\langle q_{j},x_{j}\rangle x_{j}\right]_{j=1}^{N}=\left[\frac{2\lambda}{N}u_{j}+(q_{j}-\langle q_{j},x_{j}\rangle x_{j})\right]_{j=1}^{N}.

We will apply this gradient form to the gradient descent method in Section
3.2
.

2.2.
First order model

We are also interested in the following first-order control problem:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(x_{k}-\langle x_{i},x_{k}\rangle x_{i}\right)+u_{i}-\langle u_{i},x_{i}\rangle x_{i},\\
x_{i}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\hskip 9.24994pt\forall~i\in[N],\end{cases}

with the following payoff functional

(2.15)

\tilde{\mathcal{J}}(\mathbf{u}(\cdot);\mathbf{x}(0))=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|^{2}+\lambda\|u_{i}\|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}t.

The existence of the above optimal control problem can be obtained similarly as the second order model, so here we omit the proofs and only present the theorems:

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
T>0
and given
\mathbf{u}(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}^{N}
,
there exists a pathwise unique global solution
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
to the Kuramoto system (

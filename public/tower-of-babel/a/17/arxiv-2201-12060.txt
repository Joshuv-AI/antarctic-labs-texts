A pseudodifferential calculus for maximally hypoelliptic operators and the Helffer-Nourrigat conjecture
Iakovos Androulidakis, Omar Mohsen, Robert Yuncken

Abstract
We extend the classical regularity theorem of elliptic operators to maximally hypoelliptic differential operators. More precisely, given vector fields X 1 , … , X m X_{1},\ldots,X_{m} on a smooth manifold which satisfy Hörmander’s bracket generating condition, we define a principal symbol for any linear differential operator. Our symbol takes into account the vector fields X i X_{i} and their commutators. We show that for an arbitrary differential operator, its principal symbol is invertible if and only if the operator is maximally hypoelliptic. This answers affirmatively a conjecture due to Helffer and Nourrigat. Our result is proven in a more general setting, where we allow each one of the vector fields X 1 , … , X m X_{1},\ldots,X_{m} to have an arbitrary weight. In particular, our theorem generalizes Hörmander’s sum of squares theorem to higher order polynomials. † † MSC2020 subject classification 35H10; Secondary 47G30, 35B44, 58J40, 22A22, 22E25.



Introduction

Elliptic linear differential operators are some of the most extensively studied differential operators in analysis. This is because of their wide applications in many areas of mathematics such as algebraic geometry, complex geometry, symplectic geometry and representation theory. These applications are based on the following fundamental regularity theorem, which is proved using the pseudodifferential calculus developed by Nirenberg, Kohn, Hörmander and others.

Theorem 0.1

(
[
Hör85a
, Theorem 19.5.1]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold,
D:C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)
a differential operator of order
k
. The following are equivalent

a)

The operator
D
is elliptic, i.e., for every
\xi\in T^{*}M\backslash\{0\}
,
\sigma(D,x,\xi)\neq 0
, where
\sigma
is the classical principal symbol of
D
.

b)

For every (or for some)
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and every distribution
u
on
M
,
Du\in H^{s}(M)
implies
u\in H^{s+k}(M)
, where
H^{\bullet}
are the local
L^{2}
Sobolev spaces.

Furthermore if
M
is compact, the previous statements are equivalent to the following

d)

For every (or for some)
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
D:H^{s+k}(M)\to H^{s}(M)
is Fredholm

In a celebrated article, Hörmander proved that for some non-elliptic differential operators, now called Hörmander’s sums of squares operators, one still has the regularity of solutions.

Theorem 0.2

(
[
Hör67a
]
)
.

Let
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m+1}
be vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition, i.e., for every
x\in M
,
T_{x}M
is linearly spanned by
X_{1}(x),\cdots,X_{m+1}(x)
and their higher Lie brackets
[X_{i},X_{j}](x)
,
[X_{i},[X_{j},X_{l}]](x)
etc. Then
D=\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}^{2}+X_{m+1}
is hypoelliptic, i.e., if
u
is a distribution on
M
such that
Du
is smooth, then
u
is smooth.

It is natural to try to extend Hörmander’s theorem by finding sufficient conditions for the hypoellipticity of arbitrary polynomials in the vector fields
X_{i}
. Let
P
be a noncommutative polynomial in
m+1
variables with coefficients in
C^{\infty}(M)
.
In 1979, Helffer and Nourrigat
[
HN79c
]
conjectured a generalization of both Theorem
0.1
and Theorem
0.2
which allows one to obtain hypoellipticity of operators of the form
P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{m+1})
, and also generalises several regularity results in the literature, see
[
HN79c
, Sections I.2 and I.3]
.

The goal of this article is to prove the Helffer-Nourrigat conjecture, as well as its generalisation to arbitrary filtrations of the module of vector fields on
M
. Let us start with our main theorem in a restricted case (the case
X_{m+1}=0
). We’ll give its much more general form afterwards.

Thus, consider vector fields
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
satisfying the Lie bracket generating condition. This condition gives rise to the following notion of order for a differential operator. Every differential operator can then be written as
D=P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{m})
where
P
is a noncommutative polynomial with coefficients in
C^{\infty}(M)
. The Hörmander order of
D
is the minimum of
\deg(P)
for all possible
P
s. The Hörmander order leads us to consider Sobolev spaces defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{H}^{s}(M)

\displaystyle:=\{u\in L^{2}_{loc}M:Du\in L^{2}_{loc}M\quad\text{for all }D\text{ with H\"{o}rmander order}\leq s\},\quad s\in\mathbb{N}.

(0.1)

We extend these Sobolev spaces for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
by interpolation for
s>0
and duality for
s<0
. Trivially we have

\bigcap_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\tilde{H}^{s}(M)=\bigcap_{s\in\mathbb{N}}\tilde{H}^{s}(M)=C^{\infty}(M).

The crucial step is to define a principal symbol for differential operators which is sensitive to the vector fields
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
and the structure of their iterated commutators. Generalizing Helffer-Nourrigat, we can specify a family
(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F}_{x}))_{x\in M}
of osculating graded nilpotent Lie groups, as well as a subset of unitary representations
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}
of each group (Helffer-Nourrigat call this
\Gamma_{x}
). We then define an operator-valued symbol
(x,\pi)\mapsto\tilde{\sigma}(D,x,\pi)
on
\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{F}=\bigsqcup_{x\in M}\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}
. We prove the following

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold,
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
are vector fields satisfying the Lie bracket generating condition,
D:C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)
a differential operator of Hörmander order
k
. The following are equivalent

a)

For every
x\in M
and
\pi\in\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}\mathcal{F}\backslash\{0\}
,
\tilde{\sigma}(D,x,\pi)
is injective on
C^{\infty}(\pi)
.

b)

For every (or for some)
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and every distribution
u
on
M
,
Du\in\tilde{H}^{s}(M)
implies
u\in\tilde{H}^{s+k}(M)
.

Furthermore if
M
is compact, then the previous statements are equivalent to the following

e)

For every (or for some)
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
D:\tilde{H}^{s+k}(M)\to\tilde{H}^{s}(M)
is left invertible modulo compact operators

We now explain the principal symbol
\tilde{\sigma}
as well as the space of representations
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}\mathcal{F}
. Before we proceed, let us mention that if the vector fields satisfy Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition of rank
1
, i.e.,
X_{1}(x),\cdots,X_{m}(x)
span
T_{x}M
for all
x\in M
, then Theorem
A
is precisely Theorem
0.1
. The Sobolev spaces
\tilde{H}^{s}(M)
and
\tilde{\sigma}
are equal to
H^{s}(M)
and
\sigma
respectively. In
[
HN85a
]
, Helffer and Nourrigat proved Theorem
A
in the case of rank
2
, i.e.,
X_{1}(x),\cdots,X_{m}(x)
and
[X_{i},X_{j}](x)
span
T_{x}M
for all
x\in M
. They also proved the implication
b\implies a
in the general case with no assumptions on the rank. The main innovation in our work is combining their work
[
HN79b
,
HN85a
]
with recent advances in noncommutative geometry by Debord and Skandalis
[
DS14a
]
and van Erp and the third author
[
EY19a
]
together with the
C^{*}
-algebra of singular foliations defined by the first author and Skandalis
[
AS09a
]
and their blowups defined by the second author
[
Moh22b
]
. This allows us to prove Theorem
A
with no hypothesis at all on the rank.

Principal symbol
\tilde{\sigma}
.

Suppose that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
satisfy Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition of rank
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
G
be the free nilpotent Lie group of rank
N
with one generator
\tilde{X}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{X}_{m}
for each vector field
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
. We remark that in the article, we use a better choice of nilpotent group which is smaller and more natural; for simplicity of the exposition in this introduction, we temporarily use the group
G
. Let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary representation of
G
on a Hilbert space
L^{2}\pi
. Then by taking the derivative of
\pi
, one obtains linear maps

d\pi(\tilde{X}_{1}),\cdots,d\pi(\tilde{X}_{m}):C^{\infty}(\pi)\to C^{\infty}(\pi)

where
C^{\infty}(\pi)\subseteq L^{2}\pi
is the space of smooth vectors.

We can now define
\tilde{\sigma}
. We write
D=P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{m})
for some noncommutative polynomial
P
. This is the equivalent of taking local coordinates when defining the classical principal symbol. We then define

\tilde{\sigma}(D,x,\pi):C^{\infty}(\pi)\to C^{\infty}(\pi),\quad\tilde{\sigma}(D,x,\pi)=P_{max,x}(d\pi(\tilde{X}_{1}),\cdots,d\pi(\tilde{X}_{m})),

where
P_{max,x}
is the maximal homogeneous part of
P
after replacing each coefficient
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
by
f(x)
. Note that this definition may depend on
P
since if the operator
D
can be written
D=P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{m})=Q(X_{1},\cdots,X_{m})
for two different polynomials
P,Q
, then in general
\tilde{\sigma}(D,x,\pi)
depends on the choice of
P
or
Q
(see Section
1.5
for examples). But one of our main results is that this is not the case when
\pi
belongs to a certain naturally defined subset
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}\subseteq\hat{G}
. The set
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}
can be thought of as a generalization of the cotangent space
T^{*}_{x}M
in this sub-Riemannian context. The set
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}
only depends on the vector fields
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
and not on
D
. This set was defined by Helffer and Nourrigat in
[
HN79c
]
using Kirillov’s orbit method
[
Kir62a
]
. For this reason, we call it the
Helffer-Nourrigat cone.

Theorem B
.

For each
x\in M
, for any representation
\pi\in\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}\subseteq\hat{G}
,
\tilde{\sigma}(D,x,\pi)
doesn’t depend on the presentation of
D=P(X_{1},\cdots,X_{m})
.

We remark that the set
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}
is very computable in practice. We refer the reader to Section
1.4
for the precise construction of
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}
and for various examples.

Main theorem.

We will prove a much more general form of Theorem
A
as follows. Let us allow weights on the vector fields
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
, meaning that we attach to each vector field
X_{i}
a natural number
v_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. The Hörmander order of
D
is now the minimum degree of
P
taking weights into account. The Sobolev spaces
\tilde{H}^{s}(M)
are defined as in (
0.1
) when
s
is a multiple of
\mathrm{gcd}(v_{i})
, interpolating for other values of
s
. The principal symbol
\tilde{\sigma}
in this case is defined as before, the only difference is that
P_{max,x}
is the maximal weighted homogeneous part.

We can now state the main theorem of the article.

Theorem C
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold,
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s condition,
v_{1},\cdots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
natural numbers (weights for
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
), and
D:C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(M)
a differential operator of Hörmander order
k
. The following are equivalent

a)

For every
x\in M
and
\pi\in\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}\mathcal{F}\backslash\{0\}
,
\tilde{\sigma}(D,x,\pi)
is injective on
C^{\infty}(\pi)
.

b)

For every (or for some)
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and every distribution
u
on
M
,
Du\in\tilde{H}^{s}(M)
implies
u\in\tilde{H}^{s+k}(M)
.

Furthermore if
M
is compact, then the previous statements are equivalent to the following

e)

For every (or for some)
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
D:\tilde{H}^{s+k}(M)\to\tilde{H}^{s}(M)
is left invertible modulo compact operators

Differential operators satisfying the conditions of Theorem
C
are called
maximally hypoellitpic differential operators.
Theorem
C
immediately implies Hörmander’s sum of squares theorem, by taking
v_{1}=\cdots=v_{m}=1
and
v_{m+1}=2
. The injectivity of our symbol for Hörmander’s sum of squares operator is trivial to verify. See also Corollary
1.18
for another immediate application of Theorem
C
generalising Hörmander’s theorem. Let us give right away a very simple yet nontrivial example which shows the strength of Theorem
C
.

Example 0.3
.

Let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
be natural numbers. We consider
\partial_{x}
and
x^{k}\partial_{y}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We assign the weight
1
to
\partial_{x}
and
n
to
x^{k}\partial_{y}
. Then take

D=(-1)^{n(k+n)}\partial_{x}^{2n(k+n)}+(-1)^{k+n}(x^{k}\partial_{y})^{2(k+n)}+\lambda\partial_{y}^{2n}+D^{\prime},

(0.2)

where
D^{\prime}
is any differential operator of Hörmander order
<2n(k+n)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. A simple computation using our principal symbol shows that
D
is maximally hypoelliptic if and only if

(-1)^{n+1}\lambda\notin\mathrm{spec}((-1)^{n(k+n)}\partial_{x}^{2n(k+n)}+x^{2k(k+n)}),

(0.3)

where
(-1)^{n(k+n)}\partial_{x}^{2n(k+n)}+x^{2k(k+n)}
is considered as an unbounded operator on
L^{2}\mathbb{R}
. Note that the spectrum of this operator is a discrete set converging to
\infty
. Discreteness of the spectrum of
(-1)^{n(k+n)}\partial_{x}^{2n(k+n)}+x^{2k(k+n)}
is also a consequence of Theorem
C
, see Remark
3.46
.

We refer the reader to
[
HN85a
, Sections I.2 and I.3]
for more applications of Theorem
C
. Finally, we prove the following theorem whose counterpart for elliptic operators is obvious. It allows us to deduce maximal hypoellipticity on a neighbourhood of
x
from invertibility of the symbol at
x
.

Theorem D
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold,
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
vector fields satisfying the Lie bracket generating condition,
v_{1},\cdots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
natural numbers (weights for
X_{1},\cdots,X_{m}
), and
D
a differential operator. Let
x\in M
. If for every
\pi\in\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}\mathcal{F}\backslash\{0\}
,
\tilde{\sigma}(D,x,\pi)
is injective, then for some open neighbourhood
U\subseteq M
of
x
,
\tilde{\sigma}(D,y,\pi)
is injective for every
y\in U
,
\pi\in\mathcal{T}^{*}_{y}\mathcal{F}\backslash\{0\}
. In particular
D
is maximally hypoelliptic on
U
.

All the above results extend to differential operators with coefficients in a vector bundle. It is worth reiterating that in addition to showing the hypoellipticity of the polynomial differential operators considered by Helffer and Nourrigat, the method of proof here provides a pseudodifferential calculus adapted to such operators with a well-defined notion of principal symbol. The existence of this calculus is essential for applications. For instance, the second author has shown that one can use the associated pseudodifferential calculus to prove a topological index formula for maximally hypoelliptic differential operators
[
Moh22c
]
. Similarly, one can obtain a complete description of the leading term of the heat kernel expansion of the above maximally elliptic differential operators
[
Moha
]
.

Conventions

a)

In this article, if
G
is a Lie group, then its Lie algebra is the space of
right
invariant vector fields on
G
. This convention differs by a sign from the one usually used in Lie group theory but agrees with the one usually used in Lie groupoid theory. A consequence of this convention is that the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula is given by

\BCH(X,Y)=X+Y-\frac{1}{2}[X,Y]+\frac{1}{12}[X,[X,Y]]-\frac{1}{12}[Y,[Y,X]]+\cdots

(0.4)

We will call the above formula the BCH formula. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra, then the BCH formula is a finite sum which defines a group law on
\mathfrak{g}
making it a simply connected Lie group. Throughout the article, we will treat
\mathfrak{g}
as both a Lie algebra and a Lie group.

b)

Throughout the article, especially in Sections
2.5
,
3.4
,
3.6
,
3.7
, we will define various unbounded multipliers on various
C^{*}
-algebras. Unless the multiplier is bounded, we will never take the closure of the graph. For our applications, the natural domain of ’smooth‘ densities, functions, vectors etc will be sufficient.

1.1
Generalized distributions and singular foliations

Let us recall a few things and set the notation.

a)

In this article,
M
will be a smooth manifold without boundary. We denote by
\mathcal{X}(M)
(
\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
) the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-module of
real
vector fields (with compact support) of
M
. We use
C^{\infty}(M,E)
(
C^{\infty}_{c}(M,E)
) to denote the space of smooth sections (with compact support) of a vector bundle
E\to M
.

b)

If
X\in\mathcal{X}(M)
,
x\in M
, then
\mathrm{exp}(X)\cdot x
denotes the time one flow of
X
starting from
x
, whenever it is well-defined.

c)

If
X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}\in\mathcal{X}(M)
, then we will write
\mathcal{D}=\langle X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}\rangle
for the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-module consisting of
\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}X_{i}
with
f_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

d)

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-submodule of
\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
,
U\subseteq M
an open subset. We say that a family of vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathcal{D}
generates
\mathcal{D}
on
U
if for any
Y\in\mathcal{D}
there is
f_{1},\ldots,f_{k}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
Y_{|U}=(\sum_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}X_{i})_{|U}
. We say that the family
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
generates
\mathcal{D}
at
p\in M
if it generates
\mathcal{D}
on some neighbourhood of
p
.

The module
\mathcal{D}
is called locally finitely generated if at every point
p\in M
there is a finite generating family. When the cardinality of such a family
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
is the smallest possible, it is called a minimal generating family at
p
.

e)

A
generalised distribution
is by definition a locally finitely generated
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-submodule of
\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
.

f)

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a generalised distribution and
p\in M
. The fiber of
\mathcal{D}
at
p
is the quotient vector space

\mathcal{D}_{p}=\mathcal{D}/I_{p}\mathcal{D},\quad\text{where}\quad I_{p}=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}):f(p)=0\}.

(1.1)

It is a finite dimensional vector space because
\mathcal{D}
is locally finitely generated. If
X\in\mathcal{D}
, then we write
[X]_{p}
for the class of
X
in the fiber
\mathcal{D}_{p}
.

The following result was proved in
[
AS09a
, Proposition 1.5]
for singular foliations. The proof doesn’t use Lie brackets, so it applies equally to generalised distributions.

Proposition 1.1
.

If
\mathcal{D}
is a generalised distribution, then a family of vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\in\mathcal{D}
generates
\mathcal{D}
at
p\in M
if and only if
[X_{1}]_{p},\ldots,[X_{k}]_{p}
spans the fiber
\mathcal{D}_{p}
. It is a minimal generating family at
p
if and only if
[X_{1}]_{p},\ldots,[X_{k}]_{p}
is a basis of
\mathcal{D}_{p}
.

g)

We define
\mathcal{D}^{*}:=\bigsqcup_{p\in M}\mathcal{D}_{p}^{*}
. For every
X\in\mathcal{D}
, let

\langle\cdot,X\rangle:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\xi\in\mathcal{D}^{*}_{p}\mapsto\xi([X]_{p}).

We equip
\mathcal{D}^{*}
with the weakest topology such that the natural projection
\pi:\mathcal{D}^{*}\to M
and the maps
\langle\cdot,X\rangle
for every
X\in\mathcal{D}
are continuous. By
[
AS11a
, Section 2.2]
, this topology makes
\mathcal{D}^{*}
a locally compact Hausdorff second countable space.

h)

An automorphism of
\mathcal{D}
is a diffeomorphism
\phi:M\to M
such that the pushforward of vector fields
\phi_{*}:\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)\to\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
maps
\mathcal{D}
bijectively to itself. It thus induces maps
\phi_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{p}\to\mathcal{D}_{\phi(p)}
between fibers, and by duality, a homeomorphism
\phi^{*}
of
\mathcal{D}^{*}
.

i)

A
singular foliation
is a generalised distribution
\mathcal{F}
which is closed under Lie brackets. The following lemma which is straightforward to check is extremely important.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a singular foliation, and
p
a stationary point,
i.e.,

X(p)=0
for all
X\in\mathcal{F}
. Then
\mathcal{F}_{p}
is a Lie algebra with the Lie bracket
[[X]_{p},[Y]_{p}]:=[[X,Y]]_{p}
.

1.2
Filtered foliations

Definition 1.3
.

A
filtered foliation
of depth
N\in\mathbb{N}
on a smooth manifold
M
is a filtration by generalised distributions

0=\mathcal{F}^{0}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{2}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{N}=\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)

such that

[\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{F}^{j}]\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{i+j},\quad\forall\ i,j\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.2)

Here, and throughout the article, we use the convention
\mathcal{F}^{n}=\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
for all
n\geq N
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
be smooth vector fields on
M
satisfying Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition and let
v_{1},\cdots,v_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
be weights for each
X_{i}
. We define
\mathcal{F}^{j}
to be the generalised distribution generated by all iterated Lie brackets
[X_{i_{1}},\ldots[X_{i_{k-1}},X_{i_{k}}]\ldots]
such that
v_{i_{1}}+\cdots+v_{i_{k}}\leq j
. Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition implies that
\mathcal{F}^{N}=\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
for some
N
.
1
1

1

This is only true locally. The value of
N
may be infinite if
M
isn’t compact. Since maximal hypoellipticity is a local notion, we can ignore this issue.
Inversely one can easily see that any filtered foliation where each
\mathcal{F}^{i}
is finitely generated is obtained this way. So locally, filtered foliations are an intrinsic way to define a family of weighted vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s Lie bracket generating condition.

The central geometric object in this paper is the adiabatic foliation associated to
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
which we now define. For
X\in\mathcal{X}(M)
, we denote by
\tilde{X}\in\mathcal{X}(M\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
the vector field

\tilde{X}(x,t)=(X(x),0).

If
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
is a generalised distribution, we write
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
for the generalised distribution on
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, generated by
\tilde{X}\in\Gamma(TM\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
X\in\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
be a filtered foliation. The
adiabatic foliation
associated to
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is the singular foliation on
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}:=t\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{1}}+t^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{2}}+\ldots+t^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}^{N}},

where
t\in C^{\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
denotes the smooth projection onto the second variable.

It is clear that
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
is locally finitely generated. It is involutive because of (
1.2
). Hence
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
is a singular foliation. Let
p\in M
. The point
(p,0)
is stationary. So by Lemma
1.2
,
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}_{(p,0)}
is a Lie algebra which we now describe. Since
\mathcal{F}^{i-1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{i},
it follows that
\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p}
maps naturally to
\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}
. This map is not injective in general. Nevertheless we denote by
\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}/\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p}
the quotient of
\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}
by the image of
\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p}
, and we write

\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{i=N}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}}{\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{i=N}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{i}}{\mathcal{F}^{i-1}+I_{p}\mathcal{F}^{i}},

where
I_{p}
is defined in (
1.1
). Note that, due to the non-injectivity of the maps
\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}
, the dimension of
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}
can be strictly larger than
\dim(M)
.

If
X\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
, we will use
[X]_{i,p}
to denote the class of
X
in
\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}/\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}
.

Proposition 1.6
.

The map

\displaystyle\eta_{p}:\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}\to\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}_{(p,0)},\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}[X_{i}]_{i,p}\to\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}t^{i}\tilde{X}_{i}\right]_{(p,0)}\qquad\text{with }X_{i}\in\mathcal{F}^{i}

is a well-defined isomorphism of vector spaces.

Proof.

If
X_{i}\in\mathcal{F}^{i-1}
, then
t^{i}X_{i}=t\cdot t^{i-1}X_{i}
. Hence
[t^{i}\tilde{X}_{i}]_{(p,0)}=0
. Thus
\eta_{p}
is well-defined. Injectivity and surjectivity follow from the definition of
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
.
∎

We define a Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}
by declaring the map
\eta_{p}
an isomorphism of Lie algebras. The Lie bracket is thus given by the formula

[[X]_{i,p},[Y]_{j,p}]=\big[[X,Y]\big]_{i+j,p}\in\mathcal{F}^{i+j}_{p}/\mathcal{F}^{i+j-1}_{p},\quad X\in\mathcal{F}^{i},Y\in\mathcal{F}^{j}.

The resulting nilpotent Lie algebra is called the
osculating Lie algebra
of
\mathcal{F}
at
p
. It is nilpotent because if
i+j>N
, then
[[X]_{i,p},[Y]_{j,p}]=0
. Hence the space
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}
is also a Lie group with a product via the BCH formula (
0.4
).

Proposition 1.7
.

The function
p\mapsto\dim(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p})
is upper semi-continuous.

Proof.

We will prove the stronger assertion that
p\mapsto\dim(\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}/\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p})
is upper semi-continuous for all
i
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. It is straightforward to check that
\dim(\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}/\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p})\leq k
if and only if there exists
X_{1},\cdots,X_{k}\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
,
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{l}\in\mathcal{F}^{i-1}
for some
s
such that the
[X_{1}]_{p},\cdots,[X_{k}]_{p},[Y_{1}]_{p},\cdots,[Y_{l}]_{p}
generate
\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}
. By Proposition
1.1
, this implies that
X_{1},\cdots,X_{k},Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{l}
generate
\mathcal{F}^{i}
over an open neighbourhood of
p
. The semi-continuity of
p\mapsto\dim(\mathcal{F}^{i}_{p}/\mathcal{F}^{i-1}_{p})
follows.
∎

1.3
The Debord-Skandalis action

Let
\alpha
be the
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
-action on
M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by
\alpha_{\lambda}(x,t)=(x,\lambda^{-1}t)
. These maps are automorphisms of the adiabatic foliation, so they induce maps between the fibers of
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
. At
t=0
, using Proposition
1.6
, we obtain an action by automorphisms of the osculating Lie algebras given by

\alpha_{\lambda}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}[X_{i}]_{i,p}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda^{i}[X_{i}]_{i,p},\qquad(X_{i}\in\mathcal{F}^{i}).

(1.3)

Likewise, there is an induced action
\hat{\alpha}
on the space
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}^{*}
. Again using Proposition
1.6
, we have

\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}^{*}=\left(T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}\right)\sqcup\left(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})^{*}\times\{0\}\right),

(1.4)

where
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})^{*}:=\bigsqcup_{p\in M}\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})^{*}_{p}
. Under this identification, the
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
-action on
\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}^{*}
is given by

\hat{\alpha}_{\lambda}(p,\xi,t)=(p,\xi,\lambda t),\qquad p\in M,\xi\in T^{*}_{p}M

for
t>0
, while at
t=0
it is given by the formula

\hat{\alpha}_{\lambda}(\xi)(X)=\xi(\alpha_{\lambda}(X)),\quad X\in\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p},~\xi\in\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}^{*}.

(1.5)

We refer to the all of the above actions as the Debord-Skandalis action.

.

Let

,

•

If

•

for some

1.5
Differential operators and principal symbol

Let
E\to M
be a vector bundle,
\Diff(M,E)
the algebra of differential operators
C^{\infty}(M,E)\to C^{\infty}(M,E)
not necessarily of compact support. Let
\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,E)
denote the vector space of differential operator

such that for every

can be written as sum of monomials
\alpha\nabla_{Y_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{Y_{l}}

\alpha\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M,\mathrm{End}(E))
,

for some

such that

. Here
\nabla
is any connection on

. If
E
is trivial, then we write
\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M)
instead of
\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,E)
. Then:

a)

\Diff_{\mathcal{F}}^{0}(M,E)=C^{\infty}(M,\mathrm{End}(E))
.

b)

For every
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,
X\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
, one has
\nabla_{X}\in\Diff_{\mathcal{F}}^{i}(M,E)
.

c)

For every
i,j
, one has
\Diff_{\mathcal{F}}^{i}(M,E)\Diff_{\mathcal{F}}^{j}(M,E)\subseteq\Diff_{\mathcal{F}}^{i+j}(M,E)
.

d)

If
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
D\in\Diff_{\mathcal{F}}^{i}(M,E)
with
i\geq 1
, then
[f,D]\in\Diff_{\mathcal{F}}^{i-1}(M,E)
.

Definition 1.14
.

We say that
D
has Hörmander’s order
k
if
D\in\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,E)\backslash\Diff^{k-1}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,E)

Principal symbol.

Let
D\in\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,E),

p\in M
and
\pi
an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}
on a Hilbert space denoted
L^{2}\pi
. We denote by
C^{\infty}(\pi)\subseteq L^{2}\pi
the subspace of smooth vector. If
X\in\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}
, then the differential of
\pi
at
X
gives a linear map

d\pi(X):C^{\infty}(\pi)\to C^{\infty}(\pi),\quad d\pi(X)v=\frac{d}{dt}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}t=0\end{subarray}}\pi(-tX)v,\quad v\in C^{\infty}(\pi).

It satisfies

d\pi([X,Y])=[d\pi(X),d\pi(Y)],\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}.

The principal symbol of
D
at
\pi
is a linear map

\sigma^{k}(D,p,\pi):E_{p}\otimes C^{\infty}(\pi)\to E_{p}\otimes C^{\infty}(\pi)

defined as follows. Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
such that
f(p)=1
. Then
fD
can be written as sum of monomials
\alpha\nabla_{Y_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{Y_{l}}
as above. The symbol
\sigma^{k}(D,p,\pi)
is equal to the sum where we replace each monomial by

\alpha(p)\otimes d\pi([Y_{1}]_{a_{1},p})\cdots d\pi([Y_{l}]_{a_{l},p})

and we only sum over monomials such that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}=k
. The following theorem establishes that the principal symbol is well defined when
\pi\in\mathcal{T}^{*}_{p}\mathcal{F}
. It will be proved at the end of Section
2
.

Theorem 1.15
.

Let
D\in\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,E)
,
p\in M
. If
\pi
is an irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}
which corresponds to an element of
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{p}\mathcal{F}
by Kirillov’s orbit method, then the principal symbol
\sigma^{k}(D,p,\pi)
is well defined, i.e., doesn’t depend on the choice of
f
nor on the connection on
E
nor on the way
fD
is written as a sum of monomials of the form
\alpha\nabla_{Y_{1}}\cdots\nabla_{Y_{l}}
.

Let

Proposition 1.17
.

Let
G
be connected simply connected nilpotent Lie group with lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and let

be a generating family for

. Then for any non-trivial irreducible unitary representation

is injective.

Proof.

Let

be a smooth vector in the kernel of
\pi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{\frac{v_{i}}{2}}x_{i}^{v_{i}}+x_{m+1}^{v_{m+1}}\right)
. Since the operator
\pi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{\frac{v_{i}}{2}}x_{i}^{v_{i}}\right)
is positive and

is skew-adjoint, it follows that

for each

. Since

is non-trivial irreducible, we deduce that

.
∎

It follows that the hypothesis of Theorem
C
.a is trivially satisfied for the differential operator
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-1)^{\frac{v_{i}}{2}}X_{i}^{a_{i}}+X_{m+1}^{v_{m+1}}

.

Corollary 1.18
.

The operator
\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{\frac{v_{i}}{2}}X_{i}^{v_{i}}+X_{m+1}^{v_{m+1}}
is maximally hypoelliptic.

Notice that we didn’t need to calculate the Helffer-Nourrigat cone because injectivity of the symbol holds for every non-trivial irreducible unitary representation. But in general the Helffer-Nourrigat cone can be a proper subset of

Definition 2.1

\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}

(ii)

\theta_{i}(X)(\mathcal{Q}(f))=\mathcal{Q}(L_{Y}(f))
.
∎

If
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define an unbounded multiplier
T_{n}
of
L^{\infty}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
with domain
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
as follows

T_{n}(a)_{t}=t^{n}a_{t},\quad T_{n}(a)_{p,0}=0,\quad a\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}).

We also define
T_{0}(a)=a
.
Now let
D\in\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,\Omega^{1/2}M)
and further suppose that
D
is compactly supported. By the definition of
\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,\Omega^{1/2}M)
, we can write
D
as a sum of monomials of the form
\alpha L_{Y_{1}}\cdots L_{Y_{l}}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{F}^{a_{i}}
for all
i
and
\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}\leq k
. This expression is slightly different from the one we used in Section
1.5
, where we used
\nabla_{Y}
instead of
L_{Y}
. This makes no difference in the definition of the principal symbol because
\nabla_{Y}-L_{Y}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M,\mathbb{C})
. We now define an unbounded multiplier of
L^{\infty}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
denoted by
\Theta(D)
by taking the sum

\alpha T_{k-\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}}\theta_{a_{1}}(Y_{1})\cdots\theta_{a_{l}}(Y_{l})

for each monomial in the decomposition of
D
. By Lemma
2.22
,
\Theta(D)
is well defined on
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
and

\Theta(D)(C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}))\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}).

Furthermore if
a\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})
, then
(\Theta(D)a)_{t}=t^{k}D\circ a_{t}
for
t>0
and for
p>0
,
(\Theta(D)a)_{p,0}
is a sum of
\alpha L_{\widetilde{Y}_{1p}}\cdots L_{\widetilde{Y}_{lp}}(a_{p,0})
, and one only sums over monomials such that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}=k
. Hence if
\pi\in\widehat{\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p}}
, then

\pi((\Theta(D)a)_{p,0})=\sigma^{k}(D,p,\pi)(\pi(a_{p,0})).

It is true that
\Theta(D)
may depend on the presentation of
D
as a sum of monomials. But its action on the nonzero fibers doesn’t depend on the presentation (and is equal to
t^{k}D
). Hence by (
2.18
),
\sigma(D,p,\pi)(\pi(a_{p,0}))
for
a\in C^{*}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
and
\pi\in\mathcal{T}^{*}_{p}\mathcal{F}
also doesn’t depend on the presentation of
D
as sum of monomials. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.15
for compactly supported differential operators. For general operators, it is clear that
\sigma^{k}(D,p,\pi)
only depends on
D
in a neighbourhood of

, so Theorem
1.15
follows for

. For other vector bundles, one can embed them inside
\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\Omega^{1/2}M
for
n
big enough.
∎

Throughout this section, we will treat the Helffer-Nourrigat cone as a set of representations of the osculating groups. This section is organized as follows.

•

•

•

In Section

•

In Section

•

In Section

•

In Section

•

In Section

3.1
Oscillatory integrals

If
E\to M
a vector bundle, then we denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,E)
the topological dual of

. In particular
C^{\infty}_{c}(M,E)\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,E)
. We use
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega^{\alpha}):=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega^{\alpha}M)
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
AS11a
, Section 1.2]
, see also
[
LMV17a
]
)
.

Let
\phi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
be a smooth submersion map,

a vector bundle. We say a distribution

is transverse to

if
\phi_{*}(fu)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M_{2})
for any

.

If

is transverse to
\phi
, then one can restrict

to
\phi^{-1}(x)
for any

and obtain

. The following example illustrates the use of transverse distributions for pseudodifferential operators.

Example 3.2
.

Let
p:M_{1}\times M_{2}\to M_{2}
be the projection map. By the Schwartz kernel theorem
[
Hör90a
, Theorem 5.2.1]
, an element
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M_{1}\times M_{2},\Omega^{1/2})
corresponds to a continuous map between
C^{\infty}_{c}(M_{2},\Omega^{1/2})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M_{1},\Omega^{1/2})
. It is easily seen that

is transverse to

if and only if it maps
C^{\infty}_{c}(M_{2},\Omega^{1/2})
to
C^{\infty}(M_{1},\Omega^{1/2})
.

Let
(V,\natural,\mathbb{U},U)
be a graded basis. Recall that
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
acts on
\mathbb{U}
by (
2.1
). If
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{U},\Omega^{1/2}_{r,s})
, then we define

\langle\alpha_{\lambda*}u,f\rangle=\lambda^{-1}\langle u,\alpha_{\lambda^{-1}*}f\rangle.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{C}
. We define
\mathcal{E}^{\prime k}(\mathbb{U})
to be the subspace of
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{U},\Omega^{1/2}_{r,s})
such that

a)

is transverse to the map
s:\mathbb{U}\to M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

.

b)

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
,

\alpha_{\lambda*}u-\lambda^{k}u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{U},\Omega^{1/2}_{r,s}).

(3.1)

c)

The projection
\supp(u)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is proper, where the projection comes from the inclusion
\supp(u)\subseteq\mathbb{U}\subseteq V\times U\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

The following proposition is a bundle version of a proposition due to Taylor
[
Tay84a
, Proposition 2.2]
. It shows that any

is the sum of an oscillatory integral and a Schwartz function. To state it, we need the following notation. If

is a vector space and

, then we say that

is Schwartz if it is Schwartz after trivializing
\Omega^{1}V
by any Euclidean structure on

. In the next proposition, we treat

as an element of

by (

).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let

. If

, then there exists a unique smooth function
A\in C^{\infty}((V^{*}\times U\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\setminus(\{0\}\times U\times\{0\}))
called the full symbol of

such that

a)

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}

A(\hat{\alpha}_{\lambda}(\xi),x,t\lambda)=\lambda^{k}A(\xi,x,t),

(3.2)

where
\hat{\alpha}_{\lambda}(\xi)
is defined in (
1.5
).

b)

There exists
K\subseteq U
compact such that
\supp(A)\subseteq V^{*}\times K\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

c)

If
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(V^{*}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
equal to

in a neighbourhood of
(0,0)
, then

f(X,x,t)=u(X,x,t)-\int_{V^{*}}e^{i\langle\xi,X\rangle}(1-\chi)(\xi,t)A(\xi,x,t),

(3.3)

then

, and

for some

. Furthermore

and all its derivatives in

and

are Schwartz in

uniformly in

and

.

Conversely if

satisfies

and

, then there exists

such that

.

Proof.

We choose a Euclidean metric on

and a Riemmanian metric on

. We have thus trivialized all densities that appear above. Since

is transverse to

for any

. We denote the restriction by

3.3
,

is compactly supported. Let

be the smooth function on

implies that for every

and all its derivatives in

are Schwartz in

uniformly in

. By induction one has

(3.4)

We define

(3.5)

We now check that

has the required properties

a)

if

, then the series is absolutely convergent because

. Same for all derivatives, so it follows that

b)

Since

is compactly supported, it follows that

for some

. By Condition c of Definition

c)

it suffices to show that

g(\xi,x,t)=\chi(\xi,t)v(\xi,x,t)+(1-\chi(\xi,t))\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}2^{-kn}h_{2}(\hat{\alpha}_{2^{n}}(\xi),x,2^{n}t)\in\mathcal{S}(V^{*}\times U\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Since

there exists

such that

. Decay at infinity of

easily follows from that of

.

Uniqueness of

easily follows from (
3.3
). Now let
A\in C^{\infty}((V^{*}\times U\times\mathbb{R}_{+})\setminus(\{0\}\times U\times\{0\}))
satisfing

. Let

. It is clear that

is transverse to

. By hypothesis on

\ssing(u)\subseteq\{0\}\times K\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

\alpha_{\lambda*}u-\lambda^{k}u\in C^{\infty}(V\times U\times\mathbb{R}_{+}).

(3.6)

Let

is

•

with full symbol

∎

Notice that Proposition

Definition of pseudodifferential operators and independence of the choice of basis

Let

. Following the notation of (
2.4
), for

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}_{t*}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime k}(\mathbb{U})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M\times M,\Omega^{1/2}),\quad\ u\mapsto\mathrm{ev}_{t*}(u_{t})

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}_{p,0*}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime k}(\mathbb{U})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{p},\Omega^{1}),\quad u\mapsto\mathrm{ev}_{p,0*}(u_{p,0}),

where
u_{t}
is the restriction of

to
\mathbb{U}_{t}
and

the restriction of
u
to

Definition 3.5
.

An element

is properly supported, i.e.,

.

c)

For every

Remark 3.6

In Definition

\displaystyle\leq C|\pi(Y,X,x,t)-\mathrm{exp}(\natural(\alpha_{t}(Z)))\cdot x|.

Now we consider
\natural(\alpha_{t}(X))
,
\natural(\alpha_{t}(Y))
and
\natural(\alpha_{t}(Z))
as elements of
t\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)[[t]]
. By (
2.5
), it follows that

\BCH(\natural(\alpha_{t}(Y)),\natural(\alpha_{t}(X)))_{N}=\natural(\alpha_{t}(Z))

Hence Theorem
A.1
implies that

|\pi(Y,X,x,t)-\mathrm{exp}(\natural(\alpha_{t}(Z)))\cdot x|=o(t^{N}).

Therefore

|\alpha_{t^{-1}}(k(\alpha_{t}(Z),\pi(Y,X,x,t),x))-Z|=o(1).

Continuity of
\psi
follows. Hence
\phi
is smooth.

It is straightforward to check that
\phi
satisfies Theorem
2.9
.b, c, d. For a, it is clear that
\phi
is a submersion at
(0,0,p,0)
for any
p\in U
. Since
\phi
is
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
-equivariant, it follows that
\phi
is a submersion on an
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-equivariant neighbourhood of
\{0\}\times\{0\}\times U\times\{0\}
. Restricting to such a neighbourhood, we can ensure that
\phi
is a submersion. This finishes the proof of Theorem
2.9
.

Proof of Lemma
A.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the free nilpotent graded Lie algebra of step
N
generated by elements of
V\oplus V^{\prime}
. We can extend
\natural\oplus\natural^{\prime}:V\oplus V^{\prime}\to\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
to
\natural:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)
using (
2.5
). It is then enough to construct
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\times U\to V^{\prime}
satisfying Lemma
A.6
. In the proof it will be convenient to say that a polynomial map
P:\mathfrak{g}\to V^{\prime}
is positive if there exist polynomials maps
P^{0},\cdots,P^{N-1}:\mathfrak{g}\to V^{\prime}
such that

P(\alpha_{t}(X))=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}t^{i}\alpha_{t}(P^{i}(X)),\quad\forall X\in\mathfrak{g},t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

We call
P^{0}
the homogeneous part of
P
. We start by constructing the linear part of
\psi
.

Lemma A.7
.

There exists a smooth map
\phi^{1}:\mathfrak{g}\times U\to V^{\prime}
such that

a)

for every
x\in U
,
\phi_{x}^{1}:\mathfrak{g}\to V^{\prime}
is linear and
\phi_{x}^{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{i})\subseteq\oplus_{j\leq i}V^{\prime j}
, i.e.,
\phi_{x}^{1}
is positive.

b)

for every
x\in U
,
X\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\natural(\phi_{x}^{1}(X))
and
\natural(X)
are vector fields on
M
which are equal at
x
.

Proof.

Fix a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and
V^{\prime}
. We further suppose that each element of the basis belongs to
\mathfrak{g}^{n}
or
V^{\prime n}
for some
n
. Since
\phi^{1}_{x}
is linear for
x\in U
, it is enough to define it on basis element of
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{n}
be a basis element. By Condition (ii) of Definition
2.1
, we can find smooth functions
f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

\natural(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}\natural(v_{i}),\quad\text{on }U,

where
v_{i}
are the basis elements of
\oplus_{i\leq n}V^{i}
. We then define
\phi^{1}
by

\phi^{1}(X,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x)v_{i}.\qed

(A.6)

Fix
x\in U
. In what follows we will say that an element
X\in\mathfrak{g}
vanishes at
x
if
\natural(X)(x)=0
. Since
V^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, the vector
-\phi^{1}_{x}(X)+X\in\mathfrak{g}
is well defined. It vanishes at
x
by Lemma
A.7
. We now construct the quadratic part of
\psi_{x}
. Consider

\BCH(-\phi^{1}_{x}(X),X)=\Big(-\phi^{1}_{x}(X)+X\Big)+\frac{1}{2}[\phi^{1}_{x}(X),X]+O(\left\lVert X\right\rVert^{3}).

The first term vanishes at
x
but the second doesn’t. So let

\phi^{2}_{x}(X)=\frac{1}{2}\phi^{1}_{x}([\phi^{1}_{x}(X),X]).

Since
\phi_{x}^{1}
is linear and positive,
\phi^{2}_{X}
is quadratic and positive. Now consider

\displaystyle\BCH(-\phi^{1}_{x}(X)-\phi^{2}_{x}(X),X)

\displaystyle=\Big(-\phi^{1}_{x}(X)+X\Big)+\Big(-\phi^{2}_{x}(X)+\frac{1}{2}[\phi^{1}_{x}(X),X]\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}[\phi^{2}_{x}(X),X]+\frac{1}{12}[\phi^{1}_{x}(X),[\phi^{1}_{x}(X),X]]+\frac{1}{12}[X,[X,\phi^{1}_{x}(X)]]+O(\left\lVert X\right\rVert^{4}).

We define the cubic part of
\psi_{x}
by

\phi^{3}_{x}(X)=\phi_{x}^{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2}[\phi^{2}_{x}(X),X]+\frac{1}{12}[\phi^{1}_{x}(X),[\phi^{1}_{x}(X),X]]+\frac{1}{12}[X,[X,\phi^{1}_{x}(X)]]\Big)

We continue this procedure until we have define
\phi^{N}_{x}
. Then let

\psi_{x}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi^{i}_{x}(X).

The construction of
\psi
implies that for any
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, there exists

such that

\BCH(-\psi_{x}(\alpha_{t}(X)),\alpha_{t}(X))=t^{1}Y_{1}+\cdots+t^{N}Y_{N}+O(t^{N+1}),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

and
Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{N}
vanish at

. Hence trivially

\natural(\BCH(-\psi_{x}(\alpha_{t}(X)),\alpha_{t}(X)))\sim_{N,x}0,

where we now view
\natural(\BCH(-\phi_{x}(\alpha_{t}(X)),\alpha_{t}(X)))\in t\mathcal{X}_{c}(M)[[t]]
. Since
\natural
satisfies (
2.5
), it follows that

\natural(\BCH(-\psi_{x}(\alpha_{t}(X)),\alpha_{t}(X)))=\BCH(-\natural(\psi_{x}(\alpha_{t}(X))),\natural(\alpha_{t}(X))).

By Proposition
A.3
, we get that

\natural(\alpha_{t}(X))\sim_{N,x}\natural(\psi_{x}(\alpha_{t}(X))).

It is clear that
\psi_{x}
is positive for any
x\in U
and that the map
\psi
depends smoothly on

. It remains to show Lemma
A.6
.d. By (

), we get that if

denotes the homogeneous part of

, then

\natural_{x}(L(X))=\natural_{x}(X),\quad\forall X\in\mathfrak{g}.

It is also clear that the homogeneous part of
\phi^{2}_{x}
is

X\mapsto\frac{1}{2}L([L(X),X]).

By Remark
2.6
,
\natural_{x}(\frac{1}{2}L([L(X),X]))=0
. Same for the homogeneous part of
\phi^{3}_{x},\cdots,\phi^{N}_{x}
. The proof of the lemma is thus complete.

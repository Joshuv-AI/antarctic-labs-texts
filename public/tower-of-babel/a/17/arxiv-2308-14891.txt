Abstract
. This paper is about one dimensional families of cyclic covers of the projective line in positive characteristic. For each such family, we study the mass formula for the number of non-ordinary curves in the family. We prove two equations for the mass formula: the first relies on tautological intersection theory; and the second relies on the a a -numbers of non-ordinary curves in the family. Our results generalize the Eichler–Deuring mass formula for supersingular elliptic curves; they also generalize some theorems of Ibukiyama, Katsura, and Oort about supersingular curves of genus 2 2 that have an automorphism of order 3 3 or order 4 4 . We determine the mass formula in many new cases, including linearized families of hyperelliptic curves of every genus and all families of cyclic covers of the projective line branched at four points. keywords: curve, hyperelliptic curve, cyclic cover, Jacobian, mass formula, cycle class, tautological ring, Hodge bundle, intersection theory, Frobenius, non-ordinary, p p -rank, a a -number. Introduction

Suppose
p
is a prime number and
k=\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
If
E
is an elliptic curve over
k
,
we say that
E
is
ordinary
if the number of
p
-torsion points on
E

is exactly
p
; if not, we say that
E
is
non-ordinary
(or
supersingular
).
The Eichler–Deuring mass formula states that

(1)

\sum_{[E]}\frac{1}{\#{\mathrm{Aut}}(E)}=\frac{p-1}{24},

where the sum is over the isomorphism classes of non-ordinary elliptic curves
E
over
k
.

Thus the number of isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves
grows linearly in
p
, with asymptotic rate of growth
(p-1)/12
.
From this, it is possible to determine the exact number of isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves;
for
p\geq 5
, this exact number is

\lfloor p/12\rfloor+\epsilon_{p}
where
\epsilon_{p}=0,1,1,2
when
p\equiv 1,5,7,11\bmod 12
respectively.
The exact number depends on the congruence of
p
modulo
12
because of the
contributions from the elliptic curves with
j
-invariants
0
and
1728
, which have extra automorphisms.

The Legendre family of elliptic curves is given by the affine equation

(2)

E_{t}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-t).

One proof of the Eichler–Deuring mass formula
uses the Cartier operator and results of Igusa to show that there are
(p-1)/2
values of
t
for which

E_{t}
is supersingular, see Remark
5.2
.
For a second proof,
one studies the divisor of zeroes of a section of
\mathrm{det}({\mathbb{E}})^{12}
,
where
{\mathbb{E}}
denotes the Hodge bundle; this divisor is supported
on the cusp at the boundary of the Legendre family.
Each of these proofs sheds light on the material covered by the other.

In this paper, we generalize the Eichler–Deuring mass formula to the setting of one dimensional
families
\mathcal{F}
of curves of genus
g
that are cyclic covers of the projective line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
over
k
.
We consider the locus
V_{g-1}
of non-ordinary abelian varieties, which is a codimension one cycle in the
moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{g}
of principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
, and its pullback
to the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of stable curves of genus
g
.
We define the mass formula for
\mathcal{F}
as the degree of the zero dimensional class obtained by intersecting
V_{g-1}

with the curve in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
determined by the family
\mathcal{F}
.

When the generic curve in the family is ordinary,
this degree gives a weighted count of the non-ordinary curves in
\mathcal{F}
. Each curve
X
is weighted with the usual reciprocal of the size of
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, and also with the multiplicity of intersection between

\mathcal{F}
and
V_{g-1}
at
X
.

In our first main result, Theorem

, we reinterpret
the mass formula in terms of a tautological intersection number.
Because of this, the mass formula admits a natural evaluation using a
tautological intersection theory computation from
[
COS
]
.
In our second main result, Theorem

, we reinterpret
the mass formula in terms of the
a
-numbers of the non-ordinary curves in the family.

We then determine the mass formula for important one dimensional families of curves.
These families generalize the Legendre family in several different ways and also
generalize earlier work in genus
2
from
[
IKO86
]
.
Specifically, we determine the mass formula for non-ordinary curves for:

•

Corollary
5.4
: Linearized one dimensional families of hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
for any
g\geq 2
.
See Definition
5.3
for the definition of linearized.

•

Corollary

: Families of cyclic covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
branched at
4
points, for any degree
d
and any inertia type.

We now give a more detailed introduction to the objects and results in the paper.

1.1.
Cyclic covers of the projective line

Let

be an algebraically closed field of characteristic

Let

be a (smooth, connected, projective) curve of genus

Let

be the Jacobian of

Let

We suppose that

admits a cyclic degree

A discrete invariant of

Then

More precisely, by Riemann’s Existence Theorem, there is a unique

It is given by the normalization of this equation at its singularities.
The inertia type determines the genus

of

Let
\tau\in\mathrm{Aut}(X)
be an automorphism of order

such that the quotient curve

is
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
With respect to (

\tau:(x,y)\mapsto(x,\zeta_{d}y)

where
\zeta_{d}
is a primitive

Let
{\it\mathrm{Aut}(X,\tau)}
denote the normalizer of

in
\mathrm{Aut}(X)
.

1.2.
Non-ordinary curves

The curve

is

if the number of

-torsion points in

is exactly

.
The ordinary condition is equivalent to the action of

If

Equivalent definitions are described in Section

There are some conditions on the inertia type and the congruence of

Under mild hypotheses (including the case that

]

1.3.
Main result

We fix the degree

and the inertia type

.
Let

denote the moduli space of admissible
\mu_{d}
-covers of a rational curve

A one dimensional family

When the general target curve of

\mu_{d}
-covers of

Let

be the moduli space of stable curves of genus

.
There is a forgetful morphism

(4)

\phi^{\mathcal{F}}:{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}}\to{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{g},

that records the source curve of the cover. Let
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}
be the image of
\phi^{\mathcal{F}}

and
{\delta^{\mathcal{F}}}
be its degree.

Let

denote the Hodge bundle.
If

is a curve of genus

over

are the holomorphic

-forms on

Let

denote its first Chern class. For a one dimensional family

of
\mu_{d}
-covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with inertia type
a

For a prime

, consider the characteristic

The locus

of non-ordinary curves

We define the

When the generic source curve in

The first is in terms of the intersection number

The second is by a weighted count of the non-ordinary curves in

Each curve

of the intersection of

Using Proposition

We collect both results in the following statement.

Main Theorem
.

-covers of a rational curve with inertia type

Suppose

\sum_{[X]}\frac{\alpha_{X}}{\#{\mathrm{Aut}}(X,\tau)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{Thm.\ \ref{thm:firsteq}}}}{{=}}\mu(\mathcal{F},p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{Thm.\ \ref{thm:secondeq}}}}{{=}}(p-1)\frac{{\mathrm{deg}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda_{1})}{\delta^{\mathcal{F}}},

Let

2.4.
The Hodge bundle and Chern classes

We refer to
[
Ful98
, Chapters 6-9]
for background on the Chow ring
A^{*}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{g})
.
Loosely speaking, the Chow ring is generated by equivalence classes of closed subspaces
under an equivalence relation called rational equivalence.
We denote the class of a subspace
S
by
[S]
.
The Chow ring is naturally graded by codimension, denoted in the upper index.
When only the additive structure is concerned, it is sometime useful to introduce a grading by dimension; in this case a graded piece is given an appropriate lower index.

Recall the morphisms
\pi
from (
10
) and
\phi
from (
11
).
Since
\pi
has generic isotropy
\mu_{d}
,
the degree of
\pi
is
1/d
, in the sense that pushing forward the fundamental class produces a factor of
1/d
:

\pi_{\ast}([{\mathcal{A}}_{d,a}])=[\overline{M}_{0,n}]/d
.
Similarly,
\phi^{\mathcal{F}}_{\ast}([{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}}])=\delta^{\mathcal{F}}[\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}]
.

The
Hodge bundle

\mathbb{E}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
is a locally free sheaf of rank
g
on
\mathcal{A}_{g}
.
For
A/S
an abelian scheme, the sections of
\mathbb{E}_{g}
are given by

e^{*}\Omega^{1}_{A/S}
on
S
, where
\Omega^{1}_{A/S}
is the
relative sheaf of differentials and
e:S\to A
is the identity section.
By
[
FC90
]
, the Hodge bundle extends to a locally free sheaf on
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
.

The Chern classes of
\mathbb{E}_{g}
are defined over
\mathbb{Z}
and can be studied over any field.
They yield classes in
A^{*}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{g})
.
We refer to
[
Ful98
, Chapter 3]
for background on the theory of Chern classes of a vector bundle.
For example, the first Chern class commutes with pullback
and the first Chern class of a line bundle may be represented by the divisor of one of its meromorphic sections.

We are especially interested in the first Chern class:

(12)

\lambda_{1}:=c_{1}(\mathbb{E}_{g})\in A^{*}(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{g}).

The class
\lambda_{1}
can be represented by the codimension one cycle given
by the divisor of a meromorphic section of
\det(\mathbb{E}_{g})
.

The Hodge bundle on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
(resp.
{\mathcal{A}}_{d,a}
) is the pullback of
\mathbb{E}_{g}

by the Torelli morphism (resp. and by
\phi_{d,a}
).
Over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, the fiber of
\mathbb{E}_{g}
over a moduli point
X
is naturally identified with
H^{0}(X,\Omega^{1})
.
A reference for the extension to the boundary
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\smallsetminus{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is
[
Mum83
]
.
On these moduli spaces, we continue to denote the Hodge bundle by
\mathbb{E}_{g}

and its first Chern class by
\lambda_{1}
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one dimensional family of admissible
\mu_{d}
-covers of a rational curve.
Then

{\mathrm{deg}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda_{1}):=\int_{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}}(\phi^{\mathcal{F}})^{\ast}(\lambda_{1}).

2.5.
Cycle class of the non-ordinary locus

Let
V_{g-1}
denote the locus on
{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
of non-ordinary abelian varieties.
This is the same as the locus of abelian varieties with positive
a
-number.

By
[
EvdG09
, Section 9, page 625]
,
the cycle class of
V_{g-1}
is
[V_{g-1}]=(p-1)\lambda_{1}
. We briefly summarize the argument.
The locus
V_{g-1}
is given by the vanishing of the map

\mathrm{det}(V):\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{E}_{g})\to\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{E}_{g}^{(p)}).

(Here
\mathbb{E}_{g}^{(p)}
denotes the Hodge bundle on the pullback of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
by the absolute Frobenius
of
\bar{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
.)
By tensoring with
(\mathbb{E}_{g})^{\vee}
,
this locus is given by the zero locus of the
section

s_{V}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{A}_{g}}\to\det(\mathbb{E}_{g})^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{E}_{g}^{(p)}).

So
[V_{g-1}]=c_{1}(\det(\mathbb{E}_{g})^{\vee}\otimes\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{E}_{g}^{(p)}))
.
By the definition of
\mathbb{E}_{g}^{(p)}
, it follows that
c_{1}(\mathbb{E}_{g}^{(p)})=p\lambda_{1}
.
The first Chern class is additive for line bundles and its sign is reversed by dualization.
So
[V_{g-1}]=-\lambda_{1}+p\lambda_{1}=(p-1)\lambda_{1}
.

3.
Main Theorems

In this section, we prove the main theorem stated in the introduction. The two equalities composing its statement
are proven individually in Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
.
We first recall our definition of the mass formula.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a one dimensional family of admissible
\mu_{d}
-covers of a rational curve with inertia type
a
.
We define the

to be

(13)

\mu(\mathcal{F},p)=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}[{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathcal{F}}]\cdot[V_{g-1}].

We assume that
p
is such that
the source curve of the generic point of the family
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}_{p}
is ordinary.
This implies that
V_{g-1}
is dimensionally transversal to
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathcal{F}}
. (Two subspaces of a given ambient space are said to be dimensionally transversal if the codimension of their intersection equals the sum of their codimensions.)
In this situation, the mass formula is the degree of a zero dimensional cycle supported on the set of
non-ordinary curves of
\mathcal{F}
.

3.1.
First main result

We evaluate the mass formula using tautological intersection theory.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
\mathcal{F}
a one dimensional family of admissible
\mu_{d}
-covers of a rational curve with inertia type
a
,
and
p\nmid d
a prime such that the generic curve in the characteristic
p

\mathcal{F}_{p}
is ordinary,
we have:

(14)

\mu(\mathcal{F},p)=(p-1){\mathrm{deg}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda_{1})/\delta^{\mathcal{F}},

where the symbols on the right hand side are as in Definitions
2.7
,
2.11
.

Proof.

By (
13
),
\mu(\mathcal{F},p)=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}[{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathcal{F}}]\cdot[V_{g-1}]
.
The intersection
[\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}]\cdot[V_{g-1}]
determines a class in the Chow ring of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. We compute its degree.

By
[
FvdG04
, Theorem 2.3]
, every component of
V_{g-1}
has dimension
3g-4
.
Furthermore, the generic point of each component of
V_{g-1}
represents a smooth curve
by
[
AP08
, Lemma 3.2]
.
By
[
EvdG09
, Section 9, page 625]
, the class of the non-ordinary locus
[V_{g-1}]
on
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is
(p-1)\lambda_{1}
.
So the class
[V_{g-1}]
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
equals
(p-1)\lambda_{1}\in A^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})
.

Since
\phi^{\mathcal{F}}_{\ast}([{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}}])=\delta^{\mathcal{F}}[\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}]
,
in the Chow ring of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, we have:

(15)

[\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{F}}]\cdot[V_{g-1}]=\frac{\phi_{\ast}^{\mathcal{F}}([{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}}])}{\delta^{\mathcal{F}}}\cdot(p-1)\lambda_{1}.

It thus suffices to show that the degree of
\phi_{\ast}^{\mathcal{F}}([{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}}])\cdot\lambda_{1}
equals

{\mathrm{deg}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda_{1})
.
In order to compute the degree of this class,
consider the commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi^{\mathcal{F}}}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{c}}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where
\tilde{c}
and

are constant functions.
In the next formula (

), the
\lambda_{1}

Using the projection formula for the morphism

\phi_{\ast}^{\mathcal{F}}([{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}}])\cdot\lambda_{1}={\phi_{\ast}^{\mathcal{F}}}\left([{\mathcal{A}^{\mathcal{F}}}]\cdot\lambda_{1}\right)={\phi_{\ast}^{\mathcal{F}}}\left(\lambda_{1}\right).

3.3.
Second main result

We evaluate the mass formula using

Theorem 3.5
.

For
\mathcal{F}
a one dimensional family of admissible
\mu_{d}
-covers of a rational curve with inertia type

,
and
p\nmid d
a prime such that the generic curve in the characteristic
p

\mathcal{F}_{p}

\mu(\mathcal{F},p)=\sum_{[X]}\frac{\alpha_{X}}{\#{\mathrm{Aut}}(X,\tau)},

where the sum is over the isomorphism classes of non-ordinary curves

\mathcal{F}_{p}

Furthermore,
\alpha_{X}\geq a(X)

Proof.

The hypothesis on

is dimensionally transversal to

By Proposition

\frac{m_{X}}{\#\mathrm{Aut}(X)}=\frac{\alpha_{X}[\mathrm{Aut}(X,\tau):\mathrm{Aut}(X)]}{\#\mathrm{Aut}(X)}=\frac{\alpha_{X}}{\#\mathrm{Aut}(X,\tau)}.

Substituting this in (

To compare

with

without loss of generality, we can suppose the matrix for the Cartier operator is in rational canonical form.
A local equation for the determinant is given by the product of the determinants of the blocks in the matrix.
Then

while the

This proves that
\alpha_{X}\geq a(X)
.

Suppose

and
p\equiv 1\bmod d
.
The values in the inertia type

have the property that

is a multiple of
d
.
Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{4}
equals

or
2d
.
(Since

, the sum is less than

.
If

, we can adjust to the inertia type

The hypothesis that

implies that there is a basis for

for which

. ∎

4.
Evaluating the class for cyclic covers

In this section, we review results from
[
COS
]
. Let
d\geq 2
and
n\geq 4
.
Recall that
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is a tuple of integers with
0<a_{i}<d
, whose sum is congruent to
0
modulo
d
.

4.1.
Cyclic covers branched at four points

A natural class of examples is given by moduli spaces of cyclic covers
that are branched at exactly
n=4
points.
In this situation,
we lighten the notation by suppressing the superscript
\mathcal{F}
.
So
{\mathcal{A}}_{d,a}
denotes the moduli space of (admissible)

\mu_{d}
-covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
branched at
n=4
points with inertia type
a
.
In this case,
[
COS
]
gives an explicit formula for the degree of
\lambda_{1}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
COS
, Theorem 1.2]

When
n=4
, the degree of
\lambda_{1}
on
{\mathcal{A}}_{d,a}
is

(19)

{\mathrm{deg}}_{d,a}(\lambda_{1})=\frac{1}{12d^{2}}\left(d^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{4}{\rm gcd}^{2}(a_{i},d)+\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\rm gcd}^{2}(a_{i}+a_{4},d)\right).

This theorem is proven using Atiyah-Bott localization to obtain linear relations between the degree of
\lambda_{1}
and the degrees of some zero-dimensional boundary strata in the space of admissible covers. Alternatively, one may deduce this formula from a stacky Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch computation, as done in
[
BR11
, Proposition 10.20]
in the hyperelliptic case.

4.2.
Cyclic covers with more than four branch points

In
[
COS
]
, the authors also evaluate the class
\lambda_{1}

in the higher dimensional case when the covers have more than four branch points;
this generalization is proven inductively with the base case being Theorem
4.1
.
We will need this result in Section
5
, in a situation when the base of the family is not all of
{\mathcal{A}}_{d,a}
.

First we define some notation.
Suppose
J
is a subset of
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
2\leq|J|\leq n-2
.
Then
\Delta_{J}
denotes the boundary divisor whose generic point represents
a
\mu_{d}
-cover of a rational curve with two irreducible components, intersecting in one node,
with the branch points labeled by
J
on one component, and the branch points labeled by
J^{c}
on the other. Note that
\Delta_{J}=\Delta_{J^{c}}
and therefore each divisor is counted twice when summing over all allowed subsets of
[n]
.

We need some other definitions.
Consider the universal curve
\pi:\mathcal{C}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
and the
section
\sigma_{i}
of
\pi
whose image on an
n
-marked curve is the
i
th marked point.
Define
\psi_{i}=c_{1}(\sigma_{i}^{*}(\omega_{\pi}))
, where
\omega_{\pi}
is the relative dualizing sheaf.

The general point
x
of
\mathcal{C}_{g,n}
can naturally be thought of as an
(n+1)
-th mark on the
n
-pointed curve
\pi(x)
.
This gives a birational morphism
\mathcal{C}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}
which extends to an isomorphism.
Thus we can view
\pi=\pi_{n+1}

as the projection map that forgets the data of the last marked point.
Define
\kappa_{1}=\pi_{n+1,*}(\psi_{n+1}^{2})
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
COS
, Theorem 1.3]

The class
\lambda_{1}
on
{\mathcal{A}}_{d,a}
admits the following tautological representation:

(20)

\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{24d}\left(\sum_{J\in\mathcal{P}([n])}{\gcd}^{2}\left(\sum_{j\in J}a_{i},d\right)\Delta_{J}\right),

where the sum runs over subsets
J
of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and

•

if
2\leq|J|\leq n-2
, then
\Delta_{J}
denotes the boundary divisor described above;

•

if
J=\{j\}
or
J=[n]\smallsetminus\{j\}
for some
1\leq j\leq n
, then
\Delta_{J}:=-\psi_{j}
;

•

if
J=\phi
or
J=[n]
, then
\Delta_{J}:=\kappa_{1}
.

5.
Linearized families of hyperelliptic curves of every genus

In Section
5.1
, we show that our mass formula generalizes the Eichler–Deuring mass formula.
Then, we determine the mass formula for a one dimensional family of hyperelliptic curves of every genus
g\geq 2
.

Here is a fact used in this section, and in Sections
7
and
8
:
if
X
is a hyperelliptic curve, with hyperelliptic involution denoted
\tau
, then
\tau
is in the center of

\mathrm{Aut}(X)
, so
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\tau)=\mathrm{Aut}(X)
; in this situation, the
reduced automorphism group is
\mathrm{redAut}(X):=\mathrm{Aut}(X)/\langle\tau\rangle
.

5.1.
The Legendre family as an example

Suppose
d=2
,
n=4
, and
a=(1,1,1,1)
. Then the family (
3
)
specializes to the Legendre family of elliptic curves
E_{t}:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-t)
from (
2
).

Lemma 5.1
.

In the case of the Legendre family (
2
),
Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
specialize to the Eichler–Deuring mass formula (
1
).

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
,
{\mathrm{deg}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda_{1})=1/4
; unfortunately, the proof
of that result does not simplify in this case.
We compute
\delta_{2,(1,1,1,1)}=6
: one of the ramification points is the marked point
on the elliptic curve and there are
3!
labelings of the other three ramification points.
By Theorem
3.5
,
\mu(\mathcal{F},p)=(p-1)/24
.

In the Legendre family (
2
),
\tau
is the hyperelliptic involution,
so
{\mathrm{Aut}}(E_{t},\tau)={\mathrm{Aut}}(E_{t})
.
Also, if
E_{t}
is non-ordinary, its
a
-number is
a(E_{t})=1
.
By Theorem
3.5
,
\mu(\mathcal{F},p)=\sum_{[E]}\frac{1}{\#{\rm Aut}(E)}
,
where the sum is over the isomorphism classes of non-ordinary elliptic curves
E
over
k
.
By equating these two expressions for
\mu(\mathcal{F},p)
, we obtain (
1
).
∎

There are many proofs of the Eichler–Deuring mass formula; our proof of the Main Theorem is most similar
to the one in
[

, Corollary 12.4.6, page 358]
.
Other proofs can be deduced from:
the separability of the Deuring polynomial
[
Hus04
, Theorem 4.1, chapter 13]
;
a comparison of the

-adic étale Euler characteristic of the modular curve

in characteristic

and characteristic

a computation of the constant term of the weight two Eisenstein series on

.

Remark 5.2
.

The number of isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curves
determines (and is determined by) (

), given
some additional information about elliptic curves with extra automorphisms,
as seen in
[

.
This idea shows up again for curves of genus

in Sections

and

.
It is no longer easy to switch between masses and cardinalities when

5.2.
The mass formula for linearized families of hyperelliptic curves

Definition 5.3
.

Suppose
h(x)\in k[x]
is a separable polynomial of degree
2g+1
.
A
linearized family of hyperelliptic curves
is a one dimensional family
\mathcal{F}_{h(x)}
of hyperelliptic curves of genus

whose generic fiber is given by the affine equation

(21)

X_{t}:y^{2}=h(x)(x-t).

We assume that the roots of
h(x)
do not satisfy any
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(k)
symmetry.
We also assume that the generic curve in the characteristic
p

\mathcal{F}_{h(x)}
is ordinary.

Here is our first corollary of the main theorem.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
g\geq 2
and
p
be odd.
Suppose

is a separable polynomial of degree

whose set of roots is not stabilized by any

\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(k)

Suppose

is a prime such that the generic curve in the characteristic

\mathcal{F}_{h(x)}:y^{2}=h(x)(x-t)

The proof of Corollary

is in Section

.

Remark 5.5
.

In
[

, page 381]
, Yui provides a method to compute the Cartier operator for the curve

.
One can use this method to show that there are at most

values of

for which

is non-ordinary in the family (

).
For a generic non-ordinary hyperelliptic curve

of genus

, it is known that:

by
[

, Theorem 1.1(ii)]
; and the curve

has

and
and

by

, Theorem 1]
.
However, this information is not sufficient to prove Corollary

.

5.3.
Proof of mass formula for linearized hyperelliptic families

In the proof of Corollary
5.4
, we use the following facts about
tautological classes on admissible covers and
\overline{M}_{0,n}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
g\geq 2
and
d=2
. Let
a=(1,\ldots,1)
be a tuple of length
n=2g+2
.
Consider the space of hyperelliptic admissible covers
\mathcal{A}_{2,a}
and the tautological morphism
\phi_{2,a}:\mathcal{A}_{2,a}\to\overline{M}_{0,2g+2}
of degree
1/2
.
Then:

(1)

On the universal family of hyperelliptic covers,
consider a (marked) ramification point
r
and its corresponding branch point
b
.
The class
\psi_{r}
is restricted from
\overline{M}_{g,2g+2}
,
whereas the class
\psi_{b}
is in
A^{1}(\overline{M}_{0,2g+2})
. We have:

\psi_{r}=\phi_{2,a}^{\ast}\psi_{b}/2
.

(2)

Suppose
i\in[n]
. Choose two distinct values
j,k\in[n]-\{i\}
.
Then the class
\psi_{i}\in A^{1}(\overline{M}_{0,n})
is represented by the following linear combination of boundary divisors:

\psi_{i}=\sum_{\begin{array}[]{c}I\subset[n]\mbox{ such that }i\in I\\
j,k\not\in I\end{array}}\Delta_{I}.

(3)

The following relation holds in
A^{1}(\overline{M}_{0,n})
:

\kappa_{1}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\psi_{i}-\Delta_{tot},

where
\Delta_{tot}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{2\leq|J|\leq n-2}\Delta_{J}
denotes the sum of all boundary divisors.

Proof.

The first statement is the specialization to the degree
2
setting of
[
Ion02
, Lemma 1.17]
. The second statement follows from the initial condition
\psi_{i}=0
on
\overline{M}_{0,\{i,j,k\}}
and the relation for
\psi
classes when pulled-back via forgetful morphisms
[
Koc01
, Lemma 1.3.1]
. The third statement follows from the initial condition
\kappa_{1}=0
on
\overline{M}_{0,3}
and the relation for
\kappa
classes when pulled-back via forgetful morphisms
[
Koc01
, Lemma 2.2.3]
.
The last two statements can be found in a different form in
[
AC96
, Formulas (1.9), (1.10)]
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
5.4
.

Write
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{h(x)}
.
By Theorem
3.2
,

\mu(\mathcal{F},p)=(p-1){\mathrm{deg}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda_{1})/\delta^{\mathcal{F}}
.
By hypothesis, there is no automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
that stabilizes the set of roots of
h(x)
;
it follows that
\delta^{\mathcal{F}}=1
.
It thus suffices to prove that
{\mathrm{deg}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\lambda_{1})=g/4
.

We compute the intersection numbers of the family
\mathcal{F}
with the tautological divisors appearing on the right hand side of (
20
). Let
b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 2g+1
denote the roots of
h(x)
.
Then:

(1)

If
2\leq|J|\leq 2g
, then
\mathcal{F}\cdot\Delta_{J}=0
unless
J
or
J^{c}
is
\{i,2g+2\}
for some
1\leq i\leq 2g+1
.
The reason is that the only branch point that moves in
\mathcal{F}
is the last one.
So the only time
\mathcal{F}
hits a boundary divisor is when the last branch point specializes to one of the others.

(2)

If
J=\{i,2g+2\}
for some
1\leq i\leq 2g+1
, then
\mathcal{F}\cdot\Delta_{J}=\frac{1}{2}[pt]
.
This intersection occurs when
t
specializes to the branch point
b_{i}
. The singular curve
has two components, with genera
0
and
g-1
, intersecting in two ordinary double points;
on each component, these two points are an orbit for the restriction of the hyperelliptic involution.
The coefficient
1/2=2/(2\cdot 2)
accounts for the hyperelliptic involution on each component, and the two choices of ways to identify the two points on the two components.

(3)

If
i\not=2g+2
, then
\mathcal{F}\cdot\psi_{i}=\frac{1}{2}[pt]
. To see this,
we first use Lemma
5.6
, (1) to work in
A^{1}(\overline{M}_{0,2g+2})
.
We use Lemma
5.6
(2) to replace
\psi_{i}
by a linear combination of boundary divisors.
We choose
j=2g+2
and choose
k
arbitrarily.
Then the only boundary divisor
\Delta_{I}
intersecting
\mathcal{F}
is when
I=[n]-\{k,2g+2\}
.
This is because the branch points labeled by
2g+2
and
k
must be on the second component.
Since the branch point labeled with
2g+2
is
t
and
t
can only specialize to one other branch point,
all the other branch points must be on the first component.
The result then follows from part (2).

(4)

We compute
\mathcal{F}\cdot\psi_{2g+2}=\frac{2g-1}{2}[pt]
.
The proof begins in the same way as for part (3).
In this case, we choose
j=1
and
k=2
.
If a boundary divisor
\Delta_{I}
in the expression of
\psi_{2g+2}
intersects
\mathcal{F}
, then the marked branch point
t
must be on a component alone with only one other marked branch point
b_{i}
, which must be different from
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
.
There are
2g-1
possible choices for
i\in\{3,\ldots 2g+1\}
and therefore
2g-1
divisors
\Delta_{I}
intersecting
\mathcal{F}
,
with
I=\{i,2g+2\}
. The result then follows from part (2).

(5)

We compute
\mathcal{F}\cdot\kappa_{1}=\frac{2g-1}{2}[pt]
.
To see this, note that
\kappa_{1}=\psi_{2g+2}+\sum_{i=1}^{2g+1}\psi_{i}-\Delta_{tot}
by Lemma
5.6
(3).
By part (3),
\mathcal{F}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{2g+1}\psi_{i}=\frac{2g+1}{2}[pt]
.
By part (1),
\mathcal{F}\cdot\Delta_{tot}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 2g+1}\mathcal{F}\cdot\Delta_{\{i,2g+2\}}
, which equals
\frac{2g-1}{2}[pt]
by part (2). By part (4),
\mathcal{F}\cdot\psi_{2g+2}=\frac{2g-1}{2}[pt]
.

Finally, we substitute these intersection numbers in (
20
).
The coefficient
\mathrm{gcd}^{2}(\sum_{i\in J}a_{i},d)
equals
1
if
|J|
is odd and equals
4
if
|J|
is even.
For
1\leq s\leq 2g+2
, let
C_{s}=\sum_{|J|=s}\mathcal{F}\cdot\Delta_{J}
.
By definition,
C_{s}=C_{2g+2-s}
.
By part (1),
C_{s}=0
for
3\leq s\leq 2g-1
.
So

\int_{\mathcal{F}}\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{24}\cdot\frac{2}{2}(4C_{2}+C_{1}+4C_{0}).

By part (1),

.
By parts (3)-(4),

.
By part (5),

.
This yields that
\int_{\mathcal{F}}\lambda_{1}=g/4
.
∎

The papers

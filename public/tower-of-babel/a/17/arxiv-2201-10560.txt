Proof of Theorem

Suppose that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(f_{1}(z)+i(f_{2}(z+c)-f_{2}(z)))(f_{1}(z)-i(f_{2}(z+c)-f_{2}(z)))=1\\
(f_{2}(z)+i(f_{1}(z+c)-f_{1}(z)))(f_{2}(z)-i(f_{1}(z+c)-f_{1}(z)))=1.\end{cases}

(3.1)

From the above equations we see that

have no zeros in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, where
i\neq j
,

.

Since
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
are transcendental entire functions with finite order, there exist polynomials
p_{1}(z)
,
p_{2}(z)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f_{1}(z)+i(f_{2}(z+c)-f_{2}(z))=e^{p_{1}(z)}\\
f_{1}(z)-i(f_{2}(z+c)-f_{2}(z))=e^{-p_{1}(z)}\\
f_{2}(z)+i(f_{1}(z+c)-f_{1}(z))=e^{p_{2}(z)}\\
f_{2}(z)-i(f_{1}(z+c)-f_{1}(z))=e^{-p_{2}(z)}.\end{cases}

(3.2)

In view of (
3.2

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f_{1}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{p_{1}(z)}+e^{-p_{1}(z)}\right)\\
f_{2}(z+c)-f_{2}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}\left(e^{p_{1}(z)}-e^{-p_{1}(z)}\right)\\
f_{2}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{p_{2}(z)}+e^{-p_{2}(z)}\right)\\
f_{1}(z+c)-f_{1}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}\left(e^{p_{2}(z)}-e^{-p_{2}(z)}\right).\end{cases}

(3.3)

Form (
3.3
), we can easily obtain the following two equations:

\displaystyle-ie^{p_{2}(z)+p_{1}(z+c)}-ie^{p_{2}(z)-p_{1}(z+c)}+ie^{p_{2}(z)+p_{1}(z)}+ie^{p_{2}(z)-p_{1}(z)}+e^{2p_{2}(z)}=1

\displaystyle-ie^{p_{1}(z)+p_{2}(z+c)}-ie^{p_{1}(z)-p_{2}(z+c)}+ie^{p_{1}(z)+p_{2}(z)}+ie^{p_{1}(z)-p_{2}(z)}+e^{2p_{1}(z)}=1.

(3.5)

Now we consider the following two cases.

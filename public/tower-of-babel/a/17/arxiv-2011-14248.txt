The 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -Selberg integral
Richárd Rimányi ⋄ and Alexander Varchenko ⋆

Abstract
. We prove an 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -Selberg integral formula, in which the 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -Selberg integral is an element of the finite field 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} with odd prime number p p of elements. The formula is motivated by analogy between multidimensional hypergeometric solutions of the KZ equations and polynomial solutions of the same equations reduced modulo p p .

Preliminary remarks

2.1.
Lucas’ Theorem

Theorem 2.1

(
[
L
]
)
.

For nonnegative integers
m
and
n
and a prime
p
,
the following congruence relation holds:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\binom{n}{m}\equiv\prod_{i=0}^{a}\binom{n_{i}}{m_{i}}\quad(\operatorname{mod}\ p),

where
m=m_{b}p^{b}+m_{b-1}p^{b-1}+\cdots+m_{1}p+m_{0}

and
n=n_{b}p^{b}+n_{b-1}p^{b-1}+\cdots+n_{1}p+n_{0}

are the base
p
expansions of
m
and
n
respectively. This uses the convention that

\binom{n}{m}=0
if
n<m
.
∎

2.2.
Binomial lemma

Lemma 2.2

(
[
V7
]
)
.

Let
a,b
be positive integers such that
a<p
,
b<p
,
p\leqslant a+b
. Then we have an identity in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle b\,\binom{b-1}{a+b-p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\,\binom{b-1}{p-a-1}=(-1)^{a+1}\,\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b-p)!}\,.

∎

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle b\binom{b-1}{p-a-1}=\frac{b(b-1)\cdots(a+b-p+1)}{1\cdots(p-a-1)}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}=\frac{b\cdots(a+b-p+1)(a+b-p)!a!}{(-1)^{p-a-1}(p-1)(p-2)\cdots(a+1)\,a!\,(a+b-p)!}=(-1)^{a+1}\,\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b-p)!}\,.

∎

2.3.
Cancellation of factorials

Lemma 2.3
.

If
a,b
are nonnegative integers and
a+b=p-1
, then in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle a!\,b!\,=\,(-1)^{a+1}\,.

Proof.

We have
a!=(-1)^{a}(p-1)\dots(p-a)
and
p-a=b+1
. Hence

a!\,b!=(-1)^{a}(p-1)!=(-1)^{a+1}
by Wilson’s Theorem.
∎

3.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Integrals

3.1.
Definition

Let
p
be an odd prime number and
M
an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-module. Let
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
be a polynomial
with coefficients in
M
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=\sum_{d}\,c_{d}\,x_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots x_{k}^{d_{k}}.

Let
l=(l_{1},\dots,l_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{k}
. The coefficient

c_{l_{1}p-1,\dots,l_{k}p-1}
is called the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral over the cycle
[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}

and is denoted by
\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
i=1,\dots,k-1
we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{i+1},l_{i},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{i+1},x_{i},\dots,x_{k})dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaa}=\ \int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{k}\,.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
i=1,\dots,k
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{[l_{1},\dots,l_{k}]_{p}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial x_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=0\,.

∎

3.2.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Beta integral

For nonnegative integers the classical beta integral formula says

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx=\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b+1)!}\,.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
V7
]
)
.

Let

a<p
,
b<p
,
p-1\leqslant a+b
.
Then in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{[1]_{p}}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx\,=\,-\,\frac{a!\,b!}{(a+b-p+1)!}\,.

If
a+b<p-1
, then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{[1]_{p}}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx\,=0\,.

Proof.

We have

x^{a}(1-x)^{b}=\sum_{k=0}^{b}(-1)^{k}\binom{b}{k}x^{k}\,,

and need
a+k=p-1
. Hence
k=p-1-a
and

\displaystyle\int_{[1]_{p}}x^{a}(1-x)^{b}dx\,=(-1)^{p-1-a}\binom{b}{p-1-a}.

Now Lemma
2.2
implies (
3.4
). Formula (
3.5
) is clear.
∎

4.
n
-Dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral

4.1.
n
-Dimensional integral formulas

The
n
-dimensional Selberg integral formulas for nonnegative integers
a,b,c
are

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dots\int_{0}^{1}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1)!}\,,

and for
k=1,\dots,n-1
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\dots\int_{0}^{1}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle\phantom{aa}=\,\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{a+(n-j)c+1}{a+b+(2n-j-1)c+1}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+2)!}\,,

[
Se1
,
Ao
,
AAR
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
a,b,c
are nonnegative integers such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle p-1\leqslant a+b+(n-1)c,\qquad a+b+(2n-2)c<2p-1\ .

Then we have an integral formula in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}=(-1)^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}\,.

Also, if
k=1,\dots,n-1
, and

(4.5)

\displaystyle p-1\leqslant a+b+(n-1)c,\qquad a+b+(2n-2)c<2p-2\ ,

then

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle\phantom{a}=\,(-1)^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{a+(n-j)c+1}{a+b+(2n-j-1)c+2}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}\,.

The first proof of Theorem
4.1
is given in
Sections
4.2
-
4.4
, the second in Section
5
,
and the third one is sketched in Section
6
.

Remark
.

Formula (
4.4
) can be rewritten as

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}\ \prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaaaa}=\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}\,.

Remark
.

The fact that the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral in the left-hand side of (

product in the right-hand side of (

the alternating product in the right-hand side is exactly the alternating product staying in the classical formula
(
4.1
) with just several of factorials shifted by

.

) is related to the

and is called the Selberg integrals of type

,

We call the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral (

-Selberg integral of type

.
The
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral of type

Remark
.

The integral analogous to (
4.4
) but with
x_{i}-x_{j}
factors raised to an odd power vanishes:

(4.8)

\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c+1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}=0.

Indeed, after expanding the
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2c+1}
factor, the integral (

) equals

\sum_{m=0}^{2c+1}(-1)^{m+1}\binom{2c+1}{m}\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}x_{1}^{a+m}x_{2}^{a+(2c+1-m)}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\ dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}=0,

with

x_{1}
and

and

cancel each other, making the sum 0.

4.2.
Auxiliary lemmas

Denote

(4.9)

\displaystyle P_{n}(a,b,c)\,=\,(-1)^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}\,.

The polynomial

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},a,b,c)=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}

is called the
master polynomial
. Denote

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}\,,

\displaystyle S_{k,n}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}\,,

for
k=0,\dots,n
. Then
S_{0,n}(a,b,c)=S_{n}(a,b,c)
,
S_{n,n}(a,b,c)=S_{n}(a+1,b,c)
.
By (
3.2
), we also have

\displaystyle S_{k,n}(a,b,c)=\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{\sigma_{i}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a}(1-x_{i})^{b}\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}\,

for any
1\leqslant\sigma_{1}<\dots<\sigma_{k}\leqslant n
.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have
S_{n}(a,b+p,c)=S_{n}(a,b,c)
.

Proof.

We have
(1-x_{i})^{b+p}=(1-x_{i})^{b}(1-x_{i})^{p}=(1-x_{i})^{b}(1-x_{i}^{p})
. Hence the factors

(1-x_{i})^{b}
and
(1-x_{i})^{b+p}
contribute to the coefficient of
x_{i}^{p-1}
in the same way.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
a+b+(2n-2)c<2p-2
and
c>0
, then
n<p
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

If
a+b+(n-1)c<p-1
, then
S_{n}(a,b,c)=0
.

Proof.

The coefficient
of
x_{1}^{p-1}\dots x_{n}^{p-1}
in the expansion of
\Phi(a,b,c)
equals zero.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

If
p\leqslant a+(n-1)c
, then
S_{n}(a,b,c)=0
.

Proof.

Expand
\Phi(x,a,b,c)
in monomials
x_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{d_{n}}
.
If
p\leqslant a+(n-1)c
, then each monomial
x_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{d_{n}}

in the expansion has at least one of
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
greater than
p-1
,
Hence the coefficient of
x_{1}^{p-1}\dots x_{n}^{p-1}
in the expansion equals zero.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

If
a+b+(2n-2)c<2p-1
, then
S_{n}(a,b,c)=S_{n}(b,a,c)
.

Proof.

Expand
\Phi(x,a,b,c)
in monomials
x_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{d_{n}}
.
If
a+b+(2n-2)c<2p-1
, then

(a) for each monomial
x_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{d_{n}}

in the expansion all of
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
are less than
2p-1
.

We also have

\displaystyle\Phi(1-y_{1},\dots,1-y_{n},a,b,c)=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}^{a}(1-y_{i})^{b}\,.

This transformation does not change the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral due to Lucas’ Theorem
and property (a), see a similar reasoning in the proof of
[
V5
, Lemma 5.2]
.
∎

4.3.
Case
a+b+(n-1)c=p-1

Lemma 4.7
.

If
a+b+(n-1)c=p-1
, then

(4.10)

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,c)=(-1)^{bn+cn(n-1)/2}\,\frac{(cn)!}{(c!)^{n}}\,.

Proof.

If
a+b+(n-1)c=p-1
, then
S_{n}(a,b,c)
equals
(-1)^{bn}
multiplied by the coefficient of
(x_{1}\dots x_{n})^{c}
in

\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}
, which equals
(-1)^{cn(n-1)/2}\frac{(cn)!}{(c!)^{n}}
by Dyson’s formula

(4.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{C.T.}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(1-x_{i}/x_{j})^{c}(1-x_{j}/x_{i})^{c}=\frac{(cn)!}{(c!)^{n}}\,.

Here C.T. denotes the constant term. See the formula in
[
AAR
, Section 8.8]
.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

If
a+b+(n-1)c=p-1
, then

(4.12)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaaa}P_{n}(a,b,c)\,=\,(-1)^{bn+cn(n-1)/2}\,\frac{(cn)!}{(c!)^{n}}\,.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle P_{n}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{c!\,(2c)!\dots((n-1)c)!}\,.

By Lemma
2.2
we have
a!\,(b+(n-1)c)!=(-1)^{b+(n-1)c+1}
,

(a+c)!\,(b+(n-2)c)!=(-1)^{b+(n-2)c+1}
, and so on. This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
prove formula (

) for

.

4.4.
Aomoto recursion

We follow the paper
[

]
, where recurrence
relations were developed for the classical Selberg integral.
See also
[

, Section 8.2]
.

Using Lemma
3.2
, for
k=1,\dots,n
we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\Big[(1-x_{1})\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\,\Phi(x,a,b,c)\Big]dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+1)\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}(1-x_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{k}x_{i}\,\Phi(x,a,b,c)\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle-(b+1)\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\,\Phi(x,a,b,c)\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

\displaystyle+2c\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{1-x_{1}}{x_{1}-x_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\,\Phi(x,a,b,c)\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}\,.

Lemma 4.9
.

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\frac{1}{x_{1}-x_{j}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\,\Phi(x,a,b,c)\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

equals
0
if
2\leqslant j\leqslant k
and equals
S_{k-1,n}/2
if
k<j\leqslant n
.
The
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-integral

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}\frac{x_{1}}{x_{1}-x_{j}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}\,\Phi(x,a,b,c)\,dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}

equals
S_{k,n}/2
if
2\leqslant j\leqslant k
and equals
S_{k,n}
if
k<j\leqslant n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
each of these integrals does not change if
x_{1},x_{j}
are permuted.
The four statements of the lemma hold since
\frac{x_{1}x_{j}}{x_{1}-x_{j}}+\frac{x_{1}x_{j}}{x_{j}-x_{1}}=0
,

\frac{x_{1}}{x_{1}-x_{j}}+\frac{x_{j}}{x_{j}-x_{1}}=1
,

\frac{x_{1}^{2}x_{j}}{x_{1}-x_{j}}+\frac{x_{1}x_{j}^{2}}{x_{j}-x_{1}}=x_{1}x_{j}
,

\frac{x_{1}^{2}}{x_{1}-x_{j}}+\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{x_{j}-x_{1}}=x_{1}+x_{j}
, respectively.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

For
k=1,\dots,n
we have

(4.16)

\displaystyle S_{k,n}\,=\,\frac{a+(n-k)c+1}{a+b+(2n-k-1)c+2}\,S_{k-1,n}\,.

Proof.

Using Lemma
4.9
we rewrite (
4.13
) as

\displaystyle 0=(a+1)S_{k-1,n}-(a+b+2)S_{k,n}+c(n-k)S_{k-1,n}-c(2n-k-1)S_{k,n}\,.

∎

The statements (

) for different values of

Proposition 4.11
.

Assuming inequalities (
4.5
) let
\alpha=(a+1)/c-1
,
\beta=(b+1)/c-1

(4.17)

\int_{[1,\ldots,1]_{p}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-t)\cdot\Phi(x,a,b,c)dx_{1}\ldots dx_{n}=\frac{n!c^{n}\cdot S_{n}(a,b,c)}{\prod_{i=n-1}^{2n-2}(a+b+ic+2)}\cdot P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(1-2t).

The proof is the same is in
[
Ao
]
: After expanding

from (

5.
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral from Morris’ identity

5.1.
Morris’ identity

In this section we work out the integral formula (
4.4
) for the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral from
Morris’ identity.
Suppose that
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are nonnegative integers. Then

(5.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{C.T.}\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-x_{i})^{\alpha}(1-1/x_{i})^{\beta}\prod_{1\leqslant j\neq k\leqslant n}(1-x_{j}/x_{k})^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\phantom{a}=\,\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(j\gamma)!}{\gamma!}\,\frac{(\alpha+\beta+(j-1)\gamma)!}{(\alpha+(j-1)\gamma)!\,(\beta+(j-1)\gamma)!}\,.

Morris identity was deduced in
[
Mo
]
from the integral formula (
4.1
)
for the classical Selberg integral, see
[
AAR
, Section 8.8]
.

The left-hand side of (
5.1
) can be written as

(5.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{C.T.}\,(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}\gamma+n\beta}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{-\beta-(n-1)\gamma}(1-x_{i})^{\alpha+\beta}\,,

while

(5.3)

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,c)\,=\,\operatorname{C.T.}\,\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a+1-p}(1-x_{i})^{b}\,,

where the constant term is projected to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Putting
a+1-p=-\beta-(n-1)\gamma,\,b=\alpha+\beta\,,c=\gamma
,
or

(5.4)

\displaystyle\alpha=a+b+(n-1)c+1-p,\qquad\beta=p-a-(n-1)c-1,\qquad\gamma=c.

we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

If the nonnegative integers
a,b,c
satisfy the inequalities

(5.5)

\displaystyle p-1\leqslant a+b+(n-1)c,\qquad a+(n-1)c\leqslant p-1,

then the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral is given by the formula:

(5.6)

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,c)\,=\,(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}c+na}

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}\times\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(b+(j-1)c)!}{(p-a-(n-j)c-1)!\,(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}\,,

where the integer on the right-hand side is projected
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If both inequalities (
4.3
) and (
5.5
) hold,
that is, if

(5.7)

\displaystyle p-1\leqslant a+b+(n-1)c,\qquad a+b+(2n-2)c<2p-1\ ,

(5.8)

\displaystyle a+(n-1)c\leqslant p-1,

then
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}c+na}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(b+(j-1)c)!}{(p-a-(n-j)c-1)!\,(a+b+(n+j-2)c)!}\,,

\displaystyle\phantom{aa}=\,(-1)^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}\,,

and hence (
5.6
)

(5.10)

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,c)=(-1)^{n}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!\,(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1-p)!}\,.

Notice that by Lemma
4.5
we have
S_{n}(a,b,c)=0
if inequality (
5.8
)
does not hold.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(p-a-(n-j)c-1)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(a+(n-j)c)!}{(p-a-(n-j)c-1)!\,(a+(n-j)c)!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{a+(n-j)c+1}(a+(n-j)c)!\,,

by Lemma
2.3
. This implies the Lemma
5.2
.
∎

5.2.
More on values of
S_{n}(a,b,c)

Theorem 5.3
.

If inequalities (
5.5
) hold and
a=p-1-(n-1)c-k
, then

(5.11)

\displaystyle S_{n}(p-1-(n-1)c-k,b,c)\,=\,(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}c+na}\,\frac{(nc)!}{(c!)^{n}}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\binom{b+(j-1)c}{k}}{\binom{(j-1)c+k}{k}}\,,

where the integer in the right-hand side is projected
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
∎

Notice that the projections to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of the binomial coefficients
\binom{b+(j-1)c}{k}

can be calculated by Lucas’s Theorem and both integers in the binomial coefficients

\binom{(j-1)c+k}{k}
are nonnegative and less than
p
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{(\alpha+\beta+(j-1)\gamma)!}{(\alpha+(j-1)\gamma)!\,(\beta+(j-1)\gamma)!}=\binom{\alpha+\beta+(j-1)\gamma}{\beta}\prod_{i=1}^{(j-1)\gamma}\frac{1}{\beta+i}\,

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}=\,\binom{b+(j-1)c}{p-a-(n-1)c-1}\prod_{i=1}^{(j-1)c}\frac{1}{p-a-(n-1)c-1+i}\,.

If
a=p-1-(n-1)c-k
, then this equals

\displaystyle\binom{b+(j-1)c}{k}\prod_{i=1}^{(j-1)c}\frac{1}{k+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{b+(j-1)c}{k}\frac{k!}{((j-1)c)!\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}((j-1)c+i)}\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{((j-1)c)!}\frac{\binom{b+(j-1)c}{k}}{\binom{(j-1)c+k}{k}}\,.

Substituting this to (
5.6
) we obtain (
5.11
).
∎

By Lemmas

see the

in

and Lemma
4.4
.
The values of

in a rectangle
\Omega_{i}(n,c)
are given by Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
4.4
also,
but

-Selberg integrals of smaller dimensions with the same value of

and suitable choices of values for

and

.

Theorem 5.4
.

For
(a,b)\in\Omega_{i}(n,c)
with

, we have

(5.12)

\displaystyle S_{n}(a,b,c)\,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{(n-i)ic}\,\binom{nc}{ic}\,

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n-i}\binom{p-1-(n-j)c-a}{(j-1)c}\,\prod_{j=1}^{i}\binom{p-1-(n-j)c-a}{(j-1)c}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\binom{p-1-(n-j)c-a}{(j-1)c}}\,

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle S_{n-i}(a+ic,b,c)\,S_{i}(a+(n-i)c,b+(n-i)c-p,c)\,.

,
and hence

and Lemma

can be applied to

and

The theorem follows from formula (

6.
A remarkable combinatorial identity

In this section we sketch another proof of Theorem
4.1
. We do this because at the heart of this proof there is a remarkable identity (Theorem
6.1
) for polynomials in two variables.

Notation.
Let
c,n
be positive integers. For
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
we will consider non-negative integers
0\leqslant m_{ij}\leqslant 2c
and we set
\overline{m}_{ij}=2c-m_{ij}
. For
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
define

r_{k}=\sum_{1\leqslant i<k}\overline{m}_{ik}+\sum_{k<i\leqslant n}m_{ki},\qquad s_{k}=\sum_{1\leqslant i<k}m_{ik}+\sum_{k<i\leqslant n}\overline{m}_{ki}.

We will use the (rising) Pochhammer symbol
(x)_{m}=x(x+1)(x+2)\cdots(x+m-1)
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 2,\ c\geqslant 1
be positive integers. In
\mathbb{Z}[x,y]
we have the identity

\sum_{\text{\bf m}}\left((-1)^{\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}}\prod_{i<j}\binom{2c}{m_{ij}}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}(x)_{r_{k}}(y)_{s_{k}}\right)\\
=\ \prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{((k+1)c)!}{c!}(x)_{kc}(y)_{kc}(x+y+(2n-k-2)c)_{kc}\ ,

where by
\sum_{\text{\bf m}}
we mean the
\binom{n}{2}
-fold summation

\sum_{m_{12}=0}^{2c}\sum_{m_{13}=0}^{2c}\sum_{m_{14}=0}^{2c}\ldots\sum_{m_{n-1,n}=0}^{2c}
.

The summands of the left-hand side are of degree
4c\binom{n}{2}
polynomials, and according to the theorem, their sum is
the right-hand side, which is the product of degree
3c\binom{n}{2}
, with linear factors. The reader is invited to verify that for
n=2
the theorem reduces to a hypergeometric identity, namely Dixon’s Theorem (
[
AAR
, Theorem 3.4.1]
) on the factorization of
{}_{3}F_{2}
with certain parameters. For instance the
n=2,c=2
case of
Theorem
6.1
states that the sum of the terms

(x+2)(x+3)(y+2)(y+3),\qquad-4x(x+2)y(y+2),\qquad 6x(x+1)y(y+1),\\
-4x(x+2)y(y+2),\qquad(x+2)(x+3)(y+2)(y+3)

is
12(x+y+2)(x+y+3)
(here we canceled the factor
xy(x+1)(y+1)
, which
appears in each term and on the right-hand side as well).
The explicit form of the identity for
n=3
is

\sum_{m_{12},m_{23},m_{13}=0}^{2c}(-1)^{m_{12}+m_{13}+m_{23}}\binom{2c}{m_{12}}\binom{2c}{m_{23}}\binom{2c}{m_{13}}\\
\times\prod_{k=0}^{m_{12}+m_{13}-1}(x+k)\prod_{k=0}^{2c-m_{12}+m_{23}-1}(x+k)\prod_{k=0}^{4c-m_{13}-m_{23}-1}(x+k)\\
\times\prod_{k=0}^{4c-m_{12}-m_{13}-1}(y+k)\prod_{k=0}^{2c-m_{23}+m_{12}-1}(y+k)\prod_{k=0}^{m_{13}+m_{23}-1}(y+j)\\
\ \hskip 147.95424pt=\ \frac{(2c)!}{c!}\,\ \frac{(3c)!}{c!}\ \prod_{k=1}^{c}(x+k-1)(y+k-1)(x+y+4c-k)\\
\times\ \prod_{k=1}^{2c}(x+k-1)(y+k-1)(x+y+4c-k).

Sketch of the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
Consider equation (
4.1
) for a positive integer
c
, that is, the classical Selberg integral formula in
n
dimensions. On the left-hand side we
decouple
the variables, i.e. we substitute

(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2c}=\sum_{m_{ij}=0}^{2c}\binom{2c}{m_{ij}}x_{i}^{m_{ij}}(-x_{j})^{\overline{m}_{ij}}.

We obtain

\sum_{\text{\bf m}}\left((-1)^{\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}}\prod_{i<j}\binom{2c}{m_{ij}}\cdot\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(\int_{0}^{1}x_{k}^{a+r_{k}}(1-x_{k})^{b}dx_{k}\right)\right)\\
=\ \prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\frac{(a+(j-1)c)!(b+(j-1)c)!}{(a+b+(n+j-2)c+1)!}.

Now writing
\Gamma(a+r_{k}+1)\Gamma(b+1)/\Gamma(a+r_{k}+b+2)
for the one-dimensional Selberg integrals
on the left-hand side, and substituting

x=a+1,\qquad y=-(a+2(n-1)c+b+1)\,,

the obtained identity rearranges to the statement in the theorem. ∎

We believe that the identity in Theorem
6.1
is interesting on it own right, but here is a sketch how to use it to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Consider the left-hand side of (
4.4
), and carry out the same
decoupling

of variables as we did in the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
We obtain a sum, parameterized by choices of
m_{ij}
, and in each summand we get a product of one-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integrals of the form

\int_{[1]_{p}}x_{k}^{A_{k}}(1-x_{k})^{b}dx_{k}

for some
A_{k}
. Substituting the value
-A_{k}!b!/(A_{k}+b+1-p)!
for such
a one-dimensional integral (formula (
3.4
)),
we obtain an explicit formula (no integrals anymore!) for the left-hand side of (
4.4
).
The summation Theorem
6.1
brings that sum to a product form, and one obtains exactly the
right-hand side of (
4.4
).

In this proof one has to pay additional attention to the case
a+b<p-1
, when
some integrals
\int_{[1]_{p}}x_{k}^{A_{k}}(1-x_{k})^{b}dx_{k}
have
A_{k}+b<p-1
and are equal to zero by formula
(
3.5
).
Still in this case the sum of nonzero terms is transformed to the desired product by the identity of Theorem

6.1
with parameter
c
replaced by
d:=a+b+(n-1)c+1-p
.
∎

7.
KZ equations

7.1.
Special case of
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
KZ equations over
{\mathbb{C}}

Let
e,f,h
be the standard basis of the complex Lie algeba
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
with
[e,f]=h
,
[h,e]=2e
,
[h,f]=-2f
.
The element

(7.1)

\displaystyle\Omega=e\otimes f+f\otimes e+\frac{1}{2}h\otimes h\ \in\ {\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}\otimes{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}

is called the Casimir element.
For
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
let
V_{i}
be the irreducible
i+1
-dimensional
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
-module with
basis
v_{i},fv_{i},\dots,f^{i}v_{i}
such that
ev_{i}=0
,
hv_{i}=iv_{i}
.

Let
u(z_{1},z_{2})
be a function taking values in
V_{m_{1}}\otimes V_{m_{2}}
and solving the KZ equations

(7.2)

\displaystyle\kappa\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial z_{1}}=\frac{\Omega}{z_{1}-z_{2}}\,u\,,\qquad\kappa\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial z_{2}}=\frac{\Omega}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\,u\,,

where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is a parameter of the equations. Let

{\operatorname{Sing}}[m_{1}+m_{2}-2n]
denote the space of singular vectors of
weight
m_{1}+m_{2}-2n
in
V_{m_{1}}\otimes V_{m_{2}}
,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Sing}}[m_{1}+m_{2}-2n]=\{v\in V_{m_{1}}\otimes V_{m_{2}}\ |\ hv=(m_{1}+m_{2}-2n)v,\ ev=0\}.

This space is one-dimensional if the integer
n
satisfies

0\leqslant n\leqslant\operatorname{min}(m_{1},m_{2})
and is zero-dimensional otherwise.
According to
[
SV1
]
, solutions
u
with values in

{\operatorname{Sing}}[m_{1}+m_{2}-2n]
are expressible in terms of
n
-dimensional hypergeometric integrals

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{r}u_{r}(z_{1},z_{2})\,f^{r}v_{m_{1}}\otimes f^{n-r}v_{m_{2}}

with

\displaystyle u_{r}(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}-z_{2})^{m_{1}m_{2}/2\kappa}\int_{C}W_{r}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\Psi(z_{1},z_{2},t)\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{n}\,.

Here the domain of integration is the simplex
C=\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ |\ z_{1}\leqslant t_{n}\leqslant\dots\leqslant t_{1}\leqslant z_{2}\}
.
The function
\Psi(z_{1},z_{2},t)
is called the
master function
,

\displaystyle\Psi(z_{1},z_{2},t)=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{2/\kappa}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{1})^{-m_{1}/\kappa}(t_{i}-z_{2})^{-m_{2}/\kappa}\,,

the rational functions
W_{r}(z_{1},z_{2},t)
are called the
weight functions
,

\displaystyle W_{r}(z_{1},z_{2},t)=\sum_{J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\atop|J|=r}\ \prod_{j\in J}\ \frac{1}{t_{j}-z_{1}}\ \prod_{j\notin J}\frac{1}{t_{i}-z_{2}}\,.

The fact that
u
is a solution in
{\operatorname{Sing}}[m_{1}+m_{2}-2n]
implies that

(7.3)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}(n-r)(m_{2}-n+r+1)u_{r}\,+\,(r+1)(m_{1}-r)u_{r+1}\,=\,0,\qquad r=1,\dots,n-1.

The coordinate functions
u_{r}
are generalizations of the Selberg integral. In fact,
u_{0}
and
u_{n}

are exactly the Selberg integrals. For example,

\displaystyle u_{0}(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}-z_{2})^{m_{1}m_{2}/2\kappa}\!\!\int_{C}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\!\!\!(t_{i}-t_{j})^{2/\kappa}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{1})^{-m_{1}/\kappa}(t_{i}-z_{2})^{-m_{2}/\kappa-1}dt_{1}\dots dt_{n}\,.

The change of variables
t_{i}=(z_{2}-z_{1})s_{i}+z_{1}
for
i=1,\dots,n
gives

\displaystyle u_{0}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{(-1)^{A}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{B}}{n!}\,\tilde{S}_{n}\Big(1-\frac{m_{1}}{\kappa},-\frac{m_{2}}{\kappa},\frac{1}{\kappa}\Big),

where
\tilde{S}_{n}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
denotes the Selberg integral (
1.1
),

A=\frac{n(n-1-m_{1})}{\kappa}+n
,

B=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}-2n(m_{1}+m_{2})+2n(n-1)}{2\kappa}
.
By formula (
7.3
), we obtain

(7.4)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa^{n}\,\frac{(-1)^{A}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{B}}{n!}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(1+\frac{j}{\kappa})}{\Gamma(1+\frac{1}{\kappa})}\,\frac{\Gamma(1-\frac{m_{1}-j+1}{\kappa})\,\Gamma(1-\frac{m_{2}-j+1}{\kappa})}{\Gamma(1-\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}-n-j+2}{\kappa})}\,,

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^{r}\binom{n}{r}\frac{f^{r}v_{1}\otimes f^{n-r}v_{2}}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}(m_{1}-j+1)\prod_{j=1}^{n-r}(m_{2}-j+1)}\,.

7.2.
Special case of

KZ equations over

Let
p
be an odd prime number. Let
\kappa
be a ratio of two integers not divisible by
p
.
Let
m_{1},m_{2}
be positive integers such that
m_{1},m_{2}<p

{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}

Let
V^{p}_{m_{1}}
,
V^{p}_{m_{2}}
be the
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
-modules over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,
corresponding to the complex representations

V_{m_{1}}
,
V_{m_{2}}
.
Then the KZ differential equations (
7.2
) with values in

V^{p}_{m_{1}}\otimes V^{p}_{m_{2}}
are well-defined, and
we may discuss their polynomial solutions in variables
z_{1},z_{2}

Let

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Sing}}[m_{1}+m_{2}-2n]_{p}=\{v\in V_{m_{1}}^{p}\otimes V_{m_{2}}^{p}\ |\ hv=(m_{1}+m_{2}-2n)v,\ ev=0\}.

This space is one-dimensional, if the integer
n
satisfies

0\leqslant n\leqslant\operatorname{min}(m_{1},m_{2})

Choose the least positive integers
M_{1},M_{2},M_{12},c
such that

(7.5)

\displaystyle M_{i}\equiv-\frac{m_{i}}{\kappa}\,,\quad M_{12}\equiv\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{2\kappa},\quad c\equiv\frac{1}{\kappa}\,\qquad(\operatorname{mod}\,p).

According to
[
SV2
]
, solutions
u
with values in

{\operatorname{Sing}}[m_{1}+m_{2}-2n]_{p}
are expressible in terms of
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric integrals

(7.6)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{r}u_{r}(z_{1},z_{2})\,f^{r}v_{m_{1}}\otimes f^{n-r}v_{m_{2}}

\displaystyle u_{r}(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}-z_{2})^{M_{12}}\int_{[1,\dots,1]_{p}}W_{r}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\Psi_{p}(z_{1},z_{2},t)\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{n}\,,

where
\Psi_{p}(z_{1},z_{2},t)
is the
master polynomial
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{p}(z_{1},z_{2},t)=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{2c}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{1})^{M_{1}}(t_{i}-z_{2})^{M_{2}}\,.

Theorem 7.1
.

Assume that
M_{1},M_{2},M_{12},c,n
are positive integers such that

(7.7)

\displaystyle M_{1}+(n-1)c<p,\qquad\phantom{aaaaa}M_{2}+(n-1)c<p,

\displaystyle p\leqslant M_{1}+M_{2}+(n-1)c,\qquad M_{1}+M_{2}+(2n-2)c<2p-1\ .

Then the function
u(z_{1},z_{2})
, defined by (
7.6
), is given by the formula

(7.8)

\displaystyle u(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{A}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{B}\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(jc)!}{c!}\,\frac{(M_{1}+(j-1)c)!\,(M_{2}+(j-1)c)!}{(M_{1}+M_{2}+(n+j-2)c-p)!}\,,

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{n}(-1)^{r}\binom{n}{r}\frac{f^{r}v_{1}\otimes f^{n-r}v_{2}}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}(M_{1}+(j-1)c)\prod_{j=1}^{n-r}(M_{2}+(j-1)c)}\,,

where

\displaystyle A=n(M_{1}+(n-1)c+1)\,,\qquad B=M_{12}+n(M_{1}+M_{2}+(n-1)c-p)\,.

For
n=1
this is
[
V7
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-Selberg integral formula of Theorem
4.1

and formula (
7.3
), cf.
Section
7.1

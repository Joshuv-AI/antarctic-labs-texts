Relatively endotrivial complexes
Sam K. Miller

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite group and k k be a field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 . In prior work, we studied endotrivial complexes, the invertible objects of the bounded homotopy category of p p -permutation k ​ G kG -modules K b ​ ( 𝐭𝐫𝐢𝐯 k ​ G ) K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}) . Introduction

In her doctoral dissertation
[
13

Similarly to how endotrivial modules are the invertible objects of the stable module category,

Furthermore, endotrivial complexes induce splendid Rickard autoequivalences, derived equivalences which induce “local” derived equivalences and which are a key part of Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture. Although endotrivial complexes have a name similar to that of endotrivial modules, the theory of these complexes is markedly different. We refer the reader to
[
21
, Section 6]

In this paper, we apply the ideas present in Lassueur’s work to the study of endotrivial complexes, which the author first studied in
[
21
]
. Our goal is to broadly develop analogous theory for relatively endotrivial complexes, and to use this machinery to better understand endotrivial complexes. In Lassueur’s work, there is one clear definition choice for a relatively endotrivial module, but in the endotrivial complex setting, there is more ambiguity. Remark 2.2
.

(a)

Recall the categories

(b)

There is a subtle difference between

-projectivity for modules. In the module-theoretic case, a

Furthermore, a

-projective chain complex is not the same thing as a chain complex of

-projective modules. For example, let

(c)

We briefly recall that a natural isomorphism of modules induces a natural isomorphism of chain complexes as follows. Let

be additive categories. If

are naturally isomorphic, then

induce functors of chain complex categories,

induces another natural isomorphism

Moreover, a natural isomorphism of bifunctors extends to a natural isomorphism of bicomplexes. In particular, natural isomorphisms involving internal homs or tensor products may be extended to their chain complex counterparts, as these constructions arise from taking total complexes of bicomplexes. Moreover, the Mackey formula is a natural isomorphism, as are all standard adjunctions involving induction and restriction, therefore they extend to natural isomorphisms of complexes as well.

In general, projectivity relative to a subgroup for chain complexes behaves similarly to those in the module-theoretic setting, and one may verify that many of the proofs of standard facts generalize to chain complexes with minimal changes. \operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)\subseteq{}_{kH}\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}V)
.

(b)

\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}{}_{kH}\mathcal{C}(V)\subseteq{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}V)
.

(c)

\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/N}{}_{k[G/N]}\mathcal{C}(V)\subseteq{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/N}V)
.

(d)

Let
\phi:\tilde{G}\to G
be a group homomorphism and let

be a
V
-projective
kG
-module.
\operatorname{Res}_{\phi}{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)\subseteq{}_{k\tilde{G}}\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Res}_{\phi}V)
, where
\operatorname{Res}_{\phi}
denotes restriction along the induced algebra homomorphism

.

(e)

If
V
is a
p
-permutation
kG
-module, then
\big({}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)\big)(P)\subseteq{}_{k[N_{G}(P)/P]}\mathcal{C}(V(P))
.

Proof.

All of these are proven in
[

, Lemma 2.1.1]
for modules except (e), and each of the proofs extends to the chain complex case.

For (e), suppose
M\mid V\otimes_{k}N
for some

. Then
M(P)\mid(V\otimes_{k}N)(P)\cong V(P)\otimes_{k}N(P)
in
{}_{k[N_{G}(P)/P]}\mathcal{C}
by
[
15
, Theorem 5.8.10]
, since

is
p
-permutation.
∎

If
V
is a
p
-permutation module, then
V
-projectivity is the same as projectivity relative to a family of subgroups, as we now demonstrate.

Theorem 3.7
.

(a)

Let
S\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Let
V
and
W
be two
kG
-modules. Then
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)={}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(W)
if and only if
{}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V)={}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}W)
.

(b)

If
V
is a trivial source module with vertex
P
, then
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)={}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(k[G/P])={}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(P)
.

(c)

If
V
is a
p
-permutation module, let
\mathcal{X}_{V}
be the set of
p
-subgroups

of
G
for which

. Then
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)={}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X}_{V})
.

Proof.

The proof of (a) is given in
[
12
, Corollary 2.1.4]
, and extends to chain complexes.

For (b), observe that if
V
is a trivial source module with vertex
P
, then
V\mid k[G/P]
. We have

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}k[G/P]\cong\bigoplus_{x\in[S\backslash G/P]}k[S/S\cap{}^{x}P].

This decomposition is the complete decomposition of
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}k[G/P]
into indecomposables, so
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V
must be isomorphic to a direct sum of modules of the form
k[S/Q]
for some subgroup
Q
of
G
with
Q\leq_{G}P
. If there does not exist a direct summand of
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V
isomorphic to
k[S/P]
, then
V(P)=0
, implying that
V
has vertex properly contained in a conjugate of
P
, a contradiction. Thus
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V
has a direct summand isomorphic to
k[S/P]
, and from the direct sum decomposition, all other indecomposable direct summands of
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}M
have a vertex contained in
P
.

Now, if
\mathcal{X}
is the poset of all subgroups
Q
of
S
for which
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}M
has a summand isomorphic to

, then
{}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}M)={}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X})
by Remark
3.4
. Note that if
R\leq Q\leq S,
then
{}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(R)\subseteq{}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(Q)
. By the previous paragraph’s argument,
\mathcal{X}
has as its maximal elements all
S
-conjugates of

, therefore
{}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X})={}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(P)
. A similar argument produces that
{}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}k[G/P])={}_{kS}\mathcal{C}(P)
, and the result follows by (a).

Finally, (c) follows from Proposition
[
13
, Proposition 2.2.2]
and the fact that since

is

-permutation,
V(P)=0
if and only if

is not conjugate to a subgroup of a vertex of any indecomposable summand of

.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

As a result of the previous proposition, if

is a
p
-permutation module and we want to understand

, it suffices to understand
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{X}_{V})
where
\mathcal{X}_{V}
is a set of all vertices of the indecomposable summands of

. Equivalently, we may take
\mathcal{X}_{V}
to be the set of all
p
-subgroups

for which

(see, for instance,
[

, Proposition 5.10.3]
), since adding subgroups of elements in
\mathcal{X}_{V}
to
\mathcal{X}_{V}
does not change the class of
\mathcal{X}_{V}
-projectives.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
V
be a
kG
-module. Denote by
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
the full subcategory of
{}_{kG}\mathbf{mod}(V)
consisting of
p
-permutation modules, and denote by
Ch^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv},V)
the subcategory of

consisting of chain complexes whose components are
p
-permutation modules. Let
{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}(V)
represent either
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
or

. It is an easy verification that Proposition
3.6
and Theorem

hold with
{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}(V)
replacing
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)
.

We do not necessarily assume

is

-permutation for

, but the following proposition asserts that in fact, for
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
, we can replace

with a suitable

-permutation module without altering the category. Therefore, relative projectivity in

is always equivalent to relative projectivity of with respect to a family of subgroups of

(which can be assumed to be a collection of
p
-subgroups closed under conjugation and taking subgroups).

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
V
be a
kG
-module. Then, there exists a
p
-permutation module
W
such that
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)={}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(W)
. In particular, there exists a poset of
p
-subgroups
\mathcal{X}\subseteq s_{p}(G)
closed under
G
-conjugation and taking subgroups for which
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)={}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(\mathcal{X})
.

Proof.

First we show that if
M
is a trivial source module with vertex
P\in s_{p}(G)
which belongs to
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
, then
k[G/P]
belongs to
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
. If
M\mid V\otimes_{k}N
for some
kG
-module
N
, then
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}M\mid\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}(V\otimes_{k}N)
for
S\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Since
M
has vertex
P
,
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}M
has a direct summand isomorphic to
k[S/P]
, and any other nonisomorphic direct summand is isomorphic to
k[S/Q]
for some subgroup
Q
conjugate to a subgroup of
P
. Therefore
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}(V\otimes_{k}N)
has a direct summand isomorphic to
k[S/P]
. We have
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}(V\otimes_{k}N)\cong k[S/P]\oplus N^{\prime}
, for some
kS
-module
N
, therefore,
k[S/P]
is
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V
-projective. Therefore by
[
13
, Proposition 2.2.2]
,
{}_{kS}\mathbf{triv}(P)\subseteq{}_{kS}\mathbf{triv}(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V)
.

We have that

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}k[G/P]\cong\bigoplus_{x\in[S\backslash G/P]}k[S/S\cap{}^{x}P],

and for any
x\in G
,
k[S/S\cap{}^{x}P]
is
P
-projective. Therefore,
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}k[G/P]\in{}_{kS}\mathbf{triv}(P)
, hence
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}k[G/P]\in{}_{kS}\mathbf{triv}(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V)
as well. Equivalently,

belongs to
{}_{kG}\mathbf{mod}(V)
, as desired.

Now, let
\mathcal{X}
be the set of all vertices which occur for all trivial source modules belonging to
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
. The previous claim and
[
13
, Proposition 2.2.2]
imply that

{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)\supset{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}\left(\bigoplus_{P\in\mathcal{X}}k[G/P]\right)={}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(\mathcal{X}).

The converse inclusion follows from Proposition
3.5
.
∎

In particular, if
V\neq 0
, then
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
is nonempty. If
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)={}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(\mathcal{X})
for some subposet
\mathcal{X}\subseteq s_{p}(G)
, closed under
G
-conjugation and taking subgroups, then for every

, the transitive permutation module

and all its direct summands are objects of
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
.

3.1.
Absolute
p
-divisibility

The following observation of Auslander and Carlson in
[
1
]
is crucial for the theory of
V
-projectivity: if
V
is a
kG
-module, then the
evaluation map
or
trace map

V^{*}\otimes_{k}V\to k:f\otimes v\mapsto f(v)
is always
V
-split, and splits when
\dim_{k}V
is coprime to
p
. The following arguments and definition are taken from
[
3
]
.

Theorem 3.11
.

[
3
, 2.1]

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
(possibly
p=0
). Let
M,N
be finite-dimensional indecomposable
kG
-modules, then

k\mid M\otimes_{k}N\text{ if and only if }M\cong N^{*}\text{ and }p\nmid\dim_{k}(N)

Moreover, if
k
is a direct summand of
N^{*}\otimes_{k}N
, it has multiplicity one.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
V
be a
kG
-module. The following are equivalent:

(a)

The trivial
kG
-module
k
is relatively
V
-projective.

(b)

The chain complex
k[0]
is relatively
V
-projective.

(c)

p
does not divide the
k
-dimension of at least one of the indecomposable direct summands of
V
.

(d)

{}_{kG}\mathbf{mod}(V)={}_{kG}\mathbf{mod}.

(e)

Ch^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{mod},V)=Ch^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{mod})
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (a),(c),(d) is given by
[
12
, Lemma 2.2.2]
. Now,
k[0]
is
V
-projective if and only if
k\mid V^{*}\otimes_{k}V
, and equivalently,
p\nmid\dim_{k}(V)
. This shows (b) is equivalent to (c). Now, (c) implies (e) by Proposition
[
13
, Proposition 2.2.2]
, and (e) implies (b) trivially, so we are done.
∎

In other words, relative
V
-projectivity is interesting if and only if the
k
-dimensions of all indecomposable summands of
V
are divisible by
p
, as otherwise, all objects are
V
-projective. Analogously, if
S\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
, all
kG
-modules or chain complexes of
kG
-modules are
S
-projective.

Definition 3.13
.

[
3
]

A
kG
-module for which the
k
-dimensions of all indecomposable summands of
V
are divisible by
p
is called
absolutely
p
-divisible.

Lemma 3.14
.

[
12
, Lemma 2.2.4]

Let
S\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
, and let
V
be a
kG
-module with vertex
Q\leq S
.

(a)

For every subgroup
H\geq S
,
V
is absolutely
p
-divisible if and only if
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}V
is absolutely
p
-divisible.

(b)

If
Q<S,
then for every subgroup
R\leq S
with
S\geq R>Q
,
V
is absolutely
p
-divisible if and only if
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{R}V
is.

In particular, if
V
is a
p
-permutation module,
V
is absolutely
p
-divisible if and only if
V
has no indecomposable direct summands with vertex
S
if and only if
V(S)=0
. Moreover, absolute
p
-divisibility is preserved upon restriction to a subgroup containing a Sylow
p
-subgroup. This will be an important fact in the sequel when we consider restriction to subgroups containing Sylow
p
-subgroups.

4.

-stable categories

Next, we review the stable categories corresponding to

-projectivity for modules and chain complexes. These categories are analogous to the usual stable module category, in that we factor out
V
-projective objects.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
V
be a
kG
-module.

(a)

The
V
-stable
kG
-module category
, denoted
{}_{kG}\mathbf{stmod}_{V}
, is defined as follows. The objects are
kG
-modules, and for any two
kG
-modules

, the space of morphisms from

to

-vector spaces

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{V}_{kG}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(M,N)/\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}^{V}(M,N),

\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}^{V}(M,N)
is the subspace of
\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(U,V)
consisting of morphisms which factor through a

-projective module. Note that this definition makes sense since
\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}^{V}(M,N)
is a two-sided ideal of
\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(M,N)
.

(b)

The
V
-stable
p
-permutation

-module category
, denoted
{}_{kG}\mathbf{sttriv}_{V}
, is defined as the full subcategory of
{}_{kG}\mathbf{stmod}_{V}
with
p
-permutation modules as its objects.

We have an analogous definition for categories of chain complexes.

(a)

The
V
-stable category of chain complexes of
kG
-modules
, denoted
\mathbf{stCh}^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{mod})_{V}
, is defined as follows. The objects of
\mathbf{stCh}^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{mod})_{V}
are the objects of
Ch^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{mod})
, and for any two

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{V}_{kG}(C,D)=\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(C,D)/\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}^{V}(C,D),

where
\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}^{V}(C,D)
is the
k
-subspace of
\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(C,D)
consisting of morphisms which factor through a
V
-projective chain complex.

(b)

The
V
-stable category of chain complexes of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules
, denoted
\mathbf{stCh}^{b}({}_{kG}\textbf{triv})_{V}
, is defined as the full subcategory of
\mathbf{stCh}^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{mod})_{V}
with objects chain complexes of
p
-permutation modules.

Notation:
Following the previous section, we let
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
refer to either
{}_{kG}\mathbf{mod}
or

. We write
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
for the corresponding

-stable category
{}_{kG}\mathbf{stmod}_{V}
or
\mathbf{stCh}^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{mod})_{V}
. For any morphism

, we write

to denote its image in
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
. We denote the morphism sets of
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
by
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{V}_{{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}}(M,N)
. Similarly, we let
{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}
refer to either
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}
or

. We let
{}_{kG}p\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
refer to the corresponding

-stable category
{}_{kG}\mathbf{sttriv}_{V}
or

, and use the analogous notation for morphisms, with
{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}
replacing
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
. If

, we vacuously set

and

.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
M\in{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
. The following are equivalent.

(a)

M
is isomorphic to the zero object in
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
.

(b)

\underline{\operatorname{id}}_{M}
is the zero endomorphism of
M
in
\underline{\operatorname{End}}^{V}_{{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}}(M)
.

(c)

M\in{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)
.

Proof.

(a) implies (b) is trivial since the zero object only has the zero endomorphism. If (b) holds, then
\operatorname{id}_{M}=g\circ f
for some
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
morphisms
f:M\to P
and
g:P\to M
, with
P
a
V
-projective object in
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
. In particular,
g
is split surjective, so
M
is isomorphic to a direct summand of

, hence

is
V
-projective as well. Finally, (c) implies (a) is straightforward.
∎

The above proposition also holds for
{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}
, as it is a full subcategory of
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
. We previously noted that projectivity relative to modules was compatible with induction, restriction, inflation, and the Brauer construction. We translate these compatibilities for the

-stable categories constructed here.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
H\leq G
,

, and

.

(a)

.

(b)

If

is a

-module,

induces functors

(c)

If

is a

-module,

induces functors

(d)

If

is a

-module, then
-(P)
induces a functor

This follows from Lemma

In particular, if

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
V
be a
kG
-module and let

be a

-morphism. The following are equivalent:

(a)

factors through an object in
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}(V)
.

(b)

factors through the morphism

sending
\delta\otimes v\otimes n
to
\delta(v)n
(degreewise, if necessary).

(c)

factors through any

-split epimorphism

.

Proof.

The implications (c) implies (b) implies (a) are straightforward, noting that the homomorphism in (b) is

-split. Suppose (a). Then there exists a

-projective object

and maps

and

such that

. Suppose

is a

-projectivity, there exists a morphism

such that

. Thus

, as desired.
∎

.

Let

and let

. Then

factors through an object in

{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}(V)
.

Proof.

We may replace

by a

-permutation module by Proposition

factors through an object in

if and only if

, which is

∎

As a result, we can equivalently define

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}^{V}_{{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}}(M,N)/\operatorname{Hom}_{{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}}^{V}(M,N),

where
\operatorname{Hom}_{{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}}^{V}(M,N)
is the subspace of
\operatorname{Hom}_{{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}}(M,N)
consisting of morphisms that factor through an object in

.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

, and let

. If

is a

-split epimorphism, then the image of
\varphi
in
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}}^{V}(M,N)
is an isomorphism if and only if
\varphi
is a split epimorphism and
\ker(\varphi)
is a
V
-projective object.

In particular, there exists an isomorphism

in
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
if and only if there exist

such that
M\oplus P\cong N\oplus Q
in
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi
is
V
-split surjective and
\underline{\varphi}
is an isomorphism in
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
. Then there is a
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
morphism

such that
\underline{\varphi}\circ\underline{\psi}=\underline{\operatorname{id}}_{N}
, or equivalently (since both categories are
k
-linear), that
\operatorname{id}_{N}-\varphi\circ\psi
factors through a
V
-projective
kG
-module. Equivalently,
\operatorname{id}_{N}-\varphi\circ\psi
factors through any
V
-split epimorphism, in particular
\varphi
. Write

\operatorname{id}_{N}-\varphi\circ\psi=\varphi\circ\alpha

for some
\alpha:N\to M
. We have

\operatorname{id}_{N}=\varphi\circ(\psi+\alpha),

hence
\varphi
is a split epimorphism. It follows that
\ker(\psi)
is zero in
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
for
\underline{\varphi}
to be an isomorphism, so
\ker(\varphi)
is
V
-projective. The converse of the first statement is straightforward.

Suppose now that
\underline{\varphi}
is an isomorphism. Define the object

and denote by

the canonical morphism sending
\delta\otimes v\otimes n\mapsto\delta(v)n
(componentwise if necessary). Then

is a
V
-split epimorphism since
\pi
is, and
\underline{\varphi\oplus\pi}
is an isomorphism, as

is
V
-projective, hence zero. By the first statement,

\ker(\varphi)

V
-projective, so

as desired. The converse is straightforward.
∎

Note that the above proposition also holds for
{}_{kG}p\mathcal{C}(V)

Corollary 4.7
.

{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
and
{}_{kG}p\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
satisfy the Krull-Schmidt property. In particular, any isomorphism class of objects in
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
and
{}_{kG}p\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
has a unique representative which has no
V
-projective direct summands.

Proof.

This follows since
{}_{kG}\mathcal{C}
satisfies the Krull-Schmidt property, so given any

one may write out its unique direct sum decomposition in
\mathcal{C}_{G}
and remove any

{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
. This follows for
{}_{kG}p\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
as well since it is a subcategory of
{}_{kG}\mathbf{st}\mathcal{C}_{V}
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

In general, given a homomorphism of
kG
-modules, the image of its kernel in the
V
-stable module category will depend on the choice of representative. Therefore,
{}_{kG}\mathbf{stmod}_{V}
and
{}_{kG}\mathbf{sttriv}_{V}
are not abelian categories, however, they are still preadditive and
k
-linear. Therefore, one may construct the chain complex categories

and
Ch^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{sttriv}_{V})
, as well as the homotopy categories
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{stmod}_{V})
and
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{sttriv}_{V})
. To discuss homological properties, we have to choose specific representatives of chain maps, and in general homological properties will not be well-defined for an equivalence class of complexes.

Since
{}_{kG}\mathbf{stmod}_{V}
satisfies the Krull-Schmidt property, any isomorphism class of chain complexes in

has a unique representative with no
V
-projective modules in any degree. This is obtained by taking any representative and removing all indecomposable
V
-projective summands in each degree and corresponding homomorphisms.

Definition 4.9
.

The homotopy category

is defined analogously to the non-stable setting. Two maps

It is clear that in

, both contractible complexes, i.e. complexes which are isomorphic to the zero object in

, and

-projective complexes are isomorphic to the zero object. These are precisely the complexes that are identified with the zero object.

5.
V
-endotrivial modules

We briefly recall a few necessary facts about relatively
V
-endotrivial modules, which was first introduced by Lassueur in
[
12
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
V
be a
kG
-module. A
kG
-module
M
is
endotrivial relative to
V
or
V
-endotrivial
if its
k
-endomorphism ring is the direct sum of a trivial module and a
V
-projective module. That is,
M
is
V
-endotrivial if and only if

\operatorname{End}_{k}(M)\cong M^{*}\otimes_{k}M\cong k\oplus P,\quad P\in{}_{kG}\mathbf{mod}(V).

Equivalently,
\operatorname{End}_{k}(M)\cong k\in{}_{kG}\mathbf{stmod}_{V}
.

Similar to the way that endotrivial modules arise from Heller translates,
V
-endotrivial modules arise from relative syzygies induced by
V
-projective modules. The following notion is due to Carlson, see
[
5
, Section 8]
for instance.

Definition 5.2
.

[
5
, Section 8]

A
V
-projective resolution
of a
kG
-module
M
is a nonnegative complex
P
of
V
-projective modules together with a surjective
kG
-module homomorphism
P_{0}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}M
such that the sequence

\cdots\to P_{2}\xrightarrow{d_{2}}P_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}P_{0}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}M\to 0

is exact and
totally
V
-split
, that is, for all
i\geq 1,
the short exact sequences

0\to\ker(d_{i})\to P_{i}\to\operatorname{im}(d_{i})\to 0

and

0\to\ker(\epsilon)\to P_{0}\xrightarrow{\epsilon}M\to 0

are
V
-split.

Remark 5.3
.

We have a dual notion of
V
-injective resolutions as well.

V
-protective resolutions always exist, since the canonical map
V^{*}\otimes_{k}V\otimes_{k}M\to M
is
V
-split surjective, and one may iterate this construction. There is also a relative comparison theorem: if
P
and
Q
are two
V
-projective resolutions of a
kG
-module
M
, then there is a chain map
\mu:(P_{\ast}\to M)\to(Q_{\ast}\to M)
which lifts to the identity on
M
. Using this, one may demonstrate the existence of a minimal
V
-projective resolution.

Definition 5.4
.

[
5
, Section 8]

Let
M
be a
kG
-module,
P
a minimal
V
-projective resolution of
M
with differentials
d_{i}
, and
I
a minimal
V
-injective resolution of
M
with differentials
d^{i}
. Define for all
n\geq 1:\Omega_{V}^{n}(M):=\ker(d_{n})
and for all
\Omega_{V}^{-n}(M):=\operatorname{coker}d^{n-1}
. Define
\Omega_{V}^{0}(M)
to be the
V
-projective-free part of

.

Proposition 5.5
.

[
12
, Lemmas 3.2.1, 3.2.2, 3.3.1, 3.4.1]

Let
V
be an absolutely
p
-divisible

-module.

(a)

Let

(b)

Each

, and given a vertex

.

Let

be an absolutely

-divisible

-endotrivial complexes

Remark 5.7
.

If

are absolutely

-divisible

-modules with

. Moreover, inflation, restriction, group isomorphisms, and conjugation all induce corresponding group homomorphisms:

•

\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/N}:T_{k,V}(G/N)\to T_{k,\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/N}V}(G)

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
V
be an absolutely

-divisible

-module, and let

be an indecomposable

-endotrivial

-module. Then,
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}M\cong k\oplus M^{\prime}

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V
-projective

-module

if and only if

has trivial source. In particular,

-endotrivial

The forward direction is given by

. We prove the reverse direction: suppose that

-endotrivial

-module. Then since

is

-endotrivial complexes

Notation: For the rest of this paper,

is assumed to be an absolutely

-divisible

-module.

Definition 6.1
.

Let

(a)

-projective

-permutation as well.

when it satisfies any one of the three definitions. If

]

Remark 6.2
.

It is clear to see that a

-endosplit-trivial complex is strongly

-endotrivial, and a strongly

-endotrivial complex is weakly

-endotrivial. Moreover,

-endosplit-trivial complexes are always examples of endosplit

.

By Proposition

, given any

or

.

[

Theorem 7.2

Let

Note that since

Conversely, suppose that for all

Endosplit

In

Definition 8.1
.

[

let

be a

-module. A chain complex of

-permutation modules

,

Remark 8.2
.

(a)

In particular, the Künneth formula implies
C^{*}\otimes_{k}C\simeq(M^{*}\otimes_{k}M)[0],
since a split chain complex is homotopy equivalent to the chain complex with its homology in each degree and zero differentials.

Suppose

is a

-endotrivial module, as defined in
[
12
]
, and suppose

is an endosplit

-permutation resolution for

. We have

for some

-projective

, hence

is a

-endosplit-trivial complex. Conversely, it is easy to see that any

-endosplit-trivial complex is, up to a shift in degrees, an endosplit

-permutation resolution, and it follows that

-endosplit-trivial complexes are equivalently shifted endosplit

-permutation resolutions for

-endotrivial modules. Moreover, if

is one-dimensional, then

is an endotrivial complex.

(b)

Rickard proved in
[

that endosplit

-permutation resolutions exist for any endopermutation

-module, where

is an abelian

[

that if

is odd, every endotrivial

-permutation resolution. On the other hand, some endotrivial modules such as the exotic endotrivial

-permutation resolution.

We have an equivalent characterization of endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions as follows.

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
C
be a bounded chain complex of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules. The following are equivalent:

(a)

C
is a shifted endosplit
p
-permutation resolution.

(b)

For every
P\in s_{p}(G)
, one of the following holds: either
C(P)
is contractible or
C(P)
has nonzero homology in exactly one degree.

Proof.

First, suppose
C
is a shifted endosplit
p
-permutation resolution for some
kG
-module
M
. Fix
P\in s_{p}(G)
.
\operatorname{End}_{k}(C)\cong C^{*}\otimes_{k}C
is split, and the Künneth formula implies
C^{*}\otimes_{k}C
has nonzero homology concentrated in degree zero, with
H_{0}(C^{*}\otimes_{k}C)\cong M^{*}\otimes_{k}M
, therefore

C^{*}\otimes_{k}C\simeq(M^{*}\otimes_{k}M)[0].

Applying the Brauer quotient, we obtain

C(P)^{*}\otimes_{k}C(P)\cong(C^{*}\otimes_{k}C)(P)\simeq(M^{*}\otimes_{k}M)(P)[0].

If
(M^{*}\otimes_{k}M)(P)\neq 0
, it follows again by the Künneth formula that
C(P)
has nonzero homology in exactly one degree. Otherwise, if
(M^{*}\otimes_{k}M)(P)=0
, then
C(P)^{*}\otimes_{k}C(P)\cong\operatorname{End}_{k}(C(P))
is contractible. Thus,
\operatorname{End}_{K^{b}({}_{k[N_{G}(P)/P]}\mathbf{triv})}(C(P))=0
, and
C(P)
is isomorphic to the zero complex in
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv})
, as desired.

Now, suppose condition (b) holds. Suppose for contradiction that
C^{*}\otimes_{k}C\not\simeq M[0]
, then we may write
C^{*}\otimes_{k}C\simeq D
where
D
is the unique direct summand of
D
with no contractible summands. Since
C
is bounded and
C^{*}\cong C
, there exists a unique positive natural number
i>0
for which
D_{i}\neq 0
and
D_{j}=0
for all
j>i
. Since

is either contractible or has nonzero homology in exactly one degree,

has nonzero homology concentrated in degree 0, possibly also zero. The same holds for

, as it is a direct summand of
(C^{*}\otimes_{k}C)(P)\cong C(P)^{*}\otimes_{k}C(P).
In particular,

is injective for all
P\in s_{p}(G)
, so

is split injective. Therefore,

has a contractible direct summand, a contradiction. Thus,
C^{*}\otimes_{k}C\not\simeq M[0]
for some
kG
-module

. However, the Künneth formula implies there exists a unique

for which

. We conclude

is a shifted endosplit
p
-permutation resolution.
∎

Let

be an absolutely

-divisible

be the set of

(a)

(b)

Remark 8.5
.

As before, it follows by commutativity with tensor products that applying restriction, inflation, or the Brauer construction to an endosplit

-permutation resolution. We will see in the sequel that if

The following result of Rickard in
[

]
will be of use in the sequel. We state a version provided in

Theorem 8.6
.

[

, Theorem 7.11.2]

Let

be a finitely generated

-module having an endosplit

-permutation resolution

. For any subgroup

, the complex
\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(C,C)
has homology concentrated in degree zero, isomorphic to

, and there is an algebra isomorphism

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}\circ\rho_{G}=\rho_{H}\circ\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}

\operatorname{tr}^{G}_{H}\circ\rho_{H}=\rho_{G}\circ\operatorname{tr}^{G}_{H}

induces a vertex and multiplicity preserving bijection between the sets of isomorphism classes of indecomposable direct summands of the module

As a partial converse, we can determine when the direct sum of two endosplit

-permutation resolution.

Let

be two endosplit

-permutation resolutions.

and

are contractible or

and

have nonzero homology concentrated in the same degree.

Proof.

First, suppose that for all

, either both

and

are contractible or

and

have nonzero homology concentrated in the same exact degree. Then the same holds for

implies

is an endosplit

-permutation resolution, as desired. Now suppose

is an endosplit

is either contractible or has nonzero homology in exactly one degree for any

. Therefore, both

and

are both contractible or have nonzero homology in the same unique degree as well. The result follows.
∎

In particular, setting

In

21
]
, we observed that much of the structure of an endotrivial complex could be determined by local homological data, which we coined “h-marks.” We can apply the same techniques in the relative endotriviality setting. In this section, we construct h-mark homomorphisms which determine much of the structure of a relatively endotrivial complex, particularly for weakly
V
-endotrivial complexes and
V
-endosplit-trivial complexes. We continue to assume
V
is an absolutely
p
-divisible
kG
-module, and set
\mathcal{X}_{V}\subseteq s_{p}(G)
to be the subset of all
p
-subgroups for which
V(P)=0
.

Notation:
We denote the set of
\mathbb{Z}
-valued superclass functions on
G
, that is, maps from the set of subgroups of
G
to
\mathbb{Z}
constant on conjugacy classes, by
CF(G)
. If
\mathcal{X}
is a subset of the set of subgroups of
G
which is closed under
G
-conjugation, then
CF(G,\mathcal{X})
denotes the group of
\mathbb{Z}
-valued superclass functions on
\mathcal{X}
, i.e. the group of functions
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{Z}
which are constant on conjugacy classes. We set
CF(G,p):=CF(G,s_{p}(G))
.

9.1.
h-mark homomorphisms for weakly
V
-endotrivial complexes

The definitions in this section arise from the fact that if
C
is a weakly
V
-endotrivial complex and
P\in\mathcal{X}_{V}
, then
C(P)
is an endotrivial complex since
V(P)=0
(see Definition
6.3
).

Definition 9.1
.

Let
V
be an absolutely
p
-divisible
kG
-module. Recall that for any
P\in\mathcal{X}_{V}
,
C(P)
is an endotrivial complex.

For a class of weakly
V
-endotrivial complexes
[C]\in w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
and
p
-subgroup
P
for which
V(P)=0
, denote by
h_{C}(P)
the degree
i\in\mathbb{Z}
of a representative
C
in which
H_{i}(C(P))\neq 0
, and if we have
H_{h_{C}(P)}(C(P))\cong k_{\omega}
, set
\mathcal{H}_{C}(P):=\omega\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/P,k^{\times})
. This is well-defined since
C(P)
is an endotrivial complex. We call the values of
h_{C}(P)
the
h-marks of
C
at
P
, and abusively refer to
\mathcal{H}_{C}(P)
as
the homology of
C
at
P
.

We have a map:

\displaystyle\Xi:w\mathcal{E}^{V}_{k}(G)

\displaystyle\to\prod_{P\in\mathcal{X}_{V}}\mathbb{Z}\times\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/P,k^{\times})

\displaystyle[C]

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(h_{C}(P),\mathcal{H}_{C}(P)\big)_{P\in s_{p}(G)}

It is straightforward to verify that this map is a well-defined group homomorphism via the Künneth formula and commutativity of the Brauer construction with tensor products for
p
-permutation modules.

For any weakly
V
-endotrivial complex
C
, we may regard
h_{C}
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued superclass function on
\mathcal{X}_{V}
. Indeed, the Brauer construction satisfies
C({}^{g}P)={}^{g}C(P)
for any
g\in G
and
P\in s_{p}(G)
. This gives another group homomorphism, which we call the
h-mark homomorphism,

\displaystyle h_{w}:w\mathcal{E}^{V}_{k}(G)

\displaystyle\to CF(G,\mathcal{X}_{V})

\displaystyle[C]

\displaystyle\mapsto h_{C}

One may easily verify that this is well-defined, that is, if
[C_{1}]=[C_{2}]\in w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
, the
h_{C_{1}}=h_{C_{2}}\in CF(G,\mathcal{X}_{V})
.

Lemma 9.2
.

[
21
, Lemma 5.8]

Let
C
be a bounded complex of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules with the following property: there exists a set
\mathcal{Y}\subset s_{p}(G)
which does not contain Sylow subgroups, is closed under conjugation and taking subgroups, and that satisfies that there exists some
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for all
P\not\in\mathcal{Y}
,
\dim_{k}H_{i}(C(P))=1
and
C(P)
is exact in all other degrees. Then
C
is homotopy equivalent to an complex
C^{\prime}
with the following property: there exists an
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
C^{\prime}_{i}=M\oplus N
, with
M

\mathcal{Y}
-projective, possibly 0, and a vertex of
N
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup, and for all
j\neq i
, either
C_{j}^{\prime}=0
or
C_{j}^{\prime}
is
\mathcal{Y}
-projective.

Theorem 9.3
.

We have
\ker(h_{w})\cong T_{k,V}(G,S).
In particular,
\ker(h_{w})
is the torsion subgroup of
w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
, and
w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
is a finitely generated abelian group.

Proof.

We have
T_{k,V}(G,S)\leq\ker(h_{w})
, so it suffices to show the converse inclusion. The elements of
\ker(h_{w})
are (isomorphism classes of)
V
-endotrivial complexes
C
for which, given any
P\in\mathcal{X}_{V}
,
\dim_{k}H_{0}(C(P))=1
and
C(P)
is exact in all other degrees. Any such complex
C
satisfies the hypothesis of the previous lemma, where we set
\mathcal{Y}=s_{p}(G)\setminus\mathcal{X}_{V}
in the notation of the previous lemma.

For weakly
V
-endotrivial complexes,
V
-projectivity is the same as
\mathcal{Y}
-projectivity, by Proposition
3.10
and
[
15
, Proposition 5.10.3]
. Thus,
C_{0}
has an indecomposable summand with vertex
S
, and all other indecomposable modules appearing in each degree of
C
are
V
-projective. Therefore,
C\cong M[0]
in
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{sttriv}_{V})
for some indecomposable trivial source
V
-endotrivial
kG
-module
M
with vertex
S
, thus
\ker(h_{W})\subseteq T_{k,V}(G,S)
as desired.

It follows that
\ker(h_{w})
is the torsion subgroup of
w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
, since
T_{k,V}(G,S)
is finite (as there is a finite number of isomorphism classes trivial source
kG
-modules), hence torsion, and any complex with a nonzero h-mark cannot be torsion, as successive powers of the complex will increase the absolute value of the nonzero h-mark. Additionally,
w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
must be finitely generated abelian, since the
\mathbb{Z}
-rank of
w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
is bounded below by the
\mathbb{Z}
-rank of
CF(G,\mathcal{X}_{V})
, which is equal to the number of conjugacy classes of
\mathcal{X}_{V}
.
∎

Remark 9.4
.

In particular, since
\ker(h_{w})
is the torsion subgroup of
w\mathcal{E}^{V}_{k}(G)
, we have a split exact sequence of abelian groups

0\to T_{k,V}(G,S)\to w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)\to\operatorname{im}(h_{w})\to 0,

since
\operatorname{im}(h_{w})
is a free abelian group. Unlike the situation for endotrivial complexes given in
[
21
, Remark 3.8]
, this sequence is not canonically split.

Corollary 9.5
.

Two weakly
V
-endotrivial complexes are isomorphic in
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{sttriv}_{V})
if and only if they have the same h-marks and local homology at all
P\in\mathcal{X}_{V}
.

Proof.

This follows since given two weakly
V
-endotrivial complexes which satisfy the above, they must represent the same class in
w\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
, whence the result.
∎

9.2.
h-marks for endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions and
V
-endosplit-trivial complexes

Recall that a complex of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules is a shifted endosplit
p
-permutation resolution if and only if for all
P\in s_{p}(G)
,
C(P)
either has nonzero homology in exactly one degree or is contractible.

Definition 9.6
.

Let
C
be either a
V
-endosplit-trivial complex or an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution, and let
P\in s_{p}(G)
. Denote by
h_{C}(P)
the unique degree
i\in\mathbb{Z}
for which
H_{i}(C(P))\neq 0
.

If
C
is a
V
-endosplit-trivial complex, set
\mathcal{H}_{C}(P)=[H_{h_{C}(P)}(C(P))]\in T_{k,V}(N_{G}(P)/P)
. We claim this is invariant among equivalent complexes in
e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
. Indeed, suppose
[C_{1}]=[C_{2}]\in e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
. Then
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
have isomorphic indecomposable
V
-endosplit-trivial direct summands, which we denote
C_{0}
. Therefore we may write
C_{1}\cong C_{0}\oplus P_{1}
and
C_{2}\cong C_{0}\oplus P_{2}
, with
P_{1},P_{2}
homotopy equivalent to
V
-projective complexes. Therefore, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
H_{i}(P_{1})
and
H_{i}(P_{2})
are
V
-projective
kG
-modules. Moreover, for any
P\in s_{p}(G)
,
h_{C_{1}}(P)=h_{C_{0}}(P)=h_{C_{2}}(P)
. Thus, for any
P\in s_{p}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{C_{1}}(P)

\displaystyle=[H_{h_{C_{1}}(P)}(C_{0}(P))\oplus H_{h_{C_{1}}(P)}(P_{1}(P))]

\displaystyle=[H_{h_{C_{0}}(P)}(C_{0}(P))]

\displaystyle=[H_{h_{C_{2}}(P)}(C_{0}(P))\oplus H_{h_{C_{2}}(P)}(P_{2}(P))]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{C_{2}}(P)\in T_{k,V}(N_{G}(P)/P).

As before, we call the values of
h_{C}(P)
the
h-marks of
C
at
P
, and abusively refer to
\mathcal{H}_{C}(P)
as
the homology of
C
at
P
. Note that here, we are taking the image of homology in
T_{k,V}(G)
rather than the isomorphic image. We obtain a group homomorphism:

\displaystyle\Xi:e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)

\displaystyle\to\prod_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\mathbb{Z}\times T_{k,V(P)}(N_{G}(P)/P)

\displaystyle[C]

\displaystyle\mapsto(h_{C}(P),\mathcal{H}_{C}(P))_{P\in s_{p}(G)}.

We identify
T_{k,\{0\}}(G)
with
\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})
, the group of
k
-dimension one
kG
-modules with addition given by
\otimes_{k}
. We regard
h_{C}
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-valued superclass function on
s_{p}(G)
, as before. This gives another group homomorphism, which we call the
h-mark homomorphism
,

\displaystyle h_{e}:e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)

\displaystyle\to CF(G,p)

\displaystyle[C]

\displaystyle\mapsto h_{C}

Note that the codomain of
h_{e}
is
CF(G,p)
, whereas the codomain of
h_{W}
is
CF(G,\mathcal{X}_{V})
.

Theorem 9.7
.

We have
\ker(h_{e})\cong T_{k,V}(G,S)
. In particular,
\ker(h_{e})
is the torsion subgroup of
e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
, and
e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
is finitely generated.

Proof.

Note
T_{k,V}(G,S)\leq\ker(h_{e})
. Suppose
[C]\in\ker(h_{e})
, and let
C
be an indecomposable representative of
[C]
. First suppose
C
has exactly one nonzero term - since
[C]\in\ker(h_{e})
, it follows that the nonzero term must be in degree 0. It follows that
C_{0}^{*}\otimes_{k}C_{0}\cong k\oplus P
, for some
V
-projective
kG
-module
P
, i.e.
C_{0}
is a relatively
V
-endotrivial module. Moreover,
C_{0}
is
p
-permutation and therefore has a unique trivial source
V
-endotrivial direct summand, whence the result.

Now, suppose for contradiction that
C
has at least 2 nonzero terms. Then one term of
C
must be in a nonzero degree. Suppose without loss of generality
C
has a nonzero terms in a positive degree. Let
i>0
be the greatest integer for which
C_{i}\neq 0
. We label the differentials of
C
by
d_{i}
. Since
C
is exact at
i
,
d_{i}:C_{i}\to C_{i-1}
is injective. Moreover, for any
P\in s_{p}(G)
, since
C(P)
is exact at
i
and
C_{j}(P)=0
for all
j>i
,
d_{i}(P)
is injective as well. Therefore,
d_{i}
is split injective by
[
15
, Theorem 5.8.10]
. This implies a contractible term isomorphic to
0\to C_{i}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}}C_{i}\to 0
splits off
C
, however this is a contradiction since we assumed
C
to be indecomposable. Therefore, the only elements of
\ker(h_{e})
are of the form
[M[0]]
, where
M
is a trivial source
V
-endotrivial
kG
-module, as desired. The final statements follow similarly to those in Theorem
9.3
.
∎

Remark 9.8
.

In particular, since
\ker(h_{e})
is the torsion subgroup of
e\mathcal{E}^{V}_{k}(G)
, we have a split exact sequence of abelian groups

0\to T_{k,V}(G,S)\to e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)\to\operatorname{im}(h_{e})\to 0,

since
\operatorname{im}(h_{e})
is a free abelian group. Unlike the situation for endotrivial complexes, this sequence is not canonically split, one must choose a basis for the free part of
e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
. The reason for this is that in general, given any
C\in e\mathcal{E}^{V}_{K}(V)
,
\mathcal{H}_{C}(P)\in T_{k,V}(N_{G}(P)/P)
may not have a
p
-permutation representative, and therefore we do not have a clear well-defined map from
e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
to
T_{k,V}(G,S)
.

Corollary 9.9
.

Two indecomposable
V
-endosplit-trivial chain complexes are isomorphic chain complexes if and only if they have the same h-marks and isomorphic nonzero homology.

10.
Borel-Smith conditions

In this section, we give a partial set of conditions which h-mark functions must satisfy. These conditions come from Borel-Smith functions, a type of superclass function for
p
-groups which arise both from homotopy representations of spheres (see
[
27
, Page 210]
) and from the study of endo-permutation modules (see
[
4
]
). Borel-Smith functions are defined by conditions imposed at certain subquotients of a group. We will show that these same conditions must be met for our h-mark functions, but only at certain subquotients which satisfy a condition relating to
V
. To do this, we will show that h-mark functions of endotrivial complexes are Borel-Smith functions.

Definition 10.1
.

[
4
, Definition 3.1]

A superclass function
f\in CF(G)
is called a
Borel-Smith function
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

If
p
is odd,
H\trianglelefteq L\leq G,
and
L/H\cong\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, then
f(H)-f(L)
is even.

(b)

If
H\trianglelefteq L\trianglelefteq N\leq N_{G}(H)
and
L/H\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, then
f(H)-f(L)
is even if
N/H\cong\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
, and
f(H)-f(L)
is divisible by
4
if
N/H
is the quaternion group of order 8.

(c)

If
H\trianglelefteq L\leq G,L/H\cong\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
,
H_{i}/H
the subgroups of order
p
in
L/H
, then

f(H)-f(L)=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\big(f(H_{i})-f(L)\big).

These conditions are referred to as the
Borel-Smith conditions
. The were first discovered as the conditions satisfied by the dimension function of a homology mod
p
sphere with a
G
-action. The set of Borel-Smith functions is an additive subgroup of
CF(G)
which we denote by
CF_{b}(G)
. Note that Condition (b) sometimes requires that
f(H)-f(L)
is divisible by
4
when
N/H
is a generalized quaternion 2-group. However, this requirement is redundant, since any generalized quaternion group of order at least 8 contains the quaternion group of order 8 as a subgroup, which contains its unique subgroup of order 2.

Given any set
\mathcal{X}
of subgroups of
G
that is closed under
G
-conjugation, we say
f\in CF(G,\mathcal{X})
is a Borel-Smith function if it satisfies the above conditions wherever possible. For instance, if
H\trianglelefteq L\trianglelefteq N\leq N_{G}(H)
satisfies
L/H\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
N/H\cong Q_{8}
, if
H,L\in\mathcal{X}
but
N\not\in\mathcal{X}
,
f(H)-f(L)
must be divisible by
4
, since
f(H)
and
f(L)
are defined, even though
f(N)
is not. Again, the set of Borel-Smith functions on
\mathcal{X}
is an additive subgroup of
CF(G,\mathcal{X})
which we denote by
CF_{b}(G,\mathcal{X}).

We first show the h-marks of an endotrivial complex must satisfy the Borel-Smith conditions.

Lemma 10.2
.

(a)

Let
p
be an odd prime and
G=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. If
C
is an endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules, then
h_{C}(1)-h_{C}(G)
is even.

(b)

Let
p=2
and
G=\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
. Let
H
be the unique subgroup of order 2 in
G
. If
C
is an endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules, then
h_{C}(1)-h_{C}(H)
is even.

(c)

Let
p=2
and
G=Q_{8}
. Let
Z
be the unique subgroup of order 2 in
G
. If
C
is an endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules, then
h_{C}(1)-h_{C}(Z)
is divisible by 4.

(d)

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, and let
H_{0},\dots,H_{p}
be the subgroups of order
p
in
G
. If
C
is an endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules, then

h_{C}(1)-h_{C}(G)=\sum_{i=0}^{p}h_{C}(H_{i})-h_{C}(G).

Proof.

(a) through (c) may be shown directly by the classifications of
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
given in
[
21
, Section 6]
, however we provide a direct proof. Let
C
be an endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules. It follows from
[
21
, Proposition 4.3 (b) and (c)]
that
\hat{C}:=\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/Z}C(Z)
is an endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules which satisfies that for all
1\neq H\leq G
,
h_{C}(H)=h_{\hat{C}}(H)
, and
h_{\hat{C}}(1)=h_{C}(Z).
Therefore,
D:=C\otimes_{k}\hat{C}^{*}
is an endotrivial complex satisfying for all
1\neq H\leq G
,
h_{D}(H)=0
and
h_{D}(1)=h_{C}(1)-h_{C}(Z)
.

Lemma
9.2
implies that
D
is homotopy equivalent to an endotrivial chain complex with the trivial module in degree 0 and the only other
kG
-modules which appear in
D
are projective. Moreover,
D
is acyclic in only one degree,
h_{D}(1)=h_{C}(1)-h_{C}(Z)
, and since it is endotrivial, that homology has
k
-dimension 1, and therefore must be the trivial
kG
-module, since
G
is a
p
-group for (a)-(c). It follows that
D
contains as a direct summand a truncated periodic resolution or injective hull of the trivial
kG
-module. The result now follows by considering the period the trivial
kG
-module in (a)-(c): in (a) and (b)
k
has period 2 and in (c)
k
has period 4.

(d) follows directly from the classification of
\mathcal{E}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
given in
[
21
, Proposition 6.4]
, since every element in the basis of
\mathcal{E}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
given in the proposition satisfies the condition.

∎

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
C
be an endotrivial complex. Then the h-marks of
C
satisfy the Borel-Smith conditions, i.e.
h_{C}\in CF_{b}(G,p)
.

Proof.

We will show condition (a) is satisfied, the other conditions follow by analogous arguments. Suppose
p
is odd,
H\trianglelefteq L\leq G
with
H,L\in s_{p}(G)
, and
L/H\cong\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Then
D:=\operatorname{Res}^{N_{G}(H)/H}_{L/H}C(H)
is an endotrivial complex of
k[L/H]
-modules, and
[
21
, Proposition 4.3 (a) and (b)]
imply
h_{D}(L/H)=h_{C}(L)
,
h_{D}(H/H)=h_{C}(H)
. The previous lemma implies
h_{D}(H/H)-h_{D}(L/H)
is even, thus
h_{C}(H)-h_{C}(L)
is even as well, as desired.
∎

Corollary 10.4
.

Let
V
be an absolutely
p
-divisible
p
-permutation
kG
-module, let
\mathcal{X}_{V}\subseteq s_{p}(G)
denote the set of
p
-subgroups of
G
for which
V(P)=0
, and let
C
be a weakly
V
-endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules. Then the h-marks of
C
satisfy the Borel-Smith conditions, i.e.

.

Proof.

This follows since for every
P\in\mathcal{X}_{V},

C(P)
is an endotrivial complex, and one may verify an analogue of
[
21
, Proposition 4.3(b)]
: for any pair of
p
-subgroups
P\trianglelefteq Q\in\mathcal{X}_{V}
, we have
h_{C}(Q)=h_{C(P)}(Q/P)
.
∎

Definition 10.5
.

Let
V
be a
p
-permutation
kG
-module. Say
f\in CF_{b}(G,p)

satisfies the Borel-Smith conditions at
V
if

satisfies the conditions given in Definition
10.1
whenever the
p
-subgroup
H
(in the notation of the conditions) satisfies

.

Corollary 10.6
.

Let
V
be an absolutely
p
-divisible
p
-permutation
kG
-module, and let
C
be a
V
-endosplit-trivial complex of
kG
-modules. Then the h-marks of

.

Proof.

Again, this follows since for every
P
satisfying

∎

Conjecture 10.7
.

Let

be an absolutely

-divisible

-permutation

•

, then there exists a weakly

•

11.
The case of
V=kG

In this section, we examine the case of
V=kG
. We set
V:=kG
for the entire section for ease of notation. Here,
V
-endotrivial modules are endotrivial modules in the usual sense. Therefore,
V
-endosplit-trivial complexes are shifted endosplit

Theorem 11.1

Let

be an indecomposable strongly

-endotrivial complex. Then

V
-endosplit-trivial complex. In particular, the inclusion

Proof.

First, note that

-projective chain complexes of

or
0\to P\to 0

. It suffices to show that

We have by the Künneth formula and exactness of the

where the direct sum has a finite number of nonzero terms. By the characterization of

for some projective

Remark 11.3
.

It may be possible to, in some cases, perform a local analogue of this process as well. Suppose for instance that we wish to augment a chain complex of

-modules

For (non-relatively) endotrivial complexes, the image of restriction to a Sylow

Theorem 12.6

Let

Let

12.1.

We next consider the case of restriction for

Definition 12.7
.

-endotrivial

Since for any

We break up our restriction into two parts: restriction to

. Recall that from Proposition

, then

Theorem 12.8
.

Let
N_{G}(S)\leq H\leq G
. Let

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}V
-endotrivial

-module with indecomposable

-endotrivial. If this occurs, the Green correspondent of

-permutation resolution of the Green correspondent of

First, suppose the Green correspondent of

, and
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}D

Now, suppose

is the

-endotrivial direct summand. Call the Green correspondent

Corollary 12.9
.

Let

.
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to\mathcal{E}_{k}(H)

We next examine the case in which

, and consider restriction to

. In the non-relative endotrivial module setting, there is an isomorphism

, where

, originally shown by Dade and further studied by Mazza in

. The original statement, given in an unpublished manuscript of Dade, is as follows.

Theorem 12.10
.

[

, Theorem 7.1]

Let

with

and let

be an endopermutation

-module. Then

(i.e. there exists an endopermutation module

is

Let

-divisible

-permutation

-endotrivial

is

is an endo-

, since

is

-endotrivial as well.

Putting this together, we obtain the following results for endo-

-permutation

-endotrivial modules and

-endosplit-trivial complexes.

.

Suppose

is normal in

. Then
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}:eT_{k,V}(G)\to eT_{k,\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V}(S)^{G-st}

Proof.

This follows from the arguments in the previous remark, as every endo-

-permutation

Theorem 12.13
.

Suppose
S\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
is normal in

. Then
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}:e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)\to e\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}V}(G)^{G}

Suppose

12.2.

V
-endotrivial complexes

We study the more general case of restriction for weakly

-endotrivial complexes. In this case, we still have the Green correspondence but have less control over its behavior.

Theorem 12.14
.

Let
S\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G),

, and let

be an absolutely

-divisible

-permutation

-module. Suppose

-stable indecomposable weakly

-endotrivial

-complex, and let

denote its Green correspondent. If for all

[D]\in\operatorname{im}\big(\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{L}:w\mathcal{E}^{V}_{k}(G)\to w\mathcal{E}^{\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{L}V}_{k}(L)^{G}\big)

It suffices to show that

is a weakly

-endotrivial complex, since from this it will follow by the Green correspondence that

is an endotrivial

is an endosplit

-permutation resolution, therefore any noncontractible summand is an endosplit

is noncontractible, it is an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution. It suffices to show that

has non-zero homology with

The nonzero homology of
(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{L}D)(P)

\mathcal{H}\big((\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{L}D)(P)\big)=\bigoplus_{x\in[N_{G}(P)\backslash G/L],P\leq{}^{x}L}\operatorname{Ind}^{N_{G}(P)}_{N_{G}(P)\cap{}^{x}L}(\mathcal{H}({}^{x}D(P))),

\mathcal{H}
denotes the degree in which the nonzero homology occurs. Note that

since

is weakly
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{L}V
-endotrivial.

There exists some
x\in G
for which

. Indeed, if

, there exists

such that

and

. Therefore,
\mathcal{H}\big((\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{L}D)(P)\big)
contains a 1-dimensional

-module, say

, as a direct summand. Since
(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{L}D)(P)
is an endosplit

-permutation resolution, there exists a unique corresponding indecomposable endosplit

-permutation resolution for

(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{L}D)(P)
, i.e. an indecomposable endotrivial complex. Suppose

is the unique endotrivial

-complex which is a direct summand of
(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{L}D)(P)
. Then, there exists a corresponding unique indecomposable direct summand

of
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{L}D
such that

is a summand of

.

Now, we claim

. It suffices to show that

has vertex

. We have that

(E^{*}\otimes_{k}E)(P)\cong E(P)^{*}\otimes_{k}E(P)\simeq k[0].

Therefore by the previous lemma,

is homotopy equivalent to a complex

with

containing a direct summand with vertex

. It follows that

must also have vertex

, but since there is a unique summand of
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{L}D
with vertex

by the Green correspondence,

By hypothesis, the only noncontractible summand of

is

. Therefore,

is an endotrivial

-complex for all

-endotrivial.

∎

We can also extend an argument of Lassueur in

which determines that the Green correspondence induces an isomorphism when

Theorem 12.15

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}:x\mathcal{E}^{V}_{k}(G)\to x\mathcal{E}^{\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}V}_{k}(H)^{G}

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}V

\mathfrak{X}
-projective, hence
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}V
-projective. Thus,
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}[D]=[D]\in x\mathcal{E}_{k}^{\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}V}(H)^{G}
, as desired.
∎

Remark 12.16
.

One important case where the hypothesis for Theorem
12.15
is satisfied is when
V=V(\mathcal{F}_{G})
is the following module defined by Lassueur in
[
14
]
.

V(\mathcal{F}_{G}):=\bigoplus_{P\in s_{p}(G),P\not\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)}k[G/P].

A subgroup of the group
T_{V(\mathcal{F}_{G})}(G)
generalizes the Dade group for arbitrary groups, and coincides with the generalization of the Dade group for fusion systems introduced by Linckelmann and Mazza in
[
17
]
.

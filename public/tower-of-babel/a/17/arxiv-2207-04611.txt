A Strong Law of Large Numbers under Sublinear Expectations
Yongsheng Song

Abstract
We consider a sequence of i.i.d. random variables { ξ k } \{\xi_{k}\} under a sublinear expectation 𝔼 = sup P ∈ Θ E P \mathbb{E}=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P} . We first give a new proof to the fact that, under each P ∈ Θ P\in\Theta , any cluster point of the empirical averages ξ ¯ n = ( ξ 1 + ⋯ + ξ n ) / n \bar{\xi}_{n}=(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n})/n lies in [ μ ¯ , μ ¯ ] [\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}] with μ ¯ = − 𝔼 ⁡ [ − ξ 1 ] , μ ¯ = 𝔼 ⁡ [ ξ 1 ] \underline{\mu}=-\mathbb{E}[-\xi_{1}],\overline{\mu}=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}] . Then, we consider sublinear expectations on a Polish space Ω \Omega , and show that for each constant μ ∈ [ μ ¯ , μ ¯ ] \mu\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}] , there exists a probability P μ ∈ Θ P_{\mu}\in\Theta such that lim n → ∞ ξ ¯ n = μ , P μ ​ -a.s. , \displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}=\mu,\ P_{\mu}\textmd{-a.s.}, (0.1) supposing that Θ \Theta is weakly compact and { ξ n } ∈ L 𝔼 1 ​ ( Ω ) \{\xi_{n}\}\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega) . Under the same conditions, we can get a generalization of ( 0.1 ) in the product space Ω = ℝ ℕ \Omega=\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}} with μ ∈ [ μ ¯ , μ ¯ ] \mu\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}] replaced by Π = π ⁡ ( ξ 1 , ⋯ , ξ d ) ∈ [ μ ¯ , μ ¯ ] \Pi=\pi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{d})\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}] , where π \pi is a Borel measurable function on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , d ∈ ℝ d\in\mathbb{R} . Finally, we characterize the triviality of the tail σ \sigma -algebra of i.i.d. random variables under a sublinear expectation.

2
Basic notions of sublinear expectations

Similar to linear expectations, the general framework of sublinear expectations is defined on measurable spaces.

2.1
Sublinear expectations on measurable spaces

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
be a given measurable space and let
\Theta
be a set of probabilities on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a linear subspace of the space of
\mathcal{F}
-measurable functions on
\Omega
with
\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}[|\xi|]<\infty
.
Set
\mathbb{E}[\xi]=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}[\xi]
for
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. We call
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{E})
a sublinear expectation space.

Definition 2.1

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{E})
be a sublinear expectation space. We say a random vector
\mathbf{X}=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{m})\in\mathcal{H}^{m}
is independent of
\mathbf{Y}=(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{n})\in\mathcal{H}^{n}
if for any
\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n+m})

\mathbb{E}[\varphi(\mathbf{Y},\mathbf{X})]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\varphi(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{X})]|_{\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{Y}}].

Definition 2.2

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{E})
and
(\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{\widetilde{H}},\widetilde{\mathbb{E}})
be two sublinear expectation spaces. A random vector
\mathbf{X}
in
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{E})
is said to be identically distributed with another random vector
\mathbf{Y}
in
(\widetilde{\Omega},\mathcal{\widetilde{H}},\widetilde{\mathbf{E}})
(write
\mathbf{X}\overset{d}{=}\mathbf{Y}
), if for any bounded and Lipschitz function
\varphi
,

\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)]=\widetilde{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(Y)].

In a sublinear expectation space, the fact that
\mathbf{X}
is independent of
\mathbf{Y}
does not imply that
\mathbf{Y}
is independent of
\mathbf{X}
. A sequence of random variables
(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is called i.i.d if
X_{i+1}
is independent from
(X_{1},\cdots,X_{i})
and
X_{i+1}\overset{d}{=}X_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and called
<
i.i.d if
X_{i}
is independent from
(X_{i+1},\cdots,X_{i+j})
and
X_{i+1}\overset{d}{=}X_{i}
for each
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
. It is easy to see that these two notions do not make any difference for the weak law of large numbers.

2.2
Sublinear expectations on Polish spaces

Some typical sublinear expectations, such as
G
-expectation, are defined on complete separable metric spaces. Generally, let
\Omega
be a Polish space. Denote by
C_{b}(\Omega)
the collection of bounded and continuous functions and
\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)
the collection of Borel probability measures on
\Omega
.

For
\Theta\subset\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)
, the associated sublinear expectation is defined by

\mathbb{E}[\xi]=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}[\xi],\ \xi\in C_{b}(\Omega).

We call
\Theta
a set that represents
\mathbb{E}
.
For
p\geq 1
, set
\|\xi\|_{p}:=\big(\mathbb{E}[|\xi|^{p}]\big)^{1/p}
,
\xi\in C_{b}(\Omega)
. Denote by
L^{p}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega)
the completion of
C_{b}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

Definition 2.3

A sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
defined on a Polish space
\Omega
is said to be regular if for each
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
C_{b}(\Omega)
such that
X_{n}\downarrow 0
on
\Omega
, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{n}\right]\downarrow 0
.

It follows from Theorem 12 in
[
3
]
that
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
is regular if and only if
\hat{\Theta}
is relatively compact. We also refer to
[
3
]
for the properties of the space
L^{p}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega)
.

A strong law of large numbers under sublinear expectations

Let
\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables under a sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}
. Set
\bar{\xi}_{n}=\frac{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n}}{n}
,
\underline{\mu}=-\mathbb{E}[-\xi_{1}]
,
\overline{\mu}=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]
.

[
1
]
and
[
2
]
proved that the cluster points of
\{\bar{\xi}_{n}\}
fall into
[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
. First, we shall give a new proof to this result based on the error estimates for wLLN* (
[
14
]
). Then, we investigate under which conditions the collection of the cluster points of
\{\bar{\xi}_{n}\}
coincides with the interval
[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
, which is not always the case shown by some counterexamples.

For a sequence of i.i.d random variables
\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
under a sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}
defined on a Polish space
\Omega
, we show that each
\mu\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
is a cluster point of the empirical averages supposing that the random variables
\{\xi_{k}\}
are contained in
L^{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega)
for some
\gamma>1
and
\Theta
is weakly compact.

3.1
The bounds of cluster points

Below, we give an alternative proof to the bounds of the cluster points given in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\{\xi_{k}\}
be a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables under the sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}=\sup\limits_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}
. Suppose
\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|^{1+\beta}]<\infty
for some
\beta>0
. Set
\bar{\xi}_{n}=\frac{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n}}{n}
. Then, for any
P\in\Theta
,

\underline{\mu}\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\leq\overline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.,}

where
\overline{\mu}=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]
and
\underline{\mu}=-\mathbb{E}[-\xi_{1}].

Proof.
For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\bar{\xi}_{n}^{(m)}=\frac{\xi_{m+1}+\cdots+\xi_{m+n}}{n}
. Let
e_{n}=[\alpha^{n}]
for some
\alpha>1
and let
\delta_{n}
be a sequence of positive integers. For
P\in\Theta
and
\varepsilon>0
, it follows from Theorem 5.1 in
[
14
]
that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P\left\{\bar{\xi}^{(\delta_{n})}_{e_{n}}-\overline{\mu}>\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[(\bar{\xi}^{(\delta_{n})}_{e_{n}}-\overline{\mu})^{+}]\leq\frac{c\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|^{1+\beta}]}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{e_{n}^{\beta/2}}<\infty,

(3.1)

which means that
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}^{(\delta_{n})}_{e_{n}}\leq\overline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

Similarly, we can get
\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}^{(\delta_{n})}_{e_{n}}\geq\underline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, there exists
s_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
e_{s_{n}}\leq n<e_{s_{n}+1}
.

STEP 1.
The
\xi_{n}\geq 0
case

Noting that
\bar{\xi}_{n}\leq\frac{e_{s_{n}+1}}{e_{s_{n}}}\bar{\xi}_{e_{s_{n}+1}},

we have
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\leq\alpha\overline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

Letting
\alpha\downarrow 1

we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\leq\overline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

By similar arguments, we can prove that, for any
P\in\Theta
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\geq\underline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

STEP 2.
The general case

It follows from STEP 1 that, for any
P\in\Theta
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|\xi_{1}|+\cdots+|\xi_{n}|}{n}\leq\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|],\ P\emph{-a.s.}

Noting that

|\bar{\xi}_{n}|\leq\frac{|\xi_{1}|+\cdots+|\xi_{n}|}{n},

we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\bar{\xi}_{n}|\leq\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|],\ P\emph{-a.s.}

So
\xi^{*}_{\infty}:=\sup_{n}|\bar{\xi}_{n}|<\infty
,
P
-a.s. Noting that

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n-e_{s_{n}}}}{n}+\frac{\xi_{n-e_{s_{n}}+1}+\cdots+\xi_{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n-e_{s_{n}}}{n}\bar{\xi}_{n-e_{s_{n}}}+\frac{e_{s_{n}}}{n}(\bar{\xi}^{(n-e_{s_{n}})}_{e_{s_{n}}}-\overline{\mu})+\frac{e_{s_{n}}-n}{n}\overline{\mu}+\overline{\mu}

and
|\frac{n-e_{s_{n}}}{n}\bar{\xi}_{n-e_{s_{n}}}|\leq\frac{n-e_{s_{n}}}{n}\xi^{*}_{\infty}
,
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n-e_{s_{n}}}{n}\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}
, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(\xi^{*}_{\infty}+|\overline{\mu}|)+\overline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

Letting
\alpha\downarrow 1
, we get

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\leq\overline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

On the other hand, noting that

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{n}=\frac{n-e_{s_{n}}}{n}\bar{\xi}_{n-e_{s_{n}}}+\frac{e_{s_{n}}}{n}(\bar{\xi}^{(n-e_{s_{n}})}_{e_{s_{n}}}-\underline{\mu})+\frac{e_{s_{n}}-n}{n}\underline{\mu}+\underline{\mu}

we have

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\geq-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(\xi^{*}_{\infty}+|\underline{\mu}|)+\underline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

and consequently, we get

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}\geq\underline{\mu},\ P\emph{-a.s.}

\Box

3.2
Convergence of empirical averages to cluster points

In this subsection, we investigate under which congditions the mean interval
[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
is filled up with cluster points of the empirical averages. First, we present several lemmas.

For
\kappa\in\{\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, set
\mu_{n}=\frac{\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{n}}{n}
, and designate
N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)=\{i\leq n\ |\ \kappa_{i}=\overline{\mu}\}
,
N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)=\{i\leq n\ |\ \kappa_{i}=\underline{\mu}\}
, and
k_{n},l_{n}
the numbers of elements in
N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)
and
N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)
, respectively.

Lemma 3.2

Let
\{\xi_{k}\}
be a sequence of random variables in a sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{E})
with
\mathbb{E}=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}
. Set
\bar{\xi}_{n}=\frac{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n}}{n}
. Then, for any
P\in\Theta
with
E_{P}[\xi_{n}]=\kappa_{n}
, we have, with the convention
0/0=0
,

P[|\bar{\xi}_{n}-\mu_{n}|>\varepsilon]\leq\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_{n}}{n}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{k_{n}}-\overline{\mu})^{+}]+\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\frac{l_{n}}{n}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{l_{n}}-\underline{\mu})^{-}].

Proof.

\displaystyle P[|\bar{\xi}_{n}-\mu_{n}|>\varepsilon]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[|\frac{k_{n}}{n}(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{k_{n}}-\overline{\mu})+\frac{l_{n}}{n}(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{l_{n}}-\underline{\mu})|>\varepsilon]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle P[\frac{k_{n}}{n}|\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{k_{n}}-\overline{\mu}|>\varepsilon/2]+P[\frac{l_{n}}{n}|\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{l_{n}}-\underline{\mu}|>\varepsilon/2]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_{n}}{n}E_{P}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{k_{n}}-\overline{\mu})^{+}]+\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\frac{l_{n}}{n}E_{P}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{l_{n}}-\underline{\mu})^{-}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_{n}}{n}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{k_{n}}-\overline{\mu})^{+}]+\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\frac{l_{n}}{n}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{l_{n}}-\underline{\mu})^{-}].

The second inequality follows from Chebyshev’s inequality as well as the fact that

E_{P}[\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{k_{n}}]=\overline{\mu}\ \textmd{and}\ E_{P}[\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{k_{n}}]=\underline{\mu}.

\Box

For
\mu\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
, set

\displaystyle\begin{split}\kappa_{1}(\mu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\overline{\mu}}&{\ \ \text{ if }\mu\geq\frac{\overline{\mu}+\underline{\mu}}{2},}\\
{\underline{\mu}}&{\ \ \text{ if }\mu<\frac{\overline{\mu}+\underline{\mu}}{2},}\end{array}\right.\hskip 8.5359pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 8.5359pt\kappa_{n+1}(\mu)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\overline{\mu}}&{\ \ \text{ if }\mu\geq\mu_{n},}\\
{\underline{\mu}}&{\ \ \text{ if }\mu<\mu_{n},}\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(3.2)

where
\mu_{n}=(\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{n})/n
.

Lemma 3.3

Let
N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)=\{i\leq n\ |\ \kappa_{i}(\mu)=\overline{\mu}\}
,
N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)=\{i\leq n\ |\ \kappa_{i}(\mu)=\underline{\mu}\}
, and
k_{n},l_{n}
be the numbers of elements in
N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)
and
N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)
, respectively. Then, we have

\displaystyle|\mu_{n}-\mu|\leq\frac{\overline{\mu}-\underline{\mu}}{n}.

(3.3)

Equivalently,

|\frac{k_{n}}{n}-\frac{\mu-\underline{\mu}}{\overline{\mu}-\underline{\mu}}|\leq\frac{1}{n},\ |\frac{l_{n}}{n}-\frac{\overline{\mu}-\mu}{\overline{\mu}-\underline{\mu}}|\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Proof.
Clearly, (
3.3
) holds for
n=1
. Assume that (
3.3
) holds for
i\leq n
.
For the
\mu\geq\mu_{n}
case,

\mu_{n+1}-\mu=\frac{n}{n+1}(\mu_{n}-\mu)+\frac{1}{n+1}(\overline{\mu}-\mu).

Noting that
|\frac{n}{n+1}(\mu_{n}-\mu)|\leq\frac{1}{n+1}(\overline{\mu}-\underline{\mu})
,
|\frac{1}{n+1}(\overline{\mu}-\mu)|\leq\frac{1}{n+1}(\overline{\mu}-\underline{\mu})
and
(\mu_{n}-\mu)(\overline{\mu}-\mu)\leq 0
, we have

|\mu_{n+1}-\mu|\leq\frac{1}{n+1}(\overline{\mu}-\underline{\mu}).

The
\mu<\mu_{n}
case can be proved similarly.
\Box

Lemma 3.4

Let
\{\xi_{k}\}
be a sequence of i.i.d random variables in a sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{E})
with
\mathbb{E}=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}
. Set
\bar{\xi}_{n}=\frac{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n}}{n}
. Assume
\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|^{1+\beta}]<\infty
for some
\beta>0
. For
\mu\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
and
P\in\Theta
with
E_{P}[\xi_{n}]=\kappa_{n}(\mu)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

P[|\bar{\xi}_{n}-\mu|>\varepsilon]\rightarrow 0

as
n
goes to
+\infty
.

Proof.
It follows from Lemma

\displaystyle P[|\bar{\xi}_{n}-\mu_{n}|>\varepsilon]\leq\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\frac{k_{n}}{n}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{k_{n}}-\overline{\mu})^{+}]+\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\frac{l_{n}}{n}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}}{l_{n}}-\underline{\mu})^{-}],

(3.4)

which converges to 0 as

[
9

Since

, we have, for

The desired result follows from (

\Box

Lemma

proves the convergence in probability based on the weak law of large numbers under sublinear expectations (wLLN*). By the error estimates of wLLN* given by

Lemma 3.5

Assume the conditions in Lemma

hold. For

and

Proof.
For
\mu\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
, note that

\bar{\xi}_{n}=\frac{k_{n}}{n}(\frac{1}{k_{n}}\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i})+\frac{l_{n}}{n}(\frac{1}{l_{n}}\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)}\xi_{i}).

Set
N^{+}_{\kappa}=\bigcup_{n}N^{+}_{\kappa}(n)
and
N^{-}_{\kappa}=\bigcup_{n}N^{-}_{\kappa}(n)

Let

for some

3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P\left\{\left|\bar{\xi}_{e_{n}}-\mu_{n}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{e_{n}}}{e_{n}}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(e_{n})}\xi_{i}}{k_{e_{n}}}-\overline{\mu})^{+}]+\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{l_{e_{n}}}{e_{n}}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(e_{n})}\xi_{i}}{l_{e_{n}}}-\underline{\mu})^{-}].

It follows from Theorem 5.1 in
[
14

that

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{e_{n}}}{e_{n}}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{+}_{\kappa}(e_{n})}\xi_{i}}{k_{e_{n}}}-\overline{\mu})^{+}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|^{1+\beta}]}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{k_{e_{n}}}{e_{n}}{k_{e_{n}}}^{-\beta/(1+\beta)}\leq\frac{c\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|^{1+\beta}]}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{e_{n}}^{-\beta/(1+\beta)}<\infty.

Similarly, we get

\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{l_{e_{n}}}{e_{n}}\mathbb{E}[(\frac{\Sigma_{i\in N^{-}_{\kappa}(e_{n})}\xi_{i}}{l_{e_{n}}}-\underline{\mu})^{-}]<\infty,

and consequently,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P\left\{\left|\bar{\xi}_{e_{n}}-\mu_{n}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}<\infty,

which implies that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{e_{n}}=\mu,\ P\emph{-a.s.}

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, there exists

such that

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}_{n}=\frac{n-e_{s_{n}}}{n}\bar{\xi}_{n-e_{s_{n}}}+\frac{e_{s_{n}}}{n}(\bar{\xi}^{(n-e_{s_{n}})}_{e_{s_{n}}}-\mu)+\frac{e_{s_{n}}-n}{n}\mu+\mu.

\xi^{*}_{\infty}=\sup_{n}|\bar{\xi}_{n}|<\infty
,

-a.s.

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{e_{s_{n}}}{n}(\bar{\xi}^{(n-e_{s_{n}})}_{e_{s_{n}}}-\mu)=0

P
-a.s. and
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big(|\frac{n-e_{s_{n}}}{n}\bar{\xi}_{n-e_{s_{n}}}|+|\frac{e_{s_{n}}-n}{n}\mu|\big)\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}(\xi^{*}_{\infty}+|\mu|)

\Box

In the sequel of this subsection, we consider sublinear expectations on a Polish space

Theorem 3.6

Let
\{\xi_{k}\}\subset L^{1+\beta}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega)
,
\beta>0
, be a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables under
\mathbb{E}
. Set
\underline{\mu}=-\mathbb{E}[-\xi_{1}]

\bar{\xi}_{n}=\frac{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n}}{n}

\mu\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]

Particularly,

\lim_{n}\bar{\xi}_{n}=\mu,\ P_{\mu}\textmd{-a.s.}

Proof.
Set

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{\mu}(n)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{1}&{\ \ \text{ if }\kappa_{n}(\mu)=\overline{\mu},}\\
{-1}&{\ \ \text{ if }\kappa_{n}(\mu)=\underline{\mu}.}\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(3.5)

For

Then, for

{\underline{\mu}}&{\ \ \text{ if }\sigma(k)=-1.}\end{array}\right.

is weakly compact, we can find a subsequent

{\underline{\mu}}&{\ \ \text{ if }\sigma(n)=-1.}\end{array}\right.

In other words,
E_{P_{\mu}}[\xi_{n}]=\kappa_{n}(\mu)
. The desired result follows from Lemma
3.5
.

\Box

Lemma 3.8

Under the same conditions as those in Theorem

(3.6)

\Box

A key step in the proof to Lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{\overline{\mu}}\left\{\left|\bar{\xi}_{e_{n}}-\overline{\mu}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}\leq\frac{c\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{1}|^{\gamma}]}{\varepsilon}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{e_{n}^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}},

(3.7)

for

In fact, Chebyshev’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{\overline{\mu}}\left\{\left|\bar{\xi}_{e_{n}}-\overline{\mu}\right|>\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{P_{\overline{\mu}}}[(\bar{\xi}_{e_{n}}-\overline{\mu})^{2}].

It follows from Lemma

that

\displaystyle E_{P_{\overline{\mu}}}[(\bar{\xi}_{e_{n}}-\overline{\mu})^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k=1}^{e_{n}}\frac{1}{e_{n}^{2}}E_{P_{\overline{\mu}}}[(\xi_{k}-\overline{\mu})^{2}]+\Sigma_{i<j}\frac{2}{e_{n}^{2}}E_{P_{\overline{\mu}}}[(\xi_{i}-\overline{\mu})(\xi_{j}-\overline{\mu})]

Lemma 3.9

Suppose that
\eta
is independent to
\xi
under
\mathbb{E}
for
\xi,\eta\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega)
. Then, for
P\in\Theta

, we have

E_{P}[\xi\eta]\leq E_{P}[\xi]E_{P}[\eta].

Proof.
If
\xi\geq 0
, we have, for any
Q\in\Theta
,

\displaystyle E_{Q}[\xi\eta]\leq\mathbb{E}[\xi\eta]=\mathbb{E}[\xi]\mathbb{E}[\eta]=E_{P}[\xi]E_{P}[\eta].

(3.8)

If
\xi\geq-c
for some

, it follows from (

) that, for any

E_{Q}[(\xi+c)\eta]\leq E_{P}[\xi+c]E_{P}[\eta],

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle E_{Q}[\xi\eta]\leq E_{P}[\xi]E_{P}[\eta]+c(E_{P}[\eta]-E_{Q}[\eta]).

(3.9)

For the general case, set
\xi_{n}=\max\{\xi,-n\}
. Choose

such that

. Note that

E_{P}[\xi\eta]\leq\mathbb{E}[\xi]\mathbb{E}[\eta]=E_{P}[\xi]E_{P}[\eta].

In the above lemma, if

we also have

4
On the tail
\sigma
-algebra

In a probability space, it is well-known by the Kolmogorov 0-1 law that the tail
\sigma
-algebra of a sequence of independent and identically distributed random variables is trivial. In this section, we study the triviality of the tail
\sigma
-algebra of a sequence of i.i.d random variables under a sublinear expectation.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
endowed with the metric
d(x,y):=\Sigma_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}(|x(k)-y(k)|\wedge 1)
, for
x,y\in\Omega
. This is a complete separable metric space.

Notations:

•

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\xi_{k}(\omega)=\omega(k)
,
\omega\in\Omega
;

•

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\theta_{n}\{\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots\}=\{\xi_{n+1},\xi_{n+2},\cdots\}
be the
n
-shift operator;

•

\mathcal{G}_{n}:=\sigma\{\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1},\cdots\}
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\sigma\{\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}\}
and
\mathcal{T}:=\cap_{n}\mathcal{G}_{n}
;

•

\bar{\xi}_{n}:=\frac{\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{n}}{n}
,
\xi_{\infty}^{\triangledown}:=\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}
,
\xi_{\infty}^{\vartriangle}:=\liminf\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{\xi}_{n}
.

For a regular sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}
on
(\Omega,C_{b}(\Omega))
, set

\displaystyle\Theta:=\{P\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)|

\displaystyle E_{P}[X]\leq\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X],\ \textmd{for any}\ X\in C_{b}(\Omega)\},

and let
\Theta^{e}
be the set of the extreme points of
\Theta
. By the representation theorem of sublinear expectations, we have, for
X\in C_{b}(\Omega)
,
\mathbb{E}[X]=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}[X].
This representation still holds for
X\in L^{p}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega)
,
p\geq 1
, the completion of
C_{b}(\Omega)
under the
L^{p}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}^{p}=\mathbb{E}[|\cdot|^{p}]
.

Assumption
Throughout this section, we assume that
\{\xi_{k}\}\subset L^{\gamma}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega)
, for some
\gamma>1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\Theta
, set
P_{1,d}=P\circ(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{d})^{-1}
, and
\Xi_{d}=\{P_{1,d}\ |\ P\in\Theta\}
, which is convex and closed. Designate
\Xi_{d}^{e}
the set of extreme points of
\Xi_{d}
.

Supposing
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots
is a sequence of i.i.d random variables under
\mathbb{E}
, we first give a generalization of Theorem
3.6
in the space
(\Omega,L^{1}_{\mathbb{E}}(\Omega),\mathbb{E})
. Write
\overline{\mu}=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{1}]
,
\underline{\mu}=-\mathbb{E}[-\xi_{1}]
.

Theorem 4.1

For any
\mathcal{F}_{d}
-measurable random variable
\Pi=\pi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{d})
with values in
[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
and

, and any

on

3.8
that there exists a Borel mapping

-measurable random variable

4.1
Tail
\sigma
-algebra of
<
i.i.d random variables

Let
\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}
be a regular sublinear expectation on
(\Omega,C_{b}(\Omega))
such that
\xi:=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots)
is a sequence of
<
i.i.d random variables.

Set

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\Theta}:=\{P\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(\Omega)|

\displaystyle E_{P}[X]\leq\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[X],\ \textmd{for any}\ X\in C_{b}(\Omega)\}.

It is easy to see that
\overleftarrow{\Theta}
is weakly compact and convex. Let
\overleftarrow{\Theta}^{e}
be the set of the extreme points of
\overleftarrow{\Theta}
. By Krein-Milman Theorem, one has
\overleftarrow{\Theta}=\overline{\textmd{co}}(\overleftarrow{\Theta}^{e})
, which implies that

\sup_{P\in\overleftarrow{\Theta}^{e}}E_{P}[X]=\sup_{P\in\overleftarrow{\Theta}}E_{P}[X]=\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[X],\ \textit{for}\ X\in C_{b}(\Omega).

In this section, we show that the tail
\sigma
-algebra of
<
i.i.d random variables is
\emph{trivial}
under each
P\in\overleftarrow{\Theta}^{e}
(Theorem
4.4
below). To this end, we give a characterization of the set
\overleftarrow{\Theta}
.

Set

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}:=\{P\in\overleftarrow{\Theta}|

\displaystyle E_{P}[\phi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]\leq\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)]E_{P}[\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)],\ \textmd{for any}

\displaystyle\ \phi_{n}\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \psi_{m}\in C^{+}_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),\ m,n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

In the above, we abbreviate
\phi_{n}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})
as
\phi_{n}(\xi)
for
\phi_{n}\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Lemma 4.3

\overleftarrow{\Theta}=\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}
.

Proof.
We first give some properties of
\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}
.

1)
\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}
is convex.

For
P_{1},P_{2}\in\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, clearly we have
Q:=\alpha P_{1}+(1-\alpha)P_{2}\in\overleftarrow{\Theta}
. For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\phi_{n}\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \psi_{m}\in C^{+}_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\displaystyle E_{Q}[\phi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha E_{P_{1}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]+(1-\alpha)E_{P_{2}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)]E_{P_{1}}[\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]+(1-\alpha)\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)]E_{P_{2}}[\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)]E_{Q}[\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)].

2)
\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}
is weakly compact.

Let
(P_{k})\subset\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}
converge weakly to
P
. Then
P
belongs to
\overleftarrow{\Theta}
. For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\phi_{n}\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \psi_{m}\in C^{+}_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\displaystyle E_{P}[\phi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}E_{P_{k}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)]E_{P_{k}}[\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{n}(\xi)]E_{P}[\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)].

3)
\sup\limits_{P\in\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}}E_{P}[X]=\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[X]
, for
X\in C_{b}(\Omega)
.

Set
Lip(\Omega)=\{\varphi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})|\ \varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. For
X\in Lip(\Omega)
, the assertion holds by the assumption that
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots
is
<
i.i.d under
\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}
. Noting that
Lip(\Omega)
is dense in
C_{b}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\overleftarrow{\mathbb{E}}[|\cdot|]
, we get the desired result.

Then, it follows from Hahn-Banach theorem that
\overleftarrow{\Theta}=\overleftarrow{\Theta}_{0}
.

\Box

4.2
Tail
\sigma
-algebra of i.i.d random variables

In this subsection, we investigate the triviality of the tail
\sigma
-algebra of i.i.d random variables under a regular sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}
defined on
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Although it does not hold under
P\in\Theta^{e}
generally, we shall show that the tail
\sigma
-algebra of i.i.d random variables is trivial under
each “stationary” probability
P\in\Theta^{e}
, denoted by
\Theta^{s}
, and that

\sup_{P\in\Theta^{s}}E_{P}[X]=\sup_{P\in\Theta}E_{P}[X]=\mathbb{E}[X],\ \textmd{for}\ X\in C_{b}(\Omega).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Theta_{n}
be the subset of
\Theta
such that for
P\in\Theta_{n}
one has

.
Set
\Theta_{\infty}=\cup_{n}\Theta_{n}
.

Theorem 4.6

Let
\Theta^{s}=\Theta^{e}\cap\Theta_{\infty}
.

1)

For any
P\in\Theta^{s}
and

, we have

\textmd{either}\ P(A)=0,\ \textmd{or}\ P(A)=1;

2)

For any
P\in\Theta^{s}
, we have
\xi_{\infty}^{\triangledown}=\xi_{\infty}^{\vartriangle}=m_{P}
,
P
-a.s., for some constant

; Moreover,

.

3)

\Theta^{s}
is a set that represents

:

\sup_{P\in\Theta^{s}}E_{P}[X]=\mathbb{E}[X],\ \textit{for}\ X\in C_{b}(\Omega).

To prove the above theorem, we first give a characterization of the set
\Theta
, which is a counterpart of Lemma

, and the proof is similar.

Lemma 4.7

Let
P\in\Theta
. For
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\phi_{n}\in C_{b}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\psi_{m}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we have

\displaystyle E_{P}[\phi_{n}(\xi)\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)]\leq E_{P}[\phi_{n}(\xi)]\mathbb{E}[\psi_{m}(\theta_{n}\xi)].

(4.3)

Here we write
\phi_{n}(\xi)
for
\phi_{n}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})
.

An equivalent statement of (
4.3
): Let
P_{\omega}
be a regular conditional probability of
P[\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{n}]
. Then

\displaystyle P_{\omega}\circ\theta_{n}^{-1}\in\Theta,\ P\textmd{-a.s.}

(4.4)

Proof to Theorem
4.6
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\Xi_{n}=\{P_{1,n}=P\circ(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})^{-1}|P\in\Theta\}
, which is convex and closed. Let
\Xi^{e}_{n}
be the set of extreme points of
\Xi_{n}
, and set

\Theta^{*}_{n}=\{\otimes_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}|\ \mu_{k}=\mu\in\Xi^{e}_{n},\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Claim:

\Theta^{e}\cap\Theta_{n}=\Theta^{*}_{n}
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Clearly, the conclusions 1), 3) of the theorem follow immediately from this claim. For the conclusion 2), notice that, for
P\in\Theta^{*}_{n}
, we have

\xi_{\infty}^{\triangledown}=\xi_{\infty}^{\vartriangle}=\frac{1}{n}E_{P}[\Sigma_{k=1}^{n}\xi_{k}]=:m_{P}
,
P
-a.s. By arguments similar to the proof to Theorem
3.6
,
we get the
\{m_{P}\}_{P\in\Theta^{s}}
is dense in
\big[\underline{\mu},\ \overline{\mu}\big]
.

Let us prove the claim now.

a)
\Theta^{e}\cap\Theta_{n}\subset\Theta^{*}_{n}
.

We first prove that
\theta_{n}\xi
is independent of
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}
under
P\in\Theta^{e}\cap\Theta_{n}
. In fact, for any
\phi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),\ m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{P}[\phi(\xi)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{P}[\phi(\theta_{n}\xi)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{P}[E_{P}[\phi(\theta_{n}\xi)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{P}[E_{P_{\omega}\circ\theta_{n}^{-1}}[\phi(\xi)]],

where
P_{\omega}
is a regular conditional probability of
P(\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{n})
.
Since
P_{\omega}\circ\theta_{n}^{-1}\in\Theta
by Lemma
4.7
and
P\in\Theta^{e}
, we conclude that
E_{P}[\phi(\theta_{n}\xi)|\mathcal{F}_{n}]
is a constant.

Now we show that
P_{1,n}\in\Xi_{n}^{e}
. If
P_{1,n}\notin\Xi_{n}^{e}
, there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\mu,\nu\in\Xi_{n}
,
\mu\neq\nu
such that
\alpha\mu+(1-\alpha)\nu=P_{1,n}
. Set

and
R=\nu\otimes P
. Then

,
Q\neq R
and

. This is a contradiction.

b)
\Theta^{e}\cap\Theta_{n}\supset\Theta^{*}_{n}
.

Let
P\in\Theta^{*}_{n}
. We only need to prove that
P\in\Theta^{e}
. If there exit
\alpha\in(0,1)
,

,

. Since
P_{in+1,(i+1)n}\in\Xi^{e}_{n}
and

P_{in+1,(i+1)n}=Q_{in+1,(i+1)n}=R_{in+1,(i+1)n},\ for\ i\geq 0.

Assuming that
P_{1,kn}=Q_{1,kn}=R_{1,kn}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we shall prove that

P_{1,(k+1)n}=Q_{1,(k+1)n}=R_{1,(k+1)n}.

Let
Q_{\omega}
be a regular conditional probability of
Q[\cdot|\mathcal{F}_{kn}]
. By Lemma
4.7
, we have
Q_{\omega}\circ\theta_{kn}^{-1}\in\Theta
,

-a.s., and consequently,
Q_{\omega}\circ\theta_{kn}^{-1}[(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})\in\cdot]\in\Xi_{n}
,

-a.s. Note that, for
\phi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

E_{Q_{kn+1,(k+1)n}}[\phi]=E_{Q}[\phi(\theta_{kn}\xi)]=E_{Q}[E_{Q}[\phi(\theta_{kn}\xi)|\mathcal{F}_{kn}]]=E_{Q}[E_{Q_{\omega}\circ\theta_{kn}^{-1}}[\phi(\xi)]].

Since

E_{Q}[\phi(\theta_{kn}\xi)|\mathcal{F}_{kn}]=E_{Q_{\omega}\circ\theta_{kn}^{-1}}[\phi(\xi)]=E_{P_{kn+1,(k+1)n}}[\phi],\ P_{1,kn}\textmd{-a.s.}

So we have
P_{1,(k+1)n}=Q_{1,(k+1)n}
and, similarily,

Example 5.1

Let
\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. For
\omega,\bar{\omega}\in\Omega
, define
d(\omega,\bar{\omega})=\Sigma_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{k}}|\omega(k)-\bar{\omega}(k)|
. For
\omega\in\Omega
, let
\delta_{\omega}
be the Dirac measure on
\Omega
. Set
\Theta=\{\delta_{\omega}|\ \omega\in\Omega\}
and
\xi_{n}(\omega)=\omega(n)
.
It is easy to check that
\Omega
is compact with respect to the metric
d
, and that
\xi_{n}
is continuous on
(\Omega,d)
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We claim that

\{\xi_{n}\}
is
i.i.d.
under the sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}E_{\delta_{\omega}}
.

It suffices to prove that
\xi_{m}
is independent of
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m-1}
, for any
m\in\mathbb{R}
. In fact, for any
\phi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\mathbb{E}[\phi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1},\xi_{m})]=\max\limits_{y\in\{0,1\}}\phi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1},y)\leq\max\limits_{\vec{y}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\phi(y_{1},\cdots,y_{m})=\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m})].

Therefore,

\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\phi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1},\xi_{m})]|_{x_{i}=\xi_{i}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m})].

On the other hand, assume
\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m})]=\phi(x^{0}_{1},\cdots,x^{0}_{m})
for some
(x^{0}_{1},\cdots,x^{0}_{m})\in\{0,1\}^{m}

\mathbb{E}[\phi(x^{0}_{1},\cdots,x^{0}_{m-1},\xi_{m})]=\phi(x^{0}_{1},\cdots,x^{0}_{m}),
and, consequently,

\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\phi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{m-1},\xi_{m})]|_{x_{i}=\xi_{i}}]\geq\mathbb{E}[\phi(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m})].

It follows from the above arguments that

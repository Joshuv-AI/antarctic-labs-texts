The Isoperimetric Problem in Randers Poincaré Disc
Arti Sahu Gangopadhyay, Ranadip Gangopadhyay, Hemangi Madhusudan Shah, Bankteshwar Tiwari

Abstract
It is known that a simply connected Riemann surface satisfies the isoperimetric equality if and only if it has constant Gaussian curvature. In this article, we show that Randers Poincaré disc satisfies the isoperimetric equality with respect to different volume forms. These metrics do not necessarily have constant (negative) flag curvature. Thus we show that the Osserman’s result [ 11 ] in the Riemannian case can not be extended to the corresponding Finslerian case. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 53B40, 53C60. keywords: Isoperimetric problem, Randers Poincaré disc , calculus of variations, conjugate points.

Introduction

The isoperimetric problem is a more than two millenia old problem which says that: among all the simple closed curve with fixed perimeter, the circle encloses the maximum area. For the non-zero constant curvature spaces the isoperimetric inequality is given by

L^{2}\geq 4\pi A-kA^{2},

where
L
is the length of a simple closed curve,
A
is the area bounded by the curve and
k
is the curvature of the space. The isoperimetric problem has several important applications in mathematical physics. In 1903, Poincaré stated with incomplete (variational) proof that of all the solids with a given volume the sphere has the minimum electrostatic capacity and Szegö gave the first complete proof for Poincaré’s theorem on electrostatic capacity in 1930
[
13
]
. In recent years scientists came to know that an incredibly intense magnetic fields can arise in several realistic physical system (for example, certain kind of neutron star). To study these kind of physical problems, the knowledge of the isoperimetric problem in hyperbolic manifolds and asymptotic hyperbolic manifolds turns out to be essential
[
10
]
. The study of the isoperimetric inequality has been done extensively by several mathematicians, for example see
[
1
,
6
,
9
,
15
]
. In Finsler geometry, Zhou studied the isoperimetric problem for the Berwald metric,

F(x,y)=\frac{(\sqrt{|y|^{2}-(|x|^{2}|y|^{2})-\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}+\langle x,y\rangle)^{2}}{(1-|x|^{2})(\sqrt{|y|^{2}-(|x|^{2}|y|^{2})-\langle x,y\rangle^{2})}}

with respect to the Busemann-Hausdorff volume form
[
16
]
. Zhan and Zhou studied this problem with respect to the Holmes-Thompson volume form
[
17
]
and showed that the circles
\gamma_{0}(t)=(a\cos t,a\sin t),a<1
, are the local solutions of the isoperimetric problem. Li and Mo
[
8
]
studied the isoperimetric problem for the two dimensional Funk metric

F(x,y)=\frac{(\sqrt{|y|^{2}-(|x|^{2}|y|^{2})-\langle x,y\rangle^{2}}+\langle x,y\rangle)}{2(1-|x|^{2})}.

Recently, Sahu et al.,
[
12
]
studied the isoperimetric problem for a special class of Randers metrics, which are deformations of Euclidean metrics by some suitable one-form
\beta
.

In this paper, we study the isoperimetric problem in Randers Poincaré disc model. In this model we consider the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the Poincaré metric
\displaystyle{\alpha=\frac{2\sqrt{(dx^{1})^{2}+(dx^{2})^{2}}}{1-(x^{1})^{2}-(x^{2})^{2}}}
. Consider the Randers metric
F=\alpha+\beta
, which can be thought deformation of the Poincaré metric
\alpha
by an exact one-form
\beta=df
, where
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function with
\|\beta\|_{\alpha}
is constant and
\|\beta\|_{\alpha}<1
. We call the so obtained Finsler space
(\mathbb{D},F)
as
Randers Poincaré disc
. In the present paper we study the isoperimetric problem in the Randers Poincaré disc
(\mathbb{D},F)
.

In general, a metric of the form
F=\alpha+\beta
, where
\alpha
is any Riemannian metric and
\beta
is a one form with
\|\beta\|_{\alpha}<1
, is called the Randers metric and has several application in physics and biology. Bao et. al.,
[
3
]
proved that the solution of a Zermelo Navigation problem
in a Riemannian manifold
(M,h)
with a time independent vector field
W
satisfying
h(W,W)<1
, gives a unique Randers metric on the manifold
M
and conversely.

Now we state our main results of this paper

Proposition 1.1

Let
(\mathbb{D},\alpha)
be the hyperbolic Poincaré disc where,
\displaystyle{\alpha=\frac{2\sqrt{(dx^{1})^{2}+(dx^{2})^{2}}}{1-(x^{1})^{2}-(x^{2})^{2}}}
and
\beta=df
be an exact one form with constant norm and
||\beta||_{\alpha}<1
. Then
\beta
can be explicitely expressed by

\beta=b\frac{x^{1}dx^{1}+x^{2}dx^{2}}{(1-(x^{1})^{2}-(x^{2})^{2})\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}}

with
0<b<1
. Hence
F=\alpha+\beta
is the Randers Poincaré metric on
\mathbb{D}
.

Proposition 1.2

Let
(\mathbb{D},F=\alpha+\beta)
be the Randers Poincaré disc as described in the above Proposition. Then the space
(\mathbb{D},F)
does not have constant negative flag curvature.

Theorem 1.1

Let
(\mathbb{D},F=\alpha+\beta)
be the Randers Poincaré disc. Then the circles of the form
\gamma_{0}=(a\cos t,a\sin t)
, are solutions of the isoperimetric problem, with respect to the volume forms: Busemann-Hausdorff, Holmes-Thompson, Maximum and Minimum.

Thus our result also holds for spaces which may not have constant negative flag curvature. Note that in
contrast
to the Riemannian case, where it is known that the isoperimetric equality holds on a Riemann surface if and only if it is of constant sectional curvature. For details see Osserman’s result, that is, corollary of Theorem
6
, page-
9
of
[
11
]

The sufficient conditions for the solvability of the isoperimetric problem in Randers Poincaré disc

In this section, we discuss about the sufficiency condition for the solvability of the isoperimetric problem essentially due to Hesteens
[
7
]
in a slightly modified form. Towards this, we shall focus on the calculus of variations in a parameteric form in
2
-dimensional Euclidean space.
First we recall some definitions. More details can be found in
[

,

,

.

Admissible curve:
A continuous curve
\gamma
, defined on
\left[t_{0},t_{1}\right]
, in a manifold is called an
admissible curve
, if there exists a partition
P:=\left\{t_{0}=a_{0}<a_{1}<...<a_{k}=t_{1}\right\}
of the interval
[t_{0},t_{1}]
such that
\gamma
has continuous derivative in each subinterval
\left[a_{i},a_{i+1}\right]
, for all
i=0,1,...,k-1
.

The Isoperimetric problem in Randers Poincare disc:

We discuss the isoperimetric problem in Randers Poincare disc
\mathbb{D}
, which is an open unit disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
equipped with Randers metric. Let the parametric form of an arc in
\mathbb{D}
is

\gamma(t):=(x^{1}(t),x^{2}(t))
, where the functions
x^{i}(t)
are assumed to be of class
C^{2}
and

({\dot{x}^{1}(t)})^{2}+({\dot{x}^{2}(t)})^{2}\neq 0
for any
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
.

An integral

\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}g(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))\;dt,

can be shown to be independent of the parameterization of the
arc
\gamma
if and only if the integrand
f
is positively homogeneous in
\dot{\gamma}
of degree one.

Consider the Poincaré disc
({\mathbb{D}},F)
with some volume form
dV_{\mu}
. The isoperimetric
problem under consideration can be stated as follows:

\begin{split}\text{To find a curve in $(\mathbb{D},F)$ which maximizes}\penalty\ \{A_{\mu}(D)|\penalty\ D\penalty\ \mbox{is the domain enclosed by an }\\
\text{arbitrary simple closed piecewise smooth curve under the constrained that }\\
\text{ the curve's length}\penalty\ L=l\}.\hskip 213.39566pt\end{split}

Here
A_{\mu}
denotes the area with respect to the volume form
dV_{\mu}
and
L
denotes the length of the curve.

Thus we consider the set of all admissible curves joining two fixed points for which the integral
L=\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}g(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))dt
takes a constant value
l
. To solve the problem, we look for the admissible curve
\gamma(t)=(x^{1}(t),x^{2}(t))
in
\mathbb{D}
for which the integral
A=\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}f(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t))dt
has its
extremum
.

Lagrange functional:
To solve the isoperimetric problem
in
\mathbb{D}
, we first consider the Lagrange functional:

J(\gamma)=A+\lambda L=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}h(t,\gamma,\dot{\gamma};\lambda)dt,

(8)

where,

h=f+\lambda g,

(9)

and
\lambda
is a Lagrange multiplier.

Isoperimetric extremal:
An admissible curve
\gamma(t)
is said to be an
isoperimetric extremal
, if it satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equations:

\frac{\partial h}{\partial x^{i}}-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial\dot{x}^{i}}\right)=0,\penalty\ i=1,2.

(10)

Normality of an admissible curve:
An admissible curve
\gamma(t)
satisfying the Euler-Lagrange equations is said to be
normal
, if
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are never zero functions, where

P_{i}=g_{x^{i}}-\frac{d}{dt}g_{\dot{x}^{i}},\quad i=1,2.

(11)

Weierstrass function:

The
Weierstrass
function
E
, for the function
h
defined by
(
9
), is given by

E(t,\gamma,\dot{\gamma},u)=h(t,\gamma,u)-h(t,\gamma,\dot{\gamma})-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}(u^{j}-\dot{x}^{j})\frac{\partial h(t,\gamma,\dot{\gamma})}{\partial\dot{x}^{j}},

(12)

where
h(t,\gamma,\dot{\gamma})=f(t,\gamma,\dot{\gamma})+\lambda g(t,\gamma,\dot{\gamma})
.

Second variation of
\boldmath{J}\unboldmath
along a curve:
The
second variation
of
J
along the curve
\gamma_{0}
is of the form

J^{\prime\prime}(\gamma_{0},y)=\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}2\omega(t,y(t),\dot{y}(t))dt,

(13)

where,
2\omega(t,y(t),\dot{y}(t))=\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{2}h_{x^{i}x^{j}}y^{i}y^{j}+2h_{x^{i}\dot{x}^{j}}y^{i}\dot{y}^{j}+h_{\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}^{j}}\dot{y}^{i}\dot{y}^{j}
.

The isoperimetric problem in Randers Poincaré disc with respect to different volume forms

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We begin by proving the following proposition:

Proof of Proposition
1.1

Since
\beta
is an exact one form and has constant norm with respect to
\alpha
, we can write

\beta=df=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{1}}dx^{1}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{2}}dx^{2}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\|\beta\|^{2}_{\alpha}=c^{2},

(28)

for some constant

as follows:

\|\beta\|^{2}_{\alpha}=a^{ij}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{j}}=\frac{(1-(x^{1})^{2}-(x^{2})^{2})^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{1}}\right)^{2}+\frac{(1-(x^{1})^{2}-(x^{2})^{2})^{2}}{4}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{2}}\right)^{2}.

(29)

Let us assume

r:=\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad f(x^{1},x^{2}):=\phi(r).

(30)

Differentiating the above equation with respect to

and

we get the following,

\frac{\partial r}{\partial x^{1}}=\frac{x^{1}}{\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}},\quad\frac{\partial r}{\partial x^{2}}=\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}}

(31)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{1}}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial x^{1}}=\frac{x^{1}}{\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r},\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{2}}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial x^{2}}=\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}.

(32)

Substituting these values in (

(1-r^{2})^{2}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\right)^{2}=c^{2}.

(33)

Solving the above equation we get,

\phi(r)=\frac{c}{2}\log\frac{1+r}{1-r}.

(34)

Substituting the value of

f(x^{1},x^{2})=\frac{c}{2}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}}{1-\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}}.

(35)

Now from

To prove Proposition

Yasuda-Shimada theorem

[
2

A Randers space with metric

, where
\alpha=\sqrt{a_{ij}y^{i}y^{j}}

has constant negative flag curvature

Towards the classification of finite-dimensional diagonally graded commutative algebras
Yunnan Li, Shi Yu

Abstract
. Any finite-dimensional commutative (associative) graded algebra with all nonzero homogeneous subspaces one-dimensional is defined by a symmetric coefficient matrix. This algebraic structure gives a basic kind of A A -graded algebras originally studied by Arnold. In this paper, we call them diagonally graded commutative algebras (DGCAs) and verify that the isomorphism classes of DGCAs of dimension ≤ 7 \leq 7 over an arbitrary field are in bijection with the equivalence classes consisting of coefficient matrices with the same distribution of nonzero entries, while dramatically there may be infinitely many isomorphism classes of dimension n n corresponding to one equivalence class of coefficient matrices when n ≥ 8 n\geq 8 . Furthermore, we adopt the Skjelbred-Sund method of central extensions to study the isomorphism classes of DGCAs, and associate any DGCA with a undirected simple graph to explicitly describe its corresponding second (graded) commutative cohomology group as an affine variety.



1.
Introduction

Associative algebra, as a fundamental algebraic structure, has been studied extensively for many years, with the algebraic classification of associative algebras as an ancient and longstanding topic worthy of discussion. However, since this algebraic system is huge and extraordinarily complicated, the classification of associative algebras up to isomorphism is far from being completed.

At present, there have been many achievements about the classification of associative algebras of finite dimension.
Especially, nilpotent associative algebras form a large class of associative algebras, which has fruitful classification results. For example, de Graaf completed the classification of 4-dimensional nilpotent associative algebras over arbitrary fields in
[
9
]
by constructing central extensions of smaller dimensional nilpotent associative algebras. Recently, Kaygorodov et al. discussed the classification of 5-dimensional nilpotent commutative associative algebras over the complex field also based on the Skjelbred-Sund method of central extensions
[
19
]
. In addition, Kaygorodov et al. specifically gave a geometric classification of
n
-dimensional nilpotent, commutative nilpotent and anticommutative nilpotent algebras in
[
18
]
.

On the other hand, the study of graded algebras is an active area, applied in various fields of mathematics and physics. The gradings on classes of algebras provide a powerful tool for understanding and analyzing their algebraic structures. For example, the concept of grading was widely used in the study of Lie algebras (e.g.
[
16
,
23
,
12
]
), and also other related algebraic structures, such as associative algebras
[
2
]
, pre-Lie algebras
[
8
,
20
]
and Jordan algebras
[
3
]
.
In
[
1
]
Arnold introduced the notion of
A
-graded algebra
, and mainly studied the classification of commutative
A
-graded algebras with 3 multiplicative generators and all nontrivial homogeneous subspaces of dimension 1. Also, Arnold initiated the study of
A
-graded ideals in
[
1
]
, and found that under certain circumstances, this ideal structure can be encoded as a continued fraction. Subsequently, the authors in
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
supplemented classification method of
A
-graded algebras with 3 generators in detail. General study of
A
-graded algebras and
A
-graded ideals can be found in e.g.
[
27
,
28
,
24
]
.

In this paper, we study a class of finite-dimensional commutative associative
\mathbb{N}^{+}
-graded algebras (consequently nilpotent), with all nonzero homogeneous subspaces being
1
-dimensional, and the corresponding graded multiplication can be characterized by a symmetric coefficient matrix.
This algebraic structure is actually one basic kind of
A
-graded algebras with the first generator of degree 1, and we specially call them
diagonally graded commutative algebras
(DGCAs). Based on such a characterization by coefficient matrices, we try to attack the classification problem of finite-dimensional DGCAs.

It is noted that if commutative associative algebras are endowed with graded structure, then the algebras originally classified in one isomorphism class are often further divided into several graded isomorphism classes. In addition, the classification of one algebraic structure is closely related to which field it is defined over. For example, the classification of real simple Lie algebras can be obtained by classifying involutions of compact Lie algebras based on the classification of complex simple Lie algebras. By contrast, the classification of DGCAs of low dimension has a prominent feature, that is, the isomorphism classes over any prime field are already indivisible, and do not subdivide over arbitrary field extensions.

Based on our initial classification results in low dimension,
we expect that the classification of DGCAs is simply determined by the distribution of nonzero entries in their coefficient matrices, namely the existence of a one-to-one correspondence between the isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional DGCAs and certain equivalence classes of coefficient matrices.
However, through the study of central extensions of DGCAs, we find that such an expectation collapses once the dimension
n>7
.
As a matter of fact, there appear infinitely many isomorphism classes with the same distribution of nonzero entries in their coefficient matrices.
One can compare such a variation with the classification result of Poonen
in
[
25
]
saying that there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of commutative associative unital algebras of dimension up to 6 over an algebraically closed field, but infinitely many of dimension
>6
.

Concretely, we interpret any
n
-dimensional DGCA
{\mathfrak{p}}
as a
1
-dimensional central extension of a DGCA
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
of dimension
n-1
(Proposition
3.1
), and describe the corresponding second (graded) commutative cohomology group of
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
explicitly as an affine variety. Any of its orbit closures is indeed a toric variety, under the action of the automorphism group
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})
of
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
.

This paper is organized by the upcoming three sections. In Section
2
, we introduce the notion of diagonally graded commutative algebras and their isomorphism classes, then state our classification result on DGCAs (Theorem
2.6
).
In Section
3
, we adopt the Skjelbred-Sund method to study central extensions of DGCAs, and show that the nontrivial isomorphism classes of central extensions of a DGCA
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime}
are in bijection with the
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}^{\prime})
-orbits in the Grassmannian of
1
-dimensional subspaces of
2
-cocycles (Theorem
3.5
). After that, we construct a kind of graphs associated with coefficient matrices to explicitly characterize the second (graded) commutative cohomology groups (Theorem
3.11
), which control the isomorphism classes of central extensions of DGCAs.
In particular, we obtain a criterion when all nontrivial central extensions of a DGCA form exactly one isomorphism class (Theorem
3.20
). In Section
4
,
we completely list all isomorphism classes of DGCAs of dimension up to 5.
Also, some isomorphism classes of DGCAs of dimension 6 and 7 are presented in the same manner as an illustration.

Notations.

Let
\mathbb{N}
(resp.
\mathbb{N}^{+}
) be the set of nonnegative (resp. positive) integers.
All the matrices, vector spaces, algebras and linear maps are taken over an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
unless otherwise specified. Also, we denote
\mathbbm{k}^{*}=\mathbbm{k}\backslash\{0\}
and
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
for any positive integer
n
.

2.
Diagonally graded commutative algebras and their isomorphism classes

Definition 2.1
.

An
associative algebra

A
is a vector space with a bilinear multiplication
(x,y)\mapsto xy
such that

(1)

\displaystyle x(yz)=(xy)z,\quad\forall x,y,z\in A.

When
xy=yx,\forall x,y\in A
, we say that the associative algebra
A
is a
commutative (associative) algebra
.

If the (commutative) algebra
A
is an
\mathbb{N}
-graded vector space
A=\oplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
such that
A_{n}A_{m}\subset A_{n+m}
for any
n,m\geq 0
, then we call
A
a
(commutative) graded algebra
.

An
isomorphism
between two (graded) algebras
A
and
A^{\prime}
is a (graded) linear isomorphism
\varphi:A\to A^{\prime}
such that

(2)

\displaystyle\varphi(xy)=\varphi(x)\varphi(y),\quad\forall x,y\in A.

Clearly, any finite-dimensional
\mathbb{N}^{+}
-graded associative algebra
A=\oplus_{n\geq 1}A_{n}
is
nilpotent
by its graded multiplication.

From now on, we focus on an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{N}^{+}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{p}
with a fixed homogeneous linear basis
\{p_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
such that
\deg p_{i}=i
, so call
\mathfrak{p}
a
diagonally graded
vector space with all its nonzero homogeneous subspaces one-dimensional. If
\mathfrak{p}
has a graded bilinear multiplication
(x,y)\mapsto xy,\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{p}}
, then there exists an
n\times n
coefficient matrix
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
such that

(3)

\displaystyle p_{i}p_{j}=c_{ij}p_{i+j},

where we take
c_{ij}=0
and set
p_{i+j}=0
if
i+j>n
by convention. From the relation (
1
), one can see that
\mathfrak{p}
is a graded associative algebra, if and only if

(4)

\displaystyle c_{jk}c_{i,\,j+k}=c_{ij}c_{i+j,\,k},\quad\forall\,i,j,k\geq 1.

In particular,
{\mathfrak{p}}
is commutative if and only if the coefficient matrix
C
is a symmetric matrix.

In order to classify the graded associative algebraic structures on
\mathfrak{p}
, we need to find all their isomorphism classes.

Theorem 2.2
.

Two
n
-dimensional graded associative algebras underlying
{\mathfrak{p}}
with coefficient matrices
C
and
C^{\prime}
respectively
are isomorphic under the graded algebra isomorphism
\varphi
, if and only if there exists a tuple
(b_{i})_{i\in[n]}
of nonzero coefficients such that

(5)

\displaystyle b_{i+j}c_{ij}=b_{i}b_{j}c^{\prime}_{ij},\quad\forall i,j\geq 1,

where
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
and
C^{\prime}=(c^{\prime}_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
. Here set
b_{i+j}=0
, when
i+j>n
by convention.

Proof.

As
\varphi
is a graded linear automorphism of
\mathfrak{p}
, there are nonzero coefficients
b_{i}\in\mathbbm{k},\ i\in[n]
, such that
\varphi(p_{i})=b_{i}p_{i}
, where
\{p_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
is the fixed homogeneous linear basis of
\mathfrak{p}
. Substituting it and Eq. (
3
) into Eq. (
2
), one can easily check that the relation (
5
) holds.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

The above commutative graded algebra
{\mathfrak{p}}
associated with a coefficient matrix
C
is called a
diagonally graded commutative algebra
(with the abbreviation “DGCA”) and denoted by
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
.

For any
n
-dimensional DGCA
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
, there is an associated
(n-1)
-dimensional
DGCA
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{C})
as its
truncation
, where
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
is the graded subspace of
{\mathfrak{p}}
spanned by
\{p_{i}\}_{i\in[n-1]}
and
\bar{C}=(\bar{c}_{ij})_{i,j\in[n-1]}
is the
(n-1)\times(n-1)
symmetric matrix defined by

\bar{c}_{ij}=\begin{cases}c_{ij},&i+j<n,\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Correspondingly, we call
\bar{C}
the
truncation
of
C
.

The notion of diagonally graded algebra was originally introduced by Arnold in
[
1
]
, where he called them
A
-graded algebras and actually classified all unital DGCAs with three multiplicative generators (and
p_{0}=1
). Using our notation of DGCAs, we can obtain the following result concerning about multiplicative generators of a DGCA.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any
n
-dimensional DGCA
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
, let

J_{C}=\{k\in[n]\,|\,c_{ij}=0\ \mbox{if}\ i+j=k\}.

Then
\{p_{k}\}_{k\in J_{C}}
is a minimal set of multiplicative generators of
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
.

Proof.

Indeed, we must have
1\in J_{C}
, so
J_{C}
is nonempty. According to Eq. (
3
), for any
k\in[n]
,
p_{k}
can be expressed as a polynomial of
p_{i}
’s with smaller
i
, if and only if
k\notin J_{C}
. Therefore,
\{p_{k}\}_{k\in J_{C}}
is a minimal set of multiplicative generators of
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the set of
n\times n
symmetric matrices
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
over
\mathbbm{k}
satisfying Eq. (
4
) and
c_{ij}=0
if
i+j>n
.

Introduce the equivalence relation
\sim
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
such that
C\sim C^{\prime}
if and only if the distributions of nonzero entries in
C
and
C^{\prime}
coincide.

Especially, each equivalence class in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
with respect to
\sim
has a unique
(0,1)
-matrix as its canonical representative.

At first we provide the following concise classification of DGCAs of low dimension (see Section
4
).

Theorem 2.6
.

When
n\leq 7
, two
n
-dimensional DGCAs
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
and
{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})
are isomorphic if and only if
C\sim C^{\prime}
. Namely, the set of isomorphism classes of
n
-dimensional DGCAs is in bijection with the quotient set
\mathcal{M}_{n}/\sim
of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
modulo
\sim
.
In particular, all
(0,1)
-matrices in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
as the coefficient matrices define a canonical representative system of isomorphism classes of DGCAs.

Proof.

The stated classification of DGCAs of low dimension can be checked directly, and we will do it in Section
4
.
∎

Unfortunately, the complexity of classification of DGCAs in general is far beyond our expectation.
If
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
and
{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})
are isomorphic to each other, then the positions of nonzero entries of
C
and
C^{\prime}
are the same by Eq. (
5
), so
C\sim C^{\prime}
. But the opposite is not necessarily true when
n>7
. After some trial, we find a counterexample of dimension
n=8
as follows.

Example 2.7
.

Let
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in[8]}
be any
8\times 8
symmetric matrix with nonzero entries

c_{24}=c_{42},\ c_{33},\ c_{25}=c_{52},\ c_{34}=c_{43},\ c_{35}=c_{53},\ c_{44}.

It is clear that
C\in\mathcal{M}_{8}
. Let
C^{\prime}
be the
(0,1)
-matrix such that
C\sim C^{\prime}
.

According to Eq. (
5
), if
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)\cong{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})
, then there exists
(b_{i})_{i\in[8]}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{\times 8}
such that

\displaystyle b_{6}

\displaystyle=b_{2}b_{4}c_{24}=b_{3}^{2}c_{33},

\displaystyle b_{7}

\displaystyle=b_{2}b_{5}c_{25}=b_{3}b_{4}c_{34},

\displaystyle b_{8}

\displaystyle=b_{3}b_{5}c_{35}=b_{4}^{2}c_{44}.

Hence, we have
b_{4}=\dfrac{b_{3}^{2}c_{33}}{b_{2}c_{24}}
and then

b_{5}=\dfrac{b_{3}b_{4}c_{34}}{b_{2}c_{25}}=\dfrac{b_{3}c_{34}}{b_{2}c_{25}}\cdot\dfrac{b_{3}^{2}c_{33}}{b_{2}c_{24}}=\dfrac{b_{3}^{3}c_{33}c_{34}}{b_{2}^{2}c_{24}c_{25}}.

Also,

b_{5}=\dfrac{b_{4}^{2}c_{44}}{b_{3}c_{35}}=\dfrac{c_{44}}{b_{3}c_{35}}\cdot\left(\dfrac{b_{3}^{2}c_{33}}{b_{2}c_{24}}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{b_{3}^{3}c_{33}^{2}c_{44}}{b_{2}^{2}c_{24}^{2}c_{35}}.

Therefore, we obtain the constraint
c_{25}c_{33}c_{44}=c_{24}c_{34}c_{35}
, which means that the only condition
C\sim C^{\prime}
can not guarantee the isomorphism
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)\cong{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})
.

In fact, there have already been infinitely many isomorphism classes of DGCAs of dimension 8 (see Example
3.17
).
As a result, we need to search for more elaborate technique to study the isomorphism classes of DGCAs.

3.
Classification by the Skjelbred-Sund method of central extensions

The Skjelbred-Sund method is well-known for classifying nilpotent Lie algebras
[
26
,
10
]
.
In this section, we study the classification problem of central extensions of finite-dimensional DGCAs by a graded analogue of such a method, which has also been adopted to the classification of nilpotent associative algebras of small dimension
[
9
]
; see also the recently published survey
[
17
]
for other cases where it was developed.

3.1.
Central extensions of diagonally graded commutative algebras

First we recall some terminology.
Let
A
be a commutative associative algebra,
V
a vector space, and
\theta:A\otimes A\to V
a symmetric bilinear
map.

Set
A_{\theta}=A\oplus V
. For
a,b\in A,v,w\in V
, we define

(6)

(a+v)\cdot_{\theta}(b+w)=ab+\theta(a,b).

Then
(A_{\theta},\cdot_{\theta})
is a commutative associative algebra if and only if

\theta(ab,c)=\theta(a,bc)

for all
a,b,c\in A
. All these symmetric invariant bilinear maps
\theta
are exactly the
2
-cocycles of
A
with coefficients in the trivial
A
-module
V
, when one studies the commutative cohomology of
A

[
15
]
. The space of all
2
-cocycles is denoted by

Z^{2}(A,V)
. The algebra
A_{\theta}
is called a
central extension
of
A

by
V
(such that
A_{\theta}V=VA_{\theta}=0
).

Let
f:A\to V
be a linear map, and define
\delta f(a,b)=f(ab)
. Then
\delta f
is a
2-coboundary. The space of all 2-coboundaries is denoted by
B^{2}(A,V)
. Note that
A_{\theta}\cong A_{\theta+\delta f}
and particularly
A_{\delta f}\cong A\times V
, the direct product of algebras
A
and
V
, which we call the
trivial
central extension. Therefore, we consider the second cohomology space
H^{2}(A,V)=Z^{2}(A,V)/B^{2}(A,V)
.

Correspondingly, we can define the graded version of central extension
A_{\theta}
of a commutative associative graded algebra
A
by a graded module
V
, requiring that the
2
-cocycle
\theta
is graded.
Also, we use the distinct notations
Z_{g}^{2}(A,V),B_{g}^{2}(A,V)
and
H_{g}^{2}(A,V)
for the graded version of the commutative cohomology.
Now for our main target, namely DGCAs, we actually have

Proposition 3.1
.

Given any
n
-dimensional DGCA
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
with the fixed homogeneous linear basis
\{p_{i}\}_{i\in[n]}
, let
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{C})
be its
(n-1)
-dimensional truncation as in Definition
2.3
. Then

{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
is a central extension of
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{C})
by the
1
-dimensional homogeneous vector space
V
spanned by
p_{n}
.

Proof.

In fact, we can define the graded
2
-cocycle
\theta:\bar{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{C})\otimes\bar{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{C})\to V
by

\theta(p_{i},p_{j})=\delta_{i+j,\,n}c_{ij}p_{n},\quad\forall i,j\in[n-1],

whose cocycle condition is guaranteed by Eq. (
4
).
Then
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
is naturally identified with the central extension
\bar{\mathfrak{p}}(\bar{C})_{\theta}
as commutative associative graded algebras.
∎

Conversely, for any
(n-1)
-dimensional DGCA
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
and
1
-dimensional homogeneous vector space
V
of degree
n
, the central extension
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\theta}
with
\theta\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
is clearly an
n
-dimensional DGCA.
In such a specified situation, any graded linear map from
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
to
V
is zero, so
B_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)=0
and
H_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)=Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
.
Consequently, the trivial isomorphism class of central extensions of
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
only contains
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{0}
.
Also, for any
\theta\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)

we have coefficients
\theta_{i}\in\mathbbm{k},\ i\in[n-1]
, such that

(7)

\theta(p_{i},p_{j})=\delta_{i+j,\,n}\theta_{i}p_{n},\quad\forall i,j\in[n-1].

Let
\sigma:{\mathfrak{p}}(C)\to{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})
be a graded algebra isomorphism of two
(n-1)
-dimensional DGCAs, and
V=\mathbbm{k}p_{n}
be the
1
-dimensional homogeneous vector space of degree
n
. Define

\theta_{\sigma}\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
by

(8)

\theta_{\sigma}(x,y)=\theta(\sigma(x),\sigma(y))

for any
\theta\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime}),V)
and
x,y\in{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
.

Let
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
be the graded automorphism group of
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
, consisting of diagonal automorphisms
\varphi
such that

\varphi(p_{i})=b_{i}p_{i}
for certain
b_{i}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}\ (i\in[n])

by Theorem
2.2
, so we write
\varphi=(b_{i})_{i\in[n]}
for convenience. Then Eq. (
8
) defines a right action of
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
on
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
.

As a special case of Theorem
2.2
, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

Given any
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
,

(9)

{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))=\left\{(b_{i})_{i\in[n]}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{\times n}\,\big|\,b_{i+j}=b_{i}b_{j}\ {\rm if}\ c_{ij}\neq 0\right\}.

In particular, if
C\sim C^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
, then
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))={\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime}))
.

Morevoer,
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
is a closed subgroup of the torus
(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{\times n}

of dimension
n-n(C)+t(C)
, where

n(C)=\#\{(i,j)\in(\mathbb{N}^{+})^{\times 2}\,|\,c_{ij}\neq 0,\,i\leq j\}

and
t(C)
is the number of non-redundant equalities in Eq. (
4
) satisfying

c_{ij}c_{i+j,\,k}\neq 0
.

Proof.

First by Eq. (
5
), we obtain the stated description of
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
to see that it is a closed subgroup of the torus
(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{\times n}
subjecting to certain polynomial constraints.

It remains to show that there are exactly
n(C)-t(C)
non-redundant polynomial equalities therein by the symmetry of
C
, so the dimension of
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
is obtained. In fact, for any
i,j,k\geq 1
such that

c_{ij}c_{i+j,\,k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ass_coef}}}{{=}}c_{jk}c_{i,\,j+k}\neq 0,

we have the following 4 polynomial equalities

b_{i+j}=b_{i}b_{j},\,b_{j+k}=b_{j}b_{k},\,b_{i+j+k}=b_{i+j}b_{k},\,b_{i+j+k}=b_{i}b_{j+k},

any 3 of which imply the remaining one. On the other hand,
any equality in Eq. (
4
) involving zero coefficients will not produce redundance.
∎

Example 3.3
.

Given
b\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
, by Eq. (
9
) we have
\varphi_{b}=(b^{i})_{i\in[n]}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
for any
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Example 3.4
.

Let
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j\in[6]}
be the symmetric matrix in
\mathcal{M}_{6}
with

c_{11}=c_{12}=c_{21}=c_{13}=c_{31}=c_{22}=c_{14}=c_{41}=c_{23}=c_{32}=1

and zeros elsewhere. Then Eq. (
9
) says that

{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))=\left\{(b_{i})_{i\in[6]}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{\times 6}\,\big|\,b_{2}=b_{1}^{2},\,b_{3}=b_{1}b_{2},\,b_{4}=b_{1}b_{3}=b_{2}^{2},\,b_{5}=b_{1}b_{4}=b_{2}b_{3}\right\}.

That is,
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))=\left\{(b_{i})_{i\in[6]}\in(\mathbbm{k}^{*})^{\times 6}\,\big|\,b_{i}=b_{1}^{i},\,\forall i\in[5]\right\}
is of dimension 2. On the other hand,

n(C)=6
, and
t(C)=2
counts the following non-redundant equalities

c_{12}c_{13}=c_{11}c_{22},\quad c_{11}c_{23}=c_{13}c_{14},

together to deduce the third one,
c_{12}c_{23}=c_{14}c_{22}
. So we also obtain that

\dim{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))=6-n(C)+t(C)=2.

Theorem 3.5
.

Two central extensions
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\gamma}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})_{\theta}
for
\gamma\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
and
\theta\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime}),V)
are isomorphic if and only if there exists a graded algebra isomorphism
\sigma:{\mathfrak{p}}(C)\to{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})
such that
\theta_{\sigma}
and
\gamma
are colinear in
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
.

In particular,
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\theta}\cong{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\gamma}
for nonzero
2
-cocycles
\theta,\gamma\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
if and only if
[\theta],[\gamma]
lie in the same
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
-orbit of
the Grassmannian of
1
-dimensional subspaces of
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
, where
[\theta]={\rm span}_{\mathbbm{k}}\{\theta\}
.

Proof.

As discussed in Prop.
3.1
, we can rewrite two matrices
C_{\gamma}
and
C^{\prime}_{\theta}
in
\mathcal{M}_{n}
augmented from
C
and
C^{\prime}
respectively as

D=(d_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
and
D^{\prime}=(d^{\prime}_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
instead, then

d_{ij}=\begin{cases}c_{ij},&i+j<n,\\
\gamma_{i},&i+j=n,\\
0,&{\rm otherwise},\end{cases}\quad d^{\prime}_{ij}=\begin{cases}c^{\prime}_{ij},&i+j<n,\\
\theta_{i},&i+j=n,\\
0,&{\rm otherwise}.\end{cases}

Suppose that
\varphi:{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\gamma}\to{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})_{\theta}
is a graded algebra isomorphism. By Theorem
2.2
, there exists a tuple
(b_{i})_{i\in[n]}
of nonzero coefficients such that
\varphi(p_{i})=b_{i}p_{i}
and

b_{i+j}d_{ij}=b_{i}b_{j}d^{\prime}_{ij},\quad\forall i,j\geq 1.

In particular, the restriction of
\varphi
on
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
is an isomorphism from
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
to
{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})
. We set
\sigma=\varphi|_{{\mathfrak{p}}(C)}
. Then for any
i,j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\theta_{\sigma}(p_{i},p_{j})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cocycle_action}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\theta(\sigma(p_{i}),\sigma(p_{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{i}b_{j}\theta(p_{i},p_{j})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cocycle}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\delta_{i+j,\,n}b_{i}b_{j}d^{\prime}_{ij}p_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{i+j,\,n}b_{i+j}d_{ij}p_{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cocycle}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle b_{n}\gamma(p_{i},p_{j}).

That is,
\theta_{\sigma}=b_{n}\gamma
. Conversely, if there exist a graded algebra isomorphism
\sigma:{\mathfrak{p}}(C)\to{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})
and
b\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
such that
\theta_{\sigma}=b\gamma
, one can define the graded linear isomorphism
\varphi:{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\gamma}\to{\mathfrak{p}}(C^{\prime})_{\theta}
by letting
\varphi|_{{\mathfrak{p}}(C)}=\sigma
and
\varphi(p_{n})=bp_{n}
. Indeed,
\varphi
is clearly bijective. Moreover, for any
i,j\in[n-1]
, if
i+j<n
, then

\varphi(p_{i}\cdot_{\gamma}p_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:central_extension}}}{{=}}\varphi(p_{i}p_{j})=\sigma(p_{i}p_{j})=\sigma(p_{i})\sigma(p_{j})=\varphi(p_{i})\varphi(p_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:central_extension}}}{{=}}\varphi(p_{i})\cdot_{\theta}\varphi(p_{j}).

Otherwise, if
i+j=n
, then

\varphi(p_{i}\cdot_{\gamma}p_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:central_extension}}}{{=}}\varphi(\gamma(p_{i},p_{j}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cocycle}}}{{=}}b\gamma(p_{i},p_{j})=\theta_{\sigma}(p_{i},p_{j}),

and

\varphi(p_{i})\cdot_{\theta}\varphi(p_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:central_extension}}}{{=}}\theta(\varphi(p_{i}),\varphi(p_{j}))=\theta(\sigma(p_{i}),\sigma(p_{j}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cocycle_action}}}{{=}}\theta_{\sigma}(p_{i},p_{j}),

so it is easy to see that
\varphi
is an algebra map.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Given any
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
and
2
-cocycle
\theta\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
, we have
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\theta_{\varphi_{b}}}\cong{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\theta}
for any
\varphi_{b}=(b^{i})_{i\in[n-1]}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
with
b\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.5
, we only need to show that
\theta_{\varphi_{b}}
and
\theta
are colinear.
In fact,

\theta_{\varphi_{b}}(p_{i},p_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cocycle_action}}}{{=}}\theta(\varphi_{b}(p_{i}),\varphi_{b}(p_{j}))=b^{i+j}\theta(p_{i},p_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cocycle}}}{{=}}b^{n}\theta(p_{i},p_{j}),

so
\theta_{\varphi_{b}}=b^{n}\theta
.
Furthermore, given any
b^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
, we can actually take
b\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
such that
b^{n}=b^{\prime}
, as
\mathbbm{k}
is algebraically closed. Then
\theta_{\varphi_{b}}=b^{n}\theta=b^{\prime}\theta
.
∎

Convention.
Using the notation in Eq. (
7
) we can identify any
2
-cocycle
\theta\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)

with a tuple
(\theta_{i})_{i\in[n-1]}\in\mathbbm{k}^{n-1}
subjecting to the following binomial relations,

(10)

\theta_{i}=\theta_{n-i},\quad\theta_{i+j}c_{ij}=\theta_{i}c_{j,\,n-i-j}

for any
i,j\geq 1
satisfying
i+j\leq n-1
.
So

Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)=\left\{(\theta_{i})_{i\in[n-1]}\in\mathbbm{k}^{n-1}\,\big|\,\theta_{i}=\theta_{n-i},\ \theta_{i+j}c_{ij}=\theta_{j+k}c_{jk},\ \forall i,j,k\geq 1,\ i+j+k=n\right\}

is a subvariety of the affine space
\mathbbm{k}^{n-1}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Given any
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
and nonzero
2
-cocycle
\theta
in
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
, the stabilizer of the
1
-dimensional subspace
[\theta]
of
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
under the action of
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
is given by

(11)

{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))}[\theta]=\{(b_{i})_{i\in[n-1]}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))\,|\,b_{i}b_{n-i}=b_{j}b_{n-j}\ \mbox{ if }\ \theta_{i}\theta_{j}\neq 0\},

Proof.

For any
\sigma=(b_{i})_{i\in[n-1]}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
, we know that

\displaystyle[\theta]\cdot\sigma=[\theta]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle[\theta_{\sigma}]=[\theta]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\exists\,b\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}\ \mbox{such that}\ \theta_{\sigma}=b\theta

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cocycle},\,\eqref{eq:cocycle_action}}}{{\Leftrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\exists\,b\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}\ \mbox{such that}\ b_{i}b_{n-i}=b\ {\rm whenever}\ \theta_{i}\neq 0,

which implies the desired description of
{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))}[\theta]
.
∎

3.2.
The study of central extensions via the associated graphs

Next we introduce a crucial combinatorial tool to study the central extensions of DGCAs.

Definition 3.8
.

Given any

with

with respect to

as follows. Its vertex set is

, and any two distinct vertices

are adjacent

For any

.

For any

with

and

).

Theorem 3.11
.

For any
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
, the
2
-cocycle space
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
is a subvariety of the affine space
\mathbbm{k}^{n-1}

of dimension not bigger than the number
u(C)
of generic connected components of the graph
{\rm Gr}(C)
.

Proof.

Fix any
2
-cocycle
\theta=(\theta_{i})_{i\in[n-1]}\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
. According to Remark
3.9
, we have
p+q\geq n
for any two adjacent vertices
p
and
q
in
{\rm Gr}(C)
. If
p+q=n
, then
\theta_{p}=\theta_{q}
. If
p+q>n
, we have
c_{ij}c_{j,\,n-i-j}\neq 0
for
i=n-q
and
j=p+q-n
by Definition
3.8
, and also
c_{ij}\theta_{p}=c_{j,\,n-i-j}\theta_{q}
by Eq. (
10
).
In other words,
the values of
\theta
on any connected component
{\rm Gr}^{[p]}(C)
with
p\in[n-1]
is uniquely determined by
\theta_{p}
.

From the discussion above, we know that if
\theta
takes zero on one vertex, it must vanish on the entire connected component containing such a vertex.
Particularly, any contractible connected component of
{\rm Gr}(C)
contains a vertex
p
such that
i+j=p
, and
c_{ij}\neq 0
but
c_{j,\,n-i-j}=0
, then
\theta_{p}=\theta_{n-p}=0

by Eq. (
10
) again. Hence,
\theta
vanish on all contractible connected components.

As a result,
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
is a subvariety of
\mathbbm{k}^{n-1}
isomorphic to
\mathbbm{k}^{u}
with
u\leq u(C)
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

(1) Following the proof of Theorem
3.11
, we see that the

-cocycle space

is exactly isomorphic to

generic connected components of the graph

are circuit-free, namely they are trees.

(2) By contrast, a generic connected component of
{\rm Gr}(C)
containing a circuit may force all

-cocycles vanishing on it, and

is of dimension
<u(C)
in this situation. Such a phenomenon drives us to define a more subtle notion. That is, a generic connected component is called

if it admits nonzero values of 2-cocyles.

(3) For any
(0,1)
-matrix

in
\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
and

-cocycle
\theta\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
,
\theta
is a constant function on every connected component of
{\rm Gr}(C)
, as all nonzero entries of

are 1. In particular, any generic connected component of
{\rm Gr}(C)
is
always
nonvanishing when
C
is a
(0,1)
-matrix.

with respect to

Namely,

if and only if

have the same distribution of nonzero entries.

Corollary 3.15
.

For any
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
and
\gamma\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
, the equivalence class

is a locally closed subvariety of

isomorphic to

{\rm Gr}(C)

-subset

According to Theorem

can be uniquely determined by any given values

is contractible. Especially if

, then we also have

for exactly

such that

, hence prove the result.

For any matrix

and nonzero

-cocycle
\gamma\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
, if
the central extensions

{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\theta}
with
\theta\in Z_{\gamma}
form a unique isomorphism class,
then the dimension of the
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
-orbit
O_{[\gamma]}
of the
1
-dimensional subspace
[\gamma]
, or equivalently
the number of non-redundant equalities defining
{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))}[\gamma]
in Eq. (
11
), is exactly
v(\gamma)-1
.

Proof.

By
[
6
, Prop. 1.11]
we know that the orbit
O_{[\gamma]}
is a locally closed subvariety of the Grassmannian of
1
-dimensional subspaces of
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
and

\dim\mathcal{O}_{[\gamma]}=\dim{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))-\dim{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))}[\gamma],

which is consequently the number of non-redundant equalities defining
{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))}[\gamma]
in Eq. (
11
), under the premise of those defining
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
in Eq. (

).

On the other hand, since
Z_{\gamma}
contains the
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}
in
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
, we clearly have
\dim\mathcal{O}_{[\gamma]}<\dim Z_{\gamma}=v(\gamma)
by Corollary
3.15
. Such an inequality can also be seen as follows. For any
\sigma=(b_{i})_{i\in[n-1]}\in{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
, we claim that

b_{p}b_{n-p}=b_{q}b_{n-q}

if vertices
p
and
q
are connected in
{\rm Gr}(C)
. In fact, by the connectedness we only need to check it
whenever
p
and
q
are adjacent. Now it is trivially true if

. Otherwise, by Definition
3.8
there exist
i,j,k\geq 1
such that

i+j+k=n,\ p=i+j,\ q=j+k,\ c_{ij}c_{jk}\neq 0,

and then

\displaystyle b_{p}b_{n-p}

\displaystyle=b_{i+j}b_{n-i-j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:aut}}}{{=}}b_{i}b_{j}b_{k},

\displaystyle b_{q}b_{n-q}

\displaystyle=b_{j+k}b_{n-j-k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:aut}}}{{=}}b_{i}b_{j}b_{k},

so
b_{p}b_{n-p}=b_{q}b_{n-q}
.
As a result, the number of non-redundant equalities defining
{\rm Stab}_{{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))}[\gamma]
in Eq. (
11
) is less than the number
v(\gamma)
of connected components of
{\rm Gr}(C)
on which
\gamma
does not vanish.

According to Theorem
3.5
,
the isomorphism classes of central extensions of
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
are in bijection with the
{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
-orbits in the Grassmannian of
1
-dimensional subspaces of

. If

, then

contains other

-orbits apart from

,
and the central extensions
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\theta}
with
\theta\in Z_{\gamma}
, i.e.
C_{\theta}\sim C_{\gamma}
provide more than one isomorphism class.
∎

Conjecture 3.18

For any

In the end of this section, we give a criterion when all nontrivial central extensions of a DGCA form exactly one isomorphism class. In order to prove it,
we need a technical lemma.

Lemma 3.19
.

For any matrix
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
, let
G
be one connected component of
{\rm Gr}(C)
, and
\theta,\gamma
two
2
-cocycles in
Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
.
Then
\theta
and
\gamma
are colinear on
G
, namely

\theta_{p}\gamma_{q}=\theta_{q}\gamma_{p},\quad\forall p,q\in G.

Proof.

When
G
is contractible, any
2
-cocycle vanishes on
G
, so it is trivially true.

Otherwise, if
G
is generic,
by the connectedness of
G
it is sufficient to check that

\theta_{p}\gamma_{q}=\theta_{q}\gamma_{p}
,
whenever
p
and
q
are adjacent in
G
. It is automatically true if
p+q=n
. Otherwise, by Definition
3.8
there exist
i,j,k\geq 1
such that

i+j+k=n,\ p=i+j,\ q=j+k,\ c_{ij}c_{jk}\neq 0,

then

\theta_{p}\gamma_{q}=\theta_{i+j}\gamma_{j+k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\theta_{q}\gamma_{p}=\theta_{j+k}\gamma_{i+j}

are equal to each other as
c_{ij}\theta_{i+j}c_{jk}\gamma_{j+k}=c_{jk}\theta_{j+k}c_{ij}\gamma_{i+j}
by Eq. (
10
).
∎

Theorem 3.20
.

For any matrix
C\in\mathcal{M}_{n-1}
, all nontrivial central extensions of
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
form exactly one isomorphism class of DGCAs if and only if
{\rm Gr}(C)
has exactly one nonvanishing connected component.

Proof.

As
{\rm Gr}(C)
has only one nonvanishing connected component, we know that
any two nonzero
2
-cocycles
\theta,\gamma\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
must take nonzero values on it, and are already colinear by Lemma
3.19
. So
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\theta}\cong{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\gamma}
by Theorem
3.5
, and all nontrivial central extensions of
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
give exactly one isomorphism class of DGCAs.

Conversely, suppose that all nontrivial central extensions of
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)
form one isomorphism class of DGCAs. Then for any two nonzero
2
-cocycles
\theta,\gamma\in Z_{g}^{2}({\mathfrak{p}}(C),V)
, we have
{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\theta}\cong{\mathfrak{p}}(C)_{\gamma}
, which is equivalent to the existence of
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{p}}(C))
making
\theta_{\sigma}
and
\gamma
colinear by Theorem
3.5
. In other words, there exist nonzero
2
-cocycles and all of them have the same distribution of nonzero entries, which forces that
{\rm Gr}(C)
has exactly one nonvanishing connected component by Corollary
3.15
.
∎

Conclusion.

The classification method we adopt for DGCAs is a graded modification of the Skjelbred-Sund method, which highly depends on the diagonally graded assumption.

Abstract

Glasser’s Master Theorem
[
1
]
is essentially a restatement of Cauchy’s integral Theorem reduced to a specialized form. Here we extend that theorem by introducing two new parameters, but still retain a simple form. Because of wide interest in entities involving Riemann’s zeta function, the focus is on the evaluation of improper integrals with almost arbitrary integrands involving that function, but we also consider some other instances, the purpose being to demonstrate the power of the extended theorem. This is achieved by the presentation of a large number of examples that illustrate the ubiquity of the range of possibilities. One simple outcome of the study is the use of the extended theorem to show how it is possible to evaluate an integral when series or other representations of an integrand function do not converge.

2
The basics

In Glasser’s original work
[
1
]
, a master integration identity

(2.1)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{F\!\left(a\,v\left(v+i\right)\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=F(a/4)

was presented, which can be restated to read

(2.2)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F\!\left(v\right)dv=-\pi i\sum_{j}\;\mathit{R}\!\left(F\!\left(v_{j}\right)\right)_{v_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}}\,.

The validity of (
2.2
) is subject to the condition that the integral exists and
F(v)
is meromorphic in the strip

(2.3)

\mathfrak{S}\equiv-1<\Im(v)<0\,,

where
\mathit{R}\!\left(F\!\left(v_{j}\right)\right)
denotes the residue of
F(v_{j})
for each point
v_{j}
that lies in
\mathfrak{S}
, provided also that
F(v)
satisfies the transformation

(2.4)

F(v)+F(-v-i)=0\,.

The principle upon which that result was based is here generalized, with
0<b\in\Re
, by extending the definition of
\mathfrak{S}
as follows:

(2.5)

\mathfrak{S}_{b}\equiv-b<\Im(v)<0

to yield a generalized Master equation:

Theorem 1
.

If
F(v)
is meromorphic in
\mathfrak{S}_{b}
, the integral exists and

(2.6)

F\!\left(v\right)+F\!\left(-i\,b-v\right)=0\,,

then

(2.7)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F\!\left(v\right)dv=-\pi i\sum_{j}\;\mathit{R}\!\left(F\!\left(v_{j}\right)\right)_{v_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\;b}}\,.

Proof.

Consider the contour integral that encloses
\mathfrak{S}_{b}

(2.8)

J\equiv\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F\!\left(v\right)dv+\int_{\infty-i\,b}^{-\infty-i\,b}F\!\left(v\right)dv\,.

Following a change of variables, (
2.8
) becomes

(2.9)

J=\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(F\!\left(v\right)-F\!\left(-i\,b-v\right)\right)dv=\,-2\pi i\sum_{j}\;\mathit{R}\!\left(F\!\left(v_{j}\right)\right)_{v_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}_{b}}\,,

and if (
2.6
) is true, then (
2.7
) immediately follows.
∎

Corollary 2.0.1
.

Further, with
h(v)\in\mathfrak{C}
and continuous and

(2.10)

F\!\left(v\right)-\,F\!\left(-i\,b-v\right)=h(v)\,F\!\left(v\right)

then

(2.11)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F\!\left(v\right)\,h(v)\,dv=\,-2\pi i\sum_{j}\;\mathit{R}\!\left(F\!\left(v_{j}\right)\right)_{v_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\;b}}\,.

Corollary 2.0.2
.

If
h(v)\in\mathfrak{C}
is continuous and

(2.12)

F\!\left(v\right)+\,F\!\left(-i\,b-v\right)=h(v)\,F\!\left(v\right)

then

(2.13)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F\!\left(v\right)(2-h(v))\,dv=\,-2\pi i\sum_{j}\;\mathit{R}\!\left(F\!\left(v_{j}\right)\right)_{v_{j}\in\mathfrak{S}_{\;b}}\,

Remark 2.1
.

In contrast to (
2.6
), the function
h(v)
introduced in either of (
2.10
) or (
2.12
) does not impose the existence of a condition, but merely states the obvious – there always exists
h(v)
such that

(2.14)

h(v)=1\pm F(-ib-v)/F(v)

and
h(v)
becomes of interest if
h(v)\neq 1
or
h(v)\neq 2
. If
h(v)=0
, then (
2.13
) reduces to (
2.7
). See Section
4.3
.

Remark 2.2
.

Among other possibilities, to satisfy (
2.6
) choose a function
g(v)
such that

(2.15)

F\!\left(v\right)=g\!\left(v\right)-g\!\left(-i\,b-v\right)\,;

to satisfy (
2.10

such that

(2.16)

F\!\left(v\right)=\frac{g\!\left(a-i\,v\right)\,g\!\left(a-b+i\,v\right)}{\cosh(\pi\,v)}\,,\hskip 10.0pt|b|\neq 1\,,

and to satisfy (
2.12

such that

Many other possibilities were discussed in

3
Simple examples with a common kernel
1/\cosh(\pi v/b)

3.1
Specific (
b=1
)

Consider some examples, all of which will be focussed on Riemann’s Zeta function
\zeta(s)
. Let

(3.1)

F(v)=\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\,v\right)+\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}

Then it is easily seen that
F(v)=-F(-v-i)
and therefore

(3.2)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\,v\right)+\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=2\,\gamma

because the only singularity of the integrand in
\mathfrak{S}_{1}
occurs at
v=-i/2
with residue
2\,i\gamma/\pi
. Similarly

(3.3)

\int_{-{\infty}}^{{\infty}}\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\,v\right)\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=2\,\gamma\!\left(1\right)+\gamma^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\,,

and

(3.4)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\,v\right)^{2}+\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)^{2}}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=-4\,\gamma\!\left(1\right)+2\,\gamma^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\,.

Using the same principles we also have

(3.5)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\Gamma\!\left({3}/{2}-i\,v\right)\zeta\!\left({1}/{2}+i\,v\right)-\Gamma\!\left({1}/{2}+i\,v\right)\zeta\!\left({3}/{2}-i\,v\right)\right)dv=-2\,\pi\,,

(3.6)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\,v\right)-\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh^{2}\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=-\frac{2\,\gamma\!\left(1\right)}{\pi}+\frac{2\,\pi}{3}\,,

(3.7)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\,v\right)^{2}-\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)^{2}}{\cosh^{2}\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=\frac{4\,\gamma\,\pi}{3}-\frac{4\,\gamma\,\gamma\!\left(1\right)-2\,\gamma\!\left(2\right)}{\pi}\,,

and

(3.8)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\,v\right)\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh^{3}\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=-\frac{17\,\pi^{2}}{120}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\gamma\!\left(1\right)+\frac{{\gamma\left(3\right)}/{3}+\gamma\,\gamma\!\left(2\right)-\gamma\!\left(1\right)^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\,.

All of the above can be verified numerically and numerous variations are obvious.

3.2
Application

Elsewhere
[
11
, Eq. (5.8)]
, based on an inverse Mellin transform, it has been shown that

(3.9)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{R}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=\gamma-1,

so rewriting (
3.2
) as

(3.10)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{R}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)+\zeta_{R}\!\left(\frac{3}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=\gamma

leads to

(3.11)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{R}\!\left(\frac{3}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=1

by subtracting the two.

Remark 3.1
.

The identity (
3.11
) can also be independently verified by writing
\zeta_{R}\left(\frac{3}{2}+i\,v\right)
as a Dirichlet series and interchanging the sum and integration, because both are convergent, yielding

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{R}\!\left(\frac{3}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=\overset{\infty}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\;{j^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos\left(v\,\ln\left(j\right)\right)}{\cosh\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv\,.

The resulting integral

(3.13)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\cos\!\left(v\,\ln\!\left(j\right)\right)}{\cosh\!\left(\pi\,v\right)}dv=\frac{\sqrt{j}}{j+1}

is listed
[
17
, Eq. 3.981(3)]
and the remaining sum

(3.14)

\overset{\infty}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\!\frac{1}{j\left(j+1\right)}=1

is well-known. Although the Dirichlet sum corresponding to (
3.9
) does not converge, by reversing the above logic, it is possible to derive (
3.9
) independently, thus demonstrating a novel means of discovering integral identities involving the function
\zeta(s)
living inside the critical strip

when

resides outside the critical strip, where many convergent summation and integral identities are known.

(3.73)

\displaystyle\hskip 25.0pt+2\ \sqrt{2}\zeta\!\left(\frac{\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)\sqrt{2}}{4}\right)\zeta\!\left(\frac{\left(1+\sqrt{2}\right)}{4}\right),

4
Examples of not-so-obvious convergence

4.1
Zeta

Since the integral appears to converge numerically, the use of (

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta\!\left(a+i\,v\right)}{a+i\,v}-\frac{\zeta\!\left(a+b-i\,v\right)}{a+b-i\,v}\right)dv=-2\,\pi\,,\hskip 10.0pt1-b<a<1,

suggesting that we consider the special case

(4.2)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\frac{1}{2}+i\,v}-\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{3}{2}-i\,v\right)}{\frac{3}{2}-i\,v}\right)dv=-2\,\pi\,.

Following the method discussed in Remark
3.1
, the second term of the integrand is amenable to analysis, by considering the general form

(4.3)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(a+b-i\,v\right)}{a+b-i\,v}dv=\pi.

valid if
a+b>1
- see Appendix
B
. Hence, if
b=1
we find

(4.4)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(a+i\,v\right)}{a+i\,v}dv=-\pi,\hskip 10.0pt0<a<1,

and if

we obtain

(4.5)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\frac{1}{2}+i\,v}dv=-\pi\,,

which can also be written as

(4.6)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2\,v\,\zeta_{I}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)+\zeta_{R}\!\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\,v\right)}{\frac{1}{4}+v^{2}}\,dv=-\pi\,.

Remarks:

•

The result (

) can also be obtained by writing (

) as a contour integral after a change of variables and translating it past the pole at

an identify that confounds numerical verification; this suggests that the integral is probably not numerically convergent and only meaningful as a consequence of regularization (see
[

).

•

Either of the results (

) and/or (

The algebraic relationship between

6
Summary

Here we have extended Glasser’s Master Theorem by introducing one, and and occasionally two, new parameters and demonstrated the utility of this theorem by evaluating (and numerically testing) a number of interesting improper integrals that mostly, but not exclusively, involve Riemann’s Zeta function. It appears that the identities so-established could be generalized to a large number of other special functions, also leading to what appears to be a new technique of evaluating integrals by pairing unknown with known partners living in different regimes of analytic convergence. A number of more general possibilities were presented but not pursued – for example, (
3.15
) introduces two parameters
s
and
p
, but only the simplest values for these parameters were examined. In several places, the parameter
s
, employed as a integer variable can be (numerically) generalized to real, and sometimes complex values, but the analysis is not straightforward. It is suggested that an investigation of non-integer values of
s
as it arises here would likely be fruitful. Finally, it seems obvious that a study of the generalization of the parameter
a
into the complex domain as was touched on in Section
3.3.7
is worthy of further study.

Band-limited wavelets beyond Gevrey regularity
Nenad Teofanov ∗, Filip Tomić, Stefan Tutić

Abstract
. It is known that a smooth function of exponential decay at infinity can not be an orthonormal wavelet. Dziubański and Hernández constructed smooth orthonormal wavelets of Gevrey type subexponential decay. We weaken the Gevrey type decay and construct orthonormal wavelets of subexponential decay related to the so-called extended Gevrey classes. The virtue of our construction is that prescise asymptotics of functions from such classes can be given in terms of the Lambert W W function.

1.
Introduction

We complment the studies of orthonormal wavelets of subexponential decay given in
[
6
]
and
[
20
]
, by extending their classes of to smooth functions with subexponential decay given in terms of the Lambert
W
function (cf.
[
4
,
19
]
).
Recall, a function
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

is called an
orthonormal wavelet
(ONW)
if the set

\{\psi_{m,n}:m\in\mathbb{Z},n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
where

\psi_{m,n}(x)=2^{\frac{m}{2}}\psi(2^{m}x-n),\qquad m,n\in\mathbb{Z},\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Regularity and decay properties of the function
\psi
play central role in applications. The construction of an
orthonormal wavelet
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})

(see Section
2
for the notation)
goes back to Y. Meyer,
[
18
,
16
]
.
However, if
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with all derivatives bounded, and if

\psi
is of exponential decay at infinity, i.e.

|\psi(x)|\leq Ce^{-h|x|},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1)

for some
h,C>0
, then
\psi
can not be an orthonormal wavelet
[
5
, Corollary 5.5.3]
.

Our study complements the Dziubański and Hernández construction of
orthonormal wavelets
\psi
with subexponential decay,

|\psi(x)|\leq Ce^{-|x|^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\;\sigma>1,

(2)

for some
C=C(\sigma)>0
,
[
6
]
.
The functions considered in
[
6
]
are related to the Gevrey type regularity.

By using Komatsu’s approach to ultradistributions, Moritoh and Tomoeda proved in
[
20
]
that a similar construction to the one given in

[
6
]
may lead to the following decay of
\psi
:

|\psi(x)|\leq Ce^{-|x|/l_{n,\sigma}(|x|)},\qquad|x|\;\;\text{large enough},\;\sigma>1,

(3)

for any
n=1,2,\dots
, where

l_{n,\sigma}(|x|)=(\log_{1}|x|)(\log_{2}|x|)\dots(\log_{n-1}|x|)(\log_{n}|x|)^{\sigma},

\log_{1}|x|=\log|x|
,
|x|>0
.

The decay in (
3
) is closer to exponential decay (
1
) than
the subexponential decay given by (
2
). We extend the class of ONW considered in
[
6
]
and
[
20
]
, and obtain an estimate of the form

|\psi(x)|\leq Ce^{-h\left(\ln^{2}(|x|)/W(\ln|x|)\right)}\\
\asymp e^{-h\left(\ln^{2}(|x|)/\ln(\ln|x|)\right)},\qquad|x|\;\;\text{large enough},

(4)

for every
h>0
and some
C=C(h)>0
,
(cf. Theorem
3.7
for a more general result).
Here
W
denotes the Lambert function. We note that
(
4
) is less restrictive than (
2
).

The Lambert
W
function is defined as the inverse of
ze^{z}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
and we consider here
W(x)
the restriction of its principal branch to
[0,\infty)
.
It is a convenient tool for the description of asymptotic behavior in different contexts.
We refer to
[
4
]
for a review of some applications of

[
19
]
for more details and generalizations.
Let us briefly and incompletely mention its use in different research fields, such as

[
2
]
, cosmology
[
31
]
, ecology
[

]
, and numerical analysis
[
28
]

Our construction is based on the properties of extended Gevrey classes

{\mathcal{E}}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
, see Subsection

for the definition.
A surprising feature of

is that the precise asymptotics of the corresponding associated function

(see (

function,
[

]

This is recently used to prove the surjectivity of the Borel map in sectors of the complex plane for

We consider wavelets
\psi
whose Fourier transforms

are compactly supported functions from

Recall, if
\hat{f}
has compact support

To study non band-limited ONW

[

]

In the same way as it is done in

, an example of the orthonormal wavelet

is given by the explicit construction of

does not satisfy (

2.2.
The Lambert function

The
Lambert
W
function
is defined as the inverse of
ze^{z}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
. By
W(x)
,
x\geq 0
, we denote the restriction of its principal branch, and we review some of its basic properties as follows:

(W1)\quad

W(0)=0
,
W(e)=1
,
W(x)
is continuous, increasing and concave on
[0,\infty)
,

(W2)\quad

W(xe^{x})=x,
and
\quad x=W(x)e^{W(x)}
,
x\geq 0
,

(W3)\quad

W
can be represented in the form of the absolutely convergent series

W(x)=\ln x-\ln(\ln x)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}c_{km}\frac{(\ln(\ln x))^{m}}{(\ln x)^{k+m}},\quad x\geq x_{0}>e,

with suitable constants
c_{km}
and
x_{0}
, wherefrom the following estimates hold:

\ln x-\ln(\ln x)\leq W(x)\leq\ln x-\frac{1}{2}\ln(\ln x),\quad x\geq e.

(5)

Note that
(W2)
implies

W(x\ln x)=\ln x,\quad x>1.

By using
(W3)
we obtain

W(x)\sim\ln x,\quad x\to\infty,

and therefore

W(Cx)\sim W(x),\quad x\to\infty,

for any
C>0
.
We refer to
[
11
,
4

19
]

2.3.
Defining sequences

The well known Komatsu’s approach to ultradistributions, see
[
15
]
,is based on defining sequences

M_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, which satisfy some of the conditions:

(M.1)\quad

\displaystyle(M_{p})^{2}\leq M_{p-1}M_{p+1}
,
\quad p\in\mathbb{N}
,

(M.2)\quad

(\exists A,C>0)\quad M_{p}\leq AC^{p+q}\;M_{p}M_{q},\quad p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(M.2)^{\prime}\quad

(\exists A,C>0)\quad M_{p+1}\leq AC^{p}M_{p},\quad p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(M.3)^{\prime}\quad

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{p-1}}{M_{p}}<\infty.

When
\tau>1
, the Gevrey sequence
M_{p}=p!^{\tau}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, is an example of of a sequence
which satisfy all of the above mentioned conditions.

We extend Gevrey sequences by introducing an additional parameter
\sigma
, and consider
sequences of the form
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{p}=p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}
,

p\in\mathbb{N}
,
M^{\tau,\sigma}_{0}=1
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma\geq 1

It can be proved (see e.g.
[
23
]
) that such sequences satisfy
the following properties:

(M.1)\quad

\displaystyle({M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}})^{2}\leq{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p+1}^{\tau,\sigma}}
,
\quad p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}\quad

(\exists C>0)\quad M_{p+1}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}
,

\quad p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\widetilde{(M.2)}\quad

(\exists C>0)

M_{p+q}^{\tau,\sigma}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}+q^{\sigma}}M_{p}^{2^{\sigma-1}\tau,\sigma}M_{q}^{2^{\sigma-1}\tau,\sigma},\quad p,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(M.3)^{\prime}\quad

If
\sigma>1
, or if
\sigma=1
and
\tau>1
, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}<\infty.

In fact,

follows from the estimate

\frac{M_{p-1}^{\tau,\sigma}}{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}\leq\frac{1}{(2p)^{\tau(p-1)^{\sigma-1}}},\qquad p\in\mathbb{N}.

(6)

Notice that
(M.2)\Rightarrow(M.2)^{\prime}

When
\sigma=1
i.e.
M^{\tau,1}_{p}=p^{\tau p}\asymp p!^{\tau}

\widetilde{(M.2)}

\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}

(M.2)
and
(M.2)^{\prime}

(M.3)^{\prime}
holds if and only if

Remark 2.1
.

The estimate
\widetilde{(M.2)}

{M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}}

2.5.
Extended Gevrey classes

Although in Section
3
we are interested in one-dimensional wavelets, here we decided to consider functions over

Recall that the Gevrey space
{\mathcal{G}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

consists of functions
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
such that for every compact set

|\partial^{\alpha}\phi(x)|\leq C_{K}h^{|\alpha|}|\alpha|!^{t}.

for all

.

In a ismilar fashion we introduce classes related to defining sequences

,
\tau>0
,

.

Definition 2.3
.

Let there be given
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
, and let

,

,

The extended Gevrey class of Roumieu type

\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for every compact set
K\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there are constants
C_{K}>0
and

satisfying

|\partial^{\alpha}\phi(x)|\leq C_{K}h^{|\alpha|^{\sigma}}M_{|\alpha|}^{\tau,\sigma},

(13)

for all
x\in K
and for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}.

The extended Gevrey class of Beurling type
{\mathcal{E}}_{(\tau,\sigma)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of all

\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
for every compact set
K\subset\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for all

there is a constant

satisfying

for all

and for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}.

We refer to
[
23
]
for the topologies in
{\mathcal{E}}_{\{\tau,\sigma\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{(\tau,\sigma)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Note that (
13
) and (

{\mathcal{E}}_{(\tau,\sigma)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}_{\{\tau,\sigma\}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

3.
Main results

In
[
6
]
Dziubański and Hernández constructed a band-limited orthonormal wavelet
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
which belongs to the intersection of all Gevrey classes in the frequency domain, i.e.,
\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}\in\bigcap_{t>1}{\mathcal{G}}_{t}({\mathbb{R}})
. This is done through the refinement of
related constructions from
[
10
,
17
,
12
]
.
By the Paley–Wiener theorem for Gevrey classes (cf.
[
27
, Theorem 1.6.1]
)
it follows that
\psi
has a sub-exponential decay in the space (configuration) domain, i.e.

|\psi(x)|\leq Ce^{-|x|^{1/t}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\qquad t>1,

for some
C>0
, which is close to the critical decay (
1
).

It turned out that the construction from
[
6
]
can be further refined by using ultradifferentiable functions given by sequences
M_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, which satisfy
(M.1)
,
(M.2)^{\prime}
and
(M.3)^{\prime}
, see Subsection
2.3
. Namely, Moritoh and Tomoeda considered sequences of the form

\displaystyle L^{n,\sigma}_{p}=p!(\log_{n}p)^{\sigma}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\log_{k}p,\quad p\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\sigma>1
(
\log_{1}p=\log p
),
[
20
]
. It is easy to see that for every
\sigma,t>1
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists
C>0
such that

L^{n,\sigma}_{p}\leq p!^{t}\frac{(\log_{2}p)^{n+\sigma}}{p!^{t-1}}\leq Cp!^{t},\quad p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Therefore classes generated by
L^{n,\sigma}_{p}
are contained in the intersection of all Gevrey classes. The decay of wavelets
\psi
from
[
20
]
is given by

|\psi(x)|\leq Ce^{-\frac{|x|}{l_{n,\sigma}(|x|)}},\qquad|x|\;\;\text{large enough},\;\sigma>1,\;n\in\mathbb{N},

and
l_{n,\sigma}(\cdot)=(\log_{n}(\cdot))^{\sigma}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\log_{k}(\cdot)
.

Next we extend the classes of orthonormal wavelets from
[
6
]
and
[
20
]
,
and construct a band-limited orthonormal wavelet
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with decay given in terms of the Lambert
W
function.

We first construct a cutoff function
\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\sigma>1
. For every
a>0
there exists

\phi_{a}\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})

=\cap_{\tau>0}{\mathcal{D}}_{\tau,\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\phi_{a}\geq 0
,
\operatorname{supp}\phi_{a}\subset[-a,a]
, and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{a}(x)\,dx=1
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
is closed under dilations and multiplications by a constant, it is enough to show the result for
a=1
.

The essential part of the construction is to carefully choose a sequence
a_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, which will give rise to decay properties specified by the definition of extended Gevrey classes.

Consider first the (double indexed) sequence

a(m,p)=\frac{1}{(2(p+1))^{\frac{1}{m}p^{\sigma-1}}},\qquad m,p\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

for a given
\sigma>1
.
Since

\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2(p+1))^{\frac{1}{m}p^{\sigma-1}}}<\infty

for any
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it follows that
there exists a sequence
N_{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\sum_{n=N_{m}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2(p+1))^{\frac{1}{m}p^{\sigma-1}}}<\frac{1}{2^{m}}.

Thus the sequence
a_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, given by

a_{p}:=\frac{1}{(2(p+1))^{\frac{1}{m}p^{\sigma-1}}},\qquad N_{m}\leq p<N_{m+1},

satisfies

\sum_{n=N_{1}}^{\infty}a_{p}\leq 1.

The next step in the construction is to observe the sequence of functions

\phi_{p}:=f_{a_{N_{1}}}*f_{a_{N_{1}+1}}*\dots*f_{a_{p}},

where
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a non-negative and even function such that

\operatorname{supp}f\in[-1,1]
,
\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)\,dx=1
,
and
f_{a}(x)=\frac{1}{a}f\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)
. From
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{a}(x)\,dx=1
(and
\int|(f*g)(x)|\,dx=\int|f(x)|\,dx\int|g(x)|\,dx
) it follows that

\operatorname{supp}\phi_{p}\subset[-1,1]\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{p}(x)\,dx=1,\qquad p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Let us show that for every given
\tau>0
there exists
C_{\tau}>0
such that

|\phi_{p}^{(n)}(x)|\leq C_{\tau}^{n^{\sigma}}n^{\tau n^{\sigma}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and every
p\geq p_{0}(n,\tau)
.
Note that

\phi_{p}^{(n)}=f_{a_{N_{1}}}*\dots*f_{a_{N_{m}}}*f^{\prime}_{a_{N_{m}+1}}*\dots*f^{\prime}_{a_{N_{m}+n}}*f_{a_{N_{m}+n+1}}*\dots*f_{a_{p}},

and

\|f^{\prime}_{a_{p}}\|_{1}=\frac{1}{a_{p}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{a_{p}}\left|f^{\prime}\left(\frac{x}{a_{p}}\right)\right|\,dx\leq\frac{c}{a_{p}}\leq c\,(2(p+1))^{\frac{1}{m}p^{\sigma-1}}

when
p\geq N_{m}
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be given, and consider the sequence
\tau_{m}=\frac{1}{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then for any given
\tau>0
we can choose
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
so that
\tau_{m}<\tau
, and
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
so that
N_{m}+n<p
.

By using the fact that
{\widetilde{(M.2)^{\prime}}}
implies

M^{\frac{1}{m},\sigma}_{p+q}\leq\tilde{C}^{p^{\sigma}}M^{\frac{1}{m},\sigma}_{p}

for some
\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(q)>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\phi_{p}^{(n)}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq c^{n}\,2^{\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(N_{m}+k)^{\sigma-1}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}(N_{m}+k+1)^{\frac{1}{m}(N_{m}+k)^{\sigma-1}}

\displaystyle\leq c^{n}2^{\frac{1}{m}n(N_{m}+n)^{\sigma-1}}(N_{m}+n+1)^{\frac{1}{m}(N_{m}+n)^{\sigma-1}n}

\displaystyle\leq c^{n}2^{\frac{1}{m}(N_{m}+n)^{\sigma}}(N_{m}+n+1)^{\frac{1}{m}(N_{m}+n+1)^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle{\leq}c^{n}2^{(N_{m}+n)^{\sigma}}\tilde{C}^{n^{\sigma}}\,n^{\frac{1}{m}n^{\sigma}}\leq C^{n^{\sigma}}n^{\tau n^{\sigma}},

where
C
depends on
\tau
.

The sequence
\{\phi_{p}^{(n)}\;|\;p=N_{1},N_{2},\dots\}
is a Cauchy sequence for every
n=0,1,2,\dots
. Thus it converges to a function
\phi
which satisfies

|\phi^{(n)}(x)|\leq C^{n^{\sigma}}n^{\tau n^{\sigma}},

for every
\tau>0
. Therefore
\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
, and by the construction
\phi\geq 0
,
\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset[-1,1]
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\,dx=1
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We note that the construction in Lemma
3.1
is sharp in the sense that
\phi
does not belong to any Gevrey class, i.e.
\phi\not\in\bigcup_{t>1}{\mathcal{G}}_{t}({\mathbb{R}})
. In fact, the regularity of
\phi
is related to the minimal size of its support. We refer to the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 1.3.6.]
for details.

Next we modify the construction of ONW from
[
6
]
to obtain ONW in the framework of the extended Gevrey regularity. In the proof of Lemma
3.3
we use the properties of extended Gevrey classes given in Proposition
2.5
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\sigma>1
, and let
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
be given by (
15
). Then
there exists an orthonormal wavelet
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})

such that
\widehat{\psi}\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Proof.

Take arbitrary
\tau>0
and fix
\sigma>1
. Let
a\in(0,\pi/3)
be fixed, and choose a function

\phi_{a}\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})

as in Lemma
3.1
, so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{a}(x)dx=\pi/2
.

Set

\theta_{a}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\phi_{a}(t)\,dt,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

so that
\theta_{a}
is an increasing function,
\theta_{a}(x)=0
for
x<-a
,
\theta_{a}(x)=\frac{\pi}{2}
for
x>a
, and
\theta_{a}\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
, because
\theta_{a}^{(n)}(x)=\phi^{(n-1)}(x)
for
n>1
, and

|\theta_{a}(x)|\leq(x+a)\sup\phi_{a}(x)\leq 2a\sup\phi_{a}(x),

and
\phi_{a}\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Then, by Proposition
2.5

iii)
the functions

S_{a}(x):=\sin(\theta_{a}(x))\qquad\text{ and }\qquad C_{a}(x):=\cos(\theta_{a}(x)),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

belong to
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
.

S_{a}(x)
is an increasing function, and

S_{a}(x-\pi)=0
for
x\leq\pi-a
,
S_{a}(x-\pi)=1
for
x>\pi+a
.
Similarly,
C_{2a}(x)
is a decreasing function, and
C_{2a}(x-2\pi)=1
for
x\leq 2(\pi-a)
,
C_{2a}(x-2\pi)=0
for
x>2(\pi+a)
.

Next we define

b_{a}(x)=S_{a}(x-\pi)C_{2a}(x-2\pi),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(16)

The function
b_{a}(x)
is a bell shaped function, and
b_{a}(x)=0
when
x\leq\pi-a
or
x\geq 2(\pi+a)
.
(In fact, the condition
0<a<\frac{\pi}{3}
comes from
\pi+a<2(\pi-a)
.)

By Proposition
2.5

i)
and
iv)
it follows that

b_{a}\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})

and, by a slight abuse of notation, we denote its even extension to
(-\infty,0]
by
b_{a}
as well. Then it is proved in
[
1
]
(see also
[
10
, Corollary 4.7, Chapter 1]
)
that the function
\psi
defined by

\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=e^{i\frac{\xi}{2}}b_{a}(\xi),\qquad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}},

(17)

is an orthonormal wavelet in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Finally, that
\widehat{\psi}\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
follows from

\displaystyle|(e^{i\frac{\xi}{2}}b_{a}(\xi))^{(p)}|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}\frac{i^{k}}{2^{k}}e^{i\frac{\xi}{2}}b_{a}^{(p-k)}(\xi)\right|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}\frac{1}{2^{k}}|b_{a}^{(p-k)}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}\frac{1}{2^{k}}C^{(p-k)^{\sigma}}(p-k)^{\tau(p-k)^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{p^{\sigma}}p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\leq C^{p^{\sigma}}p^{\tau p^{\sigma}}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}^{p^{\sigma}}p^{\tau p^{\sigma}},

for some
C<0
and
\tilde{C}=2C
.
∎

To prove Theorem
3.7
we use the Paley-Wiener theorem for extended Gevrey regularity.
A more general statement than Proposition
3.4
is given in
[
25
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\sigma>1
. The Fourier transform of a function
\displaystyle f\in\,\mathcal{D_{\sigma}}({\mathbb{R}})
is an analytic function, and for every
h>0
there exists a constant
C_{h}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(\eta)|\leq C_{h}\,\displaystyle e^{-h\left(\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(|\eta|)/W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(|\eta|))\right)},\qquad|\eta|\quad\text{large enough},

(18)

where
W
denotes the Lambert function.

Notice that for
\sigma=2
(
18
) amounts to (
4
) (with
\psi=\widehat{f}
).

Proof.

The analyticity of
f
follows from the classical Paley-Wiener theorem, cf.
[
12
]
. It remains prove (
18
).

Take arbitrary
h>0
and let
\displaystyle f\in\,\mathcal{D_{\sigma}}({\mathbb{R}})
for
\sigma>1
, i.e.

\displaystyle f\in\,\mathcal{D_{\tau,\sigma}}({\mathbb{R}})
for every
\tau>0
.
Let
K
denote the support of
f
. Since
f\in{\mathcal{D}_{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}}({\mathbb{R}})
, by
Definition
2.3
we get the following estimate:

|\eta^{n}\widehat{f}(\eta)|=|\widehat{f^{(n)}}(\eta)|\leq C\sup_{x\in K}|{f^{(n)}}(x)|\leq C^{n^{\sigma}+1}_{1}n^{\frac{\tau}{2}n^{\sigma}}\leq C_{2}n^{\tau n^{\sigma}},

for a suitable constant
C_{2}>0
. Now, the relation between the sequence
M_{p}^{\tau,\sigma}
and its associated function

T_{\tau,\sigma}
given by (
7
) implies that

|\widehat{f}(\eta)|\leq C_{2}\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{n^{\tau n^{\sigma}}}{|\eta|^{n}}=C_{2}e^{-T_{\tau,\sigma}(|\eta|)},\quad\quad|\eta|\quad\text{large enough}.

(19)

Then, by the left-hand side of (
9
) we get

|\widehat{f}(\eta)|\leq C_{3}e^{-B_{\sigma}\,\tau^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}\left(\ln^{\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-1}}(|\eta|)/W^{\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}(\ln(|\eta|))\right)}\qquad|\eta|\quad\text{large enough},

with
\displaystyle C_{3}=C_{2}e^{-\widetilde{B}_{\tau,\sigma}}
. By choosing
\tau=(B_{\sigma}/h)^{\sigma-1}
, we obtain (
18
), which proves the claim.
∎

In the proof of Theorem
3.7

ii)
we will use the following Lemma,
see
[
9
, Lemma 1, p.198]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be such that

\left|x^{k}\varphi^{(q)}(x)\right|\leqslant CA^{k}B^{q}m_{k,q},\quad k,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

for some
A,B,C>0
, where the numbers
m_{k,q}
are such that

kq\frac{m_{k-1,q-1}}{m_{k,q}}\leqslant\gamma(k+q)^{\theta},\qquad\theta\leqslant 1,

(20)

for some
\gamma>0
. Then the function
\varphi
also satisfies

\left|\left[x^{k}\varphi(x)\right]^{(q)}\right|\leqslant C_{1}A_{1}^{k}B_{1}^{q}m_{k,q},\quad k,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

for some other constants
A_{1},B_{1},C_{1}>0
.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
m^{s,\tau,\sigma}_{k,q}=k!^{s}q^{\tau q^{\sigma}}
,
0<s\leq 1
,
\tau>0
,
\sigma>1
. Then (
6
) implies

kq\frac{m^{s,\tau,\sigma}_{k-1,q-1}}{m^{s,\tau,\sigma}_{k,q}}=k^{1-s}q\frac{M^{\tau,\sigma}_{q-1}}{M^{\tau,\sigma}_{q}}\leq k^{1-s}\frac{q}{(2q)^{\tau(q-1)^{\sigma-1}}}\leq C(k+q)^{1-s},\quad k,q\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

for some
C>1
, and hence
m^{s,\tau,\sigma}_{k,q}
satisfies (
20
).

Now we are ready to prove the decay estimates of orthonormal wavelets and their derivatives. Although (
21
) follows from (
22
) we decided to present both proofs since
we use different arguments there. In fact, the estimates in (
21
) are in the spirit of

[
6
]
and
[
20
]
, whereas (
22
) gives an explicit dependence
of the involved constants on the order of derivatives of the wavelet
\psi
, underlying the strong ultra-analyticity
of
\psi
which is not considered in

,

]
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\sigma>1
, and let
\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be an orthonormal wavelet as in Lemma
3.3
,
so that
\widehat{\psi}\in\mathcal{D_{\sigma}}({\mathbb{R}})
is given by (
17
). Then
\psi
together with its derivatives satisfies the following estimates:

i)

for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
h>0
there exists
C>0
such that

|\psi^{(n)}(x)|\leq Ce^{-h\left(\ln^{\sigma}(|x|)W^{-1}(\ln(|x|))\right)^{1/(\sigma-1)}},\,\quad|x|\;\text{large enough},

(21)

where
W
is the Lambert function.

ii)

for every
h>0
there exists
C>0
such that

|\psi^{(n)}(x)|\leq C^{n+1}\,n!^{s}e^{-h\left(\ln^{\sigma}(|x|)W^{-1}(\ln(|x|))\right)^{1/(\sigma-1)})},

(22)

for every
0<s\leq 1
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and large enough
|x|
. Here
W
denotes the Lambert function.

Proof.

i)
Let
h>0
be given and let
n=0
. Since

\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=e^{i\frac{\xi}{2}}b_{a}(\xi)
,
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
where
b_{a}\in\,\mathcal{D_{\sigma}}({\mathbb{R}})
is even extension of (
16
), we can apply
(
19
) to obtain

\displaystyle|\psi(x)|=C\left|\widehat{b}_{a}\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|\leq C_{h}e^{-\frac{h}{A}T_{\sigma}(\left|x+\frac{1}{2}\right|)}\leq C_{h}e^{-hT_{\sigma}(\left|x\right|)},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

where we used (
11
) for the last inequality,
and replaced
h
by
\displaystyle\frac{h}{A}
for suitable
A>0
. Now (
21
) with
n=0
follows from
the left-hand side of (
9
), cf. Proposition
3.4
.

Next we estimate the derivatives of
\psi
.
Since

|\psi^{(n)}(x)|=C|(\xi^{n}e^{i\frac{\xi}{2}}b_{a}(\xi))\hat{}\,(x)|=C|(\xi^{n}g(\xi))\hat{}\,(x)|,

and
g(\xi):=e^{i\frac{\xi}{2}}b_{a}(\xi)\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
, see the proof of Lemma
3.3
,
it is sufficient to prove that
\xi^{n}g(\xi)\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

The function
g(\xi)
is compactly supported, so the same holds for
\xi^{n}g(\xi)
. Let
K
denote the support of

\xi^{n}g(\xi)
. Then we put
\max_{K}|\xi|^{n}=a_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and by the Leibnitz formula we get

\displaystyle|(\xi^{n}g(\xi))^{(p)}|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}(\xi^{n})g^{(p-k)}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}n(n-1)\cdots(n-(k-1))|\xi|^{n-k}\,|g^{(p-k)}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}n(n-1)\cdots(n-(k-1))a_{n-k}\,C^{(p-k)^{\sigma}}(p-k)^{\tau(p-k)^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq Ap^{\tau p^{\sigma}}C^{p^{\sigma}}\sum_{k=0}^{p}\binom{p}{k}n^{k}=C_{1}^{p^{\sigma}}p^{\tau p^{\sigma}},

where
A=\max\{a_{n},\dots,a_{n-p}\}
, and
C_{1}=2nAC
.

Thus
\xi^{n}e^{i\frac{\xi}{2}}b_{a}(\xi)\in\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})

for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and the estimate (
21
)
follows from Proposition
3.4
.

ii)

Take arbitrary
\tau>0
and fix
0<s\leq 1
. Since
{\widehat{\psi}}\in\mathcal{D}_{\frac{\tau}{4},\sigma}({\mathbb{R}})
, for any
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we have

\frac{|\xi^{n}\widehat{\psi}^{(m)}(\xi)|}{n!^{s}\,m^{\frac{\tau}{2}m^{\sigma}}}\leq B_{\tau}\sup_{m,n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\frac{A^{n}}{n!^{s}}\frac{B_{\tau}^{m^{\sigma}}}{m^{\frac{\tau}{4}m^{\sigma}}}\leq C_{\tau},\quad\xi\in\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\psi},

(23)

since
\displaystyle\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{B_{\tau}^{m^{\sigma}}}{m^{\frac{\tau}{4}m^{\sigma}}}
is bounded by a constant which depends on
\tau
, cf.
[
23
, Lemma 2.3]
.
Thus (
23
) gives

|\xi^{n}\widehat{\psi}^{(m)}(\xi)|\leq C_{\tau}n!^{s}m^{\frac{\tau}{2}m^{\sigma}},\quad\xi\in K,

(24)

and from Lemma
3.5
(see also Remark
3.6
) it follows that

|(\xi^{n}\widehat{\psi}(\xi))^{(m)}|\leq C^{\prime}_{\tau}A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{m}n!^{s}m^{\frac{\tau}{2}m^{\sigma}},\quad\xi\in K,

for suitable
C^{\prime}_{\tau},A_{1},B_{1}>0
.Thus

|x^{m}{\psi}^{(n)}(x)|={|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}((\xi^{n}\widehat{\psi}(\xi))^{(m)})(x)|}\\
\leq\int_{K}|(\xi^{n}\widehat{\psi}(\xi))^{(m)}|d\xi\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{\tau}A_{1}^{n}B_{1}^{m}n!^{s}m^{\frac{\tau}{2}m^{\sigma}},\quad m,n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(25)

Next we notice that

B_{1}^{m}m^{\frac{\tau}{2}m^{\sigma}}=\frac{B_{1}^{m}}{m^{\frac{\tau}{2}m^{\sigma}}}m^{\tau m^{\sigma}}\leq C\,m^{\tau m^{\sigma}},\quad m,n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}},

with
\displaystyle C=\sup_{m_{1}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{B_{1}^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}^{\frac{\tau}{2}m_{1}^{\sigma}}}=e^{T_{\tau/2,\sigma}(B_{1})}
,
that is

B_{1}^{m}m^{\frac{\tau}{2}m^{\sigma}}\leq e^{T_{\frac{\tau}{2},\sigma}(B_{1})}\,m^{\tau m^{\sigma}},\quad m,n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}.

(26)

Now, from (
25
), (
26
), (
7
),
and (
10
) it follows that there exists
C_{\tau}^{\prime\prime\prime}>0
such that

|{\psi}^{(n)}(x)|\leq(C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\tau})^{(n+1)}n!^{s}\inf_{m\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\frac{m^{\tau m^{\sigma}}}{|x|^{m}}\\
=(C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{\tau})^{(n+1)}n!^{s}e^{-T_{\tau,\sigma}(|x|)}\leq C^{n+1}\,n!^{s}e^{-\tau^{-\frac{1}{\sigma-1}}T_{\sigma}(|x|)},

(27)

for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
|x|
large enough, and for a suitable
C>0
.

Now for any given
h>0
we can choose
\tau
so that
\tau\asymp h^{{1-\sigma}}
, and
then (
22
) follows from (
27
) and the left-hand side of (
2.2
),
which completes the proof.
∎

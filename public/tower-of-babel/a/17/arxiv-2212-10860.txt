Identifying the multifractal set on which energy dissipates
in a turbulent Navier-Stokes fluid

Abstract
The rich multifractal properties of fluid turbulence illustrated by the work of Parisi and Frisch are related explicitly to Leray’s weak solutions of the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations. Directly from this correspondence it is found that the set on which energy dissipates, 𝔽 m \mathbb{F}_{m} , has a range of dimensions 𝔇 m = 3 / m \mathfrak{D}_{m}=3/m ( 1 ≤ m ≤ ∞ 1\leq m\leq\infty ), and a corresponding range of sub-Kolmogorov dissipation inverse length scales L ​ η m − 1 ≤ R ​ e 3 / ( 1 + 𝔇 m ) L\eta_{m}^{-1}\leq Re^{3/(1+\mathfrak{D}_{m})} spanning R ​ e 3 / 4 Re^{3/4} to R ​ e 3 Re^{3} . Correspondingly, the multifractal model scaling parameter h h , must obey h ≥ h m ​ i ​ n h\geq h_{min} with − 2 3 ≤ h m ​ i ​ n ≤ 1 3 -{\scriptstyle\frac{2}{3}}\leq h_{min}\leq{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{3}} .

3
Correspondence with the multifractal model

Kolmogorov’s 1941 theory (K41) of three-dimensional, homogeneous, isotropic turbulence
[
3
]
revolves around the
p
th-order velocity structure function
S_{\!p}
defined as

S_{\!p}=\left<\left|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$}+\mbox{\boldmath$r$})-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})\right|^{p}\right>_{stat.av.}\,.

(3.1)

The scaling properties of
S_{\!p}
have their origin in the invariance property enjoyed by the incompressible Euler equations under the transformation
\mbox{\boldmath$x$}^{\prime}=\lambda^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$x$}
;
t^{\prime}=\lambda^{h-1}t
and
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\lambda^{h}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{\prime}
. The scaling parameter
h
can take any value, unlike the Navier-Stokes equations where it must satisfy
h=-1
. The invariance property shows that
S_{\!p}
scales as
S_{\!p}\sim r^{hp}
. As explained in
[
3
]
, the axioms of K41 lead to
h={\scriptstyle\frac{1}{3}}
, which is consistent with the “four-fifths law”
S_{3}=-{\scriptstyle\frac{4}{5}}\varepsilon r
. The problem with the result
S_{\!p}\sim r^{p/3}
is that experimental data deviate from the linear in
p
scaling but instead lie on a concave curve
\zeta_{p}
below the line
{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{3}}p
. The multifractal model of Parisi and Frisch
[
2
,
3
]
adapted the K41 formalism by relaxing the requirement that
h={\scriptstyle\frac{1}{3}}
by insisting that the energy dissipation rate is invariant in
h
only in an average sense. They achieved this by introducing the probability
P(h)\sim r^{3-D(h)}
of observing a given scaling exponent
h
at the scale
r
. Each value of
h
belongs to a given fractal set of dimension
D(h)
. This procedure produces the result

S_{\!p}\sim r^{\zeta_{p}}\quad\mbox{where}\quad\zeta_{p}=\min_{h}\left[hp+3-D(h)\right]\,.

(3.2)

This must be constrained by the four-fifths law which requires
\zeta_{3}=1
, thus leading to the inequalities for
D(h)
and the co-dimension
C(h)
(which sum to the spatial dimension)

D(h)\leq 3h+2\quad\Rightarrow\quad C(h)\geq 1-3h\,.

(3.3)

Given that K41 and the MFM models revolve around the flow being homogeneous and isotropic, any comparison with results from the Navier-Stokes equations will likely only be valid when the flow has reached a fully turbulent state : i.e. when
T
is sufficiently large.

To make the two theories touch, it is natural to suggest a correspondence between
\mathfrak{D}_{m}
and
D(h)
and
\mathfrak{C}_{m}
and
C(h)
, which leads to a simple inequality relating
m
and
h

\frac{3}{m}\leq 2+3h\,,

(3.4)

from which we find
h\geq h_{min}
with
h_{min}=m^{-1}-{\scriptstyle\frac{2}{3}}
. The two opposite limits
m\to 1
and
m\to\infty
give

-{\scriptstyle\frac{2}{3}}\leq h_{min}\leq{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{3}}\,,

(3.5)

which is precisely the range found in
[
36
]
.

A more modern version of the multifractal model uses the theory of large deviations to re-express
P(h)
as
P(h)\sim r^{C(h)}
, where
C(h)
is the multifractal spectrum
[
3
,
35
,
6
]
.
C(h)
therefore plays the role as the co-dimension. In the theory of large deviations it is possible that
C(h)\geq 3
, the domain dimension, corresponding to
D(h)<0
, but only with an infinitesimal probability. This would correspond to
m<0
, which must be excluded here. The exponent

in (
2.15
) is bounded below by

\frac{3}{1+\mathfrak{D}_{m}}\geq\frac{1}{1+h}\,.

(3.6)

The left hand side of (

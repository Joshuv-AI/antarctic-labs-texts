Besov Regularity of Weak solutions to a Class of Nonlinear Elliptic Equations F. Zhou is the corresponding author.
Huimin Cheng

Abstract
In this article, we study a Besov regularity estimate of weak solutions to a class of nonlinear elliptic equations in divergence form. The main purpose is to establish Calderón-Zygmund type estimate in Besov spaces with more general assumptions on coefficients, non-homogeneous term and integrable index. By involving the Sharp maximal function, we establish an oscillation estimate of weak solutions in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. By deriving a higher integrability estimate of weak solutions, we obtain the desired regularity estimate which expands the Calderón-Zygmund theory for nonlinear elliptic equations. Keywords: Nonlinear elliptic equations; Higher integrability; Regularity; Besov spaces. Mathematics Subject classification: 35B65, 35J60.

Introduction

The main purpose of this article is to obtain the Besov regularity estimate of weak solutions to a class of nonlinear elliptic equations in divergence form

2.1
N
-function

Definition 2.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

We say that a function

:
[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is an
N
-function, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1) There exist derivatives
P^{\prime}(t)
and
P^{\prime\prime}(t)
of
P(t)
such that
P(0)=0
,
P(1)=1
,
P^{\prime}(t)>0
and
P^{\prime\prime}(t)>0
for
t>0
.

(2) (
\Delta_{2}
-condition) There exists
c_{1}>0
such that
P(2t)\leq c_{1}P(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. By
\Delta_{2}(P)
we denote the smallest constant
c_{1}
.

We write
f\sim g
if and only if there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
C_{1}f\leq g\leq C_{2}f
. Let
P(t)
be an
N
-function. It is obvious that
P(2t)\sim P(t)
. Defining the complementary function
\tilde{P}
of
P
by

\tilde{P}(s)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\{st-P(t)\},

(2.1)

one finds that

P(t)\sim P^{\prime}(t)t\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tilde{P}\left(\frac{P(t)}{t}\right)\sim P(t),

(2.2)

uniformly in
t\geq 0
, see
[
10
]
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
C(\epsilon,\Delta_{2}(\tilde{P}))
such that for all
t,u\geq 0
, there holds

t\,u\leq\epsilon\,P(t)+C(\epsilon,\Delta_{2}(\tilde{P}))\tilde{P}(u),

(2.3)

which is a Young type inequality (
[
10
]
). In particular,

ab\leq\epsilon\,a^{2}+C(\epsilon)\,b^{2},

(2.4)

with
a,b>0
.

Throughout this article, we assume that
N
-function
P(t)
satisfies the following two assumptions:

(P1)
P(st)\sim P(s)P(t)
,
\tilde{P}(st)\sim\tilde{P}(s)\tilde{P}(t)
,
\frac{P(t)}{t^{2}}
is monotone increasing on
(0,+\infty)
.

(P2) There is a positive constant
\hat{C}
such that

\hat{C}^{-1}\,\frac{P\left[(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]}{\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}\leq\frac{|V_{P}(\xi)-V_{P}(\eta)|^{2}}{|\xi-\eta|^{2}}\leq\hat{C}\,\frac{P\left[(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]}{\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}

(2.5)

for any
\xi,\,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
|\xi-\eta|\neq 0
.

At the end of this subsection, by involving the
N
-function, we present a modified version of the Hölder inequality as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|f(x)g(x)|\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,\tilde{P}^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\tilde{P}(|f(x)|)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)\cdot P^{-1}\left(\int_{\Omega}P(|g(x)|)\operatorname{d}\!x\right).

(2.6)

Here

and

is defined by (

\displaystyle\int_{B_{d}}Q(|\nabla\omega|)

\displaystyle\!\!\leq\!\!

\displaystyle C\int_{k_{0}B_{d}}Q\left(\mathcal{M}_{P,k_{0}d}^{\#}(|\nabla\omega|)\right)

\displaystyle C\,Q(\delta^{\kappa})\int_{B_{d}}Q(|\nabla\omega|)

\displaystyle+\,\,C\,\delta^{-n}\sup_{y\in k_{0}B_{d}}\,\,\sup_{0<R<\frac{k_{0}d}{\delta}}\left(\widetilde{T\circ P^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\left[\fint_{B_{R}}V(x,B_{R})\right]\cdot\int_{B_{d}}Q(|\nabla\omega|)

\displaystyle+\,\,\frac{C\,\delta^{-n}}{Q(r-\rho)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q(|\,u\,\chi_{\lambda B_{d}}|)+C\,\delta^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q(\,|\nabla u|\,\chi_{B_{r}\backslash B_{\rho}})

\displaystyle\int_{B_{d}}Q(|\nabla\omega|)

\displaystyle C\sup_{y\in k_{0}B_{d}}\,\,\sup_{0<R<\frac{k_{0}d}{\delta}}\left(\widetilde{T\circ P^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\left[\fint_{B_{R}}V(x,B_{R})\right]\cdot\int_{B_{d}}Q(|\nabla\omega|)

\displaystyle+\,\,\frac{C}{Q(r-\rho)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q(|\,u\,\chi_{\lambda B_{d}}|)+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q(\,|\nabla u|\,\chi_{B_{r}\backslash B_{\rho}})

\displaystyle+\,\,C\left[Q\left(\frac{k_{0}d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q(|\,F\,\chi_{\lambda B_{d}}|)

\displaystyle+\,\,C\,|k_{0}B_{d}|\left[Q\left(\frac{k_{0}d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]Q(\mu).

Moreover, if

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in k_{0}B_{d}}\,\,\sup_{0<R<\frac{k_{0}d_{0}}{\delta}}\left(\widetilde{T\circ P^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\left[\fint_{B_{R}}V(x,B_{R})\right]

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in B_{\frac{{\operatorname{dist}}(x_{0},\,\Omega)}{2}}(x_{0})}\,\,\sup_{0<R<\frac{k_{0}d_{0}}{\delta}}\left(\widetilde{T\circ P^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\left[\fint_{B_{R}}V(x,B_{R})\right],

and hence

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{Q(r-\rho)}\int_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|u|)+C\int_{B_{r}\backslash B_{\rho}}Q(|\nabla u|)

\displaystyle+\,\,C\left[Q\left(\frac{k_{0}d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]\int_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|F|)

\displaystyle+\,\,C\,|\lambda B_{d}|\left[Q\left(\frac{k_{0}d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]Q(\mu).

\displaystyle\!\leq

\displaystyle\!\!C\int_{B_{d}}Q(|\nabla\omega|)

\displaystyle\!\frac{C}{Q(r-\rho)}\int_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|u|)+C^{\prime}\int_{B_{r}\backslash B_{\rho}}Q(|\nabla u|)

\displaystyle+\,C\left[Q\left(\frac{d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]\int_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|F|)+C\,|\lambda B_{d}|\left[Q\left(\frac{d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]Q(\mu).

Filling the hole, that is, adding to both sides of previous inequality the quantity

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}Q(|\nabla u|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\nu\int_{B_{r}}Q(|\nabla u|)+\frac{C}{Q(r-\rho)}\int_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|u|)

\displaystyle+\,\,C\left[Q\left(\frac{d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]\int_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|F|)+C\,|\lambda B_{d}|\left[Q\left(\frac{d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]Q(\mu),

where

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{d}{2}}}Q(|\nabla u|)

\displaystyle\frac{C}{Q(r-\rho)}\int_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|u|)+C\left[Q\left(\frac{d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]\int_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|F|)

(3.14)

\displaystyle+\,\,C\,|\lambda B_{d}|\left[Q\left(\frac{d}{r-\rho}\right)+1\right]Q(\mu).

Since above estimates are valid for arbitrary radii

\fint_{\frac{1}{2}B_{d}}Q(|\nabla u|)\leq C\left[Q(\mu)+\fint_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|F|)+\frac{1}{Q(d)}\fint_{\lambda B_{d}}Q(|u|)\right].

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof.

Given a point

, let us write

. We have

\displaystyle\fint_{B}V(x,B)\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\fint_{B}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\fint_{B}\frac{|A(x,\xi)-A(y,\xi)|}{\frac{P\left[\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]}{\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\fint_{B}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{k}\int_{B\,\cap\,A_{k}(x)}\frac{|A(x,\xi)-A(y,\xi)|}{\frac{P\left[\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]}{\left(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\sum_{k}\int_{B}\int_{B\,\cap\,A_{k}(x)}|x-y|^{\alpha}(g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y))\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\sum_{k}\int_{B}\int_{B\,\cap\,A_{k}(x)}|x-y|^{\frac{n\alpha}{n-\alpha}}\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{n-\alpha}{n}}

\displaystyle\ \ \cdot\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\sum_{k}\int_{B}\int_{B\,\cap\,A_{k}(x)}(g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y))^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\sum_{k}\int_{B}\int_{B\,\cap\,A_{k}(x)}(g_{k}(x)+g_{k}(y))^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\int^{r_{k}}_{r_{k+1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left\|(\tau_{r\xi}\,g_{k}+g_{k})\,\left(P\left[\left(\mu^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{q}_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}\operatorname{d}\!\sigma(\xi)\frac{\operatorname{d}\!r}{r}.

Here
\tau_{r\xi}\,g_{k}(x)=g_{k}(x+r\xi)
. Since
P(|\nabla u|)\in L^{\frac{n}{n-2\alpha}}(\Omega)
and
g_{k}\in L^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}(\Omega)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|(\tau_{r\xi}\,g_{k}+g_{k})\,\left(P\left[\left(\mu^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{2R}}(\tau_{r\xi}\,g_{k}+g_{k})^{2}\,P\left[\left(\mu^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left[\int_{B_{2R}}(\tau_{r\xi}\,g_{k}+g_{k})^{2\,\frac{n}{2\alpha}}\operatorname{d}\!x\right]^{\frac{2\alpha}{n}}\left[\int_{B_{2R}}P\left[\left(\mu^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]^{\frac{n}{n-2\alpha}}\operatorname{d}\!x\right]^{\frac{n-2\alpha}{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|(\tau_{r\xi}\,g_{k}+g_{k})\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}(B_{2R})}\,\left\|P\left[\left(\mu^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\frac{n}{n-2\alpha}}(B_{2R})}.

On the other hand, noting that for each
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
r_{k+1}\leq r\leq r_{k}
, there holds

\|(\tau_{r\xi}\,g_{k}+g_{k})\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}(B_{2R})}\leq\|g_{k}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}((B_{2R})-r_{k}\xi)}+\|g_{k}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}(B_{2R})}\leq 2\,\|g_{k}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}(\varrho B_{R})},

where
\varrho=3+\frac{1}{R}
. Hence one gets

J_{1}\,\leq\,C\,\left\|P\left[\left(\mu^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\frac{n}{n-2\alpha}}(B_{2R})}\,\|\{g_{k}\}_{k}\|_{l^{q}\left(L^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}(\varrho B_{R})\right)}<\infty.

Using the fact that
u\in W^{1,P}(\Omega)
, we deduce that

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{B_{\delta}}|h|^{(1-\alpha)q-n}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{2R+|h|}}P(\mu+|\nabla u|)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int^{\delta}_{0}\rho^{(1-\alpha)q-1}\operatorname{d}\!\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{2R+|h|}}P(\mu+|\nabla u|)\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

As
\frac{P(|F|)}{|F|^{2}}F\in B^{\alpha}_{2,q}(\Omega)
, it follows that

J_{3}=C\,\left\|\frac{\Delta_{h}\left[\frac{P(|F|)}{|F|^{2}}F\right]}{|h|^{\alpha}}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\frac{\operatorname{d}\!h}{|h|^{n}};L^{2}(B_{2R})\right)}<\infty.\\

Therefore, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Xicheng Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper we study the global boundedness for the solutions to a class of possibly degenerate parabolic equations by De-Giorgi’s iteration. As applications, we show the existence of weak solutions for possibly degenerate stochastic differential equations with singular diffusion and drift coefficients. Moreover, by the Markov selection theorem of Krylov [8], we also establish the existence of the associated strong Markov family. Keywords : Maximum principle, De-Giorgi’s iteration, Stochastic differential equation, Krylov’s estimate, Markov selection. AMS 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35K10, 60H10

Introduction

Consider the following elliptic equation of divergence form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(
d\geqslant 2
):

\displaystyle\mathord{{\rm div}}(a\cdot\nabla u)=0,

(1.1)

where
a:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
is a Borel measurable function and
\nabla:=(\partial_{x_{1}},\cdots,\partial_{x_{d}})
. We introduce the following two functions:

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}(x):=\inf_{|\xi|=1}\xi\cdot a(x)\xi,\quad\mu_{0}(x):=\sup_{|\xi|=1}\frac{|a(x)\xi|^{2}}{\xi\cdot a(x)\xi}.

(1.2)

Suppose that
\lambda_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
are nonnegative measurable functions.
If
\lambda^{-1}_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
are essentially bounded, that is,
a
is uniformly elliptic, then the celebrated works of De-Giorgi
[
5
]
and Nash
[
15
]
said that
any weak solutions of elliptic equation (
1.1
) are bounded and Hölder continuous. Moreover, Moser
[
14
]
showed that any weak solutions of (
1.1
)
satisfy the Harnack inequality.

In
[
19
]
, Trudinger considered the non-uniformly elliptic equation (
1.1
) under the following integrability assumptions:

\lambda^{-1}_{0}\in L^{p_{0}},\ \mu_{0}\in L^{p_{1}}\mbox{ with $p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty]$ satisfying $\tfrac{1}{p_{0}}+\tfrac{1}{p_{1}}<\tfrac{2}{d}$,}

and showed that any generalized solutions of (
1.1
) are locally bounded and weak Harnack inequality holds.
Recently, Bella and Schäffner
[
3
]
showed the same results under the following
sharp
condition on
p_{0},p_{1}
,

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{p_{0}}+\tfrac{1}{p_{1}}<\tfrac{2}{d-1},\ \ p_{0},p_{1}\in[1,\infty],

(1.3)

where the key point is a new Sobolev embedding inequality of variational type. In this paper we are interested in a parabolic version of
[
3
]
,
and aim to establish the global boundedness for the solutions of non-uniformly parabolic equations. More precisely, we shall consider the following parabolic equation of divergence form in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\mathord{{\rm div}}(a\cdot\nabla u)+b\cdot\nabla u+f,

(1.4)

where

a:{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d},\ b:{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\ f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}

are Borel measurable functions. As in (
1.2
), we introduce

\displaystyle\lambda(x):=\inf_{t\geqslant 0,|\xi|=1}\xi\cdot a(t,x)\xi,\quad\mu(x):=\sup_{t\geqslant 0,|\xi|=1}\frac{|a(t,x)\xi|^{2}}{\xi\cdot a(t,x)\xi},

(1.5)

and suppose that
\lambda
and
\mu
are nonnegative Borel measurable functions.

First of all we introduce the following notion of weak solutions to PDE (

Consider the following SDE:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=\sqrt{2}\sigma(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}+b(t,X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\ \ X_{0}=x,

(1.15)

where

and
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\otimes{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

and

are Borel measurable functions. Note that the generator of SDE (

{\mathscr{L}}^{\sigma,b}_{t}f(x)=(\sigma^{ik}\sigma^{jk})(t,x)\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f(x)+b^{j}(t,x)\partial_{j}f(x).

Here and after we shall use the usual Einstein convention for summation: an index appearing twice in a product will be summed automatically.

It is well known that if
\sigma
and

are Lipschitz continuous in

, then SDE (
1.15
) admits a unique strong solution.
When
\sigma
is bounded measurable and uniformly elliptic and

[
13
]

showed the existence of weak solutions to SDE (
1.15
) (see
[
12
]
for bounded measurable drift

When

is the identity matrix and

is divergence free and belongs to

In particular, the stochastic Lagrangian trajectories associated with Leray’s solutions of 3D-Navier-Stokes equations are constructed.
However, when diffusion coefficient

To show the existence of weak solutions, the key step is to prove the following estimate of Krylov’s type: for any

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\int^{t}_{0}f(s,X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)\leqslant C|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}.

Note that if we let

{\mathscr{L}}^{\sigma,b}_{t}f(x)=\partial_{i}(a^{ij}(t,\cdot)\partial_{j}f)(x)+(b^{j}-\partial_{i}a^{ij})(t,x)\partial_{j}f(t,x).

Here we shall follow Stroock and Varadhan’s method

for SDEs (

2.
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathscr{D}}:=C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1})

be the space of all smooth functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
with compact supports and
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}
the dual space of

{\mathscr{D}}
, which is also called the distribution space. The duality between
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathscr{D}}
is denoted by
\langle\!\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\!\rangle
.
In particular, if
f\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}
is locally integrable and

, then

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle f,g\rangle\!\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle f(t),g(t)\rangle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\ \ \mbox{with\ \ }\langle f(t),g(t)\rangle:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t,x)g(t,x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x.

(2.1)

For
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, let
{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}:=L^{q}({\mathbb{R}};L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

and
{\mathbb{L}}^{p,q}_{x,t}:=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}))
be the space of spatial-time functions with norms, respectively,

\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{p}^{q}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{1/q},\ \|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p,q}_{x,t}}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|f(\cdot,x)\|_{q}^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x\right)^{1/p},

where
\|\cdot\|_{p}
stands for the usual

-norm.
By Minkowskii’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p,q}_{x,t}}\mbox{ if }q\geqslant p;\quad\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p,q}_{x,t}}\leqslant\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}\mbox{ if }q\leqslant p.

(2.2)

For
r>0
and
(s,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, we define

Q_{r}:=[-r^{2},r^{2}]\times B_{r}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1},\ Q^{s,z}_{r}:=Q_{r}+(s,z),\ \ B^{z}_{r}:=B_{r}+z,

and for
p\in[1,\infty]
, introduce the following localized

-space:

\displaystyle{\widetilde{L}}^{p}:=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{p}:=\sup_{z}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{B^{z}_{1}}f\|_{p}<\infty\Big\},

(2.3)

and for
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}:=\Big\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}):|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}:=\sup_{s,z}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{Q^{s,z}_{1}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}<\infty\Big\},

(2.4)

and similarly for
\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p,q}_{x,t}
.
Clearly, for
p\leqslant p^{\prime}
and

,

\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}_{t,x}\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x},\ \ \widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{x,t}\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p,q}_{x,t}.

By a finite covering technique, it is easy to see that for any
T,r>0
(see
[
21
]

\displaystyle|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|{\mathbf{1}}_{[0,T]}f|\mspace{-3mu}|\mspace{-3mu}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}\asymp\sup_{z}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{[0,T]\times B^{z}_{r}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}.

(2.5)

First of all, we have the following Gagliado-Nirenberge’s interpolation estimate.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix
{\varkappa}\in[2d/(d+2),2]
and
\theta\in[0,1]
with exception
\theta=1
and
{\varkappa}=d
. For any
r\geqslant 2
and
s\geqslant 1
with

\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{r}=\tfrac{\theta}{2}\Big(\tfrac{2}{d}+1-\tfrac{2}{{\varkappa}}\Big),\ \ s\theta\leqslant 2,

there is a constant
C=C(\varkappa,d,r,\theta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{s,r}_{t,x}}\leqslant C\|\nabla f\|^{\theta}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2,{\varkappa}}_{t,x}}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2(1-\theta)s/(2-s\theta),2}_{t,x}}^{1-\theta}.

(2.6)

Proof.

By Gagliado-Nirenberge’s interpolation inequality, we have

\|f\|_{r}\leqslant C\|\nabla f\|^{\theta}_{\varkappa}\|f\|^{1-\theta}_{2}.

Since
s\theta\leqslant 2
, by Hölder’s inequality we further have

\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{s,r}_{t,x}}\leqslant C\|\nabla f\|^{\theta}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2,{\varkappa}}_{t,x}}\|f\|^{1-\theta}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2(1-\theta)s/(2-s\theta),2}_{t,x}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

(2.7)

Proof.

Let

\theta\in[0,\frac{2}{s}\wedge 1)
such that

\|{\mathbf{1}}_{Q_{\tau_{1}}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{s,r}_{t,x}}\leqslant\|\eta f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{s,r}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|\nabla(\eta f)\|^{\theta}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2,{\varkappa}}_{t,x}}\|\eta f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2(1-\theta)s/(2-s\theta),2}_{t,x}}^{1-\theta}.

Moreover, we have

\|\nabla(\eta f)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2,{\varkappa}}_{t,x}}\leqslant\|\nabla\eta f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2,{\varkappa}}_{t,x}}+\|\eta\nabla f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2,{\varkappa}}_{t,x}}\lesssim(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{-1}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{Q_{\tau_{2}}}f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2,{\varkappa}}_{t,x}}+\|{\mathbf{1}}_{Q_{\tau_{2}}}\nabla f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2,{\varkappa}}_{t,x}}.

Since
\theta\in[0,1)
and
s\theta<2
, the desired estimate follows by Young’s inequality.
∎

We need the following elementary variational inequality.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For any
\alpha_{i},p_{i}\geqslant 1
and
\beta_{i}>0
,
i=1,\cdots,N
, there is a constant
C=C(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i},p_{i},N)>0

such that for all
f_{i}\in L^{\infty}([\tau,\sigma])
and
0<\delta-\tau\leqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\inf_{\ell\in C^{1}([\tau,\delta])}\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\alpha_{i}}|f_{i}(s)|^{p_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\!\!\!:\ell^{\prime}(s)\leqslant 0,\ell(\tau)=1,\ell(\delta)=0\Bigg\}\\
&\qquad\quad\lesssim_{C}(\delta-\tau)^{-\max\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{p_{i}}-\frac{1}{{\mathord{{\rm min}}}\beta_{i}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|f_{i}(s)|^{\beta_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}}.\end{split}

(2.8)

Proof.

(Step 1).
Fix
\beta\in(0,1)
. We first show that for any
0\leqslant f\in L^{1}([\tau,\sigma])
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{J}}(f)&:=\inf_{\ell\in C^{1}([\tau,\delta])}\Bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\alpha_{i}}f(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s:\ell^{\prime}(s)\leqslant 0,\ell(\tau)=1,\ell(\delta)=0\Bigg\}\\
&\leqslant\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}2^{\frac{\theta\alpha_{i}}{\beta}}(\delta-\tau)^{1-\alpha_{i}-\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}f(s)^{\beta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\end{split}

(2.9)

where
\theta:=1/(\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\alpha_{N})
. Let

\varepsilon:=\left(\frac{1}{\delta-\tau}\int^{\delta}_{\tau}f(s)^{\beta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}},\ \ g(s):=f(s)+\varepsilon.

Clearly, we have

{\mathcal{J}}(f)\leqslant{\mathcal{J}}(g).

Let
g_{n}:=g*\rho_{n}
be the mollifying approximation of
g
. Define

A:=\int^{\delta}_{\tau}g(s)^{-\theta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,\ \ A_{n}:=\int^{\delta}_{\tau}g_{n}(s)^{-\theta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,\ \ \ell_{n}(r):=A^{-1}_{n}\int^{\delta}_{r}g_{n}(s)^{-\theta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Since
\ell^{\prime}_{n}(s)=-A^{-1}_{n}g_{n}(s)^{-\theta}\leqslant 0
,
\ell_{n}(\tau)=1
and
\ell_{n}(\delta)=0
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(g)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}_{n}(s)|^{\alpha_{i}}g(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{n}^{-\alpha_{i}}\int^{\delta}_{\tau}g_{n}(s)^{-\theta\alpha_{i}}g(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Due to
g_{n}(s)^{-1}\leqslant\varepsilon^{-1}
and
1-\theta\alpha_{i}\leqslant 0
, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(g)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}A^{-\alpha_{i}}\int^{\delta}_{\tau}g(s)^{1-\theta\alpha_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}A^{-\alpha_{i}}(\delta-\tau)\varepsilon^{1-\theta\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A^{-\alpha_{i}}(\delta-\tau)^{1-\frac{1-\theta\alpha_{i}}{\beta}}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}f(s)^{\beta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1-\theta\alpha_{i}}{\beta}}.

(2.10)

On the other hand, by the inverse Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle A^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}g(s)^{-\theta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{-1}\leqslant(\delta-\tau)^{-1-\frac{\theta}{\gamma}}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}g(s)^{\beta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{\theta}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(\delta-\tau)^{-1-\frac{\theta}{\beta}}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}f(s)^{\beta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+(\delta-\tau)\varepsilon^{\beta}\right)^{\frac{\theta}{\beta}}

\displaystyle=(\delta-\tau)^{-1-\frac{\theta}{\beta}}\left(2\int^{\delta}_{\tau}f(s)^{\beta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{\theta}{\beta}}.

Substituting this into (
2.10
), we obtain (
2.9
).

(Step 2).
Let
p:=\max_{i=1,\cdots,N}p_{i}
. Since
\alpha_{i}\geqslant 1
,
by Jensen’s inequality with respect to the probability measure
-{\mathbf{1}}_{[\tau,\delta]}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\ell(s)=-{\mathbf{1}}_{[\tau,\delta]}\ell^{\prime}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s
, we have

\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\alpha_{i}}|f_{i}(s)|^{p_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\leqslant\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\frac{(\alpha_{i}-1)p}{p_{i}}+1}|f_{i}(s)|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\alpha_{i}}|f_{i}(s)|^{p_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\frac{(\alpha_{i}-1)p}{p_{i}}+1}|f_{i}(s)|^{p}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant N\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\frac{(\alpha_{i}-1)p}{p_{i}}+1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f_{i}(s)|^{p}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Let
{\mathscr{J}}
be the left hand of (
2.8
) and
\beta:={\mathord{{\rm min}}}_{i=1,\cdots,N}\beta_{i}
. By (
2.9
), we get

\displaystyle{\mathscr{J}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\delta-\tau)^{-\frac{(\alpha_{i}-1)p}{p_{i}}-\frac{1}{\beta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f_{i}(s)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{p}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}(\delta-\tau)^{-\max\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{p_{i}}-\frac{1}{p\beta}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|f_{i}(s)|^{\beta}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}(\delta-\tau)^{-\max\frac{\alpha_{i}-1}{p_{i}}-\frac{1}{p\beta}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\delta-\tau)^{\frac{1}{\beta}-\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|f_{i}(s)|^{\beta_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}},

where the last step is due to
\beta\leqslant\beta_{i}
and Hölder’s inequality. The proof is thus complete.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
w=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{N}):{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a bounded measurable function with support in
I\times B_{2}
, where
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite time interval.
Let
\alpha_{i}>0
,
\theta_{i}\in[0,1]
and

satisfy

\alpha_{i}p_{i}\geqslant 1,\ \tfrac{1}{\varkappa_{i}}=\tfrac{1}{p_{i}}+\tfrac{\theta_{i}}{d-1},\ \ i=1,\cdots,N.

For given

and
Q:=I\times B_{\delta}
, there are
\gamma,C>0
depending only on
\alpha_{i},\varkappa_{i},p_{i},d
such that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(w)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\eta\in C^{1}_{c}(B_{\delta};[0,1])}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|w_{i}|\nabla\eta|^{\alpha_{i}}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{i},q_{i}}_{x,t}}:\eta=1\mbox{ on $B_{\tau}$}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C}(\delta-\tau)^{-\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\|{\mathbf{1}}_{Q}\nabla w_{i}\|^{\theta_{i}}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\varkappa_{i},q_{i}}_{x,t}}\|{\mathbf{1}}_{Q}w_{i}\|^{1-\theta_{i}}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\varkappa_{i},q_{i}}_{x,t}}+\|{\mathbf{1}}_{Q}w_{i}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{\varkappa_{i},q_{i}}_{x,t}}\Big).

Proof.

Let

F_{i}(x):=\left(\int_{I}|w_{i}(t,x)|^{q_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{1/q_{i}}.

For given radial test function
\eta(x)=\ell(|x|)
, by Fubini’s theorem and the transform of spherical coordinates, we have

\displaystyle\|w_{i}|\nabla\eta|^{\alpha_{i}}\|^{p_{i}}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{i},q_{i}}_{x,t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}F_{i}^{p_{i}}|\nabla\eta|^{p_{i}\alpha_{i}}\leqslant\int^{\delta}_{\tau}|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\alpha_{i}p_{i}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s}}F_{i}^{p_{i}}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,

where
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:|x|=s\}
.
Since
\frac{1}{\varkappa_{i}}=\frac{1}{p_{i}}+\frac{\theta_{i}}{d-1}
, by the Sobolev embedding in sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s}
, we have

\|F_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s})}\lesssim\|\nabla F_{i}\|^{\theta_{i}}_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s})}\|F_{i}\|^{1-\theta_{i}}_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s})}+\|F\|_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s})},\ \ s\in[1,2].

Thus, by Lemma
2.3
with
\beta_{i}=\varkappa_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{\ell\in C^{1}([\tau,\delta])}\!\!\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}\!\!|\ell^{\prime}(s)|^{\alpha_{i}p_{i}}\!\left(\!\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s}}F^{p_{i}}\right){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\!\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\!\!\!\!:\ell^{\prime}(s)\leqslant 0,\ell(\tau)=1,\ell(\delta)=0\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\delta-\tau)^{-\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s}}F^{p_{i}}\right)^{\frac{\varkappa_{i}}{p_{i}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\varkappa_{i}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\delta-\tau)^{-\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\int^{\delta}_{\tau}\left(\|\nabla F_{i}\|^{\theta_{i}}_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s})}\|F\|^{1-\theta_{i}}_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s})}+\|F\|_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}({\mathbb{S}}^{d}_{s})}\right)^{\varkappa_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s\right)^{\frac{1}{\varkappa_{i}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\delta-\tau)^{-\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\|\nabla F_{i}\|^{\theta_{i}}_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}(B_{\delta})}\|F_{i}\|^{1-\theta_{i}}_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}(B_{\delta})}+\|F_{i}\|_{L^{\varkappa_{i}}(B_{\delta})}\right).

(2.11)

On the other hand, let

F^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}(x):=\left(\int_{I}(|w_{i}(t,x)|^{q_{i}}+\varepsilon){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{1/q_{i}}.

By the chain rule and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla F^{(\varepsilon)}_{i}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{I}(|w_{i}(t,x)|^{q_{i}}+\varepsilon){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{\frac{1-q_{i}}{q_{i}}}\!\!\!\!\int_{I}|w_{i}(t,x)|^{q_{i}-1}|\nabla w_{i}(t,x)|{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\int_{I}(|w_{i}(t,x)|^{q_{i}}+\varepsilon){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{\frac{1-q_{i}}{q_{i}}}\!\!\!\!\left(\int_{I}|w_{i}(t,x)|^{q_{i}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t\right)^{\frac{q_{i}-1}{q_{i}}}\!\!\!\!\|\nabla w_{i}(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{q_{i}}(I)}.

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, we obtain

|\nabla F_{i}(x)|\leqslant\|\nabla w_{i}(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{q_{i}}(I)}.

Substituting this into (
2.11
), we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

(ii) Recalling

3.2.

The following lemma is easy by Hölder’s inequality.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

We need the following simple De-Giorgi’s iteration lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of nonnegative real numbers. Suppose that for some
C_{0},\lambda>1
and
\delta_{j}>0
,
j=1,\cdots,m

If

, where
\delta=\delta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\delta_{m}
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=0.

Proof.

We use induction to prove that if
a_{1}\leqslant(mC_{0}\lambda^{(1+\delta)/\delta})^{-1/\delta}\leqslant 1
, then

a_{n}\leqslant a_{1}\lambda^{-(n-1)/\delta},\ \ \forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

By the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle a_{n+1}

\displaystyle\leqslant mC_{0}\lambda^{n}a^{1+\delta}_{n}\leqslant mC_{0}a_{1}^{1+\delta}\lambda^{n-(n-1)(1+\delta)/\delta}

\displaystyle=\big(mC_{0}a_{1}^{\delta}\lambda^{(1+\delta)/\delta}\big)a_{1}\lambda^{-n/\delta}\leqslant a_{1}\lambda^{-n/\delta},

where the last step is due to
mC_{0}a_{1}^{\delta}\lambda^{(1+\delta)/\delta}\leqslant 1
.
∎

Lemma 3.5

5.
Strong Markov selection

In this section we use Krylov’s Markov selection theorem to show the existence of a strong Markov solution under

(
\widetilde{\bf H}^{\sigma}
)
and
(
\widetilde{\bf H}^{b}
)
.
Let
\omega_{t}
be the coordinate process on the continuous function space
{\mathbb{C}}
and

{\mathcal{B}}_{t}:=\sigma\{\omega_{s}:s\leqslant t\}
the natural
\sigma
-filtration.
We first recall the following notion of local martingale solutions to SDE (
1.15
).

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(s,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. A probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
is called a local martingale solution of SDE (
1.15
)
starting from
x
at time
s
if

(i)

{\mathbb{P}}(\omega_{t}=x,t\in[0,s])=1
and for each
t>s

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\omega:\int^{t}_{s}(|b(r,\omega_{r})|+|(\sigma\sigma^{*})(r,\omega_{r})|){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s<\infty\right)=1.

(ii)

For any
f\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the process

M^{f}_{t}(\omega):=f(\omega_{t})-f(\omega_{s})-\int^{t}_{s}{\mathscr{L}}^{\sigma,b}_{r}f(\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

is a continuous local
{\mathcal{B}}_{t}
-martingale after time
s
.

The set of all the local martingale solutions of (
1.15
) is denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma,b}_{s,x}\subset{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
.

By Itô’s formula, it is easy to see that the law of a weak solution in Definition
4.1
is a local martingale solution.
Moreover, we also have the following opposite conclusion (see
[
10
, p314, Proposition 4.11]
).

Theorem 5.2
.

For any
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma,b}_{s,x}
, there is a weak solution
(\mathfrak{F},X,W)
starting from
x
at time
s
,
where
\mathfrak{F}=(\Omega,{\mathscr{F}},{\mathbf{P}};({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0})
is a stochastic basis, and so that

{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbf{P}}\circ X^{-1}.

One also needs the following notion about Krylov’s estimate (see Theorem
4.3
).

Definition 5.3
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty)
and
s\geqslant 0
. We call a probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
satisfy the Krylov estimate with indices
p,q
and starting from
s
if for any
T>s
,
there are constants
\kappa,\theta>0
such that for any
s\leqslant t_{0}<t_{1}\leqslant T

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}\left(\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}f(r,\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{0}}\right)\leqslant\kappa(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\theta}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}.

(5.1)

We shall denote by

the set of all the above

Remark 5.4

By a standard approximation, (

Now we show the following main result of this section.

(
\widetilde{\bf H}^{\sigma}

(
\widetilde{\bf H}^{b}

(C2)

-null set

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\theta}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}.

Lastly, for
I_{4}
we have

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{1}}_{\{t_{1}<\tau\}}{\mathbb{E}}^{{\mathbb{P}}}\left(\int^{t_{1}}_{t_{0}}f(r,\omega_{r}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r\Big|{\mathcal{B}}_{t_{0}}\right)\leqslant\kappa(t_{1}-t_{0})^{\theta}\|f\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{q,p}_{t,x}}.

Combining the above calculations, we obtain (
5.4
).
The proof is competed by Theorem
7.2
below.
∎

6.
Examples

For
R\geqslant 1
, let
\phi_{R}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a smooth increasing function with

\phi_{R}(r)=r,\ \ \ r\leqslant R;\ \ \phi_{R}(r)=R+1,\ \ r\geqslant 2R.

For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define

f_{R}^{(\alpha)}(r):=(\phi_{R}(r))^{\alpha},\ \ f_{R,n}^{(\alpha)}(r):=(\phi_{R}(r+\tfrac{1}{n}))^{\alpha}.

Clearly,

f^{(\alpha)}_{R}(r)=r^{\alpha}\ \mbox{ for }r<R\ \mbox{ and }\ \ f_{R,n}^{(\alpha)}(r)=(r+\tfrac{1}{n})^{\alpha}\ \mbox{ for }r+\tfrac{1}{n}<R.

Below we provide two examples to illustrate the assumption
(
\widetilde{\bf H}^{\sigma}
)
.

Example 6.1
.

Let
d\geqslant 3
and
0<\alpha<(\frac{d}{2}-1)\wedge(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d-1})
.
Let

\sigma(x)=f_{R}^{(-\frac{\alpha}{2})}(|x|^{2}){\mathbb{I}}_{d\times d}.

We verify
(
\widetilde{\bf H}^{\sigma}
)
for
\sigma_{n}(x)=f_{R,n}^{(-\frac{\alpha}{2})}(|x|^{2}){\mathbb{I}}_{d\times d}
.
Note that

a_{n}(x)=(\sigma_{n}\sigma^{*}_{n})(x)=f_{R,n}^{(-\alpha)}(|x|^{2}){\mathbb{I}}_{d\times d}.

Thus,

\lambda_{n}(x)=\mu_{n}(x)=f_{R,n}^{(-\alpha)}(|x|^{2}).

In particular, we have

\lambda^{-1}_{n}(x)\leqslant\phi^{\alpha}_{R}(|x|^{2}+1)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

and for
p_{1}<\frac{d}{2\alpha}
,

\mu_{n}(x)\leqslant\phi^{-\alpha}_{R}(|x|^{2})\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

On the other hand, by the chain rule, we have

\partial_{i}a^{ij}_{n}(x)=2x_{j}(f^{(-\alpha)}_{R,n})^{\prime}(|x|^{2})

and

\partial_{i}\partial_{j}a^{jj}_{n}(x)=\Delta f^{(-\alpha)}_{R,n}(|\cdot|^{2})(x)=2d(f^{(-\alpha)}_{R,n})^{\prime}(|x|^{2})+4|x|^{2}(f^{(-\alpha)}_{R,n})^{\prime\prime}(|x|^{2}).

Note that

(f^{(-\alpha)}_{R,n})^{\prime}(r)=-\alpha\phi_{R}(r+\tfrac{1}{n})^{-\alpha-1}\phi^{\prime}_{R}(r+\tfrac{1}{n})

and

\displaystyle(f^{(-\alpha)}_{R,n})^{\prime\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=-\alpha\phi_{R}(r+\tfrac{1}{n})^{-\alpha-1}(\phi^{\prime}_{R}(r+\tfrac{1}{n})+\phi^{\prime\prime}_{R}(r+\tfrac{1}{n}))

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha(\alpha+1)\phi_{R}(r+\tfrac{1}{n})^{-\alpha-2}(\phi^{\prime}_{R}(r+\tfrac{1}{n}))^{2}.

It is easy to see that for
p_{1}<\frac{d}{2\alpha+1}
,

|\partial_{i}a^{ij}_{n}(x)|\leqslant 2\alpha|x|^{-2\alpha-1}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{|x|^{2}\leqslant 2R\}}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

and due to
\alpha<\frac{d}{2}-1
,

\partial_{i}\partial_{j}a^{jj}_{n}(x)\leqslant C_{\alpha,R}.

Hence, (
4.1
) holds for
p_{0}=\infty
,
p_{1}\in(\frac{d-1}{2},\frac{d}{2\alpha+1})
and
p_{2}=q_{2}=\infty
.
Moreover, if
p_{4}<\frac{d}{\alpha}
, then

|\sigma_{n}(x)|\leqslant\phi^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{R}(|x|^{2})\in\widetilde{\mathbb{L}}^{p_{4}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

Thus, (
4.2
) holds for
p_{4}\in(d,\frac{d}{\alpha})
and
q_{4}=\infty
. Therefore,
(
\widetilde{\bf H}^{\sigma}
)

is satisfied for the above
\sigma_{n}(x)
.
In particular, by Theorem
4.3
, there exists at least one solution for the following singular SDE:

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=\phi_{R}(|X_{t}|^{2})^{-\alpha/2}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}+b(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\ \ X_{0}=x,

where
\alpha\in(0,(\frac{d}{2}-1)\wedge(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d-1}))
and
b\in\widetilde{L}^{p}
for some
p>\frac{d}{2}
satisfies
(\mathord{{\rm div}}b)^{-}=0
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
d\geqslant 3
,
\alpha\in(0,(\frac{d}{2}-1)\wedge(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{d-1}))
,
\beta\in(0,2\alpha)
and
\lambda\geqslant 0
.
For each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the following SDEs admits a unique strong solution:

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=|X_{t}|^{-\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}+\lambda X_{t}|X_{t}|^{-\beta-1}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\ \ X_{0}=x.

(6.1)

Proof.

Let
b(x):=\lambda x|x|^{-\beta-1}
, and for
R\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\sigma_{R}(x)=\phi_{R}(|x|^{2})^{-\alpha/2}{\mathbb{I}}
.
Since
\lambda\geqslant 0
and
\beta<2
, it is easy to see that
b\in\widetilde{L}^{p}
for any
p\in(\frac{d}{2},\frac{d}{\beta})
and
(\mathord{{\rm div}}b)^{-}\equiv 0
.
Let
X^{R}_{t}
solve the following SDE:

X^{R}_{t}=x+\int^{t}_{0}\sigma_{R}(X_{s}^{R}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}+\int^{t}_{0}b(X^{R}_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Let
\Phi:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be a smooth function with
\Phi(r)=1
for
|r|\leqslant 1

and
\Phi(r)=r
for
r>2
. By Itô’s formula, it is easy to see that

\sup_{R\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathbf{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Phi(|X^{R}_{t}|^{2})\right)\leqslant C.

From this, by Chebyshev’s inequality, we derive that

\lim_{R\to\infty}{\mathbf{P}}\left(\sup_{t\in[s,T]}|X^{R}_{t}|>R\right)=0,

which together with Theorem
5.5
implies that the assumptions of Theorem
7.5
is satisfied.
So, there exists a solution to SDE (
6.1
).
To show the pathwise uniqueness, note that

|\nabla b(x)|\leqslant C|x|^{-\beta-1},

and for any
R>0
,

\int_{B_{R}}|x|^{-(\beta+1)d}\det(\sigma\sigma^{*})^{-1}(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=\int_{B_{R}}|x|^{-(\beta+1)d+2\alpha d}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x<\infty.

Thus by
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
and the computations in Example 1 of
[
20
]
, we obtain the uniqueness.
∎

Example 6.3
.

Let
d=2
and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
. Consider the following diffusion matrix:

\sigma(x)=\left(\begin{aligned} &f^{(\frac{\alpha}{2})}_{R}(|x_{2}|^{2}),&0\\
&0,&f^{(\frac{\alpha}{2})}_{R}(|x_{1}|^{2})\\
\end{aligned}\right).

Let us define

\sigma_{n}(x):=\left(\begin{aligned} &f^{(\frac{\alpha}{2})}_{R,n}(|x_{2}|^{2}),&0\\
&0,&f^{(\frac{\alpha}{2})}_{R,n}(|x_{1}|^{2})\\
\end{aligned}\right),\ a_{n}(x):=\left(\begin{aligned} &f^{(\alpha)}_{R,n}(|x_{2}|^{2}),&0\\
&0,&f^{(\alpha)}_{R,n}(|x_{1}|^{2})\\

Then

\lambda_{n}(x)=f^{(\alpha)}_{R,n}(|x_{2}|^{2})\wedge f^{(\alpha)}_{R,n}(|x_{1}|^{2}),\quad\mu_{n}(x)=f^{(\alpha)}_{R,n}(|x_{2}|^{2})\vee f^{(\alpha)}_{R,n}(|x_{1}|^{2}).

Clearly, we have

\lambda^{-1}_{n}(x)=f^{(-\alpha)}_{R,n}(|x_{2}|^{2})\vee f^{(-\alpha)}_{R,n}(|x_{1}|^{2})

and

\partial_{i}a^{ij}_{n}(x)=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}a^{ij}_{n}(x)=0.

Thus, (
4.1
) holds for any
p_{0}\in(2,\frac{1}{2\alpha})
,
p_{1}=\infty
and
p_{2}=q_{2}=\infty
.
Moreover, it is easy to see that (
4.2
) holds for
q_{4}=\infty
and any
p_{4}\in(\frac{2p_{0}}{p_{0}-1},\infty)
.
Therefore,
(
\widetilde{\bf H}^{\sigma}
)
holds for the above
\sigma_{n}(x)
.
As in Proposition
6.2
, by Theorems
5.5
and
7.5
,
for any starting point
X_{0}=x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, there exists at least one solution for
the following two dimensional degenerate SDE:

\left\{\begin{aligned} {\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{1}_{t}=|X^{2}_{t}|^{\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{1}_{t}+b^{1}(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\\
{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{2}_{t}=|X^{1}_{t}|^{\alpha}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{2}_{t}+b^{2}(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
and
b=(b^{1},b^{2})\in\widetilde{L}^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for some
p>\frac{2}{1-4\alpha}
,
and for some
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

|b(x)|\leqslant C|x|,\ \ |x|>K.

We would like to say some words about the range of

moves to the unit ball, smaller

means stronger noise and so the drift

While, the uniqueness for the above example is left open, even for

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\tau
be a finite stopping time and
{\mathbb{C}}\ni\omega\mapsto Q_{\omega}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
be a
{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}
-measurable probability kernel.
Given a probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
, there exists a unique probability measure
{\mathbb{P}}\otimes_{\tau}Q\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
so that

({\mathbb{P}}\otimes_{\tau}Q)|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}}={\mathbb{P}}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}},\ \ ({\mathbb{P}}\otimes_{\tau}Q)(\cdot|{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau})(\omega)=Q_{\omega}(\cdot).

In particular,

({\mathbb{P}}\otimes_{\tau}Q)(\Gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}Q_{\omega}(\Gamma){\mathbb{P}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega),\ \ \forall\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}:=\vee_{t\geqslant 0}{\mathcal{B}}_{t}.

For each
(s,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, let
{\mathscr{C}}(s,x)
be a non-empty convex subset of
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
with

{\mathbb{P}}\{\omega:\omega_{s}=x\}=1.

We suppose that
\{{\mathscr{C}}(s,x):(s,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}
satisfies

(C1)

Let
(s_{n},x_{n})
converge to
(s,x)
. For any sequence
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\in{\mathscr{C}}(s_{n},x_{n})
, there is a subsequence
n_{k}
and
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathscr{C}}(s,x)
so that

{\mathbb{P}}_{n_{k}}
converges to
{\mathbb{P}}
.

(C2)

(Disintegration) Let
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathscr{C}}(s,x)
and
\tau\geqslant s
be a finite stopping time. For any r.c.p.d.
({\mathbb{P}}_{\omega})_{\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}}

of
{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(\cdot|{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau})
, there is a
{\mathbb{P}}
-null set
N\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}
so that

{\mathbb{P}}_{\omega}\in{\mathscr{C}}(\tau(\omega),\omega_{\tau(\omega)}),\ \omega\notin N.

(C3)

(Reconstruction) Let
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathscr{C}}(s,x)
and
\tau\geqslant s
be a finite stopping time. For any
{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}
-measurable kernel

{\mathbb{C}}\ni\omega\mapsto Q_{\omega}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
with

Q_{\omega}\in{\mathscr{C}}(\tau(\omega),\omega_{\tau(\omega)}),\forall\omega\in{\mathbb{C}},

it holds that

{\mathbb{P}}\otimes_{\tau}Q\in{\mathscr{C}}(s,x).

We have the following strong Markov selection theorem, whose proofs are completely the same as in
[
18
, Theorem 12.2.3]

(see also
[
6
]
and
[
7
, Theorem 2.7]
). We omit the details.

Theorem 7.2
.

Under
(C1)
,
(C2)
and
(C3)
, there is a measurable selection

{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\ni(s,x)\mapsto{\mathbb{P}}_{s,x}\in{\mathscr{C}}(s,x)

so that for any

(s,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and finite stopping time
\tau\geqslant s
,

\omega\mapsto{\mathbb{P}}_{\tau(\omega),x(\tau(\omega),\omega)}
is a r.c.p.d. of
{\mathbb{P}}_{s,x}
with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}
.
More precisely, there is a
{\mathbb{P}}_{s,x}
-null set
N\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}

such that for all
\omega\notin N
,

{\mathbb{P}}_{s,x}(\cdot|{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau})(\omega)={\mathbb{P}}_{\tau(\omega),\omega_{\tau(\omega)}}(\cdot).

The following two simple lemmas are used in the proof of Theorem
5.5
(see
[
7
]
).

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{G}}\subset{\mathscr{C}}
be two countably generated sub
\sigma
-algebras of
{\mathcal{B}}
. Given
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
,
let
Q_{\omega}
be a r.c.p.d. of
{\mathbb{P}}

with respect to
{\mathscr{G}}
. Then there is a
{\mathbb{P}}
-null set
N\in{\mathscr{G}}
depending on
{\mathscr{C}}
and
\xi
such that for all
\omega\notin N
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}})={\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}}),\ \ Q_{\omega}-a.s.

Proof.

Let
A\in{\mathscr{G}}
and
B\in{\mathscr{C}}
. By definition, we have

\displaystyle\int_{A}{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(1_{B}{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}})){\mathbb{P}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(1_{B}{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}})|{\mathscr{G}})(\omega){\mathbb{P}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(1_{A}1_{B}\xi)=\int_{A}{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(1_{B}\xi){\mathbb{P}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(1_{B}{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}})){\mathbb{P}}({\mathord{{\rm d}}}\omega).

Hence, for each
B\in{\mathscr{C}}
, there is a
{\mathbb{P}}
-null set
N_{B}\in{\mathscr{G}}
so that for all
\omega\notin N
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(1_{B}{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}}))={\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(1_{B}{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}})).

Since
{\mathscr{C}}
is countably generated, one can find a common null set
N_{\xi,{\mathscr{C}}}
so that for all
\omega\notin N
and
B\in{\mathscr{C}}
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(1_{B}{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}}))={\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(1_{B}{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(\xi|{\mathscr{C}})),

which in turn yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\tau
be a finite stopping time and
Q_{\omega}
be a r.c.p.d. of
{\mathbb{P}}
with respect to
{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}
.
Let
X_{t}
be a bounded continuous process. Suppose that for any
t\geqslant 0
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t}|{\mathcal{B}}_{t})\leqslant A,\ \ {\mathbb{P}}-a.s.

Then there is a
{\mathbb{P}}
-null set
N\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}
such that for all
\omega\notin N
and
t\geqslant\tau(\omega)
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(X_{t}|{\mathcal{B}}_{t})\leqslant A,\ \ Q_{\omega}-a.s.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.4
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}^{\mathbb{P}}(X_{t\vee\tau}|{\mathcal{B}}_{t\vee\tau})\leqslant A,\ \ t\geqslant 0.

By Lemma
7.3
, there is a
{\mathbb{P}}
-null set
N
such that for all
\omega\notin N
and all rational number
t>0
,

{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(X_{t\vee\tau}|{\mathcal{B}}_{t\vee\tau})\leqslant A,\ \ Q_{\omega}-a.s.

For fixed
\omega\notin N
, since (cf.
[
18
, p34. (3.15)]
)

Q_{\omega}\{\omega^{\prime}:\tau(\omega^{\prime})=\tau(\omega)\}=1,

we have for all rational number
t\geqslant\tau(\omega)

{\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(X_{t}|{\mathcal{B}}_{t})={\mathbb{E}}^{Q_{\omega}}(X_{t\vee\tau}|{\mathcal{B}}_{t\vee\tau})\leqslant A,\ \ Q_{\omega}-a.s.

Now for general

The following result provides a way of constructing a global solution from local solutions.

Theorem 7.5
.

Suppose that for each
R\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
(s,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
there is at least one local martingale solution
{\mathbb{P}}^{R}_{s,x}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma_{R},b_{R}}_{s,x}
so that
(s,x)\mapsto{\mathbb{P}}^{R}_{s,x}
is Borel measurable,
where

\sigma_{R}(t,x):=\sigma(t,\chi_{R}(x)x),\ \ b_{R}(t,x):=b(t,\chi_{R}(x)x),

and

\chi_{R}(x)=1,\ |x|\leqslant 2^{R-1},\ \ \chi_{R}(x)=0,\ |x|>2^{R}.

Fix
(s_{0},x_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
If for each
T>s_{0}
and any choice of

{\mathbb{P}}^{R}_{s_{0},x_{0}}
from
{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma_{R},b_{R}}_{s_{0},x_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}^{R}_{s_{0},x_{0}}\left(\sup_{t\in[s,T]}|\omega_{t}|>R\right)=0,

(7.1)

then there is at least one local martingale solution
{\mathbb{P}}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma,b}_{s_{0},x_{0}}
.
In particular, there is a global weak solution
(\mathfrak{F},X,W)
for SDE (
1.15
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
(s_{0},x_{0})=(0,0)
.
Let
\tau_{0}=0
. We define a sequence of stopping times recursively by

\tau_{n}:=\inf\{t>\tau_{n-1}:|\omega_{t}|>2^{n-1}\}=\inf\{t>0:|\omega_{t}|>2^{n-1}\},\ \ n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Let
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{s,x}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma_{n},b_{n}}_{s,x}
be as in the assumptions.
Define for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

Q^{n}_{\omega}:={\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}_{\tau_{n}(\omega),\omega_{\tau_{n}(\omega)}},\ \omega\in{\mathbb{C}}.

Since
(s,x)\mapsto{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}_{s,x}
is measurable,

\omega\mapsto Q^{n}_{\omega}
is a
{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau_{n}}
-measurable probability kernel on
{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathcal{B}}
,
i.e., for each
\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}
,
\omega\mapsto Q^{n}_{\omega}(\Gamma)
is
{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau_{n}}
-measurable, and for each
\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
Q_{\omega}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma_{1},b_{1}}_{0,0}
. Define for
n\geqslant 2
,

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n+1}:=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{1}\otimes_{\tau_{1}}{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}\otimes_{\tau_{2}}\cdots\otimes_{\tau_{n}}{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}.

By the construction and Lemma
7.1
, one sees that

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n+1}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau_{n}}}=(\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\otimes_{\tau_{n}}{\mathbb{Q}}^{n})|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau_{n}}}=\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau_{n}}},

and by

18

\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\sigma_{n},b_{n}}_{0,0}.

Moreover, by (
7.1

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{n}(\tau_{n}<T)=0.

Finally, by

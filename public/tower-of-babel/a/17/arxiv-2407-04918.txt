You shall not pass! - explicit diffeomorphism violation “no-go” constraints and discontinuities
Quentin G. Bailey, Kellie O’Neal-Ault, Nils A. Nilsson

Abstract
This paper collects several results in the study of the explicit symmetry-breaking limit of the effective-field theory (EFT) description of diffeomorphism and local Lorentz-symmetry breaking, where we generalize a subset of the EFT framework (the “minimal” sector). It is well known that no-go constraints may arise in cases of explicit symmetry-breaking in curved spacetime as a consequence of the Bianchi identities; we show in this work that certain terms in the action can be countenanced and used to cancel would-be no-go constraints, at least in the linearized gravity limit. Nonetheless, we go on to find more potential issues, and we show that one particular explicit breaking subset of the EFT, while evading direct no-go constraints, results in a discontinuity - unsuppressed additional polarizations for gravitational waves. In a general treatment of the explicit breaking EFT, but confined to linearized gravity, we explicitly show the existence of an extra degree of freedom, independent of coordinates. We find extra polarizations of gravitational waves in the solutions, with a scalar mode unsuppressed by any coefficient, which could render these cases ruled out by observations.

The notion that the spacetime symmetries underlying General Relativity (GR) and the Standard Model (SM) might not be exact at high energy scales has been considered as a possible signature of new physics for decades

It is generally expected that GR and the SM are not the fundamental descriptions of nature,

[
11

.
This point of view is motivated by the expectation of a single unified theory encompassing all fundamental interactions, which includes an as yet unknown quantum theory of gravity of which GR is the low-energy limit.
To experimentally probe these ideas,
one posits higher-order corrections in the form of spacetime-symmetry breaking operators (such as local Lorentz symmetry and diffeomorphism symmetry) appearing as correction terms in the low-energy EFT

The search for such signals has generated a large number of stringent constraints across a vast number of experiments and observations

The origin of any hypothetical breaking of local Lorentz and diffeomorphism symmetry can affect its phenomenological signatures;
generally,
broken diffeomorphism and/or broken Lorentz symmetry is signaled by the presence of one or more background tensor fields (
t_{\mu\nu...}
) in spacetime that couple to existing matter or fields.
Using this notion,
a theory agnostic general EFT framework for tests of these symmetries has been in use for decades
[

,
14
,
15
]

In this framework,

Exceptions occur when curved spacetime is involved:

to solve field equations and calculate observables,
one generally has to specify whether the symmetry breaking is explicit (
a priori
given backgrounds

) or spontaneous,
where in the latter case the backgrounds are vacuum expectation values of dynamical fields (
t_{\mu\nu\ldots}\to\langle t\rangle_{\mu\nu\ldots}
);
attention has been given to both cases in the literature.
It was shown in Ref.
[

]

More recently,

,
19

Alternatively, the geometry of spacetime may not even be Riemannian at all

On the other hand,

In this paper,
we will revisit a relatively simple subset of the EFT in the explicit breaking limit,
partly as an extension of the Hamiltonian results in Ref.

We show that certain combinations of traced coefficient terms in the action can be chosen to cancel some no-go terms in the constraint equations resulting from the geometric Bianchi identities.
Even so,
we go to show that other problems arise, such as the lack of a continuous limit to GR (discontinuities), by studying a special case that is ultimately ruled out by experiment.
In addition we perform a detailed study of the linearized gravity limit with explicit-symmetry breaking terms,
detailing the degrees of freedom occurring in this case,
and pointing out where discontinuities arise,

The paper is organized as follows: In Section

In Section

Finally, in Section

II
The minimal gravitational EFT with trace corrections

Symmetry-breaking terms in the EFT gravitational action are constructed
with coefficients
k_{\alpha\beta...}
contracted with the Riemann curvature
R_{\kappa\lambda\mu\nu}
and covariant derivative
terms
\nabla_{\epsilon}...R_{\kappa\lambda\mu\nu}
with increasing mass dimension
d
.
The general EFT action for the gravity sector for arbitrary mass dimension terms has been discussed elsewhere

[
15
,
42
,
44
,
45
]
.
Here we focus on the
d=4
case; however, unlike previous treatments,
we allow also for independent terms with various traces of coefficients that involve the spacetime metric
g_{\mu\nu}
.
In certain cases these terms can contribute differently to the equations of motion;
for instance,
this is clear if one compares the field equations for the Lagrange density
{\cal L}\sim s_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}
,
with symmetric two-tensor coefficients
s_{\mu\nu}
,
to a Lagrange density term
{\cal L}\sim k^{\alpha}_{~\mu\alpha\nu}R^{\mu\nu}
,
with contracted coefficients
k^{\alpha}_{~\mu\alpha\nu}
.
The presence of the metric in the latter,
due to the contraction,
affects the variation with respect to
g_{\mu\nu}
,
spoiling the naive tactic of absorbing
k^{\alpha}_{~\mu\alpha\nu}
into
s_{\mu\nu}
.

Thus,
considering couplings to Riemann curvature only,
we have,

S=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R\left(1-e_{1}u+e_{2}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}+e_{3}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}\right)+R^{\mu\nu}\left(e_{4}s_{\mu\nu}+e_{5}t_{\mu~\nu\alpha}^{~\alpha}\right)+e_{6}t_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}R^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\right]+S_{M},

(1)

where
\kappa=8\pi G_{N}
,

R^{\mu\nu}
is the Ricci tensor and
R
is the curvature scalar.
Also,

e_{1\ldots 6}
are constants,
and the subset containing
e_{1}
,
e_{4}
, and
e_{6}
makes up what is usually known as the “minimal” subset of the gravity EFT
[
15
]
,
where we have added the trace corrections
e_{2}
,
e_{3}
, and
e_{5}
,
which generalizes the minimal EFT sector.
Note that
s_{\mu\nu}
and
t_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
are not assumed traceless here.
Since we are considering explicit breaking,
we do not include dynamical terms in the action for the coefficients
[
46
]
.
We also neglect any mass-type terms for the gravitational field,
such as those considered in massive gravity models
[
47
,
48
]
or such terms in the EFT framework
[
49
]
.
The case where only
e_{1}\neq 0
and/or
e_{4}\neq 0
with explicit diffeomorphism violation has been studied with the Dirac-Hamiltonian method in references
[
18
,
19
,
50
]
;
we complement this work with a Lagrangian-based analysis.

Note here that explicit diffeomorphism violation can be seen under the “particle” (infinitesimal) diffeomorphism transformation where the coefficients
u
,
s_{\mu\nu}
, and
t_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
do not transform (
s_{\mu\nu}\overset{\rm part}{\longrightarrow}s_{\mu\nu}
) while the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
transforms as a two-tensor (
g_{\mu\nu}\overset{\rm part}{\longrightarrow}g_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g_{\mu\nu}
)
[
51
]
; conversely,
under a general coordinate transformation both
s_{\mu\nu}
and
g_{\mu\nu}
transform as two-tensors and the action (
1
) remains coordinate covariant.

The field equations are obtained from the action with a variation with respect to
g_{\mu\nu}

[
50
]
,
while holding fixed all the indexed coefficients in the “downstairs”,
co-vector position (
s_{\mu\nu},~t_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
).
This is a particular choice of background,
as physical results can differ for other choices
[
45
]
.
Note in particular,

\displaystyle s^{\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}\mu}

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\nu}s_{\mu\nu},

(2)

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}_{~\mu\alpha\nu}

\displaystyle=g^{\alpha\beta}t_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu},

so that
s_{\mu\nu}
and
t_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}
(in the lowered index position) are held fixed for the variation.
This implies that the coupling with
s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}

in (
1
) is not merely a scalar field,
like in scalar-tensor models.
We obtain from this variation,

-G^{\mu\nu}+\sum_{n=1}^{6}e_{n}E_{(n)}^{\mu\nu}+\kappa T_{M}^{\mu\nu}=0,

(3)

where the tensors
E_{(n)}^{\mu\nu}
are defined as

\displaystyle E_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=G^{\mu\nu}u+\left(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{2}-\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\right)u

(4)

\displaystyle E_{(2)}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-Rs^{\mu\nu}-G^{\mu\nu}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}-\left(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{2}-\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\right)s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}

\displaystyle E_{(3)}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-2Rt^{\mu\alpha\nu}_{~~~~\alpha}-G^{\mu\nu}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}-\left(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{2}-\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\right)t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}.

The tensors
E_{(4)\ldots(6)}
are lengthy and we display them in Appendix
A
.
In the absence of any symmetry breaking the
E_{(n)}^{\mu\nu}
’s all vanish.

One place where one runs into challenges in the explicit-symmetry breaking scenario is via the conservation laws implied by the covariant divergence of the field equations (
3
).
These must be satisfied in order for the equations to be consistent.
By taking into account geometric identities,
that is,
the traced Bianchi identities
\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}=0
,
and
assuming conservation of the matter energy-momentum tensor

\nabla_{\mu}T_{M}^{\mu\nu}=0
,
we find the covariant divergence of Eq. (
3
) to be

\sum_{n=1}^{6}e_{n}\nabla_{\mu}E_{(n)}^{\mu\nu}=0,

(5)

where

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}E_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}R\nabla^{\nu}u

(6)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}E_{(2)}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}R\nabla^{\nu}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}-\nabla_{\mu}\left(s^{\mu\nu}R\right)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}E_{(3)}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}R\nabla^{\nu}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~~\alpha\beta}-2\nabla_{\mu}\left(t^{\mu\alpha\beta}_{~~~~\alpha}R\right),

and the rest are displayed in Appendix
A
. The Bianchi identities place severe constraints on explicit-breaking models, but previous work has shown that they can be evaded in certain scenarios; see for example
[
52
,
53
]
, and more recently
[
54
]
.

The primary feature we emphasize here is that various terms that show up in Eq. (
5
) for each coupling
e_{n}
,
starting from
e_{1}
,
can in principle be cancelled by successive
e_{n+1}
terms.
For example,
consider from Eq. (
65
) the
e_{4}
contribution

\nabla_{\mu}E_{(4)}^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}R^{\alpha\beta}\nabla^{\nu}s_{\alpha\beta}-\tfrac{1}{2}s^{\alpha\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}R-R^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\beta}s^{\nu}_{~\alpha},
.
All three of these terms can be canceled by corresponding terms in

\nabla_{\mu}E_{(5)}^{\mu\nu}=-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{\alpha\beta\nu}{}_{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}R+\tfrac{1}{2}R^{\alpha\beta}\nabla^{\nu}t_{\alpha\gamma\beta}{}^{\gamma}-R^{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}t_{\beta\gamma}{}^{\nu\gamma}+...
,
by choosing
e_{5}t_{\mu\alpha\nu}^{~~~~~\alpha}=-e_{4}s_{\mu\nu}
.
This does not eliminate all the terms in
\nabla_{\alpha}E_{(5)}^{\alpha\beta}
,
however. One might be tempted to use this constraint to reduce the equations, but one would then ruin the notion of explicit breaking. As a result, variations of
s_{\mu\nu}
with respect to the inverse metric would not give zero, and
s_{\mu\nu}
would no longer be unaffected by particle diffeomorphisms.
Morever,
this substitution at the level of the action would change the field equations;
additionally the metric inverse is
g^{\mu\nu}
shows up and thus this relation equates dynamical variables to prescribed, nondynamical background coefficients.
Henceforth in this paper we set
e_{5}=0=e_{6}
for simplicity;
nonetheless, some interesting features arise from this sort of balance as we show below.

We consider now the special case of Eq. (
1
) with a two tensor
s_{\mu\nu}
as well as the scalar
u
and the saturated trace
t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}
.
The field equations from Eq. (
3
) in this case are

\displaystyle-G^{\mu\nu}+e_{1}\Big[G^{\mu\nu}u-\nabla^{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}u+g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha}u\Big]+e_{2}\Big[-G^{\mu\nu}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}-Rs^{\mu\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\beta}\nabla^{\beta}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}+\nabla^{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}\Big]

(7)

\displaystyle+e_{3}\Big[\nabla^{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}-2Rt^{\mu\alpha\nu}_{~~~~\alpha}-G^{\mu\nu}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}-g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\lambda}\nabla^{\lambda}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}\Big]+e_{4}\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}G^{\alpha\beta}s_{\alpha\beta}+\tfrac{1}{4}g^{\mu\nu}Rs^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}-2G^{(\mu\alpha}s^{\nu)}_{~\alpha}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{\alpha}s^{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla^{(\mu}s^{\nu)\alpha}-\tfrac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}s^{\alpha\beta}-Rs^{\mu\nu}\Big]+\kappa T^{\mu\nu}=0

where
G^{\mu\nu}=R^{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}R
is the Einstein tensor.
Note that a significant number of the terms are tensorially similar.
From Eq. (
5
),
the constraints implied by the traced Bianchi identities yield

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}e_{1}R\nabla^{\nu}u+e_{2}\Big[-R\nabla_{\mu}s^{\mu\nu}-s^{\nu}_{~\mu}\nabla^{\mu}R+\tfrac{1}{2}R\nabla^{\nu}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}\Big]+e_{3}\Big[2R\nabla_{\alpha}t^{\nu\beta~\alpha}_{~~\beta}+\tfrac{1}{2}R\nabla^{\mu}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}-2t^{\nu\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}\nabla^{\alpha}R\Big]

(8)

\displaystyle+e_{4}\Big[-G^{\mu\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}s^{\nu}_{~\mu}-\tfrac{1}{2}R\nabla_{\mu}s^{\mu\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}s^{\nu}_{~\mu}\nabla^{\mu}R+\tfrac{1}{2}G^{\mu\alpha}\nabla^{\nu}s_{\mu\alpha}+\tfrac{1}{4}R\nabla^{\nu}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}\Big]+\kappa\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0.

These four equations must be satisfied by consistency;
if they are not satisfied,
this limit of the framework is deemed no-go due to the clash with Riemannian geometry.
Assuming that the matter stress energy tensor is separately conserved,
and using the similarity of many of the terms,
these four equations can be re-arranged as

\displaystyle-R[\lambda_{1}\nabla_{\mu}s^{\mu\nu}+2e_{3}\nabla_{\mu}t^{\mu\alpha\nu}_{~~~~\alpha}]-[\lambda_{1}s^{\nu\mu}+2e_{3}t^{\nu\alpha\mu}_{~~~~\alpha}]\nabla_{\mu}R+\tfrac{1}{2}R\nabla^{\nu}[-e_{1}u+\lambda_{1}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}+e_{3}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}]

(9)

\displaystyle-e_{4}\tfrac{1}{2}g^{\nu\rho}G^{\mu\alpha}\Big(\nabla_{\mu}s_{\rho\alpha}+\nabla_{\alpha}s_{\rho\mu}-\nabla_{\rho}s_{\mu\alpha}\Big)=0,

where
\lambda_{1}=e_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}e_{4}
.
A striking result of (
9
) is that one can actually propose relations among the constants
e_{i}
, and
the background coefficients,
that satisfy this constraint,
which can be thought of as a kind of “detailed balance”.
Different choices of coefficient values represent distinct models, for example:

(
i
)

e_{4}=0
,

\lambda_{1}s^{\mu\nu}=-2e_{3}t^{\mu\alpha\nu}_{~~~~\alpha}
, and
e_{1}u=\lambda_{1}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}+e_{3}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~\alpha\beta}
; the field equations reduce to GR, but produces non-viable constraints on the dynamical metric.

(
ii
)

e_{4}=0
;
a subset of the constraints are eliminated (we explore this case in the next section).

(
iii
)

e_{1}=0=e_{3}
,

\lambda_{1}=e_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}e_{4}=0
;
the linearized limit satisfies the constraints.

The choice (
iii
) shows that it is possible for the effects of Lorentz violation to evade linearized no-go constraints in this limit,
providing an exception to those posited for generic explicit breaking terms in
[
55
,
45
]
.

As a warm-up example, we choose the subcase (iii) when
e_{1}=e_{3}=0
, take the constraint equations in Eq. (
9
) and impose the detailed-balance condition
e_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}e_{4}=0
.
We find,

g^{\nu\rho}G^{\mu\alpha}\left(\nabla_{\mu}s_{\rho\alpha}+\nabla_{\alpha}s_{\rho\mu}-\nabla_{\rho}s_{\mu\alpha}\right)=0.

(10)

These four equations represent local constraints on a nonlinear combination of the background and the curvature.
We do not attempt to solve these constraints in generality here but we consider that one might use them to impose constraints on the background
s_{\mu\nu}
itself; we grant that this is a philosophical departure from the concept of an
a priori
independent background
[
18
,
19
]
.
Thus, the constraints could be solved if we have

\nabla_{\mu}s_{\rho\alpha}+\nabla_{\alpha}s_{\rho\mu}-\nabla_{\rho}s_{\mu\alpha}=0,

(11)

from which we find

\nabla_{\mu}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}=0
. Therefore, in the subcase (
iii
), the trace of the coefficients
s_{\mu\nu}
must be constant in the chosen coordinates in order for the traced Bianchi identities to be satisfied.
We also notice that
R=0
(without assuming (
iii
)) reduces Eq. (
9
) to

e_{4}\tfrac{1}{2}g^{\nu\rho}R^{\mu\alpha}\Big(\nabla_{\mu}s_{\rho\alpha}+\nabla_{\alpha}s_{\rho\mu}-\nabla_{\rho}s_{\mu\alpha}\Big)=0,

(12)

which is solved by the same trace constraint on
s_{\mu\nu}
as in the previous example. Although we find the same covariant constraint equation for
s_{\mu\nu}
, the physical situation is entirely different, as we are now restricted to Ricci flat metrics.

III
Consequences of the trace-type terms in the action

In this section we consider a subset of the action (
1
),
and study its consequences in the explicit symmetry-breaking limit.
Allowing
e_{1}-e_{3}

to be nonzero,
as in the special case
(
ii
) in the previous section,
the field equations are

-G^{\mu\nu}(1+\Phi)-(e_{2}s^{\mu\nu}+2e_{3}t^{\mu\alpha\nu}_{~~~~\alpha})R-(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{2}-\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu})\Phi+8\pi G_{N}T^{\mu\nu}=0,

(13)

where we introduce the scalar combination

\Phi=-e_{1}u+e_{2}s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}+e_{3}t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~~\alpha\beta}
.
The constraint equations (
9
)
become

-\nabla_{\mu}[(e_{2}s^{\mu\nu}+2e_{3}t^{\mu\alpha\nu}_{~~~~\alpha})R]+\frac{1}{2}R\nabla^{\nu}\Phi=0.

(14)

This can be viewed as four constraints on the four would-be gauge degrees of freedom in this broken diffeomorphism action
[
51
]
.
However,
in order for this action to have any hope of matching observations in the weak-field gravity limit,
we must satisfy the weak-field limit of this constraint,

-(e_{2}s_{\mu\nu}+2e_{3}t_{\mu\alpha\nu}^{~~~~\alpha})\partial^{\mu}R_{L}=0,

(15)

where we assume an asymptotically flat spacetime in which
s_{\mu\nu}
tends to a constant background,
and
R_{L}
is the linearized Ricci tensor.
In this context of linearized gravity any would-be diffeomorphism gauge degrees of freedom vanish from the Ricci scalar
[
51
]
,
similar to other scenarios
[
28
]
.
The only plausible way to satisfy (
15
) and be consistent with perturbative constraints in the weak-field limit
[
45
]
,
is to “solve” the constraint with
the condition
R=0
.
Thus we get a scalar constraint equation
on the dynamical variables

R=0,

(16)

which holds now for the full curvature (not linearized).
The field equations then become,

-G^{\mu\nu}(1+\Phi)-(g^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{2}-\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu})\Phi+8\pi G_{N}T^{\mu\nu}=0.

(17)

Tracing the field equation (
17
) yields an equation:

\boxed{-3\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\Phi+8\pi G_{N}T^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\alpha}=0.}

(18)

This gives a wave equation for the trace

\Phi
.
Since there is no other constraint on
\Phi
,
this represents an extra degree of freedom
in the model.
Note that in general
\Phi
is not merely composed of the background
a priori
coefficients but involves components of the metric via the traces like (
2
);
thus the extra degree of freedom lies in the spacetime metric.
We examine aspects of this case in the subsections to follow.
Note that the propagation of the extra mode appears of the standard form,
so it does not appear to be a ghost mode from this perspective; we discuss this further in Section
III.2
.

III.1
Match to scalar-tensor models

The action of a general scalar-tensor theory can be written as

S=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\phi R-\frac{\omega(\phi)}{\phi}\nabla^{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\mu}\phi-V(\phi)\right)+S_{M},

(19)

where
S_{M}
is an arbitrary matter action

[
56
]
.
By setting the coupling
\omega(\phi)
to a constant and the potential
V(\phi)=0
, one obtains
the original Brans-Dicke theory
[
57
]
.
The field equations (
17
) can be directly mapped to the field equations of scalar-tensor theory as we show below.
For a general scalar-tensor model with no potential term
V(\phi)=0
,
the field equations can be written
[
58
]
as

\displaystyle G^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi G_{N}}{\phi}(T_{M})^{\mu\nu}+\frac{\omega}{\phi^{2}}\left(\nabla^{\mu}\phi\nabla^{\nu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}(\nabla\phi)^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{\phi}\left(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi-g^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{2}\phi\right),

(20)

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi G_{N}}{3+2\omega}(T_{M})^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\alpha},

where
\omega
is a free parameter such that the theory reduces to GR when
\omega\rightarrow\infty
.

First we observe that comparing the first of equations (
20
)
with Eq. (
17
),
the term proportional to
\omega
is absent in Eq. (
17
).
Therefore we must take
\omega=0
.
Manipulation of Eq. (
20
) then yields

\phi G^{\mu\nu}-8\pi G_{N}(T_{M})^{\mu\nu}-\left(\nabla^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}\phi-g^{\mu\nu}\nabla^{2}\phi\right)=0.

(21)

If we posit
\phi=1+\Phi
,
then (
21
) matches (
17
).
Also, in this limit of

In summary,

III.2
Solution for the curvature in the wave zone

We next explore the gravitational wave scenario in the weak field limit.
First note that the observables for the typical gravitational-wave measurement scenario are the six components of the Riemann tensor
R_{0j0k}
in locally freely falling coordinates
[
56
]
.
Specializing to small fluctuations
h_{\mu\nu}
around approximately flat spacetime
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}
,
the following identity holds:

\Box R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\partial_{\beta\delta}R_{\alpha\gamma}-\partial_{\beta\gamma}R_{\alpha\delta}+\partial_{\alpha\gamma}R_{\beta\delta}-\partial_{\alpha\delta}R_{\beta\gamma},

(22)

where
\Box=\delta^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}-\partial_{0}^{2}
.
With (
16
) the Ricci scalar vanishes,
and so we may substitute
R_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow G_{\mu\nu}
in (
22

From Eq. (
17

we have

G_{\mu\nu}=\lambda(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{2})\Phi+8\pi G_{N}\lambda T^{\mu\nu},

(23)

where
\lambda=1/(1+\Phi)
.
In the weak-field gravity limit,
since any fluctuations in
\Phi
are governed by (
18
) and sourced by matter stress-energy, we can (if we also ignore any homogeneous solutions) expect them to be of the same approximate size as the fluctuations
h_{\mu\nu}
in a weak-field expansion.
So we can take the
\lambda
multiplying terms in (
23

\bar{\lambda}=1/(1+{\overline{\Phi}})
,
where
\overline{\Phi}
is a constant,
zeroth order trace.
Thus we obtain the weak-field approximate equation

G_{\mu\nu}=\bar{\lambda}(\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}\Box)\Phi+8\pi G_{N}\bar{\lambda}T_{\mu\nu},

(24)

where the curvature tensor is linearized.
It is interesting to note here that the contribution to the energy density
T_{00}
on the right-hand side is
\bar{\lambda}\nabla^{2}\Phi
,
which does not appear to be bounded from below.
Nonetheless we continue our investigation to solve for the (measureable) curvature.

When inserting the expression (
24

22

s^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\alpha}

22

This leaves the field equations as

\Box R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=8\pi G_{N}\bar{\lambda}(\partial_{\beta\delta}T_{\alpha\gamma}-\partial_{\beta\gamma}T_{\alpha\delta}+\partial_{\alpha\gamma}T_{\beta\delta}-\partial_{\alpha\delta}T_{\beta\gamma})-\bar{\lambda}(\partial_{\beta\delta}\eta_{\alpha\gamma}-\partial_{\beta\gamma}\eta_{\alpha\delta}+\partial_{\alpha\gamma}\eta_{\beta\delta}-\partial_{\alpha\delta}\eta_{\beta\gamma})\Box\Phi.

(25)

Now it is clear we can use the wave equation for
\Phi

(Eq. (
18

to express the right-hand side entirely in terms of the stress-energy tensor,
whereupon we arrive at a wave equation for the curvature itself sourced by derivatives of the stress-energy tensor.
We are left with

\Box R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=8\pi G^{\prime}_{N}(\partial_{\beta\delta}{\tilde{T}}_{\alpha\gamma}-\partial_{\beta\gamma}{\tilde{T}}_{\alpha\delta}+\partial_{\alpha\gamma}{\tilde{T}}_{\beta\delta}-\partial_{\alpha\delta}{\tilde{T}}_{\beta\gamma}),

(26)

{\tilde{T}}^{\mu\nu}=T^{\mu\nu}-(1/3)\eta^{\mu\nu}T^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\alpha}

G^{\prime}_{N}=\bar{\lambda}G_{N}

Note that in standard GR,
at this stage of the analysis,
instead of the
1/3

{\tilde{T}}^{\mu\nu}

there is a
1/2

This indicates a critical difference with GR,
which appears to be unsuppressed by any small coefficient or parameter.
We exploit the resemblance of the result in (

namely
0=(1/2)(\eta_{\mu\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu})T^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\alpha}

Rearranging,
we obtain the GR result plus a correction.
Thus we have

\Box R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=(\Box R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta})_{GR}+\frac{4}{3}\pi G^{\prime}_{N}(\eta_{\alpha\gamma}\partial_{\beta\delta}-\eta_{\alpha\delta}\partial_{\beta\gamma}+\eta_{\beta\delta}\partial_{\alpha\gamma}-\eta_{\beta\gamma}\partial_{\alpha\delta})T^{\mu}_{\phantom{\mu}\mu},

(27)

where we note that
G_{N}^{\prime}
is used also in the GR piece.
We specialize now to the six measurable components
R_{0j0k}
in the gravitational wave measurements scenario.
This reduces Eq. (

\Box R_{0j0k}=(\Box R_{0j0k})_{GR}+\frac{4}{3}\pi G^{\prime}_{N}(-\partial_{jk}+\delta_{jk}\partial_{0}^{2})(T^{kk}-T^{00}).

(28)

At this stage we will use the standard retarded wave solution,
and the result can be written as

R_{0j0k}=(R_{0j0k})_{GR}+\frac{1}{3}G^{\prime}_{N}(\partial_{jk}-\delta_{jk}\partial_{0}^{2})\int d^{3}r^{\prime}\frac{[T^{kk}-T^{00}](t-R,\vec{r}^{\prime})}{R}.

(29)

Next we expand the solution for the wave zone assuming the source
T^{\mu\nu}
has compact support.
This computation makes use of known methods described in detail elsewhere
[
62
,
43
,
63
]
.
In the “far away” wave zone
we seek only the
1/r
contributions.
The leading multipole terms can be manipulated to the form,

\boxed{R_{0j0k}=-\frac{G_{N}^{\prime}}{r}\partial_{t}^{4}(I_{jk})_{TT}+\frac{G^{\prime}_{N}}{6r}(\delta_{jk}-n_{j}n_{k})\partial_{t}^{4}(n_{l}n_{m}I^{lm}-I^{ll}),}

(30)

where the time dependence is
\tau=t-r

and

1
1

1

We do not expect that the scaling of

is observable.

The first term in Eq. (

with two degrees of freedom
and the usual two polarizations.
The second term is transverse in the far away wave zone,
but has an overall term proportional to the projection of the inertia tensor
I^{ij}
that is perpendicular to

and is hence a
breathing mode

[

,

]

The latter represents an extra propagating degree of freedom,
arising from the explicit symmetry breaking term in the action.

A peculiar thing about this result is that the strength of the extra term is of order unity relative to the GR term, and there is no coefficient or parameter multiplying the extra term that could be constrained.
This result therefore shows a “discontinuity” in the solutions to this particular subset of the framework (
13

since there is no continuous limit that matches GR.
The same thing happens in the well-known massive gravity vDVZ discontinuity
[
65
]
.
Instead,
the lack of observation of a scalar (trace) mode in the observed strain in GW detectors could be used to rule out this model,
a subset of the EFT in the explicit breaking limit,
outright!

IV
Linearized limit results

In this section we explore the consequences of explicit spacetime-symmetry breaking in a context where the constraints implied by the Bianchi identities as well as the identity of the degrees of freedom can be established from field equations linear in the metric fluctuations.
To keep the scope reasonable we restrict attention to the couplings in the action (

IV.1
Initial value solution for an explicit breaking case

To illustrate some basic features we analyze a special case of (

IV.2
Momentum space matrix solution

The general coefficient case is considerably more complicated due to the anisotropic coefficients in
\overline{s}_{\mu\nu}
.
One straightforward, albeit tedious,
method to explore the dynamical content of Eq. (
33
),
is to use Fourier decomposition in momentum space,
thereby reducing the problem to an algebraic one.
Indeed this has long been used for studies of propagation effects in spacetime-symmetry breaking
[
27
,
72
,
73
,
74
,
75
,
76
,
77
,
78
]
.
Thus for the metric fluctuations we use,

h_{\mu\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int d^{4}p\,\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}(p)e^{-ip_{\mu}x^{\mu}},

(52)

with a similar ansatz for
\tilde{s}_{\mu\nu}
.
The tilde notation over fields indicates the Fourier transform in what follows.
Denoting the right-hand side of (
33
)
as
\Sigma_{\mu\nu}
,
as in (
37
),
we can write the equation in a suggestive form

M_{\mu\nu}^{\phantom{{\mu\nu}}\alpha\beta}\tilde{h}_{\alpha\beta}=\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mu\nu},

(53)

where now the “source”
\tilde{\Sigma}_{\mu\nu}
is expressed in momentum space, and thus depends on
p_{\mu}
.
The quantities
M_{\mu\nu}^{\phantom{{\mu\nu}}\alpha\beta}
are obtained by factoring out the metric fluctuations from the left-hand side of (
33
),
followed by the substitution
\partial_{\mu}\rightarrow-ip_{\mu}

[
75
]
, after which we obtain

M_{\mu\nu}^{\phantom{{\mu\nu}}\alpha\beta}=-\frac{1}{2}p^{2}\delta^{(\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\mu}\delta^{\beta)}_{\phantom{\beta}\nu}\cdots+\lambda_{1}[\overline{s}_{\mu\nu}(\eta^{\alpha\beta}p^{2}-p^{\alpha}p^{\beta})+\overline{s}^{\alpha\beta}(p_{\mu}p_{\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{2})]+e_{4}\overline{s}^{\gamma\delta}(\tfrac{1}{2}p_{\gamma}p_{\mu}\delta^{\alpha}_{\nu}\delta^{\beta}_{\gamma}+\cdots).

(54)

The system of algebraic equations in (
53
) can be recast into a
10\times 10
matrix equation for the ten components of the symmetric metric fluctuations
\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{h}_{\nu\mu}
.
We use a
10
component array with ordering
{\bf\tilde{h}}=(\tilde{h}_{00},\tilde{h}_{01},\tilde{h}_{02},\tilde{h}_{03},\tilde{h}_{11},\tilde{h}_{12},\tilde{h}_{13},\tilde{h}_{22},\tilde{h}_{23},\tilde{h}_{33})

and a corresponding array for
\Sigma_{\mu\nu}
(
{\bf\Sigma}=({\tilde{\Sigma}}_{00},{\tilde{\Sigma}}_{01},...)
).
The system of equations then takes the form

{\bf M}\cdot{\bf\tilde{h}}={\bf\Sigma}.

(55)

With this set-up,
determining
the properties of the matrix
{\bf M}

will determine the solutions in momentum space and
aid in finding the independent degrees of freedom.
Such methods have been employed previously in cases of spontaneous-symmetry breaking
[
28
]
,
and explicit symmetry breaking
[
48
]
.

IV.2.1
GR case

To simplify the process,
we first use rotational freedom to align the spatial momentum
p^{j}
along the

direction; thus for instance,

It is instructive to consider the general relativity case first,

while there are several extra polarizations compared to GR in the curvature solutions,
they are not all independent of one another.
Finally,
extra polarizations occur that are unsuppressed by a background coefficient or a constant from the framework that could be constrained by experiment,
as occurred earlier in Section
III.2
.
This last point is most crucial for understanding whether the subset of the framework (
1

All of the results in Table
2
leave unspecified the unknown, background-dependent terms in
\Sigma_{\mu\nu}
(
37

One possibility is to set them to zero by assuming
\tilde{s}_{\mu\nu}=0
.
Then
8
of the results of Table
2
are no-go; the results with a pre-factor of
1/3
for the scalar polarization
are ruled out by non-observation of scalar polarizations at current sensitivities (see discussion below).
On the other hand,
one could use observational constraints to place limits on,
for instance,
the combination
(2\Sigma_{00}-2\Sigma_{33}+\Sigma_{11}+\Sigma_{22})/3
that occurs in
the observable curvature via the GW strain.
For a given source,
we could then reconstruct

and this result would limit the size of the extra terms in
\Sigma_{\mu\nu}
and hence
\tilde{s}_{\mu\nu}

this kind of program could be carried out in future work.

V
Summary and discussion

In this paper we investigated the curvature couplings in an EFT description of explicit spacetime-symmetry breaking with the goal of determining if no-go constraints are avoidable in several scenarios.
We studied the action in (
1
),
which includes terms with coefficient traces not previously considered separately.
It is possible to countenance combinations of the terms in the action that avoid no-go constraints implied by the Bianchi identities under certain conditions, as seen in Eq. (
9
) and the “detailed balance” choices described thereafter.
In Section
III
, we examined a special subset of the generalized action including trace terms with
s^{\alpha}_{~\alpha}
and
t^{\alpha\beta}_{~~~\alpha\beta}
.
We found an extra degree of freedom in Eq.
18
,
and established a match to scalar-tensor theory in an experimentally ruled-out limit.
Further, we obtained beyond-GR gravitational-wave signals in Eq. (
30
) which show a discontinuity with GR in this subset of the EFT.

In Section
IV
we studied the linearized limit
of the framework,
including explicit diffeomorphism breaking terms in (
33
).
For a special case,
the field equations were solved as an initial-value problem in Section
IV.1
,
revealing an extra degree of freedom compared to GR (see Table
1
).
We generalized this case in Section
IV.2
by utilizing a momentum space
10\times 10
matrix equation for the metric fluctuations
and a one-at-a-time coefficient method for the EFT coefficients;
the results are summarized in Table
2
.
The main conclusions of this part of the work is that extra polarizations for GWs are predicted,
some of which could be ruled out due to having order unity strength compared to GR (discontinuity).

One can look at the current status of limits on extra polarizations from GW measurements:
ground-based detectors including LIGO and Virgo use Bayesian analysis to study different polarization modes with CBC sources
[

to test for the six polarizations

that many models predict beyond GR,
a network of five or more detectors is needed
[

]
.
Yet some studies find constraints on the GW vector and scalar mode polarizations through analysis of the stochastic GW background
[

86

and several works implement the null-stream method to test for alternative polarizations than those for GR
[
87

,
some providing constraint values when having electromagnetic signals to help provide sky localization
[

.
Additional constraints on scalar-tensor theories have been provided by pulsar binary systems

while continuous waves generated from pulsars also provide a means to search for constraints on different polarizations
[

Pulsar timing of stochastic GW backgrounds provide further constraints
[
98
,

from the International Pulsar Timing Array (IPTA)

The tightest ratio constraints for scalar-tensor mixed polarization has been given by Ref.
[
110

via Bayesian inference across pure tensor, vector and scalar polarization models.
Different set of parameter values are found for each, including the correlated luminosity distance and inclination angle,
yet Bayes factors comparing different polarizations are found.
From the binary black hole merger event GW170814,
they found a Bayes factor of 3.636 when comparing tensor to scalar and a factor 2.775 when comparing tensor to vector models.
For the binary neutron star merger event GW170817,
the factors are 60.271 and 51.043 for the tensor-scalar and tensor-vector, respectively.
The large favor for the tensor models may indicate that the prefactors of
\frac{1}{3}

Much more work can be done along the lines of investigation in this paper.
What would be the effect of explicit breaking terms on the near field, post-Newtonian limit? This could,
like the scalar-tensor case discussed in Section
III.1
,
provide another angle on the results to rule out subsets of the framework.
Additionally, what is the nature of the possible ghost-like terms found in the dispersion analysis of Section
IV

[

In this work,

and explicit-breaking limits can be studied.
Another subtlety to be explored is the choice of background;
we choose the coefficients like

It would be of interest to

[

45

Finally we include a cautionary remark to the reader.
Care is required in examining results for observables in this paper,

Overlap is quite likely,

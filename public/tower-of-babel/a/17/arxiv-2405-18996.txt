Using multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes for constructing optical orthogonal codes
Ferruh Özbudak, Paolo Santonastaso, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
. We present a new application of multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes to construct large optical orthogonal codes, with the aid of the multiplicative structure of finite fields extensions. This approach is different from earlier approaches using combinatorial and additive (character sum) structures of finite fields. Consequently, we immediately obtain new classes of optical orthogonal codes with different parameters.



Keywords
: optical orthogonal code; cyclic subspace code; linearized polynomial

1.
Introduction

In their groundbreaking paper
[
21
]
, Koetter and Kschischang established the foundations of subspace codes, which turns out to be useful for the error correction in random network coding. Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be a finite field and
m\geq 2
be an integer. We consider all
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. If
U,V
are two
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, then the
subspace distance

d_{s}(U,V)
is defined as

d_{s}(U,V)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)+\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V)-2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap V).

In this paper we consider constant dimension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear subspaces. Hence let
1\leq k\leq m
be a fixed integer.
If
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(V)=k
, then the subspace distance reads as
\dim_{s}(U,V)=2k-2\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap V)
. From now on, let
\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
denote the Grassmannian consisting of all
k
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. There is a natural action of the multiplicative group
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
on
\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
: for
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and
U\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
, let
cU
denote the action of
c
on
U
, which is defined as

cU=\{cu\colon u\in U\}\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k).

Therefore a
cyclic subspace code

C
in
\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
is a collection of elements of
\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
such that
U\in C
implies
cU\in C
, for every
c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
, see
[
17
]
. It has been an active research area to construct cyclic subspace codes with best parameters in the recent years. One of the most useful techniques in the recent years for the construction of cyclic subspace codes is via Sidon spaces
[
26
]
. Here we present an equivalent definition:
U\in\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
is a
Sidon space
of dimension
k
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
if and only if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap\alpha U)\leq 1,\mbox{ for each }\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.

This suggests a natural extension. For
r\geq 1
, let
U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}
be distinct elements of
\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
. We say that
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}\}
is an
r
-multi Sidon space
if

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{i}\cap\alpha U_{j})\leq 1,

for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and
1\leq i,j\leq r
with
i\neq j
, and for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\setminus{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
i=j
, see
[
37
]
. In particular, multi Sidon spaces have important applications in cyclic subspace codes. In this paper, using a multiplicative approach we obtain a new connection of multi-orbit cyclic subspace and codes and multi Sidon spaces to optical orthogonal codes.

Given a vector
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
, and for an integer
i\geq n
we denote by
x_{i}:=x_{i\pmod{n}}
.

Definition 1.1
.

An
(n,w,\lambda)

optical orthogonal code
(
OOC
for short)
\C
is an Hamming-metric code in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
having constant weight
w
, satisfying the following two properties:

(1)

The autocorrelation property:

\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}x_{t}x_{t+\tau}\leq\lambda,

for every
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in\C
and every integer
\tau
, with
0<\tau<n
.

(2)

The crosscorrelation property:

\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}x_{t}y_{t+\tau}\leq\lambda,

for every distinct
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1}),(y_{0},\ldots,y_{n-1})\in\C
and every non negative integer
\tau
.

Since every codeword
\mathbf{x}\in\C
has Hamming weight
w
, the autocorrelation equals
w
in the extremal case
\tau=0
. To avoid triviality, we require that
\lambda<w
.
For the applications of OOCs in optical code-division multiple access (OCDMA) communication systems, it is desirable that an OOC have large size and small maximum correlation for a given length
n
and a given weight
w
; see
[
28
,
29
]
.
As also pointed out in
[
13
]
, OOCs when used in OCDMA communication systems need to have the property that each codeword has many more 0’s than 1’s. In terms of parameters, for an
(n,w,\lambda)
OOC, this means that the parameter
n
is significantly larger than
w
.
However, there is a tradeoff among the parameters of an OOC, known as
Johnson bound
.

Theorem 1.2

(see
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\C
be an
(n,w,\lambda)
OOC. Then

(1)

\lvert\C\rvert\leq J(n,w,\lambda):=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{w}\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{w-1}\left\lfloor\frac{n-2}{w-2}\left\lfloor\cdots\left\lfloor\frac{n-\lambda}{w-\lambda}\right\rfloor\cdots\right\rfloor\right\rfloor\right\rfloor\right\rfloor.

When
\lambda=1
, the optimal set size of an
(n,w,1)
OOC is given by

\lvert\C\rvert=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{w}\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{w-1}\right\rfloor\right\rfloor.

There exist several constructions of optimal and large non optimal OOCs with
\lambda=1

When

The first construction of optimal OOC with

In this paper we will show a construction of OOCs with the aid of multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes or multi-Sidon spaces.
Consequently, we construct new classes of optical orthogonal codes with new parameters. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. We explain first the connection to multiplicative structures and finite field extensions in Section

2.
OOC codes from extension fields

In this section we will see how the problem of finding OOCs can be translated in terms of sets of integers and then we use this correspondence to describe OOCs in extension fields.
Let us start by observing that it is possible to establish a one-to-one correspondence between elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
and subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
in the following way:

\begin{array}[]{lcrc}\theta:&{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}&\longrightarrow&2^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}\\
&\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1})&\longmapsto&\theta(\mathbf{x})=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}\colon x_{i}=1\},\end{array}

so
\theta
is the map that associates each element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{n}
with its support (with respect to the Hamming metric).
Note also that
\theta
is invertible and
{\mathbf{w}}_{H}(\mathbf{x})=\lvert\theta(\mathbf{x})\rvert
, where
{\mathbf{w}}_{H}(\mathbf{x})
denotes the Hamming weight of
\mathbf{x}
. For a non negative integer
\tau
, and a non-empty subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
we define

X+\tau:=\{a+\tau\colon a\in X\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}.

The correspondence given by
\theta
allows us to translate the definition of an OOC in terms of subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
(n,w,\lambda)

Optical Orthogonal Set
(OOS)
\mathcal{S}
is a subset of
2^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
with the property that
\lvert X\rvert=w
for every
X\in\mathcal{S}
, and such that the following two properties hold:

(1)

The autocorrelation property:

\lvert X\cap(X+\tau)\rvert\leq\lambda,

for every
X\in\mathcal{S}
and every integer
\tau
, with
0<\tau<n
.

(2)

The crosscorrelation property:

\lvert X\cap(Y+\tau)\rvert\leq\lambda,

for every distinct
X,Y\in\mathcal{S}
and every non negative integer
\tau
.

As a consequence of the properties of
\theta
, we have the following correspondence, showing that constructing OOCs is equivalent to construct sets as defined in Definition
2.1
(OOS).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\C
be an
(n,w,\lambda)
OOC. Then
\theta(\C)
is an
(n,w,\lambda)
OOS. Conversely, let
\mathcal{S}
be an
(n,w,\lambda)
OOS. Then
\theta^{-1}(\mathcal{S})
is an
(n,w,\lambda)
OOC.

In the next sections, we will provide new families of OOCs by showing new constructions of OOSs.
To this aim, we show a procedure to construct OOSs, by using subsets of a finite field. Let
\omega
be a primitive element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Let
W
be a nonempty subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Denote by

\mathcal{S}(W):=\{i\colon\omega^{i}\in W\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{N}

with
N=q^{m}-1
. Note that
\lvert S(W)\rvert=\lvert W^{*}\rvert
, where
W^{*}:=W\setminus\{0\}
and that

(2)

\mathcal{S}(\alpha W)=\mathcal{S}(W)+\tau,

if
\alpha=\omega^{\tau}
.

In the following result we will see how to construct OOSs, using the multiplicative properties of subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{t}
be distinct nonempty subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and let
N=q^{m}-1
. Define

\mathcal{S}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{t}):=\{\mathcal{S}(W_{i})\colon i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}\}\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{Z}_{N}}.

The set
\mathcal{S}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{t})
is an
(q^{m}-1,w,\lambda)
OOS if and only if
\lvert W_{i}^{*}\rvert=w
for every
i
, and

(1)

for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
and every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\setminus\{0,1\}

\lvert W_{i}\cap\alpha W_{i}\rvert\leq\lambda;

(2)

for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
with
i\neq j
and every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}

\lvert W_{i}\cap\alpha W_{j}\rvert\leq\lambda.

In particular,
\lvert\mathcal{S}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{t})\rvert=t
.

Proof.

By Definition
2.1
,
\mathcal{S}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{t})
is an
(N,w,\lambda)
if and only if every set of
\mathcal{S}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{t})
has size
w
and

•

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
and for any
\tau\in\{1,\ldots,q^{m}-2\}
then
|\mathcal{S}(W_{i})\cap(\mathcal{S}(W_{i})+\tau)|=|W_{i}\cap\omega^{\tau}W_{i}|\leq\lambda
;

•

for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
with
i\neq j
and for any
\tau\in\{1,\ldots,q^{m}-2\}
then
|\mathcal{S}(W_{i})\cap(\mathcal{S}(W_{j})+\tau)|=|W_{i}\cap\omega^{\tau}W_{j}|\leq\lambda
,

and so this happens if and only if the
W_{i}
’s satisfy (1) and (2).
∎

In the next section we will see that if we choose the
W_{i}
’s as the affine spaces associated with the distinct representative of cyclic subspace codes we obtain an OOS.

3.
Constructions from cyclic subspace codes

Let
C
be a subset of
\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
, we recall that the
minimum subspace distance
of a subspace code
C
is defined as

d_{s}(C):=\min\{d(U,V)\colon U,V\in C,U\neq V\}.

As already seen in the Introduction, a subspace code
C\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
is said to be
cyclic
if for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and every
V\in C
then
\alpha V\in C
.
A
one-orbit (cyclic) code
is a subspace code of the form

C_{U}:=\{\alpha U:\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k),

where
U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=k
. Sometimes,
C_{U}
is also refereed as the
orbit
of
U
. The size of
C_{U}
is
(q^{m}-1)/(q^{t}-1)
, for some
t
which divides
m
. The largest size of such a code is
(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)
, and codes attaining this size are called
full-length one-orbit codes
. Full-length orbit codes with distance
2k-2
, which is the best possible, will be called
optimal full-length one-orbit codes
. These kinds of subspace codes are connected with Sidon spaces.
An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is called a
Sidon space
if the product of any two elements of
U
has a unique factorization over
U
, up to multiplying by some elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
More precisely,
U
is a Sidon space if for all nonzero
a,b,c,d\in U
, if
ab=cd
, then

\{a\mathbb{F}_{q},b\mathbb{F}_{q}\}=\{c\mathbb{F}_{q},d\mathbb{F}_{q}\},

where if
e\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
then
e\mathbb{F}_{q}=\{e\lambda\colon\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
.

Theorem 3.1

(see
[
26
, Lemma 34]
)
.

Let
U
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
of dimension
k
. Then
C_{U}
is an optimal full-length one-orbit code if and only if
U
is a Sidon space.

The above connection can be extended to multi-orbit cyclic subspace codes.
Let
U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}
be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of dimension
k
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and let

(3)

C=\bigcup_{i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}}C_{U_{i}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)

with minimum distance
2k-2
and of size
r\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}
.
These codes have the same minimum distance of the codes associated with Sidon spaces but they are larger.

The code
C
is uniquely defined by the subspaces
U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}
and therefore we give the following two definitions.
The set
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}\}
for the cyclic subspace code (
3
) is said to be a set of
representatives
of
C
, whereas if also its minimum distance is
2k-2
we will call it a
multi-Sidon space
, in connection with the correspondence between cyclic subspace codes with these parameters with one orbit and Sidon spaces.

We will now show how to use cyclic subspace codes to construct OOCs. First we describe the intersection properties of affine spaces associated with the subspaces of a cyclic subspace code.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
C=\cup_{i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}}C_{U_{i}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
be a cyclic subspace code with minimum distance
d
and with the property that the
C_{U_{i}}
’s are
r
distinct orbits. For every
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, let
d_{i,1},\ldots,d_{i,t}
be pairwise not
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-proportional representatives of the elements of the quotient
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/U_{i}
, with
t=(q^{m-k}-1)/(q-1)
.
Let

\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\{U_{i}+d_{i,j}\colon j\in\{1,\ldots,t\}\}.

Then

|\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})|=t\cdot r,

and

(1)

for every
V\in\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
and every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\setminus\{0,1\}

\lvert V\cap\alpha V\rvert\leq q^{k-d/2};

(2)

for every
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
with
V_{1}\neq V_{2}
and every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}

\lvert V_{i}\cap\alpha V_{j}\rvert\leq q^{k-d/2}.

Proof.

For any subspace
U
in
C
define the set of affine subspaces associated with
U

A(U)=\{U+a\colon a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}\}.

It is easy to see that
|\mathcal{A}(U)|=|A(U)|/(q-1)
and
|A(U)|=q^{m-k}-1
. Since for
U_{i},U_{j}
, with
i\neq j
we have that
A(U_{i})\cap A(U_{j})=\emptyset
, we have that

\lvert\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})\rvert=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lvert\mathcal{A}(U_{i})\rvert=r\frac{q^{m-k}-1}{q-1},

that proves the first part of the assertion. For the second part, let
V\in\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
. So,
V=U_{i}+d_{i,j}
, for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
. Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\setminus\{0,1\}
. If
\alpha\notin{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, then

\lvert V\cap\alpha V\rvert=\lvert(U_{i}+d_{i,j})\cap(\alpha U_{i}+\alpha d_{i,j})\rvert\leq\lvert U_{i}\cap\alpha U_{i}\rvert\leq q^{k-d/2}.

Otherwise, if
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}
then
V\cap\alpha V=(U_{i}+d_{i,j})\cap(U_{i}+\alpha d_{i,j})=\emptyset
. Indeed, if
V\cap\alpha V=(U_{i}+d_{i,j})\cap(U_{i}+\alpha d_{i,j})\neq\emptyset
, there exists
u,v\in U_{i}
such that
u+d_{i,j}=v+\alpha d_{i,j}
, implying that
d_{i,j}\in U_{i}
, a contradiction to our hypothesis. This proves
(1)
. Now, let
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
with
V_{1}\neq V_{2}
. Assume, that
V_{1}=U_{i}+d_{i,j}
and
V_{2}=U_{i}+d_{i,h}
, with
j\neq h
. If
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
then

V_{1}\cap\alpha V_{2}=(U_{i}+d_{i,j})\cap(\alpha U_{i}+\alpha d_{i,h})=\emptyset.

Indeed, if
(U_{i}+d_{i,j})\cap(\alpha U_{i}+\alpha d_{i,h})\neq\emptyset
, there exists
u,v\in U_{i}
such that
u+d_{i,j}=v+\alpha d_{i,h}
, implying that
d_{i,j}
and
d_{i,h}
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-proportional representative of the elements of the quotient
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/U_{i}
, a contradiction to our hypothesis. While, if
V_{1}=U_{i}+d_{i,j_{1}}
and
V_{2}=U_{h}+d_{i,j_{2}}
, with
i\neq h
, therefore

\lvert V_{1}\cap\alpha V_{2}\rvert=\lvert(U_{i}+d_{i,j_{1}})\cap(\alpha U_{h}+\alpha d_{i,j_{2}})\rvert\leq\lvert U_{i}\cap\alpha U_{h}\rvert\leq q^{k-d/2}.

This proves
(2)
and the assertion.
∎

As a consequence we immediately obtain the following result, where we show how to construct OOCs from cyclic subspace codes.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
C=\cup_{i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}}C_{U_{i}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
be a cyclic subspace code with minimum distance
d
and with the property that the
C_{U_{i}}
’s are
r
distinct orbits.
Then
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}))
is an OOS with size
r(q^{m-k}-1)/(q-1)
and parameters
(q^{m}-1,q^{k},q^{k-d/2})
.
In particular,
\theta^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})))
is an
(q^{m}-1,q^{k},q^{k-d/2})
OOC with size
r(q^{m-k}-1)/(q-1)
.

Proof.

Consider
\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
as in Proposition
3.2
. Note that for any
V\in\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
we have that

|\mathcal{S}(V)|=q^{k},

and

(1)

for every
V\in\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
and every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\setminus\{0,1\}

\lvert V\cap\alpha V\rvert\leq q^{k-d/2};

(2)

for every
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{A}(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
with
V_{1}\neq V_{2}
and every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}

\lvert V_{1}\cap\alpha V_{2}\rvert\leq q^{k-d/2}.

by Proposition
3.2
. By Theorem
2.3
we obtain the assertion.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

This construction extends the idea in
[
19
, Section 4]
of using certain affine subspaces of the kernel of subspace polynomials. Indeed, in our terms, they consider optimal full-length one-orbit codes defined by the kernel of a trinomial subspace polynomial and then they prove a result of the form of Theorem
3.3
for the studied cases. The problem with this approach is that, in general, it is hard to find in which extension suitable trinomials split completely; see e.g.
[
22
,
30
]
.

Therefore, using large cyclic subspace codes we can construct large OOCs.
For a multi-orbit cyclic subspace code
C\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{q}(m,k)
having minimum distance
d
, the sphere-packing bound (see
[
17
, Theorem 2]
) implies that

|C|\leq\frac{\left[\begin{matrix}m\\
k-d/2+1\end{matrix}\right]_{q}}{\left[\begin{matrix}k\\
k-d/2+1\end{matrix}\right]_{q}},

and so the number
r
of distinct orbits in
C
satisfies

r\leq\frac{|C|}{q^{m}-1}\leq\frac{\left[\begin{matrix}m\\
k-d/2+1\end{matrix}\right]_{q}}{(q^{m}-1)\left[\begin{matrix}k\\
k-d/2+1\end{matrix}\right]_{q}},

that is asymptotically
r
is at most
q^{(m-k)(k-d/2+1)-m}
.
Therefore,
C
define an
(q^{m}-1,q^{k},q^{k-d/2})
OOC with size at most

\frac{q^{m-k}-1}{q-1}\frac{\left[\begin{matrix}m\\
k-d/2+1\end{matrix}\right]_{q}}{(q^{m}-1)\left[\begin{matrix}k\\
k-d/2+1\end{matrix}\right]_{q}},

and hence asymptotically roughly
q^{(m-k)(k-d/2+1)-k}

By using Theorem
3.3
, we get that
C
defines an
(q^{m}-1,q^{k},q^{k-d/2})
OOC with size
r(q^{m-k}-1)/(q-1)
. For such parameters, the Johnson bound (
1
) can be approximated as follows

J(q^{m}-1,q^{k},q^{k-d/2})\approx\frac{(q^{n-k})^{q^{k-\frac{d}{2}}}}{q^{k}},

which implies that the constructions of OOCs from cyclic subspace codes cannot be either optimal or asymptotically optimal with respect to the Johnson bound.

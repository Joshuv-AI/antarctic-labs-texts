Certain subclass of harmonic functions associated with univalent functions
PRACHI PRAJNA DASH ‡ , Jugal Kishore Prajapat †

Abstract
. In this paper, we define a subclass of sense-preserving harmonic functions associated with a class of analytic functions satisfying a differential inequality. We then establish a close relation between both subclasses. Further, we obtain some characteristic properties including radius properties, convolution, coefficient estimates and their properties, growth estimates, and convex combination for the functions in the defined subclass. At last, we produce conditions for some special functions as well as harmonic univalent polynomials to belong to the defined subclass of harmonic functions.

1.
Introduction

A complex-valued harmonic function in a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
is defined as a function
f(z)=u(z)+iv(z)
, which is twice continuously differentiable and
\Delta f(z)=4f_{z\overline{z}}(z)=0
in
D
. The function
f(z)
has a canonical representation
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}
when
D
is simply connected, where
h(z)
and
g(z)
are analytic functions in
D
and are termed as analytic and co-analytic parts of
f(z)
, respectively. It is well known that
f(z)
is locally univalent in
D
if, and only if, the Jacobian
J_{f}(z)\neq 0
and
f(z)
is sense-preserving if
J_{f}(z)>0
in
D
, where
J_{f}(z)=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of complex-valued harmonic functions
f(z)
defined in the open unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, normalized by the condition
f(0)=0=f_{z}(0)-1
. As a result,
h(z)
and
g(z)
of
f(z)
have power series representations as

(1.1)

h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{0}
be a subclass of
\mathcal{H}
, defined as
\mathcal{H}^{0}=\{f(z)\in\mathcal{H}:f_{\overline{z}}(0)=0\}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Thus, for
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{0}
, (
1.1
) will become

(1.2)

h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
be another subclass of
\mathcal{H}
containing all univalent functions of
\mathcal{H}
and let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
be a subclass of
\mathcal{H}^{0}
containing all univalent functions of
\mathcal{H}^{0}
in
\mathbb{D}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{H}}
. Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
1
]
started study on the class of harmonic univalent functions. These articles
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
provide basic information about harmonic functions as well as some more recent studies.

A starlike domain with respect to a point in it is defined to be that which contains all line segments connecting any point in it to the respected point in it. It is simply called a starlike domain if the respected point is the origin. Further, a convex domain is defined to be that which is starlike with respect to every point in it. A harmonic starlike function
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}
is defined to be that which maps
\mathbb{D}
to a starlike domain. The class of starlike function in
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
. In similar, a harmonic convex function
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}
is defined to be that which maps
\mathbb{D}
to a convex domain, and the collection of such functions is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{H}}
. A close-to-convex domain is defined to be that which complement can be written as disjoint union of non-crossing half lines. A harmonic close-to-convex function
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}
is defined to be that which maps
\mathbb{D}
to a close-to-convex domain, and the collection of such functions is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
\mathcal{K}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\mathcal{S}^{*0}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}
be the subclasses of
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{H}}
,
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{H}}
respectively, where
f_{\overline{z}}(0)=0
.

1.1.
Stable analytic and stable harmonic functions

For all
|\zeta|=1
, if the analytic functions
F_{\zeta}(z)=h(z)+\zeta g(z)
are convex, starlike, close-to-convex and univalent on
\mathbb{D}
then the function
F(z)=h(z)+g(z)
is said to be stable analytic convex, stable analytic starlike, stable analytic close-to-convex and stable analytic univalent on
\mathbb{D}
, respectively. In similar, if the harmonic functions
f_{\zeta}(z)=h(z)+\zeta\overline{g(z)}
with
|\zeta|=1
are convex, starlike, close-to-convex and univalent on
\mathbb{D}
then the function
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}
is said to be stable harmonic convex, stable harmonic starlike, stable harmonic close-to-convex and stable harmonic univalent on
\mathbb{D}
, respectively. More information regarding stable harmonic functions is available in
[
7
]
. The following is one useful result of stable analytic and stable harmonic functions.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
7
]

A sense-preserving harmonic function
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}
on
\mathbb{D}
is stable harmonic univalent (respectively, convex), if and only if the analytic function
F(z)=h(z)+g(z)
is stable analytic univalent (respectively, convex) on
\mathbb{D}
. Same result holds for
f(z)
to be stable harmonic starlike on
\mathbb{D}
when the functions
f_{\zeta}(z)=h(z)+\zeta\overline{g(z)}
fix the origin on
\mathbb{D}
for all
|\zeta|=1
.

The convolution (or Hadamard product) of two analytic functions
F_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
F_{2}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
is defined by

(F_{1}\ast F_{2})(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\,b_{n}\,z^{n}.

Following the above, the convolution for two harmonic functions
f_{1}(z)=h_{1}(z)+\overline{g_{1}(z)}
and
f_{2}(z)=h_{2}(z)+\overline{g_{2}(z)}
in
\mathcal{H}
is defined as

(f_{1}\ast f_{2})(z)=(h_{1}*h_{2})(z)+\overline{(g_{1}\ast g_{2})(z)}.

Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
1
]
also proved a result for the convolution between a harmonic and an analytic function which states, if
f(z)
is harmonic convex and
F(z)
is analytic convex, then
f(z)\ast(F(z)+\gamma\overline{F(z)})
is a harmonic close-to-convex function where
|\gamma|<1
.

Recently, Ponnusamy et al.
[
9
]
(see also
[
8
]
) have studied the following subclass of
\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle C^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\left\{f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\mathcal{H}:|h^{\prime}(z)-1|<1-|g^{\prime}(z)|\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq C^{1}_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\{f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\mathcal{H}:\Re(h^{\prime}(z))>\Re(g^{\prime}(z))\right\},

and proved that its elements are close-to-convex in
\mathbb{D}
. Motivated by this study, we define the following subclass of
\mathcal{H}^{0}
:

Definition 1.1
.

For
\lambda>0

\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda):=\left\{f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\mathcal{H}^{0}:|h(z)-zh^{\prime}(z)|<\lambda-|g(z)-zg^{\prime}(z)|,\;z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}.

We see many examples of functions in
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
in the next section. Now, we consider the subclass of
\mathcal{A}
defined by us in 2023
[
10
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
\lambda>0

\Omega(\lambda):=\left\{F(z)\in\mathcal{A}:|F(z)-zF^{\prime}(z)|<\lambda,\;z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}.

The class
\Omega(\lambda)
is non-empty as the identity function satisfies the Definition
1.2
. Note that
\Omega(\lambda_{1})\subseteq\Omega(\lambda_{2})
when
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}
, and
\Omega(1/2)=\Omega
is the class defined by Peng and Zhong
[
11
]
that is closed under convolution. The functions in
\Omega
are univalent and starlike in
\mathbb{D}
, and convex are in
\mathbb{D}_{1/2}
(see
[
11
,
12
]
). Obradović and Peng
[
13
]
gave certain sufficient conditions for functions belongs to the class
\Omega
, also Wani and Swaminathan
[
14
]
obtained certain sufficient conditions and subordination properties for functions belongs to the class
\Omega
. Thus, the functions in
\Omega(\lambda)
are univalent and starlike in
\mathbb{D}
, and are convex in
\mathbb{D}_{1/2}
for
\lambda\in\left(0,1/2\right]
. We
[
10
]
observed that the function

(1.3)

F(z)=z+\dfrac{\lambda z^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{\lambda z^{3}}{4},\quad\lambda>0,\,z\in\mathbb{D},

belongs to the class
\Omega(\lambda)
. The following results we obtained in
[
10
]
for the class
\Omega(\lambda)
are being used in our main results.

Lemma 1.2
.

The radius of starlikeness of
\Omega(\lambda)
is
1/2\lambda
. Further, if
F(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
, then

(1.4)

\displaystyle|z|-\lambda|z|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\;|F(z)|\leq\;|z|+\lambda|z|^{2},

(1.5)

\displaystyle 1-2\lambda|z|\leq

\displaystyle\;|F^{\prime}(z)|\leq\;1+2\lambda|z|.

Equality occurs in both the inequalities, if and only if
F(z)=z+\lambda\eta z^{2}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
z\neq 0
, where
|\eta|=1
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
F(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
. Then
F(z)
is univalent in
|z|<1/2\lambda
, and convex in
|z|<1/4\lambda
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
F(z)\in\mathcal{A}
. If
|F^{\prime\prime}(z)|\leq 2\lambda
then
F(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
. The number
2\lambda
is the best possible.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
F(z)\in\mathcal{A}
. If

|z^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(z)+zF^{\prime}(z)-F(z)|\leq 3\lambda,

then
F(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
. The number
3\lambda
is best possible.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
F_{1}(z)
,
F_{2}(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
. Then
(F_{1}\ast F_{2})(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
, if
\lambda\leq 1
.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Association between
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
and
\Omega(\lambda)

Theorem 2.1
.

For a harmonic function
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, there exist an analytic function
F_{\zeta}(z)=h(z)+\zeta g(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
and vice versa, where
|\zeta|=1
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
and
F_{\zeta}(z)=h(z)+\zeta g(z)
, for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
|\zeta|=1
. Using Definition
1.1
, and for each
\zeta
, we can write

\displaystyle\left|F_{\zeta}(z)-zF^{\prime}_{\zeta}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|h(z)+\zeta g(z)-zh^{\prime}(z)-z\zeta g^{\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|h(z)-zh^{\prime}(z)\right|+\left|g(z)-zg^{\prime}(z)\right|<\lambda,

for any
\lambda>0
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. This implies
F_{\zeta}(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
.

Conversely, let
F_{\zeta}(z)=h(z)+\zeta g(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
for any
\zeta
, where
|\zeta|<1
. Thus, for
z\in\mathbb{D}

\left|F_{\zeta}(z)-zF^{\prime}_{\zeta}(z)\right|=\left|h(z)+\zeta g(z)-zh^{\prime}(z)-z\zeta g^{\prime}(z)\right|<\lambda.

Now, for an appropriate choice of
\zeta
, we obtain

\left|h(z)-zh^{\prime}(z)\right|+\left|g(z)-zg^{\prime}(z)\right|<\lambda,

for any
\lambda>0
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Therefore,
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
. These complete the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
1.2
, Lemma
1.3
for a function
F_{\zeta}(z)=h(z)+\zeta g(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
, where
|\zeta|=1
, and Theorem
2.1
in Lemma

, we yield the following results on radius properties of
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)

Let
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)

f(z)
is stable harmonic univalent (respectively, starlike) in
|z|<1/2\lambda
. In particular, a harmonic function
f(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)

Let

is stable harmonic convex in

. In particular, a harmonic function
f(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)

|z|<1/4\lambda
.

Corollary 2.3

Let
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)

f(z)
is stable harmonic univalent (respectively, starlike) in
\mathbb{D}
, and is stable harmonic convex in

Using Lemma

Let

2.2.
Coefficient estimates and their properties

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
with the form as in (
1.2
) of
h(z)
and
g(z)
, the following coefficient bounds hold true.

|a_{n}|\leq\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad|b_{n}|\leq\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1},

for
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. Sharpness of both bounds holds.

Proof.

Let
f(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
. Note that,
zh^{\prime}(z)-h(z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and from (
1.2
), we obtain

(2.1)

zh^{\prime}(z)-h(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)a_{n}z^{n}.

Using Cauchy’s integral formula, for
z=re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{D}
, we obtain

(n-1)|a_{n}|=\left|\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=r}\dfrac{zh^{\prime}(z)-h(z)}{z^{n+1}}dz\right|\leq\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\dfrac{\left|re^{i\theta}h^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})-h(re^{i\theta})\right|}{r^{n}}d\theta,

for
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. Now using Definition
1.1
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}

\displaystyle(n-1)|a_{n}|r^{n}

\displaystyle<\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\lambda-\left|re^{i\theta}g^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})-g(re^{i\theta})\right|\right)d\theta

\displaystyle<\lambda-\left|\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(re^{i\theta}g^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})-g(re^{i\theta})\right)d\theta\right|=\lambda.

Thus, for
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}

|a_{n}|<\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1},

as
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
. In similar, considering
zg^{\prime}(z)-g(z)
in place of
zh^{\prime}(z)-h(z)
and vice versa following from (
2.1
), for
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
we obtain

|b_{n}|<\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1}.

Sharpness of
|a_{n}|
holds for the function
f_{a}(z)
in
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, where

f_{a}(z)=z+\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1}z^{n},

where
h(z)=z+\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1}z^{n}
and
g(z)=0
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. Further, sharpness of
|b_{n}|
holds for the function
f_{b}(z)
in
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, where

f_{b}(z)=z-\dfrac{\lambda}{n-1}\overline{z}^{n},

where
h(z)=z
and
g(z)=\dfrac{-\lambda}{n-1}z^{n}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following result provides a sufficient condition for a function to belong to class
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\mathcal{H}^{0}
, it also belongs to
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
if

(2.2)

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|)<\lambda.

Proof.

Let
f(z)\in\mathcal{H}^{0}
. Now

\displaystyle|zh^{\prime}(z)-h(z)|\;

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|a_{n}||z|^{n}\;\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|a_{n}|

\displaystyle<\lambda-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|b_{n}|\;\leq\lambda-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|b_{n}||z|^{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda-|zg^{\prime}(z)-g(z)|,

which is true if (
2.2
) holds true. Thus,
f(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, if and only if (
2.2
) holds true. This completes the proof.
∎

2.3.
Growth estimates, convex combination, and characteristic properties

Theorem 2.4
.

For any
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle|z|-\lambda|z|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|f(z)|\leq|z|+\lambda|z|^{2},

(2.4)

\displaystyle 1-2\lambda|z|

\displaystyle\leq|f^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1+2\lambda|z|.

Here equality occurs for the function
f_{3}(z)=z+\lambda\eta z^{2}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, where
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
z\neq 0
and
|\eta|=1
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
and hence using Theorem
2.1
, we have
F_{\zeta}(z)=h(z)+\zeta g(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
, for all
|\zeta|=1
. Using Lemma
1.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle|z|-\lambda|z|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|h(z)+\zeta g(z)|\leq|z|+\lambda|z|^{2},

\displaystyle 1-2\lambda|z|

\displaystyle\leq|h^{\prime}(z)+\zeta g^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1+2\lambda|z|.

Now, for appropriate choices of
\zeta
, we can write

(2.5)

\displaystyle|z|-\lambda|z|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|h(z)|-|g(z)|\quad\text{and}\quad|h(z)|+|g(z)|\leq|z|+\lambda|z|^{2},

(2.6)

\displaystyle 1-2\lambda|z|

\displaystyle\leq|h^{\prime}(z)|-|g^{\prime}(z)|\quad\text{and}\quad|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1+2\lambda|z|,

which further implies

\displaystyle|z|-\lambda|z|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|h(z)+\overline{g(z)}|\quad\text{and}\quad|h(z)+\overline{g(z)}|\leq|z|+\lambda|z|^{2},

\displaystyle 1-2\lambda|z|

\displaystyle\leq|h^{\prime}(z)+\overline{g^{\prime}(z)}|\quad\text{and}\quad|h^{\prime}(z)+\overline{g^{\prime}(z)}|\leq 1+2\lambda|z|,

which is equivalent to (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), respectively. As the function
f_{3}(z)=z+\lambda\eta z^{2}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, for
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
z\neq 0
and
|\eta|=1
, equality occurs for appropriate choices of
\eta
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

The class
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
is closed under convex combination.

Proof.

Let
f_{k}(z)=h_{k}(z)+\overline{g_{k}(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
for
k=1,\,2,\,3,\,\dots,\,n,
and
\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}=1
, where
t_{k}\in[0,1]
. Note that

f(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}f_{k}(z)=h(z)+g(z),

is a convex combination of
f_{k}
’s, where
h(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}h_{k}(z)
and
g(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}g_{k}(z)
. Thus, both
h(z)
and
g(z)
are in class
\mathcal{A}
. Now

\displaystyle\left|h(z)-zh^{\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}h_{k}(z)-z\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}h_{k}^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}\left|h_{k}(z)-zh_{k}^{\prime}(z)\right|

\displaystyle<\lambda-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}g_{k}(z)-zg_{k}^{\prime}(z)\right|=\lambda-\left|g(z)-zg^{\prime}(z)\right|.

Thus,
f(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

For any
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, we have

J_{f}(z)\leq(1+2\lambda|z|)^{2}.

Here equality occurs for the function
f_{3}(z)=z+\lambda\eta z^{2}
, where
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
z\neq 0
, and
|\eta|=1
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
. Now

\left|h(z)-zh^{\prime}(z)\right|<\lambda-\left|g(z)-zg^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq\lambda.

Thus,
h(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
. Hence using Lemma
1.2
, we obtain

|h^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1+2\lambda|z|.

Now

J_{f}(z)=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\leq\;|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\leq\;(1+2\lambda|z|)^{2}.

Consider the function of
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
,
f_{3}(z)=h_{3}(z)+\overline{g_{3}(z)}=z+\lambda\eta z^{2}
, for
z\in\mathbb{D}
,
z\neq 0
, and
|\eta|=1
. Clearly,
h_{3}(z)=z+\lambda\eta z^{2}
. This implies

|h^{\prime}_{3}(z)|=|1+2\lambda\eta z|=1+2\lambda|z|,

for an appropriate choice of
\eta
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Each
f(z)=h(z)+\overline{g(z)}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
maps
\mathbb{D}
onto a domain bounded by a rectifiable Jordan curve, when
\lambda\leq 1/2
.

Proof.

Let
f(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
and let
z_{1}
,
z_{2}\in\mathbb{D}
such that
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
and
|z_{1}|\geq|z_{2}|
. For the line segment
[z_{1},z_{2}]
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left|f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\tau}d\tau+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\overline{\tau}}d\overline{\tau}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\;|h^{\prime}(\tau)|+|g^{\prime}(\tau)|\;\right)|d\tau|.

Using (
2.6
) (from Theorem
2.4
) in (
2.7
), we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left|f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|z_{2}|}^{|z_{1}|}(1+2\lambda\kappa)d\kappa

\displaystyle=(|z_{1}|-|z_{2}|)+\lambda(|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2})

\displaystyle=(|z_{1}|-|z_{2}|)\left(1+\lambda(|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|)\right)

\displaystyle\leq(|z_{1}|-|z_{2}|)\left(1+2\lambda\right)\leq(1+2\lambda)|z_{1}-z_{2}|<\epsilon,

for any
\epsilon>0
, such that
|z_{1}-z_{2}|<\delta:=\dfrac{\epsilon}{1+2\lambda}
, for any
\delta>0
. Therefore,
f(z)
is uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{D}
and consequently can be extended continuously on the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
C
be a curve defined by
f(e^{i\theta})
for
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
, and let
0=\theta_{0}<\theta_{1}<\dots<\theta_{n}=2\pi
be a partition of
[0,2\pi]
. Now using (
2.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}|f(e^{i\theta_{k}})-f(e^{i\theta_{k-1}})|

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\lambda)\sum_{k=1}^{n}|e^{i\theta_{k}}-e^{i\theta_{k-1}}|

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\lambda)\left(|e^{i\theta_{0}}|+2(|e^{i\theta_{1}}|+\dots+|e^{i\theta_{n-1}}|)+|e^{i\theta_{n}}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\lambda)2n.

Thus,
C
is rectifiable for a finite
\lambda
.

Following the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
, we have
\Re(F^{\prime}_{\zeta}(z))\geq 1-|z|>0
,
z\in\mathbb{D}
for
F_{\zeta}(z)\in\Omega
. Thus, for
F_{\zeta}(z)\in\Omega(\lambda)
,
\Re(F^{\prime}_{\zeta}(z))>0
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
if
\lambda\leq 1/2
. Therefore by the association (Theorem
2.1
) and from the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 3]
, it is concluded that
F_{\zeta}(z)
is univalent on the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
. Now, let’s consider the points,
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
and let
f(z_{1})=f(z_{2})
. The later implies

h(z_{1})-h(z_{2})=\overline{g(z_{1})-g(z_{2})}.

Case I
: Let
h(z_{1})=h(z_{2})
. Then
g(z_{1})=g(z_{2})
, and since
F_{\zeta}(z)
is univalent,
z_{1}=z_{2}
.

Case II
: Let
h(z_{1})\neq h(z_{2})
, and let
\theta=\arg\{h(z_{1})-h(z_{2})\}\in[0,2\pi)
. Then,

e^{-i\theta}\left(h(z_{1})-h(z_{2})\right)=\overline{e^{i\theta}\left(g(z_{1})-g(z_{2})\right)}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Taking conjugate both sides, we obtain

\left(h(z_{1})-h(z_{2})\right)=e^{2i\theta}\left(g(z_{1})-g(z_{2})\right),

that implies
F_{\zeta}(z_{1})=F_{\zeta}(z_{2})
, where
\zeta=e^{2i\theta}
. Hence,
z_{1}=z_{2}
. Thus,
f(z)
is univalent on the boundary of
\mathbb{D}
. These all complete the proof.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

From (
2.8
) of Theorem
2.7
and for
\lambda\leq 1/2
, it can be seen that functions in
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
map
\mathbb{D}
onto a domain which is contained in a disk of radius
2
with center at the origin. Further, the area
f(\mathbb{D})
is bounded by
4n
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
is finite.

2.4.
Producing conditions of belongingness to
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)

Here, we produce conditions for functions to belong the class
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
whose co-analytic parts include Gaussian hypergeometric functions, defined by

(2.9)

_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=F(a,b;c;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{n!\,(c)_{n}}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

for
a,\,b,\,c\in\mathbb{C}
, and
c\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}\cup\{0\}
. Here,
(x)_{n}=x(x+1)(x+2)\dots(x+n-1),

n\in\mathbb{N}
is the well-known Pochhammer symbol, with
(x)_{0}=1
. The series (
2.9
) converges absolutely in
\mathbb{D}
, and converges on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
if
\Re(c-a-b)>0
. The well-known Gauss formula
[
16
]
is defined by

(2.10)

F(a,b;c;1)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{n!\,(c)_{n}}=\dfrac{\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c-a-b)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)},

for
\Re(c-a-b)>0
and the following lemma are useful for our next result.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
17
]

For any
a,\,b,\,c\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, if
c-a-b-1>0
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(n+1)(a)_{n}(b_{n})}{n!\,(c)_{n}}=\left(\dfrac{ab}{c-a-b-1}+1\right)F(a,b;c;1).

Theorem 2.8
.

For any
a,\,b,\,c\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\eta\in\mathbb{D}
, satisfying
c-a-b-1>0
, the following results hold.

(a)

f_{4}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, if

(2.11)

F(a,b;c;1)<\dfrac{\lambda}{|\eta|}.

(b)

f_{5}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, if

(2.12)

\left(\dfrac{ab}{c-a-b-1}\right)F(a,b;c;1)<\dfrac{\lambda}{|\eta|}.

(c)

f_{6}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, if

(2.13)

\left(\dfrac{ab}{c-a-b-1}+1\right)F(a,b;c;1)<\dfrac{\lambda}{|\eta|}.

Here,

f_{4}(z)=z+\overline{\eta z\int_{0}^{z}F(a,b;c;\tau)d\tau},\;f_{5}(z)=z+\overline{\eta z\left(F(a,b;c;z)-1\right)},\;f_{6}(z)=z+\overline{\eta z^{2}F(a,b;c;z)}.

Proof.

(a)

Let
f_{4}(z)=z+\overline{\eta z\int_{0}^{z}F(a,b;c;\tau)d\tau}=z+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}I_{n}\,z^{n}}
, where

I_{n}=\eta\,\dfrac{(a)_{n-2}(b)_{n-2}}{(c)_{n-2}(n-1)!},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}.

Now

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|I_{n}|

\displaystyle=|\eta|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a)_{n-2}(b)_{n-2}}{(c)_{n-2}(n-2)!}

\displaystyle=|\eta|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{n!\,(c)_{n}}

\displaystyle=|\eta|\,F(a,b;c;1)<\lambda,

if (
2.11
) holds true. Therefore, from Theorem
2.3
, we conclude that
f_{4}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
.

(b)

Now let
f_{5}(z)=z+\overline{\eta z\left(F(a,b;c;z)-1\right)}=z+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}J_{n}\,z^{n}}
, where

J_{n}=\eta\,\dfrac{(a)_{n-1}(b)_{n-1}}{(c)_{n-1}(n-1)!},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}.

Now

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|J_{n}|

\displaystyle=|\eta|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a)_{n-1}(b)_{n-1}}{(c)_{n-1}(n-2)!}

\displaystyle=|\eta|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a)_{n+1}(b)_{n+1}}{n!\,(c)_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=|\eta|\,\dfrac{ab}{c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(a+1)_{n}(b+1)_{n}}{n!\,(c+1)_{n}}

\displaystyle=|\eta|\,\dfrac{ab}{c}\,\dfrac{\Gamma(c+1)\Gamma(c-a-b-1)}{\Gamma(c-a)\Gamma(c-b)}

\displaystyle=|\eta|\left(\dfrac{ab}{c-a-b-1}\right)F(a,b;c;1)<\lambda,

if (
2.12
) holds true. Therefore, from Theorem
2.3
, we conclude that
f_{5}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
.

(c)

Further, let
f_{6}(z)=z+\overline{\eta z^{2}F(a,b;c;z)}=z+\overline{\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}H_{n}\,z^{n}}
, where

H_{n}=\eta\,\dfrac{(a)_{n-2}(b)_{n-2}}{(c)_{n-2}(n-2)!},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|H_{n}|

\displaystyle=|\eta|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\dfrac{(n-1)(a)_{n-2}(b)_{n-2}}{(c)_{n-2}(n-2)!}

\displaystyle=|\eta|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(n+1)(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{n!\,(c)_{n}}

\displaystyle=|\eta|\left(\dfrac{ab}{c-a-b-1}+1\right)F(a,b;c;1)<\lambda,

if (
2.13
) holds true. Therefore, from Theorem
2.3
, we conclude that
f_{6}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
.

∎

Now we produce conditions for harmonic univalent polynomials to belong the class
\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
.

Theorem 2.9
.

For any
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, the following results hold

(a)

p_{1}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, if

(2.14)

\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c+2s)<\lambda\left(\Gamma(c+m)\right)^{2}.

(b)

p_{2}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, if

(2.15)

s^{2}\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c+2s)<\lambda(c+2s-1)\left(\Gamma(c+m)\right)^{2}.

(c)

p_{3}(z)\in\Omega_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}(\lambda)
, if

(2.16)

(c+s^{2}+2s-1)\Gamma(c)\Gamma(c+2s)<\lambda(c+2s-1)\left(\Gamma(c+m)\right)^{2}.

Here,
p_{1}(z),\,p_{2}(z),
and
p_{3}(z)
are harmonic univalent polynomials, given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{r@{\;}l}p_{1}(z)&=z+\overline{\eta\sum_{n=0}^{s}\begin{pmatrix}s\\
n\end{pmatrix}\dfrac{(s-n+1)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}\,\dfrac{z^{n+2}}{n+1}},\\
p_{2}(z)&=z+\overline{\eta\sum_{n=0}^{s}\begin{pmatrix}s\\
n\end{pmatrix}\dfrac{(s-n+1)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}\,z^{n+1}},\\
p_{3}(z)&=z+\overline{\eta\sum_{n=0}^{s}\begin{pmatrix}s\\
n\end{pmatrix}\dfrac{(s-n+1)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}\,z^{n+2}}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

For any
\mu\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}\cap\{0\}
, we can write

\dfrac{(-1)^{n}(-\mu)_{n}}{n!}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu\\
n\end{pmatrix}=\dfrac{\Gamma(\mu+1)}{n!\Gamma(\mu-n+1)}.

But when this
\mu=s\in\mathbb{N}
, where
s\geq n
, we can write

(-s)_{n}=\dfrac{(-1)^{n}s!}{(s-n)!}.

Using this relation on
f_{4}(z)
,
f_{5}(z)
, and
f_{6}(z)
of Theorem
2.8
, we obtain the harmonic univalent polynomials
p_{1}(z),\,p_{2}(z),
and
p_{3}(z)
, respectively as defined on (
2.9
). Now putting
a=b=-s
in the conditions (
2.11
), (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), we obtain respective belongingness conditions (
2.14
), (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), of
p_{1}(z),\,p_{2}(z),
and
p_{3}(z)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Designing Stochastic Channels
Matthew A. Graydon, Joshua Skanes-Norman, Joel J. Wallman

Abstract
Stochastic channels are ubiquitous in the field of quantum information because they are simple and easy to analyze. In particular, Pauli channels and depolarizing channels are widely studied because they can be efficiently simulated in many relevant quantum circuits. Despite their wide use, the properties of general stochastic channels have received little attention. In this paper, we prove that the diamond distance of a general stochastic channel from the identity coincides with its process infidelity to the identity. We demonstrate with an explicit example that there exist multi-qubit stochastic channels that are not unital. We then discuss the relationship between unitary 1-designs and stochastic channels. We prove that the twirl of an arbitrary quantum channel by a unitary 1-design is always a stochastic channel. However, unlike with unitary 2-designs, the twirled channel depends upon the choice of unitary 1-design. Moreover, we prove by example that there exist stochastic channels that cannot be obtained by twirling a quantum channel by a unitary 1-design.

Introduction

Quantum computers hold tremendous promise, but noise severely limits the reliability of quantum devices in the noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) era
Preskill 2018
. Quantum error correction and noise mitigation methods hinge on assumptions about the noise in question
Cao et al. 2021
;
Carignan-Dugas 2019

A stochastic channel is a completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP) map admitting a set of Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonal Kraus operators containing a strictly positive multiple of the identity
Wallman 2015

Operationally, a stochastic channel corresponds to an error occurring with some probability
p\in[0,1)
. Some nice examples are depolarizing channels, decoherence channels, multi-qubit Pauli channels and qudit Weyl-Heisenberg channels

. In this paper, we identify significant operational properties common to all stochastic channels.

The diamond distance to the identity

However, we prove that the diamond distance of a stochastic channel is its process infidelity to the identity (
theorem

1

;

;

II
Stochastic Channels

We begin by defining stochastic channels, which are informally a noiseless process probabilistically mixed with orthogonal errors.
A formal definition of a stochastic channel (in particular, the notion of orthogonal channels) is stated most naturally in terms of the Choi state, which we now review.
Let
\mathpzc{H}
be a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space and
\mathfrak{B}(\mathpzc{H})
be the set of bounded complex-linear maps from
\mathpzc{H}
to itself equipped with the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product,

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle A|B\rangle\!\rangle\equiv\tr A^{\dagger}B\quad\forall A,B\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathpzc{H}).

(1)

Then
\mathfrak{B}(\mathpzc{H})
is itself a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space and so
\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{B}(\mathpzc{H}))
is well-defined.
Canonically,
\mathpzc{H}
is the set of complex vectors, that is,
\mathpzc{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d}
for some finite positive integer
d
, and
\mathfrak{B}(\mathpzc{H})
are
d\times d
complex matrices.
We can represent the elements of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
as vectors using the column-stacking vectorization map
col
, and then elements
\Phi
of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))
by their
Choi-Jamiołkowski state
(hereafter their Choi state),

\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)\equiv\frac{1}{d}\Phi\otimes\text{id}_{d}\left(\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d})\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d})^{\dagger}\right),

(2)

where
\mathds{1}_{d}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
is the identity operator and
\text{id}_{d}
is the identity element of
\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))
.
Applying a singular value decomposition to
\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)
and using the fact that the vectorization map is invertible, we find that there exist suitable vectors

and matrices

\displaystyle\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}}l_{k}r_{k}^{\dagger}\equiv\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}}\text{col}(L_{k}^{\text{T}})\text{col}(R_{k}^{\text{T}})^{\dagger}\text{.}

(3)

Using the vectorization identity

\text{col}(ABC)=(C^{\text{T}}\!\otimes\!A)\text{col}(B)\quad\forall A,B,C\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}),

(4)

we thus see that we can write
\Phi
in a (non-unique) Kraus form as

\displaystyle\Phi(X)=\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}}L_{k}XR_{k}^{\dagger}.

(5)

Using the vectorization identity

\tr A^{\dagger}B=\text{col}(A)^{\dagger}\text{col}(B),

(6)

the orthogonality of the vectors in the singular value decomposition implies that for all
j\neq k
, we have

\displaystyle\tr L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{k}=\tr R_{j}^{\dagger}R_{k}=0.

(7)

For any CPTP map, the Choi state is a positive matrix, so that
L_{k}=R_{k}
for all

\displaystyle\Phi=\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}}\text{Ad}_{L_{k}}

(8)

where for any matrix

we define

\text{Ad}_{A}:\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})::x\longmapsto AxA^{\dagger}\text{.}

(9)

Using the cyclic invariance of the trace, the trace preservation condition on a CPTP map is

\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}}L_{k}^{\dagger}L_{k}=\mathds{1}_{d}\text{.}

(10)

A
stochastic channel
is a CPTP map
\Phi\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))
such that for some
0<\lambda\leq 1

\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d})=\lambda\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d})\text{.}

(11)

Put otherwise, a stochastic channel is a CPTP map whose set of canonical Kraus operators contains a strictly positive multiple of the identity. A stochastic channel is therefore a map

\Phi:\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\longrightarrow\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})::x\longmapsto\lambda x+(1-\lambda)\Phi^{\perp}(x)\text{,}

(12)

where
\Phi^{\perp}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))
is an error channel that is orthogonal to the identity, that is

\mathpzc{J}(\Phi^{\perp})\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d})=0\text{.}

(13)

The set of stochastic channels is manifestly convex via
eq.

12
.
The class of stochastic channels contains channels of known types. A mixed unitary channel is a quantum channel such that its Kraus decomposition may be taken in terms of a set of appropriately subnormalized unitary matrices. A mixed unitary channel is therefore always unital, which is to say that it leaves the maximally mixed state invariant. Indeed, some quantum channels might not appear to be mixed unitary at first sight. It turns out that question is actually NP-Hard
Lee and Watrous 2020
. We proceed now by way of an example that our definition of stochastic channels encompasses channels outside the mixed unitary class. Define the following
4\times 4
matrices with
\lambda\in(0,1]

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle\equiv\sqrt{\lambda}\mathds{1}_{2}\otimes\mathds{1}_{2}\text{,}

(14)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\sqrt{1-\lambda}\sigma_{z}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\text{,}

(15)

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle\equiv\sqrt{1-\lambda}\sigma_{z}\otimes|0\rangle\!\langle 1|\text{.}

(16)

where
\sigma_{z}
is the usual Pauli Z matrix. Defining the quantum channel

\displaystyle\Phi=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\text{Ad}_{A_{k}},

(17)

one can easily verify that
\Phi
is a stochastic quantum channel. One can check, furthermore, that
\Phi(\mathds{1}_{4})=\lambda\mathds{1}_{4}+(1-\lambda)\mathds{1}_{2}\otimes 2|0\rangle\langle 0|\neq\mathds{1}_{4}
.
\Phi
prepares a rank-2 state with probability
(1-\lambda)
, and with probability
\lambda
the stochastic channel
\Phi
is noiseless.
This construction generalizes to all even dimensions larger than 2.
In
appendix

A
we prove that all stochastic qubit channels are unital.
We leave open the question of whether or not all stochastic channels are unital for
d=3
.

III
Faithful Diamonds

In this section, we prove that stochastic channels are such that their diamond distance equals their process infidelity. We recall that the
trace norm
is
\|\cdot\|_{1}:\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}::X\longmapsto\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{X^{\dagger}X}
. The
induced trace norm
on superoperators is defined via

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{1}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

(18)

\displaystyle::

\displaystyle\Gamma\longmapsto\text{max}\left\{\|\Phi(X)\|_{1}\;\big|\;\|X\|_{1}\leq 1\right\}\text{.}

The completely bounded trace norm

and defined as follows

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}\big(\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\big)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

(19)

\displaystyle::

\displaystyle\Gamma\longmapsto\|\Gamma\otimes\text{id}_{d}\|_{1}\text{.}

The diamond norm induces for any quantum channel
\Phi

r_{\diamond}(\Phi)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\|\Phi-\text{id}_{d}\|_{\diamond}\text{.}

(20)

The

of
\Phi
is

\displaystyle F_{e}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\mathpzc{J}(\text{id}_{d})^{\dagger}\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=\tr\left(\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d})\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d})^{\dagger}\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)\right)

(22)

\displaystyle=\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d})^{\dagger}\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)\text{col}(\mathds{1}_{d}).

(23)

The

is
Fuchs and Van
De Graaf 1999

r(\Phi)\equiv 1-F_{e}(\Phi)\text{.}

(24)

The following theorem generalizes a similar result for generalized Pauli channels

. Note that, in our more general case of interest, the canonical Kraus operators for an arbitrary stochastic channel need not be unitary.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Phi\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))
be a stochastic quantum channel. Then

r_{\diamond}(\Phi)=r(\Phi)\text{.}

(25)

Proof.

For any CPTP map, we have the bound
Wallman and Flammia 2014

\displaystyle r_{\diamond}(\Phi)\geq r(\Phi).

(26)

Therefore we need only prove that for a stochastic map
\Phi
,

\displaystyle r_{\diamond}(\Phi)\leq r(\Phi).

(27)

To prove this, recall that by the definition of a stochastic channel we have

\displaystyle\Phi=\sum_{k}\text{Ad}_{B_{k}}

(28)

with
B_{1}=\sqrt{\lambda}\mathds{1}_{d}
for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
By the triangle inequality, we therefore have

\displaystyle\|\Phi-\text{id}_{d}\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\|\text{id}_{d}\|_{\diamond}+\|\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\text{Ad}_{B_{k}}\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\lambda+\|\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\text{Ad}_{B_{k}}\|_{\diamond}

(29)

where we have used the fact that
\|\text{id}_{d}\|_{\diamond}=1
.
Now from the definition of the diamond norm and the triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\text{Ad}_{B_{k}}\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\rho}\|\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\text{Ad}_{B_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{d}}(\rho)\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\rho}\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\|\text{Ad}_{B_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{d}}(\rho)\|_{1}.

(30)

For any operator
B
and any positive operator

,

is positive and so

Therefore using

10
, we find that for any CPTP map
\Phi
,

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\text{Ad}_{B_{k}}\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\rho}\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\tr(B_{k}^{\dagger}B_{k}\otimes\mathds{1}_{d})\rho

\displaystyle=\sup_{\rho}\tr\big((\mathds{1}_{d}-B_{1}^{\dagger}B_{1})\otimes\mathds{1}_{d}\big)\rho.

(31)

For a stochastic channel,

and so

\displaystyle\|\sum_{k=2}^{d^{2}}\text{Ad}_{B_{k}}\|_{\diamond}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\lambda.

(32)

We thus have

\displaystyle r_{\diamond}(\Phi)=1-\lambda.

(33)

∎

IV
Twirling

V
Conclusion

We have considered properties of the class of stochastic channels. We proved that the diamond distance of a stochastic channel is its process infidelity (
theorem

1
). We illuminated the relationship between stochastic channels and unitary 1-designs. In general, we pointed out that channel twirling commutes with the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism (
lemma

2
). We then demonstrated a way to access stochastic channels. The twirl of a quantum channel over a unitary 1-design is a stochastic channel (
theorem

3
). Next, we proved that twirling over unitary 1-designs results in unital channels (
theorem

4
.) We provided an example of a stochastic channel that is not unital. In
appendix

A
, we prove that all qubit stochastic channels are unital (
theorem

5
).

There are some potential avenues for future research into designing stochastic channels. One open question is whether or not a quantum channel
\Phi
exists outside the class of stochastic channels but with
r_{\diamond}(\Phi)=r(\Phi)
. Another open question is whether or not every qutrit stochastic channel is a mixed unitary channel. Future work might also explore effects obtained via twirling over different unitary 1-designs. We observe from our proof of
theorem

3
that twirling a quantum channel over any unitary 1-design results in a stochastic channel whose canonical Kraus operators can depend on the unitary 1-design in question. For instance, consider a group unitary operator basis
\mathsf{B}
and then its conjugation via any element of the unitary group,
\text{Ad}_{U}(\mathsf{B})
. The twirl with respect to the latter is a rotated version of the former.
The difference between the two twirled channels is subtle and can be missed, e.g., in
Wallman and Emerson 2016
, however, since both channels have the same diamond distance and process fidelity, the difference is typically unimportant.

Finally, the set of stochastic channels is convex.
A natural avenue for future research is to determine the shape of this convex set.

\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\gamma_{1}|^{2}+|\gamma_{2}|^{2}=1
.
We then have

\displaystyle\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{1}\\
-\overline{\gamma_{2}}\\
\gamma_{2}\\
\overline{\gamma_{1}}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\gamma_{1}}\\
\overline{\gamma_{2}}\\
-\gamma_{2}\\
\gamma_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

(47)

Subtracting the conjugate of the fourth equation and using the fact that the parameters of
\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)
are real gives

\displaystyle-\kappa_{z}\gamma_{1}=(\kappa_{x}+i\kappa_{y})\gamma_{2}.

(48)

Similarly, adding the conjugate of the second equation to the third equation and using the fact that the parameters of
\mathpzc{J}(\Phi)
are real gives

\displaystyle\kappa_{z}\gamma_{2}=(\kappa_{x}-i\kappa_{y})\gamma_{1}.

(49)

If
\gamma_{1}=0
then
eq.

48
yields
\kappa_{x}=-i\kappa_{y}
, whence by the reality of
\kappa_{x}
and
\kappa_{y}
we have
\kappa_{x}=\kappa_{y}=0
and then by
eq.

49
we conclude that
\kappa_{z}=0
.
If instead
\gamma_{2}=0
then we reach the same conclusion. If both
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are nonzero then we multiply
eq.

48
and
eq.

49
to find

\displaystyle-\kappa_{z}^{2}\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=(\kappa_{x}-i\kappa_{y})\gamma_{1}(\kappa_{x}+i\kappa_{y})\gamma_{2}

\displaystyle=(\kappa_{x}^{2}+\kappa_{y}^{2})\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2},

(50)

and so we must have
\kappa_{x}=\kappa_{y}=\kappa_{z}=0
and we conclude again that
\Xi
is unital.
∎

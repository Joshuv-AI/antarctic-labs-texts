Symplectic cohomology of compound Du Val singularities
Jonny Evans Yankı Lekili

Abstract
We compute symplectic cohomology for Milnor fibres of certain compound Du Val singularities that admit small resolution by using homological mirror symmetry. Our computations suggest a new conjecture that the existence of a small resolution has strong implications for the symplectic cohomology and conversely. We also use our computations to give a contact invariant of the link of the singularities and thereby distinguish many contact structures on connected sums of S 2 × S 3 S^{2}\times S^{3} .

1.2
Compound Du Val (cDV) singularities

It is a theorem of Reid
[
39
, Theorem 1.1]
that the Gorenstein
terminal 3-fold singularities are precisely the isolated
compound
Du Val (cDV) singularities
. These are hypersurface singularities
which (in suitable local analytic coordinates
(w,x,y,z)
) are cut
out by an equation of the form

f(x,y,z)+wg(x,y,z,w)=0

where
f
is one of the following
polynomials:

\displaystyle A_{\ell}:

\displaystyle\quad x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{\ell+1},

\displaystyle D_{\ell}:

\displaystyle\quad x^{2}+y(z^{2}+y^{\ell-2}),

\displaystyle E_{6}:

\displaystyle\quad x^{2}+y^{3}+z^{4},

\displaystyle E_{7}:

\displaystyle\quad x^{2}+y(y^{2}+z^{3}),

\displaystyle E_{8}:

\displaystyle\quad x^{2}+y^{3}+z^{5}.

The
w=0
hyperplane section has an ADE singularity at
0

\Gamma
is the ADE type of this hyperplane section, we refer to the
3-fold singularity as a
compound
\Gamma

As we have explained in Theorem
1.1
, the links of these
singularities are index positive and so we can use
\operatorname{SH}^{*}
of the
Milnor fibre for
*<0
as a contact invariant.

Remark 1.2
.

Observe that if we define
B\subset\operatorname{Link}(0)
to be the

\{w=0\}\cap\operatorname{Link}(0)
then we get a Milnor open

with binding

The page is a copy of the corresponding 4-dimensional ADE Milnor

Consider the family of

If

From an algebro-geometric perspective, the singularities

Recall that a

Inspired by this example, we record an optimistic conjecture, which

Conjecture 1.4
.

Suppose that
P\in X
is a cDV singularity and let
V
be the
Milnor fibre of the singularity. Then
P\in X
admits a small
resolution such that the exceptional set has
\ell

components if and only if
\operatorname{SH}^{*}(V;{\mathbb{C}})
has rank
\ell

negative degree.

Remark 1.5
.

In this paper, we have focused on providing evidence for one
direction of this conjecture: that the existence of a small
resolution constrains the symplectic cohomology. The converse is
plausible: we have calculated many examples and found no
counterexample. This would give an a priori way of detecting whether
a cDV singularity admits a small resolution just by knowing its
link.

Remark 1.6
.

If
P\in X
admits a small resolution whose exceptional set has

\ell
irreducible components then the link is diffeomorphic to

\sharp_{\ell}(S^{2}\times S^{3})
. Small resolutions can be
constructed by thinking of the 3-fold as a 1-parameter deformation
of an ADE singularity. This gives a classifying map from the disc to
the versal deformation space of the ADE singularity such that the
3-fold is the pullback along the classifying map of the versal
family. Brieskorn
[
9
]
, Tjurina
[
44
]
and
Pinkham
[
37
]
constructed branched coverings of the versal
ADE deformation space (branched over the discriminant locus) such
that the pullback of the versal family to the branched covering
admits a simultaneous (partial) resolution. More precisely, the
fundamental group of the complement of the discriminant locus is the
ADE Artin braid group; Brieskorn and Tyurina constructed the
branched covering corresponding to the kernel of the homomorphism to
the ADE Weyl group and found a full simultaneous resolution, while
Pinkham constructed simultaneous partial resolutions for
intermediate covering spaces. For a specific 3-fold, if the
classifying map from the disc lifts (in the sense of algebraic
topology) to one of these branched covers, then you get a small
resolution by pulling back the simultaneous partial resolution of
the versal family. In particular, the existence of a small
resolution can be read off from the monodromy of the Milnor open
book mentioned in Remark
1.2
(which is the element
of the fundamental group of the ADE Artin braid group represented by
the boundary of the disc under the classifying map).

Remark 1.7
.

admits a small

because the monodromy of the Milnor open book is stable under

Symplectic cohomology for invertible polynomials

2.1
Symplectic cohomology

Let
V
be a Liouville manifold with
c_{1}(V)=0
. Associated to
V
we
can define an invariant
\operatorname{SH}^{*}(V)
called the symplectic cohomology of

V
. Symplectic cohomology was introduced by Cieliebak, Floer, Hofer

[
23
,
14
,
11
]
and Viterbo
[
49
]
. An excellent exposition
can be found in
[
41
]
. More recent results can be learned from

[
12
]
. See also
[
28
, Sec 2.1]
for a fast
review of our sign and grading conventions. In particular, our
conventions are cohomological and the unit lives in degree zero!

Briefly,
\operatorname{SH}^{*}(V)
is an algebra over the homology operad of framed
little discs over an arbitrary commutative ring
\mathbf{k}
(in this
paper
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{C})
. In particular, it has a (graded)
commutative product, a Gerstenhaber bracket
[\,,\,]
(i.e a Lie
bracket of degree
-1
), and a Batalin-Vilkovisky operator
\Delta

(i.e. a degree
-1
operator whose Hochschild coboundary is the
bracket).

In general, symplectic cohomology is rather difficult to compute
explicitly. A fruitful approach to do such computations goes via the
open string A-model. Namely, we have an isomorphism

\operatorname{SH}^{*}(V)\simeq\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\mathcal{W}(V))

where
\mathcal{W}(V)
is
the wrapped Fukaya category of
V
. An early version of this result
based on Legendrian surgery is due to Bourgeois-Ekholm-Eliashberg
(
[
6
]
, elaborated in
[
13
]
) which concerned Hochschild
homology; a definitive version based on duality appeared in

[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also the more recent

[
10
]
).

On the other hand, even if one achieved a good understanding of

\mathcal{W}(V)
, in general, it is still a difficult algebraic
problem to compute Hochschild cohomology of
A_{\infty}
categories.

In
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
, a method to compute symplectic cohomology
for certain Milnor fibres was given based on the homological mirror
symmetry conjecture for
invertible polynomials
.

Definition 2.1
.

To an
(n+1)

(n+1)
integer matrix

determinant, we associate the polynomial

{\bf w}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}x_{j}^{a_{ij}}.

We
write
\check{{\bf w}}
for the polynomial associated to

(the

Berglund-Hübsch mirror

, see
[
4
]
).

An invertible polynomial is weighted homogeneous, that is there is a
uniquely determined weight system
(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n+1};h)
satisfying

\gcd(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n+1},h)=1

. In this

h-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}d_{i}=:d_{0}<0

In fact, there is a finite extension
\Gamma_{\bf w}
of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
acting
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}

\Gamma_{\bf w}:=\left\{(t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n+2}\ :\ \prod_{j=1}^{n+1}t_{j}^{a_{ij}}=t_{0}t_{1}\cdots t_{n+1},\ i=1,\ldots,n+1\right\},

2.3
Using mirror symmetry to compute symplectic cohomology

Pick a Morsification of
\check{{\bf w}}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}(\check{{\bf w}})
denote
the Fukaya-Seidel category of the Morsification, let

V:=\check{{\bf w}}^{-1}(1)
denote the Milnor fibre, and let
{\mathcal{W}}(V)

(respectively
{\mathcal{F}}(V)

. Choose a collection of vanishing paths for
the Morsification and let
\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{K}
(respectively

S_{1},\ldots,S_{K}
) be the corresponding vanishing thimbles
(respectively vanishing cycles). Let

{\mathcal{A}}=\operatorname{end}_{{\mathcal{F}}(\check{{\bf w}})}\left(\bigoplus_{i}\Delta_{i}\right)

. Let

A=H({\mathcal{A}})
and

denote the cohomology algebras of

{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\cal B}
(considered as
A_{\infty}
-algebras with zero

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume that
\operatorname{HH}^{2}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))=0
, that

d_{0}\neq 0
, and either Conjecture
2.2
or Conjecture

2.3

holds. Then

\operatorname{SH}^{*}(V)\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))

(2.1)

as Gerstenhaber
algebras.

In the next section, we give a formula to compute

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}))
.

Proof that Conjecture
2.2
implies Equation
(
2.1
).

If
d_{0}\neq 0
then
[
29
, Theorem 6.2]
implies that the
inclusion of categories
{\cal B}\to{\mathcal{W}}(V)
induces an isomorphism on
Hochschild cohomology. Since this map comes from a functor, it is a
morphism of Gerstenhaber algebras. Ganatra
[
20
, Theorem
1.1]
shows that
\operatorname{SH}^{*}(V)\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}({\mathcal{W}}(V))
as
Gerstenhaber algebras. Therefore, we need to
show

\operatorname{HH}^{*}({\cal B})\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w})).

(2.2)

As a first
step, we calculate
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(B)
, where
B=H({\cal B})
is the
cohomology algebra of
{\cal B}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

We have
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(B)\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))

as Gerstenhaber algebras.

Proof.

We continue to write
{\mathcal{A}}
for the endomorphism

A_{\infty}
-algebra of the vanishing thimbles and
A
for
its cohomology. Recall that the
trivial extension
algebra

T_{n}(A)
is defined
to be
A\oplus A^{\vee}[-n]
with the product

(a,b)(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(aa^{\prime},ab^{\prime}+a^{\prime}b)
. For any Lefschetz fibration
with
(n+1)
-(complex-)dimensional total space with
n>0
,
the Floer cohomology algebra
B=H({\cal B})
of the vanishing
cycles is an extension of
A^{\vee}[-n]
by
A
, where

A
is the directed Fukaya-Seidel Floer cohomology algebra
for the vanishing thimbles
[
42
, Equation 4.1 and
Proposition 5.1]
. If
A
is supported
in degree zero (as asserted by Conjecture 2.2) then this is
the trivial extension
T_{n}(A)
: the products in
B

which are not determined by the
A
-module structure of

A^{\vee}[-n]
vanish for degree reasons. To prove the
lemma, it therefore suffices to show that

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(T_{n}(A))\simeq\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))
.

Let
\mathbf{k}
be the semisimple ring

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{K}{\mathbb{C}}e_{i}
where
e_{i}\in A
is the
identity element of
HF(\Delta_{i},\Delta_{i})
. The
projection
T_{n}(A)\to A\to\mathbf{k}
makes
\mathbf{k}
into an

T_{n}(A)
-module (augmentation). Keller
[
26
, Section
4.1]
defines a Koszul-dual algebra called the

n
-Calabi-Yau completion

\Pi_{n}(A)\cong\operatorname{RHom}_{T_{n}(A)}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k})
. This is Koszul-dual
in the sense that
\mathbf{k}
is a
(T_{n}(A),\Pi_{n}(A))
-bimodule
and
T_{n}(A)\cong\operatorname{RHom}_{\Pi_{n}(A)}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k})
. Koszul duality
ensures that we can apply
[
25
, Theorem in Section
3.2]
to deduce that the Hochschild cohomologies

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(T_{n}(A))
and
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\Pi_{n}(A))
are isomorphic as
Gerstenhaber algebras. For any algebra
C
(more generally

A_{\infty}
-algebra),

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(C)\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{perf}(C))
, so

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(B)\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(T_{n}(A))\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\Pi_{n}(A))\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{perf}(\Pi_{n}(A))),

and it suffices to prove
that

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{perf}(\Pi_{n}(A)))=\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))
. In fact, we will show
a stronger result: that

\operatorname{perf}(\Pi_{n}(A))\simeq\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}).

To see this stronger
result, recall that Keller’s construction of

\Pi_{n}(\cdot)
works more generally when the input is a
dg-algebra or category, and satisfies

[
30
, Eq. (2.2)]

\operatorname{perf}(\Pi_{n}(A))\simeq\Pi_{n}(\operatorname{perf}(A)).

It was
shown in
[
30
]
(Eq. (1.7) for the statement and
Section 4 for the proof) that

\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w})\simeq\Pi_{n}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+1},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))

(2.3)

and Conjecture
2.2
is the assumption that

\operatorname{perf}(A)\simeq\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+1},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w})

so

\operatorname{perf}(\Pi_{n}(A))\simeq\Pi_{n}(\operatorname{perf}(A))\simeq\Pi_{n}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathcal{A}}^{n+1},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))\simeq\operatorname{mf}({\mathcal{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}),

as required.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

The
A_{\infty}
-algebra
{\cal B}
is quasi-isomorphic to its
cohomology algebra
B
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
,

\operatorname{HH}^{2}(B)\cong\operatorname{HH}^{2}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))
, which
vanishes by assumption, so

is intrinsically formal, and
hence quasi-isomorphic to

.
∎

Together, these two lemmas show that

\operatorname{HH}^{*}({\cal B})\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))
as

2.2
), so
Equation (
2.1
) follows.
∎

Proof that Conjecture
2.3
implies Equation
(
2.1
).

We will show in Theorem
2.15
below that if

\operatorname{HH}^{2}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))=0
then we can make a

\Gamma_{{\bf w}}
-equivariant formal change of coordinates along the

such that the pullback of

in these new coordinates equals

. If we
can make such a formal change of coordinates, it follows from

[

that

\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}+x_{0}\cdots x_{n+1})
is quasi-equivalent to

\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w})
, so Conjecture
2.3

implies

\operatorname{HH}^{*}({\mathcal{W}}(V))\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))

as

[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
,

\operatorname{HH}^{*}({\mathcal{W}}(V))\cong\operatorname{SH}^{*}(V)

2.1
) follows.
∎

2.4
Calculating
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}))

There is a formula for
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}))

which expresses it as a sum of
\Gamma_{\bf w}
-invariant pieces of
twisted Koszul cohomologies; this formula appeared in

[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
, where its context and history are
discussed. It is also explained and used in
[
29
, Theorem
3.1]
and
[
30
, Section 5.1]
. We now briefly
describe how to perform calculations in practice with this
formula; Theorem
2.14
below summarises the
answer and its proof explains how our notation fits with the
notation from
[
30
]
. We will use the notation from this
section in our calculations in Section
3
.

Definition 2.9
.

Define the character

\chi\colon\Gamma_{\bf w}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m},\quad\chi(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n+1})=t_{0}\cdots t_{n+1}.

Its kernel
\operatorname{ker}\chi
is the finite group

\operatorname{ker}\chi=\left\{(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n+1})\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n+2}:\ \prod_{j=1}^{n+1}t_{j}^{a_{ij}}=1,\ t_{0}=t_{1}^{-1}\cdots t_{n+1}^{-1}\right\}.

Definition 2.10
.

Given an element
\gamma\in\operatorname{ker}\chi
, let

\{1,\ldots,{n+1}\}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\cup\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{n+1-k}\}

be
the partition for which each
x_{i_{m}}
is fixed under the action
of
\gamma
and each
x_{j_{m}}
is not fixed under the action of

\gamma
. Let
J_{\gamma}
be a monomial basis for the Jacobian
ring of
{\bf w}|_{x_{j_{1}}=\cdots=x_{j_{n+1-k}}=0}
.

Definition 2.11
.

The set
M_{\gamma}
of
\gamma
-monomials
is the union

M_{\gamma}=A_{\gamma}\cup B_{\gamma}\cup C_{\gamma}
where

\displaystyle A_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\{x_{0}^{\beta}px_{j_{1}}^{\vee}\cdots x_{j_{n+1-k}}^{\vee}\ :\ p\in J_{\gamma},\ \beta=0,1,2,\ldots\}&\mbox{ if }x_{0}\mbox{ is fixed by }\gamma\\
\emptyset&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

\displaystyle B_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\{x_{0}^{\beta}px_{0}^{\vee}x_{j_{1}}^{\vee}\cdots x_{j_{n+1-k}}^{\vee}\ :\ p\in J_{\gamma},\ \beta=0,1,2,\ldots\}&\mbox{ if }x_{0}\mbox{ is fixed by }\gamma\\
\emptyset&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\emptyset&\mbox{ if }x_{0}\mbox{ is fixed by }\gamma\\
\{px_{0}^{\vee}x_{j_{1}}^{\vee}\cdots x_{j_{n+1-k}}^{\vee}\ :\ p\in J_{\gamma}\}&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\zeta\colon\Gamma_{{\bf w}}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
be a character of

\Gamma_{{\bf w}}
. We say that a polynomial or formal power series

p(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1})
is
\zeta
-isotypical
if

p(g\bm{x})=\zeta(g)p(\bm{x})
for all
g\in\Gamma_{{\bf w}}
. Note
that every monomial
m
determines a character
\xi(m)
such
that
m
is
\xi(m)
-isotypical. The space of formal power
series
K:={\mathbb{C}}[\![x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1}]\!]
is therefore the
completed direct sum of its
\zeta
-isotypical summands

K=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\zeta\in\hat{\Gamma}_{{\bf w}}}K_{\zeta},\quad K_{\zeta}=\left\{p\in K\ :\ p(g\bm{x})=\zeta(g)p(\bm{x})\ \forall g\in\Gamma_{{\bf w}}\right\}.

Definition 2.13
.

Given a
\gamma
-monomial
m
, we write
b_{k}
for the total
exponent of
x_{k}
in
m
, where
x_{k}^{\vee}
contributes

-1
to
b_{k}
. The character
\xi(m)
is determined by these
exponents:

\xi(m)(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n+1})=t_{0}^{b_{0}}\cdots t_{n+1}^{b_{n+1}}.

We now assume the following. For each
\gamma\in\Gamma_{{\bf w}}

let
{\bf w}_{\gamma}
(respectively
{\bf w}^{\prime}_{\gamma}
) denote the
restriction of the polynomial
{\bf w}
to the subspace where the
unfixed variables
x_{j_{1}},\cdots,x_{j_{n-k+1}}
(respectively

x_{0},x_{j_{1}},\cdots,x_{j_{n-k+1}}
) vanish. We assume that

{\bf w}^{\prime}_{\gamma}
has an isolated singularity at the origin for all

\gamma\in\Gamma
, which is the case for all our examples.

Theorem 2.14
.

Under this assumption, the Hochschild cohomology

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))
is a direct sum of
1-dimensional contributions, one from each pair
(\gamma,m)

with
m\in M_{\gamma}
such that
\xi(m)=\chi^{\otimes u}

for some
u\in\mathbb{Z}
. In this case,
(\gamma,m)
contributes
to

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{2u+n-k+1}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }m\in A_{\gamma},

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{2u+n-k+2}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }m\in B_{\gamma},

\displaystyle\operatorname{HH}^{2u+n-k+2}

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }m\in C_{\gamma},

where
k
is the number of variables amongst

\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\}
fixed by
\gamma
.

Proof.

This is just a repackaging of
[
3
, Theorem 1.2]
, based
on the exposition in
[
30
, Section 5.1]
. We briefly
explain how to translate between our notation and the notation
of
[
30
]
. For each
\gamma
there are three kinds of
contribution to Hochschild cohomology, enumerated by

[
30
, Equations 5.5-5.7]
:

•

If
x_{0}
is not fixed by
\gamma
then the Hochschild
cohomology picks up a contribution given by
[
30
, Equation
5.5]
:

\left(\operatorname{Jac}_{{\bf w}_{\gamma}}\otimes\Lambda^{\dim N_{\gamma}}N_{\gamma}^{\vee}\right)_{\chi^{\otimes u}}

where
N_{\gamma}
is the vector space spanned by the
non-fixed variables
x_{0},x_{j_{1}},\ldots,x_{j_{n-k+1}}
,

\operatorname{Jac}
denotes the Jacobian ring, and

\chi^{\otimes u}
means taking the isotypical part. Our

\gamma
-monomials from
C_{\gamma}
form an explicit
basis of this space:
J_{\gamma}
is a basis for the
Jacobian
\operatorname{Jac}_{{\bf w}_{\gamma}}
and

x_{0}^{\vee}\otimes x_{j_{1}}^{\vee}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{j_{n-k+1}}^{\vee}
is a generator of

\Lambda^{\dim N_{\gamma}}N_{\gamma}^{\vee}
; the

\chi^{\otimes u}
subscript is precisely telling us to
restrict attention to
\gamma
-monomials with

\xi(m)=\chi^{\otimes u}
. This contributes to

\operatorname{HH}^{2u+\dim N_{\gamma}}=\operatorname{HH}^{2u+n-k+2}
.

•

If
x_{0}
is fixed by
\gamma
then there are
contributions
[
30
, Equations 5.6 and 5.7]
:

\left(\operatorname{Jac}_{{\bf w}^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0}]\otimes\Lambda^{\dim N_{\gamma}}N_{\gamma}^{\vee}\right)_{\chi^{\otimes u}},\qquad\left({\mathbb{C}}x_{0}^{\vee}\otimes\operatorname{Jac}_{{\bf w}^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[x_{0}]\otimes\Lambda^{\dim N_{\gamma}}N_{\gamma}^{\vee}\right)_{\chi^{\otimes u}}

to

\operatorname{HH}^{2u+\dim N_{\gamma}}=\operatorname{HH}^{2u+n-k+1}
and

\operatorname{HH}^{2u+\dim N_{\gamma}+1}=\operatorname{HH}^{2u+n-k+2}

respectively. Our
\gamma
-monomials of type
A_{\gamma}

and
B_{\gamma}
give bases for these vector spaces.

∎

2.5
Formal change of coordinates

Recall that there exist weights

then both

are quasihomogeneous of degree

and
\chi
-isotypical. Let
|\cdot|_{0}
be the

-valuation on
the space
K={\mathbb{C}}[\![x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1}]\!]
of formal power series,
i.e.

divides

but

does

Theorem 2.15
.

Suppose that
p_{0}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1})
is a
\chi
-isotypical formal
power series which is quasihomogeneous of degree
h
and

|p_{0}|_{0}>0
. If
\operatorname{HH}^{2}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))=0
then there is
a formal change of variables

\bm{z}=(x_{0},z_{1}(\bm{x}),\ldots,z_{n+1}(\bm{x}))
such that

{\bf w}(\bm{z})={\bf w}(\bm{x})+p_{0}(\bm{x})
.

Remark 2.16
.

In particular, the theorem applies when

p_{0}(\bm{x})=x_{0}\cdots x_{n+1}
. To prove Theorem
2.15
,
we first establish a sequence of lemmas.

Lemma 2.17
.

Suppose that
\operatorname{HH}^{2}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))=0
. Then the image
of
K_{\chi}
in the Jacobian ring is trivial.

Proof.

If
m
is a
\chi
-isotypical monomial which is nontrivial in
the Jacobian ring then we can use it as part of our monomial basis

J_{\gamma}
for
\gamma=id
. It will then contribute as a type

A

id
-monomial to
\operatorname{HH}^{2}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))
. Thus if

\operatorname{HH}^{2}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{{\bf w}},{\bf w}))=0
, we deduce that any monomial

m\in K_{\chi}
is trivial in the Jacobian ring, and hence the image
of
K_{\chi}
in the Jacobian ring is zero.
∎

Lemma 2.18
.

If
p\in K_{\chi}
is trivial in the Jacobian ring then

3.1
Brieskorn-Pham

A Brieskorn-Pham singularity is an isolated hypersurface singularity
given by the vanishing of the polynomial

{\bf w}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})=x_{1}^{a_{1}}+\cdots+x_{n+1}^{a_{n+1}}

for a collection of integers

. This is an invertible polynomial with

\check{{\bf w}}={\bf w}
.

Let
\bm{\mu}_{k}
denote the cyclic group of
k
th roots of unity,
and let

l=\operatorname{lcm}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}),\qquad\nu=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\frac{1}{a_{i}}.

We have a surjective

1

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\colon\bm{\mu}_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times\bm{\mu}_{a_{n+1}}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\Gamma_{{\bf w}},

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n+1},\tau\right)=\left(\tau^{l\nu}\mu_{1}^{-1}\cdots\mu_{n+1}^{-1},\tau^{l/a_{1}}\mu_{1},\ldots,\tau^{l/a_{n+1}}\mu_{n+1}\right)

Remark 3.1
.

Under

\bm{\mu}_{a_{1}}\times\cdots\times\bm{\mu}_{a_{n+1}}
maps
isomorphically onto
\operatorname{ker}\chi
; we will use this identification to
write elements of
\operatorname{ker}\chi
as

-tuples of roots of unity.

Fix an element

to the fixed

we get

\sum_{m=1}^{k}x_{i_{m}}^{a_{i_{m}}}

J_{\gamma}=\left\{x_{i_{1}}^{b_{i_{1}}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}^{b_{i_{k}}}\ :\ 0\leq b_{i_{m}}\leq a_{i_{m}}-2\mbox{ for }m=1,2,\ldots k\right\}

for
its Jacobian ring.

Lemma 3.2

Let

Proof.

The
\gamma
-monomial

5

4.1
Scale-equivalence of bigradings

In this section, we need to work over
{\mathbb{C}}
(or at least an
algebraically closed field of characteristic zero).

Definition 4.1
.

A
\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathbb{C}}
-grading on a vector space
V
(or
bigrading

for short) is a
decomposition

V=\bigoplus_{(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathbb{C}}}V^{p,q}.

Two

\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathbb{C}}
-graded vector spaces
V=\bigoplus V^{p,q}
and

W=\bigoplus W^{p,q}
are
scale-equivalent
if there is a
nonzero
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\dim(V^{p,q})=\dim(W^{p,cq})
for
all
p,q
.

Our contact invariant will be a scale-equivalence class of

\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathbb{C}}
-graded vector spaces (in fact, we will be able to find a representative which takes values in
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
). We now explain how to construct a
\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathbb{C}}
-graded vector spaces out of a certain class of Gerstenhaber algebras.

4.2
Bigradings from Gerstenhaber algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a Gerstenhaber algebra over
{\mathbb{C}}
; in particular,
there is a Gerstenhaber bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}

satisfying:

[x,y]=(-1)^{|x||y|}[y,x],\quad(-1)^{|x||z|}[[x,y],z]+(-1)^{|y||x|}[[y,z],x]+(-1)^{|z||y|}[[z,x],y]=0

The subset

\mathfrak{g}^{1}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a complex Lie algebra and the bracket gives a
representation
\rho^{d}\colon\mathfrak{g}^{1}\to\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}^{d})
for each

\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is
finite-dimensional.

Let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{1}
be a Cartan subalgebra, that is a nilpotent,
self-normalising subalgebra. A Cartan subalgebra exists and is unique
up to automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}^{1}

taking the generalised 0-eigenspace of a regular element (an element

\zeta\in\mathfrak{g}^{1}
is regular if the generalised 0-eigenspace of

\operatorname{ad}_{\zeta}
has the least possible dimension). If

some }N\right\}.

In other words,

is a

\{\rho(H)\ :\ H\in\mathfrak{h}\}
,
with eigenvalues
\alpha(H)
. The weight-space decomposition

of the adjoint representation
has

If
\mathfrak{h}
has rank 1 then we have

-bigrading

\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathbb{C}}
-grading.

index of
C
is equal to the index of the original moduli space
minus
\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mu_{CZ}(\delta_{i})+n-3)\geq 2
, so becomes
negative. This is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

We now explain the index formula (
4.1
) from the proof. If
we fix the positions of the punctures and all the asymptotic
markers, the virtual dimension of this moduli space is (see
Schwarz’s thesis
[
40
, Theorem 3.3.11]
):

\mu_{CZ}(y)-\mu_{CZ}(z)-\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mu_{CZ}(\delta_{i})-n(m+1)

since

-m-1
is the Euler characteristic of the domain. However, the
bubbling/breaking which gives rise to the punctures at
\delta_{i}

can happen anywhere, with any asymptotic marker, and the asymptotic
markers on
y
and
z
can move in a 1-parameter family, so we
get an additional
3(m+1)+1
, which gives Equation
(
4.1
). Note that this is intermediate between the formula
in Schwarz’s thesis and the formula
[
5
, Corollary
5.4]
from Bourgeois’s thesis, where all punctures and
markers are allowed to move.

Corollary 4.5
.

Suppose that
(Y,\xi)
is a
(2n-1)
-dimensional contact
manifold which admits a contact form
\alpha
for which every
closed Reeb orbit
\gamma
satisfies

\mu_{CZ}(\gamma)\geq\max(5-n,n-1).

Let
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
be
Liouville domains with
c_{1}(V_{i})=0
and
\partial V_{i}=Y
. Suppose that
V_{i}
admits a Morse function with no critical
points of index
1
. Then (a) there is an isomorphism of Lie
algebras
f^{1}\colon\operatorname{SH}^{1}(V_{1})\to\operatorname{SH}^{1}(V_{2})
, and (b) for each

d<0
there is an isomorphism

f^{d}\colon\operatorname{SH}^{d}(V_{1})\to\operatorname{SH}^{d}(V_{2})
which intertwines the
representations

\operatorname{ad}\colon\operatorname{SH}^{1}(V_{i})\to\mathfrak{gl}(\operatorname{SH}^{d}(V_{i})).

That is,
for each
d<0
, we have a commutative diagram:

\operatorname{SH}^{1}(V_{1})
\operatorname{SH}^{1}(V_{2})
\mathfrak{gl}(\operatorname{SH}^{d}(V_{1}))
\mathfrak{gl}(\operatorname{SH}^{d}(V_{2}))
\operatorname{ad}
\operatorname{ad}
f^{1}
f^{d}

Proof.

Under the assumptions of the corollary, every element of

\operatorname{SH}^{1}(V_{i})
or of
\operatorname{SH}^{d}(V_{i})
with
d<0
can be represented
using Reeb orbits for the contact form
\alpha
(rather than
critical points of a Morse function on the filling). These Reeb
orbits lie in the cylindrical end of the symplectic completion

\hat{V}_{i}
(rather than in the filling), and these cylindrical
ends are both symplectomorphic to the half-symplectisation

[0,\infty)\times Y
, so in a suitable cochain model of symplectic
cohomology, we get identifications

f^{d}\colon SC^{d}(V_{1})\to SC^{d}(V_{2})
when
d=1
or
d<0
. Since
the contact boundary is index-positive, we know that the
differential on these cochain groups is independent of the filling

[
12
, Prop. 9.17]
, which tells us that
f^{1}

and
f^{d}
induce isomorphisms on cohomology.

By Lemma
4.3
, we know there exist almost complex
structures for which the Gerstenhaber bracket between these orbits
does not involve any contributions from curves entering the
filling. This implies that
f^{1}
is an isomorphism of Lie algebras
and that
f^{d}
intertwines the adjoint action of
\operatorname{SH}^{1}
.
∎

4.4
Bigrading on
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf})

In all our examples, we calculated
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+1},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}))

and saw that
\operatorname{HH}^{2}=0
. Moreover, we saw in Lemma
2.8

that there is an intrinsically formal algebra

such that

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+1},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}))=\operatorname{HH}^{*}(B).

We now compute the usual algebra bigrading on
\operatorname{HH}^{*}(B)
in terms of
the
\gamma
-monomial contributions from Theorem

2.14
.

Lemma 4.6
.

A
\gamma
-monomial
m
contributing to
\operatorname{HH}^{d}(B)
contributes
to the bigraded piece
\operatorname{HH}^{d-nb_{0},nb_{0}}(B)
, where
b_{0}
is
the total exponent of
x_{0}
in
m
.

Remark 4.7
.

Recall that
\operatorname{HH}^{d}=\bigoplus_{q}\operatorname{HH}^{d-q,q}
, so this is really
just saying that the bigrading of
m
is
nb_{0}
.

Proof.

Consider the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action

t\cdot(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1})=(tx_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1})
. Since this action
leaves
{\bf w}
invariant, its weights give a second grading on

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}))
. Theorem
2.14

comes from an isomorphism between

\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}))
and a suitable twisted Koszul
cohomology group (whose generators are
\gamma
-monomials)

[
3
]
. This isomorphism respects the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action,
hence this additional grading is given by the total exponent of

x_{0}
in the
\gamma
-monomials contributing to
\operatorname{HH}
.

As in Section
2.3
, let
{\mathcal{S}}=\bigoplus_{i}S_{i}
be the
generator of
\mathcal{F}(V)
given by a direct sum of vanishing
cycles. In
[
29
, Theorem 4.2]
it is shown that, we have a
generator
\mathcal{S}
of
\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{\bf w}{\bf w})
mirror to

{\mathcal{S}}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the pushforward of a generator

\mathcal{E}
of
\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+1},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w})
under the inclusion

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})\mapsto(0,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})
. In
particular,
\mathcal{S}
is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-invariant. Using this,

[
29
, Theorem 4.2]
shows that the endomorphism

A_{\infty}
-algebra
{\cal B}=\mathrm{end}(\mathcal{S})
is a formal
algebra and the grading on
B=\mathrm{H}({\cal B})
is
n
times the

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-action. Therefore, the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

∎

In fact, in all of our examples we have

4.5
Proof of Theorem
1.13

Let
X
be a cDV singularity and let
V
be its Milnor
fibre. Let
\mu
be the Milnor number of
X
. By

[
33
, Theorem 6.6]
, the Milnor fibre admits a Morse
function with precisely one minimum and
\mu
critical points
of index
3
; in particular, none of index 1. Since
X
is
terminal, McLean’s theorem
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
tells us
that there exists a contact form for which every closed Reeb
orbit
\gamma
satisfies

\mu_{CZ}(\gamma)\geq 2\operatorname{md}(X)=2
, where
\operatorname{md}(X)
is
the minimal discrepancy of
X
, which equals 1 by a theorem of
Markushevich
[
31
]
. Since
n=3
, we have

\max(5-n,n-1)=2
, so that all the assumptions of Corollary

4.5
are satisfied.

Consider the Lie algebra
\operatorname{SH}^{1}(V)
and its representation

\bigoplus_{d<0}\operatorname{SH}^{d}(V)
(where
\operatorname{SH}^{1}(V)
acts by the
Gerstenhaber bracket). By Corollary
4.5
, this Lie
algebra representation is a contact invariant of the link of
X
.

We know that Conjecture
2.2
holds for all our Brieskorn-Pham
and
cA_{n}
examples, and we are going to assume that it holds for
the Laufer examples too. By Theorem
2.6
, this tells
us that, if
V
is the Milnor fibre of
\check{{\bf w}}
, then

\operatorname{SH}^{*}(V)\cong\operatorname{HH}^{*}(\operatorname{mf}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+2},\Gamma_{\bf w},{\bf w}))

as Gerstenhaber
algebras. Therefore, the contact invariant Lie algebra representation
is equivalent to the representation

\operatorname{ad}\colon\operatorname{HH}^{1}(B)\to\bigoplus_{d<0}\mathfrak{gl}(\operatorname{HH}^{d}(B))

discussed in Section
4.4
. In particular, this gives
a
\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathbb{C}}
-grading on
\bigoplus_{d<0}\operatorname{SH}^{d}(V)
which we can
compute in terms of the
x_{0}
-powers of the contributing

\gamma
-monomials by Lemma
4.6
.

We now show that, for all of our examples, these scale-equivalence
classes of
\mathbb{Z}\times{\mathbb{C}}
-gradings distinguish the contact structures.

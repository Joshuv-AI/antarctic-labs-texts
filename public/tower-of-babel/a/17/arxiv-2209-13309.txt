Elements of a Lie algebra acting nilpotently in all its representations
Styrt O. G

Abstract




An equivalent condition for an element of a Lie algebra acting nilpotently in all its representations is obtained. Namely, it should belong to the
derived algebra and go via factoring over the radical to a nilpotent element of the corresponding (semisimple) quotient algebra.

Key words
:
Lie algebra, radical of a Lie algebra, semisimple Lie algebra, nilpotent element of a semisimple Lie algebra, nilpotent operator.

1
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. In the case of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

•

its element
a
is called
nilpotent
if the adjoint operator
\ad a
is nilpotent;

•

denote the set of all its nilpotent elements by
N_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

In the general case, denote by
\mathfrak{h}
the semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}/(\rad\mathfrak{g})
and by
\pi
the factoring homomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
. Here
is the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1
.

For any element
a\in\mathfrak{g}
, the following conditions are equivalent
:

1)

in each representation of
\mathfrak{g}
, the element
a
acts nilpotently
;

2)

a\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\pi(a)\in N_{\mathfrak{h}}
.

2
Notations and auxiliary facts

In this section, a number of auxiliary notations and statements is given.

First of all, for brevity, the following notations will be used:

•

k
is the main field;

•

E_{V}
is the identity operator in a space
V
(if the space is clear from the context, then the index can be omitted);

•

\Spec X
is the set of all eigenvalues of an operator
X
;

•

V_{\lambda}(X)
(resp.
V^{\lambda}(X)
) is the eigenspace (resp. the root subspace) of an operator
X
in a space
V
with an eigenvalue
\lambda
;

•

\mathfrak{z}(a)
is the centralizer of an element
a
of a Lie algebra.

Let
A
be an arbitrary algebra (without any requirements to the bilinear operation of multiplication). It is known (and can be proved by elementary
check) that

•

\Der A
is a Lie subalgebra in
\mathfrak{gl}(A)
;

•

for all
d\in\Der A
and
\lambda,\mu\in k
, we have
\bigl(A_{\lambda}(d)\bigr)\cdot\bigl(A_{\mu}(d)\bigr)\subset A_{\lambda+\mu}(d)
.

Lemma 1
.

If
n:=\dim A
,
k=\overline{k}
and
\cha k\notin(0;n]
, then, for each
d\in\Der A
,
\lambda\in k^{*}
and
a\in A^{\lambda}(d)
,
the operators of left and right shifts by
a
are nilpotent.

\square\quad
By condition,
|\Spec d|\leqslant n
. Further, let
X
be any of the operators
l_{a}
and
r_{a}
. Once
\mu\in k
, then

X\bigl(A^{\mu}(d)\bigr)\subset A^{\lambda+\mu}(d)
and the subset
\{m\lambda+\mu\colon m=0,\ldots,n\}\subset k
of order
n+1
is thus not contained in
\Spec d
that
implies
X^{n}\bigl(A^{\mu}(d)\bigr)=0
. Hence,
X^{n}=0
.
∎

Assume that
k=\overline{k}
and
\cha k=0
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a semisimple Lie algebra. Fix the non-degenerate symmetric bilinear Killing form
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\mathfrak{h}
. For any
a\in\mathfrak{h}
, the
operator
d:=\ad a
is skew-symmetric and, consequently,
(\Ker d)^{\perp}=\Img d
, i. e.
\bigl(\mathfrak{z}(a)\bigr)^{\perp}=[a,\mathfrak{h}]
.

Lemma 2
.

For each
e\in N_{\mathfrak{h}}
, we have
e\perp\mathfrak{z}(e)
.

\square\quad
If
a\in\mathfrak{h}
and
[a,e]=0
, then
[\ad a,\ad e]=0
implying (see Lemma in
[
1
, sec. II.8.2, p. 36]
)

(a,e)=\tr\bigl((\ad a)(\ad e)\bigr)=0
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
e\in\mathfrak{h}
be an arbitrary element. Then
(e\in N_{\mathfrak{h}})\Leftrightarrow\bigl(e\in[e,\mathfrak{h}]\bigr)
.

\square\quad
If
e\in N_{\mathfrak{h}}
, then, according to Lemma
2
,
e\in\bigl(\mathfrak{z}(e)\bigr)^{\perp}=[e,\mathfrak{h}]
.

Conversely, if
a\in\mathfrak{h}
and
e=[a,e]
, then
d:=\ad a\in\Der\mathfrak{h}
and
e\in\mathfrak{h}_{1}(d)
that implies (by Lemma
1
) nilpotence of the operator
\ad e
.
∎

Let
V
be an arbitrary subspace. It is well-known that
\mathfrak{sl}(V)
is a simple Lie algebra and the set of all nilpotent operators
V\to V

coincides with its subset
N_{\mathfrak{sl}(V)}
. For each operator
X\colon V\to V
, we have
\bigl[X,\mathfrak{gl}(V)\bigr]=\bigl[X,\mathfrak{sl}(V)\bigr]\subset\mathfrak{sl}(V)

and, by Theorem
2
, nilpotence of
X
is equivalent to the inclusion
X\in\bigl[X,\mathfrak{gl}(V)\bigr]
.

Take any Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its representation
\rho
in a space
V
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
be an arbitrary subspace. Define
the weight subspaces

V_{\xi}(\mathfrak{a}):=\Bigl\{v\in V\colon av=\bigl(\xi(a)\bigr)\cdot v\,\,\forall\,a\in\mathfrak{a}\Bigr\}\subset V\quad(\xi\in\mathfrak{a}^{*})

and, besides,
the set of
\mathfrak{a}
-
weights

P_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\bigl\{\xi\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}\colon V_{\xi}(\mathfrak{a})\neq 0\bigr\}\subset\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

Theorem 3
.

1)

If the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is solvable, then
P_{\mathfrak{g}}\neq\varnothing
.

2)

For any ideal
\mathfrak{a}\lhd\mathfrak{g}
and linear function
\xi\in P_{\mathfrak{a}}
, we have
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}]\subset\Ker\xi\subset\mathfrak{a}
.

\square\quad
Both statements are proved in
[
2
, sec. I.
V
.5]
(formulated on p. 36 as <<Theorem 1’>> and <<The Main Lemma>> respectively).
∎

Corollary 1
.

For any ideal
\mathfrak{a}\lhd\mathfrak{g}
and function
\xi\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, we have
\mathfrak{g}\bigl(V_{\xi}(\mathfrak{a})\bigr)\subset V_{\xi}(\mathfrak{a})
.

\square\quad
Assume that
\xi\in P_{\mathfrak{a}}
(otherwise, it is nothing to prove). Let
b\in\mathfrak{g}
and
v\in V_{\xi}(\mathfrak{a})
be arbitrary elements. According to
Theorem
3
, for each
a\in\mathfrak{a}
, the element
a_{0}:=[b,a]\in\mathfrak{a}
belongs to
\Ker\xi
implying
a_{0}v=\bigl(\xi(a_{0})\bigr)\cdot v=0
,

abv=bav=b\Bigl(\bigl(\xi(a)\bigr)\cdot v\Bigr)=\bigl(\xi(a)\bigr)\cdot(bv)
. So, we obtained that
bv\in V_{\xi}(\mathfrak{a})
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

If the representation
\rho
is irreducible, then
\rho(\rad\mathfrak{g})\subset kE_{V}
.

\square\quad
The ideal
\mathfrak{a}:=\rad\mathfrak{g}\lhd\mathfrak{g}
is solvable. Hence (see Theorem
3
), for some function
\xi\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, the subspace

V_{\xi}(\mathfrak{a})\subset V
is nonzero. By Corollary
1
, it is
\mathfrak{g}
-
invariant and, thus, equals
V
.
∎

Corollary 3
.

If the representation
\rho
is irreducible, then
\rho\bigl((\rad\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\bigr)=0
.

3
Proofs of the results

In this section, Theorem
1
is proved.

Return to notations and agreements of §
1
. Recall that the main field
k
is assumed to be algebraically closed and to have characteristic
zero.

\text{\ref{nilp}}\Rightarrow\text{\ref{cond}}
.

Suppose that the element
a\in\mathfrak{g}
acts nilpotently in any representation of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Consider an arbitrary ideal
\mathfrak{a}\lhd\mathfrak{g}
, the factoring homomorphism
\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}\colon\mathfrak{g}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a}
and, besides, the element

a_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)\in\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a}
. Each representation
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
naturally induces the representation

(\rho\circ\pi_{\mathfrak{a}})\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
and, therefore, the operator
\rho(a_{\mathfrak{a}})=(\rho\circ\pi_{\mathfrak{a}})(a)\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is nilpotent.
Research two special particular cases.

(i)

\mathfrak{a}:=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
,
V\neq 0
. In this case, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a}
is commutative. For any function
\xi\in(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a})^{*}
, we have
a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V),\,x\to\bigl(\xi(x)\bigr)\cdot E
and, hence, the operator

\bigl(\xi(a_{\mathfrak{a}})\bigr)\cdot E=\rho(a_{\mathfrak{a}})\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is nilpotent implying
\xi(a_{\mathfrak{a}})=0
. So, the element

\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}(a)=a_{\mathfrak{a}}\in\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a}
is trivial; in other words,
a\in\mathfrak{a}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{a}:=\rad\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{a}
is the semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
, furthermore,
\pi_{\mathfrak{a}}=\pi
and
a_{\mathfrak{a}}=\pi(a)
. So,

\ad(a_{\mathfrak{a}})\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
is a nilpotent operator, i. e.
\pi(a)=a_{\mathfrak{a}}\in N_{\mathfrak{h}}
.

\text{\ref{cond}}\Rightarrow\text{\ref{nilp}}
.

Now, suppose that
a\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\pi(a)\in N_{\mathfrak{h}}
.

By Theorem
2
,
\pi(a)\in\bigl[\pi(a),\mathfrak{h}\bigr]=\bigl[\pi(a),\pi(\mathfrak{g})\bigr]=\pi\bigl([a,\mathfrak{g}]\bigr)
,

a\in\bigl([a,\mathfrak{g}]+(\rad\mathfrak{g})\bigr)\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=[a,\mathfrak{g}]+\bigl((\rad\mathfrak{g})\cap[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\bigr).

If
\rho\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is an arbitrary irreducible representation, then, according to Corollary
3
,

\rho(a)\in\rho\bigl([a,\mathfrak{g}]\bigr)=\bigl[\rho(a),\rho(\mathfrak{g})\bigr]\subset\bigl[\rho(a),\mathfrak{gl}(V)\bigr]
and, hence, the operator
\rho(a)\colon V\to V
is nilpotent. Thus, in each
irreducible representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, its element
a
acts nilpotently. This statement still holds without requiring irreducibility:
for any representation
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, there exists an increasing chain of subrepresentations
0=V_{0}\subset\penalty\ldots\subset\penalty V_{n}=V
such that all quotient representations

V_{m}/V_{m-1}
(
m=1,\ldots,n
) are irreducible.

So, Theorem
1
is completely proved.

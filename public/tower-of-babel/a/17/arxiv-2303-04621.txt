Slip and twinning in Bravais lattices Journal: Journal of Elasticity
John M. Ball

Abstract
A unified treatment of slip and twinning in Bravais lattices is given, focussing on the case of cubic symmetry, and using the Ericksen energy well formulation, so that interfaces correspond to rank-one connections between the infinitely many crystallographically equivalent energy wells. Twins are defined to be such rank-one connections involving a nontrivial reflection of the lattice across some plane. The slips and twins minimizing shear magnitude for cubic lattices are rigorously calculated, and the conjugates of these and other slips analyzed. It is observed that all rank-one connections between the energy wells for the dual of a Bravais lattice can be obtained explicitly from those for the original lattice, so that in particular the rank-one connections for fcc can be obtained explicitly from those for bcc.

1
Introduction

This paper gives a unified treatment of slip and twinning in Bravais lattices, focussing on the case of cubic symmetry, and using the Ericksen energy well formulation, so that interfaces correspond to rank-one connections between the infinitely many crystallographically equivalent energy wells. Inevitably there is a considerable overlap with other such treatments, most notably with that of Pitteri & Zanzotto
pitterizanzotto03
(especially Chapter 8).

A main contribution is that we calculate rigorously the slips and Type 1/Type 2 twins
{\mathbf{F}}={\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbf{n}}
that minimize the shear magnitude
|{\mathbf{a}}|
for cubic lattices, these corresponding to well-known statements in the materials science literature. This reduces to the minimization of products
G({\mathbf{p}})H({\mathbf{q}})
of quadratic forms, where
{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
are such that
{\mathbf{p}}\cdot{\mathbf{q}}=0
for slip and
{\mathbf{p}}\cdot{\mathbf{q}}=2
for Type 1/Type 2 twins. The principle of minimizing
|{\mathbf{a}}|
as a means of selecting preferred slips and twins has been proposed for slips by Chalmers & Martius
chalmers1952slip
and for twins by Kiho
kiho1954
;
kiho1958
and Jaswon & Dove
jaswondove1957
. In fact the Type 1/Type 2 twins minimize the shear magnitude among all rank-one connections between the energy wells. We also calculate the conjugates to the minimizing slips, together with certain other slips for bcc, showing that they are all twins. Further, we determine all the slips for cubic lattices whose conjugates are also slips.

An apparently new observation is that all rank-one connections for the dual of a Bravais lattice can be obtained explicitly from those for the original lattice. In particular the rank-one connections for fcc lattices can be obtained explicitly in terms of those for bcc lattices (and vice versa).

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we review standard material on Bravais lattices. Here and throughout the paper we use a matrix formulation, so that the lattice vectors
{\mathbf{b}}_{1},{\mathbf{b}}_{2},{\mathbf{b}}_{3}
are written as the columns of a matrix
{\mathbf{B}}
; at least for the author this makes calculations easier to follow. We define lattice planes and the dual lattice, largely following Ashcroft & Mermin
ashcroftmermin
. Then in Section
3
we recall the Ericksen energy-well picture, whereby the macroscopic free-energy density inherits an infinite family of crystallographically equivalent energy wells from those of the lattice free-energy via the Cauchy-Born rule.

In Section
4
we review standard results for the existence
of rank-one connections as well as establishing (Theorem
4.1
) the correspondence between rank-one connections for Bravais lattices and their duals. In Section
5
we give a characterization of slip systems in terms of rank-one connections not involving lattice rotation (Theorem
5.1
), and determine the slips that minimize the shear magnitude (Theorem
5.2
) for the simple cubic, bcc and fcc lattices. In Section
6
we give a definition of twins in terms of interfaces separating nontrivially reflected lattices, and determine (Theorem
6.1
) the Type 1/Type 2 twins minimizing the shear magnitude for the simple cubic, bcc and fcc lattices. In Section
7
we address the problem of determining general rank-one connections of minimum shear magnitude, showing in particular (Corollary
3
) that the twins in Theorem
6.1
minimize the shear amplitude among all rank-one connections. Finally in Section
8
we determine (Theorem
8.1
) all slips for cubic lattices whose conjugates are also slips, and show (Theorems
8.2
,
8.3
) that the conjugates of all the specific slips discussed in Section
5
are twins of various types.

This paper focusses on cubic Bravais lattices, but extensions of the results to noncubic lattices, multilattices and crystals undergoing martensitic phase transformations would be valuable.

Bravais lattices

2.1
Definitions and basic properties

Denote by
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
the unit vector in the
i^{th}\rm
coordinate direction of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. A
Bravais lattice
is an infinite lattice of points in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
generated by linear combinations with integer coefficients of three linearly independent basis vectors
{\mathbf{b}}_{1},{\mathbf{b}}_{2},{\mathbf{b}}_{3}
. Representing these basis vectors with respect to the orthonormal basis
\{{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\}
, and letting
{\mathbf{B}}=({\mathbf{b}}_{1},{\mathbf{b}}_{2},{\mathbf{b}}_{3})
be the matrix with columns
{\mathbf{b}}_{i}
, so that
B_{ij}={\mathbf{b}}_{j}\cdot{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
, we write the corresponding Bravais lattice as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{m_{1}{\mathbf{b}}_{1}+m_{2}{\mathbf{b}}_{2}+m_{3}{\mathbf{b}}_{3}:m_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{m}}:{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}\},

where
{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the set of integers.
All lattice sites
{\mathbf{p}}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
are equivalent, i.e.

{\mathbf{p}}+{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})={\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}}).

We denote by
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
the space of real
3\times 3
matrices, by
{\mathbb{Z}}^{3\times 3}
the set of
3\times 3
matrices with integer entries, and

\displaystyle GL(3,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbf{F}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:\det{\mathbf{F}}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbf{F}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:\det{\mathbf{F}}>0\},

\displaystyle GL(3,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle=\{{\bm{\mu}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3\times 3}}:\det{\bm{\mu}}=\pm 1\},

\displaystyle GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle=\{{\bm{\mu}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3\times 3}}:\det{\bm{\mu}}=1\}=SL(3,\mathbb{Z}),

\displaystyle{\rm O}(3)

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbf{Q}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:{\mathbf{Q}}^{T}{\mathbf{Q}}={\mathbf{1}}\},

\displaystyle{\rm SO}(3)

\displaystyle=\{{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\rm O}(3):\det{\mathbf{Q}}=1\}.

For
{\mathbf{F}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
we denote the Euclidean norm of
{\mathbf{F}}
by
|{\mathbf{F}}|:=(\rm tr\,{\mathbf{F}}^{T}{\mathbf{F}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

The following standard theorem (see, for example,
Ericksen77
,
(
pitterizanzotto03
, Proposition 3.1)
) characterizes the sets of basis vectors that are equivalent in the sense that they generate the same Bravais lattice.

Theorem 2.1

{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})={\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{C})
if and only if

\mathbf{C}={\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}},\mbox{ for some {\boldmath$\mu$}}\in GL(3,\mathbb{Z}).

Proof

Let
{\mathbf{B}}=({\mathbf{b}}_{1},{\mathbf{b}}_{2},{\mathbf{b}}_{3}),\;\mathbf{C}=({\mathbf{c}}_{1},{\mathbf{c}}_{2},{\mathbf{c}}_{3})
. If
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})={\mathcal{L}}({\bf C})
then
{\mathbf{b}}_{i}=\mu_{ji}{\bf c}_{j}
for some
\bm{\mu}=(\mu_{ij})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3\times 3}
, so that
{\mathbf{B}}={\bf C}{\bm{\mu}}
. Similarly
{\bf C}={\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\prime}
for some
{\bm{\mu}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3\times 3}
.
So
{\bm{\mu}}^{\prime}={\bm{\mu}}^{-1}
and
\bm{\mu}\in GL(3,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Conversely, if
{\mathbf{B}}={\bf C}{\bm{\mu}}
then
{\mathbf{b}}_{i}={\mu}_{ji}{\bf c}_{j}
and so
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})\subset{\mathcal{L}}(\bf C)
. Similarly
{\mathcal{L}}(\bf C)\subset{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
. ∎

Corollary 1

If
{\bf F}\in GL(3,\mathbb{R})
, then
{\mathcal{L}}({\bf F}{\mathbf{B}})={\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
if and only if

{\bf F}={\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}\mbox{ for some }{\bm{\mu}}\in GL(3,{\mathbb{Z}}).

The
point group

P({\mathbf{B}})
is

\displaystyle P({\mathbf{B}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\rm O}(3):\mathcal{L}({\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{B}})=\mathcal{L}({\mathbf{B}})\}

(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\rm O}(3):{\mathbf{Q}}={\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}\text{ for some }{\bm{\mu}}\in GL(3,\mathbb{Z})\}.

2.2
Lattice planes and the dual lattice

A
lattice plane
is a plane
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})=\{{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:{\mathbf{x}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=k\}
with unit normal
{\mathbf{n}}
such that
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})\cap{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
contains 3 non-collinear points. Equivalently,
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})\cap{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
is a translate of a 2D Bravais lattice of the form

\{r_{1}{\mathbf{m}}_{1}+r_{2}{\mathbf{m}}_{2}:r_{1},r_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},

where
{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
and
{\mathbf{m}}_{1},{\mathbf{m}}_{2}
are linearly independent. (Taking without loss of generality
k=0
this can be proved by first choosing
{\mathbf{m}}_{1}
to be a nonzero vector in
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})\cap{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
of minimum length, and then
{\mathbf{m}}_{2}
a nonzero vector in
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})\cap{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
not parallel to
{\mathbf{m}}_{1}
and of minimum length.)

Then, following Ashcroft & Mermin
ashcroftmermin
, there exists
{\mathbf{p}}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
with
{\mathbf{p}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}>0
such that

{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})=\bigcup_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\left({\mathcal{L}}\left({\mathbf{B}}\right)\cap\Pi\left({\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)+r{\mathbf{p}}\right),

so that
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
is the union of its intersection with a family of planes parallel to
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})
with interplane spacing

d={\mathbf{p}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}
.

The
dual
(or
reciprocal
)
lattice
of
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
is the set

\{{\mathbf{k}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{p}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\text{ for all }{\mathbf{p}}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})\}.

The vector
{\mathbf{k}}
belongs to the dual lattice if and only if
{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for each
i
, which holds if and only if
{\mathbf{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}{\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
for
{\mathbf{r}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
. Hence the dual lattice is the Bravais lattice
\mathcal{L}({\mathbf{B}}^{-T})
.

Theorem 2.2
(see
(
ashcroftmermin
, p90)
)

\Pi({\mathbf{n}})=\{{\mathbf{x}}:{\mathbf{x}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0\}
is a lattice plane if and only if there exists
{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}}^{-T})\setminus\{0\}
with
{\mathbf{k}}
parallel to
{\mathbf{n}}
, and then
d^{-1}
is the minimum length of such a vector
{\mathbf{k}}
.

Proof

We give a slightly different proof to that in
ashcroftmermin
for the convenience of the reader. If
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})
is a lattice plane then
d^{-1}{\mathbf{n}}\cdot{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
, so that
d^{-1}{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}}^{-T})
, and there is no shorter such dual lattice vector.

Conversely, if
{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}}^{-T})\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
with
{\mathbf{k}}
parallel to
{\mathbf{n}}
then
{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}=n_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
for each
i
, and hence
{\mathbf{B}}^{T}{\mathbf{k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}n_{i}{\mathbf{e}}_{i}:={\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
. Pick
{\mathbf{m}},{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
with
{\mathbf{m}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}={\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0
and
{\mathbf{m}},{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}
linearly independent, which is possible because the vectors
{\mathbf{n}}\wedge{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
are not all parallel. Then
{\mathbf{B}}^{T}{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{m}}={\mathbf{B}}^{T}{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}=0
, so that
{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{m}}={\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}=0
and
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})
is a lattice plane.∎

Remark 1

Suppose
{\mathbf{k}}={\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{q}}
is parallel to
{\mathbf{n}}
, where
{\mathbf{q}}=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
. Then the formula for

can be rewritten as

\displaystyle d^{-1}=\frac{|{\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{q}}|}{\text{gcd}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})},

(2)

where

is the greatest common divisor of the

. Indeed

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{q}}:=\frac{{\mathbf{q}}}{\text{gcd}(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}

and the
\bar{q}_{i}
have no common factor. If

is parallel to
{\mathbf{q}}
we have that

for coprime integers

and

. Thus

and so

and thus

.

3
The Ericksen energy well picture

Suppose that the free energy per unit volume of a crystalline material with atoms at the points of the Bravais lattice
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{C})
, where
\mathbf{C}\in GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{R})
, is given by
\varphi(\mathbf{C})\geqslant 0
. For simplicity we assume that the temperature is constant, so that there are no phase transformations.

Natural requirements on
\varphi
are

(i) (
frame-indifference
)

\varphi({\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{C})=\varphi(\mathbf{C})\text{ for all }{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\rm SO}(3),

(ii) (
invariance with respect to equivalent lattices
)

\varphi(\mathbf{C}{\bm{{\bm{\mu}}}})=\varphi(\mathbf{C})\text{ for all }{\bm{{\bm{\mu}}}}\in GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{Z}).

We assume further that
\varphi(\mathbf{C})=0
iff
\mathbf{C}={\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}
for
{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\rm SO}(3)
,
{\bm{\mu}}\in GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{Z})
, so that the minimum value zero is attained only for the unstrained lattice.

As is standard, we use the Cauchy-Born rule to relate the macroscopic free-energy density
\psi
to
\varphi
, thus defining an elastic free energy

I({\mathbf{y}})=\int_{\Omega}\psi(D{\mathbf{y}}({\mathbf{x}}))\,d{\mathbf{x}}

for a deformation
{\mathbf{y}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is an open reference domain.

Choosing a reference configuration in which the crystal lattice is
\mathcal{L}({\mathbf{B}})
, where
{\mathbf{B}}\in GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{R})
, we assume that

\psi({\mathbf{A}})=\varphi({\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{B}}),\mbox{ for }{\mathbf{A}}\in GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{R}).

Thus
\psi\geqslant 0
inherits from
\varphi
the invariances

(i)
\psi({\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}})=\psi({\mathbf{A}})
for all
{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\rm SO}(3)
,

(ii)
\psi({\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1})=\psi({\mathbf{A}})
for all
{\bm{\mu}}\in GL^{+}(3,{\mathbb{Z}})
,

and
\psi
has zero set

\displaystyle\psi^{-1}(0)=\bigcup_{{\bm{\mu}}\in GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{Z})}{\rm SO}(3){\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}.

(30)

The energy wells
{\rm SO}(3){\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}
are not all distinct, because
{\rm SO}(3){\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}={\rm SO}(3){\mathbf{B}}\tilde{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}
iff

{\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}\tilde{\bm{\mu}}^{-1}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}\in P({\mathbf{B}})\cap{\rm SO}(3).

But since
P({\mathbf{B}})
is finite and
GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{Z})
is infinite, there are
infinitely many distinct energy wells
.

4
Interfaces

4.1
Rank-one connections

We are interested in possible planar interfaces between distinct constant gradients on the energy wells, that is in pairs of distinct matrices
{\mathbf{F}},{\mathbf{G}}\in\psi^{-1}(0)
with

\displaystyle{\mathbf{F}}-{\mathbf{G}}={\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbf{n}},\;\;{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},|{\mathbf{n}}|=1.

(31)

We can assume that
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathbf{1}}
, so that we are interested in the
{\bm{\mu}}\in GL^{+}(3,\mathbb{Z})
with
{\mathbf{M}}={\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}\not\in{\rm SO}(3)
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbf{n}}={\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{M}}\text{ for some }{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},|{\mathbf{n}}|=1,{\mathbf{R}}\in{\rm SO}(3).

(32)

Note that since
{\mathbf{M}}
is independent of the scale of
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
(that is it is the same for
{\mathcal{L}}(t{\mathbf{B}})
for any
t\neq 0
) the rank-one connections are also independent of scale. If (
32
) holds then since
\det{\mathbf{M}}=1
we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0,

(33)

and thus

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{a}}|^{2}=|{\mathbf{M}}|^{2}-3.

(34)

We denote by
0<\lambda_{1}\leqslant\lambda_{2}\leqslant\lambda_{3}
the eigenvalues of the positive definite symmetric matrix
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}
, which satisfy
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}=1
, and by
\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
the corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors, so that
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}
has spectral decomposition

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}=\lambda_{1}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{2}+\lambda_{3}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{3}\otimes\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{3}.

(35)

A necessary and sufficient condition for (
32
) to hold (see e.g.
(
j32
, Prop. 4)
,
(
j56
, Theorem 2.1)
,
Khachaturyan83
,
(
p37
, Lemma 1)
) is that
\lambda_{2}=1
, or (since
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}\neq{\mathbf{1}}
) that
{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}
has an eigenvalue equal to one. Then
0<\lambda_{1}<1=\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}=\lambda_{1}^{-1}
. An equivalent condition is that

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{M}}|^{2}=|{\mathbf{M}}^{-1}|^{2},

(36)

which follows from the identity

\displaystyle\det({\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}-{\mathbf{1}})=(\det{\mathbf{M}})^{2}-(\det{\mathbf{M}})^{2}|{\mathbf{M}}^{-1}|^{2}+|{\mathbf{M}}|^{2}-1.

(37)

(This implies in particular, as observed in
(
pitterizanzotto03
, Remark 8.4)
,
that there is a rank-one connection to
{\rm SO}(3){\mathbf{M}}
iff there is a rank-one connection to
{\rm SO}(3){\mathbf{M}}^{-1}
, which can be verified directly.)

There are then exactly two distinct such
conjugate
(or
reciprocal
) rank-one connections

\displaystyle{\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{+}\otimes{\mathbf{n}}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}_{+}{\mathbf{M}},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{-}\otimes{\mathbf{n}}_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}_{-}{\mathbf{M}}.

These can be calculated explicitly by comparing the spectral decomposition (
35
) with the relation

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}^{T}{\mathbf{M}}=({\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{n}}\otimes{\mathbf{a}})({\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbf{n}})

(38)

that follows from (
32
) (see e.g.
(
j32
, Prop. 4)
).
A straightforward calculation then shows that (up to a change of sign)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{n}}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\lambda_{1}}}(\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{1}+\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{3}),\;\;{\mathbf{a}}_{\pm}=\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda_{1}}}\left(\mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}}\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{1}+\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{3}\right).

(39)

From (
34
) or (
39
) we have that
|{\mathbf{a}}_{+}|=|{\mathbf{a}}_{-}|
. Also
\displaystyle{\mathbf{n}}_{+}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{-}=\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{1}}
, so that the planes corresponding to the two rank-one connections are never orthogonal.

It also follows from (
39
) that if we know one rank-one connection
{\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbf{n}}={\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{M}}
then the conjugate rank-one connection
{\mathbf{1}}+\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{n}}=\tilde{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{M}}
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{n}}=\frac{1}{4+|{\mathbf{a}}|^{2}}(2{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{a}})\otimes(2{\mathbf{a}}+|{\mathbf{a}}|^{2}{\mathbf{n}}).

(40)

From (

5.1
Slip systems and lattice invariant shears

A
slip system

(\Pi({\mathbf{n}}),{\mathbf{b}})
consists of a lattice plane
\Pi({\mathbf{n}})
with unit normal
{\mathbf{n}}
and interplane spacing
d
, and a nonzero lattice vector
{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
(the

) with
{\mathbf{b}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}=0
. If
(\Pi({\mathbf{n}}),{\mathbf{b}})
is a slip system so is
(\Pi({\mathbf{n}}),-{\mathbf{b}})

(\Pi({\mathbf{n}}),{\mathbf{b}})
with
(\Pi({\mathbf{n}}),-{\mathbf{b}})

Equivalently

{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})=({\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})\cap\{{\mathbf{x}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\leqslant 0\})\cup(({\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})\cap\{{\mathbf{x}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}>0\})+{\mathbf{b}}),

so that if the part of
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
in the half-space
\{{\mathbf{x}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}>0\}
is rigidly displaced by
{\mathbf{b}}
then
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbf{B}})
is restored.

Theorem 5.1

The following are equivalent:

\rm(i)

{\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbf{n}}={\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1},
where
{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
|{\mathbf{n}}|=1
, and
{\bm{\mu}}\in GL^{+}(3,{\mathbb{Z}})
.

\rm(ii)

{\bm{\mu}}={\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{p}}\otimes{\mathbf{q}}
, where
{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}
,
{\mathbf{p}}\cdot{\mathbf{q}}=0
, and

\displaystyle{\mathbf{n}}=\tau\frac{{\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{q}}}{|{\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{q}}|},\;\;{\mathbf{a}}=\tau{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{p}}|{\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{q}}|,

(52)

where
\tau=\pm 1
.

\rm(iii)

(\Pi({\mathbf{n}}),{\mathbf{b}})
is a slip system with interplane spacing
d
and Burgers vector
{\mathbf{b}}=d{\mathbf{a}}

The proof uses the following lemma.

Lemma 1

The matrix

{\mathbf{c}}\otimes{\mathbf{d}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3\times 3}
for some

if and only if
{\mathbf{c}}\otimes{\mathbf{d}}={\mathbf{p}}\otimes{\mathbf{q}}
for some
{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
.

Proof

We only have to prove the necessity, and may assume
{\mathbf{c}}\otimes{\mathbf{d}}
is a nonzero matrix of integers. Thus some
c_{i}\neq 0
and
c_{i}d_{1},c_{i}d_{2},c_{i}d_{3}
are integers, so that
\displaystyle{\mathbf{d}}=\frac{1}{c_{i}}{\mathbf{m}}
for some
{\mathbf{m}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}

\displaystyle{\mathbf{c}}=\frac{1}{d_{j}}{\mathbf{r}}
for some
j
and
{\mathbf{r}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
. Hence
\displaystyle{\mathbf{c}}\otimes{\mathbf{d}}=\frac{1}{c_{i}d_{j}}{\mathbf{r}}\otimes{\mathbf{m}}

{\mathbf{r}}=k{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{m}}=l{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime}
, where

are integers and neither
(r_{1}^{\prime},r_{2}^{\prime},r_{3}^{\prime})
nor
(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime},m_{3}^{\prime})
have common factors. Thus

{\mathbf{c}}\otimes{\mathbf{d}}=\frac{r}{s}{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}\otimes{\mathbf{m}}^{\prime},

where
\displaystyle\frac{r}{s}
is a rational number expressed in its lowest terms. Suppose

has a prime factor

does not divide

Proof of Theorem
5.1

(i)
\Rightarrow

5.2
Slips of minimum shear magnitude

Since if
{\bm{\mu}}={\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{p}}\otimes{\mathbf{q}}
,

{\mathbf{M}}={\mathbf{B}}{\bm{\mu}}{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}={\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{p}}\otimes{\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{q}}

we have that

|{\mathbf{a}}|^{2}=d^{-2}|{\mathbf{b}}|^{2}=|{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{p}}|^{2}|{\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{q}}|^{2}.

Hence, to minimize the shear magnitude
|{\mathbf{a}}|
we need to minimize
|{\mathbf{B}}{\mathbf{p}}|^{2}|{\mathbf{B}}^{-T}{\mathbf{q}}|^{2}
subject to
{\mathbf{p}},{\mathbf{q}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
with
{\mathbf{p}}\cdot{\mathbf{q}}=0
.

Minimizing the shear magnitude is the criterion proposed by Chalmers & Martius
chalmers1952slip
, who give a heuristic justification in terms of energetics, pointing out that the criterion of minimizing the magnitude of the Burgers vector
{\mathbf{b}}
does not determine the preferred slip planes. A review of different possible criteria is given in
cordier2002
.

In the following theorem we give the slips of minimum shear magnitude for the simple cubic, bcc and fcc lattices, reproducing in our framework classical results for the slip systems for these lattices (see, for example,
jackson2012handbook
). See below for further comments.

Remark 7

It is not in general true that the conjugate of a slip is a twin or a slip. For example we can take

\displaystyle{\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbf{n}}=2({\mathbf{e}}_{1}+3{\mathbf{e}}_{2}+5{\mathbf{e}}_{3})\otimes({\mathbf{e}}_{1}-2{\mathbf{e}}_{2}+{\mathbf{e}}_{3}),

(125)

which is a slip for both bcc and simple cubic, albeit for a high value of
|{\mathbf{a}}|^{2}
. A calculation then shows that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}=({\mathbf{1}}+\tilde{\mathbf{a}}\otimes\tilde{\mathbf{n}})({\mathbf{1}}+{\mathbf{a}}\otimes{\mathbf{n}})^{-1}=\frac{1}{211}\left(\begin{array}[]{rrr}129&114&-122\\
94&-177&-66\\
-138&-14&-159\end{array}\right),

and
{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{P}}
cannot equal
-{\mathbf{1}}+2{\mathbf{m}}\otimes{\mathbf{m}}
for any
{\mathbf{m}}
because the absolute values of the entries of
{\mathbf{Q}}
are all different, and the action of multiplying on the right by
{\mathbf{P}}
permutes the columns of
{\mathbf{Q}}
with possible changes of sign.

This shows in particular that there are rank-one connections which are neither twins nor slips. Such rank-one connections without mirror symmetry across the interface are observed for martensitic phase transformations, e.g. for
{\rm LaNbO}_{4}
(Jian & Wayman
jianwayman1995
, Jian & James
jianjames1997
) and for NiMnGa (Seiner et al.
seiner2019
).

Conjugates of slips are observed in ‘kink deformations’; see Inamura
inamura2019
, and for earlier work Starkey
starkey1968
.

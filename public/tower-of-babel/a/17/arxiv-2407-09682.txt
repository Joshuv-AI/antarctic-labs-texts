Multi-kinks and composite oscillons in a commensurable and non degenerate double sine-Gordon model
Jonathan Lozano-Mayo, Manuel Torres-Labansat

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce a commensurable and non-degenerate double sine-Gordon model, in which a partial breaking of vacuum degeneracy provides a mechanism for the emergence of static multi-kinks. These multi-kinks K n K_{n} are stable field configurations with internal structure, consisting of n n localized energy packets with well-defined separations. The properties of the multi-kinks are thoroughly analyzed, including the novel phenomenology that arises during their collisions. In particular, we observe the emergence of long-lived composite oscillons that reflect the original structure of the multi-kinks. The sub-kink’s positions and their vibration modes provide collective coordinates that are used to construct a phenomenological model, which offers a good qualitative explanation of the observed oscillon properties. Linear radiation effects are consistently incorporated into the model, revealing their important role in the synchronization of the oscillon’s vibrational components. In contrast, oscillon decay and the sudden annihilations observed in some collisions are driven by a novel staccato-like mechanism, whereby repeated crossings of the mass threshold by the dominant frequency generate a sequence of radiation bursts.

Introduction

Scalar field theories provide a versatile framework for modeling a wide variety of physical phenomena. In particular, they offer a natural setting for spontaneous symmetry breaking, a mechanism that underlies both phase transitions and the generation of particle masses through the Higgs mechanism. In the standard picture, spontaneous symmetry breaking arises as a control parameter of the potential is varied, producing multiple degenerate vacua. In some models, tuning an additional parameter can partially lift this degeneracy. The present work is devoted to exploring the dynamical consequences of such situations.

A distinctive feature of nonlinear scalar field theories is their ability to support solitons: spatially localized, stable, finite-energy configurations having a conserved topological charge
Manton and Sutcliffe (2004)
;
Vachaspati (2006)
;
Weinberg (2012)
. Even the simplest one-dimensional example, the
\phi^{4}
model with two degenerate minima, exhibits kink solutions whose dynamics remain an active subject of study
Dashen et al. (1974)
;
Campbell et al. (1983)
;
Kevrekidis and Cuevas-Maraver (2019)
. Of particular interest are kink-antikink collisions. At low impact velocities these typically produce long-lived bound states, or bions
Makhankov (1978)
. At higher velocities, collisions may lead to inelastic reflections in which the solitons escape to infinity after undergoing several bounces. The resulting outgoing-incoming velocity diagrams display resonance windows characteristic of chaotic scattering
Moshir (1981)
;
Campbell et al. (1983)
;
Anninos et al. (1991)
;
Simas et al. (2016)
. Much of this phenomenology can be qualitatively captured by reducing the full field theory to a small number of collective coordinates, most commonly the kink position and its internal (shape) mode. In this description, multi-bounce events arise from reversible energy exchange between translational and vibrational modes
Sugiyama (1979)
;
Takyi and Weigel (2016)
;
Manton et al. (2021b)
;
Manton et al. (2021a)
.

Another paradigmatic example is the sine-Gordon (SG) model
Scott (2003)
;
Cuevas-Maraver et al. (2014)
, an exactly integrable system whose analytical solutions can be constructed using the inverse scattering method
Ablowitz et al. (1973)
;
Zakharov et al. (1973)
. Besides static kinks, the SG model admits breathers and multi-soliton solutions, in which scattering occurs without radiation and solitons emerge unchanged apart from a phase shift. Many generalizations of sine-Gordon theory have been explored extensions that break exact integrability but retain some SG-like characteristics
Campbell et al. (1986)
.

Models featuring multiple degenerate vacua have also attracted significant attention. The
\phi^{6}
model is one of the most studied examples, with extensive analyses of its kink-antikink dynamics and resonance structure
Dorey et al. (2011)
;
Demirkaya et al. (2017)
;
Moradi Marjaneh et al. (2017)
;
Gani et al. (2020)
. Similar models are relevant for describing both first- and second-order phase transitions
Khare et al. (2014)
;
Suzuki and Zurek (2024)
, as well as long-range and non-exponential inter-kink forces
Manton (2019)
;
Christov et al. (2019)
;
Belendryasova and Gani (2019)
;
Christov et al. (2021)
. Some of the aforementioned models support multi-kink solutions with internal structure
Christ and Lee (1975)
;
de Brito G.P. (2014)
;
Demirkaya et al. (2017)
;
Lozano-Mayo and Torres-Labansat (2021)
;
Dorey et al. (2023)
;
Halcrow et al. (2024)
.

Nonlinear scalar theories also host oscillons: spatially localized, quasi-periodic, exceptionally long-lived configurations despite having no conserved topological charge. Beyond their intrinsic mathematical interest, oscillons appear in diverse high energy physics and cosmological scenarios, including post-inflationary reheating and phase-transition dynamics, see, e.g.,
Gleiser (1994)
;
Kolb and Tkachev (1994)
;
Graham (2007)
;
Amin and Shirokoff (2010)
;
Ollé et al. (2021)
.

In this work we introduce a commensurable and non-degenerate double
K_{n}
sine-Gordon model, which naturally brings together many of the themes mentioned above. In this model, the usual sine-Gordon potential is modulated by a second periodic term, effectively extending the periodicity from
\phi\to\phi+2\pi
to
\phi\to\phi+2\pi n
.
This structure produces
n
inequivalent kink solutions
k_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. Introducing a degeneracy-breaking parameter
\alpha
lifts the degeneracy between internal vacua while preserving the external ones, causing the individual kinks to merge into a single composite multi-kink configuration denoted as
K_{n}
.

Exact analytic expressions for
K_{n}
are not available, but we derive accurate approximate profiles that closely match the numerical solutions. These enable a detailed study of the multi-kink’s structure, including inter-kink forces, equilibrium separations, topological charge, size, and mass.

We also investigate multi-kink collisions. By varying the degeneracy-breaking parameter
\alpha
and the initial impact velocity, we uncover a diverse range of outcomes: inelastic reflections, annihilation processes, and the formation of long-lived composite oscillons.

To interpret these results, we develop an effective collective coordinate model based on the positions
x_{i}(t)
of the internal kinks and their vibrational modes. Crucially, we show that including radiation effects in the effective theory is essential to reproduce the synchronization phenomena observed in the oscillon dynamics of the full field theory. Finally, we present results that suggest that
oscillons decay through a staccato-like radiation mechanism.

The paper is organised as follows. Section
2
introduces the commensurable and non-degenerate double sine-Gordon model.
Section
3
presents the derivation of the collective coordinate model.
Section
4
analyzes collisions of the
K_{n}
multi-kinks with their corresponding
{\bar{K}}_{n}
anti-multi-kink.
Final remarks are given in section
5
.

2
The commensurable and non degenerate double sine-Gordon model

2.1
General considerations

The Lagrangian density for the scalar field
\phi(t,z)
is

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}\right)^{2}-V_{n,\alpha}.

(1)

Thus, the energy functional corresponding to this Lagrangian is given by the following expression

E\left[\phi\right]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\cal E}(t,z)dz=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}\right)^{2}+V_{n,\alpha}\right)dz,

(2)

whereas the field equation of motion is given as

\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial z^{2}}=-\frac{dV_{n,\alpha}}{d\phi}.

(3)

Focusing on static configurations, the previous equation becomes

\frac{d^{2}\phi}{dz^{2}}=\frac{dV_{n,\alpha}}{d\phi},

(4)

multiplying this equation by
d\phi/dz
and integrating yields the first order Bogomol’nyi equation
Bogomolny (1976)

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\phi}{dz}\right)^{2}=V_{n,\alpha}(\phi),

(5)

where the integration constant is selected as zero, so the kink energy obtained from eq. (
2
) is finite. We find convenient to define the superpotential
W_{n,\alpha}(\phi)

Boya and Casahorran (1989)
;
Manton (2023)
as

W_{n,\alpha}(\phi)=\int^{\phi}\sqrt{2\,V_{n,\alpha}(\phi)}\,d\phi,

(6)

this expression together with the Bogomol’nyi eq. (
5
) imply the relation
(d\phi/dz)=(dW_{n,\alpha}/d\phi)
.

2.2
The potential

In the proposed model the usual Sine-Gordon potential
(1-\cos\phi)
is modulated by a second Sine-Gordon-like term
\left(1+\cos\frac{\phi}{n}\right)
and shifted by the degeneracy-breaking parameter
\alpha
. As a result, the potential

V_{n,\alpha}\left(\phi\right)=\frac{1}{2(1+\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|)}\left(1-\cos\phi+\alpha\right)\left(1+\cos\frac{\phi}{n}\right),

(7)

takes the form shown in figure
1
.
For future reference, it is convenient to split the potential as
V_{n,\alpha}\left(\phi\right)=V_{n,0}\left(\phi\right)+\alpha\Delta_{n,\alpha}\left(\phi\right)
, with

\Delta_{n,\alpha}\left(\phi\right)=\frac{2}{2+\alpha}\left(\cos\frac{\phi}{2}\,\,\,\cos\frac{\phi}{2n}\right)^{2}.

(8)

The potential reduces to the usual Sine-Gordon form when
\alpha=0
and
n\to\infty
. In the opposite limit, as
\alpha\to\infty
, it also approaches a SG potential under the field redefinition

\phi\to\frac{\phi}{n}+\pi
. The potential
V_{n,\alpha}
is invariant under the transformations
\phi\to-\phi
and
\phi\to\phi+2\pi n
. Unless otherwise specified, we focus on solutions within the sector
\phi\in\{-\pi n,\pi n\}
sector. For simplicity, we assume that
n
is an even integer, similar results hold for odd
n
.

Figure 1:
Potential
V_{n,\alpha}(\phi)
, the parameters are selected as:
n=4
, with:
\alpha=0.1
(black-continuous line),
\alpha=0.6
(red-dotted line) and
\alpha=1.5
(blue-dashed line).

The
n+1
potential minima can be determined numerically, a useful analytical approximation can be obtained in the small-
\alpha
regime

v_{i}\approx\ 2i\pi+\frac{\alpha}{2n}\frac{\sin\left(\frac{2\pi i}{n}\right)}{\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\pi i}{n}\right)},\,\,\,\,\,\,i=\pm\left(0,1,2,....,\frac{n}{2}-1\right),\qquad v_{\pm\frac{n}{2}}=\pm n\pi.

(9)

Associated with each potential minimum, there are perturbative scalar excitations whose masses are determined by
m_{i}^{2}=\frac{d^{2}V_{n,\alpha}\left(\phi\right)}{d^{2}\phi}{\Big|}_{\phi=v_{i}}
, which, for small
\alpha
, are approximately given by:

m_{i}\approx\sqrt{\cos^{2}\left(\frac{i\,\pi}{n}\right)-\frac{\alpha}{4n^{2}}\left(6+n^{2}+(n^{2}-4)\cos\left(\frac{2\pi i}{n}\right)\right)}\,,\qquad m_{\pm\frac{n}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{2n^{2}}}.

(10)

For
\alpha=0
, the model admits
n
kink solutions
k_{i}
. As
\alpha
increases within the
0<\alpha\leq\alpha_{1}
interval, these
n
kinks merge into a single multi-kink configuration
K_{n}
, characterized by well-defined localized energy lumps. The value
\alpha_{1}
marks the onset of significant overlap among sub-kinks, will be determined later in eq. (
26
).
In the subsequent interval
\alpha_{1}<\alpha\leq\alpha_{2}
, the distinction between individual sub-kinks becomes less pronounced. The
\alpha_{2}
threshold corresponds to the value at which the internal local minima of the potential start disappearing. This critical value can be estimated by requiring that the mass
m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
of the internal scalar excitation, given in eq. (
10
), vanish. This leads to

\alpha_{2}=\frac{4n^{2}\sin^{2}(\frac{\pi}{n})}{6+n^{2}-(n^{2}-4)\cos(\frac{2\pi}{n})}.

(11)

For
\alpha>\alpha_{2}
, the field configuration converges to a single soliton, as the internal substructure associated to the multi-kinks is gradually erased.
Notice that
\alpha_{2}
has a weak dependence on
n
,
\alpha_{2}\in\{1.6,1.33\}
for
n\in\{2,\infty\}
.
Although stable soliton solutions are not expected for negative values of
\alpha
, the model admits the existence of saddle-point solutions that interpolate between false and true vacua. These unstable, energy-maximizing configurations known as sphalerons
Manton (2023)
;
Alonso-Izquierdo et al. (2023)
represent transitions over the potential barrier and will be explored in future work.

2.3
n
kinks
k_{i}
in the
\alpha=0
degenerate regime

For
\alpha=0
the potential minima are located at
v_{i}=2i\pi
with
i=\pm(0,1,2,....,\frac{n}{2})
, so the model admits
n
kink solutions
\phi_{k_{i}}\in\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
,
i=-\frac{n}{2},...,0,....,\frac{n}{2}
, each carrying the same topological charge
q_{k_{i}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}(v_{i+1}-v_{i})=1
, along with their corresponding antikinks
{\bar{k}}_{i}
, with charge

q_{\bar{k}_{i}}=-1
.
The location of the potential maxima
\Omega_{i}
deviate from those of the standard sine-Gordon model, where

\Omega_{i}=\pm(2i-1)\pi
. In this case, their positions are determined by the solutions to the transcendental equation
n\cot(\frac{\phi}{2})-\tan(\frac{\phi}{2n})=0
. Notice that for
\alpha=0
, the masses
m_{\pm\frac{n}{2}}
vanishes.

The superpotential, given in eq. (
6
) takes the form,

W[\phi]=-\frac{4n}{n^{2}-1}\left(n\cos\frac{\phi}{2}\cos\frac{\phi}{2n}+\sin\frac{\phi}{2}\sin\frac{\phi}{2n}\right),

(12)

from which the kink masses are computed as:

M_{k_{i}}=W[v_{i}]-W[v_{i-1}]=\frac{4n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}\left(\cos\frac{v_{i}}{2n}+\cos\frac{v_{i-1}}{2n}\right),\qquad i=\pm\left(1,2,....,\frac{n}{2}\right).

(13)

It is interesting to observe that, using eqs. (
10
,
13
), the masses
M_{k_{i}}
are related to the scalar field masses evaluated at the neighboring minima
v_{i}
and
v_{i-1}
through the following expression:

M_{k_{i}}=\frac{4n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}\left(m_{i}+m_{i-1}\right)
.

The soliton
\phi_{k_{i}}(z)
configurations correspond to the solutions of the equation

\frac{d\phi}{dz}=2\sin\frac{\phi}{2}\cos\frac{\phi}{2n}.

(14)

For
n=2
, eq. (
14
) admits the implicit analytical solution
\ln\left[\tan(\frac{\phi}{8})\right]+\sec(\frac{\phi}{4})=z
. For any other value of
n
, the equation must be solved numerically. However, a very good analytical approximation can be derived by noting that, within each kink
\phi_{k_{i}}\in\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}
sector, the function
\cos\frac{\phi}{2n}
varies slowly compared to
\sin\frac{\phi}{2}.
This motivates an approach we refer to as the smooth-modulation approximation, which becomes increasingly accurate as
n
, grows. Under this approximation, if the
\cos\frac{\phi}{2n}
term is evaluated at the boundary points
\phi\sim v_{i-1}
or
\phi\sim v_{i}
, the solutions of eq. (
14
) coincide with the well known SG field solution with the asymptotic behavior
\phi(z)\,\sim v_{i-1}+const\,e^{m_{i-1}z}
or
\phi(z)\,\sim v_{i}-const\,e^{-m_{i}z}
as
z\to\mp\infty
.
These results hold for all kinks except those near the absolute minima, where according to eq. (
10
) the scalar mass
m_{\pm n/2}
vanishes. In these special cases, the correct asymptotic behavior becomes

\phi(z)\,\sim\mp n\pi\pm\,\frac{4n}{z}
. This analysis suggests an ansatz for the kink solution
\phi_{k_{i}}(z)\in\{v_{i-1},v_{i}\}
, that reproduces the correct asymptotic forms in each sector, and is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{k_{i}}(z)\,=v_{i-1}\,+

\displaystyle\,4\,\tan^{-1}G_{i}\left[z\right],\qquad i=1......,\frac{n}{2},

(15)

\displaystyle G_{i}\left[z\right]=\frac{1}{a_{i}e^{-m_{i-1}z}+b_{i}e^{-m_{i}z}},\,\,\,i<\frac{n}{2},

\displaystyle\qquad G_{\frac{n}{2}}\left[z\right]=\ln\left[1+\frac{1}{c_{n}\,e^{-m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}z}+d_{n}\,e^{-z/n}}\right].

The solutions for negative values of
i
are obtained via spatial and field reflection symmetry, using the relation
\phi_{k_{(-i)}}(z)=-\phi_{k_{i}}(-z)
,
i=1......,\frac{n}{2}
. Given that
m_{i-1}>m_{i}
, the
\phi_{k_{i}}(z)
ansatz correctly reproduces the asymptotic exponential tails determined by the scalar masses
m_{i-1}
(for
z\to-\infty
) and
m_{i}
(for
z\to+\infty
). In the
i=\frac{n}{2}
case, the function
G_{\frac{n}{2}}\left[z\right]

captures both the exponential
\phi(z)\,\sim(n-2)\pi+\frac{4}{c_{n}}\,e^{-m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}z}
and long range
\phi(z)\,\sim n\pi-\frac{4n}{z}
asymptotic tail behavior. The coefficients
a_{i}
,
b_{i}
,
c_{n}
and
d_{n}
are determined by enforcing two conditions:
(i)
at the kink center the field value matches the location of the potential maxima,
i.e.

\phi_{k_{i}}(0)=\Omega_{i}
, and
(ii)
the field derivative at this point satisfies eq. (
14
). Using these conditions, the explicit expressions for the coefficients are derived as

a_{i}=\frac{\gamma_{i}\mu_{i}-m_{i}}{\gamma_{i}(m_{i-1}-m_{i})}\qquad b_{i}=\frac{m_{i-1}-\gamma_{i}\mu_{i}}{\gamma_{i}(m_{i-1}-m_{i})},\qquad i=1......,\frac{n}{2}-1,

(16)

where
\gamma_{i}=\tan\frac{\Omega_{i}-v_{i-1}}{4}
and

\mu_{i}=\sin\frac{\Omega_{i}}{2}\cos\frac{\Omega_{i}}{2n}/\left(2\sin^{2}\frac{(\Omega_{i}-v_{i-1})}{4}\right)
, whereas

c_{n}=\frac{n\nu_{n}-\kappa_{n}}{n\sin\frac{\pi}{n}-1}\qquad d_{n}=\frac{n(\kappa_{n}\sin\frac{\pi}{n}-\nu_{n})}{n\sin\frac{\pi}{n}-1},

(17)

with
\nu_{n}=\frac{\kappa_{n}}{4}(\kappa_{n}+1)(1+\gamma_{n}^{2})\sin\left(\frac{\Omega_{n/2}}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\Omega_{n/2}}{2n}\right)
,
\kappa_{n}=(e^{\gamma_{n}}-1)^{-1}
, and
\gamma_{n}=\tan\frac{\Omega_{n/2}-(n-2)\pi}{4}
.

2.4
Multi-kink
K_{n}
in the
\alpha>0

In this section, we analyze the properties of the
K_{n}
multi-kink that emerges in the
\alpha>0
phase. Although the minima
v_{i}
are no longer located at integer multiples of
2\pi
, the topological charge
Q_{n}
remains straightforward to compute. This is because the values at the absolute minima,
v_{\pm n/2}=\pm n\pi
, are not affected. Consequently, the multi-kink interpolates between
\phi_{z\to-\infty}=v_{-n/2}
and
\phi_{z\to\infty}=v_{n/2}
, yielding a total topological charge
Q_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}(v_{n/2}-v_{-n/2})=n
, which corresponds to the sum of the topological charges of the
n
constituent sub-kinks
Q_{n}=\sum_{i}q_{k_{i}}=n
.

2.4.1
Multi-kink mass
M_{K_{(n,\alpha)}}

Other multi-kink properties can be analyzed using the smooth-modulation approximation. To compute the total mass
M_{K_{(n,\alpha)}}
, we decompose it into two contributions: the sum of the internal kink masses,
M_{K(n,0)}
, and the
\alpha
-dependent binding interaction energy,
\Delta V_{K(n,\alpha)}
. These correspond to the first and second terms, respectively, in the following expression:

M_{K(n,\alpha)}\,=\int_{-n\pi}^{n\pi}\sqrt{2V_{n,0}(\phi)}d\phi+\int_{-n\pi}^{n\pi}\left(\sqrt{2V_{n,\alpha}(\phi)}-\sqrt{2V_{n,0}(\phi)}\right)d\phi\,.

(18)

The first term is the sum of the internal kink masses
M_{k_{i}}
. Using the results in eq. (
13
), it follows that

M_{K(n,0)}=\sum_{i=-\frac{n}{2}}^{\frac{n}{2}}M_{k_{i}}=\frac{8n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}\cot\frac{\pi}{2n}.

(19)

To estimate
\Delta V_{K(n,\alpha)}
, the integration domain
\phi\in\{-n\pi,n\pi\}
is partitioned into
n
subintervals of the form
\phi\in\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
. Within each subinterval, the smooth-modulation approximation is applied by taking

1+\cos(\frac{\phi}{n})\approx 1+\cos(\frac{v_{i}+v_{i+1}}{2n})
, hence
\Delta V_{K(n,\alpha)}
is readily computed as follows:

\displaystyle\Delta V_{K(n,\alpha)}\,

\displaystyle\approx\sqrt{8}\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\cos\left(\frac{(2i+1)\pi}{2n}\right)\int_{2i\pi}^{2(i+1)\pi}\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+\alpha-\cos\phi}{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}-\sqrt{1-\cos\phi)}\right)d\phi

(20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\cos\left(\frac{(2i+1)\pi}{2n}\right)\left(\sqrt{32}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\left(E\left[(i+1)\pi,-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]-E\left[i\pi,-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]\right)-16\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{\frac{32\alpha}{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}E\left[-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]-8\right)\,\csc\left(\frac{\pi}{2n}\right).

The integrals involved in the computation are expressed in terms of the incomplete elliptic integral of the second kind,
E\left[i\pi,-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]=2i\,E\left[-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]
,
evaluated at an argument that simplifies the expression to a complete elliptic integral. This allows us to explicitly evaluate the sum, leading to the final result presented in eq. (
20
). Figure
3

(a)
compares this analytical approximation with the numerical integration of the second term in eq. (
18
). The smooth-modulation approximation shows excellent agreement across the entire range of
\alpha
values.

By adding
M_{K(n,0)}
and
\Delta V_{K(n,\alpha)}
, we obtain the total multi-kink mass:

M_{K(n,\alpha)}\,=\left[\frac{8n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2n}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{32\,\alpha}{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}E\left[-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]-8\right]\csc\left(\frac{\pi}{2n}\right).

(21)

Using the expansion

\sqrt{\frac{32\,\alpha}{1+\alpha}}E\left[-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]-8\approx\alpha\left(\ln\frac{32}{\alpha}-1\right)
for
\alpha\ll 1
, the small
\alpha
limit of eq. (
21
) yields

M_{K(n,\alpha)}\,\approx\left[\frac{8n^{2}}{n^{2}-1}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2n}\right)+\alpha\ln\left[\frac{12}{\alpha}\right]\right]\csc\left(\frac{\pi}{2n}\right).

(22)

2.4.2
Multi-kink profile
\Phi_{n}(z)
and size
\sigma_{K_{(n,\alpha)}}

Although the mass and charge soliton are well defined for all positive values of
\alpha
, the emergence of a well-resolved multi-kink structure is restricted to the range
0<\alpha<\alpha_{1}
, where
\alpha_{1}
denotes the upper bound beyond which the internal sub-kinks can no longer be clearly distinguished. We now proceed to estimate the value of
\alpha_{1}
.

To begin, note that the separation between adjacent sub-kinks,
\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}
, can be inferred from the Bogomol’nyi equation, eq. (
5
), under the assumption that each sub-kink is centered at the point where the field
\phi(z)
reaches a local maximum of the potential,
i.e.

\phi(\xi_{i})=\Omega_{i}
. Accordingly
Manton (2019)
;
Lozano-Mayo and Torres-Labansat (2021)
,

\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}=\int_{\Omega_{i}}^{\Omega_{i+1}}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{2\left[V_{n,\alpha}(\phi)\right]}}.

(23)

Here
\phi(\xi_{i})=\Omega_{i}
and
\phi(\xi_{i+1})=\Omega_{i+1}
are contiguous maxima of the

V_{n,\alpha}(\phi)
potential.
Recalling that in each region
\phi\in\{\Omega_{i-1},\Omega_{i}\}
the term
1+\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{n}\right)
can be approximated as
1+\cos(\frac{v_{i}}{n})=2m_{i}^{2}
, hence a direct integration yields:

\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}\approx\frac{1}{2m_{i}}\int_{\Omega_{i}}^{\Omega_{i+1}}\sqrt{\frac{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}{\frac{\alpha}{2}+\sin^{2}\frac{\phi}{2}\ }}d\phi=\frac{1}{m_{i}}\sqrt{\frac{2(1+\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\alpha}}\,\left(F\left[\frac{\Omega_{i+1}}{2},-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]-F\left[\frac{\Omega_{i}}{2},-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]\right),

(24)

where
F\left[\phi,k\right]
is the incomplete elliptic integral function of the first kind. The previous expression can be reduced approximating
\omega_{i}\approx(2i-1)\pi
, and using the property
F\left[\frac{2i+1}{2}\pi,-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]=(2i+1)K\left[-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]
, with
K\left[-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]
being the complete elliptic integral function of the first kind, then eq. (
24
) becomes

\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}\approx\frac{2}{m_{i}}\sqrt{\frac{2(1+\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\alpha}}\,K\left[-\frac{2}{\alpha}\right]\approx\frac{1}{m_{i}}\log\left[\frac{32}{\alpha}\right].

(25)

Where the last result applies in the
\alpha\ll 1
limit. We highlight that the
\log\left[\frac{const}{\alpha}\right]
dependence for the sub-kinks separation and
\alpha\,\log\left[\frac{const}{\alpha}\right]
for the
multi-kink mass are generic results that can be deduced using the thin-wall approximation
for scalar field models in which the vacuum degeneracy is partially broken by an small
\alpha
parameter (see,
e.g.

;
de Brito G.P. (2014)
;
Demirkaya et al. (2017)

;
Dorey et al. (2023)
).

The last result in eq. (

\phi_{k_{i}}(z)
in eq. (

.
To prevent overlap between adjacent kinks, we require that the separation between sub-kinks exceeds their width, i.e.,
\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}>\sigma_{k_{i}}

\alpha<\alpha_{1}=32\,\exp{\left\{-8\frac{m_{i}}{m_{i}+m_{i+1}}\right\}}\approx 0.59\,.

(26)

Notice that the previous condition is independent of the value of
n
.

The previous results enable us to construct an approximate expression for the

(27)

(28)

with

2.5
Sub-kinks and multi-kink spectra

Considering a linear perturbation of the form
\psi(z)=\varphi(z)+\eta(z)e^{i\omega t}
, around a soliton solution
\varphi(z)
, the fluctuation spectrum is obtained from the eigenvalue equation

\left[-\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}+U_{eff}(z)\right]\eta(z)=\omega^{2}\eta(z),

(31)

where the effective potential for the linear excitations is given by
U_{eff}(z)=V^{"}_{n,\alpha}(\varphi(z))
.
Equation (
31
)
was solved numerically for two cases: (i) the
\alpha=0
kinks
k_{i}
, and (ii) the multi-kink
K_{n}
. In both cases, the resulting spectra contain only the translational zero mode and the continuum states, with no evidence of discrete internal (shape) modes.

Although no shape modes are found for the stationary
K_{n}
multi-kink configuration, we expect the existence of internal vibrational modes associated with the oscillations of its constituent sub-kinks. To investigate this possibility, we take as the initial configuration an ansatz composed of two contiguous sub-kinks separated by a distance
2a
, given by

\varphi_{(i,i+1)}(z,a)=\phi_{k_{i}}(z+a)+\phi_{k_{i+1}}(z-a)-v_{i}.

(32)

Equation (
31
) was then solved using the effective potential
U_{(i,i+1)}(z,a)=V^{"}_{n,\alpha}(\varphi_{(i,i+1)}(z,a))
.
The results for
n=4
and
i=1
are shown in figure
5
, where one observes that the effective potential exhibits a double-well structure, giving rise to two discrete modes. The first corresponds to a quasi-zero mode, whose eigenfunction

\eta_{S}(a,z)
is approximately the symmetric combination of the zero modes of
k_{i}
and
k_{i+1}

\eta_{S,A}(a,z)=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{d\phi_{k_{i}}(z+a)}{dz}\pm\frac{d\phi_{k_{i+1}}(z-a)}{dz}\right),

(33)

in contrast, the antisymmetric combination
\eta_{A}(a,z)
represents a genuine discrete mode trapped between the sub-kinks. This interpretation is supported by the results discussed in section
3.1
, where we determine the interaction force

3
Collective coordinate model

We construct a collective coordinate model to describe the dynamics of the multi-kink. Considering that the static ansatz
\Phi_{K_{n}}(z)
in eq. (
27
), obtained by superposing
n
sub-kinks, provides an accurate approximation to the exact solution, the position
X_{i}
of each sub-kink
{k_{i}}
is promoted to a dynamical variable
x_{i}(t)
. These coordinates evolve according to classical equations of motion under the forces exerted by the adjacent sub-kinks
k_{i-1}
and
k_{i+1}
. Approximate expressions for these forces, as well as for the interaction between a
K_{n}
multi-kink and its anti-multi-kink, are derived below.

3.1
Forces between
k_{i}-k_{i+1}
sub-kinks

Previously, Manton
Manton (2019)
(see also
Dorey et al. (2011)
) developed an approach to analyze kink-antikink interactions characterized by long-range power-law tails, rather than the conventional exponential decay. In what follows, we extend this framework to the multi-kink case formulating a dynamical Bogomol’nyi relation. This generalization enables us to derive the effective forces
F_{{i,i+1}}
acting between contiguous sub-kinks
\phi_{k_{i}}
and
\phi_{k_{i+1}}
that compose a multi-kink configuration
K_{n}
. These forces provide the foundation for the collective coordinate model developed in the next section.

Let us assume that the multi-kink configuration
\phi(z)
satisfies the exact static field equation eqs. (
4
,
5
), except in the region
\xi_{i}\leq z\leq\eta_{i+1}
, where the field
\varphi(z,t)
departs from the exact
\phi(z)
. Outside this region, the exact field configurations
\phi(z)
remains intact. The modified field becomes time dependent and is centered at
z=x_{i+1}(t)\equiv x(t)
, thus is written as
\varphi(z-x(t))
, where
x(t)
represents the position of the sub-kink
k_{i+1}
. At equilibrium
\varphi(z-x(t))
is expected to relax to the exact configuration
\phi(z)
and
x(t)\to\xi_{i+1}
. Substituting
\varphi(z-x(t))
, into the time dependent field equation eq. (
3
) gives

\gamma^{-2}\frac{d^{2}\varphi}{d^{2}y}-\frac{dV_{n,\alpha}}{d\varphi}+\ddot{x}\frac{d\varphi}{dy}=0,

(35)

where
y=z-x(t)
is the argument of
\varphi
and
\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-\dot{x}^{2}}
is the Lorentz contraction factor. Eq. (
35
) can be considered a equation for the field
\varphi(y)
that evolves adiabatically on time trough its dependence on
x(t)
. In what follows we shall assume that the acceleration
\ddot{x}
, and the velocity
\dot{x}
are small, so only leading terms in these quantities will be retained. Multiplying eq. (
35
) by the term
d\varphi/dy
, and utilizing the relation
d\varphi/dy=dW_{n,\alpha}/d\varphi
leads to the following expression

\frac{d}{dy}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial y}\right)^{2}-V_{n,\alpha}+\ddot{x}\,W_{n,\alpha}\right)=0,

(36)

that is easily integrated from an arbitrary value of
z
to
z=\eta_{i+1}
, where
\varphi(\eta_{i+1})=v_{i+1}
, to give the following expression

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\varphi}{dy}\right)^{2}=\left[V_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)-\ddot{x}\left(W_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)-W_{n,\alpha}(v_{i+1})\right)\right],

(37)

where we used the fact that
\varphi
satisfy the relation

\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\varphi}{dy}\right)^{2}-V_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)\right]_{z=\eta_{i+1}}\!\!\!\!\!\!=0
eq. (
5
), at the boundary.
Comparing with eq. (
5
), we observe that eq. (
37
) can be considered an adiabatic time dependent Bogomol’nyi relation, where the potential
V_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)
is replaced by an effective potential
V_{eff}=V_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)-\ddot{x}\left(W_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)-W_{n,\alpha}(v_{i+1})\right)
.

For a given trajectory
x(t)
, the solution of eq. (
37
) determines the adiabatic evolution of the field
\varphi
. Additionally, an integration of this equation throws an expression for the evolution of the coordinate
x(t)
.
The limits are selected from
z=\xi_{i}
where
\varphi=\Omega_{i}
, to
z=x(t)
with
\varphi(x(t))\to\Omega_{i+1}
, to obtain

\int_{\Omega_{i}}^{\Omega_{i+1}}\frac{d\varphi}{\sqrt{2\left[V_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)+{\ddot{x}}\left(W_{n,\alpha}(v_{i+1})-W_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)\right)\right]}}=x(t)-\xi_{i}.

(38)

This equation represents an implicit relation between the kink acceleration
{\ddot{x}}
and
x(t)
, that is the effective equation of motion for the coordinate
x(t)
. In particular, equilibrium is reached when
\ddot{x}\to 0
and
x(t)\to\xi_{i+1}
, reducing to the correct expression for the separation between the contiguous
{k_{i}}
and
{k_{i+1}}
kinks given in eq. (
23
).

Approximated analytical expressions for the equation of motion for
x(t)
and the equilibrium separation
\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}
can be obtained from eq. (
38
). The leading contribution to the integral in eq. (
38
) arises from values of
\varphi
in the region close to the potential minimum
\varphi\sim v_{i}
. Hence we expand the potential
V_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)=V_{n,0}(\varphi)+\alpha\Delta_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)\approx\frac{1}{2}m_{i}^{2}(\phi-v_{i})^{2}+\alpha\Delta_{n,\alpha}(v_{i})
, where
m_{i}^{2}=(d^{2}V_{n,\alpha}/d^{2}\phi)_{\phi=v_{i}}
is the mass of the perturbative scalar oscillations around
v_{i}
, and we verify that to leading order
W_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)\approx W_{n,\alpha}(v_{i})
. The resulting expression is readily integrated, and identifying the
k_{i}
kink mass
M_{k_{i+1}}=W_{n,\alpha}(v_{i+1})-W_{n,\alpha}(v_{i})
, the relation between
\ddot{x}
and
x
takes the form of a Newton equation of motion,

M_{k_{i+1}}\,\ddot{x}_{i+1}=F_{\{i,i+1\}}\left(\xi_{i},x_{i+1}\right)=2m_{i}^{2}\left[(\Omega_{i+1}-v_{i})(v_{i}-\Omega_{i})\right]e^{-m_{i}(x_{i+1}-\xi_{i})}-\alpha\Delta_{n,\alpha}(v_{i}).

(39)

The first contribution corresponds to the repulsive force that typically acts between kinks, while an additional attractive term arises from the negative pressure exerted by the false-vacuum region trapped between the kink’s pair. At equilibrium,
x(t)\to\xi_{i+1}
, and the condition of vanishing total force determines the equilibrium separation as

\delta\xi_{\{i,i+1\}}=\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}=\frac{1}{m_{i}}\,\ln\left[\frac{2\left(\Omega_{i+1}-v_{i}\right)\left(v_{i}-\Omega_{i}\right)}{\alpha}\right],

(40)

where, to leading order in
\alpha
, we take into account that
\Delta_{n,\alpha}(v_{i})\approx m_{i}^{2}
.
The potential energy associated to
F_{\{i,i+1\}}
is given as

U_{\{i,i+1\}}\left(x_{i+1}-x_{i}\right)=\alpha m_{i}\left[e^{-m_{i}\left[y-\delta\xi_{\{i,i+1\}}\right]}-1+m_{i}\left(y-\delta\xi_{\{i,i+1\}}\right)\right]_{y=x_{i+1}-x_{i}}.

(41)

For small oscillations of the sub-kinks around their equilibrium separations, these results allow us to determine the corresponding oscillation frequency as

\omega^{2}_{i,i+1}=\frac{\kappa_{i,i+1}}{M_{k_{i}}}\,,\qquad\kappa_{i,i+1}=\alpha\,m_{i}^{3}\,.

(42)

The previous results also apply for negative values of the indices, with the replacement
F_{-(i+1),-i}(a)\to-F_{i,i+1}(a)
.
This extension includes the case of
F_{-1,1}(a)
, taking into account that
v_{0}=0
,
\Omega_{-1}=-\Omega_{1}
leading to
\kappa_{-1,1}\equiv\kappa_{0}=\alpha\,m_{0}^{3}
.

The predicted values for
\delta\xi_{\{1,2\}}
and
\omega_{1,2}
in eqs. (
40
,
42
) are compared with the results obtained from the fluctuation spectra obtained from the solution of eq. (
31
) in figure
5
, showing a good agreement in the

\alpha\leq 0.5
regime.

3.2
Force between
K_{n}
and
{\bar{K}}_{n}
multi-kinks

As previously mentioned, the
K_{n}-{\bar{K}}n
interaction is dominated by the force between the nearest neighboring sub-kinks,
k_{\frac{n}{2}}
and
{\bar{k}}_{-\frac{n}{2}}
. We now show that Bogomol’nyi-type relations, similar to eq. (
38
), together with information extracted from the full field simulations of the
K_{n}-{\bar{K}}_{n}
collisions, see figure
15
, allow us to derive an expression for the interaction force that incorporates both attractive and repulsive contributions, associated respectively with large and short separations between the colliding solitons.

For the
k_{\frac{n}{2}}-{\bar{k}}_{-\frac{n}{2}}
field configuration, we consider the ansatz given in eq. (
34
). In this ansatz, the position of the left sub-kink is fixed at
x=x_{0}<0
, while the antikink position is promoted to a dynamical variable
x(t)
. Consequently, the effective field
\varphi(z-x(t))
is expected to satisfy eqs. (
35
) and (
36
). The field
\varphi

describes the evolution of the sub-kink within the region
\{v_{\frac{n}{2}-1}-\pi\leq\varphi\leq v_{\frac{n}{2}}+\pi\}
.

Let us first consider the large-separation regime. From figure
15
, we observe that
\varphi(z-x(t))
is localized around the absolute minimum
v_{\frac{n}{2}}=n\pi
of the potential. It is therefore convenient to integrate eq. (
36
) from an arbitrary value of
z
to
z_{r}
, where
\varphi(z_{r})=v_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
. This procedure yields an expression analogous to eq. (
37
), with the replacement
v_{i+1}\to v_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
. Performing an additional integration, we obtain the following expresion:

\int_{(n-3)\pi}^{(n+1)\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{\sqrt{2\left[V_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)+{\ddot{x}}\left(W_{n,\alpha}((n-2)\,\pi)-W_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)\right)\right]}}=x(t)+x_{0}.

(43)

The leading contribution to the integral arises from field values in the vicinity of
\varphi\sim n\pi
. This justifies the approximations
V_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)\approx\frac{1}{2}m_{\frac{n}{2}}^{2}(\varphi-n\,\pi)^{2}
,
and
W_{n,\alpha}(\varphi)\approx W_{n,\alpha}(n\pi)
. Under these assumptions, eq. (
43
) reduces to

\int_{(n-3)\pi}^{(n+1)\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{\sqrt{m_{\frac{n}{2}}^{2}(\varphi-n\pi)^{2}-2\,{\ddot{x}}\,M_{\frac{n}{2}}}}=x(t)+x_{0},

(44)

where we have identified the sub-kink mass as
M_{\frac{n}{2}}=W_{n,\alpha}(n\pi)-W_{n,\alpha}((n-2)\,\pi)
.
Since the acceleration is expected to be negative,
\ddot{x}=-|\ddot{x}|
, the integral is well defined and can be evaluated straightforwardly. This yields the following expression for the long-range interaction force
M_{\frac{n}{2}}\,\ddot{x}=-6\pi^{2}m_{\frac{n}{2}}^{2}\,\exp\left(-3m_{\frac{n}{2}}\,(x+x_{0})\right)
.

We now consider the regime in which the sub-kinks are in close proximity. Figure
15

(b)
shows that the field amplitude near the central region (
z\sim 0
) is significantly reduced, fluctuating around the false vacuum
v_{\frac{n}{2}-1}=(n-2)\pi
, rather than around the true vacuum
v_{\frac{n}{2}}=n\pi
.
This observation motivates repeating the previous analysis, but now expanding around
v_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
. In practice, this corresponds to implementing the substitutions

v_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\Longleftrightarrow v_{\frac{n}{2}}
and
m_{\frac{n}{2}}\Longleftrightarrow m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
within the integrals appearing in eqs. (
43
) and (
44
). Proceeding in this way, we obtain

\int_{(n-3)\pi}^{(n+1)\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{\sqrt{m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}^{2}(\varphi-(n-2)\pi)^{2}+2\,{\ddot{x}}\,M_{\frac{n}{2}}}}=x(t)+x_{0}.

(45)

This expression is well defined for
\ddot{x}>0
, corresponding to a positive acceleration and therefore signaling the presence of a repulsive interaction. Explicitly, the repulsive force is given by
M_{\frac{n}{2}}\,\ddot{x}=6\pi^{2}m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}^{2}\,\exp\left(-3m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\,(x+x_{0})\right)
.

By combining both the large-separation (attractive) and short-range (repulsive) contributions, we obtain the following:

F_{k_{\frac{n}{2}},\bar{k}_{-{\frac{n}{2}}}}(x)=6\,\pi^{2}\,\left(m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}^{2}\,e^{-3\,m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\,(x+x_{0})}-m_{\frac{n}{2}}^{2}\,e^{-3\,m_{\frac{n}{2}}\,(x+x_{0})}\right).

(46)

The value of
x_{0}
can be determined by observing that, in the closest-collision regime, the
k_{\frac{n}{2}}
and
{\bar{k}}{-\frac{n}{2}}
sub-kinks oscillate around the origin. Therefore, imposing the equilibrium condition
x=x_{eq}=0
fixes
x_{0}
as

x_{0}=\frac{1}{3\,\left(m_{\,\frac{n}{2}-1}-m_{\frac{n}{2}}\right)}\,\log\left(\frac{m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{m_{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)^{2}\,,

(47)

which leads to the final expression for the
k_{\frac{n}{2}}-{\bar{k}}_{-\frac{n}{2}}
interaction force

F_{k_{\frac{n}{2}},\bar{k}_{\!\!-{\frac{n}{2}}\!\!}}(x)=C_{n,\alpha}\,\left(e^{-3\,m_{\frac{n}{2}\!\!-1}\,x}\!\!\!-e^{-3\,m_{\frac{n}{2}}\,x}\right),\quad\quad C_{n,\alpha}=6\,\pi^{2}\,m^{2}_{\frac{n}{2}}\,\exp\left[-3\,m_{\frac{n}{2}}x_{0}\,\right].

(48)

We recall that, according to eq. (
10
), the scalar meson mass associated with fluctuations around the global minima behaves as
m_{\frac{n}{2}}\sim\sqrt{\alpha}
, and is therefore significantly smaller than the masses associated with fluctuations around local minima, namely
m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
. Consequently, eq. (
48
) describes a Morse-type interaction, characterized by a long-range attractive component with characteristic scale
1/m_{\frac{n}{2}}
, together with a short-range repulsive component with scale
1/m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
. The corresponding interaction potential is given by

U_{k_{\frac{n}{2}},\bar{k}_{-{\frac{n}{2}}}}(x)=\,\frac{1}{3}\,C_{n,\alpha}\,\left(\frac{e^{-3\,m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\,x}}{m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}}-\frac{e^{-3\,m_{\frac{n}{2}}\,x}}{m_{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)\,,

(49)

while oscillations around the potential minimum have a frequency given by

\omega^{2}_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{\bar{n}}{2}}=\frac{\kappa_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{\bar{n}}{2}}}{M_{\frac{n}{2}}}\,,\quad\quad\varkappa_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{\bar{n}}{2}}=3\,(m_{\frac{n}{2}-1}-m_{\frac{n}{2}})\,C_{n,\alpha}\,.

(50)

An advantage of using the Bogomol’nyi-type relations, eqs. (
44
) and (
45
), is that they do not require explicit expressions for the soliton field configurations. The ansatz in eq. (
34
) was introduced only to define the relevant kink and antikink coordinates.

A more precise determination of the
K_{n}-{\bar{K}}_{n}
interaction could, in principle, be obtained within the moduli space formalism
Manton et al. (2021b)
;
Manton et al. (2021a)
. However, this would require a more accurate characterization of the field configuration, taking into account both the short-distance behavior and the asymptotic regime of large separations between the colliding solitons, features that are not incorporated into eq. (
34
). For the purposes of the present work, we consider that the expression given in eq. (
48
) provides a sufficiently accurate description of the interaction.

3.3
Radiation energy losses

Previous investigations have mainly attributed radiation effects to nonlinear mechanisms. Examples include: (i) the decay of a single kink shape mode through the excitation of higher harmonics of a fundamental frequency lying below the scalar mass threshold
Manton and Merabet (1997)
;
Navarro-Obregón et al. (2023)
; and (ii) nonlinear radiation mechanisms in oscillons
Gleiser (1994)
;
Ollé et al. (2021)
, including the emission of staccato radiation bursts when monotonically increasing frequencies cross the mass radiation threshold
Dorey et al. (2020)
. The consequences of these mechanisms for the present study will be discussed in section
4.2.3
. Here, however, we focus our attention on a linear radiation scheme, which becomes essential for explaining oscillon stability as well as the synchronization of its vibrational components.

The
K_{n}-{\bar{K}}_{n}
collision spectra displays an ample range of characteristics frequencies

\omega_{l}
. Although many of these frequencies lie below the scalar mass thresholds
\omega_{l}<m_{i}
, there is a significant number of frequencies located above the
m_{\frac{n}{2}}
threshold that contribute to the radiative energy losses. This feature makes it possible to establish a first-order perturbative scheme in which back-reaction radiation effects are incorporated into an effective force that modulates the dynamical evolution of the collective coordinates
{x_{i}(t)}
.

Suppose we consider the field
\varphi(t,z)=\phi_{k_{i}}(z-x_{i}(t))+f_{i}(t,z)
,
where
f_{i}(t,z)
denotes the radiation field emitted by the
i
-th sub-kink and
\phi_{k_{i}}(z)
is the kink-solution in the
\alpha=0
limit. Substituting this ansatz into the field equation of motion eq. (
3
), and expanding to leading order in
f_{i}(t,z)
, we obtain

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}-m_{i}^{2}\right)f_{i}(t,z)=\ddot{x}_{i}\frac{\partial\phi_{k_{i}}}{\partial\xi},

(51)

where we neglected terms of order
{\dot{x}}^{2}_{i}
and
\alpha\frac{\partial\Delta_{n,\alpha}}{\partial\phi}
, and approximate

\frac{\partial^{2}V_{n,\alpha}[\phi_{k_{i}}(\xi)]}{\partial\phi^{2}}\approx m_{i}^{2}.

(52)

Henceforth we denote
v_{i}=\dot{x}_{i}
and
a_{i}=\ddot{x}_{i}
. The solution can then be obtained using the one-dimensional Klein-Gordon Green’s function, which yields
Campbell et al. (1983)
;
Smyth and Worthy (1999)

f_{i}(t,z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}dt^{\prime}\int dz^{\prime}\Theta(t-t^{\prime}-|z-z^{\prime}|)J_{0}\left(m_{i}\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-(z-z^{\prime})^{2}}\right)a_{i}(t^{\prime})\frac{\partial\phi_{k_{i}}}{\partial\xi^{\prime}},

(53)

where
\xi^{\prime}=z^{\prime}-x_{i}(t^{\prime})
,
\Theta
denotes the Heaviside step function, and
J_{0}
is the Bessel function of the first kind. The last term in this equation is strongly localized around the sub-kink position and can therefore be approximated as
\frac{\partial\phi_{k_{i}}(z^{\prime}-x_{i}(t^{\prime}))}{\partial\xi\prime}\approx 2\pi\delta_{D}(z^{\prime}-x_{i}(t^{\prime}))
, where
\delta_{D}
is the Dirac delta. Under this approximation, eq. (
53
) simplifies to

f_{i}(t,z)=\pi\int_{t_{1}}dt^{\prime}\,\Theta(t-t^{\prime}-|z-x_{i}(t^{\prime})|)\,J_{0}\left(m_{i}\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-(z-x_{i}(t^{\prime}))^{2}}\right)\,a_{i}(t^{\prime}).

(54)

The radiated energy per unit time is obtained from the Poynting term

S_{i}[t,z_{R}]=\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z}\right]_{z=z_{R}},

(55)

where
z_{R}
is an observation point located at the radiation region
z_{R}>x_{i}
. Differentiating the radiation field with respect to
t
and
z
, and assuming that
z\gg x_{i}
, one finds

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\pi a_{i}(t-z)-\pi\,\int_{t_{1}}^{t-z}dt^{\prime}\,\,\frac{m_{i}(t-t^{\prime})}{\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-z^{2}}}\,J_{1}\left(m_{i}\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-z^{2}}\right)\,a_{i}(t^{\prime})\,,

(56)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z}

\displaystyle=-\pi a_{i}(t-z)+\pi\,\int_{t_{1}}^{t-z}dt^{\prime}\,\,\frac{m_{i}z}{\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-z^{2}}}\,J_{1}\left(m_{i}\sqrt{(t-t^{\prime})^{2}-z^{2}}\right)\,a_{i}(t^{\prime})\,.

As previously discussed, the acceleration spectra
a_{i}(\omega)
are expected to contain a broad range of frequencies. In contrast, the field-radiation spectra
f_{i}(\omega,z_{R})
should include only propagating modes with
\omega>m_{i}
. This implies that the Bessel functions appearing in eqs. (
54
) and (
56
) effectively act as frequency filters. This interpretation is fully consistent with the numerical results presented in section
3.4
, as shown in figures
9
.

For the internal sub-kinks involved in a
K_{n}
-
\bar{K}_{n}
collision, figure
8
(
a
) shows that shortly after the collision their acceleration spectra display a broad set of characteristic frequencies. Nonetheless, two sharply contrasting patterns emerge. For the internal sub-kinks, virtually all frequency modes lie below the corresponding mass threshold
m_{i}
, whereas the external sub-kink exhibits a significant fraction of modes above the radiation threshold. Consistent with this behavior, the
f_{i}(\omega)
spectra in figures
9

shows a strong radiation suppression for the internal sub-kinks, while nearly all radiation originates from the external sub-kinks.

A complete treatment of these effects would require solving a complicated system of coupled nonlinear integro- differential equations, which lies beyond the scope of the present work. However, the previous results motivate a simple self-consistent approximation that can be implemented by proposing the relations

\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}(t)}{\partial t}\right]_{ap}\approx\pi\,\sqrt{\beta_{i}(t)}\,a_{i}(t)\,,\qquad\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}(t)}{\partial z}\right]_{ap}\approx-\pi\,\sqrt{\beta_{i}(t)}\,a_{i}(t)\,.

(57)

Here
\beta_{i}(t)
is a slow varying time-dependent function introduced to ensure that, on average, the total radiated power is incorporated correctly. The function
\beta_{i}(t)
is therefore determined by requiring that, over successive time intervals, the radiation energy losses computed from eqs. (
56
) and (
57
) remains mutually consistent

\int_{0}^{\omega_{\text{max}}}\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}(\omega)\right]\,\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z}(\omega)\right]\,d\omega\,\sim\,\int_{0}^{\omega_{\text{max}}}\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}(\omega)\right]_{ap}\,\left[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z}(\omega)\right]_{ap}\,d\omega\,.

(58)

Under this approximation, the Poynting term in eqs. (
55
) reduces to the simple expression

S_{i}[t]=-\pi^{2}\,\beta_{i}(t)\,a_{i}(t)^{2}\,.

(59)

This result allows us to obtain an explicit expression for the back-reaction force
F_{\text{rad},i}
by requiring that the work done by this force matches the radiated energy loss over the same time interval. This results in an Abraham-Lorentz type expression

F_{rad,i}=\,\ \pi^{2}\,\left(\beta_{i}\frac{da_{i}}{dt}\,+a_{i}\frac{d\beta_{i}}{dt}\,\right).

(60)

The first term corresponds to the effective damping acting on the
i
-th sub-kink, while the second term yields a correction to its effective mass.

3.4
Equations of motion

Finally, collecting the previous results we can establish the collective coordinate model that describes the
K_{n}-{\bar{K}}_{n}
collisions. From now on, we consider the
n=4
case, its extension to arbitrary
n
is straightforward. The equations of motion govern the evolution of the sub-kink components of
{\bar{K}}_{4}\sim\{{\bar{k}}_{-2},{\bar{k}}_{-1},{\bar{k}}_{1},{\bar{k}}_{2}\}
, while the corresponding components of
K_{4}
are obtained by imposing the
z\to-z
symmetry.

In this model, only interactions between a sub-kink and its nearest neighbors are taken into account. Consequently, the equations of motion take the following form

M_{k_{i}}{\ddot{x}}_{i}\,=\,{\cal F}_{i}(x_{i-1},x_{i},x_{i+1})\,,\qquad i=-2,-1,1,2,

(61)

where
{\cal F}_{i}
is constructed using eqs. (
39
,
48
) and leads to:

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{-2}(x_{-2},x_{-1})=F_{\{K_{2}-\bar{K}_{2}\}}(x_{-2})+F_{\{1,2\}}(x_{-1}-x_{-2})\,,

(62)

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{-1}(x_{-2},x_{-1},x_{1})=-F_{\{1,2\}}(x_{-1}-x_{-2})-F_{\{-1,1\}}(x_{1}-x_{-1})\,,

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{1}(x_{-1},x_{1},x_{2})=F_{\{-1,1\}}(x_{1}-x_{-1})-F_{\{1,2\}}(x_{2}-x_{1})\,,

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=F_{\{1,2\}}(x_{2}-x_{1})\,.

It is straightforward to verify that the equations of motion, eqs. (
61
,
62
), follow from the Euler-Lagrange equations applied to the Lagrangian function

L\left[x_{i},{\dot{x}}_{i}\right]=T-U\left[x_{i}\right]
, where the kinetic energy and potential terms are given as follows:

\displaystyle T=\sum_{i=-2\,,i\neq 0}^{i=2}\,\frac{1}{2}M_{k_{i}}{\dot{x}}_{i}^{2}\,,

(63)

\displaystyle U\left[x_{i}\right]=U_{K_{n},\bar{K}_{n}}(x_{-2})+

\displaystyle U_{\{1,2\}}\left(x_{-1}-x_{-2}\right)+U_{\{-1,1\}}\left(x_{1}-x_{-1}\right)+U_{\{1,2\}}\left(x_{2}-x_{1}\right)\,,

where the interaction potential
U_{\{i,i+1\}}
and
U_{K_{n},\bar{K}_{n}}
are defined in eqs. (
41
,
49
). These results allow us to verify that, to a high degree of precision, the numerical solutions of the equations of motion eq. (
61
) are consistent with the conservation of the total energy
E_{CCM}=T+U
.

Back-radiation effects on the sub-kinks can be incorporated by adding the force term
F_{{\text{rad},i}}
from eq. (
60
) to the equations of motion. However, a direct substitution of terms involving the time derivative of the acceleration leads to the well-known issue of runaway solutions. To avoid this, we adopt the perturbative method of Landau and Lifshitz, in which the first-order solution of
M_{k_{i}}{\ddot{x}}_{i}\,=\,{\cal F}_{i}

is used to evaluate the acceleration time derivative term appearing in eq. (
60
) and thereby estimate
F_{\{rad,i\}}
. Taking these considerations into account, the equations of motion take the following form:

{\tilde{M}}_{k_{i}}{\ddot{x}}_{i}\,=\,{\cal F}_{i}(x_{i-1},x_{i},x_{i+1})\,+\pi^{2}\,\,\frac{\beta_{i}(t)}{M_{k_{i}}}\sum_{j}\,(v_{j}-v_{i})\,\left[\frac{\partial{\cal F}_{j}(\xi)}{\partial\xi}\right]_{\xi=(x_{j}-x_{i})}\,,

(64)

where
{\tilde{M}}_{k_{i}}=M_{k_{i}}-\pi^{2}\frac{d\beta_{i}}{dt}.
With the inclusion of radiative effects, the total particle energy
E_{CCM}=T+U
is no longer conserved. However, one can verify that the energy loss
\Delta E_{\text{CCM}}
over a given time interval is consistent with the radiated energy obtained from the time integral of the Poynting term

E_{CCM}(t_{2})-E_{CCM}(t_{1})\approx E_{rad}(t_{1},t_{2})=\sum_{i}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\,\beta_{i}(t)S_{i}[t,z_{R}]\,dt\,.

(65)

A consistent solution of eqs. (
64
) requires knowledge of the function
\beta_{i}(t)
. To determine it, we employ a recursive approximation scheme. In the first step, we set
\beta_{i}\equiv 0
and solve the equations of motion (
61
). Once the trajectories
x_{i}(t)
and their time derivatives are obtained, all quantities entering eqs. (
54
), (
55
), and (
56
), as well as the required Fourier transforms, can be evaluated. Then, for a sequence of post-collision time intervals
t_{pc}\in\{t_{pc}-\delta t,,t_{pc}+\delta t\}
, values of
\beta_{i}(t_{pc})
are determined from eq. (
58
). Two or three iterations of the process, solving eqs. (
64
), leads to convergent results,
e.g.
for
\alpha=0.05
and
v_{i}=-0.1
:

\displaystyle\beta_{i}(t)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{{\tilde{\beta}_{i}}}{\pi}\left(\arctan\left[-m_{eff}\left(t-\left(\frac{X_{L}}{|v_{i}|}+t_{c}\right)\right)\right]+\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\,,\,\,\,\,\,m_{eff}\approx 0.0002\,,\,\,\,\,\,t_{c}\approx 6400\,,

(66)

\displaystyle{\tilde{\beta}}_{-2}

\displaystyle\approx 0.00045\,,\qquad{\tilde{\beta}}_{-1}\approx 0.003\,,\qquad{\tilde{\beta}}_{1}\approx 0.003\,,\qquad{\tilde{\beta}}_{2}\approx 0.7.

The extracted
{\tilde{\beta}}_{i}
values confirm that radiation is strongly suppressed for internal sub-kinks and originates primarily from the external one. The time dependence of
\beta_{i}(t)
captures the early energy loss during the first rebounds, followed by stabilization into a long-lived oscillon. Although obtained for a particular selection of
\alpha
and
v_{i}
, the results in eqs. (
66
) vary only weakly within the ranges
\alpha\in[0.001,0.5]
and
v_{i}\in[-0.01,-0.5]
.

5
Conclusions

The aim of this work was to construct a commensurable and non-degenerate double sine-Gordon theory that exhibits a rich phenomenology, enabling the exploration of novel multi-soliton and oscillon properties. In the degenerate limit
\alpha=0
, the model supports
n
kink solutions interpolating between consecutive absolute minima. In contrast, moving into the
\alpha>0
regime induces a partial lifting of the vacuum degeneracy, providing a natural mechanism for the emergence of static multi-kinks
K_{n}
composed of an arbitrary number
n
of internal sub-kinks. Although exact analytical field configurations could not be obtained, the smooth-modulation approximation performs remarkably well and allows for a detailed and comprehensive analysis of the multi-kink sector.

The multi-kink
K_{n}
is characterized by a topological charge
Q_{n}=n
, equal to the sum of the individual sub-kink charges, and by a mass
M_{K(n,\alpha)}
eqs. (
18
,
21
), which results from adding the sub-kink masses eq. (
19
) together with a binding-energy contribution
\Delta_{K(n,\alpha)}
eq. (
20
). In the large-
n
limit, all these quantities scale linearly with
n
. The multi-kink structure, with its well-defined internal energy lumps, becomes particularly clear in the small-
\alpha
regime, where we identified the following scaling relations:
(a) the equilibrium distance between adjacent sub-kinks obeys
\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i}\approx\frac{1}{m_{i}}\log\left(\frac{const}{\alpha}\right)
eqs. (
40
,
25
);
(b) the total size satisfies
\sigma_{K_{(n,\alpha)}}\approx\frac{2n}{\pi}\,\ln(n)\,\ln(\frac{32}{\alpha})
eq. (
30
); and (c) the binding energy scales as
\Delta V_{K_{(n,\alpha)}}\approx\frac{2n}{\pi}\,\alpha\,\,\ln(\frac{12}{\alpha})
eq. (
22
). Thereby, we can visualize the multi-kink as a large size, weakly bound n-soliton.

For the
K_{n}-{\bar{K}}_{n}
scattering, we identified three distinct dynamical regimes:
(a) Rapid annihilation, occurring after only a few bounces;
(b) Inelastic dispersion, in which the colliding configurations bounce back and separate to infinity. Unlike the familiar behavior of the
\phi^{4}
,
\phi^{6}
, and related models
Moshir (1981)
;
Campbell et al. (1983)
;
Dorey et al. (2011)
;
Dorey et al. (2021)
;
Demirkaya et al. (2017)
;
Moradi Marjaneh et al. (2017)
, no multi-bounce escape windows were observed.
(c) The formation of long-lived compound oscillons, which constitutes the most novel outcome. These composite oscillons reflect the internal structure of the parent multi-kinks and therefore possess multiple characteristic scalar masses eq. (
10
) and resonant oscillation frequencies eqs. (
42
,
50
). These features distinguishes them from most oscillons reported in the literature.

We also developed a collective coordinate model that offers a good approximation to the full field dynamics during multi-kink collisions. Its construction involves several key elements: the use of dynamical Bogomol’nyi relations to determine the inter-sub-kink forces eq. (
39
) and the
K_{n}-{\bar{K}}_{n}
interaction force eq. (
48
); and the formulation of an approximate linear radiation scheme that exploits the fact that the oscillation frequencies of the internal sub-kinks lie below the radiation threshold. As a result, radiation losses arise predominantly from the external sub-kinks, giving rise to an effective radiation-reaction force eq. (
60
). All these components are incorporated into the equations defining the collective coordinate model eq. (
64
).

The field-theoretic solutions, together with those obtained from the collective coordinate model, provide a compelling characterization of the oscillons observed in this work. They emerge as large-amplitude, long-lived configurations in which the sub-kinks synchronize with remarkable quasi-periodicity.

As discussed, incorporating linear radiation effects into the collective-coordinate framework is crucial for reproducing many of the oscillon properties observed in the full-field dynamics. However, the decay of the oscillon, as well as the observed
K_{n}-{\bar{K}}_{n}
annihilations, appears to be associated with a novel staccato-like mechanism. In this scenario, the central bion that binds the oscillon is partially confined by the outer sub-kinks, causing its dominant oscillation frequency to repeatedly cross the mass threshold. These threshold crossings generate successive bursts of radiation that progressively drain energy from the configuration and eventually trigger the abrupt collapse of the oscillon.

Overall, the results presented here outline a rich scenario that merits further exploration. They may provide insights into the emergence of coherent structures in physical systems
Scott (2003)
;
Xu et al. (2023)
, and they also suggest that extending the non-degenerate double sine-Gordon model, or similar constructions, to higher spatial dimensions, possibly including gauge fields, is a promising topic for future research.

\tau_{osc}\,\approx\frac{M^{2}_{k_{\frac{n}{2}}}}{\alpha\,\,\beta_{2}(t_{s})\,\,m^{3}_{(\frac{n}{2}-1)}}.

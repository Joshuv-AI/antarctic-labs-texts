Inverse moving point source problem for the wave equation
Hanin Al Jebawy , Abdellatif El Badia and Faouzi Triki

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the problem of identifying a single moving point source for a three-dimensional wave equation from boundary measurements. Precisely, we show that the knowledge of the field generated by the source at six different points of the boundary over a finite time interval is sufficient to determine uniquely its trajectory. We also derive a Lipschitz stability estimate for the inversion.

Introduction

Inverse source problems are of importance in several scientific areas including biomedical engineering, antenna synthesis, geology, and medical imaging
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
14
]
. In this paper we consider the inverse source problem for the wave equation. Precisely, we study the problem of determining the trajectory of a moving source in a bounded domain from a single boundary measurement. Identification of sources with time-varying locations has many significant applications such as the recovery of mobile pollution sources, or small debris in low-earth orbit, and underwater sonar systems.

We assume here that media surrounding the point source is homogeneous and isotropic, and the measurement of the wave field is provided only on a small part of the boundary of the domain. Let
\phi
to be the field generated by a single point source, that is, a solution of the following initial value problem for the three dimensional wave equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{c^{2}}\phi_{tt}-\Delta\phi=\lambda\delta({\bf x}-b(t))&\text{in }&\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)\\
&\phi({\bf x},0)=\phi_{t}({\bf x},0)=0&\text{in }&\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
T>0
is a fixed time,
c>0
is the speed of the wave,
\lambda>0
is the intensity, and
b\in C^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the position of the point source confined within a bounded domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain satisfying
\overline{D}\subset\Omega
with boundary
\Gamma
. Notice that the trajectory of the point source remains away from
\Gamma
. Define
G_{b}(T)
to be the graph of the function
b
, that is,
G_{b}(T)=\{(s,b(s));\,s\in[0,T]\}
. We also assume that source is subsonic, in other words, the speed of the
source which is the first derivative of
b
, satisfies

\|b^{\prime}\|_{C([0,T])}\leq c_{0}<c.

(1.2)

The main goal of this paper is to reconstruct the trajectory followed by the source
b
by measuring
\phi
on a part of the surface
\Gamma
.

There are few works dealing with inverse moving source problems for the wave equation, and different approaches have been used for solving it. In
[
7
,
16
]
, for example, the authors considered the problem with time-varying point sources, and they applied some algebraic direct methods for reconstructing these sources from a single boundary measurement, based on the concept of the reciprocity gap functional. Later, in
[
17
]
the author provided a generalization of this algorithm to the problem of moving sources, yet this method includes some difficulties concerning the assumptions on the trajectories followed by the sources, and no stability estimate has been provided. Another algebraic algorithm for the reconstruction of one moving source was given by
[
15
]
using the observed values of the retarded potential and all its derivatives at a single observation point. Other optimization techniques were also used in solving this problem, see for example
[
6
,
12
,
13
,
18
]
.

In our work, we are interested in identifying the trajectory
b(t)
of a single moving point source source
\delta(\cdot-b(t))
with a known intensity
\lambda>0
, from the measurement of the generated field
\phi
at six well-chosen points
{\bf x}_{i},\,i=1,\cdots,6,
located on the observation surface
\Gamma
.

Throughout the paper we denote
\cdot
, and
|\cdot|
the scalar product and the Euclidean norm respectively in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

The unique solution
\phi\in C([0,T];L^{2}_{\textrm{Loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap C^{1}([0,T]\times(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus D))
of (
1.1
) is given by
[
11
,
15
,
16
]

\phi({\bf x},t)=\frac{\lambda}{4\pi c}\frac{Y(r)}{\ |{\bf x}-b(r)|\ h({\bf x},r)},

(1.3)

where
Y
denotes the Heaviside function,
r\in C([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}\left(([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega})\setminus G_{b}(T)\right)
is the unique solution to the equation

r({\bf x},t)=t-\frac{|{\bf x}-b(r)|}{c},

(1.4)

for each fixed
({\bf x},t)
, and

h({\bf x},r)=1-\frac{b^{\prime}(r).({\bf x}-b(r))}{c|{\bf x}-b(r)|}.

We note that since
b\in C^{2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies (
1.2
), we have

h\geq 1-c^{-1}\|b^{\prime}\|_{C([0,T])}:=h_{0}>0.

(1.5)

Moreover, differentiating (
1.4
) in
t
, we deduce that

\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}({\bf x},t)=\frac{1}{h({\bf x},r)}>0,

(1.6)

and so
r
is strictly increasing in
t
.

The objective of our work is to prove that a single observation
\phi
on the surface
\Gamma
uniquely determines the source term
b
. Our strategy is to first prove that the boundary observation
\phi
uniquely determines
r
, which in return allows us to reconstruct
b
using the relation (
1.4
).
Our goal thus is to reconstruct
r
for different positions
{\bf x}
on
\Gamma
. First, we assume that

T>\underset{{\bf x}\in\Gamma,{\bf y}\in D}{\sup}\frac{|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|}{c}:=T_{0}.

(1.7)

Since the wave field is propagating with a finite speed
c
, the assumption (
1.7
) is sufficient to allow the
information on the source to arrive to the observation surface
\Gamma
.

Now, we take
{\bf x}
on
\Gamma
, fixed but arbitrary, and we define the time

t_{{\bf x}}=\sup\{t>0;\ \phi({\bf x},t)=0\}.

(1.8)

We note that from (
1.3
), and the definition of the Heaviside function, we have
\phi>0
for
r>0
. Due to
the initial conditions the set
\{t>0;\ \phi({\bf x},t)=0\}
is not empty, and considering (
1.4
) we remark that
r({\bf x},t)>0
holds for
t
large enough. Thus, for
T
satisfying (
1.7
), we guarantee that
\phi({\bf x},.)
is not zero on
(0,T)
, and thus
t_{\bf x}\in(0,T)
.

On the other hand since
r
is continuous and strictly increasing in
t
, we deduce from (
1.3
) that
r({\bf x},t_{{\bf x}})=0
. Therefore, using the relation (
1.4
), we get

|{\bf x}-b(0)|=ct_{{\bf x}}.

(1.9)

Repeating the same procedure for different
{\bf x}\in\Gamma
, one can estimate the location of
b(0)
.

Our goal now is to reconstruct
r
for different
{\bf x}
on
\Gamma
, which allows us later to reconstruct
b
using the relation (
1.4
).

Our paper is organized as follows: First, we provide in section
2
an ODE based method for the reconstruction of
r
on
\Gamma
, then we reconstruct in section
3
the trajectory followed by the source
\{b(t);t\in[0,T]\}
using the previously calculated values of
r
and the measurements of
\phi
on six well chosen observation points on
\Gamma
. The uniqueness of the reconstruction is announced in Theorem
3.1
.
Finally, stability estimates are derived in section
4
. The stability in the recovery of the trajectory is provided
in Theorem
4.3
.

2
Reconstruction of
r

Let
{\bf x}\in\Gamma
, fixed but arbitrary. Since
r
satisfies (
1.6
), we deduce from (
1.3
) that for every
t>t_{{\bf x}}
, defined in (
1.8
),
r
satisfies the following equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial r}{\partial t}({\bf x},t)+\frac{4\pi c\phi({\bf x},t)}{\lambda}r=\frac{4\pi c\phi({\bf x},t)}{\lambda}t\\
&r({\bf x},t_{{\bf x}})=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.10)

Since
\phi({\bf x},t)
is given on the boundary our goal here is to solve (
2.10
) in
r
for different
{\bf x}
on
\Gamma
.

Remark 2.1

We note that
r
is a strictly increasing function in
t
. Thus, as
t
varies between
t_{{\bf x}}
and
T
we have
0\leq r({\bf x},t)\leq r({\bf x},T)
, where
r({\bf x},T)=T-\frac{|{\bf x}-b(r({\bf x},T))|}{c}
. Therefore, in order to guarantee the reconstruction of
b(t)
for
t\in(0,T)
, we may assume that our observations continue until a later time

T_{\textrm{obs}}=T+T_{0}\geq T+\underset{{\bf x}\in\Gamma}{\sup}\frac{|{\bf x}-b(T)|}{c},

(2.11)

where
T_{0}
is defined in (
1.7
).

For
{\bf x}\in\Gamma
fixed, we consider the system (
2.10
) with
t\in(t_{\bf x},T_{\bf x})
where
T_{\bf x}=T+\frac{|{\bf x}-b(T)|}{c}
.

Proposition 2.2

The system (
2.10
) has a unique solution
r\in C^{1}([t_{\bf x},T_{\bf x}];\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Proof.

Since
\phi\in C^{1}([0,T_{\textrm{obs}}]\times(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus D))
, the result is a direct consequence of Cauchy-Lipschitz Theorem.

\blacksquare

Now, we are able to reconstruct
|{\bf x}-b(r({\bf x},t))|
for every
t\in(t_{{\bf x}},T_{\bf x})
. Since
r
is smooth and strictly increasing in
t
, thus we can reconstruct
|{\bf x}-b(r)|
for every
r\in(0,T)
. Repeating the same procedure for finite number of positions
{\bf x}_{i}
on
\Gamma
, one can estimate the location of
b(r)
. This will be explained in details in the next section.

3
Reconstruction of
b

The goal of this section is to reconstruct the trajectory followed by the source
b
using the previously calculated values of
r
on the observation surface
\Gamma
. Our algorithm requires the measurement of
\phi
on well chosen observation points
\{{\bf x_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq 6}
on
\Gamma
.

First, we define for every
{\bf x_{i}}\in\Gamma

t_{\bf x_{i}}=\frac{|{\bf x_{i}}-b(0)|}{c}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{\bf x_{i}}=T+\frac{|{\bf x_{i}}-b(T)|}{c}.

We assume in this section that the functions
r({\bf x_{i}},t)
for
t\in(t_{\bf x_{i}},T_{\bf x_{i}})
are previously constructed. Now we take
\tau\in(0,T)
, fixed but arbitrary, and
{\bf x_{1}}=(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})\in\Gamma
, then from the regularity of
r
we deduce that there exists
t_{1,\tau}\in(t_{\bf x_{1}},T_{\bf x_{1}})
such that

r({\bf x_{1}},t_{1,\tau})=\tau.

Thus, we deduce from (
1.4
) that
b(\tau)
satisfies

|{\bf x_{1}}-b(\tau)|=c(t_{1,\tau}-\tau).

Therefore,
b(\tau)
moves on a sphere of center
{\bf x_{1}}
and radius
c(t_{1,\tau}-\tau)
, which implies that
b(\tau)=(b_{1}(\tau),b_{2}(\tau),b_{3}(\tau))
satisfies the equation

(b_{1}(\tau)-x_{1})^{2}+(b_{2}(\tau)-y_{1})^{2}+(b_{3}(\tau)-z_{1})^{2}=c^{2}(t_{1,\tau}-\tau)^{2}.

(3.12)

Furthermore, taking
{\bf x_{2}}=(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})
another point on
\Gamma
, and repeating the previous procedure, we know that
b(\tau)
moves on a sphere of center
{\bf x_{2}}
and radius
c(t_{2,\tau}-\tau)
for some
t_{2,\tau}\in(t_{\bf x_{2}},T_{\bf x_{2}})
that satisfies

r({\bf x_{2}},t_{2,\tau})=\tau.

Therefore,
b(\tau)
also satisfies

(b_{1}(\tau)-x_{2})^{2}+(b_{2}(\tau)-y_{2})^{2}+(b_{3}(\tau)-z_{2})^{2}=c^{2}(t_{2,\tau}-\tau)^{2}.

(3.13)

Subtracting (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) we get

2(x_{2}-x_{1})b_{1}(\tau)+2(y_{2}-y_{1})b_{2}(\tau)+2(z_{2}-z_{1})b_{3}(\tau)\hskip-2.84526pt=\hskip-2.84526pt|{\bf x_{2}}|^{2}-|{\bf x_{1}}|^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt+c^{2}(t_{1,\tau}-\tau)^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt-c^{2}(t_{2,\tau}-\tau)^{2}.

(3.14)

Similarly, taking
{\bf x_{3}},{\bf x_{4}},{\bf x_{5}}
and
{\bf x_{6}}\in\Gamma
, we get

2(x_{4}-x_{3})b_{1}(\tau)+2(y_{4}-y_{3})b_{2}(\tau)+2(z_{4}-z_{3})b_{3}(\tau)\hskip-2.84526pt=\hskip-2.84526pt|{\bf x_{4}}|^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt-|{\bf x_{3}}|^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt+c^{2}(t_{3,\tau}-\tau)^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt-c^{2}(t_{4,\tau}-\tau)^{2},

(3.15)

and

2(x_{6}-x_{5})b_{1}(\tau)+2(y_{6}-y_{5})b_{2}(\tau)+2(z_{6}-z_{5})b_{3}(\tau)\hskip-2.84526pt=|{\bf x_{6}}|^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt-|{\bf x_{5}}|^{2}+c^{2}(t_{5,\tau}-\tau)^{2}\hskip-2.84526pt-c^{2}(t_{6,\tau}-\tau)^{2}.

(3.16)

Equations (
3.14
), (
3.15
), and (
3.16
) can be rewritten in the matrix form

XB=\frac{1}{2}A,

where

X=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}x_{2}-x_{1}&y_{2}-y_{1}&z_{2}-z_{1}\\
x_{4}-x_{3}&y_{4}-y_{3}&z_{4}-z_{3}\\
x_{6}-x_{5}&y_{6}-y_{5}&z_{6}-z_{5}\end{array}\right),

(3.17)

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b_{1}(\tau)\\
b_{2}(\tau)\\
b_{3}(\tau)\end{array}\right),

and

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{12}\\
A_{34}\\
A_{56}\end{array}\right),

(3.18)

with

A_{ij}=|{\bf x_{j}}|^{2}-|{\bf x_{i}}|^{2}+c^{2}(t_{i,\tau}-\tau)^{2}-c^{2}(t_{j,\tau}-\tau)^{2}.

(3.19)

Now, since
\Gamma
is a boundary of a connected domain one can choose the observation points
\{{\bf x_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq 6}
such that
X
becomes invertible, then
B
can be reconstructed as

B=\frac{1}{2}X^{-1}A.

(3.20)

Notice that
X
depends only on
\Gamma
. Finally, we give a brief summary of the reconstruction of
b
that passes through five mains steps.

Step 1.
Choose
\{{\bf x_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq 6}
on
\Gamma
such that the matrix
X
defined in (
3.17
) is invertible.

Step 2.
Calculate the values of
t_{\bf x_{i}}
given by

t_{\bf x_{i}}=\sup\{t>0;\ \phi({\bf x_{i}},t)=0\}.

Step 3.
Construct
r({\bf x_{i}},t)
as solutions of the equations (
2.10
).

Step 4.
For every
\tau\in(0,T)
, evaluate
t_{i,\tau}
given by

r({\bf x_{i}},t_{i,\tau})=\tau.

Step 5.
Construct the matrix
A
given by (
3.18
), and finally evaluate
b(\tau)
using the relation (
3.20
).

Then, we have the following result.

Theorem 3.1

Let
\{{\bf x_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq 6}
be fixed points on
\Gamma
chosen such that the matrix
X=({\bf x}_{2}-{\bf x}_{1},{\bf x}_{4}-{\bf x}_{3},{\bf x}_{6}-{\bf x}_{5})^{T}
is invertible. Then the knowledge of
\phi({\bf x}_{i},t),\,i=1,\cdots,6
for
t\in[0,T_{\textrm{obs}}]
where
T_{\textrm{obs}}=T+T_{0}
, determines uniquely the trajectory of the point source, that is
\{b(t);t\in[0,T]\}
.

4
Stability estimates

In this section we give a stability estimate for the reconstruction of
b
from the measurements of
\phi
on the observation points
\{{\bf x_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq 6}
. Our work is divided into three steps. In fact, we notice from the previous section that the reconstruction of
b
passes through three main stages: the reconstruction of
t_{\bf x_{i}}
,
r({\bf x_{i}},t)
and finally the trajectory
b(t)
. For this purpose, we first give the stability estimates for
t_{\bf x_{i}}
and

, then we deduce that of the source

.

Recall that

is a bounded domain of boundary
\Gamma
, satisfying
\overline{D}\subset\Omega
. We consider as in the previous section a set of observation points
\{{\bf x_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq 6}
such that the matrix
X
defined in (
3.17
) is invertible, and we suppose that we have two observations
\phi
and

corresponding respectively to two trajectories

and

Then, we establish the following stability estimates.

(Stability estimate for

Applying Gronwall’s Lemma we deduce that

\displaystyle|r({\bf x_{i},t})-\tilde{r}({\bf x_{i}},t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\phi-\widetilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\textrm{obs}})}e^{\frac{c}{\textrm{dist}(\Gamma,D)h_{0}}(t-t_{\bf x_{i}})}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\frac{cT_{\textrm{obs}}}{\textrm{dist}(\Gamma,D)h_{0}}}\|\phi-\widetilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\textrm{obs}})}.

The second case is for
t\in(\tilde{t}_{\bf x_{i}},t_{\bf x_{i}})
, then in this case we choose
t^{*}>t_{\bf x_{i}}
such that

|t^{*}-t|\leq|t_{\bf x_{i}}-\tilde{t}_{\bf x_{i}}|.

Then, we get

\displaystyle|\tilde{r}({\bf x_{i},t})-r({\bf x_{i}},t)|

\displaystyle\leq|\tilde{r}({\bf x_{i},t})-\tilde{r}({\bf x_{i},t^{*}})|+|\tilde{r}({\bf x_{i},t^{*}})-{r}({\bf x_{i},t^{*}})|+|{r}({\bf x_{i},t^{*}})-r({\bf x_{i}},t)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{r}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\textrm{obs}})}|t^{*}-t|+|\tilde{r}({\bf x_{i},t^{*}})-{r}({\bf x_{i},t^{*}})|+\|r^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\textrm{obs}})}|t^{*}-t|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{h_{0}}|t_{\bf x_{i}}-\tilde{t}_{\bf x_{i}}|+|\tilde{r}({\bf x_{i},t^{*}})-{r}({\bf x_{i},t^{*}})|.

Therefore, following (
4.22
), and the results of Theorem
4.1
, we finally deduce that

|\tilde{r}({\bf x_{i},t})-r({\bf x_{i}},t)|\leq\left(\frac{16\pi T_{\textrm{obs}}\hbox{\rm diam }(\Omega)}{\lambda h_{0}}+Ce^{\frac{cT_{\textrm{obs}}}{\textrm{dist}(\Gamma,D)h_{0}}}\right)\|\phi-\widetilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\textrm{obs}})}.

\blacksquare

Theorem 4.3

(Stability estimate for b)

Consider the two sources trajectories
b
and
\tilde{b}
with two different observations
\phi
and
\widetilde{\phi}
at the points
\{{\bf x_{i}}\}_{1\leq i\leq 6}\in\Gamma
, such that the matrix (
3.17
) is invertible, then the following stability estimate holds

\displaystyle\|b-\tilde{b}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq

\displaystyle C\hskip-2.84526pt\underset{i=1,3,5}{\sup}\hskip-5.69054pt\left(\|\widetilde{\phi}({\bf x_{i},.})-\phi({\bf x_{i},.})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\textrm{obs}})}\hskip-2.84526pt+\hskip-2.84526pt\|\widetilde{\phi}({\bf x_{i+1},.})-\phi({\bf x_{i+1},.})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\textrm{obs}})}\right),

(4.23)

for some
C=C(\Omega,\lambda,T,c,c_{0},D)>0
.

Proof.

C
denotes a generic strictly positive constant that depends on
(\Omega,\lambda,T,c,c_{0},D)
, and
which may be different from line to line.

Let
\tau\in(0,T)
, then following the work done in section
3
we deduce that

b(\tau)-\tilde{b}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}X^{-1}(A-\widetilde{A}),

(4.24)

where
X
is defined in (
3.17
), and the matrices
A
and
\widetilde{A}
are defined in (
3.18
) with observations
\phi
and
\widetilde{\phi}
respectively. Moreover, following (
3.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle A_{i\ i+1}-\widetilde{A}_{i\ i+1}=c^{2}\left[(t_{i,\tau}-\tau)^{2}-(\tilde{t}_{i,\tau}-\tau)^{2}\right]-c^{2}\left[(t_{i+1,\tau}-\tau)^{2}-(\tilde{t}_{i+1,\tau}-\tau)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=c^{2}(t_{i,\tau}-\tilde{t}_{i,\tau})(t_{i,\tau}+\tilde{t}_{i,\tau}-2\tau)-c^{2}(t_{i+1,\tau}-\tilde{t}_{i+1,\tau})(t_{i+1,\tau}+\tilde{t}_{i+1,\tau}-2\tau),

for
i=1,3,5
.
Furthermore, knowing that

r({\bf x_{j}},t_{j,\tau})=\tilde{r}({\bf x_{j}},\tilde{t}_{j,\tau})=\tau\ \forall 1\leq j\leq 6,

we get

\displaystyle{t}_{j,\tau}-\tilde{t}_{j,\tau}

\displaystyle={t}_{j,\tau}-\tilde{r}^{-1}({\bf x_{j}},\tau)

\displaystyle={t}_{j,\tau}-\tilde{r}^{-1}({\bf x_{j}},r({\bf x_{j}},{t}_{j,\tau}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{r}^{-1}({\bf x_{j}},\tilde{r}({\bf x_{j}},{t}_{j,\tau}))-\tilde{r}^{-1}({\bf x_{j}},r({\bf x_{j}},{t}_{j,\tau}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{r}^{-1}\left({\bf x_{j}},\tilde{r}({\bf x_{j}},{t}_{j,\tau})-r({\bf x_{j}},{t}_{j,\tau})\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle|{t}_{j,\tau}-\tilde{t}_{j,\tau}|

\displaystyle\leq\|(\tilde{r}^{-1})^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}|\tilde{r}({\bf x_{j}},{t}_{j,\tau})-r({\bf x_{j}},{t}_{j,\tau})|.

1.4

{t}_{j,\tau}-\tilde{t}_{j,\tau}\leq C\|\widetilde{\phi}({\bf x_{j},.})-\phi({\bf x_{j},.})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{\textrm{obs}})}.

\displaystyle A_{i\ i+1}-\widetilde{A}_{i\ i+1}\leq

5
Conclusion

In this paper we provide a reconstruction procedure of the trajectory of a point source for the wave equation
from the knowledge of the field at six well-chosen points on the observation boundary over a finite time interval.
We derived a Lipschitz stability estimate for the inversion that shows that the inverse problem is in fact well
posed. The method can be easily extended to other dimensions. In this case the reconstruction requires
measurement of the field at
2d
well chosen points where
d
is the dimension of the space. We plan in the
future works to numerically implement the method, and to recover simultaneously the intensity of the point
source.

NPASA: An algorithm for nonlinear programming - Motivation and Global Convergence Thanks: Submitted to the editors ADD DATE .
James Diffenderfer, William W. Hager

Abstract
In this paper, we present a two phase method for solving nonlinear programming problems called Nonlinear Polyhedral Active Set Algorithm (NPASA) that has global and local convergence guarantees under reasonable assumptions. The first phase consists of an augmented Lagrangian method to ensure global convergence while the second phase is designed to promote fast local convergence by performing a balanced reduction of two error estimators for nonlinear programs. After presenting error estimators for nonlinear programs and our algorithm NPASA, we establish global convergence properties for NPASA. Local quadratic convergence of NPASA is established in a companion paper [ 11 ] .

In this paper, we present a method for solving a general nonlinearly constrained optimization problem based on the recently developed
[
20
]
polyhedral active set algorithm (PASA), an efficient algorithm for optimizing a nonlinear function over a polyhedron. Let us consider the nonlinear programming problem

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}&f(\bm{x})\\
\text{s.t.}&\bm{h}(\bm{x})=\bm{0},\ \bm{r}(\bm{x})\leq\bm{0}\end{array}

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\bm{h}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
are nonlinear functions and
\bm{r}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a linear function defined by

\displaystyle\bm{r}(\bm{x}):=\bm{A}\bm{x}-\bm{b},

(3)

where
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For simplicity, we also denote the polyhedral constraint set for problem (
1
) by
\Omega=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\bm{r}(\bm{x})\leq\bm{0}\}
. Any nonlinear programming problem can be written in this form. It is assumed that
f
and
\bm{h}
are at least continuously differentiable. The algorithm for solving the nonlinearly constrained optimization problem (
1
) is based on the following steps consisting of polyhedral constrained subproblems:

1.

An augmented Lagrangian step subject to the polyhedral constraints. This step is used to ensure convergence to a stationary point even with a poor starting guess.

2.

A Newton iteration applied to the nonlinear constraints. This step amounts to a projection onto a polyhedral set.

3.

A quadratic program for estimating the multipliers.

4.

The optimization of an augmented Lagrangian over the tangent space associated with the nonlinear constraints.

In this way, the general nonlinearly constrained optimization problem is reduced to the solution of polyhedral constrained subproblems. Under suitable assumptions, the solution of subproblems 2 – 4 is locally quadratically convergent. If the starting guess is good enough, the algorithm simply loops over the final three subproblems until the convergence tolerance is satisfied. Hence, our algorithm for solving the nonlinearly constrained optimization problems uses PASA to solve a series of polyhedral constrained optimization problems and is denoted NPASA. This paper establishes a global convergence result for NPASA, while the companion paper
[
11
]
establishes a local quadratic convergence result.

4
Convergence Analysis for Phase One of NPASA

We begin our convergence analysis by performing convergence analysis for phase one of NPASA. Motivation for the global step in phase one is to ensure the robustness of the NPASA algorithm. In particular, phase two of NPASA may encounter problems when the linearization of
\bm{h}(\bm{x})
at the current iterate requires a large perturbation to be feasible resulting in an unsuccessful minimization of
\|\bm{h}(\bm{x})\|
in the Constraint Step. In such an instance, phase one serves as a safeguard to move the primal iterate to a new point before attempting phase two again. For phase one we have decided to use augmented Lagrangian techniques. First introduced by Hestenes
[
23
]
and Powell
[
31
]
and sometimes referred to as the method of multipliers, augmented Lagrangian methods and their convergence properties have since been well studied
[
3
,
26
,
28
]
and have been successful at solving nonlinear programs in practice using software packages such as ALGENCAN
[
2
]
, ALGLIB
[
5
]
, LANCELOT
[
7
,
9
]
, and MINOS
[
27
]
. Hence, the choice of an augmented Lagrangian technique is a suitable safeguard to ensure NPASA will converge to a stationary point in the case that problems arise during the constraint step in phase two.

This section is organized as follows. First, we analyze the method of multipliers scheme in Algorithm
1
, GS algorithm. In particular, we establish necessary conditions under which a subsequence of iterates of the GS algorithm converge to a stationary point for problem (
1
). During this discussion, we also develop a closed form expression for computing the multiplier error of the original nonlinear program, problem (
1
), when solving the augmented Lagrangian problem without requiring the explicit value of the inequality multiplier.
In Section
4.2
, we provide theoretical analysis for Algorithm
4
, the modified PASA algorithm, which reduces the number of necessary conditions required to establish convergence when solving the minimization problem in the GS algorithm using Algorithm
4
.

E_{m,0}
for problem (
1
) is given by
E_{m,0}(\bm{x},\bm{\lambda},\bm{\mu})=\|\nabla f(\bm{x})+\bm{\lambda}^{\intercal}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x})+\bm{\mu}^{\intercal}\bm{A}\|^{2}-\bm{\mu}^{\intercal}\bm{r}(\bm{x})
, it follows that

\displaystyle E_{m,0}(\bm{x},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}+2q_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{x}),\bm{\mu})

\displaystyle=\|\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}+\bm{\mu}^{\intercal}\bm{A}\|^{2}-\bm{\mu}^{\intercal}\bm{r}(\bm{x}).

(41)

Now note that from (
35
) we have

\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\bm{\mu}(\bm{x},\alpha)^{\intercal}\bm{A}=\frac{1}{\alpha}(\bm{x}-\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha))^{\intercal}
.
Taking the norm of both sides and squaring yields

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\bm{\mu}(\bm{x},\alpha)^{\intercal}\bm{A}\right\|^{2}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\|\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x}\|^{2}.

(42)

Additionally, right multiplying both sides of (
35

\frac{1}{\alpha}(\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x})
and using (
38
) yields

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}\bm{\mu}(\bm{x},\alpha)^{\intercal}\bm{r}(\bm{x})=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x}\|^{2}-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}(\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x}).

(43)

Combining (
41
), (
42
), and (
43
) yields (
39
).

Next, from P6 in Proposition 2.1 of
[
19
]
we have that

-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}(\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x})\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x}\|^{2}
,
for all
\bm{x}\in\Omega
. Combining this fact with (
39
) yields a lower bound for the multiplier error

\displaystyle E_{m,0}(\bm{x},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}+2q_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{x}),\bm{\mu}(\bm{x},\alpha)/\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\|\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x}\|^{2}.

(44)

In particular, by taking
\alpha=1
in (
39
) and recalling (
34

\displaystyle E_{m,0}(\bm{x},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}+2q_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{x}),\bm{\mu}(\bm{x},1))=-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{q_{k}}(\bm{x},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k})(\bm{y}(\bm{x},1)-\bm{x}).

(45)

Lastly, (
44
) and (
45
)
together with

40
).

Hence, in (

program, problem (

), in terms of the current primal iterate,
\bm{x}
, and a known KKT equality multiplier.
The inequality in (

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
\Omega

1.

\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

\{\bm{\lambda}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is updated using the formula for

in line 4 of Algorithm
1
(GS algorithm).

3.

\{q_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of real numbers such that

as

.

Then any subsequence of
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

) holds at

satisfies

, where
\bm{\lambda}^{*}
is the limit of the corresponding subsequence for
\{\bm{\lambda}_{k}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\bm{x}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be a subsequence with limit point
\bm{x}^{*}
and let
\{\bm{\lambda}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and
\{\bm{\mu}(\bm{x}_{k},1)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}

\mathcal{A}\>=\mathcal{A}(\bm{x}^{*})
. First, we show that
\{\bm{\lambda}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and
\{\bm{\mu}(\bm{x}_{k},1)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
are uniformly bounded for
k
sufficiently large. Rearranging the terms in equation (
35
) with
\alpha=1
combined with the fact that
\mathcal{A}(\bm{x}_{k})\subseteq\mathcal{A}(\bm{x}^{*})
for sufficiently large

, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{k})^{\intercal}\ \Big|\ \bm{A}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\intercal}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\lambda}_{k}\\
\bm{\mu}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x}_{k},1)\end{bmatrix}=\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{y}(\bm{x}_{k},1)-\nabla f(\bm{x}_{k})^{\intercal}

(46)

By our hypothesis that (
LICQ
) holds at
\bm{x}^{*}
, there exists a

such that the matrix
\begin{bmatrix}\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{k})^{\intercal}\ |\ \bm{A}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\intercal}\end{bmatrix}
is of full column rank, for all

. Hence, there exists a constant

such that

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\lambda}_{k}\\
\bm{\mu}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x}_{k},1)\end{bmatrix}\right\|\leq M\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{y}(\bm{x}_{k},1)-\nabla f(\bm{x}_{k})^{\intercal}\|,

(47)

for
k
sufficiently large. From property P6 in Proposition 2.1 of
[
19
]
we have

\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{y}(\bm{x}_{k},1)\|^{2}\leq\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{q}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k-1})^{\intercal}(\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{y}(\bm{x}_{k},1))
.
Hence, using the hypothesis that
E_{m,0}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{\lambda}_{k},\bm{\mu}(\bm{x}_{k},1))\leq\theta E_{c}(\bm{x}_{k})
together with (
40
) it follows that

\|\bm{x}_{k}-\bm{y}(\bm{x}_{k},1)\|^{2}\leq E_{m,0}(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{\lambda}_{k},\bm{\mu}(\bm{x}_{k},1))\leq E_{c}(\bm{x}_{k})=\|\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{k})\|^{2}
.
Combining this with the inequality in (
47
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\lambda}_{k}\\
\bm{\mu}_{\mathcal{A}}(\bm{x}_{k},1)\end{bmatrix}\right\|\leq M\left(\|\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{k})\|+\|\nabla f(\bm{x}_{k})\|\right),

(48)

for
k
sufficiently large. As
f,\bm{h}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}
and
\mu_{j}(\bm{x}_{k},1)=0
for
j\not\in\mathcal{A}
, it follows from (

) that the sequences
\{\bm{\lambda}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
and
\{\bm{\mu}(\bm{x}_{k},1)\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
are uniformly bounded for

sufficiently large. As the sequence
\{\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded by construction, there exists a constant
\Lambda
such that
\|\bm{\lambda}_{k}-\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k-1}\|\leq\Lambda
for all

sufficiently large. Now using the update formula

, we have that

\displaystyle\|\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{k})\|=\frac{\|\bm{\lambda}_{k}-\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k-1}\|}{2q_{k}}\leq\frac{\Lambda}{2q_{k}},

(49)

for all

sufficiently large. So (

as

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}E_{c}(\bm{x}_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\bm{h}(\bm{x}_{k})\|^{2}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{4q_{k}^{2}}=0.

(50)

As

and

by hypothesis, it now follows that

. Lastly, from Lemma

it follows that

which concludes the proof.

4.2
Global Convergence of GS Algorithm using Modified PASA

We now focus on establishing a global convergence result for the augmented Lagrangian problem when using Algorithm

, PASA
[

]
with modified stopping criterion.
Algorithm

is obtained by replacing all instances of
E_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1287259}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle PASA$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1287259}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle PASA$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1132362}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle PASA$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1132360}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle PASA$}}}}}}(\bm{x})

Lemma 7
.

For a given
\bm{x}

satisfy the KKT conditions in (

\displaystyle-\nabla\mathcal{L}_{q_{k}}(\bm{x},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k})(\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x})\leq-\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}_{q_{k}}(\bm{x},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k})(\bm{y}(\bm{x},\beta)-\bm{x}),\ \text{for}\ 0<\alpha\leq\beta,

(51)

\displaystyle E_{m,0}(\bm{x},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}+2q_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{x}),\bm{\mu}(\bm{x},\beta)/\beta)\leq E_{m,0}(\bm{x},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}+2q_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{x}),\bm{\mu}(\bm{x},\alpha)/\alpha),\ \text{for}\ 0<\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1.

(52)

Proof.

Suppose
0<\alpha\leq\beta
. Let
\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})=\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-\bm{x}
. By P1 in
[
19
]
with
\bm{x}\leftarrow\bm{x}-\alpha\bm{g}(\bm{x})
and
\bm{y}\leftarrow\bm{y}(\bm{x},\beta)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq(\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha)-(\bm{x}-\alpha\bm{g}(\bm{x})))^{\intercal}(\bm{y}(\bm{x},\beta)-\bm{y}(\bm{x},\alpha))

(53)

\displaystyle=(\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})+\alpha\bm{g}(\bm{x}))^{\intercal}(\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})-\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x}))

(54)

\displaystyle=\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})-\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}+\alpha\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})-\alpha\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x}).

Rearranging terms and dividing both sides by

\displaystyle-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\leq-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})+\frac{1}{\alpha}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}.

(56)

It now follows from Cauchy-Schwarz that

\displaystyle-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\leq-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}.

(57)

By swapping
\alpha
and
\beta
in the construction of (
57

\displaystyle-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\leq-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})+\frac{1}{\beta}\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|-\frac{1}{\beta}\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|^{2}.

(58)

As
\alpha\leq\beta
, by P4 in
[
19

\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|\leq\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|
. Substituting this into (

\displaystyle-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\leq-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})+\frac{1}{\beta}\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\beta}\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|^{2}=-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x}).

(59)

which is equivalent to (

).

Now suppose that
\alpha\leq\beta\leq 1
. By P5 in
[
19
]
, we have that
\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|\leq\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|
. Substituting this into (
57

\displaystyle-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\leq-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})+\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}

(60)

\displaystyle=-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}-\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}.

Again, by P5 in
[
19

-\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|
and since

it follows that

\displaystyle-\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}\leq-\frac{1-\beta}{\beta^{2}}\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|^{2}=-\left(\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|^{2}.

(62)

Combining (

\displaystyle-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})+\left(\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)\|\bm{d}^{\beta}(\bm{x})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\bm{g}(\bm{x})^{\intercal}\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\|\bm{d}^{\alpha}(\bm{x})\|^{2}

(63)

which by (

at which

. Before providing such a result,

Theorem 9

is used to solve problem (
4.1
) then Algorithm
4
either terminates in a finite number of iterations at a stationary point, or generates a sequence of iterates
\{\bm{u}_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
satisfying

\displaystyle\liminf_{i\to\infty}E_{m,0}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}+2q_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{u}_{i}),\bm{\mu}(\bm{u}_{i},1))=0.

(78)

Proof.

The proof is split into three cases.

Case 1: Phase two of Algorithm
4

In this case, only phase one of Algorithm
4
is executed for

sufficiently large. As such, from Theorem

Case 2: Phase one of Algorithm
4
is executed a finite number of times.
Here, only phase two is executed for

sufficiently large. Since phase two eventually does not branch back to phase one, by the branching criterion in phase two we have that

\displaystyle\theta E_{m,0}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}+2q_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{u}_{i}),\bm{\mu}(\bm{u}_{i},1))\leq e_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1287259}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle PASA$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1287259}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle PASA$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1132362}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle PASA$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1132360}{262144}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle PASA$}}}}}}(\bm{u}_{i})

(79)

for

Case 3: Phases one and two of Algorithm

By way of contradiction, assume that (

\tau>0

\displaystyle\tau\leq E_{m,0}(\bm{u}_{i},\bm{\bar{\lambda}}_{k}+2q_{k}\bm{h}(\bm{u}_{i}),\bm{\mu}(\bm{u}_{i},1))=-\bm{g}(\bm{u}_{i})^{\intercal}(\bm{y}(\bm{u}_{i},1)-\bm{u}_{i}).

(81)

for each iteration of GPA when

5
Convergence Analysis for Phase Two of NPASA

In this section, we provide conditions under which the constraint and multiplier steps in the LS algorithm satisfy desirable convergence properties. We first provide a result on the perturbed Newton step scheme used in the constraint step in Section
5.1
. Then we establish a convergence result for the constraint step in Section
5.2
followed by a result for the multiplier step of NPASA in Section
5.3
.

5.1
Algorithm with Infeasibility Detection for Solving Constraint Step

One method for minimizing the violation of the equality constraints in problem (
1
) is to generate a sequence of iterates
\{\bm{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
defined by
\bm{w}_{i+1}=\bm{w}_{i}+\bm{d}_{i}
, where

\bm{d}_{i}

is the solutioin to the minimization problem given by
\min\left\{\|\bm{w}-\bm{d}_{i-1}\|^{2}:\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{d}_{i-1})(\bm{w}-\bm{d}_{i-1})=-\bm{h}(\bm{d}_{i-1}),\bm{w}\in\Omega\right\}
.
Given
\bar{\bm{x}}
, PPROJ
[
21
]
uses a dual method for solving the projection problem

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\min_{\bm{x}}&\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{\bm{x}}-\bm{x}\|^{2}\\
\text{s.t.}&\bm{l}\leq\bm{A}\bm{x}\leq\bm{u},\ \ \bm{x}\geq\bm{0}.\end{array}

then reconstructs the primal solution. We would like to use this algorithm to solve for the Newton direction,
\bm{d}_{i}
. However, since
\{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\bm{h}(\bm{w})=\bm{0},\bm{w}\in\Omega\}\neq\emptyset
does not guarantee

\displaystyle\left\{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{d}_{i})(\bm{w}-\bm{d}_{i})=-\bm{h}(\bm{d}_{i}),\bm{w}\in\Omega\right\}\neq\emptyset,

(84)

it may be necessary to perturb the constraint
\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{d}_{i})(\bm{w}-\bm{d}_{i})=-\bm{h}(\bm{d}_{i})
in order to solve for
\bm{d}_{i+1}
.

In order to simplify the notation in our discussion of this problem, we express the linearized constraint set as

\left\{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\bm{M}(\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}})=\bm{c},\bm{w}\geq\bm{0}\right\}

supposing that we are given
\bm{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
\bar{\bm{w}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Ideally, we would like to determine the solution to the problem

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{w}\in\mathcal{S}}\ \frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2},

(85)

where
\mathcal{S}=\arg\min_{\bm{w}\geq\bm{0}}\left\{\|\bm{c}-\bm{M}(\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}})\|^{2}\right\}
. Since problem (
85
) involves minimizing a strongly convex objective function over a convex set, there exists a unique solution which we will denote by
\bm{w}_{\infty}
. As such, every search direction from
\bm{w}_{\infty}
leading to a point in
\mathcal{S}
must be an ascent direction. Hence, the first order optimality conditions for
\bm{w}_{\infty}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle\bm{w}_{\infty}-\bar{\bm{w}},\bm{w}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\rangle\geq 0,\ \text{for all}\ \bm{w}\in\mathcal{S}.

(86)

Since determining elements of the constraint set for problem (
85
) requires knowing all minimizers of
\|\bm{c}-\bm{M}(\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}})\|
we consider an alternative problem.

To simplify this problem, we define the sets

\mathcal{F}=\left\{[\bm{w},\bm{y}]^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}:\bm{M}(\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}})+\bm{y}=\bm{c},\bm{w}\geq\bm{0}\right\}
.
Now, given a penalty parameter
p>0
, the problem is defined by

\displaystyle\min_{[\bm{w},\bm{y}]^{\intercal}\in\mathcal{F}}\ \ f_{p}(\bm{w},\bm{y}):=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{2}\|\bm{y}\|^{2}.

(87)

Since problem (
87
) involves minimizing a strongly convex objective function over a convex set, it has a unique solution which we will denote by
[\bm{w}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}]^{\intercal}
. Note that the first order optimality conditions for
[\bm{w}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}]^{\intercal}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\langle\bm{w}_{p}-\bar{\bm{w}},\bm{w}-\bm{w}_{p}\rangle+p\langle\bm{y}_{p},\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{p}\rangle\geq 0,\ \text{for all}\ [\bm{w},\bm{y}]^{\intercal}\in\mathcal{F}.

(88)

While the number of primal variables in (
87
) is greater than that of problem (
85
), the dimension of the dual variables is the same for problems (
85
) and (
87
). Hence, by using the dual method PPROJ
[
21
]
we can solve problem (
87
) without increasing the dimension of the optimization problem.
The following result provides valuable insight on the relationship between
\bm{w}_{p}
and
\bm{w}_{\infty}
. Note that we make use of the set

\mathcal{F}_{\delta}=\left\{[\bm{w},\bm{y}]^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+m}:\bm{M}(\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}})+\bm{y}=\bm{c},\bm{w}\geq\bm{0},\|\bm{y}\|^{2}\leq\delta\right\}
.
in the proof of the following result.

Theorem 10
.

Let
[\bm{w}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}]^{\intercal}
be the unique solution to problem
(
87
)
. Then

\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{x}_{\infty}\|=O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)
,
for all
p>0
sufficiently large.

Proof.

Letting
\gamma=\min_{\bm{w}\geq\bm{0}}\|\bm{c}-\bm{M}(\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}})\|^{2}
it follows that
\mathcal{S}=\{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:[\bm{w},\bm{y}]^{\intercal}\in\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}\}
. So
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
and by defining
\bm{y}_{\infty}=\bm{c}-\bm{M}(\bm{w}_{\infty}-\bar{\bm{w}})
we have that
[\bm{w}_{\infty},\bm{y}_{\infty}]^{\intercal}\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
[\bm{w}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}]^{\intercal}
minimizes the objective in problem (
87
), we now have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{2}\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}_{\infty}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{2}\|\bm{y}_{\infty}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}_{\infty}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{2}\gamma.

(89)

Using (
89
) we observe that

\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}\leq\|\bm{w}_{\infty}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+p\left(\gamma-\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}\right)\leq\|\bm{w}_{\infty}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}
,
where the final inequality holds since
\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}\geq\gamma
, for all
p\geq 1
. Hence,

\displaystyle\|\bm{w}_{p}\|\leq\|\bm{w}_{\infty}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|+\|\bar{\bm{w}}\|.

(90)

Also, from (
89
) it follows that

\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{p}\|\bm{w}_{\infty}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+\gamma
which yields that

\lim_{p\to\infty}\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}=\gamma
. Since
\|\bm{w}_{p}\|
is bounded for all values of
p
in (
90
), there exist convergent subsequences of
\{\bm{w}_{p}\}_{p=1}^{\infty}
. Letting
\{\bm{w}_{p_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a convergent subsequence of
\{\bm{w}_{p}\}_{p=1}^{\infty}
with limit
\tilde{\bm{w}}
we observe that

\displaystyle\|\bm{c}-\bm{M}(\tilde{\bm{w}}-\bar{\bm{w}})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\bm{c}-\bm{M}(\bm{w}_{p_{k}}-\bar{\bm{w}})\|^{2}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\bm{y}_{p_{k}}\|^{2}=\gamma.

(91)

So, taking
\tilde{\bm{y}}=\bm{c}-\bm{M}(\tilde{\bm{w}}-\bar{\bm{w}})
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}[\bm{w}_{p_{k}},\bm{y}_{p_{k}}]^{\intercal}=[\tilde{\bm{w}},\tilde{\bm{y}}]^{\intercal}\in\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
.
Hence,
\tilde{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{S}
.

We now claim that
\tilde{\bm{w}}=\bm{w}_{\infty}
. Observing the optimality conditions in (
86
) it follows that we need to show that
\langle\tilde{\bm{w}}-\bar{\bm{w}},\bm{w}-\tilde{\bm{w}}\rangle\geq 0
, for all
\bm{w}\in\mathcal{S}
. Let
p\geq 1
be given. By the optimality conditions in (
88
), we have that

\displaystyle\langle\bm{w}_{p}-\bar{\bm{w}},\bm{w}-\bm{w}_{p}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq p\langle\bm{y}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}-\bm{y}\rangle,

(92)

for all
[\bm{w},\bm{y}]^{\intercal}\in\mathcal{F}
. As
\mathcal{S}=\{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:[\bm{w},\bm{y}]^{\intercal}\in\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, it suffices to show that
\langle\bm{y}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}-\bm{y}\rangle\geq 0
, for all
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\|\bm{y}\|^{2}=\gamma
. Accordingly, let
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\|\bm{y}\|^{2}=\gamma
be given and observe that

\displaystyle\langle\bm{y}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}-\bm{y}\rangle

\displaystyle=\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}-\|\bm{y}_{p}-\bm{y}\|^{2}+\|\bm{y}\|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}+\|\bm{y}_{p}-\bm{y}\|^{2}-\gamma\right).

(93)

By our choice of
\gamma
we have that
\|\bm{y}_{p}\|^{2}\geq\gamma
. This fact together with the inequality in (
93
) implies that

\langle\bm{y}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}-\bm{y}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{y}_{p}-\bm{y}\|^{2}\geq 0
. Substituting
this into (
92
) yields that
\langle\bm{w}_{p}-\bar{\bm{w}},\bm{w}-\bm{w}_{p}\rangle\geq 0
, for all
\bm{w}\in\mathcal{S}
. Thus,

\langle\tilde{\bm{w}}-\bar{\bm{w}},\bm{w}-\tilde{\bm{w}}\rangle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle\bm{w}_{p_{k}}-\bar{\bm{w}},\bm{w}-\bm{w}_{p_{k}}\rangle\geq 0
,
for all
\bm{w}\in\mathcal{S}
. So we conclude that
\tilde{\bm{w}}=\bm{w}_{\infty}
.

Next we claim that the entire sequence
\{\bm{w}_{p}\}_{p=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\bm{w}_{\infty}
. By way of contradiction, suppose that there exists a subsequence
\{\bm{w}_{p_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and a
\delta>0
such that
\|\bm{w}_{p_{k}}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\|\geq\delta
for all
k\geq 1
. Since
\{\bm{w}_{p_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded it has a convergent subsequence, say
\{\bm{w}_{q_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. As
\{\bm{w}_{q_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a subsequence of
\{\bm{w}_{p}\}_{p=1}^{\infty}
and every convergent subsequence of
\{\bm{w}_{p}\}_{p=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\bm{w}_{\infty}
, there exists a
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|\bm{w}_{q_{k}}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\|<\delta
for all
k\geq M
, a contradiction. Thus, every subsequence of
\{\bm{w}_{p}\}_{p=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\bm{w}_{\infty}
implying that
\bm{w}_{p}\to\bm{w}_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty
.

Lastly, it remains to show that
\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\|=O(1/p)
. Accordingly, let
p\geq 1
be given, let
\mathcal{B}:=\{i:\left(\bm{w}_{p}\right)_{i}=0\text{ and }1\leq i\leq n\}
denote the active set at point
\bm{w}_{p}
, and let
\mathcal{I}:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\setminus\mathcal{B}
. We now consider the problem

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{w}_{\mathcal{B}}=0}\ f(\bm{w}):=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}-\bar{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{2}\|\bm{c}+\bm{M}\bar{\bm{w}}-\bm{M}\bm{w}\|^{2}

(94)

where
\bm{w}_{\mathcal{B}}
is the vector with components
w_{i}
, for all
i\in\mathcal{B}
. Letting
\bm{u}^{*}
denote the solution to problem (
94
), we claim that
\bm{u}^{*}=\bm{w}_{p}
. In order to justify this claim, first note that since
\bm{w}_{p}
satisfies the constraint for problem (
94
) we have that

\displaystyle f(\bm{u}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq f(\bm{w}_{p})=f_{p}(\bm{w}_{p},\bm{y}_{p}).

(95)

Now suppose that
\bm{u}^{*}\neq\bm{w}_{p}
. By (
95
) and the strict convexity of
f
we have that
f(\bm{w})<f(\bm{w}_{p})
, for all
\bm{w}\in[\bm{w}_{p},\bm{u}^{*}]
, that is for all
\bm{w}
on the line segment connecting
\bm{w}_{p}
and
\bm{u}^{*}
. By our choice of
\mathcal{B}
,
(\bm{w}_{p})_{i}>0
for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
and
(\bm{w}_{p})_{i}=0=\bm{u}^{*}_{i}
for all
i\in\mathcal{B}
. Hence,
\bm{w}\geq 0
for all
\bm{w}\in[\bm{w}_{p},\bm{u}^{*}]
sufficiently close to
\bm{w}_{p}
, a contradiction.
Thus,
\bm{u}^{*}=\bm{w}_{p}
.

Now observe that we can reformulate problem (
94
) as the unconstrained optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{w}_{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{I}|}}\ \ \ \ g(\bm{w}_{+}):=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{w}_{+}-\bar{\bm{w}}_{+}\|^{2}+\frac{p}{2}\|\bm{c}+\bm{M}\bar{\bm{w}}-\bm{M}_{+}\bm{w}_{+}\|^{2}

(96)

where
\bar{\bm{w}}_{+}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{I}|}
is the vector with components
\bar{w}_{i}
, for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
, and
\bm{M}_{+}
is the matrix with columns
M_{i}
of
\bm{M}
, for all
i\in\mathcal{I}
. Since problem (
96
) is an unconstrained optimization problem with a strongly convex objective function, the first order optimality conditions are given by

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{+}\ \text{minimizes}\ g(\bm{w})\ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ \nabla g(\bm{w}_{+})=\frac{1}{p}\left(\bm{w}_{+}-\bar{\bm{w}}_{+}\right)-\bm{M}_{+}^{\intercal}\left(\bm{c}+\bm{M}\bar{\bm{w}}-\bm{M}_{+}\bm{w}_{+}\right)=0.

(97)

Letting
\bm{Q}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{P}^{\intercal}
be a singular value decomposition of
\bm{M}_{+}
and performing the change of variables
\bm{w}_{+}=\bm{P}\bm{z}
we obtain

\nabla g(\bm{w}_{+})=\bm{P}\left(\frac{1}{p}\bm{z}+\bm{\Sigma}^{\intercal}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{z}\right)-\bm{P}\bm{\Sigma}^{\intercal}\bm{Q}^{\intercal}\left(\bm{c}+\bm{M}\bar{\bm{w}}\right)-\frac{1}{p}\bar{\bm{w}}_{+}
.
Hence, multiplying both sides of equation (
97
) by
\bm{P}^{\intercal}
and simplifying yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}\bm{I}+\bm{\Sigma}^{\intercal}\bm{\Sigma}\right)\bm{z}=\bm{\Sigma}^{\intercal}\bm{Q}^{\intercal}\left(\bm{c}+\bm{M}\bar{\bm{w}}\right)+\frac{1}{p}\bm{P}^{\intercal}\bar{\bm{w}}_{+}.

(98)

Now let
r=rank(\bm{M}_{+})
and let
\sigma_{1}\geq\sigma_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\sigma_{r}
denote the non-zero singular values of
\bm{M}_{+}
. From equation (
98
), for
1\leq i\leq r
, we have

\left(\frac{1}{p}+\sigma_{i}^{2}\right)z_{i}=\sigma_{i}Q_{i}^{\intercal}(\bm{c}+\bm{M}\bar{\bm{w}})+\frac{1}{p}\left(\bm{P}^{\intercal}\bar{\bm{w}}_{+}\right)_{i}
,
or equivalently,

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{i}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}+1/p}Q_{i}^{\intercal}(\bm{c}+\bm{M}\bar{\bm{w}})+\frac{1/p}{\sigma_{i}^{2}+1/p}\left(\bm{P}^{\intercal}\bar{\bm{w}}_{+}\right)_{i}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}Q_{i}^{\intercal}(\bm{c}+\bm{M}\bar{\bm{w}})+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right).

(99)

For
i>r
it also follows from equation (
98
) that

z_{i}=\left(\bm{P}^{\intercal}\bar{\bm{w}}_{+}\right)_{i}
. Combining these observations with (
99
) yields that there exists a vector
\bm{u}^{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\bm{z}=\bm{u}^{\mathcal{B}}+O\left(1/p\right)
. As
\bm{w}_{+}=\bm{P}\bm{z}
, we have that
\bm{w}_{+}=\bm{P}\bm{w}^{\mathcal{B}}+O\left(1/p\right)
. Since
\bm{w}_{+}
is comprised of the positive components of
\bm{w}_{p}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{p}=\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}+O\left(1/p\right)

(100)

where
\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the vector satisfying
\left(\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=0
and
\left(\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\mathcal{I}}=\bm{P}\bm{u}^{\mathcal{B}}
.
From equation (
100
) there exists a constant
c_{\mathcal{B}}\geq 0
such that

\left\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}\right\|\leq\frac{c_{\mathcal{B}}}{p}
.
Moreover, since the number of subsets of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
is finite, taking

c:=\max_{\mathcal{D}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}c_{\mathcal{D}}

yields

\left\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}\right\|\leq\frac{c}{p}
.
As
p\geq 1
was taken to be arbitrary, we conclude that this inequality
holds for all
p\geq 1
.

Finally, we claim that
\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}=\bm{w}_{\infty}
, for
p
sufficiently large. By way of contradiction, suppose that there exists a
\delta>0
such that
\left\|\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\right\|\geq\delta
, for all
p
sufficiently large. Since
\bm{w}_{p}\to\bm{w}_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty
and
\left\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}\right\|\leq\frac{c}{p}
there exists a
N>0
such that
\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\|<\frac{1}{2}\delta
and
\left\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}\right\|<\frac{1}{2}\delta
, for all
p\geq N
. Hence, for all
p\geq N
, it follows that

\left\|\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\right\|\leq\left\|\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}-\bm{w}_{p}\right\|+\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\|<\delta
,
a contradiction. Since for subsets
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
there are only finitely many such vectors
\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}
, we have that
\bm{v}^{\mathcal{B}}=\bm{w}_{\infty}
for all
p
sufficiently large. Therefore,

\|\bm{w}_{p}-\bm{w}_{\infty}\|\leq\frac{c}{p}

for sufficiently large
p
.

5.2
Convergence Analysis for Constraint Step

In this section, we provide a convergence result for the constraint step in the LS algorithm. In particular, we establish a linear convergence rate for the constraint step scheme. Recall the iterative scheme for determining
\bm{w}\in\Omega
such that
\bm{h}(\bm{w})=\bm{0}
used in the constraint step of the LS algorithm:

\displaystyle(\bm{w}_{i+1},\bm{y}_{i+1})

\displaystyle=\arg\min\left\{\|\bm{w}-\bm{w}_{i}\|^{2}+\|\bm{y}\|^{2}:\nabla\bm{h}(\bm{w}_{i})(\bm{w}-\bm{w}_{i})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{i}}}\bm{y}=-\bm{h}(\bm{w}_{i}),\bm{w}\in\Omega\right\}

(101)

\displaystyle\bm{w}_{i+1}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\bm{w}_{i}+s_{i}(\bm{w}_{i+1}-\bm{w}_{i})

(102)

where
p_{i}\geq 1
is a penalty parameter and where
s_{i}\leq 1
is chosen such that

\displaystyle\|\bm{h}(\bm{w}_{i}+s_{i}(\bm{w}_{i+1}-\bm{w}_{i}))\|\leq(1-\tau\alpha_{i}s_{i})\|\bm{h}(\bm{w}_{i})\|.

(103)

Here,
\alpha_{i}
is defined by
\alpha_{i}:=1-\|\bm{y}_{i}\|
and to continue performing the scheme we require that
\alpha_{i}\in[\alpha,1]
for some fixed parameter
\alpha>0
. Note that this is the iterative scheme in the constraint step of the LS algorithm, Algorithm
2
, where we have made the change of variables
\bm{y}\leftarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{i}}}\bm{y}
to simplify the analysis in Lemma
11
. For problems in (
101
) where the constraint set is nonempty for
\bm{y}=\bm{0}
, sufficiently large choices of
p
will result in
\|\bm{y}\|<1-\alpha
, or equivalently
\alpha_{i}\geq\alpha
. For problems in (
101
) where the constraint set is empty for
\bm{y}=\bm{0}
we may not be able to satisfy the requirement
\alpha_{i}>0
, let alone
\alpha_{i}\geq\alpha

6

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a method for solving nonlinear programs, NPASA, and established global convergence properties for NPASA. In particular, under a small set of assumptions we established global convergence properties for each of the three subproblems solved during NPASA which, when combined, were able to ensure global convergence of NPASA. As noted in the introduction, a companion paper
[
11
]
focuses on establishing local quadratic convergence for NPASA. In the future, we plan on implementing this approach and performing benchmarking tests to compare the NPASA algorithm to leading methods for solving nonlinear programs.

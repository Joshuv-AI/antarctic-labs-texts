Correlations in typicality and an affirmative solution to the exact catalytic entropy conjecture
Henrik Wilming

Abstract
It is well known that if a (finite-dimensional) density matrix ρ \rho has smaller entropy than ρ ′ \rho^{\prime} , then the tensor product of sufficiently many copies of ρ \rho majorizes a quantum state arbitrarily close to the tensor product of correspondingly many copies of ρ ′ \rho^{\prime} . In this short note I show that if additionally rank ⁡ ( ρ ) ≤ rank ⁡ ( ρ ′ ) \mathrm{rank}(\rho)\leq\mathrm{rank}(\rho^{\prime}) , then n n copies of ρ \rho also majorize a state where all single-body marginals are exactly identical to ρ ′ \rho^{\prime} but arbitrary correlations are allowed (for some sufficiently large n n ). An immediate application of this is an affirmative solution of the exact catalytic entropy conjecture introduced by Boes et al. [PRL 122, 210402 (2019)]: If H ⁡ ( ρ ) < H ⁡ ( ρ ′ ) H(\rho)<H(\rho^{\prime}) and rank ⁡ ( ρ ) ≤ rank ⁡ ( ρ ′ ) \mathrm{rank}(\rho)\leq\mathrm{rank}(\rho^{\prime}) there exists a finite dimensional density matrix σ \sigma and a unitary U U such that U ​ ρ ⊗ σ ​ U † U\rho\otimes\sigma U^{\dagger} has marginals ρ ′ \rho^{\prime} and σ \sigma exactly . All the results transfer to the classical setting of probability distributions over finite alphabets with unitaries replaced by permutations.

Our aim here is to prove Lemma

Theorem 2

Let

In fact, contrary to the usual situation where results in quantum theory imply corresponding classical results, Theorem

2
Resource theories

In Refs.
[
8
,
5
,
9
,
10
]
catalysts that become correlated to the system of interest have been used to provide single-shot interpretations of the non-equilibrium free energy in quantum thermodynamics and the entanglement entropy in the framework of local operations and classical communication.
These results were approximate, in the sense that the output state of a protocol only approximated the desired state up to arbitrary accuracy as the catalyst dimension diverged.
Our result may suggest that a
strict
decrease in the free energy or entanglement entropy could imply the possibility of an exact transformation with a finite-dimensional catalyst also in those settings.
However, Ref.
[
11
]
showed already that this is not the case, at least if one demands that the final correlations between catalyst and system can be made arbitrarily small.
In the setting of this note, we cannot make the latter assumption since the mutual information between catalyst and final state of the system is always given by
H(\rho^{\prime})-H(\rho)
.
It would be interesting to know whether in the context of quantum thermodynamics a strict decrease in free energy allows for an exact conversion with finite-dimensional catalyst if one does not put a condition on the final correlations.
Indeed, Ref.
[
12
]
showed that this is true in the quasi-classical case with the additional assumption that the thermal state only has rational entries in its eigenbasis.

3
Open problems

Lemma
5
does not claim that the state
\rho^{\prime}_{n}
is close to
{\rho^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}
.
It is an open problem whether one can both arrange that the marginals of
\rho^{\prime}_{n}
are exactly equal to
\rho^{\prime}

and

\rho^{\prime}_{n}
is arbitrarily close to
{\rho^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}
in trace-distance while keeping the majorization condition
\rho^{\otimes n}\succeq\rho^{\prime}_{n}
. If true, this statement could likely have many further applications in physics and information theory.

A second open problem concerns the case
H(\rho)=H(\rho^{\prime})
. It is known from lower bounds on the catalyst dimension that in this case a finite-dimensional catalyst in general cannot be used to implement the state-transition exactly
[
13
,
14
]
. On the other hand, continuity of von Neumann entropy implies that it can be implemented arbitrarily well as the catalyst dimension diverges. However, it is presently unknown whether a catalyst with properly infinite dimensional density matrix could be used to implement the state-transition
\rho\rightarrow\rho^{\prime}
exactly in this case.

4
The proof

We formulate the proof in the quantum language, but, as mentioned above, everything transfers to the classical case by replacing density matrices with probability vectors.

Definition 3
.

For every finite-dimensional density matrix
\rho
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the following set:

\displaystyle S_{n}(\rho):=\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}\sigma_{\{i\}}\mid\rho^{\otimes n}\succeq\sigma,\sigma\geq 0,\mathrm{Tr}[\sigma]=1\},

(4)

where
\sigma_{\{i\}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus i}[\sigma]
.

Since randomly permuting subsystems is a random unitary process (in the sense of majorization), whenever
\rho^{\prime}\in S_{n}(\rho)
there exists a state
\rho^{\prime}_{n}
with
\rho^{\otimes n}\succeq\rho^{\prime}_{n}
as in Lemma
1
.
So we want to show that whenever
H(\rho^{\prime})>H(\rho)
and
\mathrm{rank}(\rho)\leq\mathrm{rank}(\rho^{\prime})
then
\rho^{\prime}\in S_{n}(\rho)
for some
n\in N
.

Lemma 4
.

For every
\rho
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
S_{n}(\rho)
is a compact convex set within the set of density matrices on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathbbm{1}/d\in S_{n}(\rho)
.
Furthermore,
S_{n}(\rho)
has full dimension within the set of density matrices.

Proof.

The set of density matrices
\sigma
such that
\rho^{\otimes n}\succeq\sigma
is closed and convex and hence compact.
S_{n}(\rho)
is convex and compact, because it is its image under a linear (and hence continuous) map.
It has full dimension since in particular it includes an open ball around
\mathbbm{1}/d
(and hence also
\mathbbm{1}/d
).
∎

We now introduce a form of typicality. Note that the statement is not in the usual form, as we claim a statement for all density matrices with certain entropies.
In other words, we require an error bound that is uniform over
all
density matrices with a certain minimal entropy.
To state the Lemma, we define the distance
d(\rho,S)
between a density matrix
\rho
and a set of density matrices
S
as
d(\rho,S)=\inf_{\sigma\in S}d(\rho,\sigma)
, where
d(\rho,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho-\sigma\right\|_{1}
denotes the trace-distance.

Lemma 5

(Typicality)
.

For every
\delta>0
and
\epsilon>0
there exists
n_{\epsilon}\in N
such that for all states
\rho^{\prime}
with
H(\rho^{\prime})>H(\rho)+2\delta
,

\displaystyle d(\rho^{\prime},S_{n_{\epsilon}}(\rho))\leq\epsilon.

(5)

Proof.

In general, a density matrix
\rho
majorizes an
\epsilon
-approximation of
\rho^{\prime}
if
H^{\epsilon/2}_{\mathrm{max}}(\rho)<H^{\epsilon/2}_{\mathrm{min}}(\rho^{\prime})
, where
H^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{max}}
is the smooth max-entropy and
H^{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{min}}
the smooth min-entropy, see
[
15
,
16
]
.
By Theorem 1 of Ref.
[
17
]
(in Ref.
[
17
]
, the bounds are stated for conditional entropies of classical random variables, but in the unconditional case, they generalize immediately to the quantum case), it follows that for every
\delta\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle H^{\epsilon/2}_{\mathrm{max}}({\rho}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\leq n(H(\rho)+\delta),

(6)

\displaystyle H^{\epsilon/2}_{\mathrm{min}}({\rho^{\prime}}^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\geq n(H(\rho^{\prime})-\delta)

(7)

for
\epsilon=2^{-\frac{n\delta^{2}}{2\log^{2}(d+3)}}
(which does not depend on
\rho^{\prime}
or
\rho
)
1
1

1

We define the smoothing of the entropies in terms of trace-distance instead of
1
-norm, which accounts for a factor of
1/2
.
.
Thus, if we take

\displaystyle n_{\epsilon}=\left\lceil\frac{2\log^{2}(d+3)}{\delta^{2}}\log(\frac{1}{\epsilon})\right\rceil,

(8)

we find that
\rho^{\otimes n_{\epsilon}}
majorizes an
\epsilon
-approximation
\rho^{\prime}_{\epsilon,n_{\epsilon}}
of
{\rho^{\prime}}^{\otimes n_{\epsilon}}
as long as
H(\rho^{\prime})>H(\rho)+2\delta
.
By the triangle inequality and monotonicity of the trace-distance under quantum channels, we find:

\displaystyle d(\frac{1}{n_{\epsilon}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\epsilon}}\mathrm{Tr}_{\{1,\ldots,n_{\epsilon}\}\setminus i}[\rho^{\prime}_{\epsilon,n_{\epsilon}}],\rho^{\prime})\leq\epsilon.

(9)

Thus
d(\rho^{\prime},S_{n_{\epsilon}}(\rho))\leq\epsilon
.

∎

The crucial additional ingredient to our proof apart from the typicality Lemma is the following simple observation about convex geometry.

Lemma 6
.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
B_{y}(\delta)
denote an open ball of radius
\delta
around
y
and let
C_{\epsilon}
be a parametrized family of closed and convex sets with the following property:

\displaystyle\forall x\in B_{y}(\delta)\quad d(x,C_{\epsilon})\leq\epsilon,

(10)

where
d
is the euclidean distance on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then
y\in C_{\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon<\delta/2
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon<\delta/2
and suppose contrarily that
y\notin C_{\epsilon}
.
Let
c
be the element in
C_{\epsilon}
closest to
y
(which is unique because of convexity of
C_{\epsilon}
).
But since
y
is the center of the ball, there must then be an element
z\in B_{y}(\delta)
with
\epsilon\geq d(z,C_{\epsilon})\geq d(z,y)>\delta/2
, which is a contradiction. To find
z
, shoot a ray through
y
and
c
. The part of the ray inside of
B_{y}(\delta)
has length at least
\delta
, so in particular there must be a corresponding element
z
with
d(z,c)>\delta/2
.
∎

Corollary 7
.

Let
C
be a full-dimensional convex set in a normed, finite-dimensional, real vector space
V
and let
C_{\epsilon}\subseteq V
be a parametrized family of closed and convex sets with the following property:

\displaystyle\forall x\in C\quad d(x,C_{\epsilon})<\epsilon,

(11)

where
d
is the distance induced by the norm.
Then for any
y\in\mathrm{int}\,C
there exists an
\epsilon_{0}
such that
y\in C_{\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
y
is an interior point, there is an open ball
B_{y}(\delta)
around it and we can apply Lemma
6
(after embedding everything in some
\mathbb{R}^{d}
).
∎

We can now prove Lemma
1
:
We start with the ”if” part.
By unitary freedom and because
\mathrm{rank}(\rho)\leq\mathrm{rank}(\rho^{\prime})
, we can assume that
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\rho^{\prime})
.
Indeed, the rank-condition simply means that the dimension of the support of
\rho
is not larger than the dimension of the support of
\rho^{\prime}
.
Furthermore, we can shrink Hilbert-space to the support of
\rho^{\prime}
, so that
\rho^{\prime}
has full rank (
\rho^{\prime}>0
). We will do so in the following.
So let
H(\rho^{\prime})-H(\rho)=2\delta>0
and
\rho^{\prime}>0
. Then
\rho^{\prime}
is an interior point of the convex set of all density matrices since it has full rank.
Moreover, it has entropy
H(\rho^{\prime})>H(\rho)+\delta
and von Neumann entropy is continuous.
Therefore, there is an open ball of states around it (within the set of density matrices) all of which have entropy
>H(\rho)+\delta
.
Hence it is an interior point of the convex set
C
of density matrices with entropy
\geq H(\rho)+\delta
and
C
is full-dimensional.
For any
\epsilon>0
set
C_{\epsilon}=S_{n_{\epsilon}}(\rho)
with
n_{\epsilon}
from Lemma
5
. Then by the same Lemma, the conditions of Corollary
7
are fulfilled if we set
y=\rho^{\prime}
. Hence there exists some
n
such that
\rho^{\prime}\in S_{n}(\rho)
.

For the ”only if” part let us start with the condition on the von Neumann entropy: We use that
H
is sub-additive,
H(\rho_{12})\leq H(\rho_{1})+H(\rho_{2})
with equality if and only if
\rho_{12}=\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}
, and strictly Schur-concave, meaning that if
\rho\succeq\rho^{\prime}
but
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are not unitarily equivalent, then
H(\rho)<H(\rho^{\prime})
. So suppose that
\rho^{\otimes n}\succeq\rho^{\prime}_{n}
and
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are not unitarily equivalent. Then we have

\displaystyle nH(\rho)=H(\rho^{\otimes n})\leq H(\rho^{\prime}_{n})\leq nH(\rho^{\prime}).

(12)

We now assume
H(\rho)=H(\rho^{\prime})
to arrive at a contradiction. Then (
12
) implies
H(\rho^{\prime}_{n})=nH(\rho^{\prime})
, which in turn implies
\rho^{\prime}_{n}={\rho^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}
. But then
\rho^{\otimes n}\succeq{\rho^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}
and
H(\rho)=H(\rho^{\prime})
, which by strict Schur-concavity implies that
\rho^{\otimes n}
and
{\rho^{\prime}}^{\otimes n}
are unitarily equivalent. But then
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are unitarily equivalent, which yields the contradiction. Hence
H(\rho)<H(\rho^{\prime})
.
To conclude
\mathrm{rank}(\rho)\leq\mathrm{rank}(\rho^{\prime})
, we use that
H_{0}(\rho):=\log\left(\mathrm{rank}(\rho)\right)
is sub-additive
[
18
]
and additive over-tensor factors as well as non-decreasing under majorization. Hence

\displaystyle H_{0}(\rho^{\otimes n})=nH_{0}(\rho)\leq H_{0}(\rho^{\prime}_{n})\leq nH_{0}(\rho^{\prime}),

(13)

which finishes the proof.

W_{3}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}U_{i}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{S_{2}\cdots S_{n}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A}\otimes\left.\left|{i}\right.\right\rangle\!\!\left.\left\langle{i}\right.\right|_{R}
, where
\left.\left|{i}\right.\right\rangle_{R}
is an orthonormal basis on
R
and the
U_{i}
are chosen such that
\mathcal{D}_{\rho^{\prime}}[X]=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i}U_{i}XU_{i}^{\dagger}
. It is always possible to find such unitaries.
Note that this last unitary only involves systems
S_{1}
and
R
. Its effect is to apply the decoherence channel
\mathcal{D}_{\rho^{\prime}}
to
S
. Importantly, it was shown in Ref.
[
2
]
that it leaves the catalyst completely unchanged, since no correlations are build up between
S_{2}\cdots S_{n}A
and
R
and the state on
R
remains maximally mixed. This is easy to see by direct computation, which we omit here.
We thus find that the final state on
S
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{C}[U\rho\otimes\sigma U^{\dagger}]=\mathrm{Tr}_{C}[W_{3}W_{2}W_{1}\rho\otimes\sigma W_{1}^{\dagger}W_{2}^{\dagger}W_{3}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\rho^{\prime}}[\mathrm{Tr}_{C}[W_{2}W_{1}\rho\otimes\sigma W_{1}^{\dagger}W_{2}^{\dagger}]]

(21)

and the final state on
C
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{S}[U\rho\otimes\sigma U^{\dagger}]=\mathrm{Tr}_{S}[W_{3}W_{2}W_{1}\rho\otimes\sigma W_{1}^{\dagger}W_{2}^{\dagger}W_{3}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{S}[W_{2}W_{1}\rho\otimes\sigma W_{1}^{\dagger}W_{2}^{\dagger}].

(22)

To compute the final states, we make use of the identities
\mathrm{Tr}_{S_{2}\cdots S_{n}}[\tau[\rho^{\otimes k}\otimes\chi_{[1:n-k]}]]=\chi_{\{n-k\}}
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{S}[\tau[\rho^{\otimes k}\otimes\chi_{[1:n-k]}]]=\rho^{k}\otimes\chi_{[1:n-k-1]}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{C}[U\rho\otimes\sigma U^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\mathcal{D}_{\rho^{\prime}}\left[\chi_{\{n\}}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\chi_{\{n-k\}}\right]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{D}_{\rho^{\prime}}[\chi_{\{k\}}]=\rho^{\prime},

(23)

where we used that
\mathcal{D}_{\rho^{\prime}}[\chi_{\{k\}}]=\rho^{\prime}
.
For the final state on
C
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}_{S}[U\rho\otimes\sigma U^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left[\chi_{[1:n-1]}\otimes\left.\left|{1}\right.\right\rangle\!\!\left.\left\langle{1}\right.\right|_{A}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\rho^{\otimes k}\otimes\chi_{[1:n-(k+1)]}\left.\left|{k+1}\right.\right\rangle\!\!\left.\left\langle{k+1}\right.\right|_{A}\right]\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{R}}{d}

(24)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\rho^{\otimes k-1}\otimes\chi_{[1:n-k]}\otimes\left.\left|{k}\right.\right\rangle\!\!\left.\left\langle{k}\right.\right|_{A}\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{R}}{d}=\sigma.

(25)

The Positive Mass Theorem with Arbitrary Ends
Martin Lesourd, Ryan Unger, and Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
. We prove a Riemannian positive mass theorem for manifolds with a single asymptotically flat end, but otherwise arbitrary other ends, which can be incomplete and contain negative scalar curvature. The incompleteness and negativity is compensated for by large positive scalar curvature on an annulus, in a quantitative fashion. In the complete noncompact case with nonnegative scalar curvature, we have no extra assumption and hence prove a long-standing conjecture of Schoen and Yau.



1.
Introduction

The Riemannian positive mass theorem is a landmark result in geometric analysis and the study of scalar curvature.

Theorem 1.1

(Schoen–Yau
[
31
]
)
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a complete asymptotically Schwarzschild manifold with finitely many ends which are all asymptotically Schwarzschild. Then the mass of each end is nonnegative.

The positive mass theorem also involves a rigidity statement when the mass is zero, but we will not concern ourselves with rigidity questions in this paper. This theorem was generalized to the (now commonplace) general asymptotically flat setting in
[
32
]
via the
density theorem
, to dimensions
\leq 7
by Schoen in
[
28
]
by studying the
strong stability inequality
, and finally to all dimensions in
[
37
]
using
k
-slicings and the convenient compactification technique of Lohkamp
[
21
]
.

This theorem was furthermore generalized to initial data sets in
[
33
]
and to dimensions
\leq 7
by Eichmair–Huang–Lee–Schoen
[
9
]
. There are numerous other contributions to this field, particularly the case of spin manifolds, which was handled by Witten
[
42
]
. We note that an alternative approach to the higher dimensional case has been proposed by Lohkamp
[
22
,
23
]
.

In this paper, we prove a generalization of this theorem to the setting of manifolds with a distinguished asymptotically flat end and otherwise arbitrary and possibly incomplete other ends. For precision, see Definition
1.9
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
3\leq n\leq 7
, be a complete non-compact manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and suppose that
(M^{n},g)
has at least one asymptotically Schwarzschild end. Then the mass of the distinguished end is nonnegative.

This theorem was long conjectured by Schoen and the third author
[
35
,
36
,
20
]
, where it was shown to imply
[
35
]
the following
Liouville theorem
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
n\geq 3
, be a complete, locally conformally flat manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature. If
\Phi:M^{n}\to S^{n}
a conformal map, then
\Phi
is injective and
\partial\Phi(M)
has zero Newtonian capacity.

Recently, the present authors
[
20
]
carried out Lohkamp’s idea in this setting, making use of good estimates on the Green’s function to simplify the asymptotics, to show that Theorem
1.3
actually follows from the impossibility of
R>0
on
T^{n}\mathbin{\#}X^{n}
for arbitrary
X^{n}
. The present paper gives a new proof completely in the spirit of what is outlined in
[
35
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Manifolds with complicated ends satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
can be constructed using connected sums
[
16
]
.

The difficulty in proving Theorem
1.2
is that some of the standard approaches to Theorem
1.1
do not seem to apply. The usual procedure of using density theorems to obtain harmonic asymptotics is hampered by the lack of control of the PDEs on the arbitrary non-compact ends. That being said, it might be possible to produce harmonic asymptotics on the asymptotically flat end via a similar argument to the density theorem. That this can be done in a complete way is not at all clear and it seems like the geometry on the arbitrary ends can be altered significantly in this process. So this approach might work for Theorem
1.2
, but it is doubtful that it can yield our quantitative Theorem
1.6
.

The reduction of Theorem
1.2
to a geometric statement about
R>0
via a Lokhamp-type compactification seems to be unavailable for the same reason. This is somewhat tantalizing because the analog of the geometric statement that proves Theorem
1.1
is known:
T^{n}\#X^{n}
admits no complete metric with
R>0
when
X^{n}
is arbitrary by
[
4
]
; cf.
[
20
]
for a similar result that applies to a topologically wider class of spaces under an additional geometric assumption for
4\leq n\leq 7
. Finally, spinor based proofs also fall short because they involve controlling boundary integrals in the arbitrary ends, about which nothing is assumed.

In view of these obstacles, the only viable approach seems to be the original proof
[
31
,
28
]
, which constructs a limiting hypersurface from solutions to the Plateau problem in the asymptotically flat end on which the mass is assumed negative. This method cannot be naively implemented however, because nothing seems to stop the solutions from venturing out into the arbitrary ends, which would ruin the asymptotic flatness and completeness of the limiting hypersurface and conformal metric that is crucial for the rest of the argument.

Remark 1.5
.

When
n=3
, Richard Schoen has pointed out to us that Theorem
1.2
follows from essentially the original argument in
[
31
]
as follows: The complete minimal surface constructed must be conformally equivalent to
\mathbb{C}
by
[
11
,
34
]
and the fact that the scalar curvature can be taken strictly positive on the asymptotically Schwarzschild end (see Section
5
below). It follows that since it is asymptotically planar, it can only enter the core a bounded amount. At this point, the rest of the argument goes through unchanged. This does not give our stronger Theorem
1.6
below, however.

To overcome the issues in dimensions
4\leq n\leq 7
, we minimize a version of Gromov’s
\mu
-bubble functional
[
13
,
14
]
, which we construct to prevent solutions from exploring the other ends. The
\mu
-bubble functional, which has recently found great success in noncompact scalar curvature problems
[
4
,
15
,
27
,
44
,
45
]
, is defined on Caccioppoli sets
\Omega
by

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}\Omega)-\int_{M}(\chi_{\Omega}-\chi_{\Omega_{0}})h\,d\mathcal{H}^{n},

where
h
is a function tending to
\pm\infty
at finite distance from the region of interest and
\Omega_{0}
is a reference set. This allows for a trivial construction of barrier surfaces and stable
\mu
-bubbles (which have prescribed mean curvature
h
) will be Yamabe positive as long as the following condition is satisfied:

(1.2)

R_{g}+h^{2}-2|\nabla_{g}h|>0.

So to use
\mu
-bubbles effectively, one has to construct
h
which blows up in the right way for the geometry to work out and yet still satisfy the inequality (
1.2
). In our case, the function
h
is identically zero in the asymptotic region.

Based on this approach, we can actually prove something stronger and more surprising than Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
3\leq n\leq 7
, be an asymptotically Schwarzschild manifold, not assumed to be complete or have nonnegative scalar curvature everywhere. Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be neighborhoods of infinity with
\overline{U_{2}}\subset U_{1}
and
D>0
. If furthermore

(1)

g
has no points of incompleteness in the
D
-neighborhood of
U_{1}
,

(2)

R_{g}\geq 0
in the
D
-neighborhood of
U_{1}
, and

(3)

the scalar curvature satisfies the largeness assumption

(1.3)

R_{g}>\frac{32}{D}\left(\frac{8}{D}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{dist}_{g}(U_{2},\partial U_{1})}\right)

on
\overline{U_{1}}\setminus U_{2}
,

then
m\geq 0
.

Figure 1.
Theorem
1.6
.

This theorem is proved in Section
4
under the additional assumption that
R_{g}>0
sufficiently far out on the end. This assumption is removed in Section
5
and Theorem
1.2
is then obtained as a corollary.

Remark 1.7
.

(1) This theorem allows for incompleteness and negative scalar curvature as long as it is far enough away from the region in which the largeness condition (
1.3
) holds.

(2) The fact that scalar curvature is only assumed to be nonnegative away from the incompleteness is particularly surprising in view of the aforementioned approaches to Theorem
1.1
, which require nonnegative scalar curvature everywhere.

(3) The condition (
1.3
) is only needed to enforce the condition (
1.2
) in our construction.

(4) The prototypical
U_{i}
is the region in
\mathcal{E}
with
|x|>r_{i}
and
r_{1}<r_{2}
. In this case, the largeness assumption (
1.3
) can be replaced by a lower bound involving
D
,
r_{2}-r_{1}
, and the Lipschitz constant of
|x|
with respect to
g
, which in turn can be estimated using

(1.4)

|\nabla_{g}|x||^{2}\leq 1+\sum_{ij}|g^{ij}-\delta^{ij}|.

See Remark
3.2
.

(5) Theorem
1.6
is interesting to consider in light of the negative mass Schwarzschild solution, which is incomplete and scalar flat and hence falls short of Theorem
1.6
because of condition (3). But our theorem shows that any perturbation which increases scalar curvature (and preserves the asymptotically Schwarzschild nature) cannot “push” the incompleteness away, so that condition (1) is not satisfied.

Definitions and conventions.
We assume all manifolds are smooth and oriented. When discussing minimal hypersurfaces, the dimensional restriction
n\leq 7
coming from the regularity theory will be implicit.

We use the notation
A\lesssim B
to denote
A\leq CB
, where the constant
C
does not depend on
A
or
B
.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
(\overline{X},d)
be its completion. For example,
\overline{X}
can be constructed by taking appropriate equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences. A point in
\overline{X}\setminus X
is called a called a
point of incompleteness
for
X
. A set
S\subset X
is said to be
complete
if its closure in
X
remains closed under the inclusion
X\to\overline{X}
.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
M^{n}
be a noncompact manifold with distinguished end
\mathcal{E}
. We say that
(M,g)
possesses a
structure of infinity
along
\mathcal{E}
if
\mathcal{E}
possesses no points of incompleteness and there exists a diffeomorphism

\Phi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\text{ball}

and the coordinate norm
|x|=((x^{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x^{n})^{2})^{1/2}
diverges as we go out along the end. The set
\mathring{M}=M\setminus\mathcal{E}
is called the
core
. Note that in our definition, the core is not assumed to be compact.

We say that
(M^{n},g,\mathcal{E})
is
asymptotically Schwarzschild
of
mass

m\in\mathbb{R}
if in the coordinates
x^{i}
,

g_{ij}-\left(1+\frac{m}{2|x|^{n-2}}\right)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\delta_{ij}\in C^{2,\alpha}_{n-1}(\mathcal{E})

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. For the spaces
C^{k,\alpha}_{\tau}
, see Definition
4.4
.

We say that
(M^{n},g,\mathcal{E})
is
asymptotically flat
of
order

\tau
if

g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\in W^{2,p}_{\tau}(\mathcal{E})

for some
p>n
and
R_{g}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{E})
. For the spaces
W^{k,p}_{\tau}
, see Definition
4.20
. We will always assume
\tau>\frac{n-2}{2}
. In this case, the
ADM mass
is defined by

(1.5)

m=\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\frac{1}{2(n-1)|S^{n-1}|}\int_{|x|=\rho}(\partial_{i}g_{ij}-\partial_{j}g_{ii})\frac{x^{j}}{|x|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

The Dirichlet problem for
\mu
-bubbles

We first prove a general existence theorem for
\mu
-bubbles but in the setting of the Dirichlet problem. A similar situation was already considered by Yau in
[
43
]
.

We always assume the dimension of the ambient manifold lies between
2
and
7
so that we may apply the regularity theory for almost-minimizing hypersurfaces
[
7
,
24
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact orientable Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial M=\partial_{0}M\amalg\partial_{-}M
, where
\partial_{0}M
and
\partial_{-}M
are closed hypersurfaces. Let
\Gamma^{n-2}\subset\partial_{0}M
be a smooth hypersurface which bounds a compact set
P^{n}\subset\partial_{0}M
. Let
h
be a real-valued smooth function on
M^{\circ}
, extending smoothly to
\partial_{0}M
, such that
h<H_{\partial_{0}M}
along
\partial_{0}M
and
h\to-\infty
along
\partial_{-}M
. Then there exists a hypersurface
\Sigma^{n-1}\subset M
with the following properties:

(1)

\partial\Sigma=\Gamma,

(2)

The interior of
\Sigma
is smooth and contained in the interior of
M
, and

(3)

The mean curvature of
\Sigma
is equal to
h
with respect to the outward normal in Lemma
2.2
below.

We view
P
as a top-dimensional chain in
\partial_{0}M
and as a codimension 1 chain in
M
.
Let
\Sigma^{n}\subset M
be a smooth hypersurface with oriented boundary equal to
\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.2
.

With the notation as above, if
\Sigma
is homologous to
P
, then
\Sigma\cup P=\partial U
, where
U\subset M
is a unique open set.

We denote this assignment by
\Sigma\mapsto U_{\Sigma}
.

Proof.

The chain
A=\llbracket\Sigma\rrbracket-\llbracket P\rrbracket
is a cycle in
M
. Using the Mayer–Vietoris sequence, it is easy to see that
H_{0}(M\setminus A)
has rank at most 2, and we want to rule out the case when it has rank 1. In this case it would be possible to find a curve intersecting
A
exactly once. (Take a short segment orthogonal to
\Sigma
and since
M\setminus A
is connected, the endpoints can be joined up without further intersections with
A
.) But
A
is homologous to zero and intersection number is a homological invariant.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the class of smooth hypersurfaces considered in Lemma
2.2
and define

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}(\Sigma)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma)-\int_{U_{\Sigma}}h\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Let
\{\Sigma_{i}\}\subset\mathcal{C}
be a sequence of hypersurfaces such that

\lim_{i}\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{i})=\inf_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{F}.

We show using a replacement argument that we can assume
\Sigma_{i}
avoids a collar neighborhood of
\partial_{-}M
. We can foliate a collar of
\partial_{-}M
by hypersurfaces
S^{\tau}_{-}=\partial_{\mathrm{top}}\Omega^{\tau}_{-}
whose normal
\eta
(pointing into the bulk, out of
\Omega^{\tau}_{-}
) satisfies
h<-\operatorname{div}\eta
for
0<\tau\leq\tau_{0}
. If
\Sigma_{i}
enters this region, we perform a replacement and define
\Sigma^{\prime}_{i}=\partial(U_{\Sigma}\setminus\Omega_{-}^{\tau})
. Then as in Chodosh–Li
[
4
]
,
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma^{\prime}_{i})<\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{i})
. So we may assume that
\Sigma_{i}
avoids a collar of
\partial_{-}M
and hence we may assume
|h|\lesssim 1
.

The condition
h<H_{\partial_{0}M}
allows for a similar replacement/barrier argument near
\partial_{0}M
. See Yau
[
43
]
.

Since
M
is compact and

is bounded in the region of interest,
\mathcal{F}(\Sigma_{i})\gtrsim-1
. We obviously have
\mathbf{M}(\Sigma_{i})+\mathbf{M}(P)\lesssim 1
by previous observations. Also,
\mathbf{M}(U_{\Sigma_{i}})\lesssim 1.
So by the BV compactness theorem, there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
i
) of the
U_{\Sigma_{i}}
’s converging in the sense of Caccioppoli sets. The limiting set will have smooth boundary away from the corner along
P
and will have mean curvature

. It also satisfies a stability inequality as shown below.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

If
h=0
near
\partial_{0}M
, then the following second variation formula holds for the functional
\mathcal{F}
defined in (
2.1
). Let
F:M\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M
be a one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms with variation vector field
X=\partial_{t}F|_{t=0}
on
\Sigma
and acceleration
Z=\nabla_{\partial_{t}}\partial_{t}F|_{t=0}
on
\Sigma
. Then

(2.2)

\delta^{2}_{X,Z}\mathcal{F}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}a_{X,Z}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\Sigma}d_{X,Z}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where

(2.3)

\displaystyle a_{X,Z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left[|(\nabla_{e_{i}}X)^{\perp}|^{2}-\mathrm{Rm}(X,e_{i},X,e_{i})\right]-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n-1}\langle e_{i},\nabla_{e_{j}}X\rangle\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}X,e_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{div}_{\Sigma}Z+(\operatorname{div}_{\Sigma}X)^{2},\quad\text{and}

(2.4)

\displaystyle d_{X,Z}

\displaystyle=-h^{2}-X(h)\langle X,\nu\rangle-\langle Z,\nu\rangle h-\langle X,\delta_{X}\nu\rangle h,

where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}
is an orthonormal frame on
T\Sigma
. The first term satisfies the following integration by parts formula: If
D\subset\Sigma
is a subdomain and
X=\varphi\nu+\hat{X}
and
Z=\zeta\nu+\hat{Z}
, then

(2.5)

\int_{D}a_{X,Z}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{D}b_{X,Z}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial D}c_{X,Z}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-2},

where

(2.6)

\displaystyle b_{X,Z}

\displaystyle=|\nabla_{\Sigma}\varphi|^{2}-(\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|A|^{2}-h^{2})\varphi^{2}+h(\zeta-2\nabla_{\hat{X}}\varphi+A(\hat{X},\hat{X})),\quad\text{and}

(2.7)

\displaystyle c_{X,Z}

\displaystyle=\langle(\operatorname{div}_{\Sigma}\hat{X})\hat{X}-2\varphi S(\hat{X})-\nabla_{\hat{X}}\hat{X}+\hat{Z},\eta\rangle,

where
S
is the shape operator and
\eta
is the conormal in
\Sigma
.

Proof.

It is well known
[
39
,
19
]
that the second variation of the area functional alone is given by

\delta_{X,Z}^{2}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}a_{X,Z}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

The first variation of the bulk term is given by
[
24
]

\delta_{X}\int_{U_{\Sigma}}h\,d\mathcal{H}^{n}=\int_{\Sigma}h\langle X,\nu\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

This shows that
\Sigma
has mean curvature
h
. Varying this gives

\int_{\Sigma}X(h)\langle X,\nu\rangle+h\langle Z,\nu\rangle+h\langle X,\delta_{X}\nu\rangle-h\langle X,\nu\rangle\langle\mathbf{H},X\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Since
h=0
near
\partial\Sigma
, there is no boundary term involving
h
. The integration by parts formula is also standard; a careful proof can be found in
[
19
]
.
∎

We also recall

Lemma 2.4
.

At points where
\hat{X}=0
,
\delta_{X}\nu=-\nabla_{\Sigma}\varphi
.

Proof.

Let

be the variation. Using

3.
Construction of the weight

In this section, we construct a general class of
\mu
-bubble weights in any manifold admitting a structure of infinity. Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be neighborhoods of the distinguished infinity with compact closures in the natural one-point compactification of
\mathcal{E}
and such that
\overline{U_{2}}\subset U_{1}
. We set
\rho=\mathrm{dist}(U_{2},\partial U_{1})
, which is finite.

A model situation for this is the following: Let
\mathcal{S}_{r}\subset\mathcal{E}
be the coordinate sphere
|x|=r
. Consider two radii
r_{1}<r_{2}
and let
U_{i}
be the set
\{x\in\mathcal{E}:|x|>r_{i}\}
. Then
U_{1}\setminus\overline{U_{2}}
is the annulus
\{r_{1}<|x|<r_{2}\}
. In this case,
\rho
is not necessarily
r_{2}-r_{1}
, but see Remark
3.2
.

We now state the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g,\mathcal{E})
possess a structure of infinity, let
U_{i}
be as above, and let
D>0
. For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a function
h\in C^{0}(M,\overline{\mathbb{R}})
, smooth where it is finite, with the following properties:

(1)

h=0
in
U_{2}
,

(2)

h=-\infty
outside the metric
D
-neighborhood of
U_{1}
, and

(3)

h
satisfies the estimates

h^{2}-2|\nabla_{g}h|\geq 0\quad\text{in $U_{2}\cup(M\setminus U_{1})$},

and

h^{2}-2|\nabla_{g}h|\geq-\sigma(\rho,D)+\eta(\varepsilon)\quad\text{in $U_{1}\setminus U_{2}$},

where

\sigma(\rho,D)=\frac{32}{D}\left(\frac{8}{D}+\frac{1}{\rho}\right)

In this context, we use the notation
\eta(\varepsilon)
to simply mean a quantity which tends to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Remark 3.2
.

In an asymptotically flat manifold, the metric distance
\rho
can be estimated in terms of the coordinate distance
|x|
using the pointwise closeness of
g_{ij}
to
\delta_{ij}
. It is easy to see then that as
r_{i}\to\infty
,
\rho\to r_{2}-r_{1}
. Alternatively, we can define the cutoff function below in terms of
|x|
, and then
\sigma
will have to involve an estimate for
|\nabla_{g}|x||
.

To prove Proposition
3.1
first recall the following elementary approximation result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
a Lipschitz continuous function. For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a smooth function
\overline{f}
on
M
such that
\|f-\overline{f}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(M)}<\varepsilon.

If
f
is smooth on some closed set
C
, we can further demand that
f=\overline{f}
on
C
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be open sets in a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with compact closure and
\overline{U_{2}}\subset U_{1}
. Then there exists a smooth function
\vartheta
, taking values in
[0,1]
, such that
\vartheta=0
on
U_{2}
,
\vartheta=1
on
U_{1}^{c}
, and

|\nabla_{g}\vartheta|\leq\frac{2}{\mathrm{dist}_{g}(U_{2},\partial U_{1}).}

Proof.

Apply the previous lemma to the distance function to
\partial U_{1}
and rescale.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Our construction is motivated by consideration of solutions to the ODE
2h^{\prime}=h^{2}
. Define

\rho_{0}=\begin{cases}0&\text{in $U_{1}$}\\
\mathrm{dist}_{g}(x,\partial U_{1})&\text{in $M\setminus U_{1}$}\end{cases}.

Let
\rho_{1}
be a smoothing of this function with Lipschitz constant
2
. By Lemma
3.3
, we may assume
\rho_{1}
to vanish in
U_{1}\setminus N_{\varepsilon}(\partial U_{1})
, where
N_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
denotes the metric
\varepsilon
-neighborhood. On
N_{\varepsilon}(\partial U_{1}),

\rho_{1}=\eta(\varepsilon).
We also assume
\sup_{M}|\rho_{0}-\rho_{1}|<\frac{1}{2}
.

Now define

h_{1}=\frac{4}{\rho_{1}-\frac{D}{2}}.

Note first that
h_{1}=-8/D
in
U_{1}\setminus N_{\varepsilon}(\partial U_{1})
and
|h_{1}+8/D|=o(\varepsilon)
in
N_{\varepsilon}(\partial U_{1})
. Secondly, note that
h_{1}=-\infty
in the region of distance
D
away from
U_{1}
in
M\setminus U_{1}
. Thirdly, we compute

\displaystyle g_{ij}

\displaystyle-\delta_{ij}-\frac{2m}{n-2}|x|^{-(n-2)}\delta_{ij}\in C^{2,\alpha}_{n-1},

\displaystyle g^{ij}

Lemma 4.9

Lemma 4.10

In an asymptotically Schwarzschild manifold, the norms defined by

||X|_{g}-|X|_{\delta}|\lesssim|x|^{-(n-2)}|X|_{\delta}\lesssim|x|^{-(n-2)}|X|_{g},

From the decay of

\left|\frac{|X|^{2}_{g}}{|X|^{2}_{\delta}}-1\right|\lesssim|x|^{-(n-2)},

Factoring the left-hand side and dividing through gives

Clearly

\frac{|X|_{g}}{|X|_{\delta}}\approx 1,

so that

\left|\frac{|X|_{g}}{|X|_{\delta}}-1\right|\lesssim|x|^{-(n-2)},

which implies the claim.

Lemma 4.16
.

The assumption

(1)

(2)

(3)

(6)

(1) follows from Proposition

and

(Meyers

Let

\displaystyle u(x)

We emphasize that the theorem is stated here for
(n-1)

Proof of Proposition
4.5
.

We rewrite (

) in the form

(4.7)

(\Pi_{\delta}^{ij}-B^{ij})\partial_{ij}u+(\Pi_{\overline{g}}*\Gamma_{\overline{g}}+\partial\log\psi)*\partial u=\Pi_{\overline{g}}*\Gamma_{\overline{g}}+\overline{g}*\partial_{n}\log\psi,

neglecting numerical constants multiplying the various terms. Comparing with Meyers’ theorem, the only thing that needs to be checked is that

Applying Myers’ theorem with

so that

Definition 4.20

(Weighted Sobolev Spaces)

For

|x|\leq r_{0}
, then
\varphi(x)=1+\frac{3}{2}t_{0}=1+O(r_{0}^{-(n-2)})
and we extend it by this constant to all of
M
. If
|x|\geq 2r_{0}
, then

\varphi(x)=1-\frac{m}{4|x|^{n-2}}.

From this is is apparent that
\tilde{g}
is asymptotically Schwarzschild with mass
\frac{m}{2}<0
. Finally, the scalar curvature is given by the usual formula

R_{\tilde{g}}=\varphi^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(-4\frac{n-1}{n-2}\Delta_{g}\varphi+R_{g}\varphi\right).

The Laplacian term has the correct sign, as is easily checked:

-\Delta_{g}\varphi(x)=\frac{m}{4}\zeta^{\prime\prime}|\nabla|x|^{-(n-2)}|^{2}+\frac{m}{4}\zeta^{\prime}\Delta_{g}|x|^{-(n-2)}\geq 0

everwhere, and
>0
for
|x|\geq 2t_{0}
.
∎

This allows us to remove the strict positivity assumption in Proposition
4.26
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We apply the conformal transformation of Lemma
4.1
to
(M,g)
to bring it into the form of Proposition
4.26
. Indeed, since
\overline{U_{1}}\setminus U_{2}
is compact and
R_{g}
is continuous,
R_{g}-\sigma(\rho,D)
has a positive minimum there. It follows that if we choose
r_{0}
sufficiently large in Lemma
4.1
, then
R_{\tilde{g}}>\sigma(\rho,D)
on
\overline{U_{1}}\setminus U_{2}
as well. Furthermore, distances will be distorted an arbitrarily small amount. So if the original mass is negative, we have a counterexample to Proposition
4.26
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If
(M,g)
is complete and has nonnegative scalar curvature everywhere, apply Lemma
4.1
with some
r_{0}
to make the scalar curvature strictly positive on the end. We may then take
U_{i}
as in Remark
1.7
(4) sufficiently far out that the scalar curvature is strictly positive on the compact set
\overline{U_{1}}\setminus U_{2}
. Now
\rho
is fixed and since for fixed
\rho
,

\lim_{D\to\infty}\sigma(\rho,D)=0,

we can find
D
sufficiently large that
R_{\tilde{g}}>\sigma(D,\rho)
on
\overline{U_{1}}\setminus U_{2}
. But
(M,g)
is complete with nonnegative scalar curvature globally, so we satisfy all the hypotheses of Theorem
1.6
with this choice of
\rho
and
D
. It follows that the original mass is nonnegative.
∎

Appendix A
Curvature estimate in homogeneously regular manifolds

In this appendix we give an independent proof Lemma
4.8
.

Definition A.1
.

Let
K\geq 0
and
i_{0}>0
be real numbers. We say that a complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
is
(K,i_{0})
-homogeneously regular
if its sectional curvatures are bounded in absolute value by
K
and injectivity radius bounded below by
i_{0}
. In the case of a noncompact manifold, we use the same definition, with the understanding that we work at a distance at least
2i_{0}
away from any incomplete points; see Definition
1.8
.

In the rest of this section,
K
and
i_{0}
are fixed and all manifolds are assumed to be
(K,i_{0})
-homogeneously regular. We work with geodesic balls
B_{\rho}(x_{0})^{g}
and use the phrase “
\Sigma
is a minimal/minimizing submanifold of
B_{\rho}^{g}(x_{0})
” to mean that
\Sigma\subset B_{\rho}^{g}(x_{0})
and that
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial B_{\rho}^{g}(x_{0})
.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
2\leq n\leq 7
,
K\geq 0
, and
i_{0}>0
. There exist universal constants
\rho_{0}\in(0,i_{0})
and
C
depending only on
n,K
, and
i_{0}
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a
(K,i_{0})
-homogeneously regular Riemannian manifold,
x_{0}\in M
, and
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
. If
\Sigma
is an area minimizing hypersurface in
B_{\rho}^{g}(x_{0})
with
x_{0}\in\Sigma
, then

|A_{\Sigma}(x_{0})|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{2}}.

In our definition of homogeneously regular, we can only obtain coordinates in which the metric is
C^{1,\alpha}
-controlled, see Lemma
A.5
below. Therefore, it might seem like this is a regularity theorem for
C^{1,\alpha}
parametric elliptic functionals, but G. De Philippis has pointed out that this is still essentially perturbative about the flat space regularity theory (due to the freedom of choosing
\rho_{0}
very small). This can be formalized using the work of Tamanini
[
40
]
. In this appendix, we give an alternative proof via a contradiction argument, in the spirit of White
[
41
]
(for an Allard-type result) and Simon
[
38
]
(for a universal density bound).

Theorem A.3

(Allard-type theorem)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
K\geq 0
, and
i_{0}>0
. There exist universal constants
\rho_{0}\in(0,i_{0})
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
C
, and
\tau>0
depending only on
n,K
, and
i_{0}
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a
(K,i_{0})
-homogeneously regular Riemannian manifold,
x_{0}\in M
,
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
, and
\Sigma
a minimal hypersurface in
B_{\rho}^{g}(x_{0})
with
x_{0}\in\Sigma
. If

\Theta(x_{0},\rho)\leq 1+\varepsilon_{0},

then

\sup_{\Sigma\cap B_{\tau\rho}^{g}(x_{0})}|A|^{2}\leq\frac{C}{\rho^{2}}.

Note that this does not follow directly from Allard’s theorem
[
1
]
because it is not possible to bound the mean curvature of
\Sigma
with respect to the underlying Euclidean structure.

Proposition A.4

(Universal density bound)
.

Let
2\leq n\leq 7
,
K\geq 0
, and
i_{0}>0
. There exists a universal constant
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
depending only on
n,K
, and
i_{0}
with the following property. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a
(K,i_{0})
-homogeneously regular Riemannian manifold,
x_{0}\in M
, and
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
. If
\Sigma
is an area minimizing hypersurface in
B_{\rho}^{g}(x_{0})
with
x_{0}\in\Sigma
, then

\Theta(x_{0},\delta_{0}\rho)\leq 1+\varepsilon_{0},

where
\varepsilon_{0}
is as in Theorem
A.3
.

Combining Theorems
A.3
and
A.4
immediately gives Theorem
A.2
. To prove these theorems, we recall a basic result about existence of harmonic coordinate charts.

Lemma A.5

(Hebey–Herzlich
[
17
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be
(K,i_{0})
-homogeneously regular and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. There exists a constant
Q_{0}
and a number
r_{0}
(depending only on
K,i_{0}
,
n
, and
\alpha
) such that for any
x_{0}\in M
, there exists a harmonic coordinate chart
x^{i}
on
B_{r_{0}}^{g}(x_{0})
sending
x_{0}
to the origin and satisfying

(A.1)

Q_{0}^{-1}\delta_{ij}\leq g_{ij}\leq Q_{0}g_{ij}\quad\text{as quadratic forms},

and

(A.2)

\|\partial_{k}g_{ij}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(U)}\leq Q_{0},

where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the domain of definition of the harmonic coordinates.

For a metric
g
, we define the area ratio at a point
x_{0}\in M
and scale
r
according to

\Theta_{\Sigma}^{g}(x_{0},r)=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{g}(\Sigma\cap B_{r}^{g}(x_{0}))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}.

The following is easily proved using the Hessian comparison theorem
[
5
, page 234]
.

Lemma A.6

(Monotonicity formula)
.

On any minimal hypersurface in a
(K,i_{0})
-homogeneously regular manifold,

(A.3)

\frac{d}{dr}\left(e^{(n-1)\sqrt{K}r}\Theta(x_{0},r)\right)\geq 0

for
r<i_{0}
.

Using this, we can bound the area ratio at nearby points; compare
[
8
, Lemma D.7]
.

Lemma A.7
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
A.3
and also let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exist constants
\beta,\eta>0
and
\rho_{0}\in(0,\min\{r_{0},i_{0}\})
(depending only on
n,K,i_{0}
, and
\varepsilon
and where
r_{0}
is the harmonic radius from Lemma
A.5
with an otherwise arbitrary choice of
\alpha
) with the property that if

\Theta(x_{0},\rho)\leq 1+\varepsilon

for some
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
, then

1-\eta\leq\Theta(y,\sigma)\leq 1+2\varepsilon

for every
y\in B^{g}_{\beta\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma
and
\sigma<(1-\beta)\rho
. With all other parameters fixed,
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\beta=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\eta=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\rho_{0}=0
.

Proof.

Using the monotonicity at
y\in B^{g}_{\beta}(x_{0})
as well as the inclusion
B_{(1-\beta)\rho}(y)\subset B_{\rho}(x_{0})
we estimate

\displaystyle\Theta(y,\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq e^{(n-1)\sqrt{K}((1-\beta)\rho-\sigma)}\Theta(y,(1-\beta)\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{(n-1)\sqrt{K}((1-\beta)\rho-\sigma)}}{(1-\beta)^{n}}(1+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq 1+2\varepsilon

for
\beta
and
\rho_{0}
well chosen.

For the lower bound, we note that monotonicity implies

\Theta(y,\sigma)\geq e^{-(n-1)\sqrt{K}\sigma}.

If
\sigma
is sufficiently small, then we can write this as

\Theta(y,r)\geq 1-\eta(\varepsilon).\qed

Proof of Theorem
A.3
.

By Lemma
A.7
it suffices to show that there exist constants
\varepsilon_{0}
and
C
such that

\sup_{y\in B_{\beta\rho}^{g}(x_{0})\cap\Sigma,\sigma<(1-\beta)\rho}\Theta_{\Sigma}^{g}(y,\sigma)\leq 1+\varepsilon_{0}

implies

\sup_{\sigma<\beta\rho}\left(\sigma^{2}\sup_{B_{\beta\rho-\sigma}(x_{0})}|A_{\Sigma}|^{2}\right)\leq C,

where
\rho<\rho_{0}(\varepsilon_{0})
. Indeed, if this is true we may take
\tau=\frac{\beta}{2}
and
\sigma=\tau\rho
.

Arguing by contradiction, we infer the existence of
(K,i_{0})
-homogeneously regular manifolds
(M^{n}_{j},g_{j},x_{j})
, numbers
\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, minimal hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{j}\subset B^{g_{j}}_{\rho_{j}}(x_{j})
,
\rho_{j}\in(0,\rho_{0}(\varepsilon_{j}))
, such that

(A.4)

\sup_{y\in B^{g_{i}}_{\beta_{j}\rho_{j}}(x_{j})\cap\Sigma_{j},\sigma<(1-\beta_{j})\rho_{j}}\Theta_{j}(y,\sigma)\leq 1+\varepsilon_{j},

but

\gamma_{j}^{2}:=\sup_{\sigma<\beta_{j}\rho_{j}}\left(\sigma^{2}\sup_{B_{\beta_{j}\rho_{j}-\sigma}(x_{j})\cap\Sigma_{j}}\right)\to\infty.

We find
\sigma_{j}<\beta_{j}\rho_{j}
and
y_{j}\in\overline{B}_{\beta_{j}\rho_{j}-\sigma_{j}}(x_{j})
such that

\gamma_{j}^{2}=\sigma_{j}^{2}\sup_{B_{\beta_{j}\rho_{j}-\sigma_{j}}(x_{j})\cap\Sigma_{j}}|A_{j}|^{2}=\sigma_{j}^{2}|A_{j}(y_{j})|^{2}.

From the definitions, we see

\sup_{B_{\beta_{j}\rho_{j}-\frac{\sigma_{j}}{2}}(x_{j})\cap\Sigma_{j}}|A_{j}|^{2}\leq 4|A_{j}(y_{j})|^{2}

and by the inclusion
B_{\frac{\sigma_{j}}{2}}(y_{j})\subset B_{\beta_{j}\rho_{j}-\frac{\sigma_{j}}{2}}(x_{j})
,

\sup_{B_{\frac{\sigma_{j}}{2}}(y_{j})}|A_{j}|^{2}\leq 4|A_{j}(y_{j})|^{2}.

By Lemma
A.5
and our choice of
\rho_{0}
, we can introduce harmonic coordinates
y_{j}^{i}
on
B_{\frac{\sigma_{j}}{2}}^{g_{j}}(y_{j})
(which we can now view as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) sending
y_{j}
to
0
and satisfying (
A.1
) and (
A.2
). Let
\lambda_{j}=|A_{j}(y_{j})|^{-1}=o(j)
and rescale
(B^{g_{j}}_{\frac{\sigma_{j}}{2}}(y_{j}),g_{j})
by
\lambda_{j}
. Since
\frac{\sigma_{j}}{2}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\to\infty,

\bigcup_{j}B^{g_{j}}_{\frac{\sigma_{j}}{2}}(y_{j})=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, the components of the rescaled metrics are given by
(g_{j})_{kl}(\frac{y}{\lambda_{j}})
, so by (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) (sub-)converge in
C^{1,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the Euclidean metric. By essentially the same calculation leading to Lemma
4.9
, Proposition
4.13
, and Schauder theory
[
12
, Chapter 6]
, the rescaled hypersurfaces
\lambda_{j}^{-1}\Sigma_{j}
(sub-)converge in
C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to a complete, smooth minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. (The coefficients of the minimal graph-type equations satisfied by the
\lambda_{j}^{-1}\Sigma_{j}
’s converge in
C^{0,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}
to their Euclidean values, which suffices for this application of Schauder theory.) The limiting hypersurface
\Sigma
has
|A(0)|=1
.

Finally, we need to show that
\Theta_{\Sigma}(0,R)=1
for every
R
. This follows from the upper bound (
A.4
) and the lower bound in Lemma
A.7
. Therefore
\Sigma
is a cone, but as it is smooth, it must be a hyperplane. This contradicts
|A(0)|=1
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
A.4
.

We argue again by contradiction. There exist Riemannian manifolds
(M^{n}_{j},g_{j},x_{j})
, numbers
\delta_{j}\to 0
, and area minimizing hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{j}\subset B_{\rho_{j}}^{g_{j}}(x_{j})
with
x_{j}\in\Sigma_{j}
such that

\Theta_{j}(x_{j},\delta_{j}\rho_{j})\geq 1+\varepsilon_{0}.

By the monotonicity formula,

\Theta_{j}(x_{j},\rho)\geq e^{-(n-1)\sqrt{K}(1-\delta_{j})\rho_{j}}(1+\varepsilon_{0})\geq 1+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}

for any
\rho\in(\delta_{j}\rho_{j},\rho_{j})
, after possibly adjusting
\rho_{0}(\varepsilon_{0})
. We now rescale with scale factor
\lambda_{j}=(\delta_{j}\rho_{j})^{-1/2}=o(j)
. By the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
A.3
, the rescaled metrics will (sub-)converge (in harmonic coordinates) to the Euclidean metric. By a slight modification of the proof of
[
39
, Theorem 34.5]
(using the fact that the area functionals with respect to the rescaled metrics are converging to the flat one in the obvious way), one can show that
\lambda_{j}^{-1}\Sigma_{j}
converge to some (Euclidean) area minimizing current
T
in the weak topology and as varifolds. Furthermore,
T
will be multiplicity one and is carried by a smooth hypersurface
\Sigma
(here we use
n\leq 7
). By varifold convergence, we have

\Theta_{\Sigma}(0,R)\geq 1+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}

for every
R>0
. But
\Sigma
is a smooth hypersurface, so its
(n-1)
-density at the origin is 1. This is a contradiction.
∎

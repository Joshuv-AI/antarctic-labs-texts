Initial Value Problems for Integrable Systems on a Semi-Strip
Alexander L. SAKHNOVICH

Abstract
Two important cases, where boundary conditions and solutions of the well-known integrable equations on a semi-strip are uniquely determined by the initial conditions, are rigorously studied in detail. First, the case of rectangular matrix solutions of the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation with quasi-analytic boundary conditions is dealt with. (The result is new even for a scalar nonlinear Schrödinger equation.) Next, a special case of the nonlinear optics ( N N -wave) equation is considered.

Cauchy problems for wave equations were successfully investigated
using the classical inverse scattering transform, Laplace transforms and also
some other methods.
The theory of initial-boundary value problems (and problems in a quarter-plane
or semi-strip) is somewhat more complicated even for the case of linear wave equations.

. The mentioned above results
and discussions are also related to the integrable nonlinear equations.
Although it is impossible to refer here to the whole variety of important publications on the
initial-boundary value problems for integrable wave equations, we would like
to list just some:
[

Usually (excluding, e.g., sine-Gordon case
[

,
37
,
42
,
48
]
),
initial-boundary value problems are overdetermined (see
[
11
,
18
,
48
]
) for such cases of integrable nonlinear equations, where
exact procedures to recover solutions from initial and boundary values exist.
Thus, the reduction of the initial-boundary conditions, which are necessary to recover solutions, becomes crucial for
solving initial-boundary value problems.

In this paper we extend and develop further the work (which was started in
[
47
]
) on the reduction
of the necessary initial-boundary conditions. Namely, a case, where boundary conditions provide direct information about an initial condition,
was investigated in
[

.
Here we study the cases, where an initial condition provides direct information about boundary conditions,
and the solutions of the well-known nonlinear integrable equations in a semi-strip are uniquely defined by the
initial conditions. (One could speak, perhaps, about additional symmetries of the solutions.)

We consider such situations using the inverse spectral transform approach
[
6
,
7

28

More precisely, we follow the scheme introduced in
[
49

[

The scheme is applicable to various integrable equations
and several interesting uniqueness and existence theorems were proved in this way (see
[
48

and references therein for more details). Most of the mentioned above uniqueness and existence theorems
were obtained for the equations with scalar solutions. Here we consider
the matrix defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger (defocusing NLS or dNLS) equation

\displaystyle 2v_{t}=\mathrm{i}(v_{xx}-2vv^{*}v),\qquad v_{t}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v,

(1.1)

which is equivalent
[
59

60

to the compatibility condition

\displaystyle G_{t}-F_{x}+[G,F]=0\qquad[G,F]:=GF-FG

(1.2)

of the auxiliary linear systems

\displaystyle y_{x}=Gy,\qquad y_{t}=Fy,

(1.3)

where

\displaystyle G=\mathrm{i}(zj+jV),\qquad F=-\mathrm{i}\big(z^{2}j+zjV-\big(\mathrm{i}V_{x}-jV^{2}\big)/2\big),

(1.4)

\displaystyle j=\left[\begin{matrix}I_{m_{1}}&0\\

matrix function.
We will consider dNLS equation on the semi-strip

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}=\{(x,\,t)\colon 0\leq x<\infty,\,0\leq t<a\},

(1.6)

\displaystyle y_{x}=Gy=\mathrm{i}(zj+jV)y

(1.7)

) a well-known self-adjoint Dirac system, also called AKNS or Zakharov–Shabat system.
Without changes in notation, we speak about usual derivatives inside domains
and about left or right
(which should be clear from the context) derivatives

{\mathcal{D}}

We should mention that, in the usual PDE setting, solutions are often considered in open domains

Since

Another equation that we study in this paper is the nonlinear optics (or

\displaystyle\left[D,\frac{\partial\varrho}{\partial t}\right]-\left[\widehat{D},\frac{\partial\varrho}{\partial x}\right]=[D,\varrho][\widehat{D},\varrho]-[\widehat{D},\varrho][D,\varrho],\qquad\varrho=\varrho^{*},

\displaystyle D=\operatorname{diag}\{d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{m}\},\qquad d_{1}>d_{2}>\cdots>d_{m}>0,\qquad[D,\varrho]:=D\varrho-\varrho D,

2.1
Dirac system and dNLS

We denote by

the fundamental solution of system (

\displaystyle u(0,z)=I_{m},\qquad m=m_{1}+m_{2}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let Dirac system (
1.7
) on
[0,\infty)

be given and assume that
V
is locally summable.
Then the Weyl function
\varphi
is an
m_{2}\times m_{1}
holomorphic matrix function, which satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\begin{bmatrix}I_{m_{1}}&\varphi(z)^{*}\end{bmatrix}u(x,z)^{*}u(x,z)\begin{bmatrix}I_{m_{1}}\\
\varphi(z)\end{bmatrix}dx<\infty.

(2.1)

The following proposition is proved in
[
20
]
(see also
[
48
, Section 2.2]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

The Weyl function always exists and it is unique.

In order to construct the Weyl function, we introduce a class of
m\times m_{1}
matrix functions

{\mathcal{P}}(z)
, which are an immediate analog of the classical pairs
of parameter matrix functions. Namely, the matrix functions

{\mathcal{P}}(z)
are meromorphic in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and satisfy
(excluding, possibly, a discrete set of points)
the following relations

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}(z)^{*}{\mathcal{P}}(z)>0,\qquad{\mathcal{P}}(z)^{*}j{\mathcal{P}}(z)\geq 0,\qquad z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}.

(2.2)

It is said that
{\mathcal{P}}(z)
are nonsingular (i.e., the first inequality in (
2.2
) holds)
and with property-
j
(i.e., the second inequality in (
2.2
) is valid).
Relations (
2.2
) imply (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
) that

\displaystyle\det\big(\begin{bmatrix}I_{m_{1}}&0\end{bmatrix}u(x,z)^{-1}{\mathcal{P}}(z)\big)\not=0.

Definition 2.3
.

The set
{\mathcal{N}}(x,z)
of Möbius transformations is the set of values
(
at the fixed

,
z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+})

of matrix functions

\displaystyle\varphi(x,z,{\mathcal{P}})=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{m_{2}}\end{bmatrix}u(x,z)^{-1}{\mathcal{P}}(z)\big(\begin{bmatrix}I_{m_{1}}&0\end{bmatrix}u(x,z)^{-1}{\mathcal{P}}(z)\big)^{-1},

where
{\mathcal{P}}(z)
are nonsingular matrix functions
with property-
j
.

Remark 2.4
.

It was shown in
[
20
]
that a family

in the form

2.2
Auxiliary linear systems for nonlinear optics equation

The nonlinear optics (
N
-wave) equation (
1.8
) is the compatibility condition
of the systems (
1.3
), where

\displaystyle G(x,t,z)=\mathrm{i}zD-\zeta(x,t),\qquad F(x,t,z)=\mathrm{i}z\widehat{D}-\widehat{\zeta}(x,t);

\displaystyle\zeta=[D,\varrho],\qquad\widehat{\zeta}=[\widehat{D},\varrho];

(2.9)

see
[
58
]
for the case
N=3
and
[
1
]
for
N>3
. We shall need
some preliminary results on the Weyl theory of the auxiliary system
y_{x}=Gy

from
[
48
, Chapter 4]
(see also
[
44
]
). The normalized fundamental solution

of such a system is defined by the formula

\displaystyle w_{x}(x,z)=\big(\mathrm{i}zD-\zeta(x)\big)w(x,z),\qquad w(0,z)=I_{m},\qquad\zeta=-\zeta^{*}.

(2.10)

Here and later we assume that
D
is a fixed matrix satisfying (

). We consider

2.10
) with locally bounded potentials
\zeta
, that is, potentials satisfying (for each

)
the inequality

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<x<l}\bigl\|\zeta(x)\bigr\|<\infty.

(2.11)

Definition 2.9
.

A generalized Weyl function
(

)
of system
(
2.10
), where
\zeta
is locally bounded, is an
m\times m
matrix function
\varphi
such that for some
M>0
it is analytic in the domain

{\mathbb{C}}^{-}_{M}=\{z\colon\Im(z)<-M\}
and the

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\leq l,\,\Im(z)<-M}\bigl\|w(x,z)\varphi(z)\exp\{-\mathrm{i}zxD\}\bigr\|<\infty

(2.12)

holds for each

l<\infty
.

Remark 2.10
.

We note that the Weyl function of the system (
2.10
) is defined
\big(
for
\zeta
bounded on
[0,\,\infty)\big)

by the analog (
2.17
) of the inequality (
2.1
) and by normalization conditions (
2.18
). If
\zeta
is bounded on
[0,\,\infty)
, this Weyl function
coincides with the normalized GW-function.

(
See the discussion after formula (
2.18
) and Definition

of the GW-function above.
)
The fact that the Weyl function satisfies (

) explains the term “generalized Weyl function”
(
or “GW-function”
)

The inverse spectral problem (ISpP) for system

)
a potential

\zeta(x)=-\zeta^{*}(x)

such that (
2.12
) is valid and the diagonal entries
\zeta_{kk}
of

\zeta_{kk}\equiv 0)
.

Notation 2.11
.

The notation

stands for
an operator mapping the pair

and
\varphi

into the corresponding potential
\zeta

(
i.e.,
{\mathfrak{M}}(D,\varphi)=\zeta)
. In other words,
{\mathfrak{M}}(D,\varphi)
stands for a solution of the ISpP.

The following theorem (i.e.,
[
48
, Theorem 4.8]
) is valid.

Theorem 2.12
.

For any
matrix function

\varphi(z)
which is
analytic and bounded in

and has the

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl(\varphi(z)-I_{m}\bigr)^{*}\bigl(\varphi(z)-I_{m}\bigr)d\xi<\infty,\qquad z=\xi+\mathrm{i}\eta,\qquad\eta<-M,

(2.13)

there is at most one solution of the ISpP.

Our next corollary for systems on

is immediate from the proof (see
[

, pp. 108–109]
) of Theorem

.

Corollary 2.13
.

Assume that (

) is valid and let relations (

) and (

. Then,

is uniquely defined on
(0,l)
.

Remark 2.14
.

Under somewhat stronger
(
than in (

)
)

\phi_{0}
and some

\displaystyle\sup_{\Im(z)<-M}\big\|\,z(\varphi(z)-I_{m})\,\big\|<\infty,\quad\det\,\varphi(z)\not=0\quad{\mathrm{for}}\quad\Im(z)<-M,

(2.14)

\displaystyle(\xi+\mathrm{i}\eta)\big(\varphi(\xi+\mathrm{i}\eta)-I_{m}-\phi_{0}/(\xi+\mathrm{i}\eta)\big)\in L_{m\times m}^{2}(-\infty,\infty)\quad\text{for all f\/ixed}\ \eta<-M,

then

is constructed in

The situation becomes simpler when

is uniformly bounded on

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{0<x<\infty}\bigl\|\zeta(x)\bigr\|\leq M_{0}.

(2.16)

We recall that a Weyl function of system (

) is introduced in another way

is an analytic

satisfying for certain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\{\mathrm{i}\overline{z}xD\}\varphi(z)^{*}w(x,z)^{*}w(x,z)\varphi(z)\exp\bigl\{{-}(\mathrm{i}zD+rI_{m})x\bigr\}dx<\infty,

and the normalization conditions on the entries

:

\displaystyle\varphi_{ij}(z)\equiv 1\qquad\mbox{for}\quad i=j,\qquad\varphi_{ij}(z)\equiv 0\qquad\mbox{for}\quad i>j.

(2.18)

When (

satisfying normalization conditions (

\displaystyle J_{k}:=\left[\begin{matrix}I_{k}&0\\
0&-I_{m-k}\end{matrix}\right],\qquad 1\leq k<m.

Now, the Weyl function is constructed

First, for each

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}(z)^{*}{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}(z)>0,\qquad{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}(z)^{*}J_{k}{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}(z)\leq 0,

excluding, possibly, isolated points. These

\displaystyle\psi_{k}(x,z)=\begin{bmatrix}I_{k}&0\end{bmatrix}w(x,z)^{-1}{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}(z)\left(\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{m-k}\end{bmatrix}w(x,z)^{-1}{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}(z)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{k}(z)^{*}&I_{m-k}\end{bmatrix}J_{k}\left[\begin{matrix}\psi_{k}(z)\\

The set of matrices
\psi_{k}(x,z)

and

In this way we recover the

\displaystyle\left\{\varphi_{i,k+1}(z)\right\}_{i=1}^{k}=\breve{\psi}_{k}(z)\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\\
\ldots\\
0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\Im(z)<-M

for any

. There is also an inverse transformation

\displaystyle\breve{\psi}_{k}^{\,}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}I_{k}&0\end{bmatrix}\varphi(z)\left[\begin{matrix}0\\

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose that the
m\times m
matrix function
\rho(x)
is absolutely continuous on

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{x}=\mathrm{i}z[D,{\mathcal{M}}]-\zeta{\mathcal{M}},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},\qquad\zeta=[D,\rho],

We note that the fact that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(z)\{\delta_{i,m-k+1}\}_{i=1}^{m}:=\begin{bmatrix}0\\

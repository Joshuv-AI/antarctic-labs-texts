Abstract
In our pursuit of finding a zero for a monotone and Lipschitz continuous operator M : ℝ n → ℝ n M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n} amidst noisy evaluations, we explore an associated differential equation within a stochastic framework, incorporating a correction term. We present a result establishing the existence and uniqueness of solutions for the stochastic differential equations under examination. Additionally, assuming that the diffusion term is square-integrable, we demonstrate the almost sure convergence of the trajectory process X ⁡ ( t ) X(t) to a zero of M M and of ‖ M ⁡ ( X ⁡ ( t ) ) ‖ \|M(X(t))\| to 0 0 as t → + ∞ t\rightarrow+\infty . Furthermore, we provide ergodic upper bounds and ergodic convergence rates in expectation for ‖ M ⁡ ( X ⁡ ( t ) ) ‖ 2 \|M(X(t))\|^{2} and ⟨ M ( X ( t ) , X ( t ) − x ∗ ⟩ \langle M(X(t),X(t)-x^{*}\rangle , where x ∗ x^{*} is an arbitrary zero of the monotone operator. Subsequently, we apply these findings to a minimax problem. Finally, we analyze two temporal discretizations of the continuous-time models, resulting in stochastic variants of the Optimistic Gradient Descent Ascent and Extragradient methods, respectively, and assess their convergence properties.

1
Introduction

For a monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous operator
M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we examine the equation

\displaystyle M(x)=0.

(1)

We assume that the set of solutions of (
1
), denoted by
{\rm zer}M:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M(x)=0\}
, is nonempty. The operator
M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be monotone if
\langle M(x)-M(y),x-y\rangle\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For
M:=\nabla f
, where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex and differentiable function with a Lipschitz continuous gradient, solving equation (
1
) is equivalent to finding the global solutions of the optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x).

(2)

On the other hand, if we define

\displaystyle M(x,y):=(\nabla_{x}\Phi(x,y),-\nabla_{y}\Phi(x,y)),

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex-concave function with Lipschitz continuous gradient, solving equation (
1
) is equivalent to finding the saddle points of the minimax problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{q}}\Phi(x,y).

(3)

Inspired by the Newton and the Levenberg-Marquardt methods, Attouch and Svaiter introduced in
[
3
]
the following continuous time equation associated to (
1
) in the setting of a real Hilbert space

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\dot{x}(t)+\mu(t)\frac{d}{dt}M(x(t))+\mu(t)M(x(t))=0\quad\forall t>0,\\
&x(0)=x_{0},\end{cases}

(4)

where
\mu:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
was assumed to be bounded, continuous and absolutely continuous on bounded intervals. Besides proving the existence and uniqueness of strong global solutions, they showed, under suitable assumptions on the parameter function, that
x(t)
converges weakly to a zero of
M
as well as
M(x(t))\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

In many instances, the evaluation of the operator is subject to noise, which motivates us to transfer (
4
) to a stochastic setting. To achieve this, we consider the formal expression of a continuous time system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&dX(t)+\mu(t)dM(X(t))=-\gamma(t)M(X(t))dt+\sigma(t,X(t))dW(t)\quad\forall t>0,\\
&X(0)=X_{0},\end{cases}

(5)

which can consistently with Itô’s chain rule be understood as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{cases}&d(X(t)+\mu(t)M(X(t)))=-(\gamma(t)-\dot{\mu}(t))M(X(t))dt+\sigma(t,X(t))dW(t)\quad\forall t>0,\\
&X(0)=X_{0},\end{cases}\end{split}

(SDE-M)

defined over a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
with the diffusion term
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
being matrix-valued and measurable,
W
a
m
-dimensional Brownian motion, and
X(\cdot)
as well as
M(X(\cdot))
are stochastic Itô processes with the same
m
-dimensional Brownian motion
W
. The parameter functions
\mu:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
and
\gamma:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
are assumed to be continuous differentiable and, respectively, integrable. For the diffusion term we assume

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\exists c_{\sigma}>0\ \mbox{such that}\ \|\sigma(t,x^{\prime})-\sigma(t,x)\|_{F}\leq c_{\sigma}\|x^{\prime}-x\|\quad\forall t>0,\\
&\sigma_{\infty}(t):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|\sigma(t,x)\|_{F}\leq\sigma_{*}\quad\forall t>0,\end{cases}

(6)

where
\|\cdot\|_{F}
denotes the Frobenius norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
.

Then, (
SDE-M
) can be rewritten as the system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&dM(X(t))=Y(t)dt+\sigma_{M}(t)d{W}(t),\\
&dX(t)=(-\mu(t)Y(t)-\gamma(t)M(X(t)))dt+\sigma_{X}(t)d{W}(t)\quad\forall t>0,\\
&X(0)=X_{0},\end{cases}

where

\sigma(t,X(t))=\sigma_{X}(t)+\mu(t)\sigma_{M}(t)\quad\forall t\geq 0

and
\sigma_{X},\sigma_{M}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
are measurable.

Our goal is to explore the existence and uniqueness of trajectory solutions for (
SDE-M
), along with their long-term behavior in terms of both almost sure convergence and convergence rates. Furthermore, we demonstrate how these properties transfer to convergence rates for stochastic numerical methods obtained through temporal discretizations of the stochastic differential equation.

1.1
Related works

The simplest continuous time approach associated to the optimization problem (
2
), for a convex and differentiable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
L_{\nabla f}
-Lipschitz continuous gradient, is the gradient flow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\dot{x}(t)=-\nabla f(x(t))\quad\forall t>0,\\
&x(0)=x_{0}.\end{cases}

Its trajectory solution
x(t)
converges to a global minimizer of
f
, while
f(x(t))
converges to the minimal function value
\min f
of
f
with a convergence rate of
o(\frac{1}{t})
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Its discrete time counterpart, the gradient method,

\displaystyle x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\gamma\nabla f(x^{k})\quad\forall k\geq 0,

where
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{1}{L_{\nabla f}}\right]
, shares the same convergence properties. Specifically, the sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
converges to a minimizer of
f
, and
f(x_{k})-\min f=o(\frac{1}{k})
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
.

In
[
25
]

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&dX(t)=-\nabla f(X(t))dt+\sigma(t,X(t))dW(t)\quad\forall t>0,\\
&X(0)=X_{0},\end{cases}

(7)

defined over a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
with
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
being a matrix-valued measurable diffusion term fulfilling (
6
), and
W
a
m
-dimensional Brownian motion. They derived for

and
\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}f(X(s))ds-\min f
upper bounds in expectation of
{\cal O}(\frac{1}{t})+{\cal O}(\sigma^{2}_{*})
. Additionally, assuming that
\sigma_{\infty}
is square-integrable, they demonstrated the almost sure convergence of the trajectory process to a minimizer of
f
and of
f(X(t))
to
\min f
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, and that the two above quantities exhibit convergence rates of
{\cal O}(\frac{1}{t})
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

There is a strong link between this stochastic differential equation and the stochastic gradient descent algorithm with constant stepsize (
[
9
]
,
[
19
]
,
[

[

)

\displaystyle x^{k+1}:=x^{k}-\gamma\nabla f(x^{k},\xi^{k})\quad\forall k\geq 0,

where

is an increasing family of
\sigma
-fields,

is
{\cal F}_{0}
-measurable,

is such that

, with the properties that
\mathbb{E}(\nabla f(x,\xi^{k+1})|{\cal F}_{k})=\nabla f(x)

Recently, in
[

, (

Furthermore, in

As for the monotone equation (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\dot{x}(t)=-M(x(t))\quad\forall t>0,\\

The significance of the correction term in solving equations governed by monotone and

\displaystyle x^{k+1}:=x^{k}-\gamma M(x^{k})\quad\forall k\geq 0,

On the other hand, for the Optimistic Gradient Descent Ascent method (

[

,
[

]

[

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{cases}&y^{k}:=x^{k}-\gamma M(x^{k})\\

2
Continuous time system: existence, uniqueness and almost sure convergence

This section is dedicated to the study of (
SDE-M
) in the setting

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{cases}&d(X(t)+\mu(t)M(X(t)))=-(\gamma(t)-\dot{\mu}(t))M(X(t))dt+\sigma(t,X(t))dW(t)\quad\forall t>0,\\
&X(0)=X_{0}.\end{cases}\end{split}

Throughout this section, we will assume that the parameter functions satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle 0<\mu_{\text{low}}\leq\mu(t)\leq\mu_{\text{up}}:=\mu(0)\ \mbox{and}\ 0<\gamma_{\text{low}}\leq\gamma(t)\leq\gamma_{\text{up}}\quad\forall t\geq 0,\ \mbox{and}\ \mu\ \mbox{is nonincreasing on}\ [0,+\infty).

(12)

Throughout the paper,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
is a filtered probability space. The expectation of a random variable
\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\mathbb{E}(\xi):=\int_{\Omega}\xi(\omega)d\mathbb{P}(\omega)
. An event

\mathbb{P}(E)=1
.

An

-valued stochastic process is a function

X(\omega,\cdot)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}

\omega\in\Omega

. Two stochastic processes

, are said to be equivalent if
X(t)=Y(t)
almost surely for all
t\in[0,T]
. This allows the definition of the equivalence relation

which associates equivalent stochastic processes to the same class.

Next, we will introduce some notions that will characterize the space where the trajectory solution of (
SDE-M
) lies.

Definition 2.1
.

(i) A stochastic process

t\geq 0
, the mapping

\displaystyle\Omega\times[0,t]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad(\omega,s)\mapsto X(\omega,s)

\mathcal{F}_{t}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,t])

denotes the product
\sigma

\mathcal{B}
is the Borel

is called
\mathcal{F}_{t}

X(\cdot,t)
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable for every
t\geq 0
.

For

, we define the quotient space as

\displaystyle S_{n}^{0}[0,T]:=\left\{X:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{$X$ is a progressively measurable continuous stochastic process}\right\}\Big/\mathcal{R},

and set
S_{n}^{0}:=\bigcap_{T\geq 0}S_{n}^{0}[0,T]
.

(ii) For

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\nu}[0,T]:=\left\{X\in S_{n}^{0}[0,T]:\quad\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X(t)\|^{\nu}\right)<+\infty\right\}.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let the diffusion term
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle X(t)=(\Id+\mu(t)M)^{-1}(\underbrace{X(t)+\mu(t)M(X(t))}_{=:Z(t)})=:J_{\mu(t)M}(Z(t))\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

where

denotes the resolvent of

with parameter

\displaystyle M_{\mu(t)}:=\frac{1}{\mu(t)}(\Id-J_{\mu(t)M}),

\displaystyle dZ(t)=d(X(t)+\mu(t)M(X(t)))=(-\gamma(t)+\dot{\mu}(t))M_{\mu(t)}(Z(t))dt+\sigma(t,J_{\mu(t)M}(Z(t)))dW(t)\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(13)

Now setting

\displaystyle F(t,z):=(\dot{\mu}(t)-\gamma(t))M_{\mu(t)}(z),\quad G(t,z):=\sigma(t,J_{\mu(t)M}(z))\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \forall t\in[0,T],

yields for all

and all

\displaystyle\|F(t,x)-F(t,y)\|+\|G(t,x)-G(t,y)\|_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ |\gamma(t)-\dot{\mu}(t)|\|M_{\mu(t)}(x)-M_{\mu(t)}(y)\|+\|\sigma(t,J_{\mu(t)M}(x))-\sigma(t,J_{\mu(t)M}(y))\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (\gamma_{\text{up}}-\dot{\mu}_{\text{low},T})\frac{1}{\mu(t)}\|x-y\|+c_{\sigma}\|J_{\mu(t)M}(x)-J_{\mu(t)M}(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \left((\gamma_{\text{up}}-\dot{\mu}_{\text{low},T})\frac{1}{\mu_{\text{low}}}+c_{\sigma}\right)\|x-y\|,

where
\dot{\mu}_{\text{low},T}:=\inf\{\dot{\mu}(t):t\in[0,T]\}\in\mathbb{R}
, since the continuous mapping
\dot{\mu}

[0,T]

This allows us to use Theorem

We define

\displaystyle Z_{T}(t):=X_{T}(t)+\mu(t)M(X_{T}(t))\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Since now
M(X_{T}(t))=M_{\mu(t)}(Z_{T}(t))
and
Z_{T}
is a solution of (

), it follows

\displaystyle d(X_{T}(t)+\mu(t)M(X_{T}(t)))

\displaystyle=(-\gamma(t)+\dot{\mu}(t))M_{\mu(t)}(Z_{T}(t))dt+\sigma(t,J_{\mu(t)M}(Z_{T}(t)))dW(t)

\displaystyle=(-\gamma(t)+\dot{\mu}(t))M(X_{T}(t))dt+\sigma(t,X_{T}(t))dW(t)\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

and thus,
X_{T}
is indeed a solution of (
SDE-M
) on
[0,T]
.

Now, recall that
J_{\mu(t)M}
is
1
-Lipschitz continuous and observe that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
\mu,\lambda>0
it holds

\displaystyle\|J_{\lambda M}(x)-J_{\mu M}(x)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|J_{\mu M}\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}x+\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)J_{\lambda M}(x)\right)-J_{\mu M}(x)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(1-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)(x-J_{\lambda M}(x))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda-\mu|\|M_{\lambda}x\|.

This will allow us to establish the continuity of
X_{T}
. Indeed, for all
t,s\in[0,T]
it holds

\displaystyle\|X_{T}(t)-X_{T}(s)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|J_{\mu(t)M}(Z_{T}(t))-J_{\mu(s)M}(Z_{T}(t))\|+\|J_{\mu(s)M}(Z_{T}(t))-J_{\mu(s)M}(Z_{T}(s))\|

\displaystyle\leq|\mu(t)-\mu(s)|\|M_{\mu(t)}(Z_{T}(t))\|+\|Z_{T}(t)-Z_{T}(s)\|,

and, since
Z_{T}
is continuous by Theorem
A.1
and
\mu
is continuous by assumption, the continuity of
X_{T}
follows.

Next, consider

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|X_{T}(t)\|^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|J_{\mu(t)M}(Z_{T}(t))-0\|^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|J_{\mu(t)M}(Z_{T}(t))-J_{\mu(t)M}(\mu(t)M(0))\|^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Z_{T}(t)-\mu(t)M(0)\|^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(2^{\nu}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|Z_{T}(t)\|+\frac{\mu_{\text{up}}}{2}\|M(0)\|\right)^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\nu-1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|Z_{T}(t)\|^{\nu}\right)+\mu_{\text{up}}^{\nu}\|M(0)\|^{\nu}\right)<+\infty.

Finally, in order to prove the progressive measurability of
X
, consider the mapping

\displaystyle\Omega\times[0,t]\rightarrow[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad(\omega,s)\mapsto(s,Z_{T}(\omega,s)).

Now, the preimage of an element of the generating set
I\times O
, where
I\subseteq[0,t]
is an interval and
O\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
an element of the generator of the Borel-
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is given by

\displaystyle Z_{T}^{-1}(I\times O)\cap I\times\Omega,

which is measurable, due to the progressive measurability of
Z_{T}
and the fact that a finite intersection of measurable sets is again measurable. This proves the progressive measurability of
X_{T}
.

Since
X_{T}
is uniquely defined on any
[0,T]
, where
T>0
, for
0<T_{1}<T_{2}
, we have
X_{T_{2}}|_{[0,T_{1}]}=X_{T_{1}}
. Thus, there exists a unique solution
X\in S_{n}^{\nu}
of (
SDE-M
).
∎

The following result establishes the almost sure convergence of
X(t)
to a
{\rm zer}M
-valued random variable as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. The notion of a martingale will play an important role in its proof.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a real-valued stochastic process such that
\mathbb{E}(|X(t)|)<+\infty
for all
t\geq 0
.

(i)

The
\sigma
-algebra generated by the random variables
X(s)
for
0\leq s\leq t
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(t):=\mathcal{U}(X(s)|0\leq s\leq t),

is called the
history
of the stochastic process
X
until (and including) time
t
.

(ii)

If

\displaystyle X(s)=\mathbb{E}(X(t)|\mathcal{U}(s))\quad\text{a.s.}\quad\forall t\geq s\geq 0,

then
X
is called a
martingale
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
{\rm zer}M\neq\emptyset
, the diffusion term
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
satisfy assumption (
6
), and
X
be the unique trajectory process of (
SDE-M
). If, in addition to (
12
),
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\sigma_{\infty}(s)^{2}ds<+\infty
, then the following statements are true:

(i)

\sup_{t\geq 0}{\mathbb{E}(\|X(t)\|^{2})}<+\infty
;

(ii)

it holds
\sup_{t\geq 0}{\|X(t)\|}<+\infty
a.s.

If, in addition,
-\infty<\dot{\mu}_{\text{up}}\leq\dot{\mu}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
and
L\mu_{\text{up}}<1
, then:

(iii)

almost surely
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|M(X(t))\|}=0
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|X(t)-x^{*}\|
exists and is finite for all
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
;

(iv)

there exists a
{\rm zer}M
-valued random variable
x^{*}
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}X(t)=x^{*}
a.s.

Proof.

In order to perform the proof, we rewrite (
SDE-M
) as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&dM(X(t))=Y(t)dt+\sigma_{M}(t)d{W}(t),\\
&dX(t)=(-\mu(t)Y(t)-\gamma(t)M(X(t)))dt+\sigma_{X}(t)d{W}(t)\quad\forall t>0,\\
&X(0)=X_{0},\end{cases}

where
\sigma(t,X(t))=\sigma_{X}(t)+\mu(t)\sigma_{M}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. Let
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
, and define the anchor function
\phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\phi(t,x,z):=\mu(t)\langle z,x-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\mu(t)^{2}}{2}\|z\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|x+\mu(t)z-x^{*}\|^{2}.

The proposed anchor function takes an additional argument – this will be taken equal to
M(X(t))
over the course of the proof. For all
(t,x,z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\phi(t,x,z)=\dot{\mu}(t)\langle z,x-x^{*}\rangle+\mu(t)\dot{\mu}(t)\|z\|^{2}

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x,z)=\mu(t)z+x-x^{*}

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}\phi(t,x,z)=\mu(t)(x-x^{*})+\mu(t)^{2}z

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}^{2}\phi(t,x,z)=I

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\nabla_{z}\phi(t,x,z)=\nabla_{z}\nabla_{x}\phi(t,x,z)=\mu(t)I

\displaystyle\nabla_{z}^{2}\phi(t,x,z)=\mu(t)^{2}I.

Observe that the remaining second partial derivatives of
\phi
are identically
0
. Hence, the Itô formula yields for all
t\geq 0

\displaystyle\phi(t,X(t),M(X(t)))

\displaystyle=\phi(0,X(0),M(X(0)))

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\dot{\mu}(s)\langle M(X(s)),X(s)-x^{*}\rangle+\mu(s)\dot{\mu}(s)\|M(X(s))\|^{2}\right)ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\big\langle(\mu(s)M(X(s))+X(s)-x^{*}),\left(-\mu(s)Y(s)-\gamma(s)M(X(s))\right)\big\rangle ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\sigma_{X}^{\top}(s)(\mu(s)M(X(s))+X(s)-x^{*}),dW(s)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle(\mu(s)(X(s)-x^{*})+\mu(s)^{2}M(X(s))),Y(s)\right\rangle ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\sigma_{M}^{\top}(s)(\mu(s)(X(s)-x^{*})+\mu(s)^{2}M(X(s))),dW(s)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\text{tr}(\sigma_{X}^{\top}(s)\sigma_{X}(s))+2\mu(s)\text{tr}(\sigma_{M}^{\top}(s)\sigma_{X}(s))+\mu(s)^{2}\text{tr}(\sigma_{M}^{\top}(s)\sigma_{M}(s))ds.

We observe for all
s\geq 0

\displaystyle\sigma_{X}^{\top}(s)\sigma_{X}(s)+2\mu(s)\sigma_{M}^{\top}(s)\sigma_{X}(s)+\mu(s)^{2}(\sigma_{M}^{\top}(s)\sigma_{M}(s))=\sigma^{\top}(s,X(s))\sigma(s,X(s)),

and denote this term by
\Sigma(s,X(s))
for improved readability. Further, it holds for all
s\geq 0

\displaystyle\ \langle\sigma_{X}^{\top}(s)(\mu(s)M(X(s))+X(s)-x^{*})+\sigma_{M}^{\top}(s)(\mu(s)^{2}M(X(s))+\mu(s)(X(s)-x^{*})),d{W}(s)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \langle(\sigma_{X}(s)+\mu(s)\sigma_{M}(s))^{\top}(\mu(s)M(X(s))+X(s)-x^{*}),d{W}(s)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \langle\sigma^{\top}(s,X(s))(\mu(s)M(X(s))+X(s)-x^{*}),d{W}(s)\rangle.

Then, performing some elementary computations, we get for all
t\geq 0

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi(t,X(t),M(X(t)))&=\underbrace{\mu(0)\langle M(X_{0}),X_{0}-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|X_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\mu(0)^{2}}{2}\|M(X_{0})\|^{2}}_{=:\xi}\\
&\quad-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}(\gamma(s)-\dot{\mu}(s))\left(\langle M(X(s)),X(s)-x^{*}\rangle+\mu(s)\|M(X(s))\|^{2}\right)ds}_{=:U(t)}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\langle\sigma^{\top}(s,X(s))(\mu(s)M(X(s))+X(s)-x^{*}),d{W}(s)\rangle}_{=:N(t)}\\
&\quad+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\text{tr}(\Sigma(s,X(s)))ds}_{=:A(t)},\end{split}

(14)

in other words

\displaystyle\phi(t,X(t),M(X(t)))=\xi-U(t)+N(t)+A(t)\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(i) As
X\in S_{n}^{2}
, it holds for all
T>0
that

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}{\|\sigma^{\top}(s,X(s))(X(s)-x^{*}+\mu(s)M(X(s)))\|^{2}ds}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{T}2(\|\sigma^{\top}(s,X(s))(X(s)-x^{*})\|^{2}+\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|\sigma^{\top}(s,X(s))(M(X(s))-M(x^{*}))\|^{2})ds\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\Bigg(2\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\|X(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}{\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(s)ds}+\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\|M(X(t))-M(x^{*})\|^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}{\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(s)ds}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2(1+\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}L^{2})\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}{\|X(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}}\right)\int_{0}^{T}{\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(s)ds}<+\infty.

By Proposition
A.2
, this means that
N
is a square-integrable continuous martingale, and
\mathbb{E}(N(t))=0
for all
t\geq 0
. Taking the expectation of (
14
) and using that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&0\leq\text{tr}(\Sigma(s,X(s))\leq\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(s),\quad\langle M(X(s)),X(s)-x^{*}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|M(X(s))\|^{2}\geq 0\quad\forall s\geq 0,\end{split}

yields for all
t\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|X(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}(\phi(t,X(t)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\|X_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\mu_{\text{up}}\langle M(X_{0}),X_{0}-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}}{2}\|M(X_{0})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -\underbrace{\mathbb{E}(U(t))}_{\geq 0}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}(N(t))}_{=0}+\mathbb{E}(A(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\|X_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\mu(0)\langle M(X_{0}),X_{0}-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{\mu(0)^{2}}{2}\|M(X_{0})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}{\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(s)ds}<+\infty.

Finally, by taking the supremum over
t\geq 0
, we establish the validity of the first claim.

(ii)
A
and
U
are continuous adapted increasing processes with
A(0)=U(0)=0
a.s., while
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}A(t)

<+\infty
holds due to the integrability condition for the diffusion term. Thus, we can use Theorem
A.3
to obtain almost surely that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\phi(t,X(t),M(X(t)))
exists and is finite, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(\gamma(s)-\dot{\mu}(s))\left(\langle M(X(s)),X(s)-x^{*}\rangle+\mu(s)\|M(X(s))\|^{2}\right)ds<+\infty.\end{split}

(15)

In other words, for all
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
there exists
\Omega_{x^{*}}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{x^{*}})=1
and for all
\omega\in\Omega_{x^{*}}
it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\mu(t)\langle M(X(\omega,t)),X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\mu(t)^{2}}{2}\|M(X(\omega,t))\|^{2}\quad\text{exists and is finite}.

(16)

This implies that for all
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
there exists
\Omega_{x^{*}}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{x^{*}})=1
and
\|X(\omega,\cdot)-x^{*}\|
remains bounded for all
\omega\in\Omega_{x^{*}}
. This proves that
\sup_{t\geq 0}{\|X(t)\|}<+\infty
a.s.

(iii) Using the monotonicity of
M
and the fact that
\mu
and
\gamma
are bounded from below by a positive constant, by (
15
), it follows
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|M(X(\omega,s))\|^{2}ds<+\infty
a.s. We denote by
\Omega_{I}
the set of probability one for which it holds
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|M(X(\omega,s))\|^{2}ds<+\infty
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{I}
.

Let
R(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X(s))dW(s)
. This is a continuous martingale with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, which, according to the Itô isometry property, satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\|R(t)\|^{2})=\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma(s,X(s))\|_{F}^{2}ds\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(s)ds\right)<+\infty\quad\forall t\geq 0.

By Theorem
A.5
, there exists a
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued random variable
R_{\infty}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\infty}
, the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\cup_{t\geq 0}{\cal F}_{t}
, which satisfies
\mathbb{E}(\|R_{\infty}\|^{2})<+\infty
, and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}R(\omega,t)=R_{\infty}(\omega)\quad\text{for every}\quad\omega\in\Omega_{R},

where
\Omega_{R}\in\mathcal{F}
is such that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{R})=1
.

Then
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R})=1
and, as we will see below,
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|M(X(\omega,t))\|}=0
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}
. Indeed, for all
\omega\in\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}
, since
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|M(X(\omega,s))\|^{2}ds<+\infty
, it holds
\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|M(X(\omega,t))\|}=0
. In addition, for all
\omega\in\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}
,
\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|M(X(\omega,t))\|}=0
.

Suppose by contradiction that there exists
\omega_{0}\in\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}
such that
\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|}>0
. Then, by Lemma
A.4
, there exist
\delta>0
satisfying

\displaystyle 0=\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|<\delta<\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|},

and a sequence
(t_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}t_{k}=+\infty,\quad\|M(X(\omega,t_{k}))\|>\delta,\quad\text{and}\quad t_{k+1}-t_{k}>1\quad\forall k\geq 0.

Let
\alpha>0
such that

and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\frac{\varepsilon}{2(|\dot{\mu}_{\text{low}}|^{2}+\gamma_{\text{up}}^{2})}<1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\frac{\varepsilon L^{2}}{1-(1+\alpha)L^{2}\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}}<\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}.

Then
\left[t_{k},t_{k}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2(|\dot{\mu}_{\text{low}}|^{2}+\gamma_{\text{up}}^{2})}\right]
are disjoint intervals for any two distinct

.

Also, by the convergence property of

and the fact that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|M(X(\omega_{0},s))\|ds<+\infty
, there exists

such that for every

,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq t_{k}}{\|R(\omega_{0},t)-R(\omega_{0},t_{k})\|^{2}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{t_{k}}^{+\infty}\|M(X(\omega_{0},s))\|^{2}ds<1.

For all
k\geq k_{0}
and all

, it holds

\displaystyle\ \|X(\omega_{0},t)-X(\omega_{0},t_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|X(\omega_{0},t)+\mu(t)M(X(\omega_{0},t))-X(t_{k})-\mu(t_{k})M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))+\mu(t_{k})M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-\mu(t)M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (1+\alpha)\|\mu(t_{k})(M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-M(X(\omega_{0},t)))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\left\|\int_{t_{k}}^{t}(\dot{\mu}(s)-\gamma(s))M(X(\omega_{0},s))ds+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\sigma(s,X(\omega_{0},s))dW(s)+(\mu(t_{k})-\mu(t))M(X(\omega_{0},t))\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ (1+\alpha)\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\left(\left\|\int_{t_{k}}^{t}(\dot{\mu}(s)-\gamma(s))M(X(\omega_{0},s))ds\right\|^{2}+\left\|\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\sigma(s,X(\omega_{0},s))dW(s)\right\|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+|\mu(t_{k})-\mu(t)|^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (1+\alpha)\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\left(2(|\dot{\mu}_{\text{low}}|^{2}+|\gamma_{\text{up}}|^{2})(t-t_{k})\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\|M(X(\omega_{0},s))\|^{2}ds+\left\|R(\omega_{0},t)-R(\omega_{0},t_{k})\right\|^{2}+\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+4\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (1+\alpha)\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\left(2(t-t_{k})(|\dot{\mu}_{\text{low}}|^{2}+\gamma_{\text{up}}^{2})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+4\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (1+\alpha)\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}+\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\left(\varepsilon+4\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}\right),

where the first inequality is the one between the geometric and the arithmetic mean, the second inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the third inequality follows from the Hölder inequality for integrals.

By making use of the Lipschitz continuity of
M
, it yields

\displaystyle\|M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (1+\alpha)L^{2}\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}+\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)L^{2}\left(\varepsilon+4\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}\right),

consequently,

\displaystyle\|M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))-M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}\leq\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\frac{L^{2}}{1-(1+\alpha)L^{2}\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}}\left(\varepsilon+4\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}\right).

Therefore, for all
k\geq k_{0}
and all

, from

\displaystyle\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))\|^{2}-\|M(X(\omega_{0},t)-M(X(\omega_{0},t_{k}))\|^{2},

we get

\displaystyle\left(1+\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\frac{4\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}L^{2}}{1-(1+\alpha)L^{2}\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}}\right)\|M(X(\omega_{0},t))\|^{2}\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}-\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\frac{\varepsilon L^{2}}{1-(1+\alpha)L^{2}\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\ \left(1+\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\frac{4\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}L^{2}}{1-(1+\alpha)L^{2}\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}}\right)\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|M(X(\omega_{0},s))\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2(|\dot{\mu}_{\text{low}}|^{2}+\gamma_{\text{up}}^{2})}}\|M(X(\omega_{0},s))\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\frac{\varepsilon}{2(|\dot{\mu}_{\text{low}}|^{2}+\gamma_{\text{up}}^{2})}\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}-\left(3+\frac{3}{\alpha}\right)\frac{\varepsilon L^{2}}{1-(1+\alpha)L^{2}\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}}\right)=+\infty,

which contradicts
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|M(X(\omega_{0},s))\|^{2}ds<+\infty
. This means that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}

\displaystyle 0=\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|M(X(\omega,t))\|=\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|M(X(\omega,t))\|=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|M(X(\omega,t))\|=0.

Then, for all
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
and all
\omega\in\Omega_{x^{*}}\cap\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle 0\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\langle M(X(\omega,t)),X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\rangle\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\underbrace{\|M(X(\omega,t))\|}_{\rightarrow 0}\underbrace{\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|}_{\text{is bounded}}=0.

Using (
16
) and that
\mu(t)\geq\mu_{\text{low}}>0
for all
t\geq 0
, it follows that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|
exists and is finite.

Next, we will employ an argument from
[
25
]
to demonstrate the existence of a subset of
\Omega
of probability one, which is independent of a previously chosen
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
, such that for all
\omega
in this subset and all
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
the limit
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|
exists and is finite.

Taking into account that
{\rm zer}M
is closed, by the separability of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
there exists a countable set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is dense in
{\rm zer}M
. Because
S
is countable,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{s\in S}{\Omega_{s}}\right)=1-\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{s\in S}\Omega_{s}^{c}\right)\geq 1-\sum_{s\in S}\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{s}^{c})=1.

Then
\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{s\in S}{\Omega_{s}}\cap\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}\right)=1
. Let
\omega\in\bigcap_{s\in S}{\Omega_{s}}\cap\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}
be fixed and choose
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
. Then there exists a sequence
(s^{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq S
such that
s^{k}\rightarrow x^{*}
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
. For every
k\geq 0
, since
\omega\in\Omega_{s^{k}}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|X(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|}\ \mbox{exists and is finite}.

Applying the triangle inequality, it follows

\displaystyle|\|X(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|-\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\||\leq\|s^{k}-x^{*}\|\quad\forall k\geq 0\ \forall t\geq 0.

This gives for all
k\geq 0

\displaystyle-\|s^{k}-x^{*}\|+\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|X(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|}\leq

\displaystyle\ \liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|}\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|X(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|}+\|s^{k}-x^{*}\|.

Letting
k\rightarrow+\infty
, we get

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|X(\omega,t)-s^{k}\|}\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, we recall that there exists
\Omega_{\text{cont}}\in{\cal F}
such that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{\text{cont}})=1
and
X(\omega,\cdot)
is continuous for every
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{cont}}
. Let
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{converge}}:=\bigcap_{s\in S}{\Omega_{s}}\cap\Omega_{I}\cap\Omega_{R}\cap\Omega_{\text{cont}}
. It holds
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{\text{converge}})=1
. For
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
, there exists
C(\omega)\in\mathbb{R}
and
T(\omega)\leq 0
such that
\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|\leq C(\omega)
for all
t\geq T(\omega)
. Because
X(\omega,\cdot)
is continuous, it holds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T(\omega)]}{\|X(\omega,t)\|}<+\infty.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\|X(\omega,t)\|\leq\max\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T(\omega)]}{\|X(\omega,t)\|},C(\omega)+\|x^{*}\|\right)<+\infty.

(iv) We will use Opial’s Lemma to prove the convergence of the trajectory process. We fix
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{converge}}
and recall that above we proved that for every
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
the limit
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|X(\omega,t)-x^{*}\|
exists. In addition,
X(\omega,\cdot)
is bounded, therefore, its set of limit points is not empty. Let
\overline{x}(\omega)
be such a limit point, which means that there exists a sequence
(t_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{X(\omega,t_{k})}=\overline{x}(\omega).

From
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}{\|M(X(\omega,t))\|}=0
and the continuity of
M
, we see that
\overline{x}(\omega)\in{\rm zer}M
. Since both conditions in Opial’s Lemma are satisfied, there exists
x^{*}(\omega)\in{\rm zer}M
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}X(\omega,t)=x^{*}(\omega)
.

Since
\omega\in\Omega_{\text{converge}}
was arbitrarily chosen, there exists a
{\rm zer}M
-valued random variable
x^{*}
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}X(t)=x^{*}
almost surely.
∎

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{\gamma_{\text{low}}t}\left(\frac{\mu_{\text{up}}^{2}L^{2}}{2}+\mu_{\text{up}}L+\frac{1}{2}\right)\distance((X_{0},Y_{0}),{\rm zer}M)^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{*}^{2}}{2\gamma_{\text{low}}\mu(t)}\quad\forall t>0,

if
0<\gamma_{\text{low}}\leq\gamma(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. If, in addition,
\int_{0}^{+\infty}\sigma_{\infty}^{2}(s)ds<+\infty
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}X(s)ds,y^{*}\right)-\Phi\left(x^{*},\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds\right)\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(t)t}\right)\quad\mbox{as}\ t\rightarrow+\infty

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}X(s)ds,y^{*}\right)-\Phi\left(x^{*},\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds\right)\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma(t)t}\right)\quad\mbox{as}\ t\rightarrow+\infty,

respectively.

Indeed, invoking Jensen’s inequality and (
19
), we have for all
t>0

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}X(s)ds,y^{*}\right)-\Phi\left(x^{*},\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}Y(s)ds\right)\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(\Phi(X(s),y^{*})-\Phi(x^{*},Y(s)))ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle(\nabla_{x}\Phi(X(s),Y(s)),-\nabla_{y}\Phi(X(s),Y(s))),(X(s),Y(s))-(x^{*},y^{*})\right\rangle ds\right).

4
Discrete time considerations

The aim of this section is to show how the ergodic upper bound results in expectation for the squared norm of the operator and for the gap function transfer to the stochastic variants of the Optimistic Gradient Descent Ascent (OGDA) method and the Extragradient (EG) method with constant step sizes, which can be interpreted as temporal discretizations of (
SDE-M
).

We will consider only equations governed by operators which are monotone, but not necessarily strongly monotone. For a stochastic variant of the Optimistic Gradient Descent Ascent (OGDA) method designed to solve variational inequalities, ergodic upper bound results for the gap function in expectation have also been derived in
[
6
]
. For the stochastic Extragradient (EG) method, ergodic upper bound results in expectation for the squared norm of the operator have also been derived in
[
16
]
(see also
[
8
,
20
]
), and for the gap function
[
6
]
. As seen in Remark
3.3
, the upper bound results for the gap function can be straightforwardly transferred via Jensen’s inequality and the gradient inequality for convex functions to the ergodic primal-dual gap when solving convex-concave minimax problems.

Throughout this section, we will assume that only a stochastic estimator
M(\cdot,\xi)
is accessible instead of
M
itself. The stochastic estimator is assumed to be unbiased, meaning that
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}(M(x,\xi))=M(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Further, it is required to have bounded variance, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}_{\xi}(\|M(x,\xi)-M(x)\|^{2})<\sigma_{*}^{2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

4.1
Stochastic Optimistic Gradient Descent Ascent (OGDA) method

Recalling the stochastic differential equation (
SDE-M
)

\displaystyle d(X(t)+\mu(t)M(X(t)))=-(\gamma(t)-\dot{\mu}(t))M(X(t))dt+\sigma(t,X(t))dW(t),

and fixing the parameter functions
\mu(t)=\gamma(t):=\gamma>0
to be constant for all
t\geq 0
, we consider the temporal discretization

\displaystyle x^{k+1}-x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})=-\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\quad\forall k\geq 1.

Equivalently,

\displaystyle x^{k+1}:=x^{k}-2\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\quad\forall k\geq 1,

(Stochastic OGDA)

where
x^{0},x^{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are given vectors and
\xi_{k}
encodes the stochasticity appearing at iteration
k
.

We consider the approximation errors

W_{k}:=M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-M(x^{k})\quad\forall k\geq 0,

and the sub-sigma algebras

{\cal F}_{k}:=\sigma(x^{0},x^{1},\xi_{0},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{k-1})\quad\forall k\geq 2.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
and
\gamma<\frac{1}{2L}
. For all
k\geq 2
it holds

\displaystyle\|x^{k+1}+\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-x^{*}\|\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle-2\gamma\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}-1)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|M(x^{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\left(\|x^{k-1}-x^{k-2}\|^{2}-\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+3\gamma^{2}\|W_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+5)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 2
be fixed. We observe that

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k+1}+\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})+2\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-\gamma^{2}\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ -2\gamma\langle x^{k}-\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*},M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}+\|\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\|^{2}-\gamma^{2}\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +2\gamma\langle x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*},M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\ -2\gamma\langle x^{k}-\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*},M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}+\gamma^{2}(\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\|^{2}-\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2})

\displaystyle\ -2\langle\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\gamma^{2}\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\gamma^{2}\big(\|W_{k-1}+M(x^{k-1}-M(x^{k}))+W_{k}\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\gamma^{2}\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+3\gamma^{2}(\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+\|M(x^{k-1})-M(x^{k})\|^{2}+\|W_{k}\|^{2}).

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\gamma^{2}\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+3\gamma^{2}(\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+L^{2}\|x^{k-1}-x^{k}\|^{2}+\|W_{k}\|^{2}).

(20)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\gamma^{2}\|-2M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})+M(x^{k-2},\xi_{k-2})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ =\gamma^{2}\|-M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})+W_{k-1}+M(x^{k-2})-M(x^{k-1})+W_{k-2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \leq 4\gamma^{2}(\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}+\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+L^{2}\|x^{k-1}-x^{k-2}\|^{2}+\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}),

therefore,

\displaystyle(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\ 4\gamma^{2}(\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}+\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+\|W_{k-2}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\ +4\gamma^{2}L^{2}\left(\|x^{k-1}-x^{k-2}\|^{2}-\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right).

(21)

Plugging (
21
) into (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k+1}+\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle-\gamma^{2}\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}+3\gamma^{2}\|W_{k}\|^{2}+\left(3\gamma^{2}+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\right)\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\left(\|x^{k-1}-x^{k-2}\|^{2}-\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{16\gamma^{4}L^{2}-\gamma^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+3\gamma^{2}\|W_{k}\|^{2}+\left(3\gamma^{2}+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\right)\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\left(\|x^{k-1}-x^{k-2}\|^{2}-\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}-1)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|M(x^{k-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+3\gamma^{2}\|W_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+5)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\left(\|x^{k-1}-x^{k-2}\|^{2}-\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right),

as claimed.
∎

The following theorem provides ergodic upper bounds in expectation for the squared norm of the operator and the gap function for (
Stochastic OGDA
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
and
\gamma<\frac{1}{4L}
. For all
K\geq 0
it holds

\displaystyle\ \min_{k=1,...,K+1}\mathbb{E}\left(\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}{\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\frac{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}{\gamma^{2}(1-16\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\left(4\|x^{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{4\gamma^{2}L^{2}(1-\gamma^{2}L^{2})}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}+8\gamma^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2}\right)+\frac{16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+11}{1-16\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\sigma_{*}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=2}^{K+2}\langle M(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle\right)\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\left(\frac{2\|x^{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}}{\gamma}+\frac{2\gamma L^{2}(1-\gamma^{2}L^{2})}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}+4\gamma\sigma_{*}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{\gamma(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+11)}{4(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\sigma_{*}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
K\geq 0
. According to Lemma
4.1
, we have for all
k\geq 2

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}(1-16\gamma^{2}L^{2})}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|M(x^{k-1})\|^{2}+2\gamma\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k-1},\xi_{k-1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}+\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\left(\|x^{k-1}-x^{k-2}\|^{2}-\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+5)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+3\gamma^{2}\|W_{k}\|^{2}.

Summing the above inequality for
k
from
2
to
K+2
and multiplying by
\frac{1}{K+1}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}(1-16\gamma^{2}L^{2})}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{2\gamma}{K+1}\sum_{k=2}^{K+2}\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\left(\|x^{2}+\gamma M(x^{1},\xi_{1})-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x^{K+3}+\gamma M(x^{K+2},\xi_{K+2})-x^{*}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{K+1}\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\left(\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}-\|x^{K+2}-x^{K+1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=2}^{K+2}\left(\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+5)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+3\gamma^{2}\|W_{k}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\left(\|x^{2}+\gamma M(x^{1},\xi_{1})-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=2}^{K+2}\left(\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+5)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+3\gamma^{2}\|W_{k}\|^{2}\right).

For all
k=2,...,K+2
we have that

\mathbb{E}(\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle)=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle|{\cal F}_{k}))=\mathbb{E}(\langle\mathbb{E}(M(x^{k},\xi_{k})|{\cal F}_{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle)=\langle M(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle

and

\mathbb{E}(\|W_{k}\|^{2})=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\|W_{k}\|^{2}|{\cal F}_{k}))\leq\sigma_{*}^{2}.

This yields

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}(1-16\gamma^{2}L^{2})}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=1}^{K+1}{\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}}\right)+2\gamma\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=2}^{K+2}\langle M(x^{k}),x^{k}-x^{*}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\left(\mathbb{E}(\|x^{2}+\gamma M(x^{1},\xi_{1})-x^{*}\|^{2})+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=2}^{K+2}\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{2}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|W_{k-2}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+5)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\|W_{k-1}\|^{2}+3\gamma^{2}\|W_{k}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\left(4\|x^{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+4\gamma^{2}L^{2}\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}+8\gamma^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2}+\frac{12\gamma^{4}L^{4}}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}\right)+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+11)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\sigma_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\left(4\|x^{1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{4\gamma^{2}L^{2}(1-\gamma^{2}L^{2})}{1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2}}\|x^{1}-x^{0}\|^{2}+8\gamma^{2}\sigma_{*}^{2}\right)+\frac{\gamma^{2}(16\gamma^{2}L^{2}+11)}{2(1-4\gamma^{2}L^{2})}\sigma_{*}^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

4.2
Stochastic Extragradient (EG) method

The stochastic Extragradient method (EG) reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}\begin{cases}&y^{k}:=x^{k}-\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\\
&x^{k+1}:=x^{k}-\gamma M(y^{k},\eta_{k})\end{cases}\quad\forall k\geq 0,\end{split}

(Stochastic EG)

where
x^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a given vector and
\xi_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
encode the stochasticity appearing at iteration
k
when evaluating
M
at
x^{k}
and
y^{k}
, respectively.

We consider the approximation errors

W_{k}:=M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-M(x^{k})\quad\mbox{and}\quad Z_{k}:=M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(y^{k})\quad\forall k\geq 0

and the sub-sigma algebras

{\cal F}_{0}:=\sigma(x^{0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\cal F}_{k}:=\sigma(x^{0},\xi_{0},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{k-1},\eta_{0},...,\eta_{k-1})\quad\forall k\geq 1.

and

\widehat{\cal F}_{0}:=\sigma(x^{0},\xi_{0})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widehat{\cal F}_{k}:=\sigma(x^{0},\xi_{0},\xi_{1},...,\xi_{k-1},\xi_{k},\eta_{0},...,\eta_{k-1})\quad\forall k\geq 1.

First, establish an inequality that will be essential to the proof of the convergence rate:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
. For all
k\geq 0
it holds

\displaystyle\|x^{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle M(y^{k},\eta_{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle+2(3L^{2}\gamma^{2}-1)\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\gamma^{2}(3\|Z_{k}\|^{2}+(1+6L^{2}\gamma^{2})\|W_{k}\|^{2}).

Proof.

Let
k\geq 0
. We have

\displaystyle\|x^{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-\gamma M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle x^{k}-x^{*},M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\rangle+\gamma^{2}\|M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle y^{k}+\gamma M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-\gamma M(x^{*})-x^{*},M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle+\gamma^{2}\|M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle y^{k}-x^{*},M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle\ -\gamma^{2}\left(2\langle M(x^{k},\xi_{k})-M(x^{*}),M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\rangle-\|M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle y^{k}-x^{*},M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\rangle-\gamma^{2}\|M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\gamma^{2}(\|M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle y^{k}-x^{*},M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\rangle-\gamma^{2}\|M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma^{2}(3\|M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(y^{k})\|^{2}+3\|M(y^{k})-M(x^{k})\|^{2}+3\|M(x^{k})-M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle y^{k}-x^{*},M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\rangle+\gamma^{2}(3L^{2}-1)\|M(x^{k},\xi_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma^{2}(3\|Z_{k}\|^{2}+3\|W_{k}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-2\gamma\langle y^{k}-x^{*},M(y^{k},\eta_{k})-M(x^{*})\rangle+2(3L^{2}\gamma^{2}-1)\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\gamma^{2}(3\|Z_{k}\|^{2}+(1+6L^{2}\gamma^{2})\|W_{k}\|^{2}),

as claimed.
∎

The following theorem provides ergodic upper bounds in expectation for the squared norm of the operator in terms of the sequence
(x^{k})_{k\geq 0}
and the gap function in terms of the sequence
(y^{k})_{k\geq 0}
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
x^{*}\in{\rm zer}M
and
\gamma<\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}L}
. For all
K\geq 0
it holds

\displaystyle\min_{k=0,...,K}\mathbb{E}\left(\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}{\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\frac{1}{2(1-3L^{2}\gamma^{2})}\|x^{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}(2+3L^{2}\gamma^{2})}{1-3L^{2}\gamma^{2}}\sigma_{*}^{2}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\langle M(y^{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle\right)\leq\frac{1}{K+1}\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|x^{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\gamma(2+3L^{2}\gamma^{2})\sigma_{*}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
K\geq 0
. According to Lemma
4.3
, we have for all
k\geq 0

\displaystyle 2(1-3L^{2}\gamma^{2})\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}+2\gamma\langle M(y^{k},\eta_{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\ \|x^{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x^{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\gamma^{2}(3\|Z_{k}\|^{2}+(1+6L^{2}\gamma^{2})\|W_{k}\|^{2}).

Summing the above inequality for
k
from
0
to
K
and multiplying by
\frac{1}{K+1}
it yields

\displaystyle\ \frac{2(1-3L^{2}\gamma^{2})}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}+\frac{2\gamma}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}{\langle M(y^{k},\eta_{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}(\|x^{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}-\|x^{K+1}-x^{*}\|^{2})+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{K+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K}3\|Z_{k}\|^{2}+(1+6L^{2}\gamma^{2})\|W_{k}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\|x^{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{K+1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{K}3\|Z_{k}\|^{2}+(1+6L^{2}\gamma^{2})\|W_{k}\|^{2}\right).

For all
k=0,...,K
we have that

\mathbb{E}(\langle M(y^{k},\eta_{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle)=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\langle M(y^{k},\eta_{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle|\widehat{\cal F}_{k}))=\mathbb{E}(\langle\mathbb{E}(M(y^{k},\eta_{k})|\widehat{\cal F}_{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle)=\langle M(y^{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle,

and

\mathbb{E}(\|W_{k}\|^{2})=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\|W_{k}\|^{2}|{\cal F}_{k}))\leq\sigma_{*}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}(\|Z_{k}\|^{2})=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\|Z_{k}\|^{2}|\widehat{\cal F}_{k}))\leq\sigma_{*}^{2}.

This yields

\displaystyle\ 2(1-3L^{2}\gamma^{2})\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}{\|M(x^{k})\|^{2}}\right)+2\gamma\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}{\langle M(y^{k}),y^{k}-x^{*}\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{K+1}\|x^{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+2\gamma^{2}(2+3L^{2}\gamma^{2})\sigma_{*}^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Local Glivenko-Cantelli Thanks: Accepted for presentation at the Conference on Learning Theory (COLT) 2023
Doron Cohen, Aryeh Kontorovich

Abstract
If μ \mu is a distribution over the d d -dimensional Boolean cube { 0 , 1 } d \left\{0,1\right\}^{d} , our goal is to estimate its mean p ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] d p\in[0,1]^{d} based on n n iid draws from μ \mu . Specifically, we consider the empirical mean estimator p ^ n \hat{p}_{n} and study the expected maximal deviation Δ n = 𝔼 max j ∈ [ d ] ⁡ | p ^ n ​ ( j ) − p ⁡ ( j ) | \Delta_{n}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\max_{j\in[d]}|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)| . In the classical Universal Glivenko-Cantelli setting, one seeks distribution-free (i.e., independent of μ \mu ) bounds on Δ n \Delta_{n} . This regime is well-understood: for all μ \mu , we have Δ n ≲ log ⁡ ( d ) / n \Delta_{n}\lesssim\sqrt{\log(d)/n} up to universal constants, and the bound is tight. Our present work seeks to establish dimension-free (i.e., without an explicit dependence on d d ) estimates on Δ n \Delta_{n} , including those that hold for d = ∞ d=\infty . As such bounds must necessarily depend on μ \mu , we refer to this regime as local Glivenko-Cantelli (also known as μ \mu -GC), and are aware of very few previous bounds of this type — which are either “abstract” or quite sub-optimal. Already the special case of product measures μ \mu is rather non-trivial. We give necessary and sufficient conditions on μ \mu for Δ n → 0 \Delta_{n}\to 0 , and calculate sharp rates for this decay. Along the way, we discover a novel sub-gamma-type maximal inequality for shifted Bernoullis, of independent interest.

Introduction

Estimating the mean of a random variable

X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

from
a sample of independent draws
X_{i}

is among the most basic problems of statistics.
Much of the theory has focused on obtaining
efficient estimators
\hat{m}_{n}

of the true mean
m
and
analyzing the decay of

\left\|\hat{m}_{n}-m\right\|_{2}

as a function of sample size
n
, dimension
d
,
and various moment assumptions on
X

(
Devroye et al. 2016
;
Lugosi and Mendelson 2019a
;
Lugosi and Mendelson 2019b
;
Cherapanamjeri et al. 2019
;
Cherapanamjeri et al. 2020
;
Lugosi and Mendelson 2021
)
.
In this work, we study this problem from a different angle. Inspired by
Thomas 2018
,
we consider a distribution
\mu

on
\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
with mean

p\in[0,1]^{d}
.
We stress that
p
is
not
a distribution:
the

do not generally sum to

\sum_{j=1}^{d}p(j)

might well diverge
for
d=\infty
.
Given
n
iid draws of
X_{i}\sim\mu
,
we denote by
\hat{p}_{n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
the empirical mean. The central quantity of
interest in this paper is
the uniform absolute deviation

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu):=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\hat{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\max_{j\in[d]}|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)|.

(1)

A few immediate remarks are in order.
First, the

\ell_{\infty}

norm in (
1

is in some sense the most interesting

norms;

\Delta_{n}^{(r)}:=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\hat{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{r}^{r}

decomposes into a sum of expectations
and the condition

\Delta_{n}^{(r)}\to 0

reduces to one of convergence

Second, it is obvious that

\Delta_{n}(\mu)\leq 1

always,
and

\Delta_{n}(\mu)\to 0
as

;
in fact,
it is well-known

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\log(d+1)}{n}}.

(2)

It is likewise clear that (

)
is not tight for all distributions:

satisfies

.
Our goal is to understand how

depends on the distribution

\mu

.

We

to encompass the infinite-dimensional case

,
where
\mu
is a distribution

\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}

(supported on the usual

.
At the same time,

to the case where
\mu
is a product measure
on
\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Thus, the components

For general

Indeed, taking

A straightforward generalization

Since

Main results

We begin with a characterization of
\mathsf{LGC}
.
For each

p\in[0,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}]^{\mathbb{N}}_{\downarrow 0}
, we define the two key quantities,

\displaystyle S(p)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}p(j)\log(j+1),

(4)

\displaystyle T(p)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\log(j+1)}{\log(1/p(j))}.

(5)

For any (not necessarily product)
probability measure
\mu
on
\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}
,
recall that
p={\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{X\sim\mu}[X]}

and define
\tilde{p}(j)=\min\left\{p(j),1-p(j)\right\}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Whenever
\tilde{p}(j)\to 0
, we have
that
{\tilde{p}}^{\downarrow}
is well-defined,
as are
S(\mu):=S({\tilde{p}}^{\downarrow})

and
T(\mu):=T({\tilde{p}}^{\downarrow})
;
otherwise,

S(\mu),T(\mu):=\infty
.

Theorem 1

(Characterization of
\mathsf{LGC}
)
.

Any

p\in[0,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}]^{\mathbb{N}}_{\downarrow 0}

satisfies
\Delta_{n}(p)\to 0

if and only if

T(p)<\infty
.
Additionally,
if
\mu

is any probability measure on

\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}

with

T(\mu)<\infty
,
then
\Delta_{n}(\mu)\to 0
.

It is immediate from the definitions
of
S
and
T

that
S(p)\leq T(p)
and hence
S(p)<\infty

whenever

p\in\mathsf{LGC}
. For these
p
, the asymptotic decay of

\Delta_{n}(p)\to 0
is determined by
S(p)
:

Theorem 2

(Coarse asymptotics of
\Delta_{n}
)
.

For every
product
probability
measure
\mu

on
\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}

such that

p=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{X\sim\mu}[X]\in\mathsf{LGC}
,
we have

\displaystyle c\sqrt{S(p)}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(p)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(p)\leq c^{\prime}\sqrt{S(p)},

where
c,c^{\prime}
are absolute constants.
Additionally,
if
\mu

is any probability measure on

\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}

and

T(\mu)<\infty
,
then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(\mu)\leq c^{\prime}\sqrt{S(\mu)}
.

This result may be informally summarized as

\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(p)\asymp\sqrt{S(p)}
,
for product measures.
In addition to the asymptotics,
we obtain the finite-sample upper bound

Theorem 3
.

For any
probability measure

\mu

on

\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}

with

T(\mu)<\infty
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}+\frac{T(\mu)\log n}{n}\right),\qquad n\geq\mathrm{e}^{3},

where
c>0
is an absolute constant.

We conjecture that the
\log n
factor
multiplying
T(\mu)

can be removed;
this would imply the

asymptotic
rate

\Delta_{n}(\mu)\lesssim\sqrt{S(\mu)/n}+T(\mu)/n
.
No further improvement is possible,
as evident from the

n\Delta_{n}(p)\gtrsim T

asymptotic lower bound:

Theorem 4

(Fine asymptotics of
\Delta_{n}
)
.

For every
product
probability
measure
\mu

on
\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}

with

such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}n\Delta_{n}(p)\geq cT(p),

where

is an absolute constant.

In contrast with the

of Theorem

the lower bounds in Theorems

are only asymptotic, and necessarily so. This is because even for a single binomial

Lemma 1

(Sub-gamma inequality for the shifted Bernoulli)

For all

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)}[\exp(t(X-s))]\leq\exp\left(\frac{pt^{2}}{2[1-t\log(1/s)/\log(1/p)]}\right).

Discussion.

We argue that the bounds
in Theorems

3

Indeed, it is known that

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-2p}{2\log(1/p-1)}

Thus, drawing intuition from the majorizing measure theorem

for
small
x

and

p=(x,0,0,\ldots)
,
we have
T(p)=1/\log(1/x)\gg x\log(1/x)\approx H(p)
,
so
T(p)\leq aH(p)^{b}

does not, in general, hold
for any constants
a,b>0
.

thus, in light of Theorem
1
,

H(p)
is not, in general, the correct measure
for controlling the decay of
\Delta_{n}(\mu)
.
(Of course, for non-product measures
\mu
,
the entropy
H(\mu)
takes coordinate correlations
into account and can be significantly smaller than

T(\mu)
and even than
S(\mu)
.)

Already
in
Thomas 2018
,
it was observed that Hoeffding’s inequality together with the union bound imply

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\left\|\hat{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon)\leq 2d\mathrm{e}^{-2n\varepsilon^{2}},\qquad\varepsilon>0,n\in\mathbb{N}.

(6)

Hence,
a sample of size
n\geq\frac{\log(2d/\delta)}{\varepsilon^{2}}

suffices to achieve

\mathbb{P}(\left\|\hat{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon)\leq\delta
.
This easily
implies (
2
),
which
is worst-case tight, as witnessed by the uniform distribution.
It was also
noted
therein
that McDiarmid’s inequality
implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\left\|\hat{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}\geq\Delta_{n}(\mu)+\varepsilon)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-2n\varepsilon^{2}},\qquad\varepsilon>0,n\in\mathbb{N},

(7)

which reduces the problem to one of
estimating
\Delta_{n}(\mu)
.
An elementary Borel-Cantelli argument
shows that
\Delta_{n}(\mu)\to 0

if and only if
\left\|\hat{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}\to 0

almost surely.
Moreover, standard information-theoretic
techniques can be used to show
that the distribution-free upper bound
in (
2
)
is worst-case tight not just for the
empirical mean
\hat{p}_{n}
,
but also for

any other
estimator
\tilde{p}_{n}
,
see Proposition
1
.
This continues to hold
even if we restrict our attention
only to product measures
\mu
,
as the proof thereof shows.

Additional estimates on
\Delta_{n}(\mu)

suggested in

Thomas 2018

include

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}p(j)(1-p(j))}

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}H(\mu)}.

The former is considerably inferior to the bound in
Theorem
3
,
while the latter does not decrease as
n\to\infty
.

4.1
Proof sketch for Lemma
1
(Sub-gamma inequality for the shifted Bernoulli)

Once we parametrize
t=a\log(1/p)/\log(1/s)
with
0\leq a<1
, proving the inequality is equivalent to showing that the following expression,
F(a)
,
is
non-positive:

\displaystyle F(a):=\log\left(\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\frac{a(1-s)}{\log\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)}-1}+(1-p)\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-\frac{as}{\log\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)}}\right)-\frac{a^{2}p\log^{2}\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)}{2(1-a)\log^{2}\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)}\leq 0.

This inequality can be demonstrated using elementary calculus techniques. The full proof is available in Appendix
A.1
.

4.2
Proof of
Theorem
3

We argue that there is no loss of generality in assuming

p\in[0,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}]^{\mathbb{N}}_{\downarrow 0}
:
replacing
X(j)

by
1-X(j)

does not affect
\Delta_{n}(\mu)

— even in the non-product case.
Decompose:

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}{\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}\vee\left[p(j)-\hat{p}_{n}(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left[p(j)-\hat{p}_{n}(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}.

To bound the lower tail

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left[p(j)-\hat{p}_{n}(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

we first invoke
the Chernoff-type bound of

Okamoto 1959

(see also
Boucheron et al. 2013
)
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(p(j)-\hat{p}_{n}(j)\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{nt^{2}}{2p(j)(1-p(j))}\right),\qquad j\in\mathbb{N},t\geq 0.

Applying
Lemma
9
with
Y_{j}=p(j)-\hat{p}_{n}(j)
and
\sigma_{j}^{2}=\frac{p(j)}{n}

yields

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left[p(j)-\hat{p}_{n}(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt{\frac{p(j)}{n}\log(j+1)}\leq 4\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}.

(8)

It remains to estimate the upper tail

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}
.
To this end,
we decompose

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
p(j)\geq\frac{1}{n}\end{subarray}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
p(j)<\frac{1}{n}\end{subarray}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

(9)

and focus on the first term

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{p(j)\geq\frac{1}{n}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}
.
For each
j
, we apply Bernstein’s inequality
(
Wainwright 2019
, Proposition 2.14)

to obtain

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2\left(\frac{p(j)(1-p(j))}{n}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3n}\right)}\right),\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Now invoke Lemma
10
with
Y_{i}=\hat{p}_{n}(i)-p(i),\ v_{i}=\frac{p(i)(1-p(i))}{n},\ a_{i}=\frac{1}{3n},\ I=\left\{i\in\mathbb{N}:p(i)\geq\frac{1}{n}\right\}

to yield

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\sup_{p(j)\geq\frac{1}{n}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 12\sup_{p(j)\geq\frac{1}{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p(j)(1-p(j))}{n}\log(j+1)}+\frac{16}{3n}\sup_{p(j)\geq\frac{1}{n}}\log(j+1)

(10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 12\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}+\frac{16}{3n}\sup_{p(j)\geq\frac{1}{n}}\log(j+1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 12\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}+\frac{16T(\mu)\log n}{3n},

where
(
10
)
holds because

\sup_{p(i)\geq 1/n}\frac{\log(i+1)}{\log(1/p(i))}\leq T
implies
\log(i+1)\leq T\log n
.
It remains to estimate the second term
in the right-hand side of
(
9
), which we
decompose as

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
p(j)<\frac{1}{n}\end{subarray}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
p(j)<\frac{1}{n}\end{subarray}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-\frac{1}{n}\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
p(j)<\frac{1}{n}\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{1}{n}-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

(11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
p(j)<\frac{1}{n}\end{subarray}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-\frac{1}{n}\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}+\frac{1}{n}.

To upper-bound
the first term

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{p(j)<\frac{1}{n}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-\frac{1}{n}\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}
,
we will use Lemmas

1

and

10
.
Recall that

p_{n}(j)-s:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}(j)-s

where
X_{i}(j)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\left(p(j)\right)
and
X_{1}(j),X_{2}(j),\dots,X_{n}(j)
are mutually independent. Let
\mathrm{e}^{-3}>s\geq p(j)
and let
0\leq t<\log(1/p(j))/\log(1/s).

Then

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\exp\left(t(p_{n}(j)-s)\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}{\exp\left(\frac{t}{n}(X_{i}(j)-s)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\left(\frac{p(j)t^{2}}{2n^{2}[1-t\log(1/s)/\ (n\log(1/p(j)))]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{(p(j)/n)t^{2}}{2[1-t\log(1/s)/(n\log(1/p(j)))]}\right).

Put
s={1}/{n}
and apply Lemma
10
with

Y_{i}=\hat{p}_{n}(i)-\frac{1}{n}
,
v_{i}=\frac{p(i)}{n}
,

a_{i}=\log(1/s)/(n\log(1/p(j)))
,
and

I=\left\{i\in\mathbb{N}:p(i)<\frac{1}{n}\right\}
, which yields,
together with
(
11
),

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
p(j)<\frac{1}{n}\end{subarray}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq 12\sup_{p(j)<\frac{1}{n}}\sqrt{\frac{p(j)}{n}\log(i+1)}+\frac{16\log(n)}{n}\sup_{p(j)<\frac{1}{n}}\frac{\log(j+1)}{\log(1/p(j))}+\frac{1}{n}

\displaystyle\leq 12\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}+\frac{16T(\mu)\log n}{n}+\frac{1}{n}.

(12)

Summing up
(
8
), (
10
), and (
12
),
we conclude that

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)\leq 28\left(\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}+\frac{T(\mu)\log n}{n}\right)+\frac{1}{n},

which proves Theorem
3
for
T(\mu)

sufficiently large
— say,

T(\mu)\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
We now assume
T(\mu)<\frac{1}{2}
and
decompose as in
(
9
)
but at
a different the splitting point:

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\leq n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j>n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}.

In order to bound the first term,

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\leq n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}
,
we follow the same steps
that we did to bound

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{p(j)\geq\frac{1}{n}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}
and get, instead of (
10
),

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\leq n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 12\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}+\frac{16}{3}\frac{T(\mu)\log n}{n({1-T(\mu)})}

(13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 12\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}+11\frac{T(\mu)\log n}{n}.

For the second term,
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j>n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}
, we note that for
j>n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}
, we have

\displaystyle p(j)\leq\frac{1}{(j+1)^{1/T(\mu)}}\leq\frac{1}{\left(n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}+1\right)^{1/T(\mu)}}<\frac{1}{n}.

It is well-known

(
Diaconis and Zabell 1991
;
Berend and Kontorovich 2013a
, Theorem 1)
that for

p(j)\leq\frac{1}{n}
,
we have

\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|\leq 2p(j)
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j>n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}\left[\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j>n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|

(14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j>n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}2p(j)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j>n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}\frac{2}{(j+1)^{1/T(\mu)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{}_{\!\!\!\!\!n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{1-T(\mu)}}}}^{\infty}\frac{2}{u^{1/T(\mu)}}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2T(\mu)\left(n^{\frac{T(\mu)}{T(\mu)-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{T(\mu)}-1}}{1-T(\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2T(\mu)}{n(1-T(\mu))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4T(\mu)}{n}.

Summing up (
8
), (
13
), and (
14
), we conclude

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 16\left(\sqrt{\frac{S(\mu)}{n}}+\frac{T(\mu)}{n}\right)

for
T(\mu)<\frac{1}{2}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
1

Theorem
3
immediately implies that
T(p)<\infty\implies p\in\mathsf{LGC}
.
Indeed, since
p\leq 1/\log(1/p)
, we have that
S(p)\leq T(p)

and hence
\Delta_{n}(\mu)\lesssim\sqrt{T(\mu)/n}+T(\mu)\log(n)/n
,
which, for finite
T(\mu)
, obviously decays to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

The other direction,

p\in\mathsf{LGC}\implies T(p)<\infty
,
is an immediate consequence of
Theorem
4
.
However, we find it
instructive to give a more
elementary and intuitive (though less quantitative) proof,
based on
an observation of
Berend 2022
.
For any
p\in[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}
, let us say that
it satisfies condition (B)
if

\displaystyle\text{(B)}\qquad\qquad\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}p(j)^{k}<\infty

(an appeal to Lebesgue’s monotone convergence theorem shows that
the above expression is either
0
or
\infty
).

Lemma 2

(
Berend 2022
)
.

If
p\in[0,1/2]^{\mathbb{N}}
does not satisfy

(B)

then
\Delta_{n}(p)\geq c>0

for some absolute constant
c
.

Lemma 3
.

For
p\in[0,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}]^{\mathbb{N}}_{\downarrow 0}
, the conditions

(B)

and
T(p)<\infty
are equivalent.

The proof for Lemmas,
3
and
2
can be found in Appendices
A.3
and
A.2
, respectively.

Remark.

Observe that (B) is permutation-invariant while
T(p)<\infty
assumes the decreasing ordering,
hence the two conditions are only equivalent on
[0,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}]^{\mathbb{N}}_{\downarrow 0}
.

Combining Lemmas
2
and
3
immediately implies that
T(p)=\infty\implies p\notin\mathsf{LGC}
.

4.4
Proof of Theorem
2

The upper estimate
\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(\mu)\lesssim\sqrt{S(\mu)}

is immediate from Theorem
3
.
Thus, it only remains to prove the lower estimate

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(p)\geq c\sqrt{S(p)}

(15)

for some absolute constant
c>0
.
This result is
actually
subsumed in our proof
Theorem
4
, but we include the somewhat
simpler proof below, which also has the advantage of yielding explicit constants.

We will use
the following
“Reverse Chernoff bound”
due to
Klein and Young 2015
:

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)
,
and
0<\varepsilon,p\leq 1/2

satisfy
\varepsilon^{2}pn\geq 3
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X\geq(1+\varepsilon)pn)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\exp\big({-9\varepsilon^{2}pn}\big).

We will also use

Berend and Kontorovich 2013a
:

Lemma 5
.

Suppose that

n\geq 2
,

p\in[1/n,1-1/n]
,
and

X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}|X-np|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}(X-np)^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}np(1-p)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{np},\qquad p\leq 1/2.

Continuing with the proof
of (
15
),
we put

\varepsilon=\sqrt{\frac{5\log(j+1)}{np(j)}}
.
Then
Lemma
4

implies that
for each

j\in\mathbb{N}

verifying

\frac{20\log(j+1)}{n}\leq p(j),

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|\geq\sqrt{\frac{5p(j)\log(j+1)}{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(j+1\right)^{45}}.

Since
the
\hat{p}_{n}(j)
,

j\in\mathbb{N}
,
are assumed to be independent,
for all natural
k\leq l

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{j\in[k,l]}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{5p(l)\log(k+1)}{n}}\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{(k+1)^{45}}\right)^{l-k+1},

(16)

whenever
p(l)\geq 20\log(k+1)/n
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle J(k):=\left\{2^{45^{k-1}}-1,2^{45^{k-1}},2^{45^{k-1}}+1,2^{45^{k-1}}+2,\ldots,2^{45^{k}}\right\}.

A repeated application of (
16
) yields,
for

p(2^{45^{k}})\geq 20\log(2^{45^{k-1}})/n
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{j\in J(k)}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{45}}\sqrt{\frac{p(2^{45^{k}})\log{(2^{45^{k}})}}{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{j\in J(k)}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{p(2^{45^{k}})\log{(2^{45^{k-1}})}}{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{45^{k}}}\right)^{2^{45^{k}}-2^{45^{k-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-2^{-45^{k}}\right)^{2^{45^{k}}-2^{45^{k-1}}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(2^{-44\cdot 45^{k-1}}-1\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\mathrm{e}}.

If follows that

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(p)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\max_{j\in J(k)}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|

(17)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}\\
np(2^{45^{k}})\geq 20\log\left(2^{45^{k-1}}\right)\end{subarray}}\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-1}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{45}}\sqrt{\frac{p(2^{45^{k}})\log{(2^{45^{k}})}}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}\\
np(2^{45^{k}})\geq 20\log\left(2^{45^{k-1}}\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{90}\sqrt{\frac{p(2^{45^{k}})\log{(2^{45^{k+1}})}}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}\\
np(2^{45^{k}})\geq 20\log\left(2^{45^{k-1}}\right)\end{subarray}}\max_{j\in J(k+1)}\frac{1}{90}\sqrt{\frac{p(2^{45^{k}})\log{(j+1)}}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}\\
np(2^{45^{k}})\geq 20\log\left(2^{45^{k-1}}\right)\end{subarray}}\max_{j\in J(k+1)}\frac{1}{90}\sqrt{\frac{p(j)\log{(j+1)}}{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}\end{subarray}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in J(k+1)\\
np(j)\geq 20\log\left(j+1\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{90}\sqrt{\frac{p(j)\log{(j+1)}}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{90\sqrt{n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>2^{45}\\
np(j)\geq 20\log\left(j+1\right)\end{subarray}}\sqrt{p(j)\log{(j+1)}}.

Additionally,
using
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|\geq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|

and
Lemma
5

for
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(p)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max_{j\leq 2^{45}}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left|\hat{p}_{n}(j)-p(j)\right|

(18)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq 2^{45}\\
p(j)\geq\frac{1}{n}\end{subarray}}\sqrt{p(j)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{90\sqrt{n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq 2^{45}\\
np(j)\geq 20\log\left(j+1\right)\end{subarray}}\sqrt{p(j)\log(j+1)}.

Finally, we combine (
17
) and (
18
)
to obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(p)+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}\Delta_{n}(p)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{180}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j>2^{45}\\
np(j)\geq 20\log\left(j+1\right)\end{subarray}}\sqrt{p(j)\log{(j+1)}}+\frac{1}{180}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq 2^{45}\\
np(j)\geq 20\log\left(j+1\right)\end{subarray}}\sqrt{p(j)\log(j+1)}

\displaystyle\geq

The goal is to upper bound

Proof of Theorem
5

We start by, conditionally on
\tilde{X}_{i}
, defining the class
F=\left\{f_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
over
\Omega=\left\{0,1\right\}^{\mathbb{N}}
,
where
f_{j}(x)=(1-a(j))x(j)+a(j)(1-x(j))
and observing that

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{f\in F}\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(X_{i})-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}f(X_{i})\right|.

Combining with

Wainwright 2019

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(\mu)\leq 2R_{n}(F),

(23)

where

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{n}(F;X):=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{f\in F}\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f(X_{i})\right|

\displaystyle R_{n}(F):=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{X}\hat{R}_{n}(F;X)=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon,X}\sup_{f\in F}\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f(X_{i})\right|

are the
(empirical and expected, respectively)

; the
\varepsilon_{i},i\in[n]
are independent Rademacher random variables
defined by
\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon_{i}=1)=\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon_{i}=-1)=1/2
.
Since

\hat{R}_{n}(F;X)

has
2/n
-bounded differences as a function of

, we can invoke McDiarmid’s inequality

[
Boucheron et al. 2013

to obtain, for all
\delta\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(\Delta_{n}(\mu)\leq 2\hat{R}_{n}(F;X)+2\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(R_{n}(F)\leq 2\hat{R}_{n}(F;X)+2\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)

[by (
23
)]

\displaystyle\geq 1-\delta.

(24)

We now turn to bounding
\hat{R}_{n}(F;X)
, by bounding the
moment-generating function (conditional on
X_{i}
) of each

for

via Hoeffding’s lemma.
Evidently, for
\lambda\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}\exp\left(\lambda\left(n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f_{j}(X_{i})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon_{i}}\exp\left(\lambda\left(n^{-1}\varepsilon_{i}f_{j}(X_{i})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\frac{(1-a(j))X_{i}(j)+a(j)(1-X_{i}(j))}{2n^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-a(j))X_{i}(j)+a(j)(1-X_{i}(j))}{2n^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\frac{1}{2n}\tilde{p}_{n}(j)\right).

By the Cramér-Chernoff method,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}_{\varepsilon}\left(n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f_{j}(X_{i})\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{\lambda\geq 0}\frac{\exp\left(\lambda^{2}\frac{1}{2n}\tilde{p}_{n}(j)\right)}{\mathrm{e}^{t\lambda}}=\exp\left(-\frac{nt^{2}}{2\tilde{p}_{n}(j)}\right).

Conditional on
X_{1},X_{2},...,X_{n}
, we apply Lemma
9
with
Y_{j}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f_{j}(X_{i})
,
\sigma^{2}_{j}=\frac{\tilde{p}_{n}(j)}{n}
and arrive with a bound for
\hat{R}_{n}(F;X)
;

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{n}(F;X)

\displaystyle:=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{f\in F}\left|n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f(X_{i})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{f\in F}\left[n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f(X_{i})\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}+\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{f\in F}\left[n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\varepsilon_{i}f(X_{i})\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle=2\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\varepsilon}\sup_{f\in F}\left[n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{i}f(X_{i})\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

(symmetry of
\varepsilon_{i}
)

\displaystyle\leq 8\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt{{\tilde{p}_{n}}^{\downarrow}(j)\log(j+1)}

(reindexing)

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{S({\tilde{p}_{n}}^{\downarrow}(j))}.

Substituting into (
24
) yields

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}_{X}\left(\Delta_{n}(\mu)\leq\frac{16}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{S({\tilde{p}_{n}}^{\downarrow})}+\sqrt{\frac{8}{n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\delta,

(25)

for all
\delta>0
.
Taking
\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{X}}[\cdot]
on both sized completes the proof.
∎

B.1
Worst-case optimality of

Proposition 1
.

There is an absolute constant
c>0

The proof relies on applying the Generalized Fano method

:

Lemma 8

(
[
Yu 1997
]
)
.

For

r\geq 2
, let

\mathcal{M}_{r}

be a collection of
r

\nu_{1},\nu_{2},...,\nu_{r}

with some parameter of interest

\theta(\nu)

taking
values in pseudo-metric space
(\Theta,\rho)

such that for all

j\neq k
, we have

\rho(\theta(\nu_{j}),\theta(\nu_{k}))\geq\alpha

and

D(\nu_{j}~\|~\nu_{k})\leq\beta.

Then

\inf_{\hat{\theta}}\max_{j\in[d]}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Z\sim\mu_{j}}\rho(\hat{\theta}(Z),\theta(\nu_{j}))\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{\beta+\log 2}{\log r}\right)\right),

where the infimum is over all estimators

\hat{\theta}:Z\mapsto\Theta
.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},...,\mu_{d}
be
the
product measures on

\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
given by

\mu_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\operatorname{Bernoulli}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha\boldsymbol{1}[i=j]\right),\qquad i\in[d],

where

\alpha\in[0,1/4]

will be chosen later.
We will invoke Lemma
8
with
r=d
,
\nu_{j}=\mu_{j}^{n}
for
j\in[d]
,

\theta(\mu_{j}^{n})=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{X\sim\mu_{j}}X
and
\rho=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
.
We begin by verifying that the conditions of Lemma
8
apply.
Indeed,
for
i\neq j
,
i,j\in[d]
we have

\rho(\theta(\mu_{i}^{n}),\theta(\mu_{j}^{n}))=\left\|\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{X\sim\mu_{i}}X-\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{Y\sim\mu_{j}}Y\right\|_{\infty}\geq\alpha

and

\displaystyle D(\mu_{i}^{n}~\|~\mu_{j}^{n})

\displaystyle=nD(\mu_{i}~\|~\mu_{j})

\displaystyle=nD\left(\frac{1}{2}+\alpha~\big\|~\frac{1}{2}\right)+nD\left(\frac{1}{2}~\big\|~\frac{1}{2}+\alpha\right)

\displaystyle\leq n\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-1/2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1/2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1-1/2-\alpha}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{1/2+\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=n\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{8-16\alpha^{2}}{1-4\alpha^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{28}{3}n\alpha^{2},

where,
as in the proof of Lemma
6
,
we used

Gibbs and Su 2002
.
Invoking Lemma
8
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\tilde{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\max_{\mu_{i},i\in[d]}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\tilde{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(1-\frac{\frac{28}{3}n\alpha^{2}-\log 2}{\log d}\right).

We choose

\alpha=\frac{1}{4}\wedge\frac{\sqrt{\log(d/2)}}{2\sqrt{7n}}

\alpha<\frac{1}{4}

and

\alpha=\frac{1}{4}

If
\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\tilde{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(1-\frac{\frac{28}{3}n\alpha^{2}-\log 2}{\log d}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\log\frac{d}{2}}\left(1-\frac{\frac{1}{3}\log\frac{d}{2}+\log 2}{\log d}\right)}{4\sqrt{7n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}{6\sqrt{7n}\log d}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{\log d}}{48\sqrt{7n}},

where we used the fact

.
If
\alpha=\frac{1}{4}

d\geq 2\mathrm{e}^{\frac{7n}{4}}

\displaystyle\sup_{\mu}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\tilde{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(1-\frac{\frac{28}{3}n\alpha^{2}-\log 2}{\log d}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8}\left(1-\frac{\frac{7n}{12}+\log 2}{\log d}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{7n}{84n+48\log 2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{17}.

It follows that

\sup_{\mu}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\left\|\tilde{p}_{n}-p\right\|_{\infty}\geq\frac{1}{17}\wedge\frac{\sqrt{\log d}}{48\sqrt{7n}}

holds for both cases.
∎

B.2
Maximal inequalities

Lemma 9

(Maximal inequality for inhomogeneous sub-Gaussians)
.

Let
Y_{1},Y_{2},...
be random variables and
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},...
positive real numbers such that

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(Y_{i}\geq t\right)\leq e^{-t^{2}/2\sigma_{i}^{2}},\qquad i\in\mathbb{N},~t\geq 0.

Let

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\sigma_{i}^{2}\log(i+1).

Then

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left[Y_{i}\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\sqrt{T}.

Proof.

By the union bound, for
t^{2}>2T
we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left[Y_{i}\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(\left[Y_{i}\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(Y_{i}\geq t\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}/2\sigma_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}\log(i+1)/2T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}i^{-t^{2}/2T}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{-t^{2}/2T}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{2T}{t^{2}-2T}.

Integrating,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left[Y_{i}\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left[Y_{i}\right]_{\scalebox{.5}{
\!\!\!\!\!
$\boldsymbol{+}$}}\geq t\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{T}+\int_{2\sqrt{T}}^{\infty}\frac{2T}{t^{2}-2T}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{T}+\sqrt{T}\frac{-\log\left(3-2\sqrt{2}\right)}{\sqrt{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{T}.

∎

Lemma 10

(Maximal inequality for inhomogeneous sub-gammas)
.

Let

Y_{i\in I\subseteq\mathbb{N}}

random variables
such that, for each
i\in I
, there
are

and

(a)

For all

(or all

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{v_{i}t^{2}}{2[1-a_{i}t]}\right).

$\boldsymbol{+}$}}}

,

To streamline the proof

By Cramér-Chernoff’s method,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf_{0<t<\frac{1}{a}}\frac{\exp\left(\frac{v_{i}t^{2}}{2[1-a_{i}t]}\right)}{\mathrm{e}^{t\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2(v_{i}+a_{i}\varepsilon)}\right).

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbb{P}}\left(Y_{i}\geq\sqrt{2v_{i}\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\left(a_{i}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{2}}+a_{i}\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta

$\boldsymbol{+}$}}\geq\sup_{i\in I}\sqrt{2v_{i}\log\frac{i(i+1)}{\delta}}+2a_{i}\log\frac{i(i+1)}{\delta}\right)

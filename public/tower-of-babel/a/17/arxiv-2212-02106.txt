Munayim Dilxat, Shoulan Gao, Dong Liu, Limeng Xia

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we consider some non-weight modules over the Lie algebra of Weyl type.
First, we determine the modules whose restriction to
U(\mathfrak{h})
are free of rank
1
over the Lie algebra of differential operators on the circle. Then we determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for the tensor products of quasi-finite highest weight modules and
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules to be irreducible, and obtain that any two such tensor products are isomorphic if and only if the corresponding highest weight modules and
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules are isomorphic. Finally, we extend such results to the Lie algebras of differential operators in the general case.

Key words:
Virasoro algebra; differential operator; polynomial module; Weyl algebra

MSC:
17B10,17B65, 17B68.

1.
Introduction

Extended symmetries have played an important role in conformal
field theories. This leads one to study
W
algebras,
which are higher-spin extensions of the Virasoro algebra, and
their superanalogues, if one takes supersymmetry into account, cf.

[
5
,
10
]
and the vast literatures therein. A fundamental
example of
W
algebras, now known as the
W_{1+\infty}

algebra, appears as the limit of the

W_{N}
algebras, as
N
goes to
\infty

[
18
]
. It is well known that the
W_{1+\infty}
algebra can
also be interpreted as the central extension of the Lie algebra of
differential operators on the circle
[
11
,
19
]
. Weight modules over
W_{1+\infty}
were studied sufficiently and have appeared in various models of two-dimensional quantum field theory and integrable systems (see
[
7
,
11
,
8
,
20
]
, etc.). However, there are few papers on non-weight modules over
W_{1+\infty}
, up to now.

Recently, many authors constructed various non-weight modules over some Lie algebras. In particular, J. Nilsson
[
16
]
constructed a class of
{\mathfrak{s}l}_{n}
-modules that are free of rank one when restricted to the enveloping algebra of its Cartan subalgebra. This kind of non-weight modules, which are called
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules, have been extensively studied. In
[
16
]
and a subsequent paper
[
17
]
, J. Nilsson showed that a finite dimensional simple Lie algebra has nontrivial
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules if and only if it is of the special linear algebra
\mathfrak{sl}({n+1},{\mathbb{C}})
or the symplectic algebra
\mathfrak{sp}({2n},{\mathbb{C}})
for some
n\geq 1
. And the idea was exploited and generalized to consider modules over infinite dimensional Lie algebras, such as the Witt algebra (the Virasoro algebra)
[
15
,
21
]
, the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and the
W(2,2)
algebra
[
6
]
, and so on.
The aim of this paper is to determine such modules for the Lie algebra
W_{1+\infty}
motivated by
[
6
]
and
[
21
]
.
We get a kind of new simple modules over
W_{1+\infty}
but they are not modules of the corresponding associative algebras (see (3.1)).
Moreover, such results can be extended to the Lie algebras of differential operators on the Laurent polynomial rings of multi-variables.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some necessary definitions and preliminary results. In Section 3, we determine all module structures on
U(\mathfrak{h})
, see Theorem 3.2. In Section 4, we give the necessary and sufficient conditions for the tensor product of a quasi-finite highest weight module
L(h,c)
and a
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free
\mathcal{D}
-module
\Omega(\lambda,\epsilon)
to be irreducible, see Theorem 4.1. Furthermore, we show that two such tensor product modules are isomorphic if and only if the corresponding quasi-finite highest weight modules and
U(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules are isomorphic, see Theorem 4.2. Consequently, we obtain a lot of new irreducible non-weight modules over the Lie algebra of differential operators. In Section 5, we construct such modules for the Lie algebras of differential operators in the general case.

Throughout this paper, denote by
\mathbb{Z},

\mathbb{Z}_{+},

\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{C}^{*}
the sets of integers, positive integers,
non-negative integers, complex numbers and non-zero complex numbers, respectively. All algebras and modules are over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
.

2.
Basics

In this section, we recall some necessary definitions and preliminary results.

2.1.
The Lie algebra of differential operators

Let
{\mathbb{C}}[t,t^{-1}]

be the Laurent polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{C}}
,
{\mathcal{D}}_{ass}=\hbox{Diff}\,{\mathbb{C}}[\,t,t^{-1}\,]

the associative algebra of all differential operators over
{\mathbb{C}}[\,t,t^{-1}\,]

which has a basis
\{t^{m}D^{n}\mid m\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
with multiplications:

(t^{m_{1}}D^{n_{1}})(t^{m_{2}}D^{n_{2}})=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{1}}{n_{1}\choose i}\,m_{2}^{i}\,t^{m_{1}+m_{2}}D^{n_{1}+n_{2}-i}

, where
D=t\frac{d}{dt}
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be the Lie algebra of
{\mathcal{D}}_{ass}
under Lie bracket given by

\bigl[t^{m_{1}}D^{n_{1}},t^{m_{2}}D^{n_{2}}\,\bigr]=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{1}}{n_{1}\choose i}\,m_{2}^{i}\,t^{m_{1}+m_{2}}D^{n_{1}+n_{2}-i}-\sum_{j=0}^{n_{2}}{n_{2}\choose j}\,m_{1}^{j}\,t^{m_{1}+m_{2}}D^{n_{1}+n_{2}-j}

for all
m_{1},\;m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z},\;n_{1},\;n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Next, we define the Verma module, which is a highest weight module with the free action of

on the highest weight vector (see

and

be the one-dimensional module over the subalgebra

\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{+}{\bf 1}=0,C{\bf 1}=c{\bf 1},D^{k}{\bf 1}=h_{k}{\bf 1}.

Then we get the induced
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-module, called Verma module:

M(h,c)=U(\widehat{\mathcal{D}})\otimes_{U(\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{+}+\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{0})}\mathbb{C}{\bf 1}.

It is well known that the Verma module

has a unique maximal
submodule
J(h,c)
and the corresponding simple quotient module is
denoted by

Introduce the generating series

2.2.
The subalgebras of the Lie algebra of differential operators

The Lie algebra
{\mathcal{D}}
has a subalgebra, which is called the Witt algebra,
\mathcal{W}=\mbox{Der}\,{\mathbb{C}}[t,t^{-1}]
(also denote by Vect(
S^{1}
), the Lie algebra of all vector fields on the circle). The Virasoro algebra Vir is the universal central extension of
\mathcal{W}
, with a basis
\{L_{n}=t^{n+1}\frac{d}{dt},C|n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and relations

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(n-m)L_{m+n}+\delta_{n,-m}\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}C,

[C,L_{m}]=0,\,\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

The Lie algebra
{\mathcal{D}}
has also a subalgebra
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
generated by
\{I_{m}:=t^{m},L_{m}:=t^{m}D\mid m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
with the following relations:

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(n-m)L_{m+n},

\displaystyle[I_{m},I_{n}]=0,

\displaystyle[L_{m},I_{n}]=nI_{m+n},\forall m,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Its universal central extension is called the twisted
Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra (see
[
3
,
4
,
13
,
14
]
).

4.
Tensor Products

In this section, we will obtain a class of irreducible non-weight modules over
\mathcal{D}
by taking the tensor products of
\Omega(\lambda,\epsilon)
with quasifinite highest weight modules and determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for two irreducible tensor products to be isomorphic.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
. Let
V
be an irreducible quasifinite highest weight module over
\mathcal{D}
. Then
\Omega(\lambda,\epsilon)\otimes V
is an irreducible
\mathcal{D}
-module.

Proof.

Let
W=\Omega(\lambda,\epsilon)\otimes V
. It is clear that, for any
v\in V
, there is a positive integer
K(v)
such that
t^{m}D^{n}(v)=0
for all
m\geq K(v)
.

Suppose
M
is a nonzero submodule of
W
. It suffices to show that
W=M
. Take a nonzero
w=\sum_{j=0}^{s}x^{j}\otimes v_{j}\in W
such that
v_{j}\in V
,
v_{s}\neq 0
and
s
is minimal.

Claim 1.

s=0
.

Let
K={\rm max}\,\{K(v_{j}):j=0,1,...,s\}
. Using
t^{m}D(v_{j})=0
for all
m\geq K
and
j=0,1,...,s
, we can deduce that

\lambda^{-m}t^{m}D(w)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}(x-\epsilon m)(x-m)^{j}\otimes v_{j}\in M,\,\forall m\geq K.

Write the right hand side as

\sum_{j=0}^{s+1}m^{j}w_{j}\in M,\quad\,\forall m\geq K,

where
w_{j}\in W
are independent of
m
. In particular,
w_{s+1}=\epsilon(-1)^{s-1}\otimes v_{s}
. Taking
m=K,K+1,...,K+s
, we see that the coefficient matrix of
w_{i}
is a Vandermonde matrix. So each
w_{i}\in M
. In particular,
w_{s+1}=\epsilon(-1)^{s-1}\otimes v_{s}\in M
. Consequently,
s=0
.

Claim 2.

W=M
.

By Claim 1, we know that
1\otimes v\in M
for some nonzero
v\in V
. By induction on
i
and using

\displaystyle t^{m}D(x^{i}\otimes v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda^{m}(x-\epsilon m)(x-m)^{i})\otimes v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda^{m}(x-m)^{i+1})\otimes v+(\lambda^{m}m(1-\epsilon)(x-m)^{i})\otimes v,

where
m\geq K(v)
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, we deduce that
x^{i}\otimes v\in M
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.,
\Omega(\lambda,\epsilon)\otimes v\subset M
. Let
X
be a maximal subspace of
V
such that
\Omega(\lambda,\epsilon)\otimes X\subset M
. We know that
X\neq 0
. Clearly,
X
is a nonzero submodule of
V
. Since
V
is irreducible, we obtain that
X=V
. Therefore,
M=W
and
M
is irreducible.
∎

Next, we will determine the necessary and sufficient conditions for two irreducible tensor products defined in Theorem
4.1
to be isomorphic.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}
, and let
V
and
V^{\prime}
be two irreducible quasifinite highest weight modules over
\mathcal{D}
. Then
W=\Omega(\lambda,\epsilon)\otimes V
and
W^{\prime}=\Omega(\lambda^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})\otimes V^{\prime}
are isomorphic as
\mathcal{D}
-modules if and only if
(\lambda,\epsilon)=(\lambda^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime})
and
V\cong V^{\prime}
as
\mathcal{D}
-modules.

Proof.

The sufficiency of the theorem is obvious. We only need to prove the necessity.
Let
\varphi
be an isomorphism from
W
to
W^{\prime}
as
\mathcal{D}
-modules.
Take a nonzero element
1\otimes v\in W
. Suppose

\varphi(1\otimes v)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}x^{j}\otimes w_{j},

where
w_{j}\in V^{\prime}
with
w_{k}\neq 0
. There is a positive integer
K=K(v)
such that
t^{m}D\cdot v=t^{m}D\cdot w_{j}=0
for all
m\geq K
and
0\leq j\leq k
. For any
m,m^{\prime}\geq K
, we have

(\lambda^{-m}t^{m}D-\lambda^{-m^{\prime}}t^{m^{\prime}}D)(1\otimes v)=\epsilon(m^{\prime}-m)(1\otimes v),

which suggests

\displaystyle\epsilon(m^{\prime}-m)(\sum_{j=0}^{k}x^{j}\otimes w_{j})=(\lambda^{-m}t^{m}D-\lambda^{-m^{\prime}}t^{m^{\prime}}D)(\sum_{j=0}^{k}x^{j}\otimes w_{j})

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left[(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda})^{m}(x-\epsilon^{\prime}m)(x-m)^{j}-(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda})^{m^{\prime}}(x-\epsilon^{\prime}m^{\prime})(x-m^{\prime})^{j}\right]\otimes w_{j}.

Then we can get that

\left[(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda})^{m}-(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda})^{m^{\prime}}\right](x^{k+1}\otimes w_{k})=0
for all
m,m^{\prime}\geq K.

So

\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}

and (
4.1
) becomes

\epsilon(m^{\prime}-m)(\sum_{j=0}^{k}x^{j}\otimes w_{j})=\sum_{j=0}^{k}((x-\epsilon^{\prime}m)(x-m)^{j}-(x-\epsilon^{\prime}m^{\prime})(x-m^{\prime})^{j})\otimes w_{j},

(4.2)

where
m,m^{\prime}\geq K
. If
k>0
, for
\epsilon^{\prime}=1
, by the coefficient of
m^{k+1}
in (
4.2
), we get

(-1)^{k+1}\epsilon^{\prime}(1\otimes w_{k})=0
, a contradiction. If
k>0
, for
\epsilon^{\prime}=0
, by the leading coefficient of
m
in (
4.2
), it is easy to see that
k=1
and
\epsilon(m^{\prime}-m)(1\otimes w_{0}+x\otimes w_{1})=(m^{\prime}-m)x\otimes w_{1}
for all
m,m^{\prime}\geq K
, which is impossible. Therefore,

k=0
and

\epsilon(m^{\prime}-m)(1\otimes w_{k})=\epsilon^{\prime}(m^{\prime}-m)(1\otimes w_{k})
for all
m,m^{\prime}\geq K
,
which forces

\epsilon=\epsilon^{\prime}.

Thus there is a bijection
\tau:V\longrightarrow V^{\prime}
such that

\varphi(1\otimes v)=1\otimes\tau(v),\quad\,\forall v\in V.

Since

\varphi(t^{m}D^{n}\cdot(1\otimes v))=t^{m}D^{n}\cdot(\varphi(1\otimes v))=t^{m}D^{n}\cdot(1\otimes\tau(v)),

we have

\displaystyle\beta^{1-n}\lambda^{m}\varphi((x-\varphi m)^{n}\otimes v)+\varphi(1\otimes(t^{m}D^{n}\cdot v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{1-n}\lambda^{m}(x-\varphi m)^{n}\otimes\tau(v)+1\otimes(t^{m}D^{n}\cdot\tau(v)),\quad\,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

Hence

\beta^{1-n}\lambda^{m}\varphi((x-\epsilon m)^{n}\otimes v)=\beta^{1-n}\lambda^{m}(x-\epsilon m)^{n}\otimes\tau(v)
for
m\geq K.
So

\varphi((x-\epsilon m)^{n}\otimes v)=(x-\epsilon m)^{n}\otimes\tau(v),\quad\,m\geq K.

Then it is easy to deduce that

\varphi(t^{m}D^{n}(1)\otimes v)=t^{m}D^{n}(1)\otimes\tau(v),\quad\,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

By

\varphi(t^{m}D^{n}\cdot(1\otimes v))=t^{m}D^{n}\cdot(\varphi(1\otimes v))
,
we can obtain

\varphi(1\otimes t^{m}D^{n}(v))=1\otimes t^{m}D^{n}(\tau(v)).

So

\tau(t^{m}D^{n}(v))=t^{m}D^{n}(\tau(v)),\,\,\forall m\in\mathbb{Z},\,v\in V.

Clearly, for
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
\varphi(c(1\otimes v))=c(\varphi(1\otimes v))
implies that
\tau(cv)=c\tau(v)
. Thus
\tau:V\longrightarrow V^{\prime}
is a
\mathcal{D}
-module isomorphism and
V\cong V^{\prime}
. The proof is finished.
∎

[t_{i}^{-1}D_{i}^{2},t_{i}D_{j}]=2D_{i}D_{j}+D_{j}
, using (

) and (

), we can obtain

D_{i}D_{j}\cdot f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\nu})=\beta^{-1}x_{i}x_{j}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\nu}),\ \text{for all}\ f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\nu})\in\mathcal{A}.

By Lemma

Sections of Lagrangian fibrations on
holomorphic symplectic manifolds

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
M
be a holomorphically
symplectic manifold, equipped with a Lagrangian fibration

\pi:\;M\to X
. A degenerate twistor deformation
(a more general version of it is called “a Tate-Shafarevich twist”) is a family
of holomorphically symplectic structures on
M

parametrized by
H^{1,1}(X)
. All members of this
family are equipped with a holomorphic Lagrangian projection
to
X
, and their fibers are isomorphic to the fibers of
\pi
.
Assume that
M
is a compact hyperkähler manifold of maximal holonomy,
and the general fiber of the Lagrangian projection
\pi

is primitive (that is, not divisible) in integer homology.
We also assume that
\pi
has reduced fibers in codimension 1.
Then
M
has a degenerate twistor deformation
M^{\prime}

such that the Lagrangian projection
\pi:\;M^{\prime}\to X

admits a meromorphic section.

1
Introduction

1.1
Holomorphic section of Lagrangian fibrations

A hyperkähler manifold, for our present purposes,
is compact, Kähler, holomorphically symplectic manifold.
We say that
M
is
a hyperkähler manifold of maximal holonomy,

or
IHS
(irreducible holomorphically symplectic) if

H^{2,0}(M)={\mathbb{C}}
and
\pi_{1}(M)=0
. The compactness and the maximal
holonomy condition is tacitly assumed throughout this paper.

A
Lagrangian fibration
on a hyperkähler
manifold is a holomorphic map
\pi:\;M{\>\longrightarrow\>}X
,
with all fibers Lagrangian with respect to the
holomorphic symplectic form on
M
.
The manifold
X
is biholomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}

if it is smooth (
[
Hw
]
).
With each Lagrangian fibration
\pi:\;M{\>\longrightarrow\>}X
we associate
a deformation
I_{t}
of a complex structure on
M
,
parametrized by
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
(
[
V1
]
).
This deformation, called

the degenerate twistor deformation
, is properly
introduced in Subsection
2.2
. It shares many
properties with the twistor deformation, known
from hyperkähler geometry; in particular,
its general fiber is non-algebraic.
Most importantly, each member of this
family is equipped with a holomorphic Lagrangian projection
to
X
, and the fibers of this projection are isomorphic
to the fibers of
\pi
.
The main result of the present paper is the
following theorem.

Theorem 1.1:

Let
\pi:\;M{\>\longrightarrow\>}X
be a Lagrangian
fibration on a compact hyperkähler manifold
of maximal holonomy, with
X={\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
.
Assume that the general fiber of the Lagrangian projection
\pi

is primitive (that is, not divisible) in integer homology.
Assume, moreover, that the fibers of
\pi
outside
of a codimension 2 subset in
X
are reduced.
Denote by
(M,I_{t})
the degenerate twistor deformation,
with
t\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
. Then for some
t_{0}\in H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
,
the Lagrangian projection
\pi:\;(M,I_{t_{0}}){\>\longrightarrow\>}X

admits a meromorphic section.

Proof:
6.3
.

1.2
C-symplectic structures and
degenerate twistor deformations

A superficially
similar result was proven in
[
BDV
]
.
Recall that a closed complex-valued 2-form
\Omega
on a smooth manifold

M
,
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}M=4n
, is called
C-symplectic
if

\Omega^{n}\wedge\overline{\Omega}^{n}
is non-degenerate,
and
\Omega^{n+1}=0
. By

[
BDV
, Proposition 2.13]
,
for any C-symplectic form
\Omega
on
M
there exists
a unique complex structure operator
I
such that

\Omega
is a holomorphic symplectic form on
(M,I)
.
The language of C-symplectic geometry is
convenient in context of deformation theory,
because it allows us to speak of deformations
of a holomorphically symplectic structure
without fixing the complex structure beforehand.
Using this language, one defines the degenerate twistor
deformation as follows.

Theorem 1.2:

Let
M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}X
be a Lagrangian fibration on a
holomorphically symplectic manifold
(X,\Omega)
, and

\eta\in\Lambda^{2}(X)
a closed

(2,0)+(1,1)
-form on the base. Then the forms

\Omega_{t}=\Omega+t\pi^{*}\eta
on
X
are C-symplectic, and
this deformation (called
the degenerate
twistor deformation
) preserves the Lagrangian
fibration and the base.

Proof:
[
BDV
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Using this observation and arguments of
linear-algebraic nature, Bogomolov, Deev and Verbitsky
proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3:

Let
\pi:\;M{\>\longrightarrow\>}X
be a Lagrangian fibration
on a holomorphic symplectic manifold
(M,\Omega)
,
and
S:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}M
a smooth (
C^{\infty}
) section.
Denote by
\eta\in\Lambda^{2}(X)
the form

-S^{*}(\Omega)
. Then
\Omega+\pi^{*}\eta
is
C-symplectic, and, moreover, the image
of
S
is holomorphic in the complex structure

I_{\eta}
induced by the C-symplectic form

\Omega+\pi^{*}\eta
.

Proof:
[
BDV
, Theorem 3.5]
.

Remark 1.4:
1.2

follows from
1.2

and an observation of N. Hitchin
(
[
Hit
, Proposition 1]
), who proved
that any submanifold
N\subset(M,\Omega)

of a holomorphically symplectic manifold,
which is Lagrangian with respect to
\operatorname{Re}\Omega

and
\operatorname{Im}\Omega
, is holomorphic Lagrangian.
Indeed, the restriction of
\Omega+\pi^{*}\eta
to
S(X)

vanishes by construction.

After
1.2

was obtained, it seemed that existence of
holomorphic sections of Lagrangian fibrations
is easily reachable by topological arguments.
After years of painstaking effort, this illusion shattered.
In fact, the topological arguments don’t work
well even for K3 surfaces. In
many years of trying we were unable to construct
a smooth section of an elliptic fibration on a
K3 surface without using arguments from algebraic
geometry.
The aim of this paper is to develop this algebro-geometric
approach further, in order to solve the topological
problem of constructing a smooth section.

1.3
Shafarevich-Tate deformations and the relative Albanese scheme

The Shafarevich-Tate group has its origins in number theory,
but in the 1980-ies it appeared in the context of complex algebraic
geometry and topology, in the works of R. Friedman and
J. Morgan on elliptic surfaces (
[
FM
]
).
Friedman and Morgan have defined the “analytic Tate-Shafarevich group”
as the first cohomology of the sheaf of fiberwise automorphisms
of an elliptic surface over its base. Using an explicit computation
of this group, they proved that any elliptic surface can be
deformed to a surface with a section.

The first attempt to generalize this notion to hyperkähler
manifolds appeared in a work of E. Markman
[
Mar
]
;
Markman produced a family of deformations of a hyperkähler
manifold of K3
[n]
-type with a Lagrangian fibration,
with the same fibers and the same base. Markman has explored
the Kählerness of the deformation, and shown that
in some situations the Kähler property is preserved.
His construction was later generalized to all hyperkähler
manifolds by A. Abasheva and V. Rogov, (
[
AR
,
A
]
) who also
generalized his result about the Kählerness.
Also, in
[
AR
, Section 6]
, the existence
of holomorphic sections of a degenerate twistor
deformation of a Lagrangian fibration was interpreted
as vanishing of a certain cohomology group related
to the Shafarevich-Tate group of this Larangian fibration.

The paper
[
V1
]
where the degenerate
twistor deformations were defined appeared at the same
time as Markman’s
[
Mar
]
; however, it
was unknown what is the precise relation between these
two classes of deformations. The Kählerness
of the degenerate twistor deformations was finally proven in

[
SV2
]
.

After the first version of this paper was published,
several important works dealing with the subject appeared.
In
[
Sa
]
, G. Saccá defined the relative Albanese variety,
a relative group scheme of fiberwise automorphisms of Lagrangian
fibration on a hyperkähler manifold without multiple fibers.
The Shafarevich-Tate
deformations defined by Markman and Abasheva-Rogov naturally
appear as cohomology of the sheaf of local sections
of this relative group scheme. Another construction
of the relative Albanese variety appeared in
[
Ki
]

by Y.-J. Kim, who used a multi-dimensional version of the
Néron model.

One of the results of G. Saccà
[
Sa
]
is especially relevant to
the present work. Using the minimal model program, she proves
that the relative Albanese variety of a hyperkähler manifold

M
admits a smooth hyperkähler compactification. This gives
a manifold with a Lagrangian fibration admitting a holomorphic
section; however, it is not clear whether this new manifold
is deformationally equivalent (or even diffeomorphic) to
M
.

After the third version of this paper was published in arxiv.org,
J. Kollár produced a version of our main result,
[
Ko3
]
.
In this paper, he shows that for any Abelian fiber space there exists
a Shafarevich-Tate twist which has a birational section.
G. Saccà conjectured that any
Shafarevich-Tate twist of a hyperkähler manifold
M
is deformational
equivalent to
M
(
[
Sa
, Remark 6.14]
),
and proved that it has the same rational cohomology
(
[
Sa
, Proposition 6.15]
).

1.4
Scheme of the proof

In this subsection, we give a rough outline of the arguments
used in this paper.

An integer homology class, generally speaking, cannot be represented
by a smooth submanifold. However, for any smooth manifold
M
,
a class
z\in H_{r}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
can be represented by
a submanifold when
r\leqslant 5
. Such results
were obtained for different dimensions and
classes of embeddings by R. Thom,
[
T
]
.
The strongest result
of this type is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.5:

For any orientable smooth
n
-manifold
V
, all elements
of the following integral homology groups can
be realized by orientable submanifolds:

H_{n-1}(V,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
H_{n-2}(V,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
H_{i}(V,{\mathbb{Z}})

for all
i\leqslant 5
.

Proof:
[
NT
, Theorem II.27]
.

In conjunction with the theory of Néron models,
Thom’s result is used to prove that, in the assumptions
of
1.1
,
for any Lagrangian fibration
\pi:\;M\to X
,
there exists a smooth 2-dimensional submanifold
S\subset M

such that the Lagrangian projection
\pi
projects

S
to
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\subset X
bijectively; in other words,
we obtain a smooth section of
\pi
over a holomorphic
line
C={\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\subset M
(
4.1
).

Taking a small neighbourhood of
C
, we can extend this
section to a small neighbourhood
U_{C}
, obtaining
a map
\varphi:\;U_{C}{\>\longrightarrow\>}\pi^{-1}(U_{C})
. Applying

1.2
, we immediately obtain
a degenerate twistor deformation of the Lagrangian projection

\pi^{-1}(U_{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}U_{C}
which makes this section holomorphic.

If we could extend this degenerate twistor deformation
to
M
, we would have been able to obtain a holomorphic section of

\pi
over a line
C\subset X
. However,
to extend the twistor deformation from
U_{C}
to
X={\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
, we need
to extend the closed
(2,0)+(1,1)
-form
\eta:=-\varphi^{*}(\Omega)

to a closed
(2,0)+(1,1)
-form on
{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
. Such an extension,
generally speaking, has an obstruction, which is
an element in
H^{1}({\cal O}_{U_{C}})

(Subsection
6.1
),
and this group is infinite dimensional, as follows from
a simple computation.

In Subsection
6.3
,
we compute this obstruction class, realizing it by a current of special type, which we call

a Dolbeault current,

and prove that it vanishes (
6.3
).

This allows us to construct a degenerate twistor
deformation
(M^{\prime},\Omega^{\prime})
and a holomorphic section
of
\pi:\;M^{\prime}{\>\longrightarrow\>}X
over a neighbourhood of a line
C\subset X={\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
.
We notice that
C
is an ample curve, that is, a curve with
an ample normal bundle. For an ample curve
C\subset X
,
any holomorphic map
\varphi:\;U_{C}{\>\longrightarrow\>}Z
from its neighbourhood
U_{C}

to a complex variety
Z
can
be extended to a meromorphic map
\widetilde{\varphi}:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}Z

(
5.3
), and this finishes the proof.

1.5
Multiple fibers and primitivity

The assumption of primitivity of the general fiber of Lagrangian
projection is clearly necessary for existence of
bimeromorphic section. In early versions of this
paper, we tried to treat it by using the notion of multiple
fibers in codimension 1: conjecturally, no Lagrangian
fibration on a hyperkähler manifold may have multiple
fibers in codimension 1. Later, we became aware of the results
of I. Hellmann
[
Hel
]
,
who constructed a Lagrangian family with multiple fibers
(in codimension bigger than 1). After analyzing this example,
it became obvious that we use the primitivity of the homology
class of the general fiber of the Lagrangian projection. In the current
version of this paper, we made this depencency more explicit.

We start by explaining the terminology, which in some of published literature
might depend on conventions.

Definition 1.6:
Let
\pi:\;M{\>\longrightarrow\>}X
be a proper holomorphic
map,
x\in X
a point,
F_{x}:=\pi^{-1}(x)

and
F_{i}
its irreducible component, with
(scheme-theoretical) multiplicity
\mu_{i}
.
Denote the greatest common divisor of
\mu_{i}

by
\mu
. A fiber is
multiple
if
\mu>1
.
A fiber
F_{x}
is
reduced

if
\mu_{i}=1
for all
i
.
A fiber
F_{x}
is
has a reduced component

if
\mu_{i}=1
for at least one
i
.

The following result should be well-known, but
we did not find it in the literature.

Lemma 1.7:

Let
f:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}Y
be a proper holomorphic
map of complex manifolds, and
y\in Y
any point.
Then
f
has a local section in a neighbourhood of
y

if and only if the fiber
f^{-1}(y)
has a component of
multiplicity 1.

Proof:
Assume that
f^{-1}(y)
has a reduced component
Z
,
and consider a transversal to
Z
local submanifold
U

of complimentary dimension intersecting
Z
in a smooth
point. Since
U\cap f^{-1}(y)=1
,
for any
y^{\prime}\in Y
sufficiently close to
y
,
we also have
U\cap f^{-1}(y^{\prime})=1
. The implicit
function theorem implies that the map

y^{\prime}\mapsto U\cap f^{-1}(y^{\prime})
is a holomorphic
map, defining a local section of
f
.

Conversely, assume that
f
has a local section

s:\;V\to X
in a neighbourhood
V\ni y
. Let
s(V)=U
;
it is a smooth local submanifold of
X
.
The intersection
U\cap f^{-1}(y)

has multiplicity 1, hence it belongs
to only one of the irreducible components
Z_{1}
of
f^{-1}(y)
.
However, unless
Z_{1}
is smooth in the intersection point

U\cap Z_{1}
, the intersection
U\cap Z_{1}
has multiplicity

>1
, which is impossible. Therefore,
Z_{1}
is a
smooth submanifold of
X
in a neighbourhood of the point

U\cap Z_{1}
, and the multiplicity of this component is 1.

The discriminant
of a proper holomorphic map

\pi
is the set of critical values of
\pi
.
By a theorem of Hwang and Oguiso (
[
HwO1
]
),
the discriminant of a Lagrangian fibration is
a divisor in
X
. We say that the fibers of

\pi

have a reduced component in codimension 1

if the fiber
\pi^{-1}(s)
is not multiple outside of a codimension
2 subset of
X
. It was conjectured that in codimension 1,
the fibers of any Lagrangian fibration are actually reduced.
In an earlier version of this paper, we
argued that the assumption of having a reduced component
for the fibers in codimension 1 is sufficient to have a
deformation admitting a bimeromorphic section.

It is clear that when a bimeromorphic section exists,
the cohomology class of a general fiber should be primitive,
and this rules out the existence of multiple fibers. We still hope
that the bimeromorphic section exists when the fibers of

\pi
have a reduced component in codimension 1.

Let
M\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
be a Lagrangian fibration on
a hyperkähler manifold, and
C\subset{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
a general line.
We use the primitivity of a fiber in Section
4
,
when we argue for the existence of a
2-dimensional submanifold
N
in
M_{C}:=\pi^{-1}(C)

such that
N\cap\pi^{-1}(pt)=1
, where
\cap

means the intersection pairing in
H_{*}(M_{C})
.
By R. Thom’s theorem (
1.4
),
the existence of such
N
is equivalent to
primitivity of
\pi^{*}([H])
in
H^{2}(M_{C})
, where
[H]

denotes the fundamental class of the hyperplane section.

Another place where the primitivity of the general fiber is used
is Subsection
6.3
. In the first
step of the proof of
6.3
,
we invoke the same argument as used in Section
4

to produce the section over a curve. Here we apply it to
extend this section to a piecewise smooth multisection of

\pi:\;M\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
, in order to prove that the
Dolbeault current associated with the section of

\pi
in a neighbourhood of a line vanishes.

In
[
KV1
]
, Kamenova and Verbitsky
have proven that
\pi^{*}([H])
is primitive in
H^{*}(M)
,
assuming that
\pi:\;M\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}
has no multiple fibers in
codimension 1. However, it does not imply that
\pi^{*}([H])
is primitive
in
H^{*}(M_{C})
. Indeed, in
[
Hel
]

I. Hellmann has shown that the Mukai system
M
of rank two and genus two
on a K3 surface admits a Lagrangian fibration
\pi:\;M\to X
with a
(very special) fiber of multiplicity 2. Therefore,

\pi
has no bimeromorphic sections. If
\pi^{*}([H])

were primitive in
H^{*}(M_{C})
,
1.1

would imply that
\pi
has no multiple fibers.

However, existence of a section of
\pi:\;M\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{n}

over a line is not sufficient. Indeed, in Hellmann’s
example, such a section exists (it exists for all
Beauville-Mukai Lagragian projections). For the benefit of
the reader, we explain this construction
now.

Let
M
be a K3 surface.
The
Beauville-Mukai manifold
is an irreducible

obtained as deformations of a line bundle of
degree
d
on a curve of genus

Fix a Beauville-Mukai system of all curves

, equipped with a line bundle of degree

Appropriately compactified, this gives a hyperkähler

and a Lagrangian fibration
\pi:\;X\to Y
,

Consider a rational pencil

. This

\sigma

takes a genus

In
[
Ko3
]
, J. Kollár proved a general
result which allows one to construct rational sections of
abelian fibrations. His result is valid for any abelian fibration
with numerically trivial relative canonical bundle (this case
covers all hyperkähler and Calabi-Yau manifolds).
Instead of degenerate twistor transform (which makes no
sense outside of holomorphic symplectic geometry),
Kollár works with the Tate-Shafarevich twist of an
Abelian fibration
\pi:\;M\to X
; it is a fibration with the same base
and the same fibers, obtained from a 1-cocycle on
X
with
coefficients in fiberwise automorphisms of
M
.
Let
F
be a general fiber of
\pi
and
d:=\dim M-\dim X
.
Kollár has shown that, given a homology class
\eta\in H_{2d}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})

such that
\eta\cap F=1
, there exists a Tate-Shafarevich twist

\pi_{\eta}:\;M_{\eta}\to X
and a rational section of this fibration.

The existence of such
\eta
implies non-existence of
multiple fibers. However, unlike the degenerate twistor
transform, the Tate-Shafarevich twist of
M
does not need
to be deformationally equivalent (or even diffeomorphic) to
M
.

It is worth comparing our main result to results
of
[
Ko3
]
. In some of the earlier versions, we assumed
that the Lagrangian fibration has reduced fibers in
codimension 1. In the current version, we drop this assumption,
following the idea of Kollár (
4.4
).
However, our final statement is different from the one in
[
Ko3
]
, because we use the
degenerate twistor deformation, which does not change
the deformational type of a manifold, and Kollár
uses the Tate-Shafarevich twist, for which we do not know
whether it preserves the deformation class.
On the other hand,
[
Ko3
]

applies to all fibrations with abelian fibers,
and our result works only for Lagrangian fibrations
on hyperkähler manifolds.

2
Preliminaries on holomorphic symplectic geometry

2.1
C-symplectic structures

In this subsection, we introduce the formalism of
C-symplectic structures, used further on to explore
the degenerate twistor deformations. We follow

[
BDV
]
and
[
SV1
]
.

Definition 2.1:
Let
V
be a real vector space. A
complex structure operator

on
V
is an element
I\in\operatorname{Hom}(V,V)
, satisfying
I^{2}=-\operatorname{Id}_{V}
.

Claim 2.2:
The eigenvalues of
I
are
\pm\sqrt{-1}\>
. Moreover,
I
is
diagonalizable over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Definition 2.3:
Let
V
be a vector space, and
I\in\operatorname{End}(V)
be a complex structure
operator. The eigenvalue decomposition
V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}=V^{1,0}\oplus V^{0,1}

is called the
Hodge decomposition
; here
I{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}_{V^{1,0}}}\right.}=\sqrt{-1}\>\operatorname{Id}
,
and
I{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}_{V^{0,1}}}\right.}=-\sqrt{-1}\>\operatorname{Id}
.

Remark 2.4:
One can reconstruct
I
from the space
V^{1,0}\subset V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}
.
Indeed, take
V^{0,1}=\overline{V^{1,0}}
, and let
I
act
on
V^{0,1}
as
\sqrt{-1}\>\operatorname{Id}
, and on
V^{0,1}
as
-\sqrt{-1}\>\operatorname{Id}
.
Since the operator
I\in\operatorname{End}(V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}})
commutes with
complex conjugation, it is
real
, that is, it preserves

V\subset V\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}
.

Definition 2.5:
An
almost complex structure
on a real
2n
-manifold

M
is an operator
I\in\operatorname{End}(TM)
satisfying
I^{2}=-\operatorname{Id}_{TM}
,
or equivalently, an
n
-dimensional subbundle

T^{1,0}M\subset TM\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbb{C}}
such that

T^{1,0}M\cap\overline{T^{1,0}M}=0
.
The almost complex structure is called
integrable

(and
M
is a
complex manifold
) if
T^{1,0}M

satisfies
[T^{1,0}M,T^{1,0}M]\subset T^{1,0}M
.

Remark 2.6:
Integrability implies existence of complex coordinates
(by the classical theorem of Newlander-Nirenberg).

Definition 2.7:
Let
(M,I)
be a complex manifold, and let
\Omega\in\Lambda^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})

be a differential form. We say that
\Omega
is
non-degenerate

if
\ker\Omega\cap T_{\mathbb{R}}M=0
. We say that it is

holomorphically symplectic
if it is non-degenerate,

d\Omega=0
, and
\Omega(IX,Y)=\sqrt{-1}\>\Omega(X,Y)
.

Remark 2.8:
The equation
\Omega(IX,Y)=\sqrt{-1}\>\Omega(X,Y)

means that
\Omega
is complex linear
with respect to the complex structure on
T_{\mathbb{R}}M

induced by
I
.

Remark 2.9:
Consider the Hodge decomposition

Equip

A complex variety is called
equidimensional

if all its irreducible components have the same dimension.
A
subvariety with multiplicities

with “mutiplicities” (positive integer numbers)
attached to each irreducible component.
We represent complex analytic cycles by a sum
\sum_{i}n_{i}Z_{i}
,

are its irreducible components, and

For every complex analytic cycle, its

\sum_{i}n_{i}[Z_{i}]
, where

is a complex
subvariety.

Fix an integer homology class

.
The Barlet space parametrizes complex analytic cycles
in

with a fixed homology class. The Barlet space
is equipped with the topology which is induced
by the Hausdorff topology on the set of subsets.
As shown in e.g.
[

]
, the Barlet space is equipped
with a complex structure, which is compatible
with the Hausdorff topology.

Definition 5.1:
The

is the moduli of equidimensional dimensional complex subspaces of

(“subspaces”
here means that we allow nilpotents in the structure sheaf).
The

is the forgetful map

from the Douady space to the Barlet space, with
multiplicity of each component of

given by the
rank of the structure sheaf of

in the general point of this component.

Example 5.2:
For
\dim Z=0
, the Douady space is the Hilbert space

of 0-dimensional subschemes, and the Hilbert-Chow morphism
is the natural projection
M^{[n]}{\>\longrightarrow\>}\operatorname{Sym}^{n}M
.

For the present purposes, the following theorem
is most important. Fix a Hermitian form
\omega
on

.
Recall that the volume
\operatorname{Vol}_{\omega}(Z):=\int_{Z}\omega^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Z}

is well defined for any complex subvariety, even singular,

and equal to the cohomology pairing
\langle[Z],\omega^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Z}\rangle

is closed (

For each complex analytic cycle

It is easy to see that the function

Theorem 5.3:

(E. Bishop’s compactness theorem)

Let

be a compact Hermitian manifold,
and

the Barlet space of all cycles homologous

Proof:
[

]
.

Remark 5.4:
This theorem also holds for pseudoholomorphic

).

5.2
Rationally connected varieties

We give a small introduction to rational connectedness,

Definition 5.5:
A compact complex manifold

Remark 5.6:
The condition “all irreducible

Definition 5.7:
Recall that a smooth rational curve in a complex manifold

\bigoplus_{i}{\cal O}(k_{i})

Claim 5.8:

Let

be a compact Kähler manifold containing
an ample rational curve. Then any

Proof:
This fact is well known in algebraic geometry

[
Ko1
]
). However, its proof is valid
for all Kähler manifolds.

Claim 5.9:

A compact Kähler
manifold which contains an ample rational curve
is rationally connected.

Proof:
See e.g.
[
H1
, Lemma 1.2]

Clearly,
rational connectedness is invariant under bimeromorphic
maps (
[

, Chapter IV, Proposition 3.3.3]
).
However, it is not that easy to define rational
connectedness for singular varieties. For example,
consider a rank 2 holomorphic vector bundle
B
over

,
where

is a smooth compact complex curve of genus
\geqslant 1
.
Let

be its projectivization. It is easy to check
that any rational curve in

is projected to a point in

hence

is not rationally connected. Now, assume that

, where

is ample. Then
the zero section of the projection

can be blown down, resulting in a variety

which can be also obtained as a compactification
of the affine cone

in

now all intersect in the singular point
of the origin of the cone, but the resolution of

is not

In the singular setting, definition of rational connectedness
can be given as follows. A variety is

if its resolution of singularities is rationally connected.
By
[

, Chapter IV, Proposition 3.3.3]

this is equivalent to the following definition.

Definition 5.10:

Let

be an irreducible compact complex variety
M

bimeromorphic to a compact Kähler manifold.
Then

is called
rationally (chain) connected

if for any proper subvariety

any two points in

can be connected by a sequence
of intersecting rational curves which intersect in points of

However, for our purposes, an easier criterion is sufficient.

Claim 5.11:

Let

be a compact complex variety, bimeromorphic to a
compact Kähler manifold, such that two generic

can be connected by
a smooth rational curve

. Then

is rationally connected.

Proof:
Let

be the Kähler resolution of singularities.
For

does not belong to the exceptional locus of

,
hence it can be lifted to

. Therefore,

To proceed further, we need to generalize the result of

Definition 6.1:
Let
\pi:\;M{\>\longrightarrow\>}X

By

6.2
Currents on complex manifolds

For a rich and detailed introduction to currents and their use in
algebraic geometry, see
[
Dem
]
.
We give a few preliminary definitions and cite basic results to facilitate
the proof of vanishing of the Dolbeault class
of a section, introduced earlier in Subsection

.
For all missing proofs, please see
[
Dem
]
.

Definition 6.6:
Let
F
be a Hermitian bundle with connection
\nabla
,
on a Riemannian manifold
M
with Levi-Civita connection, and

\|f\|_{C^{k}}:=\sup_{x\in M}\left(|f|+|\nabla f|+...+|\nabla^{k}f|\right)

the corresponding

-norm
defined on smooth sections with compact support.

Remark 6.7:
The
C^{k}
-topology is independent from the choice of a
connection and a metric (
[
Ham
]
).

Definition 6.8:
A
generalized function
is a functional on the top forms
with compact support, which is continuous in one of the
C^{i}
-topologies.

Definition 6.9:
A
k
-current
is a functional on the
(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}M-k)
-forms
with compact support, which is continuous in one of the
C^{i}
-topologies.

Remark 6.10:
Currents are forms with coefficients in generalized functions.

Definition 6.11:
The space of currents is equipped with the
weak topology

(a sequence of currents converges if it converges on all
forms with compact support). The space of currents with
this topology is a
Montel space
(barrelled,
locally convex, all bounded subsets are precompact).
Montel spaces are
reflexive
(the map
to its double dual with the strong topology is an isomorphism).

Definition 6.12:
On a complex manifold,
(p,q)
-currents
are
(p,q)
-forms with
coefficients in generalized functions.

Remark 6.13:
In the literature, this is sometimes called
“
(n-p,n-q)
-currents”; we follow the conventions from

[
Dem
]
.

Claim 6.14:
De Rham differential is continuous on currents, and
the Poincaré lemma holds for currents, as well as the
Poincaré-Dolbeault-Grothendieck lemma. Hence,
the cohomology of currents are the same
as cohomology of smooth forms,
and the
\overline{\partial}
-cohomology groups are the same as for forms.

Definition 6.15:
Let
f:\;X{\>\longrightarrow\>}Y
be a proper holomorphic map of complex manifolds,

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Y+k
, and
\alpha
a
(p,q)
-current on
X
.
Define the
pushforward

f_{*}\alpha
using

\langle f_{*}\alpha,\tau\rangle:=\langle\alpha,f^{*}\tau\rangle
,
where
\tau
is any test-form (a smooth differential form with compact
support). Then
f_{*}\alpha
is a

(p-k,q-k)
-current. One should think of

as
fiberwise integration.

Remark 6.16:
Clearly,
df_{*}\alpha=f_{*}d\alpha
for any smooth map,

\partial f_{*}\alpha=f_{*}\partial\alpha
for a holomorphic map, and so on.

Remark 6.17:
Pullbacks of currents are (generally speaking) not well-defined.

Using currents, one can give a new proof of
the following result of
[

]

Theorem 6.19:

Let

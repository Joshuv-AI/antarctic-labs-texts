Quantitative Merging for Time Inhomogeneous Markov Chains in Non-Decreasing Environments via Functional Inequalities
Nordine Anis Moumeni

Abstract
We study time-inhomogeneous Markov chains to obtain quantitative results on their asymptotic behavior. We use Poincaré, Nash, and logarithmic-Sobolev inequalities. We assume that our Markov chain admits a finite invariant measure at each time and that the sequence of these invariant measures is non-decreasing. We deduce quantitative bounds on the merging time of the distributions for the chain started at two arbitrary points and we illustrate these new results with examples.



Keywords.
Merging; Markov chains; Time-inhomogeneous; Functional inequalities; Non-decreasing environments.

Mathematics Subject Classification.
NA.

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and Background

In this article, we are interested in studying quantitatively discrete time, time-inhomogeneous Markov chains. The problem of obtaining accurate estimates for the time to reach equilibrium for a Markov chain within a time-homogeneous context is classical and has been extensively studied in the literature, see
[
17
]
. On the other hand, adapting standard techniques from the time-homogenous to the time-inhomogenous context turns out to be far from straightforward, and indeed time inhomogeneity may produce unexpected behaviors, as shown in
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
6
]
.

First note that, unlike a time-homogeneous and aperiodic Markov chain, a time-inhomogenous Markov chain may not converge in law as time tends to infinity. For time-homogenous Markov chains, studying mixing properties or the convergence to equilibrium means investigating the rate of convergence of the laws towards a reference target measure. In the time-inhomogeneous context, there is generally no time-independent reference measure. However, it still makes sense to ask how long one should wait until the law at time
t
does not depend much on the initial state. Following
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
, this observation leads us to the definition of "merging times".

Let
V
be a finite or infinite countable set and
(K_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
a family of Markov transition operators defined on
V
. Let
\mu_{0}
be a probability measure on
V
. We denote with
(\Omega\,,\,\mathcal{G}\,,\,\mathbb{P})
a probability space and such that
(X_{t}^{\mu_{0}})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
V
-valued discrete-time Markov chain driven by
(K_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}}
with initial law
\mu_{0}
defined by:

\mathcal{L}(X_{0}^{\mu_{0}})=\mu_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\forall t\geq 0\,,\,\forall z\in V\,,\quad\mathbb{P}(X_{t+1}^{\mu_{0}}=z\mid X_{t}^{\mu_{0}})=K_{t+1}(X_{t}^{\mu_{0}},z)\,.

For
t\geq 0
, let
\mu_{t}^{\mu_{0}}
be the law of
X_{t}^{\mu_{0}}
. When
\mu_{0}
is the Dirac mass at point
x
, we use the short hand notation
\mu_{t}^{x}
instead of
\mu_{t}^{\mu_{0}}
. Furthermore, we denote for
0\leq s<t
,

K_{s,t}:=K_{s+1}...K_{t}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{0,t}=K_{1}...K_{t}\,.

Merging corresponds to the property of forgetting the initial law of the Markov chain. To quantify this property, we control the distance between the distributions after
t
steps of two chains driven by the same Markov transition operators and started at two distinct initial conditions. We work with the total variation distance and when
V
is finite, it is interesting to consider the separation distance. We recall that these distances are defined as:

\forall(\mu,\nu)\,,\quad\,d_{TV}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}|\mu(x)-\nu(x)|\quad\text{and}\quad s(\mu,\nu)=\max_{x\in V}\left\{1-\frac{\mu(x)}{\nu(x)}\right\}\,.

Therefore, we aim to find bounds as precise as possible on the following quantities for
(x,y)\in V
and
t\geq 1
:

d_{TV}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y})\quad\text{or}\quad s(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y})\,.

Imitating
[
13
]
, we define the following quantities:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\eta\in(0,1)
and
x,y
in
V
. We define the
\eta
-merging time related to
x,y
and the relative-sup
\eta
-merging time related to
x,y
respectively by:

T_{\text{mer}}(x,y,\eta)=\inf\left\{t\geq 1\mid d_{TV}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y})\leq\eta\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{\text{mer}}^{\infty}(x,y,\eta)=\inf\left\{t\geq 1\mid s(\mu_{t}^{x}\mid\mu_{t}^{y})\leq\eta\right\}\,.

We may want to bound the merging uniformly over
x,y
. Hence, we consider the
\eta
-merging time and the relative-sup
\eta
-merging time defined by:

T_{\text{mer}}(\eta)=\max\left\{T_{\text{mer}}(x,y,\eta)\mid(x,y)\in V\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{\text{mer}}^{\infty}(\eta)=\max\left\{T_{\text{mer}}^{\infty}(x,y,\eta)\mid(x,y)\in V\right\}\,.

Note that the merging time coincides with the classical mixing time in the time-homogeneous context up to a constant.

In order to quantify merging, we shall rely on several functional inequalities. We start discussing Poincaré inequalities. First, given a Markov transition operator
K
with stationary probability
\tilde{\pi}
, we define the following operators:

•

K^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
K
from
\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}\right)
to
\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}\right)
,

•

Q:=K^{*}K
from
\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}\right)
to
\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}\right)
the multiplicative symmetrisation.

We recall the definition of the variance linked to
\tilde{\pi}
and the Dirichlet form linked to the pair
(Q,\tilde{\pi})
:

\displaystyle\text{ for all }f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,,\quad\operatorname{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V}f^{2}(x)\tilde{\pi}(x)-\left(\sum_{x\in V}f(x)\tilde{\pi}(x)\right)^{2}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{Q,\tilde{\pi}}(f,f)=\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\in V}

\displaystyle(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\tilde{\pi}(x)Q(x,y)\,.

The Poincaré constant of
Q
is defined as follows:

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\gamma(Q)\geq 0
be the optimal constant in the inequality:

\quad\text{ for all }f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,,\quad\gamma\operatorname{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(f)\leq\mathcal{E}_{Q,\tilde{\pi}}(f,f)\,.

Since
Q
is reversible, note that
1-\gamma(Q)
is an eigenvalue of
Q
.

It is then immediate to estimate the merging of a time-homogeneous Markov chain using this constant, see Corollary 2.1.5 in
[

]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
K
be an aperiodic and irreducible Markov transition operator with invariant probability
\tilde{\pi}
. Let
Q
be the multiplicative symmetrisation of
K
from
\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}\right)
to
\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}\right)
and
\gamma
be
\gamma(Q)
, then for all
(x,y)\in V

d_{TV}\left(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y}\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\pi}(x)}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\pi}(y)}}\right)(1-\gamma)^{t/2}.

Therefore,

\forall\eta\in(0,1)\,,\quad T_{\text{mer}}(x,y,\eta)\leq\frac{2}{\gamma}\left(\log(1/\eta)+\log\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\pi}(x)}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\pi}(y)}}\right)\right)\,.

Note that when the Markov transition operators share the same invariant probability, Theorem
1.3
still holds, see
[
18
]
.

A classical example of an application of Theorem
1.3
is as follows.

Consider a Markov transition operator
K^{N}
on
\{0,\ldots,N\}
such that:

•

K^{N}(x,y)\in[1/4,3/4]
if
|x-y|\leq 1
,

•

K^{N}
has an invariant probability measure
\tilde{\pi}^{N}
satisfying:

\forall x\in V_{N}\,,\,\,1/4\leq(N+1)\tilde{\pi}^{N}(x)\leq 4
.

Then, a comparison argument with the symmetric nearest neighbor random walk on
\{0,\ldots,N\}
implies that
\gamma((K^{N})^{*}K^{N})
is of order
1/N^{2}
, see Example
2.1
.

Therefore, an immediate application of Theorem
1.3
gives that there exists
\kappa>0
a constant independent of
N
such that:

\forall\eta\in(0,1)\,,\quad T_{\text{mer}}^{N}(\eta)\leq\kappa N^{2}\left(\log(1/\eta)+\log(N)\right)\,.

We now turn to the time-inhomogeneous context. For each
t\geq 1
, let
\tilde{\pi}_{t}
denote an invariant probability measure for
K_{t}
and
\gamma_{t}
be the Poincaré constant. Assume that
\gamma>0
is a common lower bound for all the
\gamma_{t}
’s. One might naively expect a similar bound as in Theorem
1.3
. However, R. Huang provides a counter-example in
[
6
]
, see the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
V_{N}
be
\{0,\ldots,N\}
. There exists
Q_{N}:=(K^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a sequence of nearest-neighbor Markov transition operators on
V_{N}
satisfying:

•

for
t\geq 1
,
K^{N}_{t}(x,y)\in[1/4,3/4]
if
|x-y|\leq 1
,

•

for
t\geq 1
,
K^{N}_{t}
has an invariant probability measure
\tilde{\pi}^{N}_{t}
satisfying:

1/4\leq(N+1)\tilde{\pi}^{N}_{t}(x)\leq 4,
for all
x
in
V_{N}
.

Then, for all
t\geq 1
, the Poincaré constant
\gamma((K_{t}^{N})^{*}K_{t}^{N})
is of order
\frac{1}{N^{2}}
but however,

\underset{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\liminf}\frac{\log\left(T^{N}_{\text{mer}}(0,N,1/2)\right)}{N}>0\,.

This counterexample illustrates the difficulty in grasping the merging time. Indeed, in this case, we assume bounds on the spectral gaps, kernels, and invariant probabilities that are uniform both with respect to time and space. Finally, to each transition operator, one can associate a set of conductances with values between
1
and
2
. However, despite all this and contrary to intuition, the merging time is not polynomial but exponential in
N
. In conclusion, the naive extension of Theorem
1.3
to the time-inhomogeneous context does not work. It is therefore necessary to impose hypotheses that exclude this type of case.

One approach is with
c
-stablity. In a series of papers, Saloff-Coste and Zúñiga introduce the notion of
c
-stability for time-inhomogeneous Markov chains. Under this assumption, one controls the fluctuations of the invariant probabilities. Checking
c
-stability is a challenging task, as noted by the authors. Indeed, the
c
-stability property involves the laws that are unknown and need to be estimated. Examples of time-inhomogeneous Markov chains for which it was possible to check
c
-stability are small perturbations of time-homogeneous Markov chains that furthermore satisfy some symmetries. Under the assumption of
c
-stability, a minor modification of Theorem
1.3
holds. Its proof relies on singular values theory.

In this article, we work under a different assumption: the existence of a non-decreasing finite environment.

Remark 1.5

(Notation)
.

When
\pi
is a finite measure, we denote by
\tilde{\pi}
the probability measure given by
\pi
, that is to say
\tilde{\pi}:=\frac{\pi}{\pi(V)}
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of Markov transition operators and let
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive measures. We say that
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a non-decreasing environment when the sequence
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
satisfies the following:

•

for all
t\geq 1,\pi_{t}K_{t}=\pi_{t}
,

•

for all
t\geq 1,\pi_{t}(V)<+\infty
,

•

for all
t\geq 1,
for all
x\in V\,,\,\pi_{t+1}(x)\geq\pi_{t}(x)
.

One of the advantages of a non-decreasing finite environment is the following:

Remark 1.7
.

Let
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
be a non-decreasing environment. Then, we have:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\,\forall p\in[1,+\infty]\,,\quad\ell^{p}(\pi_{t+1})\subset\ell^{p}(\pi_{t})\,.

(1)

And, for all
t\geq 1
, the operator
K_{t}:\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})\to\ell^{2}(\pi_{t-1})
is well-defined (we set
\pi_{0}=\pi_{1}
).
Inclusion
1
may be a natural property to be expected.

Note that in Definition
1.6
, the
\pi_{t}
’s are measures but not necessarily probability measures. Indeed, if they were all probability measures then they would all be the same.

Moreover, when a non-decreasing finite environment exists, an infinite number of non-decreasing finite environments exists. Indeed, let
(a_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a non-decreasing sequence of positive real numbers, if
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a finite non decreasing environment then,
\{(K_{t},a_{t}\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is also a finite non decreasing environment.

In the examples, see Section
2
, we will leverage the availability of multiple choices for invariant measures and play with these different options to, for instance, facilitate the study of the Poincaré constants or to ensure that total masses are not too large.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of irreducible aperiodic Markov transition operators and
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a sequence of finite measures. Assume that the sequence
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a finite non-decreasing environment. Let
\gamma_{t}=\gamma(K_{t}^{*}K_{t})
be the Poincaré constant associated to
K_{t}^{*}K_{t}
. We recall that
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
denotes the probability measure
\pi_{1}()/\pi_{1}(V)

Then, for all

,

\forall t\geq 1\,,\,d_{TV}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\pi}_{1}(x)}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\pi}_{1}(y)}}\right)\prod_{s=1}^{t}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{s}}\,.

(2)

Therefore, for all
\eta\in(0,1)
,

T_{\text{mer}}(x,y,\eta)\leq\min\left\{t\geq 1\mid\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\pi}_{1}(x)}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\pi}_{1}(y)}}\right)\prod_{s=1}^{t}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{s}}\leq\eta\right\}\,.

(3)

We adopt the convention:

\min\emptyset=+\infty\,.

Thus, when the minimum in

Remark 1.10
.

Note that Estimate

To ensure the assumption of a finite non-decreasing environment, the challenge lies in computing explicit invariant probability measures and our ability to compare them. Roughly speaking, there is a competition between the ratio of the total masses and the product of eigenvalues to achieve a reasonably small merging time. When the ratio of the total masses is too large, the right-hand side of Equation
2
might not tend to
0
, see Example
2.3
. In fact, the existence of a non-decreasing environment does not imply that merging will occur, see Example
2.3
. Our Estimate
3
is not always sharp, see Example
2.1
.

If
V
is a finite set and
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a family of irreducible Markov transition operators, there always exists a family of finite measures
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
such that
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a finite non-decreasing environment. However, the ratio of the total masses can increase very fast and this deteriorates our estimate.

A class of examples where the hypothesis on the existence of a finite non-decreasing environment is easy to check is Markov chains on electrical networks, see
[
9
]
for further details. Then, the sum of the conductances at a given vertex provides an explicit invariant measure. We give a definition/proposition:

Definition 1.11
.

Let
E
be a subset of
V\times V
such that if
(x,y)\in E
, then,
(y,x)\in E
. We name
E
a set of edges. A set of conductances on
E
is a function
c:E\mapsto\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

\forall(x,y)\in E\,,\quad c(x,y)\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad c(x,y)=c(y,x)\,.

We associate to the set of conductances
c
the Markov transition operator
P
defined by:

\forall(x,y)\in V\,,\quad P(x,y)=\frac{c(x,y)}{\sum_{z\in V}c(x,z)}\,.

We add the following assumption:

\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\in V}c(x,y)<+\infty\,.

(4)

Let
\pi
be the positive measure defined by:

\forall x\in V\,,\quad\pi(x)=\sum_{y\in V}c(x,y)\,.

(5)

Assumption (
4
) implies that
\pi
is a finite measure.
Note that
P
is reversible with respect to
\pi
. Indeed,

\forall(x,y)\in V\,,\quad\pi(x)P(x,y)=c(x,y)=c(y,x)=\pi(y)P(y,x)\,.

It implies that
\pi
is a finite invariant distribution for
P
. The pair
(P,\pi)
induced by
c
is called an electrical network.

Remark 1.12
.

When the Markov transition operator
P
is self-adjoint from
\ell^{2}
to
\ell^{2}
with respect to some measure then, there exists a set of conductances
c
so that:

\forall(x,y)\in V\,,\quad P(x,y)=\frac{c(x,y)}{\sum_{z\in V}c(x,z)}\,.

Note that multiple choices of set of conductances can be associated with
P
. More precisely, if
c
is a set of conductances associated with
P
, then for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
, the set of conductances
\lambda c
is also associated with
P
.

A sequence of electrical networks with non-decreasing conductances forms a non-decreasing environment. A typical example of a time-inhomogeneous Markov with a non-increasing environment is as follows: we fix a set of edges
E
, a reference set of conductances
c
and a positive constant
M>0
. Consider a sequence of sets of conductances sets
(c_{t})_{t\geq 1}
such that:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\,\forall(x,y)\in E\,,\quad c(x,y)\leq c_{t}(x,y)\leq Mc(x,y)\,.

For
t\geq 1
, let
P_{t}
be the Markov transition operator associated with
c_{t}
, see Definition
1.11
. At time
t-1
, we apply the operator
K_{t}:=\frac{1}{2}(I+P_{t})
. We will see that this type of example has several advantages, see Example
2.1
. The first one is that the sequence
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
given by (
5
) gives a finite non-decreasing environment. The second advantage is, for example, that if the operator associated with
c
satisfies a Poincaré condition, then
K_{t}
does as well, with a Poincaré constant comparable to the original one.

One method for generalizing electrical networks is to consider graphs with cycles, see
[
7
]
and
[
10
]
for a presentation of cycles.

Additionally, note that we may construct examples of sequences of electrical networks where the
\pi_{t}
’s are non-decreasing but the conductances themselves are not monotonous, see Section
2.8
.

Effective tools for studying
\ell^{2}
-merging times and complementary to spectral techniques are advanced functional inequalities: Nash inequalities and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. We refer to the lecture notes from St. Flour by Saloff-Coste
[
17
]
for details on the use of these functional inequalities in the context of time-homogeneous Markov chains with a finite state space.
Saloff-Coste and Zúñiga successfully applied these functional inequalities under the
c
-stability assumptions to estimate the merging time of a time-inhomogeneous chain.
Now, we show the results obtained using Nash and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. First, we need to define a relative point-wise distance in relation to a probability measure.

Definition 1.13
.

Let
V
be a finite set. Fix
\tilde{\pi}
a probability measure on
V
. Assume that for all
x\in V
,
\tilde{\pi}(x)>0
. We define the relative point-wise distance in relation to
\tilde{\pi}
by:

\text{for all probability measures }(\mu,\nu)\text{ on }V\,,\quad s_{\infty}(\mu\,,\,\nu\mid\tilde{\pi})=\max\left\{\left|\frac{\mu(x)-\nu(x)}{\tilde{\pi}(x)}\right|\mid x\in V\right\}\,.

Definition 1.14
.

Let
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
be a finite non-decreasing environment.
We define a dynamic merging-time in relation to the sequence
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
by:

\forall\eta\in(0,1)\,,\quad T_{\text{mer}}^{\infty}(\eta\,,(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1})=\inf\left\{t\geq 1\mid\forall(x,y)\in V\,,\,s_{\infty}(\mu_{t}^{x}\,,\,\mu_{t}^{y}\mid\tilde{\pi}_{t})\leq\eta\right\}\,.

Lemma 1.15
.

Let
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
be a finite non-decreasing environment. For each
t\geq 1
, we recall that
\tilde{\pi}_{t}
denotes
\pi_{t}/\pi_{t}(V)
. For
(x,y)
in
V
, the sequence
\left(\frac{1}{\pi_{t}(V)}s_{\infty}(\mu_{t}^{x}\,,\,\mu_{t}^{y}\mid\tilde{\pi}_{t})\right)_{t\geq 1}
is non-increasing.

We recall that a Nash inequality is defined as follows:

Definition 1.16
.

Let
T\geq 1
,
Q
be a Markov transition operator, and
\tilde{\pi}
a probability measure. Let
C,D>0
. We say that the pair
(Q,\tilde{\pi})
satisfies the
\mathcal{N}(C,D,T)
Nash inequality if the following holds:

\forall f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,,\quad\|f\|_{\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi})}^{2+1/D}\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}_{Q,\tilde{\pi}}(f,f)+\frac{1}{T}\|f\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}\right)}^{2}\right)\|f\|_{\ell^{1}(\tilde{\pi})}^{1/D}\,.

Under this assumption, we have:

Theorem 1.17
.

Let
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of irreducible aperiodic Markov transition operators on a finite set
V
and
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a sequence of positive measures.
Assume that the sequence
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a finite non-decreasing environment. For
t\geq 1
, let
Q_{t}=K_{t}^{*}K_{t}
and
\gamma_{t}
be
\gamma(Q_{t})
. Let
T\geq 1
and
C,D>0
. Assume that for all
1\leq t\leq T
, the pair
(Q_{t},\tilde{\pi}_{t})
satisfies the
\mathcal{N}(C,D,T)
Nash inequality. Assume
\pi_{1}(V)\geq 1
and define
B
by:

B=B(D,T)=2^{1/D}(1+1/T)(1+[4D])\,.

Then, for all
x,y\in V
,

\forall r\leq T\,,\,\forall t\geq r\,,\quad d_{TV}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y})\leq 2\sqrt{\pi_{t}(V)}\left(\frac{4CB}{r+1}\right)^{D}\prod_{l=r+1}^{t}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{l}}\,.

(6)

And, for all
\eta\in(0,1)
,

T_{\text{mer}}(\eta)\leq\min\left\{t\geq 0\mid\exists\,r\leq T\,,\,t-r\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad 2\sqrt{\pi_{t}(V)}\left(\frac{4CB}{r+1}\right)^{D}\prod_{l=r+1}^{t}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{l}}\leq\eta\right\}\,.

Moreover, we have for
r\leq T
and
t=2r+u,

\max\left\{s_{\infty}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y}\mid\tilde{\pi}_{t})\mid(x,y)\in V\right\}\leq 8\pi_{t}(V)\left(\frac{4CB}{r+1}\right)^{2D}\prod_{l=r+1}^{t-r}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{l}}\,.

(7)

So, for all
\eta\in(0,1)
,

T_{\text{mer}}^{\infty}(\eta\,,(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1})\leq\inf\left\{t\geq 1\mid 8\pi_{t}(V)\left(\frac{4CB}{r+1}\right)^{2D}\prod_{l=r+1}^{t-r}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{l}}\leq\eta\right\}\,.

We state a bound on the merging time in finite non-decreasing environments that satisfy a logarithmic Sobolev inequality at each time. We recall the expression of the entropy:

Definition 1.19
.

Let
\tilde{\pi}
be a probability measure in
V
. Let
f
be a non-negative function in
\ell^{1}(\tilde{\pi})
. We define the entropy of
f
with respect to
\tilde{\pi}
by:

\mathcal{L}(f\mid\tilde{\pi}):=\sum_{x\in V}f(x)\log\left(\frac{f(x)}{\tilde{\pi}(f)}\right)\tilde{\pi}(x)\,.

Note that Jensen inequality implies that
\mathcal{L}(f|\tilde{\pi})
is non-negative.

The definition of the logarithmic Sobolev constant is similar to the one of the Poincaré constant except that the variance is replaced by the entropy.

Definition 1.20
.

The standard logarithmic Sobolev constant of
Q
is the constant

\alpha(Q)\geq 0
defined as the optimal constant in the inequality:

\forall f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,,\quad f-\text{non constant and non-negative}\,,\quad\frac{\mathcal{E}_{Q,\tilde{\pi}}(f,f)}{\mathcal{L}(f^{2}\mid\tilde{\pi})}\geq\alpha\,.

Thanks to hypercontractivity, we can control the merging times for finite non-decreasing environments with the following:

Theorem 1.21
.

Let
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of irreducible aperiodic Markov transitions operators and
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a sequence of positive measures. Assume that the sequence
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a finite non-decreasing environment. For each
t\geq 1
, let
\gamma_{t}=\gamma(K_{t}^{*}K_{t})
be the Poincaré constant associated to
K_{t}^{*}K_{t}
and
\alpha_{t}
be the logarithmic Sobolev constant
\alpha(K_{t}^{*}K_{t})
. For all
s\geq 1
and
z\in V
, we define:

q_{s}=2\prod_{u=1}^{s}(1+\alpha_{u})\quad\text{and}\quad r_{z}=\min\left\{s\geq 1\mid\log(q_{s})\geq\log\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{\pi}_{1}(z)}\right)\right)\right\}\,.

For
x,y\in V
, let
r(x,y)=\max(r_{x},r_{y})
. Then,

\forall t\geq r(x,y)\,,\quad d_{TV}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y})\leq e\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}}\prod_{l=r+1}^{t}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{l}}\,.

(8)

Therefore, for all
\eta\in(0,1)
,

T_{\text{mer}}(x,y,\eta)\leq r(x,y)+\min\left\{u\geq 1\mid e\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}}\prod_{l=r+1}^{r+u}\sqrt{1-\gamma_{l}}\leq\eta\right\}\,.

(9)

Lemma 1.22
.

Let
Q
be a Markov transition operator. Assume that
Q
is aperiodic then, the logarithmic Sobolev constant
\alpha(Q)
is non-negative. And, the logarithmic Sobolev constant
\alpha(Q)
in Definition
1.20
and the Poincaré constant
\gamma(Q)
satisfy:

2\alpha(Q)\leq 1-\gamma(Q)\,.

When the logarithmic Sobolev constant is strictly positive, the Poincaré constant is not 1.

When the state set is finite and when the logarithmic Sobolev constants are uniformly bounded from below, one can control the relative-sup merging time:

Theorem 1.23
.

Let
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of irreducible aperiodic Markov transition operators and
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a sequence of positive measures. Assume that the sequence
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a finite non-decreasing environment. Assume that
V
is finite. Let
\gamma_{t}=\gamma(K_{t}^{*}K_{t})
be the Poincaré constant associated to
K_{t}^{*}K_{t}
, let
\alpha_{t}
and
\hat{\alpha}_{t}
be the logarithmic Sobolev constant
\alpha(K_{t}^{*}K_{t})
and
\alpha(K_{t}K_{t}^{*})
respectively. Assume that for all
t\geq 1
, the constants
\alpha_{t}
and
\hat{\alpha}_{t}
are bounded from below by
\alpha>0
. Let
\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\#}=\min\left\{\tilde{\pi}_{t}(x)\mid x\in V\right\}
. And, assume that it exists
\rho>0
such that:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\#}\geq\rho\,.

Define
r
by:

r=\left(\frac{\log\left(\frac{\log(1/\rho)}{2}\right)}{\log(1+\alpha)}\right)+1\,.

Let
u\geq 0
and set
t=2r+u
. Then, we find:

\max\left\{s_{\infty}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y}\mid\tilde{\pi}_{t})\mid(x,y)\in V\right\}\leq 4e^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{t-r}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}\prod_{l=r+1}^{r+u}1-\gamma_{r}}\,.

So, for all
\eta\in(0,1)
,

T_{\text{mer}}^{\infty}(\eta\,,(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1})\leq\inf\left\{u+2r\mid u\geq 1\,,\,4e^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{r+u}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}\prod_{l=r+1}^{r+u}1-\gamma_{r}}\leq\eta\right\}\,.

Remark 1.24
.

The concept of monotonous invariant measures already appeared in the literature to deal with issues of recurrence versus transience, see
[
1
]
.

In
[
4
]
, the idea of non-decreasing invariant measure is highlighted. Dembo, Huang, and Zheng establish two-sided Gaussian estimates for the transition kernel of a time-inhomogeneous Markov chain on a non-decreasing sequence of electrical networks satisfying a suitable uniform Poincaré inequality and the volume doubling property.

In our paper, in the proofs, we have succeeded in adapting ideas of
[
4
]
such as backward induction in the Poincaré case. However, unlike in
[
4
]
, some extra care is needed because we have to center our test functions with respect to measures that evolve over time.

1.2
Outline

This article is divided into three parts. Section
1
presents the motivations, background, and main theorems. In Definition
1.6
, we introduce our main hypothesis: the existence of a non-decreasing finite environment and a new merging time related to a sequence of measures, see Definition
1.14
. Each theorem extends classical functional inequalities to the non-decreasing finite environment hypothesis. The first theorem, Theorem
1.8
, assumes a Poincaré condition at each time, in addition to the non-decreasing finite environment hypothesis, and states control of the merging time in total variation distance. The second theorem, Theorem
1.17
, assumes a Nash inequality at each instance and states control of the merging time in total variation distance and yields a control on the dynamic merging-time. Finally, Theorem
1.21
assumes a Logarithmic Sobolev inequality at each time and similarly states control of the merging time in total variation distance and yields a control on the dynamic merging-time when the set
V
is finite with Theorem
1.23
. Theorems
1.8
,
1.17
and
1.23
are new tools for understanding finite Markov chains in the inhomogeneous time context.

Section
2
deals with examples and investigates five instances within the context of electrical networks. The first example serves an educational purpose, while the subsequent two illustrate the application of Theorem
1.8
. The final two examples are within the classical framework for the application of Nash inequalities and Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities.

The third section delves into the proofs and is divided into four sub-sections. The first one revisits the preliminaries and classical definitions from analysis and probability theory. The second part focuses on proving Theorem
3.6
and deals with the Poincaré constants. It includes a crucial proposition regarding the duality of operators within the inhomogeneous time context. The third segment contains the proof of Theorem
1.17
, reiterating the definition of a Nash inequality. Finally, in the fourth subsection, we adopt an entropic approach and prove Theorem
1.21
and
1.23
.

Let

•

for all

,

•

For
t\geq 1

P_{t}
be the Markov transition operator on

associated to the set of conductances
c_{t}
and let
\pi_{t}
be its reversible measure, see Definition
1.11
. We study the Markov transition operators
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
defined by:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad K_{t}=\frac{1}{2}(I+P_{t})\,.

For each
t\geq 1
, the operators
P_{t}
and
K_{t}
are reversible with respect to
\pi_{t}
. Let
\gamma_{t}
be the Poincaré constant
\gamma(K^{*}_{t}K_{t})
. We study the
K_{t}
’s instead of the
P_{t}
’s to compare Dirichlet forms.

We give a lower bound on the Poincaré constants under the following assumption:

\exists M>0\,,\forall t\geq 1,\forall x\in V_{N}\,,\quad\sum_{y\in V_{N}}c_{t}(x,y)\leq M\,.

(10)

Let
P
denote the Markov transition operator associated with the set of conductances
c_{0}
, and let
u
be its reversible measure. The operator
P
corresponds to the Markov transition operator of the simple random walk. Denote with
\gamma_{u}
the constant
\gamma(P)
. It is well known, see
[
8
]
that:

\exists\kappa>0\,,\quad\gamma_{u}=\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\,.

We define for
\pi
a finite measure on
V_{N}
:

\forall f:V_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,,\quad\text{Var}_{\pi}(f):=\pi(V_{N})\text{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(f)\,.

(11)

Note that:

\gamma_{u}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\ell^{2}(u)\\
f\text{ non-constant}\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{\mathcal{E}_{P,u}(f)}{\text{Var}_{u}(f)}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad\gamma_{t}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\\
f\text{ non-constant}\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{\mathcal{E}_{K_{t}^{*}K_{t},\pi_{t}}(f)}{\text{Var}_{\pi_{t}}(f)}\right\}\,.

We use a comparison method in order to estimate the spectral gap of
K^{*}_{t}K_{t}
. We get:

\displaystyle\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\,,\,\mathcal{E}_{P,u}(f,f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}u(x)P(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\,,\,|x-y|=1}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\neq y}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}c_{t}(x,y)\quad\text{since }c_{t}(x,y)\geq\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|=1}\,,

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\neq y}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\pi_{t}(x)P_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{P_{t},\pi_{t}}(f,f)\leq 2\mathcal{E}_{K_{t},\pi_{t}}(f,f)\,.

Note that
\forall x\in V_{N}\,,\,\forall t\geq 1\,,\,K_{t}^{*}(x,x)=K_{t}(x,x)\geq 1/2
, and then, we get:

\displaystyle\forall y\in V\,,\quad\quad\pi_{t}(x)K_{t}^{*}K_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\pi_{t}(x)K_{t}^{*}(x,x)K_{t}(x,y)+\pi_{t}(x)K_{t}^{*}(x,y)K_{t}(y,y)\,,

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\pi_{t}(y)K_{t}(y,x)+\pi_{t}(x)K_{t}^{*}(x,y)\right)\,.

We finally find:

\mathcal{E}_{K_{t}^{*}K_{t},\pi_{t}}(f,f)\geq\mathcal{E}_{K_{t},\pi_{t}}(f,f)\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_{P_{t},\pi_{t}}(f,f)\,.

Next, we compare variances:

\displaystyle\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\,,\quad\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_{t}}(f)

\displaystyle=\pi_{t}(V_{N})\operatorname{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}_{t}}(f)

\displaystyle=\pi_{t}(V_{N})\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\left(f(x)-\tilde{\pi}_{t}(f)\right)^{2}\tilde{\pi}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\pi_{t}(V_{N})\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\left(f(x)-\tilde{u}(f)\right)^{2}\tilde{\pi}_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\left(f(x)-\tilde{u}(f)\right)^{2}\pi_{t}(x)

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\left(f(x)-\tilde{u}(f)\right)^{2}u(x)\quad\text{using that }\,\pi_{t}\leq Mu\,,

\displaystyle=M\operatorname{Var}_{u}(f)\,.

We finally find that:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad\gamma_{t}\geq\frac{1}{2M}\gamma_{u}\,.

We now give a bound on the merging time .

Let

be the merging time of the time-inhomogeneous Markov chain driven by the sequence
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
. Let us summarize for all

, the sequence

is non-decreasing and:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\,\,\gamma_{t}\geq\frac{\kappa}{2MN^{2}}\quad,\,\forall x\in V_{N}\,,\quad 1\leq\pi_{t}(x)\leq Mu(x)\,.

1.8

\forall\eta\in(0,1)\,,\quad T_{\text{mer}}(\eta)\leq AMN^{2}\left(\log(N)+\log(M)+\log(1/\eta)\right)\,.

Example 2.5
.

Let
V=\{0\,,\,1\}
with

\{\{1\,,\,0\}\,,\,\{0\,,\,1\}\,,\,\{1\,,\,1\}\,,\,\{0\,,\,0\}\}

(c_{t})_{t\geq 1}

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad c_{t}(0,0)=c_{t}(1,1)=t^{1+\epsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad c_{t}(1,0)=c_{t}(0,1)=1\,.

For
t\geq 1

P_{t}
be the Markov transition operator on
V
associated to the set of conductances
c_{t}
and let
\pi_{t}
be its reversible measure, see Definition
1.11

\forall t\geq 1,\quad P_{t}(0,0)=P_{t}(1,1)=\frac{t^{1+\epsilon}}{t^{1+\epsilon}+1}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{t}(1,0)=P_{t}(0,1)=\frac{1}{t^{1+\epsilon}+1}\,.

\forall t\geq 1,\quad\pi_{t}(1)=\pi_{t}(0)=t^{1+\epsilon}+1\,.

Note that
\{(P_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a finite non decreasing environment. And, the sequence
(\tilde{\pi}_{t})_{t\geq 1}
is constant equal to the uniform probability distribution over

For

(X_{t}^{x})_{t\geq 1}
the Markov process
V

(P_{t})_{t\geq 1}

3.1
Preliminaries

From now on, we fix
V
as a countable set. It will be the state space for the Markov chain defined in Section
1
. We will sometimes need to assume that the set
V
is finite. If this is the case, we will mention it. Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be the power set of
V
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{<+\infty}(V)
be the set of finite measures on
V
non vanishing and let
\mathcal{M}_{1}(V)
be the subset of
\mathcal{M}_{<+\infty}(V)
containing the probability measures on
V
. A function of two variables
K
is said to be a Markov transition operator on
V
when
K
is a map
V\times V\rightarrow[0\,,1]
satisfying:

\forall x\in V\,,\quad K(x,.):\mathcal{P}(V)\rightarrow[0\,,1]\quad\text{is a probability measure on V .}

Let
\mathcal{F}(V,\mathbb{R}):=\left\{f:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\right\}
. When
K
is a Markov transition operator on
V
, the operator
K
acts as a right linear operator on
\mathcal{F}(V,\mathbb{R})
by:

\forall f\in\mathcal{F}(V,R)\,,\,\quad\forall x\in V\,,\,(Kf)(x)=\sum_{y\in V}K(x,y)f(y)\,.

(17)

The operator
K
also acts as an left linear operator on
\mathcal{M}_{<+\infty}(V)
as follows:

\forall\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{<+\infty}(V)\,,\quad\forall y\in V\,,\,(\mu K)(y)=\sum_{x\in V}\mu(x)K(x,y)\,.

(18)

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{<+\infty}(V)
and
p\in[1,+\infty[
, we define
\ell^{p}(\pi)\subset\mathcal{F}(V,\mathbb{R})
by:

\ell^{p}(\pi)=\left\{f\in\mathcal{F}(V,\mathbb{R})\mid\sum_{x\in V}\pi(x)|f(x)|^{p}<+\infty\right\}\,.

When
p=1
, we write:

\forall f\in\ell^{1}(\pi)\,,\quad\pi(f)=\sum_{x\in V}f(x)\pi(x)\quad\text{and }\quad\tilde{\pi}(f)=\sum_{x\in V}f(x)\tilde{\pi}(x)\,.

We used
\tilde{\pi}:=\frac{\pi}{\pi(V)}
, it denotes the element of
\mathcal{P}(V)
given by
\pi
.

We consider the following
p
-norm:

\forall f\in\ell^{p}(\pi)\,,\quad\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{p}(\pi)}=\left(\sum_{x\in V}\pi(x)|f(x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

In the
\ell^{2}(\pi)
case, we write the scalar product by:

\forall(f,g)\in\ell^{2}(\pi)\,,\quad\left\langle f\mid g\right\rangle_{\pi}=\sum_{x\in V}f(x)g(x)\pi(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\left\langle f\mid g\right\rangle_{\tilde{\pi}}=\sum_{x\in V}f(x)g(x)\tilde{\pi}(x)\,.

And, the definition of the variance of
f\in\ell^{2}(\pi)
linked to
\tilde{\pi}
is:

\operatorname{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(f)=\sum_{x\in V}f^{2}(x)\tilde{\pi}(x)-\left(\sum_{x\in V}f(x)\tilde{\pi}(x)\right)^{2}\,.

For
\pi
, which is not necessarily a probability measure, we use the notation
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}
to denote the following quantity:

\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\pi)\,,\quad\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}(f)=\pi(V)\operatorname{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(f)

Note that:

\displaystyle\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\pi)\,,\quad\text{Var}_{\pi}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi(V)}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\in V}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}\pi(x)\pi(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V}f^{2}(x)\pi(x)-\pi(V)\tilde{\pi}(f)^{2}\,.

Classicaly, we define
\ell^{\infty}(\pi)\subset\mathcal{F}(V,\mathbb{R})
by the set of bounded functions on
V
and,

\forall f\in\ell^{\infty}(\pi)\,,\quad\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\pi)}=\sup\left\{\left|f(x)\right|\mid x\in V\right\}\,.

One of the characteristics of the time-inhomogeneous context is that the invariant measures of the operators evolve with time. So, we have to consider
K_{t}
as an operator between
\ell^{p}
spaces with sometimes different measures in the domain and target spaces and other times the same measures.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
t\geq 2
,
\pi_{t-1}
and
\pi_{t}
two positive measures. Let
K_{t}
be a Markov transition operator. Assume that
\pi_{t}
is an invariant measure of
K_{t}
. We juggle between:

K_{t}:\ell^{p}(\pi_{t})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\pi_{t})\quad\text{and}\quad K_{t}:\ell^{p}(\pi_{t})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\pi_{t-1})\,.

We denote
K_{t}^{*}
the dual of
K_{t}:\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})
and
K_{t}^{\Rightarrow}=K_{t-1,t}^{\Rightarrow}:\ell^{2}(\pi_{t-1})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})
the dual of
K_{t}:\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\pi_{t-1})
. We use the notation
K_{t}^{\rightarrow}
for the dual of the operator
K_{t}
from
\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})
to
\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t-1})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

We adopt the notation of Definition
3.1
. We recall that:

\forall(x,y)\in V\,,\quad K_{t}^{*}(x,y)=\frac{\pi_{t}(y)}{\pi_{t}(x)}K_{t}(y,x)=\frac{\tilde{\pi}_{t}(y)}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}(x)}K_{t}(y,x)\,.

Then, the operator
K_{t}^{*}
is also the dual of
K_{t}:\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})
.
And, we have:

\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})\,,\,\forall g\in\ell^{2}(\pi_{t-1})\,,\quad\left\langle K_{t}f\mid g\right\rangle_{\pi_{t-1}}=\left\langle f\mid K_{t}^{\Rightarrow}g\right\rangle_{\pi_{t}}\,.

Finally, we get:

\forall(x,y)\in V\,,\quad K_{t}^{\Rightarrow}(x,y)=\frac{\pi_{t-1}(y)}{\pi_{t}(x)}K_{t}(y,x)\,.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that in general, we can not substitute
\tilde{\pi}_{t}
to
\pi_{t}
that is to say if
\pi_{t}(V)\neq\pi_{t-1}(V)
then,

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in V\,,\quad K_{t}^{\Rightarrow}(x,y)

\displaystyle\neq\frac{\tilde{\pi}_{t-1}(y)}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}(x)}K_{t}(y,x)

\displaystyle\text{but}\quad K_{t}^{\Rightarrow}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{t-1}(V)}{\pi_{t}(V)}\frac{\tilde{\pi}_{t-1}(y)}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}(x)}K_{t}(y,x)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad K_{t}^{\Rightarrow}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{t-1}(V)}{\pi_{t}(V)}K_{t}^{\rightarrow}(x,y)\,.

3.2
Poincaré case

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{<+\infty}(V)
and
K
a Markov transition operator on
V
. We assume that
\pi K=\pi
and let
K^{*}
be the dual of
K:\ell^{2}(\pi)\to\ell^{2}(\pi)
. We recall that the Dirichlet form subordinated to the measure
\pi
and the operator
Q:=K^{*}K
is defined by:

\displaystyle\forall(f,g)\in\ell^{2}(\pi)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{Q,\pi_{t}}(f,g)=\left\langle(I-Q)f\mid g\right\rangle_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in V}\sum_{y\in V}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))Q(x,y)\pi(x)\,.

Definition 3.4
.

In this context, we define
\lambda(Q,\pi)
by:

1-\lambda(Q,\pi)=\inf\left\{\frac{\mathcal{E}_{Q,\pi}(f)}{\text{Var}_{\pi}(f)}\mid f\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi})\,,\,f\text{ non-constant}\right\}\,.

(19)

We call
1-\lambda(Q,\pi)
the spectral gap of
Q
and if
1-\lambda(Q,\pi)>0
, we say that
Q
satisfies a
Poincaré condition
.

Remark 3.5
.

First, note that

1-\lambda(Q,\pi)=1-\lambda(Q,\tilde{\pi})=\inf\left\{\frac{\mathcal{E}_{Q,\tilde{\pi}}(f)}{\text{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}}(f)}\mid f\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi})\,,\,f\text{ non-constant}\right\}\,.

(20)

This quantity is independent of the normalization of
\pi
, so, we forget to specify on it. Furthermore, the constant
\lambda(Q,\pi)
is the second highest eigenvalue of
Q
.

In Sections
1.1
and
2
, we use the notation
\gamma(Q)=1-\lambda(Q,\pi)
. We have:

\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi})\,,\quad(1-\lambda(Q,\pi))\text{Var}_{\pi}(f)\leq\mathcal{E}_{Q,\pi}(f,f)\,.

(21)

Our main result is the following, it is the key to prove Theorem
1.8
and the proof paves the way to the logarithmic Sobolev case:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of Markov transitions operators and
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a sequence of
\mathcal{M}_{<+\infty}(V)
. Assume that the sequence
\{(K_{t},\pi_{t})\}_{t\geq 1}
is a finite non-decreasing environment. Let
\lambda_{t}
be the second highest eigenvalue of
Q_{t}
.

Then,

\forall t\geq 0\,,\,\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})\,,\quad\text{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}_{1}}(K_{0,t}f)\leq\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}\text{Var}_{\tilde{\pi}_{t}}(f)\prod_{s=1}^{t}\lambda_{s}\,.

(22)

And,

\left\|K_{0,t}-\tilde{\pi}_{1}(K_{0,t})\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{1})}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}\prod_{s=1}^{t}\lambda_{s}}\,.

(23)

Remark 3.7
.

The proof of Theorem
3.6
is not difficult at all. It uses a backward induction and the following:

\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})\,,\quad\text{Var}_{\pi_{t}}(f)=\inf\left\{\left\|f-c\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})}\mid c\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\,.

(24)

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

We set
\pi_{0}
equal to
\pi_{1}
. Remark
1.7
implies that:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\,\forall s\in[[1,t]]\,,\quad\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})\subset\ell^{2}(\pi_{s})\,.

Fix
t\geq 1
and
f\in\ell^{2}(\pi_{t})
. We use the following notation:

\forall s\in[[0,t-1]]\,,\quad a_{t}(s)=\left\|K_{t-s,t}f-\tilde{\pi}_{t-s}(K_{t-s,t}f)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\pi_{t-s})}\,.

Given
s\in[[0,t-1]]
, we have:

a_{t}(s+1)\leq\lambda_{t-s}a_{t}(s)\,.

(25)

Indeed,

\displaystyle a_{t}(s+1)

\displaystyle=\left\|K_{t-s}K_{t-s,t}f-\tilde{\pi}_{t-s-1}(K_{t-s}K_{t-s,t}f)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\pi_{t-s-1})}\quad\text{by variance's minimality,}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K_{t-s}K_{t-s,t}f-\tilde{\pi}_{t-s}(K_{t-s}K_{t-s,t}f)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\pi_{t-s-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K_{t-s}K_{t-s,t}f-\tilde{\pi}_{t-s}(K_{t-s}K_{t-s,t}f)\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}(\pi_{t-s})}\quad\text{using $\pi_{t-s}\geq\pi_{t-s-1}$,}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{t-s}a_{t}(s)\quad\quad\text{by Poincar\'{e} Inequality at time }t-s\,.

By iterating Equation (
25
), a reverse induction argument yields:

a_{t}(t)=\text{Var}_{\pi_{0}}(K_{0,t}f)\leq\text{Var}_{\pi_{t}}(f)\prod_{s=1}^{t}\lambda_{s}=a_{t}(0)\prod_{s=0}^{t-1}\lambda_{t-s}\,.

(26)

It yields Inequality (
22
). In order to find Inequality (
23
), remark that:

\left\|f-\tilde{\pi}_{t}(f)\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})}\,.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
be a sequence of irreducible aperiodic Markov transition operators and
(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1}
a sequence of finite measures. Assume that for
t\geq 1
,
\pi_{t}
is an invariant measure of
K_{t}
. For any
s\geq 0
,
t\geq s
, we define the operator
O_{s,t}
by:

\forall f:V\mapsto\mathbb{R}\,,\quad O_{s,t}f:=K_{s,t}f-\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}f)\,.

It is an operator from
\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})
to
\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{s})
,
i.e.
O_{s,t}:\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\to\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{s})
.

Let
O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}
be the dual of the operator
O_{s,t}
in the precise spaces:
\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{s})

Let
\mathbf{1}
be the function constant equal to 1. For any
z\in V
, for all
t\geq 1
, let
\mu_{t}^{z}
be the law of the chain at time
t
started at
z
and driven by
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
. Let
h_{t}^{z}=\frac{\mu_{t}^{z}}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}}
and
\pi_{0}=\pi_{1}
.

Then,

1.

For all
t\geq 1
, the family
(O_{s,t})_{s\leq t}
forms a semigroup.

2.

For any
t\geq s\geq 0
, we have:

\forall f:V\mapsto\mathbb{R}\,,\quad O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}f=K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}f-K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}1\tilde{\pi}_{s}(f)\,.

3.

Moreover, for all
z\in V
,

O_{0,t}^{\rightarrow}(h_{0}^{z})=h_{t}^{z}-K_{0,t}^{\rightarrow}\mathbf{1}\,.

Remark 3.9
.

Note that for all
t\geq 1
, for all
z\in V
,

h_{t}^{z}=K_{0,t}^{\rightarrow}h_{0}^{z}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{0,t}^{\rightarrow}\mathbf{1}\tilde{\pi}_{0}(h_{0}^{z})=K_{0,t}^{\rightarrow}\mathbf{1}\,.

Remark that the quantity

does not depend of

, (we recall that
\pi_{0}=\pi_{1}
).

Proof.

We prove
1.
: the family
(O_{s,t})_{s\leq t}
is a semi-group.

Note that for any
s\leq r\leq t
, for any

K_{s,r}K_{r,t}f=K_{s,t}f\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,r}f)K_{r,t}=\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}f)\,,

we find:

-K_{s,r}\tilde{\pi}_{r}(K_{r,t}f)+\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,r}f)\tilde{\pi}_{r}(K_{r,t}f)=-\tilde{\pi}_{r}(K_{r,t}f)+\tilde{\pi}_{r}(K_{r,t}f)=0\,.

Then, for any

\displaystyle O_{s,r}O_{r,t}f

\displaystyle=K_{s,r}K_{r,t}f-\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,r}K_{r,t}f)-K_{s,r}\tilde{\pi}_{r}(K_{r,t}f)+\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,r}\tilde{\pi}_{r}(K_{r,t}f))

\displaystyle=O_{s,t}f\,.

We prove
2.
, for any
t\geq s\geq 0
, we have:

O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}f=K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}.fK_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}1\tilde{\pi}_{s}(f)\,.

It is straightforward that:

O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}f=K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}f-\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}.)\right)^{\rightarrow}f\,.

We recall that the operators
K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}
and
\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}.)\right)^{\rightarrow}
are the dual of the operators
K_{s,t}
and respectively
\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}.)
from

to
\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{s})

\forall f\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\,,\,\forall g\in\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{s})\,,\quad\left\langle\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}f)\mid g\right\rangle_{\tilde{\pi}_{s}}=\left\langle f\mid\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}.)\right)^{\rightarrow}g\right\rangle_{\tilde{\pi}_{t}}=\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}f)\tilde{\pi}_{s}(g)\,.

And, we get:

\tilde{\pi}_{s}(K_{s,t}f)\tilde{\pi}_{s}(g)=\left\langle K_{s,t}f\mid\mathbf{1}\right\rangle_{\tilde{\pi}_{s}}\tilde{\pi}_{s}(g)=\left\langle f\mid\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}(g)K_{s,t}\right)^{\rightarrow}\mathbf{1}\right\rangle_{\tilde{\pi}_{t}}\,.

\forall z\in V\,,\quad K_{0,t}^{\rightarrow}\mathbf{1}\tilde{\pi}_{0}(h_{0}^{z})=K_{0,t}^{\rightarrow}\mathbf{1}\,.

3.3
Nash Inequalities and Poincaré

Some of the best bounds on
L^{2}
mixing times were shown by the use of Nash inequalities. Diaconis and Saloff-Coste introduced them in
[
5
]
to study mixing. In the time-homogeneous case, a Nash inequality is a tool used to show that when the variance of the density is extremely high, then the walk converges even faster than predicted by the Poincar’e constant. In this part, we show how to use this tool in the time-inhomogeneous case in the non-decreasing environment case.

We aim to prove Theorem
1.17
. We first prove Lemma
1.15
and then give a key proposition:

Proof of Lemma
1.15
.

For
x,y
in
V
, for
t\geq 1
, we have for any
z\in V
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi_{t+1}(V)}\left|\frac{\mu_{t+1}^{x}(z)-\mu_{t+1}^{y}(z)}{\tilde{\pi}_{t+1}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\sum_{u\in V}\left(\mu_{t}^{x}(u)-\mu_{t}^{y}(u)\right)K_{t+1}(u,z)}{\pi_{t+1}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left|\frac{\mu_{t}^{x}(v)-\mu_{t}^{y}(v)}{\pi_{t}(v)}\right|\mid v\in V\right\}\sum_{u\in V}\frac{\pi_{t}(u)K_{t+1}(u,z)}{\pi_{t+1}(z)}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left|\frac{\mu_{t}^{x}(v)-\mu_{t}^{y}(v)}{\pi_{t}(v)}\right|\mid v\in V\right\}\sum_{u\in V}\frac{\pi_{t+1}(u)K_{t+1}(u,z)}{\pi_{t+1}(z)}\quad\text{using}\,\pi_{t}\leq\pi_{t+1}\,,

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\left|\frac{\mu_{t}^{x}(v)-\mu_{t}^{y}(v)}{\pi_{t}(v)}\right|\mid v\in V\right\}\quad\text{using }\pi_{t+1}K_{t+1}=\pi_{t+1}\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi_{t}(V)}s_{\infty}(\mu_{t}^{x}\,,\,\mu_{t}^{y}\mid\tilde{\pi}_{t})\,.

By taking the supremum over

, we obtain:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad\frac{1}{\pi_{t}(V)}s_{\infty}(\mu_{t}^{x}\,,\,\mu_{t}^{y}\mid\tilde{\pi}_{t})\geq\frac{1}{\pi_{t+1}(V)}s_{\infty}(\mu_{t+1}^{x}\,,\,\mu_{t+1}^{y}\mid\tilde{\pi}_{t+1})\,.

∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
K_{r}
be a Markov transition operator. Let
\pi_{r}
and
\pi_{r-1}
two positive finite measures. Assume that
\pi_{r}
is an invariant measure for

\forall g\in\ell^{2}(\pi_{r})\,,\quad\mathcal{E}_{Q_{r},\pi_{r}}(g,g)\leq\left\|g-\tilde{\pi}_{r}(g)\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\pi_{r}\right)}^{2}-\left\|K_{r}g-\tilde{\pi}_{r-1}(K_{r}g)\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\pi_{r-1}\right)}^{2}\,.

\displaystyle=\pi_{1}(V)^{\frac{1}{q_{t}}}\pi_{t}(V)^{-\frac{1}{q_{0}}}\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}\left(\frac{\pi_{s+1}(V)}{\pi_{s}(V)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{t-s}}}

Renormalize to find:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad\left\|K_{0,t}f\right\|_{\ell^{q_{t}}(\tilde{\pi}_{0})}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{q}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})}\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}\left(\frac{\pi_{s+1}(V)}{\pi_{s}(V)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{t-s}}}\,.

Remark that for all
s\geq 1
, the function
x\to\exp\left(x\ln\left(\frac{\pi_{s+1}(V)}{\pi_{s}(V)}\right)\right)
is non-decreasing, then,

\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}\left(\frac{\pi_{s+1}(V)}{\pi_{s}(V)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{t-s}}}\leq\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}\left(\frac{\pi_{s+1}(V)}{\pi_{s}(V)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

Finally, we get:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad\left\|K_{0,t}f\right\|_{\ell^{q_{t}}(\tilde{\pi}_{0})}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{q}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})}\left(\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,

and the announced result is a consequence of the following:

\forall p\in[1,q_{t}]\,,\,\forall h:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,,\quad\left\|h\right\|_{\ell^{p}(\tilde{\pi}_{0})}\leq\left\|h\right\|_{\ell^{q_{t}}(\tilde{\pi}_{0})}\,.

∎

In the following, we prove Theorem
1.21
and Theorem
1.23
. The proof follows the same outline as the proof of Theorem
1.8
and we will reuse some of its arguments.

Proof of Theorem
1.21
.

Let
\pi_{0}:=\pi_{1}
and for all
t\geq 0
, for any
z\in V
, let
\mu_{t}^{z}
be the law at time
t
of the chain started at
z
and driven by
(K_{t})_{t\geq 1}
.

For
s\geq 1
and
t\geq s
, let
m_{s,t}
be
K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}1
and for
z\in V
, let
h_{t}^{z}
denote
\frac{\mu_{t}^{z}}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}}
.

For
s\geq 1
and
q_{s}\geq 2
, we denote
q_{s}^{\prime}
as the Hölder conjugate of
q_{s}
. For
z\in V
, define
t_{z}
by:

s_{z}=\min\left\{t\geq 1\mid\sum_{u=1}^{t}\log(1+\alpha_{u})\geq\log\left(\log\left(\tilde{\pi}_{0}(z)^{-1}\right)\right)\right\}\,.

(36)

An application of Proposition
3.8
gives:

\displaystyle\forall t\geq s\geq 0\,,\,\forall z\in V\,,\quad\left\|h_{t}^{z}-m_{s,t}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})}

\displaystyle=\left\|O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}h_{s}^{z}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}\left\|K_{0,s}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{q_{s}^{\prime}}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{0}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)}\left\|h_{0}^{z}\right\|_{\ell^{q_{s}^{\prime}}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{0}\right)}\,.

Moreover, for
x,y\in V
, if
s\geq s(x,y)=\max\left\{s_{x},s_{y}\right\}
, then,

\left\|h_{0}^{x}\right\|_{\ell^{q_{s}^{\prime}}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{0}\right)}=\tilde{\pi}_{0}(x)^{-1/q_{s}}\leq e\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|h_{0}^{y}\right\|_{\ell^{q_{s}^{\prime}}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{0}\right)}=\tilde{\pi}_{0}(y)^{-1/q_{s}}\leq e\,.

Combine the previous inequalities, Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.16
to find:

\displaystyle\forall t\geq s(x,y)\,,\quad d_{TV}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|h_{t}^{x}-m_{s(x,y),t}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|h_{t}^{y}-m_{s(x,y),t}\right\|_{\ell^{2}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq e\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{t}(V)}{\pi_{0}(V)}}\prod_{u=s(x,y)+1}^{t}\sqrt{\lambda_{u}}\,.

∎

In the second step, we prove Theorem
1.23
but first, we need the following result:

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
u\geq 2
,
\pi_{u-1}
and
\pi_{u}
two positive measures. Let
K_{u}
be a Markov transition operator. Assume that
\pi_{u}
is an invariant measure of
K_{u}
. See the notations in Definition
3.1
. Assume that:

\forall x\in V\,,\quad\pi_{u}(x)\geq\pi_{u-1}(x)\,.

We have:

\forall(a,b)\in[1,\infty]\,,\quad\left\|K_{u}^{\Rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{a}\left(\pi_{u-1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{b}\left(\pi_{u}\right)}\leq\left\|K_{u}^{*}\right\|_{\ell^{a}\left(\pi_{u}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{b}\left(\pi_{u}\right)}\,.

Proof.

Denote
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
the Hölder conjugate exponents of
a
and
b
. We get:

\displaystyle\left\|K_{u}^{\Rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{a}\left(\pi_{u-1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{b}\left(\pi_{u}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\|K_{u}\right\|_{\ell^{b^{\prime}}\left(\pi_{u}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{a^{\prime}}\left(\pi_{u-1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K_{u}\right\|_{\ell^{b^{\prime}}\left(\pi_{u}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{a^{\prime}}\left(\pi_{u}\right)}\quad\text{using that}\quad\pi_{u-1}\leq\pi_{u}\,,

\displaystyle=\left\|K_{u}^{*}\right\|_{\ell^{a}\left(\pi_{u}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{b}\left(\pi_{u}\right)}\,.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.23
.

We recall that
V
is finite and:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\sharp}\geq\rho>0\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{t}\geq\alpha>0\,.

Write
t
in the form
2r+u
.
Note that:

\forall t\geq 0\,,\quad\max\left\{\left|\frac{\mu_{t}^{x}(y)-\tilde{\pi}_{1}K_{0,t}(y)}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}(y)}\right|\mid(x,y)\in V\right\}=\left\|K_{0,t}-\tilde{\pi}_{1}K_{0,t}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}=\left\|O_{0,t}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{0}\right)}\,.

And, we have with
s=r+u
:

\displaystyle\left\|K_{0,t}-\tilde{\pi}_{1}K_{0,t}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)}=\left\|O_{0,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}\times\left\|O_{r,s}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)}\times\left\|O_{0,r}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)}\,.

We bound these different quantities. First, recall that Theorem
3.6
states:

\left\|O_{r,s}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{s}(V)}{\pi_{r}(V)}\prod_{u=r+1}^{s}\lambda_{u}}\,.

It remains to bound the two quantities:
\left\|O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}
and
\left\|O_{0,r}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)}
.

The triangle inequality gives:

\displaystyle\left\|O_{0,r}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\|K_{0,r}-\tilde{\pi}_{1}K_{0,r}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K_{0,r}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)}+\left\|\tilde{\pi}_{1}K_{0,r}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|K_{0,r}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)}.

Then, note that Theorem
3.16
gives with
q_{r}=2\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1+\alpha_{j})
:

\displaystyle\left\|K_{0,r}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|K_{0,r}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{q_{r}}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)}\left\|I\right\|_{\ell^{q_{r}}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{r}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}}\frac{1}{\tilde{\pi}_{1}^{\sharp\frac{1}{q_{r}}}}\,.

The last inequality holds by the following:

\forall f\in\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\pi}_{1})\,,\quad\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\tilde{\pi}_{1})}\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{\pi}_{1}^{\sharp\frac{1}{q_{r}}}}\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{q_{r}}(\tilde{\pi}_{1})}\,.

(37)

Choose
r\geq 1
such that:

2(1+\alpha)^{r}\geq\log(1/\rho)\,.

Then, we use the lower bound on the
\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\sharp}
’s and
q_{r}\geq 2(1+\alpha)^{r}
to find:

\forall t\geq 1\,,\quad\frac{1}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\sharp\frac{1}{q_{r}}}}\leq e\,.

Thus, we get:

\left\|K_{0,r}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{r}\right)}\leq e\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{r}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}}\,.

We bound
\left\|O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}
. We have:

\displaystyle\left\|O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\|O_{s,t}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)}=\sup\left\{\left\|O_{s,t}f\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)}\mid f\in\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)\,,\,\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}=1\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\left\{\left\|K_{s,t}f\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)}\mid f\in\ell^{1}(\tilde{\pi}_{t})\,,\,\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}=1\right\}\quad\text{by minimality of variance}\,,

\displaystyle=\left\|K_{s,t}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)}\,.

Let
\hat{q}(s,t)=2\prod_{u=s+1}^{t}(1+\hat{\alpha}_{u})
. We have by Inequality
37
with
\tilde{\pi}_{t}
instead of
\tilde{\pi}_{1}
:

\displaystyle\left\|K_{s,t}\right\|_{\ell^{1}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\hat{q}(s,t)}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}\left\|I\right\|_{\ell^{\hat{q}(s,t)}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\sharp\frac{1}{\hat{q}(s,t)}}}\left\|K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\hat{q}(s,t)}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}\,.

It remains to bound
\left\|K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\hat{q}(s,t)}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}
, we define a sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in[[s+1,t+1]]}
by:

a_{s+1}=2\quad\text{and}\quad\forall u\in[[s+2,t]]\,,\quad a_{u+1}=a_{u}(1+\hat{\alpha}_{u})\,.

We have:

\displaystyle\left\|K_{s,t}^{\Rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\pi_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\hat{q}(s,t)}\left(\pi_{t}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{u=s+1}^{t}\left\|K_{u}^{\Rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{a_{u}}\left(\pi_{u-1}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{a_{u+1}}\left(\pi_{u}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{u=s+1}^{t}\left\|K_{u}^{*}\right\|_{\ell^{a_{u}}\left(\pi_{u}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{a_{u+1}}\left(\pi_{u}\right)}\quad\text{by Lemma \ref{controloperator}}\,,

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{u=s+1}^{t}\pi_{u}(V)^{\frac{1}{a_{u+1}}-\frac{1}{a_{u}}}\quad\text{by Proposition \ref{propoUtilSobolev}}\,.

\displaystyle=\pi_{s+1}(V)^{\frac{1}{a_{s+2}}}\pi_{t}(V)^{-\frac{1}{a_{t}}}\prod_{u=s+2}^{t-1}\left(\frac{\pi_{u-1}(V)}{\pi_{u}(V)}\right)^{\frac{1}{a_{u}}}

\displaystyle\leq\pi_{t}(V)^{\frac{1}{a_{t+1}}}\pi_{s+1}(V)^{-\frac{1}{a_{s+2}}}\quad\text{using that }\pi_{u-1}(V)\leq\pi_{u}(V)\,.

Renormalize to find:

\left\|K_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\hat{q}(s,t)}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}\leq 1\,,

and, using
\hat{q}(s,t)\geq 2(1+\alpha)^{r}
, we get:

\left\|O_{s,t}^{\rightarrow}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{s}\right)\rightarrow\ell^{\infty}\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}\right)}\leq e\,.

All the inequalities above merged give:

\max\left\{\left|\frac{\mu_{t}^{x}(y)-\tilde{\pi}_{1}K_{0,t}(y)}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}(y)}\right|\mid(x,y)\in V\right\}\leq 2e^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi_{s}(V)}{\pi_{1}(V)}\prod_{l=r+1}^{r+u}\lambda_{r}}\,.

The triangular inequality gives:

\max\left\{s_{\infty}(\mu_{t}^{x},\mu_{t}^{y}\mid\tilde{\pi}_{t})\mid(x,y)\in V\right\}\leq 2\max\left\{\left|\frac{\mu_{t}^{x}(y)-\tilde{\pi}_{1}K_{0,t}(y)}{\tilde{\pi}_{t}(y)}\right|\mid(x,y)\in V\right\}\,.

And, for all
\eta\in(0,1)
, we get a bound on
T_{\text{mer}}^{\infty}(\eta\,,(\pi_{t})_{t\geq 1})
.
∎

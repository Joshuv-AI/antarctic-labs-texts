Sharp conditions for the linearization of finite elasticity
Edoardo Mainini, Danilo Percivale

Abstract
. We consider the topic of linearization of finite elasticity for pure traction problems. We characterize the variational limit for the approximating sequence of rescaled nonlinear elastic energies. We show that the limiting minimal value can be strictly lower than the minimal value of the standard linear elastic energy if a strict compatibility condition for external loads does not hold. The results are provided for both the compressible and the incompressible case.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be the reference configuration of a hyperelastic body. If
\mathbf{y}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the deformation field and
h>0
is an adimensional parameter, we introduce the scaled global energy of the body, including the stored elastic energy and the work of external forces, by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{h}(\mathbf{y}):=h^{-2}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\nabla\mathbf{y})\,d{\bf x}-h^{-1}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{i}).

Here,
\mathcal{W}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty]
is the strain energy density and

\mathbf{i}
denotes the identity map.
For every
{\bf x}\in\Omega
, the function
\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\cdot)
is assumed to be frame indifferent and uniquely minimized at rotations with value
0
. It is also assumed to
be
C^{2}
-smooth around rotations and to satisfy a suitable coercivity condition to be introduced later on.
Moreover, the load functional
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}({\bf v}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf x}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\bf v}\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}({\bf x}),

where
\mathbf{f}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a volume force field,
\mathbf{g}:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a surface force field, and
\mathcal{H}^{2}
denotes the surface measure.

In a pure traction problem,
in order to study stable equilibrium configurations we have to assume that

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{i})\leq 0

for every deformation
\mathbf{y}
such that

(1.2)

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\nabla\mathbf{y})\,d{\bf x}=0.

Under our assumptions on
\mathcal{W}
, (
1.2
) holds true if and only if
\nabla\mathbf{y}
is a constant rotation matrix, i.e.,
\mathbf{y}({\bf x})=\mathbf{R}{\bf x}+\mathbf{c}
for some
\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)
and some
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, where
SO(3)
denotes the special orthogonal group.
Thus, we need to assume that

(1.3)

\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x}+\mathbf{c})\leq 0

for every
\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)
and every
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and by taking
\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}
we get
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{c})\leq 0
for every
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, that is,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{c})=0
for every
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Hence, (
1.3
) is equivalent to the following two conditions

(1.4)

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{c})=0\ \qquad\forall\ \mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.5)

\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x})\leq 0\ \qquad\forall\ \mathbf{R}\in SO(3).

We observe that
if
\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)
exists such that

\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x})>0,

then
\mathcal{G}_{h}
is not uniformly bounded from below with respect to
h
, that is,
\inf\mathcal{G}_{h}\to-\infty
as
h\to 0
(see also Remark 2.9 below).
It is worth noting that (
1.4
) says that external loads have null resultant while it will be shown in Remark
2.1
that (
1.5
) implies they have null momentum (without being equivalent to the null momentum condition).

The choice of the scaling powers in (
1.1
) depends on the behavior of the elastic strain energy density and of the work expended by external loads for deformations which are close to a suitable rotation of the reference configuration, say
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{i}+h\mathbf{u})
, where
\mathbf{u}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and where
\mathbf{R}
belongs to the following
rotation kernel
associated to
\mathcal{L}
(that satisfies (
1.4
)-(
1.5
))

(1.6)

\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}:=\{\mathbf{R}\in SO(3):\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x})=0\}.

Indeed, by frame indifference we obtain

(1.7)

\mathcal{G}_{h}(\mathbf{y})=h^{-2}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I}+h\nabla\mathbf{u}({\bf x}))\,d{\bf x}-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u})-h^{-1}\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x}),

and if
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
, by a Taylor expansion of
\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\cdot)
around the identity matrix we formally get for every fixed
\mathbf{u}

(1.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\mathcal{G}_{h}(\mathbf{y})=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u}),

where
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}):=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u}^{T}+\nabla\mathbf{u})
and where we have introduced the quadratic form

\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{F}^{T}\,D^{2}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F},\qquad\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\quad{\bf x}\in\Omega.

Therefore, it is natural to guess that the variational limit
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{G}_{h}
as
h\to 0
can be obtained from (
1.8
) through a minimization among all
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
, namely

(1.9)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{u}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\max_{\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u}).

We stress that, if
\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I}
, this corresponds to the usual formal derivation of linearized elasticity. But for any other
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
the work done by external loads for going from
\Omega
to
\mathbf{R}\Omega
is null, so that it might be energetically convenient to consider deformations near
\mathbf{R}{\bf x}
rather than near the identity. On the other hand it is clear that if
\mathbf{R}\notin\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
this heuristic argument fails. Indeed, choosing
\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)\setminus\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
is not energetically convenient due to the behavior of the last term in the right hand side of (
1.7
) as
h\to 0
.

In the case that (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) hold and
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}\equiv\{\mathbf{I}\}
,
then (
1.9
) reduces to

\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{u}):=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u})

which is the standard form of the total potential energy of the elastic body in the linear setting. It has been shown in
[
9
]
(see also
[
8
]
) that in this case

(1.10)

\lim_{h\to 0}(\inf\mathcal{G}_{h})=\min\mathcal{E}

and if
\mathcal{G}_{h}(\mathbf{y}_{h})-\inf\mathcal{G}_{h}\to 0
as
h\to 0
(i.e., if
(\mathbf{y}_{h})
is a sequence of quasi-minimizers of
\mathcal{G}_{h}
) then

(1.11)

\mathbf{u}_{h}:=h^{-1}(\mathbf{y}_{h}-\mathbf{i})\to\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathop{{\rm argmin}}\nolimits\mathcal{E}

in a suitable sense. In particular,
\mathbf{u}_{0}
satisfies the equilibrium conditions

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\mathop{{\rm div}}\nolimits\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}_{0}))=\mathbf{f}&\hbox{ in}\ \Omega\\
&\\
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}_{0}))\,\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{g}&\hbox{ on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}
and
\mathbf{n}
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega
. In
[
11
]
we have extended the results of
[
9
]
to incompressible elasticity. Indeed, it is shown in
[
11
]
that (
1.10
) and (
1.11
) hold true by substituting
\mathcal{G}_{h}
with
the scaled incompressible global energy
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}
, defined by replacing
\mathcal{W}
with
\mathcal{W}^{I}
in the right hand side of (
1.1
),
where

{\mathcal{W}}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})&\hbox{if}\ \ \det\mathbf{F}=1\\
+\infty&\hbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and by substituting
\mathcal{E}
with

\mathcal{E}^{I}({\bf v}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}({\bf v}))-\mathcal{L}({\bf v}),

where

(1.13)

{\mathcal{Q}}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69046pt\dfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{F}^{T}\,D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}&\hbox{if}\ \ {\rm Tr}\,\mathbf{F}=0\\
+\infty&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Roughly speaking, these results can be interpreted by saying that, if (
1.4
) holds along with the strict compatibility condition

\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x})<0\ \qquad\forall\ \mathbf{R}\in SO(3)\setminus\{\mathbf{I}\},

then linear elasticity can be viewed as the variational limit of finite elasticity both in the compressible and in the incompressible case.

By assuming only (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{0}
needs not be reduced to the identity matrix, a minimizer of functional (
1.9
) is not expected to satisfy (
1.12
) in general. On the other hand, we may ask if its energy level equals the minimal value of
\mathcal{E}
: this fact
is still an open question and it represents our main focus, along with the analogous comparison between optimal energy levels in the incompressible case.

A consequence of a recent result shown by Maor and Mora in
[
12
, Theorem 5.3]
is that if (
1.4
) and (
1.5
)
hold along with a quadratic growth condition from below for
\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\cdot)
, then indeed

\lim_{h\to 0}(\inf\mathcal{G}_{h})=\min\mathcal{G}.

In this paper we extend this result to the incompressible case and to more general coercivity assumptions on the strain energy density
\mathcal{W}
,
but more than anything else we exhibit examples in which

(1.14)

\min\mathcal{G}<\min\mathcal{E}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\min\mathcal{G}^{I}<\min\mathcal{E}^{I},

where
\mathcal{G}^{I}
is defined by replacing
\mathcal{Q}
with
\mathcal{Q}^{I}
in (
1.9
).
Surprisingly enough, this shows that, at least under the sole assumptions (
1.4
)-(
1.5
), the energy level of the minimizer of
\mathcal{E}
does not necessarily provide the minimal value of the variational limit of the scaled finite elasticity functional
\mathcal{G}_{h}
.
A
gap between
\lim_{h\to 0}(\inf\mathcal{G}_{h})
(resp.
\lim_{h\to 0}(\inf\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I})
) and
\min\mathcal{E}
(resp.
\min\mathcal{E}^{I}
) may appear.

In detail, by assuming the coercivity condition

(1.15)

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})\geq C\ g_{p}(d(\mathbf{F},SO(3)))\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\end{array}

for some
p\in(1,2]
, where

(1.16)

g_{p}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\!\!t^{2}&\hbox{if}\ 0\leq t\leq 1\\
\!\!\displaystyle\frac{2t^{p}}{p}-\frac{2}{p}+1&\hbox{if}\ t\geq 1\\
\end{array}\right.

and
d(\cdot,SO(3))
denotes the distance function from rotations, for the incompressible case we will prove the following result (see Theorem
2.5
below).
If
(\mathbf{y}_{h})\subset W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a sequence of quasi-minimizers of
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}
, then by defining the
generalized rescaled displacements

\mathbf{u}_{h}({\bf x}):=h^{-1}(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{T}\mathbf{y}({\bf x})-{\bf x}),\;\;\quad\mbox{where}\quad\;\;\mathbf{R}_{h}\in\mathop{{\rm argmin}}\nolimits\left\{\int_{\Omega}g_{p}(|\nabla\mathbf{y}_{h}-\mathbf{R}|)\,d{\bf x}:\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)\right\},

there is a (not relabeled) subsequence such that

\nabla\mathbf{u}_{h}\rightharpoonup\nabla\mathbf{u}_{*}\ \hbox{weakly in}\ L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3})\qquad\mbox{as $h\to 0$},

where
\mathbf{u}_{*}\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
\mathbf{u}_{*}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}^{I}
over
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover,

\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}(\mathbf{y}_{h})\to\mathcal{G}^{I}(\mathbf{u}_{*})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\inf_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}\to\min_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}\mathcal{G}^{I}\quad\qquad\mbox{as $h\to 0$}.

Here, the precise characterization of
\mathcal{G}^{I}
is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}^{I}(\mathbf{u})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\max_{\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{u})&\hbox{if}\ \mathbf{u}\in H^{1}_{\mathop{{\rm div}}\nolimits}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\\
&\\
\ \!\!+\infty&\hbox{otherwise in}\ W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{array}\right.

where
H^{1}_{\mathop{{\rm div}}\nolimits}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the space of divergence-free
H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
vector fields.
Such a result improves the one in
[
11
]
, as it allows to obtain the characterization of the limit energy even
without the assumption
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}\equiv\{\mathbf{I}\}
. It
also generalizes a recent result of Jesenko and Schmidt
[
7
]
and reduces to it when
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}\equiv SO(3)
.
We will provide
the same statement
for the compressible case in Theorem
2.4
, thus obtaining an analogous of
[
12
, Theorem 5.3]
for the case of the
p
-growth assumption (
1.15
) (see also Remark
2.12
below).
On top of that, we will show in Theorem
2.7
that there are configurations and external loads such that the strict inequalities (
1.14
) hold, the minimization problems being cast on
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
We will end our analysis by remarking that (
1.10
) might be true even if (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) hold and
\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
is not reduced to the identity matrix: indeed, it is always possible to rotate the external forces in such a way that (
1.10
) holds for the problem with rotated forces, see Theorem
2.10
.

Let us finally mention that several other results about variational linearization of finite elasticity, including Dirichlet problems, incompressibility constraints or even theories for multiwell potentials
are found in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
10
,
14
]
.

2.
Main Results

We introduce the setting for compressible and incompressible elasticity, then we state the main results.
In the following, the reference configuration
\Omega
is always assumed to be a bounded open connected Lipschitz set in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

As basic notation,
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
is the set of
3\times 3
real matrices, endowed with the Euclidean norm
|\mathbf{F}|=\sqrt{\mathbf{F}^{T}\mathbf{F}}
.

\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\rm sym}
(resp.
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\rm skew})
denotes the subset of symmetric (resp. skew-symmetric) matrices. For every
\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
we define
{\rm sym\,}\mathbf{F}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{F}+\mathbf{F}^{T})

and
{\rm skew\,}\mathbf{F}:=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{F}^{T})
.
By
SO(3)
we denote the special orthogonal group and for every
\mathbf{R}\in SO(3)
there exist
\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\mathrm{skew}},
such that
|\mathbf{W}|^{2}=|\mathbf{W}^{2}|^{2}=2
and such that
the following Euler-Rodrigues representation formula holds

(2.1)

\mathbf{R}\,=\,\mathbf{I}\,+\,\sin\vartheta\,\mathbf{W}\,+\,(1-\cos\vartheta)\,\mathbf{W}^{2}.

Assumptions on the elastic energy density

We let

\mathcal{W}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,+\infty]
be
{\mathcal{L}}^{3}\!\times\!{\mathcal{B}}^{9}
- measurable satisfying the following assumptions, see also
[
2
,
10
]
:

(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}1}
)

{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{R}\mathbf{F})={\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{\!}\mathbf{R}\!\in\!SO(3)\quad\forall\,\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\qquad\mbox{for a.e. ${\bf x}\in\Omega$},

(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}2}
)

\min\mathcal{W}=\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathcal{W}}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})=0\quad\mbox{for a.e. ${\bf x}\in\Omega$}.

Concerning the regularity of
\mathcal{W}
, we assume that there exist an open neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
SO(3)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3}
, an increasing function
\omega:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\omega(t)=0
and a constant
K>0

such that for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\Omega

(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}3}
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\vskip 2.84544pt\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\cdot)\in C^{2}(\mathcal{U}),\;\;\;\vskip 2.84544pt|D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})|\leq K\;\;\hbox{and}\\
&|D^{2}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})-D^{2}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{G})|\leq\omega(|\mathbf{F}-\mathbf{G}|)\quad\forall\;\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G}\in\mathcal{U}.\end{array}

We assume in addition the following growth property from below: there exist
C>0
and
p\in(1,2]
such that for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\Omega

(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}4}
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{W}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})\geq C\ g_{p}(d(\mathbf{F},SO(3)))\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\end{array}

where
g_{p}:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is the strictly convex function defined by (
1.16
).
We notice that a standard application of the Hölder inequality shows that for every
\eta\in L^{p}(\Omega)
and every
h\in(0,1)

(2.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle h^{-2}\int_{\Omega}g_{p}(h|\eta|)\,dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|\eta|\leq h^{-1}}|\eta|^{2}\,dt+h^{p-2}\int_{|\eta|\geq h^{-1}}|\eta|^{p}\,dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{p}\int_{|\eta|\leq h^{-1}}|\eta|^{p}\,dt+h^{p-2}\int_{|\eta|\geq h^{-1}}|\eta|^{p}\,dt-\frac{2-p}{p}\,|\Omega|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}|\eta|^{p}\,dt-\frac{2-p}{p}\,|\Omega|

In order to consider incompressible elasticity models, starting from a function
\mathcal{W}
as above we also introduce the incompressible strain energy density by letting, for a.e.
{\bf x}\in\Omega
,

{\mathcal{W}}^{I}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{F})&\hbox{if}\ \ \det\mathbf{F}=1\\
+\infty&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Assumptions on the external forces

We introduce a body force field
\mathbf{f}\in L^{\frac{3p}{4p-3}}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and a surface force field
\mathbf{g}\in L^{\frac{2p}{3p-3}}(\partial\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, where
p
is such that (

⁢
W
4

) holds. From here on,
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{g}
will always be understood to satisfy such summability assumptions. The load functional is the following linear functional

(2.3)

\mathcal{L}({\bf v}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\bf x}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbf{g}\cdot{\bf v}\,d\mathcal{H}^{2}({\bf x}),\qquad{\bf v}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We note that since
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{3p}{3-p}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
and the Sobolev trace embedding
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2p}{3-p}}(\partial\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
imply that
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded functional over
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

We assume that external loads have null resultant

(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}1}
)

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{c})=0\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

and that they satisfy the following weak compatibility condition

(
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}2}
)

\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x})\leq 0\qquad\forall\,\mathbf{R}\in SO(3).

A crucial object in our results is the rotation kernel
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{0}
associated to a functional
\mathcal{L}
satisfying the above assumptions, which is the set defined by (
1.6
).
Such a kernel includes at least the identity matrix and represents the set of rotations that realize equality in (

⁢
L
2

).

Remark 2.1
.

Thanks to the the Euler-Rodrigues representation formula for rotations (
2.1
), it is readily seen that (

⁢
L
2

) may be rewritten as

h_{\mathbf{W}}(\theta):=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}{\bf x})\sin\theta+(1-\cos\theta)\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}^{2}{\bf x})\leq 0

for every
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
and for every
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\mathrm{skew}}
with
|\mathbf{W}|=2
.
Since
h_{\mathbf{W}}(0)=h_{\mathbf{W}}(2\pi)=0
, then

0\leq h_{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}(2\pi)=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}{\bf x})=h_{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}(0)\leq 0,

that is, by linearity of
\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}{\bf x})=0\ \quad\forall\ \mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\mathrm{skew}},

and so if (

⁢
L
2

) holds then external loads have null momentum. Therefore, (

⁢
L
2

) is equivalent to

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}{\bf x})=0,\ \ \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}^{2}{\bf x})\leq 0\qquad\forall\ \mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\mathrm{skew}},

and we mention that formulation (
2.4
) of the compatibility condition (with strict inequality) is the one appearing in
[
8
,
9
,
11
]
.
On the other hand it is worth noting that the null momentum condition does not imply the second relation in (
2.4
).
Indeed, let
\mathbf{f}({\bf x})=-{\bf x},\ \mathbf{g}\equiv 0
and let
\Omega
be the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}^{2}{\bf x})=\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{W}{\bf x}|^{2}\,d{\bf x}>0

for every
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\mathrm{skew}},\ \mathbf{W}\not\equiv 0
, despite that external loads has null resultant and null momentum.

Remark 2.2
.

The characterization (
2.4
) of (

⁢
L
2

) and Euler-Rodrigues formula entail

\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}=\left\{\mathbf{R}\in SO(3):\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}){\bf x})=0\right\}=\left\{e^{\mathbf{W}}:\mathbf{W}\in{\mathcal{X}}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}\right\}

where

{\mathcal{X}}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}:=\left\{\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\mathrm{skew}}:\;\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}{\bf x})=\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{W}^{2}{\bf x})=0\right\}.

Therefore,
we have
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{0}\Rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{T}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{0}

Energy functionals

The rescaled finite elasticity functionals
\mathcal{G}_{h}:W^{1,p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}
are defined by (
1.1
)
and the limit energy functional
\mathcal{G}:W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u})-\max_{\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}}\mathcal{L}((\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I})\mathbf{u})&\hbox{if}\ \mathbf{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\\
&\\
\ \!\!+\infty&\hbox{otherwise in}\ W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{array}\right.

where
{\mathcal{Q}}({\bf x},\mathbf{F}):=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbf{F}^{T}\,D^{2}\mathcal{W}({\bf x},\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{F}
.
By introducing the standard functional of linearized elasticity
\mathcal{E}:W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, namely

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{Q}({\bf x},\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{u}))\,d{\bf x}-\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{u})&\hbox{if}\ \mathbf{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})\\
&\\
\ \!\!+\infty&\hbox{otherwise in}\ W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{array}\right.

we immediately see that

, since
\mathbf{I}\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
and
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{0})=0

It is well-known that
\mathcal{E}
admits a unique minimizer up to infinitesimal rigid displacements (i.e., up to the addition of a displacements field

such that

is among rotations in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{L}}^{0}

\mathcal{G}
is invariant under the addition of infinitesimal rigid displacements, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{u}+{\bf v})=\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{u})
whenever
\mathbb{E}({\bf v})\equiv 0
. On the other hand, in general minimizers of
\mathcal{G}
are not unique up to infinitesimal rigid displacements (see Proposition
5.2
later on).

When considering incompressible elasticity,
the functional
\mathcal{G}_{h}^{I}:W^{1,p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{+\infty\}

&\\
\ \!\!+\infty&\hbox{otherwise in}\ W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{array}\right.

is defined by (

⁢
f
1

(5.17)

\displaystyle\min_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}\mathcal{E}^{I}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\min_{\Phi\in H^{2}(B)}\int_{B}8\Phi_{xy}^{2}+4\Phi_{xx}^{2}+4\Phi_{yy}^{2}+\int_{B}\Phi\Delta\varphi\right)-KC(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}8\tilde{\Phi}_{xy}^{2}+4\tilde{\Phi}_{xx}^{2}+4\tilde{\Phi}_{yy}^{2}+\int_{B}\tilde{\Phi}\Delta\varphi-KC(\varphi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}8\tilde{\Phi}_{xy}^{2}+2(\tilde{\Phi}_{yy}-\tilde{\Phi}_{xx})^{2}+2|\Delta\tilde{\Phi}|^{2}+\int_{B}\tilde{\Phi}\Delta\varphi-KC(\varphi,\psi).\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

By (
5.17
) and by the second statement of Lemma
5.1
we deduce

\min_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}{\mathcal{E}}^{I}\geq\int_{B}2|\Delta\tilde{\Phi}|^{2}-KC(\varphi,\psi)+\min_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{I}.

Since
\int_{B}|\Delta\tilde{\Phi}|^{2}>0
as previously observed, we may choose
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
so small that
KC(\varphi,\psi)<\int_{B}2|\Delta\tilde{\Phi}|^{2}
and deduce

\min_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}{\mathcal{E}}^{I}>\min_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{3})}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{I}

thus completing the proof.

\blacksquare

\min\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle u_{3}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=H(z)+T(x,y),

A short proof of Lagrange-Good formula using Dirac delta function
Minh-Toan Nguyen

Abstract
We give a half-page proof of the Lagrange-Good formula, using the Fourier representation of Dirac delta function.



1
Introduction

Lagrange-Good formula is the multivariate version of the classic Lagrange inversion theorem widely used in enumerative combinatorics. The formula has various proofs using complex analysis
[
4
]
, umbral calculus
[
5
]
, operator methods
[
7
]
, quantum field theory
[
1
]
and symbolic integral
[
2
]
. There are also proofs based on combinatorial arguments
[
3
]
and on a determinant formula
[
6
]
. We give here a short proof of the formula using Dirac delta function.

The formula, originally proved for complex analytic functions in
[
4
]
, holds more generally for formal power series
[
9
]
. This version of the formula is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider formal power series of
n
variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Denote
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.
Consider
n
formal power series
f_{i}(x)
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Let
g_{i}(x)
be the formal power series that solve the following system of equations

\displaystyle g_{i}=x_{i}f_{i}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}),\quad i=1,\dots,n.

(1)

Then for any formal power series
\phi
, the coefficient of
[x_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{k_{n}}]
in

\displaystyle\frac{\phi(g(x))}{\det(\delta_{ij}-x_{i}\partial_{j}f_{i}(g))}

(2)

is equal to the coefficient of
[x_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{k_{n}}]
in

\displaystyle\phi(x)f_{1}(x)^{k_{1}}\dots f_{n}(x)^{k_{n}}.

To prove results about formal power series, we will work with functions that are smooth, analytic in a neighborhood of zero and have a compact support. We denote this class of functions by
\mathcal{C}
. Algebraic formulas concerning the Taylor coefficients of these function around zero are also valid for formal power series. This is because the regularity imposed on functions in
\mathcal{C}
has no impact on the algebraic aspect of the result.

Notation.

\diag(x)
is the diagonal matrix with the diagonal
x
.
[m]f
is the coefficient of the monomial
m
in the series
f
.
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the class of smooth functions from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}
with compact support.

2
Dirac delta function

Let us now recall some basic facts about the Dirac delta function, which is defined as the generalized function
[
8
]
such that

\displaystyle\int\delta(x)\phi(x)dx=\phi(0)

(3)

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. First, we have

\displaystyle\delta(f(x))=\sum_{x_{i}:f(x_{i})=0}\frac{\delta(x-x_{i})}{|\det J_{f}(x_{i})|}

(4)

where
J_{f}(x)
is the Jacobian matrix of
f
at
x
,

\displaystyle J_{f}(x)=[\partial_{j}f_{i}(x)]_{i,j=1}^{n}

The Dirac delta function has the following Fourier representation

\displaystyle\delta(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i\langle\xi,u\rangle}d\xi

(5)

which can be written compactly as

\displaystyle\delta(u)=\int\hat{d}v\,e^{-\langle v,u\rangle}

(6)

where

\displaystyle\int\hat{d}v=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{i\mathbb{R}^{n}}dv

For any function
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle h(x)=\int du\,\delta(u-x)h(u)

By (
6
), we have

\displaystyle h(x)=\int\hat{d}vdu\,e^{-\langle v,u-x\rangle}h(u)

Applying on both sides the operator
g(\partial)
where
g
is some polynomial, we obtain the formula

\displaystyle g(\partial)h(x)|_{x=0}=\int\hat{d}vdu\,e^{-\langle v,u\rangle}g(v)h(u)

(7)

which relates operator calculus with complex integrals.

3
The proof

Let
f
be a function in
\mathcal{C}
. If
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is small enough such that the mapping
u\rightarrow\diag(x)f(u)
is a contraction, then the equation
u-\diag(x)f(u)
has unique solution. So there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for all
x\in B(0,\epsilon)
, the equation
u-\diag(x)f(u)
has unique solution, denoted by
g(x)
. Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}
and

\displaystyle I(x)=\int du\,\phi(u)\delta[u-\diag(x)f(u)],\quad x\in B(0,\epsilon).

From (
4
), we have

\displaystyle I(x)=\frac{\phi(g(x))}{\det(\delta_{ij}-x_{i}\partial_{j}f_{i}(g))}.

From the Fourier representation of Dirac delta function, we have

\displaystyle I(x)=\int\hat{d}vdu\,\phi(u)e^{-\langle v,u\rangle+\langle v,\diag(x)f(u)\rangle}.

Applying
\frac{\partial_{x_{1}}^{k_{1}}\dots\partial_{x_{n}}^{k_{n}}}{k_{1}!\dots k_{n}!}
at
x=0
on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle[x_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{k_{n}}]I(x)

\displaystyle=\int\hat{d}vdu\,\phi(u)e^{-\langle v,u\rangle}\frac{(v_{1}f_{1}(u))^{k_{1}}\dots(v_{n}f_{n}(u))^{k_{n}}}{k_{1}!\dots k_{n}!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{x_{1}}^{k_{1}}\dots\partial_{x_{n}}^{k_{n}}}{k_{1}!\dots k_{n}!}\phi(x)f_{1}(x)^{k_{1}}\dots f_{n}(x)^{k_{n}}|_{x=0}\quad\text{(by (\ref{pk}))}

\displaystyle=[x_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots x_{n}^{k_{n}}]\phi(x)f_{1}(x)^{k_{1}}\dots f_{n}(x)^{k_{n}},

which is the Lagrange-Good formula.

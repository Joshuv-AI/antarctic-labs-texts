The Critical Beta-splitting Random Tree: Heights and Related Results
David Aldous and Boris Pittel

Abstract
. In the critical beta-splitting model of a random n n -leaf binary tree, leaf-sets are recursively split into subsets, and a set of m m leaves is split into subsets containing i i and m − i m-i leaves with probabilities proportional to 1 / i ⁡ ( m − i ) 1/{i(m-i)} . We study the continuous-time model in which the holding time before that split is exponential with rate h m − 1 h_{m-1} , the harmonic number. We (sharply) evaluate the first two moments of the time-height D n D_{n} and of the edge-height L n L_{n} of a uniform random leaf (that is, the length of the path from the root to the leaf), and prove the corresponding CLTs. We study the correlation between the heights of two random leaves of the same tree realization, and analyze the expected number of splits necessary for a set of t t leaves to partially or completely break away from each other. We give tail bounds for the time-height and the edge-height of the tree , that is the maximal leaf heights. We show that there is a limit distribution for the size of a uniform random subtree, and derive the asymptotics of the mean size. Our proofs are based on asymptotic analysis of the attendant (sum-type) recurrences. The essential idea is to replace such a recursive equality by a pair of recursive inequalities for which matching asymptotic solutions can be found, allowing one to bound, both ways, the elusive explicit solution of the recursive equality. This reliance on recursive inequalities necessitates usage of Laplace transforms rather than Fourier characteristic functions.

1.2.
Outline of results

Our main focus is on two related random variables associated with the continuous-time random tree on

•

Theorem 1.1
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]

\displaystyle=\zeta^{-1}(2)\log n+O(1),

\displaystyle=(1+o(1))\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{3}(2)}\log n,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]=\zeta^{-1}(2)\log n+c_{0}-\tfrac{1}{2\zeta(2)}n^{-1}+O(n^{-2})

\mathrm{var\/}(D_{n})=\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{3}(2)}\log n+O(1).

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{n}]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\zeta(2)}\log^{2}n+\tfrac{\gamma\,\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{2}(2)}\log n+O(1),

Let

1.3.
Motivation and background

The one-parameter family at (
1.2
) was introduced in
[
3
]
in 1996 as a toy model for phylogenetic trees.
It was observed in
[
5
]
that, in splits
m\to(i,m-i)
in real-world phylogenetic trees,
the median size of the smaller subtree scaled roughly as
m^{1/2}
.
This data is not consistent with more classical random tree models, where the median size would be

O(\log m)
or
\Theta(m)
.
However, in the “critical” case
\beta=-1
of the model studied in this paper,
that median size is indeed order
m^{1/2}
, because

2\sum_{i<m^{1/2}}q(m,i)\to\tfrac{1}{2}\mbox{ as }m\to\infty.

Of course the model is not a biologically meaningful “forwards in time” model, but is
a mathematically basic model that could be used for comparison with more realistic models
[
14
]
that are deliberately constructed
in the mathematical biology literature to reproduce features of real phylogenetic trees.
The distributional results of this model might therefore be useful for some future “applied” project of that kind.

2.
The proofs

Let
\tau_{\nu}
be the holding time before a split of a subset of size
\nu
. So
\tau_{\nu}
has Exponential distribution with rate
h_{\nu-1}
. By the definition of the splitting
process, for
\nu\geq 2
we have: with
q(\nu,i)=\tfrac{\nu}{2h_{\nu-1}}\,\tfrac{1}{i(\nu-i)}
as at (
1.1
),

D_{\nu}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\tau_{\nu}+D_{i},&&\text{with probability }q(\nu,i)\tfrac{i}{\nu},\,\,i=1,\dots,\nu-1,\\
&\tau_{\nu}+D_{\nu-i},&&\text{with probability }q(\nu,i)\tfrac{\nu-i}{\nu},\,\,i=1,\dots,\nu-1.\end{aligned}\right.

Introduce
\phi_{\nu}(u)=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e^{uD_{\nu}}]
, the Laplace transform of the distribution of
D_{\nu}
; so,
\phi_{1}(u)=1
.
The equation above implies that for
\nu\geq 2
,

(2.1)

\phi_{\nu}(u)\!=\!\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\!q(\nu,k)\Bigl(\!\tfrac{k}{\nu}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(u(\tau_{\nu}+D_{k}))]+\tfrac{\nu-k}{\nu}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(u(\tau_{\nu}+D_{\nu-k}))]\!\Bigr)\\
=2\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[\exp(u\tau_{\nu})\bigr]\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k}{\nu}\phi_{k}(u)\,q_{\nu,k}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\phi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}.

Furthermore, introduce
f_{\nu}(u)=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e^{uL_{\nu}}]
, the Laplace transform of the distribution of
L_{\nu}
; so
f_{1}(u)=1
. In this case we have: for
\nu\geq 2
,

L_{\nu}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1+L_{i},&&\text{with probability }q(\nu,i)\tfrac{i}{\nu},\,\,i=1,\dots,\nu-1,\\
&1+L_{\nu-i},&&\text{with probability }q(\nu,i)\tfrac{\nu-i}{\nu},\,\,i=1,\dots,\nu-1.\end{aligned}\right.

Therefore

(2.2)

f_{\nu}(u)\!=\!\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\!q(\nu,k)\Bigl(\!\tfrac{k}{\nu}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(u(1+L_{k}))]+\tfrac{\nu-k}{\nu}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(u(1+L_{\nu-k}))]\!\Bigr)\\
=2e^{u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k}{\nu}f_{k}(u)\,q_{\nu,k}=\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{f_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}.

In particular, we make extensive use of the following
fundamental recurrence for
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu}]
:

(2.3)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu}]=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{k}]}{\nu-k}\biggr).

This follows directly from the hold-jump construction of the random tree, or by differentiating both sides of (
2.1
) at
u=0
.

2.1.
The moments of
D_{n}
.

Our first result includes one part of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

\displaystyle\zeta^{-1}(2)\log n\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]\leq\max\{0,1+\log(n-1)\},\,\,n\geq 2,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]=\zeta^{-1}(2)\log n+O(1).

Proof.

The proof has three steps.

(i)
Let us prove that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]\geq\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}\log n
. Introduce
\theta_{n}=A\log n
. Then
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{1}]=0=\theta_{1}
. If we find
A
such that

(2.4)

\theta_{n}\leq\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{\theta_{k}}{n-k}\biggr),\quad n\geq 2,

then, by induction on
n
,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]\geq\theta_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. We compute

\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{\theta_{k}}{n-k}\biggr)=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\biggl(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{A\log k}{n-k}\biggr)\\
=\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\biggl(1+A(\log n)h_{n-1}+A\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{\log(k/n)}{n(1-k/n)}\biggr)\\
=\theta_{n}+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\biggl(1+A\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{\log(k/n)}{n(1-k/n)}\biggr)\\
\geq\theta_{n}+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\biggl(1-A\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\log(1/x)}{1-x}\,dx\biggr).

The inequality holds since the integrand is positive and decreasing. Since

\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\log(1/x)}{1-x}\,dx=\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{0}^{1}x^{j}\log(1/x)\,dx=\sum_{j\geq 0}\tfrac{1}{(j+1)^{2}}=\zeta(2)=\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{6},

we deduce that (
2.4
) holds if we select
A=\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}=\zeta^{-1}(2)
.

Note.
The proof above is the harbinger of things to come, including the next part. The seemingly naive idea is to replace a recurrence equality by a recurrence
inequality
for which an exact solution can be found and then to use it to upper bound the otherwise-unattainable solution of the recurrence
equality
. Needless to say, it is critically important to have a good guess as to how that “hidden” solution behaves asymptotically.

(ii)
Let us prove that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]\leq f(n):=\max\{0,1+\log(n-1)\}
for
n\geq 2
.
This is true for
n=1,2
since
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{1}]=0
,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{2}]=1
.
Notice that
1+\log(x-1)\leq x-1
for
x\in(1,2]
. So
f(x)\leq g(x)
,
\forall\,x>1
,
where
g(x)=x-1
for
x\in[1,2]
,
g(x)=1+\log(x-1)
for
x\geq 2
, and
g(x)
is concave for
x\geq 1
.
So, similarly to (
2.4
), it is enough to show that
g(n)
satisfies

(2.5)

g(n)\geq\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\biggl(1+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{g(i)}{n-i}\biggr),\quad n\geq 2.

By concavity of
g(x)
for
x\geq 1
, we have

\frac{1}{h_{n-1}}\biggl(\,1+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{g(i)}{n-i}\biggr)\leq\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}+g\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{i}{n-i}\biggr)\\
=\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}+g\bigl(n-\tfrac{n-1}{h_{n-1}}\bigr)\leq\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}+g(n)-g^{\prime}(n)\bigl(\tfrac{n-1}{h_{n-1}}\bigr),\\

which is exactly
g(n)
, since
g^{\prime}(n)=\tfrac{1}{n-1}
for
n>1
.

(iii)

Write
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]=\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}\log n+u_{n}
, so that
u_{n}\geq 0
and
u_{1}=0
. Let us prove that
u_{n}=O(1)
. Using (
2.3
) we have

, as follows.

(2.25)

Z_{\nu}\overset{d}{=}\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\tau_{\nu}+D_{i}^{(1)})(\tau_{\nu}+D_{i}^{(2)}),&&\text{with probability }q(\nu,i)\cdot\tfrac{(i)_{2}}{(\nu)_{2}},\\
&(\tau_{\nu}+D_{\nu-i}^{(1)})(\tau_{\nu}+D_{\nu-i}^{(2)}),&&\text{with probability }q(\nu,i)\cdot\tfrac{(\nu-i)_{2}}{(\nu)_{2}},\\
&(\tau_{\nu}+D_{i}^{(1)})(\tau_{\nu}+D_{\nu-i}^{(2)}),&&\text{with probability }q(\nu,i)\cdot\tfrac{i(\nu-i)}{(\nu)_{2}},\\
&(\tau_{\nu}+D_{i}^{(2)})(\tau_{\nu}+D_{\nu-i}^{(1)}),&&\text{with probability }q(\nu,i)\cdot\tfrac{i(\nu-i)}{(\nu)_{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\tau_{\nu}
is the Exponential
(h_{\nu-1})
hold time.
The first two cases correspond to the two leaves being in the same subtree, so their heights are dependent, whereas
the last two cases correspond to the two leaves being in the different subtrees, so their heights are (conditionally) independent.

Consequently

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}|\,L_{\nu}=i]=\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{h^{2}_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{2}{h_{\nu-1}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{i}]\Bigr)\tfrac{(i)_{2}}{(\nu)_{2}}\\
+\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{h^{2}_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{2}{h_{\nu-1}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu-i}]\Bigr)\tfrac{(\nu-i)_{2}}{(\nu)_{2}}\\
+2\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{h^{2}_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]\bigr)+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]\cdot\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]\Bigr)\tfrac{i(\nu-i)}{(\nu)_{2}},

or, with a bit of algebra,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}|\,L_{\nu}=i]=\tfrac{2}{h^{2}_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{2i\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]}{\nu h_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{2(\nu-i)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]}{\nu h_{\nu-1}}\\
+\tfrac{1}{(\nu)_{2}}\Bigl((i)_{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{i}]+(\nu-i)_{2}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu-i}]+2i(\nu-i)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]\,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]\Bigr).

Using (
2.24
) we obtain then

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}q(\nu,i)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}|\,L_{\nu}=i]=\tfrac{2}{h^{2}_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{2}{h_{\nu-1}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]}{\nu-i}\\
+\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]\,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]+\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{i}]}{\nu-i}.

So, using
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu}]=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\Bigl(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]}{\nu-i}\Bigr)
, we arrive at

(2.26)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{i}]}{\nu-i}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{2\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu}]}{h_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]\,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}].

We use (
2.26
) to sharply estimate
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]
and then estimate
r_{n}=\frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{2}[D_{n}]}{\text{Var}(D_{n})}
. To start,

\tfrac{2\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu}]}{h_{\nu-1}}=2\zeta^{-1}(2)+O(\log^{-1}\nu).

Secondly,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]\,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]=\bigl[\zeta^{-1}(2)\log i+c_{0}+O(i^{-1})\bigr]\\
\times\bigl[\zeta^{-1}(2)\log(\nu-i)+c_{0}+O((\nu-i)^{-1})\bigr].\\

The leading contribution to
\sum_{i}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]\,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]
comes from

\zeta^{-2}(2)\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\log i\cdot\log(\nu-i)\\
=\zeta^{-2}(2)(\nu-1)\log^{2}\nu+2\zeta^{-2}(2)\log\nu\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\log(i/\nu)\\
+\zeta^{-2}(2)\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\log(i/\nu)\log((\nu-i)/\nu)\\
=\zeta^{-2}(2)\nu\log^{2}\nu+2\zeta^{-2}(2)\nu\log\nu\int_{0}^{1}\log x\,dx+O(\nu)\\
=\zeta^{-2}(2)\big(\nu\log^{2}\nu-2\nu\log\nu\bigr)+O(\nu).

The secondary contribution to
\sum_{i}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]\,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]
comes from
c_{0}\zeta^{-1}(2)(\log i+\log(\nu-i))
, and it equals
2c_{0}\zeta^{-1}(2)\nu\log\nu+O(\nu)
. The terms
c_{0},O(i^{-1}),O((\nu-i)^{-1})
contribute jointly
O(\nu)
. Altogether,

\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{i}]\,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{\nu-i}]=\zeta^{-2}(2)\big(\nu\log^{2}\nu-2\nu\log\nu\bigr)+2c_{0}\zeta^{-1}(2)\nu\log\nu+O(\nu).

Therefore the equation (
2.26
) becomes

(2.27)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]=\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{i}]}{\nu-i}+2\zeta^{-1}(2)\\
+\zeta^{-2}(2)\big(\log\nu-2-\gamma\bigr)+2c_{0}\zeta^{-1}(2)+O(\log^{-1}\nu).

Let us look at an approximate solution
\widetilde{E}(\nu):=A\log^{2}\nu+B\log\nu
. The RHS of the above equation is

\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)(A\log^{2}i+B\log i)}{\nu-i}+2\zeta^{-1}(2)+\zeta^{-2}(2)\big(\log\nu-2-\gamma\bigr)+2c_{0}\zeta^{-1}(2)+O(\log^{-1}\nu).\\

Here, since
\sum_{i}\tfrac{i-1}{\nu-i}=(\nu-1)(h_{\nu-1}-1)
, we have

\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\log^{2}i}{\nu-i}=\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\bigl(\log(i/\nu)+\log\nu\bigr)^{2}}{\nu-i}\\
=\tfrac{h_{\nu-1}-1}{h_{\nu-1}}\log^{2}\nu+\tfrac{2\log\nu}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\log(i/\nu)}{\nu-i}+\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\log^{2}(i/\nu)}{\nu-i}\\
=\log^{2}\nu-\log\nu+\gamma+2\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{x\log x}{1-x}\,dx+O\bigl(\log^{-1}\nu\bigr)\\
=\log^{2}\nu-\log\nu+\gamma+2(1-\zeta(2))+O\bigl(\log^{-1}\nu\bigr),

and

\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\log i}{\nu-i}=\log\nu-1+O(\log^{-1}\nu).

Therefore, with
\widetilde{E}(\cdot)
instead of
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\cdot}]
, the RHS of the equation (
2.27
) becomes

A\bigl(\log^{2}\nu-\log\nu+\gamma+2(1-\zeta(2))\bigr)+B\bigl(\log\nu-1)\\
+\zeta^{-2}(2)\big(\log\nu-2-\gamma\bigr)+2(c_{0}+1)\zeta^{-1}(2)+O(\log^{-1}\nu).

And we need this to be equal to
\widetilde{E}(\nu):=A\log^{2}\nu+B\log\nu
within an additive error
O(\log^{-1}\nu)
, meaning that

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-A+B+\zeta^{-2}(2)=B,

\displaystyle A\bigl[\gamma+2(1-\zeta(2))\bigr]-B-(2+\gamma)\zeta^{-2}(2)+2(c_{0}+1)\zeta^{-1}(2)=0,

or explicitly

(2.28)

A=\zeta^{-2}(2),\quad B=2c_{0}\zeta^{-1}(2).

With these
A
and
B
, our approximation
\widetilde{E}(\nu)
satisfies the same equation (
2.27
) as
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]
, excluding an exact value
of the remainder term
O(\log^{-1}\nu)
, of course. Consequently,
\Delta(\nu):=\big|\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]-\widetilde{E}(\nu)\big|
satisfies

(2.29)

\Delta(\nu)\leq\tfrac{1}{(\nu-1)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(i-1)\Delta(i)}{\nu-i}+O(\log^{-1}\nu),\quad\Delta(1)=0.

With
\mathcal{U}_{\nu}:=(\nu-1)\Delta(\nu)
, the resulting equation is a special case of the later equation (
2.62
) with the remainder term
O(\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu)
, when
t=2
. Applying the bound for the solution proved there, we obtain that
\mathcal{U}_{\nu}=O(\nu)
, or that
\Delta(\nu)=O(1)
. Thus

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]=A\log^{2}\nu+B\log\nu+O(1).

Combining this formula with (
2.28
),
r_{n}=\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\nu}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{2}[D_{n}]}{\text{Var}(D_{n})}
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[D_{n}]=\zeta^{-1}(2)\log n+c_{0}+O(n^{-1})
, we compute

r_{n}=\frac{\zeta^{-2}(2)\log^{2}n+2c_{0}\zeta^{-1}(2)\log n-\bigl(\zeta^{-1}(2)\log n+c_{0}\bigr)^{2}+O(1)}{\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{3}(2)}\log n+O(1)}=O(\log^{-1}n).\\

∎

Note.
We do not need the h-ansatz in the rest of the paper.

2.6.
Bounding the time-height of the random tree.

Consider now the time-height
\mathcal{D}_{n}
of the random tree itself,
that is the maximum leaf time-height.
We re-state Theorem
1.4
, together with a tail bound on
D_{n}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

(i)
For some
\rho>0
and all
\varepsilon\in(0,\pi^{2}/6-1)
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl(D_{n}\geq\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon)\log n\Bigr)=O(n^{-\rho\varepsilon}).

(ii)
For some
\rho^{\prime}
and all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl(\mathcal{D}_{n}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\log n\Bigr)=O(n^{-\rho^{\prime}\varepsilon}).

Proof.

(i)
Since the tree with
\nu
leaves has
\nu-1
non-leaf vertices, rather crudely
D_{\nu}
is stochastically dominated by the sum of
\nu-1

independent exponentials with rate
1
. Therefore, for
u<1
, the Laplace transform
\phi_{\nu}(u):=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e^{uD_{\nu}}]
is bounded above by
(1-u)^{-\nu}
.
Recall (
2.1
):

\phi_{\nu}(u)=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\phi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k},\quad\nu\geq 2.

Pick
\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon
and introduce
\alpha=\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})
(so
\alpha<1
) and
\psi_{\nu}(u)=\exp\bigl(u\alpha\log\nu\bigr)
. Let us prove that

(2.30)

\psi_{\nu}(u)\geq\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\psi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k},

if
u\in(0,1)
is sufficiently small, and
\nu>1
sufficiently large.

First note that

\psi_{k}(u)=\psi_{\nu}(u)\exp\bigl(u\alpha\log(k/\nu)\bigr),\quad k\leq\nu.

Therefore

\tfrac{1}{\psi_{\nu}(u)(h_{\nu-1}-u)}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\psi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl(u\alpha\log(k/\nu)\bigr)}{\nu-k}\\
=\bigl(1-\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr)^{-1}\cdot\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl(u\alpha\log(k/\nu)\bigr)-1}{\nu-k}\Bigr)\\
=\biggl(1+\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}+O\bigl(\tfrac{u^{2}}{h_{\nu-1}^{2}}\bigr)\biggr)\\
\times\biggl[1+\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\!\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\alpha\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}+O\biggl(\tfrac{u^{2}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\biggr)\biggr];

(where we used
|e^{x}-1-x|\leq x^{2}/2
, for
x\leq 0
).
So, since
\alpha=\zeta^{-1}(2)(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})
,

(2.31)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{\psi_{\nu}(u)(h_{\nu-1}-u)}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\psi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\Bigl(1+\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\Bigr)+O\bigl(\tfrac{u^{2}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\Bigl(1+\alpha\bigl(-\zeta(2)+\tfrac{\log(\nu e)}{\nu-1}\bigr)+O\bigl(\tfrac{u^{2}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl(\varepsilon^{\prime}-\zeta^{-1}(2)(1+\varepsilon)\tfrac{\log(\nu e)}{\nu-1}\bigr)+O\bigl(\tfrac{u^{2}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr).

To justify the inequality above:
\tfrac{\log x}{1-x}
increases for
x\leq 1
, so that

\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\log x}{1-x}\,dx-\int_{0}^{1/\nu}\tfrac{\log x}{1-x}\,dx\\
\leq-\zeta(2)+\tfrac{\nu}{\nu-1}\int_{0}^{1/\nu}\log(1/x)\,dx=-\zeta(2)+\tfrac{\log(\nu e)}{\nu-1}.

The big-O term is uniform over all
u\in(0,1)
and
\nu>1
. It follows then from (
2.31
) that there exist
u(\varepsilon^{\prime})\in(0,1)
and
\nu(\varepsilon^{\prime})>1
such that (
2.30
) holds for
u\in(0,u(\varepsilon^{\prime}))
and
\nu\geq\nu(\varepsilon^{\prime})
.
Furthermore, for
u\in(0,u(\varepsilon^{\prime}))
and
\nu\leq\nu(\varepsilon^{\prime})
,

\tfrac{\phi_{\nu}(u)}{\psi_{\nu}(u)}\leq A(\varepsilon^{\prime}):=\tfrac{(1-u(\varepsilon^{\prime}))^{-\nu(\varepsilon^{\prime})}}{\exp(u(\varepsilon^{\prime})\alpha\log(\nu(\varepsilon^{\prime}))},

since, for
\alpha\leq 1
,
\tfrac{(1-u)^{-\nu}}{\exp(u\alpha\log\nu)}
attains its maximum on
[0,\nu(\varepsilon^{\prime})]
at
\nu(\varepsilon^{\prime})
.
Combining this inequality with (
2.30
), by induction on
\nu
we obtain that
\phi_{\nu}(u)\leq A(\varepsilon^{\prime})\psi_{\nu}(u)
for all
\nu>1

and
u\leq u^{\prime}:=u(\varepsilon^{\prime})
.
The rest is easy:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl(D_{n}\geq\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon)\log n\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(u^{\prime}D_{n})]}{\exp\bigl(u^{\prime}\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon)\log n\bigr)}\leq\tfrac{A(\varepsilon^{\prime})\psi_{\nu}(u^{\prime})}{\exp\bigl(u^{\prime}\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon)\log n\bigr)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle A(\varepsilon^{\prime})\exp\Bigl[u^{\prime}\bigl(\alpha-\tfrac{6}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon)\bigr)\log n\Bigr]=\frac{A(\varepsilon^{\prime})}{n^{\tfrac{6u^{\prime}}{\pi^{2}}(\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\prime})}}.

(ii)
Predictably, we will use the union bound, which makes it necessary to upper-bound

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(D_{n}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\log n\bigr)
. To this end, we use a cruder version of the argument in the part
(i)
. Set

\alpha=1+\varepsilon/2
and choose
u=\tfrac{1}{\alpha}
. Denoting
z_{\nu}=u/h_{\nu-1}
we bound

\tfrac{1}{\psi_{\nu}(u)(h_{\nu-1}-u)}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\psi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl(u\alpha\log(k/\nu)\bigr)}{\nu-k}\\
=\tfrac{h_{\nu-1}}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\cdot\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k/\nu}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{h_{\nu-1}}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\cdot\biggl(1-\tfrac{\nu-1}{\nu h_{\nu-1}}\biggr)^{u\alpha}\\
\leq\exp\bigl(-\log(1-z_{\nu})-z_{\nu}\tfrac{\alpha(\nu-1)}{\nu}\bigr).

Since
z_{\nu}\to 0
, the last expression is below
1
for
\nu\in[\nu(\alpha),n]
. Therefore, arguing closely to the part
(i)
, we see that
\phi_{n}(u)=O(\psi_{n}(u))
. Consequently

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(D_{n}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\log n\bigr)=O\biggl(\tfrac{\psi_{n}(u)}{\exp\bigl(2u(1+\varepsilon)\log n\bigr)}\biggr)\\
=O\biggl(n^{-\tfrac{2(1+\varepsilon)}{1+\varepsilon/2}+1}\biggr),

implying, by the union bound, that

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(\mathcal{D}_{n}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\log n\bigr)\leq n\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(D_{n}\geq 2(1+\varepsilon)\log n\bigr)=O\Bigl(n^{-\tfrac{2(1+\varepsilon)}{1+\varepsilon/2}+2}\Bigr)=O\Bigl(n^{-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon/2}}\Bigr).\\

∎

2.7.
Asymptotic normality of
D_{n}
.

Here is one part of Theorem
1.7
.

Proposition 2.6
.

In distribution, and with all of its moments,

\frac{D_{n}-\zeta^{-1}(2)\log n}{\sqrt{\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{3}(2)}\log n}}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{Normal}(0,1).

In particular, this provides a proof of the first-order result

\mathrm{var\/}(D_{n})\sim\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{3}(2)}\log n,

without having to rely on the h-ansatz,
as stated in Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

By a general theorem due to Curtiss
[
6
]
, it suffices to show that for
|u|=\Theta(\log^{-1/2}n)
and properly chosen
\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}>0
, the Laplace transform
\phi_{n}(u)=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e^{uD_{n}}]
satisfies

(2.32)

\phi_{n}(u)=(1+o(1))\exp\bigl[(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2})\log n\bigr].

Recall from (
2.1
) that

(2.33)

\phi_{\nu}(u)=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\phi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k},\quad\nu\geq 2.

Define a function

\Psi_{\nu}(u)=\exp\bigl[(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2})\log\nu\bigr],\quad\nu\in[1,n];

obviously
\Psi_{1}(u)=1=\phi_{1}(u)
.
We will use induction on
\nu
to prove a stronger result, namely that there exist
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
such that for
|u|=\Theta(\log^{-1/2}n)
, the ratio
\tfrac{\phi_{\nu}(u)}{\Psi_{\nu}(u)}
converges to
1
, uniformly over
n\geq\nu\to\infty
, sufficiently fast. Pick
\delta\in(0,1/6)
, and set
\nu_{n}=\lceil\exp(\log^{\delta}n)\rceil
, so in particular
u\log\nu_{n}\to 0
. Introduce
\Psi^{*}_{\nu}(u):=1+u\alpha\log\nu
.
For
u>0
, we have

(2.34)

\tfrac{1}{(h_{\nu-1}-u)\Psi^{*}_{\nu}(u)}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\Psi^{*}_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0}u^{j_{1}+j_{2}}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr)^{j_{1}}(-\alpha\log\nu)^{j_{2}}\\
\times\biggl((1+u\alpha\log\nu)h_{\nu-1}+u\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\biggr)\\
=\Bigl(1+u\bigl(\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}-\alpha\log\nu\bigr)+O(\alpha^{2}u^{2}\log^{2}\nu)\Bigr)\cdot\bigl(1+u\alpha\log\nu-\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\Theta(\alpha)\bigr)\\
=1+\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}(1-\Theta(\alpha))+O\bigl((\alpha^{2}+1)u^{2}\log^{2}\nu\bigr)\\
\left\{\begin{aligned} &>1,&&\text{if }\nu\leq\nu_{n},\,\alpha>0\text{ and small},\\
&<1,&&\text{if }\nu\leq\nu_{n},\,\alpha>0\text{ and large}.\end{aligned}\right.

(For the bottom part we used
\delta<\tfrac{1}{6}
.) And the inequalities are interchanged if
u<0
. Combining this with (
2.33
), we conclude that

\phi_{\nu}(u)=1+O(|u|\log\nu)=\exp\bigl(O(|u|\log\nu)\bigr)
, uniformly for
\nu\leq\nu_{n}
.
So, for bounded
\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}
,

(2.35)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{\nu\leq\nu_{n}}\big|\tfrac{\phi_{\nu}(u)}{\Psi_{\nu}(u)}-1\big|=0.

Thus, we need to prove existence of
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
such that the property above holds for
\nu\geq\nu_{n}
, as well.
To this end, let us determine
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
from the condition that
\Psi_{\nu}(u)
,
(\nu\in[\nu_{n},n])
, satisfies the recursive inequality

(2.36)

\Psi_{\nu}(u)\geq(\leq)\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\biggl(\,\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\Psi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}\biggr),\quad\nu\in[\nu_{n},n].

First of all, we have

\Psi_{k}(u)=\Psi_{\nu}(u)\exp\bigl[\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)\log(k/\nu)\bigr],\quad k\leq\nu.

Therefore

(2.37)

\tfrac{1}{\Psi_{\nu}(u)(h_{\nu-1}-u)}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\Psi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)\log(k/\nu)\bigr]}{\nu-k}\\
=\bigl(1-\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr)^{-1}\cdot\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)\log(k/\nu)\bigr]-1}{\nu-k}\Bigr)\\
=\bigl(1-\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr)^{-1}\cdot\biggl(1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)\log x\bigr]-1}{1-x}\,dx+O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|\log\nu_{n}}{\nu_{n}}\bigr)\biggr).

In the final line, the bottom integral does not depend on
\nu
.
Let us first justify the remainder term. Define
f(k/\nu)
as the
k
-th term in the previous sum, (
k<\nu
), and, for continuity, set
f(\nu/\nu)=-\nu^{-1}(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2})
. It can be checked that

f^{\prime\prime}_{k}(k/\nu)
does not change its sign on
[1,\nu]
. So, replacing the sum with the integral for
k
varying continuously from
1
to
\nu
, we introduce the error on the order of the sum of absolute values of

f(k/\nu)\Big|^{\nu}_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad f^{\prime}_{k}(k/\nu)\Big|^{\nu}_{1}.

The dominant contribution to each of these terms comes from
k=1
. Since for
\sigma\in(0,1)
the function
\tfrac{z^{\sigma}-1}{z}
decreases for
z\geq\sigma^{-1}\log\tfrac{1}{1-\sigma}
, we bound

|f(1/\nu)|\leq\tfrac{\exp(|u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}|\log\nu_{n})-1}{\nu_{n}}=O\bigl(\nu_{n}^{-1}|u|\log\nu_{n}\bigr).

And the bound for
|f^{\prime}_{k}(1/\nu)|
is even better. So the sum in question is of order
O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|\log\nu_{n}}{\nu_{n}}\bigr)

uniformly for
\nu\geq\nu_{n}
. Extending the resulting integral to the full
[0,\nu]
, we introduce the second error on the order of

(2.38)

\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)\log(k/\nu)\bigr]-1}{\nu-k}\,dk=O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|\log\nu_{n}}{\nu_{n}}\bigr).

The sum of the two error terms is
O\bigl(\nu^{-1}|u|\log\nu)
, and dividing it by
h_{\nu-1}
we get
O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|}{\nu_{n}}\bigr)
.

Let us sharply estimate the bottom integral in (
2.37
). By (
2.38
), the contribution to this integral coming from
x\in(0,1/\nu_{n}]
is
O(\nu^{-1}_{n}|u|\log\nu_{n})
. And for
x\in[1/\nu_{n},1]
, we have
|u|\log(1/x)\leq|u|\log\nu_{n}\to 0
, i.e. we can use the Taylor expansion

\tfrac{\exp\bigl[\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)\log x\bigr]-1}{1-x}=\tfrac{\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)\log x}{1-x}+\tfrac{\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)^{2}\log^{2}x}{2(1-x)}+O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|^{3}\log^{3}(1/x)}{1-x}\bigr).\\

This means that, at the price of the error term of the order
\nu^{-1}_{n}|u|\log\nu_{n}+|u|^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\log^{3}(1/x)}{1-x}\,dx
,
we can use the expansion above for all
x\in(0,1]
.

So, using (
2.21
), we obtain

\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[\bigl(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\bigr)\log x\bigr]-1}{1-x}\,dx\\
=-\tfrac{\alpha_{1}\zeta(2)u}{h_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{u^{2}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl[\alpha_{1}^{2}\zeta(3)-\alpha_{2}\zeta(2)\bigr]+O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|^{3}}{h_{\nu-1}}+\nu^{-1}_{n}|u|\bigr).

Consequently, for
\nu\geq\nu_{n}\bigl(=\lceil\exp(\log^{\delta}n)\rceil)
,

(2.39)

\tfrac{1}{\Psi_{\nu}(u)(h_{\nu-1}-u)}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\Psi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}=1+\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl(1-\alpha_{1}\zeta(2)\bigr)\\
+\tfrac{u^{2}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl[\alpha_{1}^{2}\zeta(3)-\alpha_{2}\zeta(2)\bigr]+O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|^{3}}{h_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{|u|}{\nu_{n}}\bigr)\\
=1+\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl(1-\alpha_{1}\zeta(2)\bigr)+O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|^{3}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr),

if we select
\alpha_{2}=\tfrac{\alpha_{1}^{2}\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)}
, which we certainly do. Suppose
u>0
; set
\alpha_{1}=\zeta^{-1}(2)+u^{b}
,
b\in(1,2)
. Then, uniformly for
\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]
, we have

1+\tfrac{u}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl(1-\alpha_{1}\zeta(2)\bigr)+O\bigl(\tfrac{|u|^{3}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigr)=1-\tfrac{\zeta^{-1}(2)u^{b+1}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl(1+O(u^{2-b}))<1.

So, (
2.39
) becomes

\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}-u}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\Psi_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}\leq\Psi_{\nu}(u).

This equation and the equation (
2.33
) together imply, by induction on
\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]
, that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]}\tfrac{\phi_{\nu}(u)}{\Psi_{\nu}(u)}\leq 1
. Now,

\Psi_{\nu}(u)=\exp\bigl[(u\alpha_{1}+u^{2}\alpha_{2})\log\nu\bigr]\\
=\exp\Bigl[\bigl(u\zeta^{-1}(2)+u^{2}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{3}(2)}\bigr)\log\nu+O(u^{b+1}\log\nu)\Bigr]\\
\sim\exp\Bigl[\bigl(u\zeta^{-1}(2)+u^{2}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{3}(2)}\bigr)\log\nu\Bigr],

since
u^{b+1}\log n=O\Bigl(\log^{-\tfrac{b-1}{2}}n\Bigr)
and
b>1
. Therefore

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]}\tfrac{\phi_{\nu}(u)}{\Psi_{\nu}(u)}\leq 1.

Analogously, setting
\alpha_{1}=\zeta^{-1}(2)-u^{b}
, we have

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\min_{\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]}\tfrac{\phi_{\nu}(u)}{\Psi_{\nu}(u)}\geq 1.

So, for
u=\Theta(\log^{-1/2}n)>0
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{\phi_{n}(u)}{\exp\Bigl[\bigl(u\zeta^{-1}(2)+u^{2}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{3}(2)}\bigr)\log n\Bigr]}=1.

The case
u<0
is completely similar, so that the last equation holds for
u=-\Theta(\log^{-1/2}n)<0
as well.
∎

2.8.
The moments of edge-heights of the leaves

Recall that
L_{n}
denotes the edge-height of a uniform random leaf.
In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
via the two Propositions below.

Proposition 2.7
.

(2.40)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{n}]=\tfrac{1}{2\zeta(2)}\log^{2}n+\tfrac{\gamma\,\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)}{\zeta^{2}(2)}\log n+O(1).

Proof.

The straightforward recurrence for
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}]
is

(2.41)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}]=1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{k}]}{\nu-k}.

Write
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}]=A\log^{2}\nu+B\log\nu+u_{\nu}
, so that
u_{1}=0
. We need to show that
u_{\nu}=O(1)
, if we select

A
and
B
appropriately. (Sure enough, these will be the constants in the claim.) Using (
2.41
), we have

(2.42)

u_{\nu}=1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{u_{k}}{\nu-k}+A\biggl(\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{\log^{2}k}{\nu-k}-\log^{2}\nu\biggr)\\
+B\biggl(\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{\log k}{\nu-k}-\log\nu\biggr).

Here, by (
2.12
),

\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{\log k}{\nu-k}-\log\nu=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}=-\tfrac{\zeta(2)}{h_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{\log(2\pi e)}{\nu h_{\nu-1}}+O(\nu^{-2}),\\

and, combining the equation above with (
2.4
), we also have

\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{\log^{2}k}{\nu-k}-\log^{2}\nu=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)\cdot(\log(k/\nu)+2\log\nu)}{\nu-k}\\
=\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{h_{\nu-1}}+O(\nu^{-1}\log\nu)+2\bigl(-\tfrac{\zeta(2)\log\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}+\tfrac{\log(2\pi e)\log\nu}{\nu h_{\nu-1}}+O(\nu^{-2}\log\nu)\biggr).\\

Plugging the estimates above into (
2.42
) and using
\log\nu=h_{\nu-1}-\gamma+O(\nu^{-1})
, we get

u_{\nu}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{u_{k}}{\nu-k}+(1-2A\zeta(2))+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl[2A(\gamma\zeta(2)+\zeta(3))-B\zeta(2)\bigr]+O(\nu^{-1}\log\nu).\\

So, selecting
A
and
B
such that the
(A,B)
-dependent coefficients are both zeros, i. e.
A=\tfrac{1}{2\zeta(2)}
,
B=\tfrac{\gamma\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)}
, we arrive at

u_{\nu}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{u_{k}}{\nu-k}+O(\nu^{-1}\log^{2}\nu)\biggr).

From the proof of Proposition
2.3
(starting with (
2.22
)), it follows that
u_{\nu}=O(1)
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

\mathrm{var\/}(L_{n})=\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{3\zeta^{3}(2)}\log^{3}n+O(1).

Proof.

(i)
The key is

Lemma 2.9
.

Setting
\bar{v}_{n}:=\mathrm{var\/}(L_{n})
, we have

(2.43)

\bar{v}_{n}=-1+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{\bar{v}_{k}+(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{n}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{k}])^{2}}{n-k}.

Note.
In particular,
\bar{v}_{2}=0
as it should be, since
L_{2}\equiv 1
, unlike
D_{2}
which is distributed exponentially with rate
1
.

Proof.

Differentiating twice both sides of (
2.2
) at
u=0
, we get

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}^{2}]=1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{k}^{2}]}{\nu-k}+\tfrac{2}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k\in[\nu-1]}\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{k}]}{\nu-k}\\
=2\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}]-1+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{k}^{2}]}{n-k}\\
=2\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}]-1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{2}[L_{k}]}{\nu-k}+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\bar{v}_{k}}{\nu-k}.

Since
\bar{v}_{\nu}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}^{2}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{2}[L_{\nu}]
, the equation above becomes

\bar{v}_{\nu}=2E[L_{\nu}]-1+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\bar{v}_{k}+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{2}[L_{k}]}{\nu-k}-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}^{2}[L_{\nu}],

and it is easy to check that this equation is equivalent to the claim.
∎

(ii)
Using Proposition
2.7
, we compute, for

A=\tfrac{1}{2\zeta(2)}
,
B=\tfrac{\gamma\zeta(2)+\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)}
,

\bigl(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{k}]\bigr)^{2}=\Bigl(A\bigl(\log^{2}\nu-\log^{2}k\bigr)+B\bigl(\log\nu-\log k\bigr)+O(1)\Bigr)^{2}\\
=\bigl[2A(\log(k/\nu))\log\nu\bigr]^{2}+O\bigl[\mathcal{P}(\log(\nu/k))\log\nu\bigr],

where
\mathcal{P}(\eta)
is a fourth-degree polynomial. Therefore, invoking (
2.4
), we have

\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{\nu}]-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{k}])^{2}}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{4A^{2}\log^{2}\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}+O(1)\\
=\tfrac{8A^{2}\zeta(3)\log^{2}\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}+O(1)=8A^{2}\zeta(3)\log\nu+O(1).

So, since
A=\tfrac{1}{2\zeta(2)}
, the equation (
2.43
) becomes

(2.44)

\bar{v}_{\nu}=\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}\log\nu+O(1)+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\bar{v}_{k}}{\nu-k}.

Let us use this recurrence to show that, for appropriately chosen
A^{*}
,

\bar{v}_{\nu}={\mathcal{V}}_{\nu}+O(1),\quad{\mathcal{V}}_{\nu}:=A^{*}\log^{3}\nu.

Here
O(1)
is uniform over all
\nu\geq 2
. We compute

\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{3}k}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\bigl(\log\nu+\log(k/\nu)\bigr)^{3}}{\nu-k}\\
=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(\log^{3}\nu\,h_{\nu-1}+3\log^{2}\nu\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\\
\qquad\qquad+3\log\nu\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}+\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{3}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\biggr)\\
=\log^{3}\nu+\tfrac{3\log^{2}\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}+O(1)\\
=\log^{3}\nu-3\zeta(2)\log\nu+O(1).

It follows that

\displaystyle\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}\log\nu+

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\mathcal{V}_{k}}{\nu-k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{\nu}+\Bigl(\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}-3A^{*}\zeta(2)\Bigr)\log\nu+O(1)=\mathcal{V}_{\nu}+O(1),

if we select
A^{*}=\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{3\zeta^{3}(2)}
. Combining this equation with (
2.44
), and using induction we
obtain that
|\bar{v}_{\nu}-\mathcal{V}_{\nu}|\leq C
for some absolute constant
C
.
∎

2.9.
Bounding the edge-height of the random tree.

As with the time-height in section
2.6
, we use a tail bound on the edge-height of a random leaf to obtain a tail bound for the edge-height of the tree itself.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
L_{n}
denote the edge-height of the uniformly random leaf, and let
\mathcal{L}_{n}
denote the largest edge-height of a leaf.

(1)
For
\varepsilon>0
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(L_{n}\geq\tfrac{3}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon)\log^{2}n\bigr)=O\bigl(n^{-\Theta(\varepsilon)}\bigr).

(2)

Let
\beta=\min_{\alpha>1/\log 2}\bigl[\alpha+\tfrac{4\alpha^{2}\zeta(3)}{\alpha\log 2-1}\bigr]\approx 42.9
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\Bigl(\mathcal{L}_{n}\geq(1+\varepsilon)\beta\log^{2}n)\Bigr)\leq\exp\bigl(-\Theta(\varepsilon\log n)\bigr).

Proof.

(1)
First of all,
f_{\nu}(u):=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e^{uL_{\nu}}]\leq e^{u(\nu-1)}
for
u\geq 0
. By (
2.2
), we have:

f_{\nu}(u):=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e^{uL_{\nu}}]=\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{f_{k}(u)}{\nu-k},\quad\nu\in[2,n].

Consider
u=\tfrac{v}{\log n}
, and introduce
g_{k}(u)=\exp(u\alpha\log^{2}k)
,
\alpha>0
yet to be determined.
Let us prove that there exists
v=v(\alpha)
sufficiently small, and
\nu(\alpha)
sufficiently large such that

(2.45)

g_{\nu}(u)\geq\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k},\quad\forall\nu\in[\nu(a),n],

We compute

(2.46)

\tfrac{1}{g_{\nu}(u)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp[u\alpha(\log^{2}k-\log^{2}\nu)]}{\nu-k}\\
=1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp[u\alpha(\log^{2}k-\log^{2}\nu)]-1}{\nu-k}\\
\leq 1+\tfrac{u\alpha}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}k-\log^{2}\nu}{\nu-k}+O\biggl(u^{2}\log\nu\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\!\biggr).\\

The big-Oh term is
O(u^{2}\log\nu)
, and

\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}k-\log^{2}\nu}{\nu-k}=\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}+2\log\nu\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}=-\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{3}\log\nu+O(1).\\

Therefore

\tfrac{1}{g_{\nu}(u)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}\leq 1-\tfrac{\alpha u\pi^{2}}{3}+O\bigl(u\log^{-1}\nu+u^{2}\log\nu\bigr),

implying that

(2.47)

\tfrac{e^{u}}{g_{\nu}(u)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}\leq 1-u\bigl(\tfrac{\alpha\pi^{2}}{3}-1\bigr)+O\bigl(u\log^{-1}\nu+u^{2}\log\nu\bigr).

Recalling that
u=\tfrac{v}{\log n}
, we obtain that for
\alpha>\tfrac{3}{\pi^{2}}
there exists a sufficiently small
v(\alpha)>0
, and
a sufficiently large
\nu(\alpha)
, such that for
v\leq v(\alpha)
and
n\geq\nu\geq\nu(\alpha)
, we have

(2.48)

\tfrac{e^{u}}{g_{\nu}(u)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}\leq 1.

Furthermore, for
u\leq\tfrac{v(\alpha)}{\log n}
and
\nu\leq\nu(\alpha)
,

(2.49)

\tfrac{f_{\nu}(u)}{g_{\nu}(u)}\leq\tfrac{\exp(u\nu)}{\exp(u\alpha\log^{2}\nu)}\leq\exp\bigl[\tfrac{v(\alpha)\nu(\alpha)}{\log n}\bigr].

By induction on
\nu\in[\nu(a),n]
, it follows that for those
\nu
’s

f_{\nu}(u)\leq\exp\bigl[\tfrac{v(\alpha)\nu(\alpha)}{\log n}\bigr]g_{\nu}(u)\Longrightarrow f_{n}(u)\leq\exp\bigl[\tfrac{v(\alpha)\nu(\alpha)}{\log n}\bigr]g_{n}(u),

provided that
\alpha>\tfrac{3}{\pi^{2}}
, and
u\leq\tfrac{v(\alpha)}{\log n}
. So, given
\varepsilon>0
, we set
\alpha=\tfrac{3}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon/2)
,
\alpha^{\prime}=\tfrac{3}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon)
, and bound

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\bigl(L_{n}\geq\tfrac{3}{\pi^{2}}(1+\varepsilon)\log^{2}n\bigr)=O\bigl(\tfrac{\exp(u\alpha\log^{2}n)}{\exp(u\alpha^{\prime}\log^{2}n)}\bigr)\Big|_{u=\tfrac{v(\alpha)}{\log n}}=O\bigl(n^{-\Theta(\varepsilon)}\bigr).\\

This is the assertion of
(1)
.

(2)
We need a more explicit, but cruder, version of (
2.47
), again for
u=O(\log^{-1}n)
. Instead of (
2.46
), we bound

\tfrac{1}{g_{\nu}(u)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}\leq 1+\tfrac{u\alpha}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}k-\log^{2}\nu}{\nu-k}+\tfrac{2\alpha^{2}u^{2}\log^{2}\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}.

Here

\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}k-\log^{2}\nu}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}+\tfrac{2\log\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\\
\leq\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{h_{\nu-1}}+2\log\nu\cdot\log\bigl(1-\tfrac{\nu-1}{\nu h_{\nu-1}}\bigr)\leq\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{\log\nu}-2\tfrac{(\nu-1)\log\nu}{h_{\nu-1}\nu}\leq\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\log\nu}-\log 2,\\

since
\tfrac{\log^{2}x}{1-x}
is increasing,
\log x
is concave, and
\log(1+z)\leq z
, (
z>-1
). So, we replace (
2.47
) with

(2.50)

\tfrac{e^{u}}{g_{\nu}(u)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}\leq 1-u\Bigl(\alpha\bigl(\log 2-\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{\log\nu}\bigr)-1\Bigr)+u^{2}(1+4\alpha^{2}\zeta(3)\log\nu).

Given
\alpha>0
, the coefficient by
u
can be made arbitrarily close to
\alpha\log 2-1
for
\nu
sufficiently large, thus positive if

\alpha>\tfrac{1}{\log 2}
. Assuming the latter, for those large
\nu
’s, still below
n
, the RHS expression in (
2.50
)
is below
1
if
0<u\leq\tfrac{\alpha\log 2-1}{(4+\delta)\alpha^{2}\zeta(3)\log n}
,
(\delta>0)
, and
n\geq n(\delta)
. It follows that, as

n\to\infty
, we have
f_{n}(u)=O(g_{n}(u))
. Consequently, given
\alpha^{\prime}>\alpha>\tfrac{1}{\log 2}
,

(2.51)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{L}_{n}\geq\alpha^{\prime}\log^{2}n)

\displaystyle\leq n\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(L_{n}\geq\alpha^{\prime}\log^{2}n)=O\bigl(n\tfrac{e^{\alpha u\log^{2}n}}{e^{\alpha^{\prime}u\log^{2}n}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=O\Bigl(\exp\bigl[\log n-u(\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha)\log^{2}n)\bigr]\Bigr)\to 0,

if

u=\tfrac{\alpha\log 2-1}{(4+\delta)\alpha^{2}\zeta(3)\log n},\quad\alpha^{\prime}>\alpha+\tfrac{(4+\delta)\alpha^{2}\zeta(3)}{\alpha\log 2-1}.

Set

\beta=\min_{\alpha>1/\log 2}\bigl[\alpha+\tfrac{4\alpha^{2}\zeta(3)}{\alpha\log 2-1}\bigr],

and let
\hat{\alpha}
stand for the point where the minimum is attained. Given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)=\Theta(\varepsilon)
such that

\alpha^{\prime}:=(1+\varepsilon)\beta>\hat{\alpha}+\tfrac{(4+\delta)\hat{\alpha}^{2}\zeta(3)}{\hat{\alpha}\log 2-1},

implying, by (
2.51
) with
\alpha=\hat{\alpha}
, that
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{L}_{n}\geq\alpha^{\prime}\log^{2}n)=O\bigl(\exp(-\Theta(\varepsilon)\log n)\bigr)
.
∎

2.10.
Asymptotic normality of
L_{n}
.

Here is the second part of Theorem
1.7
.

Proposition 2.11
.

In distribution, and with all of its moments,

\frac{L_{n}-(2\zeta(2))^{-1}\log^{2}n}{\sqrt{\tfrac{2\zeta(3)}{3\zeta^{3}(2)}\log^{3}n}}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{Normal}(0,1).

.

Proof.

Analogously to
the proof of Proposition
2.6
, it would seem natural to show that for
|u|=\Theta(\log^{-3/2}n)
and properly chosen
\alpha_{1}>0,\,\alpha_{2}>0
, the Laplace transform
f_{\nu}(u)=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e^{uL_{\nu}}]
satisfies

(2.52)

f_{\nu}(u)=(1+o(1))g_{\nu}(u),\quad g_{\nu}(u):=\exp\bigl(u\alpha_{1}\log^{2}\nu+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\log^{3}\nu\bigr),

uniformly for
\nu\leq n
. But we could only prove (
2.52
) for
0<u\leq v\log^{-3/2}n
and a fixed
v>0
. Fortunately, that’s all we need, thanks to a relatively recent extension of the Curtis
Lemma: it suffices to prove convergence of the sequence of Laplace transforms for the parameter
v
confined to a fixed interval
(0,\sigma]
, see
[
16
]
or
[
21
]
.

Recall

(2.53)

f_{\nu}(u)=\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{f_{k}(u)}{\nu-k},\quad\nu\geq 2.

Pick
\delta\in(0,3/4)
and set
\nu_{n}=\lceil\exp(\log^{\delta}n)\rceil
.
For a constant
\alpha
, introduce
g^{*}_{\nu}(u):=\exp(u\alpha\log^{2}\nu)
. For
u>0
, we have

(2.54)

\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}g^{*}_{\nu}(u)}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g^{*}_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl(u\alpha\log(k\nu)\log(k/\nu)\bigr)}{\nu-k}\\
=\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{1}{\nu-k}\Bigl[1+u\alpha\log(k\nu)\log(k/\nu)+O\bigl(u^{2}\alpha^{2}\log^{2}\nu\log^{2}(k/\nu)\bigr)\Bigr]\\
=e^{u}\Bigl[1+\tfrac{u\alpha\Theta(\log\nu)}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}+O\bigl(u^{2}\alpha^{2}\log^{2}\nu\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\log^{2}(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\bigr)\Bigr]\\
=e^{u}\bigl[1-u\Theta(\alpha)+O(u^{2}\alpha^{2}\log^{2}\nu)\bigr]=1+u(1-\Theta(\alpha))+O(u^{2}(1+\alpha^{2}\log\nu))\\
\left\{\begin{aligned} &>1,&&\text{if }\nu\leq\nu_{n},\,\alpha>0\text{ and small},\\
&<1,&&\text{if }\nu\leq\nu_{n},\,\alpha>0\text{ and large}.\end{aligned}\right.

(For the second line we used
e^{z}=1+z+O(z^{2}/2)
, uniformly for
z<0
. For the bottom line we used
\delta<\tfrac{3}{4}
.) Combining this with (
2.53
), we conclude that

f_{\nu}(u)=\exp\bigl(O(u\log^{2}\nu)\bigr)
, uniformly for
\nu\leq\nu_{n}
.
So, for bounded
\alpha_{1},\,\alpha_{2}
,

(2.55)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{\nu\leq\nu_{n}}\big|\tfrac{f_{\nu}(u)}{g_{\nu}(u)}-1\bigr|=0.

Thus, we need to prove existence of
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
such that the analogous relation holds uniformly for all
\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]
.
Predictably, we select
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
, requiring that
g_{\nu}(u)
is the asymptotically best fit for the recurrence
(
2.53
) for all
\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]
. To begin,

(2.56)

\displaystyle g_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=g_{\nu}(u)\exp\bigl[u\alpha_{1}G_{1}(k/\nu,\nu)+u^{2}\alpha_{2}G_{2}(k/\nu,\nu)\bigr],

\displaystyle G_{1}(k/\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle:=\log\bigl(\tfrac{k}{\nu}\bigr)\log(k\nu)\leq 0,

\displaystyle G_{2}(k/\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle:=\log\bigl(\tfrac{k}{\nu}\bigr)\bigl[3\log k\log\nu+\log^{2}\bigl(\tfrac{k}{\nu}\bigr)\bigr]\leq 0.

And, since
G_{j}(k/\nu,\nu)
are
non-positive
, the Taylor
expansion of the exponential function holds for
u>0
, even though
|uG_{1}(1/n,n)|=O(\sqrt{\log n})
. (Notice that

u^{2}|G_{2}(1/\nu,\nu)|=O(1)
.)
So, proceeding analogously to (
2.37
),

(2.57)

\tfrac{e^{u}}{g_{\nu}(u)h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}=\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[u\alpha_{1}G_{1}(k/\nu,\nu)+u^{2}\alpha_{2}G_{2}(k/\nu,\nu)\bigr]}{\nu-k}\\
=e^{u}\cdot\Bigl(1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[u\alpha_{1}G_{1}(k/\nu,\nu)+u^{2}\alpha_{2}G_{2}(k/\nu,\nu)\bigr]-1}{\nu-k}\Bigr)\\
=e^{u}\cdot\biggl(1+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[u\alpha_{1}G_{1}(x,\nu)+u^{2}\alpha_{2}G_{2}(x,\nu)\bigr]-1}{1-x}\,dx+O\bigl(\tfrac{\exp(-u\log^{2}\nu\bigr)}{\nu\log\nu}\biggr),

where
uniformly
for
x\in(0,1]
:

\tfrac{\exp\bigl[u\alpha_{1}G_{1}(x,\nu)+u^{2}\alpha_{2}G_{2}(x,\nu)\bigr]-1}{1-x}=\tfrac{u\alpha_{1}G_{1}(x,\nu)+u^{2}\alpha_{2}G_{2}(x,\nu)}{1-x}\\
+\tfrac{\bigl(u\alpha_{1}G_{1}(x,\nu)+u^{2}\alpha_{2}G_{2}(x,\nu)\bigr)^{2}}{2(1-x)}+O\bigl(\tfrac{u^{3}\log^{3}(1/x)\log^{3}\nu}{1-x}\bigr).

Using (
2.56
), and (
2.21
), we have then

\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{\exp\bigl[u\alpha_{1}G_{1}(x,\nu)+u^{2}\alpha_{2}G_{2}(x,\nu)\bigr]-1}{1-x}\,dx\\
=\alpha_{1}u\bigl(-2\zeta(2)\log\nu+2\zeta(3)\bigr)+u^{2}\biggl(\tfrac{\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2}\bigl(8\zeta(3)\log^{2}\nu-24\zeta(4)\log\nu\bigr)\\
+\alpha_{2}\bigl(-3\zeta(2)\log^{2}\nu+6\zeta(3)\log\nu-6\zeta(4)\bigr)\biggr)+O\bigl(u^{3}\log^{3}\nu\bigr).

Upon expansion
e^{u}=1+u+u^{2}/2+O(u^{3})
, the bottom RHS in (
2.57
) then becomes

(2.58)

1+u\Bigl(1+\alpha_{1}\tfrac{-2\zeta(2)\log\nu+2\zeta(3)}{h_{\nu-1}}\Bigr)+u^{2}\biggl(\tfrac{\tfrac{\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2}\bigl(8\zeta(3)\log^{2}\nu-24\zeta(4)\log\nu\bigr)}{h_{\nu-1}}\\
+\tfrac{a_{2}\bigl(-3\zeta(2)\log^{2}\nu+6\zeta(3)\log\nu-6\zeta(4)\bigr)}{h_{\nu-1}}+\alpha_{1}\tfrac{-2\zeta(2)\log\nu+2\zeta(3)}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggr)+O\bigl(u^{3}\log^{2}\nu\bigr),\\
=1+u\Bigl(1+\alpha_{1}\tfrac{-2\zeta(2)\log\nu+2\zeta(3)}{h_{\nu-1}}\Bigr)+O\bigl(u^{3}\log^{2}\nu\bigr),

if, leaving
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{1}(\nu)>0
to be determined shortly, we select
\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{2}(\nu)
to make the coefficient by
u^{2}

equal to zero. Looking closer at the coefficient by
u^{2}
, we see that this

\alpha_{2}=\tfrac{4\alpha_{1}^{2}\zeta(3)}{3\zeta(2)}+O\bigl(\log^{-1}\nu\bigr).

The rest is short. Pick
\alpha_{1}=(2\zeta(2))^{-1}(1+u^{b})
,
b<2\delta/3
. Since
u=\Theta(\log^{-3/2}n)
, the bottom expression in
(
2.58
) becomes

1+u\bigl(-u^{b}+O(\log^{-1}\nu)\bigr)+O(u^{3}\log^{2}\nu)\\
=1-u^{b+1}\bigl(1+O(u^{-b}\log^{-1}\nu)+O(u^{2-b}\log^{2}n)\bigr)\\
=1-u^{b+1}\bigl[1+O((\log n)^{-\delta+3b/2})+O((\log n)^{-1+3b/2})\bigr]<1,

since
b<2\delta/3
. So, it follows from (
2.57
) that

\tfrac{e^{u}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{g_{k}(u)}{\nu-k}<g_{\nu}(u),\quad\nu\in[\nu_{n},n].

Combining this recursive inequality with (
2.55
), we conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]}\tfrac{f_{\nu}(u)}{g_{\nu}(u)}\leq 1.

Now,

g_{\nu}(u)=\exp\bigl(u\alpha_{1}\log^{2}\nu+u^{2}\alpha_{2}\log^{3}\nu\bigr)\\
=\exp\Bigl[u\bigl((2\zeta(2))^{-1}(1+u^{b})\bigr)\log^{2}\nu+u^{2}\bigl(\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{3\zeta^{3}(2)}+o(1))\log^{3}\nu\Bigr]\\
=\exp\Bigl[u(2\zeta(2))^{-1}\log^{2}\nu+u^{2}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{3\zeta^{3}(2)}\log^{3}\nu+o(1)+O\bigl(u^{b+1}\log^{2}\nu\bigr)\Bigr]\\
=(1+o(1))\exp\Bigl[u(2\zeta(2))^{-1}\log^{2}\nu+u^{2}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{3\zeta^{3}(2)}\log^{3}\nu\Bigr],

if we select
b>1/3
. Since
b<2\delta/3
, a desired
b
exists provided that
\delta>1/2
, the constraint compatible with the initial restriction
\delta<3/4
. We conclude that for
\delta\in(1/2,3/4)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]}f_{\nu}(u)\exp\Bigl[-u(2\zeta(2))^{-1}\log^{2}\nu-u^{2}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{3\zeta^{3}(2)}\log^{3}\nu\Bigr]\leq 1.

Likewise, picking
\alpha_{1}=(2\zeta(2))^{-1}(1-u^{b})
,
b<2\delta/3
, we obtain

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\min_{\nu\in[\nu_{n},n]}f_{\nu}(u)\exp\Bigl[-u(2\zeta(2))^{-1}\log^{2}\nu-u^{2}\tfrac{\zeta(3)}{3\zeta^{3}(2)}\log^{3}\nu\Bigr]\geq 1.

This verifies (
2.52
), as required.
∎

2.11.
How soon do the species part their ways?

Recall from section
1.2
the notion of
pruned spanning tree
on
t
random leaves within the tree model on
n
leaves.
Write
S_{n,t}
for the edge height of the first branchpoint in the pruned tree.
In other words, the number of edges from the root to the vertex after which the
t
sampled leaves are first split into some
(k,t-k)
leaf subsets.
Conditioned on the size
k
of the left subtree at the root of the tree with
n
leaves, the probability that the
t
sampled leaves are all in this left subtree is
\tfrac{(k)_{t}}{(n)_{t}}
. Therefore, since
q(n,k)=\tfrac{n}{2h_{n-1}k(n-k)}
, we obtain the recursion

(2.59)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S_{n,t}]=1+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{(n/k)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S_{k,t}]}{n-k}\tfrac{(k)_{t}}{(n)_{t}},\quad n\geq t\geq 2,

(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S_{k,1}]=0)
, or, introducing
\Phi_{n,t}=(n-1)_{t-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S_{n,t}]
,

(2.60)

\Phi_{n,t}=(n-1)_{t-1}+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{\Phi_{k,t}}{n-k}.

Proposition 2.12
.

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S_{n,t}]=\tfrac{\log n}{h_{t-1}}+O(1)\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Given
\alpha>0
, define

U_{\nu,t}=\Phi_{\nu,t}-\alpha\nu^{t-1}\log\nu.

Then, by (
2.60
), we have

(2.61)

U_{\nu,t}\!=\!(\nu-1)_{t-1}+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{U_{k,t}}{\nu-k}+\alpha\biggl(\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k^{t-1}\log k}{\nu-k}-\nu^{t-1}\log\nu\biggr),\\

and the coefficient by
\alpha
equals

\tfrac{\nu^{t-1}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(k/\nu)^{t-1}[\log\nu+\log(k/\nu)]}{\nu-k}-\nu^{t-1}\log\nu\\
=\tfrac{\nu^{t-1}}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(\log\nu\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(k/\nu)^{t-1}-1}{\nu-k}+\log\nu\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{1}{\nu-k}+\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(k/\nu)^{t-1}\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}\biggr)-\nu^{t-1}\log\nu\\
=\tfrac{\nu^{t-1}}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(\log\nu\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{x^{t-1}-1}{1-x}\,dx+h_{\nu-1}\log\nu+O(1)\biggr)-\nu^{t-1}\log\nu\\
=-\tfrac{\nu^{t-1}\log\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}h_{t-1}+O\bigl(\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu\bigr).

So, the equation (
2.61
) becomes

(2.62)

U_{\nu,t}\!=\!(\nu-1)_{t-1}+\alpha\Bigl(\!-\tfrac{\nu^{t-1}\log\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}h_{t-1}+O\bigl(\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu\bigr)\Bigr)+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{U_{k,t}}{\nu-k}\\
=O\bigl(\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{U_{k,t}}{\nu-k}

if we choose
\alpha=\tfrac{1}{h_{t-1}}
. Consequently, for some constant
\beta
,

|U_{\nu,t}|\leq\beta\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{|U_{k,t}|}{\nu-k}.

For a constant
B
, to be chosen shortly, we have

\beta\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu+\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{Bk^{t-1}}{\nu-k}=\beta\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu+\tfrac{B\nu^{t-1}}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(k/\nu)^{t-1}}{\nu-k}\\
=\beta\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu+\tfrac{B\nu^{t-1}}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(h_{\nu-1}+\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{x^{t-1}-1}{1-x}\,dx+O(\nu^{-1})\biggr)\\
=\beta\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu+\tfrac{B\nu^{t-1}}{h_{\nu-1}}\bigl(h_{\nu-1}-h_{t-1}+O(\nu^{-1})\bigr)<B\nu^{t-1},\\

provided that

\beta\log^{-1}\nu-B\Bigl(\tfrac{h_{t-1}}{h_{\nu-1}}+O(\nu^{-1})\Bigr)<0.

And this inequality holds for all
\nu\geq 2
, if we choose
B
sufficiently large. It follows, by induction on
\nu
, that
|U_{\nu,t}|\leq B\nu^{t-1}
. Consequently

\Phi_{\nu,t}=\alpha\nu^{t-1}\log\nu+O(\nu^{t-1}),

so that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S_{\nu,t}]=\tfrac{\Phi_{\nu,t}}{(\nu-1)_{t-1}}=\alpha\log\nu+O(1),\quad\alpha=\tfrac{1}{h_{t-1}}.

∎

Within the same notion of
pruned spanning tree
on
t
random leaves within the tree model on
n
leaves,
a more complicated statistic is the edge-length of the pruned tree, which we denote as
S^{*}_{n,t}
.
To derive the counterpart of (
2.59
), notice that the total number of ways to partition the set

[n]\setminus[t]
into two trees, the left one of cardinality
k
, with
t_{1}\leq t
vertices from
[t]
and the right one of cardinality
n-k
, with
t_{2}=t-t_{1}
remaining vertices from
[t]
, equals
\binom{n-t}{k-t_{1}}
.
Defining
S^{*}_{n,0}=0
,
S^{*}_{n,1}=0
,
\forall\,n\geq 0
, we have the recursion: for
n\geq t\geq 2
,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S^{*}_{n,t}]=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{n}{2h_{n-1}k(n-k)}\cdot\binom{n}{k}^{-1}\\
\times\sum_{t_{1}\leq t}\binom{n-t}{k-t_{1}}\Bigl(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S^{*}_{k,t_{1}}]+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S^{*}_{n-k,t_{2}}]\Bigr)\\
=1+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{n}{2h_{n-1}k(n-k)}\sum_{t_{1}\leq t}\tfrac{(k)_{t_{1}}(n-k)_{t_{2}}}{(n)_{t}}\Bigl(\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S^{*}_{k,t_{1}}]+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S^{*}_{n-k,t_{2}}]\Bigr)\\
=1+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\sum_{t_{1}=2}^{t}\tfrac{(k-1)_{t_{1}-1}(n-k)_{t_{2}}}{(n-1)_{t-1}(n-k)}\,\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S^{*}_{k,t_{1}}].

Therefore, with
\Psi_{n,t}:=(n-1)_{t-1}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[S^{*}_{n,t}]
, so that
\Psi_{n,0}=\Psi_{n,1}=0
,
\Psi_{n,t}=0
for

n<t
, we obtain

(2.63)

\Psi_{n,t}=(n-1)_{t-1}+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{t_{1}=2}^{t}\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}\tfrac{(n-k)_{t_{2}}}{n-k}\Psi_{k,t_{1}},\quad n\geq t\geq 2.

This equation is similar to (
2.60
). Because of the new factor
(n-k)_{t_{2}}
, we will use

(2.64)

(a)_{b}=\sum_{j=1}^{b}s(b,j)a^{j},

where
s(b,j)
is the signed Stirling number of the first kind, so that
|s(b,j)|
is the total number of permutations of
[b]
with
j
cycles.

We now repeat the statement of Theorem
1.8
.

Proposition 2.13
.

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[\mathcal{S}_{n,t}^{*}]=\alpha(t)\log n+O(1),\quad\alpha(t)=\biggl(h_{t-1}-\sum_{t_{1}+t_{2}=t}\tfrac{(t_{1}-1)!(t_{2}-1)!}{(t-1)!}\biggr)^{-1}.

Proof.

The argument is guided by the proof of Proposition
2.12
. Given
\alpha>0
, define

V_{\nu,t}=\Psi_{\nu,t}-\alpha\nu^{t-1}\log\nu,\quad\nu\geq t\geq 2.

By (
2.63
), we have

(2.65)

V_{\nu,t}\!=\!(\nu-1)_{t-1}+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{t_{1}=2}^{t}\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(\nu-k)_{t_{2}}}{\nu-k}V_{k,t_{1}}\\
+\alpha\biggl(\tfrac{1}{h_{\nu-1}}\sum_{t_{1}=2}^{t}\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(\nu-k)_{t_{2}}}{\nu-k}\,k^{t_{1}-1}\log k-\nu^{t-1}\log\nu\biggr).

Consider the factor by
\alpha
. By (
2.64
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(\nu-k)_{t_{2}}}{\nu-k}k^{t_{1}-1}\log k=\sum_{j=0}^{t_{2}}s(t_{2},j)\Sigma(\nu,t_{1},j),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Sigma(\nu,t_{1},j):=\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}(\nu-k)^{j-1}k^{t_{1}-1}\log k.

Recalling that
t_{1}>1
, we write

\Sigma(\nu,t_{1},0)=\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k^{t_{1}-1}\log k}{\nu-k}=\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k^{t_{1}-1}\bigl(\log\nu+\log(k/\nu)\bigr)}{\nu-k}\\
=(\log\nu)\biggl(\nu^{t_{1}-1}h_{\nu-1}+\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k^{t_{1}-1}-\nu^{t_{1}-1}}{\nu-k}\biggr)+\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k^{t_{1}-1}\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k},

and

\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k^{t_{1}-1}-\nu^{t_{1}-1}}{\nu-k}=\nu^{t_{1}-1}\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{x^{t_{1}-1}-1}{1-x}\,dx+O(\nu^{-1})\biggr)\\
=\nu^{t_{1}-1}\biggl(-\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{s=0}^{t_{1}-2}x^{s}\,dx+O(\nu^{-1})\biggr)\\
=-\nu^{t_{1}-1}h_{t_{1}-1}+O(\nu^{t_{1}-2}),

while it is easy to see that
\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{k^{t_{1}-1}\log(k/\nu)}{\nu-k}
is of order

\nu^{t_{1}-1}\int_{0}^{1}\tfrac{x^{t_{1}-1}\log x}{1-x}\,dx=O(\nu^{t_{1}-1})
.
Therefore

(2.66)

\Sigma(\nu,t_{1},0)=(h_{\nu-1}-h_{t_{1}-1})\nu^{t_{1}-1}\log\nu+O(\nu^{t_{1}-1}).

Suppose that
j>0
. Then

(2.67)

\Sigma(\nu,t_{1},j)=\nu^{t_{1}+j-1}\biggl(\nu^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\nu-1}\bigl(1-k/\nu\bigr)^{j-1}\bigl(k/\nu\bigr)^{t_{1}-1}\bigl[\log\nu+\log(k/\nu)\bigr]\biggr)\\
=\nu^{t_{1}+j-1}\biggl[(\log\nu)\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{j-1}x^{t_{1}-1}\,dx\\
\qquad+\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{j-1}x^{t_{1}-1}(\log x)\,dx+O(\nu^{-1}\log\nu)\biggr]\\
=\tfrac{(j-1)!(t_{1}-1)!}{(t_{1}+j-1)!}\cdot\nu^{t_{1}+j-1}\log\nu+O(\nu^{t_{1}+j-2}\log\nu),

and
t_{1}+j-1\leq t_{1}+t_{2}-1=t-1
. Combining (
2.66
) and (
2.67
), and using
s(b,b)=1
,
s(b,0)=0
for
b>0
, we have

\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(\nu-k)_{t_{2}}}{\nu-k}k^{t_{1}-1}\log k\\
=(h_{\nu-1}-h_{t_{1}-1})\nu^{t_{1}-1}\log\nu+\tfrac{(t_{2}-1)!(t_{1}-1)!}{(t-1)!}\nu^{t-1}\log\nu+O(\nu^{t-2}\log\nu).\\

So, the factor by
\alpha
in (

) is

\tfrac{\nu^{t-1}\log\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(h_{\nu-1}-h_{t-1}+\sum_{t_{1}=1}^{t}\tfrac{(t_{2}-1)!(t_{1}-1)!}{(t-1)!}+O(\nu^{-1})\biggr)-\nu^{t-1}\log\nu\\
=\tfrac{\nu^{t-1}\log\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(-h_{t-1}+\sum_{t_{1}=1}^{t}\tfrac{(t_{2}-1)!(t_{1}-1)!}{(t-1)!}+O(\nu^{-1})\biggr).

Consequently the equation (
2.65
) becomes

V_{\nu,t}\!=\!(\nu-1)_{t-1}+\alpha\tfrac{\nu^{t-1}\log\nu}{h_{\nu-1}}\biggl(-h_{t-1}+\sum_{t_{1}=1}^{t}\tfrac{(t_{2}-1)!(t_{1}-1)!}{(t-1)!}+O(\nu^{-1})\biggr)\\
+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{t_{1}=2}^{t}\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(\nu-k)_{t_{2}}}{\nu-k}V_{k,t_{1}}\\
=O\bigl(\nu^{t-1}\log^{-1}\nu\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{t_{1}=2}^{t}\sum_{k=2}^{\nu-1}\tfrac{(\nu-k)_{t_{2}}}{\nu-k}V_{k,t_{1}},

if we select

\alpha=\biggl(h_{t-1}-\sum_{t_{1}+t_{2}=t}\tfrac{(t_{1}-1)!(t_{2}-1)!}{(t-1)!}\biggr)^{-1}.

We omit the rest of the proof since it runs just like the final part of the proof of Proposition
2.12
.
∎

2.12.
Counting the subtrees by the number of their leaves.

Since the tree with
n
leaves has
2n-1
vertices, there are exactly
2n-1
subtrees, with the number of leaves ranging, with possible gaps, from
1
to
n
. Let
X_{n}(t)
be the number of subtrees with
t
leaves; so
X_{n}(1)=n
,
X_{n}(n)=1
, and
X_{n}(t)=0
for
t>n
. Now,
\sum_{t\geq 1}X_{n}(t)=2n-1
, so
\{u_{n}(t)\}_{t\geq 1}:=\bigl\{\tfrac{E[X_{n}(t)]}{2n-1}\bigl\}_{t\geq 1}
is the probability distribution of the number of leaves in the uniformly random subtree, i.e. the subtree rooted at the uniformly random vertex of the whole tree. Furthermore

(2.68)

E[X_{n}(t)]=\tfrac{n}{2h_{n-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{E[X_{j}(t)]+E[X_{n-j}(t)]}{j(n-j)}=\tfrac{n}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{E[X_{j}(t)]}{j(n-j)}.

So, with
\xi_{n}(t):=\tfrac{E[X_{n}(t)]}{n}
, and
h_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tfrac{1}{j}
, we have

(2.69)

\xi_{n}(t)=\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{j=t}^{n-1}\tfrac{\xi_{j}(t)}{n-j},\quad n\geq t+1,\,\,\bigl(\xi_{t}(t)=\tfrac{1}{t}\bigr).

and clearly
u_{n}(t)=\tfrac{\xi_{n}(t)}{2-n^{-1}}
.

Theorem 2.14
.

(i)

\xi_{n}(t)\in\bigl[\tfrac{1}{t^{2}},\tfrac{1}{th_{t}}\bigr],\,\,\tfrac{1}{t}\leq\sum_{\tau\geq t}\xi_{n}(\tau)\leq\tfrac{2}{t}
, the last bound implying that the sequence of
distributions
\{u_{n}(t)\}_{t\geq 1}
is tight.

(ii)
For
q\in(0,1)
,
F_{n}(q):=\sum_{t\geq 1}q^{t}\xi_{n}(t)
decreases with
n
. Consequently, the sequence of
distributions
\{u_{n}(t)\}_{t\geq 1}
converges to a proper distribution
\{u(t)\}_{t\geq 1}
.

(iii)
However,
the expected size of the uniformly random subtree is asymptotic to
\tfrac{3}{2\pi^{2}}\log^{2}n
.

We conjecture that (ii) can be improved to the stronger assertion that
\xi_{n}(t)
is decreasing with
n
, for each
t
.

[
1
]

for numerically verifying this for
n
and
t
below
1000
.

Proof.

(i)
Let us show that
\xi_{n}(t)\geq\tfrac{1}{t^{2}}
for
n\geq t>1
. By (
2.68
), we have
\xi_{t}(t)=\tfrac{1}{t}
and
\xi_{t+1}(t)=\tfrac{1}{th_{t}}
, both above
\tfrac{1}{t^{2}}
. Suppose that
n\geq t+1
is such that
\xi_{j}(t)\geq\tfrac{1}{t^{2}}
for all
j\in[t,n]
. This is true for
n=t+1
. For
n>t+1
,

\xi_{n}(t)\geq\tfrac{\xi_{t}(t)}{h_{n-1}(n-t)}+\tfrac{1}{t^{2}h_{n-1}}\sum_{j=t+1}^{n-1}\tfrac{a}{n-j}=\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}(n-t)t}+\tfrac{h_{n-1-t}}{t^{2}h_{n-1}}\\
=\tfrac{1}{t^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}(n-t)t}+\tfrac{h_{n-1-t}-h_{n-1}}{t^{2}h_{n-1}}\\
\geq\tfrac{1}{t^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}(n-t)t}-\tfrac{1}{t^{2}h_{n-1}}\cdot\tfrac{t}{n-t}=\tfrac{1}{t^{2}},

which completes the induction step. The proof of
\xi_{n}(t)\leq\tfrac{1}{th_{t}}
is similarly reduced to showing that

\tfrac{(n-1)h_{t}}{(n-t)th_{n-1}}\leq 1
for
n>t+1
.
This is so, as the fraction is at most
\tfrac{h_{t}}{h_{t+1}}\cdot\tfrac{t+1}{2t}
.

Let us prove that
\tfrac{1}{t}\leq\sum_{\tau\geq t}\xi_{n}(\tau)\leq\tfrac{2}{t}
. Introduce

Y_{n}(t)=\sum_{\tau\geq t}X_{n}(\tau)
, the total number of subtrees with at least
t
leaves, and
\eta_{n}(t):=\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Y_{n}(t)]}{n}=\sum_{\tau\geq t}\xi_{n}(\tau)
; so
\eta_{n}(1)=\tfrac{2n-1}{n}
, and
\eta_{n}(n)=\tfrac{1}{n}
. Analogously to (
2.68
), we have

\eta_{n}(t)=\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{j=t}^{n-1}\tfrac{\eta_{j}(t)}{n-j},\quad n\geq t+1.

We need to show that
\eta_{n}(t)\leq\tfrac{2}{t}
for all
n\geq t
. It suffices to consider
n>t>1
. Suppose that for some
n\geq t
and all

j\in[t,n]
we have
\eta_{j}(t)\leq\tfrac{2}{t}
. This is definitely true for
n=t
. Then

\eta_{n+1}(t)=\tfrac{1}{h_{n}}\sum_{j=t}^{n}\tfrac{\eta_{j}(t)}{n+1-j}\leq\tfrac{2}{th_{n}}\sum_{j=t}^{n}\tfrac{1}{n+1-j}=\tfrac{2h_{n+1-t}}{th_{n}}\leq\tfrac{2}{t},\\

which competes the inductive proof of
\eta_{n}(t)\leq\tfrac{2}{t}
.

(ii)
For
n\geq 2
, we have

F_{n}(q)=\sum_{t\geq 1}q^{t}\xi_{n}(t)=\sum_{t\geq 1}\tfrac{q^{t}}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{j=t}^{n-1}\tfrac{\xi_{j}(t)}{n-j}\\
=\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{1}{n-j}\sum_{t=1}^{j}q^{t}\xi_{j}(t)=\tfrac{1}{h_{n-1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{F_{j}(q)}{n-j}.\\

Therefore

F_{n+1}(q)=\tfrac{1}{h_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tfrac{F_{j}(q)}{n+1-j}=\tfrac{1}{h_{n}}\biggl(\tfrac{F_{1}(q)}{n}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tfrac{F_{j}(q)}{n+1-j}\biggr)\\
\leq\tfrac{1}{h_{n}}\biggl(\tfrac{F_{1}(q)}{n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\tfrac{F_{j}(q)}{n-j}\biggr)\leq\tfrac{1}{h_{n}}\bigl(\tfrac{q}{n}+h_{n-1}F_{n}(q)\bigr)\leq F_{n}(q),

since
F_{n}(q)\geq q\xi_{n}(t)=q
, and
h_{n}-h_{n-1}=\tfrac{1}{n}
.
Therefore, for each
q\in(0,1)
there exists
F(q)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{t\geq 1}q^{t}\xi_{n}(t)
, implying existence of

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{t\geq 1}q^{t}u_{n}(t)=2F(q)
.
For any weakly convergent subsequence of the distributions
\{u_{n_{i}}(t)\}_{t\geq 1}
, for each
x
in the unit disc there exists

\lim_{n_{i}\to\infty}\sum_{t\geq 1}x^{t}u_{n_{i}}(t)
, dependent on the subsequence, which is analytic within the disc. All these limits coincide for
x\in(0,1)
, whence they coincide for all
x
within the disc, whence on the whole disc. Since the characteristic function determines the distribution uniquely, we see that the whole sequence of the distributions converges
to a proper distribution.

(iii)

Z_{n}:=\sum_{t\geq 1}tX_{n}(t)
is the total number of the leaves, each leaf counted as many times as the
number of the subtrees rooted at the vertices along the path from the root to the leaf, which is distributed as
1
plus

L_{n}
, the edge-length of the path to the random leaf. Therefore
\tfrac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{n}]}{2n-1}=\tfrac{n}{2n-1}\bigl(1+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[L_{n}]\bigr)
, and it remains to use Proposition
2.7
.
∎

3.
Other methods

Our results here demonstrate that the analysis of recursions method is very effective at deriving sharp asymptotics for the questions
addressed here.
However, there are many other aspects of the model that could be studied,
and a wide variety of familiar general modern probabilistic techniques that could be applied.
We indicate such possibilities briefly below – see the preprint
[
4
]
for a more comprehensive account.

It is intuitively clear that
for our tree model (call it
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, say) there should be two
n\to\infty
limit structures:

(a) A scaling limit process, which is a fragmentation of the unit interval via some sigma-finite splitting measure.

(b) A fringe process, which is the local weak limit relative to a random leaf, describable as some
marked branching process.
This starts with an explicit description of the limit distribution
\{u(t)\}_{t\geq 1}
in Theorem
2.14
.

Less intuitive is a piece of structure theory.
In our discrete-time model there is no simple connection between
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and
\mathcal{T}_{n+1}
.
In the continuous-time model with our chosen rates
h_{n-1}

(and this is the reason for that particular choice),
[
4
]
shows there is a non-obvious consistency property under a “delete a random leaf and prune” operation.
This enables an inductive construction of a process
(\mathcal{T}_{n},n=2,3,4,\ldots)
, which in turn suggests the possibility of a.s. limit theorems.

Readers may notice that the issues above are somewhat analogous to those arising in the theory
surrounding the Brownian continuum random tree (CRT) as a limit of certain other random tree models

[
2
,
9
]
.
However, in contrast to the CRT setting, the two limit processes above would not capture the asymptotics of the quantities studied in this article.

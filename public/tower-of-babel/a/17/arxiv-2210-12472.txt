Optimal Antipodal Configuration of 2 ​ d 2d Points on a Sphere in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} for Covering


Abstract
We show that among antipodal 2 ​ d 2d -point configurations on the sphere S d − 1 S^{d-1} in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , the set of vertices of a regular cross-polytope inscribed in S d − 1 S^{d-1} uniquely solves the best-covering problem (this is new for d ≥ 5 d\geq 5 ) and the maximal polarization problem for potentials given by a function of the distance squared with a positive and convex second derivative ( d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 ).

2
Main result

Recall that a point configuration on
S^{d-1}
is called

if together with a point
{\bf x}
it contains
-{\bf x}
. We say that a given point set in

It will be convenient for us to recast the optimal-covering problem in terms of dot products.

Remark 2.1
.

A point configuration
\omega_{N}\subset S^{d-1}
is a solution to Problem

if and only if
\omega_{N}
maximizes the quantity

\eta(\omega_{N},S^{d-1}):=\min\limits_{{\bf x}\in S^{d-1}}\max\limits_{i=\overline{1,N}}{\bf x}\cdot{\bf x}_{i}

over all
N
-point configurations
\omega_{N}=\{{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{N}\}
on
S^{d-1}
.

We allow configurations, where points may coincide. However, if an antipodal configuration of
2d
points on
S^{d-1}
is in general position, its points are pairwise distinct.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

and

be an antipodal configuration of
2d

\eta(\omega_{2d},S^{d-1})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}.

(2)

Equality in (

is the set of vertices of a regular cross-polytope inscribed in

.

For

is a special case of theorems proved in

and in

A consequence of Theorem

for polarization

The best-covering problem is a limiting case of the polarization problem stated next. Let
f:[0,4]\to(-\infty,\infty]

and let

\mathcal{P}_{f}(S^{d-1},N):=\sup\limits_{\omega_{N}\subset S^{d-1}}P_{f}(\omega_{N},S^{d-1}).

(3)

-point polarization problem on the sphere is stated in the following way.

Problem 3.1

(

)
and

Polarization problem can also be recast in terms of dot products.
Let
g(t):=f(2-2t)
,
t\in[-1,1]
. Then
f
is a potential function if and only if
g:[-1,1]\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a function finite and continuous on
[-1,1)
such that
g(1)=\lim\limits_{t\to 1^{-}}g(t)
. For every configuration
\omega_{N}=\{{\bf x}_{1},\ldots,{\bf x}_{N}\}\subset S^{d-1}
, we have

P^{g}(\omega_{N},S^{d-1}):=\min\limits_{{\bf x}\in S^{d-1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}g({\bf x}\cdot{\bf x}_{i})=\min\limits_{{\bf x}\in S^{d-1}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}f\left(\left|{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{i}\right|^{2}\right)=P_{f}(\omega_{N},S^{d-1}).

Thus, a point configuration
\omega_{N}
maximizes the quantity
P_{f}(\omega_{N},S^{d-1})
if and only if it maximizes the quantity
P^{g}(\omega_{N},S^{d-1})
.

Let

Proof.

Pick an arbitrary subset of

Let
\Pi:=\{-1,1\}^{d}

sets

\omega_{2d}
and, hence, is a set of vertices of a
(d-1)
-simplex, which we will denote by

. For every
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\Pi

. Then the set

is contained in the closed subset of

and

. Then the simplex
F_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}
is contained in the boundary
\partial D(\omega_{2d})
of

for every

.

Since

is in general position, the origin is in the interior of

as a point on the line segment joining two points in relative interiors of opposite facets.

Let now
{\bf x}
be any point in
\partial D(\omega_{2d})
. Then
{\bf x}\neq{\bf 0}
and there is a unique set of numbers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}
such that

, we have

, where

. Let
\beta:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta_{i}

. If it were that

, then, since

, the origin and

would lie in different half-spaces relative to

, which is a supporting hyperplane for

. Since the origin is in

, we have

would be in the relative interior of the line segment joining

. Assume that the intersection of two simplices

D(\overline{\omega}_{2d})
and there are exactly
2^{d}
facets, we have

2^{-d}{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(S^{d-1})\leq{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(V))<{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(W))=2^{-d}{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(S^{d-1}).

This contradiction proves (
2
).

To complete the proof of Theorem
2.2
, assume that equality holds in
(
2
)
. Then the origin is in the interior of
D(\omega_{2d})
. Assume to the contrary that there is a facet
Q
of
D(\omega_{2d})
such that the hyperplane containing it is at a distance strictly greater than
1/\sqrt{d}
from the origin. Then by Lemma
4.1
, the radial projection of
Q
onto
S^{d-1}
has
(d-1)
-dimensional volume strictly less than
\nu
. Equality in (
2
) implies that every other facet
Y
of
D(\omega_{2d})
is contained in a hyperplane whose distance to the origin is at least
1/\sqrt{d}
. By Lemma
4.1
,
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y))\leq\nu
and, by Lemma
4.2
,
D(\omega_{2d})
has
2^{d}
facets. Then the radial projection onto
S^{d-1}
of the whole boundary of
D(\omega_{2d})
has
(d-1)
-dimensional volume strictly less than
2^{d}\nu={\rm Vol}_{d-1}(S^{d-1})
. This contradiction shows that the hyperplane containing each facet of
D(\omega_{2d})
is at a distance exactly
1/\sqrt{d}
from the origin. Each facet of
D(\omega_{2d})
must be a regular simplex. If some facet
U
were not, by Lemma
4.1
, we would have
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(U))<\nu
while for any other facet
J
, we would have
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(J))\leq\nu
leading to a similar contradiction. Let
{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{d}
be the vertices of one of the facets of
D(\omega_{2d})
. Then
\omega_{2d}=\{\pm{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,\pm{\bf a}_{d}\}
. It is not difficult to verify that
{\bf a}_{i}\cdot{\bf a}_{j}=0
,
i\neq j
. Then
\{{\bf a}_{1},\ldots,{\bf a}_{d}\}
is an orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\omega_{2d}
is the set of vertices of a regular cross-polytope inscribed in
S^{d-1}
.
∎

5
Minimum of the potential of a regular cross-polytope

In order to show the optimality of
\overline{\omega}_{2d}
for Problem
3.1
, one will need to know the quantity
P_{f}(\overline{\omega}_{2d},S^{d-1})
by locating the absolute minima of the potential of
\overline{\omega}_{2d}
on the sphere
S^{d-1}
.
This was done earlier for the vertices of a regular
N
-gon inscribed in
S^{1}
and for the vertices of a regular simplex, cross-polytope, and cube inscribed in
S^{d-1}
for Riesz potential functions (
s\neq 0
) and their horizontal translations, see the works by Stolarsky, Nikolov, and Rafailov
[
26
,
27
,
22
,
23
]
. For general potentials, this has been recently done in
[
8
]
for vertices of a regular simplex. Below, we extend one of the results of
[
27
,
23
]
by finding the absolute minima of the potential of
\overline{\omega}_{2d}
for potential functions
g
that have a convex second derivative. Our proof is different from the one in
[
27
,
23
]
and uses polynomial interpolation and convexity of
g^{\prime\prime}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume in this section that
\overline{\omega}_{2d}=\widetilde{\omega}_{2d}=\{\pm{\bf e}_{1},\ldots,\pm{\bf e}_{d}\}
, where
{\bf e}_{1},\ldots,{\bf e}_{d}
is the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
g:[-1,1]\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a function continuous on
[-1,1)
and differentiable on
(-1,1)
such that
g(1)=\lim\limits_{t\to 1^{-}}g(t)
and
g^{\prime\prime}
is convex on
(-1,1)
. Then the potential

p^{g}(\widetilde{\omega}_{2d},{\bf x}):=\sum\limits_{{\bf y}\in\widetilde{\omega}_{2d}}g({\bf x}\cdot{\bf y})

achieves its absolute minimum over
S^{d-1}
at any point of
S^{d-1}
whose every coordinate is
1/\sqrt{d}
or
-1/\sqrt{d}
(these points are vertices of the cube dual to
\widetilde{\omega}_{2d}
). Furthermore,

P^{g}(\widetilde{\omega}_{2d},S^{d-1})=d\left(g\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)+g\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\right).

We also remark that in the upcoming paper
[
10
]
, we obtain the locations of absolute minima of the potential of any configuration on
S^{d-1}
which is a tight spherical design of an even strength or a (
2m-1
)-design contained in the union of
m
parallel hyperplanes. Such is, for example,
\overline{\omega}_{2d}
for
m=2
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
h(t):=g(t)+g(-t)
. Then
h
is even and
h^{\prime\prime}
is convex on
(-1,1)
. Let
p
be the Hermite interpolating polynomial for
h
at points
t_{1}=-1/\sqrt{d}
and
t_{2}=1/\sqrt{d}
. Then the even polynomial
(p(t)+p(-t))/2
is also Hermite for
h
at
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. By uniqueness,
p
must be even. Since
p
has degree at most
3
, it has the form
p(t)=at^{2}+b
. Furthermore,
\frac{a}{d}+b=p(1/\sqrt{d})=h\left(1/\sqrt{d}\right)
.

We also have,
h(t)\geq p(t)
,
t\in[-1,1]
. Indeed, assume to the contrary that
v(t):=h(t)-p(t)
is negative for some
t=t_{0}\in(-1,1)
. Note that
v(t_{1})=v(t_{2})=v^{\prime}(t_{1})=v^{\prime}(t_{2})=0
. Then the Mean value theorem and the Rolle’s theorem imply that there are points
-1<\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}<\tau_{3}<1
such that
v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{1})<0
and
v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{2})=v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{3})=0
(if
t_{0}<t_{1}
) or
v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{1})<0
,
v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{2})>0
, and
v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{3})<0
(if
t_{1}<t_{0}<t_{2}
) or
v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{1})=v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{2})=0
and
v^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{3})<0
(if
t_{0}>t_{2}
). None of these cases is possible, since
h^{\prime\prime}
(and, hence,
v^{\prime\prime}
) is convex on
(-1,1)
. Thus,
v
is non-negative on
(-1,1)
; that is,
h(t)\geq p(t)
,
t\in(-1,1)
. We extend this inequality to the endpoints by passing to the limit.

Let
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
be an arbitrary point on
S^{d-1}
. Then

\begin{split}p^{g}(\widetilde{\omega}_{2d},{\bf x})&=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\left(g(x_{i})+g(-x_{i})\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}h(x_{i})\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}p(x_{i})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}(ax_{i}^{2}+b)\\
&=a+d\cdot b=d\cdot h\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)=d\left(g\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)+g\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\right)=p^{g}(\widetilde{\omega}_{2d},{\bf x}^{\ast}),\end{split}

where
{\bf x}^{\ast}
is any point on
S^{d-1}
whose every coordinate is
1/\sqrt{d}
or
-1/\sqrt{d}
.
∎

Proof of Corollaries
3.3
and
3.4

Proof of Corollary
3.3
.

We choose an arbitrary centered configuration
\omega_{2d}=\{{\bf y}_{1},\ldots,{\bf y}_{2d}\}\subset S^{d-1}

and let
{\bf x}^{\ast}\in S^{d-1}
be a point such that

-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\leq{\bf x}^{\ast}\cdot{\bf y}_{i}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}},\ \ \ i=1,\ldots,2d.

(5)

In fact,
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\leq\pm{\bf x}^{\ast}\cdot{\bf y}_{i}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
for all
i
. Let
-t_{1}\leq\ldots\leq-t_{2d}\leq t_{2d}\leq\ldots\leq t_{1}
denote all the dot products that
{\bf x}^{\ast}
and
-{\bf x}^{\ast}
form with points
{\bf y}_{i}
. Since
g^{\prime\prime}
is non-negative on
(-1,1)
,
g^{\prime}
is non-decreasing on
(-1,1)
, and, hence, the function
h(t)=g(t)+g(-t)
is non-decreasing on
[0,1)
. Since
t_{1},\ldots,t_{2d}\in[0,1/\sqrt{d}]
, we have

\begin{split}P^{g}&(\omega_{2d},S^{d-1})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2d}g({\bf x}^{\ast}\cdot{\bf y}_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2d}g(-{\bf x}^{\ast}\cdot{\bf y}_{i})=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2d}\left(g(t_{i})+g(-t_{i})\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2d}h(t_{i})\leq d\cdot h\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)=d\left(g\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)+g\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\right)=P^{g}(\overline{\omega}_{2d},S^{d-1}),\end{split}

(6)

where the last equality in (
6
) holds in view of Theorem
5.1
. This proves (
4
) for any centered configuration
\omega_{2d}
. In view of Corollary
3.2
, we have Corollary
3.3
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.4
.

Assume that equality holds throughout (
4
) for a given antipodal configuration
\omega_{2d}\subset S^{d-1}
. Since
\omega_{2d}
is antipodal,
{\bf x}^{\ast}
can be chosen in the beginning of the proof of Corollary
3.3
with the additional property that

\max\limits_{i=\overline{1,2d}}{\bf x}^{\ast}\cdot{\bf y}_{i}=\eta(\omega_{2d},S^{d-1})
(inequalities (
5
) will then hold in view of Theorem
2.2
). Since equality now holds throughout (
6
), we have
\sum_{i=1}^{2d}h(t_{i})=2d\cdot h(1/\sqrt{d})
. Since
0\leq t_{i}\leq 1/\sqrt{d}
,
i=1,\ldots,2d
and
h
is strictly increasing on
[0,1)
, we have
t_{i}=1/\sqrt{d}
,
i=1,\ldots,2d
.
Then
{\bf x}^{\ast}
forms only dot products
1/\sqrt{d}
and

with points of

. Thus,
\eta(\omega_{2d},S^{d-1})=\max\limits_{i=\overline{1,2d}}{\bf x}^{\ast}\cdot{\bf y}_{i}=1/\sqrt{d}
. By the uniqueness part of Theorem
2.2
, configuration
\omega_{2d}
is the set of vertices of a regular cross-polytope inscribed in

.

Appendix. Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

The assertion of Lemma
4.1
is trivial for
d=2
. Therefore, we assume that
d\geq 3
.
Let
Y
be a
(d-1)
-simplex inscribed in
H\cap S^{d-1}
whose radial projection
r(Y)
onto
S^{d-1}
has the largest
(d-1)
-dimensional volume. Let
{\bf y}_{1},\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}
be the vertices of
Y
. Let
\Omega(Y)
be the intersection of the closed unit ball
B^{d}
centered at the origin with the cone, denoted by
{\rm cone}\{{\bf y}_{1},\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}\}
, constructed as the convex hull of the rays starting at
{\bf 0}
and passing through each vertex of
Y
. Then
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y))=d\cdot{\rm Vol}_{d}(\Omega(Y))
.

Assume to the contrary that
Y
is not a regular simplex. Then there is a vertex of
Y
such that some two edges stemming out of it have non-equal lengths. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\left|{\bf y}_{3}-{\bf y}_{1}\right|\neq\left|{\bf y}_{3}-{\bf y}_{2}\right|
. Let
L
be the hyperplane that is the perpendicular bisector for the line segment with endpoints
{\bf y}_{1}
and
{\bf y}_{2}
. Observe that
{\bf 0}\in L
. Denote by
{\bf y}_{i}^{\prime}
the orthogonal projection of the point
{\bf y}_{i}
onto
L
,
i=3,\ldots,d
, and let
Y^{\prime}
be the convex hull of
\{{\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{3}^{\prime},\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}^{\prime}\}
. Since
{\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{3}^{\prime},\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}^{\prime}\in H\cap B^{d}
, we have
Y^{\prime}\subset H\cap B^{d}
and
r(Y^{\prime})\subset C:=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in S^{d-1}:x_{d}\geq a\}
. Also,
r(Y^{\prime})={\rm cone}\{{\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{3}^{\prime},\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}^{\prime}\}\cap S^{d-1}
.

Recall that a Steiner symmetrization of a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
relative to a hyperplane
L
is the set

{\rm St}(A):=\bigcup\limits_{\ell\in\mathcal{Q}}\left(\frac{1}{2}(A\cap\ell)+\frac{1}{2}(\widetilde{A}\cap\ell)\right),

where
\mathcal{Q}
is the set of all lines passing through points of
A
that are perpendicular to hyperplane
L
and
\widetilde{A}
is the reflection of
A
with respect to
L
. The Steiner symmetrization of
{\rm cone}\{{\bf y}_{1},\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}\}
is contained in
{\rm cone}\{{\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{3}^{\prime},\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}^{\prime}\}
. Since
L
passes through
{\bf 0}
, whenever
A,D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
are such that
{\rm St}(A)\subset D
, we have
{\rm St}(A\cap B^{d})\subset D\cap B^{d}
. Therefore,
{\rm St}(\Omega(Y))\subset\Omega(Y^{\prime})
, where
\Omega(Y^{\prime}):={\rm cone}\{{\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{3}^{\prime},\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}^{\prime}\}\cap B^{d}
. Since Steiner symmetrization preserves the volume, we have

\begin{split}{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y))&={d}{{\rm Vol}_{d}(\Omega(Y))}={d}{{\rm Vol}_{d}[{\rm St}(\Omega(Y))]}\\
&\leq{d}{{\rm Vol}_{d}(\Omega(Y^{\prime}))}={\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y^{\prime})).\end{split}

Since the
(d-1)
-dimensional volume of
r(Y)
is positive, so is the one of
r(Y^{\prime})
and, hence of
Y^{\prime}
. Then
Y^{\prime}
is a
(d-1)
-simplex.
Since
{\bf y}_{3}\notin L
, its projection
{\bf y}^{\prime}_{3}
is an interior point of
H\cap B^{d}
. Then there is a point
{\bf y}_{3}^{\prime\prime}\in H\cap B^{d}
into which we can move
{\bf y}^{\prime}_{3}
so that the new simplex
Y^{\prime\prime}
with vertices
{\bf y}_{1},{\bf y}_{2},{\bf y}_{3}^{\prime\prime},{\bf y}_{4}^{\prime}\ldots,{\bf y}_{d}^{\prime}
contains
Y^{\prime}
together with some open set that lies outside of
Y^{\prime}
. Then we will have
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y^{\prime}))<{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y^{\prime\prime}))
with
Y^{\prime\prime}\subset H\cap B^{d}
. The simplex
Y^{\prime\prime}
is contained in some
(d-1)
-simplex
Y^{\prime\prime\prime}
inscribed in
H\cap S^{d-1}
. Then
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y))<{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y^{\prime\prime\prime}))
. This contradicts the assumption that
r(Y)
has the largest volume over all
(d-1)
-simplices
Y
inscribed in
H\cap S^{d-1}
.

Thus, if
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(Y))
is maximal, then
Y
must be a regular simplex. At the same time, for all regular
(d-1)
-simplices
V
inscribed in
H\cap S^{d-1}
, the volumes
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(V))
are the same. Then
{\rm Vol}_{d-1}(r(V))
is maximal.

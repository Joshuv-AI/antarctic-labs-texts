Composite Inflation and further refining dS swampland conjecture
Jureeporn Yuennan, Phongpichit Channuie

Abstract
A natural combination of the first and second derivatives of the scalar potential was achieved in a framework of an alternative refined de Sitter conjecture recently proposed in the literature. In this work, we study various inflation models in which the inflaton is a composite field emerging from various strongly interacting field theories. We then examine if these three models of inflation can satisfy this further refining de Sitter swampland conjecture or not. Regarding our analysis with proper choices of parameters a , b = 1 − a a,\,b=1-a and q q , we find that some inflationary models are in strong tension with the refined Swampland conjecture. However, all of them can always satisfy the alternative refined de Sitter conjecture. Therefore, one may expect that all inflationary models might all be in “landscape” since the “further refining de Sitter swampland conjecture” is satisfied.

II.1
Composite NJL Inflation (NJLI)

The model has been proposed so far by the authors of Ref.
Channuie:2016iyy
. In the inflation sector, the action describing model of inflation in which the inflaton is non-minimally coupled to gravity in the Jordan (J) frame takes the form

\displaystyle S_{\rm{J}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}_{\textrm{\tiny{PL}}}R+\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi-\frac{\xi\,R}{2}\,\left(\varphi^{2}-\frac{v^{2}}{2}\right)-V_{J}(\varphi)\bigg]\,,

(7)

\displaystyle V_{J}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\varphi}\varphi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\varphi^{4},

(8)

with
v
being a vacuum expectation value (VEV) of the inflaton field
\varphi
. The above non-minimal coupling framework is actually inspired by Higgs-inflation investigated in Ref.
Bezrukov:2007ep
. The importance is that the non-minimal coupling (
\xi
) of the Higgs doublet field
(H)
to gravity, i.e.
\sim\xi H^{\dagger}HR
, is needed. With
\xi=0
, a realistic quartic Higgs self-interaction term generates an unacceptably large amplitude of primordial inhomogeneities
Bezrukov:2008ut
. Specifically, with
\xi\sim 10^{4}
, the model leads to a successful inflation and produces a spectrum of primordial fluctuations in good agreement with the observational data
Bezrukov:2007ep
.

Applying a conformal transformation, we can rewrite the action as minimally coupled but with a new canonically normalized field. Hence the conformal transformation can be basically implemented by making use of the following replacement:

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}\,g_{\mu\nu}=\left(1+\frac{\xi\,(\varphi^{2}-v^{2}/2)}{M^{2}_{p}}\right)g_{\mu\nu}\,.

(9)

Therefore, the action in (
7
) becomes the Einstein-frame (E) form:

\displaystyle S_{\rm E}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}_{p}R+\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{-4}\left(\Omega^{2}+\frac{6\xi\varphi^{2}}{M^{2}_{p}}\right)g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\,\partial_{\nu}\varphi-U(\varphi)\bigg]\,,

(10)

where

\Omega^{2}=\left(1+\frac{\xi\,(\varphi^{2}-v^{2}/2)}{M^{2}_{p}}\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad U(\varphi)\equiv\Omega^{-4}V_{J}(\varphi)\,.

(11)

Here, we end up with a non-canonical kinetic term for the scalar field. After introducing a new canonically normalized scalar field
\chi
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi(\varphi)\partial_{\nu}\chi(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\chi}{d\varphi}\right)^{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi\,,

(12)

where

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}=\left(\frac{d\chi}{d\varphi}\right)=\sqrt{\Omega^{-4}\left(\Omega^{2}+\frac{6\xi\varphi^{2}}{M^{2}_{p}}\right)}\,.

(13)

For small field value, i.e.
\xi\varphi^{2}\ll M^{2}_{p}
, the potential for the field
\chi
becomes to that of the original field,
\varphi
. However, it is not the case for large-field value, i.e.
\xi\varphi^{2}\gg M^{2}_{p}
. In the later case, we can figure out the solution of
\varphi
and write in terms of the field
\chi
as

\displaystyle\varphi\simeq\frac{M_{p}}{\sqrt{\xi}}\exp\left(\frac{\chi}{\sqrt{6}M_{p}}\right)\,.

(14)

The effective potential
U(\chi)
has the form

\displaystyle U(\chi)\simeq\frac{\lambda M^{4}_{p}}{2\xi^{2}}\bigg[1+\exp\left(-\frac{2\chi}{\sqrt{6}M_{p}}\right)\bigg]^{-2}\,,

(15)

where we have assumed that the field is far away from the minimum of its potential such that
\xi v^{2}\ll M^{2}_{p}
. In the limit of
\varphi^{2}\gg M^{2}_{p}/\xi\gg v^{2}
, the slow-roll parameters in the Einstein frame can be written as functions of the field
\varphi(\chi)
:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M^{2}_{p}}{2}\left(\frac{dU/d\chi}{U}\right)^{2}=\frac{M^{2}_{p}}{2}\left(\frac{U^{\prime}}{U}\frac{1}{\chi^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\simeq\frac{4M^{4}_{p}}{3\xi^{2}\varphi^{4}},

(16)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{2}_{p}\frac{d^{2}U/d\chi^{2}}{U}=M^{2}_{\textrm{\tiny{PL}}}\frac{U^{\prime\prime}\chi^{\prime}-\chi^{\prime\prime}}{U\chi^{\prime 3}}\simeq-\frac{4M^{2}_{p}}{3\xi\varphi^{2}}\,,

(17)

where “
′
” denotes derivative with respect to
\varphi
. Notice that the results we obtained here are approximately the same for those of inflationary model driven by the SM Higgs boson
Bezrukov:2007ep
and another composite inflation
Channuie:2011rq
. We can quantify the field value at the end of inflation using
\epsilon=1
. We find
\varphi_{\rm{\tiny{end}}}\simeq(4/3)^{1/4}M_{p}/\sqrt{\xi}
. The number of e-folds for the change of the field
\varphi
from
\varphi_{N}
to
\varphi_{\rm{\tiny{end}}}
is given by

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{2}_{p}}\int^{\chi_{N}}_{\chi_{\rm{\tiny{end}}}}\frac{U}{dU/d\chi}d\chi

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M^{2}_{p}}\int^{\varphi_{N}}_{\varphi_{\rm{\tiny{end}}}}\frac{U}{dU/d\varphi}\left(\frac{d\chi}{d\varphi}\right)^{2}d\varphi\simeq\frac{6\xi}{8M^{2}_{p}}\left(\varphi^{2}_{N}-\varphi^{2}_{\rm{\tiny{end}}}\right)\,,

where
\varphi_{N}
represents the field value at the horizon crossing of the observed CMB modes. After substituting
\varphi_{\rm{\tiny{end}}}
into the above relation, we obtain

. To generate the proper amplitude of the density perturbations, the potential must satisfy the COBE renormalization
U/\epsilon\simeq(0.0276\,M_{p})^{4}

Bezrukov:2008ut
. Inserting (
11
) and (
18
) into the COBE normalization, we find the required value for

\displaystyle\xi\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda}{3}}\frac{N}{(0.0276)^{2}}\,.

(19)

The spectral index of curvature perturbation

and the tensor-to-scalar ratio

are given in terms of the e-folds

:

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-6\epsilon+2\eta\simeq 1-\frac{2}{N}-\frac{9}{2{N^{2}}},

(20)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16\epsilon\simeq\frac{12}{N^{2}}.

(21)

For example, we obtain from Eq.(

) and (

\xi\sim 64,000\sqrt{\lambda},\,n_{s}\simeq 0.965
and

II.2
Glueball Inflation (GI)

This class of model assumes that inflation is driven by a composite
state in a strongly interacting theory, see

Bezrukov:2011mv
;
Channuie:2012bv
. Let us consider the following model, see also Ref.
Bezrukov:2011mv

(22)

where
\Lambda
is a mass scale. Since the inflaton field in this case has mass dimension four, it is more convenient to work in the field
\phi
with mass dimension one related to
\varphi
via

\frac{\varphi}{\Lambda^{4}}=\left(\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}\right)^{4},

(23)

with

\phi_{0}=4\sqrt{2}\Lambda.

(24)

Let us consider the situation where the model has a general non-minimal coupling to gravity of the form

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{M^{2}+\xi\Lambda^{2}\Big(\phi/\phi_{0}\Big)^{2}}{2}R+L_{GI}\right].

(25)

The coupling to gravity is characterized by the parameter
\xi
. Then, the action in the Einstein frame
reads

;
Channuie:2012bv

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\biggl[-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}_{p}R-\Omega^{-2}\left(1+\frac{3\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}\Big(\phi/\phi_{0}\Big)^{2}}{16M_{p}^{2}}\Omega^{-2}\right)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\phi_{0}}\right)^{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi-\Omega^{-4}V_{\mathrm{GI}}\biggr],

22

(27)

with
\phi_{0}\equiv 4\sqrt{2}\Lambda
. We now can introduce a canonically normalized field
\chi
related to
\phi
via

\frac{1}{2}{\tilde{g}}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi(\phi)\partial_{\nu}\chi(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\chi}{d\phi}\right)^{2}{\tilde{g}}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi\,,

(28)

where

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\chi}{d\phi}\right)^{2}=\Omega^{-2}\left(1+\frac{3\xi^{2}\Lambda^{2}\Big(\phi/\phi_{0}\Big)^{2}}{16M_{p}^{2}}\Omega^{-2}\right)\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\phi_{0}}\right)^{2}\,.

(29)

The canonically normalized field
\chi
is
such that
\chi\propto\ln\phi
. In that case the potential
reduces to
\Omega^{-4}V_{GI}\propto\ln\phi
. Therefore, in terms of the canonically normalized field, we have:

\displaystyle S_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dx^{4}\sqrt{-g}\Bigg\{-\frac{1}{2}M_{p}^{2}R+\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial_{\nu}\chi-U_{GI}(\chi)\Bigg\}\,,

(30)

with

\displaystyle U_{GI}(\chi)=\Omega^{-4}V_{GI}(\varphi)\,,

(31)

We now turn to the slow-roll analysis of the potential derived previously in Eq.(
31
). We consider here the large field regime
N_{c}^{2}\xi\Lambda^{2}\left(\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}\right)^{2}\gg M^{2}
. Hence the potential of this model
in the Einstein frame written in terms of the field
\phi
takes the form

\displaystyle U_{GI}(\phi)=\frac{2M_{p}^{4}}{\xi^{2}}\ln\Big(\frac{\phi}{\phi_{0}}\Big)\,.

(32)

with
\alpha
being a constant which is expected to be of order unity
Feo:2004mr
. It is rather straightforward to show that after inserting Eq.(
32
) into the slow-roll parameters,
\varepsilon_{i},\,i=1,2
, in this model, we find in the
\phi
variable
Channuie:2015ewa

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{M^{2}_{p}}{2}\left(\frac{dU_{\rm GI}/d\chi}{U_{\rm GI}}\right)^{2}=\frac{M^{2}_{p}}{2}\left(\frac{dU_{\rm GI}/d\phi}{U_{GI}}\frac{d\phi}{d\chi}\right)^{2}\simeq\frac{4}{3\big(\ln(\phi/\phi_{0})\big)^{2}}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M^{2}_{p}\left(\frac{d^{2}U_{GI}/d\chi^{2}}{U_{\rm GI}}\right)=M^{2}_{p}\left(\frac{dU_{\rm GI}/d\phi}{d\chi/d\phi}\right)^{\prime}\left(\frac{d\phi/d\chi}{U_{\rm GI}}\right)=0\,,

(34)

where a prime denotes derivative with respect to the field
\phi
. In the above results, we have assumed the large non-minimal coupling
\xi\gg 1
. At the end of inflation, i.e.,

, we find for this model

. In the large field limits, the number of e-foldings is given by

\displaystyle N\simeq\frac{3}{16}\Big[\left(\ln\Big(\frac{\phi_{ini}}{\phi_{0}}\Big)\right)^{2}-\left(\ln\Big(\frac{\phi_{end}}{\phi_{0}}\Big)\right)^{2}\Big]\,.

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{\phi_{ini}}{\phi_{0}}\simeq\exp\left(\sqrt{16\,N/3}\right)\,.

(36)

\displaystyle\frac{U_{ini,\,{\rm SYM}}}{\varepsilon_{ini}}=(0.0276\,M_{p})^{4}\,.

Using the above constraint, in the limit of

\displaystyle\xi\sim 4550\,N^{3/2}\,.

(38)

For this model, to the lowest order in the slow-roll approximation, the inflationary predictions in terms of the number

\displaystyle n_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-6\varepsilon_{1}+2\varepsilon_{2}\simeq 1-\frac{3}{2\,N}\,.

(39)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16\varepsilon_{3}\simeq\frac{4}{N}\,.

(40)

II.3
super Yang-Mills Inflation (SYMI)

An instructive construction of the super Yang-Mills (SYM) effective Lagrangian was proposed in Ref.
Veneziano:1982ah
, see also Ref.
Sannino:2003xe

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F^{a\mu\nu}+\frac{i}{2}\bar{\lambda}^{a}{\not{D}}_{ab}\lambda^{b},

with

where
\alpha

\Lambda
a mass scale. Since the inflaton field in this case has mass dimension three, it is more convenient to work in the field
\phi

\varphi

(43)

(80)

IV
Conclusion

In general, the space of effective theories can be consistently coupled to a theory of quantum gravity. However, it can include the set of phenomenological models which cannot be derived as a low energy effective theory of a quantum gravity in the high energy regime. Therefore, it is required for consistent EFTs not to lie in the swampland. Swampland conjecture has gained significant attention which allows us to validate or invalidate a large class of low energy effective theories. However, a natural combination of the first and second derivatives of the scalar potential was recently achieved in a framework of an alternative refined de Sitter conjecture recently proposed in the literature.

In this work, we studied various inflation models in which the inflaton is a composite field emerging from various strongly interacting field theories. We examined if these three models of inflation can satisfy this further refining de Sitter swampland conjecture or not. We discussed the theoretical viability of various composite models of inflation in light of the recent refined Swampland conjectures. Regarding our analysis with proper choices of parameters
a,\,b=1-a
and
q
, we discovered that some inflationary models are in strong tension with the refined Swampland conjecture. However, all of them always satisfied with the alternative refining de Sitter conjecture. Therefore, it was expected that all inflationary models might all be in “landscape” since the “further refining de Sitter swampland conjecture” is satisfied.

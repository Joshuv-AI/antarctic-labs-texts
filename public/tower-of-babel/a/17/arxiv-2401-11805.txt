Abstract
In this work, we investigate the problem of simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution. Leveraging the subspace assumption regarding unknown point spread functions, this problem can be reformulated as a low-rank matrix demixing problem. We propose a convex recovery approach that utilizes the low-rank structure of each vectorized Hankel matrix associated with the target matrix. Our analysis reveals that for achieving exact recovery, the number of samples needs to satisfy the condition n ≳ K ​ s ​ r ​ log ⁡ ( s ​ n ) n\gtrsim Ksr\log(sn) . Empirical evaluations demonstrate the recovery capabilities and the computational efficiency of the convex method.

Introduction

The simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution of point sources refers to the problem of concurrently achieving blind super-resolution

,

,

,

for

, multi-user multi-channel estimation

It is well-established that blind super-resolution is intrinsically ill-posed without additional assumptions

,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. The problem under consideration can be viewed as an extension of blind super-resolution, which exacerbates its complexity. Consequently, we introduce a subspace assumption and reformulate the problem of simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution as a structured low-rank matrix demixing problem.

Recent research efforts, as demonstrated in
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
, have harnessed the inherent structure of data matrices to develop various convex relaxation techniques for addressing this problem. Specifically, in
[
5
]
, inspired by joint radar and communication systems,
the authors proposed an atomic norm minimization (ANM) approach for simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution with
K=2
. Furthermore, in
[
7
]
, a nuclear norm minimization method was designed for the same problem. Additionally,
[
9
]
extended ANM to address this problem for arbitrary
K
, but the theoretical analysis is lacking.

In this paper, our focus centers on addressing the simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution problem for arbitrary values of
K
. We utilize the vectorized Hankel lift technique as introduced in
[
4
]
to leverage the low-dimensional structures within the target matrices. This approach enables a convex framework for reconstruction, and the exact recovery guarantees based on standard assumptions are established.

II
Problem formulation and proposed method

II-A

Problem Formulation

Consider a set of
K
point source signals denoted as
\{x_{k}(t)\}_{k=1}^{K}
, where the
k
-th signal can be expressed in the following form:

\displaystyle x_{k}(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{k}}d_{k,\ell}\delta(t-\tau_{k,\ell}),

where
\delta(\cdot)
represents the Dirac function,
r_{k}
indicates the number of spikes, and
\{\tau_{k,\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{r_{k}}
and
\{d_{k,\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{r_{k}}
represent the locations and amplitudes of the point source signals, respectively.

Let
y(t)
be the summation of point source signals convolved with unknown point spread functions, given by

\displaystyle y(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}x_{k}(t)\ast g_{k}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{k}}d_{k,\ell}g_{k}(t-\tau_{k,\ell}).

(II.1)

By taking the Fourier transform of (
II.1
) and subsequently sampling, we obtain that for
j=0,\cdots,n-1,

\displaystyle\bm{y}[j]=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{k}}d_{k,\ell}e^{-\imath 2\pi(j-1)\tau_{k,\ell}}\hat{g}_{k}[j],

(II.2)

where
\bm{g}_{k}:=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{g}_{k}[0]&\cdots&\hat{g}_{k}[n-1]\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

are unknown. The goal of the simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution problem is to jointly recover both
\{d_{k,\ell},\tau_{k,\ell}\}
and
\{\bm{g}_{k}\}
from (
II.2
).

As previously mentioned, simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution is an ill-posed problem without any additional assumptions. In alignment with prior research
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
, we adopt a similar approach and make the assumption that each point spread function
\bm{g}_{k}
lies within a known subspace defined by
\bm{B}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times s_{k}}
, such that:

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{k}=\bm{B}_{k}\bm{h}_{k},\quad k=1,\cdots,K,

where
\bm{h}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{s_{k}}
denotes an unknown coefficient vector.

Under the subspace assumption and by leveraging a lifting approach, we can express the measurements given in (
II.2
) as a superposition of linear observations involving
\left\{\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{k}}d_{k,\ell}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{a}_{\tau_{k,\ell}}^{\mathsf{T}}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
:

\displaystyle\bm{y}[j]=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\langle\bm{e}_{j}\bm{b}^{\mathsf{H}}_{k,j},\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}\right\rangle,\quad j=0,\cdots,n-1,

where
\bm{e}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
represents the
j
-th standard basis vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\bm{b}_{k,j}
denotes the
j
-th row of
\bm{B}_{k}
, and
\bm{a}_{\tau}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
stands as the steering vector, defined as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&e^{-\imath 2\pi\cdot 1\cdot\tau}&\cdots&e^{-\imath 2\pi\cdot(n-1)\cdot\tau}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
s_{1}=\cdots=s_{K}=s
and
r_{1}=\cdots=r_{K}=r
. Additionally, consider
{\cal A}_{k}
as a linear operator
{\cal A}_{k}:\mathbb{C}^{s\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
, defined as

\displaystyle\left({\cal A}_{k}\left(\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}\right)\right)[j]=\left\langle\bm{e}_{j}\bm{b}^{\mathsf{H}}_{k,j},\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}\right\rangle,\quad j=0,\cdots,n-1.

Consequently, the measurement model can be succinctly expressed as

\displaystyle\bm{y}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\cal A}_{k}\left(\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}\right).

(II.3)

Hence, the problem of simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution can be cast as the task of demixing a sequence of matrices
\{\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}\}
from the superimposed linear measurements of these matrices.
Upon successfully recovering the data matrices
{\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}}
, it becomes feasible to extract the frequencies
{\tau_{k,\ell}}
through spatial smoothing MUSIC
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

II-B

Proposed Method

Let
{\cal H}
denote the vectorized Hankel lifting operator, which transforms a matrix
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{s\times n}
into a matrix of dimensions
sn_{1}\times n_{2}
, defined as follows:

\displaystyle{\cal H}(\bm{X})=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}_{0}&\bm{x}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{x}_{n_{2}-1}\\
\bm{x}_{1}&\bm{x}_{2}&\cdots&\bm{x}_{n_{2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\bm{x}_{n_{1}-1}&\bm{x}_{n-1}&\cdots&\bm{x}_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{sn_{1}\times n_{2}},

where
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{s}
represents the
i
-th column of
\bm{X}
, and
n_{1}+n_{2}=n+1
. It has been demonstrated that the rank of
{\cal H}(\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural})
is at most
r

[
4
]
. Consequently, we adopt the nuclear norm minimization to promote the low rank structure of
{\cal H}(\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural})
and consider the following convex approach to recover
\{\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}\}
:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\bm{X}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|{\cal H}(\bm{X}_{k})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}},\quad\mbox{s.t.}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\cal A}_{k}(\bm{X}_{k})=\bm{y},

(II.4)

which is denoted as the Multiple Vectorized Hankel Lift (MVHL). Since this convex optimization problem can be effectively tackled using various existing software packages, our focus is narrowed down to evaluating the theoretical performance of (
II.4
) and investigating when its solution aligns with
\{\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}\}
.

III
Main results

Before presenting our main results, we will introduce some standard assumptions.

Assumption III.1
.

The matrices
\{\bm{B}_{k}\}
are independent, and the column vectors
\{\bm{b}_{k,\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{n}
of the subspace matrix
\bm{B}_{k}
are sampled independently and identically from a population
F
which obeys the following properties:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{b}\bm{b}^{\ast}\right]

\displaystyle=\bm{I}_{s}\mbox{ and }\max_{0\leq k\leq s-1}|\bm{b}[j]|^{2}\leq\mu_{0}.

(III.1)

Remark III.1
.

Assumption
III.1
is widely used in blind super-resolution
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, dual-blind deconvolution
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
, and can be satisfied by Rademacher random vector or when
\bm{b}
is uniformly sampled from a Discrete Fourier Transform matrix.

Assumption III.2
.

Let
{\cal H}(\bm{X}^{\natural})=\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^{\mathsf{H}}
be the singular value decomposition of
{\cal H}(\bm{X}^{\natural})
, where
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{C}^{sn_{1}\times r},\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
and
\bm{V}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}\times r}
. Denote
\bm{U}^{\mathsf{H}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{U}_{0}^{\mathsf{H}}&\cdots&\bm{U}_{n_{1}-1}^{\mathsf{H}}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf{H}}
, where
\bm{U}_{\ell}=\bm{U}[\ell s+1:(\ell+1)s,:]
is the
\ell
-th block of
\bm{U}
for
\ell=0,\cdots,n_{1}-1
. The matrix
\bm{X}^{\natural}
is
\mu_{1}
-incoherence if
\bm{U}
and
\bm{V}
obey that

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq\ell\leq n_{1}-1}\left\|\bm{U}_{\ell}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu_{1}r}{n}\mbox{ and }\max_{0\leq j\leq n_{2}-1}\left\|\bm{e}_{j}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{V}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{2}}}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu_{1}r}{n}

for some positive constant
\mu_{1}
.

Remark III.2
.

Assumption
III.2
is commonly adopted in blind super-resolution, and is satisfied when the minimum wrap-up distance between the locations of point sources is greater than about
2/n
.

Now we state our main results, whose proofs are deferred to Section
V
.

Theorem III.1
.

Under Assumptions
III.1
and
III.2
, the matrices
\{\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural}\}_{k=1}^{K}
are the unique optimal solution to the problem (
II.4
) with probability at least
1-c_{0}(sn)^{-c_{1}}
, provided that
n\gtrsim K\mu_{0}\mu_{1}sr\log(sn)
, where
c_{0},c_{1}
are absolute constants.

V
Proofs of Main Result

The proof of Theorem
III.1
relies on the dual certificate technique which has been widely used in analyzing low rank matrix recovery
[
15
,
16
]
and blind super-resolution
[
4
]
. Let
{\cal H}^{\ast}
be the adjoint of
{\cal H}
which maps

sn_{1}\times n_{2}
matrices to matrices of size
s\times n
.
We define
{\cal D}^{2}={\cal H}^{\ast}{\cal H}
. Given a matrix
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{s\times n}
, the matrix
{\cal D}^{2}\left(\bm{X}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{s\times n}
is given by

\displaystyle{\cal D}^{2}\left(\bm{X}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}w_{0}\bm{x}_{0}&\cdots&w_{n-1}\bm{x}_{n-1}\end{bmatrix},

where
w_{i}(i=0,\cdots,n-1)
is defined as

\displaystyle w_{i}=\sharp\{(j,k)~|~j+k=i,0\leq j\leq n_{1}-1,0\leq k\leq n_{2}-1\}.

In addition, let the operator
{\cal G}
be
{\cal G}={\cal H}{\cal D}^{-1}
. The adjoint of
{\cal G}
, denoted by
{\cal G}^{\ast}
, is given by
{\cal G}^{\ast}={\cal D}^{-1}{\cal H}^{\ast}
.
Letting
\bm{Z}={\cal H}({\cal X})={\cal G}{\cal D}(\bm{X})
, one has
{\cal D}(\bm{X})={\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{Z})~\mbox{and}~({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{Z})=\bm{0}
.
Moreover, define
\bm{D}=\diag\left(\sqrt{w_{0}},\cdots,\sqrt{w_{n-1}}\right)
. We have
{\cal A}_{k}{\cal D}(\bm{X})=\bm{D}{\cal A}_{k}(\bm{X})
for any matrix
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{s\times n}
. Consequently, the problem (
II.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\min_{\{\bm{Z}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|\bm{Z}_{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}

\displaystyle\text{ s.t. }\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\cal A}_{k}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{Z}_{k})=\bm{D}\bm{y},

(V.1)

\displaystyle~({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})\left(\bm{Z}_{k}\right)=\bm{0},~\mbox{for~}k=1,\cdots,K.

Due to the equivalence between (
II.4
) and (
V.1
), it suffices to investigate the recovery guarantee of (
V.1
).

V-A

Deterministic Optimality Condition

Recall that the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
{\cal H}(\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural})
is
{\cal H}(\bm{X}_{k}^{\natural})=\bm{U}_{k}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}
. The tangent space
T_{k}
is defined as

\displaystyle T_{k}=\left\{\bm{U}_{k}\bm{A}^{\mathsf{H}}+\bm{B}\bm{V}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}:\bm{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{2}\times r},\bm{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{sn_{1}\times r}\right\}.

The projection of
\bm{M}
onto the tangent space
T_{k}
is given by

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{T_{k}}(\bm{M})=\bm{U}_{k}\bm{U}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{M}+\bm{M}\bm{V}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}-\bm{U}_{k}\bm{U}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\bm{M}\bm{V}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}.

Theorem V.1
.

Suppose for any
k\in[K]
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}}{\cal G}{\cal A}_{k}^{\ast}{\cal A}_{k}{\cal G}^{\ast}{\cal P}_{T_{k}}-{\cal P}_{T_{k}}{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}{\cal P}_{T_{k}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4},

(V.2)

\displaystyle\mu:=\max_{i\neq j}\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{i}}{\cal G}{\cal A}_{i}^{\ast}{\cal A}_{j}{\cal G}^{\ast}{\cal P}_{T_{j}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8K}.

(V.3)

If there exists a series of
\{\bm{Y}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}\subset\mathbb{C}^{sn_{1}\times n_{2}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\ast}-{\cal P}_{T_{k}}(\bm{Y}_{k})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{48Ks\mu_{0}},

(V.4)

\displaystyle\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}}^{\perp}(\bm{Y}_{k})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2},

(V.5)

\displaystyle\left({\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{Y}_{1}),\cdots,{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{Y}_{K})\right)

\displaystyle\in\text{Range}({\cal A}^{\ast}).

(V.6)

Then
\left\{\bm{Z}_{k}^{\natural}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
is the unique solution to problem (
V.1
).

Lemma V.2
.

Suppose the conditions (
V.2
), (
V.3
), and
\left\|{\cal A}_{k}\right\|\leq\sqrt{s\mu_{0}}
holds.
Assume
\bm{M}_{k}
obeys that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\cal A}_{k}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{M}_{k})=\bm{0},\quad({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast})(\bm{M}_{k})=\bm{0}.

(V.7)

Then one has

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}}(\bm{M}_{k})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}\leq 16Ks\mu_{0}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}(\bm{M}_{k})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}.

Proof of Theorem
V.1
.

For any feasible solution of problem (
V.1
), it must have the form of
\left\{\bm{Z}_{k}^{\natural}+\bm{M}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
,
where the perturbation matrices
\bm{M}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{sn_{1}\times n_{2}}
satisfy condition (
V.7
).
It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|\bm{Z}_{k}^{\natural}+\bm{M}_{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}>\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|\bm{Z}_{k}^{\natural}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}

for any nontrivial set of
\left\{\bm{M}_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{K}
. For each
\bm{M}_{k}
, there exists an matrix
\bm{S}_{k}\in T_{k}^{\perp}
such that

\displaystyle\left\langle\bm{M}_{k},\bm{S}_{k}\right\rangle=\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}~\mbox{and}~\left\|\bm{S}_{k}\right\|\leq 1.

Therefore,
\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}+\bm{S}_{k}
belongs to the sub-differential of
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}
at
\bm{Z}^{\natural}_{k}=\bm{U}_{k}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\ast}
. By the definition of subgradient, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|\bm{Z}_{k}^{\natural}+\bm{M}_{k}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\left\|\bm{Z}_{k}^{\natural}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}+\left\langle\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\ast}+\bm{S}_{k},\bm{M}_{k}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\left\|\bm{Z}_{k}^{\natural}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}+\left\langle\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\ast},\bm{M}_{k}\right\rangle+\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\left\|\bm{Z}_{k}^{\natural}\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}+\left\langle\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\ast}-\bm{Y}_{k},\bm{M}_{k}\right\rangle+\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}\right),

where the last is due to the fact that
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\langle\bm{Y}_{k},\bm{M}_{k}\right\rangle=0
.
To this end, we need to show that

\displaystyle I_{1}:=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\left\langle\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\ast}-\bm{Y}_{k},\bm{M}_{k}\right\rangle+\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}\right)>0.

By decomposing the inner product in
I_{1}
onto tangent space and its cotangent space respectively, one can obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\langle\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\ast}-{\cal P}_{T_{k}}\left(\bm{Y}_{k}\right),{\cal P}_{T_{k}}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\langle{\cal P}_{T_{k}}^{\perp}\left(\bm{Y}_{k}\right),{\cal P}_{T_{k}}^{\perp}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}

\displaystyle\geq-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}\left\|\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\ast}-{\cal P}_{T_{k}}\left(\bm{Y}_{k}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}\left(\bm{M}_{k}\right)\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mbox{$\ast$}}}}\left(1-\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}}^{\perp}\left(\bm{Y}_{k}\right)\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}\left(-\frac{1}{48Ks\mu_{0}}\cdot 16Ks\mu_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}(\bm{M}_{k})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left\|{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}(\bm{M}_{k})\right\|_{{\footnotesize{\mathsf{F}}}}^{2}>0,

where step (a) follows from (
V.4
) and (
V.5
), step (b) is due to Lemma
V.2
, and the last inequality uses the fact that if
{\cal P}_{T_{k}^{\perp}}(\bm{M}_{k})=\bm{0}
for all
1\leq k\leq K
, then
\bm{M}_{k}=\bm{0}.

∎

V-B

Construction of Dual Certificate

Following the well developed route in
[
16
]
, the dual certificates
\{\bm{Y}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
are constructed as follows: for any
k\in[K]
, we firstly divide the linear measurements
{\cal A}_{k}
into
t_{0}
partitions, denoted
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t=1}^{t_{0}}
, and let
m=\frac{n}{t_{0}}
. Define

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{k,t}(\bm{X})=\left\{\left\langle\bm{b}_{k,j}\bm{e}_{j}^{\mathsf{T}},\bm{X}\right\rangle\right\}_{j\in\Omega_{t}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\left|\Omega_{t}\right|}.

(V.8)

Let
\bm{Y}_{k}^{0}=\bm{0}
and
\bm{\lambda}^{0}:=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\cal A}_{k,1}{\cal G}^{\ast}(\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}})
. Then the golfing scheme for constructing
\{\bm{Y}_{k}\}
are expressed as

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}^{t-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\cal A}_{k,t}{\cal G}^{\ast}{\cal P}_{T_{k}}(\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}-\bm{Y}_{k,t-1})\text{ and }

\displaystyle\bm{Y}_{k,t}

\displaystyle=\bm{Y}_{k,t-1}+\frac{n}{m}{\cal G}{\cal A}^{\ast}_{k,t}(\bm{\lambda}^{t-1})

\displaystyle\quad+\left({\cal I}-{\cal G}{\cal G}^{\ast}\right){\cal P}_{T_{k}}\left(\bm{U}_{k}\bm{V}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}-\bm{Y}_{k,t-1}\right),

\displaystyle\quad\text{ for }t=1,\cdots,t_{0},

\displaystyle\bm{Y}_{k}

\displaystyle=\bm{Y}_{k,t_{0}}.

VI
Conclusion

This paper studies the problem of simultaneous blind demixing and super-resolution. We introduce a convex approach named MVHL and rigorously establish its recovery performance. Our analysis demonstrates that MVHL can achieve exact recovery of the target matrices provided the sample complexity satisfying
n\gtrsim Ksr\log(sn)
. Furthermore, we illustrate the efficacy of MVHL through a series of numerical simulations.

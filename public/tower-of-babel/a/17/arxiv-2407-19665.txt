Uniformly distributed periodic orbits of endomorphisms on n n -tori
Daohua Yu, Shaobo Gan

Abstract
We prove that any ergodic endomorphism on an n n -torus admits a sequence of periodic orbits uniformly distributed in the metric sense. As a corollary, an endomorphism on the torus is ergodic if and only if the Haar measure can be approximated by periodic measures.

Introduction

For any natural number
n\geq 1
, denote by
M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
the set of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{Z}
. Any
A\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
can be naturally considered as a linear map from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
v\mapsto Av
; since
A(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
A
also induces a map from the
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
to itself, i.e.,
v\mapsto Av\mod 1
, still denoted by
A
. If
\det(A)\not=0,
such a map will be called an
endomorphism
on the
n
-torus. The best known endomorphism may be Arnold’s cat map on the 2-torus, which is induced by
A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}2&1\\
1&1\end{array}\right].
Related to the sophisticated construction of Birkhoff sections in his thesis
[
13
]
, Y. Shi raised the following question:

Question 1.1

(Shi’s question)
.

Does Arnold’s cat map admit a sequence of periodic orbits
\{O_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

d(O_{k})^{2}T(O_{k})\geq C

for some constant
C>0

?
Here
T(O_{k})
denotes the period of
O_{k}
and

d(O_{k})=\min\{d(x,y):x,y\in O_{k},x\not=y\}.

In this paper, we will give an affirmative answer to Shi’s question. In fact, we will prove a slightly general result, i.e., similar periodic orbits exist for any ergodic torus endomorphism. Here, “ergodic” means that the endomorphism is ergodic with respect to Leb, the Haar measure on the torus.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be an ergodic endomorphism on an
n
-torus. Then there is a positive number
C
and a sequence of periodic orbits
\{O_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that

d(O_{k})^{n}T(O_{k})\geq C\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{k\to+\infty}T(O_{k})=+\infty.

[formula]

Remark 1.2
.

For any periodic orbit
O
with period
T
, according to the definition of
d(O)
we know that
\bigcup_{x\in O}B(x,\frac{d(O)}{2})
are pairwise disjoint. We have

T(O)\left(\frac{d(O)}{2}\right)^{n}\omega_{n}\leq 1,

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of
n
dimensional unit ball.
So there is a constant
C^{\prime}=\frac{2^{n}}{\omega_{n}}
independent of
O
such that
d(O)^{n}T(O)\leq C^{\prime}
. Thus,
n
is the largest exponential index such that the equation
(*)
holds.

Definition 1.3
.

A sequence of periodic orbits
\{O_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
(*)
will be called
uniformly distributed
(in the metric sense).

Recall that a sequence of points
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in an
n
-torus is called
uniformly distributed
if

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{x_{i}}\xrightarrow{\text{weak*}}Leb\quad(k\to+\infty).

The following proposition justifies Definition
1.3
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
A
be an ergodic endomorphism on an
n
-torus and
\{O_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of periodic orbits of
A
.
Denote the periodic measure
\mu_{k}=T(O_{k})^{-1}\sum_{x\in O_{k}}\delta_{x}
. If
\{O_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly distributed, then
\mu_{k}
weak* converges to Leb.

As a corollary, we obtain the following periodic approximation result.

Corollary 1.5
.

An endomorphism
A
on the torus is ergodic if and only if
A
has a sequence of periodic measures weak* converging to Leb.

Started from
[

[

, etc..

The quantity

also appears in the study of ergodic optimization (

According to the proof of Proposition

, any limit of
\mu_{k}
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. Leb. Hence, small perturbations of ergodic endomorphisms on the torus may not admit a sequence of uniformly distributed periodic orbits.

Here, we provide an outline of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, especially the construction of the sequence of uniformly distributed periodic orbits, which is the core of this paper.
The endomorphism on an
n
-torus can be classified into two cases, one is irreducible, the other is reducible.
For the irreducible case, periodic orbits will be chosen from the eigenvectors of
A
modulo powers of some primes. These primes come from Frobenius density theorem in number theory.
By comparing the period of the eigenvector with the period of the linear recurrence sequence induced by
A
with the initial condition
0,\cdots,0,1
, we can see that one of eigenvectors has a big period, and this one is the periodic point picked out. For the reducible case, the structure theorem for modules over polynomial rings is used to simplify the problem.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
2
]
(Lifting the Exponent Lemma)
Let
p
be an odd prime,
x,y,k,m\in\mathbb{Z},k>0
.
Denote
v_{p}(m)=\max\{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}:p^{i}\mid m\}
.
Suppose that
p\mid x-y
but
p\nmid x,p\nmid y
. Then

v_{p}(x^{k}-y^{k})=v_{p}(x-y)+v_{p}(k).

Corollary 2.1
.

Given
a\in\mathbb{Z},|a|\neq 1
, and an odd prime
p
,
(a,p)=1
, let
d=\min\{n>0:p\mid a^{n}-1\}
and
v_{p}(a^{d}-1)=t
.
Then for any
k\geq t,k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
p^{k}\mid a^{n}-1
if and only if
p^{k-t}d\mid n
.

Proof.

Since
p\mid a^{n}-1
,
n=dl,l\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By using Theorem
2.1
to
x=a^{d}
,
y=1
, we know that
v_{p}(a^{n}-1)=v_{p}(x^{l}-1)=v_{p}(x-1)+v_{p}(l)=t+v_{p}(l)
. Thus
v_{p}(a^{n}-1)\geq k
if and only if
v_{p}(l)\geq k-t
, if and only if
p^{k-t}d\mid n
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
12

) (
Frobenius Density Theorem
) Let

Denote by

is irreducible on

\Lambda
has positive density in primes, where

\Lambda=\{p\in\mathbb{N}:p\text{is a prime},\,\exists a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z},s.t.,f(x)\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-a_{i})\mod p\}.

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
, there are infinitely many primes

such that

has
n

Furthermore, for such

nonzero distinct integer solutions

Proof.

According to
[

From Theorem

, we know that there are infinitely many primes

and
|c_{0}|
, s.t.,

Thus

. In other words,
f^{\prime}(a_{i})\not\equiv 0\mod p
,

. Since

,

Suppose that there is a solution

satisfying that

and

. Thus one can write

as

and

. Suppose

Then, let

. Now we have

. Since

f(x_{k+1})\equiv f(x_{k})+f^{\prime}(x_{k})sp^{l}\equiv p^{l}(r+f^{\prime}(x_{k})s)\equiv 0\mod p^{k+1},

is a solution of

Lemma 2.4
.

Given

Denote

Suppose that

Definition 2.5
.

Given
f_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},i=1,2,\dots,k
,
a sequence
\{u_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
of integers defined by

u_{m+k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}f_{j}u_{m+k-j}

(2.1)

F(x)=x^{k}-f_{1}x^{k-1}-\cdots-f_{k-1}x-f_{k}.

Recall the notation introduced by Dedekind. Two integer polynomials

and

satisfy
a(x)\equiv b(x)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m,c(x))
, if and only if

is divisible by

a(x)-b(x)=c(x)q(x)+mr(x)
with integer polynomials

and
r(x)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
15
, page 606]
(Fundamental theorem on purely periodic sequences)

Let

be an integer larger than

is a linear recurrent sequence defined by (

), with the associated polynomial

, then a necessary and sufficient condition that

where

is a polynomial of degree

in

whose coefficients are determined entirely by the

and the coefficients

of (

).

.

Given

Similar to

Lemma 2.7
.

Given

,

is defined in Definition

, and
p
is an odd prime. If the induced endomorphism of

.

Proof.

\{u_{k}\mod p^{r}\}
is periodic since

. According to Theorem

, and noting

now, the period

of

Since

is an ergodic endomorphism on an

3.1
Proof of Theorem 1.1

Lemma 3.1
.

. Then we can get that
\mu(B)\leq C_{1}C^{-1}{\rm Leb}(B)
for any Borel measurable set
B
, which implies that
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to Leb.

Now we prove the claim. Denote

R=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i},b_{i}]\subset U=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i},c_{i})\quad(b_{i}<c_{i}).

Divide
(a_{i},c_{i})
into several parts such that each one has length
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}d(O_{n_{k}})
. Notice that

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\left[\frac{c_{i}-a_{i}}{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}d(O_{n_{k}})}\right]+1\right)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{2(c_{i}-a_{i})}{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n}}d(O_{n_{k}})}=C_{1}d(O_{n_{k}})^{-n}{\rm Leb}(U).

There are at most
C_{1}d(O_{n_{k}})^{-n}{\rm Leb}(U)
numbers of blocks. Since the diameter of each block is smaller than
\frac{1}{2}d(O_{n_{k}})
, every block contains at most one element of
O_{n_{k}}
.
So

\mu_{n_{k}}(U)\leq C_{1}d(O_{n_{k}})^{-n}{\rm Leb(U)}T(O_{n_{k}})^{-1}\leq C_{1}C^{-1}{\rm Leb}(U).

Thus

\mu(R)\leq\mu(U)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mu_{n_{k}}(U)\leq C_{1}C^{-1}{\rm Leb}(U).

Letting
U\to R
, we get that
\mu(R)\leq C_{1}C^{-1}{\rm Leb}(R)
.

This completes the proof of the claim and Proposition
1.4
.

∎

To finish the proof of Corollary
1.5
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
D\in M_{s}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\det(D)\neq 0
and
\det(xI-D)
is irreducible on
\mathbb{Q}
. If there is a root
r
of
\det(xI-D)
which is also a root of unity, then
D
is periodic.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a root
r
of
g(x)=\det(xI-D)
such that
r^{m}=1
but
r^{k}\neq 1
for any
1\leq k<m
. Then
r^{i},0\leq i<m
are all the roots of
x^{m}-1
, i.e.,
r
is a primitive
m
th root of unity. Since
g(x)
is irreducible,
g(x)
must equal to
\prod_{1\leq i<m,(i,m)=1}(x-r^{i})
. Thus
D
has
\varphi(m)
numbers of different eigenvalues and all of them are primitive
m
th roots of unity. So
D
is similar to a diagonal matrix which consists of primitive
m
th roots of unity. It implies that
D^{m}=I
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
\zeta
is a root of unity, and
g(x)=x^{s}-d_{s-1}x^{s-1}-\cdots-d_{0}
is the minimal polynomial of
\zeta
on
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
D
be the companion matrix of
g(x)
. If
\zeta
is a root of
f(x)=\det(xI-A)
, then there are
P\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\det(P)\neq 0
,
B\in M_{n-s}(\mathbb{Z})
and
C\in M_{(n-s)\times s}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
PA=\left(\begin{matrix}B&C\\
0&D\end{matrix}\right)P
.

Proof.

If we consider
d(x)v
as
vd(A)
, then
there is a decomposition of
V=\mathbb{Q}^{n}
as

V=\mathbb{Q}[x]v_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Q}[x]v_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Q}[x]v_{r}

such that
\{d(x):v_{i}d(A)=0\}=(d_{i}(x))
,
d_{1}(x)|d_{2}(x)|\cdots|d_{r}(x)
(See Theorem
3.1
(1)). Here
f(x)=\prod_{1\leq i\leq r}d_{i}(x)
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
v_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
d_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
.
Since
f(\zeta)=0
and
d_{1}(x)|d_{2}(x)|\cdots|d_{r}(x)
, there is some
i
such that
d_{i}(\zeta)=0
, thus
d_{r}(\zeta)=0
. There is
h(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
such that
d_{r}(x)=g(x)h(x)
. Find
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}(1\leq i\leq n-s)
such that

\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-s},v_{r}h(A),v_{r}h(A)A,\cdots,v_{r}h(A)A^{s-1}\}

is a basis of
\mathbb{Q}^{n}
. Denote by

P=\left(\begin{matrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\cdots\\
\alpha_{n-s}\\
v_{r}h(A)\\
\cdots\\
v_{r}h(A)A^{s-1}\end{matrix}\right)

Then
PA=\left(\begin{matrix}B&C\\
0&D\end{matrix}\right)P
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.
According to Proposition
1.4
, to finish the proof of Corollary
1.5
, we only have to show that if
A
is not ergodic, then there does not exist any sequence of periodic measures converging to Leb.

Suppose on the contrary that there are periodic orbits
O_{n}=\mathrm{Orb}(v_{n},A)
with period
T_{n}
such that

\frac{1}{T_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{T_{n}-1}\delta_{A^{i}v_{n}}\to{\rm Leb}\quad(n\to+\infty).

Since
A
is not ergodic with respect to Leb, there are
\zeta
and
m
such that
f(\zeta)=0
,
\zeta^{m}=1
but
\zeta^{k}\neq 1
for any
1\leq k<m
, where
f(x)=\det(xI-A)
. Suppose that
g(x)=x^{s}-d_{s-1}x^{s-1}-\cdots-d_{0}
is the minimal polynomial of
\zeta
on
\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
3.5
, there is
P\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\det(P)\neq 0
such that
PA=EP
, where
E=\left(\begin{matrix}B&C\\
0&D\end{matrix}\right)
. Since
\zeta
is a primitive
m
th root of unity, by Lemma
3.4
,
D^{m}=I
. Denote

\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n-s}\times\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{s},\quad\pi(u,v)=v.

Write

Pv_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{n}\\
w_{n}\end{array}\right),\quad w_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}^{s}.

Since the minimal period of
w_{n}
under
D
is a common factor of
m
and
T_{n}
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\delta_{D^{i}w_{n}}&=\frac{1}{T_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{T_{n}-1}\delta_{D^{i}w_{n}}\\
&=\frac{1}{T_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{T_{n}-1}\pi_{*}\delta_{E^{i}Pv_{n}}\\
&=\frac{1}{T_{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{T_{n}-1}\pi_{*}\delta_{PA^{i}v_{n}}\\
&\to\pi_{*}P_{*}{\rm Leb}\quad(n\to+\infty).\end{split}

But the average of
m
dirac measures can not converge to any non-atomic measure. This contradiction finishes the proof Corollary
1.5
.
∎

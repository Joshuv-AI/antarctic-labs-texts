Optimal bounds for many T-singularities in stable surfaces
Fernando Figueroa, Julie Rana, Giancarlo Urzúa

Abstract
. We effectively bound T-singularities on non-rational projective surfaces with an arbitrary amount of T-singularities and ample canonical class. This fully generalizes the previous work for the case of one singularity, and illustrates the vast increase in combinatorial complexity as the number of singularities grows. We find that certain combinatorial configurations lead to relatively high bounds. We classify all such configurations, and show that their non-existence gives a strong and optimal bound. As an application, we work out in detail the case of two singularities.

Introduction

The KSBA moduli space
\overline{M}_{K^{2},\chi}
, introduced by Kollár and Shepherd-Barron in
[
KSB88
]
and proved compact by Alexeev in
[

, is the compactification of Gieseker’s moduli space of surfaces of general type with fixed invariants

,
\chi

T-singularities
are the complex 2-dimensional quotient singularities that admit a one-parameter

-Gorenstein smoothing, and thus form an important class of singularities that appear on surfaces parametrized by

]
). Since

What is this list of T-singularities after we fix

and

Effective results for this question are difficult to obtain. In
[

]
, the second and third authors fully classified the case with one T-singularity.
The purpose of the present paper is to work out the case of arbitrarily many T-singularities. We make no assumptions on smoothability of surfaces containing such singularities
. We will see that even in passing from 1 singularity to 2 singularities, the combinatorial complexity increases significantly. This is shown in Theorem
6.2

W

Definition 3.8

Remark 3.9
.

A useful fact is that a

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that
G_{E_{i}}
is a tree and that a vertex
A
in
E_{i}
has more than two neighbors. If a component
H
of
G_{E_{i}}\setminus A
is contained in
E_{i}
, then
H
cannot be contracted completely before
A
is contracted.

Proof.

Suppose that such
A
exists, and let
H
be the connected component of
G_{E_{i}}
which contracts completely before
A
.

We may assume that
H
is a path graph in the following way. Suppose it is not and choose a node
B
in
H
with more than two neighbors that is farthest from
A
. By hypothesis, every neighbor of
B
is in
E_{i}
, so in order to avoid a triple point while blowing down, one of the components of
E_{i}\setminus B
must contract before
B
. This component
H^{\prime}
cannot be the one containing
A
, since
A
contracts after
H
. Therefore,
H^{\prime}
is contained in
H
, so it is completely contained in
E_{i}
. Since
B
was chosen farthest away from
A
,
H^{\prime}
cannot contain curves with more than two neighbors, so it is a path graph.

Since the component
H
contracts independently of the other components of
E_{i}
, we may also assume that
H
contracts before any curve in the other components of
E_{i}\setminus A
.

Since
H
can be contracted, it must contain a
(-1)
-curve, and this curve must intersect two different T-chains, so
H
fully contains at least one T-chain.

If
H
contains at least two T-chains, this satisfies the hypothesis of Corollary
2.4
, which leads to a contradiction.

If
H
only contains a single T-chain, then thanks to the
(-1)
-curve in
H
that should intersect two T-chains,
A
needs to be part of another T-chain. Since
H
gets contracted before any other curve, the union of this chain and
H
satisfies the hypothesis of Corollary
2.4
, which also leads to a contradiction.

Lemma 3.11
.

We always have
s_{0}=0
.

Proof.

In order to have
s_{0}>0
we must have some
E_{i}
such that each T-chain either has no curve in
E_{i}
and does not intersect it, or it is contained completely in
E_{i}
. The presence of a
(-1)
-curve in
E_{i}
and Lemma
3.2
implies the existence of a T-chain contained completely in
E_{i}
.
For this
i
, the vertices of the graph
G_{E_{i}}
correspond exactly to the curves in
E_{i}
. In particular,
G_{E_{i}}
is a tree.

If
G_{E_{i}}
is a path graph, then
by Corollary
2.4
no divisor in a center can be contracted, a contradiction.

If
G_{E_{i}}
is not a path graph, then there is a vertex
A
with more than two neighbours.
A
cannot be contracted before any of its neighbours, as that would create a triple point in a blow-down of
E_{i}
and all blow-downs have only nodes. Therefore some component of
G_{E_{i}}\setminus A
contracts before
A
. This contradicts Lemma
3.10
.

∎

Lemma 3.12
.

We always have
s_{1}=0
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
s_{1}>0
. Then there exists an
E_{i}
such that

E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\nsubseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=1.

By definition, all the T-chains with no curve in
E_{i}
and no curve intersecting
E_{i}
get omitted from
G_{E_{i}}
, so
G_{E_{i}}
is a connected simple graph.

We also claim that
G_{E_{i}}
is a tree. This is because any potential cycle would contain vertices in
E_{i}
and vertices not in
E_{i}
, which would give at least two points of intersection between curves in
E_{i}
and curves in T-chains but not in
E_{i}
, but this is not possible by our assumption on
E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\nsubseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)
.

Since
G_{E_{i}}
is a tree, we can also say that there is more than one T-chain, because otherwise a
(-1)
-curve in
E_{i}
would create a cycle in
G_{E_{i}}
. Using that
E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\nsubseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=1
we see that there is at least one T-chain completely contained in
E_{i}
.

We now deal with two cases:

(Case I):
Every vertex in
E_{i}
is connected to at most two vertices in
G_{E_{i}}
. In particular, this means that
E_{i}
is a path graph. Since
E_{i}
contains both a
(-1)
-curve and a T-chain, we have a chain satisfying the hypotheses of Proposition
2
, so no center divisor can be contracted. This contradicts the fact that there is a T-chain contained in
E_{i}
.

(Case II):
Suppose there is a vertex
A
in
E_{i}
that is connected to at least three vertices in
G_{E_{i}}
. We consider the connected components of
G_{E_{i}}\setminus A
.

Since
E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\nsubseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=1
, we know that at least two of these components are contained in
E_{i}
. If any of these components contracts completely before
A
does, then we can apply Lemma
3.10
to arrive at a contradiction.
Hence none of these components contracts completely before
A
does. Thus, contracting
(-1)
-curves until
A
becomes a
(-1)
-curve
\tilde{A}
, we have that
\tilde{A}
has at least three neighbours.

By Remark
3.9
, at least one neighbour
B
of
\tilde{A}
must be in the image of
G_{E_{i}}\setminus E_{i}
.
On the other hand, since
E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\nsubseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=1
, we see that
B
is the only neighbour of
\tilde{A}
that is in the image of
\Gamma_{E_{i}}\setminus E_{i}
. Since at least two neighbours of
\tilde{A}
of are in the image of
E_{i}
and are not contracted before
\tilde{A}
. We have that
A
appears with coefficient at least
2
in the divisor
E_{i}
. But, by pulling-back
B
and
\tilde{A}
, this would contradict our assumption that
E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\nsubseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=1
.

So, in each case we get a contradiction. Therefore
s_{1}=0
.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

We always have

K_{S}\cdot\pi(C)\leq K_{W}^{2}-K_{S}^{2}+l,

and we have equality if and only if
X=S
.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.11
and
3.12
, we know
s_{0}=s_{1}=0
, so we can set
a=1
in Equation (
2
), giving us the desired inequality.

Now if
X=S
, then
K_{S}\cdot\pi(C)=K_{S}\cdot C=\sum_{j=1}^{l}(r_{j}-d_{j}+2)
, and so by adjunction and Equation (
1
), we obtain
K_{S}\cdot\pi(C)=K_{W}^{2}-K_{S}^{2}+l
.

On the other hand, if
K_{S}\cdot\pi(C)=K_{W}^{2}-K_{S}^{2}+l
, then we have

by Lemma
3.3
. But we know that
\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}\Big)\cdot C\geq m
as

. Therefore

.
∎

Example 3.14
.

Motivated by applications to moduli of surfaces, let us suppose that
S
is a non-rational elliptic fibration. Since
K_{W}
is ample, this elliptic fibration is over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, and
q(S)=0
. Therefore
K_{S}\equiv(p_{g}(S)-1+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{m_{i}-1}{m_{i}})F
, where
m_{1},\ldots,m_{s}
are the multiplicities of the
s
multiple fibers in the elliptic fibration, and
F
is the class of a fiber. (If there are no multiple fibers, then
K_{S}\equiv(p_{g}(S)-1)F
.) Let
\pi(C)=\sum_{i=1}^{r^{\prime}}C^{\prime}_{i}
be the decomposition of
\pi(C)
into irreducible curves. Let
a_{j}
be the number of
C^{\prime}_{i}
with

. Then the inequality above implies

\Big(\sum_{j\geq 1}ja_{j}\Big)\Big(p_{g}(S)-1+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{m_{i}-1}{m_{i}}\Big)\leq K_{W}^{2}+l.

This can be used as a non-trivial condition on the curves

to construct a surface

with

ample, and
(K_{W}^{2},l)
fixed. Moreover, we have better control over this inequality if, a priori, we understand all the possible

s with

.

From now on, we will use the notation

Proposition 4.1
.

We have
R-D\leq 2(K_{W}^{2}-K_{S}^{2})+Z-\lambda,
where

is the number of
E_{i}
such that

.

Proof.

Recalling the proof of Lemma
3.4
, if

, then
E_{i}\cdot C
is equal to

or
h
depending on the existence of

or not, respectively. Since

(Lemmas

and

), we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}\cdot C=\sum_{h\geq 0}\left(\sum_{E_{i}\in S_{h}}E_{i}\right)\cdot C=2(s_{2}-Z)+Z+\sum_{h\geq 3}\left(\sum_{E_{i}\in S_{h}}E_{i}\right)\cdot C,

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that

. Then,

E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\nsubseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=2\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\subseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=-1,

By Lemma
3.4
, the equality

or

E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\nsubseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=2\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ E_{i}\cdot\Big(\sum_{C_{k,j}\subseteq E_{i}}C_{k,j}\Big)=-1,

and the first case is impossible by Lemma

Assume that

is not simple. Since the supports of

and

are simple normal crossing divisors,

in

. Note that the T-chains corresponding to

Decorated graphs and effective boundedness

We start this section by defining a new graph which will allow us to find a bound for

. We recall that

is the number of

.

Definition 5.1
.

An

is

if
E_{i}\cdot C=1
, and if
E_{i}
is not contained in any other
E_{j}
with

Recall that

Lemma 5.2
.

There are at most
l
maximal
E_{i}
.

Proof.

Two distinct
E_{j}
’s are either disjoint or one is contained in the other. By the classification in Theorem
4.17
, we also know that any maximal
E_{i}
contains an ending
(-2)
-curve of some T-chain.
∎

We now define a particular decorated graph corresponding to the configuration of
\pi
- and
\sigma
-exceptional curves on
X
.

Definition 5.3
.

First, to each T-chain on
X
we assign a vertex. Connect two vertices by an edge if there exists a maximal
E_{i}
such that the corresponding T-chains intersect the same
(-1)
-curve
F
. Label this edge with the number of
E_{j}\subset E_{i}
satisfying
E_{j}\cdot C=1
and

E_{j}
contains both

and the ending
(-2)
-curve of exactly one T-chain. Next, if the given

uses all ending
(-2)
-curves in all of the T-chains intersecting

, then we add a loop to one of the vertices. Label this loop in such a way that the sum of weights on all edges and loops is equal to the number of

such that

.

Following this process, one can check that we obtain the graphs visualized in Tables

,
2
,
3
. So, if we add the numbers assigned to the edges of the whole decorated graph, then we obtain

, i.e. the total number of
E_{i}
’s with
E_{i}\cdot C=1
.

We will pay attention to each of the connected components of this decorated graph.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a connected component of the decorated graph constructed above. Then

has three options:

(G1)

It is a tree, and so it is formed by

There is one cycle and no loops. Therefore we have
l^{\prime}
vertices and
l^{\prime}
edges, and all of its

’s are of types T.2.1 and T.2.2.

(G3)

There is exactly one loop, and there are no other cycles. Therefore there are
l^{\prime}
vertices and
l^{\prime}
edges, and all except one maximal
E_{i}
is type T.2.1 or T.2.2.

Proof.

The key observations for this proof are: Every maximal

uses some ending
(-2)
-curves, we can have

-curves only at one end of a T-chain, and all the maximal

are disjoint. Let us divide the maximal

into builder type for (T.2.1) and (T.2.2), and non-builder type for the rest.

If there are no loops or cycles, then we are in case (G1).

Assume that there is a cycle in

. By the classification in Theorem

, one can check that all edges in the cycle correspond to an

of builder type. Now take a vertex

Assume now that

Remark 5.5
.

For the combinatorially-inclined readers, Proposition
5.4
is a restatement of the following fact about directed graphs: Every vertex in a connected digraph has outdegree at most one if and only if the directed graph is a pseudotree, i.e. it has at most one cycle/loop. In our case we can turn each connected component of our decorated graph into a digraph by replacing edges with arcs as follows: having an arc beginning at a vertex
V
means that the
(-1)
-curve in the given
E_{i}
is attached to an ending
(-2)
-curve of the
T
-chain corresponding to

. Having a loop at a vertex means that all of the
(-2)
-curves in the corresponding T-chains are used in that
E_{i}
. Because a given T-chain can only have
(-2)
-curves at one end, the outdegree of each vertex in this digraph is indeed at most one.

As the next step in our argument (Propositions
5.8
through Remark
5.17
) we study the number of
E_{j}
with
E_{j}\cdot C=1
and
E_{j}\subset E_{i}
, for every possible

classified in Theorem

and displayed in Tables

,

,

. The goal is to bound that number with respect to their T-chains.

Remark 5.6
.

This is an observation we will use frequently when
K_{S}
is nef. Let
\Gamma
be a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
in
X
. By the adjunction formula, we have
K_{X}\cdot\Gamma=-2-\Gamma^{2}
. Let
\Delta
be a
(-1)
-curve in

. Then after blowing-down

. Therefore, if

, where the

Remark 5.7
.

For any T-chain

, we have (see e.g.

]
)

We will use this a lot in the next propositions, together with the iterative description of T-chains given in Proposition

Proposition 5.8
.

Assume that
K_{S}
is nef. If we have a maximal

of type T.2.1, then we have

and

Proof.

The T-chain which has curves in

is one of three possibilities:

[2,\ldots,2,4+m]

[2,\ldots,2,3,2,\ldots,2,3+m]

[2,\ldots,2,x_{1},\ldots,x_{h},2+m],

with

. In the last case, we note that there must be

such that

. So in any case, applying Remark
5.7
gives

, with equality only in the first two cases.

Since

is nef, the curve in the other T-chain that intersects the
(-1)
-curve must have self-intersection at most

. Hence, by Remark
5.7
, we have

.

If

, then this T-chain is

-curve

using Proposition

, and obtain that they are

is negative for

If

, and in fact the given

would be contained in a maximal

of type T.2.5. For the first two of these cases we again compute the discrepancies of the curves attached to

in

.
∎

Lemma 5.18

Let

be a tree with vertex set
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}\}

be the subgraph of

w_{ij}+w_{ji}=p

Proof.

Observe that for fixed

and edge

, the sum
\sum_{j=1}^{p}w_{ij}
is the total number of vertices in the subgraph of

obtained by removing

, and so
w_{ij}+w_{ji}=p
.

V=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}\}
is the vertex set. Assume that for each
V_{i}\in V
and edge
V_{i}V_{j}\in E
, we have assigned nonnegative real numbers
a_{i}
and
z_{ij}
satisfying
a_{i}\geq z_{ij}=z_{ji}
. Then we have the following inequality

(p-1)\sum_{j=1}^{p}a_{j}\geq p\sum_{V_{i}V_{j}\in E}z_{ij}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.18
, we can assign positive integers
w_{ij}
satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{p}w_{ij}=p-1
and
w_{ij}+w_{ji}=p
. So fix an edge
\{i,j\}\in E
. Then by assumption we have

a_{j}\geq z_{ij}
.
Multiplying both sides of this inequality by
w_{ji}
, we obtain the inequality

w_{ji}a_{j}\geq w_{ji}b_{ij}

Summing over
i
and using that
\sum_{i=1}^{p}w_{ji}=p-1
, we obtain

a_{j}\cdot(p-1)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{p}w_{ji}z_{ij}.

Now taking a sum over
j
, we have

(p-1)\sum_{j=1}^{p}a_{j}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{p}w_{ji}z_{ij}=\sum_{V_{i}V_{j}\in E}(w_{ij}+w_{ji})z_{ij}=p\sum_{V_{i}V_{j}\in E}z_{ij},

where here we have used that
w_{ii}=0
, and
z_{ij}=z_{ji}
.
∎

Lemma 5.21
.

Let
G=\langle V,E\rangle
be a finite tree. Then there is a labeling
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}\}
of the vertices of
G
so that there is a bijection
\{V_{2},\ldots V_{p}\}\to E
such that each vertex maps to an edge containing it, i.e. for each
i
we have
V_{i}\mapsto V_{i}V_{j}
for some
j
. (In this labeling, vertex
V_{p}
corresponds to a leaf of the tree.)

Proof.

We induct on the number of vertices. The statement is trivial if
|V|=2
. Now suppose the lemma holds for
p-1
vertices and let
G=\langle V,E\rangle
be a finite tree with vertex set
\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}\}
. Relabel the graph so that
V_{p}
corresponds to a leaf, i.e. a vertex with only one edge. Removing
V_{p}
and its incident edge

which by the inductive hypothesis gives us a bijection

that extends to

and satisfies the desired property.
∎

Corollary 5.22
.

Let
G=\langle V,E\rangle
be a finite tree with vertex set
\{V_{1},

\ldots,

V_{p}\}
. Assume we have assigned to each vertex

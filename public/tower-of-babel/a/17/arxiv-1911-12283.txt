On the superspecial loci of orthogonal type Shimura varieties
Haining Wang

Abstract
. In this note, we study the superspecial loci of orthogonal type Shimura varieties of signature ( n − 2 , 2 ) (n-2,2) with n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . We prove a conjecture of Gross on the parametrizations of the superspecial locus in the special fiber of an orthogonal type Shimura variety and its lift in the integral model by certain homogeneous spaces. As applications, we provide a mass formula for the superspecial locus. We also indicate how Gross’s conjectures can be generalized to the Coxeter type Shimura varieties using the group theoretic method of Görtz and He.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be an odd prime. An abelian variety
A
over a field of characteristic
p
is said to be
superspecial
if it is isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves under the base change to an algebraically closed field. Consider
\mathscr{A}_{g,\mathbb{F}_{p}}
the moduli space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of principal polarized abelian varieties. Let
\mathscr{A}_{g,\mathrm{ssp}}
be the subset of
\mathscr{A}_{g,\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
which consists of superspecial points. This is a finite and closed subset of
\mathscr{A}_{g}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. The structure of
\mathscr{A}_{g,\mathrm{ssp}}
is well studied
[
KO87
,
Yu06
]
. In particular there is a uniformization of
\mathscr{A}_{g,\mathrm{ssp}}
by the double coset
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}_{f})/G^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
where
G^{\prime}
is an inner form of the symplectic similitude group
\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}
. Using this uniformization, one can give a quantitive result about the size of superspecial locus known as the mass formula see
[
Eke87
,
Yu06
]
. This is a generalization of the famous Deuring’s mass formula for supersingular elliptic curves which says

\sum_{[E]}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Aut}(E)|}=\frac{p-1}{24}.

The study of superspecial locus and its mass formula has been generalized to PEL type Shimura varieties in
[
Yu11
]
. In this note we provide a generalization of the above theme to the context of orthogonal type Shimura varieties. Being an abelian type Shimura variety, an orthogonal type Shimura variety does not admit an immediate moduli interpretation and therefore it is not clear how to define the superspecial locus. In this note, we simply define the superspecial locus to be the
minimal Ekedahl-Oort stratum
in the Ekedahl-Oort stratification of the orthogonal type Shimura variety. The Ekedahl-Oort stratifications for abelian type Shimura varieties are studied in
[
SZ17
]
and its local analogues for Rapoport-Zink spaces are studied in
[
Shen17
]
. We will not use their theory explicitly but rather our starting point is the
Bruhat-Tits stratification
of the
\mathrm{GSpin}
-type Rapoport-Zink space investigated in
[
HP17
]
. Our definition of the superspecial locus for the
\mathrm{GSpin}
-type Rapoport-Zink is the union of Bruhat-Tits strata of minimal type. The minimal Ekedahl-Oort stratum and the minimal Bruhat-Tits stratum in fact agrees by general theory explained in
[
GH15
]
. Then the superspecial locus for the orthogonal type Rapoport-Zink space is simply the image of the superspecial locus for the
\mathrm{GSpin}
-type Rapoport-Zink space under the natural étale covering map. We show this agrees with the naive way of defining the superspecial locus which is simply restricting the superspecial locus of the Siegel type Rapoport-Zink space to the
\mathrm{GSpin}
-type, then project down to the orthogonal type Rapoport-Zink space. The corresponding superspecial locus for the orthogonal type Shimura variety can be defined in an analogues way and linked to the Rapoport-Zink space via the uniformization theorem of Rapoport-Zink.

1.1.
Gross’ conjecture

We are led to study the superspecial locus of an orthogonal type Shimura variety by the manuscripts of Gross
[
Gro18a
,
Gro18b
]
. Let
(V,Q)
be a quadratic space over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
whose determinant
\mathrm{det}(V)
is a unit and whose Hasse invariant
\epsilon(V)=1
. Let
G=\mathrm{SO}(L)
be the group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
defined by a self-dual lattice
L
of
V
. Let
\mu:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow G_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
be a suitable miniscule cocharacter and
[b]\in B(G,\mu)
be the unique basic element in the acceptable part of the Kottwitz set
B(G,\mu)
defined by
[
Rap05
, 4.5, 4.6]
. Given this datum one can attach a basic local Shimura variety
\breve{\mathcal{M}}=\breve{\mathcal{M}}(G,\mu,b)
which we will refer to as the orthogonal type Rapoport-Zink space, see section
3.1
for more precise statements. We will write
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{red}}
the underlying reduced scheme of
\breve{\mathcal{M}}
. We also need to consider the nearby quadratic space
V^{\prime}
which is defined to be
V^{\prime}=V^{\Phi}_{K_{0}}
. Here
K_{0}
is the fraction field of the Witt ring
W_{0}=W(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
and
\Phi
is the operator given by
b\sigma
where
\sigma
is the Frobenius on
K_{0}
. Then the quadratic space
(V^{\prime},Q)
has the same determinant as
V
but is of Hasse-Witt invariant
\epsilon(V^{\prime})=-1
. Let
J_{b}=\mathrm{SO}(V^{\prime})
which acts naturally on
\breve{\mathcal{M}}
. Using the
Bruhat-Tits stratification
for
\breve{\mathcal{M}}
introduced in
[
HP17
]
and
[
Shen17
]
, we will introduce the
superspecial locus

\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
of
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{red}}
. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt{D})
be the unramified quadratic extension with
D
being a unit of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Gross considered the set of
oriented planes

W
of discriminant
-D
and Hasse-Witt invariant
\epsilon(W)=-1
embedded in
V^{\prime}
. Notice that such an oriented plane
W
determines a homomorphism
h:T=\mathrm{SO}(W)\rightarrow\mathrm{SO}(V^{\prime})
and the conjugacy classes of such homomorphisms is parametrized by the
K
-analytic manifold

X=\mathrm{SO}(V^{\prime})/\mathrm{SO}(W)\times\mathrm{SO}(W^{\perp}).

This is of course the non-archimedean analogue of the conjugacy classes
h:\mathbb{S}\rightarrow G_{\mathbb{R}}
where
\mathbb{S}=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the Deligne torus which form part of a Shimura datum. However
\mathrm{SO}(W^{\perp})
is not always compact and Gross introduced the following modification. Let
W=K.e
wth
\mathrm{Nm}(e)=p
, then
W
has a canonical lattice
\mathcal{O}_{K}.e
where
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is the ring of integers in
K
and
\mathcal{O}_{K}.e
provides a natural integral structure of the plane
W
via its chosen orientation. Let

Y=\mathrm{SO}(V^{\prime})/\mathrm{SO}(\mathcal{O}_{K}.e)\times\mathrm{SO}(M)

where
M
is a lattice in
W^{\perp}
with stabilizer
\mathrm{SO}(M)
. Therefore
Y
parametrizes those pairs
(W,M)
for an oriented plane
W
and a lattice
M
in the orthogonal complement
W^{\perp}
. Then
Y
is an
K
-analytic manifold whose
K
structure is induced by the orientation on
W
. Since
\epsilon(V^{\prime})=-1
, we let
\Lambda
be an almost self-dual lattice in
V^{\prime}
. Finally Gross introduced the space

Z=\mathrm{SO}(V^{\prime})/\mathrm{SO}(\Lambda,\pm)

where
\mathrm{SO}(\Lambda,\pm)
is the stabilizer of
\Lambda
with a fixed orientation of the quadratic space
\Lambda/\Lambda^{\vee}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. It is conjectured in
[
Gro18a
, Conjecture 1]
that

•

Z
is the superspecial locus
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
of
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{red}}
.

•

Y
is the set of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-points in
\breve{\mathcal{M}}
whose reductions lie in
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
.

•

X
is related to the image of the de Rham period map
\pi_{\mathrm{dR}}:\breve{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{rig}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
where
\mathcal{Q}
is the flag variety associated to
G
.

The main result of this note is the confirmation of these conjectures.

Theorem 1
.

Let
Def_{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
be the set of points in
\breve{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
whose reductions lie in
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
and let
Def_{\mathrm{ssp}}^{\mathrm{rig}}(K)
be the rigid analytic
K
-points of
Def_{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
.

(1)

There is a bijection between
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
and the homogeneous space

Z
.

(2)

There is a bijection between
Def_{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
and the homogeneous space
Y
.

(3)

The image of
\pi_{\mathrm{dR}}
restricted to
Def^{\mathrm{rig}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}(K)
is precisely the homogeneous space
X
.

In the main body of this note, part
(1)
is proved in Theorem
3.22
, part
(2)
is proved in Corollary
5.10
and part
(3)
is proved in Theorem
5.13
. We also remark that Gross made much more general conjectures about the superspecial loci of Shimura varieties. The method of this note can be applied to confirm his conjectures in the case of Coxeter type Shimura varieties
[
GH15
]
. This is indicated in section
4
of this note. In particular, one can prove Gross’ conjecture in the case of unitary Shimura varieties of signature
(n-1,1)
by simply using the Bruhat-Tits stratification introduced by Vollaard and Wedhorn
[
Vol10
]
,
[
VW11
]
. This is a simpler case as these unitary Shimura varieties are of PEL type and they do admit convenient moduli interpretations.

1.2.
Applications to Shimura varieties

We will now move to a global set-up and we will denote by
(V,Q)
a quadratic space
\mathbb{Q}
of signature
(n-2,2)
with
n\geq 3
. Suppose that the
V
has unit determinant and Hasse-Witt invariant
1
at
p
. Let
U=U_{p}U^{p}
with
U_{p}=G(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and
U^{p}
sufficiently small. Similar to the local set-up, we denote by
V^{\prime}
to be the nearby quadratic space whose signature is
(n,0)
and whose determinant is the same as
V
at
p
and Hasse-Witt invariant is
-1
. Then we write
\mathscr{S}_{U,W_{0}}
over
W_{0}
the canonical integral model for the Shimura varietiy for the special orthogonal group
G

[
Kis10
,
MP16
]
. Let
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ss}}
and
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
be the supersingular and superspecial locus of

\mathscr{S}_{U,\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
constructed in
[
SZ17
]
. The Rapoport-Zink uniformization theorem furnishes an isomorphism

I(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\breve{\mathcal{M}}\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p})/K^{p}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}_{U,W_{0}})/\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ss}}

where
I=\mathrm{SO}(V^{\prime})
is the special orthogonal group defined by the quadratic space
V^{\prime}
and
(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}_{U,W_{0}})/\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ss}}
is the completion of
{\mathscr{S}}_{U,W_{0}}
along the supersingular locus
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ss}}
.
Then part
(1)
and
(2)
of Theorem
1
imply the following double coset parametrization of the superspecial locus
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
and its
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-lift
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
in
(\widehat{\mathscr{S}}_{U,W_{0}})/\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ss}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
. Let
U_{0,p}=\mathrm{SO}(\Lambda,\pm)
,
U_{1,p}=\mathrm{SO}(\mathcal{O}_{K}.e)\times\mathrm{SO}(M)
.

Theorem 2
.

We have the following double cosets parametrizations.

(1)

There is a double coset description of the superspecial locus
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})
given by

\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})\xrightarrow{\sim}I(\mathbb{Q})\backslash I(\mathbb{A}_{f})/U_{0,p}U^{p}.

(2)

There is a double coset description of
\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
given by

\mathscr{S}^{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathcal{O}_{K})\xrightarrow{\sim}I(\mathbb{Q})\backslash I(\mathbb{A}_{f})/U_{1,p}U^{p}.

In the main body of this note, part
(1)
is proved in Theorem
6.5
and part
(2)
is proved in Theorem
6.7
.

As an application of the double coset parametrization, we provide a geometric mass formula similar to the simple mass formula for PEL type Shimura varieties given in
[
Yu11
]
. Let
x\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
be a superspecial point and let
g_{x}\in I(\mathbb{Q})\backslash I(\mathbb{A}_{f})/U_{0,p}U^{p}
be the corresponding class and we choose a representative in
I(\mathbb{A}_{f})
denoted by the same symbol. We define the finite group
\Gamma_{x}
by the following formula

g_{x}Ug^{-1}_{x}\cap I(\mathbb{Q})=\Gamma_{x}.

We define the arithmetic mass for the superspecial locus
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
using the formula

\mathrm{Mass}^{a}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\sum_{x\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{x}|}.

We also define the geometric mass of
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
to be

\mathrm{Mass}^{g}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\sum_{x\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(x)|}

where the definition of
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(x)
is geometric in nature and can be found in section
6.2
and we only mention that this group is essentially the automorphism group of
x
preserving Hodge cycles on the abelian varitiety given by

.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathrm{Vol}(U)=[I(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}):U]
. We have

(1)

\mathrm{Mass}^{a}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\mathrm{Mass}^{g}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}
.

(2)

\mathrm{Mass}^{a}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Vol}(U)\prod^{m}_{r=1}\zeta(1-2r)\frac{1}{2^{m-1}}\frac{p^{2m}-1}{2(p+1)}&\text{if $n=2m+1$};\\
\mathrm{Vol}(U)\prod^{m}_{r=1}\zeta(1-2r)L(1-m,\chi)\frac{1}{2^{m-1}}\frac{(p^{m-1}+1)(p^{m+1})}{2(p+1)}&\text{if $n=2m$}.\end{cases}

2.
Preliminaries on quadratic spaces

We begin by reviewing some standard notions in the theory of quadratic spaces and lattices in them. Then we introduce some homogeneous spaces that are conjectured by Gross to be the parametrizing spaces for the superspecial loci of the orthogonal type Shimura varieties and Rapoprot-Zink spaces considered in this note. Everything here can be found in
[
Gro18a
]
.

2.1.
Orthogonal spaces

Let
(V,Q)
be a quadratic space of dimension
n\geq 3
over
F
where
F
is a general field of characteristic not equal to
2
. Then we can define a symmetric form

[\hskip 2.84526pt,\hskip 2.84526pt]:V\times V\rightarrow F

by the formula
[x,y]=Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)
which we assume is non-degenerate. We can choose a basis
\{e_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
such that
Q(\sum^{n}_{i=1}x_{i}e_{i})=a_{1}x^{2}_{1}+a_{2}x^{2}_{2}+\cdots a_{n}x^{2}_{n}.
We call the product
\mathrm{det}(V)=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}
the
determinant
of
(V,Q)
and this is an invariant well-defined in
F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
. When
\mathrm{det}(V)\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F}F^{\times 2}/F^{\times 2}
, we say
V
has unit determinant. Let
a_{i},a_{j}
be two classes in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(F,\mu_{2})=F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
and
(a_{i},a_{j})\in\mathrm{H}^{2}(F,\mu_{2})
be their cup product in
\mathrm{H}^{2}(F,\mu_{2})=Br_{2}(F)
where
Br_{2}(F)
is the two torsion subgroup of the Brauer group of
F
. Then the
Hasse-Witt invariant
of
V
is defined as
\epsilon(V)=\sum_{i<j}(a_{i},a_{j})\in Br_{2}(F)
. Suppose that
(V,Q)
is a two dimensional quadratic space then an
orientation
of
(V,Q)
is an embedding
\iota:K\rightarrow\End(V)
such that
Q(\iota(\alpha)x)=\mathrm{Nm}(\alpha)Q(x)
for the algebra
K=F[t]/(t^{2}+\mathrm{det}(V))
. The following lemma is well known.

Lemma 2.1
.

An orientation for
(V,Q)
is equivalent to a choice of an isotropic line in
(V_{K},Q)
.

Suppose now
F=F_{v}
is a local field. Then we distinguish the following situations

•

F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
, then
(V,Q)
is determined by its dimension;

•

F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
, then
(V,Q)
is determined by its signature;

•

F_{v}
is non-archimedean, then
(V,Q)
is determined by its dimension, determinant and its Hasse-Witt invariant.

2.2.
Orthogonal lattices

We assume now
F
is a local non-archimedean field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and uniformizer
\pi_{F}
. The residue field will be denoted as
k
.
As usual we assume that the residue characteristic is not
2
. An orthogonal lattice
L\subset V
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module that spans
V
, we denote by

L^{\vee}=\{x\in V:[x,L]\in\mathcal{O}_{F}\}

the integral dual of
L
. We say
L
is
self-dual
if
L=L^{\vee}
and for
L
to be self-dual it is necessary that
\mathrm{det}(V)\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F}F^{\times 2}/F^{\times 2}
is a unit and the Hasse-Witt invaraint is
1
. We say
L
is
almost-self-dual
if
L/L^{\vee}
is a two dimensional quadratic space over the residue field
\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi_{F}
, which is isomorphic as an orthogonal space to the quadratic extension of
\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi_{F}
with its norm form. The following theorem summarizes when a self-dual or an almost self-dual lattice exists in a quadratic space.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(V,Q)
be a quadratic space over
F
of dimension
n
whose determinant is a unit.

•

If
\epsilon(V)=1
, then
V
contains a self-dual lattice
L
. There is a transitive action of the special orthogonal group
\mathrm{SO}(V)(F)
on these lattices.

•

If
\epsilon(V)=-1
, then
V
contains an almost-self-dual lattice
L
. There is a transitive action of the special orthogonal group
\mathrm{SO}(V)(F)
on these lattices.

Proof.

This is well-known and see
[
Gro18a
, Theorem 6.1]
for a complete proof.
∎

The stabilizer
H(L)
of such a lattice is a parahoric subgroups of
\mathrm{SO}(V)(F)
:

•

if
\epsilon=1
, then
H(L)
is a hyperspecial subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}(V)(F)
;

•

if
\epsilon=-1
, then
H(L)
is a parahoric subgroup of the non-quasi-split group
\mathrm{SO}(V)(F)
.

2.3.
Some homogeneous spaces

As a motivational example, consider first the Hermitian symmetric space
X
associated to the group
\mathrm{SO}(V)
for
V
a real quadratic space of signature
(n-2,2)
and recall we always assume that
n\geq 3
. Then
X
is the set of oriented negative definite two planes
W
in
V
which is a complex manifold of dimension
n-2
with a transitive action of
\mathrm{SO}(V)
. Now an oriented negative definite plane
W
in
V
gives rise to a homomorphism
h:\mathrm{SO}(W)\rightarrow\mathrm{SO}(V)
over
\mathbb{R}
whose centralizer is
H=\mathrm{SO}(W)\times\mathrm{SO}(W^{\perp})
and therefore we have
X=\mathrm{SO}(V)/H
. The tangent space at identity is given by
\Hom(W,W^{\perp})
which has a complex Hermitian structure coming from the chosen orientation of
W
.

We now consider the case
(V,Q)
is a quadratic space over a local non-archimedean field
F
with
\epsilon(V)=-1
and unit determinant. Let
K=F(\sqrt{D})
be the unramified quadratic extension of
F
. Gross introduced the following homogeneous spaces and conjectured their relation with the superspecial locus.

•

The space
X=\mathrm{SO}(V)/\mathrm{SO}(W)\times\mathrm{SO}(W^{\perp})
: We let
X
be the set of oriented planes
W
in
V
with fixed determinant
-D
and
\epsilon(W)=-1
. Then exactly as the real case we have
X=\mathrm{SO}(V)/H
where
H=\mathrm{SO}(W)\times\mathrm{SO}(W^{\perp})
.

•

The space
Y=\mathrm{SO}(V)/\mathrm{SO}(\mathcal{O}_{K}.e)\times\mathrm{SO}(M)
: However
\mathrm{SO}(W^{\perp})
is not always compact and Gross introduced the following modification. Given the orientation on
W
, we can write
W=K.e
wth
\mathrm{Nm}(e)=\pi_{F}
. Then
W
has a canonical lattice
\mathcal{O}_{K}.e
. Let
Y=\mathrm{SO}(V)/\mathrm{SO}(\mathcal{O}_{K}.e)\times\mathrm{SO}(M)
where
\mathcal{O}_{K}.e
provides a natural integral structure of the plane
W
via its chosen orientation and
M
is a self-dual lattice in
W^{\perp}
. Therefore
Y
parametrizes those pairs
(W,M)
for an oriented two plane
W
and a lattice
M
in its orthogonal complement
W^{\perp}
. Then
Y
is an
K
-analytic manifold whose
K
structure is induced by the orientation on
W
.

•

The space
Z=\mathrm{SO}(V)/\mathrm{SO}(\Lambda,\pm)
: Since
\epsilon(V)=-1
, we let
\Lambda
be an almost self-dual lattice in
V
. Another homogeneous space we will consider is
Z=\mathrm{SO}(V)/\mathrm{SO}(\Lambda,\pm)
where
\mathrm{SO}(\Lambda,\pm)
is the stabilizer of
\Lambda
with a fixed orientation on
\Lambda/\Lambda^{\vee}
.

There is a natural map

\mathrm{red}:Y\rightarrow Z.

The map
\mathrm{red}
sends
(W,M)
to
\Lambda=M+\mathcal{O}_{K}.e
with the orientation of
\Lambda/\Lambda^{\vee}
induced by the orientation of
W
. The fibers of this map
\mathrm{red}^{-1}(z)
with
z\in Z
are quite simple and can be shown to be polydiscs. The following proposition can be proved using the Moy-Prasad filtration of the parahoric subgroup
\mathrm{SO}(\Lambda,\pm)
. See
[
Gro18a
, Theorem 9.1]

Proposition 2.2
.

The fibers
\mathrm{red}^{-1}(z)
of
\mathrm{red}:Y\rightarrow Z
are polydiscs of the form
(\pi\mathcal{O}_{K})^{n-2}
.

We will show later that the homogeneous space
Z
can be used to parametrize the superspecial locus on the orthogonal type Rapoport-Zink space while
Y
can be used to parametrize the
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-lift of the superspecial locus. Finally
X
will be related to the admissible locus of the period domain.

3.
Bruhat-Tits stratification of orthogonal type Rapoport-Zink spaces

In this section we review the theory of Howard and Pappas on the Bruhat-Tits stratification of the basic Rapoport-Zink space of
\mathrm{GSpin}
type. In this section we fix an algebraic closure
\mathbb{F}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and write
W_{0}=W(\mathbb{F})

3.1.
Local Shimura varieties of orthogonal type

We fix a quadratic space

over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

throughout this section. By Theorem

\delta^{*}=-\delta

3.5.
Parametrization of the superspecial locus

Now we are ready to relate
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
to the homogeneous space
Z
introduced in §2.3. We first point out why the space
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
introduced in Definition

deserves to be called the superspecial locus.

Definition 3.19
.

Let
X
be a
p
-divisible group and
\mathbb{D}(X)(W_{0})
be its covariant Dieudonné module. Then we call
X
superspecial if

F^{2}\mathbb{D}(X)(W_{0})=p\mathbb{D}(X)(W_{0}).

Lemma 3.20
.

The set
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathbb{F})
consists of those points

such that the
p
-divisible group
X_{y}
associated to

is superspecial.

Proof.

Consider the special lattice
L^{\sharp}_{y}=\{z\in V_{K_{0}}:zM_{y}\subset M_{y}\}
. Suppose that

, then
\Phi^{2}(L^{\sharp}_{y})=L^{\sharp}_{y}
. We have the relation
\Phi(z)=F\circ z\circ F^{-1}
for

in
\End(M_{y})
. This implies by definition that

\Phi^{2}(L^{\sharp}_{y})=\{z\in V_{K_{0}}:zF^{2}M_{y}\subset F^{2}M_{y}\}
. On the other hand,
\Phi^{2}(L^{\sharp}_{y})=L^{\sharp}_{y}
implies that
\Phi^{2}(L^{\sharp}_{y})=\{z\in V_{K_{0}}:zM_{y}\subset M_{y}\}
. This implies that

for some

. But we know
X_{y}
has slope

. This implies that

. This shows that for
y\in\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathbb{F})
,
X_{y}
is superspecial.

The above argument also shows that

is superspecial if and only if
\Phi^{2}(L^{\sharp}_{y})=L^{\sharp}_{y}
. Notice by Proposition
3.13
,
\Phi^{2}(L^{\sharp}_{y})=L^{\sharp}_{y}
if and only if

. This finishes the proof.

\breve{\mathcal{M}}
is the image of the restriction of the usual superspecial locus of
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{GSp}(C(L))}
to the
\breve{\mathcal{M}}^{\Diamond}
. See the diagram in (

Theorem 3.22
.

There is a bijection between
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{ssp}}(\mathbb{F})
and the homogeneous space

Z=\mathrm{SO}(V^{\Phi}_{K_{0}})/\mathrm{SO}(\Lambda_{0},\pm)

where
\Lambda_{0}
is a fixed type
2
vertex lattice.

Proof.

One can find an element

such that its lift

whose similitude satisfies

. Then it follows that
\tilde{g}
swaps

and
x_{\bullet}
in

but then

swaps
x_{\circ}
and
x_{\bullet}
in
\breve{\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{F})

implies that

acts transitively on the set

Recall that giving an orientation on

4.
Comparison with Affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

In this section we would like indicate how the results proved in Theorem

can be extended to cover all the Coxeter type Shimura varieties. The Coxeter type Shimura varieties are introduced by Görtz and He

in terms of the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties. In the following we will review their theory and we abbreviate affine Deligne-Lusztig variety by ADLV.

4.1.
Affine Deligne Lusztig variety

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\breve{F}
be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of

G
be a connected reductive group over

\breve{G}
its base change to

Denote by

Lemma 5.4

There is an identification
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{cris},y}=\mathbb{D}(X_{x})(W_{0})

∎

Lemma 5.5

{F}^{1}\Phi_{*}(L_{x,\mathbb{F}})

6.2.
Geometric Mass formula

Finally, we deduce some quantitive results about the size of the double coset in Theorem
6.5
. This is usually refereed to as the Mass formula in the literature. See
[
Yu11
]
for a treatment in the PEL type case. Our modest goal of this short subsection is to provide an analogue of the simple Mass formula in the setting of orthogonal type Shimura varieties.

Let
x\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
be a superspecial point and let
g_{x}\in I(\mathbb{Q})\backslash I(\mathbb{A}_{f})/U_{0,p}U^{p}
be the corresponding class and we choose a representative in
I(\mathbb{A}_{f})
denoted by the same symbol. Let
U=U_{0,p}U^{p}
we define the finite group
\Gamma_{x}
by the following formula

g_{x}Ug^{-1}_{x}\cap I(\mathbb{Q})=\Gamma_{x}.

We define the arithmetic mass
\mathrm{Mass}^{a}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}
for the superspecial locus
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
using the formula

\mathrm{Mass}^{a}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\sum_{x\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{x}|}

where
|\Gamma_{x}|
is the size of the group
\Gamma_{x}
.

Next we give a geometric interpretation of the group
\Gamma_{g_{x}}
. For this, let
\tilde{x}\in\mathscr{S}^{\Diamond}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
be a lift of
x
and let
A_{\tilde{x}}
be the abelian variety corresponding to
\tilde{x}\in{\mathscr{S}^{\Diamond}_{ssp}}
. It is equipped with a polarization
\lambda_{\tilde{x}}
and a prime to
p
level structure
\epsilon^{p}_{\tilde{x}}
. We will write
\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}}=(A_{\tilde{x}},\lambda_{\tilde{x}},\epsilon^{p}_{\tilde{x}})
for this datum. Let
\mathrm{Isom}(\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}},\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the self-quasi-isogenies of
A_{\tilde{x}}
that preserve the additional structures. Recall there are quadratic spaces
\mathbf{V}_{\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}}^{p}\subset\End(V^{p}(A_{\tilde{x}}))
and
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}}\subset\End(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}})
defined in (
5.1
), (
5.2
) with
\mathbf{V}_{\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}}^{p}=\prod_{l\neq p}\mathbf{V}_{l,\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}}
. Define the group
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}},\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}})
to be the largest closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Isom}(\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}},\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that its image in
\End(V^{p}(A_{\tilde{x}}))
lands in
\mathrm{GSpin}(\mathbf{V}^{p}_{\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}})
and its image in
\End(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}})
lands in
\mathrm{GSpin}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}})
. The group
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}},\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}})
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{GSpin}(V^{\prime})
by
[
MP15
, Theorem 6.4]
. We define the group
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(x)
to be the image of
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}},\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}})
in
\mathrm{SO}(V^{\prime})
. Using this we define the geometric mass of
\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}
to be

\mathrm{Mass}^{g}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\sum_{x\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}\frac{1}{|\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(x)|}.

Proposition 6.8
.

The group
\Gamma_{x}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(x)
and therefore

\mathrm{Mass}^{g}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\mathrm{Mass}^{a}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}.

Proof.

Notice that
I^{\Diamond}(\mathbb{Q})
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{Isom}(\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}},\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}})_{\mathbb{Q}}
in the isogeny category of
(A_{\tilde{x}},\lambda_{\tilde{x}})
that preserve the Hodge cycles on
A_{\tilde{x}}
. Then by definition its image in
\End(V^{p}(A_{\tilde{x}}))
lands in
\mathrm{GSpin}(V(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f}))
and its image in
\End(\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}})
is
\mathrm{GSpin}(V^{\prime}_{p})
. Since the abelian variety
A_{\tilde{x}}
is supersingular, it follows from
[
MP15
, Theorem 6.4]
that the image of
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}},\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}})
in
\mathrm{GSpin}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{cris},\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}})
agrees with
\mathrm{GSpin}(V^{\prime}_{p})
. It is also clear that the image of
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}},\underline{A}_{\tilde{x}})
in
\mathrm{GSpin}(\mathbf{V}_{l,\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}})
agrees that of
I^{\Diamond}(\mathbb{Q})
in
\mathrm{GSpin}(\mathbf{V}_{l,\tilde{x},\mathbb{Q}}))
for all
l\neq p
. Then we can conclude that
\Gamma_{x}
agrees with
\mathrm{Isom}_{\mathbf{V}}(x)
essentially by Hasse priniciple.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
\mathrm{Vol}(U)=[I(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}):U]
.

\mathrm{Mass}^{g}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Vol}(U)\prod^{m}_{r=1}\zeta(1-2r)\frac{1}{2^{m-1}}\frac{p^{2m}-1}{2(p+1)}&\text{if $n=2m+1$};\\
\mathrm{Vol}(U)\prod^{m}_{r=1}\zeta(1-2r)L(1-m,\chi)\frac{1}{2^{m-1}}\frac{(p^{m-1}+1)(p^{m+1})}{2(p+1)}&\text{if $n=2m$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

This quantity can be calculated explicitly using the following Shimura’s Mass formula see
[
GHY01
]
. We give some details of this formula below. Notice that

\mathrm{Mass}^{a}_{\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}=\sum_{x\in\mathscr{S}_{\mathrm{ssp}}}\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{x}|}=\mathrm{Vol}(U)\mathrm{Mass}(\Lambda)

for a suitable maximal lattice
\Lambda
in
V^{\prime}
. Then the formula in this corollary follows from
[
GHY01
, Proposition 7.4, Proposition 7.5]
. Note that here we applying the formula in
[
GHY01
, Proposition 7.4, Proposition 7.5]
in the case when the quadratic space has unit determinant and Hasse-Witt invariant
-1
.
∎
